**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Rockwell Automation is in the middle of a textbook industrial-automation upcycle, and the data confirm the turn is real. Fiscal Q2 2026 (March quarter) delivered sales of $2,239M, up 12% reported and 9% organic, with enterprise operating margin of 22.5% (from 19.0%) and adjusted EPS of $3.30, up 32% year over year. Software & Control, the highest-quality segment, grew sales 20% with segment margin of 34.9%. Management raised full-year FY2026 guidance to roughly $8.9B in revenue. The cyclical backdrop is cooperating: U.S. Manufacturing PMI crossed back above 50 (52.7 in March 2026, the highest in over three years), which historically leads Rockwell's short-cycle product demand. The company has now beaten consensus four straight quarters by 10-13%, and EPS estimate revisions are broadly positive (+9.1% on the current quarter over 30 days, +7.2% on the current fiscal year over 90 days).

The difficulty is entirely on valuation. The stock has appreciated roughly 41% over the trailing twelve months and trades at approximately 47x trailing GAAP EPS and roughly 35x the FY2026 consensus of $12.98. The trailing multiple overstates expense because FY2025 GAAP EPS of $7.67 was depressed by non-recurring items (the Sensia JV impairment, an asbestos accounting-method change, higher compensation), so adjusted FY2025 EPS was $10.53. Even on a clean forward basis, the multiple has already discounted the recovery and then some. Incremental margins are excellent, but the stock now sits near the top of its 52-week range, at a premium that requires the upcycle to extend for several years and the margin gains to prove structural rather than cyclical.

We rate ROK a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale. The business quality, margin program execution, and macro tailwind are genuine, and we would not be short this name. At ~35x forward earnings with PMI-dependent demand, a $2B greenfield capex ramp, and capital returns now funded partly by rising commercial paper, the risk/reward is symmetric at best. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the high-$300s, or on confirmation that the margin step-up survives a full year of volume normalization.

## Company Overview

Rockwell is the largest pure-play industrial automation and digital-transformation company, headquartered in Milwaukee with approximately 26,000 employees. Its strategy centers on the "Connected Enterprise," integrating control hardware, software, and services. The competitive core is the integrated control architecture built around the Logix platform and the Allen-Bradley brand, which Rockwell positions as the only environment supporting discrete, process, batch, safety, motion, robotics, and power control together. The fiscal year ends September 30.

The company reports three segments (FY2025 sales / operating margin):
- **Intelligent Devices** — $3,756M, 18.0% margin (drives, motors, sensors, safety; point-in-time product sales)
- **Software & Control** — $2,383M, 29.7% margin (Logix controllers, software; highest-margin and fastest-growing)
- **Lifecycle Services** — $2,203M, 14.5% margin (solutions, consulting, services; recognized over time)

Roughly 65% of global sales flow through independent distributors, and the two largest distributors account for about 20% of total sales. The U.S. is over half of revenue. Management estimates an addressed market near $120B. Principal competitors are Siemens, Schneider Electric, ABB, Emerson, Mitsubishi Electric, and Honeywell.

## Financial Analysis

The balance sheet is conservative and the cash-flow profile has improved sharply. Debt/equity is 0.17, the current ratio is 1.14, ROE is roughly 20%, and credit ratings are investment grade (S&P A-, Moody's A3, Fitch A, all Stable). Free cash flow more than doubled to $1,358M in FY2025 from $639M, driven by cost reductions and the absence of incentive-comp payouts in Q1 FY2025 (a consequence of weak FY2024 results).

The headline FY2025 GAAP numbers are misleading. Net income attributable to Rockwell fell to $869M from $953M, dragged by the Sensia JV impairment (~$0.91/share net of noncontrolling interest), an asbestos accounting-method change (~$0.91 gross), and higher compensation. Adjusted EPS rose 7% to $10.53 and total segment operating margin expanded to 20.4% from 19.3%. The trailing multiple should be read against adjusted, not GAAP, earnings.

In Q2 FY2026, Software & Control segment margin reached 34.9% (from 30.1%) and Intelligent Devices margin reached 20.9% (from 17.7%); six-month organic growth was +9%. Incremental margins on the volume recovery are very high, which is the heart of the bull case: the FY2024 restructuring is converting a return of revenue into outsized operating leverage.

Two items warrant monitoring. First, the effective tax rate is rising: Q2 FY2026 came in at 20.2% versus 17.1%, reflecting BEPS Pillar Two (Singapore enacted), which management expects to add roughly 3 points to the rate starting FY2026. Second, capital returns are running ahead of cash generation. H1 FY2026 buybacks of $608M exceeded free cash flow of $445M, and commercial paper rose to $1,025M from $522M. The balance sheet supports this near-term, but it coincides with a step-up in capex (FY2026 guided to ~$270M from $186M) tied to the ~$2B five-year capacity plan, including a new greenfield campus in New Berlin, Wisconsin.

## Growth Analysis

Two distinct drivers underpin the recovery, and they have different durability profiles. The cyclical driver is the most important short-term lever: the 2023-2025 trough (revenue fell from $9,058M in FY2023 to $8,264M in FY2024, then $8,342M in FY2025) tracked a manufacturing PMI stuck below 50. The PMI crossover above 50 is the leading indicator for Rockwell's short-cycle business, and the Q2 organic acceleration to +9% across all three segments and all regions is consistent with that turn. The company-specific driver is margin: Software & Control reaching ~35% segment margin and the enterprise hitting 22.5% suggests the cost-productivity program is structural, not purely volume-driven.

The estimate-revision picture supports continued near-term momentum. Current-quarter consensus rose +9.1% over 30 days and +9.8% over 90 days; current-year consensus is $12.98 (up 7.2% over 90 days), and next-year sits at $14.52 (+6.0% over 90 days). The four-quarter beat streak lends credibility to forward estimates. The revisions are meaningfully positive but not at the +30% magnitude that would mark a depressed-trough name re-rating from a low base; ROK is recovering from a shallow trough at an already-premium multiple, which is the key distinction for valuation.

Secular tailwinds management emphasizes — industrial AI, reshoring, energy transition, labor-shortage-driven automation, and data-center buildout — are real and broad-based. The dissolution of the underperforming Sensia oilfield JV (closed April 1, 2026) removes a chronic drag and a source of large noncontrolling-interest losses ($120M attributable to NCI in FY2025), which should reduce earnings noise going forward.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is where the thesis breaks down for new capital. At $458, ROK trades at roughly 47x trailing GAAP EPS, about 43x trailing adjusted EPS, and approximately 35x FY2026 consensus and 31.5x FY2027 ($14.52). Against the peer group in Electrical Components & Equipment, the median P/E is 38.5 (Eaton 38.5x, Ametek 34.2x, Emerson 32.9x, Generac 80.4x). On a forward basis Rockwell is roughly in line with that peer median, but the absolute level is demanding for a business growing high-single-digit organic in the steady state, and the trailing multiple sits well above peers because GAAP earnings are temporarily depressed.

The growth-adjusted math is the constraint. With FY2027 consensus growth of roughly 12% over FY2026, a 31.5x forward multiple implies a PEG near 2.6 — below the sub-1.5 zone where rising estimates justify treating analyst targets as floors. The estimate-revision signal is positive but does not override this, precisely because the multiple already embeds continued beats: at this valuation, beats are expected (limited re-rating room) while a single PMI rollover or a guidance trim would be punished.

Analyst targets bracket a wide range: low $282, high $525, mean $464, median $480. The current price of $458 sits just below the mean and roughly 5% below the median, so the consensus already reflects the recovery. We would not treat $480 as a floor here: at a premium multiple, the target and the price share the same growth extrapolation rather than providing independent confirmation. We assign a below-average valuation score: the multiple is stretched relative to the steady-state growth rate, with no identifiable mispricing the market is missing.

## Competitive Landscape

Rockwell's moat rests on the Logix/Allen-Bradley installed base and deep North American distributor relationships, where it is the home-turf leader. Switching costs in installed control architecture are high, and the recurring-software and services mix is rising, which improves revenue quality. The Software & Control segment's growth and margin demonstrate the company is gaining where the economics are best.

Trajectory is stable-to-improving rather than dramatically widening. Rockwell competes against larger and more diversified peers (Siemens, ABB, Schneider, Emerson, Honeywell, Mitsubishi), and its cumulative TSR over the proxy measurement period lagged its peer group (172.6 vs 226.1), reflecting the prolonged revenue trough. The competitive position is solid and the secular automation/reshoring tailwinds favor an incumbent with U.S. manufacturing scale, but Rockwell is not visibly taking share from the broader European primes; it is defending a strong North American franchise while growing software. The cyber/OT security tailwind (heightened critical-infrastructure threat activity) is a modest demand vector for Rockwell's security offerings.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is cyclicality. Demand tracks manufacturing capex, PMI, and industrial production; the 2023-2025 downturn cut revenue roughly 9% from peak. The current upturn is genuine but PMI-dependent, and the stock is priced for the upcycle to persist. A PMI rollback below 50 would compress both volume and the multiple simultaneously, which is the principal downside path given the current valuation.

Distributor concentration is structural, and a destocking cycle or distress at a major distributor swings results materially. Tariff/trade policy is a recurring exposure that management says is currently EPS-neutral via pricing; the February 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling creates potential refund optionality (no asset recognized), a possible modest upside that would also trigger customer refunds. The cyber/OT product-security risk was specifically modified in the Q2 FY2026 10-Q to cite an April 2026 joint U.S. government advisory on active exploitation of internet-facing OT devices including PLCs by Iranian-affiliated actors — an unusually specific reputational/liability tail given Rockwell's critical-infrastructure footprint. Legacy asbestos liabilities ($441M accrual, $290M insurance recoveries, terminal year 2060) and the BEPS Pillar Two tax drag round out the named risks. Capital returns funded partly by rising commercial paper alongside the capex ramp warrant monitoring but are sustainable on an investment-grade balance sheet.

The score-trajectory history (6.3 in February to 5.9 in May, with Management Quality dropping from 7.0 to 5.5) reflects the capital-allocation and TSR-lag observations rather than a fundamental deterioration. The stock's price strength since then does not change the underlying read: the business improved while the entry point worsened.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and offers no contradicting signal to the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV is 34.6% versus 1-year historical vol of 29.0% — modestly rich (5.6 point spread) and in the 12th percentile of the current chain, so the market is not pricing an imminent binary event. Put/call skew is balanced (-1.3%) and the term structure is flat (34.6% near vs 35.0% far), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a near-term catalyst. The longer-dated 6-month tenor shows a more negative skew (-3.4%), consistent with some demand for downside protection further out, fitting the view that the cyclical/valuation risk is a multi-quarter concern rather than an immediate one. The high put/call volume ratio (4.40) against a moderate OI ratio (0.83) reads as tactical hedging, not informed directional positioning. P/C OI rose from 0.52 to 0.83 since early May, a mild increase in protective interest as the stock climbed.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The Q2 FY2026 results mark the start of a durable multi-year upcycle. PMI holds above 50, short-cycle product orders accelerate, and the FY2024 cost-productivity program proves structural, holding enterprise margin above 22% as volume returns. Software & Control continues mid-teens-plus growth at ~35% margins, lifting mix and recurring revenue. The Sensia clean-up removes earnings noise, the IEEPA tariff refund delivers an upside surprise, and EPS compounds toward $15-16 in FY2027 with continued beats. On 33-35x that earnings power, the stock reaches the high-$400s to ~$525, matching the Street high.

**Bear case.** Manufacturing PMI was a leading indicator on the way up and will be on the way down. A stall or rollback below 50 in late 2026 stops the organic acceleration, and the high incremental margins that drove operating leverage on the way up reverse on the way down. Trailing earnings normalize at a level that makes 47x look expensive, multiple compression toward the low end of peers (30x) on flat-to-down EPS takes the stock toward $360-380 (the prior trading range), and a >20% drawdown follows. Distributor destocking, a tariff-cost step-up that pricing cannot fully offset, or a guidance trim accelerates the move.

The base case sits between these: the recovery is real but already priced. EPS lands near $13-14 in FY2026-27 with continued modest beats, the multiple holds in the low-to-mid 30s, and the stock grinds roughly sideways to modestly higher over twelve months, delivering returns close to the dividend plus mid-single-digit appreciation. That is a hold, not a buy, because the asymmetry has been arbitraged away by the run from the low-$300s.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next fiscal Q3 2026 earnings (approximately late July / early August 2026), with PMI prints monthly as the key intra-quarter monitor.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the high-$300s (roughly $380-400) on unchanged or improving fundamentals, compressing forward P/E toward ~28x and restoring asymmetry.
- Confirmation across two consecutive quarters that enterprise operating margin holds above 22% as volume normalizes, evidencing structural rather than purely cyclical margin gains.
- A favorable IEEPA tariff refund recognition or a clear extension of organic growth into double digits with PMI sustained above 52.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- U.S. Manufacturing PMI falling back below 50 for two consecutive months, signaling the short-cycle turn is reversing.
- A cut to FY2026 revenue or EPS guidance, or organic growth decelerating below ~3% YoY.
- Evidence of distributor destocking (channel inventory build, order-to-shipment divergence) or distress at a top-two distributor.
- Continued buybacks materially above free cash flow funded by rising commercial paper without a corresponding FCF recovery.

## Conclusion

Rockwell is a high-quality industrial franchise executing well into a cyclical recovery, with margin expansion outpacing the volume rebound and a four-quarter beat streak that lends credibility to rising estimates. The fundamental trajectory is positive and the macro tailwind (PMI above 50, reshoring, automation, data-center demand) is broad-based. None of that is in dispute.

The valuation is. At ~47x trailing and ~35x forward earnings, the stock has re-rated entirely ahead of the recovery, leaving symmetric-at-best risk/reward against a PMI-dependent demand base, a $2B capex ramp, and capital returns running ahead of cash generation. The recommendation is HOLD on a growth basis: hold for the durable franchise and the upcycle, but do not chase near the top of the 52-week range. New capital should wait for a pullback toward the high-$300s or for confirmation that the margin step-up survives a full year of normalized volume.
