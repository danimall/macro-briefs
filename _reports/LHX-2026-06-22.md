**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

L3Harris is the sixth-largest U.S. defense prime, positioned as a mid-tier "trusted disruptor" between the legacy primes (Lockheed, RTX, Northrop, GD) and well-funded software/AI entrants (Anduril, Silvus). The investment case rests on three pillars: a high-margin tactical-communications and electronic-warfare franchise (CSD segment, ~25% operating margin), a structurally favorable demand environment (NATO 5%-of-GDP commitments, U.S. reconciliation funding for missile defense/munitions/shipbuilding, $40.7B backlog), and an active portfolio transformation that monetizes the 2023 Aerojet Rocketdyne acquisition at peak missile-defense demand. The most important near-term catalyst is the planned IPO of the Missile Solutions business ("Axyv," banks selected June 2026) alongside the carve-out of ~60% of space propulsion to AE Industrial Partners at ~$825M net enterprise value, with a highly unusual $1.0B U.S. Department of War direct-equity investment into Aerojet underwriting and validating the spin.

The stock has appreciated roughly +18% over the trailing twelve months but has fallen ~22% from its early-March 2026 high of $379, closing the latest session at $294.82 after a sharp intraday reversal (high $317.57, low $291.66 on June 18). The decline coincides with no meaningful improvement in forward estimates — consensus EPS revisions are essentially flat across all horizons (current year -0.6% over 90 days, next year +0.6%) — which means the de-rating reflects multiple compression in a higher-for-longer rate regime and rotation rather than fundamental deterioration. The business is sound, but the price still embeds ~22x next-year EPS, leaving little margin of safety relative to the modest organic growth the company is actually delivering.

We rate LHX a HOLD (growth_hold). The catalysts — Axyv IPO, space-propulsion close, and a remaining smaller-but-higher-margin "core" L3Harris that could re-rate — are genuine and create a credible path to upside. But the score trajectory has declined from 6.7 (February) to 5.95, driven by deteriorating management-quality and growth-prospect reads, and the valuation does not offer asymmetric upside until execution de-risks the sum-of-the-parts story. We would upgrade on a successful Axyv print or a pullback toward $270-285; we would not chase the stock at current levels.

## Company Overview

L3Harris operates platform-agnostically as prime contractor, merchant supplier, or subcontractor, with ~45,000 employees (18,000 engineers/scientists) and customers across 100+ countries. The U.S. Government accounts for 75% of revenue (directly, via primes, or through Foreign Military Sales); international end-use is 22%; no single non-government customer exceeds 5% of revenue.

The company reorganized from four to three segments in FY2026:

- **Space & Mission Systems (SMS)** — satellites/payloads, missile warning and defense, ISR, maritime systems, FAA mission networks, intelligence and cyber. Q1'26: $2,990M revenue, 10.5% operating margin.
- **Communication & Spectrum Dominance (CSD)** — tactical radios/waveforms, electronic warfare, night vision, targeting sensors. The margin engine. Q1'26: $1,855M revenue, **25.1% operating margin**.
- **Missile Solutions (MSL)** — the former Aerojet Rocketdyne: propulsion, munitions, fuzing, hypersonics, space propulsion. Q1'26: $990M revenue, 12.5% operating margin.

The durable competitive advantages sit in tactical communications (dominant share, high margin), night vision/image-intensifier tubes, and post-Aerojet solid rocket motors, where L3Harris is one of only two domestic suppliers alongside Northrop Grumman. Strategic partnerships with Palantir, Anduril, Amazon Kuiper, and Shield Capital signal an attempt to fuse hardware, software, and AI and to ally with disruptors rather than be displaced.

Note on fiscal calendar: L3Harris's "fiscal 2025" ended January 2, 2026; "Q1 2026" ended April 3, 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $17.1B (FY2022) → $19.4B (FY2023, +Aerojet) → $21.3B (FY2024) → $21.9B (FY2025, +3%). Q1'26 revenue of $5,744M was +12% YoY, with growth across all three segments (SMS +24%, MSL +18%, CSD +3%). Management disclosed that the Q1 acceleration was flattered by an extra week (13 vs. 12) and a classified-program material-procurement milestone, so the underlying organic run-rate is materially below the headline 12%. The reported acceleration is partly mechanical, which is the central reason the growth read should be tempered.

**Profitability:** FY2025 operating income was $2,110M (9.7% margin); net income $1,606M (7.3%); diluted EPS $8.53 (+8%). Q1'26 operating margin expanded to 11.3% and EPS reached $2.72 (+33% YoY), driven by the volume/timing items above plus the LHX NeXt cost program (now embedded as "e3"). ROE of 8.2% and ROA of 3.9% are modest, weighed down by the goodwill/intangible-heavy balance sheet from the L3/Harris merger and the Aerojet deal.

**Cash flow quality is strong.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $3,106M (+21% YoY); free cash flow $2,682M (12.3% FCF margin). Capex is light (~$424M FY2025, guided to ~$600M FY2026). Q1 operating cash flow was -$95M, which is seasonally normal for the business. At the current ~$53B market cap, the trailing FCF yield is roughly 5%.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt is ~$11.0B, virtually all fixed-rate; net debt ~$10.4B; D/E ~1.1x. The Altman Z-Score of 1.95 (grey zone) is misleading for a defense roll-up: it is driven by $20.0B of goodwill (~49% of assets) plus $6.3B of intangibles, not operational distress. The company carries a $2.2B undrawn revolver, comfortably services debt, and scores 5/5 on the Piotroski signals. The relevant balance-sheet story is steady deleveraging ($600M of notes repaid in FY2025; $1.8B current portion due over twelve months) alongside disciplined returns.

**Capital allocation.** L3Harris raised the dividend for the 25th consecutive year (to $1.25/quarter, ~$950M/year), repurchased $1.2B of stock (5.1M shares) in FY2025, and has $1.9B of authorization remaining. The current dividend yield is ~1.7% — well-covered by FCF, but a modest income contribution. Deleveraging is a stated priority.

**Earnings track record.** Four consecutive beats over the trailing four quarters (Q1'26 +5.9%, Q4'25 +2.4%, Q3'25 +3.8%, Q2'25 +10.3%). The beats are consistent but modest in magnitude: a defense prime delivering reliably against conservative guidance, not a company systematically outrunning estimates by double digits.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth picture is solid but not accelerating. Consensus expects ~$11.70 EPS for the current year and ~$13.65 next year (a ~17% step-up), but the estimate-revision trend is flat-to-slightly-negative: current-quarter +0.1% (30d) / -1.5% (90d), next-quarter -0.3% / -2.4%, current-year -0.0% / -0.6%, next-year -0.1% / +0.6%. There is no positive revision velocity here. This is not the >30% estimate-revision inflection that would warrant treating the stock as cheap on rapidly rising forward numbers; consensus is stable, so the valuation must be judged against the growth the company is already delivering rather than an accelerating trajectory.

The demand environment is genuinely favorable. NATO allies are committing to 5% of GDP on defense/security; the July 2025 U.S. reconciliation package ($155B defense) explicitly funds Golden Dome (missile defense), munitions, and shipbuilding — all L3Harris strongholds. The solid-rocket-motor supply base cannot keep pace with missile-defense demand (CSIS, June 2026), which advantages L3Harris/Aerojet as one of two domestic SRM suppliers. The FAA air-traffic-control modernization ($22B+ appropriated, +$1B YoY) is a tailwind for SMS Mission Networks. Backlog reached $40.7B at Q1'26 (up from $38.7B at FY2025 year-end, itself +13% YoY), with ~40% scheduled to convert within twelve months — strong forward visibility.

The growth caveats are specific: 75% fixed-price exposure means cost overruns are borne by L3Harris (Q1'26 already showed a $31M unfavorable EAC adjustment on a legacy naval sensor program); the GFY2027 budget request proposes a 23% NASA cut, a headwind for the space-propulsion businesses (which are being divested, making the timing of the AE Industrial sale astute); and the underlying organic run-rate is below the 12% Q1 headline once timing effects are stripped out. The growth thesis is real but mid-single-digit organic at the consolidated level, with the higher-margin CSD franchise growing more slowly (+3%) than the segments being restructured.

## Valuation Assessment

At $294.82, LHX trades at ~35x trailing EPS of $8.53 and ~22x consensus next-year EPS of ~$13.65 (and ~25x current-year ~$11.70). This is a full multiple for a business delivering mid-single-digit organic growth with flat estimate revisions. The stock is neither cheap enough to offer downside protection nor cheap relative to its own growth: a PEG against the ~17% headline EPS step-up looks reasonable at ~1.3x, but that step-up is partly cost-program-driven and the underlying organic growth is slower, so the quality-adjusted PEG is less attractive.

Peer comparison requires care given the heterogeneity of the aerospace/defense set. The provided peer median P/E of 73.9x is distorted by Axon (179x), Elbit (74x), and TransDigm (39x), which carry growth or commercial-aftermarket profiles unlike a defense prime. The cleaner comparison is Northrop Grumman at 17.3x — a closer-scale defense prime trading at a meaningful discount to LHX's 22x forward. On that basis, LHX is priced at a premium to its most comparable peer, which the bull case justifies via the sum-of-the-parts re-rating story rather than current earnings quality.

The sum-of-the-parts thesis is the legitimate valuation lever. A successful Axyv IPO monetizes the Aerojet missile franchise at a premium during peak missile-defense demand; the $825M AE Industrial space-propulsion carve-out (retaining ~40% plus RS-25) prunes a NASA-exposed, lower-multiple asset; and the $1.0B DoW direct-equity stake (preferred converting at 80% of IPO price, warrants at 110%) validates the carve-out valuation while funding capacity expansion at low cost to L3Harris. The residual "core" — SMS plus the ~25%-margin CSD — would be smaller, higher-margin, and could re-rate. This is sophisticated value-creation, but it is execution-dependent and the current price already partly anticipates it.

We treat the consensus mean target ($381.94) and median ($390) as aspirational rather than a floor: the estimate-revision evidence is flat, not rising, so the analyst-targets-are-floors logic (which applies to inflection stocks with rising estimates and reasonable multiples) does not hold here. The analyst low of $300 sits just above the current price. We assign a valuation score of 5.0: the multiple is full, the catalyst is real but unrealized, and cheapness is not present.

## Competitive Landscape

L3Harris's competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving. The CSD franchise (tactical comms, EW, night vision) holds dominant share with ~25% margins and is hard to replicate. Post-Aerojet, the company is one of two domestic SRM suppliers in a market where supply cannot meet missile-defense demand, a structural advantage that translates into pricing power and demand visibility.

The competitive threat is two-sided. Legacy primes (Lockheed, RTX, Northrop, GD, Boeing, BAE) carry more scale and R&D budget; well-funded disruptors (Anduril, Silvus, Ursa Major) move faster on software and AI and benefit from procurement reform (Other Transaction Authorities, the "Warfighting Acquisition System") that rewards speed. L3Harris's response — partnering with Palantir, Anduril, and Kuiper, and positioning as the "trusted disruptor" — is a credible hedge, but OTAs that favor speed also lower barriers for the same new entrants. The 5-year TSR of 178 (indexed) trailing the peer group's 238 shows that, over the medium term, L3Harris has underperformed its defense cohort despite a favorable end-market. We score competitive position 6.8: the moats in comms and SRM are genuine and the trajectory is stable, but the relative underperformance and the disruptor threat keep this short of a high-conviction read.

## Risk Assessment

**Government concentration and budget volatility.** 75% of revenue derives from the U.S. Government. FY2025 included the longest government shutdown on record (43 days, ending November 2025) and continuing-resolution funding. While the GFY2026 defense appropriation ($859B) and the reconciliation package are tailwinds, the procurement-reform pressure reshaping federal contracting is a two-sided risk: it favors defense/missile/space programs but can de-scope or renegotiate adjacent work.

**Fixed-price contract exposure (75% of revenue).** Cost overruns and inflation are borne by L3Harris. Q1'26 showed a $31M unfavorable EAC adjustment (legacy naval sensor, MSL); FY2025 included a $38M classified Maritime hit. Net EAC adjustments are currently favorable, but development-program risk is structural.

**Goodwill impairment risk.** $20B of goodwill; FY2025 testing showed reporting units clearing by >30% (>40% on the realigned Q1'26 units), and an $85M FY2025 impairment was tied to the Space Technology divestiture (cumulative historical impairments total $888M). A budget shock or contract loss could trigger material write-downs.

**Strategic-transaction execution risk.** The Axyv IPO, the AE Industrial space-propulsion close (H2 2026), and the associated carve-out costs are explicitly flagged as new risk factors. A new CFO (Kenneth Sharp, took the seat in early 2026) is executing the company's most complex strategic moves in years; fresh leadership during a transformation cuts both ways.

**SRM supply-chain bottleneck.** Industry-wide capacity is failing to keep pace with missile-defense demand — an opportunity on pricing/demand but an execution risk on capacity, microelectronics, and specialty metals previously sourced from Russia/China.

**Other:** environmental liabilities ($657M reserve, ~$482M recoverable); cybersecurity exposure as a government contractor; a 23% NASA cut in the GFY2027 budget request (headwind for the space-propulsion assets being divested anyway).

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 34.0% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 25.0% (a 9pp spread), with an elevated 5.0% near-term put skew and a put/call OI ratio that rose from 0.49 to 0.72 as the stock fell 7.4% over the past two-plus weeks. The term structure is flat (near 34% ≈ far 33.1%), and the 6-month tenor shows negative skew (-4.0%): the market is pricing uncertainty evenly across the curve rather than around a specific near-term binary, with the rising put/OI ratio reflecting downside hedging into the de-rating. Short interest is negligible at 1.5% of float (1.7 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short or squeeze dynamic. The options market is mildly cautious, consistent with a HOLD: elevated put demand into a falling price, but no acute stress signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($380-440):** The Axyv IPO prints at a premium during peak missile-defense demand, the $1B DoW stake validates the valuation, and the AE Industrial space-propulsion sale closes on schedule. The remaining "core" L3Harris — SMS plus the ~25%-margin CSD — re-rates as a smaller, higher-margin, higher-multiple entity. Backlog converts, organic growth accelerates on Golden Dome/munitions funding, and SRM scarcity drives pricing. The stock recovers toward and beyond its prior $379 high, with the consensus $390 median target validated.

**Bear Case ($232-270):** Carve-out execution stumbles (Axyv IPO delayed or prices below expectations in a soft IPO market), fixed-price EAC adjustments deteriorate as development programs run, a budget shock triggers goodwill impairment, and the higher-for-longer rate path continues to compress the ~22x forward multiple toward Northrop's ~17x. With flat estimate revisions and a full multiple, multiple compression alone could take the stock toward $245-260 (-12% to -17% from current), in line with NOC's ~17x on LHX's next-year EPS implying ~$232 if the premium fully closes.

**Base Case ($310-330):** The catalysts execute adequately but the value unlock is gradual rather than dramatic; organic growth runs mid-single-digit, margins hold, and the stock recovers modestly toward a ~24-25x multiple on next-year EPS as the Axyv path de-risks through H2 2026. This implies ~$320, roughly 9% above current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The growth thesis is positive and catalyst-driven, but the valuation does not offer asymmetric upside at current levels, and the score trajectory has declined to 5.95 on softening management-quality and growth-prospect reads. The stock has been rated BUY → BUY → HOLD → HOLD; the bar to re-upgrade is execution evidence, not narrative.

**Reassessment timeline:** The Axyv IPO process and the AE Industrial space-propulsion close are the natural reassessment points, alongside the next quarterly print (next earnings expected ~late July / early August 2026).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Axyv IPO prices at or above the implied DoW-stake valuation, validating the sum-of-the-parts thesis and de-risking the residual-core re-rating.
- A pullback toward $270-285 (analyst low $300; prior accumulation zone) that restores margin of safety on the same fundamentals.
- Positive estimate-revision velocity (next-year EPS revised up >3% over a quarter) confirming organic acceleration beyond the cost-program lever.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Axyv IPO delayed indefinitely or priced materially below expectations, signaling the value-unlock story is broken.
- A material goodwill impairment or a string of unfavorable EAC adjustments on fixed-price development programs.
- Estimate revisions turning sharply negative (next-year EPS cut >5%) or a backlog reversal.

**Base case target $320** (~24x next-year EPS, +9% from current). **Bull case $390-440** (sum-of-the-parts re-rate, +32% to +49%). **Bear case $232-260** (multiple compression toward closer-peer NOC, -12% to -21%).

## Conclusion

L3Harris is a financially sound defense prime in a structurally favorable end-market, with a high-margin tactical-comms franchise and a portfolio transformation that could unlock value through the Axyv IPO and the AE Industrial carve-out, both validated by an unusual $1B government direct-equity stake in Aerojet. The recent drawdown reflects multiple compression on flat estimates rather than fundamental deterioration, which improves the entry but does not yet create asymmetric upside: at ~22x forward EPS against mid-single-digit organic growth and flat revisions, the stock prices in much of the catalyst it has yet to deliver. The 5-year TSR lagging peers (178 vs. 238) and a CEO pay-actually-paid figure that tripled to $59.5M against that relative underperformance are governance items worth monitoring, and no open-market insider buying offsets them.

We rate LHX a HOLD with a growth_hold rationale. The catalysts are real and the path to $380+ exists, but it requires execution on the carve-outs and a re-rating of the residual core. We would become buyers on a successful Axyv print or on a pullback toward $270-285; at $294.82, the risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling.
