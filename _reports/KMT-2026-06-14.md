**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kennametal is an 85-year-old Pittsburgh-based maker of tungsten-carbide metal-cutting tools (Metal Cutting, ~62% of sales) and wear-resistant infrastructure components (Infrastructure, ~38%). After three consecutive years of revenue decline (FY22 $2.01B → FY25 $1.97B) and operating-margin compression (10.8% → 7.3%), the company posted a sharp FY26 turnaround: Q3 FY26 sales rose 22% to $592.6M, operating margin expanded to 13.4% from 9.1%, and diluted EPS reached $0.75 versus $0.41. The proximate driver is the China-led tungsten supply shock — China controls roughly 80% of global tungsten and has tightened export controls, pushing prices to record highs. KMT has raised prices and tariff surcharges ahead of the cost flow-through, generating roughly $39M of favorable price-versus-cost timing in the Infrastructure segment in Q3 alone.

The decisive analytical question is whether this earnings level persists. Eight analysts now model FY26 EPS at $3.91 (revised +60% over 90 days) and FY27 EPS at $3.90 (revised +52% over 90 days). The flat FY26-to-FY27 line is the key data point: it is the profile of a level the Street expects to hold for two years, not a windfall expected to collapse back to trend. At $34.98 the stock trades around 9x that forward number and at a 47% discount to the peer median P/E of 37. Barclays' margin-reversion thesis (Underweight, $33 target) is real, but after a roughly 20% decline from the $43.81 May high, much of that concern is already in the price.

This is a genuinely two-sided situation. The structural argument has primary-source support: KMT's internal tungsten recycling and Western vertical integration give it supply security and pricing power that China-dependent competitors lack, an advantage the company itself frames as multi-year. Against that, the same tungsten dynamic that lifted reported margins drained cash — nine-month operating cash flow fell to $69.7M from $129.7M as inventory ballooned $209M to $747M — and the company raised roughly $700M of new liquidity explicitly to fund tungsten-related working capital. Combined with net insider selling, a $330M lawsuit, and a five-year record of shareholder underperformance, the durability and cash-quality questions prevent a conviction call in either direction. We rate KMT HOLD.

## Company Overview

Kennametal (founded 1938) is a global industrial-technology company built on tungsten-carbide materials science. It operates two segments:

- **Metal Cutting (~62% of sales; FY25 $1.22B):** Milling, hole-making, turning, threading, and tooling systems under the Kennametal, WIDIA, WIDIA Hanita, and WIDIA GTD brands, serving General Engineering, Transportation, Aerospace & Defense, and Energy.
- **Infrastructure (~38%; FY25 $747M):** Engineered tungsten-carbide and ceramic components, earth-cutting tools, and metallurgical powders for Earthworks (mining/construction), Energy (oil & gas, frac), General Engineering, and Aerospace & Defense (including tungsten penetrators and armor).

The cutting-tools and wear-components industry is fragmented (hundreds of fabricators) and cyclical. KMT competes on application engineering, brand, quality, and global service rather than price. Roughly 60% of sales are non-U.S., with principal operations in Western Europe, China, and India. The company employs 8,124 people, about 1,900 unionized. The structural differentiator now in focus is internal tungsten recycling capability, which reduces reliance on third-party Chinese supply during a supply crisis.

## Financial Analysis

The financial story has two distinct chapters.

**Chapter 1 — multi-year cyclical decline (FY22→FY25).** Revenue eroded from $2.01B (FY22) to $1.97B (FY25, −4% in the final year). Operating margin fell from 10.8% to 7.3%. Net income declined from $144.6M to $93.1M, and diluted EPS from roughly $1.73 to $1.20. The decline was demand-driven — weak General Engineering, Transportation, and Earthworks — compounded by inflation, FX, and tariffs, partly offset by roughly $63M of cumulative restructuring savings.

**Chapter 2 — FY2026 turnaround on tungsten pricing.** For nine months, sales rose 12% to $1.62B, operating income rose 52% to $169.7M, and diluted EPS reached $1.49 versus $0.91. Infrastructure operating margin jumped to 18.1% in Q3 on the price-versus-cost timing benefit. Management raised FY26 revenue guidance to $2.33–2.35B.

**The cash-flow catch.** The same dynamic that lifted margins drained cash. Inventory rose $209M to $747M (current-cost basis up $263M) as tungsten prices climbed, and nine-month operating cash flow fell 46% against a rising-EPS backdrop. This is the central earnings-quality flag: earnings are real, but cash conversion deteriorated sharply, and in May–June 2026 the company raised the roughly $700M of additional liquidity (a three-year term loan plus revolver expansion) and refinanced $300M of notes (tendering the 4.625% notes due 2028) explicitly to fund near-term tungsten-related working capital. The company added leverage and interest cost to bridge a working-capital build tied to a commodity window of uncertain duration.

**Balance sheet.** Altman Z of 2.59 sits in the grey zone, typical of a capital-intensive industrial. Current ratio 2.46; debt-to-equity around 0.95; total debt approximately $614M at 3/31/26, under 1% variable-rate; investment-grade. Goodwill of $280M, all in Metal Cutting, is flagged for impairment sensitivity if demand falls. The November 2025 amended credit agreement extended maturity to November 2030, removing near-term refinancing-cliff risk.

**Capital allocation.** Dividend history dates to 1967; the current $0.20/quarter payout costs roughly $62M annually and is well covered. The $200M three-year buyback authorized in February 2024 has been executed slowly — only $70M to date, just $10M in 9M FY26 — as cash was redirected to inventory. The deliberate slowing of buybacks during the inventory build is defensible capital discipline given the working-capital draw.

## Growth Analysis

The reported growth is bifurcated by source. Reported sales growth of 22% in Q3 is roughly 19% organic, but the bulk of the margin and earnings improvement comes from pricing and surcharges running ahead of tungsten cost inflation rather than from unit-volume recovery. End-market demand is nonetheless genuinely improving in several areas: Aerospace & Defense (build-rate recovery plus defense tungsten demand), Energy (data-center power generation driving Americas demand), and Earthworks (mining/construction share gains). EMEA Transportation remains weak on lower vehicle production.

The forward signal is the strongest single input in this analysis. Consensus EPS estimates have been revised up sharply: current-year FY26 to $3.91 (+60.4% over 90 days, +143% for the current quarter), and next-year FY27 to $3.90 (+52.0% over 90 days). A revision of this magnitude (>30% over 90 days combined with revenue growth) typically signals the market systematically underestimating a fundamental change. The critical nuance is that FY27 ($3.90) is essentially flat to FY26 ($3.91): eight analysts are modeling a sustained two-year run-rate near $3.90, not a one-quarter spike followed by collapse. That directly undermines a thesis that earnings revert to a lower "normalized" level within twelve months.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in the forward estimates: three beats in the last four quarters (Q3 +13.5%, prior quarter +22.9%, the quarter before +43.2%), with one miss (−13.9%) four quarters ago. The recent trend is consistent positive surprise.

The caveat on durability remains material. The Street's flat FY27 line embeds an assumption that KMT holds the price-cost spread and that elevated tungsten prices persist. If China loosens export controls or tungsten prices normalize faster than KMT's pricing can adjust, both the margin and the inventory value compress. The revision data tells us the consensus expects persistence; it does not prove it. Metal Cutting margins are already being squeezed by higher tungsten input costs, which is the within-company evidence that the cost flow-through is real and ongoing.

## Valuation Assessment

At $34.98 the stock trades at roughly 9x consensus FY26 EPS of $3.91 and roughly 9x FY27 of $3.90. Against the peer median P/E of 37.0 (KAI 32.9, PRLB 73.5, SXI 37.0, EPAC 22.1; AIN negative), KMT trades at a 47%+ discount. The headline trailing P/E of ~19.6x is misleading at a cyclical inflection — it reflects the trailing $1.20 EPS that captures the depressed prior period, not the current earnings power.

The audit correctly flagged that the prior analysis anchored on a self-constructed "normalized" EPS of $2.50–3.00 that is not derivable from any source. No estimate, filing, or guidance figure supports that number. The only forward EPS evidence available — consensus $3.90–3.91 — points the opposite direction. I am dropping that construction. On the actual sourced forward figures, the stock is cheap, not expensive, even before any moat premium.

The valuation question is whether the earnings base the multiple is applied to is durable. Two readings coexist:

- **If the spread reverses toward FY25-style margins,** earnings power falls and the 9x multiple is applied to a number well below $3.90, in which case the discount to peers is partly justified by lower normalized profitability. But even a substantial haircut leaves the multiple unstretched.
- **If the spread and tungsten supply security persist multi-year** (the consensus reading and the brief's structural framing), then ~9x a sustained ~$3.90 is materially undervalued, and analyst targets — mean ~$37.64, median $36.00, high $47.50 — are floors rather than ceilings.

The 47% peer discount and ~9x multiple mean the bear case is substantially in the price after the pullback, and the stock sits only modestly above the Barclays $33 target. What is not in the price is the structural moat optionality, and what offsets it is the cash-quality and durability uncertainty. On balance this is a fair-value-to-modestly-cheap situation with genuine two-way risk, supporting a valuation score of 6.5 rather than the lower mark a stretched-multiple name would earn.

## Competitive Landscape

KMT is a leading producer in a fragmented industry, competing on engineering, brand, and service. The competitive position has improved over the past two quarters, partly structurally rather than purely cyclically. The China critical-mineral export shock is, per the brief, "structurally favorable to a Western tungsten-integrated player." KMT's internal recycling and vertical integration give it a secure tungsten source and pricing power that smaller, China-dependent fabricators lack. CEO Chowbey has positioned the company as a "secure source of tungsten in a tight supply." If China's weaponization of critical-mineral exports persists — consistent with the broader pattern of export-control escalation — this advantage is multi-year, not single-quarter.

The offset is EMEA Transportation weakness and the deeper concern that the underlying franchise has structurally lagged: revenue declined three straight years pre-FY26, and the proxy pay-versus-performance disclosure shows five-year indexed TSR of 92.13 versus a peer group at 267.12. The tungsten tailwind is improving trajectory now, but the longer record is of a serial decliner. The competitive-position score of 6.5 reflects a trajectory that is genuinely improving with a real structural angle, tempered by the franchise's weak long-term track record.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Tungsten price/spread duration (dominant, two-sided).** The price-versus-cost timing benefit is inherently temporary; higher-cost tungsten flows through COGS over time. Barclays cut KMT to Underweight ($33) specifically on margin mean-reversion. The same dynamic forces a cash-consuming inventory build, and Metal Cutting is being hurt by higher input costs. The consensus flat FY27 estimate bets the spread persists; that bet could be wrong.

2. **Cash conversion and fresh leverage.** Operating cash flow fell 46% YoY despite rising EPS. The liquidity raise adds interest expense to fund a working-capital build tied to a commodity window. If tungsten prices fall, inventory carries unrealized value risk and the leverage was taken on against a transient margin.

3. **Cyclicality.** Exposure to vehicle production, mining capex, oil & gas rig counts, and manufacturing PMI. FY22–25 demonstrated how fast margins erode in a downturn.

4. **Goodwill impairment.** $280M concentrated in Metal Cutting, sensitive to prolonged demand weakness.

5. **Litigation.** MachiningCloud filed suit in February 2025 seeking over $330M — material against a $2.7B market cap. KMT calls it baseless; outcome undeterminable.

6. **FX.** 60% international; meaningful translation swings.

7. **Modest positive.** The February 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating certain IEEPA tariffs may make KMT eligible for refunds.

The risk score of 5.0 reflects that the dominant risk (margin reversion) is substantially priced after the pullback, offset by the binary lawsuit and the leverage taken on against a potentially temporary margin.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 41.8% sits below one-year historical volatility of 46.2% (spread −4.4%) and in the 15th percentile of the current chain — options are cheap, suggesting no acute event premium and reasonable entry timing for hedged positioning. The term structure is in contango (41.8% near-term vs 50.4% at six months), a normal shape. The one notable signal is an elevated near-term put/call skew of 73.1% at the front week, indicating some demand for downside protection into the next few sessions, though this fades sharply at the one-month tenor (8.9% skew). The put/call volume ratio of 0.62 is not bearish. Net, the options market is not pricing a binary catalyst, and the cheap IV plus benign volume ratio argues against the AVOID framing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The tungsten supply shock is a durable, multi-year tailwind for a Western vertically-integrated, recycling-capable producer. Consensus FY26/FY27 EPS of ~$3.90 (flat year-over-year) confirms the Street expects a sustained run-rate, not a spike. At ~9x forward and a 47% peer discount, the stock is materially undervalued; cyclical recovery in Aero/Defense, Energy, and Earthworks adds organic volume on top of pricing. Restructuring delivering ~$35M annualized savings provides structural margin support independent of tungsten. As consensus catches up and the inventory build normalizes into cash, the stock re-rates toward the mid-$40s and beyond the $47.50 high target. Bull case: ~$48 (roughly 12x sustained ~$3.90, near the 52-week high).

**Bear Case.** The price-cost spread is a timing artifact; higher-cost tungsten flows through COGS and Infrastructure margins normalize toward high-single/low-double digits. The $209M inventory build carries unrealized loss risk if tungsten prices fall, and the debt raise was taken against a transient margin. Cash conversion stays weak, the lawsuit creates an overhang, and the franchise reverts to its three-year decline pattern. Barclays' $33 target is achieved, roughly 6% below the current price. Bear case: ~$30 (roughly 8x a haircut earnings base near $3.50–3.75, accounting for some reversion).

The reconciliation: the bear case is largely priced after the pullback (the stock sits just above the Barclays target), while the bull case requires both spread durability and cyclical follow-through that the consensus expects but cannot guarantee. Net insider selling into the rally — CEO, CFO, and directors disposing at $33–40 with no open-market buys — is a cautionary signal on management's own conviction at these levels, though it co-occurs with the +50-60% estimate revisions, which is the dominant forward signal and which mutes (without eliminating) the insider read.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is FY26 Q4 earnings (August 2026, fiscal year-end June 30), which will reveal whether the price-cost spread held a second quarter and whether operating cash flow recovered as inventory begins to convert.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q4 FY26 demonstrates the price-cost spread is sustained (Infrastructure margin holding above ~15%) AND operating cash flow recovers toward $40M+ in the quarter, confirming the inventory build is converting rather than permanently consuming capital.
- Tungsten export controls confirmed as structurally persistent, with management quantifying a multi-year supply-security pricing advantage, validating the flat FY27 consensus as a floor.
- A pullback toward the low-$30s on no fundamental deterioration, which would push the forward multiple toward 8x and improve asymmetry materially.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q4 FY26 shows Infrastructure margin compressing back toward high-single digits AND/OR a downward inflection in consensus FY27 estimates (the flat $3.90 line beginning to decline), signaling the spread is reversing as the bear case predicts.
- A goodwill impairment in Metal Cutting, or an adverse development in the MachiningCloud litigation.
- Operating cash flow remaining negative or near-zero for a second consecutive quarter while debt rises further, indicating the working-capital drag is structural rather than transient.

## Conclusion

Kennametal presents a genuinely two-sided setup that does not justify a one-directional call. The prior bearish framing rested on a margin-reversion thesis anchored to a self-constructed normalized EPS of $2.50–3.00 that no source supports, and the only available forward evidence — consensus FY26/FY27 EPS of ~$3.90 revised up 50-60% in 90 days and flat year-over-year — points the opposite direction. Eight analysts modeling ~$3.90 as a sustained two-year run-rate is the strongest forward signal in the data, and at ~9x forward with a 47% peer discount after the pullback, the margin-reversion risk is substantially priced. The structural moat — Western tungsten vertical integration and recycling during a multi-year China export-control shock — is real and supported by the primary filings.

What prevents a BUY is the combination of deteriorating cash conversion (operating cash flow down 46% while EPS rose), the $700M debt raise funding an inventory build tied to a commodity window of uncertain duration, net insider selling with no open-market buys, the $330M lawsuit, and a five-year record of shareholder underperformance (TSR 92 vs peer 267). These are legitimate, not boilerplate, concerns, and Industrials is a sector where the bar for conviction longs is justifiably high. The durability of the price-cost spread and the recovery of cash conversion are the decisive variables, and both resolve at the August Q4 print. Until then, the evidence supports HOLD: the bear case is largely in the price, but the cash-quality and durability questions keep this from being a conviction buy. We rate KMT HOLD with an uncertain rationale.
