## Executive Summary

Ameren is a pure-play regulated multi-utility with no merchant generation, operating in two jurisdictions that are moving in opposite directions. Missouri has become one of the most constructive regulatory environments in the sector: SB 4 (effective August 2025) extended the PISA mechanism through 2035 with an option to 2040, permitting deferral and WACC return on 85% of incremental rate base between cases, and authorized construction work in progress in rate base for new gas generation. Illinois has gone the other way. The Illinois Appellate Court upheld the ICC's multi-year rate plan orders in May 2026, locking an **8.72% allowed electric distribution ROE on a 50% equity layer** through 2027 for a segment producing 26% of consolidated revenue, against new long-term debt Ameren issued in H1 2026 at 4.80%–5.75%. The company withdrew its remaining reconciliation appeal in July 2026. That regulatory fight is over and Ameren lost it.

The near-term earnings arithmetic is the constraint on the rating. FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS was $5.35; FY2026 guidance, reaffirmed with Q2 results on July 30, is $5.25–$5.45, a midpoint of $5.35 — **zero GAAP growth**. H1 2026 EPS of $2.41 was up 15.9%, which means the implied second half is $2.94 against $3.27 last year, **down 10.1%** at the midpoint (down 7.0% at the high end, 13.1% at the low end). Roughly 32¢ of the 2025 result was a one-time revaluation of excess deferred income tax regulatory liabilities that has now rolled off, and the fall 2026 Callaway refueling outage lands in H2. Separately, 9¢ of the 33¢ H1 EPS increase — about 27% — came from equity-method marks on an $82M portfolio of non-utility "innovative energy technologies" investments, the lowest-quality line in the income statement and one that should not be capitalized at a regulated multiple.

At $108.84 the stock trades at 20.2x current-year consensus of $5.38 and 18.6x next-year consensus of $5.84, against a multi-utility peer median of 23.1x. That discount is genuine but explicable: 17.4M shares of committed forward equity represent a **6.3% increase in share count (5.9% ownership dilution)**, H1 free cash flow after dividends was **–$1,899M**, parent-level commercial paper rose from $155M to $908M in six months, and the Missouri rate case requesting +$343M of annual revenue at a 10.25% ROE will not be decided until May 2027 in a jurisdiction where the Consumers Council states bills have risen more than 30% since the start of the decade. Base case fair value is $118 (20.2x $5.84), roughly 8% above spot; adding the 2.76% dividend gives a total return near 11% over twelve months against a bear case of $92 (–15.5%). That is approximately fair, not asymmetric. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, supported by the Q2 file, which does not change the trajectory in either direction.

## Company Overview

Ameren is a St. Louis holding company whose assets are equity interests in separate legal subsidiaries; parent dividends depend on upstream distributions. Four reporting segments, with allowed returns effective January 1, 2026:

| Segment | Regulator | Allowed ROE | Rate base | % of 2025 revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ameren Missouri (integrated electric + gas) | MoPSC | per April 2025 order | $16.7B (rate-case filing) | 52% electric + 2% gas |
| Ameren Illinois Electric Distribution | ICC | **8.72%** (50% equity) | $4.6B (2026 avg) | 26% |
| Ameren Illinois Natural Gas | ICC | 9.60% (50% equity) | $3.2B | 11% |
| Ameren Illinois Transmission | FERC | 10.48% (54.98% equity) | $4.6B | 6% |
| ATXI | FERC | 10.48% (60.02% equity) | $1.8B | 2% |

Total rate base reached **$29.8B at 12/31/2025** from $25.8B in 2023, a 7.5% two-year CAGR. Coal is only 5% of consolidated rate base (11% of Ameren Missouri's), though Ameren Missouri still generated 56.5% of 2025 energy supply from coal, burning ~12.0 million tons with 96% sourced from the Powder River Basin. Rush Island retired in October 2024 and was securitized ($438M non-recourse debt in a consolidated VIE against a $432M regulatory asset).

The reported revenue line is close to non-informational. FY2025 revenue rose 15.4% to $8,799M primarily because Ameren Missouri off-system sales and capacity revenue went from $485M to $912M on MISO summer capacity pricing moving from $30 to $666.50/MW-day, offset by roughly $459M of higher energy costs; under the Missouri fuel adjustment clause 95% of net variance passes through, leaving approximately $7M of shareholder exposure. Q2 2026 ran the mechanism in reverse: revenue fell 5.8% to $2,092M as spring capacity pricing dropped from $720 to $70/MW-day, while **operating income rose 11.7% to $459M**. Rate base, allowed ROEs and net income are the only signal here.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings quality.** FY2025 net income attributable to common was $1,456M, diluted EPS $5.35 versus $4.42. The MD&A attribution shows +42¢ from the April 2025 Missouri rate order, **+32¢ from a one-time $86M tax benefit** on revaluation of excess deferred income tax regulatory liabilities, +22¢ weather, +17¢ from absence of the 2024 Rush Island charge, +17¢ transmission/Illinois equity return, +14¢ AFUDC/PISA, against –24¢ O&M, –18¢ share count and –8¢ financing. Of the 93¢ increase, roughly 54¢ was non-repeatable. The consolidated effective tax rate was 9% in 2025 and 7% in 2024; H1 2026 amortization of excess deferred taxes fell to $42M from $47M, a shrinking tailwind. The proxy's pay-versus-performance table reports FY2025 Adjusted EPS of **$5.03 against GAAP $5.35** — adjusted below GAAP, the inverse of 2024's relationship. The plausible reading is that the compensation committee excluded the tax windfall from the incentive metric, which would be a mark of rigor and would make the $5.35 guidance midpoint +6.4% off a $5.03 base rather than flat. That linkage is an inference from two separately disclosed figures, not a verified reconciliation, and the two bases should not be mixed in a multiple.

**Non-cash composition is rising.** AFUDC equity rose to $69M in H1 2026 from $39M, roughly 10.3% of net income to common. The non-service pension/OPEB credit fell to $98M from $128M, with net periodic pension cost swinging $34M adversely (–$10M income to +$24M expense); Ameren Missouri has a pension tracker, Ameren Illinois does not fully. So an increasing share of reported earnings is non-cash and contingent on future rate recovery while a historical non-cash tailwind reverses.

**Balance sheet.** At 6/30/2026: total assets $51,216M, net PP&E $41,372M, total equity $13,816M. Total debt of $21,808M ($19,064M long-term + $1,524M current maturities + $1,220M short-term) gives **debt/total capitalization of 61.2%** and D/E of 1.58x, matching the credit-agreement ratio of 61% at the parent versus 50% at Ameren Missouri and 46% at Ameren Illinois. Incremental leverage is warehoused at the holding company. Cash is $12M against $1,929M of net available liquidity under $3.2B of facilities running to December 2030. Current assets of $2,644M against current liabilities of $4,978M give a current ratio of 0.53 — structurally normal for a commercial-paper-funded regulated utility, and the reason screen-based distress scores are non-diagnostic here. The metrics that do bear on financial risk are covenant headroom under the 61% ratio, the trajectory of parent short-term borrowing, and interest expense, which rose **14.1% to $413M in H1 2026** from $362M. Average daily consolidated CP outstanding was $1,154M at 3.89% with a peak of $1,996M.

**Funding gap.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $1,191M (down $102M), capex $2,653M (up $523M, including a ~$0.6B Split Rail Solar acquisition), nuclear fuel $23M, dividends $414M — **free cash flow after dividends of –$1,899M**. This is a period result, not an annualized run rate: OCF was depressed by $223M of lower tax-credit transfers, $56M of Illinois DG timing and $50M higher interest paid, partly offset by $207M higher customer collections and $77M of collateral received. FY2025 FCF was –$775M, and the 10-K states that cash needs for capex and dividends will exceed operating cash flow "over the next several years." Parent commercial paper at **$908M versus $155M six months earlier** indicates where the gap is landing.

**Capital plan and dilution.** The 2026–2030 plan is up to $33.1B (Missouri $22.2B, Illinois $8.3B, ATXI $2.6B) — roughly $6.6B annually against $4.1B of actual 2025 capex, a ~61% step-up — supported by a ~$4B equity plan. Forward sale agreements at 6/30/2026 cover 17.4M shares: 9.6M at $106.32–$112.91 (settleable by Jan–Mar 2028), 6.4M at $91.89 (by Jan 2027), and 1.4M added in July 2026 at ~$112.62. Physical settlement yields roughly $1.8B, funding about 45% of the equity plan and leaving ~$2.2B to raise. On 276.8M shares outstanding, 17.4M represents a **6.29% share-count increase, equivalent to 5.9% ownership dilution post-issuance**; only ~1.0–1.1M shares are currently reflected in diluted EPS under the treasury stock method, so reported diluted shares of 278.7M materially understate the committed count. Rate base compounding at 7.5% against roughly 1.3% annual dilution leaves per-share rate base growth near 6%.

**Earnings surprise history and estimate revisions.** The supplied surprise table shows Q2 2026 GAAP EPS of $1.13 against a $1.19 estimate (–5.3%), while the research file records a Zacks consensus of $1.08 that $1.13 exceeded. Both cannot be right on a single basis; the $1.19 figure is most likely an adjusted-basis estimate compared against a GAAP actual. The informative fact is that guidance was **reaffirmed rather than raised** after a quarter in which H1 EPS grew 15.9%. Estimate dispersion is wide on thin coverage (Q1 2026 printed a 64% "beat" on a $0.78 estimate from 5 analysts), so consensus quarterly figures deserve limited weight. Full-year revisions are essentially flat: current year $5.38 (+0.3% 30d, +0.2% 90d), next year $5.84 (–0.2% 30d, –0.1% 90d). The current-quarter estimate of $2.34 has been cut 2.6% in 30 days and 4.0% in 90 days, consistent with the implied H2 deceleration.

## Growth Analysis

The multi-year case rests on three quantifiable legs.

**Large-load contracting.** Executed electric service agreements reached **2.8 GW** at Q2 2026 from 2.2 GW in the February 10-K, plus **3.4 GW of construction agreements** signed with developers (inclusive of the ESAs). Demand is expected to begin materializing in **2H 2027** and reach full capacity by end-2029, with no material 2026 P&L effect. Large-load customers posted $43M of net collateral in H1. Two disclosure limits matter: the 10-Q states remaining performance obligations for contracts longer than one year were **"immaterial"** — the ESAs are not RPO and should not be described as backlog — and counterparty identities are confidential in the rate proceeding. Contractual protections (~12-year minimum term, ~80% minimum demand charge, exit fees, credit support) are real but are conditional obligations of undisclosed counterparties. The 10-K adds a dedicated risk factor stating that exit fees and minimum demand charges "may not fully mitigate" forecast risk.

**Transmission.** Ameren Transmission produced $862M of 2025 revenue (9.8% of the total) but **$194M of H1 2026 net income, roughly 29% of the $671M consolidated segment total**, at a FERC formula 10.48% ROE with annual true-ups and the least regulatory lag of any Ameren jurisdiction. MISO Long Range Transmission Plan allocations total ~$1.8B (Tranche 1, construction from 2024) and ~$1.3B (Tranche 2.1, from mid-2028), with ATXI winning ~$700M of competitive projects in May 2026 and ~$2.7B of competitive work still unawarded through 2026. A June 2026 FERC filing seeks CWIP-in-rate-base and abandoned-plant incentives with a decision expected August 2026. This is the highest-quality incremental growth available to the company.

**Missouri generation.** In-service dates run from Bowling Green Solar (50 MW, March 2026) and Split Rail Solar (300 MW, June 2026) through Castle Bluff gas (800 MW, Q4 2027), Big Hollow gas (800 MW, Q3 2028) and BESS (400 MW, Q2 2028), Reform Solar (250 MW, Q4 2028) and **West Alton combined-cycle gas (2,100 MW, CCN filed July 2026, in service Q4 2031)**. Five named projects alone total ~$3.6B. CWIP inclusion under SB 4 shortens the cash-recovery lag on this build materially relative to conventional treatment.

Against that: organic volume growth excluding data centers is unremarkable. Ameren Illinois industrial volumes fell **4.2% to 10,485 GWh in 2025**; Ameren Missouri industrial was flat (4,087 vs 4,096 GWh); Ameren Missouri retail load grew 3.2% but weather contributed 22¢ of EPS. Consensus next-year EPS of $5.84 implies +8.6% growth off $5.38 — respectable but reliant on the Missouri rate order effective June 2027 landing near request and on the load ramp beginning on schedule.

## Competitive Landscape

Ameren's structural position is favorable but the "utilities win from AI" framing oversimplifies it. Item 1 explicitly flags "increased competition among utilities, independent power producers and non-traditional market entrants" for data-center supply — hyperscalers can site elsewhere, contract behind the meter, or transact directly with IPPs. Recent evidence elsewhere in the sector, including developers bidding directly for existing generation assets, suggests regulated utilities may capture less of the load-growth rent than the contracted-tariff thesis assumes.

Where Ameren is genuinely differentiated: Ameren Missouri is vertically integrated, so load growth drives generation rate base with CWIP recovery, which is where the largest returns sit. Illinois is a wires-and-pipes business only — Ameren Illinois procured power for just 28% of its 2025 kWh sales with all procurement and transmission costs passing through, and operates only three pilot solar facilities under CEJA. Revenue decoupling insulates Illinois electric distribution earnings from volume, and smart meters are fully deployed across nearly all electric and gas customers.

Federal environmental deregulation is a quiet positive specific to Ameren's asset mix. The MATS 2024 rule was repealed and the 2009 GHG endangerment finding rescinded (both February 2026, the latter in litigation), with repeal of the 2024 CO2 rule proposed and final action expected end-2026. Environmental compliance capex is guided at just **$70–100M for 2026–2030** against a $33.1B plan, extending the economic life of Labadie ($1.1B rate base) and Sioux ($0.5B) as load arrives. The offset the 10-K names is uncertainty about the value of existing renewables and, via OBBBA, tax-credit eligibility timelines for the planned solar, wind and battery build.

The September 2026 Preferred Resource Plan update is a near-term catalyst, because 2.8 GW of executed ESAs already exceeds the 1.5 GW-by-2032 load assumption in the current plan.

## Risk Assessment

**Illinois structural under-earning.** 8.72% allowed ROE on a 50% equity layer through 2027 with the appellate route closed. Under the CRGA the energy-efficiency ROE converts from a 30-year-Treasury+580bp formula (10.65% in 2026) to the ICC-approved distribution ROE beginning 2027, on a rate base growing as annual EE spend rises from $126M (2026) to $256M (2029). The November 2025 gas order disallowed $75M of planned capital from the future test year (appealed January 2026, no decision deadline), and the Illinois AG is challenging $30M of 2020 QIP investments with 2020–2023 reconciliations open against $529M of 2021–2023 QIP spend.

**PISA cap.** SB 4 extended PISA but cut the annual revenue-requirement increase cap from 2.5% to 2.25%. The 10-K states that increased capex could cause incremental deferrals to exceed the cap and be excluded from recovery. With up to $22.2B of Missouri capex planned, this is the binding constraint on how fast investment converts to cash earnings. The Illinois analogue is the 105% MYRP reconciliation cap.

**Missouri affordability and cost allocation.** The June 26, 2026 filing requests +$343M at a 10.25% ROE on 52% equity against a $16.7B rate base, decision by May 2027. Secondary reporting puts the residential impact near $13/month (~10%) following a $14/month increase from the 2025 case, with the Consumers Council of Missouri intervening and citing bill increases above 30% since 2020, and explicitly anticipating a fight over residential versus large-customer allocation. Ameren has requested an income-eligible discount rate — a mitigation and an acknowledgment that affordability is a live constraint.

**Operational concentration.** 96% of Ameren Missouri coal from the Powder River Basin with documented rail and supplier disruptions; a single NRC-licensed supplier for Callaway fuel; Callaway is one 1984-vintage unit on an 18-month refueling cycle with the next outage in fall 2026 (nuclear fell to 19.4% of Missouri supply in 2025 from 29.1% on outage timing). Three turbines collapsed at High Prairie in late 2024, and the site's USFWS permit expires May 2027 with a 30-year renewal filed March 2026 and approval uncertain. **Collective bargaining contracts covering 96% of represented Ameren Missouri employees expire in 2026.**

**Regulatory tails.** If FERC finalizes its 2021 supplemental NOPR, Ameren Illinois and ATXI lose the 50bp RTO adder — quantified at ~$19M of annual net income, roughly 6.8¢/share. The ICC and IPA must publish a study by December 2026 on whether Illinois should form its own RTO, consolidate, or maintain MISO membership. An ICC "future of gas" proceeding bears on the $3.2B Illinois gas rate base.

**Rate environment.** [unverified] Macro-rate conditions (Fed policy stance, the level of the 30-year Treasury) are not addressed in the research brief or Phase 1 data. A financing-heavy negative-FCF utility with ~$2.2B of equity still to raise remains sensitive to cost of capital and multiple, but the specific rate backdrop asserted here cannot be sourced.

## Management Quality

CEO Martin Lyons received **$14,056,510 in FY2025 total compensation, up 44.4%** from $9,731,030, with compensation actually paid of $17,245,319 and a CEO-to-NEO ratio of 4.0x. Against that, five-year cumulative TSR indexed from 2020 stands at **148.2 versus a self-selected peer group at 159.1**, having trailed in each of 2023, 2024 and 2025. That combination is uncomfortable regardless of the underlying operating record.

Insider activity through August 3, 2026 shows **no open-market purchases** in the 25-transaction window. Lyons had 28,154 shares withheld for taxes at $113.28 in February and then sold 26,818 shares outright at $111.84 on March 3 (~$3.0M), retaining 282,681. Michael Moehn, now Group President, Utilities, sold identical 6,500-share blocks on May 1 at $113.63 and August 3 at $108.96, retaining 199,689 — a cadence consistent with a pre-established plan. The pattern is routine liquidity and diversification rather than a directional signal, but the complete absence of buying leaves no offsetting conviction evidence, which matters more at a 20x multiple with flat estimates than it would at a trough.

Senior leadership has turned over substantially: four Item 5.02 8-Ks between October 3, 2025 and February 9, 2026, with Leonard Singh now signing the 10-Q as EVP & CFO, Moehn moved from CFO to Group President, and Aaron Melda newly granted a bonus agreement and RSUs dated June 1, 2026. The contents of those 8-Ks could not be retrieved, so the specific departures, reasons and any severance terms are unverified — a genuine gap on a dimension that matters.

On the positive side: the dividend was raised 5.6% to $3.00 annualized with a 56% payout inside the stated 50–60% band; the 6.4M-share forward tranche struck at $91.89 was well-timed against a current $108.84; and if the proxy's $5.03 adjusted EPS does exclude the 32¢ tax windfall, the committee is holding management to the harder number. Net score 5.0 — capital deployment into a well-supported regulated rate base, undercut by pay-performance asymmetry, no insider buying, and unverified executive churn.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($132, +21%).** The Missouri order lands near the $343M/10.25% request effective June 2027; CWIP inclusion under SB 4 compresses the cash-recovery lag on ~$3.6B of named generation projects plus West Alton; 2.8 GW of executed ESAs converts to load from 2H 2027 with the September 2026 IRP raising the load assumption above 1.5 GW-by-2032; ATXI wins a share of the ~$2.7B of unawarded MISO competitive work and FERC grants CWIP and abandoned-plant incentives in August 2026, growing the 10.48% formula-rate segment that already produces 29% of segment net income on 9.8% of revenue; environmental compliance capex stays at $70–100M for five years, extending Labadie and Sioux economics; and the forward multiple closes most of the gap to the 23.1x peer median.

**Bear case ($92, –15.5%).** The Missouri order comes in materially below request with residential allocation shifted after intervenor pressure; the PISA 2.25% cap begins excluding incremental deferrals as capex runs at $6.6B annually; Illinois continues earning 8.72% with the 2027 energy-efficiency ROE step-down and open QIP reconciliations producing disallowances; large-load demand slips beyond 2H 2027 or arrives at lower volumes, with no RPO recognition and undisclosed counterparties leaving investors unable to assess concentration; the remaining ~$2.2B of equity is issued below current forward prices; parent commercial paper keeps building and interest expense compounds off the H1 pace of +14.1%; and a steeper long end compresses the multiple toward 15–16x.

**Base case ($118, +8.4%, +11.2% with dividend).** Rate base compounds ~7.5% against ~1.3% annual dilution; FY2026 lands within $5.25–$5.45 with H2 down as guided; FY2027 reaches consensus $5.84 on the June 2027 Missouri rates and continued transmission investment; the forward multiple holds near 20x, still at a discount to peers on the Illinois ROE and funding profile.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** The reason to own is a visible multi-year rate-base compounding path with dated catalysts, not the next two quarters of EPS.

- **Base case target: $118** — 20.2x FY2027 consensus of $5.84, holding the current forward multiple. +8.4% from $108.84; +11.2% including the $3.00 dividend.
- **Bull case: $132** — 22.6x $5.84, partial close of the discount to the 23.1x peer median.
- **Bear case: $92** — 15.7x $5.84, or ~17.5x on a $5.25 outcome. –15.5% from spot.
- **Timeframe:** 12–18 months, anchored to the Missouri order (May 2027, rates effective June 2027) and the start of large-load demand in 2H 2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. A Missouri MoPSC order awarding **≥$280M of the $343M request at an ROE ≥9.75%** with large-load customers bearing a disclosed majority of allocated incremental cost — this converts the capex plan into cash earnings at the assumed return.
2. Price in the **$95–100 range** on unchanged fundamentals, which at $5.84 forward would be 16.3–17.1x and roughly a 25% discount to the peer median, restoring the margin of safety.
3. Evidence the funding gap is narrowing: two consecutive quarters of parent commercial paper below $500M with H2 2026 operating cash flow up year over year, plus the remaining ~$2.2B of equity forward-committed at or above $110.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. FY2026 guidance cut below the $5.25 low end, or FY2027 consensus revised down more than 3% — the H2 implied decline of 10.1% leaves limited cushion.
2. A Missouri order below **$200M** or an explicit finding shifting incremental cost toward residential customers, which would signal the affordability constraint is now binding on the recovery of a $22.2B Missouri plan.
3. Equity issued below $95, or evidence that PISA deferrals are being excluded at the 2.25% cap.
4. Loss or material delay of executed ESAs, or disclosure that large-load counterparty concentration exceeds ~50% in a single customer.

**Reassessment timeline:** three near-term checkpoints — the FERC decision on ATXI CWIP and abandoned-plant incentives expected August 2026, the September 2026 Preferred Resource Plan update (the load assumption is the tell), and the Q3 print in early November, which contains the fall Callaway refueling outage and the first clean test of the implied H2 deceleration. The Missouri collective bargaining contracts covering 96% of represented employees also expire during 2026.

## Conclusion

Ameren has the regulatory framework, the load pipeline and the transmission engine to compound rate base at 7.5% for five years, and the federal environmental posture has removed a compliance-capex overhang specific to its coal fleet. What it does not have at $108.84 is an entry price that pays for the execution risk.
