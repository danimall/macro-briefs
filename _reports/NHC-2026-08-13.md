## Executive Summary

National HealthCare Corporation completed the most consequential transaction in its recent history on July 1, 2026, purchasing the land and improvements of 32 skilled nursing facilities and three independent living facilities from National Health Investors (NHI) for $560 million, funded with a new $550 million unsecured credit facility of which $530 million was drawn. The NHI master lease was terminated at closing. Two things happened at once: NHC eliminated roughly $40.2 million of annual rent (1H26 NHI rent expense of $20.091M × 2) and it removed a landlord that had formally alleged four non-monetary lease defaults in September 2025 and against whom NHC faced a 2027 rent reset at "negotiated fair rental value." Converting a perpetual, resetting senior claim on cash flow into amortizing debt at Term SOFR + 1.50% against an owned asset transfers value to equity holders over time. On our arithmetic the deal is roughly GAAP-EPS neutral (+$40.2M rent saved, −$29.2M interest, −$15.9M incremental depreciation ≈ −$4.9M pre-tax, or −$0.23 per diluted share) but adds approximately $11.0 million of pre-tax cash flow annually.

The problem is what happened to the underlying business at the same time, and what the market is now paying for it. Same-facility net operating revenue growth decelerated from +8.4% in FY2025 to +2.9% in 1H26 and +3.0% in Q2 2026. Composite skilled-nursing per diem growth fell from +4.0% (FY2025) to +2.3% (1H26) to +1.5% (Q2 2026). Medicare fee-for-service patient days declined 2.6% year over year in Q1 2026 while managed care took share at roughly 80 cents on the dollar of per diem ($507.06 versus $630.44). Supplemental Medicaid revenue has fallen from $20.2M (2023) to $3.6M (1H26). And the margin engine that doubled operating income between 2023 and 2025 — agency nurse staffing expense down 66.2% in 2024 and a further 66.6% in 2025 — is exhausted, with salaries, wages and benefits at 59.3% of revenue in Q2 2026, near the low end of company history.

The reported valuation obscures this. The quoted 24.0x trailing P/E is computed against trailing-twelve-month GAAP EPS of approximately $9.24, which embeds a one-time $18.325 million recognition of pre-2025 management fees released from ASC 606 constraint on the day NHC bought the related party's facilities ($1.15 of gross diluted EPS) plus $10.0 million of unrealized equity-portfolio marks. On trailing non-GAAP EPS of roughly $7.05 the stock trades at 31.5x; on pro forma EBITDA of approximately $211.5 million and enterprise value of $3.80 billion (net of $171.0M of unrestricted equity securities) it trades at 18.0x EV/EBITDA. That is a full multiple for a skilled nursing operator running 3% organic growth, and it leaves no compensation for $530 million of unhedged floating-rate debt with undisclosed covenant thresholds, an unresolved hospice Civil Investigative Demand sitting on top of 96.5% of the company's goodwill, and Medicaid rate capacity constrained by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act's provider-tax restrictions. We move to REDUCE.

## Company Overview

NHC is a Murfreesboro, Tennessee senior-care operator founded in 1971. As of June 30, 2026 it operated or managed 80 skilled nursing facilities (10,323 licensed beds), 26 assisted living facilities (1,413 units), nine independent living facilities, three behavioral health hospitals, 34 homecare agencies and 33 hospice agencies across nine states concentrated in the Southeast. Ancillary lines include five regional pharmacies, a rehabilitation therapy business serving 48 external providers, two captive insurance companies, third-party management and accounting services, an owned real-estate portfolio leased to third parties, and a small Institutional Special Needs Plan (~1,300 members).

Two reportable segments plus "all other." In 1H26 inpatient services generated $668.4M of revenue and $65.5M of pre-tax income (9.8% margin) on +2.6% growth; homecare and hospice generated $80.5M and $15.7M (19.6% margin) on +8.1% growth. Homecare and hospice is 10.2% of revenue and carries $164.554M of the company's $170.478M of goodwill.

Roughly 15,278 employees are engaged principally through National Health Corporation ("National"), a separate affiliated entity that acts as NHC's Administrative Services Contractor and payroll provider. National is also the entity from which NHC purchased five SNFs (639 beds) for $50.5 million on June 1, 2026, and the counterparty whose management-fee receivable was released on that date.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Net patient revenues: $1,087.6M (2023) → $1,251.8M (2024, +15.1%) → $1,469.6M (2025, +17.4%). Total net operating revenues were $1,517.8M in FY2025. Most of the FY2025 increase was acquisitive: White Oak Management (closed 8/1/2024, $215.9M cash) contributed $227.5M of net patient revenue in FY2025 versus $96.1M in five months of 2024, an incremental $131.4M against a total net-patient-revenue increase of $217.8M — roughly 60% of the growth.

1H26 net patient revenues were $748.164M, +3.2%; net operating revenues $789.846M, +5.5%, with the difference flattered by the one-time management fee. Occupancy in owned and leased SNFs improved to 90.1% in Q2 2026 from 87.9% in 2023, but the incremental year-over-year gain has narrowed to roughly 70 basis points. Census recovery is largely complete.

**Margins.** Income from operations as a percentage of net operating revenues: 5.1% (2023) → 6.9% (2024) → 8.4% (2025) → 10.3% (1H26) → 12.0% (Q2 2026). The mechanism was labor: contract agency staffing collapsed (−66.2% in 2024, −66.6%/$9.3M in 2025), pulling SWB from 62.4% to 59.3% of revenue. Facility rent fell from 3.6% to 2.8% of revenue, mostly through the revenue denominator rather than lower dollar rent. Working the other way, FY2025 absorbed $17.6M of additional professional liability expense from unfavorable claims development in the captive insurer.

**The GAAP/non-GAAP gap is the single most important thing to get right on this name.**

| Period | GAAP diluted EPS | Company non-GAAP diluted EPS |
|---|---|---|
| FY2025 | $7.67 | $6.65 |
| Q1 2026 | $2.27 | $1.91 (vs $1.59, +20.1%) |
| Q2 2026 | $2.54 | $1.74 (vs $1.65, +5.5%) |
| 1H 2026 | $4.82 | $3.64 (vs $3.24, +12.3%) |

Q2 GAAP net income attributable to NHC rose 70% to $40.319M; non-GAAP net income rose 7.2% to $27.551M. The bridge is the $18.325M management-fee release plus a roughly $6.0M favorable swing in unrealized equity marks (Q2 2025 was −$5.061M). Press coverage of the quarter led with the GAAP $2.54. Anyone anchoring on the headline is reading a mid-single-digit operating quarter as a 70% growth quarter.

**Cash flow is the strongest part of the file.** FY2025 operating cash flow $185.1M against $36.4M of capex → $148.7M of free cash flow, 9.8% of net operating revenues, with OCF/net income of 1.54x. 1H26 OCF was $105.802M versus $102.074M, but the composition improved materially: working capital contributed only $9.474M versus $32.831M, so OCF before working capital rose from roughly $69.2M to $96.3M. 1H26 free cash flow was $83.635M; stripping the one-time fee (~$13.7M after tax) leaves roughly $70M of underlying half-year FCF. We do not annualize that — H2 carries the entire capital-structure change, the annualization of two acquisitions, and cash-tax timing.

**Balance sheet, before and after.** At June 30, 2026: total assets $1,536.9M, long-term debt zero (the $40.0M facility balance at 12/31/2025 was repaid during 1H26), cash $39.209M, unrestricted marketable equity securities $170.981M, total marketable securities $309.745M at fair value against $165.152M amortized cost, net PP&E $726.768M, total liabilities $408.874M, stockholders' equity $1,122.302M ($71.66 book value per share).

On July 1 that changed. The new $550M unsecured facility ($475M term loan + $75M revolver, five-year, Term SOFR + 1.25–1.75% on a leverage grid, initially +1.50%) was drawn $530M. Pro forma leverage, with inputs shown so it can be reproduced: FY2025 income from operations $128.4M + D&A $44.9M = $173.3M, plus ~$40.2M of eliminated NHI rent = ~$213M; cross-checked from 1H26 (operating income $81.188M less the $18.325M fee = $62.863M, + D&A $22.787M = $85.650M, ×2 = $171.3M, + $40.2M rent = $211.5M). Gross debt/EBITDA of $530M ÷ $211.5M = 2.51x; 2.32x net of cash; 1.51x net of cash and unrestricted equity securities. Interest coverage on pro forma EBIT of roughly $152M against ~$29M of interest is about 5.2x.

That is sound leverage for a healthcare operator. Two qualifications. First, the debt is unhedged — the 10-Q states plainly that NHC does not currently use derivative instruments to hedge interest rate exposure — so 100 basis points is $5.3M pre-tax, roughly $0.25 per diluted share after tax. Second, the credit agreement is described only as containing "customary… financial covenants" with no thresholds disclosed. Our estimated 2.3–2.5x net leverage implies headroom, but headroom against an unknown limit is not a verified fact. Any Altman-Z or Piotroski-style read computed off the FY2025 10-K predates the recapitalization entirely and should not be used as a current solvency signal.

**Capital allocation.** Dividends stepped from $0.61 to $0.67 quarterly across 2025–2026, roughly +4.7% annually; annualized $2.68 is a 1.21% yield and 34.6% of 1H26 non-GAAP earnings. The $23.561M / 136,951 shares appearing as "Repurchase of common shares" in the 1H26 cash flow statement is not a buyback — the 10-Q states the shares "were all from employee stock option exercises and were not from repurchases on the open market." Net of $19.459M of exercise proceeds, net cash out was about $4.1M, and share count still rose 0.80% from 15,536,427 to 15,661,395. The dividend is NHC's only discretionary return of capital. Separately, NHC put $6.984M into two non-controlled market-rate apartment joint ventures in Tennessee, one of which produced $935k of losses in 1H26 — a small diversification into an unrelated asset class.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth picture splits cleanly into reported growth, which will look good, and organic growth, which has slowed to roughly the rate of inflation.

Reported 2026–2027 revenue and margins will improve mechanically: ~$40.2M of rent disappears from the H2 2026 cost base, the June 2026 National acquisition (five SNFs, 639 beds) annualizes, and homecare/hospice compounds at 8%. A 79-unit assisted living facility in Tullahoma, Tennessee opens in Q2 2027.

Organic growth is the concern:

- **Rate.** Composite SNF per diem growth of +1.5% in Q2 2026 (Medicare +2.2%, managed care +2.9%, Medicaid +1.3%, private pay +3.1%) is below wage inflation. The FY2027 SNF Part A net update is +2.4% (3.3% market basket less 0.9% productivity, effective 10/1/2026), down from +3.2% in FY2026. Hospice FY2027 is +2.3% with a new service-and-spending-variation index. Home health FY2026 was a −1.3% cut and the FY2027 proposed rule pairs a 2.4% update with a temporary 3.0% PDGM budget-neutrality reduction. The 2% Medicare sequester runs through 2030.
- **Mix.** Medicare fee-for-service days fell 2.6% in Q1 2026 while private-pay days rose 5.5% and total days rose 0.9%. Payor mix within net patient revenues shifted from Medicare 31%/managed care 12% (Q2 2025) to 29%/14% (Q2 2026). Because managed care pays $507 versus $630, this substitution is a structural per-diem tax, and it is industry-wide rather than NHC-specific.
- **Medicaid.** Medicaid has been 29–30% of net patient revenues throughout. Supplemental Medicaid payments recognized in revenue fell from $20.2M (2023) to $12.7M (2024) to $7.2M (2025) to $3.605M (1H26). State rate actions partially offset (Tennessee +$3.0M/yr from 7/1/2025 and +$2.0M/yr from 7/1/2026; South Carolina +$4.2M/yr from 10/1/2025), but the OBBBA's restrictions on provider taxes and state-directed payments constrain future state rate capacity.
- **Occupancy.** At 90.1%, the runway is thin. Each additional 100 basis points is worth real dollars, but the pace of gain has halved.
- **Labor.** SWB at 59.3% of revenue is near the low end of NHC's history. Agency spend cannot fall by two-thirds a third time from a small base.

Our working forecast: FY2026 non-GAAP diluted EPS of roughly $7.30–7.50 (1H26's $3.64 annualized, with the H2 rent benefit roughly offset by new interest and depreciation), and FY2027 of roughly $7.60–8.10 on 3–4% organic revenue growth, modest operating leverage and acquisition annualization. Growth beyond that requires continued acquisition — which NHC has demonstrated it can execute, but which now consumes debt capacity rather than cash on the balance sheet.

## Valuation Assessment

Start by discarding the quoted multiple. Trailing-twelve-month GAAP EPS reconciles to roughly $9.24 (FY2025 $7.67 − 1H25 ~$3.25 + 1H26 $4.82), which is what produces the 24.0x headline. That figure contains $1.15 of one-time management fee and roughly $0.60 of net unrealized equity marks. On the equivalent trailing non-GAAP basis (~$7.05), NHC trades at **31.5x**. On our FY2027 estimate of $7.90, it trades at **28.1x**.

On an enterprise basis: $3,479M equity + $530M pro forma debt − $39.2M cash = $3,970M, less $171.0M of unrestricted equity securities = **$3,799M**. Against pro forma EBITDA of ~$211.5M that is **18.0x** (18.8x if the securities are not netted). Price/book is $222.13 ÷ $71.66 = **3.10x**. Dividend yield is **1.21%**.

Peer context is imperfect because the comparable set is heterogeneous — Acadia's P/E is negative, U.S. Physical Therapy's 456x is meaningless. The two functioning operating comparables are Option Care Health at 18.0x and Select Medical at 15.5x. Astrana at 43.6x is a different business model. NHC at 31.5x trailing non-GAAP sits at roughly a 75–100% premium to the two cleanest operator comparables. For skilled nursing specifically, 18x EV/EBITDA is above the range at which even premium owner-operators have historically cleared.

Three defensible justifications for a premium exist: NHC now owns the real estate under the large majority of its SNF portfolio (an owner-operator's earnings are not a residual after a senior rent claim, which supports a structurally higher multiple than a lessee's), it has top-decile CMS star ratings, and it has a five-year record of margin expansion and a proxy TSR index of 239.23 versus 148.38 for its selected peer group. What none of those justify is paying 28x forward earnings for 3% organic revenue growth and 1.5% pricing.

**Scenarios (from the $222.13 basis):**

- **Base — $185 (−16.7%).** 23x FY2027E non-GAAP EPS of $7.90 ≈ $182, with modest credit for the $10.92/share of unrestricted equity securities. Equivalent to roughly 16x pro forma EBITDA. This is what a high-quality, 3%-organic-growth owner-operator with moderate leverage and no analyst coverage should fetch.
- **Bull — $255 (+14.8%).** The multiple holds near 30x on FY2027 EPS of $8.50, achieved through one or two further accretive acquisitions plus the full-year rent benefit and a continued occupancy grind toward 91%.
- **Bear — $140 (−37.0%).** Multiple compresses to roughly 17x on $7.40 as Medicaid rate capacity tightens under OBBBA provider-tax restrictions, the Medicare-to-MA per-diem drag persists, professional liability development recurs, or the hospice CID produces a cash-plus-impairment event against $164.6M of segment goodwill.

Probability-weighted at 25/50/25, expected return is approximately −14%. Note also that no analyst price targets exist for this name; the reported "forward P/E" equals the trailing P/E, which is a data artifact confirming the absence of consensus estimates. There is no analyst floor to lean on here in either direction.

## Competitive Landscape

NHC's positional advantage is measurable and durable: 49 of 80 SNFs (61%) held 4- or 5-star CMS ratings at June 30, 2026 versus roughly 40% for the industry, with an average rating of 3.8 versus 3.0 (FY2025: 62.5% versus 38.6%, 3.83 versus 2.95). In a fragmented industry where managed-care contracting, hospital referral flow, and survey/enforcement exposure all correlate with star ratings, that gap is real. Whether it fully explains NHC's ability to hold ~90% occupancy while running SWB at 59% of revenue, or whether favorable Southeast market structure contributes as much, cannot be separated from the filings.

Trajectory is mixed but net positive on structure. The real-estate transaction changes what NHC is: from roughly 43 owned SNFs against 29 leased at 12/31/2025 to something above 75 owned properties after the National and NHI purchases (four Florida SNFs remain subject to a third-party operator's lease, which complicates the reconciliation; await the Q3 10-Q for the exact count). That is a genuine structural improvement, and it is worth noting that NHI's own CEO characterized the sale as "one of the most significant corporate actions in NHI's history," part of a deliberate pivot from triple-net skilled nursing into private-pay senior housing operations — SHOP grew from 5% to 25% of NHI's portfolio with a 40–50% three-year target. Both sides can be right: NHI de-levers into a growth vehicle, NHC owns assets it knows better than any third party and removes a landlord that had just alleged four defaults against it.

Working against trajectory: pricing power is below inflation, Medicare days are declining, and the payor-mix shift is toward a lower-paying payor. One clarification worth stating precisely — the decline in government programs from 64% of net patient revenues (2023) to 59% (Q2 2026) is not customer diversification. NHC discloses no customer concentration and states no individual customer or related group accounts for a significant portion of revenues. The mix shift establishes only that commercial managed care is displacing traditional Medicare, at a lower rate.

One tailwind has already been banked: the CMS minimum staffing rule finalized in April 2024 was blocked by the OBBBA until October 1, 2034 and partially rescinded by a December 2025 CMS interim final rule. That removed the sector's largest identified cost cliff. It is in the price rather than ahead of it.

## Risk Assessment

**Interest rate risk, unhedged and new.** $530M drawn on July 1 at Term SOFR + 1.50%, with no derivatives in place. 100 basis points is $5.3M pre-tax, ~$0.25 per diluted share. The Q2 10-Q correctly reported "no material changes to the risk factors" because the draw occurred one day after the balance sheet date; expect a materially modified leverage and rate risk factor in the Q3 filing. Covenant levels are not disclosed anywhere in the available filings.

**Hospice regulatory exposure where the goodwill sits.** Caris HealthCare received a Civil Investigative Demand from the U.S. Attorney for the Eastern District of Tennessee on May 21, 2024 concerning hospice billing from 2019 forward. The related qui tam action saw the United States, Tennessee, Virginia and Georgia decline to intervene on June 23, 2025, and the relators voluntarily dismissed on September 25, 2025. The filings do not state that the CID itself has been closed. Because $164.554M of goodwill (96.5% of total) sits in Homecare and Hospice, an adverse outcome is both a cash and an impairment event. Homecare/hospice is also the segment carrying the 19.6% margin and the 8.1% growth.

**Professional liability severity.** NHC is principally self-insured through two captives. Accrued risk reserves went $103.616M (12/31/2024) → $121.595M (12/31/2025) → $121.129M (6/30/2026), with the FY2025 balance increase corresponding to the $17.6M period expense for unfavorable claims activity. 1H26 was essentially flat. One bad development year is not a trend, and above-average star ratings argue for better-than-industry claims experience; the alternative reading — that Southeast nursing-home verdict severity is repricing structurally — cannot be excluded from two data points.

**Medicaid rate capacity.** 29–30% of net patient revenues, supplemental payments down 82% from 2023 levels, and OBBBA restrictions on provider taxes and state-directed payments limiting future state rate actions.

**Equity concentration in the counterparty.** NHC holds 1,630,642 NHI shares (cost $24.734M, fair value $124.353M at 6/30/2026), 65.3% of its $190.4M equity portfolio, with $99.6M of the $146.9M net unrealized gain in that single position. A 10% move is ~$19.0M pre-tax (~$0.90/diluted share after tax) straight through the income statement. Selling would crystallize a gain at roughly 5x cost.

**Governance and disclosure.** The related-party web is dense: National is simultaneously NHC's payroll provider and nominal employer of ~15,278 workers, the entity NHC managed five SNFs for, and the seller of those SNFs for $50.5M on June 1, 2026 — the date an $18.325M management-fee receivable that had been unrecognizable because collection was "constrained" became collectible and was paid. The ASC 606 treatment is defensible on its face: acquiring the facilities removed the collectibility uncertainty, which is exactly what the constraint contemplates, and the preliminary allocation ($52.198M of net assets against a $50.5M price, no goodwill) suggests NHC paid at or slightly below fair value. We draw no inference about motive. What remains is that an investor cannot see the related-party approval process or independent valuation support, and NHC does not hold earnings conference calls — for a $3.5B company that has just executed $610M of asset purchases and a $530M debt draw, the absence of a management Q&A forum is a real information disadvantage, and a notable contrast with NHI, which does host calls. An April 2025 auditor change (8-K Item 4.01) and three Item 5.02 filings between September 2025 and February 2026 are unexamined in the available record, as are two Item 8.01 8-Ks filed August 11, 2026.

**Insider activity.** Three genuine open-market sales in the March–June 2026 window totaling roughly $3.3M: director Emil Hassan (7,500 shares near $200, retaining 65,595), director Richard LaRoche (7,500 at $195.14, retaining 205,760), and SVP Operations Timothy Shelly (1,853 at $197.22, leaving only 1,294 shares directly held). One purchase: new director Lisa Piercey bought 949 shares at $184.26 on June 1. The CEO and CFO exercised deep-in-the-money options and held the net shares. The pattern is consistent with diversification by long-tenured insiders sitting on large embedded gains rather than a valuation signal; Shelly's near-complete exit of his direct position is the one item we would want explained. Option overhang is 717,622 options at a $117.91 weighted-average strike, of which only 191,793 are currently exercisable; maximum contingent issuance equals 4.58% of shares outstanding (4.38% post-issuance ownership dilution) and would bring $84.6M of proceeds. Reported Q2 diluted shares of 15,864,329 already reflect 246,674 shares of dilutive effect.

### Options Market Signal

The options market does not corroborate a bearish view, and we flag that honestly. Near-term ATM IV of 42.7% against 33.2% one-year realized volatility is rich (a 9.4-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (42.7% at one week versus 31.5% at three months), which normally indicates pricing of near-term event risk — though the August 6 earnings print has already passed and the two August 11 Item 8.01 filings are unexamined. Skew is call-heavy at −7.6% near-term, and put/call open interest is 0.03, meaning effectively no put protection is being held. ATM IV rose 1.7 points as the price fell 3.2% since the late-July snapshot. The important caveat: only 68 contracts exist across three expirations on a stock averaging 96,000 shares of daily volume on NYSE American. This is too thin to treat as informed positioning in either direction. Short interest of 5.9% of float with 7.0 days to cover is moderate and uncrowded — no squeeze dynamic, but also no confirmation that sophisticated capital shares the caution. Thin float and low liquidity are themselves a risk for anyone building or exiting a position.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (25% probability, $255, +14.8%).** The real-estate purchase permanently removes a senior claim on cash flow at a 7.18% cap rate on rent actually paid, financed unsecured at roughly 5.5%, and eliminates a 2027 fair-rental-value reset negotiated under the shadow of four alleged defaults. Reported 2027 EBITDA lands near $230M with margins stepping up visibly, and NHC — now an owner-operator with 61% of facilities at 4–5 stars, 90%+ occupancy and demonstrated acquisition capability — earns a multiple closer to 30x as the market recognizes the structural change. Homecare and hospice compound at 8% with 19.6% margins. Leverage at 2.3x net descends quickly on strong annual free cash flow, creating capacity for another White Oak-scale deal. The absence of analyst coverage means the re-rating is discovered slowly rather than pre-priced.

**Bear case (25% probability, $140, −37.0%).** Organic growth of 3% with 1.5% pricing cannot support 28x forward earnings once the one-time items and the rent step-up wash out of the comparisons in 2027. Medicaid rate capacity tightens under OBBBA provider-tax limits while supplemental payments continue their decline from $20.2M to near zero. Medicare Advantage substitution grinds the composite per diem toward 1%. Professional liability development recurs at 2025's $17.6M scale. The Caris CID resurfaces, producing both a settlement and an impairment test against $164.6M of segment goodwill. Rates move against $530M of unhedged floating debt at $0.25 per share per 100 basis points. The multiple compresses to 17x and the stock returns to its early-2026 range.

**Base case (50%, $185, −16.7%).** The business performs adequately: reported margins improve on rent elimination, organic revenue grows 3–4%, non-GAAP EPS reaches roughly $7.90 in FY2027, free cash flow runs healthy, and leverage falls. But the multiple normalizes from 31.5x trailing non-GAAP toward 23x as the market separates the one-time items from run-rate earnings and marks the growth rate to 3%. The stock underperforms while the business quietly gets better.

The asymmetry is the problem, not the business. Probability-weighted expected return is approximately −14%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim into strength now rather than around a catalyst. The next scheduled information event — the Q3 2026 10-Q, expected early November — is the first filing that will disclose the actual $560M purchase price allocation, depreciation lives, the revised leverage and interest-rate risk factors, the post-transaction owned/leased facility reconciliation, and, critically, whether same-facility growth stabilized near 3% or decelerated further with the rent-driven margin step-up masking it. Existing holders sitting on large gains should reduce to a residual position of no more than one-third of a full weight ahead of that filing. Prospective buyers should not initiate here.

**Base case target $185** (23x FY2027E non-GAAP EPS of $7.90 ≈ $182, plus modest credit for $10.92/share of unrestricted equity securities), −16.7% from $222.13. **Bull $255** (+14.8%). **Bear $140** (−37.0%). Horizon 12–18 months, with the decision points clustered around the Q3 2026 (November) and Q4/FY2026 (late February 2027) filings.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better.**
1. Same-facility net operating revenue growth reaccelerating above 5% for two consecutive quarters, or composite per diem growth returning above 3.0%, evidencing that the Medicare-to-MA drag has been offset by rate or acuity mix.
2. A price near $185–190 (below 24x our FY2027 estimate), which would restore roughly a market-neutral expected return at the base case.
3. Q3 disclosure of covenant thresholds showing leverage headroom of two turns or more, combined with an interest-rate hedge on at least half the $530M, which would materially reduce the rate sensitivity that currently costs $0.25/share per 100bp.
4. Initiation of a genuine open-market repurchase program, or evidence of a further accretive acquisition financed within existing capacity.

**Avoid triggers.**
1. Confirmation of an adverse resolution of the Caris hospice CID, or any goodwill impairment test disclosed against the $164.554M Homecare and Hospice balance.
2. Professional liability expense of $15M or more in any single year recurring after 2025, indicating structural rather than one-off tail repricing.

## Conclusion

**REDUCE.** This is a rating on price and asymmetry, not on business quality. NHC executed a genuinely value-creating transaction on July 1: it bought the real estate under 32 skilled nursing facilities at a 7.18% cap rate on rent it was already paying, financed at roughly 5.5% unsecured, terminated a master lease in active dispute, and eliminated an unknown 2027 rent reset. Cash flow improves by roughly $11M pre-tax annually and equity builds as debt amortizes against an owned asset. The operator has top-decile CMS star ratings, 90% occupancy, 10% free-cash-flow margins, and management compensation that is modest and demonstrably lagging its own performance measure.

Against that, the organic engine has slowed to 3% revenue and 1.5% pricing at the exact moment the multiple reached its high; the four tailwinds that doubled operating income from 2023 to 2025 — agency labor normalization, census recovery, the White Oak contribution and post-COVID rate catch-up — are each substantially spent; and the reported 24x trailing P/E is 31.5x once the $18.3M related-party fee release and the equity marks are removed. At 18x pro forma EV/EBITDA with $530M of unhedged floating-rate debt against undisclosed covenants, an unresolved hospice investigation sitting on 96.5% of the goodwill, a 1.21% dividend as the only genuine return of capital, share count rising 0.8%, and no earnings calls through which to interrogate any of it, the price already assumes the good outcome.

This marks a move to REDUCE off the prior HOLD. The 3.2% price decline since the prior review is not the reason. The reasons are the Q2 confirmation that same-facility growth has settled near 3.0% and per diem near 1.5%, the completed shift from a debt-free balance sheet to $530M of unhedged floating-rate borrowings, and the arithmetic showing that the headline earnings improvement is largely non-recurring. Revisit at $185–190, or on two consecutive quarters of reaccelerating same-facility revenue.
