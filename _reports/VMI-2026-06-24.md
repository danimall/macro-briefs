**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Valmont Industries is a diversified metal-products manufacturer whose Infrastructure segment (~75% of sales) is an indirect beneficiary of the U.S. grid-investment and data-center power build-out, while its Agriculture segment (~25% of sales) is in a cyclical trough compounded by an active geopolitical conflict that idled its Dubai irrigation plant. The two-speed dynamic is captured in the order book: Infrastructure backlog rose 22% year-over-year to $1.55 billion while Agriculture backlog fell 36% to $105 million. Q1 FY2026 confirmed the turn: North America Utility revenue grew 27.4% to $424 million, operating income rose 21.3%, and diluted EPS climbed 27.5% to $5.51, with management tying the demand to data-center and grid-load growth.

The headline FY2025 financials understate the underlying trajectory. GAAP operating income fell 20.8% to $415.6 million, but that decline was driven by one-time items: a $91.3 million long-lived-asset impairment (largely Solar and Access Systems), a $24.2 million Brazil legal reserve, $23.8 million of Brazil credit losses, and $15.4 million of realignment costs. Adjusted operating income rose to $537.9 million, and adjusted ROIC improved to 17.6% from 16.4%. Reported net income of $350.3 million was flattered by a 6.3% effective tax rate, a function of a $73.8 million worthless-securities deduction from the Prospera exit, versus an underlying rate near 23-25%. Earnings quality is below the headline, but the core is accelerating.

The constraint is price and positioning. The stock has appreciated roughly 76% over the trailing twelve months to ~$569, trades at ~25x current-year consensus EPS ($22.84) at a premium to its industrial-machinery peer median, sits below every published analyst price target (low $600, mean $611), and shows degrading analyst sentiment (a hold downgrade in the most recent month). Layered on top is a concentration of leadership change, a CFO transition and an auditor change in the same quarter, that warrants monitoring even though no restatement or control deficiency has been disclosed. We rate VMI a HOLD on a growth thesis: the Utility upcycle is genuine and durable, but the risk/reward after the run is balanced rather than asymmetric. We would add on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $400s where the forward multiple compresses closer to the growth rate.

## Company Overview

Valmont (founded 1946, Omaha, NE; NYSE-listed since 1968) operates two reportable segments:

- **Infrastructure (~$3.09B, ~75% of FY2025 sales):** Engineered steel, concrete, and composite structures across Utility (transmission, substation, distribution — the highest-quality franchise), Lighting & Transportation, Coatings (hot-dip galvanizing within a ~300-500 mile freight radius), Telecommunications (5G poles/towers, PIM mitigation via ConcealFab), and Solar (single-axis trackers, being deliberately shrunk).
- **Agriculture (~$1.01B, ~25% of FY2025 sales):** Valley®-brand center-pivot and linear mechanized irrigation, aftermarket parts, tubular products, and AgSense® precision agriculture (175,000+ connected devices).

Valmont is the #1 mechanized-irrigation maker globally and a leading North American utility-structure manufacturer, operating 80+ facilities on six continents with roughly 10,791 employees. Its moats are modest but real: engineering expertise on complex over-time utility contracts, 3-5 year preferred-provider arrangements, a dense North American utility manufacturing footprint, and a local-dealer irrigation network (~250 North American plus ~400 international dealers across 60+ countries). It competes primarily on price, delivery reliability, and engineering execution rather than a defensible high-margin franchise.

## Financial Analysis

The balance sheet is the clearest strength. Leverage stands at 1.05x against a 3.50x covenant, with roughly $740 million of undrawn revolver capacity, an Altman Z-score of 3.22 (safe zone), a current ratio of 2.35, and investment-grade ratings (Moody's Baa2 / S&P BBB+, both stable). ROE was 21.7% and ROA 10.5% in FY2025. This is a fortress capital structure with meaningful dry powder for buybacks and bolt-on M&A into the Utility upcycle.

Revenue has been flat-to-declining at the headline for three years — FY2022 $4.35B, FY2023 $4.17B, FY2024 $4.08B, FY2025 $4.10B (+0.7%) — masking a powerful internal mix shift. Diluted EPS of $16.79 (vs $17.19) reflects tax-rate noise rather than operating deterioration.

Q1 FY2026 marked the turn. Revenue grew 6.2% to $1.03B, gross margin expanded to 30.8% from 30.0%, and operating cash flow nearly doubled to $103.5M. North America Utility and North America Coatings (+13.3%), both data-center driven, powered the result. The offsets were Agriculture International (−32.7%, Dubai plant idled), Lighting & Transportation (−4.4%), and Telecom (−3.9%).

The earnings-surprise record reinforces forward confidence: four consecutive beats, including +15.3% in Q1 FY2026 and +76.3% in Q4 FY2025 (the latter inflated by the tax item). FCF conversion is reasonable — FY2025 FCF of $311.4M on net income of $350.3M is ~89% conversion, though that net income was tax-flattered, so cash-quality is sound. Capital allocation follows a stated ~50/50 split between growth (capex plus M&A) and shareholder returns: FY2026 capex guided up to $170-200M (from $145M) for Utility capacity, the dividend raised ~13% to $3.08 annualized, and ~$510M remaining on a $2.1B buyback authorization ($198M repurchased in FY2025, $57M in Q1 FY2026 at ~$431 average).

## Growth Analysis

The structural growth thesis is U.S. electrical-grid investment and data-center load growth, corroborated independently across the broader infrastructure layer (utility rate-base expansion, grid resiliency spending, electrification). Valmont sells the transmission and substation structures the grid requires; it is not an AI company and has no direct AI revenue, but it is a genuine picks-and-shovels beneficiary. The Utility growth and Infrastructure backlog are hard confirmation, and the raised capex guidance signals management's conviction that the demand is durable rather than a one-quarter spike.

Estimate revisions support the momentum. Current-year consensus EPS rose to $22.84 (+4.0% over 90 days), and next-year to $25.68 (+5.9% over 90 days, +1.2% over 30 days). These are positive and broadening, but they fall well short of the +30% revision velocity that would flag a true earnings-inflection BUY signal. The Infrastructure acceleration is partly offset by the Agriculture trough. The net is mid-to-high single-digit consolidated revenue growth with margin and mix tailwinds driving low-double-digit EPS growth, a respectable, durable profile rather than a parabolic one.

Agriculture is the swing factor. It is gated by farm income, grain prices, water policy, and government-funding timing, all currently soft, and the Dubai idling is an acute, ongoing drag that will not appear in most trailing data pulls. A normalization of the irrigation cycle and a restart of international ag operations would add a second growth leg; their continued weakness caps the consolidated growth rate.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$569, VMI trades at roughly 25x current-year consensus EPS and ~22x next-year ($25.68). Trailing P/E is ~34x on tax-flattered $16.79 EPS, which overstates the multiple; the forward figures are the right anchor given the earnings turn.

Against peers in Industrial Machinery & Supplies (median P/E 28.7), VMI's ~25x forward looks reasonable on headline, but the peer set is heterogeneous: Pentair at 18.2x, Donaldson at 23.0x, Flowserve at 28.7x, SPX Technologies at 44.7x, and Chart Industries showing negative trailing earnings. Adjusting for VMI's mid-to-high-single-digit revenue growth and ~22% ROE, a ~22-25x forward multiple is fair-to-full, not cheap. The stock sits below every published analyst target (low $600, median $600, high $645, mean $611), which on a name with rising estimates would normally argue targets are stale floors. The qualifier is that the analyst recommendation trend is degrading (a hold downgrade in the most recent month), which suggests the sell-side targets reflect recent momentum extrapolation rather than independent upside conviction.

We assign a valuation score of 5.0 — neither cheap nor egregiously stretched. The forward multiple is appropriate for the growth and quality on offer but does not provide asymmetric upside at the current price. Our calibration treats apparent cheapness skeptically as a value-trap signal, but VMI's issue is the opposite: the price has largely caught up to the improving fundamentals. We would become more constructive on the valuation in the low-to-mid $400s, where the forward multiple compresses toward ~18-19x and the PEG falls below the growth-justified level.

## Competitive Landscape

Valmont's competitive position is improving in its core. In Utility, it is a leading North American manufacturer entering a multi-year demand upcycle, gaining the benefit of preferred-provider relationships and a dense domestic footprint that local-freight economics favor. In irrigation, it is the global #1 by brand (Valley), with a dealer network spanning 60+ countries and a meaningful AgSense connected-device installed base. The Coatings business benefits from the same data-center-driven structural-steel demand.

Trajectory is the key dimension. The Infrastructure backlog growth signals share capture and demand strength, and the deliberate pruning of Solar (−46% in FY2025) and unprofitable ag-tech (Prospera exit) sharpens the portfolio toward the highest-return businesses. The realigned segment reporting — now organized by geography, with Solar demoted to "International Infrastructure and Solar" — confirms management is concentrating capital where the returns are. Adjusted ROIC rising to 17.6% from 14.0% over two years validates the discipline.

The caveats are real. Valmont competes on price in Coatings and international irrigation, where competition is fragmented. Some Utility and Telecom competitors have emerged from bankruptcy with lower cost structures, a latent margin risk. And the Telecom (5G) business is choppy on carrier-spend timing. On balance, the moat is modest but the trajectory is positive, supporting a competitive-position score of 7.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Agriculture trough plus active conflict exposure.** North American irrigation is soft on low grain prices and funding timing, while the international ag operation headquartered in Dubai went idle in Q1 FY2026 following the February 28, 2026 strikes on Iran. This is an acute, current drag that trailing screens miss, and its duration is outside management's control.

**Tariff / Section 232 / CBP exposure.** Valmont imported ~$220M of fabricated steel structures from Mexico in FY2025. An April 2026 proclamation sets a 10% rate for ≥95% U.S.-melted-and-poured steel content versus up to 50% otherwise. Management believes most Mexico poles qualify at 10%, and CBP cleared one of two valuation inquiries, but a second inquiry remains open with potential back-duties and penalties on an adverse finding.

**Steel commodity leverage.** Steel is ~50% of Utility net sales; a 20% move equals roughly a $110M revenue swing. Fixed-price Infrastructure contracts mean cost spikes can compress margin before pricing recovers.

**Impairment overhang.** Solar and Access Systems carry thin goodwill cushions; APAC Highway Safety and EMEA Structures show <15% fair-value cushion, and Access Systems has zero excess fair value — further impairments are plausible on softer performance.

**Governance churn.** A CFO transition (Liguori per the March 2026 proxy/Form 4s → Schwietz signing the April 2026 10-Q) coincided with a March 2026 auditor change to KPMG and a cluster of Item 5.02 8-Ks across Feb/Apr/Jun 2026. No restatement or control deficiency is disclosed; controls were deemed effective and KPMG was ratified (17.66M for vs 124k against), so this reads as orchestrated leadership refresh rather than a red flag. The concentration of finance and governance change in a single quarter is a legitimate watch-item for strategy continuity, and it tempers the management-quality score.

**Smaller items:** Brazil credit/legal exposure ($23.8M credit losses plus a ~$20M settlement, plus CALA peso volatility) and a UK Delta-legacy pension currently overfunded ~£29.4M but subject to regulatory recalculation risk.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and offers no contradiction to the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV of 30.7% is essentially in line with 1-year historical volatility of 29.9% (spread +0.8%, "fair"), sitting at the 1st percentile of its own chain; options are not pricing elevated near-term stress. The put/call skew is balanced (−1.0%), the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.33, and the term structure is in mild contango (longer-dated IV 32.8% > near-term), a normal/complacent shape. The 6-month tenor shows a steeper −4.3% put skew, indicating some demand for longer-dated downside protection, consistent with the late-cycle, post-run positioning. Short interest is light at 2.3% of float (2.0 days to cover), so there is neither a short overhang nor squeeze fuel. The implied ±6.4% move into the nearest expiry is modest. The market is not pricing a binary event, and the recent IV decline (33.6% → 30.7% since late May) alongside the +6.3% price move reflects resolved post-earnings uncertainty rather than complacency about a known catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$720).** The Utility/data-center upcycle proves multi-year and durable; Infrastructure backlog continues compounding above 20%; raised capex converts to capacity that captures share at attractive margins; the Agriculture trough bottoms and the Dubai operation restarts, adding a second growth leg; and the fortress balance sheet funds accretive buybacks and bolt-ons. Next-year EPS pushes toward $26-27 and the market awards ~27x, supporting roughly $720. This is above the high analyst target of $645 plus continued estimate revisions.

**Bear case (~$420, ~26% below current).** The data-center grid-build proves lumpier or earlier-cycle than the multi-year extrapolation implies; steel costs spike against fixed-price contracts and compress Infrastructure margin; the CBP valuation inquiry turns adverse, bringing back-duties; the Agriculture trough deepens with prolonged Dubai idling; and further impairments hit Access Systems and the thin-cushion units. The forward multiple de-rates toward ~17x on flat-to-down estimates, taking the stock to the low $400s — back to the technical support zone that prevailed through much of late 2025.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The Infrastructure growth thesis is genuine and confirmed in hard order data, but after a ~76% run the price has largely caught up to the fundamental improvement, and the stock trades below every analyst target into degrading sentiment with a leadership-churn overhang. The risk/reward is balanced, not asymmetric.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Utility growth and backlog trajectory), the resolution of the second CBP valuation inquiry, and any signal on Dubai/international ag restart.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $400s, compressing forward P/E toward ~18-19x and PEG below the growth-justified threshold.
- Infrastructure backlog growth sustained above 20% for a second consecutive quarter alongside next-year EPS revisions accelerating past +10% over 90 days.
- A confirmed Agriculture bottom (backlog stabilizing, Dubai restart) adding a credible second growth leg.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- North America Utility revenue growth decelerating below high-single digits, signaling the data-center demand was front-loaded.
- An adverse CBP determination bringing material back-duties, or fresh impairments in Access Systems / thin-cushion units.
- Operating margin compression from steel-cost pass-through failure, or any restatement / control deficiency emerging from the CFO/auditor transition.

## Conclusion

Valmont is a quality industrial with a fortress balance sheet executing into a genuine grid-investment and data-center power upcycle. Q1 FY2026 confirmed the turn, and four straight earnings beats plus rising estimates support forward confidence. The core is accelerating beneath flat headline revenue and tax-distorted reported earnings.

The constraint is price. After its trailing-twelve-month advance, VMI trades at a premium to its peer median, sits below every published analyst target with degrading sell-side sentiment, and carries an Agriculture trough deepened by an idled Dubai plant plus a concentration of finance and governance change in a single quarter. None of these is individually disqualifying, but together they leave the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate VMI a HOLD on a growth thesis and would look to add on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $400s, where the forward multiple realigns with the company's mid-to-high-single-digit growth and ~22% returns on equity.
