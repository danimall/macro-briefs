## Executive Summary

Murphy Oil's second quarter was, on the surface, the best in years: revenue from production of $926.3M (+35.6% YoY), operating income of $354.7M versus $92.2M, GAAP diluted EPS of $1.59, and total lifting cost down to $8.95/BOE from $11.95. WTI averaged $92.79 in the quarter. The problem is what happened underneath. For the first half, with WTI averaging $82.36 — above almost any mid-cycle assumption — Murphy's own non-GAAP reconciliation shows adjusted free cash flow of **negative $23.9M**. FY2025 adjusted FCF was $12.1M, down from $381.5M in FY2024. Zero shares were repurchased in H1 2026 against $100.9M a year earlier, $550.1M of authorization sits unused, and gross debt rose from $1,385.1M to $1,550.4M.

Then on August 5 management raised FY2026 capital expenditure ex-NCI from $1,200–1,300M to $1,500–1,600M, a $300M (+24%) increase at the midpoint, roughly six weeks after reaffirming the lower range. The stated drivers — the Bubale-1X discovery in Côte d'Ivoire, Bubale West-1X appraisal, incremental Eagle Ford activity, and cost overruns on Chinook #8 — are identifiable projects rather than cost leakage, and the annual incentive plan contains a free-cash-flow measure that this decision damages. That argues for conviction rather than indiscipline. It does not change the arithmetic: the equity's 2026 cash return is now the 3.65% dividend, funded alongside a growing debt balance.

Forward earnings momentum is negative and accelerating downward. Current-quarter consensus has fallen 33.4% in 90 days ($1.11 → $0.74), FY2027 is down 18.0% ($3.77 → $3.09), and Q3 company guidance implies Gulf of America oil volumes ex-NCI falling from 45,400 BOPD to 38,800 BOPD (−14.5%) with $135M of exploration expense versus $39.3M in Q2, against a WTI forward of $76.97 for the remainder of 2026 and $69.99 for 2027. The balance sheet is not the issue — 0.64x net debt/TTM adjusted EBITDA, 5.8x interest coverage, a fully undrawn $2.0B revolver to January 2031. The issue is that at ~4.0x EV/EBITDA the market is pricing a business that generates roughly no free cash flow at $80 oil. **HOLD, with new capital deferred.**

## Company Overview

Murphy is an independent E&P with an unusually broad asset mix for a $5.5B company. Q2 2026 production ex-NCI was approximately 169,000 BOEPD across four producing franchises plus a frontier exploration arm:

- **Onshore (~103,800 BOEPD, 38% liquids):** Eagle Ford (39,100 BOEPD, 26,900 BOPD oil; 35 gross wells planned online in 2026), Tupper Montney in British Columbia (58,100 BOEPD, essentially dry gas), Kaybob Duvernay in Alberta (6,600 BOEPD, 4,700 BOPD oil).
- **Offshore (~65,000 BOEPD, 88% liquids):** Gulf of America (57,100 BOEPD, 45,400 BOPD oil — Khaleesi/Mormont/Samurai on the King's Quay hub, Kodiak, Dalmatian, Lucius, St. Malo, Cascade & Chinook), held partly through MP Gulf of Mexico LLC where a third party holds a 20% noncontrolling interest; offshore Canada (7,900 BOEPD, non-operated Terra Nova and Hibernia).
- **Exploration:** Vietnam (Lac Da Vang development, Hai Su Vang appraisal), Côte d'Ivoire (four blocks), Morocco (Petroleum Agreement signed January 2026, ~75% WI), with Cameroon and Mauritania targeted for finalization in 2H26.

The structural asymmetry worth internalizing: Canada holds roughly 425 MMBOE of the ~730 MMBOE proved total, of which ~404 MMBOE is Tupper Montney gas (~2.28 Tcf), yet Canadian natural gas generated $48.0M of $926.3M (5.2%) of Q2 production revenue. Only 1.8% of proved reserves sat outside the US and Canada at year-end 2025 despite years of international exploration spend. Reserve life is roughly 11 years blended.

## Financial Analysis

**The three-year slide and the 2026 reversal.** Net income attributable to Murphy: $965.0M (2022) → $661.6M (2023) → $407.2M (2024) → $104.2M (2025), a FY2025 net margin of 3.9% on $2,689.8M of revenue. H1 2026 reversed hard: revenue from production $1,658.7M (+22.3%), operating income $492.9M versus $236.1M, net income attributable $285.2M versus $95.3M, diluted EPS $1.95 versus $0.66.

**Cost quality is mixed, and the two largest moves partly offset each other.** LOE fell to $8.95/BOE from $11.95, with US Offshore collapsing to $10.43/BOE from $20.91. The drivers split cleanly: non-repeat of 2025 workovers at Khaleesi/Marmalard/Samurai and lower FPSO rental after the Q1 2025 purchase of the *Pioneer* for $125.0M look durable; a one-time Lucius access fee is explicitly non-recurring; and lower King's Quay production-handling costs are a mechanical consequence of lower volumes, not an efficiency gain. Against that, DD&A rose to $16.16/BOE from $14.28, with US Onshore at $31.79/BOE for H1 and US Offshore at $18.21/BOE (from $11.72 in FY2023). Part of the LOE improvement and part of the DD&A increase are the same *Pioneer* transaction viewed from two income-statement lines, so the DD&A trend alone should not be read as reserve degradation. The rest reflects higher-cost recent Eagle Ford completions and mix.

**Cash flow is where the thesis breaks down.** From the company's own reconciliation:

| | Q2 2026 | H1 2026 | H1 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow (GAAP) | $655.9M | $977.1M | $658.7M |
| Property additions + dry hole | ($478.4M) | ($866.2M) | ($678.0M) |
| **Free cash flow** | **$110.0M** | **$151.4M** | **($27.2M)** |
| Dividends, NCI distributions, debt costs, withholding, acquisitions | ($72.2M) | ($175.2M) | ($120.7M) |
| **Adjusted free cash flow** | **$37.8M** | **($23.9M)** | **($147.9M)** |

The H1 figure reproduces: $151.4M − $100.3M dividends − $21.2M NCI distributions − $22.5M debt costs − $7.7M award withholding − $23.5M acquisitions = −$23.8M. Two consecutive years of near-zero-to-negative adjusted FCF, in an $80 oil environment, is the central fact.

**Balance sheet resilience is real.** At 6/30/26: total assets $10,278.1M, cash $483.9M, long-term debt $1,547.9M, ARO $1,040.6M, operating lease liabilities $713.3M, Murphy equity $5,259.0M. Current liabilities of $1,202.4M exceed current assets of $1,026.8M (a $175.6M working-capital deficit — normal for a producer carrying no meaningful inventory). Liquidity is roughly $2.48B including the fully undrawn $2.0B revolver maturing January 2, 2031. TTM adjusted EBITDA attributable of $1,664.5M against net debt of $1,066.5M gives **0.64x** (0.93x gross, 1.07x including operating leases, 1.69x including ARO). Interest coverage is **5.8x** (TTM EBIT $586.8M / interest $101.4M). The January refinancing — $500M of 6.50% 2034 notes issued, $227.5M of 2027/2028 notes redeemed at a $3.5M extinguishment cost, $100M net revolver repayment — leaves weighted average maturity at 8.7 years and coupon at 6.3%, with no meaningful wall before 2029–2030.

A screening-level Altman Z of 1.64 does not survive reconstruction; using FY2025 balances with retained earnings of $6,691.3M in X2 the score is ~1.94, and ~2.01 on 6/30/26 balances with TTM figures. The model penalizes low asset turnover and negative working capital, both structural for E&P. Solvency is not the risk here.

**One cash-flow caveat:** H1 cash income taxes paid were $5.9M against $34.5M current and $92.5M deferred tax expense, with the deferred tax liability rising from $378.3M to $464.5M. Book tax is being converted into deferred liabilities rather than cash outflows — a genuine but temporary FCF tailwind.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term volumes are going the wrong way, and the mix is deteriorating faster than the headline. Q2 production of 175,013 BOEPD including NCI was **down 10.9% YoY** on planned and unplanned downtime at Mormont, Kodiak and Samurai. Q3 guidance takes total ex-NCI volumes *up* to a 175,000 BOEPD midpoint on Tupper Montney gas while Gulf of America oil ex-NCI falls to 38,800 BOPD from 45,400 (−14.5%). Oil share of production drops from roughly 50.5% to 44.7%. Combine an ~8% decline in total oil volumes with a WTI forward of $76.97 against a Q2 average of $92.79 and $135M of guided Q3 exploration expense, and Q3 revenue and EPS should fall sharply sequentially despite higher BOE. Consensus reflects this: current-quarter EPS of $0.74, down from $1.11 ninety days ago.

The medium-term growth case rests on dated project start-ups, and these are concrete:

- **Lac Da Vang (Vietnam):** pipelines installed, FSO mobilized to final location, topsides installed. **First oil Q4 2026.**
- **Chinook #8 (Walker Ridge 425):** drilling complete, completion underway, **online Q4 2026 at ~15 MBOEPD gross** initial rate. Costs came in above plan.
- **Bubale-1X (Block CI-709, Côte d'Ivoire):** 100 feet of net pay across two reservoirs, announced June 23, 2026; Bubale West-1X appraisal spud in July, with an appraisal programme of up to five wells over 18–24 months.
- **Cello #1 and Banjo #1 (Mississippi Canyon 385):** Q1 2026 discoveries, first production Q4 2027.
- **Hai Su Vang:** HSV-2X and HSV-3X encountered hydrocarbons, HSV-4X was dry and expensed; FID pushed to **Q4 2027**, later than the FY2025 10-K framing.

The exploration bill for this is large and front-loaded. H1 exploration expense was $122.1M versus $24.9M, including $80.6M of dry-hole charges (Civette-1X, Caracal-1X in Côte d'Ivoire; Hai Su Vang-4X). FY2026 exploration expense is guided at $300M, with $100M of assumed 2H dry-hole expense — a budgeting convention rather than a prediction of failure, but a real charge if the convention holds. Capitalized exploratory well costs pending determination of proved reserves rose from $191.8M to $371.6M in six months ($150.3M Côte d'Ivoire, $114.0M Vietnam, $100.1M Gulf of America), with $22.3M across three projects suspended three years or more.

The earnings-surprise record is decent (three beats and one in-line over four quarters, including a $0.24 beat in Q3 2025 and $0.17 in Q4 2025), but Q2 landed exactly in line on non-GAAP EPS of $1.55 while revenue of $926.3M exceeded a $871M secondary-source estimate. Both consensus figures are unverified against a primary provider; the in-line print plus the capex raise, not the revenue beat, is what the stock traded on.

Growth score reflects negative revision momentum and a sequential volume/mix decline against genuine but 2027-weighted project additions.

## Valuation Assessment

At $38.38 the equity is $5.50B and enterprise value $6.57B including $1,066.5M of net debt:

| EV / TTM adj. EBITDA incl. operating leases in EV | **4.38x** |
| P/E on TTM GAAP EPS (~$2.02) | **19.0x** |
| P/E on FY2026 consensus ($3.50) | **11.0x** |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus ($3.09) | **12.4x** |
| Dividend yield ($1.40 annualized) | **3.65%** |
| Unused buyback authorization | $550.1M = 10.0% of market cap |

The profile fields showing forward and trailing P/E of 65.6x on $0.72 EPS are not reconcilable with H1 2026 diluted EPS of $1.95 and appear stale; I use filing-derived figures throughout.

Against peers the picture is muddled by composition. Peer median trailing P/E is 6.2x, but CRC (−10.6x) and CRGY (−11.1x) are loss-making, and CNX (4.4x) and CRK (6.2x) are gas-weighted. The closest oil-weighted comparator, MGY, trades at 15.8x with a 2.4% yield. MUR at 19.0x trailing GAAP and 11.0x forward sits between the gassy cheap names and MGY, with a higher yield than the median and materially more offshore/frontier risk.

The EV/EBITDA multiple of ~4.0x is inside the normal 3.5–5.0x mid-cap E&P band, which is the honest read: the stock is fairly priced, not mispriced. Two things prevent a higher valuation score.

First, **the earnings base is above the curve.** TTM EBITDA of $1.66B was earned at roughly $75 average WTI; H1 alone realized $82.36. The forward curve as of August 3 is $76.97 for the remainder of 2026 and $69.99 for 2027. On a $70 WTI 2027, EBITDA attributable plausibly compresses to $1.4–1.5B, putting the current EV at 4.4–4.7x. FY2027 consensus EPS of $3.09 — itself down 18% in 90 days — puts the stock at 12.4x, above the peer median on a lower-quality free-cash profile.

Second, **there is effectively no free-cash-flow yield.** FY2026 capex ex-NCI of $1.5–1.6B against operating cash flow that annualizes near $1.9–2.0B leaves roughly $350–450M before $200.8M of dividends, $40M+ of NCI distributions and debt costs. H1 already delivered negative adjusted FCF. A ~0–3% free-cash yield at $80 oil is not a valuation-support argument, and it explains why the sell side is neutral: 1 strong buy, 2 buy, 11 hold, 1 sell, with a mean target of $43.07 (+12.2%) and Morgan Stanley at Underweight with a $35 target following its June 29 cut.

A cheap-looking multiple in a commodity business at above-forward realized prices is the classic setup where the multiple is cheap because the earnings are not repeatable. That is my read here, and it is why valuation stays at 4.8 despite the price being 11.4% below the April 7 high of $43.34.

## Competitive Landscape

Two verifiable advantages. First, **realizations**: Q2 onshore US oil realized $99.55/bbl and offshore US $99.36/bbl against $92.79 WTI, a ~$6.7/bbl premium reflecting Gulf Coast/LLS-linked pricing rather than Midland or Cushing barrels. Second, **deepwater cash margins when uptime cooperates**: US Offshore LOE of $10.43/BOE against ~$99 realizations is a very wide margin. The binding constraint has been availability — Mormont, Kodiak and Samurai downtime in Q2, workovers at Khaleesi/Marmalard/Samurai in 2025 — not price or cost.

The strategic trajectory is the debate. Most $5B North American independents have narrowed toward single-basin scale to compress cost per barrel and maximize distributions. Murphy is widening: Côte d'Ivoire, Vietnam, Morocco, with Cameroon and Mauritania pending, funded from cash flow that would otherwise support buybacks or debt reduction. Management frames this as optionality. The counter-evidence is that after years of international spending only 1.8% of proved reserves sit outside the US and Canada, and roughly 58% of proved reserves (Canada onshore, overwhelmingly Tupper Montney gas) generate 5.2% of quarterly revenue at AECO of C$1.63/Mcf with forwards of US$1.35 for the rest of 2026 and US$1.69 for 2027. Canada onshore cash margin computes to roughly $6/BOE before severance, G&A and $4.96/BOE of DD&A — and that blends Kaybob Duvernay liquids with Tupper dry gas, so standalone Tupper is thinner. Above-market Canadian fixed-price forwards (88 MMcf/d at C$2.81 in Q3, 59 MMcf/d at C$3.00 in Q4) are a real mitigant that partially offsets this.

Approximately 19% of production and 12% of proved reserves are operated by third parties, including Terra Nova and Hibernia — a structural constraint on Murphy's ability to control uptime and capital pacing.

Cumulative indexed TSR since 2020 stands at 302.12 versus a peer group at 282.89, with FY2025 outperformance of +8.7% against peers' +0.8% despite a 74% decline in net income. The market has not, so far, penalized the exploration pivot. That is the strongest evidence against my reading of the strategy.

## Risk Assessment

**Guidance credibility (dominant near-term risk).** The 24% mid-year capex increase, arriving roughly six weeks after reaffirmation, is the largest single hit to the file. It reduces 2026 shareholder-return capacity directly and raises the probability that the 2027 programme also exceeds initial framing given a Bubale appraisal running up to five wells over 18–24 months.

**Fully unhedged oil into a declining curve.** No crude oil or natural gas derivative contracts were held at 6/30/26 or during H1. The only price protection is physical Canadian fixed-price gas. On oil, exposure is symmetric — an asset if Hormuz disruption persists, a liability against a $69.99 2027 forward.

**Exploration write-off overhang.** $371.6M of capitalized exploratory well costs pending determination, plus $100M of assumed 2H dry-hole expense on top of $80.6M already taken. Frontier concentration (Côte d'Ivoire, Vietnam, Morocco, pending Cameroon/Mauritania) adds fiscal-regime and above-ground risk against minimal booked international reserves.

**Vietnam acreage expiry.** A large majority of net undeveloped Vietnamese acreage carries 2026 expirations, with extensions being sought. Vietnam holds $114.0M of suspended well costs plus the Lac Da Vang development and Hai Su Vang FID.

**Catastrophe exposure versus insurance.** Roughly $500M of liability coverage per occurrence and ~$450M of property/well-control coverage rising to ~$850M in the Gulf of America, with $10–25M deductibles. For a deepwater operator, a severe well-control event exceeds these limits and the residual is equity risk. ARO stands at $1,040.6M.

**Unresolved disclosures.** "Other operating expenses" rose $16.0M in Q2 and $17.5M in H1 solely on an increase in a joint-venture dispute settlement provision, with no counterparty, asset or total accrual named while Legal Proceedings describes all litigation as routine. Separately, a June 11, 2026 Item 5.02 8-K (director/officer departure or election) could not be retrieved; the CEO, CFO and CAO are all confirmed in place as of the August 5 10-Q, so this is not a top-three departure, but it remains an open item.

**Offsetting factors already priced.** No impairments in Q2 2026, H1 2026, Q2 2025 or H1 2025 — a break from FY2025 (Dalmatian ~$115M) and FY2024. The regulatory backdrop is currently a tailwind (Paris withdrawal effective 1/27/2026, OBBBA deferring the methane waste-emissions charge to 2034, announced EPA repeal of the 2009 Endangerment Finding), with reversal risk on any change in administration posture. The 10-Q confirms no new or modified risk factors versus the FY2025 10-K.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 64.0% against 47.8% trailing realized volatility is rich (16.2 points), sits in the 80th percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in **backwardation** (64.0% at one month versus 46.3% at six months) — the market is pricing near-term stress rather than a longer-horizon repricing. IV expanded from 49.9% to 64.0% since July 19 while the price fell only 2.1%, so the vol bid is not a mechanical response to a large move. The nearest expiry (August 21, 14 days) implies ±11.3%, and the supplied read on near-term skew is elevated put demand (8.7%), with a put/call volume ratio of 1.21 rising to 1.75 at the three-month tenor. Longer-dated skew values carry the opposite sign and I do not infer direction from them. The put/call open-interest ratio of 0.07 is anomalously low and should not be over-read.

Short interest is 9.6% of float with 4.8 days to cover — moderate, uncrowded enough that a squeeze is not the dominant mechanic, and consistent with a market that views the name as fairly valued rather than broken. Taken together, options positioning corroborates the fundamental caution: the market expects a two-sided, near-dated move, most plausibly around oil-price resolution and the Q3 volume/exploration-expense guide.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $54 (+41%)

Hormuz disruption persists or escalates. Murphy is one of the few mid-cap E&Ps with **zero oil hedges**, so a WTI move to $100+ flows straight to earnings — the Q2 print at $92.79 WTI produced $1.59 of GAAP EPS in a quarter with 10.9% lower volumes and depressed Gulf of America uptime. Lac Da Vang first oil and Chinook #8 (~15 MBOEPD gross) both arrive in Q4 2026, restoring the oil mix that Q3 loses, and Bubale West-1X appraises a 100-foot net-pay discovery. Capex normalizes in 2027, adjusted FCF turns solidly positive, and the $550.1M buyback authorization (10.0% of market cap) reactivates. On $5.50 of EPS at 10x, the equity is worth $55; the high analyst target is $70. Balance-sheet capacity to fund this exists: 0.64x net leverage and $2.48B of liquidity.

### Bear Case — $29 (−24%)

The 2027 forward curve of $69.99 verifies as Hormuz normalizes. Unhedged realizations fall roughly 25% from H1 levels. FY2027 consensus of $3.09 proves optimistic; DD&A continues rising toward $32/BOE onshore while volumes stay flat; the $100M assumed 2H dry-hole expense materializes and Bubale West-1X or Lac Da Trang North-1X disappoints, expensing part of the $371.6M pending balance. Adjusted FCF stays negative through 2027, debt grows further, and the market applies a 3.5x EV/EBITDA multiple to $1.35B of EBITDA: $4.73B EV less $1.07B net debt = $3.66B equity, or roughly $25–29 per share. Dividend cover from free cash flow, already marginal, becomes a question.

### Base Case — $41 (+6.8%)

WTI averages $75–80, above the curve but below H1 realizations, consistent with partial de-escalation without verified normalization. FY2026 EPS lands near $3.30–3.50 and FY2027 near $3.10. Lac Da Vang and Chinook #8 start on schedule, offsetting Gulf of America decline; capex holds at the revised $1.5–1.6B; adjusted FCF is approximately breakeven for the year with no buyback. At 12x $3.40 or 4.5x EV/EBITDA on $1.5B, fair value is $40–42, essentially the analyst median of $42.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The dividend is covered by operating cash flow but not by free cash flow, so this is not an income hold; the growth catalysts are real but 2027-weighted and hostage to the oil curve, so it is not a growth hold either. Existing holders are compensated by a 3.65% yield, a genuinely resilient balance sheet, and free upside optionality on Hormuz. New capital has better entry points.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **2H 2026 adjusted free cash flow positive with buybacks resumed** — any repurchase against the $550.1M authorization, reported in the Q3 or Q4 10-Q, would confirm the capex increase was a timing trough rather than a structural returns problem.
2. **Capex held at $1,500–1,600M through the Q3 print (early November) and initial 2027 guidance at or below $1.4B**, with Lac Da Vang first oil and Chinook #8 online in Q4 2026 as guided.
3. **Consensus revisions turning positive** — FY2027 EPS stabilizing above $3.20 after the 18% cut, or a price near **$30–32**, which would put the stock at ~10x FY2027 and ~3.4x EV/EBITDA with base-case upside above 25%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **A second capex increase**, or 2027 guidance above $1.6B, which would establish a pattern rather than an event.
2. **Dry holes at Bubale West-1X or Lac Da Trang North-1X** combined with expensing of a material share of the $371.6M pending exploratory balance, or failure to extend Vietnamese undeveloped acreage.
3. **Lac Da Vang first oil slipping beyond Q1 2027 or Chinook #8 beyond Q1 2027**, which would leave 2027 volumes flat with rising DD&A and no offsetting oil mix.
4. **WTI sustained below $65** with the company still unhedged, which would put dividend cover from free cash flow in play.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print in early November is the decisive checkpoint — it will show whether the revised capex holds, whether Q3 exploration expense landed at the guided $135M, and whether Gulf of America oil recovers from the 38,800 BOPD guide. Lac Da Vang first oil in Q4 2026 is the second dated event. Any 72-hour verified normalization of Hormuz commercial transit would compress the unhedged upside case immediately and should be treated as a re-underwriting event.

## Conclusion

Murphy's score moves from 5.0 to 4.8, the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the components have separated further rather than converged. The trajectory data showed growth prospects declining from 6.5 to 5.4 and risk factors from 5.5 to 4.8 across prior reviews; the new evidence confirms both directions. Growth: Q2 volumes down 10.9% YoY, Q3 Gulf of America oil guided down 14.5%, current-quarter consensus cut 33.4% in 90 days. Risk: fully unhedged into a $69.99 2027 forward, $371.6M of pending exploratory costs, a $300M mid-year capex increase after reaffirmation, and options pricing near-term stress at 64% IV in backwardation. Valuation stays flat at 4.8 rather than improving, because the 11% decline from the April high has been more than matched by an 18% cut to FY2027 consensus — the forward multiple did not get cheaper.

The offsetting case is not trivial. Net leverage of 0.64x, 5.8x interest coverage, $2.48B of liquidity, an 8.7-year weighted average debt maturity, a ~$6.7/bbl realization premium, US Offshore LOE at $10.43/BOE, two dated Q4 2026 project start-ups, a 3.65% dividend, and an incentive plan whose free-cash-flow measure argues the capex decision reflected conviction rather than indiscipline. Murphy also outperformed its peer TSR set in 2025 despite a 74% earnings decline, which is the strongest evidence that the market accepts the exploration strategy.

What decides the call is the arithmetic of asymmetry. Base case upside of 6.8% against bear-case downside of 24% is not a position worth initiating, and a business that produced negative adjusted free cash flow with WTI at $82.36 does not have a valuation floor from cash returns. **HOLD. Own it for the yield and the unhedged optionality if already positioned; wait for either resumed buybacks with positive 2H free cash flow, or a price near $30–32, before committing new capital.**
