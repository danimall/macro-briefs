**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Northwest Natural Holding (NWN) is a 100-plus-year-old regulated gas distribution utility that has spent the last several years rebuilding itself into a multi-state, three-business platform: a core Oregon/Southwest Washington gas LDC (~88% of net income), a fast-growing Texas gas utility (SiEnergy, ~18% organic customer growth), a five-state regulated water business, and a non-regulated RNG/storage portfolio. FY2025 produced record headline results — GAAP diluted EPS of $2.77 (+37% YoY), revenue of $1,289M, and operating cash flow of $269M — but the quality of that growth matters: gas volumes actually fell 5% on a 20%-warmer-than-average year, so the gain came from Oregon rate increases, the SiEnergy acquisition, and Arizona water rates rather than underlying demand.

The strategic logic is sound. Oregon and Washington are among the most decarbonization-hostile regulatory jurisdictions for gas utilities in the country (Climate Protection Program, Climate Commitment Act, HB 3179 rate-case restrictions, line-extension-allowance phase-out by Nov 2027). Diversifying earnings toward pro-gas Texas, regulated water, and FERC-jurisdictional storage reduces dependence on a single hostile jurisdiction and a single lumpy rate case. The MX3 storage expansion (4–5 Bcf, ~$300M, 12.5% contractually-fixed FERC ROE, 25-year contracts with investment-grade counterparties, in-service end-2029) is the most attractive single asset in the story and sits outside current guidance, providing upside optionality that would lift the long-term EPS growth target from 4–6% to 5–7% once notice-to-proceed is reached.

The offsets are equally concrete. Acquisition financing pushed holding-company leverage from a ~45/55 to a ~38/62 equity/debt mix, and the resulting interest expense widened the "Other" segment drag to −$0.60 EPS in 2025. The diversification has not yet rewarded shareholders — 5-year TSR of 126.6 trails the peer group's 142.7. At $48.87, NWN trades at roughly 16x trailing EPS and ~16x the 2026 guide midpoint, near the gas-utility peer median (17.0x) and yielding ~4.0%. That is a fair price for a stable income compounder. We rate the shares a HOLD for income, with a path to upgrade on MX3 notice-to-proceed and evidence the SiEnergy/Water growth is outrunning the interest drag.

## Company Overview

NW Holdings is the parent of NW Natural, a regulated gas LDC serving ~810,000 meters in Oregon (88%) and Southwest Washington (12%), regulated by OPUC and WUTC. The company now reports four segments:

1. **NWN Gas Utility (core):** The Oregon/Washington LDC plus the Mist gas storage facility and a regulated RNG holding company. Still ~88% of consolidated net income and the cash engine.
2. **SiEnergy Gas Utility (Texas):** Acquired January 7, 2025 for ~$331.5M cash (plus the "Pines" bolt-on in June 2025). Serves Houston, Dallas, and Austin metros with ~90,000 meters, 18% organic customer growth in 2025, and a backlog of ~250,000 future meters (+30% YoY). Contributed $0.33 EPS (11% of consolidated).
3. **NWN Water Utility:** Regulated water/wastewater across OR, WA, ID, TX, AZ — ~81,000 connections, 5.6% growth. Contributed $0.35 EPS.
4. **NW Holdings Other:** Non-regulated RNG (Ohio Renewables/EDL landfill projects), interstate storage, pipeline equity stakes, and holding-company interest expense — a −$0.60 EPS drag in 2025.

The competitive moat is the standard regulated-monopoly franchise with cost-recovery mechanisms (decoupling, weather normalization, purchased-gas adjustment), supplemented by a uniquely valuable Mist gas-storage asset connected to a pipeline-constrained Pacific Northwest grid, 70 consecutive years of dividend increases (one of only three NYSE companies with that record), and an investment-grade credit profile (S&P A− issuer, A+ for NW Natural; Moody's A2 senior secured).

CEO Justin Palfreyman, the architect of the diversification strategy via his corporate-development background, took over from longtime CEO David Anderson in 2025 and is now executing the platform he built.

Several observations follow from the data:

**Growth was rate- and acquisition-driven, not demand-driven.** The headline EPS gain came from the Nov-2024 and Oct-2025 Oregon rate increases, the SiEnergy acquisition, and Arizona water rates. The recurring earnings base is real (rates do not reverse), but investors should not extrapolate 37% growth; the 2026 guide of $2.95–$3.15 implies low-single-digit growth on the 2025 adjusted EPS base of $2.93.

**Leverage rose sharply to fund the platform build.** The holding-company equity/long-term-debt mix moved from ~45/55 (2024) to ~38/62 (2025) after a $273M bridge loan (repaid), $325M of 7.00% junior subordinated debentures (March 2025), SiEnergy notes, and new first-mortgage bonds. Consolidated D/E is ~4.2x. The regulated utility itself maintains a disciplined ~50/50 regulatory capital structure; the leverage sits at the parent. The mechanical consequence is a rising interest burden, and the central financial question is whether SiEnergy/Water earnings growth outpaces this drag.

**The Altman Z-Score of 0.79 ("distress zone") is a screening false-positive.** The model is not calibrated for regulated utilities, which structurally carry high leverage and low asset turnover (0.21x). NW Natural is investment-grade, compliant with all covenants (60.7% debt/cap vs. a 70% limit), with ~$590M of liquidity at year-end. Distress risk is genuinely low; the rising interest expense, not solvency, is the real issue.

**Capital intensity is the defining structural feature.** $467M of capex in 2025, guided to $500–550M in 2026 and $2.6–2.9B over 2026–2030, supports 6–8% rate-base growth. Funding this requires continuous external financing (ATM equity ~$40–50M/yr plus long-term debt), producing ongoing dilution and rate-case dependency. FCF is structurally negative during the build phase, which in a higher-for-longer rate environment compresses per-share growth — the same headwind weighing on financing-heavy regulated utilities broadly.

**Earnings track record is mixed but in-range.** Over the last four quarters: two beats, one in-line, one miss (Q1-2026 EPS $2.33 vs. $2.41, −3.2%). This is normal seasonal-utility variance, not a deterioration signal. The qualified pension plan is underfunded by $71.5M (improved YoY). The indicated dividend is $1.97/share (~4.0% yield), with the payout ratio being moderated toward a 55–65% target; management has signaled dividend growth will accelerate from the recent ~0.5%/yr token increases.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is bifurcated. The core Oregon/Washington gas franchise is a low-single-digit, rate-base-driven compounder facing structural policy headwinds. The growth offset comes from three places:

- **Texas (SiEnergy):** Targets 15–20% annual customer growth through 2030 in a pro-gas RRC regulatory environment with cost-deferral mechanisms (HB 4384, HB 5384, GRIP) and Texas in-migration tailwinds. This is the highest-quality growth asset in the portfolio.
- **Water:** ~5.6% connection growth across five states, a steadier multi-jurisdiction compounder.
- **MX3 storage:** Not yet in guidance. A FERC-regulated expansion with a contractually fixed 12.5% ROE (vs. ~9.5% allowed on gas distribution), a 50% equity layer, and 25-year contracts with investment-grade counterparties. Permits are secured; EPC contracts are being finalized; notice-to-proceed targeted by end-2027. Management says MX3 would lift the long-term EPS growth target from 4–6% to 5–7% once NTP is achieved.

Estimate revisions are constructive but modest: current-year consensus has risen +1.6% over 90 days to $3.06, next-year to $3.23 (roughly flat over 90 days). This is a stable mid-single-digit grower with optionality, without the large positive revision velocity that would change the framing. The data-center "behind-the-meter" inbound interest management cited is genuine optionality but unannounced and unquantified; we assign it no value today.

Weighed together, the platform supports a credible 4–6% EPS growth trajectory with a path to 5–7% if MX3 reaches NTP. The recurring-rate base is durable; growth velocity is capped by the negative-FCF/dilution model and the interest drag.

NWN trades at a slight discount to the peer-median P/E with a clearly above-median dividend yield. That is reasonable rather than mispriced. The discount is justified by the company's higher holding-company leverage, the Oregon/Washington policy overhang, and the persistent TSR underperformance. The above-median yield is supported by the 70-year dividend-increase record and a payout ratio being managed into a 55–65% band, so the income case has real support.

A cheap multiple is not, on its own, a buy signal, and across utilities specifically a low headline P/E has frequently flagged structurally impaired franchises rather than bargains. Here the discount is modest and explainable. The one genuine source of upside not in the price is MX3, which is off-guidance and would re-rate the growth algorithm if it reaches NTP. Until that is contractually committed, the stock is fairly valued.

Analyst targets span $50–$61 (mean $57.5, median $58.0), implying ~17–25% upside to the mean. We treat these as aspirational rather than as floors: on a stable utility at ~16x forward with mid-single-digit growth, the logic that analyst targets act as floors for cheap inflection stocks does not hold. A base-case fair value near $54–$56 — roughly 17.5–18x the 2026 midpoint, a modest premium for the MX3 optionality and dividend record — is more defensible than the consensus mean.

## Competitive Landscape

NW Natural's direct competition is electricity, fuel oil, and propane in residential/commercial markets; the structural threat is electrification policy, not a competitor taking share. The bull framing reframes gas storage as a grid-resilience asset: management argues the regional electric grid faces a potential 9 GW capacity shortfall by 2030, and NW Natural's system "delivers ~45% more energy than any other Oregon utility over a year," with Mist/MX3 positioned as cost-effective reliability infrastructure given the single interstate pipeline serving the region. This is a coherent argument that gas storage is a regional-resilience asset rather than a stranded liability, and it aligns with the broader energy-security reframing benefiting power infrastructure.

The competitive trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. In Texas, SiEnergy is gaining share in a supportive jurisdiction with strong demographics. In Oregon/Washington, the franchise faces policy that actively restricts new gas hookups, phases out line-extension allowances, and constrains rate-case filing cadence. The diversification reduces concentration risk over time, but 88% of earnings still sit in the hostile jurisdictions, and the strategy has not yet shown up in relative TSR. The competitive position is best described as stable-with-improving-mix but unproven on a returns basis.

## Risk Assessment

The company-specific risks beyond generic utility exposure are material and concentrated:

1. **Decarbonization / gas-ban policy (the defining existential risk).** Oregon's Climate Protection Program (−50% GHG by 2035, −90% by 2050) and Washington's Climate Commitment Act directly target gas utilities. Washington building codes increasingly restrict new gas hookups (NW Natural is litigating). Oregon's OPUC ordered the phase-out of the line-extension allowance by Nov 2027 (a $13.7M rate-base hit, under appeal). The company is a defendant in two climate-related lawsuits. WA ballot measure I-2066 (protecting gas access) passed but was ruled unconstitutional and is on appeal to the WA Supreme Court. This is a slow-moving but structural threat to ~88% of earnings.

2. **Oregon HB 3179 rate-case restriction (new in 2025).** Prohibits NW Natural from filing a general rate case within 18 months of its last increase (until Jan 2027 or multi-year rules), limits increases to April–October, and adds affordability criteria — intensifying regulatory lag. The company filed an alternative rate mechanism (+1.5%, ~$15.6M) as a workaround.

3. **Acquisition integration and financing risk.** SiEnergy/Pines doubled goodwill to $371M and loaded the balance sheet with debt. Integration risk and the need to reset SiEnergy Texas rates (unchanged since 2023) are live items.

4. **Single-pipeline dependency.** The entire NW Natural distribution system connects to one interstate pipeline (Northwest Pipeline); a rupture or capacity constraint would be material. ~60% of gas is imported from Canada (USMCA tariff exposure flagged, currently immaterial).

5. **Non-regulated tail risks.** Jonah Energy (gas-reserves counterparty) "no longer maintains any company credit ratings"; Tyson Foods announced closure of a Lexington, NE plant feeding one RNG biogas project; the Ohio Renewables/EDL RNG business has a structural buy-variable/sell-fixed mismatch through 2044 (~$599M of purchase commitments post-2030). None is individually material per management, but collectively they form the tail risk of an otherwise clean regulated story.

6. **Weather/volume risk.** Two consecutive very warm years (17%, then 20% above average) pressured volumes; ~12% of WA customers and ~7% of Oregon opt-outs lack weather-normalization protection.

7. **Rate-path sensitivity.** The negative-FCF/dilution model and rising holding-company interest expense are direct headwinds in a higher-for-longer environment. A hawkish rate path compresses both per-share growth and the multiple.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 21.2% sits roughly in line with 1-year historical volatility of 20.3% (spread +0.9%, IV percentile 8% within the chain) — options are fairly priced, with no elevated event premium. The put/call skew of 17.6% indicates somewhat elevated put demand, and the term structure is in contango (near-term 21.2% vs. long-term 33.4%), a normal structure for a low-beta utility. The put/call OI ratio rose from 0.01 to 0.34 since early May, but the absolute level (0.34) and low put/call volume ratio (0.32) suggest light hedging rather than informed bearish positioning. The options market is not flagging an imminent catalyst and is broadly consistent with a stable-income view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$58–61)
The Texas SiEnergy franchise compounds customers at 15–20% and scales into a meaningful earnings contributor; MX3 reaches notice-to-proceed by end-2027, lifting the long-term growth algorithm to 5–7% and adding a FERC-regulated, 12.5%-ROE, 25-year-contracted asset; the dividend growth rate accelerates from token increases as the payout normalizes into 55–65%; and the gas-as-grid-resilience reframing gains regulatory traction, easing the Oregon/Washington overhang. In this scenario the stock re-rates toward the upper end of the peer multiple, ~18.5–19x the 2027 earnings power, reaching $58–61.

### Bear Case (~$40–43)
Oregon/Washington decarbonization policy accelerates (gas-hookup bans upheld, line-extension phase-out sustained, further rate-case restrictions), impairing the core franchise; the holding-company interest drag continues to widen faster than SiEnergy/Water growth; continued warm weather pressures volumes; and a higher-for-longer rate path compresses both the negative-FCF financing model and the multiple. The stock derates toward 13–14x on a stalled growth algorithm, approaching the 52-week low near $39 (intraday), ~14–18% below current.

### Base Case (~$54–56)
The platform delivers the guided $2.95–$3.15 in 2026 and mid-single-digit EPS growth thereafter; MX3 progresses but is not yet contractually committed; the dividend grows modestly; and the stock holds a peer-comparable multiple with a slight premium for optionality. Fair value of ~17.5–18x the 2026 midpoint, ~$54–56, ~10–15% above current including the ~4% yield.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an income-oriented HOLD with a 12–18 month horizon, anchored by the ~4.0% dividend and the MX3 catalyst path.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- MX3 reaches notice-to-proceed (targeted end-2027) or signs binding 25-year offtake contracts, formally lifting the long-term EPS growth target to 5–7%.
- Evidence over two or more quarters that SiEnergy/Water segment EPS growth is outpacing the holding-company interest drag (i.e., the "Other" segment loss stabilizes or narrows from −$0.60).
- A pullback toward the low-$40s that pushes the forward P/E meaningfully below the peer median while fundamentals are intact, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse, durable Oregon/Washington policy ruling that materially impairs the core franchise (upheld gas-hookup bans, sustained line-extension phase-out without recovery offset, or a fresh rate-case restriction).
- A dividend cut or a halt to the 70-year increase streak (would invalidate the income thesis).
- A covenant or credit-rating downgrade event tied to the rising holding-company leverage, or a SiEnergy integration write-down.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2-2026 print, the Washington rate-case outcome (new rates target Aug 2026), and progress on MX3 EPC contracting toward the end-2027 NTP target. A June 2026 8-K indicating fresh financing/material-agreement activity should be reviewed for terms and leverage impact.

## Conclusion

NW Holdings is a well-managed, investment-grade gas utility executing a credible diversification away from a hostile single-jurisdiction franchise toward a multi-state, three-business platform with off-balance-sheet optionality in the MX3 storage project. The new CEO's open-market purchase (1,225 shares at $48.75) and a director purchase are constructive alignment signals during the leadership transition, and the dividend-increase record plus a payout being managed toward 55–65% support a durable ~4.0% income stream.

The offsets keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY. The strategy has not yet beaten peers on TSR; leverage has risen materially and the interest drag is real; Oregon/Washington decarbonization policy is a structural threat to 88% of earnings; and the negative-FCF/dilution model faces a higher-for-longer rate headwind. At ~16x forward earnings and a peer-comparable yield, the stock is fairly valued with no asymmetric mispricing to exploit. The MX3 notice-to-proceed and clear evidence that SiEnergy/Water growth is outrunning the interest drag are the proof points that would convert this from an income hold into an appreciation thesis. **Recommendation: HOLD (income).**
