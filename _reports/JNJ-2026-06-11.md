**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Johnson & Johnson is executing well on the operating front. Q1 2026 revenue rose 9.9% reported (6.4% operational, 3.5% FX) to $24.06B, an acceleration from the 6.0% full-year 2025 pace, and management raised full-year guidance. The standout is that the company is out-growing its own patent cliff inside the immunology franchise: TREMFYA grew $652M year-over-year (+68% to $1.61B) while STELARA collapsed roughly 60% to $656M. Oncology compounded 22.8% (DARZALEX $3.96B +22.5%, CARVYKTI +62%, RYBREVANT/LAZCLUZE +83%), and neuroscience grew 32%. This is the cleanest fundamental momentum JNJ has shown since the 2023 Kenvue spinoff turned it into a focused two-segment pharma/MedTech company.

The problem is price and tail risk, not the business. The stock has appreciated 52% off its $149 fifty-two-week low and now trades at roughly 27.6x trailing earnings and ~20.8x the $11.57 current-year consensus EPS. That trailing multiple sits about 39% above the pharmaceutical peer median P/E of 19.9 (MRK 33.8 is the outlier; PFE 19.9, BMY 16.0, ZTS 13.0 anchor the low end). The headline GAAP earnings are also distorted: a ~$7.0B talc reserve reversal in Q1 2025 inflated FY2025 net income, so trailing EPS overstates the underlying run-rate. Against this richer valuation sits the defining idiosyncratic risk: approximately 75,000 talc plaintiffs against a remaining reserve of only ~$3.4B, after three failed Chapter 11 maneuvers pushed the litigation back into the tort system with bellwether trials running through 2026.

We rate JNJ a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The operating story is real and the dividend record (63 consecutive years, two increases in the trailing twelve months) supports owning the stock for total return. But after a 52% run that has closed most of the prior talc/patent-cliff discount, the combination of a full multiple and an open-ended, binary litigation liability that is not provisioned for on the balance sheet caps the risk-adjusted upside. A fresh buy here requires either a multiple closer to peers, a defined talc resolution, or both.

## Company Overview

Following the 2023 spinoff of its consumer-health unit (Kenvue), JNJ is a focused two-segment healthcare company:

- **Innovative Medicine (Pharma):** $60.4B FY2025 sales (64% of total), 36.9% segment pre-tax margin, across oncology, immunology, neuroscience, pulmonary hypertension, infectious diseases, and cardiovascular/metabolism. This is the higher-margin growth engine.
- **MedTech (Devices):** $33.8B FY2025 sales (36% of total), lower-margin (~12-17% pre-tax), across cardiovascular (fastest-growing at +16%), surgery, vision, and orthopaedics.

The competitive differentiation is breadth. Few peers span high-margin biologics/oncology and medical devices at this scale, which smooths the patent-cliff cyclicality that pure pharma players experience. JNJ is deliberately reshaping toward higher-growth areas: it has divested Kenvue, announced an Orthopaedics separation (October 2025, targeted 18-24 months), and bought growth through Intra-Cellular ($14.5B), Shockwave ($12.6B), Abiomed, and Halda ($3.05B). Roughly 25% of FY2025 sales came from products launched in the past five years, evidence the R&D engine is replenishing the portfolio.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is accelerating.** FY2023 $85.2B → FY2024 $88.8B (+4.3%) → FY2025 $94.2B (+6.0%) → Q1 2026 $24.06B (+9.9% reported). U.S. grew 8.3% and International 11.9% in Q1. This is genuine acceleration, not an easy comp.

**GAAP earnings are distorted by talc accounting and should not be taken at face value.** FY2025 net income of $26.8B (28.5% net margin) was boosted by the ~$7.0B talc reserve reversal recorded in Q1 2025; the prior-year period was depressed by a ~$5B charge. The reported $11.03 trailing EPS therefore overstates the underlying earnings power. Consensus current-year EPS of $11.57 and next-year of $12.72 are the better anchors, and on those figures the stock trades at ~20.8x and ~18.9x respectively.

**Cash generation is strong and steady.** FY2025 operating cash flow was ~$24.5B with FCF ~$19.7B. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was a soft $2.5B (down from $4.2B), but this reflects a $3.9B working-capital drain that includes a $0.8B Auris/Fortis litigation payment made in January 2026, a timing item rather than deterioration.

**Leverage has roughly doubled in two years to fund M&A.** Total debt is ~$55.0B (Q1 2026) versus ~$36.6B two years ago; net debt is ~$32.9B versus ~$13.5B a year earlier. This is manageable for an AAA-rated firm at a 3.56% weighted-average cost of non-current debt, and well covered by ~$20B annual FCF, but the pace bears monitoring as buybacks also accelerated (Q1 2026 buybacks of $4.0B vs $2.1B a year earlier). Goodwill (~$48.6B) plus intangibles (~$49.1B) exceed equity (~$81.2B), typical for acquisitive pharma but a reminder that $8.3B of the Intra-Cellular price was IPR&D contingent on clinical/commercial success.

The Altman Z-Score reading near 1.6 ("distress zone") is a model artifact. It is calibrated for industrial/manufacturing firms and penalizes JNJ's leverage and asset base; it does not capture an AAA-rated firm with $20B+ FCF and a 63-year dividend-growth record. Disregard it as a solvency signal.

**Earnings track record supports confidence in consensus.** Over the last four quarters JNJ delivered three beats and one in-line print (Q1 2026 +0.3%, Q3 2025 +0.5%, Q2 2025 +2.3%, Q4 2025 -0.1%). The beats are small, consistent with a mature large-cap that guides tightly, and argue for trusting current estimates rather than expecting upside surprise.

## Growth Analysis

Estimate revisions are positive but modest, not the +30% inflection velocity that signals a trough recovery. Next-quarter consensus rose 1.0% over 90 days, next-year rose 1.4%, and current-year is roughly flat (+0.3%). This is a steady high-single-digit grower, not a re-rating-from-trough story. The estimate-revision data does not flag the strong BUY pattern that 30%+ revisions plus 30%+ revenue growth would.

The qualitative growth story is the most encouraging it has been in years:

- **Oncology +22.8%** in Q1, led by DARZALEX ($3.96B, +22.5%), CARVYKTI ($597M, +62%), and RYBREVANT/LAZCLUZE (+83%). This is a deep, self-renewing franchise.
- **The STELARA-to-TREMFYA handoff is working organically.** TREMFYA at $1.61B (+68%) is cushioning STELARA's ~60% decline within the same immunology franchise. Out-growing your own patent cliff inside one therapeutic area is rare and underappreciated.
- **Neuroscience +32%**, with CAPLYTA (from the Intra-Cellular deal) contributing $270M and SPRAVATO +46%.
- **MedTech Cardiovascular +13%**, with Shockwave +18.5% and Abiomed +16%.

The offsetting drag is the patent-cliff cadence. STELARA's decline was a ~5.4% drag on total operational growth. SIMPONI biosimilars are expected H1 2026 (Europe) / H2 2026 (U.S.), and OPSUMIT generic competition is expected H2 2026 in the U.S. XARELTO and IMBRUVICA are also declining. The pharma model is a perpetual race, and JNJ is winning it for now, but the next cliffs are visible. The Orthopaedics separation should be accretive to growth and margins by shedding the slowest MedTech franchise (~+3% operational), at the cost of execution and dis-synergy risk.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the case for restraint sits. At $240.35, JNJ trades at ~27.6x trailing EPS and ~20.8x current-year consensus. Against the pharmaceutical peer median P/E of 19.9, the trailing multiple represents a ~39% premium. The peer set is dispersed: MRK at 33.8 and LLY at 40.2 (the GLP-1 hypergrowth name) sit above; PFE at 19.9, BMY at 16.0, and ZTS at 13.0 sit below. JNJ's quality, diversification, and AAA balance sheet justify a premium to PFE/BMY, but the question is whether the current premium leaves asymmetric upside.

The honest read is that it does not, at this price. On forward earnings the stock is ~20.8x a business growing high single digits operationally, which is a fair-to-full multiple rather than a cheap one. The dividend yield of ~2.26% is below the peer median of 2.8%, so the income support is also modest relative to the group. The analyst consensus mean target of $252.87 and median of $260 imply ~5-8% upside to fair value, with a high of $285 and a low of $155, a wide band that reflects genuine disagreement driven by the talc tail.

The trailing P/E is also flattered downward by the talc-reversal-inflated EPS denominator. Stripping the accounting noise and using the ~$11.57 current-year operating estimate, the stock is not screening cheap on any metric. There is no specific mispricing to point to: the market has largely re-rated the stock as the talc/patent-cliff discount unwound through the 52% run from the low. The prior multi-year discount (JNJ's 5-year cumulative TSR of $151.55 lagged the peer group's $188.57) has been partially recovered, which is the bull thesis playing out, not a forward opportunity.

Note that the "analyst targets are floors" principle does not apply here. JNJ is not a cheap inflection stock with a sub-30x multiple and rising estimates that consensus is chasing; it is a quality large-cap that has already re-rated to a full multiple, where targets and price reflect the same recovered optimism.

## Competitive Landscape

JNJ's competitive position is stable-to-improving, justifying a 7.0. The breadth advantage is structural: spanning high-margin biologics/oncology and devices at scale is rare and smooths cyclicality. The R&D engine is replenishing (25% of sales from products launched in the past five years), and the strategic reshaping (divest Kenvue and soon Orthopaedics, acquire oncology/neuro/cardiovascular growth) is coherent.

Trajectory has softened modestly versus a year ago, which is why the score is below the 8.5 we previously assigned. STELARA's biosimilar erosion is the poster child of the broader pressure (mirroring AbbVie's Humira experience), MedTech faces tariff-driven margin pressure (segment pre-tax margin fell to 14.3% in Q1 2026 from 17.7%) plus competitive pressure in pulsed field ablation and endocutters, and the Biosense Webster electrophysiology antitrust loss now carries a permanent injunction on appeal. The company is gaining share in oncology and cardiovascular devices and ceding it in legacy immunology and orthopaedics — a net-stable picture trending positive in the higher-margin segments.

## Risk Assessment

**Talc litigation is the defining idiosyncratic risk and the reason the Risk score is 5.0.** Approximately 75,000 plaintiffs as of Q1 2026, after three failed Chapter 11 ("Texas two-step") attempts (the third, Red River, dismissed early 2025) pushed the matter back into the tort system. The remaining reserve is only ~$3.4B (present value), covering executed settlements and defense costs, not a global resolution. Outcomes are binary and unpredictable: in 2026 JNJ won a three-case LA ovarian verdict but lost a $32M LA mesothelioma verdict and a $40M December 2025 bellwether. The NJ MDL Daubert (general-causation) rulings expected through 2026 are pivotal and could shift litigation valuation materially in either direction. Separately, the talc securities class action (alleging asbestos non-disclosure) was certified, affirmed by the 3rd Circuit, and the Supreme Court denied cert in April 2026, so that case proceeds. The balance sheet does not reflect a comprehensive talc settlement, leaving an open-ended tail.

**Other material risks:**
- **Patent cliffs:** STELARA (already eroding), SIMPONI biosimilars (2026), OPSUMIT generics (H2 2026). Erosion is visible and quantified.
- **IRA Medicare price negotiation:** Janssen's constitutional challenge lost at the 3rd Circuit; Supreme Court review sought December 2025. CMS has published maximum fair prices, a structural ongoing pricing pressure.
- **MedTech tariff margin pressure:** explicitly cited in filings as the cause of the segment margin decline.
- **M&A integration and goodwill risk:** ~$17.5B of 2025 deals with $8.3B of IPR&D contingent on success.

This is a Health Care name with a genuine binary liability, not a generic "risky industry" discount. The market prices some of this, but the open reserve gap means a single adverse MDL ruling or a large global-settlement number could move the stock materially.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing modest, event-specific stress rather than a fundamental break. Near-term ATM IV is 28.7% versus 16.9% one-year historical vol, a rich 11.7-point spread, and the term structure is in backwardation (28.7% near-term vs 22.2% six-month), which typically reflects hedging into a known near-dated catalyst rather than durable concern. Put/call skew is roughly balanced at 2.9% near-term and the put/call volume ratio is a low 0.37, with the P/C OI ratio having fallen from 0.93 to 0.69 as the stock rose 7%. Six-month skew is mildly negative at -2.9%, consistent with longer-dated talc tail-hedging. Short interest is negligible at 1.1% of float (3.1 days to cover). Net read: positioning is not bearish, but the rich near-term IV signals the market expects discrete volatility, plausibly tied to talc docket movement. This supports the view that the binary litigation overhang, not fundamentals, is the swing factor.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($285):** Revenue growth holds in the high single digits operationally as oncology (DARZALEX, CARVYKTI, RYBREVANT) and neuroscience (CAPLYTA, SPRAVATO) compound above 20% and TREMFYA fully offsets STELARA. The NJ MDL Daubert rulings break in JNJ's favor, compressing the talc tail and removing the discount. The Orthopaedics separation closes accretively. Forward EPS reaches ~$13+ and the market awards a ~22x multiple, supporting ~$285, the Street high. Two dividend increases in 12 months and ~$6B+ annual buybacks add total return.

**Bear Case ($155):** An adverse general-causation ruling or a multi-billion-dollar global talc settlement forces a reserve build well above $3.4B, and the litigation valuation discount widens. SIMPONI and OPSUMIT erosion plus IRA price cuts pressure pharma growth back toward low single digits, while MedTech margins stay compressed by tariffs. The multiple compresses toward the PFE/BMY 16-20x range on a de-rated earnings base. This is the analyst low of $155, roughly 36% below current.

The base case sits in between: high-single-digit operational growth, talc remaining an unresolved overhang without a catastrophic ruling, and the multiple holding near current levels. That produces a stock that grinds with earnings rather than re-rates further.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The 52% run has converted JNJ from a discounted laggard into a fairly-valued quality compounder. We hold it for the dividend and the operating momentum, but the entry point for fresh capital is not attractive given the full multiple and the open talc tail.

**Rebuttal to a BUY at this price (specific and falsifiable):** Two concrete factors prevent the upgrade despite the strong operating quarter. First, valuation: at ~20.8x current-year consensus EPS of $11.57, the stock trades at a ~39% trailing-P/E premium to the pharma peer median of 19.9 with a below-median 2.26% yield, and the consensus mean target of $252.87 implies only ~5% upside, far short of the ≥15% asymmetry a BUY requires. Second, an unpriced binary risk: ~75,000 talc plaintiffs sit against a ~$3.4B reserve that explicitly does not cover a global resolution, and the NJ MDL Daubert general-causation rulings expected through 2026 are a dated, binary event with material downside (the bear case is ~36% below current). The combination of <15% upside to consensus fair value and a quantified, near-dated litigation tail that could force a reserve build well above $3.4B is the concrete, falsifiable basis for holding rather than buying.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. A favorable NJ MDL Daubert/general-causation ruling, or a defined global talc settlement at a number the market can absorb, removing the open-ended tail.
2. A pullback toward ~$200-210 (~17x forward EPS), restoring ≥15% upside to fair value.
3. Sustained operational growth above 7% with TREMFYA/oncology continuing to outpace SIMPONI/OPSUMIT erosion through H2 2026.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. An adverse general-causation ruling or a global talc settlement requiring a reserve build materially above $3.4B (e.g., a double-digit-billion figure).
2. Operational growth decelerating below ~3% as the 2026 patent cliffs (SIMPONI, OPSUMIT) bite without offset.
3. A dividend-coverage deterioration or a debt-funded acquisition that pushes net debt materially higher without a clear FCF accretion path.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (mid-July 2026 for Q2) for growth durability and guidance; ongoing NJ MDL Daubert docket through 2026 for the talc swing factor.

## Conclusion

Johnson & Johnson is doing the hard operating work well. Revenue is accelerating, the immunology patent cliff is being absorbed organically through TREMFYA, oncology and neuroscience are compounding above 20%, and the capital-allocation record (63 straight dividend increases, two raises in 12 months) is unimpeachable. The score has drifted from 7.2 to 6.7 over the prior three reports, and our 6.4 confirms that gradual decline, driven not by fundamental deterioration but by the multiple having re-rated 52% off the low into a fair-to-full range while the talc tail remains unresolved.

We hold rather than buy because the price no longer offers asymmetry. At ~20.8x forward earnings and a ~39% premium to the peer median, with only ~5% upside to consensus fair value and an open-ended ~75,000-plaintiff talc liability provisioned at just ~$3.4B, the risk-adjusted setup favors patience. We would become buyers on a pullback toward $200-210 or on a definitive talc resolution. Existing holders should retain the position for total return; the dividend and pipeline justify ownership, but this is not the moment to add.
