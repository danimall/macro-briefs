## Executive Summary

Arch Capital Group is a $34.6 billion market cap Bermuda-domiciled specialty insurer operating across three segments: insurance, reinsurance, and mortgage insurance. The company generated $4.4 billion in net income on $19.9 billion of revenue in FY2025, with a GAAP ROE of 20.1% and free cash flow of $6.13 billion. Book value per share grew 22.6% YoY to $65.11, and management returned $1.9 billion via share repurchases.

At ~7.9x forward earnings and ~1.4x book value, ACGL is cheaper than its business quality warrants. The primary overhang is the new Bermuda 15% corporate income tax (effective January 2025), which raised the effective tax rate from 7.7% to 14.7%. This is a real structural headwind, but the market appears to have overpenalized the stock relative to the magnitude of impact. The reinsurance segment's 80.8% combined ratio, the mortgage segment's 14.6% combined ratio, and Q1 2026 net income of $1.05 billion (nearly doubling YoY) indicate core earnings power remains intact.

The primary risk is property catastrophe rate softening (down 10-20% at January 2026 renewals), which will compress reinsurance margins over the next 12-18 months. This is partially offset by Arch's demonstrated cycle discipline — the reinsurance segment already non-renewed unprofitable treaties in 2025 and can redeploy capital toward insurance lines via the MCE acquisition platform. At current prices, the stock offers approximately 18-19% upside to the $109 consensus target with a 0.37 beta providing portfolio-level risk reduction.

## Company Overview

Arch Capital operates three segments with distinct earnings characteristics:

**Insurance (47% of net premiums written):** $7.8 billion in net premiums written, up 13.4% YoY, driven primarily by the August 2024 MCE Acquisition (Allianz's U.S. MidCorp and Entertainment businesses). Combined ratio of 95.2%. This segment writes specialty casualty, construction, E&S, workers' compensation, commercial auto, and property across the U.S., U.K./Lloyd's, Europe, Canada, and Australia.

**Reinsurance (46% of NPW):** $7.6 billion in NPW, down 1.7% YoY as management deliberately non-renewed specialty treaties with inadequate pricing. Combined ratio of 80.8%, generating $1.56 billion in underwriting income (+27.5% YoY). The reinsurance segment is Arch's earnings engine and benefits from global diversification across Bermuda, U.S., Ireland, and Lloyd's platforms.

**Mortgage (6% of NPW):** $1.06 billion in NPW, down 4.7% YoY as rising mortgage rates constrain origination. Combined ratio of 14.6%, generating its fourth consecutive year of $1.0+ billion in underwriting income. The U.S. primary portfolio has $286 billion of insurance in force with a weighted average FICO of 749.

The company holds strategic equity stakes in Greysbridge/Somers (30%), Coface (29.5%), and Premia (25%), which contributed $180 million in operating affiliate income and $504 million in equity method investment income in 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $9.6B (2022) → $13.6B (2023) → $17.4B (2024) → $19.9B (2025). Revenue has roughly doubled in three years, driven by both organic growth and the MCE acquisition. The growth rate decelerated from 42% (2023) to 28% (2024) to 14% (2025), reflecting normalization after rapid scaling rather than deterioration.

FCF of $6.13 billion against net income of $4.4 billion implies a FCF/NI conversion rate well above 100%. Capex is negligible at $44 million. Operating cash flow of $6.17 billion funds the entire capital return program with room to spare.

**Capital structure is conservative.** Total debt of $2.73 billion in senior notes represents 10.1% of total capital, well below industry norms for a company of this scale. No revolving credit borrowings outstanding. Credit facilities of $925 million provide ample liquidity. The investment portfolio totals $47.4 billion with AA-/Aa3 average credit quality and 3.34-year duration on fixed income. Net investment income of $1.625 billion grew 8.7% YoY, providing a stable income floor.

**BVPS compounding is the anchor metric.** Book value per share grew from ~$35 (2022) to $65.11 (2025), a ~23% annualized rate. Share count declined from 376.4 million to 359.0 million through $1.9 billion in repurchases during 2025, with $7.8 billion returned cumulatively since the program's inception.

**Reserve position:** Net reserves of $24.5 billion with favorable prior-year development of $600 million across all three segments in 2025. IBNR of $16.7 billion (concentrated in long-tail casualty/specialty) creates estimation uncertainty, but the consistent pattern of favorable development suggests conservative reserving.

**Tax headwind quantified:** The Bermuda CIT raised income taxes from a net benefit of $35 million (2024) to $174 million (2025). On $4.4 billion of net income, this represents roughly a $200+ million annual drag. CEO Papadopoulo publicly acknowledged Bermuda "lost one of the advantages," though substance-based tax credits (Credits Act 2025) partially offset the impact. The effective tax rate of 14.7% is still below many onshore competitors, preserving some relative advantage.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is supported by several identifiable drivers:

1. **MCE integration.** The Allianz MidCorp/Entertainment acquisition contributed meaningfully to insurance segment premium growth and still has integration costs rolling off ($75 million in transaction costs in 2025, likely declining in 2026). As the platform matures, loss ratios should improve toward Arch's long-term target.

2. **Investment income expansion.** The $47.4 billion portfolio at current yields provides structural income growth as lower-yielding legacy bonds roll off and are replaced at higher market rates. At a 3.34-year duration, roughly 30% of the portfolio turns over annually.

3. **Q1 2026 momentum.** Net income of $1.05 billion ($2.88/share) nearly doubled from Q1 2025's $564 million. If sustained, this implies full-year EPS power in the $11-12 range or potentially higher.

4. **Capital deployment optionality.** With $6+ billion in annual FCF and only $1.1 billion remaining on the current buyback authorization, the company has capacity for continued repurchases, accretive acquisitions, or opportunistic capital deployment if pricing dislocates.

**Growth headwinds:** Property catastrophe rate softening of 10-20% at January 2026 renewals will compress reinsurance margins. The reinsurance segment's combined ratio has room to absorb some deterioration, but sustained competitive pressure could push it toward 85-88% over 2-3 years. Mortgage premiums are declining (-4.7%) as persistency erodes (81.8% vs. 82.1% prior year) and high mortgage rates constrain origination volumes. A recession scenario would stress mortgage delinquencies — Arch's own disaster scenario models a $931 million loss from severe housing stress.

## Valuation Assessment

At $91.92, ACGL trades at:
- **~7.9x forward P/E** — a substantial discount to the specialty insurance peer group, which typically trades at 10-14x
- **~1.41x book value** ($65.11 BVPS) — reasonable for a 20%+ ROE franchise; the P/B would need to rise to ~1.7x to reach the consensus target
- **~0.37 beta** — providing meaningful portfolio-level risk reduction

The consensus price target of $109.11 (median $110, range $95-$125) implies approximately 18.7% upside. The stock trades 11% below its 52-week high of $103.39, with the discount attributable to the Bermuda CIT and reinsurance cycle concerns.

**Why the market may be wrong:** The 7.9x multiple assumes the Bermuda tax structurally impairs the business. But at a 14.7% effective tax rate, Arch still has a tax advantage relative to U.S.-domiciled peers paying 21%+. The reinsurance segment's 80.8% combined ratio provides ~14 points of margin buffer before the segment reaches mediocre levels (95% combined). The mortgage segment's 14.6% combined ratio is nearly unprecedented in insurance and should be valued at a premium, yet it is buried within a blended multiple. A sum-of-the-parts analysis credibly supports $105-115: the reinsurance segment alone, at 10x its $1.56 billion underwriting income, would be worth $15.6 billion (roughly 45% of current market cap).

## Competitive Landscape

**Cycle discipline as competitive advantage.** The reinsurance segment's deliberate premium contraction (-1.7%) while competitors chase volume demonstrates the underwriting culture that has compounded BVPS at 23% annually. The ability to flex capital across three distinct segments — shifting toward insurance when reinsurance softens, or toward mortgage when housing fundamentals improve — is a structural advantage few peers replicate.

**MCE acquisition extends the moat.** Adding Allianz's MidCorp and Entertainment businesses gave Arch access to U.S. middle-market commercial insurance and a specialized entertainment niche. These are relationship-intensive, renewal-driven businesses that should improve premium retention and reduce the cyclicality of the insurance segment over time.

**Scale and capital advantages.** At $26.9 billion of total capital and $47.4 billion of investable assets, Arch can write large limits and participate in complex transactions that smaller specialty carriers cannot. The three rated platforms (Bermuda, U.S., Lloyd's) provide distribution flexibility. The A+ financial strength ratings support continued capacity growth.

**Competitive risks are real but manageable.** Property catastrophe reinsurance is the most competitive segment, with rates down 10-20%. Arch's response — non-renewal and capital redeployment — is the textbook response of a well-managed specialty carrier. The more concerning competitive dynamic is in casualty reinsurance, where long-tail reserves create information asymmetry and social inflation continues to produce adverse development industry-wide. Arch's track record of favorable development suggests its reserving is more conservative than peers, but this advantage is inherently backward-looking.

## Risk Assessment

### Bermuda CIT — Structural but Partially Priced
The 700bps effective tax rate increase is a permanent structural change. However, at 14.7%, Arch still pays less tax than most U.S. peers (21% statutory rate). The substance-based Credits Act 2025 provides partial offset, and Arch's Bermuda operations employ hundreds of actuaries and underwriters, maximizing credit eligibility.

### Catastrophe Exposure
Peak zone exposure of $1.9 billion (Florida Tri-County windstorm) is material against $26.9 billion of capital. The 2025 California wildfires contributed to catastrophe losses in the reinsurance segment but were absorbed within the full-year combined ratio. A major hurricane season in 2026 could produce $1-2 billion in losses, compressing quarterly earnings significantly.

### Reserve Adequacy
The 90th percentile reserve simulation of $29.0 billion vs. $24.5 billion carried reserves implies a potential $4.5 billion adverse scenario (~$12/share pre-tax). Social inflation in long-tail casualty lines is a persistent industry-wide concern. Consistent favorable development provides comfort, but past reserving performance does not guarantee future accuracy.

### GSE Reform
The mortgage segment depends on Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac requiring private mortgage insurance. Any legislative or regulatory change to GSE charters, MI requirements, or capital standards could impair this high-margin business. The January 2025 Treasury-FHFA preferred stock agreement keeps privatization discussions alive.

### Options Market Signal
No detailed options data was available for ACGL. The 0.37 beta implies low correlation with broader market moves, consistent with insurance sector earnings being driven by underwriting cycles and catastrophe events rather than macro variables. The P&C insurance sector broadly benefits from the current elevated-rate, elevated-uncertainty environment — higher investment yields and harder commercial insurance pricing both support near-term earnings.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($110-125)
- Q1 2026 earnings power annualizes to ~$11.50+ EPS, which at 10x forward P/E (a modest re-rating from 7.9x) implies $115+
- MCE integration completes, insurance segment combined ratio improves from 95.2% toward 92-93%, adding $200-300M in incremental underwriting income
- Buybacks at current pace (5%+ of market cap annually) mechanically accrete BVPS by 3-4% beyond organic growth
- Investment income continues growing as legacy bonds roll off into higher yields
- Reinsurance pricing stabilizes or re-hardens following a major catastrophe event

### Bear Case ($75-82)
- Property catastrophe cycle softens further, pushing reinsurance combined ratio above 90%
- Major hurricane season produces $2B+ in losses, creating a year of negligible earnings
- Mortgage delinquencies spike during recession, requiring $500M+ in reserve strengthening
- OECD Pillar II implementation further increases effective tax rate beyond 15%
- Reserve adequacy proves insufficient as social inflation accelerates in long-tail casualty
- Stock trades down to 1.15-1.25x BVPS, reflecting lower ROE expectations

## Conclusion

Arch Capital is a high-quality specialty insurer trading at a meaningful discount to intrinsic value. The ~7.9x forward P/E prices in the Bermuda CIT headwind and reinsurance cycle softening, but undervalues the diversified earnings power across three segments, the 20%+ ROE, and $6+ billion in annual free cash flow. The Q1 2026 near-doubling of net income suggests the core franchise is accelerating despite the tax headwind.

This has been rated BUY for three consecutive assessments, with scores trending between 7.9 and 8.1. The current analysis finds no reason to change the recommendation. Financial health and competitive positioning remain at or near prior peaks. The slight Management Quality decline in the prior report (8.5 → 8.0) reflected the CEO transition and large insider sale; with the CEO transition now formalized and Papadopoulo holding ~$84 million in shares, alignment concerns are diminishing but insider purchase confirmation would strengthen the score.

The key monitoring point is the reinsurance pricing cycle. If January 2027 renewals show further double-digit rate declines, the reinsurance segment's combined ratio could approach 90%, reducing the margin buffer that underpins the thesis.

BUY at current levels with a $110 base case target over 12-18 months.
