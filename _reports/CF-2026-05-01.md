## Executive Summary

CF Industries is the world's largest ammonia producer, operating nine manufacturing complexes across North America and the U.K. with approximately 40% of North American ammonia capacity. The company delivered strong FY2025 results — revenue up 19% to $7.1B, FCF of $1.8B, and diluted EPS of $8.97 — driven by geopolitical supply disruptions that elevated global nitrogen prices. The stock has appreciated 58% over the past year, from ~$78 to $121, largely reflecting the Iran conflict's impact on global nitrogen supply chains.

The core analytical question is whether $121 represents fair compensation for the risks embedded in the thesis. Consensus says yes: 15 of 21 covering analysts rate CF a Hold, with a mean price target of $120.42. FY2026 consensus EPS of $15.28 reflects extraordinary pricing conditions, but FY2027E already drops 29% to $10.78, flagging the cyclical peak. Henry Hub natural gas nearly doubled from the FY2025 average ($3.53/MMBtu) to $6.32/MMBtu in early 2026, creating a material unquantified margin headwind. The Yazoo City production outage removes capacity through at least Q4 2026. The entire pricing thesis rests on sustained Hormuz disruption — a binary risk that a diplomatic resolution would unwind rapidly, as the ~10% single-day decline on April cease-fire headlines demonstrated.

The combination of consensus-fair valuation, cyclical peak earnings, rising input costs, and binary geopolitical dependency warrants a HOLD. There is genuine upside if disruptions persist and deepen, but the risk/reward at current levels does not favor new capital deployment.

## Company Overview

CF Industries converts natural gas into ammonia and upgraded nitrogen products (granular urea, UAN, ammonium nitrate, DEF) through the Haber-Bosch process. The company's competitive position is anchored by two structural advantages: (1) access to low-cost North American natural gas relative to European and Asian marginal producers, and (2) unmatched scale, with the Donaldsonville, Louisiana facility representing the world's largest ammonia production complex at 4.3M tons/year gross capacity.

The product mix in FY2025 generated $7.1B in revenue: ammonia ($2.18B, 31.3% gross margin), UAN ($2.16B, 42.6%), granular urea ($1.78B, 47.0%), ammonium nitrate ($421M, 18.8%), and other products ($545M, 37.6%). CHS Inc. is the largest customer at ~13% of net sales and holds an ~11% minority interest in the CFN subsidiary.

CF is pursuing a strategic transformation toward low-carbon ammonia through carbon capture and sequestration (CCS). The Donaldsonville CCS project became operational in July 2025, capturing up to 2M metric tons of CO₂ annually and earning ~$42M in 45Q tax credits in H2 2025. The Blue Point JV ($3.7B total project cost; CF's 40% share ~$1.5B) with JERA and Mitsui reached final investment decision in April 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Margins.** FY2025 revenue of $7.1B rose 19% YoY, driven entirely by a 19% increase in average selling price ($372/ton vs. $313/ton in FY2024). Volume was effectively flat at 19.1M tons. Gross margin expanded ~390 bps to 38.5%. Operating margin reached 32.5%, and net margin 25.4%. These figures remain well below the 2022 peak ($11.2B revenue, 52.4% gross margin) but represent a significant recovery from the 2024 trough.

**Cash Flow.** Operating cash flow of $2.75B (up from $2.27B) converted to $1.80B of free cash flow after $950M of capex — a 25.4% FCF margin. OCF exceeds net income, confirming high earnings quality.

**Capital Allocation.** CF repurchased $1.37B of shares in FY2025, reducing diluted shares outstanding by 10% YoY to 162.2M (from 204M in 2022 — a 20.6% cumulative reduction). This is the primary reason EPS grew 33% while net income grew 19%. The $2.00/share annual dividend ($326M total) yields approximately 1.65%. A new $2B buyback authorization extends through 2029. In total, CF returned approximately $1.70B to shareholders through buybacks and dividends in FY2025.

**Balance Sheet.** Total long-term debt of $3.25B (all unsecured senior notes, maturities 2034-2044 after the November 2025 refinancing). Net debt of approximately $1.27B ($3.25B gross minus $1.98B cash). Interest coverage is excellent at ~15x ($2.3B operating income / $155M interest expense). The $750M revolving credit facility is fully available and undrawn. The Altman Z-Score of 1.91 appears concerning in isolation but is an artifact of the deliberately shrunk equity base from buybacks; with $1.98B cash, no maturities until 2034, and $1.8B annual FCF, near-term financial distress probability is negligible.

**The Gas Cost Headwind.** Natural gas constitutes approximately 34% of CF's total production costs. Henry Hub averaged $3.53/MMBtu in FY2025, already up 57% from $2.25 in FY2024. The 10-K discloses that Henry Hub averaged $6.32/MMBtu from January 1 to February 20, 2026. At ~34% cost share against a ~$5.4B cost base, doubling gas costs implies roughly $900M-$1B of additional annualized cost pressure. Even if offset partially by higher product pricing (historically, global nitrogen prices rise when gas costs rise since marginal producers face even steeper cost increases), this headwind would compress margins by an estimated 5-8 percentage points if sustained through FY2026 without full pass-through. The FY2026 consensus EPS of $15.28 presumably incorporates some gas cost assumption, but the magnitude of this headwind deserves explicit investor attention.

**Yazoo City Production Impact.** A November 2025 incident idled the entire Yazoo City complex (ammonia, UAN, AN production). Production is not expected to resume until Q4 2026 at the earliest. Management guided 2026 gross ammonia production at ~9.5M tons, down from 10.1M in 2025 — a ~6% production headwind. The incident also triggered $76M in asset impairment charges.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-Term Earnings Trajectory.** Current-quarter EPS of $2.70 has been revised up 33.2% over 90 days. Full-year FY2026E of $15.28 has been revised up 110.6% over 90 days, reflecting the sharp rise in global nitrogen prices driven by Middle East supply disruptions. FY2027E of $10.78 implies a 29% earnings decline — analysts already anticipate that current pricing conditions are cyclically elevated.

Explosive near-term earnings growth followed by sharp normalization is characteristic of commodity producers at cyclical peaks. CF's revenue fell 47% from the 2022 peak to the 2024 trough. The current strength is driven by geopolitical supply disruptions (Iran, Egypt, Russia production outages), not by structural demand growth. Nitrogen demand grows at low-single-digit rates tied to population growth, biofuel mandates, and agricultural productivity.

**Low-Carbon Ammonia — Long-Term Optionality.** The Blue Point JV and Donaldsonville CCS investments position CF for emerging demand in decarbonized agriculture, marine fuel, and power generation. JERA and Mitsui's certification as suppliers of low-carbon hydrogen by Japan's METI provides early demand validation. The POET partnership demonstrates agricultural demand development. However, CF's own 10-K acknowledges this market "could take several years to materialize and then ten or more years to fully develop." The $3.7B Blue Point project carries execution risk (new ATR technology at scale, permitting uncertainty for Class VI injection wells in Louisiana, CO₂ sequestration dependency on third-party infrastructure, tariff exposure on imported materials). This optionality is meaningful but not yet investable on a 12-month horizon.

**Share Count Reduction.** With $2B of remaining buyback authorization and ~$1.8B annual FCF, CF can continue reducing shares outstanding by 5-8% annually. This is a genuine, repeatable source of EPS growth independent of commodity pricing, but it cannot offset a 29% decline in underlying earnings.

## Valuation Assessment

CF trades at 13.8x trailing EPS ($8.97) and approximately 7.9x FY2026E EPS ($15.28). The 7.9x forward multiple appears cheap against the peer median P/E of 29.8x in Materials. However, the comparison is misleading: CF is a pure commodity producer with highly cyclical earnings, while the peer set (PKG, DD, RS, SW, IFF) includes diversified specialty chemical, packaging, and distribution businesses with more stable earnings profiles. A sub-10x forward P/E on peak-cycle earnings is normal for commodity producers.

The more relevant valuation framework applies normalized mid-cycle earnings. If FY2027E of $10.78 represents a partial normalization, CF trades at 11.2x — reasonable but not obviously cheap. If a more complete normalization returns EPS closer to the $6.74 realized in FY2024, the stock trades at 18x — above what a commodity producer typically commands.

**Consensus Target Reconciliation.** The mean analyst price target is $120.42, with a median of $125.00. The stock is at consensus fair value. The target range ($86-$150) reflects the wide distribution of outcomes: $86 corresponds to a normalization scenario, $150 to sustained elevated pricing.

**DCF Sensitivity.** At a 10% discount rate with $1.8B normalized FCF (approximately the FY2025 level), a terminal growth rate of 2% (commodity businesses don't grow above GDP long-term) yields an enterprise value of approximately $22.5B, or roughly $130-135/share after subtracting net debt and minority interests. This is consistent with the upper end of the consensus range and suggests modest upside only if current FCF levels are sustainable — which the FY2027 estimates question.

## Competitive Landscape

CF's position as the largest North American nitrogen producer with ~40% of ammonia capacity provides genuine structural advantages. The cost advantage from North American natural gas relative to European marginal producers (who set global prices) persists, though two factors could erode it over time: expanding U.S. LNG export capacity, which would narrow the Henry Hub-to-TTF differential, and new low-carbon ammonia projects globally that could add supply.

The competitive moat is real but narrow. Nitrogen production is a commodity business with limited differentiation beyond cost position and logistics. CF's Donaldsonville CCS project and Blue Point JV provide differentiation optionality in low-carbon ammonia, where first-mover status and scale could matter if regulatory frameworks (EU CBAM, 45Q credits) create durable pricing premiums for decarbonized production.

Near-term competitive dynamics favor CF: EU anti-dumping duties on Russian UAN extend through 2031, Chinese nitrogen export restrictions remain in place, and Middle East supply disruptions have removed marginal capacity. These are positive for pricing but are policy/geopolitical factors, not competitive attributes — they can reverse.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risk: Binary Geopolitical Dependency.** A sustained diplomatic resolution would likely return nitrogen prices toward pre-conflict levels, compressing EPS toward the $6-8 range and the stock toward $85-95. The probability of near-term diplomatic resolution appears low based on current conditions, but the risk is non-diversifiable and binary.

**Natural Gas Cost Squeeze.** Every $1/MMBtu increase in gas costs adds approximately $260-300M to annual production costs, or roughly $1.60-1.85 per diluted share. While global nitrogen prices have risen more than gas costs (preserving the North American spread advantage), the sensitivity is meaningful at current Henry Hub levels.

**Yazoo City Extended Outage.** The ~6% production reduction at current pricing implies approximately $220-250M of revenue at risk, or $1.00-1.40/share of EPS impact depending on product mix margins.

**Blue Point Execution Risk.** CF's $1.5B equity commitment plus $550M in scalable infrastructure investment concentrates meaningful capital in a project with technology, permitting, tariff, and demand risks. The Louisiana moratorium on Class VI injection wells adds regulatory uncertainty.

**Cyclical Peak Risk.** Commodity producers at cyclical peaks consistently look cheap on forward multiples. The 29% expected EPS decline from FY2026 to FY2027, and the historical precedent of 47% revenue decline from 2022 to 2024, illustrate how quickly the cycle can turn.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 160.1% dwarfs the 1-year historical volatility of 39.5% — a 120.6pp spread that ranks at the 91st percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in sharp backwardation (160.1% at 1-week, 61.3% at 1-month, 48.9% at 6-month), indicating the market is pricing a specific near-term binary event. The 1-week put/call volume ratio of 1.03 is balanced, but the 1-month ratio of 1.69 is put-heavy, suggesting institutional hedging activity in the May expiration cycle. The implied move of ±3.4% on the nearest expiry and ±9.8% at 1-month confirms meaningful near-term downside risk pricing.

For existing holders, this elevated IV creates attractive covered call or collar opportunities. For prospective buyers, the premium required to hedge a new position is extreme, making entry timing hazardous.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($140-150, +16-24%)
Sustained Middle East supply disruptions keep global nitrogen prices elevated through 2026 and into 2027. Higher selling prices more than offset the natural gas cost increase, as CF's North American cost advantage widens when global gas prices rise even faster. FY2026 EPS reaches or exceeds the $15.28 consensus, and FY2027 estimates are revised upward as supply constraints persist. The Blue Point JV progresses on schedule, validating low-carbon ammonia demand. Continued buybacks at $1.5B+/year reduce share count by another 8-10%. The stock re-rates from 7.9x toward 10x forward earnings.

### Bear Case ($85-95, -22-30%)
Diplomatic resolution of the Iran conflict normalizes Hormuz transit, sending global nitrogen prices back toward pre-conflict levels. FY2026 EPS prints closer to $10-11 as pricing normalizes and gas costs remain elevated. The Yazoo City outage extends beyond Q4 2026. Blue Point JV encounters cost overruns or permitting delays. The stock retraces to 12-13x normalized mid-cycle earnings of $7-8, implying $84-104.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. **Valuation compression to $105-110** (10x FY2027E EPS), where risk/reward improves materially even in the normalization scenario.
2. **FY2027 consensus EPS revised above $13** (from current $10.78), indicating sustained supply tightness beyond the current cycle.
3. **Henry Hub returns below $4/MMBtu** while nitrogen product prices remain elevated, restoring margin expansion.

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. **Confirmed diplomatic resolution** producing physical supply normalization through Hormuz — this would likely compress the stock 20-30% within weeks.
2. **FY2026 quarterly EPS prints below $3.00** for two consecutive quarters, signaling pricing normalization is accelerating.
3. **Henry Hub sustained above $7/MMBtu** while global nitrogen prices plateau — this would squeeze the North American cost advantage.

### Reassessment Timeline
Next quarterly earnings (expected early May 2026 for Q1 FY2026). The Q1 report will be the first to fully capture the elevated natural gas environment and current nitrogen pricing, providing critical data on whether margins are expanding or compressing. Any significant diplomatic development regarding Iran/Hormuz warrants immediate re-evaluation.

## Conclusion

CF Industries is a well-managed nitrogen producer with genuine structural advantages: North American cost position, dominant scale, strong capital allocation, and emerging low-carbon optionality. The financial profile is healthy: $1.8B FCF, ~15x interest coverage, no maturities until 2034, and aggressive buybacks reducing the share count.

At $121, the stock sits at the consensus mean target and prices in near-peak cyclical earnings. The FY2026/FY2027 EPS trajectory ($15.28 to $10.78) and the 2022-2024 precedent illustrate cyclical peak dynamics. Elevated gas costs and the Yazoo City outage create additional near-term headwinds, and the bullish thesis depends substantially on sustained geopolitical supply disruption — binary event risk that the options market is pricing at extreme IV levels.

For existing holders, maintain the position while monitoring the upgrade/downgrade triggers above. For new capital, the risk/reward at $121 does not offer sufficient asymmetry. A pullback to $105-110 (or clearer evidence of sustained pricing power via FY2027 estimate revisions) would warrant upgrading to BUY. HOLD.
