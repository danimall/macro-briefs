## Executive Summary

Valero Energy is experiencing an earnings inflection driven by the Iran/Strait of Hormuz disruption, which has widened refining crack spreads and propelled Q1 2026 EPS to $4.22 — a 32% beat vs. consensus estimates of $3.19. Current-quarter EPS has been revised upward 133.5% over 90 days, and full-year 2026 estimates now stand at $27.78 (up from $12.37 ninety days ago). Four consecutive quarterly beats average 24% surprise magnitude.

The stock has appreciated 92% over the past year, from ~$129 to $247, reflecting this improved earnings power. At approximately 9x the current run-rate ($27.78 FY2026E), the valuation appears reasonable on forward numbers. However, a specific risk prevents a BUY recommendation: LNG tankers have been observed physically transiting the Strait of Hormuz for the first time since the conflict began, with Reuters reporting a deal framework "largely negotiated." If a peace resolution materializes (25-30% probability within 60 days), Valero's crack spreads could compress 30-40% within one quarter, reducing annualized EPS from $27.78 toward $18-20 and the stock toward $180-200.

The risk is not generic cyclicality. There is a specific, observable, timed catalyst that could trigger margin compression — physical ships transiting the chokepoint that drives Valero's current elevated earnings. A 72-hour confirmation window is active; if confirmed by additional transit activity, immediate position reduction would be warranted.

## Company Overview

Valero Energy is the world's largest independent petroleum refiner, operating 13 active refineries (down from 15 following the Benicia closure) with combined throughput capacity of approximately 3.0 million barrels per day. The company operates through three segments:

- **Refining** (~90% of operating income in normal environments): 13 refineries across the U.S. Gulf Coast, Mid-Continent, West Coast, North Atlantic, and U.K.
- **Renewable Diesel** (via 50/50 DGD joint venture with Darling Ingredients): 1.2 billion gallons/year capacity
- **Ethanol**: 12 plants with 1.7 billion gallons/year capacity

The business model is pure downstream manufacturing and wholesale — no upstream production exposure and no company-owned retail stations. Valero benefits directly from the crude-to-product spread (crack spread) rather than crude oil prices themselves. Lower crude oil is generally favorable, while supply disruptions that tighten refined product markets are the primary earnings driver.

## Competitive Landscape

Valero's competitive position is strong and improving:

1. **Scale advantage**: 3.0M BPD throughput provides purchasing, logistics, and maintenance scale
2. **Complexity premium**: Heavy sour crude processing capability (Gulf Coast refineries at Nelson Complexity Index 12+) generates 20-40% higher margins than simple refiners when crude differentials are wide
3. **No upstream drag**: Unlike integrated majors, Valero's earnings improve when crude prices fall (widening consumer margins on transportation fuels)
4. **California exit is strategically intelligent**: Removes 305K BPD from the most hostile regulatory environment while tightening West Coast supply for remaining operators
5. **Gulf Coast export positioning**: As Latin American refined product demand grows, Valero's waterborne logistics from Gulf Coast refineries provide a structural export advantage

Competitive threats include potential new refinery capacity in the Middle East (Kuwait Al Zour, Nigeria Dangote) that could displace some export volumes, and long-term demand erosion from electrification. However, global refining has been in a structural capacity squeeze since 2020 (significant closures, minimal new builds in developed markets), and the industry is unlikely to over-build given ESG constraints on new projects.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Hormuz De-Escalation (SPECIFIC, TIMED, BINARY):**
If 3+ additional tankers transit by May 28, the probability of a peace resolution rises to 40-50%. A full Hormuz reopening would compress refining margins within one quarter (pass-through lag of 2-3 months for crude differentials, immediate for crack spread futures). VLO's FY2026 EPS would likely be revised downward by 30-40%, and the stock would de-rate toward $180-200 (13-14x on normalized earnings), representing 20-27% downside from current levels.

**Probability: 25-30% within 60 days. This is the specific risk preventing BUY.**

**2. Crack Spread Cyclicality:**
Even absent geopolitical resolution, refining margins are mean-reverting. The 2022-2023 cycle demonstrated margins can collapse 70% in 12-18 months. Current margins are elevated relative to any historical average except the 2022 supercycle peak.

**3. Renewable Diesel Structural Impairment:**
The DGD segment swung from +$507M operating profit to -$156M loss in one year (FY2024 to FY2025) before recovering to $139M in Q1 2026. The regulatory environment (tariffs, credit changes, OBBB restrictions) creates ongoing uncertainty for this segment's long-term profitability.

**4. Rate Environment:**
30-year Treasury yields above 5.1% compress all equity valuations via discount rate mechanics. If rate hikes materialize (45-50% probability by year-end), cyclical equities face multiple compression even before earnings deteriorate.

## Conclusion

Valero Energy is a well-managed, conservatively financed, best-in-class refiner experiencing a genuine earnings inflection. The estimate revision velocity, four consecutive earnings beats, aggressive capital returns, and strategic California exit all argue for ownership. Under normal circumstances — a demand-driven inflection with diversified revenue catalysts — this would be a clear BUY.

The specific, falsifiable reason it is not: physical LNG tankers are transiting the Strait of Hormuz for the first time since the conflict began, with a deal framework reported as "largely negotiated" by Reuters. This creates a 25-30% probability of the primary earnings catalyst reversing within 60 days, with 20-27% downside in that scenario. The asymmetric risk profile (4-5% upside to consensus target vs. 20-27% downside on deal) at a stock price already above analyst consensus targets creates unfavorable expected value for new positions.

The HOLD recommendation carries a growth orientation: if the de-escalation signal fails (50-55% probability), the stock resumes its uptrend toward $280-300. Position holders should maintain exposure with a stop-loss framework tied to sustained Hormuz commercial traffic. New capital should await either (a) geopolitical clarification, or (b) a pullback to $200-210 that prices in some margin normalization.
