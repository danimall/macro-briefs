**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ball Corporation has completed a strategic transformation into a pure-play aluminum-packaging company following the February 2024 divestiture of its aerospace business. What remains is the world's largest aluminum beverage-can manufacturer, operating consolidated regional oligopolies (4-5 players per region) with aluminum-price pass-through, multi-year supply contracts, and a stated long-term algorithm of 10%+ comparable diluted EPS growth. The Q1 2026 print supports that algorithm: net sales rose 16% YoY to $3.60B, net earnings +15% to $205M, and diluted EPS reached $0.77 versus $0.63 (+22%), with full-year guidance reaffirmed. EMEA is the standout, with comparable operating earnings rising from $354M (2023) to $495M (2025).

The investment tension is that the durable earnings engine is real but the shares offer little clear mispricing at the current $62.23. Ball trades at ~15.6x current-year consensus EPS ($4.00) and ~18x trailing, below the peer median of 19.9x and below the highest-quality comps on quality-adjusted metrics, but not at a discount wide enough to represent asymmetry. Estimate revisions are flat (+1.0% current-year over 90 days, next-quarter slightly negative), so the +30% signal that would force a cycle-adjusted re-rating is absent. The story is a mid-single-digit-to-low-double-digit compounder priced approximately fairly.

Three overhangs cap the risk/reward: a sharp cut in 2026 buyback guidance from ~$1.3B to ~$600M as management pivots capital toward M&A (Benepack) and leverage management with debt now at $7.86B; a new CEO (Ron Lewis) installed amid a dense cluster of late-2025 officer changes against a backdrop of TSR that fell to 60 versus a peer-group 100; and a one-time non-cash ~$463M UK pension settlement charge landing in 2H 2026 that will distort GAAP earnings. We rate BALL HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: hold for the recurring 10%+ EPS algorithm, but there is no independent catalyst that justifies chasing at present levels.

## Company Overview

Ball Corporation, founded 1880 and headquartered in Westminster, Colorado, employs approximately 16,000 people and is the largest aluminum beverage can producer globally. Post-transformation, the business comprises three geographic beverage-packaging segments plus a small Personal & Home Care (PHC) unit:

- **Beverage Packaging, North & Central America** — 48% of 2025 sales ($6.29B); ~50B cans shipped, ~36% regional share; largest producer in a five-player oligopoly.
- **Beverage Packaging, EMEA** — 30% of sales ($3.98B); ~38B cans, ~39% share (four-player market).
- **Beverage Packaging, South America** — 16% of sales ($2.16B); ~20B cans, ~46% share; highest-margin segment (~15%).
- **Personal & Home Care** — under 5% of sales; aerosols, reclosable bottles, aluminum slugs.

The core thesis rests on the secular "substrate shift to aluminum" (sustainability-driven substitution away from PET plastic and glass) combined with high customer retention and aluminum-price pass-through provisions covering the majority of volumes. Primary competitors are Crown Holdings and Ardagh. The February 2024 aerospace divestiture booked a ~$3.6B gain, inflating 2024 GAAP net income to $4.0B; all prior aerospace results are now discontinued operations, which is why headline revenue and income comparisons are noisy.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Continuing-operations revenue grew to $13.2B in 2025 (+11.6%), driven by +$713M volume, +$579M price/mix (mostly higher aluminum pass-through), and +$177M FX. Because aluminum is largely passed through to customers, reported revenue growth overstates underlying economic growth; margins and EPS are the better gauges. Q1 2026 sales continue that pattern, again partly a pass-through and mix effect.

**Profitability.** 2025 net earnings from continuing operations were $912M (7% net margin), with stable segment operating margins: NCA ~12%, EMEA ~12%, SA ~15%. EMEA comparable operating earnings improved to $416M (2024) from the 2023 level, the clearest sign the streamlined model is executing. Economic Value Added rose roughly 6x from $63.7M (2022) to $378M (2025), a genuine underlying improvement the market may be discounting.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow was $1.26B in 2025, up sharply from a depressed $115M in 2024, which absorbed ~$766M of aerospace-sale cash taxes (a $104M refund arrived January 2026). Capex was ~$474M in 2025, guided to ~$600M in 2026. Free cash flow comfortably funds the ~$210M annual dividend with substantial residual capacity.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** This is the central tension. Total debt rose from $5.69B (2024) to $7.01B (2025) to $7.86B in Q1 2026, funding buybacks and the Benepack acquisition. The equity base is deliberately thin ($5.4B; D/E 2.6x) because Ball aggressively returns capital. The Altman Z-Score of 1.33 nominally places Ball in the "distress zone," but that is a mechanical artifact of the shrunken equity base, not a solvency warning: the leverage covenant maximum is 4.5x, Ball was in compliance with ~$1.9-2.7B of additional borrowing capacity, senior facilities were refinanced in November 2025 out to November 2030 with limited near-term maturities, and interest coverage is solid (interest expense $314M against EBIT above $1.1B). ROE of 16.9% is flattered by the buyback-shrunken equity base.

**Capital allocation.** Ball has been a buyback machine: diluted shares fell from 320M (2022) to 276M (2025) to 267M in Q1 2026, roughly a 16% reduction in three years, with buybacks of $1.71B (2024) and $1.32B (2025). A new $4.0B authorization was approved January 2025 ($2.93B remaining). The shift is that 2026 buyback guidance was cut to ~$600M from $1.3B+, and Q1 2026 buybacks were essentially nil (~$20K). Management is redirecting cash toward M&A and leverage management. This removes a meaningful support for per-share EPS growth in the near term, which matters because roughly half of the historical EPS algorithm came from share-count reduction.

**Earnings track record.** Two beats, one in-line, and one miss over the past four quarters, with the most recent (Q1 2026) beating by 8.8%. This is a company that delivers close to consensus, not one that structurally sandbags: appropriate confidence in current estimates but not the beat-and-raise profile that supports multiple expansion.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth thesis is the 10%+ comparable diluted EPS algorithm, reaffirmed at the Q1 2026 print, built from mid-single-digit volume growth in the aluminum substrate shift, stable-to-improving margins, and (historically) buybacks. Volume recovery in 2025 and Q1 2026 confirms that the 2022-2024 North American over-capacity, which forced plant closures (Kent, WA in 2024), is normalizing. EMEA is the operating bright spot, and South America carries the highest margins.

The material qualifier is that estimate revisions are flat. Current-year consensus sits at $4.00 (up just 1.0% over 90 days), next quarter is slightly negative (-0.4% over 30 days), and next year at $4.52 is +0.5% over 90 days. This is stable, not accelerating. The +30% revision velocity that would signal a genuine earnings inflection and force cycle-adjusted re-rating is not present. With the buyback engine throttled in 2026, near-term EPS growth leans more heavily on operating execution and organic volume than in prior years, a shift that raises the bar for delivering the 10%+ algorithm and reduces the visibility of the per-share compounding that drove the historical thesis.

Growth durability is supported by the breadth of the substrate-shift tailwind (recyclability, tightening packaging regulation, ~75% global aluminum recycling rate) and Ball's #1 global position, but this is a secular grind, not a step-change. We weight growth as directionally positive but thin; the revision data does not corroborate acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At the current price, Ball trades at ~13.8x next-year EPS and ~18x trailing. The peer median in Metal, Glass & Plastic Containers is 19.9x (ATR 19.9x, SLGN 15.1x, GEF 21.5x, OI at negative earnings). On forward earnings Ball is modestly below the peer median, and its ~13.8x next-year multiple is reasonable for a low-double-digit EPS grower, but it is not a wide-enough discount to constitute asymmetric upside, and the peer set itself trades at unremarkable multiples that reflect the sector's cyclical, capital-intensive, low-margin economics.

Cycle-adjusted framing does not rescue the valuation upward: this is not a trough-earnings inflection (revisions are flat, revenue growth is largely pass-through, margins are stable rather than expanding sharply), so trailing P/E is a fair rather than misleading anchor. The valuation dimension in the current regime has been reliably anti-predictive: cheap-looking multiples in this sector frequently mark value traps rather than opportunities, and Ball's below-median forward P/E co-exists with weak relative TSR, a throttled buyback, and rising leverage. Absent a specific mispricing catalyst, we score valuation at 5.0.

Analyst targets bracket the case: low $60.00 (essentially at spot), median $72.00, high $77.00, mean $71.14. The mean implies ~14% upside, but the sentiment trend is flagged as degrading and the target distribution is wide, which we read as a fairly-valued-with-modest-upside setup rather than a clear buy. The ~1.3% dividend yield provides thin support and sits below the 2.1% peer median.

## Competitive Landscape

Ball's competitive position is a genuine strength but the trajectory has softened. The company holds #1 or leading positions across consolidated regional oligopolies (NCA ~36%, EMEA ~39%, SA ~46%), which support pricing discipline, high plant utilization, and long-term contracts with aluminum pass-through. The moat is real: capital intensity, scale economics, customer stickiness, and the sustainability tailwind favoring aluminum over PET and glass.

The competitive-position score has drifted down from 7.5 to 6.5 across prior reports, and we take it to 6.0. The reasons: the substrate-shift thesis, while intact, faced a demand hangover and North American over-capacity through 2024; the sector's structural attractiveness has not translated into shareholder outperformance (TSR 60 vs peer 100); and the level of dominance matters less than trajectory, which is stable rather than improving. Portfolio reshaping (Benepack, Florida Can, Alucan, Saudi JV sale, cups deconsolidation) is sensible bolt-on activity that deepens EMEA capacity but does not widen the moat. Competition from PET and glass remains the primary substitution threat, and deposit/packaging legislation is double-edged.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and capital-return dependency.** The model relies on stable cash flows to service debt while returning capital. A volume shock or margin compression would pressure it. Covenant headroom is adequate today, but the pivot from buybacks toward debt-funded M&A is a live capital-allocation question.

**Customer and supplier concentration.** Sales concentrate among large multinational beverage/CPG customers under long-term contracts; aluminum is sourced from few suppliers. Loss of a major customer is a stated material risk.

**Substitution risk.** A narrow product range: the majority of sales are beverage cans. A reversal of the aluminum substrate shift would be highly damaging.

**Aluminum pass-through timing and FX.** Non-pass-through volumes and timing lags create short-term margin volatility. Roughly 53% of sales are outside the US, with euro, Brazil, Argentina, Egypt, and Turkey exposure carrying currency and political/tax risk (ongoing Brazil tax assessments).

**Resolved and pending items.** The September 2025 US Customs tariff-classification challenge, flagged as an unreserved "reasonably possible" loss in the 10-K, was concluded with no liability per the Q1 2026 10-Q. Pending: the UK pension buy-out in 2H 2026 will crystallize AOCI losses into GAAP expense — economically neutral and de-risking, but it will optically dent reported earnings and could confuse headline numbers.

**Leadership transition and weak relative TSR.** The new CEO is installed amid a cluster of officer changes, against materially underperforming TSR. Execution under new leadership is the central show-me question.

We score risk factors at 5.5 — a balanced profile. The Customs resolution and the non-cash, pre-signaled nature of the pension charge reduce binary surprise risk, but leverage plus the buyback pivot plus an untested management team keep this from being a clean high score.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 31.9% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 26.6% (a 5.3-point spread) and sits at the 66th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (near 31.9% vs long-term 25.7%), signaling the market is pricing imminent event stress, most plausibly the next earnings print and the anticipated 2H 2026 pension charge. Put/call skew is balanced (-1.4%) and the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.47, so there is no directional bearish tilt. IV rose 6.4 points since May as the stock climbed 14.7%, and the P/C OI ratio fell from 1.29 to 0.78, consistent with reduced hedging demand into the rally. The implied ±7.3% move for the nearest expiry argues against paying up for entry immediately ahead of the print; a post-event IV crush would offer a cleaner accumulation window.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$77 / +24%).** The 10%+ comparable EPS algorithm holds, EMEA margin momentum continues, South America stays the high-margin engine, and the substrate shift drives durable mid-single-digit volume growth. The new management team re-establishes credibility, the buyback pivot proves temporary as leverage normalizes, and consensus re-rates the pure-play toward the higher end of the peer multiple. On ~$4.52 next-year EPS at ~17x, this supports the high analyst target.

**Bear case (~$50 / -20%).** North American volume recovery stalls, aluminum pass-through timing crimps near-term margins, the debt-funded M&A pivot dilutes returns, and the throttled buyback removes the per-share EPS support that historically drove the algorithm. Weak relative TSR persists under an unproven CEO, the pension charge muddies GAAP optics, and the shares de-rate toward the low end of the peer range on ~$4.00 EPS at ~12.5x.

**Base case (~$68 / +9%).** The company delivers close to its 10%+ EPS algorithm on operating execution with muted buyback support, EMEA improvement offsets NCA normalization, and the multiple holds near the current ~14x next-year level. This lands modestly above spot, roughly at the analyst mean minus the degrading-sentiment discount.
