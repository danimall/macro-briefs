## Executive Summary

AMG is a differentiated asset management holding company that owns minority equity interests in ~20+ independent investment firms managing a combined $882B in AUM. The stock trades at approximately 8.5x forward Economic EPS ($34.83 for FY2026), a significant discount to peers despite structural transformation: liquid alternatives AUM has grown 86% in 15 months (to $261.5B), Q1 2026 Economic EPS surged 58% YoY, and the company generated record net inflows of $22.5B (7% annualized organic growth rate). The share count has declined 30% since 2022 through buybacks, and the $514.6M convertible settlement in January 2026 permanently removed dilution overhang.

The market continues to price AMG as a traditional active equity manager in secular decline. Alternatives now represent 46% of AUM and a disproportionate share of economics through higher fee rates and performance fees. With a 22-year weighted average debt maturity, 0.9x bank leverage ratio, and no maturities until 2030, AMG carries minimal balance sheet risk. The primary risks are equity mutual fund outflows (ongoing but diminishing in significance), performance fee volatility, and the Orso Partners short thesis targeting tax-aware long-short/tax-loss-harvesting strategies and AMG's backing of AQR Capital Management. At current valuation, the stock offers meaningful upside based on its earnings trajectory and alternatives-driven transformation.

## Company Overview

AMG operates a unique business model in asset management. Rather than managing money directly, it acquires minority equity interests in independent boutique investment firms ("Affiliates"), providing them succession planning, growth capital, and distribution access while preserving their investment autonomy. This creates a diversified portfolio of investment strategies spanning private markets ($148B), liquid alternatives ($261.5B), differentiated equities ($297.8B), and multi-asset/fixed income ($174.7B).

The company generates revenue through two channels: consolidated revenue from controlled Affiliates (~$545M/quarter) and equity method income from minority-owned Affiliates (~$147M/quarter in net equity income). The latter captures the fastest-growing businesses — Verition, Garda Capital Partners, and other liquid alt/private markets managers. Total aggregate fees across all Affiliates reached $6.17B in 2025 (+18% YoY), of which consolidated GAAP revenue captures only ~34%.

Recent Affiliate partnerships include BBH Credit Partners (structured credit, January 2026), HighBrook Investors (real estate, February 2026), and an expanded position in Garda Capital Partners (fixed income relative value, February 2026).

## Growth Analysis

AMG's growth profile has accelerated materially over the past 18 months, driven by three distinct engines:

**1. Organic Inflows (+7% annualized):** Four consecutive quarters of strong net inflows totaling ~$52B. Q1 2026 alone generated $22.5B in net new assets. The organic growth rate is well above peers in traditional asset management and reflects the alternatives-heavy mix.

**2. New Affiliate Partnerships:** AMG invested $776M in new and existing Affiliates during 2025, adding strategies in structured credit, real estate, energy transition, and expanded liquid alternatives capacity. These investments compound over time as Affiliate AUM grows.

**3. Mix Shift Economics:** Liquid alternatives strategies charge higher base fees (typically 1.5-2.0% vs. 0.4-0.7% for equities) and earn performance fees (approximately 27% of total AUM is eligible for performance-based fees). As alternatives grow from 46% to 50%+ of AUM, blended fee rates rise mechanically.

Estimate revisions confirm this trajectory: current-year EPS estimates have risen 14.0% over 90 days (from $30.56 to $34.83), and next-year estimates are up 11.0% (to $39.24). While below the +30% threshold for the strongest buy signal, the consistent upward revisions across all periods signal sustained earnings momentum.

Equity AUM remains in outflow ($45.3B net outflows in FY2025, $9.1B in Q1 2026). This segment is declining in relative importance (equities are now 34% of AUM, down from ~50% two years ago), though outflows remain substantial. AMG exited certain subscale positions in 2025 (Peppertree, Comvest's private credit business, Montrusco Bolton), but these were not exclusively equity-AUM dispositions, and meaningful equity outflows persist.

At $297.82, AMG trades at:
- 8.5x FY2026 Economic EPS ($34.83)

- ~6.2x market cap / annualized Q1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA run-rate (note: on a standard EV/EBITDA basis, including ~$2.5B net debt, the multiple is approximately 8.2x annualized Q1 Adjusted EBITDA)
- 12.0x trailing GAAP P/E (distorted by one-time Affiliate transaction gains)

**Peer comparison:**
- Peer median P/E: 15.7x (SEIC at 15.7x, VCTR at 19.6x, JHG at 10.2x, FHI at 10.6x)
- AMG at 8.5x forward Economic EPS trades at a 46% discount to the peer median

The discount is partially justified by AMG's complexity (equity method accounting, Affiliate model opacity) and the ongoing equity outflows. However, the magnitude of the discount fails to reflect:
1. AMG's 7% organic growth rate vs. flat-to-negative for most peers
2. The alternatives mix shift driving fee rate expansion
3. The buyback program compounding per-share economics (at least $400M guided for 2026, potentially higher based on Q1 run-rate)
4. A debt maturity profile superior to any peer

Sell-side price targets vary considerably, with published targets ranging from approximately $332 (Argus) to $479 (Bank of America) and $495 (TD Cowen). Given the acceleration in alternatives-driven economics and the buyback yield, the stock should trade at 10-11x forward Economic EPS ($348-$383) within 12 months, even without expansion toward the peer median multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

AMG occupies a structural niche with limited direct competition. The closest analog is the "GP stakes" business at Blue Owl (OWL), Goldman's Petershill, and Dyal Capital (which is part of Blue Owl) — but these compete primarily in private equity/alternatives, while AMG spans the full spectrum of active management.

**Improving competitive dynamics:**
- The succession planning pipeline remains robust as founding managers at independent firms age. AMG's value proposition (liquidity + independence) is differentiated from private equity acquirers who typically want operational control.
- AMG's U.S. wealth platform, launched to bring Affiliate strategies to retail/HNW investors through evergreen and interval funds, opens a distribution channel unavailable to most Affiliates independently.
- The 7% organic growth rate demonstrates that Affiliate investment performance is generating real client demand.

**Deteriorating dynamics:**
- Equity AUM continues bleeding to passive strategies, with only 41% of equity products beating benchmarks on a 3-year basis (compared to 92% outperformance for liquid alternatives over the same period).
- Competition for alternative manager acquisitions has intensified as more capital chases minority stakes in high-quality GPs.

Net assessment: AMG's competitive position is stable-to-improving as the mix shift reduces dependence on the declining equity segment.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Equity Outflow Structural Decline (Medium):** Net outflows of $45.3B in equities during 2025 and $9.1B in Q1 2026. While diminishing in significance as equities fall to 34% of AUM, further acceleration would offset alternatives inflows.

**2. Performance Fee Volatility (Medium):** Q1 2026 benefited from strong performance fees at liquid alternative Affiliates. A market dislocation that hits relative-value or trend-following strategies could sharply reduce near-term earnings.

**3. Intangible Impairment Risk (Medium):** $135M impaired in 2025 on mutual fund client relationships, followed by an additional $43M aggregate impairment ($30.5M attributable to controlling interest) in Q1 2026, again tied to mutual fund AUM and revenue declines. Sensitivity analysis shows a 200bps revenue decline could add ~$80M. While this primarily reflects already-declining legacy mutual fund businesses rather than impairment across the alternatives portfolio, the recurring nature of these charges indicates an active and ongoing risk rather than a one-time event.

**4. Key Person Risk (Structural):** AMG's value depends on principals at Affiliates continuing to manage assets. Departures can trigger fund suspensions. This risk is mitigated by AMG's demonstrated ability to execute multiple Affiliate investments and dispositions in recent years, though new deal activity does not eliminate key-person risk at existing Affiliates.

**5. Orso Partners Short Position (Medium):** Disclosed April 2026 via Bloomberg, targeting tax-aware long-short/tax-loss-harvesting strategies and AMG's backing of AQR Capital Management. This is potentially relevant to the liquid alternatives growth narrative, particularly if regulatory or structural issues emerge around tax-aware strategies. The stock declined only 1.5% on the announcement, and Director Cates' subsequent $459K open-market purchase (May 6, 2026) suggests board-level confidence.

**6. Management Transition:** Thomas M. Wojcik stepped down as President and COO effective March 6, 2026. While AMG's decentralized model reduces dependence on any single corporate executive, the departure warrants monitoring for continuity of Affiliate partnership development and strategic execution.

**7. Macro Sensitivity:** Rising rates and stagflationary conditions pressure equity AUM through market declines and may slow alternatives fundraising as institutional allocation budgets tighten. However, liquid alternatives (Garda, Verition) tend to perform well during volatility, providing a natural hedge.

## Conclusion

AMG at $297.82 presents a compelling risk/reward with 24-44% upside against 16% downside. GAAP accounting obscures the true economics of the equity-method Affiliates, which represent the fastest-growing portion of the business. The market assigns a legacy active equity manager multiple to a company that is rapidly becoming an alternatives platform.

The valuation at 8.5x forward Economic EPS against 14.0% upward estimate revisions (90-day), confirmed insider buying post-short-seller disclosure, and the balance sheet profile provides concrete evidence of mispricing. The specific near-term catalyst is continued demonstration of alternatives flow momentum in Q2 2026 earnings, which should further compress the gap between AMG's multiple and its peer group.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The EV/EBITDA multiple is ~8.2x (not 6.2x) when properly calculated on an enterprise-value basis; the "12% FCF yield" is an operating-cash-flow yield before substantial recurring Affiliate investment outflows; and the Orso short thesis specifically targets tax-aware/AQR strategies central to the alternatives growth narrative — these corrections moderate but do not eliminate the valuation discount thesis.
