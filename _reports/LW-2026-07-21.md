**Research Date: July 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Lamb Weston is the number-one producer of value-added frozen potato products in North America, selling french fries and related items to quick-service restaurants, foodservice distributors, and retailers in more than 100 countries. The stock trades at $46.54, roughly 31% below its 52-week high of $67.07 and off a December 2025 gap-down that took shares from the high-$50s to the low-$40s. The core story is a two-year earnings reset: net income fell from $1.01B (FY2023) to $725M (FY2024) to $357M (FY2025), gross margin compressed from 27.3% to 21.7% over that span, and Q3 FY2026 gross margin fell further to 21.2%. The company is deliberately investing in price and trade to win back volume in an oversupplied frozen-potato market with soft restaurant traffic, which means the margin compression is partly strategic rather than a pure demand collapse.

The setup is an activist-driven turnaround in mid-flight. JANA Partners holds a board-level position and roughly 5.4M shares, adding aggressively (~$16M in April 2026 near $41–43). Starboard Value has pressed publicly for an investor day and a credible profit-growth path. A new Executive Chair (Jan Craps) was installed with a large equity package and bought $2.43M of stock in the open market at $48.65; a new CFO (James Gray) bought ~$809K at $40–44; two independent directors also bought. This clustered open-market buying near multi-year lows is the single most bullish signal in the file. Against it sits a balance sheet carrying roughly $4.0B of debt against $1.83B of equity (D/E ~4.25x), an International segment whose EBITDA fell 80% year-over-year in Q3 FY2026, recurring excess-potato write-offs, and two material lawsuits — an antitrust price-fixing class action seeking treble damages and a securities class action tied to the ERP-implementation and pricing disclosures.

We rate LW a HOLD. The recovery optionality is genuine: North America volume grew 12% in Q3, U.S. QSR traffic turned positive for the first time since late FY2024, capex is rolling off a multi-year peak (which should lift free cash flow), and a cost program targeting ≥$250M of annualized savings by FY2028 is running ahead of plan. But none of that is yet visible in the earnings line, leverage limits flexibility, and the litigation exposure is unquantified. The turnaround thesis and the balance-sheet/litigation risk are close enough to a standoff that we lack the evidence for a stronger call in either direction.

## Company Overview

Lamb Weston processes potatoes into value-added frozen products, principally french fries, and operates in a tight North American oligopoly alongside McCain, Simplot, and Aviko. The business reports two segments:

- **North America** (~66% of sales): FY2025 net sales $4.27B and Segment Adjusted EBITDA of $1.10B (~26% margin). This is the profit engine and is currently performing — volume up 12% in Q3 FY2026.
- **International** (~34% of sales): FY2025 net sales $2.19B but only $253.7M EBITDA (~12% margin), and deteriorating fast. Q3 FY2026 International EBITDA fell 80% to $18.5M.

The economics are those of a capital-intensive commodity processor: modest structural margins, a concentrated contracted-grower supply base, a handful of large chain customers with buying power, and no U.S. potato futures market to hedge input volatility. The company also holds a 50% JV (Lamb Weston/RDO Frozen) and owns brands including Grown in Idaho and Alexia.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue plateaued after a JV-consolidation-driven surge.** Sales grew from $4.10B (FY2022) to $6.47B (FY2024), lifted heavily by the FY2023 buy-in of the LW EMEA JV and by pricing, then flattened to $6.45B (FY2025). FY2026 year-to-date (39 weeks) net sales rose 1% to $4.84B, but were flat-to-down 1% at constant currency. The volume-price mix is the key dynamic: FY2026 YTD price/mix fell ~7% while volume rose 6%. Management is buying back share at the expense of unit economics.

**Profitability fell sharply.** Net income dropped 51% year-over-year to $357M in FY2025. Q3 FY2026 net income was $54.0M (EPS $0.39) versus $146.0M ($1.03) a year prior. Adjusted EBITDA declined from $1.42B (FY2024) to $1.22B (FY2025), with FY2026 YTD down 11% to $859.5M. The gross-margin trajectory (27.3% → 21.7% → 21.2% at Q3 FY2026) is the cleanest measure of the compression.

**Leverage is the central balance-sheet concern.** Roughly $4.0B of total debt sits against $1.83B of equity, producing D/E of about 4.25x and an Altman Z-Score of 1.73 (distress zone). For an asset-heavy staples processor the Z-score overstates true bankruptcy risk, and the mitigants are real: covenant compliance at February 2026, $1.26B of revolver availability, a laddered maturity schedule (senior notes 2028/2030/2032; term loans 2029–2031), and ~$2.95B of fixed-rate debt that insulates the company from further rate moves. The mechanism to watch is that further EBITDA erosion compresses coverage ratios toward covenant thresholds, which would force a choice between the dividend, the buyback, and debt paydown.

**Cash flow is holding up and about to improve.** Operating cash flow was $868M in FY2025 and $595.6M in FY2026 YTD (up $110M year-over-year on working-capital improvement). FY2025 FCF was only $230M because the company was in the tail of a major capex cycle (China, U.S., Netherlands, Argentina). Capex is now dropping from ~$638M (FY2025) toward ~$500M guided for FY2026, with only $256M spent in the first 39 weeks. As the build-out completes, the falling capex should convert into materially higher free cash flow, the most tangible lever for de-levering.

**Capital allocation is mixed.** The company pays a growing dividend (~$0.38/quarter, ~$207M/year, currently ~3.25% yield) and repurchased $294M of stock in FY2025 with ~$264M remaining on a $750M authorization as of March 2026. The buybacks were executed at ~$58 (FY2025) and ~$62 (FY2026 YTD), well above the current $46.54, so capital was returned above intrinsic value, which is a fair criticism given the leverage.

**Earnings quality: consistent recent beats.** The company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters (Q3 FY2026 +14.1%, prior quarters +3.0%, +29.9%, +33.3%). The beats came against a depressed and repeatedly reset estimate base, so they signal that management is guiding conservatively into a difficult stretch rather than that the fundamentals are inflecting upward.

## Growth Analysis

There are two ways to read the numbers, and both are partly true. Reported growth is stalled: revenue flat at constant currency, EBITDA down 11% YTD, EPS roughly halved from peak. But the underlying volume trend is positive, with U.S. QSR traffic turning positive (+1%) for the first time since late FY2024. The category demand backdrop shows early stabilization, with chicken QSR strong and burger softer.

The forward growth thesis rests on three internal levers rather than on end-market acceleration:

1. **Cost program.** The "Focus to Win" plan targets ≥$250M of annualized run-rate savings by FY2028 (~$200M by FY2027) plus ~$120M of working-capital improvement, and management now expects to exceed the $250M target. If those savings land while pricing stabilizes, the operating leverage in a fixed-cost processing business could drive a substantial earnings recovery.

2. **Footprint rationalization.** The FY2025 restructuring closed a U.S. plant and curtailed lines; FY2026 closed the Munro, Argentina plant (consolidating into the modern Mar del Plata facility) and curtailed a Netherlands line. These actions reduce excess capacity that has been driving the write-offs and underutilization.

3. **Capex roll-off.** Completing the expansion cycle frees cash for debt paydown and dividend coverage.

Consensus EPS estimates are essentially flat-to-marginally-rising: current-year $2.82 (unchanged over 90 days), next-year $3.01 (+0.4%). This is not the >30% revision velocity that flags a genuine turn; it is a stabilizing estimate base at a depressed level. The absence of accelerating positive revisions is the reason we do not treat the volume recovery as a durable growth signal yet. The swing factor is International: North America progress is being masked by an 80% International EBITDA collapse, and the escalating Middle East conflict is expected to have a more meaningful impact on Q4 FY2026 International results.

## Valuation Assessment

At $46.54 the stock trades at roughly 16.5x current-year consensus EPS of $2.82 and ~15.5x next-year $3.01. On the profile-reported basis, the trailing/forward P/E is ~21.8x on $2.50 EPS. Against packaged-foods peers (median P/E 11.2x, per-comparison group CPB 11.2x, PPC 8.0x, POST 15.7x, CALM 5.5x, CAG negative), LW carries a premium multiple despite its leverage and margin pressure. The dividend yield of ~3.25% sits above the peer median of 1.8%.

The valuation case is genuinely two-sided. On depressed FY2025–FY2026 earnings, the multiple looks full relative to peers, which is the value-trap reading: cheap-looking staples names with structural pressure tend to stay cheap-looking. On normalized earnings power, the picture changes: if the cost program delivers ~$250M+ and pricing stabilizes, EBITDA could recover toward the $1.4B FY2024 level, and mid-cycle EPS well above $3.00 becomes plausible, which would put the stock at a low-teens multiple on recovered earnings. The consensus mean target of $47.58 and median of $46.00 sit essentially at spot, with a range of $40 to $65. That range captures the standoff: the high target reflects the turnaround scenario, the low target reflects the balance-sheet-and-litigation downside.

The cheapness here does not qualify as an asymmetric buy signal because it depends on an unproven margin recovery and is not supported by rising estimate revisions or a dated catalyst. The multiple is not stretched enough to be a clear sell, either. That balance is a 5.0.

## Competitive Landscape

Lamb Weston holds the number-one North American position in a tight oligopoly (LW, McCain, Simplot, Aviko), and scale in a capital-intensive processing business is a durable advantage. The competitive trajectory, however, has weakened. The category moved from balanced supply-demand into a 2024–2026 glut as producers, including LW, added capacity into softening restaurant traffic. That triggered industry-wide price competition, which is the direct cause of the margin compression and, ironically, the backdrop to an antitrust suit alleging the opposite conduct (price-fixing in 2021–2023).

Within that, North America is holding share and gaining volume, while International expansion was poorly timed and is currently value-destructive: the 80% EBITDA collapse and recurring potato write-offs are evidence of demand-forecasting misses against contracted grower supply. The net trajectory is a strong domestic franchise partially offset by a mismanaged international footprint that the company is now rationalizing. Level is high; trajectory is stable-to-modestly-negative pending evidence that pricing has bottomed and International has been fixed.

## Risk Assessment

**Structural oversupply and price competition** is the central thesis risk. New capacity met soft traffic, forcing sustained price and trade investment. This is an industry dynamic, not purely company-specific, and it will not reverse until capacity rationalizes across producers.

**Leverage** amplifies everything. At ~$4B debt and D/E >4x with a distress-zone Z-score, any further EBITDA erosion tightens the coverage ratios and constrains capital-return flexibility. Covenant compliance and credit ratings warrant monitoring.

**International deterioration** — an 80% EBITDA decline in Q3, a $32.5M excess-potato write-off, underutilized plants, and a Middle East conflict expected to hurt Q4 — is the near-term drag masking domestic progress.

**Litigation** is the most material under-priced risk. The antitrust price-fixing class action (filed Nov 2024, consolidated Oct 2025, treble damages sought, parallel Canadian cases, motion to dismiss pending) carries unquantified exposure. A separate securities class action and derivative suit over ERP and pricing disclosures are also at the motion-to-dismiss stage. These are binary items a standard screen would miss.

**ERP transition damage** (~$95M of FY2024 losses and lost customers) is the root of both the securities litigation and a credibility deficit; the next implementation phase is paused.

**Recurring potato write-offs** ($95.9M in FY2024, $45.6M in FY2026 YTD) reflect the mismatch between contracted supply and volatile demand, with no futures market to hedge.

The read is that the market expects a large binary move without a strong directional bias, which fits the standoff in the fundamental picture.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($60–65)
The cost program lands ahead of its ≥$250M target, pricing stabilizes as industry capacity rationalizes, North America volume growth (already +12%) sustains, U.S. QSR traffic continues to recover, and the falling capex converts to free cash flow that de-levers the balance sheet. International stops bleeding as the Argentina/Netherlands rationalization takes hold. Activists (JANA on the board, Starboard pressing) force a credible profit-growth path, and the clustered insider buying near $40–49 proves prescient. EBITDA recovers toward the FY2024 $1.4B level, mid-cycle EPS exceeds $3.00, and the stock re-rates to the high-$50s/$60s. This maps to the $65 analyst high.

### Bear Case ($33–38)
Industry oversupply persists, price/trade investment continues to compress margins with no volume payoff, and International deteriorates further under Middle East pressure. The antitrust suit survives the motion to dismiss and treble-damage exposure becomes a real liability. Leverage forces a dividend or buyback cut, further damaging sentiment. EBITDA erodes below the FY2026 run-rate, coverage ratios tighten toward covenants, and the stock breaks below the $37.62 52-week low toward the mid-$30s.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD. The recovery thesis and the balance-sheet/litigation risk are close to a standoff, and we lack the evidence for a confident directional call. The upcoming fiscal Q4/full-year earnings report is the near-term natural reassessment point.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Gross margin stabilizes or expands for two consecutive quarters (i.e., above the 21.2% Q3 FY2026 trough), confirming the price/trade investment has bottomed.
- International segment EBITDA stops declining and turns sequentially positive, removing the primary drag.
- Consensus EPS revisions turn meaningfully positive (>10% over 90 days) as the cost program flows through, OR the antitrust motion to dismiss is granted, removing the treble-damage overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A dividend or buyback cut driven by leverage/covenant pressure.
- The antitrust suit advances past the motion-to-dismiss stage with a quantified material liability.
- Gross margin falls below 20% and free cash flow fails to recover despite the capex roll-off, indicating the reset is not working.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report, then the promised investor day that Starboard has demanded, which should provide the clearest read on the credibility of the turnaround plan.

## Conclusion

The bear case — leverage above 4x, an 80% International EBITDA collapse, recurring potato write-offs, unquantified treble-damage antitrust exposure, and a self-inflicted margin reset in an oversupplied category — is substantial and not fully de-risked. The bull case — a strong North America franchise gaining volume, a cost program running ahead of a ≥$250M target, a falling capex profile about to lift free cash flow, and an activist-backed leadership reset where the new Executive Chair and CFO have put ~$3.2M of their own capital into the open market near the lows — is credible but unproven in the earnings line.

The insider-buying cluster and activist involvement lift Management Quality and argue firmly against selling at $46.54. But flat estimate revisions, a premium multiple to leveraged peers on depressed earnings, and binary litigation keep this short of a buy. We maintain a HOLD: the evidence is genuinely balanced, and the next earnings report plus a Starboard-demanded investor day should resolve whether the domestic recovery and cost program can outrun the International drag and the balance-sheet constraint. The prior report's price decline on largely intact turnaround inputs improves the risk/reward at the margin, but not enough to move off HOLD until margin stabilization or litigation clarity appears.
