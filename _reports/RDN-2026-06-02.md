**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Radian Group is in the middle of the largest strategic shift in its history. The company closed a ~$1.67 billion acquisition of Inigo Limited, a Lloyd's of London specialty (re)insurance platform, on February 2, 2026, converting a pure-play U.S. private mortgage insurer into a two-segment hybrid spanning mortgage credit and global specialty lines. At the same time, it is winding down its non-core Conduit, Title, and Real Estate (homegenius) businesses. The legacy mortgage insurance (MI) franchise remains highly profitable and cyclically stable, generating roughly $600M of net income annually for five straight years, but the forward earnings profile now carries property-catastrophe and long-tail casualty reserving risk that this book never previously held.

The stock trades at $33.79, around 8% below its 2025 adjusted book value per share of $36.93 and at ~7.9x trailing/forward earnings, a discount to both its own book and to the analyst consensus mean target of $43.17 (median $44, range $37-$48). The valuation gap is real, and rising estimates (current-year consensus up 9.9% over 90 days, four consecutive earnings beats) reflect genuine earnings momentum. The bull case, articulated by RBC's May 22 Outperform initiation at ~$47, is that a diversified combined entity deserves a higher multiple than a capital-trapped single-line insurer. The bear case is that Radian paid a full price to enter an unfamiliar business potentially near a specialty-cycle peak, diluting a clean, high-ROE franchise, during a simultaneous CEO and CFO transition.

We rate RDN a HOLD with a constructive growth lean. The discount-to-book and estimate momentum keep the risk/reward from being negatively asymmetric, but the combination of integration execution risk, leadership turnover at both the CEO and CFO levels, a degrading near-term analyst tally, and options markets pricing imminent stress (near-term IV 51.4% vs 23.9% realized, backwardation) means the burden of proof sits with the transformation. We would upgrade on confirmed Inigo accretion to book value per share and a permanent CFO appointment, or accumulate on weakness toward book value in the low-$30s.

## Company Overview

Radian historically operated as one of the six dominant U.S. private mortgage insurers (alongside MGIC, Essent, Arch, Enact, and National MI). Radian Guaranty insures lenders against borrower default on low-down-payment conventional mortgages sold to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. This is a capital-intensive, high-margin, cyclical business: it carries a large embedded book of insurance-in-force (IIF) that generates recurring premium for years, and its profitability has been exceptional for over a decade because home-price appreciation built borrower equity and suppressed claims.

As of early 2026, Radian reports in two segments:

1. **Mortgage Insurance (MI)** — the legacy core, running combined ratios in the 20-30% range in benign credit environments.
2. **Specialty/Global Insurance (Inigo)** — Lloyd's Syndicate 1301, writing property, casualty, and specialty lines. Inigo founder/CEO Colin Watson joined Radian as an executive and received an equity grant, confirming the leadership was retained.

The strategic logic is that the U.S. MI market is mature, oligopolistic, and capital-trapped by GSE-mandated capital rules (PMIERs). Diversification into hard-market Lloyd's specialty lines reduces dependence on U.S. mortgage credit and adds a global underwriting platform. Arch, a peer, already validated the multi-line model with its broader global specialty operations, which both supports Radian's direction and underscores that Arch is a far more seasoned multi-line operator than Radian is today.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability has been remarkably stable.** Net income ran $600.7M (2021), $742.9M (2022), $603.1M (2023), $604.4M (2024), and $582.6M (2025), a tight ~$600M band through significant rate volatility. Trailing EPS is $4.39, putting the trailing and forward P/E both at ~7.9x. The MI book's combined ratios have been low because incurred losses stayed historically suppressed.

**Book value has compounded steadily.** Adjusted Book Value Per Share, the company's chosen performance metric, went $23.59 (2021) → $27.86 (2022) → $30.87 (2023) → $33.71 (2024) → $36.93 (2025). At $33.79, the stock trades around 0.91x adjusted book.

**Capital return has been the historical value driver.** Share count fell from ~167.9M (2022) to ~139.4M (2025), roughly a 17% reduction, alongside a growing dividend (current yield ~3.0%, comfortably covered). Radian Guaranty has consistently dividended capital up to the holding company to fund both buybacks and now the Inigo deal.

**Revenue has been modestly declining** as the highest-rate single-premium vintages run off and the book seasons, partly offset by sharply higher net investment income as the float repriced into higher rates. Total revenue sits in the $1.2-1.3B range.

**Capital adequacy is governed by PMIERs, not industrial metrics.** Radian Guaranty has maintained a comfortable cushion (multi-hundred-million to >$1B) above the GSE-mandated minimum. Standard distress screens like the Altman Z-Score are not meaningful for an insurer where "debt" includes insurance reserves; do not read a grey-zone Z-score as distress here.

The key new watch item is post-deal leverage. The Inigo acquisition was funded with a mix of holding-company cash and debt, so holdco liquidity and pro-forma capital ratios warrant close attention in the next 10-Q. The cash outlay also likely moderated the near-term buyback pace, which had been a primary driver of book-value-per-share accretion.

**Earnings momentum is positive and consistent.** The company has beaten consensus four straight quarters (2.2% to 8.1% surprises). Current-quarter estimates have been revised up 9.1% over 30 days and 13.1% over 90 days; current-year up 7.5%/9.9%. This is genuine upward revision velocity, though it falls short of the >30% threshold that would mark a true earnings inflection, and it partly reflects the addition of Inigo's contribution rather than organic acceleration of the core.

## Growth Analysis

Radian's growth thesis has shifted from "compound book value via buybacks at a high-ROE single line" to "accrete a diversified specialty platform to book value and ROE." The two paths carry very different risk profiles.

The legacy MI business is structurally low-growth. New insurance written depends on purchase mortgage origination volumes, which higher rates suppress, even as higher rates improve persistency (refinancing slows, so existing IIF stays on the books longer) and lift investment income. The net effect is modestly declining net premiums earned offset by higher float income. This is a mature, mid-single-digit-at-best franchise on its own.

Inigo is the growth lever. The Lloyd's specialty market has been in a relatively hard pricing environment, supporting near-term underwriting economics, and the deal materially diversifies Radian's earnings away from U.S. mortgage credit. If Inigo accretes to book value per share and the combined entity sustains a competitive ROE, the diversification argument for a higher multiple has teeth. The problem is timing and proof: management's specific guidance on Inigo accretion timing and combined ROE targets was not directly confirmable (the earnings transcript was unavailable), and specialty cycles turn. Entering a hard market at what could be a cyclical peak means the entry point matters enormously, and a softening cycle would pressure Inigo's margins precisely as Radian is still learning to operate it.

The rising estimates support a constructive growth view, but they blend in Inigo's contribution rather than demonstrating standalone acceleration. We score Growth Prospects at 6.0 — positive momentum, real diversification optionality, but unproven execution and a low-growth legacy core.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~0.91x 2025 adjusted book value, RDN sits within a mixed P&C insurance peer set: peer median P/E is 11.7 (RLI 11.7, SIGI 11.8, MCY 6.5, AGO 8.5, PLMR 14.9). Radian sits below the median, closer to AGO and MCY, both of which carry their own structural questions (monoline financial guaranty and California auto/homeowner concentration, respectively). Mortgage insurers as a group trade around book and at single-digit multiples because the market views them as cyclical and capital-trapped; the discount is partly structural, not pure mispricing.

The specific reason the market may be underpricing RDN is the discount to its own book value combined with rising estimates and the diversification optionality the market is not yet crediting. Adjusted book has compounded ~12% annually over five years; a stock at 0.91x a book value growing low-double-digits, generating ~$600M annual earnings and a 3% dividend, has support. Consensus targets all sit above the current price, implying 9% to 42% upside, and RBC's $47 Outperform initiation crystallizes the bull case.

The counterweight is that the discount exists for a reason the market can see clearly: integration uncertainty on a $1.67B deal into an unfamiliar business is capping the multiple, and that uncertainty resolves over 1-2 years, not weeks. The bull thesis depends entirely on Inigo accreting cleanly. If integration stumbles — reserve deficiencies, talent attrition, a softening specialty cycle, or goodwill impairment on the material new intangibles — the multiple stays compressed and the discount-to-book persists rather than closing.

We score Valuation at 6.8. The discount-to-book and target gap are genuine support, but the upside is contingent on an unproven integration rather than a deeply asymmetric mispricing; this is a fairly priced cyclical insurer. The valuation score declined from 8.0 to 7.5 across prior reports as the deal added uncertainty; we move it modestly lower again because the transition risk has crystallized (deal closed, leadership turning over) without yet producing confirming accretion data.

## Competitive Landscape

The U.S. private MI market is a stable post-2008 oligopoly of six well-capitalized players. Pricing is now driven by sophisticated, opaque risk-based "black-box" engines rather than published rate cards, and the GSEs effectively cap structural profitability through PMIERs capital rules and counterparty requirements. Credit performance has been excellent for a decade. Radian holds a durable but not improving position in this segment — it is a share-stable incumbent in a commoditized product where the main competitive risk is persistent pricing pressure across six rational competitors.

The Inigo addition changes the competitive frame. Radian is now a new entrant in Lloyd's specialty lines, competing against established global (re)insurers and against peer Arch, which has operated a diversified multi-line platform for years. Radian has no prior experience running a Lloyd's syndicate, writing global P&C, or managing delegated authority, reinsurance recoverables, and long-tail casualty reserving. Its competitive position in specialty is unproven at best.

The trajectory is the key issue. In MI, Radian's position is stable. In specialty, it is a challenger with no track record entering possibly near a cycle peak. The blended competitive trajectory is neutral-to-slightly-negative until Inigo demonstrates underwriting discipline and integration success. We score Competitive Position at 5.8.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration and execution risk (elevated).** Radian has no prior experience operating a Lloyd's syndicate. London-market culture, delegated authority, reinsurance recoverables, and long-tail casualty reserving are distinct from MI. Integration missteps, reserve deficiencies, or talent attrition would directly damage the deal thesis and the bull case.

**Specialty underwriting and reserving risk (new to this book).** Inigo carries property-catastrophe exposure, long-tail casualty development, and reinsurance counterparty risk — risk types the legacy MI book never had. A softening hard market would compress Inigo's margins.

**Goodwill/intangibles impairment.** The deal adds material goodwill and intangibles; impairment is a tail risk if Inigo underperforms.

**Leadership transition risk.** CEO Richard Thornberry is retiring around August 13, 2026, after leading since 2017. Mike Weinbach (former President of Mr. Cooper Group, ex-CEO of Chase Home Lending) is CEO-elect; his mortgage pedigree is reassuring for the core franchise. CFO Sumita Pandit departed; Daniel Kobell is interim CFO with a permanent search pending. Simultaneous CEO and CFO turnover during a bet-the-strategy integration is unusual timing and a genuine execution flag.

**Persistent core-MI risks.** Housing/credit cycle exposure (earnings hinge on low default rates tied to employment and home prices), GSE/PMIERs regulatory risk, six-way competitive pricing pressure, and mixed interest-rate sensitivity. Mortgage credit performance remains a live concern — a housing downturn would lift delinquencies and incurred losses sharply.

**Macro context.** Interest-rate movements are a mixed driver: higher rates suppress purchase origination volumes (negative for new insurance written) while supporting persistency and investment income (positive). The net is volatile rather than clearly directional. [Specific macro figures — GDP growth, 10Y yield level, Fed policy path, recession probability — are unverified against the provided sources.]

The risk concentration here — a binary integration outcome, dual leadership turnover, and new cat/reserving exposure — is meaningful and not fully priced. We score Risk Factors at 5.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term (15-day) ATM IV is 51.4% against 23.9% one-year realized volatility — a 27.5pp premium — with the term structure in steep backwardation (51.4% at 1m falling to 26.1% at 6m). ATM IV jumped from 34.0% to 51.4% over the week ending June 2 even as the stock fell 4.9%, signaling the market is bracing for a near-dated catalyst. Skew is call-heavy (-9.3% to -11.6% near-term), and put/call OI is 0.79 (call-tilted), so positioning is not bearishly tilted despite the elevated vol; the market is pricing a sizable two-sided move (implied ±8.7% on the nearest expiry) rather than a directional decline. This is consistent with uncertainty around the integration and leadership transition resolving into a binary event window. The elevated IV makes this a poor entry point for new long exposure on a fundamental view; if anything it favors patience.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (toward $44-48)

Inigo accretes cleanly to book value per share within 1-2 years, the diversified combined entity earns a competitive blended ROE, and the market re-rates a multi-line specialty insurer above the ~0.9x book and ~8x earnings that a capital-trapped single-line MI commands. Adjusted book continues compounding low-double-digits from $36.93; the discount closes; the dividend and resumed buybacks add return. Weinbach's mortgage credibility stabilizes the core while Inigo's retained leadership delivers underwriting discipline in a still-firm specialty market. RBC's $47 target is the anchor; the analyst high is $48. Five-year TSR of 208% (vs 150% peers) shows the prior strategy worked, supporting confidence in capital allocation.

### Bear Case (toward $28-31)

Integration stumbles — reserve deficiencies in unfamiliar long-tail casualty lines, talent attrition, or a softening specialty cycle that compresses Inigo's margins right as Radian is learning the business. Goodwill impairment hits the material new intangibles. The dual CEO/CFO transition slows decision-making during the critical integration window. A housing/credit downturn lifts MI delinquencies. The market keeps the multiple compressed at or below book, and the stock drifts back toward its 52-week low of $31.50 or lower. Downside of roughly 8-17% from current.

The trajectory data (HOLD → BUY across prior reports, overall 6.5 → 7.0) reflected improving estimates and the deal optimism. We reverse part of that upgrade. The estimate momentum is intact and the stock has pulled back ~12% from its early-May high of $38.50 on no new negative fundamental news, which marginally improves the entry. But the prior BUY did not adequately weight the binary integration risk crystallizing alongside dual leadership turnover, and the near-term analyst tally has degraded (a strong-buy and a buy were cut over the trailing two months, with one strong-sell appearing). On balance, this is a confident HOLD with a growth lean rather than a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August, coinciding with the CEO transition) and the next 10-Q, which should clarify pro-forma capital, holdco leverage, and early Inigo segment results.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Confirmed Inigo accretion to adjusted book value per share with management disclosing a credible combined ROE target, plus a permanent CFO appointment.
- A pullback toward or below 2025 adjusted book value (~$34 and lower, into the low-$30s) on unchanged fundamentals, widening the discount-to-book margin of safety.
- Two consecutive quarters of clean specialty-segment underwriting (combined ratio confirming the hard-market thesis) with no reserve strengthening.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Reserve deficiency or goodwill impairment disclosure on the Inigo book.
- A break below the $31.50 52-week low on deteriorating MI credit metrics (rising delinquency/incurred-loss trends).
- Material erosion of the PMIERs capital cushion or a holdco liquidity strain post-deal.
- Departure of retained Inigo leadership (Colin Watson) signaling integration failure.

## Conclusion

Radian is a steady ~$600M-earnings franchise trading at a discount to its compounding book value, with rising estimates and a credible bull case that the market under-credits the diversification optionality from the Inigo acquisition. The discount-to-book and the gap to analyst targets provide downside support and a path to upside if integration succeeds. But the company has converted a clean, high-ROE single-line insurer into an unproven multi-line specialty platform amid simultaneous CEO and CFO turnover and new cat/long-tail reserving exposure, and the options market is pricing a near-term catalyst. The upside is contingent and the catalysts are 1-2 years out; the risks are binary and crystallizing now.

We rate RDN a **HOLD** (growth lean), overall score **6.1**. We would hold existing positions for the book-value-compounding and discount-closing thesis while waiting for integration proof, and accumulate on weakness toward book value rather than chase into the transition. We would revisit toward a BUY on confirmed Inigo accretion plus a permanent CFO, or on a pullback into the low-$30s.
