## Executive Summary

NETSTREIT is a $2.06 billion single-tenant net lease REIT that has grown revenue from $96.3 million in FY2022 to $195.0 million in FY2025 — a 26.5% compound annual rate — while growing AFFO per diluted share from $1.16 to $1.31, a 4.1% compound annual rate. That divergence is the central fact about this company. Weighted average basic shares rose 67% over the same three years, from 49.5 million to 82.7 million. Capital has been converted into scale rather than into per-share earnings power, and the arithmetic explaining why is visible in the current numbers: enterprise value on a fully-settled share base is roughly $3.52 billion against $231.4 million of annualized base rent, an implied gross-rent yield of 6.57% against a 7.4% blended cash yield on Q2 acquisitions. An 80-basis-point unlevered gross spread, before general and administrative costs, produces mid-single-digit per-share compounding. It has, and there is no evidence in the current disclosures that it will produce more without a change in the capital structure.

That change is in fact management's plan, and it is the reason this is a HOLD rather than a REDUCE. Adjusted net debt to annualized adjusted EBITDAre is 3.2x against a stated 4.5x–5.5x target, implying roughly $270 million of unused debt capacity at 4.3% cost. Pairing that debt with the $714 million of already-contracted forward equity struck at $18.34 changes the accretion math materially — unlevered deployment of that equity is close to break-even to AFFO per share, levered deployment is clearly accretive. Management has raised FY2026 net investment guidance to $700–800 million, raised AFFO guidance to $1.37–$1.39, raised the dividend 2.3% to $0.225 quarterly, and stated an intent to obtain second and third agency ratings in early 2027 to access public unsecured bonds. If those steps land, FY2027–2028 per-share growth accelerates from the 4–5% run rate.

At 14.7x the FY2026 AFFO guidance midpoint with a 4.44% dividend covered at a 65% payout ratio, the stock offers approximately a fair risk-adjusted return: base case $22.00 (+8.7% price, +13.1% total return including dividends), bull $25.00 (+23.5%), bear $17.25 (-14.8%). Probability-weighted expected total return is roughly +10%, which is what a well-run 4.4%-yielding net lease REIT should return and does not clear a BUY or ACCUMULATE bar. Existing holders should own it for the covered yield; new capital has no urgency at this price.

## Company Overview

NETSTREIT is a Dallas-based, internally managed net lease REIT operating through an UPREIT structure (NETSTREIT, L.P.). As of June 30, 2026 the portfolio comprised 859 investments across 156 tenants, 28 retail industries and 46 states, with $231.4 million of annualized base rent, 100% occupancy, a 10.0-year weighted average remaining lease term, portfolio-wide unit-level rent coverage of 3.8x, and 56.5% of ABR from tenants classified as investment grade or "investment grade profile."

Three features distinguish the platform from generic net lease description. First, underwriting is anchored on unit economics rather than corporate ratings: the FY2025 10-K discloses that 55.7% of properties are leased to unrated or sub-investment-grade tenants underwritten via a proprietary "shadow rating," and management stated on the Q2 call that rent coverage and real estate fungibility are "equally as important, in some cases more important" than credit. Second, NETSTREIT originates first-lien mortgage loans (roughly $151 million of receivables at 6.0%–9.75%) that per the 10-K "generally provide us an option to acquire the property at predetermined pricing and dates" — an origination funnel rather than a yield-chasing sideline. The Q2 Speedway transaction, in which a first mortgage originated in early 2023 converted into fee ownership of 20 properties at a 6.75% initial cash yield with OP units struck at a $21.00 reference price above the then-market price, is the program working as designed. Third, the company discloses that it "utilize[s] technology tools that track cell phone use in stores" to assess store-level performance where investment-grade tenants do not provide unit reporting — unusually candid, and useful context for how the 3.8x coverage figure is produced.

Top-five tenant concentration at year-end 2025 was CVS 4.8%, Dollar General 4.7%, Food Lion/Stop & Shop 4.3%, Home Depot 3.8% and Hobby Lobby 3.6% — 21.2% in aggregate, genuinely diversified for a REIT this size. Geographic concentration is heavier: Texas 17.3%, with the South and Midwest together 77.5% of ABR.

## Financial Analysis

**Growth at the enterprise level is real.** Q2 2026 revenue was $61.3 million, up 26.9% from $48.3 million; H1 revenue $118.3 million, up 25.6%. Total assets grew from $1.61 billion (FY2022) to $2.61 billion (FY2025). FY2025 operating cash flow was $109.5 million.

**Per-share results are the relevant measure and they are modest.** The FY2026 proxy discloses AFFO per diluted share of $0.94 (2021), $1.16 (2022), $1.22 (2023), $1.26 (2024), $1.31 (2025). Cumulative growth from 2022 to 2025 was 12.9% against 102.5% cumulative revenue growth. FY2026 guidance of $1.37–$1.39 implies 4.6%–6.1% growth. Q2 2026 AFFO was $35.5 million, $0.35 per diluted share, which management characterized as +6.1% year over year; an independent recomputation from the 10-Q reconciliation produces closer to +4%, and the prior-year comparative base differs between documents. I use the company-reported figure and flag the reconciliation as unresolved.

There is genuine improvement at the margin. Revenue per basic share rose from $2.13 (FY2024) to $2.36 (FY2025), +10.8%, the best year of the series. Recurring G&A was 9.5% of revenue in Q2 2026 versus 11.3% a year earlier, with FY2026 cash G&A guided to $16.5–$17.0 million.

**Dividend coverage is comfortable.** FY2025 accumulated deficit widened $63.9 million against $7.0 million of pretax income, implying roughly $70.9 million of dividends declared, or about $0.86 per weighted average basic share against $1.31 of AFFO — a 64–65% payout. Operating cash flow exceeded dividends by roughly $39 million. On the raised $0.225 quarterly rate ($0.90 annualized) against guidance midpoint $1.38, the payout ratio is 65.2%. This is a well-covered distribution with room for continued mid-single-digit growth.

**Leverage requires both figures to be quoted.** Management reported adjusted net debt of $672.2 million and adjusted net debt/annualized adjusted EBITDAre of 3.2x, explicitly stating the adjusted figure "includes the impact of all forward equity." Reconstructing from those components: total debt ≈ $672.2M + $20M cash + $714M forwards ≈ $1,406 million; implied annualized adjusted EBITDAre = $672.2M ÷ 3.2 = $210.1 million; therefore net debt on funded capital ÷ EBITDAre = ($1,406M − $20M) ÷ $210.1M ≈ **6.6x**. The 3.2x describes the balance sheet that will exist once forwards settle; the 6.6x describes the balance sheet that exists today. Both are defensible; neither should travel alone. The mitigants on the 6.6x are substantive — the forwards are contractually committed, not speculative; there is no material debt maturity until February 2028; and total liquidity is $1.1 billion (roughly $20 million cash, $301 million revolver availability, $714 million unsettled forwards, $50 million undrawn term loan capacity) against a $2.06 billion market cap.

**Earnings quality carries two persistent drags.** Impairments were $4.2 million in Q2 2026 and $6.3 million in H1 2026 ($8.0 million in H1 2025). Management attributes these largely to purchase-price allocation on portfolio acquisitions and to disposition activity, "largely offset" by gains on sale. That explanation is consistent with a company selling assets acquired at lower cap rates three to four years ago, but a recurring several-million-dollar quarterly charge excluded from the coverage metric belongs in any normalized estimate. Second, all term debt is floating-rate swapped to fixed at a 4.3% weighted average. Hedge expirations, not debt maturities, are the near-term repricing events; the "no material maturity until February 2028" statement does not by itself protect the interest line, and the precise hedge ladder is not confirmed.

**Reported GAAP earnings are not a useful valuation input here** — D&A of $86.4 million on FY2025 revenue of $195.0 million makes trailing P/E of 139x arithmetically true and analytically empty. That said, the four consecutive GAAP EPS misses (Q3 2025 −84.8%, Q4 2025 −69.6%, Q1 2026 −16.0%, Q2 2026 −20.3% against $0.08 estimate) are informative in one narrow way: modelers have consistently underestimated the combined D&A and impairment drag. The magnitude of the misses is shrinking, but the direction has not reversed.

## The Forward Equity Position

At June 30, 2026, unsettled forward equity totaled 38,942,108 shares at a weighted average net settlement price of $18.34, or approximately $714 million of gross proceeds. Against 101,526,575 shares outstanding, the forwards equal **38.4% of the current share count**; full settlement would produce 140,468,683 shares, leaving existing holders with **72.3% of the company — ownership dilution of 27.7%.** The larger figure describes the increase in share count; the smaller describes ownership actually transferred.

Reported diluted shares of 102,788,997 include only the treasury-stock-method increment (roughly 1.3 million shares), not the full 38.9 million. Guidance embeds $0.05–$0.08 per share of TSM dilution for 3.6–5.9 million shares in FY2026, with management expecting that drag to peak in Q3 2026 and the forwards-to-outstanding ratio to normalize toward 15% through 2027. The $2.06 billion market cap excludes the forward shares entirely (101.5M × $20.24 = $2,055M).

This is disclosed, not hidden, and the offsetting asset is $714 million of cash. Enterprise value is essentially unchanged by settlement: $2,055M equity + $1,386M funded net debt = $3.44 billion today, versus $2,843M settled equity + $672M adjusted net debt = $3.52 billion post-settlement. The economic question is solely whether the proceeds get deployed at a spread.

The accretion arithmetic: $714 million deployed unlevered at a 7.4% cash yield generates roughly $52.8 million of incremental cash NOI against 38.9 million new shares — approximately $1.35 per new share before allocated G&A and before interest, against current AFFO of roughly $1.38. Unlevered, this is break-even to mildly dilutive. Levered — pairing the equity with the roughly $270 million of debt capacity available before reaching the 4.5x floor, at 4.3–4.5% — it is clearly accretive. The bull case requires both the $700–800 million annual deployment pace **and** a willingness to re-lever. Management has publicly committed to the first and has been deliberately conservative on the second.

The stated rationale for that conservatism is credible: June 2026 S&P SmallCap 600 inclusion created a liquidity window the company used to raise 9.0 million shares for $183 million, which CFO Donlan said "accelerated our needs relative to what we were expecting when we put out guidance in April"; a conservative leverage profile supports the second and third ratings sought in early 2027; and dry powder is optionality if cap rates widen. Under that reading, 3.2x is a bridge rather than a posture. It nonetheless means the current period carries pre-funded equity drag with deployment lagging.

## Growth Analysis

Three inputs support the forward growth case, and one constrains it.

Supporting: the acquisition environment. Management described competition as "significantly lower," with "virtually no competition from... the larger private institutions" and the 1031 market as the principal competitor. The supply mechanism offered is specific — buyers who assembled portfolios in 2021–early 2022 at low cap rates on five-year bank debt now face refinancings where "the refi looks a lot different, so selling the portfolios makes a lot more sense," implying persistence into 2027. This is management's hypothesis rather than a verified market statistic, but it is internally consistent with the observed outcome: portfolio deals that historically "priced away from us" have become winnable, delivering access to Chick-fil-A, Sprouts, Kwik Trip, Darden and Brinker brands. Management was appropriately unwilling to overclaim on pricing — well-marketed portfolios still clear at "substantial premium," and the ones NETSTREIT wins price at roughly par.

Supporting: the 2027 capital-markets path. Moving from a single BBB− Fitch rating and an entirely bank-financed structure to two or three ratings and public unsecured bonds would be a structural cost-of-debt improvement, not a cyclical one.

Supporting: operating leverage. G&A at 9.5% of revenue from 11.3% is real and should continue as the asset base grows against $16.5–$17.0 million of guided cash G&A.

Constraining: the spread. EV/ABR of 15.2x implies a 6.57% gross rent yield on enterprise value against 7.4% acquisition yields — roughly 80 basis points gross, before G&A. Acquisition cash yields and ABR-implied yields are not perfectly comparable (ABR is gross contractual rent, acquisition yields are typically quoted on cash NOI), so this should be read as directional rather than precise. But it is arithmetically consistent with four consecutive years of 4–5% per-share growth, and it is not the 150–200bp spread the sector typically advertises.

Estimate revisions are mixed-to-slightly-negative on the GAAP series: current-year consensus $0.32 (−3.9% over 30 days, flat over 90), next-year $0.35 (−2.4% over 30 days, +2.8% over 90). Given the AFFO guidance was raised at the low end, the GAAP decline most plausibly reflects TSM dilution and impairment assumptions. There is no positive estimate-momentum signal here.

Peer context requires care. The supplied Retail REIT comparable set (GTY, IVT, UE, AKR, WSR; median P/E 40.7x, median yield 3.9%) is dominated by shopping-center REITs rather than net lease operators, and GAAP P/E is not a usable metric across REITs with different depreciation vintages. The closest structural comparable is Getty Realty at a $2 billion market cap with a 5.8% dividend yield — 136 basis points above NTST. On yield, NETSTREIT is the more expensive of the two similarly-sized net lease REITs, which is defensible on diversification and growth but is not evidence of a discount. Against the large-cap net lease universe, NETSTREIT trades at a discount to Agree Realty, which is appropriate: NETSTREIT is roughly one-third the property count, carries BBB− versus A− (roughly four notches of cost-of-debt disadvantage), and runs higher G&A.

Total return math at the current price: 4.44% dividend plus 4.6–6.1% guided AFFO/share growth equals 9–10.5% if the multiple holds. Analyst positioning is uniformly constructive — 16 buy/strong buy, 4 hold, no sells, unchanged over four months, with a target range of $21.00 to $25.00 and a mean of $23.18. Notably, the low target sits 3.8% above spot; every covering analyst has a target above the current price. That is corroborating but not independent evidence, and consensus targets in the net lease space have historically been slow to reprice cap-rate regime shifts.

The macro input is the binding constraint on the multiple. Net lease REITs are duration proxies, and a sustained rise in long-end Treasury yields would pressure a 14.7x P/AFFO multiple on a 4.44% yielder. A 100bp move higher in the long end would plausibly compress the multiple toward 13x, which is roughly the bear case below. [The specific claims that the 30-year Treasury is "at its highest level since 2007," the 10-year is at "4.63%," and that the Fed "has delivered five consecutive holds with three hawkish dissents" are not supported by the research brief or Phase 1 data and have been removed as unverified.]

I score valuation 5.5 — fair. There is adequate income support and a modest, identifiable catalyst path (forwards settling, re-levering, 2027 bond access), but no specific mispricing thesis. A 14.7x multiple on 4–6% per-share growth is priced approximately correctly.

## Competitive Landscape

NETSTREIT's competitive trajectory is genuinely mixed rather than deteriorating.

Improving: scale is compounding (859 investments from a much smaller base), G&A efficiency is improving, and access to portfolio transactions has widened the sourcing funnel to tenants previously out of reach. The mortgage-origination-to-fee-ownership channel is a durable structural differentiator that peers do not replicate at this scale, and it produced the quarter's most interesting transaction. The Big Lots box was re-leased to a rated T.J. Maxx at more than 20% above prior rent, restoring 100% occupancy — evidence the underwriting emphasis on real estate fungibility over corporate credit has substance.

Deteriorating: the credit mix, deliberately. Management guided the investment-grade share of *new* acquisitions to "around that 30–35% investment-grade profile" against a portfolio at 56.5%. Sustained acquisition at that mix mechanically drifts portfolio credit quality lower. The stated rationale — investment-grade cap rates compressed and never re-widened, so "you're just not getting the same risk-adjusted returns there" — is coherent and supported by a six-year record of "virtually no credit loss." That record has not been tested through a full retail credit cycle, and it is being extended into a consumer environment where an analyst on the Q2 call raised Albertsons' report of "softer industry unit trends and a more cautious consumer" in grocery.

Structurally disadvantaged: the rating. BBB− against A− for the sector leader is a persistent cost-of-debt gap, which is precisely why the 2027 bond-market roadmap matters more than any single quarter's acquisition volume.

## Risk Assessment

**Retail tenant credit into a weakening consumer.** The 10-K concedes that drug store and grocery — two core categories — "have proven to be susceptible to competition from e-commerce," and cites Big Lots' 2024 bankruptcy and Walgreens' announced closure of approximately 1,200 stores as realized examples. Walgreens is named among significant multi-location relationships but its ABR percentage is not disclosed in the material available, which is a genuine sizing gap. Offsetting: 3.8x portfolio rent coverage, 21.2% top-five concentration, and a watch list management characterizes as containing "no troubled tenants," with only "a couple" of sub-1.0x-coverage assets remaining.

**Interest rate structure and hedge expiry.** All term debt is floating swapped to fixed at 4.3%. The 10-K warns that "upon the expiration of any hedge agreements we enter into, we would be exposed to then-existing market rates of interest." There is an apparent swap maturity in January 2027 against a term loan maturing 2029, which would create a repricing event well ahead of any maturity — unconfirmed and unsized, and the most important open verification item for the FY2027 earnings model.

**A cash-trap covenant keyed to tenant metrics.** The 10-K discloses the company "may be restricted from accessing some of our excess cash flow after debt service if certain of our tenants fail to meet certain financial performance metric thresholds." This is a non-standard construct that converts tenant deterioration into a liquidity constraint rather than only a revenue one. The disclosure does not identify the facility, tenants, metrics or thresholds, so it cannot be sized and may apply only to a secured subset.

**REIT qualification on forward-sale cash settlement.** A dedicated risk factor states the tax treatment of cash received on cash settlement of forward sale agreements "is unclear and could jeopardize our ability to meet the REIT qualification requirements," because it is "not entirely clear whether each forward sale agreement qualifies as a 'securities futures contract'" under Section 1032. With $714 million outstanding this has become dollar-material. It crystallizes only on cash settlement of in-the-money forwards; physical settlement is the intended and evident route. Low probability, high consequence.

**Control environment.** The FY2025 10-K continues to disclose a 2024 business email compromise producing two fraudulent transfers totaling $3.3 million and a $2.8 million net loss, stating plainly that "our internal controls did not operate effectively to prevent the loss from occurring." No material weakness is asserted and remediation is described. The dollar amount is immaterial; the continued disclosure two years on is a legitimate governance datapoint for a business whose model involves frequent large wire transfers to development partners.

**Positioning: the short interest resolves benignly.** Reported short interest is 33,134,654 shares, 39.7% of float, 11.1 days to cover. Taken at face value that is an alarming figure for a net lease REIT. It reconciles almost exactly with the forward equity program: 33.1 million shares short equals 85% of the 38,942,108 unsettled forward shares. Forward sale agreements are hedged by the bank counterparty borrowing and short-selling the shares it has contracted to buy, and that short position is extinguished by physical delivery at settlement rather than by open-market purchase. The numeric correspondence and the standard market mechanic together make this the overwhelmingly likely explanation. I cannot confirm the settlement date of the short-interest report against the June 30 forward balance, so a residual directional short position may exist. The practical implications are that neither squeeze potential nor a sophisticated bear thesis should be inferred from this figure, and that screens flagging NTST on short interest are producing a false positive.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($25.00, +23.5%).** Deployment runs at the upper end of $700–800 million net for FY2026 and continues into 2027 while the 2021-vintage refinancing wave sustains portfolio supply at par pricing. Management re-levers toward the 4.5x floor, deploying the $714 million of $18.34-struck equity alongside roughly $270 million of 4.3% debt, which converts a break-even equity tranche into a clearly accretive one. TSM dilution peaks in Q3 2026 and reverses. Second and third agency ratings arrive in early 2027, opening public unsecured bonds and compressing marginal debt cost. FY2027 AFFO/share reaches $1.52–$1.55 and the multiple re-rates to 16.5x on visible acceleration from 5% to 8% per-share growth. Long-end yields stabilize or fall, restoring net lease multiples generally.

**Bear case ($17.25, −14.8%).** The long end keeps grinding higher; cap rates widen and the P/AFFO multiple compresses toward 12.5x. The January 2027 hedge expiry reprices a term loan at materially higher rates, taking the 4.3% weighted average cost up and compressing the acquisition spread from 80bp toward zero. Consumer deterioration reaches the tenant base — grocery unit trends, dollar-store traffic, drug-store closures — and the deliberate drift toward a 30–35% investment-grade acquisition mix meets its first real credit cycle. Impairments, already $4.2 million a quarter and excluded from AFFO, accelerate and force disposals at unfavorable pricing. Deployment slows to preserve the balance sheet, leaving pre-funded equity as pure drag and FY2027 AFFO/share at $1.40. The stock retests the $17.02 52-week low.

**Base case ($22.00, +8.7% price, +13.1% total return).** Deployment lands within guidance, re-levering proceeds gradually rather than aggressively, FY2027 AFFO/share reaches roughly $1.45 (+5%), and the multiple holds near 15x. The dividend grows in line with AFFO. This is the modal outcome and it is a fair, unremarkable return.

Probability-weighting 55% base, 20% bull, 25% bear produces an expected total return of approximately +10%, appropriate compensation for the risk taken and not more.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income hold).** The thesis is a covered 4.44% distribution growing at mid-single digits, with a genuine but contingent path to acceleration in 2027. Existing holders should maintain exposure. New capital has no urgent entry signal at $20.24.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE or BUY:**
1. Adjusted net debt/EBITDAre reported at 4.0x or higher with the forward balance falling toward 20% of shares outstanding, confirming the re-levering leg of the accretion math is executing rather than being deferred. This should be visible in the Q3 or Q4 2026 supplemental.
2. FY2027 AFFO/share guidance at or above $1.48 (implying ≥7% growth), which would indicate the spread has widened beyond the current ~80bp gross.
3. A price below $18.25 with fundamentals intact, at which point the AFFO yield exceeds 7.5% and the dividend yield approaches 5% — a level at which the modest growth rate becomes adequately compensated.
4. Confirmation of a second investment-grade rating ahead of the stated early-2027 timeline, with an inaugural unsecured bond priced inside the current 4.3% weighted average cost.

**Downgrade triggers to REDUCE or AVOID:**
1. Quarterly impairments exceeding $8 million for two consecutive quarters, or any disclosure of a tenant on the watch list exceeding 2% of ABR — either would indicate the credit-drift strategy is producing losses before it has been cycle-tested.
2. Disclosure of a hedge repricing that lifts the weighted average interest cost above 5.0%, compressing the acquisition spread below 50bp gross.
3. Any activation of the tenant-metric cash-trap covenant, or FY2026 net investment guidance cut below $600 million — the latter would leave the $714 million of pre-funded equity as an extended per-share drag with no offsetting deployment.
4. AFFO payout ratio above 80% on forward guidance, which would remove the coverage cushion supporting the income thesis.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (late October 2026) are the next natural checkpoint, and specifically the quarter in which management expects TSM dilution to peak — the test is whether reported AFFO/share holds the guidance trajectory through that peak. The second decision point is the FY2027 guidance release (expected February 2027), which should reveal both the re-levering intent and the initial per-share effect of the settled forwards. The hedge-expiry question should be resolved in the FY2026 10-K.

## Conclusion

NETSTREIT is a competently run, 100%-occupied net lease portfolio with reasonable tenant diversification, a covered and growing dividend, a clean maturity ladder to February 2028, and $1.1 billion of liquidity against a $2.06 billion market capitalization. The disclosure quality is above average — management volunteered the forward-equity adjustment to leverage unprompted, discloses its shadow-rating limitations candidly, and continues to report a control failure two years after the fact.

The constraint is the spread. An 80-basis-point gross investment margin before G&A produces mid-single-digit per-share compounding, which is what the record shows: 12.9% cumulative AFFO/share growth against 102.5% cumulative revenue growth from 2022 to 2025, and five consecutive years of total-shareholder-return underperformance against the company's own selected peer group. Management's plan to widen that spread — re-lever toward 4.5x, deploy the $18.34-struck equity, secure additional ratings and access public bonds in 2027 — is coherent and, if executed, would justify a higher multiple. It has not been executed yet, and the interim period carries pre-funded equity drag, a possible early-2027 hedge repricing, and a long-end rate backdrop that is a risk to duration-proxy multiples.

At 14.7x FY2026 AFFO guidance with a 4.44% covered yield, that combination is priced approximately right. HOLD, on income.
