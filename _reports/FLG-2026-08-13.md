## Executive Summary

Flagstar has done the hard part of a bank rescue. Capital is rebuilt (CET1 13.16%, up from 12.83% at year-end 2025), liquidity of $27.2B covers $14.5B of uninsured deposits by $12.7B, the fair-value mark on held-for-investment loans has narrowed from $3.1B to $709M, Moody's upgraded in April 2026, and Q2 2026 produced the first quarter of positive total loan growth since Q4 2023 alongside a third consecutive profitable quarter. Those are real, verifiable improvements, and they explain why this coverage has moved up from a 4.3 score in April to 5.3 in June.

The Q2 10-Q, filed August 6, changes the earnings-quality picture. The allowance for credit losses on loans fell from $1,030M to $869M while H1 provision attributable to loans was $17M against $178M of net charge-offs — a $161M reserve release against H1 pre-tax income of $80M. Holding the allowance ratio flat at year-end's 1.70% would have required roughly $185M of provision and turned reported H1 net income of $55M into a loss of approximately $64M. Separately, H1 pre-provision pre-tax revenue was $98M ($883M NII plus $131M fee income less $916M expense) against $178M of net charge-offs. Pre-provision earnings do not currently cover credit costs. The release has a defensible CECL mechanic: $1,571M of the $2,800M of non-accruals carry no allowance because they are collateral-dependent and already written down to appraised value, and 42% of H1 multi-family payoffs were substandard loans leaving at par. But the release is finite, and the appraisals underpinning it largely predate the June 25, 2026 NYC rent freeze that takes effect in October.

Guidance credibility is now the binding constraint. FY2026 guidance has been cut twice this year, with the adjusted EPS midpoint falling ~29% from $0.625 to $0.45 in a single quarter. What remains requires H2 quarterly NII of $489-539M against $440M delivered in Q2 (+11% to +22%) and H2 net income of $170-245M against $55M in H1. Consensus has moved decisively away from management: current-year EPS is down 27.7% over 90 days to $0.35 and next-year down 11.2% to $1.38, versus the company's $1.60-1.70 guide. On fully diluted shares of 473.6M the stock trades at 0.93x tangible book, not the headline 0.81x, and management's own 2027 guidance puts TBV/share at $17.25-17.75 — flat against $17.51 today. The success case delivers no book compounding; the return has to come entirely from a multiple re-rating. At 7.6% below the 52-week high, with a dated credit catalyst in October and estimates falling at a 20% quarterly clip, that asymmetry is unfavorable. We move to **REDUCE**.

## Company Overview

Flagstar Bank, N.A. is an $87.7B-asset national banking association headquartered in Hicksville, New York, with $61.2B of loans, $67.5B of deposits, $8.1B of equity, roughly 340 branches across nine states, and one reportable segment. It is the successor to New York Community Bancorp following the December 2022 Flagstar Bancorp acquisition and the March 2023 purchase of assets and assumption of liabilities from Signature Bridge Bank. Those transactions pushed a multi-family-concentrated thrift through $100B of assets and into heightened supervisory standards; the Q4 2023 credit and goodwill events (goodwill written from $2,426M to zero) triggered a $1.05B March 2024 capital raise led by Liberty Strategic Capital, with Hudson Bay and Reverence participating, and the installation of Joseph Otting as CEO.

On October 17, 2025 the holding company merged into the Bank, so the Bank itself is the publicly traded registrant. Supervision is now solely by the OCC; the entity is no longer a bank holding company subject to Federal Reserve supervision or BHC-specific enhanced prudential standards; equity issuances by a national bank are exempt from Securities Act registration; and the FY2025 10-K risk factors disclose that continued SEC filing is effectively voluntary. This lowers structural cost and removes a supervisory layer while also lowering the disclosure floor. The OCC's heightened standards for large national banks still apply, but a public equity holder should demand a modestly higher information-risk premium than for a comparably sized BHC.

The strategy is a deliberate remix toward one-third CRE, one-third C&I, one-third consumer. In six months multi-family fell from 47.7% to 44.2% of loans ($28,983M to $26,931M), CRE from 15.3% to 13.5%, and C&I rose from 25.1% to 30.4% ($15,217M to $18,563M, +22%). Total HFI loans grew $255M.

## Financial Analysis

### Margin: the whole thesis

Q2 2026 NII was $440M on a 2.13% NIM, against $443M and 2.15% in Q1. H1 NIM of 2.14% is up 37bp from 1.77% a year earlier — genuine progress driven by paying down high-cost deposits and borrowings. The problem is the level: 2.13% sits roughly 80-100bp below typical regional-bank margins, and it is the arithmetic source of the earnings shortfall.

The composition explains why improvement is slow. Total loan yield is *falling*, to 4.91% in Q2 from 4.97% in Q1 and 5.12% in H1 2025, as the mix shifts toward lower-yielding C&I. On funding, interest-bearing deposits cost 3.08%, but $20.5B of CDs — a third of the deposit base — still cost 3.90%, and interest-bearing checking/MMDA cost *rose* 6bp sequentially to 2.55% as balances grew $2.2B in response to competitive pricing. Non-interest-bearing deposits are only 17.4% of the total ($11,731M) and declined $329M year-to-date. All-in cost of deposits derives to roughly 2.53% annualized ($424M of Q2 interest expense on $67,138M of average deposits). The thrift-legacy funding profile is not fixable in quarters.

Rate positioning is mild in every direction: +100bp parallel is +0.8% to NII, -100bp is -1.7%, and a 100bp short-end-only decline is +0.72%. Every shock is under 2% of NII, which confirms that the guided 55-65bp two-year NIM expansion must come from asset repricing and mix, not from the rate path. Management attributed part of the guidance cut to rates not falling as assumed, but the disclosed sensitivities cannot carry that weight; the larger driver is volume — CRE payoffs at $1.5B per quarter against $600-800M modeled shrank high-yielding assets faster than plan.

Genuine improvements: 30-89 day delinquencies down 63%, classified loans down $1.2B, H1 net charge-offs down to $178M (0.59% annualized) from $232M (0.70%). Non-accruals fell $175M, though MD&A attributes that primarily to resolution of a single bankrupt borrower.

Genuine deteriorations the headlines obscure: multi-family net charge-offs ran 1.17% annualized in Q2 2026, identical to Q2 2025 — the total rate improved only on mix and shrinkage. C&I net charge-offs rose to 0.30% from 0.08% year over year, in the book growing 22%. $51M is 90+ days past due and still accruing, versus zero at year-end. Allowance coverage of non-accruals has fallen from 45.9% to 31.0% in eighteen months.

Modification activity is the most striking single disclosure. Loans modified for borrowers in financial difficulty were $401M in Q2 2026 versus $19M in Q2 2025, and $556M in H1 versus $19M. H1 multi-family modifications cut the weighted-average contractual rate from 8.03% to 5.14% with a 1.1-year extension. Of $599M modified in the prior twelve months, only 56% was current at June 30, with 37% ($220M) 90+ days past due. The dollar amounts are small against a $61B book — roughly $9.5M of annualized NII given up — so this is a credit-quality signal about the borrower base rather than a P&L item. The coupon resets that the margin story requires are being handed back to borrowers who cannot service them.

### Capital, liquidity, and what a stress costs

CET1 13.16% ($7,937M, implying ~$60,312M of RWA), Tier 1 13.99%, total risk-based 16.58%, leverage 9.70%. Liquidity $27.2B — $18.9B on balance sheet including $4.6B at the Fed and $14.3B of HQLA, plus $5.6B FHLB and $2.7B discount window — against $14.5B of uninsured deposits (21% of total). Borrowings down to $10,937M from $12,184M. AOCL of -$547M is a rate mark on GSE/guaranteed mortgage paper, not credit; the March 2026 interagency proposal to recognize AOCI in regulatory capital would cost roughly 91bp of CET1 if finalized as drafted and fully phased in.

Solvency is not the question. Illustratively, if cumulative losses on the $8.9B of NYC collateral where at least half the units are rent-regulated reached 10% ($890M) against the entire current multi-family allowance of $439M, the incremental ~$450M pre-tax charge (~$330M after tax) would consume about 55bp of CET1. The bank stays well capitalized. The binding constraint is earnings: $450M of provision is roughly one and a half to two years of current-run-rate pre-provision earnings, and it comes straight out of tangible book.

Accumulated deficit remains -$958M. H1 operating cash flow was $108M, which we do not annualize — it embeds an unusually small non-cash provision add-back and $79M of negative other operating items.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has two legs. First, mix: $4.8B of H1 C&I originations against $6.8B of new and increased commitments, with record ~$2.8B of quarterly originations in Q2, concentrated in Specialized Industries and Regional Commercial Banking. Second, repricing: $10,056M of multi-family and CRE reprices or matures in 2027, $20.5B of 3.90% CDs can reprice down, $10.9B of borrowings at 4.37% can be retired, and $2.8B of non-accruals could return to earning status. Each mechanic is individually plausible, and together they underpin the FY2027 guide of $800-900M net income, $1.60-1.70 EPS, 2.70-2.80% NIM, 50-55% efficiency, and 10-10.5% ROATCE.

Against that, the revision data is unambiguous and recent:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current quarter | $0.10 | $0.13 | $0.14 | -24.3% | -30.9% |
| Current year | $0.35 | $0.44 | $0.49 | -19.9% | -27.7% |
| Next year | $1.38 | $1.50 | $1.56 | -8.0% | -11.2% |

Fourteen to fifteen analysts have cut current-year estimates 20% in thirty days and put next year at $1.38 against management's guide. Consensus is not merely lagging; it is explicitly rejecting a fifth of the guide.

The earnings surprise record is mixed and deteriorating at the margin: +176.5% (Dec-25), +22.3% (Mar-26), then -26.1% in June against a $0.07 estimate. Shares fell 5.9% on July 24 and multiple analysts cut forecasts.

The most consequential growth constraint is per-share. Guided 2027 TBV/share of $17.25-17.75 is flat versus $17.51 today, despite $800-900M of guided earnings. The reconciliation sits in the March 2024 warrant structure: warrants for 315,000 Series D shares at a $2,476 strike, convertible 333:1 into ~105M common shares at $7.43 per common equivalent, net-settled, expiring ~March 2031. Because settlement is net and the strike fixed, the diluted share count rises mechanically as the stock rises — the treasury-method warrant contribution grows from roughly 57M shares near $16 to roughly 66M near $20. Reported Q2 diluted shares were 473,623,332 (417.0M outstanding plus 56.8M dilutive potential shares, equal to 13.6% of existing shares or 12.0% post-issuance ownership dilution). Warrant intrinsic value at $14.275 is roughly $717M, about 12% of market cap. The structure is a direct drag on per-share accretion in exactly the re-rating scenario the bull case requires.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing multiples are meaningless here: trailing EPS is -$0.51 on the FY2025 loss, and the 473x figure carried in quote feeds is an artifact. Work forward.

**On tangible book.** TCE of $7,304M over 417.0M shares is $17.51, so $14.275 is 0.815x. But the honest comparison uses reported diluted shares: $7,304M over 473.6M is $15.42, and $14.275 is **0.926x fully diluted tangible book**. That single adjustment removes most of the apparent discount. The $250M repurchase (17.5M shares, 4.2% of outstanding) is TBV-accretive at these prices — pro forma $17.66 per basic share, +0.9% — but it does not change the diluted arithmetic materially.

**On earnings.** At $14.275: 10.3x next-year consensus of $1.38; 8.6x the $1.65 midpoint of guided 2027 EPS; 40.8x current-year consensus of $0.35. Peer median P/E is 12.5 (AUB 12.5, ABCB 15.8, ASB 11.0, AX 12.2, HOMB 12.7), with peer median yield 3.0% against FLG's 0.28%.

**Reconciling the two.** A residual-income frame with a 3% terminal growth rate: at the guided 10.25% ROATCE and an 11% cost of equity appropriate to a Ba3/BB+ issuer in an active credit workout, justified P/TBV is (10.25-3)/(11-3) = 0.91x, or about $15.90 on basic TBV. At consensus-implied ROATCE of roughly 7.9% ($1.38 on ~$17.50 of tangible book), justified P/TBV falls to about 0.61x. Cross-checking on earnings at a peer-discounted 9-10x consensus $1.38 gives $12.40-13.80.

So consensus-based value is roughly $12.50-14.00 and guidance-based value roughly $16.00-18.50. At $14.275 the market is already extending partial credit to a guide that has been reduced twice in seven months. This is not a stock where cheapness is unrecognized. It is 7.6% below its 52-week high of $15.44 and up 20.7% over the trailing year. Analyst positioning has improved (3 strong buy, 8 buy, 7 hold, sentiment trend improving; mean target $16.26, median $17.00, range $13-18), but with next-year estimates falling 8% in thirty days those targets are stale in the wrong direction — the pattern where targets lag reality upward applies to accelerating estimates, not to a 20% quarterly cut.

We treat the 0.81x headline book multiple as a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal. The discount is compensation for a specific, dated credit event and for a warrant structure that caps per-share accretion, both of which are identifiable rather than sentiment-driven.

## Competitive Landscape

Flagstar's historical edge was underwriting New York rent-regulated multi-family, a decades-long low-loss niche because below-market regulated rents produced stable occupancy. That edge has been structurally dismantled: the 2019 Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act curtailed the ability to move regulated rents toward market on turnover, and on June 25, 2026 the NYC Rent Guidelines Board voted 7-1 to freeze rents on roughly one million stabilized apartments, effective October 2026. The bank's own language is explicit: "we are curtailing future originations of loans secured by rent-regulated properties." Residual exposure remains large: $14.6B of New York multi-family (54% of the book), of which $12.8B is subject to rent regulation to varying degrees and $8.9B is secured by properties where at least half the units are regulated. Total NYC multi-family is $13.4B.

What is being built in place of it is middle-market and specialty C&I lending, the single most contested business in U.S. banking. Flagstar enters it with a Ba3/BB+ issuer rating (Baa3/BBB on deposits), a 2.13% NIM, and 17.4% non-interest-bearing deposits — structurally the higher-cost lender in most competitive situations. Rapid growth in a competed asset class from a cost disadvantage is the classic setting for adverse selection, and the C&I charge-off rate moving from 0.08% to 0.30% year over year in Q2 is the early metric to watch. It is small and could be idiosyncratic; it is also directionally the wrong way in the portfolio growing fastest.

The company's own proxy quantifies the historical gap: $100 invested at the base date became $49.02 by December 31, 2025 versus $211.47 for the peer group.

Mix change and concentration risk are distinct measures here. Multi-family has fallen 3.5 percentage points of the book in six months, which is real. The absolute rent-regulated balances remain very large, and their loss content may be rising as the freeze takes effect. The numerator of risk is shrinking slowly while its severity is potentially increasing.

## Risk Assessment

**The October rent freeze is the defining risk, and it sits ahead of the reported numbers.** The 10-Q states plainly that repricing rent-regulated loans "are incurring debt service levels that, when combined with inflationary pressure on operating costs and limits on the ability to increase rental rates, approach or exceed some properties' net operating income." The freeze removes the one variable that could rescue a 1.0x-DSCR loan repricing from a ~4% coupon: revenue growth. The Q2 provision increase of $18M was attributed in part to the freeze, a first and modest recognition. A landlord suit filed July 22, 2026 in Staten Island State Court seeks to block the freeze; the outcome is unresolved. Mitigants are real but partial: NYC ≥50%-regulated collateral is $8.9B of a $26.9B multi-family book; the 2027 repricing cohort includes New Jersey $3.3B, Pennsylvania $2.7B, Florida $1.3B and Ohio $0.9B; and of the $1.6B of multi-family that reached repricing dates in 2026, ~93% is current or paid off.

**Reserve adequacy depends on stale appraisals.** The low ACL/non-accrual ratio of 31% is defensible precisely because $1,571M of non-accruals are collateral-dependent and already written down to appraised value less costs to sell. Those appraisals largely predate the June freeze. We found no independent market data on post-freeze rent-stabilized collateral values, which is a genuine verification gap.

**Classified assets remain large versus capital.** Criticized and classified loans derive to $11,886M from the vintage table (special mention $3,411M, substandard $5,675M, non-accrual $2,800M), against the earnings presentation's ~$11.6B — a definitional gap. Classified alone is 107% of CET1, down from ~124%. The allowance is 7.3% of criticized-and-classified balances.

**Section 382.** The company flags valuation-allowance risk on deferred tax assets from "aggregation risk of new shareholder share issuances and warrant exercises." After 2024-25 cumulative losses the attributes are meaningful; an ownership change triggered by warrant exercises outside management's control could force a write-down straight through capital. The DTA balance is not quantified in the sources reviewed.

**Legal and contingent items.** The consolidated securities class action motion to dismiss is fully briefed and awaiting decision, with derivative actions stayed behind it; the aggregate accrual is characterized as immaterial, which is an accounting judgment rather than an outcome prediction. The Signature/FDIC net settlement remains open more than three years on, and the company says it "could be material" in either direction. The *Kivett* interest-on-escrow judgment ($9.3M plus a forward injunction) is at certiorari stage with a circuit split now in place after the Second Circuit's May 2026 *Cantero* ruling, and the OCC's May 15, 2026 preemption rules cut in the bank's favor. Cyber-breach settlements have a final approval hearing October 1, 2026.

**Governance and disclosure texture.** Item 1A of the Q2 10-Q states there were no changes to risk factors from the FY2025 10-K — the rent freeze appears only in the forward-looking-statement list and MD&A credit narrative. A new $167M related-party loan exposure arose because a director joined the board of an existing borrower; the counterparty is unnamed, terms are described as substantially arm's-length. Neither item is a solvency issue; both argue for a higher information-risk premium.

**Capital-return executability.** With a -$958M accumulated deficit and losses in 2024-25, the national-bank dividend test (current-year net profits plus retained net profits of the two preceding years) is relevant. The 10-Q discloses the $250M authorization but not OCC approval status. Do not assume unconditional executability.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing calm, not stress. Near-term ATM IV of 28.0% is fair against 29.8% one-year historical (spread -1.9%), IV percentile within the chain is 22%, and term structure is flat (28.0% near versus 27.9% far), implying uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than event-concentrated. Put/call skew is balanced at 2.8% near-dated; the six-month tenor shows -4.0%, which we report without a directional claim given no stated sign convention. Put/call open interest rose from 0.57 to 0.85 since July 21 while ATM IV collapsed from 37.0% to 28.0% — post-earnings vol crush with a modest build in put open interest, on very thin volume (72 contracts across five expirations, P/C volume ratio 0.02).

Short interest is effectively nil: 37,893 shares, 0.0% of float, 0.0 days to cover. There is no squeeze support and no crowded bear positioning. Neither options nor short sellers are pricing the October rent-freeze effective date. That does not validate our concern, but it does mean the risk we identify is not already embedded in derivatives pricing, and it removes any positioning-driven support for the shares.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $18.00 (+26.1%)

The 2027 mechanics are individually credible: reprice $10.1B of low-coupon multi-family and CRE, reprice $20.5B of 3.90% CDs downward, retire $10.9B of borrowings at 4.37%, return part of $2.8B of non-accruals to earning status, and keep compounding C&I at H1's pace. CRE payoffs normalize toward the guided ~$1B per quarter, so the earning-asset drag that caused the NII cut reverses. Guidance is delivered: $800-900M of net income, 2.70-2.80% NIM, 10-10.5% ROATCE. At a 10.25% ROATCE and an 11% cost of equity, justified P/TBV of ~0.91x rises toward 1.02x as the workout completes and the issuer rating migrates to investment grade, giving roughly $18 on guided TBV of $17.50. The landlord suit vacates the rent freeze, removing the incremental credit overhang. This case matches the analyst high target and requires guidance credibility to be restored, which two consecutive cuts have damaged.

### Bear Case — $10.75 (-24.7%)

The October freeze forces re-appraisal of NYC rent-regulated collateral, exposing the 31% allowance-to-non-accrual ratio. Provision normalizes toward the $185M-per-half level implied by holding coverage flat, eliminating the reserve-release bridge and returning quarterly results to breakeven or loss. Modification volume keeps rising (from $19M to $401M per quarter already) with 37% of the modified book 90+ days delinquent. C&I charge-offs continue the 0.08% → 0.30% trajectory as growth-driven adverse selection surfaces. FY2026 lands below the reduced $225-300M range, FY2027 comes in nearer consensus $1.38 than guidance $1.65, and a ~$450M cumulative provision hit takes TBV toward $16.40. At 0.65x on a bank whose earnings power has been demonstrated inadequate, that is roughly $10.75 — close to the 52-week low of $10.57 and below the lowest analyst target of $13.

### Base Case — $15.00 (+5.1%)

Progress continues but slower than guided. FY2026 lands near or slightly below the low end of $225-300M; FY2027 splits the difference between consensus $1.38 and guidance $1.65 at roughly $1.45-1.50. The freeze produces measurable but manageable provision pressure spread over 2027, absorbed by capital rather than threatening it. The buyback executes and adds modestly to TBV/share. Diluted TBV drifts up toward $15.75 and the multiple stabilizes near 0.86x basic tangible book (~0.95x diluted). That is $15.00.

Probability-weighted at 25% bull / 45% base / 30% bear: +6.5% + 2.3% - 7.4% = **+1.4%**, well below a fair required return for a sub-investment-grade bank mid-workout. The asymmetry is the problem more than the point estimate: the success case delivers flat TBV/share by management's own guidance, so upside depends entirely on multiple expansion, while the failure case erodes the book that anchors the downside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Timeframe for the thesis to resolve: two to three quarters, anchored to the October 2026 freeze effective date, the Q3 print (late October) and the Q4/FY2026 print (late January 2027).

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength rather than waiting for the Q3 print. The stock is 7.6% below its 52-week high with next-year estimates down 8% in thirty days; the October freeze and the Q3 provision decision arrive within roughly ten weeks. For holders who want residual exposure to the 2027 mechanics, retain no more than a half-weight position and size it so a move to $10.75 is tolerable. Do not add on the 0.81x book headline — the diluted figure is 0.93x.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD (any two):**
1. Q3 2026 provision of at least $90M with ACL/loans stable at or above 1.42% *and* reported net income positive — demonstrating profitability without further reserve release.
2. Q3 NII of $480M or better, putting the $1.86-1.96B FY range within reach and ending the pattern of cuts.
3. Next-year consensus stabilizing (30-day revision of -3% or better) for two consecutive months.
4. Disclosed post-freeze appraisal refresh on the NYC rent-regulated book showing collateral values within 10% of carrying assumptions.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE:** all of the above plus either a price at or below $12.00 (roughly 0.78x diluted TBV) with fundamentals intact, or clustered open-market purchases by three or more C-suite executives — a signal conspicuously absent from an eighteen-month Form 4 record dominated by grants and tax-withholding dispositions, whose only open-market-coded purchase was 95 shares at $12.85.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID (any one):**
1. Q3 or Q4 2026 provision exceeding $250M in a single quarter, or ACL/non-accruals falling below 28%.
2. A third FY2026 guidance cut, or any downward revision to the FY2027 net income range of $800-900M.
3. Total non-accruals returning above $3.2B, or 90+ days past due and still accruing exceeding $150M.
4. A Section 382 ownership change forcing a DTA valuation allowance, or suspension/withdrawal of the $250M repurchase authorization.
5. C&I net charge-offs exceeding 0.60% annualized in any quarter.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next scheduled review at the Q3 2026 release in late October, which will be the first print to carry any credit effect from the October 2026 freeze effective date. An intra-quarter reassessment is warranted if the Staten Island litigation is decided, if the securities class action motion to dismiss is ruled on, or if the FDIC/Signature net settlement is quantified.

## Conclusion

Flagstar is a genuine turnaround with a genuine problem. Capital, liquidity, funding mix, delinquencies and the loan fair-value mark have all improved materially, and the C&I build is producing measurable volume. But the reported return to profitability rests on a $161M H1 allowance release, H1 net charge-offs exceeded pre-provision pre-tax revenue, and the guidance that justifies the equity has been cut twice this year with consensus now roughly 16% below the 2027 EPS guide and falling.

The valuation does not compensate. On reported diluted shares the stock trades at 0.93x tangible book, not 0.81x, and management's own 2027 guidance shows TBV/share flat versus today because the net-settled warrant structure adds shares as the price rises. That leaves multiple expansion as the sole return source in the success case, against tangible-book erosion in the failure case, with the October rent freeze arriving before the appraisals that support the current allowance have been refreshed. Probability-weighted expected return of roughly +1.4% is below fair for the risk profile.

This is the fifth consecutive review, and the prior four HOLDs were supported by an improving trajectory. The July 24 guidance cut, the 20% thirty-day estimate reduction, and the August 6 10-Q disclosures on reserve release and modification redefaults are new information that reverses part of that trajectory. Scores fall to 4.4 overall and the rating moves to **REDUCE**. Existing owners should trim into the current price; prospective investors should wait for either $12.00 or evidence that Flagstar can earn without releasing reserves.
