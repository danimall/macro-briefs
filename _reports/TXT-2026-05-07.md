## Executive Summary

The April 30, 2026 announcement to separate the Industrial segment (Kautex + Textron Specialized Vehicles) within 12-18 months transforms Textron from a multi-industry conglomerate into a ~$12B pure-play aerospace & defense business. This is the specific catalyst that justifies a BUY at current levels: the remaining A&D company generates approximately 10-11% segment operating margins vs. Industrial's 4.5%, and the conglomerate discount — documented by Textron's TSR of 181.1 vs. peer group's 237.6 — should narrow as the market re-rates the portfolio.

At $92.37, Textron trades at ~14.2x FY2026E EPS of $6.51 and ~12.7x FY2027E EPS of $7.30. Comparable pure-play defense companies trade materially higher than Textron's current multiple. The Industrial segment removal — whether via sale generating cash or tax-free spinoff creating two focused entities — should unlock 15-30% of value over the next 12-18 months, subject to customary conditions and board approval.

Q1 2026 delivered adjusted EPS of $1.45 (GAAP EPS of $1.25), beating estimates. Textron Aviation revenue grew 22% YoY in Q1, Bell continues ramping the MV-75 FLRAA program, and Textron Systems backlog surged 27% to $3.6B. The combination of organic execution, a timed separation catalyst, and a credible path to multiple expansion makes this an actionable opportunity.

## Company Overview

Textron operates across six segments (transitioning to five in FY2026) with $14.8B in FY2025 revenue:

- **Textron Aviation** (40% of revenue): A leader in general aviation — Cessna and Beechcraft business jets, turboprops, and pistons, plus aftermarket services. $7.7B backlog.
- **Bell** (29%): Military and commercial helicopters and tiltrotors. The MV-75 (FLRAA) program — sole-source replacement for the Army's Black Hawk fleet — is the company's largest growth driver.
- **Industrial** (22%): Kautex automotive fuel systems/ADAS components and Textron Specialized Vehicles (E-Z-GO golf cars, TUG ground support). Announced for separation.
- **Textron Systems** (8%): Unmanned systems, Ship-to-Shore Connectors, electronic warfare, ATAC live training. $3.6B backlog (+27% YoY).
- **eAviation** (<1%): Pipistrel electric aircraft. Being folded into other segments.

Total backlog: $18.8B at year-end 2025, representing approximately 1.3x annual revenue. Backlog conversion timing varies by segment and program, and Textron's definition excludes unexercised contract options and potential orders under IDIQ contracts.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Trajectory:**
- FY2023: $13.68B (+6.3%)
- FY2024: $13.70B (+0.1%, strike-impacted)
- FY2025: $14.80B (+8.0%)
- FY2026 Guidance: ~$15.5B (+5%)

The FY2024 stall was attributable to a one-month Textron Aviation strike. Recovery is complete — Aviation delivered 171 jets and 146 turboprops in FY2025 vs. 151/127 in the strike-affected year.

**Profitability by Segment (FY2025):**
| Segment | Revenue | Margin | YoY Profit Change |
|---------|---------|--------|-------------------|
| Aviation | $5.96B | 11.7% | +23% |
| Bell | $4.28B | 8.5% | -2% |
| Systems | $1.25B | 14.0% | +14% |
| Industrial | $3.21B | 4.5% | -4% |

The margin disparity illustrates the separation thesis: removing Industrial's 4.5% margin from a portfolio where A&D segments average approximately 10.7% improves the consolidated segment margin mix by approximately 120-130 bps. Actual post-separation consolidated margins will depend on stranded corporate costs, transaction structure, and allocation of shared costs.

**Cash Flow and Balance Sheet:**
- Operating cash flow: $1.31B (FY2025)
- Free cash flow: $930M (6.3% FCF margin, >100% conversion of net income)
- Net debt: ~$1.85B ($3.88B debt less $2.0B cash)
- Net debt/EBITDA: approximately 1.1-1.3x (depending on EBITDA definition and treatment of finance segment debt)
- $1.0B undrawn revolver (expires 2030)

Capital allocation is aggressive: $822M in buybacks in FY2025 (retiring 10.7M shares at ~$77 average), with a new 25M share authorization (~15% of outstanding). Minimal dividend ($18M) preserves flexibility.

**Earnings Quality:**
Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.45 beat estimates, continuing a trend of positive earnings surprises. This consistency provides confidence in forward estimates.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term Growth Drivers (FY2026-2027):**

1. **MV-75 FLRAA Ramp:** Bell's military revenue grew 28% in FY2025 on prototype/development work. LRIP contract award expected late 2026/early 2027. The program's addressable market is expanding — Marine Corps evaluating MV-75 as attack helicopter replacement, and Bell signed an MoU with Korea Aerospace Industries for the South Korean HSMUH program.

2. **Textron Aviation New Products:** Beechcraft Denali (FAA engine certification Feb 2025, service entry 2026) and next-gen Citation jets (Gen3 line, expected 2027) refresh the product lineup.

3. **Textron Systems Backlog Acceleration:** The $3.6B backlog is driven by $480M Ship-to-Shore Connector and $475M ATAC Navy training contracts, providing 2.5+ years of revenue visibility.

4. **Post-Separation Multiple Expansion:** The remaining A&D entity would carry ~$12B revenue at ~10%+ consolidated margins, positioning for re-rating from current ~14x forward to 17-20x.

**Estimate Revisions:** FY2026E at $6.51 (+0.8% over 30 days, +0.3% over 90 days). FY2027E at $7.30. Revisions are modestly positive but not yet reflecting the full separation benefit. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $7.30 implies 12% YoY growth, which appears conservative given MV-75 LRIP initiation and aviation product launches.

**Risks to Growth:** Bell margin compression from MV-75 development (currently 8.5% vs. historical 10%+) will persist through FY2027 with an expected $60-110M unfavorable catch-up adjustment. This is telegraphed and quantified but will create a headline earnings headwind.

## Valuation Assessment

At $92.37, Textron trades at:
- 14.2x FY2026E EPS ($6.51)
- 12.7x FY2027E EPS ($7.30)
- ~9x EV/EBITDA (estimated)

**Peer Context:** Pure-play defense companies generally trade at higher multiples than Textron's current valuation. Comparable names such as HII and LHX have recently traded in the low-to-mid 20s and high 20s to low 30s on a forward P/E basis, respectively. Textron at 14.2x reflects a meaningful discount, though the remaining business would still carry some cyclicality from business aviation and Bell margin pressure that partially justifies a lower multiple than pure defense primes.

**Sum-of-Parts Framework:**
- A&D segments (~$11.5B revenue, ~$1.23B segment profit): At 18x segment profit = ~$22.2B value. Note: segment profit is pre-corporate costs, pre-interest, and pre-tax, so appropriate enterprise-level adjustments for ~$200M+ in corporate/shared costs and taxes are required.
- Industrial segment (~$3.2B revenue, $145M profit): At 10-12x = $1.5-1.7B value
- Less net debt (~$1.9B) and estimated corporate/tax adjustments

After adjusting for corporate costs, taxes, and separation-related expenses, the SOTP framework suggests equity value in the range of approximately $120-130 per share, with meaningful sensitivity to the assumed multiple and corporate cost allocation. This represents 30-40% upside from current levels under favorable assumptions, though investors should note the wide range of potential outcomes.

**Why the Market May Be Wrong:** Conglomerate discounts are persistent until a specific catalyst removes them. The separation announcement on April 30 is that catalyst. The market has partially repriced (stock jumped from ~$90 to $96 on the news) but has since settled back to $92 as broader market volatility obscured the signal. The 12-18 month separation timeline means the full re-rating has not yet occurred.

## Competitive Landscape

**Textron Aviation:** Broadest product range in general aviation from pistons through super-midsize jets. Competitors (Embraer, Gulfstream, Dassault) tend to focus on specific segments. The Denali and Gen3 Citations maintain product freshness. Aviation backlog declined 2% ($7.7B vs. $7.8B), suggesting the jet order cycle may be plateauing — worth monitoring but not alarming given still-elevated levels.

**Bell:** Sole-source on MV-75 FLRAA, the Army's highest-priority aviation program. No realistic competitor for production once LRIP begins. Commercial helicopter market is competitive (Airbus, Leonardo, Sikorsky) but Bell maintains a strong position in light-medium rotorcraft.

**Textron Systems:** Niche positions in unmanned systems and Ship-to-Shore Connectors provide program-of-record stability. ATAC holds important Navy adversary air training contracts, including the recent $475M award, though it competes with other commercial providers such as Draken International and Tactical Air Support in the broader adversary services market.

## Risk Assessment

**1. MV-75 Execution Risk (High):** A $60-110M catch-up charge in late 2026/early 2027 is explicitly disclosed and will depress Bell's reported margins. Program delays or cost growth beyond this estimate would be materially negative. The program represents ~27% of total U.S. Government revenue exposure.

**2. Industrial Separation Execution (Moderate):** The 12-18 month timeline introduces uncertainty — potential for below-expected sale price, tax leakage on spinoff, or management distraction. Textron's 8-K explicitly states "there can be no assurance regarding the ultimate timing or structure of the proposed separation or that a transaction will be completed." However, the formal announcement, board authorization, and management commentary suggest strong intent to execute.

**3. Tariff/Trade Policy (Moderate):** USMCA's mandatory 2026 six-year review creates uncertainty for Canadian/Mexican-sourced aviation components. Loss of preferential treatment would increase costs. Management states tariffs have not been materially adverse to date and refund processes are underway.

**4. Defense Budget Sensitivity:** A new Executive Order regarding defense contractor underperformance and potential restrictions on dividends/buybacks introduces policy uncertainty. However, FLRAA is a program of record with bipartisan support.

**5. Aviation Cycle Plateau:** Slight backlog decline (-2%) at Aviation could signal peak demand. A recession would pressure business jet orders.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($115-130, 25-40% upside):**
- Industrial separation completes at attractive valuation (12x+ segment earnings, or ~$1.7B+ cash proceeds)
- Remaining A&D entity re-rates to 18-20x forward earnings
- MV-75 LRIP contract awarded without excess cost growth
- FY2027 EPS of $7.30+ achievable, potentially higher as MV-75 production margins improve
- International MV-75 orders (South Korea, others) extend revenue visibility
- Aviation product launches (Denali, Gen3 Citations) sustain demand

**Bear Case ($75-82, 12-19% downside):**
- Recession depresses business jet orders; Aviation backlog declines further
- MV-75 cost overruns exceed $110M catch-up estimate
- Industrial separation delays, is cancelled, or produces below-expected value
- Tariff disruption from USMCA review increases aviation manufacturing costs
- Bell margin compression persists longer than expected
- Defense EO restricts capital returns

**Base Case ($105-110, 14-19% upside):**
The separation proceeds on schedule, market gradually re-rates the pure-play A&D entity toward 15-16x FY2027E EPS of $7.30 = $110-117, with some discount for MV-75 execution risk and cyclicality.

## Conclusion

Textron at $92.37 offers a specific, timed catalyst (Industrial separation targeted within 12-18 months) that should narrow a documented conglomerate discount. The company trades at 14.2x forward EPS vs. A&D peers generally in the 20-30x range. Consistent earnings execution, an $18.8B backlog, $930M in annual free cash flow, and director Kennedy's $989K open-market purchase at $95.98 provide fundamental support for the thesis.

The primary risk — MV-75 execution and associated margin compression at Bell — is quantified ($60-110M catch-up) and temporary (development phase). The secondary risk — aviation cyclicality in a potential recession — is partially mitigated by backlog visibility and defense revenue diversification.

The market has not yet fully priced the transition to a pure-play A&D entity, creating asymmetric upside for patient holders through separation completion.

**Recommendation: BUY** — 2-3% position sizing, targeting 12-18 month holding period.
