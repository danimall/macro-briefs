**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Legence is a mechanical, electrical & plumbing (MEP) engineering and installation contractor that has repositioned itself over the past two years as a direct beneficiary of the data-center build-out. Q1 2026 revenue reached $1,037.9M, up 105.1% year-over-year, with data centers and technology now representing 62% of revenue versus 40% a year earlier. Total backlog and awarded contracts stand at $5.38B, roughly double the prior-year $2.64B, and consensus EPS estimates have been revised up 38% over the past 90 days. The company's fundamentals are inflecting sharply upward, and the estimate-revision velocity is among the stronger signals in the industrials complex.

Legence carries an unusually dense stack of company-specific risks that sit alongside that momentum. Revenue concentration is intensifying rather than diversifying: a single Installation & Maintenance customer went from 6.2% to 17.5% of revenue in one year. The company reports revenue on a fixed-price, cost-to-cost percentage-of-completion basis while disclosing unremediated material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting, including insufficient journal-entry review and inadequate segregation of duties. It carries roughly $1B of floating-rate term debt, a $217M Tax Receivable Agreement liability owed to Blackstone and management that begins paying out in about a year, and a controlling sponsor (Blackstone, ~61% voting) that has sold a substantial amount of stock across three secondary offerings in five months (the April tranche alone ~$831M in proceeds). Management is receiving equity grants, not buying in the open market.

The Q1'26 GAAP net income of $17.4M was materially flattered by a one-time $20.3M tax benefit from a valuation-allowance release; pre-tax income was only $4.0M. The operating story is genuine (Adjusted EBITDA +132%, operating cash flow +$91M), but the headline overstates underlying earnings power. Weighing the real growth against the governance, control-quality, and concentration overhangs, and a forward multiple that already prices continued execution, we rate LGN a HOLD with a positive growth bias, and would look to upgrade on demonstrated remediation progress or a pullback that widens the margin of safety.

## Company Overview

Legence provides engineering, installation, and maintenance services for mission-critical building systems, concentrating deliberately on technically demanding verticals: data centers and technology, life sciences and healthcare, education, and government. It contracts both directly with owners and as a subcontractor to general contractors on larger projects.

The business runs in two reportable segments. Installation & Maintenance (~84% of Q1'26 revenue) combines design-build and plan-and-spec MEP installation with in-house fabrication, plus recurring preventive and emergency maintenance under typical 1–5 year cost-plus agreements. This is the growth engine. Engineering & Consulting (~16% of revenue) covers engineering, design, and program/project management including energy-savings performance contracts, higher-margin but slower-growing.

Operationally, Legence functions as a federated "house of brands," with decision-making decentralized to individual acquired companies and no single centralized ERP system. Management flags this structure as a source of operational risk, and it is directly implicated in the company's control weaknesses. Services are primarily fixed-price, exposing Legence to cost-estimation and overrun risk. The company IPO'd on September 15, 2025, out of a Blackstone-controlled UP-C structure with dual-class stock.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue of $1,037.9M rose 105.1% from $506.0M, split roughly evenly between organic growth (data-center-driven Installation & Fabrication) and the Bowers acquisition, which closed January 2, 2026. Gross profit was $186.2M, a 17.9% margin, down 420 basis points from 22.1% a year earlier. That compression reflects mix shift toward lower-margin large installation projects with higher subcontractor content; Engineering & Consulting gross margin also fell sharply to 30.4% from 41.0%.

Despite the gross-margin decline, Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 11.4% from 10.0% ($118.1M vs. $50.8M), because SG&A leverage improved to 11.2% of revenue from 13.7%. This is a meaningful operating-leverage signal: scale is converting into fixed-cost absorption even as project mix dilutes gross margin.

The GAAP profit picture requires normalization. Net income of $17.4M compared to a $19.1M loss a year earlier, but the swing was primarily the tax benefit from releasing a valuation allowance on TRA-related deferred tax assets. Diluted EPS was $0.13 on this basis. Operating cash flow of $120.1M (vs. $29.5M) was strong, though aided by favorable contract-liability timing. The underlying earnings power sits well below the GAAP headline.

Demand signals are robust. Backlog alone is $4.2B in remaining performance obligations. Book-to-bill was 1.2x, down from 1.4x but still expansionary, and Installation & Maintenance backlog grew to $3.54B from $1.24B. Roughly 65–75% of RPO is expected to convert within 12 months. Surety bonds outstanding jumped to $1,562M from $561M at year-end 2025, consistent with the surge in large-project bonded work.

The balance sheet is the counterweight. The term loan grew $200M in January 2026 (to fund Bowers) to $995.3M, matures December 2031, and is floating (SOFR-based) with ~$785M notional interest-rate swaps in place. Total debt is ~$1.03B against $244.6M cash and ~$169M revolver availability. A 100bp rate move adds roughly $10M of annual interest, partially hedged. The reported Altman Z-Score near 1.5 and D/E near 4.8 look alarming in isolation, but they are largely artifacts of a post-LBO capital structure and heavy goodwill/intangibles ($842M goodwill + $843M intangibles = ~48% of $3.48B assets), not operating distress; the company remains in compliance with a springing 8.5x first-lien net leverage covenant that is not currently tested. The leverage is real, the debt is floating and only partially hedged (swaps begin rolling to new tranches at end-2026), and covenant capacity permits substantial additional borrowing.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed: a 208% beat in Q3'25, then a large miss in Q4'25 (-$0.39 vs. +$0.05 estimate), then a -29% miss in Q1'26 (+$0.13 vs. +$0.18). Visibility into quarterly EPS has been poor even as the annual trajectory improves, which is consistent with lumpy percentage-of-completion revenue recognition and elevated acquisition-accounting noise.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is the strongest part of the story and rests on a broad, confirmable industry tailwind. Data-center MEP demand is visible across the infrastructure complex; hyperscaler capex commitments and the data-center/AI build-out all point to a durable multi-year cycle at the facility level. Legence is a picks-and-shovels beneficiary: it builds and services the HVAC, process-cooling, and MEP systems these facilities require, without owning the underlying data centers or AI workloads.

The estimate-revision data supports the inflection. Current-year consensus EPS has moved to $1.46 from $1.06 ninety days ago (+37.7%), next-year to $1.78 from $1.38 (+28.6%), and current-quarter to $0.33 from $0.22 (+48.5%). Revisions have stabilized over the trailing 30 days but the 90-day velocity clears the threshold that historically precedes continued re-rating. Combined with revenue growth above 100%, this is a genuine fundamental-inflection signal rather than multiple-driven noise, and we weight it as a positive input to both growth and (with caveats) valuation.

The durability question centers on end-market concentration rather than the existence of demand. With 62% of revenue and the bulk of backlog growth tied to data centers, Legence's forward growth is a leveraged bet on the hyperscaler/colocation capex cycle continuing. A capex pause would hit revenue, margins, and backlog conversion simultaneously and hard.

Growth is also acquisition-fueled (Bowers $427M, Metrix $33M in Q1'26 alone), which sustains the top line but adds integration risk, purchase-accounting drag (Bowers posted a $9.2M net loss in the period on acquisition costs and amortization), goodwill-impairment exposure, and recurring dilution when deals are part-funded with stock. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act removes the Section 179D energy-efficient building deduction for construction beginning after June 30, 2026, a modest headwind to the incentive-driven portion of demand, though the core data-center thesis does not depend on it.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is the analytically hardest dimension here and the one where the standard "cheap equals buy" reflex is most dangerous. On trailing metrics the stock is meaningless (trailing EPS is negative). On forward consensus of $1.46 for the current year, LGN at $70.80 trades at ~48x current-year and ~40x next-year EPS ($1.78). Even on the accelerating estimate base, this is a premium multiple, and it sits near the top of the guardrail range where rising estimates alone no longer justify a buy signal; at ~40-48x forward, beats are largely expected and misses are punished asymmetrically.

Peer context is genuinely difficult because the Engineering & Construction comp set is heterogeneous: ROAD at 46.4x, IESC at 39.0x, FER at 47.5x. On a headline P/E basis, LGN is broadly in line with peers, and the data-center-weighted growth rate arguably supports the upper end. But the peer P/Es are themselves elevated on the same thematic optimism, so proximity to the peer median is not independent confirmation of value; it reflects a cohort re-rating that is vulnerable to any deceleration in the underlying capex cycle.

Two facts temper the bull case on price. First, the controlling sponsor sold ~15.4M shares at $54 as recently as April 9, 2026; the stock now trades ~31% above that price, meaning public buyers are paying a substantial premium to where the best-informed holder was monetizing months ago. Second, the Q1'26 profit was flattered by a one-time tax item, so the "E" underneath the forward multiple carries more estimation risk than a clean operating base would.

Analyst targets bracket a wide range: low $80, high $125, mean $105.7, median $105. On an inflection stock with reasonable-to-rich valuation, targets often lag reality, but at ~40-48x forward, the targets and the price both embed the same growth extrapolation, so we treat the consensus as directional support rather than a firm floor. The setup offers upside if data-center backlog converts and margins hold, but the margin of safety is thin, and a low valuation score is warranted because the price already discounts strong execution while the risk stack (control weaknesses, concentration, TRA drag) is not priced.

## Competitive Landscape

Legence operates in a highly fragmented, competitive MEP and engineering-and-construction market with competitors of varying size and cost structure (union versus non-union). Its differentiation is scale combined with deliberate concentration in technically demanding, secular-growth verticals, plus in-house fabrication capacity and a recurring maintenance base that provides a degree of revenue stability.

The competitive trajectory is improving on the metrics that matter for trajectory scoring: segment revenue is growing well ahead of the industry, backlog has doubled, and the company is consolidating a fragmented market through acquisition. The federated house-of-brands model preserves local relationships and technical expertise while adding scale in bonding capacity and fabrication, a real advantage on large data-center projects where surety capacity and delivery reliability are gating factors.

The moat is moderate rather than deep. MEP contracting has low switching costs on a project basis, and the primary competitive lever, technical execution on complex, time-critical builds, is defensible but not proprietary. The strongest structural advantage is the recurring maintenance annuity attached to installed base, which grows with each completed project. The competitive position is improving, but it is tethered to a single end-market wave, and the same concentration that drives share gains today would amplify share and margin pressure if the data-center cycle turns.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is where Legence diverges most from a clean growth story, and several of these risks are specific, currently unresolved, and not obviously priced.

**Data-center concentration.** 62% of revenue and the bulk of backlog ride the data-center capex cycle, with a single customer now 17.5% of revenue (up from 6.2%). This is a leveraged bet on hyperscaler spending; a capex pause would hit revenue, margin mix, and backlog conversion at once.

**Unremediated ICFR material weaknesses.** The 10-K and Q1'26 10-Q both disclose material weaknesses in general IT controls and in COSO information/communication and control activities, including insufficient journal-entry review and personnel who can both prepare and post entries. Disclosure controls were deemed not effective as of 3/31/26. Paired with fixed-price, cost-to-cost percentage-of-completion accounting (where management estimates drive recognized profit and prior profit can reverse near completion), this is a genuine restatement/estimation red flag. Auditor 404(b) attestation begins with the FY2026 10-K, a hard-dated catalyst that could resolve favorably or surface further issues.

**Leverage and floating-rate exposure.** ~$1B term loan, floating and only partially swapped, with swaps beginning to roll at end-2026. Covenant capacity permits substantial additional debt, and the acquisitive model will keep drawing on it.

**Tax Receivable Agreement.** ~$217M owed to Blackstone/management, payments starting late 2026/early 2027, with a change-of-control acceleration clause. This diverts cash from reinvestment and functions as a takeover deterrent that benefits the sponsor over public holders.

**Sponsor overhang and governance.** Blackstone controls ~61% of voting power (controlled-company status) and has sold across three offerings in five months, with remaining sponsor shares pledged under Goldman margin loans, creating both persistent float supply and tail forced-sale risk. Related-party arrangements are dense (portions of the term loan held by Blackstone affiliates, several leases with management/parent-owned entities, promissory notes to former owners).

**Goodwill and dilution.** Recurring impairments ($25M in 2025, $17.8M in 2024) against ~$1.7B goodwill+intangibles, plus a $50M Bowers deferred-consideration payment due 12/31/26 settleable in cash or stock. ~64% of employees are under collective bargaining agreements with multiemployer pension withdrawal-liability exposure ($75.6M contributed in 2025).

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress. ATM implied volatility of 101.0% sits well above 1-year historical volatility of 70.0% (a 31-point spread, 81st percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (101.0% near-term vs. 82.9% at six months), which typically signals an anticipated catalyst. Put/call skew of 6.8% shows elevated put demand, and the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±22.4%. Since the prior snapshot, ATM IV jumped 26.6 points while the stock fell 19.7% (from $88.13 to $70.80), and the put/call OI ratio rose from 0.00 to 0.83. This confirms the fundamental caution: the market is pricing elevated uncertainty and downside protection demand into LGN, consistent with the control-quality, concentration, and leverage overhangs. For a prospective buyer, rich IV argues against paying up for near-term optionality and favors patience on entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$105–125).** The data-center capex cycle proves durable and broad-based, backlog converts at the 65–75%-within-12-months pace, and operating leverage continues to expand EBITDA margin even as project mix dilutes gross margin. Estimate revisions keep climbing as consensus catches up to the inflection, the material weaknesses are remediated ahead of the FY2026 404(b) attestation, and the sponsor's remaining stake is absorbed without a forced-sale event. At $1.78+ next-year EPS growing further the following year, the stock re-rates toward the high end of analyst targets, implying $105–125.

**Bear case (~$45–55).** Hyperscaler capex decelerates or a large customer defers, hitting the concentrated revenue base and compressing backlog conversion. The FY2026 404(b) attestation surfaces additional control deficiencies or forces a restatement given the cost-to-cost accounting exposure. Rising floating-rate interest (as swaps roll) and the TRA payment starting in ~1 year pressure free cash flow, and a Blackstone forced-sale or continued monetization overhangs the float. Multiple compresses toward 25x on a stalled or reversed earnings trajectory, implying $45–55, a 22–36% decline from current levels.

**Base case (~$85).** Data-center demand holds, backlog converts steadily, and next-year EPS lands near the $1.78 consensus with modest upside. The stock trades at ~40x that base, roughly in line with the peer cohort, with the control-remediation and sponsor-overhang risks partially resolved but not fully cleared. This implies ~$85, or roughly 20% above the current price, but with a wide distribution around it given the risk stack.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a positive 12-month growth bias (growth_hold). The fundamental inflection is real and the estimate-revision signal is strong, but the combination of a rich forward multiple, an unremediated ICFR material weakness paired with percentage-of-completion accounting, single-customer concentration, and a controlling sponsor that is a steady seller keeps the risk/reward roughly balanced rather than asymmetric. The reassessment timeline centers on the next 2–4 quarters, with the FY2026 10-K and its first 404(b) auditor attestation as the decisive event.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FY2026 404(b) attestation confirms remediation of the material weaknesses with no restatement or new deficiencies.
- Data-center backlog conversion sustains book-to-bill at or above 1.2x with the top-customer concentration stabilizing or declining below ~15% of revenue.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s (near the April sponsor sale price) that widens the margin of safety on the ~$1.78 forward EPS base.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any disclosed restatement, going-concern language, or expanded material-weakness scope in the FY2026 filing.
- A quarter showing data-center revenue growth decelerating below ~20% YoY or a book-to-bill falling below 1.0x, signaling a capex-cycle turn.
- A capital-allocation shift or a Blackstone forced-sale event that materially pressures the float, or a cut to consensus estimates of >10% over a 90-day window.

**Reassessment point:** Next quarterly earnings, and definitively at the FY2026 10-K / 404(b) attestation.

## Conclusion

Legence is a legitimate high-growth beneficiary of the data-center build-out, with expanding EBITDA margins and confirmed, broad-based end-market demand that argue for constructive positioning. The company also carries a denser-than-usual stack of company-specific risks that are not obviously reflected in a ~40-48x forward multiple: intensifying single-customer and end-market concentration, an unremediated internal-control material weakness colliding with percentage-of-completion accounting, ~$1B of partially-hedged floating-rate leverage plus a $217M TRA cash drag, and a controlling sponsor that has been a repeated seller across three offerings in five months while remaining a majority holder. The Q1'26 GAAP profit was flattered by a one-time tax benefit; normalized operating earnings are thinner than the headline.

We rate LGN a HOLD. The growth is real enough to preclude an AVOID, but the governance, control-quality, and concentration overhangs, plus a price that already discounts strong execution and sits well above where the best-informed insider was selling, preclude a BUY. We would move constructive on demonstrated remediation ahead of the FY2026 404(b) attestation or on a pullback that restores a margin of safety, and negative on any restatement, capex-cycle deceleration, or sponsor forced-sale event.
