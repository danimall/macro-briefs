**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Range Resources is a pure-play Appalachian (Marcellus) natural gas, NGL, and condensate producer trading at roughly 10.2x trailing earnings and a ~$9.0B market cap. The Q1 2026 print was strong on the surface: net income of $341.6M ($1.44 diluted EPS) versus $97M ($0.40) a year earlier, operating cash flow of $619M (+88% YoY), and revenue of $1,034M (+28%). Almost all of that improvement came from price, not volume. NYMEX gas averaged $4.97/mcf in the quarter versus $3.66 a year earlier, and realized all-in pricing rose to $3.21/mcfe from $2.48, while production was essentially flat at 198.7 Bcfe. The operating leverage that more than tripled earnings on a winter price spike works just as forcefully in reverse, and only ~35% of remaining 2026 gas is hedged (swaps near $4.06 plus three-way collars).

The forward case rests on two pillars that are both real and both uncertain. Estimate revisions are rising — current-year consensus EPS climbed +23.2% over 90 days to $4.31, and next-year to $4.66 — and the stock trades below most published targets (mean ~$47, median $46) and well under its peer-group median P/E of 32.5. Against that, the company carries the most idiosyncratic regulatory risk in its peer set: its entire inventory is concentrated in Pennsylvania, where a December 2025 citizen petition for a statewide setback rule and the pending Cecil Township setback litigation could constrain drillable acreage in a way diversified peers do not face. A January 2026 capital-structure shift traded fixed-rate debt for floating-rate revolver borrowings and drew the cash balance to near zero.

We rate RRC a HOLD. The estimate-revision momentum and discount to peers argue against being short or avoiding the name, but the earnings base is a largely-unhedged bet on a volatile gas strip that spiked in winter and was struggling to hold above $3 by late spring. With analyst sentiment degrading at the margin, elevated short interest of 10.7%, and a regulatory tail that is genuinely existential to inventory location, the risk/reward is balanced at ~$38. We need either a confirmed structural gas-price floor or resolution of the Pennsylvania setback question to move to a more committed call.

## Company Overview

Range Resources operates as a single reporting segment focused on the Marcellus Shale in southwest Pennsylvania, where it was one of the original developers of the play. The asset base is its defining feature: roughly 18 Tcfe of proved reserves with a 50-plus-year inventory life and a low base-decline rate relative to peers. That long-life, low-decline profile reduces the capital intensity required to hold production flat, which is the operating model the company runs today: maintenance capex (~$168M in Q1) against ~2.2 Bcfe/day of production rather than a growth program.

The product mix by year-end 2025 proved reserves is approximately 65% natural gas, 34% NGLs, and 1% oil. The large liquids cut differentiates RRC from dry-gas Appalachian peers and gives it exposure to propane, ethane, and condensate pricing tied to oil and export markets. Strategy is returns-focused: development funded from operating cash flow, a growing dividend (raised 11% to $0.10/quarter in February 2026), and an enlarged $1.5B buyback authorization, all anchored by an explicit balance-sheet-first philosophy. Net debt is the company's self-selected key performance metric, which carries through into executive compensation.

CEO Dennis Degner leads the company following the transition from Jeff Ventura to Executive Chairman. The stock trades on the NYSE with ~235.6M shares outstanding.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 delivered revenue of approximately $3.0B (+27% YoY) and net income of $658M, more than double the $266M reported in 2024, on higher realized gas prices. The PV-10 of reserves more than doubled to ~$11.6B on higher SEC reference pricing, a function of the price deck rather than reserve additions.

Q1 2026 extended that momentum. Natural gas, NGL, and oil sales reached $1,010M (+28% YoY); total revenue $1,034M; net income $341.6M, a 3.5x increase almost entirely attributable to the move in NYMEX gas. Operating cash flow of $619M against ~$168M of capex generated robust free cash flow before the legacy obligations discussed below.

The per-unit cost structure is competitive. For Q1 2026 (per mcfe): direct operating $0.14, transportation/gathering/processing/compression (T&G&P&C) $1.63, G&A $0.23, DD&A $0.45, and interest $0.10 (down 33% YoY). T&G&P&C is the dominant cost line and is rising on higher electricity and fuel rates, reflecting the reality of Appalachian takeaway economics rather than a company-specific problem.

The most consequential recent change is in capital structure. Total debt fell to $834M at 3/31/2026 from ~$1.2B at year-end 2025, now comprising $500M of 4.75% notes due 2030 plus $334M drawn on the revolver. In January 2026 the company fully redeemed its $600M 8.25% senior notes due 2029 at 101.375%, funded by revolver borrowings, recognizing a $12.3M early-extinguishment loss. The economics are sound: trading 8.25% fixed for roughly 5.4% floating revolver debt cut interest expense by a third. The trade-offs are that RRC moved from a near-net-cash position a year ago ($304M cash, zero revolver) to a drawn revolver and only $247K of cash, and it now carries floating-rate exposure of roughly $3.3M of annual interest per 1% rate move. Liquidity remains ample at ~$1.5B available against a $3.0B borrowing base and $2.0B of commitments reaffirmed in March 2026.

Two recurring drags depress reported free cash flow relative to clean E&P economics. A retained North Louisiana divestiture obligation of ~$260M for gathering/transportation commitments runs through 2030, costing roughly $25M per quarter (~$25M paid in Q1). Basis differentials are hedged out to December 2030 via swaps currently carrying a $12.8M liability.

Leverage is low and falling — net debt of ~$0.8B against ~$4.6B equity and ~$2.5B of annualized operating cash flow — with strong interest coverage. The financial-health signals are improving (rising margins, lower leverage, positive FCF), which supports a financial-health score in the high-6s. The cash-balance drawdown and new floating-rate sensitivity are the offsets that keep it from a 7-plus.

## Growth Analysis

This is where the bull and bear cases diverge most sharply, and where the data demands care. The headline revenue and earnings growth rates are real but price-driven, not volume-driven. Production was flat YoY in Q1, and the company runs a deliberate maintenance model. There is no organic volume-growth engine here; the "growth" is the gas strip applied to a stable production base.

Estimate revisions are genuinely positive and worth weighting. Current-quarter consensus rose +8.8% over 90 days to $0.74; next-quarter +16.5% to $0.89; current-year +23.2% to $4.31; next-year +13.1% to $4.66. The earnings-surprise record is clean — four consecutive beats, including +19.1% in Q1 2026 and +11.7% in Q4 2025. A revision velocity above +20% on the current year alongside the beat streak is a meaningful forward signal and is the primary reason this is not a lower-conviction call.

The critical qualification is that these revisions are themselves a function of the gas-price deck moving higher off the winter spike, not a structural step-change in unit economics or volumes. Revenue growth of +28% paired with flat production means the revision signal does not reflect accelerating revenue from depressed levels with expanding operating capacity that would justify aggressive forward extrapolation. It is a commodity mark. By late spring 2026, Henry Hub was struggling to hold above $3 on high storage and mild weather, which underscores that the $4.97 average underpinning Q1 was a seasonal event, not a guaranteed run-rate. The forward EPS estimates implicitly assume a constructive strip that may not hold.

The durable demand thesis — LNG export expansion plus rising power demand including data centers — is real and broad-based across U.S. gas producers, but RRC participates as a commodity supplier, not through any proprietary contract or product. That keeps the growth-prospects score moderate: the demand backdrop is supportive, but RRC's earnings trajectory is set by a price it does not control and only partially hedges.

## Valuation Assessment

RRC trades at ~10.2x trailing earnings and, on the $4.31 current-year consensus, roughly 8.8x forward. Against the peer-set median P/E of 32.5, that looks deeply discounted, but the peer median is distorted: CHRD shows a negative P/E (-112x), MUR is at 65.8x, and SM at 32.5x — these reflect trough or noisy earnings rather than a clean comp. The more relevant gas-weighted comparable is Antero (AR) at 11.1x and Matador (MTDR) at 13.8x. On that basis RRC's ~10x trailing / ~9x forward sits broadly in line with, to modestly cheaper than, its closest peers, not anomalously cheap.

The right lens for an E&P at a cyclical price high is normalized, not trough, earnings. The current multiple is being applied to earnings inflated by a winter gas spike. The forward P/E of ~8.8x looks attractive only if the elevated strip holds; on a mid-cycle gas price closer to $3.50–4.00, EPS power is materially lower and the effective multiple expands. This is the inverse of the cyclical-trough case the scoring framework is built to catch: here the trailing/forward multiple understates the cyclicality risk rather than overstating value.

The dividend yield is thin at ~1.05%, below the peer median of 2.7%, so absolute yield does not provide valuation support. The discount to analyst targets (mean $47.3, median $46, against $38.59) is the most concrete bull data point, but those published targets (Jefferies $42 Hold, Barclays $43 Equal Weight among the views in the brief) carry Hold/Equal Weight ratings, meaning the analyst community sees upside-to-target without conviction — consistent with a degrading sentiment trend, with 15 of 23 analysts at Hold. The $59 high target reflects an optimistic strip; the $36 low sits just below current price.

RRC is reasonably valued relative to gas-weighted peers, with optionality to the upside if gas holds, but the multiple does not embed the downside of mean-reverting earnings. That supports a valuation score around 6: neither a clear mispricing to exploit nor an obviously stretched multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

RRC competes against larger Appalachian operators including EQT, Expand Energy, Antero, CNX, and Coterra. Its differentiators are the long-life, low-decline inventory, a high liquids cut (34% NGLs gives liquids-price optionality dry-gas peers lack), and a low-leverage, disciplined model. The five-year cumulative TSR of 545 versus a peer-group 319 evidences durable execution.

The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving rather than share-gaining. The maintenance-mode strategy means RRC is not pressing for relative growth; it is harvesting a long-dated inventory at low cost. The most material structural threat is regional and infrastructure-driven: Appalachian takeaway and ethane/NGL offtake capacity is finite, and the 10-K notes RRC may need to curtail or divert volumes if ethane agreements lapse. Transportation is already the largest cost line, and Section 311 intrastate pipeline rate-design changes plus FERC oil-pipeline index revisions were added as new risks that could raise that cost further. These are shared regional constraints rather than RRC-specific share losses, which keeps the competitive score in the low-6s.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk profile here is heavier than a standard screen would suggest, which is why the risk score sits at 5.0 and has declined from 6.0 over recent reports.

**Pennsylvania setback regulation is the single most distinctive risk.** Because RRC's entire inventory is concentrated in Pennsylvania, the pending Cecil Township setback litigation and the December 2025 citizen petition for a statewide PA setback rule are existential to inventory location in a way they are not for diversified peers. A binding statewide setback could materially constrain the drillable footprint underpinning the 50-year inventory thesis. RGGI participation/withdrawal uncertainty in PA compounds the regulatory overhang.

**Earnings are extremely price-elastic and largely unhedged.** Net income more than tripled on a winter price spike with flat volumes; a return to ~$3 gas would compress earnings sharply, and the spring 2026 backdrop showed Henry Hub struggling above $3.

**Floating-rate exposure is newly relevant.** The January refinancing lowered cost but converted fixed to floating-rate revolver debt and drew cash to near zero, adding rate sensitivity and reducing balance-sheet optionality.

**Secondary items:** the ~$260M North Louisiana legacy obligation through 2030, NGL price seasonality on 34% of reserves, and OBBBA/tax-law changes flagged as a new risk factor.

Set against these, leverage is low, liquidity is ample, and the balance sheet provides genuine resilience: the risk is to the earnings stream and the inventory footprint, not to solvency.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign-to-constructive and does not corroborate the elevated short interest. Near-term ATM IV is 33.0% against 1-year historical vol of 32.5% (fairly priced, IV percentile 11% — cheap within its own chain). The put/call skew is -6.8% (call-heavy) and the put/call OI ratio collapsed to 0.13 from 0.81 over the prior two weeks, indicating reduced downside hedging. Term structure is in normal contango (33.0% near vs 38.5% at three months). The implied move for the nearest expiry is ±3.4%. The divergence to note: short interest of 10.7% of float (7.7 days to cover) signals institutional bearishness, yet the options market is not pricing acute downside — the call-heavy skew and low put OI suggest no informed positioning ahead of a near-term catalyst. The cheap IV makes options a reasonable vehicle for expressing a view, but the lack of put demand argues against an imminent shock.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Gas holds above $4 into 2026–2027 on LNG export ramp and power/data-center demand growth, validating the +23% current-year estimate revisions and the published mid-$40s targets. RRC's low-cost, long-life inventory converts the elevated strip into sustained free cash flow; the $1.5B buyback (≈17% of market cap) plus the growing dividend return capital while net debt continues to fall. Pennsylvania setback proposals stall or are diluted, removing the inventory tail. Re-rating toward $50–59 (the high target), roughly +30% to +53% from current.

**Bear case.** Gas mean-reverts to ~$3–3.50 on high storage and mild weather; unhedged volumes (65% of 2026 open) compress earnings, and the $4.31 EPS estimate proves a winter-spike artifact. A statewide PA setback rule advances, impairing inventory location and the long-dated reserve narrative. Floating-rate revolver debt and a near-zero cash balance reduce flexibility into a weaker price environment. Stock retraces toward the $32.60 52-week low / $36 low target, roughly -7% to -15%.

**Base case.** Gas settles in a constructive but volatile $3.50–4.50 band; earnings normalize below the Q1 windfall pace but above 2024 levels; estimate revisions plateau as the strip stabilizes. The stock trades in the high-$30s to low-$40s, with the regulatory tail capping multiple expansion. Roughly flat-to-+15% from $38.59, weighted toward the lower half of that range given degrading analyst sentiment.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (rationale: uncertain).** The combination of a low-leverage balance sheet, rising estimate revisions, and a peer-aligned-to-cheap multiple keeps us off an AVOID, while the unhedged commodity sensitivity, Pennsylvania setback tail, and degrading analyst mix keep us off a BUY. The forward earnings stream is a bet on a price RRC does not control, and we lack a confirmed catalyst to commit in either direction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A confirmed structural gas-price floor — Henry Hub holding above ~$4 through at least two consecutive quarters with the company materially increasing hedge coverage on 2027 volumes — that de-risks the forward EPS base.
- Favorable resolution or clear dilution of the statewide PA setback petition and Cecil Township litigation, removing the inventory-location tail.
- Acceleration of buyback execution materially above the Q1 $27M pace at current prices, signaling management views the stock as undervalued.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Henry Hub sustaining below ~$3 with unhedged 2026 volumes, driving negative estimate revisions for two consecutive quarters.
- A binding statewide PA setback rule or adverse Cecil Township ruling that quantifiably reduces drillable acreage.
- Revolver draw expanding alongside falling cash and rising rates such that liquidity falls below ~$1.0B available.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July), which will test whether the Q1 price windfall persisted into the spring/summer strip, plus monitoring of the PA setback rulemaking docket and the gas forward curve.

## Conclusion

Range Resources is a well-run, low-leverage, long-life Marcellus producer with genuinely shareholder-aligned governance (net debt and FCF as comp metrics, a 2026 absolute-TSR tranche, a modest 3.3x CEO pay ratio, and five-year TSR of 545 versus a 319 peer group). The Q1 2026 earnings more than tripled, estimates are rising, and the stock sits below mid-$40s analyst targets. None of that resolves the core question: the earnings base is a largely-unhedged mark on a volatile gas strip that spiked in winter and was struggling above $3 by late spring, and the company's entire inventory sits in a state weighing a setback rule that would be existential to its footprint. The recent shift to drawn floating-rate debt with near-zero cash trades cost for flexibility at the moment the price environment turned uncertain.

The price decline from the March $48 high to ~$38 has improved the entry on unchanged-to-improving fundamentals, but the magnitude of the commodity and regulatory tails keeps this balanced. We rate RRC a HOLD and would look to upgrade on a confirmed gas-price floor with expanded hedging or favorable resolution of the Pennsylvania setback question, and to downgrade if gas sustains below $3 against open volumes or a binding setback rule advances.
