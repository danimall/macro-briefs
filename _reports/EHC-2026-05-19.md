## Executive Summary

Encompass Health is the largest owner-operator of inpatient rehabilitation hospitals in the United States, with 174 facilities across 39 states generating ~$6.4B in annualized revenue. The company has delivered 10%+ revenue growth for three consecutive years through a combination of volume growth (6-8% annual discharge increases), pricing gains (3-4% annual net revenue per discharge improvement), and margin expansion (salaries/benefits declining from 54.2% to 52.5% of revenue). Q1 2026 produced 9% revenue growth, 11% EBITDA growth, and a guidance raise across all metrics.

The stock trades at 18.2x forward earnings with unanimous analyst Buy ratings and a consensus target of $140 — representing 33% upside from the current $106.10 price. The valuation premium vs. the peer median of 15.4x is modest given EHC's superior growth trajectory and market position. The 12-month price decline from $128 to $106 appears driven primarily by two regulatory concerns rather than fundamental deterioration: (1) MedPAC's January 2026 recommendation for a 7% IRF payment reduction, and (2) expansion of the Review Choice Demonstration to Texas and California. These represent specific, quantifiable risks to ~82% of revenue that the market has partially priced but whose resolution timelines remain uncertain.

The core question is whether the regulatory risks justify the current 17% discount to 52-week highs despite accelerating fundamental performance. EHC's growth trajectory, competitive dominance, and improving operating leverage make it a compelling business to own, but the MedPAC recommendation creates a genuine binary tail risk that prevents sufficient confidence for a BUY at current levels. A congressional rejection or softening of the MedPAC rate cut would be the clear upgrade catalyst.

## Company Overview

Encompass Health operates exclusively in inpatient rehabilitation, providing intensive therapy for patients recovering from stroke, hip fracture, brain injury, and neurological conditions. The company serves ~265,000 discharges annually, with 92% of admissions referred from acute-care hospitals. The payor mix is heavily Medicare-dependent: ~65% traditional Medicare fee-for-service, ~16% Medicare Advantage, ~11% managed care, and ~3% Medicaid.

The growth model centers on three drivers: (1) de novo hospital construction (8 new hospitals planned for 2026, 5+ for 2027), (2) bed additions to existing facilities (~175 beds annually), and (3) occupancy improvement from 75.9% toward 80%+. The company owns ~79% of its hospital real estate and operates 67 facilities as joint ventures with acute-care health systems, securing referral relationships.

With 173 hospitals (as of year-end 2025, 174 as of Q1 2026) in a market of 1,170 total IRFs nationally, EHC holds approximately 15% market share by facility count but a substantially larger share by discharge volume given its facilities average materially larger than the typical hospital-based rehab unit.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Growth:** Q1 2026 revenue of $1.587B (+9.0% YoY) continues the trajectory of $4.80B → $5.37B → $5.94B over 2023-2025. Growth is balanced between volume (discharge growth of 4.3% total / 1.6% same-store in Q1) and pricing (net revenue per discharge rising 3-4% annually). Full-year 2026 guidance of $6.375-6.470B implies 7-9% growth.

**Margins:** Adjusted EBITDA margins expanded from 20.2% (2023) to 21.4% (2025), driven primarily by labor cost leverage. Salaries and benefits as a percentage of revenue declined 170bps over two years (54.2% → 52.5%) as contract labor FTEs fell from 427 to 355 and employees-per-occupied-bed improved. Q1 2026 EBITDA of $348.8M (+11.2% YoY) shows continued margin expansion.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow grew from $851M → $1,003M → $1,176M over 2023-2025. Free cash flow was ~$439M in 2025 after $736M in capex, yielding a ~4.2% FCF yield at current market cap. Capex is guided to $920M-995M in 2026, with ~$225-240M maintenance and the remainder growth. This heavy reinvestment is productive — each new hospital delivers IRR exceeding 20% at maturity based on disclosed economics.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of ~$2.45B with leverage well within the 4.5x covenant maximum. No significant maturities until 2028. $824M available on the revolving credit facility. The Altman Z-Score of 1.72 is a function of the capital-intensive business model (PP&E of $4.1B), not financial distress — Piotroski F-Score of 5/5 and $1.2B OCF confirm health.

**Capital Returns:** The company returned approximately $233M to shareholders in 2025 via buybacks ($158M) and dividends (~$75M based on $0.17-0.19/quarter across the year), with the dividend raised to $0.19/quarter in July 2025. $332M remains under the buyback authorization.

## Growth Analysis

Encompass Health's growth compounding mechanism is unusually visible and durable for a healthcare services company:

1. **Demographic tailwind:** Population aged 75+ is growing ~4% annually through 2030. This age cohort generates the majority of stroke, hip fracture, and neurological conditions requiring IRF care.

2. **Supply-demand imbalance:** National IRF count has been flat at ~1,170 for over a decade while eligible patient volumes grow. Only ~15% of qualifying patients currently access IRF care, suggesting massive underpenetration.

3. **Capacity pipeline:** 8 new hospitals (389 beds) opening in 2026, 5+ in 2027, plus ~175 beds added to existing facilities annually. This 6-7% annual capacity growth, combined with occupancy at 75.9% (below optimal 80%+), provides multi-year revenue visibility.

4. **New hospital ramp economics:** De novo hospitals take 2-3 years to reach mature occupancy. The 7 hospitals opened in 2025 are currently in the ramp phase, meaning their margin contribution will increase over 2026-2027 without additional capital deployment.

5. **Medicare pricing:** Annual rate updates under IRF-PPS have averaged 2.5-3.5% over recent years. The FY2026 final rule included a 3.3% net increase effective October 2025.

Full-year 2026 consensus estimates of $6.00 EPS (current year) and $6.54 (next year) imply 9% earnings growth, consistent with the capacity expansion trajectory. Estimate revisions have been modestly positive: current year EPS up 1.4% over 90 days, next year up 1.0%.

However, the premium vs. UHS and THC reflects EHC's Medicare concentration risk — those companies have more diversified payor mixes.

At $140, EHC would trade at ~23x current-year earnings — a premium that requires continued ~10% earnings growth and no material Medicare rate reductions. The stock's decline from $127.99 (52-week high) to $106 represents a 17% drawdown during a period when fundamentals improved (Q1 beat, guidance raise, continued strong execution). This creates a potential dislocation, but the regulatory risks described below provide a specific reason for the discount.

## Competitive Landscape

Encompass Health's competitive position is the strongest dimension of the investment case:

- **Scale advantage:** At 174 hospitals, EHC is 4-5x larger than the next-largest IRF operator. This enables centralized clinical systems (ACE-IT), standardized protocols, pre-fabrication construction technology, and administrative cost absorption that smaller operators cannot replicate.

- **Referral relationships:** The joint venture model with 67 acute-care hospital partners secures referral pipelines (92% of patients come from acute care). Once a JV is established, the referring hospital has an economic incentive to maintain volume flow.

- **Quality outcomes:** EHC's discharge-to-community rates and low readmission rates serve as quantitative justification for IRF-level care vs. cheaper alternatives (SNFs, home health). In an environment where CMS increasingly requires quality documentation, this data is a competitive moat.

- **Certificate of need protection:** 36% of EHC beds operate in CON states, limiting new competitive entry. However, CON repeal in Florida (EHC's largest market) and South Carolina creates a two-edged dynamic — enabling EHC expansion but also permitting competition.

- **Barrier to displacement:** The primary competitive threat is from acute-care hospitals retaining patients in their own rehab units, or from home health/SNF substitution for lower-acuity patients. The 60% Rule (requiring ≥60% of patients to have qualifying conditions) creates a regulatory barrier to diluting IRF patient mix, protecting the intensity-based reimbursement model.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. MedPAC 7% Rate Cut Recommendation (HIGH SEVERITY, UNCERTAIN TIMING)
MedPAC recommended a 7% reduction to IRF-PPS base payments in January 2026. While MedPAC recommendations are advisory (Congress has historically implemented fewer than 50% of recommendations), the political environment for Medicare spending reduction is intensifying. A 7% rate cut applied to ~$5B in Medicare revenue would represent ~$350M in annual revenue loss — approximately 25-30% of current EBITDA. Even a partial implementation (3-4%) would be materially dilutive.

MedPAC's justification is based on IRF financial performance data (aggregate margins ~20%+) and their assessment that current rates overcompensate. The recommendation is specific, numbered, and published. Its probability of influencing future rulemaking is meaningfully higher than a generic "Medicare could cut rates" risk factor.

### 2. Review Choice Demonstration Expansion (MODERATE, NEAR-TERM)
RCD expanding to Texas and California (effective March/May 2026) brings 33 hospitals under 100% pre-claim review. EHC's Alabama hospitals failed to achieve opt-out validation in cycles 2 and 3. If similar results occur in Texas/California, this creates: (a) cash flow timing delays as claims are held pending review, (b) potential permanent revenue loss if denials increase, and (c) administrative cost increases for compliance.

### 3. Medicare Advantage Volume Shift
MA now covers ~54% of Medicare beneficiaries (projected 64% by 2034). MA plans pay less than traditional Medicare and have lower referral-to-admission conversion rates. Each percentage point shift from FFS to MA represents a modest but compounding negative to revenue per discharge.

### 4. Macroeconomic Environment
Rising rates at the long end increase the discount rate applied to EHC's growth pipeline returns and could pressure the stock's P/E multiple. However, EHC's debt is largely fixed-rate with no near-term maturities, limiting direct balance sheet impact.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 29.5% vs. historical volatility of 26.2% indicates the market is pricing modestly elevated uncertainty (+3.3pp risk premium). The flat term structure (29.5% near-term vs. 28.9% far-dated) suggests the market views risk as evenly distributed rather than concentrated around an imminent catalyst. Put/call OI ratio of 0.44 (call-heavy) and balanced skew (+1.4%) indicate no unusual bearish positioning. Short interest at 3.9% is unremarkable. The options market is not signaling extreme risk — consistent with a stock experiencing regulatory uncertainty rather than imminent fundamental deterioration.

## Investment Thesis

- MedPAC's 7% recommendation is not adopted by Congress or is significantly softened (3% or less)
- RCD expansion proceeds without material claim denials, validating EHC's compliance
- New hospital pipeline delivers on schedule, driving 8-10% annual discharge growth through 2028
- Occupancy rises toward 80%+ (from 75.9%), providing operating leverage on fixed costs
- Labor cost leverage continues as contract labor normalizes further
- Stock re-rates from 18x to 21-23x on regulatory risk dissipation
- **Math:** 21x × $6.00 2026E = $126; 23x × $6.00 = $138

- Medicare Advantage shift accelerates, compressing blended revenue per discharge
- New hospital ramp takes longer than expected, creating capex drag without proportional revenue
- Stock de-rates to 14-15x on lower earnings power ($5.20-5.40 EPS scenario)
- **Math:** 15x × $5.30 impaired EPS = $80

### Base Case ($115-120, +8-13%)

- Growth continues at guided rates (8-10% revenue, ~10% EPS growth)
- Multiple recovers modestly from current compressed level as Q2/Q3 results confirm trajectory
- **Math:** 19x × $6.00 = $114; 19x × $6.30 (early 2027E visibility) = $120

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (HOLD → BUY):**

**Downgrade Triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**

2. Q2 or Q3 2026 same-store discharge growth turns negative (indicating demand deceleration)
3. Management cuts full-year guidance by >5%
4. RCD denial rates in Texas/California exceed 25% on initial review

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings (estimated late July 2026); FY2027 IRF-PPS proposed rule (typically published April-June); RCD data availability from Texas expansion (Q3-Q4 2026).

## Conclusion

Encompass Health is a high-quality compounder with dominant market position, visible capacity-driven growth, and improving operating margins. The fundamental trajectory is positive — Q1 2026 delivered a beat, a guidance raise, and continued execution on the expansion pipeline.

The stock's 17% discount to its 52-week high is attributable to the MedPAC 7% rate cut recommendation — a specific, quantifiable threat to ~$350M of annual revenue that could reduce EPS by 25-30% if fully implemented. While Congress has historically implemented fewer than half of MedPAC recommendations, the current fiscal environment (bipartisan pressure for Medicare savings) elevates the probability above historical base rates.

At 18.2x forward earnings with 9-10% EPS growth, the valuation offers reasonable upside if regulatory risk dissipates, but insufficient margin of safety if MedPAC's recommendation gains legislative traction. The appropriate posture is to hold for the growth trajectory while monitoring the specific regulatory catalysts that would justify either upgrading (rate cut rejected) or downgrading (rate cut proposed). The current price represents a reasonable entry for investors with a 12-18 month horizon and tolerance for the binary regulatory outcome.
