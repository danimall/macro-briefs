## Executive Summary

Robert Half Inc. trades at $27.00, down 42% from its 52-week high of $46.69 and representing a 63% decline from its 2022 peak revenue period. The company is experiencing a severe cyclical downturn in professional staffing — revenue has fallen from $7.24B (2022) to $5.38B (2025) — compounded by a credible structural threat from AI automation that could permanently reduce demand for contract talent in some categories.

The investment case hinges on whether the current trough represents a normal cyclical bottom (implying massive operating leverage on recovery) or the beginning of secular decline in traditional staffing (implying the old earnings power never returns). Estimates are declining at -9% to -29% across time horizons, short interest sits at 28.4% of float, and the dividend payout ratio exceeds 177% of net income. While the company maintains strong liquidity ($464M cash, zero debt) and sequential revenue stabilization is emerging, the weight of evidence points to continued fundamental deterioration that the current valuation does not adequately compensate for.

The stock trades at 21x trailing/forward earnings on trough-level EPS of $1.33. On normalized mid-cycle earnings ($4-5/share at $6B+ revenue), the stock would be 5-7x — genuinely cheap. But reaching mid-cycle requires a hiring recovery that multiple quarters of data suggest is not materializing at historical intensity, and AI displacement creates a plausible case that mid-cycle earnings are permanently lower than the prior peak.

## Company Overview

Robert Half operates three segments: **Contract Talent Solutions** (56% of 2025 revenue, $2.99B), providing temporary/contract professionals in finance, accounting, technology, and creative roles; **Permanent Placement** (8%, $440M), earning fees on full-time hires; and **Protiviti** (36%, $1.95B), a global consulting firm competing against the Big Four in internal audit, risk, and technology consulting.

The company has operated for 75+ years, ranks #1 on Forbes' America's Best Professional Recruiting Firms for the 8th consecutive year, and employed approximately 14,500 internal staff who placed 94,300 engagement professionals in 2025. Operations span 19 countries.

**Revenue Decline and Margin Compression:**

| Operating Income | ~$977M | $76M (reported) | -92% |

| EPS | ~$6.10 | $1.33 | -78% |

Contract talent hours worked declined 14.1% in 2025, offset only partially by 3.5% bill rate increases. SG&A has not adjusted proportionally — recruiter compensation as a percentage of revenue rose from 35.3% to 37.4% in contract talent — creating acute negative operating leverage.

**Balance Sheet Strength:**
- Cash: $464M
- Debt: $0 drawn on $100M revolver (expires 2030)
- Altman Z-Score: 3.39 (safe zone)
- Current Ratio: 1.53
- FCF: $267M (5.0% FCF margin)

The balance sheet is unquestionably strong. The company could sustain current operations and the dividend for several years even without improvement. This is the primary argument against the AVOID thesis — RHI is not going bankrupt.

**Dividend Sustainability:**
- Annual dividend: $2.36/share (~$236M total)
- FY2025 net income: $133M (payout ratio: 177%)
- FY2025 FCF: $267M (FCF payout ratio: 88%)
- Q1 2026 net income: $14M (annualized ~$56M)
- Buybacks: $0 in Q1 2026 (vs. $92M in FY2025, $276M in FY2024)

FCF currently covers the dividend, but the trend is concerning. Management paused buybacks entirely in Q1 2026, signaling capital preservation. If FCF deteriorates further or the company needs to invest in AI capabilities, dividend reduction becomes probable within 12-18 months. The 8.6% yield is pricing in substantial cut risk.

**Q1 2026 Results:**
- Revenue: $1.30B (-3.8% YoY) — decline rate narrowing
- EPS: $0.14 (beat estimate of $0.13 by 8.8%)
- Q2 guidance: Revenue $1.275-$1.375B, EPS $0.20-$0.30

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is unambiguously negative on a trailing basis and only marginally improving on a sequential basis.

**Estimate Revisions — Uniformly Negative:**
| Period | Current | 90 Days Ago | Revision |
|--------|---------|-------------|----------|
| Current Quarter | $0.25 | $0.36 | -29.0% |
| Current Year | $1.32 | $1.48 | -11.3% |
| Next Year | $2.07 | $2.31 | -10.6% |

Estimates are falling across all time horizons, with the near-term quarter showing the steepest decline (-29%). This is the opposite of the estimate revision pattern that signals investability. Consensus is becoming more pessimistic.

**Sequential Stabilization — Real But Insufficient:**
Management highlights two consecutive quarters of positive sequential talent solutions growth (on same-day, constant-currency basis) and notes "revenue trends strengthening as the quarter progressed and into early April." YoY decline rates have narrowed from -14% to -3.8%. But narrowing declines are not growth. The company's own Q2 guidance midpoint ($1.325B) implies roughly flat YoY — not the robust recovery needed to rebuild margins.

**Protiviti — Mixed Signals:**
U.S. Protiviti revenue declined 6.4% in Q1 2026 while international grew 16% (reported). The U.S. weakness may reflect a deregulatory political environment reducing compliance consulting demand — a structural headwind that could persist for the political cycle. Management targets 7-9% adjusted segment margins for Q3, but this remains well below historical peaks.

## Valuation Assessment

At $27.00, RHI trades at:
- 21x trailing/forward EPS ($1.33)
- ~10.2x EV/EBITDA (estimated on $267M EBITDA)
- 0.5x revenue
- 13x next-year consensus EPS ($2.07)

**Peer comparison:**
- Korn Ferry (KFY): 13.5x P/E, 3.2% yield
- ManpowerGroup (MAN): negative P/E (losing money)
- Insperity (NSP): negative P/E
- Paylocity (PCTY): 24.2x P/E
- Peer median P/E: 13.5x

RHI at 21x trailing is expensive relative to the 13.5x peer median. On next-year estimates ($2.07), RHI trades at 13x — in line with peers. However, estimates are falling, and RHI's growth trajectory (-11% current year) is worse than KFY's.

**The Normalized Earnings Bull Case:** If revenue recovers to $6.5B (still 10% below peak), operating margins historically suggest $500M+ operating income = $4-5 EPS. At 15x mid-cycle, that's $60-75/share — enormous upside. The critical variable: what is the likelihood of returning to $6.5B+ revenue in a world where AI is displacing some contract worker categories?

**The Value Trap Case:** If AI permanently reduces addressable market by 20-30%, normalized revenue is $5-5.5B with structurally lower margins (more competitive, pricing pressure). Normalized EPS might be $2-2.50, putting fair value at $30-37. At $27, this offers limited upside with continued near-term downside risk.

The valuation is not compelling enough to override declining estimates and structural risk. The stock is cheap on normalized/peak earnings but not cheap enough on realistic forward estimates to compensate for the magnitude of uncertainty.

## Competitive Landscape

**Structural Position:** Robert Half's 75-year brand, global scale, and proprietary AI-matching technology constitute meaningful advantages in a fragmented industry. The "blended solutions" model (combining Protiviti consultants with Robert Half contract talent) is differentiated and difficult to replicate.

**AI Threat — Both Offense and Defense:** The 10-K explicitly states: "The increased availability and maturation of AI tools may enable clients to use advanced automation capabilities in lieu of services provided by the Company's contract talent personnel." Management is investing in AI tools for recruiter productivity (Protiviti Atlas AI platform, patent pending) and positioning Protiviti as an AI governance/risk consultant. The net effect is indeterminate — AI simultaneously reduces demand for some workers while creating consulting opportunities.

**Competitive Intensity:** ManpowerGroup is unprofitable. Insperity is unprofitable. Upwork trades at 11x earnings as a pure-play gig marketplace. The industry is experiencing widespread pain, which validates the cyclical component but also raises questions about whether traditional staffing models face permanent impairment.

**Market Share Trajectory:** No evidence of share gains or losses — the entire industry is contracting simultaneously. Robert Half's premium positioning (specialized finance/accounting/technology rather than industrial/clerical) provides some protection but is also more susceptible to AI displacement in the medium term.

## Risk Assessment

**1. AI Structural Displacement (High Impact, Medium-High Probability):** The company's own 10-K identifies this risk explicitly. Finance & accounting tasks (Robert Half's core) are among the most automatable. The timeline is uncertain — perhaps 3-5 years for meaningful displacement — but the directionality is clear.

**2. Estimate Revisions Deterioration (High Impact, Confirmed):** This is the strongest negative fundamental signal available, directly contradicting any recovery thesis.

**3. Dividend Cut Risk (Medium Impact, 40-50% Probability within 18 months):** Buybacks already paused. A cut would remove the yield support that likely keeps some investors in the name.

**4. Prolonged Cyclical Trough (Medium Impact, 50-60% Probability):** Global hiring activity remains subdued despite low unemployment. Companies are extending decision timelines, adopting AI tools, and structurally reducing headcount requirements.

**5. PAGA Litigation (Low-Medium Impact):** Multiple class actions and California PAGA claims regarding employee classification and compensation. Could result in material settlements.

**6. Short Squeeze Risk (Asymmetric — favors longs in isolation):** 28.4% short interest with 7.8 days to cover creates theoretical squeeze potential. However, sustained squeezes require fundamental catalysts; without one, elevated short interest simply confirms informed bearish positioning.

### Options Market Signal

The flat term structure (56.5% near ≈ 55.2% far) suggests no specific catalyst concentration. The OI put/call ratio of 2.57 indicates structural put demand exceeding call demand by 2.5x — consistent with bearish institutional positioning. Low put/call volume ratio (0.24) in the near-term suggests limited active hedging against an imminent catalyst, but the structural OI positioning is clearly bearish.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($38-42, +40-55%):** The cyclical bottom is in. Sequential talent solutions growth accelerates through 2026-2027, operating leverage kicks in as revenue crosses $6B, margins expand toward 7-8%, EPS reaches $3-4 by 2028. Dividend is maintained. AI proves to be a tailwind (recruiter productivity) rather than a headwind (demand destruction). Short squeeze provides additional catalyst. Multiple re-rates to 12-14x on recovery visibility.

**Bear Case ($18-22, -19-33%):** AI displacement accelerates, permanently reducing addressable market. Recovery in hiring activity remains anemic as companies structurally reduce headcount. Estimates continue declining. Dividend cut within 12 months removes yield floor. Protiviti U.S. weakness deepens under deregulatory environment. Stock de-rates to 10-12x on lower normalized earnings of $1.50-$2.00.

**Base Case ($24-30, -11% to +11%):** Modest cyclical recovery in H2 2026-2027 brings revenue stabilization near $5.5B. Margins recover slightly to 4-5% adjusted operating. EPS reaches $1.80-$2.20 in FY2027. Dividend maintained near-term but vulnerable. Stock trades sideways as the market debates whether structural AI or cyclical recovery dominates.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Continued estimate deterioration heading into Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July 2026). If Q2 revenue guidance ($1.275-$1.375B) comes in at the low end or below, and management commentary suggests AI displacement is beginning to affect client behavior, the stock could test the $21.83 52-week low.

**Timing window:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July). Secondary catalyst: potential dividend cut announcement (could come at any quarterly earnings release if FCF deteriorates).

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 28.4% of float with 7.8 days to cover is very elevated. This creates meaningful squeeze risk on any positive surprise. The Q1 2026 earnings beat (only 8.8%) triggered a brief spike from $27 to $30 before fading. Any positive macro data (hiring uptick, consumer confidence) could trigger short-covering.

**Borrow and options signal:** OI P/C ratio of 2.57 confirms bearish structural positioning. IV is only moderately elevated (56.5% vs 49.5% HV), suggesting borrow costs are manageable but not cheap. The options market is aligned with the short thesis.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Downside target: $20-22 (52-week low area) represents 19-26% decline from current. Upside risk: squeeze to $32-35 represents 19-30% loss. Risk/reward is approximately 1:1, below the 2:1 threshold for active shorting.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE — CROWDED SHORT.** At 28.4% SI, the trade is extremely crowded. The bear thesis is consensus among informed participants. Remaining downside to the $21-22 area (~20%) is limited relative to squeeze risk of 19-30%. The stock has already declined 42% from its 52-week high. Most of the easy money on the short side has been made. The correct positioning is to avoid ownership, not to actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry Criteria (conditions that would change the view to HOLD or BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of positive YoY revenue growth (not just sequential stabilization)
2. Estimate revisions turning positive (90-day current year revision >0%)
3. Operating margins recovering above 5% on a reported basis
4. Insider open-market purchases by C-suite (confirming private confidence in recovery)

**Timeline:** Headwinds likely persist for at least 12-18 months. The AI displacement risk extends indefinitely. A cyclical recovery, if it materializes, would likely begin showing in data by Q3-Q4 2026 earnings. Reassess after Q2 2026 earnings (late July 2026).

**Downgrade triggers (already at AVOID — what would trigger stronger conviction):**
- Dividend cut announcement
- Revenue declines re-accelerating (>5% YoY in any quarter)
- Protiviti revenue turning negative globally (currently positive internationally)

## Conclusion

Robert Half is a high-quality franchise experiencing a severe cyclical downturn compounded by credible structural disruption risk from AI. The balance sheet is strong, the brand is established, and enormous operating leverage exists on recovery. However, the current evidence — estimates declining 9-29% across all horizons, zero insider buying, and explicit AI displacement risk identified in the company's own filings — weighs decisively against ownership.

The prior score trajectory (4.6 → 5.1) reflected genuine improvement in financial health (zero debt) and sequential stabilization. However, sequential stabilization from -14% to -3.8% YoY decline is improvement in the rate of deterioration, not growth. Until estimates turn positive and revenue actually grows YoY, the risk/reward does not favor ownership. The dividend yield provides a floor, but that floor may itself be cut.

AVOID. Revisit after two consecutive quarters of positive YoY revenue growth.
