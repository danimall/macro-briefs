**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

The Andersons is a North American agriculture and renewable-fuels company mislabeled as a "Food Distributor." Its economics are those of a commodity merchant (grain elevation margins, plant nutrients, ethanol production), not a defensive consumer-staples business. That misclassification matters because the standard sector-comparison lens (Sysco, US Foods, Performance Food Group) sits at 22-50x earnings on stable distribution margins, while ANDE's ~14x forward multiple reflects a thin-margin, commodity-cyclical earnings stream that swings sharply with grain basis, fertilizer margins, and now clean-fuel tax policy. The reported 36% revenue decline since 2022 ($17.3B to $11.0B) is a commodity-price artifact, not business deterioration; gross profit has held steady at $684M-$745M across the period. Any analysis anchored on the top line misreads the company.

The investment case rests on a genuine earnings inflection. Q1 2026 attributable net income was $33.2M ($0.98 diluted EPS) versus $0.3M ($0.01) a year earlier, driven by three levers: the July 2025 buy-in of Marathon's remaining 49.9% of the ethanol JV (eliminating noncontrolling-interest leakage and adding roughly $40M of after-tax plant income in H2 2025), $26.2M of Section 45Z clean-fuel production credits after plants qualified for a higher tier, and Agribusiness swinging to a $7.4M pretax profit from a $(9.7)M loss on returning market volatility. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised up 13.9% over 90 days to $5.56, and the company beat estimates in three of the last four quarters.

The stock has responded, more than doubling from a $31.84 low to ~$78, and now trades near the consensus target band ($75 low / $80 median / $90 high). The remaining upside is not asymmetric: the earnings improvement is real but increasingly leans on a policy-dependent credit stream (45Z, legislated through 2029), the FCF profile is negative on elevated growth capex, and the trailing multiple is not compensating for the commodity cyclicality embedded in the base. This is a growth-hold — own it for the inflection and the low-$90s upside case, but the entry point after a 108% run does not offer margin of safety. We rate ANDE HOLD.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1947 and headquartered in Maumee, Ohio, The Andersons reorganized into two reportable segments effective January 1, 2025:

- **Agribusiness (~75% of revenue, $8.26B in 2025):** A diversified commodity merchandising and logistics operation built around grain elevators across the U.S. and Canada. It buys, sells, stores, and conditions corn, wheat, and soybeans, earning elevation margins (basis appreciation, carry spreads, storage fees), and runs a plant-nutrient (fertilizer) manufacturing and distribution business plus a growing premium-ingredients line. Grain storage capacity was ~271M bushels at year-end 2025, down from 291M following the Sunray, TX terminal incident and the closure of underperforming locations. The November 2024 Skyland Grain acquisition added $468M of revenue and $76M of gross profit in 2025, extending the western footprint into sorghum/corn exports via the Houston port.
- **Renewables (~25% of revenue, $2.75B in 2025):** Four wholly-owned ethanol plants (Iowa, Indiana, Michigan, Ohio) with ~405M gallons of nameplate capacity (historically running above nameplate to ~500M gallons), plus a merchandising/trade book in ethanol, co-products (distillers' grains, corn oil), and renewable feedstocks.

ANDE competes as a mid-cap, regionally focused merchant against the global mega-traders (ADM, Bunge, Cargill) and local cooperatives. Its edge is nimbleness (buying in smaller lots, serving local/regional customers on price, service, and reliability), offset by limited scale. No single customer represents 10% or more of revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trend is a distraction; gross profit is the signal.** Reported revenue fell from $17.3B (2022) to $14.8B (2023) to $11.3B (2024) to $11.0B (2025), tracking commodity prices that flow through revenue and COGS roughly in tandem. Gross profit moved in a tight band ($684M, $745M, $694M, $714M), confirming the business is stable rather than shrinking.

**Profitability is structurally thin, as expected for a commodity merchant.** 2025 net income was $95.7M (down from $114M in 2024), a ~1.1% net margin and ~0.7% operating margin. ROE was 9.6% and ROA 3.2%. The effective tax rate was unusually low (15.7% in 2025, 13.5% in Q1 2026) because of non-taxable Section 45Z credits and noncontrolling-interest effects, a benefit that is genuine but policy-contingent.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** 2025 operating cash flow was $177M (down from $331M in 2024), and FCF was approximately negative $56M on $233M of growth capex. Cash fell from $562M at year-end 2024 to $98M at year-end 2025, almost entirely because of the $425M cash purchase of Marathon's remaining 49.9% of the ethanol JV in July 2025. This was a capital-allocation choice, not distress: it eliminated NCI leakage and immediately lifted attributable earnings, and total liquidity remained around $1.9B. Debt/equity is elevated at ~1.95, but the Altman Z-Score of 4.04 sits in the safe zone and the company is in full covenant compliance, with long-term debt around $625M.

The seasonal working-capital swing is large and should not be mistaken for stress: Q1 2026 short-term debt spiked to $716.5M from $249M at year-end 2025, reflecting the normal late-winter/spring inventory build in fertilizer and grain. Operating cash flow is deeply negative in Q1 every year for the same reason. The March 2026 credit amendment reduced revolver capacity from $1,550M to $1,300M and extended term debt to 2034, a modest reduction in headroom worth monitoring given the seasonal reliance on the facility.

**Q1 2026 marks a real inflection.** The result was driven by full ethanol-plant ownership (no NCI leakage), the 45Z credits (plants qualified for the next tier after 2026 rule changes), and Agribusiness turning to profit on returning volatility and stronger fertilizer margins. Two caveats temper the annualization: Q1 uses cash heavily on seasonality, and the 45Z contribution is a policy-driven line, not organic operating leverage.

The earnings-surprise record supports moderate confidence in forward estimates: beats of 56.9% (Q1 2026), 28.5%, and 79.0% in three of the last four quarters, against a 54.2% miss in the June 2025 quarter. The variance underscores that quarterly results are volatile and hard to forecast — the beats are real but so is the downside tail.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has three components, of differing durability:

1. **Full ethanol ownership.** The TAMH buy-in converts a shared earnings stream into a fully-owned one. This is a durable, structural improvement to earnings quality; the ~$40M of incremental after-tax plant income in H2 2025 recurs regardless of policy, subject only to ethanol crush economics.

2. **Section 45Z clean-fuel credits.** This is the swing factor and the least durable component. Credits contributed $35M in 2025, with the OBBBA (July 2025) extending them through 2029 and improving carbon-intensity scoring. This is a genuine multi-year tailwind, but it is a legislated, policy-dependent stream whose value depends on lifecycle-emissions modeling, prevailing-wage and apprenticeship standards, evolving IRS/Treasury guidance, and a transfer market that trades at discounts. A meaningful share of the earnings inflection is policy, not operating momentum, and investors annualizing Q1 should discount it accordingly.

3. **Agribusiness normalization.** The 2025 environment was tough industry-wide — oversupplied grain, low prices, muted volatility compressed merchandising margins across the sector. This was cyclical, not company-specific, and the Q1 2026 swing back to profit suggests the cycle is turning. Skyland adds a structural western-footprint expansion (sorghum/corn exports) that complements the eastern ethanol/export orientation.

Estimate revisions confirm positive momentum. Current-year EPS has been revised from $4.88 to $5.56 over 90 days (+13.9%), current-quarter estimates are up 22.2%, and next-year sits at $6.03. Those revisions place ANDE firmly in positive-momentum territory. The signal is meaningful but not the extreme (>30% full-year with >30% revenue growth) inflection that would compel a BUY on its own, and much of the revision is attributable to the 45Z tier upgrade rather than broad-based revenue acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$78 the stock carries a trailing P/E of ~20.9 on the $2.79 trailing EPS figure, but on rising consensus of $5.56 current-year and $6.03 next-year the forward multiple compresses to ~14x and ~13x respectively. That is the more relevant lens given the earnings inflection.

**Peer comparison requires caution.** The Food Distributors cohort — Chefs' Warehouse (49x), Performance Food Group (49.6x), US Foods (30.6x), Sysco (22.1x) — trades at a peer-median P/E of 49.0 on stable, non-cyclical distribution margins. Applying those multiples to ANDE would be a category error; ANDE's thin, commodity-cyclical margins and policy-dependent earnings do not warrant distribution-business multiples. The peer set is not a useful valuation anchor and the apparent discount to peer median is not a mispricing signal; it reflects a fundamentally different, lower-quality earnings stream.

**On its own history and forward earnings, ANDE is reasonable but not cheap.** Third-party work (Seeking Alpha, July 2026) pegs the stock at ~14.2x forward versus a 15.2x five-year average, a modest discount to its own history. Texas Capital initiated at Buy with a $110 target in June 2026, citing differentiated ethanol/renewable-feedstock exposure. Consensus targets span $75 low / $80 median / $90 high / $81.67 mean, and the stock at ~$78 sits within that band, with the median target only ~2% above spot. On an inflection stock with rising estimates, consensus targets can lag reality, but ANDE is not the classic cheap-inflection setup (forward P/E is reasonable, not depressed relative to growth), and the durable growth rate is mid-single-digit rather than the accelerating trajectory that would justify treating the median target as a hard floor.

Applying our calibration discipline: valuation as a raw multiple sorts poorly as a predictive signal, and "cheap on trailing P/E" is a warning to check for a value trap or, here, a distortion (the $2.79 trailing EPS understates run-rate earnings). We evaluate on forward earnings, where ANDE is fairly priced rather than mispriced. A base-case ~15x on $6.00 blended forward EPS supports roughly $90, close to the analyst high, meaningful upside from $78 but dependent on the 45Z stream and the commodity cycle both cooperating. There is no independent catalyst that the market is clearly missing; the inflection is broadly recognized (three analysts at Buy, the stock already up 108%). We score valuation moderate.

## Competitive Landscape

ANDE occupies the middle ground between the agricultural mega-traders and local cooperatives. Its strategy leverages a complementary geographic footprint — the eastern operations tied to export and ethanol-driven elevation margins, the western Skyland assets capturing basis appreciation — and higher-margin niches in premium ingredients and renewable feedstocks. Against the global traders it lacks scale; against local players it has broader logistics reach and access to the ethanol value chain.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving. The renewables pivot to full plant ownership plus 45Z eligibility raises the quality and quantity of that segment's earnings, and Skyland extends the addressable footprint. But the moat is inherently narrow: elevation margins are price- and service-competitive, basis risk cannot be fully hedged, and the business is capital-intensive with thin returns. There is no evidence of durable share gains that the market has not priced. We view competitive position as stable rather than strengthening in a way that warrants a premium.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that specifically move ANDE:

1. **Unhedgeable basis risk.** Management hedges futures price risk but explicitly cannot hedge the basis component of grain, which moves on large positions and can swing profitability materially. 85% of sales contracts are ASC 815 derivatives; Q1 2026 recorded a $74.8M commodity-derivative loss in COGS (offset by physical positions, but illustrative of the mark-to-market volatility running through reported results).

2. **Section 45Z credit dependency.** A large and growing share of Renewables profit now comes from a policy-legislated credit. Eligibility depends on lifecycle-emissions modeling, wage/apprenticeship standards, and evolving Treasury guidance, and transfers trade at discounts. A future policy reversal would remove a meaningful earnings pillar. This is the single most important idiosyncratic risk not fully priced.

3. **Ethanol/biofuel structural transition.** EV adoption and shifting fuel policy could erode long-run ethanol demand and strand plant assets. Near-term fundamentals (RVO, blend rates, exports) are supportive.

4. **Geographic and input concentration.** Concentration in the Eastern Grain Belt with sensitivity to corn basis and natural-gas costs, both of which pressured ethanol economics in 2025 and early 2026.

5. **Aging, hazardous assets.** Grain-dust explosion/fire risk is explicit and realized — the Sunray, TX terminal explosion drove impairments and capacity loss (partially offset by $16.8M+ of insurance recoveries in 2025).

6. **Leverage and liquidity swings.** Heavy seasonal revolver reliance, with the March 2026 amendment cutting capacity.

7. **Litigation.** A putative class action alleging commodities/antitrust violations from past trading, settled in principle with $5.0M accrued; and a receivership-related claim (likely tied to the deconsolidated ELEMENT plant) where loss could exceed reserves.

On balance, the risk profile carries genuine binary/policy-dependent exposure (45Z, litigation, commodity mark-to-market) that is only partly priced. We score risk factors below neutral.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 48.7% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 35.3% (a 13.4-point spread) and sits at the 70th percentile of its own chain. The term structure is in backwardation (48.7% near-term vs. 42.5% at six months), pointing to the next earnings print as the catalyst. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±10.9%, consistent with a stock that gapped ~14% down on the May 6 print. Put/call skew is balanced (1.1%) and the put/call OI ratio has collapsed from 1.18 to 0.27 over the past month as the price rose 14.6%, indicating hedging pressure has unwound and positioning is not bearish. The signal is two-sided: the market expects a large earnings reaction but is not leaning short. For a fundamental holder, the rich near-term IV argues against buying at current levels ahead of the print and favors patience for a post-event volatility crush.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$90-110):** The Q1 2026 inflection proves durable. Full ethanol ownership compounds attributable earnings, 45Z credits deliver through 2029 at the upgraded tier, and a normalizing grain cycle restores merchandising margins. Consensus continues to revise upward toward $6.00+ EPS, and the market re-rates the renewables-enhanced earnings base toward 15-18x, supporting $90 on the analyst high and Texas Capital's $110 on a fuller renewables valuation. Skyland integration and premium ingredients add incremental growth.

**Bear Case (~$55-60):** The grain cycle stays oversupplied and low-volatility, compressing merchandising margins as in 2025. A policy change or unfavorable Treasury guidance erodes the 45Z stream, removing $30M+ of high-margin earnings. Natural-gas costs and weak corn basis pressure ethanol crush. On depressed earnings ($4.00-4.50 EPS) at a de-rated ~13x, the stock retraces toward $55-60, roughly a 25% drawdown from current levels.

The base case sits between: mid-single-digit organic growth, a stable-to-modestly-favorable cycle, and 45Z persisting through the legislated window, supporting ~$6.00 forward EPS at ~15x, or roughly $90.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD (growth_hold) with a 12-month horizon tied to the earnings cadence.

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
  1. A pullback toward the mid-$60s that restores margin of safety (forward P/E below ~11x on $6.00 EPS) without any fundamental deterioration.
  2. Two consecutive quarters confirming the Agribusiness margin recovery is structural rather than a single-quarter volatility rebound, with full-year consensus revised above $6.00.
  3. Clarity that 45Z credits are secure at the upgraded tier through 2029 with no adverse Treasury guidance, de-risking the largest earnings dependency.

- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
  1. A policy reversal or unfavorable IRS/Treasury guidance materially reducing 45Z eligibility or value.
  2. A return to the 2025 oversupplied-grain, low-volatility environment driving Agribusiness back to pretax losses for consecutive quarters.
  3. Covenant pressure or a further material reduction in revolver capacity that constrains the seasonal working-capital build.

- **Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (August 2026), which the options market is pricing for a ±10.9% move. The June 2026 8-K (Item 5.02 director/officer change) that could not be retrieved warrants follow-up as a governance data point.

## Conclusion

The Andersons has delivered a genuine earnings inflection, and the misleading "Food Distributor" label obscures a fundamentally sound, well-capitalized ag-commodity merchant that has upgraded its renewables earnings quality through the Marathon buy-in. The Q1 2026 result, the upward revision to current-year consensus, and three-of-four quarterly beats all point in the right direction, and the balance sheet (Altman Z 4.04, ~$1.9B liquidity, full covenant compliance) is sound despite the seasonal debt swings and the cash drawdown that funded a value-accretive acquisition.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is the entry point and the composition of the improvement. The stock has doubled off its low to trade near the consensus target band, the forward multiple (~14x) is fair rather than cheap, a meaningful slice of the inflection rests on a policy-dependent 45Z credit stream, and FCF is negative on elevated growth capex. The score trajectory has declined from 6.6 to 5.7 over three prior reports as the easy re-rating played out, and today's data confirms a stabilized-but-not-accelerating picture rather than reversing the trend; the fundamentals are intact and improving, but the risk/reward at ~$78 is balanced, not asymmetric. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the mid-$60s or on confirmation that the Agribusiness recovery and 45Z stream are both durable. Recommendation: **HOLD**, base case ~$90 (12 months), bull $110, bear $55-60.
