## Executive Summary

Cousins Properties is a Sun Belt-focused Class A office REIT trading at approximately 9.2x annualized FFO ($2.84/share) with a 4.9% dividend yield. The company's operational metrics are strong: Q1 2026 second-generation rent spreads of +28.7% (straight-line) and +15.2% (cash), leasing volume of 932K SF, and a Sun Belt positioning that benefits from continued corporate relocations out of high-tax states. Management initiated a $500M buyback program and repurchased $90M in Q1 at prices below today's $26.02.

However, the investment case faces material headwinds. EPS estimates have collapsed — down 59% over 90 days for the current year — reflecting higher interest costs on $3.8B of debt, one-time impairments, and the dilutive near-term impact of acquisitions. Net income has deteriorated from $167M (2022) to $41M (2025), and CUZ reported a net loss of $24.9M in Q1 2026. In the current rate environment, with elevated long-end Treasury yields and structural pressure on rates, the cost of capital for leveraged REITs continues to rise, compressing valuations even for operationally strong portfolios.

The stock is down approximately 15% from its 52-week high of $30.81, with 10.9% short interest suggesting institutional skepticism. Analyst consensus remains constructive (10 buys vs. 2 holds, median target $28), but the rapidly deteriorating estimate trajectory demands caution on entry timing.

## Company Overview

Cousins Properties is a fully integrated, self-administered REIT owning approximately 22 million square feet of Class A "lifestyle" office properties across seven Sun Belt markets. The portfolio is concentrated in Austin (36% of NOI) and Atlanta (31%), with smaller positions in Charlotte, Tampa, Phoenix, Dallas, and Nashville.

The company differentiates through premium asset quality — trophy buildings with extensive amenities positioned to benefit from the "flight to quality" dynamic in office leasing. The top tenant represents 8.9% of annualized rent, with technology companies comprising 53.1% of Austin rent and banking/financial firms 19.2% of Charlotte.

The company employs 351 people and maintains investment-grade credit ratings (received April 2024) with what management describes as the best balance sheet in the office REIT sector.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Trajectory:** Revenues grew from $762M (2022) to $994M (2025), a 30% increase driven by acquisitions including Sail Tower, Vantage South End, The Link, and most recently 300 South Tryon ($317.5M, February 2026). Q1 2026 rental revenue was $261.1M, up 7.4% from Q1 2025.

**FFO Performance:** FFO per share reached a cycle high of $2.84 in 2025, recovering from $2.62 in 2023. Q1 2026 FFO was $0.73/share, essentially flat with Q1 2025's $0.74 despite dilution from the 300 South Tryon acquisition and higher interest expense.

**The Earnings Gap:** The critical disconnect is between operational performance (FFO growing) and GAAP earnings (collapsing). Net income fell from $167M (2022) to $41M (2025) and was negative $24.9M in Q1 2026. The drivers: interest expense grew to $159M in 2025 (from $122M in 2024, a 30% increase) and hit $45M in Q1 2026 alone (+22.6% YoY). Depreciation from acquisitions further compresses GAAP income.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt stands at $3.8B (up from $3.3B at year-end 2025) against a market cap of $4.3B. Debt-to-equity is 0.89x. Favorably, 87% of debt is fixed rate, the credit facility was just recast to $1.2B with maturity extended to 2031, and a new $500M issuance at 4.875% came at tighter spreads than 2024 bonds. Weighted average debt maturity is 4.1 years.

**Near-Term Maturities:** Approximately $470M matures within 12 months (Colorado Tower $100M Sept 2026, 201 North Tryon $118M Oct 2026, term loans with extension options pushing to 2027-2028). Manageable given the $793M available on the credit facility, but refinancing at higher rates would further compress earnings.

**Cash Flow and Dividend:** Operating cash flow was $402M in 2025. The $1.28/share annual dividend ($0.32 quarterly) yields approximately 4.9% and appears well-covered by FFO ($2.84 = 45% payout ratio on FFO). The dividend is safe absent a severe occupancy deterioration.

**Estimate Revisions — Red Flag:** Current year EPS estimates have fallen from $0.47 to $0.19 over 90 days (a 59% decline). Current quarter from $0.14 to $0.06 (-56%). Only 2 analysts are providing estimates, which introduces noise, but the direction is unambiguously negative. This reflects the compounding impact of the acquisition-funded leverage increase and rising interest costs.

## Growth Analysis

**Organic Growth:** Same-property NOI grew 2.4% (straight-line) in 2025 and 1.7% in Q1 2026. This is modest but positive, suggesting the portfolio is stabilizing after post-COVID office disruption. The improvement in re-leasing spreads — from +3.5% cash in FY2025 to +15.2% cash in Q1 2026 — is the most encouraging data point, suggesting pricing power is strengthening as supply remains constrained.

**Acquisition-Driven Growth:** CUZ has been acquiring aggressively (300 South Tryon at $317.5M is the largest). While this drives revenue growth, it simultaneously increases leverage and dilutes near-term per-share metrics. The strategy makes sense if assets are acquired below replacement cost and lease up at strong rents — but execution risk is elevated with $3.8B in debt at 5%+ rates.

**Sun Belt Tailwinds:** Management cited "significant uptick in relocation activities" with companies like Starbucks (Nashville HQ), Apollo (seeking SE HQ), and Yamaha (CA to Atlanta). Supply constraints are real — in Atlanta's Buckhead, management noted zero options for a 100K SF tenant seeking trophy space, with asking rents of $50-60/SF versus replacement cost of ~$90/SF.

**Occupancy Gap:** Portfolio is 90.7% leased (down slightly from 91.6%) with economic occupancy at 88.3%. Closing this 2.4% gap between leased and economic represents embedded NOI upside as tenants take physical possession.

**Growth Constraints:** In the current rate environment, CUZ's cost of capital for acquisitions is approximately 5% (debt) plus equity dilution at 9x FFO. Acquiring assets at 6-7% stabilized yields produces thin accretion that takes 2-3 years to materialize. Growth is limited unless rates decline or the stock re-rates.

## Valuation Assessment

At $26.02, CUZ trades at:
- 9.2x annualized FFO ($2.84)
- ~4.9% dividend yield
- Negative trailing P/E (due to impairments/depreciation; not meaningful for REIT analysis)
- 15% below 52-week high of $30.81

**Peer Comparison:** The peer median P/E of 23.2x is not directly comparable given CUZ's depressed GAAP earnings. On an FFO multiple basis, office REITs generally trade at 8-12x FFO in the current environment, placing CUZ at the lower-middle of the range. The 4.9% yield is below the peer median of 5.8% (KRC at 6.3%, HIW at 7.9%, SLG at 5.8%), suggesting either CUZ is priced for better quality or peers are priced for higher risk.

**Analyst Targets:** The consensus median of $28 (range $24-$34) implies approximately 8% upside from current levels. With 10 buy/strong buy ratings and zero sells, analyst sentiment is constructive.

**Relative Value Case:** CUZ arguably deserves a premium to lower-quality office REITs given its Sun Belt focus, Class A asset quality, and balance sheet strength. The current 9.2x FFO multiple appears reasonable given the sector's challenges, but not deeply undervalued absent a clear catalyst for re-rating.

**Rate Sensitivity:** At elevated Treasury yields with structural upward pressure, the spread between CUZ's ~11% implied cap rate (inverse of 9.2x FFO) and risk-free rates provides modest cushion. If long-term rates continue rising, further compression of REIT multiples is likely, with potential downside to the $22-23 range where the stock already traded in February-March 2026.

## Competitive Landscape

CUZ occupies a defensible position within office REITs: Sun Belt markets with stronger fundamentals than Gateway cities, Class A trophy assets benefiting from flight-to-quality, and an integrated platform with 15-year average executive tenure. The +28.7% straight-line rent spreads demonstrate genuine pricing power that peers with lower-quality portfolios cannot replicate.

New office construction remains minimal because replacement costs ($90/SF) far exceed current achievable rents ($50-60/SF). CUZ's existing assets face limited competitive new supply for the foreseeable future.

However, the structural headwind remains: office demand is permanently lower than pre-2020 levels due to hybrid work adoption. CUZ's national lease rate of 90.7% (vs. pre-COVID norms of 93-95%) reflects this reality. Flight to quality helps CUZ take share within the office sector, but the sector itself is shrinking as employers accept less total square footage per employee.

CDP (COPT Defense Properties) at 23.2x P/E with only a 4% discount to 52-week highs represents a peer with a higher-conviction growth thesis (defense/government demand) that commands a premium multiple. CUZ lacks that singular demand driver.

## Conclusion

Cousins Properties is a well-run company in a structurally challenged sector facing acute cyclical headwinds from rising rates. The operational performance — 28.7% rent spreads, Sun Belt migration tailwinds, supply constraints — is genuinely strong and distinguishes CUZ from lower-quality office peers. Management is making rational capital allocation decisions (buybacks at $23.36, disposing of weaker assets, extending credit facilities).

The problem is timing. With estimates falling sharply, elevated long-term yields, and an uncertain rate outlook, the macro backdrop is actively hostile to leveraged real estate. The 4.9% dividend yield provides income support, but the stock's February decline to $21.03 demonstrates the magnitude of downside if rates continue rising.

At $26.02, CUZ is reasonably priced for the current environment but lacks a near-term catalyst for re-rating. The right entry point is either after estimate revisions stabilize or on a rate-driven selloff toward $22-23 where the yield rises above 5.5% and the FFO multiple compresses to 8x. Hold for income while awaiting better risk/reward on either side.
