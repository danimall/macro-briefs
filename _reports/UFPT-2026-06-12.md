**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

UFP Technologies is a contract development and manufacturing organization (CDMO) focused on single-use and single-patient medical devices, supplying custom-engineered components and finished disposables to major medical device OEMs. The business has scaled rapidly, with revenue rising from $353.8M in 2022 to $602.8M in 2025, and the medical mix is now roughly 93% of revenue as management deliberately exits non-medical end markets. The franchise rests on a defensible position (design-in stickiness, access to specialty materials, and 19 ISO 13485-certified facilities) that creates real switching costs once a component is qualified into a customer's device.

The central analytical issue is that the headline 19.5% 2025 revenue growth was almost entirely acquisition-driven. Organic growth was only about 1.5% in 2025 and essentially flat (~0%) in Q1 2026. The forward thesis therefore depends on two things: continued disciplined M&A (which management explicitly expects to supply ~50% of long-term growth, even as it admits being outbid recently and that the pipeline is "quiet"), and a second-half 2026 organic re-acceleration as four new program launches ramp. The demand signal underneath that ramp is strong: three of four new-program customers have asked UFP to double capacity, and customers are co-investing in UFP's Dominican Republic buildings, but it has not yet shown up in reported organic numbers.

At ~$238.60 the stock trades at roughly 27x trailing EPS of $8.75 and ~23x current-year consensus of $10.38. That is a premium multiple for a business currently delivering low-single-digit organic growth, partially offset by a quality balance sheet (leverage ~1.14x EBITDA, FCF margin ~13%) and the credible 2H ramp. Layered on top are concentration risk (Intuitive Surgical 24.3% + Stryker 21.5% = ~46% of 2025 revenue), a February 2026 cyber incident with unresolved scope, and 20.3% short interest. We rate UFPT a HOLD. The fundamentals support ownership for investors already long, but the current price does not offer the asymmetric upside required for a new high-conviction position.

## Company Overview

UFP Technologies custom-manufactures single-use and single-patient medical devices and components for minimally invasive/robotic surgery, infection prevention, advanced wound care, orthopedics, patient handling/surfaces, interventional and surgical devices, diagnostics, and biopharma. Representative products include protective drapes for robotic surgery, surgical/endoscopic disposables, cardiac implant components, catheter dispenser coils, and medical device packaging. The company functions as an outsourced manufacturing partner; it does not own the OEM products it supplies, and revenue flows from being designed into those devices and their disposables.

The company reports as a single operating segment and is now ~93% medical / 7% non-medical, with management actively phasing out the remaining non-medical business (including a full exit from automotive). Its competitive differentiation rests on access to specialty materials (cross-linked foam, TPU, reticulated polyurethane), engineering know-how, clean-room and regulated manufacturing (19 ISO 13485-certified, 13 FDA-registered facilities), and inclusion on customers' preferred-supplier lists. Headcount grew to 4,846 full-time plus 503 temporary employees by January 2026, up sharply from 4,146 + 189 a year earlier, with the temporary increase tied to the AJR labor situation discussed below.

R. Jeffrey Bailly, the long-time Chairman/CEO, transitioned to Executive Chairman in June 2026, with former President Mitchell Rock becoming CEO. Bailly will remain about a year as Executive Chair focused on acquisitions and key hires and retains roughly 172,000 shares.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** Revenue grew from $353.8M (2022) to $400.1M (2023), $504.4M (2024), and $602.8M (2025). Operating income rose to $92.3M in 2025 (15.3% operating margin) and net income to $68.3M (11.3% net margin). Margins have been stable across the period, in the 14.4%–16.1% operating range. Gross margin slipped to 28.3% in 2025 from 29.1%, almost entirely due to the AJR labor issue (~$6.3M incremental cost); absent it, gross margin would have been ~29.3%. Q1 2026 gross margin recovered modestly to 28.8%.

**The organic-vs-acquired distinction is the key fact.** Of the 2025 revenue increase, acquisitions contributed $168.3M (vs. $73.1M in 2024). Q1 2026 revenue rose 4.1% YoY to $154.2M with organic growth essentially flat. Within medical, the underlying trends diverge: robotics +7%, patient surfaces +11%, interventional/surgical +15%, offset by non-medical −15% and wound-care destocking at two customers. Reported Q1 EPS was $2.27 GAAP / $2.48 adjusted, beating consensus by roughly 7%.

**Balance sheet and cash generation are solid.** The Altman Z-Score of 3.62 sits in the safe zone; current ratio 2.68; debt/equity 0.55. ROA was 10.4%, ROE 16.1%, FCF margin 13.1% with FCF of $79M and operating cash flow of $91.9M in 2025. Total debt of ~$135.5M sits under a $275M BofA facility maturing June 2029 at a ~5.0–5.1% weighted rate, with leverage of ~1.14x EBITDA. The company paid down ~$54M of debt in 2025 and is in compliance with all covenants. There is no dividend and no meaningful buyback; cash is deployed into acquisitions and capacity (Dominican Republic buildings, planned APAC expansion).

**Quality-of-earnings flags.** Goodwill plus intangibles total $338M, ~51.6% of total assets, a large acquired-asset base that carries impairment risk if deal performance disappoints. The Welch acquisition (2024) earnout was written to $0 after EBITDA targets were missed in 2024 and 2025, with 2026 deemed unlikely, concrete evidence not every deal hits plan. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was light at $3.2M due to a working-capital build (receivables +$15.7M) from a strong March, which reads as timing rather than deterioration.

The earnings track record is consistent: three beats and one small miss over the last four quarters (Q1 2026 missed by 2.7%, but the three prior quarters beat by 0.9%–5.5%). Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current-year consensus up 2.5% over 30 days to $10.38, next-year up 2.0% to $11.69, though the 90-day trend is roughly flat to slightly negative, so this is incremental positive momentum rather than a sharp upward revision that would signal an earnings inflection.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two distinct components that should be evaluated separately.

**Acquisition engine.** Management explicitly targets ~50% of long-term growth from M&A. The 2024 deals (Welch, Marble, AQF/Advant) were larger; 2025 deals (AJR, UNIPEC, TPI) were smaller bolt-ons totaling ~$14.8M (AJR $2.8M, UNIPEC $7.5M, TPI $4.5M). Two cautionary signals warrant attention: management admitted being outbid on several recent deals, and the pipeline is "a little quiet right now." With organic growth running at ~1.5%, a slower M&A cadence would directly compress the consolidated growth rate. The Welch earnout-to-zero and the AJR diligence miss are concrete reminders that integration risk is real even on small deals.

**Organic re-acceleration call.** The bullish forward signal is strong. Four new program launches are underway (two robotic surgery, one infection prevention, one transfer), and three of the four customers have already asked UFP to double capacity before the programs have scaled. Customers are co-investing in UFP's new Santiago and La Romana (DR) buildings, which indicates deep design-in entrenchment. Revenue from these programs is expected to ramp "robustly" in 2H 2026. Separately, the wound-care destocking at two customers is expected to last about three quarters (into Q3 2026) before normalizing, with two new wound-care programs in development.

**Demand backdrop.** UFP's single largest revenue stream is tied to Intuitive Surgical's robotic-surgery procedure volumes. Despite Intuitive's stock decline in 2026, Intuitive raised its 2026 procedure-growth outlook in April, supportive evidence that the underlying procedure demand driving UFP's largest customer relationship remains healthy. The secular tailwinds (minimally invasive/robotic surgery growth, single-use device adoption for infection control, OEM outsourcing) remain intact.

On balance, the growth thesis is credible but back-end loaded and partly dependent on a re-acceleration that has not yet appeared in reported numbers. We score growth prospects 5.8, above average on quality and demand signals, tempered by flat current organic growth and the acquisition dependence.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$238.60, UFPT carries a market cap of ~$1.84B and trades at ~27x trailing EPS of $8.75. On forward consensus, the multiple is ~23x current-year ($10.38) and ~20x next-year ($11.69). The PEG on consensus next-year growth (~13% EPS growth from $10.38 to $11.69) is roughly 1.5x, reasonable but not cheap, particularly given that the growth is acquisition-assisted rather than purely organic.

Peer comparison is of limited value here because the "Health Care Equipment" cohort is heterogeneous and several names carry negative or distorted P/Es (STAA −70.9, IART −2.6, AHCO −16.8). The cleaner comp, LeMaitre Vascular, trades at 34.7x, above UFPT. INSP trades at 9.2x but is down 71% from its high, reflecting its own issues. UFPT's ~27x trailing multiple is therefore in the middle of a wide and noisy range, and does not screen as obviously cheap or obviously expensive against peers.

The case for UFPT being cheap rests on the 2H 2026 organic re-acceleration and continued accretive M&A: if those play out, forward earnings power exceeds the ~$10.38 currently modeled and the trailing multiple looks reasonable. The case for UFPT being fully valued rests on the fact that the market is already crediting both the M&A engine and the 2H ramp into a ~27x trailing multiple while organic growth is currently ~0%. This is not an inflection stock in the technical sense: there is no sharp upward estimate revision (+30%+) and no revenue acceleration from a depressed base, and the 90-day revision trend is flat-to-slightly-negative. The stock is a high-quality compounder priced as one, which limits the asymmetry.

Analyst price targets (low $289, high $360, mean/median $324.50) sit well above the current price, implying roughly 21%–51% upside. We treat these with some caution: they likely embed full execution of the M&A engine and the 2H ramp, neither of which is yet confirmed in the numbers. We score valuation 5.2, fair, with the multiple already reflecting the favorable forward case and limited margin of safety if the organic re-acceleration slips.

## Competitive Landscape

UFP operates in a fragmented contract-manufacturing niche, competing primarily against smaller regional foam/plastics converters. Its moat derives from scale, access to specialty materials, regulated/clean-room manufacturing, and design-in stickiness: once a component is qualified into a device, switching costs are high. This trajectory is improving on two fronts: the medical mix is rising as lower-margin non-medical exits, and the customer base is diversifying.

Customer concentration is the defining competitive risk, and it is moderating by design. Intuitive Surgical fell from 29.2% of revenue (2024) to 24.3% (2025) as Stryker grew to 21.5%; the combined two-customer dependence is still ~46%, but the trend is the right direction. Management is diversifying within Intuitive (more programs) and across new customers. The dominant competitive threat management itself flags is OEM in-sourcing: a large customer bringing programs in-house would directly remove revenue. The co-investment by customers in UFP's DR facilities and the requests to double capacity on new programs are evidence that the design-in entrenchment is deepening rather than eroding. We score competitive position 6.3.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration (dominant risk).** Top 10 customers = 68.8% of 2025 revenue; one customer was ~32% of gross receivables at year-end 2025. Any in-sourcing, dual-sourcing, or order reduction by Intuitive or Stryker would materially move the stock. This risk is moderating but remains the central swing factor.

**Cyber incident (February 2026).** An Item 1.05 material-event filing disclosed that many but not all IT systems were impacted, billing and label-making for deliveries were affected, and data was exfiltrated or destroyed, with the scope of personal information still under investigation. Management says no material impact "as of date" but flags potential litigation/regulatory exposure. ~$0.5M of nonrecurring legal cost hit Q1 2026 (combined with CEO-transition legal). The unresolved scope is an idiosyncratic tail risk.

**AJR labor issue.** A post-close E-Verify review of AJR uncovered employment-eligibility problems, triggering major workforce turnover and incremental 2025 cost. Management says inefficiency is "diminishing" and will ease as work transfers to Santiago, but it remains a 2026 drag and is a cautionary tale on diligence for an M&A-dependent strategy.

**Acquisition dependence and goodwill/intangible risk.** With ~50% of long-term growth expected from M&A, a "quiet" pipeline and recent outbids threaten the consolidated growth rate. Goodwill plus intangibles at 51.6% of assets, combined with the Welch earnout written to zero, create real impairment risk if deal performance disappoints.

**Input cost and geopolitical exposure.** Petroleum-based polymer inputs are exposed to oil-price spikes, a newly added Q1 2026 risk factor tied to the U.S./Israel–Iran conflict. With the energy backdrop unsettled, a sustained oil spike would pressure input costs. Offshore manufacturing (Dominican Republic near Haiti, Ireland, Mexico, Costa Rica, Puerto Rico) adds geopolitical and FX exposure; ~36% of long-lived assets are offshore.

We score risk factors 4.5: the concentration, unresolved cyber scope, and acquisition-model dependence are specific, partly unpriced risks.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 42.5% sits roughly in line with 1-year historical volatility of 43.5% (spread −1.0%), so options are fairly priced and IV is not signaling elevated event risk; the IV percentile within the current chain is low at 11%. Put/call skew is mildly call-heavy (−3.3% near-term), and the put/call OI ratio of 0.59 indicates more call than put open interest, so the options market is not positioning bearishly ahead of catalysts. This neutral-to-bullish options posture coexists with 20.3% short interest (days to cover 4.9), suggesting the short thesis is fundamental/valuation-driven rather than expressed through options. The high short interest is the more relevant signal: it creates two-sided risk. A strong 2H ramp print could trigger a squeeze (a tailwind for longs), while continued flat organic growth would validate the shorts.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The new-program ramps deliver in 2H 2026, re-accelerating organic growth into the mid-single digits or better, supported by three customers requesting capacity doubling and Intuitive's raised procedure outlook. Wound-care destocking normalizes by Q3 2026. The M&A engine resumes with accretive bolt-ons, and the AJR drag fully rolls off, restoring gross margin toward ~29.3%. Forward EPS exceeds the $11.69 next-year consensus, the multiple holds at ~27x, and the high short interest amplifies an upside move. This path supports the analyst high target of ~$360.

**Bear case.** Organic growth stays flat or turns negative as the 2H ramp slips or proves smaller than the "double capacity" rhetoric implies; the M&A pipeline stays quiet and outbids continue, removing the ~50% growth contribution; a customer in-sources or dual-sources a major program; the cyber incident produces material litigation/regulatory cost; or a goodwill impairment hits. With the multiple at ~27x trailing on flat organic growth, any of these compresses the multiple meaningfully. Downside to ~$185 (near the 52-week low of $173.86 and roughly 18x current-year EPS) is plausible.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** The next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (early-to-mid August 2026), which should provide the first read on whether the new-program ramps are materializing on schedule, followed by Q3 2026 (when wound-care destocking is expected to normalize). The full thesis resolves over the next 6–12 months.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic growth accelerates to mid-single digits or better in Q2/Q3 2026, confirming the new-program ramp (versus the current ~0% organic).
- A resumption of accretive M&A at disciplined multiples, restoring the ~50% growth contribution, combined with current-year estimate revisions turning sharply positive (+10%+).
- A pullback toward the high-$180s/low-$190s that compresses the forward multiple below ~18x while the 2H ramp evidence stays intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Loss, in-sourcing, or material order reduction from Intuitive or Stryker.
- A goodwill/intangible impairment charge, or a second underperforming earnout signaling broader M&A execution problems.
- Material cyber-incident litigation/regulatory cost, or organic growth turning negative for two consecutive quarters.

## Conclusion

UFP Technologies is a high-quality medical CDMO with a genuine moat, a clean balance sheet (leverage ~1.14x, FCF margin ~13%, Z-Score 3.62), and credible secular tailwinds from robotic surgery and single-use device adoption. The forward demand signals (three of four new-program customers requesting capacity doubling, customer co-investment in new facilities, and Intuitive's raised procedure outlook) are legitimately encouraging.

The constraint is that the current valuation already credits the favorable forward case. The thesis depends on a 2H 2026 re-acceleration that has not yet appeared in reported numbers, plus continued M&A from a pipeline management calls "quiet." With ~46% two-customer concentration, an unresolved cyber incident, and 20.3% short interest, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate UFPT a **HOLD** (growth-oriented), appropriate for investors already long who want exposure to the 2H ramp, while new buyers are better served waiting for either confirmation of organic re-acceleration in the August/Q3 prints or a more attractive entry price.
