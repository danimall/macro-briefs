## Executive Summary

City Holding operates one of the more profitable community bank franchises in the United States. Q2 2026 return on average assets was 1.98% and return on average tangible common equity 20.7%, against national peer medians of roughly 1.15% and 13.3% respectively for banks of comparable size. The efficiency ratio was 48.1%. Net interest margin held at 3.97%. Holding-company CET1 is 17.06% and tangible common equity to tangible assets is 9.85%, with the entire securities portfolio classified available-for-sale, so there are no unrecognized held-to-maturity marks behind the reported capital ratios. Non-performing assets fell to 0.24% of loans and net charge-offs ran 0.02% of average loans in the first half.

The problem is the price and the growth arithmetic behind it. Reported Q2 EPS rose 2.6% to $2.35 while net income declined 0.3% to $33.298 million; average diluted shares fell 2.83%. Pre-provision net revenue grew 6.4% year over year, but the gap between that and flat net income is the end of credit reserve releases (a $1.909 million provision recovery in Q2 2025 became a $0.436 million provision expense in Q2 2026) plus a higher effective tax rate (19.4% versus 18.9%). The deposit-repricing engine that produced margin expansion is close to exhausted: the cost of interest-bearing liabilities fell only 1 basis point sequentially, and the two largest interest-bearing deposit buckets sit at 0.77% and 0.84%. Modeled 12-month net income sensitivity to a 300bp rate cut deteriorated from -10.0% at year-end 2025 to -12.4% at June 30, 2026. Loans were flat in the first half at $4.502 billion after 5.4% growth in FY2025, and the four-year population growth of the footprint counties was roughly 1.0% versus 2.6% nationally.

At $144.94 the stock trades at 15.7x current-year consensus of $9.26, 15.1x next-year $9.58, 3.12x tangible book value of $46.40, and yields 2.40%. It sits 1.8% below the 52-week high of $147.58, above the $139.80 consensus mean price target and above the $143.00 median, with all five covering analysts at hold. My base case is $148 on 15.5x next-year earnings, implying a total return including dividends of roughly 4.5% over twelve months, against a bear case near $120 (-17%). That asymmetry is unfavorable, and it justifies trimming exposure rather than holding. The rating change is driven by a 7.5% price advance since the prior review against approximately 2% of upward estimate revision.

## Company Overview

City Holding is a West Virginia-chartered financial holding company in Charleston operating a single bank subsidiary, City National Bank of West Virginia, and reporting one segment. At June 30, 2026 it had $6.774 billion of assets, 95 branches and 924 full-time-equivalent employees. The network follows the I-64 corridor from Lexington, Kentucky through Virginia and the I-81 corridor through the Shenandoah Valley: 58 branches in West Virginia, 22 in Kentucky, 13 in Virginia, 3 in southeastern Ohio.

The franchise economics rest on deposit share in markets that attract little large-bank competition. Disclosed share is approximately 13% in the West Virginia counties where it operates, 24% in eastern Kentucky counties, 19% in Lawrence County, Ohio and 5% along the Virginia I-81 corridor. That position produces a deposit base of $5.340 billion of which 26.6% is non-interest-bearing, only 15% is uninsured, and only 14.9% of time deposits exceed $250,000. Cost of interest-bearing liabilities is 1.75% and the loan-to-deposit ratio is 84.3%. Deposits funded 78.9% of assets versus 73.0% for the $3-10 billion peer group.

The company runs 96 branches against a national peer median of 38 at comparable asset size (roughly 2.5x peer density) while sustaining a 48.1% efficiency ratio. With 924 FTEs across 95 branches, that is roughly 9.7 FTEs per branch inclusive of all corporate staff, achieved through heavy use of interactive teller machines, ATMs, mobile and IVR channels. The branch density is the mechanism that generates the non-interest-bearing deposit mix and the low funding cost, and management appears to have solved the payroll problem that normally accompanies it.

Loan mix at year-end 2025 was roughly 47% residential mortgage plus home equity and 52% commercial and industrial plus commercial real estate. Fee income is 25.4% of Q2 revenue, dominated by service charges (38.5% of the first-half total) and bankcard revenue (35.4%), with wealth and investment management at 16.6% growing fastest (+14.4% year over year).

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability.** FY2025 net income was $130.5 million on diluted EPS of $8.93, ROAA 1.97%, ROATCE 21.2%, efficiency 48.2%. Q2 2026 continued at ROAA 1.98% and ROATCE 20.7%. Trailing four-quarter EPS is $9.14 ($2.41 + $2.18 + $2.20 + $2.35), placing the trailing P/E at 15.9x. These are 95th-to-100th-percentile returns within the company's disclosed peer sets, and they are the basis on which 2025 cash incentives paid at 190% of target.

**Quality of earnings in Q2.** Pre-provision net revenue was $41.756 million versus $39.252 million, up 6.4%. The provision swing cost $2.345 million pretax and the higher tax rate cost $0.248 million, leaving net income down $0.089 million. All of the 2.6% EPS growth came from the 2.83% reduction in average diluted shares. On a half-year basis, PPNR grew 7.0%, net income 2.0% and EPS 4.6% on a 2.63% share count decline. The operating engine is compounding mid-single digits; reported earnings growth is currently being manufactured at the share-count line.

Provision normalization is a live headwind rather than a hypothetical. FY2025 carried a net *recovery* of credit losses of $1.4 million; the first half of 2026 carried a $1.049 million provision. Normalizing to 15 basis points of average loans (~$6.7 million annually on $4.49 billion) versus the ~$2.1 million annualized first-half rate would cost roughly $4.6 million pretax, or about $0.27 per share after tax. That is a sensitivity, not a forecast, but it frames why forward growth coexists with 7% PPNR growth.

**Net interest margin.** Year over year, earning asset yield fell 14bp to 5.24% while the cost of interest-bearing liabilities fell 20bp to 1.75%, netting +2bp of NIM to 3.97%. Sequentially the liability cost fell 1bp. Savings deposits ($1.282 billion average) pay 0.77% and interest-bearing demand ($1.324 billion) pays 0.84%, both effectively at floors. Only time deposits at 2.96% ($1.310 billion) retain meaningful repricing room. Separately, total securities yield fell from 4.22% to 3.85% year over year; the maturity of three $50 million interest-rate swaps in late 2025 and a $100 million loan fair-value hedge in March 2026 is a plausible contributor, though the swap contribution has not been isolated and the growth in tax-exempt municipals (average balance $158.4 million at a 3.31% taxable-equivalent yield versus $131.3 million at 2.79%) argues against muni mix being the drag.

Rate-cut asymmetry has worsened, which is the single most important forward-looking financial disclosure in the filings:

| Shock | 12/31/25 | 6/30/26 |
|---|---|---|
| +100bp | +3.1% | +3.0% |
| -100bp | -1.6% | -2.1% |
| -200bp | -4.8% | -6.2% |
| -300bp | -10.0% | -12.4% |

With roughly 49% of deposits already at 0.77-0.84%, cuts reduce asset yields with almost no offsetting liability relief. The direction of the next Fed move is genuinely two-sided. The convexity is not: modest hikes help modestly, meaningful cuts hurt disproportionately.

**Balance sheet and capital.** Total assets $6.774 billion, up 0.8% from year-end. Gross loans $4.502 billion, essentially unchanged from $4.503 billion at 12/31/25. All securities are available-for-sale, so the mark flows fully through AOCI and tangible equity: the net-of-tax unrealized loss improved from $(115.7) million at 12/31/24 to $(75.7) million at 12/31/25 before deteriorating to $(81.2) million at 6/30/26, equal to 12.5% of tangible equity ($81.2M / $652.0M). Gross pre-tax unrealized losses were $110.1 million. Duration is real: $1,160.8 million of the $1,583.3 million AFS amortized cost, or 73.3%, matures beyond ten years, predominantly agency MBS.

Capital: holding-company CET1 17.06%, total risk-based 17.53%, leverage 10.97%; bank-level CET1 15.01%, leverage 9.65%; TCE/TA 9.85%. Bank-level CET1 rose from 13.42% at 12/31/25 to 15.01% at 6/30/26 while parent-company cash fell from $148.9 million to $86.4 million. The $62.5 million parent cash decline closely matches first-half treasury purchases of $38.137 million plus dividends paid of $24.925 million. The reading is that the parent funded capital return from its own cash rather than upstreaming bank dividends, letting the bank retain earnings and rebuild capital; bank dividend capacity available without regulatory approval rose from $43.7 million to $83.0 million plus subsequent net profits. Ordinary intercompany timing is an equally consistent explanation. Either way, $86.4 million of parent cash covers roughly 1.7 years of the ~$48.9 million annualized dividend absent further bank dividends, so bank-level capital is the operative constraint on capital return, not the headline 17% consolidated CET1.

**Credit.** The allowance for credit losses is 0.44% of loans, or $19.839 million. That covers 22.5% of the combined criticized and classified C&I plus CRE balances of $88.302 million. Hotels are $396.1 million, 8.85% of gross loans and 21.2% of CRE, at disclosed average 1.75x debt service coverage and 58% LTV, with $3.298 million special mention and $21.947 million substandard — criticized-plus-classified of 6.37% of the hotel book against $1.940 million of allocated reserve. The trend is improving (substandard hotels were $23.712 million at 12/31/25 and no hotel loan sits in the collateral-dependent individually-evaluated bucket at 6/30/26), and consumer real estate at 47% of loans with 58-68% LTVs justifies part of the thin reserve. A 20% loss severity on the entire criticized commercial book (~$17.7 million) would nearly exhaust the allowance. This is an earnings exposure, not a solvency one: FY2025 pretax income was $161.5 million.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus has current-year EPS at $9.26 (revised +2.3% over 30 days, +1.9% over 90 days) and next-year at $9.58 (+0.9% / +1.5%). Those are positive revisions and they deserve credit — estimates rising is preferable to estimates falling, and the four-quarter surprise record is three beats and one miss with the most recent quarter beating by 3.5%. But the magnitude is 2%, not the 30%-plus velocity that signals a mispriced re-rating. Implied EPS growth is 3.7% this year and 3.5% next.

The structural constraints on getting more than that:

**Footprint demographics.** Disclosed 2020-2024 county population change: West Virginia -0.1%, Lawrence County Ohio -4.1%, Kentucky +1.4%, Virginia +4.4%, versus the U.S. average of +2.6%. Aggregate footprint population grew roughly 1.0% over four years, about 40% of the national rate, and 58 of 95 branches sit in the flat-to-shrinking West Virginia counties.

**Loan growth.** Flat in the first half. Q2 grew $10.2 million sequentially, with C&I +$12.5 million and home equity +$6.3 million offset by residential -$6.9 million and consumer -$3.1 million. Period-end deposits declined $3.5 million sequentially, though average depository balances rose $59.5 million.

**Customer acquisition.** Net new DDA accounts as a percentage of base ran +3.8% in 2021, +1.9% in 2022 and 2023, +1.8% in 2024 and +1.3% in 2025. The first-half 2026 figure of +0.8% on 2,081 net adds is a partial year and is not annualized — doubling it gives roughly 4,162, above 2025's full-year 3,548 — so the claim that 2026 is decelerating further is not supported. Gross openings of 16,175 in the first half versus 31,427 for all of 2025 imply the 2021-2025 deceleration came from higher attrition rather than slower acquisition, which is the less comfortable of the two explanations.

**Declining acquisition accretion.** Purchase accounting accretion has fallen from $3.634 million in FY2024 to $2.594 million in FY2025 to $1.088 million in the first half of 2026, a shrinking NII tailwind. The last transaction closed in Q1 2023 (Citizens Commerce Bancshares). No Item 1.01 or Item 2.01 8-K and no S-4 appear in the filing history from January 2024 through July 2026.

The genuine growth options are wealth management (+14.4%, but only 16.6% of fee income), continued buybacks, and M&A. The bank has the capital, a "Satisfactory" 2025 CRA rating sufficient for deal approval, and a demonstrated integration history. That optionality is real but undated, and a bank paying 3.1x its own tangible book has limited currency advantage in a stock deal.

## Valuation Assessment

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Price | $144.94 |
| Trailing four-quarter EPS | $9.14 → 15.9x |
| FY2026 consensus $9.26 | 15.7x |
| FY2027 consensus $9.58 | 15.1x |
| Book value/share $57.57 | 2.52x |
| Tangible book value/share $46.40 | 3.12x |
| Dividend $3.48 annualized | 2.40% yield; 37.6% payout on 2026E |
| 52-week range | $113.21 - $147.58 (1.8% below high) |

Against the regional bank peer set supplied (STEL 18.7x, TFIN 63.1x, NBHC 16.2x, FCF 12.9x, OFG 9.8x; median 16.2x, median yield 2.9%), CHCO's 15.9x trailing P/E is slightly below the peer median while its returns are far above. For a bank, superior ROE justifies a premium price-to-book, and 3.12x tangible book against 20.7% ROATCE is internally coherent — roughly 3x book for 21% returns implies a cost of equity near 9% with modest growth, which is defensible. What the multiple does not accommodate is the growth rate. Paying 15.7x forward earnings for 3.5% EPS growth is a PEG above 4. The earnings yield of 6.4% plus 3.5% growth gets to a high-single-digit expected return only if the multiple holds and the buyback continues, and the buyback is the part that requires scrutiny.

**The buyback arithmetic.** The March 25, 2026 authorization covers 1,000,000 shares (~7% of shares outstanding), explicitly at prices "accretive to the earnings per share of continuing shareholders," with 925,842 remaining at June 30. First-half repurchases were 321,173 shares at a weighted average $118.65, but the pace fell from 262,000 shares in Q1 to 59,156 in Q2 as the price rose. Executing the full remaining authorization at $144.94 would cost $134.2 million. Holding earnings constant at the first-half run rate and funding entirely from cash yielding ~3.7%, that would reduce shares 6.6% to 13.126 million, cut tangible equity to $517.8 million and tangible book value per share 15.0% to $39.45, take TCE/TA to roughly 7.99%, and lift EPS 3.7% to about $9.44. That is modestly accretive to EPS, materially dilutive to tangible book, and consumes roughly a fifth of the tangible capital cushion. The Q2 slowdown is consistent either with management applying its stated accretion discipline or with the bank-capital rebuild described above; both readings are supportable and neither implies aggressive repurchase at current prices. The practical conclusion is that the 2.6-2.8% annual share count reduction that has carried EPS growth should be assumed to slow at $145 rather than persist.

**Where consensus sits.** Analyst targets are $128 low, $139.80 mean, $143.00 median, $147.00 high, with all five covering analysts at hold and a stable sentiment trend. The price is above both mean and median and 1.4% below the highest target on the street. Treating analyst targets as floors applies to cheap stocks with accelerating estimates; here estimates are rising 2% and the stock trades at a premium to tangible book that already capitalizes best-in-class returns. Treating this consensus as stale would require identifying what five analysts covering a transparent, single-segment bank are missing, and I cannot construct that argument from the disclosures.

Base case: 15.5x next-year $9.58 = $148, cross-checked against roughly 3.0x an estimated year-end 2027 tangible book of about $50 (net income of ~$9.40/share less $3.60 of dividends, less buyback dilution, plus partial AOCI recovery) = $150. Bull case: 17.5x $9.58 = $168, requiring multiple expansion on sustained 21% ROATCE plus an accretive acquisition. Bear case: 13.0x $9.26 = $120, the multiple the stock carried for much of 2025 at a $113-125 trading range, reachable on rate cuts plus provision normalization.

Valuation score of 4.0 reflects a stretched multiple relative to delivered growth with no identified catalyst for re-rating, not structural impairment of the business.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is share in markets too small to attract scaled competition, and it is durable in the sense that no rational large bank is going to build 58 branches across flat-population West Virginia counties to contest it. Cumulative total shareholder return of $197.76 per $100 invested at year-end 2020 versus $162.30 for the KBW Nasdaq Regional Bank Index confirms the operating outperformance has been monetized.

The trajectory is what limits the score. Deposit share is high but not visibly rising; DDA attrition has increased; loan balances are flat; the 10-K names credit unions, finance companies and fintechs including crypto platforms among competitors, which is where low-cost transaction deposits and interchange are most exposed over a multi-year horizon. Fee income concentration reinforces this: service charges plus bankcard revenue are 73.9% of first-half fee income, and the standing "DDA overdrafts" loan category ($3.7 million) confirms an active overdraft program. The filings do not break out overdraft or NSF income separately from total service charges, so the exposure cannot be sized — a genuine disclosure gap rather than a quantified risk.

Level of competitive position is high; direction is flat to slightly eroding. Score 5.8.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Rate-cut asymmetry** (quantified above): -12.4% of net income at -300bp, worsened from -10.0% six months earlier, against a Fed whose next move is genuinely uncertain.
2. **Provision normalization**: reserve releases are exhausted; a move to 15bp of average loans is roughly $0.27 per share.
3. **Thin allowance**: 0.44% of loans, covering 22.5% of criticized/classified commercial balances. Earnings risk, not solvency risk.
4. **Hotel CRE**: 8.85% of loans, 21.2% of CRE, 6.37% criticized-or-classified with 0.49% reserve coverage of the segment. Trend improving.
5. **AFS duration**: 73.3% of amortized cost maturing beyond ten years, with the net-of-tax mark at 12.5% of tangible equity.
6. **Fee regulation**: ~74% of fee income in transaction and deposit-account lines, unsizable from disclosure.
7. **Concentration in lessor NAICS codes**: nonresidential lessors 14% and residential lessors 11% of loans at year-end. Within that, office is a modest $159.2 million (3.55% of loans) at 1.65x DSC and 62% LTV. The weakest disclosed sector is masonry contractors at $16.4 million, 1.04x DSC, 100% LTV — small, but with no equity cushion.
8. **Succession**: CEO Hageboeck is 63 with 21 years in the seat; the EVP of Commercial Banking retired effective 12/31/25; the CFO was promoted to Senior EVP per a 5/28/26 Item 5.02 filing, and an "Amendment to Employment Agreement" dated 4/29/26 was filed. Whether this constitutes a succession sequence is unverified. A legacy termination benefit of $1,534,017 payable over 36 months on *any* voluntary resignation is fully accrued.
9. **Governance items already known**: classified three-class board, a 280G excise-tax gross-up at 147.5% in the CEO change-of-control package, insider ownership of only 1.75%, and an unwritten policy for non-lending related-party transactions above $120,000. Aggregate change-of-control cost for the four continuing NEOs is roughly $11.05 million, about 0.54% of market cap — not a takeover deterrent.
10. **Liquidity of the security itself**: average daily volume of approximately 106,000 shares, five covering analysts, and short interest of 9.8% of float at 5.9 days to cover. That short position is unusually large for a $2 billion community bank and constrains how quickly a position can be exited into weakness.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 17.6% against 19.1% one-year realized, a fair-to-slightly-cheap spread, and sits in the 3rd percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in contango (17.6% at one month, 19.9% at three and six months), a normal or complacent configuration. The one-month tenor shows elevated put demand (put/call skew 20.3%, 15.2% aggregate), which flattens at three months (1.2%) and inverts by December (-3.3%): near-dated downside protection is being paid for, with no comparable bid further out. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±3.7%. Implied volatility collapsed from 39.0% to 17.6% since the prior snapshot as the July 22 print resolved, so the earnings premium is gone. With only 77 contracts across three expirations, confidence in these readings is low. Read directionally, the structure is consistent with holders paying for short-dated insurance on a stock near its high rather than with positioning for a sustained decline; it corroborates rather than drives the trim recommendation, and it makes protective puts inexpensive for holders who prefer to retain the position.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($168, +16%).** A best-in-class operator with 21% ROATCE, 17% CET1, a clean all-AFS securities book and 26.6% non-interest-bearing deposits deserves a scarcity premium, and 3.1x tangible book is not obviously excessive for those returns. Pre-provision revenue is compounding 7%. Estimates are rising, the surprise record is three beats in four quarters, wealth management is growing 14%, and the dividend was raised 10.1% to $0.87 with a 37.6% payout. Excess capital plus a Satisfactory CRA rating gives the bank capacity for an accretive acquisition that would restore the growth rate and reset the multiple higher. If the Fed hikes rather than cuts, the +3.0% net income sensitivity works in shareholders' favor while peers with liability-sensitive books suffer.

**Bear case ($120, -17%).** Consensus EPS growth already depends on a buyback that is 15% dilutive to tangible book at current prices and whose pace has fallen from 262,000 shares in Q1 to 59,156 in Q2. The deposit-repricing lever is spent at 0.77-0.84% deposit rates, provision normalization costs roughly $0.27 per share, purchase-accounting accretion is halving, the tax rate is rising, and loans are flat in a footprint growing at 40% of the national population rate. A 200-300bp easing cycle removes 6-12% of net income while compressing the multiple that the current price requires. Reversion to the 13x forward multiple the stock carried for most of 2025 puts it at $120 with no fundamental deterioration required.

The bull case needs multiple expansion or an acquisition; the bear case needs only mean reversion in the multiple or one rate-cut cycle. That is the asymmetry driving the rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $148 (15.5x FY2027 consensus $9.58, cross-checked at ~3.0x estimated year-end-2027 tangible book of ~$50), +2.1% from $144.94, or approximately +4.5% total return including the 2.40% dividend over twelve months. Bull case $168 (+15.9%). Bear case $120 (-17.2%). A rough probability weighting of 25% bull / 50% base / 25% bear gives an expected return near +2 to +3%, below a fair return for equity risk in a bank with negative convexity to rate cuts.

**De-risking plan.** Trim on strength into the $145-148 range, retaining no more than a half position for income continuity. There is no dated catalyst requiring immediate exit; the trim is a valuation and asymmetry decision. Holders unwilling to sell should note that one-month implied volatility at 17.6% in the 3rd percentile of the chain makes protective puts unusually cheap relative to 19.1% realized volatility. Given 106,000-share average daily volume and 9.8% short interest at 5.9 days to cover, exit liquidity is limited; scale out rather than exit in one print.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE.**
- Price below $128 (the low analyst target, roughly 13.4x FY2027 consensus) with credit metrics intact — NPAs at or below 0.30% and net charge-offs below 0.10% of average loans.
- An announced acquisition on terms accretive to tangible book within three years, restoring earning-asset growth above 4%.
- Two consecutive quarters of net interest margin at or above 3.97% *with* average loan growth above 3% annualized, demonstrating that asset yields rather than deposit repricing can carry the margin.
- Repurchases resuming above 150,000 shares per quarter alongside stable TCE/TA above 9.0%, which would validate the buyback-driven EPS algorithm rather than pause it.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.**
- Two or more Fed cuts realized with management confirming the modeled -2.1% per 100bp sensitivity in reported net interest income, without offsetting fee growth.
- Provision expense exceeding 20bp of average loans in any quarter, or criticized-plus-classified commercial balances rising above $110 million (from $88.3 million).
- Hotel substandard balances rising above $30 million, or the appearance of hotel credits in the collateral-dependent individually-evaluated bucket.
- Any adverse resolution of overdraft or NSF fee regulation that management sizes at more than 5% of fee income.
- Cumulative CEO open-market sales exceeding 40% of direct holdings, or clustered selling by three or more executives within 30 days alongside a downward estimate revision.

**Reassessment date.** The Q3 2026 earnings release in late October 2026 is the natural checkpoint: it will show whether the third-quarter provision normalizes, whether the cost of interest-bearing liabilities has bottomed, and whether the repurchase pace recovered from the 59,156-share second quarter. The September FOMC meeting is the other dated input given the rate-cut sensitivity.

## Conclusion

City Holding is a better bank than its multiple implies and a worse investment than its multiple allows. The operating record is not in dispute: 1.98% ROAA, 20.7% ROATCE, 48.1% efficiency, 17.06% CET1, 0.24% non-performing assets, an all-available-for-sale securities book that leaves no hidden marks, and a compensation structure — CEO pay up 1.5% over two years while net income rose 14.1%, no pension or SERP, mandatory two-year post-vest holding periods, pledging prohibited, zero non-audit fees to the auditor — that is more shareholder-aligned than most of the sector.

What has changed is the price. At $144.94 the stock has advanced 21.9% over twelve months and 7.5% since early July while current-year consensus rose 2%. It trades at 15.7x forward earnings for 3.5% EPS growth that is entirely share-count derived, at 3.12x tangible book, above the $139.80 consensus mean target, 1.8% below its 52-week high, with all five covering analysts at hold. Pre-provision revenue growth of 7% is being absorbed by the end of reserve releases, a rising tax rate, and halving purchase-accounting accretion, while the deposit-repricing lever that produced margin expansion has reached deposit rates of 0.77-0.84%. Modeled sensitivity to a 300bp easing cycle worsened to -12.4% of net income in six months. The buyback that has carried EPS is 15% dilutive to tangible book at this price and its pace has already fallen.

The scores decline from 5.8 to 5.4, with valuation the primary mover (4.5 to 4.0) and growth easing to 4.2 on flat loans and spent margin levers. Financial health remains high at 7.5. Existing holders should trim into the $145-148 area and reassess after the October print; prospective buyers should wait for $128 or below, or for an acquisition that restores the growth rate.
