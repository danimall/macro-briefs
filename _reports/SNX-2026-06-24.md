**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TD SYNNEX is the world's second-largest IT distributor, a working-capital-intensive intermediary connecting roughly 2,500 OEM vendors with about 150,000 reseller and retail customers. The structural identity of the business is thin-margin logistics and credit intermediation (FY25 net margin was 1.3%), but a meaningful earnings inflection is underway, and the source is identifiable. In Q1 FY2026 management recast its reporting into four segments, breaking out **Hyve Solutions**, its hyperscale/AI-infrastructure design-and-manufacture business, as a standalone unit. Hyve generated $2.15B revenue (+24% YoY) at a 7.23% operating margin, roughly three times the company's blended rate and far above the distribution segments (Americas 2.52%, Europe 1.73%, APJ 2.99%). As Hyve grows as a share of the mix, both blended margins and earnings quality improve structurally. This is the engine behind the Q1 results: revenue +18.1%, operating income +60.7%, non-GAAP EPS $4.73 versus $2.80 a year prior.

The estimate-revision data confirms the inflection is being recognized by consensus. Current-year EPS estimates rose +16.7% over 90 days to $17.11; next-year estimates rose +19.6% to $19.25. These revision velocities have historically preceded continued re-rating, and they sit alongside a clean two-beat track record (Q1 beat by 41.6%). The growth thesis is intact and well-supported.

What has changed is price. SNX has appreciated roughly 127% over twelve months, from $123.84 to $290. Prior reports rated this a BUY when the valuation cushion was wider; that cushion has now largely closed. At ~24x trailing GAAP EPS and ~17x forward non-GAAP, the stock trades above the mean analyst target of $276 and below the median of $300. Options markets are in backwardation with near-term IV at 51.3% versus 30% historical vol, pricing a ±10.5% move into the June 25 print. The Hyve story is durable, but the residual asymmetry at $290 no longer carries the BUY forward unchanged after four consecutive BUY ratings. We move to HOLD with a growth bias, looking to re-engage on any post-earnings pullback.

## Company Overview

TD SYNNEX was created by the September 2021 merger of SYNNEX and Tech Data, which explains its elevated leverage (D/E 3.05) and large intangible base (~$296M/year of merger-related amortization). The business operates two product portfolios, Endpoint Solutions (PCs, mobile, peripherals, printers) and Advanced Solutions (data center, hybrid cloud, security, networking, servers, software), plus a services layer spanning cloud orchestration, integration, logistics, and financing. Active SKU count exceeds 200,000.

The competitive moat is scale, vendor breadth, working-capital financing, and logistics/integration capability rather than technology IP. The Q1 FY2026 segment recast into Americas distribution, Europe distribution, APJ distribution, and Hyve Solutions is the most important structural disclosure in years, because it isolates the higher-margin AI-infrastructure business that increasingly drives consolidated earnings. Hyve is an ODM/contract manufacturer for hyperscale and technology customers: TD SYNNEX designs and builds the infrastructure, it does not own the end AI products. Much of Hyve's revenue runs through a customer-owned-procurement model recognized on a net basis, which both flatters segment margin optics (+262bps in Q1) and understates true volume. The relevant metric to track over time is Hyve gross billings, not reported revenue.

## Financial Analysis

The full-year trajectory shows steady recovery and margin expansion. Revenue moved from $57.6B (FY23) to $58.5B (FY24) to $62.5B (FY25, +6.9%), though roughly $2.8B of FY25 "growth" reflects a shift to net-basis revenue presentation; constant-currency growth was +6.1%. Operating income expanded faster than revenue, from $1.19B (FY24) to $1.41B (FY25, +18.5%), lifting operating margin to 2.26%. Net income was $827.7M (net margin 1.3%), and non-GAAP diluted EPS reached $13.19 versus $11.68 in FY24. Free cash flow was $1.39B (OCF $1.53B less $142M capex), confirming the capex-light, cash-generative profile of the distribution model.

Q1 FY2026 marked the inflection. Revenue of $17.16B grew +18.1% YoY (+13.2% constant currency); operating income rose +60.7% to $489M with operating margin of 2.85% (up from 2.10%); net income nearly doubled to $326.9M, and non-GAAP EPS hit $4.73 versus $2.80. Gross margin expanded to 7.30% from 6.87%, of which ~37bps was net-presentation effect and the remainder genuine margin expansion across both distribution portfolios.

On health metrics: Altman Z of 2.65 sits in the grey zone, which is normal for a thin-margin distributor and not a distress signal; the Piotroski composite is strong; current ratio is 1.21. Total borrowings are ~$4.7B (Senior Notes maturing 2026–2035), with $700M due August 2026 comfortably covered and no revolver or AR-securitization drawn at quarter-end. The legacy term loan was repaid in full in October 2025, partly funded by a $600M 5.300% Senior Notes issuance due 2035.

The swing factor is working capital. Q1 operating cash flow was negative $896M as inventory built ~$1.4B to support growth, a normal seasonal/growth pattern, but it means reported FCF will be lumpy. The cash conversion cycle remains tight at 23 days. The company runs significant adjacent financing: $3.4B of supplier-finance obligations in payables and $2.0B of receivables sold. Capital allocation has been shareholder-friendly: ~$596M of buybacks in FY25 reduced diluted shares from 95.5M (FY22) to ~80M now (a ~16% reduction), with $1.1B of authorization remaining and a dividend at $0.48/quarter (+9% YoY). Earnings quality is decent but adjustment-heavy: the ~33% GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap is dominated by persistent Tech Data merger amortization, though OCF reliably exceeds net income over full-year periods.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on two demand vectors plus a mix shift. First, the AI-infrastructure buildout directly benefits Hyve and the Advanced Solutions distribution portfolio. Hyve operating income grew +65.6% YoY in Q1 to $155M; as it compounds at a 7%+ margin inside an otherwise ~2% business, the blended margin and earnings-quality improvement is mechanical. This places SNX on the same AI-server capex cycle that drove HPE's beat. Second, a PC refresh cycle supports the Endpoint portfolio.

The estimate-revision data is the strongest forward indicator. Current-quarter EPS rose +20.1% over 90 days to $4.14. Revisions of this magnitude, around +20% across multiple line items over 90 days when combined with revenue growth above 18%, have historically preceded continued outperformance as consensus catches up. The two-quarter beat record (41.6% surprise in Q1, +1.7% in the prior quarter) supports confidence in the current consensus. UBS flagged SNX as a likely beat-and-raise into the June 25 Q2 print, where consensus is ~$4.07 EPS on ~$16.7B revenue; Q1 next-quarter guidance came in ~4% above consensus.

The growth thesis is the strongest pillar of this report and warrants the heaviest weight. The risk to it is not demand but presentation and concentration: net-vs-gross revenue distortion obscures true scale, and Hyve's single-customer concentration introduces order volatility into the segment driving the re-rating.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the call shifts. SNX trades at ~24x trailing GAAP EPS ($9.95) and roughly 17x forward non-GAAP EPS ($17.11 current-year consensus). On a forward non-GAAP basis the multiple is not stretched for a business inflecting toward double-digit EPS growth; PEG is well below 1.5 on next-year's +19.6% revised estimates. The cycle-adjustment logic applies: trailing GAAP P/E understates earnings power because Q1 represents an acceleration, not a trough, and merger amortization depresses GAAP.

The problem is the price level relative to the opportunity that prior reports captured. At $290, the stock sits above the mean analyst target of $276 and below the median of $300, with a high of $341 and a low of $200. After a 127% twelve-month run, the asymmetry has narrowed. Applying the guardrail on analyst targets: this is a momentum stock with positive revisions and a reasonable forward multiple, so consensus targets are likely to drift higher, but the stock is no longer cheap enough relative to its growth that the "targets are floors" principle delivers a wide margin of safety. The forward multiple is fair, not cheap.

Two analytical cautions reinforce a measured stance. First, valuation as a standalone signal has been reliably backwards in recent regimes: a "cheap" screen is not a buy trigger, and here SNX is not even screening cheap on a peer-relative basis without the Hyve premium. Second, the durable mid-cycle earnings power of the distribution base remains ~2% margin; the bull case capitalizes Hyve growth into the multiple, which works only if Hyve's net-presentation-flattered margins prove sustainable and its single-customer revenue holds. A base case of ~$320 (roughly 16.6x next-year $19.25 non-GAAP EPS with mild mix-driven multiple support) offers ~10% upside to current, adequate but not the asymmetric setup that justified the prior BUY stack.

## Competitive Landscape

In distribution, SNX and Ingram Micro are the two scale leaders in a consolidating field that also includes Arrow Electronics, Westcon-Comstor, ScanSource, ALSO, and Esprinet. Scale advantages in vendor terms, logistics, and working-capital financing are durable but the segment is mature, low-margin, and structurally pressured by direct-to-customer vendor sales and the secular shift to cloud/as-a-Service, which can cannibalize hardware volume.

The competitive trajectory is improving at the margin because of Hyve, where SNX competes against Jabil, Celestica, Flex, Quanta, and Wiwynn for AI-server ODM work. The Q1 evidence indicates SNX is winning share of a fast-growing pool, and the FY25-to-Q1 acceleration suggests it is currently on the right side of the cloud/AI dynamics that pressure the core. The competitive signal is positive but should be confirmed rather than treated as conclusive; the recent strength of competitive trajectory as a return predictor has softened, and Hyve's concentration means a single customer's order cadence can swing the segment narrative quickly.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that matter for this specific business:

1. **Vendor concentration and thin-margin sensitivity.** Apple is 12% of revenue, HP Inc. 10% (newly crossed in FY25). Vendor agreements are short-term and terminable on short notice; vendors can cut rebates or sell direct. At ~1.3% net margin, small rebate or pricing changes swing the bottom line materially.

2. **Customer concentration in Hyve.** One customer is 11–12% of total revenue, and the systems-design business carries heavy single-customer concentration with "limited or no volume guarantees." The segment driving the AI re-rating is the one with the most order volatility and inventory risk.

3. **Working-capital and credit cyclicality.** A demand air-pocket or customer-solvency stress directly hits cash flow and could pressure the securitization programs. Q1 OCF of -$896M illustrates how quickly the cash picture inverts during a growth/inventory-build phase.

4. **Leverage and rate sensitivity.** $4.7B of debt with rating-linked step-ups and variable-rate components; FY25 interest expense was $357M. A higher-for-longer rate path raises the cost of the working-capital financing model.

5. **China dependence in IT operations** and an expanded, relatively new AI risk factor (EU AI Act, operational, and demand-cannibalization vectors).

6. **Recurring cybersecurity intrusions** (2021, 2022, 2023 by an apparently recurring threat actor; a partner breach Nov 2024), none deemed material, but a pattern worth monitoring.

The Q1 10-Q reported no material changes to risk factors from the 10-K. The legacy France/Apple antitrust matter largely resolved favorably (fine reduced from €76.1M to €24.9M, paid; related eBizcuss civil suit dismissed November 2024, under appeal).

### Options Market Signal

The options complex is sending a near-term caution. Term structure is in backwardation, with 1-month ATM IV pricing an implied ±10.5% move, a rich 21.3-point spread over historical vol at the 81st percentile within the chain, squarely a bet on the June 25 Q2 print. The IV reading collapsed from a corrupted 317% snapshot to a clean 51.3%, so the prior figure is noise. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.3% near-term) and put/call volume and OI ratios are low (0.34 and 0.26), indicating no directional bearish positioning; the market is pricing event volatility, not a downside lean. For a HOLD with a growth bias, this argues for patience: let the binary resolve before adding.

### Insider Signal

Insiders are uniformly net sellers with zero open-market buying. Former CEO Richard Hume sold heavily and repeatedly (May–June 2026, $228–285); Chair Dennis Polk sells under a 10b5-1 plan adopted January 2026; CFO David Jordan sold 3,225 shares at $189 in April; the North America and Europe presidents also sold. Most sales were programmatic and executed below today's $290, consistent with diversification rather than conviction selling, and the estimate-revision velocity is the dominant signal here. The complete absence of buying, set against an unusually dense cadence of executive-change 8-Ks (CEO transition Sept 2024, CFO transition Oct 2025, multiple Item 5.02 filings through 2025–2026), tempers the "smart-money conviction" element of the bull case and weighs on the management-quality score.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$341, ~18% upside).** Hyve continues to compound at 60%+ operating-income growth on the AI-server cycle, its net-presentation margins prove sustainable, and the mix shift drives blended operating margin durably above 3%. Beat-and-raise execution on June 25 pushes consensus EPS toward $20+ for next year, and the market re-rates the stock on rising estimates. Continued buybacks (16% share reduction since FY22, $1.1B remaining) amplify per-share results. The PC refresh and Advanced Solutions distribution add a second growth leg.

**Bear Case (~$200, ~31% downside).** A demand air-pocket in Hyve, driven by the single dominant customer's order timing or a broader AI-capex digestion phase, reverses the margin story and exposes the ~2% core. Working capital that built inventory in Q1 becomes a liability if volume softens. Vendor rebate compression or a direct-sales shift by Apple/HP pressures the thin core margin. The stock de-rates back toward the analyst low of $200 as the AI premium unwinds. The near-term IV and the high target dispersion ($200 to $341) signal the market sees this tail as live.

**Base Case (~$320, ~10% upside).** Hyve growth moderates but remains healthy, June 25 delivers a modest beat-and-raise consistent with the +20% revision trend, and the stock holds an ~16.6x forward multiple on next-year's $19.25 non-GAAP EPS. The durable return comes from earnings growth plus buyback-driven share reduction rather than further multiple expansion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth bias). Reassessment timeline: the June 25, 2026 Q2 FY2026 print is the immediate catalyst; reassess fully on that release.**

The fundamental thesis remains intact and improving. This is a downgrade on a closed valuation gap after a 127% run that has moved the stock above mean analyst target with binary event risk priced into next week, not on deteriorating fundamentals. The growth-hold classification reflects a positive 12-month thesis (Hyve mix shift, +20% estimate revisions, beat-and-raise setup) that no longer offers the asymmetric entry that justified four prior BUYs.

**Upgrade triggers (back to BUY):**
- A post-earnings pullback to the low-$250s or below on no fundamental deterioration, restoring a >20% gap to a $320+ base case.
- Q2 confirms Hyve gross billings and segment margin durability (operating margin holding ≥7%), with next-year EPS estimates revised above $20.
- Evidence the net-presentation effect is not the primary driver of margin expansion (i.e., underlying distribution gross margin continues to climb ex-presentation).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Hyve revenue or operating income decelerates sharply (segment revenue growth below 5% YoY) signaling AI-capex digestion or single-customer loss.
- A guidance cut or a Q2 miss that reverses the estimate-revision trend (90-day revisions turn negative).
- Working capital deteriorates without volume support, a sustained negative FCF trend not explained by growth-driven inventory build.

**Base case target $320** (16.6x next-year $19.25 non-GAAP EPS, ~10% above current). **Bull $341** (~18% upside). **Bear $200** (~31% downside, the analyst-low scenario on AI-premium unwind).

## Conclusion

TD SYNNEX is a genuine AI-infrastructure beneficiary structured as a low-margin distributor, and the Hyve segment recast made that visible. The earnings inflection is validated by estimate revisions of +17-20% over 90 days plus a 41.6% Q1 beat. We have rated this stock BUY through its run from the $150s, and the call worked.

At $290, after a 127% twelve-month advance, the residual asymmetry has compressed to roughly fair value: the stock trades above mean analyst target, the forward multiple is reasonable but not cheap, and the options market is pricing a ±10.5% move into a binary June 25 print with no directional cushion. The combination argues for stepping down to HOLD rather than carrying a fifth consecutive BUY at a richer price. Investors who own SNX should hold for the Hyve growth thesis; new capital is better committed on a post-earnings pullback toward the low-$250s, where the risk/reward re-opens. Overall score 6.2, HOLD with a growth bias.
