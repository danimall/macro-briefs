## Executive Summary

Merit Medical Systems has declined 33% from its 52-week high of $97.18 to $64.17, creating a valuation disconnect between the company's fundamental trajectory and its current price. The decline was catalyzed by three factors: tariff concerns related to imports from Mexico, Ireland, and China; the CEO transition from founder Fred Lampropoulos to Martha Aronson; and broad analyst price target reductions. Q1 2026 results delivered 7.5% revenue growth, 40bps of YoY non-GAAP operating margin expansion, and non-GAAP EPS of $0.94 (up 9% YoY) — demonstrating that the business is performing through the headwinds the market is pricing.

The bull case rests on three pillars: (1) a valuation at ~15.8x forward non-GAAP EPS that is historically cheap for a medtech company compounding revenue at 7-8% with expanding margins, (2) coordinated open-market insider buying by five C-suite executives in early May 2026 at $60-63, and (3) a structural margin expansion program that has delivered 590bps of improvement since 2021 with further room. The primary risks are tariff escalation beyond what management has already mitigated, acquisition integration complexity, and China VBP pricing pressure.

## Company Overview

Merit Medical Systems manufactures proprietary medical devices for interventional, diagnostic, and therapeutic procedures across cardiology, radiology, oncology, critical care, and endoscopy. The company operates globally with approximately 7,500 employees, 11 manufacturing facilities, and ~2,200 patents. Revenue for FY2025 was $1.516B, growing at approximately 10% CAGR over the past three years.

The company recently reorganized its reporting structure into two categories — Foundational and Therapeutic — signaling a strategic emphasis on higher-ASP, higher-margin therapeutic products including the SCOUT radar localization system, Wrapsody endoprosthesis, and embolotherapy microspheres. Merit competes against much larger companies (Medtronic, Boston Scientific, BD, Teleflex) but differentiates through breadth of product line, custom procedural solutions, manufacturing integration, and rapid product development cycles.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** $1.516B in FY2025 (+11.8% YoY), with Q1 2026 at $381.9M (+7.5% reported, +5.2% constant currency). Constant-currency organic revenue increased approximately 3%, or approximately 4% excluding revenue from the divested product line. FY2026 guidance is $1.612-$1.634B, implying 6-8% growth. This represents deceleration from FY2025 but remains healthy for a mid-cap medtech.

**Profitability:** Non-GAAP operating margin improved from 14.4% (2021) to 20.3% (2025). Q1 2026 was 19.7%, up 40bps YoY despite tariff headwinds. This margin trajectory is the core of the investment case — even at modest revenue growth rates, continued margin expansion drives double-digit EPS compounding.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow grew from $145M (2023) to $297M (2025). Free cash flow was $216M in FY2025 (14.2% FCF margin). Q1 2026 FCF was $24.7M, up 26% YoY. At the current market cap of $3.83B, the trailing FCF yield is approximately 5.6%.

**Balance Sheet:** Cash of $488M, total debt obligations of $747.5M (3.0% fixed-rate convertible notes due 2029), and $697M of untapped credit facilities. Current ratio of approximately 5.08x as of March 31, 2026. The fixed-rate debt structure is advantageous in the current rate environment. Net debt is approximately $259M (using $747.5M debt obligations less $488.1M cash).

**Tax Rate Headwind:** Effective tax rate increased from 15.8% (2023) to 24.8% (2025). This partially offset operating improvements — net margin was 8.5% despite operating margin expansion. The higher tax rate appears structural and is now embedded in forward estimates.

## Growth Analysis

Merit's growth is driven by three vectors:

1. **Volume growth in existing products:** Procedure volume increases in interventional radiology, peripheral vascular, and endoscopy drive same-store sales growth of 3-5% annually.

2. **New product contribution:** SCOUT radar localization, Wrapsody, EsophyX Z+, and the newly acquired OneMark system from View Point Medical expand the addressable market. Management's stated goal of "tripling Merit's oncology market opportunity" through View Point Medical provides a medium-term revenue catalyst.

3. **Acquisition-driven expansion:** Cook Medical assets (2024), EndoGastric Solutions (2024), Biolife (2025), Pentax CryoBalloon (2025), and View Point Medical (2026) add revenue and expand into adjacent therapeutic areas.

**Growth Concerns:** Organic constant-currency growth decelerated to approximately 3-4% in Q1 2026. China VBP continues to compress pricing in Merit's largest international market. FY2026 EPS guidance of $4.01-$4.15 implies 5-8% growth — solid but not exceptional. This guidance is explicitly conditioned on no trade-policy changes implemented subsequent to the guidance date (April 30, 2026), meaning incremental tariff actions represent upside risk to the guided headwind level.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current year EPS estimates have been approximately stable over 90 days. Current quarter estimates have declined modestly — likely reflecting the View Point Medical acquisition dilution ($0.05 impact). FY2027 consensus estimates of approximately $4.40 imply 8% EPS growth. Revisions are neither strongly positive nor negative — they are stable, suggesting that the fundamental trajectory has not deteriorated despite the stock's 33% decline.

## Competitive Landscape

Merit occupies a defensible niche as a broad-line manufacturer of interventional medical devices. Against Medtronic and Boston Scientific, Merit cannot compete on scale in major platform products, but its advantage lies in the "long tail" of thousands of procedure-specific SKUs where larger competitors cannot justify dedicated R&D and manufacturing. This breadth creates switching costs at the hospital level — replacing Merit's ancillary product catalog would require sourcing from dozens of smaller vendors.

The shift toward therapeutic products (higher differentiation, higher margins, more clinical evidence barriers) strengthens the competitive moat over time. Products like SCOUT (radar localization for breast cancer surgery) and Wrapsody (cell-impermeable endoprosthesis for dialysis access) carry genuine clinical differentiation, though the 10-K frames the broader market as highly competitive with both larger competitors possessing greater resources and smaller companies focused on specific product lines.

**Vulnerabilities:** China VBP pricing pressure demonstrates that Merit's products can face commoditization risk in price-controlled markets. The OEM segment (~$205M) competes primarily on cost and is inherently lower-margin.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff Exposure (High Severity, Partially Mitigated):** Merit imports raw materials, components, and finished products from countries including Mexico, Ireland, and China, creating direct tariff exposure. The company's 10-K confirms tariffs have already "increased manufacturing costs" and describes the environment as "dynamic and uncertain." Management guided through Q1 with margin expansion, but tariff policy remains unpredictable. FY2026 guidance explicitly excludes the potential impact of trade policies implemented after the guidance date. Incremental tariff increases beyond what's currently assumed could compress margins by 100-200bps. Merit operates manufacturing facilities across multiple countries (including Texas, Florida, Virginia, Utah, Minnesota, Mexico, Brazil, Ireland, France, the Netherlands, and Singapore), providing some flexibility to shift production.

**CEO Transition (Medium Severity, Diminishing):** The founder's departure created institutional uncertainty, but insider buying by the new management team signals confidence and alignment. Early execution (Q1 beat, strategic acquisitions, portfolio pruning) is encouraging.

**China VBP (Medium Severity, Ongoing):** Volume-based procurement has compressed pricing since 2022 with no clear endpoint. China represents a meaningful portion of international revenue.

**Acquisition Integration (Medium Severity):** Five acquisitions in 18 months creates execution risk. Goodwill and intangible assets represent approximately 37% of total assets as of March 31, 2026 (approximately $1.015B), with this figure likely increasing after the View Point Medical acquisition closed in April 2026 (purchase-price allocation still under evaluation). This makes the balance sheet vulnerable to impairment charges if integration disappoints.

**Convertible Notes:** $747.5M due 2029 could create dilution if the stock price rises above the initial conversion price of approximately $86.83. Capped calls partially offset this risk.

## Conclusion

Merit Medical at $64.17 presents a favorable risk/reward for a medtech compounder experiencing transient multiple compression. The 33% drawdown has been driven by sentiment (tariff fears, CEO transition) rather than fundamental deterioration. Five senior executives reported purchases in early May 2026 at roughly $60-63 per share, including some purchases reported as 401(k) plan holdings — a positive insider signal, though it coincided with a 5,000-share director sale. At 15.8x forward non-GAAP EPS with a 5.6% FCF yield, the downside is limited by cash generation and balance sheet strength, while multiple re-rating toward even modest medtech norms (18-20x) provides 20-30% upside over 12-18 months.

The market appears to be pricing tariff risk as a more severe headwind than what management has demonstrated through Q1 execution and maintained guidance, though the conditional nature of that guidance (excluding future trade-policy changes) means the market's caution is not without basis. The insider purchase cluster supports the view that management believes the stock is undervalued at current levels.
