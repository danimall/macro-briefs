## Executive Summary

Fair Isaac Corporation is a $26B analytics company whose FICO Score serves as the de facto standard for U.S. consumer credit risk assessment, generating 91% operating margins in its Scores segment. On April 22, 2026, FHFA announced acceptance of VantageScore 4.0 (owned by Experian, TransUnion, Equifax) for conforming mortgages, ending FICO's decades-long monopoly in that channel. The stock has fallen 48% from its 52-week high of $2,218 to approximately $1,067.

The fundamental question is whether this 48% decline adequately prices the competitive threat or whether the market has overreacted. FICO's Q2 FY2026 results were strong — $691.7M revenue (+39% YoY), $11.14 EPS (+69% YoY) — yet these results were driven partly by aggressive pre-competitive price increases that may not be sustainable. The new pricing model ($0.99 per pull + $65 success fee) represents a fundamental restructuring of mortgage scoring economics. With 64% of revenue flowing through the three bureaus that own FICO's primary competitor, the structural conflict is severe and unprecedented. At approximately 25x forward FY2026E EPS ($43.21), the stock is reasonably priced for stable-to-modest growth but does not offer sufficient margin of safety given the binary range of outcomes over the next 12-18 months.

## Company Overview

FICO operates two segments: **Scores** (69% of Q2 FY2026 revenue) and **Software** (31%). The Scores business delivers credit scores primarily through Experian, TransUnion, and Equifax to banks, credit card issuers, auto lenders, and mortgage originators. The Software segment sells decision management tools (fraud detection, origination, customer management) to financial institutions, with a cloud platform transition underway.

The company employs approximately 3,758 people — extraordinarily capital-efficient for a business generating $2B+ in annual revenue. Ninety-two percent of FY2025 revenues came from the banking industry, creating high concentration in a single end-market. The U.S. accounts for approximately 85% of total revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** FY2022 $1.38B → FY2023 $1.51B (+10%) → FY2024 $1.72B (+13%) → FY2025 $1.99B (+16%) → H1 FY2026 annualized ~$2.41B (+28%). The acceleration in FY2026 is driven by B2B scores pricing (+72% YoY in Q2) and mortgage origination volumes (+95% in Q4 FY2025).

**Profitability:** Operating margins expanded from 42% (FY2023) to 58% (Q2 FY2026). The Scores segment generates 91% operating margins — essentially zero incremental cost per score delivered. Net margins reached 38% in Q2 FY2026. Free cash flow margin was 38.7% in FY2025 with H1 FY2026 operating cash flow of $397M.

**Balance sheet:** Intentionally leveraged with -$2.1B stockholders' equity (buyback-induced). Total debt: $3.64B including $1.5B at 6.00% (2033), $1.0B at 6.25% (2034), $900M at 4.00% (2028). Annual interest expense of ~$190M is covered approximately 9x by operating income. The 3.5x consolidated leverage covenant limits additional borrowing capacity. Current ratio of 0.83 is below 1.0 but manageable given recurring cash flow characteristics.

**EPS trajectory:** FY2023 $16.93 → FY2024 $20.45 → FY2025 $26.54 → H1 FY2026 annualized ~$35.46. The 30-39% EPS growth rate reflects both operating leverage and 3% annual share count reduction from buybacks.

**Capital allocation:** $1.4B in buybacks during FY2025, $774M in H1 FY2026. A new $1.5B authorization was issued in February 2026. The company issued $1.0B in new debt in March 2026 primarily to fund continued buybacks. This strategy — adding leverage to fund repurchases while facing the first credible competitive threat in decades — is aggressive.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects must be assessed across two distinct time horizons:

**Near-term (FY2026):** Management guided $2.45B in revenue. H1 results ($1.32B) suggest this is achievable. Consensus FY2026 EPS is $43.21 (up 4.9% in 90 days), and FY2027 is $54.21 (up 2.9%). These upward revisions reflect the Q2 beat but are modest in magnitude (+3-5%), suggesting analysts are not extrapolating the recent growth rate.

**Medium-term (FY2027+):** The growth picture becomes significantly more uncertain. Mortgage scoring revenue (37-47% of B2B Scores) faces competition from VantageScore at $0.99/pull vs. FICO's $0.99 + $65 model. If 20-30% of mortgage scoring volume migrates over 2-3 years, that represents $100-150M in annual revenue at risk. The pace of lender adoption is unknown and depends on system integration timelines and lender willingness to switch.

**Software platform ARR:** Growing 49% YoY to $348.8M with 136% DBNRR. This provides a genuine growth engine independent of scoring pressures. However, non-platform ARR is declining 8% YoY, and the software segment (31% of revenue) cannot offset a meaningful scoring decline on its own.

**Non-mortgage scoring:** Auto, credit card, personal loan, and insurance scoring are not directly impacted by the FHFA decision. These verticals have different switching costs and competitive dynamics. This represents approximately 53-63% of Scores revenue and provides a floor.

## Valuation Assessment

At $1,067, FICO trades at:
- ~40x trailing FY2025 EPS ($26.54)
- ~25x forward FY2026E EPS ($43.21)
- ~20x forward FY2027E EPS ($54.21)
- Forward P/E of ~34x per company profile data

Relative to application software peers: WDAY (47x), TYL (44x), OKTA (59x), DT (63x), PTC (20x). The peer median is 47x. FICO's 25x forward trades at a substantial discount to peers, but the comparison is imperfect — FICO's growth rate is higher but its competitive position is deteriorating uniquely.

The critical valuation question: what multiple does FICO deserve if mortgage scoring revenue erodes 20-30% over 3 years? If Scores revenue stabilizes 10-15% below current trajectory, normalized EPS might be $40-45 rather than the $54 consensus for FY2027. At $1,067, that would be 24-27x normalized earnings — reasonable for a high-margin, capital-light business but not cheap given the uncertainty.

The analyst consensus target median is $1,550 (45% upside), with a range of $707-$2,400. The wide dispersion reflects genuine disagreement about the VantageScore impact.

## Competitive Landscape

FICO's position in U.S. conforming mortgage scoring was the single most defensible revenue stream in financial software. FHFA's April 22 decision to accept VantageScore 4.0 converts this from monopoly to duopoly.

Critical dynamics:
1. **Bureau conflict:** Experian, TransUnion, and Equifax account for 64% of FICO's revenue. They own VantageScore. They have a direct financial incentive to shift volume to their own product (where they capture 100% of economics vs. paying FICO royalties). This is the most structurally conflicted supplier-customer relationship in enterprise software.

2. **Pricing pressure:** VantageScore is being marketed at $0.99/pull. FICO's response ($0.99 + $65 success fee) is creative but represents a fundamental repricing. The Community Home Lenders Association cited a 1,567% increase in FICO-related costs — political momentum favors lower pricing.

3. **Gradual transition:** System updates, AUS integration, and lender adoption will phase in over 12-24+ months. This is not an overnight disruption but a slow erosion that makes timing difficult.

4. **Non-mortgage moat intact:** Auto lending, credit cards, and insurance scoring maintain strong market positions with different competitive dynamics. VantageScore's acceptance in mortgages does not automatically translate to other verticals where FICO's penetration is deeply embedded in underwriting models.

5. **Antitrust litigation:** A consolidated class action alleging Sherman Act Section 2 monopolization is proceeding through discovery. The FHFA decision strengthens the "monopolist" narrative even as it paradoxically weakens the monopoly itself.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks (Not Fully Priced):**

1. **Accelerated VantageScore adoption:** If major mortgage lenders adopt VantageScore quickly (6-12 months rather than 24+), scoring revenue could decline 15-25% in FY2027. The bureau incentive alignment makes this plausible.

2. **Bureau relationship deterioration:** The three bureaus could use their leverage to negotiate lower royalty rates on non-mortgage scoring, citing the competitive alternative. They now have credible BATNA across all verticals.

3. **Leverage + revenue uncertainty:** $3.64B in debt was appropriate for a monopoly business with predictable cash flows. It is less appropriate for a business facing its first genuine competitive displacement.

4. **Antitrust liability:** If the Sherman Act Section 2 case results in damages or behavioral remedies, the financial and structural impact could be severe.

**Partially Priced Risks:**

5. **Mortgage pricing reset:** The shift from aggressive per-pull pricing to $0.99 + $65 is already somewhat reflected in the 48% stock decline.

6. **Revenue concentration:** 64% through three customers who own the competitor — the market understands this conflict post-announcement.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($1,400-1,600, 31-50% upside)

- VantageScore adoption proves slow (18-24 months before meaningful volume shifts) due to lender inertia, system integration costs, and risk model re-validation requirements
- FICO's success-fee model ($65 per funded loan) generates comparable or higher revenue per funded mortgage than the old per-pull model
- Non-mortgage scoring (auto, cards, insurance) continues growing 10-15% annually with pricing power intact
- Platform software ARR maintains 40%+ growth, diversifying revenue away from scoring
- At 20x FY2027E EPS, stock re-rates to $1,084; at 25x, to $1,355; at 30x, to $1,626

### Bear Case ($700-850, 20-34% downside)

- Bureau incentives drive rapid VantageScore adoption in mortgages within 12 months
- Competitive threat extends to auto and card verticals as bureaus leverage their distribution
- Mortgage scoring revenue declines 25-35% over 2 years, reducing total company revenue 8-12%
- Normalized EPS reverts to $35-40 as margin pressure emerges
- Antitrust litigation results in damages or behavioral remedies
- Leverage becomes burdensome with interest expense consuming 15%+ of operating income
- Stock de-rates to 18-22x reduced earnings = $630-880

### Base Case ($1,100-1,300, 3-22% upside)

- VantageScore captures 15-20% of mortgage scoring volume over 24 months
- Non-mortgage scoring grows 8-12% annually, partially offsetting mortgage erosion
- Total revenue grows 5-8% in FY2027 (decelerating from 28% pace)
- EPS reaches $48-52 in FY2027 (below current $54 consensus)
- Stock trades at 22-25x base EPS = $1,056-$1,300

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For HOLD:**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock declines below $900 (17-18x FY2027E consensus), creating sufficient margin of safety for the uncertain competitive outcome
2. Two consecutive quarters of data showing VantageScore mortgage adoption below 5% of conforming originations (proving slow transition)
3. FICO demonstrates non-mortgage pricing power with 10%+ growth in non-mortgage B2B scores despite VantageScore availability

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. VantageScore captures >15% of conforming mortgage origination pulls within 6 months of launch
2. Any of the three major bureaus publicly announces intent to preferentially distribute VantageScore or reduces FICO distribution terms
3. Management cuts FY2027 guidance below $2.3B revenue or materially reduces buyback activity (signaling cash flow concerns)
4. Antitrust case proceeds to summary judgment with unfavorable rulings on market definition

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 FY2026 earnings (expected late July/early August 2026) will be the first full quarter reflecting post-FHFA competitive dynamics. Mortgage origination mix data and any VantageScore adoption metrics will be critical.

## Conclusion

FICO is a high-quality business facing a structural competitive challenge for the first time in its history. The 91% margin Scores business was built on monopoly pricing power that is now partially contested. At 25x forward earnings, the stock has already absorbed significant pain from its 52-week high, but the range of outcomes remains too wide for high-conviction directional positioning.

Supporting factors: the non-mortgage scoring franchise, platform software acceleration (49% ARR growth), and creative pricing response ($0.99 + $65 model). Against this: the bureau conflict is structural and worsening, leverage is elevated at the wrong time, the antitrust case creates tail risk, and the 48% decline may prove to be the first leg rather than the entirety of re-pricing.

The correct posture is to observe 2-3 quarters of post-FHFA adoption data before committing capital. If adoption proves slow and non-mortgage pricing power remains intact, the stock at these levels becomes attractive. If adoption accelerates, further downside is likely despite the decline already realized.
