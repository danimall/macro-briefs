## Executive Summary

Century Aluminum is the largest domestic primary aluminum producer in the U.S., operating smelters in Kentucky, South Carolina, and Iceland with approximately 770,000 tonnes of annual capacity. The company is in the midst of a fundamental earnings transformation driven by the tripling of the U.S. Midwest Premium (MWP) from $427/tonne in 2024 to $1,295/tonne in 2025, a direct consequence of Section 232 tariff increases to 50% on aluminum imports. Q2 2026 EBITDA guidance of $315-335M implies an annualized run-rate of approximately $1.3B for a company with $5.7B market cap and enterprise value around $6B.

The stock has appreciated 237% over the past 12 months, from $16 to $55. Despite the compressed forward P/E of ~15x and favorable EV/EBITDA of ~5x on guided run-rate, three factors create material uncertainty: (1) the entire profitability thesis rests on a single policy variable (Section 232 tariffs) that could be modified; (2) every named executive sold significant shares in February-March 2026, with the CEO liquidating 300,000 shares for ~$15.5M near current prices; and (3) Glencore — the 36.4% owner and 54% revenue customer — reduced its stake by 34% in a $327M block sale. These signals from the most informed parties demand caution despite optically cheap valuation metrics.

The company has also missed earnings estimates in all 4 reported quarters, with the most recent miss at -11.2% on Q1 2026 adjusted EPS. While the forward outlook is genuinely strong (Q2 guidance significantly above Q1), the consistent failure to meet estimates introduces execution uncertainty.

## Company Overview

Century Aluminum operates three primary aluminum smelting facilities:

- **Grundartangi, Iceland** — 320,000 tpy capacity; largest and most profitable facility using renewable geothermal/hydro power; currently recovering from transformer failures that idled 2/3 of production in late 2025
- **Sebree, Kentucky** — 220,000 tpy; standard-grade and value-added billet products
- **Mt. Holly, South Carolina** — 230,000 tpy; expanding from 75% to full capacity (first metal April 2026, full production expected June 2026)

The company also holds 55% of Jamalco, a 1.4M tpy alumina refinery in Jamaica, providing partial vertical integration. A carbon anode facility in the Netherlands serves Grundartangi.

The revenue model is direct commodity exposure: realized price = LME aluminum + regional premium + product premiums. Costs are primarily electricity, alumina, and carbon. The company has limited pricing power beyond the structural advantage conferred by tariff-driven premium elevation.

Glencore plc is both the dominant customer (54% of revenue) and largest shareholder (36.4% as of Q1 2026 filings, down from ~18.5M shares to ~12.2M shares after a March 2026 block sale).

### The Valuation Paradox

On surface metrics, CENX appears deeply cheap at ~5.5x forward earnings. However, the critical question is earnings durability. The MWP at $1,295/tonne is a policy-created premium that did not exist 18 months ago. If tariffs revert to 25% (or even 35%), the premium would compress by $400-600/tonne, erasing $150-220M of annual revenue and potentially all incremental EBITDA above 2024 levels.

Analyst consensus targets of $75-86 imply 37-57% upside from current price. These targets appear based on sustained premium levels. The 3-for-3 unanimous Buy rating from covering analysts reflects the earnings power at current tariff levels.

At $55, the market is implying something less than permanent $1,295 MWP — which is rational. The stock at its 52-week high of $68.69 (reached in early April when aluminum premiums were running hot and tariff durability seemed higher) represented perhaps 7x forward earnings on the same estimates.

The recent pullback from $68.69 to $54.92 (20% decline) occurred as aluminum premiums partially consolidated and broader market concerns about tariff sustainability intensified following the IEEPA Supreme Court ruling. This repricing is rational, not a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

### Structural Position: Strong

Century occupies a unique competitive position as the only major domestic primary aluminum producer. The 50% Section 232 tariff creates an effective moat that no foreign producer can bypass without paying the duty — making CENX's domestic production inherently more valuable by the MWP differential. This is a policy-created moat, not an operational one.

The competitive position has genuinely improved:
- Mt. Holly expansion adds low-cost incremental volume
- Jamalco provides partial alumina cost insulation
- Grundartangi's low-carbon profile commands premium in ESG-conscious markets
- Inola (if completed) would solidify decade-long competitive advantage

### Competitive Risks

- Policy reversal is the primary competitive risk — the moat disappears if tariffs are reduced
- Global overcapacity (particularly Chinese) means the equilibrium aluminum price absent tariffs would be materially lower
- Energy cost volatility at Sebree (market-rate power) can quickly erode margins
- The Inola JV's 60/40 split (EGA 60% / Century 40%) gives EGA majority control of this significant growth project

## Risk Assessment

### Binary Policy Risk (Critical)

The entire investment thesis depends on the persistence of 50% Section 232 tariffs. The Supreme Court's February 2026 IEEPA ruling (denying presidential authority to impose tariffs under IEEPA) does not directly affect Section 232, but it demonstrates the legal landscape is evolving. Any political shift — trade deal, executive action, or Congressional legislation — that reduces tariffs would compress the MWP immediately and potentially by hundreds of dollars per tonne.

At $427/tonne MWP (pre-tariff-hike level), Century's EBITDA would revert to approximately $150-200M annually — implying EV/EBITDA of 30-40x on current enterprise value. The stock would likely trade in the $15-25 range.

### Operational Risk

The Grundartangi transformer failures demonstrate the concentration risk in a capital-intensive smelting operation. While insured, the 6+ month production loss at the company's largest facility shows how quickly cash generation can deteriorate.

### Governance Concerns

- Unremediated material weakness in internal controls (Jamalco consolidation)
- Financial statement restatement (March 2026 8-K: "Non-Reliance on Previously Issued Financial Statements")
- 54% revenue concentration with Glencore (also 36.4% owner — creates conflicts of interest)

### Insider Activity (Major Red Flag)

Universal C-suite selling in February-March 2026:
- CEO sold 300,000 shares (~$15.5M)
- CFO, General Counsel, EVP Operations, and all other named executives sold significant positions
- No insider purchases detected
- Glencore sold 6.3M shares ($327M), reducing position by 34%

The unanimity of selling across the entire senior management team and the dominant shareholder is the strongest bearish signal available from the most informed parties. While some of this follows year-end equity award vesting, the scale suggests conviction that current prices adequately reflect (or exceed) fair value.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 80.6% vs. 63.3% historical vol represents a 17.3pp rich premium — the market is pricing elevated uncertainty. The term structure is flat (80.6% near vs. 79.8% far), suggesting uncertainty is viewed as persistent rather than event-driven. The call-heavy skew (-4.4%) and low put/call OI ratio (0.27) indicate the options market is positioned for further upside, though the high absolute IV level reflects commodity-driven tail risk. IV compressed 20pp since May 7 (100.9% → 80.6%) as post-earnings uncertainty resolved, but remains well above historical norms.

Short interest at 11.9% with 4.5 days to cover indicates meaningful institutional bearish positioning, likely reflecting the tariff policy risk thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($75-86, +37-57%)

Tariffs persist at 50% through 2027+. Mt. Holly and Grundartangi reach full production by Q3 2026. Full-year 2026 EBITDA reaches $1.1-1.3B. Inola JV progresses on schedule. At 5-6x EBITDA (discount to broader industrials but appropriate for commodity cyclicality), equity value reaches $75-90 per share. The massive NOL carryforward ($1.5B) shelters taxes for years, boosting after-tax cash generation. Century becomes the dominant domestic supplier in a structurally short U.S. aluminum market.

### Bear Case ($25-35, -36-55%)

Tariffs reduced to 25% (bipartisan trade deal, executive discretion, or Congressional action). MWP collapses from $1,295 to $600-700/tonne. EBITDA reverts to $200-300M. Stock re-rates to $25-35. Additionally: Inola JV fails to reach definitive agreement, Grundartangi restart encounters further delays, and alumina costs spike again (as they did in late 2024 to $805/tonne).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock declines below $42 (representing >7x Q2 guided EBITDA annualized), creating sufficient margin of safety against tariff risk
- Congressional legislation codifying Section 232 tariff levels (converting executive discretion to statutory protection)
- Definitive JV agreement signed for Inola with favorable Century economics

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any announcement of tariff reduction, exemption expansion, or Section 232 modification
- Q2 2026 EBITDA below $280M (significant miss vs. $315-335M guidance)
- Additional insider selling by CEO exceeding 100,000 additional shares
- Glencore reduces stake below 25% (approaching potential strategic exit)

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected August 2026) will validate or invalidate the $315-335M EBITDA guidance and provide updated Q3 outlook. Tariff policy developments should be monitored continuously.

## Conclusion

Century Aluminum presents a genuinely challenging analytical puzzle. The earnings power at current tariff levels is real and extraordinary — $1.1-1.3B annualized EBITDA on a $5.9B enterprise value is objectively cheap by any traditional metric. The company occupies a structurally advantaged position as the dominant domestic producer in a tariff-protected market with massive capacity expansion potential via the Inola project.

However, the entire thesis reduces to a single policy variable (Section 232 tariff rates), every executive with inside information has sold shares at current levels, the company's largest shareholder reduced its position by a third, and the company has missed earnings estimates in all four reported quarters. The stock is trading roughly fairly for a tariff-dependent commodity producer with significant execution and policy risks. The analyst consensus target of $79-80 assumes tariff persistence and operational execution — reasonable base-case assumptions, but assumptions that carry 25-40% downside if violated.

HOLD with uncertain classification.
