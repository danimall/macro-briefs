## Executive Summary

Essential Properties Realty Trust is a net-lease REIT focused on single-tenant properties leased to middle-market operators in service-oriented businesses. The company has delivered consistent ~25% annual revenue growth and double-digit AFFO/share growth since its 2018 IPO, driven by a disciplined sale-leaseback origination model targeting an underserved middle-market niche. With STORE Capital and Spirit Realty removed from the public market via acquisitions, EPRT has become the primary remaining pure-play publicly traded vehicle for this strategy, creating potential platform scarcity value.

The stock currently trades at $31.18, below the analyst consensus target of $37.28. At approximately 15.4x forward AFFO ($2.00-$2.05 guidance midpoint), the valuation represents a discount to the net-lease peer group while offering superior growth. The primary risk is a $430M term loan maturing in 2027 currently swapped at 2.26% — refinancing at 5%+ adds approximately $11-14M in annual interest expense, which the AFFO payout buffer can absorb but which compresses growth. The underlying portfolio metrics (99.7% occupancy, 3.6x rent coverage, 14.4-year WALT) remain strong.

## Company Overview

EPRT was founded in 2016 and IPO'd in 2018 as an internally managed net-lease REIT targeting middle-market tenants — companies with 10-250 locations and $20M-$1B in revenue. As of Q1 2026, the portfolio comprises 2,417 properties across 48 states generating $584.2M in annualized base rent. Key portfolio attributes include 97.3% triple-net lease structures, 66.8% master leases, and 97.9% of leases with contractual rent escalators averaging 1.8% annually.

The company's competitive differentiation centers on its origination model: 95% of investments are structured as sale-leasebacks using EPRT's standardized lease template, which includes mandatory tenant financial reporting (99.2% compliance rate). Target tenants operate service-oriented businesses resistant to e-commerce displacement — car washes, dental offices, childcare centers, quick-service restaurants, convenience stores, and fitness facilities.

Management operates with 56 employees, making this one of the most capital-efficient platforms in the REIT sector. Average property investment of $3.1M limits single-asset concentration risk, and no single tenant exceeds 3.4% of rent (top 10 = 16.5%).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Earnings Growth:**

Revenue has grown from $287M (2022) to $561M (2025), a 24-25% annual pace driven by $1.0-1.3B in annual acquisitions. AFFO per share grew from $1.53 (2022) to $1.89 (2025) — a 7.3% CAGR despite 45% share dilution over the same period. The growth rate reflects accretive external acquisitions funded at positive investment spreads above blended cost of capital.

Q1 2026 results showed $158.8M revenue (beat), FFO per share of $0.54, and AFFO per share of $0.50. GAAP net income was $0.28/share. Full-year 2026 AFFO guidance of $2.00-$2.05 per share implies 6-8% growth, decelerating from prior years but still above typical REIT growth rates.

**Balance Sheet Quality:**

As of Q1 2026, total unsecured debt stands at $2.63B (including $100M drawn on the revolving credit facility), translating to approximately 4.4x net debt/EBITDAre, well within the company's 5.5x internal ceiling and appropriate for a BBB-rated net-lease REIT. Substantially all term debt is fixed-rate (after hedging), with modest floating-rate exposure from the revolver draw. Total liquidity of approximately $1.5B provides substantial runway for continued acquisitions without distressed capital market access.

The weighted average interest rate of 4.23% across the debt stack is favorable, though newer issuances (5.25% on 2029 term loan, 5.40% on 2035 notes) demonstrate the directional trajectory of funding costs. The critical maturity event is the $430M 2027 term loan currently benefiting from a swap at 2.26%.

**Capital Efficiency:**

Cash G&A as a percentage of revenue declined from 8.5% (2023) to 5.1% (2025), demonstrating operating leverage in the platform. Impairments of $12.0M (2025) represent just 0.18% of total assets — minimal credit losses. The AFFO payout ratio of approximately 64% provides a roughly 36% buffer above the dividend, among the healthiest coverage ratios in the net-lease space.

## Growth Analysis

EPRT's growth engine has three components:

1. **External acquisitions** at 7.7-8.1% cash cap rates. The company deployed $1.3B in 2025 and has pipeline visibility supported by 82% repeat/relationship-sourced deals. The investment spread above blended cost of capital drives per-share accretion, though this spread is sensitive to movements in both acquisition cap rates and the company's cost of equity and debt capital.

2. **Contractual rent escalators** averaging 1.8% annually across 97.9% of leases, providing organic NOI growth without additional capital deployment.

3. **Portfolio optimization** through dispositions at 6.5-7.3% cap rates (100-150bp tighter than acquisition yields), effectively recycling capital into higher-return opportunities.

Quality of new acquisitions is improving: rent coverage on recent deals reached 4.7-5.9x versus the portfolio average of 3.5x (as of Q1 2026), and lease terms extended to 19+ years versus the portfolio's 14.6-year WALT.

Estimate revisions show mixed signals: current quarter estimates rose 3.0% over 30 days, but full-year estimates drifted down 2.2% over 90 days. The Q1 2026 GAAP EPS result of $0.28/share is notable, though AFFO — the more relevant REIT metric — showed year-over-year growth.

- 4.0% dividend yield

Note: Recent market data suggests the stock may have moved higher since the $31.18 reference price was captured, which would reduce the forward return potential.

For REITs, AFFO-based multiples are more informative than P/E ratios given GAAP depreciation distortions. EPRT's forward AFFO multiple of ~15.4x compares to a range of 14-20x among net-lease and single-tenant REIT peers, suggesting the stock is modestly undervalued relative to its growth profile. Direct comparisons to industrial REITs (Terreno, STAG) are imperfect given different property types and risk profiles.

The specific reason the market may be mispricing EPRT: the 2027 refinancing overhang and broader rate uncertainty have compressed the multiple despite portfolio fundamentals that do not yet support a growth deceleration thesis. If 2027 refinancing proceeds orderly, the stock could re-rate toward $34-36 as the overhang lifts.

## Competitive Landscape

Following STORE Capital's take-private (2023) and Spirit Realty's merger with Realty Income (2024), EPRT has become the primary remaining publicly traded pure-play net-lease REIT focused specifically on middle-market tenants. Strategic implications:

- Institutional investors seeking middle-market net-lease exposure have limited public alternatives, though EPRT itself acknowledges it competes with traded and non-traded public REITs, private equity investors, institutional investment funds, and other capital sources — some with greater resources and lower cost of capital
- The company's relationship network (82% repeat business) creates a sourcing advantage difficult to replicate
- The middle-market focus creates structurally wider cap rates (7.7-8.1%) versus investment-grade-focused peers (typically 5.5-6.5%)

EPRT's mandatory financial reporting requirement (99.2% compliance) provides early warning of tenant distress that traditional net-lease REITs targeting rated tenants don't require.

Scale advantages are emerging: 56 employees managing $6.6B+ in assets with declining Cash G&A ratios suggest the platform can grow substantially before requiring proportional headcount additions.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risk: 2027 Refinancing ($430M Term Loan)**

The current 2.26% rate (post-swap) will likely refinance at approximately 5.0-5.5% in today's market, adding $11.8-13.9M in annual interest expense. On a $374.6M AFFO base, this compresses growth by approximately 3-4% — meaningful but manageable given the ~36% AFFO-above-dividend buffer.

The new CFO (Salisbury), who joined in 2023 and led capital markets and investor relations before being appointed CFO effective January 1, 2026, appears well-positioned to execute this proactively — potentially through a forward-start agreement or early refinancing.

**Tenant Credit Risk in Recession**

The portfolio targets middle-market, unrated operators. In a consumer-spending contraction, tenants in restaurants (casual dining, QSR), entertainment, and fitness face revenue pressure. The 3.5-3.6x average rent coverage provides substantial cushion — a tenant would need to lose over 70% of operating income before rent becomes unaffordable — but tail-risk defaults are higher than investment-grade portfolios.

**Equity Dilution**

Shares outstanding grew 45% over three years (135M to 196M). If the stock trades below intrinsic value for an extended period, the external growth model becomes dilutive rather than accretive. The February 2026 follow-on offering of 12.5 million shares at $32.20 on a forward basis was executed near fair value; issuing at significantly lower levels would be dilutive to existing holders.

**Investment Spread Sensitivity**

EPRT's growth model depends on maintaining a positive spread between acquisition cap rates and blended cost of capital. The company itself warns that if its cost of capital rises without corresponding increases in acquisition returns, this positive spread could be reduced or eliminated. Recent senior unsecured notes at 5.25-5.40% and equity issuance near a ~4% dividend yield suggest the blended cost of capital may be higher than in prior years, potentially narrowing the accretive spread from historical levels.

**Short Interest**

Reported short interest of approximately 10.9% of float with an estimated 7.2 days to cover suggests meaningful bearish institutional positioning, above-average for REITs. This likely reflects (1) the duration sensitivity of net-lease REITs in a rising-rate environment, (2) the GAAP EPS result, and/or (3) broader CRE distress fears bleeding into the sector. Elevated days-to-cover also creates potential for short squeeze on positive catalysts.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($36-40):**
- 2027 refinancing executed cleanly at favorable spread
- Acquisition pace maintains at $1.0-1.3B annually at 7.5%+ cap rates
- Consumer spending stabilizes; occupancy stays above 99%
- Multiple re-rates on platform differentiation recognition
- Path to $36-40 at 18-20x AFFO on $2.05-2.10 AFFO/share

**Bear Case ($25-27):**
- 30Y rates sustain above 5.5%, compressing cap rates and widening funding costs
- Consumer recession materializes; tenant defaults spike from 0.3% to 2-3%
- Equity market access restricted; growth model stalls
- Multiple compresses to 12-13x AFFO = $24-27/share
- Represents 14-20% downside from current price

The asymmetry favors the bull case: 17-28% upside versus 14-20% downside, with probability weighted toward positive resolution given the portfolio's current health metrics and management's proactive capital market positioning.

**Upside/downside from current ($31.18):** +13.9% to base, +21.9% to bull, -15.0% to bear. Risk/reward ratio approximately 1.5:1 on probability-weighted basis.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Occupancy declines below 97% (currently 99.7%) — would signal tenant credit deterioration overwhelming the portfolio
2. AFFO payout ratio rises above 85% (currently ~64%) — would indicate inability to absorb higher refinancing costs while maintaining dividend growth
3. Investment cap rates compress below 7.0% without offsetting cost-of-capital reduction — would eliminate the accretive investment spread that drives per-share growth

## Conclusion

EPRT presents a compelling risk/reward at $31.18 for investors with a 12-month horizon. The company's platform differentiation, consistent growth execution (7.3% AFFO/share CAGR despite 45% dilution), and portfolio health metrics support valuation re-rating toward $35-36 as 2027 refinancing uncertainty resolves.

The 4.0% dividend yield provides income support during the hold, and the ~36% AFFO payout buffer insulates against dividend risk even in a moderately adverse rate scenario. The primary risk warranting position sizing discipline (2% allocation, not conviction-level) is the macro backdrop: sustained higher rates, consumer deterioration, and the specific $430M 2027 maturity. These are quantifiable and manageable within the current financial structure, but they impose a ceiling on near-term multiple expansion. Rating: BUY with $35.50 base case target.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The investment-spread accretion supporting the BUY thesis may be narrower than presented, as blended cost of capital (given recent debt at 5.25-5.40% and equity near 4% yield) could approach acquisition cap rates of 7.7%; additionally, the reference price of $31.18 may be stale versus more recent trading levels near $33-34, which would materially reduce the forward return and narrow the risk/reward ratio.
