## Executive Summary

Warby Parker reported a Q2 2026 that looked like an inflection — revenue $235.5M (+9.8%), gross margin 57.9% versus 53.0%, a swing from a $1.8M net loss to $4.6M of net income, and EPS of $0.16 against a $0.12 consensus (+37%). The market did not accept it. The stock opened at $25.07 on August 6 after closing at $29.27 the prior session, a ~14% gap down on 6.0M shares, and consensus current-quarter EPS was cut 37% (from $0.15 to $0.09) within thirty days. The reason is disclosed in the filing itself: the U.S. Supreme Court struck down certain IEEPA tariffs in February 2026, Warby Parker determined $14.4M of refunds probable, and credited $11.8M directly to Q2/1H cost of goods sold. Management attributes 500 basis points of the 490bp gross-margin expansion to that refund. Backing it out, Q2 gross margin was 52.9% (versus 53.0% a year earlier), income from operations was **negative** $8.6M, pre-tax result was **negative** $6.5M (worse than the –$2.5M in Q2 2025), and adjusted EBITDA margin was 9.0% versus 11.7% the prior year. A material portion of the $0.16 EPS is attributable to the refund, meaning the "beat" was probably a miss on comparable economics.

Underneath the accounting, the operating trend is a slow deceleration alongside a mix shift the equity market has not yet repriced. Revenue growth has stepped down from 15.2% (FY24) to 13.0% (FY25) to 9.1% (1H26). Active customers grew only 4.1% in 1H26 against 7.0% in FY25 and 7.8% in FY24. E-commerce revenue — the channel that defined the brand — fell 0.3% in Q2 and 2.3% in 1H, so 100% of consolidated dollar growth came from physical retail (+13.6%) and the clinical exam business attached to it (+32.2%). The company retired Home Try-On, its signature digital acquisition tool, at the end of 2025. What remains is a specialty optical chain with an in-store clinic, opening stores at an accelerating pace (352 units, 15 net adds in Q2, new operating-lease originations of $31.7M in 1H26 versus $12.7M in 1H25) into a customer base growing at 4%.

The balance sheet is genuinely strong — $292.7M cash, no drawn debt, a dormant covenant, 2.27x current ratio — and the Google AI-glasses collaboration is a real, unpriced option. Neither offsets the valuation. At $26.38 the shares are ~64x current-year consensus EPS of $0.41 and ~45x next-year $0.59, both of which have been revised down over the past month, against an apparel peer median P/E of 14.8x and a 1.3% free-cash-flow yield on FY2025's best-ever $43.7M of FCF. Probability-weighted return from here is modestly negative. **REDUCE.**

## Company Overview

Warby Parker is a vertically integrated eyewear and vision-care company, structured as a Delaware public benefit corporation and reported as a single operating segment ("holistic vision care") with the two Co-CEOs jointly serving as chief operating decision maker and consolidated net income as the segment measure.

Revenue by line (Q2 2026 vs Q2 2025, $000s): eyewear $191,760 (+8.4%), contacts $26,598 (+7.9%), eye care $17,153 (+32.2%). Eyewear is 81.4% of revenue; the 10-K still states plainly that "we derive most of our revenue from the sale of one product, our glasses." Channel: retail $176,786 (+13.6%) versus e-commerce $58,725 (–0.3%); retail is now 75.1% of revenue versus 72.5% a year ago.

The clinical build-out is the most distinctive asset. 315 of 352 stores (89.5%) offered in-person eye exams at June 30, 2026, up from 285 of 323 at year-end 2025 and 194 of 237 at year-end 2023. Exams generate appointment-driven traffic and capture prescriptions, which competitors without clinics cannot replicate. They also carry employed-doctor labor cost and are cited by management as a gross-margin drag.

Scale: 237 stores (FY23) → 276 (FY24) → 323 (FY25) → 352 (Q2 26). TTM active customers 2,332K → 2,514K → 2,689K → 2,709K. TTM revenue per customer $287 → $307 → $324 → $336.

## Financial Analysis

### The tariff adjustment is the decisive number

Mechanics per the Q2 10-Q, Note 2: IEEPA tariffs were struck down in February 2026; Customs opened a refund platform in April; Warby Parker deemed $14.4M probable, recording $11.8M as a COGS benefit in Q2/1H and $2.6M as an inventory reduction that flows through COGS as inventory turns in 2H 2026. $3.4M was collected by June 30, $11.2M sat in prepaid/other current assets, and the balance was collected after quarter-end.

| Q2 2026 metric | As reported | Ex-$11.8M refund | Q2 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 57.9% | **52.9%** | 53.0% |
| Income from operations | $3.2M | **–$8.6M** | n/a (loss) |
| Pre-tax income | $5.3M | **–$6.5M** | –$2.5M |
| Adjusted EBITDA margin | 14.0% | **9.0%** | 11.7% |

On 1H26: gross margin 56.0% reported, 53.5% ex-refund, versus 54.7% in 1H25; pre-tax income $9.2M reported becomes –$2.6M ex-refund, versus +$2.4M in 1H25.

The fair caveat is that the refund reverses real tariff costs previously expensed, so the correct counterfactual is a world with no IEEPA tariffs at all, which the filings do not quantify. Durable margin plausibly sits between 52.9% and 57.9%. But the direction of the estimation error is one-sided: reported 2026 margins overstate the run rate, and the sell side appears to be catching up to that — current-year EPS cut 12.8% and next-year cut 10.7% in thirty days is exactly what a refund unwind looks like in consensus.

### Operating leverage is thinner than the headline suggests

SG&A as a percentage of revenue: 65.3% (FY23) → 59.2% (FY24) → 54.6% (FY25) → 56.6% in Q2 2026 (+150bp year over year on higher retail compensation and technology cost). Over FY23–FY25 the 610bp improvement is almost fully explained by stock-based compensation falling from $71.1M to $36.1M as the 2021 Founders Grant rolled off — SBC alone accounts for 648bp of revenue-weighted relief (10.62% → 4.14% of revenue). Core non-SBC SG&A did not improve as a share of revenue across that span. Measuring FY24 to FY25 alone shows ~460bp of SG&A improvement against ~127bp from SBC, so there was genuine leverage in the most recent full year. In Q2 2026, SBC rose again to $10.3M from $9.2M and SG&A deleveraged.

### Profitability and cash conversion

FY2025 was the first GAAP-profitable year: net income $1.641M on $871.9M of revenue, a 0.19% net margin, and operating income was still negative at –$5.3M. The company crossed into pre-tax profit only because of $8.4M of interest income on its cash balance.

Adjusted EBITDA reconciles from $1.6M of FY2025 net income by adding back $50.3M of D&A and $36.1M of SBC among smaller items. Both are economically real here: D&A reflects a store build consuming $67.0M of annual capex, and SBC settles in shares that dilute. Adjusted EBITDA is a poor proxy for owner earnings in this model.

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | 1H2025 | 1H2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | $61.0M | $98.7M | $110.8M | $69.6M | $54.1M |
| Capex | $54.7M | $66.0M | $67.0M | $32.4M | $39.0M |
| FCF | $6.3M | $32.7M | $43.7M | $37.1M | $15.1M |

1H26 operating cash flow fell 22.2% even as net income rose from $1.7M to $7.8M. The swing is working capital: –$9.5M in 1H26 versus +$17.2M in 1H25, a $26.7M reversal driven mainly by a $20.9M increase in prepaid and other assets — $11.2M of which is the tariff receivable itself. That portion should reverse into 2H OCF given post-quarter collection. The remainder includes capitalized cloud-software implementation costs, which grew from $20.3M to $25.6M gross in six months and are a recurring investment, not a timing item. I am not annualizing 1H cash flow: Q4 carries the year's highest cost load, a slice of late-December FSA/HSA demand is recognized in January, and deferred revenue fell $11.3M in 1H. As a labelled sensitivity only, 2× 1H26 FCF is $30.2M.

### Balance sheet

Cash $292.7M, no debt drawn, $120M revolver undrawn (expandable to $175M) with only $4.3M of letters of credit; the 3:1 senior net leverage covenant applies only above $30M of borrowings and is dormant. Current ratio 2.27x. Total liabilities $380.3M against $392.0M of equity, but $251.0M of those liabilities are operating leases — the leverage is landlord obligations, not financial debt. Undiscounted future lease payments total $302.3M over a 6.1-year weighted average term at a 5.8% discount rate, plus $10.5M of executed-but-not-possessed leases. Accumulated deficit $677.8M; $299.9M of NOLs with no §382 study performed, so their usable value is indeterminate.

The relevant financial risk is not solvency. It is the ratio of a $302.3M fixed rent commitment, growing quickly (1H26 new lease originations $31.7M versus $12.7M), against a business whose best-ever free cash flow was $43.7M.

## Growth Analysis

| Period | Revenue | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| FY2023 | $669.8M | +12.0% |
| FY2024 | $771.3M | +15.2% |
| FY2025 | $871.9M | +13.0% |
| 1H 2026 | $478.0M | +9.1% |
| Q2 2026 | $235.5M | +9.8% |

The composition matters more than the rate. FY2025's 13.0% decomposed into +7.0% active customers and +5.7% revenue per customer. 1H26's 9.1% decomposed into +4.1% customers and +6.3% revenue per customer ($336 from $316). Growth is migrating from acquisition to monetization of the installed base — accretive near-term through premium lens penetration and exam attach, but a finite lever.

Meanwhile store count grew ~9% year over year while customers grew 4.1%, which is consistent with new stores capturing existing-customer demand. Management names this risk in its own filing: opening in established markets "may result in inadvertent oversaturation… divert customers and sales from our existing retail stores and e-commerce channels," and approximately 65% of stores have been opened in the last five years, so the base of unproven, immature units is large. A benign alternative reading exists — active customers is a trailing-twelve-month measure that lags a step-up in openings — and the disclosed data cannot separate the two.

The e-commerce line is the most concerning datapoint. Down 0.3% in Q2 and 2.3% in 1H, with the Home Try-On program (five frames mailed free) sunset at the end of 2025 after a $2.456M inventory write-down. Whether that reflects marketing reallocation to store markets or genuine digital erosion is not determinable from the filings, but the channel has now lost its principal conversion tool, which raises the bar on any thesis assuming digital re-acceleration.

Eye care at +32.2% is the fastest-growing line and the least discretionary, which supports management's "medical necessity" framing. It is also the line that carries employed-doctor labor and is cited as a gross-margin drag. Growth is mixing toward lower-margin, more heavily regulated revenue.

**Estimate revisions are the cleanest forward read and they are negative.** Current quarter $0.15 → $0.09 (–37%), current year $0.47 → $0.41 (–12.8%), next year $0.66 → $0.59 (–10.7%), all over thirty days. This is not the estimate-revision pattern that precedes re-rating; it is the opposite.

The Google collaboration is the one genuine upside vector. Structure, verbatim: Google has committed up to $75M for Warby Parker's product development and commercialization costs, and up to $75M of equity investment **at Warby Parker's option** subject to unspecified collaboration milestones. Warby Parker develops and commercializes the eyewear; Google pays and may invest. The development tranche is a cost reimbursement recorded as a reduction of SG&A, fully offset in period — $3.3M in FY2025, $4.4M in Q2 2026, $6.4M in 1H 2026, $9.7M cumulative reimbursable costs incurred. Against $872M of annual revenue, this is R&D subsidy, not a profit stream. If the equity option were fully drawn at $26.38, ~2.84M new shares would represent +2.3% of existing shares and ~2.25% post-issuance ownership — small, and it is capital in. Samsung is named as a partner in the risk factors with no disclosed structure, economics or timeline and should not be modeled. Target shop-in-shops are mentioned only in a risk factor with no disclosed count or economics.

## Competitive Landscape

Named competitors in the 10-K: EssilorLuxottica and VSP Vision Care, plus independent ophthalmologists, optometrists and opticians. The concessions in the filing are unusually explicit — competitors have greater financial resources, longer histories, broader geography, can "engage in extensive and prolonged price promotions," and "we may be at a substantial disadvantage to larger competitors with greater economies of scale."

Two structural asymmetries deserve weight. First, "one of our lens suppliers is owned by a vertically integrated company with retail divisions that compete with us," and that supplier "may not be willing, or may become unwilling, to sell its products or services to us on acceptable terms, or at all." Second, Warby Parker has relationships with "only a few" vision-care insurers while competitors own managed-care assets. A vertically integrated rival can therefore press on input terms and on network access simultaneously. Separately, "around half of the cellulose acetate used to produce many of our frames is provided by a single supplier" — the declining top-five COGS concentration (14% in FY25 versus 18% in FY24 and FY23) is a real improvement in aggregate purchasing concentration but does not address that specific dependency, which the same filing restates.

What is improving: exam penetration at 89.5% of stores creates a recurring-traffic and prescription-capture asset that pure-retail competitors lack, and revenue per customer is rising 6%+ annually on premium lens architecture through an owned lab. What is deteriorating: the digital channel that constituted the original competitive differentiation, and customer acquisition velocity. Product concentration is unchanged to worse at ~81% eyewear; a change in channel mix and a change in revenue-line mix are different things and neither establishes diversification.

The filings also flag two long-dated substitution vectors — refractive surgery and future pharmacological vision correction that "could… make our existing products less attractive or even obsolete." These are slow-moving and largely priced in the sector.

**Competitive position score: 5.0** — stable overall, with the clinical build improving and the digital channel deteriorating; no evidence of net share gains given 4.1% customer growth.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs, bidirectional and unresolved.** Frame components come from China, Italy, Vietnam and Japan, and substantially all components ship to labs in the U.S. and China. FY2025 gross margin was hit by tariffs; 2026 got a $14.4M refund. The 10-K states that after the IEEPA ruling, "the U.S. presidential administration subsequently invoked additional tariffs under other laws resulting in a rapidly changing tariff environment," and that even for struck-down tariffs "it remains unclear whether and to whom those tariffs may be refunded." The sign of the next surprise is unknown. What is knowable is that $11.8M of one-time benefit is in the current run rate and only $2.6M remains to flow through 2H COGS.

**Margin normalization, the near-term catalyst.** Q3 and Q4 prints will show gross margin without the bulk of the refund. Consensus has already cut, but a print at ~53% gross margin against a stock at 45x forward is a repricing risk.

**Store cannibalization against a rising fixed base.** 65% of stores opened in the last five years; 15 net adds in Q2 alone; new lease originations 2.5x year over year; $302.3M of undiscounted rent commitments. If comparable-store productivity in mature markets erodes as the network densifies, deleverage runs through both gross margin (occupancy, doctor headcount) and SG&A simultaneously — which is precisely what Q2 showed ex-refund.

**Healthcare regulatory surface.** The "Apparel, Accessories & Luxury Goods" label understates this. Exposure spans the Anti-Kickback Statute, Stark, False Claims Act, HIPAA/HITECH, state corporate-practice-of-medicine and fee-splitting rules, optician licensure, and the FDA (glasses, contacts and acuity charts are regulated devices). The company has never obtained FDA marketing authorization for any product, relying on 510(k) exemption including for the Virtual Vision Test app, and concedes it "cannot provide any assurance that governmental authorities will not assert that we are engaged in the corporate practice of medicine or optometry." A 2018 credential-stuffing breach produced an HHS OCR investigation that closed in February 2025 after a civil monetary penalty was paid in December 2024 (amount not disclosed in the sections reviewed). QMSR took effect February 2, 2026. The 10-K also discloses previously identified material weaknesses in IT general controls and financial-reporting controls; disclosure controls were certified effective at June 30, 2026 with no ICFR changes in Q2, but the remediation date is not established.

**Governance.** Class B carries 10 votes; 15.7M Class B shares represent 59.3% of votes outstanding. Layered on top: staggered board, removal only for cause, no special meetings or written consent, board-amendable bylaws, blank-check preferred, supermajority charter amendments. The multi-class structure makes the company ineligible for certain major indices. As a public benefit corporation, directors must balance stockholder pecuniary interests against stakeholder interests, and holders of the lesser of 2% or $2M may bring derivative suits over that balance.

**Verification gaps that a reader should not assume away:** FY2026 revenue and adjusted-EBITDA guidance were not retrievable; the specific Google collaboration milestones, equity issuance mechanism, exclusivity, term and IP allocation are undisclosed; Samsung and Target economics are undisclosed; §382 status of the $299.9M NOLs is undetermined; the CFO transition documented in 8-Ks dated 2026-02-09 and 2025-08-07 was not read.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 72.1% is exactly in line with 1-year realized volatility of 72.1% — the market is pricing no unusual near-term dislocation despite the term structure being in mild backwardation (72.1% at 3 days versus 68.1% at 129 days). Positioning is call-tilted: put/call volume ratio 0.49, open-interest ratio 0.49, and a –13.8% near-term skew read as call-heavy. That is a genuine divergence with this recommendation and should be stated as such. The most plausible reconciliation is that 20.8% short interest with 4.9 days to cover creates persistent call demand from short-covering and squeeze speculation, and that the Google AI-glasses story attracts upside-lottery buyers. Six-month implied move of 32.9% frames the option market's own view of dispersion — wide enough to accommodate both my bear ($17.50) and bull ($35) cases.

The practical implication is that this is a REDUCE, not a short. High short interest plus call-tilted positioning plus a binary, unquantified product catalyst is a poor risk/reward for a bearish position, however stretched the multiple.

**Risk factors score: 4.0** — the margin-normalization catalyst is dated and near, the tariff regime is genuinely unresolved, and the healthcare-regulatory and single-supplier exposures are tail risks a sector screen would miss.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($35, +33%)

The tariff refund is noise around a real story: a specialty optical retailer with 89.5% exam penetration is converting into a recurring vision-care provider with a durable traffic engine, revenue per customer compounding 6%+ annually, and 352 stores against a U.S. addressable base that supports many hundreds more. The balance sheet ($292.7M cash, no debt, dormant covenant) funds the build without dilution. The Google collaboration — where Warby Parker owns the eyewear design, prescription-lens capability and retail distribution for a major platform's AI glasses — is the largest asymmetry in the equity and is completely unpriced by any disclosure; a successful launch would establish an entirely new revenue line and reframe the multiple. Google is already reimbursing development cost ($9.7M cumulative) and holds a company-optional $75M equity commitment representing only ~2.3% dilution. A $100M buyback authorization sits unused with the shares below the December high. If growth stabilizes at low double digits with 55%+ gross margin, FY2028 adjusted EBITDA near $175M supports $35+.

### Bear Case ($17.50, –34%)

Strip the $11.8M refund and Q2 2026 was a $6.5M pre-tax loss on 52.9% gross margin, worse than the year-ago quarter on both counts. The FY23–25 "operating leverage" was mostly the 2021 Founders Grant rolling off, not unit-economic improvement — core non-SBC SG&A did not improve as a share of revenue over that span, and SG&A deleveraged 150bp in Q2. The digitally native brand's e-commerce channel is shrinking and has just lost Home Try-On, its acquisition mechanism. Active customers grew 4.1% while store count grew ~9%, which is the arithmetic of cannibalization, and new lease originations tripled year over year, adding fixed cost against a $302.3M rent commitment and $43.7M of peak FCF. Consensus is already cutting (–12.8% current year, –10.7% next year), and at 45x forward the multiple has no cushion. Compression to 1.8x EV/revenue is not a stress case; it is where the stock traded nine months ago.

### Weighing them

The bull case rests substantially on an option whose terms — milestones, exclusivity, volumes, revenue share, IP allocation, launch timing — are entirely undisclosed, while the company's own partnership risk factor warns that terms with partners of that scale "may be less favorable to us" and that "we may not achieve such milestones." The bear case rests on arithmetic already in the filings. At 45x forward earnings with falling estimates, the market is paying for the option and receiving the arithmetic.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $24.00 (–9% from $26.38), bull $35.00 (+33%), bear $17.50 (–34%). Probability-weighted (45/25/30) expected return approximately –6% over twelve months.

**De-risking plan.** Trim ahead of the Q3 2026 print (expected early November 2026), which is the first quarter reported substantially without the tariff benefit and the cleanest read on underlying gross margin. Holders who want to retain the Google option should reduce to a residual position sized as a speculative call rather than a core holding. Do not initiate here. Do not short: 20.8% short interest with 4.9 days to cover and call-tilted options positioning make the mechanics unfavorable relative to the 2:1 threshold a short requires.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better.** (1) A Q3 or Q4 2026 print showing gross margin at or above 55% with the tariff contribution explicitly quantified as immaterial, confirming durable margin above the 52.9% ex-refund bound. (2) E-commerce revenue returning to positive year-over-year growth for two consecutive quarters, or active-customer growth reaccelerating above 6%. (3) Meaningful execution of the $100M buyback — say $30M+ deployed in a quarter — which would both signal management's view of value and reduce the share count. (4) A disclosed, quantified Google commercial agreement with unit economics, timing or revenue share. (5) Absent any of the above, a price near $19–20 would restore a positive probability-weighted return and warrant reassessment.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.** (1) Gross margin printing below 52% on a clean basis, implying the ex-refund trend is deteriorating rather than flat. (2) Revenue growth decelerating below 7% year over year while store count continues to grow high single digits — direct confirmation of cannibalization. (3) New tariffs imposed under alternative statutes at a magnitude comparable to the IEEPA regime, without offsetting price action. (4) Any reopening of the previously disclosed material weaknesses, or a new regulatory action touching the exam or telehealth business. (5) Continued insider selling at the current pace alongside continued non-execution of the buyback into a lower share price.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q3 2026 earnings, expected early November 2026. Interim checkpoints: any 8-K disclosing Google collaboration terms or a product launch date; monthly monitoring of consensus revisions for the current year (currently $0.41) and next year (currently $0.59).

## Conclusion

Warby Parker is a well-financed, well-branded specialty optical retailer with a genuinely differentiated clinical asset and a real, unquantified strategic option in AI eyewear. It is also a business whose reported 2026 profitability inflection was created by a $14.4M tariff refund, whose underlying gross margin is flat to slightly down year over year, whose revenue growth has decelerated for three consecutive reporting periods, whose e-commerce channel is shrinking after its acquisition tool was retired, and whose consensus earnings estimates have been cut 11–13% in the past month. The market's reaction to the August 6 print — a ~14% gap down on a nominal 37% EPS beat — was the correct read of the accounting.
