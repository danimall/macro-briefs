**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Nextpower Inc. (formerly Nextracker, rebranded November 2025) is the world's largest manufacturer of solar trackers, with ten consecutive years of GW-shipped market leadership and over 150 GW deployed across 45+ countries. The financial profile is genuinely strong: FY26 revenue of $3.56B (+20%), net income of $586M, ~$953M cash against essentially no funded debt, an inaugural Fitch BBB- rating, and a $500M buyback. The company is executing a deliberate pivot from a pure-play tracker supplier toward an end-to-end power-infrastructure platform, capped by the May 2026 agreement to acquire Prevalon Energy for up to $365M, which brings battery storage and AI-data-center power capability and lifted FY27 revenue guidance above $4B.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is specific and dated. US revenue is now roughly 75% of the total and growing fast, and that US demand sits directly under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (enacted July 4, 2025), which compressed the federal solar-credit timeline: customer projects must begin construction by July 4, 2026 to claim a four-year safe harbor, or be placed in service by December 31, 2027. IRS Notice 2025-42 eliminated the 5% cost safe harbor effective September 2, 2025 and tightened "begin construction" rules. These credits are what let NXT's EPC and developer customers finance projects, so their removal on an accelerated schedule pulls demand forward into FY26–FY27 and threatens an air-pocket in US installations in 2028 and beyond. This is not priced as a tail risk; it is a known policy schedule.

That demand-cliff risk is reinforced by three current data points that argue against paying up today: consensus EPS estimates are falling (current-quarter -7.1% over 90 days, current-year -2.4%), the 45X-credit-versus-tariff margin scissors is closing (GAAP gross margin -380bps YoY), and every named officer sold stock in May–June 2026 near 52-week highs with zero open-market buying. The fundamentals are good enough to own; they are not yet asymmetric enough at $126 to add aggressively into a falsifiable policy event that resolves over the next 18 months.

## Company Overview

Nextpower manufactures the steel-and-software systems that tilt utility-scale solar arrays to track the sun, improving energy yield. The direct customer is typically the EPC, with solar developers and project owners behind them. Founded in 2013 by CEO Dan Shugar, the company IPO'd in February 2023 as a spin-out from Flex Ltd., with TPG Rise Climate as the other major sponsor. That lineage explains several unusual balance-sheet items, notably a ~$391.9M Tax Receivable Agreement liability (85% of realized tax benefits owed to Flex/TPG) and a Tax Matters Agreement.

The business operates as a single reportable segment. Roughly 88% of revenue is trackers; the remainder is TrueCapture software, eBOS electrical balance-of-system, foundations, and robotics/monitoring. Top-5 customer concentration rose to 43% of revenue in Q3 FY26 from 30% a year earlier, though no single customer is consistently above 10–13% and the mix rotates. The geographic story is the key structural fact: US revenue grew 63% YoY in Q3 FY26, while Rest-of-World shrinks as a share.

The November 2025 rebrand to Nextpower marks a strategic identity change executed through bolt-on M&A: Bentek (eBOS), OnSight (autonomous inspection robotics), Origami Solar (roll-formed steel module frames), and Fracsun (soiling monitoring) in FY26, on top of Ojjo and SPI (foundations) prior. The Prevalon acquisition takes this further into battery energy storage and data-center power. The intent is to raise content per project and reduce dependence on the commoditizing tracker, while attaching the company to the data-center power-demand theme.

## Financial Analysis

The operating profile is high-growth and high-return with an asset-light, outsourced-manufacturing model. The nine months to December 31, 2025 produced revenue of $2.68B (+32% YoY) and net income of $435M (+21%). Q3 FY26 revenue was $909M (+34%), driven by a 26% increase in GW delivered, concentrated in the US.

Margins are compressing even as the top line grows, which is the central operating dynamic. GAAP gross margin fell from 35.5% in Q3 FY25 to 31.7% in Q3 FY26; adjusted gross margin moved 36.0% → 32.4%; adjusted EBITDA margin 27.4% → 23.5%. The cause sits inside cost of sales as a "scissors" between two large, opposing line items:

- **Section 45X credits** recognized as a reduction to cost of sales rose to $289.0M (9-mo FY26) from $150.2M a year earlier — roughly a third of gross profit. Beginning CY2025, NXT directly captures the credit on US-made torque tubes ($0.87/kg) and structural fasteners ($2.28/kg). This is the single biggest swing factor in profitability. The credit phases down 25% per year starting 2030 and ends after 2032, and is now exposed to OBBBA's Foreign Entity of Concern restrictions.
- **Tariffs** in COGS rose from $12.4M to $86.9M (9-mo, roughly 7x), driven by 50% Section 232 steel tariffs (with tracker components pulled in via a tariff-inclusion process) plus reciprocal/Section 301 tariffs.

The 45X credit currently more than offsets the tariff drag, but the spread is narrowing, which is what the gross-margin erosion reflects. The entire near-term margin trajectory hinges on (a) 45X surviving FEOC scrutiny and (b) NXT staying ahead of tariff inflation.

The balance sheet is a genuine differentiator. Cash was $952.6M at December 31, 2025 with essentially no funded debt after the Term Loan was repaid in Q4 FY25. A new $1.0B unsecured revolver (September 2025) provides $891M of availability for total liquidity near $1.8B. Fitch assigned an inaugural BBB- (Stable) rating in January 2026. Nine-month operating cash flow was $391M, modestly below the prior-year $418M because of working-capital build funding growth. Capital allocation prioritizes organic growth and M&A plus a $500M three-year buyback authorized January 27, 2026; no dividend.

The earnings-surprise record is consistently positive — four straight beats (8.6%, 17.2%, 16.9%, 13.2% over the last four quarters) — which lends some confidence to delivery against guidance. The tension is that this clean beat record co-exists with falling forward estimates, which I read as the policy/margin overhang capping how aggressively the Street will mark up out-year numbers.

## Growth Analysis

The reported growth is strong and the platform pivot adds real optionality. FY27 guidance was lifted to ≥$4B with the Prevalon announcement, implying continued double-digit revenue growth, and Prevalon opens BESS and data-center power markets that are independent of solar-credit policy. The bolt-on acquisitions raise dollar content per project. International diversification is building through the Nextpower Arabia JV (49% NXT / 51% Abunayyan) for MENA utility-scale trackers and the NX Gemini 2P launch for international markets.

The signal that prevents me from scoring growth higher is the divergence between strong reported revenue and falling forward EPS estimates. Current-quarter consensus fell -7.1% over 90 days (to $1.04 from $1.12), current-year fell -2.4% (to $4.68), and FY27 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint (~$4.40) sits slightly below FY26 (~$4.50) despite the revenue raise — management is explicitly signaling margin and 45X sensitivity. This is broadly negative earnings momentum against rising revenue, the opposite of the +30%-over-90-days revision profile that would justify treating the stock as a re-rating candidate. The revenue growth is partly a pull-forward artifact: customers are racing to "begin construction" before the July 4, 2026 OBBBA deadline, which inflates near-term backlog and risks a US air-pocket in 2028+. Backlog quality and post-2027 US visibility matter more here than the headline growth rate.

The durable growth thesis rests on whether the platform pivot (Prevalon BESS/data-center, international JVs, content expansion) can replace US solar-credit-driven demand fast enough to smooth the 2028 cliff. That is plausible but unproven, and it carries integration risk against $485M of goodwill (+31% YTD).

## Valuation Assessment

At $125.62, NXT trades at ~32.7x trailing P/E and ~27x forward on the $4.68 current-year consensus. Against the Electrical Components & Equipment peer set, the multiple is well below the group: peer-median P/E is 55.9 (WWD 46.5, RRX 49.1, NVT 55.9, POWL 56.7, VICR 99.7). On a pure multiple basis NXT looks cheap relative to peers.

I am deliberately not treating that peer discount as a strong buy signal, for two reasons. First, a low relative multiple here reflects an identifiable structural risk — the post-2027 US demand cliff and the closing 45X/tariff scissors — rather than simple mispricing. The market is discounting genuine policy-driven earnings uncertainty that the peer comps do not share to the same degree. Second, the inflection-valuation framework that would let me anchor to forward/normalized earnings does not apply: estimates are falling, not rising +30%, and FY27 EPS guidance is flat-to-down on FY26. This is the value-trap profile — cheap multiple, no independent positive catalyst to re-rate it — rather than a trough-earnings inflection.

The analyst price-target range is wide ($64 low to $182 high, $150.2 mean, $150 median), and that dispersion maps almost exactly onto the binary policy outcome. The $64 low captures a US demand collapse; the $182 high captures the platform pivot succeeding and 45X surviving intact. With the stock at $126 and forward earnings momentum negative, I read the risk/reward as roughly balanced rather than asymmetric to the upside. A modest discount to the peer group is warranted, not a re-rate trade.

## Competitive Landscape

NXT's competitive position is improving in trajectory terms, which supports a 7. It holds ten consecutive years of tracker share leadership in a fragmented market against Array Technologies, GameChange Solar, and regional low-cost entrants. The moats — scale economies in steel procurement, a domestic-content/45X-compliant US supply chain, TrueCapture software, and deep EPC/developer relationships — are real and reinforced by the move up the value chain. The platform strategy (Bentek, Origami, OnSight, Fracsun, Prevalon) is a credible attempt to increase content per project and diversify away from the commoditizing tracker, and the BESS/data-center entry attaches the company to a structural demand vector.

The offset is that the tracker market itself is price-competitive, and falling solar-panel prices can make trackers less attractive relative to fixed-tilt racking. Steel commodity exposure is unhedged. The durability of the competitive improvement is partly contingent on the same policy backdrop that creates the demand risk: domestic-content advantages are valuable only as long as the credits that reward them survive.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risks are policy and trade-related, and several are specific and dated, which is why this dimension scores low.

1. **OBBBA accelerated credit phaseout (the central risk).** Projects must begin construction by July 4, 2026 or be placed in service by December 31, 2027 to access the credits NXT's customers rely on. The September 2, 2025 elimination of the 5% cost safe harbor (IRS Notice 2025-42) already tightened the rules. With US ~75% of revenue, this is a genuine demand-cliff for 2028+, only partly mitigated by pull-forward into FY26–FY27.
2. **FEOC restrictions** could disqualify NXT or supply-chain partners from 45X/48E/45Y eligibility if Chinese ownership/control ties exist; a significant portion of NXT's steel is China-derived. Disqualification would raise COGS or erode the domestic-content bonus advantage.
3. **Tariff escalation and AD/CVD.** Steel faces 50% Section 232 tariffs; China-origin steel up to ~170% combined. Separately, NXT's improperly certified off-grid module imports created a potential AD/CVD liability management estimated as high as ~$120M plus interest — substantially reduced by a December 2025 retroactive Commerce exclusion, with residual appeal risk pending the Federal Circuit Solar Duty Waiver case.
4. **Securities litigation.** A December 2024 N.D. Cal. class action plus January/March 2025 derivative suits allege misleading statements; motions to dismiss are pending. The Flex Delaware Chancery suit over a $48.5M tax distribution was dismissed in NXT's favor (January 21, 2026), a positive resolution.
5. **TRA acceleration.** A change of control could trigger an accelerated lump-sum TRA payment, deterring acquisition and posing a liquidity drain.
6. **Integration risk** against $485M goodwill (+31% YTD) as the company makes 4+ acquisitions plus Prevalon in roughly twelve months.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 113.5% against 1-year HV of 65.4% (rich by 48 points, 87th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in steep backwardation (113.5% near-term versus 75.7% at six months) — the market is pricing an imminent event, with a ±4.8% implied move on the nearest expiry. Put/call skew of 18.2% on the front-week and a P/C OI ratio that jumped from 0.61 to 1.42 since mid-May indicate elevated hedging demand. IV collapsed 55 points since the prior snapshot (168.5% → 113.5%) as the May earnings event resolved, but the residual front-end stress and put-heavy positioning corroborate the policy-event caution. The single unusual-activity print ($130 July call, 3.1x vol/OI) is small and not a clean directional signal. The options market is not confirming a clean bullish setup; it leans toward near-term downside protection.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$182, +45%):** The platform pivot works. Prevalon closes on schedule (Q3 CY2026) and BESS/data-center power becomes a credit-independent growth engine, smoothing the post-2027 US solar air-pocket. 45X survives FEOC scrutiny, the tariff scissors stabilizes, and margins re-base near the new normal rather than continuing to erode. International JVs (Arabia, NX Gemini 2P) diversify revenue. The stock re-rates toward the peer multiple on demonstrated growth durability, and the four-beat track record continues. At ≥$4B FY27 revenue with stabilizing margins, the $182 high target is achievable.

**Bear case (~$64–80, -36% to -49%):** The pull-forward unwinds. US installations hit an air-pocket in 2028 as the July 2026 construction deadline passes, and the platform acquisitions are too small and too early to fill the gap. FEOC disqualifies key supply-chain partners, the 45X benefit shrinks while tariffs keep climbing, and gross margin continues falling below 30%. The securities class action survives the motion to dismiss. Forward EPS keeps getting revised down, and the stock de-rates toward the $64 analyst low.

**Base case (~$135, +7%):** FY27 revenue lands near $4B as guided, margins stabilize modestly below current levels, the platform pivot shows early traction without yet replacing the US solar engine, and the policy cliff remains a 2028 question the market won't resolve in either direction over the next 12 months. The stock holds a moderate discount to peers, with the wide target dispersion persisting until the OBBBA deadline and post-2027 visibility clarify.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD: the fundamentals support owning the stock, but the forward setup is not asymmetric enough to add at $126 ahead of a binary, dated policy event. Reassessment timeline: the July 4, 2026 OBBBA construction deadline, the Q3 CY2026 Prevalon close, and the next two earnings prints (which will reveal post-deadline US backlog quality) are the natural checkpoints over the next 6–12 months.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Forward EPS estimates turn positive (current-year and current-quarter revisions inflect upward for two consecutive months) alongside evidence that 45X has cleared FEOC review intact.
- Management provides credible post-2027 US backlog visibility or Prevalon/BESS revenue ramps faster than guided, demonstrating the platform can offset the solar-credit cliff.
- Gross margin stabilizes (stops the 380bps-type YoY erosion) as the 45X/tariff scissors finds a floor.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- US bookings visibly roll over post-July 2026, or FY27/FY28 revenue guidance is cut on a US demand air-pocket.
- A FEOC determination disqualifies NXT or a major supply-chain partner from 45X eligibility, or the securities class action survives the motion to dismiss with material exposure.
- Gross margin falls below ~30% on a sustained basis as tariffs outrun the credit.

## Conclusion

Nextpower is a high-quality business: global tracker leader, net-cash and investment-grade, four straight earnings beats, and an intelligent pivot toward credit-independent power infrastructure. Those attributes earn a HOLD rather than an AVOID, and the score sits just below the BUY threshold rather than far from it. What keeps it from a BUY is concrete and falsifiable: a US business that is ~75% of revenue facing a legislated demand-cliff schedule (begin construction by July 4, 2026; placed in service by December 31, 2027), a 45X-versus-tariff margin scissors that has already cut gross margin 380bps and is still closing, falling forward EPS estimates (-7.1% current quarter over 90 days), and unanimous C-suite selling near the highs with no open-market buying. The wide analyst target range ($64–$182) reflects the market's view that the outcome is binary and unresolved. At $126, with negative earnings momentum and the options market pricing near-term stress, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. I would own NXT for the platform optionality and the balance sheet, and would upgrade to BUY on the first hard evidence that post-2027 US demand visibility or the Prevalon/BESS ramp can offset the policy cliff.
