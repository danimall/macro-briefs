## Executive Summary

McCormick is no longer valued as a branded spice and flavor business. On March 31, 2026 it agreed to combine with Unilever's Foods business in a Reverse Morris Trust under which McCormick issues stock equal to 65.0% of the combined company's fully diluted equity and pays Unilever **$15.7 billion in cash**. Existing McCormick holders retain **35.0%** of the combined entity. Closing is expected by mid-2027, net leverage is guided to **4.0x or less at close**, returning to 3.0x within two years. Every judgment about this equity now runs through that transaction, its financing, and whether $600 million of run-rate net cost synergies land on schedule.

The standalone business heading into that transaction is soft. FY2025 net sales rose 1.7% to $6,840.3 million against management's own 4-6% constant-currency objective; adjusted EPS grew 1.7% to $3.00. Q2 FY2026 reported net sales +16.7% and adjusted operating income +30.1%, but decomposing management's own disclosure leaves **organic growth of +1.7%, entirely price**, with McCormick de Mexico consolidation contributing 12.3pp and FX 2.7pp. In Consumer Americas — part of the Consumer segment that is 58% of sales and 67% of segment operating income at the corporate level — organic sales were **−0.2%**, with volume/mix **−3.6pp** against +3.4pp of price, and management named price elasticity as the cause. Adjusted results also absorbed a **$27.6 million IEEPA tariff credit** that was not excluded from adjusted figures and whose underlying $30.8 million refund claim is subject to a pending government appeal. Management's guidance reconciles the gap honestly: adjusted operating income +16-20% converts to adjusted EPS of only **$3.05-$3.13, or +2% to +5%**, because the adjusted tax rate rises 2.5pp to 24.0%, a 25% noncontrolling interest is deducted, equity income moves inside operating income, and interest expense steps up.

At $54.445 the shares trade at 17.6x FY2026 consensus of $3.09 and 16.5x FY2027 consensus of $3.30, with a 3.53% dividend yield backed by a 40-year increase streak and 60% payout of adjusted EPS. That is neither cheap nor stretched for a 1-3% organic grower, and it sits within a few percent of a base case derived either from standalone earnings power or from capitalizing the deal's synergy target. The trailing P/E of 8.8x reported on screens is not a valuation signal: it reflects an **$866.8 million pre-tax and after-tax non-cash gain** on remeasuring the previously held 50% McCormick de Mexico stake, which added $3.22 to first-half GAAP diluted EPS of $4.33. Ex-gain, trailing GAAP EPS is roughly $2.79, or about 19.5x. This is the sixth consecutive HOLD on this name, and the rating sits closer to REDUCE than to ACCUMULATE.

## Company Overview

McCormick manufactures and distributes herbs, spices, seasoning mixes, condiments and flavor products, with FY2025 net sales of $6,840.3 million, approximately 39% generated outside the U.S., presence in roughly 150 countries and about 14,100 employees. Two segments: **Consumer** (58% of sales, 67% of segment operating income; McCormick, French's, Frank's RedHot, Lawry's, Cholula, OLD BAY, Zatarain's, Ducros, Schwartz) and **Flavor Solutions** (42% of sales, 33% of operating income; custom seasoning blends, coating systems and compound flavors, including FONA and Giotti).

Two corporate actions define the current situation. First, on January 2, 2026 McCormick paid **$750.0 million** for an additional 25% of McCormick de Mexico, taking its stake to 75% and consolidating a business that added $403.7 million of first-half net sales. Purchase accounting assigned $1,600.0 million of intangibles (including a $1,470.0 million indefinite-lived reacquired licensing right), $939.9 million of non-deductible goodwill, and $504.3 million of noncontrolling interest, and produced the $866.8 million remeasurement gain. Second, the Unilever Foods RMT. Post-close, reportable segments become four commercial divisions with 2025 pro forma sales of Americas Consumer $8B, International Consumer $7B, Global Food Service $4B and Global Flavor $2.5B, with a secondary London listing and an international headquarters in the Netherlands. Brendan Foley remains Chairman/President/CEO and Marcos Gabriel remains CFO.

A note on the divisional figures: they sum to $21.5 billion against the "approximately $20 billion" combined revenue cited in March. Both are rounded and both rest on Unilever management estimates from **preliminary carve-out IFRS data**; the March disclosures state that audited or reviewed standalone financial statements for Unilever Foods are not yet available and actual figures may differ materially. All Unilever Foods financials in this report should be read as unaudited management estimates.

## Financial Analysis

**Standalone trajectory.** Net sales: $6,350.5M (FY22) → $6,662.2M (FY23) → $6,723.7M (FY24) → $6,840.3M (FY25). Gross margin peaked at 38.5% in FY2024 and fell 60bp to 37.9% in FY2025 on commodity and tariff cost. GAAP operating income was $1,070.8M in FY2025, adjusted $1,094.0M at a 16.0% margin. Segment divergence in FY2025 is the more informative detail: Consumer sales rose 2.6% to $3,950.3M while **Consumer segment operating income fell 0.7% to $734.9M** (margin −60bp to 18.6%), whereas Flavor Solutions grew sales only 0.5% but lifted operating income 9.0% with margin +90bp to 12.4%. The higher-margin, brand-led half lost margin. SG&A leverage of 70bp in FY2025 came substantially from lower performance-based compensation, a low-quality source.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $962.2M against capex of $221.8M gives free cash flow of $740.4M, versus FY2023 OCF of $1,237.3M. The cash conversion cycle lengthened to 42 days from 36 in FY2024. Two disclosed arrangements flatter reported working capital: **$430.0 million of U.S. receivables sold** under a non-recourse program (cost recognized as a reduction of net sales — $8.9M in H1 FY2026, not as interest) and **$332.1 million of supplier-finance balances** inside trade payables. Neither is improper; both matter when comparing working capital to unadjusted peers.

**Balance sheet.** At May 31, 2026: cash $331.2M; short-term borrowings $1,326.6M; current LTD $9.5M; long-term debt $3,597.4M. Gross debt **$4,933.5M**, net debt approximately **$4,602M**. Current ratio 0.78. Goodwill of $6,291.9M plus intangibles of $4,937.5M equal **68% of total assets**. Two disclosures qualify the period-end optics: $449.4M of non-U.S. cash was temporarily applied to U.S. short-term debt at quarter-end, and average first-half short-term borrowings were $1,519.4M against the $1,326.6M period-end balance, with average total debt of $5,111.1M.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.78 computed from FY2025 statements should not be read as a bankruptcy signal. Negative working capital is by design (McCormick finances working capital with commercial paper and revolver draws), and asset turnover and EBIT/assets are structurally suppressed by $11.2 billion of acquisition-derived goodwill and intangibles. The company has $2.5 billion of committed revolvers, was covenant-compliant at May 31, 2026, and issued $500 million of 4.15% notes in February 2026. What the score legitimately conveys is that there is no unencumbered balance-sheet capacity to absorb a $15.7 billion cash obligation without a large new debt raise — which is precisely what the bridge and term facilities exist to do. The February refinancing, replacing 0.90% notes with 4.15% notes, is a clean illustration of the interest step-up embedded in every future maturity.

**Capital returns.** FY2025 dividends of $483.0M ($1.80/share) and buybacks of only $34.8M. The quarterly dividend was raised 6.7% to $0.48 in November 2025 — a 40th consecutive annual increase — giving an annualized $1.92 and a **3.53% yield**. FY2025 payout was 60.0% of adjusted EPS and 65.2% of free cash flow. **$402.6 million remains of a $600 million authorization dating to November 2019**; H1 FY2026 repurchases were $10.9M. Buybacks should be treated as suspended through deleveraging.

**Earnings quality in Q2 FY2026.** Reproducing the adjustment stack: adjusted operating income $336.4M versus $258.6M, an increase of $77.8M. Remove the $27.6M tariff credit (recorded in COGS, not excluded from adjusted results) and roughly $7.2M of FX benefit (management cites 27.3% constant-currency versus 30.1% reported on a $258.6M base). McCormick de Mexico's operating contribution is not disclosed; applying a 20-29% pre-tax margin band to its $204.8M of quarterly sales — derived from FY2025 equity income of roughly $67M on McCormick's 50% share, implying ~$134M of full-entity net income on roughly $800M of revenue — gives $41-59M. The residual is **$242-262M against $258.6M a year earlier, or underlying growth of roughly −6% to +1%**. The upper end is more likely if purchase-accounting amortization on the $130M customer-relationship intangible (~$8.7M annually) and intercompany eliminations compress that margin band. Management's own +2% to +5% adjusted EPS guide is consistent with the low end of this range, and it is the number to anchor on.

**Consensus mechanics.** Q3 consensus was cut 9.7% over 30 days to $0.76 while next-quarter estimate slipped 2.9% to $0.88 and the full year held at $3.09. H1 adjusted EPS was $1.46, so $1.63 in H2 lands inside the $3.05-3.13 guide. The current-quarter cut is largely a timing reallocation rather than a full-year reduction, which moderates the "estimates falling" read — but a 9.7% quarterly cut still signals softer near-term delivery, and FY2027 consensus has slipped 1.4% over 90 days. The surprise record (3 beats, 1 miss; Q2 +14.4%) supports confidence in the guided range being met, with the caveat that the Q2 beat itself contained the tariff credit.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is the weak point and it is deteriorating in the right-hand tail of profitability. FY2025 organic growth was +1.9% (volume/mix +1.2pp, price +0.7pp). Q2 FY2026 organic growth was +1.7% and **entirely price**, with total volume/mix −0.5pp and Consumer Americas volume/mix −3.6pp against +3.4pp of price. Management attributed the volume decline to price elasticity. FY2026 guided organic growth is +1% to +3%. Against the company's stated long-term objectives of 4-6% constant-currency sales growth, 7-9% adjusted operating income growth and 9-11% adjusted EPS growth, the business has been delivering roughly one-third to one-half of the sales target for three years.

The growth case therefore rests almost entirely on the transaction. Combined FY2025 revenue is guided at approximately $20 billion with adjusted EBITDA of $4.7 billion and an approximate 21% operating margin, targeting a **23-25% year-3 margin including run-rate synergies and 3-5% year-3 revenue growth**. That growth target requires acceleration from both sides: Unilever Foods' underlying sales CAGR was approximately 2.8% over the prior two years with Knorr and Hellmann's at ~70% of sales, and McCormick's own organic run rate is 1-3%. Combining two 2-3% growers does not produce a 3-5% grower without commercial synergies that management has not yet quantified.

The quantified value creation is cost, not growth: **$600 million of run-rate annual net cost synergies by year 3** (roughly two-thirds by end of year 2) at a one-time cost of approximately $300 million, plus roughly $100 million of incremental cost and revenue synergies earmarked for reinvestment. On combined FY2025 EBITDA of $4.7 billion, $600 million is a 12.8% uplift. Capitalized at 12x, that is $7.2 billion of enterprise value; net of the $300 million capture cost and multiplied by the 35.0% retained ownership, existing holders' share is roughly **$2.4 billion, or about $9.00 per existing share** on 268.8 million shares. That figure is the core of the bull case and the reason the deal is defensible on paper. It is also entirely prospective, and McCormick has committed only to providing further synergy and transition-services detail "by the end of the third quarter" — the fiscal quarter ending August 31, 2026.

## Valuation Assessment

**Reconciling the inputs.** The supplied market capitalization of $13.84 billion corresponds to the 254.0 million non-voting shares alone. Including 14.8 million voting shares, full equity value at $54.445 is approximately **$14.63 billion**. Adding net debt of $4.60 billion and noncontrolling interests of $0.58 billion gives an enterprise value near **$19.8 billion**, or roughly 13.2x the ~$1.5 billion of FY2025 EBITDA (operating income plus D&A, including 100% of McCormick de Mexico) that management used to mark McCormick's side of the deal at $21.0 billion and 13.8x. The shares therefore trade about 6% below the enterprise value assigned to McCormick in the merger, and about 5.9% below the $57.84 one-month VWAP used for the March consideration calculation.

**Earnings multiples.** On adjusted figures — the only comparable basis, since FY2026 GAAP EPS is distorted by the remeasurement gain — the stock is **17.6x FY2026 consensus of $3.09** (mid-point of guidance) and **16.5x FY2027 consensus of $3.30**. The reported 8.79x trailing P/E on screens is computed against a trailing GAAP EPS base that includes the $3.22 per-share non-cash gain; excluding that gain, the trailing multiple is materially higher (on the ~$2.79 ex-gain figure noted above, roughly 19.5x). Peer medians in the supplied set are close to unusable — Smucker at −92.4x and Conagra at −3.8x reflect impairment-driven losses, while Hormel at 29.5x and Tyson at 35.9x reflect their own depressed earnings bases. The peer **yield** median of 3.8% is more informative and sits slightly above McCormick's 3.53%, indicating no yield discount on offer.

**Deal-based valuation.** Applying the combined framework: FY2025 combined adjusted EBITDA $4.7 billion, growing to roughly $5.4 billion by year 3 with full synergies and low-single-digit organic growth. At 12x EV/EBITDA — reasonable for a 3-5% grower carrying 3.0x leverage — enterprise value is $64.8 billion; net of roughly $16.5 billion of net debt at that point, equity is $48.3 billion, of which the 35.0% retained stake is $16.9 billion, or **$62.8 per existing share undiscounted**, roughly $50 discounted at 9% over 2.5 years. At 13x the same math gives about $70 undiscounted, roughly $56 discounted. The band brackets the current price. That symmetry is the honest conclusion: at $54.445 the market is paying approximately fair value for a successful integration at a normal staples multiple, with no discount for the execution risk taken on.

**Base case: $58.** Derived as 17.5x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $3.30, consistent with the mid-point of the discounted deal math above and with the analyst mean target of $60.62 (median $60.00, range $52.00-$75.00). That is +6.5% from the current price; with the 3.53% yield, a roughly 10% twelve-month total return in the base case. Probability-weighting the scenarios below (55% base, 20% bull, 25% bear) gives an expected price of about $56.8, or approximately +8% total return — approximately fair, not compelling.

I am deliberately not scoring the valuation highly on the basis of the low headline multiple. The apparent cheapness is an accounting artifact, adjusted multiples are mid-teens on a 1-3% organic grower, and the yield sits below the peer median. Yield support and the synergy option justify a middling score, not a favorable one.

## Competitive Landscape

McCormick describes itself as the global brand leader in spices and seasonings and one of the brand leaders in condiments and sauces. The distinction matters: leadership is unambiguous in the first category and shared in the second. Supporting evidence in the filings is genuine — Flavor Solutions relationships described as active for decades, indefinite-lived brand intangibles of $3,048.8 million at FY2025 year-end (French's/Frank's RedHot/Cattlemen's at $2,320.0 million, Cholula at $380.0 million) with disclosed fair-value cushions above 20% on every brand except three with aggregate carrying value of only $45.0 million, and a low-cost-per-serving position that is relatively insulated from GLP-1-driven calorie reduction because flavor spend per meal is largely inelastic to portion size. McCormick is also a leading private-label supplier, which internalizes part of the store-brand threat.

The trajectory is where the concern sits. Consumer Americas volume/mix of −3.6pp with price +3.4pp indicates the consumer is trading down or buying less at each successive price increase — the same mid-market weakness visible across discretionary and staples categories as mortgage costs and food inflation compress household budgets. In Flavor Solutions, the 10-K concedes that competitors include larger global flavor houses that bundle flavor with texturants, enzymes and nutrition; McCormick has explicitly committed to "remaining a flavor focused business," which is a deliberate decision not to contest that adjacency. Customer concentration has not improved: Walmart approximately 12% of consolidated sales in each of 2023, 2024 and 2025; PepsiCo approximately 12% in 2025 and 13% in 2024 and 2023; **top three Flavor Solutions customers at 49% of segment sales in all three years, identical**. Revenue mix is changing through the Mexico consolidation and the four-division structure, but changing mix does not establish reduced concentration, and the concentration disclosure itself is flat.

The transaction adds real scale — a combined foodservice platform of approximately $6 billion of FY2025 sales, and Knorr and Hellmann's as anchor brands — which strengthens negotiating position against consolidated grocery buyers. Whether that scale converts into volume growth rather than merely cost extraction is unproven.

## Risk Assessment

**Deleveraging arithmetic.** At 4.0x on $4.7 billion of combined EBITDA, closing net debt is approximately $18.8 billion. Reaching 3.0x within two years on roughly $5.4-5.5 billion of EBITDA implies net debt near $16.3-16.5 billion, or $2.3-2.5 billion of paydown over two years ($1.15-1.25 billion annually). Incremental cash interest on $15.7 billion at 5.0-5.75% is $785-900 million, on top of McCormick's existing ~$196 million; combined free cash flow after that interest, cash taxes and higher combined capex is plausibly $1.8-2.3 billion against dividends near $1.3 billion and $300 million of one-time synergy costs. That leaves roughly $0.5-1.0 billion per year of discretionary paydown against a $1.15-1.25 billion implied requirement. Every input here is an estimate and Unilever Foods' standalone cash conversion is undisclosed, so this is a sensitivity rather than a forecast. It does suggest the 3.0x target leans on EBITDA growth and on the **asset sales explicitly contemplated in the bridge commitment letter**, where commitments are reduced by "the proceeds of certain asset sales and certain other events."

**Dividend growth compression.** The 40-year streak and the stated intent to maintain a ~60% payout make an outright cut unlikely. Sustaining 6-7% annual increases at 4.0x leverage while funding $300 million of synergy costs is a different proposition. The most probable outcome is token increases through the deleveraging window.

**Unresolved deal terms.** The Q2 10-Q states that the stockholders' agreement, transition services agreement, tax matters agreement and certain intellectual property and asset purchase agreements "have not been finalized and remain subject to negotiation" and that final terms "may differ materially." Works council consultation must complete pre-closing. Unilever may make a "U.S. Asset Sale Election" that would make the U.S. portion taxable. McCormick's obligation to close is **not conditioned on obtaining financing**; a severe credit dislocation before mid-2027 would be absorbed as cost. The termination fee payable by McCormick is $420 million, approximately 2.9% of current equity value. A charter amendment and shareholder approval of the issuance are required, and the dual-class structure (14.8 million voting shares carrying all votes, roughly 5.5% of total shares) survives the transaction.

**Tariff credit reversal.** The $30.8 million IEEPA refund claim rests on a CIT order that the government has appealed; a successful appeal "could delay, reduce or deny the funds." The $27.6 million already credited to Q2 COGS is roughly 35% of the year-over-year adjusted operating income increase.

**Impairment exposure.** $11,229.4 million of goodwill and intangibles, including the $1,470.0 million Mexican reacquired right and $939.9 million of Mexican goodwill whose valuations remain preliminary. The disclosed >20% cushion applies to the legacy brand portfolio, not the new Mexican intangibles.

**Operating dependencies.** McCormick de Mexico pays Grupo Herdez — its 25% minority partner — for strategic management and exclusive distribution **including customer invoicing**, $34.1 million in Q2 and $66.8 million in H1, with $115.7 million of receivables due from Herdez at May 31, 2026. This is a structural dependency on a minority holder rather than a governance flag, but it is material and would not surface in standardized data.

**Cost and policy inputs.** The Q2 10-Q added a risk factor covering the February 28, 2026 strikes on Iran and Iranian retaliation; McCormick has no material regional operations but cites conflict-driven freight inflation in every margin discussion in the quarter and expects it to offset the tariff benefit. Separately, the FY2025 10-K flags the September 2025 MAHA Commission report and the FDA's call to phase out petroleum-based synthetic dyes — reformulation and label-change cost for a seasonings and condiments portfolio.

**Concurrent systems change.** A multi-year global ERP replacement is in progress and McCormick de Mexico implemented a new ERP in Q2 FY2026, disclosed as a change in internal control over financial reporting, while the company simultaneously prepares the largest integration in its history.

Offsets: no material pending legal proceedings, investment-grade ratings with committed bridge and term facilities, $2.5 billion of revolvers, and covenant compliance.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 31.9% against 30.1% one-year realized is fair (1.8pp spread) and sits in the 12th percentile of the current chain — the options market is not pricing stress into the next month. Term structure is in contango (31.9% at one week rising to 36.8% at six months), which places the priced uncertainty in December and beyond rather than immediately, consistent with a mid-2027 closing timeline and with synergy detail due around the August 31 quarter-end. Put/call open interest of 0.45 and volume ratio of 0.24 are call-tilted; the 5.0% near-term put skew flagged as elevated put demand inverts to −1.2% at one month and −4.4% at six months. ATM IV has fallen 1.7pp since July 22 while the price rose 4.7%, which is a complacent rather than a defensive configuration. Short interest is negligible. The takeaway: no options-market corroboration of a near-term downside catalyst, and no cheap volatility to exploit — the positioning is consistent with a HOLD, and the six-month vol premium is where deal risk is being expressed.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($72, +32%).** Synergy detail delivered around the August 31 quarter-end exceeds $600 million or arrives faster than the stated two-thirds-by-year-two schedule. The shareholder vote and works council consultation clear without incident, permanent financing prices inside the assumed 5.0-5.75%, and one or two non-core divestitures accelerate deleveraging past the 3.0x target. Consumer Americas volume stabilizes as price increases anniversary. Capitalizing $600 million of synergies at 12-13x and applying the 35.0% retained share adds roughly $9-10 per existing share of value; a 13x combined multiple on $5.4-5.5 billion of year-3 EBITDA supports $70+ undiscounted. This case matches the $75 high analyst target.

**Bear case ($42, −23%).** Consumer Americas volume erosion continues as elasticity bites at each price point, keeping organic growth at the low end of +1%. The tariff appeal succeeds and the $27.6 million credit reverses. Synergy detail disappoints, or the transition services scope proves broader and longer than the "up to 24 months" framing. Financing prices wider, leverage sticks near 4.0x, the dividend increase streak flattens to a token raise, and the combined multiple compresses to 10-11x on a business growing 2%. Any shortfall against acquisition-model cash flows triggers impairment against $11.2 billion of goodwill and intangibles. That path revisits the May 2026 low of $44.82 and below.

**Base case ($58, +6.5%).** The deal closes in mid-2027 substantially on announced terms. Synergies land at roughly the guided $600 million with normal slippage. Organic growth stays 1-3%, adjusted EPS reaches the $3.05-3.13 guide and grows to about $3.30 in FY2027, and the market applies a mid-teens multiple to a levered but investment-grade staples platform. Total return of roughly 10% including the 3.53% yield over twelve months.

**On the drawdown.** The stock is 25% below its August 2025 high of $72.58 and was 38% below at the May low. The market repriced three specific things, and it repriced them correctly: the transfer of 65.0% of the combined equity to Unilever and its shareholders, the step from roughly 3.0x to 4.0x net leverage, and the confirmation in Q2 that volume in the most profitable geography is negative under price. I do not identify a mispricing here. What exists is an option on $600 million of synergies priced at roughly fair value.

**Score trajectory.** Scores have declined from 5.5 in March to 4.8 in July, driven by Financial Health (6.5 → 4.5), Competitive Position (7.5 → 5.5) and Management Quality (6.5 → 4.5). Current data confirms rather than reverses that direction, though modestly: the definitive ancillary agreements remain unfinalized, Q3 consensus was cut 9.7%, and the Q2 adjusted beat contained a contingent tariff credit. Offsetting positives are real but small — a 14.4% Q2 surprise, reaffirmed full-year guidance, an operating model and executive slate announced July 23, and one director's open-market purchase. Overall moves to 4.6. The price is 14.9% higher than at the July report low, which reduces the margin of safety on unchanged fundamentals; that alone would not justify a lower score, but combined with the near-term estimate cuts it supports a marginal step down rather than an upgrade.

**Management assessment.** Five-year indexed TSR of 79.77 against a peer group at 102.38 — cumulative −20.2% versus +2.4%, roughly 2,260bp of underperformance — while granted CEO compensation stayed in a $10.4-12.7 million band. Disclosed incentive metrics (net sales, adjusted net sales growth, adjusted EPS, gross profit, stock price, TSR) contain no return-on-capital or leverage measure, which is an odd design for a company taking leverage to 4.0x and issuing equity equal to 65% of its combined base. The June 24, 2026 retention awards of $1,000,000 cash per NEO other than the CEO vest 50% at closing and 50% six months later — standard mega-merger mechanics, but they pay on closing rather than on integration outcomes. The MdM control premium was substantial: $750 million for 25 points is $30.0 million per point against $20.16-20.17 million per point assigned to both the retained 50% and the 25% noncontrolling interest, an implied excess of roughly $246 million, which management's own disclosure attributes to a control-premium adjustment and which the reacquired perpetual license arguably justifies. Insider activity is close to neutral: one open-market director purchase (Conway, 1,100 shares at $50.21 on July 23, $55,227), and a same-day option exercise-and-sale by former CEO Kurzius (205,538 shares at $52.69) that carries little forward information. The 40-year dividend streak is the strongest evidence of shareholder alignment.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** Two near-term checkpoints: the fiscal Q3 report (quarter ending August 31, 2026), at which McCormick has committed to provide further detail on anticipated revenue and cost synergies and transition-services scope; and the proxy/shareholder vote on the charter amendment and share issuance, timing not yet disclosed. Closing is expected by mid-2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY).**
1. Synergy detail at Q3 that raises the $600 million run-rate figure, quantifies revenue synergies beyond the ~$100 million reinvestment bucket, or confines transition services to a materially narrower scope than "up to 24 months."
2. Two consecutive quarters of non-negative Consumer Americas volume/mix, which would establish that the elasticity drag is anniversarying rather than compounding.
3. Price toward **$46-48** on unchanged fundamentals, which would put the shares near 14x FY2027 consensus of $3.30 with a 4.0%+ yield and would restore the margin of safety the base case currently lacks.
4. Permanent financing placed at a blended cost at or below 5.25% with the term-loan interest-coverage covenant comfortably met, plus a disclosed divestiture that accelerates deleveraging.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID).**
1. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance cut below the $3.05 low end, or FY2027 consensus falling below $3.15.
2. Consumer Americas volume/mix worse than −3.6pp in Q3, or total organic growth below +1%.
3. Definitive ancillary agreements landing on materially worse terms — a broader U.S. Asset Sale Election, a longer or costlier TSA, or a closing-adjustment increase to the $15.7 billion cash payment.
4. Guided closing net leverage above 4.0x, a negative rating action or outlook change, or any dividend increase below 2% in the November declaration.
5. A successful government appeal reversing the $30.8 million tariff refund alongside a Q3 adjusted margin miss.

**Position management for existing holders.** Hold for the 3.53% yield and the synergy option.
