**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Enact Holdings is a pure-play U.S. private mortgage insurer, run through its North Carolina insurance subsidiary (EMICO) and a Bermuda reinsurer (Enact Re), and majority-owned by Genworth Financial (~81%). The business is high-quality on a balance-sheet basis: ~54% net margin, low leverage (D/E ~0.29), PMIERs sufficiency of 162% with a ~$1.9B excess-asset cushion, a risk-to-capital ratio of 10.0:1 against a 25:1 regulatory maximum, and an undrawn $435M revolver. The company returns capital aggressively: $93M of buybacks plus $30M of dividends in Q1 2026, a 14% dividend increase to $0.24/quarter, and a fresh $500M repurchase authorization.

The problem for the equity at $46.76 is that the operating engine is running flat while the reported earnings line is being lifted by two mechanisms that either fade or serve the parent. Q1 2026 net income was up only 1% YoY and premiums were down 1%; the entire ~9% diluted EPS gain came from a 6.7% reduction in share count. Reported net incurred losses were held down by a $39M favorable prior-year reserve release (down from $47M a year earlier), which is why the loss ratio still rose to 15% from 12%. As those releases normalize toward zero and delinquencies tick up (2.61% from 2.34%), the underlying loss trend shows through more. Consensus EPS estimates have drifted lower across every window over the past 90 days.

We move to HOLD (income rationale). The stock now trades above the consensus mean target ($46.00) and just below the high ($48.00), at ~9.9x earnings, a premium to its two closest MI peers (ESNT 8.2x, NMIH 7.5x). The appreciation case is largely spent; the durable return here is the substantial all-in capital-return yield supported by a strong balance sheet. The score steps down from 6.0 to 5.4, reflecting a higher price against a flat-to-softening operating base rather than any balance-sheet deterioration.

## Company Overview

Enact writes primary mortgage insurance that protects lenders and the GSEs (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac) against losses on low-down-payment residential mortgages (borrowers with less than 20% down). This insurance is a precondition for those loans to be sold to the GSEs, which anchors demand for the product. The company operates in a single reportable segment and competes against roughly five other private mortgage insurers and, structurally, against the government (FHA/VA) for the same low-down-payment borrower.

The defining feature of the equity is Genworth's ~81% ownership, retained after Enact's September 2021 minority IPO floated 18.4% of shares. Genworth controls the board, consents to all Enact dividends, manages the insurance subsidiaries' investment portfolio for a fee, and, per a February 2, 2026 agreement, participates in a pro-rata share repurchase designed to hold its ownership percentage constant. Enact paid Genworth $75.4M for repurchased shares in Q1 2026. Enact is effectively a controlled company, and a meaningful portion of the "buyback" is a cash conduit to a parent with legacy long-term-care liabilities.

## Financial Analysis

The balance sheet is the strongest part of the story. Q1 2026 total assets were $6.96B against $5.34B equity and only $745M of debt (6.25% senior notes due 2029). The investment portfolio is $6.13B, 99% investment grade, yielding 4.5% on a 4.9-year duration. Statutory capital is $5.3B combined, and PMIERs available-asset sufficiency runs at 162% (~$1.9B excess), with the phased-in standards fully implementing through September 2026 and no visible strain. Altman Z is ~3.36 (safe zone). This is a franchise that can continue returning capital and absorb moderate stress.

The income statement carries the nuance:

- **Net income $167.8M (+1% YoY); diluted EPS $1.18 vs $1.08.** The EPS growth outpaced net income growth entirely because diluted shares fell from 152.9M to 142.6M (-6.7%).
- **Total revenue $312.1M (+2%).** Premiums were roughly flat at $242.9M (-1%), pressured by higher ceded premiums and lower persistency; the growth came from net investment income of $70.9M (+12.5%) on higher yields and a larger invested base.
- **Loss composition.** Current-accident-year losses were $79.3M, but a $39M prior-year reserve release brought net incurred losses to $33.1M. The loss ratio rose to 15% from 12% precisely because the release shrank from $47M. As the post-COVID reserve conservatism unwinds, this benign-credit tailwind fades and the reported loss ratio normalizes upward.
- **Capital return.** $93M buybacks + $30M dividends in Q1, dividend raised to $0.24/quarter (from $0.185 a year earlier), $467.9M remaining on the $500M authorization.

The earnings surprise record is clean: four consecutive beats, with the largest (+11.5%) in Q4 2025. That supports confidence in near-term consensus. But the beats are modest (1.7%–3.2% in three of four quarters), and the earnings are of high quality relative to distressed insurers while being financially engineered upward via buybacks against a plateauing operating base. AOCI swung to -$82.7M on a $52.6M unrealized bond mark in Q1 as rates rose late in the quarter, book-value noise on a held-to-recover, investment-grade portfolio rather than an economic loss.

## Growth Analysis

Underlying growth is low-single-digit at best, and the reported growth is largely mechanical. Net income was up 1%, premiums down 1%. The reasons matter:

- **Rising reinsurance cessions cap net premium.** The quota-share ceding percentage has more than doubled, from 16.1% in 2023 to 34.25% for the 2027-1 program. This is prudent (it funds PMIERs credit and de-risks the book) but structurally suppresses net premium growth even as insurance-in-force expands.
- **Persistency fell to 80% from 84%.** Rate-driven refinancing accelerated the run-off of the in-force book. It also drove a 30% jump in new insurance written (NIW $12.8B), but that spike is a function of rate volatility rather than a durable demand shift, and the new business carries thinner economics as more is ceded.
- **Reserve releases are fading.** The single largest support to reported earnings ($39M in Q1, down from $47M) is a tailwind that normalizes toward zero in a benign cycle and reverses in a downturn.

Estimate revisions confirm the deceleration: current-year EPS revised to $4.75 (from $4.81 90 days ago, -1.3%) and next-year to $4.92 (from $5.06, -2.6%). This is negative earnings momentum on a mature book, not the >30% positive-revision signal that would flag a re-rating candidate. Growth Prospects scores 4.5.

## Valuation Assessment

At $46.76, Enact trades at ~9.9x trailing/forward EPS and ~1.23x equity (market cap $6.55B vs $5.34B equity). Against the closest mortgage-insurer peers, this is a premium, not a discount: ESNT trades at 8.2x and NMIH at 7.5x (WD at 25.9x is a mortgage-banking business, not a comparable MI). The peer median is 8.2x. There is no identifiable reason the market is mispricing this stock to the upside: the multiple already sits above its direct competitors, and the earnings supporting it are flattered by fading reserve releases.

The analyst target set reinforces this. Low $44, mean/median $46, high $48, so the stock is trading above the mean and within 2.6% of the top end. On a stock with rising estimates, consensus targets act as floors that get revised up; that principle does not apply here, because estimates are drifting lower and the multiple is not cheap relative to growth. A low-single-digit grower at ~10x with negative revisions is fairly valued to modestly rich, and cheapness that reflects a structurally capped franchise (cessions, plateauing premium, controlled-company overhang) is a warning rather than an opportunity.

The one genuine valuation support is absolute capital-return yield: ~$123M of combined buybacks and dividends in a single quarter against a $6.55B cap annualizes to a high-single-digit all-in shareholder yield. But a material portion of the buyback funds Genworth's pro-rata sale rather than reflecting pure open-market accretion, which dilutes the quality of that return to minority holders. Valuation scores 4.5.

## Competitive Landscape

The U.S. PMI industry is a rational oligopoly of about six players that has generally held pricing discipline. Enact competes on risk-based pricing (its "Rate360" engine) and disciplined selection, writing high-credit-quality business (Q1 NIW: 52% above 760 FICO, 77% purchase-oriented). Ratings momentum is a tailwind: Moody's upgraded EMICO to A2 (August 2025), S&P (A-) and A.M. Best (A-) carry positive outlooks, and Fitch affirmed A/Stable (April 2026). Higher ratings lower reinsurance and capital costs.

The trajectory, however, is stable-to-slightly-eroding rather than improving. Persistency is falling, the loss ratio is normalizing upward, and rising cessions cap the economics of new business. The GSEs and FHA are simultaneously gatekeeper and competitor, a permanent structural feature. The FHFA's July 2025 adoption of VantageScore 4.0 for GSE-delivered mortgages is a watch item with uncertain implementation impact. This is a well-run participant in a commoditized industry holding its position rather than gaining share. Competitive Position scores 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Genworth control and overhang (idiosyncratic to ACT).** Genworth's ~81% stake gives minority holders limited control, and Genworth's legacy long-term-care liabilities create an incentive to extract cash from Enact via dividends and repurchases. Any future decision by Genworth to sell down its stake is a real supply overhang. External framing (Genworth trading at a discount to its ~$4.7B ACT stake) suggests the overhang is more a GNW problem than an ACT operating problem, but it caps the multiple.

**Housing-credit cycle.** As a PMI, Enact's core tail risk is a home-price decline or recession. The current environment is unfavorable at the margin: elevated CPI (3.3%), 4.3% unemployment, deteriorated housing affordability, and delinquencies already ticking up (2.61% from 2.34%; new defaults 13,559 vs 12,237). Management attributes this to "normal loss development on newer books," but with earnings currently supported by releases, adverse development would hit reported results hard; the 10-Q explicitly warns reserve adjustments "could possibly be significant."

**GSE/PMIERs dependence.** The entire model rests on GSE eligibility and the GSEs continuing to require mortgage insurance. Changes to GSE charters, guaranty fees, or FHA pricing would be existential. This is largely priced as a permanent feature.

**Reinsurance dependence.** $1.94B of PMIERs credit comes from quota-share, excess-of-loss, and ILN-based structures. Rising cessions de-risk but pressure net premium and expose the company to reinsurance availability and pricing.

Offsetting these is a strong capital position (162% PMIERs, $1.9B cushion, 10.0:1 RTC) that provides genuine downside protection through moderate stress. On balance, the concentration of macro-housing and Genworth-specific risk against a strong-but-not-invulnerable backdrop leaves Risk Factors at 5.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV is 50.5% against 22.0% one-year historical volatility (richly priced), with a 14.6% put/call skew (elevated put demand) and a term structure in backwardation (near-term 50.5% vs long-term 25.2%), signaling the market expects an imminent move (the nearest expiry implies ±11.0%, straddling the August 5 earnings date). ATM IV has collapsed from 152.4% in mid-June, so the extreme has unwound, but current positioning still leans defensive. Short interest is elevated at 14.8% of float with 11.3 days to cover, bearish institutional positioning that also creates modest squeeze potential on any upside surprise. The put skew is a divergence worth flagging: the options market is more cautious than the fundamentals alone would suggest, consistent with housing-cycle and reserve-normalization concerns.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Credit stays benign, reserve releases persist longer than expected, and continued buybacks compound EPS on a stable operating base. Ratings upgrades lower capital costs, and the multiple re-rates modestly toward 11x on rising next-year EPS. At 11x × $4.92, the stock reaches ~$54 (+15%). The high shareholder yield provides carry while the thesis plays out, and any Genworth sell-down that removes the overhang could be a positive re-rating catalyst.

### Bear Case
The housing-credit cycle turns. Delinquencies accelerate, prior-year reserve releases reverse rather than fade, and the loss ratio climbs from 15% toward the mid-20s%. EPS compresses toward $3.75, the multiple contracts to ~8x, and the stock falls to ~$33 (-29%). A Genworth stake sale into a weak tape amplifies the drawdown via supply overhang.

### Base Case
Credit normalizes gradually, reserve releases fade toward zero, premium growth stays capped by cessions, and EPS holds roughly flat as buybacks offset a plateauing operating base. The multiple stays near 9.5-10x on ~$4.85 EPS, leaving the stock around $47, essentially flat, with the ~2.1% dividend plus buyback yield the primary return. This is the most likely outcome and is why the stock is fairly valued at current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report on August 5, 2026. Key items to monitor: the loss ratio trajectory (as reserve releases fade), the delinquency rate, persistency, and any new macro language in the MD&A on housing.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-$40s (≤~8.5x forward EPS), restoring a discount to intrinsic value and closing the premium to ESNT/NMIH.
- Estimate revisions inflecting positive alongside evidence that current-accident-year loss trends are stabilizing rather than normalizing up.
- A resolution of the Genworth overhang (structured sell-down or increased float) that removes the controlled-company discount without a disorderly supply event.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A quantified spike in delinquencies or a reserve reversal that pushes the loss ratio decisively higher, indicating the credit cycle has turned.
- A dividend or buyback cut driven by PMIERs pressure or a Genworth liquidity event.
- Consensus next-year EPS revised below ~$4.70 on deteriorating credit, confirming the operating base is contracting rather than plateauing.

## Conclusion

Enact is a well-run, strongly capitalized mortgage insurer whose reported growth is manufactured by buybacks and flattered by fading reserve releases, sitting at ~9.9x, a premium to its closest MI peers, on top of the consensus price target with estimates drifting lower. The balance sheet and capital-return profile are the durable positives; the operating engine is flat and the housing-credit cycle plus Genworth control are the two-sided swing factors. With the appreciation case largely spent and the options market pricing near-term stress, the recommendation is **HOLD** on an income basis: own it for the stable cash flows and high all-in shareholder yield, not for near-term price appreciation. Overall score 5.4, a modest step down from 6.0, driven by a higher price against a softening operating base rather than any deterioration in the franchise itself.
