## Executive Summary

Heritage Financial Corporation is a $1.13B market cap community bank headquartered in Washington State, operating 65 branches primarily along the Pacific Northwest I-5 corridor. The company completed its largest acquisition in a decade, Olympic Bancorp/Kitsap Bank, on January 31, 2026, expanding total assets from $7.0B to $8.5B. Q1 2026 performance was strong: net interest margin expanded to 3.96%, a provision reversal was recorded, and EPS of $0.48 beat consensus by 3.4% despite $5.2M in one-time merger costs.

The core investment case rests on NIM expansion (3.31% in FY2024 → 3.96% post-merger), a 3.5% dividend yield, and a moderate valuation of approximately 12.5x normalized forward earnings. Against this stands a deteriorating estimate revision trend (current quarter estimates cut 8.7% in 30 days), significant CRE concentration at 64% of loans with $676M in office exposure, and the inherent integration risks of absorbing $1.6B in new assets. The stock has appreciated 18% over the past year but still sits 5% below its 52-week high of $28.98.

The specific headwind preventing a BUY recommendation is the negative estimate revision trajectory: consensus EPS for FY2026 has been cut from $2.51 to $2.39 in the last 30 days, and FY2027 from $2.91 to $2.79. Falling estimates on a stock at peer-median valuation do not create the asymmetric setup required for a BUY.

## Company Overview

Heritage Financial Corporation operates Heritage Bank, a 97-year-old Washington state-chartered commercial bank. The bank serves small and medium-sized businesses across Washington, Oregon (Portland/Eugene metro), and Idaho (Boise), with a focus on relationship-based commercial lending and low-cost deposit gathering.

**Key Operating Metrics (Q1 2026):**
- Total assets: $8.5B
- Total loans: $5.72B (82.8% commercial)
- Total deposits: $7.25B (84.5% non-maturity, 28.5% noninterest-bearing)
- Branches: 65 (up from 49 pre-merger)
- Employees: Not disclosed but management team average tenure 9.8 years

The Olympic acquisition closed January 31, 2026 for approximately $185M in all-stock consideration (7.17M shares at $25.81/share). Olympic operated as Kitsap Bank across 16 branches in Washington, contributing $1.6B in assets, $0.9B in loans, and $1.4B in deposits.

## Financial Analysis

### Profitability Recovery

Heritage's earnings trajectory shows a clear recovery from the 2024 interest rate squeeze:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1'26 Ann. |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|------------|
| Net Income ($M) | $81.9 | $61.8 | $43.3 | $67.5 | ~$75.6* |
| EPS | $2.31 | $1.75 | $1.24 | $1.96 | ~$1.83* |
| NIM | — | 3.56% | 3.31% | 3.58% | 3.96% |

*Annualized Q1 2026 ($18.9M × 4), which understates run-rate given Q1 included only 2 months of Olympic and $5.2M in one-time merger costs.

Adjusting for merger costs after tax (~$4M), normalized Q1 annualized EPS approaches $2.20, implying a forward P/E of approximately 12.5x.

### Net Interest Margin Drivers

The NIM expansion from 3.31% to 3.96% is the most important fundamental development, driven by three factors:

1. **Olympic's deposit base** — Lower-cost funding mix reduced blended deposit costs to 1.71% (down 21bps from Q1 2025)
2. **Loan yield improvement** — Portfolio yield of 5.73% reflects both organic repricing and favorable acquired loan marks
3. **Borrowing reduction** — FHLB advances collapsed from $383M (end-2024) to $20M, eliminating higher-cost wholesale funding

Approximately 9bps of the Q1 2026 NIM reflects purchased loan accretion ($1.6M), which will decline over time. The "core" NIM of ~3.87% remains strong relative to the peer group.

### Balance Sheet Strength

Capital ratios remain well above regulatory thresholds post-merger:
- CET1: 12.2% (vs. 6.5% well-capitalized)
- Total Capital: 13.5% (vs. 10.0% well-capitalized)
- Tangible book value: ~$15.59/share (price/TBV ~1.76x)

Liquidity is ample: $3.2B available (44% of deposits), with minimal wholesale funding dependency. The bank carries only $48.5M in brokered deposits (0.67% of total).

### Credit Quality

Credit metrics improved materially in Q1 2026:
- Nonaccrual loans: $15.0M (0.26% of loans), down from $21.0M at year-end 2025
- Net charge-offs: $552K (0.04% annualized)
- ACL coverage: 1.06% of loans
- Provision: Net REVERSAL of $1.0M

The FY2025 spike in nonaccruals (from $4.1M to $21.0M) was concentrated in a few construction loans that appear to be resolving. Olympic contributed zero nonaccrual loans at acquisition.

## Growth Analysis

Heritage's growth profile is that of a mature community bank supplemented by periodic acquisitions:

**Organic:** Mid-single-digit loan growth (4-6% annually). Organic deposit growth is constrained in the current environment. The bank ceased residential mortgage origination in January 2024, eliminating a fee income source but improving focus.

**Acquisition-driven:** The Olympic deal adds immediate scale. Full-year contribution will be visible from Q2 2026 forward (Q1 only captured 2 months). Heritage has completed 11 major acquisitions since 1998, suggesting additional M&A is likely over a 3-5 year horizon.

**Headwinds to growth:** Current quarter EPS consensus was $0.59 thirty days ago and is now $0.54 — an 8.7% cut. Full-year FY2026 has been reduced from $2.51 to $2.39. FY2027 from $2.91 to $2.79. This suggests analysts are recalibrating either: (a) integration timeline expectations, (b) rate path assumptions (higher-for-longer compresses asset sensitivity benefits), or (c) credit loss normalization expectations as merger synergies take time to materialize.

The inconsistent earnings surprise record (2 beats, 2 misses in 4 quarters, including a severe -28.6% miss in Q2 2025) reduces confidence in consensus estimates as a floor.

## Valuation Assessment

At $27.48/share:
- **Trailing P/E (FY2025):** 14.0x ($1.96 EPS)
- **Forward P/E (FY2026E):** 11.5x ($2.39 consensus)
- **Forward P/E (FY2027E):** 9.8x ($2.79 consensus)
- **Price/TBV:** ~1.76x
- **Dividend yield:** 3.5%

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E is 12.4x. HFWA at 11.5x forward is slightly below median, but peers Preferred Bank (8.8x) and Tompkins Financial (7.4x) trade at significant discounts. The premium vs. PFBC and TMP likely reflects Heritage's larger scale and stronger NIM trajectory, but it is not substantial enough to constitute a clear mispricing.

**Analyst targets:** The consensus range is $30-$32, implying 9-16% upside from $27.48. With 1 Strong Buy, 1 Buy, and 4 Holds, the analyst community views HFWA as fairly valued with modest upside.

The valuation is reasonable but not cheap enough to compensate for falling estimates. A forward P/E of 11.5x on declining consensus means the effective P/E is moving against holders. The stock needs either estimates to stabilize/rise or the price to compress further to create genuine asymmetry.

## Competitive Landscape

Heritage occupies a defensible niche: large enough to serve mid-market commercial clients ($8.5B in assets places it among the top community banks in Washington) but small enough to maintain the relationship-intensive service model that retains sticky commercial deposits.

**Strengths:**
- 28.5% noninterest-bearing deposits (well above industry average of ~22%)
- SBA Preferred Lender status
- 97-year brand heritage in Washington
- Proven acquisition integration capability (11 deals)

**Weaknesses:**
- Geographic concentration (Pacific Northwest I-5 corridor)
- No technology differentiation vs. fintech challengers
- 5-year TSR of 123% lags peer group at 160%
- Exited mortgage origination — reducing fee income diversification

Heritage's competitive position is stable but not improving. Growth comes through M&A, which is episodic and carries integration risk, rather than through product innovation or geographic expansion beyond its core market.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **CRE Concentration (64% of loans):** This is the most significant quantifiable risk. Office CRE of $676M includes exposure to a sector with structural headwinds from remote work. Management notes 46% is owner-occupied and 82% carries personal guarantees, which mitigates loss-given-default but not probability-of-default if tenants vacate. In a rising-rate, slowing-economy environment, CRE repricing stress is elevated.

2. **Negative Estimate Revisions:** The 30-day decline reflects broad-based reassessment by all 6 covering analysts. The direction and magnitude suggest either emerging credit concerns not yet visible in headline metrics, or NIM compression fears as the yield curve evolves.

3. **Integration Execution:** Olympic was Heritage's largest deal in a decade. System conversion, deposit retention (particularly the 16 Kitsap Bank branches retaining the Kitsap brand), and cultural alignment are still in early stages. Any deposit attrition or unexpected credit issues in the acquired portfolio could pressure results.

4. **Uninsured Deposits (39.1%):** While improved from 41.1% at year-end, $2.83B in uninsured deposits creates vulnerability to confidence-driven outflows in a banking crisis scenario. The March 2023 regional banking stress demonstrated this risk concretely.

5. **Interest Rate Path Uncertainty:** Heritage is slightly asset-sensitive (-200bp rate shock reduces NII by 3.7%). If rates remain elevated for longer, this is modestly positive for NIM. However, if the rate environment triggers a credit downturn, the benefit evaporates.

### Mitigants
- CET1 12.2%
- $3.2B liquidity buffer
- Proven credit discipline (net charge-offs 0.04% annualized)
- Low wholesale funding dependency
- Manageable goodwill ($279M, 25% of equity)

### Short Interest Context
Short interest at 7.0% of float with 9.6 days to cover is moderately elevated for a community bank. This likely reflects skepticism related to CRE exposure or post-merger execution doubts.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($30-32, +9-16%)
- Full Olympic integration drives expense synergies and NIM sustains above 3.80%
- Estimate revisions stabilize as merger costs roll off and full Olympic accretion becomes visible
- CRE portfolio performs well through the cycle given underwriting discipline
- Additional M&A targets become available at attractive prices
- Dividend grows to $1.04+ annually as earnings power reaches $2.50+
- P/E re-rates to 13x on $2.50 normalized earnings = $32.50

### Bear Case ($22-23, -16-20%)
- CRE stress materializes — office portfolio generates elevated charge-offs requiring provision build
- Olympic integration proves more costly (deposit attrition, additional severance, system issues)
- NIM compresses back toward 3.50% as purchased loan accretion wanes and competition for deposits intensifies
- Estimate revisions continue downward, reflecting earnings of $2.10-2.20 vs. current $2.39 consensus
- P/E compresses to 10-11x on regional banking sector de-rating = $22-24

### Why Not BUY: The Specific Barrier

**Negative estimate revisions of -5% to -9% across all forward periods over just 30 days, combined with an inconsistent earnings beat record (50% miss rate), mean the current forward multiple of 11.5x is not reliably anchored to achievable earnings.** If consensus continues falling (as the 30-day trajectory implies), the effective valuation is higher than it appears. A community bank trading at peer-median multiples with deteriorating estimates does not offer asymmetric upside — it offers fair value that could become expensive if the estimate decline continues.

This is falsifiable: if Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July) beat consensus and estimates stabilize or reverse upward, the thesis changes. Specifically, if the current quarter estimate of $0.54 is beaten by >5% ($0.57+ actual) AND full-year consensus stops declining, the stock would warrant an upgrade to BUY at current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. EPS estimate revisions stabilize (30-day revision turns flat or positive for FY2026/FY2027)
2. Q2 2026 earnings beat by >5% ($0.57+ actual vs. $0.54 consensus) demonstrating merger accretion exceeding expectations
3. Stock pulls back to $24.50-25.00 (forward P/E <10.5x, dividend yield >3.8%), improving risk/reward

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Nonaccrual loans increase above 1.0% of total loans (currently 0.26%)
2. NIM compresses below 3.50% for two consecutive quarters
3. Deposit outflows exceed 5% over any quarter
4. CRE charge-offs exceed 50bps annualized

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July 2026). This is the first quarter with full Olympic contribution and without merger-closing costs. It will provide the clearest view of run-rate earnings power.

## Conclusion

Heritage Financial is a well-managed, conservatively capitalized Pacific Northwest community bank that has executed a meaningful acquisition and is demonstrating genuine NIM improvement. The 3.5% dividend yield provides income support, and the franchise is fundamentally sound.

However, the stock is not cheap enough relative to peers to provide a margin of safety against falling analyst estimates. At 11.5x forward earnings with a 50% quarterly beat rate and estimates declining 5-9% in the past month, the probability of positive surprise is not substantially above coin-flip. Income investors already positioned should hold for the dividend and NIM trajectory, but new capital should wait for either estimate stabilization or a more compelling entry point.
