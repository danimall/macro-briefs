**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

GE Aerospace is the pure-play commercial and defense engine business that survived General Electric's three-way breakup (GE HealthCare 2023, GE Vernova 2024). It operates the world's largest commercial engine installed base — roughly 50,000 commercial and 30,000 military engines — which feeds an aftermarket that generates about 70% of revenue at high margins under 10-to-25-year service agreements. The franchise is high quality: FY2025 adjusted EPS rose 38% to $6.37, free cash flow reached $7.7B, GAAP profit margin expanded 210bp to 21.8%, and the company earned two ratings upgrades from each of Moody's and S&P inside 13 months. Q1 2026 carried the momentum forward with revenue up 25%, adjusted EPS up 25%, LEAP deliveries up 63%, and backlog (RPO) jumping to $211.3B.

The constraint is price, not business quality. At $323.88 the stock trades at roughly 40x trailing and ~43x current-year EPS ($7.56 consensus), a premium to a re-rating aerospace/defense cohort (GD 21.6x, LMT 25.8x, RTX 33.1x). The high multiple is supported by the recurring, visible aftermarket annuity and accelerating units, but consensus estimate revisions have flattened — current-year EPS is essentially unchanged over 30 days and up only 1.7% over 90 days, with next-quarter revisions slightly negative. There is no estimate-velocity acceleration of the kind that would justify chasing a 43x multiple. The market is already pricing flawless execution on the 2,000+/year LEAP ramp and continued aftermarket strength.

We rate GE a HOLD with a bullish lean. The score trajectory of prior reviews (7.5 in February drifting to 7.0 by late May, with growth, management, and risk dimensions each fading a full point) reflects this maturation: the franchise has not deteriorated, but the easy gap between price and value has closed. The fundamental story remains one of the best in industrials, and we would be buyers on a pullback toward the low-$280s — near where the stock traded after the April print and roughly the $270 analyst floor — but at the current multiple with flat revisions, the asymmetry from $324 is balanced rather than favorable.

## Company Overview

GE Aerospace reports two segments. **Commercial Engines & Services (CES)**, about 73% of segment revenue, generated FY2025 revenue of $33.3B (+24%) at a 26.6% segment margin. The crown jewel is the LEAP narrowbody engine, produced through CFM International (a 50/50 JV with Safran), which is sole-source on the Boeing 737 MAX and an option on the Airbus A320neo family. GEnx and GE9X power widebodies (787, 777X). The economics run through long-term service agreements (LTSAs): engines are often sold at or below cost, and the multi-decade aftermarket on a growing installed base produces the profit.

**Defense & Propulsion Technologies (DPT)** generated FY2025 revenue of $10.6B (+11%) at a 12.3% margin, supplying engines for the F-15/F-16 (F110), F/A-18 (F404/F414), and helicopters (T408, T700), plus additive and propulsion technology. Effective January 15, 2026, CES was expanded to own the full commercial engine lifecycle and the aeroderivative business moved into DPT.

The competitive structure is a narrowbody duopoly against Pratt & Whitney (RTX) and Rolls-Royce on widebodies, with extremely high switching costs once an airframe is certified on a given engine. The proprietary FLIGHT DECK lean operating system is management's central lever for ramping output and improving aftermarket turnaround times.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability are accelerating.** FY2025 total revenue was $45.9B (+18%); adjusted revenue (ex run-off insurance) was $42.3B (+21%), with Services at $30.2B driving the mix. GAAP profit was $10.0B; continuing EPS was $8.05 and adjusted EPS $6.37. Q1 2026 sustained this: revenue $12.4B, adjusted revenue +29%, adjusted EPS $1.86. Commercial engine units rose 50% YoY (640 vs 426), LEAP units 63% (520 vs 319), and internal shop-visit revenue grew 35%.

**Cash generation is high quality.** FY2025 FCF was $7.7B (up from $6.2B), supported by CFOA of $8.5B against light capex (~$1.3B gross), reflecting the asset-light services model. FCF is the primary incentive metric for management compensation.

**The balance sheet is investment-grade and improving.** Total borrowings were $20.5B against ~$12.4B cash and an undrawn $3B revolver. Moody's upgraded to A2/P-1 (Feb 2026) and S&P to A- with Positive outlook (Apr 2026). A screening Altman Z-Score will flag GE as distressed (~0.7); this is a false signal. The asset-light services model loads the balance sheet with contract liabilities, progress collections, and a ~$36B run-off insurance reserve block, all of which mechanically depress the ratio while the company generates strong FCF and earns single-A ratings. Leverage is not the risk here.

**Capital allocation is aggressive on returns.** FY2025 repurchases totaled $7.4B; $14.5B of the prior $15B authorization had been used by Q1 2026, and the board authorized a new $20B buyback (effective Q2 2026). The dividend was raised ~31% to $0.47/quarter. The earnings quality is strong: a beat in each of the last four quarters (13.0%, 6.8%, 10.9%, 12.6%), supporting confidence in forward estimates.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is the aftermarket annuity on an expanding installed base. Internal shop-visit revenue grew 24% in FY2025 and accelerated to 35% in Q1 2026 — a direct readthrough of the aging fleet, constrained new-aircraft supply keeping older airframes flying, and post-COVID traffic recovery. CFM is targeting record 2,000+ LEAP deliveries in 2026, each of which seeds decades of future service revenue. Backlog (RPO) reached $211.3B in Q1 2026, +11% sequentially, with Services (CES RPO) at ~$181B — unusually high earnings visibility for an industrial.

Order flow confirms durability: Q1 2026 wins included United (300+ GEnx for 787s), American (A321neo on LEAP-1A), Delta (GEnx for 787-10s), and a Ryanair MoU for a ~2,000-engine CFM56/LEAP service deal. The May 2026 announcement that China would buy 200 Boeing jets (potentially up to 750) carrying GE/CFM engines (Reuters cited a potential 400–450 engine component) is a directional tailwind, but with no firm timeline or booked orders it should be treated as optionality, not backlog. GE is the engine beneficiary of a Boeing aircraft deal, not the aircraft seller.

The forward-estimate picture tempers the enthusiasm. Consensus current-year EPS is $7.56, up only 1.7% over 90 days and unchanged over 30 days; next-quarter estimates were revised down 0.6% (30d) and 2.9% (90d); next-year ($9.32) is up 1.7%. These are stable estimates, not the +30% revision velocity that signals an underpriced earnings ramp. The fundamental growth is real and durable, but consensus has largely caught up to it.

## Valuation Assessment

At $323.88, GE trades at ~40x trailing EPS and ~43x current-year consensus, falling to ~35x next-year. Against the peer cohort — GD 21.6x, LMT 25.8x, RTX 33.1x, with HWM 59.9x and BA 89.9x as the high-multiple outliers — GE sits at a clear premium to the defense-heavy primes and roughly in line with RTX, which has a comparable commercial-aftermarket mix.

A premium is defensible. GE's aftermarket annuity is higher quality and more visible than a typical defense prime's fixed-price backlog, the installed base is irreplaceable, margins are expanding, and FCF conversion is strong. On a PEG basis, ~43x current-year against mid-teens adjusted EPS growth implies a PEG near 2.5–3.0 — rich. The case for the multiple rests on aftermarket durability extending well beyond the current consensus growth window.

The constraint is that this premium already discounts flawless execution. To generate meaningful upside from $324, the market needs GE to keep beating and consensus to re-rate higher, but estimate revisions have gone flat, removing the catalyst that typically drives a high-multiple stock higher. The dividend yield of ~0.6% provides no valuation support. Applying ~38x to next-year EPS yields roughly $354 (the analyst median), about 9% upside; a more conservative 32x yields ~$298, roughly 8% downside. The distribution is symmetric, which is the core reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY.

## Competitive Landscape

GE/CFM's position is improving on trajectory, not just strong on level. CES margin and 24–35% shop-visit revenue growth evidence pricing power and share capture within a structurally favorable industry. LEAP is sole-source on the 737 MAX and competes credibly against Pratt's GTF on the A320neo. The installed base creates switching costs measured in decades; once an airframe-engine pairing is certified, the aftermarket is effectively captive.

The forward technology bet is the RISE / Open Fan program with Safran, targeting the next-generation narrowbody in the 2030s — both the largest long-term opportunity and the principal long-cycle risk if a competing architecture wins the next single-aisle platform. The LEAP-1A durability program (a remediation kit deployed to ~30% of the in-service fleet) is evidence of a real early-life wear issue, but also of management addressing it before it becomes a share liability. GE's competitive position is strengthening, which supports the high competitive-position score.

## Risk Assessment

**Run-off insurance tail.** GE retains long-term care reinsurance: ~$23.8B GAAP LTC reserves (~$25.8B future policy benefit reserves) plus structured settlements. The 10-K sensitivity tables show adverse assumption changes (e.g., 5% higher utilization) could each raise the PV of future cash flows by ~$1.2B, and statutory testing could force capital contributions. This is one of two Critical Audit Matters and a perennial overhang.

**LTSA accounting estimates.** Service revenue on the 10-to-25-year agreements uses percentage-of-completion with deep judgment on utilization and overhaul costs; the net LTSA liability was $7.2B with contracts only ~19% complete on average. A one-point profitability change moves the balance ~$0.4B. The Q1 2026 reversal of $118M of a prior tariff-related charge shows how sensitive reported profitability is to assumptions in either direction.

**Geopolitical/oil shock to air traffic.** Q1 2026 explicitly flagged the Middle East/Persian Gulf conflict reducing March departures and posing risk to shop visits, spare parts, and spare engines. The current macro environment — elevated oil prices and softening global discretionary demand — is the live swing factor for the aftermarket. Management reported no material Q1 impact and is tracking the high end of 2026 guidance, but a sustained oil spike or a broad air-travel slowdown would hit the highest-margin, highest-multiple part of the business.

**Supply chain and LEAP ramp execution.** Material availability and supplier delivery remain constraints on the LEAP target. At the current multiple, any ramp shortfall produces outsized derating because the multiple prices flawless execution.

**IRS audit and trailing spin liabilities.** GE received Acknowledgement-of-Facts IDRs in March 2026 on TCJA transition-tax computations; the IRS may assert a material amount of additional taxes (shared with GE Vernova/HealthCare per tax-matters agreements). Bank BPH (Poland) litigation reserve stands at $2.09B, GE Vernova credit-support RPO at ~$7.5B, and environmental/asbestos reserves at ~$2.2B.

**GAAP volatility from equity stakes.** Q1 2026 GAAP margin fell 490bp to 17.7%, driven largely by a $309M unrealized mark-to-market loss on GE's BETA Technologies (eVTOL) equity stake, not operational deterioration — operating margin held at 21.8%. This is a recurring source of reported-earnings noise.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 42.7% sits well above 1-year historical volatility of 31.0% (rich by 11.7pp), with the term structure in backwardation (42.7% near-term vs 35.4% six-month), pricing an imminent catalyst. Put/call skew is balanced (2.2%) and put/call OI is benign at 0.68, so the positioning is not directionally bearish, consistent with general macro/geopolitical hedging rather than a specific negative thesis on GE. Unusual call buying appeared at the $345 strike (June 12 expiry, 3.5x vol/OI), indicating some upside positioning. The rich near-term IV argues against chasing here and supports waiting for the volatility event to resolve before adding.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$405, +25%):** The LEAP ramp executes cleanly toward target, shop-visit revenue stays in the 25–35% range, the China engine opportunity converts to firm orders, and the aftermarket annuity durability drives consensus EPS estimates materially higher — re-establishing the estimate-revision velocity that justifies a 43x+ multiple. Continued buybacks and dividend growth compound returns. This matches the analyst high target.

**Bear Case (~$270, -17%):** A sustained oil spike or broad air-travel slowdown (the Q1-flagged Middle East risk materializing) compresses shop-visit demand; a LEAP ramp shortfall or durability cost overrun pressures CES margins; an adverse LTC reserve charge or IRS assessment surprises; and the multiple compresses toward the low-30s on flat revisions. This is roughly the analyst floor.

**Base Case (~$354, +9%):** GE continues mid-teens adjusted EPS growth, beats modestly, and the multiple holds near current levels as next-year EPS anchors the valuation. The stock tracks the analyst median.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (bullish lean).** The specific concern preventing a BUY is concrete and falsifiable: GE trades at ~43x current-year consensus EPS with current-year estimates revised **+0.0% over 30 days and only +1.7% over 90 days**, and next-quarter estimates revised **down 0.6% (30d) and 2.9% (90d)**. The estimate-revision velocity that justifies adding to a 43x multiple stock is absent — revisions are flat to slightly negative, not the +30% pace that signals underpriced earnings. With base-case upside of ~9% against a ~17% drop to the bear case, the risk/reward from current levels is balanced-to-unfavorable, and near-term IV is rich, signaling a pending volatility event. A BUY requires either a pullback toward the low-$280s/$270 floor that restores asymmetry, or a resumption of double-digit upward EPS revisions. Neither condition is met today.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-year or next-year EPS consensus revised up >5% over a 90-day window (re-establishing estimate velocity).
- A pullback to the low-$280s or below (~$270–285), restoring ≥15% upside to the analyst median with limited downside.
- Firm, dated China engine orders (the 400–450 engine opportunity) converting to backlog.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Shop-visit revenue growth decelerating below ~10% YoY, signaling the aftermarket annuity is rolling over.
- A material adverse LTC reserve charge or statutory capital contribution requirement.
- A LEAP ramp shortfall (deliveries tracking materially below the 2,000 target) or CES margin compression below ~24%.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, ~July). Monitor the Middle East/oil situation continuously given its direct readthrough to aftermarket demand.

## Conclusion

GE Aerospace is one of the highest-quality franchises in industrials: an irreplaceable installed base, a multi-decade high-margin aftermarket annuity, accelerating units, strong FCF, dual ratings upgrades, and a deep backlog. The operating execution under Culp has been excellent and the competitive position is strengthening. The thesis has not deteriorated — the score trajectory from 7.5 toward 7.0 reflects the closing of the price-to-value gap, not business decline.

At ~43x current-year EPS with flat estimate revisions and rich near-term volatility, the stock prices flawless execution, and the path to meaningful upside from $324 requires either continued beats that re-accelerate consensus or a lower entry point. The balanced risk/reward argues for patience. We rate GE a HOLD with a bullish lean and would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-$280s or on a resumption of double-digit upward EPS revisions.
