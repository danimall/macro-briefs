**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AAR Corp is a leading independent aerospace aftermarket provider riding two reinforcing tailwinds: an aging global commercial fleet that requires more parts and maintenance, and OEM production constraints at Boeing and Airbus that keep older aircraft flying longer. After a deliberately messy FY2025 — GAAP EPS of $0.35 depressed by a $55.6M FCPA settlement and a $72.4M loss on the Landing Gear Overhaul divestiture — the company has produced a clean recovery in FY2026. Nine-month adjusted diluted EPS reached $3.51 versus $2.75 a year earlier, Q3 sales rose 25% (14% organic), and the balance sheet was materially de-risked: a $273.9M equity raise plus sharply improved operating cash flow cut net leverage from above 3.0x to 2.17x, inside the 2.0–2.5x target.

The business is structurally higher-quality than a screening tool keying on trailing GAAP EPS would suggest. The Parts Supply segment, the highest-margin and fastest-growing unit at roughly 14% operating margin, grew 45% in Q3 (36% organic in new-parts distribution, 55% organic in government). Three FY2026 acquisitions (HAECO Americas, ADI, pending A-R-T) extend the airframe MRO and government product franchises, and a string of contract wins (USAF pallet contracts up to $450M, exclusive Triumph actuation distribution) supports the forward order book. The four-quarter beat streak (7–14% surprises) and rising current-year consensus (+4.1% over 90 days) confirm the operating momentum.

The constraint is price. AIR has risen sharply over the past twelve months to a record $132, now trades at roughly 27x the $4.90 current-year consensus EPS, and sits near the high end of the $125–$150 analyst target range with no dividend to cushion downside. The fundamental story is genuinely improving, but the market has caught up to it. We rate AIR a HOLD on a growth basis: the thesis is intact and warrants ownership, but the risk/reward at a record multiple does not support adding fresh capital here. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the high-$110s or on confirmation that the FY26 cash-flow improvement is durable rather than a working-capital timing effect.

## Company Overview

AAR is a global aerospace and defense aftermarket solutions provider operating in over 20 countries, headquartered in Wood Dale, Illinois, with a fiscal year ending May 31. It is not an OEM; it sells parts, repairs aircraft, and provides supply-chain and software services to commercial airlines, MROs, OEMs, and government customers including the U.S. DoD, the State Department, and foreign militaries. Revenue splits roughly 71% commercial and 29% government.

The company operates four segments (FY2025 third-party sales):

- **Parts Supply ($1,099.6M, 14.3% operating margin)** — the franchise asset. Sells used serviceable material (USM) and distributes new OEM parts under exclusive agreements (Unison, Chromalloy, Ontic, FTAI, Triumph actuation). Highest-margin and fastest-growing segment.
- **Repair & Engineering ($884.9M, 9.2%)** — airframe MRO (hangars in Miami, Oklahoma City) and component MRO, reshaped by the FY2024 Product Support acquisition and the FY2026 HAECO Americas deal.
- **Integrated Solutions ($695.3M, 5.2%)** — government supply-chain logistics, the State Department WASS fleet contract, power-by-the-hour programs, and Trax, the cloud MRO ERP software business acquired in 2023 (high-margin recurring revenue).
- **Expeditionary Services ($100.7M, 10.0%)** — military pallets, shelters, containers.

AAR's competitive edge is independence combined with global scale and breadth across parts, repair, and software. Its peer set spans HEICO, Curtiss-Wright, Woodward, VSE, V2X, Triumph, and Hexcel.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is robust and accelerating.** Sales moved from $1,990.5M (FY23) to $2,318.9M (FY24) to $2,780.5M (FY25, +19.9%), and FY26 is tracking toward roughly $3.3B on guidance of ~19% total / ~12% organic growth.

**FY2025 GAAP earnings badly understate the underlying business.** Operating income rose 43% to $185.2M, yet net income fell to $12.5M from $46.3M because of the FCPA settlement — largely non-deductible, which spiked the effective tax rate to 67.9% — and the LGO divestiture loss. Any analysis anchored to trailing GAAP EPS misreads this company. The clean view is FY26: 9-month net income of $137.0M versus a $21.5M loss a year earlier.

**Margins are improving via mix.** Consolidated gross margin holds near 19%, but the shift toward Parts Supply distribution and Trax software, and away from divested low-margin lines, is pushing adjusted operating margin toward the guided 10.2–10.5%. Q3 adjusted EBITDA margin was 12.1%, on adjusted EBITDA of $102.1M (+26%).

**Cash flow has historically been the weak spot, and this is the key item to watch.** FY25 operating cash flow was only $36.1M against $185M operating income, driven by working-capital build (inventory +$109M, receivables +$83M). This is structural — rapid growth ties up inventory in this model. The FY26 improvement is material: Q3 operating cash flow was $74.7M versus negative $18.7M a year earlier. The growth story depends on this improvement persisting rather than reflecting timing.

**The balance sheet was meaningfully de-risked in FY26.** FY25 ended with roughly $977M of debt (including $550M of 6.75% Senior Notes due 2029) and $75.4M of interest expense. The equity offering, which raised the diluted share count from 35.4M to 39.5M, plus stronger cash generation cut net debt to $816.5M. Equity grew to $1.64B from $1.21B. TTM adjusted EBITDA reached $376.2M. This is the single most important positive change since the FY25 10-K.

**Capital allocation is growth-directed.** No dividend since 2020; buybacks are minimal ($10.1M in FY25, ~$42.5M remaining). Capital flows to M&A ($222M deployed YTD FY26) and organic capacity (hangars). For a HOLD, the absence of yield support matters — there is no income floor under the stock at a record price.

## Growth Analysis

The structural driver is durable. The global commercial fleet is aging, and older aircraft require more parts and MRO; Boeing and Airbus production delays extend the service lives of existing aircraft. Both dynamics feed directly into USM, parts distribution, and airframe MRO — AAR's core. This is a multi-year demand tailwind rather than a single-year cyclical pop, which raises the durability of the growth thesis.

Organic momentum is genuine, not purely acquired. Q3 organic growth was 14.1%, with new-parts distribution up 36% organically and government up 55% organically. The contract pipeline supports the forward book: USAF pallet awards up to $450M, exclusive Triumph actuation distribution on Boeing/Airbus platforms, the Trax expansion with Air Atlanta Icelandic, and the completed Oklahoma City airframe MRO expansion (first aircraft in March 2026).

The M&A program is additive but raises integration stakes. HAECO Americas integration is reportedly ahead of schedule, ADI is exceeding expectations, and A-R-T is expected to close in Q4 FY26. Goodwill and intangibles total roughly $841M, so execution and impairment risk now carries real weight.

The estimate and surprise data confirm the recovery. The company has beaten in each of the last four quarters by 7–14%, and current-year consensus has been revised up 4.1% over 90 days to $4.90, with next year at $5.65. Revisions are positive but moderate — they do not reach the +30%-over-90-days threshold that would flag a sharp, underappreciated re-rating. Estimates are rising in line with delivery, not racing ahead of price. Growth prospects score 7.5: durable structural demand, genuine organic acceleration, and a confirmed beat pattern, tempered by the working-capital intensity that converts growth into cash only with a lag.

## Valuation Assessment

At $132, AIR trades at roughly 27x the $4.90 current-year consensus and ~23x the $5.65 next-year figure. PEG is reasonable against mid-teens earnings growth, and the multiple is justifiable for a deleveraging aftermarket compounder with a structural tailwind. The question is whether the current price offers asymmetric upside, and the answer is no longer clearly yes.

The peer table is of limited use here — the listed comparables (PSN, FLY, LUNR, HXL, ARXS) are a heterogeneous mix of space, materials, and loss-making names, producing a distorted median P/E of 64.7. The more relevant aftermarket peers (HEICO, Curtiss-Wright, Woodward, VSE) trade at premium multiples themselves, so AAR at ~27x forward is not an outlier within its true cohort. But it is not cheap either.

The constraints on the valuation score: the stock sits near the top of the $125–$150 analyst range, with the $128 median below the current price, and there is no dividend yield to anchor downside. Prior appreciation is not itself a reason to mark a stock down, and the run is fundamentally justified by the earnings recovery, but the market has closed the gap between price and the improved earnings power. The Seeking Alpha "structural earnings power not reflected in the multiple" thesis was more compelling when the stock was lower; at $132 the re-rating has largely occurred.

A simple base case: 25x the $5.65 next-year EPS yields a ~$141 target, roughly 7% above current, which aligns with the $150 high analyst target as the bull and the $125 low as the near-term floor. That upside is real but modest, and it sits against a balance of risks (FCPA NPA window, integration, working-capital, the uncapped A220 claim) that warrant a discount. Cheapness as a standalone signal has historically been an unreliable buy trigger; here the stock is not even cheap, so we score valuation 5.0 — fair, with the multiple having caught up to the fundamentals.

## Competitive Landscape

AAR's position is improving on trajectory, which is what matters most. As the leading independent aftermarket player, it is gaining share in the high-margin distribution business and deliberately shifting its mix toward higher-margin parts and Trax software while exiting commoditized lines like Landing Gear Overhaul. The breadth across parts, repair, and software is a genuine differentiator versus narrower competitors, and independence from any single OEM is a structural advantage as airlines seek to diversify their supply chains.

The exclusive distribution agreements (Unison, Chromalloy, Ontic, FTAI, Triumph) and the new Triumph actuation and Otto Instrument wins extend the moat in new-parts distribution. The board's defense and aviation pedigree (former DoD acquisition chief Ellen Lord, Gen. Pace, Gen. McNabb, former acting FAA Administrator Billy Nolen) is a tangible asset for the government segment. Competitive position scores 7.0: improving share in the franchise segment, widening breadth, and structural tailwinds, balanced against the reality that the aftermarket is competitive and well-capitalized peers (HEICO in particular) are formidable.

## Risk Assessment

**FCPA / compliance overhang.** Self-reported violations in Nepal and South Africa were resolved in December 2024 via a DoJ Non-Prosecution Agreement (18-month term running into roughly mid-2026) and an SEC Cease-and-Desist. An NPA breach could reopen prosecution. In a related Nepal criminal proceeding, CEO John Holmes was assigned AAR International's sentence — a ~$0.9M fine and a 1.5-year prison term under Nepalese law as then-president — which the company refuses to participate in on due-process grounds. This is an idiosyncratic governance and headline risk a standard data pull would miss.

**Government budget exposure.** Roughly 25% of sales go to the U.S. government. The 10-K flags proposed State Department budget cuts pressuring the WASS contract, a Section 232 trade investigation into aircraft/engine parts imports, and general continuing-resolution risk.

**Acquisition integration and goodwill.** Three FY26 acquisitions on top of FY24 Product Support and FY23 Trax leave ~$841M of goodwill and intangibles. Integration missteps or impairment would hit the stock hard.

**Inventory valuation** is KPMG's flagged Critical Audit Matter. The $958M inventory balance rests on subjective write-down assumptions tied to fleet-usage trends.

**Uncapped A220 performance guarantee.** A lawsuit seeking at least $32M with no financial cap; loss is "reasonably possible" but not estimable. A low-probability, high-uncertainty tail.

**Cyclicality and concentration.** 71% commercial exposure is sensitive to air-travel cycles, fuel, and recession; one regional airline customer was in payment default (FY25 AR $29.4M, $7.5M past due). Management explicitly flagged the Middle East conflict as a watch item in Q3 FY26 — a sustained oil shock would pressure airline customers via the fuel-cost channel.

The cluster of FCPA NPA timing, integration load, the uncapped A220 claim, and cyclical/government concentration are specific risks not fully priced at a record multiple. Risk factors score 5.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 54.7% is rich relative to 1-year historical volatility of 41.2% (a 13.5pp spread), sits at the 82nd percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (54.7% near-term versus 45.9% long-term), which typically signals an imminent event being priced — most likely the next earnings report and the resolution of the FCPA NPA window. Positioning itself is constructive: the put/call skew is call-heavy (-4.4%), the put/call volume ratio is a low 0.38, and the put/call OI ratio fell from 0.73 to 0.46 over the past month as the stock rose 19%. The implied ±10.8% move into the next expiry, combined with rich IV, argues against buying the stock here ahead of a high-vol event without a margin of safety.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$150).** The aging-fleet tailwind and OEM production delays sustain double-digit aftermarket demand for years. Parts Supply distribution and Trax continue to drive mix-shift margin expansion toward and beyond 11%. The three FY26 acquisitions integrate cleanly and prove accretive, the working-capital drag normalizes so growth converts to free cash flow, and the FCPA NPA lapses cleanly in mid-2026 removing a headline overhang. Estimates continue rising, the beat streak extends, and the stock re-rates toward the high end of the analyst range on $5.65+ next-year EPS.

**Bear Case (~$105–110, roughly 17–20% downside).** Working-capital intensity reasserts itself and cash flow disappoints, undermining the deleveraging narrative just as the stock prices in perfection at a record multiple. A State Department budget cut pressures WASS, an integration stumble or goodwill impairment surfaces, or the A220 / Nepal situations escalate into quantifiable liabilities. With no dividend support and IV signaling an imminent catalyst, any disappointment against a strong twelve-month run produces a sharp multiple compression back toward the low-$100s.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a growth-oriented HOLD. The thesis is sound and the business merits ownership, but the entry point at a record price near the top of the analyst range does not offer the asymmetry required for a fresh BUY.

- **Reassessment timeline:** The next quarterly print (FY26 Q4, typically reported in July) and the FY2026 10-K, which will confirm whether cash-flow conversion is durable and clarify the FCPA NPA status and acquisition purchase accounting.

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
  1. A pullback toward the high-$110s or lower on no fundamental deterioration, restoring a 15%+ gap to a $141 base-case target.
  2. Confirmation that the FY26 operating cash-flow improvement persists (full-year OCF materially exceeding FY25's $36.1M and converting >60% of net income), validating that growth is self-funding.
  3. Clean lapse of the FCPA Non-Prosecution Agreement window without breach, removing the compliance headline overhang.

- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
  1. Organic revenue growth decelerating below mid-single digits alongside a cut to FY27 guidance.
  2. A goodwill impairment charge against the FY26 acquisition stack, or evidence of integration failure at HAECO/ADI.
  3. A quantified adverse outcome on the A220 performance guarantee, or an NPA breach reopening FCPA prosecution.

## Conclusion

AAR has executed a genuine recovery and balance-sheet repair: adjusted earnings power that the FY25 GAAP optics obscured is now visible, net leverage is back inside target, organic growth is mid-teens, and the aging-fleet aftermarket tailwind is durable. The score has improved from 5.5 in March to 6.2 today, reflecting the de-risked balance sheet and confirmed operating momentum, and the dimension trajectory is now correctly weighted toward the strong growth profile (7.5) and improving competitive position (7.0) rather than the misleading trailing GAAP picture.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is price. The stock trades at ~27x forward EPS near the top of a $125–$150 analyst range, carries no dividend cushion, and the options market is pricing an imminent high-volatility catalyst. The fundamental improvement is real, but the market has captured most of it. We would add on weakness toward the high-$110s or on confirmation of durable cash conversion, and we would revisit the call at the FY26 fourth-quarter report.
