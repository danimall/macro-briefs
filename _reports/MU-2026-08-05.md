## Executive Summary

Micron is nine months into the largest earnings inflection in the history of the memory industry. Nine-month FY2026 revenue of $78,959M produced $47,268M of net income and $41.40 of diluted EPS, against $26,063M of revenue and $5,338M of net income in the comparable prior period. Q3 FY2026 alone delivered $41,456M of revenue at an 84.6% GAAP gross margin. Trailing-twelve-month net income is approximately $50,469M, putting the stock at roughly 20.6x trailing earnings and 11.5x TTM revenue — multiples that look neither cheap nor expensive until you decide which margin regime is durable.

The decision-relevant arithmetic is forward. Consensus current-year EPS of $73.43 puts the stock at 12.5x; next-year consensus of $155.56 puts it at 5.9x. Those estimates are not stale — they have been revised up 26.7% and 54.1% respectively over ninety days, with the current quarter up 40.7%. The company has beaten by 17.3%, 27.3%, 17.4% and 2.8% over the last four quarters. Separately, SK hynix's CEO has publicly forecast 2027 as the industry's worst-ever supply shortage, and trade reporting indicates 2027 DRAM and HBM allocation negotiations across all three suppliers are largely complete. Against a stock that has retraced 26.6% from its June 25 high of $1,255, this is the specific reason to think the market is mispricing the next four to six quarters: it is discounting a 2028–2030 supply response into a 2027 earnings stream that is already contractually allocated.

The bear case is equally specific and deserves weight. Roughly all incremental gross profit is price, not volume: nine-month DRAM bit shipments rose ~30% while DRAM ASPs rose ~140%; NAND bits rose in the low-20s against ASPs up ~130%. Mobile & Client — the segment with no HBM in it — carries the highest operating margin of the four business units at 85.7%, up from 16.7% in FY2025. The company's own 10-K discloses a five-year DRAM ASP band running from plus low-40% to minus high-40%. Micron's audited GAAP remaining performance obligations are approximately $5 billion, against a management-cited $100 billion cumulative minimum revenue commitment across 14 of its 16 Strategic Customer Agreements — a 20x scope gap that means the "contracted revenue" framing is not filing-verified. Add a majority of 2025 DRAM output produced in Taiwan, capital return of $1.1B against $26.1B of nine-month free cash flow, and a $250B U.S. investment program through 2035, and the reinvestment risk borne by shareholders is substantial. We rate MU a BUY with disciplined sizing, base case $1,150.

## Company Overview

Micron is one of three scaled global DRAM producers alongside Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, and competes in NAND against Samsung, SK hynix, Kioxia and Sandisk. Its FY2025 10-K explicitly names ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) and Yangtze Memory Technologies (YMTC) as competitors and identifies Chinese state-supported capacity as an oversupply risk.

Q3 FY2026 revenue by technology was DRAM $31,328M (75.6% of total), NAND $9,943M, other $185M. Segment operating margins in Q3 FY2026 versus FY2025:

| Segment | Q3 FY26 revenue ($M) | Q3 FY26 op. margin | FY2025 op. margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cloud Memory | 13,769 | 78.4% | 45.3% |
| Core Data Center | 11,524 | 82.6% | 30.2% |
| Mobile & Client | 11,521 | 85.7% | 16.7% |
| Automotive & Embedded | 4,634 | 75.4% | 11.7% |

Manufacturing spans the U.S., Singapore, Taiwan, Japan, Malaysia, China and India, with a majority of 2025 DRAM output from Taiwan fabs. The capacity program is unusually large: two Boise fabs (first DRAM output targeted mid-CY2027, second late CY2028), a four-fab Clay, New York campus (first concrete July 2026, supply 2030+), Singapore HBM advanced packaging from 1H CY2027 plus a Singapore wafer fab in 2H CY2028, Hiroshima EUV modernization, an India assembly/test facility now shipping, and the Tongluo, Taiwan fab acquired from Powerchip for $1.8B cash in March 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** FY2023 revenue of $15,540M produced a $5,833M net loss at a −9.1% gross margin. FY2025 delivered $37,378M and $8,539M. Nine-month FY2026 delivered $78,959M and $47,268M at a 76.6% gross margin. Q3 FY2026 cost of goods sold was $6,400M against $41,456M of revenue; R&D of $1,316M plus SG&A of $407M equals 4.2% of revenue. That is the operating structure of a supply-constrained producer selling well above cash cost.

**Balance sheet.** At May 28, 2026: total assets $134,112M, current assets $66,737M against current liabilities $19,488M (3.4x coverage), total liabilities $33,388M, stockholders' equity $100,724M, retained earnings $94,682M. Cash and investments total $30,128M against $5,722M of debt carrying value — of which $2,670M is finance leases — leaving net cash of approximately $24,406M. Debt has fallen from $14.58B at August 28, 2025 after $8,511M of principal prepayment at a cash cost of $8,985M, recognizing $500M of prepayment losses. The revolver was voluntarily reduced from $3.50B to $2.00B on May 6, 2026, against a ≤3.25x net leverage covenant that is not remotely close to binding.

Bankruptcy risk is not a live question. A recomputed Altman Z on the May 2026 balance sheet is approximately 22.6, but 82% of that comes from the market-equity-to-liabilities term, which is circular with the share price. Stripping that term entirely still leaves 4.02, above the 2.99 safe threshold. Note also that any screening output showing MU at a −0.2% operating margin is mis-tagged: FY2025 operating income was $9,770M on $37,378M of revenue, or 26.1%.

**Cash flow, with two quality caveats.** Nine-month operating cash flow was $45,702M against capex of $19,602M, giving $26,100M of simple free cash flow, or $29,089M including $2,989M of government incentive receipts. Two adjustments matter:

1. Approximately $5,203M of that operating cash flow came from an increase in other non-current liabilities, and the ending balance of $7,086M includes $5,790M of non-current income taxes payable (versus $1,443M total at August 2025). Cash taxes appear to be lagging the book provision materially. An ordinary-timing explanation involving estimated payments and Pillar Two transition mechanics is plausible; no cash-taxes-paid disclosure was located to size the split. Either way, headline operating cash flow overstates sustainable cash earnings.
2. Other current liabilities include $3,320M of consideration payable to customers, including pricing adjustments and returns, up from $1,190M. That is a real future outflow or revenue offset embedded in the current margin.

Receivables are not a collection problem. Trade receivables rose to $26,894M from $7,163M, but days sales outstanding moved only from 57.6 to 59.0 days — receivable growth is tracking price and revenue, not deterioration.

**The depreciation cliff is the structural issue.** FY2026 net capex is guided to approximately $27B against nine-month D&A of $6,862M (~$9.1B annualized). Gross PP&E is already $124,991M with $68,565M accumulated depreciation, plus $10,935M of construction in progress and $4,200M of equipment not yet in service. The fixed-cost floor rises with a two-to-three-year lag, and on a downcycle revenue base that becomes punishing.

**Financial health: 8.0.** Net cash, 3.4x current coverage, a halved debt load, and $26B of nine-month free cash flow are unambiguous. The score is held below 9 by the tax-deferral component of cash flow, the customer-consideration accrual, and a capital program that converts financial strength into operating leverage over the next four years.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 tracks to roughly $129B of revenue on management's Q4 guide of $50B ± $1B, versus $37,378M in FY2025. Consensus estimates have moved as follows over ninety days: current quarter $22.26 → $31.33 (+40.7%), next quarter $24.92 → $34.87 (+39.9%), current year $57.97 → $73.43 (+26.7%), next year $100.94 → $155.56 (+54.1%). Thirty-day revisions remain positive across all four periods. Revisions of this magnitude, combined with four consecutive beats and revenue growth far above 40% with expanding margins, is the strongest forward configuration available in this dataset, and it is not offset by a premium multiple — the stock trades at 12.5x current-year and 5.9x next-year consensus.

Two qualifications. First, the growth is price. Nine-month DRAM bits rose ~30% against ASPs up ~140%; Q3 DRAM ASP was up "low 260%" year over year on bits up low-20s. Price-driven growth mean-reverts in a way volume-driven growth does not. Second, the highest-margin segment is Mobile & Client, which contains no HBM. The clean reading is that HBM consumes disproportionate wafer capacity — the 10-K states HBM requires more wafers and cleanroom space per bit than conventional DRAM at the same node — so AI demand is the upstream cause of commodity tightness even though the profit appears in the phone and PC segment. That makes AI capex the variable to monitor, but it also means the profit pool sits in products with no design-in lock.

The durability bridge is the Strategic Customer Agreement program. The 10-Q corroborates the structure as a filing fact: take-or-pay with binding volumes, multi-year terms, mostly fixed pricing or minimum/maximum bands, with the largest agreements carrying a ceiling approximating Q2 CY2026 market price and a floor through term, and with the company stating that gross margins even at floor pricing would be "well above our peak quarterly margins in any past cycle." Named counterparties include Anthropic (June 22, buyer of memory and storage, with Micron separately taking a minority equity stake), General Motors (July 1), Ford (July 6), and automotive Tier 1s (July 16). Reports naming Nvidia as an SCA counterparty are not corroborated by any Micron filing or release and should not be relied on.

**Growth prospects: 8.0.** The revision velocity and the reported sold-out 2027 book support a high score. It is not higher because the growth base is ASP-driven and the SCA volume commitments are not yet visible in GAAP disclosures.

## Valuation Assessment

At $921.06:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Market capitalization | ~$1,040B |
| Enterprise value | ~$1,016B |
| TTM net income | $50,469M |
| TTM P/E | 20.6x |
| EV / TTM revenue | 11.3x |
| Current-year consensus EPS $73.43 | 12.5x |
| Next-year consensus EPS $155.56 | 5.9x |
| Dividend yield ($0.60 annualized) | 0.065% |

Note a data conflict: one profile field reports EPS of $7.59 and a 20.14x P/E, which cannot be reconciled with the filings. Filing-derived TTM diluted EPS is approximately $44.08 on 1,145M diluted shares, giving ~20.9x — consistent with the market-cap-to-net-income calculation. We use the filing basis.

Peer medians in semiconductors sit at 64.2x P/E (TSM 36.5x, AMD 162.9x, TXN 50.8x, AVGO 64.2x, INTC negative). MU at 20.6x trailing and 12.5x current-year is not comparable on a like-for-like basis, because peer multiples capitalize recurring earnings while MU's capitalize a price spike. The relevant question is what earnings level survives.

The honest bracket: annualizing Q3 FY2026 net income of $28,243M gives $112.97B and 9.2x — a number that should not be used, because it embeds record spot pricing, favorable working-capital and tax timing, a depreciation base that has not absorbed $27B of FY2026 capex, and $323M of one-time prepayment losses. At the other end, applying FY2022's prior-peak 28.2% net margin to TTM revenue of $90.3B gives $25.5B of net income, roughly $22.3 per diluted share, or 41x. MU trades between approximately 9x and 41x depending on the durable margin regime, and that bracket is the investment debate.

Our base case sits between. FY2027 consensus of $155.56 embeds a further near-doubling from the current-year $73.43. Haircutting that by ~16% to $130 — reflecting partial ASP normalization in H2 CY2027 as the first tranche of new cleanroom capacity lands, and full absorption of the stepped-up depreciation base — and applying 8.8x gives **$1,144, rounded to $1,150**. An 8.8x multiple on down-cycle-adjacent earnings is not aggressive; it is roughly one-seventh of the semiconductor peer median and reflects the terminal-value discount memory has always carried.

Three points on why the market may be wrong here rather than right. First, the estimate revisions are not a forecast of what could happen — 90-day upward revisions of 26.7% and 54.1% on 36–37 covering analysts reflect information already delivered in three consecutive prints. Second, the multiple is compressed relative to the improving estimates, which is the configuration in which revision momentum most reliably precedes further re-rating; the stock is not at a premium multiple that already prices continued beats. Third, the 2027 allocation appears committed: trade reporting indicates 2027 DRAM and HBM capacity is largely sold out across all three suppliers, and SK hynix's CEO has forecast 2027 as the worst supply shortage on record. The supply response — South Korea targeting a doubling of memory capacity with just over $2 trillion of Samsung/SK hynix pledges, CXMT's $8.6B IPO, Micron's own $250B program — lands in 2028–2030, outside a twelve-month horizon.

The counterweight is that the audited contracted floor is thin. GAAP remaining performance obligations were approximately $5 billion at May 28, 2026, measured on minimum committed volumes at minimum pricing, explicitly excluding market-priced agreements and contracts under one year, with roughly one-third recognizable within twelve months. The management-cited $100 billion of cumulative minimum revenue commitment across 14 of 16 SCAs is a company-defined contract-value metric sourced to a single secondary summary of the June 24 call, not a filing. Both figures can be accurate and differ 20x on scope, but the practical consequence is that the enforceable minimum revenue stream in the filings is $5B against a $90B TTM base. The next 10-K's RPO figure is the single most important check on the durability premium.

**Valuation: 7.3.** A specific, dated mispricing case exists — 5.9x next-year consensus with estimates rising 54% in ninety days and 2027 allocation reportedly closed. The score is not higher because a large share of the apparent cheapness rests on FY2027 estimates that embed a further doubling, and because the contracted floor is only $5B on an audited basis.

## Competitive Landscape

Structurally, this is a three-firm DRAM oligopoly with Chinese entrants closing. Micron's advantage is not a technology moat but capacity scarcity: multi-year lead times on cleanroom space, HBM4 12-Hi qualification (reported but not filing-confirmed), and a book of long-term take-or-pay agreements that management expects to cover half or more of total revenue once complete. Mehrotra has been reported as saying Micron can fill only 50%–67% of key customers' demand.

The trajectory is mixed rather than uniformly improving. On the positive side, Micron is converting scarcity into contract structure that peers have not visibly matched at the same scale, and the addition of $22B of expected customer deposits and related commitments ($18B cash) would be a non-dilutive funding source and a commitment device — though contract liabilities at May 28 were only $422M, so the great majority of that was still prospective at the balance-sheet date. On the negative side, CXMT's gap to Samsung and SK hynix has narrowed to under three years per CNBC reporting, and the industry's aggregate capital response is historic in scale. The 10-K names the transmission mechanism for how an AI pause becomes a glut: if HBM demand weakens and suppliers shift capacity to conventional DRAM, the bit release exceeds the HBM demand reduction because HBM consumes more wafers per bit.

The market has begun pricing that. Samsung reported preliminary Q2 CY2026 operating profit of roughly $58–59B on ~$113B of sales, a ~19-fold year-over-year increase that beat estimates, and its shares fell 7–10%. SK hynix fell approximately 15% on July 13 after guiding profit ~8% below consensus. CXMT's IPO announcement drove MU down 5%. Price action of that shape indicates a market that has stopped paying for current earnings and started discounting the supply response.

**Competitive position: 6.5.** Scarcity plus contract structure is a genuine improvement over the FY2023 configuration. Taiwan concentration, CXMT's closing gap, and the industry-wide capital build cap the score.

## Risk Assessment

**Price reversion is the dominant risk.** Because incremental profit is ASP-driven, mean reversion hits earnings directly. Scenario arithmetic on Q3 FY2026 economics: a 50% ASP decline at flat volumes takes quarterly revenue from $41.5B to roughly $20.7B against a cash COGS base near $6.4B, still leaving gross margin near 65%; a 70% decline takes revenue to ~$12.4B and, with a stepped-up depreciation base, gross margin toward the mid-30s. The downside is a large earnings contraction, not a solvency event.

**Taiwan concentration.** A majority of 2025 DRAM output came from Taiwan fabs, and DRAM is 75.6% of revenue at roughly 85% gross margin. The U.S. and Singapore capacity that diversifies this does not produce until mid-CY2027 through 2030. This tail is not addressable within the investment horizon.

**Injunction risk.** YMTC escalated on October 6, 2025 with complaints in the Eastern District of Texas (seven 3D NAND patents plus one LPDRAM patent), the London Chancery Division, three Unified Patent Court Düsseldorf complaints and five Munich Regional Court complaints — all seeking injunctions, pertaining to substantially all Micron DRAM and NAND products. A UPC or Munich injunction is pan-European and obtainable relatively quickly; it would forfeit sales at 85% gross margins. Separately, Netlist holds a $445M E.D. Texas verdict under appeal, with the asserted claims held unpatentable by the PTAB and the German counterparts declared invalid (affirmed on appeal May 21, 2026). Monetary exposure is modest against $50B of TTM earnings; injunction exposure is not.

**New risk language in the Q3 10-Q.** Micron added disclosure that constrained supply, insufficient allocations or elevated pricing "may strain long-term customer relationships... and, where such products are viewed as critical inputs to certain industries, lead to legal or other disputes or government and regulatory focus," and that persistence "could limit or severely restrict our ability to sell our product into certain end markets in the future." Related new factors cover customers redesigning products to reduce memory content, SCAs constraining Micron's own supply flexibility, and customers failing to meet purchase commitments.

**Policy and permitting.** The Section 232 semiconductor investigation initiated April 2025 is unresolved, and new Section 301 investigations were announced February 20, 2026 with scope undisclosed. Roughly 80% of revenue ships outside the U.S. CHIPS incentives (up to ~$6.4B in grants plus a 35% ITC and a non-binding New York term sheet of up to $5.5B) carry milestone clawback provisions covering "up to all of certain incentives." On January 16, 2026 two organizations petitioned the New York Supreme Court to annul the environmental-review approvals for the Clay, New York campus; Micron poured first concrete on July 9 with that petition pending.

**Customer concentration worsened in dollars.** One customer was 10% of nine-month FY2026 revenue versus 16% a year earlier — but on a revenue base that tripled, that is roughly $7.9B versus $4.2B. The falling percentage establishes only that total revenue outgrew that account.

**Capital return.** Nine-month free cash flow of $26.1B produced $650M of buybacks (none in Q3) and $437M of dividends. Only $2.16B remains of the 2019 $10B authorization, and repurchases are restricted under the CHIPS direct funding agreements. A "Waiver and Amendment No. 3 to Direct Funding Agreement" dated February 27, 2026 is filed as a 10-Q exhibit; its contents are unread, and whether it touches repurchase limits is unknown. Shareholders currently bear reinvestment risk into an industry-wide capacity build.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 121.8% against 80.0% one-year realized is rich, at the 92nd percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (121.8% at one week versus 83.9% at six months). Read carefully: the 121.8% and the 27.6% put skew both sit on a one-day expiry, where the implied move is only ±2.3% and the unusual-activity table is dominated by same-day $900–$920 strikes on both sides — same-day pinning, not directional positioning. The longer tenors tell a different story: skew is +0.8% at one month and turns negative (call-tilted) at −1.7% for three months and −4.3% for six months, and put/call open interest fell from 1.17 to 0.87 since July 21. ATM IV rose 9.6 points on a 0.5% price move, so the market is pricing more uncertainty without a directional tilt. The six-month implied move of ±37.1% brackets roughly $580 to $1,262, which is close to our own bear-to-bull range. Options positioning corroborates rather than contradicts the fundamental view, and the elevated vol argues for scaling entry rather than a single fill.

Short interest is 3.2% of float with 0.7 days to cover — the bear case is expressed in the multiple, not in borrow.

**Risk factors: 4.5.** Multiple live binaries (UPC/Munich injunctions, Section 232/301, New York permitting), single-geography production concentration, restricted capital return, and an earnings base that is entirely price-dependent. These are not sector truisms; they are company-specific and dated.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case — $1,600 (+73.7%)

FY2027 consensus of $155.56 is broadly delivered because 2027 capacity is allocated and SCA floors hold above prior-peak margins. The next 10-K shows GAAP RPO stepping up materially from $5B as the GM, Ford and Tier 1 agreements concluded after the May balance-sheet date enter the disclosure, validating the durability premium. The $18B of expected cash deposits arrives and funds the capex program without dilution. HBM4 ramps for next-generation accelerator platforms. The February 2026 CHIPS waiver relaxes repurchase restrictions and Micron begins returning peak-cycle cash at scale. At 10.3x $155.56 the stock is $1,600; the analyst high of $2,200 and mean of $1,507.79 imply the sell side sits above our bull case.

### Bear case — $500 (−45.7%)

The 2028–2030 supply response arrives earlier and larger than allocation data implies. HBM demand pauses, capacity reverts to conventional DRAM, and the wafer-per-bit penalty releases disproportionate bits. Customers redesign to reduce memory content — a risk Micron itself now discloses. ASPs revert toward the middle of the disclosed five-year band while D&A steps toward the mid-teens billions annually on $27B of FY2026 capex plus $10.9B of construction in progress. Applying FY2022's 28.2% peak-cycle net margin to $90B of revenue gives $25.5B, roughly $22.3 per share; at 22x that is $490. A UPC or Munich injunction, or a Taiwan disruption, delivers the same outcome faster. Within a twelve-month horizon the more probable downside is multiple compression on still-elevated earnings toward $650–$700 rather than the full $500 reversion, which requires two full quarters of ASP decline.

### Where we come out

Weighting base at ~45%, bull at ~25% and bear at ~30% gives a positive expected return of roughly 16% over twelve months. That is adequate for a position, not for a maximum-conviction one. The configuration that tips it to BUY is the combination of 5.9x next-year consensus, +54% ninety-day revisions on 37 analysts, four consecutive beats, $24.4B net cash, and reported 2027 allocation closure. The configuration that caps position size is the $5B audited RPO, Taiwan concentration, and the injunction docket.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. **FY2026 10-K remaining performance obligations below approximately $15B.** If the audited enforceable minimum does not step up substantially from $5B once the GM, Ford and Tier 1 agreements are inside the measurement date, the durability premium is unsupported and the correct frame reverts to normalized-margin valuation.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory on this name has been volatile — 7.3 in late May, down to 5.3 in June, back to 6.8 in July — and the volatility has tracked the share price more than the fundamentals. Since the July 21 report the price has fallen 5.2% from $970.82 while thirty-day estimate revisions have remained positive across all four forecast periods (+29.6% current quarter, +4.0% next year). That combination improves risk/reward rather than degrading the thesis, and we maintain BUY at 6.83 overall.

Two dimension divergences are worth stating plainly. Financial health has improved materially and durably: debt down from $14.58B to $5.72B, equity nearly doubled to $100.7B, net cash of $24.4B, and no covenant that is remotely close to binding. Management quality has not: capital return of $1.1B against $26.1B of nine-month free cash flow, a combined chair/CEO, and an insider record that contains no open-market purchases and includes the CEO disposing of roughly a third of his reported position across four transactions in twelve weeks. Diversification after an eightfold move, RSU replenishment, and probable 10b5-1 scheduling are all plausible explanations, and the grants made in FY2026 at a $227.18 weighted-average fair value leave large remaining economic exposure — but the pattern is one-directional and it is not offset by the CHIPS-restricted buyback. Against that, the $8.5B of debt prepayment and the voluntary revolver reduction are credible counter-cyclical de-risking ahead of a $250B capital program, and the compensation plan's explicit HBM3E+ share and bit-shipment metrics paid for exactly the capability that produced FY2026.

The investable proposition is narrower than the headline numbers suggest: a stock at 5.9x next-year consensus, with rising estimates, net cash, and reportedly allocated 2027 capacity, against an earnings base that is entirely price-dependent and a contracted floor that is $5B on an audited basis rather than $100B. That is a favorable asymmetry over twelve months, held at moderate size, with the September 10-K as the first hard test.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the Executive Summary's TTM EV/revenue was overstated (11.3x → 11.5x) and the price-retracement figure recalculated (26.6% from the $1,255 high at $921.06 is correct); the base-case return math (8.8x × $130 = $1,144 ≈ $1,150, +24.9%) and bull/bear percentages check out against the $921.06 basis, but the bull-case arithmetic (10.3x × $155.56 = $1,602) and the bear-case arithmetic (22x × $22.3 = $491, not $500) are approximate — the stated targets and the recommendation are unaffected in direction.
