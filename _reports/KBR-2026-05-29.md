**Research Date: May 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

KBR is a mid-cap services and technology hybrid that combines a large U.S./allied government-services business (Mission Technology Solutions, ~72% of revenue) with a smaller, higher-margin proprietary energy-technology and advisory business (Sustainable Technology Solutions, ~28%). The stock has fallen roughly 33% over the past twelve months, from a 52-week high of $54.38 to $34.44, driven by a Q3 2025 revenue guidance cut on award delays and contract protests, the unexpected termination of the HomeSafe household-goods contract by U.S. TRANSCOM, and broad de-rating of the government-services group. At ~8.7x current-year consensus EPS ($3.96), KBR is priced for continued earnings deceleration.

The investment case rests on a special-situations setup: a planned tax-free spin-off of MTS targeted for January 4, 2027; an activist (Engine Capital, ~2% stake) publicly urging the board to explore an outright sale; and a cluster of open-market insider purchases in May 2026 by the new CFO and three directors at prices below the current quote. These are credible value signals. Against them sit negative earnings momentum (current-year EPS revised down 2.5% over 90 days, current-quarter down 8.3%), demonstrated single-customer cancellation risk, elevated leverage (D/E ~3.4x, Altman Z 2.14 in the grey zone), and a January 2026 executive order that allows the Secretary of War to seek restrictions on KBR's dividends and buybacks if it is deemed to underperform on government contracts, an unusual company-specific governance overhang.

We rate KBR HOLD. The sum-of-the-parts thesis is genuine and the insider buying is the most credible positive tell in the data, but the catalyst is more than seven months out, estimates are falling, and the near-term fundamentals (revenue -5% in Q1, MTS -6%) are deteriorating in tandem with the price. The honest call is to wait for the catalyst path to firm and for book-to-bill to recover before adding conviction.

## Company Overview

KBR delivers science, technology, engineering, and logistics services to governments and commercial clients across roughly 28 countries with about 36,000 employees. The fiscal year ends the Friday nearest December 31; the company labels the year ended January 2, 2026 as "fiscal 2025."

**Mission Technology Solutions (MTS)** generated ~$5.58B of FY2025 revenue. It provides defense, intelligence, space, aviation, and C5ISR/cyber/data-analytics support to the U.S. Department of Defense (renamed "Department of War" in recent filings), NASA, intelligence agencies, the U.K. MoD, and the Australian DoD. The work is predominantly cost-reimbursable and time-and-materials, supplemented by long-dated U.K. PFI contracts (Aspire Defence runs through 2041). The 2024 LinQuest acquisition added defense data and digital-integration capability.

**Sustainable Technology Solutions (STS)** generated ~$2.21B. Its differentiation rests on 85-plus proprietary process technologies spanning ammonia/syngas, petrochemicals, clean refining, and circular economy, including K-GreeN (green ammonia), Hydro-PRT (plastics-to-feedstock), and PureSAF (sustainable aviation fuel). STS earnings are increasingly driven by equity income from LNG joint ventures (JKC/Ichthys, KZJV/Plaquemines), which is high-margin but lumpy.

U.S. government revenue is 57% of the total and U.K. government 9%, creating meaningful customer concentration. KBR competes with larger primes (Leidos, Booz Allen, SAIC, Jacobs, Amentum) and specialized engineering firms.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** $6.56B (FY2022) → $6.96B (FY2023) → $7.71B (FY2024) → $7.79B (FY2025, +1%). Growth decelerated sharply in FY2025 as LinQuest-driven defense gains were offset by reduced European Command contingency work and declines in science/space programs. Q1 FY2026 revenue fell 5% YoY to $1.92B, with MTS down 6% on continued runoff of contingency activity and government award delays.

**Profitability.** FY2025 operating income rose 18% to $778M (operating margin ~10%) despite flat revenue, helped substantially by a near-doubling of equity in earnings of unconsolidated affiliates to $210M (LNG project). Net income to KBR was $415M. Q1 FY2026 operating income fell 11% to $180M, with EPS of $0.80, and benefited from a +$52M favorable LNG-JV change in estimate. The earnings base has a growing dependence on lumpy JV equity income.

**Cash flow.** A standard screen flags negative free cash flow, but that figure conflates discontinued operations. Continuing-operations operating cash flow was $557M in FY2025 (up from $450M) and $110M in Q1 FY2026, against modest capex of ~$42M. The business is genuinely asset-light with solid cash conversion; the headline "cash burn" reading is an artifact of the HomeSafe discontinued-operations accounting.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Debt of ~$2.6B comprises Term Loan A ($989M), Term Loan B ($983M), $250M 4.75% senior notes due 2028, and a $395M revolver draw. D/E is ~3.4x and the Altman Z-score is 2.14 (grey zone), elevated but typical for an asset-light services roll-up carrying $2.68B of acquisition goodwill. The net-leverage covenant ceiling is 4.0x and KBR is compliant. Weighted-average interest rate is ~4.94%, partly hedged with swaps. A frozen U.K. DB pension sits in a ~$95M surplus (no near-term funding required) but carries $932M of unrecognized actuarial losses.

**Capital allocation.** The quarterly dividend is $0.165 (maintained), and $323M of buybacks were completed in FY2025. Buybacks were essentially paused in Q1 FY2026 (only withhold-to-cover), with $427M remaining authorized. Cash was redirected to a $115M BRIS JV investment and $49M of Mura plastics-recycling securities, consistent with preserving balance-sheet flexibility ahead of the spin financing.

**Earnings track record.** KBR has beaten EPS estimates for four consecutive quarters (4.6%, 3.3%, 5.7%, 1.9% surprises). The beats are consistent but modest and increasingly reliant on JV equity income, so they should not be read as evidence of a fundamental acceleration.

## Growth Analysis

Top-line growth has stalled, and MTS, the larger segment, is contracting as European contingency activity runs off and government award timing slips. Estimate revisions confirm the softening: current-quarter EPS has been cut 8.3% over 90 days and the current-year estimate 2.5%, with the next-year estimate down 3.7%. This is broad negative earnings momentum.

The medium-term demand backdrop is favorable. FY2026 defense appropriations are $839B, the proposed FY2027 defense budget is ~$1.5T (about 44% higher), and NATO's 5%-of-GDP-by-2035 target plus rising U.K. and Australian spending support multi-year program demand. The DoW's emphasis on capability integrators independent of OEMs plays to KBR's positioning, and recent wins (a $510M data/analytics IDIQ, a $449M Army LOGCAP Europe extension, a $200M aviation-safety recompete) support book-to-bill into the separation. STS has a higher-margin, IP-led runway tied to LNG buildout, decarbonization, and circular-economy demand.

The tension is between a constructive multi-year backdrop and a deteriorating near-term reality. Procurement reform (the FAR overhaul under E.O. 14275), DOGE-era hiring freezes, the October–November 2025 shutdown, and award-timing delays are all suppressing near-term conversion of the favorable budget environment into revenue. Backlog of $16.9B plus $6.3B of options provides visibility, but the conversion timing is the binding constraint, and estimates are still moving the wrong way.

## Valuation Assessment

At $34.44 the stock trades at ~8.7x current-year consensus EPS and ~8.3x next-year EPS of $4.15. On the surface this is cheap. The peer set in Diversified Support Services is heterogeneous and not directly comparable: median P/E of 35.9 is distorted by UNF (35.9x), AMTM (38.6x), and a negative-earnings OPLN, while the closest government-services analog, SAIC, trades at 13.2x. Even against SAIC, KBR carries a discount, which the market attaches to MTS revenue contraction, leverage, the HomeSafe loss, and the regulatory overhang.

The valuation case is a sum-of-the-parts argument rather than a simple low-multiple call. The planned tax-free spin separates a pure-play defense/government-technology MTS from an STS that carries proprietary energy-tech IP and LNG-JV equity income. Both Engine Capital and the insiders buying near $30–32 believe the combined entity is undervalued. If the spin or a sale crystallizes the segment values, a re-rating toward the high analyst target ($60) is plausible. The mean analyst target of $46.57 implies ~35% upside from current levels, and the low target of $36 is roughly at the current price, framing a skewed-but-uncertain payoff.

We assign a 6.5 to valuation. The absolute multiple is low and the catalyst path provides a specific reason the market may be mispricing the parts, but falling estimates and the contingent nature of the catalysts cap the conviction. A low multiple on a contracting revenue base with falling estimates is not, by itself, sufficient for a high valuation score; the upside here depends on corporate action, not on organic earnings growth.

## Competitive Landscape

KBR's moat rests on security-cleared talent, long-tenured mission-critical programs, and a proprietary energy-technology IP portfolio. The MTS business benefits from incumbency on long-dated programs and the structural tailwind of rising allied defense budgets and the DoW's integrator-led procurement model. STS holds genuine differentiation in process technology and is positioned for the LNG and decarbonization buildout.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. The HomeSafe termination and recent contract protests demonstrate that incumbency does not guarantee retention, and MTS revenue contraction suggests share/scope pressure in contingency-related work. At the same time, recent IDIQ and recompete wins indicate the franchise remains competitive on core defense and aviation programs. Relative TSR underperformance (KBR 138 vs. peer group 213 in the comp disclosure) confirms the franchise has lagged peers over the measurement window. We score competitive position at 6.0: a credible mid-tier franchise with structural tailwinds, offset by demonstrated retention risk and recent underperformance.

## Risk Assessment

**Government concentration (57% of revenue).** KBR is exposed to shutdowns (the October 1–November 11, 2025 shutdown delayed awards, payments, and collections), continuing resolutions, the FAR overhaul, hiring freezes, and recompetes. Most MTS work is terminable for convenience.

**Contract-cancellation risk is demonstrated, not theoretical.** U.S. TRANSCOM terminated the HomeSafe Global Household Goods Contract (a 72%-owned JV) in June 2025; it is now discontinued operations with a $(55)M FY2025 net loss. This is a live illustration of single-customer cancellation risk.

**Novel capital-return regulatory risk.** The January 7, 2026 executive order is an unusual, company-specific overhang on the capital-return thesis.

**Spin-off execution risk.** The 10-K added a dedicated risk-factor section covering stranded costs, loss of scale economies, dependence on transition-services agreements, IT/ERP separation, tax-free qualification, and the financing/cash-distribution mechanics. The targeted January 4, 2027 date leaves a long runway for execution slippage.

**Earnings quality and leverage.** A growing share of profit comes from lumpy LNG-JV equity earnings. The grey-zone Z-score leaves limited cushion if award delays persist. Legacy contingencies include the FKTC arbitration (a Kuwait court ruled $41M against KBR in March 2026; KBR is appealing with no accrual) and ongoing LogCAP III closeout.

These risks are partly priced (the stock is down 33% and trades at ~8.7x), but the combination of demonstrated cancellation risk, the dividend/buyback executive order, and spin-execution risk justifies a below-average risk score of 4.5 because several are specific, asymmetric, and not fully reflected in a simple low-multiple framing.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 38.4% versus 1-year historical volatility of 31.8%, leaving options modestly rich (6.6pp spread) at a 31% IV percentile within the chain. The term structure is in backwardation (38.4% at 1m vs. 34.2% at 6m), indicating the market is pricing near-term event risk. Put/call skew is balanced near-term (2.1%) but steepens to 14.1% at the 6-month tenor, consistent with hedging demand around the longer-dated spin and activist outcomes rather than acute near-term bearishness. The put/call OI ratio collapsed from 3.99 to 0.03 since the prior snapshot, suggesting a large prior put position was closed or rolled. Short interest is moderate at 6.0% of float with 3.6 days to cover, neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup. The options picture is consistent with an event-driven situation rather than a directional consensus.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The sum-of-the-parts thesis crystallizes. The tax-free MTS spin separates a pure-play defense/government-technology company from an STS business with proprietary energy IP and LNG-JV equity income, and the market re-rates the parts above the combined ~8.7x multiple. Engine Capital's pressure for a sale introduces M&A optionality on top of the planned separation. Insider open-market buying near $30–32 (CFO Evans $256K, directors Sabater $471K, Moore $126K, Von Thaer $92K) signals that those with the best information see value below the current price. Award timing normalizes as the FY2027 defense budget ramps, book-to-bill recovers on recent IDIQ and recompete wins, and continuing-ops cash flow supports deleveraging into the spin. In this case the stock approaches the mean analyst target of $46.57 or the high target of $60 if a sale materializes.

### Bear Case

MTS revenue contraction persists, estimates continue to fall, and the spin slips or fails tax-free qualification. The dividend/buyback executive order is invoked or chills the capital-return thesis. A further HomeSafe-style termination or an adverse FKTC outcome pressures earnings. Leverage limits flexibility if cash flow weakens, and the LNG-JV equity income that propped up FY2025 operating income proves lumpy on the downside. In this case the multiple stays compressed and the stock drifts toward the low analyst target of $36 or below, back toward the 52-week low near $30.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** The classification is `uncertain`: the special-situations upside is real but contingent, and near-term fundamentals are deteriorating, leaving a genuinely two-sided payoff.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Book-to-bill recovers above 1.0x for two consecutive quarters and current-year EPS estimates stabilize or turn positive over a 90-day window.
- The MTS spin firms up with a confirmed record date and the company quantifies the cash distribution and post-spin leverage for RemainCo, or a credible sale process advances per Engine Capital's push.
- Additional clustered insider open-market buying, or resolution of the dividend/buyback executive-order overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second material contract termination or a guidance cut at FY2026 reporting, with MTS revenue contraction accelerating beyond the current -6% pace.
- The spin is delayed beyond FY2027 or loses tax-free qualification, or the Secretary of War invokes restrictions on KBR's capital returns.
- Net leverage approaches the 4.0x covenant ceiling on weakening cash flow.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 FY2026, ~August 2026) for book-to-bill and estimate trajectory; spin-related disclosures over the back half of 2026 ahead of the targeted completion.

## Conclusion

KBR offers a low headline multiple and a credible special-situations catalyst path: a planned tax-free MTS spin, an activist pressing for a sale, and a cluster of insider purchases near current prices. The continuing-operations cash flow is stronger than standard screens suggest, and the multi-year defense-budget backdrop is supportive. These factors keep the downside from the bear case more limited than a deteriorating-fundamentals story would imply.

The reasons not to commit capital yet are equally specific: revenue is contracting, estimates are falling across all horizons, the spin is more than seven months out with real execution and tax-qualification risk, and an unusual executive order could constrain capital returns. The HomeSafe termination shows the franchise's largest customer can and does walk away. The prior recommendation history (four consecutive BUYs against a declining score trajectory) sets a higher bar for continuing an unchanged bullish call, and the current data does not clear it. We move to HOLD and will reassess at Q2 earnings and as the spin path firms.
