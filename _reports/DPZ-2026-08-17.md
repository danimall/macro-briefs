## Executive Summary

Domino's remains the best-run asset in a consolidating category. It operates 22,531 stores in more than 90 markets, roughly 99% franchised, generating $4.94B of FY2025 revenue off $1.72B of assets. Operating margin expanded 101 basis points over three years to 19.31% in FY2025, H1 FY2026 operating income rose 6.3% to $462.4M with margin up 40bp to 19.7%, and the two highest-return profit pools — supply chain (+14.1% segment income in FY2025, +18.1% in Q2 FY2026) and international royalties (+10.7%) — are compounding well. Pizza Hut has just been sold out of Yum after ten consecutive quarters of U.S. same-store declines, Papa John's is closing locations, and Papa Murphy's is closing 68. On a five-year view, Domino's underperformance versus its restaurant peer index (cumulative TSR 118.24 vs. 137.50) is entirely multiple compression against ~5% annual Adjusted EBITDA compounding, not operating failure.

The domestic franchise engine — the largest profit pool and the sole driver of U.S. unit development — has stopped growing. U.S. stores segment income rose just 1.8% in FY2025 to $575.3M as segment G&A climbed 10.3%, and grew only 2.3% in Q2 FY2026. U.S. same-store sales decelerated from +3.4% to +0.1%, international turned to −0.1%, and management attributes the shortfall to average ticket, specifically a failed "Premium Series" marketing launch. The FY2026 U.S. net-store outlook was trimmed to ~175 citing franchisee profitability, guidance was lowered in April and only reaffirmed in July, and H1 actuals run below the guide on three of five metrics. Consensus has responded: current-year EPS down 1.2% over 90 days, next-year down 0.6%, with three EPS misses in the last four quarters (−3.4%, −8.1%, −1.4%).

The rating change to REDUCE is driven by risk/reward compression rather than fresh deterioration. Since the last review the stock has moved from roughly $319 to $346.27 — a 22% rally off the June 23 low of $283 — while estimates fell. At 18.2x FY2026 consensus of $19.02 and 16.6x FY2027 of $20.92, DPZ is no longer priced for disappointment, and FY2027 consensus embeds three headwinds we think are underweighted: reversal of the 53rd week (~$0.35-0.40 of EPS), the July 2027 refinancing of $1,319M at 4.178% into a ~5.0-5.2% market (~$0.26-0.42 after tax), and a royalty base growing off flat comps. Base case $375 (+8.3%), bear $270 (−22.0%), bull $450 (+30.0%). Probability-weighted total return including the 2.30% dividend is roughly 4-5% over twelve months, below the required return for a 4.5x-levered equity with a covenant structure that can suspend capital returns.

## Company Overview

The economic architecture matters more here than the headline revenue line. Q2 FY2026 revenue of $1,194.4M split as U.S. stores $380.9M (31.9% of revenue, 48.1% of segment profit), supply chain $731.7M (61.3% of revenue, 27.4% of profit), and international franchise $81.8M (6.8% of revenue, 24.5% of profit). Supply chain is a food-distribution business running roughly a 12% gross margin; international is near-pure royalty income at an approximately 3.0% average rate. Consolidated revenue therefore tells you very little about either consumer demand or profit direction.

U.S. franchisees pay a 5.5% royalty, a $0.375 per-digital-transaction technology fee, and a 6.0% national advertising fund contribution. They are **not** contractually required to purchase food from Domino's; the glue is a 50% supply-chain profit share recorded as a reduction of supply chain revenue, which totaled $193.0M in FY2025 (up from $164.0M and $138.7M in the two prior years). Grossing that back up implies a total supply chain profit pool near $513M of which Domino's retained about 62%, down from roughly 64% in 2023.

Refranchising has accelerated sharply: 1 store in FY2023, 2 in FY2024, 37 in FY2025 (Maryland), and 77 in Q2 FY2026 alone (Virginia, Michigan) for $19.8M. Company-owned count fell from 262 at Q1 FY2026 to 186, or 2.6% of the 7,231 U.S. system. This is strategically coherent (capital-light, higher-quality earnings) but it degrades disclosure quality: the company-owned store P&L is now the only store-level economics Domino's publishes, and it describes a shrinking, selectively composed base.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** FY2023-FY2025 revenue grew 5.07% then 4.96% to $4,940.0M; operating income grew to $954.0M (19.31% margin); diluted EPS reached $17.57 on a share count falling from 35.40M to 34.24M. FY2025 net income grew only 3.0% against 8.5% operating income growth, and the gap is identifiable and largely non-operating: the effective tax rate rose from 19.1% to 21.9% as excess equity-compensation tax benefits collapsed from $22.2M to $3.2M (options exercised fell from 270,424 to 77,415), and other income swung from +$22.1M to −$2.5M on mark-to-market of the residual DPC Dash stake. The tax normalization is probably permanent rather than temporary: $25.1M of foreign tax credits sit fully valuation-allowanced, and foreign pre-tax income is only $17.3M of $770.3M.

**Segment profit is where the thesis lives.** FY2025 segment income: U.S. stores $575.3M (+1.8%), supply chain $320.1M (+14.1%), international $288.5M (+10.7%). The largest pool is flat; growth is being carried by a distribution business that shares half its incremental profit back to franchisees and by royalties collected from third-party master franchisees.

**H1 FY2026.** Revenue $2,345.0M (+3.9%), operating income $462.4M (+6.3%), net income $275.6M (−1.8%), diluted EPS $8.21 (+0.9%). The net income decline reflects an $18.4M unrealized DPC Dash loss and lapping a prior-year $8.1M gain, offset by a $7.8M aircraft sale gain. Q2 U.S. company-owned store gross margin fell 4.2 points to 11.4% (food +0.8pt to 30.3% of sales, labor +1.0pt to 30.9%, higher insurance); supply chain gross margin improved to 12.0% on procurement productivity.

**Cash flow quality.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $792.1M (+26.8%) is not a clean run-rate. Working capital contributed +$52.8M versus −$44.0M in FY2024 (a $96.8M swing, dominated by a $53.6M payables/accrued inflow), and adding the DPC Dash reversal (+$24.6M), the excess-tax-benefit swing (+$19.1M) and net income (+$17.5M) accounts for $158.0M of the $167.2M increase. Normalizing working capital to zero puts FCF near $619M rather than the headline $671.5M, a 5.4% yield on the $11.46B market cap rather than 5.9%. Corroborating the normalization, H1 FY2026 OCF *fell* 3.9% to $352.6M while operating income rose 6.3%.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt of $4,883.6M at June 14, 2026 against $164.8M unrestricted cash and $187.9M restricted. Stockholders' equity is −$3,982.4M, the cumulative result of debt-financed recapitalizations and buybacks rather than operating losses; standard distress screens (Z-score) are uninformative here and should not be read either way. The meaningful metrics: gross debt/FY2025 EBITDA of $1,042.8M = 4.68x; net debt/EBITDA 4.53x; on the proxy's Incentive Adjusted EBITDA of $1,089.0M, 4.33x. Interest coverage 4.87x on operating income, 5.16x on cash interest paid.

The structure is a whole-business securitization with $4,766.1M of notes at a ~4.24% weighted-average stated coupon. Two features drive risk asymmetry. First, principal amortization is suspended only while the Holdco Leverage Ratio stays at or below 5.0x; at approximately 4.45x, EBITDA can fall roughly 11% before amortization resumes (definitional uncertainty applies, since indenture "Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA" may differ from the proxy measure). Second, failure to repay or refinance $1,319.0M by the July 2027 anticipated repayment date triggers at least 5% additional interest **and** directs all cash flows except a weekly management fee to debt repayment, which would halt the dividend and buyback.

**Capital allocation.** FY2024 through H1 FY2026 deployed $913.0M to retire 2,175,743 shares at an average of roughly $419.6, about 21% above today's $346.27. The monthly Q2 cadence declined as the price fell (234,960 shares at $365.80 → 139,870 at $349.58 → 74,335 at $310.90), which is consistent with steady-dollar or plan-driven mechanics rather than opportunism; we draw a conclusion about process design, not motive. The quarterly dividend was raised 14.4% to $1.99 ($7.96 annualized, 45% of FY2025 EPS, 2.30% yield). H1 FY2026 capital returned of $299.5M equalled 95% of H1 FCF of $313.6M, leaving no internally generated cushion at 4.3-4.7x leverage.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth picture rests on three separable engines with very different trajectories.

**U.S. same-store sales are the binding constraint.** Q2 U.S. SSS of +0.1% (H1 +0.5%) came from transaction growth offset by lower average ticket, a filing-confirmed statement, not just call commentary. Management's diagnosis is specific and testable: the "Premium Series" / Slice Sauce launch lapped the stronger Parmesan Stuffed Crust window and "the messaging wasn't compelling enough." Remediation is dated: Parmesan Stuffed Crust added to "Best Deal Ever" at the start of Q3, plus a new signature pizza later in Q3. If that works, U.S. segment income can reaccelerate without structural change; if it doesn't, the ticket problem becomes a category price-war problem.

**Unit growth is a derivative of franchisee returns.** The FY2026 U.S. net-store outlook was trimmed to ~175 with the CFO citing temporary pressure on franchisee profitability and a more cautious development pipeline. With 99% of the system franchised, Domino's cannot open stores its franchisees will not fund. International guidance of ~800 net openings depends on master franchisee health, and two publicly traded master franchisees are under scrutiny: Domino's Pizza Enterprises held a July 30, 2026 call explicitly covering balance sheet review and franchisee profitability, and Domino's Pizza Group plc was downgraded to sell on August 12 for inflation-driven margin pressure. Domino's does not own these entities, but the FY2025 10-K discloses that the largest unnamed master franchisee runs 3,524 stores across 12 markets, roughly 23% of the international base that supplies ~25% of segment profit.

**Supply chain and international royalties are working, with caveats.** Supply chain segment income grew 14.1% in FY2025 and 18.1% in Q2, and capex stepped up 69% over two years to $57.4M as distribution capacity is built (finance lease obligations doubled to $125.9M in H1, explained by previously disclosed commitments commencing; leases not yet commenced fell from $161.6M to $96.5M). Part of the Q2 supply chain revenue gain is mechanical: of the +$44.6M increase, roughly $19M came from a 2.2% food-basket price increase, and refranchising 77 stores converts eliminated intersegment revenue (about $400K per store annually) into external revenue worth roughly $31M annualized. The headline "revenue beat" reported on July 20 was franchisee ingredient purchases plus accounting geography, not consumer demand: U.S. retail sales grew 1.9% while supply chain revenue grew 6.5%.

**FY2027 arithmetic is underappreciated.** FY2026 is a 53-week year with a 17-week Q4; H2 contains 29 of 53 weeks. Management's mid-to-high-single-digit operating income guidance is therefore achievable on roughly flat comps; H1's +6.3% is already inside it. The problem transfers to FY2027, which loses that week (~2% of revenue, roughly $0.35-0.40 of EPS), absorbs the July 2027 refinancing step-up ($0.26-0.42 after tax at 5.0-5.5%), and grows royalties off a base with 0.1% U.S. comps. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $20.92 implies roughly 10% growth against that stack. We model $20.00-20.50 as more realistic absent a genuine comp reacceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $346.27, with 33.079M shares, market cap is $11.46B and enterprise value $16.17B ($11.46B + $4.884B debt − $0.165B unrestricted cash).

Against the supplied restaurant peer set — EAT 21.4x, TXRH 33.1x, CAKE 29.2x, CAVA 126.6x, BROS 71.0x, median 33.1x — DPZ is the cheapest name in the group. That comparison is not usable at face value: the median is inflated by two high-growth concepts, and none of these is an asset-light, 4.5x-levered franchisor. Against the franchisor cohort DPZ historically traded with (mid-20s multiples), 18.2x forward is a genuine discount, and against its own history the derating is severe: the five-year TSR series (148.40 → 92.19 → 111.25 → 117.50 → 118.24) shows four years of flat equity value against 5.1% Adjusted EBITDA compounding.

The question is whether that derating is finished. Two considerations argue it is not, and they are why we score valuation 6.0 rather than 7+. First, a multiple discount is only mispricing if there is a catalyst to close it; the identified catalyst here is a single Q3 product launch aimed at a ticket problem in a category where the number-two player, newly owned by LongRange Capital and freed from Yum's portfolio optics, has just launched a nationwide $3 Personal Pan Pizza value menu. Price competition attacks exactly the variable Domino's has diagnosed as broken. Second, estimates are still falling (current year −1.2% over 90 days) and three of the last four quarters missed, which is the profile of a multiple that continues to compress rather than one that snaps back.

Scenario framework:
- **Base $375** — 18.5x FY2027 EPS of ~$20.25 (below consensus for the 53rd-week reversal and interest step-up). +8.3% price, ~10.6% total return with the dividend.
- **Bull $450** — U.S. SSS reaccelerates to low-single-digit positive on the Q3 launch, franchisee margins stabilize, unit guidance restored, multiple re-rates to ~21x FY2027 $21.50. Matches the high sell-side target. +30.0%.
- **Bear $270** — U.S. SSS flat to negative through FY2027, franchisee profitability pressure forces further development cuts, EBITDA declines toward the covenant test and buybacks are paused; ~14.5x on ~$18.50. Consistent with the $270 low sell-side target. −22.0%.

Weighting 20/45/35, expected price return is roughly +2%, or 4-5% including the dividend. That is below the cost of equity for a levered consumer discretionary name, which is the arithmetic behind REDUCE. TD Cowen's July 21 target raise to $310 — still 10% below the current price — is a fair proxy for how the sell-side marginal buyer views the rally.

## Competitive Landscape

The backward-looking competitive picture is the strongest part of the story. Pizza Hut's U.S. comps fell 6% in Q1 2026, its tenth consecutive quarterly decline, and Yum agreed on June 17, 2026 to sell the brand in two transactions totaling $2.7B (LongRange Capital $1.5B ex-China; Yum China $1.2B) alongside 250 further U.S. closures. Papa John's is guiding to 2-4% North American declines. Papa Murphy's is closing 68 corporate stores. Little Caesars' U.S. systemwide sales were roughly flat in 2025 at an estimated $4.42B. Domino's holds roughly 23% of the U.S. pizza category against 40-50% share for burger and sandwich category leaders, with over 85% of U.S. retail sales digital, and it grew transactions in both delivery and carryout while QSR industry order counts were flat, although the company declined to disclose the underlying order figures, which makes that claim unverifiable.

The forward-looking picture is more ambiguous, and this is why the competitive score moves down rather than up on competitor exits. A private-equity-owned Pizza Hut repositioning on price is plausibly a *more* aggressive value competitor than Pizza Hut inside Yum, in a category where MTY's CEO describes the customer as going "where the pizza is the cheapest at any given time." Second, aggregator dependence cuts both ways: the 10-K states that aggregator marketplace orders "may not have the same level of store-level profitability" as owned channels, which is a plausible partial explanation for order growth coexisting with franchisee margin pressure, though the company has not disclosed channel-level economics. Third, the 50% supply chain profit share is the price of a voluntary purchasing arrangement — franchisees are not required to buy from Domino's, and that obligation has grown from $138.7M to $193.0M in two years, faster than segment income.

## Risk Assessment

**Covenant-linked capital return risk (highest-conviction unpriced item).** The securitization requires the Holdco Leverage Ratio at or below 5.0x for amortization to remain suspended; at approximately 4.45x, roughly 11% of EBITDA decline consumes the cushion. Separately, failure to refinance $1,319.0M by July 2027 adds at least 5% interest and sweeps all cash flow except a weekly management fee. The consequence is that a franchisee-driven earnings decline would simultaneously reduce cash flow and restrict the dividend and buyback, a compounding, mechanical downside that a 4.87x interest coverage ratio does not capture.

**Refinancing cost, quantified and manageable.** Blended coupon on the July 2027 tranche is 4.178%; Domino's own September 2025 issuance priced at 4.930% (5yr) and 5.217% (7yr). At 5.0-6.25%, incremental after-tax interest is $8.4M to $21.3M, or $0.26 to $0.66 per share (1.5-3.7% of FY2025 EPS). The larger cumulative issue is repricing the whole $4,766.1M stack from ~4.24% to ~5.2% over 2027-2032, roughly $35.7M after tax, about $1.10 per share, with the 2.662% 2021 notes ($826.6M, October 2028) carrying the steepest step-up.

**Concentration.** Single-product dependence is explicit in the 10-K; cheese alone is roughly 25% of the company-store food basket, and food plus labor is 55-65% of store sales. On the counterparty side, one unnamed international master franchisee runs 3,524 stores in 12 markets (~23% of international units), and two listed master franchisees are conducting balance-sheet and franchisee-profitability reviews. A growing international revenue mix does not reduce this counterparty concentration; if royalty growth is routed through the same large operators, it increases it.

**Disclosure quality is degrading.** As company-owned stores shrink to 186 (2.6% of U.S. units), the only published store-level P&L becomes a weaker proxy. The Q2 4.2-point gross margin collapse to 11.4% is directionally corroborated by management's own development-guidance cut, but the magnitude should not be extrapolated to 7,045 franchised stores; market mix (Maryland, Virginia, Michigan sold) may explain a meaningful share.

**Other named risks.** GLP-1 drugs are an explicit 10-K risk factor with no quantification. A 10% adverse FX move reduces near-100%-margin international royalty revenue by roughly $30.0M. California AB 1228 and state joint-employer standards persist; casualty reserves were $51.2M at FY2025 year-end and their valuation is PwC's sole Critical Audit Matter. The residual DPC Dash stake ($17.7M carrying value after a 50.5% H1 decline) is now immaterial to the balance sheet but generated $18.4M of H1 EPS noise — roughly $0.43 per diluted share after tax — and is a mildly negative read-through on China unit economics, though the share-price decline may reflect Hong Kong market conditions rather than store performance.

**Governance and insider signal.** The CEO transition is internal and pre-announced: Joe Jordan (COO/President U.S.) becomes CEO with Russell Weiner retiring in October 2026 into an executive chairman role. Continuity reduces strategic-discontinuity risk but does not signal a reset, and Jordan ran U.S. operations through the period when U.S. segment income growth fell to 1.8%. Across ~25 Form 4 filings through July 22, 2026 there were **no open-market purchases** — all activity was option exercise-and-sell (Weiner ~$3.59M on July 17; Jordan ~$1.70M on July 16; recurring monthly sales by the CTO), plus new 10b5-1 plans for the CFO and Chief Supply Chain Officer adopted May 27. Executives may face holding requirements satisfied through RSUs, and exercise-and-sell of legacy $136.89-strike options is a liquidity decision; the absence of buying is not evidence of a negative internal view, only the absence of positive confirmation after a 26% drawdown from the 52-week high. Compensation mechanics have real teeth — FY2022 "compensation actually paid" to the CEO was −$5.57M — and maximum award-based dilution is modest at ~652,000 shares (~1.9%).

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 41.8% versus 29.1% one-year realized is rich (82nd percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (41.8% at 3 days, 35.3% at one month, 37.2% at six months). The 3-day tenor is largely mechanical and should be discounted; the more informative reading is that one-month vol at 35.3% with an 8.2% implied move prices meaningful uncertainty into the fall, with no scheduled earnings until October. Positioning is constructive rather than bearish: put/call skew is call-heavy at −16.2%, put/call OI fell from 1.15 to 0.66 since July 23 while the price rose 5.2%, and the put/call volume ratio is 0.93. The one countervailing datapoint is the six-month tenor's 1.83 put/call volume ratio — longer-dated protection being accumulated against near-dated call buying. Short interest of 10.9% of float at 3.2 days to cover means a strong Q3 comp print could squeeze; that is the primary risk to the REDUCE call and argues for trimming in stages rather than exiting.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($450, +30.0%).** The ticket miss is a marketing execution error lapping an unusually strong prior-year product window, and it is fixed within two quarters. Parmesan Stuffed Crust in "Best Deal Ever" plus the new signature pizza restore U.S. SSS to low-single-digit positive; franchisee profitability stabilizes and the U.S. development pipeline reopens; supply chain (+18.1% in Q2) and international royalties (+10.7%) keep compounding double-digit; Pizza Hut's disruption during ownership transition and Papa John's closures transfer share to the only national operator adding units. FY2027 EPS reaches $21.50 on a 3.5% annual share-count reduction, and the multiple re-rates to 21x, still below the franchisor cohort and well below DPZ's own history. Transaction growth against a flat QSR industry in Q2 is genuine evidence for this path.

**Bear case ($270, −22.0%).** The ticket problem is category-driven, not company-driven. LongRange-owned Pizza Hut at $3 personal pans, Papa John's discounting to defend traffic, and a U.S. consumer under mortgage-rate and food-cost pressure make ticket recovery impossible without traffic loss. U.S. SSS stays flat to negative; franchisee profitability erodes further (the company-store 4.2-point margin decline proves directionally representative); U.S. development is cut again; and international openings undershoot ~800 as master franchisees conduct balance-sheet reviews. FY2027 EPS lands near $18.50 as the 53rd week reverses and interest reprices. EBITDA drifts toward the 5.0x covenant test, the buyback pauses to preserve headroom, the EPS support from a 3.5% annual share retirement disappears, and the multiple compresses to ~14.5x. This case does not require a recession; it requires flat comps to persist for four more quarters.

**Our read.** The operating business is better than the EPS line suggests and the competitive setup is favorable, which is why this is REDUCE rather than AVOID. At 18.2x forward with estimates falling, three misses in four quarters, and FY2027 consensus that we believe is $0.40-0.90 too high before any comp assumption, the price no longer compensates for a leverage structure that converts an 11% EBITDA decline into a capital-return suspension. The stock is 8% below our base case and 22% above our bear case; that asymmetry got worse, not better, during the 22% rally off the June low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim roughly one-third to one-half of the position into current strength ($340-365), which is the upper half of the range the stock has traded since the April 27 guidance cut. Retain a core position; the franchise is high quality, the dividend is covered at a 45% payout, and short interest of 10.9% at 3.2 days to cover creates squeeze risk on a good print. Do not add. Holders wanting to defer the decision can monetize the rich near-term vol (41.8% IV vs 29.1% realized) by writing calls into the October report rather than selling stock outright.

**Targets from the $346.27 basis:** base $375 (+8.3%; 18.5x FY2027 EPS of ~$20.25), bull $450 (+30.0%), bear $270 (−22.0%). Twelve-month probability-weighted total return including the 2.30% dividend: approximately 4-5%.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE (measurable):**
1. U.S. same-store sales at or above +2.0% in the Q3 FY2026 report, with management confirming ticket rather than transactions as the driver of the improvement.
2. FY2027 consensus EPS stops falling and turns positive over a 30-day window (currently $20.92, −0.6% over 90 days).
3. The U.S. net-store outlook is restored above ~175 with explicit commentary that franchisee profitability has stabilized, or the company discloses systemwide franchisee store-level EBITDA that contradicts the company-store margin decline.
4. Price toward $300-310 with fundamentals unchanged, which would restore roughly 20%+ upside to the base case; that combination would warrant ACCUMULATE.

**Downgrade triggers toward AVOID:**
1. U.S. SSS negative in Q3 or Q4 FY2026, or a second reduction to FY2026 guidance.
2. Any disclosed pause or material reduction in the buyback attributed to leverage or covenant headroom, or Holdco Leverage Ratio disclosed above 4.75x.
3. A named master franchisee restructuring, covenant breach, or store-closure program affecting more than roughly 5% of the international base, or a cut to the ~800 international opening guide.
4. July 2027 notes not addressed by Q1 FY2027 (i.e., no refinancing announcement by roughly March 2027), which would put the +5% penalty interest and cash-sweep mechanics into live consideration.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q3 FY2026 report (expected October 2026, the first under the incoming CEO after the October transition) is the decisive checkpoint; it contains the first read on the Q3 product launch and on whether the H2 comp acceleration implied by reaffirmed guidance is materializing. A secondary checkpoint is any Q4/FY2027 guidance issued with the February 2027 10-K, which will reveal how much of the 53rd-week benefit management expects to give back.

## Conclusion

Domino's is a high-quality, asset-light franchisor whose operating margin, supply chain productivity and international royalty stream continue to compound while its two largest competitors shrink. That is not in dispute, and it is why we retain a core position rather than exiting. What has changed is the price paid for a business whose largest profit pool grew 1.8% last year and 2.3% last quarter, whose U.S. same-store sales are +0.1%, whose consensus estimates have fallen for three consecutive months, and which has missed EPS in three of the last four quarters.

At $346.27 — 18.2x FY2026 and 16.6x FY2027 consensus, 15.5x EV/EBITDA, a 5.4% normalized FCF yield and a 2.30% dividend — the discount to the franchisor cohort and to DPZ's own history is real but no longer large enough to fund the specific risks in front of it: a FY2027 EPS bridge that must absorb the 53rd-week reversal and the securitization repricing, a ticket problem being attacked by a newly private-equity-owned Pizza Hut on price, master franchisee counterparties conducting balance-sheet reviews, and a covenant structure in which an 11% EBITDA decline suspends the capital returns that have been supplying the EPS growth. Eight percent upside to base against 22% downside to bear, with buybacks having been executed 21% above today's price and no insider open-market buying after a 26% drawdown, is an unfavorable distribution.

**REDUCE.** Trim into strength, hold a core, and require evidence from the October report — U.S. same-store sales at or above +2.0% and estimates turning up — before restoring exposure.
