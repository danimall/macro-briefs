## Executive Summary

City Holding Company is a $1.8 billion market cap community bank headquartered in Charleston, West Virginia, operating 96 branches across West Virginia, Kentucky, Virginia, and Ohio. The company is an exceptional operator by nearly every banking metric: 1.97% ROA, 21.2% return on tangible common equity, a 48-49% efficiency ratio, and net charge-offs of just 2 basis points in 2025. Capital ratios are roughly double regulatory minimums, and the bank recorded a negative credit provision (net recovery) for the full year.

The fundamental issue preventing a stronger recommendation is organic growth exhaustion. Net new DDA accounts — the leading indicator of deposit franchise vitality — decelerated from +8,860 in 2021 to +3,548 in 2025 and just +420 in Q1 2026. The bank operates in Appalachian markets with stagnant-to-declining populations, limiting its ability to grow deposits and loans organically. Consensus estimates project EPS growth from $8.93 (2025) to $9.50 (2027E), representing only ~3% annualized growth. At 13.7x trailing earnings and 2.8x tangible book, the stock's multiple is fair for quality but already reflects the bank's operational excellence. There is no specific catalyst that would cause re-rating, and five sell-side analysts unanimously rate the stock HOLD with a $131.40 mean target (5.4% upside from current levels).

The stock is best characterized as an income hold: a well-run bank generating ~2.8% dividend yield with modest capital appreciation potential from buyback-driven EPS accretion and potential M&A upside.

## Company Overview

City Holding operates through City National Bank of West Virginia, a retail-oriented community bank providing commercial banking, consumer banking, mortgage banking, and wealth management services. The 96-branch network follows the I-64 corridor (Lexington, KY to Lexington, VA) and the I-81 corridor through Virginia's Shenandoah Valley.

**Loan Portfolio ($4.51 billion):**
- Residential real estate + home equity: 47% ($2.13B)
- Commercial real estate: 41% ($1.87B) — including $399M in hotels (9% of total loans)
- Commercial & industrial: 10% ($454M)
- Consumer: 1% ($47M)

**Deposit Franchise ($5.30 billion):** The bank holds top-3 deposit market share positions in most core markets, with only ~15% estimated uninsured deposits — well below the levels that triggered deposit flight at SVB/Signature in 2023.

**Credit Quality:**

Net charge-offs were 0.02% of average loans in 2025 — just $0.9 million on a $4.4 billion portfolio. The company recorded a net recovery of credit losses ($1.4 million credit to the P&L). Non-performing assets stood at 0.27% of total loans in Q1 2026, declining from 0.38% a year earlier. The allowance/loans ratio of 0.44% appears low in absolute terms but is appropriate given the pristine charge-off history.

**Capital & Liquidity:**

CET1 of 16.9% is more than 2.6x the well-capitalized threshold. Tangible equity/tangible assets stands at 9.65%. The loan-to-deposit ratio of 84.1% provides ample liquidity headroom, supplemented by $1.8 billion in unused FHLB/Fed borrowing capacity and $762 million in unpledged securities.

**Capital Return:**

Operating cash flow of $131.4 million (2025) versus minimal capex ($2.9 million) yields effective free cash flow of $128.4 million. The bank is returning capital aggressively: dividends of $3.48/share annualized (~37% payout ratio), plus active buybacks that reduced shares outstanding by 3.7% over the past year (14,650K → 14,111K). A new 1-million-share repurchase authorization (7% of outstanding shares) was approved in March 2026.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth options are constrained:

**DDA Account Growth Deceleration:**
- 2021: +8,860 net new accounts (+3.8%)
- 2022: +4,544 (+1.9%)
- 2023: +4,768
- 2024: +4,497 (+1.8%)
- 2025: +3,548 (+1.3%)
- Q1 2026: +420 (annualized ~0.8%)

This trajectory reflects population stagnation in core Appalachian markets (West Virginia: -0.1% since 2020; Lawrence County, OH: -4.1%). The deposit franchise is approaching saturation in existing geographies.

**Loan Growth:** Loans grew 5.4% in 2025, healthy for a community bank, but increasingly reliant on out-of-footprint commercial lending, which grew from 19% to 35% of commercial loans in one year. This extension into unfamiliar markets carries higher credit risk and lower information advantage.

**Consensus Estimates:** EPS is projected at $9.05 (2026E) and $9.50 (2027E), representing ~1.3% and ~5.0% growth respectively. Buyback-driven share count reduction accounts for roughly half of this per-share growth.

**M&A Optionality:** The 10-K risk factors reference "issuance of shares as consideration in connection with the Merger," suggesting acquisition activity may be in progress. With CET1 at 16.9%, CHCO has enormous capacity to acquire smaller community banks in contiguous markets. This represents the primary growth lever but is binary and unconfirmed.

## Competitive Landscape

CHCO holds #1-#3 deposit share in most core geographies, employee turnover is under 6% (exceptional for banking), and the deposit franchise is extraordinarily sticky (59.5% of assets in transaction/savings accounts).

However, DDA account growth deceleration suggests the competitive moat is not translating into franchise expansion. In markets with flat-to-declining populations, even a dominant player faces a zero-sum environment where incremental deposits can only come from competitor share gains or de novo market entry.

The out-of-footprint commercial lending expansion (19% → 35% of commercial loans in one year) represents management's recognition that local markets are insufficient for target growth rates. This dilutes the information advantage that has historically produced pristine credit quality.

## Risk Assessment

**Geographic/Demographic Risk (Moderate):** Core markets in WV and eastern KY are tied to energy industries facing secular headwinds. Population stagnation limits organic growth.

**Hotel Concentration (Moderate):** $399M in hotel/motel loans (9% of total portfolio) with 58% LTV and 1.75x DSC are manageable metrics, but the sector is highly cyclical. Any consumer spending pullback would pressure hotel revenues along I-64/I-81.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity (Low-Moderate):** 84% of loans maturing after one year are variable rate, making the bank asset-sensitive. In a rate-hike scenario, this benefits NIM. In a rate-cut scenario, NIM compresses but funding costs decline simultaneously.

**Out-of-Footprint Credit Risk (Low-Moderate):** The rapid expansion of commercial lending outside the branch footprint reduces the information advantage that historically produced 2bp net charge-offs.

**Options Market Signal:** Near-term ATM IV of 24.4% vs. historical vol of 19.9% represents a 4.5pp premium, suggesting the market prices modestly elevated uncertainty. The term structure is flat (24.4% near vs. 23.2% far), put/call skew is balanced, and OI P/C of 0.46 is call-heavy. No meaningful bearish positioning is evident. The 53 total contracts across all expirations reflects extremely thin options liquidity — signals from this data are low-confidence.

**Short Interest:** 9.0% of float with 7.9 days to cover is elevated for a community bank. This could reflect fundamental bearishness or structural factors (borrowing for tax purposes, merger arbitrage if an acquisition is in play). The 7.9 days to cover creates meaningful squeeze potential if positive catalysts emerge.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($135-140, +8-12%):**
- M&A announcement using excess capital to acquire a complementary franchise in a growing market (Virginia, North Carolina)
- NIM expansion continues as deposit costs reprice lower faster than loan yields
- Hotel portfolio performs through economic slowdown; credit quality remains pristine
- Buyback pace accelerates given new 1M-share authorization and stock near 52-week low levels
- Rate environment (higher for longer or hikes) benefits asset-sensitive balance sheet

**Bear Case ($110-115, -8-12%):**
- Consumer spending cliff materializes, stressing hotel portfolio
- DDA growth stalls completely, forcing reliance on wholesale funding
- Out-of-footprint commercial loans produce unexpected credit losses
- Rate cuts compress NIM faster than funding costs decline
- Demographic headwinds in core markets become insurmountable for growth

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
1. Confirmed M&A announcement that expands into a growing demographic market at <2.0x TBV
2. DDA account growth re-accelerates to +5,000/year for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating franchise revitalization
3. Stock declines to $113-115 (12.5x forward earnings / ~2.5x TBV), providing 8%+ total return to consensus with dividend

**Downgrade Triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. Net charge-offs exceed 30bp in any quarter, signaling out-of-footprint credit deterioration
2. NIM compresses below 3.60% (from current 3.97%), indicating balance sheet duration mismatch
3. Stock appreciates to $135+ without fundamental improvement, compressing total return potential below 5%

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings release (July 2026, Q2 results) — focus on DDA account growth, hotel portfolio performance metrics, and any M&A commentary.

## Conclusion

City Holding is a well-managed, well-capitalized community bank generating top-quartile returns on equity with minimal credit risk. The 2.8% dividend yield is well-covered (37% payout ratio) and growing (10% increase in 2025). Active buybacks reduce share count by ~3-4% annually. These attributes make it a sound income-generating hold.

The barrier to a BUY recommendation is specific and measurable: DDA account growth has decelerated to +420 in Q1 2026, indicating the deposit franchise is approaching saturation in its current markets. Without deposit growth, the bank cannot fund meaningful loan growth without shifting toward wholesale funding or further out-of-footprint lending — both of which dilute the franchise quality that justifies the premium P/TBV multiple. Consensus EPS growth of ~3% annualized is insufficient to drive re-rating, and the stock trades at peer-median multiples with no identifiable catalyst for multiple expansion. Total return of ~8% (5.4% price appreciation to consensus + 2.8% yield) is adequate for income-oriented portfolios but does not meet the asymmetric upside threshold for an active BUY.
