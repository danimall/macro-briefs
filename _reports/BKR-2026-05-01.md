## Executive Summary

Baker Hughes has executed an impressive strategic pivot from traditional oilfield services toward energy technology, with its Industrial & Energy Technology (IET) segment delivering 10% revenue growth and 21% EBITDA growth in 2025 while the legacy Oilfield Services & Equipment (OFSE) segment contracted 8%. The $32.4B IET backlog (2.4 years of segment revenue), surging LNG and data center power demand, and a consistent earnings beat streak (4 consecutive quarters, average +13% surprise) validate the transformation thesis. Q1 2026 was particularly strong: revenue beat by $260M and EPS by 17.5% despite Middle East disruptions.

The problem is the price. BKR has rallied 97% from its 52-week low of $34.56 to $69.02, now trading at 22.2x trailing earnings — a 21% premium to the energy peer median P/E of 18.3x. Simultaneously, consensus estimates are deteriorating rapidly: current quarter EPS was cut 23% in the last 30 days, current year estimates fell 11%, and even next year projections declined 5%. The $13.6B all-cash Chart Industries acquisition, funded by $9.5B in new debt, will push gross leverage from ~1.3x to approximately 3x EBITDA. Management's de-leveraging plan depends on sustained ~$2.5B annual FCF, ~$3B in divestiture proceeds, and $325M in cost synergies — all of which require flawless execution during a period of elevated geopolitical risk, OFSE cyclical softness, and tariff uncertainty.

The underlying business transformation is real and strategically sound. But at current levels, the stock prices in substantial execution on Chart, continued IET momentum, and stable macro conditions — leaving limited margin of safety against multiple risks materializing simultaneously.

## Company Overview

Baker Hughes operates two distinct segments that increasingly resemble separate businesses. **OFSE** ($14.3B, ~52% of 2025 revenue) covers traditional oilfield services — well construction, completions, production solutions, and subsea systems. This segment is directly correlated with global rig counts, which declined 7% in 2025 to 1,818, and management expects continued softness through most of 2026. **IET** ($13.4B, ~48% of revenue) covers gas technology equipment and services, industrial products, climate technology, and digital solutions. This is the growth engine, with orders reaching $14.9B in 2025 (+14% YoY) and Climate Technology orders nearly doubling to $1.63B.

The company operates in 120+ countries with significant Middle East exposure (UAE was 10% of receivables). The pending $13.6B Chart Industries acquisition — expected to close Q2 2026 — will add LNG processing equipment, data center cooling technology, and water treatment capabilities, pushing IET toward 55%+ of combined revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** 2025 revenue was flat at $27.7B, masking the divergent segment trajectories. Adjusted EBITDA grew 5% to $4.82B (17.4% margin) as IET's margin expansion offset OFSE contraction. Net income declined 13% to $2.6B, driven by a $470M swing in mark-to-market equity securities losses and OFSE volume declines.

**Cash flow generation remains strong.** Operating cash flow hit a record $3.81B in 2025, with free cash flow of $2.54B (9.2% FCF margin). FCF/Net Income conversion of approximately 98% signals high earnings quality. This cash generation capacity is the primary engine for post-Chart de-leveraging.

**Balance sheet is about to transform.** Current net debt stands at approximately $2.4B against $4.82B EBITDA (~0.5x leverage). Post-Chart close, the $9.5B in new senior notes (plus assumption of Chart debt) will push total debt to approximately $15-16B, implying gross leverage of roughly 3x EBITDA. Even after factoring in the Waygate Technologies divestiture ($1.45B, expected H2 2026) and other portfolio sales (~$1.5B additional), net leverage will likely remain above 2x for 12-18 months.

**Q1 2026 provided encouragement.** Revenue of $6.59B beat estimates by $260M, adjusted EPS of $0.58 beat by 16.2%, and adjusted EBITDA margin reached 17.6%. Record IET orders, with power systems and digital orders surging, partially offset Middle East disruptions. Multiple analysts raised price targets to $75-80 following the report.

**Estimate trajectory is negative despite the beat.** BKR just beat Q1 estimates convincingly, yet forward consensus is being cut aggressively. Current quarter EPS estimates fell 23.1% in the past 30 days (from $0.61 to $0.47), current year estimates fell 11.2% (from $2.59 to $2.30), and next year declined 5.0% (from $2.95 to $2.80). These cuts likely reflect analysts incorporating OFSE weakness, Middle East uncertainty, tariff impacts, and dilution/interest cost from Chart financing. The stock is rallying into falling estimates — a pattern that frequently resolves downward.

## Growth Analysis

**IET is the thesis.** The segment's $32.4B remaining performance obligations provide 2.4 years of revenue coverage. Three secular growth vectors support multi-year demand: (1) global LNG infrastructure buildout, with demand growing ~7% in 2025; (2) AI-driven data center power demand, where Baker Hughes' aeroderivative gas turbines and Chart's cooling technology are directly positioned; (3) climate technology solutions (CCUS, hydrogen, geothermal), with orders nearly doubling in 2025.

**Aeroderivative supply chain tightness** is a double-edged signal. Extended lead times constrain near-term growth but demonstrate that demand exceeds supply, supporting pricing power. The GE Aerospace partnership provides critical turbine supply, but also represents concentration risk — loss or disruption would materially impair the IET growth trajectory.

**Chart acquisition creates potential for step-change growth.** Management targets IET orders exceeding $40B by 2028 post-Chart (from $14.9B standalone in 2025). $325M in cost synergies and cross-selling opportunities across LNG processing, power generation, and water treatment provide a plausible path. The risk is execution: integrating a $13.6B acquisition while simultaneously managing OFSE decline, Middle East disruptions, and de-leveraging.

**OFSE is in structural-cyclical decline.** Global rig counts are falling, upstream spending is expected to decline modestly in 2026, and Brent has been volatile. Management acknowledged "continued soft conditions through most of 2026." While OFSE still generates meaningful cash flow (~$2.6B EBITDA in 2025), the segment will be a drag on consolidated growth for the foreseeable future.

## Valuation Assessment

BKR trades at 22.2x trailing EPS ($2.60) and approximately 30x current-year consensus ($2.30). Against the energy peer median P/E of 18.3x, this represents a 21% premium on trailing and a 64% premium on current-year estimates. The dividend yield of 1.3% is less than half the peer median of 3.0%.

**The premium reflects the IET growth story, but is difficult to justify given the estimate trajectory.** If BKR achieves $2.30 in FY2026 EPS, the stock at $69 trades at 30x — a valuation that would be generous for a pure-play industrial technology company, let alone one with a declining OFSE segment and ~3x leverage.

**Sum-of-parts framework:** Valuing OFSE at 6-7x EBITDA ($15.7-18.3B) and IET at 12-14x EBITDA ($29.8-34.7B) yields a combined EV range of $45.5-53.0B. Subtracting post-Chart net debt of approximately $12-13B (after divestitures) yields equity value of $32.5-40.0B, or roughly $33-40 per share on ~992M shares outstanding. This implies fair value on a post-acquisition, fully-leveraged basis is materially below the current price. Even adding $325M in synergies at a blended 12x multiple adds only ~$4/share.

**A more generous framework** using 15-16x for IET (reflecting pure energy tech comps) and 7x for OFSE yields equity value of approximately $48-55 per share — still below the current price and dependent on full synergy capture and successful de-leveraging.

The analyst consensus mean target of $69.71 essentially equals the current price. The median target of $71.00 implies ~3% upside. The high target of $85.00 requires 23% upside and likely assumes full Chart synergy realization plus continued IET margin expansion. The low target of $48.00 represents -30% downside and likely reflects an OFSE downturn or Chart integration difficulties.

## Competitive Landscape

Baker Hughes is differentiated among the Big 3 OFS players. SLB is pivoting toward digital/technology but remains more upstream-weighted. Halliburton is most exposed to North American completions. BKR's IET segment positions the company uniquely at the intersection of LNG infrastructure, data center power, and industrial decarbonization — structural growth markets with lower correlation to drilling cycles.

The Chart acquisition, if successfully integrated, will widen this differentiation. Chart's LNG processing equipment, heat exchangers, and data center cooling capabilities are complementary, and the combined entity would offer a more complete solution set for gas-to-power and LNG customers.

Competitive risks include: (1) GE Aerospace supplier dependency, which could constrain growth or be leveraged against BKR in negotiations; (2) SLB and other technology-focused industrial companies competing for the same data center and LNG opportunities; and (3) energy transition pace uncertainty — if clean energy adoption decelerates, the Climate Technology Solutions business may underperform.

The competitive position is improving. IET backlog visibility, growing power/digital orders, and the Chart deal collectively strengthen BKR's moat versus traditional OFS peers. However, the stock already reflects a meaningful re-rating from OFS multiples toward industrial technology multiples.

## Risk Assessment

**Chart acquisition execution (HIGH).** This is the dominant risk. Post-close leverage of ~3x EBITDA leaves limited room for error. The de-leveraging plan requires: (a) sustained FCF of ~$2.5B/year, (b) ~$3B in divestiture proceeds (Waygate at $1.45B is confirmed; remaining ~$1.5B is less certain), and (c) $325M in cost synergies. Management paid $258M to terminate Chart's prior merger agreement with Flowserve — signaling strong conviction but also aggressive pursuit that may have inflated the acquisition price. If oil prices weaken further, OFSE cash flows decline, or integration falters, the leverage becomes a binding constraint on capital returns and strategic flexibility.

**OFSE cyclical pressure (HIGH).** Rig counts are declining, upstream spending is expected to fall modestly, and Middle East operational disruptions are weighing on the segment. OFSE generated $2.62B in EBITDA in 2025, down 9% YoY. Further deterioration would directly impair consolidated cash flow and the de-leveraging timeline.

**Estimate trajectory (MODERATE-HIGH).** A stock at 30x forward earnings with falling estimates is a vulnerable setup — see Financial Analysis for the specific revision figures.

**Middle East geopolitical disruption (HIGH).** Baker Hughes has significant operations in the region (UAE at 10% of receivables). Ongoing conflict directly impacts OFSE operations and creates supply chain uncertainty for IET equipment.

**Mexico customer credit risk (MODERATE).** BKR has issued credit default swaps totaling $775M (with $287M notional remaining) related to its primary Mexico customer (likely Pemex). While exposure has declined from 9% to 4% of receivables, a Pemex default would crystallize losses.

## Conclusion

Baker Hughes has built a genuinely differentiated energy technology platform, and the IET segment's backlog, LNG tailwinds, and data center power exposure represent real structural growth advantages over traditional OFS peers. The consistent earnings beat streak and Q1 2026 strength validate management execution. However, the stock at $69 — near its 52-week high, at 22x trailing and 30x current-year estimates while consensus is being cut 11-23% across forward periods — prices in a best-case outcome on Chart integration, de-leveraging, and macro stability. Post-acquisition gross leverage of ~3x EBITDA introduces meaningful balance sheet risk that did not exist 6 months ago. The sum-of-parts analysis suggests fair value in the $48-55 range on a fully-leveraged basis, implying the current price embeds substantial optimism. HOLD with a positive growth view — the business transformation is worth owning at the right price, but the risk/reward at $69 does not compensate adequately for the execution risks ahead. A pullback toward $55-58 would create a compelling entry point.
