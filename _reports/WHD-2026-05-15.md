## Executive Summary

Cactus Inc. is a founder-led manufacturer of wellhead equipment and spoolable pipe technologies that executed a transformative acquisition on January 1, 2026, purchasing 65% of Baker Hughes' surface pressure control business for $344.5M. This immediately internationalized the company, adding $537.5M of backlog concentrated in the Middle East and increasing quarterly revenue run-rate from ~$261M to ~$388M. Q1 2026 delivered adjusted EPS of $0.70 (beat by 8.6%), demonstrating early execution credibility.

The problem is price. At $57.83, WHD trades at 54x trailing EPS and approximately 21x FY2026 consensus EPS ($2.76), while near-term estimates are falling — current quarter revisions down 17% over 90 days. The stock has appreciated 30.5% over the past year and trades within 3% of its 52-week high. The Baker Hughes JV introduces Middle East conflict exposure, a $186-231M contingent put obligation in 2028, and integration complexity. The Bender family sold ~$20M in stock within two months of deal closing. These factors collectively suggest the market is pricing optimistic execution with limited discount for material risks.

The 12-month thesis rests on: (1) FY2027 estimate revisions turning positive (+5.6% over 30 days), (2) Spoolable Technologies margin expansion (36.6% EBITDA margins), (3) synergy realization from the JV ($15M annualized target), and (4) a potential recovery in U.S. rig counts if oil prices remain elevated. These are legitimate growth drivers that warrant holding through the integration period rather than selling, but insufficient to justify adding at current levels.

## Company Overview

Cactus Inc. designs, manufactures, and rents pressure control equipment (wellheads, frac trees) and spoolable pipe technologies for the oil and gas industry. The company operates through three segments as of 2026:

- **Pressure Control (65% of FY2025 revenue):** Wellhead systems and frac trees for U.S. onshore unconventional drilling. Manufacturing in Bossier City, LA; Suzhou, China; and Hai Duong, Vietnam.
- **Spoolable Technologies (34% of FY2025 revenue):** FlexSteel-branded composite spoolable pipe for production/gathering applications. Manufacturing in Baytown, TX.
- **Cactus International (new Jan 2026):** Surface pressure control operations primarily in the Middle East, acquired from Baker Hughes.

The company serves 300+ customers, with one customer representing 17% of 2025 revenue — a concentration that has increased from 10% in 2023.

## Growth Analysis

This reflects: (1) continued U.S. rig count softness, (2) Middle East conflict disrupting JV operations, (3) tariff cost absorption.

**Medium-term drivers (FY2027+):**
1. **Baker Hughes JV maturation:** $537.5M backlog converting to revenue; $15M annualized synergies; international presence provides counter-cyclical diversification vs. U.S. onshore
2. **Spoolable Technologies growth:** Higher-diameter pipe offerings gaining traction; segment margins expanding; less cyclical than drilling-tied Pressure Control
3. **Oil price environment:** Management notes customer sentiment has "recently turned more bullish" due to higher commodity prices and expects natural gas-directed drilling to accelerate (natural gas prices +61% in 2025)
4. **FY2027 estimates rising:** +5.6% over 30 days to $3.53, implying 28% EPS growth vs. FY2026

The growth thesis is real but back-loaded. Near-term (2-3 quarters), the company faces integration friction, Middle East operational uncertainty, and tariff headwinds. Beyond that, the JV backlog conversion and higher commodity prices create a credible path to $3.50+ EPS.

## Competitive Landscape

Cactus occupies a differentiated position within OFS:
- **Pressure Control:** One of the largest U.S. wellhead suppliers with vertically integrated manufacturing and a rental fleet model that creates recurring revenue. The Baker Hughes JV provides Middle East scale that no other mid-cap OFS company possesses in surface pressure control.
- **Spoolable Technologies:** FlexSteel competes with Mattr and NOV. The 36.6% EBITDA margin suggests pricing power and/or operational efficiency advantages.
- **Cost structure:** Low-cost manufacturing in China/Vietnam was historically an advantage but is now a liability under 50% Section 232 tariffs. Competitors with domestic-only manufacturing face higher steel input costs regardless, partially offsetting Cactus's tariff burden.

The competitive trajectory is improving: international diversification via the JV, growing spoolable pipe portfolio, and strong service reputation. The risk is that the tariff regime structurally impairs the low-cost manufacturing advantage that underpinned historical margins.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Middle East Conflict (HIGH — Active):** The CEO specifically called out "highly uncertain" near-term Middle East activity. The JV's concentrated customer base ("a small number of which account for the preponderance of the Joint Venture's revenue") creates outsized exposure to specific NOCs. Logistics disruptions are impacting operations. Q2 Pressure Control guidance is "approximately flat" specifically because Middle East conflict offsets domestic improvement.

**2. Tariff Exposure (MEDIUM-HIGH):** 50% Section 232 tariffs on steel imports plus elevated China-specific tariffs directly impact Cactus's manufacturing cost structure. The company's low-cost advantage from Chinese/Vietnamese plants may be structurally impaired. Inventory values increased $27M due to tariffs in 2025.

**3. Baker Hughes Put Right (MEDIUM — Dated):** Exercisable January 2028. At 6x TTM EBITDA, Cactus could owe $186-231M. This is manageable given current cash generation but constrains capital allocation flexibility.

**4. Near-term Estimate Deterioration:** If Q2 misses (the one miss in recent history was Q2 2025 at -9.2%), sentiment could shift rapidly given the stock is near 52-week highs.

**5. Customer Concentration:** 17% from one customer (up from 10% in 2023). Loss would be material.

## Conclusion

Cactus Inc. is a well-run, financially strong company executing an ambitious international expansion during a period of improving oil services fundamentals. The Baker Hughes JV creates genuine long-term value potential — $537.5M of backlog, Middle East diversification, and synergy opportunities. Balance sheet quality is exceptional (zero net debt, $292M cash, 20%+ FCF margins).

The issue is valuation relative to near-term fundamentals. At $57.83, the stock trades within 3% of its 52-week high while current-quarter estimates have declined 17% over 90 days. Insider selling of $20M by the founding family within weeks of deal closing adds a cautionary signal. Middle East conflict is actively disrupting JV operations. The market is pricing FY2027 execution today, leaving limited margin of safety.

The stock warrants holding for existing positions because the 12-month growth thesis is intact: FY2027 estimates are rising (+5.6%), spoolable pipe margins are expanding, and the JV backlog provides multi-quarter revenue visibility. If oil prices remain elevated and U.S. rig counts recover, the earnings power is materially above current consensus. New money should wait for either a pullback to $48-50 or stabilization of near-term estimates before committing capital.
