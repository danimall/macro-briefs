## Executive Summary

Hormel is midway through a genuine but modest operating recovery from a FY2025 trough that was two-thirds non-cash. Q2 FY2026 delivered 2.5% net sales growth (3.3% organic), 70 basis points of gross margin expansion, adjusted operating income up 10.9%, and profit growth in all three segments — Retail +13.5%, Foodservice +10.8%, International +20.3%. Management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of $1.43–$1.51 adjusted diluted EPS against $1.37 in FY2025. The most concrete driver cited repeatedly in the Q2 MD&A is "improved performance across the turkey manufacturing network," which supports the reading that part of the FY2025 collapse was self-inflicted operational underperformance now being corrected rather than a permanent loss of earning power.

The recovery is shallower than the quarter suggests. Six-month adjusted EPS of $0.74 is up only 5.7% year over year, six-month gross margin is up 20 basis points rather than 70, and six-month Retail segment profit is actually down 1.7%. Volume fell 2.6% in the first half (organic −2.3%) after falling 2.3% in FY2025, and Retail organic volume was down 3.7%. FY2025 net sales of $12.106 billion were 2.8% *below* FY2022 through a period of substantial food inflation, which means pricing has been fully offset by lost volume. Advertising was cut 6% to $75 million in the half despite management stating an intent to increase it. The brand-support reinvestment that Retail volume needs has not yet been funded, and when it is, it will consume part of the margin recovery.

At $24.475 the stock is 16.4x consensus FY2026 adjusted EPS of $1.49, 15.7x FY2027 consensus of $1.56, and 12.1x FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of roughly $1.28 billion on an enterprise value of $15.47 billion. The 4.78% yield is real and is the primary reason to own the shares, but it is covered thinly: FY2025 dividends of $633 million exceeded free cash flow of $534 million by $99 million, the FY2026 increase was cut to 1% to preserve the 60-year streak at minimum cost, and payout is 78.5% of consensus adjusted EPS. Add an unreserved direct-purchaser antitrust trial scheduled to begin October 8, 2026, roughly $940 million of book value in management's own "heightened risk of impairment" bucket, a Garudafood equity stake carried $23 million above its quoted market price, and a refinancing bridge that adds roughly $0.08 per share of interest cost in FY2028–FY2030, and the expected return does not clear the bar for new capital. Own it for the yield and the recovery optionality; wait for the Q3 print and the trial outcome before adding.

## Company Overview

Hormel Foods is a 135-year-old branded packaged food manufacturer headquartered in Austin, Minnesota, generating $12.1 billion of FY2025 net sales across roughly forty brands including SPAM, Planters, Skippy, Jennie-O, Applegate, Columbus, Herdez and Hormel Black Label. It is an S&P 500 Dividend Aristocrat with 391 consecutive quarterly dividends paid since 1928 and 60 consecutive years of increases.

Three reportable segments, with sharply divergent economics. Using first-half FY2026 disclosures: **Retail** produced 60.6% of net sales but only 41.3% of segment profit, at a 6.9% segment margin. **Foodservice** produced 33.2% of sales and 51.3% of segment profit, at 15.7%. **International** produced 6.1% of sales and 7.4% of profit, at 12.3%. Foodservice is the highest-quality asset in the portfolio and the only segment compounding both volume and profit.

The moat is trademark-based, not technology-based — the 10-K discloses only thirteen U.S. and six foreign granted patents. Durability rests on brand equity, shelf position, and a direct-selling Foodservice organization. Management has already written down two of those trademarks (Planters $59.1 million, Chi-Chi's $2.9 million) when royalty-method cash flow assumptions deteriorated, which is the cleanest available evidence on how the moat is actually performing in the Retail portfolio.

Portfolio reshaping has been active. Justin's: 51% sold December 15, 2025 for $77.3 million cash at a $23.5 million pre-tax gain, with Hormel retaining 49% of successor Joy Topco, L.P. as a minority partner alongside sponsor Forward Consumer Partners. Whole-bird turkey: sold April 24, 2026 for $61.2 million consideration — only $21.2 million cash plus a $40.0 million secured 6% seller note maturing December 2030 — at a $61.0 million pre-tax loss. Hormel retained the Jennie-O brand and became a co-manufacturing customer of the divested operation. Direction of travel is right: exiting capital-intensive, commodity-exposed, low-return operations while keeping the branded value-added layer. Execution is uneven: branded assets cleared above carrying value, commodity assets did not, and two-thirds of the turkey consideration is a five-year receivable that converts operating risk into counterparty credit exposure.

## Financial Analysis

**The FY2025 trough decomposed.** GAAP operating income fell 33% to $718.6 million and net income fell 41% to $478.2 million, but $234.5 million of the $312.6 million of pre-tax adjustments were non-cash fourth-quarter impairments: $163.7 million on the ~30% Garudafood equity stake (which flipped International from +$92.1 million segment profit to a $(80.4) million loss) and $70.8 million of Retail intangibles. Adjusted operating income fell 11% to $1,018.5 million; adjusted diluted EPS fell 13.3% to $1.37.

The genuine operating deterioration was gross margin: 15.6% versus 17.0%, a 134 basis-point compression management attributes to pork belly, beef and nut costs moving against the company simultaneously. Cost of products sold rose 3.2% on 1.6% sales growth. Reported SG&A *fell* 0.9%, but adjusted SG&A rose 0.8% — the GAAP decline came from lapping $28.75 million of prior-year antitrust settlements, booking $10.76 million of FY2025 settlement proceeds, and cutting advertising 9% to $148 million. Hormel funded part of FY2025 earnings by reducing brand support in a year volumes were already falling.

**The first-half FY2026 inflection is real and partly mechanical.** Q2 gross margin expanded 70 basis points to 17.4% and total segment profit rose 12.6%. GAAP diluted EPS fell 12.1% to $0.29 solely because of the $61.0 million turkey divestiture loss booked in SG&A; adjusted EPS rose 14.3% to $0.40, beating consensus of $0.36 by 11%. The half is more sober: adjusted EPS $0.74 versus $0.70, gross margin 16.5% versus 16.3%, Retail segment profit down 1.7%. Guided cadence is holding — $0.74 is 50.3% of the $1.47 adjusted midpoint, with a Q1 decline followed by Q2 growth exactly as guided.

**Cash flow and dividend coverage are the central financial tension.**

| | FY2024 | FY2025 | 1H FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | $1,267M | $845M | $528M |
| Capex | $256M | $311M | $151M |
| Free cash flow | $1,011M | $534M | $377M |
| Dividends paid | $615M | $633M | $320M |

FY2025 dividends exceeded FCF by $99 million and cash fell $71 million. The OCF decline was substantially working capital: a $172 million inventory build (higher raw material costs, strategic build, snack-nut recovery following Suffolk, Virginia production disruptions) plus a $69 million payables reduction. First-half FY2026 OCF improved 44% largely because the prior-year period contained a $156 million inventory build versus $23 million this year — a comparison effect, not a structural improvement. The half-year $377 million should not be annualized: the fiscal calendar concentrates promotional and holiday build unevenly, incentive payments landed in the first half, and $100 million of divestiture proceeds sit in investing. As a labeled sensitivity, second-half conversion matching FY2025's full-year pattern puts FY2026 FCF at $550–700 million against approximately $644 million of dividends at the $1.17 rate on 550.3 million shares — coverage of roughly 0.79x to 1.09x.

Payout ratios: 78.5% of consensus FY2026 adjusted EPS of $1.49, 87% of the guided GAAP midpoint of $1.34, 133% of FY2025 GAAP EPS. The FY2026 increase was 1% — the smallest increment that preserves Aristocrat status. The FY2025 risk factors explicitly contemplate the share price declining "if the Company modifies its approach to dividend distributions."

**Balance sheet is investment-grade and not the problem.** At April 26, 2026: total debt $2,856.3 million across four fixed-rate tranches, cash $826.8 million plus $33.1 million of securities, net debt $1,996.4 million — 1.56x approximate adjusted EBITDA of ~$1,282 million, 2.03x FY2025 reported EBITDA of $982.9 million. The $750 million revolver is fully undrawn to March 2030; all covenants including minimum interest coverage are met. Current ratio 1.94, down from 2.47 only because the $500 million 4.800% March 2027 note was reclassified to current maturities, well covered by cash on hand. An automated Altman Z-Score of 1.26 flagging "distress" is a screening artifact — it dropped the retained-earnings and market-equity terms; recomputing the standard public-manufacturer formula from FY2025 10-K inputs gives Z = 3.53, safe zone. There is no solvency question here, and any thesis built on forced deleveraging is unsupported.

Two balance-sheet items do matter. First, goodwill ($4,871.9 million) plus intangibles ($1,585.6 million) equal 81.1% of shareholders' equity, and management has flagged $258.9 million of International goodwill (less than 20% fair-value cushion) plus $683.3 million of indefinite-lived intangibles as at heightened impairment risk. Additional non-cash charges over FY2026–FY2027 are a reasonable base case rather than a tail. Second, the debt is cheap and temporary: blended cash interest is approximately 2.74%, and the 10-Q marks long-term debt fair value at $2.5 billion against $2.856 billion carrying — a $356 million discount quantifying how far below market the 2020–2021 coupons sit. Refinancing the $750 million 2028s (1.700%) and $1,000 million 2030s (1.800%) at a hypothetical 5.0% adds roughly $56.8 million of pre-tax interest, about $0.08 per share after tax at a 22% rate, or 5.4% of consensus FY2026 EPS. Not a solvency issue; a quiet drag on the FY2028–FY2030 earnings bridge that consensus targets do not appear to carry.

## Growth Analysis

The structural picture is low-to-no growth with an unresolved volume problem. FY2025 net sales were 2.8% below FY2022 — roughly −0.95% per year nominal through a high-inflation period. Volume was 4,189.7 million pounds in FY2025 versus 4,288.3 million in FY2024 (−2.3%, organic −0.8%), and fell again to 2,001.6 million pounds in the first half of FY2026 (−2.6%, organic −2.3%).

Beneath that, the segments diverge:

- **Foodservice** grew organic net sales 5.5% in FY2025 and 7.0% in the first half of FY2026, with organic volume growth in both periods — the only segment achieving that. Drivers named repeatedly: customized solutions, branded pepperoni, premium prepared proteins, Austin Blues, Natural Choice, Fontanini, branded bacon. A direct sales force selling labor-saving value-added proteins into operators facing their own labor shortages is a position private label attacks with difficulty. One caution: Foodservice uses market-based pricing on certain lines, so falling commodity costs mechanically reduce Foodservice net sales — management lists this as a risk to its own outlook.
- **Retail** grew organic net sales −0.3% in the half on organic volume down 3.7%. The product-class split is the tell: six-month Perishable revenue rose 5.4% to $4,457.2 million while Shelf-stable fell 7.0% to $1,542.7 million, a $116.8 million decline of which Justin's deconsolidation explains only about $29.4 million. The remainder is the strategic exit from non-core private-label snack nut items plus continued nut/shelf-stable softness — the same portfolio that absorbed the $59.1 million Planters impairment and suffered a Suffolk, Virginia production disruption and a Little Rock, Arkansas fire. On the evidence in these filings, the 2021 Planters acquisition is not performing to the assumptions that underwrote it.
- **International** grew organic net sales 6.1% in the half with segment profit up 14.8%, driven by China and SPAM exports, with capacity expansion at Jiaxing. It is 5.9% of sales and carries the thinnest goodwill cushion, unhedged yuan/rupiah/peso exposure, and Brazilian tax deficiency notices.

FY2026 guidance is organic net sales +1% to +4%, segment profit growth from all three segments, adjusted EPS $1.43–$1.51, capex $260–290 million, gross margin up year over year, advertising up year over year. Consensus sits at $1.49 for FY2026 (above the guidance midpoint) and $1.56 for FY2027, implying roughly 4.7% forward EPS growth. Estimate revisions are flat: current quarter −4.3% over 90 days, current year +1.9%, next year +0.6%. There is no estimate-revision inflection signal here of the kind that would justify treating consensus targets as stale floors.

Two identified FY2027 tailwinds are worth naming. The $22.0 million corporate restructuring is essentially complete — $21.8 million of the total already recognized with $121 thousand of liability remaining at April 26, 2026 — so whatever savings it produces should appear in FY2027 run-rate SG&A. And the turkey manufacturing network repair, credited across Retail, Foodservice and cost of products sold, is a genuine self-help lever with visible effect. Against those: the promised advertising increase has not yet been funded and will consume margin when it is, and the refinancing bridge arrives from FY2028.

The honest read: the entire packaged-food group has derated on impaired earnings, and Hormel sits in the middle of it at 16.4x forward adjusted earnings while yielding roughly 110 basis points more than the group. The higher yield is compensation the market is demanding for thinner coverage and negative volume, not evidence of mispricing.

Total-return arithmetic is the cleanest way to frame this. A 4.78% yield plus 3–5% forward EPS growth, with a flat multiple, produces 8–10% annually. That is a fair-to-slightly-attractive expected return for a staple, and it is why the shares are not an AVOID. What prevents a stronger call is the quality of each component. The yield is funded at 78.5% of adjusted EPS with FCF coverage near 1.0x and a board that just signaled its constraint with a 1% raise. The growth requires the advertising reinvestment to restore Retail volume without eating the margin recovery, which has not been demonstrated. And the multiple faces an identified $0.08-per-share interest headwind from FY2028 that current consensus does not obviously reflect.

Analyst targets are low $25.00, mean $26.875, median $27.00, high $30.00, with sentiment improving (one upgrade to strong buy) and a 2/1/7/0/0 distribution. Notably the *low* target sits above spot, which says the sell side sees limited downside. I would not weight that heavily: this is not the profile — rising estimates plus a compressed multiple — where consensus targets act as stale floors. Estimates are flat, and seven of ten analysts are at hold, so the targets and the price are reflecting the same modest recovery view rather than providing independent confirmation.

Cycle-adjusted, normalized earning power is the strongest bull input. FY2022 adjusted operating margin territory near 10.5% on $12.1 billion of sales would imply roughly $1.27 billion of operating income versus $1.02 billion adjusted in FY2025. If Hormel recovered even half that gap, adjusted EPS approaches $1.75–$1.80 and the stock at 17x would be $30–31. That is the bull case, and it requires reversing four years of volume decline — a hurdle the five-year record argues against.

## Competitive Landscape

FY2025 is best explained as a margin squeeze from both ends. On inputs, three separate commodity complexes — pork bellies, beef and nuts — moved adversely at once, which is precisely the correlation Hormel's stated "balanced business model, with diversification across raw material inputs" is designed to prevent. On outputs, the 10-K acknowledges consumers trading down to private label and value offerings, limiting pricing recapture in Retail. Both are partly sector-wide; press coverage of the CEO transition described relentless inflation causing pain at Hormel "and its competitors."

But the sector-wide framing does not fully absolve the company. Hormel's own proxy-selected peer group returned $103.27 on a $100 base invested at the FY2020 close through FY2025 while Hormel returned $55.66, dividends reinvested — trailing in every one of five years. Part of that is multiple normalization from an elevated 2020 defensive-staples starting point, evident in the FY2021 gap (87.16 versus 104.50) opening immediately. But a company whose net sales are lower than four years earlier, whose volumes have declined three years running, and whose largest segment carries a 6.9% margin has a composition and capital-deployment problem that predates the current inflation cycle.

The competitive trajectory therefore splits. Foodservice is genuinely gaining, on volume as well as price, in a channel where the direct-selling model and labor-saving product design are defensible. Retail is genuinely losing, concentrated in shelf-stable snack nuts and nut butters where private label attacks most easily, where two production facilities were disrupted, and where the Planters trade name has already been written down. Retail is 60% of sales, so its deterioration dominates the aggregate. Add explicit 10-K acknowledgment of GLP-1 weight-management medications as a demand factor for a portfolio anchored in processed meat, snack nuts and nut butters, and the structural direction of Retail volume is unfavorable regardless of how well the turkey network is now running.

## Risk Assessment

**Turkey antitrust trial, October 8, 2026 — the nearest dated binary.** *In re Turkey Antitrust Litigation* (N.D. Ill.) alleges price-fixing from at least 2010 to 2017 with treble damages sought. Summary judgment motions submitted in January 2026 remain pending, and on May 20, 2026 the court ordered the first trial, involving only the direct-purchaser class, to begin October 8, 2026. Hormel has recorded **no liability**, stating loss is neither probable nor reasonably estimable. The company has settled related claims before ($28.75 million in FY2024, further settlements in FY2025), establishing willingness to transact, but the class trial is unreserved and unquantified, and certain direct-action plaintiffs have opted out to proceed individually. Against roughly $644 million of annual dividends and roughly $827 million of cash, an adverse trebled verdict is the one scenario that could force a capital-allocation decision.

**Impairment tail.** Roughly $940 million of book value sits in management's own heightened-risk bucket ($258.9 million International goodwill with under a 20% cushion, $683.3 million of indefinite-lived intangibles). Garudafood is carried at $247.4 million against a $224.2 million quoted market value on the Indonesia Stock Exchange, with an unamortized basis difference of $132.3 million — a no-impairment judgment made twelve months after a $163.7 million write-down of the same asset. Management's sensitivity disclosure (a 10% cash-flow decline or 100bp discount-rate increase produces no material goodwill impairment) is reassuring on goodwill specifically. These charges are non-cash and hit GAAP EPS and book equity, not the dividend or covenants, but they suppress reported earnings and the ratios that screen off them.

**Customer concentration, flat and not improving.** Walmart was 15.6% of FY2025 consolidated gross sales less returns, a customer of both Retail and International; the top five were approximately 38%. The Q2 10-Q discloses one customer above 10% of net accounts receivable. Channel mix is shifting toward Foodservice, but the disclosed concentration metric has not improved, and mix shift is not evidence of reduced concentration.

**Commodity exposure with short hedge tenor.** Hormel does not hedge grain, natural gas or diesel beyond two fiscal years and does not hedge lean hogs beyond one. Open positions at April 26, 2026: 31.0 million bushels corn, 210.0 million pounds lean hogs, 3.8 million MMBtu natural gas, 7.7 million gallons diesel. Management expects beef and nut costs above historical averages in FY2026. The 10-K concedes that market-based pricing on certain lines and pricing lead times may prevent full recovery of cost increases.

**Operational disruption has been recurring rather than episodic.** A single fiscal year contained a Little Rock peanut butter facility fire, Suffolk snack-nut production disruptions, a voluntary Class 1 chicken recall in foodservice channels, and HPAI in the turkey supply chain in both FY2024 and FY2025 — following a Planters Listeria recall the prior year. The 10-K also identifies Austin, Minnesota as supporting "a meaningful amount" of production.

**Governance and execution.** As of the May 28, 2026 10-Q both CEO and CFO were interim, with Paul Kuehneman holding principal financial officer and principal accounting officer roles simultaneously. Ghingo was named permanent CEO on July 28, 2026, effective October 26, 2026 — a real de-risking, and Morningstar called it in line with the previously announced timeline. But three concurrent transformations (T&M, corporate restructuring, an Oracle Cloud ERP migration touching internal controls) have run through this vacuum, and roughly eighteen Item 5.02 officer/director change events since March 2023 sit against a 10-K that touts a 38-person officer team averaging 22 years of service. A less obvious dependency: pork harvest facilities Hormel relies on operate under government waivers to line-speed regulations, and the 10-K states harvest capacity and costs may be negatively affected if those waivers are not made permanent.

**Capital allocation.** Zero shares repurchased in FY2025 and zero in the first half of FY2026, despite 3,677,494 shares remaining authorized and the stock trading as low as ~$21.47 in April 2026. Management's stated waterfall places repurchases last. Meanwhile the FY2025 10-K discloses a ~$28.7 million corporate aircraft construction-and-lease commitment with FY2027 delivery. No officer or director made an open-market purchase between December 2025 and July 2026; two directors sold (Bhojwani 20,200 shares at $24.51 in July 2026; Murano 13,400 at $24.00 in December 2025), while grants dominated — including 436,300 options to the interim CEO on January 2, 2026. The absence of insider buying at multi-year lows is a mild negative given blackout constraints and existing holdings, and I attribute no motive to two individual sales, but combined with zero buybacks at ~12x adjusted EBITDA it does not evidence opportunistic capital deployment.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 37.4% is rich against 30.6% one-year realized volatility, and the term structure is in backwardation (37.4% at one week, 38.5% at one month, 28.2% at three months, 26.7% at six months). ATM IV rose 7.9 points from 29.5% on July 22 while the price fell 2.9% — rising uncertainty into an event, not resolved uncertainty. The one-month tenor (August 28 expiry) prices a ±6.4% implied move with 6.0% put/call skew indicating elevated put demand; that expiry brackets the Q3 FY2026 print, which Hormel flagged as forthcoming on August 3. Last year's Q3 report produced a single-day decline from $29.02 to $25.22, roughly −13%, which explains why the market is paying up for near-dated protection.

Two secondary observations. Put/call open interest of 0.30 remains call-tilted, so the standing structural bet is not bearish — this is event hedging layered on constructive positioning. And the September 25 tenor shows a put/call volume ratio of 4.00, unusually put-heavy; that expiry sits before the October 8 trial start, so it reads more as a hedge into the earnings-plus-litigation-headline window than a position on the verdict itself. Short interest of 6.6% of float at 5.0 days to cover is modest and does not indicate a crowded bearish view. Net: the options market corroborates near-term binary risk without contradicting a fundamentally neutral stance.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $30.50 (+24.6%)

Normalized earning power is materially above trailing. FY2025 adjusted operating margin of 8.41% compares to 10.5% in FY2022; closing half that gap on $12.1 billion of sales adds roughly $125 million of operating income, or about $0.18 per share. The self-help levers are identified and partly proven: the turkey manufacturing network is already contributing, cited across all three segments in Q2; the $22.0 million restructuring is 99% recognized with savings landing in FY2027 SG&A; low-return commodity operations (sow production, whole-bird turkey) have been divested, structurally improving ROIC and cutting HPAI exposure. Foodservice, at a 15.7% margin, compounds organic sales at 7% with volume growth and is winning where private label cannot easily follow. Leverage at 1.56x adjusted EBITDA and an undrawn $750 million revolver leave capacity for a bolt-on that improves mix. A permanent CEO takes office October 26, 2026, removing eighteen months of leadership uncertainty; roughly 18.1 million underwater employee options mean the new management team's incentives require share-price recovery from here. On $1.56–$1.60 of FY2027 EPS at 19–19.5x — a modest re-rating toward the group as coverage improves — the stock is $30–31, plus a 4.78% yield.

### Bear Case — $19.75 (−19.3%)

Volume is the whole argument. Four years of flat-to-down revenue through the highest food inflation in four decades means Hormel cannot price without losing units, and Retail organic volume down 3.7% in the most recent half says the trend is intact. The margin recovery is partly funded by cutting advertising 6% while promising to raise it; funding that promise consumes the gross margin gain. Commodities remain adverse (beef and nuts above historical averages), and Foodservice's market-based pricing means falling input costs mechanically compress its net sales. The dividend is the binding constraint: 78.5% of guided adjusted EPS, coverage near 1.0x on FCF, and a 1% raise that signals the board's own read. Roughly $940 million of flagged intangibles plus a Garudafood stake carried $23 million above market imply further non-cash charges that keep GAAP earnings and book equity under pressure. From FY2028, refinancing $1.75 billion of 1.7–1.8% notes at market rates costs roughly $0.08 per share, 5.4% of current earnings. An adverse October 8 trebled verdict against zero reserve is an unquantified charge to a balance sheet with $827 million of cash and $644 million of annual dividend obligation. On $1.45 of stressed EPS at 13.5x — where derated staples with negative volume trade — the stock is $19.60, essentially the 52-week low of $19.70.

### Base Case — $26.50 (+8.3%, ~13% total return with dividend)

FY2026 lands near the guided adjusted midpoint of $1.47 to consensus $1.49, FY2027 reaches $1.56 as restructuring savings offset the advertising increase, volumes stabilize without recovering, the antitrust matter resolves through settlement at a manageable figure, and the multiple stays near 17x as thin dividend coverage caps re-rating. 17x $1.56 = $26.52, in line with the $27.00 analyst median.

Probability-weighted at 30% bull / 45% base / 25% bear: expected price roughly $25.90, about +5.8% plus the 4.78% yield, or ~10.6% total return. Positive, but not with the margin of safety or confidence that justifies new capital ahead of two dated binaries within eight weeks.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** Existing holders should retain exposure for the 4.78% yield and the modest recovery underway. New capital should wait.

This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for repeating it should be higher. What has changed since the prior review is genuinely constructive — a permanent CEO named for October 26, guidance reaffirmed after a Q2 adjusted EPS beat of 11%, all three segments growing profit, and the restructuring effectively complete — and the overall score moves up accordingly, continuing the recovery from the 4.0 low in May. What has not changed is decisive: volume is still falling, dividend coverage is still near 1.0x, revisions are flat rather than rising, and the two nearest catalysts are both two-sided. The price is 2.9% lower than at the July review on unchanged fundamentals, which improves risk/reward at the margin without altering the rating.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE):**
1. Q3 FY2026 (late August) showing gross margin expansion of 50+ basis points year over year *with* total volume decline narrowing to better than −1.0%, and advertising expense actually up year over year rather than down.
2. Turkey antitrust resolution — summary judgment granted, or settlement at a disclosed amount below approximately $150 million — removing the unreserved liability.
3. Price at or below $22.00, where the yield reaches 5.3% and the multiple falls to 14.1x FY2027 consensus, restoring adequate margin of safety against the bear case.
4. Resumption of buybacks under the existing authorization, which would signal the board sees the equity as the best marginal use of capital.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**

## Conclusion

Hormel is a financially sound, structurally challenged staple in the early part of a self-help recovery. The balance sheet is not in question — 1.56x adjusted EBITDA leverage, an undrawn $750 million revolver, $827 million of cash against a $500 million March 2027 maturity, and a recomputed Altman Z of 3.53. The operating recovery is real: Q2 gross margin up 70 basis points, adjusted operating income up 10.9%, all three segments growing profit, with the turkey manufacturing network repair as the identifiable driver. Foodservice, at a 15.7% margin growing 7% organically with volume, is a genuinely good business.

What holds the rating at HOLD is that the recovery is being built on a shrinking base. Net sales are lower than four years ago, volumes have fallen three years running, Retail organic volume is down 3.7%, and shelf-stable revenue fell 7.0% in the half. The 4.78% yield — the primary reason to own these shares — consumes 78.5% of guided adjusted earnings with free cash flow coverage near 1.0x, and the 1% increase tells you what the board thinks of its own flexibility. Layer on roughly $940 million of flagged intangibles, an equity stake carried above its quoted market price, an $0.08-per-share refinancing headwind from FY2028, an unreserved trebled-damages trial beginning October 8, and a Q3 print in two weeks priced for a ±6.4% move with elevated put demand, and the ~10.6% probability-weighted total return does not compensate for the dispersion.

Hold for the income and the option on normalization. Add at $22.00 or below, or after the Q3 print demonstrates that volume stabilization and advertising reinvestment can coexist with margin expansion.
