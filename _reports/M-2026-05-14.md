## Executive Summary

Macy's is executing the second year of a three-year turnaround ("Bold New Chapter") that has produced the first positive comparable sales growth in three years (+1.5% in FY2025). However, the headline improvement masks deteriorating fundamentals: adjusted EBITDA declined 7% YoY to $1.84B, adjusted EPS fell from $2.64 to $2.32, and FY2026 guidance of $1.90-$2.10 implies continued earnings erosion. The GAAP improvement was significantly aided by a $328M one-time interchange fee settlement.

The company faces a deteriorating consumer environment [unverified — specific CPI, gas price, and savings rate figures not supported by research brief] while operating in an industry facing structural displacement from off-price, direct-to-consumer, and AI-driven commerce platforms. Bloomingdale's (+7.4% comp) is a genuine bright spot benefiting from Saks Global's distress, but it represents roughly 12% of total revenue and cannot offset the broader Macy's nameplate deceleration.

At approximately $18/share, the stock trades 25% below the $24 Arkhouse takeover offer rejected in mid-2024. Real estate assets may be worth $10-12B against a ~$5B market cap. This creates a theoretical value floor, but the market has discounted this gap for years because monetization requires either a takeover or aggressive asset sales that management has shown no urgency to pursue.

## Company Overview

Macy's operates 665 stores across three brands: the Macy's nameplate (mainstream department store), Bloomingdale's (premium-to-luxury), and Bluemercury (luxury beauty/spa). The company employs ~90,134 people and generates $21.8B in annual revenue, supplemented by $669M in credit card revenues (Citibank partnership through 2030) and $188M from Macy's Media Network.

CEO Tony Spring's turnaround plan involves closing ~150 underperforming Macy's locations (80+ completed, remainder extended to 2028), investing in "go-forward" stores (200 now "reimagined"), and accelerating Bloomingdale's and Bluemercury growth. A new China Grove fulfillment center with AI/robotics supports the digital shift (35% of net sales).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** Total net sales declined 2.4% to $21.76B in FY2025, driven by ~$700M in closed-store sales losses. Comparable sales grew +1.5%, a meaningful sequential improvement from -0.9% (FY2024) and -6.0% (FY2023). However, FY2026 guidance calls for comp sales of -0.5% to +0.5%, suggesting the improvement may not hold.

**Profitability:** Gross margin compressed 40 bps to 38.0% on tariff impacts. Operating income improved to $1.03B from $909M, but this included the $328M settlement gain. Adjusted EBITDA declined for the third consecutive year: $2.24B → $1.98B → $1.84B, representing an 18% cumulative decline. This trajectory is the most important metric: the core business continues to shrink even as comp sales turn positive.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $1.43B and FCF of $1.06B remain healthy. FCF yield of approximately 21% at the current ~$5B market cap appears attractive, but FCF must fund $740-800M annual capex, $197M dividends, and debt service on $2.43B of outstanding obligations.

**Balance Sheet:** Debt-to-equity of 3.34x is elevated. The company improved its maturity profile by issuing $500M at 7.375% due 2033 while retiring ~$838M of older debt. Current ratio is healthy at 1.49x. The split credit rating (Ba1/BB+/BBB-) signals that Macy's sits on the boundary between investment grade and junk — any operational deterioration could trigger rating agency action.

**FY2026 Guidance:** Sales $21.4-21.65B (down 1-2%), adjusted EPS $1.90-$2.10. This implies earnings per share falling ~14-18% year-over-year on an adjusted basis, before considering the possibility that the macro environment deteriorates further than management's assumptions.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture contains a fundamental contradiction. Management has declared positive comp sales a turning point, but total revenue, adjusted EBITDA, and adjusted EPS are all declining and guided to decline further.

**Positive signals:**
- Bloomingdale's +7.4% comp (benefiting from Saks Global distress)
- Bluemercury: 20 consecutive quarters of comp growth
- Digital mix expanding (35% of sales)
- Macy's Media Network +7% ($188M, high margin)
- Credit card revenues +25% to $669M

**Negative signals:**
- Adjusted EBITDA declining for 3rd consecutive year
- FY2026 EPS guided below FY2025 adjusted
- Macy's nameplate comp only +0.4% — barely positive
- Absolute revenue declining as stores close
- Tariff headwinds persisting (40 bps gross margin drag)
- Store closure timeline extended from 2027 to 2028 (delay, not acceleration)

The core problem: Bloomingdale's and Bluemercury represent ~12-15% of total revenue. Even with strong performance at those brands, the Macy's nameplate (85%+ of revenue) is generating negligible comp growth in a period where consumer spending should theoretically be supported by low unemployment. When the macro environment deteriorates further, the nameplate is highly vulnerable.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$18/share and ~$5B market cap, Macy's trades at roughly 9x FY2026 guided adjusted EPS midpoint ($2.00). This appears optically cheap but is typical for declining department store businesses with uncertain futures.

**Valuation anchors:**
- Forward P/E: ~9x on $2.00 midpoint guidance
- EV/EBITDA: ~4.1x on $1.84B adjusted EBITDA (approximately $7.5B EV including debt)
- FCF yield: ~21% on $1.06B FCF
- Price/Book: ~1.3x

**Real estate value:** Multiple analyses peg Macy's real estate at $10-12B, with Herald Square alone potentially worth $2-3B. Against a ~$7.5B EV, this implies the operating business is valued at negative-to-zero. However, this gap has persisted for years because: (1) real estate is difficult to monetize without disrupting retail operations, (2) CRE conditions are poor [unverified — specific CMBS distress rate and refinancing wall figures not supported by research brief], and (3) management has shown no urgency to pursue separation.

**Takeover floor:** The $24/share rejected offer provides a reference point, but Arkhouse has not returned with a firm offer despite the stock being 25% cheaper. The takeover premium is not a reliable catalyst without an active bid.

The valuation is not expensive enough to short but not cheap enough to buy given declining earnings, consumer headwinds, and no identifiable catalyst for re-rating within 12 months.

## Competitive Landscape

The traditional department store model faces structural headwinds from multiple vectors:

1. **Off-price retail** (TJX, Burlington, Ross) continues taking share from full-price department stores
2. **Direct-to-consumer brands** bypass the wholesale channel entirely
3. **E-commerce pure plays** (Amazon, Shein, Temu) compete on price and convenience
4. **Specialty retail** captures specific categories more effectively

Macy's competitive position is improving at the margin — reimagined stores outperform the fleet, and Bloomingdale's is capturing luxury demand displaced by Saks Global's distress. But this tailwind is temporary; Saks Global will eventually stabilize under new ownership or liquidation will be completed, removing the disruption benefit.

The company's moat consists primarily of real estate locations (irreplaceable in many cases), brand recognition, and credit card data assets. These are defensive moats that slow decline rather than drive growth.

## Risk Assessment

**Consumer Environment (Critical):** Management noted lower-income consumers are "more choiceful" and flagged uncertainty around gas prices, Middle East conflict, and tariffs as potential headwinds.

**Tariff Uncertainty:** The Supreme Court's February 2026 ruling struck down reciprocal tariffs, with the President subsequently imposing a 150-day 10% tariff on imports. This creates binary outcomes: tariff relief would benefit gross margins, but tariff escalation would compress margins further. Management guidance assumes pre-ruling tariff levels persist.

**Earnings Trajectory:** Three consecutive years of adjusted EBITDA decline with FY2026 guidance implying further erosion. A company with declining earnings and no clear path to earnings growth within 12 months faces continued multiple compression.

**Leverage:** 3.34x D/E with 7.375% coupon on recent issuance. Any credit event in retail would disproportionately impact split-rated issuers.

**Real Estate Market Timing:** Extension of store closures to 2028 reflects poor CRE conditions. Macy's real estate monetization options may be constrained for at least 12-18 months.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
- Takeover premium: Arkhouse or another buyer returns at or above $24/share (representing ~33% upside)
- Tariff relief flows directly to gross margin, beating FY2026 guidance
- Bloomingdale's luxury momentum accelerates as Saks Global exits key locations
- Real estate monetization gains urgency (potential REIT spin or sale-leaseback)
- Consumer proves more resilient than bearish signals suggest

### Bear Case
- Consumer recession materializes, driving Macy's nameplate comps significantly negative
- Tariff escalation compounds margin pressure
- Adjusted EBITDA declines below $1.6B in FY2026, triggering credit rating downgrades
- Real estate values decline with CRE distress, eroding the theoretical floor
- No takeover materializes and stock drifts toward $12-14 (6-7x further-compressed earnings)

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Consumer earnings from major retailers will establish whether the broader consumer is weakening. If those reports confirm deterioration, Macy's — as a pure-play consumer discretionary name with declining earnings — would likely follow. FY2026 guidance already implies EPS decline; any guide-down in the first half would trigger further selling.

**Timing Window:** Q1 FY2026 earnings (likely June 2026). If tariff headwinds and consumer weakness compound, management may need to revise down the $1.90-$2.10 range established in March.

**Short Interest and Squeeze Risk:** Macy's has historically had moderate short interest. Takeover speculation creates squeeze risk — any Arkhouse bid headlines could spike the stock 20%+ in a session. This is the primary risk to a short thesis.

**Borrow and Options Signal:** Without current options data, assessment is limited. The takeover premium embedded in the name likely keeps IV elevated relative to realized.

**Downside Target and Risk/Reward:** Bear case target $12-14 (33-22% downside from $18). Squeeze/takeover risk to $24 (33% upside). Risk/reward is approximately 1:1 at current levels — unfavorable for a short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The takeover overhang creates unacceptable squeeze risk for short positioning. The stock is cheap enough on asset metrics that event-driven buyers provide a floor. The correct positioning is avoidance, not active shorting.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry Criteria:**
1. Two consecutive quarters of positive Macy's nameplate comp growth exceeding +2% (demonstrating turnaround has real momentum beyond Bloomingdale's)
2. Adjusted EBITDA stabilizing or growing quarter-over-quarter
3. Active takeover bid at a meaningful premium (creates a floor for entry)
4. Consumer confidence inflecting positively with 2+ months of sequential improvement

**Timeline:** Headwinds expected to persist through at least Q2 FY2026 (October 2026). The consumer environment must improve before Macy's operational progress translates to financial improvement, and current data suggests it will worsen first.

## Conclusion

Macy's is executing a coherent turnaround that has produced positive comp sales growth for the first time in three years. This is genuine progress. However, adjusted EBITDA and EPS continue declining, FY2026 guidance implies further deterioration, and the consumer environment is worsening.

There is no specific growth thesis or catalyst justifying a 12-month hold. Adjusted earnings are declining. Guidance is for further decline. The consumer environment is worsening. The takeover premium has not materialized in over a year. Real estate monetization is constrained by CRE conditions. The only identifiable upside catalysts (takeover bid, tariff relief) are binary events with probabilities too low to constitute a holding thesis.

At 4.75 overall and with declining adjusted EBITDA in a worsening consumer environment, M is AVOID until either (a) operational metrics demonstrate the turnaround is self-funding at the enterprise level, or (b) a credible strategic transaction creates a defined floor.
