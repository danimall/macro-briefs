## Executive Summary

First Financial Bancorp is a $22B-asset Midwestern regional bank trading at 8.9x forward earnings following a strong Q1 2026 that beat consensus on EPS. The company has completed three acquisitions in under two years (Agile Premium Finance, Westfield Bancorp, BankFinancial Corp), with systems conversion for the final deal imminent in June 2026. Revenue grew 29% YoY in Q1 2026, NIM is resilient at 3.99%, and forward estimates are being revised upward.

The valuation remains compelling at sub-9x forward earnings with a 3.3% dividend yield, particularly given the differentiated fee income model (Bannockburn FX, leasing, franchise lending) that generates approximately 29% of revenue from non-NII sources. As of Q4 2025 (post-Westfield), capital ratios remain above regulatory minimums (CET1 11.32%), credit quality is stable (full-year 2025 NCOs 25bps), and the bank's asset-sensitive balance sheet benefits from the Fed holding rates at 3.50-3.75%. The primary risks — acquisition integration execution, a $37M classified FX receivable, and competitive pressure from larger regionals on loan pricing — are quantifiable and manageable relative to the upside embedded in cost synergies and Chicago market expansion.

## Company Overview

First Financial Bancorp operates through First Financial Bank, an Ohio-chartered commercial bank with approximately 152 banking centers (pro forma including BankFinancial's 18 locations) across Ohio, Indiana, Kentucky, and Illinois. Founded in 1863 and headquartered in Cincinnati, the company differentiates through specialty lending platforms and fee-generating businesses:

- **Commercial Banking**: C&I, franchise lending (First Franchise Capital), insurance industry lending (Oak Street Funding), insurance premium financing (Agile Premium Finance)
- **Bannockburn Global Forex**: FX, commodity hedging, and advisory for middle-market clients; 11 offices nationally
- **Equipment Leasing** (Summit Funding Group): Operating and capital leases
- **Wealth Management** (Yellow Cardinal Advisory Group): $3.9B+ AUM
- **Retail Banking**: Traditional deposit franchise across stable Midwestern markets

The specialty platforms generate national revenue streams largely independent of rate cycles, providing earnings diversification that most sub-$50B banks lack.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Power**: Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.77 (reported $0.71 including noise) grew 22% YoY. According to consensus estimate data, full-year FY2026 consensus is approximately $3.28, revised up from approximately $3.12 over the prior 30 days. FY2027 consensus is approximately $3.43. At $30.44, the stock trades at approximately 9.3x FY2026E and 8.9x FY2027E.

**Net Interest Margin**: 3.99% in Q1 2026, guided stable at 3.99-4.04% assuming no rate cut. The asset-sensitive balance sheet (Year 1 NII +3.2% per +100bps) benefits from Fed holding. NIM has remained in a tight 3.95-4.05% corridor despite rate cuts in late 2025, demonstrating disciplined liability management.

**Fee Income Diversification**: Through 9 months of 2025 (pre-acquisitions fully contributing), fee income comprised ~29% of revenue:
- Bannockburn FX: $43.0M
- Leasing: $60.5M (+25.4% YoY; note: gross leasing income before ~$39.9M in related expenses)
- Wealth Management: $23.3M
- Mortgage: $17.8M

This mix is unusual for a bank of this size and provides meaningful insulation from NIM compression.

**Capital Position**: As of December 31, 2025 (post-Westfield closing), CET1 stood at 11.32%, Tier 1 at 11.60%, and total capital at 15.46% — all well above regulatory minimums. Tangible book value was $15.74/share as of Q4 2025, down from $16.19 at September 30, 2025, reflecting goodwill and intangibles from the Westfield acquisition. The tangible common equity ratio declined to 7.79% (or 8.74% excluding AOCI). Post-BankFinancial capital ratios (Q1 2026) have not yet been reported in SEC filings and will be a key data point at the next earnings release.

**Credit Quality**: Full-year 2025 net charge-offs of 25bps are stable (though Q4 2025 annualized NCOs were modestly higher at 27bps). Allowance at 1.38% of total loans provides adequate coverage. Office CRE exposure of $347.5M (3.0% of portfolio) with one $12.5M nonaccrual is manageable. Classified assets rose 5.1% in 2025 but organic classified assets actually declined — the increase was entirely acquisition-related.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is being driven by three concurrent vectors:

**1. Acquisition Synergies (Near-term, quantifiable)**: Westfield cost savings expected fully realized by Q3 2026; BankFinancial by Q4 2026. The BankFinancial deal reportedly generated an $8.9M bargain purchase gain (acquired below fair value), per Q1 2026 earnings commentary. Combined, these deals added approximately $3.5B in assets and approximately $3.0B in deposits.

**2. Chicago Market Expansion (Medium-term)**: BankFinancial's 18 branches give FFBC its first retail consumer banking presence in metropolitan Chicago. The company is actively hiring commercial bankers, wealth advisers, and private bankers to leverage this new footprint. This is a large MSA where FFBC's relationship model and specialty platforms should differentiate.

**3. Organic Specialty Growth**: Leasing grew 25.4% YoY in 2025. Agile Premium Finance (acquired Feb 2024) is scaling into an established insurance premium financing market. Bannockburn benefits from elevated FX volatility — the current geopolitical environment supports this revenue stream.

**Estimate Revisions**: Based on consensus data, FY2026 EPS has been revised up approximately 5% in the last 30 days and approximately 3.7% over 90 days. FY2027 up modestly over the same period. This positive momentum reflects confidence in integration execution and organic growth.

**Offset**: ICRE portfolio experiencing elevated payoffs due to larger regionals offering aggressive sub-200bps spreads with no covenants. Management expects moderation in Q2 2026 but acknowledges near-term loan growth headwinds from this competitive dynamic.

Against true peers (mid-cap regional banks with comparable profitability profiles), FFBC's sub-9x forward multiple appears to represent a discount given above-average fee income diversification and an approximately 18% ROATCE. The discount likely reflects: (a) small-cap bank neglect, (b) acquisition digestion concerns, and (c) general regional bank macro anxiety from the 2023 bank failure cycle.

If the market re-rates FFBC to approximately 10.5x FY2027E as integration completes and cost savings materialize: $3.43 × 10.5 = $36.00, representing 18% upside plus the 3.3% yield.

## Competitive Landscape

FFBC operates in a competitive Midwestern banking market where three dynamics shape positioning:

**Strengths**: Specialty fee platforms (Bannockburn, leasing, franchise, insurance premium) provide national revenue diversification unavailable to most sub-$50B peers. These platforms grew mid-to-high single digits organically and are rate-cycle resilient. The community banking model with local decision-making drives sticky deposit relationships — adjusted uninsured deposits are only 26% of total.

**Pressures**: Large regionals (PNC, Fifth Third, Huntington) compete aggressively on C&I and CRE pricing with sub-200bps spreads and covenant-lite structures. FFBC explicitly refuses to match these terms, accepting slower loan growth in exchange for credit discipline. FinTech and digital banking (including stablecoins under the GENIUS Act) create long-term deposit competition risk — a risk FFBC explicitly acknowledges in its 2025 10-K.

**Trajectory**: The competitive position is stable-to-improving through acquisition-driven scale gains (now $22B in assets vs. ~$17B two years ago) and geographic expansion into Chicago. However, FFBC is a price-taker in the broader rate environment, and the specialty platforms carry concentration risk (particularly Bannockburn's reliance on a small number of large FX clients, as disclosed in FFBC's risk factors).

## Risk Assessment

**1. Acquisition Integration (Moderate-High)**: Three deals in two years strains management bandwidth. BankFinancial systems conversion in June 2026 is the near-term execution risk. Failure to achieve projected cost savings would reduce EPS by an estimated $0.05-0.10. Mitigation: Management has indicated Westfield integration is tracking well; management has a multi-decade M&A track record.

**2. Bannockburn Classified Receivable ($37M)**: A single terminated FX trade produced this unusual classified asset. Collections are occurring (down from $45M to $37M), but full loss would represent ~$0.30/share impact. Management expects full recovery.

**3. Interest Rate Sensitivity**: A -100bps rate scenario reduces NII by 4.4% in Year 1. If the Fed eventually cuts again (current probability low given inflation dynamics), NIM compression would pressure earnings. Current held rates at 3.50-3.75% are the base case through at least H2 2026.

**4. CRE Exposure in Rising Rate Environment**: $347.5M non-owner-occupied office (3% of loans) in an environment where 30Y rates have breached 5% and CMBS distress rates are at all-time highs. FFBC's portfolio is suburban Class A/B with low nonaccruals, but systemic CRE stress could worsen.

**5. Competitive Loan Pricing**: Larger banks offering unsustainable terms are poaching ICRE borrowers. If this persists, loan growth targets become harder to achieve without compromising credit standards.

**6. Geographic Concentration**: Heavy Midwestern exposure means a regional economic downturn (auto sector, manufacturing) would disproportionately impact asset quality.

**7. Floating Rate Debt Cost**: $150M subordinated notes at 9.30% (SOFR + 509bps) represent a meaningful interest expense drag that persists until redeemed.

**8. Post-Acquisition Capital Depletion**: The Q4 2025 TCE ratio of 7.79% (below management's >8% target) and CET1 of 11.32% reflect capital consumed by Westfield. BankFinancial (all-stock deal) adds further intangible assets. Capital rebuilding may limit near-term buyback capacity despite the existing 5,000,000-share repurchase authorization (under the December 2023 two-year plan effective January 2024; no shares purchased through Q4 2025).

## Conclusion

FFBC offers an asymmetric setup at sub-9x forward earnings with accelerating estimate revisions, a 3.3% yield, and identifiable catalysts (cost savings realization, Chicago expansion) over the next 12 months. The primary risks — integration execution and competitive loan pricing — are manageable given management's track record and capital position. The market appears to be applying a generic "regional bank risk" discount (driven by 2023 failures and current CRE concerns) to a bank with 4.0% NIM, 25bps full-year NCOs, 26% adjusted uninsured deposits, and differentiated national fee platforms.
