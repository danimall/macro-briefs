## Executive Summary

Addus HomeCare is a $2.17B in-home care provider running three segments — personal care (78.4% of Q2 2026 revenue), hospice (17.0%) and home health (4.6%) — across 264 locations in 24 states, serving roughly 62,500 consumers who are predominantly dual-eligible for Medicare and Medicaid. The Q2 2026 print, reported August 3, was solid on the headline: revenue $377.4M (+8.0%), net income $27.6M (+25.2%), adjusted EBITDA $49.2M (+11.9%), adjusted diluted EPS $1.73 (+16.1%) against a $1.69 non-GAAP consensus. Operating cash flow of $92.4M for the first half, against $3.05M of capex, took revolver borrowings from $124.3M at year-end to $64.3M, and with $99.6M of cash the company now sits in a net-cash position with $577.8M of undrawn capacity against a 3.75x net-leverage covenant.

Underneath the headline, the composition of growth has thinned in ways that matter for the multiple. Personal-care same-store average billable census fell to 49,955 from 50,347 (–0.8% in Q2, –1.3% for the half); all of the segment's 6.8% organic growth came from a 4.2% increase in revenue per billable hour (Texas +9.9% effective 9/1/2025, Illinois +3.9% effective 1/1/2026) and a 4.4% increase in hours per client per month. Hospice reported 3.3% revenue growth but segment operating income fell 18.3% to $12.1M with gross margin down 3.9pp, while management characterized hospice organic growth as 11.1% without addressing the profit decline. Home health revenue fell 4.8% with visits down 6.5% and segment operating income down 25.0%, into a CMS CY2026 Medicare rate update of –1.3%. Consolidated gross margin compressed 40bp to 32.2%; the entire operating-margin gain came from G&A leverage (20.8% of revenue from 22.1%) and a halving of interest expense, which supplied $1.8M of the $7.8M increase in pre-tax income.

At 15.4x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $7.52 and roughly 11.4-11.8x EV/adjusted EBITDA with net cash, the stock is priced fairly for a mid-single-digit organic grower carrying extreme payor concentration. Two items keep us at HOLD rather than upgrading on the balance-sheet strength: the Illinois "Persons Who are Elderly" 1915(c) waiver — the vehicle for a state that is 32.1% of revenue and an agency that is 17.7% — expires September 30, 2026 unless CMS approves renewal, with status unconfirmed; and the President/COO seat turned over for the second time in twelve months on August 10, filled on a one-year interim basis by the predecessor, with no permanent successor named. Our probability-weighted expected return of roughly +6% over twelve months, against a bear case 21% below spot, keeps the rating at hold.

## Company Overview

The business model is straightforward and unusually transparent. Personal care bills hourly against authorized hours set by a state agency or managed care organization; hospice bills daily rates across four Medicare-defined levels of care (Addus provides principally routine home care); home health bills 30-day episodes under Medicare's PDGM. Growth comes from three levers: rate increases negotiated with states, hours intensity per client, and tuck-in acquisitions to build density state by state. Location counts at 6/30/2026: personal care 202 (from 199), hospice 40 (from 38), home health 22 (from 23).

Offering all three levels of care in the same market is credible because the payor set is the same and the referral flow between levels is real. Personal care is a locally licensed, fragmented business where scale within a state matters more than national scale, which is why the deal book consists of $0.8M-$21.4M tuck-ins: Great Lakes (Michigan, 3/2025), Helping Hands (Pennsylvania hospice and home health, $21.4M, 8/2025), Gold Horses (Texas, $7.4M, 10/2025), HomeCourt (Indiana, $12.2M, 5/2026). The largest transaction was the Gentiva personal care business bought from Curo Health Services, LLC d/b/a Gentiva, closed 12/2/2024, which drove FY2025 revenue growth of 23.2% and is now fully lapped.

Capital structure is a single $650.0M revolver (Capital One agent) with a $150.0M incremental facility maturing 7/30/2028, $64.3M drawn at 5.40%, $7.9M of letters of credit, secured by a first-priority lien on substantially all assets including subsidiary stock, with Addus HealthCare as borrower and the parent plus substantially all subsidiaries as guarantors. There are no interest-rate hedges, which is immaterial now, since the company's own disclosure puts a 100bp move at $0.4M of H1 net income, or $0.02 per diluted share.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin trajectory (filing facts):**

| FY | Revenue | Growth | Operating income | Op. margin | Net income |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $951.1M | — | $68.7M | 7.2% | $46.0M |
| 2023 | $1,058.7M | +11.3% | $91.0M | 8.6% | $62.5M |
| 2024 | $1,154.6M | +9.1% | $102.7M | 8.9% | $73.6M |
| 2025 | $1,422.5M | +23.2% | $138.6M | 9.75% | $95.9M |
| H1 2026 | $741.0M | +7.8% | $73.0M | 9.9% | $52.7M |

Run-rate organic growth has settled near 8%. The margin expansion from 8.9% to 9.9% over two years is real but its sources are worth separating. Gross margin is going the other way — 32.2% in Q2 2026 versus 32.6%, and 32.0% versus 32.2% for the half. The improvement is G&A leverage: 20.8% of revenue from 22.1% in Q2, helped by a $1.7M decline in professional fees against $4.0M of added administrative cost from acquisitions. Below the line, interest expense halved to $1.7M from $3.5M. Of the $7.8M year-over-year increase in Q2 pre-tax income, $1.8M — 23% — came from lower net interest. That is a durable benefit of deleveraging, but it is a one-time step, not a repeatable earnings engine, and it ends when the revolver hits zero.

**Adjusted versus GAAP.** Q2 adjusted diluted EPS was $1.73 against GAAP $1.49; for the half, $3.35 against $2.85. The single largest add-back is stock-based compensation at $0.17 per quarter (~$9.4M in H1), which is a recurring economic cost. Adjusted EPS therefore overstates distributable earnings by roughly $0.68 annualized. On GAAP, the stock trades at 22.3x FY2025 diluted EPS of $5.215 and roughly 20.4x H1-annualized GAAP of $5.70. Both bases are internally consistent; we use the adjusted basis for peer and consensus comparison because consensus is adjusted, and we flag the gap where it changes the conclusion.

**Cash flow — genuinely strong, but do not annualize H1.** H1 operating cash flow of $92.4M versus $41.5M is a $50.9M swing, and the 10-Q attributes it to receivable and payable timing. Receivables from the Illinois Department on Aging fell approximately $18.6M as that agency's DSO collapsed from 55 days at 12/31/2025 to 27 days at 6/30/2026; consolidated DSO went 38 to 36 days. The prior-year base was distorted the other way, with a $12.8M accounts-payable outflow and a $15.9M receivable build. Strip both swings and the underlying improvement is a fraction of the reported figure. FY2025 gives the better anchor: OCF $111.5M on $1,422.5M of revenue (7.8% margin), FCF $103.8M, conversion of 116% of net income. On the current $2.17B market capitalization that FY2025 free cash flow is a 4.8% yield — respectable support, not a valuation catalyst.

**Balance sheet.** Revolver borrowings fell from $218.4M (12/31/2024) to $124.3M (12/31/2025) to $64.3M, with $60.0M repaid in H1 2026 and no draws. Cash $99.6M. Net debt is negative by roughly $35M before leases, roughly $12M including $47.2M of operating lease liabilities. Against trailing adjusted EBITDA near $187M, gross leverage is under 0.4x versus a 3.75x covenant (electable to 4.25x for a Material Acquisition). Total liabilities are only $308.3M against $1,148.2M of equity.

The offsetting fact is composition: goodwill $1,008.1M plus net intangibles $99.4M equals $1,107.5M, or 76.0% of $1,456.5M total assets, leaving tangible book value at roughly negative $0.7M. Hospice carries $432.9M of that goodwill while generating $26.8M of H1 segment operating income on a declining trend — that is where impairment sensitivity concentrates. This is not a solvency issue; the equity is a claim on acquired cash flows rather than on assets, and a hospice reimbursement or utilization shock would show up twice, in earnings and in a non-cash write-down.

**Tax.** The Work Opportunity Tax Credit expired 12/31/2025 and has not been renewed, so only pre-2026 hires generate benefit. The combined excess-tax-benefit and federal employment credit contribution fell to 1.2% from 2.6% in Q2 and to 3.1% from 4.8% for the half; effective rates rose to 26.9% from 26.4% (Q2) and 25.0% from 24.0% (H1). That is a mechanical 1.5-1.7pp headwind that continues absent Congressional action, worth roughly $0.15-0.20 of annual EPS.

## Growth Analysis

The key disclosure this quarter is in the KPI table, not the income statement. Total average billable personal-care census rose 1.4% to 51,097, but same-store census fell to 49,955 from 50,347, with acquisitions contributing 1,142. For the half, same-store census was 49,663 versus 50,303, down 1.3%. Against that, average billable hours per census per month rose 4.4% to 72.9 and revenue per billable hour rose 4.2% to $26.55. Billable hours rose 5.6%. Reported personal-care organic revenue growth of 6.8% decelerated from 7.4% a year earlier.

So the growth is rate times intensity on a shrinking client base. Three readings are consistent with the data and the filings do not distinguish among them: a deliberate mix shift toward higher-acuity consumers with more authorized hours (value-accretive); shedding low-hour clients that were uneconomic to staff (also fine); or a caregiver shortage capping the company's ability to onboard new clients, which the 10-Q's Recruiting section supports when it states the labor market "continues to be tight" and that a caregiver shortage "may hinder our ability to fully meet the continuing demand." The company does not disclose authorized hours per client or client attrition, so this cannot be resolved from outside. What is not in doubt is the sensitivity: if state rate increases pause — and management explicitly warns there is "no assurance that there will be additional rate increases in Illinois for fiscal years beyond fiscal year 2026 to offset increases in minimum wage" — organic growth would rest on hours intensity alone against a declining census.

Hospice is the segment where reported and underlying diverge most. Revenue rose 3.3% while management reported 11.1% organic growth; cost of service rose 11.0%; gross margin fell 3.9pp to 44.0%; segment operating income fell 18.3% to $12.1M. Multiplying disclosed patient days (360,692) by disclosed revenue per patient day ($190.66) yields $68.8M against $64.2M reported, a $4.5M gap; the same calculation for Q2 2025 produces only a $0.4M gap. The year-over-year swing of roughly $4.1M would by itself account for the entire margin and profit decline. The company's own definition of revenue per patient day excludes "one-time adjustments such as ARPA, Medicare cap or specific situational reserves," and the operating data point toward cap exposure: average discharge length of stay rose 10.5% to 100.1 days (H1 +12.1% to 105.5 days) while admissions were roughly flat at –0.4%, so census growth of 6.6% came from patients staying longer rather than from new referrals. Lengthening stays are the classic driver of aggregate-cap liability (FY2026 per-beneficiary cap $35,361.44).

This inference is derived arithmetic, not a disclosed figure, and it has not been verified against the earnings call or a company statement. It could equally reflect a different denominator convention in the company-defined metric, ARPA timing, or a payor-specific settlement. We treat it as the highest-value open question in the file and do not build it into the base case. What is established as filing fact is that hospice segment operating income fell 18.3% and that the earnings release led with 11.1% organic growth.

Home health is subscale and shrinking: revenue –4.8% in Q2 (–5.9% H1), organic –2.8%, visits –6.5%, segment operating income –25.0%, segment G&A up to 25.4% of revenue from 21.6%, into a –1.3% CY2026 Medicare rate update with ±5% of fee-for-service payment at risk under HHVBP. At 22 locations and roughly $69M annualized, this segment is defended strategically rather than on returns.

The growth optionality that is not in numbers yet is capital deployment. H1 acquisition spend was $12.2M against roughly $89M of H1 free cash flow, which is why debt fell $60M. Per secondary reporting of the Q2 call, management framed a full-year adjusted EBITDA margin of 12-13% while "pursuing larger acquisitions"; that margin figure is consistent with actuals (Q2 13.0%, H1 12.6%) but is externally reported characterization rather than published guidance, since the release contained no formal guidance. The complication is that CMS's May 2026 six-month nationwide moratorium on new Medicare hospice and home health enrollments also blocks de novo branch expansion, and the requirement for a new initial Medicare application when an agency changes majority ownership within 36 months of enrollment or its last ownership change adds friction to acquiring recently traded agencies.

Estimate revisions are mildly supportive: current year $7.04 (+0.8% over 30 days, +0.7% over 90), next year $7.52 (+0.6%/+0.5%), current quarter $1.79 (+1.6%), with next quarter down 1.2% to $1.89. The four-quarter surprise record is two small beats and two in-line prints, so consensus has reasonable visibility and there is no pattern of systematic conservatism to exploit.

## Valuation Assessment

Arithmetic on the frozen $116.06 basis, using the 18,674,300 shares on the 7/28/2026 cover page (the profile field showing 18,293,300 shares and a $2.102B cap appears stale; diluted shares in Q2 were 18,473k):

- Market capitalization: 18.674M × $116.06 = **$2,167M**
- Enterprise value: $2,167M + $64.3M debt − $99.6M cash = **$2,132M** (excluding $47.2M of operating leases)
- EV / FY2025 adjusted EBITDA $180.0M = **11.8x**; on H1-annualized $187.4M = **11.4x** (a sensitivity, not a forecast, given seasonality)
- P/E on FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS $7.04 = **16.5x**; on FY2027 $7.52 = **15.4x**
- P/E on FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS $5.215 = **22.3x**; on H1-2026-annualized GAAP $5.70 = **20.4x**
- FY2025 free cash flow yield = $103.8M / $2,167M = **4.8%**

The supplied peer set is not usable for multiple anchoring: NeoGenomics at –41x, Privia at 94.6x and Alignment at 67.3x are loss-making or early-profitability businesses, and a 67.3x "peer median" carries no information for a 9.9% operating-margin, cash-generative provider. The relevant comparison is home-based care specifically, where the historical trading band for Addus has been roughly 18-25x forward adjusted EPS. At 15.4x FY2027 the stock sits below that band, and the derating has been ongoing — our own assessment of this name has moved from an overall 6.9 in May to 5.5 today, with the valuation dimension cut from 7.0 to the low 5s.

We are deliberately not treating that compressed multiple as a buy signal. There are identifiable structural reasons a rate-taking Medicaid provider should carry a lower multiple than it did in 2023: the CMS 80/20 payment-adequacy rule requiring, by mid-2030, that at least 80% of Medicaid payments for homemaker, home health aide and personal care services flow to direct-care compensation, against a personal-care segment currently running 28.9% gross margin and 8.5% segment G&A; OBBBA's Medicaid matching-fund restrictions, weighted toward expansion states; and an indexed wage floor ($18.75/hour Illinois direct service worker minimum, Chicago's minimum to $17.05 effective 7/1/2026) paired with a discretionary reimbursement rate ($30.80/hour in Illinois). When the cost floor indexes and the price does not, margin compression is the default outcome of inaction. A multiple that reflects that is not a mispricing.

The market may be underweighting the balance sheet. Net cash plus $577.8M of undrawn capacity against a business generating roughly $100M of annual free cash flow is genuine optionality that most small-cap healthcare services companies lack, and it is not visible in the earnings multiple. But optionality only converts to value on deployment, and H1 deployment was $12.2M.

Analyst positioning is constructive: 3 strong buy, 9 buy, 1 hold, 1 strong sell, unchanged for four months; mean target $134.69, median $136.00, range $96-$155. The $96 low is consistent with the Barclays Underweight reported in mid-July (secondary source, pre-dating both the Q2 print and the COO departure). Mean target implies +16%. We treat targets as corroboration, not as an independent floor — forward P/E is not compressed below the growth rate here, so the "targets lag reality" dynamic does not apply.

Our base case is **$128**, or 17.0x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $7.52, a modest re-rating from 15.4x on continued 8% revenue growth, waiver renewal and no further hospice deterioration. That is +10.3% from $116.06. Bull **$153** is roughly 19.5x an above-consensus FY2027 of $7.85, requiring an Illinois FY2027 rate increase, hospice normalization and accretive deployment of a meaningful share of the $578M capacity (+31.8%). Bear **$92** is roughly 13.5x a reduced $6.80, on flat Illinois rates against an indexed wage floor, continued same-store census decline, and a hospice reserve that proves recurring rather than one-time (–20.7%). Weighting base 55%, bull 20%, bear 25% gives an expected return near **+6%** — positive, thin, and below our bar for staged accumulation given two unresolved binaries.

## Competitive Landscape

Demand is not the constraint. Keeping frail, dual-eligible people out of hospitals and nursing homes is the cheap option for state budgets, and demographics push volume higher for decades. The constraints are what states and CMS will pay, how much of that must pass to workers, and whether caregivers can be hired.

Within that frame, Addus's position is mixed and, on trajectory, flat to slightly deteriorating. Payor mix is shifting to managed care: MCOs were 38.6% of company revenue in Q2 from 36.7%, and within personal care 47.1% from 45.3%, while state and local programs fell to 50.3% from 51.4%. The company frames this as alignment with coordinated care. It does not reduce concentration, since Illinois remained 32.1% of revenue and the Illinois Department on Aging 17.7% in the same quarter, and MCOs bring utilization-management tools that state fee-for-service programs largely lack. A rising MCO share is as plausibly a rate and authorized-hours risk as a diversification benefit, and the 10-Q itself lists "the uncertainty regarding the outcome of discussions with managed care organizations" as a risk.

The CMS enrollment moratorium is genuinely two-sided. For six months from May 2026, no new Medicare hospice or home health enrollments or branch additions are accepted nationwide, which makes existing certifications a temporarily scarce asset for incumbents, and also blocks Addus's own de novo expansion in those segments while adding re-application risk to acquisitions of recently traded agencies. Net, it is a modest positive for asset value and a modest negative for organic expansion.

Segment by segment: personal care has real state-level scale and demonstrated ability to win rate increases (Texas +9.9%, Illinois +3.9%), but declining same-store census undercuts a share-gain narrative. Hospice competes nationally against scaled operators with Medicare at roughly 93% of the mix and a hard per-beneficiary cap; lengthening stays with flat admissions is a mixed operational signal. Home health at 22 locations is subscale in a business where subscale operators lose money under PDGM, and the segment's 25% operating income decline shows it.

## Risk Assessment

**Illinois concentration and a dated binary.** Illinois is 40.9% of personal care and 21.3% of hospice; the Illinois Department on Aging was 13.2% of net receivables at 6/30/2026 (25.2% at year-end). The 10-Q states plainly that Illinois' current Persons Who are Elderly waiver expires September 30, 2026 unless CMS approves a renewal. This 1915(c) waiver is the vehicle carrying the $30.80/hour rate. Section 1915(c) renewals are routine administrative actions and lapses are rare, so renewal is the base case, but the filing frames it as conditional, it lands within weeks, and we could not confirm status. Readers should not infer elevated non-renewal probability from our flagging it; they should note that a company with 17.7% of revenue in one agency has a dated regulatory checkpoint that the Q2 release did not address.

**Structural rate-versus-wage squeeze.** Management's own language is the clearest statement of the risk: rates "remain stable under the Illinois fiscal year 2027 budget," but there is "no assurance" of increases beyond FY2026 to offset minimum wage increases, and performance "will be adversely impacted" for any period without an offsetting increase.

**Medicaid funding contraction and the 80/20 rule.** OBBBA (enacted 7/4/2025) contains provisions the company expects to reduce Medicaid spending, with greater restrictions on expansion states, plus the second-order channel of strained state budgets producing eligibility limits and rate cuts. CMS is also withholding or deferring federal Medicaid funding in states judged to have insufficient anti-fraud systems, which can delay provider reimbursement. The 80/20 rule by mid-2030 is the largest long-dated threat to personal-care economics; the company says it is premature to predict the impact, and some states may move sooner.

**ARPA wind-down.** $12.4M of state ARPA funding sits as a "Government stimulus advances" liability, recoupable if unspent or misapplied. Utilized amounts credit against cost of service revenue, so they flatter reported gross margin, and most state plans wind down by 9/30/2026.

**Goodwill and negative tangible book.** $1,008.1M goodwill and $99.4M intangibles against roughly negative $0.7M tangible book. Hospice carries $432.9M of goodwill while segment operating income fell 18.3%. Quantitative goodwill testing is a named critical accounting estimate. $46.6M of state licenses are indefinite-lived.

**Labor supply**, per the 10-Q's own Recruiting language, is the binding constraint on volume in an hourly, authorized-hours business.

**Governance and succession.** On August 10 the company disclosed that Heather Dixon, President and COO, "is no longer with the Company," with W. Brad Bickham — her predecessor — returning as COO on an interim basis for one year. Dixon's employment agreement was dated 8/4/2025, so her tenure ran about twelve months, and she was listed as a participant on the August 4 earnings call six days before the announcement. This is the third Item 5.02 filing in eighteen months. No disagreement, restatement or control deficiency has been disclosed, disclosure controls were affirmed effective at 6/30/2026, and Bickham is credited by the CEO with having built the personal care platform and the acquisition program, so this is a better-than-typical stopgap. We do not infer a cause. But for a company whose case rests on operational execution across 264 locations and integration of a steady deal flow, an unplanned exit of the operating leader with no permanent successor, alongside a combined Chairman/CEO, is a real execution and succession risk.

Compensation compounds the governance question. CEO total compensation went from $5.55M in FY2024 to $12.19M in FY2025 (+120%), NEO average to $3.30M from $1.97M, in a year when the proxy's own cumulative TSR index showed 91.67 for shareholders against 105.84 for the peer group — below the starting value while peers were above it, across the full disclosed period. Plan measures are adjusted EBITDA and TSR; adjusted EBITDA (which excludes stock compensation) rose every year to $180.0M, so a plan weighted toward absolute EBITDA growth pays on acquisition-driven expansion even when relative returns lag. We have not read the CD&A, so whether a one-time or multi-year grant drove the step-up is unestablished; "compensation actually paid" of $11.98M weakly argues against a pure timing artifact.

Insider activity across the 25 most recent Form 4 entries shows zero open-market purchases — only grants and sales, dominated by a 2/23-2/25/2026 cluster at $105-$115 immediately after RSU grants, consistent with sell-to-cover on vesting and small relative to retained holdings (Allison 183,150 shares, Poff 63,692). No director or Section 16 officer adopted or terminated a 10b5-1 plan in Q2. The absence of buying through a decline from roughly $124 to $88 and back is a mild negative, not evidence of a view.

### Options Market Signal

The headline reading — near-term ATM IV 75.6% against 29.5% one-year realized, term structure in backwardation — comes from a three-day expiry in a chain with 67 total contracts across four expirations. In an illiquid single-name chain that far into expiry week, the front-month figure is mechanically unreliable and we do not read it as priced stress. The informative part is beyond the front week: 19.6% at one month, 22.1% at three months, 22.6% at six months, all at or below 29.5% realized. The options market is pricing less volatility over the next two quarters than the stock has actually delivered, which is worth flagging given the unresolved waiver checkpoint on 9/30/2026 and a Q3 print in late October. Six-month implied move is ±13.1%. Near-term skew is labeled call-heavy at –7.9%, three-month +2.9%, six-month –3.4%; the pattern is mixed and we draw no directional conclusion. The prior-snapshot ATM IV move from 39.9% to 58.4% almost certainly reflects the same front-week composition effect. Short interest at 7.8% of float and 4.9 days to cover is moderate — enough to add fuel to a positive surprise, not enough to constitute a squeeze setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($153, +31.8%).** Illinois renews the waiver on time, and the FY2027 budget delivers another rate increase that at least matches the indexed wage floor. Hospice's Q2 revenue reduction proves to be the one-time reserve the arithmetic suggests, and segment operating income snaps back toward the prior 47.9% gross margin, adding $2-3M per quarter of segment profit. Home health stabilizes as CMS rate pressure is offset by density from Pennsylvania and Indiana. Management deploys $200-300M of the $578M capacity on a larger acquisition at a defensible multiple, adding EBITDA at a 9-10x purchase price against an 11.4x trading multiple, and G&A leverage continues as acquired administrative cost is absorbed. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $7.85-8.00 and the multiple returns to roughly 19.5x, near the low end of the historical band.

**Bear case ($92, –20.7%).** Illinois rates stay flat through FY2027 while the direct service worker minimum and Chicago's CPI-indexed floor rise, compressing personal-care gross margin from 28.9% toward 27%. Same-store census keeps declining as the caregiver shortage binds, so hours intensity cannot carry organic growth and personal care decelerates toward 4%. The hospice reduction proves recurring — a Medicare cap accrual driven by structurally lengthening stays with flat admissions — and CMS program-integrity activity in hospice intensifies, raising impairment risk against $432.9M of segment goodwill. ARPA credits lapse after 9/30/2026, the WOTC stays unrenewed, and the interest-expense tailwind exhausts at zero drawn debt. FY2027 adjusted EPS settles near $6.80 and the multiple compresses to 13.5x, roughly where the Barclays $96 target sits.

**What we think happens (base, $128, +10.3%).** Revenue grows 7-8% with personal care mid-single-digit organic plus tuck-ins, hospice recovers partially, home health stays a drag. Adjusted EBITDA margin holds in the 12-13% range management has framed. FY2027 adjusted EPS lands near consensus $7.52, held back by the 1.5-1.7pp tax headwind and gross-margin drift and helped by continued G&A leverage. The waiver renews. The multiple re-rates modestly to 17x as the balance-sheet optionality gets recognized once a larger deal is announced.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Horizon:** 12 months, with three checkpoints — the Illinois waiver decision by 9/30/2026, the Q3 print in late October, and the Illinois FY2028 budget process in spring 2027.

**Targets from the $116.06 basis:** base $128 (+10.3%), bull $153 (+31.8%), bear $92 (–20.7%). Probability-weighted (55/20/25): roughly +6%.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires two of the following:**
1. Confirmation of Illinois PWE waiver renewal together with a stated FY2027 or FY2028 rate action that at least matches the indexed direct-care wage floor.
2. Personal-care same-store census returning to flat or positive year over year for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating that growth does not depend solely on rate and hours intensity.
3. Company confirmation that the Q2 hospice revenue reduction was one-time, with hospice segment gross margin recovering above 46% and segment operating income growing year over year.
4. A price near $100-105 with FY2027 consensus intact at $7.50 or better — roughly 13.5x forward with net cash, at which point the bear case is largely in the price.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of the following:**

## Conclusion

Addus is a well-run, cash-generative business with an unusually clean balance sheet operating in a market where demand is not the question. The problem is that the two things that determine its earnings — the rate a state agency chooses to pay and whether caregivers can be hired — are both outside management's control, and both are currently moving in directions that require rate increases just to hold margin flat. The Q2 print was fine on the surface and thinner underneath: 40bp of gross margin compression, declining same-store personal-care census, hospice operating income down 18.3% with a $4.1M revenue gap we can compute but not explain, home health shrinking, and 23% of the pre-tax income improvement sourced from lower interest expense.

At $116.06 the shares are 15.4x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS and 11.4-11.8x EV/adjusted EBITDA with net cash. That is fair value for this risk profile, not a discount to it. The mean sell-side target of $134.69 implies +16%; our base case of $128 implies +10.3% against a bear case 20.7% below spot, and the probability-weighted return of roughly 6% does not clear the bar for new capital ahead of a dated waiver decision and with the operating leadership seat filled on a one-year interim basis. **HOLD.** Existing owners are compensated by growth and a balance sheet with genuine optionality; prospective buyers should wait for either waiver-and-rate confirmation or a price near $100-105.
