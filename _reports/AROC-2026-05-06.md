## Executive Summary

Archrock is one of the largest U.S. contract natural gas compression services providers with approximately 4.8 million available horsepower (4.6 million operating horsepower) deployed at 95%+ utilization. The company is in a rare position where structural demand growth (LNG exports, data center power, well maturation) is coinciding with industry-wide capacity tightness, enabling pricing power that has expanded contract operations margins from 67% to 73% in a single year. Revenue grew 29% in 2025 and Adjusted EBITDA nearly doubled over two years to $901M, while leverage declined from 3.2x to 2.6x despite approximately $1.36B in total acquisition consideration.

The stock has appreciated 66% over the past year to $39.83, and at 19.5x forward earnings on $2.04 FY2026E EPS, valuation is no longer deeply discounted. However, estimate revisions remain positive (+13% current quarter over 90 days), the industry investment cycle has years to run, and AROC's scale advantages are widening through consolidation. The primary risks are CFO insider selling ($6M in March), elevated near-term options volatility suggesting informed positioning ahead of potential catalysts, and binary geopolitical outcomes for oil/gas that could alter the demand trajectory.

The BUY recommendation rests on a specific mispricing thesis: consensus analyst targets ($38-42) reflect only near-term earnings and fail to capture the compounding value of AROC's recurring-revenue model in a multi-year natural gas infrastructure buildout. The stock should command a premium to historical multiples given the structural demand uplift from LNG and power generation that did not exist at prior cycle peaks.

## Company Overview

Archrock provides natural gas compression services under fixed-fee monthly contracts, operating as infrastructure-as-a-service for midstream operators and E&P companies. The business model is straightforward: AROC owns, installs, operates, and maintains compression equipment at customer sites for recurring monthly fees. Average equipment deployment duration is approximately 6 years, creating highly predictable revenue streams.

**Revenue mix:** Contract Operations (85%) and Aftermarket Services (15%). Geographic concentration is in the Permian Basin and Eagle Ford (~75% of operating HP), with operations across 41 states. The company's ~1,350 employees service approximately 4,800 compressor units.

The compression services model has limited direct commodity price exposure — AROC generates revenue from fixed monthly fees regardless of whether natural gas prices are $2 or $5/MMBtu. What matters is production volume and the physical need to move gas through infrastructure. As wells mature and reservoir pressures decline, compression requirements increase even if drilling activity slows.

### Balance Sheet and Leverage

Total debt stands at $2.41B against a $6.6B market cap, yielding debt/equity of 1.92x. However, leverage relative to cash flows has improved rapidly: net debt/EBITDA declined from 3.2x to 2.6x over the past year despite approximately $1.36B in total acquisition consideration. The January 2026 refinancing ($800M at 6.0% due 2034) and April 2026 redemption of the 6.25% 2028 Notes eliminated near-term maturities and reduced weighted average interest cost.

Regarding liquidity, Archrock reported $579M available at December 31, 2025. Following the January 2026 issuance of $800M in senior notes (proceeds used to repay revolver borrowings), available revolver capacity increased substantially. However, the April 1, 2026 redemption of $800M in 2028 Notes was funded separately. Post-refinancing, the company maintains its $1.5B revolving credit facility (due May 2028) with significant available capacity, though the precise post-April liquidity figure depends on the funding source for the redemption. Interest expense of $165M (2025) consumes approximately 18% of EBITDA — elevated but declining as leverage compresses. The covenant threshold (5.25x total debt/EBITDA) provides substantial headroom at current 2.6x.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.16 is a false positive for this business model. Asset-heavy compression lessors will always show low revenue/asset ratios (0.34x) by design — the equipment generates recurring fees over decades. With $622M operating cash flow and 95% utilization, there is no genuine financial distress.

### Cash Flow and Capital Allocation

Operating cash flow of $622M in 2025 funded $502M in capex ($348M growth + $111M maintenance + $43M other). It is important to note that the company's own definition of adjusted free cash flow — which accounts for the full investing cash outflow of $606.9M including acquisitions — was only $15.2M for the year, and adjusted free cash flow after dividends was negative $126.4M. The gap between simplified OCF-minus-capex ($120M) and company-defined adjusted FCF reflects the substantial acquisition spending during the year.

2026 capex guidance of $400-445M ($250-275M growth, $125-135M maintenance) represents a moderation from 2025's aggressive spending. With the acquisition phase largely complete, free cash flow generation should improve materially in 2026.

The dividend ($0.88/share annualized, 2.3% yield) is well covered. Share repurchases totaled $97M since April 2023. Combined, capital returns are conservative relative to capacity, suggesting upside to shareholder distributions as leverage reaches target levels (likely 2.0-2.5x range).

## Growth Analysis

### Structural Demand Drivers

Four independent secular forces support multi-year compression demand growth:

1. **LNG Export Capacity:** According to EIA forecasts, U.S. LNG exports are projected to grow from approximately 15.1 Bcf/d in 2025 to 18.6 Bcf/d in 2027 (approximately 23% growth) as Corpus Christi Stage 3, Golden Pass, Port Arthur Phase 1, and Rio Grande LNG come online through 2027-2028. Each additional bcf/d of export capacity requires upstream and midstream compression to move gas to export facilities.

2. **Data Center Power Demand:** Growing electricity demand from AI/data center buildout is expected to support incremental natural gas power generation. Management explicitly cites this as a growth driver. EIA forecasts support rising electricity demand and natural gas's role in meeting it, though the precise compression-per-GW relationship depends on pipeline routing and field-level conditions.

3. **Well Maturation:** As shale wells age, gas-to-oil ratios rise and reservoir pressures decline, requiring more compression per barrel produced. The existing U.S. well inventory will need progressively more compression annually regardless of new drilling.

4. **West Texas Associated Gas Overproduction:** Reuters reports extreme gas oversupply in the Permian — "producers must pay to have it taken away." This is directly bullish for compression services: more gas that needs to be moved through gathering systems.

### Acquisition-Driven Growth Slowing to Organic

After approximately $1.36B in total acquisition consideration over 2024-2025 (TOPS at ~$1.0B total including $869M cash and ~6.9M shares valued at $139M; NGCS at ~$349M total including $297M cash and ~2.3M shares valued at $53M), management has shifted focus toward organic growth and integration. The 2026 growth capex budget of $250-275M is directed at new large HP and electric motor drive units responding to customer demand. Fleet high-grading (selling non-strategic small HP units) simultaneously improves fleet economics.

Estimate revisions confirm the trajectory: current quarter EPS estimate has risen 13% over 90 days to $0.52, and next quarter is up 12% to $0.53. Full-year 2026 at $2.04 and 2027 at $2.29 imply 12% EPS growth from 2026 to 2027 on an organic basis.

At $39.83, AROC trades at approximately:
- 19.5x FY2026E EPS ($2.04)
- 17.4x FY2027E EPS ($2.29)
- ~10.1x EV/EBITDA on 2026 guidance midpoint ($890M)

**Peer comparison:** Among contract compression peers, Kodiak Gas Services (KGS) — which reported 4.456M fleet HP at year-end 2025 — trades at a significantly higher P/E multiple. USA Compression Partners (USAC), as an MLP, is less directly comparable on P/E but relevant on EV/EBITDA. Broader oilfield services names like Weatherford (16.7x) and Tidewater (13.3x) are lower, but these are drilling/vessel companies with fundamentally different business models and risk profiles. Against the most comparable peer (KGS — also a contract compression provider), AROC trades at a meaningful discount on EV/EBITDA despite comparable scale.

The discount reflects: (1) AROC's higher leverage, (2) the stock's 66% run over the past year reducing optionality, and (3) near-term earnings timing. However, the underlying business quality — recurring revenue, high utilization, margin expansion — argues for continued re-rating.

The analyst consensus target range of $38-42 implies minimal upside at current prices. This narrow range understates the possibility that compression enters a sustained supercycle: if AROC can sustain 72-73% margins and compound EBITDA at even 8-10% organically, a move to 10-11x EV/EBITDA (reflecting infrastructure-like recurring revenue quality) would support a $45-50 stock price within 12-18 months. The market is pricing this as a cyclical oilfield services company when the business model is closer to an infrastructure REIT.

This is the specific mispricing: consensus treats AROC's forward P/E as a ceiling rather than recognizing that 95% utilization + multi-year demand visibility + declining leverage = multiple expansion, not compression.

## Competitive Landscape

Archrock held the largest fleet among U.S. contract compression providers at year-end 2025, with approximately 4.8 million available horsepower, particularly dominant in large HP units (>1,000 HP/unit, where 74% of fleet by HP is concentrated). However, this leadership position is now contested: USA Compression Partners completed its acquisition of J-W Power in January 2026, adding approximately 1.0 million HP to USAC's prior 3.9 million HP fleet, potentially giving USAC a comparable or larger total fleet. Kodiak Gas Services reported 4.456 million fleet HP at year-end 2025. The trend toward larger centralized compression — driven by multi-well pad development, longer laterals, and centralized gas lift — structurally favors AROC's fleet composition and its concentration in large HP units.

Archrock's utilization of 95-96% reflects tight industry conditions, though utilization varies across competitors — USA Compression reported average utilization of 92.0% in 2025. Nonetheless, utilization across major providers remains elevated by historical standards, supporting pricing power. Contract operations revenue grew 30% in 2025 on a combination of acquisitions, rate increases, and increased average operating horsepower — reflecting favorable supply/demand conditions across the industry.

The consolidation trend (AROC's TOPS/NGCS acquisitions; Kodiak's deals; USAC's J-W Power acquisition) is removing capacity from the market and concentrating it among fewer, more disciplined operators. Post-consolidation, the industry has shifted from fragmented price competition to an oligopoly with rational capital allocation.

AROC's competitive position remains strong. The combination of scale (one of the largest fleets with particular strength in large HP), geographic density (Permian/Eagle Ford concentration), and technology investment (remote monitoring, electric motor drives) supports the moat against smaller competitors who lack the logistics infrastructure to service accounts at AROC's cost structure.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **Geopolitical Oil/Gas Price Volatility:** While AROC has limited direct commodity exposure, sustained oil below $70/bbl would slow Permian drilling activity and reduce associated gas production over 12-18 months. EIA forecasts continued Permian associated-gas growth in 2026-2027 driven partly by rising gas-to-oil ratios, but a prolonged demand shock or geopolitical resolution that substantially reduces global oil prices could slow new well additions at the margin — though already-producing wells still need compression regardless of new drilling.

2. **Leverage in a Rising Rate Environment:** $2.4B in debt with variable-rate revolver exposure. Each 100bps rate increase adds approximately $10-15M in annual interest expense. With 30Y yields above 5%, refinancing costs on future debt issuances could be higher than the 6.0% recently locked in.

3. **Customer Concentration:** Top 5 customers represent 35% of revenue. Loss of a major midstream operator through M&A, insolvency, or competitive displacement would create a step-function revenue decline.

4. **CFO Transition:** CFO Douglas S. Aron notified Archrock of his intention to retire, per an 8-K filed March 25, 2026. He will remain CFO until a successor is named or December 31, 2026, whichever is earlier. His sale of approximately $6M in stock in late March 2026 (reducing holdings by 26%) is consistent with pre-retirement planning but introduces execution risk during the transition, particularly as integration of recent acquisitions continues.

5. **Electric Transition Timeline:** State-level methane regulations (Colorado, New Mexico) could accelerate demand for electric motor drive units, rendering portions of the gas-powered fleet less competitive over a 5-10 year horizon. Replacement capex would be substantial.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV at 54.2% is nearly double the 28.3% historical volatility — the richest implied premium among our coverage set (+25.9pp spread). The term structure is in steep backwardation (54.2% near-term vs. 39.4% 6-month), indicating the market is pricing a specific near-term catalyst or binary outcome. Put skew at 7.9% confirms elevated downside hedging demand.

Since May 1, IV has risen 8.6pp while the stock advanced 2.8% — informed positioning is adding protection alongside positive price action. This divergence warrants caution: the options market sees event risk the equity market is not pricing.

Possible catalysts explaining the IV spike: (1) upcoming earnings or operational update, (2) CFO departure announcement, (3) sector-wide repositioning on geopolitical implications for gas compression demand, or (4) large institutional hedging ahead of position accumulation. The 5.6% short interest with 4.2 days-to-cover is modest and does not explain the IV pattern.

This options signal does not invalidate the fundamental thesis but suggests the near-term risk/reward for new entry is less favorable than 30-60 days ago. Position sizing should reflect the elevated event risk premium.

## Conclusion

Archrock occupies a structurally advantaged position in a tightening market. The combination of 95% fleet utilization, 73% contract margins, multi-year demand visibility from LNG and power generation, and declining leverage creates a compounding profile that consensus does not fully capture at $38-42 price targets.

The primary reason this is a BUY rather than a HOLD despite the 66% price appreciation is that the demand drivers are secular and multi-year, not cyclical — and the market continues to apply cyclical oilfield services multiples to what is functionally a recurring-revenue infrastructure business. The stock trades at 19.5x forward earnings against 12%+ EPS growth and 2.3% dividend yield, which is reasonable for a cyclical name but cheap for an infrastructure compounder.

The specific risks preventing higher conviction are: (1) the elevated options-implied volatility indicating informed participants are pricing near-term event risk, (2) CFO retirement announcement and associated $6M in stock sales that could signal transition uncertainty, and (3) the stock sitting below its 52-week high with limited analyst target upside remaining — suggesting the easy money has been made and further appreciation requires earnings delivery. Position sizing should reflect these near-term uncertainties while maintaining exposure to the structural thesis.
