## Executive Summary

Archrock is the largest provider of large horsepower outsourced natural gas compression services in the United States, operating approximately 4.8 million available horsepower. Long equipment lead times support pricing power, while U.S. natural gas production growth (record 107.7 Bcf/d in 2025), LNG export buildout, and management-cited AI data center power demand provide multi-year demand visibility. Contract operations adjusted gross margins expanded from 67% in FY2024 to 73% in FY2025, driven by pricing power in a supply-constrained environment with 95%+ fleet utilization.

The financial profile is strong: FY2025 revenue grew 29% to $1.49B, adjusted EBITDA reached $901M (60% margin), and leverage declined to 2.6x from 3.2x a year earlier. Q1 2026 generated $91.9M in adjusted free cash flow (versus negative $48.4M a year prior). Full-year 2026 EBITDA guidance of $865-915M supports continued deleveraging and capital returns. At ~21x trailing P/E, AROC appears attractively valued relative to its growth profile and competitive positioning, though peer comparisons are complicated by differing business models across the energy services sector.

The stock has appreciated 66% over 12 months and trades within 2% of its 52-week high. Analyst consensus targets cluster at $38-42, meaning base case upside is moderate (~1-7% to mean target). However, structural competitive advantages, continued margin expansion potential, and secular demand tailwinds (LNG, data centers, maturing well profiles) support further re-rating above current consensus. The primary risk scenario involves a sustained commodity downturn reducing compression demand or a peace deal resolving Hormuz disruptions and deflating energy sector multiples broadly.

## Company Overview

Archrock provides natural gas compression services through two segments: Contract Operations (85% of revenue), where Archrock owns, operates, and maintains compression equipment at customer well sites under fixed monthly fees; and Aftermarket Services (15%), providing parts and maintenance to customers operating their own equipment. The fleet is concentrated in high-value large horsepower units (74% of operating HP in 1,000+ HP units), deployed primarily in the Permian Basin and Eagle Ford (~75% of operating HP).

The business model generates recurring, fee-based revenue with limited direct commodity price exposure. Initial contracts run 12-36 months (up to 60 months for the largest units), then convert to month-to-month, with average equipment stays of ~6 years at a single location. This creates substantial revenue visibility and switching costs, particularly for large-HP installations that require significant logistics to relocate.

## Growth Analysis

Archrock's growth is driven by four reinforcing secular forces:

1. **U.S. natural gas production growth:** Record 107.7 Bcf/d in 2025, with EIA projecting continued growth to approximately 110 Bcf/d in 2026 and 113 Bcf/d in 2027. Even modest production growth translates directly to compression demand as gathering systems expand.

2. **LNG export expansion:** EIA forecasts significant growth in LNG exports through 2027 (approximately 13% in 2026 and 9% in 2027 based on April 2026 STEO data). Each new export facility requires extensive midstream compression infrastructure.

3. **AI data center power demand:** Management cites data-center power demand as a potential driver of natural gas infrastructure demand, referencing it in the 10-K and earnings commentary as a favorable industry fundamental supporting long-term natural gas consumption growth.

4. **Maturing well profiles:** As wells age, reservoir pressures decline and gas-to-oil ratios increase, requiring progressively more compression. The U.S. shale base is aging, creating organic demand growth independent of new drilling activity.

**Fleet high-grading strategy** amplifies economic returns: Archrock sold ~325K HP of small units in 2025 while acquiring/building large HP. Large units generate higher monthly fees, lower per-HP operating costs, and longer customer retention. This explains the margin expansion from 67% to 73% without requiring oil price increases.

**Estimate revisions** show moderate positive momentum: current quarter EPS estimate up 13.1% over 90 days, current year up 8.6%. These are solid but below the +30% threshold that signals a strong fundamental acceleration. The company is growing steadily rather than experiencing a step-function improvement.

**Constraint on growth rate:** Long equipment lead times physically limit how fast the fleet can expand. Archrock has $251M in purchase commitments, mostly due 2026, representing ~7% fleet HP growth. This caps the upside growth rate but also supports a durable competitive position for incumbent fleet holders.

At ~21x forward earnings for a business growing revenue at high-single-digit organic rates (excluding acquisitions), the multiple is reasonable but no longer cheap. The prior entry opportunity at $21-24 offered a much more compelling risk/reward.

## Competitive Landscape

Archrock's competitive position is a key dimension of the investment thesis:

1. **Largest large-HP fleet among outsourced providers:** Scale enables better geographic coverage, faster redeployments, preferred vendor agreements, and bulk purchasing leverage.

2. **Long equipment lead times support pricing power.** Industry reports cite extended Caterpillar engine lead times that constrain new fleet additions across the industry. Archrock's own 10-K characterizes the natural gas compression services business as "highly competitive with low barriers to entry." The lead-time advantage benefits large incumbent fleet operators like Archrock but does not constitute an absolute barrier to competition, particularly from smaller-scale entrants or customers choosing to own rather than outsource compression.

3. **95%+ utilization sustained for multiple years.** This confirms the fleet is right-sized for demand and pricing power exists (you can't increase pricing at 70% utilization; you can at 95%).

4. **Sticky customer relationships:** Average 6-year equipment stays, ~300 customers, integration into customer SCADA systems and operations. Switching costs are real (logistics, downtime, re-engineering).

5. **Fleet high-grading creates a quality gap.** Competitors maintaining older, smaller fleets face a structurally inferior margin profile. Archrock's deliberate investment in large-HP, modern, lower-emission units positions it as the preferred vendor for ESG-conscious operators.

6. **Industry safety leadership:** 0.22 TRIR (total recordable incident rate) is industry-best and matters for contracts with major E&P companies that require safety pre-qualification.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Commodity price sensitivity (indirect but real):** While fee-based contracts provide insulation, a sustained downturn in natural gas production (below $2/Mcf for extended periods) would slow new compression demand and could lead to contract non-renewals. A peace deal resolving Middle East tensions could deflate energy sector multiples broadly.

2. **Leverage at $2.4B** consumes significant cash flow ($165M annual interest expense, ~11% of revenue). While deleveraging is progressing, the debt level limits capital return acceleration and creates vulnerability if EBITDA declines. The $1.5B credit facility matures in May 2028, representing a near-term refinancing event that must be managed.

3. **CFO departure:** Doug Aron has announced his retirement but will continue as CFO until a successor is named or December 31, 2026, whichever is earlier. This introduces management transition risk during a period of active M&A integration (TOPS, NGCS), though the extended transition timeline mitigates sudden disruption. A $3.7M acceleration of long-term incentive compensation expense in Q1 2026 was related to an executive retention agreement.

4. **Tariff exposure:** Imported steel and equipment parts face potential tariff escalation. Management acknowledges pricing passthrough delays. Impact is moderate given long-term contracts and industry-wide cost increases.

5. **Near-term earnings volatility:** Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.42 reportedly missed consensus estimates. The recent earnings track record is mixed, reducing confidence in near-term consensus delivery, though underlying operational metrics (revenue growth, margin expansion, EBITDA) remained solid in Q1.

## Conclusion

Archrock merits a BUY recommendation based on structural competitive advantages (long equipment lead times supporting pricing power, 95%+ utilization, largest large-HP fleet among outsourced providers), strong secular demand tailwinds (LNG exports, data center power demand, maturing wells), demonstrated margin expansion, and reasonable valuation relative to its growth profile. The stock has appreciated significantly, and near-term upside is moderate (7-15% to base case target), which prevents a high-conviction sizing recommendation. The primary risk is energy sector de-rating if geopolitical tensions resolve, but the compression business model's fee-based, must-run characteristics provide more downside insulation than E&P producers. Position at 2% portfolio weight with stops below $32 (representing thesis invalidation at a structural competitive/demand level rather than normal volatility).

The specific constraint on higher conviction is valuation at the margin: the stock trades near analyst consensus mean ($39.67) with only $42 upside to the high target, meaning near-term risk/reward is approximately 1.2-1.5:1 rather than 2:1+. Recent quarterly execution has been mixed, and the earnings track record introduces uncertainty that limits the appropriate conviction level despite the strong structural thesis. This remains a BUY because the competitive positioning and secular growth drivers justify further re-rating, but position sizing should reflect the proximity to 52-week highs and consensus targets.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Corrections to peer valuation comparison (peer median unreliable), FY2026 EPS midpoint ($1.89 vs. $2.04), credit facility maturity (May 2028), post-redemption liquidity reduction, and softened moat language collectively reduce the magnitude of the valuation discount argument and modestly weaken the balance-sheet quality pillar of the BUY thesis.
