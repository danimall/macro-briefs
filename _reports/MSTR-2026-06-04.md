**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Strategy Inc (formerly MicroStrategy) is a leveraged bitcoin holding company wrapped around a small, declining enterprise analytics business. As of April 26, 2026, it held approximately 818,334 bitcoin worth roughly $64 billion, funded by continuous at-the-market issuance of common equity, preferred stock, and convertible notes. The software business — Strategy One and Strategy Mosaic — generated $124.3M of Q1 2026 revenue (about 1% of assets) and, by management's own admission, does not produce enough cash to cover the company's financing obligations.

The investment case has deteriorated in a specific, identifiable way. The entire model depends on issuing stock above bitcoin net asset value, which turns dilution into accretion (rising "Bitcoin Per Share"). That premium has compressed and reversed: MSTR's ~$42.7B market capitalization now sits below the ~$64B value of its bitcoin holdings. When a company trades below NAV and sells shares to buy more of the underlying asset and to pay its own dividends, it transfers value away from existing holders. Management's own key performance metric, BTC Yield, fell from 11.0% in Q1 2025 to 3.2% in Q1 2026, which they explicitly attribute to the stock trading at a lower premium. This is the crux of the problem, and it is visible in the primary filings rather than in commentary.

The stock has fallen roughly 67% from its 52-week high of $457.22 to $126.55, and bitcoin sits underwater against the company's ~$57.7B cost basis for the first time in this regime. We do not treat the price decline alone as a thesis — but here the decline reflects a genuine structural change: the funding flywheel has stalled, the senior dividend/interest stack has grown roughly 25x year over year to $229.5M per quarter, estimate revisions are falling across every forecast period, and binary regulatory and index-exclusion catalysts remain live. We rate MSTR **AVOID**.

## Company Overview

Strategy operates as a hybrid with two mismatched parts. The first, comprising roughly 99% of assets, is a bitcoin treasury operation that grew holdings from 447,470 BTC at year-end 2024 to 672,500 at year-end 2025, 762,099 at March 31, 2026, and 818,334 by April 26, 2026. The second is a legacy business-intelligence software vendor — Strategy One (cloud BI/analytics) and Strategy Mosaic (semantic/data layer) — which is the only reportable segment but is economically immaterial to the investment case.

The software business is in structural transition. Q1 2026 revenue of $124.3M (+11.9% YoY) was carried entirely by cloud subscriptions (+58.7%) offsetting declines in perpetual licenses (-24.3%) and product support (-15.9%). On-premise licenses reach end-of-support December 31, 2026, which forces the remaining base toward cloud. On an operating basis the segment is roughly breakeven; the reported Q1 segment net loss of $489M is almost entirely tax-allocation noise.

The actual engine is the capital-markets machine: continuous issuance of common stock, five series of preferred stock (STRF, STRC, STRE, STRK, STRD), and convertible notes, with proceeds deployed into bitcoin. The company's stated objective is to grow Bitcoin Per Share faster than diluted share count. That objective only works when the equity trades at a premium to the bitcoin it holds.

## Financial Analysis

The income statement is now dominated by bitcoin fair-value swings under ASU 2023-08, adopted January 1, 2025. Earnings are a leveraged bet on the bitcoin price rather than an operating result:

| Metric | Q1 2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Software revenue | $111.1M | $124.3M |
| Unrealized loss on digital assets | $(5.91)B | $(14.46)B |
| Loss from operations | $(5.92)B | $(14.47)B |
| Income tax benefit | $1.73B | $1.92B |
| Net loss | $(4.22)B | $(12.54)B |
| Net loss to common | $(4.23)B | $(12.77)B |
| Diluted LPS | $(16.49) | $(38.25) |

FY2025 net loss was $(3.85)B, with violent quarterly bitcoin marks: Q1 −$5.91B, Q2 +$14.05B, Q3 +$3.89B, Q4 −$17.44B. Trailing EPS is −$15.23 and the trailing/forward P/E of −2.94 is not a meaningful metric for this entity.

The balance sheet at March 31, 2026 shows total assets of $54.3B (down from $61.6B at year-end 2025 on the bitcoin decline). Digital assets are carried at $51.65B against a cost basis of $57.69B — the bitcoin position is approximately $6B underwater. The USD Reserve sits at roughly $2.14–2.21B and is explicitly not segregated and can be eliminated at management's discretion. Long-term debt is $8.16B net, mezzanine preferred is $8.98B, and stockholders' equity is $36.65B, but retained earnings flipped to an accumulated deficit of $(6.47)B.

Cash flow reveals the mechanism. Operating cash flow was barely positive at $14.0M in Q1 2026, helped by USD Reserve interest income. Investing was −$7.25B (bitcoin purchases) and financing was +$7.15B (ATM equity and preferred sales). The company sells securities to buy bitcoin and to pay its own dividends and interest. In Q1 2026, $220.3M of dividends and interest were funded directly via ATM common-stock sales.

Dilution is extraordinary. Share count moved from roughly 13.7M (2023, split-adjusted) to 226M at year-end 2024 to 346M (Class A+B) at March 31, 2026. In Q1 2026 alone, 33.5M Class A shares were sold for $5.29B, and assumed diluted shares reached 378.8M. Standard solvency screens (Altman Z appearing "safe," debt/equity around 0.24) are misleading because equity is inflated by volatile bitcoin marks and the operating business is immaterial. The real solvency drivers are the bitcoin price and continuous capital-markets access, neither captured by conventional ratios.

## Growth Analysis

There is no durable organic growth engine. Software revenue growth of 11.9% is modest and structurally capped by the perpetual-license runoff. The "growth" management markets is Bitcoin Per Share accretion, and that metric depends entirely on issuing equity above NAV. With the stock below NAV, the mechanism now subtracts value per share rather than adding it. BTC Yield falling from 11.0% to 3.2% year over year quantifies the deceleration.

Estimate revisions confirm negative earnings momentum across every period. Current-year EPS estimates were cut 48.4% over 30 days and 55.6% over 90 days; next-year estimates fell 55.7% over 90 days; next-quarter fell 50.1% over 90 days. The current-quarter +79% 30-day revision is noise produced by the impossibility of forecasting bitcoin marks — the same metric was down 14.2% over 90 days. The earnings-surprise record reflects the same problem: a −1,517.9% miss in Q4 2025 and a −770.4% miss in Q1 2026 against a +34,929% beat in Q2 2025. These are not operating surprises; they are bitcoin-price prints landing on dates analysts cannot predict.

Growth from here requires bitcoin to rally enough to restore a premium to NAV and reopen accretive issuance. That is a bet on a single volatile asset, not a company-specific growth thesis.

## Valuation Assessment

MSTR cannot be valued on earnings multiples because reported earnings are bitcoin marks. The relevant anchor is net asset value. Bitcoin holdings of roughly $64B (April 26 basis) against debt of ~$8.16B and preferred of ~$8.5B+ leave a rough equity claim that is being measured against a $42.7B market cap. The market is no longer assigning the historical NAV premium; the shares trade at or below the value of the underlying bitcoin net of the senior stack.

This matters for two reasons. First, the premium that justified the entire equity-issuance model has evaporated, so the company's primary value-creation lever is now value-destructive. Second, the absence of a premium removes the only reason a holder would prefer MSTR over a spot bitcoin ETP, which offers cheaper, pure-play, tax-efficient exposure without a preferred-dividend treadmill or dilution overhang.

Analyst price targets (low $163, mean $351.5, median $345, high $570) sit far above the $126.55 price, and 13 of 14 ratings are Buy or Strong Buy. We do not treat these as floors here. They appear to extrapolate a restored NAV premium and a bitcoin recovery rather than reflecting the present structural dynamics, and they have not been updated to the falling-estimate, below-NAV reality. The guidance to treat analyst targets as floors applies to cheap inflection stocks with rising estimates; this is the opposite — falling estimates, a reversed flywheel, and a security whose value is a leveraged derivative of one asset. The current multiple does not offer asymmetric upside; the asymmetry runs the other way, because below-NAV issuance bleeds per-share value even if bitcoin merely holds steady.

## Competitive Landscape

Strategy's competitive position has weakened in both of its arenas. In bitcoin exposure, spot bitcoin ETPs (live since January 2024) deliver lower-cost, tax-efficient, pure-play access, and the 10-Q candidly notes MSTR's stock fell relative to bitcoin when ETPs launched. Copycat treasury companies — for bitcoin and now Ethereum and other tokens — have fragmented the leveraged-crypto-equity trade and added supply of similar vehicles. Yield-based MSTR option-income ETFs compete with Strategy's own preferred stock for yield-seeking capital.

In software, Strategy is a small, mature BI vendor against far larger platforms (Microsoft Power BI, Tableau/Salesforce, Snowflake, Databricks). It is a declining-license business transitioning to cloud, immaterial against the bitcoin position. The remaining moat is first-mover brand and deep capital-markets access, both of which are eroded precisely by the NAV-premium compression that ETPs and copycats have driven.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is concentrated and several catalysts are live:

1. **Reversed flywheel / reflexivity.** Below-NAV issuance to buy bitcoin and fund dividends is dilutive. This is the dominant idiosyncratic risk and is not a sector truism — it is a specific, currently-active value drain.

2. **Bitcoin underwater vs. cost basis.** Fair value ($51.65B) sits below cost ($57.69B). Further declines erode equity and impair capital-raising capacity, which is the funding lifeline.

3. **Escalating senior dividend/interest stack.** Five preferred series (including STRC at a variable rate raised to 11.5%) plus $8.25B of convertibles. Cash dividends were $229.5M in Q1 2026, up from $9.2M a year earlier. The planned shift to twice-monthly STRC dividends and repeated rate hikes signal effort to hold the flagship preferred near par — a stress tell. The software business covers none of this.

4. **Deferred-tax valuation allowance.** Management placed a full $2.23B valuation allowance against domestic deferred tax assets, signaling caution on realizing gains; future sales above cost basis would trigger cash tax.

5. **Index-exclusion overhang.** MSCI deferred but did not kill its proposal to exclude digital-asset-treasury companies and opened a broader consultation while limiting index share-count increases. Exclusion would force mechanical index-fund selling.

6. **Regulatory reclassification.** If bitcoin were deemed a security, Strategy could face Investment Company Act status; residual uncertainty remains after the March 2026 SEC interpretation.

7. **Custody concentration.** Holdings sit with three custodians (Coinbase 41%, Anchorage 38%, Fidelity 21%), insured for only a fraction, and not FDIC/SIPC members.

8. **Explicit liquidity language.** The 10-Q states the company does not expect the software business to generate sufficient cash flow to satisfy financial obligations or liquidity needs over the next twelve months.

The B- credit rating (S&P, October 2025, affirmed December 2025) is below investment grade. Near-term convertible dilution is limited (conversion prices mostly $200–$672, well above $126.55), but holder put dates in 2027–2029 create refinancing pressure if bitcoin and the stock stay weak.

### Options Market Signal

Options confirm near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 102.8% against 1-year historical volatility of 69.7% — rich by 33.1 points, at the 86th percentile of the chain. The term structure is in backwardation (102.8% near-term vs. 79.3% long-term), the market's signal of an imminent event window. Put/call open interest is 1.74 (put-heavy), and the heaviest unusual activity is concentrated in near-dated puts ($119, $122 strikes for June 5; $112 for June 12; $105 for September 18). The June 8 annual meeting (STRK ratification vote) and ongoing bitcoin volatility frame the window. The options market is pricing two-sided risk with a put tilt, consistent with the structural fragility described above rather than a setup for a clean recovery.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Bitcoin rallies sharply, restoring a NAV premium and reopening accretive issuance. Strategy's first-mover brand and deep capital-markets access let it compound Bitcoin Per Share again, and the equity provides leveraged upside to bitcoin with a yield-instrument overlay. If bitcoin recovers to prior highs and the premium returns, the stock has substantial upside given its leverage, supporting the elevated analyst targets ($345–$570). Genuine insider alignment (CEO "comp actually paid" of −$52.9M in 2025) means management is economically exposed to the same volatility.

**Bear Case.** Bitcoin stays flat or falls. Below-NAV equity sales to buy bitcoin and fund a $229.5M/quarter (and rising) dividend stack steadily bleed per-share value. BTC Yield, already down to 3.2%, compresses further. An MSCI exclusion forces index selling. The deferred-tax valuation allowance and the explicit liquidity language prove prescient. The stock continues toward and below NAV, and the convertible put dates in 2027–2029 add refinancing pressure. In this scenario the stock revisits the 52-week low ($104.17) or lower, roughly 19% below current levels with further downside if bitcoin breaks.

The asymmetry is unfavorable because the bull case requires an exogenous bitcoin rally while the bear case operates mechanically even if bitcoin merely holds steady, through dilutive below-NAV issuance.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The clearest near-term catalysts are the June 8, 2026 annual meeting (STRK ratification, twice-monthly STRC dividend approval) and the next earnings print, both of which will quantify the dividend treadmill and any further BTC Yield compression. A MSCI exclusion decision in a future review is a larger binary catalyst, but it is not firmly dated. The structural value drain (below-NAV issuance) is continuous rather than event-driven.

**Timing window.** The continuous-dilution thesis plays out over quarters, not days. The June meeting is imminent but is unlikely on its own to drive a step-change; the MSCI decision is the higher-impact catalyst but undated, making the carry uncertain.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 12.0% of float (39.5M shares) with only 2.1 days to cover. That is elevated but not extreme, and the low days-to-cover means a sharp bitcoin rally could squeeze the stock quickly. MSTR's beta to bitcoin makes upside spikes of 20–40% in days entirely possible, as seen repeatedly in the price history.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options confirm the bearish lean modestly (put/call OI 1.74, near-dated put concentration), but near-term IV at 102.8% makes put protection expensive, and the balanced put/call skew (-1.3%) shows the options market is not one-sidedly bearish. High IV raises the carrying cost of expressing the short via puts.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target is the 52-week low near $104 (about −19%) with further downside on a bitcoin break. Upside risk on a bitcoin rally is open-ended and rapid. With a reward of roughly 19–30% against squeeze risk that can exceed that in a single bitcoin move, the risk/reward falls short of the 2:1 bar for an active short.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamental thesis is sound — the flywheel has reversed and below-NAV issuance is value-destructive — but shorting a high-beta, leveraged bitcoin proxy with 102.8% IV and rapid squeeze potential is unfavorable on mechanics. This is a stock to avoid owning, not to short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria.** We would reconsider if (a) MSTR re-establishes and sustains a durable premium to bitcoin NAV (mNAV meaningfully above 1.0), restoring accretive issuance and a rising BTC Yield; (b) the company demonstrates a path to funding its preferred-dividend and interest obligations without relying on below-NAV common-stock sales; and (c) MSCI definitively resolves the index-exclusion question in the company's favor, removing the forced-selling overhang. A bitcoin recovery that pushes holdings well above the ~$57.7B cost basis would also materially change the picture.

**Timeline.** The headwinds — NAV-premium compression, dividend escalation, falling estimates, and index/regulatory overhang — are structural and likely to persist for at least 6–12 months absent a sustained bitcoin rally. The June 8 annual meeting and subsequent earnings prints are the near-term checkpoints; the MSCI consultation outcome is the larger undated swing factor.

## Conclusion

MSTR is best understood as a leveraged, actively-managed bitcoin holding company financed by perpetual preferred stock and convertible notes, with a small legacy software business attached. Its central value-creation mechanism — issuing equity above bitcoin NAV to grow Bitcoin Per Share — has reversed now that the stock trades around or below the value of its bitcoin (~$42.7B market cap vs. ~$64B holdings). Below-NAV issuance to buy bitcoin and to fund a $229.5M/quarter and rising dividend stack the operating business cannot cover transfers value away from common holders even if bitcoin merely holds steady. Bitcoin is underwater against the ~$57.7B cost basis, estimate revisions are falling across every period, management has booked a full valuation allowance on domestic deferred tax assets, and live index-exclusion and regulatory catalysts remain unresolved. The offsetting positives — first-mover brand, deep capital-markets access, genuine insider alignment, and full upside leverage to a bitcoin rally — do not outweigh a structurally negative funding dynamic and an unfavorable risk/reward. We rate MSTR **AVOID**.
