## Executive Summary

Twilio has completed one of the more convincing cost transformations in mid-cap software. Non-GAAP income from operations went from $2.5M in 2021 to $924M in 2025, and Q1 2026 extended the trend: gross profit rose $102.7M year over year while total operating expenses rose only $18.1M, producing GAAP operating income of $107.7M against $23.1M a year earlier. The incremental operating margin on incremental gross profit was 82.4%. Dollar-based net expansion improved to 114% from 107%. Free cash flow over the trailing four quarters was $899M on $5,301M of revenue, and the balance sheet carries roughly $1.35B of net cash against $1.0B of low-coupon notes due 2029 and 2031.

Three things keep this from being a buy at $197.52. First, the headline growth rate overstates the economics. The Q1 MD&A discloses that $46.1M of the $234.4M revenue increase was incremental application-to-person carrier fees passed through at cost, with an identical $46.1M added to cost of revenue. Stripping that out, growth was 16.1%, not 20.0% — still an acceleration from 12% a year earlier, and the same adjustment reveals gross margin expanding ~70bp rather than contracting ~100bp, but the reported top line is inflated by a mechanism that produces no gross profit dollars. Second, consensus has stopped moving. FY2026 non-GAAP EPS estimates rose 5.4% over 90 days but 0.0% over the last 30; FY2027 rose 4.0% and 0.2%. The Q2 consensus of $1.32 implies +10.9% year-over-year EPS growth versus roughly +34% in Q1. Management's reported full-year revenue guide of 14-15% sits below the first quarter's 20% actual, which arithmetically requires second-half deceleration regardless of what the exact quarterly bases are. Third, price. At 29.7x FY2027 consensus EPS of $6.65, 5.4x EV/sales and 32x EV/FCF, with the analyst mean target at $210.72 (+6.7%), the market is already paying for the transformation.

The immediate complication is timing. Q2 2026 results land within days, and the options market is pricing a ±14.3% move on the August 7 expiry with near-term ATM implied volatility at 192.4% against 59.1% realized. This is the decisive test of whether 114% expansion and ~16% underlying growth are durable or a one-quarter step-up. Buying two days before that print, at a multiple that already embeds success, is a poor use of capital. HOLD, with a defined entry range and specific upgrade conditions.

## Company Overview

Twilio sells programmable communications: APIs that let developers embed SMS/MMS, RCS, voice, email and video into their own applications, plus packaged software (Flex for digital engagement centers, marketing campaigns, Verify for authentication) layered on customer-data capabilities acquired with Segment. FY2025 revenue was $5,067M, up 13.7%.

Three structural features of the revenue model matter more than the product taxonomy.

**Consumption, not subscription.** In Q1 2026, 75% of revenue was usage-based, up from 73%. Revenue allocated to remaining performance obligations for contracts with durations exceeding one year was $158.5M — about 2.8% of an annualized revenue base. Deferred revenue and customer deposits were $159.6M, and the 10-Q states outright that this balance "is not a meaningful indicator of our future revenue." Neither figure is backlog. Most usage customers have no long-term financial commitment and can reduce consumption at any time without penalty.

**Pass-through-heavy cost structure.** Cost of revenue consists primarily of fees paid to network service providers. Carrier fees are largely outside Twilio's control and in some markets change weekly. When U.S. carriers raise A2P messaging fees, Twilio passes them through at cost — recognizing equal revenue and cost of revenue, which inflates reported growth and mechanically depresses gross margin with no effect on gross profit dollars.

**Outsourced infrastructure.** A substantial majority of cloud infrastructure runs on AWS under an agreement continuing until terminated. Non-cancelable purchase commitments to cloud and network providers totaled $273.2M as of March 31, 2026, with terms up to five years.

Two disclosure changes reduce what outsiders can verify. Twilio collapsed to a single operating and reportable segment (CODM is the CEO; profitability measure is consolidated net income), removing separate visibility into the Segment/CDP business. And it discontinued Active Customer Accounts as a key metric beginning Q1 2026, immediately after the count rose from ~325,000 to ~402,000. Both are defensible under the accounting standards and management's own rationale, but together they remove the denominators needed to decompose growth into new-logo versus expansion sources. DBNE is now the only cohort metric, and it excludes new accounts entirely — the MD&A separately quantifies $70.9M of Q1 revenue from accounts not captured in DBNE.

| Non-GAAP operating income | $924M (18.2%) | $714.4M |
| Operating cash flow | $1,003M | — |

| Stock-based compensation | ~$599M (11.8% of revenue) | — |

Derived from the FY2025 statements: ROA 0.35% ($33.8M ÷ $9,770.9M assets), ROE 0.43% ($33.8M ÷ $7,821.5M equity), net margin 0.67%, operating margin 3.11%, asset turnover 0.52x.

Those profitability ratios are accurate and nearly useless in isolation. GAAP net income is compressed by ~$599M of annual SBC, ~$65M of acquired-intangible amortization, and ~$182M of equity-method losses and impairment. Cash-based measures describe the same company differently: 18.6% FCF margin, ~$1.35B net cash, cash taxes of $21.2M in Q1 2026 on $102.2M of pretax income against $2.9B of federal NOLs. Both sets of numbers are facts; the relevant question is which the equity is being priced on, and the answer is the non-GAAP/cash frame.

### Q1 2026: where the leverage came from

| Metric | Q1 2026 | Q1 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1,406.9M | $1,172.5M (+20.0%) |
| Dollar-based net expansion | 114% | 107% |
| Gross profit | $684.2M | $581.6M (+17.7%) |
| GAAP gross margin | 48.63% | 49.60% |
| Total operating expenses | $576.6M | $558.5M (+3.2%) |
| GAAP operating income | $107.7M | $23.1M |
| Non-GAAP operating income | $278.9M (19.8%) | $213.4M (18.2%) |
| GAAP net income / diluted EPS | $90.1M / $0.57 | $20.0M / $0.12 |
| Free cash flow | $132.3M | $178.3M |

The mechanism is straightforward: gross profit up $102.7M, opex up $18.1M, operating income up $84.6M — an 82.4% incremental operating margin. R&D rose 3.1%, sales and marketing fell 0.1%, G&A rose 11.4%. This is genuine expense restraint rather than a one-time credit, and the MD&A flags no individual expense fluctuation as significant.

Two adjustments are necessary to read this correctly.

**Pass-through.** Removing the disclosed $46.1M of incremental A2P fees: growth was ($1,406.9 − $46.1) ÷ $1,172.5 − 1 = **16.1%**, so carrier fees supplied roughly 3.9 points of the reported 20%. On the same basis gross margin was $684.2 ÷ $1,360.8 = **50.28%** versus 49.60%, i.e. **~70bp of expansion** rather than ~100bp of contraction, and the incremental gross margin on non-pass-through revenue was $102.7 ÷ $188.3 = **54.5%**, above the 48.6% company average. Caveat: the $46.1M is described as incremental year over year, so the Q1 2025 base likely embeds earlier fee layers, and the FY2025 10-K separately cites adverse international messaging mix as a margin headwind. Running the same exercise on FY2025 (where the filing rounds margin to whole percentages) implies margin still fell ~150bp ex-pass-through. The favorable ex-pass-through dynamic is one quarter, not an established trend.

**Free cash flow timing.** FCF fell 25.8% despite net income quadrupling, driven by working capital: Twilio paid $141.0M in March 2026 under its 2025 bonus program (the accrual fell from $136.2M to $34.6M), accrued expenses declined $155.8M versus $94.3M a year earlier, and receivables consumed $74.9M versus contributing $8.5M — roughly $145M of incremental drag. Annualizing Q1 ($529M) would badly understate the business against FY2025's actual $945M. A normalized frame: FY2025's 18.6% FCF margin applied to ~$5.80B of FY2026 revenue implies ~$1.08B, contingent on the margin holding, the bonus re-accruing, and receivable growth moderating. That is a sensitivity, not guidance.

### Capital structure and the share-count question

Debt is $1.0B par — $500M of 3.625% notes due 2029 and $500M of 3.875% due 2031, both issued March 2021, carried at $992.7M, in covenant compliance, and trading below par. Cash and equivalents of $542.0M plus $1,804.3M of short-term marketable securities gives $2,346.3M of liquidity and ~$1.35B net cash. Q1 interest income of $17.5M nearly covered $18.8M of cash interest paid. Current ratio 4.66x; long-term debt/equity 0.13x; total liabilities/equity 0.23x. For a software business, this is a conservative and efficient structure — the debt is cheap legacy paper that the securities portfolio effectively neutralizes.

The buyback deserves precision. From December 31, 2024 to March 31, 2026, Twilio deployed $1,108M ($854.6M in FY2025 for 8.0M shares; $253.4M in Q1 2026 for 2.088M shares) and repurchased ~10.09M gross shares — 6.6% of the opening count. Ending Class A shares fell from 152,610,697 to 152,105,999, a decline of 504,698 or **0.33%**. By subtraction, ~9.59M shares were issued for RSU vesting, options, ESPP and donations. The program is functioning as a cash settlement of equity compensation rather than a per-share value transfer. The counter-reading is equally valid: absent the program the count would have risen ~6.3%, and repurchases averaged $106.8 (FY2025) and $121.4 (Q1 2026) against $197.52 today, which marks well. For modeling purposes, share count should be treated as roughly flat. Reported diluted shares did fall 2.5% (161.79M to 157.76M) as the dilutive overhang shrank from 8.45M to 5.33M and unvested RSUs from 13.66M to 12.07M.

Forward SBC pressure is easing: $885.6M of unrecognized RSU cost amortizing over 2.0 years is a ~$443M annual run-rate versus ~$599M expensed in FY2025 and ~$546M annualized in Q1. On $5.8B of revenue that moves SBC from 11.8% toward ~8-9% — the same buyback dollars should retire more net shares going forward. $892.0M of authorization remained at quarter-end, expiring December 2027, equal to 3.0% of market capitalization.

## Growth Analysis

The verified growth evidence is mixed in a specific way: current-quarter results improved more than forward expectations did.

**Supportive.** DBNE at 114% from 107% is a seven-point improvement in the only cohort metric still disclosed. Underlying growth of 16.1% ex-pass-through accelerated from 12% a year earlier. Four consecutive non-GAAP EPS beats — $1.19 vs $1.07, $1.25 vs $1.11, $1.33 vs $1.26, $1.50 vs $1.30 — average roughly +11%. FY2026 consensus EPS of $5.71 is up 5.4% over 90 days; FY2027 at $6.65 is up 4.0%. Sell-side distribution has improved to 6 strong buy / 20 buy / 4 hold / 2 strong sell.

**Constraining.** Revisions have flatlined over the trailing 30 days (+0.0% current year, +0.2% next year), which matters because the stock has advanced 8.8% since late May. Q2 consensus of $1.32 implies +10.9% year-over-year EPS growth against roughly +34% in Q1. Secondary reporting puts Q2 revenue guidance at +15.5-16.5% and full-year at +14-15%; neither figure was verifiable in a company filing, and they should be treated as unconfirmed. But the direction they imply does not depend on the exact numbers: a full-year guide below the first quarter's actual requires second-half deceleration, and the rough arithmetic points toward ~11% in H2 versus 20% in Q1.

There are two readings. Management has a track record of guiding conservatively on a book with no contracted visibility, and Q1 itself beat by 15.5%. Alternatively, the A2P tailwind laps as prior fee increases anniversary, and the DBNE step-up reflects a level shift from AI voice pilots and the RCS launch rather than a new run-rate. The Q2 print resolves this and it arrives within days.

On the AI question, the structure of Twilio's model is genuinely differentiated from the seat-based application software that has been derating. Twilio meters messages, calls and verifications. If enterprise agents transact with consumers, the transport volume is billable regardless of how many human seats the customer licenses. That is the opposite exposure to the seat-count compression pressuring the application layer. The offsetting facts are that Twilio's customers own the agents and models — Twilio supplies the rails — and that management's own risk language flags agentic systems as a fraud and abuse vector that "may evade existing fraud and compliance controls," which could invite carrier or regulatory response. Nothing in the filings quantifies AI-driven volume, and the reported guidance does not appear to embed it.

The one growth input that concerns me is R&D up 3.1% while the company positions itself as foundational AI infrastructure. Cost restraint that flatters near-term margins is finite; a platform facing OTP substitution, new channel gatekeepers (RCS, Apple Messages for Business) and up-stack competition needs to fund product velocity. Margin expansion sourced from opex discipline rather than gross margin has a ceiling.

## Competitive Landscape

Twilio's moat is real and narrow. The durable pieces are global carrier interconnection at scale (direct authorizations and number resources across many jurisdictions), a licensed-telecom regulatory and compliance stack (KYC/know-your-traffic, robocall mitigation, call authentication, number portability), and a developer-led self-service distribution motion that requires no direct sales coverage. Customer concentration is genuinely low: no customer organization exceeded 10% of revenue in Q1 2026 or Q1 2025, and none exceeded 10% of gross receivables at either balance sheet date. That is a real strength relative to most infrastructure businesses.

The pressures are equally documented. Regional network operators own the termination Twilio rents and offer limited developer functionality on top of their own physical infrastructure. CRM and CX vendors attack from the application layer; standalone CDP vendors attack Segment. The filing concedes relatively low barriers to entry in parts of the market and that some competitors list lower prices. Enterprise customers demand price reductions as volume scales and may build in-house. Moving up-stack into Flex and marketing puts Twilio in competition with its own ISV partners and, per the filings, potentially with customers.

Two mix dynamics cut against the moat narrative. International was 36% of revenue in Q1 2026, unchanged year over year, and the filings state plainly that gross margin on internationally terminating messages is structurally lower because of higher non-U.S. carrier fees — so geographic expansion is inherently margin-dilutive. And the shift toward usage-based revenue (75% from 73%) means messaging and voice grew faster than the subscription products. That is a composition change, not diversification, and it moves the mix toward the lower-margin, lower-visibility, more macro-sensitive portion of the portfolio at the same time segment reporting was collapsed.

Trajectory assessment: DBNE at 114% and the ~70bp of ex-pass-through gross margin expansion argue the position is improving modestly. The mix shift, disclosure narrowing and thin R&D growth argue against extrapolating that improvement.

## Risk Assessment

**Carrier fee treadmill (structural).** The filings acknowledge that even successful pass-through leaves gross profit dollars unchanged while depressing gross margin, and that Twilio "may be unable to respond in these ways in the future without a material negative impact." In some markets fees change daily or weekly while customer pricing cannot. This makes both reported revenue growth and reported gross margin unreliable as standalone signals in every future period, not just this one.

**OTP obsolescence (specific, unquantified).** The 10-Q states that "if user authentication practices evolve to reduce or eliminate the use of one-time passwords, our revenue could be adversely affected." Verify monetizes OTP delivery volume; passkeys and device-bound credentials are actively displacing SMS OTP across the industry. Twilio does not size this exposure. It is the risk I would most want quantified and it is not disclosed.

**Zero contracted visibility against a consumer-linked book.** With 75% of revenue usage-based, RPO beyond one year of $158.5M, and the filing's own statement that usage revenue is "impacted by general consumer sentiment and activity," a consumer spending slowdown transmits within weeks. This is a live macro exposure: the 30-year mortgage rate at a one-year high, a fourth consecutive weekly increase, and a negative housing wealth effect all argue for weaker discretionary transaction volume into 2027. Twilio has no backlog to buffer it.

**Goodwill.** $5,292.5M of goodwill plus $125.4M of intangibles equals 69.6% of $7,784.1M of equity, legacy from SendGrid and Segment. The 10-Q notes adverse conditions or changed assumptions "have in the past resulted, and could in the future result" in impairment. Tangible equity is ~$2,366M.

**Syniverse.** Carrying value fell from $301.6M to $275.1M in one quarter on $27.2M of share-of-losses, up 40% year over year; FY2025 carried $101.2M of losses plus an $80.6M impairment. At the Q1 run rate, straight-line, the remaining carrying value is consumed in roughly ten quarters absent a turn. Separately, Syniverse routes and delivers A2P messages for Twilio, which paid it $31.0M in Q1 (down from $38.7M) — a strategically relevant supplier is financially deteriorating. The losses are non-cash to Twilio and are excluded from the non-GAAP measure on which the equity is valued and management is paid, so this ~$109M annualized drag (2.1% of revenue) is invisible in the headline framework.

**Tax.** In February 2026 the IRS notified Twilio of intent to audit the FY2023 federal return. $42.8M is reserved for non-income-based taxes ($21.8M domestic, $21.0M foreign) with ongoing discussions in jurisdictions where Twilio historically did not collect indirect taxes. The OBBB Act may raise the effective rate as international operations expand.

**Unresolved disclosure item.** An 8-K dated March 24, 2026 carried Item 5.02 (officer/director change) together with Item 7.01 (Reg FD), a pattern consistent with a publicized leadership announcement. The identity and content could not be verified. Timing places it one week before the stock began advancing from ~$122. I treat this as an open verification gap rather than evidence either way.

**Management alignment.** Every observed insider transaction from March 31 to July 6, 2026 was a sale or a grant. Director Andrew Stafman sold 1,675,000 shares for ~$314.8M across May 12 ($193.54) and May 27 ($184.14), reducing a reported 2,295,000-share position to 620,000 — a 73% reduction — while remaining a director and accepting the annual grant. The CEO sold continuously under a 10b5-1 plan including a same-day option exercise-and-sale at $235.88 on June 4; holdings ended at 207,203 shares. The CFO's holdings fell from ~147,000 to 109,724. Programmatic sales carry limited information by design, and diversification after a near-doubling is ordinary. What is not ordinary is that across roughly eleven reporting insiders, over a four-month window in which the stock traded from $122 to $238, no one bought a share on the open market. The exemption that normally discounts insider selling — rapidly rising estimates, +30% or more over 90 days — does not apply here; revisions are +5.4%.

Compensation is keyed to non-GAAP income from operations, organic revenue growth and free cash flow. That plan pays out on pass-through revenue carrying zero gross profit and on operating income excluding ~$500M of annual SBC and ~$109M of Syniverse losses. No GAAP, per-share or gross-margin metric appears among the measures. The Q1 2026 PSU grant covers 210,301 target shares settling at 0-200% through December 2028 with an added relative TSR condition — at maximum 420,602 shares, about 0.28% of shares outstanding, which would leave existing holders at roughly 99.7% of prior ownership. Material to the pay discussion, immaterial to capitalization. Five-year TSR indexes at 42 versus a peer index of 258.

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 192.4% against 59.1% one-year realized (a 133-point spread, 92nd percentile within the chain), the term structure is in steep backwardation (192.4% at two days, 100.2% at sixteen, 79.1% at thirty-seven, 73.8% at 107), and the nearest expiry prices a ±14.3% move. Directionally, positioning leans upside. Put/call volume is 0.27 and put/call open interest 0.49 — both call-tilted — and the single unusual print is 746 contracts of August 7 $215 calls against 123 open interest (6.1x), speculative upside exposure into the release. Short interest is 3.6% of float with 2.1 days to cover, so there is no crowded bearish position and no squeeze fuel.

The practical takeaway: volatility is expensive relative to realized, the event is binary, and the crush after the print is more reliable than the direction. Buying the stock into this is paying for optionality that is currently richly priced. Waiting costs little.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $258 (+30.6%)

Q2 confirms that 114% DBNE and ~16% ex-pass-through growth are durable, and management's 14-15% full-year framing proves conservative as it has for four straight quarters. Operating leverage persists at anything close to the Q1 incremental rate, taking non-GAAP operating margin from 19.8% toward 22-23% and FY2027 EPS above consensus toward $7.20 on the historical ~11% average beat. Agentic AI drives metered message, voice and verification volume — a usage-based rail benefits from agent transactions in a way seat-based software does not. The valuation allowance release puts a large deferred tax asset on the balance sheet and removes the GAAP-optics discount. Declining SBC (from 11.8% of revenue toward ~8-9%) means the remaining $892M of buyback authorization begins retiring net shares rather than offsetting issuance. At 36x $7.20, the stock is $259; Goldman's reported $300 target implies ~45x FY2027, which requires a re-rating to premium infrastructure status.

### Bear Case — $138 (-30.1%)

H2 deceleration proves real rather than conservative: the A2P pass-through anniversaries, RCS and AI voice pilots turn out to be a level shift rather than a growth rate, and the exit rate settles near 11-12%. A consumer slowdown compounds it — with 2.8% of a year's revenue under long-duration contract and 75% of revenue usage-based, there is no buffer, and the filings say so explicitly. OTP substitution begins visibly eroding Verify volume. Gross margin resumes compressing as international mix grows and carrier fees rise faster than customer pricing can follow. Opex restraint at 3% growth proves unsustainable and R&D must be refunded. FY2027 estimates fall toward $6.30 and the multiple compresses to 22x, giving $139. A goodwill review against $5.29B of carrying value, or a further Syniverse write-down, would add a headline shock to a derating already underway. The February 2026 trading range of $105-$120 shows how quickly this name reprices.

### Base Case — $206 (+4.3%)

Twilio delivers roughly in line with its guided range: FY2026 revenue near $5.80B (+14.5%), non-GAAP operating margin holding near 20%, FY2027 EPS at or slightly above the $6.65 consensus, and free cash flow near $1.0-1.1B. At 31x $6.65 — a modest premium to the current 29.7x, reflecting net cash and improving FCF conversion but discounted for the absence of contracted visibility — fair value is $206. That is 4.3% above spot and 2% below the $210 analyst median, which is the honest arithmetic for a good business at a full price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive — operating leverage, 114% expansion, declining SBC, an unused buyback and a metered exposure to agentic volume — but the entry price at $197.52 leaves roughly 4% to base case against 30% to bear case. Existing holders should stay; new capital should wait.

**Reassessment timeline:** immediate. Q2 2026 results land within days and are the single decisive datapoint. The follow-up checkpoint is the Q3 print (late October/early November 2026), which will show whether H2 deceleration is guidance conservatism or reality.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Price in the $160-170 range with fundamentals intact** — that is 24-26x FY2027 consensus of $6.65 and roughly 26x EV/FCF, a level at which the base case offers 20%+ upside rather than 4%.
2. **Q2 revenue growth of 17%+ with DBNE at or above 112% and gross margin ex-pass-through flat-to-up**, confirming that Q1 was a trend rather than a step-up, accompanied by a full-year guidance raise above 15%.
3. **Clustered open-market insider buying**, or a change in buyback pace sufficient to reduce net share count by more than 2% annualized — either would materially change the management-alignment read.
