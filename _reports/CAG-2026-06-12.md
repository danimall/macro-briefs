**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Conagra Brands is a leveraged, multi-year-declining packaged-food company whose stock has fallen 39.9% over the trailing twelve months to $13.45, sitting near its 52-week low of $12.53 and roughly 40% below the $22.55 high. The headline distortion that dominates the file is that FY25's GAAP EPS of $2.40 was inflated by a one-time ~$253M tax-valuation-allowance release (0.3% effective tax rate); normalized earnings power is closer to the ~$2.02 adjusted figure, which has itself declined from $2.66 in FY21. The nine months of FY26 produced a GAAP net loss of $(299M), or $(0.63) per share, driven by a $771.3M goodwill impairment and $197M of brand-intangible write-downs centered on Birds Eye. The trailing P/E of -135.6 is meaningless; the appropriate frame is forward earnings, where consensus FY26 EPS sits at $1.70 and FY27 at $1.61, both falling.

The dominant near-term question is the dividend. At $1.40 annualized against the share price, the yield is roughly 10% — a level the market assigns to distressed payouts, not healthy staples. Operating cash flow has fallen to $895.6M for nine months of FY26 from $1.35B a year earlier, while dividends consumed $502M and capex is guided to ~$450M for the year. A new CEO inheriting a turnover-heavy leadership team and a balance sheet with $7.23B of debt and $9.73B of goodwill (about 50% of assets) has both motive and cover to reset expectations. The most probable identifiable catalyst over the next two quarters is a dividend cut, which would remove the primary support holding the equity.

We rate CAG AVOID with an overall score of 3.9. The contrarian elements are real and worth weighting honestly: directors bought stock on the open market near the lows (Chairman Lenny +25,000 shares at $14.34; Mulligan +17,500 at $14.31), and Q3 FY26 showed a Refrigerated & Frozen organic-volume recovery of +3.9% as supply constraints lapped. These are offset by falling estimates across every horizon, a Refrigerated & Frozen reporting unit written to zero excess fair value (maximum future-impairment risk), customer concentration in Walmart, and uniformly bearish sell-side positioning. CAG is a value trap absent a catalyst that re-rates the multiple, and the clearest catalyst points down.

## Company Overview

Conagra is one of North America's largest branded packaged-food companies, organized into four segments. Grocery & Snacks (~$4.9B FY25 sales) is the highest-margin segment (~21% operating margin), housing shelf-stable brands like Slim Jim, Duncan Hines, and Angie's BOOMCHICKAPOP. Refrigerated & Frozen (~$4.66B) holds Birds Eye, Marie Callender's, Reddi-wip, and Hebrew National, and is the troubled segment driving both the impairments and the recent volume recovery. International (~$957M) is heavily Mexico-weighted with peso exposure. Foodservice (~$1.09B) supplies restaurants. The company also holds a stake in the Ardent Mills flour-milling JV, which contributed $182M of equity earnings in FY25 but is contracting ($98M in nine months of FY26 versus $125M a year prior).

The portfolio is a collection of mature center-store and frozen brands. The competitive advantage rests on brand recognition and shelf presence, which are eroding against well-resourced branded peers and rising private-label penetration as value-seeking consumers trade down. The structural overhang traces to the 2018 Pinnacle Foods acquisition, which added the goodwill and frozen-portfolio weakness that now dominate the balance sheet and the impairment cycle.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is in a sustained decline.** FY25 net sales fell 3.6% to $11.61B, with organic declines in three of four segments. The nine months of FY26 continued the trend, with sales down 4.9% to $8.40B. The one encouraging data point is Q3 FY26's Refrigerated & Frozen organic volume recovery, but this primarily reflects lapping prior-year supply constraints rather than a demand inflection.

**Profitability is distorted by one-time items and eroding underneath them.** FY25 GAAP net income of $1.15B / $2.40 EPS was flattered by the tax-valuation-allowance release; normalized at a ~23% tax rate, underlying EPS is much closer to the adjusted figure. The trajectory of adjusted EPS — $2.66 (FY21) → $2.41 → $2.77 → $2.67 → $2.02 (FY25) — is the cleaner read, and it points down. Gross profit fell to $1.99B for nine months of FY26 from $2.30B, and Q3 adjusted gross margin contracted roughly 112 bps. All four segments posted operating-profit declines on input-cost inflation, unfavorable operating leverage, and increased trade investment.

**The FY26 net loss is structural-balance-sheet, not one-off noise.** The goodwill impairment plus brand-intangible write-downs (Birds Eye −$180M) drove the nine-month GAAP loss. With $9.73B of goodwill remaining (~50% of total assets) and the Refrigerated & Frozen unit carried at zero excess fair value, the impairment cycle is not clearly over.

**Cash flow is softening.** Operating cash flow fell to $895.6M for nine months of FY26 from $1.35B a year prior, reflecting lower profits and the lapping of a ~$140M one-time receivables acceleration in FY25. FY25 FCF was $1.30B (11.2% margin). The decline matters most for dividend coverage: $502M of dividends plus ~$450M of guided capex against an OCF run-rate that has dropped roughly a third leaves thin cushion.

**The balance sheet is leveraged and goodwill-heavy.** Total debt is ~$7.23B against equity of $8.16B (down from $8.93B at FY25 year-end, eroded by impairments). The current ratio of 0.71 is weak, with reliance on commercial paper and a $2.0B revolver maturing June 2030. Covenant headroom remains intact (net debt/EBITDA cap 4.5x; EBITDA/interest floor 3.0x). Management is deleveraging via divestiture proceeds — Chef Boyardee ($607M) and frozen-fish brands ($42M) sold in FY26 — but the November 2025 refinancing locked in higher coupons (~5.0% weighted average on $1.0B of new notes versus the 4.6% notes retired), which raises forward interest expense.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth thesis here. Revenue has declined for multiple years, estimates are falling across every horizon, and the categories Conagra operates in face secular volume pressure. The estimate revision picture is uniformly negative: current-year EPS down 1.5% over 90 days, next-year down 10.9% over 90 days, and next-quarter at $0.37 (down 7.4% over 90 days). This is the inverse of the inflection signal that would support a long position — estimates are being cut, not raised, and the next-year cut is accelerating.

The earnings surprise record is mixed (2 beats, 2 misses over four quarters), with the most recent quarter a 3.6% miss. The forward demand environment is challenged by consumers trading down to private label amid inflation fatigue, retailer pricing pressure (Walmart ~29% of sales), and a flagged demand headwind from GLP-1 weight-loss drugs that disproportionately affects packaged center-store categories.

The only genuine green shoot is the Q3 FY26 Refrigerated & Frozen organic-volume recovery, which is largely a lapping effect rather than reaccelerating demand. The brief's own read — that margin recovery is "losing steam" — captures the problem: even if volume stabilizes, the path back to FY21-level earnings power requires margin expansion that input-cost inflation, tariff exposure, and elevated trade investment are working against.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing and forward P/E of -135.6 reported in the data is an artifact of the GAAP loss and should be ignored. On consensus forward EPS of $1.70 (FY26), CAG trades at roughly 7.9x earnings. Against the peer median P/E of 21.1 and a packaged-foods peer set that includes Campbell's (11.2x), McCormick (8.1x), and Hormel (28.8x), Conagra screens cheap. The ~10% dividend yield versus a 3.9% peer median appears to offer absolute yield support.

The discount is earned, not a mispricing. The valuation framework here requires a specific reason the market is wrong, and the evidence runs the other way. The cheap multiple reflects three structural impairments the market is correctly pricing: (1) declining and unsustainable-at-current-payout earnings power, with the yield itself a market signal that a dividend cut is being discounted; (2) maximum future-impairment risk on the goodwill base with the largest segment at zero fair-value cushion; and (3) a 5-year TSR of 78.6 versus a 113.3 peer group, quantifying company-specific underperformance beyond the sector's shared headwinds.

A yield-support valuation thesis only works if the dividend holds. The math argues it may not: the nine-month dividends against falling OCF and guided capex, a new CEO with incentive to reset, and multiple sell-side analysts openly modeling a cut. If the dividend is reduced 30-50% to free up deleveraging capacity, the primary support for the stock disappears, and the yield-based valuation floor moves down with it. On a normalized ~$2.02 adjusted-EPS base, even a 9-10x multiple yields a fair value only modestly above the current price, and that multiple is not obviously achievable while estimates fall and impairments recur. The consensus mean target of $14.59 implies limited upside, and the recent downgrades (Bernstein to Underperform, PT $12; BofA Underperform, PT $13) place the most current sell-side views at or below the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

Conagra occupies a structurally challenged corner of consumer staples. The core dynamics are: persistent volume declines as consumers trade down to private label; retailer concentration, with Walmart at ~29% of consolidated net sales compressing pricing and shelf space; a flagged GLP-1 demand headwind on center-store categories; and e-commerce lowering barriers to entry for challenger brands. Peers face similar pressures, but Conagra's 5-year TSR underperformance versus its own peer group points to company-specific weakness — chiefly the debt and goodwill burden from the Pinnacle deal and the underperformance of the frozen portfolio.

The Birds Eye write-down is the clearest admission that a core asset is structurally underperforming. The competitive trajectory is deteriorating: market share is under pressure from private label, gross margins are contracting, and the brand-intangible impairments are an accounting acknowledgment that the value of the portfolio's anchor brands is declining. Serial divestiture of low-growth brands (Chef Boyardee, frozen fish) is rational deleveraging but also shrinks the asset base rather than rebuilding the competitive position.

## Risk Assessment

**Impairment risk is elevated and concentrated.** The Refrigerated & Frozen reporting unit was written to zero excess fair value over carrying amount in Q2 FY26, and management explicitly flags "heightened risk of future impairments" if discount rates, margins, or consumer trends worsen. With the goodwill base remaining, this is the single largest balance-sheet risk and the most likely source of further GAAP losses.

**Litigation is a recurring cash drain.** Conagra paid $141.1M in FY26 YTD to settle the bulk of diacetyl/PAM cooking-spray product-liability claims, with ~$44.3M more agreed and a $25M compensatory jury verdict (Esparza) under appeal. A final $16.7M lead-paint legacy payment was made in Q2 FY26.

**Leverage and refinancing.** The $7.23B of debt, a 0.71 current ratio, and refinancing at higher coupons raise forward interest expense. A downgrade would impair commercial-paper access.

**The dividend cut is the dominant near-term risk.** A ~10% yield, a payout consuming most of post-capex OCF, deteriorating cash flow, and a new CEO inclined to reset combine into a credible cut scenario that the market is partially pricing but that would still pressure the stock on confirmation.

**Customer concentration and declining JV earnings.** Walmart at ~29% of sales, and Ardent Mills equity earnings falling to $98M from $125M, remove a high-margin profit stream.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing modest, complacent near-term risk rather than acute stress. Near-term ATM IV of 30.4% sits fair against 28.1% one-year historical vol (2.3% spread), at the 2nd percentile within the current chain, with a contango term structure (near 30.4% vs long-term 38.6%). Put/call skew of 6.7% shows elevated put demand, and put/call OI ratio is 0.67, down from 0.94 over the past month, suggesting some unwinding of hedges. The implied move on the nearest expiry is only ±4.9%. The 38.6% three-month IV with steepening skew is the more telling signal: the market is pricing event risk further out (consistent with a dividend-decision or strategy-reset catalyst from new management), not imminent. Short interest at 13.3% of float (3.5 days to cover) confirms institutional bearish positioning without the crowding that would create acute squeeze risk.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Conagra is a deeply out-of-favor, leveraged turnaround where the contrarian signals are credible. Directors bought stock on the open market near the lows — Chairman Lenny +25,000 shares at $14.34 ($359K), Mulligan +17,500 at $14.31 ($250K), both immediately after the CEO change — which is the most informed bullish data point in the file. Q3 FY26 showed Refrigerated & Frozen organic volume recovering, suggesting the volume cycle may be bottoming. Active deleveraging via divestitures (Chef Boyardee, frozen fish) is reducing the Pinnacle debt burden, and a new CEO (John Brase) could drive a portfolio reset that surfaces value. At ~7.9x forward earnings and a ~10% yield, the stock prices in significant pessimism; if the dividend holds and volumes stabilize, the multiple could re-rate toward the low-double-digits, implying a $17-19 range. Pay-for-performance alignment is genuine (CEO comp-actually-paid swung negative as the stock fell), so management incentives track shareholders.

**Bear Case.** Earnings power is declining (adjusted EPS $2.66 → $2.02 over five years), estimates are falling across every horizon with the FY27 cut accelerating, and the Refrigerated & Frozen unit sits at zero impairment cushion against a goodwill base that could produce further GAAP losses. The ~10% yield is a market signal of a probable dividend cut, with the payout consuming most of post-capex OCF that has fallen to $896M (nine months). Walmart concentration, GLP-1 demand pressure, private-label trade-down, recurring PAM litigation cash outflows, and higher refinancing coupons all compound. The new CEO has both motive and cover to reset the dividend, which would remove the primary support for the equity and reprice it toward the $11-12 range. Sell-side ratings are predominantly Hold-to-Underperform (current distribution: 1 strong buy, 1 buy, 11 hold, 3 sell, 2 strong sell), with the most recent rating actions (Bernstein, BofA) at Underperform.

The bear case is better supported. The bull case rests on the volume recovery being durable (it is largely a lapping artifact), the dividend holding (the math argues against it), and a turnaround executing under new leadership (unproven). The director buying is meaningful but is a single, partially offset signal against a much larger body of deteriorating evidence.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The clearest dated catalyst is a potential dividend cut, most likely to surface around the FY26 year-end results (fiscal year ends late May, so the Q4/full-year report is the natural window) or at a new-CEO strategy reset. A cut of 30-50% is credibly modeled by multiple analysts. A secondary catalyst is continued impairment on the Refrigerated & Frozen unit, which has zero cushion and could be written down again at the next testing date. Neither is precisely dated, which limits the short setup.

**Timing window.** The dividend catalyst resolves over the next one to two quarters as the new CEO sets capital-allocation policy. This is within a reasonable carry window but not imminent, and the timing is not tightly anchored to a known date.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 13.3% of float (49.1M shares), 3.5 days to cover. This is elevated but below the >25% threshold that creates dangerous crowding. The crowd is already partly in the trade, which limits incremental downside capture, and any positive surprise (dividend held, volume recovery confirmed, divestiture at a strong price) could trigger a covering rally.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options confirm bearish lean but near-term IV is fair-to-cheap and complacent, with the P/C OI ratio falling. The options market is not pricing an imminent collapse, and the steeper three-month IV suggests the catalyst is viewed as further out. This raises the cost-effectiveness question for a short — there is no acute near-term mispricing to exploit cheaply via puts, and the equity short carries a ~10% dividend obligation that the borrower pays.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** If a dividend cut confirms, fair value moves toward $11-12 (roughly -11% to -18% from $13.45), with downside to ~$10 in a combined cut-plus-impairment scenario (-26%). Upside risk (dividend held, volume inflection, divestiture catalyst, acquisition interest in a sub-$7B-cap staples name) could push the stock to $16-17 (+19% to +26%). The reward:risk is roughly 1:1 to 1.5:1, below the 2:1 threshold that would justify an active short. The ~10% dividend carry cost on the borrow further erodes the short's economics.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The bearish thesis is well-supported, but the setup fails the short bar. The primary catalyst is not precisely dated, short interest is already elevated (limiting incremental downside), the ~10% dividend creates a meaningful carry cost for the short seller, and the reward:risk falls below 2:1. The director open-market buying near the lows is a specific squeeze/reversal risk that an active short would have to fight. This is a name to avoid owning, not one to actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (what would change the view to HOLD or better):**
- A dividend reset to a sustainable level (a 30-50% cut) that is *digested* by the market, combined with confirmation that the new payout is comfortably covered by post-capex free cash flow. A right-sized dividend would remove the overhang and could make the deleveraging story investable.
- Two consecutive quarters of stabilizing-to-positive organic volume *with* gross-margin expansion, demonstrating the Q3 FY26 recovery is demand-driven rather than a lapping artifact.
- A halt to the impairment cycle — no further goodwill or brand-intangible write-downs at the next testing date — and evidence the Refrigerated & Frozen unit's fair value has rebuilt cushion.
- Positive estimate revisions over a 90-day window, reversing the current broad-based cuts.
- Continued, accelerating insider open-market buying beyond the initial director purchases.

**Timeline.** The headwinds — declining earnings power, the dividend question, impairment risk, and the new-CEO strategy reset — are likely to persist for at least the next two to four quarters. The fiscal Q4/full-year report (results for the fiscal year ending late May) and the new CEO's first capital-allocation framework are the key reassessment points. Until the dividend question resolves and estimates stop falling, the risk/reward does not support ownership.

## Conclusion

Conagra is a leveraged, structurally declining packaged-food business priced cheaply for good reason. The trajectory of the scores reinforces the call — the stock has been rated AVOID across the last three assessments, and the underlying fundamentals (falling estimates, serial impairments, margin compression, a Refrigerated & Frozen unit at zero fair-value cushion) have not improved since. The ~39% price decline over the trailing year reflects genuine fundamental deterioration: the FY26 nine-month GAAP loss, the goodwill and Birds Eye write-downs, the falling OCF, and the CEO change are all real, and the falling consensus estimates confirm the market is repricing the business, not overshooting.

The contrarian case — director buying near the lows, the Q3 volume recovery, deleveraging via divestitures, a new CEO catalyst — is the most interesting part of the file and is the reason this is not a higher-conviction short. It does not clear the bar for ownership. The dominant near-term catalyst is the dividend decision, and the math points to a cut that would remove the stock's primary support. With estimates falling, impairment risk live, and the valuation discount earned rather than mistaken, the appropriate stance is to stay on the sidelines. We rate CAG AVOID, overall score 3.9, and would revisit only on a digested dividend reset combined with margin and estimate stabilization.
