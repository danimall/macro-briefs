**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Six Flags Entertainment (NYSE: FUN) is the entity created by the July 2024 "merger of equals" between Cedar Fair (the accounting acquirer) and legacy Six Flags, trading under Cedar Fair's old ticker. It is North America's largest regional amusement park operator. The equity is now a highly-levered option on a turnaround: ~$5.3B of gross debt sits against a $1.8B market cap, and FY2025 produced a headline net loss of $1.60B (−$15.89/share) that was roughly 95% non-cash goodwill and trade-name impairment. Stripping the writedown out, the business still generated $792M of Adjusted EBITDA and $327M of operating cash flow, but Adjusted EBITDA fell $83M year-over-year and modified EBITDA margin compressed 610bps to 27.1%. The underlying problem is leverage and declining unit profitability, not an income-statement collapse.

The setup is an activist-driven restructuring. Two activists (Dendur, Sachem Head) sit on the board, H Partners is buying, the CEO has changed (Zimmerman out, Reilly in), and management has executed a coherent "shrink to strengthen" plan: sell six low-margin parks to the Enchanted Parks/EPR partnership, cut $50M of quarterly costs, refinance the 2027 note wall, and direct proceeds toward debt reduction. Q1 2026 showed the plan producing early results: same-park attendance +4%, active pass base +6%, per-cap +6%, and a $48M narrowing of the seasonal EBITDA loss. The most credible bullish tell is insider conviction: a director bought $5.9M of stock at ~$23.5 and the Executive Chair bought at $19, both well above today's $17.21.

We rate FUN a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The stock has fallen 48% over twelve months and 31% since mid-June, which improves risk/reward if the operational turn holds. But the equity value is dominated by whether the debt can be delevered before the January 2027 Partnership Parks cash call, and that will not be resolved by the price data available today. Q1 is seasonally immaterial; the July–August peak (roughly 70% of annual attendance) is the genuine test, and management is explicitly cautious about a "more promotional environment" pressuring admission yields. We want peak-season confirmation and disclosed divestiture terms before moving to a directional call.

## Company Overview

FUN operates the largest regional amusement park portfolio in North America. The FY2025 10-K reported 41 parks (26 amusement, 15 water, 9 resorts) across the US, Canada and Mexico. Following the divestiture program, the operating footprint shrinks to roughly 21 amusement parks, 14 water parks and 8 resorts across 13 states plus Canada and Mexico.

Revenue is driven by admissions, in-park food/merchandise/games, and accommodations/extra-charge/out-of-park spend. FY2025 in-park revenue was $2.93B against $255M out-of-park. The company reports a single segment and is managed park-by-park. Competitive assets include irreplaceable regional real estate, exclusive Warner Bros./DC theme-park rights (US ex-FL/Vegas, plus Canada/Mexico), exclusive Peanuts rights (US/Canada), and Looney Tunes licensing, alongside international licensing (Qiddiya City / Aquarabia, Saudi Arabia).

The cost structure is highly fixed (full-time labor, maintenance, insurance, utilities and leases do not vary with attendance) and highly seasonal, with roughly 70% of attendance concentrated in Q2–Q3 and peaking in July–August. This operating leverage cuts both ways: attendance misses are disproportionately damaging, and attendance gains flow through quickly.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 headline versus underlying.** Revenue was $3.10B, up 14.4% year-over-year, but this reflects a full year of the combined company versus only a half-year of legacy Six Flags in 2024, not organic growth. Attendance was 47.4M (+13.8%) and per-cap spending $61.90 (+1.0%, essentially flat). The operating loss of −$1.375B (versus +$310.5M in 2024) was driven by a $1.518B Q3 goodwill and trade-name impairment on the legacy Six Flags and Schlitterbahn reporting units. Goodwill fell from $3.30B to $2.07B. Net loss was −$1.60B.

The impairment is a management admission that the merger overpaid or underdelivered. The more important number is Adjusted EBITDA of $792M, down $83M year-over-year, with modified EBITDA margin down 610bps to 27.1%. Underlying profitability deteriorated on lower attendance despite cost cuts. Legacy Cedar Fair parks alone saw a 2.1M-visit organic decline in 2025 on Midwest weather and fewer operating days.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow was +$327M (down from $373M); capex was $480M, producing FCF of −$152M. The negative free cash flow reflects elevated merger-integration capex. The balance sheet is the dominant issue: ~$5.2B gross debt at year-end (rising to ~$5.39B by Q1'26), equity of $550M (down from $2.04B pre-impairment), D/E near 14x, current ratio 0.69, and cash of just $91M. The Altman Z of −22.6 is distorted by the impairment-driven negative EBIT and overstates distress, but the leverage and liquidity are real. There is no dividend; restricted-payment covenants are breached with pro forma leverage above 5.5x.

**Q1 2026 — early operational turn.** Net revenue rose 12% to $225.6M; same-park attendance +4%, per-cap +6% ($69.26), active pass base +6%. The Adjusted EBITDA loss narrowed $48M to −$123M (Q1 is a seasonal loss). Operating costs were cut $50.4M (−12%), including full-time wages −$15.2M, maintenance −$9.5M, supplies −$8.2M and SG&A −$17.5M. Through May 3, same-park attendance was +4% and the active pass base ~5M units. Impairments continued: $38.6M goodwill impairment plus a $28.0M loss on a disposal group in Q1'26, taking equity down to $279M.

2026 guidance: capex $400–425M, cash interest $320–330M, cash taxes $25–30M. The cash-interest step-up comes from the January 2026 refinancing, which swapped 5.375–5.5% notes for $1.0B of 8.625% notes due 2032, raising the cost of capital and pressuring free cash flow available for deleveraging.

## Growth Analysis

Reported revenue growth is a merger-accounting artifact and should be ignored. The real growth question is whether same-park attendance and per-cap spending can recover. Q1 2026 provides the first encouraging data point, and the 6% larger active pass base builds deferred revenue into the peak season. Management is re-tooling legacy Six Flags parks "to Cedar Fair standards," which implicitly concedes those parks were the underperformers behind the impairment.

The earnings surprise record is volatile: 2 beats and 2 misses over four quarters, with a −291.7% miss in Q4'25 and a +40.8% beat in Q1'26. This reflects a seasonal, weather-dependent business where quarterly EPS swings wildly around a small base. Estimate revisions are mixed: the current quarter is roughly flat (+8.3% over 90 days, −2.5% over 30), next quarter has been cut (−8.2% over 90 days), and next year has been revised sharply higher (+233.8% over 90 days to $0.44, +76.1% over 30 days). The next-year revision reflects the deleveraging and cost-out story rebuilding earnings off a depressed base, but it does not clear the +30% inflection threshold on a clean basis: the current-year estimate is essentially zero, and next-quarter revisions are negative.

Read holistically, the growth thesis is a recovery story with early operational proof but no confirmation from the season that actually matters. The July–August peak, against a management-flagged promotional environment, is the test that will validate or break the trajectory. We score growth prospects moderately, acknowledging the Q1 turn and the coherent restructuring but discounting for the unproven peak and the fact that reported growth is optical.

## Valuation Assessment

Standard multiples are unusable here. Trailing and forward P/E are negative (−1.05x) because of the impairment. The peer set (PRKS 16.6x, PLNT 18.6x, OSW 33.1x P/E, median 18.6x) is not directly comparable given FUN's leverage and loss position. The right frame is EV/EBITDA and the equity-as-option construct.

On EV/EBITDA: at $17.21, market cap is ~$1.76B; net debt of ~$5.2B implies enterprise value of ~$7.0B against $792M of Adjusted EBITDA, or ~8.8x. That is not obviously cheap for a business whose underlying EBITDA is declining. The Enchanted Parks/EPR divestiture removes ~$47M of low-margin Adjusted EBITDA (the six "Specified Parks" generated ~$290M revenue), and undisclosed proceeds should reduce debt, but until the transaction value is public, the deleveraging benefit cannot be quantified. This is a critical open item.

The bull case for the equity is entirely a function of financial leverage: if the market re-rates the business toward a higher EBITDA multiple as deleveraging progresses and attendance recovers, the equity value expands non-linearly given the ~14x debt-to-equity structure. Analyst targets span $19 low to $33 high (mean $26.31, median $28), well above spot, and the recommendation split is constructive (2 strong buy, 7 buy, 5 hold). But those targets embed a recovery that the data has not yet confirmed, and a Tier-3 independent analyst carries a $13.75 Sell target arguing the valuation implies an 8% 5-year EBITDA CAGR that flat deferred revenue does not support.

We treat cheapness here with skepticism: a compressed (negative) multiple on a levered, EBITDA-declining, activist-restructuring situation is a value-trap warning absent a confirmed catalyst, not a buy signal. The insider buying at $19–23.5 and the identified deleveraging catalyst partially offset this, but the asymmetry is not yet clearly favorable at $17.21. We score valuation slightly below neutral.

## Competitive Landscape

FUN's moat is real but static: irreplaceable regional park real estate and exclusive licensed IP (Warner Bros./DC, Peanuts, Looney Tunes) create genuine barriers to entry, and the company is the largest regional operator by scale. Trajectory is the relevant question, and it is mixed. The impairment and the "re-tool to Cedar Fair standards" language confirm the legacy Six Flags parks were competitively weak. The divestiture of six underperforming parks is a deliberate contraction to concentrate capex on the strongest assets: a defensible strategic move, but one that shrinks the footprint rather than grows share.

The company competes for discretionary leisure spend against streaming, sports, dining and travel, and is directly exposed to consumer-spending softness. Industry cost pressures include seasonal wage inflation, statutory minimum-wage increases, higher insurance and litigation costs, and tariffs on imported rides. Q1's same-park attendance and pass-base gains suggest the operating fixes are gaining traction, but the improvement needs peak-season confirmation before it can be called durable share stabilization. We score competitive position modestly above neutral, weighting the improving Q1 trajectory against the shrinking footprint and macro exposure.

## Risk Assessment

**Debt and liquidity are the dominant risk.** The ~$5.3B debt against a $1.8B market cap carries variable-rate exposure (~$1.5B term loan plus revolver) where +100bps SOFR adds $18.7M of interest. The covenant caps Net First Lien Leverage at 5.0x, stepping to 4.5x by 2027; restricted-payment baskets are already blocked above 5.5x. The January 2026 refinancing into 8.625% notes signals a materially higher cost of capital.

**The January 2027 Partnership Parks cash call** — the buyout of Six Flags Over Georgia / White Water Atlanta LP interests — is a non-discretionary use of cash competing directly with deleveraging. A default here could cross-default the credit agreement and hand Time Warner control. The company declined the Six Flags Over Texas buyout in December 2025, which may lead to that park's sale.

**Merger integration failure is now quantified** in the $1.52B impairment, and continued impairment risk remains on the residual $2.07B goodwill (with $38.6M taken in Q1'26 alone). **Attendance is weather-dependent and seasonal**, amplifying downside on any peak-season miss. Management explicitly flags rising litigation and settlement costs with "exceptionally high verdicts."

**Governance is in wholesale reset**: an extraordinary density of Item 5.02 filings between October 2025 and July 2026, including CEO, CMO, Chief Legal Officer, interim finance lead and CFO changes. This is disruptive, though the activist involvement (Dendur, Sachem Head, H Partners) provides a coherent strategic thread.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 85.2% versus 68.3% one-year historical vol — rich, at the 85th percentile of the current chain — with the term structure in backwardation (85.2% near-term vs 76.5% at 6 months). The nearest-expiry implied move is ±19%, consistent with a binary peak-season/earnings event. Put/call OI ratio has swung from 0.19 to 1.57 as the stock fell 30.9% since mid-June, indicating a shift toward defensive positioning. The 3-month skew of +11% flags elevated near-term put demand. The market is bracing for a material move around the summer peak, which argues against paying up for the stock ahead of that resolution. Short interest is high at 22.7% of float (8.4 days to cover), which creates squeeze potential on any positive attendance surprise but also reflects a substantial informed bearish contingent.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The −$1.6B loss is 95% non-cash; the business generates $792M Adjusted EBITDA and $327M operating cash flow. The activist-driven "shrink to strengthen" plan is producing early results (Q1 attendance +4%, pass base +6%, per-cap +6%, $50M cost-out), the EPR divestiture provides debt-paydown proceeds, and the 2027 note wall has been addressed. Insiders are buying: a director bought $5.9M at ~$23.5 and the Executive Chair at $19. As leverage falls and attendance recovers, the highly-levered equity re-rates non-linearly. Analyst targets of $26–28 mean/median imply 50%+ upside; a squeeze on the 22.7% short float could amplify any positive peak-season print. Bull case ~$28–33.

**Bear case.** The $5.3B of debt against a $1.8B market cap leaves little equity cushion; underlying Adjusted EBITDA is declining (−$83M, −610bps margin), not growing. The peak season disappoints in a promotional environment, per-cap yield erodes, and the January 2027 Partnership Parks cash call competes with deleveraging just as the 8.625% coupon raises interest expense. Goodwill impairment continues. An independent analyst carries a $13.75 target. Bear case ~$12–14, roughly −20% to −30% from spot and testing the 52-week low of $12.51.

The crux is the divergence between insider conviction (buying well above spot) and sell-side/short skepticism, and it will be resolved by peak-season attendance and disclosed divestiture terms, neither of which is available today.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: we lack sufficient evidence for a confident directional call in either direction until the July–August peak reports and the EPR transaction terms are disclosed.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Peak-season same-park attendance confirms the Q1 trend (Q2/Q3 same-park attendance +4% or better) with per-cap holding up in the promotional environment.
- Disclosed EPR/Enchanted Parks proceeds materially reduce net debt (e.g., net leverage moving toward or below the 5.0x covenant with a credible path to 4.5x).
- Continued clustered open-market insider buying at or above current levels, or FY2026 EBITDA guidance stabilizing/growing versus the FY25 $792M base.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Peak-season attendance turns negative or per-cap yield erodes materially, signaling the Q1 turn was seasonally immaterial noise.
- Any covenant breach, forced restructuring, or inability to fund the January 2027 Partnership Parks buyout without new debt.
- Further large goodwill impairment on the residual $2.07B, or cash falling toward critical levels against the interest and cash-call schedule.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report (early August), which captures the start of the peak season, followed by the Q3 report covering July–August. The January 2027 Partnership Parks obligation is a hard secondary checkpoint.

## Conclusion

FUN is a genuine turnaround with credible early evidence and equally credible existential risk. The impairment-dominated loss overstates income-statement distress while any screen that misses the balance sheet understates the leverage. Management's restructuring is coherent, the insider buying at $19–23.5 is the strongest bullish tell in the file, and the pullback since mid-June improves risk/reward. But the equity is a levered option whose value hinges on deleveraging that cannot be assessed until the divestiture terms are public and the peak season is in the books, and the options market is pricing a ±19% move into that resolution. The score has improved steadily (3.7 → 4.3 → 4.6, now 4.5) as management-quality and growth trajectories firmed, and the current data confirms rather than reverses that trend without yet justifying a directional call. We maintain HOLD (uncertain) and will revisit at Q2/Q3 earnings.

**Recommendation: HOLD** (overall score 4.5/10)
