## Executive Summary

Getty Realty Corp is an internally managed net lease REIT focused on convenience, automotive, and single-tenant retail real estate, operating 1,191 properties across 45 states with near-perfect occupancy (99.7%). The company reported strong Q1 2026 results (AFFO $0.63/share, +6.8% YoY) and raised full-year guidance to $2.50-$2.52/share, reflecting continued execution on its acquisition strategy and lease extension program.

At $33.28, GTY trades at approximately 13.2x 2026E AFFO with a 5.8% dividend yield — a modest discount to the broader net lease REIT peer group (NNN at 14-15x). The portfolio's transformation away from pure petroleum toward car washes, QSR drive-thrus, and auto service centers is progressing well. However, the investment case faces three specific headwinds that prevent an upgrade to BUY: (1) elevated institutional short interest at 18.2% of float with 15.3 days to cover, (2) declining forward EPS estimates (next-year estimate down 6% in 30 days), and (3) a structural dilution issue where shares outstanding have grown 29% since 2022, partially offsetting per-share growth.

The stock functions primarily as an income vehicle — the 5.8% yield is well-covered at a 79% AFFO payout ratio with 3-4% annual AFFO growth providing modest dividend growth potential. Total return from current levels is capped by proximity to analyst consensus targets ($34 median) and the compressed acquisition spread environment (8.0% yields vs. 5.7% cost of new debt = only 230bps).

## Company Overview

Getty Realty Corp traces its lineage to Getty Oil (founded 1955) and has operated as a net lease REIT since 1997. The company owns 1,191 properties leased primarily under triple-net terms (tenants pay taxes, maintenance, insurance, and environmental costs), with a weighted average lease term of 10.1 years. The portfolio's primary property types include convenience stores (~63%), express car washes (~20%), automotive service centers, and QSR drive-thrus.

The business operates with just 31 employees — an extremely lean structure enabled by the triple-net model where tenants handle property-level operations. Revenue is generated almost entirely from base rental income ($55.8M in Q1 2026) with minimal operating cost exposure. The company's master/unitary lease structure (62 master leases covering 962 properties) provides cross-default protection, meaning a tenant default on one property triggers exposure across their entire portfolio of leased GTY sites.

Key tenants include ARKO Corp (148 properties, ~12% of revenue) and Global Partners LP (127 properties, ~10% of revenue). The intentional diversification strategy has reduced single-tenant concentration while expanding the property type mix beyond traditional gas stations.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** Revenue grew from $165.6M (FY2022) to $221.7M (FY2025), compounding at approximately 10.2% annually. Q1 2026 revenue from rental properties reached $57.4M (+11.0% YoY). Growth is acquisition-driven — the company deployed $273M across 76 properties in 2025 and $30.3M across 29 properties in Q1 2026, all at 8.0% initial cash yields.

**AFFO per share:** Grew from approximately $2.14 (2022) to $2.43 (2025), with 2026 guidance at $2.50-$2.52. This implies 3-4% annual AFFO/share growth — a rate that lags revenue growth due to meaningful equity dilution (shares outstanding grew from 46.7M to ~60.5M, with 5.5M additional forward shares pending).

**Balance sheet:** Total debt of $997M (entirely senior unsecured notes) against total equity of ~$967M implies D/E of 1.03x. All debt is unsecured with staggered maturities from 2028-2036, weighted average rate of 4.5%, and weighted average maturity of 6.0 years. The $450M revolving facility is fully undrawn. With ~$171.5M in forward equity proceeds pending, total liquidity exceeds $625M against a committed pipeline of $125M.

**Cash flow quality:** FCF margin of 57.3% in FY2025 reflects the minimal capital expenditure requirements of a triple-net landlord. The AFFO payout ratio of approximately 79% ($1.93 annual dividend / $2.43 AFFO) provides adequate coverage with retained cash flow available for reinvestment.

**Capital cost compression risk:** Recent debt issuances priced at 5.52-5.76% versus legacy notes at 3.43-3.65%. Current acquisition yields of 8.0% against new debt costs of ~5.7% produce only a 230bps spread — meaningfully tighter than the 400-500bps available when rates were lower. This mathematically constrains the accretiveness of future acquisitions absent a rate decline.

## Growth Analysis

GTY's growth model is straightforward: acquire properties at cash yields above the blended cost of capital, fund acquisitions through a mix of retained AFFO, ATM equity, and unsecured notes. The $125M+ committed pipeline (43 properties, 10 tenants) provides near-term visibility.

The 3-4% annual AFFO/share growth rate is modest but consistent. Growth is constrained by three factors: (1) the declining acquisition spread noted above, (2) ongoing equity dilution (29% share count growth over 3 years), and (3) the limited scale of the addressable acquisition market for niche convenience/automotive properties relative to larger net lease REITs like Realty Income.

Estimate revisions are negative: next-year EPS estimate has declined 6.0% in the past 30 days and 3.4% over 90 days. Current-year estimate revisions are modestly positive (+0.4% over 30 days) but this reflects only one analyst's coverage. The negative revision trajectory, though based on thin coverage, signals limited earnings momentum.

The environmental liability wind-down provides a modest positive GAAP earnings tailwind — $7.8M was reversed in Q1 2026 alone, and the legacy lookback periods have all expired. This removes an overhang but is not a recurring growth driver.

GTY's P/AFFO of 13.2x compares to the broader net lease sector at 14-15x. The discount reflects the petroleum/environmental legacy, smaller scale, and EV transition overhang.

**Analyst targets:** Consensus median of $34.00 (range $33.00-$37.00) implies only 2.2% upside to median, with maximum upside to $37 of 11.2%. This compressed upside to targets limits the capital appreciation thesis.

**Income value:** At a 5.8% yield with 3-4% dividend growth, GTY offers approximately 8.8-9.8% total return potential — attractive for an income strategy but insufficient risk-reward for a growth-oriented BUY given the specific risks outlined below.

## Competitive Landscape

GTY occupies a defensible niche within the net lease REIT universe. Its focus on convenience/automotive properties creates a specialized acquisition capability that larger, more generalist peers (Realty Income, NNN REIT) cannot replicate easily. The 10.1-year WALT and 99.7% occupancy demonstrate the stability of the tenant base.

The portfolio diversification strategy is demonstrably working: 2025 acquisitions included 28 QSR drive-thrus, 24 convenience stores, 15 auto service centers, and 9 car washes. This reduces exposure to any single property type and positions the portfolio around "automobility" broadly rather than petroleum specifically.

Competitive advantages include the internally managed structure (eliminates external management fee leakage), master lease cross-default protection, and specialized knowledge of environmental assessment and remediation for gas station sites (a barrier to entry for less experienced acquirers).

The long-term competitive threat is real: EV adoption will gradually reduce the economic viability of petroleum-centric locations over a 10-20 year horizon. Management's response — acquiring car washes, QSR, and service centers — is appropriate but the pace of transformation needs to continue accelerating relative to EV penetration curves.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Elevated Short Interest (18.2% of float):** This is the most immediate tactical concern. With 15.3 days to cover and 7.03M shares short, institutional investors have meaningful bearish positioning. The options market confirms uncertainty: near-term IV at 37.7% vs. 19.9% HV implies the market prices imminent binary risk, and the term structure is in steep backwardation (37.7% near-term vs. 28.2% at 6 months). While the put/call OI ratio of 0.09 suggests the market isn't heavily positioned for downside in longer-dated options, the elevated short interest signals sophisticated investors disagree with the current valuation.

**2. Negative Estimate Revisions:** Next-year estimates declining 6.0% in 30 days contradicts the thesis of improving fundamentals. Though based on single-analyst coverage (thin data), the direction matters. Consensus is not catching up to improving results — it's drifting lower despite the Q1 beat.

**3. Dilution Pace:** 29% share count growth over 3 years means investors are paying for growth that partially accrues to new shareholders. At current issuance rates, annual dilution runs approximately 7-8%. AFFO/share growth of 3-4% means total AFFO is growing 10-12% but individual shareholders capture less than half that.

**4. EV Structural Transition:** While lease terms provide 10.1 years of contractual protection, residual value of petroleum-dependent locations will decline as EV adoption accelerates. This creates a gradually increasing discount rate for long-dated lease income.

**5. Tenant Credit Opacity:** Most tenants are unrated and privately held. While ARKO (publicly traded, improving financials) and Global Partners provide visibility, the remaining 78% of revenue derives from tenants whose credit health is difficult to monitor.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty with steep backwardation into longer-dated expiries. Only 27 contracts exist across 3 expirations, suggesting thin liquidity. The put/call OI ratio of 0.09 (dominated by calls), combined with the 18.2% short interest, creates an unusual dynamic — shorts are likely using the stock directly rather than options for bearish positioning. The elevated near-term IV likely reflects post-earnings positioning uncertainty around the April 22 report date that hasn't yet normalized.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($36-37, +8-11%):**
- AFFO guidance is conservative and actuals trend toward $2.55-$2.60 through 2026
- Environmental liability releases continue providing GAAP earnings uplift
- Pipeline of $125M+ closes at accretive yields, demonstrating sustained deal flow
- Rate cuts materialize, compressing cap rates and expanding acquisition spreads
- Short interest unwinds as bears capitulate on stable results

**Bear Case ($28-30, -9-16%):**
- Short sellers are correct: ARKO or another major tenant shows credit deterioration
- Acquisition spreads compress further if rates remain elevated or increase
- Continued dilution at 7-8% annually erodes per-share value faster than AFFO grows
- EV transition narrative intensifies, multiple contracts further
- Consumer spending weakness (tariff-driven) impacts tenant operations

**Why not BUY:** Three specific, falsifiable conditions prevent a BUY despite the 6.3 overall score: (1) Short interest at 18.2% represents informed institutional bearish positioning — given the thin analyst coverage (8 analysts, 5 Hold), the shorts may have private information about tenant credit or portfolio quality not visible in public data. (2) Forward estimates declining 6.0% at 30 days contradicts positive earnings momentum — if estimates were rising, the yield + growth combination would clearly warrant BUY. (3) The stock trades at 96% of the analyst median target ($33.28 vs $34.00), offering only 2.2% upside to consensus — insufficient margin of safety given the identified risks.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
- Short interest declines below 10% of float (indicating bearish thesis has weakened)
- Forward estimates stabilize or reverse to positive (next-year estimate revision turns positive)
- Stock pulls back to $30-31 range (providing 10%+ upside to consensus median and 6.3%+ yield)
- Clear catalyst such as large portfolio acquisition announcement or material rate cut benefiting acquisition economics

**Downgrade triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
- Any tenant representing >5% of ABR files for bankruptcy or misses rent payments
- AFFO guidance reduced below $2.40/share (implying per-share contraction)
- Dividend coverage falls below 85% (AFFO payout ratio exceeds 85%)
- Short interest exceeds 25% of float with deteriorating operational metrics

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely July 2026 for Q2 results) will provide visibility on: (1) pipeline conversion, (2) whether Q1's environmental tailwind was one-time vs. recurring, and (3) whether the negative estimate revision trend reverses.

## Conclusion

Getty Realty is a well-run, niche net lease REIT generating consistent mid-single-digit AFFO/share growth with a well-covered 5.8% dividend yield. The triple-net structure, 10.1-year WALT, and near-perfect occupancy provide income stability. The diversification strategy away from pure petroleum is progressing appropriately.

The stock offers approximately 8.8-9.8% total return potential (yield + growth), attractive for income-oriented portfolios but insufficient for a BUY given: proximity to consensus targets (2.2% upside to median), elevated short interest signaling informed bearish positioning, and negative forward estimate revisions. Hold for income characteristics, with re-evaluation warranted if the stock pulls back to provide better entry economics or if the short thesis demonstrably fails.
