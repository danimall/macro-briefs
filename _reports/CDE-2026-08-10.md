## Executive Summary

Coeur Mining is a materially different company than it was eighteen months ago. Two all-stock acquisitions — SilverCrest (closed February 2025, $1.59B, adding Las Chispas) and New Gold (closed March 20, 2026, $6.939B, adding Rainy River and New Afton) — took the share count from 399.2M at year-end 2024 to 1,028.5M at June 30, 2026, and total assets from $4.70B to $15.20B. Q2 2026 revenue of $1,085.6M was a record, up 126% year over year; H1 2026 free cash flow of $654.3M represents a 33.7% margin; and the balance sheet now carries $1,052.3M of cash against $705.3M of debt for a net cash position of $347.0M, with a $972.3M undrawn revolver and an explicit investment-grade off-ramp built into the March 2026 credit agreement. For a company that posted three consecutive years of GAAP losses in 2021–2023 under a restrictive facility, that is a structural change in financial flexibility.

The forward picture is less clean. Coeur's first guidance revision after the New Gold close cut FY2026 gold production from 680–815 koz to 630–750 koz and copper from 50–65 Mlb to 40–50 Mlb, while raising adjusted cost-applicable-to-sales guidance at New Afton (gold midpoint +32%, copper midpoint +69%) and Rainy River (+27%). Adjusted CAS guidance for all five legacy mines was left unchanged, so the operational downgrade is localized entirely to the $6.94B of newly acquired assets, which now represent roughly 71% of segment assets. Consensus has responded: FY2026 EPS estimates have fallen 27.1% in 90 days to $1.40 and FY2027 by 12.9% to $1.98, after Q1 and Q2 misses of 25.4% and 63.3% respectively. FY2026 guidance of roughly $2.3B adjusted EBITDA requires an H2 step-up of 41.3% over H1, of which only about $187M is the mechanical roll-off of a non-cash acquired-inventory charge and about −$290M is the drag from a lower assumed price deck, leaving a residual near $497M that must come from volume growth and unit-cost improvement.

At $17.865 the stock trades at 7.8x guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA, 11.0x LTM adjusted EBITDA, 12.8x consensus FY2026 adjusted EPS and 1.77x book, with an 8.2% FCF yield on the guide. That is a reasonable price for the assets if the guide holds and metal prices stay near spot (gold ~$4,265, silver ~$64, both roughly 6.5% above the guidance deck). It is an expensive price if the H2 ramp slips a second time or if gold and silver revisit the levels they touched in June 2026 ($3,823 and $62.84 realized). Probability-weighted, the expected return is modestly positive; the dispersion is wide enough, and the driver exogenous enough, that neither accumulation nor exit is compelling. HOLD.

## Financial Analysis

**Q2 2026 income statement mechanics.** Revenue $1,085.6M less cost applicable to sales excluding amortization $549.7M, amortization $256.0M, G&A $22.7M, exploration $34.1M and other $6.6M gives operating income of $216.5M, a 19.9% operating margin. Net interest of $11.0M, other income of $9.9M and a $0.3M debt-extinguishment loss produce $215.1M pre-tax; a $93.2M tax provision (43.3% effective rate) leaves $121.9M of net income and $0.12 of diluted EPS.

Two non-cash acquisition-accounting items suppress reported profitability. First, $9.586B of the New Gold consideration was allocated to PP&E and mining properties (the allocation exceeds the $6.939B purchase price because it is offset by a $2.794B deferred tax liability), taking consolidated PP&E from $2.745B to $12.163B. FY2026 amortization is guided at $1,100–1,200M against $355.8M recognized in H1, so quarterly amortization steps up materially in H2. Second, H1 CAS included $225.4M of acquired-inventory purchase-price allocation, with $264M guided for the full year.

The second item matters more than it appears. Coeur's adjusted EBITDA and adjusted net income reconciliations do not add back the acquired-inventory PPA charge. H1 adjusted EBITDA of $953.2M is therefore depressed by roughly $225M of non-cash, non-recurring cost, and the implied H2 charge of only ~$38.6M delivers roughly $187M of reported EBITDA improvement with no operational content. Any H1-to-H2 comparison that ignores this misreads the business in both directions. The item is cash-neutral, so free cash flow is unaffected.

**Cash flow quality is the strongest part of the story.** H1 operating cash flow was $854.1M against $199.8M of capex, giving $654.3M of free cash flow. Operating cash flow before working-capital changes was $958.1M, above reported OCF, meaning working capital was a modest use of cash rather than a source — the desirable direction. Q2 alone produced $513.2M of OCF and $387.5M of FCF, but that quarter benefited from a $20M insurance receipt related to the November 2025 Wharf crusher fire (including roughly $10M credited to CAS that does not appear to be added back in the adjusted EBITDA reconciliation) and from the lightest capex quarter of the year. H2 capex is guided at $320–405M versus $199.8M in H1. Annualizing Q2 FCF is not a forecast.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue grew 131% in H1 2026 to $1,941.8M. The decomposition matters more than the headline. New Gold contributed $438.1M in Q2 and $572.3M in H1 (post-close only); Las Chispas contributed $186.9M in Q2. H1 realized prices were gold $4,235/oz and silver $76.92/oz versus $2,845 and $32.96 a year earlier — gold +49%, silver +133%. Q2 gold production of 163,490 oz was a company record, +51% year over year.

The most useful disclosure for judging underlying growth is Coeur's own pro forma: on a combined basis as if New Gold had been owned since January 1, 2025, H1 2026 revenue was $2,276.7M with net income of $368.6M, against H1 2025 revenue of $1,417.7M and a **net loss of $(231.5)M**. The same asset base lost money one year earlier at lower metal prices. Price, not operating improvement, drove the swing.

**The H2 guidance bridge is the central operational question.** FY2026 guidance assumes $4,000 gold, $60 silver, $6.00 copper, and calls for roughly 690 koz gold, 20 Moz silver, 45 Mlb copper, ~$2.3B adjusted EBITDA and ~$1.5B FCF.

Bridging the EBITDA step-up: +$187M from the inventory step-up roll-off, approximately −$290M from the lower assumed price deck versus H1 realized prices (gold −$235/oz on ~430 koz; silver −$16.92/oz on ~11.2 Moz), leaving a residual near $497M from volume and unit cost. On a quarterly basis H2 requires ~215 koz of gold versus Q2's 163.5 koz, or +31.5% above a quarter that already contained a full three months of New Gold.

Evidence for the ramp: Rochester and Wharf are heap-leach operations with a physical lag between ore placement and metal recovery. Rochester placed 8.04 Mt in Q2; Wharf placed 1.41 Mt versus 0.37 Mt in Q1 after the crusher fire, with contract crushing now demobilized and secondary/tertiary upgrades complete. Leach kinetics alone should deliver a step-up without new operational achievement. Evidence against: grades are falling where the ramp is largest. Rochester's silver grade was 10.2 g/t in Q2 versus 12.2 g/t in Q1 and 20.3 g/t in H1 2025 — roughly halved year over year. Palmarejo gold went from 2.0 g/t (H1 2025) to 1.3 g/t; Kensington from 4.8 to 4.3 g/t. Lower grades require proportionally more tonnage for the same ounces.

Longer-term growth is constrained in a way the metal-price rally obscures. Legacy reserves at December 31, 2025 were 4.35 Moz gold and 274.4 Moz silver, struck at $2,200/oz and $26.00/oz. Coeur's own disclosed sensitivity is that +$200/oz gold raises reserves only 6% and +$2.00/oz silver only 3%. With spot roughly 94% above the gold reserve price, the naïve expectation of a large reserve re-rating is not supported by the company's own numbers — reserves are constrained by geology, cut-off grade, mine plan and permitted footprint. Derived reserve lives at FY2025 production rates: Kensington 5.1 years, Las Chispas 5.4 years, Palmarejo 9.2 years, Wharf 12.8 years, Rochester ~22 years on gold. Growth must come from drilling (Mexico exploration roughly doubled to a company-wide FY2026 budget of $147–169M) and from M&A, where authorized-share headroom is now only ~271.5M shares (21%).

One genuine future tailwind that screens will miss: the inherited Royal Gold stream at Rainy River steps down from 60% of silver to 30% at 3.1 Moz cumulative deliveries (2,155,142 oz delivered, 69.5% of threshold — roughly 3.8 years at current rates) and from 6.5% of gold to 3.25% at 230,000 oz (135,477 delivered, 58.9% — roughly 7 years).

Growth prospects score 4.8: aggregate growth is real but acquisition- and price-driven, forward estimate momentum is sharply negative (FY2026 −27.1%, FY2027 −12.9% over 90 days), organic grade trends are adverse, and reserve life at two assets is around five years.

## Competitive Landscape

Coeur's structural advantages are jurisdiction and balance sheet. All assets sit in the US, Canada and Mexico, with no exposure to Africa, Central Asia or South America. The company holds meaningful unhedged exposure to gold, silver and now copper, which is unusual among mid-tier producers and makes the equity a high-beta vehicle for precious-metals flows. With $2.02B of liquidity, net cash and an investment-grade pathway written into the revolver, Coeur is positioned to act as a consolidator in a weak-price environment rather than a distressed seller — a genuine change from 2021–2023.

The cost position is mixed. Las Chispas at $853/oz and Palmarejo at $1,016/oz are genuinely low-cost. Kensington at $2,327/oz and Rainy River at roughly $1,752/oz excluding the step-up are not. The guided H2 mix shift toward Rochester and Wharf improves the blend, while the New Afton and Rainy River cost increases work the other way.

The trajectory evidence is where competitive positioning weakens. Within roughly 4.5 months of closing the largest deal in company history, Coeur cut production guidance and raised unit costs at both acquired mines and at neither legacy mine — precisely the risk the FY2025 10-K pre-flagged when it warned there was no guarantee Coeur was "aware of all relevant information, including all potential liabilities of New Gold." Part of that revision plausibly reflects the lower price deck (lower prices raise cut-off grades) and part is definitional if the guided Rainy River CAS is stated inclusive of the raised $244M step-up, which the company's disclosure practice suggests. Separately, resource estimates have been revised down materially at both prior acquisitions and at legacy Kensington: Las Chispas measured-and-indicated gold −57% and silver −56%; Kensington M&I −51% and inferred −58%; Silvertip inferred silver −29%, lead −33%, zinc −43%. Coeur attributes these to "technical revisions," which at newly acquired assets commonly reflects conforming NI 43-101 estimates to SEC S-K 1300 definitions and the acquirer's own models — a reporting-standard effect rather than orebody deterioration. At Las Chispas, however, proven-and-probable reserves also fell 17% (gold) and 16% (silver), so conversion accounting cannot explain the whole move.

Streams and royalties transfer a substantial share of price upside away from shareholders. Palmarejo delivers 50% of gold production to Franco-Nevada at the lesser of $800/oz or spot — on the order of $150–170M of annual gross value at current prices. The Rochester ASARCO net smelter return switched on when quarterly silver exceeded $31.68/oz, taking Rochester royalty expense from nil in 2024 to $11.7M in 2025; aggregate US royalty expense rose 134% to $31.3M.

Competitive position scores 5.0: improving scale and jurisdiction quality, deteriorating grade and resource trajectory, unresolved integration performance at 71% of segment assets.

## Risk Assessment

**Unhedged price exposure into a violent market.** 2026 has been a boom-bust year: gold fell roughly 30% and silver roughly 54% from their 2026 peaks by early July before partially recovering. Coeur's own realized prices went from $4,383/$82.85 in Q1 to $4,140/$71.18 in Q2, with June the low month at $3,823/$62.84. Commerzbank cut its year-end 2026 gold forecast to $4,500 in late July, its second cut in two months. With no hedges, EBITDA and the equity price are both direct functions of a variable management disclaims control over.

**Las Chispas concentration.** The portfolio's highest-margin asset (58% Q2 operating margin) carries three intersecting issues: an operating permit "set to expire in November 2026" and currently mid-amendment in a Mexican permitting environment the 10-K describes as subject to government actions "intended to slow or halt the normal processing of permits"; a derived reserve life of about 5.4 years with reserves plus M&I gold falling roughly 95 koz beyond depletion in 2025; and the entirety of Coeur's $625.8M of goodwill, tested without impairment as of December 31, 2025. That combination is the most plausible trigger for a future impairment charge.

**Guidance risk.** A second downward revision would arrive on top of a 27% estimate cut and two large misses, and would call into question the merger's underwriting. Q3 results are the test.

**Provisional purchase accounting.** Reclamation fair value, unrecognized tax benefits and deferred taxes remain open through approximately March 2027. PP&E of $12.16B, the $3.11B DTL, the $423.5M ARO and $1,100–1,200M of amortization guidance can all move.

**Mexico.** H1 Mexico revenue of $728.4M is the largest single-country contribution. Disclosed risks include cartel-related security concerns affecting third-party deliveries to Palmarejo, civil disobedience restricting concession access, late-2024 tax increases levied on EBITDA and revenues from gold and silver, ejido surface-access renegotiation, and a $30.2M VAT claim written down by $26.0M in 2021 and now in USMCA arbitration. Contractor labor is 1,602 of 2,093 in Mexico.

**Governance and capital allocation.** The proxy pay-versus-performance table shows CDE's TSR index at 49/32/31/55/172 for FY2021–FY2025 against a company-selected peer group at 89/80/87/95/243 — underperformance in every year of the five-year window, +72% versus +143% cumulatively. Reserve-and-resource growth sits in the compensation scorecard alongside relative TSR and ROIC, and a metric rewarding reserve growth can be satisfied by acquisition as well as by drilling; the presence of rTSR and ROIC cuts the other way, and sector-wide consolidation is the dominant strategic response across mid-tier gold, so I draw no motive conclusion. Insider behavior skews toward monetization: four new Rule 10b5-1 plans adopted between mid-May and mid-June 2026 (Krebs up to 250,000 shares, roughly 12% of his ~2.15M-share position; Nault 240,000; McGrath 30,000; Beckelheimer 20,000) against a single identified open-market purchase of 635 shares at $16.95 and a 39,000-share sale at $18.60 by the retiring chief accounting officer. Plan adoption after an extended merger blackout is unremarkable in itself, and the February transactions at $24.63–$27.15 were grants and tax withholding rather than open-market sales. Capital returns have begun: a $750M authorization (March 2026) with $121M/6.7M shares repurchased through July 31 at a Q2 average of $18.44 — above the current price — and an inaugural $0.02 semi-annual dividend paid June 10, 2026, annualizing to a 0.22% yield.

**Silvertip.** $230.0M of segment assets, zero revenue, a $(10.9)M Q2 operating loss, $16M of H1 exploration spend and $15M of guided FY2026 study capital, suspended since February 2020 with resources revised down again in 2025.

Risk factors score 3.8: several specific, dated or quantified downside catalysts (November 2026 permit expiry, provisional PPA, goodwill concentration, second guidance-cut risk) sit alongside an unhedged commodity book.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $26.50 (+48%)

Gold holds above $4,250 and silver above $65, keeping realized prices 6%+ above the guidance deck. Heap-leach kinetics at Rochester and Wharf deliver the mechanical H2 recovery step-up (Wharf placed 1.41 Mt in Q2 versus 0.37 Mt in Q1; Rochester placed 8.04 Mt), New Afton reaches its reported 16,000 tpd Q4 target, and FY2026 adjusted EBITDA lands near $2.45–2.6B with FCF above $1.5B. The $630M of remaining buyback authorization retires 3–4% of the float, the balance sheet moves toward the two-of-three investment-grade threshold that unsecures the revolver and cuts pricing by 32.5bp, and the Rainy River silver stream step-down comes into modelling range. On $2.6B of EBITDA at 9.5–10.3x — a mid-tier multiple, not a premium — enterprise value of $24.7–26.8B plus ~$0.8B of net cash gives $25–27 per share. Consensus targets have room: the mean is $23.41 and the high $35.00.

### Bear Case — $11.50 (−36%)

Gold retraces toward $3,500 and silver toward $45, levels within the range already traded in 2026. FY2027 EBITDA compresses to $1.2–1.4B; at 7x that is $8.4–9.8B of enterprise value plus roughly $0.7B of net cash, or $8.85–10.20 per share, against a 52-week low of $9.885. Independently or additionally, the H2 ramp misses: Rochester's halved silver grade and Palmarejo's 1.3 g/t gold require more tonnage than plan, a second guidance cut follows the first, and the market reprices the New Gold underwriting. The Las Chispas permit amendment slips past November 2026 or the reserve trajectory forces a goodwill test failure on the $625.8M balance. Falling estimates (−27% in 90 days) and two consecutive misses of 25% and 63% establish that this branch is already partially in motion, not hypothetical.

### Base Case — $19.50 (+9%)

FY2026 adjusted EBITDA lands near $2.15B — a roughly 11% haircut to the operational residual in the guide, partially offset by spot prices above the deck. Apply 8.5x, in line with mid-tier producers and discounted for five-year reserve lives at two assets and unhedged exposure, for $18.3B of enterprise value; add ~$0.6B of year-end net cash after buybacks and dividends for $18.9B of equity, or roughly $18.40 per share. Cross-checks: FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $1.98 at 10x gives $19.80; normalized FCF of $1.4B at a 7.5% yield gives $18.24. Base case $19.50 sits at the upper end of that cluster on the assumption that spot holds near current levels.

Probability-weighted at 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear: 0.45(+9.1%) + 0.25(+48.3%) + 0.30(−35.6%) = **+5.5%** expected return. Positive, but thin against 74% realized volatility and insufficient margin of safety for accumulation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Existing holders are compensated for the risk by an 8.2% FCF yield on guidance, a net-cash balance sheet and $630M of remaining buyback capacity. Prospective buyers have no urgent entry signal: the guidance package that supports the multiple has been cut once, the operational residual required in H2 is large, and consensus estimates are moving the wrong way.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (expected early November 2026), which is the first clean read on the heap-leach ramp, New Afton's throughput trajectory, and whether Rainy River unit costs stabilize at the revised guide. The Las Chispas permit amendment resolves by November 2026 on a separate track.

## Conclusion

Coeur has converted itself from a balance-sheet-constrained, sub-scale producer into a seven-mine, net-cash business generating $654M of half-year free cash flow, and it did so by issuing 632M shares — reducing legacy holders to 62.1% of the company. At $17.865 the market pays 7.8x guided FY2026 EBITDA and 12.8x consensus adjusted EPS for that outcome, a discount to a 20.7x peer median that is partly warranted by five-year reserve lives at two mines, 38% Mexico revenue exposure, streams that divert $150M+ of annual price upside, and an entirely unhedged commodity book.

The decisive question is whether FY2026 guidance holds. Roughly $497M of the implied 41% H2 EBITDA step-up must come from volume and unit cost rather than from the inventory step-up roll-off or the price deck, at assets where Rochester's silver grade has halved year over year and Palmarejo's gold grade has fallen from 2.0 to 1.3 g/t. Heap-leach recovery lag makes the ramp physically plausible; the fact that the first post-close revision cut both acquired mines while leaving all five legacy mines untouched makes a second revision a live possibility. Consensus has already marked FY2026 EPS down 27% in 90 days after misses of 25% and 63%, which means the 36% decline from the January high reflects fundamental deterioration rather than sentiment alone.

Weighted across scenarios the expected return is approximately +5.5%, with a 30% probability branch that costs 36%. Hold the position for the FCF yield and the option on metal prices; wait for either the Q3 print or a price in the $14–15 range before committing new capital.
