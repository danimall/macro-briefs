## Executive Summary

Crescent Energy has transformed itself over the past 18 months through three major acquisitions (SilverBow, Ridgemar, Vital Energy), growing production from ~150 MBoe/d to a record 341 MBoe/d in Q1 2026. The integration appears to be succeeding: operating costs fell 19% YoY to $14.00/Boe, Permian synergies of $120M are ahead of schedule, and Q1 adjusted EPS of $0.53 beat consensus by 52%. Analyst estimates have more than doubled over the past 90 days, with current-year EPS now at $2.45 (vs. $1.21 ninety days ago).

The challenge is that this operational success sits atop $5.5B+ of debt carrying 7-9.75% coupons, inside a KKR-controlled structure with no insider buying activity, at a time when forward oil curves ($57-61/Bbl) trade below the $65.34 SEC reserve pricing assumption. The stock has rallied 51% from its 52-week low to $13.13, reflecting much of the operational improvement. At ~5.4x forward earnings and 3.1x EV/EBITDAX, CRGY is cheap relative to cash generation, but the leverage profile and commodity sensitivity create binary risk that the current price incompletely discounts.

The position warrants holding for existing holders given the earnings momentum, but the risk/reward is insufficiently skewed for new capital deployment at current levels.

## Company Overview

Crescent Energy is a KKR-sponsored E&P company operating across the Eagle Ford (South Texas), Permian Basin, and Uinta Basin (Utah). The company pursues a private equity-style consolidation strategy, acquiring assets at valuations it considers accretive and extracting synergies through integration. It also operates a growing minerals and royalties business (Crescent Royalties/CRF) that produces 11 MBoe/d at effectively zero capex.

The company went through a corporate simplification in April 2025, eliminating its Up-C structure. KKR retains control through Non-Economic Series I Preferred Stock, which gives it Board appointment rights and veto power over material corporate actions. The company is externally managed by a KKR affiliate under a Management Agreement renewed through December 2027.

Revenue mix is 69% oil, 20% natural gas, 11% NGLs. Approximately 63% of oil production and 60% of gas production is hedged, which provides downside protection but limits participation in elevated commodity prices.

## Growth Analysis

Production growth has been extraordinary on an acquisition basis: from ~150 MBoe/d in FY2022 to 341 MBoe/d in Q1 2026. The 90-day estimate revision of +103% on current-year EPS (from $1.21 to $2.45) reflects the market recognizing Vital Energy synergies are materializing faster than expected.

The growth story going forward depends on:
1. **Permian inventory development:** The Vital merger brought substantial drilling inventory. PUD reserves of 199.2 MMBoe require $2.4B to develop over the next several years.
2. **Minerals & Royalties expansion:** The CRF segment is growing organically through third-party development on acreage Crescent owns minerals for. Q1 2026 EBITDAX of $34M from this segment is zero-capex cash flow.
3. **Further M&A:** Management has signaled continued acquisition interest. The KKR pipeline provides deal flow, though each acquisition adds integration complexity.

Organic production growth (excluding acquisitions) is likely low-single-digit given the Eagle Ford's maturity and capital allocation toward deleveraging. The growth thesis is effectively "acquisition-driven consolidator in a fragmenting basin," which works well in a rising oil price environment but creates compounding leverage risk if commodity prices deteriorate.

## Valuation Assessment

At $13.13, CRGY trades at:
- ~5.4x FY2026E EPS ($2.45)
- ~3.1x EV/LTM EBITDAX ($2.07B EBITDAX, ~$6.4B EV on $4.3B equity + net debt)
- ~$12,600 per flowing Boe/d (vs. peer transactions at $15,000-25,000)
- 3.7% dividend yield

Relative to peers: CRK at 6.7x, CNX at 4.9x, MGY at 17.0x. CRGY's forward multiple is below the peer median, reflecting the leverage discount and KKR governance structure.

The analyst consensus target of $17.36 (median $18.00) implies 32-37% upside. Analyst coverage is uniformly bullish (11 Buy/Strong Buy vs. 3 Holds, zero Sells).

The bull case for valuation is straightforward: at 5.4x earnings with $2.07B of EBITDAX, the company generates nearly 20% FCF yield on its equity if oil stays at current levels (~$70/Bbl WTI equivalent). However, two issues prevent a higher valuation score: (1) the forward oil curve at $57-61 implies 2027 earnings power significantly below current levels if hedges roll off, and (2) the GAAP trailing P/E is negative (-12.7x) because unrealized derivative losses distort reported results, making the stock screen poorly for fundamental investors.

The estimate revision signal (+103% over 90 days) is genuinely powerful and would normally trigger a BUY signal under inflection-stock principles. However, at $13.13 (71% above the 52-week low, 8% below the 52-week high), much of this revision is already reflected in the price. The stock rallied from $7.68 to $14.29 precisely as these revisions materialized.

## Competitive Landscape

Crescent occupies a distinct niche as a KKR-backed serial acquirer in the E&P space. This model has advantages:
- Access to KKR's deal-sourcing pipeline and capital markets relationships
- Ability to acquire smaller, subscale operators that larger E&Ps cannot economically pursue
- Minerals/royalties segment provides differentiation and embedded optionality

Relative to pure-play Permian operators (e.g., FANG, EOG), Crescent lacks the drilling inventory depth and returns-on-invested-capital track record. Relative to other consolidators, the KKR control structure creates governance risks that don't exist at traditional C-corps. The 60-67% hedging ratio is conservative relative to peers like FANG (largely unhedged), which reduces upside participation in the current elevated commodity price environment.

Operating cost improvement to $14.00/Boe is competitive and demonstrates integration execution. If management can sustain this trajectory toward $12-13/Boe over the next 12 months, the peer comparison improves materially.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Leverage at commodity cyclical sensitivity (HIGH).** At $5.5B of debt with 7-9.75% coupons and ~$385M in annual interest, WTI sustained below $55/Bbl would strain debt servicing. The forward curve ($57-61) is already below SEC reserve pricing ($65.34), meaning current reserve estimates may be revised downward, potentially triggering borrowing base reductions.

2. **Selling pressure overhang.** Liberty Mutual Foundation sold 32.6M shares at $12.33 on May 7 (7 days before this analysis). KKR distributed 36.8M shares in December 2025. Combined ~70M shares (~21% of outstanding) changed hands from long-term holders. Additional distributions from KKR vehicles cannot be ruled out.

3. **KKR governance / controlled company.** No corporate opportunity doctrine. Manager not fully dedicated. Board appointed by single preferred holder. Common shareholders have limited influence over strategic direction. External management fees (~$78M annual) represent a permanent value extraction.

4. **Integration execution.** Three transformative acquisitions in 18 months. Midstream constraints in Permian and Uinta are specifically flagged. $2.4B of PUD development obligations constrain FCF flexibility.

5. **Convertible dilution.** $690M of 2.75% convertibles due 2031 represent ~53M potential shares at current prices, a ~16% dilution risk partially mitigated by the capped call structure.

- Additional block sales by KKR vehicles exceeding 25M shares

## Conclusion

Crescent Energy presents a genuine operational turnaround — production records, cost improvements, and estimate revisions all confirm the acquisition strategy is working. However, the stock at $13.13 has already discounted much of this improvement (up 71% from the low). The remaining upside requires either sustained elevated oil prices (which the forward curve does not support) or continued aggressive deleveraging at a pace that leaves little room for operational stumbles.

The 13% short interest, $5.5B debt stack, KKR governance structure, and forward curve positioning below reserve pricing create a risk profile that demands a wider margin of safety than the current price offers. At $10.50-11.00, the risk/reward becomes compelling. At $13.13, the asymmetry is insufficient for new capital, though existing holders are adequately compensated by the 3.7% dividend and reasonable probability of continued near-term earnings strength while oil remains elevated.

HOLD with reassessment at next quarterly earnings or on a meaningful price pullback.
