## Executive Summary

L3Harris Technologies trades at $339.62 with a market capitalization of $63.4B, having rallied roughly 59% from its 52-week low of $208.11 in April 2025. The stock is now 10% below its 52-week high of $379.23, reached in early March 2026. FY2025 FCF reached $2.68B (12.3% margin), the company beat EPS estimates in all four quarters of FY2025, and management guides FY2026 to $23.0-23.5B in revenue (+7% organic), segment operating margins in the "low 16% range" (roughly 300bps of expansion from FY2025), and $3.0B+ in free cash flow.

The headline catalyst is the planned Missile Solutions IPO in H2 2026, backed by a first-of-its-kind $1B convertible preferred equity investment from the Department of Defense. This separates the Aerojet Rocketdyne solid rocket motor business into a dedicated entity, unlocking value in a supply-constrained munitions market where the Pentagon is actively seeking to scale production. Combined with a $1.27B Virginia manufacturing expansion and the VAMPIRE counter-drone system entering high-volume production, L3Harris is positioned in the areas of highest defense spending priority: munitions, missile defense (Golden Dome), and counter-UAS.

The primary concerns are valuation (roughly 29x FY2026 EPS, a premium to peers like GD at 21.7x and NOC at 23.1x), negative estimate revision momentum (current-year EPS consensus has fallen 6.3% over 90 days), and the $26.5B goodwill/intangibles load from acquisitions. The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty with IV at 38.2% versus 23.5% realized, in backwardation. Despite these cautions, the structural defense spending tailwind, FCF trajectory, and Missile Solutions IPO catalyst support a BUY recommendation.

## Company Overview

L3Harris Technologies is a $21.9B-revenue defense prime headquartered in Melbourne, Florida, operating across space, air, land, sea, and cyber domains. The company was formed from the 2019 merger of L3 Technologies and Harris Corporation, with the 2023 acquisition of Aerojet Rocketdyne ($4.7B) adding critical solid rocket motor capabilities. It employs approximately 45,000 people, including 18,000 engineers and scientists, and serves customers in over 100 countries.

Revenue composition: 75% U.S. Government (including foreign military sales), 22% international direct, and the remainder commercial. The business is structured across four segments transitioning to three in FY2026:

| Segment | FY2025 Revenue | Operating Margin | Backlog |
|---------|---------------|-----------------|---------|
| Communication Systems (CS) | $5.67B | 25.2% | $6.9B |
| Integrated Mission Systems (IMS) | $6.63B | 12.2% | $12.2B |
| Space & Airborne Systems (SAS) | $6.95B | 12.3% | $11.4B |
| Aerojet Rocketdyne (AR) | $2.85B | 9.5% | $8.2B |

Total funded backlog stands at $38.7B, providing approximately 1.8 years of revenue visibility.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY2025 revenue of $21.9B grew 2.5% YoY, depressed by the divestiture of the CAS disposal group (aviation/pilot training, sold for $820M net in March 2025). Underlying organic growth was approximately 3%, with IMS growing 7.6% and AR growing 10%. FY2026 guidance of $23.0-23.5B implies 7% organic growth acceleration.

**Profitability:** Reported operating income was $2.11B (9.7% margin), but this understates underlying profitability due to $769M in acquisition-related intangible amortization, $167M in LHX NeXt transformation costs, and $82M in divestiture losses. CS at 25.2% operating margin is among the best in the defense industry. FY2026 segment margin guidance of "low 16%" (versus approximately 14.8% in FY2025) implies roughly $3.7B in segment operating income on the midpoint revenue guide.

**Earnings:** Diluted EPS of $8.53 grew 8.4% YoY, with quarterly surprise magnitudes of +2.4% to +10.3%. Current-year (FY2026) consensus stands at $11.65, implying 37% EPS growth. This includes margin expansion from LHX NeXt cost savings and the completion of the CAS disposal earnings drag.

**Free Cash Flow:** FCF of $2.68B ($14.34/share) significantly exceeded net income of $1.61B, reflecting high-quality earnings with substantial depreciation/amortization addbacks. FCF margin of 12.3% is strong for the defense sector. FY2026 guidance of $3.0B+ would represent a 12.8-13.0% FCF margin and a ~12% yield on current market cap. FCF conversion (FCF/Net Income) at approximately 166% is excellent.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $10.9B against stockholders' equity of $19.6B (D/E of 1.1x). Cash of $1.07B. Net debt declined from $11.6B to $10.4B in FY2025, and commercial paper was paid down from a peak of $2.8B to zero. Interest coverage of approximately 3.5x is adequate. Goodwill of $20.0B and intangibles of $6.5B represent 64% of total assets.

**Capital Returns:** FY2025 returned $2.1B to shareholders ($1.2B buybacks, $903M dividends), essentially all of its FCF. The 1.4% dividend yield is modest but supported by strong cash flow. Share count declined from 191.3M to 186.8M (2.4% reduction).

## Growth Analysis

L3Harris's growth trajectory hinges on three drivers:

**1. Defense Spending Cycle:** The global defense environment is the most favorable in decades. NATO allies have committed to 5% of GDP on defense/security. The U.S. reconciliation package added $155B specifically targeting munitions, Golden Dome missile defense, and shipbuilding. Germany published its first military strategy since WWII, committing to expanded defense responsibility. Japan is exporting warships for the first time. These are structural, multi-year spending commitments with 3-5 year procurement cycles that convert to backlog.

**2. Munitions Ramp-Up:** The Missile Solutions IPO and $1B Pentagon preferred equity investment are designed to accelerate solid rocket motor production at a time when munitions stockpiles are depleted and demand significantly exceeds supply. The $1.27B Virginia manufacturing expansion will more than double production capacity. Aerojet Rocketdyne is effectively the sole domestic provider of many solid rocket motor types, holding a near-monopoly in a supply-constrained market where the DoD is mandating production increases.

**3. Margin Expansion:** The LHX NeXt transformation program transitions from investment phase ($167M in FY2025 implementation costs) to harvest phase in FY2026, as these costs drop to zero and savings flow through. Management guides segment operating margins to the "low 16% range," roughly 120bps above FY2025. Combined with 7% revenue growth, this produces the double-digit EPS growth implied by FY2026 guidance.

The VAMPIRE counter-drone system entering high-volume production addresses the highest-priority battlefield requirement validated in Ukraine: affordable counter-UAS capability. New variants span land, maritime, air, and electronic warfare applications.

The counter to the growth story is the negative estimate revision trend: FY2026 EPS consensus has fallen from $12.43 to $11.65 (-6.3%) over 90 days, and current-quarter estimates fell from $2.72 to $2.57 (-5.2%). This likely reflects uncertainty around the Missile Solutions separation mechanics, tariff exposure, and defense topline flatness before reconciliation funds flow. If revisions accelerate further, the growth thesis becomes questionable at current multiples.

The IPO could serve as a value crystallization event if the market assigns a premium multiple to the munitions-focused entity.

The valuation is the weakest part of the thesis. At 29x forward, LHX requires execution on both growth and margin expansion to justify the multiple. A score of 5.0 reflects adequate-but-not-cheap pricing for a high-quality defense franchise with a specific catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

L3Harris occupies a differentiated position in the defense tier structure. Unlike LMT or NOC, which compete primarily as platform primes on large programs (F-35, B-21), L3Harris operates as a platform-agnostic provider of critical subsystems, sensors, communications, and propulsion across many platforms. This diversified exposure reduces single-program cancellation risk.

**Key competitive advantages:**

1. **Sole-source solid rocket motor production:** The Aerojet Rocketdyne acquisition created a near-monopoly in many solid rocket motor types. With munitions production as the Pentagon's top industrial priority, this is the most strategically valuable position in the defense supply chain.

2. **CS segment margins:** 25.2% operating margin in tactical communications and night vision is best-in-class. This franchise has pricing power derived from proprietary waveforms, classification requirements, and interoperability standards.

3. **Platform-agnostic model:** Serving as prime, merchant supplier, or subcontractor across hundreds of programs provides revenue resilience.

4. **Strategic partnerships:** Collaborations with Anduril, Palantir, Amazon Kuiper, and Shield Capital position L3Harris at the intersection of traditional defense and emerging technology without requiring the company to build all capabilities internally.

**Competitive risks:** Non-traditional entrants (Anduril, SpaceX, Shield AI) are increasingly winning contracts through Other Transaction Authority, which bypasses traditional procurement barriers. The DoD's emphasis on acquisition speed favors these nimbler competitors. L3Harris's partnership strategy partially hedges this risk, but competitive displacement in select areas (autonomy, AI-native systems) is a medium-term concern.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Missile Solutions IPO Execution Risk:** The planned H2 2026 IPO involves separating a recently acquired business, managing the $1B convertible preferred stock conversion, and maintaining operational continuity. Execution failures — delayed IPO, unfavorable valuation, loss of synergies — could disappoint the market.

**2. Estimate Revision Momentum:** If the downward revision trend accelerates, the 29x multiple compresses. The primary question is whether revisions reflect one-time items (separation costs, tariff uncertainty) or structural growth deceleration.

**3. U.S. Government Budget Concentration:** 75% of revenue from U.S. Government. The company navigated a 43-day government shutdown in Oct-Nov 2025 without material impact, but continuing resolutions, sequestration risk, or a political shift away from defense spending would directly affect revenue.

**4. Fixed-Price Contract Risk:** 75% of revenue on fixed-price terms. MD&A disclosed $38M negative EAC adjustments on a classified Maritime program and $25M on the Canadian Maritime Helicopter Program. Cost overruns on fixed-price development contracts remain an ongoing risk.

**5. Tariff Exposure:** The 10-K explicitly flags new and increased tariffs beginning in early 2025, affecting imported materials and Canadian operations. While FY2025 was not materially impacted, FY2026 cost structures could be affected if exemptions are not obtained.

**6. Goodwill Impairment Risk:** $20B in goodwill (48% of total assets). All reporting units cleared 30% in FY2025 impairment testing, and the $85M impairment on the Space Technology disposal group was isolated to a divestiture. Rising discount rates or program cancellations could trigger broader impairments.

The call-heavy OI and declining IV support the fundamental thesis. The backwardated term structure warrants attention: the market sees more risk in the next 1-4 weeks than over 6 months, possibly related to upcoming earnings or geopolitical developments.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($400-$443; +18-30%)

Defense spending accelerates as reconciliation funds flow into FY2027 procurement. Missile Solutions IPO in H2 2026 achieves a premium valuation (20x+ operating income), crystallizing value and re-rating the stub. Segment margins hit the guided "low 16%" range, driving FY2026 EPS toward $12.00-12.50. FCF reaches $3.2B+. VAMPIRE wins additional international contracts. The stock re-rates to 30-33x forward earnings on proven execution.

### Bear Case ($280-$310; -9 to -18%)

Estimate revisions continue declining, with FY2026 EPS settling at $10.50-11.00 on tariff headwinds, Missile Solutions separation costs, and slower-than-expected munitions ramp. IPO is delayed to 2027 or achieves a below-expectations valuation. Fixed-price contract overruns widen. The market compresses the multiple to 25-27x on slowing earnings momentum and general risk-off sentiment. Defense spending flatlines under continuing resolutions.

## Conclusion

L3Harris Technologies is a high-quality defense franchise positioned in the highest-priority spending categories (munitions, missile defense, counter-UAS) during the most favorable global defense cycle in decades. FY2025 demonstrated strong FCF generation, consistent earnings beats, and progress on the LHX NeXt operational transformation. The Missile Solutions IPO represents a concrete, timed catalyst for value crystallization in H2 2026.

The stock is not cheap at 29x forward earnings, and negative estimate revision momentum adds risk. The $26.5B goodwill load and 75% fixed-price contract exposure require continuous monitoring. However, the combination of 7% organic revenue growth guidance, meaningful margin expansion, $3B+ FCF, and the IPO catalyst creates a favorable risk-reward profile at current levels. The prior score trajectory (6.7 → 6.4) reflected growing valuation concern as the stock rallied; the current 6.3 score continues this trend. Despite the declining trajectory, the structural thesis has strengthened with the DoD preferred equity investment and manufacturing expansion announcements, supporting a BUY recommendation with a $390 base case target.
