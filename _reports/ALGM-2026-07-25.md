**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Allegro MicroSystems is the world's leading supplier of magnetic sensor ICs, paired with a fast-growing application-specific power IC franchise. The investment story over the past year has been a textbook analog-semiconductor cycle: revenue collapsed 31% from a ~$1.05B FY24 peak to $725M in FY25 as automotive customers digested inventory, then inflected sharply into recovery in FY26. Nine-month FY26 net sales reached $646.9M (+21.6% YoY) with a return to GAAP profitability ($1.8M net income vs. a -$58.2M loss the prior-year period), and the December quarter grew 28.9% with Power ICs up 42.8%. The recovery is broad-based across automotive (xEV/ADAS content growth) and industrial, with data center emerging as the fastest-growing contributor.

The market has recognized this. The stock has appreciated roughly 124% over six months to a 52-week high of $71.77 before pulling back to the mid-$40s amid a semiconductor-sector profit-taking selloff and the fading of momentum. At $46.03, ALGM trades at roughly 45x the $1.03 current-year consensus EPS and ~31x the $1.49 next-year estimate. This premium multiple requires two things to hold: continued double-digit revenue growth off the trough, and gross-margin recovery from the current ~46-47% toward the company's historical mid-50s peak. The first is happening; the second is the central unresolved question and the swing factor for the thesis.

We rate ALGM a HOLD (growth_hold). The franchise quality is high: a widening moat in magnetic sensors, deliberate positioning into AI data-center power conversion and robotics, an asset-light and now solidly FCF-generative model. But the valuation prices in much of the near-term upside, the balance of risks (automotive concentration, rare-earth supply exposure, a residual ~32% Sanken stake, and near-total leadership turnover) is unusually heavy for an analog name, and the options market is pricing an imminent ±25% move with rich implied vol. The score trajectory has been improving (4.8 → 5.8 → 6.1), and the fundamental recovery confirms that trend, but a fourth consecutive HOLD at a higher price with the same unresolved margin question warrants patience on entry rather than a rating upgrade.

## Company Overview

Allegro is a fabless analog and mixed-signal designer headquartered in Manchester, New Hampshire, with ~4,250 employees. It operates a wholly-owned assembly-and-test facility (AMPI) in the Philippines and outsources wafer fabrication to UMC, Polar, Tower, and TSMC. The company reports as a single segment but sells across two product lines:

- **Magnetic Sensors (~60% of revenue):** Hall-effect, GMR, and TMR ("XtremeSense") sensors measuring current, position, speed, and angle. TMR, acquired via Crocus, is the strategic growth pillar.
- **Power ICs (~40%, growing fastest):** motor drivers, voltage regulators, PMICs, LED drivers, and isolated gate drivers (from the Heyday acquisition), built on proprietary 120V BCD process technology.

End markets skew ~71% automotive (xEV powertrain, ADAS, ICE, body electronics) and ~29% industrial and other. The company ships ~2.1B units annually to more than 15,000 customers, with roughly 90% of sales outside the U.S. Greater China is the single largest and fastest-growing geography. Approximately 55% of sales flow through distributors.

The moat rests on the broadest magnetic-sensor portfolio in the industry, automotive-grade reliability (AEC-Q100 Grade 0) that also wins rugged industrial business, proprietary high-voltage/high-temperature packaging, ~1,860 active patents, and 2-4 year design cycles that create genuine switching costs. No single competitor spans all of Allegro's lines, though it faces larger, better-resourced rivals in TI, Infineon, NXP, Melexis, and onsemi.

## Financial Analysis

The financial picture is a cyclical trough giving way to a sharp recovery, with cash-flow generation the standout feature.

**Revenue and profitability.** Q3 FY26 (December quarter) automotive rose 27.9% and industrial 31.3%. By product, Power ICs grew 42.8% and Magnetic Sensors 21.0%. The company returned to GAAP profitability, though the quality of reported net income is distorted: the December-quarter effective tax rate of roughly -1,593% (a large benefit from the OBBB Act restoring domestic R&D expensing) inflated net income to $8.4M against operating income of only $9.6M (4.2% operating margin). The cleaner read is operating income and a normalized tax rate, which shows a business still early in its margin recovery.

**Gross margin — the key metric.** Gross margin recovered to 46.7% in Q3 (46.0% for the nine months) from 45.4% a year prior, but remains ~8-9 points below the mid-50s historical peak. This gap reflects lingering fixed-cost underabsorption from the trough. Whether margins re-inflate on higher volumes and richer mix (TMR, isolated gate drivers, data-center content) is the single most important variable for the thesis. If margins stall near 47%, the current multiple is difficult to defend.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** The asset-light model shines in recovery: nine-month operating cash flow was $127.4M (vs. $41.6M the prior year) against just $21.2M of capex — strong free-cash-flow conversion. Cash stood at $155.2M; total debt ~$287M (term loan paid down voluntarily from $345M to $285M), with $256M of undrawn revolver capacity and equity of $964M. Net leverage sits well below the 4.0x covenant. The January 2026 Amendment No. 4 repriced the term loan to SOFR+1.75% (from +2.00%) with 0% amortization and a 2030 maturity, cutting Q3 interest expense to $4.9M from $7.8M. The balance sheet carries $203M goodwill and $245M intangibles from acquisitions.

**Earnings quality caveats.** Beyond the tax distortion, the Polar Semiconductor equity investment (~10.2% stake) has flipped from small gains to recurring losses — $3.6M in Q3 and $6.7M for the nine months, with carrying value down to $25.0M. This is a modest but deteriorating drag that also ties Allegro's supply chain to a sub-scale domestic fab from which it buys in-process wafers (~$29.8M over nine months).

## Growth Analysis

Allegro sits at the intersection of three secular content-growth themes, which is precisely why the stock re-rated:

1. **Vehicle electrification and ADAS.** Rising sensor and power content per vehicle as architectures move from 12V to 48V to 800V, plus electro-mechanical braking and steer-by-wire. This is the core of the automotive franchise.
2. **AI data centers.** Allegro's motor drivers spin cooling fans and pumps, and its TMR/Hall current sensors and isolated gate drivers enable high-density 48V/800V power conversion for GaN and SiC systems. Isolated gate drivers remain in customer sampling for next-generation designs — future optionality, not yet material revenue.
3. **Robotics / "physical AI."** Multiple motors plus position and current sensors per robot joint.

The mechanism connecting these to earnings is content growth: even flat unit volumes in a given end market translate to revenue growth as the dollar content of Allegro silicon per vehicle or per rack rises. The estimate-revision data supports near-term momentum but is not the extreme inflection signal that would flag a clear buy. Current-year EPS estimates rose 6.5% over 90 days (though down 2.2% over 30 days), and next-quarter estimates rose 2.6%. Next-year estimates were cut 2.7% over 90 days. Revision velocity is positive but modest, well short of the +30% threshold that would carry standalone weight. The four-quarter earnings track record is four straight in-line prints (surprises of 2-5%), indicating tight visibility but no upside surprise pattern.

The broader semiconductor demand environment is supportive at the infrastructure layer — data-center power delivery, electrification content, and robotics are confirmed multi-year themes — but Allegro's own risk language warns that front-loaded AI infrastructure investment could reverse and make downturns sharper. The growth is real; its durability through the next cycle is the open question.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E is meaningless (negative on GAAP due to tax and one-time distortions), so forward and normalized metrics govern.

Against Semiconductor peers, the picture is mixed. Peer median P/E is 30.2x (SWKS 30.2x, QRVO 27.0x, CRUS 20.8x), with several names showing negative or distressed multiples (MXL -53x, CBRS 494x). Allegro's ~31x next-year multiple is a premium to that peer median, which is defensible given its magnetic-sensor leadership, higher structural gross margins, and the electrification/AI content-growth optionality — but only if margins recover and growth durability holds. On PEG, the ~31x forward multiple against next-year EPS growth of ~45% (from $1.03 to $1.49) yields a PEG below 0.7, which looks reasonable on the surface. The problem is that the $1.49 estimate embeds a margin recovery that has not yet been demonstrated, and next-year estimates were revised down over the trailing 90 days.

The lessons of this system caution against treating "cheap" as a buy signal and against penalizing prior appreciation. Neither applies cleanly here: ALGM is not cheap on any near-term metric, and the 124% run is not itself a reason to avoid. The binding constraint is that the current price already discounts the successful transition from cyclical auto supplier to AI/electrification content story. Analyst consensus targets — mean $56.55, median $55.00, range $48-$67 — sit above the current price, but the sentiment trend is degrading (a strong-buy was downgraded to hold in the most recent period). On a stock at 45x current-year earnings, consensus targets reflect the same growth extrapolation as the price rather than independent confirmation of undervaluation.

Our fair-value framework: applying a normalized mid-cycle margin recovery toward 50% gross margin and ~$1.35 of normalized current-year earnings power at a 30-35x multiple supports a base case around $45-50 — essentially the current price. The bull case, requiring margins toward the mid-50s and sustained data-center content ramp, supports $60-67. The bear case, a margin stall and automotive softening, points to $32-36. This is a symmetric-to-slightly-unfavorable setup at $46, which drives the below-neutral valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

Allegro's competitive position is improving, which is the strongest pillar of the thesis. It holds the #1 share position in magnetic sensors with the broadest portfolio in the industry, and its power IC line is growing faster than sensors, diversifying the revenue base away from pure automotive sensing. The deliberate pivot into data-center power delivery and robotics — supported by leadership hires from Texas Instruments and Wolfspeed with power-semiconductor pedigree — positions the company for content growth themes the market is only beginning to credit at the sensor/power-IC layer.

The moat characteristics are durable: automotive-grade reliability applied as a baseline, proprietary high-voltage packaging, ~1,860 patents, and multi-year design cycles that lock in revenue and raise switching costs. The counterweight is scale. TI, Infineon, NXP, and onsemi are far larger and better-resourced, and while none spans Allegro's full product range, they compete aggressively in adjacent power and sensing categories. In the data-center power segment specifically, Allegro is a newer entrant against established analog incumbents, and isolated gate drivers remain in sampling rather than revenue.

The trajectory — gaining Power IC share, entering high-growth adjacencies, widening the sensor lead — supports a competitive-position score of 7.5. This aligns with the prior report's assessment and the fundamental data confirms it.

## Risk Assessment

Allegro carries an unusually heavy risk load for an analog franchise, which is why the risk score sits at neutral despite the recovery.

- **China rare-earth / samarium export restrictions.** Samarium is used in Allegro's magnetic sensors, and the 2025 restrictions also caused auto customers to pause production, hitting demand downstream. This is a genuinely differentiated supply-chain risk versus peers.
- **Automotive concentration (~71%) plus cyclicality.** The FY24→FY25 -31% revenue collapse demonstrates the downside amplitude. Any xEV/ADAS slowdown or EV subsidy reduction hits the growth thesis directly.
- **Single assembly/test facility (AMPI, Philippines).** Hard to replace quickly; the 10-K newly flags a Philippine "state of energy emergency" and Strait of Hormuz shipping-cost pressure amid the Middle East conflict.
- **Foundry concentration** in Taiwan (UMC, TSMC) adds earthquake and cross-strait geopolitical exposure, with no long-term contracts for some suppliers.
- **Sanken overhang.** Former controlling shareholder Sanken Electric still holds ~32.2% after the 2024 buyback. This is a large potential share-supply overhang that would pressure the stock if sold.
- **AI-demand reflexivity.** Management explicitly warns that front-loaded AI infrastructure investment could reverse, making the next downturn more abrupt — the flip side of the narrative driving the re-rating.
- **Leadership turnover.** Near-wholesale executive refresh in ~2 years (CEO, sales, products, operations, CHRO, new directors), with an internally-promoted CEO unproven in the top seat. The hires are strategically aligned to the data-center pivot but execution risk is real.

Most of these are partially priced given the stock's volatility, but the concentration of binary supply-chain and governance overhangs alongside a full valuation keeps the risk score at 5.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is flashing near-term stress. ATM implied volatility of 114.5% is rich versus 64.5% historical vol (a 50-point spread) and sits at the 84th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (114.5% near-term vs. 101.7% long-term), which typically signals the market pricing an imminent catalyst. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±25.3%. Put/call skew is balanced (-1.6%), but the put/call OI ratio jumped from 0.14 to 1.55 since early June while the price fell 8.7%, and put/call volume ratio is elevated at 19.82. Short interest is 15.8% of float (4.5 days to cover), consistent with institutional bearish positioning. The combination of rich IV, backwardation, rising put OI, and elevated short interest argues for patience on entry and against chasing the stock into a volatile print.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($60-67)

Allegro is transitioning from a cyclical auto-chip supplier to a structural content-growth story spanning electrification, AI data-center power, and robotics. Revenue continues its double-digit recovery, Power ICs sustain 40%+ growth, and gross margins re-inflate toward the mid-50s peak on higher volumes and richer mix (TMR, isolated gate drivers, data-center content). The asset-light model converts this into rapidly rising free cash flow, and the debt paydown plus lower financing cost amplify EPS. At a sustained 35x on normalized earnings power approaching $1.80-2.00, the stock reaches $60-67, matching the analyst high target and the recent 52-week high.

### Bear Case ($32-36)

Gross margins stall near 47% as competition from larger analog players compresses pricing and fixed-cost absorption lags. Automotive demand softens on EV subsidy cuts or a cyclical rollover, and rare-earth supply disruption or a demand air pocket triggers a sharper downturn (which management itself warns AI front-loading could exacerbate). The Sanken 32% stake begins to unwind, adding share supply. Multiple compresses toward the peer median of ~30x on cut estimates, and the stock returns to the low-to-mid $30s, roughly 22-30% below the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth rationale: a franchise worth owning through the electrification/AI content cycle, but at a price that offers little margin of safety today.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Gross margin recovers above 50% for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating the mix/volume leverage the bull case requires.
- Next-year EPS estimate revisions turn decisively positive (reversing the -2.7% 90-day trend) while revenue growth holds above 20%.
- A pullback toward the high-$30s to low-$40s, which would restore asymmetric upside to the analyst mean of $56.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Gross margin stalls or declines below 46% while revenue growth decelerates below 10% YoY, signaling the recovery has run its course without margin leverage.
- Sanken announces or executes a disposition of its ~32% stake at a discount.
- Automotive revenue contracts YoY for two consecutive quarters, indicating a cyclical rollover rather than a mid-cycle plateau.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next quarterly earnings report is the natural checkpoint, given the options market is pricing a ±25% move around it. Monitor gross margin, automotive/data-center segment growth, and any 8-K activity around the Sanken stake or further leadership changes.

## Conclusion

Allegro is a high-quality analog franchise recovering strongly from a deep 2025 trough, with genuine multi-year content-growth optionality in EVs, AI data-center power delivery, and robotics — themes confirmed broadly at the infrastructure layer. The competitive position is improving, the balance sheet is sound, and the asset-light model is generating strong free cash flow. Those attributes support ownership.

The constraint is price and risk. The stock discounts a successful transition and a margin recovery that has not yet been demonstrated. Layered on top are automotive concentration, a differentiated rare-earth supply exposure, a Sanken overhang, and near-total leadership turnover, with the options market pricing an imminent move. The improving score trajectory reflects real fundamental progress, and this analysis confirms rather than reverses it, but a fourth consecutive HOLD at a higher price with the same open margin question does not clear the bar for a BUY. We rate ALGM a **HOLD** and would become more constructive on either confirmed margin re-inflation above 50% or a pullback toward the low-$40s.
