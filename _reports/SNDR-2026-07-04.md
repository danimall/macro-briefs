**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

*Class B Common Stock | NYSE: SNDR | Industrials — Cargo Ground Transportation | ~$3.3B market cap | $35.80*

## Executive Summary

Schneider National is a well-capitalized, multimodal truckload/intermodal/logistics operator in the third year of a deep freight recession. The reported numbers describe a trough: FY2025 net income of $104M (down from $458M at the 2022 peak), an operating ratio that worsened to 97.0% for the year and 97.6% in Q1 2026, and net income that fell 22% YoY in the most recent quarter with all three segments' operating income declining 20-21%. The 2025 revenue increase of 7% was almost entirely acquired; the December 2024 Cowan acquisition added Dedicated and Logistics volume while diluting margins and raising interest expense. Underlying legacy Network volume fell 4% and brokerage volume declined.

Against this deteriorating reported picture, the stock has rallied 39% over twelve months to $35.80, sitting near its $39.27 52-week high, and trades at roughly 64x trailing earnings. The entire bull case rests on a cyclical recovery that is visible only in rising sell-side estimates — current-year EPS consensus rose 9.5% and next-year 13.8% over 90 days — not in reported results. This is a "survive the trough, lever the upturn" profile with a genuinely strong balance sheet (near-zero net debt, positive free cash flow even in the downturn), but the market has already priced a recovery that reported fundamentals have not begun to deliver.

We rate SNDR a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The financial resilience and rising estimates argue against an AVOID, but the valuation offers no margin of safety against a recovery that fails to materialize on schedule, and a live, unquantified nuclear-verdict liability plus shareholder-unfriendly dual-class governance cap the upside. This continues a two-report AVOID history that has improved to a marginal call as the balance-sheet and estimate-revision data have strengthened; we do not see enough to justify a BUY at current prices.

## Company Overview

Schneider National, headquartered in Green Bay, Wisconsin, is one of North America's largest transportation and logistics providers, operating three reportable segments:

- **Truckload (~44% of revenue, largest profit contributor):** Split into Dedicated (equipment and drivers committed to customers under long-term contracts, ~8,500 trucks) and Network (one-way spot/contract freight, ~3,700 trucks). Dedicated is the strategic growth engine and the industry's preferred defensive niche due to contracted, stickier revenue.
- **Intermodal (~19%):** Asset-based, door-to-door container-on-rail with company drayage, ~26,400 company-owned containers, running on CSX, Union Pacific, and CPKC.
- **Logistics (~24%, asset-light):** Freight brokerage (traditional plus Power Only using company trailer pools), 3PL/supply chain, and warehousing, including the FreightPower digital marketplace.

The company's structural advantages are scale, a multimodal platform that lets it reallocate capital across modes through the cycle, a low-leverage balance sheet, and a captive insurance company. Strategy centers on Dedicated growth, asset-light/specialty expansion, and technology-enabled cost transformation.

## Financial Analysis

**Cyclical trough with fundamentals still compressing.** The peak-to-trough deterioration is severe:

| Metric | 2022 (peak) | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $6,604M | $5,290M | $5,674M | $1,398M |
| Net income | $458M | $117M | $104M | $20.4M |
| Op. ratio (GAAP) | ~91% | 96.9% | 97.0% | 97.6% |

Despite higher revenue, net income fell from $117M to $104M because Cowan added salaries (+13%, +$185M), depreciation and amortization (+9%), operating supplies (+14%), and interest expense (+$17M). Insurance costs rose 24% in 2025 on rising premiums and adverse prior-year claims development, a structural pressure tied to the hardening insurance market.

**Cash generation and balance sheet are genuine strengths.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $637M and free cash flow $348M (up from $306M as capex was cut). Q1 2026 free cash flow turned positive at $48M versus -$5M a year earlier as transportation-equipment capex was slashed to roughly $41M from $114M. Total debt of ~$399M against ~$3.0B of equity and $265M of cash plus securities leaves net debt minimal; the company is fully covenant-compliant with ~$332M of undrawn credit capacity. FCF conversion in the trough (FCF/net income above 100% for 2025) signals high-quality earnings and defensive staying power. The 2026 capex guidance of $400-450M implies expected fleet reinvestment, which will reduce free cash flow as volumes recover.

**Capital returns are modest and dilution-focused.** The quarterly dividend is $0.10/share (~1.1% yield). The new $150M three-year buyback authorized in January 2026 is largely intended to offset dilution, with only ~$5M repurchased in Q1'26.

This is a financially healthy but cyclically depressed business; the balance sheet is a clear strength, with financial health held back by thin trough profitability and the acquisition-added debt/interest load. ## Growth Analysis

The forward growth thesis is entirely a cyclical-recovery bet. Reported results show no organic growth: legacy Network volume down 4% in 2025, brokerage volume shrinking (partly self-inflicted by tightened carrier qualification to reduce cargo-theft exposure), and all three segments' operating income declining in Q1 2026.

The offsetting signal is estimate momentum, which is the most reliable forward indicator in the data. Consensus current-year EPS rose from $0.84 to $0.92 over 90 days, next-year from $1.38 to $1.58, and next-quarter estimates rose 15.8% over 90 days. Revisions are broadly positive and the sell-side is modeling a freight-rate recovery off the trough. This does not meet the high-conviction signal that pairs +30% estimate revisions with 30%+ revenue growth; SNDR's revenue is flat and its revision velocity, while positive, is moderate. The recent 30-day revisions have largely flattened (current quarter -0.0%, current year +0.8%), suggesting the initial upgrade wave has already occurred.

Genuine positives exist beneath the surface: Network revenue per truck per week rose 7% YoY in Q1 2026 (a rate/productivity gain), management is executing headcount and professional-services cost reductions to protect margins, and the Dedicated tilt via Cowan is strategically sound as the industry's most defensible revenue category. If freight rates recover and Cowan synergies materialize, operating leverage on a 97.6% operating ratio is substantial: a 200bps OR improvement on ~$5.7B of revenue is over $110M of pre-tax income. The growth case is real but unconfirmed by reported data, warranting a middling score.

## Valuation Assessment

At $35.80 the stock trades at ~64x trailing earnings on $0.59 TTM EPS and ~39x the $0.92 current-year consensus. Even on the optimistic $1.58 next-year estimate, the forward multiple is ~23x, reasonable for a cyclical recovering off a trough, but that estimate is a projection, not delivered earnings, and the stock already discounts it.

Peer comparisons are distorted by trough earnings across the group: ARCB at 70x, MRTN at 103x, and WERN/RXO/HTLD showing negative P/Es. The peer median P/E of 102.9 is not a meaningful anchor when the entire sector is at cyclical lows. On a normalized basis, SNDR earned $458M at the 2022 peak (roughly $2.62/share on today's ~175M total shares) and $117M in 2024 (~$0.67); mid-cycle earnings power somewhere between those extremes would support the current price only if the recovery arrives.

The valuation problem is timing and cushion. The stock sits near its 52-week high after a 39% rally and above its entire 2025 trading range, meaning the market has already priced a recovery that reported results have not begun to deliver. The analyst mean target of $34.64 and median of $34.50 sit slightly below the current price, with a high of $39.00 (9% upside) and a low of $27.00 (25% downside). On a genuine recovery stock at a reasonable multiple we would treat consensus targets as stale floors, but here the stock trades above the mean target while forward P/E remains near 39x current-year, so the guardrail against treating targets as floors applies. There is no independent catalyst to argue the market is mispricing this cheaply; the setup is asymmetric to the downside because a delayed recovery derates a fully-priced stock. Cheapness is absent, and the valuation score reflects a stretched multiple against unproven forward earnings.

## Competitive Landscape

The truckload/logistics industry is highly fragmented and intensely price-competitive, currently mired in overcapacity and soft rates. SNDR competes with large TL carriers, brokers, LTL operators, growing private fleets, and digital brokerage entrants. Its differentiation is multimodal flexibility (allowing capital reallocation across modes and cycles) and a strong balance sheet that lets it survive the trough better than leveraged peers.

Two structural dynamics matter most. First, the proposed $85B Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern merger would create the first transcontinental railroad and could reduce SNDR's rail-contract leverage in Intermodal while risking integration-related service disruption; the STB paused its review in May 2026, leaving a multi-quarter overhang. Second, "nuclear verdict" insurance/legal inflation is an industry-wide margin and balance-sheet threat that has directly hit SNDR. On trajectory, SNDR's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving (the Dedicated tilt strengthens revenue durability), but the company has modestly lagged its peer group on TSR (137.2 vs. 149.3 in the March 2026 proxy), and there is no evidence of decisive share gains. As an Industrials name, the sector carries a structurally negative alpha profile in our framework, which reinforces a need for company-specific edge that is not clearly present here. A neutral score is appropriate.

## Risk Assessment

**Nuclear verdict (material, unquantified):** A 2024 adverse jury verdict stemming from a 2017 fatal accident allegedly caused by a Schneider driver exhausted an entire policy year's excess insurance tower. Interest is accruing on the judgment and management explicitly cannot estimate resolution timing. This is the single most important idiosyncratic risk; it hit interest expense in 2025 and reflects broader industry exposure to escalating awards, rising premiums, and shrinking excess coverage.

**Prolonged freight recession:** The business is highly cyclical with limited long-term volume guarantees outside Dedicated. Overcapacity keeps rates depressed and Q1 2026 shows the trough persisting. A recovery that slips further into 2026-2027 would strand a fully-priced stock.

**Cowan integration:** All of 2025's growth rode on Cowan, yet it diluted margins and added debt. Synergy realization is unproven, and $337M of goodwill could face impairment if Dedicated underperforms.

**UP-NS merger:** A structural Intermodal risk with an unresolved STB review.

**Governance:** The Schneider family Voting Trust owns 100% of Class A (10 votes/share) and ~41% of Class B, giving ~94% of total voting power on ~69% of the economic equity. SNDR is a controlled company exempt from fully independent compensation and governance committees, with heavy anti-takeover provisions. Class B holders are economic passengers with no governance leverage and no realistic takeover-premium optionality, a legitimate valuation/governance discount.

Additional risks include owner-operator reclassification, tariff-driven freight-demand softness, and the uncertain implications of the February 2026 EPA rule eliminating federal vehicle GHG standards for equipment residual values. The Q1 2026 10-Q reports no material changes to risk factors versus the FY2025 10-K.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 30.0% against 39.8% one-year historical volatility — a -9.8% spread that places IV in the 6th percentile of the current chain, meaning options are cheap relative to realized vol. The put/call skew is -8.5% (call-heavy) at the front month, and the term structure is roughly flat (near 30.0% vs. far 31.7%). Near-dated positioning is mildly constructive and complacent rather than pricing imminent distress. The one caution is the 3-month tenor, which shows IV spiking to 46.4% (14.1% implied move), consistent with elevated uncertainty around the next earnings print. Separately, short interest is elevated at 13.8% of float with 6.9 days to cover, indicating meaningful institutional bearishness and creating modest squeeze potential on any positive surprise. The options and short-interest signals point in opposite directions, reinforcing the two-sided, uncertain read.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$45-50):** Freight rates recover off the multi-year trough in H2 2026, and the operating ratio compresses back toward the mid-90s. Operating leverage on flat-to-rising revenue drives EPS toward the $1.58 next-year consensus and beyond. Cowan synergies materialize, lifting Dedicated margins. The fortress balance sheet lets SNDR reinvest into the upturn while leveraged peers cannot. Rising estimates continue, the nuclear verdict resolves without further material charge, and the stock re-rates on delivered mid-cycle earnings power.

**Bear Case (~$25-27):** The freight recession extends into 2027, rate recovery fails to arrive on schedule, and a fully-priced stock derates as consensus estimates roll back over. The nuclear verdict resolves adversely or a new large verdict lands, and hardening insurance costs keep compressing margins. Cowan disappoints, triggering goodwill impairment. The UP-NS merger closes on terms unfavorable to SNDR's Intermodal economics. At $25-27 the stock would trade back to its late-2025 range, roughly 25% below current levels, matching the low analyst target.

The base case is a slow, uneven recovery already largely reflected in the price, leaving limited upside and asymmetric downside if timing disappoints.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).**

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural reassessment point is the Q2 2026 earnings print (late July / early August 2026), which will test whether the freight rate recovery embedded in rising estimates is showing up in reported operating-ratio improvement. The 3-month options tenor pricing a 14% implied move confirms the market views this print as a binary.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Operating ratio improves below 96.5% (from 97.6%) in a reported quarter, confirming genuine margin inflection rather than estimate optimism.
2. Two consecutive quarters of positive organic (ex-Cowan) volume or revenue-per-truck growth alongside continued positive estimate revisions.
3. A pullback toward the low-to-mid $20s that restores a margin of safety against the recovery thesis, or explicit resolution of the nuclear verdict without material incremental charge.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q2/Q3 2026 operating ratio deteriorates further or estimates begin falling (30-day revisions turning negative), signaling the recovery has stalled while the stock remains richly valued.
2. An adverse nuclear-verdict resolution or a new large verdict, or a Cowan goodwill impairment.
3. UP-NS merger approval on terms that materially impair Intermodal contract economics.

## Conclusion

Schneider is a defensively financed cyclical trading on a recovery that its reported fundamentals have not yet delivered. The balance sheet is genuinely strong, free cash flow is positive even in the trough, and rising 2026-27 estimates point to a sell-side consensus that the freight cycle is bottoming. But at ~64x trailing and ~39x current-year earnings, near its 52-week high and above the mean analyst target, the stock offers no valuation cushion; the market is paying today for a margin inflection that Q1 2026 has not begun to show. Layered on top are a live, unquantified nuclear-verdict liability, an unresolved UP-NS merger overhang, unproven Cowan integration, and dual-class governance that leaves Class B holders as economic passengers.

The trajectory has improved from a two-report AVOID as the balance-sheet and estimate data have strengthened, and we move to HOLD to reflect that. We require confirmation — an actual operating-ratio inflection in reported results, or a more attractive entry price — before recommending purchase. For now the risk/reward is balanced-to-slightly-negative, and patience through the Q2 2026 print is the appropriate posture.
