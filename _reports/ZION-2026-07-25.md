**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Zions Bancorporation is an ~$88 billion-asset commercial bank operating across 11 Western states through a distinctive seven-affiliate model. Over the last twelve months the stock has recovered 25.6% to $69.29, driven by two overlapping forces: a net-interest-margin recovery as the Fed's 2025 rate cuts lowered deposit costs faster than asset yields, and normalization of credit after an October 2025 fraud scare. Q1 2026 delivered diluted EPS of $1.56 (up 38% YoY), NIM of 3.27% (up 17bps), a $7M provision release, CET1 of 11.6%, and tangible book value per share of $41.75 (up 19% YoY). Consensus current-year EPS estimates have been revised up 18% over 30 days and 21.8% over 90 days, though a large piece of that revision reflects a one-time ~$215M pre-tax gain from selling legacy Visa Class B shares recognized in Q2 2026, not a run-rate change.

The business is executing well. Deposit mix is improving (noninterest-bearing deposits at 35% of total), credit is benign (net charge-offs of 0.03% annualized, classified loans down to 3.80% from 4.82%), and liquidity is dramatically stronger than the 2023 crisis period at 134% coverage of uninsured deposits. The $50M NDFI fraud charge-off from late 2025 appears contained: total non-bank financial institution exposure is only $2.0B (3.3% of loans) with $10M nonaccrual after the problem loans were cleaned up.

The reason this remains a HOLD rather than a BUY is that the recovery is largely realized in the price, and a specific overhang caps the upside. Book equity of $7.3B carries a ~$1.9B unrealized AOCI loss, $1.5B of which sits in the held-to-maturity portfolio and is invisible in reported book value. These losses are currently excluded from regulatory capital, which flatters the 11.6% CET1 ratio. The March 2026 Basel III Endgame proposal to include AOCI in regulatory capital is the key swing factor: if enacted as proposed, it would compress the effective capital cushion and could constrain buybacks. At ~11.5x distorted trailing earnings but ~10.2x the cleaner ~$6.81 next-year consensus, Zions trades at a discount to its regional-bank peers (median P/E 12.7). Risk/reward is roughly balanced after the run.

## Company Overview

Zions operates through seven affiliate banks, each with its own local brand and management authority: Zions Bank (Utah/Idaho/Wyoming), California Bank & Trust, Amegy Bank (Texas), National Bank of Arizona, Nevada State Bank, Vectra Bank Colorado, and The Commerce Bank of Washington/Oregon. This decentralized model gives it a genuine commercial and small-business franchise concentrated in high-growth Western markets, with local decision-making. Commercial & industrial loans are the largest segment at roughly 30% of loans, supplemented by SBA lending, municipal banking, and capital-markets fee businesses.

Two structural features distinguish Zions from typical regional banks. First, it operates as a single national association regulated primarily by the OCC rather than as a Fed-regulated bank holding company. This carries real consequences: share repurchases require OCC prior approval, all mergers require shareholder approval, and corporate governance runs under the less-developed National Bank Act framework. Second, geographic concentration is high: Utah, Idaho, Texas, and California together account for 77% of commercial lending, 72% of CRE, and 71% of consumer lending. This concentration exposes the bank to above-average-growth Western economies but leaves it correlated to regional real estate and energy cycles.

## Financial Analysis

Standard bankruptcy and strength screens (Altman Z, Piotroski) are not meaningful for a bank; bank-specific metrics are the relevant lens.

The most recent full filing, Q1 2026, showed strong momentum across the board. Diluted EPS of $1.56 was up 38% from $1.13 a year earlier. Net interest income rose 6% to $662M, and NIM expanded 17bps to 3.27%. The efficiency ratio improved to 65.0% from 66.6%. The bank recorded a $7M provision release versus an $18M provision a year earlier, with net charge-offs of just $4M (0.03% annualized) against $16M (0.11%) in Q1 2025. ROTCE reached 15.5% and CET1 climbed to 11.6% from 10.8%. Tangible book value per share grew to $41.75 from $34.95, a 19% gain.

The NIM expansion is the core earnings story, and the mechanism matters. The Fed's 2025 rate cuts lowered deposit costs (down 28bps) faster than asset yields repriced downward, widening the spread. Zions is also running its low-yielding legacy securities portfolio (down to ~$18B) into higher-yielding loans, a structural mix-shift tailwind that continues independent of the rate path. Part of this NIM recovery is a sector-wide phenomenon affecting funding-liability-sensitive regionals, not solely company-specific execution.

The deposit franchise is improving. Noninterest-bearing deposits rose to 35% of total from 32%, partly from migrating a consumer product to a non-interest offering. Customer deposits (ex-brokered) grew to $73.1B, and the loan-to-deposit ratio sits at a comfortable 80%. Credit quality is benign and improving: NPAs at 0.48% of loans, classified loans down to 3.80% from 4.82% YoY, and ACL/loans at 1.16%. Even office CRE nonaccruals fell from 4.0% to 2.3%.

Capital return is steady: a ~$67M quarterly common dividend ($0.45/share), $77M repurchased in Q1, and a new $225M buyback authorized for the rest of 2026 (subject to OCC approval). The 2.57% dividend yield is FCF-covered and modest relative to peers.

The dominant balance-sheet issue is AOCI and securities losses. AOCI reflects a ~$1.9B net unrealized loss driven by the mark-to-market decline on fixed-rate AFS securities, with $1.5B ($1.2B after-tax) of losses embedded in securities transferred from AFS to HTM. These losses are currently excluded from regulatory capital because Zions is not an advanced-approaches bank, so they do not depress the reported CET1 ratio. Tangible common equity is only ~7.1% of assets, partly because of this AOCI drag. The forward risk is regulatory: the March 2026 Basel III Endgame proposals would require most AOCI to be included in regulatory capital. Management "expects to remain well capitalized," but this is the leverage point that could constrain capital return if enacted.

The Q2 2026 GAAP EPS of $3.05 (beating consensus by ~59%) is inflated by the one-time ~$215M pre-tax Visa Class B share-sale gain disclosed as a subsequent event. Underlying run-rate EPS is closer to the ~$1.56 Q1 level, trending up, not $3.05. The current-quarter and next-quarter estimate revisions of +0.4% (30d) and +1.0–1.1% (90d) are far more representative of the true earnings trajectory than the headline +18% current-year revision, which absorbs the one-time gain.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is bank-earnings growth, not revenue expansion in a secular-growth sense. The near-term earnings trajectory is genuinely positive: NIM recovery, favorable deposit-mix shift, securities-portfolio remixing into loans, and credit normalization all support continued EPS improvement through 2026. The four-quarter beat record (three beats, one miss; the Q2 CY2026 miss of -5.7% on the $1.85/$1.74 estimate/actual, offset by prior beats of 7.3%, 10.4%, and 2.8%) suggests reasonable but not overwhelming forward visibility.

The estimate revision picture requires care. The current-year consensus jumped from $6.40 to $7.80 over 90 days (+21.8%), which on its face is a strong signal. But this revision is dominated by the non-recurring Visa gain rather than a fundamental run-rate acceleration. The forward-looking indicators — next-quarter (+1.1%) and next-year (+1.3%) revisions — are modestly positive but nowhere near the +30% threshold that would flag a durable earnings reacceleration. The next-year consensus of $6.81 is below the distorted current-year figure, confirming that the current-year number is inflated by the one-time item. On a clean basis, this is a mid-single-digit-growth regional bank riding a cyclical NIM tailwind.

Longer-term growth optionality comes from the Basis Multifamily Finance acquisition (agency lending — Fannie Mae DUS and Freddie Mac Optigo origination/servicing), a strategic tuck-in that strengthens the multifamily and CRE capital-markets franchise, subject to regulatory approval. Western-market population and business-formation trends continue to favor the deposit and lending franchise. The structural growth ceiling is set by the bank's regional footprint and its inability to pursue insurance or merchant-banking acquisitions under national-bank rules.

## Valuation Assessment

At $69.29, Zions trades at 8.8x trailing EPS of $6.01. That trailing figure is distorted upward by the ~$215M one-time Visa gain now embedded in the trailing twelve months, so the trailing multiple is not a clean anchor. On the cleaner next-year consensus of $6.81, the stock trades at ~10.2x forward. The peer set — ONB (12.7x), CFR (14.2x), WTFC (13.1x), UMBF (11.8x), COLB (12.2x) — carries a median P/E of 12.7. On both the reported trailing figure (8.8x) and the cleaner forward figure (~10.2x), Zions trades at a discount to peers that partly reflects the AOCI/HTM overhang and higher geographic concentration.

The valuation is not a clear mispricing in either direction. The bull argument is that tangible book value grew 19% YoY to $41.75, the stock trades at ~1.66x TBV, and a 15.5% ROTCE arguably supports a higher multiple. The bear argument is that the ~$1.9B AOCI loss (of which the $1.5B HTM portion is invisible in reported book value) means economic tangible book is materially lower than reported, and the Basel III Endgame proposal could crystallize that gap into a regulatory capital constraint. Analyst targets span $68 (low) to $85 (high) with a $75 median, placing the current price at the bottom of the range and ~8% below the median target.

A cheap-looking regional bank multiple has historically been a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal, and that caution applies here: the discount to peers on clean forward earnings is not deep enough to compensate for the AOCI regulatory overhang and the geographic/CRE concentration. There is no specific catalyst identified that would drive a re-rating from here; the NIM-recovery and credit-normalization catalysts that drove the run are now largely priced. The valuation score reflects a stock trading near fair value with balanced risk/reward.

## Competitive Landscape

Zions competes in attractive, above-average-growth Western markets but faces the classic regional-bank squeeze from larger money-center banks (scale and technology advantages), credit unions, and fintechs. The 10-K devotes unusual attention to emerging-tech disruption — stablecoins, tokenized deposits, the GENIUS Act, and AI — signaling that management sees deposit disintermediation as a real medium-term threat.

Within the regional peer set, Zions differentiates itself on credit discipline (sub-60% CRE LTVs, substantial guarantor and sponsor support) and a granular Western commercial deposit base. Its five-year cumulative TSR of 159.5 modestly beats its peer group's 152.7. Competitive trajectory is stable rather than clearly improving: the NIM recovery is largely a sector tailwind shared by liability-sensitive regionals, and the transparent NDFI disclosure after the October 2025 fraud scare was a credibility-positive response but not a share-gaining move. The Basis Multifamily tuck-in incrementally strengthens the CRE capital-markets franchise. The competitive position is defensible and holding, without evidence of accelerating share gains that would justify a higher score.

## Risk Assessment

**NDFI / non-bank financial institution lending.** In late 2025, Zions took a $50M charge-off on revolving lines to two related commercial borrowers financing mortgage origination (alleged borrower fraud), contributing to a broad October 2025 regional-bank selloff. The Q1 2026 10-Q shows the event is contained: the exposure is well-diversified, with a 0.7% classified ratio after the problem loans were cleaned up. Management raised NDFI reserves to 1.40%. The incident revealed a fraud-detection control gap in a niche vertical but appears discrete and resolved.

**Interest rate / AOCI risk.** The unrealized AOCI loss, with $1.5B buried in HTM (marked at amortized cost, invisible in book value), is the structural overhang. The bank remains modestly asset-sensitive (earnings-at-risk +7.4% in +200bps, -7.2% in -200bps). The Basel III Endgame proposal to include AOCI in regulatory capital is the key regulatory swing factor and the single most important forward risk to capital return.

**CRE concentration in Western markets.** CRE is 22% of loans, with multifamily the largest bucket ($4.1B), plus industrial ($3.1B) and office ($1.6B). About 46% of multifamily and 31% of office matures within 12 months. Credit metrics are currently strong (term CRE weighted-average LTV under 60%, office LTV 57%), but the office criticized-loan ratio is still 7.8% and refinancing risk is elevated in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

**Deposit flight / systemic contagion.** 46% of deposits ($35B) are uninsured, but available liquidity of $46.9B covers uninsured deposits 134% without selling securities, a materially stronger position than in 2023. Instant electronic deposit movement remains a tail risk the 10-K explicitly flags.

**Other.** Niche oil & gas / leveraged / enterprise-value lending (Amegy energy exposure) carries commodity volatility. Regulatory risks include a proposed 10% credit-card rate cap, the GENIUS Act on stablecoins, and OCC-specific governance constraints. Two long-running civil cases (LifeScan/J&J 2017; Roche 2019) alleging Zions liability for a borrower's fraud head to trial April 2027; management believes accruals are adequate.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV of 27.5% sits below the 31.0% one-year historical vol (a -3.5% spread), so options are cheap relative to realized volatility, a favorable entry-timing backdrop rather than a stress signal. The term structure is in contango (27.5% near-term vs 31.9% at six months), consistent with a normal, non-anxious market. Put/call skew of 4.2% shows modestly elevated put demand, and the P/C open-interest ratio rose from 0.14 to 0.58 over the past month, suggesting some incremental hedging into the post-earnings period. The implied one-month move is ±6.0%. Short interest is low at 5.6% of float (4.0 days to cover), meaning the bearish view is uncrowded. On balance, options positioning is close to neutral with a mild hedging tilt, with no strong contradiction of the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The NIM recovery has further to run as the securities-to-loans remix continues and deposit costs stay contained. Credit is genuinely benign (0.03% NCOs, provision releases), and the NDFI incident is resolved. Tangible book value compounds at a mid-teens ROTCE, deposit mix keeps improving toward noninterest-bearing, and the $225M buyback shrinks share count at ~1.66x TBV. If the Basel III Endgame AOCI proposal is softened or delayed, the capital cushion overhang lifts and the multiple re-rates toward the 12.7x peer median. Bull target ~$85 (analyst high), roughly 12x the ~$6.81 next-year clean EPS plus continued TBV accretion.

**Bear Case.** The Basel III Endgame proposal is enacted as drafted, folding the AOCI loss into regulatory capital, compressing the effective cushion and constraining or halting buybacks. A CRE refinancing wave (46% of multifamily and 31% of office maturing within 12 months) collides with elevated vacancies and higher-for-longer rates, driving criticized-loan migration in the office and multifamily books. Deposit competition re-intensifies, reversing the NIM tailwind. Another idiosyncratic credit/fraud pocket surfaces given the demonstrated control gap. Bear target ~$54 (roughly 8x clean forward EPS, near the October 2025 selloff levels), about 22% below current price.

The base case sits between these: continued mid-single-digit clean earnings growth, TBV compounding, a stable dividend, and a multiple that stays near current levels absent a Basel resolution. The recovery that drove the run is largely realized, and the AOCI/Basel overhang caps the near-term re-rating case.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a **growth_hold**: a positive but modest 12-month earnings trajectory with the upside largely priced and a specific regulatory overhang capping the re-rating case.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Basel III Endgame AOCI proposal softened, delayed, or finalized in a form that does not materially compress Zions' effective capital cushion, removing the primary overhang and clearing the path to sustained buybacks.
- Clean (ex-Visa-gain) quarterly EPS run-rate accelerating above ~$1.65 with NIM sustaining above 3.30%, confirming the mix-shift tailwind is durable rather than cyclical.
- A pullback toward the low-$60s (roughly 9x clean forward EPS / ~1.4x TBV), where the discount to peers compensates for the AOCI risk.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Basel III Endgame enacted as proposed, driving a quantified reduction in CET1 that forces a buyback suspension or dividend reassessment.
- Reversal in credit metrics: classified loans re-accelerating above 4.5%, office/multifamily criticized ratios climbing, or a new NDFI-style fraud charge-off, signaling the control gap is not resolved.
- NIM compression below ~3.10% as deposit competition returns, reversing the core earnings driver.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q3 CY2026 earnings release (October 2026) for clean run-rate confirmation, and any formal action on the Basel III Endgame rule. The April 2027 LifeScan/Roche trial is a longer-dated monitoring item.

The trajectory has been HOLD → HOLD → HOLD across three prior reports (6.1 → 6.7 → 6.3), with Valuation declining from 7.2 to 5.5 as the stock re-rated and Risk Factors improving from 4.5 to 6.0 as the NDFI incident resolved. The current data confirms rather than reverses this pattern: fundamentals are solid and improving, the risk profile is better than a year ago, but the price appreciation has consumed most of the valuation cushion. With three consecutive HOLDs, the bar for continuing the call unchanged is higher, but the AOCI/Basel overhang combined with a full valuation relative to clean forward earnings leaves risk/reward balanced. The valuation score is set on current price versus forward fundamentals, not on the prior run.

## Conclusion

Zions has executed a clean recovery. NIM has expanded to 3.27%, credit has normalized with 0.03% charge-offs, CET1 has strengthened to 11.6%, and tangible book value per share is up 19% YoY. The October 2025 NDFI fraud incident is contained. Liquidity is far stronger than during the 2023 regional-bank crisis. Management is disciplined, with an unusually egalitarian pay structure (CEO at 2.5x average NEO comp) and TSR modestly ahead of peers.

The reason to hold rather than buy is that the recovery is largely in the price after a 25.6% twelve-month advance, and a specific overhang caps the upside. The ~$1.9B AOCI/HTM unrealized loss, much of it invisible in reported book value and currently excluded from regulatory capital, is the swing factor the Basel III Endgame proposal could crystallize into a capital constraint. On clean forward earnings the stock trades at ~10.2x, a modest discount to peers that fairly reflects that risk and the bank's geographic and CRE concentration. Estimate revisions look strong at the headline level but are distorted by a one-time Visa gain; the true run-rate trajectory is mid-single-digit growth.

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** Own it for continued TBV compounding, a covered 2.57% yield, and steady buybacks, but the asymmetry that would justify a BUY is absent at $69 with the Basel overhang unresolved. Reassess on a pullback toward the low-$60s or a favorable resolution of the AOCI capital rule.
