## Executive Summary

HEICO delivered the strongest operating quarter in its disclosed history in fiscal Q2 2026 (quarter ended April 30, 2026): revenue $1,375.7M (+25.3%), operating income $350.4M (+41%), diluted EPS $1.66 (+48%) against a $1.33 consensus. The quality of the quarter is better than the headline. Flight Support Group organic growth was +19% and Electronic Technologies Group +17%, with only $75.7M of the quarter's growth attributable to acquisitions, and the 10-Q states explicitly that "sales price changes were not a significant contributing factor" — meaning the 290bp of consolidated operating margin expansion came from volume leverage (SG&A fell 140bp to 15.9% of sales) and mix, not from pushing price through to airlines. Both segments inflected together: FSG margin 26.2% from 24.1%, ETG 26.5% from 22.8%. Consensus has followed: FY2026 EPS estimates are up 8.2% over 90 days to $6.11 and FY2027 up 9.1% to $6.93, and the company has beaten in four consecutive quarters.

The valuation is where the analysis turns. The supplied market capitalization of $20.1B corresponds to the 55.171M Common (HEI) shares alone; HEICO has 139.659M total shares across two economically identical classes. Using the observable relationship between Class A Form 4 transaction prices and same-day Common closes (≈0.75x on both 5/29 and 6/10/2026), total equity value is approximately **$43.6B**, enterprise value roughly **$46.5B**, and the stock trades at **34x TTM EBITDA, 9.5x TTM revenue, and a 2.0–2.3% free cash flow yield**. On the Common price specifically the multiples are 65.6x TTM EPS, 60.2x FY2026E and 53.0x FY2027E. Sell-side targets bracket rather than lead the price: mean $386.53 (+5.2%), median $400 (+8.8%), high $472, low $290, with UBS at Neutral.

The judgment: this is a genuinely improving franchise at a price that already discounts the improvement. My twelve-month base case is $395 (+7.5%), bull $470 (+27.9%), bear $265 (−27.9%); probability-weighting those at roughly 55/20/25 produces an expected return near +3%. That is a **HOLD**. For anyone establishing new exposure, Class A economics are identical while trading at roughly a 25% discount to the Common; the voting premium on HEI is a real cost that has nothing to do with the operating business.

## Company Overview

HEICO is a serial acquirer of niche aerospace and defense franchises, run since May 2025 by co-CEOs Eric and Victor Mendelson (co-chairmen since September 2025), who succeeded their father, who had led HEICO since 1990. Two reported segments:

**Flight Support Group (69% of TTM revenue).** The core asset is FAA Parts Manufacturer Approval (PMA) replacement parts for jet engines and airframes — functional equivalents to OEM spares sold at a discount. FY2025 product-line detail: aftermarket replacement parts $1,922.3M, repair and overhaul $748.8M, specialty products $446.1M. FSG also distributes military parts to the DoD and allied militaries.

**Electronic Technologies Group (31%).** A collection of high-reliability electronics niches — electro-optical simulation and test, power conversion, underwater locator beacons, traveling-wave-tube amplifiers, radiation-hardened memory, crashworthy fuel systems, and the Exxelia passive-component platform. FY2025: $1,142.2M defense/space/aerospace, $270.9M other industries (medical, telecom, industrial).

Two structural features define the model. First, HEICO typically buys 80–96% of a business and leaves founder-management in place with a retained stake plus a put right — 32 separate subsidiary positions with puts extending to fiscal 2036, aggregating $536.7M of redeemable noncontrolling interests at April 30, 2026. Second, Lufthansa Technik holds a 20% interest in HEICO Aerospace Holdings at the subsidiary level, a partnership with a major MRO operator dating to the 1990s. No customer exceeded 10% of consolidated sales in any of the last three years; geographic mix in FY2025 was 61.9% US, 38.1% international.

**Cash conversion is the strongest evidence of earnings quality.** Operating cash flow rose $448.7M → $672.4M → $934.3M across FY2023–FY2025; FY2025 FCF of $861.4M (OCF less $72.9M capex) equals 19.2% of sales, and OCF exceeded consolidated net income of $745.6M — the right direction for an amortization-heavy acquirer. H1 FY26 OCF was $470.6M (+15%) against a $173.5M working-capital build (accrued-expense payments for FY2025 incentive comp and payroll taxes on option exercises, $65.1M higher receivables, $40.5M more inventory to support backlog).

Company-stated debt/equity 53.3%, all covenants met, investment-grade ratings confirmed in the July 16, 2026 notes release. This is appropriate leverage for a cash-generative acquirer with 9x coverage.

Two balance-sheet items require honesty. Goodwill ($4,197.4M) plus intangibles ($1,715.2M) total $5,912.5M — 61.6% of total assets and $1,140.4M more than HEICO shareholders' equity of $4,772.1M, so **tangible book value is negative $1.14B**. That is the arithmetic consequence of thirty years of buying earnings streams, not a distress signal, but it means the equity has no asset backing; value rests entirely on the durability of acquired cash flows, with annual goodwill testing (October 31; no impairment in FY2025) as the pressure point in any demand downturn. And intangible amortization is stepping up: $137.9M in FY2025 rising to a guided $163.1M in FY2027, roughly $25M of pre-tax GAAP headwind or about $0.14 per share after tax — non-cash, so it depresses GAAP EPS and book value without touching free cash flow or debt service.

**Return on capital.** NOPAT of roughly $957M (TTM operating income taxed at the 17.1% H1 rate) against invested capital of $7,768.6M (total equity + redeemable NCI + debt − cash) gives **ROIC ≈ 12.3%**. That is goodwill-inclusive and comfortably above a plausible cost of capital, but the spread is not wide enough to justify a 34x EBITDA multiple on its own.

**Financing cost is rising at the margin.** The July 16, 2026 issuance of $550M at 4.950% due 2031 and $650M at 5.400% due 2036 carries a blended 5.194% coupon, roughly 19bp above the April 30 revolver rate (~$2.3M of incremental annual interest on $1.2B) in exchange for fixed-rate paper, staggered maturities beyond the July 2028 revolver, and restored acquisition capacity. The arbitrage between HEICO's ~12.3% ROIC and its cost of acquisition capital is narrowing rather than widening.

**Financial health: 7.3.** Strong conversion, moderate leverage, high coverage, well-termed maturities, investment grade. Marked down from higher for negative tangible book, a $536.7M and growing put obligation outside conventional net-debt math, and a pro forma capital structure (post-Cook Defence, post-notes) that no filing yet discloses.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is running at 20% for the half and 25% for the quarter, and it is mostly organic: FSG organic +19% in Q2 with $17.4M acquired, ETG organic +17% with $58.3M acquired; for the half, FSG +16% organic ($36.1M acquired) and ETG +12% ($79.8M acquired). This matters because HEICO's growth is routinely dismissed as roll-up arithmetic, and in the current period it is not.

Three demand engines are visible in the filings:

1. **Commercial aftermarket volume.** FSG aftermarket parts were $1,922.3M in FY2025 and the segment margin gain came from mix within that line. R&D expensed in cost of sales — the pipeline that generates new PMA approvals — rose $95.8M → $111.3M → $120.9M across FY2023–FY2025 and $68.4M in H1 FY26 versus $56.3M, growing faster than sales.
2. **ETG's non-aerospace end markets have turned.** The medical/telecom/industrial component line fell from $306.8M (FY2023) to $259.1M (FY2024), recovered to $270.9M (FY2025), and is running $150.1M in H1 FY26, +13.1%. This was ETG's two-year drag and its reversal is a real contributor to the segment's 370bp margin jump alongside aerospace mix.
3. **Deliberate expansion into ground-based and European/NATO defense.** Within eight months HEICO added Rockmart (military aircraft fuel containment, January 2026), Southwest Antennas (ground-based defense and law-enforcement antennas, 90%, April 2026), and Cook Defence Systems (announced June 3, 2026; 80% owned; UK-based armored fighting vehicle track systems and running gear; ~130 employees; most revenue outside the US; terms undisclosed). With European rearmament budgets rising, this is well-timed diversification away from pure commercial-aviation cyclicality, though it may also simply be where reasonably priced family-owned assets are available now that commercial aerospace assets have become expensive.

**Firm-order momentum is improving.** Remaining performance obligations — which HEICO defines as contractually committed or firm customer orders — were $2,622.6M at April 30, 2026, up 24.4% from $2,107.6M at October 31, 2025. That is directional evidence of order strength. It is **not** forward-revenue coverage: at 53% of TTM revenue, most FSG demand is book-and-ship and never appears in RPO.

Consensus has $6.11 for FY2026 (+24.7% over FY2025's $4.90 reported basis, and about 9% above TTM $5.60) and $6.93 for FY2027, implying 13.4% EPS growth. Revisions are positive but modest in velocity: +8.2% and +9.1% over 90 days, +0.7% and +1.1% over 30 days — decelerating rather than accelerating, and well short of the magnitude that signals a mispriced inflection. Management issues no numeric revenue or EPS guidance; the only forward statement in the 10-Q is qualitative ("we expect increased net sales at both the FSG and ETG"), with capex raised to $85–95M.

**Growth prospects: 7.4.** Durable high-teens top-line with organic depth, a widening product-approval pipeline, and a credible defense leg. Constrained by the absence of numeric guidance, dependence on continued M&A for a meaningful share of the algorithm, and 13% consensus EPS growth for FY2027 that does not support the current multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

HEICO's PMA franchise attacks the most profitable pool in commercial aerospace — OEM spares — by selling FAA-approved equivalents at a discount. The economics favor HEICO (airlines have persistent incentive to escape OEM list pricing) but the OEMs control the certification-adjacent ecosystem: service bulletins, maintenance manuals, and long-term service agreements that discourage PMA adoption. HEICO's defense is R&D scale ($120.9M in FY2025, +20% in H1 FY26) and the resulting breadth of approved parts.

HEICO is increasingly buying its way inside the OEM tent. Across FY2023–FY2025 it completed a repeated series of exclusive-license-plus-asset transactions with Honeywell, in each case as buyer and licensee: exclusive rights to produce, sell and repair Honeywell's fixed and portable Emergency Locator Transmitter line (March 2023); exclusive capability on Boeing 737NG/777 cockpit displays and legacy displays (December 2023, expanded May 2024 to military 737NG/777 variants); and exclusive capability on Boeing 777 AIMS and 737NG/P-8/E-7 Versatile Integrated Avionics hardware (December 2024). On those platforms HEICO becomes the OEM-sanctioned sole source on very long-lived fleets rather than a certified alternative airlines must be persuaded to adopt. This is a structurally better position than PMA and the strongest evidence in the filings that the moat is widening. The caveat is real: none of these transactions was disclosed as individually material, so revenue and margin contribution is unquantified, and the pipeline depends on OEMs continuing to divest legacy support lines — a supply HEICO does not control.

Cook Defence, a UK armored-vehicle track systems business, was placed **inside Flight Support Group**. Beginning in Q3 FY2026, FSG's reported segment margin and organic-growth disclosures will blend a ground-vehicle manufacturer with the aviation aftermarket, reducing the interpretability of the single most important disclosure in the model.

A distinction the mix narrative tends to blur: HEICO's end-market mix is broadening (FSG defense & space $686.9M, ETG defense & space $716.6M, ETG other industries $270.9M in FY2025), which reduces sector cyclicality. It does not improve customer diversification, because no customer exceeded 10% of sales in any of the last three years to begin with.

**Competitive position: 7.4.** Improving trajectory, evidenced by the Honeywell sole-source series, rising R&D intensity, and simultaneous margin expansion in both segments.

## Risk Assessment

**Cyclicality with thin contractual protection.** FSG aftermarket revenue is largely book-and-ship; RPO covers 53% of TTM revenue. A decline in global flight hours would appear in revenue with little cushion. HEICO has almost no backlog to absorb a downturn in demand.

**Mix reversal.** One quarter of 290bp consolidated margin expansion, partly driven by ETG's non-aerospace lines recovering from a two-year trough, is not a trend. Management provided no numeric guidance to anchor it.

**Tax rate normalization.** Effective rates fell 20.0% → 17.5% → 16.6% (FY2023–FY2025) and were 17.1% in H1 FY26, driven by discrete Q1 benefits from stock-option exercises of $6.2M, $13.6M, $27.2M and $22.3M respectively. The Q2 FY26 standalone rate was **21.2%**, closer to structural. At a 21% rate, TTM EPS would be roughly 5% lower at identical pre-tax income. A large in-the-money option pool (3,584 thousand outstanding at a $126.81 weighted-average strike) means the benefit can persist, but it is share-price-linked and would fade in exactly the scenario where the multiple compresses.

**Redeemable noncontrolling interests.** The aggregate redemption amount grew $366.2M → $467.4M → $536.7M across October 2024, October 2025 and April 2026 — **+46% in 18 months against 16% revenue growth**. Roughly $191.4M is currently or imminently puttable, of which ~$94.6M could be payable in FY2026. Upward remeasurements are charged directly to retained earnings ($29.1M FY24, $72.4M FY25, $21.5M H1 FY26) and largely bypass the income statement; the accounting presentation is GAAP-mandated and implies nothing about intent. Economically this is a deferred, escalating, non-discretionary cash claim that competes with new M&A and rises as acquired businesses perform. Historical exercises have been staggered and modest ($26.6M FY24, $5.8M FY25, $12.4M H1 FY26), so bunching is a scenario rather than an expectation.

**Inventory valuation.** Deloitte's sole Critical Audit Matter in the FY2025 audit is inventory valuation at FSG's distribution and aftermarket parts units. Inventory rose from $1,295.3M (October 2025) to $1,410.5M (April 2026), of which $747.9M is finished goods — roughly 159 days on Q2 cost of sales. Aftermarket distribution inherently carries slow-moving stock; a demand air pocket would pressure write-down estimates that flow straight through gross margin.

**Acquisition price discipline.** Goodwill as a share of purchase price was 61.3% for Wencor, 40.8% for FY2025 deals, and **63.1% for the four H1 FY2026 deals** ($859.5M consideration, $542.4M goodwill). Deal pace is accelerating: acquisition cash outflow of $219.3M (FY24) → $629.8M (FY25) → $821.3M in H1 FY26 alone, plus Cook Defence at undisclosed terms. Higher goodwill share is consistent with paying up for growth.

**Governance.** Dual-class structure gives public Class A holders roughly 13% of votes for about 60% of the economics. Incentive metrics are EBITDA, net income attributable to HEICO, and cash flow — with no return-on-capital or relative-TSR measure. That design structurally rewards continued acquiring regardless of the return earned on deployed capital, and the proxy's own pay-versus-performance table shows five-year cumulative TSR of $304.2 against a peer index at $329.2. Against that, all FY2025 option grants (733 thousand at a $218.94 weighted-average strike) carried performance conditions for the first time, and share-based compensation expense nearly doubled to $34.4M as a result.

**Unresolved disclosure gaps.** Cook Defence purchase price, revenue and earnings contribution are undisclosed. The revolver is described as $2.0B in both the 10-K and Q2 10-Q but $2.2B in the July 16 press release, with an unread June 17, 2026 Item 1.01 8-K in between — most plausibly an accordion draw, but unconfirmed. Post-notes, post-Cook net leverage is not disclosed anywhere; the 1.74x figure predates both events.

**Risk factors: 5.3.** No binary regulatory event, no covenant cliff, no customer concentration, negligible short interest. Marked down for the combination of tax normalization (~5% mechanical EPS headwind), mix reversibility, a growing off-income-statement put claim, an inventory estimate flagged by the auditor, and sensitivity to interest rates that the current multiple does not price.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $470 (+27.9%)

The Q2 margin structure is the new baseline rather than a mix artifact. Both segments sustain 26%+ operating margins on mid-to-high-teens organic volume, SG&A leverage continues as the 2026 acquisition cohort integrates, and ETG's non-aerospace recovery extends into FY2027. RPO growth of 24.4% since October foreshadows continued firm-order strength. The Honeywell sole-source franchise compounds, adding high-margin revenue on 777/737NG/P-8/E-7 fleets that will fly for decades, while Cook Defence, Southwest Antennas and Rockmart capture European rearmament and ground-based defense budgets with limited commercial-aero correlation. The option-exercise tax benefit persists given a $126.81 average strike against a $367 price. FY2027 EPS beats consensus at roughly $7.30 and FY2028 approaches $8.55 on 18% growth; the market holds a 55x forward multiple. $470 matches the street high.

### Bear Case — $265 (−27.9%)

Q2's 290bp margin expansion proves mix-driven and reverses as FSG aftermarket mix normalizes and ETG's non-aerospace rebound annualizes. The effective tax rate normalizes toward 21%, mechanically removing about 5% of EPS. Airline capacity cuts on softening demand; with RPO covering only 53% of revenue, flight-hour softness hits revenue within two quarters, and $747.9M of finished-goods inventory at 159 days forces write-downs through gross margin at the exact business the auditor flagged. Simultaneously, an elevated long end compresses the multiple on a 2% FCF-yield security. FY2028 EPS lands flat near $6.90 and the multiple de-rates to 38x, roughly where lower-growth peers trade. $265 sits just above the $256.11 52-week low and below the $290 street low. A goodwill impairment against $4.2B of goodwill and negative $1.14B tangible book would extend the downside.

### Base Case — $395 (+7.5%)

Organic growth moderates to low-teens as comparisons harden, consolidated operating margin settles near 24.5% (above FY2025's 22.7%, below Q2's 25.5%), the tax rate drifts toward 19–20%, and FY2027 lands near consensus $6.93 with FY2028 around $7.90 on 14% growth. Twelve months out the market values the forward year at 50x — modest compression from today's 53x — giving $393–398. Cook Defence contributes as promised but is not individually material; capital allocation remains 100% M&A with no buyback support and roughly a 0.06% dividend yield.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Base case $395 (+7.5%), bull $470 (+27.9%), bear $265 (−27.9%). Probability-weighted at roughly 55% base / 20% bull / 25% bear, expected return is approximately +3% over twelve months — below what the risk warrants for new capital, and not weak enough to force existing holders out of a franchise with 20% organic-led growth and improving margins. Horizon: 12 months, with the first decision point on **August 25, 2026** (Q3 FY2026 results).

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE, then BUY):**
1. **Price toward $300–310** with FY2027 estimates intact — that is 43–45x FY2027E consensus, restoring a defensible margin of safety on a business growing EPS 13–15%. The analogous entry in Class A is at roughly 0.75x that level.
2. **Q3 FY2026 (Aug 25) evidence that Q2 was a baseline:** consolidated operating margin at or above 25%, FSG organic growth at or above 15%, and RPO growth of 20%+ — three conditions together, since any one alone can be mix.
3. **Capital-allocation or compensation change:** introduction of a return-on-capital or relative-TSR metric into incentive design, or the initiation of buybacks alongside M&A, which would address the structural incentive to acquire irrespective of returns.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE, then AVOID):**
1. Consolidated operating margin back below 23% for two consecutive quarters — mix reversal confirmed.
2. Sequential RPO decline, or FSG organic growth falling to high single digits, indicating flight-hour softness against 53% backlog coverage.
3. A large acquisition at goodwill above 70% of purchase price that pushes net leverage above 3.0x including redeemable NCI, or disclosure that Cook Defence plus Q3 borrowings has already done so.
4. Inventory days above ~180 with gross margin compression — the Critical Audit Matter converting into write-downs.
5. Any goodwill impairment charge against the $4,197.4M balance.

**Practical note for holders:** with near-term implied volatility at the 3rd percentile of the current chain and below realized, collar or put protection across the August 25 print is inexpensive relative to the ±10.6% move the September tenor is pricing.

## Conclusion

HEICO's operating performance is not in question. Q2 FY2026 was the best quarter in the company's disclosed history, both segments expanded margin simultaneously, the growth was mostly organic and explicitly not price-driven, cash conversion runs at 19% of sales, leverage is 1.74x with 9x interest coverage, and the Honeywell sole-source license series is the clearest evidence in the filings of a widening moat. Four consecutive earnings beats and FY2026/FY2027 estimates up 8–9% over 90 days confirm the operating momentum.

The price does not leave room to be paid for it.
