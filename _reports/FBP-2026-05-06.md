## Executive Summary

First BanCorp is a $3.7B market cap regional bank headquartered in San Juan, Puerto Rico, with $19.1B in total assets and dominant market positioning as the island's second-largest locally-based bank. The company delivered record pre-tax, pre-provision income of $131M in Q1 2026, with EPS of $0.57 representing 21% YoY growth, driven by NIM expansion to 4.75% and disciplined credit management.

At 10.7x forward earnings, FBP trades at a 30% discount to peer median P/E (15.3x) despite superior profitability metrics: ROA of 1.89%, ROE of ~17.5%, and 17 consecutive quarters above 1.5% ROA. The discount reflects Puerto Rico concentration risk, but this is offset by a structural NIM tailwind from $1.4B in securities at 1.66% yield rolling into assets at 5-8%+ yields, plus $90B+ in federal reconstruction spending providing a multi-year demand catalyst. The stock has appreciated 20% over the past year but remains inexpensive relative to earnings power, with estimate revisions trending positively and a 9% combined shareholder yield supporting the valuation floor.

The primary concern is single-jurisdiction concentration (77% of loans in Puerto Rico) compounded by political uncertainty—the Trump administration terminated 5 of 7 PROMESA oversight board members in August 2025 with no replacements named, creating governance risk around the fiscal recovery. This risk is real but non-imminent, and the valuation compensates adequately for a patient holder.

## Company Overview

First BanCorp operates through FirstBank Puerto Rico across three geographies: Puerto Rico (~77% of loans), Florida (~19%), and the U.S./British Virgin Islands (~4%). The company runs six business segments spanning consumer/retail banking, commercial and corporate lending, mortgage banking, treasury/investments, U.S. operations, and Virgin Islands operations.

The bank crossed $13B in total loans for the first time since 2010 in Q3 2025, with growth led by C&I and commercial mortgage in both Puerto Rico and Florida. Total deposits stand at $16.7B with a low-cost deposit franchise that underpins the industry-leading NIM.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability (Exceptional for the sector):**
- Q1 2026 ROA: 1.89%, consistent with 17+ quarters above 1.5%
- Net interest margin: 4.75% in Q1 2026, expanded from 4.21% (9M 2024) to 4.55% (9M 2025) and continuing to widen
- Efficiency ratio: ~50%, indicating strong operating leverage
- Net income trajectory: $299M (FY2024) → $345M (FY2025) → $355M+ annualized run rate (Q1 2026)

**NIM Expansion Mechanism:** FBP holds $4.6B in available-for-sale securities with a weighted average yield of 2.02%. Approximately $1.4B of these securities, yielding 1.66% on average, will mature over the next 12 months and be redeployed into loans (7-8%+ yields) or new securities (4-5%+ yields). This creates a 200-400bp pickup on each dollar redeployed, providing a mechanical NII tailwind largely independent of Fed policy decisions. Even in a 200bp rate cut scenario, management's sensitivity analysis shows only an 8.4% NII reduction—well within the offset capacity of the portfolio roll.

**Capital Position:**
- CET1: 16.67% (regulatory minimum: 4.5%)
- Total Capital: 17.93%
- ACL/Loans: 1.89% coverage ratio
- NPAs: 0.62% of total assets
- Net charge-offs: 0.62% annualized (manageable, trending stable)

**Capital Return:**
- Q1 2026: $50M buybacks + $31.5M dividends = 92% payout ratio
- Share count reduced from 169.3M (Dec 2023) to ~153.7M currently—a 9.2% reduction in ~2.5 years
- New $200M buyback authorized October 2025
- TruPS fully retired in H1 2025, eliminating 7%+ floating-rate subordinated debt
- Total shareholder yield: ~9% (6% buyback + 3% dividend)

**Revenue:**
- Net interest income: $646M (9M 2025) vs. $598M (9M 2024), +8% growth
- Non-interest income stable at ~$130M annualized
- Total revenue growing through NIM expansion and loan growth, partially offset by rate sensitivity on variable-rate assets

## Growth Analysis

FBP's growth is driven by three reinforcing tailwinds:

1. **Securities portfolio redeployment:** The $1.4B maturing at 1.66% creates a 2-3 year mechanical earnings growth engine. At a conservative 300bp reinvestment pickup, this contributes ~$42M in incremental annual pre-tax income as the portfolio turns over, equivalent to roughly 12% earnings growth from this source alone.

2. **Federal reconstruction spending:** Over $90B in federal funds are earmarked for Puerto Rico through 2035, with only ~32% of FEMA's $37.2B Public Assistance obligation disbursed to date. This multi-year spending supports employment (unemployment at 5.6%), real estate values, and commercial loan demand across the island.

3. **Florida expansion:** The 19% loan allocation to Florida provides geographic diversification and higher growth potential. C&I participations and commercial mortgage originations are driving expansion without the need for costly de novo branch builds.

**Growth constraints:** Management guided FY2025 loan growth to 3-4%, below prior mid-single-digit guidance, reflecting slower consumer/auto lending. EPS growth is primarily efficiency-driven (NIM expansion + buybacks) rather than volume-driven. Consensus expects $2.24 EPS for FY2026 and $2.39 for FY2027—roughly 7% annual growth—which appears conservative given the Q1 2026 annualized run rate of $2.28+.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current year up +3.6% over 30 days, next year up +1.6%. The revision velocity is below the 30% threshold for a strong inflection signal, but the direction is uniformly positive and the 4-quarter beat streak (average surprise of +13.4%) suggests consensus remains too low.

## Competitive Landscape

FBP operates in a consolidated Puerto Rico banking market alongside Popular Inc. (BPOP), which reported $833M in net income for FY2025 compared to FBP's $345M. The competitive intensity has declined following years of bank consolidation, and both institutions benefit from the improving economic environment.

**Competitive advantages:**
- Low-cost deposit franchise (cost of deposits declining even as rates remain elevated)
- Entrenched distribution network across Puerto Rico's branch system
- Tax advantages: IBE Act benefits, Act 60 of 2019, and new Act 65-2025 create a structurally lower effective tax rate (~22%) vs. mainland peers
- Relationship-based commercial banking model with deep local market knowledge
- Dual-market strategy (PR + Florida) provides growth optionality without sacrificing core market positioning

**Competitive position trajectory:** Improving. FBP is gaining share through the reconstruction spending cycle, expanding in Florida, and maintaining NIM superiority through deposit franchise quality. The 2020 BSPR acquisition significantly expanded market position and the bank is now operating at scale in its core market.

## Risk Assessment

**Geographic concentration (Primary risk):** 77% of loans in Puerto Rico creates single-jurisdiction risk. An economic downturn, natural disaster, or adverse policy change affecting the island would disproportionately impact FBP. The recent PROMESA oversight board disruption introduces fiscal governance uncertainty. If the board's authority is undermined or replacements adopt a less disciplined fiscal approach, it could threaten the recovery narrative.

**Interest rate sensitivity:** FBP is moderately asset-sensitive. In a 200bp down scenario, management's model shows an 8.4% NII decline. However, this is substantially buffered by the securities roll-off tailwind. The Fed's current trajectory actually favors FBP's positioning.

**Political/regulatory risk:** The "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" and potential changes to territorial tax treatment could affect Puerto Rico's favorable tax regime. Any reduction in federal funding to territories would directly impact the reconstruction spending catalyst.

**Credit risk—office CRE:** $508.7M in commercial mortgage exposure to office real estate maturing within 12 months represents a watch item, though Puerto Rico office dynamics differ from mainland markets given limited new supply and government tenant demand.

**Short interest:** At 8.0% of float with 6 days to cover, short interest is elevated but not at extreme levels. This likely reflects skepticism centered on jurisdiction concentration. The 6-day-to-cover metric suggests modestly crowded positioning that could fuel upside acceleration on continued earnings beats.

**What prevents this from being a higher-conviction call:** The PROMESA board disruption is a genuine unknown. Five vacancies with no timeline for resolution creates a tail risk scenario where fiscal discipline unravels, which could trigger rating agency action on Puerto Rico's debt and secondary effects on FBP's operating environment. The lack of resolution creates irreducible uncertainty.

- NIM continues expanding toward 5.0% as securities portfolio rolls over
- Reconstruction spending accelerates as government shutdown resolves and FEMA disbursements resume

## Conclusion

FBP warrants a BUY recommendation based on: (1) a 30% P/E discount to regional bank peers, (2) a mechanical NIM expansion tailwind from securities redeployment, (3) a 9% total shareholder yield, and (4) a multi-year economic catalyst in Puerto Rico reconstruction spending. The 4-quarter beat streak with an average surprise of 13.4% suggests consensus remains conservative.

The primary risk that justifies sizing this at 2% rather than 3-4% is the unresolved PROMESA board situation and inherent single-jurisdiction concentration. These are real risks with uncertain timelines, but they are compensated by a valuation that embeds meaningful pessimism relative to the company's actual financial performance. The stock rewards patience through its dividend and buyback while the reconstruction spending thesis plays out over multiple years.
