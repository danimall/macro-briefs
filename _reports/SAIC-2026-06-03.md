**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SAIC is a ~$7.26B-revenue pure-play technology services provider to the U.S. government, with 98% of revenue from federal customers (roughly 52% Department of War, 46% intelligence and civilian agencies). The investment debate is unusually clean: the company is deliberately shrinking a low-margin revenue base to defend and expand margins, generating ~$580M of annual free cash flow and retiring roughly 10% of its shares per year. The June 1, 2026 reaction to the Q1 FY2027 print — a ~13% surge to a 52-week high of $123.41 — shows the market currently rewarding the margin-and-cash story over the revenue weakness.

The Q1 FY2027 quarter (ended May 1, 2026) is a credible turning point. Revenue grew 2% year-over-year (the first growth in several quarters), GAAP operating margin jumped to 9.4% from 6.4%, net income rose 69% to $115M, diluted EPS reached $2.61 versus $1.42, and book-to-bill recovered to roughly 1.1x with backlog growing to $22.9B. Management raised its full-year profit outlook. Current-year and next-year consensus EPS estimates have risen 3.2% and 1.1% respectively over 90 days, and the stock trades at ~14.7x trailing earnings — a discount to genuine government-services peers.

We rate SAIC a HOLD with a constructive 12-month growth bias. The thesis is that margin expansion (adjusted operating margin moving from 8.9% toward double digits), a recovered book-to-bill, and aggressive buybacks compound EPS even on a flat-to-down revenue base, with re-rating optionality if revenue stabilizes. We stop short of a BUY because organic revenue is still guided to decline 2-4% in FY27, an unresolved DOJ antitrust criminal investigation carries a genuine suspension/debarment tail for a 98%-government contractor, leverage is elevated (D/E 3.57, Altman Z-score 1.96), reported EPS is flattered by one-time tax benefits, and the post-earnings price now sits slightly above the $109.80 consensus mean target.

## Company Overview

SAIC is a mid-tier government technology integrator headquartered in Reston, Virginia, with roughly 23,000-24,000 employees (a large majority cleared, ~25% veterans/active military) executing ~1,700 active contracts and task orders. Offerings span IT modernization, digital engineering, AI, mission systems support, training and simulation, and ground-vehicle support.

Two reportable segments drive the business:

- **Defense & Intelligence (~$5.58B, 77% of revenue, ~8.6% adjusted operating margin)** — the larger but lower-margin segment, weighted toward cost-reimbursable enterprise IT.
- **Civilian (~$1.68B, 23% of revenue, ~13.6% adjusted margin)** — the higher-margin, strategically prioritized segment.

Contract mix is 62% cost-reimbursement, 22% time-and-materials, and 16% firm-fixed-price. The heavy cost-reimbursable weighting reflects lower-risk but lower-margin work, particularly in Defense. SAIC competes against larger primes (Lockheed, Northrop, GD, RTX), pure-play peers (Booz Allen, CACI, Leidos, Parsons, Peraton, Amentum), and commercial entrants (Accenture, Deloitte, IBM). It differentiates on scale, prime-contractor positioning, a cleared workforce, and incumbency.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** SAIC has been shrinking: $7.70B (FY23) → $7.44B (FY24) → $7.48B (FY25) → $7.26B (FY26, -3%), an organic decline of ~3.3% in FY26 ex-SilverEdge. On February 11, 2026, management cut FY27 guidance to organic decline of 2-4% (revenue $7.0-7.2B) from prior guidance of 0-3% growth, citing "two recent unfavorable recompete award decisions and unchanged market conditions." Q1 FY2027 then returned to +2% reported revenue (only +0.5% organic ex-SilverEdge), so the company is one quarter into testing whether the decline is bottoming.

**Margins — the bright spot, and improving.** GAAP operating margin moved 10.0% (FY24) → 7.5% (FY25) → 7.2% (FY26), but the FY24 figure was inflated by a $240M divestiture gain and FY26 was depressed by $16M of executive transition costs and tax-audit costs. Adjusted operating margin tells the real story: 8.9% → 9.4% → 9.7%. The Q1 FY2027 GAAP operating margin of 9.4% (from 6.4%) confirms the mix-shift mechanism is working — SAIC is trading commoditized enterprise IT revenue for higher-margin technology-transformation and IP-based work.

**Earnings and cash flow.** Net income was $358M in FY26 (4.9% net margin), ROE 23.9% (flattered by thin equity), ROA 6.7%. The FY26 effective tax rate of 7.5% (vs 15.5% prior) reflected a $47M IRS audit-settlement benefit and the OBBBA's reinstatement of immediate R&D expensing — both one-time tailwinds. Operating cash flow grew steadily: $396M (FY24) → $494M (FY25) → $609M (FY26), with FCF of $577M (~7.9% margin) against minimal capex (~$32M). The company repurchased ~4.0M shares for ~$422M in FY26 and pays a $0.37 quarterly dividend (~1.4% yield); share count fell from ~48M to ~43M.

**Balance sheet — the principal risk flag.** D/E is 3.57 and the Altman Z-score is 1.96 (grey zone). Total debt of ~$2.5B sits against thin equity of ~$1.4-1.5B; goodwill ($2.94B) plus intangibles ($0.73B) exceed total equity, so book equity is effectively all intangible. Liquidity is adequate (current ratio 1.2, $1.0B undrawn revolver, $300M receivables-purchase facility, covenant compliant), debt is well-laddered (2028-2033 at 4.9-5.9%), and operating cash flow comfortably covers obligations. Piotroski F-score is 5/5 on available signals.

**Earnings quality caveat.** Net income has been flat-to-down ($477M FY24 → $358M FY26), but diluted EPS is supported by the ~10% share-count reduction and one-time tax benefits. Normalized earnings power is lower than the headline EPS suggests.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is bifurcated between revenue (declining) and earnings (growing). FY27 organic revenue is guided to decline 2-4%. The decline concentrates in "larger and commoditized Enterprise IT programs," which management frames as strategic shedding — but it also reflects competitive recompete losses, and that distinction is the central uncertainty.

The forward-looking signals are more constructive than the trailing revenue line. Q1 FY2027 funded backlog rose to $3.74B and net bookings of $2.1B produced the ~1.1x book-to-bill. The July 2025 budget reconciliation package added ~$150B of new non-border defense spending and ~$175B of border security/enforcement spending available through GFY2029, aligned with SAIC's "Undersea Dominance" (Naval) and "Border of the Future" focus areas. The October 2025 SilverEdge acquisition (~$203M) adds IP/product-based offerings consistent with the margin-up strategy.

Estimate revisions are mixed and worth parsing carefully. Near-term quarterly estimates are falling (current quarter -2.9%/30d, next quarter -6.2%/30d), but full-year estimates are rising (current year to $9.91, next year to $10.84). The pattern is consistent with quarterly timing/lumpiness against an improving annual earnings trajectory driven by margin. The four-quarter beat record (39.0%, 32.9%, 17.9%, 59.1%) gives reasonable confidence in the full-year direction, though the magnitude of those beats also signals the company sets conservative quarterly guidance.

The 12-month growth thesis: margin expansion toward double digits plus ~10% annual share reduction generates double-digit EPS growth on a flat-to-stabilizing revenue base, with Q1's recovered book-to-bill providing an early signal that the revenue decline may bottom in FY27/FY28.

## Valuation Assessment

At $113.36, SAIC trades at ~11.4x the rising FY27 consensus of $9.91. Against the genuine government-services peer set — Booz Allen, CACI, Leidos, KBR — trading in the low-to-mid teens, SAIC is at a modest discount, which its growth deficit partly justifies. KBR, the closest listed comparable in the screen, trades at 10.3x; the broader "Diversified Support Services" median of 35.9x is distorted by non-comparable names (UniFirst 35.9x, Amentum 38.8x) and is not a useful anchor.

The asymmetry is moderate rather than compelling. On the upside: a cheap multiple, rising full-year estimates, ~$580M FCF supporting buybacks, and re-rating optionality toward peer multiples if revenue stabilizes. Re-rating to 14x on FY27 EPS of $9.91 implies ~$139; on next-year $10.84 it implies ~$152. On the downside: the analyst mean target of $109.80 and median of $111.50 now sit slightly below the current price after the +13% pop, meaning the easy valuation gains have been captured. The low target of $85 (~-25%) reflects the revenue-decline and one-time-EPS-quality concerns. We treat consensus targets here as roughly fair rather than as floors, because guidance still calls for organic revenue decline and the multiple already prices the cash-return mechanics.

We score valuation 6.0 — genuinely cheap on forward earnings versus comparable peers, but with limited margin of safety after the rally and real overhangs that justify part of the discount.

## Competitive Landscape

SAIC occupies a mid-tier position in a structurally pressured industry. The procurement environment is shifting toward multi-award IDIQ vehicles, GSA Schedules, small-business set-asides, and Other Transaction Authorities (OTAs), all of which lower entry barriers and intensify price competition. The 10-K explicitly notes "fewer sole-source awards" and "increased emphasis on cost competitiveness." SAIC has been losing recompetes on commoditized enterprise IT, where it competes primarily on price.

Trajectory is mixed-to-slightly-improving. The strategic repositioning toward higher-margin technology transformation, AI, and IP/product offerings (SilverEdge, the renamed Solutions & Technology Group) is the right direction for margins and is showing up in adjusted-margin expansion. Backlog growth and the 1.1x book-to-bill in Q1 are early evidence the competitive bleeding may be slowing. Against this, the proxy's cumulative TSR index has badly lagged the peer group (SAIC index 113.75 vs peer 195.65), confirming the market has discounted SAIC's competitive standing relative to defense-services peers. The current rally is either the start of a re-rating if the growth inflection holds or a relief bounce if it does not.

## Risk Assessment

**DOJ antitrust criminal investigation (most under-appreciated).** SAIC received Federal Grand Jury subpoenas from the DOJ Antitrust Division in April 2022 and October 2023; the production process is ongoing and the company "cannot estimate" potential fines or penalties. For a 98%-government contractor — where suspension or debarment is an existential outcome — this deserves more weight than its quiet placement in the legal-proceedings footnote. The lack of resolution after multiple years is itself a concern.

**Revenue erosion and recompete losses.** SAIC lost two recompetes and is guiding to organic decline in FY27. The risk is that "strategic shedding" is partly a euphemism for competitive losses; a company can only shrink-to-margin for a limited time before it becomes terminal decline. Book-to-bill and organic growth are the metrics to monitor.

**Extreme government concentration and budget cycle exposure.** 98% government revenue exposes SAIC to continuing resolutions, sequestration, and shutdowns. The October 2025 43-day shutdown cost ~$26M of FY26 revenue, and a subsequent DHS-specific shutdown affected ICE/CBP funding directly relevant to the "Border of the Future" work.

**Capital-return regulatory threat.** The 10-K newly flags an executive order that could restrict buybacks/dividends for defense contractors that underperform on critical supply contracts. Since buybacks are SAIC's primary EPS-growth lever amid flat net income, any constraint here would materially alter the equity story.

**Leverage and goodwill.** D/E 3.57, Z-score 1.96, variable-rate term loans, and goodwill ($2.94B) exceeding equity create impairment risk if revenue declines persist or the stock falls.

**Leadership instability.** A CEO departure (Townes-Whitley), CFO-turned-interim CEO (Reagan), other executive exits, and two reorganizations in two years add execution risk mid-pivot. Reagan's appointment has not yet been confirmed as permanent.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is neutral-to-complacent and does not contradict the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV of 37.8% is essentially in line with 1-year historical volatility of 38.3% (spread -0.6%), with an IV percentile of 30% — the market is not pricing elevated near-term uncertainty after the earnings move. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.1%) and put/call OI is low at 0.33, indicating no concentrated downside hedging. ATM IV fell 12.3 points (50.0% → 37.8%) as the price rose 9.3% through the print, the classic post-event volatility crush. The term structure is in contango (1m 37.8% < 6m 41.9%) with a modest 6-month put skew of -4.0%, suggesting some demand for downside protection on a longer horizon, consistent with the unresolved revenue and DOJ overhangs rather than any imminent catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$139-152)

The margin inflection is structural and compounds. Adjusted operating margin moving from 8.9% to a sustained 10%+, combined with ~10% annual share reduction and ~$580M FCF, drives double-digit EPS growth on a stabilizing revenue base. Q1's 1.1x book-to-bill and backlog confirm the revenue decline is bottoming, aided by the defense and border reconciliation spending in SAIC's focus areas. The DOJ matter resolves without material penalty. A re-rating to ~14x FY27 EPS of $9.91 supports ~$139; on next-year EPS of $10.84, ~$152.

### Bear Case (~$85)

"Strategic shedding" proves to be competitive decline; organic revenue keeps falling beyond FY27, and margin gains cannot offset volume loss indefinitely. The DOJ antitrust investigation results in fines, a guilty plea, or — worst case — suspension/debarment exposure for a 98%-government contractor. The buyback-restriction executive order constrains the primary EPS lever. One-time tax benefits roll off, exposing flat-to-down normalized net income. The stock de-rates toward the analyst low of $85 (~-25%).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is that margin expansion, a recovered book-to-bill, ~10% annual share reduction, and rising full-year estimates produce double-digit EPS growth at a cheap multiple, with re-rating optionality if revenue stabilizes. The Q1 FY2027 print and raised profit outlook arrest and modestly reverse the prior declining score trajectory (5.3 → 5.3 → 4.5), but the still-declining organic revenue base, DOJ overhang, and post-rally price hold it below a buy.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 FY2027 (expected ~September 2026) confirms a sustained book-to-bill ≥1.0x and organic revenue inflection toward flat or positive, validating that Q1 was not a one-quarter blip.
- Adjusted operating margin sustains ≥10% across two consecutive quarters with stable revenue, demonstrating the mix-shift is durable rather than mechanically dependent on revenue shedding.
- Material favorable resolution of the DOJ antitrust investigation, removing the binary suspension/debarment tail.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Organic revenue decline accelerates below the guided -4% floor, or book-to-bill falls back below 1.0x, signaling competitive (not strategic) erosion.
- An adverse DOJ development (indictment, plea, or suspension/debarment notice), or imposition of buyback/dividend restrictions under the flagged executive order.
- Goodwill impairment or covenant pressure from leverage if the stock or revenue deteriorates materially.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 FY2027 earnings release (~September 2026), with the FY27 organic-growth and margin trajectory as the decisive variables. Confirmation of Jim Reagan as permanent CEO is a secondary item to monitor.

## Conclusion

SAIC presents a clean bull/bear tension: shrinking revenue against expanding margins and aggressive capital return. The Q1 FY2027 print is a genuine positive, and the market's rally to a 52-week high reflects it. With full-year estimates rising, ~$580M FCF, ~10% annual share reduction, and a multiple that discounts genuine government-services peers, there is a credible 12-month path to double-digit EPS growth and modest re-rating.

We hold rather than buy because the organic revenue base is still guided to decline 2-4% in FY27, the unresolved DOJ antitrust criminal investigation is a materially under-priced binary risk for a 98%-government contractor, leverage is elevated, reported EPS is flattered by one-time tax benefits, and the post-earnings price now sits slightly above the consensus mean target. The setup is constructive but not yet asymmetric enough for a higher-conviction call; the Q2 FY2027 print will determine whether the margin-and-bookings inflection is the start of a re-rating or a relief bounce. **HOLD.**
