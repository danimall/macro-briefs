**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Dominion Energy has stopped trading on its fundamentals. On May 18, 2026, NextEra Energy agreed to acquire Dominion in an all-stock transaction at an exchange ratio of 0.8138 NextEra shares per Dominion share, valuing Dominion at roughly $75.97 per share at announcement (a ~23% premium to the prior close, ~$66.8B of equity and ~$420B enterprise value). The stock gapped from the low-$60s to the high-$60s on the news and has since drifted to $68.02. The combination would form the largest regulated electric utility in the world, with the strategic prize being Dominion's regulated monopoly over Northern Virginia's Data Center Alley, the densest concentration of AI/data-center electricity demand on the planet.

The valuation question has therefore narrowed to two variables: whether the deal closes, and what NextEra's stock is worth when it does. The current ~$68 price sits roughly 10% below the ~$76 announcement value, a spread that prices in real completion risk. The gating item is the Virginia State Corporation Commission, which faces intense political and consumer scrutiny over market concentration and rate impacts, alongside FERC, NRC, and North/South Carolina approvals. Because the consideration is all NextEra stock rather than cash, Dominion holders bear NextEra's share-price risk, and NextEra fell ~2% on announcement and subsequently slid more than 10% as the Street balked at the price paid. The realized payout to a Dominion holder is the exchange ratio multiplied by NextEra's price at close, not a fixed $75.97.

The underlying franchise is healthy. Dominion affirmed FY2026 operating EPS guidance of $3.45–$3.69 and a 5–7% long-term growth target; Q1 2026 operating EPS rose to $0.95 from $0.93, and the Virginia segment benefited from a constructive 2025 biennial review. These fundamentals now matter mainly as a backstop to the deal, the floor under the stock if the merger breaks. We rate Dominion HOLD. The risk/reward of buying the spread depends on a Virginia regulatory outcome we cannot handicap with confidence and on a deteriorating acquirer currency, which is not an attractive entry. Existing holders with a constructive view on deal completion can carry the position; we would not add here.

## Company Overview

Dominion is a Richmond, Virginia–based regulated multi-utility that has transformed into a substantially pure-play regulated electric utility after multi-year portfolio cleanup, most notably the 2024 sale of its gas distribution businesses to Enbridge. The company serves about 3.6 million regulated electric customers across Virginia, North Carolina, and South Carolina, plus roughly 500,000 regulated gas customers in South Carolina.

Operations run through three core segments. Dominion Energy Virginia (DEV) is the largest earnings contributor and the strategic crown jewel, owing to its service territory over Northern Virginia. Dominion Energy South Carolina (DESC) is a smaller regulated electric and gas franchise. Contracted Energy houses offshore wind, solar, and the Millstone nuclear station in New England. Dominion is one of the leading developers of regulated offshore wind, anchored by the ~$10B+ Coastal Virginia Offshore Wind (CVOW) project, now 50% owned after bringing in Stonepeak as a noncontrolling financing partner while Dominion retains operational control.

The franchise's defining asset is its position in Data Center Alley. Dominion has roughly 51 GW of contracted data-center capacity with customers including Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta, Equinix, CoreWeave, and CyrusOne (these are electric supply relationships, not equity stakes). As a vertically-integrated regulated monopoly sitting directly in the path of AI-driven load growth, Dominion offered NextEra a clean entry into one of the fastest-growing electricity markets globally, which is what drove the premium bid.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 operating revenue rose to $5.02B from $4.08B, up 23% YoY, driven by fuel cost pass-throughs and DEV growth; FY2025 revenue was approximately $16.5B versus ~$14.5B prior. GAAP EPS in Q1 was $0.69 versus $0.77 a year earlier, depressed by nuclear-decommissioning-trust mark-to-market losses and impairments, but operating EPS, the metric management and the rate construct run on, rose to $0.95 from $0.93. FY2025 operating EPS was $3.42.

The segment bridge supports the franchise quality. DEV operating earnings jumped roughly +$109M YoY, helped by the 2025 biennial review outcome (+$105M) and rider equity returns (+$84M), indicating the Virginia regulatory construct is currently constructive. South Carolina was softer at -$26M. The earnings surprise record is solid (three beats and one in-line over the last four quarters, with the September 2025 quarter beating by 9.5%), which lends credibility to the affirmed FY2026 guidance midpoint of $3.57.

The balance sheet is the defining financial characteristic, and it cuts both ways. Long-term debt stood at $44.1B as of March 31, 2026. Capex runs roughly $12–13B annually against a multi-year data-center grid buildout, CVOW, and nuclear program, so free cash flow is structurally negative and funded by a mix of debt and equity. Interest expense rose to $561M in Q1 from $481M, making the equity story sensitive to rates and to rating-agency thresholds. The company has relied on external financing: diluted share count rose from 852M to 880M YoY, and the 10-Q references a roughly $5.1B equity injection request at the utility level. The Altman Z-score screens around 0.8 ("distress"), but this is a well-known false signal for regulated utilities, since structural leverage is supported by rate-base-earning assets and reliable regulated cash flows. The affirmed guidance and constructive Virginia review are the more reliable health indicators.

Estimate revisions are mildly negative: current-quarter consensus has slipped 1.1% over 90 days, and the broader pattern is flat-to-down. This is not deterioration that threatens the thesis, but it confirms there is no organic earnings acceleration to lean on; the equity case rests on the deal, not on rising estimates.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth story is genuinely attractive and is precisely what NextEra paid up to acquire. Data Center Alley represents the first sustained US power-demand growth in two decades, with Dominion's ~51 GW of contracted capacity feeding a rate-base expansion plan that supports the 5–7% long-term operating-EPS growth target. The 2025 biennial review and rider equity returns demonstrate the Virginia construct is converting load growth into recoverable, earning rate base, the mechanism by which a regulated utility monetizes demand.

Two structural protections matter. Virginia's new GS-5 tariff requires large data centers to post $1.5M/MW in collateral, an under-appreciated guardrail against stranded-cost risk if AI demand cools. And the contracted nature of the data-center relationships, plus the regulated rate-recovery model, insulate Dominion from the merchant-power volatility that affects unregulated generators.

The constraint is the funding model. Mid-single-digit EPS growth is real but capped by persistent equity dilution and structurally negative FCF in a higher-for-longer rate environment, where each refinancing repricing higher chips at the per-share growth rate. This is the standard regulated-utility growth profile: durable, recoverable, low-beta, and dilution-limited. As a standalone, Dominion is a mid-single-digit compounder with a sound demand tailwind, which is why we score growth prospects at 6.0 rather than higher. The acquisition removes the standalone growth question and replaces it with NextEra's combined growth profile, which a Dominion holder would inherit if the deal closes.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation must be framed as merger arbitrage, not a standalone multiple. At $68.02, the stock trades against an announcement-implied value of ~$75.97, a gross spread of roughly 12%. The published analyst target band ($64 low, $69.25 mean, $76 high) straddles both the current price and the deal value, which is what you would expect when the market is uncertain about completion.

The critical nuance: the deal is all-stock at a fixed 0.8138 exchange ratio, so the actual payout equals 0.8138 multiplied by NextEra's price at close. Part of the spread compression since announcement reflects not just deal risk but a weaker acquirer currency. A Dominion holder is taking a combined view: probability of close times NextEra's share price at close.

On a standalone basis, Dominion trades at ~20.1x trailing earnings and ~19x the FY2026 guidance midpoint, versus a multi-utility peer median P/E of 22.6 (Sempra 31.1, Xcel 22.6, DTE 24.0, ConEd 17.9, PG&E 13.0). The ~3.9% dividend yield is above the peer median of ~3.0%. The standalone discount to peers is modest and roughly justified by Dominion's higher leverage and dilution load; there is no compelling standalone mispricing. The case for the stock is the deal premium, not the multiple.

We score valuation 5.5. The spread offers a defined but unattractive return: ~12% gross if the deal closes at the announcement value, but the realized payout floats with a weakening NextEra, and a deal break would likely return the stock toward the pre-announcement low-$60s, an ~8–10% downside. This is roughly symmetric risk/reward on a binary regulatory outcome we cannot confidently handicap. The absence of an independent catalyst beyond the deal keeps the score at neutral.

## Competitive Landscape

Dominion's competitive position is strong and improving, which is the basis for the 7.0 score. Its regulated monopoly over Data Center Alley is a structural moat that cannot be replicated: the world's densest data-center cluster is captive to its wires, and Virginia's regulatory framework provides earning rate base on the buildout. This sits inside a broader utility consolidation wave driven by AI-demand growth: AES (~$33.4B, GIP/EQT), Constellation–Calpine ($16B), Blackstone–TXNM ($11.5B). US power prices are up roughly 40% over five years, with double-digit increases in data-center hubs. Dominion's franchise and its entry point into PJM are precisely what NextEra valued at a premium.

The source of the deal risk is that same strength: market concentration and consumer rate impacts are drawing political and regulatory backlash in Virginia. The franchise dominance that makes Dominion attractive to NextEra makes the merger contentious before the SCC. The competitive moat is intact and arguably widening as load grows, but its strategic value is now mediated through the acquisition.

## Risk Assessment

**Merger execution / regulatory risk (dominant).** The deal must clear FERC, NRC, and three state commissions (Virginia SCC, North Carolina, South Carolina). Virginia is the gating item. Lawmakers are questioning whether the SCC has adequate time and authority to review before the next General Assembly session; a state senator has suggested an SCC judge recuse herself; consumer and clean-energy advocates are raising rate and concentration concerns. The expected 12–18 month timeline leaves the spread exposed for an extended carry. This is the single most important thread to monitor and the largest reason we hold rather than add.

**All-stock currency risk.** Because consideration is 0.8138 NextEra shares, Dominion holders bear NextEra's share-price risk. NextEra has been weak post-announcement (down >10% at one point), so the realized value of the deal has eroded even before any change in completion odds.

**CVOW project risk.** The offshore wind project carries construction/cost-overrun risk, tariff exposure on imported components, and costs not expected to be recovered from customers ($258M of such charges in FY2025, a $58M Q1 reversal). The 50% JV structure cedes some decision-making and creates partner-capital dependence.

**Data-center concentration.** Demand is heavily concentrated in Loudoun County. Interconnection-queue backlogs and stranded-cost risk are flagged in the 10-K, partially mitigated by the GS-5 collateral requirement.

**Leverage / rate sensitivity.** $44B+ of long-term debt against a heavy capex program in a higher-for-longer rate environment pressures FCF and rating-agency headroom; interest expense rose ~17% YoY in Q1.

**Governance watch.** CEO Robert Blue's comp rose to $16.0M (from $12.9M in 2024) against five-year TSR of 97 versus a peer group at 159. Rising pay against persistent relative underperformance is a watch item, and arguably part of why the board accepted an all-stock sale at a premium.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and consistent with a low-volatility merger situation rather than an imminent binary. Near-term ATM IV is 20.3% against 1-year historical vol of 20.8% (fairly priced, 17th percentile within the current chain). Put/call skew is balanced at 1.5%, the put/call volume ratio is very low at 0.05, and the term structure is flat (20.3% near vs. 18.4% far), implying uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a near-term event. The implied move at the nearest expiry is ±3.4%. Short interest is low at 3.4% of float with 2.9 days to cover, so there is no crowded positioning in either direction. The 748.5% ATM IV reading in the options-delta feed is a corrupted thin-options artifact and is non-informative. The market is pricing a slow-burn regulatory process, not a near-dated catalyst, which fits the 12–18 month timeline.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The Virginia SCC approves the merger, possibly with conditions, within the 12–18 month window. Dominion holders receive 0.8138 NextEra shares; if NextEra recovers toward its pre-announcement level, the realized value approaches or exceeds the ~$76 announcement figure, roughly 12% above the current price plus dividends collected during the carry. The combined entity becomes the largest regulated utility in the world with unmatched exposure to AI load growth. Bull case ~$76.

**Bear case.** Virginia regulatory and political opposition derails or materially conditions the deal, or the spread stays wide for an extended period while NextEra's currency stays weak. A clean break returns Dominion to its standalone valuation, roughly the pre-announcement low-$60s. Standalone, the company remains a sound mid-single-digit compounder, so the downside is buffered by fundamentals rather than catastrophic. Bear case ~$62, roughly 9% below current.

The asymmetry is roughly 12% up versus 9% down on a binary regulatory outcome whose odds we cannot estimate with confidence, with the upside further diluted by a weakening acquirer currency. That balance does not clear the bar for a BUY, but the fundamental floor and the live premium do not justify an AVOID either.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The thesis is dominated by a regulatory outcome and an acquirer share price we cannot confidently forecast, which is the honest reason for the hold rather than a directional call.

**Reassessment timeline:** Track the Virginia SCC procedural calendar and any FERC/NRC docket milestones over the next 12–18 months; the next natural fundamental checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Virginia SCC sets a constructive procedural schedule or signals approval intent, compressing perceived completion risk, while the spread remains 8%+.
- NextEra's share price recovers such that the exchange ratio implies a value meaningfully above current price (e.g., NEE recovers and 0.8138 × NEE > ~$74), restoring the spread's attractiveness.
- A clear standalone re-rating case emerges if the deal breaks but the data-center rate base accelerates — i.e., a falling-knife entry near the low-$60s with intact 5–7% growth guidance.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Virginia SCC formal rejection, recusal-driven delay, or onerous conditions that NextEra is unlikely to accept, signaling deal break with the stock still pricing premium.
- NextEra share price deteriorates further such that the implied deal value falls below ~$66 (near the standalone floor), eliminating the premium.
- Material CVOW cost overrun or non-recovery charge that impairs the standalone backstop, or a guidance cut below the $3.45–$3.69 range.

## Conclusion

Dominion is a quality regulated franchise sitting on the best demand tailwind in the US utility sector, but its equity has become a vehicle for a single bet: that NextEra closes its all-stock acquisition and that NextEra's stock holds value through the process. At $68.02 against an announcement value of ~$75.97, the ~12% gross spread offers a defined but unattractive payout, eroded by a weak acquirer currency and gated by a Virginia regulatory review that is drawing genuine political opposition. The downside is buffered by a sound standalone business that would re-rate to the low-$60s on a break, so the risk/reward on adding here is roughly symmetric on a binary outcome we cannot handicap. We rate Dominion HOLD. Existing holders with conviction on deal completion can carry the position and collect the ~3.9% dividend during the wait; we would not initiate or add at current levels. We want clarity on the Virginia SCC's procedural posture before changing the call.
