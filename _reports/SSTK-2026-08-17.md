## Executive Summary

Shutterstock is a licensed-content marketplace whose revenue is now shrinking at roughly 17% year over year, with the deterioration visible in every operating metric the company discloses. Subscribers fell to 951,000 in Q2 2026 from a 1,079,000 peak in Q1 2025 — six consecutive quarterly declines, and the pace is accelerating (-28k, -39k, -42k in the last three quarters). Paid downloads are down 18.4% from the Q1 2025 level. Management attributes the Content decline (-17.1% in Q2) to "weakness in new customer acquisition," which is the signature of demand substitution rather than churn among existing accounts. The Data, Distribution and Services segment — the AI-era offset that carried the FY2025 growth story — grew 34% in Q2 2025 and then fell 16.5% in Q2 2026 and 27.8% in H1, with the data component specifically down 26% in the half.

Three events since the last review have removed the structural supports under the equity. The Getty Images merger of equals terminated on July 7, 2026 after the UK CMA demanded divestiture of Shutterstock's global editorial business (Backgrid, Splash) and Getty declined the condition; the DOJ had cleared the deal unconditionally in February. CEO Paul Hennessy left on July 13, with CFO Rik Powell taking the interim CEO seat while retaining CFO duties, and the Chief Accounting Officer resigns effective August 28. On July 22 the board suspended the dividend "to focus on reducing debt," two quarters after raising it from $0.33 to $0.36. Guidance for the remainder of 2026 has been withdrawn and the Q2 earnings call was cancelled pending a strategic update. The $173.7M goodwill impairment recorded in Q2 was struck against a reporting-unit fair value of $363.7M derived from the company's own July 1–13 average market capitalization plus a 10% control premium — implying about $8.95 to $9.00 per share. At $5.425 the same methodology indicates a further shortfall of roughly $143M.

The equity trades at 0.38x EV/LTM revenue and about 1.6x enterprise value on annualized H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA, with normalized free cash flow plausibly in the $50–70M range against a $200M market capitalization. Those numbers are low for a reason that is specific and identifiable: with guidance withdrawn, an accelerating volume decline, a broken growth offset, $140.1M of net debt and tangible book value of -$228.4M, there is no defensible terminal value. Our probability-weighted outcome from $5.425 is approximately flat with a bear case near $2.25 and a bull case near $11.50 contingent entirely on a strategic transaction whose existence is inference rather than disclosure. That distribution supports **REDUCE**: trim into the pending strategic update rather than underwrite a terminal value that management itself has declined to forecast.

## Company Overview

Shutterstock operates a two-sided marketplace. Independent contributors upload images, footage, music and 3D models; the company indexes them, licenses them to businesses, and pays royalties triggered by downloads. It recognizes revenue gross of contributor royalties as principal, except through third-party resellers where it recognizes net. It reports as a single operating segment with the CODM evaluating net income, disclosing revenue across two offerings.

**Content** was $165.7M in Q2 2026 (75% of revenue), spanning the flagship Shutterstock brand and editorial, Pond5, TurboSquid, PicMonkey, PremiumBeat, Splash News, Backgrid, Bigstock, and Envato (acquired July 2024). **DDS** was $56.1M (25%) and bundles three unrelated businesses: metadata licensing to AI labs for model training, Giphy (acquired from Meta in 2023 under a CMA-ordered divestiture), and Shutterstock Studios custom production.

Geographic mix is diversified — North America $118.1M (53%), Europe $59.2M (27%), rest of world $44.5M (20%) in Q2 — but carries an unhedged asymmetry: roughly 29% of H1 revenue is non-USD while contributor royalties are denominated in USD, so currency moves hit revenue without a natural cost offset. The genuine structural assets are library scale and the rights-clearance layer: contractual IP indemnification capped at $10,000 per customer with unlimited indemnification available to certain enterprise accounts. That feature is one uncleared web-scraped or generative alternatives cannot replicate, and it is consistent with enterprise accounts proving stickier than self-service.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue inflected negative in H2 2025 and then deteriorated sharply.** Reported FY figures were $827.8M (2022), $874.6M (2023), $935.3M (2024) and $989.9M (2025). The +5.8% FY2025 print is not representative: H1 2025 grew 17%, which implies H2 2025 revenue of $480.3M declined roughly 4% against the prior-year half (this H2 comparison is a derived approximation resting on the company's stated 17% H1 growth; the H2 2025 dollar figure is exact). FY2025 growth was carried by the Envato acquisition anniversary and front-loaded data-licensing deliveries. Q1 2026 revenue was $199.2M (-17.9%) and Q2 2026 $221.8M (-16.9%), for H1 of $421.0M (-17.4%).

**Operating metrics confirm volume erosion, not price weakness.** LTM ARPC rose from $244 to $292 across six quarters while subscribers fell 11.9% and paid downloads fell 18.4%. Using the company's own ARPC definition against LTM revenue of $901.3M implies roughly 3.09 million paying customers, down on the order of 18–19% year over year against ARPC up about 10%. Rising ARPC on a shrinking customer count is arithmetically consistent with disproportionate loss of small self-service accounts; it is not evidence of pricing power.

**Underlying margins have held; the base has not.** H1 2026 GAAP operating loss was $186.2M, containing the $173.7M impairment and a $33.0M FTC legal contingency. Excluding both gives +$20.5M of operating income (4.9%); further excluding $6.5M merger costs and $9.0M severance gives roughly $36.0M (8.6%). Company-defined adjusted EBITDA was $65.1M in Q2 (29.3% margin) against $82.2M (30.8%) a year earlier, and $107.8M in H1 (25.6%) against $145.6M (28.6%). Adjusted EBITDA fell 21% on a 17% revenue decline — modest negative operating leverage. Cost of revenue rose from 40% to 42% of revenue, which management attributes to "AI token usage fees and website hosting costs which do not fluctuate with revenue." The generative-AI product line currently carries semi-fixed inference cost on a shrinking revenue base.

**Cash flow is the most consequential line.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $17.991M against $21.710M of capex and $0.301M of content acquisitions, producing GAAP free cash flow of -$4.02M. Q2 standalone was worse, roughly -$9.6M. The company paid $26.0M of dividends in the same period, and cash fell $45.0M to $133.2M. The company's $41.6M adjusted free cash flow reconciles exactly by adding back the $35.0M FTC payment, $10.15M of merger cash costs and a $0.477M Giphy reimbursement — both large add-backs are legitimately non-recurring, and $9.0M of largely cash severance is not added back. Against FY2025's $166.7M OCF and $123.8M FCF (12.5% margin), the swing to roughly break-even GAAP FCF in six months is the sharpest change in the business.

Do not annualize H1. DDS revenue swung from $21.0M in Q1 to $56.1M in Q2, the FTC payment landed entirely in Q2, and accounts receivable released $9.7M of cash in H1 2026 versus consuming $55.7M in H1 2025. As a labeled sensitivity, doubling H1 adjusted FCF gives ~$83M; stripping the working-capital release and assuming no further one-timers puts a normalized figure closer to $50–70M. A declining subscription business also carries a structural working-capital drag as the $198.4M deferred revenue balance unwinds, which will suppress conversion further if subscriber losses continue.

**Balance sheet: not a near-term solvency problem, but leverage matters against a shrinking numerator.** At 6/30/2026: cash $133.2M; revolver $155.0M plus term loan $118.3M for $273.3M of carrying debt; net debt $140.1M. Equity $363.7M against $400.0M goodwill and $192.1M net intangibles gives tangible book value of -$228.4M (-$6.18 per share). The July 2024 credit agreement provides a $125M term loan and $250M revolver, both senior unsecured, maturing 2029 at SOFR + 1.375–1.750% plus a 10bp credit spread adjustment, with $94M of remaining capacity net of letters of credit. Covenants are a consolidated leverage ratio and interest coverage ratio, with compliance asserted at 6/30/2026; the numerical thresholds and the permitted add-back definition of Consolidated EBITDA are not disclosed in what was reviewed.

The headline 0.48x current ratio is an artifact: the $155M revolver is classified current only because the company can draw and repay at will, not because it matures. Excluding the revolver and deferred revenue, adjusted current assets/liabilities is 1.20x. On annualized H1 adjusted EBITDA of $215.6M (sensitivity, not forecast), gross leverage is 1.27x and net leverage 0.65x, with interest coverage around 14x on $7.6M of H1 interest expense. EBITDA would need to fall on the order of 60% from the current run rate before typical 3.5x/3.0x covenants bind. The same applies to standard distress screens: an Altman Z'' computed on reported LTM EBIT prints -1.97, but re-running with the non-cash impairment and one-time FTC charge added back and the revolver reclassified gives roughly 1.43. Neither figure is measuring balance-sheet fragility here; both are measuring a write-off and a classification convention. A FY2025 Piotroski score of 6–7 of 9 is stale and materially misdescribes a company now running -17% revenue with negative GAAP FCF.

**Earnings-surprise record.** Three misses in the last four reported quarters with comparable consensus (-26.7%, -5.0%, +1.4%, -17.9%). Q2 2026 non-GAAP adjusted EPS was $0.82 against a GAAP diluted loss of $4.25; no consensus comparison on a matched basis was obtainable, so we make no surprise claim on the most recent quarter. The pattern supports low confidence in forward estimates, which is consistent with management's own decision to withdraw guidance.

## Growth Analysis

The growth question reduces to whether the -17% run rate decelerates, and there is no supportive evidence in the disclosed data.

Content is down 14.6% in H1 with new customer acquisition named as the driver. Subscriber losses are accelerating quarter by quarter. Paid downloads — the unit that triggers royalties and therefore the truest volume metric — are down 18.4% from peak. Substitution by generative image tools directly attacks the self-service tier where volume is highest and switching cost is lowest, and the mix data (ARPC up, customers down ~18%) is exactly what that substitution would produce.

DDS was the entire offset thesis, and it reversed within four quarters: +34% in Q2 2025 to -16.5% in Q2 2026, with the data component down 26% in H1. Management's lumpiness explanation is credible on its face given the $21.0M-to-$56.1M sequential swing, and $35.3M of contracted-but-unsatisfied performance obligations relating primarily to data deals, recognized over five years, evidences continuing demand. But $35.3M over five years is small against a segment whose recent quarterly revenue ranged from $21.0M to $56.1M, and the alternative explanations — front-loaded initial corpus deals that do not renew at the same scale, or labs reducing dependence on licensed stock libraries — are not excluded by anything in the filings.

Cost actions are large but do not close the gap. Management cites more than $70M of realized annualized opex reduction over 18 months plus $60M more targeted by year-end, $130M cumulative if delivered. Q2 2026 revenue annualizes to $887.2M against Q2 2025's $1,068.0M — a $181M run-rate decline. Because roughly 40% of revenue flows to contributor royalties and payment processing, incremental revenue loss carries well under 100% contribution margin, so the true EBITDA gap is smaller than $181M. But adjusted EBITDA has already fallen 21% on a 17% revenue decline, which tells you the cuts are not yet outrunning the erosion.

Editorial is the one franchise with a defensible growth or at least stability case: celebrity, red-carpet and news imagery requires a photographer physically present, which generative models cannot replicate. The CMA's insistence on divesting it as a merger condition is regulator evidence that the asset has standalone competitive weight. That supports asset value, not consolidated growth.

## Valuation Assessment

At $5.425 and 36.947M shares, market capitalization is $200.4M. Adding $140.1M of net debt gives enterprise value of $340.5M.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV / LTM revenue ($901.3M) | 0.38x |
| EV / annualized H1 adj. EBITDA ($215.6M, sensitivity) | 1.58x |
| Price / book ($9.84/sh) | 0.55x |
| Tangible book value per share | -$6.18 |
| Cash per share / net debt per share | $3.61 / -$3.79 |
| Normalized FCF yield ($50–70M on $200.4M) | 25–35% |
| Trailing P/E on FY2025 GAAP EPS $1.25 | 4.3x (stale) |

Peer medians in Interactive Media & Services sit at 21.6x P/E (CARS 21.6x, QNST 15.1x, YELP 12.2x, TRIP 173.8x, GTM negative). SSTK cannot be compared on P/E given the LTM loss, and the reported -1.04 trailing/forward P/E is an artifact of the impairment. The relevant comparison is that low-growth internet assets like Yelp clear at 12x earnings while SSTK clears at 0.22x sales, and the gap is a market judgment on revenue direction, not on multiple compression alone.

Two things about this valuation are genuinely favorable. First, the multiple compression exceeded the earnings compression: enterprise value fell over the period while annualized adjusted EBITDA fell about 23% from the FY2025 run rate, so the asset is mechanically cheaper on current fundamentals. (The EV/EBITDA compression from prior periods rests on prior-period EV levels not established in the sources; the level today is 1.6x.) Second, with the dividend suspended, roughly $53M of annual cash is redirected to debt reduction; at $50–70M of normalized FCF, net debt of $140.1M could be substantially retired inside two to three years, which transfers value to equity if the revenue line stabilizes.

Three things prevent a high valuation score. Every cheap metric is computed on a base that is 17% smaller than a year ago and still shrinking, with management declining to forecast forward. Debt is 58% of enterprise value at gross, so a 30% further decline in EBITDA hands most of the incremental value to creditors before equity participates. And the company has pinned reporting-unit fair value to its own market capitalization: back-solving the July impairment gives an implied market cap of $330.6M, about $8.95–9.00 per share, against $200.4M today; applying the same 10% control premium indicates a further shortfall near $143M at the October 1 annual test. That write-down is non-cash and does not touch EBITDA-defined covenants, and management could switch to an income approach, but it would take equity to roughly $220M and tangible book toward -$370M.

Scenario framework from $5.425:

- **Bear — $2.25 (-59%).** Revenue decline holds at or past -20%, cost actions lag, FY2027 adjusted EBITDA falls toward $110–120M, working-capital unwind suppresses cash conversion, a further impairment lands in Q4 2026, and leverage becomes the governing variable. At 2.5x EV/EBITDA on $110M with net debt near $130M, equity is roughly $145M.
- **Base — $6.25 (+15%).** Decline moderates to low-to-mid teens, cost cuts deliver most of the $130M target, FY2027 adjusted EBITDA lands near $160–170M, the market keeps the multiple depressed at 1.9–2.1x while debt falls to roughly $100M net. Equity of $210–230M.
- **Bull — $11.50 (+112%).** A strategic transaction: a whole-company sale at 3.5–4x normalized EBITDA, or an editorial divestiture at a premium multiple plus evidence of volume stabilization and a DDS re-acceleration. At 3.75x on $175M less $110M net debt, equity is roughly $545M less a discount for execution.

Weighting bear 40%, base 40%, bull 20% gives roughly $5.70, or +5% from spot. The distribution is wide, the left tail is fat, and confidence in every leg is low because guidance has been withdrawn.

## Competitive Landscape

The most informative competitive datapoint is that the two largest licensed-stock players are contracting at the same time. Getty Images reported Q2 2026 on August 10 with, per secondary summaries, negative free cash flow, subscriber declines, iStock and Agency pressure, and Guggenheim engaged for a balance-sheet process running from Q3 into Q4. Those claims are secondary-sourced and unverified against Getty's own filings, and should be treated as such. If accurate, simultaneous deterioration at #1 and #2 is more consistent with demand substitution than with share transfer between them.

Shutterstock's remaining moats are library scale, brand, and the indemnification and rights-clearance layer that enterprise buyers require. Those are consistent with ARPC rising while customer count falls — the enterprise tier appears to be holding. The moat does not extend to the self-service tier, where a marginal image subscription competes against a general-purpose model that produces adequate output at near-zero marginal cost. Trajectory is what matters for scoring, and the trajectory is share of wallet contracting inside a contracting category.

Generative AI cuts both ways, and in H1 2026 both cuts were negative: it compresses core licensing while the DDS offset that was supposed to monetize the library also declined, and the company now absorbs AI token and hosting costs that management explicitly names as a gross-margin headwind on a shrinking base.

## Risk Assessment

**Structural substitution (primary).** Content -17%, downloads -18% from peak, six quarters of subscriber decline with acceleration, new-customer acquisition named as the cause, and a peer deteriorating in parallel. This is the variable that determines whether the equity is worth $11 or $2.

**Mechanical further impairment.** Goodwill remains $400.0M against $363.7M of equity, with the reporting unit's carrying value set equal to its market-derived fair value and no cushion. Indicated shortfall at current price is roughly $143M. Non-cash, but it eliminates the book-value floor argument and puts tangible book near -$370M.

**Revenue quality in DDS.** $56.1M of unbilled receivables equals 100% of the quarter's DDS revenue ($36.8M in AR, $19.3M in Other Assets). The company holds $30.5M of equity instruments received as consideration for revenue contracts, carried at cost under the measurement alternative because they lack readily determinable fair values, and recorded a $5.0M investment impairment in Q1. Some arrangements contain financing elements ($5.6M). Accounts receivable concentration worsened: two customers at 16% and 12% at 6/30/2026 versus one at 15% at year-end. A meaningful slice of the segment's revenue is recognized ahead of cash, some of it settled in illiquid private securities.

**Control environment.** Interim CEO and CFO are the same person and signs both officer certifications; the Chief Accounting Officer departs August 28. Management concluded disclosure controls were effective at 6/30/2026 with no internal-control changes, but the staffing configuration is thin for a company mid-strategic-review with a large impairment and a regulatory settlement in the same quarter.

**Regulatory.** The FTC filed and simultaneously settled a Section 5 / ROSCA complaint on 5/13/2026 regarding subscription enrollment and cancellation; $35M was paid in June and the consent order requires disclosure and process changes to the sign-up and cancellation funnel. That constrains the exact mechanics that drive self-service conversion at a moment when new-customer acquisition is already the stated weak point. Accrued non-income taxes of $38.4M reflect persistent multi-jurisdiction indirect-tax exposure.

**Illiquid assets.** The Meitu stake fell from $40.0M to $21.4M, generating $18.3M of unrealized losses through the income statement in H1, and is contractually locked until March 2027 — unavailable to fund the deleveraging the board has prioritized.

**Contributor side.** Royalties payable rose to $98.3M from $94.2M despite falling downloads. Declining volumes reduce per-contributor earnings, which over time pressures upload supply — the input side of the two-sided model.

**Unknown strategic update.** The single largest near-term catalyst, promised "in the coming weeks," has unknown content. It could be a sale, an editorial divestiture, or a deeper restructuring with additional charges. Direction is not inferable from disclosure.

### Options Market Signal

Put/call open-interest ratio moved from 0.33 to 0.99 between the May 28 and August 17 snapshots while the price fell 64.9%. Positioning has rotated toward puts, consistent with holders buying protection or new bearish positioning rather than with capitulation-driven call speculation. Short interest is 7.1% of float with 1.0 days to cover on 1.55M shares — uncrowded and easily covered, so squeeze mechanics are not a meaningful support and the absence of heavy short interest also means the bearish view is not yet a consensus trade. The options structure corroborates the fundamental read without adding independent information; we do not treat it as a driver.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

Normalized free cash flow of $50–70M against a $200.4M market capitalization is a 25–35% yield, and with the dividend suspended all of it now services $140.1M of net debt at 1.27x gross leverage against a 2029 maturity and $94M of undrawn revolver. If the revenue decline moderates toward high single digits as enterprise and editorial hold while self-service attrition exhausts itself, adjusted EBITDA stabilizes near $160–180M and the equity re-rates from 1.6x to 2.5x EV/EBITDA on removal of terminal-value doubt. Cost actions of $130M cumulative, if delivered, restore margin on the smaller base. The CMA's identification of editorial as separately marketable establishes that a discrete asset carries standalone value; a divestiture would both retire debt and reveal sum-of-parts. The configuration of a ~29.6% founder holder, a vacant permanent CEO seat, a suspended dividend to build flexibility, an engaged strategic advisor, and a promised strategic update is the setup that often precedes a corporate transaction. DDS lumpiness is real — the segment moved from $21.0M to $56.1M in one sequential step — so two negative quarters may not define a trend, and $35.3M of contracted five-year data obligations sit outside deferred revenue.

### Bear Case

The company is a declining asset with an identified structural cause, and the market is pricing it accordingly rather than mispricing it. Six quarters of accelerating subscriber loss, downloads -18%, and new-customer acquisition explicitly named as the weakness describe substitution, not cyclicality. The one offset, data licensing to AI labs, reversed from +34% to -16.5% inside four quarters, and its revenue is 100% unbilled at quarter-end with $30.5M of the historical consideration held in illiquid private equity. Management has withdrawn guidance, which removes any anchor for underwriting FY2027. Debt is 58% of enterprise value: a 30% EBITDA decline from the current run rate leaves equity holders with a residual, and the deferred-revenue unwind in a shrinking subscription book suppresses cash conversion precisely when it is needed for deleveraging. Capital allocation has destroyed value: 5.5M lifetime shares repurchased at an average $48.86 against $5.425 today, the Envato acquisition contributing to a $173.7M write-down, and a dividend raised two quarters before suspension. A further ~$143M impairment is arithmetically indicated at the October test. No officer or director has bought a share in the open market at any point through August 7, 2026. Stock-based compensation of roughly $50M annualized is about 25% of market capitalization per year, and replacement grants struck near $5 would be far more share-dilutive per dollar of expense than the 4.016M unvested awards (about 10.9% of existing shares, or 9.8% ownership dilution if all vest at an $18.63 average grant price).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE. Base case $6.25 (+15%), bull $11.50 (+112%), bear $2.25 (-59%). Probability-weighted outcome roughly $5.70 (+5%).**

**De-risking plan.** Existing holders should trim ahead of the promised strategic update rather than into it. The update is the largest near-term price determinant and its direction is not inferable from anything disclosed; positioning for an unknown binary is not a risk-adjusted use of capital when the weighted expected return is approximately flat. A reasonable approach is to cut the position by half to two-thirds now, retaining a residual stub sized to survive a -59% bear outcome, and to treat the October 1 annual goodwill test and the Q3 report as the second decision point. Prospective investors should not initiate; there is no margin of safety in a 15% base-case upside against a 59% bear leg.

**Upgrade triggers (to HOLD, then ACCUMULATE).** (1) A reported quarter in which the subscriber count declines by fewer than 20,000 sequentially, or paid downloads stabilize above 95 million — deceleration in volume is the single measurable that would restore a terminal value. (2) DDS revenue above $50M for two consecutive quarters with unbilled receivables falling as a percentage of segment revenue, confirming that the H1 decline was delivery timing. (3) Reinstated full-year guidance with adjusted EBITDA at or above $190M, plus net debt reduced below $100M. (4) Clustered open-market purchases by three or more officers or directors once any trading window opens. Any two of these would justify HOLD; three would justify ACCUMULATE, most plausibly in the $4.50–5.00 range where the bear case is largely absorbed.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID).** (1) Q3 revenue decline steeper than 18% year over year, or a sequential subscriber loss above 45,000. (2) A strategic update that includes an equity raise, a distressed asset sale, or a covenant amendment. (3) Any disclosure of a material weakness in internal control, or a further senior finance departure after the August 28 CAO exit. (4) Adjusted EBITDA below $50M in a single quarter, which would move leverage toward the zone where undisclosed covenant thresholds plausibly bind.

**Reassessment timeline.** The strategic update, expected within weeks of the August 7 10-Q; the October 1 annual goodwill test; and the Q3 2026 report. All three land inside the next four months, so the horizon on this call is short by design.

## Conclusion

Shutterstock is inexpensive on every static measure and unmodelable on every forward one. Enterprise value of $340.5M against $901.3M of LTM revenue and $50–70M of normalized free cash flow would be compelling if the revenue line were stable; it is contracting 17% year over year with accelerating unit attrition, an explicit management attribution to new-customer weakness, a peer deteriorating in parallel, and AI data licensing having reversed from +34% to -16.5% within four quarters. The events of July removed the supports that previously justified holding: the merger's implied floor, the dividend, forward guidance, and the CEO. The company's own impairment methodology values the equity near $9.00 per share and, applied to today's price, indicates a further $143M write-down at the October test.

The strategic update, deleveraging capacity from the suspended dividend, and the CMA-validated separability of the editorial franchise are real sources of optionality, which is why this is not a short and why we stop short of AVOID. Optionality on an unknown binary, sitting above $140.1M of net debt and -$228.4M of tangible book, does not compensate for a 59% bear leg against 15% of base-case upside. **REDUCE.**
