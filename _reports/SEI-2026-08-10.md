## Executive Summary

Solaris Energy Infrastructure has, in under two years, transformed from the oilfield logistics company it was through 2023. Power Solutions generated zero revenue in 2023, $38.6M in 2024, $333.5M in 2025 and $286.9M in the first half of 2026 alone. In Q2 2026 it was 72% of revenue and 80% of segment Adjusted EBITDA. The demand driver is the gap between AI data-center load growth and grid interconnection timelines, and the constraint on capturing it is access to gas turbines, which Solaris has been buying aggressively, including by curing a defaulted, suspended Baker Hughes order held by Colusa Power to jump a multi-year manufacturing queue. Total assets went from $1.13B (12/31/2024) to $2.14B (12/31/2025) to $4.21B (6/30/2026).

The constraint is the price and the arithmetic underneath it. Three corrections matter before any multiple is calculated. First, the widely-quoted $3.6B market capitalization is stale: the 10-Q cover page shows 65,831,540 Class A plus 10,734,852 Class B shares (exchangeable 1:1), giving as-exchanged equity of $4.56B at $59.585 and $5.69B including the 18,941,057 shares underlying the two convertible series. Enterprise value is approximately $6.37B, which is 15.9x FY2026E Adjusted EBITDA of $399.4M rather than the ~12x a stale-share screen produces. Second, the August 5 "raised guidance" describes a sequential step-down: Q3 Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $90–105M has a midpoint 10% below the $108.3M just delivered. Third, roughly $10M per quarter of the Q2 step-up is the Genco acquisition converting third-party turbine rental expense (inside Adjusted EBITDA) into depreciation (outside it): Power equipment rental expense fell from $31.1M to $21.3M sequentially while leasing depreciation rose from $12.6M to $26.4M.

We rate the shares HOLD. Growth is genuine and largely contracted; the balance sheet, the counterparty concentration and the estimate trend are not supportive of paying up. Consensus EPS has been revised down 7.1% (30-day) and 16.0% (90-day) for the current year and 9.1% for next year, which removes the usual justification for treating analyst targets ($73 low, $94 mean, $120 high, all above spot) as a floor. Our base case is $64 (+7% from $59.585), bull $95 (+59%), bear $34 (−43%). That expected value is modestly positive and does not clear the bar for adding capital into an 86%-implied-volatility security with a covenant test starting this quarter and 26.1% of float sold short.

## Company Overview

Two reportable segments. **Solaris Power Solutions** leases mobile gas turbine generator sets under multi-year contracts (Solaris is lessor under ASC 842) and, increasingly, delivers turnkey plants including balance of plant, battery storage, energy management systems, commissioning, O&M, and, under the July 2026 amendments, cost-plus natural gas procurement. Customers per the Q2 MD&A include three companies in the AI computing sector plus several energy customers. **Solaris Logistics Solutions** builds and operates proppant and fluid management systems for well completions; this is the legacy franchise (the company was Solaris Oilfield Infrastructure until September 2024).

The pivot began with the September 2024 acquisition of Mobile Energy Rentals for $323.1M and accelerated through three 2026 transactions: Genco (March 16, $483.2M, accounted for as an asset acquisition under ASC 805-50 with no goodwill), the Baker Hughes NovaLT16 novation from Colusa (March 13, $131.2M of closing payments plus $364.9M non-cancellable remaining and up to $130.2M contingent), and GESA (closed July 1, ~$263.9M preliminary, ~80% funded in stock at $73.42 per share). Financing came via $1.3B of 6.375% senior unsecured notes due 2031 and a $650M MUFG revolver, both dated May 12, 2026. Corporate ratings assigned in Q2: S&P BB−, Moody's Ba3, Fitch BB.

The 10-K describes the segment's "primary competitor" as the electricity grid, a framing that defines both the opportunity (interconnection queues are long) and the risk (they eventually clear).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and segment mix.** Total revenue grew from $313.1M (FY2024) to $622.2M (FY2025), and 1H2026 revenue was $415.6M against $275.7M in 1H2025 (+51%). Power revenue rose 129% to $286.9M as weighted-average deployed capacity earning revenue went from roughly 500 MW to 930 MW, while Logistics revenue fell 15% to $128.8M. Logistics Adjusted EBITDA margin nonetheless improved to 37.2% from 32.3%, because the lost revenue was low-margin pass-through trucking. Logistics segment Adjusted EBITDA has declined from $115.1M (2023) to $97.6M (2024) to $88.9M (2025) to a ~$96M annualized 1H26 pace, a stagnant business now serving as a modest internal funding source.

**Quality of the Q2 print.** Q2 Adjusted EBITDA of $108.3M was $24.7M above Q1's $83.6M, but the composition is not what a lessor's improving unit economics would look like. Power *leasing* revenue was flat sequentially at $105.7M versus $105.4M on a 4% increase in deployed MW (910 → 950). Power *service* revenue more than doubled, from $23.2M to $52.6M, accounting for essentially the entire sequential Power revenue gain, and trucking/mobilization expense inside Power rose from $6.0M to $15.9M with labor from $9.8M to $13.5M. Mobilization costs scaling with service revenue is the signature of commissioning and project work rather than recurring capacity rent. Layer on the Genco reclassification (~$9.8M/quarter of rental expense removed from Adjusted EBITDA, ~$13.8M/quarter of depreciation added below it) and the guidance framing becomes legible: management guided Q3 below Q2 because a decomposable share of Q2 does not repeat at the same margin.

The alternative reading deserves weight. The July amendments explicitly add recurring balance-of-plant, battery, energy-management, O&M and cost-plus gas scope to two contracts, so ancillary services may be migrating from lumpy commissioning into a durable line. And the Genco benefit, while non-repeatable, is permanent. Either way, $108.3M should not be annualized. FY2026E Adjusted EBITDA of $399.4M (1H actual $191.9M + Q3 midpoint $97.5M + Q4 midpoint $110.0M) is the appropriate anchor, and it already embeds guidance rather than results.

**Cash flow.** 1H26 operating cash flow of $265.5M compares with $49.9M a year earlier, but $181.3M came from a deferred-revenue swing as customer prepayments took the balance from $5.8M to $192.4M ($185.2M of it ASC 842 advance lease payments). Ex-prepayment, 1H26 OCF was roughly $84M. Capex was $835.2M in 1H26 with roughly $1.0B guided for the remainder, implying full-year capex near $1.84B and free cash flow near negative $1.25B. FY2025 free cash flow was negative $437.7M; FY2024 negative $129.1M. This is a company consuming cash to build a contracted asset base, and it will remain so for at least two more years.

**Asset returns.** The unit economics that justify the capex look strong. Gross equipment held for lease was $2,568.4M at 6/30/26, of which $1,138.8M is construction-in-progress earning nothing, leaving $1,429.6M in service. Q2 owned-asset leasing revenue (total leasing $105.7M less $27.9M of sublease income) was $77.8M, or $311.2M annualized, a 21.8% gross yield on in-service equipment cost before O&M, SG&A and tax. Because the in-service base includes ~$523M of Stateline assets producing zero lease revenue, the yield on revenue-producing assets is higher still. Separately, an asset-light sublease spread contributed $6.6M in Q2 ($27.9M income less $21.3M rental expense) and should not be blended into asset-return math.

**Capital structure.** Debt at principal totals $2,542.2M: $1,300.0M of 6.375% senior notes due 2031 (issued by Solaris LLC, guaranteed by the parent, senior to both convertible series), $339.7M of Stateline term loan at roughly 10.25% (rising to $360.7M in July, **non-recourse to the Company**), $155.0M of 4.75% 2030 converts at a $26.39 strike with no capped call, and $747.5M of 0.25% 2031 converts at a $57.20 strike with a capped call struck at $57.20 and capped at $88.00. Cash is $824.1M unrestricted plus $70.7M restricted; the revolver has $575M available after $75M of letters of credit.

Net debt of $1,718.1M is 5.23x TTM Adjusted EBITDA of $328.6M, 4.30x FY2026E of $399.4M, and 3.90x a $440M Q4 run-rate. On the company's attributable presentation it is 3.59x FY26E. The binding test is not leverage. From Q3 2026 the MUFG agreement requires trailing-four-quarter consolidated EBITDA to consolidated cash interest expense of at least 3.00x. Forward twelve-month corporate cash interest, using the 10-Q's own estimates, is $92.9M ($83.6M senior notes + $7.4M 2030 converts + $1.9M 2031 converts), excluding Stateline's $34.8M. Against TTM EBITDA of $328.6M that is 3.54x; EBITDA could fall about 15% before a breach. This is an inference, not a computed covenant position: high-yield definitions of "consolidated EBITDA" routinely permit pro forma and run-rate adjustments for the Genco and GESA acquisitions and contracted capacity, and Stateline is very likely outside the restricted group. Treat 3.54x as an order-of-magnitude flag. It is nonetheless the reason a heavier position is unattractive: leverage sits at the top of its tolerance band during the largest capex year in the company's history.

**Convertible dilution, stated properly.** The 18,941,057 convert shares equal 24.7% of the 76,566,392 as-exchanged shares outstanding, but post-issuance ownership dilution is 19.8%. Settlement is at the Company's election in cash or stock, so dilution is a choice traded against cash. Capped calls cover only the 2031 series and stop working above $88.00; there is no hedge on the 2030 notes, which are deeply in the money. Convertible holders have captured a large share of the equity appreciation: at 6/30/26 the 2030 notes were marked at $495.5M on $155.0M par and the 2031 notes at $1.2B on $747.5M par, roughly $790M of embedded equity value above par.

Score: **Financial Health 4.5.** Liquidity of ~$1.4B and no corporate maturity before 2030 are real defenses, and the Stateline loan is ring-fenced. Against that: 5.2x trailing net leverage, ~$1.25B of 2026 cash burn, a coverage covenant with modest inferred headroom beginning this quarter, and 44% of gross lease equipment not yet earning.

## Growth Analysis

The forward evidence is stronger than the current-period evidence, which is the crux of the name.

Future minimum lease payments to be received rose from $2,233.0M (12/31/25) to $6,102.6M (6/30/26), with $3,035.7M in the "thereafter" bucket. This is the ASC 842 lessor disclosure, not a company-defined backlog: it includes leases expected to commence on *estimated* future dates, excludes ASC 606 service revenue, excludes the new cost-plus gas scope, and excludes unexercised extension options. Used carefully, a near-tripling in six months is the single best evidence that contracted demand is real.

Three commercial developments underpin the 2027 inflection. The February 2026 Hatchbo agreement (originally >500 MW, then 530 MW) was converted in July into a final operating agreement for a turnkey ~660 MW plant with a tenor extended to as much as 18 years (10-year base plus 8-year extension), with revenue and deployments starting Q1 2027 and scaling through 2028. An April 24, 2026 agreement with a new customer for ~640 MW was expanded in July to add balance of plant, storage and cost-plus gas procurement on a ten-year initial term plus a five-year option, with deployments beginning late 2026. An energy customer expanded contracted microgrid capacity from 60 MW to ~80 MW and extended tenor from four to six years. Management characterizes these collectively as adding more than $100M of expected annual Adjusted EBITDA. Capacity guidance moved from ~2,200 MW by early 2028 to ~3,200 MW by end-2029.

The earnings track record supports execution credibility: four consecutive beats, at +38.0%, +29.3%, +66.0% and +12.7%. The surprise magnitude is decelerating, and the Q2 beat was measured on a non-GAAP adjusted pro forma basis ($0.39 versus roughly $0.31 consensus) while GAAP diluted EPS was $0.26.

The counterweight is the estimate trend, and it is unambiguous. Current-quarter consensus has fallen from $0.29 to $0.21 over 90 days (−27.7%), next quarter from $0.25 to $0.20 (−21.8%), current year from $1.35 to $1.13 (−16.0%), next year from $2.56 to $2.33 (−9.1%). Revisions of this direction and magnitude on a company simultaneously reporting 51% revenue growth reflect the timing of when contracted capacity converts into recognized revenue rather than a demand problem: Stateline, Hatchbo and the April customer all sit in 2027. But timing risk in a levered lessor with covenant tests is exactly the risk that gets priced.

Score: **Growth Prospects 7.0.** Contracted, multi-year, large in absolute terms, with capacity roughly tripling on management's plan. Held below 7.5 by the falling revision trend and by the fact that the 2027 step-up is a forecast dependent on delivery schedules from a supplier that has already demonstrated slippage.

Push the analysis to 2027, where the thesis actually lives. If the ~$100M of contract-expansion EBITDA lands and Stateline commences, FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA of $550–620M is reasonable; call it $580M. Net debt excluding converts by end-2027, after another ~$1.0B of capex against roughly $350–450M of operating cash flow, is plausibly $1.9B. At 14x, EV of $8.12B less $1.9B of net debt is $6.22B, or **$65 per share** on 95.5M if-converted shares. At 17x on $650M: $95. At 11x on $480M (a delayed ramp): $34.

That distribution (base +7%, bull +59%, bear −43%) is the valuation conclusion. There is no margin of safety at $59.59, and there is no identified mispricing to exploit: the shares are neither demonstrably cheap on 2027 numbers nor egregiously expensive if the ramp lands on schedule. The screen-level cheapness some data feeds show is an artifact of a share count roughly 20–25% too low.

The 14 covering analysts (5 strong buy, 9 buy, 0 hold) with a $73–$120 target range should not be treated as a floor. Targets function as floors on inflection names where estimates are *rising*; here estimates have fallen in every period over both 30 and 90 days, and the brief's reconstruction shows the May 14 consensus average of $86.76 (range $60.60–$116) predates both Q2 results and the July drawdown. The only post-Q2 datapoint identified is Wells Fargo's $73 target dated August 3, before earnings, and the FY2025 10-K lists a Wells Fargo affiliate among the "Designated Parties" exempt from the corporate opportunity doctrine, indicating a shareholder relationship. That is a disclosure fact, not an allegation, but it argues for discounting that single datapoint rather than leaning on it.

Score: **Valuation 4.5.** Fair-to-slightly-rich against the risk profile, with the market's own pricing (a 31% drawdown from the June high, a 37% rally off the July 29 low of $43.42, and 26.1% short interest) indicating a genuinely two-sided debate rather than a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat, such as it is, rests on three legs. First, turbine slot access. The Colusa novation is the clearest evidence: Solaris paid Colusa $66.9M for delivery rights to 30 NovaLT16 units and paid Baker Hughes $64.3M to satisfy overdue milestone invoices and suspension-related costs, curing a defaulted, suspended order to jump a queue. Total cost including up to $130.2M of contingent per-unit consideration reaches roughly $626M for ~500 MW, about $1.25M/MW. Roughly $197M of that is a premium above manufacturer price paid for schedule: expensive, but rational when turbine availability rather than demand is the binding constraint. Second, speed to power versus interconnection queues. Third, and newly added, in-house execution: GESA brings aftermarket repair, maintenance, installation and commissioning capability across multiple generation technologies.

Supplier concentration, the one concentration measure that genuinely improved, corroborates the strategy. One supplier was 51% of FY2025 purchases and 71% of accounts payable; in 1H26 the top two were 27% and 13% of purchases with AP split 28/19/16. That is measured diversification, not inferred.

The structural questions are unresolved. Contracts run two to eighteen years against 25-year turbine depreciation (engine cores at 30,000 fired hours), creating real residual-value exposure. The 10-K concedes customers may use Solaris "only as backup/bridge power until line power is received." The 21.8% gross asset yield exists because interconnection is scarce today; renewal pricing five to ten years out depends on whether it stays scarce. Solaris cannot control that, and its $12.8M investment in SISU (SCR emissions systems, 29.7%, unconsolidated) and $5.0M SAFE in Deployable Energy (a small modular reactor developer at a $150M post-money cap, where the technology belongs to Deployable and Solaris is a minority investor and prospective commercialization partner) are option premiums, not answers.

Score: **Competitive Position 5.8.** Trajectory improving on supply access, service capability and supplier diversification; offset by grid substitution risk, residual-value exposure, and customer concentration moving the wrong way.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration, worsening.** The largest customer went from 12.2% of revenue (FY2023) to 17.4% (FY2024) to 47.3% (FY2025) to 58% (1H26) to **63% (Q2 2026)**, and from 38% of trade receivables at 12/31/25 to **68%** at 6/30/26. Within Power, a single data-center customer was 96% of segment revenue in 2024 and 88% in 2025. Adding Hatchbo, the April customer and GESA has broadened *what* Solaris sells without changing *who* pays. Two mitigants are worth stating: the concentration is partly a timing artifact, since 660 MW, 640 MW and ~900 MW of contracted capacity are not yet generating revenue and could plausibly reduce concentration materially in 2027–2028; and $185.2M of advance lease payments received reduces near-term counterparty exposure. The aggravating fact is that the MUFG credit agreement contains a mandatory prepayment and automatic revolver-availability reduction triggered by early termination or suspension of certain Material Contracts. Sophisticated lenders sized this risk explicitly in the documentation.

**No counterparty is named in any filing.** The Power customers are described only as "MZX Tech LLC"/"CTC Property LLC" (Stateline), "Hatchbo, LLC," and an unnamed "affiliate of an investment grade, global technology company and industry leader in AI computing." The "hyperscaler" characterization is a sell-side description reported by secondary sources. Whether parent-level credit support or a guarantee exists behind the entities producing 63% of revenue is unknown. That is the largest single unverified fact in the file, and it caps how much comfort the contracted-revenue disclosures can provide.

**Stateline.** Formed April 2025 (Solaris 50.1% / MZX 49.9%, MZX being a customer affiliate), consolidated as a VIE. At 6/30/26 it held $523.1M of equipment held for lease and $370.4M of liabilities including $339.7M of ~10.25% term debt, and the 10-Q states plainly that "no leases had commenced, and therefore no lease revenue was recognized." The FY2025 10-K had expected commencement in 2026. Its own covenants (fixed charge coverage ≥1.35x, leverage ≤3.50x) begin testing Q1 2027. It absorbed a $5.0M Adjusted EBITDA loss attributable to the NCI in 1H26, and it capitalized $12.0M of $13.1M of interest, which keeps the P&L clean without stopping the cash cost. Two mitigants: $28.0M of receivables and $17.7M of deferred revenue at Stateline indicate billing has begun even absent ASC 842 commencement (phased projects can bill mobilization and readiness fees for months before a lease commences); and the term loan is non-recourse with maximum loss limited to the $86.4M equity investment. Governance is a live issue: the board is 2 Solaris / 2 MZX, and termination of the management agreement would let the partner purchase Solaris's equity interests and cost Solaris its seats on a project representing roughly 41% of Power generation assets as of the 10-K.

**Commitments and financing need.** Purchase commitments total $1,477.2M ($743.2M in the remainder of 2026), of which $364.9M to Baker Hughes is non-cancellable; other supplier commitments carry termination penalties of 5% to 90% depending on timing. Reaching ~3,200 MW from ~950 MW implies roughly $2.5B of additional gross capex at the implied ~$1.14M/MW, against $1.48B committed. The balance requires future orders and future financing at BB−.

**Tax Receivable Agreement.** The liability grew from $76.3M to $109.2M in six months. The 10-K disclosed an early-termination/change-of-control estimate of ~$159.0M discounted ($206.0M undiscounted) at the 12/31/25 share price, a figure that scales with the stock and would rise in any acquisition, effectively taxing a takeout.

**Disclosure hygiene.** The Q2 10-Q states there were "no material updates" to the 10-K risk factors despite the balance sheet doubling, $1.3B of new senior debt, three acquisitions and concentration moving from 47% to 63%. That is a soft signal, not a finding, but it is not the practice of a company erring toward caution.

Resolved risks deserve credit: the *Pirello* securities class action following the March 2025 Morpheus Research short report was voluntarily dismissed without prejudice on February 17, 2026, two derivative suits were dismissed, and the Masaba patent claim is final and unappealable after the PTAB invalidation and the lapsed July 14, 2026 appeal deadline.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 85.9% against 77.2% one-year realized, rich by 8.7 points, with the nearest expiry pricing a ±12.3% move and the term structure flat (85.9% near versus 87.1% at six months). Put/call skew at the near tenor is balanced (−0.6%), put/call volume is 0.66 and open-interest put/call 0.89, so options positioning does not corroborate a directional bearish view despite the short interest. The level matters, not the tilt: with uncertainty priced evenly across three and six months at high absolute levels, the market is pricing wide dispersion around when the 2027 ramp resolves. That is consistent with a HOLD and inconsistent with sizing a position either way. For an owner, the 86% near-term IV makes covered-call overwriting an economically rational way to be paid for the flat-to-modest base case.

Score: **Risk Factors 3.2.** Concentration at 63%/68% and rising, an unnamed counterparty with unverified parent support, a fifteen-month-old $523M project with zero recognized lease revenue and covenants starting Q1 2027, an interest-coverage test starting this quarter with inferred ~15% headroom, and a 19.8% ownership dilution overhang. These are specific, dated and asymmetric.

## Management Quality

The positive is substantive: Solaris imported an entire proven public-company team from Aris Water Solutions after its October 2025 sale to Western Midstream. Zartler (Chairman, Co-CEO) was Aris's Executive Chairman, Amanda Brock joined as Co-CEO and director in October 2025 after serving as Aris's CEO, and Stephan Tompsett became CFO in February 2026 after serving as Aris's CFO. Director Max Yzaguirre chaired the Texas PUC and sat on the ERCOT board. That is directly relevant experience scaling capital-intensive contracted infrastructure. Liability management has been competent: repeatedly refinancing from a 2024 term loan through a Goldman bridge into $1.3B of unsecured 6.375% notes with no maturity before 2030, at the cost of $32.0M of 2025 and $6.0M of 2026 prepayment penalties. Funding roughly 80% of GESA and a large share of Genco with stock issued at $73.42 and ~$57 per share, near the highs, is shareholder-friendly timing.

The offsets are real. A Co-CEO structure is ambiguous on accountability, and Kyle Ramachandran, CFO since the 2017 IPO, was moved to President-only in February 2026. The 10-K discloses no written employment agreements with executives and no key-person insurance. Compensation escalated sharply: PEO total compensation went from $3.82M (2024) to $13.87M (2025), with compensation actually paid at $27.14M against a TSR index of 685.58 versus a peer group at 165.41, so it is directionally earned. But the Q2 2026 grant of 458,000 PSUs with a $58.4M aggregate grant-date fair value (~$127.51 per unit against a ~$70–78 share price) shifted weighting to 90% absolute TSR / 10% relative TSR from a prior 50/50 split, and unrecognized stock compensation went from $38.0M to $132.6M in six months. Absolute-TSR weighting is defensible when the business model has changed enough to invalidate the legacy peer group, which is arguably true here; the structural effect is still less relative-performance discipline in a sector where the entire cohort has re-rated.

Insider transaction prices are asymmetric. KTR Management, the legacy MER seller, exited entirely: 2,114,783 shares at $50.15 (November 2025) and 2,000,000 at $70.75 (April 30, 2026), roughly $247.6M realized. Director Keenan (Yorktown) sold 2,000,000 shares at $74.50 on May 6, retaining over 5M units. Compensation Committee Chair Ray Walker sold 56,841 shares at $72.11, leaving 5,760 shares. The Chief Legal Officer sold ~$2.75M at $73.90–$75.33. Against that, director A.J. Teague bought at $43.12 and again at $72.98, and Zartler and Ramachandran bought at ~$25 in September 2025. Sponsor and acquisition-seller exits are the normal terminal phase of those positions and should not be read as valuation views; Teague's purchase at $72.98 contradicts a uniformly bearish reading. The Walker sale to a residual 5,760 shares is the observation least explained by portfolio mechanics. On balance: meaningful buying clustered at $24–43, meaningful selling at $70–77, stock now $59.59.

Score: **Management Quality 4.5.** Credible operators with good financing execution, offset by clustered insider distribution near the peak, a long-term incentive redesign that reduces relative-performance discipline, governance structures that are loose for a company with $2.5B of debt, and a related-party lease guarantee.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($95, +59%).** The three AI-sector contracts ramp on schedule: Stateline commences and scales, Hatchbo's ~660 MW turnkey plant starts Q1 2027 under an 18-year tenor, the 640 MW April customer deploys from late 2026, and the July amendments' recurring balance-of-plant, storage, O&M and cost-plus gas scope converts ancillary revenue from lumpy commissioning into a durable line. FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA reaches $650M+, leverage falls through 3x on its own, concentration drops toward 30–40% as three counterparties reach scale, and the market re-rates a contracted 18-year-tenor asset base toward 17x. Turbine slot scarcity persists, protecting the 20%+ gross asset yields, and the Colusa deliveries arrive on schedule from September 2026. Consensus targets ($94 mean) are met.

## Conclusion

Solaris has executed a genuine business transformation and assembled a contracted asset base that a slower competitor cannot easily replicate. The $6.10B of future minimum lease payments receivable, up from $2.23B six months earlier, and the 21.8% gross yield on in-service equipment are the strongest facts in the file. The company has also used its balance sheet cleverly, curing a defaulted turbine order to jump a manufacturing queue and funding acquisitions largely with stock issued near its highs.

What prevents a constructive rating at $59.59 is the combination of when the growth arrives and what sits between here and there. Sixty-three percent of Q2 revenue and 68% of receivables rest with one counterparty whose parent credit support is not disclosed anywhere in the filings. A $523M project fifteen months old has recognized zero lease revenue and faces its own covenant tests in Q1 2027. Corporate interest coverage looks to be running near 3.5x against a 3.00x test that begins this quarter, during a year in which the company will consume roughly $1.25B of cash. Consensus earnings estimates have fallen in every period over both 30 and 90 days, and Q3 EBITDA guidance sits 10% below the quarter just reported for reasons that decompose into an accounting reclassification and project-linked service revenue. On correctly-counted shares the enterprise trades near 15.9x FY2026E EBITDA, not the ~12x a stale screen produces.

That leaves a business worth owning and a price that offers no margin of safety. HOLD, with the Q3 print and Stateline commencement as the checkpoints that will move this in either direction.
