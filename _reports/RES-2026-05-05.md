## Executive Summary

RPC, Inc. is a mid-tier U.S. oilfield services company undergoing a strategic pivot from pressure pumping dependence toward wireline and downhole tools, punctuated by the $245M Pintail acquisition in April 2025. The financial profile presents a paradox: a pristine balance sheet ($210M cash, 0.05x debt/equity, Altman Z-Score 4.44) coexists with collapsing earnings (operating margin from 18% in 2022 to 2.8% in 2025, FCF from $214M to $53M). The stock has rallied 60% over the past year from its $4.17 low to $7.76, now trading above the analyst consensus mean ($6.44) and near the high target ($8.00).

The investment case is genuinely balanced. On the downside: 52x trailing P/E, negative estimate revision momentum (current quarter estimates down 60% in 90 days), structural pressure pumping oversupply, no electric frac capability, and continued analyst downgrades. On the upside: net cash of ~$160M ($0.73/share) provides a hard floor, the Pintail integration diversifies the revenue base, bonus depreciation under OBBBA will improve cash taxes materially on $150-180M annual capex, and 37.6% short interest creates squeeze risk that constrains bearish positioning. At 1.5x book value with no solvency risk, the downside to book ($5.05) is quantifiable and limited.

The recommendation is HOLD rather than AVOID because the bearish thesis — cyclical downturn in a structurally challenged OFS segment — was likely fully priced when the stock traded at $4.17 (below book value) for months. The subsequent rally reflects both sector rotation and Pintail's contribution. The stock is not cheap enough to buy given deteriorating earnings, but the balance sheet and crowded short eliminate the conditions for a structurally impaired AVOID call.

## Company Overview

RPC operates through two segments: Technical Services (94.4% of 2025 revenue) encompassing pressure pumping, wireline, downhole tools, coiled tubing, and cementing; and Support Services (5.6%) covering rental tools, pipe inspection, and well control training. The company is headquartered in Atlanta, Georgia with operations concentrated in the Permian Basin, mid-continent, Gulf of America, Rocky Mountain, and Appalachian regions.

The Rollins family controls >50% of voting power through ~61% ownership, qualifying RPC for the NYSE Controlled Corporation exemption. This means no requirement for a majority-independent board or independent compensation/nominating committees.

Revenue mix has shifted dramatically in 2025: pressure pumping declined from 47.7% (2023) to 29.8% ($485M), while wireline surged from 1.3% to 19.4% ($316M), almost entirely from the Pintail acquisition. Downhole tools (Thru Tubing Solutions) represents 24.2% ($394M) and is the primary organic growth vector via proprietary A10 motor technology.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

Revenue in 2025 was $1.63B, up 15% from 2024's $1.41B, but this recovery is Pintail-driven. Excluding Pintail's $296M contribution (9 months), organic revenue declined as pressure pumping revenue fell 17.4% YoY ($587M to $485M).

The profitability trajectory is severe:
- Operating income: $288M (2022) → $245M (2023) → $98M (2024) → $45M (2025)
- Operating margin: 18.0% → 15.2% → 6.9% → 2.8%
- Net income: $218M → $195M → $91M → $32M
- EPS: ~$1.00 → ~$0.90 → $0.43 → $0.15

Adjusted EBITDA was flat at $233M in both 2024 and 2025, suggesting underlying cash-generating capacity is stable. The GAAP deterioration reflects $161M in D&A (up 22% from Pintail's assets) and $20.3M in acquisition-related employment costs.

### Cash Flow

Free cash flow collapsed from $214M (2023) to $129M (2024) to $53M (2025). The 2025 decline was driven by lower net income and a $37M working capital use (versus a $117M source in 2024, which itself benefited from a one-time $53M tax refund). Capex of $148M in 2025 is guided to $150-180M for 2026.

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet is unambiguously strong:
- Cash: $210M
- Total debt: $50M (Pintail seller note, maturing April 2028)
- Net cash: ~$160M ($0.73/share)
- Stockholders' equity: $1.10B ($5.05/share book value)
- Current ratio: 3.24x
- Undrawn $100M revolving credit facility
- Altman Z-Score: 4.44

At $7.76, the stock trades at 1.53x tangible book value. Deducting net cash, the operating business is valued at $7.03/share against $5.05 book — implying the market prices some franchise value above liquidation, which is reasonable given the cash-generating capability even in downturns.

### Bonus Depreciation Impact

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act provides 100% bonus depreciation on assets placed in service after January 19, 2025. With $150-180M annual capex, this materially reduces RPC's cash tax obligations. The 2025 effective tax rate was abnormally high at 43.3% (due to non-deductible acquisition costs on low pretax income). Bonus depreciation should push the cash tax rate well below the statutory rate, potentially adding $20-40M to after-tax cash flow relative to what the current FCF run-rate implies. This is a legitimate near-term catalyst for FCF improvement that the market may not be fully pricing.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are weak on current trajectory:
- Analyst estimates for the current quarter: $0.03 EPS (down 60% from 90 days ago at $0.06)
- Current year estimate: $0.21 (down 23% in 90 days)
- Next year estimate: $0.30 (down 15% in 90 days)
- Revision momentum is uniformly negative across all periods

The organic growth vectors are limited:
1. **A10 downhole motor** — gaining market share with 100+ completed runs at major operators, but contribution to revenue remains small relative to total
2. **Pintail wireline integration** — the largest wireline provider in the Permian, contributing ~$395M annualized revenue, but facing the same Permian completion market weakness
3. **Natural gas frac pump testing** — technology diversification, but behind competitors who already offer electric fleets

Pressure pumping is explicitly being managed for cash harvest, not growth. Management laid down a fleet in October 2025 and stated "we're not investing aggressively within pressure pumping." This is rational given structural oversupply but caps upside.

At current oil prices (~$65/bbl average in 2025, potentially higher given current market conditions), the U.S. rig count has declined 6.3% YoY to 562. Elevated oil prices from geopolitical disruptions could improve activity, but RPC's own CEO warned about "continued challenging conditions over the near term."

## Valuation Assessment

At $7.76 per share:
- Trailing P/E: 52x ($0.15 EPS)
- Forward P/E (current year): 37x ($0.21 est.)
- Forward P/E (next year): 26x ($0.30 est.)
- Price/Book: 1.53x
- EV/EBITDA: ~6.6x (using $233M EBITDA, $1.69B market cap, -$160M net cash = $1.53B EV)

Against peers: median peer P/E is 27.5x, making RPC's trailing 52x look expensive. However, EV/EBITDA of ~6.6x is not unreasonable for an OFS company at cyclical trough earnings. The balance sheet quality (net cash position) genuinely differentiates RPC from leveraged peers.

The stock at $7.76 is above the analyst consensus mean of $6.44 and median of $6.20, and effectively at the high target of $8.00. Sell-side views the current price as fairly-to-fully valued.

The valuation paradox: on trailing/near-term earnings, the stock is expensive. On balance sheet and trough-cycle cash generation ($233M EBITDA), it is not unreasonable. The key uncertainty is whether EBITDA can hold at $233M or deteriorates further given the negative revision trend. If EBITDA declines to $180M (a reasonable bear case given continued pressure pumping weakness), EV/EBITDA rises to ~8.5x — still not egregious but no longer offering upside.

## Competitive Landscape

RPC operates in a structurally challenged competitive position within pressure pumping:

**Weaknesses:**
- No electric frac fleet capability (growing customer requirement)
- Mid-tier scale — cannot match Halliburton's integrated offering or Liberty Energy's technology leadership
- Legacy Tier 2 equipment with lower utilization
- E&P consolidation reducing addressable customer base (management acknowledges losing customers)
- 15% revenue concentration in a single undisclosed private E&P

**Strengths:**
- Pristine balance sheet enables survival through extended downturns where leveraged competitors may exit
- Pintail acquisition makes RPC a leading wireline provider in the Permian
- Thru Tubing Solutions (A10 motor, UnPlug technology) provides differentiated IP-protected tools
- Willingness to idle fleets preserves pricing discipline
- Geographic diversification across multiple U.S. basins

The competitive trajectory is mixed. Pressure pumping is deteriorating (share loss to electric frac, structural oversupply), but wireline and downhole tools are stable-to-improving. The strategic pivot is rational but incomplete — it will take years for the higher-margin, less capital-intensive service lines to fully offset pressure pumping's decline.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **Structural pressure pumping oversupply** — Industry-wide efficiency gains add pump-hour capacity while E&P capital discipline limits activity. This is secular, not cyclical.

2. **No electric frac transition path** — Competitors have invested heavily; RPC has not. Customer preference is moving away from diesel. Every year of delay widens the gap.

3. **15% single-customer concentration** — Undisclosed private E&P. Loss or acquisition of this customer would be devastating.

4. **Negative estimate revision momentum** — This typically precedes further price weakness.

5. **37.6% short interest (6.7 days to cover)** — This creates binary risk in both directions: a squeeze could drive the stock significantly higher on modest positive news, while the crowded positioning reflects informed bearish views on fundamentals.

6. **Controlled corporation governance** — Rollins family can take capital allocation actions (dilutive M&A, reduced buybacks, sustained dividends during earnings collapse) without minority shareholder recourse.

### Options Market Signal

ATM IV collapsed from 1,603% to 79% as the stock rallied from $6.58 to $7.76. The prior extreme IV likely reflected event risk around earnings (reported February 3, 2026). Current 79% IV remains elevated relative to typical mid-cap OFS names, reflecting genuine uncertainty about the fundamental trajectory. The options market is not cheap, suggesting elevated risk is recognized by derivatives traders.

### Mitigating Factors

- Balance sheet eliminates solvency/liquidity risk entirely
- Book value at $5.05/share — the stock would need to decline 35% to reach book
- Dividend yield of 2.1% provides modest income during holding period
- Bonus depreciation improves cash flow trajectory from current trough

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($9.00-10.00)

- Oil prices sustained above $100/bbl drive U.S. completion activity recovery
- Pintail integration exceeds expectations; wireline market share expands
- A10 motor achieves broader adoption, growing downhole tools revenue
- Bonus depreciation materializes as $30-40M annual cash tax benefit, pushing FCF back toward $100M+
- Short squeeze on 37.6% SI amplifies fundamental improvement
- EPS recovers toward $0.40-0.50 on volume and pricing improvement

### Bear Case ($5.00-5.50)

- Oil prices decline to $60-70/bbl, reducing U.S. drilling activity further
- Pressure pumping pricing continues to deteriorate as oversupply persists
- Pintail revenue declines as Permian completions weaken
- Single 15% customer lost or reduces activity
- Estimates continue falling; EPS stays below $0.20
- Stock de-rates toward book value ($5.05)

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock declines below $5.50 (approaching book value + net cash, creating meaningful margin of safety)
- Estimate revisions inflect positive (current quarter or next quarter estimates revised up by 10%+)
- Electric frac fleet announcement or partnership — would address the single largest competitive gap
- Q2/Q3 2026 earnings showing sequential EBITDA improvement without Pintail one-time benefits

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Stock exceeds $9.00 on no fundamental improvement (pure squeeze-driven, >45x next-year earnings)
- Loss of 15% customer confirmed or implied through revenue miss >20%
- FCF turns negative for two consecutive quarters despite bonus depreciation benefits
- Management increases capex above $200M without corresponding revenue growth (capital destruction)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely late April/early May 2026 for Q1, or July 2026 for Q2). The key variable is whether H1 2026 EBITDA run-rate confirms or contradicts the $233M annual pace. Monitor monthly rig count data and Permian completion trends as leading indicators.

## Conclusion

RPC presents a genuinely balanced risk/reward at $7.76. The stock is expensive on current earnings (52x trailing, 37x current-year forward) and above analyst consensus targets. Estimate momentum is uniformly negative. The competitive position in pressure pumping is structurally challenged by the lack of electric frac capability.

However, the fortress balance sheet eliminates permanent capital loss risk. The Pintail acquisition diversifies revenue away from the weakest segment. Bonus depreciation should improve cash flow from the current trough. And 37.6% short interest with 6.7 days to cover means the stock faces material squeeze risk on any positive catalyst, making active bearish positioning dangerous despite weak fundamentals.

The appropriate recommendation is HOLD. The stock is too expensive to buy at 52x earnings with negative estimate momentum and above all analyst targets. But it is not appropriately characterized as AVOID given: (1) the balance sheet eliminates structural impairment risk, (2) the bearish thesis was already priced when the stock traded below book value for months, (3) the short is too crowded to add to, and (4) specific positive catalysts (bonus depreciation, A10 traction, potential oil price tailwinds) could drive near-term upside. Investors should wait for either a pullback below $5.50 (creating adequate margin of safety for a BUY) or fundamental deterioration (confirming an AVOID) before taking a directional position.
