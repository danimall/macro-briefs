## Executive Summary

S&P Global trades at $412.61, representing a 29% discount to its 52-week high of $579.05, while generating $5.1B in annual free cash flow and expanding adjusted operating margins to 51.8% in Q1 2026. The stock's decline from the mid-$500s to low-$400s over the past nine months has been driven primarily by rising long-term interest rates compressing financial data/analytics multiples, combined with concerns about AI disruption, regulatory risks, and Mobility spin execution, rather than by any operational deterioration. Q1 2026 revenue grew 10.4% YoY with adjusted EPS of $4.97 beating consensus, estimates are flat-to-slightly-positive, and three insiders — including the CEO, a director, and the CEO of S&P Dow Jones Indices — made open-market purchases totaling $2.6M within a 3-day window at prices near current levels.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) the stock now trades at ~21x forward earnings ($19.62 FY26E), well below its peer median of 27.9x and its own historical range of 25-32x; (2) the mid-2026 Mobility spinoff removes the lowest-margin segment and creates a purer financial infrastructure business commanding a higher multiple; and (3) the structural earnings power of the Ratings (64% operating margin) and Indices (69% operating margin) segments, which together generate approximately $4.3B in operating income on less than $70M of combined annual capex.

The primary risk is that elevated rates persist or increase further, suppressing debt issuance volumes (52% of Ratings revenue is transaction-based) and continuing to compress duration-sensitive equity multiples. At 21x forward earnings with a 33% FCF margin and a declining share count, the risk/reward is asymmetric to the upside.

## Company Overview

S&P Global operates five segments serving capital and commodity markets:

- **Market Intelligence** ($4.9B revenue, 20% margin): Capital IQ Pro platform, data feeds, analytics, and enterprise workflow
- **Ratings** ($4.7B revenue, 64% margin): Credit ratings on $4.3T in billed issuance (2025), covering corporates, structured finance, sovereigns
- **Commodity Insights** ($2.3B revenue, 41% margin): Platts commodity price benchmarks, upstream data, advisory
- **Indices** ($1.85B revenue, 69% margin): S&P 500 and associated index products; $5.48T in linked ETF AUM
- **Mobility** ($1.7B revenue, 22% margin): Automotive data, being spun off mid-2026

The company's moat derives from being essential market infrastructure: credit ratings are deeply embedded in regulatory frameworks and market practices, the S&P 500 is the global equity benchmark, and Platts assessments serve as reference prices in commodity contracts. Approximately 79% of revenue is recurring (subscriptions + asset-linked + surveillance fees).

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement:**
FY2025 revenue of $15.3B grew 8% YoY on 42.2% GAAP operating margins, up from 32% in FY2023 as IHS Markit integration costs rolled off. Q1 2026 accelerated to 10.4% revenue growth with adjusted operating margins reaching 51.8%. Adjusted diluted EPS has compounded at approximately 13% annually from $14.06 (2023) to $17.83 (2025), with consensus projecting $19.62 for FY2026 and $22.20 for FY2027.

**Cash Flow:**
FCF of $5.14B in FY2025 represents a 33% FCF margin on just $195M in capex. FCF exceeds reported net income by approximately 15%, indicating high earnings quality. Operating cash flow of $5.65B provides interest coverage well in excess of 20x on $287M in annual interest expense.

**Balance Sheet:**
Total debt of $13.1B produces a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 0.42x, manageable given $5.1B in FCF (debt/FCF of 2.6x). The $36.5B in goodwill and $16.3B in intangibles (86% of total assets) are predominantly from the 2022 IHS Markit merger, with additional amounts from subsequent bolt-on acquisitions including With Intelligence ($1.8B, November 2025), and do not represent financial distress risk — they represent the capitalized value of acquired franchises. Current ratio of 0.82 reflects $715M in commercial paper classified as current, which revolves continuously.

**Capital Return:**
$5.0B in buybacks (9.3M shares) in FY2025. Share count declining from 318M to 305M over two years. New 30M-share repurchase authorization (~10% of outstanding) approved November 2025. At current prices, buyback yield exceeds 4%.

## Growth Analysis

Guidance of 6-8% organic revenue growth and $19.40-$19.65 adjusted EPS for FY2026 represents mid-single-digit growth, with analysts projecting acceleration to $22.20 in FY2027 (+13%). The modest deceleration from FY2025's 8% growth reflects a high base in Ratings transaction revenue and FX headwinds, partially offset by continued index AUM growth.

**Structural growth drivers:**
1. **Passive investing secular shift**: Every dollar flowing into S&P-linked ETFs generates recurring asset-linked fee revenue. ETF AUM linked to S&P DJI indices grew 25% YoY to $5.48T in 2025.
2. **AI-related debt issuance**: Management noted AI infrastructure financing drove investment-grade issuance growth in 2025. At $100B+ in annual hyperscaler capex, much funded by corporate debt, this is a durable tailwind.
3. **Private markets expansion**: The $1.8B With Intelligence acquisition positions SPGI in alternatives data as AUM shifts toward private credit, PE, and infrastructure.
4. **Post-spin margin expansion**: Removing Mobility (22% margin) leaves an approximately 45-46% blended margin business, versus 42% today.

**Estimate revisions:** Flat to marginally negative over 30 and 90 days (current year -0.1%, next year +0.6%). Estimates are stable at a level of growth that supports the valuation.

## Competitive Landscape

S&P Global operates in oligopolistic markets with extraordinarily high barriers to entry:

**Ratings (64% margin):** Three firms control global credit ratings. Regulatory frameworks embedding credit ratings in bank capital calculations, insurance regulations, and pension mandates create structural demand that is largely non-discretionary. No new global ratings agency has achieved scale in decades. Competitive trajectory: stable — market share shifts between S&P, Moody's, and Fitch are measured in basis points.

**Indices (69% margin):** The S&P 500 is the defining US equity benchmark. Network effects are self-reinforcing: the more capital tracks the index, the more liquid it becomes, attracting more capital. $5.48T in linked AUM creates a royalty stream with essentially zero marginal cost. Competitive trajectory: improving — passive share continues to grow secularly.

**Commodity Insights/Platts (41% margin):** Commodity price benchmarks are deeply embedded in physical contracts globally. Once a benchmark becomes the reference price in thousands of contracts, switching costs are extreme. The energy transition creates new benchmark opportunities (carbon, hydrogen).

**Market Intelligence (20% margin):** The most competitive segment, facing Bloomberg, LSEG, FactSet, and smaller specialists. The IHS Markit merger created scale advantages, but margins lag peers. AI represents both opportunity (proprietary data advantage) and threat (competitors replicating analytics).

Removing Mobility concentrates the portfolio in capital markets infrastructure where barriers are highest and margins widest.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Rate Environment / Issuance Volume Risk (Medium-High)**
With long-term rates elevated and potential for further increases, debt issuance volumes could decline materially. The 52% transaction-based portion of Ratings revenue (~$2.4B) is cyclically exposed. A 20% decline in issuance would reduce Ratings revenue by ~$490M, or approximately $1.00-$1.30 in EPS after accounting for incremental margins and taxes.

**2. Spinoff Execution Risk (Medium)**
The Mobility separation involves $2B in new debt, management distraction, and potential dis-synergies. The $2B private debt offering (May 18, 2026) for Mobility Global suggests execution is on track. Market reception of the spinoff entity is uncertain.

**3. Duration Sensitivity (Medium)**
At 21x earnings, SPGI is a long-duration equity. Further increases in long-term yields compress the multiple mechanically. Each 1x multiple point represents ~$20/share — moving from 21x to 19x would imply ~$375.

**4. AI Disruption (Low-Medium)**
The 10-K extensively discusses AI threats. The primary defense is proprietary data that cannot be replicated (credit relationships, physical commodity price reporting, index methodology). The risk is more acute in Market Intelligence where analytics compete with general AI capabilities.

**5. Regulatory Intensification (Low-Medium)**
EU AI Act, ESG ratings regulation, and DORA all create compliance costs but also barriers to entry for smaller competitors and demand for SPGI's compliance products.

## Conclusion

S&P Global at $412 represents a high-quality franchise at a cyclically depressed valuation. The 21x forward P/E is a 25% discount to peers and sits at the low end of the stock's historical valuation range, despite maintained guidance and a simplification catalyst (Mobility spinoff) directly ahead. These factors create a floor that limits sustained downside even in adverse macro scenarios.
