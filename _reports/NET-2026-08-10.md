## Executive Summary

Cloudflare delivered the strongest operating quarter in its history as a public company. Q2 FY2026 revenue of $696.1M grew 35.9% year over year, an acceleration from 33.5% in Q1 and roughly 30% for FY2025 as a whole, five to six points of re-acceleration at a $2.8B run-rate. Dollar-based net retention improved 600 basis points to 120%, large customers (>$100K annualized) rose 26.6% to 4,698, and channel partners now originate 31% of revenue versus 25% a year ago. Management raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $2.864–2.870B from $2.805–2.813B against a $2.81B consensus and lifted adjusted EPS guidance to $1.25–1.26 from $1.19–1.20. The fundamental trajectory is improving, and the estimate-revision record confirms it: FY2027 consensus EPS has risen 10.0% in 90 days to $1.67, with four consecutive quarterly beats behind it.

The problem is price and the composition of the margin story. At $309.64 the equity is worth $110.2B on 356.08M Class A and Class B shares combined (the $99.4B market capitalization in some data feeds corresponds to Class A alone and understates equity value by roughly 10%). That is 38.1x enterprise value to FY2026 guided revenue and 185x FY2027 consensus EPS, a forward PEG near 5.7 on 32% expected earnings growth. Two disclosures argue the multiple is not supported by the underlying operating economics. First, non-GAAP operating margin in Q2 was 13.8%, essentially unchanged from 14% a year earlier despite 36% revenue growth; scale produced zero incremental margin. The guided Q3 step to 17.6% implies an 82.6% incremental operating margin, which is arithmetically achievable only if the absolute cost base is flat to down sequentially, which is what removing approximately 1,100 people (20% of the workforce) produces. The margin expansion is driven by a headcount cut rather than operating leverage. Second, gross margin fell from 75% to 71.8%, with the largest driver a $30.1M increase in "third-party technology services costs" that the 10-Q does not define. Cloudflare's architectural argument is that incremental products carry near-zero marginal infrastructure cost; a cost line growing 53% against 36% revenue growth challenges that argument, and roughly $86M of annualized gross profit is at stake at guided revenue.

Three near-term binaries sit on top of this that a 38x revenue multiple does not accommodate: the Capitalization Changes, scheduled for implementation as early as September 2026 and facing eight stockholder complaints (seven consolidated) alleging the structure is "not entirely fair" to non-founders; the September 15, 2026 default blocking of AI training and agent crawlers on ad-supported pages, which places Cloudflare adversarially between publishers and the largest AI and search platforms; and the August 15, 2026 cash settlement of $1,293.75M of zero-coupon 2026 Notes, which removes roughly $50M of annual interest income (about $0.14 per share, or 11% of the FY2026 non-GAAP EPS base) with no offsetting interest-expense saving. We rate the shares REDUCE: the business is not deteriorating, but the expected risk-adjusted return from this price is negative and the downside skew is severe.

## Company Overview

Cloudflare operates a distributed edge network spanning more than 335 cities in more than 125 countries, selling network security, application performance, zero-trust access, and developer compute from a single general-purpose platform. It reports as one operating segment and holds 390 issued patents with 75 pending. Revenue splits geographically 51% United States, 27% EMEA, 14% APAC, 8% other; international is 49% of the total, down slightly from 51%.

Product-line revenue is not disclosed, so the widely repeated framing of four business "acts" (application services, zero trust/SASE, the Workers developer platform, and an emerging AI-traffic layer) cannot be sized from primary filings. Remaining performance obligations stood at $2,732.0M at June 30, 2026, with 64% expected to convert within twelve months, and management stated current RPO grew 35% year over year. RPO is contracted-but-unrecognized revenue under the company's own definition; it excludes certain cancellable and variable amounts and should not be read as total backlog or total future demand.

One disclosure change matters for monitoring: beginning in 2026 Cloudflare stopped publishing total paying customers, stating the metric "has become significantly less important to us over time." The rationale is defensible as revenue concentrates in large accounts and shifts toward consumption pricing on Workers. It nonetheless removes the cleanest series for tracking the self-serve and SMB funnel, the same cohort the MD&A identifies as experiencing elevated churn, while the Q2 press release simultaneously cited "record growth in total paying customers."

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory and the guided reversal.** FY2024 revenue was $1,669.6M; FY2025 approximately $2,168M (+29.8%); Q1 FY2026 $639.8M (+33.5%); Q2 FY2026 $696.1M (+35.9%). Sequential growth in Q2 was +8.8%. The FY2026 guide of $2,867M at midpoint implies H2 revenue of $1,531.2M against an estimated $1,176.6M in H2 FY2025, or roughly +30.1%, and the Q3 guide of $736.5M is only +5.8% sequentially. Two readings fit: management is guiding conservatively, which its four-quarter beat record and the $57M midpoint raise support; or Q2 contained some pull-forward. The filings do not adjudicate. The guided deceleration should be carried into any model regardless.

**Gross margin.** Q2 gross profit was $499.5M on $696.1M, a 71.8% margin versus 75% a year earlier. Cost of revenue rose to $196.5M from roughly $128.1M, a $68.4M, 53% increase against 36% revenue growth. The 10-Q names the quarterly drivers: +$30.1M third-party technology services, +$20.9M co-location/network/bandwidth, +$10.1M depreciation. The largest of these is undefined in the filing. The most economically consequential interpretation is third-party compute and inference capacity supporting AI workloads, consistent with the December 2025 Replicate acquisition and Workers AI growth; if that is right, Cloudflare is renting capacity rather than amortizing its own edge, which is a structurally different cost curve. Equally consistent alternatives (third-party software licensing embedded in delivered services, public-cloud storage or egress for object workloads, or dual-running migration costs) cannot be ruled out from the disclosure, and no transcript was available. This is an inference, not an established fact, and it is the single most important unresolved item for the multiple.

A counterpoint: co-location and bandwidth attributable to free customers fell $9.4M in the quarter and $19.8M in the half, and that expense sits in sales and marketing. Free-tier delivery is getting cheaper while paid-tier delivery gets more expensive, which points to new workload types rather than general traffic growth as the source of the compression.

**Profitability and operating leverage.** GAAP loss from operations was $(205.7)M (−29.6% margin) including a $150.7M restructuring charge; excluding it, roughly $(55.0)M or −7.9%. GAAP net loss was $(170.0)M, $(0.48) per share on 354.3M weighted-average shares, against $(50.4)M a year earlier. Non-GAAP income from operations was $96.1M, 13.8% of revenue, versus 14% a year earlier. That is the central financial fact of the quarter: 36% revenue growth produced no non-GAAP margin expansion on the company's own definition. Operating expenses grew S&M +26% to $276.1M, R&D +19% to $159.5M, G&A +23% to $118.9M (including $14.5M of professional fees for third-party accounting, consulting and legal work, plausibly recapitalization- and litigation-related though not attributed in the filing). Headcount changes year over year were S&M +4%, R&D +5%, G&A −13%.

The Q3 guide changes the picture, for a specific reason. Midpoint non-GAAP operating income of $129.5M on $736.5M is a 17.6% margin, a 377 basis point sequential step. The implied incremental margin is (129.5 − 96.111) ÷ (736.5 − 696.061) = 82.6%. That requires the absolute cost base to be roughly flat to down sequentially, which the workforce reduction (substantially complete by September 30, 2026) delivers. Cutting a fifth of the company is a legitimate way to expand margin. It is a less durable source of leverage than scale, and it carries revenue risk in a sales organization whose headcount grew only 4% year over year, risk that would not surface until Q4 or FY2027.

**Stock-based compensation and cash flow quality.** Q2 SBC was $159.9M including $27.9M within restructuring, or 23.0% of revenue. H1 SBC per the cash flow statement was $274.1M. Unrecognized SBC stands at $1,138.6M for RSUs/PSUs over a weighted-average three years plus $83.2M for performance options over 2.9 years. H1 operating cash flow was $275.9M against capex of $115.2M and capitalized software of $20.2M, producing free cash flow of $140.5M (10.5% margin) versus $86.1M (9%). Q2 alone: OCF $117.6M, FCF $56.4M (8%).

Two quality flags. FCF of $140.5M is 0.51x the $274.1M of SBC: cash generation is real but substantially funded by paying employees in equity, and 23% of revenue is at the high end of the software cohort. Deferred revenue contributed +$127.1M of the half's OCF, so a meaningful portion is collections timing. Accounts payable and accrued liabilities related to property and equipment stood at $71.4M versus $51.1M a year earlier, indicating accrual-basis network investment running ahead of cash capex. As a labeled sensitivity, annualizing Q2 FCF gives $226M, but that ignores $98.6M of restructuring severance already paid with $24.3M still accrued and deferred-revenue seasonality; applying the H1 margin of ~10–11% to guided revenue gives a more defensible ~$300–315M for FY2026, still embedding the restructuring drag.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: cash and equivalents $1,663.8M plus available-for-sale securities $2,499.0M for $4,162.8M of liquid assets, against $3,293.75M of convertible principal ($1,293.75M 2026 Notes now current, $2,000M 2030 Notes), both zero-coupon. Net cash is +$869.0M. Cash interest paid in the half was $32 thousand. Total stockholders' equity $1,620.0M against an accumulated deficit of $(1,397.8)M. The $400M revolver maturing May 2029 is undrawn, with maximum net leverage and minimum interest coverage covenants in compliance. Current ratio is 1.82; excluding the maturing 2026 Notes it is 3.58, so the headline figure understates liquidity because the note is fully cash-covered.

**The 2026 Notes settlement — mechanics and the overlooked earnings effect.** The notes mature August 15, 2026, and on May 15, 2026 Cloudflare irrevocably elected combination settlement: principal in cash, Class A shares for excess conversion value. Conversion price $191.34, 6.762M underlying shares. Illustratively at $309.64: conversion value $2,093.8M, excess over principal $800.1M, shares delivered 2.584M; the capped calls (strike $191.34, cap $250.94) pay at most $59.60 per underlying share, or $403.0M total, returning 1.302M shares. Net incremental issuance is roughly 1.28M shares, or 0.36% ownership dilution (existing holders go from 100% to 99.64%). Actual settlement uses 40-day observation-period VWAPs and the capped calls began expiring in increments in July, so this is an illustration rather than a forecast. The dilution is immaterial; the structural point is that above the $250.94 cap, every incremental dollar of appreciation converts to unhedged issuance. The claim that "the converts are hedged" holds only to the cap, and the stock trades 23% above it.

The consequential effect is on earnings. Q2 interest income was $39.9M against $3.1M of interest expense, implying a portfolio yield near 3.9% on roughly $4.13B of average liquid assets. Paying $1,293.75M in cash removes that capital, forgoing approximately $50M of annual interest income with no offsetting interest saving because the notes are zero-coupon. Against an FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guide of $1.25–1.26, which includes interest income, that is roughly an 11% headwind to the FY2027 base before any operating improvement. Management has not visibly quantified it, so we cannot rule out that consensus already contemplates it, but it materially raises the operating bar to grow FY2027 EPS to the $1.67 consensus: the required operating improvement is closer to $0.55 than $0.41.

**The 2030 Notes.** $2,000M principal, zero coupon, June 2030, conversion price $247.67 (8.075M underlying shares), fair value $2,540.1M at June 30 (127% of par). The 130% contingent-conversion trigger of $321.97 was not met in Q2. The associated capped calls cap at $469.73, well above spot, so at current prices this issue is effectively fully hedged. The 10-Q discloses that in connection with the Capitalization Changes the 2030 Notes become convertible during a window from the 25th scheduled trading day before the ex-dividend date through the day prior, an off-cycle conversion opportunity created by the recapitalization.

**Maximum contingent dilution.** The antidilutive schedule totals 29,487 thousand shares (2026 Notes 6,762k, 2030 Notes 8,075k, options 4,757k, RSUs/PSUs 9,796k, ESPP 97k) against 355,931 thousand outstanding, 8.3% of shares, or 7.65% ownership dilution if all were issued, down from 34,190k a year earlier. This is maximum contingent capitalization subject to PSU market conditions, ~3-year service periods, and note settlement mechanics that at current prices deliver far fewer shares than the gross count. It is not the current fully diluted capitalization. Separately, 160,097 thousand shares are reserved for future issuance, which is authorization, not expected issuance.

## Growth Analysis

The demand evidence is the strongest part of the case and deserves credit without qualification. Net retention at 120%, up from 114%, means the installed base alone is contributing 20 points of growth before any new logos. Current RPO grew 35%, ahead of reported revenue growth, which is the sequencing you want. Channel mix rising from 25% to 31% of revenue suggests distribution leverage rather than pure direct-sales headcount arithmetic, relevant given the 20% cut. Estimate revisions confirm: current-quarter consensus +7.3% over 90 days, current year +6.0% to $1.26, next year +10.0% to $1.67, with 29–33 contributing analysts and four consecutive beats averaging roughly 7% upside.

The strategic driver management articulates is that AI shifts traffic toward machine-to-machine patterns Cloudflare is positioned to police and monetize. The concrete moves are real: from September 15, 2026, Cloudflare will block AI training and agent crawlers by default on ad-supported pages absent owner opt-in, and will require AI companies to segregate crawlers into Search, Agent, and Training categories; controls extend to free accounts. Integrations with beehiiv (June 23) and Patreon (July 10) show adoption. An "Agents Week" launch on August 4 introduced agent-payment rails, though that rests on a single low-tier source and is unconfirmed by filings. No filing establishes the flow of funds under pay-per-crawl: whether AI operators pay Cloudflare which remits to publishers, whether Cloudflare acts as agent, or whether it merely enforces bilateral arrangements. We do not model revenue from this layer, and neither should anyone underwriting a 38x revenue multiple on its promise.

Against the positives: guided H2 growth of ~30% versus 36% delivered in Q2; a Q3 sequential guide of +5.8% versus +8.8% achieved; the MD&A's enumerated macro effects (lengthening sales cycles, pipeline slowdowns, higher DSOs, higher pay-as-you-go and SMB churn, slower payment, with uncertainty potentially persisting through 2026); and the removal of the paying-customer series that would have let investors verify the self-serve funnel through that churn. Growth prospects score 8.0, since the acceleration and retention improvement are genuine and confirmed by rising estimates, but the growth is being guided down, not up, from here.

The distribution matters more than the point estimate: 43.5% of downside against 29.2% of upside, on a business whose fundamentals are improving. That asymmetry, not any deterioration in the business, drives the rating. Valuation score 2.7.

## Competitive Landscape

Cloudflare competes simultaneously in CDN and application security (Akamai, Fastly, hyperscaler security services), zero trust and SASE (Zscaler, Palo Alto, Netskope, Cisco), edge compute (AWS Lambda@Edge, Vercel, Fly.io, hyperscaler edge products), and a newly created AI-traffic policy layer where the relevant counterparties are model developers and publishers rather than infrastructure vendors. On trajectory, the dimension that matters, the evidence is favorable: 600bp of net-retention improvement is the clearest single indicator of expanding wallet share, and Morgan Stanley's identification of Workers as the top growth vector aligns with the shift toward consumption pricing.

The AI-crawler position is differentiated and double-edged. Cloudflare's aggregate traffic position lets it set a default that AI operators must respond to; its site owners are the customers, and the AI companies are counterparties whose access is being conditioned. Under the strictest setting, blocking Training would also block Googlebot. That leverage invites regulatory attention, retaliation, and the possibility that the largest AI operators route around the layer or negotiate directly with large publishers. None of this is quantified in filings.

Two structural dependencies temper the moat: China network presence rests on a commercial relationship with JD Cloud, a single counterparty in a high-regulatory-risk jurisdiction; and the June 2025 Workers KV outage, caused by a third-party storage failure, demonstrated dependence on infrastructure Cloudflare does not own. The two most recent acquisitions were talent purchases rather than capability purchases in any revenue sense: VoidZero at $164.2M with $143.0M (87%) in goodwill attributed primarily to assembled workforce plus $106.4M of separate retention awards, and Replicate at $57.4M with $46.6M goodwill and two-year intangible lives. Buying teams is a reasonable strategy in developer tooling; the two-year lives suggest management does not expect the acquired technology to hold standalone value, so the return depends on retention. Competitive position 7.0: improving, with the improvement partly resting on a position that creates new adversaries.

## Risk Assessment

**Recapitalization litigation and September implementation (highest near-term specificity).** The Capitalization Changes, approved at the June 2026 annual meeting, create a non-voting Class C and a Series FF preferred, exchange substantially all co-founder Class B for equal amounts of Series FF plus Class A, and split each existing share into one existing-class share plus one Class C (economically a 2-for-1 in which half the new shares carry no vote). Disclosed effects: co-founder relative voting power unchanged, non-founder Class B holders lose voting power, future issuances expected to be Class C so equity issuance no longer dilutes control. Eight complaints seek to enjoin the changes (seven consolidated), alleging they are "not entirely fair" because they "allegedly allow the Co-Founders to maintain control and retain significant liquidity opportunities, without sufficient consideration in return," and the eighth alleges Article X required a two-thirds vote for Proposal 4. We have not read the Series FF certificate of designation and do not infer motive; legitimate rationales exist, including M&A currency and index considerations. The vote tallies are unverified (EDGAR returned 403 on the June 30 8-K), which is directly material to the charter claim.

**Restructuring execution.** The new risk factor states that the shift to an "agentic AI-first operating model, including our use of AI and other automation tools and our related recent workforce reduction, may not achieve the benefits we expect in the time we expect, if at all." The reduction touches a sales organization that grew only 4% year over year, in a business that just accelerated to 36%. Total charges up to $165M ($125M cash severance, $35M SBC, $5M other); $150.7M taken in H1 with $98.6M paid and $24.3M accrued.

**Gross margin composition.** A 300bp compression whose largest driver is undefined, growing 53% against 36% revenue growth, in a business whose valuation rests on low marginal cost.

**Valuation cushion.** At 38x revenue and 185x FY2027 EPS, there is essentially none. This is the dominant risk.

**Other specifics.** Content and customer-conduct liability, distinctive to Cloudflare given free-tier scale. Third-party infrastructure dependence. The OFAC review closed with a No Action Letter on April 9, 2026, a real de-risking, but the filing does not state the BIS export-control matter is closed, so treat it as open. Tariffs on non-U.S. network equipment are named in the MD&A as a cost pressure. Founder dependence, with Prince and Zatlyn controlling ~49.3% of voting power and insiders/5% holders 66.0%. Unidentified Item 5.02 officer or director changes in 8-Ks dated February 10, June 30 and July 1, 2026, so a senior departure cannot be ruled out.

Risk score 3.8: multiple dated binaries, a stretched multiple, and an undefined cost line, against low near-term solvency risk.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is priced for near-term event risk without a directional consensus. Near-term ATM IV of 67.6% is rich against 62.0% one-year realized (a 5.6-point spread) and sits at the 83rd percentile within the chain, with the term structure in backwardation: 67.6% at one week, 59.6% at one month, 55.4% at three months, back to 59.4% at six months. That shape prices imminent stress, consistent with the August note settlement and the September recapitalization implementation, with the six-month tenor re-steepening around the same window. Put/call skew is 4.4% at the front (elevated put demand per the source characterization) but flips negative at three and six months (−2.2% and −4.7%); the six-month put/call volume ratio of 1.09 versus 0.31 at three months is the one place downside is being accumulated in size. Volume and open-interest put/call ratios of 0.54 and 0.39 remain call-tilted overall, and short interest is only 3.5% of float at 3.0 days to cover, so positioning is not crowded short. The single flagged unusual print is a $310 call for August 14 at 3.4x open interest, near-term upside speculation into the settlement window. The market is paying up for protection into September without abandoning upside exposure, which corroborates our event-risk framing rather than a directional bearish thesis. Elevated IV also argues against buying the stock outright here for anyone seeking exposure; selling covered upside into rich near-term vol is the more coherent way to trim.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (target $400, +29.2%)

Cloudflare is the network layer for a traffic pattern that is being rewritten. If internal AI-tool adoption genuinely rose more than 600% in Q1 as management describes, the 20% workforce reduction is a productivity event rather than a capacity cut, and the guided 17.6% Q3 non-GAAP margin becomes the floor of a multi-year climb toward 20%+, a path that takes FY2027 EPS toward $1.90 even after absorbing the interest-income loss. Gross margin recovers as third-party AI capacity is insourced onto owned edge infrastructure. The crawler-control and agent-payment layer converts from a policy tool into a disclosed revenue line, adding a stream with no incumbent competitor. Balance sheet risk is low: $4.16B of liquid assets, +$869M net cash, zero-coupon debt, $32 thousand of cash interest paid in the half, an undrawn revolver. Four consecutive beats and rising estimates argue the guide is conservative again.

### Bear Case (target $175, −43.5%)

The Q2 quarter contained no operating leverage at all: 36% revenue growth produced a non-GAAP operating margin of 13.8% versus 14% a year earlier. The margin expansion investors are paying 38x revenue for arrives entirely from removing 1,100 people, and the implied 82.6% incremental margin cannot repeat once the cost base resets. Gross margin fell 300bp on a cost line the company will not define, worth roughly $86M of annualized gross profit, which suggests AI-attach revenue carries a materially worse marginal economic profile than legacy edge services. The interest-income loss from the August settlement removes ~$0.14 per share (11% of the FY2026 base) that must be recovered before FY2027 EPS grows at all. Cutting a fifth of the company from a sales organization that grew 4% year over year risks pipeline damage that surfaces in Q4 or FY2027, exactly when management has guided growth down to ~30%. The September recapitalization is being litigated by eight plaintiffs on entire-fairness grounds while both co-founders sell economic interest daily and no insider has bought a share on the open market. Free cash flow of $140.5M covers only half of $274.1M of stock compensation. At 185x FY2027 consensus and PEG 5.7, a single quarter of sub-30% growth or further gross-margin compression compresses the multiple toward 18–20x revenue, which is $175.

### Management Quality

The compensation architecture is price-aligned: PSU tranches with 90-day VWAP hurdles at $156, $203, $263, $343, $446 and $579, with the first two achieved in July and September 2025 and the third very likely met at current prices. Against that, the Form 4 record from May 28 to August 6, 2026 is uniform and one-directional with no open-market purchase by any insider. Prince converted Class B to Class A and sold 100% same-day across nine observed trading days, roughly 450,000–470,000 shares for approximately $110–125M gross, retaining ~6.93M Class B; Zatlyn ran the identical structure across six days; Seifert exercised and sold 55,000 performance options struck at $44.72 on August 4, two days before earnings, for roughly $16.5M. The 10-Q's trading-arrangement disclosure named only a terminated 1,020-share purchase plan by a director, so 10b5-1 status for these sales is unconfirmed and we do not infer information advantage; Seifert sold ahead of a beat that moved the stock up 16%, which argues against informed selling. Routine diversification during record results is low-signal.

What lowers the score is not the selling itself but the combination: a recapitalization that structurally severs founder control from founder economic ownership at the moment founders are steadily reducing economic ownership, now facing eight fiduciary-duty complaints; retirement of the paying-customer metric from reviewed disclosure while continuing to cite it favorably in unaudited commentary; and two acquisitions where 87% of consideration (VoidZero) and the majority of consideration (Replicate) landed in goodwill with two-year intangible lives and, for VoidZero, retention awards exceeding the cash purchase price. Management quality 4.8.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

## Conclusion

Cloudflare's Q2 was a strong quarter and the operating trajectory is improving on every demand metric that matters: revenue accelerating to 35.9%, net retention up 600bp to 120%, large customers up 26.6%, current RPO up 35%, guidance raised, four consecutive beats, and FY2027 consensus EPS up 10% in 90 days. Nothing in this report argues the business is deteriorating.

The rating rests on price and on the composition of the margin story. Thirty-six percent revenue growth produced zero non-GAAP operating margin expansion in the quarter actually reported; the expansion investors are paying for arrives from removing 20% of the workforce, and the implied 82.6% incremental margin does not repeat. Gross margin fell 300bp on a cost line the company does not define, in a business whose valuation depends on low marginal cost. The August 15 cash settlement removes roughly 11% of the non-GAAP EPS base with no offsetting saving. A September recapitalization that decouples founder control from founder economics is being litigated by eight plaintiffs while founders sell daily and no insider buys. Against a bull case worth +29%, the bear case is −43.5% and the probability-weighted expected return is slightly negative.

At 38.1x enterprise value to guided revenue, 185x FY2027 consensus earnings and a forward PEG of 5.7, the shares require everything to go right and price in the assumption that it will. Trim exposure into strength; revisit at the Q3 print or in the $240s.
