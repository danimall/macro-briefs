**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Chord Energy is a pure-play Williston Basin (Bakken/Three Forks) independent E&P, formed from the 2022 Oasis-Whiting merger of equals and now the basin's leading consolidator. The investment case is straightforward and high-quality: production of 275,615 Boepd (57% oil), a 0.4x-levered balance sheet with $2.19B liquidity and no near-term maturities, $507.5M of Q1 2026 operating cash flow, projected free cash flow of $1.4-1.5B at $80 WTI, and capital returned through a $1.30/quarter base dividend (~3.8% yield) plus $881M of remaining buyback authorization. The October 2025 acquisition of XTO's Williston assets for $542M closed with no goodwill recognized, accretive and consistent with the consolidator strategy.

The valuation is genuinely inexpensive. At $133.74, the stock trades at ~6.2x current-year consensus EPS ($21.52) and ~8.5x next-year ($15.80), against a 3.8% dividend yield and a free-cash-flow yield north of 18% at the $80 deck. Estimate revisions have been large (+297% current-year, +398% next-quarter over 90 days), Q1 2026 beat by 43.7% ($4.56 vs. $3.17), and the sell-side distribution is constructive: 13 Buy/Strong Buy versus 3 Hold and zero Sell, with a low price target ($135) sitting at the current price and a mean of $173 implying roughly 30% upside.

We rate CHRD a HOLD with a constructive, growth-leaning bias rather than a BUY, and we want to be precise about why. The bull case is real and we are not anchoring on prior price appreciation. The restraint is grounded in three things the data shows directly: (1) the magnitude of those estimate revisions is mechanically driven by a higher oil-price deck after the late-February WTI spike rather than by company-specific operating leverage — analysts have re-marked their crude assumptions, not discovered new structural earnings power; (2) consensus itself pencils next-year EPS 27% below current-year, the market's own statement that 2026 reflects elevated rather than sustainable earnings; and (3) two live, Williston-specific binaries (the Dakota Access Pipeline final decision and the MHA Nation riverbed mineral-ownership litigation) plus single-basin concentration widen the plausible bear-case drawdown beyond what we want from an incremental long here. The near-term options market reinforces caution, pricing imminent stress through a backwardated, rich IV structure.

## Company Overview

Chord operates almost entirely in the Williston Basin of North Dakota and Montana, with limited non-operated Marcellus interests. Roughly 158,027 Bopd of the Q1 production is crude. Essentially all net acreage is held by production as of year-end 2025, which removes drilling-obligation pressure and lets the company optimize timing.

The operational differentiator is the 3-mile and 4-mile long-lateral well program, which lowers per-foot drilling and completion costs and improves capital efficiency in a mature basin. Chord also runs an integrated in-house marketing operation that buys and resells third-party barrels to optimize transport and meet pipeline specs; this inflates gross revenue but is near-zero margin (Q1: $515M purchased sales against $510M purchased expenses), and should be netted out when assessing true economics.

Chord is the natural acquirer of Williston assets divested by majors, and the XTO purchase is the clearest expression of that role. With low leverage and operational scale, the company is positioned to consolidate a basin where the majors are reallocating capital elsewhere.

## Financial Analysis

The most important interpretive point in the file is that FY2025 reported net income of ~$44.5M (down from $848.6M in 2024) is an accounting artifact, not an operational collapse. The decline was driven by a non-cash goodwill impairment recorded mid-2025; a standardized screen would misread this as deterioration. The underlying business is cash-generative.

Q1 2026 confirms the health: net income $108.6M and oil/NGL/gas revenue of $1.15B. Unit costs are competitive — LOE $9.87/Boe, gathering/processing/transportation $2.70/Boe, production taxes ~7.5% of revenue. The one cost metric to monitor is DD&A at $15.20/Boe, which is rising as proved-developed reserves decline; depletion-rate escalation is a real signal in a mature basin and bears on long-run capital efficiency.

Q1 capex was ~$345M, leaving healthy free cash flow. Capital return is via the base dividend plus opportunistic buybacks: $70.7M repurchased in Q1 at ~$126.53/share, with $881.4M remaining on the $1B authorization. Share count fell from ~60.1M at year-end 2024 to ~56.3M.

The balance sheet is a genuine strength and the primary reason Financial Health scores 7.5. There are no revolver borrowings; total debt is $1.5B across two tranches ($750M 6.000% notes due 2030, $750M 6.750% notes due 2033). The undrawn revolver provides $1.97B and cash $226M, the borrowing base was reaffirmed at $2.75B in May 2026, and leverage sits near 0.4x. The 2026 notes were refinanced in 2025, so there are no near-term maturities. One minor blemish: an out-of-period tax error ($41.8M benefit recorded in Q1 2026) tied to the mid-2025 goodwill-impairment deferred-tax treatment, deemed immaterial by management but worth flagging on internal-controls grounds.

The Q1 derivative book showed a $241.5M net loss, almost entirely unrealized ($223M), as WTI spiked more than 50% after Middle East conflict escalation and Chord's collars (floors ~$62-82, ceilings ~$74-82) capped upside participation. This is the hedge book working as designed: it drags in a rising-price environment and provides a floor in a falling one. It is not an earnings-quality problem, but it does mean reported non-GAAP earnings power is somewhat understated at high spot prices and somewhat protected at low ones.

## Growth Analysis

Growth Prospects score 5.5, and the framing matters. The Williston is a mature basin, and Chord's thesis is free-cash-flow-per-share and capital return, not production growth. Management raised full-year 2026 oil guidance by roughly 2,000 bpd with no capex increase after Q1 volumes came in ~3% above the guidance midpoint despite weather and midstream constraints, which is a genuine positive on capital efficiency. The long-lateral program is the lever that keeps the basin economic at lower prices, and analysts cite economics that work even at normalized ~$70 WTI.

The estimate-revision data is large on its face but is not the durable operating inflection the magnitude implies. The revisions are predominantly the mechanical consequence of analysts re-marking their oil-price decks higher after the late-February crude spike. The clearest evidence is that consensus next-year EPS sits 27% below current-year; analysts are explicitly modeling 2026 as an elevated-price year that reverts. For a software or secular-growth name, revisions of this size would be a strong forward signal; for a commodity cyclical, they primarily reflect the strip, and their predictive value for forward equity returns is materially lower. We are weighting them as confirmation of near-term cash generation, not as a structural growth thesis.

Reserve replacement is the longer-term growth question. Approximately 31% of proved reserves are PUD, requiring ~$3.0B of future development capex, and the rising depletion rate signals that maintaining production requires steady reinvestment. The XTO bolt-on extends inventory but is incremental, not transformational. M&A optionality (Chord as the disciplined Bakken acquirer) is a real but lumpy growth path.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation scores 6.5 — genuinely inexpensive, but on earnings the market itself flags as cyclically elevated.

The 3.8% base dividend is well-covered, and free-cash-flow yield exceeds 18% at the $80 deck. Against the peer set, the comparison is muddied by distorted multiples: SM Energy at 33.4x and Murphy at 66.1x are not clean comps, while the cleaner cyclical comps — Matador at 13.8x and Range at 10.3x — sit above Chord's forward multiple. The peer-median P/E of 33.4 is not a meaningful anchor here given the dispersion.

The sell-side distribution is the strongest element of the bull case and deserves direct engagement. The price-target range runs from a low of $135 (essentially the current price) to a high of $224, with a mean of $173 and median of $175. Sixteen analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy against three Holds and zero Sells, and the recent revision was an increase of one Strong Buy rating. The entire sell-side floor sits at or above the current price, which implies the analyst community sees minimal downside and ~30% upside to the mean. That is a constructive setup and a legitimate reason not to be bearish.

Where we differ from a clean BUY conclusion: the ~8.5x next-year multiple is fair-to-cheap, not deeply mispriced, once one accepts that next-year EPS is already marked down 27% on the expectation of lower realized prices. The 6.2x current-year figure flatters the stock at what is plausibly a cyclical earnings high. A reasonable normalized-earnings view (mid-cycle ~$70-75 WTI) puts the multiple in the low-double-digits, in line with the cleaner peers, leaving the stock cheap-but-not-screaming on through-cycle power. The valuation supports holding and supports the constructive bias; it does not by itself clear the asymmetric-mispricing bar.

## Competitive Landscape

Competitive Position scores 7.0. Chord's scale, ~0.4x leverage, and long-lateral operational efficiency make it the logical consolidator of Williston assets as majors divest. The XTO acquisition reinforced that role on accretive terms with no goodwill. Held-by-production acreage and in-house marketing add flexibility. Pay-vs-performance disclosure shows cumulative TSR of 398 against a peer group at 283, evidence of real relative outperformance over the measurement period.

The trajectory question — and the reason we trim from a prior 7.5 — is basin maturity. Rising DD&A and a 31% PUD share of reserves indicate that maintaining the asset base requires ongoing reinvestment and that the most productive locations are being drawn down. The long-lateral program is a durable edge but operates within a mature basin whose remaining inventory depth is the key competitive-longevity variable. Chord's position is strong and stable rather than structurally widening.

## Risk Assessment

Risk Factors score 5.0, the lowest dimension, because the idiosyncratic risk set is unusually concentrated and includes two live binaries the market is actively pricing.

**Single-basin concentration.** Essentially all production is Williston. Any region-specific takeaway constraint, regulatory action, or weather event hits the whole portfolio simultaneously — Q1 already saw adverse weather and midstream constraints.

**Dakota Access Pipeline (DAPL).** The Army Corps completed its final EIS in December 2025 with a decision expected around Q1 2026, and three of five alternatives could require rerouting or removal. A DAPL shutdown would materially weaken Williston crude differentials until rail capacity adapts. We note the timeline tension the audit raised: with this report dated mid-2026, the decision is at or past its expected window and the stock held $134-150 through the post-Q1 period without a feared adverse outcome materializing. This cuts the risk weighting somewhat — the catalyst has not detonated and may be slipping — but the EIS process is not resolved, the adverse alternatives remain on the table, and Chord's own filings carry it as the basin's signature infrastructure risk. We treat it as an unresolved overhang rather than a dated event already behind us.

**MHA Nation riverbed litigation.** The Department of the Interior reversed a 2020 decision and now claims Missouri River riverbed minerals belong to the MHA Nation rather than North Dakota; the case is in active D.C. District Court litigation (joint status report ordered February 2026). An adverse outcome could affect royalty and ownership economics on tribal-land operations. This is a genuinely unusual, Chord-specific legal risk not captured by generic E&P screens.

**Commodity leverage and hedge drag.** Earnings are highly sensitive to WTI, and the hedge book caps upside in rising-price regimes while providing downside protection. This two-sidedness is generic to E&Ps, which is why we do not over-weight it, but it is the dominant earnings variable.

**Reserve replacement / depletion.** Rising depletion and the $3.0B PUD development requirement are the slow-burn risks to long-run FCF.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress that supports caution on incremental entry. Near-term ATM IV is 49.9% against 1-year historical volatility of 38.0% — rich by 12 points, in the 76th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 49.9% > 3-month 43.2%), which typically signals the market expects an imminent event, consistent with a near-term catalyst (potential DAPL-related newsflow, oil-price volatility around the June 16-17 FOMC, or geopolitical risk). The near-dated put/call skew is elevated at 3.6%, and put/call open interest rose to 0.75 from 0.61 over three days as the stock climbed, indicating hedging demand building into strength. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±5.3%. None of this overrides the fundamental picture, but it confirms that the cheapest entry is unlikely to be in the next week and that downside protection is being bid.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

Chord trades at single-digit forward earnings with a covered dividend and an 18%+ FCF yield at $80 oil. The 0.4x leverage, $2.2B liquidity, and clean maturity profile make it durable through a downturn, while $881M of buyback capacity provides a continuous bid at sensible prices. The XTO acquisition extends Williston inventory accretively, management raised oil guidance without raising capex, and the sell-side sees the current price as the floor with ~30% upside to a $173 mean. If oil holds near or above the current strip, 2026 FCF of $1.4-1.5B funds dividends, buybacks, and accretive M&A simultaneously. Bull case ~$200-224, in line with the top of the analyst range, achievable if WTI stays elevated and the DAPL/MHA overhangs resolve benignly.

### Bear Case

The current multiple flatters peak earnings; consensus itself models next-year EPS 27% below current-year. A reversion in WTI toward $70-75 compresses FCF and removes the premium embedded in the estimate-revision surge, and the hedge floors only partially cushion realized cash flow. A single-basin event — an adverse DAPL final decision widening Williston differentials, or an unfavorable MHA Nation ruling on royalty economics — hits the entire portfolio. Rising depletion and the $3.0B PUD requirement pressure long-run reinvestment economics. In a combined scenario ($80-85 oil plus an adverse basin-specific catalyst), the stock could revert to ~$100-105, roughly 22-25% below current. Bear case ~$100-110.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-leaning, constructive bias). Reassessment timeline: next earnings (early-to-mid Q3 2026), with interim monitoring of any DAPL decision and the MHA litigation docket.**

This is a growth-oriented hold: we see a credible 12-month appreciation path toward the ~$173 consensus mean if oil cooperates and the basin binaries resolve benignly, but the combination of commodity two-sidedness on what consensus flags as peak earnings, plus two live idiosyncratic legal/regulatory catalysts, keeps the risk/reward short of the threshold we require to add an incremental long in a sector with a poor recent track record of rewarding momentum entries.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A benign or final resolution of the DAPL decision that removes the differential overhang, combined with the stock holding above ~$130, which would compress the plausible bear-case drawdown below ~15%.
- Confirmation that 2027 inventory and reserve replacement support sustained production at maintenance capex (i.e., evidence the next-year EPS markdown is conservative rather than structural), shifting the durable-earnings picture.
- A pullback toward ~$110-115 on no fundamental deterioration, which would widen the upside-to-consensus enough to clear the bar.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse DAPL final decision forcing rerouting/removal and materially widening Williston crude differentials.
- An adverse MHA Nation ruling materially impairing royalty/ownership economics on tribal-land operations.
- WTI sustained below ~$60 alongside a leverage move above ~1.5x or a cut to the base dividend.

**Reassessment point:** Next quarterly earnings, plus any 8-K disclosure on DAPL or the MHA litigation, whichever comes first.

## Conclusion

Chord Energy is one of the higher-quality independent E&Ps available: low-cost, low-leverage, cash-generative, well-managed, and cheaply valued at ~8.5x next-year earnings with a 3.8% covered dividend and substantial buyback capacity. The constructive sell-side view — a low target at the current price and ~30% upside to the mean — is credible, and we explicitly reject any framing that treats the stock's prior rally or a recent oil-price narrative as a reason to be bearish.

The reason we land at HOLD rather than BUY is specific and not generic sector hedging. The large estimate revisions reflect a higher oil deck rather than durable operating inflection; consensus itself models a 27% earnings decline next year, so the multiple flatters cyclically elevated earnings; and the idiosyncratic risk set (DAPL, MHA Nation, single-basin concentration, rising depletion) is unusually concentrated, with the options market actively pricing near-term stress through a rich, backwardated structure. Existing holders should retain the position for its yield, capital-return profile, and balance-sheet quality. We would become buyers on a benign resolution of the DAPL overhang or a pullback toward the low-$110s that widens the margin of safety.
