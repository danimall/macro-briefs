## Executive Summary

Academy Sports & Outdoors is a 322-store sporting goods retailer concentrated in the southern U.S., trading at approximately 10.5x trailing earnings and 9.2x forward FY2026 consensus of $6.32. The valuation is cheap relative to the peer median P/E of 34.8x and reflects investor skepticism that is largely warranted: comparable sales declined 1.5% in FY2025 (transactions down 4.2%), the company has missed EPS estimates in 3 of its last 4 quarters, and FY2026 revenue guidance of ~$6.27B came in 3% below Street expectations, triggering an 11% selloff in March.

The balance sheet is adequate (Altman Z-Score 4.1, current ratio 1.89x, net debt ~$151M), and share repurchases have reduced the float by 19% over two years, partially insulating EPS from operating declines. However, the company has pulled forward $195M in inventory at pre-tariff rates, creating a 14.9% inventory-to-revenue mismatch that could require markdowns if consumer demand deteriorates further. Operating cash flow has fallen from $536M to $435M over two years, yet total shareholder returns ($234M) exceeded GAAP free cash flow ($222M) in FY2025.

The central question is whether ASO's depressed multiple already prices in the headwinds or whether further deterioration in consumer spending, tariff costs, and comp-store trends will compress earnings below the current $5.54 trailing EPS. With estimate revisions trending negative across all forward periods, 20% short interest, and the consumer discretionary sector facing record-low sentiment combined with elevated energy costs, the balance of risks does not support a conviction call in either direction.

## Company Overview

Academy Sports & Outdoors operates as a full-line sporting goods and outdoor recreation retailer across 322 stores in 21 contiguous states, headquartered in Katy, Texas. The company was founded in 1938 and went public in 2020 through a KKR-backed IPO at $13/share. Stores average ~70,000 gross square feet in off-mall power centers.

Revenue splits roughly into four segments: Outdoors (31%, including firearms at ~6% of total), Apparel (27%), Sports & Recreation (22%), and Footwear (20%). The merchandise strategy blends ~78% national brands (Nike, Under Armour, Columbia, Yeti) with ~22% private label across 19 proprietary brands, which drive higher gross margins.

Geographic concentration is pronounced: 117 of 322 stores (36%) are in Texas. The company is expanding into new markets in the mid-Atlantic and Midwest (Pennsylvania, Ohio, Maryland, Illinois), with 24 stores opened in FY2025 and 20-25 planned for FY2026. Three distribution centers can support approximately 450 stores, providing expansion capacity without major DC investment.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY2025 revenue of $6.05B grew 2.0% YoY, driven entirely by new store openings contributing $142.8M. Comparable sales declined 1.5%, with transactions falling 4.2% partially offset by a 2.9% increase in average ticket. The traffic deterioration is the more concerning metric, suggesting the value proposition is reaching fewer customers. Revenue has been declining from a COVID-era peak of approximately $6.77B.

**Profitability:** Operating margin compressed from 13.2% (FY2023) to 9.1% (FY2024) to 8.5% (FY2025). Gross margin improved 90 bps to 34.8% on favorable merchandise mix and private label penetration. The margin compression is entirely a function of SG&A deleverage: selling costs rose 8.2% ($120.6M), driven by $84.8M in new store costs and $13.1M in technology investment. Adjusted EBITDA has declined 21% over two years from $846M to $666M. Net income fell 10% to $377M.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $435M declined $93M YoY, pressured by lower net income and a $119M working capital drag from tariff-driven inventory pre-purchases. GAAP free cash flow was $222M against capex of $213M. The company returned $234M via buybacks ($199M) and dividends ($35M), meaning capital returns exceeded free cash flow generation, a pace sustainable only temporarily.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $481M ($400M in 6.00% senior secured notes + $85.8M variable-rate term loan at 7.72%), both maturing November 2027. Cash of $330M. Net debt approximately $151M. A $1.0B ABL facility ($992M available) provides ample liquidity. Debt-to-equity of 0.59x is manageable. The November 2027 maturity wall requires attention within the next 18 months but does not constitute near-term distress risk.

**Inventory:** The most critical near-term financial variable. Inventory grew to $1.50B (+14.9%) against only 2.0% revenue growth, creating a 12.9pp mismatch. Management attributes this to tariff pre-buying at favorable rates. If demand weakens or tariff assumptions prove incorrect, this excess inventory could require promotional markdowns that compress the 34.8% gross margin. Conversely, if competitors face tariff cost increases while ASO sells through pre-bought inventory, it could provide a temporary competitive advantage.

**Buybacks:** Share count has declined from 83.9M diluted (FY2023) to 68.0M diluted (FY2025), a 19% reduction. This has partially insulated EPS from operating declines: trailing EPS of $5.54 is only 17% below the FY2023 peak of $6.70 despite a 21% EBITDA decline. $436.6M remains authorized through December 2027.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is effectively negative. Comparable sales have been declining, and FY2026 guidance implies continued weakness. The growth story rests on two pillars:

**Store expansion:** 20-25 new stores planned for FY2026, with DC capacity supporting up to ~450 total stores (40%+ growth runway). New stores average ~$13M annual revenue. However, expansion into unfamiliar markets (PA, OH, MD, IL) carries execution risk, and pre-opening expenses ($14.6M in FY2025) plus lease obligations (15-20 year terms, $2.37B total future lease payments) create fixed-cost commitments regardless of store performance.

**E-commerce:** Online represented 11.7% of merchandise sales in FY2025, growing 13.6% YoY. The myAcademy Rewards loyalty program (launched July 2024) provides first-party data for personalization. This channel is growing but remains a small portion of the total.

**Estimate trajectory:** Consensus estimates are falling across all forward periods. FY2026 EPS has been revised down from $6.48 to $6.32 over 90 days (-2.5%). FY2027 EPS has been cut from $7.11 to $6.93 (-2.5%). The 30-day revisions are less negative than 90-day revisions for the current quarter (+1.6% vs. +3.2%), but next quarter and full-year revisions continue trending lower. This negative momentum typically predicts further disappointment rather than stabilization.

**Earnings track record:** 1 beat and 3 misses in the last 4 quarters. The misses have been meaningful: -5.5%, -10.9%, and -16.0%. This pattern indicates deteriorating management visibility into demand and undermines confidence in forward guidance.

## Valuation Assessment

ASO trades at ~10.5x trailing EPS ($5.54), ~9.2x FY2026E consensus ($6.32), and approximately 5.6x EV/EBITDA ($666M adjusted EBITDA, EV ~$3.9B). The forward P/E sits at a steep discount to the specialty retail peer median of 34.8x.

The discount is partially justified by the declining trajectory. Peer Five Below trades at 34.8x with higher growth expectations. SBH at 7.9x is the closest comp by valuation but carries its own structural challenges. The more relevant comparison is Dick's Sporting Goods (DKS), which trades at approximately 15-17x forward earnings with stronger comps and more premium positioning.

On a FCF yield basis, $222M GAAP FCF on a $3.7B market cap yields approximately 6.0%. This is attractive for a retailer with a clean balance sheet and share reduction program, but the FCF trajectory is declining ($330M adjusted → $222M GAAP), and the inventory build could temporarily suppress FCF further.

The analyst consensus price target ranges from $50 to $70, with a median of $60 and a mean of $60.17 against the current price of ~$58. The tight range and proximity to current price suggest limited conviction in either direction from the Street. 8 Buy ratings versus 10 Holds, with no Sells — a lukewarm consensus.

The valuation provides genuine downside support. At the current share count, a $50 price implies ~9x trailing earnings and ~5x EV/EBITDA, levels that would likely attract acquirer interest given the company's real estate and brand value. But upside requires earnings stabilization or growth, and the data does not yet support that.

## Competitive Landscape

ASO occupies a distinct niche: value-oriented sporting goods for middle-income families, primarily in the southern U.S. This differentiates it from Dick's Sporting Goods (more premium, national footprint, ~3x ASO's revenue) and mass merchants (Walmart/Target offering commodity sporting goods without specialization). The "good/better/best" pricing strategy serves a broad customer base.

Competitive advantages include: (1) regional density in the Southeast and Texas creating distribution efficiencies and brand awareness; (2) 22% private label penetration providing margin cushion and product differentiation; (3) value positioning that may prove relatively resilient in a consumer downturn versus premium-oriented competitors; (4) DC capacity for 40%+ store expansion without major infrastructure investment.

Competitive risks include: (1) major brands (Nike, Under Armour) expanding direct-to-consumer channels, potentially reducing ASO's traffic advantage; (2) competitors becoming "increasingly promotional" per the 10-K, pressuring margins; (3) limited geographic diversification compared to nationally distributed peers; (4) organized retail crime driving elevated shrinkage that ASO continues to flag as above historical levels.

## Risk Assessment

**Consumer spending deterioration** is the dominant risk. Consumer sentiment at 47.6, elevated gasoline prices, and accelerating energy costs are directly compressing spending capacity for ASO's core middle-income customer. The 4.2% transaction decline in FY2025 may accelerate as consumers cut discretionary entertainment and dining expenditures. ASO's product categories (sporting goods, outdoor recreation) are discretionary and deferrable.

**Tariff exposure** is acute. A significant portion of ASO's merchandise originates from China, Vietnam, Bangladesh, and Cambodia. The pre-buy inventory provides temporary cost insulation but creates markdown risk if demand softens. FY2026 guidance already incorporates tariff assumptions, and any escalation beyond current levels could compress margins further.

**Negative comp-store sales** continuing into FY2026 while the company accelerates store openings creates SG&A deleverage risk. Fixed lease commitments (15-20 year terms) mean underperforming stores cannot be easily unwound. The company has never closed a store.

**Refinancing risk** is moderate. $485.8M in debt matures November 2027. Refinancing in a potentially higher-rate environment could add $10-15M+ in annual interest expense.

**Geographic concentration** in Texas (36% of stores) creates weather, economic, and regulatory risk. A Texas-specific downturn would disproportionately impact results.

The term structure is in backwardation (47.7% near-term vs. 41.1% at 6 months), indicating the market sees imminent stress, likely related to post-earnings digestion and tariff uncertainty. This diverges from the 10.5x trailing multiple, which might appear to offer value; the options market suggests fundamental risks are not yet fully priced.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($68-72)
- Tariff pre-buy provides 2-3 quarter cost advantage over competitors, enabling stable or expanding gross margins while peers absorb higher costs
- New store openings drive revenue growth that offsets comp declines, with stores ramping to maturity over 3-5 years
- Share buybacks continue at current pace, reducing diluted shares toward 60M by FY2027 and boosting EPS to $7+ even on flat operating income
- Consumer spending stabilizes in H2 2026, comps turn positive, triggering a re-rating from 10x to 12-13x forward earnings
- At 12x FY2027E of $6.93 = $83, implying 43% upside; a more modest 11x FY2026E of $6.32 = ~$70

### Bear Case ($38-42)
- Consumer spending accelerates downward through 2026 as energy costs and inflation erode purchasing power; comps decline 3-5%
- Pre-buy inventory requires markdowns to clear, compressing gross margins 100-200 bps
- New store productivity disappoints in unfamiliar Midwest/mid-Atlantic markets, dragging SG&A higher
- Estimate revisions continue lower, with FY2026 EPS falling toward $5.50-5.75, and the multiple compresses to 7-8x on deteriorating fundamentals
- At 7.5x $5.75 = $43, implying ~26% downside from current levels

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Comparable sales turn positive for a single quarter, demonstrating traffic stabilization
2. FY2026 EPS estimates begin trending upward (30-day revision turning positive)
3. Inventory-to-revenue growth gap narrows below 5pp, indicating successful sell-through of pre-buy inventory

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Comparable sales decline accelerates beyond -3% in any quarter
2. Gross margin falls below 33% (indicating markdown activity to clear inventory)
3. Operating cash flow falls below $350M annualized, making the current buyback + dividend program unsustainable

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q1 FY2026, likely June 2026). This will provide the first read on FY2026 demand trends, tariff cost impacts, and inventory sell-through. A miss combined with guidance reduction would warrant downgrade.

## Conclusion

Academy Sports trades at a depressed valuation — 10.5x earnings, 5.6x EBITDA, 6% FCF yield — for a profitable retailer with a clean balance sheet and active buyback program. The discount reflects real problems: three earnings misses in four quarters, negative comp traffic, bloated inventory, uncertain tariff exposure, and a deteriorating consumer spending environment.

The stock has rallied 55% from its April 2025 lows near $36, meaning much of the "cheap on numbers" thesis has already been partially captured. At $58, the stock sits 7% below the 52-week high and 3% below the analyst median target of $60. The risk/reward from here is roughly symmetric: the bull case requires earnings stabilization that forward estimates do not yet support, while the bear case requires further consumer deterioration that may already be partially reflected in the low multiple.

With 20% short interest, negative estimate revisions, and an options market pricing near-term stress in backwardation, the prudent approach is to wait for evidence of fundamental stabilization before taking a directional position. HOLD, with the next earnings release as the primary reassessment point.
