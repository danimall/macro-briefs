## Executive Summary

S&T Bancorp is a $9.9 billion-asset community bank headquartered in Indiana, Pennsylvania, operating 72 branches across Pennsylvania and Ohio. The bank delivered Q1 2026 EPS of $0.94 (beating estimates by 6.7%), expanded its NIM to 3.90%, and set a record for customer deposit inflows exceeding $8 billion. Capital levels are strong (CET1 14.32%), and the bank is returning capital aggressively — approximately $50M in buybacks in Q1 2026 alone, annualizing to ~12% of market cap.

Three specific headwinds prevent a stronger recommendation: (1) the stock is trading within 1.5% of its 52-week high and essentially at the analyst consensus median target of $45, leaving minimal upside priced in; (2) imminent crossing of the $10 billion asset threshold will trigger Durbin Amendment interchange fee caps (typically 30-40% reduction in card interchange revenue) and materially higher compliance costs; and (3) nonaccrual loans doubled in Q4 2025 to $55.6M while the allowance coverage ratio fell from 363% to 168%, with CRE concentration at 49.6% of loans — a level that warrants regulatory attention during any downturn in commercial real estate. The current valuation does not discount these quantifiable, near-term headwinds.

## Company Overview

S&T Bancorp operates as a full-service commercial and consumer bank with a total loan portfolio of $8.1 billion, split approximately 68.5% commercial (CRE $3.6B, C&I $1.5B, construction $380M) and 31.5% consumer (primarily residential real estate at $2.5B). The bank also manages $2.1 billion in wealth management assets. Revenue is driven primarily by net interest income ($350M in FY2025), supplemented by noninterest income from debit/credit card fees ($18.3M), deposit service charges ($16.4M), and wealth management fees ($12.4M).

The bank competes in a high-density financial institution market in western Pennsylvania and eastern Ohio, differentiating through local decision-making, relationship banking, and personalized service. It currently benefits from being below the $10 billion Durbin Amendment threshold, which provides a fee revenue advantage relative to larger competitors — an advantage that will disappear imminently.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability:** FY2025 net income was $134.2M ($3.49 diluted EPS), with ROA of 1.38% and ROTCE of 12.62%. These returns are above-average for a community bank of this size, though they have compressed from the 2023 peak (ROA 1.56%, ROTCE 17.15%) as deposit costs normalized.

**Net Interest Margin:** NIM expanded 8 bps to 3.90% in FY2025 as liability costs fell 30 bps (to 2.79%) faster than asset yields declined 13 bps (to 5.74%). The bank holds $500M in SOFR receive-fixed swaps maturing over 3-5 years, providing downside protection in rate-cut scenarios while 60% variable-rate loan exposure benefits from higher-for-longer rates.

**Deposit Franchise:** Noninterest-bearing deposits represent 27.2% of total deposits — a strong franchise indicator. Customer deposits grew 2.9% in FY2025 while brokered deposits fell 19.9%, demonstrating genuine deposit gathering ability versus purchased funding. Q1 2026 produced record inflows above $8B.

**Capital:** CET1 at 14.32% is approximately 780 bps above the well-capitalized minimum, providing substantial buffer for organic growth, M&A, and continued aggressive capital return. The bank returned $89.6M in FY2025 through dividends ($53M) and buybacks ($36.6M), accelerating buybacks dramatically to ~$50M in Q1 2026 alone.

**Credit Quality — Deteriorating:** Nonaccrual loans doubled to $55.6M (0.69% of total loans) in Q4 2025, driven by three commercial relationships totaling $25.3M. Net charge-offs rose to 0.18% from 0.11%. The allowance for credit losses declined to 1.15% from 1.31%, reducing coverage of nonaccruals from 363% to 168%. This compression in coverage during a period of rising problem loans is a yellow flag.

## Growth Analysis

S&T's growth profile is typical of a mature community bank: low-single-digit organic loan growth (4.3% in FY2025), modest NII expansion driven by margin management rather than balance sheet growth, and EPS supported by share count reduction rather than top-line acceleration.

Consensus estimates project FY2026 EPS of $3.73 (+6.9% YoY) and FY2027 EPS of $3.82 (+2.4%). The 90-day estimate revision for FY2026 is +2.9% — modestly positive but well below the magnitude that signals a fundamental re-rating. Loan growth guidance for Q2 2026 is "low single-digit," consistent with the mature franchise profile.

The primary growth optionality is M&A. Management has explicitly stated "M&A remains on the table," and the logic is compelling: acquiring a $2-3B bank would jump the $10B threshold meaningfully, spreading the incremental regulatory cost over a larger revenue base. Bylaws amendments in early 2026 could be structural preparation. However, no target has been identified publicly, and bank M&A is inherently uncertain.

## Valuation Assessment

At $45.34 per share, STBA trades at:
- 13.0x trailing P/E (FY2025 EPS $3.49)
- 12.1x forward P/E (FY2026E EPS $3.73)
- ~1.56x tangible book value
- 3.3% dividend yield

Relative to peers: the peer median P/E is 14.0x, peer median yield is 2.8%. STBA's 12-13x multiple is modestly below the peer median, but its higher dividend yield and stronger capital return offset this on a total return basis.

The critical valuation issue is proximity to targets. The consensus analyst median target is $45.00 — the stock trades at $45.34, already above it. The high target is $48.00, implying just 5.9% upside. With 6 of 7 analysts rating the stock HOLD, the sell-side does not see meaningful appreciation potential from current levels. Given that estimate revisions are only +2-3% (not the 30%+ magnitude that would make consensus targets stale), this ceiling is meaningful.

At 12.1x forward earnings, the stock is not expensive. But whether it deserves a premium multiple given the approaching $10B headwinds is doubtful. If Durbin reduces interchange revenue by $5-7M annually (a reasonable estimate for a bank this size), that represents ~$0.13-0.18 in EPS, or roughly 4-5% of earnings. The stock would need to trade at 11.5x forward earnings to incorporate this hit, which at $3.73 EPS implies $43 — approximately 5% below current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

S&T's competitive position is stable. The 27% noninterest-bearing deposit mix, above-average returns, and 5-year TSR outperformance (191.7% vs peer group 149.2%) demonstrate a well-managed franchise. The bank's relationship-banking model protects against deposit flight better than rate-sensitive institutions.

However, crossing $10B removes the interchange fee advantage versus larger competitors. Additionally, the CRE concentration at 49.6% of loans exceeds the interagency guidance threshold for heightened supervisory attention, which could constrain growth optionality if regulators impose informal limits.

The wealth management platform ($2.1B AUA) provides fee diversification that many sub-$10B banks lack, but it contributes only ~$12.4M in annual revenue — a small complement to $350M in NII.

## Risk Assessment

**1. $10 Billion Threshold (Specific, Near-Term, Quantifiable):** At $9.9B in assets, crossing is imminent. Durbin Amendment interchange caps will reduce card fee revenue by an estimated $5-7M annually. CFPB supervision adds compliance costs. Management has flagged "substantial costs" for system upgrades and specialized personnel. Combined EPS impact: $0.15-0.25 on an annualized basis.

**2. CRE Credit Quality Deterioration (Specific, Developing):** Three commercial relationships totaling $25.3M went nonaccrual in Q4 2025. CRE charge-offs doubled to 0.21%. In the current environment of CRE refinancing stress, a 49.6% CRE concentration is a genuine vulnerability. If charge-offs normalize to 0.30-0.40% (still below historical stress levels), the EPS impact would be $0.20-0.35.

**3. Loan Growth Stagnation:** Q1 2026 showed a decline in loan balances despite the Q4 2025 uptick. If the aggressive buyback program reflects a lack of lending opportunities rather than pure confidence, the growth trajectory flattens further.

**4. Tariff Exposure (New Risk Factor):** The 2025 10-K added new language about tariff impacts on commercial borrowers. Western PA's manufacturing and industrial base could face credit stress from trade disruption, particularly in the C&I portfolio.

**5. Geographic Concentration:** Single-region exposure to western PA/eastern OH magnifies any regional economic downturn.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($48-52):**
- NIM continues expanding as liability costs fall further; Q1 2026 beat extrapolates through the year
- M&A announcement of a $2-3B bank creates value through scale and threshold-jumping
- Credit quality stabilizes (Q4 nonaccruals prove idiosyncratic, not systemic)
- Buybacks reduce share count by 5%+ annually, mechanically lifting EPS
- Forward P/E re-rates to peer median of 14x on $3.73 EPS = $52

**Bear Case ($38-40):**
- CRE credit deterioration accelerates; 2-3 additional relationships go nonaccrual, requiring meaningful provision expense
- $10B threshold crossed without M&A, absorbing the full regulatory cost without revenue diversification
- Loan demand remains weak, NIM compresses as rate cuts proceed, and fee income declines from Durbin
- Regional economic weakness in PA/OH from tariff impacts
- Forward P/E compresses to 10.5x on $3.73 EPS = $39

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back below $41 (11x forward EPS of $3.73), providing 15%+ upside to fair value
2. Announcement of a specific M&A target that would meaningfully jump the $10B threshold with accretive terms
3. Q2/Q3 2026 earnings demonstrate loan growth reacceleration AND credit quality stabilization (nonaccruals declining below $40M)

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Nonaccrual loans exceed $80M (1% of loans), or a major CRE loss event materializes
2. Organic asset growth crosses $10B without a corresponding M&A strategy, triggering immediate Durbin impact on an undiversified base
3. NIM compresses below 3.60% as rate environment shifts against the bank's positioning

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings (expected late July 2026). Q2 results will reveal whether loan growth guidance is achievable and whether Q4/Q1 credit issues were isolated or trend-forming.

## Conclusion

S&T Bancorp is a well-run community bank with above-average returns, a strong deposit franchise, and aggressive capital return. The ~3.3% dividend yield supported by 45% payout ratio and meaningful buybacks provides an attractive income profile. However, at the current price — essentially at the 52-week high and above the analyst consensus target — the stock offers limited near-term appreciation potential. The specific headwinds of Durbin Amendment impact ($0.15-0.25 EPS), CRE credit deterioration (nonaccruals doubling with coverage falling), and uncertain loan growth trajectory prevent an upgrade to BUY. The stock is worth holding for its income characteristics and M&A optionality, but the risk/reward at $45.34 is approximately symmetric, with identifiable downside catalysts that the market has not yet priced.

The specific, falsifiable reason this is not a BUY despite the 6.25 overall score: the stock is priced at $45.34 versus a consensus median target of $45.00 and a high target of $48.00. With only +2.9% estimate revisions over 90 days (insufficient to make targets stale), the 5.9% maximum upside to the high target does not compensate for the quantifiable $10B threshold headwind ($0.15-0.25 EPS) or the doubling of nonaccrual loans in Q4 2025. A 5-6% upside versus 10-15% identifiable downside in the bear case is not BUY risk/reward.
