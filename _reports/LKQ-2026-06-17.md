**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

LKQ Corporation is the largest global distributor of alternative (non-OEM) vehicle parts, now organized into three segments — North America, Europe, and Specialty — following the September 2025 divestiture of its Self Service business. The investment case has shifted from a steady-state distributor story to an event-driven special situation. As of January 26, 2026, the board is running a full strategic-alternatives review that explicitly includes "a potential sale of all or a portion of the Company," an outgrowth of a cooperation agreement signed with activists Ancora Catalyst and Engine Capital in early 2025. The Specialty segment is separately in an active sale process. With a ~$6.4B equity market capitalization against ~$13.7B of revenue and ~$847M of free cash flow, the valuation debate now hinges more on the outcome of that review than on near-term operating results.

The operating business is in multi-year decline. Net income has nearly halved since 2022 ($1.15B → $608M); revenue fell ~5% in 2025 with organic parts-and-services revenue down 2.7%; operating margin compressed to 7.3% from 8.3%; and Q1 2026 showed continued organic softness (-1.6% consolidated, Europe -4.0%) with net income falling to $79M from $169M. The headwinds are partly structural (OEM encroachment via telematics, design patents and certified-shop programs; the EV transition shrinking the mechanical-parts pool; declining repairable-claims frequency from ADAS) and partly cyclical, with the European aftermarket under competitive and economic stress (evidenced by the $44M Mekonomen impairment).

The countervailing force is valuation and optionality. At ~8.4x forward consensus EPS ($2.97) and a ~13% FCF yield, after a 31% drawdown from the 52-week high, much of the bearish operating case is in the price. All nine covering analysts sit above the current quote, with the most bearish target ($33) 26% above spot. The company is investment-grade with comfortable covenant headroom (2.6x leverage vs 4.0x max, 7.4x interest coverage vs 3.0x min) and generates real cash. The setup is a structurally declining operating business carrying a live break-up/take-out option with meaningful downside protection from the FCF base, where the positive and negative cases roughly cancel. We rate the shares HOLD.

## Company Overview

LKQ distributes alternative vehicle parts (aftermarket, salvage/recycled, and remanufactured collision and mechanical parts) plus specialty vehicle accessories. The three reportable segments are:

- **North America** (FY25 revenue $5.65B, 41% of total): The leading alternative collision-parts and paint/body/equipment (PBE) distributor in the U.S. and Canada, operating Keystone and Bumper to Bumper. Highest-margin segment, but FY25 Segment EBITDA margin fell to 14.4% from 16.3%. The North America aftermarket sources roughly 44% of purchases from its top six vendors and carries heavy Taiwan dependence.
- **Europe** (FY25 revenue $6.31B, 46%): The broadest aftermarket hard-parts footprint across ~20 countries (Euro Car Parts, Rhiag, Stahlgruber), with 900,000+ SKUs. EBITDA margin 9.3% (down from 9.9%). A multi-year ERP consolidation is underway.
- **Specialty** (FY25 revenue $1.69B, 12%): RV, towing, marine and performance accessories (Fabtech, Warn). Held for potential sale; took a $52M goodwill impairment in FY25.

The company's moat rests on its scale distribution network, inventory breadth and availability, and relationships with insurers who steer collision-repair part selection. It competes against OEMs, who control a majority of collision parts by dollar and are actively restricting alternative-parts usage, and against fragmented local, regional, and internet distributors.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year trajectory shows steady erosion of a still-cash-generative core:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 12.79 | 13.87 | 14.36* | 13.65 |
| Operating income ($M) | 1,581 | 1,357 | 1,199 | 993 |
| Pretax income ($M) | 1,518 | 1,235 | 958 | 800 |
| Net income ($M) | 1,150 | 938 | 693 | 608 |

\*As originally reported; FY25 10-K restates to $13.82B for discontinued-operations presentation.

The 2025 revenue decline was driven by organic parts-and-services revenue down 2.7% — North America -2.3% (weak PBE and repairable claims, competition) and Europe -4.3% (heightened competition, weak economy). FX (+1.7%) partly offset the decline. Margins compressed across the board: gross margin 38.6% (from 38.9%), operating margin 7.3%, and North America Segment EBITDA margin down 190bps in a single year. Q1 2026 extended the trend — North America EBITDA margin 14.1%, Europe 7.8% — with net income hit by the $44M Mekonomen impairment.

Free cash flow is the bright spot but carries quality caveats. FY25 FCF was $847M (6.2% margin), aided by lower capex and an inventory drawdown. Operating cash flow declined YoY ($1,063M vs $1,121M), and FCF is partly supported by supply-chain financing ($457M payables outstanding at Q1'26) and payment-term extensions. Q1 is seasonally FCF-negative (-$96M).

The balance sheet is adequate but levered. Total debt was $3.9B at Q1'26, D/E 2.32, weighted-average interest rate 5.0%. Goodwill of $5.4B is ~36% of assets, contributing to an Altman Z of 2.15 (grey zone). Covenant headroom is comfortable, and the investment-grade rating is intact. A $500M term loan matures January 2027 and will be refinanced.

Capital allocation has pivoted to deleveraging and caution. The $0.30/quarter dividend (~$310M/yr) is well-covered. Buybacks were cut to $159M in 2025 (from $360M) and nearly halted in Q1'26 ($1M). Self Service sale proceeds ($410M EV) went to debt paydown, with ~$708M net debt repaid in 2025.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth prospects are weak, and this is the dimension where the bearish case is clearest. Organic revenue is contracting in both major segments, and the negative drivers are partly secular: declining repairable-claims frequency as ADAS reduces accidents and insurers total more vehicles; OEM efforts to claw back collision-parts share; and the EV transition shrinking the mechanical-parts SKU pool (engines and transmissions are among LKQ's largest North America categories). The growth-by-acquisition model that built the company has reversed into divestiture and balance-sheet simplification.

The "accelerating deterioration" framing overstates the trend. Estimate revisions are decelerating on the forward year: next-year EPS ($3.32) is +0.5% over 30 days and only -5.3% over 90 days, versus current-year -8.2% over 90 days. Q3 2025 delivered a +10.1% beat, and management reaffirmed full-year guidance on the Q1 2026 call while citing sequential improvement in North America. The earnings momentum is negative but stabilizing at the forward horizon rather than collapsing. We do not see the +30% upward estimate-revision signal anywhere in the data — the opposite, in fact — which keeps Growth Prospects firmly below-average at 3.5.

## Valuation Assessment

LKQ trades at ~8.4x forward consensus EPS ($2.97), versus a Distributors peer median P/E of 40.7. The peer set is heterogeneous and largely unhelpful: GPC's 238x reflects depressed trough earnings, ARMK at 40.7x is a services business, and POOL at 17.6x is the most comparable but operates in a different end market. The cleaner read is absolute: a ~13% FCF yield and a sub-9x forward multiple on a business that, while declining, still generates real cash.

The key valuation anchor is the strategic review. Self Service was monetized at $410M EV; Specialty is in an active sale process; and the board is reviewing a full sale. The ~$6.4B equity cap against revenue and FCF frames a deleveraged break-up or take-out scenario that, on standard distribution multiples, would clear well above the current quote. All nine analysts sit above $26.23, with the low target at $33 (+26%) and the high at $47.5. We do not treat consensus targets as floors here, given the falling estimates, but their unanimity above spot is consistent with the market pricing the operating decline rather than overpricing it.

We do not find an asymmetric long either. The forward multiple is cheap, but the calibration evidence is unambiguous that a low multiple alone is not a buy signal — it frequently marks a value trap absent an independent catalyst. The catalyst here (the strategic review) is real but binary and unscheduled, and a no-deal outcome leaves a structurally declining business compounding downward. The cheapness and the optionality offset the operating risk rather than overwhelming it, supporting a Valuation score of 5.5 rather than anything stronger.

## Competitive Landscape

LKQ's structural advantages — its salvage network, inventory breadth, insurer relationships and distribution scale — remain genuine. But the competitive trajectory is deteriorating, which is what the score measures. OEMs are intensifying efforts to restrict alternative-parts usage through telematics data lockouts, design patents, embedded software that prevents part recycling, and certified-shop programs; the FY25 10-K notes the "frequency and intensity… increasing over time." In Europe, competitive pricing pressure drove organic revenue down 4.3%, and the Mekonomen distress (rising leverage, dividend suspension) reads through to broader aftermarket weakness. In North America, PBE faces competition compounding the structural claims-frequency decline. The Specialty goodwill cushion sits at just 11% above carrying value. Against a market leader losing share at the margin, we score Competitive Position 4.5.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by company-specific, partly event-driven factors:

1. **Strategic-review uncertainty (new risk factor).** The review may yield no transaction, divert management, incur substantial costs, risk employee/customer attrition, and trigger change-of-control redemption of senior notes at a premium. The 10-K flags stock and bond prices as vulnerable to speculation. This is a two-sided risk — the dominant upside catalyst and a source of downside if it ends in nothing.

2. **Uni-Select securities class action (April 2026, subsequent event).** A fresh litigation overhang alleging materially false statements about the Uni-Select/FinishMaster acquisition and integration, connecting directly to the business line now showing organic declines and margin dilution.

3. **Secular operating headwinds.** OEM encroachment, EV transition, and declining repairable claims are slow but persistent pressures on the core revenue pool.

4. **Concentration and tariff exposure.** North America aftermarket is heavily Taiwan-dependent (top six vendors = 44% of purchases); 2025 tariffs forced price increases that were dilutive to margins.

5. **Goodwill impairment risk.** $5.4B goodwill, with Specialty's cushion at 11% ahead of a contemplated sale.

6. **Earnings quality.** FY25 FCF was flattered by inventory drawdown, lower capex, and growing supply-chain financing reliance; underlying operating cash flow declined YoY.

The mitigants are the IG rating, comfortable covenant headroom, and a real FCF base. These risks are largely disclosed and partly priced, which keeps the score at a below-average but not distressed 4.5.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term event risk. Near-term ATM IV is 78.2% against 36.3% one-year historical vol (a 41.9-point spread, 82nd percentile), with a sharply backwardated term structure (78.2% near-term vs 43.4% long-dated) signaling the market expects imminent resolution, consistent with strategic-review headline risk. The P/C OI ratio has risen from 0.78 to 1.80 over the past month. Near-term skew is heavily call-tilted (-183.3%), which is unusual for a name with falling estimates and points to hedged or speculative positioning around a possible deal outcome rather than a clean directional bearish bet. The implied move on the nearest expiry is only ±3.3%, but the elevated IV across the chain means option-implied uncertainty is high. We read this as confirmation that the equity is now trading as an event, not a fundamental, and that any new position should respect the binary nature of the catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The strategic review produces a transaction — a full sale or a sum-of-the-parts break-up — at a premium to the equity cap. Self Service and the Specialty process establish that the parts are saleable. A take-out at ~7-8x EBITDA on a deleveraged basis, or a strategic acquirer paying for the distribution network and insurer relationships, supports a $38-42 equity value (+45-60%). Even absent a deal, the FCF yield with stabilizing forward estimates and reaffirmed guidance offers downside support, with continued debt paydown gradually transferring value to equity. Activist board representation keeps the pressure on for shareholder-friendly outcomes.

### Bear Case
The review ends with no transaction, management attention having been diverted and deal costs incurred. The operating decline continues — organic revenue keeps contracting, OEM share-claw and EV/claims headwinds compound, European pricing pressure persists, and a Specialty goodwill impairment lands. The Uni-Select litigation escalates. Estimates resume their downward drift, the multiple stays at ~9x or de-rates further on a shrinking earnings base, and the stock drifts to $20-21 (-20%). The dividend is safe but the equity becomes a value trap on a structurally declining business.

The probability-weighted asymmetry is roughly balanced-to-mildly-positive: the dominant near-term catalyst skews favorable, but the no-deal path leaves a genuinely impaired operating story. Neither case dominates with enough conviction to support a directional rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD on uncertainty — the positive (cheap valuation, live break-up option, FCF support) and negative (secular operating decline, no-deal risk, litigation) cases roughly offset, and we lack sufficient evidence for a confident directional call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A confirmed transaction (full sale, Specialty sale on attractive terms, or take-out offer) at an implied equity value above ~$33-35.
- Two consecutive quarters of stabilizing-to-positive organic revenue in North America alongside positive forward EPS revisions, demonstrating the operating decline has bottomed independent of a deal.
- Clustered open-market insider buying — currently absent, with Form 4 activity limited to routine grants and tax-withholding sales.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The strategic review concludes with no transaction AND organic revenue declines accelerate beyond ~-4% consolidated.
- A material adverse development in the Uni-Select securities litigation, or a Specialty goodwill impairment that signals broader writedown risk against the $5.4B goodwill balance.
- A dividend cut or covenant pressure indicating the FCF floor is eroding.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release and any strategic-review update; the January 2027 term-loan refinancing is a secondary date to monitor for credit-market read-through.

## Conclusion

LKQ presents a balanced risk/reward at $26.23. The operating business is in genuine, partly structural decline, and after a 31% drawdown the shares trade at a level where the operating deterioration is largely in the price, with all nine analysts above spot. Overlaying this is a live, activist-driven strategic review with a credible break-up/take-out option, an IG balance sheet with comfortable covenant headroom, and forward estimates that are stabilizing rather than collapsing.

The bearish operating thesis and the bullish event/valuation thesis offset, leaving no asymmetric edge in either direction. The Consumer Discretionary sector context argues against any BUY conviction, while the FCF floor and the favorable-skewing strategic catalyst argue against an AVOID. We rate LKQ **HOLD (uncertain)**, with the strategic-review outcome the single most important variable to monitor and a clear set of upgrade and downgrade triggers to act on as that event resolves.
