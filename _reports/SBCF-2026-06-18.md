**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Seacoast Banking Corporation has remade itself over the second half of 2025. Two acquisitions, Villages Bancorporation ($829M, closed October 1) and Heartland Bancshares ($111M, closed July 11), added roughly $5.3B of assets and $4.2B of deposits, lifting total assets ~37% YoY to $20.8B at YE2025 (and $21.1B at Q1 2026). The defining feature of the VBI deal is its funding profile: a deposit-rich, under-loaned franchise in The Villages retirement community that hands Seacoast cheap, sticky retiree deposits to redeploy into loans. That funding advantage shows in cost of deposits of 1.54% and an FTE NIM that expanded to 3.83% in Q1 2026 from 3.66%, with adjusted ROTE reaching 16.3%.

The reported Q1 numbers obscure this. GAAP EPS of $0.29 was depressed entirely by a deliberate $39.5M pre-tax loss on a January securities repositioning, in which roughly $277M of bonds yielding 1.9% were sold and reinvested at ~4.8%. Strip that out and adjusted EPS was $0.62, up 42% sequentially and 111% YoY, with adjusted ROA of 1.31% and an adjusted efficiency ratio of 55.3%. The underlying earnings power is inflecting sharply higher post-acquisition, and management's 2026 guidance (adjusted EPS of $2.48–2.52 and adjusted ROTE near 16% by Q4) frames a clean normalization story.

The counterweight is credit. Nonaccrual loans rose 32% sequentially to $95.0M, NPAs climbed 30% to $99.3M (0.47% of assets), and the NPL ratio rose to 0.75% from 0.57%, yet the Q1 provision was only $0.8M and ACL coverage slipped to 1.39% from 1.42%. Rising problem loans against minimal reserving is the single most important thread to monitor. Paired with ~50% CRE concentration, a Florida franchise that historically endures deeper real-estate downturns than the national average, and an unfinished VBI system conversion (expected Q3 2026), the risk profile is meaningfully two-sided. At ~12x forward adjusted EPS, below the regional-bank peer median, the stock is fairly valued for an earnings story that still needs to prove its credit assumptions. We rate SBCF a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Seacoast is one of the largest community/regional banks headquartered in Florida, operating through Seacoast National Bank. As of Q1 2026 it carried $21.1B in total assets, $16.6B in deposits, and 104 full-service branches concentrated in Florida with a small Georgia presence. It reports as a single segment spanning commercial and consumer banking, wealth management ($2.8B AUM, +33% YoY), mortgage, and insurance.

The business model combines organic expansion with serial M&A. Seacoast recruits bankers from larger regionals and acquires smaller Florida institutions, positioning itself as a consolidator of choice in a fragmented state market. Its operating edge rests on a granular, low-cost core deposit base (87% of deposits sit in savings, money-market, and demand accounts) and small average loan sizes ($439K overall, $951K commercial), reflecting a relationship-banking orientation rather than concentrated large credits. That granularity is genuinely valuable: deposit cost discipline is what drives the NIM expansion that anchors the bull case.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 results.** Net income was $144.9M (+20% YoY) on diluted EPS of $1.57 (adjusted $1.84). Net interest income grew 28% to $553.5M and FTE NIM expanded 34bps to 3.58%. The provision rose to $51.3M from $16.3M in 2024, but roughly $24.6M of that was day-one CECL acquisition provisioning tied to the deals rather than recurring credit cost. Reported ROA was 0.84% (adjusted 0.98%) and ROTE 10.58% (adjusted 12.16%).

**Q1 2026.** Reported net income of $31.9M ($0.29 diluted) was held down by the securities loss. Adjusted net income was $67.8M. NIM expansion was driven by acquired-loan accretion (26bps, $12.1M), higher securities yields, and falling deposit costs. Organic deposits grew 7% annualized.

**Capital and liquidity are strong.** Tier 1 capital was 14.6%, total risk-based capital 16.0%, CET1 11.66%, and TCE/TA 9.24%. The bank held $808M cash plus $9.1B of available borrowing capacity against uninsured deposits of ~37% (31% net of collateralized public funds), implying liquidity coverage of roughly 160–184% of uninsured deposits. Seacoast repurchased ~$10M of stock in Q1 and another ~$10M post-quarter, with $140M remaining on a $150M authorization through December 2026.

**Earnings quality and the accretion question.** Two cautions sit inside the headline figures. First, 26bps of the 3.83% NIM is purchase-accounting accretion that runs off over time; core NIM ex-accretion is 3.57%, so the headline margin overstates durable run-rate. Second, the firm carries an AOCI loss of ~$78.6M, AFS unrealized losses of $131M, and an HTM book with a ~$98M unrealized loss (fair value $478M vs. $576M amortized cost). The January repositioning addressed part of the AFS drag, but the rate-sensitivity of equity remains live: EVE is liability-sensitive (-17.4% in a +300bp shock), even as NII is modestly asset-sensitive over a 13–24 month horizon.

The earnings-surprise record supports moderate confidence in the adjusted trajectory: three beats and one miss over four quarters, with the Q4 2025 miss (-13.5%) the exception in an otherwise positive cadence (+20.4%, +13.5%, +5.4% in the surrounding quarters).

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is real but partly mechanical. The 37% asset growth and 28% NII growth are acquisition-driven; the organic component (7% annualized deposit growth, wealth AUM +33%) is healthy but more modest. Management guides to adjusted revenue growth of 29–31% for 2026, assuming two 25bp Fed cuts. The Fed-cut assumption is the swing variable: a deposit-rich, asset-sensitive bank benefits as deposit costs fall faster than asset yields, which is precisely the dynamic that drove 35bps of YoY NIM expansion.

The estimate-revision picture is the cautionary note. Consensus FY2026 EPS sits at $2.51 (the midpoint of guidance), and Q1 came in ahead at $0.62 vs. $0.59 estimated. But the 90-day revision trend is slightly negative across all horizons (current quarter -2.9%, next quarter -3.2%, full year -0.3%, next year -1.1%), with no positive 30-day momentum. This is the opposite of a strong positive revision velocity that signals an undermodeled inflection. Consensus already embeds the recovery; analysts are not raising numbers. The growth thesis is therefore about delivering on a plan the market has priced, not surprising it to the upside.

The durable growth lever is the M&A engine itself. Seacoast acknowledges target scarcity could raise prices and pressure returns, and goodwill/intangibles now total ~$1.2B (~45% of equity), creating impairment risk if acquired returns disappoint. The strategy compounds book value when deals are accretive and the integration holds, and destroys it when they are not.

## Valuation Assessment

At $30.59, SBCF trades at a trailing P/E of 20.5x on depressed GAAP EPS of $1.57. The more relevant figure is forward adjusted EPS: against $2.51 consensus, the stock trades at ~12.2x forward earnings. That is below the regional-bank peer median P/E of 16.0x on a trailing basis, and roughly in line on forward adjusted numbers.

| Metric | SBCF | Peer Median |
|---|---|---|
| P/E (forward adj.) | ~12.2x | 16.0x (trailing) |
| Dividend yield | 2.4% | 3.0% |
| 52-week discount | -14% from high | -2% to -12% |

The forward multiple looks undemanding relative to a peer set running 9.9x (PFS) to 19.8x (FBK), but the comparison cuts both ways. The peer median P/E of 16.0x is inflated by SFNC's negative earnings; the cleaner comparables (WAFD 12.0x, BANC 16.0x, PFS 9.9x) bracket SBCF closely. Seacoast's adjusted ROTE of ~16% is above the typical regional, which would justify a premium, but that ROTE leans on accretion that runs off, and the credit trend introduces a discount that the rest of the peer group does not carry to the same degree.

The dividend yield of 2.4% sits below the 3.0% peer median, so income support is thinner than the group. The stock is down ~14% from its $35.55 January high but up 24% over the trailing year; neither extreme is informative on its own.

The market is not obviously wrong here. A cheap-looking forward multiple is justified by genuine offsets (accretion run-off, CRE credit migration, integration risk). This is a fairly priced normalization story where the multiple already discounts the obvious risks. The valuation score reflects a balanced setup rather than asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Seacoast's competitive position is improving at the margin. The VBI franchise in The Villages, one of the nation's fastest-growing retirement communities, supplies sticky, low-cost retiree deposits that are difficult for competitors to replicate. Combined with the broader granular deposit base, this gives Seacoast a structural funding edge in a falling-rate environment where deposit-rich banks expand margins.

The bank competes against national banks, larger Southeast regionals, credit unions, and increasingly fintechs and digital banks. Its risk factors specifically call out stablecoins (the 2025 GENIUS Act) and "debanking" enforcement, signs that management is tracking the structural threats to deposit franchises. The relationship-banking model with small average loan sizes is a defensible niche, and the role as a Florida acquirer-of-choice gives it a pipeline most subscale peers lack.

The persistent governance flag complicates the picture. The proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows SBCF's cumulative 5-year TSR at 119.8 versus a peer group at 187.4, a substantial lag despite net income growth and rising CEO comp. The market has been skeptical of whether the acquisitive strategy translates into per-share value. The wealth segment and granular deposits are improving, but the through-the-cycle return on the M&A engine has not yet earned a premium rating from investors.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit migration is the central risk.** The deterioration is concentrated in CRE owner-occupied (+$23M) and non-owner-occupied (+$27.6M). Management states ~81% of nonaccruals are real-estate-secured with collateral well in excess of balances, which may make this lumpy migration of seasoned acquired loans rather than a genuine credit cycle. But the combination of climbing problem loans and near-zero reserving is the kind of pattern that precedes negative provisioning surprises. If this is the leading edge of Florida CRE stress rather than acquisition seasoning, the adjusted-earnings story weakens materially.

**CRE concentration.** Roughly 50% of loans are CRE, at 224% of bank risk-based capital (below the 300% regulatory guideline but elevated). Office exposure is modest ($550M, mostly suburban); the largest CRE segment is retail ($1.4B). Florida's filings explicitly acknowledge deeper recessions and sharper real-estate declines than the national average, plus hurricane/climate exposure and rising property-insurance costs that pressure collateral values.

**Integration risk.** The VBI system conversion is not complete, carrying deposit/customer attrition risk and the accounting complexity of the Series A convertible preferred. Goodwill/intangibles at ~45% of equity create impairment risk if returns disappoint.

**Rate/AOCI risk.** A re-acceleration of long-end rates would pressure tangible book value, given the -17.4% EVE sensitivity in a +300bp shock and the ~$98M HTM unrealized loss.

**Unconfirmed leadership changes.** Two Item 5.02 officer/director 8-Ks (filed 3/26/2026 and 5/29/2026) could not be retrieved. Leadership changes mid-integration would be material and should be verified before any position is established.

### Options Market Signal

The reported ATM implied volatility reading of 350.5% (up from 106.3%) against a -1.4% price move is almost certainly a thin-options data artifact for a $2.9B regional bank rather than a meaningful signal, and we treat it as non-informative. More usable: the put/call open-interest ratio fell from 2.25 to 1.23, a modest unwind of prior bearish positioning. Short interest is moderate at 5.3% of float with 5.6 days to cover, neither a crowded short nor a clean bullish setup. There is no options-implied confirmation of either an imminent catalyst or acute stress.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($38, ~24% upside).** VBI integration completes cleanly in Q3 2026, the credit migration proves to be seasoned acquired-loan lumpiness rather than a Florida CRE cycle, and management delivers adjusted EPS at the top of the guidance range with adjusted ROTE near 16% by Q4. As GAAP EPS normalizes off the one-time securities loss, the optical trailing multiple collapses and the stock re-rates toward the high end of the peer band. The deposit-rich VBI funding base supports continued loan growth and NIM resilience even as accretion runs off. At ~15x normalized EPS near the top of guidance, that supports the analyst high of $38.

**Bear Case (~$25, ~18% downside).** Nonaccruals continue climbing and the bank is forced into a catch-up provision, exposing the under-reserving in Q1. Florida CRE stress broadens against the bank's 50% concentration, integration attrition erodes the VBI deposit base, and accretion run-off pulls core NIM below 3.5%. Estimate revisions turn decisively negative, the M&A premium evaporates, and the stock retraces toward its 52-week low of $24.46, roughly 10x on a haircut EPS near $2.40.

The base case sits between: the normalization story plays out broadly as guided, credit proves manageable but not pristine, and the stock grinds toward the mid-$30s as adjusted earnings power becomes visible in GAAP results, without a decisive re-rating given the unresolved credit and integration overhangs.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July), with the decisive read coming in Q3 2026 results when the VBI conversion completes and the credit trend either stabilizes or worsens.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Nonaccruals stabilize or decline in Q2 *and* ACL coverage rebuilds toward 1.45%+, removing the under-reserving concern.
- VBI system conversion completes on schedule with no material deposit attrition, confirming the durability of the cheap funding base.
- Estimate revisions turn positive (+30d revisions above zero) as analysts mark to the adjusted-earnings inflection, with the stock still below ~13x forward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A catch-up provision materially above run-rate (e.g., >$15M in any quarter) confirming credit migration is genuine cycle stress, not seasoning.
- NPL ratio rising through ~1.0% with continued CRE concentration in the deterioration.
- Goodwill impairment indication or evidence of VBI deposit flight post-conversion.
- Confirmation that the unexplained Item 5.02 filings represent a destabilizing executive departure mid-integration.

## Conclusion

Seacoast has executed a genuine scale transformation, and the underlying earnings power (adjusted EPS up 111% YoY, adjusted ROTE of 16.3%, NIM expanding to 3.83% on a 1.54% deposit cost) is inflecting in a way that a naive GAAP screen misreads entirely. The VBI deal is the kind of deposit-rich, liquidity-accretive acquisition that supports both margin and growth. Capital and liquidity are strong, and management's 2026 guidance frames a credible normalization path.

What holds us back from a BUY is the balance of the evidence. The credit trend is deteriorating against near-zero provisioning, the franchise carries 50% CRE concentration in a state prone to sharp real-estate downturns, the VBI conversion is unfinished, and 26bps of the headline NIM is accretion that runs off. Estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative, meaning consensus already embeds the recovery rather than underestimating it. At ~12x forward adjusted EPS, the stock trades below the peer median, a discount that reflects that the obvious risks are priced and whose upside requires clean execution on credit and integration. The five-year TSR lag versus peers (119.8 vs. 187.4) shows that the market has not historically rewarded this strategy with a premium.

We rate SBCF a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale: the catalyst (GAAP normalization into Q4 2026) is real, but the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We would turn constructive on confirmation that credit migration is seasoning rather than cycle stress, ideally on a pullback toward the high-$20s that widens the margin of safety.
