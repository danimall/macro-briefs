## Executive Summary

Kinder Morgan is executing well against the strongest structural demand backdrop U.S. gas midstream has had in a decade. H1 2026 revenue rose 12.3% to $9,305M, Adjusted EBITDA rose 14.7% to $4,738M, and Adjusted EPS rose 35.5% to $0.84. Natural gas transport volumes were up 7.3% and gathering volumes up roughly 20%, which is genuine physical throughput growth rather than accounting. Management raised full-year guidance to Adjusted EBITDA more than 5% above budget and Adjusted EPS more than 12% above original forecast, and the backlog stands at $9.6B — 92% natural gas, with more than 60% tied to power generation and local distribution demand. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised up 5.8% over 90 days to $1.56. Q2 non-GAAP Adjusted EPS of $0.37 beat the ~$0.31 non-GAAP consensus by roughly 19%, the third beat in four quarters.

Three things keep this a HOLD rather than an upgrade. First, the composition of the 2026 beat. Products Pipelines EBDA rose 18% while total delivery volumes fell 7.6% in Q2 and crude/condensate volumes fell 14.3% in H1; every driver management names is a commodity margin — butane blending spreads, transmix pricing, wider crude spreads. Terminals' Liquids increase includes early-termination payments from a customer refinery closure, which is compensation for permanently lost business. Midstream's gain includes marketing margin from colder Texas winter weather. Second, consensus itself is signaling this does not persist: next-year EPS of $1.54 sits *below* current-year $1.56, and the two largest backlog projects (SSE4 Phase 1 Q4 2028, MSX as early as Q2 2028) do not contribute inside a twelve-month horizon. Third, the five-year record on the metric management itself selects. Distributable cash flow per share went $2.40 → $2.19 → $2.10 → $2.19 → $2.42 from 2021 to 2025, up 0.8% cumulatively, while cumulative TSR trailed the disclosed peer group in every one of those five years.

At $31.225 the stock trades at 20.0x FY2026E consensus EPS, 20.3x FY2027E, and approximately 10.9x annualized H1 Adjusted EBITDA on an enterprise value of ~$102.8B, with a 3.81% dividend yield on the guided $1.19. That is neither cheap nor stretched. The dividend is well covered on distributable cash flow (49% payout on 2025 DCF/share) and thinly covered on GAAP or adjusted earnings (71-76%), and the total 2026 capital program plus dividends exceeds expected operating cash flow by roughly $0.9-1.1B, funded with incremental debt. Own KMI for the yield and the gas-demand optionality; the risk/reward from here — roughly +9.5% total return to base case against −21% to bear case — does not support new capital at this price.

## Company Overview

KMI owns or operates approximately 78,000 miles of pipelines, 136 terminals, over 700 Bcf of working natural gas storage, and ~6.9 Bcf/year of gross renewable natural gas production. Four reportable segments, sized by H1 2026 Segment EBDA:

| Segment | H1 2026 | H1 2025 | Change | Share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Natural Gas Pipelines | $3,231M | $2,889M | +11.8% | 65.6% |
| Products Pipelines | $663M | $562M | +18.0% | 13.5% |
| Terminals | $639M | $575M | +11.1% | 13.0% |
| CO₂ | $394M | $331M | +19.0% | 8.0% |
| **Total** | **$4,927M** | **$4,357M** | **+13.1%** | |

Asset concentration mirrors earnings concentration: $54.0B of the $74.1B balance sheet sits in Natural Gas Pipelines. Within that segment, Midstream EBDA rose 26% in H1, East 4%, West 7%.

The moat is positional. Interstate pipelines carry FERC-granted eminent domain authority and long-lived rights-of-way into the demand centers now growing fastest — Gulf Coast liquefaction, Texas and Southeast power generation, LDCs. The 10-K is explicit that substantially all land under KMI's pipelines is third-party owned and that permitting and rights-of-way difficulties "have caused, and may continue to cause, delays in or cancellations of our construction projects." The same barrier that protects the installed base constrains its expansion, and it constrains new entrants more.

Revenue mix in Q2 2026: $1,365M firm services, $732M fee-based, $366M leasing, $2,001M commodity sales. Commodity sales are largely offset by cost of sales and contribute little margin, with the exception of the CO₂ segment, which produces rather than purchases what it sells.

One quality-of-earnings positive worth stating: GAAP EPS of $0.39 exceeded Adjusted EPS of $0.37 in Q2, because Certain Items were a net $(46)M of unsettled derivative mark-to-market gains that management strips out. Adjusted results were *lower* than reported. That is the opposite of the usual non-GAAP direction and argues against a presentation concern this quarter. The 19% beat figure is a non-GAAP-to-non-GAAP comparison; no published GAAP consensus was available, so the $0.39 should not be characterized as a beat.

### Cash flow, and what the free cash flow figure actually means

H1 2026 operating cash flow was $3,451M, up 22.8%. Capital expenditures were $1,786M, giving simple free cash flow of $1,665M. Against dividends paid of $1,319M that leaves $346M — before the $503M Monument acquisition, $142M of contributions to unconsolidated investments and $77M of distributions to noncontrolling interests. Net of those, H1 ran an approximate $280M cash deficit, funded by a $311M net debt increase. Cash rose $26M to $89M.

Management's stated "approximately $1 billion in free cash flow during the quarter" reconciles to operating cash flow less capex (Q2 capex $982M). It is accurate on that definition and should not be read as $1B of discretionary cash.

For the full year the filing lays out $1,111M of sustaining capital and $4,091M of expansion capital ($3,119M PP&E, $473M JV contributions, $505M acquisitions), totaling $5,202M, with $2,411M remaining after H1. Dividends declared are guided to $1.19/share, roughly $2,648M in cash on 2,225M shares. Adding ~$155M of NCI distributions, total 2026 cash uses approximate $7.8B against full-year operating cash flow plausibly in the $6.7-6.9B range (H1 was $3,451M, and Q1 is seasonally the strongest quarter for winter gas). That implies roughly $0.9-1.1B of incremental debt. This is a sensitivity, not a forecast — H1 OCF also benefited from a $157M receivables release and a net $11M cash tax refund.

The deleveraging framing deserves precision. Company-defined net debt at 6/30/26 was $32,027M. Management guides to year-end net debt/Adjusted EBITDA of 3.6x. If FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA lands at $9.3-9.6B, 3.6x implies year-end net debt of $33.5-34.6B — net debt rising $1.5-2.6B from mid-year while the ratio falls. The ratio improves because EBITDA grows faster than debt, not because debt is repaid. That is a standard and legitimate midstream model, but it is not self-funding.

### Balance sheet, coverage and covenants

Total debt principal was $32,144M at 6/30/26 versus $31,823M at year-end. The current portion roughly doubled to $2,443M from $1,226M, and commercial paper went to $338M (3.92% weighted average) from $13M. Working capital deficit widened $1,491M to $(3,059)M — $878M from senior notes rolling into the twelve-month window, $325M of CP partly funding Monument, and $310M of net receivables/payables movement. KMI stockholders' equity is $31,631M against an accumulated deficit of $(9,657)M, a legacy artifact of the 2014 MLP roll-up and historical distributions exceeding GAAP net income.

Solvency evidence is comfortable. H1 interest coverage (operating income + equity earnings + other, divided by net interest) was 3.87x; Adjusted EBITDA to cash interest paid was 5.39x. The revolver was amended and restated on May 21, 2026, extending maturity from August 2026 to May 2031, with a maximum Consolidated Net Indebtedness/EBITDA covenant of 5.50x. With no revolver borrowings, $338M of CP and $10M of letters of credit, roughly $3.2B of availability remains. Against a 3.6x guided actual, EBITDA could fall roughly 35% before the covenant binds. Moody's upgraded to Baa1/stable from Baa2/positive in March 2026 — an upgrade deeper within investment grade — and the filing states that a one- or two-notch downgrade would not require additional derivative collateral at current mark-to-market positions.

Standard screens read this business badly. A properly recomputed classic Altman Z on FY2025 inputs comes to approximately 1.22, nominally "distress." The decomposition explains why: the retained-earnings term contributes −0.196 on a $10.2B accumulated deficit, and asset turnover of 0.23x is what a $74B pipeline balance sheet on $17B of revenue looks like by construction. Neither term measures debt service capacity. The correct reading is "highly levered, deliberately negative working capital," not elevated bankruptcy risk. The residual and non-trivial exposure is financing access: $2,443M of current maturities plus CP must be rolled continuously, and the working capital deficit widened $1.5B in six months.

Cash taxes are the one item I would flag for modelers. H1 book tax expense was $559M against a net $11M cash tax *refund*; deferred taxes contributed $576M to operating cash flow and the deferred tax liability grew $548M. Accelerated depreciation on $40.5B of net PP&E plus $3.1B of current-year expansion capex sustains this. If expansion capex normalized down, the deferral compresses and OCF-to-EBITDA conversion degrades. No cash tax cliff is evidenced in the filing; the point is that current OCF quality depends partly on the capex program continuing.

**Financial Health: 6.3.** Strong coverage, wide covenant headroom, an upgraded rating and a five-year revolver against $32B of debt, a widening working capital deficit, a dividend-plus-growth-capex program that requires ~$1B of new debt annually, and operating cash flow flattered by tax deferral. Appropriate leverage for the asset class, but capital structure efficiency is not improving on a per-share basis.

## Growth Analysis

The volumetric evidence in the gas franchise is real. Q2 natural gas transport was 47,886 BBtu/d against 44,818 (+6.8%); H1 was 48,830 versus 45,509 (+7.3%). Gathering rose 25.6% in Q2 to 4,637 BBtu/d. NGL transport went to 52 from 39 MBbl/d. Firm services revenue rose $190M in H1. This is contracted, physical throughput growth driven by LNG feedgas, Texas and Southeast power load, and industrial demand.

The backlog is $9.6B, 92% natural gas, with more than 60% supporting power generation and LDC demand, plus board contingent approval for nearly $400M of additional projects not yet in the backlog. Named projects and timing:

- **South System Expansion 4**: ~$3.5B total, KMI share ~$1.8B including Elba Express, ~1.3 Bcf/d. Final EIS June 2026; FERC certificate orders expected around end-July 2026. Phase 1 Q4 2028, Phase 2 Q4 2029.
- **Mississippi Crossing**: ~$1.7B, in service as early as Q2 2028.
- **NGPL Amarillo Expansion**: ~$200M, up to 550,000 Dth/d, fully subscribed under long-term contracts, includes new data center development; targeted Q3 2028.
- **South Texas Enhancement**: ~$90M, Q2 2028.
- **Houston Ship Channel terminal expansion**: backed by long-term customer commitments, in service 2027-2028.
- **Western Gateway** with Phillips 66 (a co-developer and refinery supply source, not a customer): not FID'd, subject to commercial agreements and permitting, and appears not to be in the backlog.

Roughly $660M of KMI-share projects went into service in Q2 — the Gulf Coast Express expansion (+~570 MMcf/d), TGP's $235M Cumberland lateral serving TVA's new gas plant, and the Hiland Express conversion to NGL service. Monument Pipeline closed May 1 for $503M with $264M allocated to PP&E, $236M to customer-relationship intangibles and **zero goodwill** — a disciplined allocation that says KMI paid for assets and contracts rather than a premium.

Two constraints on the growth score. First, timing. Nearly all backlog conversion lands 2028-2029, and consensus next-year EPS of $1.54 is below current-year $1.56. The sell-side is explicitly modelling a 2027 pause, which is consistent with commodity-margin normalization plus the mechanical step-up in reported interest expense as capitalized interest falls when construction-in-progress converts to in-service assets, alongside rising DD&A. KMI also increased fixed-to-variable swap notional from $3,500M to $4,250M, taking variable-rate debt to $4,588M (14% of total) — modest in size, but a position that benefits from lower short rates.

Second, composition. Products Pipelines EBDA rose 18% while H1 refined product volumes fell 3.5% (jet fuel −6.4%, gasoline −3.5%) and crude/condensate fell 14.3%. Terminals liquids utilization slipped to 93.2% from 94.3%, and the H1 Liquids increase includes payments for early termination of storage agreements tied to a customer refinery closure. A defensible counter-reading exists — butane blending and transmix spreads have been wide for years and KMI treats the Texas intrastate marketing book as a structural asset-backed business — but the physical volume declines are not arguable.

**Growth Prospects: 6.8.** Contracted gas volume growth is genuine and the backlog is well-subscribed and gas-weighted, but the earnings inflection is 2028-2029 loaded, consensus models a flat-to-down 2027, and roughly a third of 2026 segment upside is spread-, weather- and termination-driven.

## Competitive Landscape

The gas franchise is strengthening. 7.3% H1 transport volume growth and 25.6% Q2 gathering growth outpace U.S. gas demand growth, indicating share capture on the interconnects that matter — LNG feedgas, gas-fired power, LDC load. The NGPL Amarillo Expansion is fully subscribed under long-term contracts and explicitly includes new data center development. Richard Kinder's framing — that demand growth makes "our existing highly utilized assets more valuable" — is the correct read on scarcity: FERC permitting, rights-of-way and public opposition that constrain KMI's own expansion constrain replication of its footprint far more.

The liquids side is eroding. Refined product volumes down 3.5%, crude and condensate down 14.3%, terminals liquids utilization down 110bp, and a customer refinery closure that removed storage business and produced a termination payment. The 10-K's competition risk factor also names customers building their own facilities and, for CO₂, carbon capture developers competing to supply CO₂ buyers for enhanced oil recovery.

Net: an improving 66%-of-earnings gas segment and a declining ~27% liquids/terminals franchise, with a CO₂ segment whose hedge coverage declines from roughly 83% in 2026 to roughly 40% by 2028.

**Competitive Position: 6.8.** Trajectory is positive where it matters most and negative where the volumes are physically shrinking.

## Risk Assessment

**Portland Harbor Superfund allocation — the dated, unquantified item.** The final remedy is estimated at more than $2.8B with cleanup exceeding ten years and approximately 90 PRPs, including four KMI facilities. The non-judicial allocation is anticipated to be complete by December 31, 2026, and until then KMI states it is "unable to reasonably estimate the extent of our liability." Total accrued environmental liabilities are $174M against $9M of probable recoveries. This is a specific, dated event within the investment horizon where the range of outcomes is unbounded by disclosure.

**Goodwill.** $20,084M of goodwill equals 63% of KMI stockholders' equity and 27% of total assets. The May 31, 2026 annual test, using an EBITDA-multiple market approach for six of seven reporting units, found every unit's fair value exceeded carrying value "by at least 10%." That phrasing establishes a floor, not a cushion, and a 10% margin on a comparables-multiple method is thin in a sector where multiples move with gas prices and long-end rates.

**Permitting and steel tariffs on a $9.6B program.** SSE4 and MSX both depend on FERC certificates. Separately, a dedicated 10-K risk factor notes the Federal Circuit ruled in August 2025 that many current-administration tariffs exceed presidential authority, with the decision stayed pending Supreme Court review — two-way uncertainty on the cost basis of $4.1B of annual expansion capital.

**Litigation.** The pension ERISA class action seeks damages "in excess of $100 million," though a February 10, 2026 Magistrate recommendation rejected or significantly narrowed several plaintiff theories. The Freeport LNG Winter Storm Uri claim (~$104M plus fees and interest) was revived when the 14th Court of Appeals reversed KMI's summary judgment in April 2025. A quiet positive: EPA's January 27, 2026 proposal to approve eight state implementation plans would, if finalized, remove Good Neighbor Plan requirements from KMI facilities.

**Rate sensitivity.** KMI is a levered 3.8%-yield instrument with $32B of debt, and multiple compression from a higher long end is a meaningful driver of the bear case, largely outside management's control. [The specific claims about Fed meeting holds, dissent counts, and September-hike contract probabilities are not supported by the provided sources and have been removed.]

**Governance-adjacent candor.** The 10-K states that "nothing in our governing documents or credit agreements prohibits us from borrowing to pay dividends," and that if cash flow proves insufficient KMI "will be forced to take actions such as reducing dividends." Given the ~$1B annual funding gap, that disclosure is not boilerplate.

**Risk Factors: 6.0.** Well-disclosed risks, but the Portland Harbor allocation date, the 10% goodwill floor and the CO₂ hedge roll-down are specific items with asymmetric downside that a 20x multiple does not obviously price.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $37.50 (+20.1% price, ~+24% total return)

The gas volume growth is contracted and durable, and the demand drivers behind it (LNG liquefaction ramp, gas-fired power for data center load, Texas and Southeast industrial expansion) are supported by KMI's own filings and disclosed backlog. The $9.6B backlog is 92% gas, more than 60% power/LDC, with the Amarillo project fully subscribed. If FERC certificates for SSE4 and MSX land on schedule and the backlog converts at typical midstream build multiples, incremental EBITDA against a ~$9.5B base by 2029 supports mid-single-digit compounding with dividend growth accelerating above the current 2%. Butane blending and transmix spreads prove structural rather than cyclical, so 2027 estimates get revised up rather than down. Moody's Baa1 and a five-year revolver support a modest re-rating toward the ~11.75x EV/EBITDA that premium fee-based peers command. At 11.75x $10.2B of FY2027 EBITDA, less ~$35B of net debt and $1.25B of NCI, equity is $83.6B, or $37.50 per share.

### Bear Case — $24.50 (−21.5%)

Commodity spreads normalize and the one-time items do not repeat: Products EBDA reverts toward volume reality (refined products −3.5%, crude −14.3%), the refinery-closure termination payments do not recur, and Texas intrastate marketing margin mean-reverts on a normal winter. Reported interest expense steps up as capitalized interest falls with backlog conversion, and DD&A rises on $40.5B of net PP&E plus $3.1B of new assets, compressing EPS while EBITDA still grows. Meanwhile the long end stays elevated, compressing a 3.8%-yield levered instrument's multiple to 9.75x. The Portland Harbor allocation, completing by December 31, 2026, produces a liability materially above the $174M accrued, and a rate-driven decline in sector multiples pushes one or more of the seven reporting units below its "at least 10%" goodwill cushion. At 9.75x $9.2B EBITDA less $35B net debt and $1.25B NCI, equity is $53.5B, or $24.00-25.00.

### Base Case — $33.00 (+5.7% price, ~+9.5% total return with dividend)

FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA lands near $9.5B and FY2027 near $9.9B as new in-service assets and a full year of Monument offset commodity-margin normalization — the outcome consensus is already modelling in a flat 2027 EPS of $1.54. Net debt rises to ~$34-35B, leverage ratio prints 3.6x, the dividend grows 2-3% to roughly $1.22. At 11.0x FY2027E EBITDA of $9.9B, enterprise value is $108.9B, less $35B net debt and $1.25B NCI, giving $72.7B of equity, or $32.60-33.00 per share on 2,230M shares. The dividend does most of the work in the total return.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** Own for the 3.81% yield, covered at 49% of 2025 distributable cash flow per share, backed by 66%-of-earnings gas infrastructure with contracted volume growth. Do not add new capital at $31.225.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (mid-October 2026), with two earlier checkpoints — FERC certificate orders on SSE4 and MSX, and the Portland Harbor non-judicial allocation, anticipated complete by December 31, 2026.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Price at or below $27.50** with fundamentals intact — that is ~17.9x FY2027E consensus, ~9.9x EV/EBITDA and a 4.33% yield, which restores asymmetry.
2. **FY2027 consensus EPS revised above $1.65** (currently $1.54), specifically driven by firm-services revenue rather than commodity-margin line items. That would falsify the "2026 upside is transient" thesis in a checkable way.
3. **Distributable cash flow per share guided above $2.75 for 2027** (versus $2.42 in 2025), demonstrating that the $9.6B backlog converts to per-share cash flow rather than repeating the flat 2021-2025 pattern.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **Portland Harbor allocation assigning KMI liability above ~$600M** — roughly 3.5x current total environmental accruals — or any goodwill impairment charge at one of the seven reporting units, which would confirm the "at least 10%" cushion was a floor rather than a buffer.
2. **Year-end 2026 net debt/Adjusted EBITDA printing above 4.0x** against the 3.6x guide, indicating the funding gap is widening faster than EBITDA.
3. **Full-year Products Pipelines and Terminals combined EBDA declining year over year in 2027** while volumes continue falling, confirming that 2026 segment strength was entirely spread-driven.
4. **Any dividend action other than an increase**, or explicit management commentary invoking the 10-K's "reducing dividends" language.

## Conclusion

Kinder Morgan is doing what a well-run gas infrastructure owner should be doing in 2026: growing contracted volumes 7.3%, building a $9.6B backlog that is 92% natural gas and more than 60% tied to power and LDC demand, extending its revolver five years, and earning a rating upgrade. Q2 beat non-GAAP consensus by 19% and guidance was raised. Estimate revisions are positive across every window.

What holds the score at 6.1 and the recommendation at HOLD is the gap between segment optics and durable per-share cash generation. Roughly a third of 2026 segment upside traces to butane blending spreads, transmix pricing, winter weather in the Texas marketing book and termination payments from a customer's refinery closure, while the physical volumes in the liquids franchise fell. Consensus 2027 EPS below 2026 is the market's own acknowledgment of this. The backlog is real but lands 2028-2029, and the honest historical record — distributable cash flow per share up 0.8% from 2021 to 2025, cumulative TSR below the disclosed peer group in all five years — is the reason a 20x forward multiple and ~11x EV/EBITDA is not a bargain. Add a $32B debt load, a ~$1B annual funding gap, a $20.1B goodwill balance carried on a 10% fair-value floor, and the Portland Harbor allocation resolving inside the year, and the asymmetry is roughly +9.5% total return to base against −21% to bear.

Hold the position for the covered 3.81% yield and the gas-demand optionality. Revisit for new capital at $27.50, or when 2027 firm-services revenue growth — not commodity margins — proves the guidance raise is repeatable.
