**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

MDU Resources has finished a two-year transformation into a pure-play regulated energy delivery company. The May 2023 Knife River spin-off (construction materials) and the October 2024 Everus separation (construction services) leave three rate-regulated segments: Electric, Natural Gas Distribution, and a FERC-regulated Pipeline business centered on the Bakken. The reported revenue history (down from $6.97B in 2022 to $1.88B in 2025) is an artifact of deconsolidation, not organic decline; on a continuing-operations basis revenue grew 6.7% in 2025 to $1,875M with income from continuing operations of $191.4M ($0.93 diluted EPS).

The investment case turns on a genuine but moderate growth thesis layered onto a stable income franchise. MDU's electric and pipeline segments sit at the intersection of accelerating data-center load (Applied Digital is adding 350 MW on top of an existing 180 MW, structured to require minimal new MDU capital) and Bakken associated-gas pipeline demand (the Bakken East open season drew ~1.4 Bcf/day of requests, ~40% already under Precedent Agreements, with a State of North Dakota backstop of up to $50M/year for 10 years). Rate base grew 16.0% in 2025 and 8.7% on a 5-year CAGR; customer growth runs 1-2% per year, above the national average. Management affirmed FY2026 EPS guidance of $0.93-$1.00 after a weather-driven Q1 miss.

The stock has re-rated ~29% over the past year and now trades at ~22.7x trailing/forward earnings against a peer median of 19.3x. That premium captures the data-center optionality but leaves limited margin of safety on a name growing earnings in the mid-single digits. Against the growth tailwind sit three real frictions: a cured-but-notable Intermountain Gas covenant breach (Q3 2025), West Coast decarbonization pressure on the gas franchise (~35% of gas revenue in Washington), and negative free cash flow that requires continued equity issuance (~7.4M forward-sale shares still to settle through 2027). This is a hold for investors who want exposure to the data-center/grid-reliability theme through a defensive utility vehicle, not a high-conviction entry at the current multiple.

## Company Overview

MDU operates three regulated segments:

- **Electric (Montana-Dakota Utilities):** Serves ~146,550 retail customers across 185 communities in North Dakota, Montana, South Dakota, and Wyoming. Rate base ~$1.8B; roughly 40% of owned generation is renewable. FY2025 segment net income $64.9M.
- **Natural Gas Distribution (Montana-Dakota, Cascade, Intermountain):** ~1.08M customers across 8 states. Washington (~35% of gas revenue) and Idaho (~24%) are the largest. Rate base ~$2.2B. FY2025 segment net income $56.1M.
- **Pipeline (WBI Energy Transmission):** FERC-regulated, 3,800+ miles, ~350 Bcf of storage, strategically located near four producing basins including the Bakken. FY2025 net income $68.2M, the fastest-growing and highest-margin segment, with operating income up from $69M (2023) to $101M (2025).

The business is a vertically integrated regulated monopoly in mostly rural, fast-growing territories. The constructive elements are above-average customer growth, multi-state regulatory frameworks with tracking mechanisms and decoupling that reduce regulatory lag, and a Bakken-adjacent pipeline footprint that differentiates it from a typical local distribution company. Headquarters is Bismarck, ND; CEO Nicole Kivisto, CFO Jason Vollmer; ~2,096 employees.

## Financial Analysis

**Continuing operations are growing despite the optical revenue collapse.** Reported revenue fell from $6.97B (2022) to $4.66B (2023) to $1.76B (2024) to $1.88B (2025), entirely reflecting the Knife River and Everus deconsolidations now in discontinued operations. Continuing-operations revenue grew 6.7% in 2025, and income from continuing operations rose to $191.4M ($0.93 diluted EPS) from $181.1M. Any model reading raw consolidated revenue trends will badly misjudge this company.

**Profitability is typical for a regulated utility.** Operating margin 15.5%, net margin 10.2%, ROE ~6.9%, ROA ~2.5%. Operating cash flow was $473M in 2025 against capex of $770M (elevated by the $264.6M final Badger Wind payment), producing negative free cash flow of -$297M. Negative FCF during a heavy rate-base-growth phase is normal for a utility; the capital is recovered through rates over time, funded by debt and equity in the interim.

**Balance sheet is leveraged but within utility norms.** Total assets $7.62B, long-term debt $2.53B, equity $2.77B, D/E ~2.75, current ratio 0.84. The Altman Z-Score of 1.08 ("distress zone") is a false alarm; it reflects the structurally high leverage and low asset turnover (0.25x) inherent to regulated utilities, not genuine bankruptcy risk. All debt-issuing entities carry investment-grade ratings, and subsidiary funded-debt/cap ratios (47-51%) sit below the 65% covenant ceiling.

**The one real balance-sheet flag is subsidiary-level.** Intermountain Gas failed its minimum interest coverage ratio for the period ended September 30, 2025, an event of default that triggered cross-defaults at MDU Energy Capital and at revolving credit agreements held by MDU and Intermountain. Waivers cured the defaults, and the company was in compliance at 12/31/25 and 3/31/26. This is not a near-term solvency risk, but it signals coverage stress at a subsidiary and warrants monitoring in upcoming filings.

**Earnings delivery has been weak.** The trailing four-quarter record is one beat and three misses, including a -43% miss in Q2 2025 and a -7.2% miss in Q1 2026 (EPS $0.39 vs $0.42 consensus, on heating degree days down 13-30% across territories). Consensus EPS revisions are broadly flat-to-slightly-negative: current-year estimate $0.97 (essentially unchanged over 90 days), next-year $1.05 (down 1.4% over 90 days). There is no estimate-revision tailwind here.

**Dividend** is $0.14/quarter (~2.66% yield), with an 87-year uninterrupted payment history and a 60-70% payout target on regulated earnings.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three identifiable, mostly funded drivers:

1. **Data-center load (Applied Digital, near Ellendale, ND).** Existing 180 MW (~21% of the generation portfolio), served via MISO market purchases requiring minimal new MDU capital. An additional 350 MW Electric Service Agreement is approved by the NDPSC, with ~50 MW online in Q1 2026 and another ~50 MW by end Q2 2026. A separate ~50 MW data center near Leola, SD awaits a permit. The structure matters: Applied Digital is the customer, not a partner, and the load is sourced through MISO to require minimal incremental capital while spreading fixed-cost recovery across other ratepayers. This is a lower-capital-intensity earnings driver than utilities building dedicated generation for hyperscalers.

2. **Bakken East Pipeline (the swing factor).** A potential 350-mile, $2.7-3.2B project, incremental to the ~$2.5B base capex plan and not yet in guidance. The March 2026 binding open season drew the demand detailed above. Phase 1 targets November 2029, Phase 2 November 2030. At $2.7-3.2B the project would roughly double current debt if balance-sheet-funded, which is why management explicitly references partnerships. The demand signal is strong; the final investment decision and financing structure are the items to watch.

3. **Rate-base compounding.** Rate-base growth was boosted by the 49% Badger Wind interest (122.5 MW, placed in service December 31, 2025). Other pipeline projects — Line Section 32 (190 MMcf/d, FERC-filed March 2026, in service late 2028) and the Minot Industrial Pipeline — plus the JETx 345-kV transmission line (50% co-owned, ~2029, with CWIP incentive rate) extend the runway.

The aggregate picture is a utility that should compound EPS in the mid-single digits off the base plan, with upside optionality from Bakken East and the data-center ramp. The MISO secular backdrop is supportive: NERC and MISO flag grid reliability concerns as baseload coal retires, and MISO's adoption of "direct loss of load accreditation" (effective 2028) pulls MDU's identified capacity need forward from 2034 to 2028, supporting rate-base growth. The offset is West Coast decarbonization pressure on the gas franchise.

## Valuation Assessment

At $21.14 the stock trades at ~22.7x trailing/forward EPS. The data does not show an earnings inflection that would justify treating the trailing multiple as understated; current-year consensus ($0.97) and next-year ($1.05) imply mid-single-digit growth, and estimate revisions are flat-to-negative. This is a stable-growth utility being valued on forward earnings, which is the correct lens.

The premium is defensible if the data-center load growth and Bakken East optionality materialize; those are genuine differentiators versus a generic LDC. But the premium also means the market has already priced much of the optionality. The valuation is full, and a low-yield, premium-multiple utility growing mid-single digits offers limited asymmetric upside at this price. Analyst targets run $21.00 (low) to $28.00 (high), mean $23.29, median $23.00; the current price sits at the bottom of the analyst range, which provides modest support but is consistent with a fully-valued read.

The higher-for-longer rate environment is a specific headwind for this name: negative FCF requires continued equity and debt issuance, and rising discount rates compress the present value of long-dated rate-base growth while raising the cost of the dilution already underway.

## Competitive Landscape

MDU's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving. It is a regulated monopoly in growing rural territories, with above-average customer growth and constructive regulatory frameworks. Its genuine edge over a typical LDC is the Bakken-adjacent pipeline footprint feeding off record associated-gas production, plus a territory that hosts real, contracted data-center demand. The pipeline segment is gaining share of company earnings and growing operating income faster than the regulated electric/gas businesses.

The counterweight is the West Coast decarbonization push. Washington and Oregon present building-electrification codes, the Climate Commitment Act cap-and-invest, and the Climate Protection Program. MDU is litigating on multiple fronts (Ninth Circuit on the WA energy code, WA Supreme Court on Initiative 2066, Oregon Court of Appeals on the CPP, which Cascade joined in April 2026). Adverse outcomes would slow gas customer additions and raise compliance costs. The net trajectory is positive in the electric/pipeline segments and pressured in the WA/OR gas franchise.

## Risk Assessment

- **Intermountain Gas covenant breach (cured, but a flag).** The Q3 2025 interest-coverage default and resulting cross-defaults, cured via waivers, signal subsidiary leverage stress worth monitoring.
- **Coyote Station coal/environmental overhang.** MDU owns 25% of this ND coal plant (one of four co-owners, cannot act unilaterally) and faces a shifting EPA rule matrix (Regional Haze, MATS, GHG, coal ash). Outcomes range from new compliance capex to stranded-asset risk if costs are not recoverable.
- **Decarbonization threat to the gas franchise** (described above), the most structurally durable headwind.
- **Data-center concentration.** The risk factors now explicitly flag that large high-volume customers increase revenue concentration and counterparty credit risk, a direct nod to Applied Digital exposure. If a single data-center customer reduces or terminates load, the earnings impact would be visible.
- **Capital-market dependence and dilution.** Negative FCF requires continuous issuance. ~7.4M forward-sale shares (~3.5% of shares outstanding) remain settleable through 2027 from the December 2025 offering priced at $19.70; a $400M ATM program is in place but unused. Bakken East financing is the larger open question.
- **Earnings sensitivity to weather.** The Q1 2026 and Q2 2025 misses were weather-driven, but decoupling and weather normalization only partly mute this.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 38.1% against 1-year historical volatility of 21.6% — rich, with the term structure in backwardation (1-month 38.1% vs 6-month 34.1%), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress or an event. The put/call skew of 4.5% shows elevated put demand, and the put/call OI ratio jumped from 0.07 to 0.57 since early May alongside a 6% price decline. The options market is leaning modestly cautious. For a low-beta utility, a 38% near-term IV and ±7% implied move (nearest expiry) is consistent with the cautious read on valuation and the earnings-delivery track record. Short interest is moderate at 6.3% of float (5 days to cover), neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$26-28).** Data-center load ramps on schedule (350 MW incremental on minimal capital, accreting to EPS), Bakken East reaches FID with a partnership structure that limits balance-sheet strain, and rate-base growth compounds at the high end of the range. EPS reaches the top of the $0.93-$1.00 guide in 2026 and steps up toward $1.10+ in 2027 on Badger Wind and data-center contributions. The multiple holds at a premium to peers on the differentiated growth profile. This reaches the high analyst target of $28.

**Bear Case (~$16-17).** Higher-for-longer rates compress the multiple toward the peer median (19.3x), the Bakken East financing dilutes more than expected or slips, West Coast decarbonization rulings go against the gas franchise, and continued equity issuance caps per-share growth. A reversion to the 52-week low ($15.76) would imply ~25% downside, consistent with a multiple compression to peer levels on a low-yield name with negative FCF.

**Base Case (~$22-23).** EPS in the middle of guidance, the multiple holds modestly above peers on data-center optionality, and total return is driven by the ~2.7% dividend plus mid-single-digit earnings growth. This aligns with the analyst median of $23.00.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is a defensive utility with a real, mostly-funded data-center load tailwind and Bakken East pipeline optionality, anchored by an 87-year dividend and affirmed FY2026 guidance. The hold is justified by the visible 2027 EPS step-up from data-center ramp and Badger Wind, not by current valuation, which is full.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are Q2 2026 earnings (data-center ramp progress, weather-adjusted trends) and any Bakken East final-investment-decision announcement.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Bakken East reaches FID with a partnership/financing structure that materially limits MDU's incremental balance-sheet burden, adding visible rate-base growth at attractive returns.
- Forward P/E compresses toward or below the peer median (~19x, roughly $18) on unchanged fundamentals, restoring margin of safety.
- Consensus EPS revisions turn positive (+5% or more over 90 days) on data-center load accretion, reversing the current flat-to-negative trend.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second covenant breach or any indication subsidiary coverage stress is worsening rather than curing.
- An adverse WA/OR decarbonization ruling that materially impairs the gas franchise.
- A dividend coverage problem — payout ratio pushing above the 70% target ceiling on a sustained basis, or a guidance cut below $0.93.
- A material reduction or termination of data-center load contracts.

## Conclusion

MDU is a cleanly transformed regulated utility with a credible, mostly-funded growth story built on data-center load and Bakken pipeline demand. The fundamentals are sound, the dividend is durable, and the data-center load is a low-capital earnings driver that differentiates it from peers. The constraint is price: at ~22.7x with a 2.7% yield, the stock trades at a premium to peers that already discounts much of the optionality, while estimate revisions are flat-to-negative, the earnings-delivery record is weak (three misses in four quarters), and negative FCF guarantees continued dilution into a higher-for-longer rate backdrop. Director Kelley's February open-market purchase at $20.85 is a constructive signal, but it does not change the conclusion that the risk/reward is balanced near current levels. We rate MDU a HOLD, with the Bakken East FID and a more attractive entry multiple as the path to an upgrade.
