**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Union Pacific runs the largest railroad in North America, roughly 32,000 route-miles across 23 western states, half of a functional Western duopoly with BNSF. The operating business is high-quality by every measure that matters for a Class I rail: a 59.8% full-year operating ratio, ~40% operating margin, ~16% ROIC, ~$9.3B of annual operating cash flow, and a right-of-way footprint that cannot be replicated. Q2 2026 was a record quarter: revenue $6.86B (+12%), diluted EPS $3.41 (+7%), OR 59.7%, with a 19% surge in domestic intermodal offsetting coal and international-intermodal declines. Management raised 2026 reported EPS growth guidance to the high-single-digit range. The operating layer is strong.

The problem for the equity is that the operating layer is no longer what sets the price. Since the July 2025 announcement of the ~$85B acquisition of Norfolk Southern, UNP trades primarily on the probability that the Surface Transportation Board approves the deal and on the terms of any conditions imposed. The transaction would create the first U.S. transcontinental single-line carrier (~50,000 miles), but it adds ~$20B of cash consideration funded by new debt plus ~225M new shares, it has paused the buyback that historically drove per-share compounding, and it faces the STB's toughest post-2001 merger standard. The Board rejected the initial application as incomplete in January 2026, forcing a refile; close is now guided to 2027, with a merger end-date of January 28, 2028. The July 23, 2026 CN competitive-access MOU neutralizes a key opponent, but approval remains a binary event a year-plus out.

At $292.56 (recent close; the profile snapshot references $304+), the stock sits below its 52-week high of $315.99, up ~28% over twelve months, at roughly 24x trailing EPS and ~23x current-year consensus of $12.75. That multiple embeds a favorable merger outcome. We rate UNP HOLD. The business quality is real and the execution is validated, but the current price offers no margin of safety against a regulatory outcome we cannot handicap with confidence, and the buyback pause has removed the near-term per-share tailwind. We hold pending STB clarity rather than as a directional call.

## Company Overview

Union Pacific Railroad (UPRR) is managed as a single reportable segment, the integrated rail network, but reports freight revenue across three commodity groups:

- **Bulk** (~33% of freight revenue, ~$7.6B in 2025): grain and grain products, fertilizer, food and refrigerated, coal and renewables.
- **Industrial** (~37%, ~$8.6B): industrial chemicals and plastics, metals and minerals, forest products, energy and specialized markets.
- **Premium** (~30%, ~$7.0B): automotive and intermodal (domestic and international containers).

The competitive structure is a duopoly. In the western U.S., BNSF (Berkshire-owned) is the only other Class I carrier, and the two split the region. Barriers to entry are effectively absolute, since no one is building a new transcontinental right-of-way, and pricing power comes from multi-year contracts with recurring core-price gains. Mexico is a genuine differentiator: roughly $2.9B of 2025 freight revenue (~13%) touches Mexico via six crossings, more than any peer, which is both an advantage and a tariff/trade-policy exposure.

## Financial Analysis

The headline financial-health flag, an Altman Z-Score of 1.4, nominally in the "distress zone," is a false signal. It is driven by a deliberately thin equity base (~$18.5B against ~$59B of cumulative treasury stock from buybacks), not by any genuine solvency concern. A business generating ~40% operating margins, ~16% ROIC, and $9B+ of annual operating cash flow with investment-grade credit is not distressed. The Z-Score misreads financial engineering.

FY2025 results: revenue $24.5B (+1% YoY), operating income $9.85B (+1%), net income $7.14B, diluted EPS $11.98 (+8% aided by share count reduction), operating ratio 59.8% (10 bps better YoY), ROE ~40%, ROIC 16.3%. Operating cash flow was $9.3B; free cash flow was ~$2.3B after $3.8B capex and $3.2B dividends. Capex ran ~15% of revenue, disciplined for a rail. Volume grew only 1% in 2025; the earnings growth came from price plus productivity (headcount fell 3% while carloads rose 1%, so workforce productivity improved ~7%).

Q2 2026 net income was $2.0B. The one blemish, OR deteriorating 0.7 points, was entirely fuel-driven: diesel spiked ~60% ($3.86/gal vs. $2.42), and the two-month fuel-surcharge lag means cost hits margin before recovery. Underlying pricing and productivity held. H1 2026 operating cash flow rose 21% to $5.5B, with 100% cash-flow conversion, and adjusted debt/EBITDA improved to 2.5x (from 2.7x at FY2025) as buybacks were paused.

Leverage is appropriate for the model: total debt ~$31.8B, adjusted debt/adjusted EBITDA 2.7x at year-end (well within the ~$49.7B debt capacity), with a low current ratio (0.91) that reflects intentional reliance on commercial paper and receivables facilities, normal for a Class I. The financial-health score of 6.5 reflects genuinely strong cash generation and returns, tempered by the pending leverage escalation the NS deal will bring (new debt, new covenants, potential rating-agency pressure) and the removal of the buyback lever that has flattered per-share metrics.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth story is modest and price/productivity-led rather than volume-led. The durable growth engine is core pricing above rail cost inflation plus continued operating-ratio improvement under CEO Vena's precision-scheduled-railroading discipline. Q2 2026's domestic intermodal growth is real but partly cyclical, since tight truck capacity is currently shifting freight to rail, a favorable but reversible tailwind. Coal is a swing factor (up 20% in 2025 on high gas prices) in secular decline.

Estimate revisions are positive but muted: current-year consensus $12.75 (+1.3% over 30 days, +1.8% over 90 days), next-year $13.88 (+1.3%/+1.9%), current-quarter $3.23 (+3.2%/+3.3%). This is steady, positive earnings momentum, not the +30% revision velocity that would flag a mispriced acceleration. The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in consensus: three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with beats in the 1-4% range (most recently +4.1% for Q2 2026). Management's high-single-digit reported-EPS growth guidance is consistent with the modest organic algorithm plus fuel-surcharge recovery.

The transformational growth case is the NS acquisition, a coast-to-coast single-line carrier that management argues would win truck-competitive share and unlock synergies. That is a 2027-and-beyond story contingent on approval, integration of two never-before-combined Class I networks, and absorption of the new shares. It is optionality, not a base-case organic trajectory. The growth score of 5.8 reflects a solid mid-to-high-single-digit organic earnings path with the merger upside sitting outside the base case as a binary.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$292–304, UNP trades at roughly 24x trailing EPS of $11.98 and ~23x current-year consensus. Against the Industrials peer set, that headline multiple looks cheap (peer median P/E is 35.3: Deere 32.2, Boeing 88.8, Eaton 38.5, Honeywell 35.3, Uber 17.2), but that peer group is a poor comparable. The right benchmark is the rail cohort (CSX, CPKC, NSC, CNI), against which UNP historically trades at a premium for its best-in-class OR and returns, and where mid-20s forward multiples are roughly the norm. UNP is trading in line with its own rail-history range, not at a discount to it.

The analyst-target framework is informative: consensus mean $308, median $313, low $239, high $363. The stock sits close to the mean/median target with the high case (~$363, ~19% above current) essentially a full-synergy merger-success scenario and the low case (~$239, ~18% below) a deal-failure or hard-conditions scenario. That symmetry is the point: the current price already discounts a favorable-but-not-perfect merger outcome. The dividend yield is ~1.9% (recently raised to $1.38/quarter), thin support relative to the multiple.

We are deliberately skeptical of treating the ~24x multiple as "cheap" simply because it is below a mismatched Industrials median. Growth is mid-single-digit organic; the multiple is not obviously mispriced downward, and the buyback pause has removed the per-share compounding that previously justified paying up. The estimate-revision signal is positive but far below the +30% threshold that would argue for a cycle-adjusted re-rating. This is a quality business at a full price, with the upside optionality (merger synergies) offset by the downside optionality (merger failure, leverage, dilution). The valuation score of 4.5 reflects a stretched-to-fair multiple with no independent catalyst pointing to near-term mispricing correction, and a meaningful risk that the embedded merger premium reverses.

## Competitive Landscape

UNP's competitive position is strong and, on the operating layer, improving. Freight-car velocity rose 8% in 2025 and 5-7% in 2026, terminal dwell hit record lows, and workforce productivity improved mid-single-digits. Vena's turnaround took OR from 62.3% (2023) toward 59.8%, best-in-class among Class I peers. The Western duopoly structure and irreplaceable network are durable moats.

The NS deal would reshape the industry. A UNP-NS transcontinental carrier would be the first coast-to-coast single-line operator (~50,000 miles, 43 states), likely pressuring BNSF toward a defensive CSX combination. Whether this strengthens or complicates UNP's position depends entirely on the STB's conditions: competitive-access mandates or divestitures could dilute the very single-line advantage the deal is meant to create. The CN MOU secures a rival's support in exchange for competitive access, reducing opposition.

The competitive-position score was marked down from 8.5 (February) to 7.0 (June). We hold at 7.0. The markdown reflects the reality that the merger both concentrates market power (a positive for pricing) and introduces regulatory conditions that could impose competitive-access obligations (a partial offset), leaving the net trajectory less unambiguously improving than pre-announcement. The core franchise remains excellent.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant idiosyncratic risk is the NS transaction, and it is genuinely binary. The STB rejected the initial application as incomplete in January 2026, an unusual and adverse procedural signal. The refiled application (April 30) was accepted "in abeyance" pending supplemental information on May 28. The Board can impose "materially burdensome" conditions, and shippers, states, and Amtrak advocates are intervening. This is the first major Class I merger tested under the STB's tougher post-2001 "enhance competition" standard. A $2.5B termination fee applies under certain conditions if UNP walks.

Secondary risks: leverage escalation (~$20B new debt plus ~225M shares, new covenants, rating-downgrade risk); integration risk (neither party has attempted a combination of this scale); the buyback pause (only $26M repurchased in H1 2026 vs. $2.7B in H1 2025, removing the per-share tailwind and replacing it with dilution risk on close); fuel-price volatility (visible in Q2's fuel-driven OR miss); coal secular decline; and Mexico/tariff exposure that the 10-K notes is already dampening iron-ore volumes and international intermodal.

The risk-factor score of 4.5 reflects that the merger is a specific, dated, asymmetric event with material downside if it fails or draws heavy conditions, the kind of concentrated binary the market cannot fully price ahead of the ruling. This matches the trajectory markdown from 6.5 to 4.5 as the STB path proved rougher than initially assumed. The CN MOU improves approval odds but does not eliminate the binary.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 43.5% against 22.3% one-year historical volatility, rich, at the 76th percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (43.5% near-term vs. 24.7% at six months), a classic signal of an imminent catalyst. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.2%) and put/call volume and OI ratios are low (0.17 and 0.16), so this is not a directional bearish bet; it reads as event risk around earnings and merger headlines rather than a positioning tell. ATM IV rose from 31.0% to 43.5% as the stock climbed 17.3% since June 1, consistent with elevated uncertainty accompanying the rally. For a prospective buyer, the rich near-term IV argues against paying up for optionality here; for a holder, it confirms the market shares our view that the setup is event-driven.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$363, ~+24%):** The STB approves the NS deal on manageable conditions in 2027. The CN MOU and proposed small-railroad divestitures prove to be the template that clears remaining opposition. UNP integrates NS competently (Vena's operating credibility is the argument) and the transcontinental single-line network wins truck-competitive share, delivering synergies on top of a mid-single-digit organic algorithm. Buybacks resume post-close on the ~94M authorized shares. Fuel normalizes, restoring the OR. The stock re-rates to the high analyst target as synergy visibility improves.

**Bear Case (~$239, ~-18%):** The STB rejects the deal or attaches materially burdensome conditions (broad competitive-access mandates, forced divestitures) that gut the single-line rationale. UNP pays or absorbs friction near the termination fee, and the merger premium embedded in the current price unwinds toward standalone fair value. Absent the deal, UNP is a low-single-digit organic grower at ~24x, a full multiple with limited upside. Coal decline, Mexico tariff drag, and elevated fuel compound the derating.

**Base Case (~$308–313, roughly flat to +6%):** The deal grinds through 2027 with a mix of conditions and divestitures, approval more likely than not after the CN MOU but not certain, and the stock trades in a range around consensus targets while the regulatory process resolves. Organic earnings compound in the high-single-digits, offset by the paused buyback. The equity marks time until the STB rules.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (uncertain). The 12-month horizon is dominated by the STB process, which resolves in 2027 rather than within a clean 6-12 month window, one reason the setup is a hold rather than a directional bet.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- STB issues a favorable or clearly conditional-but-workable decision, or leading indicators (additional rival MOUs, DOJ/STB staff signals) push approval probability decisively higher, with the stock still below ~$280.
- A pullback to the low-$250s or below on merger-timeline noise while organic fundamentals (OR sub-60%, positive estimate revisions, intermodal momentum) remain intact, which would restore a margin of safety against a deal that ultimately clears.
- Resumption of the buyback (post-close) at prices that meaningfully compound per-share value.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- STB rejection, or conditions severe enough to eliminate the single-line synergy case, without the stock having already fully repriced.
- Adjusted debt/EBITDA guided materially above ~3.5x on close with rating-agency downgrade actions.
- Organic deterioration: OR sustained above ~61% ex-fuel, or current-year estimate revisions turning negative alongside volume declines beyond coal/international-intermodal normalization.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q3 2026 earnings release, subsequent STB procedural milestones (supplemental filings, evidentiary schedule), and any new competitive-access or divestiture agreements that shift approval odds. The definitive reassessment is the STB decision itself, expected in 2027.

## Conclusion

Union Pacific is a superbly-run franchise: a Western duopolist with industry-leading operating metrics, strong cash generation, and management that has delivered on operating-ratio improvement. On operating fundamentals alone, this would be an easy quality hold, arguably a buy on any dislocation. But the equity is now a levered bet on a regulatory outcome. At ~24x trailing earnings below its 52-week high, the price embeds a favorable NS merger resolution, while the outcome remains binary, a year-plus out, and subject to the STB's most rigorous merger standard in a generation. The CN MOU converts a low-probability approval into a plausible one, not a certainty.

The overall score has declined from 6.8 (February) to 5.6 (June), driven by markdowns in risk, competitive position, and management quality as the merger path proved rougher than first assumed. Our current 5.7 confirms that trajectory has stabilized: the operating business is if anything stronger (record Q2, raised guidance), but the valuation offers no cushion and the dominant variable is unresolvable at present. This has been a HOLD across three prior reports; we maintain it, on the specific observation that risk/reward is symmetric around consensus targets, with meaningful upside on approval and meaningful downside on rejection, and the current price fairly splitting the difference. We would become buyers on a pullback that restores margin of safety, or on decisive evidence that approval probability has moved materially higher.
