## Executive Summary

Noble Corporation is one of the three largest offshore drilling contractors globally, operating 25 floaters and 5 ultra-harsh jackups following its 2024 Diamond Offshore acquisition and recent jackup divestiture program. The stock has rallied 135% over the past twelve months from ~$21.74 to ~$51, driven by the Iran-conflict-fueled surge in Brent, offshore supply tightness, and a $7.5B backlog providing multi-year visibility. At the current price, the stock trades at the analyst consensus median of $50.50 and approximately 10x guided 2026 EV/EBITDA — fair for a cyclically exposed driller at what management frames as a "transitional year."

The bull case centers on a 2027 earnings recovery supported by backlog that already exceeds 2026 levels, structural supply constraints in deepwater rigs, and sustained elevated crude prices. The bear case rests on organic deterioration — floater dayrates down 5.7% YoY, utilization declining, 3 of 4 quarterly earnings misses, broad insider selling, and $2B of 8.0–8.5% coupon debt acquired at what may prove to have been the cycle peak. The evidence is genuinely mixed. Neither the bullish nor bearish thesis dominates with sufficient conviction to warrant a directional recommendation.

## Company Overview

Noble Corporation emerged from Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 2021 and has since pursued an aggressive consolidation strategy, completing the Maersk Drilling combination in 2022 and the Diamond Offshore acquisition in September 2024. The resulting fleet of 30 rigs (25 floaters, 5 ultra-harsh jackups post-divestiture) positions Noble as a Tier-1 deepwater drilling contractor.

Revenue is earned through dayrate contracts, primarily with supermajor customers: ExxonMobil (23.7% of backlog), Shell (19.5%), BP (16.2%), and TotalEnergies (12.6%). Geographic exposure skews toward the US Gulf (35.3% of backlog), Guyana (23.7%), Suriname (12.9%), and the North Sea (11.4%). The company is incorporated in England & Wales with headquarters in Houston.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

2025 revenue of $3.286B grew 7% YoY, but this is almost entirely attributable to the Diamond acquisition adding approximately $626M in floater revenue. Organic trends are deteriorating:

- Floater average dayrate: $402,703 (down 5.7% from $427,192)
- Floater utilization: 67% (down from 69%)
- Jackup utilization: 64% (down from 77%)
- Jackup operating days: 2,911 (down 21%)

Net income collapsed 52% to $217M ($448M in 2024) despite higher revenue. The margin compression is driven by three factors: drilling services costs rose 14% to $1.92B, D&A surged 37% to $585M on the larger asset base, and interest expense nearly doubled to $162M from Diamond's incremental debt. An $82.7M impairment charge on three rigs added to the pressure.

### Cash Flow

For a D&A-heavy offshore driller, P/E is a misleading indicator. The critical metric is cash flow generation:

- **2025 OCF**: $952M — strong, 4.4x net income, reflecting the capital-intensive business model
- **2025 FCF**: $432M (13.2% margin) on an $8B market cap = **~5.4% FCF yield**
- **2026 guided EBITDA**: $940M–$1,020M against $590–640M capex guidance implies FCF of $300–430M, or **~3.7–5.4% FCF yield**
- **Dividend**: $2.00/share annually (~4% yield), well covered by FCF

The FCF yield combined with the dividend yield provides meaningful downside support. Even at the low end of 2026 guidance, FCF covers the $320M annual dividend commitment.

### Balance Sheet

The Diamond acquisition tripled debt from ~$600M to ~$2B:
- $1.4B 8.000% Senior Notes due 2030
- $550M 8.500% Senior Secured Second Lien Notes due 2030
- $550M undrawn revolving credit facility (maturity April 2028)

Net debt is approximately $1.5B. Net Debt/guided midpoint EBITDA is ~1.5x — moderate, not stressed. The Altman Z-Score of 2.46 ("grey zone") reflects the post-acquisition leverage increase and compressed EBIT margins. Current ratio of 1.67 is adequate.

Annual interest expense of ~$160M is a permanent drag on net income at these coupon rates, but manageable relative to OCF of $952M.

### Earnings Trajectory

The earnings surprise history is concerning: 3 misses in 4 quarters, with the June 2025 quarter missing by 76.3%. The Q1 2026 report (April 27, 2026) showed a narrow 3% beat — the first positive surprise in four quarters. Estimate revisions are mixed:

- Current quarter: revised **up 47%** over 30 days (from $0.17 to $0.24), and **up 106%** over 90 days (from $0.12 to $0.24) — the most positive signal in the dataset
- Next quarter: revised **down 31%** over 30 days
- Current year (FY2026): revised down 6.4% over 30 days, down 9.7% over 90 days ($1.17 → $1.06)
- Next year (FY2027): revised down 8.2% over 90 days ($2.70 → $2.48)

The current-quarter upward revision reflects the market incorporating Q1 results and adjusting for jackup divestitures. The downward pressure on FY2026 and FY2027 estimates is the more structurally relevant trend, though the magnitude of decline has moderated.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term Headwinds (2026)

Management explicitly calls 2026 "a transitional year from an earnings perspective." Revenue guidance of $2.8–3.0B represents a 9–15% decline from 2025's $3.286B, driven by jackup divestitures ($424M sold) and continued dayrate moderation. Floater average dayrates are likely to remain under pressure as new fixtures in the $375–400K/day range replace contracts signed at higher rates.

### 2027 Recovery Thesis

The most important forward-looking data point is that **2027 backlog already exceeds 2026 levels**. The $7.5B total backlog represents 2.5x TTM revenue. Specific contract awards provide concrete support:

- Aker BP Noble GreatWhite: $473M, 3-year contract
- ExxonMobil CEA: extended to February 2029 for four drillships
- ExxonMobil Nigeria: $292M, 2-year contract
- Petrobras Noble Courage: 3-year extension
- BP Trinidad Noble Developer: $375K/day

These awards, totaling $565M in Q1 2026 alone and $1.3B in Q4 2025, provide tangible evidence that the 2027 recovery is building on contracted commitments from creditworthy counterparties. This is the strongest argument against a purely bearish view.

### Structural Supply Dynamics

Global deepwater rig supply is constrained. Fleet retirements have reduced available capacity from historic highs, and newbuild construction remains minimal. If Brent crude sustains at elevated levels (the base case in the current geopolitical environment), deepwater project economics remain attractive, and rig demand should recover. Noble's CEO previously projected that deepwater dayrates could reach $600K/day as the market tightens — that thesis is being tested, but the supply side of the equation remains supportive.

## Valuation Assessment

P/E is the wrong primary metric for this business. At $50.33 with trailing EPS of $1.35, the 35.7x trailing P/E reflects massive D&A charges ($585M) that depress GAAP earnings relative to cash generation.

**EV/EBITDA** is the appropriate anchor:
- Enterprise value: ~$8B market cap + ~$1.5B net debt = ~$9.5B
- 2026 guided EBITDA midpoint: $980M
- **EV/EBITDA: ~9.7x**

Peer comparison:
- **Valaris (VAL)**: 7.5x trailing P/E (different capital structure and D&A profile make P/E comparison misleading)
- **Patterson-UTI (PTEN)**: Negative P/E, 3.4% yield — primarily onshore land driller, limited comparability
- **Helmerich & Payne (HP)**: Negative P/E, 2.5% yield — also primarily onshore

Among pure offshore peers, Valaris trades at a discount, reflecting Noble's larger scale and contract backlog advantage. Noble's ~10x EV/EBITDA is fair for a cycle-trough year with visible 2027 improvement.

**FCF yield**: ~5.4% on 2025 actuals, ~3.7–5.4% on 2026 guidance. Combined with the ~4% dividend yield, this provides meaningful return even if the stock trades sideways.

The analyst consensus: mean target $49.20, median $50.50, high $59.00, low $32.00. At $50.33, the stock sits at the median consensus target. The Street views this as fairly valued — 2 buy, 8 hold, 0 sell.

## Competitive Landscape

Noble's competitive position is improving structurally through consolidation. The Maersk Drilling (2022) and Diamond Offshore (2024) acquisitions created the fleet scale and geographic diversity to compete for the largest supermajor programs. The jackup divestiture sharpens the portfolio toward highest-specification deepwater assets where barriers to entry are highest.

Key competitive advantages:
- Fleet scale and specification (25 floaters, predominantly 7th generation)
- Multi-year relationships with ExxonMobil, Shell, BP, TotalEnergies, Petrobras
- $7.5B contracted backlog providing revenue visibility
- Post-consolidation industry structure with fewer competitors

Key competitive concerns:
- Dayrate trajectory is negative (down 5.7% YoY)
- Utilization declining despite favorable oil prices
- Customer concentration risk (72% of backlog from 4 customers)
- UK North Sea regulatory tightening reducing activity in a key geography

## Risk Assessment

### Idiosyncratic Risks

**1. Dayrate and Utilization Deterioration (HIGH):** The 10-K states "the near-term outlook for both floaters and jackups over the next several quarters continues to present lingering utilization headwinds compared to 2023-2024 levels." If floater dayrates decline from $400K to $350K/day, the impact on EBITDA would be substantial — roughly $150–200M annually across the fleet.

**2. Customer Concentration (MODERATE-HIGH):** 72% of backlog from 4 customers. The ExxonMobil CEA reprices semi-annually to market rates, meaning dayrate declines flow through directly. Any reduction in ExxonMobil's Guyana program would have outsized impact.

**3. Integration Execution (MODERATE):** Diamond's 11 floaters are still being operationally integrated. The $82.7M impairment on three rigs suggests some acquired assets are marginal. Further impairments are possible per management's own warning.

**4. Leverage from Diamond Acquisition (MODERATE):** $2B of 8.0–8.5% coupon debt is expensive. At current rates, refinancing at lower coupons is unlikely. The ~$160M annual interest expense is manageable (1.7x coverage on current OCF) but becomes stressed if OCF declines by 40%+.

**5. Geopolitical (MODERATE):** Operations in Guyana, Nigeria, and North Sea create multi-theater geopolitical exposure. The Q1 2026 call referenced Iran conflict-related operational disruption.

**6. Insider Selling (MODERATE):** Every insider transaction in recent data is a sale. CEO Eifler sold ~$4.7M, CFO Barker sold ~$7.9M in Feb-March 2026. While largely vesting-driven, the breadth and volume are notable. No open-market purchases from any insider.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV of 43.2% vs. 41.0% historical vol is roughly fair (2.2pp premium). The term structure is in mild backwardation (43.2% near-term vs. 40.0% 6-month), consistent with modest near-term uncertainty rather than extreme stress. Put/call OI ratio of 0.20 and volume ratio of 0.23 are heavily call-skewed, suggesting directional bullish positioning. IV has collapsed 21pp since mid-April (from 64.3% to 43.2%), coinciding with the post-Q1 earnings de-risking. The options market is not signaling significant downside concern at current levels.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($59–65, +17–29%)
- Brent sustains at elevated levels, deepwater economics improve, dayrates stabilize and inflect upward into 2027
- 2027 EBITDA approaches $1.2–1.3B as backlog converts at improving rates
- At 8–9x forward EV/EBITDA on $1.2B+ EBITDA, equity value reaches $59–65
- Structural supply constraints manifest as the limited newbuild pipeline intersects growing deepwater demand
- Jackup divestiture and fleet focus drive margin improvement

### Bear Case ($32–38, –24–36%)
- Diplomatic resolution of Iran conflict sends Brent lower, collapsing deepwater project economics
- Dayrates continue declining toward $350K/day; utilization stays below 65%
- 2026 EBITDA comes in at or below $940M guidance floor; 2027 recovery fails to materialize
- Further impairments on Diamond-acquired rigs
- At 7–8x bear-case EBITDA of $850M, EV collapses to ~$6–6.8B, equity to $28–33 per share
- FCF compresses, dividend sustainability questioned

### Base Case ($49–55, –3% to +9%)
- 2026 plays out as the "transitional year" management describes, with EBITDA near $980M midpoint
- Dayrates stabilize in the $380–410K range; utilization improves modestly in H2 2026
- 2027 backlog conversion begins showing up in forward estimates
- Stock trades near 9.5–10x forward EBITDA, broadly range-bound with dividend yield as the primary return component

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. Two consecutive quarters of floater dayrate increases above $410K/day, signaling cycle turn
2. FY2027 consensus EPS estimates revised upward by >10% from current $2.48 level
3. Stock pulls back to $40–42 (≤8x guided EBITDA), providing asymmetric upside to 2027 recovery

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. Floater utilization falls below 60% for two consecutive quarters
2. Management cuts the dividend or suspends the buyback authorization entirely
3. 2026 EBITDA tracks below $900M, signaling the trough is deeper than guided

### Reassessment Timeline
- **Q2 2026 earnings** (late July / early August): Key data point on dayrate trajectory and 2027 backlog conversion

## Conclusion

Noble Corporation faces a period where the near-term data (declining dayrates, earnings misses, insider selling) conflicts with forward-looking indicators ($7.5B backlog with 2027 exceeding 2026, healthy FCF generation, structural supply constraints, elevated oil prices). The stock has already priced in meaningful recovery from its $21.51 low, and now trades precisely at analyst consensus.

At ~10x guided EV/EBITDA with a ~4% dividend yield and ~5% FCF yield, the valuation is fair for the current risk profile. The 2027 recovery thesis has concrete support in backlog data but remains conditional on sustained crude prices and dayrate stabilization. The HOLD rating reflects this genuine uncertainty: the stock is not cheap enough to buy with conviction, and the forward indicators, particularly the backlog composition, are too constructive to recommend avoiding.

The path to a BUY recommendation runs through either (a) a price pullback to $40–42 that creates asymmetric upside, or (b) visible dayrate improvement confirming the cycle has turned. The path to an AVOID would require evidence that the 2027 backlog is being renegotiated at lower rates or that utilization is in structural decline.
