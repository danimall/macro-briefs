**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Clorox is a high-quality consumer-staples franchise experiencing a genuine earnings downcycle layered on top of self-inflicted operational disruption. Roughly 80% of sales sit in #1 or #2 share positions across midsized cleaning, household, and food categories, which historically supports pricing power and ~45% gross margins. That franchise quality is intact. Near-term earnings momentum is not: management cut FY2026 organic sales guidance to -9% (the bottom of the prior -5% to -9% range), nine-month gross margin compressed 200bps to 42.7%, and consensus current-quarter EPS estimates have been cut 20.7% in the past 30 days. The combination of falling estimates and a multi-quarter pattern of guidance disappointment is the dominant signal.

The balance sheet is being stretched at the worst possible time. Three large cash events collided in FY2026: a $476M payment to P&G to acquire the remaining Glad venture interest, the ~$2.25B debt-financed GOJO/Purell acquisition that closed April 1, 2026, and continued buybacks ($254M) plus dividends ($452M). Nine-month operating cash flow fell to $282M from $687M, commercial paper spiked to $1.59B, and book equity swung to a -$67M deficit. The headline Altman Z-Score of 1.33 and 15.8x debt/equity are capital-structure artifacts of decades of buybacks rather than operational distress, and investment-grade ratings remain intact. But layering $3.6B+ of incremental gross debt onto a franchise with declining organic sales raises refinancing and rating-agency risk that did not exist a year ago.

The bull case rests on a ~5% dividend yield near an 11-year low, imminent Dividend King status in 2027, a completed (if messy) SAP ERP transition, and GOJO adding away-from-home hygiene growth. The bear case is more grounded in current data: chronic guidance misses, five-year cumulative TSR of 63.4 versus a peer group at 137.2, four years of flat-to-declining revenue, rising leverage into a softening top line, and a debt-funded acquisition of a category (hand sanitizer) facing post-COVID normalization. At ~16x trailing earnings on falling forward estimates, with the yield providing only partial downside support, the asymmetry does not favor ownership. We rate CLX **AVOID** and detail below why the stock is not a viable short despite the deteriorating fundamentals.

## Company Overview

Clorox manufactures and markets consumer and professional products across ~25 countries with sales in ~100 markets and roughly 7,600 employees. The portfolio is organized into four reportable segments (nine-month FY2026 mix):

- **Health and Wellness (~38%)** — Clorox bleach, disinfecting wipes, Pine-Sol, Tilex, Liquid-Plumr, Formula 409, plus CloroxPro/Clorox Healthcare professional lines. This is the highest-EBIT segment (~$472M nine-month segment EBIT).
- **Household (~27-29%)** — Glad bags and wraps, Fresh Step/Scoop Away cat litter, Kingsford charcoal.
- **Lifestyle (~16-18%)** — Hidden Valley dressings, Brita filtration, Burt's Bees personal care. This is the weakest segment, down 13% over nine months.
- **International (~17%)** — Clorox, Poett, Clorinda, Glad, Brita abroad.

The competitive moat rests on brand equity, shelf placement, distribution scale in mass and club channels, and trusted reputation in cleaning and disinfecting. Geographic concentration is high (86% of FY2025 sales were domestic), and these are mature, high-penetration categories with structurally limited organic growth.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue has been flat-to-declining for four years.** Annual revenue: FY2022 $7.11B, FY2023 $7.39B, FY2024 $7.09B, FY2025 $7.10B. Portfolio pruning (Argentina, the Better Health VMS business sold at a $118M loss) has offset modest pricing, leaving the top line essentially stagnant.

**FY2025 was a recovery year that is now reversing.** Net income rebounded to $810M (11.6% net margin) from $280M in FY2024 and $149M in FY2023, when results were depressed by the August 2023 cyberattack, inflation, and restructuring. Gross profit reached $3.21B (45.2% gross margin) as commodity costs normalized.

**FY2026 is deteriorating across the board:**
- Nine-month net sales -7% to $4.77B, with volume and net sales both down ~6-7%. A meaningful portion is the reversal of pull-forward shipments made into Q4 FY2025 ahead of the ERP cutover, a timing distortion rather than pure demand loss, but management's decision to cut organic guidance confirms real underlying softness.
- Nine-month gross margin compressed on higher manufacturing and logistics costs against lower volume.
- Nine-month diluted EPS -10% to $3.47. Q3 EPS rose 3% to $1.54 on cost savings and reduced advertising/SG&A, but trimming brand investment to protect near-term EPS is not a durable growth lever.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY2025 produced $981M operating cash flow and $761M FCF (10.7% FCF margin), a normal year for an asset-light staples model. The nine-month FY2026 operating cash flow decline was driven primarily by the $476M Glad payment to P&G on March 2, 2026.

Book equity swung from $321M (6/30/25) to its current deficit (3/31/26), the cumulative product of buybacks and dividends exceeding retained earnings. The Z-Score and debt/equity figures materially overstate distress: they are artifacts of a near-zero equity denominator, not operating weakness. The Piotroski F-score of 4/5 (the single fail being current ratio <1, normal for a negative-working-capital staples model) better captures underlying quality. Investment-grade ratings are covenant-critical because a downgrade below IG would trigger derivative collateral calls.

The genuine concern is incremental leverage. Notes and loans payable jumped to $1.59B in commercial paper at 3/31/26 to fund GOJO and the P&G payment, on top of $2.49B long-term debt. Post-close, Clorox drew $1.25B from a delayed-draw term loan and plans to refinance into long-term debt. The current ratio is 0.84, with current liabilities exceeding current assets by $504M.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are weak on every available forward indicator. Estimate revisions are the strongest tell, and they are uniformly negative: current-quarter EPS consensus has been cut 20.7% in 30 days (from $2.10 to $1.66), next-quarter is down 9.4% over 90 days, current-year is down 5.9% over 90 days, and next-year is down 7.6% over 90 days. The 30-day acceleration in the current-quarter cut is the opposite of the trough pattern that would support a constructive call.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed and decelerating: the prior four quarters were +28.7%, +7.0%, -4.0%, and +4.7%. The large FY2025 Q4 beat reflected pull-forward shipments that are now unwinding, so it overstates underlying momentum.

The GOJO/Purell acquisition is the principal organic-growth lever management is leaning on. The strategic logic of extending hygiene into the away-from-home and professional channels alongside CloroxPro is reasonable, but Purell hand-sanitizer demand is post-COVID normalized and faces difficult comps. A deal in a normalizing category, debt-financed into a declining top line, is a bet rather than a confirmed accelerant. The Q4 FY2026 print (the first reporting period to include any GOJO contribution, as the deal closed after Q3 ended), which will also absorb a flagged $20-25M oil headwind, is unlikely to reverse the negative revision trajectory.

## Valuation Assessment

CLX trades at ~16x trailing EPS of $6.52 and ~17.4x forward on the current-year $5.56 consensus, but that consensus is falling, so the effective forward multiple is creeping higher as estimates come down. Against household-products peers (median P/E 15.0), Clorox sits modestly above the median: REYN 15.0, IPAR 19.0, CENTA 13.5, ENR 7.1, with WDFC the outlier at 38.0. There is no obvious discount to exploit; CLX is priced like or above an average peer while delivering below-peer growth and carrying above-peer leverage and integration risk.

This is not a cyclical-trough mispricing where trailing P/E understates normalized earnings power. The opposite is closer to true: FY2025's $810M net income was a recovery peak that FY2026 is walking back, so trailing earnings overstate forward power. Applying the cycle-adjustment principle in reverse, the appropriate multiple should be applied to a falling, not rising, estimate base.

The ~5% dividend yield reflects the depressed share price more than dividend strength. With 48 consecutive years of increases and Dividend King status due in 2027, a cut is unlikely, but coverage is thinning: nine-month dividends against operating cash flow were only sustainable because of one-time items, and the payout will need FCF to normalize back toward the FY2025 $761M level to remain comfortably covered alongside rising debt service.

The mean analyst target of $105.29 (median $97.00) sits modestly above the current price, with a wide $83-$163 range reflecting genuine disagreement between the value-trap and turnaround camps. The high target is a stale outlier; the median essentially says "fairly valued." Given falling estimates and the multiple offering no discount, we assign a below-average valuation score. The yield prevents a deeply low score, but there is no specific reason the market is mispricing this stock to the upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Clorox's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating rather than collapsing. The franchise sits in defensible #1/#2 positions, and management reported that private-label share held flat in Q3 FY2026, with consumers still favoring trusted brands and "value, not just lowest price." That claim is plausible given the share positions, but it coexists with intense competitor promotion and discounting, most acute in Food (Hidden Valley) and the broader Lifestyle segment.

Structurally, the company faces larger, better-resourced competitors (P&G, Kimberly-Clark, Church & Dwight, Reckitt), persistent retailer power (Walmart at 27-28% of sales, top-five customers ~half, with no long-term contracts), and post-COVID normalization in cleaning and disinfecting. Motley Fool commentary in 2026 has repeatedly framed P&G and Kimberly-Clark as the "better buys," with Clorox the relative laggard, consistent with the five-year TSR gap. The GOJO deal is a credible attempt to lean into the secular away-from-home hygiene tailwind, which is the clearest positive in the competitive picture, but it does not change the mature-category reality of the core portfolio.

## Risk Assessment

**GOJO integration and leverage (most material, newly elevated).** A debt-financed acquisition among Clorox's largest, closing into a weak organic-sales environment. Synergy realization, integration execution, and rating-agency action are front-and-center. An IG downgrade would trigger derivative collateral calls and strain supply-chain financing.

**ERP transition fallout.** The ~$580M, five-year SAP cloud ERP went live in Q3 FY2026 after a transition publicly described as "extremely messy," distorting shipments across two fiscal years and creating internal-control change risk. Forecasting visibility remains impaired.

**Customer concentration.** Walmart at 27-28% of sales, top five ~half, all order-by-order with no long-term contracts. Retailer pricing technology and private-label shelf decisions are persistent threats.

**Commodity/tariff.** Resin, sodium hypochlorite, soybean oil, and jet fuel exposure; management flagged a Q4 oil headwind, alongside ongoing tariff uncertainty.

**Cybersecurity.** The August 2023 cyberattack materially affected results; the insurance recoveries booked in FY2025 ($70M in nine-month FY2025) are now lapping, creating tough YoY comps.

**Offsetting positives.** The Glad/P&G repurchase obligation is now resolved (Clorox retains the core IP licenses royalty-free), and the Z-Score distress flag is a false alarm. These do not outweigh the leverage-into-weakness profile.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is mildly cautious but not flashing acute stress. Near-term ATM IV of 32.9% is modestly rich versus 28.6% one-year historical volatility, but sits at only the 11th percentile within the current chain, and IV has collapsed from 62.7% to 32.9% since mid-May as the post-earnings event premium bled off. The 1-week put/call skew of 4.1% indicates elevated near-term put demand and a put/call volume ratio of 1.61, consistent with hedging into the next print, while the put/call OI ratio of 0.51 is benign. The flat term structure (near 32.9% versus far 34.5%) shows uncertainty evenly distributed rather than concentrated on a single binary event. The market is mildly hedged but not pricing a collapse, consistent with our AVOID-not-short conclusion.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** CLX is a Dividend King-in-waiting (2027) yielding ~5% near an 11-year low, with the ERP transition behind it as an efficiency enabler, GOJO adding away-from-home hygiene growth, and stable private-label dynamics. Much of the sales decline is an accounting artifact of pull-forward reversal, so underlying demand is softer-but-not-broken. A finance-literate director bought 5,000 shares at $85.82 near the bottom. If organic sales stabilize and GOJO accretes, the stock re-rates toward peers and the yield compresses. Bull target ~$120 (roughly 19x a recovered ~$6.30 next-year EPS, near the prior valuation range).

**Bear case.** Chronic guidance misses, front-end estimates falling sharply, five-year TSR roughly half the peer group, four years of flat revenue, and a debt-funded acquisition of a normalizing hand-sanitizer category into a weakening top line. Book equity is negative, commercial paper has spiked, and the Q4 oil headwind plus difficult cyber-recovery and pull-forward comps point to another disappointing print in early August. Bear target ~$80 (~14.5x a further-trimmed ~$5.50 EPS, near the 52-week low), with downside extending if a rating-agency action materializes.

The weight of current evidence (falling estimates, deteriorating cash flow, rising leverage, and below-peer growth at a peer-level multiple) favors the bear case over the next two to three quarters. The yield caps but does not eliminate downside.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The clearest dated catalyst is the Q4 FY2026 earnings report in early August 2026, which will print against the organic guidance, absorb the oil headwind, lap easier cyber-recovery and pull-forward comps, and include the first weeks of GOJO contribution. The setup favors a continuation of negative revisions, but the magnitude of any miss versus an already-lowered bar is uncertain, since much of the bad news is guided.

**Timing window.** ~8 weeks to the August print. That is within a reasonable carry window, but the catalyst is already partially discounted given the guidance cut and the drawdown from the 52-week high.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 8.8% of float with 2.5 days to cover, uncrowded and easy to cover. This means low squeeze risk but also that informed sellers are not piling in, consistent with a "known-weak, fully-disclosed" situation rather than an undiscovered short.

**Borrow and options signal.** Borrow is unlikely to be expensive at <9% SI. The options market shows only mild near-term put skew with IV at the 11th percentile of its own chain: positioning is cautious but not confirming an aggressive bearish thesis, and IV has already deflated. This is a warning sign for a fresh short, as the easy downside has been captured.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Downside to ~$80 (bear case) is roughly -19% from $98.30; upside risk from a yield-supported bounce, a guidance-beat-against-low-bar, or M&A/activist interest could be +15-20%. That is roughly 1:1, below the 2:1 threshold required to justify the carry and the asymmetry of shorting a 5%-yielding Dividend King near multi-decade-low valuation.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamentals are weak and the next catalyst is bearish-leaning, but the thesis is largely consensus and already discounted: the stock has fallen materially from its high, short interest is light, the dividend yield provides a floor, and the bear/bull asymmetry is roughly symmetric. This is a stock to avoid owning, not to actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.** Headwinds (declining organic sales, falling estimates, leverage into a softening top line, and ERP/GOJO execution risk) are likely to persist through at least the next two to three quarters (mid-2026 into the FY2027 setup).

**Re-entry criteria (would move us to HOLD or constructive):**
- Organic sales stabilizing to flat-or-better for two consecutive quarters, confirming the -9% is the trough rather than a way station.
- A halt and reversal in EPS estimate revisions, specifically current-year consensus stabilizing or rising over a 60-90 day window rather than the current -5.9% 90-day trend.
- Evidence of GOJO accretion with maintained IG ratings and commercial paper being termed out into long-term debt at manageable cost.
- A valuation reset to a clear discount to peers (forward P/E below ~13x on credible estimates) that would compensate for the leverage and execution risk.

**Timeline.** We expect the headwinds to persist 6-12 months. The next natural reassessment point is the Q4 FY2026 earnings report in early August 2026, which will test whether organic sales and estimates are bottoming or continuing lower.

## Conclusion

Clorox is a good franchise going through a bad stretch at a leveraged moment. The brand portfolio, #1/#2 share positions, and ~5% yield are real, and the headline distress signals (Z-Score, debt/equity) are capital-structure artifacts that overstate solvency risk. But the forward-looking data is uniformly negative: organic guidance cut to -9%, current-quarter estimates cut 20.7% in a month, gross margins compressing, operating cash flow declining under one-time outflows, and a debt-financed acquisition of a normalizing category closing into a declining top line. The stock trades at or above a peer-level multiple on falling estimates, with the dividend yield offering only partial downside protection.

This is the fourth consecutive cautious call on CLX, and the trajectory has been correctly negative: scores have declined alongside deteriorating fundamentals, and the price has followed. There is no identifiable near-term growth catalyst, only a debt-funded acquisition into weakness and a guidance path pointing lower, so a HOLD justified by a 12-month growth thesis is not supportable. The call is **AVOID** until organic sales and estimates demonstrably bottom. The stock is not a viable short: the bear thesis is consensus, the drawdown is well advanced, short interest is light, and the yield provides a floor that compresses the remaining downside.
