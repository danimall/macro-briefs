## Executive Summary

BioMarin Pharmaceutical is a rare disease biotech generating $3.2B in FY2025 revenue (+13% YoY) with $725M in free cash flow (22.5% FCF margin) and strong pipeline optionality through VOXZOGO's indication expansion. The stock trades at $50, down 25% from its 52-week high and roughly flat vs. 5 years ago despite 74% cumulative revenue growth since 2021. At ~10x FY2026E earnings ($5.02 consensus) and ~2.5x FY2026E revenue (including Amicus), the valuation is compressed vs. profitable biotech peers.

The fundamental problem preventing a higher-conviction call: BioMarin has missed earnings in 3 of the last 4 quarters (Q3 FY2025 missed by 142%, Q4 by 138%, Q1 FY2026 by 30%). This pattern, combined with the $4.8B all-cash Amicus acquisition that adds ~$4.3B in new debt to a previously fortress balance sheet, means the market requires proof that management can execute before it re-rates the stock. The valuation discount is real but so is the earnings visibility problem.

## Company Overview

BioMarin develops and commercializes therapies for genetically defined rare diseases. The company has 8 commercial products (ROCTAVIAN withdrawn Dec 2025) led by VOXZOGO ($927M, achondroplasia), VIMIZIM ($792M, MPS IVA), NAGLAZYME ($485M, MPS VI), and PALYNZIQ ($433M, PKU). The company closed its $4.8B acquisition of Amicus Therapeutics on April 27, 2026, adding Galafold (Fabry disease) and Pombiliti + Opfolda (Pompe disease) to the portfolio. Post-close, BioMarin guides FY2026 revenue of $3.825-3.925B.

The pipeline centers on VOXZOGO lifecycle management: Phase 3 in hypochondroplasia, Phase 2 in idiopathic short stature, Noonan syndrome, Turner syndrome, and SHOX deficiency. BMN 333 (longer-acting CNP analog) is in Phase 1 with PK data exceeding target exposure. BMN 351 targets Duchenne muscular dystrophy (Phase 1/2, 5% dystrophin expression).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Growth:**
FY2025 revenues reached $3.22B (+12.9% YoY), driven by VOXZOGO (+26%) and PALYNZIQ (+22%). KUVAN declined 18% on generic competition. FY2026 guidance of $3.825-3.925B implies ~20% growth, substantially boosted by Amicus (which contributes roughly 8 months post-close). Organic growth likely in the 12-14% range.

**Profitability:**
Gross margins are excellent at 77.7%. Operating margin was depressed to 12.7% in FY2025 by ~$240M in ROCTAVIAN restructuring charges and a $221M Inozyme IPR&D write-off. Normalized operating margins are improving and management targets 35%+ GAAP operating margins by 2027-2028.

**Cash Flow:**
Operating cash flow of $828M (+44% YoY) and FCF of $725M represent the strongest cash generation in company history. CapEx is minimal at $103M. This FCF capacity is the primary backstop for the new debt load.

**Balance Sheet Transformation:**
Pre-Amicus: $2.05B cash, $600M debt (convertible notes due 2027), net cash position. Post-Amicus: ~$4.3B total debt ($850M 5.5% notes due 2034, ~$2.8B term loan, plus existing converts). Pro forma net debt of ~$2.2-2.5B. Annual interest expense increases from $11M to an estimated $200M+. Net leverage roughly 2.5-3.0x EBITDA — manageable but a meaningful departure from historic capital structure.

**Key Concern:** At 30Y Treasury yields above 5.1%, carrying $4.3B in debt at blended rates likely around 5-5.5% creates roughly $220-240M in annual interest expense. This reduces the FCF available for deleveraging from $725M to approximately $500-520M, implying a 4-5 year deleveraging timeline to return to pre-deal leverage.

## Growth Analysis

VOXZOGO is the primary growth driver with multiple expansion vectors:
1. **Achondroplasia penetration:** ~29,000 patients globally; ~3,500 currently on therapy. Penetration headroom of 5-8x remaining.
2. **Hypochondroplasia (Phase 3):** 2-3x more prevalent than achondroplasia. Approval could double VOXZOGO's TAM.
3. **Other growth disorders (Phase 2):** ISS, Noonan, Turner, SHOX collectively represent very large patient populations.
4. **BMN 333:** Longer-acting successor provides lifecycle management and competitive defense.

Amicus adds two growing franchises: Galafold (oral Fabry therapy with convenience advantage vs. infused ERTs) and Pombiliti + Opfolda (next-gen Pompe therapy). Combined, these should add $600-800M in annual revenue.

FY2026E consensus of $5.02 EPS and FY2027E of $6.68 implies 33% earnings growth year-over-year, driven by revenue scale, margin expansion (ROCTAVIAN costs eliminated, overhead leverage), and Amicus contribution.

## Valuation Assessment

At $50.02 per share:
- **Forward P/E (FY2026E):** 10.0x on $5.02 EPS
- **Forward P/E (FY2027E):** 7.5x on $6.68 EPS
- **EV/Revenue (FY2026E):** ~3.3x (including pro forma debt)
- **Price/FCF (FY2025):** 13.4x (pre-incremental interest)

Peer context: EXEL trades at 16.5x, HALO at 23.7x, KRYS at 39.1x. BioMarin's 10x forward P/E represents a 58% discount to the profitable biotech peer median of ~24x. Even adjusting for the new leverage and recent miss history, a 15-18x multiple on $5.02 would imply $75-90, consistent with the analyst consensus median of $86.

The market is pricing BMRN as if the recent miss pattern will persist and Amicus integration will impair earnings. If BioMarin delivers 2 consecutive quarters at or above consensus (Q2 and Q3 FY2026), the re-rating path to $70-85 is straightforward: 14-17x normalized forward earnings.

**Why the market may be wrong:** The Q1 miss was driven by a $31M one-time NAGLAZYME manufacturing charge and lumpy government ordering patterns (explicitly acknowledged in the 10-K). Management raised full-year guidance despite the Q1 miss, suggesting back-half loading rather than structural shortfall. The Q3/Q4 FY2025 misses were contaminated by ROCTAVIAN restructuring charges that are now fully behind the company.

**Why the market may be right:** Three misses in four quarters is a pattern, regardless of the explanations. Credibility rebuilding takes time. The Amicus integration adds operational complexity precisely when execution must be flawless.

## Competitive Landscape

BioMarin's rare disease portfolio benefits from limited competition in most indications. Enzyme replacement therapies for MPS IVA, MPS VI, and CLN2 have no approved alternatives. The primary competitive threats are:

1. **VOXZOGO:** Ascendis Pharma (TransCon CNP, Phase 3), BridgeBio (infigratinib, later stage), Tyra Biosciences. BioMarin is actively litigating against Ascendis on patent grounds. Competition is 2-3 years from market at earliest.

2. **Galafold (Fabry):** Oral drug competing against infused ERTs (Fabrazyme, Replagal) with convenience advantage. Migalastat (oral) for amenable mutations gives structural market share gains.

3. **Biosimilar risk:** NAGLAZYME patents expired Nov 2023. VIMIZIM key patents expire 2029-2032. The complexity of ERT manufacturing and small patient populations provide natural protection — biosimilar economics are challenging when total market size is <$800M — but this is a real long-term headwind for ~40% of current revenue.

BioMarin's competitive position is stable-to-improving in the near term (VOXZOGO gaining share, no biosimilar entrants yet) but faces a 3-5 year window of increasing competitive pressure on both its growth driver (achondroplasia alternatives) and legacy products (biosimilar risk).

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Risks:**
1. **Amicus integration and leverage ($4.3B debt):** Execution risk on a transformative deal during a period of elevated interest rates. If revenue synergies disappoint or integration costs exceed expectations, the deleveraging timeline extends and equity becomes a residual claim on a leveraged credit.

2. **Earnings credibility:** The forward estimates ($5.02 FY2026, $6.68 FY2027) require flawless execution plus Amicus contribution. Another miss in Q2 or Q3 would likely push the stock to new lows.

3. **VOXZOGO accelerated approval risk:** If confirmatory studies fail to verify clinical benefit, the drug's U.S. approval could be withdrawn.

4. **DOJ investigation:** Subpoena regarding sponsored testing programs for VIMIZIM/NAGLAZYME carries unquantifiable legal/financial risk.

5. **Customer concentration:** 37% of revenue from 3 customers.

**Options Market Signal:**

The flat term structure (37.1% near vs. 35.6% far) suggests the market views uncertainty as evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a specific event. Put/call OI ratio of 0.46 and volume ratio of 0.32 show modest call-biased flow, consistent with some speculative upside positioning. The implied ±8.8% monthly move reflects that the market expects continued volatility. The balanced put/call skew (-2.1%) suggests no extreme directional conviction from options participants.

Short interest at 5.6% with 6.9 days to cover is moderate — enough to provide fuel for a short squeeze on positive catalysts, but not at crowded levels that would deter new short positions.

## Conclusion

BioMarin presents a genuine valuation disconnect: a profitable, $3.2B-revenue rare disease company growing ~15% organically with $725M FCF, trading at 10x forward earnings — roughly half the peer median multiple. The median analyst target of $86 implies 72% upside. The pipeline optionality from VOXZOGO's indication expansion is substantial and underappreciated.

The barrier to a BUY recommendation is specific and falsifiable: **three consecutive earnings misses have destroyed near-term credibility, and the $4.3B debt load from Amicus creates a new leverage risk that did not previously exist.** The market is rationally demanding proof that management can hit numbers while integrating a major acquisition. Until Q2 FY2026 earnings demonstrate this capability, the appropriate posture is to hold the stock for its structural growth trajectory while acknowledging that the re-rating catalyst is approximately 3 months away. The risk/reward is favorable on a 12-month view (10x → 14x forward = $70 target, +40%), but the next 90 days carry binary risk around execution.
