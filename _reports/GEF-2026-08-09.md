**Research Date: August 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Greif has spent twenty months converting itself from a levered, containerboard-integrated industrial packager into a four-segment, low-leverage converting business. Gross debt fell from $2,729.1M at October 31, 2024 to $1,030.4M at June 30, 2026 (–62%), net debt to $741.9M, and covenant leverage to 1.1x against a 4.00x threshold. Net interest expense fell 51% year over year in the third fiscal quarter, to $7.7M from $15.8M. Two divestitures funded this: the containerboard business and CorrChoice sheet-feeder system for $1,804.7M cash (closed August 31, 2025, sold to Packaging Corporation of America) and approximately 173,000 acres of Soterra timberland for $462.0M against a $231.4M carrying value (closed October 1, 2025). The February 27, 2026 refinancing pushed the revolver and both term loans to a 2031 maturity, leaving only two receivables-securitization facilities maturing in 2027 as near-dated obligations.

The operating result is better than the demand environment. Q3 FY2026 net sales rose 3.5% to $1,165.6M, Adjusted EBITDA rose 24.7% to $183.4M, and Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 267bps to 15.7%. Adjusted diluted Class A EPS was $1.61 against $0.86 a year earlier, a 43.8% beat versus the $1.12 consensus. Management raised FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance to $615–635M and adjusted free cash flow to $305–325M, and consensus current-year Class A EPS has risen 11.1% over 90 days to $4.31 with next year up 6.0% to $4.77. The composition of the growth matters: Greif's own disclosure implies consolidated organic volume of approximately –1.8% (Customized Polymer Solutions +1.5%, Durable Metal Solutions –3.1%, Sustainable Fiber Solutions –4.0%, Innovative Closure Solutions +5.5%), with price/mix contributing roughly +4.5% and currency +2.1%. The CEO's characterization on the call was blunt — "it's all self-help. We're not really relying on the market" — alongside a statement that the company does not anticipate a significant inflection in demand patterns.

The constraint is price. Correcting the equity base for Greif's two listed share classes (24.78M Class A at $88.68 plus 21.36M Class B at approximately $111.00 gives roughly $4,568M of equity value, not the $2.2bn Class-A-only figure that appears in standard data feeds), enterprise value is approximately $5,441M and EV/FY2026E Adjusted EBITDA is roughly 8.7x. Class A trades at 20.6x the $4.31 current-year consensus and 18.6x the $4.77 out-year against a 16.4x peer median. Consensus mean and median targets of $87.20 and $90.00 bracket spot; the two identifiable published views sit below it (Bank of America Sell/$74, Truist Hold/$78, with one unresolved report of a raise to $97). Five insiders including the CEO sold into the print on August 3 with no offsetting open-market purchases. Base case is $94–95 (+7%), bull $113 (+28%), bear $65 (–27%). Own it; do not add here.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1877 and headquartered in Delaware, Ohio, Greif manufactures containment and shipping products for other manufacturers across approximately 35 countries: steel drums, intermediate bulk containers, plastic drums and jerricans, fibre drums, closures, and recycled paperboard. End markets are industrial and diffuse — chemicals, lubricants, coatings, food and beverage, personal care, agrochemicals, pharmaceuticals, automotive, construction. Roughly 38% of the workforce is union-represented.

The reporting architecture changed three times in fourteen months, which is the single most important practical fact for anyone screening this name. Fiscal 2024 ended October 31, 2024; fiscal 2025 was an eleven-month transition period ending September 30, 2025 (Form 10-KT); fiscal 2026 is a twelve-month year ending September 30, 2026. Quarter-ends shifted from January/April/July/October to December/March/June/September. Segments moved from three geographic units to four material-based units in Q1 FY2025, then were reallocated again effective October 1, 2025. Containerboard moved to discontinued operations from Q3 FY2025. The consequence is that most automated annual growth, margin and cash-flow series spanning FY2024 through FY2026 are unreliable. One check confirms the nine-month comparatives are sound: the FY2026 cash flow statement shows beginning-of-period cash of $216.4M for the prior-year nine months, which cannot be the October 31, 2024 balance of $197.7M, so the comparative genuinely runs October 1, 2024 to June 30, 2025.

Q3 FY2026 segment detail: CPS $383.8M sales / $64.3M Adjusted EBITDA (16.8% margin); DMS $405.6M / $64.0M (15.8%); SFS $346.5M / $42.5M (12.3%); ICS $29.7M external / $12.6M. ICS is the quality asset — 34.0% gross margin on total sales including intersegment (versus 27.5% a year prior) with high-single-digit volume growth — but at roughly $51M of total sales it cannot move the consolidated number yet. Geographic mix is now U.S. 49.3%, EMEA 35.3%, APAC and other Americas 15.4%; the shift toward non-U.S. revenue is a mechanical result of divesting two U.S.-centric assets rather than international expansion.

## Financial Analysis

**Capital structure.** Total debt of $1,030.4M (long-term $687.4M, current portion $12.5M, short-term borrowings $330.5M) less $288.5M cash gives net debt of $741.9M, matching the company's own non-GAAP table. Adjusted net debt of $668.0M over trailing Credit Agreement EBITDA of $621.6M is 1.07x. The 2026 credit agreements provide an $800M revolver ($593.1M undrawn), a $100M Term A-1 and a $400M Term A-2, all maturing February 27, 2031, at a 4.88% weighted rate at quarter end. Short-term borrowings are receivables securitizations — a $200M U.S. facility maturing May 2027 at 4.73% and a €100M European facility at 2.98% maturing April 2027. Covenants require leverage ≤4.00x and interest coverage ≥3.00x; the headroom is large enough that the covenant package is not a live consideration.

One flattering item inside the interest line: the swap book has shrunk from $562.5M notional to $370.0M at a 1.99% average fixed rate against 4.88% actual borrowing costs. Reclassified gains fell to $5.4M for nine months from $15.6M, with only $7.4M expected over the next twelve months and maturities running 2027–2029. The effect is low single-digit millions annually — immaterial against $625M of EBITDA, but it means reported interest understates the underlying cost of debt going forward.

**Margins and quality of the improvement.** Q3 gross margin was 23.4% versus 22.7%; SG&A fell $18.8M (–11.2%) to 12.8% of sales from 14.9%; operating profit rose 69.4% to $107.9M. The cost program has achieved $90M of cumulative run-rate savings, described as the high end of the fiscal-year commitment and delivered early, with a reaffirmed target of at least $120M by end of FY2027.

Nine-month SG&A of $487.3M against $488.4M looks flat, which appears to contradict $90M of savings. The reconciliation is the $40.0M non-cash special charitable contribution booked in Q1 FY2026 (537,000 shares) and added back within "other costs." Excluding it, first-half SG&A was approximately $297.8M against $320.1M, or –7.0%. Greif does not state which income-statement line carries the donation, so the allocation to SG&A is inference rather than disclosure; a partial allocation to cost of products sold is possible. Either way, Q3 is the cleaner read on the cost program.

The margin trajectory withstands a mix test. Adjusted EBITDA margin went from 12.74% in FY2024 ($694.3M on $5,448.1M) to 14.31% for nine months FY2026 to 15.73% in Q3. The divested containerboard business ran at roughly a 19.2% EBITDA margin, above the consolidated level, so removing it should have depressed the consolidated figure. That margin rose approximately 176bps anyway is meaningful evidence that pricing and cost work, not favorable arithmetic, drove the gain.

**Cash flow requires care.** Nine-month GAAP free cash flow was $51.5M ($170.0M operating cash flow less $118.5M capex). Adjusted free cash flow of $196.0M adds back $100.0M of cash taxes on the Soterra sale, $13.7M on containerboard, $9.3M of integration ERP spend, $3.6M of acquisition costs and $17.9M of other nonrecurring items. The add-backs are legitimate and transparently labeled, but $113.7M of the $144.5M is real cash tax paid, so any FCF-yield screen built on GAAP cash flow will flag Greif for reasons that are explained and non-recurring.

The Q4 shape embedded in guidance deserves scrutiny. Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $615–635M against $462.7M realized implies $152.3–172.3M in Q4, a 6% to 17% sequential decline from $183.4M. Adjusted FCF guidance of $305–325M against $196.0M implies $109–129M, roughly double the $57.7M produced in Q3. Full-year tax guidance of 24–28% GAAP against 22.8% at nine months and 17.9% in Q3 implies a materially higher Q4 rate. The bridge management offers is a working-capital release: inventory was deliberately built at elevated cost because of Middle East volatility, and nine-month operating working capital consumed $53.8M with $87.5M of that use falling in Q3. Sequentially lower EBITDA, a doubling of cash conversion and a higher tax rate can all be true simultaneously — Q4 is a seasonally stronger collections quarter and the residual Middle East impact is quantified at roughly $20M — but this is the single largest execution risk to the guidance investors are now paying for. Consistent with that, the current-quarter consensus was revised down 8.3% over 90 days to $1.14, the only negative revision line in the set.

**Capital allocation.** Nine-month repurchases totaled 2,185,049 shares for $151.4M (1.81M Class A, 0.37M Class B), taking shares outstanding from 47.50M to 46.14M (–2.9%). Netting the 537,000 donated shares, the reduction was 1.65M. A $300M authorization approved December 9, 2025 could not commence until the residual 2017 authorization was exhausted, which explains why Q3 repurchases were only $2.9M; the July 28, 2026 announcement adds a stated intent for up to a further $150M. Combined capacity of roughly $450M is close to 10% of combined-class equity value. The dividend was raised 10.7% (Class A to $0.62 quarterly), implying roughly $140.8M annualized, or 45% of the adjusted FCF guidance midpoint. Capex is running approximately 3.7% of sales against 3.4% in FY2024 on a comparable footing — no underinvestment, no step-up.

**One balance-sheet flag.** Goodwill of $1,719.0M plus other intangibles of $794.2M equals $2,513.2M, or 44.1% of total assets and 83.8% of Greif shareholders' equity. The FY2024 10-K disclosed that Ipackchem — acquired March 2024 for approximately $569M and carrying $273.8M of goodwill — received only a qualitative Step 0 assessment. Ipackchem sits in CPS, which carries $650.5M of goodwill and produced a 3.7% nine-month GAAP operating margin ($37.8M on $1,033.7M) against a 14.1% Adjusted EBITDA margin, the gap being a $77.8M nine-month D&A load. CPS Adjusted EBITDA grew 33.6% over nine months and 73% in Q3, which moves away from an impairment trigger. This is a watch item rather than a current problem.

A note on screening artifacts: a standardized Altman Z-Score computed from the October 31, 2024 balance sheet returns 1.77, nominally distress. Recomputed on June 30, 2026 balances with the corrected market equity value, Z is approximately 2.86 (2.51 using book equity) — grey zone. With 1.1x covenant leverage, $593.1M undrawn and nothing material due before 2031, the Z-Score adds nothing here.

## Growth Analysis

Top-line growth is thin and its composition is unfavorable for durability. Weighting Greif's disclosed per-segment volume contributions by prior-year segment sales produces approximately –$20.5M of volume, or –1.8% consolidated, against roughly +4.5% from price/mix and +2.1% from currency. Price realization ahead of input costs and a weaker dollar are the least persistent earnings sources available. Nine-month raw material costs fell to 52.2% of sales from 55.5% while manufacturing costs rose to 25.5% from 22.6% — input relief partly offset by fixed-cost deleveraging on lower volumes.

Management has been consistent and unflattering about demand for three consecutive filing cycles. The FY2024 10-K warned that depressed 2023–2024 demand would persist through 2025. The Q3 FY2026 MD&A describes a "subdued industrial demand environment, with marginal demand improvement building from a low base" and states that no significant inflection is anticipated. On the call the CEO noted five years of volatility, declined to call a turn, and cited housing at historic lows and chemicals under pressure. Flavor and fragrance and pharmaceutical were called out as attractive end markets; chemicals and housing as pressured.

What is actually underwritable over the next four to six quarters:

1. **The remaining cost program.** $90M achieved against a target of at least $120M by end of FY2027 leaves roughly $30M of incremental run-rate EBITDA with reasonable visibility, worth roughly 5% on the FY2026 guidance midpoint.
2. **Tuck-in M&A.** Explicitly non-transformative, screened on EBITDA margins "well above 18%" and FCF conversion above 50%. Envaplast (June 2, 2026, $61.7M net consideration, premium small polymer containers for agrochemicals, placed in CPS) is the template; management expects "a number of similar acquisitions a year." At this cadence M&A adds perhaps 1–2% to EBITDA annually.
3. **The RISI dispute as a free option.** Greif implemented a $60/ton uncoated recycled board increase in April and announced a second $60/ton increase in June, fully implemented with non-RISI customers, while RISI has not recognized the June increase. Mill operating rates are 96%. Nothing for the June increase sits in guidance, so publication would be unguided SFS upside; continued non-recognition sustains a drag. The proportion of URB volume tied to the index is not disclosed, so the magnitude cannot be sized.
4. **Buyback-driven per-share growth.** Roughly $450M of capacity against $4.57bn of combined-class equity value.

Consensus captures this: FY2026 Class A EPS at $4.31 (+11.1% in 90 days) and FY2027 at $4.77 (+6.0%), implying roughly 10.7% out-year EPS growth. That is a respectable industrial compounding rate built primarily on cost and capital-return arithmetic, not volume. The revision magnitudes are positive but well short of the threshold that signals a genuine earnings inflection, and the negative revision to the current quarter matches the guided sequential step-down.

## Valuation Assessment

The starting point is a data correction. Greif has two NYSE-listed classes with charter-fixed economics in a 1.0 : 1.5 Class A : Class B ratio for dividends and earnings allocation, confirmed by Q3 GAAP diluted EPS of $1.35 Class A and $2.05 Class B. Class A: 24,776,629 shares at $88.68 = $2,197.4M. Class B: 21,359,678 shares at approximately $111.00 = $2,370.9M. Combined equity value approximately $4,568.3M. Adding net debt of $741.9M and $130.8M of redeemable and other noncontrolling interests gives enterprise value of approximately $5,441M.

- **EV / FY2026E Adjusted EBITDA (guidance midpoint $625M): 8.7x.** Against trailing Credit Agreement EBITDA of $621.6M: 8.8x.
- **Class A P/E: 20.6x FY2026 consensus $4.31; 18.6x FY2027 $4.77.** Trailing sits near 21–22x. Blended across both classes (because Class B trades below Class A per unit of earnings entitlement), the multiple is approximately 18.7x current-year and 16.9x next-year.
- **Peer set:** Silgan 15.1x, Ball 16.4x, Aptar 19.9x, median 16.4x. O-I is loss-making and uninformative. Class A at 20.6x is a 26% premium to the peer median and above all peers except Aptar.
- **PEG on Class A:** 18.6x forward against 10.7% out-year EPS growth = approximately 1.74.
- **Yield:** Class A 2.80% ($2.48 annualized), covered at roughly 45% of adjusted FCF, against a 1.7% peer median.

Running the same EV math off the $2.2bn Class-A-only market cap that appears in standard feeds produces approximately 4.9x EV/EBITDA. That would look like deep value and is simply an error; the profile field showing EPS of $0.31 alongside a 22.4x P/E is internally inconsistent for the same reason.

**A share-class point that affects execution rather than the business call.** Per unit of economic entitlement, Class A costs $88.68 and Class B costs $74.00 ($111.00 ÷ 1.5) — a 19.8% Class A premium. Class B yields 3.35% versus 2.80%, and Class B holds full voting power while Class A is non-voting unless four quarterly dividends fall cumulatively into arrears. The most likely explanation is Class B's inferior liquidity and probable index ineligibility, which are real and possibly permanent costs; concentrated family ownership suppressing the Class B price independently of fundamentals is an alternative I cannot exclude without float and volume data. An investor who wants Greif's cash flows and can tolerate the liquidity can currently buy them roughly 20% cheaper in GEF.B. The Class B price of $111.00 comes from a third-party quote widget rather than a filing and should be re-verified; all EV and dual-class figures scale with it.

**Scenario construction (base on EV/EBITDA, cross-checked on Class A EPS multiples):**

- **Base $94–95 (+7%).** FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA of roughly $660M (guidance midpoint plus the residual cost program, flat volumes, no currency help) at 8.7x = $5,742M EV; less roughly $700M net debt and $131M NCI = $4,911M equity, +7.5%. Cross-check: $4.77 FY2027 consensus at 19–20x = $91–95.
- **Bull $113 (+28%).** Volume inflection plus the full $120M program plus RISI recognition takes FY2027 EBITDA to $700M at a re-rated 9.5x = $6,650M EV, $5,819M equity. Cross-check: $5.40 Class A EPS at 21x = $113.
- **Bear $65 (–27%).** Price/cost spread reverses as resin and OCC deflate while contract prices reset, volumes decline another 3%, and the dollar firms; EBITDA reverts toward $560M (approximately the reconstructed twelve-month FY2025 comparable of roughly $559M) at 7.5x = $4,200M EV, $3,369M equity. This is close to the $65–73 range in which the stock traded from March through July 2026.

Probability-weighting at 50/30/20 base/bull/bear gives roughly +6.5% price return plus 2.8% dividend, near 9% total. Weighting the bear at 25% instead drops it toward 6.5%. That sensitivity to a single assumption is the substance of a HOLD: the distribution centers close to a fair return with meaningful two-sided dispersion, and it does not clear a BUY or ACCUMULATE bar. Consensus corroborates the framing without driving it — mean $87.20 and median $90.00 against spot $88.68, with low $74 and high $100. Because the stock is not cheap relative to its improving estimates, I do not treat consensus targets here as a floor.

## Competitive Landscape

Greif's own filings describe the competitive basis as "price, quality, customer service and on-time delivery" and state that "certain of our competitors are substantially larger and have significantly greater financial resources." That is not a franchise description. The defensible advantages are three: a footprint in approximately 35 countries that lets multinational chemical and coatings customers consolidate supply; regulatory-grade qualification for hazardous-goods transport under DOT and UN packaging standards, which creates requalification friction on switching; and reconditioning and lifecycle services, which establish a circular-economy position and simultaneously expose Greif to a flagged U.S. EPA review of the reconditioning framework.

The structural squeeze is disclosed plainly. Customer consolidation has "increased the concentration of our largest customers, resulting, in some cases, in increased pricing pressures," while supplier consolidation has "resulted in limited sources of supply and increased cost pressures." Some customers have vertically integrated, shrinking the addressable base outright. Greif competes in capital-intensive industries where producers run as long as prices cover marginal cost, which is precisely the 2023–2026 pattern.

Trajectory is genuinely mixed. Margin is improving (+267bps in Q3, +176bps versus FY2024 despite divesting a higher-margin business), ICS is compounding at high-single-digit volumes on 34% gross margin, and CPS captured +8.8% price/mix through a period management described as repricing "virtually day-to-day" against dramatic resin increases — evidence of real commercial discipline. Against that, unit volumes are contracting in the two largest segments, and the containerboard exit removed Greif's integrated paper position, leaving a more purely converting-exposed business with less vertical protection. The exit was nonetheless a rational trade: $1,804.7M for roughly $223M of annualized EBITDA is approximately 8.1x for the most commodity-priced, most capital-intensive asset in the portfolio, and the proceeds retired debt costing over $130M annually. Soterra was cleaner still — approximately $2,671 per acre for an asset generating roughly 2% on realized value, at 2.0x carrying value.

I score competitive position slightly below neutral: the moat is service and footprint rather than technology, the customer set is consolidating against Greif, and volume trajectory is negative even as margin trajectory improves.

## Risk Assessment

**Q4 guidance concentration (highest near-term).** The raised guidance requires a doubling of quarterly adjusted free cash flow, a 6–17% sequential EBITDA decline, and a materially higher tax rate simultaneously. The cash element depends on releasing inventory deliberately built at elevated cost. A working-capital miss in the November print is the most probable near-term negative catalyst, and the –8.3% revision to the current-quarter consensus suggests the sell side already sees the step-down.

**Price/cost reversal.** All of the FY2026 earnings growth traces to price/mix (+4.5%) and currency (+2.1%) against declining volume. If resin and recovered fibre deflate while contract prices reset downward, or the dollar firms, the mechanism runs in reverse with no volume cushion.

**Russia — unquantified and structurally awkward.** The FY2024 10-K disclosed Russian operations at approximately 3% of sales but roughly 9% of operating profit and 2% of assets, with strict currency controls such that Greif "has been generally unable to transfer money out of Russia." Three times the profit share relative to revenue share implies unusually high local margins on cash that does not convert to distributable form, and cessation would trigger an impairment by the company's own account. No updated figure is available post-divestiture and the new four-segment structure does not isolate the geography, so the FY2024 percentages should not be treated as current — but nor should the exposure be assumed away.

**Named Iran exposure.** The forward-looking enumeration in the Q3 FY2026 release and 10-Q now specifically cites conflicts "such as with Iran," where FY2024 listed Middle East conflicts generically. Management quantified the FY2026 impact at approximately $20M and attributed the inventory build to it. This exposure and its resolution timing are not further specified in the sources reviewed, so treat its persistence into the next two quarters as unresolved rather than assured.

**Goodwill and intangible concentration.** $2,513.2M, or 83.8% of Greif equity, with Ipackchem's $273.8M last tested qualitatively, inside a CPS segment running a 3.7% GAAP operating margin. Improving segment EBITDA mitigates but does not eliminate this.

**Adjustment intensity.** Excluding D&A and disposal gains, add-backs reaching Adjusted EBITDA were approximately $103.9M for nine months FY2026 (22.5% of the total) against $64.2M (16.4%) prior year. The $40.0M share donation transferred roughly 1.16% of shares of real value while being excluded from adjusted metrics. The presentation is transparent; the trend is the thing to watch.

**Other identified items.** Joint-venture partners hold put rights priced at fixed multiples against $92.4M of redeemable noncontrolling interests — a formula-priced contingent cash obligation invisible to debt screens. EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation post-consumer-resin mandates create both cost and supply-availability risk; PFAS scrutiny is broad; U.S. fibre drum volumes are in secular decline. Environmental reserves are modest at $16.8M (including $9.8M for Diamond Alkali), and the 10-Q states there are no material pending legal proceedings. The restructuring line carries an undecomposed $17.1M "other charges" component within the $42.0M nine-month total.

**Governance.** CEO total compensation rose from $4.35M in FY2023 to $9.44M in FY2025 with comp-actually-paid at a five-year high of $13.82M, in the same year the company-selected Adjusted EBITDA measure hit a five-year low of $511.3M. The mitigating facts carry real weight: FY2025 was an eleven-month, containerboard-excluded period and is not comparable; relative TSR — an explicit plan metric — outperformed sharply at 156.66 versus a 103.66 peer index; comp-actually-paid is a mark-to-market construct; and the board plausibly credited management for realizing $1.8bn and $462M on two disposals. The optics warrant attention without supporting an inference of misalignment.

**Insider activity.** On August 3, 2026 — the first open window after the July 28 release — CEO Ole Rosgaard sold 40,087 Class A shares at $86.68 ($3.47M), roughly 31% of his disclosed Class A position, joined by the Chief Commercial Officer, a director and two SBU general managers. No insider made an open-market purchase in the February–August 2026 period reviewed. Two offsets: a November 2025 PSU/RSU settlement of 170,210 plus 55,654 Class A shares supplies a routine post-vest diversification rationale, and the Chief Commercial Officer's precise 2,000-share monthly cadence from March through July is characteristic of a pre-arranged plan I could not confirm. Rising consensus estimates argue against reading the selling as a fundamental signal. The defensible narrow statement: no insider has expressed conviction through a purchase, and the CEO reduced roughly a third of his disclosed stake at prices 20–30% above where the same insiders transacted from March to July.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin — 72 contracts across four expirations, with put/call volume and open-interest ratios both reported at zero, so positioning inferences carry little weight. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 31.7% sits 7.1 points above 24.6% one-year realized, meaning the market is charging up for the near term even though the IV percentile within the chain is only 8%. Term structure is in contango (31.7% at one month, 33.9% at three, 36.3% at six), the complacent configuration — no near-term dislocation priced, with uncertainty rising into 2027. The nearest expiry (August 21) implies a ±5.1% move, which is unremarkable given no scheduled catalyst before the November fiscal-year print; the three-month tenor spanning that print implies ±8.9%. The data source characterizes the one-month skew as call-heavy. Short interest is negligible at 304,306 shares and 1.3 days to cover, so there is neither a crowded bear position nor squeeze fuel. Net: options add no challenge to the fundamental read, but rich near-term IV against realized argues against buying premium here, and the contango pattern is consistent with a market that sees the risk as a 2027 price/cost question rather than an August event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($113, +28%).** The cost program reaches its $120M target on schedule, adding roughly $30M of run-rate EBITDA. RISI recognizes the June $60/ton URB increase, delivering unguided SFS upside. Industrial volumes inflect from a five-year trough as chemicals destocking completes and housing stabilizes, turning the –1.8% volume drag into a modest positive with high incremental margins on a footprint that has already absorbed $24.9M of restructuring. Tuck-in acquisitions on the Envaplast template add 1–2% annually at above-18% margins and above-50% cash conversion. With 1.1x leverage, roughly $450M of buyback capacity retires close to 10% of the combined-class float. FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA reaches $700M, the multiple re-rates toward 9.5x on demonstrated cyclical de-risking, and Class A EPS approaches $5.40 at 21x.

**Bear case ($65, –27%).** The entire FY2026 earnings gain reverses. Resin and recovered fibre deflate, index-linked contracts reset downward, the dollar firms, and the price/mix plus currency contribution that supplied roughly 6.6 points of the 3.5% revenue growth turns negative against continued volume contraction. The Q4 working-capital release disappoints, cutting adjusted FCF below the $305M floor and undermining confidence in the $625M EBITDA base. CPS goodwill — $650.5M in a segment at 3.7% GAAP operating margin with Ipackchem last tested qualitatively — takes an impairment. Russian operations are curtailed, removing a disproportionately profitable earnings stream and forcing a write-down. EBITDA reverts toward the reconstructed twelve-month FY2025 comparable near $559M, the multiple compresses to 7.5x, and the stock returns to the $65–73 range where it traded four months ago.

**Base case ($94–95, +7%).** Volumes stay approximately flat to slightly negative through FY2027, the residual cost program delivers roughly $30M, currency contributes nothing, and tuck-ins add a point or two. FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA reaches roughly $660M at an unchanged 8.7x, buybacks offset dilution and add modestly to per-share figures, and the 2.80% dividend carries the rest. Total return in the high single digits.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria
