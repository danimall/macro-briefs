## Executive Summary

American Water Works is the largest publicly traded U.S. regulated water and wastewater utility, serving ~14 million people across 14 states through ~3.5 million connections. The investment case is mechanical: invest capital into rate base, obtain regulatory recovery, compound earnings at 7-9%. Over 2021-2025 the company delivered on the operating half of that promise. Compensation-plan Adjusted EPS compounded 7.3% annually from $4.27 to $5.66, while shareholders received nothing. The proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows $100 invested on 12/31/2020 was worth $93.81 at 12/31/2025 against $158.66 for the disclosed peer group. The entire negative return came from multiple compression off a very high 2020 starting premium.

The Q2 2026 print (July 29) was the best operating datapoint in a year. Revenue rose 6.2% to $1,355M while O&M was flat at $481M against $480M, producing 10.8% operating income growth and positive operating leverage on a materially larger asset base. Leverage improved 160 basis points in the first half to 57.8% debt-to-capitalization (against a 70% covenant limit) as AWK physically settled 3.4M forward shares for $476M at an effective $139.84, above the current market price. The dividend was raised ~8% to $0.8950 quarterly. Those facts stabilize a score trajectory that had fallen from 7.0 in February to 5.5 in June.

Three things prevent an upgrade. First, valuation: 22.3x the $6.02-$6.12 adjusted guidance range and 20.6x 2027 consensus of $6.57, against a water-peer median P/E of 21.9x and a 2.65% yield below the 2.9% peer median. Second, the Essential Utilities merger: an all-stock deal at 0.305 AWK shares per Essential share implies ~89M new shares (a ~45% increase in count, or 31% ownership dilution for existing holders) for a combination management says will be accretive only in 2028, with ~$150M of pre-closing costs and Pennsylvania approval still outstanding. Third, Pennsylvania. On July 16 the PUC authorized ~$74.9M of annualized revenue on a request understood to be ~$160M, at a 9.55% allowed ROE and 54.2% equity ratio, on a 4-1 vote, in a state where the Governor has issued affordability principles and installed a Special Counsel for rate matters. Pennsylvania is AWK's largest jurisdiction (~2.5 million people served) and simultaneously the largest source of merger synergy and merger approval risk.

## Company Overview

Operations sit almost entirely in one Regulated Businesses segment, with a smaller Other segment dominated by the Military Services Group (50-year contracts on 18-19 installations; ~$7.9B of remaining performance commitments at 12/31/25, of which $1.1B is parent-guaranteed and the rest guaranteed by wholly owned subsidiaries).

Earnings are not volume-driven. State commissions set rates to recover prudent operating costs, depreciation, financing costs and taxes plus an authorized return on rate base. Growth is therefore a function of capital deployment velocity and regulatory willingness to grant timely recovery. AWK's disclosed 2026 capital plan is $3.7B including acquisitions, of which $1.8B (including $346M of acquisitions) was spent in the first half. Against $31.9B of net PP&E at 6/30/26 and roughly $0.95B of annual depreciation, $3.7B of gross investment implies ~8.6% net rate-base growth, arithmetically consistent with the 7-9% EPS target and conditional entirely on recovery.

Two structural features matter. The fair-market-value ratemaking statutes in several states allow rate base to be set at acquisition value rather than depreciated original cost, which is what makes buying distressed municipal systems economically viable. And only **two of 14 jurisdictions have a revenue decoupling mechanism**, so in the other twelve, revenue remains exposed to weather and conservation. That exposure matters in a year where guidance is unusually back-half loaded.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement trajectory.** Operating revenues grew from $4,234M (FY2023) to $4,684M (FY2024) to approximately $5.13-5.14B (FY2025 — a derived figure reconstructed from net income of $1,111M at a 21.6% net margin and cross-checked against FCF of −$1,067M at a −20.8% margin; treat as approximate). Operating margin held in a tight 35.5-36.7% band across all three years, which is what functioning rate recovery looks like. FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS was $5.69; adjusted EPS ~$5.64.

**First half 2026.** Revenue $2,562M (+6.0%), operating income $933M (+8.5%), GAAP EPS $2.61 (+3.2%), adjusted EPS $2.62 (+4.4%). Below the operating line the story reverses: interest expense rose 11.9% to $330M, and interest income collapsed from $44M to $15M because the ~$795M secured seller note from the 2021 Homeowner Services divestiture came off the balance sheet (it appears at $795M at 12/31/25 and $0 at 6/30/26; guidance explicitly excludes incremental interest income on that note through February 13, 2026). That is roughly $29M of pre-tax income gone for reasons unrelated to the utility. The regulated business absorbed it and still grew adjusted EPS 4.4%, so the underlying operating performance is better than the headline growth rate. The offset, which I have not quantified, is that the note's collection freed ~$795M of cash that reduced borrowing (current LTD fell from $1,479M to $446M in H1), so the net drag is smaller than $29M gross.

**The guidance arithmetic requires acceleration.** H1 adjusted EPS of $2.62 against the $6.07 midpoint implies ~$3.45 in H2, versus $3.13 in H2 2025 — **+10.2% required against H1's actual +4.4%**. Management confirmed on the call that most 2026 growth is concentrated in the second half as newly authorized revenues begin flowing in Q3. The 2025 split was 44.5%/55.5% versus an implied 43%/57% in 2026, so the shape is not anomalous; the magnitude of required year-over-year acceleration is the new element. With Pennsylvania rates effective mid-August and only two jurisdictions decoupled, a cool or wet third quarter is a live path to the low end of guidance.

**Regulatory pipeline.** $216M of additional annualized revenue authorized since January 1, 2026 ($111M general rate cases, $105M infrastructure surcharges), roughly 4.2% of FY2025 revenue. Six general rate cases plus one surcharge filing pending for $494M requested (~9.6% of revenue if fully granted, which history says it will not be). Largest pending: Missouri, $179M on $1.6B of investment, using a fully forecasted future test year for the first time — a favorable procedural development if granted. It is not clear whether the $74.9M Pennsylvania award sits inside the $216M authorized figure or is still counted in the $494M pending; do not treat them as additive.

**Cash flow and capital structure.** FY2025 operating cash flow $2,059M against capex $3,126M produced free cash flow of **−$1,067M**; dividends paid were $633M. This is a feature of the model, not a defect, but it defines the risk. Against ~$2.1-2.3B of expected 2026 OCF and a $3.7B program, AWK needs roughly $1.4-1.6B of external funding in 2026 before dividends. The dividend is not covered by free cash flow at all; it is sustained by regulatory recovery of a growing rate base plus continuous debt and equity issuance. Payout is ~59% of 2026 consensus EPS, which is comfortable; the real question is capital-markets access, not payout ratio.

Recalculated leverage at 6/30/26: total debt of $15,991M ($14,043M long-term including redeemable preferred, $1,499M short-term, $446M current portion) against $11,665M of common equity gives debt/capitalization of **57.8%** and debt/equity of **1.37x**, both improved from 59.4% and 1.46x at year-end. That matches management's stated 58%. Working capital deficit narrowed from −$2,556M to −$1,560M. Interest coverage on H1 figures is 2.83x EBIT/interest — thin by industrial standards, normal for a water utility. Liquidity: $2.75B revolver undrawn, $2.60B commercial paper program with $1,590M outstanding, $84M of letters of credit. A $500M long-term issuance printed in late May 2026 at 4.625%.

Two 2026 financing items to track: $1,035M of 3.625% Exchangeable Senior Notes due 2026 at American Water Capital Corp (principal settleable in cash if the conditional exchange feature triggers), and the remaining **4,694,836 forward shares** from the August 2025 agreements, which must settle by 12/31/2026. Those equal 2.36% of existing shares (2.31% post-issuance ownership dilution) for roughly $650M of expected proceeds. Because the stock at $135.31 sits below the $139.657 initial forward price, physical settlement is currently favorable to AWK, which receives above-market proceeds. The June settlement at an effective $139.84 confirms that.

A note on screens: an Altman Z-score computed on this balance sheet returns roughly 0.99, nominally "distress." That reading should be discarded. Altman's model was calibrated on manufacturers and structurally penalizes rate-regulated utilities through permanently negative working capital (commercial paper is standing funding) and 0.145x asset turnover. The relevant solvency evidence is 58% debt/cap against a 70% covenant, 2.8x coverage, an undrawn $2.75B revolver, and a framework permitting recovery of financing costs.

## Growth Analysis

The 7-9% EPS and dividend growth target through 2030 is arithmetically coherent and was reaffirmed on July 29. Consensus agrees but not enthusiastically: current year $6.08, next year $6.57 (+8.1%). Estimate revisions are flat — current-year unchanged over 30 days and −0.2% over 90 days; next year +0.1% and −0.1%. There is no earnings-momentum signal here in either direction, which is itself informative: the Q2 beat did not move the sell-side's out-year numbers.

Three growth engines and their current condition:

**Mandated capital.** EPA PFAS drinking water standards, the Lead and Copper Rule Revisions and the Lead and Copper Rule Improvements all compel spending that becomes recoverable rate base. The Pennsylvania order itself acknowledged PFAS treatment expansion and lead service line elimination as investment drivers. This engine is intact and is the most durable part of the thesis.

**Acquisitions.** ~52,000 connections added through 6/30/26 including Nexus Water Group (closed early, June 1, adding ~47,000 connections and ~70 employees; goodwill rose $126M to $1,282M), with ~57,000 connections representing ~$236M of deals under agreement across six states, targeting ~2% customer growth in 2026. The FY2025 10-K is explicit that this is getting harder: rising competition from other regulated utilities, governmental entities and financial buyers means "prices for suitable acquisition candidates may increase," and there are situations where AWK "may pay more than the ultimate fair value of the utility assets as set by the regulatory commission." Pennsylvania tightened its municipal-acquisition valuation procedures in June 2024, adding public notice requirements and a mandatory rate impact notice. Nexus's final rate base and post-closing purchase price adjustments are not yet settled, so its earnings contribution is not fixed.

**Rate relief.** This is where the deterioration sits. Pennsylvania at 47% of request, 9.55% ROE and 54.2% equity ratio, on a 4-1 vote, in a state that has moved to an affordability-first posture with gubernatorial involvement. Management's framing is that the three completed 2026 cases (West Virginia, Maryland, Pennsylvania) "authorized recovery of nearly 100% of the capital investments we have made in each state," implying the haircut landed on O&M allowances, ROE and capital-structure parameters rather than capital recovery. If accurate, the near-term rate-base earnings engine is much less impaired than the 53% headline cut suggests. I could not read the PUC order and cannot verify the composition of the disallowance. The adverse reading is that a lower authorized ROE and equity ratio permanently compress earned returns on the entire Pennsylvania rate base — durable, not one-time.

AWK does not have the data-center load growth that has re-rated electric utilities. Water utilities receive the regulatory-mandate tailwind without an equivalent large-load demand tailwind, and AWK's disclosed growth drivers contain no material data-center component. That asymmetry helps explain why AWK trades at a premium multiple while carrying similar financing burdens to some electric peers.

Against water peers — median P/E 21.9x, median yield 2.9%, median beta 0.50 — AWK carries a modest P/E premium with a below-median yield. Essential Utilities, the merger target, trades at 18.8x with a 3.6% yield. Within the peer set, only CWT (22.7x) and AWR (22.4x) are comparable, and neither carries a pending 31% dilution.

The bull framing for valuation is that the derating is a completed event. AWK entered 2021 at an extreme premium; the 2022-2023 rate shock compressed it; the multiple is now at parity-plus rather than a 40% premium; and 7-9% EPS growth plus a 2.65% yield delivers 9.6-11.6% total return with no further multiple change. That framing is legitimate for a low-beta holding.

The bear framing is that 22.3x forward is not a price at which the market is wrong. A utility that must raise $1.5-2.0B of net external capital annually, issues equity every year, and shows 2.83x interest coverage is the archetype that suffers when the long end steepens. Sell-side consensus is $140.18 mean / $140.00 median against a $128 low and $155 high, implying 3.6% capital appreciation, or total return roughly equal to the dividend. The recommendation distribution (0 strong buy, 3 buy, 9 hold, 1 strong sell, unchanged for four months) matches. On stocks trading at reasonable multiples with rising estimates, analyst targets lag reality; here estimates are flat and the multiple is at a premium to the peer group, so the targets are a fair read.

**The merger arithmetic deserves specific attention.** At 0.305 shares, implied consideration is $41.27 per Essential share. AWK issues ~89M shares against WTRG's ~$11B market cap, or roughly $12.0B of stock, an implied premium of about 9%. Trailing arithmetic suggests the deal should be accretive: AWK is issuing paper at 23.8x to acquire earnings capitalized at 18.8x. Management nonetheless guides to accretion only in **2028**, "the first full year following completion," on "preliminary estimates that are subject to change." The gap is presumably explained by ~$150M of pre-closing costs, integration expense, Essential's lower standalone growth trajectory (5-7% versus AWK's 7-9%), and assumed debt. I cannot reconcile it precisely, and that is a material verification gap: a transaction that dilutes existing holders by 31% of ownership and produces no promised accretion for two years is being underwritten on estimates the company itself flags as preliminary.

Valuation score: **4.8**. The multiple is defensible but offers no margin of safety, the yield is inferior to peers, and the merger inserts a 2027 earnings air pocket.

## Competitive Landscape

AWK's advantages are real: scale in an overwhelmingly municipal industry, FMV ratemaking statutes, demonstrated regulatory relationships (the PA PUC Chairman explicitly cited PAWC's distressed-system work — Dimock, Rock Spring, East Dunkard receiverships — as a "public benefit that warrants recognition as part of the Commission's consideration of management performance"), and a cost of capital smaller peers cannot match ($2.75B revolver, $2.60B CP program).

Trajectory, which matters more than level, is mixed. Improving: the Essential merger removes Aqua Pennsylvania, Essential's regulated subsidiary and AWK's most direct large-scale competitor for Pennsylvania municipal systems, and adds geographic diversity and scale against a tougher regulator. Deteriorating: acquisition prices are rising by the company's own disclosure; the FMV pathway is being narrowed in the largest state; and affordability politics compress the achievable spread between invested and recovered capital.

Post-merger, AWK owns a natural gas local distribution business — a new risk category the 10-K explicitly names, bringing different regulatory constructs, commodity dynamics, methane and decarbonization exposure, and a different safety-liability profile. Investors who own AWK as a water pure-play, historically part of the premium multiple, will own something structurally different. The anticipated loss of pure-play status is likely already contributing to the multiple compression the proxy's TSR table documents.

A caution on the same-bidder logic: a commission focused on affordability may view reduced bidding competition for municipal systems as a harm rather than a benefit and condition or reject approval accordingly. The merger agreement's "burdensome effect" clause — permitting either party to walk generally without a fee if regulators impose unacceptable conditions including forced dispositions — exists precisely for that scenario.

## Risk Assessment

**Pennsylvania regulatory and political risk.** The single most important item, now quantified rather than theoretical. PAWC's own release references Governor Shapiro's affordability "statement of principles," notes the case was filed before the Governor's letter, and says it looks forward to engaging with the Governor's Special Counsel prior to future filings. The next Pennsylvania case is likely harder, not easier.

**Merger approval.** Three states approved (understood to be Kentucky, Ohio, Virginia), Texas settled in principle, Pennsylvania pending along with HSR expiration. Target close end of Q1 2027. Termination fees are asymmetric: $835M AWK-to-Essential versus $370M the other way (2.26x, or ~3.1% of AWK's market cap). Fee asymmetry in a stock-for-stock merger of unequals typically scales with relative size, so I draw no inference about negotiating posture from it.

**PFAS and CERCLA.** The 10-K is unusually candid: EPA's April 2024 PFOA/PFOS hazardous-substance designation creates joint-and-several, no-fault liability, and while EPA reaffirmed in September 2025 that it generally does not plan to pursue passive receivers, "its enforcement discretion does not alter CERCLA's underlying liability framework or restrict private party claims." Separately, PUCs may disallow recovery for treating a contaminant "in the absence of a regulatory standard"; AWK can be compelled to spend before it can recover.

**California.** Monterey Peninsula Water Management District eminent domain litigation against California American Water, filed December 2023, still pending. SWRCB cease-and-desist orders require material Carmel River diversion reductions. California's inverse-condemnation regime allows suits without proof of negligence, and the 10-K notes the PUC has disallowed rate recovery of such losses at other utilities.

**Financing dependence.** Structurally negative FCF, $3.7B 2026 program, 58% debt/cap. The 10-K flags that a significant goodwill write-down would reduce equity and could push debt/capitalization toward the 70% covenant limit; goodwill is $1,282M.

**Other.** 26 of 74 collective bargaining agreements expire during 2026, an above-average renegotiation load in a year of affordability-based rate requests. A realized October 2024 cybersecurity incident is disclosed, with nation-state actors named among threats. 75 dams, the majority earthen.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($155, +14.6%).** Second-half revenue converts as authorized, 2026 lands at or above the $6.12 top of guidance. Missouri's $179M request on a forecasted future test year is granted substantially, validating a procedural improvement that carries to future filings. Pennsylvania approves the merger without burdensome conditions, and the market begins underwriting 2028 accretion rather than 2027 dilution. Mandated PFAS and lead spending keeps rate base compounding at 8-9%. The multiple re-expands toward the top of the sell-side target range ($155) as the merger de-risks. Total return with the dividend approaches 17%.

**Base case ($145, +7.2% plus 2.65% yield).** 22x on 2027 consensus $6.57 = $144.5. Guidance is met near the midpoint. Pennsylvania approves the merger with modest conditions; the deal closes in Q1 2027, and 2027 is a transitional year with dilution and integration costs offsetting operating growth. Rate-base compounding continues at 7-9% but authorized ROEs drift lower as affordability politics spread beyond Pennsylvania. The multiple holds near current levels. Total return ~10% over 12-18 months.

**Bear case ($117, −13.5%).** Two independent paths, either sufficient. First, the long end steepens further and a utility requiring $1.5-2.0B of annual external capital at 2.83x interest coverage derates toward the 18.5x that Essential currently commands, which on ~$6.30 of merger-cost-burdened 2027 EPS is $117. Second, Pennsylvania conditions or rejects the merger, triggering the "burdensome effect" walk-away; AWK loses two years of strategic focus and ~$150M of costs, and the multiple compresses on a broken growth narrative. Weather in the twelve non-decoupled jurisdictions is the near-term accelerant given the required +10.2% H2 acceleration.

Probability-weighting 50% base / 20% bull / 30% bear gives roughly 2.5% expected capital appreciation plus the 2.65% yield, or ~5.2% expected total return. On a ~0.5-beta regulated asset that is approximately fair — not compelling, not impaired.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (fourth consecutive). Rationale: income_hold.** The return is dominated by an 8%-growing dividend and regulated rate-base compounding, not by a re-rating catalyst. Existing holders are adequately compensated; new capital has no urgency at $135.31.

Score trajectory has run 7.0 (Feb) → 5.9 (May) → 5.5 (Jun) → 5.4 now. The near-flat read is the signal: Q2's operating leverage, the 160bp leverage improvement, the 8% dividend increase and the above-market forward settlement arrested the deterioration. The stock's rally from the early-June low of $120.57 consumed the improvement, leaving risk/reward where it was.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price of **$118-124** with fundamentals intact — that range puts the stock at 18-19x 2027 consensus and a 2.9-3.0% yield, at or above the peer median yield, restoring margin of safety.
2. Pennsylvania PUC approval of the Essential merger without material conditions or forced dispositions, combined with a quantified 2027 accretion bridge (not the current "accretive in 2028" placeholder). This is the largest single de-risking event available.
3. Q3 2026 adjusted EPS confirming the H2 ramp — roughly $2.00 or better against the $2.07 current-quarter consensus — with full-year guidance narrowed to the upper half of $6.02-$6.12.
4. Missouri granted substantially on its forecasted future test year, establishing a regulatory-lag improvement that carries forward.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. 2026 guidance cut or narrowed to the bottom of the range, which would indicate the newly authorized revenues are not converting on schedule and would mark the first genuine guidance failure of this cycle.
2. Pennsylvania imposing burdensome merger conditions, or either party invoking the walk-away right.
3. Authorized ROE below ~9.4% in the Missouri or Kentucky decisions, which would establish that the Pennsylvania parameters were a regional trend rather than a single-state outcome.
4. Debt/capitalization rising back above 60%, or any goodwill impairment against the $1,282M balance, either of which shortens the distance to the 70% covenant.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print (late October) is the decisive near-term checkpoint, because it is the first quarter containing the Pennsylvania rates effective August 13 and will resolve whether the required H2 acceleration is materializing. The Pennsylvania merger decision is the larger, later binary, with a targeted close by end of Q1 2027.

## Conclusion

American Water Works is a well-run regulated monopoly executing a coherent rate-base compounding strategy, and Q2 2026 demonstrated that the operating machine works — flat O&M on a materially larger asset base, 10.8% operating income growth on 6.2% revenue growth, leverage down 160 basis points, and an 8% dividend increase. The company also absorbed a ~$29M non-operating interest-income headwind and still grew adjusted EPS 4.4% in the first half.

What prevents a constructive rating is the combination of price and pending structural change. At 22.3x 2026 guidance and 20.6x 2027 consensus, AWK carries a premium to the water-peer median on a below-median 2.65% yield. The company must raise $1.5-2.0B of net external capital annually, dilutes shareholders every year through equity forwards, and is about to dilute them by 31% of ownership in a merger that management does not claim will be accretive until 2028, while the approval that matters most sits with a commission that just granted 47% of the largest subsidiary's rate request in an explicitly affordability-driven proceeding. The five-year record is instructive: the company delivered 7.3% adjusted EPS growth and shareholders lost 6.2% cumulatively because the multiple compressed. The multiple that compressed is still above the peer group.

Own it for the growing dividend and the defensiveness that a regulated essential-service monopoly provides. Add on weakness toward $118-124, or on evidence that Pennsylvania will approve the merger cleanly. Do not pay 22x for a two-year dilution window.
