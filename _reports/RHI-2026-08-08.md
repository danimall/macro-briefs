## Executive Summary

Robert Half is in the fourth year of the deepest professional-staffing downcycle on record by duration: revenue has fallen from $7,238.1M (2022) to $5,378.5M (2025), a 25.7% peak-to-trough decline across three consecutive down years, and diluted EPS has fallen 77.9% from $6.03 to $1.33. The operating trend in the first half of 2026 is genuinely improving: adjusted operating income rose 53% year over year in Q1 to $28.7M, permanent placement returned to growth at +2.9% in Q2, Talent Solutions has now posted three consecutive quarters of sequential adjusted revenue growth, and the Q3 guidance midpoint of $0.49 sits above the $0.47 consensus. That is the bull case, and it is supported by filings rather than narrative.

The problem is price and mix. The stock has moved from $21.86 (March 16, 2026 close) to $42.74, and the entire re-rating has occurred while consensus estimates fell: current-year EPS is now $1.27 versus $1.32 ninety days ago, next-year $1.97 versus $2.07, and next-quarter $0.39 versus $0.46, a 12.2% cut in thirty days. The forward multiple has expanded from roughly 29.8x current-year earnings at the July 23 print ($37.85 close) to 33.7x today on a lower estimate. Sell-side price targets, mean $35.00 and median $31.00 against a $50.00 high, sit 18% to 27% below spot, and the ratings distribution (4 buy, 3 hold, 3 sell, 1 strong sell) is unusually negative for a stock at its 52-week high. Short interest of 26.7% of float with 9.4 days to cover means a meaningful part of the move up is mechanical rather than fundamental, and it cuts both ways from here.

The two halves of the business are moving in opposite directions and the profitable half is the one deteriorating. Protiviti generated $112.4M of the $182.6M FY2025 consolidated adjusted operating income (61.6% of profit on 36.3% of revenue) while the contract talent segment, RHI's historic core, produced a $25.1M reported operating loss. Protiviti revenue went from -0.1% (FY25) to -4.9% (Q2'26) to guided approximately -6% (Q3'26), with adjusted gross margin compressing from 23.7% to 21.6% to 18.5%. The talent recovery is being offset, roughly one-for-one at the consolidated adjusted operating income line, by erosion in the segment that carries the P&L. We move to **REDUCE** from HOLD: fundamentals are not collapsing, but the price now embeds most of a normalized recovery that estimates are moving away from, and the expected return from $42.74 is negative on a probability-weighted basis even crediting the 5.5% dividend.

## Company Overview

Robert Half (founded 1948) operates three reportable segments. **Contract talent solutions** ($2,990M, 55.6% of FY2025 revenue) places engagement professionals — RHI's own employees while on assignment — in finance and accounting, technology, marketing and creative, legal, and administrative and customer support roles, earning a pay-bill spread at a 39.0% gross margin. **Permanent placement** ($440M, 8.2%) is contingent-fee search at a 99.8% gross margin with essentially all cost in SG&A; management states explicitly that it is more cyclically sensitive than contract talent. **Protiviti** ($1,950M, 36.3%) is a wholly-owned global consulting subsidiary formed in 2002 from Arthur Andersen's internal audit and risk practice, competing directly against the Big Four in internal audit, regulatory compliance, risk, technology and digital consulting.

Geographically, FY2025 was $4.17bn U.S. (77.6%) and $1.21bn international. Headcount at year-end was roughly 14,500 internal staff, of which ~7,100 sit in Protiviti, with ~94,300 engagement professionals placed during the year. Two structural features deserve emphasis. First, there is genuinely no customer concentration: the 10-K states the company is not dependent on a single customer or limited group, and no government contract exceeded 1.0% of service revenues. Second, there is essentially no backlog: the 10-K states order backlog is not material to talent solutions and is realized within twelve months at Protiviti, and quantifies no figure. Combined with management's own language that "future results cannot be reliably predicted by considering past trends," this is a business with no contracted revenue floor and very short visibility.

The blended-solutions cross-sell (RHI contract talent staffed onto Protiviti engagements, disclosed as intersegment eliminations of $116.8M in Q1 2026 alone) is a real cost advantage against Big Four pyramid staffing. It is also a two-way transmission channel, and it turned negative for the first time in the disclosed series in Q1 2026 (-0.9% reported, -2.8% adjusted) after five quarters of growth.

## Financial Analysis

**Reading the income statement correctly matters more here than at almost any comparable company.** Employee deferred compensation trust accounting routes the change in RHI's obligation to employees through SG&A while the matching investment income appears below operating income, with zero net effect on pretax or net income. Trust income was $106M in 2025 and a $100.9M gain in Q2 2026 produced a *reported operating loss of $62.3M* against adjusted operating income of +$38.6M (2.9% margin). Any analysis keyed to GAAP operating income will mis-rank this stock badly in both directions. The usable series are adjusted operating income, pretax income, and cash flow.

On that basis:

| | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $7,238.1M | $6,392.5M | $5,795.8M | $5,378.5M |
| YoY | — | -11.7% | -9.3% | -7.2% |
| Gross margin | 42.7% | 40.3% | 38.8% | 37.2% |
| Adjusted operating income | $976.8M* | — | $335.6M | $182.6M |
| Adjusted operating margin | 13.5%* | — | 5.8% | 3.4% |
| Diluted EPS | $6.03 | $3.88 | $2.44 | $1.33 |
| Diluted shares | 109.2M | 106.1M | 103.0M | 100.3M |

*2022 reported basis.

Segment adjusted operating income for FY2025: contract talent $48.6M (1.6% margin, down from 3.9%), permanent placement $21.6M (4.9%, down from 9.5%), Protiviti $112.4M (5.8%, down from 8.1%). The sum reconciles to the $182.6M MD&A total. On a reported basis the two talent segments together lost $12.1M, meaning Protiviti's $88.6M more than accounted for all consolidated reported operating income.

**Protiviti's margin compression is diagnosable and unattractive.** FY2025 revenue was flat (-0.1%) but average hourly bill rate fell 7.3% while billable hours rose 7.2%: the company substituted lower-cost, less-experienced resources into engagements. Management attributes this to mix of contractors versus full-time staff and experience levels, not to pricing pressure, and a demand shift toward lower-complexity managed-service work is an equally consistent explanation. Either way, the "configure-to-fit" resourcing model that is Protiviti's stated advantage against the Big Four and the bill-rate decline are the same mechanism viewed from two sides, and in 2025–H1 2026 the spread did not hold: adjusted gross margin 23.7% → 21.6% → 18.5% (the latter including ~$7M of severance).

**Balance sheet: resilient. Capital structure: increasingly strained by the distribution policy.** At 12/31/25 there was zero drawn funded debt, a $100M revolver (signed May 2025, matures May 2030, undrawn, covenants in compliance, $10.1M standby LCs), stockholders' equity of $1,275.9M on total assets of $2,856.3M, and a 1.53x current ratio (down from 1.656x). The screening debt/equity of 1.24x is misleading: removing the $772M fully-funded deferred compensation obligation (matched by dedicated trust assets held by an independent trustee) leaves roughly $808M of liabilities against cash and no funded debt. Solvency risk is genuinely low.

The cash-return arithmetic is the real issue:

| | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | $410M | $320M |
| Capex | $56M | $53M |
| FCF | $354M | $267M |
| Dividends | $220M | $238M |
| Buybacks | $276M | $92M |
| Total returns | $496M | $330M |
| Deficit vs. FCF | -$142M | -$63M |

Cash: $731.7M (12/31/23) → $537.6M → $464.4M → $278.4M (3/31/26) → $324.7M (6/30/26), a 55.6% decline over 30 months. Two consecutive years of distributions exceeding free cash flow have been funded from the balance sheet, and open-market repurchases went from 3.5M shares/$249M (2024) to 1.7M/$80M (2025) to **zero in Q1 2026** while the $0.59 quarterly dividend was held in both the February and August 2026 declarations. The board's priority ordering is clear: preserve the dividend, stop discretionary returns, protect liquidity.

FCF has held up better than earnings partly because working capital *releases* cash as revenue declines: 2025 OCF of $320M included a $99M positive working-capital contribution. That reverses on recovery: accounts receivable rose to $821.4M at 6/30/26 despite declining revenue. Q2 2026 operating cash flow was $109M against a $59M dividend, which is comfortable, but Q1 carries bonus payments and the payroll-tax reset and should not be extrapolated from Q2. A full-year FCF of roughly $230–270M against a $236M dividend leaves no cushion.

Reconstructing the full nine-factor Piotroski framework from 2024–2025 data yields approximately 5 of 9, with four of five fundamental *change* signals negative (ROA down, current ratio down, gross margin down, asset turnover down). The Altman Z of 3.39 is held down almost entirely by EBIT/assets of 0.027, i.e. depressed earnings rather than balance-sheet stress: for an asset-light, net-cash services firm, Z-score carries little information either way.

One data note: the profile lists both trailing and forward P/E at 35.36, which is inconsistent with the reported $1.32836 trailing EPS ($42.74 / $1.32836 = 32.2x). I use computed multiples throughout. The listed 5.82% dividend yield likewise does not reconcile to the declared $0.59 quarterly rate at $42.74 (5.52%); I use the forward declared rate.

## Growth Analysis

The inflection in talent solutions is real and quantified. Adjusted year-over-year growth by contract-talent specialization from the Q1 2026 8-K reconciliation tables:

| | Q4'24 | Q1'25 | Q2'25 | Q3'25 | Q4'25 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Finance & accounting | -9.8% | -10.0% | -10.8% | -10.7% | -7.8% | **-6.3%** |
| Admin & customer support | -9.4% | -15.2% | -13.3% | -12.1% | -12.5% | **-11.8%** |
| Technology | -4.1% | -1.3% | +0.4% | -1.9% | -1.2% | **-0.3%** |

Finance and accounting has improved 450bps from trough; technology is roughly flat; international total revenue turned positive on an adjusted basis for the first time in Q1 2026 (+0.4%). Q2 2026 delivered permanent placement +2.9%, the first year-over-year growth in that line and the most cyclically sensitive one, with July intra-quarter permanent placement running +4% in the first three weeks. Q3 guidance of $1.31–1.41bn revenue and $0.45–0.53 EPS implies Talent Solutions at roughly +3% year over year, the first guided positive figure.

Three constraints on that thesis:

**1. Administrative and customer support is not participating.** Six consecutive quarters of double-digit adjusted decline with no recovery slope, falling to $149.3M in Q1 2026 from $165.6M. RHI's own risk factors now state that "the increased availability and maturation of AI tools may enable clients to use advanced automation capabilities in lieu of services provided by the Company's contract talent personnel." Management does not attribute this line's decline to AI, and return-to-office normalization, offshore BPO substitution and client insourcing are all consistent with the same data. At roughly 11% of revenue this is not existential, but it is a persistent drag of about one point of consolidated growth annually and a haircut to terminal mix.

**2. Protiviti is going the other way, and it is where the profit is.** U.S. Protiviti adjusted growth: +3.6% (Q1'25) → -0.7% → -5.6% → -5.6% → -6.3% (Q1'26), with total Protiviti -4.9% in Q2 and guided to about -6% in Q3. Management's stated cause is softness in the U.S. financial-services regulatory environment, a risk the 10-K disclosed explicitly, noting that enforcement priorities fluctuate and Congress periodically considers relaxing SOX and Dodd-Frank requirements. Protiviti *international* grew +8.1% adjusted in Q1, which argues for a U.S.-policy headwind rather than franchise impairment, but policy cycles do not turn on a schedule. A reported ~$45M annualized Protiviti savings program would restore roughly 2.3 points of segment margin if delivered; that figure rests on a single secondary source and is unverified against any filing.

**3. The macro transaction cycle is not cooperating.** RHI monetizes job-changing behavior, not employment levels. Per management, professional (college-educated) unemployment held at 2.8% at December 2025, with overall unemployment at 4.4%, coexisting with hiring and quit rates that remain subdued and well below post-Covid highs while job openings stay well above historical levels. That configuration keeps white-collar transaction volume frozen, which is the specific variable RHI needs to reverse. [Additional 2026 labor-market data beyond the December 2025 figures in the brief is unverified.]

Net: consensus is modeling recovery ($1.27 in 2026 to $1.97 in 2027, +55%) while simultaneously cutting the near-term path. Growth prospects are positive in direction and unreliable in magnitude.

## Valuation Assessment

At the frozen $42.74 basis:

- **Trailing:** 32.2x FY2025 EPS of $1.33; 0.80x FY2025 revenue; ~3.5x book value.
- **Forward:** 33.7x current-year consensus $1.27; **21.7x next-year consensus $1.97** — on estimates cut 3.2% and 4.4% respectively over 90 days.
- **Enterprise value** (market cap less $324.7M cash, no funded debt) ≈ $3.98bn: **21.8x** FY2025 adjusted operating income of $182.6M, 11.8x 2024's $335.6M, 4.1x 2022's $976.8M.
- **Peer set:** median P/E 15.4x (KFY 14.5x, PCTY 23.2x, PAYC 15.4x; MAN and NSP loss-making). RHI trades at roughly 2.2x the peer median on current-year earnings and ~1.4x on next-year.
- **Dividend:** $2.36 annualized = 5.52% yield, ~186% of 2026E EPS of $1.27 and ~88% of FY2025 FCF.

The correct frame for a cyclical at trough is normalized earnings, not trailing. Running that honestly: adjusted operating income of $400M — between 2024's $336M and 41% of 2022's peak — plus ~$10M net interest, taxed at 28% on ~100M shares, produces about $2.80 of EPS. At 14–15x that is $39–42. Getting to a $54 bull case requires adjusted operating income near $500M and roughly $3.60 of EPS at 15x, which in turn requires contract-talent volume recovery, Protiviti stabilization, and the operating leverage to convert. The leverage is real — a 10% contract-talent revenue recovery adds $299M of revenue at a 39.0% gross margin, or $117M of gross profit, and management explicitly held revenue-producing headcount flat through 2025 to preserve capacity. But recruiter commissions and incentive compensation are variable, so realized incremental margin will be materially below 39%.

The conclusion is that $42.74 already discounts a substantially normalized outcome. The stock is expensive on trough earnings and roughly fair-to-full on plausible mid-cycle earnings, with the multiple having expanded from ~29.8x to ~33.7x current-year since the July 23 print while the estimate underlying it fell. Cheapness is not the mispricing here; the mispricing risk runs the other way.

On analyst targets: the guardrail that consensus targets act as floors applies to inexpensive inflection stocks with rising estimates. RHI is neither: it trades at 33.7x current-year with falling estimates, and the consensus mean ($35.00) and median ($31.00) sit *below* spot. When published targets are 18–27% under the market price on a stock at its 52-week high, that is a signal price has run ahead of modeled fundamentals, not evidence of stale conservatism. The ratings mix (4 sell-or-worse of 11) reinforces it.

Valuation score 3.5: a compressed-earnings multiple with a genuine recovery mechanism, but with the recovery already in the price and the near-term estimate path negative.

## Competitive Landscape

RHI's own 10-K is unusually candid that in talent solutions "the barriers to entry are quite low," competitors are many, some larger, and new entrants arrive continually, with competition driven by price and service reliability. Its differentiation claim rests on scale in remote and out-of-market placement — global brand, global candidate database, AI-driven matching — advantages it argues local and regional firms cannot match. That is management's assertion, and the segment data neither confirms nor refutes it because the entire industry is contracting: the proxy's peer-group TSR index ended 2025 at 48.26 versus RHI's 50.57, meaning the whole peer set lost roughly half its value over five years. RHI marginally outperformed over five years and underperformed that set by about 600bps in 2025 alone. Segment-level peer growth comparison is unavailable; the downturn is clearly industry-wide.

Two trajectory items are negative. First, the AI-substitution risk is now in the filing rather than in speculation, and the specialization most exposed to routinization is the one not recovering. The 10-K describes RHI as "currently evaluating how generative AI tools could enhance the business," a tentative posture for a company whose core product is candidate matching, though legal conservatism in a risk-adjacent disclosure is a plausible explanation. Second, Protiviti's bill-rate mix deterioration erodes the economics of its cost advantage against the Big Four even as it wins hours. Protiviti holds a U.S. patent pending on its Atlas enterprise AI platform with the standard no-assurance caveat.

The offsets are real: no customer concentration (rare in Industrials), full ownership of all offices since the 1986 franchise repurchase, a single consolidated brand since 2022, and a cross-sell channel the Big Four cannot cheaply replicate. Competitive position score 4.2: a scaled, structurally sound franchise whose trajectory on share of a shrinking market is unproven and whose largest specialization faces a partly secular headwind.

## Risk Assessment

**Protiviti U.S. regulatory demand (highest consequence).** The segment carrying 61.6% of FY2025 adjusted operating income is guided to -6% with gross margin down 520bps from FY2024. If enforcement priorities stay soft through 2027, consolidated adjusted operating income cannot inflect on talent recovery alone.

**Dividend coverage.** $236M annually against 2026E net income near $128M and full-year FCF of roughly $230–270M, with the buyback already sacrificed. There is no funded debt and a $100M undrawn revolver to 2030, so this is defensible for several more quarters, but it is not covered by earnings, and a recovery reverses the working-capital tailwind into a receivables funding need precisely when coverage is thinnest.

**Short interest.** This is a two-sided risk. It plausibly amplified the 95% move off the March low (including the 25%+ three-session gain July 13–16), and it means any positive surprise can produce further violent upside. It equally means the marginal holder base is unstable if the Q3 print disappoints.

**Litigation, unquantified.** Multiple certified or putative class and representative actions alleging exempt misclassification, wage-statement violations, and failure to compensate time spent in the interview process, plus California PAGA claims. The company says outcomes are unpredictable and warns that an adverse ruling could force changes to employee compensation plans, a structural cost increase to the recruiter model, not just a settlement. No accrual figures were obtained; Item 3 was not read.

**Disclosure gaps that a buyer should price.** An August 3, 2026 8-K pairs Item 5.02 (officer/director change) with Item 7.01; the Reg FD component is almost certainly the dividend declaration, but the identity and nature of the personnel change is unverified. It was not a CFO departure — Buckley signed the 10-Q on August 4. The Q2 2026 10-Q text was not retrievable, so all Q2 figures derive from secondary reporting of the company's release. The ~$45M Protiviti savings program rests on one unverified secondary source. Rising deferred tax valuation allowances ($26.4M → $33.2M) on international NOLs indicate loss-making foreign talent entities.

**Governance and structure.** No backlog and no long-term contracts; clients hold nonexclusive arrangements terminable on short notice. Management's own words: visibility into future revenue is limited.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV of 53.5% sits below one-year realized volatility of 57.1% and at the 19th percentile of the current chain, with term structure in contango (53.5% front, 57.0% at six months) after a post-earnings crush from 61.7% on July 23. Put/call open interest rose from 0.29 to 0.80 over the same window while the stock gained 12.9%, and front-month put/call *volume* is 2.78 with the source flagging elevated put demand (+4.4% skew) at the 12-day tenor; the sign inverts at three and six months. Read together: the market is not pricing a near-term dislocation, but positioning has shifted toward downside protection in the front month during the rally, and the cheapness of near-dated volatility makes protective puts an efficient alternative to outright selling for holders who want to stay exposed to squeeze upside. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±9.2%; the December tenor prices ±27.1%. The contango and low IV percentile argue against paying for a crash hedge and in favor of reducing size.

Risk score 4.3: the specific catalysts (Protiviti guidance, dividend coverage, unresolved litigation, squeeze mechanics) are not adequately reflected at a 52-week-high price with falling estimates.

## Management Quality

The compensation structure is unusually well aligned. CEO grant-date compensation fell 30% from $8.97M (2021) to $6.24M (2025); 2025 "compensation actually paid" was **negative $4.95M** for the CEO and negative $1.86M for the average NEO as unvested equity was marked down; the CEO-to-average-NEO ratio is 1.8x, a notably flat structure. Insider holdings are large and were not reduced — CEO Waddell holds 1,571,432 shares (~$67M at $42.74), Chairman Messmer 684,158.

Against that: **no open-market purchases by any officer or director between March 2025 and May 2026**, a window in which the share price fell from $53.43 to $24.82. The only open-market transaction was a director selling 6,505 shares at $28.87. Blackout mechanics around the February 10-K and April proxy limit windows, and absence of buying is weak evidence, but it is not supportive.

Capital allocation timing was poor: $249M of repurchases in 2024 and $80M in 2025 at prices as high as $53, then zero in Q1 2026 at $25 with 5.6M shares still authorized. Equity grant share counts roughly doubled in March 2026 versus March 2025 (CEO 177,276 vs. 84,839) as the price halved, holding grant dollar values roughly flat — conventional constant-dollar sizing, not evidence of self-dealing, but it means issuance accelerates during drawdowns and a larger pool participates in any recovery. Diluted shares nonetheless fell from 100.3M to 99.9M between FY2025 and Q1 2026. Retained earnings ran to approximately zero in the 2025 XBRL data as cumulative distributions consumed accumulated profits; this is not a legal constraint on dividends given $1.28bn of equity, and may be a tagging artifact, but it does mark the point at which the distribution policy is running on paid-in capital plus current earnings.

Score 4.5: tight pay alignment and large retained stakes, offset by pro-cyclical buyback timing, no conviction buying at the low, and a dividend maintained above earnings coverage.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($54, +26%).** The transaction cycle thaws. Permanent placement, the leading indicator, is already positive and running +4% in July; finance and accounting improves through zero; the preserved revenue-producing headcount converts a 10% contract-talent revenue recovery into a large gross-profit increment at 39.0% incremental gross margin. Protiviti's ~$45M savings program lands, restoring ~230bps of segment margin, and U.S. financial-services regulatory demand normalizes as enforcement priorities cycle back, while international Protiviti continues at +8%. Adjusted operating income reaches roughly $500M by 2028, EPS near $3.60, and 15x produces $54. A 26.7% short base accelerates the move. The 5.5% dividend is paid throughout from a net-cash balance sheet.

**Bear case ($27, -37%).** The recovery stays shallow because hiring remains frozen. Administrative and customer support keeps declining double digits, permanently removing ~1 point of annual growth. Protiviti's U.S. decline extends past -6% with gross margin below 19%, taking consolidated adjusted operating income below FY2025's $182.6M. The working-capital tailwind reverses as receivables build, FCF falls below the $236M dividend, and the board either draws the revolver or reconsiders the payout. Estimates continue the cuts already visible (next-quarter -12.2% in thirty days), 2027 EPS lands near $1.40, and 19x returns the stock to the $26–28 range it occupied for most of the last twelve months.

**Base case ($38, -11%).** Talent solutions grows low single digits through 2027 while Protiviti stabilizes at structurally lower margin; 2027 EPS lands in the $1.75–1.95 range against the current $1.97 consensus; normalized mid-cycle EPS of roughly $2.80 supports $39–42 at 14–15x, and the market does not pay above that for a business with no backlog, no contracted revenue, and one segment facing partial automation substitution. Fair value $36–40, midpoint $38.

Probability-weighted at 50% base / 20% bull / 30% bear: -11.4% price return, roughly -6% including the dividend. That is the arithmetic behind the downgrade.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** This is a trim, not an exit and not a short. It differs from HOLD because the probability-weighted return from $42.74 is negative and the multiple expanded ~4 turns while every forward estimate was cut. It differs from AVOID because the balance sheet carries no funded debt, adjusted operating income is *improving* year over year, Q3 guidance exceeded consensus, and 26.7% short interest with 9.4 days to cover makes aggressive negative positioning hazardous.

**De-risking plan.** Reduce position size by roughly half into current strength, retaining a residual for the operating-leverage optionality and the 5.52% yield. For holders unwilling to sell into a possible squeeze, near-dated implied volatility at 53.5% against 57.1% realized and the 19th percentile of the chain makes a September or December put spread an economically reasonable substitute for outright selling. Do not add.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE (any two):**
1. Protiviti revenue decline narrows to better than -3% year over year with adjusted gross margin recovering above 20%, confirming the savings program and demand stabilization.
2. Next-year consensus EPS stops falling and rises above $2.07 (the 90-day-ago level), with next-quarter revisions turning positive.
3. Price at or below $34, which puts the stock at roughly 17x next-year consensus and near the base-case midpoint discount.
4. Clustered open-market insider purchases by two or more C-suite officers — absent for the entire drawdown and therefore informative if it appears.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID (any one):**
1. Q3 2026 EPS below the $0.45 guidance low end, particularly if driven by Protiviti rather than the tax line, which would break the Q3 bridge that requires cost savings, seasonality and a normal tax rate all to work.
2. Quarterly dividend reduced or suspended, or the revolver drawn to fund distributions.
3. Contract talent adjusted growth failing to reach positive by Q4 2026, indicating the inflection has stalled.
4. Disclosure of a material accrual or adverse ruling in the wage-and-hour/PAGA matters that changes recruiter compensation economics.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q3 2026 print (late October) is the decisive checkpoint: it tests the $0.45–0.53 guide, the Protiviti savings program, whether Talent Solutions delivered the guided ~+3%, the effective tax rate after Q1's 56.1% anomaly, and whether the intersegment cross-sell decline was noise or trend. Interim monitoring: the Q2 10-Q detail (balance sheet, cash flow, tax footnote, Item 3) once retrievable, and the unresolved August 3 Item 5.02 personnel change.

## Conclusion

Robert Half's operating trough appears to be behind it, and the evidence for that is in the filings rather than in commentary: adjusted operating income up 53% year over year in Q1 2026, permanent placement back to growth, three consecutive quarters of sequential adjusted revenue gains in Talent Solutions, guidance above consensus, and no funded debt anywhere in the capital structure. That case is legitimate and it explains why the score trajectory has improved from 4.6 in March to the 5.0–5.1 band.

What has changed is the price relative to the numbers. The stock has advanced 95% off its March low to within 3% of its 52-week high while consensus cut current-year EPS 3.2%, next-year 4.4%, and next-quarter 12.2%, pushing the current-year multiple from roughly 29.8x at the July print to 33.7x, or 2.2x the peer median, against published targets whose mean and median sit 18% and 27% *below* spot. Meanwhile the segment producing 61.6% of adjusted operating income is guided to -6% with 520bps of gross-margin erosion, the dividend runs near 186% of forecast earnings against a cash balance down 55.6% in 30 months, the buyback has been switched off to protect it, and the specialization most exposed to automation has failed to recover for six straight quarters.
