**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

BGC Group is one of a handful of scaled global interdealer brokers (IDBs), intermediating trades between the world's largest banks, trading firms, and commodity producers across energy/commodities, rates, FX, credit, and equities. The company is compounding revenue rapidly: full-year 2025 revenue rose 30% to $2.94B, and Q1 2026 delivered a record $955.5M (+43.8% YoY, +23.0% organic ex-OTC Global). Every asset class grew, the high-margin electronic franchise (Fenics) hit a record $206.9M in quarterly revenue, and the FMX exchange venture is taking share from CME in U.S. Treasuries (41% central-limit-order-book share, up from 33% a year earlier). This is an improving business with an embedded, bank-validated call option on disrupting CME's near-monopoly in U.S. rate futures.

The counterweights are equally concrete. The bulk of revenue is volume-sensitive brokerage tied to trading activity, rates, volatility, and issuance, a tailwind in the current higher-rate regime that management explicitly frames as reversible. The FMX futures challenge is being waged from a tiny base against CME's open-interest network effects and is unproven. Governance is the defining caveat: Cantor Fitzgerald and the Lutnick family control ~74.7% of voting power through super-voting Class B shares, control just passed from Howard Lutnick (now U.S. Commerce Secretary) to his son Brandon via a family voting agreement, and the operating company runs on a three-co-CEO structure amid pervasive related-party dealing with Cantor. Insider activity shows no open-market conviction buying, only routine grant-related dispositions and small executive sales.

At ~$11.21 (recovering to ~$11.30) the stock trades near ~24x current-year consensus EPS of $1.41 and ~36x trailing EPS of $0.31, after a run from the mid-$8s in late 2025 to a $12.88 high in June 2026. Estimate revisions have drifted modestly negative across every window. The growth is real and the FMX optionality is worth owning, but the setup has moved from a clear buy earlier in the year to a hold: the market now prices the compounding story, and the asymmetry that existed at $9 has narrowed. We rate BGC a HOLD (growth-hold rationale), constructive on the franchise but disciplined on entry.

## Company Overview

BGC intermediates trades across five asset classes, executed through Voice, Hybrid, and Fully Electronic modes:

- **Energy, Commodities & Shipping (ECS)**: now the largest asset class at ~37% of brokerage revenue after the April 2025 OTC Global acquisition made BGC the world's largest ECS broker by revenue.
- **Rates** (~29%): government bonds, rate futures/options, interest-rate swaps.
- **FX** (~15%), **Credit** (~10%), **Equities** (~9%, mostly equity derivatives).

The company reports as a single brokerage-services segment with ~2,487 front-office producers as of Q1 2026, up ~15% YoY on the OTC Global deal. The geographic footprint centers on the U.K. (~38% of revenue) and the U.S. (~31%).

Two structural engines differentiate BGC from a plain-vanilla IDB. **Fenics** is the higher-margin electronic franchise (Fenics Markets + Fenics Growth Platforms), targeting pre-tax margins ≥25% for Fenics Integrated. **FMX** is a majority-owned exchange venture building a U.S. rates-and-FX ecosystem (cash Treasuries, SOFR and Treasury futures, and spot FX) in direct competition with CME. In April 2024, ten major banks and market-makers (BofA, Barclays, Citadel Securities, Citi, Goldman, JPMorgan, Jump, Morgan Stanley, Tower Research, Wells Fargo) invested $171.7M for 25.75% of FMX at a $666.7M post-money valuation, with a further 10.3% earnout tied to volume targets.

BGC competes with TP ICAP and Tradition in voice/hybrid, Tradeweb and MarketAxess in electronic, CME via FMX in futures, and Marex, StoneX, and Clarksons in ECS.

Q1 2026 pre-tax income was $115.4M (up from $80.0M), net income to common $84.1M, diluted EPS $0.17 (up from $0.11). Growth was broad-based: ECS +120% (OTC Global plus organic), Rates +27.5%, FX +19.1%, Equities +34.3%, Credit +8.2%.

The cost structure is improving at the margin. Total compensation ran ~65% of revenue in Q1 2026 (56.2% cash + 8.8% equity), and the equity-comp mix is declining (8.8% vs 11.4% a year earlier), which is margin-accretive. A cost-reduction program targets ~$35M in annualized savings, with $16.3M of charges recognized in Q1 2026. Pre-tax margin sits at ~12%.

Two balance-sheet items require correct interpretation, both of which a naive screen misreads:

- **Q1 2026 total assets ballooned from $4.41B (YE 2025) to $5.86B**, but this is almost entirely matched-principal settlement timing (fails-to-deliver receivables of $1.83B offset by fails-to-receive payables of $1.63B) that nets out and settles within days. This is not a leverage event.
- **The –$910.4M retained deficit** is a legacy artifact of the July 2023 Up-C partnership-to-C-corp conversion and heavy historical equity-based compensation, not operating losses. The company is solidly profitable and generated ~$373M of FCF in 2025.

Debt is manageable but real: fixed-rate senior notes total $1,549.5M (8.0% due 2028, 6.6% due 2029, 6.15% due 2030), plus a $700M revolver with $240M drawn (repaid $25M in May 2026), for a weighted-average debt cost of 6.55%. Cash was $851.5M and non-GAAP liquidity $878.4M at Q1 2026. Credit ratings sit at the low end of investment grade: Fitch BBB- (stable), S&P BBB- (stable), JCR BBB+ (stable), Kroll BBB (positive). Goodwill of $648.6M plus intangibles of $428M are rising with acquisitions.

Capital allocation has shifted from dividends (held at $0.02/qtr) toward buybacks ($386.9M remaining on a $400M authorization). Repurchases in Q2 2026 ran at ~$11.27/share versus ~$9.17 in Q1; buying accelerated as the stock rose, which is the opposite of a value-conscious repurchase pattern.

## Growth Analysis

The revenue growth is durable at the franchise level and broad-based across asset classes, which distinguishes it from a single-product volatility spike. Producer headcount is up ~15% YoY, Fenics electronic revenue is compounding near 20%, and FMX UST cash Treasuries hit record Q1 2026 ADV of $89.7B with 41% order-book share. The OTC Global acquisition (~0.75x revenue, ~14.5% of Q1 revenue) was bought cheaply and made BGC the #1 ECS broker, a well-executed, accretive deal.

Three qualifiers temper the growth score. First, Q2 2026 guidance of $785M–$845M is sequentially lower than Q1's $955M; Q1 benefited from a volatility spike (the Iran conflict that began late February) and is seasonally strong. Management noted revenue was already +41% YoY through February 27, so the conflict was only incremental, but the sequential step-down illustrates how volume-sensitive the model is. Second, consensus EPS estimates have drifted modestly lower: current-year estimates fell 1.1% over 90 days, next-year 0.9%, and the current quarter 1.5%. This is negative revision momentum, not an upward move that would flag a re-rating candidate. Third, the earnings track record is solid but unspectacular: 2 beats and 2 in-line over the last four quarters, with the most recent (Q1 2026) landing exactly in line. Consensus visibility is reasonable; upside surprises are not the base case.

The FMX futures challenge is the highest-upside growth lever and the least certain. FMX Futures launched Treasury futures (May 2025) atop SOFR futures (Sep 2024), and open interest is growing off a tiny base against CME's entrenched network effects. The cash-Treasuries win proves FMX can take electronic share; the far higher-value futures battle is being fought from weakness against CME's deep, self-reinforcing liquidity pools.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$11.21, BGC trades at ~36x trailing EPS of $0.31 and ~24x current-year consensus of $1.41 (~$1.58 next year). On the reported forward P/E metric the profile shows ~30.7x, which mirrors the trailing measure; on consensus current-year EPS the multiple is closer to the low-20s.

Against peers, the picture is mixed. Investment-banking and brokerage comparables trade at a median ~22.0x P/E (PJT 22.0, MC 23.2, PIPR 19.7, HLI 21.8, SNEX 23.0). BGC sits at a modest premium, arguably justified by its 30%+ revenue growth versus the more cyclical advisory-heavy peers, but not a screaming discount.

The critical valuation discipline here: cheapness alone is not a buy signal, and this stock is not cheap on reported forward metrics. The bull case rests on the FMX call option, which the market is now at least partly pricing after the run to a $12.88 June high. Estimate revisions are drifting negative, which argues against treating current consensus targets (mean/median $15.50, range $15.00–$16.00) as reliable floors: analyst targets act as floors on stocks with rising estimates, and BGC's estimates are falling. Only two analysts cover the name, so the target dispersion carries low information content. The asymmetry that existed near $9 has narrowed materially; at ~$11, the upside to consensus (~38%) is real but rests on FMX optionality and continued volume tailwinds, both of which are two-sided.

We score valuation a 5.2: no longer offering the clear asymmetric upside of earlier in the year, and with negative revision momentum and a full multiple relative to the peer group's growth.

## Competitive Landscape

The IDB industry has consolidated over a decade into a few scaled players (BGC, TP ICAP, Tradition), giving BGC pricing scale and a broad product footprint. Its competitive trajectory is improving in several concrete ways: the OTC Global deal established BGC as the #1 ECS broker, Fenics electronic revenue is compounding near 20%, and FMX UST is taking documented share from CME (33% → 41% order-book share year-over-year).

The moat structure is nuanced. In voice/hybrid brokerage, the moat is producer relationships and scale, defensible but not expanding rapidly. In electronic execution, BGC competes with Tradeweb and MarketAxess, both formidable. The FMX futures venture is the offensive bet: if it gains durable share in U.S. rate futures (the world's deepest futures complex, long dominated by CME), the equity optionality is large, and ten major banks have already validated a $667M valuation with capital at risk plus a volume-linked earnout. CME's moat is open-interest network effects (traders concentrate where liquidity is deepest), which is the hardest moat to attack from a small base. The cash-Treasuries win is meaningful; the futures battle remains contested from weakness. On balance, BGC's competitive position is improving, with the trajectory better than the current level.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Cantor/Lutnick control and conflicts**: the single most distinctive risk. Cantor Fitzgerald and the Lutnick family control ~74.7% of voting power via 10-votes-per-share Class B stock. BGC is intertwined with Cantor through clearing services, a clearing-capital agreement, a $400M-each-way intercompany credit line, admin-services charges (~$36M/quarter), FX hedging profit-sharing, and 52%-owned Tower Bridge. CF&Co acts as BGC's underwriter/advisor. Class A holders have minimal governance power, and the buyback authorization explicitly permits repurchases from Cantor and affiliates, meaning buybacks partly serve as an insider liquidity mechanism.

2. **Succession/key-person transition**: Howard Lutnick fully divested and left to become U.S. Commerce Secretary (Oct 6, 2025); control passed to son Brandon Lutnick (~74.9% voting power via a new Lutnick Family Voting Agreement), while day-to-day management runs through a three-co-CEO structure (Abularrage, Aubin, Windeatt). Leadership-transition risk is elevated.

3. **Revenue cyclicality/volume dependence**: transaction-based revenue is tied to volumes, volatility, rates, and issuance. Management explicitly notes the current higher-rate, higher-volatility regime is a tailwind that could reverse. A verified de-escalation of Middle East tensions and a lower-volatility regime would compress volumes.

4. **Integration and internal-control risk**: rapid M&A (OTC Global $309M, Macro Hive, AMCOM, Sage). OTC Global, Macro Hive, and AMCOM are excluded from internal-control-over-financial-reporting assessment in year one.

5. **Leverage/refinancing**: ~$1.79B debt at 6.55% average cost with 2028/2029/2030 maturities; BBB- ratings sit at the low end of investment grade in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

6. **FMX execution risk**: heavy investment against an entrenched CME; success requires sustained bank-partner engagement to hit volume/earnout targets and is not guaranteed.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign-to-constructive in the near term. One-month ATM IV of 32.6% sits essentially in line with 1-year historical volatility of 31.3% (fair, IV percentile just 4% within the current chain), and near-term skew is call-heavy (-11.4%) with a low put/call volume ratio of 0.15. The term structure is in steep contango (near-term 32.6% vs 6-month 58.4%), a normal/complacent structure, and ATM IV collapsed from 56.9% to 32.6% over the past month as the post-earnings event premium bled out. Near-term positioning is calm-to-mildly-bullish rather than pricing imminent downside. Short interest is low at 2.9% of float (2.1 days to cover), so there is neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup. The elevated longer-dated IV (59% at 3 months) reflects the two-sided nature of the FMX and volume-regime bets rather than a directional signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$16+):** Revenue keeps compounding at 20%+ organic as Fenics electronification and producer additions extend across all five asset classes. FMX futures gains durable share against CME, the 10.3% earnout triggers, and the market re-rates the exchange optionality embedded in a ~$4B market cap. The cost program delivers $35M in savings, equity-comp mix continues declining, and pre-tax margins expand toward the mid-teens. The name re-rates toward the consensus $15–$16 range and beyond as FMX is valued as a standalone exchange asset.

**Bear case (~$8):** A verified de-escalation of geopolitical tensions and a return to lower volatility compress brokerage volumes, exposing the volume-sensitivity of the model. FMX futures stalls against CME's network effects, the earnout lapses, and the growth premium in the multiple deflates. Integration issues surface in the internal-control-excluded acquisitions, and the Lutnick/Cantor governance overhang reasserts itself as a valuation discount. Estimate revisions continue drifting negative and the stock retraces toward the mid-$8s where it traded in late 2025.

**Base case (~$12–$13):** Revenue growth moderates from the Q1 spike toward the mid-teens organically, Fenics compounds, FMX makes incremental but unspectacular futures progress, and margins improve modestly on the cost program. The stock holds a modest premium to the ~22x peer median on its superior growth but does not re-rate dramatically absent a decisive FMX gain in share.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings report, when we can measure revenue against the $785M–$845M guide, track FMX futures open-interest trends, and gauge whether estimate revisions stabilize or continue falling. FMX monthly volume/share data is a continuous input.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FMX Futures open interest and ADV compound sequentially for two consecutive quarters, signaling durable share capture from CME rather than launch-base noise.
- Consensus EPS revisions turn positive (current-year estimate rising rather than the current -1.1% 90-day drift), confirming the fundamental momentum is re-accelerating.
- A pullback to the low-$10s that restores asymmetry against the ~$15 consensus, or clear evidence the cost program is expanding pre-tax margin above ~14%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A verified low-volatility regime shift that compresses brokerage volumes below guidance for two consecutive quarters, exposing the volume-sensitivity risk.
- Material governance or related-party action that transfers value to Cantor/Lutnick at Class A holders' expense.
- An integration or internal-control issue surfacing in OTC Global/Macro Hive/AMCOM, or FMX futures share stalling with the earnout lapsing.

## Conclusion

BGC is a scaled, improving interdealer broker compounding revenue at 30%+ with a bank-validated exchange-disruption call option in FMX and an improving electronic (Fenics) mix. The franchise quality is real, the balance-sheet "red flags" (retained deficit, ballooning total assets) are accounting artifacts rather than distress, and the cost program plus declining equity-comp mix should support margin. Those positives are offset by heavy volume-sensitivity that management itself frames as regime-dependent, a FMX futures challenge being waged from weakness against CME's network effects, concentrated Lutnick/Cantor control amid a leadership transition, no insider conviction buying, and estimate revisions that have drifted modestly negative.

At ~$11 following the run from the mid-$8s, the market now prices the compounding story, and the asymmetry that justified a BUY earlier in 2026 has narrowed. This is a growth-hold: own it for the FMX optionality and the durable IDB franchise, but the risk/reward argues for patience: accumulate on a pullback to the low-$10s or on confirmation that FMX futures share is compounding, rather than chasing near record highs. We rate BGC **HOLD**.
