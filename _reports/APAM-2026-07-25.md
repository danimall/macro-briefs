**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Artisan Partners is a high-fee, active investment manager that generates exceptional profitability from an asset-light structure, pays out roughly 80% of quarterly cash plus a special annual dividend, and sits squarely on the losing side of the multi-decade migration from active long-only equity into passive index products and alternatives. The investment debate reduces to a single tension: this is a high-quality cash machine that is organically shrinking in its core business. At ~$39.34 the stock offers a high-single-digit variable dividend yield and trades at ~9.7x trailing earnings, a large discount to its asset-management peer group. That discount is the market's rational adjustment for persistent equity outflows and the absence of a credible organic-growth engine, not a mispricing waiting to close.

Two recent developments crystallize the bear case rather than resolve it. In June 2026 a single sub-advisory mandate termination removed roughly $5.7B (about 3% of AUM), and management has begun an orderly wind-down of the entire U.S. Value team through Q3 2026. Both events are direct realizations of the two named structural risks in the firm's filings: contracts terminable on short notice, and secular pressure on underperforming long-only equity franchises. The offsetting positives are real but thinner: headline AUM has recovered to ~$183.4B on market strength, fee rates have held stable at ~67 bps, and management is pursuing a disciplined pivot into credit and real estate alternatives (the Grandview acquisition, its first-ever deal, closed January 2026). Credit and alternatives are the only asset classes generating net inflows, but at ~11% and ~3% of AUM respectively, they are far too small to offset equity attrition in the near term.

We rate APAM a HOLD on an income basis. The dividend yield and stable fee economics justify continued ownership for a total-return investor willing to accept a variable payout, but the equity-outflow overhang caps price appreciation and the multiple has no visible catalyst to re-rate. The score trajectory has declined from 6.0 (March) to a range around 5.0-5.2 over four reports, and nothing in the current data reverses that trend; the U.S. Value wind-down confirms the deterioration in organic health. We would upgrade only on a stabilization of equity net flows or a demonstrable acceleration in the credit/alternatives pivot.

## Company Overview

Artisan Partners manages $173.0 billion in AUM as of March 31, 2026 across 12 autonomous investment teams and 27 strategies. The defining feature of the business is its "autonomous franchise" model: each investment team operates independently with a direct revenue-share compensation structure, generally receiving roughly 25% of the asset-based revenue their strategies generate plus a share of performance fees. This aligns talent economically and produces genuine long-term outperformance in several strategies, but it makes the firm heavily dependent on retaining individual portfolio managers.

The company is the public holding company and general partner of Artisan Partners Holdings LP, owning about 88% of Holdings under an Up-C structure. That structure generates a $305M Tax Receivable Agreement liability (potentially ~$420M ultimately) and $346M of deferred tax assets, and the TRA can accelerate on a change of control, a meaningful takeover deterrent.

Distribution splits roughly 62% Intermediated Wealth and 38% Institutional, with about 74% of AUM held by U.S.-domiciled clients. Roughly 48-50% of AUM is invested in non-U.S. securities or non-USD currencies, so a strong dollar is a direct revenue headwind. By asset class, the book is approximately 86-88% equity, 11% credit, and 3% alternatives. That mix is the strategic center of the story: Artisan is trying to shift away from long-only equity, where assets are leaving, into credit and alternatives, where they are arriving.

## Financial Analysis

The profitability profile is strong and the fee economics are unusually durable for the industry. Q1 2026 revenue was $303.0M, up 9% year over year, though that gain came from a 9% rise in average AUM (market appreciation) rather than organic growth. Management fees are essentially the entire revenue line at ~99.9%. The weighted average fee rate held at 67.4 bps versus 67.5 bps a year earlier, defying the fee compression that has hit most active managers; Artisan Funds and Global Funds earn ~87.8 bps, separate accounts ~47.9 bps.

GAAP and adjusted operating margins were both 31.1%, with compensation running a steady ~56% of revenue. Q1 2026 net income to APAM was $58.0M ($0.76 GAAP EPS, $0.87 adjusted EPS), down modestly from $61.1M/$0.82 a year prior. The decline was entirely non-operating: a $7.6M net investment loss on seed and franchise-capital hedge investments versus a $10.9M gain in the prior-year quarter. The franchise-capital compensation structure (~$147.8M of unvested awards hedged by matching investments) nets to zero over vesting but creates quarter-to-quarter noise in non-operating income, so adjusted EPS of $0.87 is the better run-rate gauge. ROE exceeds 80% because the equity base is thin (~$433M) relative to earnings, which is normal for an asset-light manager that distributes nearly all its cash.

The balance sheet is clean. Total debt is only $190M of fixed-rate unsecured notes (Series E 4.53% due 2027, F 3.10% due 2032, G 5.43% due 2030), against $271M cash and an undrawn $100M revolver, leaving net debt near zero. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was a robust $181.8M. The debt covenant that would force a prepayment offer if average quarterly AUM fell below $45B is not a near-term concern at ~$182B. The Altman Z-Score sits in the grey zone, but that is a structural artifact of asset-light financials (minimal inventory, thin equity, high payout), not a distress signal.

Capital allocation is the primary attraction for income investors. APAM pays out roughly 80% of quarterly cash generation as a variable quarterly dividend, plus a special annual dividend from the retained 20%. Q1 2026 dividends declared totaled $1.58/share ($1.01 quarterly + $0.57 special), with a further $0.77 quarterly dividend declared for the March quarter. That supports a high-single-digit yield at ~$39, but the payout is explicitly variable and contracts with AUM and earnings.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is negative and structurally so. Q1 2026 net client cash outflows were -$3.1B, with equity strategies losing -$4.2B partially offset by +$1.1B into credit and alternatives. Management stated plainly that if recent performance and market trends persist, equity net outflows could continue throughout the remainder of 2026. With the majority of AUM in long-only equity, the firm is directly exposed to the secular reallocation toward passive and alternatives, a risk it flags in its own filings.

The U.S. Value team wind-down (Value Equity, U.S. Mid-Cap Value, Value Income — roughly $7.5B combined at 3/31/26) will pressure Q2-Q3 organic flows further. This is a shrink-to-quality story where management is pruning underperforming franchises to protect long-term culture and integrity, accepting near-term revenue loss in the process.

The estimate revision picture is mildly mixed but does not signal a turn. Current-quarter EPS estimates ticked up +1.9% over 30 days but are down -1.1% over 90 days; current-year estimates are essentially flat at $3.83 (down 0.2% over 30 days, down 1.7% over 90 days); next-year sits at $4.01. There is no earnings acceleration here that would trigger a cycle-adjusted valuation re-rating; revisions are hovering around flat, consistent with an AUM level that recovers on market beta while organic flows leak.

The growth optionality lies entirely in the credit and alternatives pivot. High Income ($13.5B), Custom Credit Solutions, EMsights EM debt, Antero Peak, and the newly acquired Grandview real estate platform are the only inflow generators. Grandview added ~$0.9B AUM and $2.1M of Q1 revenue against a $22.5M upfront cash cost plus up to ~$25.9M contingent consideration and up to $50M of seed capital commitments. It is strategically important as diversification but financially immaterial today. For the pivot to change the growth trajectory, credit and alternatives would need to scale by multiples from their current ~14% combined share, and the firm's own 10-K acknowledges that its long-only reputation is "an impediment" to selling alternatives. This is a multi-year proposition with meaningful execution risk, not a near-term catalyst.

## Valuation Assessment

APAM trades at ~9.7x trailing and forward P/E with a high-single-digit dividend yield, against an asset-management peer median P/E of 33.1 and median yield of 2.6%. On the surface this looks like a deep-value opportunity. The peer set is heterogeneous — WisdomTree at 40.7x, Acadian at 33.1x, Cohen & Steers at 25.2x, StepStone at negative earnings, and Hamilton Lane at 13.3x — and the low-multiple comparables (HLNE, and StepStone at a -42% discount to its 52-week high) are alternatives-focused managers with their own idiosyncratic pressures. The most relevant read is that APAM's discount to traditional-manager peers reflects a specific structural problem, not a broad-sector mispricing.

The discipline here is to treat a cheap multiple on a structurally challenged franchise as a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal. There is no identified catalyst to close the gap. The earnings base is not at a cyclical trough that will normalize higher; it is at a level being eroded by organic outflows, with only market appreciation providing offset. The stock has appreciated ~16% off its July 1 low of $33.97, recovering with AUM, but that recovery is beta-driven and does not signal a fundamental turn. Analyst targets underscore the ceiling: consensus mean is $36.75 and median $35.50, both below the current $39.34, with a high of $43 and a low of $33. On a challenged franchise trading below analyst consensus, those targets function as a ceiling; the analyst-targets-as-floor logic applies only to cheap, high-momentum inflection stories, which this is not.

The fair-value case rests on the dividend. Valued as a variable-payout cash machine yielding high single digits with stable fee rates and no leverage risk, the current price is defensible on income grounds. But the payout is levered to AUM and would contract in any market drawdown or accelerating outflow, so the yield is not a fixed anchor. We score valuation at 5.0 — neither compellingly cheap (because the cheapness is structural impairment, not mispricing) nor stretched (the absolute yield and margin profile provide genuine support). The valuation dimension has historically sorted the wrong way when cheapness lacks a catalyst, and that caution applies directly here.

## Competitive Landscape

Artisan's genuine differentiation is long-term investment outperformance in a subset of strategies: International Value has added +508 bps annualized since inception versus MSCI EAFE, Global Value +296 bps, Non-U.S. Growth +467 bps, and EMsights strategies +600-750+ bps. It has defended a premium fee rate against industry-wide compression and serves an institutional-quality client base with longer horizons. Those are real moats.

The trajectory, however, is deteriorating, which is what the competitive score must weight. Several flagships are underperforming and fueling the outflows: Global Opportunities lags its benchmark over 1/3/5-year windows, U.S. Small-Cap Growth is negative over 5 years, Developing World is deeply negative, and the now-exiting Value Equity trailed the Russell 1000 across all periods. The firm does not offer passive, target-date, "solutions," or impact products, and it concedes in its own filings that its long-only reputation impedes alternatives sales. The 5-year cumulative TSR of 121.7 has badly lagged the peer group's 196.1, a direct market verdict on the equity-outflow overhang.

The strategic response — pruning weak equity franchises while growing credit and alternatives — is the right long-term posture, but competitively the firm is defending share in a shrinking category while trying to establish credibility in categories where it is a latecomer with an unhelpful brand association. Trajectory over level drives the 4.0 score: a differentiated manager losing share in its core with an unproven expansion is a lower score than a challenger gaining share.

## Risk Assessment

**Persistent equity net outflows** are the central risk and are actively materializing rather than latent. Q1 flows, the June mandate loss, and the U.S. Value wind-down all point the same direction, and management has explicitly warned of continued equity outflows through 2026.

**Client-concentration and mandate-termination risk** is now realized. Substantially all revenue comes from contracts terminable on short or no notice; the two largest intermediary relationships are each ~9% of AUM, and the largest consultant influences ~4%. A single termination just removed ~3% of AUM. (Note: the specific $5.7B figure derives from a syndicated summary of Artisan's own July 10 AUM release and should be corroborated against the next 10-Q, though the underlying event is company-disclosed.)

**Key-person dependence** is acute given the revenue-share model. Portfolio manager departures have historically driven outflows and consultant "hold" recommendations, and non-competes run only one year and are not enforceable everywhere.

**Compensation-structure friction** is a named risk that rises as the firm pushes into alternatives with economics different from the traditional equity model. The 10-K states that new comp arrangements have "in the past led to friction within our investment teams."

**Structural/TRA risk** — the ~$305M TRA liability can accelerate on a change of control, which would also risk automatic termination of the 1940-Act mutual fund advisory agreements. **FX exposure** on ~50% non-USD AUM adds revenue volatility, and the $45B AUM covenant, while far off, is a tail risk in a severe market decline.

The dividend risk deserves emphasis: it looks attractive but is variable and levered to AUM, so it will contract in any drawdown or continued outflow. This is not a safe fixed payout. We score risk factors at 4.5 — several specific, non-priced catalysts for downside exist (further mandate losses, PM departures, continued equity attrition), and the concentration risk has just demonstrated it can move ~3% of AUM in a single event.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. ATM implied volatility has risen to 36.2% versus 25.8% trailing historical volatility (a rich 10.4pp spread), IV jumped ~10 points over the past month alongside a ~13% price recovery, and the term structure is in backwardation (36.2% near-term versus 32.9% at six months), which signals the market expects a defined near-term event. The put/call skew of 6.8% shows elevated put demand. This is consistent with uncertainty around Q2-Q3 flows and the U.S. Value repositioning, and it confirms the fundamental caution: the market is hedging a downside catalyst, not positioning for a re-rating. The rich near-term IV also argues against chasing the recent bounce, since implied vol tends to crush after the earnings/AUM catalyst resolves.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** APAM is a high-margin (31% operating margin), asset-light franchise with a rock-solid balance sheet (net debt near zero, $271M cash), a fee rate that has defied industry compression at ~67 bps, and a high-single-digit variable dividend yield. Management is showing rare discipline in pruning underperforming franchises to protect long-term culture, and the credit/alternatives pivot (High Income, EMsights, Grandview real estate) targets the only categories generating inflows. If equity outflows stabilize and the pivot scales, the stock re-rates toward the peer group off a deep discount, and the dividend provides a floor to total return in the interim. Bull case ~$46-48 (return toward the 52-week high of $48.46 on flow stabilization plus dividend).

**Bear Case.** The core long-only equity business (86-88% of AUM) is in secular decline, and the June mandate loss plus U.S. Value wind-down demonstrate the concentration and outflow risks are live, not theoretical. Market beta is the only thing masking organic shrinkage; in a market drawdown, AUM, revenue, and the variable dividend all fall together, and the "cheap, high-yield" thesis unwinds fast. The credit/alternatives pivot is too small (~14% of AUM) and too early to offset equity attrition, and the firm's long-only brand impedes the transition. Estimates are flat-to-down, analyst targets sit below the current price, and there is no catalyst to close the valuation gap. Bear case ~$34 (retest of the July low of $33.97, roughly -14% from current).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The thesis rests on the dividend yield and stable fee economics, not on expected price appreciation. Own APAM for its variable high-single-digit yield with the understanding that the payout and the stock are levered to AUM.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Equity net flows turn positive, or net outflows narrow materially (e.g., equity outflows below -$1B in a quarter) for two consecutive quarters, signaling stabilization of the core.
- Credit and alternatives combined AUM inflect meaningfully higher (e.g., combined net inflows exceeding equity net outflows for two quarters), demonstrating the pivot is offsetting core decline.
- A pullback toward the low-$30s that pushes the yield high enough to compensate for the outflow risk, restoring asymmetric total-return math.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second material mandate loss or a portfolio-manager departure from a large strategy, indicating the concentration/key-person risk is compounding.
- A dividend cut driven by AUM/earnings decline that removes the income thesis.
- Adjusted operating margin compression below ~28% signaling the fee-rate defense is failing or comp friction is escalating.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and the monthly AUM releases through Q3 2026, which will reveal the flow impact of the U.S. Value wind-down and whether the credit/alternatives pivot is gaining traction. The elevated implied volatility suggests the next print is a defined catalyst.

## Conclusion

Artisan Partners is a well-run, highly profitable active manager that returns nearly all its cash to shareholders and defends a premium fee rate against industry compression. It is also organically shrinking in the business that generates the bulk of its assets, and the June 2026 mandate loss plus the U.S. Value wind-down confirm that the structural outflow problem is accelerating, not abating. The ~9.7x P/E and high dividend yield look cheap, but the discount reflects a genuine secular headwind rather than a mispricing, and there is no organic-growth catalyst to re-rate the multiple. Analyst consensus sits below the current price, estimates are flat-to-down, and the options market is pricing a near-term downside event.

We rate APAM a HOLD on an income basis. The dividend and fee economics justify continued ownership for a total-return investor comfortable with a variable payout, but the equity-outflow overhang caps appreciation. The score of 4.95 sits in line with the four-report trajectory around 5.0, and the current data confirms rather than reverses the decline. Own it for the yield; do not expect the multiple to work in your favor until organic flows stabilize or the credit/alternatives pivot demonstrably scales.
