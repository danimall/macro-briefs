## Executive Summary

Redwood Trust is a specialty finance company undergoing a strategic transformation from a balance-sheet-intensive REIT toward a capital-efficient, originate-to-distribute mortgage banking platform. The company achieved record $22.7B of mortgage banking production in FY2025 (more than doubling the prior year) and followed with another record $8.5B quarter in Q1 2026. Operating leverage is materializing: cost per loan fell 30% QoQ in Q1 2026 to below 20bps, and the April 2026 Castlelake joint venture (up to $8B of prime jumbo capacity) validates the platform model with meaningful third-party capital.

The core challenge is that GAAP results remain obscured by a Legacy Investments segment that destroyed $147M of value in 2025, offsetting nearly all mortgage banking earnings. With Legacy now reduced to 19% of allocated capital (from 33% mid-2025) and losses narrowing to $13M in Q1 2026, sustained profitability appears proximate. The stock trades at a 22% discount to $7.12 book value and offers a ~13% dividend yield.

Against this, material risks persist: $297M of convertible notes maturing in 2027, HEI regulatory/litigation exposure that could impair $148M of assets, elevated mortgage rates constraining volume growth, and structural sensitivity to credit market disruptions that could shut down securitization channels. The options market is pricing near-term stress (48.1% IV in backwardation vs. 35.6% HV), warranting caution despite the apparent value.

## Company Overview

Redwood Trust operates across four segments in the non-agency residential mortgage ecosystem:

- **Sequoia Mortgage Banking** (including Aspire): Acquires prime jumbo and expanded-credit/non-QM loans from originators, distributing via proprietary SEMT® securitizations or whole-loan sales. Achieved 7% estimated jumbo market share in 2025, up from 1-2% historically.
- **CoreVest**: Originates residential investor bridge and term loans (primarily to small operators owning <100 units), distributed via CAFL® securitizations or sales.
- **Redwood Investments**: Holds retained securitization interests (subordinate tranches, IOs), MSRs, and securities.
- **Legacy Investments**: Non-core wind-down portfolio containing 2021-2022 vintage bridge loans with elevated delinquencies, re-performing loan securities, and HEI positions.

The company has 351 employees, is headquartered in Mill Valley, CA, and operates with $23.7B in total assets (largely driven by consolidated securitization VIEs with non-recourse ABS liabilities).

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three pillars:

**1. Volume Scaling:** From ~$10.7B combined mortgage banking production in FY2024 to $22.7B in FY2025 to $8.5B in Q1 2026 alone ($34B annualized run rate). The Castlelake JV adds up to $8B of incremental prime jumbo capacity without requiring equity raises. Market share expansion is driven by bank retrenchment from non-agency lending, Basel III capital requirements, and Redwood's platform efficiency.

**2. Operating Leverage:** Cost per loan declining consistently (30% QoQ improvement in Q1 2026 to below 20bps) driven by technology investment (2,500 AI agentic workflows) and volume throughput. With only 351 employees, marginal volume requires minimal incremental fixed cost.

**3. Platform Expansion — Aspire:** The expanded-credit/non-QM platform reached $1.6B lock volume in Q1 2026 (~$6.4B annualized), is now securitizing independently, and completed its first whole-loan sale to a bank in Q4 2025. This addresses a larger addressable market than traditional prime jumbo.

**Constraints on Growth:**
- Mortgage rates above 7% suppress purchase and refi volumes broadly
- The current environment of elevated rates provides no rate relief catalyst in the near term
- Analyst estimates reflect this caution: $0.97 current-year EPS, $1.02 next year — modest sequential growth

**Estimate Revision Context:**
Current-year EPS estimates have risen 10.8% over 90 days (from $0.88 to $0.97). This is positive but below the 30% threshold that would constitute a strong BUY signal. The upward drift reflects the platform's operational improvement being gradually recognized.

## Competitive Landscape

Redwood occupies a distinctive position in the mortgage ecosystem:

**Improving competitive trajectory:**
- Market share expansion from 1-2% to 7% in jumbo (category leadership)
- Bank retrenchment from non-agency lending is structural (capital requirements, regulatory burden), creating a multi-year tailwind
- Proprietary securitization platforms (SEMT®, CAFL®) with established investor bases are difficult to replicate
- Operating cost advantage improving (below 20bps per loan in Q1 2026)
- Third-party capital validation via Castlelake JV ($8B commitment)

**Competitive threats:**
- Well-capitalized private credit firms (Rithm Capital, PennyMac) compete in BPL/non-agency space
- Rising conforming loan limits ($1.249M in high-cost areas) compress the addressable jumbo market
- If GSE privatization proceeds, Fannie/Freddie could become more aggressive competitors in near-prime segments
- Aspire competes with established non-QM platforms (Angel Oak, Homepoint) that have longer track records

**Key differentiator:** The combination of origination capability + proprietary securitization infrastructure + retained interest investing makes Redwood a vertically integrated platform. Most competitors do only one of these functions.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Convertible Note Maturity ($297M due 2027):** This is the most concrete near-term risk. If the stock remains below conversion price, $297M must be refinanced or repaid in cash within ~18 months. With $202M unrestricted cash and ~$2B additional debt capacity, this is manageable but will require proactive capital market activity in an uncertain rate environment.

**2. Legacy Portfolio Resolution:** Remaining legacy assets (~$700M across bridge loans, REO, HEI, and subordinate interests) contain Level 3 assets whose fair values are model-dependent. Further write-downs of $50-100M are plausible if residential investor loan workouts deteriorate or the housing market weakens.

**3. HEI Regulatory/Litigation Risk:** Class action lawsuits against HEI originators, CFPB characterization of HEI as mortgage credit, and Washington State regulatory inquiry threaten the $148M HEI portfolio and potentially broader non-QM market sentiment. Complete impairment would reduce BV by ~$1.18/share.

**4. Securitization Market Access:** The business model depends on functioning private-label MBS markets. The 2020 COVID episode demonstrated that these markets can freeze entirely, forcing Redwood to hold loans on balance sheet and consume liquidity. In the current environment of credit stress (private credit cascade concerns, BDC NAV pressures), a disruption to non-agency securitization markets cannot be excluded.

**5. Rate Environment:** Elevated long-term rates mean mortgage rates remain high. This constrains origination volume and could trigger mark-to-market losses on retained securities if rates spike further.

**6. Executive Order on Institutional SFR:** Trump administration policy targeting large institutional investors in single-family homes could impact CoreVest borrower demand, though 86% of borrowers own <100 units (potentially below enforcement thresholds).

## Conclusion

Redwood Trust presents a genuine transformation story with measurable operational improvement, but the investment case requires patience and tolerance for near-term uncertainty. The 22% discount to book value and 5.7x forward P/E provide valuation support, and the mortgage banking platform's scaling (record volumes, declining costs, Castlelake validation) is quantifiable progress. However, the combination of legacy resolution risk, convertible maturity overhang, HEI regulatory exposure, and an unfavorable rate environment prevents a higher-conviction call. The options market's backwardation and elevated put skew confirm that near-term risk is not fully resolved.

The appropriate stance is to hold for the identified catalysts: Legacy approaching breakeven, convertible refinancing clarity, and sustained EAD coverage of the dividend. The 12.9% yield provides meaningful carry while waiting. If fundamentals continue their current trajectory over the next two quarters, this will likely merit an upgrade. If Legacy losses re-accelerate or the convertible becomes a liquidity event, it will merit a downgrade.
