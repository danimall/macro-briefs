## Executive Summary

Equitable Holdings trades at $38.22, roughly 32% below the 52-week high of $56.61 and 34% below the median analyst price target of $58. The stock has declined sharply since late February 2026, coinciding with broader risk-off sentiment and the announcement of an all-stock merger with Corebridge Financial (CRBG). At ~5.3x forward non-GAAP operating earnings ($7.25 consensus for FY2026), EQH is priced as if the business is structurally impaired. The company generated a 25.6% non-GAAP operating ROE in 2025, reduced its share count by 21% over three years, and operates a vertically integrated retirement-insurance-asset management model with $175B in Retirement account value and $867B in AllianceBernstein AUM.

The bear case is real: four consecutive quarterly earnings misses, negative earnings estimate revisions across all periods (-7.6% over 90 days for current year), GAAP equity that has turned negative due to unrealized investment losses, and an equity-market-sensitive revenue model facing a deteriorating macro backdrop. The Corebridge merger introduces integration risk, regulatory approval uncertainty, and dilution (EQH shareholders will own ~49% of the combined entity). The options market is pricing imminent stress with near-term IV at 59.6% versus 31% historical volatility and a backwardated term structure.

Despite these risks, the magnitude of the discount — 5.3x operating earnings for a 25%+ ROE business with a clear growth engine in RILAs, aggressive capital return, and a pending merger that is >10% EPS accretive by 2028 — justifies a BUY recommendation. 12 of 13 covering analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy with a $49-$65 target range, though the persistent earnings misses warrant caution on position sizing.

## Company Overview

Equitable Holdings traces its origins to 1859 and operates an integrated "retirement value chain" through three segments: Retirement (the largest, $1.55B in 2025 operating earnings), Asset Management via its ~68% ownership of AllianceBernstein ($571M, +19% YoY), and Wealth Management through Equitable Advisors ($220M, +21% YoY). A Corporate & Other segment ($599M loss) houses legacy variable annuity blocks, individual life, and the Closed Block.

The company's strategic model links product manufacturing (annuities, insurance), asset management (AB), and distribution (4,600 advisors) in a vertically integrated structure. Equitable represented 16% of AB's total AUM at year-end 2025.

On March 26, 2026, Equitable announced a definitive all-stock merger with Corebridge Financial (CRBG), the former AIG life and retirement subsidiary, valued at approximately $22B. The combined entity would manage ~$1.5T in AUMA, serve 12M+ customers, and generate >$5B in operating earnings with >$500M in annual run-rate synergies by 2028. The deal is expected to close by year-end 2026, subject to regulatory and shareholder approvals.

## Financial Analysis

### Operating Earnings vs. GAAP

The critical distinction for EQH is between GAAP net income and non-GAAP operating earnings. GAAP net income swung from +$1.28B in 2024 to -$1.38B in 2025, a $2.7B delta driven almost entirely by non-cash items: $2.38B in variable annuity MRB fair value adjustments (interest rate movements), $1.34B in investment losses (primarily the RGA reinsurance transaction), and a $499M one-time Venerable novation loss. These items have no bearing on the company's ability to generate recurring cash flows.

Non-GAAP operating earnings declined 13% to $1.74B ($5.64/share), reflecting higher interest credited to policyholders (+$501M from account value growth), reduced fee revenue (-$320M, largely from the RGA reinsurance), and higher commission/DAC costs from strong sales. Offsetting factors included +$433M in net investment income (higher portfolio yield of 4.35% vs. 3.80% in 2023) and -$243M in lower policyholder benefits.

Non-GAAP operating ROE was 25.6%. Very few financial services companies sustain ROE above 20% through a full cycle.

### Balance Sheet

Total assets stood at $318B at year-end 2025, dominated by separate account assets and insurance reserves. GAAP stockholders' equity turned negative at -$74M, driven by AOCI adjustments (unrealized losses on available-for-sale securities from the higher rate environment). Equity excluding AOCI was approximately $6.6B, the economically relevant figure.

Long-term debt was $3.84B (stable). Insurance subsidiary RBC ratios exceeded 400%, and the holding company maintained $1.24B in highly liquid assets (down from $1.98B after aggressive capital return). AM Best affirmed its A (Excellent) financial strength rating with stable outlook in March 2026 (prior to the merger announcement), and subsequently placed ratings under review with developing implications — standard procedure following a major merger.

The investment portfolio ($120B general account) is 98.2% investment grade in fixed maturities, with $23B in mortgage loans (66% average LTV, 20% office exposure) and $18.3B in private credit (95% investment grade, up 29% YoY). The office mortgage exposure ($4.7B) is a watchpoint given ongoing CRE stress, though LTV discipline provides a buffer.

### Capital Return

EQH has been among the most aggressive capital returners in the insurance sector. In 2025: $1.45B in share repurchases (7% of shares), $314M in common dividends, $758M to increase AB ownership, and $444M in preferred stock redemption — totaling ~$2.97B deployed. The share count declined from 378M (2022) to 298M (2025). Approximately $1.0B remains on the current buyback authorization, though the Corebridge merger may alter near-term buyback cadence.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow was $714M in 2025, likely depressed by the RGA transaction timing. The holding company is dependent on subsidiary dividends ($2.64B received in 2025, including an extraordinary $1.5B dividend from Equitable America). Ordinary dividend capacity for 2026 from Equitable America is estimated at $408M.

## Growth Analysis

### RILA Growth Engine

The Structured Capital Strategies (SCS) RILA product is EQH's clearest organic growth driver. First-year premiums grew from $11.3B (2023) to $14.3B (2024) to $15.4B (2025), a 36% increase over two years. Total Retirement account value reached $175B at year-end 2025, up 16% YoY. The spread lending business ($17.5B, +36% YoY) represents an additional growth vector within Retirement.

### Wealth Management Momentum

Wealth Management net new assets of $8.4B in 2025 nearly doubled the $4.8B in 2024. AUA reached $122B. Revenue per advisor grew to $440K TTM. Operating earnings increased 21% YoY to $220M.

### AB: Organic Headwinds, Scale Tailwinds

AllianceBernstein's AUM reached $867B, up from $793B, driven by market appreciation. However, net long-term outflows worsened to $11.3B in 2025 from $2.2B in 2024, with actively managed equities losing $22.5B. The Corebridge merger directly addresses this: ~$100B in Corebridge assets are expected to migrate to AB management over time, substantially offsetting organic outflow pressure.

### Estimate Revisions: Negative

Consensus current-year EPS has declined from $7.85 to $7.25 over the past 90 days (-7.6%), and next-year EPS from $9.31 to $8.88 (-4.6%). The 30-day revision pace is negative across all periods. This negative momentum suggests analysts are still reducing estimates in response to the macro environment and possibly merger-related uncertainty.

### Earnings Execution

EQH has missed consensus EPS estimates for four consecutive quarters, with misses ranging from -2.4% to -15.3%. The most recent miss (Q4 2025: $1.73 vs. $1.77, -2.4%) was the narrowest, suggesting potential stabilization, but the track record substantially reduces confidence in forward estimates. Consensus estimates for FY2026 ($7.25) and FY2027 ($8.88) should be viewed skeptically — there is a reasonable probability they continue to decline.

## Valuation Assessment

At $38.22, EQH trades at approximately:
- 5.3x FY2026E non-GAAP operating EPS ($7.25)
- 4.3x FY2027E non-GAAP operating EPS ($8.88)
- ~2.9% dividend yield
- Negative trailing P/E on GAAP basis (misleading for an insurer)

The peer median P/E is 26.2x (though the peer set includes non-comparable businesses like Jack Henry and Erie Indemnity). Against more relevant insurance/financial conglomerate peers, EQH's ~5.3x operating earnings multiple is deeply discounted. The sell-side consensus price target of $58 (median) implies 52% upside. Even the lowest analyst target of $49 implies 28% upside from the current price.

For a business generating 25.6% operating ROE with a clear capital return framework and a pending accretive merger, the valuation embeds substantial pessimism. The negative GAAP trailing P/E creates screen-based exclusion (many quantitative investors filter on GAAP earnings), which may contribute to the discount.

### Where the Discount is Justified

The discount partially reflects: (1) four consecutive earnings misses, (2) equity market sensitivity in a deteriorating environment, (3) merger execution risk, (4) potential credit losses in the investment portfolio (office CRE, private credit), and (5) legacy VA guarantee tail risk. A fair operating P/E for this risk profile is likely 7-8x, implying $51-$58 on FY2026E consensus, roughly aligned with the analyst target range.

## Competitive Landscape

EQH's integrated retirement value chain (manufacturer, manager, distributor) provides a structural advantage versus standalone annuity writers or pure-play asset managers. The SCS RILA product's growth trajectory demonstrates competitive strength in a high-demand product category. The Corebridge combination would create a top-tier US-based retirement and insurance platform with meaningful scale advantages in product development, distribution, and asset management.

AllianceBernstein's challenges mirror the broader active management industry, though AB's fixed income and alternatives franchises provide partial diversification against the passive equity shift.

The productivity initiative ($150M run-rate savings by 2027, $120M achieved) demonstrates operational discipline, and the willingness to de-risk legacy blocks (RGA transaction, Venerable novation, Bermuda captive) shows proactive management of tail exposures.

## Risk Assessment

### Equity Market Sensitivity (HIGH)

Fee income across all three segments is driven by account values and AUM, which are equity-market-linked. A 10% equity market decline adds $744M to MRB liabilities. In a recession scenario with 20%+ equity drawdowns, operating earnings across Retirement, Asset Management, and Wealth Management would compress simultaneously. Given the current 45-50% recession probability environment, this is a first-order risk.

### Merger Execution Risk (HIGH)

The deal requires regulatory approvals from multiple state insurance departments, federal antitrust clearance, and shareholder votes at both companies. Regulatory pushback, market deterioration affecting the exchange ratio economics, or integration difficulties could delay or alter terms.

### Credit and Investment Portfolio Risk (MEDIUM-HIGH)

The $4.7B in office mortgage exposure is vulnerable to ongoing CRE stress. The $18.3B private credit portfolio, while 95% investment grade, has grown rapidly. In a credit cascade scenario, mark-to-market losses could pressure statutory capital and constrain subsidiary dividends.

### Legacy VA Guarantees (MEDIUM)

Despite substantial de-risking, MRB liabilities remain rate- and equity-sensitive. A 50bps rate decrease adds $605M to liabilities. Hedging programs don't qualify for GAAP hedge accounting, perpetuating income volatility.

### S&P Negative Outlook (MEDIUM)

S&P's negative outlook on the A+ insurance subsidiary rating could widen to a downgrade, increasing funding costs and reducing product competitiveness.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 59.6% against 31% historical volatility — a 28.5pp spread — indicates the market expects roughly twice the normal price movement. The term structure is backwardated (59.6% near-term vs. 41.9% at 6 months), consistent with imminent event pricing. Put/call volume ratio is 1.60 with elevated 11.2% put skew. This diverges from the fundamental BUY thesis and warrants caution on position sizing. The elevated IV likely reflects both broader market stress and merger-related binary risk.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($55-58)

The Corebridge merger closes on schedule by year-end 2026, with $500M+ synergies validated. AB receives the ~$100B AUM transfer, reversing organic outflows. Operating EPS reaches $7.50+ in FY2026 (above current consensus) as RILA growth and spread lending momentum accelerate. The market re-rates the combined entity at 7-8x forward operating earnings, consistent with the franchise quality and scale. Target: $55-58 (44-52% upside).

### Bear Case ($28-32)

Equity markets decline 15-20% in a recession, compressing fee revenue across all segments. The merger faces regulatory delays or deteriorating terms. Office CRE losses materialize at 10-15% of the $4.7B exposure, requiring reserve increases. AB outflows accelerate. Operating EPS declines to $5.50-6.00, and the market applies a 5x distressed multiple. Target: $28-32 (17-27% downside).

**Base case target:** $52 (7.2x FY2026E operating EPS of $7.25). 36% upside from $38.22 over 12-18 months, driven by merger close catalyst and operating earnings stabilization.

**Bull case target:** $58 (7.5x FY2027E operating EPS of $8.88, discounted to present). 52% upside. Requires merger close, synergy validation, and equity market stabilization.

**Bear case target:** $30 (5.0x stressed operating EPS of $6.00). 21% downside. Recession, merger delay, and credit losses compound.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, anchored by expected merger close (year-end 2026), Q1 2026 earnings (next datapoint for operational trends), and regulatory approval milestones through H2 2026.

**Upside/downside from current:** +36% to base case, +52% to bull case, -21% to bear case. Risk/reward ratio of approximately 1.7:1 on base case, 2.5:1 on bull case.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. FY2026 Q1 operating EPS misses by >10% and management reduces full-year guidance below $6.50 — would signal structural earnings deterioration beyond macro cyclicality.
2. Corebridge merger terminated or materially restructured (e.g., exchange ratio changed unfavorably by >10%, significant asset divestitures required).
3. S&P downgrades insurance subsidiary ratings below A — would impair competitive positioning in annuity distribution.

## Conclusion

Equitable Holdings at $38.22 offers a compelling risk/reward skew for investors willing to underwrite merger execution risk and short-term macro headwinds. The franchise quality and capital return track record are inconsistent with a 5.3x operating earnings multiple. The Corebridge merger, if consummated, creates a $1.5T AUMA platform with >$500M in synergies and addresses AB's organic outflow problem.

The risks are quantifiable and manageable: equity market sensitivity, consecutive earnings misses, negative estimate revisions, and elevated options-implied volatility. Position sizing should reflect these risks — this is a standard-weight BUY rather than a maximum conviction position. Enter at current levels with a 12-18 month horizon targeting $52, and re-evaluate at Q1 2026 earnings and key merger approval milestones.
