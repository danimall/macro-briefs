## Executive Summary

Fluor Corporation is a $7.6B global EPC (engineering, procurement, construction) company monetizing a $2.4B windfall from NuScale Power shares while simultaneously absorbing a $665M litigation charge from the Santos judgment. The core business is performing adequately — Urban Solutions revenue grew 25% YoY through September 2025, backlog stands at $25.5B (81% reimbursable), and consensus 2026 EPS estimates have risen 18% over the past 90 days to $2.76, implying a normalized forward P/E of ~19x.

The growth thesis rests on three pillars: (1) secular demand in pharma infrastructure, mining electrification, and nuclear remediation driving book-to-burn above 1.0x in 2026; (2) aggressive share repurchases ($800M+ targeted from NuScale proceeds) compressing shares outstanding by ~14% in twelve months; and (3) reimbursable contract mix shift to 82% reducing the catastrophic lump-sum risk that historically plagued Fluor's earnings. These factors support holding through what should be an improving earnings trajectory in FY2026-2027.

The barriers to a BUY rating are material: the Santos appeal outcome is binary ($665M exposure with insurance disputes), a new CEO has only ~10 months tenure while the turnaround architect (Constable) retires in May 2026, a securities class action creates ongoing litigation overhang, and the options market is pricing imminent stress with near-term IV at 75.2% in steep backwardation. These risks are concentrated in the next 6-12 months, making timing premature for aggressive positioning.

## Company Overview

Fluor operates through three segments: **Urban Solutions** ($6.6B revenue for 9 months ending September 2025) covers life sciences, mining, infrastructure, and advanced manufacturing; **Energy Solutions** ($2.6B) handles oil & gas, chemicals, LNG, and power; **Mission Solutions** ($2.1B) provides U.S. government services including DOE nuclear remediation and DOD programs. The company completed its NuScale Power monetization in April 2026 (total proceeds ~$2.4B) and divested its Stork maintenance business for $61M.

A key strategic shift since 2020 has been moving toward reimbursable contracts (now 82% of backlog), which transfer cost-overrun risk to clients in exchange for lower margins. This structurally reduces earnings volatility — the lump-sum projects that caused Fluor's 2019-2020 crisis now represent only 18% of backlog.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY2025 revenue of $15.5B declined from $16.3B in FY2024, but this is distorted by the $653M Santos revenue reversal. Underlying organic growth continued in Urban Solutions (+25% YoY) and Mission Solutions (+9%).

**Profitability:** FY2025 reported an operating loss of -$378M, driven entirely by the Santos charge. Core EBITDA was approximately $499M per proxy performance metrics. Normalized operating margins of 2.5-4.5% are thin but industry-standard for large EPC firms. The trailing EPS of -$0.31 reflects this charge, not operational deterioration.

**Cash Flow & Balance Sheet:** Operating cash flow was -$387M in FY2025, impacted by the Santos payment and infrastructure project working capital. Cash stands at $2.1B (down from $2.8B). Long-term debt of $1.07B includes $575M convertible notes (conversion price $45.37, currently in-the-money). Net cash position remains positive. Current ratio of ~1.9x is healthy.

**Capital Return:** Fluor is executing an aggressive buyback — $365M in 9 months through September 2025, with an $800M target from November 2025-February 2026. Share count has declined from 171M to ~147M (14% reduction). No dividend is paid.

**Backlog:** $25.5B as of year-end FY2025, with ~50% expected to convert to 2026 revenue. Book-to-burn projected above 1.0x in 2026. Remaining unsatisfied performance obligations of $26.9B.

**Earnings Trajectory:** Consensus estimates reflect meaningful improvement — FY2026E EPS of $2.76 and FY2027E of $3.25, representing 18% annual growth. At the current $52.49, the stock trades at ~19x FY2026E and ~16x FY2027E.

## Growth Analysis

The 90-day upward revision of +18% to current-year estimates is the most compelling quantitative signal. This reflects:

1. **Pharma infrastructure demand:** Multi-billion dollar awards in Indiana (Eli Lilly complex) position Fluor in a secular growth market where capital spending is multi-year and expanding.

2. **Nuclear remediation stability:** The six-year Portsmouth Gaseous Diffusion Plant extension in Ohio provides decades-long recurring revenue with low risk.

3. **Mining electrification:** Copper demand from energy transition drives multi-billion project awards in Fluor's metals & mining practice.

4. **Data center construction:** An emerging pipeline opportunity as hyperscalers build facilities requiring Fluor's large-scale EPC capabilities.

5. **Share count reduction:** The 14% reduction from buybacks provides mechanical EPS growth even on flat revenue.

The earnings surprise record is mixed (2 beats, 2 misses in 4 quarters), with significant variance — a 49.7% beat in Q3 followed by a -4.8% miss in Q4. This volatility reflects the distorting effects of the Santos charge and legacy project adjustments rather than structural unpredictability in core operations.

## Valuation Assessment

At $52.49, Fluor trades at ~19x FY2026E EPS of $2.76 and ~16x FY2027E of $3.25. The analyst consensus mean target is $53.50 (1.9% upside), with a range of $40-$61.

The forward P/E of ~19x is reasonable for the EPC sector given: (a) above-market growth trajectory (+18% earnings growth), (b) net cash balance sheet, and (c) secular tailwinds. However, the stock is already trading near the midpoint of the analyst range, and the mean target implies essentially no upside. The stock has rallied 52% from its May 2025 lows, pricing in much of the NuScale monetization benefit and backlog growth.

A sum-of-parts approach values: (1) core EPC at 15-18x normalized EBITDA of ~$500M = $7.5-9.0B enterprise value, less (2) Santos/litigation contingencies of $700M-$1B, plus (3) remaining NuScale cash proceeds = approximately $7.0-8.5B equity value, or $48-58 per share on ~147M diluted shares. Current price sits at the midpoint of this range.

The market is not obviously mispricing Fluor in either direction. The valuation reflects the growth trajectory and the litigation overhang in roughly equal measure.

## Competitive Landscape

Fluor competes with KBR, Jacobs, AECOM, Bechtel, and Technip Energies. Its position is stable with improving trajectory in select verticals:

**Improving:** Urban Solutions (pharma, mining) is gaining share in a rapidly expanding market. Fluor's ability to execute multi-billion dollar programs differentiates it from smaller competitors. The 82% reimbursable mix shift makes Fluor's risk profile more comparable to professional services firms than traditional EPC.

**Stable:** Mission Solutions provides reliable government work with contract extensions. Nuclear remediation expertise is specialized and barriers to entry are high.

**Challenged:** Energy Solutions faces timing uncertainty from trade policy and client capital discipline. Chemical sector clients have explicitly postponed commitments. Legacy projects still carry $394M in backlog with $117M in unfunded losses — this tail continues to drag.

**TSR underperformance:** FY2025 TSR of 248 vs. peer group TSR of 388 is a notable red flag. Peers have delivered substantially better returns despite Fluor's NuScale windfall, suggesting the market views Fluor's execution risk as above-average.

## Risk Assessment

**Santos Litigation ($665M):** The highest-impact binary risk. An Australian court overrode the contractual liability cap — an unusual and concerning precedent. Payment was expected in Q4 2025 while appeal is pending. Five of 30+ insurers are disputing coverage. If insurance recovery fails partially or fully, net exposure could exceed $665M after tax implications.

**Securities Class Action (September 2025):** Shareholders allege misleading statements about infrastructure project costs between February-July 2025. Discovery is in early stages. Settlement risk is 2-4 years out but creates ongoing uncertainty.

**NuScale-Related Litigation:** A separate class action (filed March 2026) names Fluor regarding NuScale's stock decline. As Fluor held a majority stake and sat on NuScale's board, liability theories are plausible.

**Leadership Transition:** New CEO Breuer (~10 months tenure), retiring Chairman Constable (May 2026), and 10+ officer changes in 24 months create execution risk at a critical moment.

**Convertible Debt Dilution:** $575M converts at $45.37/share — currently in-the-money. Capped calls mitigate dilution, but fully diluted share count is higher than the ~147M basic count.

**Credit Rating Trigger:** Current ratings are one notch above the level that would require collateralizing the $2.2B revolving credit facility with U.S. assets — a meaningful liquidity constraint if triggered.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 75.2% is 44% above the 1-year historical volatility of 52.3%, and the term structure is in steep backwardation (75.2% near-term vs. 46.6% at 6 months). Put skew at 3.1% indicates elevated hedging demand. The market is pricing a ±10.5% move on the nearest expiry — roughly $5.50 in either direction. This backwardation structure typically signals that participants expect a specific near-term catalyst (likely related to Santos payment/appeal news or the Q1 2026 earnings expected around May 2026).

This options structure is inconsistent with a BUY recommendation. The implied move exceeds typical quarterly earnings volatility for an EPC company.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($58-61):**
- Santos appeal succeeds or insurance fully covers the loss, removing $665M overhang
- FY2026 earnings deliver at or above $2.76 consensus as Urban Solutions mega-projects ramp
- Buyback continues at accelerated pace, pushing shares below 140M
- Book-to-burn >1.0x validates growing backlog
- New CEO establishes credibility; David Einhorn thesis plays out
- Forward P/E re-rates to 20-22x on clean earnings = $55-72 range

**Bear Case ($34-40):**
- Santos appeal fails; insurance disputes materially reduce recovery; total cash impact exceeds $800M
- Infrastructure project overruns expand beyond the three already identified ($54M in 2025)
- Securities class action advances with adverse discovery
- Leadership transition creates strategic drift; key client relationships suffer
- Energy Solutions backlog continues declining as chemical clients defer
- Stock re-rates to 12-14x FY2026 on lingering litigation = $33-39

3. Securities class action settlement discussions emerge at meaningful amounts (>$200M)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings release (likely May 2026). The Santos appeal timeline and insurance dispute proceedings will also drive reassessment over the next 3-6 months.

## Conclusion

Fluor's improving operational trajectory — 18% EPS estimate revisions, $25.5B reimbursable-weighted backlog, aggressive buybacks — provides a credible growth thesis over 12-18 months. Normalized earnings power of $2.76-$3.25 per share supports the current valuation if litigation risks resolve favorably. However, the concentration of binary risks (Santos appeal, securities class action, leadership transition) combined with options market stress signals and middling execution history (2/4 quarterly beats, peer TSR underperformance) prevents confidence in recommending new capital. The stock has already rallied 52% from its lows, analyst consensus target is essentially flat at $53.50, and insider activity shows only selling. The appropriate posture is to hold for the growth thesis to mature while monitoring the litigation and earnings catalysts that will determine direction.
