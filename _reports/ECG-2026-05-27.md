**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Everus Construction Group is a specialty electrical and mechanical contractor that has emerged as one of the primary physical-layer beneficiaries of the AI data center buildout. Since its October 2024 spinoff from MDU Resources, the company has delivered 4 consecutive earnings beats, grown revenue 31.5% YoY in FY2025 (with Q1 2026 at +25% YoY), and expanded EBITDA margins from 8.1% to 8.6%. Record backlog of $3.68B provides significant near-term revenue visibility, while analyst estimates have risen materially over the past 90 days with no signs of deceleration.

The stock has appreciated 155% from its post-spinoff low of $56.10 to $148.54, and trades at approximately 32-36x forward earnings — a substantial premium to the construction peer median of 26x. This premium is supported by strong organic revenue growth (mid-teens on a full-year 2026 basis, including SE&M), 44% EBITDA growth, structural positioning in a labor-constrained buildout cycle, and continued estimate upside. The specific, falsifiable risk is customer concentration: one client now represents ~17% of revenue (up from <10% in 2024), creating binary exposure to any single hyperscaler pausing or cancelling data center programs.

## Company Overview

Everus operates through two segments: **Electrical & Mechanical (E&M)** at ~78% of revenue, and **Transmission & Distribution (T&D)** at ~22%. The E&M segment is the growth engine, driven by data center construction for hyperscale clients. T&D provides a stable, higher-margin base with approximately 55-60% MSA-recurring revenue and utility-focused infrastructure work.

The company was spun off from MDU Resources on October 31, 2024, is headquartered in Bismarck, North Dakota, and employs approximately 9,400 workers — 85% unionized, which provides critical access to skilled labor in a constrained market. Everus operates across most U.S. states through 15 wholly owned operating companies under 19 local brands.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Growth:** FY2025 revenue was $3.75B (+31.5% YoY), with E&M growing 43.8% driven by $876.8M in incremental commercial revenues (predominantly data centers). Q1 2026 sustained this trajectory at $1.04B (+25% YoY). Management's 2026 guidance of $4.3-4.4B implies approximately 15-17% total revenue growth including contributions from the SE&M acquisition.

**Profitability:** Operating margin expanded to 7.1% from 6.7%; EBITDA margin reached 8.5% (8.6% in Q1 2026). Net income of $201.8M (+40.7% YoY) translated to ROE of approximately 32% (on ending equity) — exceptional for a construction services business. T&D margins expanded further (EBITDA margin 13.3% in Q1, +240bps YoY), reflecting operating leverage on utility infrastructure work.

**Earnings Quality Note:** In FY2025, changes in estimates on prior-period performance obligations added $113.7M to revenue and $83.7M to net income ($1.64/share) — approximately 41% of net income growth came from revised estimates on previously-recognized work. In Q1 2026, cumulative catch-up adjustments positively impacted operating revenues/gross profit by $55.9M, net income by $41.5M, and diluted EPS by $0.81. Of the reported Q1 2026 net income of $58.3M ($1.14 diluted EPS), a substantial majority reflected these prior-period estimate revisions rather than solely current-period project execution. While this is normal for percentage-of-completion accounting, investors should understand that reported earnings include meaningful "look-back" adjustments that can fluctuate significantly quarter to quarter.

**Balance Sheet:** The company operates with minimal leverage — approximately 0.5x net debt/EBITDA following the SE&M acquisition. Current ratio of 1.76 and Altman Z-Score of 3.92 indicate no financial stress. Working capital intensity is increasing ($770M in receivables, up 30% YoY), which is normal for a rapidly growing construction firm but warrants monitoring.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $156.8M in FY2025, with Q1 2026 delivering $143.7M (versus $7.1M prior year) due to timing benefits. CapEx guidance of $90-100M for 2026 leaves ample free cash flow for debt service, M&A, and potential shareholder returns.

**Backlog:** $3.68B as of Q1 2026, up 20% YoY. The company estimates $2.93B of backlog (approximately 80%) will be recognized within 12 months. Separately, remaining performance obligations totaled $3.09B, of which $2.60B is expected within 12 months. This backlog provides meaningful but not complete revenue coverage relative to the $4.3-4.4B 2026 guidance, with new awards expected to supplement the existing book throughout the year.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three pillars:

1. **AI Data Center Demand (Primary Driver):** Everus is one of a small number of specialty contractors with the scale, expertise, and union labor relationships to execute large data center electrical/mechanical packages. The AI infrastructure buildout — confirmed by $150B+ in announced hyperscaler capex — creates a multi-year demand runway. Management noted in Q1 2026 that they received their first large award in a new geography supporting a new high-tech client, suggesting their addressable customer base is expanding.

2. **Grid Infrastructure (T&D Segment):** Data centers require massive power delivery infrastructure. T&D benefits from both traditional utility hardening/undergrounding programs and incremental grid capacity needed to serve data center loads. This segment grew only 1.4% in FY2025 but expanded margins significantly and is positioned for acceleration.

3. **M&A (Supplementary):** The SE&M acquisition (closed April 2, 2026) adds Southeast geographic presence and pharmaceutical/healthcare end-market diversification at high-teens EBITDA margins. Management targets $100-300M revenue acquisitions in adjacent markets. The tax matters agreement restricting transactions expires October 2026, opening additional strategic flexibility.

While Q1 2026 delivered 25% YoY revenue growth, full-year 2026 guidance implies approximately 15-17% total revenue growth (including SE&M's contribution). The forward growth rate, while still well above construction-sector norms, should not be extrapolated from Q1's pace alone.

Estimate revisions confirm the market is still catching up to near-term performance. However, the precise magnitude of consensus EPS estimates varies across data providers. Available sources suggest FY2026 consensus EPS in the range of $4.10-$4.62, implying a forward P/E of approximately 32-36x at the current stock price. The company's track record of beating estimates — Zacks confirms +50% surprises in recent quarters — suggests forward estimates may still have upside, though the large Q1 catch-up benefit complicates the normalized run-rate assessment.

## Valuation Assessment

At $148.54 per share:
- Trailing P/E: 37.6x (on $3.95 EPS, which includes $1.64/share in catch-up revenue recognition)
- Forward P/E: approximately 32-36x (depending on consensus source; estimates range from $4.10 to $4.62)
- EV/EBITDA: 21.8x (on 2026E midpoint of $352.5M)

**Peer comparison:** The construction peer median P/E is 26x, with MYRG at 49.2x and Tetra Tech at 16.5x. Everus trades at a premium to most peers but below MYRG, which shares data center/electrical infrastructure exposure. Primoris (26x) and Fluor (20.4x) are larger but with lower growth rates and less direct AI infrastructure exposure.

**Why the market may still be underpricing this stock:** If the company continues to beat estimates through FY2026 (plausible given backlog visibility and Q1 performance), current-year EPS could reach $5.00+, placing the forward P/E at 27-30x — in line with peers on materially faster growth.

**Constraint on further upside:** At 32-36x forward, the valuation already reflects meaningful optimism. A reversion to peer-median multiples (26x) on current estimates would imply ~$107-120/share (downside risk). The stock needs continued beat-and-raise cycles to sustain the premium, and the large Q1 catch-up benefit raises questions about the sustainability of reported earnings at annualized rates.

## Competitive Landscape

Everus's competitive position is strengthening along several dimensions:

- **Labor access:** 85% unionized workforce in a market where skilled electrical/mechanical workers are the binding constraint on project delivery. Non-union competitors cannot scale as quickly for large data center programs.
- **Design-phase engagement:** Management emphasizes being engaged early in projects ("brought in during design"), creating embedded switching costs and allowing better cost estimation for fixed-price bids.
- **National scale with local brands:** 15 operating companies provide geographic diversification while maintaining customer relationships and local knowledge.
- **Contract mix management:** Deliberate shift from 59% fixed-price to 52% (and growing cost-plus) reduces blow-up risk on large projects while preserving margin discipline.

The primary competitive risk is from larger national players (Quanta, EMCOR, MasTec) who have greater geographic reach and diversification, and from private firms like Rosendin Electric that compete directly in data center electrical work. However, the demand environment is sufficiently large that market share competition is less of a concern than execution capability.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer Concentration (PRIMARY RISK):** One customer has grown from <10% to ~17% of revenue in a single year. Two E&M customers represent 21% and 10% of that segment. Based on the company's disclosure that it generates significant revenue from data center contracts and that this concentration appeared in the E&M segment's commercial submarket, this likely reflects hyperscale data center client(s), though Everus does not identify the specific customer. Any pause, cancellation, or internalization of electrical/mechanical work by this customer could remove $600M+ in annual revenue. This risk is acknowledged but not fully priced — the market appears to view hyperscaler capex as durable, which it may be, but the company-specific concentration is the vulnerability.

**Data Center Cycle Duration:** Management explicitly acknowledges uncertainty about "the longevity of this type of work and if it will be sustained in the future." While AI infrastructure demand has multiple confirming signals (hyperscaler capex guidance, government support, backlog data), Everus is levered to the timing and pace of physical construction — any pause for permitting, power availability, or capex rationalization could materially impact revenues given the company's concentrated exposure.

**Cumulative Revenue Recognition:** As detailed in the Financial Analysis section, the large Q1 2026 catch-up benefit ($0.81 of $1.14 EPS) means normalized earnings power may be materially lower than reported figures suggest in any given quarter. Excluding catch-up adjustments, Q1 net income would have been approximately $16.8M and EPS approximately $0.33.

**Tariff Exposure:** 52% fixed-price contracts carry material cost pass-through risk if copper, aluminum, and electrical component prices increase from tariffs. The shift toward cost-plus mitigates this for new contracts but legacy fixed-price work remains exposed.

**Spinoff Execution:** First full year as standalone public company. IT migration, standalone insurance costs, and loss of MDU shared services create near-term friction and cost headwinds.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($180-200, +20-35%):** Data center demand persists through 2027+, Everus continues beating estimates, FY2026 EPS reaches $5.00+, FY2027 consensus rises to $5.50-6.00+, and the stock re-rates to 30-33x forward as institutional ownership increases post-spinoff. SE&M integration adds margin accretive revenue. New geographic wins with additional hyperscalers diversify customer base.

**Bear Case ($100-115, -22-33%):** Primary hyperscale customer pauses or cancels construction programs (permitting delays, power availability, or AI capex rationalization). Revenue growth decelerates to single digits, and the stock reverts to construction peer median P/E (26x on $4.10 = $107, or lower if estimates are cut). Tariff-driven cost overruns on legacy fixed-price contracts compress margins. Cumulative revenue recognition adjustments reverse in subsequent quarters, revealing lower normalized earnings.

## Conclusion

Everus Construction Group represents a legitimate AI infrastructure beneficiary with proven execution: 4 consecutive earnings beats, strong organic revenue growth, record backlog, expanding margins, and a clean balance sheet. The stock is expensive at approximately 32-36x forward earnings relative to construction peers (median 26x), but the combination of mid-teens-plus revenue growth, EBITDA margin expansion, and structural positioning in a labor-constrained data center buildout supports a premium multiple.

The primary risk is specific and measurable: one customer at 17% of revenue creates binary exposure to hyperscaler capex decisions. Additionally, earnings quality warrants scrutiny — the large Q1 2026 cumulative catch-up benefit ($0.81 of $1.14 EPS) means normalized earnings power is difficult to assess with precision. This prevents maximum conviction sizing but does not negate the fundamental growth story. The appropriate position size is 1.5-2.0% of portfolio, with a 12-month target of $172 and a stop-loss equivalent to the bear case of $110 (26% downside).

**Recommendation: BUY** at 1.5-2.0% sizing, acknowledging that the premium valuation requires continued execution and that customer concentration represents a specific, idiosyncratic risk that must be monitored quarterly.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Q1 2026 catch-up adjustments ($0.81 of $1.14 EPS from prior-period estimate revisions) and potentially lower consensus EPS ($4.10 vs. $4.62 depending on source) materially affect the normalized earnings assessment and forward P/E calculation underpinning the price target.
