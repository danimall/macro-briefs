## Executive Summary

RB Global operates an omnichannel marketplace for used commercial assets and vehicles across two anchor brands: Ritchie Bros. in industrial and construction equipment, and IAA in damaged and total-loss vehicles, acquired in March 2023. The investment debate has narrowed to a single question: was FY2025's weakness a cycle trough or a structural take-rate problem? FY2025 revenue grew 7% to $4,590.7M while gross transaction value grew only 2% to $16.20B, and the growth that did arrive came from buyer-side price increases (transactional buyer revenue +8%) against falling seller revenue (-1%) and marketplace services (-6%). GAAP operating income fell 6% to $713.4M. The stock's total shareholder return index fell 29.7% in 2025 while the company's own proxy peer index rose 25.5% — roughly 55 points of underperformance and the first peer lag in five years.

Q1 2026 answered the volume question favorably. GTV rose 13% to $4,340.9M, with CC&T GTV up 27% on 12% lot growth after FY2025's -2%/-13%, international GTV up 53%, and Australia revenue at $71.4M against $17.1M. Costs of services grew 1% and SG&A 4% against that 13% GTV expansion, the operating leverage the platform model is supposed to produce. Management raised FY2026 guidance to 6-9% GTV growth and roughly 8% adjusted EBITDA growth at the midpoint, excluding any BigIron contribution, and has a demonstrated pattern of guiding GTV conservatively while over-delivering on EBITDA (FY2025 actual adjusted EBITDA of $1,399.7M came in above the raised $1.35-1.38B range).

What stops us from upgrading is price and the quality of the confirming evidence. At $111.23 the shares trade 25.3x the current-year consensus of $4.40 and 22.9x next year's $4.85, roughly 16.1x TTM adjusted EBITDA of $1,434.5M on an enterprise value of ~$23.1B, and a 3.5% FCF yield on FY2025 free cash flow of $719.2M. That is a premium to Copart, the only genuine operating comparable at 19.3x, despite RB Global carrying 1.4x net leverage against Copart's net cash and running structurally lower margins. Consensus EPS estimates are flat over 90 days and down 0.5-1.3% over 30 days across all four forecast periods. The March 2026 quarter beat by 0.7% after three quarters of 9.7-12.6% beats. Management flagged that Q1's CC&T strength contained pent-up supply release and favorable auction-calendar timing and should not be extrapolated, and Q1 adjusted EBITDA contained roughly $5.4M of non-recurring Canadian Digital Services Tax recovery. Q2 results are scheduled for today; near-term implied volatility of 37.4% against 28.2% realized prices a ±6.7% move. We stay at HOLD with a $121 twelve-month base case, and would become buyers toward $95-100 or on a second consecutive quarter of volume-led CC&T growth.

## Company Overview

RB Global matches sellers (consignors) with buyers in roughly 170 countries through combined physical yards and digital bidding platforms, employing approximately 8,000 full-time staff at year-end 2025, about 5,300 of them at auction and branch sites. The Ritchie Bros. brand covers earthmoving, lifting, material handling and vocational trucks, supplemented by online-only formats (IronPlanet, GovPlanet, Marketplace-E, Boom & Bucket). IAA is a digital marketplace for damaged, total-loss and low-value vehicles. Adjacent data and services brands include Rouse (rental benchmarks and valuations), SmartEquip (parts procurement) and VeriTread (heavy-haul transport, taken to 100% ownership in Q1 2026 for ~$12.3M after a minority put exercise).

Reading the financials requires understanding the revenue architecture. Service revenue splits into transactional seller fees, transactional buyer fees and marketplace services. Separately, inventory sales revenue records the **full gross proceeds** of assets RB Global purchases and resells, so a shift from commission contracts toward inventory or guarantee contracts inflates revenue while adding principal risk. FY2025 inventory sales revenue grew 18% to $1,088.5M and Q1 2026 grew 32% to $336.9M. Management therefore steers investors to GTV — total proceeds across all channels, explicitly non-GAAP and not presented in the financial statements — and to inventory rate (inventory return divided by inventory sales revenue).

The company reports as a single operating segment with three goodwill reporting units (Ritchie Bros., IAA, Services). That mismatch is a real analytical constraint: no IAA-standalone or Ritchie Bros.-standalone economics exist publicly, so no sum-of-the-parts or reporting-unit goodwill-cushion work is possible from filings.

The 10-K attributes buyer-revenue growth directly to "changes to our buyer fee structures, which were implemented in late-2024 and early-2025." That is a one-time pricing reset, not a repeatable growth engine.

Operating income fell 6% on 7% revenue growth because SG&A rose 17% to $905.2M — driven by the binding arbitration decision on the former CEO's departure, Q3 2025 restructuring, higher legal and consulting fees, and cloud service costs plus commencing amortization of cloud implementation costs. Adjusted EBITDA $1,399.7M (+7%); unadjusted EBITDA $1,195.7M (-1%). GAAP diluted EPS $2.04 (+1%); adjusted diluted EPS $4.00 (+15%). Inventory rate fell 90bp to 5.3% on "unfavorable asset mix" — more principal risk taken at a lower return.

**The adjusted-versus-GAAP gap deserves precision.** Adjusted net income available to common of $747.0M against GAAP $382.2M is a $364.8M gap. Of that, $282.4M (77%) is amortization of acquired intangibles — non-cash, overwhelmingly IAA-related, and defensible to exclude for cash-earnings purposes, since it runs off over time without any change in operating performance. The remainder includes $76.7M of share-based payments (up 36% year over year), $53.7M of executive transition costs, $19.7M of legal/advisory, $19.4M of acquisition/integration, $17.2M of restructuring and $15.8M of deconsolidation loss. Share-based comp is a recurring annual expense excluded every year; reversing it at the 20.2% effective tax rate reduces FY2025 adjusted EPS by roughly $0.33 to about $3.67. Applying the same haircut to the FY2026 consensus of $4.40 gives roughly $4.07, or 27.3x — a more honest cash multiple than the headline 25.3x.

**Cash generation is the strongest part of the story.** FY2025 operating cash flow $978.2M against net income of $428.4M (the $483.4M D&A load explains most of the gap); capex $259.0M; free cash flow $719.2M, 15.7% of revenue and $3.86 per share. On the $20.7B market capitalization that is a 3.5% FCF yield.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt of $2,471.5M at 12/31/2025: ~$1.0B Credit Agreement term loans (USD $926.3M at 5.17%, CAD $73.0M at 4.06%, both maturing April 2030), $550.0M 6.750% secured notes due March 2028, $800.0M 7.750% unsecured notes due March 2031, plus $137.5M revolver. The April 2025 amendment was genuinely value-additive: revolver up from $750.0M to $1.3B, term loan down from $1.225B to $950.0M, maturity extended from September 2026 to April 2030, bank spread cut ~85bp for $3.9M of charges. Interest expense fell 18% to $191.6M as a result. Adjusted net debt/adjusted EBITDA is 1.4x on TTM figures at 3/31/2026 ($1,937.0M against $1,434.5M), with $1,016.8M undrawn committed revolver and confirmed covenant compliance.

The screened Altman Z of 1.78 is largely a measurement artifact. Goodwill ($4,668.0M) plus intangibles ($2,464.5M) plus operating-lease ROU assets ($1,545.5M) total roughly $8.68B of the $12.14B asset base, so asset turnover of 0.38 understates throughput badly — $16.2B of GTV flows through the platform against $4.6B of recognized revenue. Working capital/assets of 0.014 reflects auction proceeds payable ($687.1M at 3/31/2026), which is consignor money, not borrowings. The score contains a legitimate kernel: $2.5B of debt sits against a thin tangible base, and management names the leverage-ratio covenant as the most restrictive constraint in a sustained downturn, where EBITDA would fall faster than debt.

**Series A Preferred.** $482.0M in temporary equity, convertible at $71.58 (adjusted from $73.00), paying 5.5% plus as-converted participation in common dividends. Full conversion equals roughly 6.73M common-equivalent shares, +3.6% to the count, taking existing holders to ~96.5% of prior ownership — contingent, conversion-dependent dilution excluded from the reported 187.5M Q1 diluted count because the two-class method governed. On 1 February 2027 holders may elect to raise the dividend to 7.50%, subject to the company's right to redeem. Cost of a step-up: $482.0M × 2.0pp ≈ $9.6M annually. Redeeming instead consumes ~$482M of cash or revolver. Either is manageable against $1.4B adjusted EBITDA, but one arrives on a known date. Q1 2026 already showed the drag: $6.7M of preferred dividends plus $4.6M of allocated earnings reduced $135.6M of net income to $124.6M available to common.

**Q1 2026 detail, with two adjustments.** GTV $4,340.9M (+13%); revenue $1,234.6M (+11%); operating income $217.5M (+15%); net income $135.6M (+20%); GAAP EPS $0.66; adjusted EPS $1.01; adjusted EBITDA $362.7M (+11%); inventory rate improved 80bp to 9.0%. CC&T GTV +27% with lots +12%. First adjustment: Canada's retroactive repeal of the Digital Services Tax let RB Global derecognize an $11.2M liability, $5.4M in costs of services and $5.8M in SG&A. Excluding it, operating income growth was ($217.5 - 11.2)/$189.5 - 1 = **8.9%**, not 15%, and adjusted EBITDA growth roughly 9.4% rather than 11% (only the SG&A portion was treated as an adjusting item). Second adjustment: the 10-Q states that "a portion of the quarter's volume growth reflects the release of pent-up supply, as sellers deferred decisions throughout 2025."

Do not annualize Q1 operating cash flow of $224.1M. Within the -$98.6M working-capital change, receivables moved -$191.8M and auction proceeds payable +$232.7M — two sides of end-of-quarter auction timing. Four times Q1 gives ~$896M, *below* FY2025's actual $978.2M despite higher earnings, because Q1 carries the annual incentive payout and, in 2026, settlement of the former-CEO arbitration liability (accrued liabilities fell from $348.1M to $211.5M over the quarter). Growing FY2025 OCF with guided adjusted EBITDA implies roughly $1.0-1.06B for FY2026, a more defensible frame.

## Growth Analysis

Three distinct engines are running, and they carry different durability.

**CC&T cycle recovery — highest torque, least confirmed.** FY2025 CC&T GTV was -2% with lots -13%; Q1 2026 was +27% with lots +12%. Drivers per management: higher ASPs on improved mix and strong U.S. pricing, additional auction events in Canada and the U.S., organic growth in all regions, J.M. Wood and Smith Broughton inclusion, and favorable FX. The structural demand case management names — data-center expansion, manufacturing activity and reshoring — supports used-equipment values and fleet turnover, which is the direct input to Ritchie Bros. GTV. Against that, one quarter containing pent-up supply and calendar timing is thin evidence, and management explicitly cautioned against extrapolation.

**Contract-driven automotive volume — most visible.** The GSA award extends RB Global's five-year fleet-marshaling relationship into remarketing fleet-return vehicles through IAA's marketplace at roughly 35,000 vehicles annually, with full run rate expected in Q2 2026 and ASPs management describes as accretive to salvage. IAA is the sole salvage partner for Suncorp in Australia at an estimated 65,000 units annually once fully operational, with first units processed in Q3 2025 — the principal driver of Australia revenue reaching $71.4M from $17.1M. Both are contract-driven rather than economically cyclical. Total-loss frequency, the swing variable for organic salvage volume, is favorable: the repair-cost-to-used-vehicle-price differential "continued to narrow, though it remains positive" in FY2025 and "expanded" in Q1 2026, with third-party data relayed by management putting frequency at 22.6% versus 21.9%.

**Vertical and geographic roll-up — accretive to GTV, dilutive to take rate.** J.M. Wood ($213.6M, closed July 2025), Smith Broughton (~$38M, November 2025), Blackmon (April 2026, >$60M of 2025 GTV), and BigIron (~$350M, HSR early termination granted 21 April 2026, expected Q2 close) extend coverage into agriculture, land and livestock. Simultaneously RB Global exited capital-intensive processing: SYNETIQ dismantling contributed into a 40%-owned JV with LKQ Europe (a $19.7M Q2 2025 charge), DDI sold, Xcira wound down. The pivot toward asset-lighter fee-based GTV is coherent, but it is a pivot into structurally lower take-rate verticals — management conceded on the Q1 call that agriculture and real-estate expansion will pressure reported take rates, and the 10-Q notes acquired businesses contributed at a lower average buyer fee rate.

**What consensus expects.** Current-year EPS of $4.40 and next-year $4.85 imply 10% and 10% growth. Guidance of 6-9% GTV and ~8% adjusted EBITDA growth excluding BigIron implies roughly $1.51B of FY2026 adjusted EBITDA — but TTM at 3/31/2026 is already $1,434.5M, so the guide embeds deceleration from Q1's +11%. Estimate revisions do not show inflection in either direction: current-year is +1.0% over 90 days but -0.5% over 30 days; next-year is +0.1% and -0.6%. The magnitudes are inside noise, but the 30-day direction is uniformly negative across all four periods, and the March-quarter beat narrowed to 0.7% from a prior run of 9.7%, 12.6% and 11.1%. Visibility is tightening, not loosening.

## Valuation Assessment

All arithmetic from the $111.23 basis and 186.3M shares.

- Market capitalization $20.72B; adjusted net debt $1.937B; preferred $0.482B → **enterprise value ~$23.14B**
- EV / TTM adjusted EBITDA $1,434.5M = **16.1x**; on FY2026E ~$1.51B = **15.3x**
- P/E on current-year consensus $4.40 = **25.3x**; on next-year $4.85 = **22.9x**
- P/E on FY2025 GAAP EPS $2.04 = **54.5x**; adding back after-tax acquisition amortization (~$0.97/share) gives ~$3.01 and **37x**
- FCF yield on FY2025 $719.2M = **3.5%**
- Dividend $1.24 annualized = **1.11%** yield; 31% of adjusted EPS, 61% of GAAP

The quote feed's "forward P/E" of 52.2 is a trailing-GAAP artifact, not a consensus forward multiple; the genuine forward figure is 25.3x.

**Peer context requires care.** The supplied comparable set is heterogeneous — three government IT services firms (LDOS 11.2x, CACI 21.7x, SAIC 12.9x) and Amentum (36.8x) share almost no business characteristics with a transaction marketplace. Copart at 19.3x is the only real operating comparable, and RB Global trades at a premium to it on every consistent basis while carrying 1.4x net leverage against Copart's net cash position, lower operating margins, and a fee structure whose reported take rate is declining by design. Copart is itself 39% below its 52-week high, which matters: the salvage-auction complex has already derated, capping the multiple RB Global can realistically re-rate toward.

**Where the market may be wrong.** The bull mispricing argument is that FY2025's 29.7% derating priced a structural take-rate problem, when the actual issue was a cyclical CC&T volume trough plus a non-repeatable pricing reset. If CC&T normalizes to mid-single-digit GTV growth with the yard-throughput gains intact, FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $5.10-5.20 and 27-28x is defensible on a business with 16% FCF margins and a duopoly position in salvage. Consensus mean and median targets of $127.91 and $132.00 sit 15% and 19% above spot.

**Where that argument breaks.** On 25.3x forward earnings against ~10% EPS growth, the PEG is roughly 2.5, so this stock is not cheap relative to rising estimates, and consensus targets are not independent confirmation of upside; they reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price. Adjusted EPS excludes $76.7M of recurring share-based comp, so the true cash multiple is nearer 27.3x. The 3.5% FCF yield sits below the current 10-year Treasury, a poor absolute-yield setup for a business that must refinance $550M at 6.750% in 2028 and $800M at 7.750% in 2031. And the stock has already recovered 19% from its 31 March low of $93.58, so the drawdown-driven asymmetry that existed in March has been consumed.

Base case: 25x next-year consensus $4.85 = **$121** (+8.8%). Bull: 28x ~$5.10 = **$142** (+27.7%). Bear: ~21x $4.20 = **$88** (-20.9%). Valuation scores 5.0 — full but not absurd, with the asymmetry roughly balanced rather than favorable.

## Competitive Landscape

In salvage, the 10-K says RB Global "primarily compete[s] with Copart, Inc." — a two-player structure. The moat rests on execution and buyer liquidity, not contractual lock-in. Insurance-carrier supply agreements are generally cancellable on **30 to 90 days' notice**, institutional volume commitments are non-binding, and the 10-K names disintermediation risk explicitly: online marketplaces without meaningful physical presence, and insurance companies selling directly to end users through such platforms.

The most defensible asset is physical: zoned, permitted yard acreage in capacity-constrained markets, plus regulatory infrastructure (state salvage titling, "who may buy" restrictions, DOT transport rules) that raises entry costs for asset-light digital challengers. Within that footprint, management claims roughly 25% incremental effective capacity versus pre-transaction levels, achieved by compressing sign-to-settle cycle times through branch incentives, IAA loan payoff, title procurement and virtual inspection. Corroborating filing facts: costs of services grew 1% against GTV +13% in Q1 2026 and 1% against revenue +7% in FY2025; adjusted EBITDA as a percentage of GTV expanded to 8.4% from 7.8%; IAA posted three consecutive quarters of automotive unit-share gains through Q3 2025. The 25% figure is management's unaudited operational estimate with no defined baseline, and some cost restraint reflects mix (lower CC&T transportation costs, non-recurrence of prior-year profit-sharing) rather than pure efficiency. Fungible yards and cross-trained staff that absorb catastrophe surges without incremental fixed cost are a genuine, hard-to-replicate advantage.

CC&T is structurally fragmented — the 10-K concedes some competitors have "significantly greater financial and marketing resources and name recognition." Fragmentation caps pricing power but creates the roll-up runway RB Global is exploiting under a disciplined stated filter: fill-in geography, or a vertical plus expertise scalable across the network.

Trajectory reads as improving on volume and share, flat-to-eroding on price. Take rate fell 160bp to 20.7% in Q1 2026. Management calls the decline substantially optical — mechanically true, since tiered buyer fees decline as a percentage at higher price points and ASPs rose. But management also concedes agriculture, real estate and BigIron will pressure reported take rates structurally. Both are true. Investors should judge fee dollars, not percentages, and accept that GTV growth will convert to revenue at a declining rate.

## Risk Assessment

**Supplier concentration — the largest quantified idiosyncratic risk.** Approximately 23% of FY2025 consolidated revenue came from vehicles supplied by the three largest supplier customers, on agreements generally cancellable by either party on 30 to 90 days' notice. Only one year of disclosure exists at this granularity, so the trend is unknown. Geographic diversification (Australia 4.2x, Canada +16%) does not establish supplier diversification, and should not be read as such.

**Canada Revenue Agency assessment with an unreserved tail.** A December 2024 Notice of Assessment covering 2010-2015 totals C$79.1M (C$37.7M tax plus C$41.4M interest and penalties), asserting a Luxembourg subsidiary was Canadian-resident. RB Global paid a C$39.5M statutory deposit in February 2025, filed a Tax Court appeal in June 2025, and the Crown moved for dismissal in October 2025. Separately, the CRA requested 2016-2020 information in late 2024 and again in September 2025 — covering exactly the remaining five years of the subsidiary's life — with **no assessment issued** and **nothing recorded** at either 12/31/2025 or 3/31/2026. The assessed amount is manageable against $1.4B adjusted EBITDA. The 2016-2020 exposure cannot be sized from public disclosure, and per-year extrapolation is not defensible because interest accrues differently by year and subsidiary profitability by year is undisclosed. Treat as an unquantified tail, not a modeled liability.

**Goodwill concentrated in IAA.** For the 12/31/2025 annual test, RB Global performed qualitative assessments for Ritchie Bros. and Services but a **quantitative** assessment for IAA. No impairment resulted. One reading is that IAA's fair-value cushion is thinnest; an equally plausible reading is that quantitative testing is conservative practice for the largest and most recently acquired unit. The disclosure does not distinguish them, and with one reportable segment no external cushion analysis is possible.

**Unquantified environmental exposures.** Authorities have investigated IAA's role at the Lower Duwamish Waterway Superfund Site (Seattle) and a subsidiary's role at the Pyrite Canyon Plume (Jurupa Valley, CA). "Our potential liability at these sites cannot be estimated at this time." Superfund allocation is joint-and-several. Neither has become a material legal proceeding.

**Leverage and refinancing.** 1.4x net leverage with $1.0B undrawn to April 2030 is comfortable, but $550M at 6.750% matures in 2028 and $800M at 7.750% in 2031. Management names the leverage covenant as the binding constraint in a sustained downturn, the scenario in which EBITDA falls faster than debt.

**Volume drivers outside company control.** Automotive volume equals accidents times total-loss frequency, which depends on the spread between repair-cost inflation and used-vehicle prices — favorable now, reversible. Named structural threats: ADAS and autonomous vehicles reducing accident frequency, consumers dropping collision coverage, underinsurance delaying total-loss designations, state title-processing delays. CC&T depends on infrastructure spend, data-center construction, interest rates and used-equipment values.

**Rising principal risk and capital-allocation competition.** Inventory sales revenue grew 18% in FY2025 and 32% in Q1 2026; inventory rate improved to 9.0% from 8.2%, so 2026 execution is better than FY2025's 5.3% full year. Guarantee exposure is modest and disclosed: $166.2M of assets under contract at 3/31/2026, 98% expected to sell by 6/30/2026, with true at-risk equal to the shortfall to guarantee. Separately, roughly $601M of announced acquisition spend (J.M. Wood, Smith Broughton, BigIron) competes with a $500M buyback authorization that remained **fully undrawn** at 3/31/2026.

**Governance and structural items.** A 20% shareholder-rights-plan trigger, unlimited board-issuable preferred, advance-notice by-laws and OBCA incorporation with the 10-K's warning that U.S. civil liabilities may not be enforceable collectively lower the probability of a change-of-control premium. 28% of FY2025 revenue and 32% of operating expenses are non-USD with no hedging of revenues or expenses.

**Unquantified near-term item.** Management flagged Middle East operational and safety disruption plus "issues with a market alliance partner," explicitly unquantified, alongside fuel-cost headwinds partly passable through.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $142 (+27.7%)

FY2025 was a CC&T volume trough compounded by a one-time buyer-fee reset that flattered revenue and disguised the underlying volume weakness; the market extrapolated the wrong variable and derated the stock 29.7% while peers rose 25.5%. Q1 2026 reversed the volume line (CC&T GTV +27%, lots +12%, international +53%) while costs of services grew 1%, demonstrating that the yard-throughput gains are real and that incremental GTV drops through at high margin. GSA at ~35,000 vehicles annually reaches full run rate in Q2 2026, Suncorp scales toward ~65,000 units, BigIron adds a new vertical, and management has a demonstrated pattern of guiding GTV low and beating on EBITDA (FY2025 actual $1,399.7M exceeded the raised $1.35-1.38B range). The ~8% EBITDA guide excludes BigIron entirely. Data-center and reshoring construction sustains used-equipment values and fleet turnover. With $500M of buyback authorization fully undrawn and 1.4x leverage, FY2027 adjusted EPS of $5.10-5.20 at 28x supports $142.

### Bear Case — $88 (-20.9%)

Q1's CC&T strength was pent-up supply, auction-calendar timing and acquisition inclusion, and normalizes toward the FY2026 guide's implied deceleration or below; management said as much. Strip $11.2M of DST recovery and Q1 operating income growth was 8.9%, not 15%, with roughly $5.4M of that benefit sitting inside adjusted EBITDA unadjusted. The take rate declines structurally as agriculture, real estate and acquired platforms dilute blended economics, so GTV growth converts to revenue and EBITDA at a falling rate. Adjusted EPS excludes $76.7M of recurring share-based comp, so the cash multiple is ~27.3x, a premium to Copart despite 1.4x leverage, lower margins and a 3.5% FCF yield below the 10-year Treasury. Any of the three largest suppliers — 23% of revenue combined — can leave on 30 to 90 days' notice. The CRA could assess 2016-2020 with nothing reserved. A construction slowdown reverses CC&T just as comparisons harden. At 21x $4.20 the shares are worth $88, below the $93.58 fifty-two-week low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** Base case $121 over twelve months, derived as 25x next-year consensus adjusted EPS of $4.85 — the same multiple the shares carry today on current-year estimates, applied to a year forward. Cross-check: ~16x FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of roughly $1.62B gives an EV of $25.9B, less ~$2.4B of net debt and preferred, or ~$126 per share. Base case implies **+8.8%** from $111.23; bull $142 implies **+27.7%**; bear $88 implies **-20.9%**. That distribution is roughly symmetric, which is a hold rather than a buy.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Q2 2026 CC&T GTV growth above 10% with lot growth above 5%** — a second consecutive volume-led quarter would convert the Q1 inflection from a pull-forward artifact into a trend, and would justify paying 27-28x forward.

## Conclusion

RB Global has done the operational work. The April 2025 refinancing removed a 2026 maturity wall, cut spreads 85bp and reduced interest expense 18%. The reorganization delivered more than $25M of annualized run-rate savings with costs of services flat against 13% GTV growth. Portfolio pruning traded capital-intensive dismantling for fee-based marketplace GTV, and the acquisition program follows a stated, disciplined filter. Q1 2026 produced the volume-led quarter the FY2025 bear case said would not come, and 1.4x net leverage against $719M of free cash flow leaves ample room to fund both BigIron and the buyback.

The price already reflects most of this. At 25.3x current-year adjusted EPS — closer to 27.3x on a cash basis that respects $76.7M of recurring share-based comp — and 16.1x TTM adjusted EBITDA on a 3.5% FCF yield, RB Global trades at a premium to Copart while carrying more leverage, lower margins and a reported take rate management concedes will keep falling on vertical mix. Estimate revisions are flat over 90 days and slightly negative over 30. The March-quarter beat narrowed to 0.7% after three quarters near 10%. Q1's headline growth contained $11.2M of one-time tax recovery and, by management's own account, pent-up supply and favorable calendar timing. Against that, three named risks sit unreserved and unquantified: 23% of revenue on 30-to-90-day cancellable supplier contracts, five un-assessed CRA years, and two Superfund-adjacent sites.

This is a good business at a fair-to-full price with a genuine but single-quarter cyclical inflection and a binary event pricing today. The new information since the prior review — flat price, a 30-day negative drift in estimates, a compressed beat, and a 10.6-point IV spike into the print — argues for maintaining the rating rather than upgrading. We would pay 20x for this asset. That is $98. Owners should hold through the print and reassess against the CC&T lot-count line; new capital should wait for either a second confirming quarter or a price in the mid-to-high $90s.
