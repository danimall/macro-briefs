**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Lockheed Martin is the world's largest pure-play defense prime, with $75.0B in FY2025 revenue, a $193.6B total backlog, and entrenched incumbency on multi-decade franchise programs (F-35, PAC-3, THAAD, Trident, Orion). The demand backdrop is the strongest in a generation: the FY2027 defense request seeks a $1.5T budget with proposed increases of roughly +62% for F-35, ~8x for PAC-3/MSE, and ~9x for THAAD, plus Golden Dome and Next Generation Interceptor — all core Lockheed franchises. On demand alone, this would be a clean buy.

The demand is not converting cleanly to earnings. FY2025 net income fell to $5.0B and diluted EPS dropped to $21.49, the second consecutive annual decline, despite 5.6% revenue growth. The drag is recurring reach-forward losses on fixed-price development programs that management explicitly warns are not finished: a ~$1.8B cumulative loss on a classified Aeronautics program, ~$1.46B on a classified MFC program, ~$670M on the Canadian Maritime Helicopter Program, plus fresh F-16 and C-130 charges in Q1 2026 that show the problem spreading from troubled development work into mature production lines. Q1 2026 free cash flow turned negative (-$291M), cash fell to $1.9B, buybacks halted, and Lockheed paid down $1.0B of debt, shifting toward balance-sheet defense.

Three structural concerns are layered on top, and the market is repricing them: Lockheed lost the NGAD sixth-generation fighter competition to Boeing's F-47, putting the long-term Aeronautics growth engine in question; a securities class action and a derivative suit tied to the classified-program disclosures are now live; and an Executive Order under consideration could restrict buybacks, dividends, and executive comp at underperforming contractors — the exact category Lockheed's execution stumbles risk placing it in. At ~24x trailing earnings on a falling EPS base, the stock is not cheap relative to its own degraded earnings power, and the bull case requires faith that the program losses are one-time cleanup rather than a margin reset. We rate LMT AVOID.

## Company Overview

Lockheed operates four segments, overwhelmingly dependent on the U.S. Government (72% of 2025 sales, 63% to the DoD), with 28% international (most of which is Foreign Military Sales contracted through the U.S. Government):

- **Aeronautics — $30.3B / 6.9% margin.** Home to the F-35 (27% of total consolidated revenue), plus F-16, C-130J, F-22. A record 191 F-35s delivered in 2025; production backlog of 336 aircraft as of Q1 2026. This is the largest segment and the locus of the program-loss problem.
- **Missiles & Fire Control (MFC) — $14.5B / 13.8% margin.** The standout grower (+14% sales) and highest-margin segment. PAC-3, THAAD, JASSM, LRASM, GMLRS, PrSM, Javelin, hypersonics. Backlog surged to $46.7B.
- **Rotary & Mission Systems (RMS) — $17.3B / 7.6% margin.** Sikorsky helicopters, Aegis, radars. Margins hit by program losses (CMHP).
- **Space — $13.0B / 10.3% margin.** Trident II, Orion, Next Gen OPIR, GPS III, NGI, hypersonics. Includes the 50%-owned ULA joint venture.

A clean bifurcation is emerging: MFC and Space are thriving on the missile-defense rearmament cycle, while Aeronautics and RMS are the loss centers. The investment debate reduces to whether the missile/space tailwind is large enough to offset the Aeronautics franchise risk and the recurring fixed-price losses.

## Financial Analysis

The headline of FY2025 and Q1 2026 is margin erosion from program execution, not demand weakness.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 66.0 | 67.6 | 71.0 | 75.0 |
| Operating profit ($B) | 8.35 | 8.51 | 7.01 | 7.73 |
| Net income ($B) | 5.73 | 6.92 | 5.34 | 5.02 |
| Diluted EPS | — | $27.55 | $22.31 | $21.49 |
| Free cash flow ($B) | — | 6.23 | 5.29 | 6.91 |

Revenue grew 5.6% in 2025, driven by MFC (+14%, missile production ramp) and Aeronautics (+6%, F-35). The earnings drag was concentrated in reach-forward losses: a $950M loss on the Aeronautics classified program, $570M on the Canadian Maritime Helicopter Program, $95M on the Turkish Utility Helicopter Program, and $140M of unfavorable C-130 adjustments, plus a $479M non-cash pension settlement charge. FY2024 had been hit by an even larger $1.4B MFC classified loss and a $555M Aeronautics classified loss. Two consecutive years of nine-figure-plus reach-forward charges is the pattern that matters; these are not isolated cleanups.

FCF of $6.9B in 2025 (9.2% margin) was aided by lower tax payments under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. The trajectory deteriorated sharply in Q1 2026: FCF swung to -$291M (from +$955M YoY) on working-capital timing as F-35 contract assets ballooned $2.9B, and cash fell from $4.1B to $1.9B in a single quarter. Management ran no buybacks (vs $750M prior year) and repaid $1.0B of debt, a defensive posture consistent with the looming capital-return EO and the cash bleed from loss-making contracts over their remaining life.

On the balance sheet, the headline Altman Z-Score of 1.53 ("distress zone") is a false positive. Equity is only $6.7B against $59.8B of assets, producing a ~7.9x debt-to-equity ratio and ~75% ROE. This thin equity is engineered by years of aggressive buybacks ($3.0B in 2025, $3.7B in 2024) plus a $7.5B accumulated other comprehensive loss from pension. Total debt of $21.7–22.9B against $7–8.5B annual operating cash flow, supported by a $193B backlog and government termination protections, is manageable. The Z-Score model does not fit this capital structure. The real financial issue is margin quality and the recent cash-flow inflection, not solvency. We score Financial Health at 5.0 — a downside filter that flags the negative FCF quarter, the cash drawdown, and the thin equity cushion, while recognizing the underlying franchise and backlog are sound.

## Growth Analysis

The top-line growth story is durable and broad-based. Global rearmament across Europe, the Middle East, and the Pacific is driving genuine demand for munitions and missile defense, with multiple confirming signals (multi-front conflict, NATO spending increases, and a U.S. budget request that targets Lockheed's franchises). The MFC backlog and the FY2027 procurement increases are the most credible part of the bull case.

The disconnect between revenue growth and earnings growth is the problem. Consensus EPS estimates are essentially flat — current year $29.89, next year $32.11 — with 90-day revisions of +0.1% and +0.5%. There is no estimate-revision inflection here; the strong revision signal that flags genuine earnings momentum is absent. Revenue is growing 5–6% while net income has fallen two years running. The recurring analyst caveat is on point: not all of this growth flows to the bottom line, because fixed-price losses and supply-chain cost pressure intercept it on the way.

The longer-term growth picture is clouded by the NGAD loss. With Boeing winning the F-47 sixth-generation franchise, Aeronautics' growth engine beyond F-35 production and sustainment is uncertain. The "F-55/Ferrari" F-35 derivative Taiclet is pitching is early-stage and unfunded, and defense analysts have been skeptical. F-35 itself carries a sustainment overhang after the Pentagon reportedly declared the TR-3/Block 4 upgrade "predominantly unusable." We score Growth Prospects at 5.5 — the demand is real and broad, but the conversion to per-share earnings growth is impaired and the long-term Aeronautics trajectory has a genuine question mark.

## Valuation Assessment

At $510.95, Lockheed trades at ~24x trailing EPS of $21.49 and roughly 17x consensus 2026 EPS of $29.89. The forward multiple looks reasonable in isolation, but the comparison is misleading because the forward number embeds a clean recovery from the reach-forward losses that management itself warns may continue.

| Ticker | P/E | Div Yield |
|---|---|---|
| HWM | 61.4 | 0.2% |
| GD | 22.6 | 1.9% |
| NOC | 17.3 | 1.8% |
| TDG | 39.3 | 7.4% |
| BA | 88.8 | — |
| **LMT** | **24.8** | **2.6%** |
| Peer median | 39.3 | 1.8% |

Against the peer median P/E of 39.3, LMT screens cheap. But the median is distorted by Boeing (88.8, depressed earnings), Howmet (61.4, commercial-aero growth premium), and TransDigm (39.3, aftermarket margins). The cleaner comparables are the defense primes: Northrop at 17.3 and General Dynamics at 22.6, both with cleaner execution records, lower leverage, and intact growth franchises. Northrop in particular holds the B-21 and Sentinel sole-source positions Lockheed cannot match, and trades at a discount to LMT. Lockheed at 24.8x trailing earnings on a falling EPS base, with a lost NGAD franchise, recurring program losses, and securities litigation, is not obviously mispriced to the upside.

Cycle adjustment does not rescue the case. The framework for treating a high trailing multiple as cheap applies to inflection stocks with rapidly rising estimates, and Lockheed's estimates are flat. There is no earnings inflection underway; the trailing multiple reflects degraded earnings power that consensus does not expect to re-rate sharply. Treating the 24x as "trough" would require evidence the program losses are behind the company, which management's own language contradicts.

The ~26% drawdown from the $692 high is the bull's entry argument. At a >30% drawdown threshold the burden would be on the bull to identify what the market is missing; at ~26% the burden is shared, but the sellers here are not obviously wrong. They are pricing a specific, documented cluster of problems: recurring fixed-price losses, negative Q1 FCF, the NGAD loss, securities litigation, and a capital-return regulatory threat. The 2.6% dividend yield provides some support, but with FCF turning negative and a potential EO on capital returns, even the income case carries a tail risk. We score Valuation at 4.5 — the multiple is not stretched on optical forward earnings, but the compressed look reflects genuine structural concerns rather than a clean mispricing, and cheapness without a recovery catalyst is a value-trap signature.

## Competitive Landscape

Lockheed's moat on existing franchises remains wide: a near-monopoly on Western fifth-generation fighters (F-35), the dominant missile-defense interceptor position (PAC-3/THAAD/NGI), high FMS switching costs with seven F-35 partner nations, and cost-plus structure on ~40% of revenue. On level, the competitive position is strong.

On trajectory, which is what matters for scoring, the picture is deteriorating. The NGAD loss to Boeing is a generational franchise setback: the next-generation air-dominance program that would have anchored Aeronautics for decades went to a competitor. The F-35 TR-3/Block 4 sustainment problems add a reputational overhang to the flagship. The 5-year cumulative TSR of 156.4 versus the peer group's 230.5 quantifies how much Lockheed has lagged its defense peers, consistent with the NGAD loss and execution issues. MFC and Space are competitively strong and gaining on the missile-defense cycle, but the Aeronautics franchise, the largest segment, faces structural questions. We score Competitive Position at 5.0 — strong incumbency offset by a deteriorating trajectory in the flagship segment.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk cluster is unusually dense and not yet fully priced:

1. **Fixed-price development losses remain "live."** Management uses unusually direct language that additional material losses are possible on the classified Aeronautics and MFC programs. New Q1 2026 F-16 ($125M) and C-130 ($85M) charges show the problem spreading into mature production. This is an ongoing earnings-volatility source, not a one-time event.

2. **Securities litigation.** A putative securities class action (filed 7/28/2025, amended 1/12/2026, S.D.N.Y.) alleges misstatements about the classified programs and F-35 over 1/23/2024–7/21/2025, with a related derivative suit. These flow directly from the reach-forward losses.

3. **Executive Order on capital returns (novel, company-specific).** A potential EO could restrict buybacks, dividends, and executive comp for contractors failing performance requirements, implemented via DFARS clause. Given Lockheed's recent execution failures, this is a pointed threat to the capital-return appeal that has historically supported the stock.

4. **Cash-flow inflection.** The negative Q1 FCF, cash drawdown, halted buybacks, and $1.0B debt paydown together signal management is prioritizing balance-sheet defense. The acquisition-reform shift toward progress payments instead of performance-based payments is an additional cash-flow headwind.

5. **Supply chain.** Tariffs cost ~$485M of 2025 cash flow (mostly expected recoverable); China rare-earth export controls and sanctions on LMT over Taiwan FMS create input risk.

6. **Concentration.** 72% U.S. Government, F-35 at 27% of revenue, exposure to continuing resolutions and shutdowns.

Insider signal is negative-to-neutral: no open-market purchases during a drawdown of roughly a quarter from the high, with the MFC and Aeronautics segment presidents selling shares in late February/early March 2026. The absence of any insider buying into the decline is a caution. We score Risk Factors at 4.0 — multiple specific, partly unpriced catalysts for downside, with the litigation and capital-return EO being the least-priced.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV at 27.3% sits essentially in line with 1-year historical vol of 26.6% (spread 0.7%), and IV percentile within the current chain is just 2% — options are not pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. ATM IV collapsed from 47.0% to 27.3% over the prior week as the post-earnings event passed, with the price falling 5.6%. The put/call skew is call-heavy (-8.2%) at the front, and the P/C OI ratio is a benign 0.82. The options market is not confirming an acute bearish thesis; it reads as complacent rather than fearful. This matters for the short assessment below: the market is not pricing a near-term collapse, which both reduces the urgency of the AVOID and removes the asymmetry needed to short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($600–680).** The FY2027 budget tailwind is real and broad. If the classified-program losses prove to be one-time cleanup and margins normalize, MFC's 13.8% margins scale across a growing backlog and the missile-defense supercycle drives mid-single-digit revenue growth into double-digit EPS growth. On 2027 normalized EPS of ~$32–34 at a 19–20x multiple, the stock reaches $620–680. The 2.6% dividend and $8.3B remaining buyback authorization provide support if the capital-return EO does not bite.

**Bear Case ($450).** The reach-forward losses prove structural rather than cyclical, additional charges materialize on the classified Aeronautics and MFC programs, the securities litigation drags on, and the capital-return EO restricts buybacks and dividends. EPS stays flat-to-down around $21–23, the multiple compresses toward the Northrop/GD defense-prime range of 17–20x on a degraded earnings base, and the NGAD loss caps the long-term Aeronautics growth rate. This implies $450, roughly 12% below current.

The asymmetry is roughly balanced-to-negative from here: the bull case requires faith that management's own warnings about continuing losses are conservative, while the bear case requires only that the documented problems persist. With flat estimates, no insider buying, and deteriorating cash flow, the burden of proof sits with the bulls, and they have not met it.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** There is no specific, dated downside catalyst. The next earnings report (Q2 2026, ~late July) could disclose additional reach-forward charges, but management reaffirmed 2026 guidance in May, and the budget tailwind provides a counterweight. The securities litigation is slow-moving with no near-term ruling. The capital-return EO is a possibility, not a scheduled event.

**Timing window.** Diffuse. The fundamental concerns are real but lack a trigger that resolves on a known date. A catalyst this uncertain is not worth the carry.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is just 1.4% of float, days to cover 2.0. This is an uncrowded short; informed investors are not pressing the bearish view, which is itself a caution. It leaves room, but the absence of crowd confirmation alongside flat estimates suggests the bears do not see a near-term break either.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options are not confirming a bearish thesis: front skew is call-heavy, IV percentile is low, and the P/C OI ratio is benign. The market is complacent, not fearful. Borrow is cheap and unconstrained at 1.4% SI.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target ~$450 (-12%); upside risk to ~$600+ (+17%) on a budget-driven re-rating or a clean quarter. That is worse than 1:1 against the short. The budget cycle caps downside asymmetry.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** Lockheed is a financially sound prime with execution problems, not a collapsing business. There is no dated catalyst, the options market is complacent, short interest is negligible, and a powerful budget tailwind limits downside asymmetry. This is a name to avoid owning, not to short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:**
- Evidence the fixed-price program losses are contained — two consecutive quarters with no new reach-forward charges on the Aeronautics or MFC classified programs and no spread into additional production lines.
- A return to positive, growing free cash flow with resumed buybacks, confirming the Q1 2026 cash inflection was working-capital timing rather than structural.
- Valuation compression to ~17–19x forward EPS (toward the Northrop/GD defense-prime range), or a credible, funded path for the Aeronautics franchise post-NGAD that the market can underwrite.
- Resolution or clear bounding of the securities litigation and removal of the capital-return EO threat.

**Timeline.** The execution headwinds are likely to persist 12–24 months: fixed-price development losses bleed over the remaining contract life, and the NGAD franchise question will not resolve quickly. We expect the budget tailwind to support the top line throughout, which limits downside, but margin recovery and franchise clarity are the gating items for an upgrade.

**Upgrade path.** If a clean Q2/Q3 2026 demonstrates loss containment plus the budget tailwind converting to rising EPS estimates (a +30% revision signal would be decisive), the case flips to HOLD or BUY quickly. We will reassess at the late-July Q2 print.

## Conclusion

Lockheed Martin sits at the intersection of the best demand environment in a generation and a cluster of self-inflicted execution problems. The missile-defense and F-35 budget tailwind is genuine and benefits MFC and Space directly. But the company has posted two consecutive years of declining EPS despite revenue growth, carries recurring fixed-price program losses that management explicitly warns are not finished, turned FCF negative in Q1 2026, lost the NGAD sixth-generation franchise to Boeing, faces securities litigation tied to the very losses driving the thesis, and confronts a novel regulatory threat to its capital-return model. At ~24x trailing earnings on a falling EPS base, with flat consensus estimates and no insider buying into the drawdown, the stock is not priced for the structural concerns that are accumulating.

The investment debate is whether the program losses are cyclical cleanup or a structural margin and competitiveness reset. Management's own language — that additional material losses are possible — argues against the optimistic reading. We rate LMT **AVOID** with a fair-value range of $450–520. The budget tailwind caps the downside and removes any case for shorting, but it does not justify owning the stock at the current price given the unresolved execution, litigation, and capital-return risks. We would revisit on evidence of loss containment, restored free cash flow, or a meaningful valuation reset toward the cleaner defense primes.
