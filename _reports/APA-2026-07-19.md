**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

APA Corporation is a mid-cap ($12.5B) diversified independent oil & gas producer running a three-region core (Permian ~60%, Egypt ~33%, North Sea in run-off ~7%) plus long-dated frontier optionality in Suriname, Alaska, and Uruguay. The investment case reduces to a tension between two genuine facts: the stock is cheap (~8.2x trailing, ~6-7x forward EPS, ~3.6x EV/EBITDA by one third-party estimate), and the discount is earned. Management has executed a real cost-reduction program ($350M annualized savings achieved, $450M targeted by end-2026), cut total debt from $6.0B to $4.5B in a single year, and holds a genuine re-rating catalyst in the TotalEnergies-operated GranMorgu deepwater project targeting first oil in 2028. Against that, US gas realizations went negative (–$0.32/Mcf Q1 2026, –$2.20/Mcf in the Q2 supplemental), forcing active curtailment; a $0.9-1.2B Gulf of America decommissioning tail liability is growing in disclosure prominence with minimal reimbursement; and roughly a third of production sells to a single Egyptian state counterparty with a documented payment-delay history.

The near-term signal is deteriorating even as the balance sheet strengthens. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised down 10.1% over 30 days (to $5.79), and next-quarter estimates are down 21.6% over the same window, reflecting the negative Waha gas dynamic and the fact that 2025's headline operating cash flow strength (+26% to $4.5B) was substantially a one-time Egyptian receivable collection that did not repeat — Q1 2026 OCF fell 49% to $554M. The earnings-beat track record is strong (four straight beats, most recently +20.6% in Q1 2026), but that reflects conservative guidance against realized-price tailwinds, not a durable structural improvement.

We rate APA a HOLD. The valuation is supportive and the Suriname catalyst is de-risking on the operator's schedule, but the cheapness sits alongside a falling-estimate trend, a genuine tail liability, and commodity/gas dynamics that are actively working against the near-term earnings line. The durable return is the ~2.9% dividend plus debt-funded deleveraging, with Suriname the option that justifies patience rather than an immediate buy. We would upgrade on a confirmed positive estimate-revision turn or a verified structural resolution of the Waha gas drag.

## Company Overview

APA is the holding company parent of Apache Corporation, structured as a geographically diversified independent E&P rather than a Permian pure-play. The portfolio breaks into three producing regions and a frontier growth bucket:

- **United States (Permian Basin)** — ~60-62% of production, the core cash engine across the Midland and Delaware basins, expanded by the April 2024 all-stock Callon acquisition (~$4.5B including debt; ~120K net Delaware acres plus 25K Midland). The company is running ~5 rigs, down from 7 in 2025, prioritizing capital efficiency over volume growth.
- **Egypt** — ~32-33% of production, APA's longest-running international asset in the Western Desert. Sinopec holds a one-third noncontrolling interest, which represents real cash leakage ($430M in distributions to Sinopec in 2025). A new EGPC gas sales agreement effective January 2025 carries a $2.65/MMBtu floor with improved incremental economics, and Egypt awarded 2M additional net exploration acres in Q3 2025.
- **North Sea (UK)** — ~7% of production, being wound down. APA expects to cease North Sea production before 2030 because the 38% UK Energy Profits Levy plus decommissioning obligations make reinvestment uneconomic. This is structural shrinkage of the asset base.
- **Frontier optionality** — Suriname Block 58 ("GranMorgu") is the marquee catalyst: a deepwater development operated by TotalEnergies (APA is a working-interest partner alongside state-owned Staatsolie), targeting first oil in 2028 and now a separate reporting segment. APA also holds a 50% Alaska interest (a 2025 discovery flow-tested ~2,700 b/d) and Uruguay exploration acreage.

FY2025 production averaged ~464 MBoe/d (~51% crude), with ~38% of production and ~26% of proved reserves international. The diversification provides an offshore and international growth runway most Permian peers lack, at the cost of sovereign, FX, fiscal, and non-operated-partner risks.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings and cash flow.** FY2025 revenue was $17.84B, down from $19.47B in 2024. Net income to common rose to $1.43B ($3.99/diluted share) from $804M ($2.27), but that increase was largely mechanical — 2024 carried $1.1B of impairments (North Sea $796M, US $315M) that did not recur. Underlying 2025 benefited from cost cuts. Operating cash flow was flattered by collection of past-due Egyptian receivables, a working-capital tailwind that reversed in Q1 2026. Any normalized FCF estimate must strip the EGPC catch-up; this is the single most important adjustment for anyone underwriting the "60% of FCF returned" framework.

**Cost program.** The differentiator this cycle is genuine cost-out: $350M annualized savings by year-end 2025, raised to a $450M target by end-2026 (an incremental $100M added in Q1). Lease operating expense fell to $8.86/boe (2025) from $10.16 (2024). 2026 upstream capex is guided to ~$2.1B, down from $2.7-2.9B, split ~$1.8B Permian/Egypt development, ~$230M Suriname, ~$70M Alaska/Suriname exploration.

**Balance sheet.** Debt is all fixed-rate at ~5.62% weighted average, D/E ~0.42, with ~$4.0B undrawn on revolvers and no commercial paper outstanding. Roughly $3.6B has been repaid since 2021. Post-Q1 redemptions of 2027/2028 notes ($425M) plus 2026 maturity repayments removed another ~$555M. Current ratio of 0.82 is the one weak signal, though normal for E&P. The deleveraging reduces refinancing risk materially versus prior cycles.

**Capital allocation shift.** The framework nominally returns ~60% of FCF via the $0.25/quarter dividend plus buybacks, but buybacks were essentially paused in Q1 2026 (zero shares) and only 2.8M shares repurchased in Q2 at ~$35.25. Since Q4 2021, APA has repurchased 98.2M shares with 21.9M remaining authorized. The pivot from buybacks to debt reduction is prudent de-risking, but it tempers the near-term shareholder-return story that a cheap E&P typically leans on.

## Growth Analysis

APA is not a volume-growth story. It is running fewer rigs, curtailing US gas, winding down the North Sea, and prioritizing capital efficiency and balance-sheet repair. Near-term production is roughly flat-to-modestly-declining, and the earnings trajectory is now being pulled down by the negative Waha gas dynamic.

The estimate-revision picture confirms deteriorating near-term momentum: current-quarter consensus is down 6.7% over 30 days, next-quarter down 21.6% over 30 days (and –13.4% over 90 days), current-year down 10.1% over 30 days (though still +9.8% over 90 days), and next-year down 8.3% over 30 days. The 90-day figures show the earlier oil-price recovery lifting estimates before the recent gas-driven markdown; the 30-day trend is uniformly negative. This is the opposite of the >+30% revision velocity that would flag a momentum buy — the momentum is running the wrong way in the immediate window.

The durable growth thesis is Suriname. GranMorgu de-risking is proceeding on the operator's schedule: TotalEnergies has awarded Halliburton integrated drilling/completions contracts, and Tenaris has opened a Suriname service center, corroborating a 2028 first-oil timeline. This is a multi-year option that materially changes the production and cash-flow profile late this decade, but it accrues to APA via working interest, not operatorship — timing and capital control sit with TotalEnergies. Alaska (2,700 b/d flow test, appraisal ongoing) is a smaller, earlier-stage add. The growth score reflects a company managing decline in mature assets while banking on a non-operated frontier project that is real but distant.

## Valuation Assessment

APA screens cheap on every conventional metric. Trailing and forward P/E are both ~8.2x on the reported figures, and consensus forward EPS of $5.79 (current year) implies ~6x. A third-party May 2026 note pegged forward P/E at ~6.7x and EV/EBITDA at ~3.6x with a $46.72 target. Against peer medians (P/E 19.2x, though the E&P peer set is heterogeneous — AR 11.1x, RRC 10.1x, OVV 19.2x, PR 22.4x, CHRD negative), APA sits at the low end even of the gas-weighted comparables.

A low multiple in this sector is anti-predictive as a standalone signal — E&P names trade cheap for structural reasons, and rewarding a compressed multiple without a catalyst is how value traps get bought. Here the discount reflects identifiable drags: negative Waha gas economics that turn US gas from an asset into a curtailed liability, the Gulf of America decommissioning tail, single-counterparty Egyptian exposure, a peer-lagging TSR (196.8 vs 295.1 peer group over the measurement period), and near-term estimate cuts. The market is discounting these, not mispricing the earnings power.

The offsetting fact is the Suriname option, which the trailing multiple does not capture, and the deleveraging that reduces balance-sheet risk. On a sum-of-parts basis a bull can argue the Permian cash engine plus Egypt gas plus a 2028 Suriname re-rating is worth well above the current ~$35. But that requires the frontier project to convert on schedule and the gas drag to normalize. We score valuation modestly above neutral: the price offers a reasonable margin of safety and real upside optionality, but the discount is largely earned, and the current estimate downtrend argues against treating consensus targets (mean $41.96, median $41.00, high $62.00) as a firm floor. We treat the mean/median as achievable if oil realizations hold and Suriname stays on track, not as a stale target that will be revised up imminently — estimates are being revised down currently.

## Competitive Landscape

APA competes as a mid-cap diversified independent against larger Permian-focused pure-plays. Its differentiation is geographic and optionality diversification rather than scale: the Permian cash engine plus Egypt gas growth plus Suriname deepwater upside gives it an offshore/international runway most Permian peers lack. The trade-off is exposure to sovereign risk (Egypt/EGPC counterparty, Middle East instability added as a new Q1 2026 risk factor), FX (Egyptian pound shortages), fiscal policy (UK EPL at 38%), and non-operated-partner dependence (Suriname timing controlled by TotalEnergies).

The competitive trajectory is mixed. Cost-per-boe reduction and portfolio high-grading (New Mexico Permian sold Q2 2025 for $571M against $282M carrying value; prior Central Basin/Shelf and mineral-rights sales; full Kinetik exit) show discipline and improving unit economics. But the North Sea run-off is a structural asset-base contraction, US gas is being curtailed rather than grown, and the cumulative TSR materially lagging the peer group indicates the market has not credited the diversification premium. We score competitive position at neutral: the moat is not eroding, but it is not visibly widening in a way the market rewards.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are specific and largely underappreciated relative to the sector's usual "cyclical commodity" framing, which is why we score this dimension below neutral.

1. **Negative Waha gas / curtailments (acute, company-specific).** US gas realized $1.02/Mcf in 2025. APA is curtailing ~137 MMcf/d gas plus 12,300 b/d NGL at Alpine High. The "gas marketing" gains are increasingly a purchased-volume arbitrage, a lower-quality and more volatile earnings source than production margin.

2. **Gulf of America decommissioning tail ($878M accrued at Q1 2026).** Legacy assets sold to Fieldwood, which went bankrupt twice; the successor (GOM Shelf) told BSEE it cannot fund obligations. Only ~$41M is expected to be reimbursed from security. This is a real, growing cash-flow liability with minimal offset and rising disclosure prominence.

3. **Egypt concentration and counterparty risk.** ~⅓ of production sells to EGPC, a single state entity with a history of FX-driven payment delays. The 2025 OCF beat was largely EGPC catch-up. Sinopec's minority interest leaks cash. Regional instability was added as a new risk factor in Q1 2026.

4. **North Sea decommissioning inflation.** UK EPL at 38% through 2030; a September 2025 OPRED "clear seabed" consultation could materially raise decommissioning obligations against a $2.9B total asset retirement obligation.

5. **Frontier execution risk.** Suriname, Alaska, and Uruguay are long-cycle, capital-intensive, and (for Suriname) non-operated. Uruguay faces injunctions against seismic/drilling.

6. **Commodity leverage.** A $1/bbl oil move ≈ $21M quarterly revenue; realized oil fell 14% to $66.92/bbl in 2025, though the Q2 supplemental showed firming to $93.20 US / $99.90 international.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 45.7% sits essentially on top of 1-year historical vol of 45.4% (fair, 0.2% spread), with IV at just the 9th percentile of the current chain — the options market is not pricing elevated fundamental stress despite the recent price weakness. Put/call skew is balanced (0.1%), and put/call volume (0.73) and OI (0.38) ratios are unremarkable. The term structure is in mild backwardation (near-term 45.7% > long-term 43.6%), pointing to some near-term event pricing (the August 6 earnings call), and the 6-month tenor shows a –4.7% put skew consistent with modest downside hedging into year-end. IV has compressed 8.7pp since the June snapshot (54.4% → 45.7%) as the stock drifted down 5.4%, indicating resolved uncertainty rather than building fear. Short interest of 11.7% of float (5.5 days to cover) reflects real institutional skepticism but is not at squeeze extremes. Net read: the options market is neutral-to-mildly-cautious, neither confirming a strong bull case nor flagging imminent collapse.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$47-62).** Cost-out execution continues, delivering the $450M annualized savings target and pushing unit costs lower. Oil realizations hold near the firmer Q2 levels ($93 US / $100 international), driving strong Permian and Egypt cash flow. Debt continues falling toward a level that reopens buybacks at accretive prices. Suriname GranMorgu stays on the operator's 2028 first-oil schedule, and the market begins to credit the option in 2026-2027, re-rating APA off its ~6-7x forward multiple toward the peer median. The Waha gas drag proves transitory as Permian gas takeaway expands. Analyst high target $62 captures this scenario.

**Bear case (~$24-28).** Oil rolls back toward the low $60s (a Hormuz de-escalation that reopens Gulf transit pressures crude), while Waha gas stays negative and curtailments deepen. The Gulf of America decommissioning liability accretes toward the top of its $1.2B range with no reimbursement, consuming cash. Egyptian payment delays recur, and the one-time 2025 OCF strength is exposed as unrepeatable. Estimate cuts continue, and the peer-lagging TSR persists as the diversification discount widens. A ~25-30% drawdown from current levels is plausible on a combined oil and gas downdraft.

**Base case (~$41).** Oil holds in a middling $75-90 band, cost savings land near target, debt reduction continues, and Suriname stays on schedule without yet being fully credited. The dividend (~2.9%) plus incremental deleveraging provides the return floor, and the stock drifts toward the consensus mean/median as the frontier option gains visibility. This is roughly consensus and implies ~19% upside to the mean target over 12 months, weighted against a genuine ~25% downside if the gas and oil dynamics turn.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The August 6, 2026 earnings call is the near-term checkpoint — watch for gas-curtailment trajectory, the pace of estimate revisions post-print, and any update on the Gulf of America contingency. Suriname operator milestones through 2027 are the longer-horizon driver toward 2028 first oil.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Consensus current-year and next-quarter EPS estimates turn positive over a 30-day window (reversing the current –10% / –22% cuts), confirming the earnings trough.
- Structural resolution of the Waha gas drag — Permian takeaway expansion or sustained positive realizations that end active curtailment.
- Buybacks resume meaningfully (>$100M/quarter) alongside continued debt reduction, signaling the balance-sheet-repair phase is substantially complete.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Gulf of America decommissioning accrual steps materially toward or beyond the $1.2B top of range with no offsetting reimbursement.
- Egyptian receivable balances deteriorate or a new payment-delay cycle begins, threatening the OCF base.
- Suriname first-oil timeline slips beyond 2028 or capex materially escalates under TotalEnergies operatorship.
- Oil realizations break sustainably below ~$60/bbl while gas stays negative.

## Conclusion

APA offers a coherent self-help story — real cost reduction, aggressive deleveraging, portfolio high-grading, and a genuine long-dated re-rating catalyst in Suriname — trading at a mid-single-digit forward multiple. That combination keeps the stock interesting and supports the improving trajectory in its financial-health and risk scores over recent reports. But the discount is largely earned rather than a mispricing: negative Waha gas realizations are forcing curtailment, the decommissioning tail is growing with minimal offset, a third of production depends on a single Egyptian counterparty, the 2025 cash-flow strength was substantially non-recurring, and near-term estimates are being revised down across every tenor. The four-quarter beat streak reflects conservative guidance against price tailwinds.

We rate APA a HOLD. The valuation and the Suriname option argue for patience, and the dividend plus deleveraging provides a return floor, but the falling-estimate trend and the specific tail liabilities prevent us from underwriting asymmetric upside at ~$35. The next earnings print and the estimate-revision direction that follows it are the swing factors. We would turn constructive on a confirmed positive revision turn or a structural fix to the gas drag; we would exit on a decommissioning-liability escalation, an Egyptian payment cycle, or a Suriname timeline slip.
