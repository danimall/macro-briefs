## Executive Summary

Envista Holdings is a $4.3B dental products company executing a credible turnaround after two devastating years of goodwill impairments ($1.4B combined in FY2023-2024). FY2025 delivered 8.3% reported revenue growth, $231M in free cash flow at 114% conversion, and four consecutive quarterly earnings beats averaging +13.4% surprise. The balance sheet has improved materially: net debt-to-EBITDA sits near 0.8x, interest expense declined 41% over two years, and management retired $116M of convertible notes at maturity while repurchasing $166M in stock at an average price of ~$18/share.

The central question is whether the recovery trajectory justifies the current price of ~$26.80, approximately 19x FY2026E adjusted EPS of $1.41. This multiple sits below the healthcare supplies peer median of 32.9x and below direct competitor Merit Medical at 32.9x, but Envista's profitability profile (adjusted EBITDA margin of 13.7% vs. peer levels typically in the 20s) and the quality of FY2025's growth (roughly half of the 6.5% core growth came from a non-recurring deferred revenue timing benefit in the aligner business) make the discount partially justified. The 2026 guidance of 2-4% core revenue growth and 7-13% EBITDA growth acknowledges this normalization.

The stock is neither cheap enough to be compelling nor expensive enough to avoid. Earnings momentum is real, the balance sheet trajectory is favorable, and zero sell-side analysts rate this a sell. But $3B in residual goodwill (53% of assets), upcoming China VBP rounds in both implants and orthodontics, tariff-driven margin pressure, and a deceleration to low-single-digit organic growth create enough execution risk that paying a premium to current levels requires more evidence of sustained margin expansion.

## Company Overview

Envista was spun out of Danaher Corporation in September 2019, inheriting a portfolio of over 30 dental brands organized into two segments. **Specialty Products & Technologies** (64% of FY2025 revenue, $1.75B) houses the premium Nobel Biocare implant system, the value-tier Alpha-Bio and Implant Direct implant lines, and the Ormco orthodontics business including the Spark clear aligner platform. **Equipment & Consumables** (36%, $967M) encompasses DEXIS imaging systems, Kerr restorative materials, and Metrex infection prevention products.

Key structural features: 85% of revenue derives from consumables, services, and spare parts (high recurring revenue); 53% of sales are international; and Henry Schein distributes approximately 12% of total sales with no master distribution agreement in place. The company employs the "Envista Business System" (EBS), a lean methodology inherited from Danaher, as its operational improvement framework.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Growth

Revenue recovered to $2.72B in FY2025 from a trough of $2.51B in FY2024, with reported core sales growth of 6.5%. The composition of that growth is critical: management disclosed a 4.5% period-over-period benefit from "timing of deferred revenue recognition related to clear aligner treatment plans." Stripping this out, underlying organic growth from volume and pricing was approximately 2%. Q4 2025 showed strong acceleration to 10.8% core growth, but this was against favorable year-ago comparisons and further aided by the aligner timing effect.

Management's 2026 guidance of 2-4% core revenue growth implicitly acknowledges the step-down as the deferred revenue tailwind normalizes. Consensus estimates reflect this: FY2026E revenue of approximately $2.78B implies ~2% growth, with FY2027E estimates around $2.90B (~4%).

### Profitability

Adjusted EBITDA margin reached 13.7% in FY2025, up from trough levels, with Q4 exiting at 14.8%. The 2026 guide for 7-13% EBITDA growth on 2-4% revenue growth implies continued margin expansion toward 15%+. SG&A declined from 46.1% to 42.5% of sales, reflecting $35M+ in G&A reductions. R&D spending increased 15% to $114M (4.2% of sales), a positive reinvestment signal.

Gross margins of 54.7% are stable but below the FY2023 level of 56.1%, partly reflecting tariff-driven input cost increases and China VBP pricing pressure. GAAP profitability remains compressed by a 73.5% effective tax rate in FY2025 due to a one-time subsidiary restructuring charge; management expects this to normalize toward 20-25%, creating a meaningful earnings uplift already embedded in the $1.35-$1.45 adjusted EPS guidance.

### Cash Flow and Balance Sheet

Free cash flow of $231M (114% FCF conversion) on modest CapEx of $45M demonstrates a capital-light business model generating real cash. Operating cash flow of $276M was down from $337M in FY2024 due to working capital timing, not structural deterioration.

Total debt of approximately $1.5B (2028 Term Loans, 2028 Convertible Notes, and revolver) against cash of $1.21B puts net debt near $300M. Interest expense declined from $63M (FY2023) to $37M (FY2025). The revolver has $632.5M available. The 2025 Convertible Notes ($116.3M) were retired at maturity, simplifying the capital structure.

The critical balance sheet overhang: goodwill and intangible assets of $2.99B represent 53% of total assets, even after $1.4B in impairments. The FY2025 annual test showed fair values exceeding carrying values, but the assumptions (revenue growth, operating margins, discount rates) are sensitive to any deterioration in the recovery trajectory.

### Earnings Quality

Four consecutive quarterly beats averaging 13.4% surprise is a strong pattern. Consensus estimates have been revised upward by 10.5% over the past 90 days for the current year, though the 30-day revision trend is essentially flat (+1.0% for the current quarter, -0.0% for the full year). This suggests estimates have caught up to the improved run rate and further upside surprises may moderate.

## Growth Analysis

Envista's growth profile is bifurcated. The structural tailwinds — aging populations, emerging market underpenetration, dental digitization, and DSO consolidation — provide a favorable backdrop for low-to-mid single-digit industry growth. Envista's emerging market franchise ($586M, 22% of sales, built from under $30M since 2011) is a genuine competitive asset.

Against this, several vectors create growth headwinds. China's volume-based procurement (VBP) for dental implants (Round 2 expected Q2 2026) and orthodontics (first round expected H2 2026) will compress pricing in a market representing ~7% of sales. The deferred revenue timing tailwind in aligners is non-recurring. Tariff escalation on global trade routes affects both input costs and demand for discretionary dental procedures.

Spark clear aligners (1 million cases shipped) represent the highest-growth opportunity but remain a distant second to Align Technology's Invisalign. Nobel Biocare's premium implant franchise has stabilized after share losses in prior years. The Equipment & Consumables segment faces the most pressure from economic cyclicality in dental capital equipment spending.

The 2026 guide of 2-4% core growth and 7-13% EBITDA growth reflects a realistic assessment: modest topline with continued margin expansion from operational improvement and cost reductions.

## Valuation Assessment

At $26.80, NVST trades at approximately 19x FY2026E adjusted EPS of $1.41, and roughly 96x trailing GAAP EPS (distorted by the 73.5% tax rate). The forward multiple sits well below the healthcare supplies peer median of 32.9x (Merit Medical) and below Lantheus at 24.6x, but above Haemonetics at 16.5x. Direct competitor Dentsply Sirona trades at a negative P/E due to its own restructuring challenges.

The discount to peers is partially justified by lower profitability and residual goodwill risk. However, the magnitude of the discount — roughly 40% below the peer median — appears to overstate these differences for a company with 85% recurring revenue, 114% FCF conversion, and declining leverage.

On an EV/EBITDA basis, the stock trades at approximately 11-12x FY2026E EBITDA, which is reasonable for a healthcare supplies business in recovery mode. A DCF framework using 2-4% revenue growth, gradual margin expansion to 16-17% EBITDA margin, and a 10% WACC yields a fair value range of $25-30, suggesting the stock is near fair value with the current assumptions.

The analyst consensus median target of $29.00 (8% upside) and mean of $28.69 corroborate the view that the stock is approximately fairly valued, with the range of $21-$35 reflecting the wide distribution of potential outcomes.

## Competitive Landscape

Envista operates as a #2 or #3 player across most of its categories. In implants, Straumann dominates the premium tier while Envista's Nobel Biocare competes on clinical heritage and an increasingly digitized workflow (DTX Studio). In clear aligners, Spark is gaining traction but remains a fraction of Invisalign's installed base and brand recognition. In imaging and consumables, Dentsply Sirona is the primary competitor, though it faces its own operational challenges.

Nobel Biocare appears to have stabilized after several years of share erosion. Spark's growth is encouraging but the clear aligner market is increasingly competitive, with Align and numerous other entrants all active. The breadth of Envista's portfolio is an advantage as DSOs consolidate and prefer to source from fewer suppliers, but this advantage depends on maintaining product quality and innovation velocity across 30+ brands.

The EBS operational framework provides a structural advantage in cost management, evidenced by the $35M+ in G&A reductions achieved in FY2025. Whether this translates into sustained competitive advantage or merely catches Envista up to peers remains to be demonstrated.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill/Intangible Impairment ($3B, 53% of assets):** The single largest risk. Two consecutive years of impairments totaling $1.4B demonstrate that the carrying values are sensitive to growth and margin assumptions. While FY2025 showed no impairment and fair values exceeded carrying values, any reversal in the recovery trajectory — particularly if 2026 core growth comes in below the 2-4% guide — could trigger additional charges.

**China VBP Pricing Compression:** Two VBP rounds expected in 2026 (implants Q2, orthodontics H2) will create temporary demand disruption (destocking) and permanent price reductions in a market that represents ~7% of sales. Management has some experience managing VBP transitions from Round 1 but the cumulative effect on margins is negative.

**Deferred Revenue Accounting Risk:** The Spark aligner revenue recognition involves estimating future refinement aligner usage rates. Changes in assumptions could shift revenue between periods. The 4.5% FY2025 benefit from this timing makes the reported growth profile fragile.

**Henry Schein Concentration (12% of sales, no contract):** A disruption in this distribution relationship would have immediate material impact. The absence of a master agreement gives Henry Schein significant leverage.

**Tariff Exposure:** With manufacturing operations across the U.S., Europe, and Asia, and 53% international sales, tariff escalation creates direct cost pressure and indirect demand pressure through economic uncertainty.

**Convertible Notes ($493M due 2028):** If conversion is triggered, dilution risk is real. The cash settlement requirement for principal reduces, but does not eliminate, shareholder dilution.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($32-35, +19% to +31%)

Spark aligner adoption accelerates, Nobel Biocare regains share in premium implants, and EBS-driven cost improvements push EBITDA margins toward 17-18%. Tax rate normalization to 22% on $1.55-$1.65 adjusted EPS at 20-22x forward P/E supports $32-35. China VBP impact proves manageable. Remaining buyback authorization provides price support. Continued earnings beats re-rate the multiple toward peer levels.

### Bear Case ($20-22, -18% to -25%)

Core growth disappoints at 0-1% as China VBP disrupts demand, tariff costs compress margins, and the aligner revenue tailwind proves fully non-recurring. Consumer spending weakness reduces demand for elective dental procedures. EBITDA margins stall at 14-15%. The forward multiple compresses to 15-16x on $1.30 adjusted EPS, yielding $20-21. Goodwill impairment risk re-emerges, creating headline risk even if non-cash.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)

1. **Two consecutive quarters of core revenue growth ≥4% excluding deferred revenue timing effects**, confirming underlying demand acceleration rather than accounting flattery.
2. **Adjusted EBITDA margin reaching 16%+ on a trailing four-quarter basis**, demonstrating that EBS-driven cost improvements translate into durable profitability improvement.
3. **Stock price pullback to $22-23** (approximately 16x FY2026E EPS), creating a more favorable risk/reward ratio with the same recovery thesis intact.

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)

1. **Core revenue growth below 1% for two consecutive quarters**, indicating the recovery has stalled and goodwill impairment risk is re-emerging.
2. **Management cuts FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance below $1.25**, signaling margin expansion is not materializing.
3. **Goodwill impairment charge in FY2026**, which would confirm the recovery thesis has failed and the balance sheet overhang is worsening.

### Reassessment Timeline

Next quarterly earnings (likely late April/early May 2026) is the primary checkpoint. The Q2 2026 China VBP implant round and H2 2026 orthodontics VBP are secondary catalysts that will test the company's ability to manage pricing pressure.

## Conclusion

Envista's operational recovery is real: the earnings beats, margin improvement, balance sheet deleveraging, and management's willingness to buy back stock aggressively at lower prices all confirm genuine progress. The valuation at ~19x forward earnings reflects this recovery but does not overpay for it, trading at a reasonable discount to healthcare supplies peers that is partially justified by lower profitability and residual goodwill risk.

The evidence does not support an active avoidance recommendation. Earnings momentum is positive, estimates are rising, and leverage is declining. The risk/reward from current levels is roughly balanced (19-31% upside to bull case vs. 18-25% downside to bear case). However, the evidence also does not compel a buy: 2026 core growth of 2-4% is modest, the deferred revenue flattery in FY2025 makes the growth trajectory appear stronger than it is, and $3B in goodwill on a $4.3B market cap business creates asymmetric downside risk not fully offset by the upside potential.

HOLD is the appropriate recommendation. The stock is roughly fairly valued at $26-29. The next two to three quarters of data — particularly whether margin expansion sustains through China VBP headwinds and tariff uncertainty — will determine whether the recovery thesis deserves a higher multiple or whether execution risk argues for a lower entry point.
