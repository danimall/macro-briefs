## Executive Summary

BlackRock is the world's largest publicly traded asset manager with $14.0 trillion in AUM as of December 2025, up 22% year-over-year. The company completed a series of acquisitions in 2024-2025 (GIP, HPS, Preqin, ElmTree), building a $323B private markets platform shifting its revenue mix toward higher-fee products. On an as-adjusted basis, 2025 operating income grew 18% to $9.6B, EPS grew 10% to $48.09, and the company generated $7.5B in operating cash flow. Record net inflows of $698B demonstrate organic growth remains intact.

The stock has drawn down 21% from its October 2025 high of $1,220, driven by the HPS Corporate Lending Fund redemption gating event in March 2026 (9.3% of shares requested vs. 5% quarterly cap), broader market weakness from the Iran/Hormuz crisis, and falling EPS estimates. At $959, the stock trades at approximately 18x 2026 as-adjusted EPS ($53.63 consensus) and 15.6x next-year estimates ($61.45). This represents a meaningful discount to the peer median P/E of 26.1x and sits 17% below the lowest analyst price target of $1,150.

The specific risk preventing a higher-conviction rating is the HPS fund gating, which directly challenges BlackRock's strategy of distributing private credit to retail/wealth channels. If redemption pressure persists for two or more quarters, it could impair the company's $400B private markets fundraising target and create mark-to-market contagion across the broader $2.8T private credit market. This risk is quantifiable and time-bounded: Q2 2026 redemption data (expected July) will either confirm or defuse it.

## Company Overview

BlackRock operates across five interconnected business lines: (1) ETFs/iShares ($5.5T, the global market leader), (2) institutional index management ($3.7T), (3) active management ($2.5T institutional, $1.3T retail), (4) alternatives/private markets ($424B direct + $252B via GIP/HPS), and (5) financial technology (Aladdin/Preqin, $2.0B in revenue). The firm employs approximately 24,900 people across 30+ countries.

The 2024-2025 acquisition spree expanded BlackRock's capabilities. GIP (infrastructure, closed October 2024) added marquee infrastructure assets. HPS (private credit, closed July 2025) brought $116B in private credit AUM and established BlackRock as a top-5 private credit manager. Preqin (data analytics, closed March 2025) provides private markets data to 200,000+ users. ElmTree (real estate, closed September 2025) added net-lease real estate. Together, these acquisitions shifted private markets from 7% to 12% of base fees while representing only 2% of average AUM, reflecting higher fee rates (roughly 6x the blended average).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** Total revenue grew 19% to $24.2B in 2025. Base advisory fees and securities lending revenue increased 19% to $19.2B, driven by 9% organic base fee growth plus market appreciation and acquired AUM. Technology services revenue grew 24% to $2.0B, including approximately $210M from Preqin. Performance fees of $1.4B (+18%) were increasingly sourced from alternatives ($1.25B from alternatives alone).

**Profitability:** The GAAP/as-adjusted divergence requires careful parsing. GAAP operating margin contracted from 37.1% to 29.1% due to $2.6B in acquisition-related charges, including $775M intangible amortization, $738M acquisition-related compensation, $720M contingent consideration fair value changes, and $122M transaction costs. On an as-adjusted basis, operating margin was essentially flat at 44.1% (vs. 44.5%), and operating income grew 18% to $9.6B. As-adjusted EPS of $48.09 grew 10% year-over-year.

**Balance Sheet:** Total GAAP assets of $170B are inflated by $68B in separate account assets (pass-through, no balance sheet risk) and consolidated investment products. Goodwill and intangibles of $63.3B (up from $46.7B) reflect the acquisition activity. Long-term debt of $12.8B against $16.9B total liquidity and $7.5B annual operating cash flow yields a net leverage ratio below 1x by the company's credit facility covenants. The Altman Z-Score of 1.05 is structurally inapplicable to fee-based asset managers; BlackRock's actual credit risk is investment-grade.

The $8.4B contingent consideration liability (GIP: $4.8B, HPS: $3.5B) is the most consequential balance sheet item. Settlement in stock creates potential dilution of up to 13.8M shares (9% of current count) if maximum performance milestones are achieved. The $720M noncash fair value increase in 2025 will generate ongoing GAAP earnings volatility.

**Cash Flow & Capital Return:** Operating cash flow (excluding consolidated investment products) was $7.5B. The company returned $5.3B to shareholders through $3.3B in dividends ($20.84/share, 2.4% yield) and $2.0B in repurchases. The January 2026 authorization of 7M additional shares for repurchase signals management's confidence in the stock.

**Earnings Surprises:** Four consecutive quarterly beats: +10.3%, +10.3%, +1.7%, +6.7%. This pattern supports confidence in consensus estimates, though the narrowing beat magnitude in Q3 2025 (1.7%) warrants monitoring.

## Growth Analysis

BlackRock's growth operates on three axes:

**Organic flows:** $698B in 2025 net inflows (5% organic asset growth rate) is a record. ETF inflows of $527B drove the majority, but active inflows of $136B (up from $62B) signal improving traction in higher-fee products. The secular shift to passive investing and ETF wrapper adoption (fixed income ETFs, model portfolios, active ETFs) provides a multi-year tailwind.

**Private markets expansion:** The pivot to alternatives is the primary growth thesis. Private markets base fees grew from 7% to 12% of total in one year, at roughly 6x the average fee rate. BlackRock's target of $400B in private markets AUM by 2030 (from $323B currently) implies a ~4% CAGR, which appears conservative absent HPS fund gating contagion. The GIP/EQT consortium's $10.7B bid for AES Corp demonstrates active deployment.

**Technology:** Aladdin + Preqin revenue grew 24% to $2.0B with 16% organic ACV growth. This revenue is recurring, high-margin, and serves as the foundation for whole-portfolio analytics spanning public and private markets. The Preqin integration creates a reinforcing loop: private markets data → better analytics → more private markets allocations → more assets flowing through BlackRock.

**Estimate revisions are negative but shallow.** Current quarter estimates fell 2.4% over 30 days; current year estimates fell 1.4%. This reflects market-level AUM pressure (equities declining in Q1 2026) rather than fundamental business deterioration. Next-year estimates are essentially flat (-0.8% over 30 days, +0.2% over 90 days), suggesting analysts view current weakness as transitory.

- 20.0x trailing as-adjusted EPS ($48.09)
- 17.9x forward consensus EPS for 2026 ($53.63)
- 15.6x forward consensus EPS for 2027 ($61.45)
- 2.7x book value ($360/share)
- 2.4% dividend yield

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E of 26.1x (led by KKR at 54.5x and BX at 39.8x) puts BLK at a discount on a forward basis. BNY Mellon at 15.7x and Ameriprise at 13.5x are more traditional asset managers with lower growth profiles. BlackRock's combination of scale, technology revenue, and private markets expansion justifies a premium to custody banks but not to pure-play alternative managers with higher growth rates and performance fee optionality.

**Analyst targets:** Consensus median of $1,300, low of $1,150, high of $1,368. Even the low target implies 20% upside from current levels. The 15/17 Buy/Strong Buy ratings (with 0 Sells) reflect unanimous sell-side constructiveness, though sentiment has degraded marginally (Hold count rose from 3 to 4 over the past 2 months).

**Historical context:** BLK's 52-week range is $774-$1,220. The current $959 price sits at the 38th percentile of this range. The stock is 21% below its October high, a pullback driven by identifiable factors (HPS gating, geopolitical risk, estimate revisions) rather than fundamental deterioration.

At 18x forward earnings with 15% expected EPS growth (from $48 adjusted to $61 over two years), the PEG ratio is approximately 1.2x — reasonable for a business of this quality and scale.

## Competitive Landscape

No competitor simultaneously leads in ETFs, operates a dominant financial technology platform, and ranks among the top-5 alternative asset managers. Specific competitive dynamics:

**ETFs/Index:** Vanguard and State Street are the primary competitors. BlackRock's iShares franchise commands roughly 35% of the global ETF market. Fee compression continues, but scale advantages (lower marginal costs, better securities lending revenue, wider product range) create sustainable margins. The fixed income ETF category and active ETF wrapper are growth areas where BlackRock has first-mover advantages.

**Alternatives:** The GIP/HPS/ElmTree acquisitions position BlackRock to compete with Blackstone, Apollo, KKR, and Carlyle. The differentiation is BlackRock's ability to distribute alternatives through its existing wealth/retail channels and integrate private markets data (Preqin) into its analytics platform (Aladdin). This cross-selling capability is unproven at scale.

**Technology:** Aladdin's installed base of $21.6T+ in monitored assets creates switching costs comparable to enterprise software platforms. The integration of Preqin data into Aladdin for whole-portfolio public-private analytics is a competitive advantage no rival can replicate without their own data asset.

**Moat assessment:** The combination of scale (cost advantage in passive), technology (switching costs), and distribution (network effects across institutional, wealth, and retail channels) creates a durable competitive advantage. The primary risk to this advantage is fee compression eroding the economic value of scale in index products.

## Risk Assessment

**HPS Fund Gating (HIGH, NEAR-TERM):** The 9.3% redemption request vs. 5% quarterly cap is the most immediate risk. If Q2 2026 redemption requests remain elevated, it would (a) impair BlackRock's private credit fundraising narrative, (b) create mark-to-market pressure on the $26B fund, and (c) raise broader questions about liquidity mismatch in private credit vehicles offered to retail investors. This aligns with the world model's credit cascade thesis (60-68% probability, 14 data points) and Jamie Dimon's warning that private credit losses will be "larger than feared."

**Market-Dependent Revenue (STRUCTURAL):** A 10% decline in global equity markets would reduce AUM by approximately $780B (equity AUM of $7.8T) and base fees by roughly $850M (applying blended equity fee rates), equal to approximately 4.5% of total revenue. In the current stagflationary environment with 40-45% recession probability, this is a live risk.

**Contingent Consideration Dilution (MEDIUM):** Maximum potential dilution of 9% over the next 3-5 years as GIP and HPS earn-outs settle. The $720M fair value charge in 2025 will recur with stock price movements, creating ongoing GAAP noise.

**Integration Execution (MEDIUM):** Four acquisitions in 12 months is aggressive. Key person risk across GIP (Adebayo Ogunlesi) and HPS (Scott Kapnick) leadership. Cultural integration across infrastructure, private credit, data analytics, and real estate platforms with different compensation structures and investment philosophies.

**Geopolitical/Macro:** Oil at $107, dual-chokepoint disruption, and stagflation create headwinds for asset prices and client risk appetite. However, volatility can be a net positive for BlackRock: (a) cash management AUM grows in flight-to-quality environments, (b) Aladdin usage increases when clients need risk analytics, and (c) ETF trading volumes surge, benefiting iShares market share.

**ESG Political Risk (DECLINING):** The Texas court striking down anti-ESG blacklisting is a positive precedent. This risk has been overpriced; the actual financial impact has been minimal (state pension mandates lost were small relative to $14T AUM).

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
BlackRock at $959 represents a high-quality franchise at a cyclical discount. The 18x forward as-adjusted P/E (2026) and 15.6x next-year earnings price in substantially more risk than the fundamentals warrant. Four consecutive earnings beats averaging 7.3% suggest consensus is achievable or beatable. The private markets transformation, taking base fee share from 7% to 12% in one year, is the most significant revenue mix shift in BlackRock's history and is early in flowing through to earnings. Technology revenue growing 24% at scale provides a recurring, high-margin earnings stream increasingly independent of market levels. The stock sits below the lowest analyst target ($1,150) by 17%, unusual for a company of this quality. Share repurchases at these levels (7M share authorization) are accretive. M2 expansion (+4.9% YoY) with a 3-6 month lag should support asset prices and AUM growth in H2 2026.

### Bear Case
The HPS fund gating could mark the start of broader private credit distress. If the credit cascade materializes (60-68% probability per the world model), BlackRock's $323B private markets platform faces write-downs, redemption pressure, and reputational damage precisely when the company is trying to scale these products to retail investors. The $8.4B contingent consideration creates up to 9% dilution. Stagflation (core PCE 3.1%, oil $107) simultaneously compresses equity valuations (reducing market-dependent AUM) and prevents rate cuts that would support fixed income flows. The 21% drawdown from highs could extend to 30-35% in a recession scenario, taking the stock to $850-$900 — roughly where it bottomed in April 2025. Estimate revisions are negative across all periods.

## Conclusion

BlackRock merits a BUY at $959. The specific risk that could prevent appreciation — HPS fund gating spreading to broader private credit distress — is quantifiable and time-bounded. Q2 2026 redemption data (July reporting) will either confirm or defuse this risk. The probability-weighted outcome favors buying: in the base case (no private credit contagion, moderate market recovery), the stock re-rates toward analyst consensus of $1,290 (34% upside). In the bear case (credit cascade + recession), the April 2025 low of $774 provides a reference for maximum downside (20% from current), creating approximately 1.7:1 upside/downside asymmetry on the base case alone.

The business is high-quality: $14T AUM, 44% adjusted margins, $7.5B operating cash flow, four consecutive earnings beats, and a competitive position no peer can replicate across ETFs, active management, alternatives, and financial technology. The negative estimate revisions (-1.4% for current year) are shallow relative to the 10% as-adjusted EPS growth delivered in 2025 and reflect market-level AUM pressure rather than structural deterioration. At 18x forward as-adjusted earnings with 15% expected two-year EPS growth, the valuation is not demanding for this growth profile.

Position sizing should reflect the elevated macro uncertainty (stagflation, credit cascade risk, Hormuz binary event) and BlackRock's direct exposure to asset prices. A standard rather than overweight allocation is appropriate, with the option to add on further weakness toward $900 or on confirmation that HPS redemption pressure is subsiding.
