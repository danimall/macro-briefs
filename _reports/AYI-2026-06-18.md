**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Acuity Inc. is an industrial-technology company that has spent the last two years repositioning from a commoditizing North American LED-luminaire manufacturer toward a higher-margin "intelligent spaces" platform built on building controls, spatial-intelligence software, and a full-stack audio/video/control system (Q-SYS). The investment case turns on a single structural fact: the company runs at two speeds. Acuity Intelligent Spaces (AIS) grew sales 44.7% in fiscal Q2 2026 with operating margin nearly doubling to 11.4%, while Acuity Brands Lighting (ABL), still 83% of FY2025 revenue, declined 2.8% in the same quarter and is now guided to flat-to-down low-single-digits for the year. The bet is whether AIS can grow into enough of the revenue mix to offset a stagnant lighting base before the lighting drag swamps the mix benefit.

The financial profile is genuinely strong. Free cash flow was $533M in FY2025 (12.3% FCF margin), the balance sheet carries modest net leverage after the company repaid two-thirds of the $600M term loan used to fund the $1.2B QSC acquisition within roughly 14 months, and the Altman Z-score of 3.12 sits comfortably in the safe zone. H1 FY2026 diluted EPS rose 19.1% to $6.91, supported by both genuine operating improvement and a share count that has been ground down from ~34.6M (FY2022) to ~30.3M. That last point matters: net income actually fell 6.2% in FY2025, and a meaningful slice of the EPS growth narrative is buyback-driven.

We rate AYI a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The AIS inflection is real and the balance sheet is clean, but two facts keep us from a BUY: consensus estimates are stable-to-falling (current-year EPS revised down 1.3% over 90 days, next-year down 1.9%), and there is no dated near-term catalyst that resolves the core-lighting weakness. At ~22.4x trailing earnings against a consensus that is no longer rising, the asymmetry is balanced rather than favorable. We would upgrade on either an ABL demand stabilization confirming organic growth has bottomed, or a re-acceleration of net estimate revisions.

## Company Overview

Acuity (renamed from Acuity Brands, Inc. effective March 2025) operates two segments:

- **Acuity Brands Lighting (ABL)** — the legacy core: luminaires plus advanced electronics including drivers, the nLight controls platform, and sensors. Brands include Lithonia, Holophane, Gotham, Juno, eldoLED, nLight, and SensorSwitch. ABL was roughly $3.6B of FY2025 revenue (83% of sales) sold through independent agencies, distributors, retail, direct corporate accounts, and OEMs.
- **Acuity Intelligent Spaces (AIS)** — the growth engine: Atrius (data/spatial-intelligence software), Distech Controls (building-management/HVAC controls), and Q-SYS/QSC (full-stack AV and control). AIS was ~$764M of FY2025 revenue (17%) sold through system integrators into airports, universities, sports venues, hospitality, and themed entertainment.

The manufacturing footprint is concentrated in Mexico: 55% of finished products are made there across seven facilities, several under Maquiladora status, with roughly 8,200 of ~13,800 employees in Mexico. This is simultaneously a cost advantage (duty-free under Maquiladora) and a structural exposure to USMCA, tariffs, and Mexican labor dynamics. Competitive differentiation rests on the North American lighting brand/channel position, the nLight controls platform, ~1,700 patent assets, and the newer software/AV stack that pushes the company up the value chain into stickier, higher-margin revenue.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory (fiscal year ending August 31):** FY2022 $4.01B → FY2023 $3.95B → FY2024 $3.84B → FY2025 $4.35B (+13.1%). The headline FY2025 acceleration is almost entirely inorganic. QSC contributed $428.6M of the $504.6M revenue increase. The core ABL business grew just 1.1%, with corporate accounts and retail declining within that.

**Margins.** FY2025 gross margin expanded to 47.8% from 46.4% on the higher-margin AIS mix, but operating margin compressed to 13.0% from 14.4%, driven by $29.7M of special charges (impairments and severance), higher SD&A from QSC integration, and intangible amortization. Net income fell 6.2% to $396.6M, and diluted EPS declined to $12.53 from $13.44, hit by a one-time $30.9M non-cash pension settlement charge in Q4 FY2025.

**FY2026 first-half inflection.** H1 revenue was $2.20B (+12.3%), H1 net income $217.3M (+18.0%), and H1 diluted EPS $6.91 (+19.1%). H1 operating margin recovered to 13.3% from 12.4%, and Q2 gross margin reached 49.3%. The segment split is the story: AIS is now ~23% of revenue with Q2 sales +44.7% and operating margin up to 11.4% from 5.8%; H1 AIS operating profit rose 215%. ABL Q2 sales fell 2.8% (H1 −0.8%), with management citing project business that did not recur and taking $5.9M of severance/productivity charges in the segment.

**Earnings quality and surprise track record.** Four consecutive beats: +14.5% (Q ending Jun-25), +6.4% (Sep-25), +1.2% (Dec-25), +2.5% (Mar-26). The beats are getting smaller as the easy comparisons fade. EPS growth in H1 FY2026 is supported by buybacks ($103M in H1) in addition to operating improvement, so the "double-digit EPS" framing carries a share-count tailwind.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $601.4M; H1 FY2026 OCF rose to $229.9M from $191.6M. The company funded the QSC deal with cash plus the term loan and has aggressively de-levered — $200M repaid in FY2025 and another $200M in H1 FY2026, leaving $200M on the term loan plus $500M of 2.15% notes due 2030. Total debt fell to $697.1M at 2/28/2026 from $896.8M at 8/31/2025. Current ratio 1.95, D/E 0.75, ROE 14.6%, ROA 8.3%. This is a financially healthy company with high-quality cash conversion, which supports a financial-health score of 7.5.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is concentrated in AIS, the only part of the business compounding. Distech is growing organically alongside the acquired Q-SYS platform, and the migration toward bundled hardware-plus-controls-plus-cloud-software revenue is a genuine mix-shift toward stickier, higher-margin sales.

The constraint is arithmetic. ABL is the overwhelming majority of revenue and is guided flat-to-down LSD. For total-company growth to remain robust, AIS has to grow fast enough and large enough to overwhelm a stagnant-to-shrinking lighting base. At 23% of revenue growing ~40%+, AIS contributes roughly 9-10 points of revenue growth before the ABL drag; if ABL declines 1-2%, the lighting base subtracts ~1 point on its larger weight. The net is solid but decelerating, and it depends entirely on AIS momentum holding.

The estimate-revision data argues against treating this as an accelerating inflection. Consensus current-year EPS was revised down 1.3% over 90 days (to $19.52), next-year down 1.9% (to $21.28), and current-quarter and next-quarter estimates were each cut 2.5%. Over the most recent 30 days, estimates are flat — no further deterioration, but no re-acceleration either. This differs from a strong upward-revision trend that would signal a true earnings inflection; here, the strong AIS narrative has not been enough to push consensus higher because analysts are simultaneously marking down the lighting outlook. The growth picture is durable but mid-single-to-high-single-digit, not the kind of compounding that warrants a premium re-rating. We score growth prospects at 6.0, holding the level from the prior report.

## Valuation Assessment

At $317.62, AYI trades at ~22.4x trailing EPS and roughly 16.3x the current-year consensus. The forward multiple on its face looks undemanding for a business with a 40%-plus-growing software/AV segment and high-teens ROIC. The problem is what the forward multiple is built on: estimates that are flat-to-falling rather than rising.

The peer set is not especially useful as a direct anchor. The Electrical Components & Equipment comparables — POWL (56.7x), ENS (29.0x), VICR (99.7x), ST (154.0x), MRCY (negative) — carry a median P/E of 99.7x, distorted by trough or near-trough earnings at several names. Acuity's ~22x is the cheapest in the group, but that reflects a more mature, slower-growing core (lighting) than the higher-multiple specialty names rather than a clean mispricing. The honest read is that Acuity is reasonably valued for a business whose largest segment is stagnant and whose growth segment, while excellent, is not yet large enough to define the consolidated multiple.

Analyst targets span $295 low to $435 high, with a $352.5 mean and $342.5 median — roughly 8-11% above the current price at the midpoint. On a normal high-momentum inflection name I would treat consensus targets as a floor, but the guardrail applies here: estimates are not rising, so there is no mechanism by which analysts revise targets upward as the company reports. The targets are static because the fundamentals are static. The IEEPA tariff-refund optionality (unquantified, following the February 2026 Supreme Court and March 2026 CIT rulings) is a genuine but unsizable upside that I do not capitalize into the base case.

I score valuation 5.5. The multiple is not stretched, but cheapness without a rising-estimate catalyst is fair value, not an asymmetric setup. A low multiple relative to a distorted peer median is not, on its own, a reason to expect re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

Acuity's competitive position is improving at the margin but contested where it matters most. In core lighting, the company holds a strong brand and channel position in North America with the nLight controls platform as a differentiator, but the LED-luminaire business is commoditizing and demand is macro-driven (non-residential construction, rates, energy-efficiency codes). Industry read-through from peers in May 2026 showed mixed equipment demand sector-wide, suggesting ABL's softness is partly macro/project-timing rather than pure share loss, but Acuity's flat-to-down guide sits at the weaker end of the peer range.

In AIS, the company is moving up the value chain into stickier, higher-margin platform revenue, and the Q-SYS acquisition has been integrated cleanly (purchase accounting finalized with no measurement-period adjustments). This is the most contested arena: management explicitly cites well-resourced global technology and software companies, building-solutions providers, and AV/cloud-control startups as competitors, plus emerging AI dynamics. The moat in AIS is narrower and newer than the entrenched position in lighting. The trajectory is positive — gaining mix and margin in the higher-value segment — but the durability of the AIS advantage against deep-pocketed tech entrants is unproven. We score competitive position 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Core lighting demand.** The single biggest swing factor. With the bulk of revenue tied to non-residential construction and renovation and management trimming ABL guidance, any further deterioration in project releases directly pressures the consolidated growth and margin story.

**Mexico/tariff/USMCA concentration.** With 55% of finished goods made in Mexico, ABL gross profit in FY2026 was explicitly pressured by higher tariff costs. Loss of Maquiladora status, USMCA renegotiation, or labor disruption (≈48% of the workforce is under collective-bargaining agreements expiring within a year) would hit costs directly. This is a company-specific exposure that the broader industrials cohort does not share to the same degree.

**Goodwill and intangibles.** The QSC deal added $394.6M of goodwill and $713.9M of intangibles. Total goodwill is now $1.5B and intangibles ~$1.06B — together ~56% of total assets. Amortization runs ~$24M/quarter. If AIS growth assumptions disappoint, non-cash impairment risk is material. Current segment momentum points the other way, but the balance-sheet composition has shifted decisively toward intangible value.

**Pension mark-to-market.** Plans are marked annually in Q4, producing lumpy non-cash charges. Expect potential Q4 noise.

**Refinancing.** The June 2027 term-loan/revolver maturity and a May 2026 new financing agreement indicate refinancing is in motion in a higher-for-longer rate environment, a manageable but monitorable item given modest net leverage.

### Options Market Signal

The options term structure is in steep backwardation, with near-term (1-week) ATM IV at 118.2% against 1-year historical vol of 33.1% — the market is pricing imminent event-driven stress, almost certainly the upcoming earnings print. The longer-dated tenors are calm (6-month IV 32.8%, essentially at historical vol), so the backwardation reflects an earnings-date binary rather than sustained directional fear. Put/call skew is balanced at 2.2%, the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.37, and the put/call OI ratio collapsed from 2.06 to 0.40 over the past month as the price rose 15.3%. The market is not positioned bearishly; the shift away from puts as the stock recovered toward $306-317 reads as neutral-to-mildly-constructive into the print, consistent with a HOLD. The elevated near-term IV makes options expensive to buy and argues against pre-positioning ahead of the result.

We score risk factors 5.5. The risks are concentrated and identifiable (lighting demand, Mexico/tariff, intangible impairment) rather than diffuse, and several are not yet fully priced given the calm consensus.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$370-400).** AIS sustains 30%+ growth and reaches 30%+ of revenue within 18 months, dragging consolidated growth and margins up while ABL stabilizes around flat. Operating margin pushes toward 15%+, FY27 EPS exceeds the $21.28 consensus, and the multiple re-rates toward the high-end specialty peers as the market accepts Acuity as a software/AV platform rather than a lighting cyclical. IEEPA tariff refunds provide unquantified upside. Continued buybacks compound EPS. At ~18x a $21+ FY27 number plus mix re-rating, ~$370-400 is achievable.

**Bear Case (~$250-265).** ABL demand deteriorates further as non-residential construction weakens into a consumer/macro slowdown, dropping the segment to down-MSD. AIS growth decelerates as deep-pocketed tech competitors enter the AV/intelligent-spaces arena, and the goodwill faces impairment scrutiny. Estimates continue the 90-day downward drift. Tariff costs compress ABL gross margin further. The multiple compresses toward 14x trailing as the market re-rates back to a lighting cyclical, implying ~$250-265, near the 52-week low.

**Base Case (~$330-345).** AIS continues to grow ~40% but ABL stays flat-to-down, producing mid-single-digit consolidated growth and gradual margin improvement. EPS reaches roughly the $19.52 current-year / $21.28 next-year consensus, buybacks add 2-3% to per-share growth, and the multiple holds near 16-17x forward. This lands around $330-345, in line with the consensus mean/median and ~5-9% above the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold). Reassessment timeline: next earnings report (fiscal Q3 2026, expected late June/early July 2026).**

This is the specific, falsifiable basis for not upgrading to BUY despite the overall score: consensus EPS estimates have been revised *down* over the trailing 90 days and are flat over 30 days — there is no rising-estimate catalyst, which the system's own track record identifies as the prerequisite for a high-conviction long. Cheapness relative to a distorted peer median, absent rising estimates, is fair value rather than asymmetric upside. With ABL guided flat-to-down and analysts marking estimates lower, the stock lacks the upward revision momentum or organic re-acceleration that would justify paying up here.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. ABL organic revenue returns to positive YoY growth (any quarter), confirming the lighting base has bottomed and removing the single biggest drag.
2. Net consensus current-year EPS estimates turn positive over a 30-day window (reversing the 90-day downward trend), signaling analysts are catching up to the AIS mix shift.
3. AIS reaches ~30% of consolidated revenue while holding 30%+ growth, demonstrating the mix shift is large enough to define the multiple.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. ABL revenue declines accelerate to down-MSD or worse on a YoY basis for two consecutive quarters.
2. Any goodwill/intangible impairment charge against the QSC-related balance.
3. AIS operating-margin expansion stalls or reverses, indicating competitive pressure in the high-multiple segment.

## Conclusion

Acuity is a well-run, financially healthy company executing a credible transition from a commoditizing lighting business toward a higher-margin intelligent-spaces platform. The AIS segment's growth and margin doubling are real and the balance sheet is clean after rapid deleveraging. But the core lighting business — still the overwhelming majority of revenue — is stagnant-to-shrinking, consensus estimates are flat-to-falling rather than rising, and the stock trades near fair value at ~16x forward with no dated catalyst that resolves the ABL drag. The trajectory of prior scores (6.1 → 6.6 → 6.5) and three consecutive HOLDs reflects a thesis that has been correctly characterized and has not materially changed: improving financial health and management alignment, offset by a growth story that depends on one segment outrunning the larger one. The risk/reward is balanced — roughly 5-9% upside to the base case against meaningful downside if lighting weakens further. We maintain HOLD and would revisit on the fiscal Q3 print.
