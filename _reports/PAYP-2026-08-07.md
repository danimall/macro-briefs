## Executive Summary

PayPay's June-quarter report, filed as a 6-K on August 7, 2026 (accession 0001193125-26-338991), is the strongest operating print the company has produced since its March 12, 2026 IPO. Revenue rose 27.4% year over year to ¥109,788mn, operating profit rose 87.1% to ¥29,865mn, operating margin expanded 867bps to 27.2%, and Financial Service segment profit went from ¥1,468mn to ¥7,385mn. Over three years, revenue has roughly doubled (¥201.2bn in FY2023 to ¥380.7bn in FY2026, a 23.7% CAGR) while operating income swung from −¥20.5bn to +¥80.1bn and gross margin widened 1,150bps to 71.4%. The operating-leverage story is real, quantified, and accelerating.

The same filing also makes clear what PayPay has become. Total assets are ¥5,493,719mn, of which ¥2,631,267mn (48%) is loans and advances to customers, funded in part by borrowings that rose from ¥574,505mn at March 31 to ¥757,093mn in a single quarter. Return on assets is 1.19% against return on equity of 37.27%, approximately 12.6x leverage on estimated book equity of ~¥435bn. Provisions for loss allowance rose 58.7% to ¥8,314mn, from 6.1% to 7.6% of revenue, while revenue grew 27.4%. In the eight weeks before this filing, management committed ¥132bn to acquire 70.2% of T&D Financial Life Insurance and ¥100bn to subscribe for 2.13% of Seven & i's shares (payment scheduled August 17, 2026), together roughly 13.6% of market capitalization and about 53% of estimated book equity.

The valuation is not stretched on forward earnings. Consensus FY2027 EPS of ¥123.31 implies ~20x at the current price, and FY2028 consensus of ¥159.67 implies ~15.8x on ~30% expected earnings growth. But trailing "13.4x earnings" is an artifact: FY2026 net income of ¥115,034mn exceeded operating income of ¥80,082mn, with a TTM income-tax credit of roughly $202mn, most plausibly deferred-tax-asset recognition. Normalizing TTM pretax income at a Japanese statutory-like rate puts the trailing multiple near 26x. Consensus forward P/E sitting *above* trailing P/E is the sell side saying the same thing. The case for owning this at $15.40 rests on estimates I cannot audit against primary filings, given a credit book that has never been tested through a cycle, a business-mix shift into insurance underwriting, ¥100bn of unhedged single-stock equity beta about to land on the balance sheet, a controlled-company structure with 0.02% insider ownership and no US-style compensation disclosure, and a probable lock-up expiry within weeks. HOLD.

## Company Overview

PayPay Corporation, founded in 2018 and headquartered in Shinjuku, Tokyo, operates Japan's largest QR-code mobile payments platform with 1,994 employees. It listed ADSs on Nasdaq at $16.00 on March 12, 2026 and remains a controlled company: 253.10m of 677.14m shares are in the float (37.4%), implying ~62.6% held outside the float, consistent with continued majority ownership by the SoftBank Corp / LY Corp group. As a Japanese foreign private issuer it files 20-F and 6-K, not 10-K/10-Q, and files no proxy statement.

Two reported segments, FY2026 (year ended March 31, 2026):

| Segment | FY2026 (¥mn) | FY2025 (¥mn) | Growth | Share of gross revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Payment | 311,217 | 248,254 | +25.4% | 82% |
| Financial Service | 72,366 | 53,640 | +34.9% | 19% |
| Eliminations | (2,921) | (2,816) | — | (1%) |
| **Total** | **380,662** | **299,078** | **+27.3%** | 100% |

The Payment segment provides settlement plus credit products — revolving credit, installments and cash advances. The Financial Service segment provides internet banking (deposits, remittance, lending, FX), securities intermediary and app-based investing, point management, and loan management, plus insurance and merchant marketing.

The classification matters. The market-data feed labels PayPay "Software – Infrastructure." Its balance sheet is that of a bank and consumer lender. Any framework that applies software peer multiples or software cash-flow screens to PAYP will misprice it in both directions. The widely reported Altman Z-Score of 0.4 is a classification artifact — a deposit-and-borrowing-funded loan book mechanically produces a sub-1 Z-Score — and should not be read as distress evidence, nor as a clean bill of health.

### Earnings quality: the tax line

FY2026 net income of ¥115,034mn exceeded operating income of ¥80,082mn by ¥35bn. TTM income tax is a credit of roughly $202mn; TTM pretax margin is 23.38% against a profit margin of 30.62%; the effective rate is reported as not applicable. The mechanical read is recognition of previously unrecognized deferred tax assets once sustained profitability made utilization probable. EDGAR full-text search confirms "deferred tax assets" language in the FY2026 20-F (accession 0001193125-26-289382) and the May 6, 2026 results 6-K (0002080845-26-000005), but the tax note itself was unreadable, so the size and nature of the benefit are unverified. Alternative explanations — a non-recurring gain, a rate change, a reorganization item — cannot be excluded.

Two consequences. First, trailing P/E of ~13.4x at $15.40 is not a run-rate multiple. Applying a ~30.6% rate to TTM pretax income of ~¥94,524mn gives normalized net income of ~¥65.6bn, or roughly 26x. Second, and more encouraging, the June quarter appears to have been taxed normally: profit of ¥19,749mn against operating profit of ¥29,865mn implies roughly 34% of pretax income absorbed by tax and minorities. If that holds, forward consensus is being built on a normalized basis, and the distortion is confined to the FY2026 comparison rather than the forward estimates.

### Cash flow

The swing to −¥54,252mn operating cash flow should not be read as working-capital deterioration without qualification. For a consolidated bank and consumer lender under the IFRS presentations common among Japanese financial groups, growth in loans and advances and purchases of investment securities sit within operating activities. With a ¥2.63trn book expanding, operating cash flow is negative in growth quarters by construction, and FY2026's +¥375bn is the same artifact in reverse. The corollary is that headline "FCF yield" and "P/FCF" statistics circulating for this name capture depositor float and loan-book movements rather than owner cash flow, and should be discarded as valuation inputs. Enterprise value of ~$14.7bn is similarly misleading: it adds net debt while excluding the loan assets that debt funds.

What is a genuine change is the funding mix. Borrowings rose ¥182.6bn (+31.8%) in one quarter to ¥757,093mn. Against an estimated equity base of ~¥435bn, that is a material shift toward wholesale funding into a Bank of Japan normalization cycle. The offsetting asset-yield benefit is unknown: the interest-rate sensitivity disclosure sits in the 20-F I could not open, as do the deposit-versus-wholesale split, debt maturities and covenants.

### Credit

Provisions rose 58.7% while revenue rose 27.4%; as a share of revenue they went from 6.1% to 7.6%. Against the loan book, the quarter's provision is 0.316%. I decline to annualize that to a cost-of-risk forecast: quarterly provisioning is lumpy, IFRS 9 stage migration and model updates distort single periods, and "loans and advances" almost certainly blends high-yield revolving card receivables with lower-yield bank lending. As a labelled sensitivity, a sustained ~1.3% annual cost of risk on ¥2.6trn would be ~¥34bn against ~¥94bn of TTM operating profit — absorbable if asset yields are card-like, painful if they are not. The mix, staging and coverage disclosures needed to resolve this are unavailable.

**Financial Health: 5.0.** Profitability and operating leverage are strong and improving; cash and investments of ~¥491bn are substantial. Against that: ~12.6x leverage, a 32% one-quarter increase in borrowings, ¥232bn of commitments against ~¥435bn of equity, an earnings line flattered by a tax item, and — decisively for scoring — no verifiable capital adequacy, loan composition, deposit stability or maturity schedule for what is functionally a financial institution.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is among the better ones available in the current market. Revenue growth of 27.4% in the June quarter matched the FY2026 rate of 27.3%, with no deceleration; the three-year CAGR is 23.7%. Operating profit growth of 87.1% against 27.4% revenue growth is textbook operating leverage on a fixed-cost platform, and gross margin has widened every year since FY2023.

Estimate revisions confirm the direction. Consensus current-year (FY2027) EPS has risen from ¥112.26 to ¥123.31 over 90 days (+9.8%) and next-year from ¥150.18 to ¥159.67 (+6.3%); the current-quarter estimate is up 14.9% over 90 days. Analyst coverage has shifted toward the bullish end (three strong buys from two, eleven buy-or-better against two holds). This is a positive earnings-momentum signal, but the magnitude sits well below the threshold at which revisions become a standalone buy trigger, and the analyst base is thin — seven to ten contributors on a five-month-old listing.

Two qualifications weigh on how much to pay for the growth. First, an increasing share of it is balance-sheet-funded credit growth rather than platform fee growth. Financial Service revenue grew 34.9% in FY2026 versus 25.4% for Payment, and segment profit there rose more than fivefold in the June quarter while provisions rose 58.7%. Lending growth converts capital into revenue at a leverage multiple; it is lower-quality growth than payment volume growth, and it consumes equity.

Second, point expenses were ¥16,041mn in the quarter, 14.6% of revenue. The margin-expansion thesis assumes that ratio keeps declining. Japanese cashless competition has historically been fought with points and cashback, and a re-escalation by Rakuten, NTT Docomo or the Sumitomo Mitsui / Olive complex is the single cleanest way the operating-leverage story breaks. Consensus three-year revenue growth of 19.6% already embeds deceleration from the current 27%.

There is no company guidance in evidence. No earnings call transcript was obtainable for this issuer, so management's own forward framing — including on point spend, credit appetite and the insurance integration — is unverified. That is a meaningful gap for a business whose mix is actively changing.

**Growth Prospects: 7.0.**

Working FX is inferred at ¥163.7/USD from the revenue pair (TTM ¥404,296mn = $2.47bn) and cross-checks against P/S of 4.22x. It is approximate, and USD returns on the ADS carry unhedged yen translation.

Two honest observations pull in opposite directions.

Against the stock: the headline cheapness is illusory. Anyone anchoring on "13x earnings" is anchoring on a tax credit. On normalized trailing earnings PayPay trades at ~26x, and at 4.1x book for a lender whose cost of risk has never been observed through a cycle and whose capital is about to absorb ¥232bn of commitments, including ¥100bn of mark-to-market single-stock exposure. The 37% ROE that justifies 4.1x book is itself partly a product of the tax item and of leverage that has just increased. A sum-of-the-parts framework — a platform multiple on the 82%-of-revenue Payment business, a book-value multiple on the bank, credit and insurance stack — is the honest structure, and the segment balance-sheet disclosure required to build it is unavailable.

For the stock: on forward consensus the multiple is genuinely undemanding. Roughly 20x an FY2027 estimate that is itself rising, on 27% revenue growth and 87% operating profit growth, falling to ~16x FY2028, is not a premium multiple. Implied PEG on FY2027→FY2028 earnings growth is approximately 0.7. Consensus targets run $17.23 low, $23.86 median, $24.14 mean, $31.93 high — even the lowest published target is 12% above spot. The stock trades 3.8% below its $16.00 IPO price and 38% below its March 23 high of $24.89 despite the operating profit doubling since.

The forward arithmetic is supportive, but I discount it for a specific reason rather than a general one: the estimates cannot be triangulated against a primary filing. The only recorded quarterly surprise for this company is a 37.5% miss (March 2026 quarter: ¥33.13 estimated, ¥20.71 actual), and it is unverified whether consensus is compiled on an IFRS-reported or adjusted basis. Cheap forward multiples built on thin, unauditable estimates for a levered financial with an opaque loan book are the configuration in which apparent value fails to convert.

**Valuation: 5.5.** Modestly constructive on forward earnings, not compelling on quality-adjusted evidence.

## Competitive Landscape

PayPay is the scale leader in Japanese QR payments, a market that has consolidated into an oligopoly against Rakuten Pay/Rakuten Point, NTT Docomo's d-payment/d-point, au PAY, and the Sumitomo Mitsui / Olive stack. Evidence of position rather than assertion: gross margin has expanded 1,150bps in three years to 71.4%, revenue per employee is ~$1.24m and profit per employee ~$379k on a 1,994-person base, and the company has demonstrably migrated a payments user base into higher-margin credit and banking products (Financial Service segment profit ¥1,468mn → ¥7,385mn year over year).

The July 31, 2026 Seven & i alliance (6-K 0002080845-26-000023) extends the competitive frame from cashback to ecosystem bundling: payments plus loyalty ID plus banking plus credit plus, pending closing, life insurance, plus physical convenience-retail distribution. Reuters reported Seven & i receiving ¥300bn total, ¥100bn each from SoftBank Corp, PayPay and Sumitomo Mitsui Card. The literal relationship deserves precision: Seven & i disposed of treasury shares and PayPay paid cash for 2.13% of issued shares. PayPay is an investor in and commercial alliance partner of Seven & i. Seven & i is not a customer, and PayPay does not own or control 7-Eleven's loyalty assets. Tier-3 reporting characterizing the deal as combining ~74m PayPay users with ~39m 7iD members is unconfirmed by any filing and should not carry weight.

One structural caution embedded in the deal: Sumitomo Mitsui Card, a direct competitor in Japanese payments, invested the same ¥100bn alongside PayPay. The alliance is non-exclusive on its face, which limits how much competitive advantage the ¥100bn purchases.

Trajectory is improving — share leadership, widening margins, expanding product surface — but point spend at 14.6% of revenue confirms the fight is still partly a subsidy war.

**Competitive Position: 6.5.**

## Risk Assessment

**Business-mix shift into insurance underwriting.** The ¥132bn purchase of 70.2% of T&D Financial Life (SPA dated June 4, 2026; 6-K 0002080845-26-000016) takes PayPay into long-duration insurance liabilities with a different risk architecture: solvency-margin regulation, actuarial reserve adequacy, asset-liability duration matching. Closing conditions, regulatory approvals, expected close date, acquiring entity (parent versus subsidiary) and purchase-price allocation are all unverified.

**Concentrated listed-equity exposure.** The ¥100bn Seven & i subscription, payable August 17, 2026, places roughly 23% of estimated book equity into a single listed retail stock. Whether marks flow through P&L or OCI depends on an IFRS 9 election I cannot verify; either route injects equity-market beta into a payments company's reported results and capital.

**Capital deployment versus capital base.** ¥232bn committed in eight weeks equals ~13.6% of market capitalization and ~53% of estimated book equity, against cash and investments of ~¥491bn, with borrowings up ¥182.6bn in the same quarter. Funding source is not disclosed in available evidence.

**Credit cycle.** Provisions compounding at roughly twice revenue growth on a ¥2.63trn book, in products (revolving credit, cash advances) that carry the highest loss rates in the lineup. Under expected-credit-loss accounting, rising provisions on a fast-growing book are expected and say little about vintage quality; without stage migration, NPL ratios and coverage, growth-driven provisioning cannot be distinguished from deterioration.

**Funding and rate risk.** Wholesale borrowings up ~32% in a quarter into BoJ normalization. Net sensitivity unknown.

**Governance and controlled-company structure.** ~62.6% of shares outside the float; insider ownership 0.02%, economically trivial; no DEF 14A exists and no US-style compensation or pay-versus-performance disclosure. The parent co-invested ¥100bn alongside its subsidiary in the same counterparty transaction. That structure requires related-party scrutiny that could not be performed — the AGM materials (6-K 0002080845-26-000012; results 0001193125-26-289102) and the 20-F related-party note were unreadable.

**Share supply.** Shares outstanding rose 15.22% year over year and 4.73% quarter over quarter. A customary 180-day IPO lock-up from March 12, 2026 would expire in early-to-mid September 2026, within weeks. This is market convention, not verified: the 424B4 (0002080845-26-000001) could not be opened, so lock-up length, covered parties and early-release triggers are unknown. Short interest is modest — 3.47m shares, 2.1% of shares outstanding, ~1.4% of float, 2.5 days to cover — so this is not a crowded bearish position.

**Earnings-quality normalization.** If the FY2026 tax credit was one-off DTA recognition, reported EPS faces a mechanical headwind as the effective rate normalizes, even with strong pretax growth. The June quarter suggests this is already occurring.

**Verification gap as a risk in itself.** Every SEC Archives document request returned HTTP 403 — the 20-F (0001193125-26-289382), all 6-K exhibits, the prospectus, the S-8 (0001193125-26-103096), the Schedule 13G (0001140361-26-021677). Filing content was corroborated by exact arithmetic reconciliation against independently compiled aggregates, which is unusually strong evidence for the income statement and cash-flow statement. It is no substitute for reading risk factors, the tax note, the loan-book composition, capital adequacy or the related-party section of a levered financial institution.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term (August 21, 13 days) ATM implied volatility is 124.7% against 83.7% one-year historical volatility — a 40.9pp premium, 93rd percentile within the chain — with pronounced backwardation (124.7% near, 92.7% at three months, 79.8% at six months) and an implied move of ±17.5% on the nearest expiry. ATM IV rose from 84.5% to 124.7% between July 29 and August 7 while the stock rose 3.8%. The data source characterizes the 52.6% put/call skew as elevated put demand. Against that, open interest is call-tilted at 0.27.

Two cautions on interpretation. First, the chain contains only 30 contracts across four expirations, so this is a thin market and the signal quality is correspondingly low. Second, there is no scheduled earnings event in the window — the June quarter was released July 30 — which leaves the August 17 Seven & i payment and any lock-up-related supply as the plausible referents. The reading I take: option sellers are demanding a large premium for near-dated risk while longer tenors price something closer to realized volatility, and I would not initiate here on rich near-term vol into an unidentified catalyst. It corroborates, rather than drives, the decision to wait.

**Risk Factors: 3.5.** Multiple concentrated and partly binary exposures, at least one imminent, and the diagnostics needed to price them are unavailable.

## Management Quality

The assessment turns on capital allocation, not reputation. Five months after IPO, management committed roughly half of estimated book equity in eight weeks: ¥132bn for control of a life insurer and ¥100bn for a 2.13% stake in a convenience-store operator, in the same quarter that borrowings rose ¥182.6bn, with no dividend and net share issuance of +15.2% year over year. The strategic logic is coherent — buy distribution and product breadth rather than buy volume with cashback — and the Seven & i stake may carry commercial economics (acceptance, data, exclusivity terms) that make it more than a passive position. Those terms are unverified.

Alignment is weak. Insider ownership is 0.02% of shares outstanding. No proxy statement exists, so there is no US-style compensation or pay-versus-performance data for this issuer; a scoring framework should not assume otherwise. Twelve Form 4 transaction lines across five officers between April and June 2026 are all option exercises (code M) at $8.11–$8.30, roughly half the IPO price, with no open-market purchases and no reported open-market sales in the retrieved window (accessions 0002114216-26-000002, 0002114265-26-000006, 0002114231-26-000010, 0002114231-26-000007, 0002114249-26-000009, 0002114249-26-000003). Exercise-and-hold is mildly constructive, but during a lock-up window it is also the only available action, and I cannot exclude transactions outside the retrieval window. Alignment here comes from the controlling parent, whose interests are not necessarily identical to minority ADS holders', particularly given the parallel ¥100bn investment by SoftBank Corp in the same counterparty.

**Management Quality: 4.5.**

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $24.00 (+56%)

Operating leverage continues at the demonstrated rate. Point expenses fall below 12% of revenue as ecosystem bundling replaces cashback, driving operating margin from 27% toward the low 30s. The Seven & i alliance converts into measurable acceptance and loyalty economics rather than a passive stake. Financial Service scales at high incremental margin with cost of risk contained near 1% of loans. FY2028 EPS reaches or exceeds consensus ¥159.67, and the market applies ~24x — a defensible multiple for a 25%+ compounder with an entrenched oligopoly position — giving ¥3,832 per share, roughly $23.40 at ¥163.7, with the consensus mean of $24.14 as corroboration. The DTA continues to suppress cash taxes, supporting reported EPS above my normalized case.

### Bear Case — $11.00 (−29%)

Cost of risk normalizes higher than the current 0.32% quarterly run rate as the consumer credit book seasons; provisions consume the Financial Service margin gain. A competitive re-escalation in points resets Payment segment margins. The T&D acquisition closes into an insurance business requiring capital support and delivering integration friction, while the Seven & i stake marks down and injects volatility into reported earnings and capital. Lock-up expiry in September releases supply from a ~62.6% block into a 37.4% float. FY2028 EPS is cut ~25% to ~¥120 and the multiple compresses to ~14x, giving ¥1,680, or ~$10.30; a 2.5x book multiple on ~¥518bn of projected equity gives ~$11.70. Range $10.50–11.50; midpoint $11.00.

### Base Case — $18.00 (+17%)

Revenue growth decelerates toward the consensus 19–20% path; operating margin holds in the high 20s; cost of risk rises modestly but stays absorbable; FY2028 EPS lands near consensus ¥159.67 and the market pays ~18.5x, reflecting a discount for leverage, governance opacity and mix uncertainty. ¥159.67 × 18.5 = ¥2,954, or ~$18.05.

Probability weighting of 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear produces an expected return of roughly +13%. For a name where the balance sheet cannot be audited, the estimate base is thin and one recorded quarter missed by 37.5%, that expected return does not clear the bar for initiating. It clears the bar for holding.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (uncertain).** The classification is deliberate: I have positive forward-earnings arithmetic and an unresolvable evidence gap on the balance sheet, which leaves insufficient confidence for a stronger call in either direction.

- **Base case:** $18.00 (18.5x FY2028 consensus EPS ¥159.67 at ¥163.7/USD), +17% from $15.40.
- **Bull case:** $24.00 (24x FY2028 EPS), +56%.
- **Bear case:** $11.00 (14x a 25%-reduced FY2028 EPS; cross-checked at ~2.5x projected book), −29%.
- **Timeframe:** 12–18 months, spanning the T&D closing, two to three quarters of cost-of-risk data, and the lock-up window.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Disclosure of loan-book composition with stage-1/2/3 migration, NPL ratio and coverage showing cost of risk stabilizing at or below ~1.0% annualized for two consecutive quarters, alongside capital-adequacy metrics.
2. Point expenses falling below 13% of revenue with operating margin held at or above 27%, confirming the margin expansion is structural rather than a subsidy pause.
3. Price near $12.00–12.50 (approximately 13x FY2028 consensus) with FY2027/FY2028 estimates still rising, which would restore genuine asymmetry against the bear case.
4. Verified lock-up terms plus expiry passing without material supply pressure, removing the single most identifiable near-term overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Provisions exceeding ~9% of revenue for two consecutive quarters, or any disclosure of NPL formation outpacing loan growth.
2. FY2028 consensus EPS revised down more than 10% from ¥159.67, or a quarterly miss on the scale of the March 2026 quarter's 37.5%.
3. Operating margin contracting below ~22% on renewed point/cashback spending.
4. Equity issuance or further debt-funded acquisitions beyond the ¥232bn already committed without disclosed return hurdles, or evidence that the ¥100bn Seven & i mark is materially impairing reported capital.
5. Any indication that the T&D transaction requires incremental capital support at closing.

**Next reassessment point:** the September-quarter 6-K (expected late October / early November 2026), which should provide the first post-Seven & i-payment balance sheet, the second data point on cost of risk, and any T&D closing update. An interim checkpoint is the lock-up window in September 2026.

## SEC Trigger Review

**Accession 0001193125-26-338991 — Form 6-K, filed and accepted August 7, 2026, period June 30, 2026, primary document `fy26q1_quarterly_fs.htm`.** Existence, filing date and period were verified through EDGAR. ## Conclusion

PayPay has produced a genuine and quantified profitability inflection: revenue compounding at 27%, operating margin up 867bps year over year to 27.2%, operating profit up 87%, and a Financial Service segment whose profit rose more than fivefold. On forward consensus of ¥123.31 (FY2027) and ¥159.67 (FY2028), the stock trades at roughly 20x and 16x with ~30% expected earnings growth, which is not an expensive multiple, and every published analyst target sits above the current price.

The reasons not to buy it here are specific rather than atmospheric. Trailing earnings are flattered by a tax credit that puts the normalized trailing multiple near 26x. The company is a ~12.6x levered lender with a ¥2.63trn loan book whose composition, staging, coverage and capital adequacy I could not verify, growing provisions at twice the rate of revenue. In eight weeks management committed roughly half of estimated book equity to a life-insurance acquisition and a single-stock equity position, funded in part by a 32% one-quarter increase in wholesale borrowings, in a governance structure where insiders own 0.02%, no compensation disclosure exists, and the controlling parent invested alongside in the same transaction. A probable lock-up expiry falls within weeks, and near-dated options are pricing a ±17.5% move against an unidentified catalyst.

Probability-weighted expected return of roughly +13% against a −29% bear case, on estimates that cannot be audited against primary filings, supports maintaining exposure rather than adding to it. HOLD, with the September-quarter report and the cost-of-risk disclosure as the decision point.
