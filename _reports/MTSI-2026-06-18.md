**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

MACOM has shifted from a steady compound-semiconductor specialist into one of the cleaner public-market expressions of the AI optical-interconnect buildout. Revenue grew 32.6% in FY2025 to $967.3M and the company posted record quarterly revenue of $289.0M in Q2 FY26 (+22.5% YoY), led by Data Center at $98.2M as pluggable optical modules and active-copper-cable volumes ramped with AI infrastructure spending. Operating income rose 76% to $129.7M in FY25, operating cash flow was $235.4M, and free cash flow reached $192.8M at a 19.9% margin. The headline FY25 GAAP net loss of $54.2M is an accounting artifact: it reflects a one-time, non-cash $193.1M loss on extinguishment of the 2026 convertible notes, not operating deterioration. Normalized, the business is profitable, FCF-generative, and carries a strong balance sheet ($664.8M cash and short-term investments, Altman Z of 3.07, D/E of 0.58).

The estimate-revision picture is strongly positive. Next-quarter consensus EPS rose +27.0% over 30 days (+27.1% over 90 days), next-year +21.9% (30d), and current-year +12.3% (30d), with revenue growth durably above 20%. Those revisions, combined with a defensible compound-semiconductor fab moat and a counter-cyclical Industrial & Defense segment (43.4% of revenue), validate the growth thesis. The signal is real and broad-based.

What stops this from being a BUY is the price. At ~$367 against ~$1B trailing revenue, MTSI trades at roughly 30x sales and ~73x consensus FY26 EPS of ~$5.03. The stock has risen ~181% over twelve months and prices flawless AI-capex durability. Three idiosyncratic risks compound: China grew to 34% of Q2 revenue as export-control pressure escalates, Customer A reached 18% of revenue, and insiders sold broadly with zero open-market buying, including a director's near-complete exit. The fundamentals justify owning the franchise; the multiple does not justify adding at current levels. We rate MTSI a HOLD with a growth bias, and would accumulate on a meaningful pullback toward the low-$300s where the forward multiple compresses closer to the growth rate.

## Company Overview

MACOM designs and manufactures high-performance analog, RF, microwave, millimeter-wave, optical, and photonic semiconductors. The company is fabrication-differentiated, operating its own wafer processing across GaAs, GaN, InP, and specialized silicon at facilities in Lowell MA, Ann Arbor MI, RTP NC, and Limeil-Brévannes France, supplemented by external foundries and Asian assembly/test. It reports as one operating segment but serves three end markets:

- **Industrial & Defense (I&D): 43.4%** ($419.8M FY25) — radar, RF jammers, electronic countermeasures, space electronics, test & measurement, medical.
- **Data Center: 30.3%** ($292.8M FY25) — analog ICs and photonic components for high-speed optical interconnects (100G → 1.6T/3.2T), the fastest-growing segment.
- **Telecom: 26.3%** ($254.6M FY25) — 5G/6G infrastructure, SATCOM, PON/FTTx, long-haul/metro.

The competitive moat rests on a broad catalog (thousands of standard and custom devices, 6,000+ end customers), deep compound-semiconductor process IP that silicon cannot easily replicate at high frequency and power, and entrenched defense design-in positions with long program lifecycles. Named competitors include ADI, Broadcom, Credo, Marvell, MaxLinear, Microchip, NXP, Qorvo, Semtech, Skyworks, and Sumitomo, plus rising Chinese entrants. The I&D business provides counter-cyclical ballast that distinguishes MACOM from pure-play data-center semis.

## Financial Analysis

The top line is strong and accelerating with expanding margins:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q2 FY26 (Apr-26) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $648.4M | $729.6M | $967.3M (+32.6%) | $289.0M (+22.5% YoY) |
| Gross margin (GAAP) | 59.5% | 54.0% | 54.7% | 56.9% |
| Operating income | $107.4M | $73.7M | $129.7M | $50.8M (17.6% margin) |
| Net income (GAAP) | $91.6M | $76.9M | ($54.2M) | $46.3M |

The FY25 GAAP net loss was driven entirely by the loss on extinguishment of the 2026 convertible notes (the December 2024 exchange). Q2 FY26 confirms the underlying earnings power: 56.9% GAAP gross margin (58.5% non-GAAP), 16% net margin, and non-GAAP EPS of $1.09. Six-month FY26 net income was $95.1M against a year-ago loss. Margin expansion is driven by scale, better fab yields and higher utilization at RTP and Lowell, and richer mix toward higher-margin data center products. FCF conversion well above net income (adjusted for the convert charge) signals high-quality earnings.

The balance sheet is among the strongest in the peer set. Current ratio is 3.71, and cash plus short-term investments is split $98.5M cash and $566.3M investments. Debt is almost entirely the $344.3M 0.00% convertible notes due December 2029 (conversion price ~$174.03, deeply in the money at current levels). The remaining $160.7M 2026 converts were settled in cash in March 2026 plus 1.26M shares for the conversion premium, removing near-term maturity risk. Stockholders' equity is $1.42B and the accumulated deficit is narrowing.

On capital allocation, there is no dividend and no buyback. Cash is being deployed into capacity (the RTP fab ramp, rising capex), tuck-in M&A, and the new IQE minority investment. R&D intensity is high at roughly 25% of revenue ($244.5M FY25), consistent with a company protecting a process-technology moat.

The earnings-surprise record is mixed rather than uniformly strong: over the last four quarters, one beat, one in-line, and two small misses (all within ±2%). This is a company executing close to consensus rather than consistently blowing past it, which tempers confidence that current estimates are conservative.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is the strongest part of the story and is confirmed by the estimate-revision data. Consensus EPS revisions are broadly positive and accelerating where it matters most:

| Period | Current Est. | 90d Ago | 90d Rev. |
|--------|-------------|---------|----------|
| Current Quarter | $1.35 | $1.14 | +17.6% |
| Next Quarter | $1.57 | $1.23 | +27.1% |
| Current Year | $5.03 | $4.47 | +12.4% |
| Next Year | $6.84 | $5.57 | +22.9% |

Next-quarter and next-year revisions exceeding +20–27% over 90 days are a meaningful forward signal, and they co-occur with revenue growth above 20%. This is the pattern that typically precedes continued upward consensus migration as analysts catch up to a fundamental acceleration. The growth is broad-based across the segment mix: Data Center grew 36–48% and led Q2 at $98.2M (~14.5% QoQ) on AI optical-module and active-copper-cable demand, while I&D grew roughly 19–22% on defense-program strength, providing diversification away from a single end market.

The durability case rests on the structural shift of optics displacing copper at 800G/1.6T/3.2T inside and between data centers, a TAM that expands as accelerator clusters scale. This is corroborated across the broader AI infrastructure complex, where multiple component, networking, and power suppliers are reporting accelerating data-center demand simultaneously, evidence that the demand is broad-based rather than a single-company story. MACOM sits in the picks-and-shovels layer: it supplies analog ICs and photonic components (TIAs, drivers, lasers, pluggable modules) rather than owning a hyperscaler product, so its growth tracks AI infrastructure capex by the large operators and optical-module makers.

The qualifier on growth durability is that the highest-growth, highest-margin business is also the most cyclical. The FY25 10-K explicitly added language tying demand to capital spending on Data Center infrastructure to support AI. With Data Center now ~32–34% of revenue, any AI-capex pause would hit MACOM disproportionately at the margin line.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the asymmetry breaks against the stock. On consensus FY26 EPS of ~$5.03, the forward P/E is ~73x; on FY27 EPS of $6.84 it is still ~54x. Even crediting the rapid estimate revisions and the genuine acceleration, these are premium multiples, not trough-cycle multiples being misread.

The peer comparison shows wide dispersion (FSLR at 17.5x, AMKR at 43.8x, RMBS at 68.8x, LSCC at four-digit P/E, SITM negative), with a peer-median P/E of 68.8x distorted by trough-earnings names. MTSI's forward multiple sits above that range on a much larger absolute market cap (~$30B). The stock is not cheap on any reasonable normalization.

For genuine inflection stocks at reasonable multiples (forward P/E <30x or PEG <1.5), rising estimates make analyst targets floors rather than ceilings. MTSI does not qualify for that treatment: at ~73x forward earnings it is a premium-multiple name, where the target and the price reflect the same growth extrapolation and are not independent confirmation. The risk is asymmetric in the wrong direction: beats are expected and produce little re-rating, while any miss or AI-capex wobble produces sharp derating. The stock has already shown that beta, with intraday and multi-day swings of 8–15% around AI-sentiment events (the early-June drawdown is one example).

Consensus targets bracket the current price tightly: low $345, mean $398, median $392, high $450. The implied upside to the mean is modest (~9% from $367) and the downside to the low target is comparable. That is a balanced-to-unfavorable risk/reward at the multiple, which is why we score valuation low. The valuation score reflects the absence of a margin of safety, not a view that the business is impaired. A meaningful pullback toward the low-$300s would compress the forward multiple closer to the high-30s/low-40s and bring the risk/reward into BUY territory.

## Competitive Landscape

MACOM's moat is structural and trajectory-positive. The proprietary compound-semiconductor fab capability (GaAs/GaN/InP) is difficult for pure-CMOS rivals to replicate at high frequency and power, and it underpins the laser/photonics franchise that the AI optical buildout is consuming. Gross margin expansion (54.0% FY24 → 56.9% Q2 FY26 GAAP) is direct evidence the company is converting scale and mix into improving economics rather than competing on price. The 6,000+ customer base and entrenched defense design-ins with long lifecycles add stickiness that pure-play data-center semis lack.

The IQE plc strategic investment (announced 4/27/2026, <12% equity plus $15M convertible loan notes, board seat, long-term epitaxial-wafer supply agreements) is a vertical-integration move that secures epi capacity for the optical/RF ramp and directly addresses the sole-source supplier risk flagged in the 10-K. It strengthens the supply-chain position, though it adds a minority-investment risk vector (no control over a struggling UK epi supplier), which management added as a new risk factor.

The competitive tension to watch is at the customer and geographic level rather than at the product level. Customer A reached 18% of Q2 revenue and Customer C 12%, a concentration increase from no 10%+ customer in FY25. Against larger rivals (Broadcom, Marvell, Credo), MACOM differentiates by process technology rather than CMOS scale, a defensible niche, but one where the largest optical-module and networking customers hold meaningful negotiating leverage as they concentrate. Rising Chinese entrants and China's domestic semiconductor push are a longer-term share threat in commoditizing segments.

## Risk Assessment

The risks here are concrete and several of them are not yet fully priced into a stock trading at a euphoric multiple:

1. **AI-capex cyclicality is the swing factor.** Data Center at ~32–34% of revenue is the growth engine and the key downside vector. An AI infrastructure-spending pause would hit the highest-margin business hardest. The 10-K's explicit addition of AI-capex-dependence language confirms management views this as material.

2. **China dependence is growing, not shrinking.** China was 28.4% of FY25 sales and grew to ~34% of Q2 FY26 revenue ($99.2M of $289M) even as U.S. export-control rhetoric escalates. BIS restrictions, tariffs, or claw-back-style actions targeting a third of revenue would be a material headwind.

3. **Customer concentration creeping up.** Customer A at 18% and Customer C at 12% introduce single-counterparty risk where a design loss or order timing shift could create an air pocket.

4. **Convertible-note dilution.** The 2029 converts are deeply in the money at ~$174 conversion price, dilutive, and contain fundamental-change repurchase provisions that could deter an acquisition.

5. **Manufacturing yield risk** on less-mature GaN/InP processes, where minor process deviations cause margin volatility.

6. **Concentrated ownership.** Susan Ocampo beneficially owns 12.5%, able to exert significant influence over corporate matters.

7. **Executive turnover cluster.** Three Item 5.02 8-Ks since August 2025 (Aug 2025, Nov 2025, Jan 2026) coincide with heavy insider selling. The underlying departure/appointment details warrant follow-up; CEO/CFO signatures remain Daly/Kober on the latest 10-Q.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent, elevated stress. Near-term ATM IV is 181.0% against 1-year historical volatility of 50.8%, richly priced (spread of 130.2%), at the 86th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 181.0% > long-term 75.1%), the classic signature of the market pricing a specific near-dated event. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.7%, consistent with the stock's demonstrated AI-sentiment beta. Skew is balanced (put/call skew -1.2%, P/C volume ratio 0.36, P/C OI 0.36), so positioning is not directionally bearish; the message is one of elevated uncertainty and event risk, not a one-sided bearish bet. The longer-dated 6-month skew of -5.0% does carry a mild downside lean. This is not a cheap-vol entry, and the market expects volatility to spike near-term, a reason to wait for a pullback rather than chase.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
MACOM is a structural winner in AI optical interconnect, the layer that scales as optics displace copper at 800G/1.6T/3.2T. Data Center growing 36–48% on a base of ~$300M, plus +20% defense growth and a compound-semiconductor fab moat, supports durable 20%+ revenue growth. Margins are expanding (56.9% GAAP GM), FCF is strong (~20% margin), the balance sheet is strong ($664.8M cash, near-zero-coupon converts), and estimate revisions are accelerating (+27% next-quarter, +22% next-year). The IQE deal secures epi supply for the ramp. If AI capex sustains and MACOM continues to take optical share, FY27 EPS of $6.84 grows further and the stock can reach the bull target as estimates migrate higher. Bull case ~$450 (consensus high), roughly +23% from current.

### Bear Case
The valuation prices flawless execution at ~30x sales and ~73x forward EPS. Any AI-capex pause hits the highest-margin Data Center business disproportionately; the stock has already shown 8–15% single-event drawdowns on AI-sentiment shocks. China at 34% of revenue is a growing export-control liability. Customer A at 18% concentrates risk. Pervasive insider selling with zero open-market buying, including a director's near-complete exit, signals insiders are monetizing strength near the top. In a multiple-compression scenario where AI sentiment cools and a quarter disappoints, a derating to the low-$200s is plausible, given how much premium is embedded. Bear case ~$240–260, roughly -30% to -35% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth bias). Reassessment timeline: next earnings report (typically early August 2026), and the IQE deal close (~Q3 FY26).**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$300s, compressing the forward P/E to the high-30s/low-40s, where the multiple aligns with the growth rate and risk/reward turns favorable.
- Evidence of broadening customer base (Customer A concentration declining below 15%) alongside continued +20% Data Center growth, reducing single-counterparty risk.
- A confirmed reduction in China revenue dependence (below ~25%) without a corresponding revenue air pocket, de-risking the export-control overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Data Center revenue sequential decline or guidance cut signaling an AI-capex pause, given the segment's outsized margin contribution.
- A material new China export-control action directly impairing the ~34% of revenue sourced there.
- Continued cluster insider selling alongside any fundamental deterioration (a miss plus guidance cut), which would convert the governance flag from "monetizing strength" to a leading indicator.

**Reassessment anchors:** Q3 FY26 earnings (Data Center growth rate, China mix, Customer A concentration), the IQE close and any follow-on minority-investment disclosures, and the resolution of the executive-turnover cluster.

## Conclusion

MACOM is a high-quality, fundamentally improving business and a legitimate beneficiary of the AI optical-interconnect buildout. The score trajectory (financial health up materially as the convert charge normalized out, growth prospects strengthening on accelerating revisions) reflects genuine improvement, and the new data confirms rather than reverses that trend. We are not lowering scores because the stock has appreciated; appreciation driven by real revenue growth and margin expansion is not a sell signal. The constraint is valuation: at ~30x sales and ~73x forward earnings, the market is already paying for flawless AI-capex durability, leaving no margin of safety, while three concrete risks (China dependence, customer concentration, broad insider selling) and an options market pricing imminent stress argue for patience. This is the third consecutive HOLD, and the bar for continuing it is met by the combination of an excellent business and an unattractive entry multiple. We would own the franchise on a pullback to the low-$300s and rate it HOLD here with a growth bias.
