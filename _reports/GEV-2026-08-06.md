## Executive Summary

GE Vernova has completed one of the more dramatic operating turnarounds in large-cap industrials. Two years after separating from GE, Power segment EBITDA margin has moved from 9.9% (FY2023) to 14.7% (FY2025) to 17.6% in H1 2026, and Electrification from 3.7% to 14.9% to 18.2% on revenue that grew 51% over two years. Remaining performance obligations reached $176.3B at June 30, 2026, or 4.26x trailing-twelve-month revenue of $41.4B, with equipment RPO roughly doubling year over year from $49.7B to $87.8B. Q2 2026 orders of $24.2B rose 88% organically, and management states gas power is essentially sold out through 2030 with H1 2026 equipment orders priced more than 20% above Q4 2025. The balance sheet is genuinely strong: approximately $2.9B of funded debt against $13.1B of cash, $53M of FY2025 interest expense, two undrawn $3.0B facilities, and BBB/BBB+ ratings with positive outlooks.

The difficulty is price. Reported earnings are unusable in both directions. FY2025 net income of $4,884M includes roughly $2.9B from a US valuation-allowance release (effective tax rate of −72.5%), and H1 2026 net income of $5,413M includes $4,428M of gains on business interests dominated by a $3,992M non-cash, non-taxable remeasurement of the previously held 50% of Prolec GE. Free cash flow tells a similar story: H1 2026 FCF of $9,897M rests on a $13,695M operating inflow from contract liabilities, which the 10-Q attributes to down payments and slot-reservation deposits at Power. Management's own FY2026 guidance of $11.5–12.5B implies H2 free cash flow of roughly $1.6–2.6B, below H2 2025's approximately $2.53B. Adjusting for the distortions, the multiple is roughly 43x EV to FY2026E adjusted EBITDA and 66x FY2026 adjusted EPS, not the 29x trailing P/E or 23x price-to-guided-FCF that screens display.

The single operational fact that keeps us at HOLD rather than BUY: gas turbine gigawatts delivered *declined* in H1 2026, to 7.5 GW from 8.2 GW, while revenue grew 22%. Power's growth came from services, price and aeroderivative mix. The equipment-revenue thesis, and the justification for a 43x EBITDA multiple, requires roughly doubling output from 15.3 GW (FY2025) to a 30 GW rate by 2030. That ramp is entirely prospective. We see 13% upside to a $1,150 base case against 25% downside to a $760 bear case, which does not clear the bar for new capital in a sector where selection has historically added no alpha.

## Company Overview

GE Vernova was distributed to GE shareholders on April 2, 2024 with approximately 274.1 million shares. It employs roughly 75,000 people across about 600 sites in 97 countries, including 91 manufacturing plants (41 Power, 17 Wind, 33 Electrification). Roughly 70% of employees work in manufacturing, engineering or field services. The company states its installed base generates approximately 25% of the world's electricity.

**Power** (FY2025 revenue $19,767M, 52% of total) is the economic core: Gas Power $16,006M, Steam $1,937M, Nuclear $1,018M, Hydro $806M. Roughly 7,000 gas turbines are installed, of which about 1,800 sit under long-term service agreements with average remaining life near 10 years. The high-efficiency HA-class fleet numbers 126 units with roughly 3.6 million operating hours; 51 HA units sit in RPO.

**Electrification** ($9,642M, 25%) is the fastest-growing segment — Grid Solutions $6,620M, Power Conversion & Storage $2,049M, Software $973M — and now the most directly levered to data-center demand.

**Wind** ($9,110M, 24%) covers roughly 24,000 serviced turbines out of a ~59,000-unit installed base, with approximately 60% of onshore equipment RPO in the United States.

R&D is being scaled to roughly $5B cumulative over 2025–2028, with company-funded spend rising from $982M (FY2024) to $1,197M (FY2025). The internal allocation has shifted hard: Power R&D rose from $324M (2023) to $550M (2025) and Electrification from $324M to $430M, while Wind was cut from $248M to $161M.

One structural item deserves flagging: GEV does not own the "GE" name or monogram. It licenses them under an agreement that may be terminated in certain circumstances, which would force a global rebranding.

The margin story is broad-based and, in two of three segments, dramatic. Electrification added 11.2 points of EBITDA margin in two years; Power added 4.8 points through FY2025 and roughly another 290bp into H1 2026, reaching 18.8% in Q2. Wind moved the other way: EBITDA of −$1,033M (2023), −$588M (2024), −$598M (2025) and −$657M in H1 2026 alone — cumulative losses of about $2.9B, with the first half of 2026 already worse than all of 2025.

### The earnings line requires reconstruction

FY2025's $4,884M of net income is not a clean number. Pre-tax income was $2,828M against a tax *benefit* of $2,051M. Removing approximately $2.9B of valuation-allowance release leaves roughly $1,984M, or about $7.19 per diluted share against the $17.69 reported. FY2025 pre-tax income also leaned on items below the operating line — $795M of other income (including a $198M unrealized gain on the China XD Electric stake), $459M of non-operating pension income, and $269M of equity-method income — against a GAAP operating margin of only 3.6%.

H1 2026 is distorted in the opposite direction and more severely, carrying the $3,992M Prolec remeasurement gain plus $330M from Proficy and $264M from the China XD exit, at a 10.5% effective tax rate. Q2 2026 standalone diluted EPS of $2.47 at a 29.8% rate is the only recent clean print.

The reported multiples reflect this distortion. The company profile shows both trailing and forward P/E at 29.13, an internally inconsistent pair derived from TTM GAAP EPS of roughly $34.88 that embeds the tax release and the business-interest gains. That figure should not be used.

Reconstructing from consensus: quarterly estimates of $2.08 (Q1 actual), $2.47 (Q2 actual), $4.25 (Q3E) and $6.49 (Q4E) sum to approximately $15.29 of FY2026 adjusted EPS. Our independent bottom-up normalization arrives at roughly $14.0. Both imply a forward P/E of 66–73x. The "current year" consensus of $30.85 in the revision table is a GAAP-inclusive-of-gains figure and is not comparable to the quarterly series.

### Cash flow: real cash, but the wrong kind for valuation purposes

| | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | $2.6B | $5.0B | $1,528M | $10,680M |
| Free cash flow | $1.7B | $3.7B | $1,169M | $9,897M |

The $13,695M contract-liability inflow is the driver. The balance-sheet counterpart rose from $25,774M to $39,944M (Power $27,679M, Electrification $9,113M, Wind $3,150M). TTM free cash flow of roughly $12.4B against TTM adjusted EBITDA of roughly $4.1B is a 3.0x conversion ratio — the signature of a deposit surge, not of earnings.

Management's guidance concedes the fade within the same fiscal year: FY2026 FCF of $11.5–12.5B less H1's $9,897M leaves roughly $1.6–2.6B for H2, below H2 2025's approximately $2.53B. Annualizing the H2 midpoint of ~$2.1B gives a normalized run-rate near $4.2B, which puts price-to-normalized-FCF around 65x rather than the 23x that guidance-based screens produce. Two real cash headwinds compound this: cash taxes paid rose to $1,258M in H1 2026 from $363M a year earlier as the deferred shield unwinds, and GEV made a voluntary $516M pension contribution in June 2026.

The offsetting bull argument is legitimate: as long as the order book keeps expanding and lead times keep lengthening, prepayments are a recurring working-capital *source*, and customers are financing GEV's capacity expansion at zero cost. That is a genuine competitive advantage of the negative-working-capital model. It is not, however, earnings.

### Balance sheet: screens are wrong, but they point at a real risk

An automated screen on the FY2025 10-K produces an Altman Z-Score of 1.08 and "debt/equity" of 4.12. Both are definitional artifacts. The 4.12 is total liabilities over equity; actual funded debt at June 30, 2026 was $2,794M of long-term borrowings (roughly $2.9B with finance leases) against $13,120M of cash — net cash of approximately $10.2B, or true debt/equity near 0.22x. FY2025 interest expense was $53M. The Z-Score penalty comes from negative working capital of −$8,397M, of which contract liabilities of $39,944M are 71.5% of current liabilities; excluding them, working capital is roughly +$31.5B.

The risk the screen is groping toward is real but different in kind: $39.9B of collected customer cash must be discharged through manufacture and delivery. That is execution risk, not refinancing risk. In an adverse demand scenario, cancellations, refund claims, idle-capacity under-absorption and inventory write-downs would arrive together, which is the scenario the 10-K's capacity-expansion risk factor describes.

One consequence of Prolec: goodwill rose from $4,439M to roughly $9.75B and intangibles to roughly $4.7B, so combined intangibles of about $14.5B now exceed total equity of $13,115M. Tangible book value is modestly negative. For a net-cash industrial that is tolerable, but it raises the stakes on impairment.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance was raised for a second time on July 22: revenue $45.5–46.5B (from $44.5–45.5B), adjusted EBITDA margin 12–14% reaffirmed, free cash flow $11.5–12.5B (from $6.5–7.5B). Power organic growth was raised to 18–20% with margin reaffirmed at 17–19%; Electrification revenue lifted to $14.5–15.0B at 18–20% margin. Q3 guidance calls for Power organic growth of 17–19% at 17–18% margin and Wind revenue down low double digits with EBITDA about breakeven.

Three growth engines, in descending order of confidence:

**Electrification is the cleanest.** Data-center orders were $2.7B in Q2 and more than $5B in H1 2026, against roughly $2.4B for all of FY2025. Equipment backlog exceeded $40B, up 69%. Transformers and switchgear are a global bottleneck, and Prolec adds five US manufacturing sites. FY2026 guidance embeds $3.1B of Prolec revenue.

**Power pricing is real; Power volume is not yet.** H1 2026 equipment orders priced more than 20% above Q4 2025 as higher-priced slot reservations converted. But gigawatts sold fell to 7.5 GW in H1 2026 from 8.2 GW (Q2: 3.3 GW versus 5.2 GW), and Power's Q2 revenue of $5.48B missed the roughly $5.6B consensus. Annualizing H1 gives roughly 15 GW, flat against FY2025. Management frames this as timing and mix (aeroderivatives carry fewer gigawatts per unit at higher dollars per kilowatt; unit count actually rose to 29 from 21 in Q2), which the 134% organic order growth supports. Both readings can be true simultaneously. What is not in dispute is that the stated capacity roadmap — 20 GW exit rate by end-Q3 2026, 24 GW in 2028, 30 GW by 2030 — has not begun to appear in shipments.

**Wind is a managed contraction.** Q2 orders were 147 turbines / 0.6 GW versus 381 / 1.6 GW. RPO is shrinking, R&D has been cut 35% from 2023, and roughly 60% of onshore equipment RPO sits in a US market where federal policy has turned unfavorable. At $600M–$1.3B of annual EBITDA drag against roughly $6.0B of FY2026E consolidated adjusted EBITDA, Wind is a 10–20% headwind to earnings power. Q3 breakeven guidance, if delivered, would be a meaningful positive surprise.

Estimate revisions are mildly positive: next quarter +6.3% over 30 days, next year +1.9% over 30 days and +2.6% over 90 days, current quarter −2.9%. The high-conviction revision signal (+30% over 90 days) is absent, and the earnings track record — one beat, one in-line, two misses in four quarters, including a 18.9% shortfall last quarter — argues for discounting rather than extrapolating consensus.

We treat these as corroboration rather than confirmation: at 44x forward EBITDA, analyst targets and the share price are drawing on the same AI-capex growth extrapolation, so proximity to target carries little independent information. Treating targets as floors applies only to stocks that look cheap against improving estimates; GEV does not.

Prior appreciation is not our objection. The stock is 14.9% below its July 6 high of $1,195.94 and 5.6% below the level at our last review, while guidance was raised twice in the interim — a modest improvement in entry. Our objection is the level of the properly-adjusted multiple against an earnings path that we model below consensus and that depends on a manufacturing ramp not yet in evidence.

**Valuation score: 4.5.** Demanding but not absurd for the growth; the asymmetry (13% to base, −25% to bear) does not support new capital.

## Competitive Landscape

Heavy-duty gas turbine capacity is currently the binding constraint on incremental global power supply, and there are effectively three Western suppliers: GEV, Siemens Energy and Mitsubishi Power. The 10-K also names Westinghouse, Framatome and Rolls-Royce in Power; Vestas, Siemens Gamesa, Nordex, Envision and Goldwind in Wind; and Hitachi Energy, Schneider Electric, Mitsubishi Electric and ABB in Electrification.

The pricing shift is documented rather than asserted: Power EBITDA margin expanded 480bp from FY2023 to FY2025 and roughly a further 290bp into H1 2026, and H1 2026 equipment orders priced more than 20% above Q4 2025. GEV's control of scarce manufacturing slots through 2030, with more than half of 2031 slots expected sold by year-end 2026, is the mechanism. Services RPO of $88.5B — of which Power is $72.4B, recognized over 10–15 years — annuitizes the installed base, though it embeds estimated life-of-contract sales rather than firm cash backlog.

Geographic mix is shifting toward policy-supported regions: FY2025 US revenue $17,341M (+18%, 45.6% of total) and Middle East & Africa $5,389M (+28.5%), against Europe −8.8% to $7,594M.

The competitive trajectory in Wind is the inverse: turbine orders fell from 2,290 units (FY2023) to 1,212 (FY2024) to 854 (FY2025), and the R&D reallocation reads as managed retreat toward a services-and-repower annuity. GEV has disclosed no divestiture intent, and the Tax Matters Agreement's business-continuity covenant names Gas Power specifically, not Wind.

Two competitive risks are underweighted in the current price. First, state-sponsored Chinese entrants improving quality and expanding outside their home markets, which the 10-K names explicitly. Second, the customer mix itself: the 10-K's newly emphasized language on "nontraditional customers, such as hyperscalers" warns that such counterparties "may have limited operating histories, different contracting practices, or weaker credit profiles… may depend on external financing, subsidies, or project milestones, and may delay payment, seek to renegotiate terms, or default." GEV's growth is concentrating in exactly the customer set its own filings flag as unfamiliar and financing-dependent.

## Risk Assessment

**Slot reservation conversion.** Per CNBC's account of the July 22 call, Gas Power backlog is 53 GW with a further 63 GW in slot reservation agreements. SRAs sit *outside* RPO, yet generate the deposits driving free cash flow. The 10-K states counterparties "may not place orders equal to the value of their reservation amount or at all." SRA terms — refundability, forfeiture, expiry, cash collected — are not disclosed in the filings, so downside cannot be quantified. Conversion has been running well (H1 orders converted at 20%+ higher pricing, implying counterparties would rather pay up than walk), but this is an unresolved evidence gap.

**Capacity outpacing demand.** The 10-K warns GEV "could be over-invested in our facilities and could incur excess or idle capacity, under-absorption of fixed costs, production inefficiencies, inventory build and write-downs, penalties under supply agreements, lower margins, and impairment of long-lived assets." The 30 GW target requires roughly doubling FY2025 output.

**Explicit AI-sentiment beta.** GEV now discloses in Item 1A that "because we manufacture and sell products used in AI infrastructure, our performance and the market price of our common stock are frequently linked to AI investment trends and sector sentiment." A company writing this into its risk factors is telling holders that the multiple is set by a variable outside its operational control. Against a backdrop where AI-capex durability is the dominant open macro question, this is the largest single source of unpriced downside.

**Prolec.** Total consideration of $9,656M allocated 98.2% to goodwill ($5,313M) and intangibles ($4,172M), with only about $400M of goodwill tax-deductible. Prolec generated $1,344M of revenue and a $166M pre-tax loss from February 2 to June 30, 2026, largely on inventory step-up and short-life trademark amortization that should unwind. Electrification D&A rose from $43M to $380M in H1 — roughly $675M annualized — which flatters adjusted EBITDA while depressing GAAP EPS. Prolec is excluded from FY2026 internal-control-over-financial-reporting assessment under the SEC's newly-acquired-business accommodation, creating a one-year control-assurance gap on roughly 7% of guided revenue.

**LTSA estimation.** With $72.4B of Power Services RPO recognized over 10–15 years on a young HA fleet (126 units, ~3.6M operating hours), modest changes in durability or cost assumptions move reported margins materially. Warranty liability stood at $1,595M at June 30, 2026, and three separate risk factors state that warranty, penalties and cost overruns "have represented, and may in the future represent, a meaningful portion of our expenses."

**IRS audit tail.** The Q2 10-Q newly discloses that GEV was informed by GE during Q1 2026 of an update to the IRS audit of GE's 2016–2020 consolidated returns, which could produce material indemnification obligations under the Tax Matters Agreement, "not reasonably estimable at this time and for which no liability has been accrued." Against $10.2B of net cash, most plausible outcomes are absorbable; the update itself is the signal.

**Tariffs.** Approximately $250M of FY2025 cost after mitigations, guided to $100–200M in 2026, against roughly $20B of annual purchasing across 100+ countries with sole-source exposure in semiconductors, specialty metals and rare earths.

**Entrenchment.** Classified board through 2029 with removal only for cause, no shareholder special meetings or written consent, board-only vacancy filling, blank-check preferred, Section 203 and exclusive Delaware forum, layered on top of Tax Matters Agreement restrictions. GEV is well insulated from external pressure.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $1,400 (+37.5%)

GEV controls one of three Western franchises in the binding constraint on global electricity supply, at a moment when data centers have become the marginal buyer in both generation and grid equipment. RPO of $176.3B covers 4.26x revenue; equipment RPO doubled year over year; Electrification equipment backlog crossed $40B. Pricing is up more than 20% on new orders, and management targets $200B of backlog in 2027. The 20 GW exit rate lands by end-Q3 2026 and scales to 24 GW in 2028, converting the order book into equipment revenue at the new pricing. Electrification holds 18–20% margins with Prolec's purchase-accounting drag unwinding. Wind reaches breakeven in Q3 as guided and stops consuming $600M–$1.3B annually. Net cash of $10.2B funds the ramp with no dilution, and $3.0B of remaining buyback authorization retires shares. FY2027 EPS reaches $23–25, and a 58–60x multiple — sustainable while capacity is sold out — gets to $1,400.

### Bear Case — $760 (−25.3%)

The prepayment tailwind reverses. Management's own guidance implies H2 2026 free cash flow of $1.6–2.6B, below H2 2025, and the market re-anchors on roughly $4B of normalized FCF rather than $12B of guided FCF. The 63 GW of slot reservations convert more slowly or at worse terms than the 53 GW of firm backlog, and 2027 orders decelerate as hyperscaler capex plans are trimmed — the risk the company itself discloses under AI-sentiment beta. The manufacturing ramp slips: H1's gigawatt decline proves to be capacity friction rather than mix, Power revenue continues to miss as it did in Q2, and fixed-cost absorption disappoints. Wind losses stay near the H1 run rate. Prolec integration disappoints against $9.75B of goodwill and negative tangible book, and an impairment follows. Cash taxes normalize toward book rates. The multiple compresses toward the 34.6x peer median on $22 of FY2027 EPS: $760.

### Base Case — $1,150 (+13.0%)

Orders decelerate from the extraordinary Q2 pace but stay above 1.5x book-to-bill; the 20 GW exit rate arrives roughly on schedule with modest slippage; Wind reaches approximately breakeven and stays there; Electrification holds guided margins as Prolec's step-up amortization rolls off. FY2027 adjusted EPS of $22, roughly 12% below consensus. At 52x — a premium to ETN's 38.5x justified by faster growth and oligopoly pricing, a discount to the current 66x on FY2026 — that is $1,150. Free cash flow normalizes toward $5–6B as prepayments and earnings converge.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** There is a specific, catalyst-linked 12-month appreciation case; the multiple caps its magnitude and the ramp evidence is not yet in hand.

- **Base case: $1,150** — 52x our FY2027 adjusted EPS estimate of $22 (built from $52–53B revenue, $8.3B adjusted EBITDA, $1.7B D&A, $450M pension income, $350M net interest, 25% tax, 262M shares). **+13.0% from $1,017.96.**
- **Bull case: $1,400** — approximately 60x on $23 of FY2027 EPS; requires visible progress toward 24 GW and Wind profitability. **+37.5%.**
- **Bear case: $760** — 34.6x peer-median multiple on $22. **−25.3%.**
- **Timeframe:** 12–18 months, anchored to the Q3 2026 print (late October, testing the 20 GW exit rate and Wind breakeven), the Q4/FY2026 print with FY2027 guidance (late January 2027), and the $200B backlog milestone management has targeted for 2027.
- **Reward/risk:** 0.5:1 to base, roughly 1.5:1 bull-to-bear. Insufficient for new capital in a sector where selection has not historically added alpha.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Gas turbine gigawatts delivered showing a genuine sequential ramp — H2 2026 shipments above 9.0 GW, versus 7.5 GW in H1 — confirming the 20 GW exit rate in shipped volume rather than stated capacity.
2. Wind EBITDA at or above breakeven for two consecutive quarters, removing a $600M–$1.3B annual drag from consolidated earnings.
3. Price toward $850–880, which would put the stock near 40x our FY2027 estimate and within 5% of the low analyst target, restoring roughly 30% upside to base against 12% downside.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Contract liabilities balance declining sequentially without a commensurate acceleration in equipment revenue recognition — the mechanical signature of prepayment reversal or SRA lapse.
2. Total RPO declining sequentially, or Gas Power slot reservations disclosed below 55 GW, indicating conversion failure rather than conversion at price.

4. Two or more hyperscalers guiding 2027 capex below 2026 levels, which would attack the demand assumption underlying the 30 GW roadmap.

Our score declines modestly to 6.15 from 6.3, continuing the trajectory of the past three reviews, and the reason is information rather than price. The business is better than it was two years ago by a wide margin, the balance sheet is a genuine asset, and the order book gives multi-year visibility that few industrials can match. What the price now requires is a doubling of gas turbine output by 2030 from a base that shrank in the first half of 2026. That is a specific, observable, quarterly-reportable test. Hold existing positions through the ramp; add only on delivery evidence or on a decline toward $850–880.
