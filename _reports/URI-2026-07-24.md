**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

United Rentals is the largest equipment-rental company in the world, with ~1,774 branches, a ~$23.8B fleet, and roughly 15% share of a fragmented North American market. The investment story over the past two quarters has reversed. The FY2025 10-K described a decelerating business (revenue +4.9% versus +7.1% in 2024) with contracting margins (adjusted EBITDA margin down 120bps, Specialty gross margin down 450bps). The Q2 2026 10-Q, filed July 22, reversed that read: revenue rose 11.8% to $4.41B, equipment rentals +12.7%, diluted EPS +21.1% to $12.03, General Rentals margin turned back up (+70bps to 35.8%), and management raised full-year guidance to $17.5–17.8B revenue and $7.975–8.125B adjusted EBITDA. Average fleet OEC grew 7.1% in the quarter, meaning URI is buying equipment again, a leading tell that management sees durable demand from data centers, semiconductor fabs, LNG/energy, and reshoring projects.

The stock has responded, running roughly 42% over the trailing year and jumping ~10.7% on the Q2 print to trade near $1,140, an all-time high. That is the crux of the HOLD. The operating recovery is real and confirmed by rising consensus estimates (current-year EPS revised +3.8% in 30 days) and a beat streak that resumed (Q1 +7.2%, Q2 +9.6% surprises after two prior misses). What is no longer compelling is the entry price. At ~27x trailing EPS and ~23x forward, URI trades below the peer median P/E of 30.1 but the peer set is heterogeneous, and the discount is warranted given sub-investment-grade leverage ($14.2B debt, Ba1/BB+), a two-sided cyclical exposure, and a narrowing goodwill cushion. Consensus targets (mean $1,164, median $1,165) sit only ~2% above the current price, and the stock is squarely mid-range against a $715 low and $1,550 high target.

We rate URI a HOLD (growth_hold) with a base-case fair value near $1,180. The business is high-quality, capital allocation is disciplined, and the demand cycle has legs, but the risk/reward is roughly balanced at current levels. We would become more constructive on a pullback into the low-$900s or on evidence that Specialty margins have stabilized and used-equipment residuals are holding.

## Company Overview

United Rentals operates the largest equipment-rental network in the world across all 49 continental U.S. states, every Canadian province, and a smaller footprint in Europe, Australia, and New Zealand. Its fleet carries an original equipment cost (OEC) of ~$23.8B across ~1.1M units with an average age of ~49 months. The business runs two segments:

- **General Rentals (~66% of revenue):** construction, aerial, industrial, and general tools/light equipment.
- **Specialty (~34% and growing):** trench safety, power/HVAC, fluid solutions, mobile storage/modular, and surface protection mats (via the March 2024 Yak acquisition). Specialty was ~7% of revenue in 2013 and is now ~32%.

Equipment rentals are ~86–87% of total revenue. End markets split roughly 48% industrial/non-construction, 48% commercial construction, and ~4% residential. Customer concentration is minimal: the largest customer is ~1% of revenue and the top 10 are ~5%, while key accounts, many using the proprietary Total Control fleet-management platform, drive ~69% of rental revenue, providing stickiness. URI's scale advantages come from purchasing power, cross-branch fleet-sharing logistics, national-account service, and established used-equipment resale channels. The main structural competitors are Herc Rentals and Ashtead's Sunbelt; the remaining ~85% of the market is a fragmented tail of regional and independent operators, giving URI a long runway for organic share gains and bolt-on M&A.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue: deceleration then reacceleration.** FY2023 revenue was $14.33B (+23% including the Ahern deal), FY2024 $15.35B (+7.1%), and FY2025 $16.10B (+4.9%), a clear slowdown. That trajectory reversed in 2026: Q2 revenue rose 11.8% to $4.41B, equipment rentals +12.7%, with average OEC +7.1% and fleet productivity +3.4%. Specialty rentals grew 24.8% in the quarter, and General Rentals +6.6%. First-half revenue was $8.40B (+9.6%). Management's raised full-year guidance implies a return to high-single/low-double-digit growth.

**Profitability and margins.** FY2025 net income was $2.494B (−3.1%), diluted EPS $38.61, net margin 15.5% (down ~130bps), and adjusted EBITDA margin 45.5% (down 120bps). The FY2025 margin pressure was concentrated in Specialty gross margin, which fell 450bps to 43.6% on Yak-related depreciation, higher delivery costs from fleet repositioning, and a shift toward lower-margin ancillary revenue; General Rentals slipped 90bps to 35.2% on labor and delivery inflation. Q2 2026 marked a turn: net margin rose to ~17.1%, adjusted EBITDA margin to 46.6%, and General Rentals margin improved to 35.8% (+70bps) on fixed-cost absorption as volume returned. Specialty margin remained soft at 44.4% (−140bps) on ancillary/re-rent mix, but grew gross-profit dollars strongly.

**Cash flow and capital intensity.** This is an asset-heavy model: gross rental capex was ~$4.15B in 2025. Free cash flow (per the financial-health screen) was ~$4.81B (FCF margin ~29.9%). That FCF funds a large capital-return program, and conversion looks healthy relative to the capital intensity of the business.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt is $14.2B, ~29% variable rate; a 1-point rate move is ~$30M after-tax earnings impact. Net debt/EBITDA runs roughly ~1.9x adjusted EBITDA, appropriate for an asset-backed rental company whose debt is collateralized by a liquid, resaleable fleet. Ratings are Ba1 (Moody's) / BB+ (S&P), sub-investment-grade, but S&P moved the outlook to Positive by July 2026. The Altman Z-Score of 1.65 nominally lands in the "distress zone," but that screen penalizes the high leverage and low working-capital ratio inherent to the rental model; interest coverage (~5.5x EBIT/interest), covenant headroom, and ~$3B liquidity indicate a financially sound issuer. We treat the Z-score as a false negative here.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on the ongoing mega-project construction cycle — data centers, semiconductor fabs, LNG/energy infrastructure, and reshoring/manufacturing — which favors the largest rental players who can supply broad fleets to complex jobsites. URI's Specialty segment (power/HVAC, fluid solutions) is directly levered to this build-out, which explains the +24.8% Specialty rental growth in Q2.

The clearest confirmation is management's decision to grow fleet again: +7.1% average OEC in Q2, fleet OEC now ~$23.8B versus $22.48B at year-end 2025. Companies do not add capital-intensive fleet into weakening demand. Estimate revisions support the reacceleration: current-year EPS was revised +3.8% over 30 days and +3.5% over 90 days to $49.00, next-year to $54.07 (+2.2% over 90 days). These are positive but modest revisions — well short of the +30% signal threshold — reflecting a business recovering from a shallow slowdown rather than a trough. The earnings track record resumed its beat pattern (Q1 +7.2%, Q2 +9.6%) after two consecutive misses in late 2025 (−5.8%, −6.8%), which raises confidence in near-term consensus but does not yet establish a durable multi-year acceleration.

We score Growth Prospects at 6.8: the reacceleration is genuine and cycle-confirmed, but this is late-cycle, rate-sensitive demand, and the revision velocity is directional rather than explosive.

## Valuation Assessment

URI trades at ~27x trailing EPS ($38.61) and ~23x consensus current-year EPS ($49.00), against a peer-group median P/E of 30.1 (Fastenal 41.1x, Watsco 31.5x, Applied Industrial 30.1x, Wesco 24.5x, Core & Main 21.2x). On a headline basis URI looks cheaper than the median, but the peer set is heterogeneous: Fastenal and Watsco are asset-light distributors carrying structurally higher multiples, while Wesco and Core & Main are lower-multiple, more cyclical names closer to URI's economics. Adjusted for URI's sub-IG leverage and cyclicality, the ~23x forward multiple is fair, not cheap.

The cycle-adjusted lens matters. URI is emerging from a shallow slowdown, not a trough, so forward earnings power is the right anchor. On the raised guidance and consensus $49 EPS, the stock sits at ~23x, a reasonable multiple for a company guiding to double-digit revenue growth and mid-40s EBITDA margins. But the estimate-revision magnitude is modest, so analyst targets should not be treated as floors. Target spread signals genuine two-sided uncertainty rather than a mispricing the market has missed.

The 42% trailing-year run and the all-time-high print are not, by themselves, reasons to mark down the valuation; appreciation driven by the demand recovery is warranted. We hold the valuation score at 5.2 because there is no identified catalyst that would drive a re-rating from here: the recovery is now consensus, the multiple already reflects it, and the residual/leverage risks cap the upside. This is a well-priced quality compounder near fair value, not an asymmetric opportunity. Cheapness relative to a mixed peer median is not sufficient support for a higher score.

## Competitive Landscape

URI's competitive position is stable-to-improving. Its ~15% North American share leads a fragmented industry where ~85% of the market sits outside the top players, giving durable structural advantages: fleet breadth, national-account coverage, cross-branch fleet-sharing logistics, the Total Control software platform, and established used-equipment channels. Key accounts drive revenue stickiness that smaller competitors cannot replicate.

The current up-cycle structurally favors scale players because mega-projects require broad, complex fleets delivered reliably to large jobsites, a capability the largest three rental firms possess and independents do not. Specialty growth demonstrates URI is winning share in the highest-value, most-differentiated categories. The main competitive tension is the Herc/Ashtead/Sunbelt consolidation dynamic; Herc winning H&E in early 2025 (after URI walked away) tightened the top tier, but URI's decision to redirect that capital to buybacks rather than overpay preserved returns. We score Competitive Position at 7.3, down modestly from the 8.5 peak of early 2026 to reflect the narrowing goodwill headroom (reporting units exceeded carrying value by ≥32% in the Oct 2025 test, down from ≥60% a year earlier) and the ongoing Specialty margin dilution as URI takes on more lower-margin ancillary and services revenue.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Cyclicality (most important).** Costs are largely fixed, so a construction/industrial downturn compresses margins quickly. Demand is strong now, but this is a late-cycle, rate-sensitive business, and a higher-for-longer rate path is a genuine tail risk if mega-project momentum stalls.

2. **Leverage and refinancing.** $14.2B debt at sub-IG ratings; December 2025 notes were issued at 5⅜% versus 3¾% in 2021, illustrating rising refinancing cost. The ~29% variable-rate exposure adds sensitivity in a higher-for-longer environment.

3. **Used-equipment residual normalization.** Gains on sale of rental equipment are declining (FY2025 sales-of-rental-equipment margin ~44.9%, down from ~50% in 2023) as the post-COVID used market normalizes. A soft used market pressures both reported margin and fleet-refresh economics, a deterioration not fully captured by the reacceleration narrative.

4. **Specialty margin mix.** The growth engine carries structurally lower-margin ancillary/delivery revenue, diluting rental gross margin even as it grows dollars. Watch whether Specialty margins stabilize as Yak integration matures.

5. **Tariffs and equipment cost inflation.** Repeatedly flagged; tariffs raise replacement capex and could pressure returns if not passed through.

6. **Goodwill.** $7.1–7.2B, with the impairment cushion narrowing from ≥60% to ≥32% headroom year-over-year, still passing comfortably, but a variable to monitor if the cycle turns.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term options are pricing elevated event risk. ATM IV is 50.8% versus 42.4% trailing historical vol (rich, 79th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (50.8% near-term versus 36.9% at six months), signaling the market expects imminent stress, consistent with the July 22 earnings print. Put/call skew of 12.1% shows elevated near-term put demand. ATM IV collapsed from 103.9% to 50.8% since the mid-June snapshot as the earnings binary approached and partially resolved. Short interest is low at 2.7% of float (2.7 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short setup. The elevated near-term put skew is a modest caution flag against the constructive operating story, but the low SI and post-earnings IV normalization suggest positioning is not extreme.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$1,400–1,550)
The mega-project demand cycle (data centers, fabs, LNG, reshoring) proves multi-year rather than late-cycle, driving sustained double-digit rental growth. Specialty margins stabilize as Yak integration matures, General Rentals fixed-cost absorption continues to lift margins, and adjusted EBITDA margin recovers toward 47%+. The $5.0B buyback authorization (plan $1.5B in 2026) shrinks the share count meaningfully, and S&P delivers the ratings upgrade the Positive outlook implies, lowering refinancing cost. At ~26–28x a rising ~$56 EPS, the stock reaches $1,450–1,550.

### Bear Case (~$715–850)
The rate-sensitive construction/industrial cycle rolls over. Fixed costs compress margins fast, used-equipment residuals continue to soften (further hurting both margin and fleet economics), and specialty mix dilution deepens. Higher-for-longer rates raise the cost of the debt stack, and the narrowing goodwill cushion triggers an impairment scare. Earnings fall toward $40 and the multiple compresses to ~18x, taking the stock to the low-$700s, the analyst low target.

### Base Case (~$1,180)
Demand holds at high-single/low-double-digit growth through the raised guidance range, margins recover modestly, and the company executes on buybacks. On ~$52–54 forward EPS at ~22–23x, the stock is worth roughly $1,180, approximately in line with the current price and consensus mean/median.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold). Reassessment timeline: next earnings (Q3 2026, ~late October) and any change in the mega-project demand read.**

The score trajectory (7.0 → 7.0 → 6.2, now 6.3) reflects a business that improved fundamentally while the stock ran ~42%, compressing the risk/reward. New data confirms the operating recovery but does not reverse the valuation constraint; the recommendation history (HOLD → BUY → HOLD) has cycled with price, and the current HOLD reflects a fully-valued quality name rather than deterioration. We are explicitly not marking scores down for the price rise, but the asymmetry has narrowed.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback into the low-$900s to mid-$900s on no fundamental deterioration, restoring 20%+ upside to base case.
- Specialty gross margin re-expands for two consecutive quarters (toward 46%+) and used-equipment sale margins stabilize above ~45%, evidencing the mix and residual headwinds have troughed.
- S&P delivers the ratings upgrade (BB+ → BBB−), lowering refinancing cost and validating the balance-sheet resilience.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Equipment-rental revenue growth decelerates below +3% YoY for two consecutive quarters, signaling the mega-project cycle is rolling over.
- A guidance cut or a goodwill-impairment charge as the headroom narrows further below the ≥32% cushion.
- Fleet OEC growth turns negative (management shrinking fleet), the clearest signal management sees demand deteriorating.

## Conclusion

United Rentals is a high-quality, well-managed compounder that just delivered a convincing operating reacceleration: Q2 revenue +11.8%, EPS +21.1%, raised guidance, resumed beats, and fleet growth signaling confidence in the mega-project cycle. Capital allocation is disciplined: the H&E walk-away and $64M break-up fee, the $5.0B buyback authorization, and the dividend raise to $7.88 annualized all point to a shareholder-friendly team, though the absence of open-market insider buying and CEO selling near $985 temper the enthusiasm. The balance sheet is sound despite the misleading distress-zone Altman screen.

The constraint is price. After a 42% run to an all-time high, the stock trades at ~23x forward earnings with consensus targets only ~2% above spot and a wide $715–$1,550 target range that reflects genuine two-sided cyclical and rate risk. The recovery is now consensus, the multiple reflects it, and the used-equipment residual and Specialty-margin overhangs cap the upside. This is a fair-value, roughly balanced risk/reward. We rate URI a HOLD and would look to add on a pullback into the low-$900s or on confirmation that Specialty margins and used-equipment residuals have stabilized.
