## Executive Summary

International Seaways is one of the largest independent tanker operators globally, with 70 vessels (8.4M DWT) spanning VLCCs, Suezmaxes, Aframaxes, and product carriers, plus 4 LR1 newbuilds delivering in 2026. The stock has rallied 137% over the past year from ~$31 to ~$74, driven by a Q4 2025 rate surge (VLCC spot rates more than doubled YoY), the Hormuz naval blockade, and the non-renewal of both Iranian and Russian oil waivers, all of which structurally increase tanker ton-mile demand.

INSW's 82% spot market exposure makes it a high-beta beneficiary of this environment: the active US naval blockade of Hormuz is physically disrupting crude oil transit, oil above $100/bbl incentivizes longer-haul trade route substitution, and increased sanctions enforcement against the shadow fleet is removing non-compliant tonnage from the market. The balance sheet is exceptionally strong (Altman Z-Score 3.79, D/E 0.32, $724M total liquidity), providing both downside protection and the capacity for countercyclical acquisitions.

Consensus EPS estimates have been revised upward by 46–56% over the past 90 days, and at ~12x trailing earnings with FY2026E EPS of $7.14, INSW trades at a meaningful discount to peer median P/E of 20.7x. The primary risk remains tanker rate cyclicality: the newbuild orderbook has expanded to 17% of the existing fleet. However, current geopolitical and supply-demand fundamentals support sustained rate strength through at least H2 2026.

## Company Overview

International Seaways outsources commercial management to six specialized pools and technical operations to two third-party managers, allowing the company to run with just 66 shoreside staff overseeing 2,697 seafarers. The fleet consists of 29 crude tankers (12 VLCCs, 13 Suezmaxes, 4 Aframaxes) and 41 product carriers (1 LR2, 7 LR1s, 33 MRs), with 62 owned and 8 chartered-in.

The January 2026 acquisition of the remaining 50% of Tankers International (TI), the leading independent VLCC pool, gives INSW control of the commercial platform managing its VLCC fleet and has enabled expansion into a new Suezmax pool. INSW now earns management fees from third-party participants while gaining direct control over commercial decisions.

Revenue mix is approximately 82% spot market and 18% time charters. The company also operates a niche Crude Tankers Lightering business providing ship-to-ship transfer services in the US Gulf, Grand Bahama, and Panama.

Full-year FY2025 reflects a cyclical downturn from the 2023 peak: revenue declined 21%, net income dropped 44%, driven primarily by product carrier rate weakness (MR averages fell from ~$31K/day to ~$24K/day; LR1s from ~$50K to ~$37K). However, Q4 2025 marked a sharp reversal: net income surged to $128M versus $36M in Q4 2024 (+257%), as VLCC spot rates jumped to $75,566/day (from $35,572 in Q4 2024) and Suezmax rates reached $52,802/day (from $29,700).

INSW has beaten consensus EPS in 4 consecutive quarters, with surprises of +26.3%, +5.6%, +21.6%, and +25.4%. This pattern suggests analyst estimates systematically underestimate INSW's earnings power during rate upswings.

### Balance Sheet

INSW's balance sheet is among the strongest in the tanker sector:

- **Altman Z-Score:** 3.79 (Safe Zone)
- **Current Ratio:** 3.71
- **Debt-to-Equity:** 0.32
- **Net Debt/Capitalization:** 16.5%
- **Total Liquidity:** $724M ($167M cash + $557M undrawn revolver)
- **Net Loan-to-Value:** ~13%
- **Unencumbered Vessels:** 31 of 69 (44%)

The September 2025 Norwegian bond issuance ($250M at 7.125%, maturing 2030) was used to exercise purchase options on 6 VLCCs previously under an Ocean Yield sale-leaseback at SOFR+405bps. While the coupon is relatively high, the transaction eliminated $22M/year in mandatory amortization, unencumbered 6 VLCCs, and replaced floating-rate exposure with fixed-rate debt. Total debt maturities are well-spread between 2030 and 2037.

### Cash Flow and Capital Allocation

OCF was $380M in FY2025 ($547M in FY2024). Since 2020, INSW has returned over $1 billion to shareholders through dividends and buybacks. The Q4 2025 supplemental dividend of $2.03/share, the largest in company history, representing 87% of adjusted net income, signals management's confidence in current rate strength. Including the regular $0.12 quarterly payment, the total Q4 dividend was $2.15/share.

Remaining newbuild commitments are modest at ~$189M for 4 LR1s, of which ~$158M is financed via ECA facility. The fleet renewal strategy has been executed well: 12 older vessels sold in 2025 for ~$246M (recognizing $42.5M in gains), with another 7 vessels agreed for sale in early 2026 for ~$216M (expected gains ~$80M).

### Carrying Value Risk

Nine MRs have carrying values exceeding estimated market values by $43.3M in aggregate, and 1 VLCC exceeds by $1.7M. No impairment has been recorded as management's undiscounted cash flow estimates support the carrying values. This warrants monitoring: if MR rates remain depressed, write-downs are possible.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis for INSW is primarily rate-driven, with secondary contributions from fleet optimization and the TI platform acquisition.

**Near-term rate catalysts:**
- The active US naval blockade of Hormuz is physically disrupting crude transit. Two tankers have been interdicted. This directly increases tanker demand through longer routing and supply rerouting.
- Non-renewal of both Iranian and Russian oil waivers compresses supply from two major producers simultaneously, forcing buyers to source crude from more distant origins (West Africa, US Gulf, Latin America), increasing ton-mile demand.
- Continued Red Sea/Houthi disruption forcing Cape of Good Hope rerouting adds ~10-14 sailing days per voyage.
- Chinese crude imports +4.4% in 2025, with December 2025 at 13.2M b/d (+17% YoY), critical for VLCC demand.

**Estimate trajectory:** Current quarter EPS estimates revised up 46.6% over 90 days to $2.25. Next quarter up 55.6% to $1.60. FY2026E stands at $7.14 (up 46% from 90 days ago), with FY2027E at $4.41 (up 53.5%). The FY2027 step-down reflects consensus expectations that rate strength will moderate, but this assumption may be too conservative given ongoing geopolitical disruption.

**Supply-side concern:** The newbuild orderbook has expanded to 17% of the existing fleet (from 7% two years ago), with a VLCC orderbook surge of 19M dwt. This is the primary structural headwind to sustained rate strength over a 2-3 year horizon. Management counters that nearly half the global fleet will reach 20 years of age by the time these newbuilds deliver, and sanctions enforcement targeting non-compliant tonnage exceeds the orderbook size. Both points are valid but require sustained regulatory enforcement to hold.

**TI platform value:** The full consolidation of Tankers International creates a recurring management fee stream, enhanced market intelligence through controlling the commercial operations of third-party VLCC and Suezmax owners, and a modest competitive moat. The financial impact is not yet quantified in estimates but should provide incremental EBITDA contribution.

INSW trades at a significant discount to the peer median P/E. At ~10.4x FY2026E EPS, the valuation embeds substantial skepticism about rate sustainability. Even applying a cyclical discount for 82% spot exposure, INSW appears inexpensive relative to peers like Antero Midstream (20.7x) or DT Midstream (27.8x), which are admittedly more stable midstream businesses.

The dividend yield appears low at 0.66% because it reflects only the regular quarterly $0.12/share. Including the Q4 supplemental dividend (annualized from recent run-rate), the effective yield is substantially higher, though supplemental dividends are inherently variable. At Q4 2025 payout rates (87% of adjusted net income), a $7.14 FY2026E EPS would translate to roughly $6.21/share in total dividends, or an ~8.4% yield at $74.

The most directly comparable peers (LPG at 8.6x, KNTK at 13.7x) suggest a tanker-specific P/E range of 8-14x is appropriate. At 10.4x forward, INSW is in the lower half of this range despite improving rate fundamentals and the TI acquisition.

**Deutsche Bank's $80 target** (raised from $63 in March 2026) implies modest upside from current levels. The analyst consensus mean of $75.20 is essentially at the current price, with a range of $56-$90.

## Competitive Landscape

The tanker industry is highly fragmented; no single owner controls rates. INSW competes with Frontline, DHT, Euronav, Teekay Tankers, Nordic American, and Scorpio Tankers, as well as integrated oil companies with captive fleets and state-owned shipping lines. INSW's competitive advantages include:

1. **Fleet diversification** spanning VLCCs to MRs, allowing participation across multiple rate environments
2. **Balance sheet strength** enabling countercyclical fleet renewal
3. **TI platform ownership** providing commercial control and fee income
4. **Low operating cost structure** through third-party management and pool participation

The competitive disadvantage is that tanker shipping has no structural moat: vessels are largely fungible commodities, and the industry is subject to periodic overbuilding.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**1. Tanker Rate Cyclicality (HIGH).** Management explicitly states that geopolitical instability has "significantly benefitted" results and normalization could cause rates to "decline significantly." The current rate environment is historically elevated and could reverse if a comprehensive peace deal is reached in the Middle East, sanctions enforcement relaxes, or newbuilds deliver into weakening demand.

**2. Newbuild Orderbook (MODERATE-HIGH).** The orderbook expanded from 7% to 17% of the fleet over two years. While scrapping and aging offset some delivery pressure, the supply pipeline creates a medium-term ceiling on rate strength. Most deliveries are concentrated in 2027-2028.

**3. China-US Port Fee Regulations (MODERATE).** INSW owns 13 Chinese-built vessels (9 above the 55K dwt threshold). The mutual fees were suspended for one year in November 2025, but reinstatement would impose meaningful voyage costs on approximately 20% of the fleet.

**4. Tax Structure Risk (MODERATE).** The Bermuda redomiciliation for OECD Pillar Two compliance requires demonstrating strategic management is effectively conducted from Bermuda. If challenged, the tax impact would be material given INSW currently pays virtually zero income taxes. Separately, Section 883 exemption depends on ownership concentration thresholds.

**5. Carrying Value Gap (LOW-MODERATE).** The $43.3M carrying value excess on 9 MRs could result in impairment charges if product carrier rates don't recover. This would be a non-cash charge but would reduce book value.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 70.3% versus 36.5% 1-year historical volatility, a 33.7pp spread indicating the market is pricing significant near-term uncertainty. The term structure is in steep backwardation (70.3% near-term vs. 50.0% at 6 months), consistent with event-driven risk pricing around the current geopolitical environment. Put/call OI ratio of 0.21 suggests heavily call-biased positioning, which aligns with the bullish rate thesis but also creates crowding risk if rates disappoint. The 84th percentile IV rank means options are expensive; covered call or collar strategies could be appropriate for managing the elevated premium environment.

Short interest at 4.6% with 2.3 days to cover is low and uncrowded, providing no meaningful squeeze risk but also indicating the bearish camp is not well-represented.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($90-100)

The Hormuz blockade persists or escalates. Dual oil waiver non-renewal compresses global supply, forcing buyers to source crude from longer-haul origins. VLCC rates sustain $70K+/day through 2026. FY2026 EPS reaches $8-9 (above consensus) as rate strength proves more durable than expected. Sanctions enforcement removes shadow fleet tonnage faster than newbuilds deliver. The TI platform acquisition generates $15-20M in incremental EBITDA from third-party management fees. INSW re-rates to 11-12x forward earnings. Total dividends (regular + supplemental) exceed $7/share for a ~9-10% effective yield. The expiring poison pill (April 2026) opens the door to activist or strategic interest.

### Bear Case ($45-55)

A comprehensive Middle East de-escalation materializes within 3-6 months. Oil falls to $80-85. Tanker rates correct sharply: VLCCs return to $40K-50K/day, product carriers below $20K/day. FY2026 EPS comes in at $4-5 (well below consensus). The newbuild orderbook pressures rates further in 2027-2028. China-US port fees are reimposed, adding $2-3M per year in costs for affected vessels. MR carrying value impairments of $30-40M reduce book value. INSW de-rates to 8-9x trough earnings.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $90** — 12.5x FY2026E EPS of $7.14 = $89, rounded to $90. This assumes rates moderate from Q4 2025 peaks but remain elevated relative to FY2025 averages, consistent with ongoing Hormuz disruption and sanctions enforcement.

**Bull case target: $100** — 12x $8.50 FY2026E EPS (reflecting rate upside) = $102.

**Bear case target: $50** — 10x $5.00 trough earnings = $50. Represents ~33% downside from current levels.

**Timeframe:** 6-12 months. Primary catalysts: (1) Q1 2026 earnings (late May 2026) will confirm whether Q4 2025 rate strength extended into Q1; (2) pace of Hormuz blockade evolution; (3) Q2 2026 earnings and tanker rate trajectory through summer.

**Upside/downside from current ($74.49):** +21% to base case, +34% to bull case, -33% to bear case. Risk/reward is approximately 2:1 at current levels (21% upside vs. 33% downside with higher probability-weighted upside given active blockade).

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. VLCC spot rates fall below $45K/day for two consecutive months, indicating the rate cycle has turned decisively
2. A comprehensive Middle East peace deal with concrete de-escalation of the Hormuz blockade (specific chokepoint reopening, not rhetoric)
3. FY2026 consensus EPS estimates revised down more than 20% from current $7.14 level

## Conclusion

INSW is a well-managed, well-capitalized tanker operator with direct exposure to the most significant crude oil supply disruption in decades. At ~10.4x FY2026E earnings with estimates rising 46-56% over 90 days, the valuation does not fully reflect the probability-weighted rate environment created by concurrent Hormuz, Iran waiver, and Red Sea disruptions.

The TI platform acquisition adds strategic value that the market has not yet priced. The expiring poison pill creates optionality around activist or M&A interest. Consensus estimates appear conservative given the 4-quarter track record of systematic beats.

The stock is appropriate for investors with tolerance for cyclical volatility and a 6-12 month horizon. The primary risk, a rapid normalization of tanker rates through geopolitical de-escalation, has diminished given the absence of scheduled peace talks, the escalation from blockade declaration to physical enforcement, and the simultaneous Russian waiver non-renewal. BUY with a $90 base case target.
