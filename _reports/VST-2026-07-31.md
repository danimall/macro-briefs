## Company Overview

Vistra is an integrated competitive retail electricity and power generation company operating in deregulated U.S. markets. Despite the utilities sector label, there is no rate base and no regulated return; earnings come from the spread between wholesale generation economics and retail/wholesale sales, actively hedged with derivatives.

Five reportable segments: Retail; Texas (ERCOT generation); East (PJM, MISO, ISO-NE, NYISO); West (CAISO); and Asset Closure. Retail sells electricity and gas in 16 states plus D.C. through TXU Energy, Ambit, Dynegy, Energy Harbor and other brands, delivering 30,109 GWh in Q1 2026, down from 33,323 GWh a year earlier on a warm Texas winter (heating degree days 73–77% of normal versus 105–113% in Q1 2025). Generation spans nuclear (Comanche Peak in ERCOT; Beaver Valley, Perry, Davis-Besse in PJM), combined-cycle and peaking gas, declining coal, and solar/storage.

The structural advantage described in the filings is the use of the retail book as a sales channel for generation output, reducing reliance on third-party hedge counterparties and associated collateral posting. Neither Constellation nor Talen carries a comparable retail franchise, which is also why $2,688M of Vistra's $2,810M of goodwill sits in the Retail reporting unit.

Coal is in managed decline: 4,578 MW of announced retirements through mid-2028 (Baldwin, Coleto Creek, Kincaid, Newton, Miami Fort), with Coleto Creek and Miami Fort earmarked for gas repowering. Repowering reuses existing interconnection rights, which are the scarcest asset in the current market.

## Financial Analysis

**GAAP earnings are not the operating signal.** FY2025 net income attributable to Vistra fell 64% to $944M while Adjusted EBITDA rose 5.4% to $5,838M. Q1 2026 net income swung +$1,297M year over year to $1,029M, and the 10-Q attributes $1,290M of that swing to the change in unrealized commodity hedging — a $723M unrealized gain in Q1 2026 against a $567M unrealized loss in Q1 2025. Segment GAAP figures are equally unusable: Retail booked −$724M and Texas +$2,091M in Q1 2026 purely because intersegment hedging places a $765M unrealized loss in Retail against a $1,722M unrealized gain in Texas.

The supplied profile data reflects this distortion. The stated trailing P/E of 25.1x implies an EPS near $5.92, consistent with last-twelve-months GAAP EPS inflated by the Q1 2026 hedging gain, while the separately supplied EPS of $2.18 corresponds to FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS (which would put the trailing multiple at 68x). Neither is a usable anchor. We use consensus of $9.95 (2026) and $11.10 (2027).

**The underlying Q1 operating bridge.** Adjusted EBITDA +$259M splits into +$445M favorable realized revenue net of fuel (higher PJM/ISO-NE realized capacity prices, better Texas energy margins, Lotus plants), −$79M retail margin from excess volumes sold at lower wholesale prices, −$23M weather-driven retail consumption, −$57M higher plant operating/outage costs, and −$27M higher SG&A. Retail Adjusted EBITDA fell 63% to $68M from $184M — a genuine decline on a small base against a much warmer Texas winter.

**Balance sheet.** At March 31, 2026: total assets $41,308M, liabilities $35,698M, equity $5,610M of which $2,476M is preferred liquidation preference, leaving roughly $3,134M of common book equity. Long-term debt including current portion was $19,163M, plus $750M of receivables financing and $641M of forward repurchase obligation to Nuveen. Gross debt to FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA is roughly 3.3x, or about 3.7x including preferred.

Screens returning an Altman Z-Score near 0.76 and debt/equity above 7x are measuring presentation, not solvency. Current liabilities include $4,209M of gross commodity derivative liabilities against $3,186M of current derivative assets; after offsetting instruments and collateral, the total net derivative position was −$1,044M. Book common equity is mechanically depressed by $7,303M of treasury stock from buybacks. Meanwhile, S&P moved Vistra Operations to BBB− on December 2, 2025 and the unsecured notes to BBB− on March 20, 2026, and Fitch upgraded to BBB− on March 16, 2026. Liquidity was $4,173M at March 31, 2026, up $1,390M from year-end.

The genuine vulnerability the screen gestures at is real but conditional: the collateral release reverts if either agency withdraws or downgrades below investment grade, subject to a 60-day grace period, and collateral posting scales with commodity prices ($1,516M of cash and eligible assets plus $3,189M of letters of credit posted at March 31, 2026, with $677M of uncollateralized exposure under credit-contingent features). A ratings reversal coinciding with a commodity price spike is the stress scenario worth modeling.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $4,070M against $2,752M of capital expenditures including nuclear fuel, for $1,318M of free cash flow after all capex — a 2.6% FCF yield on market cap, though a substantial share of capex is growth (solar, storage, uprates, new gas) rather than maintenance. Cash taxes remain negligible: zero federal income tax paid in Q1 2026 and Q1 2025, with guidance of roughly $9M federal and $65M state over the next twelve months and no expected corporate AMT exposure in 2026.

## Growth Analysis

**Contracted nuclear is the durable growth layer.** Two 20-year PPAs total 3,809 MW: AWS takes 1,200 MW of Comanche Peak output beginning Q4 2027 and ramping to full capacity by 2032; Meta takes 2,609 MW from the PJM fleet (1,268 MW Perry, 908 MW Davis-Besse existing, plus 433 MW of uprates), with partial delivery starting late 2026 and full operating delivery by year-end 2027. Of the total, 3,376 MW comes from existing capacity running at 92–101% capacity factors, converting a large slice of merchant nuclear output into contracted revenue. Perry's NRC license renewal through 2046 was a prerequisite and was approved in July 2025. Nuclear fuel is onshore and in inventory for 2026–2027 and contracted through 2030 without Russian deliveries.

No pricing is disclosed for either PPA, and the aggregate uprate capital cost is undisclosed. This is the central valuation unknown. Related caution: the $7,474M "remaining performance obligations" figure in the Q1 10-Q is a capacity-only, fixed-and-determinable measure and is not a contracted-revenue backlog; the energy components of the AWS and Meta PPAs are not visible in it. Any valuation treating $7.5B as the contracted value of the hyperscaler deals is wrong by an unknown margin.

**Inorganic growth is deliberately PJM/ISO-NE weighted.** Lotus closed October 22, 2025 (2,600 MW, $1,231M final price, no goodwill recorded). Cogentrix — signed December 31, 2025, expected to close in H2 2026 — adds 5,500 MW across five PJM facilities, four in ISO-NE and one in ERCOT for approximately $2.3B cash net of $1.5B assumed debt plus 5,000,000 shares at an agreed $185. Implied enterprise value of roughly $4.7B on 5,500 MW is about $855/kW, materially below new-build combined-cycle cost, which is the correct frame for a market where interconnection queues and supply-chain constraints are the binding limit on new supply.

Dilution is immaterial: 5.0M shares against 337.2M outstanding is a 1.48% issuance, leaving existing holders with 98.54% of an enlarged 342.2M count. Because the contract values shares at $185 against a $148.62 market price, Vistra transfers stock worth roughly $743M against $925M of agreed value — an apparent economic benefit near $182M, conditional on the share count being fixed rather than collared. That contract term is unverified.

**Organic pipeline.** Up to 2,000 MW planned in ERCOT: 860 MW of west Texas peakers targeted for 2028 (Texas Energy Fund loan diligence "in final stages," with the filing explicit that diligence is not an award), the Coleto Creek repowering up to 600 MW, and over 500 MW of summer uprates at existing gas plants. Permian gas at −$1.05/MMBtu in Q1 2026 supports very cheap fuel for west Texas generation. Offsetting this, management discloses that supply-chain and labor constraints "led to the deferral or abandonment of some planned capital expenditures for our solar and battery projects."

**Estimate revisions are mixed in an informative way.** Full-year 2026 consensus is up 11.3% over 90 days and 1.2% over 30 days. But the current quarter was cut 18.4% in 30 days to $1.75 from $2.15, while the next quarter was raised 10.3% to $2.59. The two-quarter sum moved from $4.49 to $4.34, a 3.3% reduction — largely intra-year timing and weather mix rather than a deterioration in the year. Next-year consensus slipped 2.7% to $11.10. This does not meet the threshold of a strong inflection-driven buy signal (no +30% revision velocity, and the near-term cut is real), but it does contradict any claim that the 32% drawdown reflects falling estimates.

The reported earnings surprise record — one beat, three misses across four quarters — should be discounted heavily. The 112% Q1 2026 "beat" was the unrealized hedging gain; the 77% Q4 2025 "miss" was the reverse. What the record does establish is that consensus has poor visibility into Vistra's reported EPS, which raises the variance around the August 7 print.

## Valuation Assessment

At $148.62, Vistra trades at 14.9x 2026 consensus of $9.95 and 13.4x 2027 consensus of $11.10, for a PEG of roughly 1.29 on 11.6% forward earnings growth. The dividend is $0.92 annualized for a 0.62% yield — a rounding error, not a valuation support.

Peer medians in the supplied set are 17.4x P/E and 3.4% yield (ETR 28.1x, EXC 16.6x, PEG 17.4x, WEC 22.5x, EIX 7.9x). Those are rate-regulated utilities growing EPS 5–8% with authorized returns; the comparison is directionally useful in that Vistra trades at a 14% discount to the median on a materially higher growth rate, but it is not the right cohort. The proper comparables are Constellation (pure-play nuclear, trading at a premium), Talen (smaller, more concentrated PJM merchant) and NRG. Merchant IPPs carry structural discounts to regulated utilities because earnings are unhedged beyond a two-year horizon, and Vistra's own disclosures quantify that: the 2027 book is 41% open in Texas gas, 32% open in East nuclear/renewable/coal and 44% open in West gas.

The decisive valuation question is whether the earnings base is normalized or cyclically inflated. Q1 2026 East gross capacity revenue rose to $321M from $73M, a 4.4x increase reflecting PJM capacity auction escalation, and East Adjusted EBITDA rose 56% to $801M while Texas rose 20% to $586M. PJM capacity prices are auction-cleared and have cleared at or near administrative caps in recent auctions. If capacity clearing prices mean-revert meaningfully, we would expect $1.0–1.5B of the EBITDA base to be at risk, implying normalized EPS closer to $7.50–8.00 and a de-risked multiple of 18.6–19.8x — full, not cheap, for a merchant generator.

That range brackets our judgment. On consensus, the stock is inexpensive relative to both growth and quality. On de-risked earnings it is fairly valued. The correct valuation score is moderately positive rather than strong, and we are deliberately cautious about reading a compressed multiple as an opportunity when the compression coincides with a specific, identifiable question about earnings durability.

Analyst targets are wide and stale: mean $221.94, median $221.00, low $106, high $320, against 4 strong buys, 15 buys, no holds and 1 strong sell. The mean implies 49% upside. On stocks that appear cheap relative to rising estimates, consensus targets typically lag reality. Here, however, we discount the mean, because the targets predate the drawdown; a $221 target implies roughly 22x 2026 consensus. Our base case sits well below consensus for that reason.

**Base case: $170.** 17x 2026 consensus of $9.95, or roughly 17x a de-risked 2027 EPS near $10.00. That is a modest premium to the regulated peer median (17.4x) on the growth differential, offset by merchant earnings variability. +14.4% from current.

## Competitive Landscape

Vistra's competitive position is improving, with an important geographic qualification.

**Scarcity of dispatchable capacity is the durable advantage.** Vistra owns a large nuclear and gas fleet in the two markets with the most acute AI-driven load growth, at a time when new-build is limited by interconnection queues, turbine lead times and labor. The same constraint that protects incumbents restricts Vistra's own pipeline, which management discloses directly. The frame that makes Cogentrix at ~$855/kW look like disciplined capital deployment is that in-place dispatchable capacity is scarce and expensive to replicate.

**The integrated retail book differentiates Vistra from Constellation and Talen.** Retail absorbs generation output as a sales channel, reducing third-party hedge counterparty exposure and collateral requirements. Retail is also currently the weak segment — Q1 Adjusted EBITDA down 63% on warm Texas weather and excess volumes sold at lower wholesale prices — so the structural benefit is not visible in the current quarter.

**The regional picture complicates the popular narrative.** Average around-the-clock settled prices, Q1 2026 versus Q1 2025: PJM Western Hub $97.41 versus $53.91 (+81%), PJM AEP Dayton +47%, MISO Indiana +50%, NY Zone A +46%, ISO-NE Mass Hub +14%. ERCOT North Hub $32.18 versus $30.91 (+4%), ERCOT West Hub −2%, CAISO NP15 −29%. Investors underwriting Vistra as an ERCOT AI-power story are mismatched to where earnings are actually inflecting. Part of the divergence is weather — East heating degree days were 107% of normal versus Texas at 77% — but ERCOT's flat pricing despite documented load growth suggests solar, storage and new gas additions are keeping pace with demand, and management notes that rising renewable capacity generally depresses market heat rates.

Both M&A transactions confirm where capital is going: Lotus spans PJM/ISO-NE/NYISO/CAISO, and Cogentrix places nine of ten facilities in PJM and ISO-NE. This is a coherent strategy, but it means the earnings mix is shifting toward auction-set capacity revenue rather than the ERCOT energy scarcity thesis most investors associate with the name.

## Risk Assessment

**PJM capacity mean reversion** is the largest unpriced-in-the-bull-case risk. A 4.4x increase in gross capacity revenue drove the East inflection; the mechanism is administratively capped. This is the single item most likely to invalidate the forward earnings anchor.

**Open 2027 book with doubled market risk.** Hedge levels at March 31, 2026: Texas nuclear/renewable/coal 100%, East nuclear/renewable/coal 68%, Texas gas 59%, East gas 90%, West gas 56%. Average value-at-risk (95%, 60-day) was $469M in Q1 2026 versus a $224M FY2025 average, with a high of $491M versus $316M. VaR scales with underlying volatility as well as position size, and PJM Western Hub prices rose 81% while Tetco M3 gas rose 50%, so the doubling is not solely position growth. The Texas 2027 gas hedge improved from ~43% at year-end, so direction of travel is toward less risk. Regardless, 2027 earnings warrant wide bands rather than point estimates.

**Nuclear PTC gross-receipts definition.** Transferable nuclear PTC revenue fell to $220M in FY2025 from $545M in FY2024 because higher power prices pushed receipts above the phase-out threshold. The 10-Q states that Treasury guidance differing from Vistra's interpretation "could result in a material change to PTC revenues recorded in 2024 and 2025," recognized as a change in estimate. That is a live, two-sided, retroactive exposure against $765M of booked revenue. The PTC also functions as a built-in downside hedge, providing more support as unit revenues decline — bull cases that add PTC revenue to high power prices are double-counting.

**Preferred resets, October and December 2026.** Series A ($1.0B, currently 8.000%) resets October 15, 2026 to the 5-year Treasury plus 6.93%; Series B ($1.0B, currently 7.000%) resets December 15, 2026 to 5-year Treasury plus 5.74%. At a 4.0% 5-year rate, the combined incremental coupon is roughly $57M per year ($1.0B × 2.93% + $1.0B × 2.74%). Preferred dividends were $49M per quarter in Q1 2026 (roughly $196M annualized), so this is approximately a 29% increase in the preferred burden. Vistra can redeem on those dates. Date-certain, quantifiable, and typically missed by screens.

**Moss Landing.** Insurance limits of $500M were fully collected as of February 2026, so headroom for this event is exhausted. The remaining 350 MW battery carries $316M of net book value with a targeted mid-2026 restart that management says "creates uncertainty as to when or if the facility will return to service." ASAOC remediation is estimated at ~$110M (~$70M spent, ~$40M accrued) and explicitly excludes hazardous-waste removal identified during demolition. Litigation against Vistra and LG Energy Solution is pending. At 0.6% of market cap, this is bounded relative to the thesis, but the downside is not fully quantified and CAISO is the smallest segment ($56M of Q1 Adjusted EBITDA).

**Cogentrix closing.** FERC approval and HSR clearance outstanding, expected H2 2026, with $150M of combined termination fees. Cogentrix standalone EBITDA, contracted position and the terms of the ~$1.5B assumed debt are undisclosed.

**Counterparty concentration.** One unnamed counterparty represented $298M, or 40%, of total net wholesale credit exposure at March 31, 2026. Creditworthiness cannot be independently assessed.

**Litigation and regulation.** The Dorrell antitrust suit (nuclear-sector compensation suppression, D. Md., Vistra and Constellation among ~25 defendants) had motion-to-dismiss argument set for May 13, 2026 with no disclosed outcome. Ohio HB6 RICO matters remain stayed. Winter Storm Uri exposure largely resolved favorably after the Texas Supreme Court denied plaintiffs' mandamus on March 30, 2026. Environmental relief is broad but predominantly proposed or held in abeyance (GHG standards repeal proposal, April 13, 2026 CCRMU rescission proposal, CCR deadline extension to 2031, regional haze requiring no new controls), with challenges filed against nearly every action. Both of the past year's largest structural positives — environmental cost avoidance and the collateral release — are contingent rather than settled and should not be capitalized at full weight.

**Guidance basis is stale.** The externally reported 2026 Adjusted EBITDA range of $6.8–7.6B was, per the company's own press-release note, prepared as of November 6, 2025 on October 31, 2025 market curves — roughly nine months stale. We were unable to verify the range, the reported May 7, 2026 reaffirmation, or the "$10B+ cash generation through 2027" figure against a primary company document. Treat all three as unconfirmed.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing a specific, imminent event rather than a regime change. Near-term ATM IV of 72.3% against 49.2% one-year realized volatility is rich by 23.1 points, and the term structure is in backwardation (72.3% near-dated versus 51.1% at six months). One-month IV of 63.8% implies a ±10.1% move — consistent with the August 7 earnings date, four business days ahead of the tenor. Over the prior ten sessions, ATM IV rose 12.7 points from 59.6% while the price fell 8.7%, so the market added uncertainty premium as the stock declined.

Directional positioning is not one-sided. Put/call volume ratio is 1.00, put/call open interest 0.82 (call-tilted), and the headline put/call skew of 0.3% is characterized as balanced. Skew readings across tenors are mixed (+3.0% at one month, −3.5% at three, −5.1% at six), and we do not assign direction to those signs without a stated convention. The flagged unusual activity — 1,634 contracts of $148 puts expiring the next day at 6.8x open interest — is same-day pinning and carries little information. Short interest is 4.3% of float at 2.9 days to cover, so the position is uncrowded in both directions.

Practical read: entering a week ahead of a print at 64% one-month implied volatility, following an 18.4% cut to the current-quarter estimate, is poor entry timing regardless of the fundamental view. For holders, the rich near-term vol makes covered-call overwriting or put-financed accumulation more attractive than outright adds.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $230 (+55%)

2026 guidance is reaffirmed or raised on current curves at the August 7 print, delivering toward the upper half of the reported $6.8–7.6B Adjusted EBITDA range. PJM capacity prices hold, ERCOT summer scarcity re-emerges as data-center load outpaces solar and storage additions, and the 2027 hedge book is progressively locked in above 75% at attractive prices. Cogentrix closes in H2 2026, adding 5,500 MW acquired near $855/kW into a market where new dispatchable capacity is scarce. Meta deliveries begin on schedule late 2026, and the AWS ramp from Q4 2027 converts merchant nuclear into 20-year contracted revenue at pricing the market has not yet seen disclosed. The remaining $1,477M of buyback authorization retires roughly 3% of shares at prices below the $159.12 average paid in early 2026. Applying 20x to 2027 EPS of $11.50 yields $230, in the vicinity of the $221 analyst median.

### Bear Case — $118 (−21%)

The next PJM capacity auction clears materially lower, reversing most of the $248M year-over-year increase in East gross capacity revenue and a large share of the 56% East EBITDA gain. ERCOT prices stay flat as solar, storage and new gas additions absorb load growth, so the Texas segment does not fill the gap. With 32–44% of the 2027 book open and VaR at $469M, 2027 EPS lands near $7.50 rather than $11.10. Treasury guidance on nuclear PTC gross receipts goes against Vistra's interpretation, forcing a negative change in estimate against $765M of booked revenue. Preferred resets add roughly $57M of annual cost, and the multiple compresses to 15x on de-risked earnings. $112–118, below the 52-week low of $132.66.

### What Would Have to Be True for the Drawdown to Be a Mispricing

At 32% off the high, the burden of proof sits with the bull case. The falsifiable claim is narrow: 2026 consensus rose 11.3% over 90 days while the multiple compressed from roughly 24.6x to 14.9x, so if the earnings base is durable, the compression overshot. Durability rests on two verifiable items — PJM capacity revenue persistence and 2027 hedge progression — both of which are disclosed quarterly. If the August 7 filing shows the 2027 Texas gas hedge above 70% (from 59%) and East nuclear above 80% (from 68%) at prices supporting the consensus range, the mispricing case strengthens materially. If hedges progress at unfavorable prices, or if management reframes 2027 on current curves, the sellers were right and the derating was rational rather than excessive.

## Conclusion

Vistra is a better business than it was a year ago and a cheaper stock than it was ten months ago. Adjusted EBITDA is compounding, the balance sheet reached investment grade with all secured liens released and $2.4B of floating debt swapped into fixed at lower coupons, 3,809 MW of nuclear output is contracted for 20 years to two hyperscalers, and Cogentrix adds 5,500 MW of dispatchable capacity at roughly half of new-build cost.
