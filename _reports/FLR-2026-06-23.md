**Research Date: June 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Fluor is a global engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) contractor whose 2025 reported results were distorted by a single legal event, the Santos (Australia) judgment that produced a $643M revenue reduction and a $649M December 2025 cash payment, turning consolidated gross profit negative for the year. Stripped of that charge, the underlying EPC business remained marginally profitable, and Q1 2026 confirmed an operating rebound: $160M net income attributable to Fluor versus a -$241M loss a year earlier, and positive $110M operating cash flow. The more durable change is on the balance sheet. The complete exit from NuScale generated roughly $2.43B of cash since September 2025, leaving ~$3.2B of cash and marketable securities against only $1.07B of debt. Management has redirected that capital into a $1.4B 2026 buyback target, repurchasing $516M (11M shares) in Q1 alone and cutting the share count from 169M to ~140M, a ~17% reduction.

The bull case is a de-risked, cash-rich EPC firm shrinking its float into a favorable data-center and energy demand cycle, with 82% of backlog now on lower-risk reimbursable terms. The complications are equally concrete. Q1 2026 GAAP EPS of $0.14 missed the $0.62 consensus by 77.5% (driven by a $96M Mission Solutions charge tied to a trebled False Claims Act verdict), and the stock fell roughly 15% on the print. New awards more than halved year-over-year to $2.7B from $5.8B. Near-term quarterly estimates are rising sharply (+29% to +34% over 90 days) but full-year estimates are flat-to-down (-6.1% over 90 days), a divergence that argues against reading the quarterly revisions as a clean acceleration. A multi-front litigation cluster — the Santos appeal (July 2026), a securities class action over infrastructure cost overruns, four derivative suits, a NuScale-related suit, and the FCA verdict — will not resolve before late 2026.

We maintain a HOLD. At ~$54, near the 52-week high of $57.50 and above the analyst mean target of $50.69, the stock trades at roughly 21x the $2.58 current-year EPS estimate and ~16.3x the $3.30 next-year estimate. That is reasonable versus the closest comparable (AECOM at 14.5x) and cheap versus the peer median of 28.4x, but the value-trap risk on a heterogeneous peer set, the unresolved litigation tail, and the new-award decline keep the risk/reward balanced rather than asymmetric. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and nothing in the current data warrants either a confident upgrade or a downgrade.

## Company Overview

Fluor, headquartered in Irving, Texas, is one of the world's largest publicly traded EPC firms, organized into three operating segments based on the Q1 2026 10-Q:

- **Urban Solutions** (~67% of Q1 revenue, $2.44B): The growth engine, spanning life sciences/pharmaceutical facilities, mining and metals, infrastructure, an emerging data-center pipeline, and the professional staffing business. This segment carries $19.0B of the $25.7B total backlog.
- **Energy Solutions** (~19%, $703M): Traditional oil and gas, refining, chemicals, and energy-transition work. It was the highest-margin segment in Q1 (10.5% segment margin) on favorable project closeouts.
- **Mission Solutions** (~14%, $523M): U.S. government work, including DOE nuclear site management (South Carolina, Ohio), DOD support, and emergency/disaster response.

A structural feature of the model matters for interpreting the headline numbers: roughly 58% of consolidated revenue is "at-cost" pass-through (subcontractor costs, customer-furnished materials) carrying no meaningful markup, which is why Fluor emphasizes a non-GAAP adjusted net revenue metric. The strategic shift over recent years has been toward reimbursable contracts (now 82% of backlog) and away from fixed-price/lump-sum work, which has historically produced catastrophic megaproject losses. Loss-making legacy backlog has shrunk to $169M from $255M.

The financial story is a 2025 earnings collapse driven by one legal judgment, followed by a Q1 2026 operating rebound and a balance-sheet transformation.

Several adjustments are needed to read this correctly. The 2025 negative gross profit was primarily the revenue reduction booked for the Santos judgment; without it the EPC business was modestly profitable. The 2024 net income of $2.1B was inflated by a $634M tax benefit and large NuScale mark-to-market gains, not operating performance. Q1 2026 marks the cleaner picture: a $124M gain on the CFHI (China fabrication JV) sale, and underlying net earnings excluding equity-method items of $96M versus $88M a year earlier.

The balance sheet is now unusually strong for an EPC firm: ~$3.2B cash and marketable securities against $1.07B total debt (2028 Notes $506M; 2029 convertible $575M). Capital allocation has pivoted to buybacks, with board authorization expanded by 30M shares. There is no common dividend.

The qualifier on the rebound is the Q1 2026 GAAP EPS miss, which reflects the $96M Mission Solutions charge from the FCA verdict and other items. Over the last four quarters Fluor has recorded one beat and three misses, including the -77.5% miss on the most recent print and a -23.3% miss in Q2 2025. Reported GAAP earnings remain lumpy and hard to forecast, which limits the confidence to place in consensus estimates.

## Growth Analysis

The growth signals point in different directions, which is the central reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY.

On the positive side, near-term quarterly estimate revisions are strongly positive: the current quarter is up 29.2% over 90 days (and +7.8% in the last 30), and the next quarter is up 34.4% over 90 days (+17.2% in 30). Revisions of this magnitude on turnaround stocks are typically a strong forward signal. The data-center build-out and energy capex are real secular tailwinds the company is positioned to capture, and management cites strengthening pipeline activity in energy, commodities, and advanced technologies.

On the cautionary side, the full-year estimates are not confirming. Current-year EPS consensus is $2.58, flat over 30 days and down 6.1% over 90 days; next-year consensus of $3.30 is flat over 30 days and up only 1.5% over 90. The strong quarterly revisions are not translating into rising annual estimates, which suggests analysts are pulling earnings forward across quarters rather than raising the level. New awards more than halving year-over-year is a forward headwind for backlog conversion in 2027 and beyond (the prior-year figure included a large life-sciences win), and total backlog was roughly flat at $25.7B. The data-center pipeline is encouraging but currently amounts to a "limited notice-to-proceed" on one U.S. project rather than committed scope.

The growth thesis therefore rests on the demand cycle materializing into awards faster than the current award decline implies. That is plausible but not yet evident in the booking data, which is why we weight growth prospects as middling rather than strong.

The peer median makes FLR look cheap, but the dispersion is wide and the most directly comparable EPC firm, AECOM, trades at 14.5x. On that basis FLR at ~16.3x next-year is roughly in line with the closest comp rather than meaningfully discounted. A reflexive "cheap versus peers" read here is unreliable: this is a lumpy-earnings EPC business with an active litigation tail, and a compressed multiple in such names frequently reflects justified risk rather than mispricing.

The most credible source of valuation support is the buyback. A $1.4B program against a $7.5B market cap, executed near depressed prices (~$46 average in Q1), provides per-share accretion and a demand floor, distinguishing FLR from a generic value trap. Offsetting it, the 2029 convertible (conversion price $45.37) is in the money at $54, adding diluted shares and partially eroding buyback accretion.

The stock currently sits above the analyst mean ($50.69) and median ($50.50) targets, with a high of $60 and low of $40. On turnaround stocks with reasonable valuations and broadly rising estimates, analyst targets often lag and function as floors. That dynamic does not cleanly apply here because full-year estimates are flat-to-down — the targets are not stale in the way they would be on a name with rising annual consensus. Trading above the mean target after a 10.8% run is a mild caution, but it removes the cushion that a below-target entry would provide.

## Competitive Landscape

Fluor competes in the global EPC oligopoly alongside Bechtel (private), Jacobs, KBR, Worley, and AECOM, with broader infrastructure overlap against names like MasTec. The 10-K explicitly flags intense competition pressuring contract prices and margins.

The constructive element of the competitive story is the deliberate de-risking: 82% reimbursable backlog and the near-complete runoff of loss-making legacy projects. This addresses the industry's chronic problem of fixed-price megaproject losses, which are precisely Fluor's history. If the demand cycle in data centers and energy delivers, Fluor's scale and reference base position it well for reimbursable, lower-risk scope.

The cautionary elements are the trajectory indicators. Fluor's five-year cumulative TSR (248) now lags its peer group (388), a reversal from 2024 when it led. The cost-overrun problem persists even within the reimbursable book, with a $37M Middle East mining JV overrun in Q1 2026 and a securities class action specifically alleging undisclosed cost growth on three infrastructure projects. The competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding rather than improving, consistent with the declining trajectory in our prior reviews.

## Risk Assessment

Fluor's risk profile is dominated by specific, active, dated items rather than generic sector cyclicality:

1. **Santos / Australia litigation.** A referee panel valued claims at ~AUD $790M and ruled the AUD $236M contractual liability cap did not apply. An appeal is set for July 2026, and insurance recoveries remain in negotiation (some insurers disputed coverage but agreed to a standstill). The July appeal is a near-term binary that the options market is partly pricing.

2. **Securities class action and derivative suits.** September 2025 shareholder suits (class period Feb 18–July 31, 2025) allege misleading statements about rising costs on three infrastructure projects, followed by four derivative actions and books-and-records demands. This overhang ties directly to the company's structural cost-overrun history.

3. **Trebled FCA verdict.** A March 2026 jury awarded $15M (subject to trebling to ~$45M plus fees) on a DOD/Afghanistan materials-management claim, driving the Q1 charge. Final judgment is possible by Q3 2026 and is appealable.

4. **Credit-rating trigger.** A one-notch downgrade (to BB/Ba2) would force Fluor to collateralize its $2.2B credit facility with liens on U.S. assets. Covenants include a 0.60 debt-to-cap ceiling and $1.1B minimum liquidity. The strong cash position mitigates near-term refinancing risk but the trigger is a tail to monitor.

5. **Project execution and government exposure.** Cost-estimating risk is the structural EPC hazard; $1.1B of backlog is unfunded government work exposed to shutdowns and spending cuts.

6. **Convertible dilution.** The 2029 convertible is in the money above $45.37, partially offsetting buyback accretion.

The aggregate is a meaningful, multi-front legal tail that will not fully resolve before late 2026. We score risk factors low not because EPC is cyclical (that is permanently priced) but because of the concentration of specific, near-dated litigation outcomes with asymmetric downside.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is complacent in the near term and more cautious around catalysts. One-month ATM IV is 47.2% versus one-year historical volatility of 54.4%, with IV sitting at the 7th percentile of its own chain. Put/call volume (0.15) and OI (0.33) ratios are low, and skew is balanced at 2.2%, indicating no meaningful demand for downside protection. The term structure is in contango, with three-month IV jumping to 55.2% and six-month to 52.3%, capturing the July Santos appeal and Q2 earnings. The implied move for the nearest expiry is ±9.6%. The market is not pricing imminent downside, but cheap near-term IV combined with elevated three-month vol concentrates uncertainty around the next earnings print and the appeal. Short interest at 8.9% of float (2.7 days to cover) is moderate and not a squeeze setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($60+):** The NuScale exit has converted a volatile equity stake into a fortress balance sheet, and management is shrinking the float ~17% into a favorable data-center and energy demand cycle. Q1 2026 confirms the underlying EPC business is profitable once the one-time charges are stripped out. The strong near-term quarterly revisions presage backlog conversion accelerating through 2026, the Santos appeal recovers some value or insurance recoveries materialize, and the buyback at depressed prices compounds per-share earnings on the $3.30+ next-year base. At 18x on a rising next-year number plus buyback accretion, the stock reaches the $60 analyst high.

**Bear Case ($40):** New awards more than halving year-over-year proves a leading indicator of a backlog plateau, the data-center pipeline stays at "limited notice-to-proceed" rather than committed scope, and the litigation cluster produces adverse outcomes (Santos appeal denied with limited insurance recovery, securities class action survives dismissal, FCA judgment finalized at ~$45M). GAAP earnings remain lumpy with continued misses, the convertible dilutes buyback accretion, and a credit-rating downgrade forces facility collateralization. The multiple compresses toward AECOM's ~14.5x on a flat-to-down full-year number, taking the stock to the $40 analyst low, roughly 26% below current.

The base case sits between these: a de-risked but slow-converting backlog, buyback-driven per-share support, and litigation that resolves with mixed but manageable outcomes, keeping the stock range-bound near current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (early August) and the Santos appeal (July 2026) are the two near-term catalysts that should clarify both the operating trajectory and the litigation tail.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Full-year consensus EPS turns positive on revisions (current $2.58 rising above $2.75) alongside a quarter of new awards recovering toward $5B+, confirming the quarterly revision strength is flowing into backlog rather than being pulled forward.
- The Santos appeal resolves favorably or material insurance recoveries are confirmed, removing the largest litigation overhang.
- A pullback to the low-to-mid $40s, restoring a margin of safety below the analyst mean target with the buyback intact.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- New awards decline for a second consecutive quarter while backlog falls below $24B, signaling a structural booking slowdown.
- A credit-rating downgrade to BB/Ba2 forcing facility collateralization, or a covenant approach (liquidity toward the $1.1B floor).
- Adverse outcomes clustering on the securities class action and FCA judgment beyond current reserves, or a renewed lump-sum cost-overrun surfacing in the reimbursable book.

**Reassessment point:** Next earnings (early August 2026) and the July Santos appeal ruling.

## Conclusion

Fluor has done the hard work of de-risking its capital structure: exiting NuScale, building a strong cash position, and committing to a buyback that has already cut the float ~17%. The one-time nature of the 2025 loss is well-established, and the underlying EPC business is profitable. Those facts support a floor under the stock and a coherent capital-return thesis.

What prevents an upgrade is the conflict in the evidence. The strong near-term quarterly revisions are not flowing through to full-year estimates, which are flat-to-down. New awards more than halved year-over-year. Q1 GAAP EPS missed by 77.5% and the company has missed three of its last four quarters, making forward estimates hard to trust. A genuine multi-front litigation cluster remains unresolved into late 2026. The stock trades near its 52-week high, above the analyst mean target, after a 10.8% run, in a sector where company-specific selection has historically struggled to beat the market. The buyback is a real catalyst, but it is offset by the convertible dilution and the booking decline.

We maintain HOLD: the appreciation case (buyback accretion, demand cycle, balance-sheet strength) and the caution case (flat full-year estimates, award decline, litigation tail, premium-to-target price) are roughly balanced. The setup favors waiting for the August earnings print and the July Santos ruling, or for a pullback toward the low-to-mid $40s that would restore asymmetry. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD; the bar to change has been met by neither side of the ledger.
