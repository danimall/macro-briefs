**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Texas Pacific Land Corporation is misclassified by its GICS energy-E&P tag. It is a land and royalty holding company that owns roughly 881,000 contiguous surface acres and ~224,000 net royalty acres in the Permian Basin, monetizing third-party drilling, disposal, and infrastructure activity without bearing exploration capex or well operating cost. The result is a financial profile few public companies can match: 74.2% operating margin, 60.3% net margin, ~$498M of free cash flow in FY2025, and, until October 2025, zero debt. The 191,000 royalty acres granted in an 1888 land grant carry zero book value, so reported returns understate the true asset base. This is a genuinely exceptional business.

The investment question is about price and about a strategic change that alters the risk profile. TPL trades at roughly 60x trailing earnings and ~48x the current-year consensus of $8.66, a valuation completely disconnected from E&P peers (median P/E 15.0) and from any conventional royalty comparable. That premium reflects perpetual-royalty quality, a fortress balance sheet, and embedded optionality around data centers and produced-water desalination. The optionality is increasingly priced in while the near-term earnings trend is negative: consensus current-year EPS has fallen 9.4% over 90 days, next-year 8.6%, driven by weaker realized commodity prices and a rising depletion charge on acquired royalties.

Compounding the valuation debate is a deliberate change in what TPL is. For decades it was a passive royalty and return-of-capital vehicle. In 2025–26 it opened a $500M revolver (its first meaningful debt capacity), suspended its special dividend to fund a ~$490M acquisition year, and pivoted surface acreage toward data-center siting and desalination. The cash-return machine is becoming a capital-deployment growth story, and execution on that pivot is unproven. We maintain HOLD. The franchise is worth owning at the right price; at ~$416 against falling estimates and unproven deployment, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

TPL's origins trace to an 1888 land grant from the Texas & Pacific Railway. It operated as a business trust until converting to a Delaware corporation in January 2021. The economic model is difficult to replicate: TPL owns land and mineral rights but does not drill, complete, or operate wells. It captures value across the well lifecycle (royalties on production, easements for pipelines and power lines, commercial leases, land and material sales such as caliche and sand) while third parties bear the capital and operating burden.

Two reportable segments:

- **Land and Resource Management (~62% of 2025 revenue):** oil and gas royalties from perpetual non-participating royalty interests plus acquired net royalty acres, easements (roughly 30-year terms with CPI-escalating renewals), commercial leases, and land/material sales.
- **Water Services and Operations (~38% of 2025 revenue):** sourced and treated water sales, plus produced-water royalties collected via subsidiary TPWR for allowing disposal on TPL surface.

The strategic asset is the contiguous surface acreage overlaying active Permian acreage. TPL controls both the surface (for water sourcing, rights-of-way, disposal, and increasingly data-center siting) and mineral royalties in the same footprint. That dual control over one of the most active oil basins on earth is the core of the moat.

TPL's balance sheet and margin profile are among the strongest of any US public company.

| Net income | $405.6M | $454.0M | $481.4M | $142.9M (+18.4%) |
| Operating margin | ~77% | 76% | 74.2% | 77% |

| Free cash flow | — | $461M | $498M | $136.4M |

Several dynamics matter for scoring:

**Volume offsetting price.** In 2025 realized price per Boe fell 14.3% ($39.87 to $34.18) as WTI dropped ~15%, yet oil and gas royalty revenue grew 10.3% because TPL's production share rose from 26.8 to 34.6 MBoe/d on operator development and royalty acquisitions. Q1 2026 production reached 37.1 MBoe/d. More wells drilled on TPL acreage plus purchased royalty acres more than offset lower per-unit prices; this growth does not depend on the oil price rising.

**Fortress balance sheet.** Altman Z-Score 8.46, D/E 0.11, equity 89.9% of assets, zero funded debt through FY2025. The October 2025 $500M undrawn revolver (matures 2029, accordion to $750M) is the first meaningful debt capacity in the company's history, signaling a more active capital-deployment posture rather than balance-sheet strain.

**Capital allocation pivot.** FY2025 saw no special dividend (versus a $3.33/share special in 2024) and only $8.4M of buybacks. Instead TPL deployed ~$490M: $450.7M for 17,306 Midland Basin net royalty acres (November 2025), smaller land/royalty deals, and a $50M Bolt investment. Management set an explicit ~$700M target cash balance above which it will return the majority of FCF. The redirection of cash from shareholder returns to acquisitions is the central change to the thesis, and it is deliberate.

**Rising depletion.** DD&A jumped from $25M (2024) to $62.5M (2025) as acquired royalty interests are depleted. This is a real, growing non-cash cost of the acquisition strategy and will continue to scale, pressuring reported net income relative to the pre-acquisition, near-zero-depletion model.

A 3-for-1 stock split was effected December 22, 2025; all figures are retroactively adjusted.

The earnings-surprise record is uneven: 2 beats and 2 misses over four quarters, with a large Q2'25 miss ($5.05 actual vs. $18.51 estimate) that reflects estimate noise around a specific quarter rather than an operational failure. Recent quarters (Q4'25 +3.0%, Q1'26 +1.5%) have modestly beaten. Financial Health scores 8.5; the debt profile, margins, and cash generation are near best-in-class, with the only marks against being the recent cash drawdown into acquisitions and rising depletion.

## Growth Analysis

Growth has three legs, of differing durability and visibility.

**Royalty/production growth (durable, quantifiable).** Production share has compounded on operator activity on existing acreage plus acquired NRA. This is the reliable core. It is subject to two headwinds visible now: falling realized prices (unhedged) and the deeply negative Waha Hub gas basis (–$1.40/mmbtu in Q1'26) from Permian takeaway constraints, which suppresses gas royalty realizations.

**Water and produced-water (structural).** Produced-water royalties (~$124M in 2025 and growing) benefit from a Permian producing >20M bbl/day of water. Seismic Response Area restrictions on deep injection create scarcity value for pore space and support the desalination case. This leg is growing but carries the regulatory risk discussed below.

**Data-center/AI-power optionality (high-upside, unproven).** This is the dominant 2026 narrative. In Q1'26 TPL sold land for $42.5M to a power-plant developer supporting data centers, recognizing $20.9M revenue plus a financing receivable (payments through 2046, 7.5% imputed rate) and a separate water-supply agreement with put/call options tied to milestones, its first realized data-center-linked transaction. Project Kilby (announced ~June 2026) involves Chevron developing a ~2.67 GW gas-fired facility in Reeves County co-located with a Microsoft data center under a reported 20-year power agreement; TPL's role is landowner and brackish-water supplier, not power or data-center operator. The $50M Bolt investment (Schmidt-chaired) adds minority equity, warrants, and a right of first refusal to supply water.

The data-center thesis is real and fits TPL's assets: West Texas is emerging as a hyperscaler power hub on cheap stranded gas and land, and few landowners can match TPL's contiguous surface position. The distinction for growth scoring is that these deals remain optionality, not recurring quantifiable revenue. Most of the value is prospective.

The near-term signal cuts against the narrative. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised down 9.4% over 90 days (5.6% in the last 30), next-quarter down 15.2% over 90 days, next-year down 8.6%. On our framework, growth prospects is the most reliable winner-selection dimension, and here the forward earnings trend is negative even as the multi-year story improves. That tension holds Growth Prospects to 5.5: the long-run asset story is strong, but the 12-month earnings trajectory is being cut, and the data-center upside is not yet in the numbers.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$416 and ~$28.7B market cap on ~$481M net income, TPL trades at ~60x trailing earnings and ~48x the $8.66 current-year consensus. Against E&P peers this is off the chart:

The peer comparison is not the right anchor: TPL bears no exploration risk or capex, carries no debt, and holds perpetual royalties, so it deserves a large premium to operating E&Ps. But even against high-quality royalty and land models, ~48x forward is demanding. The company's own dividend yield of 0.58% reflects the pivot away from cash return; there is no yield support at this price.

The estimate-revision picture removes the case for treating this as a story that justifies looking through a high trailing multiple. TPL is not a trough-earnings turnaround with rising estimates; estimates are falling across every window. The strongest calibration signal (30%+ upward revisions on a cheap turnaround stock) is absent here; the signal present is the opposite. This is a premium-multiple stock with negative near-term revision velocity, precisely the setup where a high multiple is vulnerable: beats are expected, and any disappointment derates hard.

The data-center optionality is what supports the premium, and it is genuinely valuable and hard to replicate. But optionality that is unquantified and undated is difficult to underwrite at 48x. The market is paying today for milestones (Kilby offtake ramp, desalination commissioning, Bolt monetization) that have not yet converted to recurring revenue. Analyst targets span $248 (low) to $639 (high), with a $443.5 mean, a wide dispersion that signals how much of the value is contested optionality rather than modelable earnings.

We score Valuation 3.5. The multiple is stretched against current and forward fundamentals with negative revision momentum, and while the business quality is real, that quality is thoroughly reflected in the price. The premium already embeds the bull case.

## Competitive Landscape

TPL's moat is structural and among the widest in any sector we cover. The contiguous surface acres over active Permian acreage, combined with royalty interests in the same footprint, cannot be assembled today at any reasonable cost. Perpetual NPRIs generate royalty income in perpetuity with no reinvestment requirement. The surface position gives TPL first claim on the emerging monetization vectors: water sourcing, disposal pore space, easements, and now data-center land and power siting.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving on the asset side. Produced-water scarcity (as SRAs restrict injection) raises the value of TPL's pore space and desalination alternative. The data-center pivot layers a picks-and-shovels revenue stream on top of the royalty base; TPL becomes a landlord and water utility to AI infrastructure, an optionality standard E&P screens miss entirely.

The offsets to a higher competitive score: TPL is a price- and activity-taker with no control over operators' drilling decisions, and ~40% of 2025 revenue came from just three customers, all in one basin. The moat protects the asset, not the pace of monetization. We score Competitive Position 8.0, reflecting a genuinely elite and durable position with the trajectory improving, modestly below prior 9.0 marks to reflect that the concentration and activity-taker dynamics cap how much the company controls its own outcomes.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity exposure, fully unhedged.** Management explicitly runs unhedged, capturing full upside (as in the March/April 2026 Hormuz-driven oil spike) and full downside. Realized oil fell to $59.48/Bbl in Q4 2025 before recovering. With the broader energy backdrop hinging on a two-sided Hormuz binary, TPL's realizations are directly levered to an outcome no one controls.

**Customer and geographic concentration.** ~40% of revenue from three customers, 100% of assets in the Permian. All three are investment-grade supermajors, which limits credit risk, but activity concentration means an operator slowdown hits revenue directly.

**Water-business regulatory risk (SRAs).** The Texas Railroad Commission indefinitely suspended deep produced-water injection in Culberson and Reeves counties in January 2024. Since produced-water royalties depend on disposal on TPL land, SRA expansion is a genuine threat, partially offset because scarcity strengthens the desalination case.

**Desalination execution.** $48.3M spent cumulatively through Q1'26 on unproven produced-water desalination; first inlet barrels expected mid-2026. Capital-at-risk plus environmental/discharge liability if it underperforms.

**New-venture risk.** The $50M Bolt stake and data-center land deals sit outside the historical core, with lack-of-control, illiquidity, and execution risk newly disclosed as a risk factor in the FY2025 10-K.

**Governance transition.** Murray Stahl (Horizon Kinetics co-founder, TPL director, and intellectual architect of the long-term thesis) died April 7, 2026. TPL seated Horizon co-founder Peter Doyle on the board and the Strategic Acquisitions Committee on May 5, 2026, signaling continuity. The succession appears managed, but the loss of the strategy's architect during a strategic pivot is a real risk.

**Valuation risk.** After a >50% H1 2026 run, the stock carries the risk that any conversion delay on the data-center optionality or a further leg down in oil compresses the multiple sharply.

We score Risk Factors 5.0: the balance-sheet resilience mutes downside, but the combination of unhedged commodity exposure, SRA regulatory risk, unproven ventures, and a stretched multiple against falling estimates presents several specific, not-fully-priced downside catalysts.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
TPL owns an irreplaceable perpetual-royalty and surface-acreage franchise with 74% operating margins and zero net debt. Production share keeps compounding on operator activity and acquisitions independent of the oil price. Water and produced-water royalties grow structurally as SRA restrictions raise pore-space and desalination value. The data-center pivot converts from optionality to recurring revenue: Project Kilby's Chevron/Microsoft complex ramps, the desalination facility commissions successfully mid-2026, Bolt monetizes, and TPL is re-rated as a diversified AI-infrastructure landlord and water utility rather than a royalty stock. In that scenario the stock revisits and exceeds the 52-week high of $547.20 and moves toward the $639 analyst high.

### Bear Case
Oil sags on a verified Hormuz reopening, and TPL's unhedged realizations fall. Estimates continue to be cut (they are already down ~9% over 90 days). The data-center deals stay optionality, delayed or de-scoped; desalination disappoints at commissioning. The ~$490M acquisition year proves dilutive on a per-share basis as depletion rises faster than incremental royalty income. The premium multiple compresses toward a still-rich but lower level as the market reprices the growth pivot. Downside toward the $269 52-week low and the $248 analyst low, roughly 35-40% from current levels.

The distribution is roughly symmetric around the current price, which is why this is a HOLD rather than a directional call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The evidence does not support a confident directional call. The franchise quality argues against AVOID; the stretched multiple against falling estimates and unproven deployment argues against BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-to-mid $300s (roughly 20% lower), where the forward multiple compresses to ~40x and the data-center optionality is more of a free call, restoring asymmetry.
- Data-center revenue converting from optionality to disclosed, recurring run-rate — e.g., a quantified Project Kilby water-supply revenue stream or a signed second hyperscaler deal — combined with a turn in estimate revisions to positive.
- Successful desalination commissioning (mid-2026) demonstrating economic throughput.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Estimate revisions accelerating downward alongside a sustained oil decline below $70 WTI that compresses royalty revenue.
- SRA expansion materially curtailing produced-water disposal on TPL acreage.
- Evidence the ~$490M acquisition program is dilutive to per-share FCF as depletion scales.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (early August 2026), the mid-2026 desalination commissioning result, and any Project Kilby / data-center revenue disclosure. The Hormuz situation is a near-term swing factor for realizations.

## Conclusion

TPL is one of the highest-quality asset bases in US public markets: perpetual Permian royalties, contiguous surface acres, 74% operating margins, and a debt-free balance sheet, with genuine and hard-to-replicate optionality in data-center land, power siting, and produced-water desalination. None of that is in dispute.

The dispute is price and timing. At ~48x current-year earnings against consensus estimates falling ~9% over 90 days, an unhedged commodity position, and a deliberate pivot from shareholder returns toward $490M of acquisitions whose per-share payback is unproven, the stock already reflects the bull case. The strategic shift from cash-return machine to capital-deployment growth story is real and worth watching, but execution is unproven and the near-term earnings trend is negative. We maintain HOLD, consistent with the prior sequence of HOLDs, and would become buyers on a meaningful pullback or on evidence that the data-center optionality is converting into recurring revenue with estimate revisions turning up.
