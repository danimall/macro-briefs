## Executive Summary

Collegium is a specialty pharmaceutical rollup that acquires branded central-nervous-system assets, harvests their cash flow with a shared salesforce, and funds the next purchase with debt. The model has worked mechanically well: net product revenue compounded from $463.9M in FY2022 to $780.6M in FY2025, and FY2025 operating cash flow of $329.3M on $1.7M of capex produced roughly $327.6M of free cash flow, or 42% of revenue. Cash conversion remains the strongest element of this file.

The August 6, 2026 print broke the growth arithmetic. Management reduced FY2026 net product revenue guidance to $825-855M from $865-895M and adjusted EBITDA to $445-470M from $475-500M, attributing the cut principally to faster-than-expected Nucynta generic erosion. Guidance embeds $65-75M from Azstarys, acquired May 12. Subtracting the Azstarys midpoint from the revenue midpoint leaves approximately $770M of base revenue against $780.6M reported in FY2025 — the legacy portfolio is now shrinking by roughly 1.4%, and every dollar of reported 2026 growth was bought. The quarter itself beat on adjusted EPS ($1.92 versus $1.75 consensus, +9.7%); the stock fell 18.5% on the guide.

The valuation is undeniably low. Enterprise value of roughly $2.04B (equity $944M + $1,107M funded debt + $121M deferred royalty − $130M cash) is 4.46x the $457.5M adjusted EBITDA midpoint, and consensus non-GAAP EPS of $7.65 for FY2026 puts the shares at 3.8x. That is cheap for identifiable reasons rather than through neglect: Nucynta is in terminal decline and is licensed rather than owned, Xtampza turned negative at −14% in Q2, Belbuca — the largest and only growing legacy asset — faces an April 12, 2027 ANDA trial, an assumed arbitration claim exceeds $500M against a $19.85M escrow, and $1.1B of debt sits ahead of the equity on a balance sheet with negligible tangible asset coverage. Prior reports rated COLL HOLD three consecutive times with scores declining from 6.0 to 5.3. The guidance cut is new, adverse, company-specific information that resolves part of that uncertainty in the wrong direction. Moving to REDUCE.

## Company Overview

Collegium has essentially no discovery organization. R&D is de minimis relative to revenue; the FY2025 10-K describes a strategy of acquiring differentiated, abuse-deterrent or extended-release CNS products and commercializing them through an established specialty salesforce with DEA/REMS-compliant controlled-substance distribution. The moat is commercial and regulatory rather than scientific, and the patents protect formulation and delivery rather than new chemical entities.

Q2 2026 portfolio (10-Q):

| Product | Q2 2026 net revenue | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| Belbuca (buprenorphine buccal film) | $57.7M | +10% |
| Jornay PM (evening-dosed methylphenidate) | $46.1M | +41% |
| Xtampza ER (abuse-deterrent oxycodone) | $45.0M | −14% |
| Nucynta franchise (incl. authorized generic) | $35.2M | −24% |
| Azstarys (SDX/d-MPH, from May 12) | $12.9M | n/a |
| **Total net product revenue** | **$196.9M** | |

The strategic direction is a deliberate migration out of branded opioids into ADHD. Belbuca and Xtampza came from the 2022 BioDelivery acquisition, Nucynta is in-licensed from Grünenthal, Jornay PM came with Ironshore in 2024, and Azstarys arrived with the Corium/Commave purchase in May 2026. ADHD is now roughly 30% of revenue and effectively all of the growth.

The Azstarys logic is consolidation rather than market expansion. Zevra's FY2025 10-K, filed before the deal closed, listed Jornay PM as a competitor to Azstarys. Collegium bought share within evening/once-daily methylphenidate rather than adding a new addressable market. Two features justify the interest: Azstarys IP runs through 2037, materially longer than anything else Collegium owns, and the serdexmethylphenidate prodrug is a distinct chemical entity scheduled IV, which limits automatic generic substitutability in a class where ordinary methylphenidate ER products have none of that protection.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and the 2026 inflection.** Net product revenue: $463.9M (FY2022) → $566.8M (FY2023) → $631.4M (FY2024) → $780.6M (FY2025), the last a 23.6% gain driven by the first full year of Jornay PM. The revised FY2026 guide of $825-855M less the $65-75M Azstarys contribution implies base revenue of approximately $770M, down about 1.4% from FY2025. The composition:

- **Nucynta** fell 12% in the first half but 24% in Q2 alone, so erosion is accelerating following the late-February Hikma authorized generic and Q1 generic ER entry. Approximately $5.1M of the $35.2M was AG revenue.
- **Xtampza** turned negative for the first time (−14%) on volume and gross-to-net dynamics.
- **Belbuca** grew 10% and is now the largest product at roughly a $230M annualized run rate.
- **Jornay PM** grew 41% in dollars against roughly 13% prescription growth, and is guided to $190-200M for FY2026.

The 28-point gap between Jornay's dollar and script growth is the most important unresolved question in the model. It was contributed by net price realization, gross-to-net improvement, or channel timing. If it reflects a durable step-down in rebating as payer contracts consolidated post-Ironshore, the franchise is worth roughly what the reported growth rate implies. If it is timing, the durable growth rate of the only healthy asset is low-to-mid teens rather than forty. Neither the 10-Q nor the release resolves it. Azstarys prescriptions grew roughly 2% year over year, which is hard to square with the "significant growth potential" framing that supported paying $706.3M.

**Margins and the amortization distortion.** GAAP results are heavily obscured by acquired-intangible amortization booked inside cost of goods sold. FY2025 revenue of $780.6M less gross profit of $463.3M gives $317.3M of total cost of revenue, of which cash product cost was roughly $95.4M; the ~$221.9M residual is amortization, and it ties exactly to the movement in net intangibles from $891.4M to $669.5M with no impairment or additions. Cash gross margin was therefore roughly 88% against a reported 59%.

The Nucynta intangible was fully amortized as of June 30, 2026. Disclosed remaining amortization is approximately $110.5M for the balance of 2026 and $145.7M for 2027, implying FY2026 amortization of about $228.9M and an ~$83M step-down next year. Holding all else constant, that alone restores GAAP profitability in 2027 from the Q2 2026 GAAP net loss of $(15.1)M. Two qualifications matter. The swing is entirely non-cash: it does not affect operating cash flow, adjusted EBITDA, or the EBITDA-based credit-agreement tests. And the asset became fully amortized because its revenue is disappearing — the accounting relief and the revenue loss describe the same event. Any 2027 "GAAP inflection" narrative carries no cash content.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 OCF was $329.3M against $1.7M of capex. First-half 2026 OCF was $128.4M, roughly flat year over year despite higher EBITDA, absorbing about $34.4M of cash interest, $37.5M of cash taxes, and Azstarys transaction costs. First-half figures should not be annualized: the Jornay deferred royalty is serviced semi-annually, ADHD demand is second-half weighted around back-to-school, and rebate accruals settle with a lag. Taking FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $445-470M and deducting roughly $70M of cash interest on the larger term loan, $75-85M of cash taxes, ~$2M capex, and deferred-royalty servicing gives approximately $250-290M of free cash flow, a 26-31% yield on the $944M market cap. A naive first-half annualization gives ~$256M, so the two approaches converge.

**Capital structure — the central constraint.** The Azstarys purchase re-levered the company materially. Consideration of $706.3M (roughly $613.6M cash at close, $42M escrow, $38.5M contingent, $12.2M other payable) plus up to $135M of milestones was funded with about $355.6M of cash on hand and a $300M delayed-draw term loan. Term notes principal rose to ~$865.5M from ~$571M at year-end 2025. Convertible notes of $241.5M (2.875%, due 2029, conversion price $36.56, ~6.6M shares) are out of the money at $29.11. The Jornay deferred royalty obligation carries at ~$121.4M with disclosed fair value of ~$142.6M. Cash fell to $129.5M from roughly $387M of cash plus securities at year-end.

Derived leverage: funded principal $1,107.0M less $129.5M cash equals $977.5M net, or 2.14x the $457.5M adjusted EBITDA midpoint; including the deferred royalty at carrying value, 2.40x. That is manageable in isolation. The complication is the November 18, 2028 springing maturity, which accelerates the term loan if more than $50M of converts remain outstanding and liquidity is below $350M. Functionally, the converts are a 2028 problem, and with the stock 20% below the conversion price, cash settlement or refinancing is the base case. That is a real constraint on buybacks and further M&A through 2027-2028.

A screening Altman Z-Score near 1.4 places COLL in the distress zone. That reading is mechanical — very large acquired intangibles and goodwill in total assets, a modest retained-earnings base, and small equity market value relative to total liabilities — and is poorly calibrated for a business generating a ~30% FCF margin with a 2028/2029 maturity profile. It does, however, point at the correct fragility: there is almost no tangible asset coverage behind $1.1B of debt, so if revenue durability breaks, recovery value is thin and the equity absorbs the loss first.

**Working capital — a definitional trap.** Receivables rose from ~$211M to ~$285M and inventory from ~$54.9M to ~$121.3M between December 31, 2025 and June 30, 2026. Those are balances, and $58.5M of the AR increase plus $69.9M of the inventory increase came in through the Azstarys acquisition. Organically, AR consumed only ~$15.3M of cash and inventory ~$2.4M in the half. Accrued rebates rose from ~$318M to ~$406.9M, of which ~$81.3M was assumed. Comparing period-end balances without that adjustment would produce a false conclusion of deteriorating receivable quality. One genuine question remains: $69.9M of acquired Azstarys inventory is large against roughly a $100M annualized revenue run rate, though a $35.3M fair-value step-up and DEA-quota-driven safety stock are equally plausible explanations.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth has become a function of acquisition cadence. On management's own revised numbers, the base is contracting, and the company must keep buying assets — with progressively less balance-sheet capacity — to show top-line growth.

The 2027 bridge is roughly neutral. Azstarys moves from a $70M partial year to perhaps $110-130M (+$45M), Jornay adds $25-30M at a normalized script-driven rate, and Belbuca adds maybe $15M pre-cliff. Against that, Nucynta sheds another $60-80M toward a residual and Xtampza another $20M or so. That lands 2027 revenue flat to modestly down, which is consistent with consensus non-GAAP EPS of $7.33 for next year versus $7.65 for the current year — the sell side already models a small decline, with higher interest expense and Azstarys cost of goods absorbing the mix benefit.

Estimate revisions have turned. Current-quarter consensus fell 15.7% over 30 days and 11.9% over 90 days; next quarter fell 8.4% and 3.8%. Full-year figures are still marginally positive on a 90-day basis (+2.6% current year, +6.8% next year) but negative over 30 days (−0.9% and −3.7%), and those full-year marks were struck largely before the guidance cut. The direction of travel is down. There is no earnings-inflection signal here, and none of the conditions that would justify treating consensus price targets as stale-to-the-upside are present.

Beyond 2027 the growth question narrows to two assets. Jornay PM and Azstarys must carry an eroding opioid base and $1.1B of debt, and the Jornay deferred royalty is a first claim on the growth franchise's cash. A DEA quota constraint, payer step-edit, or ANDA filing against Jornay would hit revenue, cash flow, and royalty coverage at once.

## Valuation Assessment

Two framings, and the gap between them is the entire debate.

**Cash and non-GAAP.** EV of ~$2.04B against $457.5M of adjusted EBITDA is 4.46x, or 4.20x excluding the deferred royalty. Consensus non-GAAP EPS of $7.65 for FY2026 puts the shares at 3.8x, and $7.33 for FY2027 at 4.0x. Free cash flow of $250-290M against a $944M market cap is a 26-31% yield. Even with zero growth, applying two-thirds of that FCF to debt reduction transfers roughly $170-190M per year from creditors to equity holders, which is 18-20% of the current market cap annually.

**GAAP.** Trailing GAAP EPS is $1.73, a 16.8x multiple at $29.11, and Q2 was a GAAP loss. Adjusted EBITDA excludes share-based compensation, acquisition costs, and amortization; the $2.2M of *accelerated* SBC in a single quarter suggests the ongoing run rate is not trivial. Any comparison of COLL's 4.5x to peers must be made against comparably defined peer non-GAAP EBITDA.

**Peers.** Median pharmaceutical peer P/E is 13.4, but the set is heterogeneous and unhelpful: AMPH 12.3x, PAHC 13.4x, INVA 3.9x (a royalty vehicle also priced for terminal decay), PCRX 196x, PRGO negative with a 10.4% yield. The most instructive comparison is INVA at 3.9x — the market applies single-digit multiples to pharmaceutical cash streams it believes are finite. COLL is priced in that cohort, and the pricing is a judgment about terminal value, not about current profitability.

**How much did the print actually re-rate the stock?** EBITDA guidance fell 6.5% at the midpoint; EV fell roughly 9% ($2.25B to $2.04B) and equity fell 18.5%. The equity move is largely the mechanical consequence of leverage applied to a proportionate EV decline. That argues against characterizing the selloff as a sentiment overshoot that the market will simply reverse.

**Scenario values.**

- **Base ($35, +20%):** 2027 adjusted EBITDA roughly flat at $450M, 4.5x EV multiple = $2.03B EV. Six quarters of ~$250M annualized FCF reduces net obligations from ~$1.10B toward ~$930M including the royalty. Equity ~$1.10B on ~32M shares.
- **Bull ($52, +79%):** Jornay's price realization proves durable and Azstarys scripts re-accelerate under Collegium's salesforce, Belbuca settles with generic entry in 2030 or later, EBITDA reaches $500M, multiple expands to 5.25x on restored growth visibility, net obligations ~$880M.
- **Bear ($17, −42%):** Belbuca loses in April 2027 or settles for near-term entry, Xtampza and Nucynta continue contracting, EBITDA falls toward $360M at a 4.0x multiple with net obligations near $1.0B as litigation and refinancing costs consume cash. Leverage amplifies the multiple compression into the equity.

Probability-weighting 30% bear / 44% base / 19% bull, plus a 7% adverse-arbitration tail near $6, gives roughly $30.80, about 6% above spot. A modestly positive point estimate against −42% bear-case downside on a levered residual claim is unfavorable asymmetry.

**Analyst targets.** Consensus is low $43, high $60, mean $53.2, median $56, from five covering analysts (2 strong buy, 2 buy, 1 hold). Those imply 48-106% upside and were set before the guidance cut. On a stock with falling estimates and a reduced guide, targets are a lagging indicator likely to be cut rather than an independent floor; I am not treating them as confirmation.

## Competitive Landscape

**Branded opioid analgesics are contracting structurally.** Prescription volumes are declining, PBMs are pressuring branded pricing, DEA quotas constrain supply, litigation persists, and generics are now substituting directly. Xtampza at −14% and Nucynta at −24% are consistent with sector contraction rather than isolated execution failure. Belbuca's +10% is the exception, plausibly because buprenorphine-based chronic pain therapy sits in a more favorable prescribing and regulatory position than full mu-agonists — and Belbuca's exclusivity is precisely what is under ANDA attack.

**Nucynta is a licensed asset in decline with an adversarial licensor.** Collegium does not own Nucynta; it licenses it from Grünenthal, which filed suit on February 2, 2026 against both Collegium and Hikma relating to the authorized generic, with Hikma seeking indemnification from Collegium. Authorized generics are routine lifecycle practice, and the dispute may be narrow — the alleged breach and damages sought are not disclosed. But the structure matters economically: in an AG arrangement where the partner controls pricing and Collegium participates through supply and profit share, Collegium captures a smaller share of a faster-deflating revenue pool than a brand-only erosion curve implies. That is the most plausible mechanical explanation for the size of the guidance miss.

**ADHD is crowded but growing.** Zevra's 10-K maps the competitive set for Azstarys: Concerta (J&J), Qelbree (Supernus), Quillivant/QuilliChew (Tris), Ritalin and Focalin (Novartis), Metadate CD (UCB), Daytrana (Noven), Jornay PM, Adhansia XR, plus multiple other branded and generic methylphenidate products. Differentiation rests on dosing timing and scheduling. Collegium's two structural advantages are Azstarys IP through 2037 and the Schedule IV prodrug's substitution barrier.

The execution question is price. $706.3M base consideration (up to $841.3M with milestones) against a roughly $100M annualized Azstarys revenue run rate is approximately 7.1x revenue, or 8.4x if all milestones are earned, for an asset whose prescriptions were growing about 2%. That is defensible only if Collegium's commercial infrastructure can materially re-accelerate the script trend, and there is no evidence yet either way.

## Risk Assessment

**1. NSP Pharma arbitration — a claim larger than the market cap.** The Q2 10-Q discloses an arbitration assumed with Ironshore in which the claimant seeks damages in excess of $500M, against a $19.85M indemnity escrow (under 4% coverage). Claimed damages routinely exceed awards by an order of magnitude, and the absence of a material accrual indicates management and its auditors do not consider a material loss both probable and estimable under ASC 450. No hearing date, reserve, or probability assessment is disclosed, so the exposure cannot be sized. Because $1.1B of debt ranks ahead of the equity, an adverse award of even a fraction of the claim would fall disproportionately on shareholders.

**2. Belbuca patent cliff on the largest product, dated April 2027.** An earlier composition patent expires in 2027; the December 2032 method patent is the one that matters. Alvogen's ANDA case is set for trial April 12, 2027. Ascent served a Paragraph IV notice in June 2026, with Collegium suing in July and a 30-month stay running. In January 2026 the court denied BioDelivery's motion to enforce a prior 2022 final judgment on procedural grounds — not a merits ruling, but it removed a shortcut. Trial dates in ANDA litigation slip frequently and settlement with a dated entry is the modal outcome, but the timing correlation is what makes this consequential: an adverse April 2027 result would remove the only growing legacy asset at roughly the moment Nucynta finishes eroding.

**3. Concentration in one growth asset with a prior claim on its cash.** Jornay PM plus Azstarys is about 30% of revenue and effectively all growth, and Jornay carries the ~$121.4M deferred royalty obligation serviced out of its revenue.

**4. Opioid-sector legal and regulatory overhang.** Opioid litigation and government subpoenas, a Walgreens claim exceeding $14M tendered for indemnity, and ongoing Xtampza patent litigation involving Purdue's successor entity Knoa Pharma LLC.

**5. Refinancing.** The November 18, 2028 springing maturity converts the 2029 converts into a 2028 issue and constrains capital allocation through 2027-2028, with liquidity already reduced to $129.5M.

**6. Undisclosed upstream Azstarys economics.** Zevra's 10-K describes tiered royalties on Azstarys net sales plus up to $590M of remaining regulatory and sales milestones under the Commave license, and Collegium acquired the Commave licensee entity itself. Collegium's Q2 10-Q does not itemize an inbound Azstarys royalty. The rate is redacted and it is entirely possible the obligation was bought out, restructured, or retained by non-acquired entities. This is a verification gap rather than an established negative, but if a low-double-digit to mid-teens royalty transferred, Azstarys' contribution margin sits well below Collegium's ~88% base cash gross margin and the effective purchase multiple is higher than 7x revenue.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility of 175-179% is extraordinary for a $944M specialty pharma name and reflects post-print repricing in a thin options market. The put/call open-interest ratio reading collapsed from 7.88 to 0.00 within a single session, which is a data artifact rather than a positioning shift; I draw no directional conclusion from it. The practical read is that options are an expensive way to express either side here, and the data quality does not support using them to confirm or challenge the fundamental view.

Short interest is more informative: 19.3% of float with 10.8 days to cover. Institutional positioning is meaningfully bearish and broadly aligned with the concerns above, but the crowding also means any settlement of the Belbuca litigation, a clean Q3 print, or a strategic development could produce a violent upward move. That is a specific argument against expressing this view through a short position and part of why the recommendation is REDUCE rather than AVOID.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($52, +79%)

Collegium generates $250-290M of annual free cash flow against a $944M market cap, and net leverage of 2.14x is comfortable with nothing due before 2028. Applying most of that cash to debt reduction transfers roughly 18-20% of the current market cap per year from creditors to equity. Nucynta's decline is a known, finite cliff on an in-licensed asset; once it is fully eroded — plausibly during 2027 — the remaining portfolio of Belbuca, Xtampza, Jornay PM, and Azstarys can grow on mix alone. Jornay's net price improvement proves durable, Collegium's ADHD salesforce re-accelerates Azstarys scripts toward the class growth rate, Azstarys IP to 2037 with a Schedule IV prodrug provides the longest exclusivity runway the company has ever owned, and Belbuca settles with generic entry at or beyond 2030. The 2027 amortization step-down of roughly $83M restores GAAP profitability, correcting a screen-level misclassification that currently suppresses the multiple. On stable EBITDA and a 5.25x multiple, the equity is worth roughly $52.

### Bear Case ($17, −42%)

The base business is already declining and the guidance cut arrived within one quarter of a $706M levered acquisition, which raises the question of whether management understands its own erosion curves. Nucynta's decline accelerated from −12% in the half to −24% in the quarter, and Collegium does not control the price at which its own franchise is being cannibalized. Xtampza has turned negative. Jornay's headline 41% growth is 13% scripts plus 28 points of price and channel effects that may not repeat. Azstarys scripts grow 2% and were bought at 7-8x revenue, possibly encumbered by an undisclosed royalty. Belbuca, the only growing legacy asset, goes to trial in April 2027. If it loses, Jornay and Azstarys alone must service $1.1B of debt behind which there is almost no tangible asset coverage, and an arbitration claim exceeding $500M sits unaccrued against a $19.85M escrow. Liquidity has fallen from $387M to $129.5M ahead of a 2028 springing maturity, and the $150M buyback was executed at $34.80-35.81 — roughly 20% above the current price. On $360M of EBITDA at 4.0x, the levered equity is worth about $17.

### Assessment

Both cases are internally coherent, which is why the multiple is 4.5x. Probability-weighted, the expected value sits modestly above spot, but the distribution is wide, the left tail is uncapped by the arbitration claim, and the single largest swing factor — the durability of Jornay's price realization — is not disclosed. The dimensions that historically discriminate most reliably are the weak ones here: management quality (guidance cut post-acquisition, buybacks 20% above spot, sustained insider selling with zero purchases, elevated executive churn, an unreconciled 2025 CEO compensation figure of ~$1.7M that leaves the equity-at-risk question open) and idiosyncratic risk (a dated April 2027 binary on the largest product plus a claim exceeding market cap). Cheapness alone does not offset that combination.

The score trajectory of 6.0 → 5.8 → 5.3 anticipated deterioration; the guidance cut confirms it with specific numbers. Note that the valuation score is held at 6.0 rather than reduced: the price fell 18.5% while EBITDA guidance fell 6.5%, so the shares are mathematically cheaper than at the last review. The downgrade rests on the operating and risk dimensions, not on the price action.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $35 (+20%), bull $52 (+79%), bear $17 (−42%). Probability-weighted expected value of roughly $31 (+6%) is not adequate compensation for a levered equity carrying a dated patent binary and an unaccrued claim larger than its market capitalization.

**De-risking plan.** Trim existing exposure on strength toward $33-35, the level at which the company itself was repurchasing in Q2 and at which the base case offers little residual upside. For holders who retain a position, size it as a speculative levered-cash-flow holding rather than a core one, and do not add ahead of the April 2027 Belbuca trial. Do not short: 19.3% short interest with 10.8 days to cover, a $150M repurchase authorization, and possible litigation settlement all create meaningful squeeze risk against a stock already 42.7% below its 52-week high.

**Upgrade triggers (to HOLD, then ACCUMULATE).**
1. **Q3 2026 print (early November) holds the $825-855M / $445-470M guide** and Jornay prescription growth converges upward toward 20%+, closing the gap to dollar growth and establishing that the price benefit is demand-supported rather than timing.
2. **Belbuca litigation resolves favorably** — an Alvogen settlement with generic entry in 2030 or later, or a merits win at the April 2027 trial. This is the single largest re-rating catalyst.
3. **Deleveraging with the converts addressed:** net funded debt including the deferred royalty below 1.75x adjusted EBITDA and a disclosed plan for the $241.5M of converts well ahead of the November 18, 2028 springing maturity.
4. **A cluster of open-market insider purchases.** Three or more officers or directors buying in the open market below $32 would be the first conviction signal in this file since at least November 2025.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID).**
1. A second cut to FY2026 revenue or adjusted EBITDA guidance, or FY2027 guidance below approximately $800M revenue / $420M EBITDA.
2. An adverse Belbuca outcome, or an Alvogen settlement permitting generic entry before 2029.
3. Any accrual, adverse interim ruling, or award in the NSP Pharma arbitration, or itemized disclosure of a material inbound Azstarys royalty that materially reduces the asset's contribution margin.
4. Jornay dollar growth converging down toward script growth (dollar growth below 20% year over year), which would confirm the 28-point gap was timing.
5. Net leverage above 3.0x including the deferred royalty, or liquidity below $100M with revolver reliance.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next scheduled decision point is the Q3 2026 report in early November 2026, which is the first clean quarter with a full period of Azstarys and the first test of the revised guide. The structural decision point is the April 12, 2027 Alvogen trial, with any settlement announcement likely to precede it by one to two quarters.

## Conclusion

Collegium is a well-run cash extraction machine attached to a portfolio whose exclusivity is contracting faster than management forecast one quarter ago. The cash flow is real: $250-290M annually against a $944M market cap, 4.2-4.5x EV/adjusted EBITDA, 2.14x net leverage, and nothing due before 2028. That is why the stock is not a zero and why the bull case can be argued honestly.

What the August 6 guidance cut established is that the base business no longer grows. Guidance midpoint $840M less $70M of Azstarys equals $770M against $780.6M in FY2025, so all reported 2026 growth was purchased with $356M of cash and $300M of new debt, and liquidity fell from roughly $387M to $129.5M in the process. Two of four legacy franchises are now in decline, the third goes to trial in April 2027, the fourth is licensed from a counterparty currently suing the company, and the growth engine's reported 41% expansion rests substantially on price effects the filings do not explain. Behind that sits $1.1B of debt with negligible tangible asset coverage, a November 2028 springing maturity, and an unaccrued arbitration claim exceeding the market capitalization. Insiders have sold from $34 to $49 without a single open-market purchase, and the company bought back stock at $35.
