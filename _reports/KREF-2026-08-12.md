## Executive Summary

KREF is a levered commercial mortgage REIT in the middle of a credit workout, and the workout is not finished. Book value per share has gone $14.76 (12/31/24) → $13.04 (12/31/25) → $11.87 (3/31/26) → $10.24 (6/30/26), a cumulative decline of 30.6%. The sequence matters more than the level: the quarterly declines were 9.0% and then 13.7%, meaning the most recent mark-down was the largest, and it followed two prior quarters in which management characterized the repositioning reserves as substantially complete. On the Q2 call, CEO Matt Salem conceded the process remains reactive to live bids — "some of this is discovery... where are the clearing values... we haven't finalized all these yet" — with roughly 16% of the portfolio on the watch list and nearly half of that being actively marketed.

Two developments justify moving off the AVOID rating carried in three prior reviews. First, on July 21 the board established a committee of solely independent directors to review strategic alternatives, explicitly including "the sale of the Company or its assets, a merger or other strategic transaction," confirmed independently by Bloomberg. That establishes a mechanism for realizing value above the 0.74x book multiple at which the stock trades. Second, the liability structure and management's own capital commitment argue against the tail-impairment case: 79% of financing is non-mark-to-market, $2,255.5M of $3,030.6M of secured financing principal is non-recourse, KREF states it has never received a margin call on its repurchase facilities, and the CEO and President/COO bought 60,000 and 40,000 shares on the open market at $6.04 and $6.03 two days after announcing a 60% dividend cut.

What does not justify buying it here is the entry price. At $7.60, KREF sits just below the $7.75 consensus mean and 4.8% above the $7.25 median, after rallying 7.6% since mid-June on the review headline while the underlying numbers deteriorated. Current-year consensus EPS has moved from +$0.04 to −$0.53 over 90 days; next-year consensus is down 45.5% to $0.29. Net interest income fell 40% year over year in Q2 to $18.2M, while H1 credit costs of $193.4M ran 4.4x the $44.4M of net interest income generated. Total leverage is 4.3x against management's stated 3.5–4.0x target — higher not because borrowings grew but because equity fell faster than debt. The $0.40 annualized dividend yields 5.3%, against a mortgage-REIT peer median of 14.9%, and is covered at exactly 1.00x on a measure that excludes actual charged-off losses. Probability-weighted, we get roughly flat price return plus the dividend, against a bear case 24% lower. That is a REDUCE.

## Company Overview

KREF originates floating-rate senior mortgage loans on transitional US and, increasingly, European commercial real estate. It funds roughly 65–75% of each loan with repurchase agreements, term lending agreements, CRE CLOs, asset-specific financings, a $700M revolver (now $350M drawn for the first time) and a $643.5M secured term loan, and earns the spread. At June 30, 2026, 98.3% of loans by principal were floating-rate, weighted-average coupon 6.6%, all-in yield 6.8%, weighted-average LTV 66% excluding risk-rated 5 loans, average commitment $101.3M, and weighted-average maximum maturity 1.9 years assuming all borrower extension options are exercised.

The company has no employees. It is managed by an indirect KKR subsidiary for a base fee of 1.5% of adjusted equity annually plus 20% of trailing-twelve-month Distributable Earnings above a 7% hurdle. Q2 2026 management fee was $5.382M ($10.893M in H1); no incentive compensation was earned in either period, consistent with earnings running below the hurdle. Total related-party fees were $7.389M in Q2. KKR beneficially owned 10,000,001 shares, 17.1% of the common, at June 30 — a fixed absolute holding whose percentage has risen because KREF has been retiring stock, not because KKR has been buying.

Portfolio mix at June 30 (December 31, 2025 in parentheses): multifamily 45.9% (40.3%), office 21.1% (22.8%), industrial 14.3% (17.9%), life science 12.4% (13.7%), hospitality 3.3%, student housing 2.5%. Geographically, the UK rose to 7.3% from 2.9% and Spain to 3.5% from zero; management targets Europe at 20–25% of the portfolio.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings, on three bases.** Q2 2026 GAAP net loss to common was $(121.8)M, or $(1.95) per share, against $(61.9)M / $(0.96) in Q1 and $(35.4)M / $(0.53) a year earlier. Distributable Earnings were $(0.58) in Q2 and $(0.06) in Q1. Distributable Earnings *before realized losses* — management's preferred coverage measure — were +$0.10 and +$0.20. The pre-print third-party consensus of −$0.31 does not identify its basis; the result missed on every measure that can be constructed.

**Net interest income is contracting quickly.** Interest income fell to $85.5M in Q2 from $95.9M in Q1 and $112.3M a year earlier; interest expense fell to $67.3M from $69.7M and $82.1M. Net interest income of $18.2M was down 40% year over year and 31% sequentially. H1 net interest income of $44.4M compares with $61.5M. The mechanism compounds: heavy repayments shrink earning assets, nonaccruals strip yield from assets still carried on the balance sheet, and the resulting equity erosion raises leverage even as the portfolio shrinks.

**Credit costs dominate the P&L.** H1 2026 provision for credit losses was $148.6M (versus $74.7M in H1 2025), plus a $44.8M fair-value markdown on a loan moved to held-for-sale — the Philadelphia loan was marked from $115.1M amortized cost to $70.4M, a 39% haircut, which is a useful calibration of how far actual bids can sit below carrying value. Combined H1 credit cost of $193.4M was 4.4x net interest income. The allowance rose to $291.9M from $201.9M at year-end 2025 and $117.1M at year-end 2024; as a percentage of outstanding loan principal it went from 3.77% to 6.65%, the ratio rising both because the numerator grew and the denominator shrank. Total reserves in book value equal $4.97 per share against a $10.24 book.

**Risk migration has not stopped.** Risk-rated 5 loans went from 2 loans / $305.7M / 5% of the portfolio at year-end 2024, to 4 / $572.2M / 11% at year-end 2025, to 5 / $674.1M / 15.0% at June 30. Two loans migrated from 4 to 5 in Q2 alone (Chicago office $90.7M with $14.3M unfunded; Carrollton TX multifamily $43.7M), and a $42M Dallas multifamily loan moved from 3 to 4. Weighted-average portfolio risk rating drifted 3.1 → 3.2 → 3.3. Collateral valuations on the risk-5 loans used cap rates of 5.9%–13.3% and discount rates of 9.3%–14.9%, a range wide enough to signal how judgment-dependent these marks remain.

**A covenant worth monitoring.** KREF's facilities include a trailing-four-quarter interest-income-to-interest-expense covenant of 1.30:1. Computed from reported GAAP lines, Q2 standalone coverage was $85.487M ÷ $67.305M = 1.270x; Q1 was 1.376x; H1 combined 1.324x. Excluding $2.8M of deferred financing cost amortization from Q2 expense lifts it to 1.325x. Three caveats are material: the test is trailing four quarters and is currently carried by stronger 2025 quarters; credit-agreement definitions almost certainly differ from GAAP presentation, given that consolidated CLO and CMBS trust gross-ups inflate both lines; and KREF affirmatively states it was in compliance with all financial covenants at June 30, 2026. This is a sensitivity, not a breach. It is nonetheless the covenant most exposed to additional nonaccruals — each one removes interest income while the matched financing keeps accruing expense — and it appears nowhere in the earnings presentation.

**Liquidity and other covenant headroom.** Stated liquidity was $721.6M ($83.1M cash, $350.0M undrawn revolver, $33.8M available under existing arrangements, $254.8M of repayments held at a servicer), down from $886.6M. The gap between management's framing on the call ("$2.6 billion of undrawn capacity") and the filed figure for borrowings available against already-approved and pledged collateral ($33.8M) is worth holding in mind; both are accurate, only the latter is immediately drawable. The secured term loan requires minimum consolidated tangible net worth of $650.0M against $931.8M of KREF stockholders' equity — $281.8M of headroom, versus $172.5M of net loss booked in H1 alone. The revolver requires $75.0M minimum cash against $83.1M actual.

**Leverage and non-earning capital.** Debt-to-equity rose to 2.6x from 2.1x and total leverage to 4.3x from 3.9x, above the 3.5–4.0x target. REO held-for-investment ($360.4M), REO held-for-sale ($132.6M) and real-estate equity method investments ($224.6M) total $648.3M of investment amount — slightly more than the $604.0M of common book equity — in assets that lost $4.0M in Q2 and $7.2M in H1. Add $674.1M of nonaccrual risk-5 loans, which produced $9.7M of Q2 interest income versus roughly $19M at the portfolio coupon.

**One accounting artifact to discard.** Total assets rose to $6,559.3M from $6,464.6M while net loans held-for-investment fell $1,064.6M to $4,081.2M. The increase came from consolidated CMBS trust assets rising $736.1M; those liabilities are non-recourse and KREF's net economic interest in the consolidated trusts was $24.4M. Total assets are not a measure of exposure here.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth to analyze in the near term, by design and by circumstance. Management expects repayments above $2B for 2026 — more than 35% of the beginning-of-year portfolio, against roughly $1.5B in each of 2024 and 2025 — and is deliberately slowing originations because leverage sits above target. Q2 originations were three loans totaling roughly $350M at 58% weighted-average LTV, at spreads of +2.6% to +2.9% versus legacy office loans at +2.3% to +4.4%. Salem put target levered ROE on new money at "12% area" and noted some legacy loans out-earn that.

Estimate revisions confirm the direction. Current-quarter consensus fell 33.9% in 30 days and 40.3% in 90 days to $0.09; next-quarter consensus went to −$0.00 from $0.06; current-year moved from +$0.04 to −$0.53; next-year fell 45.5% to $0.29. This is the mirror image of the inflection pattern that would justify looking past trailing losses. Estimates are falling while the stock has risen 7.6% since mid-June.

The genuine recovery mechanism is redeployment. Roughly $1.1B of capital currently sits in non-earning REO, equity-method real estate and nonaccrual loans. Recycled at a 12% levered ROE it would be a large increment to earnings. But management's own resolution timeline is staggered across years: West Hollywood condos selling now, Raleigh multifamily near-term, Philadelphia office REO "this year," Mountain View "second quarter next year," Portland medium-term, and Seattle and South Boston life science explicitly "longer term." Deleveraging from 4.3x toward 3.5–4.0x also constrains how much released capital can be re-levered.

Portfolio quality on new money is real. The 2025 and 2026 vintages ($1,491.6M combined amortized cost) are entirely rated 3 or better. Of the $674.1M of risk-5 loans, $578.8M — 86% — originated in 2022 or earlier, on business plans underwritten near zero SOFR. This is more a vintage problem than an underwriting-culture problem, which is the constructive read. But the legacy book still dominates.

## Valuation Assessment

At $7.60 against a June 30 book value of $10.24, KREF trades at 0.742x stated book, a 25.8% discount. Adjusting for July repurchases (1,397,265 shares at $7.24), the multiple is essentially unchanged at roughly 0.74x. Undepreciated book was $10.38.

Peer comparison is unhelpful on earnings — KREF has none — and unflattering on income. Mortgage REIT peers in the comparable set show a median P/E of 8.4x and a median dividend yield of 14.9%; ARI, FBRT, PMT and RWT all yield 10.5%–17.1%. KREF yields 5.3%. Peers trade 31%–40% below their 52-week highs; KREF is 23.9% below its $9.983 high and 44.8% above its $5.25 April low. On next-year consensus EPS of $0.29, KREF trades at 26x. Neither the earnings multiple nor the yield supports the stock. Price-to-book is the only anchor, and the denominator has fallen 30.6% in eighteen months and is still being discovered against live bids.

The valuation case therefore rests on two catalysts rather than on a static multiple.

**Buybacks.** Q2 repurchases of 5,722,469 shares at a $6.63 average cost $37.95M and generated approximately $0.35 per share of book value accretion on our calculation (5.72M × ($10.24 − $6.63) = $20.66M ÷ 59.01M shares), reproducing management's ~$0.32 figure within rounding. Converting $1 of cash into roughly $1.55 of book value with no credit or execution risk is a better use of capital than a 12% target ROE on fresh CRE exposure. Share count has gone from 68.7M at year-end 2024 to roughly 57.2M. Salem stated that future repurchases "will be part of the strategic review process" and would require a conversation with the committee, and remaining authorization was $37.0M at June 30. The April 2026 modified program also permits repurchase of Series A preferred, which trades at a discount to its $25.00 liquidation preference — a third-party report from March suggested a yield above 9%, implying roughly $17.70, but this is unconfirmed, five months stale and no preferred repurchases have been disclosed.

**The strategic review.** Announced July 21 and disclosed in Note 19, it contemplates sale of the company or assets, a merger, or continuation of the business plan, with no definitive timetable. KKR is simultaneously the Manager collecting roughly $22M a year, the 17.1% shareholder, a co-investor in several workout assets, and a potential bidder; Salem stated on the call that "KKR has not submitted a proposal for any transaction to date." A friction any acquirer must price: non-renewal of the management agreement other than for cause triggers a fee of 3x the trailing 24-month average of management fees plus incentive compensation, which on observed fees of roughly $22.3M annually implies approximately $67M, or $1.14 per share — about 11% of common book value. Whether a merger or asset sale triggers it at all is not disclosed.

Scenario framework from the $7.60 basis:

| Case | Price | Return | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bull | $9.50 | +25.0% | Transaction at roughly 0.90–0.95x an adjusted book near $10, or credit marks settle and buybacks resume with book stabilizing. Matches the $9.50 analyst high. |
| Base | $7.85 | +3.3% | Review extends into 2027, one further modest reserve quarter takes book to $9.50–9.75, stock holds ~0.80x. Near the $7.75 consensus mean. |
| Bear | $5.75 | −24.3% | Additional marks take book to $8.50–9.00, review concludes with continuation, dividend held at $0.40 with no coverage cushion, multiple compresses to 0.65x. 52-week low is $5.25. |

Weighting the base case at 45%, bear at 30% and bull at 25% gives an expected price of $7.63, or +0.4%, and roughly +5.7% including the $0.40 dividend. For a 4.3x-levered CRE credit book in active workout with 12% short interest, that is below a fair required return.

## Competitive Landscape

KREF competes with Blackstone Mortgage Trust, Starwood Property Trust, Claros Mortgage Trust, Ladder Capital, Arbor Realty and TPG RE Finance, plus banks, debt funds, insurers and the CMBS market.

The genuine competitive asset is access to the KKR real assets platform: a global valuation committee, K-Star as an affiliated special servicer, KKR Capital Markets for financing execution, and the ability to co-invest with KKR affiliates in workouts. In a workout cycle this is a real capability, and it is visible in the restructuring template — the Cambridge life-science loan was recut in March with a $20.2M principal repayment, a $17.3M subordinated piece written off, $14.4M of new sponsor equity injected, and the surviving $62.9M senior re-rated 3. It is also the source of the conflict: the Mountain View office JV (68.9% KREF / 31.1% KKR affiliate) and the Seattle life-science tenant-in-common (74.6% / 25.4%) both carry shared decision-making on Level 3-valued assets, meaning a meaningful slice of book value is not arm's-length observable.

Where KREF is idiosyncratically exposed is life science: 12.4% of loans plus two REO/JV assets. Management has now modified 39% of life-science exposure, up from 19% at year-end, and Salem's own read is that Boston-area markets are "further behind" the West Coast. The offsetting cross-current is AI-driven office absorption. The Mountain View JV executed a full-building lease with OpenAI as tenant this year, expects partial occupancy this quarter, and plans to bring the property to market by Q2 2027. Lease economics — rent, term, credit support, free-rent structure — appear only in oral management remarks with no filed disclosure, so the value impact cannot be quantified.

The property-mix shift should not be read as de-risking. Multifamily rose to 45.9% from 40.3% largely because office and industrial principal repaid; office principal fell roughly $296M in absolute terms. Meanwhile multifamily produced two new risk-5 loans (San Diego $115.7M at $500,787 per unit; Carrollton TX $43.7M) and a new risk-4. Every direct measure of concentration and quality — average risk rating, risk-5 share, allowance ratio, and repo counterparty exposure as a percentage of equity (64.0% → 69.3%, with Morgan Stanley at 36.2%) — moved the wrong way over the same period the mix "diversified."

## Risk Assessment

**Credit migration is still running.** Risk-5 exposure has tripled as a share of the portfolio in six quarters. Nearly half of the 16% watch list is being marketed into markets management itself calls illiquid, with a target of complete reduction by year-end 2026 — a nine-month timeline for more than $674M of impaired assets.

**Unfunded commitments into impaired collateral.** Total future funding commitments were $360.0M, with $241.8M due within a year, including $82.9M unfunded on the risk-5 Boston life-science loan and $14.3M on the risk-5 Chicago office loan. Fundings are conditioned on construction certifications, debt-yield tests or new leases, which provides partial protection, but KREF may be obliged to advance capital into assets it has already reserved against.

**Near-dated maturities on impaired loans.** Minneapolis office ($194.4M, nonaccrual since June 2023) had a maximum maturity of July 2026; Carrollton multifamily matures August 2026; San Diego multifamily November 2026. On the financing side, $366.1M of secured financing matures in 2026 and $1,011.6M in 2027.

**Dividend coverage.** Q2 Distributable Earnings before realized losses of $5.969M covered $5.858M of common plus $0.114M of participating dividends — 1.00x, with no cushion. Including realized losses, H1 DE was approximately −$0.64 per share against $0.35 declared. Operating cash flow of $27.8M in H1 fell short of $32.2M common plus $10.7M preferred dividends paid, with the $15.1M gap funded from repayment proceeds. The REIT distribution test is based on taxable income, which excludes unrealized CECL provisions, so $0.40 may satisfy the legal requirement comfortably; the economic coverage is thin.

**Rate positioning is ambiguous.** MD&A narrative still says rising rates increase net income, but the sensitivity table shows both a 100bp decrease and a 100bp increase would increase expected next-quarter cash flows ($1.6M and $0.4M respectively) — a rate-floor and nonaccrual artifact. The portfolio is now close to rate-neutral, and the qualitative and quantitative disclosures are not aligned.

**European execution.** UK 7.3%, Spain 3.5%, plus a French industrial position, targeting 20–25%. FX forwards are not designated as accounting hedges, generating P&L noise (a $4.2M forward loss against a $1.5M translation gain in Q2, reversed in Q1).

**Earnings visibility is poor.** The trailing four quarters produced two beats and two large misses (−152.5% and −129.4% surprise). That argues for wide confidence intervals around the $0.29 next-year consensus.

**Positioning.** Short interest is 12.0% of float at 4.3 days to cover. That is high enough to create a sharp squeeze on any deal headline, which is a specific reason to prefer trimming over shorting. J.P. Morgan downgraded KREF to Underweight around July 17, four days before the print and the review announcement; sell-side distribution has since shifted to 2 buy / 3 hold / 1 strong sell from 1 / 4 / 1.

**Unresolved items.** A January 9, 2026 Item 5.02 8-K remains unread; no executive departure is evident since Salem, Mattson and CFO Decious all signed or transacted subsequently. Board chair Ralph Rosenberg's December 3, 2025 sale of 125,289 shares at $8.25 to zero *direct* holdings does not establish that he holds no indirect economic interest through KKR entities, and his current title was not proxy-verified. Whether a $74.4M nonaccrual mezzanine loan is additive to the $674.1M of risk-5 principal is ambiguous; minimum verified nonaccrual is 15.0% of loan principal and possibly 16.6%.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($9.50).** The Q2 mark-down is qualitatively different from Q4's and Q1's: the $44.8M held-for-sale adjustment and the shift toward note sales and short sales mean marks are being set against actual market feedback rather than models, so Q2 may be closer to a clearing level. Salem made a specific, falsifiable commitment on the largest remaining problem — the Boston life-science loan — stating KREF is "fully reserved" and does "not anticipate negative book value implications on that particular asset going forward." If that holds, book stabilizes near $10, the CEO and COO's April purchases at $6.04 look well timed, and the strategic review produces a transaction. A whole-company sale or asset-level liquidation at 0.90–0.95x book, net of frictions, lands in the $9.00–9.50 range. Continued buybacks at a 26% discount to book add roughly $0.30–0.35 per share per $40M deployed. The 2025–2026 vintages at 58% LTV are clean, repayments above $2B release capital, and Bay Area and Seattle assets carry an option on AI-driven space absorption that the Mountain View OpenAI lease has already partly monetized.

**Bear case ($5.75).** Management has said the most significant book value impact is behind it for three consecutive quarters, and each quarter's decline was worse than the last; BTIG pressed exactly this on the Q2 call and the answer conceded that clearing values are still being discovered. Sixteen percent of the portfolio remains on the watch list, two new risk-5 loans appeared in June, multifamily — the growing share of the book — is now generating problems, and Boston life science is the market management itself says is furthest behind. Net interest income at $18.2M a quarter cannot absorb credit costs running at $97M a quarter. Leverage at 4.3x is above target and rising through equity erosion, which simultaneously compresses the interest-coverage test, shrinks tangible net worth headroom ($281.8M against $172.5M of H1 losses), and raises repo counterparty exposure to 69.3% of equity. The strategic review may end in "continuation of its business plan"; KKR has submitted nothing, the $1.14-per-share termination-fee friction and the external management structure narrow the buyer set, and buybacks — the most reliable book value defense — are now subject to committee approval during the exact quarters when marks are still settling. On $0.40 of dividends and no earnings, a return to 0.65x a book of $8.75 is $5.69.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $7.85 (+3.3%), bull $9.50 (+25.0%), bear $5.75 (−24.3%). Probability-weighted expected return of roughly +0.4% on price and +5.7% including the dividend does not compensate for a 4.3x-levered credit book whose marks are still being discovered.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into the current review-driven strength rather than waiting. The stock is up 7.6% since mid-June and 44.8% off the April low on a headline, not on results; the Q3 print (expected late October 2026) is the next asymmetric event, with a live risk of another reserve quarter. Investors who want to retain optionality on a transaction should hold no more than a residual position and should treat the $9.00–9.50 area as the exit for that residual.

**Upgrade triggers to HOLD or better.**
1. Book value per share flat or up quarter over quarter at Q3 2026, with the allowance ratio stable at or below 6.65% and no new loan migrating to risk-5.
2. A definitive 8-K disclosing a merger agreement, asset-portfolio sale, or externalization/internalization transaction at an implied value of 0.85x book or better.
3. Total leverage back inside 3.5–4.0x with the watch list reduced below 10% of the portfolio, and continued repurchases authorized by the review committee at a discount to book.

**Downgrade triggers back to AVOID.**
1. Any disclosure of a financial covenant waiver, amendment, or non-compliance — with the trailing-four-quarter interest-coverage test (1.30:1) and the $650.0M minimum tangible net worth as the two specific items to monitor.
2. Book value declining another 10%+ in Q3, or risk-5 exposure exceeding 18% of loan principal.
3. Termination of the strategic review with a stated continuation of the existing plan, absent book value stabilization.
4. Suspension of the common dividend, or a first-ever margin call on a repurchase facility.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q3 2026 results, expected late October, plus any interim 8-K from the strategic review committee. A transaction announcement at any point supersedes this framework.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory into this review had been improving (3.9 → 4.1 → 4.2) on three consecutive AVOID ratings, and the composite now lands modestly lower at 3.95 while the recommendation moves up to REDUCE. That divergence is deliberate. The dimension scores fell because Q2 delivered genuine fundamental deterioration — the largest quarterly book value decline of the cycle, net interest income down 40% year over year, current-year consensus swinging from +$0.04 to −$0.53, and next-year estimates cut 45.5%. The recommendation improved because the specific concerns that supported AVOID — no visible path to value realization, and tail risk of forced deleveraging — are partially answered by an independent-director strategic review that explicitly contemplates a sale, by a liability structure that is 79% non-mark-to-market with no margin call in the company's history, by affirmed covenant compliance, and by the two executives who set the reserve marks committing personal capital at $6.03–6.04.

What remains is an unattractive entry. KREF trades at 0.74x a book value that has fallen 30.6% in eighteen months and is being marked against live bids on 16% of the portfolio; it yields 5.3% against a peer median of 14.9%; and it sits just below the consensus mean target after a headline-driven rally. Existing holders have been handed liquidity into which to trim ahead of the October print. Prospective buyers should wait either for confirmation that book value has stopped falling or for a signed transaction — and should recognize that the price at which the risk/reward turns favorable is closer to the mid-$6s than to $7.60.
