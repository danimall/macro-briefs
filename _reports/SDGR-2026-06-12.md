**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Schrödinger runs two businesses with very different economics under one roof. The software segment, a physics-based molecular-simulation platform licensed to large pharma, biotech, and materials customers, is a high-margin, recurring-revenue franchise growing in the mid-teens, with 2026 annual contract value (ACV) guided to $218M–$228M. The drug discovery segment applies that platform internally and through partnerships, generating lumpy milestone, royalty, and equity-stake returns. The company remains unprofitable on a GAAP basis, with FY2025 net loss of roughly $(103)M against ~$256M revenue, and consensus earnings estimates have moved sharply lower over the past 90 days (FY26 EPS revised from roughly breakeven to $(2.23), an 80.9% downward revision).

The bull case rests on the "platform → co-founded biotech → big-pharma exit" flywheel, which received its clearest validation yet on April 27, 2026, when Eli Lilly agreed to acquire Ajax Therapeutics for up to $2.3B. Ajax is a Schrödinger co-founded company whose lead asset (AJ1-11095, a Type II JAK2 inhibitor for myelofibrosis) was designed on the Schrödinger platform. Schrödinger holds an equity stake carried at modest book value, so any realized proceeds would flow through as a non-operating, non-recurring gain and validate the model. The stock has nonetheless fallen 36% over the trailing year and sits near the low end of its 52-week range ($10.95–$23.75), reflecting depressed sentiment, lagging total shareholder return versus peers (22.6 vs. 125.6 over the proxy measurement period), and 24.9% short interest.

We rate SDGR a HOLD with an "uncertain" classification. The software franchise is genuinely differentiated and the Ajax catalyst is real, but it is a one-time event that does not change the recurring cash-burn trajectory, and falling estimates plus a negative forward P/E (no GAAP earnings to anchor to) make this a stock trading on optionality rather than fundamentals. We need either a clear path toward operating breakeven or accelerating software ACV growth before the risk/reward justifies a long.

## Company Overview

Schrödinger, incorporated in 1990 and based in New York, develops a computational platform built on quantum mechanics and molecular dynamics that predicts molecular properties before synthesis, compressing early-stage drug and materials discovery. The company reports two segments:

- **Software**, the core, high-margin engine. Customers license the platform on subscription/hosted terms; the user base includes most large global pharmaceutical companies plus biotech, materials, electronics, and aerospace firms. Two 2026 developments matter: a transition toward hosted (cloud) delivery and an early-access release of a new product ("Bunsen").
- **Drug Discovery**, which applies the platform through collaborations (fee-for-service plus milestones and royalties, including a Novartis agreement) and proprietary/co-founded programs where Schrödinger takes equity stakes in spinouts.

A deliberate strategic pivot is underway: Schrödinger is retreating from capital-intensive independent late-stage clinical development and refocusing on the software platform plus partnered and royalty-bearing discovery. This concedes the highest-upside (but highest-risk) value capture in exchange for a model better matched to software economics. The company employs approximately 850 people.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** FY2025 revenue represented +23% growth over FY2024's ~$208M, continuing a multi-year climb ($138M FY21 → $181M FY22 → $217M FY23 → $208M FY24 → $256M FY25). The FY24 decline against FY23 illustrates the central forecasting problem: drug-discovery milestone revenue is lumpy and distorts the top line. The cleaner signal is software ACV, which Q1 2026 put at $28.4M (+12% YoY).

**Profitability.** The company remains GAAP-unprofitable. Net loss ran $(187)M in FY24 and a positive $+41M in FY23. These swings are driven less by operating instability than by non-cash mark-to-market on equity stakes in co-founded companies and milestone timing. R&D spend of approximately $173M is the dominant cost line and the primary reason for the persistent losses. Investors should track software ACV and recurring subscription growth as the truer measure of underlying health; GAAP net income is a poor guide here.

**Earnings surprise pattern.** The last four quarters show three beats and one miss, but the pattern is noisy: Q4 2025 beat by 339.7% (a $0.45 actual EPS vs. $(0.19) estimate, almost certainly driven by a non-operating gain), while Q1 2026 missed by 25.2% ($(0.81) vs. $(0.65)). The volatility confirms that quarterly EPS is not a reliable forward signal for this business.

**Estimate revisions.** This is the most concerning data point. FY2027 consensus EPS has moved from $(1.02) to $(1.56), a 53.4% deterioration. Whatever the mechanical drivers (likely reflecting a reset of equity-stake and milestone assumptions to a more conservative baseline), the direction of earnings momentum is negative. This works directly against any inflection-stock thesis: rising estimates plus accelerating revenue is a strong buy setup, but here estimates are falling.

**Balance sheet.** The headline Altman Z-Score near 1.44 ("distress zone") is misleading. Schrödinger carries little traditional debt; reported liabilities are dominated by deferred revenue, which is prepaid customer cash and a positive for a subscription business, and operating leases. The company holds a large cash and marketable-securities position funding its burn, so near-term liquidity risk is low. The central financial question is the burn rate and whether the company can move toward breakeven before needing to return to the capital markets, which would be dilutive.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth engine is software. Mid-teens ACV growth, the hosted-delivery transition, and the Bunsen launch all point to a healthy recurring franchise. Materials-science applications offer optionality beyond pharma. This is the part of the business that deserves a software multiple.

The drug-discovery and co-founded-company engine offers higher-magnitude but far less predictable growth. The Ajax/Lilly transaction demonstrates that the flywheel can produce large returns; Nimbus was an earlier proof point. Lilly's near-weekly acquisition cadence and Big Pharma's appetite for early-stage M&A are genuine tailwinds for a company that co-founds platform-designed biotechs. But these returns are episodic, illiquid until a liquidity event, and impossible to model quarter to quarter.

The combination produces a growth profile that is real but hard to underwrite. Software revenue growth is steady; total company growth and earnings are dominated by lumpy, non-recurring items. With consensus estimates falling rather than rising, the inflection-stock buy signal that would normally accompany revenue growth of this magnitude is absent. We score growth prospects at the midpoint to reflect a solid software trajectory offset by deteriorating earnings visibility.

## Valuation Assessment

SDGR cannot be valued on trailing or forward P/E — there are no GAAP earnings, and the forward P/E of approximately -9.3 is meaningless. The relevant frameworks are (a) a software multiple on recurring ACV/revenue and (b) the option value of the equity-stake portfolio and royalty streams.

At a ~$1.09B market cap against FY25 revenue and a 2026 ACV midpoint of ~$223M, the stock trades around 4–4.5x revenue. That is a discount to high-growth software comps, but the discount is partly warranted: roughly a third of revenue is lumpy drug-discovery income that does not deserve a software multiple, and the company is burning cash. The lone same-sector peer provided (BridgeBio, P/E -18.1, 20% below its 52-week high) is not a clean comparable, as it is a clinical-stage pharma rather than a software/discovery hybrid.

The analyst consensus is constructive: six buys, two holds, a mean target of $20.88 and median of $20.00 against a $14.60 price, with a range of $13.00–$30.00. That implies ~43% upside to the mean. We treat these targets with caution: on a stock with falling estimates, negative GAAP earnings, and a value substantially dependent on episodic equity-stake gains, consensus targets reflect a sum-of-the-parts optimism that requires the flywheel to keep delivering Ajax-scale exits. They are not the kind of "floor" that lagging targets on a cheap, accelerating-estimate stock would be.

We score valuation at 4.5. The revenue multiple is not stretched, but the absence of a profitability anchor, the falling estimate trajectory, and the dependence on non-recurring catalysts to justify the upside leave us unable to identify a clean, falsifiable mispricing in either direction.

## Competitive Landscape

Schrödinger's differentiation is physics-based, first-principles simulation rather than purely data-driven machine learning. Proponents argue this generalizes better to novel chemical space where training data is sparse. The competitive moat is reinforced by more than two decades of accumulated proprietary methods and validation data, and by an expanding roster of externally validated proof points: multiple co-founded biotechs (Nimbus, now Ajax/Lilly) produced platform-designed molecules that attracted multi-billion-dollar pharma interest.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. The validation events strengthen the platform's credibility and the moat is real. At the same time, the field is crowded with well-funded AI-biotech entrants and Big Tech cloud/AI offerings that could commoditize parts of the discovery stack, and the company's total shareholder return has badly lagged its peer group. We score competitive position at 6.0: a differentiated and improving platform position, tempered by genuine competitive pressure from ML-only approaches and the risk that "good enough and cheaper" wins some use cases.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Lumpy, milestone-dependent revenue** makes quarterly results volatile and forecasting difficult; the Q1 2026 miss and Q4 2025 beat illustrate the swings.
2. **Falling consensus estimates** signal negative earnings momentum and undermine the inflection thesis.
3. **Customer/partner concentration** — a meaningful share of revenue comes from a small number of large pharma collaborators; non-renewal of a major customer would be material.
4. **Equity-stake valuation volatility** — carrying values of stakes in private co-founded companies swing net income and are illiquid until a liquidity event.
5. **Hosted/cloud transition execution risk** could disrupt revenue recognition timing and ACV optics during 2026.
6. **Strategic pivot risk** — stepping back from independent clinical development concedes the highest-upside value capture; platform monetization is now paramount.
7. **Cash burn and dilution** — persistent losses mean continued capital-markets reliance if the burn does not move toward breakeven.

We score risk factors at 4.0. The specific, near-term risks — falling estimates, milestone lumpiness, transition execution, and a burn rate with no stated breakeven path — are material and not fully offset by the cash position. These are idiosyncratic risks that can move the stock against the current optimistic consensus.

### Options Market Signal

Options data shows ATM implied volatility falling from 106.2% to 85.1% between early-May snapshots while the price rose 5.7% to $13.10, and the put/call open-interest ratio rising modestly from 0.05 to 0.13. The IV compression suggests some resolution of near-term uncertainty (consistent with digestion of the Ajax news and Q1 results) rather than an impending binary event. The low absolute put/call ratio indicates the options market is not aggressively positioned for downside, which sits in tension with the 24.9% short interest in the equity: the bearish bet is being expressed through borrow, not puts. Elevated absolute IV (85%+) confirms the market continues to price this as a high-uncertainty name.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** The software franchise compounds in the mid-teens to its guided 2026 ACV and beyond, with the hosted transition and Bunsen expanding the addressable market into materials science. The co-founded-biotech flywheel keeps producing Ajax-scale exits, with Lilly's deal delivering quantifiable, non-recurring cash that the market underappreciates. Sentiment is washed out, setting up a squeeze on positive catalysts. Analyst mean target implies ~43% upside. Bull case: $24–30.

**Bear Case.** Falling estimates prove prescient: software ACV growth decelerates, the hosted transition disrupts revenue recognition, and drug-discovery milestones disappoint. The Ajax gain is recognized as a one-time event that does nothing to change a structural cash-burn problem, forcing dilutive capital raises. ML-only competitors erode pricing on commoditizable use cases. The stock revisits the low-$11 area (recent support) or the 52-week low near $10.95, roughly 25–33% downside.

**Base Case.** The software business performs in line with guidance, the Ajax proceeds provide a one-time boost without changing the trajectory toward breakeven, and the stock oscillates in the mid-teens as the market waits for the next proof point. We see no clean directional edge from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The thesis for holding rather than avoiding rests on a specific, identifiable asset: a differentiated, growing software franchise that retains real value and supports a ~4–4.5x revenue floor, combined with a validated co-founded-biotech flywheel and a low near-term liquidity risk given the cash-and-securities cushion. We hold because the differentiated platform and the cash-rich balance sheet make the downside less severe than the falling-estimate optics suggest, while the absence of a breakeven path and negative earnings momentum prevent a confident long.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (expected early August 2026) and any 8-K quantifying Schrödinger's realized proceeds from the Ajax/Lilly transaction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Software ACV growth re-accelerates above ~15% YoY for two consecutive quarters AND consensus estimate revisions turn positive over a 90-day window.
- Management articulates and demonstrates progress toward a credible operating-cash-breakeven path (e.g., cash burn narrowing meaningfully quarter over quarter).
- A second Ajax-scale liquidity event materializes, confirming the flywheel is repeatable rather than episodic.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Software ACV growth decelerates below ~8% YoY, signaling the core franchise is stalling.
- Cash burn accelerates and management signals a near-term equity raise at depressed prices.
- A major software customer/collaborator non-renewal is disclosed, or estimate revisions deteriorate by a further 20%+ over 90 days.

## Conclusion

Schrödinger is a differentiated business: a physics-based simulation platform with a growing recurring-revenue base and a validated mechanism for converting that platform into large, episodic biotech exits. The Eli Lilly–Ajax deal is the strongest external proof yet that the model works. But the investment case today is built on optionality rather than fundamentals: there are no GAAP earnings to anchor a valuation, consensus estimates are falling sharply, the cash-burn trajectory has no stated breakeven, and 24.9% short interest reflects a meaningful bear consensus that the options market has not contradicted. The 36% price decline over the trailing year accompanies deteriorating earnings estimates rather than purely sentiment, so we do not treat the drop as a clean buying opportunity. We rate SDGR a HOLD and will revisit on the next earnings print and the Ajax proceeds disclosure, looking for either re-accelerating software ACV with positive estimate revisions or a credible path toward breakeven before moving to a constructive stance.
