**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Teledyne is a diversified industrial-technology conglomerate built through a disciplined acquire-and-integrate playbook into four segments: Digital Imaging (52% of sales, anchored by FLIR), Instrumentation (24%, highest margin), Aerospace & Defense Electronics (17%, fastest-growing), and Engineered Systems (7%). FY2025 revenue was $6.12B (+7.9%), operating margin expanded to 18.8%, and free cash flow of $1,074M converted at roughly 120% of net income. The business has real moats (niche, design-in-locked product lines with no single commercial customer above 10% of sales) and is a direct beneficiary of rising U.S. and European defense budgets. Q1 2026 confirmed momentum: sales +7.6%, net income +20.3%, EPS $4.85 vs $3.99, and a backlog of $4.87B with ~71% recognizable within twelve months.

The investment question is price and tail risk, not business quality. At $615 the stock trades at ~25x consensus FY2026 EPS ($24.16) and ~33x trailing ($18.88), against organic growth that is mid-single-digit once acquisitions are stripped out. The consensus mean target of $737 implies meaningful upside, but the company's own cumulative TSR has lagged its peer group (132 vs 193), and two specific overhangs justify discounting the bull case. First, the FLIR reporting unit's FY2025 impairment test showed fair value only ~8% above carrying value, and the FLIR trademark only ~3%, with management itself adding 50bps of discount-rate risk premium for FLIR's chronic forecast misses. A modest forecast cut or rate move could trigger a multi-billion-dollar non-cash impairment. Second, a January 2026 executive order contemplates barring "major defense contractors" from buybacks and dividends; TDY made zero repurchases in Q1 2026 despite $1.6B authorized, which reads as caution.

The setup is a high-quality compounder with a fair-to-full valuation and two identifiable binary-ish risks that the market has partly, but not fully, discounted. We rate TDY HOLD with a constructive bias, upgradeable on either a meaningful pullback toward the low-$500s or resolution of the buyback/impairment overhangs.

## Company Overview

Teledyne was spun off from Allegheny Teledyne in 1999 and has compounded primarily through acquisitions, the largest being FLIR (~$8B, May 2021). The four reporting segments for FY2025:

- **Digital Imaging — $3.16B (52%), 16.7% op margin.** Visible/infrared/X-ray sensors, cameras, MEMS, high-reliability semiconductors, and the FLIR thermal/surveillance/unmanned-systems franchise. The strategically central but lowest-margin large segment.
- **Instrumentation — $1.46B (24%), 27.5% op margin.** Marine instrumentation (subsea interconnects, sonar, AUVs), environmental monitoring, and electronic test & measurement (oscilloscopes, protocol analyzers). The margin engine.
- **Aerospace & Defense Electronics — $1.06B (17%), 24.8% op margin.** Defense electronics, harsh-environment interconnects, avionics data systems, satcom. Grew +36% in 2025, +14% in Q1 2026; the clearest beneficiary of the defense cycle.
- **Engineered Systems — $0.44B (7%), 10.7% op margin.** Defense/space/energy systems integration, electrochemical/hydrogen energy. Low-margin, exposed to NASA budget cuts.

The GICS "Information Technology" label is misleading. With roughly 25% of sales to the U.S. government (20% to DoD), Teledyne behaves more like a defense-and-instrumentation industrial than a software/hardware tech name, with the cyclicality and regulatory exposure that implies. Forty-eight percent of sales are international.

## Financial Analysis

The fundamentals are high-quality and stable:

- **Revenue:** $6.12B FY2025, +7.9% YoY (2024: $5.67B; 2023: $5.64B). 2024 was nearly flat, so the 2025 acceleration is meaningful, though ~$270M of it was acquisition-driven (mostly Qioptiq).
- **Profitability:** Operating income $1,149.8M (+16.2%), op margin 18.8% (up from 17.4%). Net income $894.8M (+9.2%), diluted EPS $18.88 (+9.7%), net margin 14.6%. A note of caution: the margin gain partly reflects the absence of a 2024 $52.5M trademark impairment rather than purely organic improvement.
- **Cash flow:** Operating cash flow $1,191.3M, FCF $1,074M (17.6% FCF margin), implying FCF conversion of ~120% of net income — high earnings quality. Capex is light (~$117M, ~$150M planned for 2026), giving FCF a ~3.7% yield on market cap.
- **Balance sheet:** Total debt ~$2.49B (all fixed-rate senior notes, no floating exposure), equity $10.5B, D/E 0.45, current ratio 1.64, net debt ~$2.1B. The $450M 1.60% notes due April 2026 were repaid post-Q1 from cash. Remaining notes mature 2028/2030/2031. Altman Z of 3.03 sits in the safe zone, though modestly so given the asset composition.
- **Asset composition:** Goodwill is $8.69B and intangibles $2.1B; roughly 71% of the $15.3B asset base is intangible, a direct consequence of the acquisition-led model and the source of the impairment tail discussed below.
- **Q1 2026:** Sales $1,560.1M (+7.6%), operating income +13.5%, net income +20.3%, EPS $4.85. Cost of sales improved to 56.8%. Cash built to $521M.
- **Tax tailwind:** The "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" delivered ~$30M cash-tax reduction in 2025 and an estimated $60–70M in 2026.

Capital allocation in 2025 was balanced: $821M on acquisitions, $400M on buybacks (~0.8M shares at ~$507.52), and $162M to retire $177M of notes at a $15M gain. A $2.0B buyback authorization was approved in July 2025 with $1.6B remaining, but no repurchases occurred in Q1 2026.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on the durability of the defense cycle and Teledyne's M&A cadence. The A&D segment growth is driven by rising U.S. and especially European defense budgets (Ukraine/Russia, NATO rearmament). Management explicitly states it is "currently benefiting from increased global defense spending." Multiple governments are committing multi-year procurement, and the backlog (71% recognizable within twelve months) gives near-term visibility.

The qualifier is organic versus acquired growth. Headline 7.9% revenue growth flatters the underlying picture; the 2025 acquisitions (Qioptiq, Micropac, Maretron, TransponderTech), with Q1 2026 adding DD-Scientific, account for the gap. Strip those out and organic growth is mid-single-digit. Consensus reflects this: FY2026 EPS of $24.16 (+1.6% revised over 90 days) and FY2027 of $26.06 (+1.4%) imply ~8% earnings growth, modest for the multiple. Estimate revisions are stable rather than accelerating; there is no +30% revision velocity here that would flag an earnings inflection.

The earnings-surprise record is favorable: four consecutive beats, including +8.5% in Q4 2025 and +4.8% in Q1 2026, supporting confidence in the current consensus. Mix dynamics deserve monitoring — Instrumentation, the margin engine, saw Q1 2026 segment margin compress (24.5% vs 27.0%) on product mix, while fast-growing A&D dilutes consolidated margins with lower-margin acquired revenue. The growth is real and reasonably durable, but it is steady-compounder growth, not a re-rating-worthy inflection.

With FY2027 consensus implying ~8% EPS growth, the PEG is well above 1.5; this is not a cheap stock on growth-adjusted terms, and there is no specific catalyst that suggests the market is mispricing it downward.

The consensus mean target of $737 (median $750, high $803, low $614) implies ~20% upside, and analyst targets on inflection names often lag reality. But TDY's estimates are stable, not surging, so the target gap reflects analyst optimism about the defense cycle as much as any identifiable mispricing. The lagging cumulative TSR is evidence that the market has already discounted the goodwill, regulatory, and organic-growth concerns rather than overlooking value.

The valuation is fair-to-full. We assign a 5.0 — neither stretched enough to warrant an AVOID nor cheap enough to anchor a BUY. A low multiple alone is not a buy signal, and here the multiple is not even low; the score reflects the absence of an asymmetric setup at the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

Teledyne competes in fragmented niches rather than head-to-head with a single peer, and its 10-K candidly notes competitors often have "greater name recognition and greater resources." Its moats are structural: technical specialization, design-in and qualification lock-in (especially in defense and high-reliability applications), and long-tenured engineering talent. No single commercial customer exceeds 10% of sales, and products ship to 100+ countries.

The competitive trajectory is broadly stable-to-improving in defense (A&D share gains via rising budgets and successful integration of Qioptiq/Micropac) but faces specific headwinds. EU "in-country manufacturing" requirements could disadvantage TDY's U.S./Canada-centric footprint on European defense bids, the very market driving its fastest growth. China has placed two Teledyne legal entities on its Unreliable Entity/Export Control lists and restricts gallium/germanium/rare-earth exports used in imaging and defense products. Environmental Instrumentation and the Engineered Systems energy business face headwinds from the administration's rollback of EPA and green-energy initiatives. Test & Measurement is exposed to AI/data-center capex volatility, which management newly flags as a risk.

The net read is a wide-moat portfolio with improving defense positioning offset by identifiable geographic and regulatory frictions: a competitive position that is solid but not unambiguously widening.

## Risk Assessment

Several idiosyncratic risks are not fully priced:

1. **FLIR impairment tail (most material).** FLIR carries $5.19B of goodwill; the FY2025 quantitative test cushion was only $619M. The FLIR indefinite-lived trademark ($635.8M) sat only ~3% above carrying value, and management added 50bps of discount-rate risk premium in December 2025 because FLIR has repeatedly missed sales projections. A modest forecast deterioration or rate increase could trigger a multi-billion-dollar non-cash impairment. It would not affect cash flow, but it would dent equity, headline EPS, and sentiment.

2. **January 2026 defense-contractor executive order.** A Presidential EO contemplates prohibiting "major defense contractors" from buybacks or dividends "at the expense of accelerated procurement," and may cap executive salaries. With ~25% government sales, TDY may be in scope; management flags potential "material adverse impact" on repurchases. The zero buybacks in Q1 2026 are the tell; this overhang is real and under-appreciated for a company whose capital-return story matters.

3. **Government exposure and shutdown risk.** A 43-day shutdown in fall 2025 delayed awards, licenses, shipments, and payments; 81% of government contracts are fixed-price (cost-overrun exposure); NASA budget cuts hurt Engineered Systems in 2025 and again expected in 2026; Defense Electronics has seen increased terminations for convenience.

4. **China/rare earths/export controls** and **tariff uncertainty** (Feb 2026 Supreme Court ruling created tariff-refund uncertainty) add second-order frictions.

On the positive side, the long-feared FLIR export-compliance liability resolved in February 2026 for just $1.0M (with a separate $1.5M FCA settlement), substantially clearing the legal overhang while the accounting/operational risk remains.

### Options Market Signal

The options surface is sending a sharply elevated near-term stress signal that warrants caution but appears largely technical. Near-term ATM IV spiked to 151.5% (one-week tenor) against 1-year historical volatility of just 24.9% — a ~127pp premium — and the term structure is in steep backwardation (151.5% near-term vs 28.7% at six months). The put/call volume ratio of 4.30 and a 3.9% put skew indicate elevated near-term put demand. The one-week 151.5% reading reflects thin near-expiry contracts and an imminent event; the more representative one-month tenor sits at 31.3% IV. The 18.33 one-month put/call volume ratio still points to hedging activity. The market is pricing near-term event risk consistent with the buyback/impairment overhangs. For a holder, this argues for waiting for the near-term event to clear rather than chasing, and the cheap longer-dated IV (28.7% at six months) is reasonably priced for protection if desired.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($750–803)

Defense spending proves durable across multiple multi-year government commitments, A&D continues compounding double-digit, the M&A engine keeps adding accretive bolt-ons, FLIR forecasts stabilize and the impairment cushion rebuilds, the executive order is clarified in a way that permits buybacks, and the $1.6B remaining authorization is deployed at attractive prices. Estimate revisions turn positive, analysts move targets up, and the stock re-rates toward the consensus median of $750 (~22% upside) with a path to the $803 high target if defense multiples expand.

### Bear Case ($480–520)

FLIR misses its forecasts again, triggering a multi-billion-dollar non-cash impairment that dents EPS and sentiment; the executive order is implemented in scope, freezing the buyback and removing a capital-return lever the multiple partly relies on; a government shutdown or NASA cut pressures Engineered Systems and government-heavy revenue; organic growth disappoints once acquisition contribution is stripped out. The stock de-rates toward the 52-week low of $483 (~22% downside) as the market re-prices the goodwill-heavy balance sheet and regulatory exposure.

The risk/reward is roughly symmetric (~20% upside to mean target, ~22% downside to the 52-week low). That symmetry, combined with a market-multiple valuation, is what keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next scheduled reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July), with the near-term options-implied event as an interim checkpoint.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$500s / 52-week-low region ($483–520) on unchanged fundamentals, restoring a margin of safety against ~$24 FY2026 EPS (~21–22x forward).
- Resolution of the January 2026 executive order in a way that explicitly permits buybacks, followed by deployment of the remaining authorization at sensible prices.
- FY2026 organic revenue growth re-accelerating above ~6% with the FLIR impairment cushion widening materially above the ~8% level, plus a sustained positive shift in estimate revisions.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A FLIR goodwill or trademark impairment announcement, or a December-test cushion narrowing below ~5%.
- The executive order implemented in scope, freezing capital return, combined with stalling A&D growth (below ~8% YoY).
- Two consecutive quarters of organic growth below 3% or a guidance cut.

## Conclusion

Teledyne is a well-run, cash-generative defense-and-instrumentation compounder with a genuine structural tailwind in global defense spending and a high-quality balance sheet (modest fixed-rate debt, 120% FCF conversion). The trajectory of the underlying business is solid. The reasons not to buy at $615 are price and tail risk: the stock trades at a market-multiple ~25x forward against mid-single-digit organic growth, the consensus target gap reflects defense-cycle optimism more than identifiable mispricing, and two specific overhangs (a thin FLIR impairment cushion and an executive order that has already frozen the buyback) sit unresolved. The recent score trajectory (7.0 → 6.5 → 6.7) and the management-quality and risk-factor declines reflect these developments, not a price-driven misread; the stock is roughly flat over the period while these overhangs emerged.

We rate TDY **HOLD** with a constructive bias. This is a name to own at a better price or after the overhangs clear, not to chase at a full multiple with near-term event risk in the options market. Maintain on watch; act on a pullback to the low-$500s or on a clean resolution of the buyback and impairment questions.
