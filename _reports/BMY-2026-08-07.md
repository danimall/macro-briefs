## Executive Summary

Bristol Myers Squibb delivered its fourth consecutive earnings beat on July 30, 2026 — Q2 non-GAAP diluted EPS of $2.04 against a $1.60 consensus, a 27.2% surprise — and consensus 2026 EPS has been revised up 10.1% in thirty days to $6.95. The stock has responded, closing at $64.15, up 43% over twelve months and 5.8% below the $68.10 fifty-two-week high. On the headline arithmetic the shares look inexpensive: 9.2x 2026 consensus, 9.8x 2027, a 9.8% free cash flow yield on FY2025's $12,845M, and a 3.93% dividend consuming 39% of that cash flow.

The composition of the improvement matters more than its magnitude. Q2 gross product sales fell 18% to $18,179M while net product sales grew 6% to $12,588M, because BMS cut the U.S. list price of Eliquis and total gross-to-net adjustments collapsed from 46% to 30% of gross sales. That converted a high-list/high-rebate structure into a low-list/low-rebate one, blunting the IRA maximum fair price that took effect January 1, 2026. It is a balance-sheet reset, not a demand engine: the rebates-and-discounts liability fell from $8,844M to $5,654M, a $3,135M working-capital outflow, and first-half operating cash flow declined to $4,497M from $5,871M. Reported 2026 Eliquis growth of 20–25% (secondary-sourced, unconfirmed in filings) therefore substantially overstates underlying volume, and the effect does not repeat in 2027.

Consensus already reflects this: 2027 EPS of $6.55 is 5.8% *below* 2026. The medium-term picture also deteriorated in the June quarter's disclosures. Merck's 2.5% Keytruda royalty and Roche's Tecentriq royalty both terminate December 31, 2026, removing roughly $670M of annualized, essentially cost-free pretax income (~$0.26/share after tax) on top of the AstraZeneca diabetes stream that ended in December 2025. BMS signed agreements in April 2026 permitting a Dr. Reddy's Orencia subcutaneous biosimilar as early as February 2028 on a $3,705M product that HHS also selected for IRA price-setting from 2028. Seven generic filers served Paragraph IV notices on Camzyos between May and June 2026. The company is paying $1.5B upfront plus $2.0B in anniversary payments for *half* the economics of BioNTech's pumitamig, which is evidence of how little internal optionality it held in the PD-L1×VEGF class. Fair value is approximately where the stock trades. We rate BMY HOLD for the covered dividend, with $68+ the trim zone rather than an extension of the re-rating.

## Company Overview

BMS discovers, develops and sells prescription medicines in oncology, hematology, immunology, cardiovascular and neuroscience. It reports as a **single segment** — the CEO allocates resources on consolidated net income "rather than on a product or franchise basis" — so no franchise-level margin data exists and any bottom-up profitability estimate is inference.

Management's own two-bucket framing is the operative lens:

| Portfolio | FY2025 | FY2024 | 1H 2026 | 1H 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Growth | $26,409M (+17%) | $22,563M | $13,787M (+13%) | $12,159M |
| Legacy | $21,785M (−15%) | $25,737M | $10,699M (−5%) | $11,311M |
| **Total** | **$48,194M (0%)** | **$48,300M** | **$24,462M (+4%)** | **$23,470M** |

The Growth Portfolio has already overtaken Legacy in size. Its largest components are Opdivo ($10,049M FY25), Orencia ($3,705M), Yervoy ($2,900M), Reblozyl ($2,327M, +31%), Breyanzi ($1,358M, +82%), Opdualag ($1,185M) and Camzyos ($1,068M, +77%), with Opdivo Qvantig scaling from $238M in FY25 to $424M in 1H26 alone. Legacy is dominated by Eliquis ($14,443M FY25), now the single largest product and the single largest exclusivity exposure, alongside Revlimid ($2,951M, −49%) and Pomalyst ($2,733M, −23%).

Two structural features constrain earnings quality. First, roughly **36% of 1H26 net product sales** ($8,633M of $23,756M) run through profit-sharing or royalty-bearing alliances; payments to alliance partners inside cost of products sold reached $4,272M in 1H26 versus $3,615M, and COGS rose 11–12% on flat-to-up volumes "primarily due to higher alliance profit sharing." Reblozyl alone carries a 20–24% royalty payable to Merck ($289M in 1H26). Second, distribution is concentrated: the three largest U.S. wholesalers took ~90% of gross U.S. sales in 1H26, up from ~87% in FY2025.

A frequently confused relationship is worth stating precisely: BMS *licenses Reblozyl from* Merck and pays Merck 20–24% of net sales; separately Merck *licenses Winrevair from* BMS and pays BMS 22% of net sales ($255M to BMS in 1H26, up from $124M). BMS does not develop or commercialize Winrevair; Merck does, with BMS receiving royalty economics as the licensor.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2023 $45,006M → FY2024 $48,300M → FY2025 $48,194M (−0.2%). 1H26 $24,462M (+4%); Q2 $12,973M (+6%). FY2025 U.S. average net selling prices fell 4%; in 1H26 they rose 4%, driven substantially by the Eliquis list-price mechanics rather than pricing power.

**GAAP versus operations.** GAAP net income swung from $8,025M (FY23) to −$8,948M (FY24) to $7,054M (FY25). The FY2024 loss is explained by acquisition accounting: Acquired IPRD of $13,373M (including a $12,122M non-deductible charge for Karuna) and $8,872M of intangible amortization. FY2025 Acquired IPRD fell to $3,721M and amortization to $3,317M, the latter because the Revlimid acquired product right fully amortized in Q4 2024 and Pomalyst in Q4 2025 — a mechanical GAAP tailwind now largely harvested. GAAP diluted EPS $3.46 in FY2025; non-GAAP $6.15 after $6,124M of pretax specified items. Q2 2026: GAAP $1.62, non-GAAP $2.04. Any consensus comparison must be non-GAAP-to-non-GAAP.

Note an internal inconsistency in the supplied market data: the reported trailing and forward P/E are both 14.12, which corresponds to trailing-twelve-month EPS of roughly $4.54, not to FY2025 GAAP EPS of $3.46 (18.5x) or to 2026 consensus non-GAAP $6.95 (9.2x). We use the consensus estimates directly and label the basis throughout.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 OCF $14,156M, capex $1,311M, FCF $12,845M (26.7% margin). 1H26 OCF fell to $4,497M from $5,871M. The mechanism is disclosed: a −$3,135M working-capital swing in rebates and discounts (versus +$254M in 1H25) as the accrued liability was paid down from $8,844M to $5,654M following the Eliquis list-price reduction. This is a one-time normalization, and 1H26 should not be annualized. A labeled sensitivity adding back the ~$3.1B abnormal outflow gives roughly $7.6B of underlying 1H generation, annualizing near the FY2025 level — but that sensitivity ignores seasonality, cash-tax timing, the $600M Hengrui upfront payable in Q3 2026 and BioNTech anniversary payments beginning the same quarter. Treat FY2026 free cash flow as likely between $9B and $12B rather than a point estimate.

**Balance sheet.** Net debt has fallen $6.8B in eighteen months, from $38.5B (Dec 2024) to $31.7B (Jun 2026). FY2025 actions: €5.0B of euro notes issued through BMS Ireland Capital Funding DAC and fully guaranteed by the parent; $8.7B of principal repurchased for $9.1B cash with a $356M redemption loss; $1.9B repaid at maturity. Current ratio improved to 1.53x from 1.26x. Ratings A2/A stable; the $5.0B revolver is undrawn, extended to January 2031, with no financial covenants. Gross debt/equity is 1.93x at June 30, 2026.

Screening outputs on this name require correction. An Altman Z of 1.03 and an operating margin of −1.4% are artifacts: BMS presents no operating-income line, and −1.4% of $48,194M is −$675M, which matches the FY2025 "Other (income)/expense, net" of $674M almost exactly. Actual FY2025 pretax income was $9,328M; adding $1,891M of interest expense gives EBIT of roughly $11,219M, or 12.5% of assets. Re-deriving Z with correct EBIT and market equity produces approximately 2.39 — grey zone, not distress. Likewise the "debt-to-equity 3.87" is total liabilities over equity. The genuine constraint is different and does bind: book equity of $22,319M sits against $21,740M of goodwill and $17,387M of other intangibles, so further write-downs compress the equity base directly, and the 10-K itself concedes uncertainty about when business-development capacity can expand. On the evidence, this is a heavily levered investment-grade issuer with strong cash conversion whose leverage limits optionality.

**Off-balance-sheet contingencies.** $18.3B of potential R&D milestones and $21.5B of sales-based milestones as of FY2025, plus BioNTech's $2.0B of anniversary payments (Q3 2026–2028) and up to $7.6B contingent, and Hengrui's $600M upfront, $175M in 2027, a conditional $175M in 2028 and up to $14.3B contingent. These are conditional and should be modeled probabilistically, not summed.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term earnings line is improving and the multi-year earnings line is not. Both statements are supported by the same data.

**Positive.** Four consecutive beats (+6.9%, +11.9%, +10.4%, +27.2%). Consensus 2026 EPS revised from $6.30 to $6.95 (+10.3% over ninety days) and 2027 from $6.17 to $6.55 (+6.2%). Growth Portfolio revenue +15% in Q2 to $7,560M. Q2 effective tax rate of 18.8% versus 25.9%, and 16.5% ex-specified items. The strategic productivity initiative targets ~$2.0B of annual savings by end-2027, with FY2025 SG&A −14% and R&D −11% as evidence of delivery; 1H26 restructuring was a net *credit* of $(31)M as site disposals generated gains. Genuine label expansions: Sotyktu approved for active PsA in the U.S. (March 2026), EU and Japan (May 2026); Opdivo + AVD in first-line advanced classical Hodgkin lymphoma in the U.S. (March) and EU (June), on SWOG 1826 showing a 58% reduction in progression-or-death risk versus brentuximab vedotin + AVD; Breyanzi in Japan for MCL and MZL. Near-term regulatory calendar: iberdomide RRMM PDUFA August 17, 2026; Camzyos adolescent oHCM PDUFA September 30, 2026; Reblozyl in myelofibrosis-associated anemia March 11, 2027; mezigdomide combination May 13, 2027.

**Negative, and decisive for the multiple.** Consensus 2027 EPS is 5.8% below 2026. Three forces drive that. (1) The Eliquis gross-to-net reset flatters 2026 and does not repeat. (2) The royalty cliff: Keytruda (2.5%, shared BMS/Ono 75/25) and Tecentriq royalties both terminate December 31, 2026, together $334M in 1H26 — roughly $670M annualized of income carrying no COGS, SG&A or R&D, so the EPS hit is close to the full pretax amount net of tax, about $0.26/share. FY2025 royalties contributed approximately $2.7B of pretax income against $9,328M total, roughly 29%. The growing Winrevair royalty partially offsets. (3) The exclusivity cascade is no longer confined to Legacy. Pomalyst generics entered in March 2026 (Q2 revenue $204M versus $708M, −71%) with IRA maximum fair price landing on the same genericized asset from January 1, 2027. Eliquis composition-of-matter patents and SPCs expire in the EU in November 2026, with generics already marketed in the UK and Poland. Camzyos, growing 74% in 1H26, drew seven Paragraph IV filers in two months. Orencia faces a company-authorized subcutaneous biosimilar from as early as February 2028 plus IRA-negotiated pricing in the same year. The widely cited U.S. Eliquis 2028 cliff appears only in secondary reporting and is not confirmed in the filings we reviewed; the pending Azurity 505(b)(2) challenges a formulation patent, not composition of matter.

Pipeline execution has been uneven: ODYSSEY-HCM (Camzyos in non-obstructive HCM), ARISE (Cobenfy adjunctive schizophrenia), RELATIVITY-098 (adjuvant melanoma) and INDEPENDENCE (Reblozyl in myelofibrosis) all missed primary endpoints, and Librexia ACS was stopped in November 2025 on interim futility. The milvexian atrial fibrillation readout has reportedly slipped to Q1 2027, though that timing comes from secondary reporting rather than filings.

Estimate revisions of +10% over ninety days are positive but well short of the +30% threshold that flags a genuine earnings inflection, and the shape — 2026 peak, 2027 decline — is the opposite of an inflection. This is not a stock where rising estimates argue for multiple expansion.

Pfizer at 20.0x trailing is the only structurally comparable large-cap, and its multiple reflects depressed trailing earnings rather than a growth premium. The controlling question is what a market-clearing multiple should be on $6.55 of 2027 earnings with visibility that worsens in 2028. ## Competitive Landscape

BMS is defending its immuno-oncology base rather than expanding it. Opdivo revenue declined 3% in Q2 and 4% in 1H26, attributed to lower U.S. demand as Opdivo Qvantig takes share. Combining both formulations, the nivolumab franchise grew from $4,862M to $5,055M in 1H26, about +4%. The subcutaneous conversion is lifecycle management ahead of nivolumab biosimilars — a formulation with its own patent life that retains patients when intravenous biosimilars arrive — not incremental demand.

The PD-1 patent estate that let BMS collect economics from competing checkpoint inhibitors (Merck's Keytruda at 2.5%, Roche's Tecentriq at a single-digit rate) expires at the end of 2026. After that, BMS participates in the checkpoint class only through its own products.

The next contested frontier is PD-L1×VEGF bispecifics, and BMS bought in rather than built. The BioNTech collaboration on pumitamig splits global profits and losses 50/50, with BioNTech as originator, for $1.5B upfront plus $2.0B of anniversary payments and up to $7.6B contingent. ROSETTA Lung-02 interim Phase II data in May 2026 showed encouraging activity across PD-L1 levels. The price paid for half the economics is informative about how strategically important management judges the class to be and about how thin its internal position was.

Two structural headwinds compound. FDA draft guidance issued in October 2025 aims to streamline biosimilar development, including by minimizing comparative clinical efficacy studies — directly compressing expected exclusivity value on Opdivo, Yervoy, Orencia and Reblozyl. And U.S. payer mix is deteriorating: FY2025 U.S. gross-to-net rose to 53% from 49% on Part D redesign and higher government channel mix, with BMS paying a 10% manufacturer discount below the $2,000 out-of-pocket cap and 20% above it, while 340B contract-pharmacy utilization expands.

The December 2025 U.S. Government Agreement is genuinely two-sided. BMS provides Eliquis free to Medicaid from January 1, 2026, donates API to a strategic reserve, offers direct-to-patient cash access to five products at ~80% off list, adopts more balanced developed-market launch pricing and expands domestic production. In exchange it receives tariff relief until January 2029 covering patented pharmaceuticals under the April 2, 2026 proclamation, and exemption from future U.S. pricing mandates while the agreement holds. BMS itself warns that comparable agreements struck by competitors "could result in reduced prices and reimbursement" for its products — the relative advantage is likely to be competed away.

Trajectory, which matters more than level: share is flat-to-eroding in the largest franchise, the royalty moat is expiring on a fixed date, exclusivity is being contested inside the Growth Portfolio, and 36% of net product sales carry partner economics that cap incremental margin.

## Risk Assessment

**Celgene CVR litigation — $6.4B notional, no accrual.** The successor trustee alleges BMS failed to use "diligent efforts" to obtain FDA approval of liso-cel (Breyanzi) before a contractual milestone, avoiding a $6.4B payment. In December 2025 the court denied BMS's motion in substantial part, found the plaintiff to be the successor trustee, and dismissed two of five claims; in May 2026 it stayed the original trustee's crossclaims. No trial date. Against $22,319M of book equity, an outcome near notional would be highly material. Countervailing: the ruling addressed standing and pleading sufficiency, not merits; "diligent efforts" claims are difficult to prove; damages if any could be far below notional; and a parallel individual CVR-holder suit was dismissed with prejudice in May 2026. This is the largest genuinely unpriced tail in the name.

**Impairment against thin equity.** Intangible impairments were $136M (FY23), $2.9B (FY24) and $949M (FY25). 1H26 added $830M of IPRD write-downs ($420M in Q2), described as a partial write-down of an oncology asset on recent clinical results and of a radiopharmaceutical asset on indication realignment; IPRD fell from $7,600M to $6,770M. With $21,740M goodwill and $17,387M other intangibles against $22,319M equity, the cushion is thin.

**Policy and legal, with no remaining escape route.** The constitutional challenge to the IRA is exhausted — district court loss April 2024, Third Circuit affirmance September 2025, certiorari denied May 2026. Maximum fair price applies to Eliquis from January 2026, Pomalyst from January 2027, and Orencia negotiation for 2028. BMS lost at district court on the HRSA 340B cash-rebate challenge in May 2025 with the D.C. Circuit argued in November 2025 and undecided. Arkansas sued in July 2026 over contract-pharmacy conditioning. A Texas AG/qui tam action on Plavix promotion was remanded to state court. The 2008–2012 IRS transfer-pricing appeal remains open, with BMS noting resolution "could have a material impact."

**Trial-conduct flag.** In December 2025 BMS disclosed irregularities from clinical trial execution at a small number of ADEPT-2 sites in Alzheimer's disease psychosis, excluded that data before database lock in consultation with the FDA, and is enrolling additional patients on DMC recommendation while remaining blinded. This is a cost and timeline setback on the flagship expansion for the Karuna acquisition (which produced a $12.1B non-deductible IPRD charge in FY2024), following the April 2025 ARISE miss. The handling — pre-lock exclusion with FDA consultation and maintained blinding — is also the textbook correct response to site-level misconduct, and no efficacy inference in either direction is supportable.

**Concentration and governance.** Three wholesalers took ~90% of gross U.S. sales in 1H26 (up from ~87%), with 71% of trade receivables in three customers. This is channel rather than end-demand concentration, but it is worsening on the disclosed measure. BMS remains under a 2004 SEC consent order limiting channel inventory to roughly one month, with no exceedances reported. The Chair and CEO roles are combined; we did not verify whether an independent lead director offsets this.

**Verification gaps that a reader should treat as open.** 2026 guidance specifics, including the 20–25% Eliquis growth framing, come from secondary reporting on July 30, 2026 and appear in no filing we reviewed. The U.S. Eliquis expiry date is unconfirmed. Milvexian readout timing is secondary-sourced. No proxy or Form 4 data was available, so no insider-transaction conclusion can be drawn in either direction; the 10-Q confirms only that no director or officer adopted or terminated a 10b5-1 arrangement in Q2 2026.

### Options Market Signal

The headline reading — near-term ATM IV of 58.9% against 27% one-year realized, in backwardation, "pricing imminent stress" — is an artifact of a one-day expiry (August 7) and should be discounted, as should the 40.6%→80.0% ATM IV change in the delta snapshot. The informative tenors are the one-month at 24.9% (below realized), three-month at 28.0% and six-month at 32.3% with a 14.3% implied move. That structure prices a modest premium to realized volatility over the medium term without signaling acute event risk. Put/call open-interest ratio of 0.56 is call-tilted, volume ratio 0.69, and short interest is just 2.2% of float at 3.3 days to cover — there is no crowded bearish position here. The three-month put/call volume ratio of 1.81 is the one hedging tell, consistent with protection around the September 30 Camzyos PDUFA and Q3 results. Skew values are reported without a directional inference: the summary characterizes near-term positioning as elevated put demand while the tabulated one-, three- and six-month skews are negative, and the sign convention is not defined in the source. Net: options positioning neither confirms nor contradicts the fundamental view, and does not support a stress narrative.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case → $80 (+24.7%)

Growth Portfolio revenue, already larger than Legacy at $26.4B FY2025 and compounding 13–17%, absorbs the exclusivity losses faster than modeled. The $2.0B productivity program lands by end-2027 with restructuring turning cash-positive as sites are sold. Near-term approvals convert: iberdomide (August 17, 2026 PDUFA), Camzyos adolescent oHCM (September 30, 2026), then mezigdomide and Reblozyl in myelofibrosis in 2027. Milvexian AF reads out positively in Q1 2027, giving BMS a genuine successor asset in anticoagulation ahead of the Eliquis cliff — the single highest-value binary in the story. Camzyos litigation settles on terms that push generic entry well past 2030. Net debt continues down from $31.7B, the $5.0B buyback authorization is finally activated, and the multiple re-rates to 11.4x on $7.00 of 2027 earnings as the market concludes the trough is shallower than feared.

### Bear Case → $49 (−23.6%)

The 2026 earnings peak is confirmed as artificial. The Eliquis gross-to-net reset laps, removing the flattering comparison; Keytruda and Tecentriq royalties (~$0.26/share) disappear on January 1, 2027; Pomalyst faces IRA pricing on an already-genericized base; and Orencia carries both government-set pricing and a company-authorized biosimilar in 2028. Camzyos generics arrive earlier than expected out of seven-filer litigation. 2027 EPS lands near $6.00 rather than $6.55, and the multiple compresses to 8x as investors price the 2028 Eliquis U.S. cliff on a product representing roughly 30% of revenue. An adverse Celgene CVR ruling or a further intangible write-down against $22.3B of equity accelerates the de-rating. Free cash flow, already down $1.4B in 1H26, disappoints again as the $600M Hengrui and BioNTech anniversary payments land in Q3. The analyst low of $40 illustrates how far the tail extends.

### Which resolves

The base case sits closest to consensus because both tails require information not yet available — the milvexian readout for the bull, the pace of 2027 erosion for the bear. What the current price reflects is a business generating $12.8B of FY2025 free cash flow, deleveraging on schedule, paying a covered 3.93% dividend, and facing a documented earnings decline in 2027 with a larger step down likely in 2028–2029. At 9.8x 2027 consensus, that is priced approximately correctly. The score trajectory (6.5 → 5.5 across four prior HOLDs) has been declining for the right reasons, and this quarter's data mostly confirms it: a strong beat of low quality, plus two new competitive negatives in Orencia and Camzyos, against a 10% price advance.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income_hold).** Existing holders own it for a 3.93% dividend at 39% of free cash flow, backed by A2/A ratings and an undrawn covenant-free revolver. Prospective buyers have no entry signal at $64.15, 5.8% below the fifty-two-week high, with the probability-weighted twelve-month total return near 5%.

**Targets (basis $64.15):** Base $67 (+4.4%; +8.3% with dividend) at 10.2x 2027 consensus $6.55. Bull $80 (+24.7%) at 11.4x $7.00. Bear $49 (−23.6%) at 8.2x $6.00. Analyst range $40–$80, mean $66.21, median $68.00.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE):**
1. A price below $55 (roughly 8.4x 2027 consensus) with 2027 estimates holding at or above $6.50 — a valuation-driven upgrade, since the operating story does not improve on its own timetable.
2. Positive topline from Librexia AF (milvexian) when it reads out, currently reported as Q1 2027. This is the only disclosed catalyst capable of changing the 2028–2030 earnings shape rather than the 2026–2027 level.
3. Activation of the $5.0B buyback authorization at or below ~10x forward earnings, ending the ~0.4% annual share drift, combined with two consecutive quarters of operating cash flow at or above the normalized ~$3.5B quarterly pace once the rebate unwind is complete.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. 2027 consensus EPS revised below $6.20 (currently $6.55), which would confirm that the royalty cliff and Legacy erosion exceed Growth Portfolio and cost-program offsets.
2. A materially adverse development in the Celgene CVR case — a merits ruling against BMS, a disclosed accrual, or a settlement above roughly $1.5B — given zero accrual today against $22.3B of book equity.

## Conclusion

Bristol Myers Squibb is executing well on the levers available to it. Net debt is down $6.8B in eighteen months, the cost program is visible in the expense lines, the Growth Portfolio has overtaken Legacy, and four consecutive earnings beats culminated in a 27% Q2 surprise that pulled 2026 consensus up 10%. Management also engineered an unusually clever gross-to-net reset on Eliquis, collapsing total rebate adjustments from 46% to 30% of gross sales and turning an 18% decline in gross product sales into 6% net growth while simultaneously satisfying IRA and Medicaid commitments.

None of that changes the shape of the earnings stream. Consensus 2027 EPS of $6.55 sits below 2026's $6.95 because the Eliquis reset does not repeat, roughly $670M of cost-free royalty income terminates on December 31, 2026, and Pomalyst, Orencia and Camzyos each carry dated or contested exclusivity inside the window the Growth Portfolio was supposed to bridge. The June-quarter disclosures added two specific negatives — a company-authorized Orencia subcutaneous biosimilar from as early as February 2028 and seven Paragraph IV filers on Camzyos — while the $6.4B Celgene CVR claim advanced past its likeliest early off-ramp with no accrual recorded.

At $64.15 the shares yield 3.93% on a payout consuming 39% of free cash flow, trade at 9.8x 2027 consensus and offer a 9.8% free cash flow yield. That is a fair price for a business whose earnings peak this year, not a mispricing. Hold for the income and the deleveraging; trim into strength above $68; revisit as a buy near $55 or on a positive milvexian atrial fibrillation readout, whichever comes first.
