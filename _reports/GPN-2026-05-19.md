## Executive Summary

Global Payments trades at $68.18, down 48% from its 2024 highs and 25% below its 52-week high of $90.64, following the January 2026 completion of its $22.7B Worldpay acquisition and simultaneous divestiture of Issuer Solutions. At 4.9x forward adjusted EPS ($13.87 consensus FY2026) and approximately 5.5-6.0x EV/EBITDA, the stock is pricing in substantial execution failure on the largest transaction in its history.

The critical analytical question is whether this discount reflects permanent impairment or transient dislocation. Evidence supports the latter: Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $2.96 beat consensus by 3.7%, management reaffirmed full-year guidance of $13.80-$14.00, Genius platform bookings nearly doubled, and 90-day estimate revisions are modestly positive (+2.0% for FY2026). However, several factors prevent a more constructive stance: ~$20B in debt against an $18.7B market cap, an Altman Z-Score of 1.43, the dual complexity of integrating Worldpay while separating Issuer Solutions infrastructure, and management's demonstrated poor track record on capital allocation (buybacks executed at $84-$102/share now deeply underwater).

The entire legacy payments sector is depressed — FIS trades at 8.5x P/E (-47% from its 52-week high), FISV at 9.6x P/E (-68% from its high). GPN's discount is sector-wide, not solely company-specific. Re-rating, if it occurs, will likely be a sector event requiring either macro relief or demonstrable synergy delivery across the group.

## Company Overview

Global Payments is a pure-play merchant acquiring and payment processing company following its January 2026 restructuring. The company acquired 100% of Worldpay from FIS/GTCR for approximately $22.7B ($6.2B cash + 43.3M GPN shares) while simultaneously selling its Issuer Solutions segment to FIS for ~$7.7B cash.

Post-transaction, GPN operates through a single Merchant Solutions segment with three pillars: (1) Point-of-Sale & Software Solutions (Genius POS platform, vertical software for restaurants, retail, education, real estate), (2) Integrated & Embedded Solutions (payments embedded in partner business management software), and (3) Core Payments Solutions (direct merchant acquiring via sales forces, ISOs, and financial institution referral channels).

The company operates in 37 countries with approximately 26,000 employees. Revenue is predominantly transaction-based (percentage of transaction value or per-transaction fees), supplemented by software subscriptions and value-added services.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** Pro-forma Q1 2026 adjusted net revenue was $2.86B (+29.5% YoY), driven by the Worldpay consolidation. Stripping out the acquisition, organic revenue grew approximately 5% on a constant-currency basis, consistent with management's full-year guide. The legacy Merchant Solutions segment generated $7.71B in FY2025 (flat YoY reported, ~7% organic after adjusting for AdvancedMD and Payroll Solutions divestitures).

**Profitability:** Merchant Solutions operating margin expanded 210bps to 35.5% in FY2025. However, consolidated operating margin declined to 22.8% from 25.5% due to $737.5M in corporate acquisition/transformation costs (up from $308.5M in FY2024). These costs are explicitly temporary, with management targeting $650M+ in annual run-rate operating income benefit by H1 2027. Free cash flow was $2.04B in FY2025, representing a 26.5% FCF margin on revenue.

**Balance Sheet:** Total long-term debt stands at approximately $19.5B post-transaction. Key components include $16.4B in senior unsecured notes, $6.2B issued in November 2025 at 4.50-5.55% rates, a $2.0B convertible at 1.5% due 2031, and a $1.5B Silver Lake convertible at 1.0% due 2029. The revolving credit facility has $4.1B available. The required leverage covenant is 3.75x. Both convertible notes have conversion prices ($139 and $157) that are approximately 100-130% above the current stock price, meaning no near-term dilution but also no conversion value for holders.

**Debt serviceability:** Interest expense was $649.6M in FY2025 and will increase with the $6.2B November 2025 issuance. Against approximately $2.04B in FCF, interest coverage is adequate but leaves limited margin for error, particularly in a rising-rate environment.

**Capital Returns:** Management has been aggressively returning capital — $1.19B in buybacks in FY2025 at $84-$102/share (now deeply underwater), a $550M ASR launched in February 2026, and a $2.5B board authorization. Dividends run approximately $238M annually ($0.25/quarter). The $7.5B capital return target through 2027 appears aggressive given the debt load.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three vectors:

1. **Organic merchant volume growth (~5% constant-currency):** This is the baseline — secular cash-to-digital migration continues, but GPN's enterprise-heavy Worldpay portfolio grows slower than fintech challengers serving SMB. The 5% guide is credible given Q1 delivery.

2. **Genius platform cross-sell into Worldpay's merchant base:** Bookings nearly doubled YoY; Genius Mobile attracted 500 locations in under 60 days. If GPN can migrate Worldpay's undifferentiated enterprise merchants onto higher-margin technology solutions, revenue per merchant should expand. This is a 2-3 year execution story.

3. **$650M+ cost synergies by H1 2027:** Platform consolidation, headcount rationalization, and technology modernization. The $1B technology investment program is the vehicle. Partial delivery would be meaningfully accretive at current market cap.

Estimate revisions support modest optimism: FY2026 EPS has risen from $13.60 to $13.87 over 90 days (+2.0%), and FY2027 consensus sits at $16.26, implying 17% EPS growth. However, FY2027 estimates have declined 2.7% over 90 days, suggesting analysts are trimming medium-term expectations even as near-term delivery is confirmed.

The growth profile is respectable (5% organic + synergies = potentially 12-15% EPS growth through FY2027) but not exceptional. The company is not in an earnings acceleration scenario that would justify aggressive re-rating from current levels alone.

## Valuation Assessment

This is the central tension in the GPN story. The stock appears cheap on almost every metric:

|--------|-----|-------------|------------|
| Forward P/E (adj) | 4.9x | 19.4x | Extreme discount |
| GAAP P/E | 27.1x | 9.6x (FISV) | Premium |
| EV/EBITDA (est) | ~5.5-6.0x | ~10-12x (sector) | Deep discount |
| Dividend yield | 1.5% | ~0% (ex-FIS) | Modest |

The 4.9x forward adjusted P/E is genuinely cheap, but the GAAP/non-GAAP gap is critical to understand. GPN's GAAP EPS of $4.43 versus adjusted EPS consensus of $13.87 implies approximately $9.44/share in adjustments. These include acquisition-related amortization (~$4-5/share from $17B+ in goodwill and $4.2B in acquired intangibles), transformation and integration costs (~$2-3/share from the $737.5M corporate cost run-rate), and share-based compensation (~$1-2/share).

The amortization is non-cash and permanent — it will persist for decades as goodwill impairment tests and intangible amortization run through the P&L. The transformation costs are temporary and should largely normalize by H1 2027. SBC is a real cost that creates future dilution. On balance, "true" normalized earnings likely sit between GAAP and adjusted — perhaps $8-10/share by FY2027, placing the stock at 7-9x normalized P/E.

**Analyst consensus:** The median analyst target is $82 (20% upside), with 12 Buy / 19 Hold / 2 Sell ratings. The majority of analysts are not recommending aggressive buying, and the 19 Holds versus 12 Buys suggests significant uncertainty about timing and catalyst. Our view aligns with the consensus HOLD.

**Bear case pricing:** At 4.9x adjusted P/E, the market is clearly skeptical about synergy delivery, competitive positioning versus fintech disruptors, and the ability to service ~$20B in debt in a rising-rate environment. However, the 48% drawdown has occurred alongside no earnings miss, maintained guidance, and similar compression across the entire peer group. The incremental bear case would require an actual earnings miss, guidance cut, or credit event — none of which has materialized.

## Competitive Landscape

Post-Worldpay, GPN is one of the world's largest merchant acquirers alongside Fiserv/Clover. The strategic thesis — combining Worldpay's scale with GPN's technology differentiation (Genius POS) — is logical but unproven at scale.

**Competitive threats are real and growing:**
- Stripe and Adyen continue gaining share in e-commerce and enterprise with superior developer tooling
- Block (Square) and Toast dominate SMB restaurants
- Shopify Payments captures merchant acquiring from its commerce platform
- These competitors are growing revenue 15-30% YoY versus GPN's ~5%

The filing explicitly acknowledges nontraditional competitors "may be less risk averse" and may "minimize or remove the role of traditional card networks, acquirers, issuers and processors." This is an existential long-term concern for legacy acquirers.

GPN's competitive response — the Genius platform and vertical software approach — is sound conceptually. If the company can bundle POS technology, software, and payments into sticky relationships, it can defend margins even as raw payment processing commoditizes. The Genius bookings doubling is encouraging but remains small relative to the total base. The critical question is pace of adoption across Worldpay's enterprise portfolio.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration complexity (High):** Simultaneously integrating Worldpay while separating Issuer Solutions infrastructure from FIS is the defining risk of the next 12-18 months. Management explicitly acknowledges this dual challenge. Historical base rates for mega-merger integration in payments are mixed — FIS's own Worldpay integration was widely viewed as a failure.

**Leverage (High):** The 3.75x leverage covenant provides some cushion but limited margin for EBITDA disappointment.

**Competitive erosion (Medium-High):** Fintech challengers growing 3-5x faster organically. Enterprise merchants with non-exclusive contracts can redirect volume without penalty. The Worldpay portfolio particularly exposes GPN to price competition on commodity acquiring services.

**Worldpay chargeback/credit risk (Medium):** The filing notes Worldpay serves "high-risk merchants who promise future delivery of goods and services." Credit loss allowances doubled in FY2025 to $50.2M before Worldpay was on the books. In a consumer downturn, this could accelerate.

**Macro sensitivity (Medium):** Payment volumes correlate with consumer spending. In a stagflationary environment with negative real wages and potential recession, transaction volume growth could slow below the 5% organic guide.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($85-95, +25-40%):** Worldpay integration proceeds smoothly, $650M+ synergies begin materializing in H2 2026 results, Genius platform adoption accelerates across combined merchant base, and the sector re-rates as macro fears prove overstated. At 6.5x FY2027E adjusted EPS of $16.26, the stock would trade at $106. Even at 5.5x, it reaches $89. A single quarter of above-guide results with visible synergy traction could catalyze significant multiple expansion from these compressed levels. China UnionPay partnership ($120M equity income, growing 72% YoY) provides underappreciated optionality.

**Bear Case ($50-55, -20-27%):** Integration proves more complex than guided, organic growth decelerates below 5% as consumer spending weakens, synergy realization is delayed beyond H1 2027, and rising rates increase refinancing costs on the $6.2B 2028-2035 notes. A guidance cut would likely drive the stock to 4x FY2027 estimates ($65) or worse. Credit rating downgrade becomes a possibility if EBITDA disappoints while leverage remains elevated.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q2 2026 results (expected August) demonstrate organic revenue growth ≥6% with visible synergy contribution to margins
2. Management provides specific, quantified synergy realization milestones that de-risk the $650M target
3. Stock declines below $60 on no incremental fundamental deterioration, compressing forward adjusted P/E to <4.3x

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Organic revenue growth declines below 3% for two consecutive quarters
2. FY2026 EPS guidance reduced below $13.50 (implying synergy delay)
3. Credit rating placed on negative watch by any major agency

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected early August 2026). This will be the first quarter for assessing combined organic growth and cost trends with Worldpay more fully integrated into operations.

## Conclusion

GPN is a HOLD with an uncertain outlook. The valuation at 4.9x forward adjusted P/E argues against further selling — the bear case (integration failure, competitive erosion, macro weakness) appears largely discounted. However, the absence of a specific near-term catalyst for re-rating, combined with genuine execution risk on the largest transaction in company history, leverage approaching market cap parity in a rising-rate environment, and management's demonstrated willingness to destroy value through poorly-timed buybacks, prevents a constructive stance.

The analyst consensus (12 Buy / 19 Hold / 2 Sell, median target $82) accurately reflects the balance of evidence: upside exists, but the timing and magnitude depend on integration execution that won't be visible until H2 2026 earnings at earliest. The declining score trajectory (6.3 → 5.9 previously) stabilizes here — Q1 beat and maintained guidance arrest the deterioration without providing sufficient evidence of improvement to upgrade.

Relative to the prior HOLD at 5.9, the current assessment at 5.5 reflects: (1) the entire legacy payments sector is in distress (sector-wide rather than company-fixable), (2) the debt load is costly to service/refinance in the current rate environment, and (3) the earnings beat was modest (+3.7%) without the magnitude needed to drive re-rating.
