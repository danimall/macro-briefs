**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Atlantic Union Bankshares is the largest regional bank headquartered in the lower Mid-Atlantic, a ~$37.6B-asset commercial franchise spanning Virginia, Maryland, the Greater Washington metro, and now expanding into North Carolina. The April 2025 acquisition of Sandy Spring Bancorp roughly doubled the balance sheet and share count, and Q2 2026 marked the first quarter in two years with zero merger-related costs; the integration is functionally complete and generating cross-sell synergies (Sandy Spring now contributes ~27% of swap volume and ~32% of FX revenue). Operating metrics are strong: adjusted operating ROTCE of 20.11%, adjusted ROA of 1.47%, a 47.47% efficiency ratio, and net charge-offs of just 3 basis points annualized. On the operating fundamentals alone, this is an upper-tier regional bank.

The investment question is not quality but price. AUB has appreciated 27.6% over the trailing year to $42.23, sitting close to its 52-week high of $43.62 and trading at roughly 2.0x tangible book value ($20.77/share) and ~11x forward earnings. The most-cited bull metric, ~12% annual tangible book value growth, is real and dilution-adjusted, but the reported net interest margin flatters run-rate earnings power: the headline 9bp NIM expansion to 3.94% was accretion-driven, while core NIM (ex-purchase-accounting accretion) rose only 1bp to 3.46%. As the ~$145M/year of Sandy Spring accretion amortizes down, reported margin faces a structural fade that core loan repricing must offset. Every negative in the guidance revision traced to the funding side: deposit mix migration into higher-cost interest-bearing accounts, a 94.1% loan-to-deposit ratio at the top of management's preferred range, and quarter-end borrowings.

We maintain a HOLD. The prior three reports have all been HOLDs with a declining score trajectory (6.1 → 6.0 → 5.7), and the current data neither reverses nor materially extends that decline; it confirms a fairly-valued, well-run bank where the operating story (loan growth, capital return, TBVPS compounding) is roughly balanced against the accretion fade and funding-cost pressure. Own AUB for its ~3.5% dividend and steady book-value compounding, not for a near-term multiple re-rating. Reflecting the calibration that Financials BUYs carry negative alpha by default and that a "cheap" valuation label is not itself a buy signal, we require a distinct catalyst before upgrading, and none is dated within the next two quarters.

## Company Overview

Atlantic Union Bank, the operating subsidiary of Atlantic Union Bankshares (Glen Allen, Virginia), is a full-service commercial bank. Its growth engine is commercial lending — C&I, commercial real estate, construction, and multifamily — supplemented by retail/consumer banking, mortgage, and fee businesses (treasury management, interest-rate swaps, FX, and fiduciary/wealth management). The Sandy Spring acquisition (closed April 1, 2025) added a large Maryland/Greater Washington franchise, taking total assets from ~$24.6B to ~$37.6B and lifting the former Sandy Spring book to roughly one-third of loans.

Strategic posture has shifted toward organic execution. Management stated on the Q2 call that there are "no additional acquisitions currently planned during this phase," refocusing the story on integration harvest, capital return, and a de novo branch build-out in North Carolina (10 planned branches in Raleigh and Wilmington, first opening July 2026, most complete by 2027). This "densification" is a multi-year capital and expense commitment embedded in the $742–752M full-year opex guide.

## Financial Analysis

**Q2 2026 results (reported July 21, 2026).** GAAP net income was $161M, GAAP EPS $1.11, net income available to common $158M. Adjusted operating EPS of $0.94 beat the $0.92 consensus, excluding a $32.3M pre-tax gain on the sale of AUB's equity interest in Berrings Insurance. Management noted it has already reached its medium-term ROA and ROTCE targets.

**Net interest income and margin.** The reported 9bp NIM expansion was driven by higher loan accretion and higher loan yields, while core NIM ex-accretion rose only 1bp. FTE net interest income was $329.7M, up $12.8M QoQ. Full-year 2026 FTE NII is guided to $1.32–1.33B (slightly lowered on funding pressure), with FY NIM tightened to 3.90–3.95%. Purchase-accounting accretion is guided at $140–150M for 2026, a material but declining tailwind. The partial offset: ~$800–900M/quarter of variable loans maturing at ~5% reprice to ~6.10%, a 100–110bp pickup that management expects to "grind" core margin higher.

**Balance sheet and growth.** Loans held-for-investment were $28.7B at 6/30/26, up 10.4% annualized (period-end) and ~6% on average; YTD annualized growth is 6.4%, with FY guidance toward the higher end of mid-single-digit. Deposits of $30.5B grew only ~1% annualized, pushing the loan-to-deposit ratio to the top of the preferred 90–95% band. Deposit growth is the binding constraint; quarter-end short-term borrowings were required to fund loan growth.

**Credit quality.** This is a genuine strength. Net charge-offs were just 3bp annualized in Q2 and YTD, and FY NCO guidance was lowered to 5–10bp. Nonperforming assets stand at 39bp of loans HFI; criticized/classified improved to 4.4% from 4.5%. The ACL is 115bp of loans ($331M total). The Chief Credit Officer flagged that industry-wide losses have run below normal for a decade, so eventual normalization (higher losses) is a "when," not "if," across the sector, but AUB's current book is clean.

**Capital and returns.** CET1 is 10.41% (within the preferred 10–10.5% range) even after AOCI/HTM unrealized losses. Tangible book value per share is $20.77, up 4.2% QoQ and 13% YoY, with ~12% TBVPS growth guided for 2026. The $250M buyback authorization is being executed cautiously — only ~$10M in Q2 at an average $37.76, with ~$240M remaining and a stated ~4-year earn-back discipline. The quarterly dividend is $0.37 (flat QoQ), a ~3.5% yield.

**Earnings quality and delivery.** The four-quarter surprise record shows three beats and one narrow miss (Q2 2026 +1.8%, Q1 2026 +1.1%, Q4 2025 +11.8%, Q3 2025 -0.8%). Estimate revisions are modestly positive across every window (current year $3.77, +0.9%/30d, +1.2%/90d; next year $4.03, +0.5%/30d, +0.9%/90d). This is positive earnings momentum, but at a magnitude (low-single-digit revisions) that signals steady execution rather than a turnaround, well short of the >30% revision velocity that would flag a mispriced turnaround.

## Growth Analysis

AUB's growth is dependable but not accelerating. Loan growth in the mid-single-digit range, positive but low-single-digit estimate revisions, and ~12% guided TBVPS compounding describe a bank executing well within its capacity. Historical net income was flat-to-declining 2021–2024 ($264M → $235M → $202M → $209M) before rising to $274M in 2025; the Sandy Spring deal and rate environment lifted profitability upward, and that step-change is now in the base.

The forward growth levers are: (1) core loan repricing (the 100–110bp variable-loan pickup grinding core NIM higher), (2) the North Carolina de novo build-out (double-digit pipeline growth cited in NC and Greater Washington, but a multi-year payoff), and (3) buyback execution as a per-share accretion tool. Against these sit two structural headwinds — the accretion fade and funding-cost migration — that cap the pace at which reported earnings can compound. The Growth Prospects score of 5.7 reflects a solid but decelerating trajectory (down from 6.8 in May), and I weight it as a directional positive only in combination with the stronger financial-health signal, consistent with the observation that growth-prospects separation has thinned to near-noise levels recently.

## Valuation Assessment

At $42.23, AUB trades at ~11.2x the $3.77 current-year consensus EPS and ~10.5x the $4.03 next-year estimate, roughly 2.0x tangible book value, and yields ~3.5%. Against the regional-bank peer set, the trailing P/E of ~12.6x sits modestly below the peer median of 13.7x when measured on trailing EPS; on a forward basis, AUB is broadly in line to slightly below peers (HWC 14.7x, ABCB 13.7x, HOMB 11.5x, ASB 10.1x). The dividend yield of 3.5% exceeds the 2.8% peer median. This is a fair-to-slightly-cheap multiple, not a bargain.

The reasons not to reward the cheapness with a high valuation score are specific. First, the 2.0x tangible book multiple already reflects the ~20% ROTCE; the market is paying for the profitability, so there is no obvious mispricing to arbitrage. Second, the reported earnings power is inflated by annual accretion that will decline; a bank at 11x forward earnings where a meaningful slice of margin is temporary is not as cheap as the headline suggests. Third, the calibration record is clear that a low P/E is a value-trap warning absent an independent catalyst, and here the catalyst set (NC branches, buyback completion, core repricing) is gradual and already partly discounted. Fourth, the five-year TSR of 128 trails the peer group's 153; strong operating metrics have not yet translated into peer-beating shareholder returns, which suggests the market is discounting exactly the accretion-fade and funding-pressure dynamics identified above.

Analyst targets (low $43, mean $47, median $47.5, high $50) imply ~11% upside to the mean. On a stock with rising estimates I would treat those as floors, but AUB is a steady compounder with low-single-digit revisions, so the consensus target is a reasonable central estimate rather than a stale lagging anchor. My base case fair value is ~$45–46 (12x the $3.80 blended forward EPS plus dividend), roughly 7–9% above spot including the yield. That is a modest positive, not the asymmetric upside a high valuation score requires. Valuation scored 5.0 — fairly priced.

## Competitive Landscape

AUB is now the largest regional bank headquartered in the lower Mid-Atlantic, competing against super-regionals, money-center banks, and community banks across Virginia, Maryland, D.C., and North Carolina. Its ~47% efficiency ratio and ~20% ROTCE place it in the upper tier of regional-bank profitability, and the Sandy Spring deal built genuine density in the attractive Greater Washington market with revenue synergies (swaps, FX) already flowing. The North Carolina expansion extends the franchise into growth markets.

The trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving rather than clearly widening. Positives: clean integration, cross-sell traction, and market-share density in Greater Washington. Offsets: the industry backdrop of intense deposit competition (which is precisely what pressured AUB's guidance), regulatory scrutiny of CRE concentration (~275% of capital), and the five-year TSR lag versus peers. The competitive-position score of 6.0 reflects a strong-but-not-widening moat; I hold it flat rather than extend it, mindful that the competitive-position dimension has recently sorted mildly backwards in the calibration data and should not carry weight on its own.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE and construction concentration.** With CRE, construction, and multifamily cited as the Q2 growth drivers and CRE at ~275% of capital, AUB carries sector-wide regulatory scrutiny. Charge-offs are pristine today, but that is below any reasonable normalized rate; the risk is that credit normalizes higher across the cycle, and AUB's concentration amplifies that sensitivity.

**Accretion-dependent reported margin.** The reported NIM is flattered by declining purchase-accounting accretion, with core NIM well below the headline. If core repricing does not offset the accretion roll-off, reported margin and earnings power fade; the market appears to be discounting this, given the TSR lag.

**Funding-cost pressure.** The lowered NII guidance came entirely from the deposit side: migration from non-interest-bearing to interest-bearing/sweep accounts, new IB deposits at 3–3.5%, June spot deposit cost of 195bp, and a loan-to-deposit ratio forcing borrowings. This is the metric to watch, not credit.

**Rate-path assumption.** Guidance embeds a 25bp Fed hike in September 2026, an unusual hiking assumption reflecting a macro regime of geopolitical (Iran-related) uncertainty. A different path (either faster hikes or a surprise cut) moves NII materially given ~50% IB / 40% overall deposit betas.

**Execution and geographic concentration.** The NC de novo build-out is a multi-year, expense-heavy commitment with new-market execution risk. Heavy exposure to Virginia, Maryland, and the Greater Washington footprint carries some sensitivity to federal government employment dynamics.

Netting these, the risk score is 5.3 — moderate idiosyncratic risk not fully priced, weighted toward the accretion fade and funding pressure that the multiple has only partly discounted. Consistent with calibration, I do not let risk factors drive the overall score; they serve as context.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 41.4% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 28.5% (a 12.8pp spread), and IV jumped from 11.8% to 41.4% since the prior snapshot; the market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty despite a clean Q2 print. The put/call skew of 12.0% at the front month (widening to 22.3% at three months) indicates elevated put demand and downside hedging. The term structure is in contango (near-term 41.4% below six-month 43.8%), a normal/complacent shape. For a HOLD, the takeaway is that the options market is pricing a wider distribution than the fundamentals suggest, with a modest downside tilt, consistent with our view that the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetrically favorable, and that patient entry (post-vol-normalization) is preferable to chasing near the 52-week high.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($50 / +18%).** Core loan repricing (the 100–110bp variable-loan pickup) offsets the accretion fade, holding reported NIM near 3.95%; deposit growth catches up to loans, relieving the funding constraint; the NC build-out ramps ahead of plan; and management accelerates the ~$240M remaining buyback at ~2.0x book, adding per-share accretion. TBVPS compounds at ~12% and the multiple holds at ~12x forward, driving the stock toward the analyst high of $50. Credit stays benign.

**Bear case ($34 / -19%).** Accretion rolls off faster than core repricing offsets, and funding-cost migration continues, compressing reported NIM below 3.90% and stalling NII. CRE/construction concentration meets a credit normalization cycle, lifting charge-offs toward normalized levels and forcing higher provisioning. The rate-hike assumption proves wrong in a disruptive direction. The stock derates toward ~1.6x tangible book (~$33) as the market re-prices the earnings-quality discount already visible in the TSR lag.

The base case sits between: ~$45–46 fair value (12x blended forward EPS plus dividend), roughly 7–9% total return including yield, over 12 months. The distribution is roughly symmetric — modest upside to base and bull, comparable downside to bear — which supports HOLD rather than BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 2026 earnings release (October 2026), which will show the first full post-CFO-transition quarter, the September Fed decision's impact on funding costs, and early NC branch economics.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Core NIM (ex-accretion) expanding by ≥3bp per quarter for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating that loan repricing is outrunning the accretion fade and funding drag.
- Deposit growth reaccelerating to mid-single-digit annualized, pulling the loan-to-deposit ratio back below 92% and removing the funding constraint on growth.
- A pullback toward ~$36–38 (roughly 1.75x tangible book) on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore asymmetric upside, OR an acceleration in buyback execution (≥$50M/quarter) at attractive prices.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Net charge-offs rising above ~25bp annualized or a material increase in criticized/classified CRE, signaling credit normalization arriving faster than the sector expects.
- Reported NIM falling below 3.85% with core NIM flat-to-down, confirming the accretion fade is not being offset.
- A guidance cut to full-year NII or a reversal in the positive estimate-revision trend.

## Conclusion

Atlantic Union Bankshares is a well-managed, top-quartile-profitability regional bank with a clean acquisition integration, pristine current credit, a disciplined capital-return posture, and steady ~12% tangible-book compounding. Those are the durable positives that justify continuing to own it for the ~3.5% dividend and book-value growth. The offsetting realities — a reported margin flattered by declining accretion, funding-cost migration that already forced a guidance trim, and a five-year TSR that trails peers — explain why the stock trades at a fair rather than cheap multiple near its 52-week high after a 28% run.

The three prior reports were all HOLDs on a gently declining score path, and today's data confirms that regime rather than breaking it. There is no dated near-term catalyst to force a re-rating, the valuation offers a modest positive rather than asymmetric upside, and the sector calibration argues against rewarding a Financials name absent a distinct company-specific catalyst. We reiterate **HOLD** (growth_hold) with a modestly constructive bias: a genuine buying opportunity would emerge on a pullback toward the high-$30s or on clear evidence that core NIM is grinding higher fast enough to offset the accretion fade. Overall score 5.7.
