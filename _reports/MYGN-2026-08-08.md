## Executive Summary

Myriad Genetics' July 30, 2026 second-quarter report broke the business model. Revenue of $190.7M fell 10.5% year over year while total test volume fell only 1%; average revenue per test declined 9%, and within the largest franchise the deterioration was far worse: hereditary cancer revenue fell 13% on volume growth of 8%, implying realized price per test down roughly 19%. Management described the cause as "increased payer friction." Full-year revenue guidance was cut from $860–880M to $770–790M (a $90M, 10.3% reduction at the midpoint, and 9.7% below the $863.6M FactSet consensus reported at the time), adjusted gross margin guidance was cut to 66–67%, and adjusted-EBITDA guidance was withdrawn rather than lowered. Consensus EPS for the current year has been revised from +$0.05 ninety days ago to −$0.69; next year from +$0.16 to −$0.34.

The financing structure converts this from a bad year into a constrained one. The July 2025 OrbiMed credit agreement carries a single financial covenant: a minimum trailing-twelve-month **revenue** test, measured **monthly**, starting at $615M at December 31, 2025 and escalating quarterly to $974M by December 31, 2029. Cost reduction does not help compliance. Selling a product line mechanically reduces TTM revenue and, under the disclosed mandatory-prepayment terms, may divert proceeds to debt repayment rather than operations. That reverses the standard turnaround sequence, and it is the reason both announced initiatives — the consultant-led "Ascend" efficiency program and a portfolio review — address the P&L problem while leaving the covenant problem untouched.

At $3.26 the stock trades at approximately 0.41x enterprise value to guided 2026 revenue for a business with 67% gross margins, roughly at reported book value of $3.18 per share, but at about 2.6x tangible book of $1.23. The price is at the bottom of the post-print analyst target range ($3.00 Wells Fargo, $3.20 BofA at Sell) against a $4.00 median. Much of the damage is discounted, which is why this is not a maximum-conviction avoid. But the value driver — realized reimbursement per test — is outside management's control, the covenant clock runs monthly, cash burn of roughly $55–70M annualized against $115.2M of cash implies under two years of self-funded runway, and an unaccrued False Claims Act case with treble-damages exposure sits against a $312M equity. Expected return is modestly positive on a point estimate and clearly negative on a risk-adjusted basis given a bear case near $1.20. **REDUCE.**

## Company Overview

Myriad is a Salt Lake City molecular diagnostics laboratory that develops and performs genetic tests for disease risk, prognosis and therapy selection, recognizing revenue when results are released to the ordering provider. FY2025 revenue of $824.5M split across four legacy lines: Hereditary Cancer $372.4M (45.2%), Prenatal $186.3M (22.6%), Mental Health/GeneSight $144.1M (17.5%) and Tumor Profiling $121.7M (14.8%). From 2026 the company reports three categories: Cancer Care Continuum, Prenatal Health and Mental Health, and it now reports as a single operating segment, which reduces external visibility into line-level profitability.

The genuinely durable assets are narrow but real: two decades of clinical and payor history behind MyRisk/BRACAnalysis in hereditary cancer; FDA-approved companion diagnostics (BRACAnalysis CDx, MyChoice CDx) tied to PARP-inhibitor therapy, which creates pharma-linked demand that is harder to displace; GeneSight as the category-defining pharmacogenomic test in psychiatry; and structurally high gross margins near 70%. What the disclosures do not support is pricing power. The business is overwhelmingly domestic ($768.0M U.S. versus $56.5M rest-of-world in FY2025), and the international book is shrinking rapidly: RoW revenue fell from $81.5M in 2023 to $56.5M in 2025, with Tumor Profiling RoW down from $33.5M to $11.9M, alongside a decision to discontinue EndoPredict in the U.S. in H1 2026.

The pipeline is scientifically credible and commercially pre-revenue. Precise MRD (ctDNA minimal residual disease) is available in breast, colorectal and renal cancer, but the first MolDX local coverage determination request — for breast — was only submitted in July 2026. Coverage has been requested, not obtained. FirstGene, a single-draw multi-condition prenatal screen at eight weeks, launched in July 2026. Prolaris + AI launched in Q2 2026 with PathomIQ as the AI partner; the economics of that arrangement are not disclosed.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin.** FY2023 through FY2025 revenue ran $753.2M, $837.6M, $824.5M. H1 2026 revenue was $391.1M versus $409.0M, and Q2 2026 was $190.7M versus $213.1M. Gross margin compressed from 71.2% in Q2 2025 to 66.6% in Q2 2026, and from 69.9% to 67.7% on a half-year basis. Adjusted EBITDA swung from +$14.5M in Q2 2025 to −$16.9M in Q2 2026, and from +$14.3M to −$21.4M for the half.

**The volume/price decomposition is the whole case.** Q2 2026 volume of 379,000 tests was down 1%; revenue per test was down 9%. By line: hereditary cancer volume +8% with revenue −13% (implied price per test −19.4%); prenatal volume −9% with revenue −16% (implied price −7.7%); mental health volume +4% with revenue −3% (implied price roughly −6%, attributed partly to a write-off of aged receivables). The commercial engine is working and the reimbursement engine is not.

An offset: Q2 included an $11.0M unfavorable prior-period revenue true-up, equal to 5.8% of quarterly revenue. Excluding it, Q2 revenue would have been $201.7M, down 5.4% year over year rather than 10.5%. Prior-period true-ups have swung materially: +$7.2M in FY2023, +$21.5M in FY2024, immaterial in FY2025, then +$3.0M in Q1 2026 and −$11.0M in Q2 2026. Ernst & Young designated "Measurement of revenue" a Critical Audit Matter in its February 24, 2026 opinion for exactly this reason. The practical implication cuts both ways: FY2024's reported growth was flattered by roughly 2.6 points of favorable estimate revision, and 2026's decline is being amplified by the reverse. Underlying trajectory is worse than 2024 suggested and less catastrophic than Q2 2026 suggests. That does not make the hereditary-cancer price erosion benign; the UnitedHealthcare GeneSight coverage change that cut Mental Health revenue $26.1M (−15%) and revenue per test roughly 20% in FY2025 is the template for how permanent these resets can be.

**Guidance embeds stabilization, not recovery.** Guided FY revenue of $770–790M less H1 actual of $391.1M implies H2 of $378.9–398.9M, or roughly $189.5–199.5M per quarter against Q2 actual of $190.7M. Versus H2 2025 of $415.5M, that is a further 4.0% to 8.8% year-over-year decline. There is no cushion in the guide for additional collections deterioration.

**The cost problem is quantifiable.** Taking the 66.5% adjusted-gross-margin midpoint on $780M gives $518.7M of full-year adjusted gross profit; less H1's $265.4M leaves roughly $253.3M on about $388.9M of H2 revenue, or ~65.1%, a further step down from H1's 67.9%. Holding adjusted opex flat at H1's $299.0M would produce H2 adjusted EBITDA near −$36M and a full-year figure near −$57M. Treat that as a sensitivity showing the size of the hole, not a forecast: it ignores Ascend savings and collections seasonality. Annualizing H1 inputs, restoring adjusted-EBITDA breakeven at the guided revenue and margin requires adjusted opex of roughly $543M against an H1 run rate of $598M, approximately **$55M of annualized cost reduction**. Disclosed strategic-realignment costs to date are $2.3M in Q2 and $4.0M in H1. No savings target, timeline or restructuring charge has been quantified for Ascend.

**Management is spending into the decline.** Q2 sales and marketing rose 15.6% to $83.1M while revenue fell 10.5%, taking S&M from 33.7% to 43.6% of revenue, a 984 basis-point deterioration. G&A fell 13.5% to $57.8M and R&D was roughly flat at $25.0M, so the entire net opex increase is commercial. The defensible reading: the expanded sales force is producing units (Cancer Care Continuum volume +6%, hereditary cancer +8%), and with compliance measured on revenue, the sales force is the last thing management can responsibly cut. The unfavorable reading: incremental spend is being converted into tests reimbursed at sharply lower rates, so it is not generating gross profit. The next two quarters of collections data adjudicate this; suspending rather than lowering EBITDA guidance suggests management does not yet know either.

**Liquidity and capital structure.** Cash fell from $149.6M at year-end to $115.2M at June 30, 2026. H1 operating cash flow was −$24.0M and company-defined adjusted free cash flow −$30.8M. Prepaid taxes fell $10.2M over the half, most plausibly a refund, so underlying H1 burn was closer to −$34M. A normalized $55–70M annual burn at the current cost structure implies roughly 1.6 to 2.1 years of runway on cash alone, before cuts or asset sales. Total assets fell from $1,027.6M to $706.6M during FY2025 and to $638.9M by June 30, 2026; equity fell from $701.1M to $303.9M. Current ratio 2.21, with **no debt in current liabilities**.

The OrbiMed facility is $200M in structure: $125M funded term loan at closing plus a $75M delayed draw available at the company's election through June 30, 2027, subject to undisclosed conditions, which means it cannot be counted as committed liquidity. Interest is the greater of one-month SOFR or 2.50% plus 6.50%, 10.4% at year-end, plus an accrued exit fee, a prepayment premium and a 0.5% undrawn fee. No principal amortization until September 30, 2029; maturity July 31, 2030; secured by substantially all assets. A first amendment was executed January 5, 2026 covering cash-management matters.

The covenant is the operative constraint: a minimum TTM revenue test at each month-end, $615M at December 31, 2025, escalating quarterly to $974M by December 31, 2029. The intermediate steps are not disclosed. On a linear reading of $22.44M per quarter, thresholds would be roughly $704.8M at December 31, 2026, $794.5M at December 31, 2027 and $884.3M at December 31, 2028. Under that assumption the guided $780M midpoint clears end-2026 with about 10.7% headroom, but flat revenue at $780M would breach the end-2027 test by roughly $14.5M, requiring ~1.9% growth off the guided midpoint to comply. **This is an interpolation, not a filing fact.** A back-weighted schedule, common in growth-lender structures, would push the binding date into 2028; a front-weighted one would tighten it. OrbiMed already amended once and, as a specialist healthcare credit fund holding a 10.4% first-lien position against a borrower with $115M of cash, has strong incentive to amend rather than accelerate. Breach is therefore best modeled as a repricing and dilution event rather than an automatic liquidation.

Solvency screens are dominated by the accumulated deficit ($1,200.0M) and the FY2025 $319.4M impairment, both of which sit in the retained-earnings and EBIT terms. The Altman Z-Score reads below the 1.81 distress threshold on every variant tested, but the near-term picture is better described by $115.2M of cash against $125M of non-amortizing debt with no maturity until 2030. The risk channel is the monthly revenue covenant and the burn rate, not a maturity wall.

## Growth Analysis

There is no near-term growth. Guided FY2026 revenue of $780M at the midpoint is 5.4% below FY2025 actual, and the implied H2 run rate is 4–9% below prior-year. Prenatal volume is down 9% in the quarter and 11% for the half, which in a generally growing NIPT market points to unit share loss; the filings do not name the recipients, so treat the attribution as inference. International revenue is down 30.7% over two years and EndoPredict is being withdrawn from the U.S. Whether that is focus or retreat, it shrinks the revenue base against which the covenant is measured.

The forward case rests on three pre-revenue items. Precise MRD has a credible scientific package (949-sample prospective MONITOR-Breast published in *Future Oncology*, MONSTAR-SCREEN-3 interim data at ASCO 2026) but requested its first Medicare coverage determination only in July 2026, a 2027-or-later revenue event, not guaranteed, and Myriad is a late entrant into a ctDNA-MRD market where established competitors already hold coverage. FirstGene launched in July 2026 into the same prenatal channel that is currently losing volume. Prolaris + AI has a July 2026 individual-patient-data meta-analysis across 14 studies and more than 8,000 patients behind it, with undisclosed partner economics.

Estimate momentum is the sharpest negative signal in the file. Current-year consensus EPS moved from +$0.05 to −$0.69 over ninety days (six analysts); next year from +$0.16 to −$0.34 (eight analysts); current quarter from +$0.04 to −$0.16. Q2 actual EPS of −$0.25 missed the −$0.06 estimate by 324%. The pattern of two beats and two misses across four quarters, with the misses accelerating in magnitude, indicates deteriorating forecast visibility rather than conservative guidance. Growth prospects score 3.0: unit demand in the core franchise is intact and the pipeline is real, but revenue, margin and earnings are all contracting simultaneously with no dated catalyst that reverses them inside twelve months.

## Valuation Assessment

At $3.26 on 95.65M shares, market capitalization is $311.8M. Adding $125M of debt principal and deducting $115.2M of cash gives enterprise value of approximately $321.6M, or **0.41x guided FY2026 revenue** of $780M. Capitalizing the $89.9M present value of operating leases as debt-like takes EV to roughly $411M, or 0.53x. There is no meaningful earnings or EBITDA multiple; both are negative. Reported book value is $3.18 per share, so the stock trades at roughly 1.0x book, but $139.0M of intangibles and $47.1M of goodwill mean tangible book is about $117.8M, or **$1.23 per share**, putting the stock at ~2.6x tangible book. The frequently cited "trades below book" framing does not survive that adjustment.

The supplied peer set (XNCR, VIR, FTRE, SRPT, ADMA) is a clinical-stage and specialty biotech group with a median P/E of 23.5 driven by two profitable names and three negative-earnings names. It is not a usable comparable for a $780M-revenue diagnostics service business, and I place no weight on it. The relevant benchmark is what the market pays for high-gross-margin diagnostics revenue, and 0.41x is deep into distressed territory; it prices either continued structural decline or a meaningful probability of a lender-driven restructuring.

Is that mispricing? Partly. A 67%-gross-margin business with $780M of revenue and roughly $55M of identified cost reduction to adjusted-EBITDA breakeven is not worth 0.41x revenue if reimbursement stabilizes at the new level. But cheapness here is not accompanied by an independent catalyst: the price driver is payor behavior, which management does not control; the cost program has no quantified target; and the asset-sale route that would ordinarily monetize the discount is impeded by the revenue covenant and mandatory prepayment mechanics. Post-print sell-side targets bracket the stock tightly on the downside (Wells Fargo $3.00, BofA $3.20 at Sell, TD Cowen $4.00, against a $4.00 median and a stale $10.00 high), and the rating distribution (1 strong buy, 1 buy, 4 hold, 1 sell, 1 strong sell) reflects genuine disagreement about whether the reimbursement pressure is structural or cyclical.

I score valuation 4.5. The multiple is low in absolute terms and supports a modestly positive point estimate of expected return, but the discount is largely explained by identified structural problems rather than mispricing, and the absence of a hard asset floor near the current price ($1.23 tangible book) removes the margin of safety a 0.4x revenue multiple would normally imply.

## Competitive Landscape

Payors, not competitors, are the proximate cause of the earnings collapse in the two largest franchises. Hereditary cancer volumes rose 8% while realized price fell roughly 19%; mental health volumes rose 4% while price fell about 6%. In molecular diagnostics a laboratory can win the ordering physician and still lose the economics, because the payor sits between them. The FY2025 disclosure of UnitedHealthcare's GeneSight coverage change (announced around October 2024, effective through 2025, removing $26.1M of revenue) establishes that a single payor decision can eliminate a mid-eight-figure revenue stream with roughly a year of notice, and that Myriad has no offsetting pricing leverage.

Prenatal is the clearer competitive tell: units down 9% in the quarter and 11% for the half while the overall screening market grows. Named competitors across Myriad's categories include Natera, Labcorp, Quest, BillionToOne, Guardant and Foundation Medicine, but the filings do not attribute the lost prenatal volume, so the share-loss read is inference rather than documented fact.

On MRD, Myriad is seeking rather than holding Medicare coverage, which places it behind incumbents in the fastest-growing segment of oncology diagnostics. Competitive trajectory is deteriorating on price realization in cancer and on units in prenatal, partially offset by demonstrable unit gains in hereditary cancer and a credible companion-diagnostic moat tied to PARP inhibitors. Score 3.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Reimbursement (dominant).** A 45%-of-revenue franchise losing roughly a fifth of realized price in a year is not sustainable. The variable that determines whether this equity is worth $1 or $6 is whether hereditary-cancer revenue per test stabilizes. No public disclosure yet distinguishes structural rate erosion from revenue-cycle execution failure.

**Covenant.** Monthly-tested, escalating, revenue-based, on a declining revenue base, secured by substantially all assets. Cost cuts are neutral to it; divestitures tighten it and may divert proceeds to mandatory prepayment. Timing depends on an undisclosed step schedule, the single highest-value piece of missing diligence.

**Litigation.** A qui tam False Claims Act complaint (filed November 2022, unsealed January 2025, served April 2025) alleges remuneration to physicians at Carolina Urology Partners in exchange for referrals. DOJ declined to intervene, a meaningful mitigant, but the court ruled on the motion to dismiss in July 2026 and the case largely proceeds. **No accrual has been recorded.** FCA damages are trebled, and the company has form: FY2023 included $112.8M of legal settlement expense. Against a $312M market cap this is a genuine tail.

**Payor and Medicare concentration.** Medicare was 13% of FY2025 revenue, up from 12% in each of the prior two years; one payor was 13% of accounts receivable. The 2026 mix shift toward Cancer Care Continuum does not diversify payor exposure; the direct concentration measures are flat to worse.

**Further impairment and reflexivity.** Goodwill fell from $286.3M to $51.6M in FY2025 and to $47.1M at mid-2026 (Mental Health $29.8M, International $17.3M), with FY2025 valuations using 16–17% discount rates against forecasts since cut twice. Net developed technology of $123.7M is also exposed. The FY2025 goodwill trigger was the share price itself, creating a loop in which a falling stock forces write-downs that further reduce reported equity.

**Dilution.** Roughly 10.8M unvested RSUs and PSUs equal 11.3% of shares outstanding and 10.2% post-issuance ownership dilution if fully issued; PSU vesting is conditioned on FY2027 revenue and adjusted EPS plus relative TSR, so a portion may never vest. Because RSUs are granted on dollar-value targets, a falling price mechanically increases share issuance: 4.9M RSUs were granted in 2025 at a $6.59 weighted-average fair value versus $23.66 for the prior unvested cohort. Share count rose from 91.3M to 93.5M to 95.6M on compensation alone, with ~54M shares of authorized headroom remaining.

**Fixed-cost rigidity.** $124.8M of future operating lease payments ($89.9M present value, 9.0-year weighted-average term), including a West Salt Lake City amendment adding ~63,000 square feet with $18.2M of future rent starting in FY2026, capacity committed against a growth plan since withdrawn.

**Tax.** Gross deferred tax assets of $183.2M carry a $150.8M valuation allowance; NOLs and research credits are subject to Section 382/383 limitation. The FY2025 $29.2M tax benefit included a $32.5M non-repeatable discrete item.

### Options Market Signal

Options signal here is thin and should carry minimal weight. Put/call open-interest ratio moved from 0.20 to essentially nil between the July 23 and August 8 snapshots, which reflects negligible open interest rather than a directional view; this is not a name where informed positioning shows up in the options chain. The equity market signal is more informative: 15.2M shares traded on July 31 versus a 1.54M average, with the stock printing a $2.74 low on August 3 before recovering to $3.26, indicating forced repositioning has largely cleared. Short interest of 8.7% of float at 5.2 days to cover is moderate and uncrowded, enough to produce a squeeze on any quantified Ascend savings announcement or covenant amendment, which is one reason a short position is unattractive despite the weak fundamentals.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~25% probability, $6.50, +99%).** The $11.0M prior-period true-up proves to be a one-time reset rather than a permanent rate cut; ex-true-up Q2 revenue was down only 5.4%. Hereditary-cancer revenue per test stabilizes in H2 as prior-authorization and revenue-cycle execution normalize, leaving an 8%-growing unit franchise at 67% gross margin. Ascend delivers the ~$55M of annualized cost reduction, restoring adjusted-EBITDA breakeven in 2027 and modest positive EBITDA thereafter. Revenue re-inflects to ~$820M in 2027, clearing even a linear covenant step. Precise MRD wins MolDX coverage for breast, adding a genuine growth line. At 0.85x revenue on ~$820M with net debt near $30M, equity is worth roughly $6.50.

**Base case (~45% probability, $3.85, +18%).** Reimbursement stabilizes at the depressed level without recovering. Revenue holds near $770M in 2027, Ascend delivers most but not all of the required savings, and adjusted EBITDA hovers near breakeven. Cash falls toward $85M and the covenant requires an amendment, granted at the cost of fees, repricing or warrants. At 0.55x revenue on ~$770M with ~$40M net debt and ~100M shares, equity is worth roughly $3.85.

**Bear case (~30% probability, $1.20, −63%).** Payor pressure spreads further into hereditary cancer and prenatal, the way it did from GeneSight in 2025. Revenue falls toward $700M in 2027, adjusted EBITDA runs −$40M, the monthly covenant is breached, and the amendment comes with meaningful equity issuance or a distressed raise. Remaining goodwill and developed technology are written down, taking reported book toward tangible book of $1.23. An adverse FCA outcome, with damages trebled and no accrual, is the accelerant. At 0.30x revenue on $700M with ~$70M net debt and ~115M shares post-dilution, equity is worth roughly $1.20.

Probability-weighted expected value is approximately $3.72, or +14% from $3.26. That point estimate is modestly positive, but it embeds a 30% probability of a 63% loss with a low-confidence bull case dependent on a variable management does not control. For a $312M equity carrying an unaccrued treble-damages case and a monthly-tested revenue covenant, that is a below-fair risk-adjusted return.

**On the score trajectory.** Prior reviews ran 4.3 (May, AVOID), 3.8 (June, AVOID), 4.0 (July 23, HOLD) at $5.70. The July upgrade was issued seven days before the guidance cut and is reversed by the new data. The 43% price decline since is fully explained by confirmed fundamental deterioration (a $90M guidance cut, a withdrawn profitability guide, and consensus EPS revised from positive to −$0.69), so I make no claim that the market is mistaken about the direction. The overall score declines to 3.43 on that deterioration while the recommendation moves from HOLD to REDUCE rather than back to AVOID, because $3.26 now sits at the bottom of the post-print target range and embeds a substantial portion of the impairment that $5.70 did not.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Existing holders should trim into any strength toward the $3.85 base case, retaining at most a residual position sized to survive the bear outcome. Do not add. Prospective buyers should wait for the Q3 2026 report (expected late October / early November 2026), which is the first observation that separates a one-time collections reset from structural rate erosion. The specific test: hereditary-cancer revenue per test, and whether prior-period true-ups turn neutral or favorable.

**Base / bull / bear targets.** Base $3.85 (+18%); bull $6.50 (+99%); bear $1.20 (−63%). Twelve-to-eighteen-month horizon, anchored to the Q3 and Q4 2026 prints, the FY2027 guide in February 2027, and any MolDX determination on Precise MRD for breast.

**Upgrade triggers (to HOLD, then ACCUMULATE).**
1. Hereditary-cancer revenue per test flat or improving sequentially for two consecutive quarters, with no unfavorable prior-period true-up above $3M.
2. A quantified Ascend savings target of $50M or more annualized with a disclosed timeline and restructuring charge, plus H2 2026 adjusted opex below $290M.
3. Disclosure of the intermediate covenant step levels showing a back-weighted schedule, or a lender amendment that resets thresholds to the reduced revenue base — either of which removes the 2027–2028 binding constraint.
4. A MolDX positive coverage determination for Precise MRD in breast.

**Avoid triggers (to AVOID).**
1. Q3 2026 revenue below $185M, or FY2026 guidance cut again below $770M, which under a linear covenant reading would compress end-2026 headroom to single digits.
2. Cash below $85M at any quarter-end without a disclosed, condition-free path to the $75M delayed draw.
3. An accrual recorded on the FCA matter, or a court ruling materially expanding the counts in play.
4. Any equity issuance at or below $3.00, or a covenant amendment carrying warrants or convertible features.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next decision point is the Q3 2026 earnings release. A second reassessment follows the FY2027 guidance issuance in February 2027, which is where management's stated optimism about "our 2027 financial profile" becomes testable and where the covenant path becomes visible in a full-year number.

## Conclusion

Myriad's problem is that its largest franchise is winning tests and losing price, and its debt covenant is written on revenue. Hereditary cancer volume grew 8% in Q2 2026 while implied realized price fell roughly 19%; total volume was down 1% while revenue per test fell 9%. Management responded by cutting revenue guidance $90M, cutting the margin guide, withdrawing adjusted-EBITDA guidance, and launching a consultant-led cost program plus a portfolio review — the first of which is neutral to the monthly TTM revenue covenant and the second of which tightens it, both mechanically and through the disclosed mandatory-prepayment terms.

At 0.41x enterprise value to guided revenue for a 67%-gross-margin diagnostics business, the discount is severe and creates real upside if reimbursement stabilizes: roughly $55M of annualized cost reduction on a stabilized $780M revenue base restores adjusted-EBITDA breakeven, and unit demand in the core franchise is growing. But the discount is explained rather than mispriced. Consensus EPS has been revised from +$0.05 to −$0.69 for the current year, tangible book is $1.23 per share so there is no asset floor near the current price, cash of $115.2M against $55–70M of annualized burn leaves under two years of self-funded runway, roughly 10% of ownership dilution sits in unvested equity that grows as the price falls, and an unaccrued False Claims Act case with trebled damages sits against a $312M equity. Expected return is +14% on a point estimate against a 30% probability of a 63% loss.

**REDUCE.** Trim into strength, do not add, and require two quarters of stabilizing revenue per test plus disclosure of the covenant step schedule before re-engaging. Shorting is not attractive here: 8.7% short interest at 5.2 days to cover, a stock already 62% below its 52-week high, and a lender with strong incentive to amend rather than accelerate together create asymmetric squeeze risk on any operational or financing good news.
