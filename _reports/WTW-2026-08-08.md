## Executive Summary

Willis Towers Watson delivered the quarter the bulls needed. Q2 2026 revenue rose 9% to $2,466M with organic growth of 5% (accelerating from roughly 3% in Q1, given H1's +4%), adjusted operating margin expanded 100bp to 19.5%, and adjusted diluted EPS rose 17% to $3.35. On July 30 management paired that with "Propel," a two-year AI and automation program targeting ~$400M of gross run-rate savings ($350M net of reinvestment) for ~$625M of cash investment, and a ~30% adjusted operating margin target for 2028. The board simultaneously added $1.5B to the repurchase authorization, taking capacity to roughly $2.0B, or 6.2% of the market cap. Consensus estimates have moved up across every period — FY2026 to $19.84 (+1.7% in 30 days) and FY2027 to $22.80 (+2.7%).

The margin claim has a real track record behind it. Adjusted operating margin, the company-selected compensation measure, ran 19.9% (2021), 20.9%, 22.0%, 23.9%, 25.2% (2025) — 530bp in four years with no down year. FY2025 generated $1,775M of operating cash flow against $229M of capex for $1,546M of free cash flow, a 15.9% FCF margin, with operating cash flow at 1.11x net income. Share count fell from 99.8M at end-2024 to 93.0M at June 30, 2026, a 6.8% reduction in eighteen months, executed in H1 at an average price of $272.92.

The obstacle is price. The shares closed June 30 at $261.37 and now trade at $344.82, a 31.9% move that sits 2.3% below the 52-week high of $352.79. Consensus mean target is $371.58 (+7.8%) and median $381 (+10.5%). Citi downgraded to Neutral on August 3 on the view that AI payback is too distant relative to what is now embedded. Against that, three specific things deteriorated in the first half: net debt rose $1,729M to $4,903M, the ex-fiduciary current ratio fell from 1.81x to 1.44x as cash dropped from $3,132M to $1,627M, and interest expense rose 20-22% year over year — with a $625M cash restructuring now beginning. Management's own MD&A states P&C "market conditions continue to soften, with the exception of U.S. Casualty and a few specialty product lines," and roughly 44% of H1 revenue is broking, where commissions are a percentage of premiums WTW does not set. Our base case is $376 (+9.1%), bull $405 (+17.5%), bear $282 (-18.2%). That distribution is a HOLD.

## Company Overview

WTW is an Irish-incorporated, New York-headquartered advisory, broking and solutions firm with approximately 46,900 employees at December 31, 2025, serving clients in more than 140 countries. It operates two reportable segments.

**Health, Wealth & Career (55% of FY2025 revenue, down from 60% in 2023)** spans Health & Benefits, Wealth (retirement actuarial and plan administration, plus investment advisory and the LifeSight master trust), Career (compensation benchmarking and employee-experience tools), and Benefits Delivery & Outsourcing (a private Medicare and individual exchange plus plan administration). Retirement relationships are described as long-term with high retention; Investments revenue is majority-retainer; Global Outsourcing runs on three-to-five-year contracts.

**Risk & Broking (45%, up from 40% in 2023)** comprises Corporate Risk & Broking, which places more than $34 billion of premium annually across specialty lines including Aerospace, Construction, Direct & Facultative, FINEX, Credit Risk, Marine and Natural Resources, plus Insurance Consulting & Technology, which sells software and advisory to insurers.

WTW takes no underwriting risk for its own account. Revenue by service in H1 2026 was Broking $2,140M, Consulting $1,715M, Outsourced administration $602M, Other $283M — roughly 44% premium-sensitive and 56% fee-based, a higher fee mix than pure-play brokers. That cuts both ways: less torque in hard markets, more insulation in soft ones.

Two structural features are genuinely durable rather than boilerplate. No single client individually exceeded 10% of consolidated revenue in 2025, 2024 or 2023, and WTW places business with approximately 2,500 carriers with none individually representing a significant concentration of premiums placed in any of those years. The client roster includes approximately 93% of the FTSE 100, 89% of the Fortune 1000 and 92% of the Fortune Global 500. Note that the HWC-to-R&B mix shift reflects where divestitures landed and where growth occurred; it says nothing about counterparty diversification, which was already at the floor.

The GAAP/adjusted wedge is unusually wide and the cause is identifiable: transaction and integration expense was $61M in Q2 2026 versus $2M a year earlier ($102M versus $2M for H1), primarily share-based compensation and other transaction costs tied to the Newfront acquisition. That single line accounts for $0.65 of the $0.92 gap between GAAP and adjusted EPS. GAAP operating margin fell 150bp while adjusted rose 100bp; both statements are true and both matter.

Decomposing the +17% adjusted EPS growth is instructive. Adjusted net income rose 10.9% ($285M to $316M) while adjusted EPS rose 17.1%, the 6.2pp gap being the 6.0% reduction in diluted shares. Holding shares flat, adjusted EPS would have been $3.16 (+10.5%), so buybacks contributed roughly $0.19 of the $0.49 increase. Management guides a ~$0.35 full-year FX tailwind, implying roughly $0.09 per quarter or another ~18%. Underlying operational improvement therefore accounts for something in the range of 40-45% of reported adjusted EPS growth.

**Balance sheet, read around the fiduciary funds.** At June 30, 2026: cash $1,627M, fiduciary assets $11,846M exactly matched by fiduciary liabilities, total assets $30,559M, shareholders' equity $7,688M, total debt $6,530M. Fiduciary balances are 39% of total assets and are client trust funds, not WTW resources. Excluding both sides, total liabilities/equity is 1.38x rather than the 2.69x a screen reports; true debt/equity is 0.85x. The company's disclosed capitalization ratio was 45.9% at June 30 versus 44.2% at December 31.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.3 is an artifact. Fiduciary assets inflate four of five denominators, WTW's negative retained earnings are a buyback consequence rather than accumulated losses, and the tool substitutes book for market equity. Substituting market equity ($32.07B against $21,478M of liabilities) and an ex-fiduciary balance sheet produces roughly 2.80-3.40 depending on the retained-earnings treatment. The model is calibrated on manufacturers and is not diagnostic here.

What is a real concern is the direction of travel. Net debt rose from $3,174M to $4,903M in six months, taking estimated net leverage from ~1.19x to ~1.84x on an estimated $2,670M of FY2025 adjusted EBITDA. H1 uses of cash — $1,039M acquisitions, $750M buybacks, $178M dividends, $114M capex, totaling $2,081M — far exceeded $474M of operating cash flow, funded by the cash balance plus $775M of delayed-draw term loan (SOFR + 62.5-125bp, maturing March 2029). The ex-fiduciary current ratio fell from 1.81x to 1.44x. The $1.5B revolver was undrawn and all covenants were met, so this is reduced flexibility rather than distress — but the $625M Propel cash spend now begins from a thinner base.

A modeling caution: H1 2026 FCF of $360M should not be annualized. FY2025 delivered $1,546M against H1 2025's $217M, meaning the first half was 14% of the year. Revenue and margin are second-half weighted (Career in H2, Benefits Delivery peaking in Q4) and H1 absorbs the annual incentive payout — accrued incentive compensation fell from $821M to $407M over the half. Anchor on the FY2025 15.9% FCF margin plus management's guided "continual improvement."

Tangible book value is deeply negative: goodwill $9,735M plus intangibles $1,444M against $7,688M of equity, an excess of $3,491M. Normal for a serial acquirer of people businesses, but book equity provides no downside protection and impairment remains a live risk given $1,273M already taken.

The accumulated deficit (-$699M at June 30) reconciles exactly as a capital-return artifact: -$296M + $526M H1 net income - $178M dividends - $750M buybacks = -$698M. Cumulative repurchase authorization since April 2016 totals $13.2B.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth accelerated to +5% in Q2 from roughly +3% in Q1. Segment detail: R&B +7% organic with 100bp of adjusted margin expansion; HWC +4% with 30bp. FY2026 guidance calls for mid-single-digit organic in both segments — HWC split high-single in Health, low-single in Wealth, low-to-mid single in Career, low-single in Benefits Delivery; R&B mid-single in CRB, low-to-mid single in ICT — with ~100bp of annual adjusted margin expansion in R&B and "incremental" expansion in HWC.

Consensus embeds FY2026 adjusted EPS of $19.84 and FY2027 of $22.80, implying 14.9% growth. Revisions are positive but modest: +1.7%/+1.8% on FY2026 over 30/90 days, +2.7%/+2.8% on FY2027. This does not clear the threshold that marks a genuine earnings inflection; it is the signature of a steady compounder where estimates drift up as buybacks and margin gains accumulate. The earnings surprise record supports moderate confidence in consensus: three beats and one narrow miss over four quarters, with the Q2 beat at 6.5%.

Propel frames the medium-term case. The arithmetic on management's own target: if 30% adjusted operating margin is achieved on roughly $11.0B of 2028 revenue (mid-single-digit organic compounded from ~$10.0B in 2026), adjusted operating income would be ~$3,300M against FY2025's $2,446M — a 35% increase, ~10.5% CAGR. Layered onto a share count shrinking 4-5% annually, that frames mid-teens adjusted EPS growth before incremental financing costs. Every input is a management aspiration, and the cost-to-achieve ratio of 1.6x ($625M/$400M) means the program consumes cash for two years before the run-rate benefit is fully visible.

Three growth headwinds are specific and disclosed. First, soft P&C pricing is management's own characterization, and with 44% of revenue in broking, commissions on premiums WTW does not set are directly exposed. Second, the Individual Marketplace is structurally maturing — the 10-Q states most corporate employers have already implemented a Medicare exchange and remaining growth from public and not-for-profit employers "may be more episodic"; lower Individual Marketplace commissions were an explicit drag in both Q2 and H1. Third, Career revenue was flat organically in Q2, with communications and broad-based pay work offset by constrained Middle East revenue from the ongoing conflict.

Also note the composition of the guided 2026 adjusted EPS items: Willis Re joint venture -$0.30, Newfront -$0.10, FX +$0.35. Net of the three, roughly $0.05 of benefit, all of it exchange rates. The portfolio additions are net dilutive this year; growth is carried by margin and buybacks.

## Valuation Assessment

The provided forward P/E field (20.88) is identical to the trailing figure and appears to be a data duplication; we use consensus estimates directly.

At $344.82:
- FY2026 consensus $19.84 → **17.4x**
- FY2027 consensus $22.80 → **15.1x**; implied growth 14.9% → **PEG ≈ 1.01**
- FY2025 FCF $1,546M / $32.07B market cap → **4.8% FCF yield** (4.2% on the ~$37.05B enterprise value)
- EV / estimated FY2025 adjusted EBITDA ($2,670M) → **~13.9x** (EBITDA estimate derived from the 25.2% adjusted margin plus $226M depreciation; treat as approximate)
- Dividend $3.84 annualized → **1.11% yield**, payout ~23% of FY2025 FCF, growing 4.3%

**Peer context.** Insurance broker medians on the supplied set are 34.0x P/E and 1.3% yield: BRO 19.2x, ERIE 20.7x, RYAN 44.5x, GSHD 34.0x, AJG 35.7x. WTW's 17.4x FY2026 is a wide discount to AJG and the mid-cap specialty names. That discount is not new and is not obviously wrong: WTW grows organically at mid-single digits against AJG's high-single-to-double-digit, carries a 56% fee/consulting mix that dampens hard-market torque, and has a history of impairments and portfolio churn that peers do not share. The 10-K also names Marsh & McLennan as WTW's principal competitor in three separate arenas beyond broking — pensions (Mercer), insurance consulting (Oliver Wyman) and exchanges (Mercer again) — an unusually broad overlap with a substantially larger firm.

**Why we do not score cheapness highly here.** The specific catalyst that would close the peer gap has already fired. The stock closed June 30 at $261.37 and now trades at $344.82; the July 30 combination of +5% organic, +100bp adjusted margin, +17% adjusted EPS, the 2028 margin target and the $1.5B buyback increase re-rated the shares 31.9% in roughly five weeks. Consensus mean target is $371.58 and median $381 — 7.8% and 10.5% above spot. Citi's August 3 downgrade to Neutral cited exactly this: "AI payback is a bit too far in the future" and the stock "appears to more reasonably discount the outlook." KBW moved the other way, raising its target to $394. Both sides hinge on the same fact — whether the 2028 target is credible and how much of it belongs in today's price.

A further consideration on multiple durability: the ~30% 2028 figure is an **adjusted** margin, and WTW explicitly declines to reconcile forward-looking non-GAAP measures to GAAP. Propel's $625M of cash cost comprises severance, retention, organizational design, contractor termination and system implementation, with "an immaterial portion" expected to be capitalized. These will very likely be treated as non-recurring and excluded from the adjusted series, which is standard sector practice and disclosed in advance, but it means GAAP operating margin will run materially below 30% through 2028. Q2 2026 is a live demonstration on a different transaction: GAAP margin down 150bp while adjusted rose 100bp. Investors paying a multiple on the adjusted series should track whether GAAP converges toward it in 2029.

## Competitive Landscape

The evidence supports that the "One WTW" strategy is being executed rather than merely stated: 530bp of adjusted margin expansion over four years, a deliberately shrinking revenue base from divestiture, and share count down 6.8% in eighteen months. Human capital metrics corroborate — voluntary rolling twelve-month attrition of 9.8% in 2025 versus 10.1% in 2024, described as well within target, with more than 6,700 hires.

Trajectory is mixed rather than uniformly improving. R&B is the outperformer (+7% organic, +100bp margin in Q2) under Lucy Clarke, recruited from Marsh in July 2024 where she ran Marsh JLT Specialty and Global Placement. The hire may be contributing, though prior-cycle rate hardening flowing through renewals and acquisition contribution are equally consistent with the result. HWC is the softer half: +4% organic, +30bp margin, Career flat, Individual Marketplace structurally maturing, and revenue share down from 60% in 2023 to 55% in 2025.

The more important competitive question the 10-K raises is demand destruction rather than share loss. Insureds are "retaining a greater proportion of their risk portfolios than previously" via captives, self-insurance pools, risk retention groups and mutuals, while new entrants include banks, accounting firms, new brokers and carriers themselves. The Q2 forward-looking factors add a sharper version: "our ability to compete with artificial intelligence technologies offered by new or existing competitors." If AI compresses the cost of risk advisory across the industry, WTW's $625M efficiency program could end up funding a price war rather than margin expansion. That is the structural bear case for the broking half, and it is the same uncertainty Citi flagged from the opposite direction.

Offsetting this, the intermediation position is unusually well-diversified. No client above 10% of revenue, approximately 2,500 carrier relationships with no concentration, and a client base spanning 93% of the FTSE 100. Revenue visibility rests on retention characteristics rather than contract: disclosed remaining performance obligations total only $1,482M against ~$9.9B of annualized revenue, and the 10-Q states explicitly that because most contracts are cancellable with less than one year's notice and no substantive penalty, "the majority" of obligations are excluded. RPO understates economic recurrence and simultaneously reveals that the base is contractually thinner than a subscription business — a demand shock would transmit faster than retention history implies.

## Risk Assessment

**Soft P&C pricing.** Management's own MD&A language, quantified exposure through $34B+ of annual premium placement and 44% broking mix, partially mitigated by 56% fee revenue and disclosed counter-cyclicality in health, benefits and administration. This is the highest-consequence disclosure in the filing and is at least partly priced sector-wide.

**Propel execution.** The Q2 10-Q added a risk factor specific to the AI Acceleration Plan and identifies it as the *only* material change from the FY2025 10-K. Its concerns are specific: the plan may reduce or restructure roles, distract human capital and slow platform enhancements; AI initiatives may create **correlated** errors-and-omissions liability if systems do not operate as intended; and there is "no assurance that our intended governance processes will sufficiently mitigate risks, especially given the complexity and scale of the Plan and the limited time to implement." For a firm selling professional judgment under E&O exposure, embedding AI into client-facing workflows is a different risk class than back-office automation.

**Unquantified legal and regulatory matters.** Forward-looking factors reference "the impacts of pending competition law and regulatory investigations" with no quantification available. The provision for claims, lawsuits and other proceedings was $298M at June 30 versus $281M at year-end. Item 3 of the 10-K is a bare cross-reference. Treat this as an open exposure of unknown size.

**Financing and structure.** Interest expense rose 22% in Q2 and 20% in H1 on the December 2025 $1.0B notes and the term-loan draws. The notes are obligations of Willis North America and Trinity Acquisition, guaranteed by a six-entity obligor group that generated only $1,252M of the $4,878M H1 consolidated revenue (26%) and owed $17.6B of net intercompany payables to non-guarantors, up from $16.3B. Creditors are structurally subordinated; the holding company structure may prevent receipt of dividends from subsidiaries in needed amounts.

**FX and pension.** WTW is structurally short sterling — 20% of expenses against 13% of revenue in H1 — with only $175M notional of designated hedges. The guided ~$0.35 FY2026 adjusted EPS FX tailwind reverses if rates move. Pension AOCI was -$2,180M at June 30; pension income is non-cash and flows through GAAP net income, GAAP operating margin, adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EPS but not free cash flow, which is precisely why FCF margin sits in the executive scorecard.

**Impairment.** Negative tangible book of -$3,491M with $1,273M of accumulated goodwill impairment already recognized, Newfront goodwill not yet assigned to reporting units and the purchase price allocation still preliminary (the pre-combination service allocation was already revised down $18M in Q2).

**Dilution is modest.** Award overhang at June 30 was ~1.71M units (1.84% of shares, reducing existing holders to 98.2%), with no options outstanding. The full $400M Newfront contingent tranche at $344.82 is ~1.16M shares (1.25% of existing, 98.8% retained). Maximum combined incremental ownership dilution of ~3.1%, only on full satisfaction of performance and service conditions, against a buyback that removed 6.8% of shares in eighteen months.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 24.6% against 30.1% one-year realized — cheap, at the 4th percentile within the current chain, with a flat term structure (24.6% near versus 25.1% far). Put/call open-interest ratio is 0.15 and volume ratio 0.23, both heavily call-tilted, with balanced one-month skew (-1.2%) widening modestly at six months (-4.0%). Implied move on the nearest expiry is ±4.0% over 12 days. Short interest is 4.5% of float with 5.6 days to cover.

The reading is straightforward: the options market prices no near-term event risk and almost no hedging demand after a 32% five-week advance. That is confirmation of the fundamental picture (no binary catalyst pending) but also a marker of complacency — protection is cheap precisely because nobody wants it. For an existing holder who does not want to reduce the position, twelve-month puts at 25.1% IV against 30.1% realized are the cheapest expression of the soft-market and Propel-execution risks. The June-to-August options delta shows ATM IV moving from 399.8% to 24.6%; the prior print appears anomalous and we read nothing into the change beyond the +35.1% price move over the window.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $405 (+17.5%)

Propel delivers on schedule and the four-year 530bp margin record extends. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $22.80 or better and the market applies 17.8x — still a wide discount to AJG at 35.7x — on the view that 30% adjusted operating margin in 2028 makes WTW structurally comparable to higher-multiple peers. The mechanism: $350M of net run-rate savings on ~$11.0B of revenue is ~320bp of margin, R&B compounds 100bp annually under Clarke, Newfront's ~$250M of 2026 revenue at ~26% adjusted EBITDA margin turns accretive in 2027 as integration costs roll off, and ~$2.0B of buyback capacity removes 5-6% of shares annually. Estimate revisions of +2.7% on FY2027 in thirty days and a PEG near 1.0 mean the current consensus targets ($371.58 mean, $381 median, $406 high) drift up as consensus catches the margin path. KBW's move to $394 is the leading edge of that.

### Bear Case — $282 (-18.2%)

Soft P&C pricing deepens and broking organic growth decelerates from +7% toward +2%, while HWC's Individual Marketplace maturation and flat Career revenue mean the fee half cannot offset. Propel's $625M consumes cash through 2028 while savings are competed away — the 10-K's own AI-competition risk factor made explicit — so gross savings arrive but net pricing does not. GAAP operating margin stays 400-500bp below the adjusted series, and after 2028 investors stop paying an adjusted multiple. FY2027 lands at $21.00 rather than $22.80, the multiple compresses to 13.5x, and the stock returns to the spring range. Amplifiers: the $0.35 FX tailwind reverses, an unquantified competition-law matter crystallizes, or a Newfront/legacy goodwill impairment lands against negative tangible book. The bear value sits below the street low of $303, which is where a genuine organic-growth disappointment would take it.

### What resolves it

Two data points. Q3 2026 organic growth — a print at or above +5% with R&B holding +7% would validate that the soft market is being absorbed; a deceleration to +3% or below would reopen the bear case. And the initial FY2027 guidance in early 2027, specifically whether adjusted margin expansion is guided above 100bp at the enterprise level (evidence Propel savings are reaching the P&L) and whether GAAP operating income begins converging toward adjusted.

**Base case target: $376** — 16.5x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $22.80. The multiple sits above WTW's own recent range and below the broker peer median, reflecting mid-single-digit organic growth plus a credible margin program. Upside 9.1%, plus 1.1% dividend.

**Bull case: $405** (17.8x FY2027) — +17.5%, essentially the street high of $406.

**Bear case: $282** (13.5x a reduced $21.00 FY2027) — -18.2%, below the street low of $303.

**Probability-weighted expected return:** roughly 4-5% total over twelve months at 50/22/28 weighting. Approximately fair.

**Timeframe:** reassess at the Q3 2026 print (late October) and again at initial FY2027 guidance (early February 2027).

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY if:**
1. Price falls to **$300-310** with organic growth intact at ≥+4% — that restores 20%+ upside to base case and a PEG below 0.9 on FY2027.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if:**

**Position management for existing holders:** hold the position. With twelve-month IV at 25.1% against 30.1% realized, protective puts are the cheapest available hedge against the soft-market and Propel-execution risks, and the flat term structure means there is no vol premium to pay for duration.
