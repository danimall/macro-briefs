## Executive Summary

West Pharmaceutical has re-accelerated. After two years of stagnation (revenue of $2,949.8M in FY2023, $2,893.2M in FY2024), H1 2026 net sales grew 17.3% reported and 13.9% organic, and management raised FY2026 guidance on July 23 to $3.345-3.380B of revenue (10-11% organic, up from 7-9%) with adjusted EPS of $8.85-9.05. Gross margin has recovered 320bp from the FY2024 trough of 34.5% to 37.7% in Q2 2026, and the Proprietary Products segment (81% of sales) is earning 42.6% gross margins with operating profit up 37.2% in H1. The mechanism is identifiable rather than promotional: High-Value Product components and delivery devices moved from 59% to 63% of sales in twelve months, Biologics from 38% to 42%, capex fell 41% year over year as the 2022-2024 capacity build finished and began absorbing, and pricing contributed roughly 3.7 points of the 13.9% organic growth. A component supplier that can net 220bp of gross-margin expansion while absorbing plant inflation has real pricing power, and the 10-K explains why: components are qualified onto a customer's drug filing, and switching requires regenerating equivalency data.

The problem is what you pay for it, and what the guidance actually contains. At $346.84 the stock is 38.8x the $8.95 guidance midpoint, 34.8x FY2027 consensus of $9.96, 40.8x TTM adjusted EPS of $8.50, and 8.2x book against a 15.5% FY2025 ROE. Free cash flow of $468.9M in FY2025 supports a 1.92% yield on market cap. Behind the headline raise, the arithmetic implies H2 2026 revenue of $1,628-1,663M against H2 2025's $1,609.6M (+1.1% to +3.3% reported) and implied H2 adjusted EPS of $4.35-4.55 versus $4.00, or +9% to +14% against H1's +37%. Much of that deceleration is mechanical: the SmartDose 3.5mL revenue transferred to AbbVie on July 1, a Q2 order pull-forward reverses, and the $48.5M H1 FX tailwind fades. The market's reaction on July 23-24, from an intraday $386 to a $328.20 close the next session, was a 15% drawdown on a beat-and-raise, which establishes that this holder base is valuation-sensitive and reads the second half correctly.

Three unresolved liabilities sit against that multiple. The May 2026 cyber incident involved data exfiltration plus system encryption; operations have recovered and the direct P&L cost was small (~$7M of West Vantage revenue, a 3.3pp segment gross-margin hit), but notification, remediation and litigation exposure is unquantified and unaccrued, with management stating insurance limits may be insufficient. A securities class action covering February 2023-February 2025 pleads §20A, an insider-trading claim against officers, with the motion to dismiss fully briefed since March 31, 2026 and undecided; no accrual has been taken. Michel Lagarde, an external hire from Thermo Fisher, becomes CEO on August 31, inheriting momentum, a 38.8x multiple, and a segment (West Vantage, 18% of revenue) whose gross margin has fallen 17.5% → 16.5% → 14.8% across FY2024, FY2025 and H1 2026. HOLD. The franchise is worth owning; the price is not worth adding to.

## Company Overview

West manufactures the primary containment and delivery components that sit between an injectable drug and the patient: elastomeric stoppers and seals, plungers, films and coatings, washing/inspection/sterilization services, vials and cartridges, self-injection devices, and attached analytical-lab and regulatory support. Revenue scales with customers' injection volumes, not with drug pricing.

Two segments. **Proprietary Products** ($2,492.1M, 81% of FY2025 sales) sells West's own catalog. **Contract-Manufactured Products** ($582.0M, 19%), renamed **West Vantage** in Q1 2026, designs and assembles customer-owned devices. The 10-Q states that the rename does not alter segment composition or restate prior periods; it is presentational.

Roughly 10,800 employees; 56.7% of FY2025 sales outside the United States. R&D of $74.3M in FY2025 is 2.4% of sales, and H1 2026 R&D of $35.5M grew 0.3%, modest for a business marketed as an innovation platform. The flagship launch is the Synchrony prefillable syringe system (January 2026).

The moat is a regulatory switching cost, not a patent estate. West's own risk factors note that regulation "makes it difficult to change components and devices produced by one supplier with those from another supplier, due to the large amount of data and information that customers must generate to demonstrate that the components and devices are equivalent," and that a regulatory change reducing that data burden "would increase competitive pressure." The company adds that no business segment "is wholly dependent on a single intellectual property asset, license, or technology." Named competitors: Datwyler and Aptar in elastomers; SMC, Phillips Medisize, Sharp and PCI in device assembly. Customer in-sourcing is disclosed as a live competitive vector in both the 10-K and 10-Q.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** FY2022 $2,886.9M; FY2023 $2,949.8M (+2.2%); FY2024 $2,893.2M (−1.9%); FY2025 $3,074.1M (+6.3% reported, +4.3% organic); H1 2026 $1,717.2M (+17.3% reported, +13.9% organic); Q2 2026 $872.3M (+13.8%/+12.7%).

FX matters: translation added $56.4M in FY2025 and $48.5M in H1 2026, or 3.4 of H1's 17.3 reported points. Stripping it, the H1 organic $204.2M splits into $149.7M of volume/mix and $54.5M of price. Price on the prior-year base of $1,464.5M is 3.7 points, a high price contribution for a components supplier and the cleanest quantitative evidence for the switching-cost thesis. Q2 Proprietary grew 15.5% organically, HVP components +18.4% and HVP delivery devices +29.2%.

One qualification on the FY2025 base: MD&A discloses that Proprietary growth was offset by "approximately $47 million in customer incentives received in connection with volumes achieved during 2024 that were not repeated in 2025." On the reading that West received those payments, FY2025 Proprietary organic growth was closer to 7.0% than the reported 4.8%, and FY2024 was flattered. The phrasing is ambiguous as to direction, so I treat this as a plausible adjustment rather than a fact. If it holds, the 2026 inflection is the second year of a recovery rather than a discontinuity, a mildly more durable framing.

**Margins.** GAAP operating margin: 25.4% (FY2022) → 22.9% → 19.7% → 19.0% (FY2025) → 20.7% H1 2026 → 20.5% Q2. Adjusted operating margin 22.6% in Q2 versus 20.2% in FY2025. Gross margin troughed at 34.5% in FY2024 and reached 37.7% in Q2 2026. Proprietary Products gross margin has nearly round-tripped: 43.1% (FY2023) → 38.6% (FY2024) → 40.5% (FY2025) → 42.6% (Q2 2026).

The recovery is entirely gross-margin driven; operating expense has not participated. SG&A rose 16.3% in FY2025 to $393.6M (12.8% of sales vs 11.7%), with Corporate SG&A up 33.8%. H1 2026 SG&A rose another 18.1% to $217.1M with Corporate and unallocated up 40.0%. Stock-based compensation doubled from $8.7M to $17.5M on "improved financial performance metrics and increased mark to market charges." Some of this reverses with the share price; some is structural cost added at the top of a cycle.

**Segment divergence is the most important disclosure in the file.** H1 Proprietary operating profit +37.2% to $401.1M on 16.5% organic growth. West Vantage operating profit −8.9% to $28.5M on 3.4% organic growth (Q2: +0.8%), with gross margin at 14.8% versus 16.8% a year earlier. Management attributes the Q2 deterioration to cyber downtime plus inflationary plant spend, but the erosion predates the attack: FY2024 17.5% → FY2025 16.5%, with FY2025 segment operating profit down 12.3% before any cyber effect. Essentially the entire equity story is Proprietary Products; West Vantage is an 18%-of-revenue margin drag whose strategic rationale (device assembly pulling through proprietary components) is asserted but not yet visible in its own economics.

**Earnings.** FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS $6.79 (adjusted $7.29) versus FY2024 $6.69/$6.75 and FY2023 $7.88/$8.08. GAAP net income was flat at $493.7M against 6.3% revenue growth: the $105.5M gross-profit gain was absorbed by $55.1M more SG&A, $30.2M more other expense, and a tax rate of 20.2% versus 18.4% (a smaller stock-comp benefit, $4.5M vs $19.5M). H1 2026 GAAP diluted EPS $4.07, adjusted $4.50, the latter +36.8%.

The beat record is consistent: four consecutive quarters, +15.4%, +10.2%, +25.8% and +12.8% on adjusted EPS. That supports confidence in the FY2026 guide, but current-year consensus of $8.96 sits at the guidance midpoint of $8.95, so there is no cushion between the street and the company.

**Cash flow is the clear blemish.** FY2025 was high quality: OCF $754.8M against $493.7M net income (1.53x), capex $285.9M, FCF $468.9M, 15.3% FCF margin. H1 2026: OCF $213.9M (down $92.6M) against net income of $292.8M, a 0.73x ratio, with capex $85.9M and FCF $128.0M versus $160.0M, a 20% decline while adjusted EPS grew 37%. "Changes in assets and liabilities" consumed $188.9M versus $4.0M. Receivables rose $137.6M to $712.0M; accrued salaries fell $38.8M as FY2025 incentives were paid.

Most of this is the mirror image of FY2025, whose OCF improvement management itself attributed partly to incentive-payment timing. But derived DSO extended from 68.2 days at 12/31/25 to 75.7 days at 6/30/26, roughly 7.5 days, more than a pure run-rate effect explains, and worth watching against a customer base where one account is 15.8% of sales. I have not annualized H1 FCF; doing so would embed a non-recurring incentive swing and ignore the ~$136M of SmartDose proceeds received July 1 (a Q3 investing inflow). H2 2026 conversion is the test.

**Balance sheet.** At 6/30/26: cash $435.8M, total debt $202.9M, equity $2,990.2M. Net cash of $232.9M. A standardized screen reporting "debt/equity 0.34" is measuring total liabilities to equity; actual debt-to-equity is 0.064. Debt is a $130.0M term loan at 5.08% due July 2, 2027 and $73.0M of Series C notes at 4.02% due July 5, 2027, a technical maturity concentration, immaterial against $435.8M of cash plus $497.7M undrawn on a $500M revolver, with covenant compliance confirmed. Goodwill is only $108.7M and net intangibles $6.4M against $4,076.4M of assets: an organically built manufacturer with effectively no impairment overhang. Net PP&E of $1,686.3M fell $40.1M in H1 despite $85.9M of capex, reflecting $91.4M of D&A, FX, and the $42.1M reclassification into held-for-sale, not a shrinking asset base.

**Capital allocation.** Capex has fallen from ~13% of sales in FY2024 to 9.3% in FY2025 to roughly 5.0% annualized in H1 2026, with capitalized interest collapsing from $14.6M (FY2025) to $3.0M (H1 2026), corroborating project completion rather than deferral. Buybacks accelerated: H1 2026 repurchases of 1,760,610 shares for $454.3M at an average $258.03, with $545.7M left of the February 2026 $1.0B authorization. Monthly Q2 averages escalated: $269.93 April, $309.50 May, $328.13 June. H1 buybacks plus $31.5M of dividends totalled $485.8M against $292.8M of net income and $128.0M of FCF, drawing cash from $791.3M to $435.8M and reducing equity despite positive earnings. Diluted shares fell from 75.3M (FY2023) to 71.3M (Q2 2026), a 5.3% reduction. The dividend ($0.22/quarter, ~0.25% yield, under 10% of guided EPS) is not part of the return case.

The buyback deserves a fair reading in both directions. The $258.03 H1 average is 26% below today's price, so the capital was well deployed in hindsight. Against that, purchases at $328 in June against a 38.8x forward multiple, funded partly from the cash balance rather than from FCF, look more like the residual use of cash (capex cut, no disclosed M&A pipeline) than a valuation judgment.

## Growth Analysis

The organic inflection is real and mechanically explicable. Three forces stack:

1. **Mix.** HVP components moved 46% → 48% of sales and HVP delivery devices 13% → 15% between H1 2025 and H1 2026, while Standard Packaging fell 22% → 19%. Biologics rose to 42% of sales; Pharma fell to 24% and Generics to 16%. HVP carries structurally higher margin, so mix alone lifts consolidated gross margin without any volume leverage.
2. **Absorption.** The 2022-2024 capacity build is complete and now spreads fixed cost over a larger revenue base; MD&A cites "higher plant absorption" directly, and the capitalized-interest decline confirms projects closing out.
3. **Restructuring.** The January 2025 plan targets $35-40M of annualized savings against $28-30M of charges; the December 2025 SmartDose-related plan targets $15-20M against $12-16M. Combined, $50-60M, or 1.6-2.0% of FY2025 revenue.

Against that, the forward growth rate is lower than H1 suggests, and consensus knows it. FY2027 consensus of $9.96 implies 11.3% adjusted EPS growth on FY2026's $8.95, respectable but a third of H1's pace. The identifiable drags on the second half are the SmartDose 3.5mL divestiture, the Q2 pull-forward that management explicitly flagged as the primary driver of the +29.2% HVP delivery-device number, tougher comparisons, and a fading FX tailwind. Underlying organic momentum is almost certainly better than +2%, but an investor extrapolating +17% into 2027 is extrapolating a number that contains 3.4 points of FX and a pull-forward of a divested product.

Estimate revisions are positive but not of inflection magnitude: current year +4.0% over 30 days and +3.9% over 90; next year +4.3% over both windows. This confirms the guidance raise; it is not evidence that consensus is systematically underestimating an earnings step-change. The revision velocity that would justify overriding a premium multiple would require roughly an order of magnitude more, and it is not present here.

R&D at 2.4% of sales is the quiet constraint on the durability of the mix shift. West's growth comes from customers migrating to higher-specification components as they move to biologics and self-administration, which West facilitates; it does not come from a proprietary product pipeline of its own funding.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive set bifurcates sharply, and consolidated numbers obscure it.

In elastomers and primary containment, West competes with Datwyler and Aptar in what functions as an oligopoly, with qualification barriers enforced by drug regulators rather than by patents. Here West earns 42.6% gross margins and took 3.7 points of price in a period when it also cited plant-cost inflation. That is the definition of pricing power in a components business.

In device manufacturing and assembly (West Vantage), the 10-K concedes a "very competitive" market where customers "look to reduce costs by sourcing from low-cost locations," against Phillips Medisize, SMC, Sharp and PCI. West earns 14.8% gross margins there, down from 17.5% two years earlier, with Q2 organic growth of +0.8%.

Structural demand drivers are visible in the mix data: biologics penetration, home self-administration driving HVP delivery devices, and the GLP-1 build-out. Named countervailing forces: pharmaceutical consolidation concentrating purchasing power, group purchasing organizations, customer in-sourcing (the 10-K notes that some potential customers compete with West), and needle-free injection cited explicitly as substitution risk.

One competitive risk was materially resolved. The FY2025 10-K flagged that Crystal Zenith, FluroTec and B2-coating are licensed from Daikyo (49%-owned) under agreements expiring in 2027. On July 14, 2026 the parties executed an amended crosslicense plus two distributorship agreements, each with a ten-year term. That removes a genuine 2027 discontinuity on technologies West sells but does not own. Economic terms are unread, so a renewal on worse royalty terms would only surface later in gross margin.

Trajectory assessment: improving in the 81% of revenue that matters, deteriorating in the 18% that does not, with the moat mechanism intact but explicitly conditional on regulatory equivalency standards remaining burdensome. A 6.8 rather than a higher score reflects that bifurcation and the fact that the moat's foundation is a regulatory regime West does not control.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration, direction unknown.** The FY2025 10-K discloses top-ten customers at 47.6% of sales and one customer at 15.8% ($485.9M), buying from both segments. West does not name it, and I have no comparable prior-year figure, so concentration may be rising, falling or flat. The Biologics and HVP mix shift does not address this; product mix and customer mix are different measures, and it would be an error to read the former as diversification.

**Oral GLP-1 substitution, named by management.** The risk factors state that if customers "opt for products delivered via alternative means, such as oral GLP-1s," or "reconfigure their drug product or develop new drug products requiring less frequent dosing," sales and profitability may suffer. The dosing-frequency point is the subtle one: West's revenue scales with injections, so monthly dosing replacing weekly is a volume headwind even if injectable delivery persists. Reporting from the Q2 call put GLP-1 elastomers at ~10% of revenue, but that figure is single-sourced to a tier-3 aggregator and is not in any filing; it should not be treated as established.

**Realized cyber incident with unquantified residual liability.** The only risk factor modified in the Q2 10-Q discloses that in May 2026 West suffered "a material cybersecurity attack in which certain data was exfiltrated by an unauthorized party and certain systems were encrypted," with systems taken offline globally for containment, law enforcement notified, and operations "fully recovered." Quantified: ~$7M of lost West Vantage revenue, a 3.3pp segment gross-margin hit, and $7.5M of H1 professional fees bundled with unrelated legal matters. Item 1.05 8-Ks were filed May 11 and May 20. "Contained" describes operations, not liability: notification, customer remediation and litigation exposure are unquantified and unaccrued, and management states insurance limits may be insufficient. The incident also demonstrates operational tail risk in a footprint where "the manufacturing of certain product lines is concentrated in one or only a few of our plants."

**Securities class action pleading insider trading.** Filed May 5, 2025 in E.D. Pa. on behalf of purchasers from February 16, 2023 through February 12, 2025, alleging §§10(b), 20(a) and **20A** violations in connection with public statements and "certain current and former officers' transactions in the Company's stock." Amended October 15, 2025; motion to dismiss fully briefed as of March 31, 2026 and undecided. No accrual; the company states it cannot estimate a loss or judge probability. I assign no loss estimate, but an undecided MTD on a §20A claim against officers is a binary that a 38.8x multiple does not accommodate.

**West Vantage.** 18% of revenue with gross margin down 270bp over two years, operating profit declining in both FY2025 and H1 2026, and Q2 organic growth of +0.8%. The cyber outage explains part of Q2; it does not explain FY2024→FY2025.

**Governance cadence and CEO transition.** Ten Item 5.02 filings in 21 months. Eric Green, CEO and chair for eleven years, announced retirement March 9, 2026 (eight days after receiving a full annual grant of 6,986 RSUs and 16,630 options; treatment on departure is not determinable from the Form 4s). Michel Lagarde, from Thermo Fisher, starts August 31. The proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows a WST TSR index of 59 at 2025 against a peer index of 118, roughly 59 index points of relative underperformance over the covered window. Average NEO compensation jumped 117% to $3.80M, producing an anomalous 1.9x CEO-to-NEO ratio; new-hire sign-on equity amid heavy 2025 turnover is the likely explanation, but I did not confirm it. On the positive side, PSU metrics include ROIC alongside revenue growth, the right measure for a company exiting a heavy capex cycle, and "compensation actually paid" has swung with the share price, indicating genuine equity leverage.

**Insider activity: no signal either way.** Across 30 Form 4 entries from February to June 2026 there were **no open-market purchases** by any officer or director. Two small exercise-and-sell transactions occurred (CHRO Favorite, 2,817 shares at $305.20, ~$860K, retaining 16,828 shares; CAO Winters, 896 shares at $300.67, ~$269K). Everything else is routine grants, code-F tax withholding and identical 791-share director grants. Favorite adopted a 10b5-1 plan June 2, 2026 for up to 15,659 shares, effective from September 8. Green retains ~172,900 shares. Given the §20A allegations covering 2023-2025, the absence of large discretionary selling in 2026 is relevant; but exercise-and-sell at $300-305 is not a bullish tell, and the complete absence of buying during a 47% twelve-month advance is the reason Management Quality sits at 5.2 rather than higher.

**Tax restructuring.** A multi-year legal-entity optimization produced $4.6M of costs in 2024, $4.9M plus $4.9M of tax charges in 2025, and a $12.0M one-time tax cost in Q1 2026. Unrecognized tax benefits rose from $56.1M to $67.5M in six months. I record this as fact without inferring motive; the disclosed rationale is plausible.

**Accounting distortion ahead.** The SmartDose 3.5mL sale closed July 1 for ~$136M, with an expected pre-tax gain of $55-60M in Q3, roughly $0.81/share pre-tax and ~$0.65 after tax on 71.3M shares, which adjusted EPS will exclude. Comparing Q3 GAAP EPS to non-GAAP expectations will manufacture a spurious beat. West also retains the SmartDose contingent-consideration liability ($3.3M) and recorded $6.2M of related expense in H1, so this is a variant divestiture, not a platform exit.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($440, +26.9%).** The mix shift is structural: biologics and self-administration keep pulling customers up the HVP curve, and West prices that migration. Gross margin continues past 38% as absorption improves on a completed asset base, restructuring delivers $50-60M of annualized savings, and West Vantage recovers toward 17% gross margin as its own capacity build absorbs (H1 2025 segment capex was $43.6M versus $10.7M in H1 2026, so under-absorption is a real explanation for part of the erosion). H2 2026 cash conversion normalizes, restoring the FY2025 pattern of OCF at 1.5x net income and FCF near $470M and rising. The incoming CEO keeps capex near 6-7% of sales and continues repurchasing against a net-cash balance sheet, compounding the 5.3% share reduction already achieved. FY2027 EPS reaches $10.50 and the market pays 42x for a regulator-protected franchise with 15%+ ROE, no goodwill and net cash.

**Bear case ($248, −28.5%).** H2 2026 delivers the +1-3% reported revenue growth the guidance implies, and the market reads it as the run-rate rather than as divestiture-plus-FX arithmetic. The DSO extension proves to be collection slippage at concentrated customers rather than timing, and FY2026 FCF lands near $300M against a 1.9% starting yield, which the multiple cannot survive. Oral GLP-1 uptake accelerates and a large customer signals reduced injectable volumes or lower dosing frequency; because West's revenue scales with injections, that hits volume before it hits headlines. The class-action MTD is denied and discovery on officer stock sales begins. Cyber remediation and notification costs surface with no insurance offset. The new CEO resets guidance, steps up capex to fund capacity he judges insufficient at 5% of sales, or pursues a sizeable acquisition into a business with no goodwill history. At 27x a trimmed $9.20, the stock returns to the January 2026 area.

**Base case ($370, +6.7%).** Proprietary Products keeps compounding at high single digits organically with modest further margin gain; West Vantage stabilizes without recovering; H2 cash conversion partially normalizes; FY2027 EPS lands near consensus $9.96; the multiple holds at 37x. That is a fine business outcome and a mediocre equity outcome, because the multiple already discounts it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive (10-11% organic growth, margin recovery, $50-60M of restructuring savings, net cash, and a shrinking share count), but base-case upside of 6.7% against 28.5% bear-case downside does not justify new capital at $346.84.

## Conclusion

West Pharmaceutical is one of the better-quality manufacturing franchises in health care supplies: a regulator-enforced switching cost, 42.6% gross margins in the segment that produces 81% of revenue, demonstrated pricing power worth 3.7 points of organic growth, net cash of $232.9M, effectively no goodwill, and a 5.3% reduction in diluted shares since FY2023. The FY2026 inflection is genuine and mechanically explained by mix, absorption of a completed capital program, and restructuring.

The constraint is price and the composition of what has been delivered. At $346.84 the stock is 38.8x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS and 34.8x FY2027 consensus, a PEG above 3, on a 1.92% FCF yield. H1's +17.3% revenue growth contains 3.4 points of FX and a pull-forward of a product line now owned by AbbVie; the guidance implies +1.1% to +3.3% reported H2 revenue growth and +9% to +14% adjusted EPS growth. Free cash flow fell 20% while adjusted EPS grew 37%, and buybacks at rising prices exceeded both earnings and FCF, drawing the cash balance down $355M. Three unresolved binaries — exfiltrated-data liability, an undecided motion to dismiss on a §20A insider-trading claim, and an external CEO arriving August 31 into an 18%-of-revenue segment with three consecutive periods of margin erosion — are not compensated at this multiple.

The score declines modestly to 5.9 from 6.2, and this is the third consecutive HOLD. The two clearest new positives since the last review (a beat-and-raise, and the ten-year Daikyo renewal removing the 2027 license cliff) are offset by two new negatives of similar weight: the H1 cash-conversion deterioration with a 7.5-day DSO extension, and the H2 deceleration the guidance headline conceals. It is not a downgrade because the franchise economics are improving where it matters and the balance sheet carries net cash. Own it if you own it. Add at $280-290, or on evidence that H2 cash conversion reverts to the FY2025 pattern.
