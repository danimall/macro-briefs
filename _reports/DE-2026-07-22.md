**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Deere is trading through the bottom of a severe agricultural downcycle. Consolidated revenue has fallen roughly 25% from peak (from ~$61.3B in FY2023 to ~$45.7B in FY2025) and net income has halved from ~$10.2B to ~$5.0B over the same span. The Q2 FY2026 print (reported May 21, 2026) confirmed the trough thesis rather than resolving it: total sales and revenues rose 5% to $13.4B and diluted EPS came in at $6.55, beating consensus for a fourth consecutive quarter, but the composition mattered more than the headline. Production & Precision Ag (large row-crop equipment, the highest-margin segment) fell 14% with operating profit dropping from $1,148M to $706M, while Construction & Forestry rose 29% (order book up >60% since November, partly on data-center construction) and Small Ag & Turf rose 16%. The stock fell about 7% on the beat because the market fixated on the large-ag guidance (U.S./Canada large-ag units down 15–20% for FY2026).

The bull case is straightforward: FY2026 net income of $4.5–5.0B represents a trough, the used-equipment overhang is clearing (inventory down >50% from the 2024 peak), and construction plus small ag are already recovering while large ag has yet to turn. Management reinforces the trough read by targeting a dividend payout of 25–35% of mid-cycle (not trailing) earnings, an explicit statement that $5B is not the run-rate. The IEEPA tariff invalidation and accepted ~$272M CBP refund claim remove a material portion of the ~$600M FY2025 tariff drag.

The problem for the equity is price. At $586.88, DE trades at roughly 32x trailing and ~32x forward earnings, back within striking distance of its $674 52-week high, with consensus estimates flat-to-negative over 90 days (current-quarter EPS revised down 7.1%). The precision-technology SaaS engine meant to structurally lift through-cycle margins is, by management's own disclosure, adopting "slower than expected," and Leap Ambitions targets were refined downward in December 2025. The trough thesis is intact and worth holding through the cycle, but the valuation already prices the recovery. We maintain HOLD with a growth-hold rationale.

## Company Overview

Deere is the world's leading agricultural-equipment manufacturer, operating one of the most defensible brand franchises in industrials. The business runs through four reportable segments:

- **Production & Precision Ag (PPA)** — high-horsepower tractors, combines, and the precision-tech stack. The largest and highest-margin segment (~$17.3B FY2025 sales), also the most cyclical.
- **Small Ag & Turf (SAT)** — smaller tractors, dairy/livestock, turf and compact utility equipment (~$10.2B FY2025 sales).
- **Construction & Forestry (CF)** — earthmoving, roadbuilding (Wirtgen), compact construction, forestry (~$11.4B FY2025 sales).
- **Financial Services (John Deere Financial)** — the captive finance arm funding equipment sales and dealer inventory (~$890M FY2025 net income).

The competitive moat rests on three pillars: the John Deere brand and its entrenched independent dealer network; the captive finance arm that supports sales across the cycle; and a differentiating precision-agriculture technology stack (Operations Center with ~440,000 monthly active users, See & Spray targeted spraying scaled to ~5M acres, Precision Essentials with >90% second-year renewal, JDLink connectivity). The strategic thesis (the "Smart Industrial Operating Model" and "Leap Ambitions") is to shift from selling hardware toward recurring software/SaaS-linked revenue that would raise switching costs and dampen cyclicality.

## Financial Analysis

The revenue trajectory tracks the downcycle: $61.3B (FY2023) → $51.7B (FY2024) → $45.7B (FY2025). Operating Return on Operating Assets, the company-selected performance measure in the proxy, fell from a peak 52.5% (FY2023) to 21.7% (FY2025).

Q2 FY2026 (ended May 3, 2026) showed the bottoming with a sharp intra-segment divergence:

- **Total net sales & revenues +5%** to $13.4B; **diluted EPS $6.55** vs. $6.64 prior year, roughly flat; fourth consecutive beat (11.5% surprise).
- **PPA: sales −14%** to $4.5B, operating profit $1,148M → $706M. Large ag still falling.
- **SAT: sales +16%** to $3.49B, operating profit $574M → $719M. Recovering.
- **CF: sales +29%** to $3.79B, operating profit $379M → $561M. The standout, order book up >60% since November, >80% of production slots filled, driven partly by data-center construction (projected >$100B in 2026).

FY2026 guidance: net income $4.5–5.0B; equipment-operations operating cash flow $4.5–5.5B; capex ~$1.4B. Segment margin guidance is PPA 11–13% (down from 15.4%), SAT 13.5–15%, CF 10–12%, with Financial Services net income ~$860M.

On the balance sheet, headline leverage looks alarming (D/E around 3.1x and an Altman Z-Score near 1.0 nominally in "distress" territory) but this is an artifact of consolidating John Deere Financial's receivables/loan book funded by debt. The mechanism is not operational distress; Deere runs the captive finance arm with matched asset/liability interest-rate profiles and is committed to maintaining an "A" rating. The genuine risk here is credit quality of the finance book, not solvency (see Risk Assessment).

Capital allocation is deliberately conservative through the trough: quarterly dividend of $1.62 with a stated 25–35% payout of mid-cycle earnings, plus ~$500M repurchased in H1 FY2026. Cash-flow guidance comfortably covers the dividend and modest buyback.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has two distinct components with different durability.

The near-term component is cyclical recovery. Used high-horsepower inventory is down >50% from the 2024 peak, a necessary precondition for a new-equipment demand recovery. European ag retail is up double digits per the earnings call; South America remains weak (down ~15%). Large ag (PPA) is guided down 15–20% for FY2026, so the segment that drives the most operating leverage has not yet turned. The recovery is real but staged: CF and SAT lead, PPA lags. Consensus reflects this — current-year EPS of $18.11 rising modestly (+1.2% over 90 days) but next-year at $22.85 (a ~26% implied recovery) with slightly negative recent revisions (−0.8% over 90 days).

The structural component, precision-ag SaaS monetization, is the more important lever and the one running behind schedule. The 10-K discloses "slower than expected customer adoption of some of our precision technology solutions and SaaS subscription services," and that building SaaS infrastructure "has delayed the timing of realization of the expected benefits." Management adjusted its Leap Ambitions targets in December 2025. There is genuine counter-evidence — Precision Essentials at >90% second-year renewal, See & Spray at ~5M acres with 50–60% herbicide savings, JDLink Boost kits up 25% last quarter — but the engine intended to de-cyclicalize the franchise is lumpier than the bull case requires.

A less-expected growth vector is the data-center construction tailwind flowing through CF. This is not agricultural demand, and it diversifies Deere's near-term earnings base, consistent with the broad data-center infrastructure buildout. Whether CF strength persists depends on the durability of the AI-capex cycle rather than farm income.

Estimate revisions do not clear the +30%/90-day threshold that would flag a strong inflection buy signal — current-year revisions are modestly positive but current-quarter revisions turned negative. This is a staged cyclical recovery being priced roughly in line with fundamentals, not a mispriced trough.

## Valuation Assessment

At $586.88, DE trades at ~32x trailing EPS ($18.50) and ~32x forward on consensus current-year EPS ($18.11). Against the ag-machinery peer set — CNH at 33.4x, TTC at 26.7x, AGCO at 10.7x, LNN at 21.0x, with a peer median of 26.7x — Deere sits above the median on both a trailing and forward basis. The premium to AGCO is defensible on Deere's superior franchise, precision-tech optionality, and captive finance; the premium to the median as a whole is harder to justify at a cycle where the highest-margin segment is still contracting.

The cycle-adjusted lens is the right frame given the trough. On next-year consensus of $22.85, the stock trades at ~25.7x, not cheap for a business whose structural growth engine is behind schedule. Even on a normalized mid-cycle earnings assumption of $22–24, the multiple sits in the mid-20s. That is a full multiple for a cyclical industrial at trough earnings, and it reflects the market's confidence that FY2026 is the bottom and a V-shaped recovery follows. The risk is that the recovery is U-shaped (large ag bottoming later than guided), in which case the forward multiple looks expensive against slower earnings growth.

Analyst targets span a wide range: current-price marks near $607, mean $648, median $640, with a high of $812 (RBC's $752 Outperform sits in this cohort) and a low of $500 (Jefferies at $550). The dispersion captures the cycle debate. Because the stock is not cheap relative to its growth (forward P/E of ~32x against a staged recovery, PEG well above 1.5 on this year's flat trajectory), the guidance principle that analyst targets act as floors on cheap inflection stocks does not apply here — the targets reflect the same cycle extrapolation as the price.

We do not treat the valuation as a value trap, but neither does it offer asymmetric upside. The multiple is stretched relative to the near-term earnings the company will actually deliver in FY2026, and the re-rating case depends on SaaS monetization that management concedes is delayed. A valuation score of 4.5 reflects the balanced-to-slightly-unfavorable setup: cheapness is not the reason to own this, and history shows a low-multiple sort is not a reliable buy trigger in any case.

## Competitive Landscape

The recurring analyst frame of Caterpillar versus Deere as opposite cycle stories captures the dynamic. Caterpillar has ridden energy, construction, and data-center demand to cycle peaks; Deere's larger ag exposure has kept it in a trough. This explains Deere's slight 5-year TSR underperformance (218 vs. peer group 222 per the proxy).

Within agriculture, Deere's differentiation is durable: the integrated precision-tech + captive-finance + dealer ecosystem raises switching costs against CNH and AGCO. The Operations Center's ~440,000 monthly active users and >90% Precision Essentials renewal rates are evidence of a widening moat if the SaaS conversion executes. The competitive position is stable-to-improving on the technology dimension, though the trajectory has softened relative to earlier expectations — the prior competitive-position scores of 8.5 have moderated to reflect the disclosed adoption slippage and the reality that the SaaS thesis is not yet converting to the recurring-revenue mix management targeted. A 6.8 acknowledges a strong, defensible franchise whose competitive advance is running slower than the bull case assumed.

The right-to-repair litigation (MDL antitrust class action plus the FTC and five state AGs) is a competitive-model overhang: an adverse outcome could force Deere to open its repair ecosystem, eroding a switching-cost lever and dealer-service economics. This is the competitive risk with the most potential to reshape the moat.

## Risk Assessment

**Agricultural cycle / farm income** is the dominant risk. FY2025 saw lower volumes, heavier reliance on sales incentives, and elevated receivable write-offs; management expects "certain of these conditions to persist" into FY2026. If large ag does not bottom on the guided timeline, the earnings trough extends.

**Financial Services credit deterioration** is the quiet risk that compounds a prolonged downturn. The allowance for credit losses on retail notes and leases increased in 2025 on higher expected losses and elevated ag/turf delinquencies. Because the finance book is levered against equipment collateral, a longer trough hits Deere through credit losses and residual-value writedowns beyond just volume.

**Tariffs / trade** were a ~$600M FY2025 drag. The IEEPA invalidation and accepted refund claim materially de-risk this (H1 FY2026 net tariff impact was ~$372M) but Deere remains a net U.S. exporter exposed to both input and retaliatory tariffs, plus rare-earth dependence on China for motors and batteries.

**Right-to-repair litigation** is a material, brand-sensitive regulatory overhang with no clear resolution date and potential to force business-model changes.

**Precision-tech adoption** is the growth thesis's soft spot: the engine meant to de-cyclicalize Deere is disclosed as adopting slower than planned.

Most of these risks are known and at least partially priced. The idiosyncratic tail that is less priced is the right-to-repair outcome and the finance-book credit quality if the ag trough extends. A risk score of 5.5 reflects that the cycle risk is well understood but the credit-quality and litigation tails carry asymmetric downside not fully captured at the current forward multiple.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 37.6% sits rich versus 1-year historical vol of 30.7% (a 7.0pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (37.6% near vs. 31.7% long-term), signaling the market is pricing imminent stress into the August 20 Q3 print. The put/call skew is balanced (−1.0%) and put/call volume ratio is low (0.36), so positioning is not directionally bearish. IV has collapsed from a prior 95.0% reading, indicating the earlier event-uncertainty spike has resolved. The implied ±2.2% near-term move is modest. Net read: the options market prices ordinary earnings-event uncertainty ahead of Q3, not directional conviction. This neither confirms nor challenges the fundamental HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($700–812).** FY2026 marks the definitive cycle bottom. Used inventory clearing, European ag recovering double digits, and CF's data-center-driven order book pull consolidated earnings sharply higher into FY2027, toward the $22.85 consensus and beyond. Precision-ag SaaS renewals hold above 90% and See & Spray scaling accelerates, structurally lifting through-cycle margins and compressing the equity's cyclicality discount. The IEEPA refund and lower tariff drag add a clean earnings tailwind. On $24–26 normalized EPS at a 30x franchise multiple, the stock re-rates toward the RBC $752 case and higher.

**Bear Case ($480–500).** Large ag fails to bottom on the guided timeline; farm income stays depressed on low commodity prices and high financing costs. Financial Services delinquencies and residual-value losses accelerate, compounding the volume hit. Precision-tech adoption continues to disappoint, removing the de-cyclicalization catalyst and leaving Deere valued as a peak-multiple cyclical at trough earnings. Right-to-repair rulings force model changes. The forward multiple compresses toward the peer median on flat earnings, taking the stock to the $500 analyst low or below.

The base case sits between these: a staged recovery led by CF and SAT with PPA turning in FY2027, consensus roughly holding, and the multiple staying full but not expanding. That yields a fair value near current levels; the recovery is largely priced.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The cycle-trough thesis is credible and the franchise is high quality, but at the current forward multiple with the SaaS engine behind schedule and estimates flat-to-negative, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- PPA (large ag) unit outlook stabilizes or turns positive in the Q3 (Aug 20, 2026) or Q4 print, confirming the trough — the segment carrying the most operating leverage.
- Next-year (FY2027) EPS revisions turn decisively positive (>+5% over 90 days), signaling the recovery is being underestimated.
- A pullback toward the low-$500s (near the Jefferies/analyst-low target), which would restore forward-P/E-to-growth asymmetry.
- Precision-tech recurring-revenue metrics accelerate (SaaS attach rates, Operations Center MAU growth) with management re-raising Leap Ambitions targets.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Financial Services delinquencies and allowance for credit losses accelerate materially, indicating a prolonged rather than V-shaped downturn.
- FY2026 net income guidance cut below the $4.5B floor.
- An adverse right-to-repair ruling (MDL or FTC action) that forces model changes.
- Large-ag unit declines steepen beyond the guided 15–20% into FY2027.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 FY2026 earnings on August 20, 2026 is the next checkpoint; it will test whether large ag is genuinely bottoming and whether CF's data-center-driven strength persists.

## Conclusion

Deere is a best-in-class cyclical industrial passing through the bottom of a sharp ag downcycle, with a four-segment divergence that a headline read misses: construction/forestry and small ag/turf are recovering while large ag still falls. The fourth consecutive earnings beat, the IEEPA tariff relief, the clearing used-inventory overhang, and the mid-cycle dividend policy all support the trough thesis. But at ~32x trailing / ~32x forward, back near its 52-week high, with consensus estimates flat-to-negative and the precision-tech SaaS engine running behind schedule, the equity already prices the recovery. The score trajectory has drifted lower (6.5 → 5.6 over five months) on softening growth, management, and competitive dimensions, and the current data confirms rather than reverses that drift — no fundamental deterioration, but no fresh catalyst to justify the full multiple either. Own the franchise through the cycle; wait for either a large-ag confirmation or a pullback toward the low-$500s before adding. **HOLD.**

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: forward P/E was overstated as cheaper than reality — at consensus current-year EPS of $18.11 the forward multiple is ~32x (not ~27x), matching the ~32x trailing multiple, so the equity is even less attractive on valuation than the report's HOLD rationale implied.
