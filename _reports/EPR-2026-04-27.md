## Executive Summary

EPR Properties is a $4.2B specialty REIT exclusively focused on experiential net lease real estate — theatres, golf entertainment, attractions, fitness, and ski properties. The company trades at ~10.8x trailing FFOAA ($5.12/share) with a 6.4% dividend yield, above the peer median yield of 4.8%, reflecting a risk discount. The discount is warranted.

Three overlapping risks define the investment case: (1) Topgolf, EPR's single largest tenant at 14.2% of revenue, is deteriorating — Callaway sold its majority stake at a ~45% valuation haircut, same-store sales declined 12% in Q1 2025, and management turned over; (2) theatres remain 148 of EPR's properties despite stated intent to reduce exposure, with ~$119M in impairments over 2023-2024 and a stagnant box office (+1% in 2025); and (3) the company faces $630M in 2026 debt maturities during a period of elevated rates. The recently closed $342M Six Flags park acquisition adds execution risk with an unproven operator (Enchanted Parks) that must rebrand six parks by end of 2026.

While the dividend is well-covered (68.8% FFOAA payout ratio) and the balance sheet is adequate at 5.0x net debt/EBITDA, the combination of tenant concentration, secular headwinds, and refinancing risk creates a risk profile that the ~10.8x multiple does not sufficiently discount. Analyst consensus is overwhelmingly HOLD (8 of 12 analysts), with no meaningful upward estimate revisions, and 10.4% short interest signals institutional skepticism.

## Company Overview

EPR Properties was formed in 1997 and operates as a self-administered Maryland REIT with 54 employees. The company's $7.0B investment portfolio spans 278+ experiential properties across 43 states and Canada, primarily under triple-net leases where tenants bear operating costs. Revenue sources include minimum rent ($531M), percentage rent ($51M), straight-line rent adjustments ($36M), and mortgage/financing income ($68M).

The portfolio breaks down as: 148 theatre properties, 60 eat & play venues (including Topgolf), 26 attraction properties, 27 fitness & wellness facilities, 11 ski properties, and smaller allocations to lodging, gaming, and cultural venues. A legacy education segment ($0.4B, 6% of investments) is being wound down through dispositions.

EPR's strategic direction involves three simultaneous transitions: reducing theatre exposure, exiting education, and growing into attractions and fitness/wellness. The April 2026 acquisition of six former Six Flags parks for $315-342M represents the largest single execution of this strategy.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue** grew 2.9% in 2025 to $718.4M, driven by $16.4M in minimum rent increases from new acquisitions and 1.6% lease renewal rate escalators, plus $7.5M in percentage rent gains from theatre tenants. This growth rate is modest for a REIT deploying capital at EPR's pace.

**FFOAA per diluted share** recovered to $5.12 from $4.87 in 2024, a 5.1% increase, but still below 2023's $5.18. The 2025 improvement was partially driven by absence of the $51.8M in impairments that burdened 2024. Stripping out impairment volatility, the underlying growth trajectory is roughly 2-3% annually.

**Operating cash flow** of $421M (2025) improved 7.1% from $393M (2024). Against this, maintenance capex was only $5.2M (reflecting the triple-net structure), producing approximately $416M in maintenance free cash flow. The dividend consumed ~$269M ($3.52 × 76.5M shares), leaving ~$147M for investment and debt reduction — a healthy retained cash flow profile.

**Balance sheet leverage** at 5.0x net debt/adjusted EBITDAre (Q4 2025, down from 5.3x) is acceptable for a net lease REIT, though on the higher end of the 4.0-5.5x range typical for investment-grade peers. Total debt stands at $2.93B, 99% unsecured.

**The critical near-term issue is $629.6M in 2026 debt maturities.** This includes $179.6M in Series B private placement notes (August 2026) and approximately $450M in other obligations. The company has a $1.0B undrawn revolver and a $400M ATM equity program, providing liquidity, but refinancing $630M at current rates (4.5-5.5%) versus the weighted average of ~4.32% will incrementally pressure interest expense by $10-15M annually, directly reducing FFOAA by $0.13-0.20/share.

**Earnings quality indicators:** AFFO of $398M vs. net income of $275M shows a positive gap, consistent with a REIT where depreciation exceeds maintenance capex. Straight-line rent adjustments ($36M) and below-market lease amortization create some GAAP revenue that does not correspond to cash collection — a standard REIT feature, not a red flag, but it inflates reported revenue relative to cash collections.

## Growth Analysis

EPR's organic growth is constrained by three factors:

**Lease escalators average 1.5-2.0% annually.** This is the primary organic revenue growth mechanism for a triple-net REIT, and it barely keeps pace with current inflation levels. At 3.3%+ CPI, EPR's real revenue from existing leases is declining.

**External growth depends on acquisition volume and yields.** The company invested $288.5M in 2025 and has already exceeded 85% of its 2026 acquisition target (primarily the Six Flags deal). Acquisition yields in experiential real estate have compressed — EPR must achieve 7.5%+ initial yields to generate accretive returns after 4.75%+ cost of debt. The spread between cap rates and financing costs has narrowed materially since 2021.

**Portfolio recycling (theatre reduction, education exit) generates capital but not growth.** EPR disposed of $141.8M in properties in 2025. Selling lower-growth assets (theatres, education) and reinvesting in higher-growth assets (fitness, attractions) can improve portfolio quality, but the net effect on near-term FFOAA growth is approximately neutral because disposition proceeds must be reinvested at similar or better yields.

Estimate revisions confirm this tepid outlook: current-year (FY2026) estimates have declined 1.6% over 90 days to $2.99, and next-year estimates have declined 3.7% to $3.10. Only 2-3 analysts cover the company actively, providing limited consensus rigor, but the direction is negative. These estimates appear to be EPS rather than FFOAA — EPR's FFOAA of $5.12 in 2025 is substantially higher than reported EPS of $3.28 due to depreciation add-backs. Regardless, the revision trend is downward.

The Six Flags acquisition could add $25-35M in annual rent (assuming 7.5-8.5% cap rate on ~$342M), translating to roughly $0.33-0.46/share in incremental FFOAA. But this estimate depends entirely on Enchanted Parks executing as an operator — an entity with no public operating history running parks that must rebrand from Six Flags by end of 2026.

## Valuation Assessment

EPR trades at ~10.8x trailing FFOAA ($5.12), a meaningful discount to the peer median P/E of 23.0x. Direct comparison to peers like Lamar Advertising (P/E 23.4x, billboard monopolist) or GLPI (P/E 16.0x, gaming leases with regulated monopoly tenants) overstates the discount because EPR's tenant quality and industry durability are materially weaker.

The 6.4% dividend yield exceeds peer medians (~4.8%) and provides genuine income support. At a 68.8% FFOAA payout ratio, the dividend is well-covered, and EPR has grown it ~3.5% annually. On a dividend discount basis, assuming 3% dividend growth and a 9% required return, fair value is approximately $58-60/share — close to current price, suggesting the income thesis is largely priced in.

The discount to peer multiples reflects real risk: theatre exposure (largest property count), concentrated tenants (top 3 = 39.3%), and a niche property type (experiential) that has limited alternative-use value if tenants default. A Topgolf venue or theatre that goes dark has few natural replacement tenants. This contrasts sharply with GLPI's casino properties (regulated scarcity) or LAMR's billboard locations (permanent infrastructure with de minimis capital needs).

If EPR successfully diversifies into attractions and fitness while reducing theatre exposure over 3-5 years, a re-rating toward 12-13x FFOAA ($62-67/share) is plausible. But the transition period involves executing the Six Flags integration, managing Topgolf risk, and refinancing debt — all simultaneously, in a consumer spending environment under pressure.

## Competitive Landscape

EPR occupies a unique niche as the only publicly traded REIT exclusively focused on experiential properties. This specialization creates genuine expertise in underwriting experiential assets — build-to-suit capabilities, operator relationships, and proprietary underwriting data accumulated over nearly 30 years. The recent hire of Ben Fox (CIO, previously at Realty Income overseeing ~7,000 properties) strengthens the acquisitions capability.

The competitive moat is narrow. EPR's assets are not scarce — experiential venues can be built new, and private equity competes aggressively for net lease deals. The company's advantage lies in willingness to structure flexible financing (both leases and mortgages) and accept property types that larger net lease REITs avoid. This flexibility cuts both ways: it provides deal flow but also results in a portfolio of assets with lower alternative-use value and higher re-tenanting risk than mainstream net lease properties.

EPR's competitive position is stable but not improving. Theatre exposure is declining slowly, while new categories (fitness, attractions) are growing. The portfolio transformation is directionally correct but multi-year in execution.

## Risk Assessment

### Tenant Concentration (Critical)
Top 3 tenants generate 39.3% of revenue: Topgolf (14.2%), AMC (13.6%), Regal (11.5%). AMC carries significant debt and was a bankruptcy candidate as recently as 2021. Regal (Cineworld) emerged from bankruptcy in 2023. Topgolf is transitioning ownership at a depressed valuation. A single tenant failure in this group would reduce revenue by 11-14% and force a dividend reduction.

### Topgolf Deterioration (High)
Same-store sales declined 12% in Q1 2025, and average customer visit frequency of 1.2x/year suggests the concept may face novelty fatigue. Callaway's sale of its Topgolf stake at a ~45% valuation haircut to Leonard Green signals distress. While EPR's triple-net lease structure provides contractual protection, lease modifications or deferrals become likely if venue economics deteriorate further. The new CEO's prior experience (Chuck E. Cheese bankruptcy restructuring) is not reassuring precedent.

### Refinancing Risk (Elevated)
Refinancing at 100-150bps above current weighted average cost will compress FFOAA by $0.13-0.20/share, a 2.5-4.0% hit to earnings.

### Consumer Spending Sensitivity (Elevated)
All experiential properties depend on consumer willingness to spend on discretionary leisure. EPR's tenants are directly exposed — theatre attendance, golf entertainment visits, and amusement park traffic are among the first categories consumers cut in a spending contraction.

### Six Flags Execution Risk (Moderate)
If Enchanted Parks underperforms, EPR owns purpose-built amusement parks with limited alternative uses.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 27.4% exceeds 1-year historical volatility of 22.6% by 4.8 percentage points, and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 27.4% > long-term 24.0%), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress. The 1-month put/call volume ratio of 1.29 confirms elevated near-term put demand. While the 6-month term structure normalizes, the near-term signal aligns with the fundamental risks of upcoming debt maturities and consumer spending weakness.

Short interest at 10.4% of float (5.8 days to cover) represents meaningful institutional bearish positioning — above the typical 3-5% for REITs and consistent with the concentrated tenant and theatre exposure risks.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($62-67)
EPR successfully executes the Six Flags integration, Topgolf stabilizes under new ownership/management, and consumer spending proves more resilient than expected. The portfolio transition toward attractions and fitness accelerates, driving a re-rating from ~10.8x to 12-13x FFOAA. Debt refinancing proceeds at acceptable rates. Dividend growth continues at 3-4%. At 13x a modestly growing FFOAA of $5.15-5.25, the stock reaches $67.

### Bear Case ($40-44)
Topgolf requests rent deferrals or restructuring. Theatre impairments resume. Consumer spending contraction reduces percentage rents and pressures tenant coverage ratios. Debt refinancing at 5.5%+ rates compresses FFOAA to $4.70-4.80. The dividend is cut or frozen. At 9x impaired FFOAA ($4.75), the stock trades to $43. At 8.5x, the stock reaches $40.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Topgolf lease restructuring or rent deferral announcement (timeline uncertain but probability elevated given same-store sales declines and ownership transition). Secondary catalyst: Q2 2026 earnings (late July/early August) revealing consumer spending impact on percentage rents and theatre performance. Tertiary catalyst: debt refinancing execution in H2 2026 potentially at unfavorable terms.

**Timing window:** 3-6 months. Q2 2026 earnings and debt refinancing both fall within this window. No single catalyst has a binary resolution date, making timing imprecise.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 10.4% short interest with 5.8 days to cover. Moderately crowded but not at squeeze levels. Institutional ownership is typical for a mid-cap REIT. The 6.4% dividend yield creates a ~6.4% annualized carry cost for shorts (must pay dividend), which is significant.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options market confirms bearish thesis — backwardated term structure, 1.29 put/call volume ratio near-term, IV rich to historical. Borrow should be available given mid-cap float, but dividend carry is expensive.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case $40-44 vs. current $55.55 = 21-28% downside. Bull case $62-67 = 12-21% upside. Reward:risk of roughly 1.3:1 to 1.5:1 after accounting for dividend carry — below the 2:1 threshold.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The bearish thesis has merit but lacks a specific timed catalyst with binary resolution, the 6.4% dividend carry is expensive for shorts, and the reward:risk ratio falls below 2:1. The correct expression is to avoid owning the stock, not to actively short it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:**
1. Topgolf stabilization: 2 consecutive quarters of positive same-store sales growth reported by Topgolf/Leonard Green, confirming the turnaround is gaining traction.
2. Tenant concentration reduction: top 3 tenants below 35% of revenue (from current 39.3%), demonstrating portfolio diversification progress.
3. Refinancing completion: 2026 maturities refinanced at weighted average rate below 5.25%, confirming credit market access at acceptable cost.
4. Valuation: price below $48 (approximately 9.4x trailing FFOAA) would provide sufficient margin of safety to compensate for tenant risk, offering a 7.3%+ dividend yield with coverage above 65%.

**Timeline:** Headwinds are expected to persist through at least Q4 2026. The Topgolf transition, Six Flags integration, and debt refinancing all resolve over a 6-12 month window. Earliest reassessment point: Q2 2026 earnings (July/August 2026).

## Conclusion

EPR Properties offers a 6.4% dividend yield on a portfolio of experiential real estate undergoing a multi-year strategic transition. The dividend is well-covered, the balance sheet is adequate, and the company's niche positioning is unique. However, three compounding risks — Topgolf deterioration, theatre structural headwinds, and 2026 debt maturities during a pressured consumer spending environment — create a risk profile that the current multiple does not sufficiently discount.

No identifiable catalyst exists to drive re-rating over the next 12 months. Estimate revisions are negative. Insider activity shows no open-market conviction buying, and one director liquidated their entire position in March 2026. The options market is pricing near-term stress via backwardation.

Income-oriented investors may find the yield compelling, but the probability of dividend disruption (via Topgolf restructuring, theatre impairments, or refinancing-driven FFOAA compression) is elevated relative to alternative income sources in the REIT sector with lower tenant concentration and more durable demand drivers.
