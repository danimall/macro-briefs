## Executive Summary

The dominant fact governing any position in CTRA is corporate rather than fundamental: Coterra Energy no longer trades as an independent security. Devon Energy and Coterra announced an all-stock merger on February 2, 2026, and the transaction closed in May 2026. The surviving entity operates under the Devon Energy name and DVN ticker, with headquarters in Houston. Standardized data feeds that still surface CTRA financials, an "earnings surprise" table, and an options snapshot are describing a going concern that has been absorbed. Anyone holding what was CTRA now owns Devon shares at the merger exchange ratio, and the correct analytical frame is a Devon thesis.

On a standalone basis through its final reporting period, Coterra was a financially healthy, cash-generative multi-basin E&P: FY2025 revenue of $7.29B (+34% YoY), net income of $1.72B, operating cash flow of $4.02B against $2.29B capex for roughly $1.73B of free cash flow. The balance-sheet fingerprint of the year (cash falling from $2.04B to $114M while net PP&E rose from $17.9B to $22.1B and long-term debt climbed to $3.57B) reflects a debt- and cash-funded Permian acquisition spree that made Coterra a more oil-weighted, Delaware-concentrated operator and an attractive merger partner. That same period included a Q4 2025 adjusted EPS miss ($0.39 vs. $0.45 consensus) on rising expenses, indicating execution softness heading into the close.

We rate CTRA a HOLD with an "uncertain" rationale. This reflects that the security has been converted into Devon stock, and the go-forward thesis depends on integration outcomes (the ~$1B 2027 synergy target, a strategic portfolio review with potential asset sales, and the 70%-of-FCF return framework) that cannot yet be scored on delivered results. The declining score trajectory across prior reports (6.4 in early May to 5.8 by mid-June) captured the loss of standalone identity; today's data confirms that trend rather than reversing it.

## Company Overview

Coterra Energy (legacy SEC CIK 858470, the former Cabot Oil & Gas shell) was a Houston-based diversified independent oil and gas exploration and production company, itself formed by the 2021 merger of Cabot Oil & Gas and Cimarex Energy. Its asset base spanned three principal areas:

- **Permian Basin (Delaware)** — the oil-weighted growth engine, expanded materially through 2024–2025 bolt-on acquisitions.
- **Marcellus Shale (Appalachia)** — natural gas production inherited from legacy Cabot's dry-gas position.
- **Anadarko Basin** — a mixed oil, gas, and NGL footprint.

This multi-basin structure gave Coterra a balanced oil/gas commodity mix, a genuine differentiator against pure-play peers. Pre-merger market cap was roughly $25.4B (April 2026 reporting). The combined Devon-Coterra entity is a ~$58B enterprise-value producer anchored in the Delaware Basin (~750,000 net acres, more than half of combined production and cash flow), with retained optionality across the Anadarko, Eagle Ford, Marcellus, and Rockies. The deal fits the broader "fewer, bigger, better" shale consolidation wave (ExxonMobil/Pioneer, Chevron/Hess, Diamondback/Endeavor), whose logic is that top-tier Delaware inventory is scarce, and scale lowers breakevens and enables longer laterals across contiguous acreage.

## Financial Analysis

Coterra's final standalone financials were solid and typical of a commodity-cyclical E&P:

- **Revenue:** $7.29B (2025) vs. $5.46B (2024), a +34% increase. The cyclicality is stark across the full window: $9.51B (2022, price spike) → $5.68B (2023) → $5.46B (2024) as gas and oil prices normalized, then the 2025 recovery, part of which was acquired volume rather than organic price.
- **Net income:** $1.72B (2025) vs. $1.12B (2024); the 2022 peak was $4.07B.
- **Cash flow:** Operating cash flow of $4.02B against capex of $2.29B, yielding free cash flow of ~$1.73B — healthy conversion.
- **Margins:** Operating income of $2.45B implies a 33.6% operating margin. D&A of $2.37B reflects the capital intensity of shale E&P.
- **Balance sheet:** Cash fell to $114M while net PP&E rose to $22.1B (+$4.2B), and long-term debt rose to $3.57B from $1.59B (2023). Leverage remained modest relative to $14.8B of equity and $4B of operating cash flow, consistent with investment-grade quality, but the pace of leverage build warrants attention.

The mechanism behind the year's balance-sheet shift is a large 2025 Permian acquisition program (industry reporting points to Franklin Mountain Energy and Avant Natural Resources as 2024–2025 deals; the specific 2025 deal financials could not be fully confirmed from primary filings, so treat the attribution as directional). The strategic effect was to shift Coterra toward oil and the Delaware Basin, which is what made it a logical merger target. On a go-forward basis, Devon guided to a $1.25B debt paydown post-close, which addresses the leverage creep directly.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone growth trends are now largely academic, but they inform the combined entity's starting position. The 2025 revenue jump was partly acquired rather than organic, and the four-quarter earnings record shows softening: three misses in the last four quarters, culminating in the Q4 2025 adjusted EPS of $0.39 (down from $0.49 a year earlier) on rising expenses. That pattern argues for caution on near-term cost control at the combined company.

The forward growth thesis is Devon's. Management's first combined outlook (June 9–11, 2026) guided to ~1.38M boe/d 2026 production (including ~500K bbl/d oil), a return of up to 70% of free cash flow to shareholders, a $1.25B debt repayment, and a strategic/financial portfolio review that could include asset sales. The durability of the growth case rests on Delaware inventory depth and the ~$1B annual pre-tax synergy target by 2027. Natural gas demand from power generation and data centers is a plausible tailwind for the retained gas-weighted assets, consistent with the broader confirmation of data-center load growth, though this is an option on the combined portfolio rather than a proven earnings driver. Because these are guidance items rather than delivered results, the growth score sits in the middle of the range.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive position of the combined company is stable-to-improving on trajectory. Consolidation concentrates cash flow in the scarce, high-quality Delaware Basin while retaining multi-basin optionality, which lowers breakevens through longer laterals and contiguous acreage. Scale is a genuine advantage in a business where inventory depth is the perpetual constraint. Coterra's own standalone TSR tracked its peer group closely (187.8 vs. 185.3 for 2024), indicating no chronic underperformance heading into the deal.

The offsetting factor is that the combined entity's competitive edge now depends on integration execution and on which legacy-Coterra assets survive the portfolio review. A market leader that has to rationalize a newly enlarged portfolio faces near-term uncertainty about which assets it keeps. The trajectory is favorable, but the moat-widening case is contingent on delivering the synergy target, which keeps this dimension mid-range rather than high.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Merger execution / integration risk (now dominant):** The thesis hinges on synergy realization by 2027. Cultural integration and portfolio rationalization are the central watch items, and the Q4 2025 cost pressure raises the bar on execution.
2. **Commodity price exposure:** Revenue swung on price alone across 2022–2024. The gas weighting is two-sided: a tailwind if power and data-center demand lifts gas, a headwind in oversupplied gas markets. Oil and natural gas price volatility directly hit cash flow, and commodity beta remains material.
3. **Capital intensity and depletion:** ~$2.3B of annual capex is required just to sustain and grow production; inventory quality and drilling-location depth are perpetual shale concerns.
4. **Leverage creep:** Debt more than doubled from 2023 to 2025 to fund acquisitions, with cash drawn to a thin $114M. The planned $1.25B paydown partially mitigates this.
5. **Portfolio-review / asset-sale risk:** Devon has signaled potential sales of non-core (likely legacy Coterra) assets, creating uncertainty about which assets survive and at what proceeds.
6. **Structural data-integrity risk for holders:** Automated feeds still treat CTRA as a live going concern. Any decision based on the CTRA data pull rather than the Devon reality would be materially misleading.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case:** The Devon-Coterra combination delivers the synergy target by 2027, executes the debt paydown, and returns up to 70% of free cash flow. Delaware Basin scale lowers breakevens, the retained gas assets benefit from rising power and data-center demand, and the portfolio review surfaces value through disciplined non-core sales. In a constructive oil/gas backdrop, the combined FCF machine re-rates alongside consolidated large-cap peers.

**Bear Case:** Synergies slip or under-deliver, integration frictions and the Q4 2025 cost pressure persist, and a soft gas market or weaker oil realizations compress commodity revenues. The portfolio review sells assets into a weak bid, and leverage taken on for the Permian buildout weighs on the multiple. Energy-sector beta drags returns even if company-specific execution is adequate.

Both cases are Devon cases. CTRA as an independent equity has no forward path; its bull and bear outcomes are entirely subsumed into the combined company's execution.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Because CTRA no longer trades independently, the position is a Devon holding and should be managed as such.

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY on the Devon successor):** (1) evidence of on-track synergy capture toward the 2027 target in the next two quarterly updates; (2) completion of the debt paydown restoring balance-sheet flexibility; (3) a portfolio review that monetizes non-core assets at accretive multiples with proceeds directed to debt reduction or buybacks.
- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) explicit reduction of the synergy target or a slipped timeline; (2) continued cost overruns compressing combined margins below guidance; (3) a debt-funded return program that outruns free cash flow in a weak commodity environment; (4) asset sales executed at distressed valuations.
- **Reassessment timeline:** The next Devon quarterly report and any formal update on the strategic portfolio review. Re-underwrite the position entirely under the DVN ticker; do not manage it off CTRA data.
