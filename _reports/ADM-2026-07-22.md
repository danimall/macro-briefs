**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Archer-Daniels-Midland is a scale operator in the global agricultural origination, processing, and trading complex, running through three segments: Ag Services & Oilseeds (~77% of segment revenue), Carbohydrate Solutions (~13%), and Nutrition (~9%). The business is coming off a multi-year earnings recession that took net income down roughly 75% from the 2022 peak ($4.34B to $1.08B) and cut adjusted return on invested capital from 13.6% to 6.3%. The core Crushing sub-segment collapsed 81% in 2025 on compressed soy/canola crush margins, weak biodiesel demand, deferred U.S. biofuel policy, and disrupted U.S.-China soybean flows.

The stock has rallied ~59% over the trailing twelve months, from roughly $54 to $86, and now trades near its 52-week high of $87.67. The rally reflects a genuine but tentative recovery: Q1 2026 delivered a beat-and-raise (total segment operating profit +2% YoY to $764M, adjusted EPS $0.71) after management cut guidance three times in 2025, with Carbohydrate Solutions up 48% and Nutrition up 42% on ethanol/45Z clarity and portfolio actions. Consensus EPS estimates have risen roughly 12% over 90 days for both the current and next fiscal year, the first constructive revision trend in over a year.

The problem is that the price has moved ahead of the fundamental story. At ~$86, ADM trades above the $75.3 mean and $76.0 median analyst targets; the sell-side skews cautious (one buy, seven holds, three sell/strong-sell). The recovery hinges almost entirely on exogenous factors ADM does not control: the Section 45Z Clean Fuel Production Credit, Renewable Volume Obligations, and crush spreads, while the core Crushing engine swung to a $79M loss in Q1 2026. Overlaying this is an unresolved intersegment-sales accounting scandal whose securities-fraud class action survived a motion to dismiss in March 2025, negative rating outlooks from all three agencies, and a suspended buyback signaling management's own defensive posture. We rate ADM a HOLD. The setup lacks the asymmetric upside required for a BUY and the specific, timed downside catalyst required for a short.

## Company Overview

ADM describes itself as "an essential global agricultural supply chain manager and processor," operating in roughly 180 countries with consolidated subsidiaries in about 75. Its economic moat is physical infrastructure (ports, rail, barges, storage, crush and milling plants) and the trade-flow optionality that scale provides. It is not a branded consumer business with pricing power; earnings are set by crush and grind spreads, commodity basis, biofuel policy, and trade flows, most of which are exogenous.

The three reportable segments:

- **Ag Services & Oilseeds (AS&O)** — the commodity-cyclical engine ($61.6B revenue in 2025). Origination, merchandising, transport/storage of grains; oilseed crushing; refined vegetable oils and biodiesel feedstocks. Includes a 22.5% equity stake in Wilmar International carried at roughly $4.0-4.1B. This is where earnings collapsed.
- **Carbohydrate Solutions** — corn/wheat wet and dry milling: sweeteners, starches, and ethanol, plus carbon-capture initiatives tied to 45Z credits ($10.7B revenue).
- **Nutrition** — higher-margin Human and Animal Nutrition ($7.5B revenue). The intended value-add growth pillar, which has chronically underperformed the capital invested in it.

ADM sits within the consolidated "ABCD" grain-trading oligopoly (ADM, Bunge, Cargill, Louis Dreyfus). The competitive structure is favorable, but it does not confer pricing power because margins are determined by spreads set outside the company.

## Financial Analysis

The trailing record shows a pronounced descent from the 2022 cyclical peak:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $101.6B | $93.9B | $85.5B | $80.3B |
| Net income | $4.34B | $3.48B | $1.80B | $1.08B |
| Adj. EPS | — | — | $4.74 | $3.43 |
| Total segment op. profit | — | — | $4.21B | $3.24B |
| Adjusted ROIC | 13.6% | 12.2% | 8.3% | 6.3% |

Revenue fell 21% cumulatively since 2022; much of that is commodity-price pass-through (revenue and COGS move together), so margin is the better lens, and margins compressed materially. FY2025 gross profit fell 13% to $5.0B, operating margin is roughly 0.2%, and net margin is 1.3%. Adjusted ROIC of 6.3% is well below a reasonable cost of capital for a capital-intensive trader, which is why five-year TSR (131) lags the peer group (214) by a widening margin.

2025 earnings absorbed more than $1.0B of specified items: $372M of revaluation losses on venture/alt-protein investments, $283M of restructuring, and a $179M impairment of capitalized internal-use software, a candid admission that a multi-year digital-transformation effort underdelivered, after which management "pivoted away from large global implementations."

Cash flow quality is the most important nuance. Reported 2025 operating cash flow was strong at $5.5B (up from $2.8B in 2024), with FCF near $4.2B. But this was driven by working-capital release (roughly $1.5B of inventory drawdown as commodity prices fell, plus a $1.1B brokerage-customer payables swing) rather than underlying earnings power. The mechanism reversed in Q1 2026: as prices rose, working capital consumed $1.4B. Headline OCF strength should not be read as durable earnings.

The balance sheet is investment-grade but stretched for a commodity trader. Altman Z of 2.57 sits in the grey zone, D/E is roughly 2.3, and reported debt understates true leverage because ADM also uses about $2.1B of accounts-receivable securitization plus supplier-payable programs. All three rating agencies carry a negative outlook, which matters directly for a business dependent on commercial paper and securitization for working-capital funding.

The capital-allocation shift is a tell. ADM executed $2.3B of buybacks in 2024, then zero in 2025 and none in Q1 2026, while maintaining the roughly $1.0B/year dividend and its Dividend King status. Management is prioritizing balance-sheet defense over shareholder returns, a reasonable posture given the negative outlooks, but one that contradicts the more upbeat Q1-beat narrative.

## Growth Analysis

The bull thesis rests on the Q1 2026 recovery and rising estimates. Both are real, but neither is organic growth. Revenue is declining, not accelerating. The recovery is a margin-and-policy story: Carbohydrate Solutions rose 48% on ethanol margins and 45Z clarity, and Nutrition rose 42% on portfolio actions, while AS&O fell 34% and Crushing swung to a $79M loss on negative mark-to-market timing.

Estimate revisions are positive: current-year consensus EPS is up 12.3% over 90 days to $4.73, next-year up 11.7% to $5.46, and current-quarter up 30.6% over 90 days. The earnings-surprise record is clean, with four consecutive beats of 6.6% to 11.0%. This supports confidence that consensus is not stale to the downside.

Two qualifications temper the growth score. First, the strongest forward signal (estimate revisions above 30% combined with revenue growth above 30%) does not fully apply here, because the revenue leg is missing. This is an earnings recovery off a trough, not a revenue-growth inflection. Second, the very-near-term revision trend has softened: current-quarter estimates fell 5.9% over the last 30 days even as the 90-day figure remained strongly positive, suggesting the initial policy-driven optimism is being trimmed at the margin.

Durability is the crux. The recovery depends on 45Z (ADM generated no significant benefit in 2025), higher 2026/2027 RVOs, and crush spreads, all outside management control. Portfolio simplification (a $500M+ cost program through 2029/30, cottonseed and animal-feed JVs, facility rationalization) provides a self-help lever, and the Nutrition franchise offers a theoretical structural growth path, but returns there have not yet justified the invested capital. Growth prospects are recovering but low-quality and policy-contingent.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$86, trailing P/E of 38.6x on depressed GAAP EPS of $2.23 is meaningless; that figure reflects trough earnings weighed down by $1.0B+ of specified items. The relevant metrics are forward: ~18.2x current-year consensus ($4.73) and ~15.8x next-year ($5.46). Those multiples are not stretched for a business with earnings recovering.

The peer set is heterogeneous and offers limited anchoring: Bunge 33.0x, Darling 41.4x, Ingredion 9.7x, Vital Farms 10.5x, median 33.0x. ADM's ~18x forward sits between Ingredion and Bunge. On its face, ADM looks cheaper than the median, but that median is distorted by two names, and cheapness relative to a distorted peer group is not a mispricing thesis.

The more telling reference point is that ADM already trades above the mean and median analyst targets, with a low of $60 and a high of $95. Analyst targets function as floors on cheap, high-momentum inflection stocks where consensus lags reality, but that requires the stock to be below where analysts think it belongs. Here the opposite is true: after the run, the market has re-rated ADM above the sell-side's fair-value estimates, and the recommendation distribution is bearish. That is a caution flag, not evidence of upside asymmetry.

At $86, the stock prices the $5.46 next-year figure at ~15.8x, which embeds continued policy tailwind and crush recovery. If 45Z benefits disappoint or crush spreads stay compressed (the core engine is still loss-making), forward EPS rolls back toward trough levels and 18x forward on trough-ish earnings looks expensive. The setup offers no clear reason the market is mispricing the stock to the upside. We score valuation below neutral: fair-to-full after the run, with the burden of proof on the bulls given the price is above consensus.

## Competitive Landscape

ADM's position within the ABCD oligopoly is structurally intact; scale and infrastructure remain the moat, and the FY2025 downturn was partly sector-wide (Bunge and the broader ingredients group faced the same crush compression, weak biofuel demand, and trade dislocations). But the trajectory is what matters, and ADM's trajectory is deteriorating relative to peers on the metrics that count. Adjusted ROIC halved over five years, and five-year TSR of 131 versus the peer group's 214 shows a widening competitive gap, not a stable one.

Part of the underperformance is self-inflicted rather than cyclical: the accounting scandal and restatement, the $179M digital write-off, and Nutrition's chronic failure to earn its capital. Nutrition was supposed to be the differentiating, less-commoditized franchise (clean-label, postbiotics, flavors), but returns there have not justified the investment. Industry-level pressures (state-owned competitors with non-commercial return objectives, capacity additions pressuring margins, and shifting consumer preferences away from HFCS and edible oils) add secular headwinds to the value-add pivot. A market leader losing relative share on ROIC and TSR warrants a below-neutral competitive score.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant idiosyncratic risk is the intersegment-sales accounting matter and its litigation tail. In January 2024 ADM announced an investigation into intersegment sales practices and placed its CFO on leave; a November 2024 8-K (Item 4.02) confirmed non-reliance on previously issued financials, a restatement. The securities-fraud class action survived a motion to dismiss in March 2025, meaning it cleared the pleading stage and proceeds toward discovery, alongside consolidated derivative suits. ADM states it cannot estimate a reasonably possible loss. The FY2025 10-K does not carry a dedicated risk factor for the matter, folding it into generic legal-proceedings language, a soft disclosure of a material governance failure. The SEC/DOJ regulatory resolution status could not be confirmed from the filings reviewed and should be treated as open. This is a live, unquantifiable binary that the market has not clearly priced given the stock's run.

Additional risks:
- **Negative rating outlooks across all three agencies**, a genuine threat to funding cost and working-capital flexibility for a business reliant on commercial paper and A/R securitization.
- **Biofuel policy dependence** — 45Z regs, RVO levels, and small-refinery exemptions can swing crush and RPO margins in either direction. This is both the primary tailwind and the primary downside.
- **Wilmar impairment risk** — the ~$4.0B carrying value exceeded market value ($3.4B) at YE2025; ADM concluded no other-than-temporary impairment but flagged reassessment. Market value recovered above carrying by Q1 2026, temporarily relieving the risk.
- **Crush cyclicality and trade policy** — U.S.-China soybean disruption, EU Deforestation Regulation timing, and abundant global stocks are all margin-compressive.
- **Governance concentration** — combined Chair/CEO role at a company that just experienced a control breakdown, plus repeated Item 5.02 leadership changes through 2026.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 36.8% is rich versus 26.8% one-year historical vol (a 10-point spread, 84th percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (36.8% near-term versus 29.6% at six months), pricing imminent event stress consistent with the upcoming earnings print. The implied move at the nearest expiry is ±8.1%. Put/call skew is balanced (0.5%) and put/call volume is light (0.10), so there is no directional bearish tilt in positioning. IV has compressed sharply, from 50.0% to 36.8%, as the stock rose 17.4% since the prior snapshot, indicating the market de-risked into the rally. The net read: elevated near-term uncertainty around the next earnings event, but no consensus directional bet. This argues against chasing the stock into the print and does not confirm a bearish thesis strongly enough to warrant a short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($95).** The Q1 2026 beat-and-raise marks a durable trough. 45Z regulations and higher 2026/2027 RVOs restore crush and ethanol margins; Nutrition and Carbohydrate Solutions sustain their double-digit growth; the $500M+ cost program and portfolio JVs lift structural ROIC back toward double digits; the accounting litigation settles for an immaterial sum; rating agencies revise outlooks to stable and the buyback resumes. On next-year EPS of $5.46 at ~17x, the stock reaches the analyst high of $95, roughly +10% from current levels.

**Bear Case ($60-65).** Policy tailwinds disappoint: 45Z benefits underwhelm, crush spreads stay compressed, and the core Crushing engine remains loss-making. Working-capital tailwinds reverse, as they already did in Q1, exposing weak underlying cash generation. The securities class action produces an adverse or costly outcome, or the SEC/DOJ matter resurfaces. A rating downgrade raises funding costs. Forward EPS rolls back toward the $3.40s trough, and the multiple compresses toward the low-teens, taking the stock to the analyst low of $60, roughly -30% from current levels.

The base case sits between these. On next-year EPS of $5.46 at ~14x, a reasonable multiple for a policy-dependent commodity recovery with unresolved governance overhang, fair value is roughly $76-78, close to the analyst mean/median and modestly below the current price. The risk/reward from $86 is symmetric-to-slightly-unfavorable: roughly +10% to the bull case against -30% to the bear.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction: the recovery and positive revisions are real, but the stock trades above consensus targets with material unresolved risk and no organic growth. The natural reassessment point is the next earnings release (early August 2026), where the ±8.1% implied move and IV backwardation signal the market expects a meaningful reaction.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- A confirmed second consecutive quarter of Crushing recovery (segment returning to positive operating profit) with 45Z benefits actually flowing through, demonstrating the recovery is durable rather than mark-to-market timing.
- Resolution of the intersegment-sales accounting matter (litigation settlement at an immaterial figure and/or confirmed closure of any SEC/DOJ inquiry) removing the governance/legal binary.
- Rating-agency outlook revision to stable and resumption of buybacks, signaling management confidence and improving per-share dynamics, ideally on a pullback toward the mid-$70s where valuation offers a margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- A guidance cut or a Q2 miss that reverses the beat-and-raise narrative, or 90-day estimate revisions turning negative.
- Adverse development in the securities class action (unfavorable ruling, large reserve) or confirmation of an SEC/DOJ enforcement action.
- A rating downgrade, or a Wilmar impairment charge as the carrying value re-diverges from market value.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (early August 2026), with the biofuel policy calendar (45Z final regulations, 2026/2027 RVO decisions) as the key intervening variable.

## Conclusion

ADM has done enough in Q1 2026 to interrupt a punishing two-year earnings recession: a beat-and-raise, double-digit growth in two of three segments, and a 12% upward revision to forward consensus. That trajectory is improving, consistent with the modest upward drift in our scoring over recent quarters. But the improvements are policy-contingent and low-quality: revenue is still declining, the core Crushing engine is loss-making, headline cash flow is a working-capital artifact, and the recovery depends on 45Z, RVOs, and crush spreads that ADM does not control.

Against that, the stock at ~$86 has already run 59% and trades above the mean analyst target, near its 52-week high, while a securities-fraud class action that survived a motion to dismiss, negative outlooks from all three rating agencies, and a suspended buyback signal genuine, partly unpriced risk. The risk/reward from here is symmetric-to-unfavorable, roughly +10% to the bull case against -30% to the bear, and the options market is pricing near-term event stress into the August print. There is neither the upside asymmetry to justify a BUY nor a specific, timed downside catalyst with favorable mechanics to justify a short (short interest is only 4.8% of float, momentum is positive, and the dividend provides support). We rate ADM a HOLD and would revisit constructively on a pullback toward the mid-$70s or on confirmed, durable Crushing recovery plus resolution of the accounting overhang.
