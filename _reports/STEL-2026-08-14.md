**Research Date: August 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Stellar Bancorp no longer exists as a standalone public company. The merger of Stellar Bancorp, Inc. into Prosperity Bancshares, Inc. (NYSE: PB), and of Stellar Bank into Prosperity Bank, became effective July 1, 2026. This is documented in Prosperity's Form 10-Q for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 (filed August 6, 2026) under "Subsequent Event," which states that Prosperity "issued 19,371,499 shares of its common stock and paid approximately $578.66 million in cash for all outstanding shares of Stellar common stock," and is corroborated by Prosperity's July 1, 2026 completion release. The per-share formula was 0.3803 PB shares plus $11.36 in cash.

The market data attached to this ticker is terminal, not live. The last daily close in the record is $39.32 on June 30, 2026 — the final trading session before the merger became effective. The canonical pricing snapshot for this report returns no authoritative price and flags six errors, including `schwab_quote_unavailable` and `missing_authoritative_price`. The $2.002 billion "market cap," the 19.09x trailing P/E and the 16.75x forward P/E in the profile are arithmetic performed on that stale close (50.94 million shares × $39.32 ≈ $2.003 billion), not observations of a functioning market. A reader who anchors on the $39.32 print will conclude the stock trades at a modest discount to a $2 billion deal value. That inference is wrong in direction: using Prosperity's own disclosed Q2 2026 buyback prices ($68.11–$69.04) and stock-issuance valuations (~$69.01–$69.10) as the proxy for PB's price around the close, the realized per-share consideration was $37.35–$37.60, so the terminal quote sits roughly 4.6%–5.3% **above** what holders actually received.

The rating is AVOID because there is no security to buy, not because the business collapsed. On the substance of the transaction, the board sold well. Stellar exited at approximately 1.73x tangible book value (using derived TBV/share of $21.60) and 18.5x FY2025 GAAP net income of $102.9 million, for a bank generating a 0.95% ROA, a 6.28% ROE and roughly 9.7% straight ROTCE, with net income declining in each of the two prior years on a balance sheet that had gone nowhere since the 2022 merger of equals. That price reflects the scarcity value of a $10.8 billion Houston deposit franchise to an in-market consolidator plus 10.75% tangible capital and cost-takeout potential — not Stellar's own earning power. Former holders now own approximately 16.1% of Prosperity's post-issuance shares plus cash, and their forward return is entirely a function of Prosperity's execution.

## Company Overview

Stellar Bancorp was a Texas-incorporated bank holding company (SEC CIK 0001473844, SIC 6021), headquartered at 9 Greenway Plaza in Houston, with 1,053 employees and 52 banking offices across Houston, Beaumont, East Texas and Dallas at the time of closing. Its EDGAR name history — CBFH, Inc. (2009–2017) → CBTX, Inc. (2017–2022) → Stellar Bancorp, Inc. (effective October 3, 2022) — traces the defining corporate event: the October 2022 combination of CBTX and Allegiance Bancshares into a roughly $10.9 billion-asset Houston commercial bank. Goodwill of $497.3 million recognized at December 31, 2022 is the accounting footprint of that deal.

The franchise was a relationship-driven commercial bank serving small and mid-sized businesses, professionals and individuals: C&I lending, commercial real estate including multifamily and construction/land development, 1-4 family residential, factoring, and a standard deposit and treasury-services suite. Prosperity's decision to install Stellar's CEO as Vice Chairman and Stellar's President as Prosperity Bank's Houston Area Chairman is consistent with the value residing in the Houston lending relationships and the deposit base rather than in a product or technology asset.

One caveat on evidentiary depth: Stellar's own Item 1 Business and Item 1A Risk Factors could not be retrieved for this work — the EDGAR text endpoints returned null for the delisted registrant and the section extractor resolved only to a Part III-only annual-report amendment covering Items 10–15. Consequently the granular loan mix, deposit composition, customer concentration and management-identified risk set are unverified. Nothing in this report should be read as a claim about Stellar's disclosed risk factors.

**Capital was the strength.** Tangible common equity at December 31, 2025 was $1,668.654M − $497.318M − $71.018M = $1,100.318 million. Against tangible assets of $10,238.258 million, that is a 10.75% TCE/TA ratio — genuinely strong for a $10.8 billion commercial bank and a direct source of acquisition appeal, because a buyer can lever excess capital more efficiently than a subscale seller can.

**Profitability was the weakness, and it was deteriorating.** FY2025 ROA was 0.95% ($102.872M on average assets of $10,856.2M) and ROE 6.28% ($102.872M on average equity of $1,638.3M). Straight ROTCE was 9.71%; adding back $21.528 million of other-intangible amortization at the 19.5% FY2025 effective tax rate lifts it to 11.35%, which reconciles with the ~11.48% ROTCE Stellar reported for Q1 2025. Goodwill of $497.3 million equals 45.2% of tangible common equity, so reported ROE materially understates cash-earning power — but even the adjusted 11.35% sits at or barely above a plausible cost of equity for a single-metro Texas bank.

**The decline was a margin and expense problem, not a volume problem.** Net income fell 21.2% over two years ($130.5M → $115.0M → $102.9M) while total assets were flat to slightly lower. Pretax income tracked the same path ($161.9M → $145.0M → $127.8M). The one Stellar-specific margin datapoint available — 4.2% NIM in Q1 2025 with sequentially declining net interest income — is consistent with asset yields rolling over against stagnant earning-asset volumes. FY2025 net interest income, efficiency ratio, loans, deposits and asset-quality metrics are unverified.

**Cash-flow quality was adequate rather than strong.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $97.004 million came in below net income of $102.872 million. For a bank, provisions and amortization usually push OCF above net income, so a shortfall points to accrual or working-capital timing drags (loans held for sale, accrued interest receivable, deferred taxes are the likely candidates) that cannot be resolved without the cash-flow statement detail. Capex of $4.398 million against $8.058 million of D&A and $106.1 million of net premises indicates a bank spending below depreciation — consistent with a management team that had stopped building for growth.

**One unresolved balance-sheet swing.** Cash and equivalents fell from $911.2 million to $419.5 million across FY2025, a $491.8 million reduction in the ending balance. Redeployment into securities or loans, or repayment of wholesale funding, are the benign explanations; deposit outflow is the adverse one. The available data does not distinguish them.

**Earnings delivery was solid at the end.** The last four reported quarters produced two beats and two in-line results, with Q1 2026 EPS of $0.58 against a $0.50 estimate (+15.3%) and Q2 2025 EPS of $0.51 against $0.46 (+12.1%). That record has no bearing on realized value: the exchange ratio and cash component were fixed on January 27, 2026 with no disclosed collar, so post-announcement operating performance accrued to the buyer, not to selling shareholders.

## Growth Analysis

There is no forward growth case for STEL as an instrument. The relevant historical judgment is that the growth engine had stalled well before the sale. Total assets moved from $10,900.4M (2022) to $10,647.1M (2023) to $10,905.8M (2024) to $10,806.6M (2025) — a compound change of approximately −0.3% per year across three years in one of the fastest-growing metropolitan economies in the United States. Company-selected EPS in the proxy shows no durable trend either: $1.57 (2020), $2.85 (2021), $1.48 (2022, merger charges), $2.45 (2023), $2.15 (2024).

A bank that completes a large in-market merger of equals and then cannot grow earning assets for three consecutive years is not compounding franchise value; it is generating a shrinking share of a growing market while carrying merger-created intangibles equal to roughly 30% of reported GAAP equity. That, more than any single quarter, is the growth conclusion.

For former holders, growth now runs through Prosperity. Prosperity's own pre-Stellar Q2 2026 balance sheet grew 14.1% to $43.87 billion, with loans up 14.8% and deposits up 14.5%, almost entirely from the American Bank Holding (January 1, 2026) and Southwest Bancshares (February 1, 2026) closings rather than organic expansion. Prosperity's tax-equivalent NIM rose 29bp year over year to 3.47%, with cost of total interest-bearing liabilities down to 2.13% from 2.38%, and it disclosed $73.2 million of outstanding accretable discounts before Stellar's marks are added — a mechanical, finite boost to future net interest income. Reported CFO commentary putting exit-2026 NIM at 3.70%–3.75% and 2027 at 3.80%–3.85% is consistent with that trajectory but was not verified against a filing or transcript and should be treated as unconfirmed.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation exercise here is a realized-value assessment, not a forward one.

**Realized consideration.** Prosperity's exact filed consideration was 19,371,499 shares plus approximately $578.66 million in cash. Two independent divisions of that disclosure agree on the share count: $578.66M ÷ $11.36 = 50.938M shares, and 19,371,499 ÷ 0.3803 = 50.937M shares — approximately 50.94 million STEL shares outstanding at closing. Applying PB's disclosed Q2 buyback weighted-average of $68.34 gives 0.3803 × $68.34 = $25.99 in stock plus $11.36 cash = **$37.35 per STEL share**, or $1.903 billion aggregate. At PB $69.00 the figure is $37.60, or roughly $1.915 billion. Back-solving from the rounded "~$2 billion" announcement press figure implies a PB price of about $73.36 in late January, so PB shares drifted roughly 6%–7% lower between announcement and close — a normal outcome for a 69.6%-stock deal with a fixed exchange ratio.

**Exit multiples.** Derived TBV/share was $1,100.318M ÷ 50.94M = $21.60 (the share count is measured at the July 1, 2026 close and applied to December 31, 2025 equity; modest repurchase activity means the true year-end count was likely marginally higher and TBV/share marginally lower, with a 1% difference worth about $0.22). That gives:

- Price/TBV: $37.35 ÷ $21.60 = **1.73x** at the closing window; **1.82x** at the announcement-implied $39.26.
- Price/FY2025 GAAP earnings: $1.903B ÷ $102.872M = **18.5x** at close; 19.4x at announcement.
- Cash component: $11.36 ÷ $37.35 = 30.4% of consideration, funded from Prosperity's balance sheet liquidity ($1.68B cash, $7.62B undrawn FHLB capacity, $8.45B discount-window availability at June 30, 2026).

For a bank earning a 0.95% ROA, a 6.28% ROE and roughly 9.7% straight ROTCE with declining net income on a flat balance sheet, 1.7–1.8x tangible book and 18–19x trailing earnings is a full price. Note precisely what this does and does not establish: Stellar's undisturbed pre-announcement share price is not available, so the takeover **premium to market** cannot be computed. A high multiple of book is not the same as a large premium to the prior trading price, and the two should not be conflated.

**Why the valuation score is low.** The last available quote of $39.32 exceeds realized consideration of $37.35–$37.60 by 4.6%–5.3%. There is no forward participation attached to that print; the security was delisted, the consideration paid, and any residual STEL quotation is a data artifact. A "cheap-looking" 16.75x forward P/E on this ticker is a computation on a dead price. That is the definition of a valuation with zero asymmetric upside and a live risk of transacting against stale data, which is why the dimension scores 2.0 despite the underlying transaction having been priced generously for sellers.

**For those still holding the consideration.** Prosperity's 1H 2026 diluted EPS was $2.84 (Q2 $1.67, including an $8.2 million Visa Class B-2 exchange gain net of securities sales worth roughly $0.06–$0.08 pre-tax per share), on ROA 1.55%, ROE 8.14% and a Q2 efficiency ratio of 45.99% (1H 52.44%, distorted by $43.3 million of merger expenses). At PB's high-$60s price that is roughly 11.5–12x annualized run-rate earnings before Stellar accretion. That is a starting point for a separate underwriting exercise, not a recommendation issued here.

## Competitive Landscape

Stellar's competitive position is best read through Prosperity's consolidation cadence. Within six months Prosperity closed three whole-bank deals: American Bank Holding (18 offices, ~$306.8M of stock, $185.9M preliminary goodwill), Southwest Bancshares/Texas Partners Bank (11 offices, ~$282.6M of stock, $134.9M preliminary goodwill) and Stellar (52 offices, 19.37M shares plus $578.66M cash). Stellar was roughly three times the combined equity consideration of the other two.

Stellar's 52 offices folded into a Prosperity network of 311 locations, including 62 already in the Houston area. That direct overlap is what makes a target valuable to a cost-focused acquirer and simultaneously makes standalone survival difficult for the subscale competitor. Prosperity states its growth strategy explicitly as internal growth, efficient operations and acquisitions, supported by centralized data and loan processing designed to capture economies of scale. Prosperity's 45.99% Q2 efficiency ratio against Stellar's falling earnings on a flat asset base quantifies the gap.

The rate environment also cuts against the idea that Stellar sold at a sector trough. Prosperity's Q2 disclosure shows asset yields up and deposit costs down across every interest-bearing category (money market 2.63% → 2.44%; time deposits 3.67% → 3.26%; savings 0.71% → 0.51%), with total deposit cost improving to 1.32% from 1.37%. The funding squeeze of 2022–2024 was reversing. Stellar's P&L had not yet captured that improvement, which is part of why the buyer could pay 1.7–1.8x book and still expect an accretive outcome.

Prior reports scored competitive position as high as 7.0, reflecting deal certainty and franchise scarcity value. With the transaction complete, there is no continuing competitive position to score for this registrant; the dimension is marked down to 3.5 to reflect a franchise absorbed rather than defended, not because anything about the Houston deposit base worsened.

## Risk Assessment

**Instrument risk is now the dominant risk.** The security was delisted from the NYSE effective with the July 1, 2026 closing. The canonical valuation snapshot returns no authoritative price and flags a market-date mismatch against the June 30, 2026 daily close. Any order routed against a stale $39.32 quote, or any model that treats the $2.002 billion market cap as live, is operating on data that no longer describes a tradable instrument. The practical exposure is transactional and analytical error, not equity drawdown.

**Verification gaps that bear on confidence.** Stellar's Item 1A risk factors, FY2025 net interest income and margin, efficiency ratio, loans, deposits, nonperforming assets, allowance coverage, weighted-average diluted shares and reported FY2025 diluted EPS are all unverified. Insider transaction history is entirely unexamined — the roughly 24 filings dated July 1, 2026 through a single filing agent are consistent with merger-close Form 4 activity but were not read, and no conclusion about insider buying or selling ahead of the January 2026 announcement is supported. The merger proxy and fairness opinion were not reviewed, so sale-process quality, competing bids and advisor multiple ranges are unknown. Goodwill and core deposit intangible on the Stellar acquisition are not yet disclosed; Prosperity's Q3 2026 10-Q, carrying the purchase-accounting allocation and fair-value marks on Stellar's loans and deposits, is the single most informative missing document.

**Risks inherited by former holders (Prosperity's risks).**

1. *Concurrent triple integration through March 2027.* Prosperity is integrating three acquisitions closed within six months in overlapping Texas markets, with Stellar branches retaining their name until conversion scheduled for March 2027 — a nine-month runway. Prosperity's own forward-looking language flags the possibility that anticipated benefits "are not realized when expected or at all." Commercial-lender attrition and deposit runoff in Houston, where the overlap is direct, are the specific transmission channels. Against that, Prosperity is an experienced serial acquirer, and installing Franklin as Vice Chairman and Vitulli as Houston Area Chairman is a deliberate retention mechanism.

2. *Charge-off spike, largely mechanical.* Prosperity's 1H 2026 net charge-offs were $43.5 million versus $5.7 million a year earlier (0.35% vs 0.05% of average loans annualized), including a $39.2 million increase in C&I. The offsetting evidence is substantial: zero provision for credit losses in Q2 and 1H, nonperforming assets down to $130.6 million (0.52%) from $150.8 million (0.69%), allowance coverage of nonperforming loans up to 321% from 243%, and day-one acquisition allowances of $53.3 million (PCD) and $39.3 million (PSL) recorded on the American and Southwest deals under ASU 2025-08, a mandated standard adopted January 1, 2026. Charge-offs against acquired loans carrying acquisition-date reserves consume allowance without touching provision. "Substantially mechanical" is not "entirely": $36.1 million of gross charge-offs came on **originated** loans, with originated net charge-offs of $34.1 million annualizing near 0.35%, and the C&I concentration of that warrants monitoring. The accounting change was not a management election and no inference about intent is drawn from it.

3. *Unrecognized HTM losses.* Prosperity's held-to-maturity book carried $11.993 billion of amortized cost against $11.101 billion of fair value at June 30, 2026 — $896.0 million of gross unrealized losses, up from $852.3 million at year-end, and equal to 10.8% of $8.31 billion of shareholders' equity. This is duration risk on agency mortgage paper ($877.3 million of the loss sits in $11.481 billion of agency MBS), not credit risk; the AOCI opt-out and HTM classification exclude these marks from the correctly stated 15.94% CET1 ratio by regulatory design; and liquidity backstops make forced monetization unlikely. It is an economic adjustment to the value of capital, not a solvency issue.

4. *Concentration.* Real estate is 79.9% of Prosperity's loans excluding the warehouse program, with C&I at 13.9% including commitments to oil and gas producers secured by proven developed producing reserves plus service, equipment and midstream companies. The combined footprint is overwhelmingly Texas. Stellar's addition deepens Houston concentration specifically. This is structural.

5. *Scale threshold.* Pro forma assets of roughly $54–55 billion ($43.87B plus ~$10.8B) cross $50 billion. Principal enhanced-prudential thresholds sit at $100 billion, and no filing language on supervisory consequences was located; this is flagged as an inference to verify rather than an established constraint.

No options data was available for this ticker, consistent with the delisting; no options-based read is offered.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (as it applied to the transaction, now realized).** Stellar's board recognized that a $10.8 billion Houston franchise earning sub-cost-of-equity returns on a flat balance sheet was worth more inside a scale consolidator than outside one, and monetized 10.75% tangible capital and cost-takeout potential at 1.73–1.82x tangible book and 18.5–19.4x trailing earnings. Holders received 30.4% of value in cash and the balance in shares of a bank with a 45.99% efficiency ratio, 15.94% CET1, expanding NIM and $73.2 million of accretable discounts before Stellar's marks. If Prosperity converts three acquisitions without lender attrition and NIM reaches the reported 3.80%–3.85% 2027 path, the stock component compounds from here.

**Bear case.** Value realized fell 6%–7% below the announcement headline because the exchange ratio was fixed and PB drifted from roughly $73 to $68–69. A mostly-stock sale struck when the seller's own stock had underperformed locks in relative underperformance rather than curing it: Stellar's own proxy shows $100 invested at the start of the five-year measurement window worth $100.49 at FY2024 against $123.92 for its selected peer group, trailing in four of five years. Former holders now carry concurrent triple-integration execution risk in overlapping Houston markets, a legacy originated-loan charge-off rate running near 0.35% annualized with C&I concentration, an $896 million unrecognized HTM mark equal to 10.8% of equity, and Texas real-estate and energy-linked concentration — with the Stellar purchase-accounting allocation still undisclosed.

**Base judgment.** The transaction was a defensible board response to a weak five-year record, priced generously relative to Stellar's own earning power. Neither case is actionable through STEL, because the instrument is gone.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria for STEL**: none exist. Stellar Bancorp, Inc. merged out of existence on July 1, 2026 and cannot be repurchased at any price. The appropriate action is administrative: remove STEL from active coverage and from any screening universe, and confirm deregistration so that automated pipelines stop pricing the June 30, 2026 close as live. Related housekeeping: any process computing "revenue" of $6.3 million or an Altman-based distress flag for this CIK is producing an artifact of bank-inapplicable standardized tagging, not a signal.

**For readers holding the consideration** (cash plus 0.3803 PB shares per former STEL share, equal to roughly 16.1% of Prosperity's post-issuance equity), the reassessment path runs through Prosperity, and the decision requires separate underwriting. Three dated checkpoints define it:

1. **Prosperity's Q3 2026 10-Q** — the first disclosure of goodwill, core deposit intangible and loan and deposit fair-value marks on Stellar. This determines whether Prosperity's own accounting supports the price it paid, and sizes the accretion tailwind added to the existing $73.2 million of accretable discounts.
2. **Originated-loan credit trend through Q3–Q4 2026** — the test is whether originated net charge-offs (approximately $34.1 million in 1H, near 0.35% annualized) stabilize or continue rising, and whether Prosperity's provision stays at zero. A resumption of provisioning alongside rising C&I charge-offs would move the charge-off spike from mechanical to genuine.
3. **March 2027 systems conversion** — the checkpoint for lender and deposit attrition in Houston, where Prosperity's 62 legacy offices overlap Stellar's 52. Watch combined deposit balances and Houston commercial loan growth through the conversion window.

Escalation trigger for holders: verified deposit runoff or commercial-lender departures in the Houston market combined with NIM tracking below the reported 3.70%–3.75% exit-2026 path would signal that integration is consuming the synergies the price assumed. Upgrade trigger: Q3 purchase accounting confirming conservative Stellar marks, provision remaining at zero on stable originated credit, and NIM at or above 3.70% by year-end.

## Conclusion

**Recommendation: AVOID — 3.8/10.** This rating reflects the absence of an investable instrument rather than deterioration in the underlying franchise, and it breaks a run of three prior HOLDs on a rising score trajectory precisely because the terminal event that trajectory anticipated has now occurred. Stellar Bancorp merged into Prosperity Bancshares effective July 1, 2026; holders received 0.3803 PB shares plus $11.36 cash per share, worth roughly $37.35–$37.60 at the closing window against a residual $39.32 quote that overstates realized value by 4.6%–5.3%.

On the merits of the exit, the board acted reasonably. A bank generating a 0.95% ROA and a 6.28% ROE on assets that had not grown in three years, with net income down 21.2% over two years and 45.2% of tangible common equity represented by merger goodwill, sold at 1.73–1.82x tangible book and 18.5–19.4x trailing earnings after five years of essentially flat total return against a peer group up roughly 24%. The premium came from what a consolidator could do with a Houston deposit franchise and 10.75% tangible capital, not from Stellar's own earning power. Two Stellar directors joined Prosperity's holding-company board and its CEO became Vice Chairman, which is consistent with a buyer paying for relationships and lender continuity — and also a standard negotiated feature of friendly bank M&A, from which no intent is inferred.

The forward question is Prosperity's, and it is not answered here. The material open items are the Q3 2026 purchase-accounting allocation for Stellar, the trajectory of originated-loan C&I charge-offs now running near 0.35% annualized, an $896 million unrecognized HTM mark equal to 10.8% of equity, and execution on three concurrent Texas integrations through March 2027. Coverage of STEL should close.
