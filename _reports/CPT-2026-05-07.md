## Executive Summary

Camden Property Trust is a high-quality Sunbelt-focused multifamily REIT trading at $103.95, representing a 13.5% discount to its 52-week high and approximately 15.4x its 2026 Core FFO guidance midpoint of $6.75/share. The company's investment-grade balance sheet (A-/A3/A-), 4.1% dividend yield with only 63% payout ratio, and unprecedented share repurchase activity (~$800M since mid-2025) indicate a well-capitalized operator navigating a cyclical trough in its Sunbelt markets.

The central challenge is that organic growth has stalled. Same-store NOI declined 0.7% in Q1 2026 as new apartment supply in core Texas, Florida, and Southeast markets outpaced rent growth. Analyst estimates are falling across every time horizon (-31% to -43% over 90 days), and the options market is pricing near-term stress (IV 28.9% vs. 19.2% HV, with backwardation). The thesis requires patience: new multifamily construction starts collapsed in 2023-2024 as rates rose, meaning supply should thin by late 2026/2027, but the stock offers limited upside until same-store growth turns positive or the DOJ RealPage litigation resolves.

At current levels, CPT represents a reasonable income hold for investors who want Sunbelt multifamily exposure with strong downside protection (high credit quality, dividend well-covered, aggressive buybacks creating a floor). It does not offer attractive risk/reward for appreciation-focused investors until same-store growth inflects positive or the DOJ RealPage litigation resolves.

## Company Overview

Camden Property Trust is a vertically integrated multifamily REIT that owns, operates, develops, and acquires apartment communities. As of Q1 2026, the portfolio comprised 174 properties with 59,416 apartment homes across 15 high-growth markets. The geographic concentration is heavily Sunbelt: Texas (27.4% of book value), Florida (18.3%), Southeast (23.0%), with Washington D.C. metro (11.9%) and Denver/Phoenix providing diversification.

The company's average lease term of approximately 14 months means the portfolio reprices to market conditions relatively quickly — an advantage when rents are rising, but a headwind during periods of supply-driven softness like the current environment. CPT employs roughly 1,640 people and has been recognized by FORTUNE as one of the 100 Best Companies to Work For 18 consecutive years, a differentiator in an industry where property-level execution depends on employee quality.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and NOI Performance:** FY 2025 total property revenues grew 1.9% to $1.574 billion, while same-store revenue growth was a modest 0.8%. Same-store NOI grew just 0.3% as expenses (property taxes, insurance, repairs) grew 1.7%. The deterioration accelerated in Q1 2026: same-store revenue growth fell to +0.2% and same-store NOI turned negative at -0.7%. This is the first negative quarterly NOI print in the available data and represents the core fundamental challenge.

**FFO and Per-Share Metrics:** Core FFO for FY 2025 was $6.88/diluted share, essentially flat versus $6.85 in 2024. Q1 2026 Core FFO was $1.70/share (beating consensus of $1.67 by 1.8%), but full-year 2026 guidance implies a modest decline to $6.75/share at midpoint. The 6% share count reduction from buybacks ($270.7M in 2025, ~$544M through April 2026) partially masks what would otherwise be a steeper per-share decline.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $4.25 billion with a weighted average maturity of 5.4 years and weighted average interest rate of 4.1%. Interest coverage of 6.0x is declining (from 6.7x a year ago) as newer debt comes at higher rates — the February 2026 unsecured notes were issued at 4.90%. Approximately $910.7M of debt matures in the remainder of 2026, including $502M of notes, but the recent credit facility extension to March 2030 and ample liquidity ($1.2B revolver) eliminate any near-term refinancing risk. The 90.6% unencumbered asset base provides substantial financial flexibility.

**Capital Allocation:** The aggressive buyback is the standout feature. At an average repurchase price of approximately $103-110/share, management is allocating over $800M to share reduction, extraordinary for a REIT. The company simultaneously acquired $422.9M of properties in 2025 and $171.3M in April 2026 (Atlanta, Orlando), and sold $451.5M of older assets. This dual strategy of portfolio upgrading plus share count reduction on an A-rated balance sheet represents disciplined capital deployment.

**Dividend:** The $4.24 annualized dividend yields approximately 4.1% at current price. At 63% of Core FFO guidance, coverage is robust with significant cushion. The dividend appears very safe even in a moderate stress scenario.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the primary weakness. Same-store revenue growth decelerated from 0.8% (FY 2025) to 0.2% (Q1 2026), and same-store NOI has turned negative. Analyst estimates are declining sharply: current quarter EPS estimates fell 31.3% over 90 days, next quarter fell 42.8%, current year fell 42.3%, and next year fell 36.5%. This deteriorating earnings momentum directly contradicts a BUY thesis.

The growth recovery depends on the multifamily supply cycle. New apartment construction starts fell dramatically in 2023-2024 as financing costs rose, meaning deliveries should peak and begin declining by late 2026/early 2027. Management states supply is "manageable and moderating," but Q1 results suggest the competitive pressure from recent deliveries in markets like Austin, Nashville, and Orlando has not yet abated.

Demographic tailwinds remain intact: millennials and Gen Z at peak rental formation age, mortgage rates near cycle highs keeping homeownership unaffordable, and continued population migration to Sunbelt metros. These structural demand drivers should support a growth recovery once supply normalizes, but the timing remains uncertain. The development pipeline ($492M under construction, $491M in planning) positions CPT for the next upcycle but adds execution and cost risk in a period of tariff uncertainty on construction materials.

## Valuation Assessment

At $103.95, CPT trades at approximately 15.4x its 2026 Core FFO guidance midpoint of $6.75/share. Against the peer group: UDR at 25.1x P/E, MAA at 39.5x, ESS at 30.1x. The peer median is 39.5x trailing P/E, making CPT appear cheap at 29.1x trailing P/E — but this comparison is somewhat misleading because CPT's GAAP EPS ($3.54) is distorted by gains on property sales and impairments.

On an FFO basis (the appropriate metric for REITs), CPT at 15.4x Core FFO is below the apartment REIT sector average of approximately 17-20x for investment-grade operators. The discount reflects: (1) Sunbelt supply headwinds, (2) decelerating same-store growth, (3) unresolved DOJ litigation, and (4) the higher-rate environment compressing REIT multiples sector-wide.

The consensus analyst target is $113.50 median (range $102-$131), implying 9.2% upside plus the 4.1% dividend yield. With 15 of 24 analysts at HOLD, the Street consensus matches the fundamental picture: reasonably valued but lacking near-term catalysts for re-rating.

Management's aggressive buybacks at current levels imply a discount to internal NAV estimates, providing some valuation support, but without same-store growth re-acceleration, multiple expansion is unlikely.

## Competitive Landscape

CPT's competitive position within multifamily REITs is solid but not improving. The company has among the highest credit ratings in the apartment REIT sector, operates in structurally growing Sunbelt markets, and has a vertically integrated development capability.

However, competitive intensity is elevated. CPT's core markets — Houston, Dallas, Austin, Atlanta, Nashville, Orlando — have among the highest rates of new apartment supply delivery in the country. The portfolio reprices quickly (14-month average lease), which means pricing power is sensitive to local supply/demand balance. Same-store revenue growth of 0.2% against expense growth of 1.9% indicates that CPT currently lacks the pricing power to pass through cost inflation.

The active portfolio recycling (selling older Houston/Phoenix assets, buying newer Southeast properties) should improve portfolio quality over time, but near-term operational metrics reflect a company whose competitive position is being diluted by oversupply in its core geographies.

## Risk Assessment

**Supply Overhang (HIGH):** The most significant near-term risk. Texas (27.4% of book value) has been one of the most overbuilt multifamily markets in 2024-2025. If supply deliveries extend into 2027, the growth recovery thesis extends with it.

**DOJ Antitrust Litigation (MEDIUM-HIGH):** While the $53M class action settlement removes one overhang, the DOJ lawsuit (filed January 2025 with 10 states) is ongoing and potentially more consequential. Government enforcement actions can result in injunctive relief restricting revenue management software usage, structural remedies, and larger penalties. The prospective commitments in the class settlement suggest operational restrictions are likely regardless.

**Interest Rate Risk (MEDIUM):** $900.8M in floating rate debt plus $910.7M maturing in 2026. New debt at 4.90% (February notes) is higher cost than maturing obligations. Interest coverage declining from 6.7x to 6.0x. In a sustained high-rate environment, REIT cap rates remain elevated and limit property value recovery.

**Rent Control/Regulatory Risk (MEDIUM):** Multiple CPT markets (D.C., Denver, some Florida municipalities) are actively considering rent control legislation. Short lease terms (14 months) mean any control would bind quickly.

**Leadership Transition (LOW-MEDIUM):** The March 2026 CEO transition from Campo to Jessett is a planned succession of long-tenured executives, reducing execution risk. However, any new CEO's strategic direction creates uncertainty until established.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term uncertainty. Near-term IV of 28.9% is 9.7pp above the 19.2% one-year historical volatility. The term structure is in backwardation (28.9% near-term vs. 24.5% at 6 months), indicating the market expects current stress to moderate over time. Put/call volume ratio of 4.0 and OI ratio of 2.04 confirm elevated protective demand. This positioning is consistent with investors hedging downside around the supply/litigation overhang rather than expecting a specific near-term catalyst to the upside.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($120-131)
- Supply deliveries peak in H1 2026 and recede sharply by year-end, allowing same-store revenue growth to re-accelerate to 2-3% by mid-2027
- DOJ litigation settles or resolves without material operational restrictions beyond those in the class settlement
- Continued buyback activity (at $297.8M remaining + potential new authorization) reduces shares outstanding by another 3-4%, supporting per-share metrics
- Rate environment moderates, allowing cap rate compression and REIT multiple expansion from 15.4x toward sector norms of 17-19x Core FFO
- Portfolio recycling into higher-growth Southeast markets enhances organic growth trajectory

### Bear Case ($88-95)
- Supply overhang persists through 2027 as developer incentives and slow lease-ups extend competitive pressure
- DOJ reaches adverse settlement requiring material restrictions on revenue management practices, reducing CPT's pricing power permanently
- Rates remain elevated, keeping REIT multiples compressed and making the 4.1% dividend yield less competitive
- Same-store NOI remains negative for 3-4 consecutive quarters, forcing guidance cuts below $6.50 Core FFO
- Consumer weakening increases bad debt and reduces rent growth capacity

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Same-store revenue growth inflects above 1.5% for two consecutive quarters, confirming supply normalization
2. Analyst estimate revisions turn positive (currently declining 31-43%; reversal would signal consensus catching up to improved fundamentals)
3. Price decline to $95 or below without fundamental deterioration (4.5% yield, ~14x Core FFO — valuation alone would justify upgrade)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Same-store NOI declines exceed -2% for two consecutive quarters, indicating supply headwind is structural rather than cyclical
2. DOJ settlement requires operational changes that permanently impair pricing capability
3. Interest coverage falls below 4.5x, indicating balance sheet stress

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July/early August) will provide critical data on whether same-store deceleration is bottoming. The DOJ litigation timeline is less predictable but likely extends into 2027.

## Conclusion

Camden Property Trust is a high-quality apartment REIT in a cyclical trough. The A-rated balance sheet, well-covered 4.1% dividend yield, and aggressive buyback program provide downside protection, while the supply overhang and declining estimates limit near-term appreciation potential. The stock is fairly valued at 15.4x Core FFO given the current growth headwinds — cheap enough to hold for income, but not cheap enough to buy given the negative estimate momentum and unresolved litigation risk. The correct posture is to hold for income and reassess when same-store growth data inflects or valuation compresses further.
