## Executive Summary

Alcoa is the Western world's only large-scale, vertically integrated, publicly traded pure-play aluminum company, with operations spanning bauxite mining, alumina refining, and aluminum smelting across 25 sites in 8 countries. The stock has surged 160% from its April 2025 low of ~$24 to ~$63, driven by rising LME aluminum prices (up ~14% YTD), geopolitical supply tightening from the Iran conflict, and elevated U.S. Midwest premiums supported by 50% Section 232 tariffs on Canadian imports. Estimate revisions have been sharply positive: current-year EPS consensus has risen 50% over the past 90 days to $7.13.

The bull case is straightforward: aluminum supply is structurally tight (China capacity cap, low inventories, conflict-disrupted trade), Alcoa has enormous operating leverage to prices, and the tariff regime provides an effective moat for its U.S. smelting operations. The bear case centers on the Q1 2026 earnings miss ($1.40 vs. $1.54 consensus), deteriorating alumina economics (goodwill written to zero), unresolved Australian mine approvals that threaten long-term bauxite quality, persistent San Ciprián losses, and the basic cyclical reality that a 160% rally in a commodity stock priced the current aluminum environment aggressively. At 16.3x trailing earnings and ~8.8x current-year estimates, the stock is reasonably valued for this point in the cycle but offers limited margin of safety if aluminum prices soften.

## Company Overview

Alcoa operates through two segments: **Alumina** (bauxite mining and alumina refining, primarily in Australia, Brazil, and Spain) and **Aluminum** (smelting, casting, and energy generation in Canada, Iceland, Norway, the U.S., and Australia). In 2024, the company completed the acquisition of Alumina Limited, consolidating full ownership of the AWAC joint venture and simplifying its corporate structure. Alcoa produced approximately 9.6M metric tons of alumina and 2.3M metric tons of aluminum in 2025, generating $12.8B in revenue.

The company's vertically integrated model provides partial cost insulation — it mines its own bauxite, refines its own alumina, and owns significant hydroelectric capacity in Brazil, Canada, and the U.S. Its ELYSIS® joint venture with Rio Tinto is developing zero-carbon smelting technology, though commercialization remains years away.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:** Revenue grew 8% YoY to $12.8B in FY2025, driven by a 19% increase in aluminum realized prices to $3,376/mt. Net income swung from $60M (FY2024) to $1,157M, though this includes $786M from the Saudi Arabia JV sale and $197M in mark-to-market gains on Ma'aden shares. Stripping out these items, underlying profitability improved but less dramatically than headline numbers suggest. Segment Adjusted EBITDA actually declined 6% to $1,940M as the Alumina segment fell from $1,408M to $882M on lower alumina prices (API down 11%).

**Cost Structure:** Alumina adjusted operating cost rose to $317/mt (from $309/mt) on higher caustic soda and labor costs. Aluminum operating cost rose more sharply to $2,600/mt (from $2,410/mt), reflecting higher alumina input costs and tariff impacts. Section 232 tariff costs totaled $571M in 2025. Restructuring charges were substantial at $918M, primarily from the $856M Kwinana refinery permanent closure.

**Balance Sheet:** Current ratio of 1.44x, total debt of ~$2.44B against $1.60B cash (net debt ~$840M), and an undrawn $1.25B revolver. Credit ratings remain sub-investment-grade (Ba1/BB/BB+, with S&P outlook positive). Debt maturity profile is clean through 2028 following the refinancing. The Altman Z-Score of 2.04 sits in the "grey zone," reflecting the company's still-negative retained earnings (-$271M) and cyclical earnings variability. Debt-to-equity at 1.62x is elevated but manageable for a capital-intensive commodity producer.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $1,185M in FY2025 (up from $622M). Capex of $618M, yielding approximately $567M in free cash flow (~4.4% FCF margin). Capex is guided to $750M in 2026 ($675M sustaining, $75M return-seeking). The $0.40/share annual dividend ($105M) is conservatively covered. No share buybacks have been executed despite a $500M authorization since July 2022.

**Q1 2026:** Revenue of $3.19B missed the $3.27B consensus. Adjusted EPS of $1.40 missed the $1.54 estimate by 9%. Net income of $425M was up sequentially from $213M in Q4 2025. The miss after three consecutive beats introduces uncertainty about the company's ability to capture the current aluminum price environment as cleanly as consensus models assume.

**Ma'aden Exposure:** Alcoa holds 85.98M shares of Ma'aden valued at $1.4B at year-end 2025 (8.4% of Alcoa's market cap). These are marked to market through earnings, creating significant non-operational P&L volatility. A 20% decline in Ma'aden stock would produce ~$280M in pre-tax losses.

## Growth Analysis

Estimate revisions are the strongest fundamental signal: current-year EPS consensus has risen from $4.75 (90 days ago) to $7.13 today (+50%), with current-quarter estimates up 81% over the same period. This reflects elevated LME aluminum prices, tight supply conditions, and favorable tariff dynamics.

The structural growth case rests on: (1) **China's aluminum smelting capacity cap** (~45M mt) limiting global supply response to rising demand, (2) **rising demand** from EV manufacturing, renewable energy infrastructure, and packaging, (3) **Section 45X tax credits** for domestic aluminum production ($63M in FY2025 benefits), and (4) the **tariff regime** keeping Midwest premiums elevated.

However, several factors temper the growth outlook. The Alumina segment is deteriorating: goodwill was written to zero, reflecting management's own DCF models showing worsening long-term economics. Australian bauxite grade is declining as existing mine areas deplete, and new mines (Myara North, Holyoake) aren't expected to commence before 2029. Lower bauxite quality mechanically increases refining costs (more caustic soda, more energy per ton). Meanwhile, the emerging alumina surplus from Chinese and Indonesian refinery expansions is a headwind to pricing.

Next-year EPS consensus of $6.42 implies a 10% decline from FY2026's $7.13, which is consistent with cyclical peak earnings. FY2023 saw a $651M net loss from a similar starting point of elevated expectations.

## Valuation Assessment

At $62.97, Alcoa trades at 16.3x trailing EPS ($4.37) and approximately 8.8x current-year consensus EPS ($7.13). The forward multiple looks reasonable, but the trailing multiple already reflects what appears to be peak-cycle earnings. Against the peer group median P/E of 23.5x, Alcoa screens cheap, but peer comparison is limited: the peer set (IP, Ball, Amcor, IFF, Reliance) shares a sector classification but has fundamentally different business models with more stable earnings profiles. A commodity cyclical should trade at a discount to stable-margin peers.

The analyst consensus median price target of $76 implies 21% upside, with a range of $42-$92. Morgan Stanley's $80 target (Overweight) anchors the bull case, citing structural market tightness and operating leverage. The $42 low target reflects the bear-case earnings reversion scenario.

At this price, Alcoa is valued at roughly $16.6B market cap against ~$567M FY2025 FCF (3.4% FCF yield), and guided-higher 2026 capex ($750M vs. $618M) will compress FCF further. EV/EBITDA on FY2025 Segment Adjusted EBITDA of $1,940M works out to approximately 9x — fair for a commodity producer mid-cycle but not cheap given the declining Alumina segment EBITDA.

The valuation is not mispriced in either direction at current levels. The market is correctly pricing elevated aluminum prices and strong near-term earnings momentum, but also pricing limited cyclical downside, which may be too optimistic given the Q1 miss and the structural challenges in the Alumina segment.

## Competitive Landscape

Alcoa's position as the only large-scale, vertically integrated, publicly traded Western pure-play aluminum company is genuinely differentiated. Key competitors include Rio Tinto (diversified, aluminum is a minority segment), Norsk Hydro (similar integration, Norwegian-listed), Rusal (Russian, under sanctions), and Hindalco (Indian). Century Aluminum (CENX) is a smaller U.S.-focused peer.

The competitive dynamics are favorable at present: China's ~45M mt smelting cap constrains global supply growth, geopolitical disruptions have tightened markets further, and the 50% Section 232 tariff on Canadian imports inflates Midwest premiums to levels that effectively subsidize Alcoa's U.S. smelting operations (Warrick, IN and Massena, NY). Alcoa states tariff costs are "fully covered by the Midwest premium," though this dynamic is inherently dependent on policy continuity.

The company's competitive position is stable to modestly improving in the Aluminum segment but deteriorating in the Alumina segment. The declining bauxite grade in Western Australia is a structural headwind that competitors with access to higher-quality reserves don't face to the same degree.

## Risk Assessment

**Australian Mine Approvals (HIGH — Top Risk):** The strategic assessment framework agreed in February 2026 provides an 18-month national interest exemption, but the company must complete a comprehensive assessment of all current and future mine areas under the EPBC Act by August 2027. The $36M penalty for unauthorized clearing of the Northern Jarrah Forest demonstrates regulatory willingness to enforce environmental standards. If approvals for new mine areas (Myara North, Holyoake) are further delayed beyond 2029, Alcoa faces either continued cost escalation from processing lower-grade bauxite or production curtailments at its largest refining complex. This risk is partially reflected in the alumina goodwill write-down but is difficult for equity investors to quantify.

**Commodity Price Cyclicality (HIGH):** Aluminum prices ranged from $2,285 to $2,968/mt in 2025. The current elevated price environment is substantially driven by geopolitical factors (Iran conflict, Hormuz disruption) that could reverse. A diplomatic resolution bringing aluminum prices back toward $2,200-2,400/mt would compress Alcoa's aluminum segment EBITDA by 30-40%. The company does not generally hedge aluminum exposure.

**San Ciprián Losses (MODERATE):** The Spain complex lost approximately $152M in 2025 (implied from the $38M minority interest attribution). The smelter restart is targeting full capacity by mid-2026, but European energy costs remain structurally elevated. The JV partner's put option adds contingent liability risk.

**Section 232 Tariff Dependency (MODERATE):** The 50% tariff on Canadian aluminum imports is a policy decision that could be reversed, modified, or circumvented. Alcoa's economic model in the current environment depends on the tariff-inflated Midwest premium offsetting $571M in tariff costs on its own Canadian imports. Policy change would compress margins at Canadian smelters and reduce the premium benefit at U.S. operations simultaneously.

**Ma'aden P&L Volatility (MODERATE):** The $1.4B Ma'aden position flows through earnings at mark-to-market, making reported EPS an unreliable proxy for operational performance in any given quarter.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing significant near-term uncertainty. ATM IV of 79.5% is 28pp above 1-year historical volatility of 51.5%, placing it at the 88th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in steep backwardation (79.5% near-term vs. 58.9% at 6 months), indicating the market is pricing an imminent catalyst or stress event. This is consistent with the Q1 earnings miss and broader commodity price uncertainty tied to geopolitical developments. The OI put/call ratio of 0.56 (call-heavy) suggests directional positioning remains bullish, but elevated near-term IV means options premiums are expensive for establishing new positions. ATM IV has fallen from 139.7% around earnings (April 16) to 79.5% now, indicating post-earnings uncertainty is normalizing but remains elevated.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($80-92):** LME aluminum sustains above $3,000/mt through 2026 as the Iran conflict keeps supply tight, the Chinese capacity cap limits production response, and global demand from EVs and infrastructure spending grows 3-5% annually. Alcoa's operating leverage delivers EPS of $8+ in a sustained high-price environment. Section 232 tariffs remain intact, preserving Midwest premium. Australian mine approvals proceed on schedule, stabilizing bauxite quality by 2029. Morgan Stanley's $80 target reflects structural tightness; the high-end $92 target assumes a prolonged commodity supercycle.

**Bear Case ($35-42):** Diplomatic resolution of the Iran conflict sends aluminum to $2,200-2,400/mt, compressing Aluminum segment EBITDA by 30-40%. Alumina prices continue declining as Chinese/Indonesian refinery capacity comes online. Australian mine approvals are further delayed, forcing production curtailments or elevated costs. Section 232 tariffs are modified or removed under a new policy framework. Earnings revert toward $2-3/share (similar to FY2024 normalized), and the stock trades at 14-18x trough earnings, implying $35-42. The Q1 2026 miss may be the first signal of this reversion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $48-52 range (15-20% lower), providing sufficient margin of safety against commodity price reversion
2. LME aluminum price sustains above $3,200/mt for 2+ consecutive months, suggesting structural rather than geopolitical pricing
3. Australian mine approvals receive definitive regulatory clearance ahead of schedule

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. LME aluminum falls below $2,400/mt for more than 2 consecutive weeks
2. Q2 2026 EPS misses consensus by >10% for a second consecutive quarter, signaling inability to capture current price environment
3. Australian regulators reject or further delay mine area approvals beyond the August 2027 strategic assessment deadline

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (likely July 2026) and any developments on the Iran conflict that could materially alter aluminum supply dynamics.

## Conclusion

Alcoa is well positioned in a favorable commodity environment, and estimate revision momentum is strong. The structural tightness in global aluminum supports elevated pricing through at least the near term. However, the stock has already captured much of this move with a 160% rally, the most recent quarter missed estimates, the Alumina segment is deteriorating structurally, and the Australian mine approvals represent a material unresolved risk. At ~9x current-year EPS, the valuation is fair for mid-cycle but offers limited margin of safety if aluminum prices revert even modestly. The recommendation remains HOLD, consistent with the prior three assessments. The improving score trajectory reflects genuinely better aluminum market conditions, but the Q1 miss and the magnitude of the rally since the 52-week low argue against upgrading at this price level. A pullback to the $48-52 range would meaningfully improve the risk/reward.
