## Executive Summary

Goodyear is a levered, contracting, high-fixed-cost manufacturer whose equity represents roughly 24% of an $8.3B enterprise value. That single structural fact governs everything else: at 3.6x net debt to a constructed TTM adjusted-EBITDA proxy of ~$1,760M, a 15% swing in the enterprise value moves the equity by roughly 60%. The company completed a $2.2B asset-sale program in 2025 and used the proceeds to repay $1,759M of debt net. By March 31, 2026 it had drawn $530M on the first-lien revolver and $403M on the European revolver — both at zero on December 31, 2025 — with gross debt up $787M in the quarter and total unused credit availability down $1,446M. The deleveraging was event-driven. There is no second $2.2B of non-core assets.

The operating break came in Q1 2026. Worldwide units fell 11.6%, replacement volume fell 17.8% globally and 23.2% in the Americas, and segment operating income fell to $95M from $195M — a 2.4% margin against 4.6% a year earlier and 5.8% for FY2025. The mechanism is explicit in the MD&A: conversion costs were a $155M headwind from "the effect of lower tire production on fixed cost absorption," against only +$15M from price/mix. For two years Goodyear traded volume for price and the trade paid; in Q1 the unabsorbed overhead on the volume forgone exceeded the margin captured on the volume retained. Management guided ~$90M of unabsorbed overhead for Q2 2026 alone and said in both the 10-K and 10-Q that it has "limited visibility to global tire unit volumes."

The prior three reviews rated GT AVOID at 3.5, 3.6 and 3.6. The score moves down to 3.25 not because the stock fell — it is up 14% from $6.19 at the June 18 review — but because the forward earnings base has been cut hard while the price rose. Current-year consensus swung from +$0.46 to -$0.53 over 90 days; next-year fell 38.6% to $0.65; every one of the four forecast periods was revised down again in the last 30 days. Two genuine positives arrived since June: the July 31 tentative USW master agreement removes near-term U.S. strike risk, and the July 16 Fayetteville closure — approved *with* the union — targets ~$90M of 2027 and ~$270M of annual 2028+ Americas benefit. Both matter. Neither answers the question of how a business generating negative free cash flow funds $535-565M of restructuring charges through 2027 while $820M of 2027 notes and a $200M Mexican facility (November 2026) sit inside current liabilities.

## Company Overview

Goodyear operates 49 manufacturing facilities in 19 countries across three geographic segments, plus company-owned retail service, aviation/race tires, retreading and commercial fleet solutions. FY2025 net sales were $18,280M on 158.7M units, down 4.7% in volume. Consumer tires were $12,234M and commercial $3,124M; original equipment was ~19% of sales, with the balance replacement and services. The United States is ~85% of Americas sales; China 44% and India 21% of Asia Pacific.

"Goodyear Forward," launched 2023 and declared complete in 2025, generated ~$2.2B of gross divestiture proceeds across three transactions. The retained obligations are where the economics live. The off-the-road business went to Yokohama for ~$905M, with Goodyear receiving trademark royalties and *supplying* OTR tires to Yokohama for up to five years — Yokohama is now a Goodyear customer for that product. The polymer chemicals business went to G-3 Chickadee for $650M, and Goodyear entered a **15-year master supply agreement requiring minimum quarterly purchases or a shortfall fee** — here Goodyear is the payer. That obligation was created precisely as unit volumes began contracting at double-digit rates, and the 10-K does not disclose the minimum quantities or the shortfall formula.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Net sales have fallen 12.1% from the 2022 peak of $20,805M. FY2025's $598M decline decomposes to divestitures −$671M (net of +$268M of new product-supply revenue), volume −$669M, FX −$18M, price/mix +$370M and Goodyear Forward +$64M, so organic revenue ex-divestitures was down only ~1.3%. Q1 2026 sales fell 8.7% to $3,881M.

The FY2025 net loss of $1,721M was almost entirely non-cash and should be decomposed rather than annualized. Pretax loss was $133M. Against that sat $1,567M of tax expense, of which $1,453M was net discrete, driven mainly by a full valuation allowance on ~$1.4B of U.S. net deferred tax assets; a $674M complete write-off of North America goodwill; and $201M of pension settlement charges. Partly offsetting were $816M of asset-sale gains. Goodwill fell from $756M to $42M; shareholders' equity from $4,681M to $3,233M and to $3,004M at March 31, 2026.

The valuation allowance carries a forward-looking statement that is more informative than any guidance in the file. The Q1 10-Q says: "We do not believe that sufficient positive evidence required to release valuation allowances on our U.S. and foreign deferred tax assets will exist within the next twelve months." Foreign net DTAs of ~$1.5B carry a ~$1.3B allowance, including a $1.1B full allowance in Luxembourg. The mechanical consequence is cash tax on profitable jurisdictions with no offset for U.S. and Luxembourg losses — $66M of tax expense on a $180M pretax loss in Q1 2026, and $150-175M of cash taxes guided for the year.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 reported operating cash flow was $796M against $826M of capex, for free cash flow of −$30M. That figure is flattered: MD&A attributes $486M of the operating cash flow to deferred revenue and deferred income received under the trademark license, product supply and transition support agreements. Removing it gives operating cash flow of ~$310M and free cash flow of approximately **−$516M**. The Q1 2026 variance discussion corroborates the non-recurring character by citing $185M of that same item as a year-over-year headwind.

Q1 2026 operating cash flow was −$718M with $175M of capex. Q1 is structurally Goodyear's cash trough (Q1 2025 was −$538M) and $650M of working-capital build should substantially reverse, so this quarter cannot be annualized. Two things distinguish it from the prior year: the one-time divestiture cash is gone, and segment operating income is $100M lower.

Goodyear has published component cash requirements for 2026 but no free-cash-flow guidance: capex ~$725M (cut from ~$825M), interest ~$425M, rationalization payments ~$225M, cash taxes $150-175M, pension ~$25M, and working capital a ~$100M source. Building from the TTM adjusted-EBITDA proxy of ~$1,760M (TTM segment operating income $957M + TTM D&A $1,014M − ~$210M of annualized corporate costs), a stabilization case lands near breakeven; a case in which Q1's ~50% year-over-year segment-income decline persists lands at −$270M to −$500M; and a bottom-up build from Q1's actual ex-working-capital cash generation lands near −$895M. These approaches do not reconcile tightly — roughly $135M is unbridgeable between the segment-income-derived math and reported cash flow, likely accrual and payment timing. Treat the range as the finding: **FY2026 free cash flow plausibly −$300M to −$800M**, funded by borrowing, and the $225M rationalization guide predates Fayetteville.

**Balance sheet and liquidity.** Total debt at March 31, 2026 was $6,985M ($483M notes payable and overdrafts, $1,226M current long-term debt and finance leases, $5,276M non-current), versus $6,198M at year-end. Net debt of $6,262M against $723M of cash; adding $1,047M of operating lease liabilities gives ~$7,309M of lease-adjusted obligations. Net debt to the adjusted-EBITDA proxy is ~3.6x — not extreme for a tire manufacturer in isolation, but the composition of liquidity is the issue.

Only $113M of the $723M cash sits at the Parent and guarantor subsidiaries; ~$187M sits in jurisdictions with transfer requirements (China, South Africa, Serbia, Argentina). The first-lien facility carries a springing covenant — if Available Cash plus first-lien availability falls below $275M, EBITDA/interest must be at least 2.0x. That sum was $1,826M, so the test is remote, but revolver availability is doing essentially all the work, and availability is a borrowing-base function of eligible receivables, inventory, appraised trademark value, machinery and equipment. Closing plants reduces eligible machinery. The $505M base erosion is unexplained by receivables and inventory, both of which rose, and remains unattributed.

The tighter-looking constraint is at Goodyear Europe B.V., which must keep net debt/EBITDA at or below 3.0x under both the European revolver and the pan-European receivables securitization, where breach is an Early Amortisation Event. Goodyear states compliance at March 31, 2026. With EMEA segment operating income of $1M in the quarter, $114M for all of FY2025, and $403M now drawn, headroom cannot be assessed from the disclosure — GEBV standalone EBITDA is not published. This is the single most important undisclosed number in the capital structure.

Liability management has been competent: the first-lien revolver was extended from June 2026 to May 2030 and the European securitization from October 2027 to October 2032. Obligations due within one year total $1,709M, including $700M of 4.875% and $120M of 7.625% notes due 2027 and the $200M Mexican facility maturing November 22, 2026.

Capex of $725M guided against TTM D&A of $1,014M is a 0.71x ratio — the asset base is shrinking — set against management's own risk-factor admission that its capex/sales ratio is below peers. No dividend and no repurchases since at least 2023, which is the correct choice at this leverage. Dilution is immaterial: maximum near-term dilution from all outstanding awards is roughly 4M shares, ~1.4% of the count.

## Growth Analysis

There is no revenue growth to analyze; there is a volume contraction to diagnose. The most decision-relevant unresolved question in the entire file is the decomposition of the 23.2% Americas replacement decline into industry contraction, share loss, and deliberate lower-tier SKU exit. Management cites all three. That decomposition determines whether Q1 2026 was a trough or a new baseline, and it is not resolvable from the filings.

What the evidence does support:

- **OE volume grew 3.4% globally** (Americas +8.2%, EMEA +8.1% on new fitments). Today's OE fitments seed replacement demand in three to five years, so this is a real forward asset even though OE is only ~19% of sales and carries lower margin.
- **Asia Pacific is genuinely performing** — 12.5% operating margin in Q1 2026, up from 9.5%, on modestly lower sales. It is 11% of revenue and cannot carry the consolidated result.
- **Price/mix has traction**: +$465M to FY2025 segment operating income, +$370M to net sales, +$36M in EMEA in Q1 2026. The premium pivot is commercially real. It contributed only +$15M consolidated in Q1, however, which is the point of failure.
- **The 10-K states Goodyear is at times capacity constrained on higher-margin large-rim tires, particularly in the U.S.** That juxtaposition with a 1,750-person plant closure supports reading Fayetteville as a mix decision rather than pure retrenchment.

Against that, consensus is falling at a rate that overwhelms the qualitative positives. Current-quarter estimates went from −$0.08 to −$0.62 in 90 days; current-year from +$0.46 to −$0.53; next-year from $1.06 to $0.65. All four periods were cut again in the last 30 days. This is the mirror image of the inflection signal that would justify looking through a high trailing multiple: estimates are collapsing while the share price rises.

The self-help arithmetic is nonetheless the strongest bull input. Fayetteville targets ~$270M of annual Americas benefit from 2028 against FY2025 Americas segment operating income of $735M — a ~37% uplift on a segment that generated a 1.8% margin last quarter. Combined with ~$325M of incremental 2026 Goodyear Forward savings and the EMEA sales-and-distribution plan ($100-110M charge, ~400 heads), a 2028 earnings power well above current consensus is arithmetically constructible. The path runs through two years of cash outflow.

On every static measure the stock is cheap: below tangible book, 4.7x EV/EBITDA, 10.9x next-year earnings against a 13.5x peer median. That cheapness is the value-trap configuration rather than the mispricing configuration, for three specific reasons.

First, the multiple is applied to an EBITDA base that is falling and to an equity stub that absorbs all of the variance. Holding net debt at $6.6B and applying 5.5x, a $1.55B EBITDA supports about $6.60 of equity value; $1.3B supports about $3.15; $2.0B supports about $16. The distribution of outcomes is nearly binary and the leverage sits against the holder, not for them.

Second, the cheapness is not accompanied by a catalyst that resolves inside the funding window. The catalyst that exists — Fayetteville — costs $535-565M in 2026-27 to deliver its benefit in 2028, and the cash/non-cash split of those charges is not disclosed. A material share is asset write-downs and pension termination benefits, so the true cash burden is likely well below $535M, but "likely well below" is not a number an investor can underwrite when parent-level available cash is $113M.

Third, book value provides weaker support than it appears. Equity of $3,004M already absorbs −$3,569M of accumulated other comprehensive loss including −$1,904M of unrecognized pension items, and $435M of indefinite-lived Cooper Tire intangibles were tested at FY2025 year-end with fair values that only "approximated" carrying value. The Q1 10-Q explicitly warns that "a continued and sustained decline in volumes" could require "a material, non-cash impairment charge in a future period." With units down 11.6%, tangible book of $8.01 has a visible path lower.

Analyst targets bracket the price tightly: low $6.60, mean $7.46, median $7.00, high $9.00, with a 1/2/6/1 strong-buy/buy/hold/sell distribution that has not moved in three months. On a stock with collapsing estimates and negative free cash flow, consensus targets clustered at the current price are not a floor — they reflect the same uncertainty the rest of the file expresses.

Valuation scores 4.0. There is real option value in a 24% equity stub with a credible $270M self-help program, which is why this is not a 2 or 3. But cheap-and-deteriorating with a financing dependency is the configuration that historically destroys capital.

## Competitive Landscape

The industry structure is a barbell and Goodyear sits in the compressed middle. Two risk-factor statements in the FY2025 10-K are unusually candid: "We continue to believe that our manufacturing footprint is **less cost-competitive** than that of our principal competitors," and "We believe that our ratio of capital expenditures to sales is **lower** than the comparable ratio for our principal competitors." Those are self-asserted filing facts about a company competing against Bridgestone and Michelin at the top.

At the bottom, low-cost imports have taken enough share to force a documented sequence of write-downs: a $125M impairment of lower-tier indefinite-lived Cooper intangibles in FY2024 explicitly attributed to lower-tier import competition, the complete $674M write-off of North America goodwill in FY2025, and $435M more of Cooper intangibles now carried at approximately fair value. The FY2025 10-K attributes the 4.3% Americas replacement volume decline to "increased competitiveness in the U.S. from the lower tier market." Goodyear's answer is to exit lower-tier SKUs deliberately and shrink the footprint — coherent strategy, but it concedes the low end permanently.

The brand retains value (NASCAR exclusivity, LMGT3/LMP2 endurance contracts through 2029), Asia Pacific margins are expanding, and Goodyear is the only remaining U.S.-based tire manufacturer. That last position produces a paradox worth stating precisely: tariffs are a ~$300M annual *cost* to Goodyear, not a benefit, because it imports finished tires from its own foreign plants and roughly two-thirds of raw materials are petroleum-based commodities brought into the U.S. Goodyear sued to overturn the IEEPA tariffs and won at the Supreme Court on February 20, 2026, recognizing a $60M estimated refund. Trajectory is what matters for scoring, and the trajectory — share ceded at the low end, cost position conceded at the high end, capex below peers — is negative. Score 3.5.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Negative free cash flow re-levering a just-de-levered balance sheet.** $787M of gross debt added and $1,446M of availability consumed in one quarter, against a plausible FY2026 free cash outflow of $300-800M and $535-565M of Fayetteville charges through 2027.

**2. Fixed-cost absorption in a shrinking volume base.** Conversion costs were a $402M FY2025 headwind and $155M in Q1 2026 alone, with ~$90M of unabsorbed overhead guided for Q2. The 10-K's statement that "operating income fluctuates disproportionately with changes in our net sales" is now the operative risk.

**3. A pre-flagged non-cash impairment.** $435M of Cooper intangibles at fair value approximating carrying value, with the 10-Q explicitly warning of a material charge on continued volume decline. Hits book equity and GAAP earnings, not liquidity or covenants.

**4. GEBV covenant opacity.** A ≤3.0x net debt/EBITDA test in both the European revolver and the securitization, with EMEA segment income of $1M last quarter and $403M drawn. Breach of the securitization test is an Early Amortisation Event. Compliance is stated; headroom is undisclosed.

**5. Structurally punitive cash taxes.** $150-175M guided for 2026 with full U.S. and Luxembourg valuation allowances and no expected release within twelve months.

**6. EU antitrust investigation.** European Commission unannounced inspections since January 30, 2024 regarding replacement tires in the EEA. No reserve, no charges, unquantified. The parallel U.S. civil MDL has gone Goodyear's way — dismissal February 25, 2025, and leave to amend denied March 31, 2026.

**7. Interim CFO into the hardest execution window.** Christina Zamarro departed per a June 26, 2026 Item 5.02 filing; Scott Deakin is interim, corroborated by his July 1 Form 4 listing him as "Interim EVP & CFO." Six separate Item 5.02 8-Ks were filed during 2025. The Q1 10-Q was signed by the VP and Controller as principal accounting officer.

**8. Non-U.S. labor.** The U.S. USW master agreement reached tentative agreement July 31, 2026 (terms unreleased), but ~19,000 associates outside the U.S. are covered by contracts that expired or expire in 2026 across ten countries.

**9. IRS Notice of Proposed Adjustment** proposing to disallow $1.5B of income from a 2021 intercompany IP sale. Goodyear states a write-off would not materially affect results of operations because the offsetting DTAs are already fully allowanced; cash tax, interest and penalty exposure is not quantified.

**10. Chemical take-or-pay.** A 15-year minimum-purchase obligation with undisclosed quantities and shortfall formula, created as volumes contract. FY2025 binding commitments total $5,520M with $1,517M due in 2026.

Contingent liabilities look contained: product liability reserves $428M, asbestos gross accrual $107M against a $57M insurance receivable with ~30,550 pending claims declining and only $4M of quarterly cash outflow, workers' comp $146M, environmental $79M.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 76.6% against 48.4% one-year realized — an 85th-percentile reading within the current chain and rich by 28 points. The term structure is in backwardation (76.6% at one month versus 56.9% at six), which is consistent with the August 5 Q2 print carrying the event risk; the nearest expiry prices a ±13.5% move. Put/call skew is described as balanced at −1.0% for the front month, so the pricing is about magnitude rather than direction. Open-interest put/call has risen to 1.09 from 0.08 at the prior snapshot, indicating protection has been accumulated, while front-month volume put/call of 0.81 is call-heavy — positioning is genuinely two-sided into the print. The practical implication for a bear is that the obvious short expression is expensive; the implication for a bull is that the market is not pricing a benign quarter. Short interest of 17.3% of float with 3.3 days to cover completes the picture: crowded enough to squeeze on any volume stabilization, liquid enough not to trap.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $12.00 (+70%)

Q1 2026 was the trough, and the −23.2% Americas replacement print reflected industry destocking plus Goodyear's own deliberate lower-tier SKU exit rather than durable share loss. Working capital reverses through H2, delivering the guided +$100M full-year source. Raw materials contribute ~$100M in Q2. The Fayetteville charges are more non-cash and pension-funded than the headline $535-565M suggests, so the cash bill is manageable inside existing $2,975M availability. The USW agreement lands on acceptable economics. Fixed-cost leverage that ran violently against the company in Q1 runs equally violently in reverse: with ~$325M of 2026 Goodyear Forward savings, ~$90M of 2027 and ~$270M of 2028+ Americas benefit, and OE fitment wins seeding replacement demand, adjusted EBITDA rebuilds toward $2.0B by 2028. At 5.5x with net debt reduced to $6.3B, that is roughly $16 of equity value; a $12 target discounts execution and timing. Asia Pacific at 12.5% margins and a genuine premium-mix franchise support the re-rating. This is the shape of the outcome the single published $9.00 buy target implies.

### Bear Case — $3.50 (−50%)

Q1 was the new baseline. The Americas replacement decline is structural share loss to low-cost imports, confirmed by the sequence of write-downs already taken. FY2026 free cash flow lands near −$700M, funded entirely by revolver draws, and the borrowing base continues eroding as plant closures shrink eligible machinery and receivables shrink with volume. A $435M Cooper intangible impairment takes tangible book to roughly $6.50. GEBV's 3.0x covenant tightens as European EBITDA approaches zero, forcing either an equity-funded repayment or a securitization amortisation event. The 2027 note maturities ($820M) and the November 2026 Mexican facility have to be refinanced with bonds already trading at 93.8% of carrying value. Restructuring cash of ~$550M competes with $425M of interest and $725M of capex. Cash taxes of $150-175M are paid on consolidated losses. At $1.3B of EBITDA and 6.0x, the equity is worth roughly $3.15, and a dilutive equity raise at a depressed price is the mechanism that gets it there.

The bear case does not require anything new to happen. It requires Q1's run-rate to persist for four quarters.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** Q2 2026 results after the close on **August 5, 2026** (call August 6), containing $205-225M of Fayetteville charges, ~$90M of guided unabsorbed overhead, and the first read on whether Q1's $650M working-capital build reversed. Beyond that, the Q3 print (further $65-85M of Fayetteville charges) and the November 22, 2026 Mexican facility maturity.

**Timing window.** One day. That is the core problem — the catalyst resolves immediately with no room to build a position, and the two largest components of the bad news (the restructuring charge and the overhead) are already disclosed. Q2 also carries a ~$100M raw-material tailwind and the probable working-capital reversal.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 17.3% of float short, 38.8M shares, 3.3 days to cover. Below the 25% crowded threshold but well above normal. With a 24% equity stub, any evidence of volume stabilization or a Fayetteville cash-split disclosure below expectations produces a 20-30% move against the short. The stock has already demonstrated this: +14% since June 18 on falling estimates.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options are actively hostile to the trade. Front-month IV of 76.6% versus 48.4% realized means puts embed a ±13.5% expected move; skew is balanced rather than put-biased; front-month volume put/call of 0.81 is call-heavy. Paying an 85th-percentile IV for a one-day catalyst whose main content is pre-announced is negative expectancy. Selling calls or buying put spreads improves the economics but not enough to change the verdict.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target $3.50 (−50%) over 12-18 months; near-term downside on a bad print is perhaps $5.75-6.00 (−15 to −18%). Upside risk on a benign print or an announced volume stabilization is $9.00 (+27%), with a further tail from strategic interest in a sub-tangible-book asset. Near-term reward:risk is roughly 1:1 against rich option premium and a 17.3% short base.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The bearish fundamental case is sound and the balance sheet is genuinely stressed, but the timing is wrong: the catalyst is one day away, its principal negative content is already public, Q2 carries two mechanical tailwinds (raw materials, working-capital reversal), the crowd is already positioned, and the option market charges an 85th-percentile premium to express the view. Avoid owning it; do not pay to be short it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.** Fourth consecutive AVOID. The bar for repeating a call is higher, and the justification here is that the two developments that improved the story since June 18 (USW tentative agreement, Fayetteville approval) are outweighed by a 90-day consensus swing from +$0.46 to −$0.53 for the current year, a $787M single-quarter increase in gross debt, and a $1,446M reduction in available liquidity — all while the share price rose 14%. Risk/reward deteriorated on both the numerator and the denominator.

**Re-entry criteria — specific and checkable:**

1. **Two consecutive quarters of Americas replacement volume down less than 5% year over year**, with management explicitly separating industry contraction from deliberate SKU exit. This is the single input that determines whether Q1 2026 was a trough or a baseline, and it is checkable in the Q3 2026 and Q4 2026 MD&A.
2. **One quarter of positive free cash flow with segment operating margin at or above 4.5%**, demonstrating that unabsorbed overhead is reversing rather than compounding. Q2's ~$90M of guided overhead makes this a Q4 2026 or 2027 test.
3. **Disclosure of the Fayetteville cash-versus-non-cash split, and of GEBV headroom against the 3.0x covenant.** Both are currently unknowable from the filings and both are load-bearing for solvency analysis. A cash share materially below $300M plus demonstrated European headroom would remove the two largest unquantified risks.
4. **A permanent CFO appointed**, with the first Form 4 showing open-market purchase rather than grant. Across a share-price range from $8.25 down to $5.64 and back, every insider transaction in the trailing record was a grant, an RSU/PSU conversion, or a tax withholding. Not a single open-market purchase. That is not proof of a bearish internal view, but it removes the strongest available bullish confirmation.
5. **Price basis for reconsidering the option value: $4.50-5.00.** At that level the equity stub is ~$1.3-1.45B against the same enterprise, and the ~$270M 2028 Americas benefit is worth materially more per share of downside risked. Buying a 24% residual claim at 0.88x tangible book with tangible book itself at impairment risk is not adequate compensation; at roughly 0.55-0.60x it begins to be.

**Timeline.** The headwinds persist through 2027. Fayetteville's benefit is ~$90M in 2027 and ~$270M annually from 2028, while its cash outflow largely completes by end-2027. That is the earliest plausible window in which the operating and financing lines cross. **Next reassessment: the August 5, 2026 Q2 print**, which supersedes several elements of this analysis — specifically the working-capital reversal, revolver balances, borrowing-base movement, and any Cooper intangible impairment.

**What would change this to a positive view faster than expected:** disclosure that Q2 free cash flow was positive with revolver balances repaid, combined with Americas replacement volume down single digits. That combination would falsify the core bear mechanism and justify revisiting immediately.

## Conclusion

Goodyear's problem is arithmetic, not narrative. The equity is 24% of the enterprise, so every dollar of EBITDA variance is levered roughly four times into the share price, and EBITDA has just fallen by half on a year-over-year basis in the most recent quarter. The company sold $2.2B of assets, repaid $1,759M of debt, and within one quarter had drawn $933M on two revolvers that had been undrawn, with unused availability down $1,446M and only $113M of cash sitting at the entities that guarantee the debt. Management has told investors in writing that it does not expect U.S. profitability sufficient to release the valuation allowance within twelve months, and that it has limited visibility to global tire volumes.

The self-help path is real. A ~$270M annual Americas benefit from 2028 against a segment that earned $735M in FY2025 is a meaningful uplift, the union has agreed to the plant closure, OE volume is growing, Asia Pacific is expanding margins, and the balance sheet has been term-extended to 2030 and 2032 on its two most important facilities. If Q1 2026 was the trough, the fixed-cost leverage that destroyed the quarter reverses and the equity is worth multiples of $7.06.

That is a financing bet on a two-year bridge, priced at 0.88x a tangible book value that carries a pre-flagged impairment, with consensus estimates falling 15-19% in the last 30 days alone, no permanent CFO, no insider buying across a 47% drawdown, and 17.3% of the float already short. **AVOID.** The 4.7x EV/EBITDA multiple is not the reason to own this; the ~$300-800M of 2026 free cash outflow that has to be borrowed is the reason not to.
