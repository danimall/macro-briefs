**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Bank OZK is a $40.8 billion regional bank built around a single high-conviction franchise: large national commercial real estate (CRE) construction lending through its Real Estate Specialties Group (RESG). That concentration produced a long record of disciplined underwriting (company-wide net charge-offs at or below 0.50% every year from 2011 through 2024) and above-average returns. It is now producing the central tension in the stock. Net charge-offs spiked to 1.18% in Q4 2025, the highest since the financial-crisis era, and nonperforming assets rose to 1.08% of the portfolio in Q1 2026, roughly double a year earlier and still climbing. Earnings have gone flat (FY2025 net income $699.3M versus FY2024 $700.3M), and Q1 2026 net income of $159.3M missed slightly, down 6.8% year-over-year.

The bull case rests on one fact: management deliberately doubled the allowance for credit losses from roughly $300M in mid-2022 to $632M at year-end 2025, ahead of the deterioration. Because losses were pre-funded, the Q4 2025 charge-off spike hit the income statement softly without a large parallel reserve build. If the CRE downturn is in its "late innings" as CEO George Gleason argues, with recovery into 2027, the bank is positioned to harvest a thin-priced, over-reserved book at a stock trading at 8.2x earnings and yielding 3.8%.

The bear case is equally concrete. NPAs are still rising while megabank peers (BofA, PNC, Wells) are reducing problem CRE faster, suggesting OZK is later in its cleanup. The problem credits are concentrated in office and life sciences, two of the most distressed CRE subsectors, and the bulk of past-dues sit in just five loans whose outcomes hinge on binary asset sales and recapitalizations currently in process. Consensus EPS estimates have been cut across every forward period, and the bank is voluntarily shrinking its highest-margin business (real estate from 54% to 52% of loans) into rising competition and surging repayments. We rate OZK a HOLD. The valuation is genuinely cheap, but the discount reflects real concentration and cycle risk rather than an identifiable market error.

## Company Overview

Bank OZK, headquartered in Little Rock, Arkansas, is a regional bank with approximately $40.8 billion in total assets at year-end 2025. It has been led by Chairman and CEO George Gleason since 1979, a tenure of more than 45 years that is inseparable from the company's identity and underwriting culture.

The defining feature is RESG, which originates large national CRE construction and development loans. RESG competes on speed, certainty of execution, and structuring expertise rather than on price, and it has historically commanded high margins. This is the company's moat and its dominant risk in equal measure. After a multi-year de-risking effort, real estate fell to 52% of total loans in Q1 2026 from 54% in Q4 2025, as management deliberately shrinks CRE exposure and grows other lending lines.

A material caveat on this report: standard bank metrics that normally anchor regional-bank analysis (net interest margin, efficiency ratio, CET1 and other regulatory capital ratios, deposit mix and cost of funds, tangible book value, and the formal dividend/buyback policy) could not be verified against primary SEC filings during research. The analysis below leans on financial-press reporting of the quarterly results, and the capital and margin conclusions should be confirmed against the FY2025 10-K and the latest 10-Q before any position is sized.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability is flat and resilient, not growing.** Q4 2025 net income of $171.9M fell 3.5% year-over-year. Reported EPS is $6.18 trailing. Expenses rose roughly 12% quarter-over-quarter in Q1 2026 on higher compensation and operating costs, pressuring the efficiency profile even as revenue stayed flat.

**Credit quality is the entire story, and it is deteriorating.** The Q4 2025 net charge-off spike was driven by four previously criticized or classified loans. The Q1 2026 ratio came back to 0.57%, well off the spike but more than double the 0.25% of a year earlier. NPAs rose from 0.99% in Q4 2025. Five loans accounted for $409.5M of past-dues, with problems concentrated in office and life sciences. As of the Q1 print, two of those loans were in a sale process, one in negotiation, and two being recapitalized, outcomes that are binary and headline-sensitive.

**The reserve build is the genuine cushion.** The deliberate multi-year build ran ahead of expected CRE stress. The crux of the "no cause for alarm" narrative is that the Q4 2025 charge-off spike drew on existing reserves while the full-year ACL still grew by roughly $12M. Whether the cushion is adequate from here depends on whether NPAs are near a peak or still building, and the Q1 data points to the latter.

**Loan production and repayments are working against the book.** RESG originations were volatile across recent quarters ($700M in Q3 2025, $1.61B in Q4 2025, $1.0B in Q1 2026 — the slowest Q1 in five years), while repayments rose to $1.6B in Q1 2026, up $850M year-over-year, as borrowers refinance into a more liquid debt market. Management guides to more than $5B of 2026 RESG originations, roughly flat with the prior two years. A high-yield book that is shrinking through accelerated payoffs is a headwind to net interest income before the new diversification lines scale.

We score Financial Health 5.5. The reserve foresight is a real positive and the long charge-off track record argues against a disorderly outcome, but rising NPAs, flat earnings, cost inflation, and the inability to verify capital and margin metrics cap the score below the prior 6.0.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the weakest part of the case and the dimension that most reliably sorts winners from losers. The signals here are negative across the board.

Consensus estimates are falling. Current-year EPS has been revised to $5.93 from $5.99 ninety days ago, next-year EPS to $6.42 from $6.47 ninety days ago, and next-quarter EPS to $1.50 from $1.52 ninety days ago. The revision trend is broadly negative across every forward period, the opposite of the rising-estimate momentum that precedes re-rating. The stock has rallied roughly 9% over the trailing window while estimates were being cut, the unfavorable form of price/fundamental divergence.

The engine of historical growth, high-margin RESG originations, is being deliberately reduced. Diversification away from CRE concentration is prudent risk management, but it shrinks the highest-margin niche before replacement lines reach scale, creating a multi-year transition during which earnings growth is likely to stay muted. Repayments running at $1.6B per quarter and the slowest Q1 origination in five years compound the drag. Management's qualitative optimism about 2027 is not yet visible in either the production numbers or the estimate revisions.

We score Growth Prospects 4.5, unchanged from the prior read. There is no identifiable near-term growth catalyst, and consensus is moving the wrong direction.

## Valuation Assessment

On headline metrics OZK looks inexpensive. It trades at 8.2x trailing and forward earnings against a regional-bank peer median of 13.7x (HOMB 11.5x, AUB 16.8x, ABCB 13.7x, HWC 14.7x, AX 10.9x), and yields 3.8% versus a peer median of 2.8%. That is a roughly 40% earnings-multiple discount and a yield premium.

The question is whether that discount is a mispricing or a justified reflection of risk. The evidence points to the latter. A cheap multiple at a CRE-concentrated lender whose NPAs are rising, whose estimates are being cut, and whose problem credits sit in office and life sciences is the signature of a value trap absent a specific catalyst that resolves the concern. The peers trading at higher multiples (HWC, ABCB, AUB) carry more diversified, lower-concentration books; the closest comparables on multiple are AX (10.9x, also a higher-risk specialty lender) and HOMB (11.5x). On that adjusted basis OZK's discount narrows considerably and is largely explained by RESG concentration and the open question of whether the reserve build is sufficient.

The discount is not without support. Cycle-adjusted earnings power is meaningfully above the current trough if Gleason's 2027 recovery thesis holds, and the 3.8% yield provides a floor for an income-oriented holder. But the catalysts that would close the discount (favorable resolution of the five problem loans, a confirmed CRE recovery, an NPA peak) are binary and not yet visible in the data. We score Valuation 5.5, down from the prior 7.0. The cheapness is real but prices genuine risk rather than flagging a clear error.

## Competitive Landscape

RESG is a real moat. Speed, certainty of execution, and structuring capability on large, complex national construction projects are difficult to replicate, and the franchise's underwriting discipline through prior cycles is genuine. The problem is that the moat is most valuable when capital is scarce, and the CRE debt market is re-liquefying.

The industry backdrop is turning cautiously positive: U.S. Bancorp reported its first linked-quarter CRE growth in nearly three years, M&T expected CRE growth to resume in Q2 2026, and unfunded CRE commitments rose for a second consecutive quarter per the St. Louis Fed, reversing nine quarters of decline. For OZK this is a mixed blessing. Returning capital compresses the structuring edge on new deals, intensifies competition, and accelerates the repayments that are shrinking the book. The same liquidity that signals industry recovery erodes the conditions under which OZK's moat earns its premium.

There is also a relative-positioning concern. Megabank peers are reducing problem CRE faster (BofA nonperforming commercial loans down 44% year-over-year, PNC down 26%, Wells down 2.6%) while OZK's NPAs are still rising, indicating OZK is later in its credit cleanup. We score Competitive Position 5.0. The structural moat is intact, but its trajectory is softening at the margin in a re-liquefying market, and the bank is voluntarily reducing its strongest business.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE / construction concentration (dominant).** Even after diversification, roughly 52% of loans are real estate, heavily weighted to large national construction projects. This concentration drove the Q4 2025 charge-off spike and the doubling of NPAs.

**Idiosyncratic large-loan risk.** The credit picture is hostage to a small number of large projects whose outcomes turn on asset sales and recapitalizations in process — lumpy, binary, and headline-driving.

**Cycle-timing risk.** Management is explicitly betting the downturn is in its late innings with a 2027 recovery. If rates stay higher-for-longer or CRE distress deepens, the reserve cushion could prove insufficient and the "isolated losses" thesis fails. Management has cited tariff-driven materials-cost spikes pushing the construction backlog to a four-year low, a demand/origination risk for the core franchise, which leaves little margin for the optimistic timing case.

**Margin and repayment pressure.** Surging liquidity is compressing the structuring edge and accelerating repayments, shrinking the high-yield RESG book and pressuring net interest income.

**Key-person and governance risk.** Gleason's 45-year tenure is central to the underwriting culture; succession is a material governance consideration. Insider transaction data and the proxy could not be reviewed.

**Data verification gap.** Capital ratios, NIM, efficiency ratio, deposit mix, and tangible book value could not be confirmed against primary filings. For a bank, these determine resilience. This unverified base raises the uncertainty around any conclusion.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-dated ATM IV of 27.5% sits slightly above one-year historical volatility of 24.9% (a fair-to-mildly-rich 2.6-point spread), but the term structure is in backwardation (1-month 27.5% versus 6-month 24.2%), which typically signals an imminent catalyst rather than steady-state concern. The 1-month put/call skew of 4.8% reflects elevated put demand, and the put/call open-interest ratio jumped from 0.42 to 0.97 since the prior snapshot as the stock rallied, indicating hedging or positioning building into the rally. Short interest is elevated at 14.5% of float with 14.7 days to cover, confirming institutional bearishness. The combination of rising puts, backwardation, and high short interest argues for caution into the next print and is consistent with a two-sided, not asymmetric-upside, setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** OZK entered the CRE downturn over-reserved by design ($300M to $632M ACL), so realized losses hit earnings softly while the bank stayed solidly profitable at roughly $700M of annual net income. The five problem loans resolve through orderly sales and recapitalizations, NPAs peak in 2026, and a CRE recovery in 2027 lets the franchise re-accelerate its highest-margin business. At 8.2x earnings and a 3.8% yield, a re-rating toward the peer median (13.7x) plus normalized earnings power supports a path to the high-$50s to low-$60s. Bull case target $61, consistent with the high end of the analyst range.

**Bear case.** NPAs continue rising, OZK proves later in its cleanup than recognized, and one or more of the five problem credits resolves below carrying value, forcing an incremental reserve build that the thinning cushion no longer absorbs. Estimates keep falling, the diversification transition pressures earnings before new lines scale, and the multiple compresses toward the low end of the specialty-lender range or the stock revisits the low-$40s on a credit scare. Bear case target $40, roughly 22% below the current price and consistent with the analyst low.

**Base case.** Credit stabilizes but does not cleanly improve through 2026, earnings stay roughly flat, the discount persists because the risk it prices is real, and the stock trades in a $48–$55 band supported by the yield. Base case fair value approximately $52, near consensus mean ($52.33) and current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The classification is deliberate: the bull and bear cases are both credible, the deciding variables (problem-loan outcomes, NPA trajectory, the adequacy of the reserve cushion) are unresolved, and key balance-sheet metrics could not be verified. This is the third consecutive HOLD on a declining score trajectory (6.5 in March to 5.7 in May to 5.1 now), and the data does not yet justify either an upgrade or a downgrade.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next quarterly earnings release (Q2 2026, expected mid-to-late July 2026) is the natural reassessment point, particularly for the NPA trend and the status of the five problem loans.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- Nonperforming assets peak and decline for two consecutive quarters (below the 1.08% Q1 2026 level), confirming the cleanup is progressing.
- Favorable resolution of the five problem credits (sales/recaps at or near carrying value) without an incremental reserve build.
- Consensus EPS estimates stabilize and turn positive on revisions, reversing the current broadly-falling trend.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- NPAs rise above roughly 1.5% or a problem loan resolves at a material loss requiring a fresh reserve build that draws the ACL down meaningfully.
- A quarterly net charge-off ratio that re-approaches the Q4 2025 1.18% level on new (not previously disclosed) credits.
- Verified deterioration in regulatory capital or tangible book value, or a dividend coverage concern, once primary filings are reviewed.

## Conclusion

Bank OZK is a well-run, founder-led specialty lender working through the late stage of a CRE credit cycle. The reserve build that pre-funded the Q4 2025 charge-off spike is genuine and differentiates OZK from less-prepared peers, and the 8.2x multiple plus 3.8% yield offer real downside support. But the offsetting facts are equally hard: NPAs are still rising, the bank is later in its cleanup than the megabanks, consensus estimates are falling across every forward period, the highest-margin business is shrinking into rising competition, and the credit outcome depends on a handful of binary, headline-sensitive problem loans. The roughly 9% rally against falling estimates is the unfavorable form of divergence, and the options market is pricing near-term stress.

The cheapness is real but prices risk rather than flagging an error, the growth signal is negative, and the regional-bank cohort as a whole has been a poor source of selection alpha. We rate OZK a HOLD and would want either a confirmed NPA peak with clean problem-loan resolutions, or verified capital strength alongside stabilizing estimates, before moving more constructive. A fresh charge-off on undisclosed credits or a loss-making loan resolution would move this to AVOID. Reassess at the Q2 2026 print.
