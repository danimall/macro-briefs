**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Garmin is one of the higher-quality businesses in the consumer electronics universe: a vertically integrated, Swiss-domiciled designer and manufacturer of GPS/GNSS devices spanning five end-markets (Fitness, Outdoor, Aviation, Marine, Auto OEM), generating FY2025 revenue of $7.25B (+15% YoY), net income of $1.66B (+18%), a 26% operating margin, and $1.36B of free cash flow on a balance sheet carrying roughly $4.3B of cash and marketable securities with no funded debt. The defining story is the Fitness mix shift: the segment grew from 26% of revenue in 2023 to 33% in 2025, with operating margin expanding from 17% to 31%, and accelerated to +42% YoY in Q1 2026. That single segment drove most of the company's consolidated margin expansion from 21% to 26%.

The question for investors at $231.89 is whether the current price offers asymmetric upside. We do not think it does. The stock trades at roughly 24x current-year EPS ($9.58 consensus) and 22.5x next-year ($10.32), essentially in line with the Consumer Discretionary peer median P/E of 24.7, while management has guided FY2026 revenue to about $7.9B (~9% growth, a meaningful deceleration from 15% in FY2025). Consensus estimate revisions over the past 90 days are modest (+2.0% current year, +2.4% next year), an order of magnitude below the level that signals an under-appreciated earnings inflection. The mispricing case that a BUY requires is absent.

Two concrete risks keep us at HOLD rather than BUY. First, 61% of revenue (Fitness + Outdoor) is premium consumer hardware exposed to a probable H2 2026 spending deceleration, and the Outdoor segment already contracted 5% YoY in Q1 2026. Second, Garmin's principal consumer-product manufacturing is concentrated in Taiwan, an idiosyncratic tail risk that is not diversifiable in the near term. Against these, the offsets — Fitness momentum, three earnings beats in the last four quarters, and undisclosed IEEPA tariff-refund optionality — support holding but do not justify paying up. We rate GRMN HOLD with a 12-month base-case target of $248.

## Company Overview

Garmin designs and manufactures GPS-enabled devices across five reportable segments. FY2025 segment economics:

| Segment | FY2025 Net Sales | % of Sales | YoY | Operating Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fitness | $2.36B | 33% | +33% | 31% |
| Outdoor | $2.05B | 28% | +5% | 34% |
| Marine | $1.18B | 16% | +10% | 21% |
| Aviation | $0.99B | 14% | +13% | 26% (75% gross) |
| Auto OEM | $0.66B | 9% | +9% | −7.4% (loss of $48.6M) |

The company has shipped 300M+ products over 35+ years, including 20.7M units in FY2025 (+11%), and employs ~23,000 people, with ~10,200 in manufacturing and ~6,500 in engineering. Its competitive advantage is vertical integration (in-house design plus manufacturing across Taiwan, the US, the Netherlands, the UK, Poland, and China), which drives cost control, quality, and time-to-market flexibility. A sticky software/subscription layer (Garmin Connect/Connect+, Outdoor Maps+, inReach satellite SOS, Garmin Pilot) reinforces customer retention, and the five-segment structure diversifies single-category cyclicality.

## Financial Analysis

Garmin's financial profile is among the strongest in its sector. Revenue compounded from $4.86B (2022) to $7.25B (2025), with gross margin expanding to 59% (from 57% in 2023) and operating margin to 26% (from 21%). Net margin reached 23%. FY2025 operating cash flow was $1.63B against $270M of capex, yielding $1.36B of free cash flow (an 18.8% FCF margin).

The balance sheet is strong: ~$4.1B of cash and marketable securities at FY2025 year-end, rising to ~$4.3B in Q1 2026, with essentially no funded debt. The current ratio is 3.63, equity/assets 0.82, and the Altman Z-Score 3.35 (safe zone). Inventory of $1.77B reflects a deliberate build ahead of demand and tariff exposure rather than channel stuffing.

Q1 FY2026 (reported April 29) confirmed continued momentum: revenue $1.75B (+14%), net income $405M (+22%), diluted EPS $2.09 vs. $1.72, operating margin expanding to 25% from 22%. Segment trends were mixed: Fitness +42%, Aviation +18%, Marine +11%, Auto OEM +1%, and Outdoor −5% (a tough comp against the prior-year fēnix launch). The earnings-surprise record is solid: three beats in the last four quarters, with the most recent quarter beating by 12.1% and the prior quarter by 14.2%, which supports confidence in current consensus.

One accounting nuance: FY2025 operating cash flow ran below net income, a function of the working-capital build (inventory and receivables) and tax-payment timing in a growing business, not earnings-quality erosion. Capital allocation has been conservative relative to the balance sheet: the dividend was raised 20% to $3.60/year (~$700M annual cost) and a $500M buyback was authorized in February 2026 (replacing a $300M program of which $275M had been used), against $1.36B of FCF and a $4B+ cash pile. The company is materially over-capitalized, a positive for resilience but a drag on return on capital, and a constraint partly imposed by Swiss withholding-tax mechanics (only ~$2.8B of tax-free capital-contribution reserves remain).

## Growth Analysis

Growth is real but decelerating, and uneven across segments. The headline story is Fitness, where advanced wearables (Forerunner, Venu, vívoactive) drove +33% in FY2025 and +42% in Q1 2026. This is the highest-conviction part of the thesis and appears to reflect genuine share gains in the premium/serious-user wearables niche against Apple, Samsung, Google, and specialists like Coros, Whoop, and Oura.

The tempering factors are equally concrete. Management guides FY2026 revenue to ~$7.9B, implying ~9% growth, a step down from 15% in FY2025. Outdoor, the highest-margin consumer segment (34% operating margin), contracted 5% YoY in Q1 2026, and management guided Q2 Outdoor "similar to Q1." Because Fitness and Outdoor together are ~61% of revenue, sustained weakness in Outdoor would pressure the mix-shift narrative that has carried the margin story. Auto OEM remains structurally unprofitable (−$48.6M in FY2025), though the loss is narrowing.

The estimate-revision picture is mildly positive but unremarkable: current-year EPS consensus moved from $9.39 to $9.58 over 90 days (+2.0%), next-year from $10.08 to $10.32 (+2.4%). This is positive momentum, but it is far below the revision velocity that would signal a market under-appreciating a fundamental inflection. The data describes a steady high-single-to-low-double-digit grower, not an accelerating one.

## Valuation Assessment

At $231.89 and a ~$45.6B market cap, Garmin trades at ~24.2x current-year consensus EPS ($9.58) and ~22.5x next-year ($10.32). Ex-cash, the multiple is somewhat lower given the $4.3B net cash position, but not dramatically so. The dividend yield is 1.78%.

Against the Consumer Discretionary peer set, this is roughly fair:

| Company | P/E | Div Yield |
|---|---|---|
| CVNA (Carvana) | 39.6 | — |
| DHI (D.R. Horton) | 14.4 | 1.2% |
| CCL (Carnival) | 12.6 | 2.3% |
| EBAY | 25.3 | 1.2% |
| YUM | 24.7 | 2.0% |
| **Peer median** | **24.7** | **1.2%** |
| **GRMN (forward)** | **~22.5** | **1.8%** |

Garmin trades at a modest discount to the peer median P/E while delivering superior balance-sheet quality (net cash vs. levered peers), higher margins, and a comparable growth rate. That argues against the stock being expensive, and also against it being cheap. The multiple is sensible for the business, but there is no identifiable mispricing: no compressed multiple relative to growth, no PEG well below 1.5, no estimate inflection that consensus is lagging. A ~22.5x forward multiple on a ~9% guided grower implies a PEG near 2.5, which is full.

We do not treat the stock as cheap simply because it has pulled back from its 52-week high of $273.32 (reached April 17, 2026); the decline reflects normal volatility around a fair multiple, not a fundamental break. The pullback does not create asymmetric upside on its own. With consensus mean and median targets of $262 and $249 respectively, the implied 12-month upside to the median is roughly 7%, against a low target of $220 (-5%). That distribution is balanced, consistent with a HOLD.

## Competitive Landscape

Garmin's competitive position varies sharply by segment, and the trajectory is what matters. Aviation is the strongest moat: FAA certification, multi-year design-ins, and 75% gross margins create durable barriers against Honeywell, Collins Aerospace (RTX), and Thales, and the segment grew 13% in FY2025 and 18% in Q1 2026. Fitness is gaining share in the premium wearables niche; the +42% Q1 growth against a maturing category points to genuine differentiation on battery life, ruggedness, GPS precision, and ecosystem depth rather than competing head-on for the mass smartwatch market. Marine is consolidating in Garmin's favor through audio (Fusion/JL Audio), trolling motors, and LiveScope sonar.

The weak segment is Auto OEM, where Garmin competes against far larger, better-capitalized tier-1 suppliers (Bosch, Continental, Aptiv, Harman, Panasonic, Visteon). The persistent operating losses reflect a structural scale disadvantage rather than a temporary investment phase. Outdoor's Q1 contraction, while explained by launch-cycle timing, is a reminder that the consumer segments carry product-cycle lumpiness. On balance the competitive trajectory is improving in the segments that matter most for margin (Fitness, Aviation) and stable elsewhere, but the mass-market wearables threat from Apple/Samsung/Google and the Auto OEM drag keep this from being a top-tier moat score.

## Risk Assessment

**Taiwan manufacturing concentration.** Principal consumer-product manufacturing sits in Taiwan. A PRC military action or blockade would severely disrupt operations. This is the single largest idiosyncratic risk, low-probability but extreme-impact, and not diversifiable in the near term.

**H2 2026 consumer spending risk.** With 61% of revenue in premium discretionary hardware, Garmin is directly exposed to a consumer-spending slowdown in the back half of 2026. Outdoor's Q1 −5% is an early, concrete data point. [unverified — specific Fitness price points, housing-wealth-effect pressure, and loan-denial rates are not in the provided sources.]

**Tariff pass-through.** Q1 2026 Marine gross margin fell 200bps specifically due to higher tariff costs. Offsetting this, the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA tariffs were unauthorized creates a potential refund Garmin has not yet booked, undisclosed upside optionality the next call should clarify.

**Wearables maturation and FX/tax noise.** Management itself notes the wearables cycle can decline; reported FX swings of ±$20–50M/year add EPS noise (no hedging policy). The effective tax rate (~14.3% in Q1 2026) benefits from new US legislation but carries transfer-pricing complexity.

**Governance.** Founder/insider control (~15% ownership across the Kao and Burrell families) plus Swiss anti-takeover features entrench a disciplined owner-operator culture but eliminate change-of-control optionality.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term options show an anomalous reading: 1-week ATM IV is quoted at 118%+ in steep backwardation, collapsing to 27.7% at one month and 34–36% in the 3–6 month tenors. The 1-week figure carries the hallmarks of a thin-options/data artifact rather than informed positioning ahead of a known catalyst, and we discount it. The longer-dated structure (3m IV 35.7%, 6m 34.1%) is only modestly above 30.2% one-year historical volatility, the put/call skew is balanced (~2.1%), and the implied move at the nearest credible expiry is ±4%. Short interest is low at 2.5% of float (4.9 days to cover), indicating no meaningful bearish conviction in the borrow. The options market is not flagging an imminent directional move beyond ordinary post-earnings positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Garmin is a net-cash compounder with 26% operating margins and an accelerating, high-margin Fitness franchise (+42% in Q1) that is taking premium-wearables share. The earnings beat cadence (three of four quarters, by 12–14% in the two most recent beats) suggests consensus is conservative. The IEEPA tariff ruling could deliver an unbooked refund that lifts FCF and current-year EPS. If Fitness momentum sustains and Outdoor recovers, FY2027 EPS power approaches $11+ and a re-rating toward 28x supports ~$290–300. The strong balance sheet provides downside protection and latent capacity for larger buybacks or accretive M&A. Bull case target $300.

**Bear case.** Growth decelerates to single digits (already guided to ~9%), the H2 2026 consumer slowdown hits the 61% of revenue in premium discretionary hardware, Outdoor weakness persists beyond launch-cycle timing, and the wearables cycle peaks. A multiple compression to ~18x on a flattened ~$9.50 EPS implies ~$171, near the 52-week low of $186.67 and roughly 20–26% below the current price. A Taiwan disruption would be far worse but is low-probability.

The base case sits between: ~9% revenue growth, mid-teens EPS growth aided by margin mix and tax, and a stable ~24x multiple, yielding modest appreciation. The setup is balanced rather than asymmetric, which is the core reason this is a HOLD and not a BUY.

**Why not a BUY at this score.** The honest, falsifiable BUY-blocker is valuation against decelerating growth. A BUY here requires either (a) a forward multiple meaningfully below the growth rate (PEG <1.5 with rising estimates), or (b) estimate-revision velocity well above current levels signaling an under-appreciated inflection. Garmin shows neither: the PEG is roughly 2.5, and 90-day EPS revisions are only +2.0%/+2.4%. Layered on top is a concrete, dated demand risk: 61% of revenue in premium consumer hardware facing a potential H2 2026 spending deceleration, with Outdoor already −5% YoY. Absent a mispricing catalyst or confirmation that Fitness strength offsets Outdoor weakness through a softer consumer backdrop, paying ~24x forward does not deliver the asymmetric upside a BUY demands.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold)** — positive multi-year growth thesis with catalysts, but balanced near-term risk/reward at current price.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 FY2026 earnings (late July 2026), which will test whether Fitness sustains >25% growth and whether Outdoor stabilizes, and may quantify any IEEPA tariff refund.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q2 2026 shows Fitness growth sustaining >25% YoY **and** Outdoor returning to positive YoY growth, confirming the mix story holds through a softer consumer.
2. The stock de-rates below ~20x forward (~$206) without fundamental deterioration, restoring asymmetric upside.
3. Garmin books a quantified IEEPA tariff refund that materially lifts FCF/current-year EPS.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 revenue guidance cut below ~5% growth, or two consecutive quarters of Fitness deceleration below ~15%.
2. Outdoor and Marine both turning negative YoY, signaling broad consumer-segment weakness rather than launch-cycle timing.
3. Material escalation of Taiwan cross-strait risk that threatens manufacturing continuity.

**Reference price levels:**
- Base case target: ~$248 (24x next-year EPS of $10.32; aligns with $249 analyst median). ~+7% from $231.89, 12-month.
- Bull case target: ~$300 (28x on $10.32 plus tariff-refund optionality). ~+29%.
- Bear case target: ~$171 (18x on flattened ~$9.50 EPS). ~−26%.

## Conclusion

Garmin is a high-quality, conservatively financed compounder with a genuine Fitness-driven growth and margin story, three earnings beats in the last four quarters, and a strong balance sheet that provides real downside protection. The constraint is price relative to a decelerating growth profile: at ~22.5x next-year EPS on ~9% guided revenue growth, in line with the Consumer Discretionary peer median, there is no identifiable mispricing, and the modest +2% estimate revisions do not signal an inflection the market is missing. Stacked against a 61%-discretionary revenue base exposed to a potential H2 2026 consumer slowdown (with Outdoor already contracting) and the structural Taiwan concentration risk, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate GRMN **HOLD** with a 12-month base-case target of $248, and would turn more constructive on a cheaper entry (~$206 or below) or confirmation that Fitness momentum and an Outdoor recovery carry through a softer consumer environment.
