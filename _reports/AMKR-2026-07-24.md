**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Amkor is the world's largest U.S.-headquartered outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) provider and the #2 global player behind ASE Technology. It performs the back-end of chip manufacturing, packaging and testing wafers that fabless firms, IDMs, and foundries fabricate elsewhere. The company sits at the center of two reinforcing tailwinds: the AI/HPC advanced-packaging demand cycle (2.5D/3D integration, HBM stacking, CoWoS-style interposers, co-packaged optics) and the supply-chain regionalization push that favors a U.S.-domiciled OSAT operating an Arizona facility funded partly through the CHIPS Act, a 35% investment tax credit, and, as of July 23, 2026, a $1.5B multi-year prepayment from NVIDIA.

The NVIDIA agreement is the single most important development in the file and post-dates the last 10-K and 10-Q. It reframes the 2026 capital program from a standalone risk into a partially customer-funded growth engine and validates Amkor's advanced-packaging technology for the highest-growth end market. Before that announcement, the story was flat-to-modest revenue growth, compressed margins, and a roughly 3x jump in capex ($2.5–3.0B guided for 2026 versus $904.6M in 2025) that drives free cash flow sharply negative. The prepayment does not eliminate the execution risk, but it materially de-risks the demand side of the Arizona bet and begins diversifying revenue toward AI/data center, away from a mix where Apple alone is ~30% of sales.

We maintain a HOLD (growth_hold). The fundamental thesis has genuinely improved: Q1 2026 revenue rose 27.5% YoY, current-year EPS estimates have been revised up 18.8% over 90 days, and the company has a credible path to margin inflection if Arizona/AI volumes fill new capacity. But at ~$65 the stock has tripled from its 52-week low of $20.87, trades at roughly 31x current-year consensus EPS ($2.08), and next-year estimates have started rolling over (-2.0% over 30 days). Extreme customer concentration, structurally thin ~14% gross margins levered to utilization, deeply negative near-term FCF, and controlling-family stock sales during the run cap conviction. The risk/reward at current price does not yet warrant a BUY.

## Company Overview

Amkor recognizes revenue as a service: customers generally consign wafers and Amkor packages and tests them over a ~2–3 week cycle, with no binding backlog. The 2025 revenue mix skews heavily to advanced products: Advanced Products (flip-chip, wafer-level, memory, 2.5D, HDFO) were 82.8% of the $6.71B in sales ($5.56B), with mainstream wirebond/leadframe/power at 17.2%. Advanced SiP modules were roughly $3.08B.

End markets in 2025: Communications (smartphones) 46%, Computing (data center/AI/PC) 20%, Automotive/Industrial 19%, Consumer 15%. The company operates factories across China, Japan, Korea, Malaysia, the Philippines, Portugal, Singapore, Taiwan, and Vietnam (opened 2024), with the Arizona facility under construction since 2H 2025 and an R&D Center of Excellence in Korea. It employs 30,800 people, roughly 95% in Asia-Pacific.

The competitive differentiators are advanced-packaging technology leadership (HDFO/SWIFT/S-Connect, 2.5D, interposers, co-packaged optics, copper hybrid bonding), geographic diversity enabling supply-chain regionalization, and multi-decade customer relationships. Amkor competes with ASE, JCET, and Powertech, and increasingly with foundries (TSMC's in-house advanced packaging) and IDMs' captive capacity.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** The top line is choppy and cyclical: $7.09B (2022 peak) → $6.50B (2023) → $6.32B (2024) → $6.71B (2025, +6.2%). Momentum is re-accelerating. Q1 2026 sales were $1.685B, up 27.5% YoY, led by Communications +42%, Auto/Industrial +28%, and Computing +19%.

**Margins.** This is the central financial tension. Gross margin ran 18.8% (2022) → 14.5% (2023) → 14.8% (2024) → 14.0% (2025), with Q1 2026 improving to 14.2% (from 11.9% a year earlier) on higher utilization. The high fixed-cost base means margins swing hard on volume: Q1 2026 delivered a 230bp YoY gross-margin gain purely on utilization. The near-term drags are the Vietnam ramp and higher material content in advanced packaging. Operating margin was 7.0% in 2025, net margin 5.6%, net income $373.9M.

**Cash flow and the capex wall.** Operating cash flow was $1.096B in 2025 against $904.6M of capex (13.5% of sales), producing company-defined FCF of $307.9M (down from $533.6M in 2023). The pivotal issue is 2026 capex guidance of $2.5–3.0B, roughly 3x 2025, driven by Arizona. This drives FCF deeply negative in 2026; Q1 2026 already posted -$71.3M FCF, with capital-expenditures-payable jumping to $488.7M from $243.5M. Purchase obligations stood at $1.03B at 3/31/26 ($947M due within 12 months). The mechanism to watch: the NVIDIA prepayment enters the balance sheet as a contract liability (customer advance), not equity, offsetting a meaningful portion of the cash outlay without diluting shareholders.

**Balance sheet.** Solid and investment-grade-like. Cash plus short-term investments were ~$1.85B at 3/31/26 against total debt of $1.414B, a net cash position. D/E is 0.81, current ratio 2.27, Altman Z 2.84 (grey zone, normal for a capital-intensive manufacturer), Piotroski 5/9. The company is in compliance with all covenants. Recent financing: $500M 5.875% notes due 2033 (issued Sept 2025, redeeming the 2027 notes); a $500M Term A Loan (SOFR+1.75%, ~5.45%) due 2030; and an undrawn $1.0B revolver. Leverage is low enough to fund the build even before the NVIDIA advance.

**Capital return.** The quarterly dividend is $0.08352 (~$82M/year), raised only ~1% in November 2025, a signal of capital preservation ahead of Arizona. Policy is to return 40–50% of cumulative FCF; there are no buybacks beyond tax-withholding surrenders. Management has explicitly flagged a potential dividend cut as a risk.

**Tax.** The 2025 effective rate was 15.4%, below 21% due to foreign reduced rates. Korea's conditional reduced tax rates expired in 2025, and Singapore/Vietnam reductions expire over time, creating a structural upward drift in the effective rate. OECD Pillar Two/QDMTT and OBBBA add complexity. This is a quiet multi-year EPS headwind.

**Earnings quality signal.** The company has beaten consensus for four straight quarters (Q1 2026 +15.6%, Q4 2025 +53.7%, Q3 2025 +17.6%, Q2 2025 +37.3%), which raises confidence in the near-term estimate trajectory even as the mix of that trajectory shifts (see below).

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs. First, the AI/HPC advanced-packaging cycle is real and broad-based, visible in hyperscaler capex, HBM roadmaps, and merchant OSAT demand. Amkor and ASE are the primary merchant beneficiaries alongside TSMC's captive packaging. Second, regionalization: as the only U.S.-HQ'd OSAT at scale, Amkor is positioned for made-in-America supply chains, reinforced by the CHIPS Act award (up to $407M, none yet received), the 35% ITC, and the NVIDIA-funded Arizona expansion. Third, the NVIDIA prepayment adds a marquee AI/data-center anchor and validates Amkor's technology for accelerated computing.

Estimate revisions confirm the near-term inflection: current-quarter EPS was revised +48.2% over 90 days (to $0.47), current-year +18.8% (to $2.08). This clears the bar for an earnings inflection off a cyclical trough, and I weight it as a supportive signal. The qualifier is that the revisions are decelerating and turning mixed at the margin: the current quarter was flat over the last 30 days (-0.1%), and next-year EPS was revised -2.0% over 30 days (to $2.30, essentially flat from 90 days ago at +0.2%). The 90-day surge reflects the utilization recovery; the flat 30-day and softening out-year reflect the market digesting the depreciation load that Arizona brings online in 2026–27. The growth signal is strong on the current-year recovery but not yet extended into durable out-year acceleration, which is the swing factor the NVIDIA deal is meant to resolve over the next several quarters.

The Computing segment's trajectory is the metric to watch. If AI/data center rises materially above its 20% share, the concentration overhang eases and the growth durability improves. If AI volumes disappoint, the incremental Arizona depreciation crushes margins, the "capacity ahead of demand" risk management explicitly flags.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$65, Amkor carries a market cap of ~$18.0B and trades at roughly 31x current-year consensus EPS ($2.08) and ~28x next-year ($2.30). That is not a cyclical-trough multiple that a forward lens would rescue: earnings are already re-accelerating, and the 31x forward multiple reflects the inflection rather than obscuring it. On PEG terms, with next-year EPS growth of roughly 10% consensus, the ratio sits near 3x, which is full.

Peer comparison is not clarifying here: the semiconductor peer set is dominated by loss-makers and blow-off multiples (SITM -762.8x, SMTC -434.3x, LSCC 1012.9x, RMBS 68.8x). The peer median P/E of ~69x is distorted and not a usable anchor. Against the broader OSAT frame, Amkor is a thin-margin, capital-intensive service business, and a market-cap multiple in the low-30s forward is toward the upper end of where a structurally 14%-gross-margin OSAT has historically been valued outside of pure AI-euphoria windows.

The analyst target set (low $60.00, mean $76.13, median $77.50, high $90.00) brackets the current price, with the stock at the very bottom of the range. On an inflection stock trading below 30x next-year with rising estimates, consensus targets can lag, but here the out-year estimates have flattened, so the "targets are floors" logic applies only weakly. The valuation is best characterized as fair-to-slightly-full: the AI optionality and NVIDIA anchor justify a premium to Amkor's historical multiple, but the current price does not offer the asymmetric upside a BUY requires, particularly with FCF turning sharply negative during the build. The calibration caution that "cheap" screens are anti-predictive in this regime is not the issue here; AMKR is not cheap, and I am not rewarding a low multiple.

The prior report cut this dimension from 5.0 to 4.0. I bring it back to 5.0: the stock has pulled back from a $96.68 high to $65 while the NVIDIA deal materially improved the fundamental setup, which is improving risk/reward rather than deteriorating thesis. But the tripling off the low and the flattening out-year estimates keep me from scoring valuation as a positive.

## Competitive Landscape

Amkor's moat is technology leadership in advanced packaging plus a structural regionalization advantage no Asian competitor can replicate: it is the U.S.-HQ'd OSAT that can build domestic capacity with government support and a hyperscaler prepayment. The June 2026 media framing of "ASE vs. Amkor" as the two merchant ways to play the OSAT AI theme captures the competitive reality: this is effectively a duopoly at the leading edge of merchant advanced packaging, with TSMC's captive capacity as the third pole.

The trajectory is improving. The NVIDIA agreement is direct evidence of share capture in the fastest-growing end market and of customers designing Amkor into next-generation AI platforms. Q1 2026 segment growth outpaced the broader semiconductor cycle.

The offsetting pressure is China's government-subsidized "China-for-China" OSAT push (JCET and peers), which intensifies price competition. Compounding it, BIS export controls bind U.S. firms like Amkor but not foreign competitors such as ASE and JCET, creating asymmetric share-loss risk in China. Longer term, packaging/test ASPs structurally decline, and foundries offering turnkey packaging could recapture some advanced work. On balance the position is improving, which supports a competitive-position score above the midpoint but below the high-conviction threshold given the ASP and China dynamics.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration — the dominant risk.** Apple was 29.8% and Qualcomm 11.1% of 2025 sales; the top 10 were 72%. Communications is 46% of revenue. There is no backlog and no binding customer commitment. A material reduction from Apple would be severely damaging. The NVIDIA deal begins to diversify this, but Apple remains a persistent overhang.

**Arizona execution / capex.** ~$2.5–3.0B of 2026 spend against a facility whose ramp and cost carry uncertainty; equipment bought for specific customers may not redeploy. FCF turns sharply negative and could require incremental debt. The NVIDIA prepayment de-risks demand but not construction, cost, or timing execution.

**Operating leverage cuts both ways.** With high fixed costs, if AI/Arizona volumes fill capacity, incremental margins are strong; if they disappoint, new depreciation crushes margins.

**Geopolitical/trade.** BIS export controls, tariffs, China-Taiwan and Korea tensions, and ~95% of PP&E and workforce in Asia. The Q1 2026 10-Q added or expanded language on tariffs, recent U.S. military action in Venezuela, Middle East and China-Taiwan conflict, Arizona construction-material (steel) supply, and a new large-scale ERP implementation risk.

**CHIPS Act conditionality.** Up to $407M award (none received yet), with milestone requirements and clawback rights; the 35% ITC is meaningful but conditional.

**Governance and dividend.** The Kim family controls ~49.3% (effectively a controlled company), reducing float and impeding takeovers. Management explicitly flags a potential dividend cut ahead of Arizona spend.

**Tax drift.** Korea reduced-rate expiration plus Pillar Two create multi-year upward pressure on the effective rate.

Given the concentration, the binary nature of the Arizona ramp, and the FCF swing, the idiosyncratic risk set is material and not fully priced, which warrants a risk score of 4.5.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing significant near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 106.0% versus 1-year historical volatility of 71.1%, rich by ~35 points, in the 73rd percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (106.0% near-term vs 93.4% at six months), signaling the market expects an imminent catalyst, consistent with the fresh NVIDIA news and an upcoming print. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±24%. Put/call skew is essentially balanced near-term (-0.3%) but steepens with tenor (-6.9% at six months), indicating growing downside hedging demand further out. Put/call OI ratio is 1.30. Short interest is 14.5% of float with only 1.4 days to cover, elevated bearish positioning but not a squeeze setup given the low cover ratio. The market is pricing a two-sided outcome with elevated volatility, which argues for patience on entry rather than chasing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$90).** The NVIDIA prepayment proves to be the leading edge of a broader AI/HPC advanced-packaging capture. Computing rises materially above 20% of revenue, Arizona fills at strong incremental margins, gross margin inflects toward the high-teens, and FCF recovers by 2027 as the customer advance offsets capex. Current-year EPS of $2.08 grows meaningfully into 2027, and the stock re-rates on durable AI exposure toward the high-$80s/low-$90s (roughly the 52-week high and the top analyst target).

**Bear case (~$45, -31%).** AI demand normalizes or Apple reduces allocation; Arizona depreciation lands before volumes fill, compressing already-thin margins; FCF stays negative into 2027 and pressures the dividend; and out-year estimates continue to roll over. On a de-rated multiple applied to flat-to-lower earnings, the stock retraces to the mid-$40s, where it traded as recently as March 2026.

**Base case (~$77).** The NVIDIA deal de-risks the build and supports mid-teens EPS growth, but concentration and margin uncertainty keep the multiple from expanding materially. EPS grows toward $2.30–2.50, and the stock trades to roughly consensus mean/median ($76–78) over 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD (growth_hold): the fundamental thesis has improved and I expect appreciation potential, but current price/risk does not warrant adding to a full position.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 results showing Computing/AI segment revenue accelerating past ~25% of total mix, confirming the NVIDIA deal is broadening the customer base.
- Gross margin inflecting sustainably above ~15% on utilization, demonstrating the operating-leverage upside without Arizona depreciation drag.
- A pullback toward the low-$50s that restores asymmetry, absent any fundamental deterioration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Evidence of Apple allocation reduction or a top-customer loss (watch the top-10 concentration disclosure).
- Out-year EPS revisions continuing to fall alongside a guidance cut, or a dividend reduction signaling FCF strain.
- Arizona ramp delays or cost overruns that push FCF-negative through 2027 with no NVIDIA offset materializing on the balance sheet.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and the associated capex/FCF update, plus confirmation that the NVIDIA prepayment has appeared as a contract liability on the balance sheet. The options market's backwardation implies a near-term catalyst is being priced.

## Conclusion

Amkor's thesis has genuinely improved. The NVIDIA prepayment converts the Arizona capex wall from a standalone risk into a partially customer-funded growth engine and stamps Amkor's advanced-packaging technology as AI-relevant. Q1 2026 revenue growth, four consecutive earnings beats, and current-year estimate revisions confirm an earnings inflection off the cyclical trough. Amkor is the sole U.S.-HQ'd OSAT positioned to capture the regionalization dividend.

The reasons for restraint are equally concrete. The stock has tripled from its low to ~$65 and trades at ~31x current-year earnings with next-year estimates already flattening and rolling over slightly. Gross margins remain structurally thin at ~14% and are hostage to utilization. FCF turns deeply negative in 2026. Customer concentration (Apple ~30%, top-10 72%) is extreme, a new CEO is overseeing the largest capex program in company history, and the controlling family monetized stock into the rally. The options market is pricing a ±24% near-term move.

Recommendation: **HOLD** (growth_hold), overall score 5.75, consistent with the improving-but-capped trajectory this name has shown across prior reports. The right posture is to own a partial position for the AI/regionalization optionality while waiting for either segment-mix and margin confirmation or a better entry price before scaling up.
