**Research Date: July 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Tapestry operates as a one-brand growth story inside a two-brand structure. Following the August 2025 divestiture of Stuart Weitzman, the company runs Coach and Kate Spade, and Coach generates essentially all of the segment profit. Coach's execution has been exceptional: Q3 FY2026 revenue grew 31.5% at a 35% segment operating margin, driving consolidated nine-month FY2026 revenue to $6.13B (+15.9%) and operating income up 47% to $1.47B. Management raised FY2026 guidance to roughly $6.95 EPS on ~$7.95B revenue and articulated a long-term goal of Coach reaching $10B in revenue. The company has bucked the China-led luxury slowdown, growing Greater China +30%+, and Barron's reported that no listed luxury peer outperformed Tapestry on trailing-year TSR.

The reported financials are misleading and must be normalized. GAAP FY2025 EPS of $0.82 and a "distress zone" Altman Z-Score of 1.79 reflect an $854.8M Kate Spade impairment, ~$268M of terminated-Capri costs, and a negative accumulated deficit driven by years of buybacks under Maryland retirement accounting, not operating distress. Adjusted FY2025 EPS was $5.10, and actual leverage is modest: ~$2.4B total debt against ~$1.5B annual FCF and ~$1.9-2.0B EBIT. The FTC-forced collapse of the Capri acquisition worked in Tapestry's favor: it redeployed the war chest into ~$4B of well-timed buybacks (much near $78, now ~$149) and avoided integrating declining brands.

The recommendation is HOLD. The business quality and earnings momentum are real, and estimate revisions are broadly positive (+7.3% current-year over 90 days). At ~$149 near the 52-week high, the stock trades at roughly 21x forward adjusted EPS ($6.98 FY26 consensus), below the peer median P/E of 26.0 on that basis. On forward earnings the multiple is not cheap for a discretionary name carrying a 230bps tariff drag, Kate Spade impairment risk, and single-brand concentration. The stock has already re-rated to reflect the good news, and the asymmetry that would justify a BUY is no longer present. This is a growth hold, with a clear upgrade path on a pullback or a Kate Spade recovery.

## Company Overview

Tapestry is a house of accessible-luxury accessories and lifestyle brands operating two reportable segments after the Stuart Weitzman sale to Caleres (completed August 4, 2025 for $109M):

- **Coach** — the growth engine and profit center. FY2025 net sales were $5.60B (+10%); nine-month FY2026 sales reached $5.27B (+26%). Coach carries a ~78-79% gross margin. It represents ~86% of nine-month FY26 sales and effectively all of the company's ~$1.93B segment operating profit.
- **Kate Spade** — the failed turnaround. Sales declined ~10-11% for three consecutive periods (FY2025 $1.20B, -10%; nine-month FY2026 $840M, -11%), and the brand is roughly breakeven at the operating line and shrinking.

Distribution is primarily direct-to-consumer (retail and e-commerce), with smaller wholesale and licensing channels. North America is ~64% of sales, with Greater China the fastest-growing region (+30%+), followed by Other Asia and Europe. The competitive edge is Coach's "Amplify" brand repositioning: emotional-connection marketing, Gen Z acquisition, and demonstrated pricing power, which management repeatedly cites as the driver of gross-margin gains. This has produced a rare combination of accelerating revenue and expanding margin.

## Financial Analysis

**Normalized earnings power, not reported numbers, is the correct lens.** FY2025 reported net income fell 77.6% to $183.2M (EPS $0.82) due to ~$951M of non-recurring charges. Adjusted FY2025 net income was $1.13B (+13%), EPS $5.10, with adjusted operating margin expanding ~130bps to 20.0%. FY2025 operating cash flow was $1.22B and FCF $1.09B (a 16% FCF margin).

FY2026 shows acceleration and normalization. Nine-month revenue grew 15.9% (+18.5% ex-Stuart Weitzman/FX); operating income rose 47% to $1.47B (24.0% margin); nine-month diluted EPS was $5.58 versus $3.12 prior year. Q3 FY2026 revenue was $1.92B (+21%) at a 22.3% operating margin. Operating cash flow surged to $1.46B for the nine months from $770M. The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in forward estimates: four consecutive beats, including +26.0% in the March 2026 quarter and +19.8% in December 2025.

**The balance sheet is stronger than screens suggest.** The Altman Z-Score of 1.79 and D/E of 6.67x are artifacts of a -$3.2B accumulated deficit created by aggressive buybacks (retired shares are charged against retained earnings under Maryland law) plus the Kate Spade impairment. Actual leverage is modest: total debt (senior notes maturing 2027-2035) sits well within the 4.0x net-leverage covenant. Cash is $1.05B with a $2.0B undrawn revolver and $2.0B commercial paper capacity; current ratio 1.87. The company de-risked substantially in FY2025 by redeeming the entire $7.5B+ of Capri acquisition financing.

**Capital allocation is disciplined and shareholder-friendly.** Tapestry executed a $2.0B accelerated repurchase in FY2025 at an average price of $77.64, authorized a new $3.0B buyback in September 2025 ($2.19B remaining as of March 2026), and raised the dividend to $0.40/quarter. Share count has fallen from ~264M (FY22) to ~202M, a ~23% reduction that meaningfully levers per-share results. Financial Health scores 7.0: strong FCF conversion, well-timed buybacks, and appropriate leverage for the business model, with the caveat that the equity base is thin and the trailing-cash swing has been large.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests almost entirely on Coach. The +31.5% Q3 growth at a 35% segment margin reflects a durable combination of volume, Gen Z customer acquisition, and net pricing improvements rather than promotional-driven volume. Greater China growing +30%+ against a sector-wide China slowdown is the clearest evidence that this is a brand-specific share-gain story, not a beta trade.

Estimate revisions are supportive across every window: current-year EPS revised +7.3% over 90 days to $6.98, next-year +7.8% to $7.79, and current-quarter +9.2%. These are positive but do not reach the +30% threshold that would flag a systematic underestimation of an earnings recovery. The revisions confirm momentum; they do not signal a hidden trough. The mechanism behind the growth — pricing power plus customer acquisition — is credible and multi-quarter, and the world model's confirmation of broad discretionary weakness (Gap mid-market crack, BKE comp deceleration from +7.0% to +2.2%, NY Fed household worries) makes Coach's continued outperformance a genuine share-gain story rather than sector tailwind.

Two offsets temper the growth score. First, Kate Spade is a ~14% revenue drag declining at -11%, which subtracts from consolidated growth. Second, the FY26 revenue guide implies deceleration from the +26% Coach pace as comparisons harden. Growth Prospects scores 6.8: a real, durable single-brand acceleration partly offset by Kate Spade's decline and normalizing comparisons.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$149, TPR trades at approximately 21.4x forward adjusted EPS and ~19.1x next-year EPS ($7.79). The 48.5x trailing P/E in the data reflects the impaired GAAP FY25 figure and is meaningless; the correct anchor is forward adjusted earnings. Peer medians are P/E 26.0 and yield 2.2%, but the peer set is heterogeneous (LULU at 9.8x, NKE at 30.3x, RL at 26.0x, VFC at 27.3x), and several peers trade at deep 52-week discounts (LULU -49%, NKE -48%) reflecting their own troubles. Tapestry sits near its 52-week high, having risen ~37% over the trailing year and re-rated on genuine fundamental improvement.

The valuation dimension is where the analysis diverges from the recent trajectory. Prior reports moved the valuation score from 4.0 to 7.0 as the earnings story confirmed, but at the current price the asymmetry has compressed. Forward P/E of ~21x with a PEG near 1.5 (EPS growth of roughly 12-14% into FY27) is a fair multiple for a high-quality compounder, not a mispricing. The analyst consensus mean target of $167 (median $167) implies ~12% upside; the range of $88 to $230 is unusually wide, reflecting genuine disagreement about the durability of Coach's momentum against the tariff and single-brand risks. A cheap-looking trailing multiple here is precisely the kind of value-trap signal that has proven anti-predictive; the trailing figure is distorted, and the forward figure is not cheap. Valuation scores 5.0: fairly valued to modestly full, with no identifiable mispricing that would reward a higher score. The dividend yield of ~1.1% provides negligible downside support.

## Competitive Landscape

Coach holds a leadership position in "accessible luxury" handbags, a market the FTC's Capri litigation formally recognized as distinct, where Coach, Kate Spade, and Michael Kors are the dominant U.S. players. Coach's structural shift from a discount/outlet-reliant model toward full-price, brand-led selling with pricing power and younger-customer acquisition widens its moat. While LVMH, Kering, and Burberry have struggled with the China slowdown, Coach grew Greater China +30%+ and outperformed all listed luxury peers on TSR.

Coach's competitive trajectory is improving: gaining share, expanding gross margin, and building brand equity with a younger cohort. Kate Spade is the offsetting deterioration, with gross margin down ~390bps YTD FY26 on tariffs and channel mix. On balance, the consolidated competitive position is improving because Coach's momentum dwarfs Kate Spade's decline in both revenue weight and profit contribution. Competitive Position scores 7.0. The world model's caution on Consumer Discretionary as a negative-alpha sector requires company-specific outperformance evidence; Coach's China share gains against a declining sector supply exactly that evidence.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs are the most quantified near-term headwind.** Management guided a ~230bps operating-margin hit in FY2026; nine-month FY26 tariffs already cut gross margin ~150bps (180bps in Q3), and the de minimis exemption removal (effective August 29, 2025) adds cost. A new 10% Section 122 global tariff took effect February 24, 2026. The offsetting wildcard is the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating IEEPA tariffs: Tapestry has remitted ~$115M and may receive refunds, but conservatively booked no receivable, creating potential upside if refunds materialize.

**Kate Spade impairment risk is live.** After the $854.8M FY2025 impairment, management explicitly warns that continued profitability deterioration could trigger an additional impairment of the remaining $383M goodwill and ~$88M net brand intangibles. This is the largest brand-specific binary and is not fully priced.

**Single-brand concentration** means the entire investment case rides on Coach's continued momentum and pricing power. Any sign that Coach's pricing power is maturing or that Gen Z acquisition is slowing would remove the growth engine. **Discretionary/consumer-cyclical exposure** compounds this against a challenging macro; the H2 consumer cliff (45-55% probability) and mid-market weakness are real, though Coach's China and premiumization skew provide some insulation. **Pillar Two global minimum tax** is raising the effective rate (17.6% YTD FY26 vs. 13.6% prior). The Capri securities litigation was dismissed without prejudice March 31, 2026, but plaintiffs filed an amended complaint April 30, 2026; probability of material impact appears low.

Risk Factors scores 5.5: the tariff drag and Kate Spade impairment are specific and partly unpriced binaries, offset by a de-risked balance sheet and potential tariff-refund upside.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 61.1% is rich versus 41.6% one-year historical vol (a 19.5pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (61.1% near-term vs 49.9% long-term), signaling the market is pricing imminent stress ahead of the late-July/early-August FY26 earnings and fiscal year-end. IV jumped 11.6pp in a single session (49.5% to 61.1%), consistent with event positioning. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-0.6%), and the put/call OI ratio of 0.64 is not bearish, though the put/call volume ratio of 1.26 shows some near-term hedging. The signal is elevated event uncertainty rather than a directional bearish tilt, and the rich near-term IV argues against chasing here; wait for a post-print vol crush to improve entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($190-230):** Coach sustains 20%+ growth on pricing power and Gen Z acquisition, tracks toward the $10B revenue target, and consolidated adjusted operating margin holds in the low-to-mid 20s. IEEPA tariff refunds (~$115M) materialize as an upside surprise. Kate Spade stabilizes, removing the impairment overhang and adding a call option. Buybacks continue shrinking the share count ~3-4%/year, compounding EPS toward $8.50+ in FY27. At ~26x that figure the stock reaches the high end of the analyst range.

**Bear Case ($100-115):** Coach's pricing power matures and comparisons harden, decelerating growth to high single digits. Tariffs run above the 230bps guide, Kate Spade requires a further ~$470M impairment, and discretionary weakness bites the North American core (64% of sales). The stock de-rates to ~15x a flatter ~$7 EPS as the single-brand concentration risk reprices, implying ~25-30% downside from current levels.

The base case sits between these: Coach delivers roughly the guided ~$6.95 FY26 EPS, momentum normalizes but stays positive, tariffs land near guidance, and Kate Spade avoids a further impairment. At ~22x a FY27 EPS of ~$7.79, fair value is roughly $170, in line with the consensus mean target and implying ~12% upside, attractive but not the asymmetry that would justify a BUY at the current price near the 52-week high.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Reassessment timeline: the next natural checkpoint is the FY2026 fourth-quarter and full-year print (early-to-mid August 2026), which will confirm the ~$6.95 guide, the tariff run-rate, and any Kate Spade impairment decision, followed by the FY27 guidance.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-$130s or below on unchanged fundamentals, restoring a forward P/E near 18x and a clear >20% path to base-case fair value.
- Kate Spade returns to flat-to-positive sales growth or management removes the further-impairment warning, converting the call option and removing a binary downside.
- IEEPA tariff refunds are recognized or Coach growth re-accelerates with next-year estimate revisions turning above +10% over 90 days.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Coach organic growth decelerates below ~10% YoY, signaling the pricing-power engine is maturing.
- A further Kate Spade impairment is announced alongside evidence of broader North American discretionary weakness in Coach.
- Adjusted operating margin contracts materially below 20% on tariffs running above the 230bps guide with no offsetting refund.

## Conclusion

Tapestry is a high-quality business at a fair price. Coach's execution is among the best in global consumer discretionary — accelerating revenue, expanding margins, pricing power, and China share gains against a declining sector — and the balance sheet is far stronger than screening metrics imply. Management has allocated capital well, buying back ~$4B of stock near $78 and de-risking the Capri debt. The earnings-surprise record and positive estimate revisions support confidence in the forward numbers.

The reason for a HOLD rather than a BUY is price. At ~$149 near the 52-week high, the stock trades at ~21x forward adjusted EPS with a PEG near 1.5, offering roughly 12% to base-case fair value: a fair return for the quality, but not the asymmetric upside required given the tariff drag, the live Kate Spade impairment risk, and the single-brand concentration. The stock has already re-rated to capture the good news. Own it for Coach's durable compounding, add on a pullback toward the low-$130s or a Kate Spade recovery, and reassess at the August FY26 print. The recommendation continues the recent upgrade trajectory's spirit while acknowledging that the mispricing that justified the prior BUY has largely closed at the current price.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the Executive Summary's claim that ~21x forward EPS is a "premium to the peer median P/E of 26.0" was inverted (21x is *below* the 26.0 median, i.e., a discount on a forward basis) — this reframes the valuation as less full than the HOLD narrative implies.
