## Executive Summary

MasTec is compounding revenue and earnings at rates well above its historical trend while its equity has de-rated violently. H1 2026 revenue rose 28.3% to $8.20B, adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 120bps to 8.1%, 18-month backlog reached $21.39B (+30.0% YoY), and management raised FY2026 guidance on July 30 from $17.5B revenue / $8.79 adjusted EPS to $18.2B / $9.30. Consensus FY2027 EPS has risen 10.2% over 90 days to $12.62, including upward revisions after the Q2 print. Against that, the stock closed at $272.46, 38% below the $441.43 high set on May 6 and 16% below the pre-earnings July 30 close of $324.44.

The de-rating is explicable without a collapse in earnings power. At $434 in mid-May the shares traded at roughly 47x the current FY2026 adjusted EPS guide and about 70x GAAP, after a ~170% twelve-month gain, with sell-side targets as high as $550 and at least one concentrated holder at ~20% portfolio weight. Q2 broke a five-quarter beat streak by missing non-GAAP consensus by 0.6% ($2.22 vs. $2.23), management reduced the Communications outlook, and the raised guide implies a demanding Q4: revenue of ~$5.07B (+28.6% YoY) and adjusted EPS of ~$2.71 (+31%), both derived by subtraction from the FY and Q3 guides. A crowded, richly-valued position unwound into a composition disappointment.

What remains at $272.46 is a business at 29.3x FY2026 guided adjusted EPS and 21.6x FY2027 consensus, against a Construction & Engineering peer median P/E of 44.3x, with rising estimates and a broad demand set spanning grid modernization, data-center electrical work, renewables and midstream. The offsets are substantive and quantified: pro forma gross debt rose 47% to ~$4.05B (~2.9x LTM adjusted EBITDA) to fund the $1.6B Superior acquisition at an undisclosed EBITDA multiple; H1 free cash flow was negative $68M before counting $125.6M of equipment financed via finance leases outside the capex line; DSO rose from 65 to 72 days; and one-third of guided adjusted EPS is non-GAAP add-back, putting the GAAP multiple near 44x. Base case fair value of $292 is ~7% above spot against a bear case near $185 (−32%). That combination is a hold, not an entry.

## Company Overview

MasTec is a North American infrastructure engineering and construction contractor with approximately 37,000 employees across ~780 locations. It aggregates craft labor and equipment rather than owning assets; on most projects, materials are supplied by customers. Competitive advantage rests on labor scale, pre-qualification credentials, safety record, geographic density and surety bonding capacity ($13.4B of performance and payment bonds outstanding at 6/30/2026).

Four segments generate revenue. In Q2 2026: Clean Energy & Infrastructure $1,622.1M at 7.9% EBITDA margin (utility-scale renewables, gas generation, heavy civil, general building including data centers); Power Delivery $1,245.8M at 9.1% (transmission, distribution, substations, grid hardening, storm restoration); Communications $888.9M at 8.2% (fiber, wireless, data-center interconnect, install-to-the-home); Pipeline Infrastructure $642.8M at 18.4% (midstream gas, water, CO₂, integrity work).

Roughly 40% of consolidated revenue runs through master service agreements — recurring frameworks with no minimum purchase obligation, generally cancellable on short or no notice, down from 45–46% a year ago. The remainder is discrete project work where fixed-price estimate risk concentrates. Goodwill of $2,359.6M plus intangibles of $730.0M equal 28.3% of the $10,925.5M asset base, reflecting a long history of acquisition-led growth.

## Financial Analysis

**The 2026 acceleration is genuine and broad.** Q2 revenue grew 23.4% to $4,373.6M (organic ~18%, acquisitions $195.1M); H1 grew 28.3% to $8,202.4M (organic ~23%). Operating income margin improved to 5.2% in Q2 from 4.5%, and to 4.5% in H1 from 3.0%. Adjusted EBITDA reached $384.2M (8.8%) in Q2 versus $274.8M (7.8%), and $667.9M (8.1%) in H1 versus $438.5M (6.9%). Net income attributable to MasTec rose to $130.1M from $85.8M in the quarter.

**Margin expansion is disproportionately dependent on one cyclical segment.** Pipeline delivered 18.4% EBITDA margin in Q2 and 19.9% in H1, against 11.5%/11.9% a year earlier and roughly 14.9% for FY2025. Pipeline contributed $118.5M of $442.2M total segment EBITDA in Q2 — 26.8% of segment profit on 14.7% of revenue. Management attributes this to "improved efficiencies as well as the effects of project mix," language consistent with either a durable mix shift toward integrity and water/CO₂ work or a transient run of favorable projects. Two related facts matter: net income attributable to non-controlling interests rose to $24.4M in H1 (11.3% of consolidated net income) from $6.8M, which MD&A attributes specifically to higher activity at Pipeline entities with minority holders; and H1 earn-out fair-value increases also related primarily to Pipeline. Consolidated adjusted EBITDA includes 100% of these entities; per-share earnings do not. Any EV/EBITDA multiple on MasTec is therefore modestly flattered relative to a pure equity claim.

**Communications is the one segment moving backwards.** Revenue grew only 6.2% in Q2 with EBITDA margin down 170bps to 8.2%, on costs to exit certain install-to-the-home markets, project close-outs and wireline mix; wireless activity declined on customer project timing. Management reduced the segment's full-year outlook. This is the second cycle in three years of carrier-capex-driven volatility: Communications fell sharply in FY2024, rebounded ~32% in FY2025, and is now decelerating.

**Cash conversion is the weakest part of the story.** H1 operating cash flow was $120.3M against $215.4M of consolidated net income — 0.56x. Contract assets consumed $463.1M (versus $240.6M in H1 2025) and receivables $143.6M (versus $41.1M), partly offset by $198.5M from payables. Unbilled receivables rose to $1,894.2M from $1,573.5M and retainage to $590.2M from $428.4M; DSO went from 65 days at year-end to 72. Cash capex of $188.3M puts derived H1 free cash flow at −$68.0M (−$47.6M including asset-sale proceeds). Critically, an additional $125.6M of property and equipment was financed through finance leases and similar arrangements, excluded from investing activities, and FY2026 guidance contemplates ~$290M of cash capex plus ~$285M of finance-lease equipment purchases. Screens using operating cash flow minus capex overstate free cash flow here by roughly $285M annually.

Seasonality is real and management-disclosed — working capital builds through summer and fall and converts in winter — so H1 negative free cash flow is not annualizable. The honest full-cycle anchor is FY2025 operating cash flow of ~$546M on $14.3B of revenue, a 3.8% cash margin, itself down roughly half from FY2024's ~$1,122M. Two mechanisms modestly flatter the reported figures: ~$333M of non-recourse receivable sales in H1 (~$161M outstanding off balance sheet) with $11.9M of discount charges in interest expense, and a supplier finance program with $137.3M outstanding inside payables. Both are disclosed, industry-standard, and small against a ~$4.2B receivable-plus-contract-asset book.

**The balance sheet changed materially in July.** Debt at 6/30/2026 was $2,752.9M against $315.6M of cash and $3,600.0M of equity. To fund Superior, the revolver was upsized from $1.9B to $2.25B, a new $700M delayed-draw term loan was fully drawn, and $600M was borrowed on the revolver. Pro forma gross debt is ~$4,053M and net debt ~$3.75B. LTM adjusted EBITDA through 6/30/2026 is $1,379.5M (FY2025 $1,150.1M less H1 2025 $438.5M plus H1 2026 $667.9M), putting pro forma gross leverage at ~2.94x and net at ~2.72x; against the $1.6B FY2026 guide, ~2.53x. The 2026 term loan requires a consolidated leverage ratio no greater than 3.50x, stepping to 4.00x for the quarter of a permitted acquisition above $200M plus four subsequent quarters — so the covenant cushion is widest exactly during peak integration risk and reverts about five quarters after closing. The company's reported EBITDA definitions differ from the credit-agreement definitions, so these are approximations of the covenant calculation, not substitutes for it. Maturities are front-loaded but manageable: $600M term loan and $600M of 4.500% notes in 2028; $550M, $72.7M and $400M in 2029.

For a labor-and-equipment aggregator running working capital as its principal capital use, ~2.7x net leverage with negative interim free cash flow and a rising receivable book is adequate rather than comfortable. That supports a Financial Health score of 5.5, down from prior work, driven by the 47% debt increase and cash-conversion deterioration rather than by the share price.

## Growth Analysis

Backlog of $21,391M is up 30.0% year over year, but its definition must be kept straight. It is an 18-month estimate, not remaining performance obligations of $16.3B: backlog includes $8.4B of estimated MSA revenue not in RPO and excludes ~$3.2B of RPO and MSA revenue beyond 18 months. Roughly 40% of backlog sits under agreements with no minimum purchase commitment, cancellable on short notice, and the company states backlog is "an uncertain indicator of future revenue and earnings." Of the $16.3B RPO, ~$6.6B converts in 2026.

Four demand vectors are visible, and they differ in durability:

1. **Grid modernization and electrification** (Power Delivery, +19.2% in Q2) is the most durable and least policy-dependent pool, driven by load growth, hardening and storm restoration, at the second-best segment margin (9.1%) and improving. This aligns with confirmed data-center load growth showing up in regulated-utility volumes across the sector.
2. **Data-center electrical infrastructure** now appears in three segments, and Superior adds ~3,000 electricians explicitly positioned in data centers. MasTec is compensated for construction services; it does not own facilities or hold recurring compute revenue. The economics are project-margin economics, and the payment stream depends on hyperscaler and developer capex continuing.
3. **Renewables** (CE&I, +43.4% in Q2, backlog $7,791M, +58%) is the largest segment and the most policy-exposed. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act accelerated the phaseout of IRA clean-energy credits with safe-harbor mechanics that reward starting projects inside a defined window. Part of CE&I's extraordinary backlog and revenue growth plausibly reflects pull-forward of project starts, which would imply a 2027–2028 air pocket. This is an inference from disclosed policy mechanics and timing, not a company statement; the alternative is that data-center-adjacent power generation and heavy civil — genuinely secular — accounts for most of it. Neither reading can be confirmed from the current filings, and the resolution is the single largest swing factor in FY2028 earnings power.
4. **Midstream and pipeline integrity** benefits from increased federal support for oil and gas and from domestic energy-security priorities, but is historically the lumpiest of MasTec's businesses and was flat in revenue in FY2025.

Government entities supplied 15% of Q2 revenue, up from ~13%, a modest stabilizer. Foreign operations are ~1%.

**Estimate momentum is a genuine positive.** Consensus current-quarter EPS rose 7.0% in 30 days to $2.96 (against guidance of $2.98), current-year to $9.31 (against $9.30 guided), next-quarter +12.2% to $2.73, and FY2027 +7.2% in 30 days and +10.2% in 90 days to $12.62. Because the current-year figure now matches management's post-Q2 guide, these revisions incorporate the print — analysts raised FY2027 while the stock fell 16%. The magnitude (+7–12%) is meaningfully positive but well below the threshold where revision velocity alone carries a buy case.

The constraint on the near term is the implied Q4 shape: $18.2B less H1 $8.202B less guided Q3 $4.93B leaves ~$5.07B for Q4 (+28.6% YoY), with implied Q4 adjusted EBITDA of ~$450M (8.9% margin) and adjusted EPS of ~$2.71 (+31%). Superior consolidation supplies part of that step-up, but the organic/inorganic split is not disclosed, and Superior's standalone revenue and EBITDA are undisclosed. Growth Prospects score 7.75: the top line and backlog are strong and broad, but Communications has rolled over, the largest segment carries an unresolved policy pull-forward question, and the highest-margin segment is the most cyclical.

## Competitive Landscape

MasTec competes with Quanta Services, Primoris, IES Holdings, EMCOR, Comfort Systems, thousands of regional contractors, and customers' in-house crews. Barriers are craft-labor availability, pre-qualification, safety record and bonding capacity rather than technology. Contracts are frequently won on price and MSAs are periodically re-competed.

The trajectory is mixed. Positive: $13.4B of bonding capacity (up 23% in six months, a proxy for prequalification headroom); 37,000 craft employees with geographic density; Superior adding scale in data-center electrical work at a moment when that skill set is scarce; government revenue rising to 15%. Negative: top-ten customers were 37% of H1 2026 revenue versus 35% in H1 2025 — flat to slightly worse. AT&T falling below the 10% disclosure threshold is a denominator effect from growth in CE&I, Power Delivery and Pipeline, not a reduction in aggregate dependence on a small counterparty set. Segment mix has genuinely broadened; customer concentration has not. The proxy also shows cumulative TSR of $318.82 against a peer group at $355.80, with MasTec lagging in each of the five disclosed years — a check on any best-in-class-operator framing. Competitive Position: 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Clean-energy policy pull-forward.** OBBBA's accelerated IRA phaseout with begin-construction safe harbors rewards early project starts. If a material portion of CE&I's 58% backlog growth is pulled-forward work, FY2028 renewables volume declines from an elevated base. Unresolvable from current disclosure; the tell will be CE&I backlog and award commentary in the Q3 and Q4 prints.

**The implied Q4.** ~$5.07B revenue (+28.6%) and ~$2.71 adjusted EPS (+31%) with an undisclosed Superior contribution. A shortfall converts a raised guide into a cut and would reset the FY2027 estimate base.

**Superior integration and financing.** $1.6B deployed (~$1.2B cash, 1,219,498 shares valued at $410.5M at close) plus a three-year earn-out with no disclosed fair value, targets or cap. Purchase accounting remains preliminary until the Q3 10-Q, and Superior's standalone financials are undisclosed, so the acquisition multiple cannot be assessed. Share dilution is small: 1.54% of pre-existing shares issued, or 1.52% ownership dilution. Total earn-out liabilities across all deals were $57.4M at 6/30/2026, with an undiscounted range of $9M–$69M but no stated maximum.

**Contract estimate risk.** Transaction prices at 6/30/2026 included ~$265M of unapproved change orders and claims in resolution, up from ~$229M, concentrated in CE&I and Power Delivery — the two segments driving growth. Reassuringly, cumulative catch-up adjustments contributed only +0.7% to revenue in Q2 and H1, down from +0.9%/+1.3%, which is consistent with estimate discipline rather than reserve harvesting.

**Joint-and-several JV liability and surety cross-default.** MasTec holds 25–50% interests in eleven civil construction JVs where members are jointly and severally liable, and $1.3B of the bond balance was issued on behalf of proportionately consolidated non-controlled JVs. The 2026 term loan carries cross-default provisions with the surety indemnity agreement, making a surety dispute a potential credit event rather than a commercial one.

**Self-insurance and cost inputs.** Gross unpaid claim liability rose 11.5% in six months to $342.1M against $27.7M of recoveries, with only $4.2M of captive cash. Management expects a high-cost environment to persist through 2026, citing steel, aluminum and copper tariff changes, fuel effects from Middle East conflict, and immigration actions affecting labor availability.

**Governance and related parties.** MasTec performs construction for a professional soccer franchise majority-owned by its Chairman and CEO: $44.4M of H1 2026 revenue (up from $29.6M) with $40.7M of receivables outstanding at 6/30/2026, equal to ~31% of quarterly net income attributable to MasTec. Disclosed and presumably audited, but a material receivable from a counterparty controlled by the two people who control the company. Additional related-party items include a $2.9M aircraft lease and $1.8M of subcontracting; the Cross Country Infrastructure relationship was terminated in Q1 2026, a genuine reduction in surface area. Goodwill and intangibles at 28.3% of assets carry impairment risk; Pipeline already holds $115.3M of accumulated goodwill impairment.

**Balance-sheet framing.** A screening Altman Z of 2.33 understates solvency: the input used zero EBIT (MasTec did not present a separate operating income subtotal before 2026) and book rather than market equity. Recomputed on 6/30/2026 data with market equity, Z ≈ 4.0 — safe zone. Bankruptcy risk is not the issue here; cash conversion and pro forma leverage are, and Altman captures neither well for a working-capital-intensive contractor.

Risk Factors: 4.5. Several dated, quantifiable binaries sit within the next two quarters, and a 38% drawdown has priced some but not all of them.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 57.6% against 51.1% one-year realized — rich by 6.5 points, but at only the 4th percentile within the current chain, and the term structure is in contango (57.6% at 12 days versus 62.2% at 103 days). The market is pricing wide outcomes without near-term panic: ±9.1% on the August 21 expiry, ±15.7% by September 18, ±27.4% by November 20. That November tenor spans the Q3 report, where Superior purchase accounting and the Q4 trajectory will be disclosed, and the implied range there is consistent with the guidance-shape risk identified above.

Positioning is ambiguous rather than directional. Put/call volume ratio is 5.48 (6.09 at the front tenor) while put/call open interest is only 0.25 — heavy put turnover against a call-dominated standing book, which reads as protection being traded around the event rather than accumulated. Near-term skew of 1.8% is characterized as balanced. ATM IV fell from 135.7% on June 18 to 57.6% as the price fell 28.2%, so the option market has normalized rather than escalated through the drawdown. Short interest is 6.8% of float at 3.6 days to cover — no crowded short, and no squeeze mechanic to lean on. Nothing here overrides the fundamental read; it corroborates that the equity's principal binary is the late-October print.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $370 (+35.8%)

Backlog of $21.4B converts as guided; Q4 delivers ~$5.07B and ~$2.71 adjusted EPS; Pipeline margins prove structurally higher on integrity, water and CO₂ mix rather than transient project luck; Power Delivery compounds high-teens on load growth with Superior lifting data-center electrical share; Communications stabilizes as install-to-the-home exits annualize. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $13.50–14.00 against consensus $12.62, working capital converts in Q4 as it historically has, leverage falls below 2.3x by mid-2027, and the multiple recovers to 27x on rising estimates. Sell-side targets averaging $427 pre-print become a reasonable anchor rather than a stale one, and the $250M unused authorization is deployed at depressed prices.

### Bear Case — $185 (−32.1%)

CE&I's backlog surge proves partly safe-harbor pull-forward, and 2027 renewables awards decelerate as the pipeline empties. Pipeline margin reverts toward the FY2025 14.9% level, removing roughly a quarter of segment EBITDA growth, while minority holders continue taking ~11% of consolidated net income. Communications declines for a third year. Q4 revenue lands nearer $4.6B, forcing a guidance trim and resetting FY2027 to $10.50. Working capital fails to convert, leaving FY2026 free cash flow negative after ~$575M of true equipment spend, and pro forma leverage sits above 3.0x as the covenant step-down to 3.50x approaches five quarters after closing. Superior's undisclosed multiple proves high, the earn-out accretes, and intangible amortization widens the GAAP wedge further. At $10.50 of FY2027 adjusted EPS and 17.5x, the shares are $184.

### Base Case — $292 (+7.2%)

FY2026 guidance is broadly met with Q4 modestly below the implied bar. FY2027 adjusted EPS of ~$12.00 (5% below consensus) reflects Pipeline normalizing toward 16%, Communications flat, and full-year Superior. At 23x — a discount to the 44.3x peer median that reflects the 33% non-GAAP wedge, 2.7x net leverage and sub-1x cash conversion — fair value is $292, corroborated at ~$297 on 14.5x FY2027 EV/EBITDA.

Probability-weighting 55% base, 22% bull, 23% bear gives an expected return of roughly +4%, below what a stock with 57.6% implied volatility and a dated Q4 binary should offer.

## Conclusion

MasTec's operating performance has improved on nearly every top-line measure: 28.3% H1 revenue growth, 120bps of adjusted EBITDA margin expansion, $21.4B of backlog, a raised FY2026 guide, and FY2027 consensus EPS up 10.2% in 90 days. The 38% decline from the May high reflects multiple compression on a crowded position that had reached roughly 47x adjusted earnings, compounded by a 0.6% non-GAAP miss and a reduced Communications outlook, rather than a collapse in earnings power. At 29.3x FY2026 guided adjusted EPS and 21.6x FY2027 consensus versus a 44.3x peer median, the valuation risk that existed at $434 has largely been removed.

What prevents a constructive rating is the combination of four specific items: a Q4 that must deliver +28.6% revenue growth and +31% adjusted EPS growth with an undisclosed Superior split; a highest-margin segment running 350–500bps above its own FY2025 level with 11% of consolidated net income leaking to minority holders; pro forma debt up 47% to fund an acquisition whose multiple cannot be assessed; and cash generation that funds neither the growth nor a buyback — $575M of true annual equipment spend, negative H1 free cash flow, DSO up seven days, and a $250M authorization left untouched while the shares fell from $434 to $272. Management chose $1.6B of leveraged M&A over repurchasing its own equity at a 38% discount to the high, and no insider has bought in the open market at any point in the disclosed window.

Base case $292 against bear case $185 gives roughly 7% upside for 32% downside, or a low-single-digit probability-weighted return. Existing holders should stay with the growth; new capital should wait for either the $235–250 zone or the late-October print that resolves the Q4 shape and the Superior economics.
