## Executive Summary

Applied Industrial's fiscal 2026 (ended 6/30/2026) ended stronger than it started, and the shape of the year matters more than its totals. Full-year revenue rose 8.8% to $4,966.7M with organic growth of 5.4%, but the June quarter produced organic growth of 9.7% (described by management as the strongest in more than three years) with EBITDA margin up 63bp to 13.13% and GAAP diluted EPS of $3.17, roughly 7.6-8.6% above street estimates depending on the basis used. Decomposing the company's own price/volume approximations, Q4 organic growth was roughly $119M, of which ~$90M (≈7.4 points) was volume versus ~$29M (≈2.4 points) price. For a distributor of consumable industrial components, a volume-led acceleration is a materially better signal than a price-led one. Management said Q1 FY2027 organic sales were running ~+7% as of August 13.

The balance sheet and returns profile justify a premium multiple in the abstract. Net debt of $135.2M against $618.2M of FY26 EBITDA is 0.22x, against a 3.75x covenant. Free cash flow of $460.5M converted at 111% of net income, capex is 0.47% of sales, and ROIC on invested capital that includes $1,017.5M of goodwill and intangibles is approximately 21% — well above any plausible 8-9% cost of capital. Management repurchased 1,162,863 shares in FY26 at an average $272.79, roughly 24% below the current price, and pre-emptively repaid $310M of revolver ahead of the January 2026 expiry of a 1.58% pay-fixed swap.

The question is not franchise quality; it is what the market is already paying for it. At $357.11 the stock trades 32.6x trailing EPS of $10.95, 30.0x the $11.90 midpoint of FY27 guidance, 21.5x EV/FY26 EBITDA, ~20.1x EV on my FY27 EBITDA estimate of ~$661M, and a 3.50% FCF yield with a 0.57% dividend. Guided FY27 EPS growth is 8.7% at the midpoint. That is roughly a 3.4x price/earnings-to-earnings-growth ratio on a business whose own preferred demand correlate — Manufacturing Capacity Utilization — was flat at 75.7 versus 75.6 through the year. Consensus estimate revisions are stable (+1.7% for next year over 90 days), not inflecting. The stock gapped to an all-time high of $374.80 on the print and closed the session down 1.5%, which is how a market behaves when good news is already owned. I retain HOLD for a fourth consecutive quarter, and the reason has changed: previously the thesis was awaiting confirmation of the demand inflection; now the inflection is confirmed and the price has moved with it, leaving the valuation as the binding constraint.

## Company Overview

Founded in Cleveland in 1923, Applied Industrial is a value-added technical distributor of industrial motion, fluid power, flow control and automation products, operating ~580 facilities with ~6,900 employees across seven countries and carrying more than 9.4 million SKUs. It is a non-exclusive authorized distributor for thousands of manufacturers, meaning AIT is the manufacturers' customer and receives purchase incentives from them, while selling to industrial end users. The 10-K's structural argument is that suppliers confine direct selling to large OEM volume transactions and refer maintenance and repair demand to distributors; that referral dynamic is the basis of the channel position.

Two segments:

| Segment | FY26 Sales | Mix | Gross margin | Operating margin | EBITDA margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Service Center (~420 sites) | $3,184.2M | 64% | 29.2% | 13.4% | 14.0% (13.7% prior) |
| Engineered Solutions (~160 sites) | $1,782.5M | 36% | 32.4% | 11.8% | 14.4% (14.8% prior) |

Service Center is local break-fix MRO — bearings, motors, belting, drives, hydraulics, filtration, plus fabricated rubber shops and conveyor service crews. Engineered Solutions is fluid power (AIT claims the largest U.S. position), specialty flow control, and advanced automation (machine vision, cobots, RFID, motion control); it engineers, integrates and repairs rather than simply distributing.

Product mix (9M FY26): Power Transmission 27.3%, General MRO 23.4%, Fluid Power 22.4%, Bearings/Linear/Seals 17.0%, Specialty Flow Control 9.9%. End markets are broad — General Industry ~38%, Industrial Machinery ~14%, Food ~11%, Metals ~10%, Forest Products ~8% — and no customer exceeds 5% of sales. Geographically the U.S. is 88% of revenue, Canada 6%, other 6%.

One clarification on the AI-adjacency question, which matters because the broader market is paying up for datacenter exposure. The 10-K says AIT's fluid conveyance and robotic capabilities are "providing new growth opportunities in areas such as datacenter infrastructure and semiconductor manufacturing." AIT sells components and integration services into those facilities; it does not build them. No revenue figure for these verticals is disclosed in any filing. The exposure is qualitative and should not be capitalized as a quantified AI theme.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings trajectory**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $4,412.8M | $4,479.4M | $4,563.4M | $4,966.7M |
| Growth | — | +1.5% | +1.9% | +8.8% |
| Gross margin | 29.2% | 29.8% | 30.3% | 30.3% |
| Operating margin | 10.7% | 11.1% | 10.9% | 11.1% |
| Net income | $346.7M | $385.8M | $393.0M | $414.5M |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $10.12 | $10.95 |

FY26's 8.8% breaks into acquisitions +3.1% ($142.2M, essentially Hydradyne), FX +0.3%, and organic +5.4% ($245.2M, of which ~$136M volume and ~$109M price).

**Where the margin expansion came from — and where it did not.** Consolidated gross margin was flat at 30.3%, absorbing a 30bp drag from LIFO. SD&A improved 10bp to 19.3% of sales. Full-year EBITDA margin rose only 12bp (12.44% vs 12.32%), which means essentially all of the year's margin improvement landed in Q4's +63bp. Within segments, the widely repeated "Engineered Solutions mix is margin-accretive" framing does not hold at the profit line for FY26: ES EBITDA margin *fell* to 14.4% from 14.8% and ES operating margin fell to 11.8% from 12.2%, while Service Center EBITDA margin *rose* to 14.0% from 13.7%. Two mechanical explanations cover most of it — $37.1M of the company's $40.1M of intangible amortization sits in ES, and ES SD&A runs 20.6% of segment sales versus 15.8% for Service Center — and the most likely proximate cause is Hydradyne, which ran a 6.1% net margin versus 8.2% consolidated in 9M FY26 while carrying fresh amortization on $126.05M of acquired intangibles. Q4 ES EBITDA margin did improve to 15.1% from 14.8%, the first evidence of reversal. Investors should treat the structural mix-accretion claim underpinning the 14% five-year EBITDA target as a management hypothesis pending FY27-FY28 evidence, not a demonstrated trend.

**Cash flow quality is the strongest part of the financial profile.**

| | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | $492.4M | $484.1M |
| Capex | $27.2M | $23.6M |
| Free cash flow | $465.2M | $460.5M |
| FCF / net income | 1.18x | 1.11x |

FY26 FCF dipped ~1% on a $65.8M working-capital swing driven by $61.5M of receivables build on a stronger second half. This is volume-driven rather than collection deterioration: DSO improved to 55.3 days from 56.6, receivables over 90 days past due fell to 1.1% from 2.1%, the bad-debt provision fell to 0.09% of sales, and inventory turns improved to 4.5x from 4.3x. Capex guidance of $35-40M for FY27 still leaves capital intensity under 0.8% of sales; AIT leases most of its 580 facilities ($213.2M of operating lease assets against $131.7M of owned property, net).

**Balance sheet.** Total debt $262.3M (revolver $74.0M plus $188.3M AR securitization), all floating at roughly 4.44-4.55%, against $127.1M of cash. Net debt/EBITDA 0.22x; the company reports credit-agreement net leverage below 0.2x against a 3.75x maximum. Liquidity is $825.8M available on a new $900M five-year revolver entered October 2025, plus an uncommitted $800M accordion. Current ratio fell to 2.58x from 3.32x solely because $261M of idle cash was deployed into buybacks and debt paydown. Goodwill and intangibles of $1,017.5M equal 33.8% of assets and 54.7% of book equity, with no impairment at the 1/1/2026 test date but $232.4M of cumulative historical write-downs since FY2002 — $167.6M of that in Engineered Solutions, the segment now being expanded by acquisition.

Two screening artifacts are worth flagging because they make AIT look weaker than it is. Standardized data feeds carry a `ppe_net` of $13.9M against the audited $131.7M, a mis-mapped tag that corrupts any fixed-asset or depreciation ratio built on it. And the frequently cited "debt/equity 0.62" is total liabilities over equity; interest-bearing debt to equity is 0.141. Recomputing the nine Piotroski signals from FY25/FY26 filings yields approximately 8 of 9, with the single miss being the current-ratio decline described above.

**Returns.** NOPAT of $418.7M (operating income $549.5M at a 23.8% effective rate) on invested capital of $1,996.9M implies ROIC of ~21% on ending capital and 20.8% on average. Reported ROE is 22.3% with debt/equity of 0.14, so the return is not a leverage artifact, and the invested-capital base includes the full goodwill balance rather than a written-down one.

**Two earnings mechanics that will shape FY27.** First, the January 2019 pay-fixed swap (1.58% weighted-average fixed rate; all-in 2.48% on $384M as of 6/30/2025) matured 1/31/2026. Net interest expense went from $0.6M in FY25 to $7.9M in FY26, and swap settlement receipts of $12.1M (FY25) and $5.8M (FY26) cease entirely. Q4 net interest of $3.556M annualizes to ~$14.2M and $262.3M at ~4.5% implies ~$11.8M gross, so a normalized FY27 range of roughly $11-14M implies a $3-6M pre-tax ($0.06-0.12 per share) headwind. Treat that as a sensitivity, not a forecast — it does not adjust for cash rebuild, further paydown, or M&A borrowing. Second, LIFO expense tripled to $21.5M pre-tax ($0.43/share) from $7.7M ($0.16), a ~$0.27 year-over-year EPS drag, and it is why reported gross margin was flat despite favorable mix and positive price. The LIFO reserve is $254.4M, half the $509.0M carrying value, with 13.2% of domestic inventory dollars in layers added in the 1970s. If cost inflation moderates, LIFO expense falls and gross margin gets a tailwind not embedded in FY27 guidance, which explicitly assumes "broader inflationary headwinds." The reverse case — a unit inventory decline liquidating 1970s layers — would produce a large one-time gross-margin gain of very low earnings quality that should not be capitalized.

## Growth Analysis

Guidance for FY2027: diluted EPS $11.65-$12.15, total sales growth +4.0% to +6.5%, EBITDA margin 12.5-12.8%, explicitly excluding future acquisitions and share repurchases. Intermediate five-year objectives were raised to $7B of sales and 14% EBITDA margin.

Building the guidance from the bottom validates it as achievable rather than stretched. Midpoints imply sales of ~$5,227M and EBITDA of ~$661M (+7.0%). Subtract ~$63M of D&A (per the amortization schedule, $37.4M intangibles plus ~$26M property) and ~$12M of interest for ~$586M pre-tax; at a 23.8% rate that is ~$446M, and on a ~37.25M average share count (FY26 averaged 37.857M; Q4 averaged 37.409M; July repurchases take the run rate lower) that is ~$11.98, modestly above the $11.90 midpoint. FY27 growth guidance of +4.0-6.5% is all-organic, against FY26 organic of +5.4%, Q4 organic of +9.7%, and Q1 quarter-to-date of ~+7%. Either the guide is conservative or management genuinely expects second-half comparisons to bite; the release points to the latter, citing "more difficult comparisons as fiscal 2027 plays out."

Three constraints temper the growth case.

**Growth is entirely a U.S. story.** Of $245.2M of FY26 organic growth, $242.2M (98.8%) came from the United States. Canada organic was +$1.2M (+0.4%) for the year and −5.9% in Q3; other international was +$1.8M (+0.7%). Foreign operations are 12% of sales and contributed nothing to growth. AIT is functionally a levered play on U.S. industrial activity and reshoring. The benign reading is that Canadian and Mexican softness reflects trade-policy uncertainty — a risk the FY26 10-K newly emphasizes — and is therefore cyclical.

**The five-year target is M&A-dependent, and FY26 M&A was $11.4M.** Compounding $4.967B at 5% organic reaches $6.339B, leaving roughly $661M of acquired revenue to hit $7B — approximately $1.0B of purchase price at ~1.5x sales. FY26 deployment was $11.4M (essentially one $9.0M deal) versus $293.4M in FY25. Capacity is not the constraint: $460M of annual FCF, 0.22x leverage, $825.8M of revolver availability. The 10-K itself supplies the likely explanation, noting that private equity firms "increasingly compete with us for acquisitions, which can increase the cost of potential acquisitions and reduce the number of suitable opportunities." Hydradyne integration bandwidth is another plausible cause. No filing states a deliberate pause, and I draw no motive inference, but the target arithmetic requires deal flow that FY26 did not produce.

**Margin expansion is back-loaded.** The $7B/14% objective implies a 7.1% sales CAGR, a 9.7% EBITDA CAGR, and +156bp of EBITDA margin. FY27 guidance delivers only +6 to +36bp, meaning roughly 120-150bp is deferred to FY2029-FY2031, and it depends on the ES mix thesis that FY26 results did not support.

Two areas show absolute contraction. Applying 10-Q mix percentages to segment sales, specialty flow control fell from ~$372M to ~$360M over nine months (≈−3%), and Chem/Petrochem fell from ~$230M to ~$210M (≈−9%). These are approximations from rounded percentages and should be read as directional only, but Q3 MD&A language ("modest demand improvement across our flow control operations") is consistent with a business coming off a weak base.

The macro backdrop cuts both ways. ISM PMI moved from 49.0 (June 2025) to 53.3 (June 2026), from contraction to expansion; Manufacturing Capacity Utilization was flat at 75.7 versus 75.6, and ~75-76 is not a high-utilization replacement-cycle reading. The opposing macro consideration is that AIT sells into plant run rates; a genuine industrial slowdown compresses volume, price, and supplier rebate income simultaneously. Analyst targets are tightly clustered around spot: mean $359.5, median $365, high $380, low $317, with 6 buys and 2 holds and no rating changes. The stock closed August 14 at 99.3% of the consensus mean. On stocks that look cheap against rising estimates, consensus targets function as lagging floors. That logic does not extend to a name at 30x forward with +1.7% revisions; here the target and the price reflect the same set of assumptions, and proximity offers no independent confirmation in either direction.

Two elements of genuine upside optionality sit outside the base multiple. First, FY27 guidance excludes buybacks, and the board authorized 3.0 million shares (~8.1% of outstanding) on 4/22/2026 with 2,854,252 remaining at year-end; retiring even 1 million shares would add roughly $0.20-0.25 to FY27 EPS. Second, if product-cost inflation moderates, the $21.5M LIFO expense reverses toward the $7.7M FY25 level, a ~$0.25-0.30 per-share tailwind not in guidance. Against those, the swap roll-off costs $0.06-0.12 and the effective tax rate rose to 23.8% from 21.6% partly on a shrinking R&D credit "due to lower qualifying activities."

**Scenario framework (12 months):**

- **Base — $370 (+3.6%):** FY27 EPS of ~$12.10 (slightly above guidance midpoint, reflecting the ~7% quarter-to-date organic run rate and modest buyback) at 30.5x, in line with the current multiple. - **Bull — $425 (+19.0%):** Organic growth holds 8-9% on datacenter, semiconductor, food and reshoring demand; LIFO drag reverses; ES EBITDA margin recovers toward 15%+ as Hydradyne integrates; M&A restarts at ~1.5x sales. FY27 EPS ~$12.50 at 34x.

Probability-weighting 55% base / 25% bull / 20% bear gives ~$361, about +1.1% plus a 0.57% dividend. That is below any reasonable cost of equity and the distribution is negatively skewed (−28.6% downside versus +19.0% upside). It is not sufficiently unfavorable to justify actively trimming a quality compounder that just accelerated, but it does not support new capital.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K describes industrial distribution as highly fragmented and names competitor categories rather than firms: specialist and general-line distributors across bearings, power transmission, fluid power, flow control, rubber, linear motion and automation; tool/safety/MRO suppliers; OEMs and integrators; and catalog/e-commerce sellers. Competition is "largely focused in the local service area."

Three structural observations.

**The moat is local and technical, not purchasing scale.** AIT's products attach to customers' most critical operating assets, where high cost of failure, specification requirements and system complexity demand local inventory, application expertise and aftermarket support. That is why AIT earns ~30% gross margins against the low-to-mid-20s typical of broadline industrial resale, why e-commerce disintermediation has advanced more slowly in technical MRO than in commodity MRO, and why 9.4 million SKUs across 580 leased local facilities can still produce a 21% ROIC. On a stated addressable market "over $80 billion," AIT's $5.0B of revenue implies roughly 6% share, and 5.4% organic growth in a year when capacity utilization was flat is consistent with modest share gain.

**The trajectory is improving in fluid power and mixed elsewhere.** Engineered Solutions organic growth of 12.9% in Q4 in the segment where AIT claims the largest U.S. fluid power position is the strongest single competitive data point in the release. Against that, specialty flow control appears to have contracted in absolute dollars over nine months and ES EBITDA margin declined year-over-year.

**Supplier authorizations are the structural vulnerability, and rebate income is an unquantified pro-cyclical lever.** The 10-K states that key supplier relationships, though longstanding, are "terminable by either party," and that authorizations vary by geography and product line, particularly in fluid power, flow control and automation, exactly the growth segment. Separately, "in some cases, to earn incentives, we must achieve year-over-year growth in purchases with the supplier," and in a downturn AIT may be "less inclined to build inventory to take advantage of certain incentive programs." That is a downturn amplifier layered on top of volume loss, and AIT does not quantify supplier incentives anywhere in its filings. For a distributor where such income is typically a meaningful share of gross profit, this is a genuine disclosure gap and one reason the bear-case margin compression could exceed a simple operating-leverage estimate.

One discipline point on the frequently repeated diversification claim: changing revenue mix (Industrial Machinery 13.2%→14.2%, Fluid Power 20.8%→22.4%, Chem/Petrochem 6.9%→5.8%) establishes shifting end-market exposure, not improved customer diversification. The actual concentration disclosure — no customer above 5% — is separately stated and flat-to-favorable.

## Risk Assessment

Solvency is not a live question: Altman Z of 4.48, net leverage 0.22x, roughly 17x covenant headroom. The risks that matter are earnings durability and multiple compression.

**1. Cyclicality is the dominant exposure and is not priced at 30x forward.** The 10-K states plainly that "our business depends heavily on the operating levels of our customers." Management's own preferred correlate, Manufacturing Capacity Utilization, was flat at ~75.7 through FY26, and the 10-K notes the Manufacturing Industrial Production index "decreased slightly over the fiscal year." A downturn hits volume, price and rebate income together.

**2. Supplier authorization termination and unquantified rebate dependence**, as described above.

**3. Execution risk on raised intermediate targets.** 120-150bp of the 156bp EBITDA-margin objective is deferred beyond FY28, and roughly $660M of acquired revenue is required against $11.4M of FY26 M&A.

**4. Interest-rate and inflation mechanics.** All $262.3M of debt is floating; the 1.58% swap subsidy that flattered FY21-FY25 is gone, and any future M&A finances at ~4.5% rather than ~2.5%. FY27 guidance explicitly assumes continued inflationary headwinds; if tariffs push product costs higher, LIFO expense rises further.

**5. Goodwill and intangibles at 54.7% of book equity**, with $167.6M of historical impairments in the very segment now being expanded by acquisition. A book-value and credibility risk in a downcycle, not a solvency risk.

**6. Verification gaps.** A 6/25/2026 8-K under Item 5.02 (departure/election of directors or officers) could not be retrieved. It was not a CEO or CFO change — both signed the April 10-Q and Schrimsher was quoted as CEO on 8/13/2026 — but the contents are unknown. No Q4 call transcript was available, so any Q&A quantification of datacenter exposure, tariff pass-through, or M&A pipeline specifics is not in evidence.

**7. Insider selling.** Roughly $13M of discretionary open-market sales between February and May 2026 with no offsetting purchases: CEO Schrimsher 19,207 shares at $284.49-$288.29 (~12.8% of position), Fluid Power VP/GM Hoffner 16,000 shares (~28% of position), a sales VP ~27% of position, and a director 5,000 shares. Two mitigants matter. All February sales cleared at $284-$292, roughly 20% below the current price, which is inconsistent with informed selling into a top; and Director Andrews' 3,845-share June transaction was an exchange-fund contribution disclosed in the 10-Q, a diversification exchange rather than a cash exit. August 8-11 transactions were annual grants and Code-F tax withholding, not discretionary sales. Whether the February sales were executed under pre-existing 10b5-1 plans cannot be verified. The signal is soft; it would carry more weight if paired with deteriorating fundamentals, which is not the case. It carries somewhat more weight than it otherwise would because estimate revisions are only mildly positive rather than strongly rising.

Put/call open-interest ratio of 0.68 is call-tilted. The one item to note is a 3.6% near-term put/call skew described as elevated put demand at the front tenors, flipping to −2.0% at three months. Read together with a 2.3% short interest and 2.7 days to cover, positioning is benign: no crowded bearish bet, modest near-term downside hedging, and post-event vol cheap enough that anyone wanting protection into the late-October Q1 print can buy it inexpensively. Nothing here contradicts the fundamental read; the low IV percentile is mildly favorable for buying optionality rather than stock.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($425, +19%).** The June quarter marked a genuine cyclical turn, not a restocking blip. Volume-led organic growth of 7.4% in Q4 and ~7% quarter-to-date in Q1 FY27, corroborated by ISM's move to 53.3, means FY27 guidance of +4.0-6.5% is conservative. Guidance excludes buybacks against a 2.85M-share authorization and excludes any M&A against $460M of annual FCF and 0.22x leverage. LIFO expense of $21.5M reverses if input inflation moderates, adding $0.25-0.30 per share of unguided margin. Engineered Solutions EBITDA margin already turned up in Q4 (15.1% vs 14.8%) as Hydradyne integrates, restoring the mix-accretion thesis and putting the 14% five-year target within reach. A ~21% ROIC business with 111% FCF conversion and no customer above 5% of sales holds a 34x multiple in an expansion.

**Bear case ($255, −29%).** Manufacturing capacity utilization never left the mid-70s, and the Q4 volume surge was deferred-maintenance catch-up plus channel restocking ahead of tariff-driven price increases. Comparisons harden through FY27 exactly as management warns, organic growth turns negative, and supplier rebate income — pro-cyclical, growth-conditioned, and entirely unquantified — falls alongside volumes, compressing gross margin by more than operating-leverage math implies. The swap subsidy is gone, all debt floats at 4.5%, and the tax rate is rising. The five-year target requires ~$1.0B of acquisitions in a market where private equity is bidding up assets, so either the target slips or AIT overpays. At that point a cyclical distributor earning ~$10.20 re-rates to the ~25x it traded at ten months ago.

**What tips the balance toward neither.** The bull case is credible and the operating evidence supports it. The problem is that at $357.11 investors pay 30x forward and 20x EV/EBITDA for approximately 9% guided EPS growth, with consensus targets clustered at $359.5 mean and $365 median, revisions drifting +1.7%, and the stock having gapped to $374.80 on the print and closed the day lower. Probability-weighted expected return of roughly +1.7% including the dividend does not compensate for a −29% bear case. Owning the compounding is reasonable; paying up for it here is not.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria
