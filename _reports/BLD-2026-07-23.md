**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TopBuild Corp no longer exists as an independent public company. On July 1, 2026, QXO, Inc. (NYSE: QXO) completed its acquisition of TopBuild in a transaction valued at roughly $17 billion, and BLD became a wholly owned subsidiary. The SEC XBRL entity is now reported under the renamed legal entity "QXO Insulation, LLC." Former BLD holders have already received merger consideration: a per-share election of $505.00 in cash or 20.200 QXO shares, with 91.0% electing cash. After proration, cash-electing holders received approximately $249.71 in cash plus 10.211 QXO shares per BLD share. There is no independent BLD common stock to buy or sell.

The $354.53 "current price" and $9.9B market cap embedded in the source data are stale, pre-close reference figures. They are not a live quote and materially understate what holders actually received; the $505 cash election is roughly 42% above that number, consistent with a normal takeover premium. Any thesis framed as "BLD as a going concern" is moot. The only live security relevant to a forward-looking investor is QXO, and evaluating QXO requires a separate analysis of a leveraged building-products roll-up whose shares were down about 16% year-to-date as of early July 2026 and whose stated $300M+ synergy and $18B+ revenue targets are unaudited management guidance for 2030.

Given that BLD does not trade, we assign an AVOID recommendation; there is no position to establish. The scores below reflect the standalone business quality captured in the FY2025 10-K (a cash-generative, high-return leader in a cyclical trough) but are deliberately anchored low on valuation and growth because the security itself has been retired and the residual exposure (QXO stock, for the minority who elected or were deemed to elect equity) carries integration, leverage, and cyclical risk that the market was pricing skeptically.

## Company Overview

Prior to the close, TopBuild was North America's largest installer and specialty distributor of insulation and related building products, operating more than 450 locations across the U.S. and Canada. It reported two segments:

- **Installation Services** (~59% of net sales; $3.18B in 2025): insulation installation across walls, attics, floors and roofing assemblies, plus complementary products and commercial roofing. FY2025 operating margin 18.5%.
- **Specialty Distribution** (~47% of gross sales, $2.52B in 2025 before ~$297M of intercompany eliminations): distribution of insulation and mechanical insulation to contractors, including a less-cyclical repair/replacement mechanical insulation business. FY2025 operating margin 12.8%.

The business held a genuine scale advantage in a fragmented, low-barrier industry, deriving purchasing power, building-science expertise, and breadth across installation plus distribution. Its value proposition is tied to energy efficiency and benefits structurally from tightening building codes and, more recently, data-center construction demand.

That business is now a component of QXO, the acquisitive building-products distributor led by Brad Jacobs (XPO, GXO, RXO, United Rentals, United Waste). QXO frames the combined entity as targeting more than $18B annual revenue, more than $2B adjusted EBITDA, and at least $300M of annual synergies by 2030, with a longer-term goal of $50B revenue within a decade through M&A and organic growth.

## Financial Analysis

The FY2025 10-K (filed February 26, 2026, before the deal closed) captures a business in a cyclical soft patch that had levered up to consolidate, the profile that makes a company a roll-up target.

Key dynamics:

- The +1.5% revenue growth in 2025 was entirely acquisition-driven (+8.8%), masking an 8.1% organic volume decline as single-family residential demand weakened through the year and multifamily stayed weak.
- Margin compression reflected lower volume, customer price pressure on residential distribution products, $14.5M of one-time branch consolidation/headcount costs, and $11.4M of inventory step-up amortization from the SPI acquisition.
- The company deployed roughly $1.9B on acquisitions (Progressive in July 2025, plus SPI). Goodwill rose from $2.11B to $3.05B and intangibles from $558M to $1.35B; total assets grew from $4.74B to $6.61B.
- Leverage rose materially: about $2.0B of new debt was issued in 2025 (refinanced term loan, delayed-draw term loan, 5.625% Senior Notes), lifting interest expense by roughly $31M. Reported D/E was 1.85. The XBRL "long-term debt" line of only $11M is a classification artifact; the MD&A and cash-flow statements confirm the ~$2B issuance and should override the headline figure.
- Altman Z-Score of 2.17 sits in the grey zone, driven by leverage and a goodwill-heavy balance sheet (equity/assets 0.35), not liquidity distress. Current ratio was a healthy 1.94; ROE 22.5%; ROA 7.9%.
- Capital return: $434M of buybacks in 2025 (following $966M in 2024), reducing share count from ~30.5M to ~28.3M. No dividend.

The financial health score of 5.0 reflects strong free cash flow generation offset by rising leverage, a grey-zone Z-score, and goodwill/intangibles at 67% of total assets. It is moot for a standalone equity thesis given the takeout.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone growth had stalled: organic volume fell 8.1% in 2025, and the only top-line growth came from acquisitions. Longer-run secular tailwinds (energy-efficiency codes, data-center insulation and waterproofing, gradual multifamily recovery) were real but not sufficient to offset the residential cyclical downturn. Elevated interest rates suppressing single-family construction, the primary driver of insulation demand, reinforced the headwind through the year.

Growth prospects for the retired BLD security are not a coherent question. To the extent a former holder retains QXO exposure, the growth thesis migrates to QXO's ability to hit its 2030 revenue and synergy targets through integration and further M&A. Those are management targets, not delivered results, and the market has been skeptical. The growth score of 4.0 reflects the stalled standalone trajectory and the speculative, unproven nature of the acquirer's forward targets.

## Valuation Assessment

There is no live valuation to assess for BLD. The stock was taken out at $505/share cash-equivalent, and the residual value now depends on QXO's share price for the minority who took or were deemed to take stock.

For context on where the standalone business would have screened: at the pre-deal reference price, peers traded at a median P/E of roughly 13.4 (TMHC 10.7, TOL 11.1, MTH 13.4, IBP 22.1, SKY 22.0). TopBuild's higher-quality installation-plus-distribution model and 22.5% ROE would have justified a premium within that band, but the takeout price already crystallized any upside. Cheapness relative to peers is not a reason to own a security that no longer exists. A low multiple without an independent catalyst is a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal; the valuation score is 3.0, reflecting a security retired at a fixed price and residual QXO exposure that carries no identified mispricing in the bull's favor.

## Competitive Landscape

Within its industry, TopBuild was the clear scale leader in a fragmented, ~$800B building-products distribution market, with a differentiated combination of labor-intensive installation and specialty distribution. That competitive position was stable-to-strong on quality but in a demand trough on volume. Folded into QXO, the combined entity gains breadth (QXO is a leading roofing/waterproofing distributor and a large player in lumber/building materials) but also inherits integration complexity, since QXO is moving into larger, labor-intensive businesses that differ from pure distribution.

The competitive score of 5.5 reflects a durable market-leading franchise now subordinated to a roll-up strategy whose execution is unproven. The trajectory question is no longer about BLD's share gains but about whether QXO can integrate a labor-heavy installer while capturing procurement and cross-selling synergies against a soft residential backdrop.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is definitional: BLD is no longer a tradeable security, so any "investment" premised on it is based on stale data. For the residual QXO exposure, the material risks are integration execution, combined leverage, and continued residential cyclicality.

Standalone risks documented in the FY2025 10-K remain instructive for the underlying business now inside QXO:

1. **Cyclicality / rate sensitivity** — demand is tied to residential new construction and manufacturing; 2025 already showed the downside with single-family demand weakening throughout the year.
2. **Supplier concentration in fiberglass** — reliance on a limited number of large suppliers, with a history of shortage/oversupply pricing swings.
3. **Customer pricing pressure** — consolidated homebuilders exert persistent downward pressure, explicitly cited as a 2025 margin driver.
4. **Labor and immigration enforcement** — skilled-trade shortages plus heightened immigration enforcement risk to workforce availability and cost.
5. **Goodwill/intangible impairment** — $4.4B combined (67% of assets); a construction downturn could trigger non-cash impairments (the company carries $762M of accumulated impairment from the 2008–2010 crash as a reminder of severity).
6. **Integration risk** — the 10-K devoted an entire section to M&A integration risk, prescient given the company was itself absorbed within nine months of its own acquisition spree.

The 91% cash election and roughly 78% approval of votes cast signal that holders largely preferred certain cash over QXO equity, a mild vote of caution on the combined entity's near-term prospects, consistent with QXO's YTD decline and KeyBanc's post-approval price-target cut to $28 from $32. The risk score of 4.5 reflects the retirement of the security plus the leverage and integration overhang attached to the residual QXO exposure.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
There is no bull case for BLD as a standalone equity; the position has been closed at $505/share cash-equivalent. For a former holder who took or was deemed to take QXO stock, the bull case rests entirely on QXO: successful integration of a high-quality installer, delivery of the 2030 synergy target, and re-rating as the residential cycle recovers and data-center demand accelerates. KeyBanc's Overweight rating implies meaningful upside from the depressed ~$28 level, contingent on execution.

### Bear Case
For residual QXO holders, the bear case is a leveraged roll-up integrating a labor-intensive business into a soft residential market, with synergy targets that are unaudited and years out, combined leverage elevated by the ~$17B deal, and a stock the market has already marked down ~16% YTD. The 91% cash election signals that the informed holder base preferred to exit rather than underwrite that risk.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria**: BLD cannot be re-entered; it has been acquired and delisted. There is no future scenario in which BLD trades as an independent equity. An investor interested in the underlying insulation/distribution franchise must evaluate QXO on its own merits: leverage trajectory, synergy delivery cadence, integration milestones for the installation business, and residential/data-center demand.

**Timeline**: The merger closed July 1, 2026. There is no waiting period after which BLD becomes investable again.

**Monitoring for QXO exposure (residual holders)**: Track QXO's ability to hit its 2030 synergy target, quarterly progress on integrating the labor-intensive installation segment, combined leverage and interest coverage, and the residential construction demand backdrop (mortgage rates, housing starts). QXO analyst posture (KeyBanc Overweight, $28 PT post-cut) is the near-term reference.

## Conclusion

TopBuild was a high-quality, cash-generative market leader that entered 2025 in a cyclical trough, levered up to consolidate, and was itself consolidated into QXO's roll-up within nine months. The transaction closed July 1, 2026, at $505/share cash-equivalent, a ~42% premium to the stale reference price still circulating in market data. There is no independent BLD equity to own, and any analysis premised on BLD as a going concern is obsolete.

Our recommendation is **AVOID**. The underlying business quality was genuinely strong (22.5% ROE, $697M FCF, market-leading scale), but the security has been retired and cannot be bought or sold. Investors seeking exposure to this franchise must analyze QXO, a leveraged building-products roll-up whose shares the market has been pricing skeptically (down ~16% YTD, price target cut post-approval) and whose synergy and revenue targets remain unaudited guidance. The overall score of 4.5 reflects the retirement of the security and the integration/leverage overhang on the only remaining exposure, rather than any deterioration in the operating business the FY2025 filings describe.
