## Executive Summary

Palomar has done what it said it would do on the top line. Gross written premium reached $1,260.3M in the first half of 2026, up 34.3%, with net earned premium up 59.4% to $548.4M as the company retained a larger share of its own risk. Four straight quarters of adjusted-EPS beats (6.2%, 3.0%, 5.1%, 21.4%) and consensus adjusted EPS rising 4.3% over 90 days to $10.05 for 2026 and $11.30 for 2027 put the forward multiple at 13.6x and 12.1x. Against roughly 28% expected adjusted-EPS growth this year, that is not an expensive stock on an earnings basis, and the sell-side low target of $152 sits above the current price.

The Q2 10-Q, filed August 5, changed the quality of that growth in ways that matter. GAAP net income rose 6.8% in the first half to $95.5M while net earned premium rose 59.4%. The adjusted combined ratio excluding catastrophes went from 71.1% to 76.4% — roughly 530 basis points of underlying margin erosion in a period when catastrophe losses were net *favorable* in both years. Roughly 337bps of the 920bp loss-ratio increase is attributable to $18.5M of pre-tax losses on livestock put options booked inside losses and LAE without hedge accounting, and a further large portion to a deliberate decision to retain more crop premium and loss (net earned premium ratio 43.9% → 51.9%). Those are mechanical and partly reversible. What is not reversible is the mix: casualty is now the largest line at 32.0% of GWP growing 45.5%, earthquake — the only line with a defensible moat — grew 0.9% and fell from 30.0% to 22.5% of the book, and the company reports a single segment with no line-level loss ratios, so casualty pricing adequacy cannot be verified from public disclosure.

The balance sheet also changed character. A debt-free insurer took on $295.8M of term debt in one quarter to fund the $314.0M Gray Surety acquisition, which has produced a $5.8M net loss in five months and which, on the company's own pro forma disclosure, would have *reduced* H1 2025 net income. Tangible book value fell 15.6% to $744.2M as goodwill and intangibles went from $61.1M to $236.8M. At $136.82 the shares trade at 3.65x book ($37.46) and 4.81x tangible book ($28.42). That multiple requires the mid-20s adjusted ROE to persist through a mix shift into longer-tailed, more competitive lines. Three prior reports carried a BUY at prices between roughly $110 and $138; the price is essentially unchanged from the late-July snapshot while the underwriting-quality evidence has weakened. That combination argues for stepping back to HOLD rather than repeating the call.

## Company Overview

Palomar is a specialty P&C insurance holding company founded in 2014, domiciled in Delaware, headquartered in La Jolla, California, and listed on NASDAQ. It underwrites through four rated carriers — PSIC (admitted, Oregon/California-commercially domiciled, A), PESIC (excess & surplus, Arizona, A), FIA (surety, New Jersey, A, acquired 1/1/2025), and PCSC (contract surety, Louisiana, **A-**, acquired 1/31/2026) — plus a Bermuda internal reinsurer (PSRE) and a consolidated Hawaii reciprocal exchange (Laulima) treated as a variable interest entity. Laulima's ~$42.1M of H1 GWP (3.3% of total) is consolidated, but its assets can only settle its own obligations and its creditors have no recourse to Palomar's general credit, so those economics are not straightforwardly shareholder economics.

The book was recategorized into five lines in 2026, with prior periods restated:

Distribution runs through retail agents, program administrators, wholesale brokers, and fronting partnerships. The gap between the GAAP and non-GAAP lines is the central fact of this quarter. GAAP ROE fell from 22.7% to 19.9% while adjusted ROE rose from 25.3% to 26.4% — the two measures point in opposite directions. The bridge from $95.5M GAAP to $126.9M adjusted net income (+32.9%) rests on stock-based compensation of $16.2M (up 60.8%), intangible amortization of $15.2M (from $2.1M), $7.4M of transaction expenses, and $2.3M of catastrophe-bond expense, net of $4.9M of investment gains and $5.0M of tax.

Two of those add-backs deserve skepticism. Stock compensation is a recurring economic transfer to employees, and the company's own tax footnote confirms it is consequential: the effective rate of 22.5% (H1) and 24.7% (Q2) exceeds the 21% statutory rate "primarily due to non-deductible executive compensation expense." An expense that pushes the tax rate above statutory is being added back as non-representative. Intangible amortization is genuinely non-cash and the add-back is standard practice among specialty insurers, but $93.8M of amortizable intangibles remain with a disclosed run-off of $14.2M for the rest of 2026, $15.0M in 2027, $13.0M in 2028 and declining thereafter — the add-back persists for years and grows with each acquisition. Consensus estimates in the data ($10.05 FY2026, $11.30 FY2027) are on the adjusted basis, consistent with the $2.36 Q2 actual against a $2.22 estimate, so the forward multiples below are internally consistent; the GAAP multiple is materially higher.

| **Adj. combined ex-cat** | **76.8%** | **73.1%** | **76.4%** | **71.1%** |

Catastrophe losses were net favorable in both periods, so this is attritional deterioration. The loss ratio rose 920bps while the expense ratio improved only 150bps — the offset from ceding commissions on the new lines is incomplete. Management identifies three drivers: growth in lines inherently subject to attritional loss (casualty, crop, inland marine/property), a deliberate 2026 decision to retain more crop premium and loss, and lower excess-of-loss reinsurance pricing lifting the net earned premium ratio from 43.9% to 51.9%. Separately, $18.5M of pre-tax derivative losses on livestock put options (no hedge accounting elected) sit inside losses and LAE, worth roughly 337bps on $548.4M of net earned premium; excluding that line, the H1 loss ratio computes to 30.6% rather than 33.9%.

The honest read is that a large share of the compression is mechanical — a reinsurance-structure choice plus a mark on a $12.4M-notional derivative position whose fair value fell from $9.9M to $5.0M. The uncomfortable read is that the mix shift is permanent, casualty and crop are structurally lower-margin than earthquake, and 76.4% adjusted ex-cat in a zero-catastrophe half-year is therefore the *best case* rather than the run rate. With one segment and no line-level ratios, those explanations cannot be distinguished from public filings.

Reserve development is a genuine positive: net favorable prior-year development of $14.3M in Q2 (vs $6.5M) and $24.7M in H1 (vs $10.8M), attributed to inland marine, property, and prior-year crop. It is not attributed to casualty. Note that the $24.7M release is supporting the current loss ratio; current-accident-year net incurred losses were $210.7M in H1 2026 against $95.8M a year earlier.

### Balance sheet and cash flow

Total assets grew $913.2M in six months to $3,964.2M. Investments reached $1,678.3M and premiums receivable $655.9M (+$203.0M). Reinsurance recoverable on unpaid losses is $523.8M, more than half of stockholders' equity — reinsurance does not relieve Palomar of primary liability. Goodwill and intangibles went from $61.1M to $236.8M. Equity rose only $38.3M to $980.9M against $95.5M of net income, because $64.1M of repurchases and $12.7M of other comprehensive loss absorbed most of it; tangible equity fell $137.4M to $744.2M.

Leverage: $295.8M term loan against $980.9M equity, 23.2% debt-to-equity, from zero. That is unremarkable for a P&C holding company, and there are no revolver or FHLB borrowings. The covenant package is the notable feature — permissible debt-to-capital, minimum consolidated net worth, minimum risk-based capital, and **minimum A.M. Best financial strength rating**. A catastrophe or reserve charge severe enough to trigger a downgrade would be simultaneously a distribution problem and a credit event on $295.8M of debt. Compliance was confirmed at 6/30/2026 and the thresholds are typically set with headroom, so this is a tail transmission channel rather than a live concern.

Investment portfolio quality is good: $1,502.9M of AFS fixed maturities against $1,523.0M amortized cost, a $20.1M net unrealized loss attributed to rates rather than credit, no allowance for credit losses, and no Level 3 securities. Composition is $741.7M corporate, $702.3M mortgage/asset-backed, $58.9M governments/munis, plus $129.5M equities. One forward liquidity item: $164.3M of unfunded limited-partnership commitments (against $40.9M invested), roughly 17% of equity, with no disclosed call schedule, on a balance sheet that must stay liquid for catastrophe claims.

Operating cash flow of $214.2M in H1 was up only 2.9% year over year against 59% net earned premium growth, with premiums receivable up $203.0M. Crop premium recognition concentrates in Q3 by management's explicit description, so H1 figures should not be annualized; the flat cash-flow comparison nonetheless suggests conversion is lagging earned premium growth.

## Growth Analysis

The multi-year record is unambiguous: revenue $327M (2022) → $376M (2023) → $554M (2024) → $876M (2025); net income $52M → $79M → $118M → $197M; FY2025 GWP approximately $2.0bn with $7.17 GAAP and $7.86 adjusted diluted EPS. Management's 55% GWP CAGR framing starts from $16.6M and should not be extrapolated, but the recent absolute growth is real and only partly acquired.

Forward consensus: FY2026 adjusted EPS $10.05 (+27.9% on FY2025's $7.86), FY2027 $11.30 (+12.4%). Revisions are positive but modest — +4.3% over 90 days on the current year, +1.9% on next year, with the current quarter marked down 1.6% over 30 days. This is steady positive momentum, not the >30% revision velocity that flags a genuine earnings inflection, so the appropriate treatment is confirmatory rather than thesis-driving. The FY2027 deceleration to 12% is worth noting: consensus already expects growth to normalize sharply.

The composition of growth is where I part company with the prior BUY thesis. Earthquake — a peril most admitted carriers exclude, supported by a $3.9bn excess-of-loss tower and repeat catastrophe-bond issuance, with genuine pricing power and few direct competitors — grew 0.9%. Whether that reflects deliberate seismic aggregate management, rate softening, or market saturation is not disclosed. Meanwhile the growth engine is E&S casualty (32.0% of GWP, +45.5%), where Palomar is a recent entrant competing against far larger, longer-tenured specialists, and where growth is described as coming from "new business generated with existing partners... expansion of our distribution footprint, and new partnerships" — a distribution story rather than a proprietary underwriting edge. Crop (+88.3%) operates inside a federally administered program where the USDA Risk Management Agency sets rates, terms, and forms under a Standard Reinsurance Agreement with the FCIC; the profit pool is a policy variable, and the 10-K warns that federal reinsurance levels could be reduced by legislation. Surety (+178.7%) was bought, twice.

Product and geographic diversification did improve — no line exceeds 32% versus earthquake at 30% a year ago, and California fell from 32.3% to 25.6% of GWP. That is diversification of *product and geography*. The filings disclose no program-administrator, fronting-partner, or customer concentration data, so counterparty diversification is unknown rather than confirmed.

## Competitive Landscape

Palomar's competitive position is not uniform. Earthquake is the franchise — modeling capability plus persistent access to $3.9bn of XOL limit, a $20.0M per-event retention, and four outstanding catastrophe bonds through Torrey Pines Re ($360M closed Q2 2026 for 6/1/26–6/1/29, $525M to 2028, $420M to 2027, with the $200M 2023 bond matured). That capacity cannot be assembled quickly by a new entrant. It is also the line growing 0.9%.

E&S casualty is the growth engine and the least defended position. PESIC now writes 45.6% of consolidated GWP. The E&S market has expanded as admitted carriers retrenched, but this is a crowded market of larger specialists and it is precisely where social inflation, third-party litigation funding, and adverse jury awards concentrate. Palomar's own risk factors flag all three. Crop competes on distribution and expense efficiency inside a federal program, not on underwriting differentiation. Surety is structurally attractive — low loss ratios in benign credit, fee-like economics, uncorrelated with earthquake — but credit-cyclical, with contract surety losses clustering when contractors fail. PCSC carries A-, one notch below the other carriers.

Two industry dynamics cut both ways. Management cites lower XOL pricing at the 6/1/2026 renewal, which is a margin tailwind for a heavy ceder — but cheap reinsurance is also what lets small competitors write large limits, so it lowers the barrier to entry in Palomar's own niches. And the AI risk factor explicitly contemplates competitive displacement: "our competitors may incorporate AI more effectively than we do, causing us to lose market share."

The trajectory judgment: scale and diversification are improving while the average defensibility of the revenue base is declining. That combination supports a competitive-position score below neutral despite a growing franchise.

## Risk Assessment

**Casualty reserve opacity (highest weight).** Casualty is 32.0% of GWP and growing 45.5%; E&S casualty losses emerge over three to seven years; favorable development is attributed to inland marine, property, and crop, not casualty; and single-segment reporting provides no line-level loss ratio. Heavy quota-share cession further dampens the net P&L signal from any single accident year. This is the risk most likely to matter in 2028-2029 and least verifiable today.

**Catastrophe concentration.** California was 25.6% of H1 GWP; adding Hawaii (4.0%) and Washington (3.0%), Pacific-seismic geography is roughly a third of the book. Retention is $20.0M per earthquake event and $11.0M for other perils — about 2% of equity — against a $3.9bn tower. The exposure is less a single event than the annual cost and availability of renewing that limit, and the possibility of multiple retentions in one year.

**Crop concentration into Q3.** Crop is the highest-variance line: 88% growth, higher 2026 retention, federal program economics, livestock derivative marks without hedge accounting, and premium recognition concentrated in the third quarter.

**Leverage and the rating-linked covenant.** $295.8M of variable-rate SOFR debt with pricing keyed to debt-to-capital, plus covenants on RBC and A.M. Best rating, creates a reflexive channel from an underwriting event to a credit event.

**Acquisition execution.** Gray Surety cost $314.0M against $208.9M of identified net assets and $105.1M of non-deductible goodwill, has lost $5.8M in five months, and on pro forma disclosure would have reduced H1 2025 net income ($79.8M pro forma vs $89.5M actual). Measurement-period adjustments moved $16.1M out of goodwill and into amortizing intangibles — the conservative direction, but it raises future amortization. The purchase price allocation stays preliminary until 1/31/2027.

**Holding-company capital geography.** 2026 subsidiary dividend capacity without approval totals about $202.9M, but PSIC alone is 87% of it. PESIC — writing 45.6% of GWP — can upstream only $7.4M because Arizona uses the lesser of 10% of surplus or prior-year net income. Growth capital is trapped where growth is fastest, and the new dividend and upsized buyback compete for it.

**Governance.** Chairman and CEO roles are combined. Classified board and supermajority provisions sunset in 2027, a defined improvement. PSU metrics are gross written premium and adjusted ROE with a relative-TSR modifier versus the S&P 1500 P&C Insurance Index; rewarding gross premium growth in a business where cost of goods is estimated years later is a design tension, partially offset by the ROE co-metric and TSR modifier.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $180 (+31.6%)

Purchase-accounting drag largely disappears in H2 2026: only $10.0M of the $26.5M VOBA balance remains and $7.4M of transaction costs do not repeat, so the GAAP/adjusted gap narrows mechanically and reported ROE converges upward toward the adjusted 26.4%. The 530bp underlying margin decline proves to be what management describes — retained crop risk, a non-recurring derivative mark, and mix shift with offsetting ceding-commission economics — leaving the true attritional trend closer to the 30.6% ex-derivative loss ratio. Crop delivers a strong Q3 with the higher retention working in Palomar's favor. Lower XOL pricing at the 6/1/2026 renewal expands net margin on the ceded book. Consensus FY2027 adjusted EPS of $11.30 proves conservative given four consecutive beats and estimates rising; $12.00 at 15x gets to $180. Buybacks continue with $163.2M of authorization remaining, executed as they were in Q2 at an average near $111. The sell-side low target of $152 re-bases higher.

### Bear Case — $105 (-23.3%)

The mix shift is structural and 76.4% adjusted ex-cat in a zero-catastrophe half-year is the ceiling, not the run rate. A normal-to-severe catastrophe year, a crop loss year, or the first adverse casualty development on a book that has grown 45% into the longest-tail line in the industry pushes the combined ratio above 90% and GAAP earnings down sharply. At 3.65x book and 4.81x tangible book, the derating on a single bad quarter is violent — the stock traded to $100.81 as recently as June 2026 on far less provocation. Debt service at $4.9M per quarter, $164.3M of unfunded LP calls, a ~$12.2M quarterly dividend, and PESIC's $7.4M upstream capacity all compete for the same capital. A rating or RBC deterioration severe enough to breach the credit agreement would convert an underwriting problem into a financing problem. The $105 target is roughly 2.4x a projected $43 book value, still above the 52-week low.

### Base Case — $155 (+13.3%)

13.7x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $11.30. Cross-checked on book: equity of $980.9M plus roughly $305M of retained earnings through end-2027, less approximately $50M of dividends and $100M of buybacks and modest AOCI movement, gives about $1,135M on roughly 25.5M shares, or $44.5 book value per share; $155 is 3.48x that, a slight compression from today's 3.65x as the mix matures. This assumes the underlying combined ratio stabilizes in the mid-70s, crop delivers a normal Q3, and no material catastrophe or casualty reserve event.

Probability-weighted at 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear: (0.50 × 13.3%) + (0.25 × 31.6%) + (0.25 × -23.3%) = **+8.7%** over twelve months. Positive, but thin against 35% realized volatility, a Q3 event window, and Financials-sector selection that has historically required a distinct company catalyst to beat the market.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** Existing owners should retain exposure — there is a credible path to $155 driven by identifiable mechanics, not hope. New capital has no urgent entry signal at 3.65x book with the underlying combined ratio moving the wrong way.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Q3 2026 print (late October/early November) showing adjusted combined ratio ex-catastrophe at or below 76% with the loss ratio flat-to-better sequentially**, confirming that the H1 deterioration was retention and derivative mechanics rather than structural mix erosion. This is the single most important test.
2. **Full-year GAAP EPS tracking toward $8.00 or better with GAAP ROE stabilizing at or above 20%**, evidencing that the purchase-accounting drag has rolled off and the GAAP/adjusted gap is closing.
3. **Price toward $118-122** (approximately 3.1-3.2x current book), which would restore roughly 27-31% upside to the $155 base case and take the probability-weighted return above 15%.
4. Disclosure of line-level or disaggregated underwriting results (ASU 2024-03 arrives FY2027) that verifies casualty pricing adequacy, or Gray Surety turning GAAP-profitable with the VOBA balance exhausted.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. **Adverse prior-year reserve development in casualty**, or the disappearance of the favorable development that contributed $24.7M in H1 — the current loss ratio is being supported by releases.
2. **Adjusted combined ratio ex-catastrophe above 80% in any quarter absent a named catastrophe**, which would confirm the structural-margin bear case.
3. **A catastrophe or reserve event large enough to prompt an A.M. Best action or RBC deterioration**, given the covenant linkage on $295.8M of debt.
4. Another debt-funded acquisition before Gray Surety demonstrates positive GAAP contribution, or goodwill impairment on the $105.1M balance.
5. Suspension of the buyback while the dividend is maintained, which would signal capital constraint at the holding company.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 earnings release, which combines the crop premium-recognition quarter with hurricane-season exposure and is the first clean read on H2 margin after the VOBA roll-off. Interim checkpoints: the September 2 dividend payment and any 8-K on reinsurance placement or acquisition activity.

## Conclusion

Palomar is a genuinely fast-growing specialty insurer with a defensible earthquake franchise, four consecutive earnings beats, rising consensus, a conservative investment portfolio, and a management team that repurchased 558,974 shares at an average of $114.68 while the stock traded in the $106-121 range. At 13.6x FY2026 and 12.1x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS, the forward earnings multiple is undemanding relative to the growth and the mid-20s adjusted ROE.

What has changed with the Q2 filing is the quality of the earnings behind that multiple.
