## Executive Summary

Smucker's FY2027 investment case rests on one arithmetic identity. Management guided adjusted EPS to $9.75–$10.25 against FY2026's $9.15, while simultaneously guiding net sales down 3–4% and stating it intends to lower coffee prices as green coffee costs decline. Growing earnings 7–12% on a shrinking top line is only possible through gross-margin recapture, and effectively all of the available recapture sits in one segment: U.S. Retail Coffee, where FY2026 segment margin collapsed to 21.2% from 28.3% despite 22 points of price realization, because commodity costs, tariffs and adverse volume/mix (–5 points) more than absorbed the pricing. Coffee is now 37% of consolidated net sales. If green coffee deflates as assumed, the guide is achievable and $120.73 is 12.1x a reasonable number. If it does not, the company will have surrendered retail price before recovering cost, and the hedge book appears thinner heading into the reset than it was a year earlier.

The balance sheet limits the margin for error. Net debt of $6.91B against a ~$2.02B adjusted EBITDA proxy is roughly 3.4x, cash on hand is $58.6M with $421.0M of commercial paper funding at a 4.03% weighted-average rate, and goodwill plus other intangibles of $10.9B stands against $5.5B of book equity on a $16.2B asset base. Two consecutive years of impairment ($1.98B in FY2025 and $961.7M in FY2026, taking Sweet Baked Snacks goodwill to zero) have pushed retained earnings to –$101.3M, with the March 2026 dividend paid from capital surplus. That last item is mechanical rather than a solvency signal, but the underlying point is not: the company has recognized roughly $3.25B of pre-tax value destruction against a $5.4B Hostess purchase price in under three fiscal years, and the segment is still contracting (FY2026 net sales –18% as reported, –7% ex-divestitures, volume/mix –8 points, margin halved to 10.0%).

Against that, the operating case has genuine merits: free cash flow of $1,156.2M covers the dividend 2.5x at a 40.6% payout, interest coverage is 5.3x, the maturity ladder is long ($150.0M due in FY2027, $4.70B beyond FY2032), Away From Home is compounding on both price and volume with margin expanding to 25.0%, and unrecognized tariff-refund claims plus the C$109.0M Voortman action are free options. The problem is price. The stock has advanced 36.8% from its April low of $88.25, sits 5.4% below its 52-week high, and trades within 3.7% of the consensus mean target of $125.19. Probability-weighted total return over twelve months is roughly +6% against a 4.63% ten-year Treasury [unverified] and a bear case near $98. That is less than fair compensation for a 3.4x-levered, volume-declining portfolio whose earnings recovery depends on an unhedged commodity assumption.

## Company Overview

Smucker is a North America–centred branded packaged food and pet food manufacturer with leading share positions in coffee (Folgers, Café Bustelo, licensed Dunkin'), peanut butter (Jif), fruit spreads, frozen handheld (Uncrustables), sweet baked goods (Hostess), dog snacks (Milk-Bone, Pup-Peroni) and cat food (Meow Mix). Beginning in Q4 FY2026, Away From Home met ASC 280 thresholds and became a fifth reportable segment; International remains in "Other." FY2025 and FY2024 were recast.

FY2026 segment detail (fiscal year ended April 30, 2026):

| Segment | Net sales | Δ YoY | Segment profit | FY26 margin | FY25 margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| U.S. Retail Coffee | $3,304.9M | +18% | $701.5M | 21.2% | 28.3% |
| U.S. Retail Frozen Handheld & Spreads | $1,853.9M | (1)% | $444.7M | 24.0% | 22.7% |
| U.S. Retail Pet Foods | $1,600.0M | (4)% | $473.3M | 29.6% | 27.6% |
| Sweet Baked Snacks | $971.3M | (18)% | $97.2M | 10.0% | 18.6% |
| Away From Home | $879.0M | +15% | $220.1M | 25.0% | 23.1% |
| Other (International) | $441.8M | +1% | $69.6M | 15.8% | 16.3% |

Two structural facts frame everything else. Walmart and subsidiaries were 34% of FY2026 net sales and $187.4M of Walmart receivables was 29% of net trade receivables; top-10 customers were roughly 60% of sales, and the company discloses no long-term supply contracts with any major customer. Substantially all coffee production sits in New Orleans, with Milk-Bone and fruit spreads each at single sites; Keurig Dr Pepper is the primary supplier of single-serve pods and the sole source of liquid coffee for Away From Home while competing in packaged coffee, and Graham Packaging is single-source for Folgers packaging.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue composition matters more than the headline.** FY2026 net sales of $9,050.9M rose 4% (+5% ex-divestitures and FX), decomposed as net price realization +9 points and volume/mix –4 points. All growth was price, and it was concentrated in coffee. GAAP gross margin fell 530bp to 33.5%; adjusted gross margin fell 330bp to 34.9% from 38.2%. Adjusted operating income of $1,678.3M (18.5% of sales) declined 8% from $1,824.7M (20.9%), and adjusted EPS fell 10% to $9.15 from $10.12 on 106.9M diluted shares.

GAAP operating income swung to +$360.2M from –$673.9M, but the improvement is almost entirely the absence of prior-year charges: roughly $1.0B less impairment and the lapping of a $310.1M net pre-tax divestiture loss. GAAP net loss was $138.7M (–$1.30 per share), which is why the reported trailing P/E of –92 in market data feeds is not a usable input. A further $116.8M year-over-year GAAP gross-profit headwind came from unallocated derivative marks: no commodity or FX derivative qualifies for hedge accounting, so all marks run through cost of products sold. That distorts GAAP margin without affecting cash or segment profit.

**Cash flow improved, with caveats on durability.** Operating cash flow rose to $1,473.6M from $1,210.4M and free cash flow to $1,156.2M from $816.6M, a 12.8% FCF margin and an 9.0% FCF yield on the current market capitalization. Management attributes the increase to lower cash taxes from payment timing and lower taxable income, lower inventories as input inflation moderated, derivative settlement timing, and reduced transition-services payments, partly offset by lower net income adjusted for non-cash items. Three of those four drivers are working-capital or tax timing. A useful sensitivity: if FY2027 FCF repeats $1,156.2M, then after the company's own projected $469.3M of dividends roughly $687M is available for debt reduction, broadly consistent with the ~3x leverage target. If FCF normalizes toward the FY2025 level near $817M, only about $350M is available. The supplier finance program ($325.1M outstanding at April 30, 2026 versus $340.4M a year earlier; $1,270.1M paid through the program in FY2026) declined modestly and therefore did not flatter the year.

**Leverage is real but not acute.** Total debt of $6,963.7M (current LTD $150.0M, short-term borrowings $420.9M, long-term $6,392.8M) fell from $7,677.6M. Against an adjusted EBITDA proxy of $2,024.7M (adjusted operating income $1,678.3M plus $346.4M depreciation; amortization is already excluded from adjusted operating income), net debt/EBITDA is approximately 3.4x and interest coverage 5.3x. The $2.0B revolver maturing March 2030 is undrawn and backs the CP program; management states compliance with its interest coverage covenant and expects compliance for the next twelve months. The March 2027 term loan, drawn at $650M, was amortized to $450M by January 31, 2026 with a further $100M prepaid.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.38 and current ratio of 0.78 should not be read as distress. Two of the five Z inputs are distorted here: EBIT/assets of 0.022 uses GAAP operating income that absorbed $961.7M of non-cash impairment (adjusted, it is roughly 0.103), and working capital is negative by financing policy (commercial paper, 0–180 day supplier terms, and a supplier finance program rather than idle cash). What the screens correctly flag is leverage combined with intangible-heavy asset composition. The counterpoint I take seriously: adding back impairments assumes they are non-recurring, and Smucker has now impaired the same reporting unit in two consecutive years.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus of $10.03 for the current fiscal year matches the guidance midpoint almost exactly, so there is no upside surprise embedded in the Street number. Revisions are positive but small: current year +0.3% over 30 days and +0.9% over 90 days, current quarter +0.4%/+0.2%. This is nowhere near the magnitude that signals a genuine earnings inflection; it is consensus converging on management's own range.

More telling is the following-year figure: $9.02, down roughly 10% from $10.03, carried by only five analysts. The small panel warrants low confidence and the number rose 3.0% over 30 days, but read literally it means the Street models the FY2027 coffee margin recapture as a one-year benefit rather than a new run rate. On $9.02 the stock trades at 13.4x, not 12.1x, which materially changes the "cheap" characterization.

Underlying volume growth is absent. FY2026 volume/mix was –4 points consolidated: coffee –5, Sweet Baked Snacks –8, Pet –5, with growth confined to Uncrustables and Away From Home. Away From Home is the standout, delivering +10 points of price *and* +6 points of volume/mix with margin expanding to 25.0%, and its elevation to reportable-segment status following the senior leadership reorganization is consistent with capital and attention shifting toward foodservice and Uncrustables. Set against that, the Hostess trademark was reclassified from indefinite- to finite-lived (adding $38.8M of annual amortization) with management citing "reprioritization of our investments in growth brands outside of the reporting unit," a decision to stop investing behind a $971M revenue segment.

Category headwinds are portfolio-specific rather than generic. The 10-K now carries explicit language on litigation and regulation touching "ultra-processed food," artificial dyes and GRAS ingredients, and on consumer trends toward weight management including medication use. For a portfolio anchored in sweet baked goods, sweetened coffee and fruit spreads, these are direct exposures, and the Sweet Baked Snacks impairment discussion cites "an evolving regulatory environment" as a contributing factor. Private-label trade-down is addressed in a dedicated risk factor and is the natural consequence of taking 9 points of price into a soft discretionary environment.

Peer comparison is of limited use because the supplied peer set is internally inconsistent: Conagra's P/E is negative, McCormick's 8.8x looks like a data artifact against a 29.5x median that is driven by Hormel (29.5x) and Tyson (35.9x) at depressed earnings. What can be said is that SJM's 12–13x adjusted multiple and 3.6–3.8% yield sit below the historical range for quality large-cap staples, and that discount is defensible on three grounds the market has correctly identified: 3.4x leverage that precludes buybacks (none in FY2025 or FY2026), volume declines in three of five segments, and 34% single-customer concentration without contractual protection.

The valuation question is whether the discount is now too wide. Three things argue it is not wide enough at this price. First, the entire FY2027 earnings improvement is a commodity spread, and management is cutting retail price ahead of confirmed cost relief; the 10-K itself warns green coffee markets will "remain challenging due to ongoing and significant price volatility" and that the company can "only partially mitigate" the risk. Second, the observable hedge posture points the other way from safety: gross commodity notional was $403.1M at January 31, 2026 versus $1,698.1M at April 30, 2025, with pledged margin collateral down to $2.7M from $37.5M. Those are dates nine months apart and the position may have been rebuilt by fiscal year-end, so this is a hypothesis rather than a confirmed policy change; but if coverage is genuinely lower, FY2027 coffee cost is more spot-exposed than FY2026's. Third, the 10-K expects only "some moderation relative to prior periods" in green coffee costs and cautions that markets "remain challenging due to ongoing and significant price volatility." [unverified — the report's specific claims of Brent near $90, impaired Hormuz transit, and ocean freight rates up ~300% in five months do not appear in the research brief or Phase 1 data.]

Scenario framework, twelve months:

- **Base $128** — 12.8x the $10.00 guidance midpoint. Coffee margin recovers partially, Sweet Baked Snacks stabilizes at low-double-digit margin, leverage moves toward 3x. Consensus median target is $127.00, mean $125.19. Price return +6.0%; +9.6% including dividend.
- **Bull $142** — 14.0x $10.15 (upper half of guide) as coffee margin retraces toward FY2025 levels, tariff refunds deliver a one-time cash benefit, and multiple expansion follows confirmed deleveraging. Above the $137 high target. Price return +17.6%.
- **Bear $98** — approximately 11x an $8.75–$9.00 outcome where green coffee stays elevated after retail price cuts, with a Pup-Peroni intangible write-down and continued Sweet Baked Snacks erosion. Price return –18.8%.

Weighting 55% base / 20% bull / 25% bear gives expected total return of roughly +6% including dividends. Against a 4.63% ten-year Treasury [unverified] and 28.6% realized equity volatility, that is below fair compensation. The valuation score of 5.2 reflects a genuinely low multiple on cash flow paired with a thin margin of safety at this specific price and a catalyst that is a commodity bet rather than a company-controlled event.

## Competitive Landscape

FY2026 results reveal where pricing power is real and where it is not. Coffee took 22 points of price and lost only 5 points of volume/mix, strong nominal pass-through, but margin fell 710bp, so the pricing recovered cost rather than capturing value. Away From Home won on both price (+10) and volume/mix (+6) with margin up 190bp. Pet Foods had only 1 point of price available and lost 5 points of volume/mix, yet margin improved 200bp via lower marketing spend; margin bought with reduced brand investment is not indefinitely repeatable and sits directly alongside the company's disclosure that Pup-Peroni's indefinite-lived intangible is the one asset whose fair value cushion is under 10%. Sweet Baked Snacks had essentially no pricing power: 1 point of price for the full year and negative net price realization in the first nine months, "reflecting lower net pricing across the majority of the portfolio," against –7 to –8 points of volume/mix. That is discounting into volume decline.

The 10-K discloses a relative competitive disadvantage rather than a generic commodity exposure: green coffee cost increases "could have an adverse impact on our profitability, as compared to that of our competitors," and tariffs "may harm our competitive position" versus rivals sourcing from lower-tariff countries. Smucker's coffee portfolio skews mainstream and value-oriented, where absolute shelf price points matter most and the capacity to absorb spikes is weakest. The filing also notes that e-commerce is "lowering barriers to entry."

Product mix is shifting toward coffee and Away From Home, which improves the growth profile. It does not diversify customer risk: Walmart at 34% of sales is flat-to-worse than prior disclosure, and the 29% receivables concentration is the harder measure. A changing product mix and unchanged customer concentration establish different things.

Trajectory across the portfolio is deteriorating in three segments, improving in one, and two-speed in another. That supports a competitive position score of 4.2.

## Risk Assessment

**Coffee cost and elasticity (primary).** Guidance depends on cost deflation, price cuts are planned ahead of confirmed relief, and hedge coverage is possibly reduced. The FY2026 elasticity relationship (–5 volume for +22 price) is untested in reverse.

**Further impairment.** Goodwill and indefinite-lived intangibles in Coffee ($2.1B/$1.3B) and Pet Foods ($1.6B/$1.1B) are roughly 80% of remaining totals, with Pup-Peroni explicitly named as within a 10% cushion at the February 1, 2026 test. $793.2M of net finite-lived trademarks, largely Hostess, remain exposed. With $10.9B of intangibles against $5.5B of equity, further write-downs erode a meaningful share of book equity and could become a rating-agency consideration.

**Customer and operational concentration.** Walmart is 34% of sales with no long-term contracts; substantially all coffee production is in New Orleans during hurricane season; KDP is the sole source of liquid coffee while competing in packaged coffee; Graham Packaging is single-source for Folgers packaging. The concentration has already produced an event: the Emporia, Kansas fire after January 31, 2026 disrupted production and was expected to reduce Q4 sales, with insurance recoveries under evaluation and no subsequent quantification found.

**Financing.** Commercial paper of $421.0M funds at 4.03%. The 10-K names a downgrade below investment grade among its forward-looking risks; that would directly restrict CP capacity. Rising short rates would raise SJM's funding cost mechanically. [unverified — the report's specific claim that three FOMC members dissented for an immediate hike and that a September hike has been priced does not appear in the research brief or Phase 1 data.]

**Governance and organizational.** Three Item 5.02 8-Ks were filed February 10, February 26 and March 3, 2026, whose contents I could not retrieve. What is confirmed: the Q3 10-Q references "several senior leadership updates," a "Form of Special One-Time Grant of Restricted Stock Agreement" exhibit was filed, a new CMO received new-hire-pattern grants in March 2026, and the 10-Q was signed by Tucker Marshall as "Chief Financial Officer | Executive Vice President, Frozen Handheld and Spreads and Sweet Baked Snacks." A sitting CFO carrying P&L responsibility for two operating segments, including the most troubled one, is a bandwidth and internal-control consideration; whether the arrangement is interim or permanent is unresolved. A multi-year Oracle Cloud ERP implementation is in progress with its own risk factor.

**Contingencies, two-sided.** Unaccrued: Folgers canister serving-size class actions in the W.D. Missouri, with loss neither probable nor estimable. Unrecognized assets: tariff-refund claims initiated April 2026 (nothing recognized; DOJ appeal announced) and the Voortman action seeking C$109.0M plus C$5.0M punitive, with C$42.5M of non-refundable RWI proceeds already received and any recovery shared. Both should be valued near zero in a base case and treated as optionality.

Several FY2027 figures — the EPS range, the –3% to –4% sales guide, the ~3x leverage target, and the stated intention to lower coffee prices — rest on secondary reporting dated June 9, 2026 rather than the primary release, which was not retrievable. They are corroborated across four outlets and reconcile arithmetically with the 10-K, but they are not filing facts.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning does not corroborate a bearish view, and I flag that as a caution against this recommendation. Near-term ATM IV of 23.6% is cheap against 28.6% one-year realized vol and sits in the 2nd percentile of the current chain; term structure is in contango (23.6% near versus 27.5% at six months), skew is balanced at –0.7% near-term, and put/call open interest is call-tilted at 0.43, down from 0.50 three weeks ago while the stock rose 2.2%. ATM IV compressed 4.9 points over that window. Two unusual prints are deep in-the-money calls: 3,601 September $95 contracts against 185 open interest and 3,600 August $110 contracts against 809, which read more like stock-replacement or covered-call activity than directional speculation. Short interest is uncrowded at 5.4% of float and 3.6 days to cover.

Event risk is concentrated at the Q1 FY2027 print on August 26: one-week IV of 23.6% implies a ±3.0% move, but the September 18 tenor jumps to 33.5% with an ±8.1% implied move, and the three-month tenor prices ±10.6%. The market expects the first quarterly datapoint on the coffee margin reset to move the stock roughly 8%. I would rather trim ahead of it than own the binary.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($142, +17.6%).** Green coffee deflates through FY2027 as the 10-K's "moderation in input cost inflation" language suggests. Coffee segment margin retraces from 21.2% toward the mid-20s on a lower revenue base, delivering most of the guided $0.60–$1.10 of EPS improvement. Away From Home compounds at mid-teens with 25% margins and Uncrustables capacity from McCalla ramps. Free cash flow repeats near $1.15B, funding $469M of dividends and roughly $687M of debt reduction, taking leverage to ~3x and reopening the buyback (~1.1M shares remain authorized). Tariff refunds deliver an unmodelled one-time cash benefit. A staples name earning $10+ with 3x leverage and a covered 3.8% yield re-rates to 14x. The reduced hedge notional, if deliberate, pays off in this scenario.

**Bear case ($98, –18.8%).** Retail coffee prices are cut, competitively forced or voluntarily, but green coffee stays elevated. Coffee margin fails to recover and FY2027 adjusted EPS lands near $8.75. Sweet Baked Snacks continues to discount into an –8 point volume decline, triggering a write-down of part of the $793.2M of net finite-lived trademarks; Pup-Peroni's sub-10% cushion is breached. The FY2026 tax and working-capital tailwinds reverse, cutting FCF toward $800M and leaving only ~$350M for deleveraging after the dividend, which stalls the ~3x target. Walmart responds to two years of price increases by reallocating shelf space toward private label. A ratings outlook change raises CP cost against $421M outstanding. The multiple compresses to 11x on $8.75. The bear case requires only that one commodity assumption fail while the price concession has already been made.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $128 (+6.0% price, +9.6% with dividend), bull $142 (+17.6%), bear $98 (–18.8%). Probability-weighted total return of roughly +6% over twelve months is below the return available on a 4.63% ten-year Treasury [unverified] on a risk-adjusted basis, with downside magnitude roughly double base-case upside.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength, ahead of the August 26 Q1 FY2027 print for which options price an ±8% move. Full elimination is not warranted: the dividend is covered 2.5x by free cash flow at a 40.6% projected payout, the FY2027 maturity is only $150.0M against an undrawn $2.0B revolver, and the tariff and Voortman claims are genuine unpriced options. For income-oriented holders, retaining a reduced core position at roughly half prior weight is reasonable; new capital should wait.

**Upgrade triggers (back to HOLD, then ACCUMULATE):**
1. Q1 FY2027 (August 26) shows U.S. Retail Coffee segment margin recovering to 24%+ with net price realization turning negative and volume/mix flat or better, evidence the price-for-cost trade is working in reverse.
2. FY2026 10-K Item 7A commodity sensitivity, or the Q1 10-Q, confirms commodity notional rebuilt materially above the $403.1M January 31, 2026 level, removing the spot-exposure concern.
3. Price reverting toward $105–$110 (roughly 10.5–11.0x the guidance midpoint) with guidance intact, which would restore a double-digit expected return and a genuine margin of safety.
4. Net debt/adjusted EBITDA reported at or below 3.0x with a buyback resumption announced.

**Avoid triggers:**
1. Any further impairment at Pup-Peroni or against the $793.2M of net finite-lived trademarks, or FY2027 guidance withdrawn or cut below $9.00.
2. A ratings outlook change to negative or a downgrade to sub-investment-grade, which would restrict CP capacity against $421M outstanding.
3. Evidence of Walmart shelf-space loss: a step-down in the customer concentration percentage accompanied by a sequential sales decline in the affected segments.
4. Confirmation that the reorganization behind the February–March Item 5.02 filings involved unplanned senior departures with the CFO's dual operating role becoming indefinite.

**Reassessment timeline.** August 26, 2026 (Q1 FY2027 results) is the decisive near-term checkpoint; the Barclays Global Consumer Staples Conference follows. A full re-underwrite is warranted after the Q2 print in late November, by which point two quarters of coffee cost and price data will exist.

## Conclusion

The score has declined steadily from 6.4 in February to 4.8 in July, and this review takes it to 4.5. The decline is not driven by price action: the stock is up 2.3% since the last review and 36.8% off its April low, which by itself would improve nothing and worsen nothing about the business. It is driven by three specific things the FY2026 10-K and proxy make clearer than before. First, the FY2027 earnings recovery is a single-variable commodity spread on which management has chosen to cut retail price ahead of confirmed cost relief, with hedge coverage plausibly reduced. Second, capital-allocation evidence is poor: roughly 60% of a $5.4B acquisition written off in under three fiscal years, five-year TSR of 88.48 trailing the peer index in four of five years, no buybacks, and incentive metrics (adjusted EPS, adjusted operating income, net sales, free cash flow) that all add back the impairments. Third, four C-suite executives — CEO, CFO, Chief Legal Officer, Chief People Officer — sold between $111.05 and $116.695 in the three weeks after guiding above consensus, with no open-market purchase by any officer or director in the reviewed window. Post-vest liquidity is the routine explanation and cannot be ruled out, but a cluster of four with zero offsetting buying is weak corroboration of the confidence in the guide.

What keeps this at REDUCE rather than AVOID is cash: $1,156.2M of free cash flow, a dividend covered 2.5x at a 40.6% payout, 5.3x interest coverage, a long maturity ladder, an undrawn $2.0B revolver, and a genuinely excellent Away From Home franchise growing 15% at a 25% margin. There is a business here worth owning at a price. Twelve times the guidance midpoint, 5.4% below the 52-week high and 3.7% below the consensus mean, with the year's decisive datapoint arriving in two weeks at an ±8% implied move, is not that price.

**Analyst price targets:** low $107, high $137, mean $125.19, median $127.00.
