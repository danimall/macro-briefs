## Executive Summary

Goosehead is a capital-light personal-lines insurance agency whose franchise royalty structure (20% of gross commissions in a policy's first term stepping to 50% on renewal) converts new-business production into a compounding renewal annuity. That mechanism is genuine, and the Q2 2026 operating detail contains real evidence of improvement: policies in force +15% to 2.053 million, total written premium +14% to $1.335 billion, client retention up to 86% from 84%, franchise productivity for tenured franchises +32% to $48,042, and 70% more franchises producing over $100,000 of monthly gross new-business commissions than a year earlier. The 16% year-over-year decline in franchise count, the datapoint most likely to be misread from a screen, reflects deliberate consolidation into multi-producer agencies, and the network's absolute output grew.

The problem is price. Since our last review on June 19 at $36.88, the stock has advanced 77% and now sits 93.6% above its May 28 low of $33.68 and 28.2% below the August 2025 high of $90.79. Over the same window, consensus for the current quarter fell 11.4% and for next quarter 11.4%, while the full-year estimate declined only 2.2% because H1 beats offset H2 cuts. Current-year consensus adjusted EPS is $2.15 (down from $2.20 ninety days ago) and next-year $2.70 (down from $2.74). Management's own FY2026 revenue guidance of 12–19% organic growth implies H2 revenue growth of +3.5% to +16.6% (midpoint +10.1%) versus +21.7% actual in H1. Price rose while the near-term earnings path was marked down.

Underneath the headline, the composition of growth deteriorated. Q2 total revenue rose 21% but Core Revenue (the company's own measure, which excludes contingent commissions) rose 10%, or about 16% adjusting for a disclosed $4.0 million prior-year carrier recovery. Contingent commissions rose 180% to $15.7 million and supplied $11.2 million of the $19.4 million revenue increase, 58% of the total. Premium retention has fallen 98% → 95% → 90% → 88% across four semiannual observations, compressing the spread over client retention from 14 points to 2. Renewal commissions declined 9%. Auto rates are down mid-single digits and home is flat, so the pricing component of book growth is now a headwind rather than a tailwind. At 24.1x FY2027 consensus with estimates being cut, the asymmetry has inverted. We move to REDUCE with a $68 base case, $90 bull, $45 bear.

## Company Overview

Goosehead places home, auto, umbrella, flood and ancillary personal-lines policies with a panel of more than 200 carriers and earns commissions. It takes no underwriting risk, operates one reportable segment, and generates all revenue in the United States. Distribution runs through three channels as of June 30, 2026:

- **Franchise network**: 898 operating franchises and 2,190 franchise producers. Royalty steps from 20% (first term) to 50% (renewal terms). Renewal royalty fees grew 15.7% to $52.5 million in Q2.
- **Corporate agency**: 583 sales agents across 17 owned locations, up from 14 a year ago. Goosehead keeps 100% of commission but carries full compensation cost.
- **Enterprise Sales / embedded insurance**: roughly $3 million of Q2 new-business commissions and agency fees, up about 70%, with Vivint named as a partnership and Planet Home operating as an embedded franchise.

The company is an Up-C. Goosehead Insurance, Inc. holds 23,803 thousand of 35,516 thousand LLC units (67.0%); pre-IPO members, principally the founding Jones family, hold 11,713 thousand units (33.0%) exchangeable 1-for-1 into Class A plus an equal number of non-economic Class B votes. A Tax Receivable Agreement obliges the company to pay pre-IPO members 85% of realized cash tax savings; the recorded liability was $174.5 million at June 30, 2026. Any valuation built on the quoted $1.55 billion market cap understates the equity by roughly 49%.

Mark Miller retires as CEO effective December 31, 2026; Mark Jones Jr., the co-founder's son and current President and COO, becomes CEO on January 1, 2027. A new CFO (John Martin) and new General Counsel (Martin Thornthwaite) were appointed in the first half of 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and its composition.** FY2025 revenue was $365.3 million (+16%); H1 2026 was $206.5 million (+21.7%). Q2 2026 revenue of $113.4 million was +21%, but Core Revenue of $95.6 million was +10%. Adjusting the prior-year base for the disclosed $4.0 million one-time carrier recovery ($3.0 million renewal commissions, $1.0 million renewal royalty fees), Core Revenue grew about 15.5% ($95.633M ÷ $82.785M) and total revenue about 26.0% ($113.389M ÷ $90.027M). That adjustment is legitimate and materially improves the core read. It does not change the fact that contingent commissions (13.9% of Q2 revenue and 10.5% of FY2025 revenue) supplied the majority of reported growth and are, in the company's own words, "unpredictable and dependent on insurance company underwriting and forces of nature." Part of the increase is structural (the 10-Q cites new contingent commission agreements executed in the period); part is a benign loss-ratio cycle that a single heavy catastrophe season would reverse.

**Margins.** Q2 adjusted EBITDA was $37.9 million on $113.4 million of revenue, a 33.5% margin versus 31%. H1 was $62.4 million at 30% versus 26%. Adjusted operating expenses grew 16% against 21% revenue growth, so operating leverage is real. But $11.2 million of incremental near-100%-margin contingent revenue mechanically lifts the margin: stripping that increment from both revenue and EBITDA gives 26.2% ($26.7M ÷ $102.2M), which over-corrects by assuming zero underlying improvement and ignoring the prior-year recovery. The honest reading is that reported margin expansion of roughly two points overstates the underlying rate.

A second input deserves attention: capitalized software development doubled to $11.5 million in H1 2026 from $5.7 million. Adjusted EBITDA adds back all depreciation and amortization, so rising capitalization flatters the metric while the cash is spent. Amortization rose to $3.3 million from $1.9 million and net intangibles to $48.4 million from $39.7 million, corroborating the ramp. At the H1 pace, roughly $23 million annually would equal about 17–18% of LTM adjusted EBITDA.

**Cash flow.** H1 2026 net income rose 129% to $25.1 million while operating cash flow fell 13% to $38.8 million and free cash flow, after $3.1 million of PP&E and $11.5 million of capitalized software, fell 34% to $24.2 million. Management attributes the gap to a $9.5 million decrease in cash received for commissions and agency fees, a $6.2 million adverse swing in franchisee receivables, and the absence of a prior-year $4.7 million non-cash impairment add-back. This is not annualizable: contingent commissions accrue when policies are placed and collect later ($29.1 million of FY2025's $38.4 million remained receivable at year-end), so accrual-heavy periods show earnings ahead of cash by construction. Total commissions and agency fees receivable actually fell from $36.6 million to $24.7 million and bad-debt expense declined, which argues against a collections problem. Conversion of 39% of adjusted EBITDA to FCF in H1 is nonetheless the weakest part of the picture.

One flag rather than a conclusion: receivable from franchisees rose 43% to $20.2 million while franchise count fell 11%, taking per-franchise receivable from roughly $14.0K to $22.5K against a $50K allowance. Franchise production per unit rose 32%, so the balance may simply track a larger book; declining bad-debt expense supports that. Unresolved.

**Balance sheet.** Total equity is negative $188.5 million, and screens will return an Altman Z-Score around 1.15. The mechanism is traceable and non-operating: the January 8, 2025 credit agreement ($300 million term loan, $75 million revolver) repaid roughly $93 million of prior debt and funded a $175 million special distribution from the LLC plus a $146 million special dividend of $5.91 per Class A share. Accumulated deficit moved from $(15.4) million to $(133.4) million entirely through that dividend, offset by net income. Additional paid-in capital fell from $58.9 million to $5.6 million on buybacks charged against APIC.

Direct leverage measures are the relevant test. Notes payable principal was $323.0 million ($297.0 million Term B-1 at SOFR+300, $26.0 million revolver), with $49.0 million of revolver availability and a $280.5 million balloon due January 8, 2032. LTM adjusted EBITDA of $131.3 million ($113.6M FY2025 + $62.4M H1 2026 − $44.7M H1 2025) gives 2.46x gross and 2.28x net of $23.7 million cash, against a 5.0x covenant. LTM interest expense of $22.9 million implies 5.7x coverage. Debt is floating with no disclosed hedge, and disclosed forward interest obligations total $112.8 million. Total contractual obligations are $675.2 million including the $174.5 million TRA.

The cost of the 2025 recapitalization is visible in the income statement: FY2025 consolidated net income *fell* to $44.5 million from $49.1 million despite 16% revenue growth, because interest expense tripled from $7.3 million to $23.8 million. Shareholders received $321 million of one-time cash in exchange for a permanent pre-tax drag of roughly $16 million a year. Cash is now $23.7 million versus $34.4 million at year-end, with $26 million drawn on the revolver in H1 to help fund buybacks.

## Growth Analysis

**What is working.** Unit growth is intact and independent of pricing: policies in force +15%, TWP +14%, franchise written premium +14.4% to $1,096 million, franchise producers +5% to an all-time high, producers per franchise up from 1.94 to 2.44. Average monthly payment to a franchise rose more than 35% to over $28,000, and the count of franchises producing over $100,000 of monthly gross new-business commissions rose about 70%. Client retention improved for a third consecutive observation to 86%, its best since the hard market began, with management seeing no structural barrier to exceeding the prior 89% high. Enterprise Sales grew about 70% to roughly $3 million of quarterly new business. Digital Agent 2.0 launched in Texas slightly ahead of schedule; an AI voice assistant handles about 20% of inbound service calls.

**What is not.** The pricing component of book growth has reversed. Premium retention fell from 98% (Dec 2024) to 88% (Jun 2026), compressing the spread over client retention from 14 points to 2. Renewal commissions declined 9% year over year to $21.0 million (roughly +4.6% adjusting for the prior-year $3.0 million recovery, still far below 15% unit growth). Management characterizes the market as auto down mid-single digits and home flat, with renewal pricing falling faster than new-business pricing.

The corporate channel is the weakest link: 583 agents (+22%) produced only +10% corporate written premium, and productivity per tenured agent fell 9% to $27,907, with 55% of agents under one year of tenure. This is where compensation spend is concentrated, with a further second-half ramp flagged on the call.

Franchise onboarding has slowed to 10 gross adds in Q2 versus 16 a year earlier, against a base falling 27% from the 2023 peak of 1,226. Consolidation into larger agencies is economically sound, but with rate no longer contributing, unit growth must carry the entire load, and the vehicle for adding units is being added to more slowly.

**What guidance says.** FY2026 organic revenue growth of 12–19% applied to $365.3 million gives $409.1–434.7 million. Less H1 actual of $206.5 million, implied H2 is $202.6–228.2 million versus $195.7 million in H2 2025: +3.5% to +16.6%, midpoint +10.1%. The mechanical driver is the contingent-commission comparison: FY2025 contingents were $38.4 million with only $9.0 million in H1, implying roughly $29.4 million in H2 2025 against $26.4 million already booked in H1 2026. The guidance raise was attributed to contingent commissions, which is a narrow source.

## Valuation Assessment

Construct the capitalization correctly. Class A shares of 23,803 thousand at $65.20 equal $1,552 million, matching the quoted market cap. Adding 11,713 thousand exchangeable LLC units gives 35,516 thousand units and total equity value of **$2,316 million**. Net debt of $299.3 million ($323.0M − $23.7M) gives enterprise value of **$2,615 million** excluding the TRA and $2,790 million including it.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV / LTM revenue ($402.2M), ex-TRA | 6.5x |
| EV / LTM adj. EBITDA ($131.3M), ex-TRA | 19.9x |
| EV / LTM adj. EBITDA, incl. TRA | 21.2x |
| Price / LTM adj. EPS ($2.12) | 30.8x |
| Price / FY2026 consensus ($2.15) | 30.3x |
| Price / FY2027 consensus ($2.70) | 24.1x |
| Peer median P/E (BRO 19.2, ERIE 20.7, WTW 15.2, AJG 35.7, RYAN 44.5) | 20.7x |

The forward multiple looks defensible in isolation: 24.1x on 25.6% consensus EPS growth is a PEG near 0.94. The difficulty is the shape of the path to that number. FY2027's $2.70 requires acceleration in a market where auto pricing is negative, renewal commissions are shrinking, and the contingent line that supplied 58% of Q2 growth faces a hard comparison. FY2027 consensus has already been trimmed 2.4% in thirty days, and the near-quarter cuts of 11.4% indicate where the revisions are heading.

The 2026 adjusted EBITDA math reinforces this. Guidance midpoint revenue of $421.9 million at roughly 31% margin implies about $130–135 million, essentially flat against LTM $131.3 million. Paying 19.9x LTM EV/EBITDA for a year of flat EBITDA requires conviction in 2027.

**Scenario values (all on 35.516 million units, net debt $299.3 million, TRA excluded from EV):**

- **Base $68 (+4%).** FY2027 adjusted EBITDA $150 million × 18x = $2,700 million EV, less net debt = $2,401 million, or $67.6 per unit. Cross-check: 25x FY2027 consensus $2.70 = $67.5. Assumes core revenue growth of low-to-mid teens, contingents flat, no multiple expansion.
- **Bull $90 (+38%).** Client retention returns above 89%, Digital Agent 2.0 scales beyond Texas, softer carrier market lifts bind and package rates, Enterprise Sales compounds; FY2027 adjusted EBITDA $160 million × 22x = $3,520 million EV → $90.7 per unit.
- **Bear $45 (−31%).** Contingent commissions normalize toward $20–25 million on a worse catastrophe year, renewal commissions keep contracting, core growth slips to high single digits; FY2027 adjusted EBITDA $130 million × 14.5x = $1,885 million EV → $44.7 per unit. This matches the low sell-side target of $44 and sits within the $33.68–$45 band where the stock traded from March through June 2026.

Weighting base 50%, bull 20%, bear 30% gives an expected value of $65.5, or +0.5% from $65.20. The analyst mean of $68.92 (+5.7%) and median of $72.00 (+10.4%) bracket the base case; with estimates falling we do not treat those targets as floors. Valuation score 4.0: the multiple is not extreme, but there is no identified mispricing at this price and the distribution is skewed left.

## Competitive Landscape

Goosehead is a pure-play personal-lines organic grower in an industry whose large brokers are diversified, commercial-weighted acquirers. That is genuine differentiation on the way up: no acquisition multiples paid, no integration risk, franchise economics that push customer-acquisition cost and fixed overhead onto franchisees, and carrier breadth above 200 that captive agents cannot match. It also means no acquisition lever to smooth a slowing organic cycle and no commercial-lines ballast when personal-lines pricing softens.

The trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. Positive: consolidation is producing more output from fewer relationships. A 16% smaller franchise network delivered 14% more premium and 16% more renewal royalties, with total producers, total premium and the count of $100K/month agencies all up in absolute terms, which survivorship arithmetic cannot explain. Client retention is rising. Share is under 1% of the addressable market, so the runway is not the binding constraint.

Negative: the corporate channel is losing productivity while adding headcount; the franchise base from which unit growth must come is shrinking with onboarding at 10 per quarter; and the market's bear case, that AI comparison shopping erodes the value of an advice-and-choice intermediary, has not been tested. Management's response is to own the channel rather than deny the threat, and its stated success metric (new-business production per active producer rather than digital adoption) is the economically correct and falsifiable one. Digital Agent 2.0 is live in one state. The five-year proxy TSR comparison (GSHD 63 versus peer group 134, indexed to 100 at year-end 2020) establishes that the market has already re-rated Goosehead sharply against broker comparables. Competitive position 5.5: real operational improvement inside the franchise network, offset by corporate-channel deterioration and an unresolved technology question.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Contingent commission reversal.** 13.9% of Q2 revenue, 58% of Q2 revenue growth, near-100% incremental margin, entirely at carriers' discretion and driven by carrier loss ratios. A heavy catastrophe season would remove $15–20 million of revenue and a similar amount of EBITDA, roughly 12–15% of the run rate, with no offsetting cost relief. The FY2026 guidance raise was attributed to this line.

**2. Premium retention and renewal-book erosion.** 88% and falling, with the spread over client retention at 2 points. Renewal commissions already negative year over year. Management's counter, that a softer market improves carrier appetite, bind rates and unit retention, is credible, and both mechanisms are real. The net effect is the central open question and it is not resolved.

**3. Carrier concentration.** Two carriers represented approximately 19% and 13% of FY2025 revenue on our reading of the 10-K (exact percentages not independently re-verified). The 10-Q adds specific caution that diminished carrier capacity following the 2025 Southern California wildfires and Central Texas floods could slow new-business revenue.

**4. Liquidity and floating-rate debt.** $23.7 million cash, $49 million revolver availability, $26 million already drawn, no disclosed interest-rate hedge on $323 million of SOFR-based debt, and a $280.5 million balloon in January 2032. Unhedged floating exposure is a live cost risk. Leverage of 2.28x net against a 5.0x covenant provides ample covenant headroom; the constraint is cash, not compliance.

**5. Contingent dilution reactivating.** 3,235 thousand options were excluded as anti-dilutive in Q2 2026, up from 435 thousand a year earlier, because the price decline pushed them out of the money. At $65.20, 94% above the May low, a portion re-enters the money. Full conversion equals 9.1% of existing units and 8.3% post-issuance ownership dilution. Strikes and vesting are unverified, so this belongs in scenario work, but the direction is unambiguous: the rally that repaired the multiple also revives the dilution.

**6. Up-C and TRA.** $174.5 million liability, $6.2 million due within a year and $105.6 million after five, with each future unit exchange creating additional liability (222 thousand units exchanged in Q2 alone). Deferred tax assets of $209.8 million underpin the arrangement and depend on sustained taxable income.

**7. Governance and transition.** Near-complete C-suite turnover within twelve months, with founder-family control increasing (Executive Chairman, co-founder director, incoming CEO) while founder-family economics decline through ongoing trust sales. The Mark & Robyn Jones Descendants Trust 2014 reduced LLC units from 7,214,193 (April 28) to 6,745,180 (August 4), roughly 469,000 units for approximately $26 million, executed at $40–45 in May and $65–70 in late July and early August. The pattern is consistent with a programmatic plan, but the most recent tranches were sold into the current price band.

**8. Disclosure changes.** NPS (77, down from 89) was retired in favor of CSAT (4.1), which has no comparable prior period and will not be on a trailing-twelve-month basis until September 30, 2026. The rationale is defensible and the metric that matters, client retention, is still disclosed and improving. Four 8-Ks (March 31, April 8, April 22, July 14, 2026) were identified only by item classification and are unread.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 65.4% against 60.9% one-year realized, a modest 4.5-point premium at the 33rd percentile of the current chain. Term structure is flat (65.4% near, 65.1% far), put/call skew is balanced at 1.0%, and put/call volume of 0.50 is call-tilted. The nearest 11-day expiry prices a ±10.3% move; the December tenor prices ±32.7%. The options market prices high absolute uncertainty, appropriate for a stock that fell sharply and then rallied within twelve months, but no directional bear bet and no distress. That is a mild divergence from our REDUCE, and we note it rather than dismiss it. For holders trimming into strength, selling upside calls into 65% implied volatility is a reasonable mechanic for staging exposure down.

Short interest of 11.4% of float with 3.1 days to cover is elevated. A portion of the 77% seven-week advance is plausibly covering rather than fundamental repricing, given that near-term estimates fell over the same period. We cannot size that contribution, but it argues against treating the move as independent confirmation of the thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($90, +38%)

The rate cycle turning softer is a net positive. Carrier appetite broadens, bind rates and package rates improve, and client retention, already up three consecutive observations to 86%, returns above the prior 89% high, which alone adds several points to core revenue growth without any pricing help. Franchise consolidation continues to compound: producers per franchise rise from 2.44, average monthly franchise payment grows from $28,000, and the 70% increase in $100K/month agencies extends. Digital Agent 2.0 rolls beyond Texas and converts the AI narrative from threat to moat, with production per active producer as the visible proof. Enterprise Sales scales from roughly $12 million annualized toward a material channel on the Planet Home embedded-franchise template. Contingent commissions prove partly structural through the new agreements executed in 2026 rather than purely cyclical. FY2027 adjusted EBITDA reaches $160 million and the market pays 22x, still below Ryan Specialty's 44.5x P/E and near Arthur J. Gallagher's 35.7x, for a growing unit-growth compounder with 5x-plus interest coverage and $144.6 million of buyback authorization outstanding.

### Bear Case ($45, −31%)

The three-year growth record was substantially a hard-market artifact. Premium retention at 88% and a 2-point spread over client retention show the pricing contribution is nearly exhausted; renewal commissions are already declining 9%. Contingents normalize on a worse catastrophe year, removing $15–20 million of near-100%-margin revenue and taking adjusted EBITDA back toward $130 million. The corporate channel keeps absorbing compensation dollars at falling productivity while the franchise base shrinks and onboarding runs at 10 per quarter, so unit growth decelerates just as pricing turns negative. Free cash flow, already down 34% in H1 with capitalized software doubling, fails to convert; the $23.7 million cash balance and drawn revolver limit the buyback that management calls the price "meaningfully dislocated" but did not execute at $41. Estimates continue the trajectory already established, down 11.4% for the near quarters, and the multiple compresses toward the 20.7x peer median on a decelerating grower. The stock returns to the $34–45 range where it spent March through June.

**Why we side with the bear-skewed distribution at this price:** the bull case is plausible and largely intact as an operating thesis; it simply no longer clears the bar at $65.20. At $36.88 in June, base-case upside was roughly 84% against limited additional downside. At $65.20, base-case upside is 4% against 31% downside, and the probability-weighted expected return is approximately zero.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Horizon 6–12 months, anchored on the Q3 2026 print (late October 2026) and the January 1, 2027 CEO transition.

**Targets:** base $68 (+4%), bull $90 (+38%), bear $45 (−31%). Probability-weighted expected value $65.5, +0.5%.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into the current strength rather than waiting for the Q3 print, which carries the hardest contingent-commission comparison of the year (roughly $29.4 million of H2 2025 contingents to lap). We would reduce existing exposure by roughly half now and hold the remainder into the October report to retain participation if core revenue growth is confirmed in the mid-teens ex-contingent. Holders unwilling to sell can write upside calls into 65% implied volatility. No new capital at this price.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE:**
1. Core Revenue growth of 15% or better for two consecutive quarters with the ex-contingent margin flat or expanding. A KBW analyst put this specific question to management on the Q2 call and it remains unanswered.
2. Premium retention stabilizing at or above 88% for two consecutive semiannual observations with client retention at or above 88%, indicating the renewal-book bleed has stopped.
3. A price near $50–52, which restores roughly 30% upside to the base case, or free cash flow conversion returning above 60% of adjusted EBITDA on a trailing-twelve-month basis with capitalized software growth normalized.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. FY2027 consensus cut below $2.40 (an 11% reduction from $2.70), which would put the stock above 27x on a decelerating base.
2. Contingent commissions falling more than 30% year over year in any quarter without offsetting core acceleration.
3. Franchise producer count declining year over year (the unit-growth engine failing at the same time pricing is negative), or corporate productivity per tenured agent falling a further 10%.
4. A covenant amendment request, a revolver draw taking availability below $20 million, or suspension of the buyback authorization.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q3 2026 results, expected late October 2026, with the FY2027 guidance framework at the Q4 print in February 2027 the second decision point. The January 1, 2027 CEO transition and the first capital-allocation decisions under Jones Jr. are the governance checkpoint.

## Conclusion

Goosehead's operating engine is in better shape than the franchise count suggests, and the Q2 detail supports that: 15% policy growth, 14% written-premium growth, rising client retention, and a franchise network producing more from fewer, larger agencies. Compensation design is a genuine positive: incentives run on Core Revenue and adjusted EBITDA, so management is not paid on the contingent-commission line that flattered the quarter. Seven insiders bought roughly $1.12 million in the open market near the lows, and Durable Capital added approximately $11.3 million at $57.75–$62.00 in the six trading days after earnings.

None of that resolves the pricing question at $65.20. The stock has advanced 77% in seven weeks while near-quarter consensus fell 11.4% and current-year and next-year estimates were trimmed. Core Revenue grew 10% (about 16% adjusted) against 21% headline, with 58% of the growth from the line the company calls unpredictable. Premium retention has fallen ten points in eighteen months and renewal commissions are declining. On a properly constructed capitalization of $2.32 billion equity and $2.62 billion enterprise value, the stock trades at 19.9x LTM adjusted EBITDA that guidance implies will be roughly flat this year, and 24.1x an FY2027 estimate that requires reacceleration and is already being trimmed. Upside to base is 4%; downside to bear is 31%. That distribution warrants reducing exposure and revisiting near $50.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the Executive Summary's specific "H2 2026 consensus adjusted EPS $1.16 vs $1.31 / 5.5% growth" figures and the "5.5% H2 exit rate" were not reproducible from the Phase 1 data (which provides only current-quarter/next-quarter/current-year/next-year estimates, not H2 splits) and have been removed; the recommendation's characterization of a "mid-single-digit exit rate" now rests on the guidance-implied H2 revenue midpoint (+10.1%) rather than an unverifiable EPS figure.
