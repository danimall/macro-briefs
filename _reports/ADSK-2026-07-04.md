**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Autodesk is a mature, high-quality subscription-software franchise that has been repriced sharply on a strategic decision (the $3.6B all-cash MaintainX acquisition) rather than any deterioration in the underlying business. The stock is down roughly 40% year-to-date and trades near 52-week lows around $200, at approximately 15x forward earnings, even as Q1 FY27 delivered 18% revenue growth (16% constant currency), a beat-and-raise on full-year guidance, and $893M of operating cash flow in a single quarter. The gap between the fundamental trajectory and the price action is the investment opportunity.

Three factors drive the BUY call. First, the accounting-practices investigation that has hung over the shares since March 2024 (the FCF/non-GAAP margin probe that delayed the FY24 10-K and drew Starboard and securities litigation) has moved toward resolution: the federal securities class action was dismissed with prejudice in January 2026 (now on appeal). Second, insiders responded to the MaintainX-driven selloff with a clustered set of open-market purchases: CEO Anagnost (~$499K), CFO Moorjani (~$494K), and two independent directors (Smith ~$794K, Cahill ~$378K), all within roughly four weeks and near 52-week lows. That combination of C-suite and multiple-director buying signals that management views the drawdown as overdone. Third, the growth engine remains intact and broad-based: AECO (~50% of revenue) grew 20%, RPO stands at $7.81B, and deferred revenue at $4.46B.

The central debate is MaintainX. At $3.6B debt-and-cash-funded, it is Autodesk's largest bet in years and pushes the company into a new adjacency (maintenance/asset operations, closing the "Design → Make → Operate" loop). The bull case (TAM expansion, lifecycle data platform, AI-relevant operating data) and bear case (near-term dilution, added leverage, new-market execution) are both credible. Our view is that the market has priced the deal as if it will destroy value while ignoring the option value of the "operate" phase and the continued strength of the core, and that the insider-buying cluster and multiple compression to ~15x forward tilt the risk/reward favorably.

## Company Overview

Autodesk is the global leader in design-and-make software, serving architecture, engineering, construction and operations (AECO), product design and manufacturing, and media and entertainment. It reports as a single segment but discloses four product families:

- **AECO** — $970M in Q1 FY27, +20% YoY, ~50% of revenue. Driven by AEC Collections, Revit, Autodesk Forma, and Autodesk Construction Cloud.
- **AutoCAD & AutoCAD LT** — $474M, +15%. AutoCAD-family products alone are roughly 25% of total revenue, a concentration the company explicitly flags.
- **Manufacturing** — $367M, +19%; Fusion, Inventor, MFG Collections.
- **Media & Entertainment** — $86M, +13%; Maya, 3ds Max, Flow Production Tracking.

The economic profile is that of an entrenched subscription franchise: 97% recurring revenue, net revenue retention slightly above 100–110% in constant currency, and a dominant installed base in professional CAD/BIM. The moat rests on deep workflow entrenchment (Revit and AutoCAD are de facto industry standards), file-format lock-in, an API/partner ecosystem (Autodesk Platform Services), and enterprise switching costs. Strategy centers on platform convergence, cloud products (Fusion, Forma, Flow), and agentic AI built on proprietary industry data. The MaintainX acquisition extends this from "Design & Make" into "Operate," targeting facilities/factory operations and CMMS workflows.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Annual revenue has compounded from roughly $5.0B (FY23) to $5.5B (FY24) to $6.1B (FY25) to approximately $7.3B (FY26, +17%). Q1 FY27 revenue was $1.93B, broad-based across geographies (Americas +16%, EMEA +21%, APAC +17%) and all four product families. Q1 operating income jumped to $541M (28% margin) from $233M, though the year-over-year comparison is flattered by two items: $75M lower restructuring charges and a $54M one-time ESPP catch-up charge in the prior-year quarter. Management guides non-GAAP operating margin near 39% for FY27, which is the cleaner read. Net income was $491M ($2.32 diluted EPS) versus $152M ($0.70), with roughly $56M of the pre-tax jump coming from a non-recurring gain on strategic private-company investments. Normalizing for these items, the underlying operating trajectory is expanding margins on high-teens revenue growth.

**Cash flow.** Q1 operating cash flow was up from $564M, aided by seasonal AR collection ($859M AR reduction after the Q4 billing peak). FCF margin runs in the low-30s percent range, the number that matters most for a software franchise, and it confirms the earnings are backed by cash.

**Balance sheet.** Cash and marketable securities total $3.31B against $2.5B of senior notes (2.4%–5.3% coupons, laddered 2027–2035) plus an undrawn $1.5B revolver. Total equity is only ~$3.19B with an accumulated deficit of ($1.3B), the product of years of aggressive buybacks rather than operating losses. RPO of $7.81B ($5.38B current) is a key forward-revenue indicator. A low Altman Z-Score is a false positive here — negative book equity and large deferred-revenue liabilities (prepaid customer commitments, a favorable item) are typical of asset-light SaaS. Real credit quality is solid: modest net leverage and compliance with a maximum 3.5x leverage covenant.

**Capital allocation.** Buyback-heavy, no dividend. $448M repurchased in Q1 (~2M shares near $240), with ~$7.0B remaining across two $5B authorizations. The one caution: RPO and deferred revenue declined ~6% QoQ off the Q4 peak, which management attributes to seasonality and the multi-year-contract annual-billing transition. Because revenue is recognized ratably, forward-revenue metrics are worth monitoring as leading indicators, though the current-quarter decline appears mechanical rather than demand-driven.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has durability from multiple sources. Full-year revenue growth is above the prior pace, and the largest family (AECO) is also the fastest-growing, tied to construction-cloud adoption and BIM standardization. The channel shift — TD Synnex falling from 20% to 9% of revenue, and indirect channel dropping from 45% to 28% — is disruptive optically but strategically positive: direct selling improves margins and gives Autodesk more customer data, which feeds the AI strategy.

The two forward levers are cloud/platform migration and AI. Fusion, Forma, and Flow move the installed base from perpetual-mindset tool users to platform consumers. Agentic AI built on Autodesk's proprietary design/engineering/construction data is the differentiated angle; the moat is the data corpus, not the model. MaintainX adds a new TAM in asset operations that, if integrated well, extends the lifecycle platform and generates operating data valuable to the same AI thesis.

Growth is macro- and rate-sensitive despite the recurring base, because demand ultimately tracks construction and manufacturing capex. The higher-for-longer rate environment is a headwind to new-project starts. The recurring revenue and net retention above 100% provide a floor that pure cyclicals lack.

## Valuation Assessment

At roughly $200 and ~15x forward earnings, ADSK trades well below where a 97%-recurring, high-teens-growth software franchise with low-30s FCF margins would normally clear. Quality software peers with slower growth trade at meaningfully higher multiples, and Autodesk's own multiple has compressed from the high-20s/30s to the mid-teens over the past year on the accounting overhang and MaintainX skepticism, not on any collapse in growth or margins.

The valuation score here is a genuine asymmetric-upside call rather than a low-multiple value trap. The distinction matters given the risk that "cheap" screens the wrong way. The reason the market is mispricing this is identifiable: a ~40% drawdown that layered accounting-investigation fear (now largely resolved with the class-action dismissal) on top of a single strategic-deal reaction. The forward P/E sits below the revenue growth rate, and estimates are being revised up on the beat-and-raise, so the multiple is discounting a deceleration that the numbers do not yet show.

On a base-case framework, roughly $10 of FY27 GAAP-adjusted EPS power (consistent with ~39% non-GAAP operating margins on ~$7.6–7.8B revenue) at a still-conservative 22x forward multiple — a discount to quality-software peers — supports a base target near $250. A re-rating toward 25x on successful MaintainX integration and continued high-teens growth supports the bull case near $300. The bear case, MaintainX destroying value plus a growth stall, caps the multiple in the low-teens and pins the stock near $170.

The split analyst view (downgrades citing MaintainX uncertainty versus an upgrade calling it "a compelling buy" at ~15x near 52-week lows) is consistent with a mispriced, controversial name where the fundamentals are stronger than sentiment. On a setup like this, consensus targets should lag rather than lead.

## Competitive Landscape

Autodesk sits atop professional design software with entrenched standards. Revit and AutoCAD are workflow-critical, and file-format lock-in plus enterprise switching costs sustain the moat. The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving in the core (AECO share gains, construction-cloud adoption) and expanding into a new adjacency via MaintainX.

The filings are candid about the two structural threats. First, low barriers to entry in software combined with a cloud transition that lowers switching costs — the more workflows move to the cloud, the easier it is (in theory) for customers to migrate. Second, AI as a double-edged sword: competitors or new entrants embedding AI faster could erode Autodesk's advantage, though Autodesk's proprietary domain data is a real counterweight. The competitive-position score is calibrated to reflect a strong but not widening moat — the prior trajectory (down from 8.5 to 7.5 across recent reports) reflects legitimate concern that cloud/AI dynamics compress rather than extend the moat over time. We hold at 7.0, treating the position as durable but not improving, and pair it with growth and financial health as the load-bearing dimensions rather than relying on competitive position alone.

## Risk Assessment

**Accounting-investigation overhang.** The March 2024 Audit Committee probe into FCF and non-GAAP margin practices — specifically the use of upfront versus annual billings to shift cash-flow timing — delayed the FY24 10-K, triggered a Nasdaq delisting notice (later cured), and drew Starboard and securities litigation. The federal securities class action was dismissed with prejudice on January 26, 2026, with plaintiffs' appeal opening brief filed May 27, 2026. This is largely, though not fully, behind the company. We could not independently confirm a formal SEC/DOJ closure, so we treat any "fully resolved" characterization as unconfirmed.

**MaintainX integration and leverage.** The all-cash, debt-and-cash-funded deal is the swing factor for the next 12–18 months. It moves Autodesk into a new market (asset operations) with execution risk, adds leverage, and creates near-term P&L dilution. The 10-Q added a specific risk factor that the deal may not close or deliver synergies.

**AutoCAD concentration.** AutoCAD/AutoCAD LT is ~25% of revenue, and a key growth assumption is migrating those users to higher-value Collections and cloud — a transition management admits has sometimes stalled.

**Business-model transition friction.** Named-user plans, the new transaction model (direct billing with Solution Providers), annual billing of multi-year contracts, and Flex consumption pricing all distort billings and cash-flow timing, complicating forecasting into FY27. The 6% QoQ RPO decline is largely seasonal/transitional but bears monitoring.

**Macro/FX.** 65% of revenue is international, with tariff/trade-war exposure (extensively updated for IEEPA developments through Feb 2026) and sensitivity to construction/manufacturing capex.

The risk score sits at 5.5 — below neutral — because the MaintainX outcome is genuinely binary and the appeal on the securities case remains open. These are specific, not sector-generic, risks, and they are not fully priced despite the drawdown.

### Options Market Signal

The put/call OI ratio fell sharply from 1.86 to 0.72 between the May 7 and May 25 snapshots while the price was roughly flat ($243 → $241). The collapse in put demand indicates that bearish positioning unwound heading into the MaintainX announcement and Q1 print, consistent with reduced downside hedging rather than escalating fear. This does not contradict the BUY thesis; it suggests the options market is not pricing an imminent collapse.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($300).** MaintainX integrates cleanly, adds a credible "operate" leg to the platform and generates operating data that strengthens the AI moat. Core growth holds in the high teens, non-GAAP margins reach ~39%+, the accounting appeal is dismissed, and the multiple re-rates toward 25x forward as the overhangs clear. Buybacks continue to shrink the share count.

**Bear case ($170, ~15% downside from ~$200).** MaintainX proves a distracting, dilutive bet into an unfamiliar market; leverage rises into a higher-for-longer rate environment; the appeal is reinstated; and the AutoCAD-to-Collections migration stalls while cloud/AI competition compresses the moat. The multiple stays pinned in the low-teens.

**Base case ($250).** The core compounds at high-teens growth with expanding margins, MaintainX is neutral-to-modestly-accretive over 18 months, the accounting matter fully resolves, and the multiple partially re-rates toward 22x as sentiment normalizes.

The tilt to BUY rests on the combination of a resolved-to-resolving legal overhang, a beat-and-raise, an intact and broad-based growth engine, and, most actionably, a clustered open-market insider-buying event that directly contradicts the market's deal skepticism.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target: $250.** ~$10 of forward EPS power at 22x, a discount to quality-software peers, implies roughly 25% upside from ~$200.
- **Bull case target: $300.** 25x forward on successful MaintainX integration and continued high-teens growth, ~50% upside.
- **Bear case target: $170.** Low-teens multiple on a growth stall plus a value-destructive deal, roughly 15% downside from current levels.
- **Timeframe: 12–18 months**, tied to MaintainX close and early integration milestones, the securities-appeal resolution, and successive earnings prints demonstrating margin delivery.
- **Upside/downside from current (~$200):** +25% to base, +50% to bull, -15% to bear — an asymmetric ~2.5–3:1 reward/risk to base.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. If constant-currency revenue growth decelerates below 10% YoY on a clean (non-FX, non-comparison-flattered) basis in the next two quarters.
2. If MaintainX integration produces a guidance cut to FY27 non-GAAP operating margin below the mid-30s%, or if net leverage exceeds ~2.5x with no deleveraging path.
3. If the securities class-action appeal is reinstated and materially reopens the accounting-practices exposure, or a formal SEC/DOJ action surfaces.

## Conclusion

Autodesk offers an asymmetric setup: a dominant, 97%-recurring software franchise growing 18% with low-30s FCF margins, repriced to ~15x forward on a strategic-deal reaction and a legal overhang that is largely resolving. The insider-buying cluster (CEO, CFO, and two independent directors near 52-week lows) is a rare signal that the drawdown is overdone. The MaintainX pivot is the genuine risk and the reason the position warrants disciplined sizing rather than maximum conviction, but the balance of evidence — improving valuation on unchanged-to-improving fundamentals, a de-risking legal matter, and management putting personal capital to work — supports a **BUY** with a 12–18 month base-case target of $250. The score trajectory (6.7 → 6.8 → 6.6) reflected the alternating HOLD/BUY debate around the deal; the additional evidence of class-action dismissal and the insider cluster tips the current call back to BUY, with the recognition that the lower price on intact fundamentals improves the risk/reward rather than degrading it.
