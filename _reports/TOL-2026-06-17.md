**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Toll Brothers is the largest U.S. luxury homebuilder, operating in 24 states across five geographic segments and selling to an affluent move-up buyer (average delivered home price ~$1.0M in Q2 FY2026, $1.69M in the Pacific region). The investment question is whether the company is mid-cycle or late-cycle; solvency and franchise quality are both strong. Reported FY2025 net income fell 14% to $1.35B and Q2 FY2026 net income fell 26% YoY, with core home cost of revenue rising to 76.1% as the company leans on incentives and a higher-margin-dilutive spec mix to hold sales pace. The franchise is outperforming mass-market peers but underperforming its own year-ago results.

The bull-bear tension sits in a single divergence: net signed orders turned up +7% in units in Q2 (concentrated in North +26% and South +24%), while deliveries fell 14% and backlog is still shrinking (−11% YoY to $6.32B). If the order momentum sustains, FY2027 deliveries can reaccelerate; in the meantime, near-term EPS keeps declining and consensus cut the current-quarter estimate ~16% in the last 30 days. The reported H1 "beat-and-raise" was partially carried by ~$370M of non-recurring asset and JV-sale revenue tied to the multifamily exit, so the underlying earnings trajectory is softer than headline results suggest.

We rate TOL a HOLD. The stock trades at ~11.6x trailing EPS, a discount to its own historical quality and to several peers on a quality-adjusted basis, but the discount is earned by a normalizing margin cycle and falling near-term estimates rather than a clear mispricing. The new-CEO growth pivot (first M&A in years, 8–10% community-count target, exit from capital-intensive multifamily) is a genuine but unproven narrative shift. We see balanced risk/reward into the next two prints and prefer to reassess on either a confirmed margin trough or a pullback that prices the deceleration more fully.

## Company Overview

Toll Brothers builds detached and attached single-family homes, master-planned communities, and (via City Living) urban high-rise condominiums. Its strategic differentiation is the affluent, move-up/luxury customer, who management argues is more insulated from the affordability and mortgage-rate pressures that have hit entry-level buyers hardest. Toll runs a capital-light land model: of ~76,800 home sites controlled at Q2 FY2026, ~58% were optioned rather than owned, with $814M of deposits controlling $7.24B of land through VIE arrangements. This limits balance-sheet exposure to a land-price correction.

Two structural shifts define the current period. First, spec ("quick move-in") homes rose to ~54% of FY2025 deliveries (from 49% in FY2024); spec builds turn faster but carry lower gross margins and shift inventory risk onto Toll. Second, in September 2025 the company agreed to sell roughly half its Apartment Living portfolio plus the operating platform to Kennedy Wilson (~$380M total, ~$330M net proceeds in H1 FY26), sharpening focus on the core for-sale business. CEO succession completed March 30, 2026: 22-year veteran Karl Mistry replaced Douglas Yearley, who moved to Executive Chairman.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (10-K).** Revenue was $10.97B (+1% YoY), home sales revenue $10.84B (+3%). Operating income fell ~16% to $1.72B. Home cost of revenue rose to 74.4% of home sales (from 73.4%), reflecting incentive-driven margin compression. FCF was ~$1.03B, cash $1.26B, and debt-to-total-capital a conservative 0.25. The company repurchased $651M of stock in FY25, continuing a multi-year share-count reduction from ~117M diluted shares in 2022 to ~99M, with current shares outstanding at ~93.5M.

**H1 FY2026 (10-Q, Q2 ended Apr 30, 2026).** Q2 diluted EPS was $2.72; H1 net income $471.5M (−11%). Q2 deliveries fell 14% to 2,491 units on lower entering backlog. Q2 home cost of revenue worsened to 76.1% (from 74.0%), the weakest in the period reviewed, on continued incentives plus elevated inventory impairments. Net signed contracts rose +7% in units (2,834) and +8% in dollars ($2.81B), but backlog at quarter-end was down to $6.32B / 5,394 homes (−8% / −11% YoY), signaling muted near-term deliveries. Management raised FY2026 guidance to 10,400–10,700 deliveries, ~26.1% adjusted home-sales gross margin, and ~10.1% SG&A.

**Earnings quality caveat.** A meaningful portion of H1 reported results came from non-operating items: a $284M Apartment Living asset sale (+$18.8M pre-tax gain) and $69M of JV interest-sale gains, partially offset by $57.8M of other-than-temporary impairments on Rental Property JVs. Roughly $370M of H1 revenue was non-recurring. The core homebuilding margin is deteriorating independent of these items, with gross margin trending from ~27%+ toward ~26%.

**Capital allocation and liquidity.** Disciplined. Q2 buybacks of 1.22M shares at avg $143.72 (below the current ~$153), 8.1M shares remaining on the December 2023 20M-share authorization, quarterly dividend raised to $0.26 in March 2026, and a February 2026 refinancing that extended the $2.38B revolver and $650M term loan to 2031. Altman Z-score sits around 2.74 (grey zone), which is normal for an asset-heavy homebuilder and not a distress signal given the conservative leverage. We score Financial Health 7.0 — strong balance sheet and shareholder-friendly capital return, tempered by clearly decelerating earnings and the non-recurring flattering of H1.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is bifurcated. Trailing earnings are contracting (FY25 net income −14%, Q2 FY26 −26%), deliveries are falling (−14% in Q2), and backlog is still shrinking (−11%). Consensus reflects this: the current-quarter EPS estimate was cut 15.7% over 30 days to $2.90, and the next-year estimate slipped 1.7% over 90 days to $14.14. There is no estimate-revision BUY signal here; the dominant revision trend is negative for the near term.

The forward case rests on the order/delivery divergence. Net new orders up 7% in units, with luxury orders at a multi-year high per management, suggest the affluent buyer is re-engaging even as deliveries lag. If that order momentum holds, deliveries can reaccelerate into FY2027. Management's 8–10% community-count growth target and the Buffington Homes of Arkansas acquisition (~1,500 home sites, 9 communities, the first M&A in years) point to a deliberate expansion strategy under new CEO Mistry. But community-count growth into a market where Toll is paying for sales with incentives is a volume-over-margin trade until the rate environment eases.

Given the high-rate macro backdrop and broad affordability pressure — demand was "negatively impacted" by affordability, weak consumer confidence, and geopolitical volatility beginning March 2026 — we view durable growth as conditional on rate relief that has not yet arrived. We score Growth Prospects 5.0, consistent with the recent trajectory: an order recovery worth tracking, but no near-term earnings growth and falling estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

TOL trades at ~11.6x trailing EPS ($13.49) and ~12x the FY26 consensus of $12.78. Against homebuilding peers — NVR 15.8x, Lennar 13.6x, TopBuild 23.0x, Taylor Morrison 10.7x, peer median 15.8x — Toll sits below the median. On its luxury-franchise quality and the lower cyclicality of its affluent buyer, a discount to NVR/LEN looks unwarranted on the surface.

The discount is earned by trajectory, not mispriced. Trailing EPS is near a cyclical peak being normalized lower (margins compressing 100–200bps annually, spec-mix structural drag), and the current quarter's estimate was just cut ~16%. This is the classic value-trap pattern in cyclicals: a low multiple on earnings that are rolling over. The cycle-adjusted case cuts both ways — if FY2027 deliveries reaccelerate on the order recovery and incentives ease as rates fall, normalized earnings power supports a re-rating toward the peer median. But that requires rate relief and an unproven volume ramp.

Analyst targets bracket a wide range: low $115, mean $164, high $187 (KBW Outperform ~$161 on a preference for Toll over mass-market peers). At the current ~$153, the mean implies ~7% upside. We do not treat analyst targets as floors here because the inputs are deteriorating, not improving — the inflection-stock condition (rising estimates plus accelerating revenue) is absent. We score Valuation 5.5: a modest discount to quality offset by a normalizing margin cycle and negative near-term revisions, with no clean catalyst to close the gap.

## Competitive Landscape

Toll's moat is its position as the dominant U.S. luxury homebuilder serving an affluent, higher-cash-buyer demographic. The Q2 order acceleration while mass-market peers struggle corroborates the relative-insulation thesis; KBW's upgrade explicitly cited a preference for Toll over Lennar. The capital-light land model and the multifamily exit improve balance-sheet focus and reduce cyclical land-price exposure.

The trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. The spec-home shift dilutes margin and moves inventory risk onto Toll, eroding part of the build-to-order premium. Mountain and South delivery profits fell sharply in Q2 (income before tax −38% / −29%), and the franchise's heavy California/Pacific exposure is margin-rich but volatile. The new-CEO growth pivot could expand the addressable footprint, but it is unproven and partly a volume play in a soft market. We score Competitive Position 6.0 — a strong, defensible franchise with a roughly stable-to-modestly-improving relative position, gaining share at the order level versus peers but not widening its margin moat.

## Risk Assessment

**Margin compression from incentives** is the central near-term risk. Home cost of revenue rose 100–200bps YoY in both FY25 and H1 FY26, and management explicitly expects "a continuation of the elevated incentive levels and slower sales paces." Whether the raised 26.1% full-year gross margin guide holds is the key swing factor for FY26 EPS.

**Spec-mix structural drag** persistently pressures gross margin independent of the rate cycle. **Affordability and rates** remain a macro overhang. **Geographic concentration** in California/Pacific and a weakening Mountain region adds dispersion. **JV guarantee exposure** is ~$417M on $1.50B of JV debt plus ~$122.5M retained post-Kennedy Wilson (with reimbursement agreements). **Tariffs/input costs** were cited as a forward risk on the Q2 call. **Management transition** is a fresh risk factor, though the internal-continuity succession (Mistry, a 22-year veteran) is a governance positive.

We score Risk Factors 5.0, an improvement from prior 4.5, reflecting the de-risking from the multifamily exit and the locked-in 2031 liquidity, against a still-deteriorating core margin and macro-housing headwinds. No insider open-market buying; insider activity is routine (Yearley sold ~$11.5M via option exercise in early 2026), a neutral signal.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 62.1% vs 1-year HV of 34.3% — rich, at the 83rd percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (62.1% near vs 38.5% at three months). This points to imminent event risk being priced, consistent with the June FOMC and the housing-rate sensitivity. Positioning is call-heavy: put/call skew −22.9%, P/C volume ratio 0.23, P/C OI ratio 0.33 (down sharply from 1.64 a month prior as the stock rallied +20.4%). The options market is not pricing a bearish lean; the put/call collapse alongside the rally suggests positioning has flipped bullish, a mild caution against chasing here given the elevated near-term IV. Short interest is modest at 5.7% of float, 3.8 days to cover.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The order recovery is the leading edge of an affluent-buyer reacceleration that flows into FY2027 deliveries. The new CEO's expansion strategy (8–10% community-count growth, accretive small M&A) compounds a capital-light, low-leverage franchise that buys back stock below intrinsic value. If rates ease, incentives roll off and margins recover toward the high-20s, normalized EPS supports a re-rating toward the peer median (~15.8x), implying a bull-case ~$200. Target $187–200.

**Bear case.** Margins keep compressing as incentives stay elevated and the spec mix dilutes pricing; deliveries fall further on a shrinking backlog; a consumer cliff in H2 2026 (housing wealth effect, negative real wages, weak confidence) hits even the affluent buyer. The non-recurring H1 gains do not repeat, exposing a contracting core. EPS troughs below consensus and the multiple compresses toward Taylor Morrison's ~10.7x, implying ~$115–120. Target $115.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** Reassessment timeline: next earnings (Q3 FY2026, ~August 2026) and the June 16–17 FOMC for the rate path.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q3 home-sales gross margin holds at or above the guided ~26.1% AND order growth sustains >5% in units, confirming the margin trough and order recovery.
- A pullback toward the high-$120s to low-$130s (where management was buying), which would price the deceleration more fully and improve risk/reward.
- A durable move lower in 30-year mortgage rates that visibly reduces required incentives.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Home cost of revenue rising above ~77% (gross margin below ~23%) with management guiding incentives higher.
- Orders rolling back to negative YoY, breaking the recovery thesis, alongside a backlog decline steepening beyond ~15%.
- Evidence the affluent buyer is cracking (cancellation rates rising materially, Pacific/California deliveries weakening).

**Current read:** at ~$153, ~7% below the consensus mean of $164 and ~33% above the bear case (~$115), the risk/reward is balanced. We need either margin confirmation or a better entry to move off HOLD.

## Conclusion

Toll Brothers is a high-quality, conservatively financed luxury homebuilder whose affluent franchise is outperforming the mass market on orders even as its core earnings normalize downward. The bull case (order recovery → FY2027 delivery reacceleration → margin recovery on rate relief) is plausible but unproven and conditional on a macro housing environment that remains hostile. The bear case (persistent incentive-driven margin compression, shrinking backlog, a consumer cliff) is live and partly already visible in the 76.1% Q2 cost of revenue and the ~16% cut to the current-quarter estimate. The ~11.6x trailing multiple is a discount to quality but earned by the deceleration, and the H1 results were flattered by non-recurring gains.

We rate TOL a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The trajectory has improved modestly over the past quarter (financial health and risk de-risking), but growth prospects have softened, and we lack the evidence for a confident directional call. We would turn more constructive on confirmed margin stabilization plus sustained order growth, or on a pullback toward the high-$120s where the deceleration would be more fully priced.
