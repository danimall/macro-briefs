## Executive Summary

Lindsay Corporation reported a severe Q2 FY2026 earnings miss on April 2, with EPS of $1.15 versus the $1.70 consensus — a -32.4% shortfall that sent the stock from ~$117 to ~$103 in a single session. The miss was driven by infrastructure segment revenue collapsing 58% (non-recurrence of a $20M Road Zipper project), irrigation margin compression from lower-margin MENA project revenue, and continued weakness in North American irrigation demand. The stock has partially recovered to ~$112.65 but remains 25% below its July 2025 highs.

The balance sheet is a clear strength: $186M in cash against $115M in fixed-rate debt (3.82%, due 2030), an undrawn $50M revolver, and a 3.71 current ratio. Management has deployed $55.5M in buybacks over H1 FY2026 — a 40x acceleration from the prior year — signaling confidence in intrinsic value. However, the underlying business is deteriorating across multiple dimensions: North America irrigation revenue is in cyclical decline (corn prices -10%, farm income forecast -1%), the MENA mega-project revenue that drove FY2025 growth comes at significantly lower margins, and infrastructure revenue is inherently lumpy. Current-year EPS estimates have been cut 20.6% in 30 days to $5.08, putting the stock at ~22x revised earnings — no longer cheap for a cyclical business facing declining estimates.

The prior BUY recommendation was predicated on the balance sheet strength and a more favorable estimate trajectory. The Q2 miss and subsequent estimate cuts invalidate the near-term growth thesis. The balance sheet provides a durable floor, but without earnings stabilization or a visible catalyst, the stock lacks upside conviction.

## Company Overview

Lindsay Corporation operates in two segments: **Irrigation** (84% of FY2025 revenue) and **Infrastructure** (16%). The irrigation business manufactures center pivot and lateral move systems under the Zimmatic brand, supplemented by FieldNET IoT technology platforms for remote monitoring and variable rate irrigation. The infrastructure segment produces the Road Zipper System (moveable barrier for traffic lane management), crash cushions, and road marking products.

The company has ~1,275 employees and production facilities across the U.S., Brazil, France, China, Turkey, and South Africa. It distributes through 200+ independent U.S. dealers and international networks. Market cap is approximately $1.17 billion.

## Financial Analysis

### Income Statement Trajectory

Lindsay's revenue trajectory reflects cyclical decline partially masked by lumpy project revenue:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 FY2026 Ann. |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|-----------------|
| Revenue ($M) | 770.7 | 674.1 | 607.1 | 676.4 | ~627 |
| Gross Margin | 25.8% | 31.6% | 31.5% | 31.2% | 26.4% |
| Operating Margin | 12.3% | 15.2% | 12.6% | 13.0% | 10.4% |
| EPS (diluted) | ~$5.94 | ~$6.54 | $6.01 | $6.78 | ~$5.50 implied |

FY2025 revenue growth of 11% was driven almost entirely by international irrigation (+39%, $294M), specifically a $100M+ MENA mega-project. North America irrigation declined 9%. The FY2025 rebound was therefore concentrated and margin-dilutive rather than broad-based.

Q2 FY2026 confirmed the quality-of-revenue concern: revenue fell 16% YoY, gross margin compressed 650 basis points to 26.9%, and operating income dropped 59%. The irrigation segment's gross margin fell to 25.9% (from 29.9%) as MENA project deliveries dominated the revenue mix.

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet remains a genuine strength and provides a meaningful floor under the equity:

- **Cash: $186.1M** (down from $250.6M at FY2025 end, depleted by $55.5M in buybacks and $27.5M in capex)
- **Long-term debt: $115M** fixed at 3.82%, due September 2030
- **Net cash position: ~$71M**
- **Current ratio: 3.71** — exceptional liquidity
- **D/E: 0.58** — conservative
- **Altman Z-Score: 3.63** — well within the safe zone
- **Undrawn $50M revolver** through August 2030

At the current market cap of ~$1.17B, cash represents ~16% of enterprise value. This provides significant capital return capacity and downside protection.

### Cash Flow

FY2025 operating cash flow was $132.9M, converting at 179% of net income. FCF was $90.4M (13.4% margin). H1 FY2026 operating cash flow was $58.5M, tracking roughly in line with the prior year despite lower earnings, partially due to working capital release.

### Capital Allocation: Aggressive Buybacks

Management repurchased $55.5M of stock in H1 FY2026 versus $1.4M in H1 FY2025. The Board authorized a new $150M program in November 2025 ($125M remaining). Shares outstanding declined from ~10,860K to ~10,450K. The dividend yields approximately 1.3%.

The buyback acceleration during an earnings downturn is a double-edged signal. It suggests management believes the stock is undervalued, but it also depletes the cash buffer during a period when the core business is weakening. At $112.65/share, the remaining $125M authorization represents ~11% of the float.

The Q2 miss was not a one-quarter aberration. North America irrigation faces cyclical headwinds (corn -10%, USDA net farm income forecast -1%), and international revenue growth depends on winning additional MENA mega-projects. Brazil remains under pressure from elevated interest rates. The infrastructure segment is inherently unpredictable.

### Medium-Term: MENA-Dependent

The new $80M+ MENA supply agreement (announced December 2025, ~$70M in FY2026) provides near-term backlog support — total backlog rose to $151.8M from $127.0M. But this creates a problematic dependency. One customer represented 13% of FY2025 revenue, and the company has specifically added new risk factor language about "dependence on a limited number of key customers or projects."

If the MENA pipeline delivers additional sovereign-funded projects, Lindsay's top line has support. If it doesn't, the company faces a revenue cliff on top of a weak North American market.

### Secular Tailwinds

The long-term case for center pivot irrigation conversion (flood-to-pivot), global water scarcity, and precision agriculture technology remains intact. Lindsay's FieldNET platform and IoT capabilities provide differentiation. The new galvanizing facility (early 2027 startup) suggests management is investing in manufacturing capacity. However, these are multi-year, not multi-quarter, drivers.

### Bonus Depreciation

Restoration of 100% bonus depreciation under recent legislation could stimulate farm equipment purchases, but no meaningful impact has materialized yet. This remains a potential positive catalyst that hasn't converted to orders.

The stock has declined 5% while estimates have declined 20%, making it meaningfully more expensive on a forward basis despite a lower price. This is the core reason the valuation score declines.

On an EV/FCF basis using FY2025 FCF of $90.4M and current EV of ~$1.10B, the stock trades at ~12.2x — reasonable for a cyclical industrial with net cash. But FY2026 FCF is likely lower given the earnings deterioration.

## Competitive Landscape

Lindsay occupies defensible positions in both segments:

**Irrigation:** Four manufacturers dominate U.S. center pivots (Lindsay/Zimmatic, Valmont/Valley, Reinke, T-L). Lindsay's technology platform (FieldNET, variable rate irrigation, GPS guidance) differentiates within a market where the physical product is increasingly commoditized. The 200+ dealer network creates switching costs. International expansion, particularly in MENA, leverages these capabilities into markets with fewer established competitors.

**Infrastructure:** The Road Zipper System has limited direct competition — it competes primarily against fixed concrete barriers rather than comparable moveable systems. This is a genuine moat, though the TAM is constrained and revenue is lumpy.

The competitive concern is that Lindsay's margin profile is shifting: growth in international irrigation comes at lower margins than U.S. dealer-channel revenue, gradually diluting overall profitability even as revenue grows.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. MENA Concentration (HIGH)
One customer = 13% of revenue. The first mega-project is largely complete; a second ($80M+) is underway. The Iran-Hormuz conflict introduces direct geopolitical risk to MENA deliveries — management has flagged this in the 10-Q but reported no significant delays yet. If the conflict escalates further (naval blockade, oil above $102), delivery disruption probability increases materially.

### 2. Agricultural Cyclical Downturn (HIGH)
North America irrigation revenue declined 9% in FY2025 and continues weakening. Corn prices -10%, USDA farm income forecast -1%, Brazilian interest rates elevated. Farmer capital equipment purchases are discretionary and highly sensitive to commodity prices. Diesel costs above $5.29/gallon further compress farm operating margins.

### 3. Infrastructure Lumpiness (MEDIUM-HIGH)
The 58% quarterly revenue decline from a single project's non-recurrence demonstrates this risk. IIJA funding expires September 2026, creating additional uncertainty for the highway safety product line.

### 4. Margin Dilution from Revenue Mix (MEDIUM)
International irrigation grew from 41% to 52% of segment revenue, at meaningfully lower margins. Q2 FY2026 irrigation gross margin of 25.9% (vs. 29.9% YoY) confirms the compression. If international continues growing faster than domestic, the blended margin profile deteriorates.

### 5. Tariff Exposure (MEDIUM)
Steel and component costs are exposed to tariff regimes. The company has manufacturing across seven countries, providing some flexibility, but notes "marginal" cost increases from tariffs.

### 6. Environmental Remediation (LOW-MEDIUM)
$10.6M accrual for groundwater contamination at Lindsay, Nebraska; EPA/NDEE remediation plan not yet approved; actual costs could exceed the accrual.

### 7. X-Lite Litigation (LOW-MEDIUM)
Ongoing state-level lawsuits in Tennessee, California, and Missouri regarding the X-Lite highway safety product. No accrual established; insurance coverage exists. Federal case was dismissed.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 36.8% is 8.6 percentage points above 1-year historical volatility of 28.2% — the options market is pricing elevated uncertainty following the Q2 miss. Put/call skew of 3.9% (elevated put demand) at the 1-month tenor confirms bearish positioning. The flat term structure (near 36.8% ≈ far 35.9%) indicates the market sees uncertainty as persistent rather than event-driven. IV has declined from 50.4% two weeks ago (post-earnings spike) to 36.8%, suggesting the worst of the post-earnings repricing is absorbed, but the residual premium above HV indicates continued concern. Short interest at 6.2% of float with 3.1 days to cover is manageable — no squeeze risk, but some informed bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($135-140)
- MENA pipeline delivers additional sovereign projects beyond the current $80M agreement, supporting international irrigation revenue at $280-300M+ annually
- Bonus depreciation restoration drives a North America irrigation recovery in late FY2026/FY2027
- Infrastructure wins additional Road Zipper projects, lifting segment back to $110-120M annually
- Aggressive buyback program ($125M remaining) supports EPS even on flat revenue
- Earnings normalize to $6.50+ in FY2027 → 21x = $137

### Bear Case ($85-95)
- MENA revenue cliff materializes as no new mega-projects are secured; conflict disrupts deliveries
- North America agricultural downturn deepens — corn below $4/bushel, farmer capex frozen
- Margin compression continues as international mix grows (irrigation gross margin drifts toward 24-25%)
- Infrastructure remains depressed post-IIJA
- FY2026 EPS comes in below $5.00 → 18x trough = $90

### Base Case ($120-125)
- MENA project executes on schedule (~$70M FY2026), but no incremental mega-project signed
- North America irrigation stabilizes at current levels (flat to -5%)
- Infrastructure partially recovers in H2 FY2026 as new projects are awarded
- FY2026 EPS of ~$5.00-5.20, FY2027 of ~$6.00-6.50
- Buybacks continue at current pace, reducing share count to ~10.2M by FY2026 end
- 20-21x FY2027 EPS of $6.30 = $126-132; discounted 12 months = ~$120-125

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. **Estimate stabilization or improvement:** Current-quarter EPS estimate of $1.21 holds or rises over the next 30 days, suggesting the Q2 miss was the trough
2. **New MENA project announcement:** A third large sovereign supply agreement ($50M+) would extend revenue visibility and validate the international growth thesis
3. **North America irrigation orders inflect:** Quarterly order commentary indicating positive YoY unit growth in the U.S. dealer channel would signal the cyclical downturn is ending

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. **Q3 FY2026 miss with further estimate cuts:** If the next quarter also misses by >15% and FY2026 estimates are cut below $4.50, the cyclical downturn is deeper than the balance sheet can support through buybacks alone
2. **MENA delivery disruption:** Any disclosure of delays, order reductions, or cancellations linked to the Iran conflict would eliminate the primary revenue support
3. **Cash burn acceleration:** If cash falls below $120M while debt remains at $115M, the net cash position advantage effectively disappears

### Reassessment Timeline
Next earnings report: Q3 FY2026, likely late June/early July 2026. This will reveal whether the Q2 deterioration was a trough or further decline.

## Conclusion

Lindsay Corporation retains a strong balance sheet, defensible niche positions in irrigation technology and moveable barriers, and long-term secular tailwinds from water scarcity and precision agriculture. These structural attributes are why the stock doesn't warrant an AVOID despite the near-term deterioration.

However, the Q2 FY2026 earnings miss, sharply negative estimate revisions, MENA concentration risk amplified by the Iran conflict, and agricultural cyclical headwinds collectively remove the conviction required for a BUY recommendation. At ~22x revised FY2026 EPS, the stock is priced for earnings stability that has not been demonstrated. The correct posture is to wait for evidence of trough formation — either through estimate stabilization, a new MENA project, or North American order recovery — before re-engaging. HOLD.
