**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

National HealthCare Corporation operates 80 skilled nursing facilities, 26 assisted living facilities, and a homecare/hospice franchise (Caris) concentrated across nine Southeastern and Midwestern states. The company has executed well through the post-COVID labor recovery: FY2025 net operating revenue grew 16.1% to $1.52B, operating margin expanded from 6.9% to 8.4%, and non-GAAP net income rose 35% to $104M as agency (temp) nurse staffing costs collapsed 66.6% year-over-year. The stock has risen sharply over the trailing twelve months to $228, and now trades at roughly 34x non-GAAP EPS against a fragmented SNF industry that historically supports mid-single-digit-to-low-teens multiples.

The dominant recent development is the resolution of NHC's largest company-specific risk. Landlord National Health Investors (NHI) had alleged non-monetary lease defaults in mid-2025, threatening to terminate NHC's occupancy of roughly 35 facilities (~32 SNFs and 3 ILFs). NHC resolved this by agreeing in April 2026 to purchase the underlying real estate for $560M, a transaction that closed July 1, 2026. This converts NHC from a lessee under litigation risk into the owner, eliminates roughly $32M/year of base rent, and management describes it as accretive to earnings and cash flow. A standardized read of the 10-K risk factors would flag the NHI dispute as an unresolved existential threat; in fact it has been converted into an owned-asset base and a modest earnings tailwind.

The tension in the story is between quality and price. NHC is genuinely better-run than the industry (65% of its SNFs rated 4-5 stars vs. 39% industry), carries a fortress balance sheet, and has just de-risked its footprint. But the two engines that drove the earnings inflection — agency-labor normalization and the White Oak plus NHI accretion — are largely spent, organic growth is decelerating (Q1 2026 revenue +2.2% as White Oak anniversaried), and a new FY2027 Medicare case-mix recalibration threatens to turn a headline +2.4% rate into a net negative just as the margin tailwind matures. At 34x earnings with no clear organic growth catalyst, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate NHC a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1971 and headquartered in Murfreesboro, TN, NHC is a vertically integrated post-acute and senior care operator. As of Q1 2026 it operates or manages 10,323 licensed SNF beds (the core business), 1,413 assisted living units, 9 independent living facilities, 3 behavioral health hospitals, 34 homecare agencies, and 33 hospice agencies through wholly-owned Caris Healthcare. Ancillary operations include 5 regional pharmacies, an Institutional Special Needs Plan ("NHC Advantage," ~1,300 members), and two captive insurance subsidiaries (workers' comp in TN; general/professional liability in the Cayman Islands).

The company reports two segments: Inpatient Services (~87% of net patient revenue) and Homecare & Hospice, plus an "All Other" bucket for corporate, rental, and insurance activity. The continuum-of-care model links SNFs to home health, hospice, and senior living, designed to capture patients across the care journey and internalize referrals.

NHC's defining operational feature is quality. Its SNFs materially out-rate the industry on the CMS Five-Star system, averaging 3.85 stars versus 2.98 industry (Q1 2026). In a referral-driven business where hospitals, physicians, and families select facilities on reputation, this differential is a genuine and durable moat, and it supports the rising census that underpins the revenue trend.

## Financial Analysis

**Balance sheet strength is a real asset.** Altman Z-score of 3.53 sits comfortably in the safe zone; equity is 70% of assets; debt/equity of 0.42 was near-zero on the operating balance sheet before the July 2026 acquisition. NHC paid down $97M of debt in 2025, ended the year with only $40M drawn on its credit facility, and fully repaid that in Q1 2026 (zero debt at 3/31/26). Cash of $85.5M plus $172.8M of unrestricted marketable equities provided ample liquidity going into the NHI purchase, which is being funded by a combination of cash and new borrowings. Piotroski signals are all positive: positive net income, ROA, and operating cash flow, with OCF exceeding net income and a current ratio of 1.82.

**Revenue and margin trajectory has been strong but is decelerating.** Net operating revenue climbed from $1.03B (2022) to $1.14B (2023) to $1.31B (2024) to $1.52B (2025). The FY2025 growth was driven by 8.4% same-facility growth plus a full year of the White Oak acquisition (closed August 2024). Operating margin expanded from 5.1% (2023) to 6.9% (2024) to 8.4% (2025). The single most important driver was labor: salaries, wages, and benefits fell from 62.4% to 60.7% of revenue as agency nurse staffing collapsed 66.6% in 2025 (following a 66.2% drop in 2024). Composite SNF per diem rose 4.0% and census improved to 89.7% from 88.6%.

The deceleration is now visible. Q1 2026 net operating revenue grew only 2.2%, though non-GAAP net income still rose 21.1% to $30.1M on continued margin and interest-expense improvement, with census at 90.0%. The critical point for a forward view: the agency-labor tailwind is largely harvested. Agency staffing cannot fall another 66% from here; it is close to a normalized baseline. Once White Oak and the NHI accretion fully anniversary, the underlying organic revenue growth rate looks closer to the low-to-mid single digits than the 16% headline.

**Read GAAP earnings carefully.** FY2025 GAAP net income of $120M is inflated by $22.3M of unrealized gains on marketable equity securities ($31.0M in 2024). Management's non-GAAP net income ($104M in 2025 vs. $76.9M in 2024) strips these out and is the better gauge of operating trend. GAAP diluted EPS is $7.67; non-GAAP is $6.65. A further wrinkle: roughly 70% of NHC's $189M equity portfolio ($131.9M) is NHI common stock carrying $107M of embedded unrealized gain, so reported earnings and comprehensive income are partly tied to the share price of the company's former landlord.

**Cash generation is high quality.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $185M and FCF $149M (FCF margin ~10%), with FCF conversion above net income, a mark of clean earnings once the mark-to-market noise is excluded. Capital allocation has been shareholder-friendly but modest: a growing dividend ($0.61 to $0.64/quarter) and buybacks that largely offset option-exercise dilution.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth picture is materially weaker than the trailing numbers suggest, which is the core reason the score trajectory has declined (growth prospects fell from 7.5 to 6.5 across prior reports; we mark it lower still at 5.5).

The two engines that produced the earnings inflection are both maturing. Agency-labor normalization drove the bulk of the margin expansion, and with agency spend down sharply over two consecutive years, the remaining runway is small. Acquisition-driven revenue growth (White Oak in 2024, the $50.5M National Health Corporation SNF purchase, and now the $560M NHI real estate deal) provides one-time step-ups but not organic compounding once anniversaried. The NHI purchase is accretive because it eliminates the base rent, but that is a level shift in earnings, not a growth rate.

The organic growth levers that remain are incremental: census is already at 90.0% (limited room), per diem grows ~4%, and state Medicaid increases in TN and SC add ~$7.2M annualized. The development pipeline (a 79-unit AL facility in Tullahoma, TN for Q2 2027; two JV multi-family developments) is real but small relative to a $1.5B revenue base.

Working against this, the FY2027 SNF proposed rule pairs a headline +2.4% Medicare rate with a PDPM case-mix "budget neutrality" recalibration that CMS estimates at roughly -4.3%, which could produce a net negative reimbursement outcome. With Medicare at 31% of net patient revenue, this is a specific, dated forward risk that arrives precisely when the labor tailwind is exhausted. Decelerating organic revenue, a spent margin tailwind, and a potential reimbursement cut argue for a mid-single-digit forward earnings growth rate at best, insufficient to justify a 34x multiple without further multiple compression.

## Valuation Assessment

At $228, NHC trades at ~34x non-GAAP EPS ($6.65) and, per external analysis, ~16.4x EV/EBITDA. The stock is near its 52-week high of $231.7.

Peer context is complicated by the heterogeneity of the Health Care Facilities group. Option Care Health trades at 16.2x, Select Medical at 15.5x, while Astrana Health (65.4x) and US Physical Therapy (130.3x) sit far higher and are not clean SNF comparables. The peer median P/E of 65.4x is distorted by these outliers. The more relevant SNF/post-acute comparables (OPCH, SEM) trade in the mid-teens, well below NHC. NHC deserves a premium for its balance sheet, quality ratings, and owned real estate, but a 2x premium to the most comparable operators is difficult to defend when organic growth is decelerating to mid-single digits and a reimbursement cut looms.

The framework's cautions cut against a bullish valuation read rather than for it. The stock is not cheap; there is no compressed multiple to argue is mispriced. Nor is this an inflection story with rising estimates that make the trailing multiple misleading; the inflection has already occurred and is reflected in both the price and the trailing earnings. The maturing margin tailwind means the forward earnings trajectory does not support paying up. The dividend yield is a thin 1.2%, offering no valuation support. The absence of published analyst price targets removes one triangulation point, but the single external view available (a "Hold" citing rich valuation) aligns with our read.

A reasonable base-case fair value, applying a ~28x multiple to ~$7.00 of forward non-GAAP EPS to reflect the de-risking and quality premium, lands around $196, below the current price. The stock has run ahead of the fundamentals that drove it, and the valuation score reflects that there is no identifiable mispricing in NHC's favor at $228.

## Competitive Landscape

The SNF industry is highly fragmented, locally competitive, and referral-driven; no operator dominates nationally. NHC's competitive position is stable-to-improving on two axes. Its quality-rating premium is a genuine moat in a business where referral sources and families choose on reputation, and it supports the census gains that have lifted revenue. Additionally, the NHI real-estate purchase shifts a substantial portion of NHC's beds from leased to owned, structurally lowering fixed occupancy cost and removing landlord dependency, improving the durability of its cost base.

The sector-wide tailwind of agency-labor normalization is not unique to NHC, but NHC executed better than most. Against these positives, the competitive position does not translate into pricing power against government payors, which set roughly 61% of revenue. NHC's edge is operational quality and real-estate control, not the ability to raise prices, and that limits how much the moat can offset reimbursement pressure. We score competitive position at 6.5, a real and improving moat, capped by payor-mix constraints.

## Risk Assessment

**Reimbursement concentration is the primary forward risk.** Government payors are ~61% of net patient revenue (Medicare 31%, Medicaid 29-30%). The FY2027 SNF proposed rule's PDPM case-mix recalibration is a new, specific risk that was not present a year ago and could turn 2027 reimbursement net negative. This is the key variable for forward estimates. Partially offsetting: the TN and SC facility-specific Medicaid increases and the deferral of the CMS federal minimum staffing rule to 2034 (blocked by the July 2025 legislation, partially rescinded December 2025), which neutralizes a meaningful cost risk.

**Professional-liability self-insurance is a recurring, lumpy earnings swing factor.** NHC is largely self-insured through captives with no cap on claims. Reserves rose to $126.5M in Q1 2026 from $121.6M at YE25 and $103.6M at YE24. 2025 saw $17.6M of unfavorable claims activity, and reserves grew another ~$5M in Q1 2026 alone. Given industry-wide rising wrongful-death and negligence litigation severity, this is the most likely source of a negative earnings surprise.

**Related-party ecosystem warrants ongoing scrutiny.** NHC, NHI, and National Health Corporation share historical lineage, and non-executive Chairman Robert G. Adams sits on both the NHC and NHI boards, directly relevant given the related-party-adjacent NHI purchase. NHC also manages five SNFs owned by "National" (~$1.4M/quarter fees) and recently completed a $50.5M SNF acquisition from National Health Corporation. The NHI deal was third-party advised (Blueprint), which mitigates fairness concerns, but the density of affiliations is a governance flag to monitor.

**Investment portfolio concentration.** With 70% of the equity book in NHI stock, NHC's non-operating income and comprehensive income are tied to its former landlord's share performance, adding a non-operating volatility source.

The July 1 completion of the NHI purchase removed what had been the dominant risk (landlord termination of ~35 facilities), which is why the risk profile has improved materially even as the reimbursement risk has emerged. On balance, the idiosyncratic risks that remain — reimbursement recalibration, liability captive volatility — are specific and not fully priced at 34x, warranting a middling risk score of 5.5.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 36.9% sits modestly rich to the 32.0% one-year historical volatility (a 4.9-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (36.9% near-term vs. 34.1% at six months), indicating the market is pricing some near-term stress, consistent with the just-closed acquisition, the coming reimbursement rule, and elevated single-name volatility after a strong run. The put/call skew is balanced (-0.6%), so options positioning is not directionally bearish. The implied move of ±9.5% into the nearest expiry is meaningful and argues against chasing the stock at the high. Short interest is a modest 6.2% of float with 4.6 days to cover — uncrowded, with no squeeze dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($270-290).** The NHI purchase de-risks the footprint permanently and is accretive. Census holds at 90%+, per diem continues at ~4%, and the FY2027 case-mix recalibration comes in milder than the -4.3% estimate (CMS proposals frequently soften in final rules). The quality-rating moat sustains a premium multiple, the balance sheet allows opportunistic acquisitions in a fragmented market, and the development pipeline (Tullahoma AL, TN JV multi-family) begins to contribute. Under this scenario NHC compounds non-GAAP EPS at high-single digits and holds a ~34x multiple, supporting $270-290.

**Bear Case ($150-165, roughly -30% from current).** The case-mix cut lands near CMS's estimate and turns net Medicare reimbursement negative just as the agency-labor tailwind is fully spent, compressing margins in 2027. Organic revenue growth settles at ~2-3% (as Q1 2026 already signals), the market re-rates the stock toward the mid-teens EV/EBITDA and low-20s P/E consistent with SNF peers, and the professional-liability captive delivers another year of unfavorable development. A 20x multiple on ~$6.75 non-GAAP EPS implies ~$135-155.

**Base Case ($196).** Organic growth decelerates to mid-single digits, the NHI accretion provides a one-time step-up, the case-mix cut is a modest drag, and the multiple compresses from 34x toward ~28x as growth normalizes. This yields a fair value around $196, below the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (growth_hold — the operating quality and NHI accretion runway justify holding, but valuation precludes adding at current levels).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the $175-190 range (roughly 28x forward non-GAAP EPS), restoring a margin of safety against the quality and de-risking case.
- The final FY2027 SNF rule confirms a net-neutral-to-positive reimbursement outcome (case-mix recalibration materially softer than -4.3%), removing the primary forward earnings risk.
- Evidence that organic same-facility revenue reaccelerates above 5% and margins hold, demonstrating the growth story extends beyond the acquisition step-ups.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The final FY2027 rule confirms a net-negative reimbursement cut near CMS's estimate, with management guiding to 2027 margin compression.
- A material adverse professional-liability development ($25M+ unfavorable claims activity in a year), signaling the captive is a structural drag rather than lumpy noise.
- A governance event around the related-party ecosystem (NHI/National) that raises transaction-fairness concerns, or clustered C-suite open-market selling.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural reassessment points are the Q2 2026 earnings release (which reflects the signed agreement and pre-close positioning, with full ownership economics beginning in Q3 2026) and the finalization of the FY2027 SNF rule (typically late summer/early fall). These two events will resolve most of the forward uncertainty.

**Upside/downside from current ($228):** base case ~-14%, bull case ~+22%, bear case ~-32%. The distribution is skewed to the downside from here, which supports HOLD rather than BUY.

## Conclusion

NHC is a well-managed, quality-differentiated post-acute operator that has executed well: it cut agency-labor costs by roughly two-thirds in each of the last two years, expanded operating margin from 5.1% to 8.4%, maintained a debt-free operating balance sheet, and resolved its single largest company-specific risk by purchasing the disputed NHI real estate rather than litigating over occupancy. The de-risking is genuine and the moat (quality ratings, owned real estate, care continuum) is durable.

The problem is price. After a strong twelve-month run, the stock trades at ~34x non-GAAP EPS with both of its earnings engines — agency-labor normalization and acquisition accretion — largely harvested, organic revenue decelerating to ~2%, and a new FY2027 Medicare case-mix cut emerging as the labor tailwind expires. The valuation reflects the good news and offers no margin of safety; our base-case fair value of ~$196 sits below the current price. This is a good business at a full price, with the risk/reward skewed modestly to the downside. We rate NHC a HOLD and would become buyers on a pullback toward the high-$170s to $190, or on confirmation that the FY2027 reimbursement outcome is benign. The declining score trajectory across prior reports (BUY→BUY→HOLD) reflects the same reality: the fundamentals remain solid, but the price has caught up with and moved past them.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the Q2 2026 earnings release is not the first quarter to reflect NHI acquisition economics — the deal closed July 1, 2026 (start of Q3), so full ownership economics begin in Q3 2026, not Q2; this shifts the timing of the key reassessment catalyst but does not change the HOLD thesis.
