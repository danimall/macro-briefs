## Executive Summary

Veeva is one of the highest-quality business models in enterprise software: 84% subscription revenue, 44.9% non-GAAP operating margin in FY2026, no borrowed debt, $7.3B of cash and investments equal to 19% of market capitalization, and operating cash flow of $1,415M on $3,195M of revenue (44.3% conversion) against essentially no capital intensity ($70.3M of net PP&E). The regulatory-validation burden in GxP workflows (21 CFR 11, EU Annex 11, ICH E6(R3) and the rest) is a genuine switching cost, and in R&D and Quality the company's own filing states that no single vendor competes across the full Development Cloud or Quality Cloud suite. That half of the business is 55% of revenue and grew subscription revenue 19.1% in the April quarter.

The complication is that Veeva is really two businesses with opposite trajectories, and the market has spent the last four months repricing the composite without new fundamental information. Commercial Solutions subscription revenue grew roughly 10.6% in Q1 FY27 versus 19.1% for R&D and Quality. Two forces press on it, and the filings let us name both without apportioning them: Salesforce, Veeva's outgoing platform supplier, now sells a competing life-sciences CRM using IQVIA-licensed software and has publicly claimed commitments from large Veeva CRM customers; and pharma companies are cutting field-force headcount, which mechanically shrinks seat-based CRM revenue with no competitive loss required. The wind-down terms are hard-dated: no Salesforce-platform sales to new customers since September 1, 2025, a 150%-of-September-2025-seats ceiling at existing accounts, zero sales after September 1, 2030, and legacy CRM support ending December 31, 2029. Veeva cannot grow the legacy product; every incremental seat must be won on Vault CRM.

The valuation question is therefore about entry, not quality. At $234.59 the stock trades at 25.9x FY2027 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $9.06 and 23.4x FY2028's $10.03. Consensus earnings growth between those years is only 10.7%, GAAP EPS is $5.44 (43x trailing) because stock-based compensation runs 14.8% of revenue, and the entire move from the April 10 low of $148.05 (+58%) came from multiple expansion, since ninety-day current-year estimates rose only 2.4%. The stock sits near the analyst median target of $235.00 against a mean of $244.14. Our base case is $250 (+6.6%), bear $175 (−25.4%), bull $305 (+30%). That distribution, combined with an unresolved margin question and no FY2027 guidance in hand, argues for holding existing exposure and waiting.

## Company Overview

Veeva sells cloud software, data, and consulting exclusively to the life-sciences industry — pharmaceutical, biotech, and MedTech. Revenue is reported in two buckets. **R&D and Quality Solutions** (Development Cloud and Quality Cloud: clinical, EDC, eTMF, CTMS, safety, RIM, QMS, quality docs) was 53% of FY2026 subscription revenue and 55% of total revenue, and moved to 54% of subscription revenue in Q1 FY27. **Commercial Solutions** (Vault CRM Suite plus legacy Veeva CRM, PromoMats, Crossix analytics, and Data Cloud assets OpenData, Link, Compass) is the remainder. Everything in R&D/Quality and the new CRM runs on Veeva's proprietary Vault platform; legacy CRM runs on Salesforce's platform.

Scale: FY2026 revenue $3,195M, 1,552 customers (from 1,477 and 1,432 in the two prior years), 7,928 employees (+637). Segment customer counts sum to 1,963 against 1,552 total, so roughly 411 accounts — about 26% — buy across both halves. Geography: 60% North America, 28% Europe, 12% other for subscription revenue. Top-10 customers were 28% of total revenue in each of FY2024, FY2025 and FY2026, flat, which matters below.

Veeva is a Delaware public benefit corporation, and its own 10-K concedes the board "might choose to accept an offer that does not maximize short-term stockholder value" in a change-of-control situation. Combined with 66⅔% removal thresholds, blank-check preferred, no action by written consent, and DGCL §203, takeover defenses are above average. Single class of common stock since the October 2023 Class B retirement is the offsetting shareholder-friendly feature.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin.** FY2024 revenue grew 10%, FY2025 16%, FY2026 16%, Q1 FY27 16%. That consistency is unusual and is the strongest fact in the file. What produced FY2026's 22.7% non-GAAP EPS growth ($6.60 → $8.10) on 16% revenue growth was operating leverage, not demand acceleration: non-GAAP operating margin expanded 292bp to 44.87% and GAAP operating margin 350bp to 28.68%, as R&D fell from 25% to 24% of revenue, S&M from 14% to 13%, and G&A from 10% to 9% on headcount growth of 8.7% against 16% revenue growth.

**The margin engine paused in Q1 FY27, and this is the central open question.** Non-GAAP operating margin was 44.78% versus 46.10% a year earlier (−132bp). Total gross margin fell 77% → 75%. Two drivers are disclosed. Subscription gross margin fell 88% → 86%, with cost of subscription up 27% against subscription revenue up 15%, "primarily related to computing infrastructure and data costs." Professional services gross margin fell 23% → 20% on headcount expansion. The 10-K separately names among expected future expenditures "cost incurred in connection with our use of large language models associated with our Veeva AI offerings" and "data acquisition costs associated with our Data Cloud offerings." Non-GAAP EPS growth decelerated to +13.7% ($1.97 → $2.24).

Two readings are consistent with the evidence. The structural reading: AI inference and third-party data acquisition are variable costs that scale with usage, so Veeva's 86-88% subscription gross margin has a new floor below where it was. The timing reading: Q1 absorbed the Ostro close (March 9), a deliberate services headcount build ahead of revenue, and does not yet reflect the FY2027 G&A tailwind from the $30.6M FY2026 litigation charge rolling off (no such charge appears in the Q1 FY27 non-GAAP reconciliation). One quarter does not settle it. The direction reverses the entire FY2026 EPS story, which is why it matters.

**Cash flow, with the seasonality correctly handled.** FY2026 operating cash flow was $1,415M, exceeding net income of $909M by $506M, of which $473M was stock-based compensation. Q1 FY27 OCF of $1,127M must not be annualized: accounts receivable fell from $1,259.7M to $568.0M, contributing a +$696.6M working-capital release, and management states explicitly that Q1 is seasonally the strongest collection quarter with subsequent quarters "substantially less." Excluding the receivables swing, Q1 underlying OCF was roughly $430M. Cash taxes paid were only $7.2M against an $86.6M book provision because of the OBBBA R&D expensing election — a real but explicitly finite tailwind, with FY2027 the final year of the two-year election.

**Balance sheet.** Total liabilities of $1,825.2M at April 30, 2026 are 81% deferred revenue ($1,476.5M) — customer prepayment, economically float rather than a refinancing claim. There is no borrowed debt line and no interest expense. Current ratio 4.89, Altman Z 5.12. Cash and investments went $6.6B → $7.3B, of which only $119M sits outside the US. Of the $5.42B available-for-sale portfolio, $4.26B matures beyond one year; the net unrealized position swung from +$29.0M to −$7.8M in the quarter, taking AOCI from +$8.2M to −$19.8M. Disclosed sensitivity: ±100bp moves portfolio value by roughly $99-102M. These are AOCI marks, not P&L, on high-quality paper the company expects to hold to maturity.

**The interest-income dependency deserves explicit sizing.** Other income, net was $278.1M in FY2026 — 30.3% of GAAP operating income and 23.3% of pre-tax income — and $74.4M in Q1 FY27. Annualizing the Q1 rate at a 25% tax rate implies roughly $1.35 per share of after-tax interest inside consensus EPS of $9.06. Strip it out and operating EPS is approximately $7.71, against enterprise value per share of $189.65 ($38.13B market cap less $7.3B, on 162.44M shares), or 24.6x. That is the honest operating multiple. A sustained rate-cut cycle compresses this line; the buyback also converts interest-earning assets into retired shares, trading interest income for share count.

**One economic adjustment the non-GAAP presentation obscures.** SBC of $473M is 14.8% of revenue and accounts for the $2.66 gap between GAAP EPS of $5.44 and non-GAAP $8.10. FY2026 free cash flow of roughly $1,345M (OCF $1,415M less capex; PP&E is small and net) becomes materially lower after charging SBC. Whichever basis a reader prefers, the multiple is defensible for the quality but is not depressed.

## Growth Analysis

The composite growth rate has been 16% for three consecutive reporting periods, which is both the bull case (durability) and the bear case (no acceleration). Underneath it:

- **R&D and Quality subscription grew ~19.1% in Q1 FY27** (+$63M) and added ~$247M (+19%) in FY2026. Customer count reached 1,196 from 1,125 and 1,078. Management guides this to keep rising as a share of both subscription and total revenue — which is guidance about mix and implicitly about Commercial's slower path.
- **Commercial subscription grew ~10.6% in Q1 FY27** (+$32M) and ~11.6% in FY2026. Veeva itself discloses that pharma companies "have reduced the number of sales representatives they employ due to an increased preference for digitally-enabled sales channels, which negatively impacted sales of our solutions, including Veeva CRM." Seat-based pricing means the secular direction of pharma commercial models works against this line irrespective of competition.
- **Professional services grew 23% in Q1 FY27**, the fastest line, at 20% gross margin. That is dilutive to blended margin and creates channel tension with the systems-integrator partners Veeva also relies on. It appears to be a land-grab to secure Vault CRM implementations and therefore subscription stickiness, but it lowers the blended margin while it lasts.
- **A portion of subscription growth is contractual, not commercial.** Renewal pricing escalates by the lower of 4% or August CPI, and Veeva cites the inflation adjustment as a contributor to both FY2026 and Q1 FY27 subscription growth.

Estimate revisions are stable and modest: current-year consensus $9.06 (+2.4% over 90 days, flat over 30), next year $10.03 (+1.5%/flat). The earnings-inflection pattern that would justify aggressive positioning — 30%+ ninety-day revisions alongside accelerating revenue — is absent here. Veeva is a steady mid-teens grower whose EPS growth is converging toward its revenue growth as margin expansion pauses. Four consecutive beats of 2.6-4.5% support confidence in consensus but not in upside surprise of a magnitude that re-rates the stock.

Veeva AI is described qualitatively across the filings and appears only as a cost line; there is no disclosed AI revenue, pricing, or attach rate. Any AI-driven growth estimate would be unsupported. Ostro ($90M, closed March 9, 2026; $44.4M to goodwill) is on-strategy for patient engagement and immaterial at 2.8% of one year's operating cash flow.

**Forward visibility is structurally weak, and this is not widely appreciated.** Q1 FY27 Note 8 states that remaining performance obligations for noncancellable subscription contracts longer than one year were "not significant." The majority of orders are one-year; multi-year master agreements generally include termination for convenience; and the EU Data Act, effective September 12, 2025, lets EU customers (28-29% of subscription revenue) cancel without cause. Management further disowns deferred revenue, calculated billings, and normalized billings as "precise indicators," and normalized billings is itself adjusted for renewals that closed and billed after period end — a managed metric. Deferred revenue slipped from $1,488.8M to $1,476.5M sequentially, which in the annual collection quarter is not evidence of anything, but it also is not the backlog cushion a casual screen assumes.

Where that money gets deployed from here tells us what management thinks of $234.

Scenario arithmetic, all from the $234.59 basis:

This is above the April low of $148.05, which is where a genuine growth scare would take it. **−25.4%.**

At 50/25/25 weights the probability-weighted return is roughly +4%. That is below the cost of equity and well inside the ±13.5% move the options market prices for the next quarterly print.

## Competitive Landscape

The single most important analytical discipline with Veeva is refusing to treat it as one competitive position.

**R&D and Quality — improving.** Veeva's filing states that "no single vendor offers products that compete with all of our Veeva Development Cloud or Quality Cloud applications." Competitors named are IQVIA, Dassault Systèmes, OpenText, Oracle and Honeywell — each partial. GxP validation against a specific enumerated set of regulations, ISO 9001 certification, and the practical cost of revalidating and migrating clinical or quality data create switching costs that survive the weak contractual lock-in described above. Customer count grew from 1,078 to 1,196 over two years and revenue is compounding at 19%. This is where the equity value is.

**Commercial — deteriorating, with a contractual clock.** The Salesforce agreement expired September 1, 2025. During the wind-down to September 1, 2030, Veeva may not sell Salesforce-platform applications to new customers, and sales to existing customers cannot exceed 150% of seats in use at September 1, 2025. Legacy CRM support ends December 31, 2029. Salesforce may terminate the wind-down early for material breach or unremedied third-party IP claims, and on twelve months' notice if Veeva is acquired by specified companies. Salesforce has built a life-sciences CRM and IQVIA has licensed its CRM software to Salesforce, so Veeva faces a combined platform-plus-vertical-software offering from its outgoing supplier. Veeva discloses that "certain customers have chosen" to buy CRM from a competitor and that Salesforce "has announced that certain large Veeva CRM customers have committed to purchasing its CRM solutions."

The magnitude is not disclosed — no customer names, no seat counts, no revenue at risk — and the underlying Salesforce announcement was not independently verified. The Commercial slowdown cannot be attributed cleanly, because Veeva discloses an independent cause in field-force reduction. The 150% cap cuts both ways: it caps legacy revenue, but it also forces onto Veeva's own Vault platform a migration Veeva wants anyway, and on which it stops paying Salesforce hosting fees.

**Concentration has not improved even as mix has.** R&D/Quality went from 53% to 55% of total revenue, but top-10 customers were 28% in each of three consecutive years. Product diversification away from seat-based CRM is real; reduced dependence on a handful of large pharma buyers is not supported. Veeva warns that "as a customer's total spend on Veeva solutions increases, we expect purchasing scrutiny at renewal to increase as well," and that life-sciences consolidation "has accelerated in recent years," with prior instances of subscription reductions following customer mergers.

**The AI layer is rented.** Veeva's AI agents use large language models from Anthropic and Amazon hosted on Amazon Bedrock; it also depends on Amazon Redshift for a commercial data-science application and on Zoom's partner tool, described as "critical," for Engage Meeting. Veeva owns the agents and the applications, not the models. The differentiation claim — domain depth and regulatory validation on top of commodity models — is defensible in GxP workflows, but the cost of the model layer is already visible in gross margin while the revenue is not yet disclosed.

## Risk Assessment

**Ranked by ability to change the investment case:**

1. **Salesforce wind-down and CRM share loss (high, partly priced).** Hard dates, a seat ceiling, confirmed but unquantified defections, and a botched-migration path that Veeva itself warns "may lead to outages or performance problems with Vault CRM or other Vault applications" — meaning migration failure could contaminate the R&D/Quality franchise carrying growth.
2. **Margin structure under AI and data costs (high, unresolved).** Cost of subscription +27% on subscription revenue +15%. If this is structural, EPS growth converges to mid-teens and the current multiple is the ceiling, not the floor.
3. **No contracted backlog plus new EU cancellation rights (moderate, underappreciated).** RPO for noncancellable multi-year contracts is "not significant"; one-year terms with terminate-for-convenience are the norm; EU customers gained without-cause cancellation under the EU Data Act. Legal exit barriers are lower than the 76% gross margin implies. Practical stickiness — validation, data migration, 26% cross-segment attach — is the real defense.
4. **Interest-income and rate exposure (moderate).** Roughly 23% of pre-tax income is portfolio interest. A cutting cycle compresses it; a hiking cycle helps. The ±100bp portfolio sensitivity of ~$100M runs through AOCI, not earnings.
5. **End-market policy risk (moderate, specific).** IRA drug-pricing reform, most-favored-nation pricing proposals, FDA/NIH/Medicaid funding cuts, tariffs on life-sciences inputs, and — specific to Crossix — potential limits on direct-to-consumer advertising. Washington's My Health My Data Act has already forced Veeva to narrow Crossix audience segments, an accomplished revenue-product impairment.
6. **Key person and equity reflexivity (moderate).** The 10-K singles out founder-CEO Peter Gassner as "critical," notes he holds a significant portion of outstanding stock and vested options, and warns that declining equity value impairs recruiting and retention in a market where Veeva uses no non-compete agreements anywhere in the world.
7. **Concentration (low-moderate).** 28% top-10, flat, moderate in absolute terms for enterprise software.
8. **What is not a risk.** No borrowed debt, no covenants, no refinancing wall, clean disclosure record (no Item 4.01/4.02, no restatement, ICFR effective with no changes), and management's stated view that no legal proceeding would be materially adverse. The $30.6M FY2026 litigation charge appears concluded — it does not recur in the Q1 FY27 reconciliation — though the counterparty and direction are not disclosed and should not be attributed to any specific dispute.

**Material verification gaps that constrain confidence in either direction:** no FY2027 guidance or earnings-call commentary was obtained, and Veeva designates full-year subscription revenue and normalized billings guidance as its own best momentum indicators; no Form 4 insider data; the contents of four recent Item 5.02 8-Ks (three between November 2025 and April 2026) were not read; no proxy statement, so no CEO pay or pay-versus-performance detail; and no disclosed Veeva AI revenue.

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 42.9% sits modestly above one-year realized volatility of 39.8% — slightly rich, but at only the 5th percentile within the current chain, which is consistent with a quiet pre-catalyst window rather than stress. The market is pricing meaningful two-sided event risk into the next print with no directional consensus, which matches our read of the margin question as genuinely open.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($305, +30%)

The R&D and Quality franchise is the whole story and it is compounding at 19% with a documented breadth advantage no single competitor matches, 1,196 customers, and validation-based switching costs. Commercial's drag is finite and self-liquidating: the 150% seat cap forces a migration onto Veeva's own platform, ending Salesforce hosting payments and improving unit economics once complete. Q1's margin compression proves transitory — Ostro integration and a services headcount build ahead of revenue, with the FY2027 G&A tailwind from the $30.6M litigation charge rolling off not yet in the comparison — and non-GAAP operating margin resumes toward 46%+. FY2028 EPS reaches $10.15-10.50, the multiple recovers to 30x as investors re-separate regulated vertical software from horizontal seat-based SaaS, and the remaining $1.6B of buyback capacity is deployed against a shrinking share count. Veeva AI begins converting into disclosed revenue, giving the story an ingredient it currently lacks.

### Bear Case ($175, −25.4%)

Commercial subscription growth decelerates from 10.6% toward mid-single digits as more of the "number of our customers [who] have informed us of their intent to move to Salesforce" convert, compounded by continued pharma field-force reduction that removes seats regardless of vendor. Blended revenue growth breaks below 14%. Subscription gross margin settles below 85% because LLM inference and third-party data acquisition are variable costs that scale with the very products management is promoting, so operating margin drifts down rather than up. Interest income — 23% of pre-tax income — compresses in a cutting cycle. EPS growth falls to high single digits, and a business with no contracted backlog, one-year terms, terminate-for-convenience rights, fresh EU cancellation rights over 28% of subscription revenue, and 28% top-10 concentration gets valued at 18x rather than 25x. The floor is the April low of $148.05, which the market found four months ago on considerably less information.

**Which is more likely?** Neither dominates on current evidence, which is why this is a HOLD rather than a directional call. The bull case requires a margin datapoint we do not have; the bear case requires defection quantification Veeva has not disclosed. Both resolve at the next print.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD** (hold rationale: the evidence does not support a stronger call in either direction until the FY2027 margin and Commercial-growth trajectory are visible)

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint at the Q2 FY27 report expected early September 2026 (last reported June 3, 2026; the September 18 options tenor carries 50.6% IV consistent with that date).

**Scenario summary from $234.59:**

| Case | Target | Return | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bull | $305 | +30.0% | 30x FY2028 EPS ~$10.15, margin recovery + AI monetization |
| Base | $250 | +6.6% | 25x FY2028 consensus $10.03 |
| Bear | $175 | −25.4% | 18x FY2028 EPS ~$9.60, structural margin and CRM erosion |

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Non-GAAP operating margin returns to 46%+ with subscription gross margin back to 87%+ for two consecutive quarters, confirming Q1's compression was timing.
2. Commercial subscription revenue growth re-accelerates above 13% year over year, or management discloses Vault CRM migration progress above 80% of the legacy base with no incremental customer defections.
3. Price toward $190-200 — roughly 19-20x FY2028 consensus — which would restore 25%+ upside to base case and match the range where the company itself was buying aggressively in Q1 FY27.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. Commercial subscription growth falls below 7% year over year, or a named top-10 customer defection is disclosed.
2. Subscription gross margin below 85% for two consecutive quarters, which would establish AI and data cost as structural rather than transitional.
3. FY2027 full-year subscription revenue or normalized billings guidance cut, or total revenue growth guided below 13%.
4. Buyback pace slows materially at prices above $220 while authorization capacity remains — management's own revealed valuation view is a legitimate input, and it bought heavily at $163-189.

**Reassessment date:** the Q2 FY27 report (early September 2026). A secondary checkpoint arrives with the FY2028 fiscal year beginning February 1, 2027, when ASU 2024-03 forces far more granular expense disaggregation and will make the AI and data cost line directly observable.

## Conclusion

Veeva earns high marks where it counts most: balance-sheet resilience and capital structure efficiency (no debt, $7.3B of liquid assets, 44.3% OCF-to-revenue, trivial capex, a first-ever buyback executed counter-cyclically at a $176 average versus $234.59 today), and a defensible regulated-vertical moat in the 55% of revenue growing 19%. Management's capital allocation over the last two quarters has been demonstrably good, and the disclosure record is clean on every item that typically signals trouble.

What prevents a stronger rating is the arithmetic of entry. The stock has re-rated from roughly 16.3x to 25.9x forward non-GAAP EPS in four months on a 2.4% ninety-day estimate revision, sits near the analyst median target of $235.00, and offers about 6.6% to a base case built on consensus against 25% downside if either of two unresolved questions breaks badly — whether Q1's 132bp margin contraction is timing or the new cost of selling AI, and how much CRM revenue Salesforce and IQVIA take under a wind-down that Veeva cannot pace. The options market prices a 13.5% move around the print that answers both. Holding through that with existing exposure is reasonable; committing new capital at consensus, ahead of the answer, with no FY2027 guidance in hand, is not.

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Add on weakness toward $190-200 or on two quarters of restored subscription gross margin; trim if Commercial subscription growth drops below 7% or subscription gross margin holds below 85%.
