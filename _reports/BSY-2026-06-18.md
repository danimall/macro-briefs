**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Bentley Systems is the purest public-market exposure to infrastructure-engineering software, with a recurring-revenue model that ranks among the highest-quality in application software: 93% of revenue recurring, 99% account retention, 109% net revenue retention, ~82% gross margin, and a 34.6% free-cash-flow margin in FY2025. The fundamentals delivered through Q1 2026 — revenue +14.5% (+11.9% constant currency), ARR $1.495B (+11.5% cc), a third earnings beat in four quarters — are consistent with a durable compounder.

The stock has nonetheless fallen 41.8% over the trailing twelve months to a 52-week low of $28.42, against a high of $59.25. A drawdown of that magnitude on intact fundamentals requires a specific explanation, and there is one. Growth has decelerated gently but persistently (constant-currency ARR 12.5% → 12% → 11.5%; NRR 110% → 109%), the near-zero-cost convertible financing that flattered prior earnings is being refinanced into ~5% bank debt — doubling interest expense year-over-year in Q1 — and a dual-class structure with a 20%-voting-floor debt covenant sits alongside family insider-selling plans for ~1.85M shares in 2026. The market is repricing a mature, expansion-driven business whose re-acceleration case (AI/digital-twin monetization, SMB Virtuosity) is real but unproven.

At ~21x current-year consensus EPS of $1.38, BSY trades at roughly half the application-software peer median P/E (39.3x), and analyst targets ($40–$61, mean $45.4) imply 36–108% upside. But estimate revisions are flat, and analyst sentiment is degrading (hold count rising, buy count slipping). The risk/reward is genuinely balanced: a fine business at a fair-to-cheap multiple with no near-term catalyst to compress the gap. We rate BSY a HOLD (growth-hold rationale), with a clear path to upgrade on evidence of monetization-driven re-acceleration.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1984 and public since September 2020, Bentley develops software used to design, build, and operate physical infrastructure. Revenue splits across four sectors by attributable ARR: Public Works/Utilities (~59%; roads, rail, bridges, water, electric/gas networks), Resources (~27%; mining, oil & gas, pipelines, renewables), Industrial (~9%), and Commercial/Facilities (~5%). This mix is deliberately countercyclical — government public-works spending tends to offset weakness in privately financed industrial and commercial work.

The product portfolio spans five families: Bentley Open Applications (MicroStation-based design tools — OpenRoads, OpenBridge, STAAD); Seequent (subsurface/geoprofessional modeling — Leapfrog, PLAXIS); Bentley Infrastructure Cloud (ProjectWise, SYNCHRO, AssetWise); Bentley Asset Analytics (AI-powered monitoring); and the Cesium + iTwin digital-twin platform (Cesium acquired September 2024). The scale base is substantial: ~42,000 accounts across 189 countries, with more than 70% of revenue from accounts of 10+ years' tenure and no single account exceeding 2% of revenue.

CEO Nicholas Cumins, the first non-family chief executive, took the role July 1, 2024; founder Greg Bentley moved to Executive Chair and President. Werner Andre is CFO.

## Financial Analysis

The model is high quality on every recurring-revenue metric that matters.

| Revenue ($M) | 1,228 | 1,353 | 1,502 (+11.0%) | 424 (+14.5%) |
| Subscriptions ($M) | 1,080 | 1,223 | 1,377 (+12.5%) | 392 (+14.7%) |
| Operating income ($M) | 231 | 302 | 363 | 126 |
| Operating cash flow ($M) | 417 | 435 | 538 | 193 |
| FCF ($M) | — | — | 520 (34.6% mgn) | — |
| ARR ($M) | 1,175 | 1,283 | 1,462 | 1,495 |

A few framing points. FY2023 net income ($327M) was inflated by a one-time $143M tax benefit (effective tax rate −78%); normalized tax is ~20%, so 2023 net income is not a useful anchor. The E365 consumption-based subscription mix has risen to 46% of ARR (from 41% in 2023), a structurally favorable shift toward usage-based revenue that grows with customer activity rather than seat count.

The balance sheet screens poorly on mechanical distress measures — the Altman Z-Score of 1.39 reads as "distress" — but this is a false positive. The negative working capital that drives the score is structural: $526M of Cloud Services Subscription deposits plus $284M of deferred revenue sit in current liabilities, which is customers pre-paying, a sign of strength. Combined with $2.48B of acquisition goodwill (~70% of assets), the Z-Score penalizes a business that generates 34% FCF margins and retains 99% of its accounts. Real leverage is moderate (total debt ~$1.12B, roughly 1.0x equity), well within the 3.0x net-secured-leverage covenant, with comfortable interest coverage.

The convertible-note refinancing is a structural earnings headwind. The $678M 0.125% 2026 converts matured January 15, 2026 and were repaid with revolver borrowings at ~5.3%; in April 2026 the company raised a $550M senior secured term loan (SOFR + 100–200bps) to term those out. Interest expense more than doubled year-over-year in Q1'26 ($8.2M vs $3.8M). The $575M 2027 converts (conversion price ~$83 vs a $29 stock) are deeply out of the money and will need cash refinancing rather than equity conversion. The operating story partially masks this, and it will keep rising as the years of near-free financing roll off.

Capital allocation is disciplined and FCF-funded: a ~$0.07/quarter dividend (raised from $0.06), a $500M buyback authorization through December 2028 ($441.6M remaining; $125M repurchased in 2025, $40M in Q1'26), light capex (~$18M/yr), and small programmatic tuck-in M&A (~$101M in 2025).

## Growth Analysis

Growth quality is exceptional but maturing. Nearly all of it is expansion within a loyal, decade-plus account base, with new-logo contribution only ~3% (mostly SMBs via the Virtuosity e-store). That makes the growth durable, but it also caps the rate: constant-currency ARR growth has eased from 12.5% (2023) to 12% (2024) to 11.5% (2025 and Q1'26), and NRR slipped from 110% to 109%.

The earnings track record supports confidence in near-term estimates — three beats in four quarters, with the most recent (Q1'26, $0.38 vs $0.36) a 5.9% beat. But the forward estimate-revision picture is flat: current-quarter EPS revised -0.9% over 30 days, next-quarter -1.1%, current-year +1.8% over 90 days, next-year +0.6%. This is well short of the revision velocity that would signal an earnings inflection. Consensus expects $1.38 this year and $1.58 next (~14% growth), consistent with low-double-digit compounding rather than re-acceleration.

The bull case for re-acceleration rests on AI/digital-twin monetization (Asset Analytics per-asset cloud pricing, Bentley Copilot, AI-native "+" apps) and scaling the low-touch Virtuosity SMB motion. Q1'26 strength in Seequent (mining/resources) and a services rebound (+30%, Maximo-related) are encouraging but not yet evidence of a step-change in the growth rate. Until monetization shows up in ARR acceleration or net retention reversing higher, the structural-ceiling view is the base case.

## Valuation Assessment

At $29.34 the stock carries a 33.6x trailing P/E but ~21.3x forward on current-year consensus of $1.38, and ~18.6x on next-year $1.58. Against the application-software peer median of 39.3x, BSY trades at a roughly 15% discount on a trailing basis and a wider discount on forward earnings:

The discount is partly justified — BSY grows slower than several premium-multiple peers and carries the convert-refinancing drag and governance overhang — but the gap is wide for a business with 99% retention and a 34% FCF margin. On a PEG basis (~21x forward / ~14% forward EPS growth) the multiple sits around 1.5x, which is reasonable for the quality but not cheap enough to be a screaming value.

This is a cheap-but-no-catalyst situation. A compressed multiple on a high-quality name is attractive only if something forces a re-rating, and there is no dated trigger here. Analyst targets ($40 low, $44.5 median, $61 high) imply 36–108% upside, but with sentiment degrading (one buy moved to hold over the past two months) and estimates flat, those targets are more likely to drift down toward the price than to pull the price up. A cheap multiple absent a catalyst carries value-trap risk as much as opportunity. We score valuation moderately positive on the absolute multiple and peer discount, but withhold a high mark because the mispricing has no identified mechanism to close.

## Competitive Landscape

Bentley's moat is comprehensiveness: it is the only vendor spanning the full infrastructure lifecycle (design → build → operate) across disciplines, sectors, and geographies. Competitors are either broader but less infrastructure-focused (Autodesk, Trimble, Hexagon, Dassault) or compete only in slices (AVEVA in asset performance, Esri in GIS, GE Vernova in grid). The 99% account retention and >70%-of-revenue-from-10-year-plus accounts are hard evidence of stickiness, and the deliberate countercyclical sector mix gives revenue stability that pure private-construction peers lack.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving at the platform layer: the Cesium/iTwin digital-twin stack and embedded AI position Bentley to lead the infrastructure digital-twin transition, and the consumption-based E365 shift aligns revenue with customer activity. The offsetting concerns are China/APAC softness (~18% of revenue; China essentially flat with subscription declines in existing accounts and a structural preference for perpetual licenses) and the unproven monetization of AI. On balance the position is widening at the technology layer while growth matures in the core, which supports a solidly above-average competitive score.

## Risk Assessment

**Convertible refinancing and rising interest cost.** Near-free converts swapped for ~5% bank debt, interest expense doubling YoY, the 2027 converts requiring cash refinancing. Manageable given FCF, but a persistent drag that the market is partly pricing.

**Dual-class control and the 20%-voting-floor covenant.** Class A (Bentley family) carries 29 votes per share vs Class B's 1; the family controls ~53.5% of voting power. If family voting power falls below 20%, it is an event of default that can accelerate all debt. There is ample cushion today, but two directors filed 10b5-1 plans in Q1'26 to sell up to 1.85M shares in 2026 — programmatic, not a fundamental signal, but a multi-year watch item tied directly to debt covenants.

**FX exposure.** ~59% of revenue is non-US, 33% non-USD denominated, and the company does not hedge beyond natural matching. Dollar strength is a direct reported-growth headwind (the Q1'26 reported-vs-cc gap reflects a favorable FX tailwind that will reverse).

**Growth ceiling.** With new-logo contribution at ~3% and NRR edging down, the structural growth rate may be capped in the low double digits absent successful AI/Virtuosity monetization.

**Goodwill concentration.** $2.48B of goodwill (~70% of assets); a sustained slowdown or further stock decline could raise impairment risk, though none has occurred.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is bearish and consistent with the price action. Put/call open-interest ratio jumped from 1.29 to 4.90 over the past month (put/call volume 4.25), with an 8.5% near-term put skew indicating elevated demand for downside protection. Near-term ATM IV (45.3%) is rich versus 36.4% trailing realized vol (a 9-point spread), so the market is paying up for hedges into a stock already at a 52-week low — though the IV percentile within the current chain is only 8%, suggesting this elevated-vs-HV level is not extreme by the stock's own recent standards. The term structure is in contango (45.3% near vs 48.9% at six months), a normal/complacent shape rather than acute event fear. Combined with 11% short interest (8.5 days to cover), the positioning corroborates the bearish thesis the fundamentals support, but does not point to an imminent binary. For a HOLD, the takeaway is that the downside is well-flagged and crowded, which caps both incremental selling pressure and the near-term re-rating odds.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$45, ~53% upside).** AI/digital-twin monetization (Asset Analytics per-asset pricing, Copilot) and the Virtuosity SMB motion re-accelerate ARR toward 13%+, NRR turns back above 110%, and the multiple re-rates partway toward the peer median as the convert refinancing proves a non-event. At ~28x next-year $1.58 that is ~$44; analyst high of $61 assumes full re-rating plus estimate upgrades.

**Bear case (~$24, ~18% downside).** Growth continues to decelerate toward high single digits, China/APAC weakness persists, the 2027 convert refinancing prints at higher rates and compresses EPS, family selling pressures the float, and the multiple holds at ~15-16x trough sentiment on flat estimates. That is roughly $23-24.

The asymmetry is mildly favorable on the absolute multiple and peer discount, but the catalyst path to the bull case is unproven and the timing is undefined. Fair-to-cheap valuation, decelerating-but-durable growth, a quiet earnings headwind, and no dated trigger together describe a balanced hold rather than a buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Reassessment timeline: next earnings (Q2'26, ~early August 2026), with the 2027 convert refinancing path and any update to family 10b5-1 selling as interim monitors.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Constant-currency ARR growth re-accelerates above ~13% OR net revenue retention reverses back above 110%, evidencing AI/digital-twin or Virtuosity monetization.
- Forward EPS estimate revisions turn meaningfully positive (current-year/next-year revisions accelerating, not flat), confirming consensus is catching up to an inflection.
- Price pullback toward the bear-case zone (~$24-26) on unchanged fundamentals, widening the margin of safety enough to compensate for the absent catalyst.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Constant-currency ARR growth decelerates below ~9% or NRR falls below 105%, signaling the expansion engine is stalling.
- The 2027 convert refinancing or further term-loan additions materially raise the leverage/interest profile, or the 20%-voting-floor cushion erodes on accelerated family selling.
- A guidance cut or a goodwill impairment on the $2.48B balance.

## Conclusion

Bentley is a best-in-class infrastructure-engineering software franchise that has de-rated to a reasonable absolute multiple after a 42% drawdown. The business is intact — recurring revenue, retention, and FCF all confirm quality — and the discount to peers is real. But growth is decelerating, the convert refinancing is a genuine if manageable earnings headwind, governance carries a structural overhang, and there is no near-term catalyst to force the cheap multiple higher. Analyst sentiment is degrading and estimate revisions are flat, so the wide gap to consensus targets is as likely to close downward as upward.

We rate BSY a **HOLD** (growth-hold rationale). The 12-month appreciation case is real but rests on unproven AI/digital-twin monetization; the downside is well-flagged and partly priced. Owners should hold for the optionality and monitor the upgrade triggers; new money should wait for either evidence of re-acceleration or a price closer to the bear-case zone before committing.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the trailing peer-discount was overstated (BSY trailing P/E 33.6x vs peer median 39.3x is ~15%, not ~45%), which modestly narrows the "cheap-vs-peers" leg of the valuation case underpinning the HOLD.
