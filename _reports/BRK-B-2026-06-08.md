**Research Date: June 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Berkshire Hathaway is a $1.05 trillion holding company built on three durable advantages: ~$177B of low-cost insurance float, $727.2B of shareholders' equity, and a ~$373.5B cash-and-Treasury-bill buffer that ranks among the largest corporate liquidity positions in the world. The operating businesses are healthy: BNSF net earnings rose 13.4% with the operating ratio improving to 65.6%, manufacturing/service/retail pre-tax earnings grew 5.7%, and insurance underwriting delivered $2.27B pre-tax in a clean no-catastrophe quarter. Q1 2026 net earnings attributable to shareholders jumped to $10.1B from $4.6B a year earlier, though most of that swing is mark-to-market noise on the equity portfolio rather than a change in operating power.

The investment case has shifted in a way the headline numbers obscure. Greg Abel became CEO on January 1, 2026, and the central question is no longer whether the businesses generate cash but whether Abel allocates the cash mountain as skillfully as Buffett did over six decades. The first five months show a markedly more active style: the ~$9.5B OxyChem acquisition (closed January 2), the $6.8B all-cash Taylor Morrison homebuilder deal at a 24% premium (announced May 31), a $10B anchor commitment to Alphabet's ~$84.75B equity raise, and a portfolio cut from 42 to 29 names with 16 positions sold. This breaks from late-Buffett-era inertia and introduces allocation risk that did not exist under the prior regime.

We rate BRK-B a HOLD. The stock trades at $486.97, between the analyst consensus low of $481 and median of $510, with little asymmetry at current levels. The clearest signal comes from management itself: buybacks have gone essentially dormant ($235M in Q1 versus tens of billions in prior years), which says Abel does not view the stock as meaningfully below intrinsic value even as he deploys cash into outside acquisitions and equities. Full-year net income fell to $67.3B in 2025 from $89.6B in 2024, and 5-year total shareholder return of 217.0% has lagged the peer group's 234.6%. We see no near-term catalyst that re-rates the stock and a genuine open question about the new allocator's track record. That combination keeps us neutral.

## Company Overview

Berkshire operates a decentralized conglomerate across four pillars. Insurance is the economic engine: GEICO (auto), Berkshire Hathaway Primary Group, and Berkshire Hathaway Reinsurance Group (NICO, General Re, TransRe) collectively generate ~$177B of float, the low- or negative-cost insurance liabilities Berkshire invests for its own account. BNSF Railway operates one of North America's largest rail networks across 28 states. Berkshire Hathaway Energy holds regulated utilities (PacifiCorp, MidAmerican, NV Energy), interstate gas pipelines, international utilities, renewables, and HomeServices real estate brokerage. The Manufacturing, Service & Retailing segment spans Precision Castparts, Lubrizol, Marmon, Clayton Homes, See's, Pilot Travel Centers, McLane, and dozens more.

Beyond the operating businesses sits a ~$288B marketable equity portfolio concentrated 61% in five names (American Express, Apple, Bank of America, Coca-Cola, and Chevron), with the Amex stake alone at 22.2% of the portfolio. The structural advantage is permanence: fortress liquidity, negative-cost float, and a culture that lets Berkshire buy and hold businesses other acquirers cannot. The vulnerability that flows from the same structure is that returns depend heavily on how well the central allocator deploys the float and cash.

## Financial Analysis

**Balance sheet.** Total assets stood at $1.25T at March 31, 2026, with Berkshire shareholders' equity of $727.2B, up $9.8B from $717.4B at year-end 2025. Standard industrial solvency screens (Altman Z-Score, asset turnover) are not meaningful for a diversified insurer; the relevant gauges are equity, float (at negative cost), and the cash/T-bill buffer.

**Liquidity.** Insurance and other operations held the cash and U.S. Treasury bills at quarter-end, a war chest earning T-bill yields. The equity portfolio declined from $298B at year-end as Abel trimmed positions; roughly $24B of equities were sold in Q1, generating $7.2B of taxable gains. Management maintains a hard floor of keeping at least $30B in cash, leaving over $340B of deployable capital.

**Earnings.** Most of the Q1 swing is optical: GAAP requires the equity portfolio be marked to market, and after-tax investment losses were only -$1.2B this quarter versus -$5.0B in Q1 2025. Operating earnings are the signal, and they were broadly higher. The full-year net income trend deserves attention: $90.9B (2021), -$22B (2022, mark-to-market driven), $97.1B (2023), $89.6B (2024), and $67.3B (2025). The 2025 decline of roughly 25% is real and is the backdrop against which the new CEO operates. The effective tax rate rose to 17.4% in Q1 2026 from 9.2% a year earlier.

**Segment detail.** Insurance underwriting was driven by GEICO contributing $1.42B but showing a deteriorating loss ratio (73.9% versus 69.0% prior, with bodily-injury severity up 12–14% and the expense ratio up 2.6 points). Insurance investment income fell 7.2% to $3.3B on lower rates. BNSF net earnings reached $1.38B. BHE net earnings rose 1.3% to $1.11B, but US utility earnings fell 16% on wildfire costs. Manufacturing/service/retail pre-tax earnings reached $4.23B.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Q1 operating cash flow was $10.4B against capex of $5.0B, with BNSF and BHE guiding to ~$12.4B of additional capex over the rest of 2026. The most consequential capital-allocation fact is that buybacks have essentially stopped: $235M in Q1, a rounding error against the scale of the company. Management is choosing acquisitions and equities over repurchasing its own stock, which is a statement about where it sees relative value.

## Growth Analysis

Berkshire's organic growth is modest and tied to the underlying economy: rail volumes, insurance premium growth, utility rate base, and consumer spending. Estimate revisions are mixed. Current-year consensus EPS was revised down 13.6% over 90 days (from $24.02 to $20.75), though it ticked up 1.6% over the most recent 30 days; next-year EPS of $21.47 is roughly flat over 90 days (-1.5%). There is no estimate-revision momentum that would signal the market is systematically underestimating an earnings acceleration. The earnings surprise record is also two-sided: the company beat by 4.8% in Q3 2025 and 0.2% in Q2 2025 but missed by 15.9% in Q4 2025 and 2.3% in Q1 2026. Visibility into quarterly GAAP earnings is structurally poor because of mark-to-market volatility.

The growth story now runs through capital deployment rather than organic expansion. The Taylor Morrison acquisition is a counter-cyclical housing bet that, combined with Clayton Homes, makes Berkshire roughly the fourth-largest US homebuilder. It is being made into a soft housing demand picture: building-products businesses already report soft demand and a slow housing market. The bet could prove shrewd if Abel is buying a trough, or premature if housing weakens further. The Alphabet position is a passive equity stake, not a Berkshire AI capability: the AI build-out belongs to Alphabet, and Berkshire's exposure is purely as a shareholder in a mega-cap.

We score Growth Prospects 5.0, reflecting low organic growth, mixed estimate revisions, and deployment-driven upside that is unproven under the new allocator.

## Valuation Assessment

BRK-B trades at a trailing P/E of 14.5x and a forward P/E of 22.7x on consensus estimates. The forward multiple sitting above trailing reflects the consensus expectation that 2026 GAAP earnings normalize lower from elevated prior-year marks. The provided peer set (Blue Owl at 84.4x, Klarna at negative earnings) is not a usable comparable group; Berkshire is best valued on price-to-book and sum-of-the-parts. At $727.2B of equity, the stock trades at roughly 1.4x book value [unverified — actual per-share book value: N/A], near the upper end of the post-2020 range and well above the ~1.2x level at which Berkshire has historically repurchased stock aggressively.

The buyback signal is the cleanest valuation read available. Berkshire repurchases only when the price is meaningfully below conservatively estimated intrinsic value; the near-total halt says the people with the best information do not consider the stock cheap. That is a falsifiable, concrete reason the valuation does not support a BUY: the company's own allocator, with full knowledge of intrinsic value, is declining to buy at this price while actively deploying capital elsewhere.

Analyst targets span $481 (low) to $570 (high), with a median of $510 and mean of $520. The current price sits essentially at the low end of the target range, implying roughly 5% upside to the median and 17% to the high. On a quality business this is not unreasonable, but it is not an asymmetric setup that justifies a high valuation score. We score Valuation 5.0 — fairly priced, with the stock's own buyer absent.

## Competitive Landscape

Berkshire's competitive position remains strong in aggregate but is no longer uniformly improving, which is why the trajectory matters. Insurance faces rising loss-cost inflation, with GEICO's loss ratio deteriorating and BHRG seeing property reinsurance rates and volumes soften on increased competition. The offset is the new 10-year whole-account quota-share with Tokio Marine (effective April 1, 2026), which adds meaningful premium volume. BNSF is operating well, though potential Class I rail consolidation is both an opportunity and a disruption risk. BHE remains a capital-hungry regulated grower whose defining vulnerability is wildfire tail liability, the same risk that impaired PG&E and Edison.

The moat — permanent capital, negative-cost float, decentralized acquisition capability — is intact and difficult to replicate. What has changed is that the allocation skill that converted those structural advantages into industry-leading returns is now being exercised by a new person. The 5-year TSR trailing the peer group indicates the late-Buffett era already lagged, which is partly why Abel is deploying cash more aggressively. We score Competitive Position 7.0, reflecting a durable moat with a softening insurance pricing backdrop and an unresolved question about whether the new regime sustains the historical edge.

## Risk Assessment

**Allocation risk (the dominant variable).** The thesis historically rested on Buffett and Munger's capital-allocation judgment. Abel now controls deployment of the cash buffer, and three large commitments in five months are being made without a multi-year track record to assess. This is not solvency risk; it is the risk that returns on deployed capital fall short of the Buffett-era bar.

**PacifiCorp wildfire liability.** Cumulative probable losses of ~$2.9B have been accrued through Q1 2026, with ~$2.3B already paid. The development here turned favorable: on April 8, 2026, the Oregon Court of Appeals reversed and remanded the *James* Phase I liability verdict (which had produced ~$1.25B in net damages to 201 plaintiffs), staying further trials and pushing parties toward mediation. This could discharge $719M in posted bonds and reduce the tail. Material additional losses remain reasonably possible and are not estimable.

**Equity concentration.** With 61% of the portfolio in five names and the Apple position substantial, a sharp decline in a few holdings would hit equity and insurance statutory surplus.

**Kraft Heinz impairment.** The equity-method carrying value exceeds fair value by $1.4B; a charge is possible if the gap persists.

**GEICO underwriting.** The deteriorating loss ratio and rising bodily-injury severity reflect social inflation that bears watching.

**Other.** HomeServices antitrust settlement ($250M over four years, on appeal); declining BNSF coal volumes on utility retirements; catastrophe tolerance of up to ~$15B from a single event against $151.9B of unpaid P&C loss reserves; Taylor Morrison entering a weakening housing market.

### Options Market Signal

Short interest is negligible at 1.0% of float with 2.3 days to cover; there is no bearish positioning of note and no squeeze dynamic. Beta is 0.617, consistent with defensive characteristics. The absence of provided options data limits any read on put demand or implied volatility, but the low short interest supports the view that the market sees Berkshire as a low-volatility, fairly-priced holding rather than a stock pricing imminent stress.

We score Risk Factors 6.5: the balance sheet eliminates solvency and refinancing risk, the wildfire overhang improved materially in April, but allocation risk under the new CEO and equity concentration are genuine and not fully priced.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Abel deploys the cash hoard at attractive returns, with OxyChem and Taylor Morrison proving to be well-timed counter-cyclical buys and the Tokio Marine quota-share lifting insurance volume. Operating earnings compound mid-single-digits, the wildfire tail shrinks as the *James* remand favors PacifiCorp, and a defensive market environment drives multiple expansion. The stock reaches the $570 analyst high, roughly 17% above current levels.

**Bear Case.** Abel's accelerated deployment dilutes returns — Taylor Morrison runs into a housing downturn, the Alphabet stake gets caught in an AI-capex-disappointment derating, and GEICO's loss ratio continues to deteriorate. Full-year earnings stay soft, the multiple compresses toward 1.2x book (the historical buyback zone), and the stock falls toward the lower end of its trading range. A single large catastrophe within the tolerance or a Kraft Heinz impairment adds downside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale — we lack the evidence for a confident directional call in either direction. The defining variable (Abel's allocation track record) will not resolve for several quarters.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Buybacks resume at scale (>$5B in a quarter), signaling management again sees the stock below intrinsic value — the single cleanest upgrade signal.
- Stock pulls back toward 1.2x book with operating earnings and float intact, restoring asymmetry.
- Evidence that the Abel-era deployments (OxyChem, Taylor Morrison) are generating returns above the cost of capital, demonstrated through segment-earnings disclosure over the next 2–3 quarters.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A large value-destructive acquisition (>$15B at a premium multiple) that signals undisciplined deployment.
- GEICO loss ratio continuing past 76–77% combined with a major catastrophe loss near the $15B tolerance.
- Material adverse reversal in the PacifiCorp wildfire litigation that pushes accrued losses well above the current ~$2.9B.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly 10-Q (Q2 2026, expected early August) for buyback activity, GEICO loss-ratio trajectory, and any update on the Taylor Morrison deal close. The next proxy will reveal Abel's compensation structure, a key governance read on alignment.

## Conclusion

Berkshire Hathaway is one of the highest-quality, most financially resilient enterprises in public markets, and nothing in the Q1 data threatens that. Operating earnings are healthy, the balance sheet is a fortress, the wildfire overhang improved in April, and the stock carries genuine defensive value. None of that makes it a buy at $486.97.

The decision rests on two concrete, falsifiable facts. First, the company's own allocator, with the best possible information on intrinsic value, has stopped buying the stock. Second, the entire historical thesis rested on Buffett-era allocation skill, and that skill is now being exercised by a new CEO whose track record consists of three large commitments made in five months with no multi-year results to judge them by. Until either the buyback resumes or Abel's deployments demonstrate Buffett-caliber returns, the risk/reward at a price wedged between the consensus low and median targets does not support a higher conviction call. We rate BRK-B a HOLD.
