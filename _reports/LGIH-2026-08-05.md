## Executive Summary

LGI Homes reported a Q2 2026 revenue beat ($501.5M homebuilding revenue, +3.7% year over year) and raised full-year average selling price and gross-margin guidance for the second consecutive quarter. Shares rose about 10% on August 4 and are now 10.7% below the 52-week high of $69.50 and roughly 85% above the March low of $33.55. The operating picture behind the beat is weaker than the headline. Closings on the comparable metric rose 3.2% to 1,365 (the "1,440, up 8.8%" figure in the release includes 75 currently or previously leased single-family-rental homes, a balance-sheet disposal). Absorption was 3.0 homes per community per month, identical to Q2 2025. GAAP diluted EPS fell 14.7% to $1.16 from $1.36. The incremental closings came from community count (149.7 average vs 146.0) and wholesale timing, not from retail sales velocity.

Two disclosures dominate the risk assessment. First, the cancellation rate reached **47.4% of gross orders in H1 2026** versus 24.2% a year earlier. Reconstructing the arithmetic from net orders of 2,260, gross order intake rose roughly 29% while cancellations rose roughly 152%. Demand at the top of the funnel improved; mortgage qualification and closing conversion failed. For a builder whose buyers put down $1,000–$10,000 and whose base depends on FHA, USDA and VA origination, the Q2 10-Q attributes the cancellations "primarily due to financing challenges and buyer sensitivity to market conditions," and management notes mortgage rates trended upward during the quarter. Second, the August 1, 2025 credit amendment set the minimum EBITDA-to-interest-expense covenant at **1.15x through December 31, 2026, reverting to 1.50x** thereafter, tightened the leverage cap to 55% inside that window, and bars share repurchases and dividends through year-end 2026. A derived trailing coverage estimate of roughly 1.22x clears the relief floor and sits below the snapback test.

The valuation reconciles the two views investors hold on this stock. At 0.674x book value per share of $91.73, LGIH looks like a discounted asset. At ~18.4x the single-analyst current-year estimate of $3.36 and ~19.7x a guidance-derived $3.14, against a homebuilding peer median P/E of 10.7x, it is expensive on earnings. Both are true because the asset base earns approximately 3.4% on equity. A standard residual-income decomposition (11% cost of equity, 3% terminal growth) implies that a 0.674x book multiple already prices a sustainable ROE near 8.4%, roughly 2.5x the current run-rate. The discount to book is not unpriced optionality on recovery; it is the market's compensation for a return well below cost of equity, with a recovery already partly embedded. Peers CCS (13.6x), GRBK (10.7x), MHO (10.7x), KBH (10.4x) and DFH (8.7x) offer housing exposure at materially lower earnings multiples with, in most cases, less leverage and no covenant-relief window expiring in five months. We move from HOLD to AVOID.

## Company Overview

LGI Homes builds and sells primarily entry-level, move-in-ready single-family homes across 36 markets in 21 states, reported in five segments (Central, Southeast, Northwest, West, Florida), under the LGI Homes and Terrata Homes brands. It has closed over 80,000 homes since 2003. Headcount was 1,056 at December 31, 2025.

Three structural features define the business and all three matter to the current call:

**Build-first, sell-later.** The 10-K states the model "is based on building homes before a sales contract is executed and a customer deposit is received," with required deposits of $1,000–$10,000, and notes "there is little negative impact to the potential homebuyer from the cancellation of the purchase contract." This converts quickly in strong demand and traps capital in finished inventory when demand weakens.

**An owned, self-developed land bank.** At June 30, 2026, LGIH owned or controlled 57,406 lots, of which **50,522 (88%) were owned outright**; of owned lots, 33,775 (67%) were raw or under development. Option deposits were $14.2M against $257.0M of commitments. The company wound down land-banking arrangements entirely in H1 2026 ("real estate not owned" went to zero), moving opposite the industry's shift toward optioned, off-balance-sheet lot supply.

**A centralized, high-throughput sales system** targeting renters converting to ownership. Absorption rate is therefore the single most informative operating metric, and it has not recovered.

Adjacent activities include LGI Living (build-to-rent/SFR), a growing wholesale channel selling completed homes in bulk to institutional buyers, lot sales, and equity-method joint ventures including LGI Mortgage Solutions. Mortgage economics are accessed only through that JV, not a consolidated captive lender.

FY2025 was the break: revenue −22.6%, closings −22.3%, net income −63.0%, EPS from $8.30 to $3.12, and absorption from 3.8 to 2.7 homes per community per month while average community count rose from 130.5 to 144.4. Ending community count still fell from 151 to 144 as closeouts outpaced openings.

H1 2026 stabilized volume (−3.1% closings) while margin continued to erode. Q2 homebuilding gross margin fell 310bp to 19.8% from 22.9%.

### The capitalized-interest drag

LGIH capitalizes 100% of interest incurred. In Q2 2026, interest incurred was $29.0M and capitalized was $29.0M, with **reported interest expense of zero**. The economic interest burden arrives later inside cost of sales as inventory sells:

- Capitalized interest charged to cost of sales: $33.4M (FY2023) → $42.1M (FY2024) → $45.5M (FY2025); Q2'26 **$16.5M vs $11.8M**; H1'26 $26.4M vs $20.1M.
- As a share of homebuilding revenue: 2.4% (Q2'25) → **3.3% (Q2'26)**. FY2026 guidance assumes capitalized interest plus purchase accounting of approximately **3.5%**.

Roughly 90bp of the 310bp Q2 margin decline is attributable to this line; adjusted gross margin, which strips it, fell less severely (25.5% → 23.2%). The relevant point is that this drag reflects debt already incurred on land already owned and lengthening holding periods (67% of owned lots are raw or under development), so it continues flowing through COGS regardless of near-term operating improvement.

### Cash flow: not yet self-funding

Operating cash flow was **−$143.7M (FY2024)** and **−$140.0M (FY2025)** against reported net income of $196M and $73M. H1 2026 turned positive at **+$59.0M** versus −$213.5M in H1 2025, but the composition matters. Accounts payable rose **$42.6M** (balance $16.2M → $58.8M, a 3.6x increase); real estate inventory consumed only $19.9M of cash versus $240.3M in H1 2025 because the company largely stopped buying land; cash taxes were $16.0M versus $59.9M. Excluding the AP build alone, H1 operating cash flow was roughly $16.4M on $29.1M of net income.

As a labeled sensitivity rather than a forecast: if the payables build reverses in H2 and land spend moves toward replacement of consumed lots, full-year 2026 operating cash flow plausibly lands between modestly negative and roughly +$100M. A normalized construction — net income of about $73M plus ~$5M of D&A, less the inventory required to support 150–160 communities — points to approximately breakeven. Owned-and-controlled lots fell from 70,899 (12/31/2024) to 57,406 (6/30/2026), a 19% reduction in eighteen months. That is a defensible downturn response and it funded real deleveraging, but it is cash from shrinking the pipeline rather than from operating conversion.

### Balance sheet and covenants

At June 30, 2026: total assets $3,918.6M, of which real estate inventory is $3,512.6M (90%); cash $61.1M; notes payable net $1,580.9M; equity $2,132.7M; goodwill $12.0M. Debt/capital 42.6%, net debt/capital 41.6% (from 44.1%/43.2% at year-end 2025). Liquidity $468.0M. Debt fell $128.6M in Q2 and $75.9M net over H1.

Maturity ladder: revolver $449.0M drawn (17.8% April 2028, 82.2% April 2029); $400M 8.750% notes due December 2028; $300M 4.000% notes due July 2029; $400M 7.000% notes due November 2032; $50M SFR loan at 6.433% due July 2030. Nothing matures before April 2028. The refinancing item to note is the $300M 4.000% notes: replacing them at current levels (the company's 2028s at 8.75%, 2032s at 7.00%) adds roughly $9–14M of annual interest. Fair-value marks are consistent with a wide but non-distressed spread — the 8.75% 2028s marked at $428.7M on $400M, the 4.00% 2029s at $286.2M on $300M.

The covenant package is the most decision-relevant disclosure. Per the FY2025 10-K, the amended Credit Agreement requires tangible net worth ≥ ~$1.512B plus 50% of post-2024 earnings and equity issuance; leverage ≤ 55.0% through December 31, 2026 and ≤ 60.0% thereafter; liquidity ≥ $100.0M; **EBITDA-to-interest-expense ≥ 1.15x through December 31, 2026 and ≥ 1.50x thereafter**; and restrictions on share repurchases and dividends through December 31, 2026.

A reproducible estimate of headroom (derived, not a covenant calculation): TTM EBITDA on the company's own definition = $148.4M (FY2025) − $69.7M (H1'25) + $69.9M (H1'26) = **$148.5M**. TTM interest incurred ≈ **$121.7M**, using FY2025 interest incurred estimated at ~$125M from the disclosed H1'25 figure of $62.5M plus H1'26 of $59.2M. Implied coverage ≈ **1.22x**. Reaching 1.50x on $121.7M of interest requires EBITDA of ~$182.6M, **+23% from trailing**; if paydown reduces interest incurred to ~$110M, required EBITDA falls to ~$165M (+11%).

Caveats matter here and I will not overstate the finding. The Credit Agreement's contractual definitions of EBITDA and interest expense are not in evidence and may be more permissive than these proxies; the test is trailing-four-quarter, so seasonal H2 earnings plus continued deleveraging could clear 1.50x by the test date without any amendment; lenders routinely extend such windows; and the company states it was in compliance with all covenants at June 30, 2026. My characterization of the amendment as negotiated relief is an inference from the structure of the terms and their common expiry date, not a company statement. Headroom on the other three tests is comfortable: tangible net worth of $2,120.6M against a requirement near $1,563M, leverage far inside the 55% cap, liquidity 4.7x the $100M floor (though largely revolver availability rather than cash).

**Financial health score: 4.0.** No near-term maturities, real deleveraging, and adequate liquidity offset by two years of negative operating cash flow, 90% of assets in inventory, an 8.75% coupon in the stack, a coverage covenant reverting in five months against derived coverage near 1.22x, and a buyback prohibition that removes the capital-return lever through 2026.

## Growth Analysis

Full-year 2026 guidance: closings 4,600–5,400; year-end active communities 150–160 (151 already reached at June 30); ASP $360,000–$370,000 (raised); homebuilding gross margin 19.0–21.0% and adjusted 22.5–24.5% (raised for a second consecutive quarter); SG&A 15.0–16.0% of revenue; tax rate ~26.5%.

The closings range is ±8% around the midpoint and the halves are not equally probable. The low end (4,600) requires H2 closings of ~2,354, essentially flat versus H2 2025's ~2,366. The high end (5,400) requires ~3,154, or +33% year over year, with absorption flat and H1 closings down 3.1%. The low half is the more defensible planning assumption, which matters because a midpoint-based earnings bridge (5,000 closings × $365,000 ASP, 20.0% homebuilding gross margin, 15.5% SG&A, ~$22M other income, 26.5% tax) produces pre-tax income near $100M, net income near $73M, and diluted EPS near **$3.14** — a forward P/E of ~19.7x and an ROE of ~3.4%. H1 pre-tax was $40.9M, so the midpoint requires ~$59M in H2.

Positives on the growth side: community count is already at the low end of the full-year target, backlog rose to 1,298 homes / $525.5M from 808 / $322.5M, ASP guidance was raised, and margin guidance was raised twice. Qualifications: 269 of the 1,298 backlog units relate to bulk sales agreements (versus 91 a year earlier), so bulk moved from 11.3% to 20.7% of backlog, with lower margins and different conversion risk; the company itself defines backlog to include wholesale contracts "with varying terms" and warns it may be affected by cancellations. With nominal deposits and roughly half of gross contracts cancelling, backlog carries unusually low predictive content and should not be read as contracted revenue.

Segment mix is deteriorating where it matters. Southeast, which produced $47.7M of FY2025 segment pre-tax income (the largest of any segment), saw H1 2026 revenue −28.3%, closings −29.4%, absorption from 4.1 to 3.0, community count from 35 to 30, and pre-tax income from $26.3M to $11.6M. West (+25.8% revenue) and Central (+1.3%) offset it from lower-margin geographies. Florida posted pre-tax losses of $(6.5)M in FY2025 and $(4.8)M in H1 2026.

Two headwinds are dated and mechanical. The Section 45L energy-efficient homes credit terminates for homes closing after June 30, 2026 under the July 2025 tax act; it was still reducing the Q2 effective rate, so the ~26.5% FY2026 guide likely understates the run-rate from Q3 forward. And the 19% land-bank reduction, while cash-positive now, constrains community-count growth in the late 2020s given a stated three-to-four-year raw-land-to-community cycle. With ~11.5 years of lot supply at guidance midpoint, that constraint binds beyond the current forecast horizon.

On estimate revisions: the single covering analyst raised current-year EPS from $2.02 to $3.36 over 90 days (+66%) and next year from $3.13 to $3.94 (+25.9%), but cut both by ~1% over 30 days. A +30% 90-day revision is normally a strong forward signal. I am weighting it lightly here for three reasons: coverage is one analyst, so it reflects a single model catching up to Q1's low-base beat rather than broad consensus repricing; the 30-day direction is negative; and even on the revised numbers the stock trades at 15.7x next year versus a peer median of 10.7x on current earnings, so the revisions do not create a cheap multiple. The signal is strongest when rising estimates leave the stock cheap. They do not here.

**Growth prospects score: 4.5.** Volume has stabilized and margin guidance improved, but absorption is flat, the largest profit segment is contracting, the tax rate steps up permanently from Q3, and the guidance range's high half is not supported by H1 run-rates.

Peer set: CCS 13.6x, GRBK 10.7x, MHO 10.7x, KBH 10.4x, DFH 8.7x. LGIH's forward multiple on consensus is roughly 1.7x the peer median trailing multiple, and its EV/EBITDA is nearly 20x.

The reconciliation of 0.67x book with 20x earnings is the 3.4% return on equity. A residual-income framing makes the implication explicit: justified P/B ≈ (sustainable ROE − g) / (cost of equity − g). Solving with an 11% cost of equity and 3% terminal growth, the observed 0.674x book implies a sustainable ROE of approximately **8.4%** — around $179M of net income, or roughly $7.70 per share, versus the ~$3.14 the FY2026 guidance bridge supports. Put differently, the market is already capitalizing a mid-cycle earnings power roughly 2.5x the current run-rate at about 8x. Alternatively, applying the 10.7x peer multiple to today's price implies mid-cycle EPS of $5.80 and ROE of 6.3%. Under either construction, a meaningful recovery is priced, and the discount to book is compensation for a sub-cost-of-equity return rather than free optionality.

The book value denominator is itself uncertain in both directions. Ninety percent of assets are real estate inventory carried at cost, and impairments have resumed ($6.7M in FY2025 across 4 of 144 communities; $4.7M in H1 2026), concentrated in Florida and Central in both periods. Conversely, land held for years at historical cost could be carried below market in appreciating submarkets. I cannot quantify either, so 0.67x should be read as a multiple of an imprecise denominator.

The single analyst target of $93 (high = low = mean = median) implies 1.01x book and roughly 24x FY2027 consensus. It is a genuine bull anchor but a one-source datapoint that is not independent confirmation of value, and I would not build a base case on it.

**Valuation score: 4.5.** Not a value trap in the conventional cheap-multiple sense, because it is not cheap on earnings; the asset discount is defensible but already discounts a recovery to roughly 8% ROE, and cheaper builders with better returns are available in the same industry.

## Competitive Landscape

Management describes the demand backdrop directly in the Q2 10-Q: "Persistently high mortgage rates continue to be a key pressure point for entry-level buyers. During the quarter, mortgage rates trended upward... Additionally, subdued consumer sentiment continues to impact buyers' willingness to purchase new homes." That is a fresh headwind, not a continuing one, and the Q2 10-Q's forward-looking factors reference "the ongoing U.S. government shutdown," which, given LGIH's reliance on FHA/USDA/VA origination, is a direct closing-timing risk.

LGIH's own 10-K enumerates structural disadvantages: low barriers to entry, competitors with greater financial and operational resources, competitors more geographically diversified and "better able to withstand any future regional downturns," and market share in certain markets "lower as compared to some of our competitors." At roughly 4,700–5,000 annual closings, LGIH is about an order of magnitude smaller than the largest national builders. The most consequential gap in this cycle is mortgage: LGIH accesses origination economics only through an equity-method JV, which limits its ability to fund rate buydowns at the scale peers with consolidated captive lenders can. Given that its cancellation problem is explicitly attributed to "financing challenges," this is a live competitive constraint, not a theoretical one.

The wholesale channel is the clearest competitive tell. Bulk sales to institutional purchasers went from 9.2% of closings (FY2024) to 15.7% (FY2025) to 18.8% (H1 2026) to **21.6% (Q2 2026)**, with Q2 wholesale revenue of $73.5M on 295 closings, and management attributing the Q2 increase to "a previously contracted bulk sales agreement." Bulk transactions typically price below retail, so the mix shift clears inventory and generates cash at the cost of margin per unit. It reads as channel substitution driven by weak retail conversion. Importantly, LGIH does not disclose named-customer concentration, so I cannot establish whether wholesale is concentrated in a small number of buyers; the singular reference to one bulk agreement and 269 bulk backlog units raises the question without resolving it, and a changing mix is not evidence of reduced concentration risk.

The build-to-rent arm functions as a second inventory outlet. PP&E net rose from $107.1M to $149.6M in H1 2026 with $66.3M of homes transferred from inventory to held-for-lease (versus $41.4M a year earlier), $28.4M of PP&E sale proceeds and a $5.5M gain on disposal (versus a $2.3M loss). Leasing unsold spec homes converts illiquid inventory into a yielding asset, and later sales book gains. The business line is independently sensible; the presentational consequence is that headline closings growth of 8.8% includes 75 previously leased homes, against a comparable 3.2%. Financing is through LGI Living – SFR 1, LLC ($50M drawn, expandable to $125M, 6.433%, July 2030) with a parent-level limited recourse guaranty that springs to the full loan amount upon specified events, plus parent liquidity and net-worth maintenance tests tied to the loan size.

**Competitive position score: 4.0**, reduced from the prior assessment on new Q2 evidence: absorption flat while community count rose, cancellations doubling, the largest profit segment contracting sharply, and wholesale reliance climbing.

## Risk Assessment

**Cancellation conversion.** 22.8% (FY2024) → 32.8% (FY2025) → **47.4% (H1 2026)** versus 24.2% in H1 2025. Derived gross orders rose ~29% while cancellations rose ~152%. Two alternative readings are possible from the disclosure and I cannot distinguish them: LGIH may have loosened its pre-qualification screen, inflating both numerator and denominator; or bulk contract re-papering could register as simultaneous cancellations and new gross orders (bulk backlog units went from 91 to 269, large enough to distort these statistics). The company cautions these operational figures "should be used only as a general gauge."

**Covenant reversion.** The 1.15x coverage relief expires December 31, 2026 and the test reverts to 1.50x. Derived trailing coverage near 1.22x implies a required EBITDA increase of ~23%, or a ~$12M annual interest reduction, or an amendment. The buyback and dividend prohibition also lapses at that date; LGIH repurchased $23.6M in H1 2025 and zero in H1 2026 despite $157.3M of remaining authorization.

**Unresolved auditor change.** An 8-K filed June 10, 2026 under Item 4.01 (Changes in Certifying Accountant) could not be retrieved. The identity of outgoing and incoming firms, whether resignation or dismissal, and whether any Item 304(a) disagreements or reportable events were disclosed are all unknown. Routine rotation with no disagreements is the overwhelmingly likely explanation and should be presumed absent contrary evidence. It nonetheless sits in the same window as a 63% earnings decline, resumed impairments and a covenant amendment, and it is an open governance item.

**Tax and program dependency.** The 45L credit terminates for closings after June 30, 2026 — a permanent effective-rate step-up from Q3. The 10-K describes FHA and USDA backing as "particularly important... to our business," specifically citing USDA zero-down/100% financing, and the Q2 10-Q's forward-looking factors reference "the ongoing U.S. government shutdown," making origination timing a direct closing risk.

**Concentration and structure.** Undisclosed wholesale customer concentration; the springing full-recourse feature on the SFR loan; 88% owned land exposing LGIH to land-price and carry risk peers have shifted off balance sheet; ~11.5 years of lot supply at guidance midpoint.

**Short interest.** 20.4% of float short, 2,974,359 shares, 7.9 days to cover. This is two-sided: it evidences a widely held bear case (limiting how much of the downside is unpriced) and creates squeeze mechanics on positive surprises, as the two-day move around the Q2 print demonstrated.

**Management alignment.** Structure is genuinely shareholder-exposed: CEO compensation actually paid was **−$3,291,079** in FY2025 on $5,182,051 reported, PSUs are tracking at 51.6% of target, payouts are capped at 100% of target if absolute TSR is negative, and the five-year TSR index stands at 40.59 versus a peer 179.02. Against that: no open-market purchase by any officer or director appears in the reviewed Form 4 window, including at $41.54–$43.41 in March 2026 with the stock near 0.46x book. All executive transactions follow a grant-then-sell-to-cover pattern. Blackout windows, 10b5-1 constraints and existing large holdings (Lipar retains 630,702 shares, ~2.7% of shares out) are plausible explanations, and the corporate buyback restriction does not bind individuals. The absence of dip-buying is a non-confirmation rather than a bearish signal. 2026 grant repricing (RSUs at $42.91 weighted-average grant-date fair value versus $86.51 on the beginning balance) roughly doubles shares issued per dollar of grant, shifting recovery upside toward management; maximum contingent dilution from unvested RSUs and target PSUs is ~669,000 shares, about 2.9% of shares outstanding, spread over multiple years and conditioned on performance. **Management quality score: 4.5.**

**Risk factors score: 3.5** — a dated covenant reversion, a doubling cancellation rate, an unresolved auditor change, a permanent tax step-up and undisclosed channel concentration constitute more unpriced binary exposure than the current multiple accommodates.

That combination indicates the market is pricing near-term event risk rather than a directional regime change. The read for a fundamental bear: options positioning does not confirm the thesis. When 20.4% of the float is short and the options market shows call-tilted open interest with rich near-term vol, the mechanics favor waiting rather than pressing.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($80; +29%)

Mortgage rates fall, entry-level qualification improves, and the 47.4% cancellation rate reverts toward the 22–25% range — a conversion problem is more rate-responsive than a traffic problem, and gross order intake up ~29% shows the funnel is intact. Community count of 151 already at the low end of guidance provides an absorption-driven earnings lever without capital. Adjusted gross margin guidance has been raised twice, and self-development supports margin as lower-cost lots deliver. Continued paydown (debt −$128.6M in Q2) plus seasonal H2 earnings clears the 1.50x coverage test on the contractual definition, the buyback prohibition lapses December 31, 2026, and $157.3M of authorization is deployed at a large discount to $91.73 book value. On a path back to 8–9% ROE ($7.00–$7.70 EPS), 10–11x supports $75–$85, and the single analyst target of $93 sits at 1.01x book. Short interest of 20.4% and 7.9 days to cover amplifies any positive surprise.

### Bear Case ($40; −35%)

Mortgage rates rise further and cancellations stay near 45%+. FY2026 closings land at the low end (4,600), gross margin at the low end (19.0%) as the capitalized-interest load builds toward the guided 3.5% of revenue and wholesale mix climbs above 22%, so FY2026 EPS comes in nearer $2.50 than $3.14. The 45L credit loss lifts the effective tax rate from Q3. H2 operating cash flow reverses as the $42.6M payables build unwinds and land spend resumes. Coverage fails to reach 1.50x by the test date, requiring a further amendment that extends the buyback prohibition and adds pricing. Impairments broaden beyond Florida and Central, pressuring the book value that supports the asset case. The multiple converges toward the peer median of 10.7x on depressed earnings, or toward the 0.45–0.50x book at which the stock traded in March 2026 — $40 to $46.

### Base Case ($54; −13%)

Closings land in the lower half of guidance (4,700–4,900), gross margin near 19.5%, FY2026 EPS $2.85–$3.15, and FY2027 EPS below the $3.94 consensus at roughly $3.40–$3.60 as rates stay elevated into mid-2027. Coverage is managed through continued deleveraging with a modest amendment or a narrow pass, and buybacks resume only late in 2027. The stock settles at 0.58–0.60x book, or 15–16x FY2027 earnings, around **$54**.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The nearest dated items are the Q3 print in late October 2026 (first quarter without the 45L credit, first test of whether the H1 payables build reverses, and the third consecutive quarter of a 40%+ cancellation rate if trends hold) and the December 31, 2026 covenant reversion, disclosed in the FY2026 10-K in February 2027. **Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamental concerns are real but the catalyst is negotiable rather than forced, 20.4% short interest approaches crowded, options positioning is call-tilted, protection is rich, and the asymmetry does not clear. Avoid owning; do not press the short.

## Conclusion

LGI Homes has done the sensible things a levered, inventory-heavy builder should do in a weak entry-level market: stopped buying land, reduced debt by $128.6M in a quarter, cut owned-and-controlled lots 19% in eighteen months, held community count at the low end of the full-year target, and raised gross-margin guidance twice. Those actions bought balance-sheet time and produced a genuine Q2 revenue beat.

They have not fixed the return on capital or the conversion problem. The guidance bridge supports approximately $3.14 of FY2026 EPS and a 3.4% ROE. Nearly half of gross orders cancelled in the first half. GAAP homebuilding gross margin fell 310bp with roughly a third of that from an interest load already committed to land already owned, and the FY2026 guide assumes that drag rises to about 3.5% of homebuilding revenue. The coverage relief that permitted a 1.15x test expires in five months against derived trailing coverage near 1.22x, and the same amendment bars the buyback that would otherwise be the obvious use of a 0.67x-book share price.

The price is where the call turns. At $62.04 the stock carries ~18.4x consensus current-year earnings and ~19.9x EV/EBITDA against a homebuilding peer median P/E of 10.7x, and the discount to book already capitalizes a mid-cycle ROE near 8.4% — roughly 2.5x the current run-rate. Investors who want entry-level housing exposure into a possible 2027 rate decline can buy the same cyclical option in better-capitalized builders at 10–13x earnings without a covenant window closing on December 31, 2026 or an unresolved change in certifying accountant. **AVOID**, with re-entry near $46 or on demonstrated coverage clearance plus a cancellation rate back below 30%.
