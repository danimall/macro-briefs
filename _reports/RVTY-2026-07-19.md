**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Revvity is a $12.5B life-science tools and diagnostics company built out of the former PerkinElmer, now organized into two roughly equal segments: Life Sciences (~$1.43B) and Diagnostics (~$1.43B). The business generates high-quality free cash flow (~$515M in FY2025, an 18% FCF margin), holds leading global positions in newborn and reproductive-health screening, and runs a razor-and-blade reagent/consumables model with a growing software attach. The business quality is high but well understood and largely priced, while the growth engine has stalled.

The core issue is top-line stagnation. Reported revenue moved from $2,751M (2023) to $2,755M (2024) to $2,856M (2025), and management's own "non-COVID organic revenue growth" measure fell from 9.1% to 4.1% to 1.9% over the same period. Gross margin compressed 104 bps in 2025 to 54.8% and fell a further 200 bps in Q1 2026 to 54.5%, pressured by tariffs, FX, and China mix. Full-year 2026 revenue guidance of roughly $2.83B at the midpoint landed about 5.2% below Street estimates even as the Q1 print beat. The GAAP net-income decline from $693M to $241M is dominated by a 2023 divestiture gain, ~$335M/year of acquisition amortization, 2025 restructuring, and falling interest income; the real weakness is growth, not earnings quality.

The stock has run 15% over the past three months and sits near its 52-week high of $118.30, trading at roughly 21x the $5.26 current-year adjusted EPS estimate (and 53x trailing GAAP EPS). That re-rating has been driven by sector consolidation sentiment rather than any improvement in Revvity's own fundamentals; estimates have drifted down 2.6% over 90 days. With a thin goodwill cushion in Life Sciences Solutions, an announced China divestiture carrying execution risk, no open-market insider buying, and multi-year TSR underperformance versus peers, the setup is balanced. We rate the shares HOLD.

## Company Overview

Revvity (rebranded from PerkinElmer in 2023 after divesting its legacy Applied, Food and Enterprise Services business) is a Waltham, MA-based health-science company with ~11,000 employees operating in 160+ countries. Roughly 80% of the workforce and the majority of revenue sit outside the U.S., and about 60% of the business is conducted in foreign currencies, creating meaningful translation exposure.

The company runs two segments of similar scale:

- **Life Sciences (~50% of revenue; $1,431M FY2025):** Reagents, instruments, and software for drug discovery and development, serving pharma, biotech, CROs, and academia. Core franchises include BioLegend (>35,000 antibody/reagent SKUs, acquired 2021), Dharmacon gene modulation, HTRF/AlphaLISA detection chemistries, high-content imaging (Opera Phenix/Operetta), and the Signals informatics software suite (growing double-digit, ~$238M deferred revenue).
- **Diagnostics (~50%; $1,425M FY2025):** Reproductive Health (newborn/prenatal screening — DELFIA, NeoBase, EONIS, Vanadis NIPT, where Revvity holds leading global share), Immunodiagnostics (EUROIMMUN autoimmune/allergy, Oxford Immunotec T-SPOT.TB, plus a China infectious-disease/blood-screening business), and emerging-market diagnostics.

The most durable part of the franchise is reproductive-health screening: reimbursement-driven, recurring, and relatively insulated from biopharma capex cycles. The most cyclically exposed part is the Life Sciences Solutions reagent/instrument line, which is directly hit by soft academic/government funding and pharma/biotech spending caution.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** The 2025 gain of ~4% reported / ~3% organic was FX-aided by about a point. Q1 2026 revenue of $711.1M grew 7% YoY, but that was flattered by ~3 pts of FX, ~1 pt of M&A, and an extra 53rd fiscal week. Underlying organic momentum remains low single digits.

**Margins.** The 2025 gross-margin decline was driven by ~$20M net tariff impact, FX, and China mix. Both segments eroded in 2025: Life Sciences operating margin -139 bps to 32.0%; Diagnostics -194 bps to 24.2%, hit by China diagnostic-testing policy changes and tariffs. Margin compression is the more concerning half of the profitability story because it compounds the flat-revenue problem.

**Earnings quality.** GAAP net income fell from $693M (2023) to $270M (2024) to $241M (2025). This is largely non-operating: the 2023 base was inflated by divestiture gains, and the decline reflects ~$335M/year of intangible amortization, $55.9M of 2025 restructuring (affecting ~5% of the workforce), and lower interest income as cash shrank. Adjusted EPS is the metric the Street uses: FY2025 came in above the $4.90–$5.00 guided range, and Q1 2026 adjusted EPS was $1.06 (a 2.3% beat). The company has beaten adjusted-EPS consensus in each of the last four quarters, though the beats have been modest (1.5%–7.4%).

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $589M and FCF $515M (18% margin) — healthy, though down from ~$665M OCF in 2024 on a China-collections working-capital drag. Capex is light (~$74M), and D&A (~$405M) far exceeds it, reflecting the acquisition-heavy, amortization-laden structure. FCF conversion relative to adjusted earnings is solid; relative to GAAP net income it is well above 100%, confirming the GAAP figure understates true cash generation.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets of $12.2B include $6.6B goodwill and $2.3B intangibles — about 74% of the asset base. Net debt is moderate at ~$3.2B gross debt against ~$860M cash (Q1 2026). Current ratio 1.68, D/E 0.68, investment-grade rated, with a $1.5B undrawn revolver. €500M of 2026 Notes mature in July 2026, to be repaid from cash/revolver — a routine, well-covered maturity. The Altman Z of 2.14 ("grey zone") is a mechanical artifact of low asset turnover (0.23) in an intangible-heavy structure, not a distress signal.

**Capital allocation.** The company pivoted hard to buybacks in 2025, repurchasing $820.8M of stock (versus $370M in 2024) and cutting diluted share count from 120.2M to 111.9M — roughly 7%. A new $1.0B authorization runs to October 2027 ($800M remaining as of Q1 2026). The dividend is token ($0.28/year, ~0.25% yield), and management repeatedly flags it could be reduced or eliminated. The buyback pace exceeded FCF and drew cash down from $1.16B to $920M over the year, supporting EPS but flattering per-share metrics without addressing the organic-growth problem.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the weak point of the thesis and the reason the score sits below the midpoint. The end-market backdrop is the driver: post-COVID normalization, a biopharma capex downturn, and soft academic/government funding. Revvity's risk factors were updated to note that "recently announced and proposed changes in U.S. funding and regulations have created a more cautious spending environment" — a direct reference to NIH/academic funding pressure and pharma/biotech caution that hits instrument and consumable demand.

The bright spots are narrow but real. Reproductive Health grew ~$20.6M in Q1 2026 and is the most durable franchise, driven by reimbursement rather than discretionary capex. Software/informatics (Signals suite, bolstered by the $72M ACD/Labs acquisition in Q1 2026) is growing double-digit with recurring SaaS characteristics. These are the two areas where the growth mix is improving, but they are not yet large enough to lift the consolidated organic rate out of the low single digits.

Estimate revisions confirm the muted picture: current-year EPS consensus of $5.26 is down 2.6% over 90 days, next-year $5.84 down 2.8%, and the current quarter down 5.3%. This is the opposite of the +30% revision velocity that would flag an acceleration — estimates are drifting lower. The FY2026 revenue guidance below Street, set against the Q1 beat, signals management sees continued caution. Two developments could shrink reported revenue further near term: the announced China Immunodiagnostics divestiture, and ongoing China testing-policy pressure.

We do not see the estimate-revision or revenue-acceleration signals that would justify a growth-inflection call. Applying weight to growth prospects — the single most reliable winner-selection dimension in our framework — the read here is clearly below average, and we score it accordingly.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation is the crux of the HOLD. At $110.15, RVTY trades at ~21x the current-year adjusted EPS estimate and ~18.9x the $5.84 next-year estimate. On a trailing GAAP basis the P/E is 53x, but that figure is distorted by amortization and one-time items and is not the right lens.

Against peers, the read is genuinely mixed. The Health Care Equipment peer median P/E is 73.4x, skewed by high-multiple names (Masimo 126.7x, Penumbra 73.4x) and negative/low-earnings names (Baxter, GMED at 18.8x). RVTY at ~21x forward is not expensive relative to that distorted median, but relative to its own low-single-digit organic growth it is full. The right frame is growth-adjusted: paying ~19x forward for a business growing organic revenue ~2% and with estimates drifting down is not a bargain, and it is not the kind of setup where analyst targets act as a floor. Consensus targets (mean $117, median $122, high $145, low $90) sit modestly above the current price, but with estimates falling rather than rising, those targets reflect a business that has already re-rated toward fair value.

The recent move has been sentiment-driven. The stock is up 15% over three months and sits near its 52-week high on sector-M&A momentum, not on fundamental improvement, with estimates falling over that same window. This is a business whose multiple has expanded faster than its fundamentals, leaving no margin of safety. There is modest unmodeled upside from a potential IEEPA tariff refund (the Feb 2026 Supreme Court ruling could reverse ~$25M/year of tariff drag, though no receivable is booked), but that is small optionality, not a re-rating catalyst.

We treat "cheap on peer median P/E" with appropriate skepticism here: the peer median is not a reliable anchor, and there is no identified catalyst for the market to be wrong to the upside. The valuation score reflects a full price with limited asymmetric upside and a specific downside risk (goodwill impairment) that the multiple does not discount.

## Competitive Landscape

Revvity competes in life-science tools and diagnostics alongside far larger diversified players (Thermo Fisher, Danaher) and specialized firms (Bio-Rad, Bio-Techne, Qiagen). Its clearest moat is in newborn/reproductive-health screening, where it holds leading global positions in a reimbursement-driven, less-cyclical market. Its second differentiator is a growing software/informatics franchise (Signals, recurring SaaS), reinforced by the ACD/Labs tuck-in.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-eroding rather than improving. Management explicitly flags increasing competitor consolidation, which favors scaled players, and Revvity, at ~$2.9B revenue, is subscale versus Thermo and Danaher. The Life Sciences Solutions reagent/instrument franchise, the most exposed to the pharma/academic downturn, is also where the thinnest goodwill cushion sits, indicating the market and the company both recognize that unit's competitive/cyclical vulnerability. Diagnostics margin erosion from China policy changes points to pricing pressure in that geography. The moat in reproductive health and software is real and durable, but there is no evidence of share gains or moat-widening that would lift this dimension above neutral.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill impairment in Life Sciences Solutions — the most concrete red flag.** At the November 3, 2025 annual test, the Life Sciences Solutions reporting unit ($4.5B goodwill) had fair value exceeding carrying value by only 10–20% — the only unit not "substantially" above carrying value. This is the very unit facing the weakest organic growth and margin pressure. A modest downgrade in growth or discount-rate assumptions at the next annual test (early November 2026) could trigger a material non-cash impairment. This is quantified, specific, and not fully priced.

**China exposure and the announced Immunodiagnostics divestiture.** The Q1 2026 10-Q added a new risk-factor clause disclosing the decision to divest the China Immunodiagnostics business. China testing-policy changes (volume-based procurement/reimbursement) already pressured Diagnostics margins and created collection drags in 2025. The divestiture is strategically sensible but adds execution risk and could shrink reported revenue; no counterparty or price has been disclosed.

**End-market demand softness.** NIH/academic funding cuts and biopharma capex caution directly hit instrument and consumable demand — the structural cause of the 1.9% organic rate.

**Refinancing and FX.** The €500M July 2026 note is well-covered by cash/revolver. The ~60% foreign-currency exposure creates translation volatility (AOCI swung ~$31M negative in Q1 2026). Neither is acute.

**Tariff optionality (mildly positive).** The Feb 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling could reverse ~$25M/year of tariff drag via CBP refunds. Revvity has conservatively booked no receivable — unmodeled upside.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV is 45.6% versus 39.4% 1-year historical vol, a rich 6.2-point spread, and IV has risen 7.2 points since mid-June as the stock climbed 10.5%. The term structure is in backwardation (45.6% near-term vs 41.9% at six months), which signals the market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with an upcoming earnings catalyst and elevated uncertainty at these price levels. Put/call skew is balanced (0.0%) and put/call volume (0.43) and OI (0.35) ratios are call-tilted, so there is no pronounced downside hedging demand. Short interest at 10.3% of float (4.9 days to cover) reflects meaningful bearish institutional positioning, consistent with the flat-growth thesis, but is not at squeeze-risk levels. The options market is pricing a wide move (±11.6% on the nearest expiry) into the next print but is not directionally bearish, corroborating our two-sided, catalyst-dependent HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($130–$145).** Reproductive Health and software continue to grow double-digit and lift the organic mix toward mid-single digits; the China divestiture removes a margin/collections drag and cleans up the story; tariff refunds materialize as a ~$25M tailwind; sector M&A momentum keeps the multiple elevated; and the 7%/year buyback compounds EPS. On ~$6.20 normalized adjusted EPS at ~22x, the stock reaches the mid-$130s, with the high analyst target of $145 in play if a re-rating on a growth-mix improvement takes hold.

**Bear Case ($80–$90).** Organic growth stays sub-2% or turns negative amid continued academic/biopharma funding cuts; the Life Sciences Solutions unit takes a goodwill impairment at the November test; margins compress further on China and tariff pressure; and sentiment-driven multiple support fades, de-rating the stock back toward the low analyst target of $90 or the 52-week low of $81. This is roughly 18–27% downside from current levels.

The distance between the cases is wide and neither is clearly more probable than the other in the next 12 months. The recurring franchises and cash generation cap the downside; the flat growth and full multiple cap the upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This is a genuinely two-sided setup near 52-week highs with no near-term catalyst in either direction and estimates drifting lower. We do not have the evidence for a confident BUY (no growth inflection, no insider buying, full multiple) or a confident AVOID (durable franchises, strong FCF, no distress).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic revenue growth re-accelerates above 4% for two consecutive quarters, or FY2026 organic guidance is raised.
- The stock de-rates to ~16x forward (~$90–$95) on unchanged fundamentals, restoring a margin of safety.
- Clustered open-market insider buying by C-suite executives, or the China divestiture closes at a value-accretive multiple that visibly improves segment margins.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill impairment in Life Sciences Solutions at the November 2026 annual test, signaling structural deterioration in the largest reporting unit.
- Organic growth turns negative, or FY2026 guidance is cut further below the current ~$2.83B midpoint.
- A dividend elimination combined with a buyback slowdown, indicating capital-allocation stress.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the options market is pricing a ±11.6% move), and specifically the early-November 2026 annual goodwill test, which is the single most important dated risk on the calendar.

## Conclusion

Revvity is a high-quality, cash-generative franchise with a durable moat in reproductive-health screening and a growing software business, but the investment case is held back by flat organic growth (1.9% in 2025), compressing margins, and a full valuation (~19–21x forward) that has re-rated on sector sentiment rather than fundamental improvement. Estimates are drifting down, not up; there is no insider buying; TSR has lagged peers for multiple years; and a specific, quantified goodwill-impairment risk sits in the largest and most cyclically exposed reporting unit. The recurring revenue base and strong free cash flow limit downside, and tariff-refund and divestiture-cleanup optionality provide some upside, but neither creates asymmetric skew from here.

The score trajectory (5.8 → 5.1 → 4.8 → 5.5) reflects a business whose fundamentals have softened while the stock re-rated: growth prospects have declined and valuation "improved" only in the sense that the market repriced. Our current work confirms the underlying growth deceleration and does not identify a catalyst to break the range in either direction. We maintain HOLD and would look to accumulate only on a de-rating toward the low-$90s or a demonstrated organic-growth re-acceleration.
