**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Clorox is a mature, defensive consumer-staples franchise whose investment case has narrowed to a single durable positive, a ~5.1% dividend yield supported by #1/#2 brand share in most categories, set against an unusually crowded cluster of execution risks. The stock has fallen ~24% from its July 2025 high of $132 to ~$96, and the decline tracks genuine fundamental deterioration: nine-month FY26 organic sales fell 6%, gross margin compressed 200bps to 42.7%, and consensus EPS estimates have been cut across every window (current-quarter -21.6% over 90 days, next-year -10.2%).

Three developments dominate the near-term picture. First, CEO Linda Rendle announced on May 28, 2026 that she is stepping down for health reasons, creating a leadership vacuum at the moment the company is integrating its largest-ever acquisition (GOJO/Purell, closed April 1, 2026) and stabilizing a newly cut-over ERP system. Second, that ~$2.25B acquisition is debt-financed onto a balance sheet with negative Clorox stockholders' equity (-$67M at 3/31/26), pushing total debt toward ~$5B against a ~$7B revenue base; the investment-grade rating is now the linchpin of the credit story. Third, the reported 7% nine-month revenue decline is substantially a mechanical reversal of a Q4-FY25 retailer pre-buy ahead of the ERP cutover, so the headline overstates the underlying softness even as the 6% organic figure confirms real demand pressure from value-seeking consumers.

We move to HOLD (income) from the prior AVOID stance. The case for owning here is narrow and defensive: a well-covered yield, a franchise trading roughly in line with household-products peers (15.5x trailing P/E vs 15.0x peer median), and a director open-market purchase near the lows. The case against upgrading further is falling estimates, a leadership air-pocket, rising leverage, and a five-year TSR (63%) that has badly lagged peers (137%). The dividend anchors the downside; the fundamentals do not yet justify betting on appreciation.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1913 and headquartered in Oakland, California, Clorox is a multinational manufacturer of household and professional cleaning, hygiene, and lifestyle products, generating ~$7.1B in FY2025 net sales across ~25 countries with ~7,600 employees. Over 80% of sales come from brands holding #1 or #2 share in their categories, a moat rooted in brand equity and retail shelf presence.

The business runs across four reportable segments:
- **Health & Wellness (~38%):** Clorox bleach, Pine-Sol, Liquid-Plumr, Formula 409, plus the CloroxPro/Clorox Healthcare professional lines. The highest-margin segment and the strategic core.
- **Household (~27-29%):** Glad bags/wraps, Fresh Step/Scoop Away cat litter, Kingsford charcoal (seasonal, Mar–Sep).
- **Lifestyle (~16-18%):** Hidden Valley, Brita, Burt's Bees.
- **International (~15-17%):** the ex-U.S. franchise.

Roughly 86% of sales are U.S.-based, in mature, high-penetration categories where growth requires innovation, pricing, and share gains rather than category expansion. Management's "IGNITE" strategy centers on portfolio pruning (Argentina divested Mar 2024, Better Health VMS divested Sep 2024), a ~$580M multi-year ERP/digital overhaul now completed, cost savings, and the recent doubling-down on health and hygiene via the GOJO/Purell acquisition.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue has been flat for four years**: ~$7.1B in FY22, $7.4B in FY23, and ~$7.1B again in FY25. This is a no-growth top line by design; the value creation, when it happens, comes from margin and capital return rather than volume expansion.

**FY2025 profitability recovery, heavily distorted by one-timers.** Net income jumped to $810M (FY25) from $280M (FY24) and $149M (FY23), and diluted EPS rose ~190% year over year. The magnitude is misleading: prior-year earnings were depressed by the Argentina divestiture loss and a pension settlement charge, while FY25 benefited from cyberattack insurance recoveries (now lapping and fading, a headwind to "other income" comparisons going forward) but absorbed the VMS divestiture loss. Gross margin recovered to ~45% as commodity/inflation pressures eased and cost savings landed; net margin was ~11.6%.

**Q3/9M FY2026 shows renewed softness.** Nine-month net sales fell 7% to $4,772M (from $5,116M), with organic sales down 6%. The reported decline is largely a mechanical reversal of the Q4-FY25 retailer pre-buy ahead of the ERP cutover (retailers stocked up, then drew down), and Q3 alone was roughly flat. The 6% organic figure is the truer read: real, but not a collapse. Nine-month gross margin compressed 200bps to 42.7% on higher manufacturing/logistics costs and lower volume. Segment EBIT weakness was broad on a nine-month basis (Health & Wellness -21%, Household -27%, Lifestyle -13%), with International the lone bright spot (+5%, aided by FX and Asia). Nine-month diluted EPS was $3.47, down 10%.

**The balance sheet requires careful reading.** The Altman Z-Score of 1.33 ("distress zone") is a false alarm; it is driven by a structurally tiny equity base (only $321M at 6/30/25, and negative $67M in Clorox stockholders' equity at 3/31/26), a product of decades of buybacks and dividends rather than operational distress. The D/E of 15.82 and ROE of 256.7% are artifacts of the same thin equity. The legitimate concern is that leverage is genuinely rising: at 3/31/26, long-term debt was $2,487M and notes/loans payable (commercial paper) jumped to $1,591M from $4M, funding the GOJO deal and the $476M P&G Glad buyout. Post-close, the company drew $1,250M from a new Delayed Draw Term facility and plans to term out with long-term debt, pushing total debt toward ~$5B+. The company is in compliance with its 4.0x minimum interest-coverage covenant with ample cushion, and maintains investment-grade ratings, the critical variable, since a downgrade below IG would trigger derivative collateral calls and pressure supply-chain financing.

**Cash flow remains solid beneath the noise.** FY25 operating cash flow was $981M and FCF $761M (~10.7% margin). Nine-month FY26 OCF fell to $282M from $687M, but this is dominated by the one-time $476M P&G Glad termination payment; underlying generation is intact. Dividends of ~$602M were paid in FY25 (raised 2% in July 2025, continuing a long streak), and nine-month FY26 saw $452M in dividends plus $254M in buybacks, continued returns even while levering for GOJO. On the current ~$5.52 FY26 EPS estimate, the ~$4.96 annualized dividend implies a payout near 90% of earnings but comfortably covered by FCF.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth outlook is the weakest link, and estimate revisions confirm it. Consensus has cut the current quarter by 21.6% over 90 days, next quarter by 12.7%, current year by 5.7%, and next year by 10.2%. This is broad-based negative earnings momentum, not a single-quarter timing artifact.

Three offsetting factors matter for the forward view. First, the reported revenue decline overstates the underlying trend: the ERP pre-buy reversal is mechanical, and organic softness reflects a value-seeking consumer trading down to smaller packs and private label, a real but manageable pressure. Second, the completed ERP/digital program means the ~$10M–$26M/quarter of "digital capabilities" adjustments that suppressed reported EPS will roll off, providing a mechanical adjusted-EPS tailwind into FY27. Third, the GOJO/Purell acquisition adds a higher-margin professional-hygiene franchise adjacent to CloroxPro/Clorox Healthcare, with structural tailwinds in institutional hygiene. Purchase-price allocation and pro-forma financials will not be visible until the Q4-FY26 filing, so accretion is unquantified.

Netting these out: the base business is a low-single-digit-at-best organic story facing near-term demand headwinds; the growth thesis depends on transformation-cost rolloff plus GOJO accretion converting to per-share earnings, neither of which is proven yet. Growth Prospects score low.

## Valuation Assessment

Clorox trades at 15.5x trailing EPS ($6.52) and ~17.5x the ~$5.52 FY26 consensus estimate. Against the Household Products peer set, this is roughly market-in-line, not cheap:

| REYN | 15.0 | 3.9% |
| IPAR | 19.0 | 3.3% |
| WDFC | 38.0 | 1.8% |
| CENTA | 13.5 | — |
| ENR | 7.1 | 6.2% |
| **Peer median** | **15.0** | **3.3%** |
| **CLX** | **15.5** | **5.1%** |

On the multiple, CLX sits right at the peer median while offering the second-highest yield in the group. That yield premium is the clearest source of relative value, and it is well-covered by FCF. But the multiple itself is not distinguishing: a 15.5x P/E on an earnings base that consensus is cutting is a fair price for a no-growth franchise with elevated execution risk, not a mispricing.

The equity-lesson discipline here matters: a "cheap-looking" multiple on falling estimates is a value-trap signal, not a buy signal, unless an independent catalyst is identifiable. Clorox does not offer one. The transformation-cost rolloff and GOJO accretion are potential 12-18 month tailwinds, but both are unproven and unquantified, and the CEO vacuum raises the probability of execution slippage. Analyst targets span a wide $77–$155 (mean $104.6, median $97), and the current price sits essentially at the median target, consistent with a stock the sell side views as fairly valued rather than deeply discounted. The 13 holds against 2 buys and 4 sell/strong-sell ratings reinforce a neutral-to-cautious consensus.

We do not treat the analyst target as a floor here, because this is a stable-multiple staple with falling estimates rather than a stock with rising estimates at a reasonable multiple. Valuation scores below neutral, held up only by the yield.

## Competitive Landscape

Clorox's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding. The moat is real (80%+ of sales in #1/#2 brands, deep retail relationships) but under pressure from two directions. Private label and value-seeking consumers are compressing branded pricing power across cleaning, bags/wraps, litter, and food, a sector-wide dynamic that hits Clorox's premium positioning directly. And the company competes against far larger, better-capitalized multinationals (P&G, Church & Dwight, Reckitt, SC Johnson, Colgate) with greater R&D and financial resources, a disadvantage Clorox explicitly acknowledges.

The five-year TSR gap (63% for Clorox versus 137% for its peer group) reflects persistent underperformance driven by a no-growth top line, cyberattack disruption, margin volatility, and heavy transformation spend. Two factors argue against further deterioration in the trajectory. International is growing (+5% nine-month), and the GOJO/Purell acquisition is strategically coherent, repositioning the portfolio toward higher-margin professional hygiene with structural demand tailwinds. But the Walmart concentration is a live and intensifying risk: the retailer rose to ~28% of consolidated sales in Q3-FY26, top-5 customers are roughly half of sales, and there are no long-term contracts. A shelf-space shift toward private label or a pricing demand from Walmart is a direct earnings threat with no contractual cushion.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dense with company-specific, partly binary threats that are not fully priced:

1. **Leadership vacuum at a pivotal moment.** Rendle's health-driven departure coincides with GOJO integration, ERP stabilization, and a mid-transformation portfolio. The June reorg elevating Chris Hyder to COO partially bridges the gap, but no permanent CEO is named. Execution risk is concentrated in this window.

2. **Rising leverage on negative equity, IG rating as linchpin.** The investment-grade rating protects against derivative collateral calls; a downgrade would cascade. The term-out of commercial paper to long-term debt must go smoothly.

3. **Walmart concentration.** No long-term contracts; order-by-order sales. Direct, uncontracted earnings exposure.

4. **ERP stabilization risk.** U.S. cutover completed in Q3-FY26; the system is in its fragile stabilization phase with flagged risks around order processing, invoicing, inventory, and internal controls over financial reporting.

5. **Private-label / value-seeking consumer.** The core secular threat to a premium branded portfolio, already visible in soft Lifestyle and Household volumes.

6. **Commodity/tariff and insurance-recovery lapping.** Resin, sodium hypochlorite, cardboard, soybean oil, and jet fuel exposure, with management flagging tariffs and Middle East conflict as forward margin risks. Fading cyberattack insurance recoveries make year-over-year "other income" comparisons look worse than operating reality.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 38.8% is rich to 1-year historical vol of 29.6% (a 9.2pp spread, 75th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (38.8% near-term vs 30.9% at six months), consistent with an anticipated event: the CEO search resolution, GOJO integration progress, and Q4-FY26 results. The implied one-month move is ±9.1%. Put/call skew is balanced (1.0%) and the put/call volume ratio is a benign 0.57, so positioning is not aggressively bearish, but the P/C OI ratio rose from 0.51 to 0.87 over the last six weeks, indicating growing hedging demand. Short interest at 10.8% of float (4.4 days to cover) is elevated but not extreme. The rich near-term IV argues against chasing here; an income-oriented buyer is better served entering after the near-term event vol crushes.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$115-125):** The ERP transition stabilizes cleanly, the transformation-cost rolloff lifts adjusted EPS into FY27, GOJO/Purell proves accretive to the high-margin health-and-hygiene franchise, gross margin normalizes back toward 45%, and organic sales reaccelerate as the value-seeking-consumer pressure eases with moderating inflation. A permanent CEO is named credibly, removing the governance overhang. On a normalized ~$6.50+ EPS at 18x plus the yield, the stock re-rates toward the mid-$110s to mid-$120s. Director Breber's open-market purchase at ~$85.82 signals at least one insider sees this outcome as underpriced.

**Bear Case (~$77-80):** Organic softness persists as private label takes share, gross margin fails to recover, GOJO integration disappoints or drags on the balance sheet, and the leadership vacuum produces strategic drift. A credit-rating downgrade below IG triggers collateral and financing stress. Estimates continue to fall, and the ~90% dividend payout comes under scrutiny if FCF weakens. The stock retests the analyst-low $77 area, roughly 20% below current levels, though the ~6.4% yield at that price provides meaningful support.

**Base Case (~$97-100):** The most likely outcome is range-bound. Estimates stabilize but do not inflect, the transformation-cost tailwind and GOJO accretion offset ongoing organic pressure, the CEO search resolves without a negative surprise, and the yield holds investors through the transition. The stock earns its dividend and drifts near current levels while the fundamental questions resolve over 12-18 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income).** The thesis rests on the well-covered dividend yield anchoring the downside while multiple company-specific uncertainties resolve, not on expected price appreciation.

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q4-FY26 earnings report (expected early August 2026) is the next natural checkpoint: it will disclose GOJO purchase-price allocation, pro-forma financials, and post-close leverage detail, and will show whether ERP stabilization and margin trends are improving. Because GOJO closed April 1, 2026 (after the Q3-FY26 period ended March 31, 2026), Q4-FY26 is the first reporting quarter to include any Purell contribution. The CEO search resolution is the second key event.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A credible permanent CEO is named AND estimate revisions turn positive (a stabilizing-to-rising next-year consensus after four consecutive negative windows).
- Q4-FY26/Q1-FY27 results show gross margin recovering toward 44-45% with organic sales returning to flat-or-better.
- GOJO/Purell demonstrably accretive with the commercial paper successfully termed out and the IG rating affirmed post-close.
- A pullback toward the high-$70s/low-$80s where the yield exceeds 6% and the risk/reward turns clearly asymmetric.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A credit-rating downgrade below investment grade, or covenant/interest-coverage cushion narrowing materially.
- Evidence of a dividend cut or coverage deterioration (FCF falling below the ~$600M dividend obligation on a sustained basis).
- Walmart shelf-space or pricing action that materially impairs the top line, or ERP stabilization failures disrupting order/invoice processing.
- Continued acceleration of negative estimate revisions alongside organic sales worsening below -6%.

## Conclusion

Clorox has moved from an AVOID to an income HOLD, reflecting a genuine change in risk/reward rather than a change in the underlying business. The ~24% drawdown has repriced the stock to roughly peer-median multiples while pushing the dividend yield to ~5.1%, well-covered by free cash flow and backed by a franchise where 80%+ of sales carry #1/#2 share. That yield, plus a director's open-market purchase near the lows, forms the defensive floor.

The reasons not to upgrade are equally concrete: estimates are being cut across every window, the top line is organically soft, leverage is rising onto a negative-equity balance sheet with the investment-grade rating as the critical dependency, and the CEO vacuum arrives mid-integration of the largest acquisition in company history and mid-stabilization of a freshly cut-over ERP. The "cheap" multiple on falling estimates is a value-trap warning, not a buy signal, absent a proven catalyst. The transformation-cost rolloff and GOJO accretion are plausible 12-18 month tailwinds, but both are unquantified and hostage to execution.

We hold for the yield and the option on a clean CEO transition plus margin normalization, while waiting for estimate stabilization and Q4-FY26 clarity before committing to appreciation. Overall score: 4.35/10.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the stated drawdown from the July 2025 high was overstated (~42% vs the correct ~24-27% depending on reference low), which somewhat inflated the "repriced/defensive floor" framing underpinning the HOLD-for-yield case.
