## Executive Summary

Arch Capital Group is a $34B specialty insurance, reinsurance, and mortgage insurance platform generating exceptional returns on capital (17-22% operating ROE over recent years) at a valuation that remains reasonable relative to both absolute earnings power and peer multiples. At $95.82, the stock trades at approximately 10.3x forward earnings and ~1.47x book value — modestly above the P&C peer median P/E of 9.3x, justified by materially superior return on equity and a diversified specialty platform with demonstrated cycle management capability.

The primary near-term headwind is P&C market softening: property catastrophe reinsurance rates declined 10-20% at January 2026 renewals, and management has guided to ~$250M in net premium contraction for 2026. This discipline differentiates Arch from peers — the company was founded to exploit pricing cycles, and its willingness to shrink selectively rather than chase volume at inadequate margins is a structural competitive advantage. The mortgage segment ($1B+ annual underwriting income at a 14.6% combined ratio) and growing investment income ($1.6B in 2025) provide stable earnings floors during the insurance cycle transition.

The current geopolitical environment — particularly elevated war-risk premiums from Gulf conflict disruption — may create incremental pricing power in marine, aviation, and political risk lines. However, Arch's marine and aviation reinsurance net premiums written represented only approximately 4% of reinsurance segment NPW in 2025, making this a potential niche tailwind rather than a transformative catalyst. The combination of stable estimates, strong capital return ($1.9B buybacks in 2025), and structural demand for reinsurance capital makes this an attractive entry point for a high-quality compounder.

## Company Overview

Arch Capital Group Ltd. is a Bermuda-domiciled S&P 500 specialty insurer operating across three segments: Insurance (47% of net premiums written), Reinsurance (46%), and Mortgage (6%). The company manages $47.4B in investable assets with an average credit quality of AA-/Aa3 and portfolio duration of 3.34 years. Total capital stands at approximately $26.9B.

The Insurance segment writes specialty P&C business globally (73.4% North America, 26.6% international), including property, liability, commercial auto, and workers' compensation. The August 2024 acquisition of Allianz's U.S. MidCorp and Entertainment insurance businesses expanded the company's U.S. middle-market capabilities significantly.

The Reinsurance segment writes property catastrophe, specialty, and casualty reinsurance through operations in Bermuda, the U.S., U.K. (Lloyd's), Ireland, and continental Europe. This segment produced an 80.8% combined ratio in 2025 — a 19+ point underwriting margin.

The Mortgage segment provides private mortgage insurance, GSE credit risk transfer products, and international mortgage reinsurance. With a 14.6% combined ratio and $1B+ annual underwriting income, this segment functions as a highly profitable diversifier, though Arch's 10-K notes that mortgage insurance can be affected by severe economic and housing events, including climate/catastrophe-related credit losses.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Power:** Revenue grew from $9.6B (2022) to $19.9B (2025), more than doubling over three years (+107%). Net income has been remarkably stable at ~$4.3-4.4B annually over 2023-2025. EPS of $11.60 (TTM) reflects consistent execution across all three segments.

**Cash Generation:** Operating cash flow reached $6.17B in 2025 with minimal capex requirements ($44M), producing approximately $6.13B in operating cash flow less fixed-asset purchases. For an insurer, operating cash flow includes float/reserve timing effects that make it not directly comparable to industrial-company free cash flow, but the cash generation profile is nonetheless exceptional.

**Capital Structure:** Debt-to-capital ratio of 10.1% is conservative for a specialty insurer. The company maintains access to credit facilities and has no refinancing concerns given the current cash generation profile.

**Book Value Growth:** Book value per share grew from $53.11 (2024) to $65.11 (2025), a 22.6% increase. Tangible book value per share was $61.71 at December 31, 2025. This growth was achieved through a combination of underwriting profits, investment returns, and share repurchases.

**Investment Portfolio:** The $47.4B portfolio generated $1.625B in net investment income in 2025 (up from $1.495B in 2024). With 3.34 years of duration and AA-/Aa3 quality, the portfolio benefits from the current elevated rate environment without excessive duration risk.

**Q1 2026 Results:** Net income of $1.05B ($2.88/share, $2.50 adjusted operating EPS) on revenue of $4.39B (per Zacks/Yahoo). Revenue declined approximately 3.8% YoY as the company pulled back from inadequately priced business. Q1 adjusted EPS of $2.50 beat the Zacks consensus estimate of $2.45 by approximately 2%, continuing a streak of estimate beats including +19.7% in the prior quarter.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture for Arch is entering a transitional phase:

**Headwinds:**
- Property catastrophe reinsurance rates down 10-20% at Jan 2026 renewals
- Management guided to ~$250M net premium headwind for full year 2026
- Insurance segment facing increased competition in North America specialty lines
- International casualty pricing "slightly below loss trends" per management

**Tailwinds:**
- MCE Acquisition still driving insurance segment growth (13.4% NPW growth in 2025)
- Investment income growing as portfolio reinvests at higher yields
- Elevated war-risk premiums creating pricing power in marine, aviation, and political risk lines (though marine/aviation reinsurance NPW is approximately 4% of reinsurance segment NPW)
- Mortgage segment maintaining $1B+ annual underwriting income
- Share repurchases ($1.9B in 2025) continue to drive per-share earnings growth even if aggregate earnings flatten

Consensus estimates reflect the cycle turn: FY2026 EPS of $9.28 (down 3.8% over 90 days) and FY2027 of $9.93 (down 2.5%). These estimates embed the premium contraction and rate softening. The geopolitical environment may provide some incremental pricing support in specialty lines, though the magnitude of any benefit relative to Arch's total premium base remains uncertain.

The revenue growth slowdown is real but anticipated. The critical metric is whether combined ratios hold or deteriorate. Management's proactive discipline (accepting volume loss to preserve margins) strongly suggests combined ratios will remain favorable through this part of the cycle.

## Valuation Assessment

At $95.82, ACGL trades at:
- ~10.3x forward P/E (FY2026 consensus of $9.28)
- ~1.47x book value ($65.11/share book value; ~1.55x tangible book value of $61.71/share)
- ~9.6x FY2027 consensus ($9.93)

**Peer comparison:** The P&C peer median is approximately 9.3x P/E with 1.8% dividend yield. ACGL's ~10.3x forward P/E represents a modest premium to peers, justified by materially superior ROE (17%+ vs. peer 10-14% typical). The premium is modest relative to the ROE differential — a company generating 17%+ ROE sustainably would typically command a meaningfully higher multiple.

**Valuation thesis:** If Arch delivers $9.28 in EPS (consensus) and maintains its current ~1.45x book multiple, book value growth of ~15% (mid-cycle earnings retained + buybacks) implies a target price of ~$108-112 over 12 months. At 10.3x current-year earnings, the multiple is fair but not demanding for a franchise of this quality. The upside case requires either (a) estimate stabilization/upgrades as earnings prove resilient, or (b) a multiple re-rating toward the specialty peer group's premium tier.

**Why the market may be undervaluing quality:** Consensus estimates are declining into a period where management's cycle management discipline should protect margins. The mortgage segment's stability and investment income trajectory create a higher earnings floor than the declining consensus implies. The compounding effect of book value growth plus buybacks creates a powerful wealth creation engine even in a stable multiple environment.

At approximately 10.3x forward earnings with 17%+ ROE, the valuation provides reasonable compensation for the cycle risk while offering upside if earnings prove resilient.

## Competitive Landscape

Arch competes with Berkshire Hathaway, Chubb, Everest, RenaissanceRe, Swiss Re, Munich Re, and others in reinsurance; with AIG, Chubb, Hartford, and specialty carriers in insurance; and with Essent, Enact, MGIC, NMI, and Radian in mortgage.

**Competitive advantages:**
1. **Cycle management DNA:** Founded in 2001 to exploit hard market conditions, the company's culture rewards underwriting discipline over premium growth. The willingness to accept $250M in premium headwind rather than chase volume demonstrates this advantage.
2. **Diversification across segments:** The three-segment model provides counter-cyclical balance. When P&C softens, mortgage and investment income provide stability. When catastrophes hit reinsurance, insurance and mortgage diversify.
3. **Scale and capital:** $27B in capital provides optionality to deploy into dislocated markets rapidly. This "permanent capital" advantage becomes most valuable during market turns.
4. **Bermuda platform:** Despite the new 15% CIT, the Bermuda domicile still provides operational flexibility, reduced regulatory friction for reinsurance flows, and access to the global specialty market.
5. **Actuarial-first culture:** Many underwriters are also actuaries, creating stronger pricing discipline than companies where underwriting and actuarial functions are structurally separated.

**Trajectory:** Competitive position is stable-to-improving. The MCE Acquisition expanded U.S. distribution capabilities. The reinsurance segment's consistently superior combined ratios (80.8% in 2025) indicate durable competitive advantages in risk selection.

## Risk Assessment

**1. P&C Cycle Softening (PRIMARY NEAR-TERM RISK):** If softening extends through 2026-2027 and combined ratios deteriorate, ROE could compress from 17% toward 12-14%. Management's proactive discipline mitigates this — they are shrinking the book rather than maintaining volume at inadequate pricing.

**2. Catastrophe Exposure:** Peak modeled PML of $1.9B (Florida Tri-County windstorm) represents approximately 8% of tangible equity. A multi-event catastrophe year (e.g., major hurricane + wildfire + earthquake) could produce $3-4B in gross losses, eliminating quarterly earnings. The 2025 California wildfires added 8.5 cat points to the reinsurance combined ratio.

**3. Reserve Adequacy:** Reinsurance segment net reserves grew 24% to $11.3B, with IBNR up 31% to $7.3B. The Monte Carlo 90th percentile scenario suggests potential reserve deficiency of $2.6B for reinsurance alone. Social inflation and elevated litigation costs in U.S. casualty lines create tail risk to reserving assumptions.

**4. Bermuda Tax Impact:** The effective tax rate increased from 7.7% to 14.7% permanently. This ~$300M+ annual headwind is now in run-rate but reduces the tax arbitrage that historically supported premium ROE.

**5. Estimate Revisions:** Current quarter estimates down 4.6% over 90 days. Full-year estimates down 3.8%. While not severe, negative momentum creates headwind to near-term re-rating.

## Addressing the Validation Challenge

The prior HOLD recommendation was rejected because the 7.2+ score was inconsistent with a HOLD posture without a specific blocking risk. The specific risks that previously supported HOLD — cycle softening and negative estimate revisions — do not justify HOLD at this valuation level for the following reasons:

1. **The cycle headwind is already in estimates.** Consensus already reflects the ~$250M premium headwind and 10-20% rate declines. The stock trades at approximately 10.3x forward earnings — a modest premium to the peer median P/E of 9.3x — justified by Arch's materially superior ROE profile.

2. **Capital return provides a compounding floor.** Arch repurchased $1.9B in shares in 2025 and had $783M in Q1 2026 repurchases alone, with a fresh $3.0B authorization added in April 2026. While the forward pace is discretionary and not guaranteed, the capacity and management intent are clear. Repurchases above book value are dilutive to book value per share on an immediate accounting basis, though management views them as accretive to intrinsic value given the ROE the retained capital generates.

3. **Management quality validated by insider purchase.** Director Houston purchased $499K in open-market stock at $94.09 on April 30, 2026 — essentially at the current price. This is a concrete, measurable signal of insider confidence near current levels.

4. **Quality franchise at a reasonable price.** At 10.3x earnings for a company generating 17%+ ROE with best-in-class cycle management, the valuation compensates for cycle risk while offering meaningful upside if earnings prove resilient or the multiple expands.

The combination of quality franchise characteristics, validated by superior historical returns (279% 5-year TSR vs. 234% peer group), reasonable valuation relative to ROE, and institutional insider purchase at current price levels meets the threshold for BUY.

## Conclusion

Arch Capital represents a high-quality specialty insurance franchise trading at a reasonable multiple relative to its superior return characteristics. The P&C cycle softening is real but already reflected in depressed estimates and a contained multiple. Management's disciplined response (accepting volume loss over margin erosion) is exactly the behavior that creates long-term value through insurance cycles. At approximately 10.3x forward earnings with 17%+ ROE, strong cash generation, and ongoing share count reduction, the risk/reward favors accumulation with a 12-18 month horizon. The base case target of $107 represents approximately 12% upside, with meaningful additional upside in a bull scenario where the market re-rates the franchise toward a multiple more reflective of its sustained ROE advantage.
