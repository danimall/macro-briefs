## Executive Summary

Chemed Corporation operates two leading U.S. businesses: VITAS Healthcare (the nation's largest hospice provider, ~65% of revenue) and Roto-Rooter (the nation's largest plumbing/drain service provider, ~35%). Both hold dominant positions in fragmented markets, giving CHE a combination of demographic-driven healthcare demand and recession-resilient home services revenue. The stock trades at ~21x trailing EPS and ~16x FY2026 consensus, a meaningful discount to its five-year average, after declining 34% from its 52-week high of $602.

The decline has been earned. Chemed missed earnings estimates in three of the last four quarters, including a -10.1% miss in Q4 2025 that triggered a 15% single-day drop. Operating margins compressed 170bps to 13.4% in FY2025, net income fell 12% despite 4% revenue growth, and operating cash flow declined. Management guided 2026 as a "transition year" with 55% of earnings back-half weighted, below-consensus EPS guidance of $23.25-$24.25, and a VITAS revenue range so wide (5.5-16.5% growth) that it signals genuine uncertainty about the trajectory. A 319,000-record cybersecurity breach at VITAS, newly disclosed tariff exposure, and persistent Medicare reimbursement constraints add risk layers that didn't exist a year ago.

At $385, the valuation is beginning to reflect these problems, but we do not yet have evidence that the earnings decline has bottomed. Q1 2026 results (expected April 23) will be the first test. Until execution data confirms stabilization, the risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling.

## Company Overview

Chemed Corporation is a Cincinnati-based holding company managing two wholly owned subsidiaries with minimal operational overlap. Corporate functions are limited to capital allocation, financial reporting, legal, and executive selection.

**VITAS Healthcare** provides end-of-life hospice and palliative care across 15+ states, caring for over 22,000 patients daily. Revenue is almost entirely Medicare/Medicaid per diem reimbursement (>95%), making VITAS a price-taker whose margins are structurally tied to the spread between labor/supply costs and government-set reimbursement rates. Certificates of Need in key states (Florida, California) provide meaningful barriers to entry. VITAS completed the $85M acquisition of Covenant Care's hospice operations in 2024, expanding geographic reach.

**Roto-Rooter** is the largest U.S. provider of commercial and residential plumbing, drain cleaning, excavation, and water restoration services. The brand has been in operation since 1935 and is among the most recognized service marks in the country. The company operates through both company-owned locations and independent franchisees. Competition is highly fragmented, though private equity has increasingly entered home services.

The two-business portfolio provides natural diversification: VITAS is counter-cyclical (hospice demand is demographically driven, insensitive to economic conditions) while Roto-Rooter has both emergency (inelastic) and discretionary (cyclical) demand components.

The 2025 results mark a clear deterioration. Revenue growth decelerated from 7.4% to 4.1%, while operating income fell 7.7% and net income fell 12.2%. The margin compression was broad-based: gross margin contracted 181bps YoY in Q4 to 34.8%, and adjusted operating margin fell 202bps to 18.3%.

At the segment level, VITAS Q4 revenue grew only 1.9% YoY ($418.8M) on 1.3% days-of-care growth and a 2.2% Medicare rate increase, but margins were squeezed by acuity mix shift and Medicare Cap adjustments. Roto-Rooter Q4 revenue declined 3.7% YoY ($220.6M), with residential revenue down 3.1% and water restoration plunging 10-20% depending on commercial vs. residential. The declining "natural leads" trend — customers finding Roto-Rooter organically — continues, with the company paying more for digital leads to offset, raising customer acquisition costs.

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet remains fundamentally sound. Debt-to-equity is 0.57x with zero long-term debt at year-end 2025. The Altman Z-Score of 4.33 is well within the safe zone. Return on equity is 27.1% and return on assets is 17.2%.

However, cash has declined sharply: $264M (2023) → $178M (2024) → $74.5M (2025). The current ratio has compressed from 1.61 to 1.05. An April 2026 8-K filing indicates Chemed entered into a new material financial obligation — likely a credit facility — which makes sense given the cash drawdown and the company's continued share repurchase activity ($174.6M in FY2025 at ~$436/share average).

Goodwill of $667M represents approximately 43% of total equity, reflecting cumulative acquisition history including the Covenant Care deal.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow in FY2025 was reported at $388.3M for the full year (per management commentary), down from $417.5M in FY2024. Capital expenditures were $62.8M. The XBRL data shows only $32.7M in operating cash flow, which may reflect a Q4 snapshot or timing differences in working capital. Regardless, the trajectory is downward, and with aggressive buybacks consuming $174.6M and dividends adding another ~$25M, capital returns exceeded reported annual FCF on a gross basis if we use the XBRL figure. Taking the full-year management-reported OCF of $388M and subtracting $63M CapEx yields approximately $325M in FCF — a healthier picture but still representing a ~15% decline from the prior year.

## Growth Analysis

**VITAS long-term tailwinds remain intact.** The aging U.S. population is a structural demand driver — Baby Boomers are entering the 75+ age cohort where hospice utilization rates are highest. VITAS' scale allows more efficient compliance, training, and geographic expansion than most of its ~5,000+ competitors.

**Short-term headwinds are significant.** VITAS' wide 2026 revenue guidance range implies management cannot yet forecast demand with reasonable precision. The Medicare per diem rate increase for FY2026 is 2.6%, which will trail labor cost inflation in many markets. The Florida Medicare Cap risk — where per-admission caps increase at national average rates while VITAS' Florida costs rise at local rates — creates a structural revenue clawback risk.

**Roto-Rooter is under pressure.** Residential demand is declining, water restoration has collapsed, and the shift from organic to paid customer acquisition raises structural cost questions. PE-backed competitors and AI-driven lead generation platforms are eroding brand-based moats. Management described Q4 results as "below expectations" for Roto-Rooter. With consumer spending under pressure from elevated energy costs and weak sentiment, Roto-Rooter's non-emergency discretionary services face continued headwinds.

The combined picture is mid-single-digit revenue growth driven primarily by VITAS demographics and Medicare rate increases, with Roto-Rooter contributing zero-to-negative growth near-term. FY2026 consensus EPS of $23.88 implies roughly flat earnings vs. FY2025's approximately $21.50 GAAP ($18.34 trailing reported in the data). FY2027 consensus of $26.62 implies a return to low-teens earnings growth, which requires Roto-Rooter stabilization and VITAS margin recovery — neither of which is yet visible in the data.

## Valuation Assessment

At $385, CHE trades at:
- ~21x trailing EPS ($18.34)
- ~16.1x FY2026E EPS ($23.88)
- ~14.5x FY2027E EPS ($26.62)
- ~$5.2B market capitalization

The sell-side consensus price target is $443 (mean), with a $400-$500 range. The stock currently trades below the low end of the analyst target range, which is unusual and reflects either the post-earnings selloff not yet being fully incorporated into formal targets or genuine disagreement about fair value.

Historically, CHE has traded at 22-28x forward earnings. At 16x FY2026E, the stock is at a meaningful discount to its own history. Two interpretations exist: (1) the market is overreacting and the valuation discount creates opportunity, or (2) the market is appropriately repricing for structurally lower margins, deteriorating Roto-Rooter competitive position, and increased regulatory/reimbursement risk.

Given three consecutive misses and falling estimates, we lean toward interpretation (2) being partially correct — a 22-25x multiple is warranted only if earnings growth resumes and margins stabilize. If FY2026 EPS lands at the midpoint of management guidance ($23.75) and the stock re-rates to 20x, that implies ~$475. If estimates continue to fall and FY2026 EPS comes in at $22, 18x yields ~$396 — roughly the current price.

The stock appears fairly valued for a scenario where 2026 is indeed a transition year with back-end-loaded improvement. It is cheap if management executes; it has downside if the transition takes longer.

## Competitive Landscape

**VITAS** holds the #1 position in U.S. hospice by census. Scale advantages in compliance infrastructure, clinical training, and G&A leverage are meaningful in a market dominated by small community-based providers. Certificate of Need protections in Florida and California provide direct barriers to new entrants. The 2017 DOJ settlement's Corporate Integrity Agreement concluded in June 2023, removing a compliance overhang.

Competitive threats include: (1) health systems building in-house hospice programs, (2) national/regional consolidators (e.g., Amedisys, now part of UnitedHealth/Optum), and (3) not-for-profit hospices that don't face the same margin pressure from Medicare constraints.

**Roto-Rooter** has the strongest brand in U.S. plumbing services and operates the largest national footprint. However, brand recognition is becoming less durable as customer acquisition shifts to digital channels. Private equity-backed roll-ups (e.g., Neighborly) are building competing national platforms. The moat is narrowing, and the company's defensive response — increasing paid digital leads — directly pressures margins.

## Risk Assessment

### Regulatory/Reimbursement Risk (High)
VITAS derives >95% of revenue from Medicare/Medicaid with zero pricing power. If the Medicare Hospital Market Basket Index underestimates actual cost inflation, margins compress mechanically. The 2% sequester, scheduled through FY2027, is an ongoing drag. The Florida Medicare Cap creates additional per-admission revenue clawback risk. CMS's Special Focus Program for hospices, paused in February 2025, could resume and subject VITAS programs to additional scrutiny.

### Cybersecurity Liability (Moderate-High)
The October 2025 breach compromised 319,177 patient records including SSNs, medical diagnoses, and treatment details. Class-action litigation is probable. State AG investigations in California and Texas are underway. Financial impact remains unquantified but could include litigation settlements, regulatory fines, increased cyber-insurance premiums, and referral relationship damage.

### Tariff Exposure (Moderate — New)
Newly disclosed in the 10-K. Steel tariffs affect Roto-Rooter equipment costs. Pharmaceutical and vehicle tariffs affect both segments. VITAS cannot pass through tariff-related cost increases given fixed per diem reimbursement. Roto-Rooter's ability to pass through depends on competitive dynamics.

### Consumer Spending Weakness (Moderate)
Roto-Rooter's discretionary services (non-emergency plumbing, water restoration) are sensitive to consumer spending. Record-low consumer sentiment, elevated fuel costs, and declining residential demand all point to continued pressure on Roto-Rooter's top line.

### Concentration Risk (Moderate)
VITAS' dependence on a single payer (Medicare) and significant geographic concentration in Florida create non-diversifiable regulatory risk.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$475-$500; +23% to +30%)
- 2026 proves to be a genuine transition year, with VITAS census growth accelerating above 4% and margin recovery materializing in H2
- Roto-Rooter stabilizes as water restoration demand mean-reverts and paid lead economics prove sustainable
- FY2026 EPS reaches the upper end of guidance ($24.25) or exceeds it, restoring credibility
- Multiple re-rates from 16x to 20x forward earnings as the miss streak breaks
- Demographic tailwinds for hospice are structural over a 5-10 year horizon
- New credit facility enables accretive acquisitions in fragmented hospice market

### Bear Case (~$320-$340; -12% to -17%)
- Q1 2026 represents a fourth consecutive miss, confirming that management cannot forecast reliably
- Medicare reimbursement rate increases (2.6%) prove insufficient against labor and tariff-driven cost inflation, compressing VITAS margins further
- Roto-Rooter residential demand continues declining as consumer spending weakens
- Cybersecurity litigation materializes with $50M+ in settlements/costs
- FY2026 EPS comes in below guidance (~$22), estimates get cut again, and multiple compresses to 15x — yielding $330
- Cash depletion forces drawdown on the new credit facility, limiting buyback capacity

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. **Q1 2026 earnings beat consensus** ($5.30 EPS) by any margin, breaking the three-miss streak. This would warrant upgrading, particularly if VITAS margin commentary improves.
2. **Roto-Rooter revenue returns to positive YoY growth** for two consecutive quarters, indicating the competitive/demand headwinds are stabilizing.
3. **Stock declines below $340** (14.5x FY2026E) without fundamental deterioration beyond what's already priced, creating a valuation cushion sufficient to offset uncertainty.

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. **Q1 2026 EPS misses by >5%** alongside a further guidance cut, indicating the transition year is failing.
2. **FY2026 EPS estimate falls below $22**, implying the consensus has lost confidence in the recovery trajectory.
3. **Cybersecurity-related litigation or regulatory action with quantified costs >$75M**, materially impairing the balance sheet.

### Reassessment Timeline
Q1 2026 earnings on April 23, 2026 is the next critical data point. The back-end-loaded guidance means Q3 2026 earnings (likely October) will be the definitive test of the transition thesis.

## Conclusion

Chemed Corporation owns two genuinely advantaged businesses in large, fragmented markets. The hospice demographics are as close to a certainty as exists in long-term demand forecasting. Roto-Rooter's brand, though under digital pressure, still commands meaningful consumer recognition and an unmatched national footprint. Zero long-term debt, a 27% ROE, and an 11.8% five-year dividend growth rate demonstrate disciplined financial management historically.

Against these strengths, the stock has earned its 34% decline. Three consecutive earnings misses, margin compression in both segments, a cybersecurity breach, newly disclosed tariff exposure, declining cash reserves, falling analyst estimates, and a "transition year" framework with back-end-loaded earnings all argue for caution. The options market agrees: put-heavy positioning and IV backwardation ahead of the April 23 earnings release reflect informed skepticism.

At $385 (~16x FY2026E), the stock is not expensive, but it is not a clear mispricing either. The valuation requires earnings to stop deteriorating, and we don't yet have that evidence. We recommend waiting for the Q1 2026 report before committing capital. A clean beat would change the calculus materially. Another miss would likely push the stock toward $340, opening a more compelling entry point.

**Rating: HOLD.** Wait for Q1 2026 earnings to resolve the fundamental uncertainty before acting.
