## Executive Summary

Old Republic is two businesses moving in opposite directions at a valuation that already assumes both work. Specialty Insurance, 64% of premiums and fees, saw its combined ratio deteriorate 480 basis points year-over-year in Q2 2026 to 95.5% — the top of management's 90-95% full-cycle target — and segment underwriting income halved to $59.3M from $119.9M. Title Insurance, 36% of premiums and fees, printed a 95.1% combined ratio, its first quarter inside the target range in several years, with segment pretax operating income up 130.5% to $55.9M. Net of the two, H1 2026 operating EPS fell 12.3% to $1.44 and the consolidated combined ratio rose to 96.0% from 94.7% in FY2025 and 92.6% in FY2023.

The decomposition matters more than the headline. Of the 4.8-point Specialty combined-ratio deterioration, roughly 3.2 points came from a swing in prior-year development (from 2.9 points favorable to 0.3 points adverse), driven by approximately $40M of adverse development in a representations-and-warranties book ORI exited in Q4 2024. The current accident-year loss pick was essentially flat (65.6% versus 65.4%), and management disclosed that accident-year 2025 long-haul trucking reserves — the source of the Q4 2025 charge — are holding, with commercial auto and property developing favorably. The remaining 1.4 points is expense ratio, attributable to seven sub-scale start-up operating companies, IT modernization running duplicate systems for "the next few years," and the presentational gross-up of a large auto-warranty program. Only the last is an accounting artifact; the rest is real cash with no disclosed payback date.

The problem is price. At $43.27 the stock trades at 1.71x June-30 book value per share of $25.33, 13.4x FY2025 operating EPS of $3.23, 14.7x the current-year consensus of $2.94 and 13.1x next year's $3.30. The reported 9.5x trailing P/E reflects $374.3M of H1 investment gains, $250.7M of which was unrealized equity fair-value change routed through the income statement; that multiple is not comparable to the peer median of 10.0x. Consensus estimates have been cut 4.0% for the current year over 90 days, ORI has missed operating EPS in three of the last four quarters, and the shares sit 0.5% below the analyst mean target of $43.50. Probability-weighting a $46 base, $52 bull and $37 bear produces roughly a 7-8% expected total return including the 2.92% regular dividend — approximately fair, with the reserve-development and counterparty questions unresolved. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1923 and headquartered in Chicago, Old Republic operates roughly 21 separately managed insurance companies across two reportable segments plus a corporate/other unit that carries the holding-company debt and runs a structural pretax operating loss ($35.6M FY2025; $31.1M H1 2026).

**Specialty Insurance** ($5,184.8M of FY2025 net premiums and fees, 64.4% of total) is a concentrated commercial writer rather than a broad-market P&C carrier. Commercial auto is 42.6% of segment net premiums earned in Q2 2026, with long-haul trucking a disclosed concentration; workers' compensation is 15.4% (down from 17.5% a year earlier), property 11.1%, general liability 11.0%, financial indemnity 6.4%, home/auto warranty 6.6%. ORI writes much of this through loss-sharing structures — retrospectively-rated policies, high-deductible programs, and quota-share cessions to captives owned by insureds — and earns $175.8M of claims-handling and risk-control fee revenue alongside the underwriting. Forty-four percent of gross loss reserves are ceded. A meaningful share of segment economics is therefore fronting, servicing and float rather than pure risk-bearing.

**Title Insurance** ($2,858.6M, 35.5%) insures real-estate title. Seventy-eight percent of FY2025 title revenue came through independent agents, which drives commission expense of roughly 63% of title net premiums earned ($1,784.8M on $2,594.4M). That is why the title loss ratio is only about 3% while the expense ratio runs 92-98%. Economically this is a transaction-processing franchise with a low-frequency severity tail.

Two balance-sheet features distinguish ORI. Goodwill is only $173.2M (0.6% of assets), so book value approximates tangible book. And the $17,091.2M investment portfolio (6/30/2026) holds $2,679.0M of equities at a $1,318.2M cost basis, a $1,360.8M embedded pretax unrealized gain equal to 22.4% of shareholders' equity. Under GAAP those marks flow through earnings, which structurally decouples reported net income from operating results.

Premiums and fees grew 20.1% from FY2023 to FY2025 while net operating income grew 6.6%. In H1 2026 the relationship inverted: premiums and fees +6.1%, operating income -13.9%, operating EPS -12.3%. Underwriting income has fallen in each of the last five years, from $785.9M in 2021 to $365.9M in 2025 — a 53.4% decline in the company's own selected compensation performance measure.

**The growth number requires an adjustment.** Reported Specialty net premiums written rose 9.0% in Q2 and 7.2% in H1. Excluding the retail write-up on a large auto-warranty program — where ORI records the dealer's retail mark-up as both premium and offsetting commission — growth was **1.6% in Q2 and 2.2% in H1**. Because both sides are deferred and amortized over 4-7 year policy lives, the gross-up simultaneously inflates the reported expense ratio. Underlying specialty growth is roughly one-fifth of the headline, and part of the apparent cost deterioration is non-economic. Any screen using reported NPW growth for ORI is wrong in both directions.

**Cash flow and reserves.** Operating cash flow was $481.1M in H1 2026 against $365.5M a year earlier, and $1,164.3M in FY2025 covering net operating income 1.47x — healthy for an insurer growing reserves ($14,775.7M gross at 12/31/25 to $15,326.9M at 6/30/26). One item to monitor: Specialty prior-year loss payments rose 14.1% in H1 ($962.6M to $1,098.4M), faster than premium growth, consistent with commercial-auto severity working through a payout pattern in which 35.0% of incurred losses pay in year one and 74.5% within three years.

**Reserve adequacy is the pivotal financial question.** Consolidated one-year favorable development averaged 2.8% of opening net reserves over FY2023-25 (4.1%, 2.0%, 2.4%), understated because $31.7M of FY2025 workers'-comp discount accretion is booked as unfavorable development. But the triangles disclose that all accident years 2011-2021 developed favorably while more recent years have been neutral-to-adverse. In Q2 2026 consolidated favorable development was 0.1 points versus 2.1 points a year earlier. If development normalizes to zero, roughly 2 points of combined ratio disappears — about $160M pretax on current premium volume, or $0.52 per diluted share after tax on 246.3M shares, with no change in underlying performance.

**Investments.** Fixed income is 99.2% investment grade with no defaults and a $1.6M credit-loss allowance. Book yield rose to 4.73% in Q2 from 4.64%, new corporate purchases at 4.89%. Duration is only 3.9 years against long-tail workers'-comp and general-liability obligations; short-term investments rose from $1,613.6M to $2,233.1M while fixed income fell $548.6M, most plausibly warehousing the May debt proceeds ahead of the August maturity. AOCI fell from +$163.1M to +$8.5M in H1 (a $0.61/share fixed-income mark, about 2.4% of opening book), offset by $0.82/share of equity gains. The portfolio holds no derivatives, hedge funds, private equity, securities lending or hedging, which is unusually plain for a $17B portfolio.

**Debt.** ORI issued $700M of 5.700% notes due 2036 on May 18, 2026 to pre-fund the August 26, 2026 maturity of $550M of 3.875% notes. Debt-to-equity is temporarily 37.6%; retiring the $550M leaves $1,734.0M against $6,072.8M of equity, or 28.6%, with $150M of net new permanent capital. The refinancing costs $18.6M of incremental pretax interest, netting to roughly $11.4M pretax after investing the extra $150M at ~4.8% — about $0.04 per share annually. Interest and other charges already rose 50.3% in Q2 ($17.6M to $26.5M). FY2025 interest coverage was 15.3x. The $650M 3.850% 2051 notes trade at $468.0M, 28% below par — a duration mark, not a credit signal, representing roughly $182M of economic value to shareholders not captured in GAAP book.

**Capital return runs above earnings.** FY2025 declared dividends of $3.66/share ($897.4M) including a $2.50 special declared in December 2025, plus $124.7M of buybacks (3.2M shares at $38.71) equals $1,022.1M against $792.6M of operating income attributable to shareholders — a 129% payout. Average diluted shares fell from 285.5M (FY2023) to 246.3M (Q2 2026), a 13.7% reduction in about two and a half years. Specialty statutory RBC fell to 508% of company action level from 549%. Up to $984.8M can be dividended from subsidiaries to the parent in 2026 without regulatory approval, of which $347.5M had been received through June 30.

Financial health scores 7.0: solvency is genuinely not a question (A+ AM Best, 508% RBC, 15.3x coverage, IG portfolio, overfunded pension, no covenant or refinancing risk), but the score is held below 8 by a reinsurance recoverable of 1.14x equity with heavy unrated concentration, an above-100% payout coinciding with a deteriorating combined ratio, and a declining RBC ratio. Standard bankruptcy screens (the sub-1.0 Altman Z-Score returned by industrial-firm models) are not diagnostic for an insurance holding company where $15.3B of policy reserves sit in current liabilities.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth engine here at present. Underlying Specialty premium growth of 1.6-2.2% is below nominal GDP. Workers' compensation, historically the most profitable and most favorably developing line, is shrinking as a share of Specialty premium. Commercial-auto earned-premium growth comes from rate plus new business "partially offset by a decline in renewal retention ratios" — ORI is losing accounts to hold price. Management framed a slight sequential retention improvement as evidence competitors are now raising rates, which is a hopeful reading of one data point and an implicit acknowledgment that ORI has been pricing ahead of the market.

Title is the real growth vector, and it is a mix story rather than a volume story. Commercial title rose to 25.4% of net premiums earned from 23.0%, attributed specifically to data centers plus "a mix of several other industries," with premiums and fees +10.7%. Commercial title carries far higher premium per file than residential. The corroborating evidence that this is strategic rather than incidental: effective January 1, 2026 ORI secured title reinsurance above a $25.0M net retention on in-force and newly issued policies, disclosed as necessary "given Title Insurance's increased appetite to write larger commercial title policies." Previously title reinsurance was immaterial and most residential policies were under $1.0M. Two qualifications: roughly half the year-over-year combined-ratio improvement is the absence of a ~$15M prior-year litigation settlement (2.1 points), and direct operations fell to 22.0% of production from 22.8%, so growth is skewing toward the higher-commission agency channel.

Inorganic growth was well executed. The Everett Cash Mutual acquisition closed July 1, 2026 following a sponsored demutualization: cash consideration of approximately $12.8M against an expected acquisition gain of approximately $125M, implying acquired net assets of roughly $138M — a gain of about 9.8x cash paid, or $0.51 per diluted share pretax. Separately, ECM members, employees and directors subscribed for 927,418 ORI shares for $38.0M in cash; note the direction, ECM constituents paid ORI, diluting existing holders 0.38%. The gain is one-time, non-cash, based on an incomplete purchase-price allocation, and embeds an assumption that acquired reserves are adequate at fair value. It should not be capitalized into an earnings multiple, and it is unclear from the filings whether ORI will report it inside or outside "net operating income," which will materially affect how Q3 headline operating EPS reads.

Consensus captures the trajectory: current-year $2.94 (cut 4.0% over 90 days from $3.07), next year $3.30 (cut 1.0%), next quarter $0.81 (cut 3.9%). Three of the last four quarters missed, by 5.0%, 15.1% and 17.1%. There is no estimate-revision inflection signal here; the direction is negative and the miss pattern is recent. Per-share growth over the next 12-24 months is more likely to come from a 2-4% annual reduction in share count and 4-6% book value compounding than from underwriting improvement. Growth prospects: 4.5.

The 9.5x screen multiple is the single largest trap in this name. H1 2026 GAAP EPS of $2.63 was up 47% while operating EPS of $1.44 was down 12.3%; the entire gap is $374.3M of investment gains, $250.7M of it unrealized equity marks. Against a peer median P/E of 10.0 (KNSL 13.7, FAF 10.0, THG 9.9, FNF 16.9, MCY 6.5), ORI on comparable operating earnings at 13.4-14.7x trades at a premium, not a discount. It also carries a below-median dividend yield contribution relative to FNF's 4.4% and FAF's 3.3% on the regular payout alone.

A dividend-discount framing on book value is the cleanest anchor. With sustainable operating ROE of 13%, a 9% cost of equity and 4% growth, justified P/B is (0.13-0.04)/(0.09-0.04) = 1.8x. At 12.1% ROE (the Q2 annualized figure) it is 1.62x; at 11% it falls to 1.40x. Applied to a mid-2027 book value of roughly $27.00 (assuming 6-7% BVPS growth net of the regular dividend and no repeat of a $2.50 special), those multiples imply $37.80 to $48.60. The current 1.71x sits in the middle of that band and implies the market is underwriting sustained low-13% operating ROE — achievable, but requiring either the expense ratio to inflect or reserve releases to persist at 2 points, neither of which is currently evident.

Cross-checking on earnings: 13.5x next year's $3.30 gives $44.55; 15x gives $49.50; 12x gives $39.60. Analyst coverage is thin (three estimates, one buy and two holds) with a mean target of $43.50, high $48, low $39; the shares trade 0.5% below mean. Targets should not be treated as floors here: this is a stable-multiple insurer with falling estimates, not an inflection stock with rising estimates.

One item genuinely not in the price: roughly $182M of economic value from the 2051 notes trading 28% below par, or about $0.74 per share, which GAAP book carries at amortized cost. Set against that is the $282.5M deferred tax liability on net unrealized investment gains at 12/31/2025, which reduces the realizable value of the equity portfolio embedded in book.

Valuation: 4.8. Fairly priced with no identified mispricing, at roughly double the price-to-book ORI commanded earlier in the decade, on estimates that are being revised down.

## Competitive Landscape

In Specialty, ORI competes as a relationship- and service-intensive fronting and loss-sharing specialist for trucking and industrial accounts rather than as a capital-only underwriter. The FY2025 10-K makes the mechanism explicit: quota-share reinsurance is most often effected with assigned-risk plans "or captive insurers owned by insureds." That model is hard for balance-sheet-only competitors to replicate and generates $175.8M of fee revenue, but it also means gross balance-sheet growth overstates risk-bearing growth and that returns are partly fee-like.

The industry backdrop explains both the opportunity and the squeeze. American Transportation Research Institute research (May 2026) found trucking liability premiums up 18.6% from 2021 to 2024 to 10.2 cents per mile while heavy-duty crash rates fell 2.6%, injury crash rates fell over 15% from their 2019 peak, and fatal crash rates fell nearly 14% — yet surveyed fleets reported a 33.1% increase in per-mile liability losses, with the $5-10M excess layer up 34% and the $10-15M layer up 45%. Frequency is improving and severity is worsening faster, which defeats experience-rating and makes reserve estimates dependent on assumptions about future jury behavior. Two forward items widen the exposure: a $604M Texas verdict against C.H. Robinson in July 2026 following a Supreme Court decision permitting negligent-hiring claims against freight brokers, and a House bill to raise the federal minimum motor-carrier insurance requirement to $5M.

In Title, ORI is one of a small national oligopoly (FNF, First American, Stewart) competing primarily for agent and realtor relationships rather than on price. Its 78% agency mix is lower-margin and lower-capital than a direct-heavy competitor, and less fixed-cost exposed in downturns. The move up-market into large commercial title, backed by the new $25M-retention treaty, puts ORI into a segment where FNF and First American have historically been stronger.

Trajectory assessment is mixed. Negative: commercial-auto retention declining as ORI holds price, workers' compensation shrinking, and an expense ratio worsening as seven start-ups launched in five years plus a new property company announced April 6, 2026 dilute scale. Positive: disciplined pruning (RMIC mortgage insurance sold May 2024; the transactional-risk book exited Q4 2024), the ECM niche entry, the title commercial pivot, and a flat current-accident-year Specialty loss pick indicating underwriting discipline is holding on new business. Competitive position: 5.0, a modest improvement from the prior report's 4.5 on the title mix evidence and confirmation that the current-year loss pick has not deteriorated.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Reinsurance counterparty concentration in an unrated entity — the largest unquantified exposure.** At 12/31/2025 the ten largest Specialty reinsurers were roughly 65% of consolidated recoverable on paid and unpaid losses, and Day One Insurance, Inc. alone was 24.0% of the total. On total recoverable of $6,609.0M, that implies approximately $1,586M, equal to 26.8% of year-end shareholders' equity. The top-ten composition is 37.2% domestic unrated, 29.0% foreign unrated, 30.3% A-or-better rated, 3.5% assigned-risk pools — two-thirds unrated. Total recoverable at 6/30/2026 was $6,933.0M, or 1.14x equity. Day One is most plausibly a captive or fronting vehicle of a large insured, and ORI states that such balances are "substantially collateralized by irrevocable letters of credit, securities and other financial instruments." That is a genuine mitigant, but "substantially" is unquantified, the entity's identity, ownership, rating and collateral coverage could not be verified from any source, and the company has now disclosed that the credit-loss allowance rose from $23.0M to $39.0M in H1 specifically from "applying historic default rates to a larger balance of **uncollateralized** recoverables." Allowance coverage remains 0.56% of recoverable. Either reading is consistent with disclosure; the ambiguity at this magnitude is itself the risk.

**2. Reserve-release dependence.** The 2-3 point annual favorable-development tailwind comes predominantly from accident years 2011-2021. That pool is finite and maturing; Q2 favorable development was 0.1 points against 2.1 points a year earlier. Normalization to zero removes ~$160M of pretax underwriting income.

**3. Commercial-auto severity, structural.** 42.6% of Specialty net premiums earned at a 72.3% FY2025 loss ratio. Net retention is only $8.5M per liability occurrence, so nuclear verdicts largely hit reinsurers, but that protection is purchased annually and reprices, and rising cession is precisely what grows the counterparty exposure in risk 1. The two risks are causally linked.

**4. An unresolved reserve-triangle anomaly.** Commercial-auto accident year 2025 shows initial incurred losses of $1,529.7M with "IBNR plus expected development on reported losses" of **negative $151.4M**. Read literally, case reserves plus paid exceed the booked ultimate — the inverse of a normal first-year posture, consistent with management declining to load bulk IBNR on top of the Q4 2025 case strengthening. A presentational or allocation convention is an equally plausible explanation. This could not be resolved from the filing and bears directly on whether the AY2025 pick is conservative or thin.

**5. Run-off transactional risk.** The ~$40M Q2 charge is in a book exited in Q4 2024, but financial-indemnity triangles show repeated adverse development (AY2019 $149.5M to $190.5M; AY2022 $182.2M to $207.1M; AY2023 $173.6M to $215.6M). Further creep is plausible; Q2 financial-indemnity loss ratio was 101.2% versus 56.4%.

**6. Expense-ratio persistence.** Specialty expense ratio rose to 30.4% in H1 from 28.1%. The IT double-running cost is expected to persist "over the next few years" with no quantification; seven start-ups need 3-5 years each to become accretive. Corporate & Other operating expenses rose 17.5% in H1, stock compensation more than doubled from $19.5M (FY2023) to $46.7M (FY2025), and 401(k)/profit-sharing contributions rose from $58.2M to $91.1M.

**7. Title cyclicality with a concentrated demand driver.** Title is 36% of premiums and fees and directly levered to mortgage rates and housing turnover. The current recovery leans on commercial volume tied to data-center construction, a capex-cycle-dependent source. Title statutory surplus is only $632.1M and statutory net income has declined three consecutive years ($152.3M to $140.1M to $129.3M) even as GAAP results improved.

**8. Capital-return arithmetic.** A 129% payout, a declining RBC ratio and a deteriorating combined ratio cannot all persist. The most likely adjustment is the special dividend, which is board-discretionary and should not be capitalized as recurring.

Management quality: 6.0. The capital-allocation record is strong (13.7% share retirement, an ECM bargain purchase at 9.8x cash paid, two disciplined business exits, 85 years of uninterrupted dividends and 45 consecutive increases), compensation metrics are correctly chosen for an insurer (underwriting income, combined ratio, operating ROE, BVPS growth including dividends), and there have been no Item 5.02 departures since September 2024, no restatement, no auditor change and no material weakness. Offsetting, and the reason this is not a 7: there is no open-market C-suite buying of any size — the only open-market purchase in the window is a director's 300 shares at $39.90 — while Jeffrey Lange sold 14,075 shares at $43.76, roughly 53% of his holding, and Stephen Oberst exercised 208,000 options with 163,193 shares disposed. Option-exercise selling near a 52-week high is weak evidence either way, but the absence of buying at 1.71x book alongside a self-described discipline that "repurchasing shares at valuations above book value per share has an immediate dilutive impact" — and zero repurchases in May and June — suggests management does not see the stock as cheap here. CEO compensation actually paid rose 291% from 2021 to 2025 while the company's own selected measure, underwriting income, fell 53.4%; that divergence is mechanically explained by share-price marks on unvested equity and by mix (the FY2021 figure included the divested RFIG mortgage run-off at peak releases), and no motive should be inferred, but shareholders have been paid by multiple expansion and capital return rather than by underwriting improvement.

Risk factors: 4.5.

Low confidence; do not trade on it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case → $52 (+20% price, ~23% total return)

Title sustains its Q2 improvement: the commercial mix moves past 26-27% of net premiums earned on data-center and infrastructure title, the segment holds inside 90-95% for a full year, and segment pretax operating income roughly doubles off the FY2025 base with the $25M-retention treaty capping severity. In Specialty, the expense ratio improves as the auto-warranty gross-up laps, three or four of the seven start-ups reach scale, and the IT duplicate-running period ends — 150 basis points recovered on $8.2B of premium is $123M pretax. Accident-year 2025 long-haul trucking reserves continue to hold, financial-indemnity development stops at the $40M already taken, and commercial-auto retention stabilizes as competitors raise rates. ECM is accretive from Q3 with a ~$125M gain rebuilding capital that funds resumed buybacks against the $631.1M remaining authorization. Operating EPS reaches $3.50-3.60 in FY2027 on a share count below 240M; the market pays 14.5x, or 1.9x a $27 book. A repeat special dividend adds several points of total return.

### Bear Case → $37 (-14.5% price, -12% total return)

Reserve development normalizes to zero and the 2-point tailwind disappears, taking $160M of pretax underwriting income with it. Financial-indemnity run-off produces two more charges given the triangle history. The negative $151.4M AY2025 commercial-auto IBNR figure proves to mean what it literally says, and the accident-year 2025 trucking pick requires strengthening as social inflation continues to outrun rate — ATRI's evidence that per-mile liability losses rose 33.1% while crash rates fell is the mechanism. The expense ratio does not improve because ORI keeps launching sub-scale units (a new property company in April 2026, E&S expansion at seven operating companies). Consolidated combined ratio passes 97%, operating ROE falls to 11%, and the justified P/B compresses to 1.40x on a $26 book. Title's commercial tailwind reverses with the AI capex cycle while residential volumes stay depressed. The special dividend is cut to preserve a falling RBC ratio, removing the total-return support. Tail risk: an adverse resolution of the Day One collateral question against $1,586M of recoverable.

### Base Case → $46 (+6.3% price, ~9.2% total return with the regular dividend)

Title holds roughly in the low-to-mid 90s with commercial mix providing offset to weak residential volumes. Specialty combined ratio stabilizes around 94-95% as favorable commercial-auto and property development partly offsets a declining reserve-release tailwind and a flat-to-slightly-improving expense ratio. Operating EPS lands near the $3.30 consensus for next year — down from FY2025's $3.23 on a per-share basis only because share count keeps shrinking. Book value per share compounds to roughly $27.00 by mid-2027 at a maintained 1.70x multiple. Probability-weighting 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear yields approximately +4.6% price appreciation plus 2.9% dividend, or 7-8% expected total return.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD** — income_hold. The holding case rests on capital return (2.92% regular yield with 45 consecutive increases and 85 years of uninterrupted payments, plus a discretionary special and $631.1M of buyback authorization) supported by an A+ balance sheet, against operating earnings that are declining and a multiple that already reflects a 13% ROE the company is not currently earning. ## Conclusion

Old Republic's balance sheet is not in question — 508% Specialty RBC, 15.3x interest coverage, a 99.2% investment-grade fixed-income portfolio with no defaults, an overfunded frozen pension, $984.8M of available subsidiary dividend capacity, and debt-to-equity normalizing to 28.6% after August. The capital-allocation record is strong: 13.7% of shares retired in two and a half years, two sub-scale businesses pruned, and an acquisition producing an expected ~$125M gain on $12.8M of cash.

What is in question is earnings power at the current price. Operating EPS fell 12.3% in H1 2026, the consolidated combined ratio has risen from 92.6% to 96.0% over three years, the reserve-release tailwind that has flattered results is concentrated in accident years 2011-2021 and shrinking, and consensus for the current year has been cut 4.0% to $2.94. Two-thirds of the Q2 Specialty deterioration came from a book ORI already exited, which is genuinely reassuring about the ongoing franchise; the remaining third is an expense-investment cycle with no disclosed end date. Title's move into its 90-95% target range on a real commercial and data-center mix shift, backed by a deliberate new $25M-retention reinsurance treaty, is the most encouraging development in the company.

At $43.27 — 1.71x June-30 book, 13.4x FY2025 operating EPS, 14.7x current-year consensus, 0.5% below the analyst mean target, 7.5% below the 52-week high — the shares price a sustained 13% operating ROE that Q2 delivered at 12.1% and falling. The 9.5x screen multiple is an artifact of unrealized equity marks and should not be mistaken for cheapness against a peer median of 10.0x. Two questions could change the assessment in either direction and neither is answerable from current public disclosure: the collateral coverage behind $1,586M of recoverable from an unrated counterparty equal to 27% of equity, and the negative $151.4M accident-year 2025 commercial-auto IBNR entry. Hold for the income and the balance sheet; the entry price for new capital is meaningfully lower.
