## Executive Summary

Howmet's operating results are as good as anything in industrials. Q2 2026 revenue rose 24.1% to $2,547M, operating income rose 36.5% to $711M, and adjusted EPS of $1.33 beat the $1.23 non-GAAP consensus by 8%, the fourth consecutive beat. Engine Products — the franchise, at 54% of revenue — grew sales 32% and expanded segment EBITDA margin 470 basis points to 37.7%. Consolidated incremental margin was 45.9% on my reconstruction ($227M of incremental adjusted EBITDA on $494M of incremental revenue), which ties to management's ~46% characterization. FY2026 guidance was raised to $10.0-10.1B revenue (+21-22%), $5.27 adjusted EPS and $1.9B free cash flow. Cost of goods sold has fallen from 71.9% of sales in FY2023 to 62.7% in Q2 2026, roughly 920 basis points of gross-margin expansion across ten quarters, and it was achieved while alloy costs rose (the LIFO reserve grew to $376M from $333M in six months).

The balance sheet is the strongest it has been since the 2020 separation despite a $1.9B acquisition. Net debt of $3,938M against $2,924M of TTM segment adjusted EBITDA is 1.35x, well inside the 3.75x covenant; FY2025 interest coverage was 13.5x; there are no revolver borrowings; only $451M of debt matures within a year. Fitch upgraded to A− in February 2026, S&P to BBB+ in September 2025, and Moody's moved the Baa1 outlook to positive on May 7, 2026. The Altman Z-score of 2.78 that appears on screens is an artifact of substituting book equity into the public-firm model's X4 term; using market equity lifts the score above 13. Solvency is not the debate.

The debate is entirely price and three specific risks. At $281.88 the stock trades at 53.5x the guided 2026 adjusted EPS, 45.8x the $6.15 next-year consensus, 39.9x EV to TTM segment adjusted EBITDA, and a 1.68% FCF yield on guidance. Peer median P/E is 26.6x; the closest quality comparable, TransDigm, trades at 39.3x trailing. Guidance itself implies H2 adjusted EPS of roughly $2.68 against H1's ~$2.59 (after removing the ~$0.18-0.19 Savannah divestiture gain), only +3.5% sequentially — management is not embedding continued acceleration. Meanwhile top-two customer concentration worsened by roughly 300 basis points in twelve months to ~24% (GE ~14%, RTX ~10%), those same customers operate captive superalloy furnaces capable of producing airfoil castings in-house, the CEO is 72 and holds both the chair and CEO titles with no disclosed successor, and the $1.9B CAM/Brunner acquisition is currently margin-dilutive with synergies pushed to 2027 one quarter after closing. Base case $300 (+6.4%), bull $370 (+31%), bear $195 (−31%). HOLD.

## Company Overview

Howmet manufactures engineered metal components for jet engines, airframes, industrial gas turbines and heavy trucks. It is the legal successor to Alcoa Inc., having spun off Alcoa Corporation in 2016 and Arconic Corporation in April 2020. FY2025 revenue of $8,252M split across four segments:

| Segment | FY2025 Sales | Seg. Adj. EBITDA | Margin | FY2023 Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Engine Products | $4,320M | $1,438M | 33.3% | 27.2% |
| Fastening Systems | $1,745M | $530M | 30.4% | 20.6% |
| Engineered Structures | $1,148M | $243M | 21.2% | 12.9% |
| Forged Wheels | $1,039M | $296M | 28.5% | 26.9% |
| **Total** | **$8,252M** | **$2,507M** | **30.4%** | **23.9%** |

Every segment expanded margin materially over two years; Fastening Systems and Engineered Structures nearly doubled theirs. End-market mix in FY2025: commercial aerospace 53%, defense aerospace 17%, commercial transportation 15%, gas turbines 11%, other 4%. Geographically 72% of shipments originate in North America, 22% in Europe. Roughly 25,430 employees, of whom ~3,860 are unionized; the critical Whitehall, Michigan UAW casting agreement does not expire until April 2028.

The moat is qualification, not machining. Airfoils operate above the melting point of the alloys from which they are cast, and every part is flight-qualified to a specific engine program, making supplier requalification slow and expensive. The 10-K states Howmet can produce more than 90% of all structural and rotating aero engine components, which means it competes for content on any new engine architecture rather than defending one part family.

Revenue compounded 13.4% annually from FY2022 to FY2025 while operating income more than doubled and net income more than tripled. The mechanism is operating leverage on a fixed-cost casting and forging base, visible in the COGS ratio: 71.9% (FY2023) → 68.9% (FY2024) → 65.8% (FY2025) → 62.7% (Q2 2026).

Q2 2026 segment detail versus Q2 2025: Engine Products sales +32% to $1,373M with margin 37.7% vs 33.0%; Fastening Systems +37% to $589M with margin 30.1% vs 29.2% (the 10-Q attributes the muted margin gain to the lower-margin CAM and Brunner businesses); Engineered Structures −13% to $269M but margin 23.8% vs 22.1% on deliberate product rationalization and the Savannah divestiture; Forged Wheels +14% to $316M with margin 27.8% (management noted aluminum pass-through inflates revenue while diluting percentage margin). By end market, gas turbines were the fastest line at +38% to $321M, which the 10-K attributes specifically to data-center build-out.

**Cash flow quality requires three adjustments.** Six-month FCF was $838M versus $478M. Three items temper it. Accounts payable contributed $279M to operating cash flow while supplier-finance program balances rose to $377M from $266M — a $111M increase, with terms extended up to ~120 days, so roughly 40% of the AP swing traces to lengthened payment terms rather than operational efficiency. (The offsetting explanation is legitimate: AP rises mechanically with 24% revenue growth, a $334M inventory build, and $87M of assumed liabilities from the acquisitions.) Separately, "other customer receivable sales" ran $671M in H1 2026 versus $406M a year earlier, a 65% increase in an uncommitted program, though combined receivable sales of $1,322M versus $1,235M limit the aggregate distortion. Finally, the $1.9B FY2026 FCF guide requires ~$1,062M in H2 against H2 2025's $953M, with capex above $300M in H2 — achievable at +11% H2 growth after +75% in H1, but back-half loaded. I would not annualize any single quarter of this company's cash flow.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Net debt at June 30, 2026 was $3,938M ($4,501M total debt less $563M cash) against TTM segment adjusted EBITDA of $2,924M, or 1.35x. Interest coverage was 13.5x in FY2025 and net interest expense fell from $218M (2023) to $151M (2025) through active liability management: $625M of 5.900% 2027 notes redeemed, a $140M term-loan prepayment, and a $500M 4.550% 2032 issuance. In 2026 the company issued $1,200M gross across three tranches to fund CAM, prepaid the ¥29,702M JPY term loan, and cross-currency-swapped the 6.750% 2028 bonds into JPY at 3.880% and the 4.850% 2031 notes into EUR at 3.720%. Guided incremental net interest from 2026 actions is ~$38M annually, modest for $1.2B of capital. Liquidity comprises a new $1,000M five-year revolver to 2031, a $600M 364-day facility maturing February 2027, and a $1,000M CP program with $450M outstanding at 4.018%. The 364-day facility renewal in February 2027 is the one financing item to monitor.

Two genuine balance-sheet negatives: goodwill plus intangibles of $5,953M is 44.9% of assets and exceeds total equity of $5,732M, so tangible book value is approximately −$221M; and combined pension/OPEB is underfunded by $588M with ERISA funded status around 63%, though 2026 cash contributions are guided at only ~$60M and the FY2025 $89M UK pension settlement charge indicates active de-risking.

**A data note.** The supplied profile shows trailing and forward P/E both at 67.25x, implying $4.19 of EPS, which reconciles to neither FY2025 diluted EPS of $3.71 (76.0x) nor the guided $5.27 (53.5x). I use the derived figures below.

## Growth Analysis

The demand backdrop is the most favorable in a generation. Narrow-body engine production is recovering after the FAA cleared 737 MAX rate increases in October 2025; the LEAP and GTF installed base is entering its first heavy shop-visit cycle, which drives high-margin spares; defense budgets are rising; and industrial gas turbines have acquired a new secular driver in data-center power, where Howmet's +38% growth aligns with the 10-K's attribution to accelerating data-center build-out.

Company-level growth signals:

- FY2026 revenue guide of $10.0-10.1B implies +21-22% over FY2025.
- Q3 2026 revenue guided to ~$2.58B, characterized in market coverage as ~4% above prior expectations.
- Consensus EPS revisions are positive but moderate: current year $5.21 (+3.1% in 30 days, +4.2% in 90 days), next year $6.15 (+2.0% in 30 days). This is well short of the +30% velocity that signals a genuine earnings inflection; it is confirmation of an in-progress ramp, and at a 45-53x multiple the revisions are largely already capitalized.
- Four consecutive beats averaging 6.6% ($1.33 vs $1.26, $1.22 vs $1.12, $1.05 vs $0.98, $0.95 vs $0.92), which raises confidence in consensus but also means beats are expected rather than re-rating events.
- Engine Products headcount rose ~1,445 net in 2025 after ~1,205 in 2024, and capex is guided above $500M in 2026 and higher in 2027 for gas-turbine and commercial-aero capacity. Capacity is the binding constraint, and management is finally spending to relieve it.

Two qualifications matter for the growth score. First, guidance implies sequential deceleration: FY $5.27 less H1 adjusted ~$2.59 (H1 GAAP $2.77 less the ~$0.18-0.19 after-tax Savannah gain) leaves ~$2.68 for H2, +3.5% half-over-half, even though it is +34% year over year. Second, rising capex means free cash flow may not scale with earnings. If FY2027 operating cash flow reaches $2.3B against $700M of capex, FCF would be roughly $1.6B — below the FY2026 guide. The 1.68% FCF yield therefore may not expand quickly, which matters when the equity is valued on cash generation.

## Competitive Landscape

Howmet's position is strong and its trajectory is improving. Three structural sources of advantage: product breadth (>90% of structural and rotating engine components, so it competes for content on any architecture); qualification barriers (flight-critical castings and forgings are certified program by program, making incumbency durable across a multi-decade program life); and capacity scarcity (the industry underinvested through 2020-2023, and Howmet's headcount additions and rising capex indicate it is running near its limits). Scarce qualified capacity plus long certification cycles is the cleanest explanation for how a metals-forming business reached a 37.7% segment EBITDA margin.

The competitive threats are more specific than the sector story implies. Precision Castparts, owned by Berkshire Hathaway, overlaps essentially every Howmet product line and is not capital-constrained. More consequentially, the 10-K discloses that several of Howmet's largest customers operate captive superalloy furnaces and produce airfoil investment castings for their own use. Howmet's two largest customers — GE Aerospace at ~14% and RTX at ~10% of H1 2026 sales, ~24% combined — therefore possess demonstrated technical capability to insource. When a supplier earns 37.7% segment EBITDA margins from counterparties who can make the part themselves, that margin becomes a negotiating target at the next contract cycle. This is an inference about bargaining dynamics rather than a disclosed company view, but it is the most credible mechanism by which the margin trajectory reverses.

Concentration moved the wrong way. FY2025 was RTX ~11% / GE ~11% (~22%); the prior-year six-month period was RTX ~11% / GE ~10% (~21%); H1 2026 is GE ~14% / RTX ~10% (~24%). Meanwhile aerospace fell from 69% to 68% of six-month revenue as gas turbines grew. The end-market shift broadens the demand drivers; it does not diversify the counterparty exposure, and only the latter bears on pricing leverage. Both facts are compatible — GE's aviation ramp and its power franchise can each lift GE's share — and rising GE share is partly a quality-of-demand positive. But the concentration measure deteriorated, and both figures are rounded to whole percentages, so the true change spans roughly 100 to 500 basis points.

Forged Wheels is the weakest link. The 10-K flags an increasing number of Chinese, Taiwanese, Indian, Korean and Turkish aluminum wheel suppliers, in the one segment where the technical moat is thinnest and where reported growth is partly aluminum pass-through rather than volume.

## Risk Assessment

**Boeing production rates, named and unhedged.** The 10-K states directly that Boeing production rates "have had and are expected to have a material impact on the financial performance of Howmet," citing the FAA's withholding of MAX rate increases until October 2025 and Boeing's late-2024 work stoppage. A new labor action, quality escape or rate freeze transmits immediately to Engine Products and Engineered Structures. At 53.5x forward earnings there is no valuation cushion for this.

**Customer concentration plus insourcing optionality**, discussed above. Twenty-four percent of revenue rests with two counterparties who are also partial competitors.

**Succession.** John Plant is 72, holds both Executive Chairman and CEO titles, has no separate independent chair among nine board nominees, and no publicly disclosed successor. A large share of the equity story — +1,453% since the April 2020 separation versus +178% for the S&P Aerospace & Defense index — is attributed to his operating and capital discipline. Four Item 5.02 officer-change 8-Ks in twelve months compound this: CFO Ken Giacobbe replaced by Patrick Winterlich (from Hexcel) effective December 1, 2025; Chief Legal Officer Lola Lin resigned September 2025 leaving a ~seven-month vacancy until Jonathan Arena's April 2026 appointment. The ages of the reassigned officers (Marchuk 68, Chanatry 65) are consistent with orderly generational turnover, and Winterlich's hire reads as an upgrade in aerospace-specific financial leadership. But refreshing CFO and CLO simultaneously ahead of a $1.9B integration adds execution risk.

**CAM integration.** The April 6, 2026 acquisition of CAM from Stanley Black & Decker cost ~$1,812M net, plus ~$120M for Brunner in February. Purchase accounting allocated $1,082M to goodwill (56% of the ~$1,929M consideration) and $425M to intangibles (78% combined). Stub-period contribution was ~2% of consolidated sales and ~1% of consolidated net income, and the 10-Q attributes Fastening Systems' muted margin gain to the acquisitions. Management indicated on the August 6 call that CAM synergies now shift to 2027. Deferring synergies one quarter after close, on a business acquired at a derived 5-6x revenue, is the single most important item to monitor in capital allocation. That derived multiple rests on a disclosure rounded to the nearest percent and CAM's standalone financials are not independently verifiable — treat it as an estimate.

**Tariffs**, disclosed qualitatively with no quantification, against 72% North American and 22% European shipment origination and cross-border metals sourcing.

**Legacy separation contingencies.** Howmet is secondarily liable on an Alcoa energy supply agreement running to 2047, guaranteed up to an estimated present value of ~$1,141M, with Arconic indemnifying 50% and Alcoa posting an $80M surety bond; Howmet carries $5M of fair value and views default as remote. Grenfell Tower proceedings (Howmet joined June 21, 2024) are fully indemnified by Arconic Corporation, making Arconic's credit quality the residual exposure; next case management conference is December 13, 2027 with trial anticipated 2028-2029. The French tax matter resolved favorably in April 2026 at ~$4M against a $21M reserve.

**Negative tangible book value** of approximately −$221M means a severe downturn requiring goodwill impairment testing would hit reported equity hard, though it is not a covenant issue at 1.35x leverage.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not corroborating a bearish view. Near-term ATM IV of 32.3% sits essentially on top of 30.5% one-year realized volatility (spread 1.8%, IV percentile 16% within the chain), and IV collapsed from 43.0% on July 22 to 32.3% post-print — the earnings event has been resolved and priced out, with no residual event premium. Skew is call-heavy at the one-week tenor (−6.2%) and modestly call-heavy at six months (−3.4%), put/call volume ratio is 0.51, and put/call open interest is 0.86 and falling (from 0.94). The six-month tenor prices a ±17.4% move, which brackets neither my bull nor bear case — the market is pricing continuation, not dislocation. Short interest of 2.0% of float and 2.3 days to cover confirms an uncrowded long. The takeaway for a HOLD: nobody is positioned for the derating that constitutes the bear case, which means a disappointment would be absorbed by unhedged holders. Cheap, evenly distributed volatility also means collar or covered-call overwriting is not attractively priced for existing holders.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $370 (+31%)

Aerospace demand extends through 2028-2029 on the narrow-body build rate plus the first heavy shop-visit cycle for LEAP and GTF, while gas-turbine demand from data-center power adds a second, uncorrelated growth engine that grew 38% in Q2 and is not yet at scale. Capacity scarcity keeps pricing firm; Howmet's rising capex converts into 37%+ incremental-margin capacity rather than commoditized volume. CAM synergies land in 2027 and Fastening Systems margins re-converge toward the segment's 30%+ standalone level, adding a second earnings lever on top of Engine Products. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $6.80 against the $6.15 consensus, supported by a beat pattern averaging 6.6%. The multiple holds near 55x because the market continues to treat Howmet as a scarce, credit-upgrading, 22%-ROIC compounder in the strongest aerospace cycle in decades. Leverage at 1.35x leaves room for further buybacks (~$697M authorized) and bolt-ons.

### Bear Case — $195 (−31%)

The compression mechanism does not require a demand collapse, only a pause. A Boeing rate disruption, a gas-turbine order digestion quarter, or one contract cycle in which GE and RTX — 24% of revenue, both capable of insourcing airfoil castings — extract price, is enough to take FY2027 EPS to $5.60-6.00 and the multiple to 32-35x. That combination is $192-210. Guidance already implies only +3.5% sequential half-over-half EPS growth, so the sequential acceleration the multiple embeds is not in management's own numbers. Reported cash flow is somewhat better than underlying conversion: roughly 40% of the H1 AP tailwind traces to lengthened supplier-finance terms, and uncommitted receivable sales rose 65%. Rising capex means free cash flow may not scale with earnings, so the 1.68% FCF yield could stay compressed. The $1.9B acquisition is dilutive with synergies deferred, buybacks are running at escalating prices ($134.20 average in H1 2025, $240.10 in H1 2026, $276.61 in July 2026), and the CEO who is credited with the operating discipline is 72 with no named successor.

### Management Assessment

The forward-looking indicators are mixed, which is why this dimension sits below the operating record. On the positive side: CEO ownership of 2,471,367 shares (~$697M) is enormous and long-dated economic alignment; liability management has been genuinely value-additive (net interest expense down from $218M to $151M across two years, three rating upgrades); the Savannah divestiture at $230M for ~$130M of revenue coincided with a 170-basis-point margin expansion in the segment, consistent with real pruning; the dividend rose to $0.12 from $0.10 and the Class A preferred was redeemed. On the negative side: 2025 CEO compensation of $70.5M on the summary table and $134.8M "compensation actually paid" — 3.2x the 2024 figure — against an indexed TSR of 728 versus a 230 peer index, with the grant's vesting and performance conditions unverified because the full proxy CD&A was not retrievable; no return-on-capital metric among the four performance measures despite $1.9B of acquisition deployment; $800M of repurchases executed at 45-55x forward earnings; no open-market insider purchases, with Chief Administrative Officer Neil Marchuk selling ~$22.7M across two 2026 tranches (most consistent with pre-retirement diversification by a 68-year-old, and unreadable as a signal absent confirmation of Rule 10b5-1 status). The insider selling is low-signal against rising estimates and record results. The buyback price discipline and the deferred CAM synergies are the substantive criticisms.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (fifth consecutive). Existing holders should maintain exposure. New capital has no urgent entry signal.

**Distinguishing HOLD from ACCUMULATE and REDUCE.** ACCUMULATE requires positive expected return with adequate near-term asymmetry; here base-case upside of 6.4% against bear-case downside of 30.8% fails that test even though probability-weighted expected return is modestly positive at roughly +5%. REDUCE requires unfavorable expected return or clearly adverse asymmetry into a dated catalyst; there is no such catalyst — the Q2 event has passed, guidance was raised, estimates are rising, options positioning is call-tilted, and short interest is 2.0%. Shorting or trimming a business printing 24% revenue growth with raised guidance and a rising credit profile against no timed catalyst is the wrong trade. HOLD is the honest resolution.

**Targets (all from the $281.88 basis):**
- **Base case: $300** (+6.4%). FY2027 adjusted EPS $6.40 (modest beat on $6.15 consensus, consistent with the 6.6% average surprise) × 47x; cross-checked at $307 via FY2027 segment EBITDA of $3.81B at 33x EV/EBITDA less ~$3.5B net debt over ~398M shares.
- **Bull case: $370** (+31.3%). FY2027 EPS $6.80 × 55x; consistent with the $375 high analyst target.
- **Bear case: $195** (−30.8%). FY2027 EPS $5.60-6.00 at 32-35x on a Boeing rate disruption, gas-turbine pause, or rate-driven multiple compression.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with decision points at the Q3 print (late October 2026), the FY2026 report and FY2027 guide (February 2027), and the February 2027 renewal of the $600M 364-day revolver.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE if any of:**
1. Price reaches $240-250 with FY2027 consensus intact at or above $6.15 — that is 39-41x forward, roughly TransDigm's multiple, restoring 20%+ upside to the base case.
2. Management quantifies CAM synergies with a dollar target and a 2027 delivery schedule, and Fastening Systems margin recovers above 31% for two consecutive quarters, evidencing the $1.9B deployment is accretive.
3. FY2027 consensus is revised above $6.75 (a further +10%) on gas-turbine capacity coming online, which would put the current price at 42x forward.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of:**
1. Engine Products segment EBITDA margin declines for two consecutive quarters from 37.7%, indicating either price concession to GE/RTX or cost inflation outrunning pass-through.
2. FY2026 free cash flow tracks below $1.7B against the $1.9B guide, or H2 operating cash flow requires further growth in supplier-finance balances beyond the $377M June level to hit the guide.
3. Boeing announces a rate reduction, work stoppage or FAA production freeze, or GE/RTX combined concentration exceeds 28% of sales.
4. A CEO succession announcement lands without an internal successor named at least two quarters in advance, or a further Item 5.02 departure at CFO or segment-president level.

**Reassessment timeline:** next natural point is the Q3 2026 print in late October, where the two things to check are the H2 cash-flow bridge (~$1,062M of FCF required in H2, against $198M of H1 capex versus a >$500M full-year guide) and whether Fastening Systems margin has begun to recover post-purchase-accounting step-up.

## Conclusion

Howmet is one of the best-run industrial businesses in the market. Engine Products at 37.7% segment EBITDA margin, ~46% consolidated incremental margins, 920 basis points of gross-margin expansion in ten quarters achieved against rising alloy costs, 1.35x net leverage, 13.5x interest coverage, three rating-agency upgrades in under two years, and 22% ROIC constitute an operating record that is almost entirely filing-sourced rather than narrative. The Q2 print and the raise to $10.0-10.1B revenue and $5.27 adjusted EPS confirm the trajectory, and the forward multiple compressed from roughly 55.5x to 53.5x as the guidance raise outpaced the flat share price.

What has not changed is that the equity capitalizes essentially all of the visible runway.
