## Executive Summary

Crescent Energy is a KKR-managed upstream E&P company that has executed approximately $6B in acquisitions since its 2021 formation, most recently the $3.1B all-stock Vital Energy merger (closed December 2025) that gave it a scaled Permian Basin position. The stock has appreciated 55% over the past year but still trades at ~3.2x EV/EBITDA against a peer median of ~4.5x, with a levered FCF yield of approximately 23% on FY2025 numbers. Consensus current-year EPS estimates increased 57% over the past 30 days to $2.03, reflecting the full-year contribution from Vital Energy and $190M in targeted synergies.

The investment case rests on the magnitude of the valuation discount relative to the quality of cash flow generation. FY2025 operating cash flow was $1.68B (+37% YoY) on $3.58B in revenue, with levered free cash flow of $856M. The company carries $5.5B of debt at a weighted-average coupon near 8%, implying $440M+ in annual interest expense — manageable at current oil prices but a material risk if WTI falls below $60. The KKR management agreement extracts an annual base fee now approaching $79M plus non-cash equity-based compensation of $245M in FY2025, effectively a 6-7% annual dilution tax on shareholders that explains much of the persistent valuation discount. Despite these structural headwinds, the combination of a 23% FCF yield, accelerating estimate revisions, and a $400M buyback authorization at prices management has already demonstrated willingness to execute at ($8.21 average in 2025) creates sufficient upside to warrant a BUY.

## Company Overview

Crescent Energy was formed in December 2021 through the merger of Contango Oil & Gas and Independence Energy, KKR's primary upstream oil and gas platform. The company operates across three core basins: the Eagle Ford (Texas), the Permian Basin (Texas/New Mexico, acquired via Vital Energy), and the Uinta Basin (Utah). It also operates a minerals and royalties platform ("Crescent Royalties") with $355M in Eagle Ford minerals acquisitions announced in February 2026.

FY2025 production averaged 260 MBoe/d (40% oil, 58% liquids). 2026 guidance calls for 320-335 MBoe/d, a ~25% increase reflecting the full-year Vital Energy contribution. The company is externally managed by KKR Energy Assets Manager LLC, which holds Non-Economic Series I Preferred Stock giving it the exclusive right to appoint all directors. This controlled-company structure is central to the valuation discount.

Market capitalization stands at approximately $3.8B, with ~328M Class A shares outstanding after the April 2025 corporate simplification eliminated the Up-C structure. The stock trades on NYSE at $12.63 as of April 16, 2026, approximately 10% below its 52-week high of $14.02.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Production

Revenue grew from $2.38B (FY2023) to $2.93B (FY2024) to $3.58B (FY2025), a 22% YoY increase in FY2025 driven almost entirely by acquisitions. Production scaled from an estimated ~175 MBoe/d (FY2023) to ~235 MBoe/d (FY2024) to 260 MBoe/d (FY2025), with 2026 guidance of 320-335 MBoe/d representing another step-change from the Vital Energy integration.

### Profitability

GAAP net income was $167M in FY2025 versus a $138M loss in FY2024. The improvement is partially obscured by $245M in non-cash equity-based compensation (primarily KKR Manager PSUs) and $137M in transaction costs. Adjusted net income of $470M ($1.80/diluted share) and adjusted EBITDAX of $2.07B (+29% YoY) provide a cleaner picture. GAAP operating margin of 6.4% understates economic profitability; the adjusted EBITDAX margin of approximately 58% is more representative of underlying operations.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow of $1.68B (+37% YoY) and levered free cash flow of $856M (+36% YoY) demonstrate strong cash conversion. Development capex was $903M, up from $745M, reflecting the larger asset base.

### Balance Sheet

Long-term debt approximately doubled from $3.05B (December 2024) to $5.52B (December 2025) following the Vital Energy merger. The debt stack includes:

- 9.250% Senior Notes due 2028: $500M
- 7.625% Senior Notes due 2032: $1.1B
- 7.375% Senior Notes due 2033: $1.0B
- 8.375% Senior Notes due 2034: $600M
- Assumed Vital Energy notes: ~$2.3B
- Revolving credit facility: $2.0B elected / $3.9B borrowing base

The weighted-average maturity is approximately 6 years, and the company reports ~$2B of liquidity after using $714M of divestiture proceeds to repay the revolver. Management's Net LTM Leverage calculation of 1.5x uses a credit-agreement-adjusted EBITDAX of $3.22B that includes pro forma contributions from acquired assets. The GAAP debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 1.07x.

Annual interest expense will run approximately $440M+ in FY2026 (FY2025 was $298M, reflecting only 16 days of Vital Energy debt). This absorbs roughly 26% of FY2025 operating cash flow, a material constraint on shareholder returns and financial flexibility.

### Capital Allocation

The dividend stands at $0.48/share annualized, yielding approximately 4.2% at current prices. The $400M buyback authorization (expanded from $150M in February 2026) is the more important shareholder return mechanism: at $12.63/share, the full authorization could retire approximately 32M shares or roughly 10% of the outstanding count. Management already repurchased $33.5M of stock in the first nine months of FY2025 at an average price of $8.21, now worth approximately 54% more. This buyback track record, combined with the levered FCF yield, suggests aggressive repurchases are likely in 2026.

## Growth Analysis

### Production Growth Through Acquisition

Crescent's growth model is explicitly acquisition-driven. The company has completed SilverBow ($1.0B, July 2024), Ridgemar ($905M, January 2025), Vital Energy ($3.1B, December 2025), and $355M in minerals acquisitions. 2026 production guidance of 320-335 MBoe/d represents a 25% increase over FY2025's 260 MBoe/d.

The synergy target from Vital Energy has been doubled from $95M to $190M annually. Management is "right-sizing capital intensity" on Permian assets by running only 1-2 rigs versus Vital's prior program, reflecting a belief that Vital was over-drilling relative to the return profile of its inventory.

### Estimate Revisions

Current-year EPS estimates rose from $1.30 to $2.03 over the past 30 days (+57%), and next-year estimates rose from $1.34 to $2.05 (+52%). Current-quarter estimates rose from $0.31 to $0.44 (+40%). The revisions reflect both the Vital Energy contribution and higher confidence in synergy realization. At $2.03 FY2026E EPS, the forward P/E is approximately 6.2x — well below the peer median of 43.4x (though that median is distorted by NOG and MUR; the more representative MGY trades at 12.7x).

### Minerals Platform

The Crescent Royalties platform represents a structural growth vector with zero drilling capital requirements. The $355M in Eagle Ford minerals acquisitions add high-margin, zero-capex revenue. As this platform scales, it should improve the overall FCF margin of the consolidated business and reduce capital intensity.

### Earnings Surprise Track Record

Three beats in four quarters, with the sole miss a marginal 2.0% shortfall in Q3 2025. The Q4 2025 beat of 41.1% ($0.49 vs. $0.35 estimate) and Q2 2025 beat of 43.0% suggest estimates are consistently too low, supporting confidence in current forward numbers.

## Valuation Assessment

At $12.63, CRGY trades at:

- **EV/EBITDA:** ~3.2x FY2025 adjusted EBITDAX (~2.8x on pro forma FY2026E with synergies)
- **Forward P/E:** ~6.2x FY2026E ($2.03 EPS)
- **Levered FCF Yield:** ~23% on FY2025 numbers
- **Price/Book:** ~0.74x ($5.2B equity / 328M shares = ~$15.85 book value)
- **Dividend Yield:** ~4.2%

Against peers: MGY at 12.7x P/E, CNX at 9.2x, and the broader peer median at 43.4x (inflated by NOG at 55x and MUR at 43x). The analyst consensus target median is $16.00, with a range of $13.00 to $19.00, implying 3-51% upside from current levels. Twelve of 15 analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy; none rate Sell.

The valuation discount is structural, driven by: (1) the KKR management fee and PSU dilution, which reduce effective shareholder cash flow by 6-7% annually; (2) the $5.5B debt load at ~8% weighted-average cost; and (3) integration risk from three major acquisitions in 18 months. These are real costs. But even adjusting for the KKR fee drag (~$79M/year in base fees plus dilution), the adjusted FCF yield remains above 15%, well above the sector average.

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) permanently restoring EBITDA-based interest deduction and 100% bonus depreciation further improves the after-tax FCF profile, particularly for a company with $5.5B of debt.

## Competitive Landscape

Crescent competes with COP and EOG in the Eagle Ford, and with MTDR and DVN in the Permian. Its differentiation comes from:

1. **Multi-basin diversification** — Eagle Ford, Permian, Uinta — reducing single-basin concentration risk
2. **Minerals/royalties platform** — zero-capex, high-margin revenue stream that most pure E&P peers lack
3. **KKR deal flow** — proprietary access to PE-backed acquisition targets and institutional capital markets relationships (the $3.9B borrowing base reflects lender confidence)
4. **Operational playbook** — applying lower capital intensity and higher-return drilling programs to acquired assets

The company is disadvantaged by its smaller scale relative to COP and EOG, its higher leverage, and the KKR fee structure that makes it more expensive to own per unit of production than comparable independents. As Tier 1 Permian inventory is drilled out, Crescent's diversified basin position and minerals exposure become relatively more valuable.

## Risk Assessment

### Commodity Price Sensitivity (High)

At sub-$60 WTI, reserves (currently priced at $65.34/Bbl, which management acknowledges is above the forward curve) would face downward revisions. The Oklahoma properties ($276M carrying value) have limited cushion and would likely trigger impairment charges. The company hedges 58% of 2026 oil production at ~$67/Bbl, providing a 12-month buffer.

### Leverage Risk (High)

In a sustained downturn ($50-55 WTI), the company would struggle to service debt, maintain the dividend, and fund development capex simultaneously. Net LTM Leverage (1.5x on management's calculation) could deteriorate to 2.0x+ rapidly if EBITDAX contracts.

### KKR Fee Structure (Moderate-High)

The ~$79M annual base fee plus the 10% PSU allocation and the escalation mechanism (1.5% of equity issuance proceeds permanently added to the base fee) represent a structural discount that cannot be eliminated without a change-of-control event or renegotiation.

### Integration Execution (Moderate)

Three major acquisitions in 18 months — SilverBow, Ridgemar, Vital Energy — each with different basin characteristics and operational cultures. The doubling of the Vital synergy target from $95M to $190M is ambitious. Permian operations are being intentionally scaled back (1-2 rigs), which either validates management discipline or risks underinvesting in the acquired asset base.

### Ridgemar Earn-Out (Low-Moderate)

Up to $170M in contingent payments if WTI averages above $70/Bbl quarterly in 2026. At current pricing (~$83 per the ceasefire environment), these payments may partially or fully trigger, creating an incremental cash drain.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 50.4% matches the 1-year historical volatility exactly, suggesting the options market views current uncertainty as normal for this stock. The put/call OI ratio of 0.25 and volume ratio of 0.40 are both strongly call-skewed, consistent with the bullish analyst consensus. However, the term structure shows mild backwardation (50.4% near-term vs. 47.4% at 3 months), with the 3-month put/call skew flipping to +8.6%, indicating institutions are buying protection at the 3-month horizon, potentially around Q1 2026 earnings or oil price uncertainty. Short interest at 13.2% of float (3.3 days to cover) reflects meaningful skepticism, likely centered on leverage and the KKR fee structure. The short position creates a technical setup where positive catalysts (synergy announcements, buyback execution, oil price strength) could drive outsized moves via covering.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($16-19, +27% to +50%)

- Oil remains above $75 through the current geopolitical environment, driving FY2026 EBITDAX toward $3.5B+
- Vital Energy synergies exceed the $190M target as Permian operations are optimized under Crescent's lower capital intensity model
- Aggressive buyback execution ($400M authorization) retires 8-10% of shares, driving per-share metrics higher
- The minerals platform scales, improving overall FCF margins and reducing capital intensity
- Valuation re-rates from ~3.2x to ~4.0x EV/EBITDA, closer to the E&P peer average, as integration risk recedes

### Bear Case ($7.50-9.00, -29% to -41%)

- Oil declines to $55-60 on ceasefire extension and OPEC+ production increases
- Reserve impairments in Oklahoma and potentially the Permian (carrying value based on $65.34/Bbl)
- Vital Energy integration takes longer and yields lower synergies than the $190M target
- Leverage moves above 2.0x, triggering credit rating pressure and higher borrowing costs
- The KKR fee escalator continues to dilute shareholder value with no governance mechanism for shareholders to push back

## Conclusion

Crescent Energy offers a deep-value proposition in the E&P sector: a 23% levered FCF yield, sharply accelerating estimates, three-basin diversification including a scaled Permian entry, and a growing minerals platform. The stock trades at 3.2x EV/EBITDA against a peer group at 4.5x, with 12 of 15 analysts rating it Buy or Strong Buy and a consensus target of $16.00.

The risks are proportional to the discount. The $5.5B debt load at ~8% cost creates significant sensitivity to oil below $60. The KKR management agreement extracts 6-7% annually through fees and PSU dilution, a structural cost that shareholders cannot control. And the company is integrating three major acquisitions simultaneously, with the $190M Vital Energy synergy target requiring rigorous execution.

The current energy environment — with oil at $83 on ceasefire dynamics, structural supply damage from Middle East infrastructure destruction, and dual waiver non-renewals constraining Iranian and Russian supply — provides a favorable backdrop for deleveraging. If oil remains above $70, Crescent's cash flow generation should allow it to reduce leverage from 1.5x toward 1.0x within 18-24 months while executing meaningful buybacks and maintaining the 4.2% dividend. The risk/reward from $12.63 favors the upside. BUY with a 12-month base case target of $15.50.
