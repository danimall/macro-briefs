**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hamilton Lane has declined approximately 40% from its December 2025 high to $81.40, while the recurring economics of the business accelerated. Fee-related earnings (FRE), the high-quality recurring core, grew 25% to $344.5M at a 50% margin; management and advisory fees grew 14% to $584.2M; operating cash flow grew 41% to $424.9M; and the management-fee rate on average fee-earning AUM rose from 0.59% in March 2022 to 0.67% in March 2026, running counter to the industry fee-compression narrative. The selloff is sector-wide, as most listed alternatives managers derated on rising long-term yields and skepticism about the private-credit and retail-democratization push, and is not driven by company-specific earnings deterioration.

The signal that tips this from a watch-list name to an actionable one is the breadth and conviction of insider buying. Between February and June 2026, six senior insiders (both Co-Chairmen, both Co-CEOs, the COO, and a director) bought stock on the open market (transaction code "P," the highest-conviction insider signal), with Executive Co-Chairman Hartley Rogers deploying over $13M at $76–$93. Insiders at a private-markets allocator have unusually good visibility into forward fundraising and NAV trends, the exact variables the bear case worries about. Their revealed preference is to buy into the decline.

At ~11x forward EPS with consensus EPS estimates roughly stable (current-year $6.10, next-year $7.40) and a PEG below 1.0, the valuation has compressed below the firm's growth rate. Every published analyst target sits above the current price, including the most bearish at $102 (+25%), with a median of $141 (+73%). The principal company-specific risk, evergreen fund redemption gates, attaches to the lumpy incentive-fee line (~23% of revenue), not the recurring management/advisory base (75%+ of revenue) that is tied to committed and invested capital rather than market NAV. I rate HLNE a BUY with disciplined position sizing, reflecting the genuine sector-level uncertainty around private markets and rate sensitivity.

## Company Overview

Hamilton Lane is one of the largest dedicated private-markets investment firms globally, with ~785 professionals and a $1.05 trillion total asset footprint as of March 31, 2026 (fiscal year ends March 31). The footprint splits into two economic engines:

- **Discretionary AUM: $141.8B** — full investment discretion, earning asset-based management fees plus carried interest. This is the high-value revenue base.
- **Non-discretionary AUA: $905.3B** — advisory assets earning fixed fees for allocation, monitoring, reporting, and manager diligence. Up 10% YoY, this book matters less for direct revenue than for the proprietary data scale and GP relationships it feeds, a genuine moat.

Management and advisory fees of $584.2M in FY26 break down as: specialized funds 64% ($374.4M, +19% YoY, the primary growth driver), customized separate accounts 24% ($141.5M, +5%), reporting/monitoring/data 8% ($35.8M, +22%), and advisory 4% ($20.5M, –10%). HLNE's structural advantages are its 30+ year track record, a proprietary fund database fed by the AUA book, deep GP relationships, and an early leadership position in evergreen (perpetual, semi-liquid) funds and private-wealth democratization.

The legal structure is an "Up-C" holding company whose only material asset is its interest in the operating entity, Hamilton Lane Advisors, LLC. It is a Nasdaq "controlled company"; Class B holders (management) carry 10 votes per share. A Tax Receivable Agreement obligates the firm to pay 85% of realized tax-basis step-up benefits to legacy members ($235M TRA payable on the balance sheet).

## Financial Analysis

| Total revenues | $713.0M | $759.0M | +6% |

| Operating cash flow | $300.8M | $424.9M | +41% |

The recurring engine is what drives value. Management and advisory fees have averaged more than 75% of total revenue over the past five years and are tied to committed/invested capital rather than market NAV, so they are largely insulated from market volatility. This is a structural advantage over traditional asset managers whose fees scale with AUM marks. Fee-earning AUM reached $81.5B (+13% YoY), with specialized-funds FEAUM up 24% to $40.6B.

The "weak" line items are deceptive. Incentive fees fell 12% and total revenue grew only 6%, but incentive fees are inherently realization-dependent and lumpy. Unrealized carried interest grew to a record $1.55B (+23% YoY) across 124 vehicles, a large, diversified store of future incentive fees not yet recognized. The recurring FRE engine grew 25%.

The balance sheet is robust: $361M cash, $278M debt (net cash position; ~0.7x gross debt/EBITDA), exceptional ROE/ROA for an asset-light model, 50% FRE margins, and 41% operating-cash-flow-to-revenue. A screening Altman Z-Score (~1.5) flags "distress," but this is a false signal: the model is calibrated for manufacturers and penalizes asset managers carrying consolidated fund investments and low tangible-asset ratios. With these margins and minimal leverage, the financial position is strong.

The four-quarter earnings record shows four consecutive beats (Q1 +27.4%, Q2 +37.0%, Q3 +14.5%, Q4 +2.2%), supporting confidence in forward consensus. Capital allocation is shareholder-aligned: the dividend was raised 11% to a $2.40 annual target, and a $100M buyback was re-authorized with the first repurchases in FY26 ($20M).

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on the recurring base, not the lumpy carry. Management and advisory fees compounding at 14%, with the highest-fee specialized-funds segment up 19% and FEAUM in that segment up 24%, indicates the mix is shifting toward higher-margin, stickier revenue. The 8bps increase in the realized fee rate (0.59%→0.67%) is the clearest evidence: HLNE is not just gathering more assets but earning more on each dollar, against a backdrop where the sector narrative is fee compression.

Evergreen funds are the dominant growth vector. Evergreen incentive fees rose 67% to $116.2M of the $174.8M total, and evergreen allocated carried interest grew 52% to $239.5M. Management guided April evergreen net inflows above $265M, signaling continued momentum into the new fiscal year. The August 2025 Executive Order opening 401(k)s to alternatives is a multi-year TAM expansion that HLNE, as an allocator and data provider rather than a single-strategy GP, is well-positioned to monetize through distribution partnerships.

On estimate revisions, consensus is roughly stable: current quarter +5.5% over 30 days (–2.0% over 90 days), current year $6.10 (+1.6% / –4.8%), next year $7.40 (–0.6% / –5.1%). The 90-day softness reflects the same sector derating that hit the stock, not a fundamental cut to the FRE trajectory; recent 30-day revisions have stabilized. This is not the +30% upward-revision profile that would warrant maximum conviction, but it is a stable-estimate base against a price that fell 40%; the revision trend has not deteriorated in step with the price. The combination of 25% FRE growth and rising fee rates against flat-to-slightly-lower estimates suggests consensus is conservatively modeling the recurring engine.

## Valuation Assessment

At $81.40 the stock trades at ~11x forward EPS (forward P/E 11.0) and 13.75x trailing, against next-year consensus EPS of $7.40 implying an ~11x forward multiple and a PEG below 1.0 on the ~14–17% recurring earnings growth rate. This is a quality compounder priced like a melting business.

Peer comparison is imperfect here: the supplied "Financial Services" comps (Klarna at negative earnings, Blue Owl at 81.5x with a 9.7% yield) are not clean read-throughs to a private-markets allocator. The more relevant cohort is listed alternatives managers (Blackstone, KKR, Apollo, StepStone, Ares), which historically trade at high-teens to low-20s forward multiples on fee-related earnings. Against that set, HLNE at 11x forward sits at a roughly 40–50% discount, despite a higher-quality, lower-beta revenue base (committed-capital fees vs. NAV-linked fees) and a 25% FRE growth rate that exceeds most peers.

I treat a low multiple skeptically as a standalone signal; cheapness alone is often a value trap. What converts it to a genuine valuation opportunity here is the presence of independent catalysts: the multi-executive insider buying cluster, the rising fee rate, the record unrealized carry backlog, and a recurring engine accelerating into the price decline. The valuation is cheap and there is a specific reason to believe the market is mispricing the recurring base by extrapolating the lumpy incentive-fee weakness and sector rate fears onto a structurally insulated fee stream.

Every published analyst target sits above the current price: low $102 (+25%), median $141 (+73%), mean $136 (+67%), high $179 (+120%). On a name trading at 11x forward with a PEG below 1.0 and rising fee rates, these targets function as floors rather than ceilings; consensus has not yet caught up to the recurring-fee durability. A reversion to even 14x forward on $7.40 next-year EPS implies ~$104, near the most bearish published target.

## Competitive Landscape

HLNE competes with traditional and alternative managers (Blackstone, KKR, Apollo, StepStone, fund-of-funds peers) for GP access, separate-account mandates, and increasingly retail distribution. Its differentiation is its solutions/advisory and data model: with a $905B AUA book feeding a proprietary database across thousands of funds, HLNE operates more as an allocator and "arms dealer" to the private-markets ecosystem than as a single-strategy GP. This makes it lower-beta to any one strategy's performance and gives it pricing leverage in mandate competition, visible in the rising fee rate.

The competitive trajectory is improving, not just stable. Specialized funds (the highest-fee segment) grew 19%, reporting/data/analytics grew 22%, and the firm holds an early leadership position in evergreen structures that peers are now racing to build. The principal competitive risk is that the larger balance-sheet managers (Blackstone, Apollo) bring more distribution muscle to the retail-democratization push; HLNE counters with its allocator-neutral positioning and data moat, which are harder to replicate than a distribution agreement.

## Risk Assessment

**Evergreen redemption risk** is the central new company-specific risk. Evergreen vehicles permit periodic investor redemptions, and the FY26 10-K materially expanded language warning that material redemption activity could force the firm to impose gates, suspend redemptions, or sell illiquid assets, with reputational and revenue consequences. This is real, but it must be sized correctly: it attaches to the incentive-fee line and the evergreen carry, which together sit in the lumpy ~23% incentive-fee portion of revenue, not the 75%+ recurring management/advisory base that drives FRE. A redemption wave would dent the lumpy line that the market already discounts; it would not impair the recurring engine on which the valuation thesis rests. This is the reconciliation the prior HOLD failed to make.

**Valuation/operational risk** rises with the push into daily-priced interval funds, which increase NAV-strike frequency and model risk; an NAV error in a daily-priced vehicle could trigger SEC scrutiny. **Non-contractual revenue** (separate accounts and advisory mandates terminable on 30–90 days' notice) applies to a portion of revenue, though retention has historically been strong. **Up-C/TRA structure** ($235M payable, change-of-control acceleration) and **controlled-company governance** (dual-class, staggered board) reduce minority-shareholder influence, a feature investors must accept.

**Rate sensitivity** is the macro risk most tied to the recent stock weakness. Higher long-term yields pressure fundraising pace, slow deal realizations, and improve the relative attractiveness of fixed income versus private markets. This is a genuine headwind to the pace of FEAUM growth and carry realization. It is not, however, a thesis-defining structural impairment: the fee base is tied to committed and invested capital, so even a slower fundraising environment compounds the recurring base from a higher level rather than shrinking it. I weight rates as a monitoring item affecting growth velocity, not as a permanent compression of the asset-gathering model. A verified easing of long-end yields would relieve the multiple directly.

Short interest is elevated at 11.8% of float (4.4 days to cover), reflecting the bearish sector positioning. This is moderate rather than extreme and creates some squeeze potential on positive fundraising data points (the June 16 insider-driven move of ~5% is a small illustration).

### Options Market Signal

The available options data is limited but informative. Between May 18 and May 19, 2026, ATM implied volatility rose from 61.4% to 66.8% (+5.4pp) on a +1.9% price move, while the put/call OI ratio fell from 3.32 to 2.54. The still-elevated put/call ratio confirms defensive positioning consistent with the short interest, but the decline in that ratio alongside rising IV suggests some unwinding of the most bearish bets. IV in the mid-60s is rich relative to a beta of 1.16, signaling the market is pricing meaningful uncertainty, consistent with a distressed entry where the borrow and hedging cost is elevated but the fundamental case is intact.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($141–$179)

The recurring engine compounds at 14–17% with rising fee rates, the sector rate fear abates as long-end yields stabilize, and evergreen flows continue above the April pace. FRE re-rates back toward the alternatives-peer cohort at 16–20x forward. On $7.40 next-year EPS at 19x, the stock reaches ~$141 (median analyst target). The $1.55B unrealized carry backlog converts over coming years, layering incentive fees on top of the recurring base. The high case ($179) requires both a full sector re-rating and acceleration in 401(k)/retail democratization flows.

### Bear Case ($60–$65)

Long-term yields grind higher, fundraising slows materially, and an evergreen redemption wave during a market drawdown forces gates, denting the incentive-fee line and damaging the firm's reputation in the channel it has bet on. Incentive fees fall further and the multiple stays compressed at 9–10x forward on cut estimates. A 9.5x multiple on a stressed $6.50 EPS implies ~$62, roughly 24% below the current price. The governance discount (controlled company, TRA acceleration) caps any takeout optionality.

The asymmetry favors the long side: ~73% to the median analyst target versus ~24% downside to the bear case, with the recurring base providing a floor on earnings that the bear scenario does not fully account for.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $122** — 16.5x forward on $7.40 FY-next EPS, a multiple that still discounts HLNE versus the alternatives peer cohort to account for genuine sector and rate uncertainty. This sits between the bear ($62) and bull ($141–179) cases. Upside of ~50% from $81.40.

**Bull case target: $141–$179** — full re-rating to peer multiples plus evergreen/retail acceleration. Upside of ~73% to ~120%.

**Bear case target: $62** — ~24% downside, on a redemption-gate event plus sustained rate pressure cutting estimates and holding the multiple at ~9.5x.

**Timeframe: 12–18 months**, tied to: the next two fiscal-quarter prints (FRE and management-fee growth, plus monthly evergreen net-flow disclosures), the trajectory of long-term Treasury yields, and progress on 401(k)/retail distribution. The next earnings report is the first natural checkpoint.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Management/advisory fee growth decelerates below ~8% YoY for two consecutive quarters (signals the recurring engine is breaking, not just the lumpy carry).
2. Evergreen net flows turn negative or HLNE imposes redemption gates on any flagship evergreen vehicle (the central risk materializing).
3. The management-fee rate on average FEAUM reverses below ~0.62%, indicating the mix-shift pricing tailwind has stalled.

## Conclusion

HLNE is a high-quality, asset-light private-markets allocator whose recurring fee engine accelerated even as the stock fell ~40% in a sector-wide rate-driven derating. At 11x forward earnings with a PEG below 1.0, every published analyst target above the current price, and a broad multi-executive open-market insider buying cluster (~$18M+, including over $13M from the Co-Chairman) into the decline, the risk/reward is constructively skewed. The principal company-specific risk, evergreen redemption gates, attaches to the lumpy ~23% incentive-fee line, not the structurally insulated recurring base that drives the valuation. Insiders with the best visibility into forward fundraising are buying the exact uncertainty the bears cite.

I rate HLNE a BUY. The position should be sized with discipline: this is a Financial Services name where the broader sector rate and private-credit concerns are genuine, and the macro backdrop (elevated long-end yields, private-credit redemption pressure across the alternatives complex) is a real headwind to the pace of growth. The case is a distressed-valuation entry on durable cash flow with strong insider alignment, not a structurally impaired business, and the catalysts that distinguish it (the insider cluster, the recurring-FRE acceleration, and the rising fee rate) are company-specific rather than borrowed sector beta.
