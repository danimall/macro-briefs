## Executive Summary

AECOM is the world's largest architectural and engineering design firm, ranked #1 globally in six major infrastructure end markets. The stock has declined 47% from its November 2025 high of $135.52 to $71.28, driven primarily by a Q2 FY2026 (reported May 11, 2026) cash flow miss — H1 operating cash flow collapsed to $74M vs. $342M in the prior year period. The market has repriced ACM as though the business is fundamentally impaired.

Net service revenue grew approximately 4% as reported (2% constant currency) in Q2 FY2026, with the Americas design business growing 8% constant currency and specific subsegments like Americas Transportation +11.6% and Water/Environment +9.0%. Gross margins expanded to 7.7-7.8% from 6.1% two years ago. Adjusted EPS beat estimates in three of the last four quarters. The cash flow shortfall traces to a specific, identifiable cause: contract asset claims ballooned from ~$400M to ~$680M due to delayed resolution timelines. Historical patterns show AECOM's cash flow is heavily H2-weighted.

At $71.28, the stock trades at approximately 11.9x forward FY2027 consensus EPS of ~$6.69 — a substantial discount to infrastructure engineering peers and the company's own historical range. The CFO purchased $101K in open-market shares at $71.12 on May 14, three days after the earnings report, and the CEO reportedly purchased approximately $300K in open-market shares at $71.02 on the same day. These are discretionary purchases at effectively the current price.

## Company Overview

AECOM provides professional infrastructure consulting services — design, engineering, planning, program management, and construction management — across transportation, water, environment, facilities, and energy end markets. Revenue splits approximately 50/50 between government and private clients, with 78% from the Americas and 22% international.

The company operates an asset-light model requiring minimal capex ($137M in FY2025, <1% of revenue). Its primary asset is the expertise of its 51,000 employees. The business generates revenue by billing clients for professional time, with pass-through revenue (subcontractor costs) representing roughly half of reported revenue but contributing no margin. This distinction matters: headline revenue trends obscure underlying business momentum.

Backlog stood at $39.7B as of September 2025 (+6.1% YoY), though the most recently reported total backlog was $26.2B at March 31, 2026. The difference reflects in part AECOM's backlog definition, which includes gross revenue (with substantial pass-through content) and awarded-but-not-yet-signed work. Remaining unsatisfied performance obligations (RUPO) were $19.7B at FY2025 year-end, with approximately 60% expected to be recognized within 12 months. The company's 10-K cautions that there is no assurance the full backlog will be realized and that backlog differs materially from RUPO due to pass-through content and termination provisions.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Margin Trajectory:**

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 FY2026 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|------------|
| Revenue | $13.15B | $14.38B | $16.11B | $16.14B | $7.63B |
| Gross Margin | 6.4% | 6.6% | 6.7% | 7.5% | 7.7% |
| Adjusted EPS | $3.40 | $3.71 | $4.52 | $5.26 | $3.18 (est.) |

FY2025 headline revenue was flat because pass-through revenue declined (53% → 50% mix). The underlying net service revenue grew meaningfully. This pattern continued in H1 FY2026 — the margin expansion from 6.4% to 7.7% over three years reflects genuine operational improvement, not revenue mix gimmickry.

**Cash Flow — The Core Controversy:**

H1 FY2026 operating cash flow: $74M vs. $342M prior year. Free cash flow was only $14.5M vs. $289.4M prior year. The deterioration traces to:
- DSO increased from 74 to 83 days ($425M working capital absorption)
- Contract asset claims ballooned from ~$400M to ~$680M
- Management cited "longer than anticipated claims resolution process" and delayed Middle East payment timing

Management has reiterated full-year FY2026 FCF guidance of approximately $400M, which would require roughly $385M in H2 FCF — a meaningful recovery but below FY2025's full-year FCF of approximately $685M. AECOM's historical cash flow pattern concentrates in fiscal H2 (April-September), supporting the recovery thesis, though the magnitude of recovery needed remains substantial. The claims represent revenue already recognized under ASC 606 and are recorded as contract assets and other non-current assets — these are not unconditional billed receivables but rather amounts subject to claim resolution processes. While AECOM records claims only when it is probable they will result in additional contract revenue and the amount can be reliably estimated, the timing and ultimate collection amount carry meaningful uncertainty.

**Capital Allocation:**

$444M in share repurchases in H1 FY2026 (5% of current market cap in 6 months). At an average repurchase price of ~$95-100, these were executed well above current levels but at reasonable valuations. $884M remains on the authorization. Annual dividend of $1.24/share provides 1.7% yield.

**Balance Sheet:**

Net debt of ~$1.72B (total debt $2.75B, cash $1.03B). Leverage ratio within the 4.0x covenant. The March 2026 credit amendment extended maturity to 2031 and reduced spreads — banks affirming credit quality during the stock's decline. Total debt-to-equity is approximately 1.2x ($2.75B debt / $2.27B stockholders' equity), though total liabilities-to-equity is significantly higher at approximately 4.2x given the company's substantial operating liabilities. Leverage is manageable for a professional services firm with predictable cash flows and minimal asset intensity.

## Growth Analysis

AECOM sits at the intersection of five secular demand drivers, each independently funded and politically durable:

1. **U.S. IIJA spending** — multi-year federal infrastructure program with funds still being deployed through 2030+
2. **Data center buildout** — driving facilities design, power infrastructure, and water cooling demand
3. **National defense acceleration** — rapid acceleration in national defense spending globally, with bipartisan support
4. **Water infrastructure crisis** — PFAS remediation, drought mitigation, flood resilience
5. **Energy transition** — transmission, grid modernization, renewables permitting

Consensus estimates project FY2026E EPS of approximately $5.99 (+13.9% YoY), within management's guided range of $5.90–$6.10, and FY2027E EPS of approximately $6.69 (+11.7%) per consensus aggregator data. These estimates have been modestly rising: +0.7% over the past 30 days for FY2026, +1.2% for FY2027. Estimates are not declining despite the stock's 47% drawdown, confirming that the sell-off is a multiple compression event rather than a fundamental deterioration event.

Management's margin expansion trajectory (6.1% → 7.7% and likely heading toward 8-9%) creates embedded earnings growth even at flat revenue. Every 100bps of margin expansion on $16B revenue = ~$160M in additional gross profit, or approximately $0.90/share after tax.

The valuation is unambiguously cheap relative to: (a) peers, (b) the company's own history, (c) growth rate, and (d) the quality of the business.

## Competitive Landscape

AECOM holds the #1 ranking in six of the largest infrastructure design categories globally. Scale advantages are meaningful in this industry:
- Ability to invest in AI/digital capabilities that smaller firms cannot afford
- Cross-selling across end markets (a transportation client can also become a water and facilities client)
- Government prequalification requirements favor firms with demonstrated global capacity
- Multi-decade client relationships with repeat business

The company's deliberate exit from self-perform construction (2020-2022) repositioned the business toward higher-margin advisory work with lower risk. Construction management was retained after strategic review, reflecting strong data center demand.

Competition from Jacobs, WSP, Fluor, and ATKNS exists but the market is fragmented enough that AECOM's #1 position in multiple categories provides differentiated win rates on large, complex programs.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Cash Flow Recovery Failure / Claims Resolution Risk (Primary Risk):** If the ~$680M in contract asset claims proves partially uncollectible or resolution extends well beyond FY2026, the cash flow thesis breaks. If H2 recovery falls short of the ~$385M needed, it would signal a more structural issue and could force further multiple compression. **Probability: 20-25%.** Mitigant: AECOM records claims only when probable of collection; approximately 50% of total revenue is from government clients, though the specific client composition of the $680M claims balance is not disclosed in filings.

**2. Legacy Discontinued Operations:** The DOE project and refinery litigation continue generating unpredictable losses ($70M in H1 FY2026). These are not in adjusted EPS but consume real cash. Further adverse rulings could total $50-100M over the next 2 years. Largely priced at current levels but creates noise.

**3. Government Spending Risk:** 50% government revenue creates sensitivity to budget sequestration, shutdowns, or policy changes. However, IIJA funds are already appropriated and flow through multi-year state DOT programs, insulating AECOM from near-term federal budget dynamics.

**4. Interest Expense Burden:** With $2.75B in total debt, AECOM faces ~$184M in annual interest expense. The $1.2B of Senior Notes at 6% fixed through 2033 represents elevated cost locked in. A 1% rate increase on floating-rate debt adds ~$10.8M in annual interest expense. The March 2026 amendment locked much of the debt through 2031, but at current FCF levels, interest coverage remains adequate but tight.

**5. International Softness:** Asia and Middle East project delays dragged H1 results. International segment gross profit declined 7.3% in H1 FY2026. If global recession materializes, international revenue (~22%) faces further pressure.

**6. IRS Tax Audit:** $81.4M reserved for uncertain tax positions. Resolution could require additional cash outflows, though reserves may prove adequate.

## Conclusion

ACM presents a high-conviction opportunity to buy the world's #1 infrastructure design firm at a substantial discount to peer multiples following a working capital dislocation. The insider purchases at the current price within days of the disappointing earnings report signal that those closest to the situation view the cash flow miss as timing-driven.

The business fundamentals remain strong: net service revenue growing, margins expanding, three earnings beats in the last four quarters, strong backlog positioning, and exposure across five secular growth vectors. At approximately 11.9x FY2027 EPS with consensus expecting 12% growth, the risk/reward is asymmetrically favorable. The primary risk — that claims prove uncollectible or resolution timelines extend significantly — is real and should be monitored, but management's willingness to invest personal capital at this price and the reiteration of full-year FCF guidance provide some reassurance.

The stock requires patience (9-12 month horizon for full re-rating) and acceptance of near-term volatility, but the valuation provides substantial margin of safety.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The latest reported backlog ($26.2B at March 31, 2026 vs. $39.7B cited from September 2025), the distinction between claims-based contract assets and unconditional receivables, FY2026 FCF guidance of ~$400M (below FY2025's ~$685M), and consolidated NSR growth of ~4% (not high-single-digits) collectively suggest the recovery thesis carries more uncertainty than the report's tone implies. The recommendation is contingent on H2 FY2026 cash-flow conversion and claims resolution progress.
