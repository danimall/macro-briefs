## Executive Summary

Chart Industries is a $9.9B industrial equipment manufacturer being acquired by Baker Hughes in an all-cash transaction announced July 2025, with closing expected Q2 2026. The stock trades at $207.19, above the analyst consensus mean target of $204.43, indicating the market has already priced in successful deal completion with minimal spread remaining. The fundamental business is irrelevant to the near-term investment case; this is purely a merger arbitrage situation.

The risk profile is unfavorable. GTLS has missed earnings estimates in all 4 reported quarters, current-year EPS estimates have been cut 42% over 90 days (from $12.99 to $7.57), the company carries $3.7B in debt with an Altman Z-Score of 1.41 (distress zone), and 11.8% of the float is sold short. International regulatory approvals remain pending. If the deal breaks, the standalone equity value is substantially lower given $3.7B in debt, no permanent CEO, and operating restrictions that have limited its competitive response during the pendency period.

## Company Overview

Chart Industries designs and manufactures process technologies and equipment for gas and liquid molecule handling across four segments: Repair, Service & Leasing (31% of sales), Heat Transfer Systems (29%), Specialty Products (26%), and Cryo Tank Solutions (15%). The company serves LNG infrastructure, hydrogen, carbon capture, data center cooling, nuclear, and water treatment end markets from 62 global manufacturing locations.

The $4.4B Howden acquisition in March 2023 doubled revenue. In June 2025, Chart agreed to merge with Flowserve, then broke that deal less than two months later to accept Baker Hughes' higher offer, paying Flowserve a $266M termination fee. Stockholders approved the Baker Hughes deal in October 2025, and the HSR antitrust waiting period expired in November 2025. CEO Jillian Evanko resigned in January 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY2025 revenue was $4,264M (+2.5% YoY), with growth driven entirely by Heat Transfer Systems (+19.5%) while other segments declined.

**Profitability:** Reported net income collapsed 81% to $40.7M, primarily due to the $266M Flowserve termination fee and $22.8M in merger-related costs. Excluding these items, underlying operating performance was stable — gross margin expanded slightly to 33.7%. Heat Transfer Systems operating margins surged from 22.5% to 29.4%.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $3,656M yields a D/E ratio of 1.99x. Interest expense of $307.8M (7.2% of sales) consumes the majority of operating cash flow. The 9.5% unsecured notes due 2031 are particularly expensive. Altman Z-Score of 1.41 places the company in the distress zone on a standalone basis.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow declined to $292.7M from $503M in 2024 due to working capital timing. FCF was $202.8M (4.8% margin). Cash on hand of $366M provides limited cushion relative to the debt load.

**Backlog:** Total backlog of $5,886M (+21.5% YoY) with a 1.33x book-to-bill ratio provides strong forward visibility for Baker Hughes post-close. FY2025 orders of $5,678M were up 13.4%.

## Growth Analysis

Growth analysis is largely moot given the pending acquisition, but the underlying business trajectory is relevant to deal-break scenarios:

- Specialty Products backlog surged 42% YoY driven by carbon capture, nuclear, and data center demand
- Heat Transfer Systems benefiting from LNG infrastructure buildout
- Theodore, Alabama facility startup costs depressing current Specialty Products margins but positioned for future improvement
- $5.9B backlog (1.38x annual revenue) provides multi-year visibility

However, forward EPS estimates have collapsed: current-year consensus fell from $12.99 to $7.57 (-42%) over 90 days, and next-year estimates declined from $14.36 to $11.10 (-23%). The most recent quarter produced a -117% EPS surprise (estimate $2.03 vs. actual -$0.35). These revisions suggest fundamental earnings power deterioration beyond one-time merger costs.

## Valuation Assessment

The stock trades at $207.19 against:
- Analyst consensus mean: $204.43
- Analyst consensus median: $210.00
- Analyst low target: $169.00
- Analyst high target: $212.00

The trailing P/E of -202x (reflecting EPS of $0.33 distorted by merger costs) is meaningless. The forward P/E is similarly distorted by the pending acquisition. What matters is the deal price versus current trading level.

At $207.19, the stock trades *above* the analyst consensus mean and within $5 of the high target at $212. All 7 covering analysts rate GTLS as HOLD — none have Buy ratings. This implies the deal consideration is approximately $207-212 per share, leaving 0-2% upside to completion versus 19-30% downside to the $145-170 range if the deal breaks (based on the analyst low of $169 and the stock's trading range of $140-155 in June 2025 before the Baker Hughes bid).

**Peer comparison:** Peers trade at a median P/E of 27.9x. On forward EPS of $7.57, Chart would trade at approximately $211 at that multiple — but this comparison is distorted by the merger premium embedded in both the EPS path and the multiple.

The risk/reward is clearly asymmetric and unfavorable: ~2% upside versus ~20-30% downside.

## Competitive Landscape

Chart holds strong competitive positions in niche markets:
- Only a "small number of global manufacturers" compete in brazed aluminum heat exchangers
- Global scale versus regionally-focused cryo tank competitors
- 160+ years of hydrogen equipment heritage
- Howden acquisition added rotating equipment, broadening the moat

Baker Hughes' willingness to pay a premium (driving Chart to break a prior merger agreement and pay $266M) and expected $325M in synergies confirm the strategic value of Chart's asset base. The competitive position is a strength, but it benefits Baker Hughes shareholders post-close, not current GTLS holders buying at $207.

## Risk Assessment

**Deal Break Risk (Primary):** International regulatory approvals remain pending. While HSR cleared and stockholder approval was obtained, deals can fail on international competition grounds. If the deal breaks, Chart's termination exposure could be up to $508M (its own termination fee to BKR + Flowserve reimbursement). The standalone entity would face no permanent CEO and impaired credibility.

**Leverage Risk (If Deal Breaks):** Interest coverage of ~2.0x (adjusted) provides minimal margin for error.

**Earnings Deterioration:** Four consecutive quarterly misses. Q1 2026 reported EPS of -$0.35 versus $2.03 expected. Even accounting for merger-related costs, the magnitude of the miss suggests operational challenges.

**Short Interest:** 11.8% of float sold short with 3.8 days to cover represents elevated bearish institutional positioning. This could reflect skepticism about deal completion or positioning for deal-break downside.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 4.4% is extraordinarily low relative to 1-year historical volatility of 24.2%. The IV percentile is 9th — meaning options are pricing almost no uncertainty. This is consistent with a near-certain deal close, with the stock pinned near the deal price. The 3-month IV of 7.0% (with a 5.3% implied move) suggests some uncertainty around the expected Q2 close date. Put/call skew of 3.7% shows modest put demand. The flat term structure and compressed IV suggest the market assigns high probability to deal completion within 2-3 months.

The disconnect between minimal implied vol and 11.8% short interest suggests shorts may be positioned for a tail event (deal break) that the options market is underpricing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case:** Baker Hughes deal closes as expected in Q2 2026, shareholders receive ~$207-212 per share. Upside of approximately 0-2% from current levels, earned over 1-3 months. The record backlog and strong competitive position ensure no material adverse change triggers.

**Bear Case:** International regulatory conditions require divestitures that cause Chart or Baker Hughes to walk away. Alternatively, geopolitical disruption (Middle East conflict affecting Chart's operations) triggers a material adverse change clause. In either scenario, the stock returns to standalone value of $140-170, a decline of 18-32%. The company would face potential $508M termination liability, no permanent CEO, and 4 consecutive earnings misses with collapsing estimates.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** International regulatory decision (timing uncertain but expected within 1-3 months). If conditions are imposed that either party deems unacceptable, the deal fails. Additionally, any material adverse change to Chart's business is non-trivial given consecutive earnings misses and collapsing estimates.

**Timing window:** Q2 2026 — the company's stated closing expectation. The catalyst resolves within 1-3 months.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 11.8% SI with 3.8 days to cover is elevated but not extreme. Institutional ownership is high. The stock is pinned near deal price with minimal daily volatility (daily range of $0.20-0.50), limiting squeeze dynamics. Deal-arb flows provide natural selling pressure that keeps the stock near deal value.

**Borrow and options signal:** IV at 4.4% (9th percentile) makes put purchases extremely cheap. Buying puts is the preferred expression over shorting stock in a merger-arb situation because the deal close creates a known ceiling. The low cost of protection favors defined-risk bearish positions.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Deal break → $145-170 (based on June 2025 pre-bid trading and analyst low of $169). Upside risk: deal closes at ~$210 = $3 loss. Downside to $160 on deal break = $47 gain. Risk/reward approximately 15:1 on the tail event.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE (stock short) / CHEAP PUT OPPORTUNITY.** The expected value of a directional short is negative because deal close probability is high (>85%). However, the extremely cheap IV (4.4% vs. 24.2% HV) makes OTM puts an exceptional risk/reward tail hedge. For investors seeking deal-break protection or speculative asymmetry, June-July puts at $180-190 strikes offer outsized payoff relative to premium. Not recommending an outright short because the base case is deal completion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** This stock becomes interesting only if: (a) the Baker Hughes deal breaks and the stock falls below $150, at which point the fundamental business ($5.9B backlog, niche competitive positions, secular energy transition demand) would be deeply undervalued relative to normalized earnings; or (b) the deal closes and the position is moot.

**Timeline:** Headwinds persist until deal resolution, expected within 1-3 months. If the deal closes, the investment opportunity disappears. If it breaks, a 3-6 month recovery period would be needed before reassessing.

## Conclusion

GTLS at $207.19 offers approximately 0-2% upside to deal completion versus 18-32% downside on deal failure. All 7 covering analysts rate it HOLD. The extremely compressed options IV (4.4% vs. 24.2% HV) means the market is pricing near-certainty of deal close, creating cheap asymmetric protection for anyone wanting to bet on the tail.

For new capital, there is no reason to own this stock. The upside is capped at the deal price, the downside on deal failure is severe, and the time value of money is better deployed elsewhere. Current holders should understand they are running a merger-arb position with unfavorable entry price (above consensus), not an investment in Chart Industries' fundamentals.
