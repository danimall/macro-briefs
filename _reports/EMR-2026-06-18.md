**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Emerson has completed a multi-year transformation from a diversified conglomerate into a pure-play industrial automation and software company. The company divested Copeland/Climate Technologies (a $14B Blackstone transaction with a ~$10.6B pretax gain) and InSinkErator, and redeployed capital into National Instruments ($8.2B) and the full takeout of AspenTech (~$7.2B, closed March 2025). The result is a higher-margin portfolio: gross margin expanded 380bps over two years to 52.8% in FY2025, adjusted EBITA margin reached 26.0%, and free cash flow hit 18% of sales. Adjusted diluted EPS rose from $4.44 (FY23) to $6.00 (FY25), a 35% increase, and management has guided FY2026 adjusted EPS to $6.45–$6.55 with raised conviction after the Q2 report.

The fundamental story is sound, but the near-term setup is modest. Underlying organic growth was only +0.5% in Q2 FY2026, depressed by transient drags (AspenTech renewal timing ~-2pts, Middle East ~-1pt) but masking a mixed end-market backdrop: U.S. underlying +9% offset by Europe -4% and China -9% to -13%. Estimate revisions have been flat across all horizons (current-year EPS $6.51, essentially unchanged over 90 days), and the company has missed consensus in three of the last four quarters, albeit by pennies. The setup does not warrant a cycle-adjusted valuation premium.

At $149 (recent close), Emerson trades at ~23x the midpoint of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance (~$6.50) and ~37x trailing GAAP EPS, the latter distorted by ~$1.35–1.42/share of non-cash acquisition amortization. The stock sits in the middle of its 52-week range ($122.6–$165.2) and has appreciated ~17% over the trailing year. The valuation is reasonable for the quality but not asymmetrically cheap, and there is no dated catalyst to force a re-rating. We maintain a HOLD with a constructive lean, classified as a growth hold on the secular automation/AI thesis playing out over a multi-year horizon.

## Company Overview

Emerson reports on a September fiscal year and now operates five segments grouped into three platforms:

- **Software & Systems (~33% of sales):** Control Systems & Software (DeltaV, Ovation, plus AspenTech) and Test & Measurement (NI). Highest margin (~30% adjusted EBITA), the strategic centerpiece.
- **Intelligent Devices (~55%):** Sensors (Rosemount, Micro Motion) and Final Control (Fisher, ASCO, Bettis valves/actuators). The cash-generative core, benefiting from power and LNG demand.
- **Safety & Productivity (~12%):** mechanical/electrical tools and diagnostics.

International sales are ~59% of total, with emerging markets exceeding one-third. The competitive moat rests on installed-base lock-in (control systems and instrumentation are embedded in customer process plants and carry high switching costs), supplemented by trusted brands and an increasingly integrated hardware-software-AI stack. Backlog was ~$9.5B as of March 31, 2026, with roughly 75% expected to convert within 12 months.

The strategic logic of the transformation is to control the data layer from sensor (Rosemount) through control system (DeltaV/Ovation) to enterprise optimization (AspenTech), positioning Emerson to monetize an "automation + software + AI" value proposition against ABB, Siemens, Honeywell, Schneider Electric, Rockwell, and AVEVA.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability trajectory ($M):**

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | 15,165 | 17,492 | 18,016 (+3%) |
| Gross margin | 49.0% | 50.8% | 52.8% |
| Adj. EBITA margin | 23.4% | 24.7% | 26.0% |
| Adj. dil. EPS (cont. ops) | $4.44 | $5.49 | $6.00 |
| Operating cash flow | 2,710 | 3,317 | 3,676 |
| Free cash flow | 2,347 | 2,898 | 3,245 |

The margin trajectory is the cleanest signal of business-mix improvement: the gross margin and EBITA gains reflect the shift toward higher-value software and instrumentation. FCF conversion is high-quality. GAAP figures are distorted by transformation noise: FY2023's $13.2B net income included the ~$8.4B after-tax Copeland gain and ~$2.1B InSinkErator gain, so adjusted EPS and FCF are the right anchors.

**Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended 3/31/2026):** Revenue of $4.56B (+3% reported, +0.5% underlying); adjusted diluted EPS $1.54 (+4%, a $0.01 miss vs. $1.55 estimate). Test & Measurement rebounded +12% underlying on aerospace/defense and semiconductor strength.

**Balance sheet — a deliberate read past the screen.** A naïve credit screen flags Emerson as distressed (Altman Z 1.36, current ratio 0.88, D/E 2.07), but this is a false alarm for two mechanical reasons. First, ~$27.6B of goodwill and intangibles from the NI/AspenTech deals inflates the asset base, dragging EBIT/assets ratios. Second, a temporary ~$4.2B commercial-paper spike to fund the AspenTech buyout loaded current liabilities (short-term debt jumped from $0.5B to $4.8B), pushing working capital negative. Total debt rose to ~$13.1B (debt/capital 39%, net debt/net capital 36%). Interest coverage is healthy at ~8.6x GAAP (~11.6x adjusted), ratings are investment-grade, and the Q2 10-Q already shows active deleveraging: long-term debt fell from $8.3B to $7.6B, and €500M of notes were repaid. This is a discretionary-acquisition leverage event, not financial distress.

**Capital allocation:** The dividend was raised to $2.22/share annualized in November 2025, extending a ~68-year increase streak (Dividend Aristocrat). FY2026 plan: ~$2.2B returned (~$1.0B buybacks + ~$1.2B dividends), with a 50M-share authorization added in November 2025. Current yield is ~1.5%.

**Earnings quality:** The gap between GAAP EPS (~$4.04 FY25) and adjusted EPS ($6.00) is structural — intangible amortization runs ~$1.35–1.42/share. This is non-cash but real; it reflects the price paid for NI and AspenTech. Investors should anchor on adjusted EPS and FCF while recognizing the amortization is a genuine economic cost of the deals.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is durable but mid-single-digit. FY2026 guidance calls for ~4.5% net sales growth, ~3% underlying, and adjusted EPS of $6.45–$6.55 (~8% growth at the midpoint, with margin expansion and buybacks contributing). The secular tailwinds cited in the 10-K and corroborated by current demand are broad-based: electrification, LNG and energy security, power generation including nuclear, reshoring, life sciences, and decarbonization. Final Control is seeing direct benefit from power and LNG capex; Test & Measurement is recovering on aerospace/defense and semiconductor demand.

The industrial-AI narrative provides the optionality that distinguishes Emerson from a legacy industrial. AspenTech is now 100% Emerson-owned, and its AVA industrial-AI agent (launched May 2026), the Aramco digital corrosion-management collaboration, and the SiMa.ai edge-AI silicon partnership embed a higher-multiple software story inside the wrapper. Emerson owns the IP; SiMa.ai is a chip supplier and Aramco a co-development customer.

Two cautions temper the growth score. First, estimate revisions are flat — current-year consensus EPS is essentially unchanged over 30 and 90 days, and next-year ($7.17) shows no meaningful upward revision. This is a steady, well-modeled franchise rather than an underestimated inflection. Second, the AspenTech term-license model makes Software & Systems revenue and margin structurally lumpy (a ~2.5–3pt EBITA swing in Q2), which will recur as noise. The mixed geography (strong Americas, weak China and Europe) also caps the near-term underlying growth ceiling.

## Valuation Assessment

At $149, Emerson trades at ~23x the FY2026 adjusted EPS midpoint (~$6.50) and ~21x the FY2027 consensus ($7.17). On trailing GAAP EPS of $4.03 the multiple is ~37x, but that figure is depressed by amortization and is the wrong denominator.

**Peer context.** Against the Electrical Components & Equipment cohort — AME at 34.2x, ROK at 47.5x, ETN at 38.5x, GNRC at 80.4x, peer median 47.5x — Emerson screens cheaper on a GAAP P/E basis. But this comparison is partly an artifact: those peers' P/Es are reported on GAAP earnings less burdened by acquisition amortization, and ROK/GNRC/ETN carry higher organic growth and cleaner re-rating stories tied to data-center power and electrification. On an adjusted forward basis (~23x), Emerson sits below the cohort but for defensible reasons: slower underlying organic growth (~3%) and a lumpier earnings stream.

The valuation is fair, not asymmetrically mispriced. The dividend yield (~1.5%) provides modest support, FCF yield (~3.8% on ~$3.2B against an ~$85B cap) is adequate but not compelling, and there is no dated catalyst to compress the gap to peers. Consensus targets (mean $163, median $164, high $203, low $104) imply ~10% upside to the median, a reasonable total-return setup but not an aggressive entry. We assign a 5.0 — the stock is appropriately priced for its quality and growth, with neither a clear value trap nor an identified mispricing.

One unmodeled potential upside: the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA tariffs were unauthorized, plus the April 2026 CBP refund portal, means Emerson may be owed refunds on tariffs already paid (tariffs cost ~0.8pts of Q2 gross margin). No refund benefit was booked as of 3/31/2026. The amount and timing are uncertain, so we do not capitalize it, but it is a small option on the upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Emerson's competitive position is stable-to-improving. The installed-base lock-in in process control and instrumentation is a genuine moat — DeltaV/Ovation systems and Rosemount/Fisher hardware are embedded in customer plants with high switching costs and long replacement cycles. The AspenTech integration deepens this by adding the enterprise-optimization layer, allowing Emerson to sell an integrated data stack rather than discrete components.

The trajectory evidence supports a modestly positive read: gross margin is expanding faster than most peers, Test & Measurement is gaining share in aerospace/defense and semiconductor test, and the industrial-AI product velocity (AVA, edge-AI hardware, named collaborations) is genuine. Against ABB, Siemens, Honeywell, Schneider, and Rockwell, Emerson is competing from a position of scale in process automation where it is a leader, and from a challenger position in software where AspenTech gives it differentiated assets.

The risk to the moat is that several cited tailwinds (hydrogen, carbon capture, advanced grid management) depend on technologies not yet widely adopted, where Emerson must keep pace with well-capitalized rivals. Discrete-automation softness and Chinese weakness also pressure the near-term competitive read in specific geographies.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs and trade policy (most material, evolving).** Q2 gross margin took an ~0.8pt tariff hit, offset by price actions but margin-dilutive. The IEEPA ruling creates both a potential refund upside and a reminder of how exposed the cost structure is to a volatile trade regime.

**AspenTech renewal-timing volatility.** Upfront term-license recognition makes Software & Systems lumpy quarter to quarter, a recurring source of optical noise that obscures underlying demand and complicates the earnings narrative.

**Geopolitical and end-market concentration.** ~59% international exposure, China down 7–13%, Europe soft, Middle East cutting ~1% from sales. FX and disruption risk are amplified by the emerging-market footprint.

**Goodwill/intangible impairment.** The $27.6B carrying value is large; a sustained process/automation downturn could trigger charges, though end-market demand currently argues against this.

**Integration execution.** The bull case depends on realizing NI and AspenTech synergies; the 10-K acknowledges the narrower business "may encounter more volatility and be more vulnerable to changing market conditions."

These risks are largely known and reasonably priced. The most underappreciated is the structural lumpiness of software revenue, which could cause a sentiment-driven drawdown on a single soft renewal quarter despite intact demand.

### Options Market Signal

The put/call skew of 4.5% shows modestly elevated put demand, and the put/call volume ratio (0.41) and OI ratio (0.12) are call-heavy on open interest, consistent with neutral-to-mildly-constructive positioning. Net read: the options market is not flagging an imminent dislocation, consistent with the HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$185–203):** The automation + software + AI integration compounds — AspenTech and NI synergies lift Software & Systems margins, the AI product suite (AVA, edge-AI) converts to recurring revenue, and LNG/power/reshoring capex sustains Intelligent Devices growth above the modeled 3% underlying. Margin expansion continues toward high-20s adjusted EBITA, FCF compounds at high-single-digits, and the multiple re-rates toward the peer cohort (high-20s forward adjusted) as the software mix becomes more visible. A material IEEPA tariff refund provides incremental upside. Apply ~27x forward to ~$7.20+ FY27 EPS → ~$195+.

**Bear Case (~$104–115, roughly -23% to -30% from current):** China and Europe weakness deepens into a broader process-automation downcycle, software-renewal timing produces a string of soft Software & Systems quarters, and the AI narrative is slower to monetize than hoped. Underlying growth stalls near 0–1%, margins plateau, and the multiple compresses to ~16–17x forward adjusted EPS on disappointed expectations. The $27.6B intangible base raises impairment risk in a downturn. This maps to the analyst low of $104.

The balance is roughly symmetric around a base case of high-single-digit total return. The franchise quality and deleveraging trajectory limit the probability of the deep bear, while the lack of an organic-growth acceleration or estimate-revision catalyst limits the probability of the full bull.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a constructive lean, classified as a growth hold on a 12–18 month horizon tied to the automation/AI thesis maturing.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Underlying organic growth re-accelerates above ~5% with positive EPS estimate revisions exceeding the flat trend (e.g., current-year consensus moving above $6.70 on rising estimates).
- A pullback toward the low-$130s (~20x forward adjusted EPS) that widens the discount to peers without fundamental deterioration.
- A confirmed, quantified IEEPA tariff refund or evidence that AspenTech AI products are converting to material recurring revenue.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A China/Europe-led process-automation downcycle pushing underlying growth toward zero for two consecutive quarters.
- A goodwill/intangible impairment charge or stalled deleveraging (net debt/net capital rising rather than falling).
- A clustered insider-selling pattern alongside guidance cuts, or an adverse outcome from the watch-worthy May 2026 executive departure.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 FY2026 earnings report (~early August 2026), with the FY2026 guidance update and any tariff-refund recognition as the key items to monitor.

## Conclusion

Emerson is a high-quality industrial automation and software franchise that has successfully repositioned toward higher-margin, higher-growth markets. The margin expansion, strong FCF generation, durable moat, and credible industrial-AI optionality are all genuine. The trajectory of declining composite scores in prior reviews reflects a fair-value reset rather than fundamental deterioration — the franchise has not weakened. But the stock is fairly priced at ~23x forward adjusted EPS, underlying organic growth is mid-single-digit and lumpy, estimate revisions are flat, and there is no dated catalyst to force a re-rating. Management's capital allocation is disciplined, but the absence of any insider buying at current levels and routine officer sales offer no positive confirmation. We maintain HOLD, holding for high-single-digit total return and accumulating on weakness toward the low-$130s rather than chasing at the middle of the range.
