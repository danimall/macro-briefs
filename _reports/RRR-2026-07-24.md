**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Red Rock Resorts is a pure-play operator of "locals" casinos in the Las Vegas regional market, holding a ~59% economic interest in Station Casinos LLC. It is the clear leader in a structurally advantaged niche: Nevada's SB-208 supply restrictions plus RRR's ~454-acre entitled land bank create a durable, hard-to-replicate moat that a standard screen misses. The business generates best-in-class margins (73% casino margin, ~42% Adjusted EBITDA margin on $2.01B FY25 revenue) from a cash-based, slot-dominated, repeat-visitor model that is less cyclical than the Strip.

The stock has stabilized and recovered since our prior review, rising from ~$59 in mid-June to ~$62.78, and 30-day estimate revisions have turned positive (+7% to +9% across windows) after 90-day cuts. Wells Fargo upgraded with a $75 target on July 14, underwriting the central bull case: near-term earnings softness is self-inflicted (Green Valley Ranch room renovations, Durango-related depreciation, construction disruption) and reverses into capex-driven EBITDA growth as Durango Phase II ramps, GVR completes, and North Fork management fees begin in Q4 2026. This is a trough-to-recovery setup with identifiable catalysts.

The offsets keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY. RRR carries ~$3.55B of debt (~4x EBITDA) with roughly $1.8B floating, into a rate path that is now biased higher-for-longer; a 1% rate move costs ~$17.3M of annual interest. Capital returns (a $1.00 special dividend and buybacks) are being partly funded by revolver draws, which rose to $310M at Q1'26 from $155M at year-end. The company is a single-market discretionary bet on a Las Vegas locals economy showing early signs of consumer softness (housing down ~1% YoY, unemployment 5.2-5.4%), sits under ~90% family voting control with a Tax-Receivable-Agreement takeover deterrent, and the options market is pricing near-term stress (rich near-term IV, backwardation, put/call OI at 3.01) into the August 4 print. On ~26-33x forward EPS versus a peer median near 21.6x, the stock is fairly-to-fully valued, with analyst targets ($59-78, mean ~$71.70) providing modest upside but no wide margin of safety.

## Company Overview

Red Rock Resorts operates through Station Casinos LLC and is the dominant player in the Las Vegas locals gaming market. As of 12/31/2025 the portfolio comprised seven major resorts plus roughly 14 smaller casinos and taverns, with 16,553 slots, 328 table games, and 2,734 hotel rooms. Flagship properties are Red Rock (Summerlin), Green Valley Ranch (Henderson), and Durango, which opened in December 2023 in the fast-growing southwest valley and is the primary growth asset. Over 90% of the Las Vegas population lives within five miles of a RRR property, and management notes there are no major competing casino sites within roughly five miles of Durango.

The business is a repeat-visitor, resident-oriented model rather than a Strip tourist operation. Roughly 80% of casino revenue is slots, hold percentages are consistent quarter to quarter, and the business is overwhelmingly cash-based. This produces stable, high-margin cash flow that depends on local resident routine visitation and long-run Las Vegas population growth (+1.6% in 2025) rather than on convention flows or air travel.

Corporate structure is an Up-C: RRR holds a ~59% economic interest in and 100% voting control of Station Holdco, with the Fertitta family holding ~90% of voting power via 10-vote Class B shares while owning ~41% of the economics. This structure materially distorts standard financial ratios and requires careful adjustment (discussed below).

**Revenue and profitability (FY):**

| Adjusted EBITDA ($M) | ~744 | ~746 | 796 | 849 |
| Net income to RRR ($M) | 205 | 176 | 154 | 188 |
| Operating cash flow ($M) | — | — | 548 | 610 |

FY25 revenue grew 3.7%, led by casino revenue +5.0% (slot handle +3.9%, Durango ramp), partly offset by a 5.2% decline in room revenue from GVR hotel renovations. Adjusted EBITDA rose to $848.6M, with EBITDA growth outpacing revenue on operating leverage. This is a steady low-single-digit organic grower with strong margin quality.

Q1 2026 showed the near-term trough. Revenue rose 1.9% YoY to $507.3M, but operating income fell 6.9% and Adjusted EBITDA slipped to $212.6M from $215.1M. The drivers were the continued GVR renovation drag (rooms -9.3%), SG&A +9.2% (partly Mono reimbursable costs), and D&A +15.6% from new assets. Table drop (-3.6%) and sports write (-5.0%) softened, though slot handle remained positive (+1.2%). The EBITDA dip is largely self-inflicted construction disruption plus higher depreciation on new assets, not demand erosion, but the softening table and sports metrics bear watching against the broader consumer backdrop.

**Balance sheet and leverage — the central financial issue.** Total debt was ~$3.55B at Q1'26 against ~$849M LTM EBITDA, roughly 4x gross leverage versus a secured-net-leverage covenant maximum of 5.00x (management reports compliance). Roughly $1.8B is variable-rate (SOFR + 1.50-2.00%), so a 1% rate move drives ~$17.3M of annual interest, partially hedged via two zero-cost collars ($750M notional, 5.25% cap / 2.89% floor, through April 2029). The maturity ladder is well-spread (4.50% notes 2028, 4.625% 2031, TLB 2031, 6.625% notes 2032), and 2025 interest expense fell 11.8% on rate cuts and repayment. In a rate environment now biased higher-for-longer, the floating-rate slug is a drag on the recovery math.

Free cash flow was ~$291M in 2025 (OCF $610M less capex $319M). 2026 capex guidance of $375-425M (Durango Phase II began January 2026) will compress near-term FCF, and management is simultaneously funding a raised buyback ($900M authorization, ~$486M remaining; 635,657 shares repurchased at ~$60.32 in Q1'26), the base dividend, and a $1.00 special dividend paid February 2026 — partly through revolver draws. This is defensible in good times but reduces the cushion against the 5.0x covenant if the locals economy softens.

**A critical adjustment:** the reported Altman Z (~179.7), ROA (~1,527%), and ROE (~171%) are artifacts of the Up-C structure and 2011 fresh-start accounting, not signs of extraordinary strength. GAAP equity attributable to RRR is only ~$142M because 41% of economics sits in non-controlling interest, so a D/E of ~18x is a structural artifact. The real picture is a ~4x-levered, high-margin, stable cash generator. The current ratio of 0.79 is normal for a cash-based casino. Any assessment scoring RRR off the raw screener ratios would badly misread it.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is modest but specific. The base business is a low-single-digit organic grower tied to Las Vegas population growth and in-migration. Layered on top are three identifiable catalysts:

1. **Durango Phase II** (expanded casino, 36-lane bowling, luxury theaters, new F&B), which began construction January 2026 and is the primary organic driver.
2. **Green Valley Ranch renovation completion**, which reverses the 2025-2026 room-revenue drag.
3. **North Fork (Mono)** Native American project, expected to open Q4 2026, adding a management-fee income stream (4% development fee + 30% management fee), though with a litigation overhang.

The earnings-surprise record supports near-term confidence but shows decelerating magnitude: beats of 100%, 36.1%, 21.5%, then 3.2% over the last four quarters. The most recent beat was small, indicating tighter visibility. Estimate revisions tell a two-part story: 90-day revisions are negative (current year -11.7%, next quarter -17.1%), reflecting the cuts that drove the earlier score decline, but 30-day revisions have turned positive across all windows, and next-year estimates are essentially flat over 90 days (-0.4%) while up 7% over 30 days. This is a recent turn in the revision trend rather than a durable acceleration, and it does not reach the >30% velocity that would flag a strong standalone buy signal.

Consensus current-year EPS is $1.91 and next-year $2.45, implying next-year growth of ~28%, most of which is the recovery from the construction trough rather than structural acceleration. The growth is real and the catalysts are dated, but the near-term P&L is going backward and the broader consumer environment is a headwind to a discretionary locals business.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing EPS of $3.12, RRR trades at ~20x. But the trailing figure appears to include items that make forward multiples the more meaningful gauge: on consensus current-year EPS of $1.91 the forward P/E is ~33x, and on next-year $2.45 it is ~26x. Against a Casinos & Gaming peer group where the profitable comparables cluster at MCRI 21.6x and CHDN 15.9x (peer median 21.6x; PENN, CZR, and GDEN carry negative or distorted multiples), RRR is at a premium to peers on forward earnings while delivering only modest through-cycle growth.

Analyst positioning is constructive: 15 buy/strong-buy versus 2 holds, sentiment "improving," with a target range and mean as noted above. At $62.78 that implies ~14% upside to the mean and ~19% to the Wells Fargo $75 target. The targets sit above current price, which provides modest support. But because RRR is at a premium multiple with only recovery-driven growth rather than a cheap-relative-to-rising-estimates setup, these targets reflect the same recovery extrapolation the market is already partially pricing — they are not independent confirmation of deep undervaluation.

The stock is roughly fairly valued: the moat and margins justify a peer-level-to-slightly-premium multiple, but the leverage, floating-rate exposure, single-market concentration, and near-term trough cap the upside. There is no specific reason to believe the market is materially wrong on price today. A valuation score in the mid-range reflects fair value with modest analyst-target support, not asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

This is RRR's strongest dimension. The company is the clear #1 in the Las Vegas locals segment (its main comparable is Boyd's locals segment; the Strip is a distinct market). The competitive advantages are structural and durable:

- **SB-208 supply cap** (1997) restricts where new casinos can be built in the valley, and RRR's ownership of six gaming-entitled development parcels (~454 acres) plus master-plannable footprints effectively lets it control the pipeline of new locals-casino supply.
- **Locals model economics** — cash-based, slot-heavy, consistent hold — produce best-in-class margins and lower cyclicality than the Strip.
- **Market-share gains** cited by Wells Fargo, alongside the Durango growth corridor with no competing sites within roughly five miles.

Against this are secular threats the market only partially prices: California tribal gaming expansion (66 facilities; California is Nevada's largest out-of-state visitor source), online and sports betting, and newly cited "predictive markets" siphoning gaming spend. Neither has materially dented results to date, but both are slow-building headwinds. The trajectory is stable-to-improving on share, with structural threats that argue against maximum conviction. The prior competitive-position score declined from 7.5 to 6.5; current data supports a stable ~6.5 — a wide moat with a slowly narrowing long-term edge.

## Risk Assessment

The risk cluster is meaningful and not fully priced:

- **Single-market concentration.** 100% of owned operations are in one metro, fully exposed to Las Vegas unemployment, housing (median ~$470-480K), and California visitation. A regional slowdown hits directly with no geographic diversification. Early Q1'26 softness in tables and sports write is a data point to monitor against a broader discretionary-spending cooling.
- **Leverage and rate sensitivity.** In a higher-for-longer rate regime, covenants restrict distributions and investments; a downturn compressing EBITDA would pressure the 5.0x covenant against a company simultaneously funding capex, dividends, and buybacks partly via the revolver.
- **Governance and structure.** ~90% family voting power via Class B shares, controlled-company exemptions, and a Tax Receivable Agreement (85% of exchange tax benefits flow to pre-IPO owners, accelerated to present value on change of control) that functions as a takeover deterrent and entrenches family control. Minority Class A holders have limited influence. CEO compensation jumped to $13.3M in 2025 from ~$3.3M, mitigated by strong relative TSR (303.9 index vs 91.5 peer).
- **North Fork litigation.** ~$117M advanced, ~$80.6M on nonaccrual with recovery "not assured," a $425M completion guaranty, and an adverse procedural development (California Supreme Court denied Mono's review in April 2026).
- **Union organizing.** Culinary-affiliated certification at GVR (no CBA yet) plus NLRB litigation at multiple properties; successful unionization would pressure the margin advantage.
- **Unconfirmed June 12, 2026 Item 5.02 filing** (director/officer change) that could not be verified — flagged as an open item ahead of the next disclosures.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 46.7% is rich versus 35.4% historical volatility (an 11.3-point spread, 69th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (46.7% near-term versus 39.2% at six months), which markets typically show ahead of a discrete event — here the August 4 Q2 print. Put/call open interest of 3.01 indicates heavy put positioning, and the implied move at the nearest expiry is ±10.8% (with a ±15% implied move on the broader nearest-expiry chain). The prior snapshot's 153.5% IV reading appears to have been a corrupted data point; the current 46.7% is the clean figure. Short interest is elevated at 11.2% of float (4.0 days to cover). Positioning is defensive/hedged, consistent with a HOLD and a balanced-to-cautious near-term risk/reward.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$78).** The near-term earnings trough is self-inflicted and temporary. GVR renovations complete, Durango Phase II ramps, North Fork fee income begins in Q4 2026, and the locals market-share gains Wells Fargo cites drive Adjusted EBITDA higher on the sector's best margins. Estimate revisions continue the 30-day upturn, the stock re-rates toward the $75-78 target range, and shareholder returns (base + special dividend + buyback) are supported by recovering FCF. Rate cuts, if they materialize, relieve the floating-rate interest burden.

**Bear case (~$50-53, roughly -16% to -20%).** A softening Las Vegas locals consumer — visible in weakening housing wealth, elevated unemployment, and the early table/sports softness — pressures the discretionary core just as capex peaks and revolver draws rise. The 4x leverage becomes less comfortable if EBITDA slips toward the 5.0x covenant, forcing a pullback in capital returns. Higher-for-longer rates keep the ~$1.8B floating balance expensive. Secular pressure from California tribal gaming and online betting compounds. The stock retraces toward its 52-week low near $50.52.

The two-sidedness is genuine. The recovery thesis has dated catalysts and a supportive analyst base, but the leverage/rate exposure and single-market discretionary concentration into a cooling consumer environment cap conviction, and the stock is not cheap enough on forward earnings to provide a margin of safety.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The thesis is a specific 12-month recovery driven by Durango Phase II, GVR completion, and North Fork fee income, but the offsets (leverage, rate exposure, sector consumer risk, fair-to-full valuation) prevent an upgrade to BUY.

**Base case target: ~$68** (roughly 26-27x next-year EPS of ~$2.45, or ~12x recovering EBITDA), approximately +8% from current, over a 12-month horizon anchored to the Durango Phase II ramp and GVR completion.

**Bull case target: ~$78** (+24%), if EBITDA growth materializes and estimate revisions sustain their upturn.

**Bear case target: ~$50-53** (-16% to -20%), if the locals consumer softens materially and leverage/rate pressure compresses the multiple back toward the 52-week low.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 (Aug 4) confirms the EBITDA inflection with Adjusted EBITDA re-accelerating YoY and full-year guidance reaffirmed or raised, plus 90-day estimate revisions turning net positive.
- Net leverage declining toward ~3.5x with capital returns funded by FCF rather than revolver draws.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s that restores a clear margin of safety versus the recovery earnings power.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Slot handle turning negative or a broad-based Q2 revenue decline signaling genuine locals-demand erosion (not just renovation drag).
- Net leverage rising toward the 5.0x covenant, forcing suspension of the buyback or special dividend.
- An adverse North Fork completion-guaranty crystallization or a material unionization outcome that structurally raises labor costs.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the August 4, 2026 Q2 report, with North Fork's Q4 2026 opening and Durango Phase II progress as subsequent milestones.

## Conclusion

Red Rock Resorts is a high-quality, wide-moat operator in the structurally attractive Las Vegas locals market, with best-in-class margins and a scarce, supply-protected land bank that generic screens miss. It is in a self-inflicted near-term earnings trough that a credible bull case (Wells Fargo $75) expects to reverse into capex-driven EBITDA growth, and the recent 30-day estimate-revision turn plus the stock's recovery argue against extending the prior score decline. The score stabilizes at ~5.4 rather than falling further.

The reasons it stops at HOLD rather than BUY are concrete: ~4x leverage with ~$1.8B floating-rate debt into a higher-for-longer environment, capital returns partly funded by rising revolver draws, single-market discretionary concentration into a cooling consumer, Up-C family entrenchment that limits minority-holder influence, and a forward multiple (~26-33x) at a premium to the peer median with only recovery-driven growth to justify it. The right posture is to hold for the recovery thesis while monitoring the Q2 EBITDA inflection, leverage trajectory, and locals-demand indicators, and to demand either confirmed acceleration or a cheaper entry before adding.
