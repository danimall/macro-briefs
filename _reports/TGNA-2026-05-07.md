## Executive Summary

Tegna Inc is a $3.2 billion broadcast media company operating 64 television stations across 51 U.S. markets, reaching 39% of U.S. TV households. The stock trades at $20.03 against a $22.00/share all-cash merger agreement with Nexstar Media Group — a deal that technically closed on March 19, 2026, but is currently frozen by a federal court preliminary injunction after 13 state attorneys general challenged it on antitrust grounds.

The investment case is binary: if the merger completes, shareholders receive $22/share (10% upside from current levels). If the deal unwinds, the stock reprices to standalone value, which on a blended even/odd-year EBITDA basis suggests $13-16/share (20-35% downside). The 10% arb spread fairly compensates for the genuine legal uncertainty — a federal judge has already found antitrust harm likely, Nexstar is appealing to the Ninth Circuit, and trial could extend through 2026-2027.

As a standalone entity, TGNA generates $283M in free cash flow on an off-election year and has a solid balance sheet (2.78x leverage, $738M undrawn revolver). However, structural secular headwinds — cord-cutting at 5.9% annually, declining linear TV advertising, and network affiliation renegotiation risk — limit standalone upside absent the merger premium. The stock is a HOLD given the legal uncertainty: neither the spread nor the standalone risk/reward justifies a directional BUY or AVOID call today.

## Company Overview

TEGNA Inc, originally Gannett Co. (renamed 2015), is the largest independent owner of Big Four network affiliates (ABC, CBS, FOX, NBC) in the top 25 U.S. markets. Revenue breaks down as: Distribution (54%, $1.47B), Advertising & Marketing Services (43%, $1.17B), Political (1%, $39M in off-year; $373M in 2024), and Other (1%).

The company also operates Premion, a connected TV advertising platform reaching all 210 U.S. TV markets, providing digital revenue diversification. TEGNA produces over 1,700 hours of local news per week and operates multicast networks (True Crime Network, Quest).

The defining corporate event is the pending $6.2 billion acquisition by Nexstar Media Group at $22.00/share in cash, representing a 31% premium at announcement. The deal received shareholder approval (November 2025), FCC Media Bureau approval, and DOJ sign-off, and technically closed March 19, 2026. However, a preliminary injunction issued April 18, 2026 by Judge Troy Nunley (U.S. District Court, Sacramento) blocks integration pending antitrust trial.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Earnings Cyclicality.** FY2025 revenue of $2.71B declined 13% from FY2024's $3.10B. This is overwhelmingly a political cycle effect: political revenue dropped from $373M to $39M (-$334M). Adjusting for political, the underlying business declined approximately 3-4% — driven by cord-cutting impacts on distribution revenue (-1%) and softness in AMS (-4%, partially from the Gray Media/Premion partnership dissolution).

**Profitability.** Operating income fell 44% to $443M; adjusted EBITDA declined 38% to $579M. Operating margins compressed from 25% to 16% on the revenue decline. Even in this off-year trough, the company generated $326M in operating cash flow and $283M in FCF — a 10.4% FCF margin.

**Balance Sheet.** The company used $550M in cash to retire debt maturing in March 2026, reducing the cash position from $693M to $291M while total long-term debt stands at $2.53B. All debt is fixed-rate. Maturities: $440M (2027), $1.0B (2028), $1.1B (2029). The leverage ratio of 2.78x is well below the 4.50x covenant, and the $738.7M undrawn revolver provides ample liquidity.

**Capital Allocation.** Management was aggressive on buybacks from 2022-2024, reducing share count by 28% (224M → 161M). No buybacks occurred in 2025 due to merger restrictions. The $0.50/share annual dividend yields approximately 2.5%.

**Earnings Surprise Track Record.** Three beats and one in-line result over the last four quarters (4.3%, -1.4%, 16.6%, 12.0%). This suggests modest positive execution bias, though the magnitude is small on absolute EPS that ranges $0.33-0.50/quarter.

## Growth Analysis

TGNA faces structural growth headwinds on a standalone basis:

**Distribution Revenue Erosion.** Pay-TV subscribers are declining approximately 5.9% annually. TEGNA has historically offset volume declines with rate increases during retransmission consent negotiations, but this strategy has diminishing runway as the subscriber base shrinks. Distribution revenue declined 1% in FY2025 despite contractual rate escalators.

**Advertising Secular Decline.** Digital media now accounts for 54% of local ad spend (BIA Advisory). AMS revenue declined 4% in FY2025. Competition from Google, Meta, and programmatic platforms continues to compress traditional TV advertising budgets.

**Political Advertising Tailwind (2026).** The 2026 midterm cycle should generate $200-300M in political revenue, driving significant YoY growth. This is the primary near-term growth catalyst and is already reflected in the current-year consensus of $3.04 EPS (vs. $1.34 in FY2025).

**Premion/Digital.** The CTV platform provides a growth avenue, but Gray Media's partnership exit in 2025 reduced distribution reach. Premion contributes a small minority of total revenue, and the dissolution cost $20.8M.

**Estimate Revisions.** Current-year EPS estimates are essentially flat (+0.8% over 90 days), but next-year estimates declined 5.9% — reflecting both political cycle normalization and standalone secular concerns. There is no positive estimate revision momentum.

## Valuation Assessment

At $20.03, TGNA trades at 14.9x trailing and 11.6x forward P/E. The forward multiple reflects the 2026 political cycle boost. On normalized (blended even/odd-year) earnings, the effective multiple is higher.

**Merger-Adjusted Valuation.** The stock's value is overwhelmingly determined by merger outcome probability. At $22.00 deal price and $20.03 current price, the implied annualized arb return depends entirely on timeline — if closed within 6 months, the annualized return is approximately 20%. If the deal extends 18 months, annualized return compresses to approximately 7%.

**Standalone Valuation Floor.** If the deal fails, using a 5.5x blended EBITDA multiple (average of $579M odd-year and $931M even-year = $755M) yields enterprise value of $4.15B, minus $2.53B debt, plus $291M cash = $1.91B equity value, or approximately $11.90/share. This is the bear-case floor, though FCF generation ($283-633M annually) provides a higher fundamental anchor around $13-16/share depending on assumptions about terminal growth.

**Peer Context.** The peer median P/E of 65.7x is heavily distorted by Nexstar (65.7x) and WBD (93.8x), both of which face unique circumstances. Fox at 13.5-14.9x is the cleanest comparable — TGNA's forward P/E of 11.6x represents a modest discount, partially justified by merger uncertainty.

The stock offers a 10% spread to the deal price with genuine binary risk. This is neither cheap enough to justify a BUY (given the real probability of deal failure) nor expensive enough to AVOID (given that deal completion remains the most likely single outcome).

## Competitive Landscape

TEGNA holds a strong competitive position within a structurally challenged industry. Its 39% household reach approaches the FCC ownership cap, its Big Four affiliation portfolio is the largest among independents, and its local news operations are deeply entrenched.

However, the competitive moat is narrowing:
- Network affiliates face reduced leverage as networks build direct-to-consumer streaming platforms (Peacock, Paramount+)
- Key affiliation agreements expire in 2026-2028 and may renew on less favorable terms
- Digital competitors are capturing an increasing share of local advertising budgets
- The FCC broadcast license franchise, while still valuable, is being marginalized as distribution shifts to IP-based delivery

The proposed Nexstar combination would create a 259-station entity reaching 80% of households, dramatically improving negotiating leverage. If the deal fails, TEGNA remains competitively viable but faces continued margin compression from secular headwinds.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risk: Merger Outcome (Binary).** The legal standard for overturning a preliminary injunction on appeal is high (requiring clear error or abuse of discretion). Trial proceedings could extend through 2026-2027. If the deal fails, TEGNA receives a $125M termination fee but the stock reprices to $13-16.

**Regulatory/Political Complexity.** The FCC Chairman stated the Media Bureau approval was "not a final action." The case involves unusual jurisdictional complexity — FCC policy vs. antitrust court authority. The Trump administration has been favorable to broadcaster consolidation, but courts operate independently.

**Secular Business Decline.** Cord-cutting combined with digital advertising displacement creates ongoing revenue pressure that cannot be fully offset by rate increases or cost management.

**Debt Maturities.** If the deal fails and the business faces accelerated revenue decline, refinancing $2.54B in debt maturing 2027-2029 at favorable rates is not guaranteed in a higher-rate environment.

**Political Cycle Dependency.** Revenue volatility of $300M+ between even/odd years creates difficulty in establishing steady-state valuation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($22.00/share, +10%):** The Ninth Circuit overturns the preliminary injunction or Nexstar offers sufficient divestitures to satisfy antitrust concerns. Deal closes, shareholders receive $22.00 cash. If closing occurs on the current appeal timeline, the implied annualized return could exceed 15%.

**Bear Case ($13-16/share, -20% to -35%):** The injunction stands, trial confirms antitrust harm, deal collapses. TGNA receives $125M termination fee but reprices to standalone value. Secular decline in linear TV accelerates. Network affiliations renew on unfavorable terms. Stock gravitates toward $13-16 range, with downside to $11-12 in a severe scenario.

**Base Case ($19-21/share, -5% to +5%):** Legal proceedings extend 12-18 months. Stock trades in a range reflecting deal uncertainty, with the spread narrowing on positive legal developments and widening on negative ones. Shareholders collect the 2.5% dividend while waiting.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Ninth Circuit overturns preliminary injunction or signals favorable review (deal probability >70%)
2. Nexstar proposes divestitures accepted by the court, narrowing the antitrust case
3. Stock drops below $17.50 on standalone business concerns while deal remains alive (widening spread creates asymmetric arb opportunity)

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Ninth Circuit affirms the preliminary injunction with language suggesting high likelihood of permanent injunction
2. Nexstar signals willingness to walk away or invokes termination provisions
3. FCC full commission reverses Media Bureau approval

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next meaningful legal development from Ninth Circuit expected within 3-6 months. Any court scheduling of trial date would provide critical timing clarity.

## Conclusion

TEGNA is a competent operator in a structurally declining industry whose investment thesis is entirely consumed by the binary $22/share Nexstar merger outcome. The 10% spread to deal price fairly reflects the genuine legal uncertainty — a federal judge has found antitrust harm likely, but the deal has significant institutional momentum and political support.

The business generates meaningful FCF and maintains a manageable balance sheet, providing a floor well above zero in a deal-failure scenario. However, the lack of organic growth prospects, negative estimate revision momentum, and structural secular headwinds preclude a standalone investment thesis that would justify buying the stock independent of the merger.

HOLD reflects the genuine inability to assign a confident probability to deal completion. Pure merger-arbitrage investors with specific legal expertise may find the spread attractive; fundamental investors without a view on Ninth Circuit antitrust jurisprudence should wait for legal clarity before establishing positions.
