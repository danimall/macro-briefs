**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Global-e Online is the leading platform for cross-border, direct-to-consumer (D2C) e-commerce, serving more than 1,500 brands and reaching shoppers in 200+ destinations. The company crossed into GAAP profitability in 2025 ($68M net income, $0.39 diluted EPS) after years of large losses, and Q1 2026 extended the operating momentum: revenue +33% to $252M, GMV +40% to $1.74B, Adjusted EBITDA +59% to $50.2M (19.9% margin, up 330bps), with management raising full-year guidance. The balance sheet is a genuine strength: roughly $620M in liquid assets against essentially no debt, with a $200M buyback authorization that is offsetting stock-based compensation dilution.

The central interpretive issue is that the headline GAAP profitability turnaround overstates the year-over-year improvement. The swing from a Q1'25 GAAP loss to a Q1'26 profit is substantially driven by the Shopify commercial-agreement (warrant) amortization collapsing from $37.0M to $0.5M, with the asset now essentially fully amortized. Sales and marketing fell from $63.9M to $34.4M YoY mostly for this non-cash reason. The underlying operating leverage measured by Adjusted EBITDA (+59%) is real, but a reader anchoring on GAAP EPS growth will materially overestimate the rate of fundamental improvement.

The dominant near-term overhang is the U.S. repeal of the $800 de minimis duty-free exemption in spring 2025. CBP collected over $1B in new de minimis tariffs by December 2025 and sub-$800 parcel volumes into the U.S. fell roughly 54%. The U.S. is 50% of GLBE revenue, and the 20-F explicitly names dependence on the "personal importation model." Q1'26 results suggest the company is navigating this so far (total GMV still +40%, though U.S. revenue share ticked down to 50% from 53%), and management has launched tariff-mitigation products (3B2C, expanded Duty Drawback) that turn complexity into a sales argument. The bull and bear cases hinge on whether tariff complexity ultimately drives merchants toward GLBE or compresses cross-border volumes structurally. At ~21x forward EPS ($1.50 FY26 consensus) on rising estimates, the valuation is reasonable for the growth profile but does not offer the asymmetric upside that would justify a high-conviction long ahead of an unresolved binary. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Global-e enables brands and retailers to sell internationally "as simply as selling domestically." The platform bundles localization (currency, language, pricing), duties and taxes calculation/collection, international logistics and fulfillment, payments, and conversion-optimization analytics. The company acts as merchant of record for transactions on its platform, which is structurally important for its tax, sanctions, and regulatory profile.

Revenue splits into two lines. In Q1'26, service fees were $120.8M (48%) and fulfillment services $131.3M (52%), against GMV of $1.74B, an implied take rate near 14.5%. Revenue is geographically diversified by merchant outbound region: U.S. 50%, EU 21%, UK 19%, other 10%.

The defining strategic asset is the Shopify partnership. GLBE powers Shopify's white-label "Managed Markets" offering, now rolling out Version 2.0, expanding into Canada and the UK with merchants migrating off the legacy version. The relationship was established through a multi-year commercial agreement involving Shopify warrants issued in 2021, the amortization of which has just rolled off and is the single most important accounting dynamic in the recent numbers.

As an Israeli foreign private issuer, GLBE files Form 20-F (annual) and 6-Ks rather than 10-K/10-Q/8-K. The latest FY2025 20-F was filed March 26, 2026.

## Financial Analysis

The five-year trajectory shows a shift from heavy losses to profitability:

| Metric ($M) | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 245 | 409 | 570 | 753 | 962 |
| Gross profit | 91 | 158 | 234 | 339 | 436 |
| Operating income | -66 | -189 | -137 | -68 | +72 |
| Net income | -75 | -195 | -134 | -76 | **+68** |
| Diluted EPS | -0.74 | -1.24 | -0.81 | -0.45 | **+0.39** |
| R&D | 30 | 81 | 98 | 105 | 123 |

Revenue compounded at roughly 41% over 2021–2025, with FY2025 revenue +28% YoY to $962M. GAAP gross margin sits near 45% (FY2025), with non-GAAP gross margin at 47% in Q1'26, up from 45.4%, modest gross-margin expansion alongside scale.

The cash-flow picture requires care. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was −$72.6M and FCF −$72.9M, almost identical to Q1'25's −$72.6M. This is a recurring seasonal working-capital drag, not deterioration: the large Q4 holiday "funds payable to customers" balance unwinds in Q1 (roughly a −$65M swing) alongside payables and accruals. Full-year FCF is positive. A standardized screen flagging "negative operating cash flow" would misread this; the merchant-of-record float ("funds receivable" and "funds payable to customers" each ~$182M) is a pass-through, not net assets.

The balance sheet is genuinely strong. Total assets of $1.46B against total liabilities of $530M leave $933M equity. Liquid resources are roughly $620M (cash $246M + short-term deposits $303M + marketable securities $74M) with essentially no debt. The caveat is that approximately $375M of goodwill plus $52M of intangibles (largely from the Borderfree acquisition) represent about 29% of assets; this is not a risk in the absence of impairment triggers, but it limits tangible-book support.

Capital allocation has turned shareholder-friendly. The $200M buyback authorized in 2025 has seen roughly $131M repurchased to date including $59M in Q1 2026. With stock-based compensation around $10M per quarter, the buyback is roughly offsetting dilution, keeping the share count flat to slightly down (~169M to ~168M weighted average).

The accounting wedge that matters most: the Shopify commercial-agreement asset amortization collapsed from $37.0M in Q1 2025 to $0.5M in Q1 2026 (the asset is now nearly fully amortized). This is the dominant reason GAAP S&M fell from $63.9M to $34.4M and the dominant reason a Q1'25 GAAP loss became a Q1'26 GAAP profit. Adjusted EBITDA growth of +59% reflects real operating leverage, but anyone extrapolating GAAP EPS growth will overstate the improvement.

## Growth Analysis

The operating fundamentals are accelerating. Q1'26 GMV and revenue both ran above the FY2025 full-year rate, and management raised full-year 2026 guidance to revenue $1,220–1,280M (~27–33% growth), GMV $8,530–8,880M, and Adjusted EBITDA $264.5–289.5M. The Q2 guide of $278.5–285.5M implies continued mid-to-high-20s revenue growth.

Estimate revisions are positive but not at the velocity that signals a sharp acceleration. Current-quarter EPS estimates rose 9.3% over 30 days and 7.4% over 90 days; current-year estimates rose 4.3%/4.0%; next-year rose 3.8%. This is broadly rising consensus, consistent with the beat-and-raise, but it falls short of the +30% revision threshold that would mark a fundamental re-rating opportunity. The earnings track record is mixed: a 91% beat in Q2'25, a 9% beat in Q4'25, a 6% miss in Q3'25, and a 4% miss in Q1'26, so forward estimates deserve moderate rather than high confidence.

Growth drivers are concrete. Shopify Managed Markets V2.0 is expanding into Canada (early access) with the UK to follow, and a large batch of merchants is migrating off the legacy version. New enterprise launches span LVMH-owned Fresh, Alo Yoga, Figs, Bandai Namco, Coperni, Quadrant, the Roland Garros store, Universal Music Japan, and Shanghai Tang. The new 3B2C (business-to-business-to-consumer) offering and expanded Duty Drawback directly address tariff cost mitigation, converting the regulatory headwind into a product wedge.

One signal to watch is take-rate compression: GMV (+40%) is consistently outgrowing revenue (+33%). If this gap persists, it likely reflects mix shift toward the lower-take Shopify white-label channel and larger enterprise accounts. The implication is that revenue growth could decelerate faster than GMV even if the platform keeps winning volume.

## Valuation Assessment

At a current price near $31.79 (off a $33.61 reference) and a market cap of approximately $5.6B, GLBE trades at roughly 48x trailing GAAP EPS ($0.39) but about 21x consensus FY26 EPS of $1.50 and roughly 16.6x FY27 of $1.92. On Adjusted EBITDA, the FY26 guide midpoint (~$277M) against an enterprise value of roughly $5.0B (market cap less ~$620M net cash) implies ~18x EV/EBITDA. Motley Fool cited roughly 35x FCF.

The trailing multiple is not the right anchor given the recent profitability transition, but the forward multiple is also distorted by the Shopify amortization roll-off: FY26 GAAP earnings benefit from the same non-cash tailwind that flatters Q1'26. Adjusting for that, the company is being valued at roughly 18x forward EBITDA for a business compounding GMV at ~40% with ~20% and rising EBITDA margins. That is not expensive for the growth profile, and it is not cheap enough to call asymmetric given the unresolved tariff binary.

The PEG framing is supportive but not decisive: ~21x forward EPS against ~28% revenue growth implies a PEG under 1.0 on revenue, though EPS growth is muddied by the amortization base effect. Analyst targets span $34 (low) to $64 (high), with a $45.6 mean and $44 median, meaningful implied upside, though Truist cut its Hold target twice (to $37, then $34) on tariff concerns, illustrating the dispersion. With 12 of 13 covering analysts at Buy or Strong Buy and one Hold, sentiment is constructive but targets have been drifting in opposite directions among individual shops.

I score valuation 5.5. The business is reasonably priced for its quality and growth, but two factors prevent a higher mark: the forward EPS multiple borrows from a non-cash amortization tailwind, and the de minimis overhang is a genuine, unresolved risk to the U.S. half of the revenue base that justifies the lack of a clear discount.

## Competitive Landscape

GLBE is the clear leader in cross-border D2C enablement, with the Shopify alliance functioning as both a distribution moat and a concentration risk. The platform's breadth across localization, duties/taxes, logistics, payments, compliance, and analytics in a single merchant-of-record stack raises switching costs for the brands on the platform. Competitors include in-house solutions, ESW, Reach, Zonos, and broader logistics and payments players, none of which match GLBE's combination of Shopify integration and end-to-end coverage.

Protectionism is reshaping the industry, and it cuts both ways. Tariffs and the de minimis repeal raise the cost and complexity of cross-border selling, pressuring volumes near-term, while simultaneously increasing the value of a platform that handles duties, drawback, compliance, and tariff mitigation. The bull articulation is "tariffs as a lead-generation mechanism." Q1'26 data leans toward GLBE navigating the transition, with the dip in U.S. revenue share consistent with some U.S. drag being offset by EU and other-region strength. The trajectory of the competitive position is stable-to-improving on product breadth, with the principal threat being Shopify deciding to internalize more of the value chain over time. Score: 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**De minimis repeal and tariffs (dominant overhang).** This is the proximate cause of repeated post-earnings sell-offs and the crux of the bull/bear debate. The risk is not yet resolved by a single quarter of resilient GMV. The 20-F flags dependence on the personal importation model.

**Shopify dependence.** Managed Markets is both a major growth channel and a single point of concentration. The 20-F names the ability to adapt GLBE's platform to Shopify as a risk. Strategic dependence on a partner that could in principle build or buy competing capability caps the moat.

**Merchant-of-record exposure.** GLBE carries payment regulation, AML/anti-corruption, sanctions/export-control, and multi-jurisdiction tax-collection obligations.

**Take-rate compression.** GMV outgrowing revenue points to mix shift toward lower-take channels; sustained, this would decelerate revenue faster than volume.

**FX and Israel-specific risk.** Multi-currency GMV creates translation exposure; incorporation and operations in Israel carry geopolitical risk tied to ongoing hostilities.

**Governance.** As a founder-led foreign private issuer, GLBE does not file a US-style proxy; minority-shareholder governance leverage is limited and insider ownership is concentrated.

I score risk factors 4.5. The de minimis binary, Shopify concentration, and the GAAP-vs-underlying-earnings interpretive trap are specific, material risks that are not fully resolved at the current price.

### Options Market Signal

The put/call skew is elevated at 25.9% near-term, indicating demand for downside protection, and the put/call open-interest ratio is 1.59. The term structure is in contango (1m 53.4% < 6m 60.8%) and the longer-dated skew flips negative (-5.1% at 6m), so the protection bid is concentrated in the front end rather than the back. The combined read is near-term event/sentiment caution layered on a structurally normal longer-dated profile, which fits a stock whose post-earnings moves are driven by tariff narrative rather than operational misses. The elevated front-end put skew is a divergence worth respecting against the constructive sell-side rating distribution.

### Insider and Institutional Signal

Two notable holders trimmed into the 2026 weakness: Spyglass Capital sold ~$26M and Deepwater halved its position (~$9M sold) in May 2026. This is a sentiment caution flag rather than a confirmed fundamental signal; it coincides with the tariff-driven drawdown rather than any operational miss, and it is not clustered C-suite open-market selling. Short interest is low at 3.1% of float (2.2 days to cover), so the bear case is uncrowded. Combined with the founder-led structure and the buyback offsetting SBC, management alignment is moderate; the limited FPI disclosure and concentrated insider ownership keep the management score at 5.5.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Tariff complexity becomes a structural tailwind: merchants increasingly need a platform that handles duties, drawback, and compliance, and GLBE's 3B2C and Duty Drawback products convert the de minimis headwind into a lead-generation engine. Shopify Managed Markets V2.0 scales across Canada and the UK, GMV continues compounding at ~40%, Adjusted EBITDA margin expands past 20%, and the market re-rates the stock toward the upper end of the analyst range. Forward EBITDA growth in FY27 at a higher multiple supports a price near $55–64.

**Bear case.** The de minimis repeal structurally compresses cross-border volumes into the U.S. (half of revenue), take-rate compression accelerates as the Shopify white-label mix grows, and the GAAP earnings tailwind from the warrant amortization roll-off masks decelerating underlying growth once the base effect fully laps. Revenue growth slows toward the high teens, the forward multiple compresses, and the stock retraces toward its 52-week low. Downside to roughly $24–27.

The base case sits between: GLBE navigates the tariff transition with GMV growth decelerating gradually, revenue lands within or near the raised guidance, and the stock re-rates modestly as consensus catches up but remains range-bound until the tariff question resolves.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive (accelerating GMV, real operating leverage, a fortress balance sheet, and tariff-mitigation products with concrete enterprise wins), but the de minimis binary and the GAAP-vs-underlying-earnings distortion keep this short of high conviction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters confirming the take rate stabilizes (revenue growth converging back toward GMV growth) while U.S. revenue share holds at or above 50%, demonstrating the de minimis headwind is contained.
- Forward EPS estimate revisions accelerating past +15% over 90 days on Adjusted-EBITDA-driven (not amortization-driven) earnings, with the stock trading below ~18x forward EPS.
- Evidence that 3B2C/Duty Drawback are materially adding new enterprise logos and offsetting U.S. parcel-volume drag in the GMV detail.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- U.S. revenue share falling below ~45% with total GMV growth decelerating below 25%, indicating structural cross-border volume loss.
- Guidance cut on the FY26 revenue or EBITDA range, or a clear sign Shopify is internalizing Managed Markets economics.
- Take-rate compression accelerating (GMV-revenue growth gap widening beyond ~8–10 points) without offsetting margin expansion.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 print (guided revenue $278.5–285.5M), which will test whether the tariff transition continues to be navigated and whether take-rate compression is stabilizing.

## Conclusion

Global-e is a high-quality, founder-led category leader in cross-border D2C enablement, with genuine operating leverage (Adjusted EBITDA +59%, ~20% margin), accelerating GMV (+40%), and a debt-free balance sheet returning capital via buyback. Those strengths are real and the growth is broad-based. The reasons to hold rather than buy are equally concrete: the headline GAAP profitability turnaround is substantially a non-cash Shopify amortization roll-off; the de minimis repeal is an unresolved structural risk to half the revenue base; take-rate compression is visible in the GMV-revenue gap; and at ~21x forward EPS (flattered by the same amortization tailwind) the valuation is fair rather than asymmetric. The constructive analyst distribution and meaningful target upside are offset by elevated near-term put skew and notable institutional trimming into weakness. The balance of evidence supports holding the position for the growth optionality while monitoring the tariff and take-rate data points that would resolve the thesis in either direction. HOLD.
