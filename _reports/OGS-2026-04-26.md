## Executive Summary

ONE Gas is a 100% regulated natural gas distribution utility serving approximately 2.3 million customers across Oklahoma, Kansas, and Texas. The company has delivered solid operating income growth ($350M in 2022 to $457M in 2025), driven by constructive regulatory outcomes totaling approximately $116M in new rate relief during 2025 alone. Its three-state footprint benefits from favorable regulatory mechanisms (PBRC, GSRS, GRIP) that allow interim capital recovery between formal rate cases.

The core tension in the ONE Gas investment case is that the rate base growth story ($5.8B current, expanding via ~$800M annual capex) is real, but the capital required to fund it renders the company persistently free cash flow negative. FY2025 FCF was approximately negative $136M, and management is funding the gap through equity issuances (12% dilution since 2022) and debt. At $88.09, the stock trades at ~20.2x trailing EPS and ~18.5x FY2026 consensus of $4.77 — in-line with gas utility peer medians. The 3.1% dividend yield provides income but grows at only ~2% annually.

This is a stable, defensible business earning fair returns for income-oriented holders. The stock is reasonably priced, regulatory tailwinds are intact, and the Oklahoma mandatory rate case filing (by June 2027) is the next major earnings catalyst. However, the combination of dilution, pension cost escalation ($5.7M to $18.6M in FY2026), and limited free cash flow generation means the total return profile over 12 months is modest.

## Company Overview

ONE Gas was spun off from ONEOK in January 2014 and operates as the largest natural gas distributor in Oklahoma (~89% market share), the largest in Kansas (~72%), and the third-largest in Texas (~13%). Natural gas costs are fully passed through to ratepayers at cost, so commodity price fluctuations do not directly affect profitability. Revenue derives from delivery charges authorized by state regulators.

**Revenue composition by state (ex-gas costs):**
- Oklahoma: ~91% fixed-charge revenues, with weather normalization adjustment (WNA) mechanisms
- Texas: ~66% fixed-charge revenues
- Kansas: ~52% fixed-charge revenues

This fixed-charge structure provides meaningful insulation from volumetric risk, though weather still affected results in FY2024 (heating degree days at 80-83% of normal) and FY2025 (88-94% of normal).

The company added ~23,000 new customers annually in both 2024 and 2025, reflecting population growth in its Oklahoma and Texas markets. A new $120M pipeline project in southeast Oklahoma (Bennington Natural Gas Hub, completion Q3 2028) targets the growing natural gas-fueled power generation market, including Western Farmers Electric Cooperative's Hugo plant.

## Financial Analysis

### Earnings Trajectory

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Operating Income | $350M | $378M | $399M | $457M |
| Net Income | $222M | $231M | $223M | $264M |
| Diluted EPS | $4.08 | $4.14 | $3.91 | $4.37 |
| Diluted Shares | ~54.4M | ~55.8M | ~57.1M | ~60.5M |

Operating income grew 31% over three years, but diluted EPS grew only 7% over the same period because shares outstanding increased ~11%. This gap between operating income growth and EPS growth is the defining financial feature of this company: rate base expansion funds future earnings, but the equity issuance required to finance capex dilutes existing shareholders.

FY2025 was a recovery year from the weather-impaired FY2024. Adjusted EPS (including deferred carrying costs) was $4.48 vs. $3.94 in FY2024. The primary drivers were $92.2M in new rate revenue and $5.3M from customer growth, partially offset by $16.8M higher depreciation, $13.8M ad valorem taxes, and $12.8M higher employee costs.

### Balance Sheet & Capital Structure

Total assets reached $8.85B at FY2025. Long-term debt stood at $2.13B, with $737M in commercial paper outstanding, backstopped by a $1.5B revolving credit facility expanded in October 2025. Total debt-to-capital was 47.6% against a 70% covenant limit — ample headroom.

Credit ratings are A3/A- (Stable) from Moody's and S&P. A $250M unsecured term loan matures September 2026 and will need refinancing in the current higher-rate environment. The current ratio of 0.60 is typical for utilities relying on commercial paper programs.

### Cash Flow Deficit

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Operating Cash Flow | $940M | $368M | $579M |
| Capex (incl. asset removal) | ~$669M | ~$708M | ~$715M |
| Free Cash Flow | ~$271M | (~$340M) | (~$136M) |
| Dividends Paid | ~$146M | ~$150M | ~$163M |

FY2023 operating cash flow was elevated by working capital timing. The normalized FCF picture is negative, requiring external capital to fund both capex and dividends. Management guides to ~$800M capex in FY2026, so the FCF deficit will persist.

### Pension Headwind

Pension expense is expected to increase from $5.7M in FY2025 to $18.6M in FY2026 — a $12.9M incremental headwind that equates to roughly $0.15/share after tax. Management purchased a $41.6M group annuity contract in August 2025 to begin de-risking the plan, but the near-term cost step-up is not reflected in headline consensus estimates.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus EPS estimates show modest growth:
- FY2026E: $4.77 (+9.2% YoY)
- FY2027E: $4.98 (+4.4% YoY)

Estimate revisions are mildly positive: FY2026 is up +1.2% over 90 days, FY2027 up +1.2% over 90 days. The revision trend is constructive but not accelerating.

Growth drivers:
1. **Rate base expansion**: ~$800M annual capex on a ~$5.8B rate base implies ~6-7% annual rate base growth before dilution
2. **Customer additions**: ~23,000/year (~1% customer growth)
3. **Regulatory recovery mechanisms**: PBRC (OK), GSRS (KS), GRIP (TX) allow interim capital recovery between rate cases
4. **Texas HB 4384**: Enacted June 2025, allows deferral and recovery of costs on assets placed in service but not yet in rates — directly reduces regulatory lag
5. **Natural gas demand from power generation**: The Bennington pipeline project targets this secular trend

Growth limiters:
1. **Dilution**: ~3% annual share count growth erodes 40-50% of rate base growth on a per-share basis
2. **Rising operating costs**: Employee costs, taxes, insurance all increasing faster than inflation
3. **Pension step-up**: $12.9M incremental cost in FY2026
4. **Regulatory lag**: Despite interim mechanisms, formal rate cases remain the primary vehicle for earning authorized returns

The earnings growth profile is 4-6% annually on a per-share basis — consistent with a regulated utility but below the ~7% average for gas utilities with more favorable capital structures.

## Competitive Landscape

ONE Gas benefits from natural monopoly positions in its three-state footprint. No competitor can replicate its distribution infrastructure. The competitive dynamics are primarily regulatory: the company competes for favorable rate treatment and must demonstrate prudent management to justify authorized returns.

**Regulatory positioning is strong:**
- Oklahoma: 9.4% authorized ROE with annual PBRC streamlined reviews
- Texas: 9.8% authorized ROE with GRIP filings; HB 4384 reduces regulatory lag
- Kansas: GSRS provides interim capital recovery

The Texas consolidation (effective January 2026) into a single statewide division is operationally efficient but created a mixed outcome in the most recent rate case: $14.5M approved vs. $41.1M requested. While GRIP filings added $26.5M, the base rate case outcome suggests Texas regulators applied scrutiny to the initial request.

The structural risk from electrification is real but distant in ONE Gas's markets. Oklahoma, Kansas, and Texas are among the most natural gas-friendly states, with no building electrification mandates on the horizon. Growing natural gas demand for power generation (data centers, AI infrastructure) is a modest positive that the Bennington pipeline project targets directly.

## Risk Assessment

**Regulatory Execution (Medium-High):** The Oklahoma PBRC mandatory rate case filing (by June 2027, test year ending December 2026) is the most significant near-term event. Oklahoma represents the largest division, and any shortfall from the current 9.4% authorized ROE would materially impact earnings.

**Capital Market Access (Medium):** Persistent negative FCF requires ongoing equity issuances and debt financing. The $250M term loan maturing September 2026 will be refinanced at higher rates.

**Dilution (Medium):** The ~3% annual share count growth is a structural headwind. The ATM program has $225.5M remaining capacity, and a 2.5M share forward agreement must be settled by December 2026.

**Pension Cost Step-Up (Low-Medium):** The FY2026 pension expense increase may not be fully reflected in consensus estimates, translating to roughly $0.15/share after tax.

**Weather (Low-Medium):** While WNA mechanisms and fixed-charge structures provide partial insulation, FY2024 demonstrated that warmer-than-normal winters still reduce earnings. HDDs have been below normal for two consecutive years.

**Electrification (Low, Long-term):** ONE Gas's three-state footprint is among the most favorable for natural gas utilities. This risk is relevant on a 10+ year horizon but not a near-term concern.

## Investment Thesis

- Natural gas demand growth for power generation accelerates, supporting customer additions above 23,000/year

## Conclusion

ONE Gas is a competently managed, defensively positioned regulated gas utility with constructive regulatory relationships and steady rate base growth. The FY2025 results demonstrated the earnings power of the business when regulatory outcomes are favorable and weather normalizes. The stock's 3.1% yield and ~5% EPS growth trajectory produce a total return profile of 7-9% annually, fair compensation for a regulated utility.

At $88.09, the stock trades at the peer median multiple with no clear mispricing in either direction. The positive earnings revision trend is encouraging but marginal. Persistent negative FCF and associated dilution are the primary structural drags that prevent a higher conviction level. The pension cost step-up adds a discrete near-term headwind. For income-oriented investors already holding the position, the defensive characteristics and stable regulatory environment justify maintaining exposure. New capital would be better deployed at $78-80, where the yield and valuation math become more compelling.
