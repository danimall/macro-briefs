## Executive Summary

EnerSys is the global market leader in industrial stored energy solutions, operating across energy systems (data center/telecom backup), motive power (forklifts/AGVs), and specialty (defense/aerospace) segments. The company has executed a multi-year margin expansion from 22.3% gross margin in FY2022 to 30.2% in FY2025, driven by mix shift toward proprietary Thin Plate Pure Lead (TPPL) technology, pricing discipline, and IRA Section 45X manufacturing credits ($184.6M in FY2025). Revenue growth has been modest (+1% in FY2025) but earnings power has expanded substantially: EPS grew approximately 38% YoY in FY2025 to $8.99 and is forecast to continue growing in the mid-teens range.

The investment thesis centers on three converging tailwinds: (1) AI-driven data center construction creating secular demand for high-density backup power where TPPL technology is competitively advantaged, (2) a new CEO (Shawn O'Connell, effective May 23, 2025) executing an operational reset ("EnerGize") that targets cost reduction and portfolio focus, and (3) an early-stage telecom/broadband recovery after one of the deepest capex downturns since the 1990s. The critical risk is Section 45X dependency: roughly 40% of current operating earnings derive from IRA manufacturing credits whose political sustainability is uncertain.

## Company Overview

EnerSys generates $3.62B in annual revenue through four segments. Energy Systems (42.3% of revenue) supplies UPS systems, backup power, and thermal management enclosures to data centers, telecoms, and utilities. Motive Power (41.0%) provides batteries and chargers for material handling equipment — forklifts, AGVs, and warehouse automation. Specialty (16.4%) serves defense, aerospace, medical, and premium transportation markets. New Ventures (<1%) is an early-stage effort targeting EV fast charging and demand charge reduction.

The company operates in 100+ countries with ~10,858 employees and manufacturing across the Americas, EMEA, and Asia. No single customer represents more than 10% of revenue, a meaningful diversification advantage in an industry often characterized by OEM concentration. EnerSys holds a leadership position based on its proprietary TPPL battery technology, which offers superior power density and faster recharge versus conventional lead-acid, positioning it for data center applications requiring high-output, short-duration backup. Management states that "significant capital investment would be required" for competitors to replicate TPPL manufacturing, though the technology is not patent-protected.

## Financial Analysis

### Profitability Trajectory

EnerSys has delivered an exceptional margin expansion cycle:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Revenue | $3.36B | $3.71B | $3.58B | $3.62B |
| Gross Margin | 22.3% | 22.7% | 27.4% | 30.2% |
| Operating Margin | 6.1% | 7.5% | 9.8% | 12.8% |
| Net Income | $144M | $176M | $269M | $364M |
| EPS | $3.38 | $4.13 | $6.50 | $8.99 |

Net income has grown 153% over three years on essentially flat revenue, reflecting pure margin execution. However, approximately $185M of the $465M operating income in FY2025 derives from Section 45X credits booked as a COGS reduction. Normalizing for this, operating margin is approximately 7.7% and net income approximately $180M–$200M. The stock's current valuation is pricing in continued credit availability.

### Cash Flow and Balance Sheet

FY2025 operating cash flow of $260M was suppressed from $457M in the prior year due to approximately $192M in prepaid IRA tax credit receivables from the IRS. By Q2 FY2026, the company reported receipt of its U.S. federal tax refund, partially alleviating this cash flow headwind. Normalized FCF is closer to $300M-$350M. CapEx increased to $121M as the company expands TPPL capacity and invests in the planned lithium gigafactory.

Net debt of approximately $769M (total debt of $1,112M less $343M cash) against reported adjusted EBITDA implies moderate leverage on a reported basis. However, excluding the $184.6M of Section 45X benefits from EBITDA (approximately $559M reported per the proxy), underlying EBITDA is closer to $374M, implying ex-45X leverage of approximately 2.1x. Available credit lines of $653M provide ample liquidity. The current ratio of 2.7x shows no near-term liquidity stress. Debt-to-equity is approximately 0.58x ($1,112M total debt / $1,916M stockholders' equity).

Capital allocation is balanced: $154M in buybacks in FY2025 (reducing share count), $37.5M in dividends, and $206M for the Bren-Tronics defense acquisition. The buyback program at prices well below current levels was accretive.

### Earnings Beat Record

Four consecutive quarters of beats: +5.7%, +0.3%, +7.7%, +0.8%. The Q3 FY2026 result (adjusted EPS ex-45X of $1.84, up 50% YoY) was described as "a company record for our third fiscal quarter." However, reported GAAP diluted EPS of $2.40 was down 17% YoY, and adjusted diluted EPS including 45X of $2.77 was down 11%, reflecting the outsized 45X contribution in the prior-year comparable period. This divergence underscores the importance of monitoring both reported and ex-45X metrics.

## Growth Analysis

### Data Center Secular Tailwind

AI infrastructure buildout is creating unprecedented demand for backup power in data centers. EnerSys's TPPL batteries are suited for high-power-density, short-duration applications that modern hyperscale facilities require. This is the company's primary organic growth vector in Energy Systems and has already begun to offset the telecom weakness that depressed segment revenue 3.7% in FY2025. By Q3 FY2026, Energy Systems revenue was up 2.6% in the quarter and 8.2% for the first nine months, driven by robust data-center and industrial demand alongside recovering communications spending.

### Telecom Recovery

Management characterized the recent telecom/broadband capex pause as the deepest since the 1990s. Early "green shoots" are appearing, and Q3 FY2026 results confirmed recovering communications demand. 5G densification and fiber-to-home buildout are expected to restart investment in coming quarters. A normalized telecom environment would provide meaningful organic growth to Energy Systems, though specific timing and magnitude remain analyst estimates rather than confirmed guidance.

### Defense Expansion

The $206M Bren-Tronics acquisition (July 2024) gives EnerSys exposure to rising global defense budgets through portable lithium power for military applications. Specialty segment revenue grew 10.8% in FY2025 and benefits from the structural defense spending theme.

### Estimate Trajectory

Consensus estimates call for continued mid-teens EPS growth. Revisions are modestly positive (+2.4% over 90 days for current quarter, +1.1% for current year), directionally supportive and consistent with steady fundamental improvement, though not the explosive 30%+ revision velocity that signals strong momentum.

## Valuation Assessment

ENS trades at approximately 20-22x forward consensus EPS estimates, depending on the fiscal year used. This represents a reasonable multiple for a differentiated industrial company with mid-teens growth.

Relative to peers: the Electrical Components & Equipment peer median P/E is 86.2x (skewed by VICR at 86x, POWL at 49x, ST at 122x). ENS trades at a substantial discount to this median. However, these peers are smaller, more volatile businesses, and the comparison is imperfect. A more appropriate industrial peer comp would be Vertiv (25-30x), Eaton (30-35x), or Generac (25x) — companies with similar data center/power infrastructure exposure.

Against this cohort, ENS at ~21x forward is modestly cheap, particularly given its 14% growth rate and improving margin profile. The discount likely reflects legitimate 45X uncertainty rather than a market mispricing.

The stock has appreciated substantially from its 52-week low of approximately $76.60, but this reflects fundamental improvement (EPS more than doubled over three years), not mere multiple expansion.

**Analyst targets:** Based on available data, the consensus mean target is approximately $188 with a high around $208. The extent of current upside or downside to consensus depends on the current trading price, which should be verified at the time of investment decision.

## Competitive Landscape

EnerSys holds the #1 or #2 global position in industrial stored energy. Key competitive dynamics:

**TPPL Technology Moat:** Management states that replicating TPPL production requires "significant capital investment." The company has invested hundreds of millions in purpose-built facilities (Springfield, MO is the flagship TPPL plant). This is a genuine manufacturing process advantage with high barriers to entry, though TPPL technology is not patent-protected. Unlike commodity flooded lead-acid, TPPL requires specialized metallurgy and proprietary manufacturing know-how, creating a capital-intensity barrier rather than an IP barrier.

**Manufacturing Reshoring:** The Tijuana closure and consolidation to U.S. TPPL facilities achieves three objectives simultaneously: tariff mitigation, 45X credit maximization, and product mix upgrade. Competitors without U.S. manufacturing scale face a structural disadvantage if 45X credits persist.

**Data Center Positioning:** In the emerging high-power backup market for AI data centers, EnerSys's TPPL batteries are positioned as cost-effective solutions for short-duration, high-cycling applications. The company competes against lithium-ion solutions and UPS systems from various power infrastructure providers. TPPL's advantage is its established reliability and cost profile for specific backup duration requirements, positioning ENS in a defined niche within the broader data center power ecosystem.

**Customer Relationships:** No customer concentration risk (none >10%) and established sales channels across 100+ countries provide a durable distribution advantage that mid-market competitors cannot replicate quickly.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Section 45X Credit Dependency (HIGH)

This is the dominant idiosyncratic risk. $184.6M in FY2025 credits represents ~40% of operating earnings ($464.7M operating income). The political environment has already introduced uncertainty: executive orders paused IRA fund disbursements pending review, and the $199M DOE award for the Greenville lithium plant is under review. If credits are eliminated or significantly reduced, EPS would decline approximately 40-50% from current levels. The market partially prices this risk (explaining the discount to peers), but not fully.

Mitigant: Even without 45X, ENS would be a ~8% operating margin industrial company earning approximately $4.50-5.00/share, implying the stock would trade at approximately $90-115 (20-23x) in a no-credit scenario. Under current law, Section 45X credits apply in full through 2029, phase down over 2030-2032 (75%, 50%, 25% for most eligible components), and generally expire after 2032. Legislative risk before the statutory phaseout is the primary concern.

The ex-45X leverage profile of approximately 2.1x net debt/EBITDA means the balance sheet provides less cushion than headline metrics suggest in a credit-elimination scenario.

### 2. Revenue Growth Dependence on Telecom Recovery

FY2025 revenue was flat (+1%). Growth to the $10+ EPS level requires either: (a) telecom capex recovery, (b) data center revenue acceleration, or (c) continued margin expansion. While Q3 FY2026 data confirms early recovery momentum, sustained multi-quarter growth remains to be proven. If telecom remains depressed and data center ramp is slower than expected, consensus EPS may prove too optimistic.

### 3. Tariff Exposure

~40% international revenue and global supply chains create tariff vulnerability. The Tijuana closure addresses one vector, but components sourced from Asia (electronics, lithium cells) remain exposed. Management cites 6-9 month lag for pricing pass-through.

### 4. Verkor/Gigafactory Execution

The planned lithium-ion gigafactory with French startup Verkor SAS in South Carolina depends on $199M DOE funding (under review), Section 45X credits, and successful technology transfer. This is an option with binary outcomes: success creates a lithium cell manufacturing capability; failure wastes invested capital and leaves the company reliant on third-party cells.

### Options Market Signal

Based on available data, near-term IV of approximately 42.9% sits modestly rich to 38.3% historical volatility (4.6pp spread), with elevated put skew in the near-term expiry. The flat term structure suggests uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a specific catalyst. Put/call OI ratio is extremely low, indicating minimal structural hedging demand. The 6-month expiry shows put volume modestly exceeding call volume, suggesting some institutional protection at the intermediate horizon. Overall, options pricing appears moderately cautious but not signaling acute stress. (Note: options and short-interest data are time-sensitive and should be verified with current market data sources.)

Short interest at approximately 4.2% of float with 3.4 days to cover is unremarkable — neither crowded nor absent. This confirms modest skepticism rather than concentrated bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (25-30% upside from current levels)

Data center revenue accelerates beyond consensus as AI infrastructure buildout intensifies. Telecom continues recovering momentum seen in Q3 FY2026. Section 45X credits remain intact through their statutory phaseout period. EnerGize cost program delivers ahead of $20M savings target. FY2028 EPS reaches $13-14, and the stock trades at 20x forward.

### Bear Case (35-45% downside)

Section 45X credits are eliminated or reduced by 50%+ through legislative action. Telecom recovery stalls. Data center growth is captured by lithium-ion competitors. EPS normalizes to $4.50-5.50 without credits. Stock trades at 20-23x normalized = $90-125.

### Base Case (15-20% upside)

Credits persist in current form through FY2028 at minimum. Data center growth provides mid-single-digit organic revenue growth. EnerGize delivers modest cost savings. FY2028 EPS reaches $11.50-12.00. Stock trades at 21-22x forward.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalysts: Q4 FY2026 earnings (scheduled May 20, 2026; Q4 guidance: adjusted EPS ex-45X of $1.91-$2.01), FY2027 guidance, legislative clarity on IRA credits (H2 2026), telecom recovery evidence in Q1-Q2 FY2027.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Legislative action reducing or eliminating Section 45X credits by >50% — immediate exit.
2. Two consecutive quarters of Energy Systems revenue decline >5% YoY (would signal data center thesis is not materializing).
3. Management guides FY2027 EPS below $9.50 (would indicate fundamental deterioration beyond credit risk).

## Conclusion

EnerSys presents a differentiated industrial growth story anchored by proprietary TPPL technology, structural data center demand, and defense spending tailwinds. The valuation at approximately 21x forward earnings is reasonable for a company delivering mid-teens EPS growth with multiple expansion catalysts. The critical binary risk — Section 45X credit dependency representing ~40% of operating earnings — prevents maximum conviction and demands moderate position sizing (2-3% portfolio weight). The combination of favorable growth visibility, a new CEO executing operational improvements, competitive moats in a secularly growing market, and reasonable valuation supports a **BUY** recommendation. The PEG of ~1.5x on structural growth compares favorably to power infrastructure peers trading at 25-35x forward.

Position sizing should reflect the 45X binary: this is a 2-3% position with clear exit criteria rather than a high-conviction overweight.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The valuation framework in this report contains fiscal-year labeling inconsistencies and uses a stock price/market cap that may not reflect the current trading level; the ex-45X leverage is approximately 2.1x (not 1.3x), and normalized ex-45X net income is closer to $180M-$200M (not $200M-$225M). These corrections increase the downside severity in a credit-elimination scenario and may reduce the base-case upside range. Investors should verify the current market price and rebuild forward P/E calculations accordingly before acting on this recommendation.
