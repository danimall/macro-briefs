## Executive Summary

Trimble is executing one of the more compelling industrial-to-software pivots in the market today. The company has divested lower-margin hardware businesses (Ag to AGCO for $1.9B, Mobility to Platform Science), expanded gross margins from 57% to 69% over three years, and achieved 14% organic ARR growth to $2.39B. Q1 FY2026 (reported May 6, 2026) showed revenue of $940M (+11.8% YoY reported), non-GAAP EPS of $0.79 vs. $0.73 consensus, and a guidance raise to $3.55 non-GAAP EPS for FY2026.

Despite these fundamentals, the stock closed at $63.42 on May 6, 2026, falling 7.24% on earnings day, placing it at approximately 17.9x FY2026E non-GAAP EPS of $3.55. This is materially below where comparable vertical software businesses trade (25-30x) and suggests the market remains skeptical about the durability of the transformation or is focused on risks not captured in headline earnings beats. The discount reflects lingering concern around the material weakness in internal controls (which remains unresolved with an adverse ICFR opinion from KPMG), macro sensitivity in construction end markets, and a 5-year TSR that has trailed peers by approximately 140 percentage points.

Analyst consensus price targets range from $79-$103, implying meaningful upside from the current price. Short interest is minimal at 2.6% of float. The primary risks are the unresolved material weakness remediation and potential construction activity slowdowns from tariff uncertainty and elevated interest rates.

## Company Overview

Trimble operates at the intersection of physical-world positioning technology and enterprise workflow software, serving construction, transportation, and geospatial industries. The company generates approximately $3.6B in annual revenue across three segments:

- **AECO (Architecture, Engineering, Construction & Owners):** 42% of revenue ($1.50B), 34.2% segment operating margin. Software platforms for building design, construction management, and owner operations.
- **Field Systems:** 43% of revenue ($1.54B), 31.1% segment operating margin. GNSS hardware, machine control systems, and positioning software. Includes JVs with Caterpillar, Nikon, and Hilti.
- **Transportation & Logistics:** 15% of revenue ($549M), 21.9% segment operating margin. Carrier/shipper TMS, Transporeon marketplace.

The "Connect & Scale" strategy aims to create an end-to-end platform connecting design → build → operate across the construction lifecycle, generating recurring subscription revenue with deep switching costs.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Margins:**

Total FY2025 revenue of $3.59B declined 3% reported, but organic revenue grew 6% after adjusting for divestitures (10% headwind) and the 53rd-week comparison. Subscription & services revenue grew 9% organically, and ARR reached $2.39B (+14% organic).

Gross margins expanded from 57.3% (FY2022) to 65.1% (FY2024) to 69.1% (FY2025). This 1,180bps improvement over three years reflects the mix shift toward software, services, and recurring revenue — now 79% of total revenue — and the exit of lower-margin hardware portfolios. Non-GAAP operating margin reached 27.5%, up 210bps YoY. Adjusted EBITDA was $1.05B (29.2% margin).

**Q1 FY2026 Results:**

Revenue of $940M grew 11.8% YoY on a reported basis. Non-GAAP EPS of $0.79 beat consensus by approximately 8% and grew 30% YoY from $0.61. Management raised full-year guidance to ~$3.875B revenue and ~$3.55 non-GAAP EPS, with Q2 guided at ~$950.5M revenue. Despite the headline beat, the stock fell 7.24% on the day of the report, suggesting the market focused on factors beyond the top-line numbers — potentially guidance quality, organic vs. reported growth mix, or the ongoing accounting overhang.

**Free Cash Flow:**

FY2025 FCF of $361M was depressed by a $277M Ag divestiture tax payment. Normalizing for this, underlying FCF is approximately $638M, implying a ~4% FCF yield on the current ~$15B market cap — reasonable for a company investing heavily in platform development.

**Balance Sheet:**

Debt of $1.4B (fixed-rate senior notes) against EBITDA of $1.05B yields net leverage of approximately 1.1x. Cash of $253M is thin, but the undrawn $1.25B revolving credit facility provides ample liquidity. Goodwill of $5.24B (56% of total assets) reflects the acquisition-heavy history and creates impairment risk in a downturn.

**Capital Return:**

Trimble repurchased approximately $875M in stock during FY2025 and authorized a new $1B buyback program in December 2025. At current prices around $63, this represents significant capital return. The combination of declining share count (241.5M diluted, down from 249.1M in FY2023) and expanding EPS creates a compounding effect.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three vectors:

1. **ARR Compounding:** At 14% organic growth, $2.39B ARR is the primary value driver. Construction digitization penetration remains low (estimated <30% for advanced workflows), providing years of runway. Cross-selling across the connected platform (design, build, operate) increases customer LTV.

2. **Margin Expansion:** The path from 27.5% to 30%+ non-GAAP operating margin is visible as the software mix increases and divested hardware costs fully roll off. Each percentage point of operating margin at $3.8B revenue adds approximately $38M in operating income.

3. **AI Monetization:** The Document Crunch acquisition (AI-powered contract analytics for construction) represents Trimble's strategy to layer AI capabilities on its proprietary data estate of physical-world information. Construction-specific AI applications face limited competition from horizontal AI providers who lack domain training data.

The Q1 reported growth of 11.8% YoY compares to 6% organic growth in FY2025, though direct comparison is complicated by the impact of divestitures, FX, and the 53rd-week year-ago comparison. A like-for-like organic Q1 growth figure would provide clearer confirmation of acceleration. If FY2026 delivers $3.875B at 27.5-28% operating margins, the earnings power approaches $3.55-3.70 non-GAAP EPS — representing approximately 13-18% growth over FY2025.

## Valuation Assessment

At $63.42 (May 6 close), Trimble trades at approximately 17.9x FY2026E non-GAAP EPS of $3.55, and approximately 15.7x FY2027E of $4.04. These multiples are cheap for the quality of the business:

- **Gross margin of 69%** places Trimble in the territory of pure-play software companies, not industrial hardware.
- **14% organic ARR growth** at this margin profile typically commands 25-35x forward earnings.
- **Peer comparison within Electronic Equipment & Instruments** suggests Trimble trades at a meaningful discount. On a forward P/E basis, peers such as Keysight (KEYS, ~29x forward), Teledyne (TDY, ~26x forward), Roper (ROP, ~21x forward), and Zebra (ZBRA, ~14x forward) show a range that generally exceeds Trimble's current ~18x forward multiple, particularly among the higher-quality names.

The discount reflects: (a) the material weakness overhang creating accounting uncertainty, (b) residual perception of TRMB as a hardware company despite 79% software/services/recurring revenue, (c) the 5-year TSR underperformance creating skepticism about re-rating timing, and (d) whatever factors drove the 7% post-earnings selloff on May 6.

At 25x FY2026E EPS of $3.55, fair value is approximately $89 — consistent with analyst consensus targets. At 22x (applying a discount for accounting concerns and post-earnings uncertainty), fair value is $78.

Applying FY2027E EPS of $4.04 at 22-25x yields a range of $89-101, consistent with the analyst target range of $79-$103.

## Competitive Landscape

Trimble's moat derives from the integration of physical-world positioning data with enterprise workflow software. Few competitors span both layers:

- **Autodesk (ADSK)** is the primary competitor in AECO software but lacks Trimble's positioning hardware and field data collection capabilities.
- **Hexagon AB** competes in positioning/geospatial but has a weaker construction lifecycle platform.
- **Procore (PCOR)** competes in construction project management but at a narrower slice of the workflow.

The strategic JVs with Caterpillar (machine control), Nikon (survey), Hilti (building measurement), and AGCO (agriculture) create both competitive advantages (deep integration into OEM equipment) and risks (partner-becomes-competitor). The JV relationships lock in distribution for years but create dependency.

Construction industry digitization is early-innings. Most workflows still rely on manual processes, paper drawings, and disconnected point solutions. Trimble's connected platform approach — from architectural design through field execution to asset operations — creates switching costs that compound with adoption depth. Each competitor (Autodesk in design, Procore in management, Oracle in ERP) holds a piece of the value chain, and consolidation is inevitable.

## Risk Assessment

**Material Weakness in Internal Controls:** This is the dominant idiosyncratic risk. Trimble identified material weaknesses in business combination accounting, then discovered additional weaknesses during remediation. This caused four delayed SEC filings, Nasdaq non-compliance, and an auditor change (EY dismissed, KPMG appointed in April 2025 amid material-weakness disclosures, though no disagreements were reported with EY). KPMG issued an adverse opinion on internal control over financial reporting in the FY2025 10-K. The company hired a new CAO (Kenneth Bement, Sept 2025) with FASB experience and is pursuing remediation, but the material weaknesses remained unresolved as of the FY2025 10-K filing date. Management has stated that remediation will not be considered complete until controls operate for a sufficient period and are tested as effective. For a company with $5.2B in goodwill and 23 divestitures since 2020, this is not trivial. The 10-K notes that the deficiencies did not result in a material misstatement to the FY2025 consolidated financial statements.

**Macro Sensitivity:** Construction activity is sensitive to interest rates and economic confidence. Tariff uncertainty may delay new project starts. However, 79% recurring revenue provides a floor — once implemented, construction technology platforms are deeply embedded in workflows and don't get cancelled with individual projects.

**Tariff/Hardware Exposure:** Despite the software shift, $1.14B in hardware revenue remains, with supply chain dependencies on Asian manufacturing (via Jabil, Benchmark Electronics). Trimble has diversified its fulfillment network to regional distribution, partially mitigating direct tariff impact.

**AI Disruption:** Generative AI could commoditize some software capabilities. However, construction-specific AI requires domain data that horizontal AI providers lack. The greater risk is that hyperscalers enable smaller competitors to develop capabilities faster than Trimble can innovate.

**Insider Selling:** CEO Painter sells 7,500 shares monthly under a 10b5-1 plan, and SVP Schwartz sold ~$1.1M post-vesting. No open-market purchases. This is routine diversification for a CEO with significant equity exposure (comp = $19.1M/year), given the simultaneous guidance raise and $1B buyback authorization.

### Options Market Signal

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($89-103, 40-62% upside from $63.42):**
- Market re-rates TRMB from industrial technology multiple (18-20x) toward vertical software multiple (25-30x) as ARR approaches $3B and software/services/recurring revenue exceeds 85%.
- FY2027 delivers $4.00-4.20 non-GAAP EPS on continued organic growth acceleration and operating leverage.
- Material weakness remediation completes without further issues, removing the accounting overhang.
- AI monetization inflects through construction-specific applications built on proprietary data.
- At 25x $4.04 FY2027E EPS = $101.

**Bear Case ($50-54, 15-21% downside from $63.42):**
- Construction recession from sustained high interest rates and tariff uncertainty compresses organic growth to low-single digits.
- Material weakness issues recur or expand, triggering renewed Nasdaq concerns and investor confidence loss.
- Multiple compresses to 14-15x as market maintains industrial hardware perception.
- Key JV partner (e.g., Caterpillar) develops competing technology in-house.
- At 14x $3.55 FY2026E EPS = $50.

## Conclusion

Trimble represents a well-advanced software transformation trading at a notable discount to its fundamental quality. The 69% gross margin, 14% organic ARR growth, and 27.5% non-GAAP operating margin profile warrant a meaningfully higher multiple than the current ~18x forward earnings. The Q1 beat and guidance raise confirm execution on headline metrics, though the 7.24% post-earnings selloff on May 6 signals that the market is not yet ready to re-rate the stock — likely reflecting the unresolved material weakness (with KPMG's adverse ICFR opinion), questions about the quality or composition of growth, or broader positioning ahead of macro uncertainty.

The material weakness issue is the primary governance concern and the most likely explanation for the persistent discount. Remediation remains incomplete, and until management can demonstrate sustained effective controls tested over a sufficient period, this overhang will persist. The stock offers approximately 2:1 upside-to-downside on a base case and is appropriate for investors who can tolerate the accounting uncertainty and near-term negative sentiment for 12-18 months while the software economics become undeniable to the broader market.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The post-earnings selloff (-7.24% on May 6) and unresolved material weakness with adverse ICFR opinion challenge the near-term re-rating thesis; the updated stock price ($63.42) improves the mathematical upside but the market's rejection of the Q1 "beat" warrants caution on timing.
