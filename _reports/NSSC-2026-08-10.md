## Executive Summary

NAPCO has become a recurring-revenue company. In the March 2026 quarter, service revenue of $24.929M was 50.7% of sales but roughly 76% of gross profit (service $24.929M × 90.4% ≈ $22.5M against total gross profit of $29.489M). The engine is cellular communicators — principally StarLink fire radios replacing retiring copper POTS lines in commercial buildings — sold through distributors and monetized as monthly connectivity at ~90% gross margin. That stream grew 15.4% in Q3 FY2026 and is at or near a $100M annualized run rate. The rest of the business (door locking, intrusion, access control) is a modest-margin hardware operation that exists largely to install the radios.

The financial profile is strong: no funded debt, $124.95M of cash and marketable securities, an undrawn $20M revolver, FY2025 free cash flow of $51.411M on $181.621M of revenue (28.3% margin, above net income), a 12–16% effective tax rate courtesy of the Dominican Republic free-zone plant, and an Altman Z-score of 7.14. Four consecutive non-GAAP beats of 10–22% support confidence in near-term consensus.

The rating is a HOLD rather than an accumulation candidate because of the combination of price and the quality of the growth beneath the headline. At $40.775 the stock is 27.4x our estimated FY2026 ex-item EPS of ~$1.49 and 25.2x FY2027 consensus $1.62 — essentially the 27.0x peer median — while consensus FY2027 earnings growth is only 8.7%. The equipment "recovery" is narrower than the +10.9% nine-month print suggests: intrusion and access revenue grew 3.3% on price of +10.8% against volume of **−7.5%**, and even after roughly 10% cumulative price increases, FY2026 equipment revenue looks likely to land near $105M, still ~7% below the FY2024 peak of $113.09M. Layer on a $16M securities settlement that is accrued but not yet paid, a second class action (Patel, class period 2/5/2024–2/3/2025) carrying **no accrual and no company-estimable range**, a founder who has reduced his stake ~71% with no offsetting insider buying anywhere in the 27-month Form 4 record, a share-repurchase program that went from 1,220,899 shares at an average $30.14 in FY2025 to zero in the first nine months of FY2026 with only ~1.0% of shares still authorized, and the promotion of the former CFO — a named defendant in all three pending actions and the officer in place during the FY2024 inventory-costing material weakness — to CEO in July 2026. Base case $46 (+12.8%); bear case $29 (−28.9%). That asymmetry does not justify new capital ahead of an imminent Q4/FY2026 print.

## Company Overview

NAPCO, founded 1969 and headquartered in Amityville, NY, designs and manufactures electronic security hardware and sells cellular connectivity services. It reports one operating segment with two revenue streams and three product lines. Nine-month FY2026 revenue: door-locking products $48.819M, intrusion and access alarm products $25.481M, services $72.207M — $146.507M total, +11.9% year over year.

Brands include NAPCO Security Systems, Alarm Lock, NAPCO Access Pro, Marks USA and Continental Instruments. Recurring-revenue product families are StarLink (commercial fire and intrusion communicators), Prima, MVP Access, iSecure and iBridge.

Two structural features define the business. First, route to market: NAPCO sells to independent distributors, dealers, installers and central stations, **with no long-term agreements** — business runs on purchase orders, distributors also carry competitors' lines, and some receive extended payment terms. Reported revenue is therefore partly a function of distributor inventory decisions, which is exactly what happened in FY2025 when equipment revenue fell 15.7% and one distributor alone drove a $6.4M decline. Second, manufacturing: essentially all production runs through a single Dominican Republic free-trade-zone facility on a 99-year land lease expiring 2092, and the FY2025 10-K states **no alternative third-party factory has been identified**. The free-zone status exempts NAPCO from Dominican income tax and is the reason the effective rate was 13% in FY2025 and 12% in FY2024. Long-lived assets at 3/31/26: DR $8.540M, US $5.732M. Revenue is 98.8% domestic ($144.813M US vs. $1.694M foreign for 9M FY2026): a US-demand business on an offshore cost base.

The demand thesis management articulates is the multi-year replacement of copper phone lines used for commercial fire signaling. Because commercial fire is code-driven and AHJ-inspected, conversion is non-discretionary once copper service is withdrawn, and swapping out a UL-listed communicator is a permitted job, creating real switching friction once installed. Management describes fire radios as the majority of the installed RSR base and the runway as "several years," with the cloud MVP access platform as the next catalyst and "meaningful revenue contribution expected later in 2026." These call comments are secondary-sourced (Security Systems News, May 20 2026) rather than filing-verified, and we found no independent sizing of the remaining conversion pool.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue: a cyclical round trip, with a narrower recovery than the headline

| Period | Revenue | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| FY2022 | $143.593M | — |
| FY2023 | $169.997M | +18.4% |
| FY2024 | $188.820M | +11.1% |
| FY2025 | $181.621M | −3.8% |
| 9M FY2026 | $146.507M | +11.9% |

FY2025's decline was entirely equipment: equipment revenue $95.291M (−15.7%) against service $86.330M (+14.0%). MD&A attributes it to distributor destocking, the absence of a large FY2024 commercial real-estate project, and tariff-driven softness, and quantifies customer-level impacts ($6.4M from one distributor, $9.4M from three door-locking customers).

The FY2026 recovery decomposes as follows (9M):

| Line | Revenue | YoY | Price | Volume |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Door locking | $48.819M | +15.3% | +6.3% | +9.0% |
| Intrusion & access | $25.481M | +3.3% | +10.8% | **−7.5%** |
| Services | $72.207M | +13.0% | n/a | n/a |

This is the single most important disclosure in the 10-Q. Intrusion and access grew only because NAPCO pushed roughly 11 points of price through a line whose units are still shrinking (the 10-Q attributes the volume decline primarily to access control). Door locking is genuinely healthy but decelerated within the year: Q3-only price/volume was +5.0%/+5.4% versus +6.3%/+9.0% for nine months. Equipment growth slowed from +10.9% (9M) to +8.4% (Q3), while service accelerated from +13.0% to +15.4%.

Sizing the equipment gap: Q4 FY2025 equipment revenue was ~$28.29M (FY2025 $95.291M less 9M FY2025 $67.0M). At ~8% Q4 growth, FY2026 equipment lands near $105M — roughly 7% below the FY2024 peak of $113.09M, *after* cumulative price increases of ~10%. Underlying unit volumes remain materially below FY2024 two years later. That is our calculation on a stated Q4 assumption, not guidance, and the alternative reading of Q4 re-acceleration as the MVP platform ships is plausible but unevidenced.

### Margins: mix shift is doing the heavy lifting

| Period | Gross margin | Equipment GM | Service GM |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2024 | 53.9% | 29.4% | 90.5% |
| FY2025 | 55.6% | 23.6% | 91.0% |
| 9M FY2026 | 58.4% | 27.4% | 90.3% |
| Q3 FY2026 | 60.0% | 28.7% | 90.4% |

Equipment margin is recovering from the FY2025 trough (tariff cost plus fixed-cost under-absorption) toward the FY2024 level. The pricing-discipline claim is corroborated in the filings rather than only on the call: returns, rebates and allowances fell to 5% of gross revenue in 9M FY2026 from 7%, and refund liabilities declined to $3.756M from $4.790M.

One historical caution: FY2023→FY2024 gross margin stepped up 10.8 points, with COGS *falling* from $96.764M to $87.066M while revenue rose $18.8M. The benign explanation — service mix shift plus normalization of the FY2022–23 semiconductor and freight cost spike — is the one we weight most heavily, and we found no disclosure that FY2024 annual results were restated. But that same window is the subject of the FY2024 inventory-costing material weakness and the Patel class period, so we would not treat the FY2024 margin level as an unquestioned baseline.

### The GAAP/non-GAAP wedge

Reported 9M FY2026 operating income was $27.208M, down 20.4%, solely because of the $16.0M litigation settlement charge booked in Q3. Ex-charge: 9M operating income $43.208M (+26.4%), 29.5% margin vs. 26.1%; 9M net income $39.527M (+24.4%) vs. $31.774M; Q3 operating income $14.812M, a 30.1% margin vs. 25.4%. Q3 ex-item EPS of ~$0.39 on ~35.83M diluted shares matches the reported non-GAAP $0.39, which beat non-GAAP consensus of ~$0.34 on in-line revenue of $49.167M. GAAP Q3 was a $(0.01) loss and should not be compared to the $0.34 non-GAAP consensus. The Q3 effective tax rate of (102.0)% is an artifact of a near-zero pre-tax base.

### Cash flow and the settlement timing

FY2025: operating cash flow $53.527M, capex $2.116M, FCF $51.411M (28.3% of revenue, above net income of $43.406M). 9M FY2026: OCF $43.491M, capex $1.512M, FCF $41.979M.

The $16.0M settlement was **accrued, not paid** — it sits in current liabilities at 3/31/26. Net income fell $14.267M, the accrual added $16.0M back through working capital, and the ~$1.733M tax benefit appears non-cash, so the effect on 9M FY2026 OCF was approximately neutral (the +$4.779M deferred-tax source of cash and the deferred tax asset falling from $6.476M to $1.697M complicate exact attribution). The consequence is prospective: roughly $16M of cash exits in a future period, subject to documentation and court approval — about 31% of one year of FY2025 free cash flow. FY2027 FCF should be modeled net of that. We have not annualized quarterly cash flow; NAPCO's working capital swings with distributor ordering and FY2025 showed those swings are large.

### Balance sheet

At 3/31/26: cash $114.408M plus marketable securities $10.544M = $124.952M. No funded debt; the $20M HSBC revolver (SOFR + ~1.26–1.36%, expires 2/9/2029) is undrawn but secured by substantially all domestic assets with subsidiary stock pledges and covenants restricting dividends and borrowings. Total assets $222.009M, total liabilities $44.227M (of which $16.0M is the litigation accrual), equity $177.782M. Current ratio 4.95x, down from 6.75x at 6/30/25; the decline is the accrual, not operational deterioration. Inventories $33.384M current plus $10.012M non-current, reserve $4.915M (down from $5.515M). Receivables $28.527M. FY2025 screens: ROA 21.9%, ROE 25.7%, net margin 23.9%. Reported D/E of 0.18 reflects total liabilities; interest-bearing leverage is zero, and NAPCO earns interest rather than paying it ($2.618M interest income plus $0.346M other income in 9M FY2026).

**Financial health score: 7.5.** Cash generation, conversion above 100%, zero debt and a low tax rate are unambiguous. Held below 8 by the pending ~$16M cash outflow, the unreserved Patel exposure, and a control history (2023 Item 4.02 non-reliance; FY2024 inventory-costing material weakness remediated 6/30/25; two changes in certifying accountant within seven days in October–November 2023) that argues against treating reported figures as beyond question.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on RSR. Q3 service revenue of $24.929M annualizes to $99.7M; management's ">$100M annualized run rate" implies a March exit rate of ≥~$8.33M/month. Either way the milestone is at hand, at >90% gross margin. Growth has been 13–15% and the driver — copper retirement forcing cellular conversion in code-inspected commercial fire systems — has durable direction even if magnitude is unverified.

Three constraints temper the score:

1. **Equipment is the RSR funnel.** Radios get installed on hardware jobs. Sustained equipment unit weakness — intrusion/access volumes −7.5% for 9M, −5.1% in Q3 — eventually starves RSR additions. The two streams are not independent.
2. **Price-led growth is not repeatable.** A second consecutive year of double-digit price increases in a category with declining units is unlikely, and part of the increase was tariff pass-through (cost recovery, not pricing power).
3. **Consensus growth is modest.** FY2026 consensus $1.49 to FY2027 $1.62 is +8.7%. Estimates are stable (current year +1.4% over 90 days, next year +0.6%) with only two contributing analysts; the current-quarter estimate was trimmed 5% to $0.38 in the last 30 days. There is no estimate-revision inflection signal: the +30%/90-day pattern that would argue for aggressive positioning is absent.

Against that, the beat record (four straight, +10.3% to +21.6%) suggests $1.62 is beatable; we model FY2027 nearer $1.70–1.75. R&D remains 6.9% of revenue ($12.581M FY2025; $10.131M 9M FY2026), and the January 2026 StarLink Connect MAX2 (tri-carrier 5G) plus the cloud MVP access platform are identified vehicles. MVP's "meaningful revenue later in 2026" is the one identifiable step-function catalyst, and it is unquantified.

**Growth prospects: 6.0.** Real, high-quality, mid-teens recurring growth diluted by a hardware business whose units are still below the 2024 peak.

Peer medians in Electronic Equipment & Instruments are P/E 27.0 (DBD 24.5, CXT 22.0, OSIS 27.0, BMI 32.5, MIR 177.7). NSSC at 25–27x forward sits right at that median. It deserves a premium on balance sheet and margin structure — zero debt versus levered peers, 58% gross margin, a 12–16% tax rate — but the growth differential that would justify a large premium is not evident in consensus (8.7% FY2027 EPS growth versus BMI, which we regard as roughly flat-to-modest, and OSIS). On PEG, 25.2x against even a generous mid-teens earnings growth assumption gives ~1.7 — above the threshold at which we would treat analyst targets as stale floors rather than consensus extrapolation. The $48–53 target band (mean $50.33, six covering analysts) implies 18–30% upside, but with only two analysts maintaining estimates it is thin corroboration, not independent confirmation.

A more useful sanity check is sum-of-parts. Value the RSR stream at 12x annualized revenue ($99.7M → $1,196M) — a defensible multiple for a 90%-gross-margin annuity growing 15% with UL-listing switching friction — add the equipment business at ~1.0x revenue ($105M) and $125M of net liquid assets, and you get $1,426M against a $1,457M market cap. At 14x RSR the figure is $1,626M, or $45.5 per share. The market is already paying close to full value for the annuity and giving the hardware business little credit, which is analytically correct but leaves limited slack.

**Valuation: 5.2.** Fairly priced with modest upside. There is no identifiable mispricing to underwrite: the multiple is at the peer median, the FCF yield is ~3% pro forma for the settlement, and the near-term earnings estimate has been trimmed. Unquantified IEEPA tariff-refund optionality (the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling and March 2026 CIT refund ruling, with no amount recorded) is a genuine off-model positive but cannot be sized and is partially offset by replacement tariffs under other statutes.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K's own framing — approximately 12 US competitors, most of which "may have substantially greater resources" — is the right lens. NAPCO is a ~$200M-revenue specialist against divisions of far larger diversified hardware and access-control companies, and against dedicated alarm-communication vendors in its highest-margin niche.

The most consequential external datapoint we found is Telguard's April 8, 2026 announcement of the **TG-7FX Sole Path Fire Communicator**, a streamlined cellular solution for commercial fire alarm systems. That is a targeted entry into precisely the category that generates NAPCO's ~90%-margin annuity, and NAPCO's January 2026 StarLink Connect MAX2 launch reads as the response. The 90%+ margin is the most attractive and therefore most contestable line in the P&L. RSR gross margin is already drifting down modestly — 90.3% in 9M FY2026 vs. 91.1% prior year, 90.4% vs. 90.8% in Q3. The drift is small and could equally reflect carrier cost or product mix within RSR, but it is the earliest indicator we would monitor for price competition.

On the equipment side, the persistent volume decline in access control (−7.5% for 9M, −5.1% in Q3, with growth carried entirely by price) is the datapoint most consistent with share loss rather than destocking, since management's own narrative says the channel has normalized ("the channel seems smooth right now") and door-locking volumes have turned positive. The alternatives — longer project cycles in access, or deliberate de-emphasis in favor of the cloud MVP platform — cannot be distinguished from the available disclosure.

Durable advantages remain: the DR free-zone cost and tax base (a real, structural edge in hardware, and the reason net margin screens at 23.9%), an installed base of code-inspected fire communicators with genuine replacement friction, and 55+ years of distributor relationships. The offsetting structural weakness is that NAPCO does not own the end customer — the dealer does — and has no contractual lock on distributors.

**Competitive position: 5.5.** Solid moat in the annuity, but trajectory is stable-to-slightly-eroding at the margin: direct competitive entry into the core RSR category, RSR margin drifting down, and access-control units contracting.

## Risk Assessment

**Litigation — the largest unpriced item.** Three active matters. *Zornberg* (EDNY, class period 11/7/2022–8/18/2023) reached a settlement in principle on 5/1/2026 at $16.0M **net of insurance reimbursements**, accrued in Q3 and pending court approval. *Patel* (EDNY, filed 4/25/2025) names the Company, Chairman/CEO and former CFO; co-lead plaintiffs were appointed 3/10/2026, with defendants' response due 7/13/2026. **No accrual; the company states it cannot estimate a range of loss.** *Minzer* (Delaware Chancery) is a derivative action alleging ineffective ICFR and inventory controls plus breach of fiduciary duty and unjust enrichment relating to stock sales by the Chairman/CEO and former CFO. No accrual. Given Zornberg settled at $16M net of insurance *after class certification*, a second case covering a later period is a material open exposure and we would not assume carriers absorb it. Patel's class period maps onto the FY2024 window of record gross margins and the inventory-costing material weakness; the plaintiffs' theory appears to run from the February 2024 disclosures to the February 3, 2025 miss. We are not asserting FY2024 margins were misstated — the temporal overlap is suggestive of the theory, not proof.

**Single-site manufacturing (high severity, low probability).** Essentially all production is in one DR facility with no identified alternative. A hurricane, political disruption or labor event has no near-term mitigation, and relocation would destroy the ~13% tax rate. This tail is not diversifiable without breaking the economics.

**Customer concentration is worsening on the only direct measure.** No customer exceeded 10% of net revenues in FY2025 or 9M FY2026, but at 3/31/26 three customers were 10%, 13% and 14% of accounts receivable (~37% combined) versus two at 11% and 13% (~24%) at 6/30/25. AR concentration at a single date is sensitive to ordering and payment timing, so this is not equivalent to a revenue-concentration test, but the mix shift toward service has not diversified the counterparty base, and FY2025 demonstrated that individual distributor decisions can move consolidated revenue by 3–5 points.

**Other items:** RSR deceleration is the largest valuation risk given service is ~76% of gross profit; tariffs are two-sided (unquantified refund optionality against continuing exposure under non-IEEPA authorities, plus fuel cost inflation the 10-Q attributes to conflict in Iran); a portion of locking/access demand depends on federal and state school-security funding; FX sensitivity is modest (±$815K annual operating income on a 10% USD/DOP move); and key-person risk is reassigned rather than resolved by the July 2026 transition (disclosed ages 79 and 72 in the FY2025 10-K). The filings also carry standalone risk factors on stockholder activism and short selling, unusual at this size.

**Risk factors: 4.5.** The unreserved second class action and the single-site concentration are specific, material and not obviously priced at a peer-median multiple.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin (24 contracts across three expirations), so read it lightly. Near-term ATM IV of 42.4% against 42.6% one-year historical vol is fair — no near-term event premium, and the ±6.5% implied move for the August 21 expiry is unremarkable. The informative tenor is September 18 (38 days), which spans the expected Q4 FY2026 / annual print: IV rises to 50.8% with a 10.0% put/call skew that the data source characterizes as elevated put demand, and a 13.8% implied move. The December tenor shows a −6.6% skew. Put/call open-interest ratio of 0.04 means very little standing put inventory, so the near-dated skew is priced but not positioned in size. Term structure is in contango (42.4% near vs. 45.9% long), a complacent shape.

Separately, short interest is 10.0% of float at 6.8 days to cover — meaningful institutional bearish positioning. The most plausible short theses are the price-led equipment growth, the unreserved Patel exposure, and the multiple. That positioning cuts both ways: a strong Q4 print with a new buyback authorization would face forced covering.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $56 (+37%)

RSR compounds through $100M and beyond at 15%+ as copper retirement continues across a large, unconverted commercial-building base; the cloud MVP access platform begins contributing meaningfully in the back half of calendar 2026, adding a second RSR leg; equipment volumes finally recover as the channel normalizes ("the channel seems smooth right now"), letting revenue growth come from units rather than price; equipment gross margin completes its return toward 29–30% on continued discount discipline (returns/rebates already down to 5% of gross revenue from 7%); the IEEPA tariff refund lands and partially offsets the $16M settlement cash outflow; Patel is dismissed at the pleading stage; and the board authorizes a new repurchase program, deploying $125M of net liquid assets. FY2027 EPS reaches ~$1.85 and the market pays 30x for a ~55% recurring-revenue business with no debt and a 13% tax rate.

### Bear Case — $29 (−28.9%)

Price-led growth exhausts: after two years of double-digit increases, intrusion/access volumes keep contracting and door-locking deceleration (Q3 volume +5.4% vs. +9.0% for nine months) continues, so FY2027 equipment revenue is flat to down. Telguard's sole-path fire communicator and other entrants compress RSR pricing, and the 90.3% service margin drifts toward the high 80s. Patel settles or is adjudicated in the $15–25M range with limited insurance, taking cumulative litigation cash out to $30M+ and validating the market's governance discount. RSR growth decelerates to high single digits and the multiple compresses to ~19x on $1.55, putting the stock back at the $29.27 fifty-two-week low. A DR disruption would be worse and is not in this number.

### Base Case — $46 (+12.8%)

RSR grows 13–15% through FY2027 to roughly $115M; equipment grows mid-single digits with price contribution fading and volumes roughly flat; consolidated revenue reaches ~$220M; ex-item operating margin holds near 29–30%; FY2027 EPS lands at ~$1.72 (above the $1.62 consensus, consistent with four straight beats). At the 27.0x peer median: 27 × $1.72 = $46.4. The sum-of-parts cross-check (14x RSR revenue plus 1.0x equipment revenue plus net cash = $45.5) supports the same range. Patel resolves for a manageable amount or drags on unresolved; the $16M settlement is paid without operational consequence.

Probability-weighting 50% base / 22% bull / 28% bear gives an expected return of roughly +6.5%, a fair but not compelling prospective return consistent with HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** Existing owners should stay: the recurring-revenue engine is compounding, the balance sheet is unlevered, and the base case is positive. New capital has no urgent entry signal at 27.4x FY2026E and 25.2x FY2027 consensus with a 12.8% base-case upside against a 28.9% bear case.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q4/FY2026 earnings release and FY2026 10-K, expected in the second half of August 2026 (the FY2025 10-K was filed 2025-08-25). That single event resolves several open questions at once: full-year equipment volume trajectory, whether Q4 equipment re-accelerated or continued decelerating, FY2027 guidance if any, any new buyback authorization, ICFR status, and Patel procedural developments following the 7/13/2026 defendants' response deadline.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Volume, not price.** Intrusion and access unit volumes turn positive, or the FY2026 10-K shows equipment growth with a price contribution below ~5% — evidence the recovery is demand-driven rather than pass-through.
2. **A new, sizeable repurchase authorization actually used.** Only 359,741 shares (~1.0% of outstanding) remain authorized and zero were bought in 9M FY2026, versus 1,220,899 at an average $30.14 in FY2025 including 745,000 at $25.22 near the lows. Management demonstrated genuine countercyclical discipline once; a renewed authorization with purchases near current levels would signal that the board sees value at $41 and would also address the founder-sell-down alignment gap.
3. **Patel resolved at ≤$10M or dismissed**, removing the unquantified liability — worth roughly a point of multiple on its own.
4. **Price near $34–35** (approximately 21–22x FY2027 consensus, the July 2026 trading range), which would restore a bear-case-adjusted margin of safety without requiring any thesis change.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. **RSR gross margin below 89%** for two consecutive quarters, or RSR revenue growth below 10% — the earliest evidence that Telguard-style competition is repricing the annuity that carries ~76% of gross profit.
2. **Patel accrual above $20M**, or any new securities/derivative action, or any new ICFR material weakness in the FY2026 10-K.
3. **A second consecutive quarter of negative unit volumes across both equipment lines**, which would indicate the FY2025 destocking cycle was masking share loss.

We would treat any disruption at the Dominican Republic facility as an immediate exit event given the absence of an identified alternative and the tax dependency.

## Conclusion

NAPCO is a better business than its multiple implies on the hardware line and roughly a fairly priced one on the annuity. Half of revenue and three-quarters of gross profit now come from a 90%-margin cellular connectivity stream with real switching friction, funded by an unlevered balance sheet holding $125M of liquid assets and generating 28% free-cash-flow margins at a 13% tax rate. That combination deserves a premium to a generic electronic-equipment peer, and at 25–27x forward earnings the market is granting one.

The market is not granting a growth premium, and we think that is correct. Consensus FY2027 earnings growth is 8.7%; intrusion and access units are down 7.5% with growth carried by 10.8% price; equipment revenue after two years of price increases remains ~7% below the FY2024 peak; and a direct competitor announced a product aimed squarely at the fire-radio annuity in April 2026, with RSR margin already drifting 0.8 points lower year over year.
