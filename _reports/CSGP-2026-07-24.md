**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CoStar Group owns the most valuable commercial real estate (CRE) data franchise in the world: a 35-year database, the industry's largest research department, ~89% subscription renewal, and ~90% recurring revenue, which in 2025 generated $672M of segment Adjusted EBITDA. Since early 2024, management has been spending that cash engine's output on an aggressive residential-marketplace land grab (Homes.com, Apartments.com, plus the 2025 Domain and Matterport acquisitions), which drove consolidated GAAP operating income to a $72M loss in FY2025 and net income to just $7M. The stock has fallen roughly 72% from its July 2025 high of $97.43 to $27.14, and the trailing P/E of 453x is a mathematical artifact of near-zero trailing earnings rather than a meaningful valuation signal.

The central question is whether this is a broken business or a story where GAAP profit collapsed by design. The evidence favors the latter: Residential segment Adjusted EBITDA has narrowed from -$361M (2024) to -$230M (2025) to just -$29M in Q1 2026 (vs. -$85M a year earlier), and consolidated operating income turned positive in Q1 2026 ($3M). Revenue continues to compound at ~19-23%. The July 2026 CFO change, promoting STR veteran Robin Rossmann explicitly "to drive margin expansion and profitable growth," signals the board has internalized the activist critique even after rebuffing the activist at the June 2026 shareholder vote.

We rate CSGP a HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 5.75. The operating improvement is real and the balance sheet is sound (Altman Z 4.76, D/E 0.21, undrawn $1.1B revolver). The setup remains two-sided: forward estimates are drifting lower (next-year EPS revised -1.3% over 30 days), a large fading interest-income tailwind ($213M in 2024 to $10M in Q1'26) will make reported pre-tax income look worse even as operations improve, ~$6.7B of goodwill/intangibles create a live impairment overhang with the stock near book, and $500M of early-2026 buybacks were executed at $44.27, well above today's price. We would upgrade to BUY on a confirmed margin turn at reasonable multiples, and downgrade on a Homes.com stall or a goodwill writedown.

## Company Overview

CoStar operates two product-based segments (recast in Q4 2025 from a prior geographic structure):

- **Commercial Real Estate** — FY2025 revenue $1,787M (+18%). Core brands: **CoStar** ($1,259M, the flagship CRE intelligence subscription platform plus STR hospitality benchmarking and Visual Lease), **LoopNet** CRE marketplace ($312M), and "Other CRE" ($216M, +181%, driven by the Matterport acquisition plus BizBuySell and Ten-X auctions).
- **Residential Real Estate** — FY2025 revenue $1,460M (+20%). Brands: **Apartments.com**, **Homes.com**, and Land.com (US); **Domain** (Australia, acquired Aug 2025); and **OnTheMarket** (UK).

The commercial franchise is a high-margin subscription annuity with no true competitor at scale; the closest business analog is a data-analytics compounder like Verisk. The competitive battleground is residential, where Homes.com is spending heavily to challenge Zillow while Apartments.com dominates rentals. Internationally, CoStar is assembling a marketplace footprint (Domain in Australia, OnTheMarket in the UK, LoopNet in Spain/France, a July 2026 30% stake in Wikicasa in Italy, and planned CoStar/LoopNet launches in France and Australia). Founder-CEO Andrew Florance, whose loss is specifically named as a material risk in the 10-K, dominates the company.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue growth is strong and steady; GAAP profit collapsed by choice.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 2,182 | 2,455 | 2,736 | 3,247 (+19%) | 897 (+23%) |
| Operating income ($M) | 451 | 282 | 5 | (72) | 3 |
| Net income ($M) | 369 | 375 | 139 | 7 | 3 |
| Residential Adj. EBITDA ($M) | — | (44) | (361) | (230) | (29) |
| Commercial Adj. EBITDA ($M) | — | 536 | 602 | 672 | 161 |

**The story is the Homes.com investment cycle.** Since launching Homes.com monetization in February 2024, CoStar poured hundreds of millions into marketing and sales. The mechanism is straightforward: the marketing spend to establish Homes.com traffic and agent membership is front-loaded, while subscription revenue ramps behind it. If revenue continues to grow ~20% while incremental marketing spend flattens, the segment crosses breakeven; the Q1 trajectory is consistent with that path.

**The interest-income cliff is an underappreciated modeling trap.** Net interest income fell from $213M (2024) to $110M (2025) to just $10M in Q1 2026 as the ~$4.7B cash hoard was deployed into ~$3.9B of M&A (Matterport ~$1.9B, Domain ~$1.9B) and buybacks. Investors comparing year-over-year pre-tax income will see deterioration driven purely by cash deployment, not operations. This is why the trailing GAAP multiple is nearly useless here: the same cash that used to generate reported income now sits in operating assets whose earnings show up gradually.

**The balance sheet is healthy.** Current ratio 2.84. Debt is a single $1.0B 2.8% senior note due 2030; the $1.1B revolver is undrawn; the leverage covenant is 4.5x with ample headroom. Cash fell to ~$1.3B (Q1'26) after the M&A and buyback wave but remains sufficient. Free cash flow was thin at $123M in 2025 due to elevated capex (~$307M, largely the Richmond, VA campus, substantially complete in H1 2026), which should ease and support FCF recovery.

The consistent earnings-beat record (4 straight beats: +22.5%, +10.9%, +25.5%, +27.7%) lends some credibility to near-term consensus, though the beats are against depressed absolute EPS levels.

## Growth Analysis

Top-line growth is durable and broad-based, driven by full-year Domain contribution, the Matterport step-up, and Homes.com membership gains. Management's 2026 framing has Commercial growth *moderating* (no more Matterport acquisition step-up) and Residential growth *accelerating* (full-year Domain plus more Homes.com members).

The commercial franchise is the durable compounder — ~89% renewal rates, ~90-93% recurring subscription revenue, and segment Adjusted EBITDA growing steadily ($536M → $602M → $672M). This is the part of the business that would command a premium multiple standalone.

Residential is the growth option. The bull case requires Homes.com to reach Zillow-like engagement and monetization at scale profitably; management explicitly warns it "may not generate an acceptable return." The narrowing loss trajectory is the strongest evidence that the option is moving into the money, but it is not yet proven: the segment is still losing money and revenue growth here depends on continued paid-traffic acquisition and agent subscription conversion.

On estimate revisions, the signal is mixed and does not clear the bar for a strong BUY. Current-year EPS was revised +3.4% over 90 days (to $1.36) and current-quarter +5.5%, but next-year EPS was cut -1.3% over 30 days and -0.3% over 90 days. The revision picture is positive near-term, softening out-year, not the +30% velocity that would signal the market is systematically underpricing an earnings recovery. Revenue is growing >20%, but the estimate revisions are too muted to trigger the strongest growth signal, so we weight the operating-segment trajectory (Residential loss narrowing) more heavily than the consensus EPS path.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E of 453x is meaningless: it reflects a near-zero trailing GAAP earnings base distorted by the deliberate Homes.com spend and the fading interest income. This is exactly the situation where cycle-adjusted earnings power, not trailing multiples, must anchor the analysis.

On forward consensus, CSGP trades at ~20x current-year EPS ($1.36) and ~15x next-year EPS ($1.78) at $27.14. Against Real Estate Services peers (median P/E 41.5x, with FSV at 39.9x, CWK at 41.5x, KW at 96.3x), CSGP screens cheap on forward earnings, but the peer set is heterogeneous and the more relevant comparison is CoStar's own asset quality. A data-analytics annuity with a call option on residential should not be benchmarked to brokerage-heavy peers like JLL (16x) or CWK.

The clearer anchor is normalized earnings power. If Residential reaches breakeven and the Commercial franchise (segment Adjusted EBITDA growing high-teens) is valued as the durable annuity it is, consolidated earnings power meaningfully exceeds the current ~$1.36 consensus. The analyst mean target of $44.45 (median $44.00), roughly 60% above the current price, reflects this normalized view. Management itself bought back $500M via ASR at $44.27 in early 2026, signaling the board saw fair value near that level. We treat the improving segment trajectory as the primary support rather than the analyst targets, because the sentiment trend is degrading (targets are being trimmed, one downgrade recorded) and next-year estimates are drifting lower.

We assign a valuation score of 6.0, moderately constructive. The forward multiple is reasonable for the asset quality and the residential option carries genuine upside, but this is not a screaming mispricing. The offset: the ~72% drawdown from the high means many sophisticated holders sold, and the falling next-year estimates plus the goodwill overhang give the market a legitimate reason for skepticism. A cheap screen alone is not a buy signal; the specific catalyst here is the profitability pivot under the new CFO, and until that shows in reported results the discount is partly deserved.

## Competitive Landscape

CoStar's CRE data business has no peer at scale; the moat is the accumulated database, the largest research department in the industry, and switching costs embedded in ~89% renewal rates. That moat is intact and arguably widening as the company layers in STR hospitality data, Matterport digital twins, and international coverage.

The competitive dynamic in residential is less favorable and the trajectory is where the risk sits. Homes.com is challenging an entrenched Zillow, and success depends on buying consumer traffic, much of it routed through Google search rankings. The 10-K adds new and expanded language warning that AI-powered platforms could provide agents, data, and content "without directing users to our marketplaces" and that generative AI "may reduce the barrier to entry for new competitors." This is the same AI-disintermediation risk pressuring marketplace and application-layer businesses broadly: if consumers get answers directly from an AI interface rather than clicking through to a listing marketplace, the traffic-monetization model erodes. Apartments.com's rental dominance is more defensible, but the growth engine (Homes.com) is the more contested front.

Competitive position score of 6.0 reflects an intact-and-widening CRE moat offset by a contested residential build and a structural AI-traffic risk that is not fully priced.

## Risk Assessment

- **Homes.com ROI is unproven.** Management explicitly warns it may not earn an acceptable return; it is the single biggest swing factor and the reason the stock has derated.
- **Goodwill/intangible impairment (~$6.7B; goodwill $4.9B).** The 10-K names "significant underperformance" and "decline in market cap relative to book value" as impairment triggers. With the stock down materially from its high, these triggers are edging toward relevance. No impairment was recognized through Q1 2026, but a residential stumble or a sustained low market cap could force a large non-cash writedown.
- **AI / Google disintermediation.** New/expanded risk language; residential traffic depends heavily on Google rankings and is exposed to AI search substitution.
- **Interest-income cliff.** Reported pre-tax income will look worse year-over-year purely from cash deployment, potentially obscuring the operating improvement for investors reading headline numbers.
- **Legal/regulatory.** An assumed $99M litigation accrual (Brown v. Matterport; the Delaware Supreme Court substantially affirmed a $79M award plus interest, with up to ~$17M additional interest possible); an STR hospitality antitrust class action; and broad scrutiny of multifamily algorithmic rent-pricing (CoStar states it does not offer price recommendations, but customers may be affected).
- **Governance and key-person risk.** Founder-CEO Florance is central; FY2025 CEO comp of $36.4M against $6.5M of proxy net income and a TSR that trailed the peer group (112.4 vs. 122.5) is a pay-versus-performance flag. An unusually dense cluster of five Item 5.02 leadership changes across seven months (Jan-Jul 2026), plus newly adopted Executive Severance Plans, is consistent with a company under activist pressure reshaping leadership.
- **FX.** ~14% of revenue is now foreign and rising with Domain, with no hedging.

We assign risk_factors 4.5. The goodwill impairment overhang against a beaten-down stock, the unproven residential ROI, the AI-traffic threat, and the governance density are specific, material, and not fully priced. These are idiosyncratic downside catalysts, not sector truisms.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 70.9% sits well above 1-year historical volatility of 41.9% (a rich 29-point spread, 84th percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (70.9% near-term vs. 63.6% at six months), a classic signal of imminent event risk, most likely the next earnings print. The put/call open-interest ratio of 3.24 and put/call volume ratio of 1.83 show heavy put positioning, consistent with hedging or bearish conviction. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±17.2%. The put/call skew itself is roughly balanced (2.5%), so this reads more as broad demand for downside protection into a catalyst than a one-sided directional bet. For a HOLD, this reinforces patience: entry is better established after the near-term IV crush and the next earnings resolution rather than ahead of it. (The prior-snapshot ATM IV reading of 1565% is a corrupted thin-options artifact and is disregarded.)

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$50-70):** Residential crosses breakeven in 2026-27 as marketing spend flattens against 20%+ revenue growth; the new CFO's margin mandate accelerates the pivot; consolidated EBITDA re-rates toward the $1B+ level implied by combining a profitable residential segment with the Commercial annuity. Applying a mid-30s multiple to normalized EPS approaching $2.00+ and crediting the international marketplace optionality gets to the analyst high target of $70. The commercial franchise alone, valued as a data-analytics compounder, supports a floor well above the current price.

**Bear Case (~$20-24):** Homes.com fails to reach profitable scale against Zillow; AI search erodes marketplace traffic; a goodwill impairment forces a large non-cash writedown that dents book value and sentiment; next-year estimates continue to drift lower; and the stock re-rates to a value-trap multiple as the market concludes the residential capital was destroyed. Downside to the low-$20s, roughly 15-25% below current levels.

**Base Case (~$36):** The residential loss continues to narrow and the segment approaches breakeven over 2026-27, the Commercial franchise compounds high-teens EBITDA, and the stock re-rates partway toward normalized earnings power as reported profitability improves. This sits between the bear and bull scenarios and roughly 33% above the current price, but is gated by execution and the persistence of the interest-income and estimate-revision headwinds.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July / early August) is the near-term catalyst and the event the options market is pricing. The full margin-pivot thesis should be judged over 12-18 months as the new CFO's plan shows in reported results and the Richmond capex headwind rolls off.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Residential segment Adjusted EBITDA turns positive, or the quarterly loss narrows below ~-$15M with confirmed line-of-sight to breakeven, alongside sustained 20%+ revenue growth.
2. Next-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive (currently -1.3% over 30 days) and the analyst target trend stabilizes or turns up.
3. The stock trades below ~$26 (near the analyst low target) with fundamentals intact, restoring clearer asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A goodwill/intangible impairment charge, or a management statement flagging one.
2. Homes.com revenue growth or agent-membership additions stall, or Residential losses re-widen sequentially.
3. Evidence of AI-driven traffic erosion showing up in marketplace engagement metrics or Commercial renewal rates falling below the mid-80s.

## Conclusion

CoStar is a dominant, high-quality CRE data franchise deliberately depressing its GAAP earnings to fund a residential-marketplace build whose economics remain unproven but are improving quickly. The ~72% drawdown reflects genuine skepticism about that build, worsened by an interest-income cliff that flatters the bear narrative and a goodwill overhang that could crystallize into a writedown. The offsetting evidence is substantial: the Residential loss has collapsed to a ~-$29M quarterly run-rate, consolidated operating income turned positive in Q1 2026, the balance sheet is safe, and a CFO change signals a real pivot to profitability. On forward earnings the stock is not expensive, but the falling next-year estimates and the specific idiosyncratic risks keep the setup balanced rather than asymmetric.

We rate CSGP a **HOLD (growth_hold)**, overall score **5.75**, and we hold the recommendation unchanged because the profitability turn, while promising, is not yet confirmed in reported results. This is a name to accumulate into a confirmed margin turn or a further pullback toward the low-$20s, and to exit on an impairment or a residential stall.
