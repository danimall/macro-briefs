## Executive Summary

Central Garden & Pet is a deliberately shrinking, deliberately simplifying multi-brand consumer products roll-up whose reported financials no longer describe the underlying business. Reported net sales have declined three consecutive fiscal years (FY2022 $3,338.6M → FY2025 $3,129.1M, −6.3% cumulative) while gross margin expanded 220bp and FY2025 net income of $162.8M exceeded FY2022's $152.2M. In the third quarter of fiscal 2026, reported sales fell 8.2% to $882.4M while organic sales grew 2.4%; the gap is almost entirely the April 13, 2026 contribution of the pet distribution business (FY2025 revenue $474M, 15.1% of consolidated sales) to a Phillips-controlled entity. Management raised fiscal 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance to "$2.85 or better" from "$2.70 or better" on August 5-6.

The balance sheet is the strongest and least-appreciated part of the story. At June 27, 2026 Central held $996.7M of cash against $1,193.0M of fixed-rate senior notes coupon-ed at 4.125%-5.125% with no maturity before February 2028, an undrawn $600M asset-based revolver (~$465M net availability, springing 1.00x fixed-charge covenant only), and net debt of $196.3M against TTM adjusted EBITDA of $376.8M — 0.52x. Interest income of $6.5M offset 46% of Q3 interest expense. On all-class shares of 62,543,301, market capitalization at $37.85 is roughly $2,367M, enterprise value $2,563M, and EV/TTM adjusted EBITDA 6.8x. FY2025 free cash flow of $291.1M is a 12.3% yield on that equity value. The supplied $1.93B market cap reflects the Class A line only.

The case for waiting is that the cheapness is structural, not temporary, and the operating improvement is thinner than the segment narrative suggests. Nine-month segment adjusted EBITDA improved $15.8M (Pet +$9.5M, Garden +$6.3M) while corporate expense worsened $10.0M, netting +$5.8M — consolidated adjusted EBITDA growth of 1.7%. Organic growth decelerated from +3.3% year-to-date to +2.4% in Q3. Top-five customers were ~54% of net sales in both FY2025 and FY2024, with three retailers at roughly 80% of Garden sales into a US home-improvement channel. Across roughly 25 Form 4 filings from February through July 2026 there were no open-market insider purchases and three open-market sales; buyback spend fell 81% year over year to ~$22.7M at an average of ~$28.55 while the board simultaneously added $100M of authorization. We rate CENTA **HOLD** with a base case of $41, bull $50, bear $28.

## Company Overview

Central operates two reportable segments. **Pet** (FY2025 net sales $1,802.0M, 57.6% of total) spans dog and cat treats and chews (Nylabone, Cadet, Best Bully Sticks, TDBBS), small animal and wild bird (Kaytee, Wild Delight), aquatics (Aqueon, Zilla), animal health and pest control (Farnam, Adams, Zoëcon, Altosid, ClariFly), live fish and small animals (Segrest/SunPet), and Arden Companies outdoor cushions, a durable-goods line with no conceptual relationship to pets. **Garden** (FY2025 net sales $1,327.1M) covers grass seed and fertilizer (Pennington, Alaska, Ironite, Lilly Miller), garden seed (Ferry-Morse), controls (Sevin, Amdro, Daconil), growing media (Jiffy), the DoMyOwn e-commerce channel, and Bell Nursery, the primary live-goods supplier to Home Depot in the Northeast and Mid-Atlantic.

The portfolio is the product of more than 60 acquisitions since 1992. Branded products were 79% of FY2025 sales, up from 78%; the remaining ~21% ($664.7M) was third-party goods, most of which has now been divested.

Two assets in the Business section are genuinely hard to replicate. Central is the sole domestic provider of (S)-Methoprene, an insect growth regulator it also supplies to third parties including manufacturers of Frontline Plus, though a key input for that compound comes from a single supplier. Second, in a category with more than 2,500 US pet supplies manufacturers of which roughly two dozen are multi-category, Central's ability to present a broad assortment to Walmart, Home Depot, Costco, Lowe's and Amazon is the actual moat, rather than any individual brand.

Governance: Class B common carries 10 votes per share. Founder and Chairman William E. Brown, age 84, beneficially controlled approximately 56% of voting power as of September 27, 2025. Central has never paid a dividend, and the credit facility and indentures restrict dividends. CEO Niko Lahanas was promoted from CFO in September 2024; CFO Brad Smith was promoted from Pet segment CFO simultaneously, an internal, finance-led succession consistent with the margin-and-simplification agenda now branded "Project Horizon."

## Financial Analysis

### Margin expansion on a shrinking base

| Gross margin | 29.7% | 28.6% | 29.5% | 31.9% |

| Net income | 152.2 | 125.6 | 108.0 | 162.8 |

FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS was $2.55 versus $1.62; non-GAAP $2.73 versus $2.13. The improvement was concentrated in Garden, whose operating income rose 74% to $142.4M (10.7% margin) from $81.9M, while Pet rose more modestly to $215.7M. At the adjusted EBITDA level the divergence is sharper: **Pet went backwards** ($265.6M FY2025 versus $267.4M FY2024) while Garden went $146.6M → $189.7M. Garden's FY2024 base was depressed by roughly $20.3M of facility-closure charges and a grass-seed inventory write-down management sized at $15-20M. A meaningful share of the FY2025 recovery is the absence of prior-year charges rather than new earnings power.

Pet's Q3 non-GAAP operating margin expanded from 15.8% to 19.0%. This is largely mix arithmetic: removing a ~$474M-revenue, low-margin distribution business mechanically lifts the remaining segment's margin, and should not be read as 320bp of operational improvement.

The more important number is the corporate line. Nine-month segment adjusted EBITDA improved $15.778M; corporate worsened $9.997M (−$68.060M versus −$58.063M), for consolidated adjusted EBITDA of $351.112M versus $345.331M, or +1.7%. **Corporate cost growth consumed 63.4% of segment-level improvement.** The Q3 increase (+28.9%, +$7.0M) is attributed to third-party M&A costs and payroll for "data improvement and AI readiness," neither excluded from non-GAAP. The M&A portion should be transient; the data/AI build reads as structural, and the company has not split the two.

### Cash generation

Nine-month operating cash flow was $207.6M versus $149.8M, against capex of $33.9M — roughly $173.7M of nine-month free cash flow. FY2025 full-year OCF was $332.5M with capex of $41.4M, giving $291.1M of FCF, 9.3% of sales. One definitional caution: inventories fell $86.6M year over year, but roughly $69M of inventory left the balance sheet via the non-cash Pet Distribution contribution, so the genuine working-capital contribution to OCF is materially smaller than the balance-sheet delta implies. A normalized free cash flow run rate in the $230-270M range is a more defensible anchor than the FY2025 print — still a 10-11% yield on $2,367M of equity value.

### Capital structure

All debt is fixed-rate with no maturity before February 2028: $299.3M of 5.125% notes due 2028, $496.6M of 4.125% notes due 2030, $397.1M of 4.125% notes due 2031. The 2030 and 2031 notes trade below carrying value ($477.3M and $376.9M fair value respectively), a function of low coupons against current rates — economically favorable to Central and a reason open-market debt repurchases would be accretive if management chose that route. The November 7, 2025 Fourth Amended credit agreement provides a $600M ABL revolver with a $400M accordion to November 2030 at SOFR+1.00-1.50%, undrawn with no letters of credit outstanding at Q3.

Gross leverage is 3.17x on TTM adjusted EBITDA of $376.8M; net leverage 0.52x. Pro forma for the ~€340M (~$388M) TRIXIE upfront payment and assuming zero TRIXIE EBITDA, net debt rises to roughly $584M, or 1.55x — still conservative. The interest-income offset (46% of Q3 interest expense) is a rate-cycle benefit that reverses if short rates fall.

This combination of a 12% trailing FCF yield, 0.52x net leverage, no near-term maturities, sub-market coupons, and an undrawn revolver supports a financial health score of 7.5, unchanged from the prior review. The offsets to a higher score are goodwill plus net intangibles of $972.7M (25.7% of assets) tested only qualitatively for two consecutive years, and the fact that a large share of the FY2025 cash-flow strength came from working-capital release that cannot repeat at the same magnitude.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is the only meaningful top-line metric here, and it is running +3.3% for nine months but decelerating to +2.4% in Q3. Against likely category inflation, real volume growth is close to zero. Pet grew 1.7% organically in Q3; Garden 2.9% reported.

Consensus captures this: current-year EPS of $2.97 and next-year $3.11 imply 4.7% earnings growth. Annual revisions have been mildly positive (+1.4% and +1.3% over 90 days), which is the relevant signal. The headline "estimates broadly falling" reflects near-term quarterly estimates — current quarter −46.6% over 90 days and next quarter −33.3% — which is largely mechanical: fiscal Q4 (July-September) is seasonally loss-making, and the removal of pet distribution earnings plus elevated corporate and transaction spend widens that loss. Nine-month non-GAAP EPS of $3.04 already exceeds the full-year "$2.85 or better" floor, implying a Q4 loss of roughly $0.19 versus an actual −$0.06 last year. That is arithmetic off a floor with demonstrated conservatism behind it, not a forecast, but it does mean the near-term print carries a wider seasonal loss than the prior year.

**TRIXIE Heimtierbedarf** (announced July 27, 2026) is the largest acquisition in company history: 80% of the Tarp, Germany-based business for up to €400.0M, comprising ~€340.0M upfront plus a €60.0M earn-out on 2026 performance, expected to close in the first half of fiscal 2027. TRIXIE has close to 600 employees, serves over 30,000 pet retail stores, carries more than 6,000 products, generates approximately 90% of sales from own brands, and introduces ~500 new products annually. Central states the deal brings non-US sales to approximately 10% of the total in a European pet supplies market it sizes at €10B growing mid-single-digit (Euromonitor).

TRIXIE's revenue and EBITDA are not disclosed, so the multiple paid cannot be verified. Backing into the "~10% of net sales outside the US" statement against a post-divestiture US base of roughly $2.7-2.8B implies TRIXIE revenue somewhere in the $160-250M range (midpoint ~$205M / €180M), which would put €400M for 80% at roughly 2.8x revenue on a 100% equity basis and 12-14x EBITDA at 15-18% margins. Whether TRIXIE carries debt — and therefore whether that is an equity or enterprise multiple — is unknown. On the midpoint, TRIXIE would add roughly 7% to pro forma revenue and, at a 16% margin, roughly $33M of EBITDA (Central's 80% share ~$26M), which is meaningful but not large relative to earnings per share in year one after amortization and financing opportunity cost.

The December 12, 2025 Champion USA acquisition (~$57M cash, cattle fly control) extends the Zoëcon/Starbar/ClariFly livestock franchise. Roughly $56M of the purchase price remained unallocated in "other assets" at June 27, 2026, so incremental amortization is not yet determined.

Growth prospects score 5.0: low-single-digit organic growth barely above inflation, consolidated EBITDA growth of 1.7% because corporate costs are eating the segment gains, and an M&A-dependent top line whose economics are not yet verifiable.

Central trades at a wide discount to nominal peer multiples, and part of that discount is justified: no dividend, dual-class control with 56% of votes held by an 84-year-old chairman and no public succession disclosure, three consecutive years of reported revenue decline, and 54% of sales concentrated in five retailers.

The part of the discount that looks excessive is the cash-flow arithmetic. At 6.8x EV/EBITDA with 0.52x net leverage and a 10-12% free cash flow yield, the market is paying nothing for the balance sheet optionality or for the possibility that Project Horizon (13 facilities closed since 2022, two opened) converts into sustained margin. That is a real value argument. What is missing is a dated catalyst. The TRIXIE close is guided only to "first half of fiscal 2027" — a six-month window ending March 2027 — and the accretion math cannot be checked. The $128.0M of remaining buyback authorization (5.4% of all-class market cap) is the cleanest available lever, but management has been running it at a trickle, and roughly $388M of the cash is now earmarked.

Consensus targets are low $39.00, mean $45.25, median $46.00, high $50.00 — the current price sits below the *low* target, which would ordinarily read as a floor. But this is a five-analyst set that includes one strong-sell rating, Canaccord's post-Q3 target raise magnitude is unverified, and Central does not carry 30%+ estimate revisions where targets systematically lag. Treat the target range as corroboration of cheapness, not as an independent thesis.

Valuation score 6.0, up modestly from the prior review at an essentially unchanged price, reflecting the $2.70 → $2.85 guidance raise. Cheap with absolute yield support, but the discount has identifiable structural causes and no near-term re-rating trigger.

## Competitive Landscape

**Garden** is a clear number-two position behind Scotts Miracle-Gro in US lawn and garden consumables, competing alongside Spectrum Brands and S.C. Johnson. Economics are governed by shelf allocation at three customers — Home Depot ~37% of Garden sales, Walmart ~29%, Lowe's ~14% — and by weather. The FY2025 Lowe's "Vendor Partner of the Year" award in Lawn & Garden is a modest positive signal on standing. The grass-seed oversupply cycle running since FY2023 is an industry condition management describes as beginning to moderate.

**Pet** is structurally more attractive: highly fragmented, with premiumization and humanization as durable tailwinds. Central's advantage is assortment breadth across dog/cat, aquatics, small animal, bird, equine and pest control — a portfolio few competitors can match — rather than any single dominant brand. Central largely avoids pet food, where Mars and J.M. Smucker dominate; Spectrum Brands is the closest structural analogue in supplies.

The most revealing strategic decision in the filings is the distribution exit. Central's own 10-K names Phillips Pet Food & Supplies as a competitor in pet distribution. Contributing that business into a Phillips-controlled entity in exchange for ~$31M cash, a $5M note, and a 20% stake valued at $26.1M is an explicit judgment that scale in pet distribution belongs to a consolidator and that Central's capital earns more in branded manufacturing. The trade releases roughly $57M of net assets and ~$69M of inventory and frees management attention, but it converts a controlled asset into a dependency: Central designates itself *not* the primary beneficiary of the VIE, and the 10-Q's forward-looking list newly cites "the impact of the new pet distribution partnership on our… ability to distribute and promote our pet branded products." Central recognized $14.8M of transition-services reimbursements and $5.8M of branded product sales to the new entity in Q3; the TSA revenue steps down as three-month-to-three-year terms expire, and its income-statement placement is unconfirmed.

TRIXIE fits the same pattern of redeploying into branded assets, but the international execution record cuts the other way: Central decided in March 2025 to wind down its UK operation (~$10.0M of one-time costs) in favor of direct export, sixteen months before agreeing to buy a German platform. The 10-Q's forward-looking risks specifically name "challenges related to entering into international markets."

Competitive position score 5.5. Margin trajectory and category breadth are positives; loss of route-to-market control, customer concentration that is flat to slightly worse, and unproven European operating capability offset them.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration, severe and not improving.** Walmart 17% of FY2025 net sales (16% prior two years); Home Depot 16% for three consecutive years; top five approximately 54% in both FY2025 and FY2024. Within Garden, three customers are roughly 80% of sales. The TRIXIE deal changes geographic mix; on the disclosed evidence it does not reduce US big-box concentration, and at ~10% of pro forma sales it cannot move the top-five percentage materially. Retail consolidation, private-label expansion by these same customers, and just-in-time inventory practices transmit destocking decisions directly into quarterly results.

**Macro exposure through the home-improvement channel.** Home Depot and Lowe's foot traffic is the transmission channel into Garden. Garden consumables are more defensive than housing durables, but at ~37% and ~14% of Garden sales respectively, this is a likely source of a near-term organic-growth disappointment.

**Goodwill and intangibles.** $972.7M, or 25.7% of assets, with intangible impairments in each of the last three years ($1.0M / $12.8M / $11.5M). Central performed only qualitative goodwill assessments in FY2024 and FY2025, and the disclosed methodology compares aggregate segment fair value against total market capitalization, making a sustained share-price decline a plausible trigger for a quantitative retest. Any charge would be non-cash and would not affect the fixed-charge-coverage covenant or adjusted EBITDA.

**TRIXIE execution and undisclosed obligations.** Roughly $388M of upfront cash (16% of market capitalization) into an untested geography, with the multiple unverifiable, the euro exposure unhedged as far as disclosed, and the retained ~20% carrying put rights exercisable over at least three years with a Central call thereafter — a conditional future cash claim of undisclosed size, strike mechanism, and classification.

**Newly named credit risk.** The 10-Q's forward-looking list adds "potential credit risk with certain brick-and-mortar pet specialty retailers," language not present in the FY2025 risk factors. Q3 receivables were $460.2M (seasonal peak), and Central retained the divested unit's receivables. No counterparty is named and no reserve quantified.

**Single-supplier dependency.** Sole domestic (S)-Methoprene provider, with a key input from a single supplier — concentrated, asymmetric exposure on a high-margin, hard-to-substitute line.

**Governance and workforce.** Dual-class structure with ~56% of votes held by an 84-year-old chairman and no public succession disclosure; Class A holders cannot influence outcomes. FY2025 attrition of 29% against a workforce over 6,000. Two material weaknesses were disclosed in FY2023 (Live Plants, Green Garden) and remediated in FY2024 during an ERP consolidation from 46 systems to 6; controls were effective in Q3 FY2026.

**Modest positives on the risk side.** Less than 15% of cost of goods sold is sourced outside the US, so tariff exposure is smaller than the sector narrative implies (Q3 included $3.6M of IEEPA tariff refunds, excluded from non-GAAP). Short interest is only 3.2% of float at 2.8 days to cover, with no crowding or squeeze dynamic. The Nite Glow litigation resolved at a $7.2M judgment, immaterial at this scale.

Risk factors score 5.0: multiple concentrated, quantifiable exposures without a single catastrophic binary.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($50, +32%)

Reported revenue decline is by design, and screens keyed on it misprice the business. Organic growth of ~3% with 33.5% nine-month gross margin, Project Horizon network consolidation (13 closures, two openings since 2022), and the removal of ~$474M of low-margin distribution revenue leave a structurally higher-margin company than the FY2022-FY2024 financials show. The balance sheet has pre-funded a large acquisition without touching capital markets: $996.7M cash, $465M undrawn revolver, sub-market fixed coupons, no maturity before 2028. TRIXIE brings a 90%-own-brand, ~500-new-products-per-year platform with 30,000+ European specialty relationships into a €10B market growing mid-single-digit, and post-close leverage of ~1.55x before any TRIXIE EBITDA leaves capacity for the remaining $128.0M buyback authorization. Corporate cost growth reverses as M&A fees roll off, restoring the full segment improvement to the consolidated line. At 12-13x forward earnings versus a peer median above 23x, a re-rating toward 15-16x on FY2027 earnings of $3.11-3.35, plus buyback accretion, produces $50.

### Bear Case ($28, −26%)

Organic growth decelerating from +3.3% to +2.4% is barely above inflation, meaning real volume is flat, and 54% customer concentration transmits any big-box destocking directly into results. Weakness in the home-improvement channel that supplies ~80% of Garden sales pressures the top line. Corporate cost growth proves structural rather than transactional — the "data improvement and AI readiness" payroll does not roll off — and consolidated EBITDA growth stays near 1.7% while the segment story looks fine. Pet's apparent margin transformation is revealed as mix arithmetic once the TSA reimbursement stream ($14.8M in one quarter) steps down. TRIXIE closes at a full multiple, absorbs management attention, and repeats the UK experience; the undisclosed put obligation on the retained 20% surfaces as a larger cash claim than expected. A sustained share-price decline triggers a quantitative goodwill retest against $972.7M of goodwill and intangibles. On 10x a flat $2.85 of earnings, the stock is $28.50, near the low end of the past year's range.

### Base Case ($41, +8.3%)

Organic growth of 2-3% continues, FY2026 non-GAAP EPS lands modestly above the $2.85 floor near consensus $2.97, and FY2027 delivers $3.10-3.20 before meaningful TRIXIE contribution. Corporate costs partially normalize. The multiple stays in a 12.5-13.5x band because the structural discount factors — dual-class control, no dividend, retailer concentration, M&A-dependent top line — do not resolve. 13.0x FY2027 consensus $3.11 gives $40.4; a 7.0x EV/EBITDA on TTM $376.8M gives $2,638M of EV, $2,441M of equity after $196M of net debt, or $39.0 per share. Blending toward the upper end for the guidance raise and buyback optionality, $41.

Probability-weighting base at 55%, bull at 20%, and bear at 25% gives an expected return of approximately +4.5%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing owners should maintain exposure; prospective buyers have no urgent entry signal at $37.85, roughly 7% below the 52-week high of $40.795 after a 25% twelve-month advance.

- **Base case target: $41 (+8.3%).** 13.0x FY2027 consensus EPS of $3.11, cross-checked at 7.0x EV/TTM adjusted EBITDA of $376.8M less $196.3M net debt on 62.54M all-class shares ($39.0).
- **Bull case: $50 (+32%).** 16x FY2027 consensus, or ~14.3x $3.50 with TRIXIE contribution and buyback accretion. Matches the analyst high.
- **Bear case: $28 (−26%).** 10x flat $2.85 on organic deceleration and a Garden destock, near the upper end of the 2025 trough range ($25.97 low).
- **Timeframe: 12-18 months**, tied to the fiscal Q4/FY2026 report (late November 2026), the fiscal 2027 guidance that accompanies it, and the TRIXIE close guided to the first half of fiscal 2027 (October 2026-March 2027).

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE or BUY:**
1. Corporate expense growth reversing — nine-month or full-year corporate adjusted EBITDA drag returning toward the $58M nine-month FY2025 level, so that segment-level gains reach the consolidated line and total adjusted EBITDA grows mid-single-digit or better.
2. Buyback resumption at scale — deployment of a substantial share of the $128.0M authorization, particularly after the TRIXIE upfront payment clears, which would convert the 12% FCF yield into per-share accretion.
3. TRIXIE economics disclosed at 10x EBITDA or better with revenue and margin figures published, plus confirmation of euro hedging; or a price near $32-33, which would restore roughly 25% upside to the base case.

**Downgrade triggers to REDUCE or AVOID:**
1. Organic growth turning negative, or Garden organic growth below zero for two consecutive quarters, which would indicate the home-improvement channel weakness is transmitting.
2. Fiscal 2027 guidance implying non-GAAP EPS below $3.00 including TRIXIE, indicating the acquisition is dilutive in year one and the corporate cost build is permanent.
3. A quantitative goodwill retest disclosed, an impairment charge against the $972.7M of goodwill and intangibles, or quantification of a material receivable reserve against the newly cited pet-specialty retailer credit risk.

**Reassessment timeline:** the fiscal Q4/FY2026 report and FY2027 guidance in late November 2026 is the natural decision point, with the TRIXIE closing 8-K as an interim checkpoint.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory (6.8 → 6.8 → 6.0, now 5.8) has declined while the share price has been essentially flat since the June review ($37.78 → $37.85). That is not a price-driven downgrade. Three things changed on the fundamentals: management raised fiscal 2026 guidance (positive), the nine-month reconciliation revealed that corporate cost growth absorbed 63.4% of segment-level EBITDA improvement so consolidated growth was only 1.7% (negative), and the company committed roughly 16% of its market capitalization to a German acquisition at an undisclosed multiple sixteen months after exiting the UK (unresolved). Alongside that, organic growth decelerated, and there has been no open-market insider buying across roughly 25 Form 4 filings since February 2026, with buyback spend cut 81% while the stock traded at $28-32.

What holds the rating at HOLD rather than lower is the balance sheet, which is better than the automated screens suggest: $996.7M of cash, 0.52x net leverage, no maturity before February 2028, sub-market fixed coupons, an undrawn $600M revolver, and a 10-12% free cash flow yield. At 6.8x EV/adjusted EBITDA and 12-13x forward earnings against a peer median above 23x, the downside is cushioned by cash generation rather than by growth. What keeps it from ACCUMULATE is the absence of a dated catalyst: the TRIXIE close is a six-month window with unverifiable economics, the buyback is dormant, organic growth barely exceeds inflation, and the discount has identifiable structural causes — dual-class control with unresolved succession, no dividend, and 54% of revenue running through five retailers into the US home-improvement channel. Expected return of roughly +4.5% on a 55/20/25 weighting of base, bull and bear does not justify adding at the current price.
