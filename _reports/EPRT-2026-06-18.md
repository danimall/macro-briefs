**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Essential Properties Realty Trust is an internally-managed, single-tenant net-lease REIT with a differentiated franchise: it originates sale-leasebacks with unrated, middle-market operators of service- and experience-based businesses (car washes, dental/medical, early childhood education, QSR, automotive service), capturing 150-200bps higher cap rates than investment-grade-focused peers like Realty Income or Agree. The portfolio of 2,417 investments runs at 99.7% occupancy, a 14.6-year weighted-average lease term, 3.5x rent coverage, and 65.3% of ABR under master leases. The economic engine is straightforward: invest at 7.7% cash cap rates against a ~4.2% post-swap cost of debt, fund the spread with continuous accretive equity issuance, and compound AFFO/share. That model has worked: AFFO/share rose from $1.34 (2021) to $1.89 (2025) and Q1 2026 AFFO grew 23% YoY.

The thesis is intact but increasingly two-sided. The same model that produces a positive spread is entirely dependent on cost of capital remaining below earned cap rates. With the stock at $29.52 below the $31-$32 levels at which EPRT raised forward equity in early 2026, the cost of equity is rising precisely when accretive growth needs cheap capital. Management explicitly warns that if WACC rises without a corresponding cap-rate increase, "this spread will be reduced or eliminated."

Two company-specific warning signs deserve weight: the provision for real-estate impairment jumped to $16.8M in Q1 2026 from $5.9M a year earlier (+186%), concentrated in car washes (13% of ABR, the single largest category) and QSR; and rent coverage on Q1 2026 vintage acquisitions fell to 3.1x versus 4.7-5.9x in recent quarters, suggesting the company is reaching for yield as competition intensifies. We rate EPRT a HOLD on its income profile. The 4.2% well-covered dividend and low ~3.5x leverage make it a durable compounder, but at ~23x trailing earnings and a premium to NAV, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

EPRT targets the underserved middle-market segment of net-lease: smaller ($2-100M) sale-leaseback transactions with operators that the larger, IG-focused REITs avoid. As of 3/31/2026 the portfolio comprised 2,257 owned properties, 150 properties securing mortgage loans, and 10 ground leases, generating $584.2M of annualized base rent across 48 states and 662 brands. The average investment per property is only $3.0M, producing low-basis, fungible "small box" assets that are easier to re-tenant or sell.

The structural design of the book is the differentiator. In Q1 2026, 100% of investments were internally-originated sale-leasebacks, avoiding broker competition; 65.3% of ABR sits under master leases, which reduce a bankrupt tenant's ability to cherry-pick which units to keep; and 99.0% of ABR requires unit-level financial reporting, a rarity in net-lease that enables proactive credit monitoring. Industry exposure is led by car washes (13.0%), medical/dental (12.2%), early childhood education (10.9%), entertainment (9.4%), QSR (8.5%), and automotive service (8.3%). Diversification discipline is real: no tenant exceeds 3.2% of ABR (top 10 = 15.8%), with stated goals of <5% per tenant and <1% per property over time. The largest tenants are EquipmentShare (3.2%), Whistle Express Car Wash (1.9%), and Chicken N Pickle (1.5%).

## Financial Analysis

The growth profile is healthy and the balance sheet is among the strongest in the sector.

- **Revenue:** Q1 2026 total revenue was $158.8M versus $129.4M a year earlier (+22.7%), driven by a 277-property (+14%) increase in the portfolio plus contractual escalators.
- **Net income:** Q1 2026 net income was $59.95M versus $56.28M. FY2025 net income was $253.7M versus $203.6M in 2024.
- **AFFO:** Q1 2026 AFFO of $105.8M versus $85.7M (+23%). AFFO/share has compounded from $1.34 (2021) to $1.89 (2025).
- **Cap-rate spread:** Q1 2026 investments closed at a 7.7% cash / 8.8% GAAP cap rate against a ~4.2% post-swap cost of debt. This spread is the entire thesis and its chief vulnerability.
- **Balance sheet:** Total debt $2.63B; pro forma net debt/annualized adjusted EBITDAre of ~3.5x, well below the 5.5x internal target and among the lowest in the sector. Weighted-average debt maturity 4.0 years; 100% of debt fixed or swapped to fixed; no secured debt; $900M undrawn on the $1.0B revolver after the June 2026 notes termed out the drawn balance.
- **Capital raising:** Share count rose from 187.5M (12/31/24) to 216.2M (3/31/26), funded by forward equity (Feb 2026: 12.5M shares at $32.20 = ~$383M) plus ATM issuance. This funds accretive growth but is the source of per-share dilution risk and explains why the stock trades on AFFO/share momentum rather than absolute net income.
- **Cash flow quality:** Q1 operating cash flow of $99.8M comfortably covered the $67.6M dividend. The ~$373M negative investing cash flow reflects growth acquisitions, not distress.

The earnings cadence has been steady but recently wobbled: three beats and one miss over the trailing four quarters, with Q1 2026 GAAP EPS of $0.28 missing the $0.33 estimate by 14.9% (a figure distorted by impairment timing and share-count growth; AFFO/share remains the relevant operating metric). Estimate revisions are essentially flat: current-year consensus at $1.34 (-0.7% over 90 days) and next-year at $1.40 (+0.9%).

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three pillars. First, a scalable, relationship-driven origination engine: 57% of Q1 deals were with repeat counterparties and 100% were internally sourced, which captures higher cap rates and supports G&A leverage as the portfolio scales. Second, a large addressable market: the middle-market sale-leaseback space is fragmented and underserved by IG-focused capital. Third, balance-sheet dry powder: at ~3.5x leverage against a 5.5x target plus an undrawn revolver, EPRT can keep growing accretively even if equity issuance slows.

The constraint is cost of capital. AFFO/share growth has been a function of both spread and equity issuance volume. With the stock below recent raise levels, the implied cost of equity has risen, which mechanically narrows the accretive spread on new deals. The Q1 vintage rent coverage of 3.1x is the early evidence that the company is accepting weaker credits to keep deal volume up as the spread tightens. The late-cycle net-lease trade-off is to maintain growth by stretching on credit, or to protect credit by slowing growth and diluting AFFO momentum.

## Valuation Assessment

At $29.52 the stock trades at roughly 23x trailing earnings (trailing EPS ~$1.28; ~$1.89 AFFO/share for 2025 implies a high-teens AFFO multiple depending on share count) and yields 4.2% on the newly raised $0.32 quarterly dividend. Against the diversified-REIT peer set, EPRT screens richer on growth-adjusted quality but its headline P/E of ~23x is below the distorted peer median of 35.2x (peers like LXP at 35x, AAT at 81x, and GNL at negative net-income distortions that make the median unreliable). On yield, EPRT's 4.2% sits below the peer median of 5.6%, reflecting its superior balance sheet and growth, but also confirming the premium-to-NAV characterization that a Seeking Alpha note flags.

The valuation is fair rather than cheap. The case for upside is that EPRT deserves a premium to slower-growing, more-levered peers and that the analyst target band of $33-$40 (mean $37.10) implies roughly 12-36% upside. The case against treating those targets as floors: estimates are flat, the model faces a genuine cost-of-capital headwind, and the cheapness signal in the broader market has been an unreliable predictor when there is no independent catalyst. A new institutional holder (Baron Real Estate Income Fund) initiated a position citing the middle-market focus, while a recent Seeking Alpha note rates it Hold at "a premium to NAV." We anchor a base-case fair value around $32, roughly the level at which capital was willing to fund the company via forward equity in February 2026, implying modest upside but not the asymmetry a BUY requires.

## Competitive Landscape

EPRT competes against larger, lower-cost-of-capital peers (Realty Income, NNN REIT, Agree Realty, Broadstone) whose advantage is scale and access to cheaper IG debt. EPRT's moat is segment focus rather than scale: by underwriting unrated middle-market operators with internal credit analysis (Moody's RiskCalc plus unit-level rent coverage), it earns 150-200bps higher cap rates while mitigating risk through diversification, master leases, low per-asset basis, and mandatory tenant reporting. The origination model is differentiated and scalable.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-eroding at the margin. The 3.1x Q1 vintage coverage and rising impairments indicate that capital is flowing into the middle-market net-lease niche and competing away some of the spread that defined the franchise, which is why we score competitive position above neutral but short of high conviction.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is cost-of-capital spread compression. The business depends on raising debt and equity below earned cap rates; rising rates or a falling stock price directly throttle accretive growth, and management says so explicitly. This is a live headwind, not a theoretical one.

The second risk is the one the impairment data is now flagging. The Q1 2026 impairment provision is concentrated in car washes and QSR, the two largest service categories. Car washes have seen sector-wide overbuilding and consolidation stress, and several of EPRT's top tenants are car-wash operators (Whistle Express, Super Star, Flagstop). The 99.7% occupancy headline masks 7 vacant properties and 3.4% of leases below 1.0x unit-level coverage, plus ~14.6% of ABR at implied B+ or below ratings. The high-yield, unrated-tenant model produces credit losses even in a benign occupancy environment.

Additional risks: master-lease concentration cuts both ways (a multi-property tenant default could be outsized), low ~1.7-1.8% rent escalators erode real returns in inflationary periods, and geographic concentration in Texas (13.1% of ABR) exceeds the disclosed 10% threshold. None of these is acutely binary, but cumulatively they justify a below-neutral risk score because the credit-loss and spread-compression risks are not fully priced into a premium-to-NAV multiple.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 20.8% is roughly fair to the 18.1% one-year historical vol (spread +2.7%), and IV sits at the 10th percentile within its own chain: the market is not pricing an imminent catalyst. The 6.0% put/call skew points to modestly elevated put demand, and the put/call volume ratio of 7.00 indicates hedging activity, consistent with a cautious tone on a name that has drifted from its $34.73 high. (The reported jump in one snapshot to a four-digit IV is a thin-options data artifact and is non-informative.) The term-structure contango (near-term 20.8% < long-term 27.7%) is a normal shape. No near-term event risk is priced, but the put skew aligns with the cautious fundamental read.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$37-40):** EPRT continues compounding AFFO/share at mid-to-high-single digits as it grows the portfolio with ample dry powder and a differentiated origination engine. The cost-of-equity headwind reverses as the stock re-rates toward recent raise levels, and impairments prove a normal, contained cost of the high-yield model rather than the start of a credit cycle. At a higher AFFO multiple on a recovering AFFO/share base, the stock reaches the upper analyst band of $40. The 4.2% growing dividend provides carry while waiting.

**Bear case (~$24-25):** The cost of equity stays elevated, the accretive spread narrows or disappears, and AFFO/share growth decelerates sharply. Car-wash and QSR credit stress broadens, the 3.1x vintage coverage signals the company stretched into weaker deals, and impairments continue to rise, forcing a multiple de-rating toward the peer yield median of 5.6%, which on the current dividend implies a price near $23. A premium-to-NAV name with decelerating growth and rising credit losses is vulnerable to a 15-20% drawdown.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- AFFO/share growth re-accelerates above ~7% with stable-to-rising rent coverage on new vintages (back toward 4.5x+), confirming the company is not reaching for yield.
- The stock de-rates to a ~5%+ dividend yield (~$25-26) on unchanged fundamentals, restoring asymmetric upside.
- A sustained decline in the long end of the curve (10Y materially lower) that re-widens the accretive spread, evidenced by management raising acquisition-volume guidance.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Impairment provisions continue climbing above the Q1 2026 $16.8M run-rate for two consecutive quarters, or car-wash/QSR coverage deteriorates below 1.0x across a widening set of tenants.
- A dividend that ceases to grow or whose AFFO coverage compresses below ~1.4x, signaling the model is no longer self-funding.
- Forced equity issuance at progressively lower prices (well below current levels), confirming negative cost-of-capital spread on new deals.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, ~late July) is the natural checkpoint: watch the impairment line, new-vintage rent coverage, acquisition cap rates versus cost of equity, and AFFO/share guidance.

## Conclusion

EPRT is a high-quality, well-governed net-lease REIT with a differentiated middle-market franchise, sector-low leverage, and a track record of accretive AFFO/share compounding. Those qualities argue against avoiding the name. But the franchise sits at the intersection of two pressures: a cost-of-capital headwind that raises the cost of the equity capital the model runs on, and early credit stress in its two largest tenant categories that the occupancy headline obscures. At ~23x trailing earnings and a premium to NAV with flat estimates and no near-term catalyst, the risk/reward is balanced. The 4.2% well-covered and growing dividend, combined with the optionality of dry powder if the rate environment improves, supports holding for income. We rate EPRT a **HOLD (income)** with a base-case fair value around $32 and would turn constructive on either a de-rating toward a 5%+ yield or evidence that new-vintage credit quality has stabilized.
