## Executive Summary

Southern Copper is delivering the best financial results in its history, and that is precisely the problem with owning it at $200. First-half 2026 net sales rose 38.4% to $8,540.4M, operating margin reached 59.8%, and net income attributable to shareholders hit $3,246.8M ($3.93/share). Net debt at June 30 was $664.5M against roughly $11.1B of annualized EBITDA — leverage of approximately 0.06x — with no debt maturity until $51.2M comes due in 2028. Cash cost of copper net of by-products was a credit of $(0.03)/lb for the half. On any measure of balance-sheet quality or through-cycle asset durability, this is a top-decile mining company.

None of that earnings growth came from producing more copper. First-half copper output fell 3.8% year over year, with Toquepala down 11.5% and Cuajone down 9.9% on lower ore grades, and full-year 2026 guidance of 917,000 tonnes is 4.1% below 2025. The revenue increase is entirely a function of realized prices: Q2 2026 LME copper averaged $6.04/lb against a 10-year COMEX average of $3.46/lb cited in the company's own 10-K, silver averaged $73.49/oz versus $28.25 in 2024, and molybdenum $29.44/lb versus $21.21. Applying 2026 guided by-product volumes, reverting silver and moly to 2024 averages alone would add roughly $0.78/lb back to net cash cost. The negative cash cost is a price phenomenon, not a widening moat.

The valuation prices the peak as durable. At $200.11 the shares trade 29.5x trailing twelve-month EPS of $6.78, 25.8x the FY2026 consensus of $7.74, and 27.7x the FY2027 consensus of $7.22 — consensus itself models a year-over-year earnings decline. The stock sits 19% above the $168.25 mean analyst target and 21% above the $165 median, with 11 of 18 covering analysts at sell or strong sell and sentiment classified as degrading. Layered on top: Peru contributed 34.2% of FY2025 pre-tax income into an election year following two presidential removals in five months; Tía María faces seven active lawsuits at 42% construction completion; Los Chancas is physically occupied by illegal miners; and a Peruvian labor-shares judgment turns on a conversion ratio the company and plaintiffs read 10-million-fold apart, with the contingency explicitly not estimable. Probability-weighted twelve-month total return is negative. We recommend reducing exposure rather than shorting — the 88.9% Grupo México control stake leaves a thin float and 8.4 days to cover.

## Company Overview

Southern Copper is a Delaware-domiciled, 88.9%-owned indirect subsidiary of Grupo México, held through Americas Mining Corporation. Public float is roughly 11%. The company operates five open-pit copper mines, three underground polymetallic mines, two smelters and nine refineries across Peru and Mexico, organized in three segments: Mexican open-pit (Buenavista del Cobre, La Caridad/Pilares) at $7,623.0M of FY2025 net sales (55.7% of segment total); Peruvian operations (Toquepala, Cuajone, Ilo) at $5,247.6M (38.4%); and IMMSA, the Mexican underground unit, at $807.8M (5.9%).

Product mix in FY2025: copper $10,033.8M (74.8% of revenue), molybdenum $1,405.1M (10.5%), silver $973.9M (7.3%), zinc $529.9M (3.9%), other $477.2M. By-products rose to 25.2% of revenue from 23.4%, driven almost entirely by silver revenue growing 65.6%. Revenue by customer location is genuinely diversified — Mexico 24.2%, United States 13.9%, Switzerland 13.8% (almost certainly trading houses rather than end use), China 9.5%, Japan 7.3% — though the company does not disclose what share of revenue prices off COMEX versus LME, which limits any precise estimate of tariff-premium leverage.

Between 80% and 90% of production sells under annual or longer-term contracts priced off exchange averages at delivery. Provisionally priced copper is marked to forward LME or COMEX monthly until settlement, which makes reported revenue and receivables pro-cyclical beyond the physical business. At year-end 2025 the net provisional-price receivable was $156.2M positive versus $(41.6)M a year earlier.

Reserves are estimated at a long-term copper price of $3.30/lb — roughly 45% below current spot. That conservatism is real and explains the absence of impairment risk, but it also frames how far current realizations sit above the company's own planning assumption.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (audited).** Net sales $13,420.0M (+17.4%); cost of sales ex-D&A $5,359.2M; D&A and depletion $868.4M; operating income $7,001.7M for a 52.2% margin (48.6% in 2024, 42.4% in 2023). Incremental operating margin was 72.8% — revenue rose $1,986.6M while cost of sales rose only $517.8M — because the increase was price, not volume. Net income attributable to SCC $4,334.9M, EPS $5.24 as originally reported (restated to approximately $5.15 for 2026 stock dividends). Effective tax rate 36.4%, structurally permanent because Peruvian (29.5%) and Mexican (30%) statutory rates exceed the 21% U.S. rate, leaving $2,377.2M of foreign tax credits under a full valuation allowance.

Operating cash flow was $4,752.1M against capex of $1,325.3M, for free cash flow of $3,426.8M (25.5% of revenue). Cash dividends of $2,485.1M consumed 72.5% of FCF. Operating cash flow was held back by a $761.6M increase in trade receivables as year-end copper spiked — a price-driven working capital build rather than a collections issue, and one that reverses in the opposite direction if prices fall.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: cash $5,665.0M plus short-term investments $1,664.9M, total liquidity $7,329.9M; long-term debt $7,994.4M following the June 24 issuance of $1,250M of 5.350% notes due 2036 earmarked for Peru and Tía María; net debt approximately $664.5M; SCC stockholders' equity $12,632.2M. All debt is fixed-rate senior unsecured, with nothing due until $51.2M in 2028 and $6.8B thereafter. The only financial covenant sits on the legacy Yankee bonds (Minera México EBITDA/interest ≥ 2.5:1, in compliance). Ratings Baa1/BBB+/BBB+. Interest coverage on FY2025 figures was 18.9x; current ratio 3.89x; ROE 42.9%; ROA 21.7%.

A note on data hygiene: a screening feed circulating a debt-to-equity ratio of 1.94 does not reconcile to the filed balance sheet under any conventional definition — it approximates assets/equity. Total liabilities to equity is 0.93x and long-term debt to equity 0.61x.

**1H 2026.** Net sales $8,540.4M (+38.4%); operating income $5,103.6M (59.8% margin); Q2 operating margin 61.2%; net income $3,246.8M; EPS $3.93. Capex of $864.7M was up 56.2% year over year against a full-year budget of $1,925.5M. Peruvian inflation ran 3.8% in the half versus 1.3% a year earlier, pushing gross costs the wrong way even as net cash cost went negative on by-product credits.

**Earnings quality flags.** Three items modestly flatter reported results. Asset retirement obligations *declined* from $546.1M to $471.1M on $(112.4)M of estimate changes, of which $61.0M was credited to cost of goods sold — 0.9% of FY2025 operating income, and a recurring non-cash tailwind in both 2024 and 2025. A $471M closure liability for five open pits, three underground mines, two smelters and nine refineries looks light against industry benchmarks, and the company states that in Mexico there is currently no enacted law or contract requiring closure remediation, so the obligation rests on its own judgment of historical government practice. Second, the positive provisional-pricing receivable position adds mark-to-market gain that reverses on any price decline. Third, the Critical Audit Matter in the FY2025 audit is capitalization of leachable material on Mexican leach pads — a judgment-heavy estimate against a $1,114.5M long-term leach inventory balance. None of these is individually material to a company earning $4.3B, but together they mean reported peak earnings are slightly better than underlying cash economics.

**Financial health score: 8.0.** Near-zero net leverage, no refinancing cliff, all-fixed-rate debt, 18.9x coverage, $7.3B of liquidity, and 25%+ FCF margins. The deductions are that the cash dividend has grown to roughly $3.7B annualized against a $1.93B capex budget, which at mid-cycle prices would consume essentially all free cash flow, and that the $3B repurchase authorization is effectively exhausted (119.5M shares for $2.9B, no activity since Q3 2016, roughly $86M of headroom) — so there is no buyback lever without a new board authorization.

## Growth Analysis

Earnings growth and volume growth are moving in opposite directions, and only one of them is durable.

**Volumes are contracting.** FY2025 copper production was 2,108.2 Mlb (956.5 kt). First-half 2026 was 1,016.8 Mlb, down 3.8%, with the two Peruvian mines down 9.9% and 11.5% on grade. Full-year guidance of 917,000 t is 4.1% below 2025 — raised only modestly from the 911,400 t plan set in February. Grade decline at Toquepala and Cuajone is a geological fact, not a cyclical one.

**The growth pipeline is real but concentrated and back-end loaded.** Tía María ($1,805M budget, 120 kt/yr SX-EW cathode) reached 42% completion at June 30 with $1,101M committed and $693M invested, guided to start in 2H2027 — meaningful progress from 24% complete and $790M committed at year-end. El Pilar ($551M, 36 kt/yr) begins construction Q1 2027 for 2H2029 production. Together those two projects underpin the disclosed path from 917 kt in 2026 to roughly 1.06 Mt by 2029, a 15% increase over three years. Beyond that, Michiquillay (225 kt/yr potential) had its preoperational period extended three years in April 2025 with $375.0M of remaining purchase price deferred and explicitly not a present obligation, and Los Chancas is physically occupied by illegal miners — a loss of site control, not a permitting delay. Peru's disclosed decade pipeline is roughly $10.3B with a further $10.2B under discussion in Mexico, but capital plans are not production.

**Consensus already models the earnings roll-over.** FY2026 consensus EPS is $7.74 and FY2027 is $7.22 — the out-year is 6.7% *lower*. Quarterly estimates of $1.84 (Q3) sit below the $2.01 Q2 actual despite copper holding near highs, reflecting the volume guide and Peruvian cost inflation.

**Revision trend is genuine but shallow and price-tracking.** Current quarter estimates are up 7.7% over 30 days and 13.2% over 90; next quarter up 10.1% and 12.4%. But current-year estimates are up only 4.6% over 30 days against a 10.2% *cut* over 90 days, and next year is up 5.7% over 90 days. Roughly one percentage point of the 90-day current-year decline is mechanical restatement from the May stock dividend; the rest tracked the March–May copper pullback, and the 30-day recovery tracked the July–August rebound. The estimate series is a copper-price derivative with a lag. The high-conviction revision signal — +30% or more over 90 days on an inflecting earnings base — is not present here, and would not apply in any case to a company at 26x forward earnings on peak realizations.

Delivery record is solid but unremarkable: three beats and one in-line over four quarters, with surprise magnitudes of 1.4%, 6.1%, 1.3% and 0.0%. This is a company whose results are set by exchange prices, so estimate accuracy reflects price forecasting more than execution.

**Growth prospects score: 5.5.** Real project growth arriving in 2027–2029, offset by four years of declining or flat production in the interim, grade depletion at the two Peruvian mines, two of four growth projects impaired or deferred, and a consensus out-year below the current year.

## Valuation Assessment

**Current multiples.** Trailing twelve-month EPS is $6.78 ($1.33 + $1.54 + $1.90 + $2.01), giving a P/E of 29.5x at $200.11 — which reconciles with the reported trailing P/E of 29.6x. Forward multiples: 25.8x FY2026 consensus of $7.74; 27.7x FY2027 consensus of $7.22. Enterprise value is $167.6B ($167.0B equity plus $0.66B net debt), for 21.3x FY2025 EBITDA of $7,870M or 15.1x an annualized 1H26 EBITDA of roughly $11.1B. Free cash flow yield on FY2025 actuals is 2.05%. Cash dividend yield is $4.40 annualized ÷ $200.11 = 2.20%.

**On the dividend framing.** The Q2 10-Q presents a "total estimated dividend" of $3.23/share comprising $1.10 of cash plus $2.13 of stock-dividend value computed at $177.32. Read as a yield, that summed figure implies roughly 6.4%. The actual cash yield is 2.20%. The stock component is a pro-rata share distribution funded from treasury: it dilutes no holder's percentage ownership, transfers no cash, and reconciles exactly within equity ($2,895.9M capitalized from retained earnings in FY2025, reappearing as $2,184.3M of APIC and $711.6M of treasury reduction). The disclosure is complete and the arithmetic transparent, but the two components are not economically equivalent and cannot be added into a yield. Investors sizing this position on a 6%+ payout are mispricing it. One practical consequence: treasury fell from 65.5M shares at year-end to 50.3M at June 30, and the August distribution of 0.0120 per share consumes roughly 10.0M more — at that pace, treasury is exhausted in about five quarters.

**The core valuation question is whether current earnings are the right base.** Guided 2026 copper volume is 2,021.6 Mlb, so each $0.10/lb move in realized price is roughly $202M of revenue that flows almost entirely to pre-tax margin. After the 36.4% effective tax rate plus statutory workers' participation (8% Peru, 10% Mexico), marginal after-tax retention is roughly 55–60% — about $110–120M, or $0.13–0.14/share, per $0.10/lb. Working the reversion arithmetic: copper from $6.04 to the 10-K's cited 5-year COMEX average of $4.23/lb removes roughly $2.40–2.60 of EPS; silver from $73.49 to the 2024 average of $28.25 removes about $0.72; molybdenum from $29.44 to $21.21 removes about $0.34. Full reversion to five-year averages would put EPS near $4.00–4.30, implying $80–105 at a 20–25x multiple. That is not a forecast — it is the sensitivity that a ~26x forward multiple asks investors to ignore.

**Peer and history context.** Reserves are carried at $3.30/lb. The 10-year COMEX average is $3.46/lb, the 5-year $4.23/lb. Realized 2026 prices are 43–75% above those anchors. A cyclical earning a 60% operating margin at the top of its price cycle historically trades *below* market multiples, not at 26x forward. The COMEX-over-LME premium — the specific 2025 tailwind — has already compressed from $0.31/lb in 2025 to $0.12/lb in Q2 2026, and 2026 policy has moved toward *narrower* protection: Section 232 proclamations in April and June 2026 lowered duties and relaxed the U.S.-content threshold from 95% to 85%.

**Sell-side positioning is unusually negative for a stock at these levels.** Mean target $168.25, median $165, low $128.50, high $250.00. Current price is 19% above the mean. The ratings distribution — 0 strong buy, 1 buy, 6 hold, 5 sell, 6 strong sell — is one of the most bearish in the large-cap materials universe, and the one-month change was a downgrade from buy to hold. Analyst targets function as floors on cheap, inflecting stocks; they do not function that way on a stock trading 19% above consensus with a majority of sells. Here the target dispersion ($128.50 to $250.00, a 1.9x range) is itself the signal: the sell side is modeling copper price scenarios, not company execution.

**Valuation score: 3.5.** The multiple is stretched against a normalized earnings base, the out-year consensus declines, the yield offers no support at 2.2%, there is no buyback capacity, and the FCF yield of roughly 2% is below the risk-free rate. This is not a value trap in the classic sense — it is a high-quality asset priced for a commodity regime to persist.

## Competitive Landscape

SCCO's structural advantage is long reserve life at low strip in Sonora and southern Peru, combined with polymetallic by-products that genuinely diversify revenue. It is not a scale leader in copper — mid-large rather than Freeport or Codelco scale — and the mix shift to 25.2% by-products meaningfully reduces single-metal price sensitivity.

One non-obvious and underappreciated advantage: as an integrated producer with two smelters, SCCO *manufactures* sulphuric acid as a smelting by-product and is a net seller, with 353,000 t contracted for 2026 delivery. Reporting through mid-2026 attributes part of the copper spike to an acid supply squeeze following the Hormuz disruption and Chinese export restrictions. Peers who must buy merchant acid for SX-EW leaching face a cost shock that SCCO largely avoids and partly monetizes. The filings do not disclose acid revenue separately — it sits inside "Other," $477.2M in FY2025 — so the benefit cannot be sized, but the direction is favorable and durable while acid stays tight.

Against that, competitive *trajectory* is the weaker read. Grade decline is reducing output at the two Peruvian mines; global copper output share is flat to declining through 2027; site control has been lost at Los Chancas; and Michiquillay slipped three years. Molybdenum offtake is concentrated with two counterparties (Molymet ~70% of Peru concentrate through 2028, Molymex ≥80% of Mexican concentrate through 2026). Labor peace in Peru is unusually secure — six unions, 50.8% unionized, CBA extensions running to 2027–2033 with no-stoppage provisions — while Mexico carries the Taxco strike now in its nineteenth year and a San Martín arbitration under amparo challenge.

One market-structure note that cuts against a simple scarcity narrative: reporting in July 2026 put global visible copper inventories at their highest since 2003 while prices set records. Record prices coexisting with record visible stocks is consistent with location arbitrage and specific mine disruptions (Grasberg, Kamoa-Kakula) rather than uniform physical scarcity. If Hormuz transit normalizes, Chinese acid exports resume, and the disrupted third-party mines restart, three of the four price drivers unwind simultaneously.

**Competitive position score: 5.5.** Excellent asset quality and a genuine acid advantage, offset by declining volumes on grade, two impaired growth projects, and concentrated offtake.

## Risk Assessment

**Peru political and legal exposure (highest-magnitude unpriced risk).** Peru contributed $2,319.2M of FY2025 pre-tax earnings, 34.2% of consolidated pre-tax income, and holds Toquepala, Cuajone, Ilo, Tía María, Los Chancas and Michiquillay. The FY2025 risk factors document a president removed October 10, 2025, an interim president impeached February 17, 2026, a replacement installed February 18, and general elections in April 2026. The election outcome and the incoming government's mining stance are unresolved. Mitigating factor: only $37.3M (0.8%) of group cash was held in Peru at year-end versus 68.2% in the United States, so cash exposure is minimal even though asset and earnings exposure is not.

**Tía María litigation.** Seven separate lawsuits are pending against the Peru Branch seeking to void the EIA, cancel the project, annul the concession or construction licence, or suspend works — plaintiffs include the Arequipa regional government, a district municipality, a water users' association and four individuals. The Supreme Court's Carpio Lazo ruling upheld the EIA and the Arequipa case was decided in the company's favor on January 23, 2026 with an appeal expected, but with $693M invested and $1,101M committed at 42% completion, an adverse injunction is a genuinely asymmetric event. Contingency amounts are not reasonably estimable.

**Peruvian labor-shares judgment.** A 1996 suit reached final judgment against the company in 2009 and is in execution. On December 22, 2025 a Lima court ordered delivery of 8,488,383 investment shares treating one labor share as equal to one current investment share; the company appealed, obtained an amparo on February 11, 2026 nullifying the underlying attachments, and enforcement was provisionally suspended. The company argues the correct conversion is 10,000,000 labor shares to one investment share — a ten-million-fold interpretive gap. Context for scale: remaining investment shareholders hold 0.71% of the Peruvian Branch and the entire non-controlling interest carries at $66.8M, so an adverse ruling delivering 8.49M shares would represent a large multiple of current NCI. The Superior Court of Lima is expected to rule during 2026. Not estimable, and not visibly priced.

**Commodity price concentration.** Discussed above; the single largest driver of forward returns and entirely outside management control.

**Mexican regulatory and legal.** The May 2023 Mining Law amendments cut concession terms from 50 to 30 years and added water restrictions and closure guarantees, still before the Supreme Court. From the 2014 Buenavista spill the company faces an appealed dismissal of PROFEPA's criminal complaint, a second criminal complaint filed by SEMARNAT in October 2023, three surviving collective actions and roughly 45 individual civil suits. September 2024 judicial reform introduced popular election of judges, which matters given the volume of Mexican litigation.

**Governance and related-party structure.** SCCO is 88.9% controlled with an 11% float. Most Mexican electricity is purchased from MGE, a Grupo México subsidiary, under a contract running to 2032, with SCCO taking roughly 88.5% of MGE's output; two Grupo México wind affiliates sell 98.1% and 83.5% of their output to SCCO. Treasury stock includes 50,208,315 Grupo México shares carried at roughly $434.7M at June 30, so approximately $0.4B of reported book equity is in substance an investment in the control person. The Mexican defined-benefit plan holds 30% of assets in Grupo México shares, up from 25%. The company's own risk factors caution that the controlling shareholder could cause it to obtain loans, increase dividends or provide other funding. In January 2026 the board approved up to MXN 1.5B (~$87M) of voluntary contributions to Sonora state programmes, of which MXN 500M went to IMSS Sonora in February and a further MXN 500M to SEMARNAT on July 24, 2026 — SEMARNAT being the agency with an outstanding criminal complaint against a subsidiary. Nothing in the filings connects the contributions to the litigation, the amounts are immaterial against $4.3B of net income, and community relations and permitting continuity are ordinary explanations. We flag the counterparty overlap as a fact an investor should know exists, and draw no inference of motive.

**Management alignment.** Total stock-based compensation expense in FY2025 was $1.7M for the directors' plan plus $0.7M and $0.4M for two employee purchase plans — under $3M at a company earning $4.3B. Executives participate in Grupo México share plans, not SCCO equity, so senior management's equity alignment runs to the parent rather than to SCCO minority holders. Form 4 activity through August 5, 2026 shows only routine 400-share quarterly director grants and a 100-share director sale; there were no open-market purchases by C-suite executives. Given the controlled float and negligible equity compensation, this is an absence of signal rather than a bearish one. A CEO transition occurred between filings — the FY2025 10-K was signed by Oscar Gonzalez Rocha (age 87, 22-year incumbent) and the Q2 10-Q by Leonardo Contreras Lerdo de Tejada, with two Item 5.02 8-Ks filed in April 2026. The effective date, permanence and stated reason are unverified; Contreras' reported holding was roughly 1,400 shares.

**Management quality score: 4.8.** Capital allocation execution is competent — near-zero net leverage, 42.9% ROE, conservative $3.30/lb reserve pricing, disciplined project financing. Alignment with minority shareholders is weak: no SCCO equity comp for executives, no insider buying, an exhausted buyback authorization with no new mandate, a dividend presentation that sums cash and non-cash components, and pervasive related-party dependence on the parent.

**Risk factors score: 3.8.** Multiple unquantifiable binary contingencies (labor shares, Tía María injunctions, Buenavista criminal complaints), 34% of pre-tax income in a politically unstable jurisdiction with an unresolved election, and a tariff-premium tailwind already reversing. The market appears to be pricing the copper cycle, not the jurisdictional tail.

### Options Market Signal

The options market prices this as ordinary volatility rather than an imminent event. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 52.7% sits essentially on top of 51.0% one-year realized (spread 1.6%, "fair"), the term structure is flat at 52.7% near versus 52.7% far, and the nearest-expiry implied move is ±4.5%. Put/call skew of 1.4% is balanced; put/call volume of 0.12 and open-interest ratio of 0.32 are call-tilted. In other words, options positioning offers no confirmation of the bearish valuation case — there is no crowded downside hedge, no volatility premium to harvest, and no evidence of informed positioning ahead of a catalyst. That is consistent with a market treating SCCO as a pure copper-beta instrument. The six-month implied move of ±23.7% is the honest framing of the distribution.

Short interest requires care. The reported short percentage of float is 23.0%, with shares short of 11,023,738 and days to cover of 8.4. Note the reported shares short (11.0M) equal only about 1.3% of the 834.3M shares outstanding, so the 23.0%-of-float figure implies a substantially narrower float definition than shares outstanding; the two figures are not internally consistent, and readers should treat the 23.0% figure with caution. Regardless of the exact denominator, days to cover of 8.4 is high, and with an 88.9% controlled register any sizeable short position in this name is structurally squeeze-prone. This is the principal mechanical reason we recommend trimming rather than shorting.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $250 (+25%)

Copper's move is structural rather than cyclical. Electrification, grid buildout and data-center electrical demand create a multi-year deficit that current mine supply cannot fill; incremental supply requires prices well above the $4.23/lb five-year average. Third-party disruptions at large operations persist, visible inventories are misleading because they are location-arbitraged rather than freely available, and the sulphuric acid squeeze structurally disadvantages SX-EW competitors while SCCO monetizes acid as a by-product. Silver's strength has independent monetary drivers and holds above $60/oz. In that world FY2027 EPS reaches $8.50–9.00 rather than the $7.22 consensus, Tía María starts on schedule in 2H2027 adding 120 kt at negligible incremental cost, and the path to 1.06 Mt by 2029 de-risks. A 28–29x multiple on $8.75 gives $245–255, matching the $250 high analyst target. The fortress balance sheet — 0.06x net leverage, no maturity until 2028, all fixed-rate — means SCCO captures the entire upside without financing constraint, and 34% of the copper price increment converts to distributable cash within a year.

### Bear Case — $125 (−38%)

The current earnings base rests on three reversible supply shocks. Hormuz transit normalizes, Chinese acid export restrictions ease, and the disrupted third-party mines restart — removing the acid squeeze and the disruption premium simultaneously. The COMEX-over-LME premium, already compressed from $0.31/lb to $0.12/lb, goes to zero as the 2026 Section 232 proclamations lowering duties and relaxing content thresholds take full effect. Chinese industrial demand stays weak, capping the marginal buyer. Copper settles near $5.00/lb and silver near $45/oz, taking FY2027 EPS to $5.50–6.00 while guided volumes still fall 4% and Peruvian cost inflation of 3.8% erodes margins. At a 21–23x multiple — still a premium to the group for balance-sheet quality — that is $120–130, consistent with the $128.50 low analyst target. A deeper tail exists: full reversion to five-year average copper and 2024 by-product prices implies EPS of $4.00–4.30 and a $85–105 range, near the $91.65 fifty-two-week low. Adverse resolution of the labor-shares execution or a Tía María injunction would compound it.

### Our View

The bull case requires a specific commodity regime to persist for years; the bear case requires only that three identifiable supply disruptions resolve. That is asymmetric against the current price. We weight bull 30%, base 45%, bear 25%, giving a probability-weighted price return of approximately −7.0% and a total return near −4.8% including the 2.20% cash dividend. That is not thesis impairment — the business is excellent — but it is a below-fair expected risk-adjusted return with unfavorable downside skew.

## Conclusion

Southern Copper is a high-quality mining business at a low-quality entry point. Net debt of $664.5M against roughly $11.1B of annualized EBITDA, no maturity until 2028, a 61.2% Q2 operating margin, 42.9% ROE and reserves booked at $3.30/lb make this one of the most resilient balance sheets in the sector. The problem is what the market is paying for it: 29.5x trailing and 25.8x FY2026 consensus earnings for a company whose copper output falls 4.1% this year, whose consensus FY2027 EPS is 6.7% *below* FY2026, whose negative net cash cost is a by-product-price artifact worth about $0.78/lb, and whose tariff premium has already compressed from $0.31/lb to $0.12/lb. The stock trades 19% above the $168.25 mean analyst target with 11 of 18 ratings at sell or strong sell.

Layer on 34.2% of pre-tax income in Peru through an unresolved election cycle, seven active lawsuits against a project at 42% completion, a labor-shares execution turning on a ten-million-fold conversion dispute that the company states cannot be reasonably estimated, an exhausted buyback authorization, and a 2.20% cash yield that is materially lower than the 6.4% a summed cash-plus-stock dividend implies. Probability-weighted twelve-month total return is approximately −5%.

**REDUCE.** Trim into strength; retain a small residual for the Tía María and structural-copper optionality; revisit at $150–160 or on confirmed volume stabilization.
