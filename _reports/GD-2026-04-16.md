## Executive Summary

General Dynamics is a $90B aerospace and defense conglomerate with franchise positions across nuclear submarines (sole-source Columbia-class), main battle tanks (sole-source Abrams), and large-cabin business jets (Gulfstream). Revenue grew 10.1% in 2025 to $52.6B, with a $118B total backlog up 30% YoY providing exceptional multi-year visibility. Free cash flow recovered to $4.0B (94% of net earnings), and ROIC improved to 14.2% from 12.3% two years ago. The company recently received a $15.4B Columbia-class contract modification and announced its 29th consecutive annual dividend increase.

At 21.9x forward earnings, GD trades at a 48% discount to its A&D peer median of 42.1x. This discount reflects legitimate concerns: DOGE-related pressure on the $13.5B GDIT business (Technologies segment margins guided down 30bps), a Q4 2025 EPS miss of -1.1%, and 90-day consensus estimate revisions of -5.7% for FY2026. However, these headwinds are concentrated in the lower-margin IT services segment (~26% of revenue) while the structural demand drivers — European rearmament, the Columbia/Virginia submarine programs, and Gulfstream's fleet renewal — are accelerating in the hardware segments that generate 74% of revenue. The dual-theater military environment (active naval operations in the Persian Gulf plus the largest Ukraine air attacks of 2026) creates procurement urgency across submarines, combat vehicles, and munitions that directly benefits GD's core franchises.

## Company Overview

General Dynamics operates four segments: **Aerospace** (25% of 2025 revenue, Gulfstream business jets), **Marine Systems** (32%, nuclear submarines and surface combatants), **Combat Systems** (17%, armored vehicles and munitions), and **Technologies** (26%, government IT services and C5ISR electronics). The company employs approximately 117,000 people across 65+ countries.

The portfolio structure is unusual in the sector: GD is the only prime that pairs sole-source military hardware platforms with a premium commercial aviation franchise. Gulfstream cycles correlate with corporate profitability rather than government budget cycles, providing a hedge against defense spending fluctuations.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $39.4B (2022) → $42.3B (2023) → $47.7B (2024) → $52.6B (2025), a 3-year CAGR of 10.1%. The two fastest-growing segments in 2025 were Aerospace (+16.5%) and Marine Systems (+16.6%), both driven by program ramps rather than one-time awards.

**Profitability:** Operating income grew to $5.4B (10.3% margin), with segment margins ranging from 7.0% (Marine Systems) to 14.4% (Combat Systems). The key margin story is Gulfstream's learning curve: as the G700 and G800 mature in production, Aerospace margins are expanding toward 14.0% in 2026 from 13.3% in 2025. Marine margins improved from 6.5% to 7.0% as supplier cost overruns stabilized. Technologies margins are the weak spot, guiding down to 9.2% from 9.5%.

**Cash flow quality:** Operating cash flow of $5.1B represented 122% of net earnings in 2025, up from 109% in 2024. FCF of $4.0B (94% NI conversion) reflects genuine earnings quality. Capex rose to $1.16B (+27% YoY) as the company invests in submarine production capacity and Gulfstream manufacturing.

**Balance sheet:** The Altman Z-Score of 2.28 sits in the grey zone, driven by $7.0B of long-term debt (down from $9.2B in 2022). D/E of 2.23x is elevated but declining. Current ratio of 1.44 is adequate. The company holds $2.3B cash plus a $5B undrawn credit facility with no commercial paper outstanding. Net interest expense of $314M will rise modestly to ~$340M as $500M of notes maturing in mid-2026 are refinanced at higher rates.

**Capital return:** The dividend was raised to $1.59/quarter in March 2026 (29th consecutive increase), yielding 1.9%. Share repurchases totaled $637M in 2025, primarily for dilution offset, with 6.8M shares remaining authorized (2.5% of float).

**Earnings consistency:** Three beats in four quarters, with the sole miss a narrow -1.1% in Q4 2025 ($4.17 actual vs. $4.22 estimate). The miss appears to be a single-quarter anomaly driven by Technologies segment softness rather than a pattern of deteriorating visibility.

## Growth Analysis

**Backlog:** $118B total backlog (+30% YoY) and $179B total estimated contract value provide extraordinary multi-year revenue visibility. The backlog-to-revenue ratio of 2.2x means GD has over two years of revenue secured before winning a single new contract. By segment:
- Marine Systems: $55.1B backlog (+27% YoY) — anchored by Columbia-class, Virginia-class, DDG-51 destroyers
- Combat Systems: $27.2B (+60% YoY) — European rearmament driving $9.2B in international vehicle orders
- Technologies: $14.3B (+3% YoY) — weakest growth, reflecting DOGE pressure
- Aerospace: $21.8B — Gulfstream book-to-bill of 1.2x even as deliveries accelerated 16.5%

**European rearmament:** Combat Systems' 60% backlog growth is the clearest quantitative signal of Europe's defense spending acceleration. NATO members increasing defense budgets to 2-3% of GDP creates a multi-year procurement cycle for armored vehicles, munitions, and support systems where GD's European operations (Piranha, Pandur, ASCOD) are well-positioned.

**Submarine industrial base:** The Columbia-class is a 12-boat, $125B+ program of record — the DoD's #1 acquisition priority. Electric Boat is hiring 8,000 workers in 2026 and the Navy is investing $900M in automated submarine component factories. The $15.4B contract modification in March 2026 and $1.27B Virginia-class support contract in April 2026 confirm funding trajectory. This program provides revenue visibility measured in decades.

**Gulfstream fleet renewal:** The completion of an entirely new aircraft family (G300, G400, G500, G600, G700, G800) positions Gulfstream for margin expansion as R&D declines and production learning curves reduce unit costs. The G700 and G800 in particular are competing for the replacement cycle of 595+ G650/G650ER aircraft in service.

**Estimate revisions:** FY2026 EPS consensus has been cut 5.7% over 90 days (from $17.28 to $16.30), but has stabilized over the last 30 days (+0.1%). FY2027 estimates show a similar pattern: -5.2% over 90 days, -0.3% over 30 days. The stabilization after the initial cut suggests the DOGE impact has been largely priced into estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

GD trades at 21.9x trailing and forward EPS of $15.45, reflecting a significant discount to the A&D peer median of 42.1x. Even against the most comparable peer (NOC at 19.6x), GD's premium is modest and justified by its faster revenue growth (10.1% vs. NOC's ~6%) and commercial aerospace diversification.

**Consensus targets:** Mean $393, median $390, range $327-$444. At $339, the stock trades 13% below the median target and 31% below the high target. The recent Deutsche Bank downgrade to Hold ($387) and Jefferies target cut to $380 reflect post-rally caution rather than fundamental deterioration. Wells Fargo initiated at Overweight/$400.

**Valuation math:** At $16.30 FY2026E EPS and the current 21.9x multiple, the stock prices at $357. The peer discount provides a margin of safety: applying a conservative 23x multiple (still well below peer median) to $16.30 yields $375. If FY2027 EPS of $18.05 is achieved and the multiple holds at 22x, the implied price is $397.

**Dividend yield:** 1.9% is in line with the peer median (1.4% for NOC/LHX). The 29 consecutive years of increases and strong FCF coverage (FCF payout ratio ~28%) make this a secure income component.

The discount to peers is partially explained by GDIT's lower-margin, lower-growth profile dragging down the consolidated multiple. A sum-of-parts approach that values the defense hardware segments at 25x and GDIT at 15x would yield enterprise value implying a stock price well above $400. The market is applying a conglomerate discount that understates the franchise value of the hardware businesses.

## Competitive Landscape

GD holds monopoly or near-monopoly positions in three categories:

1. **Nuclear submarines:** Electric Boat is the sole designer and primary builder of Columbia-class SSBNs. Virginia-class is a duopoly with HII. No new entrant can credibly challenge this position — the barriers include decades of specialized engineering, security clearances, and a $13B+ capital base.

2. **Main battle tanks / combat vehicles:** Sole-source producer of Abrams (M1A2 SEP) and Stryker for the U.S. Army. International competitors exist (Rheinmetall, KNDS), but switching costs for the U.S. military are prohibitive.

3. **Large-cabin business jets:** Gulfstream competes with Bombardier (Global series) and Dassault (Falcon) but leads in the large and ultra-large cabin segments. The completed fleet renewal gives Gulfstream the most modern lineup in the industry.

**Technologies/GDIT** faces a more competitive landscape — Leidos, SAIC, Booz Allen, and Accenture Federal all compete for government IT contracts. This is the segment most vulnerable to budget pressure and the one where GD has the least defensible position.

## Risk Assessment

**DOGE / government spending cuts:** The Technologies segment ($13.5B revenue) is directly exposed to government efficiency initiatives. GDIT filed a WARN notice for 74 layoffs in April 2026, and segment margins are guiding down 30bps. If DOGE actions intensify, GDIT could see revenue declines of 3-5%, shaving $0.15-0.25 from EPS. This risk is partially mitigated by the segment's ~26% revenue share and the administration's stated support for defense hardware spending.

**Fixed-price contract risk:** 51% of U.S. government revenue is fixed-price. On multi-year submarine and vehicle programs, cost overruns cannot be passed through. The 2024 Marine margin compression from supplier cost growth demonstrated this risk. With oil above $100 and diesel at record highs, material and transportation cost inflation could pressure margins in 2026-2027.

**Submarine execution:** Electric Boat is attempting to hire 8,000 workers while managing the simultaneous ramp of Columbia-class and sustained Virginia-class production. Skilled worker shortages and delivery delays are documented. The Navy's $900M investment in automated factories addresses the problem, but execution risk remains material given program complexity.

**Tariff impact:** Tariffs reduced Aerospace margins by 30bps in 2025. The G300's mid-cabin airframe supplier is Israel-based, adding geopolitical supply chain risk. Further tariff escalation (50% China tariffs under discussion) could pressure Technologies segment component costs.

**Continuing resolution risk:** GD's 2026 guidance assumes timely FY2026 budget passage. A prolonged CR would delay new program starts and compress Technologies margins further. Political dynamics around deficit spending ($1.5T+ defense budget) create meaningful uncertainty.

**Concentration:** 68% of revenue from the U.S. government (57% DoD). All contracts are terminable for convenience, though the practical probability of terminating the Columbia-class or Abrams programs is negligible.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($400-440, +18% to +30%)
- European rearmament drives Combat Systems backlog conversion at 14%+ margins over 3-5 years
- Columbia-class ramp provides 20+ years of Marine revenue visibility; margins expand toward 8.5% as production learning curve matures
- Gulfstream Aerospace margins reach 15%+ as G700/G800 production matures and R&D normalizes
- Dual-theater military environment accelerates procurement timelines and potential for supplemental defense appropriations
- FY2027 EPS reaches $18-19 at a 23-24x multiple
- DOGE impact proves temporary as hardware budgets remain protected

### Bear Case ($290-310, -9% to -14%)
- DOGE actions expand to defense hardware programs, compressing budgets beyond GDIT
- Submarine execution falters — cost overruns on Columbia-class force margin revisions below 7%
- Gulfstream demand softens as macro deterioration hits corporate aviation demand (recession probability 45-50% in current environment)
- FY2026 EPS comes in below $16.00 as multiple segments disappoint
- Tariff escalation and energy cost inflation pressure margins across all segments
- Multiple compresses to 18-19x on deteriorating estimates

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**

## Conclusion

General Dynamics combines franchise-quality defense positions with a premium commercial aviation business, backed by improving cash flow generation. At 21.9x earnings — a 48% discount to A&D peers — the stock reflects GDIT's DOGE exposure while undervaluing multi-decade submarine program visibility and Gulfstream margin expansion potential. Estimate revisions have stabilized after a 5.7% cut, and near-term options volatility creates an entry point for investors with a 12-18 month horizon. The 2.2:1 upside/downside ratio supports a BUY recommendation, with the primary risk being execution challenges at Electric Boat and an intensification of government efficiency cuts beyond IT services.
