## Executive Summary

OPENLANE reported a clean second quarter on August 4, 2026 — revenue $554.6M (+15.1%), Adjusted EBITDA $103.2M (+19%), Operating Adjusted EPS $0.40 against roughly $0.35 consensus — and raised full-year guidance for the second time this year, to $385-400M of Adjusted EBITDA and $1.40-1.50 of Operating Adjusted EPS. The volume driver is real and mechanically supported: commercial units rose 39% to 276,000 as off-lease vehicles began maturing, and OPENLANE's private-label software sits inside the remarketing workflows of most North American OEM captives, giving it first look at that supply. Gross merchandise value rose 41% to $10.5B.

The market faded the print. The stock traded as high as $42.20 on August 4 and $42.69 intraday on August 5 before closing that session at $38.22 and settling at $37.18 — an 11-12% round trip from the post-earnings high on a beat-and-raise. Three disclosed facts explain most of the reaction. First, the volume win is arriving at lower economics: auction and related fees per vehicle fell to $538 from $563 (-4.3%) and marketplace yield on GMV fell 40bps to 2.5%, so Marketplace Adjusted EBITDA grew 27% against GMV growth of 41%. Second, Adjusted Free Cash Flow fell to $53.4M from $86.5M (-38%) while Adjusted EBITDA rose 19%, because growing the marketplace pulls AFC receivables and the 25-30% of that book not funded through securitization conduits is a direct call on corporate cash — the H1 finance-receivable build was $216.7M against only $134.8M of incremental securitization obligations. Third, stripping the non-recurring $15.9M Canadian digital-services-tax reversal, H1 Adjusted EBITDA grew 8.6% rather than the headline rate, and full-year guidance implies 11-15% growth rather than 16-20%.

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on the name, and the bar for repeating it should be higher than for changing it. The case for an upgrade exists: estimates are rising (current year $1.46, +5.7% over 90 days; next year $1.71, +4.3%), the capital structure was materially simplified with the May 2026 elimination of the Series A Preferred, leverage is 1.0x against a 3.5x covenant, and every published analyst target ($43-52, mean $46.22) sits above the current price. The case against is the arithmetic: at 21.7x next-year consensus with ~17% expected earnings growth, a 45% profit contribution from a lender to independent used-vehicle dealers whose 31+ day delinquencies doubled to 0.6% and whose allowance rose from 0.8% to 1.1% of receivables, an unresolved 13.4% sponsor stake with registration rights, and no open-market insider purchase in six months of Forms 4, the asymmetry is roughly +10% to base case against -25% to bear. That is a hold, not an accumulation.

## Company Overview

OPENLANE (formerly KAR Auction Services, renamed May 2023) operates digital wholesale used-vehicle marketplaces and a captive floorplan lender. Two reportable segments:

**Marketplace** runs digital wholesale platforms in the U.S., Canada and Europe plus 14 vehicle logistics centers in Canada — the only meaningful physical footprint left after the 2022 sale of the ADESA U.S. physical auction network to Carvana. The economically distinctive asset is the private-label SaaS platform that OEMs and captive finance companies brand as their own; the 10-Q states it supports the majority of North American manufacturers, captive finance companies and commercial off-lease sellers. Revenue is predominantly agency/consignment — OPENLANE records buyer and seller fees rather than vehicle proceeds — with purchased-vehicle (principal) transactions at roughly 2% of volume, concentrated in Europe where the local convention differs.

**Finance (AFC)** provides 30-90 day inventory-secured floorplan loans, primarily to independent used-vehicle dealers, through roughly 90 locations serving about 15,000 unique dealers. Roughly 70-75% of receivables are securitization-funded; the remainder comes from corporate cash and working capital.

Scale: ~1.5 million transactions and $28.8B of GMV in 2025 against a management-sized U.S./Canada addressable market of ~15 million wholesale vehicles annually. The commercial and dealer relationships carry no binding volume commitments, and the 10-K discloses that many customer agreements are terminable by the customer for convenience on notice.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue compounded 8.4% annually from $1,519.4M (2022) to $1,934.5M (2025). Operating income moved $173.7M → $(5.2)M → $182.2M → $196.6M, the 2023 loss reflecting goodwill impairment (cumulative impairments now $250.8M in Marketplace and $161.5M in Finance). Adjusted EBITDA progressed $231.2M → $272.0M → $293.4M → $332.6M.

**Two earnings-quality items deserve explicit handling.**

First, FY2025 reported net income of $177.7M is not a clean base. Income taxes were $14.5M on $192.2M of pretax income — a 7.5% effective rate versus 30.4% in FY2024 and 26.3% year-to-date in 2026 — with Q4 2025 taxes recorded as *negative* $27.8M. Normalizing FY2025 at 26.3% yields roughly $141.6M, meaning approximately $36M (~20% of reported net income) came from a discrete tax item. A valuation-allowance release is the most plausible cause given prior-year language about the U.S. net deferred tax asset, but that is inference; the annual tax footnote was not read. What is established by arithmetic is the direction. FY2026 guidance embeds $59-63M of tax expense at a normalized rate, so year-over-year GAAP net income comparisons understate underlying improvement.

Second, the trailing P/E of -63.9 shown in the data feed (and the -$0.96 trailing EPS) is an accounting artifact of the October 2025 preferred repurchase, which produced a ~$242.2M deemed dividend reducing income attributable to common stockholders. It has no bearing on operating performance and the same figure appearing in the "forward P/E" field is a data error — forward consensus is a positive $1.46.

**Q2 2026 detail (vs. Q2 2025):** revenue $554.6M vs. $481.7M; auction and related fees $259.0M vs. $213.9M (+21%); SaaS and other $73.4M vs. $63.1M (+16%); purchased vehicle sales $114.9M vs. $98.5M (+17%); finance revenue $107.3M vs. $106.2M (+1%); operating profit $67.5M vs. $47.4M (+42%); net income $44.3M vs. $33.4M; Adjusted EBITDA $103.2M vs. $86.7M. Units 481,000 vs. 380,000 (commercial 276,000 vs. 198,000; dealer consignment 205,000 vs. 182,000). Marketplace gross margin slipped to 31.6% from 32.1%, hurt by $4.9M of transportation margin compression from elevated North American fuel prices and the rising share of gross-recorded purchased vehicle sales.

**Segment mix matters for how this should be valued.** Q2 Adjusted EBITDA split Marketplace $56.7M / Finance $46.5M (55%/45%); for the half, $108.3M / $91.6M. Finance revenue fell 1% for the half as total finance revenue yield dropped to 16.8% from 18.2% and net finance margin to 12.8% from 13.6%, offset by receivable growth (managed receivables $2,622.5M, +11.7%) and lower funding cost. Segment operating profit was flat for the half at $75.5M vs. $75.2M. Credit signals are mixed: 31+ day delinquencies rose to 0.6% of managed receivables from 0.3%, allowance to $30.0M (1.1%) from $19.0M (0.8%), yet net credit losses for the half *fell* to $14.6M from $18.6M and annualized provision improved to 1.4% of average receivables. The delinquency doubling is off a very low base and is not a loss event; the allowance build is management's own estimate moving.

**Cash flow requires the company's own framework.** Because AFC payables sit in operating activities, finance receivables in investing and securitization obligations in financing, OCF less capex is not owner earnings. Q2: OCF $52.8M − capex $13.8M = $39.0M free cash flow; after severance/other and netting the $186.2M receivable increase against $197.9M of incremental securitization obligations, Adjusted Free Cash Flow was $53.4M versus $86.5M. For the half, the receivable build consumed $216.7M against $134.8M of new securitization obligations — roughly $80M absorbed by corporate cash and working capital. Neither the quarterly nor half-year figure should be annualized: Q4 is the seasonally weakest volume quarter, consignor settlement timing swings period-end working capital, and H1 included a one-time C$13.9M ($10.0M) cash tax refund.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: cash $189.7M, restricted cash $28.1M, total assets $5,006.4M, stockholders' equity $1,571.2M (up from $1,240.7M, principally the $289.8M reclassification of preferred temporary equity). Corporate debt is a single instrument — $547.2M of 2025 Incremental Term Loans at SOFR+250, due October 2032, amortizing 0.25% quarterly, with fair value $548.6M (par, no credit stress priced). No revolver borrowings; $405.7M available under U.S./Canadian revolvers due June 2028; €40M of European lines undrawn. The 3.5x maximum Consolidated Senior Secured Net Leverage covenant, tested only when revolver loans are outstanding, stood at **1.0x actual**. A +100bp rate move costs roughly $1.4M per quarter.

AFC's securitization facilities (U.S. $2.0B committed; Canadian C$500M) **both expire January 31, 2028**, collateralized by $2,643.8M of receivables against $1,897.1M of gross obligations. They are not secured by OPENLANE's general assets, but a termination event permits the banks to cause the stock of AFC Funding Corporation to be transferred, and the securitization agreements incorporate the Credit Agreement's financial covenants — so a corporate breach cross-triggers AFC funding. A single expiry date for both facilities is a concentrated refinancing event 18 months out.

On the Altman Z-Score screening below the 1.81 distress threshold: recomputation using June 30 figures also lands near 1.7, but the model penalizes any company consolidating a captive lender — $1,887.0M of securitization obligations sit in current liabilities against $2,621.1M of current finance receivables, and $2,970.2M of the $5,006.4M asset base belongs to a lending business with structurally low revenue-to-assets. Direct solvency evidence points the other way: 1.0x leverage, zero revolver drawn, debt at par, no maturity before 2028. The genuine leverage question is AFC's funding-market access, not corporate solvency.

## Growth Analysis

The wholesale channel is emerging from a four-year supply drought. Production disruption in 2020-2023 suppressed new-vehicle supply, pushed used prices to records and shrank wholesale volumes; new-vehicle supply has recovered, lease originations have been healthy for several quarters, and those leases mature from 2026 onward. OPENLANE's claim to disproportionate capture rests on its private-label position with most North American commercial off-lease sellers — the vehicles route through its software first. Q2's commercial units +39% is the first strong confirmation of that mechanism in reported numbers rather than in narrative, though management attributes it to two drivers: off-lease growth *and* the onboarding of a new private-label customer.

Three qualifications temper the growth score.

**Unit economics are moving against the volume.** Fees per vehicle $538 vs. $563 (-4.3%) while GMV per vehicle rose 10.6% to $21,830. Company-stated causes — richer commercial mix (structurally lower yield) and higher car values — are mechanical and benign. Competitive fee pressure would produce an identical observable, and the disclosed data cannot distinguish them. Note also a presentation change effective Q4 2025 that folded reconditioning, inspection and certain transportation revenue into the auction line (Q2 2025 "Auction fees" of $134.9M became "Auction and related fees" of $213.9M), so any pre-Q4-2025 fee-per-unit series is not comparable and the company simultaneously shifted its headline metric to yield-on-GMV.

**Underlying growth is slower than headline.** FY2026 guidance of $385-400M less H1 actual of $199.9M implies H2 of $185-200M, flat to modestly below H1, versus H2 2025 actual of $163.1M (+13% to +23%). H1 contained the $15.9M non-recurring Canadian DST benefit; excluding it, H1 Adjusted EBITDA was $184.0M vs. $169.5M (+8.6%), and full-year guidance implies +11% to +15% over FY2025's $332.6M. Management flagged "leaning into additional investments in 2026" tied to go-to-market spend — deliberate margin reinvestment.

**Geographic and segment composition.** Q2 U.S. revenue rose 24% to $339.2M while foreign revenue rose 3% to $215.4M. The CEO stated dealer volume contracted in Canada while growing ~31% in the U.S. Canada is where the 14 logistics centers and the 2023 Manheim Canada acquisition sit — the asset-heavy portion — and represented 58% of foreign revenue. The mix shift toward asset-light U.S. dealer volume is favorable for margins in principle but raises Canadian utilization questions; the $7.0M loss on sale of excess Montreal property in April 2025 is consistent with having acquired more physical capacity than needed. Finance revenue is flat to declining as yields follow prime rates.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current quarter $0.38 (+2.2% 30d), next quarter $0.33 (+3.4%/+6.2%), current year $1.46 (+4.5%/+5.7%), next year $1.71 (+4.0%/+4.3%). This is the signature of a company delivering slightly ahead of plan, not a step-function inflection. It does not reach the magnitude at which revision velocity becomes a standalone buy signal. Earnings track record supports moderate confidence in consensus: three beats and one miss over four quarters, surprises of +10.8%, +12.4%, -6.1%, +24.8%.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K names competitors specifically: Manheim, ADESA U.S. (now Carvana), America's Auto Auction, ACV Auctions and EBlock in the marketplace; NextGear Capital for AFC. Manheim and NextGear are both Cox Automotive businesses, so OPENLANE's largest marketplace competitor and its largest floorplan competitor share a parent capable of bundling. OPENLANE sold 913,000 units in H1 against a management-sized ~15 million annual TAM, so it remains a minority participant in the channel it is best positioned within.

The private-label commercial franchise is the genuine moat and is evidenced rather than asserted — OPENLANE's software is embedded in OEM captives' own remarketing workflows, which creates switching friction and first look at off-lease supply. AFC is complementary in a disclosed way: it extends credit to increase marketplace transactions, leverages a local dealer base to drive registrations, and provides a bundling channel.

The most economically important competitive scenario the 10-K names is de-exclusivity: "the simultaneous listing and selling of vehicles on multiple online sales platforms in North America." If sellers list the same unit across OPENLANE, ACV and Manheim, industry fee yields compress structurally. Yield already fell 40bps year over year; the company attributes it to mix and car values, and I cannot distinguish that from early competitive pressure using disclosed data. The reverse risk — a market reversal toward physical auctions — would strand the asset-light model.

AFC's competitive setting is constrained by a disclosed structural dynamic rather than a cycle: growth in franchise and public dealership used-car retailing shifts volume toward buyers who do not use floorplan financing at all. AFC's ~15,000 independent dealers are the segment of the market most exposed to consumer credit tightening and used-vehicle affordability — a relevant exposure.

Trajectory assessment: improving in share and volume (commercial +39%, U.S. dealer +31%, new private-label onboarding, repossession services +$4.8M), deteriorating in per-unit economics and flat in the lender. Net 6.1.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Take-rate compression is the mechanism by which a volume win fails to become a profit win.** Off-lease supply arrives as low-yield commercial volume. If commercial mix keeps rising while yield keeps falling, GMV and units can decouple from Marketplace EBITDA — as they already partially have (GMV +41% vs. Marketplace Adjusted EBITDA +27%).

**2. Customer concentration is increasing even though no customer reaches 10% of revenue.** The 10-K states no single customer exceeded 10% of 2025 consolidated revenues while warning that loss of key customers would be materially adverse. The Q2 commercial surge was driven substantially by *one newly onboarded private-label customer* that is unnamed, unquantified, and — per the 10-K's general language on such agreements — potentially terminable for convenience. A broadening revenue mix does not establish reduced customer concentration; here the two facts point in opposite directions.

**3. AFC funding and credit.** Both securitization facilities expire January 31, 2028; the U.S. facility permits banks to take AFC Funding Corporation's stock after a termination event; securitization covenants incorporate the corporate Credit Agreement's covenants. Delinquencies doubled to 0.6% and the allowance rose to 1.1% from 0.8% of receivables. Neither is a loss event, but both are the leading edge of the exposure most sensitive to consumer credit stress.

**4. Corporate liquidity is consumed by growth.** The 25-30% of AFC's book funded off corporate cash and working capital is a recurring call that Adjusted EBITDA does not capture. Buyback capacity ($156.6M remaining of the April 2025 authorization, expiring December 31, 2026) and receivable growth compete for the same dollars.

**5. Fuel and geopolitics now appear as a quantified P&L line.** The Q2 10-Q names the Iran conflict twice and ties it to $4.9M of transportation margin compression, and states the conflict and tariffs could affect the number of off-lease vehicles available to the wholesale industry — a channel to the core growth thesis, not just a cost line.

**6. Sponsor overhang.** Ignition Acquisition Holdings LP (Apax) converted into 16,424,728 shares on May 28, 2026 — 13.4% of shares outstanding — and holds registration rights from June 2020 agreements, giving a registered path to exit a ~$610M position. The dilution has already occurred and is in the share count; what remains is float supply. There is no filing or credible news evidence of a planned sale, so this is unresolved in both directions.

**7. Carvana counterparty dependence.** OPENLANE relies on Carvana complying with payment obligations and on AFC's right to occupy space in Carvana's physical auction locations under the 2022 divestiture commercial agreement — a dependency on a named competitor.

**8. Control and disclosure quality.** The Q2 10-Q corrected a $355M error in the FY2025 10-K's pledged-receivables disclosure ($2,448.2M actual vs. $2,803.5M previously disclosed) — a 14% overstatement of securitization collateral in an audited annual filing. No restatement occurred and management concluded disclosure controls were effective. Most plausibly a tagging or compilation error with no economic effect, but a control-quality observation to carry forward with a CFO in his second year and an ERP conversion in flight. Separately, a Third Amendment to the Credit Agreement dated June 30, 2026 was filed as an exhibit with no 8-K and no note discussion; its substance is unverified and should be assumed neither favorable nor adverse.

**9. Unhedged FX.** Roughly 43% of FY2025 revenue was foreign and the company states it has entered no foreign exchange contracts; a 1% move in the month-end CAD rate moves net income by ~$1.6M via intercompany loans.

Retired risks worth noting: the 10-K's four Series A Preferred risk factors — 14% as-converted dilution, Apax influence and board designation rights, preferential liquidation and dividend rights, and the 105%-of-liquidation-preference change-of-control put — are moot following the May 2026 conversion and Certificate of Elimination. Anyone reading the FY2025 10-K alone would materially overstate capital-structure risk.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress that the fundamentals do not obviously support. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 41.2% against 31.7% one-year realized (rich by 9.5 points, 64th percentile within the chain), the term structure is in backwardation (41.2% at one month vs. 34.4% at six months), and the put/call skew is 8.9% with the data source characterizing it as elevated put demand. More telling is the change: since the July 22 snapshot, ATM IV rose from 39.2% to 50.9% (+11.6pp) and the put/call open-interest ratio rose from 25.45 to 41.15 while the stock fell 6.9%. The nearest expiry prices a ±11.4% move.

Two caveats before drawing conclusions. The chain contains only 25 contracts across four expirations, so ratio statistics are fragile and a handful of institutional put positions can dominate. And the six-month tenor shows a skew of -0.2% — essentially flat — meaning the protection demand is concentrated in the front end rather than expressing a durable bearish view.

The most consistent reading is hedging around a specific near-term event rather than a fundamental short thesis, and the obvious candidate is the Apax block. Short interest of 2.9% of float with 2.0 days to cover confirms there is no crowded bearish position. For a HOLD, the practical implication is that anyone wanting exposure is better paid selling volatility than buying stock here, and that a resolution of the sponsor stake — either a completed secondary or evidence of a hold — is the single event most likely to compress the front-end premium.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $49 (+31.8%)

The off-lease wave is a multi-year supply restoration, not a single-quarter comparison. Lease originations have been healthy for several quarters and mature progressively from 2026, and OPENLANE's private-label software sits ahead of most of that supply. If commercial units compound at 25-30% through 2027 while yield stabilizes near 2.5% (mix is now largely rebased), Marketplace Adjusted EBITDA can grow 20%+ on operating leverage over a fixed technology base, and FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA reaches $440-460M against FY2026 guidance of $385-400M. The U.S. dealer channel growing 31% adds high-yield volume that offsets commercial mix dilution. The capital structure is now simple and cheap — one term loan at SOFR+250 due 2032, 1.0x leverage against a 3.5x covenant, $405.7M of undrawn revolver — leaving room to fund AFC growth and repurchase stock. Buybacks in H1 averaged $30.73, below the current price. If Apax exits in an orderly registered sale and the front-end volatility premium collapses, the stock re-rates toward 28x $1.71 as the two overhangs the market is discounting (take rate, sponsor supply) resolve. Consensus targets already sit at $43-52.

### Bear Case — $28 (-24.7%)

The volume inflection is a mix shift disguised as growth. Fees per vehicle are already down 4.3%; if the industry moves toward simultaneous multi-platform listing — the scenario the 10-K names explicitly — yields compress structurally and OPENLANE processes more cars for less money. Marketplace gross margin has already slipped to 31.6% from 32.1%, transportation margin is exposed to fuel with the Iran conflict quantified at $4.9M in one quarter, and management is deliberately raising go-to-market spend. Ex the $15.9M DST reversal, H1 Adjusted EBITDA grew 8.6%. Meanwhile the lender providing 45% of segment profit is at the front edge of deterioration — delinquencies 0.3% → 0.6%, allowance 0.8% → 1.1%. AFC's borrower base of ~15,000 independent dealers is precisely the cohort that fails first in a used-vehicle affordability squeeze, and both securitization facilities come due January 31, 2028 with a termination remedy that reaches AFC Funding's equity. Adjusted Free Cash Flow already fell 38% while EBITDA rose, and growth consumes corporate liquidity. Layer a 13.4% registered-resale-capable sponsor stake, four executives selling outright into strength with zero open-market purchases, and a $355M collateral-disclosure error in the audited 10-K, and the multiple compresses to 16-18x on FY2027 estimates that are themselves revised down.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** Existing owners have a positive 12-month path; new capital has no urgent entry.

- **Base case: $41** — 24x FY2027 consensus EPS of $1.71, cross-checked against 12.5x an FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA of roughly $430M less ~$400M of net corporate debt on ~124M shares. **+10.3% from $37.175.**
- **Bull case: $49** — 28.5x $1.71, requiring yield stabilization at or above 2.5% for two consecutive quarters and resolution of the Apax stake. **+31.8%.**
- **Bear case: $28** — 18x a compressed $1.55 FY2027, reflecting continued fee-per-unit erosion plus AFC provisioning toward management's ~2% long-term expectation. **-24.7%.**
- **Probability-weighted return:** approximately +7% at 50/25/25 base/bull/bear — positive but below the threshold that justifies adding, particularly against 25% downside and an unresolved float overhang.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the Q3 print (late October/early November 2026) as the first decisive checkpoint and the buyback authorization expiry (December 31, 2026) as a capital-allocation tell.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Marketplace yield on GMV holds at or above 2.5% for two consecutive quarters *with* commercial units still growing 20%+ — this separates mix rebasing from competitive fee erosion, the single most important open question.
2. Adjusted Free Cash Flow returns to growth alongside Adjusted EBITDA (i.e., the securitization conduits fund the incremental receivable build rather than corporate cash), or the securitization facilities are extended beyond January 2028 on comparable terms.
3. The Apax position is resolved — a completed registered secondary at a modest discount, or a 13D/G amendment indicating intent to hold — combined with the front-end volatility premium normalizing toward realized vol.
4. A price near **$31-32** on unchanged guidance, which would put the stock near 18x FY2027 consensus and inside the sum-of-parts range with a genuine margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. AFC 31+ day delinquencies above 1.0% of managed receivables, or annualized net credit losses above 2.0%, in any quarter — this would confirm the consumer-credit channel rather than portfolio seasoning.

## Conclusion

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on OPENLANE, and the trajectory (6.4 → 6.5 → 6.2 → 6.2 → 6.0) reflects a business whose operating results are improving while the terms on which they are being bought have not improved commensurately. The Q2 print confirmed the thesis that mattered most — off-lease supply is flowing through OPENLANE's private-label position, commercial units grew 39%, and guidance was raised for a second time. The capital structure is the cleanest it has been since the Carvana divestiture: one term loan, 1.0x senior secured net leverage against a 3.5x covenant, zero revolver drawn, no preferred, debt trading at par.

What has not improved is the price paid per dollar of that improvement. At $37.175 the stock is 25.6x FY2026 Operating Adjusted EPS guidance, 21.7x next-year consensus, and 12.5x FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA for a business that draws 45% of segment profit from a floorplan lender whose revenue is flat, whose yield is falling with prime rates, whose delinquencies doubled and whose allowance rose from 0.8% to 1.1% of receivables — into a macro setting where consumer credit is the live question. Ex the non-recurring DST reversal, H1 Adjusted EBITDA grew 8.6%; fees per vehicle fell 4.3%; Adjusted Free Cash Flow fell 38% while Adjusted EBITDA rose 19%. Consensus targets of $43-52 are supportive, but they have not moved in three months across two guidance raises, which suggests they already anticipated them.

Two overhangs are genuinely unresolved and are being priced in the options market rather than the equity: a 13.4% sponsor stake with a registered path to exit, and the January 2028 expiry of both securitization facilities. Neither is a reason to sell a position; both are reasons not to build one at 21.7x forward with 25% bear-case downside. Own it for the off-lease cycle if you already do. Wait for either yield stabilization at 2.5% with continued unit growth, or a price near $31-32, before adding.
