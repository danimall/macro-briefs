**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Charles Schwab is the rare large-cap financial where the central earnings driver is mechanically improving regardless of the macro path. The 2022–23 deposit/rate stress, which forced reliance on $10B+ of FHLB borrowings and triggered client cash-sorting, is reversing. In Q1 2026, net interest income rose 16% YoY *despite lower asset yields*, driven entirely by the wind-down of expensive wholesale funding: interest expense fell 22%, bank-deposit funding cost dropped to 0.20% from 0.72%, and average FHLB borrowings collapsed to $1.3B from $10.7B. Net interest margin expanded to 2.88% from 2.53%. This is the most important dynamic in the story and it does not require rising rates to continue; it is a self-healing balance sheet repricing its own funding base.

The valuation has not caught up to the earnings recovery. At $88.70, Schwab trades at roughly 14.4x the $6.15 current-year EPS consensus and ~12.3x the $7.22 next-year estimate, against a peer median of 19.2x, for a franchise generating 40% return on tangible common equity and a 49% pre-tax margin. Estimate revisions are positive across every horizon (+5.1% current-year, +6.3% next-year over the trailing 90 days), and the company beat in three of the last four quarters. Capital return is accelerating ($2.4B buyback in Q1, a 19% dividend hike, $12.1B remaining authorization), and CEO Rick Wurster put $1.85M of personal capital into the stock at $84.23 in late May while publicly arguing it is undervalued.

The single binding constraint is the AOCI-inclusive Tier-1 leverage ratio at 6.8%, the low end of management's 6.75–7.00% target. This is directionally self-resolving: the $10.1B pre-tax HTM unrealized loss is shrinking as rates fall and securities roll off, and asset duration has shortened to 1.7 years. A constraint that is already loosening while still permitting accelerating buybacks does not justify withholding a constructive rating on a name trading five turns below peers with rising estimates and a CEO buying in the open market. The risk/reward skews positive, and I am moving to a BUY.

## Company Overview

Schwab operates one of the largest U.S. wealth-management and brokerage franchises: $11.77 trillion in client assets, 39.1 million active brokerage accounts, and $493 billion in balance-sheet assets as of 3/31/2026. It is structurally a bank wrapped around a brokerage, monetizing client cash through net interest revenue (sweep deposits funding investment securities and loans), asset management fees, trading revenue, and a bank-deposit-account fee from TD.

Two reportable segments: **Investor Services** (~78% of revenue; Q1'26 net revenue $5.08B, pre-tax income $2.51B) covers retail brokerage, advisory, banking/trust, and retirement services. **Advisor Services** (~22%; Q1'26 net revenue $1.41B, +22% YoY; pre-tax income $679M, +53% YoY) provides custody and trading for independent RIAs and is the faster-growing segment, helped by RIA long/short strategy margin balances. The embedded TD Ameritrade client base (acquired 2020) and a low-cost, scale-driven model are the core moats. Management frames an $85T+ U.S. investable-wealth TAM against its $11.8T as the organic runway.

## Financial Analysis

The trajectory is a sharp recovery from the rate-stress trough:

- **FY2025:** revenue ~$22.6B (from ~$19.4B FY24); net income $8.85B (from $5.94B); adjusted diluted EPS $4.87 (vs. $3.25).
- **Q1 2026:** total net revenue $6.48B (+16% YoY); net income $2.48B (+30%); diluted EPS $1.37 (+38%); pre-tax margin 49.2% (vs. 43.8%); ROE 23%; ROTCE 40%.
- **NIM 2.88%** (from 2.53%), driven by the funding mechanism described above. Interest expense $818M (-22% YoY).
- **Asset gathering:** Q1'26 core net new assets $140B; daily average trades +34% YoY to 9.9M; margin loans $126.7B (+52% YoY), of which ~$21B is RIA long/short strategy leverage.
- **Capital return:** $2.4B buyback in Q1 (~$12.1B remaining on the $20B authorization); 19% dividend hike to $0.32/quarter. Issued $1.5B Series L preferred (Apr 2026); redeeming $2.1B Series I (Jun 2026), optimizing the preferred stack.

The balance-sheet caveat: Schwab carries $10.1B pre-tax ($7.7B after-tax) of HTM unrealized losses plus ~$3.9B on AFS. As a Category III bank it currently excludes most AOCI from regulatory capital, so reported Tier-1 leverage is 8.9% while the AOCI-inclusive figure is 6.8%. That 6.8% is the metric management runs to and explains the disciplined-but-rising pace of buybacks. The mechanism that fills this hole — securities rolling off at lower-than-replacement yields as rates normalize, plus shorter duration (1.7 years) — is the same one expanding NIM. FCF and accrual quality are not the relevant gauges for a bank; margin trajectory, ROTCE, and the funding-cost glide path are, and all are strongly positive. (The Altman Z-Score is non-meaningful for a leveraged bank/broker and should be disregarded.)

## Growth Analysis

Three layers of growth are stacking:

1. **NII recovery (largest, near-term).** The NII increase came purely from funding-cost improvement. As the remaining wholesale funding rolls off and bank-deposit costs continue to reprice down, this continues independent of Fed direction. NII sensitivity disclosures show a gradual +200bp adds ~7.3% and -200bp cuts ~7.6%, but the base-case funding-cost glide path is positive even on a flat-rate scenario.

2. **Organic asset gathering.** Core net new assets against an $85T+ TAM, plus DAT growth and the jump in margin balances, drive fee and interest revenue across all monetization channels. Advisor Services pre-tax income up 53% YoY is the standout.

3. **Strategic call options.** Forge (private-market trading, closed March 2026, $636M), spot crypto (bitcoin/ethereum, phased 2026 rollout with Paxos as sub-custodian), and client-facing AI agents (June 2026). These are immaterial to near-term numbers but extend the franchise into private markets and digital assets without speculative-trading brand dilution.

The estimate-revision pattern confirms momentum, with revisions accelerating in the most recent 30 days: earnings are recovering from a depressed base while consensus rises, and the stock trades well below the multiple that would price it in.

## Valuation Assessment

At $88.70, SCHW trades at ~14.4x the $6.15 CY EPS consensus and ~12.3x the $7.22 next-year figure. The peer median in Investment Banking & Brokerage is 19.2x (GS 19.0x, MS 19.2x, IBKR 36.2x, HOOD 40.1x; RJF the outlier discount at 14.3x). Schwab earns 40% ROTCE and a 49% pre-tax margin, quality metrics that justify a premium to, not a discount from, GS/MS. The five-turn gap to the peer median is the mispricing.

This is a cycle-recovery name where forward and normalized earnings, not trailing multiples, are the correct lens. EPS is inflecting (+38% YoY in Q1), revisions are positive across all horizons, and forward P/E sits far below the 30x threshold where the multiple already prices continued beats. On these stocks, consensus targets lag: the mean target of $115.85 (~31% upside) and median $118 will revise upward as consensus catches the funding-cost story. The low analyst target of $84 is essentially the current price, so the most bearish covering analyst sees almost no downside, which bounds the risk asymmetrically.

A simple bridge: 17x (still a discount to peers, in line with Schwab's own pre-stress range) on $7.22 next-year EPS = ~$123. Even 16x on the $6.15 CY figure = ~$98, ~11% above current. The valuation offers asymmetric upside with a downside floor set by the most bearish analyst at current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

Schwab competes against Fidelity (private, ~$5T+ workplace assets), Morgan Stanley/E*Trade, Vanguard, Robinhood, and Interactive Brokers. Industry dynamics are competitive on commissions, deposit rates, and fund expense ratios, but Schwab's scale and embedded TD Ameritrade base anchor a durable cost advantage. The trajectory is stable-to-improving: Advisor Services revenue +22% and pre-tax income +53% YoY indicates share gains in the RIA channel, and core net new assets show the gathering engine intact.

Two strategic moves carry execution risk. Wurster is pursuing a "total asset capture" strategy targeting Fidelity's workplace/recordkeeping assets — ambitious and execution-dependent (sourced to trade press, treat as directional). The crypto/AI rollout is positioned around long-term wealth-building rather than speculative trading, a deliberate contrast to Robinhood. The structural tailwind is the same one driving NIM: normalizing rates reduce cash-sorting pressure and let Schwab reprice deposits down faster than asset yields fall. I score competitive position 7.0 — stable franchise with a faster-growing advisor segment, tempered by genuine price competition and the unproven workplace push.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Client cash-sorting / rate sensitivity (central risk).** A renewed inflation impulse forcing a fast rate rise would reignite cash-sorting and reliance on costly wholesale funding, repeating the 2022–23 episode. This is the bear case ($75-78) and it is a macro tail, not a fundamental deterioration thesis. The Fed held at 3.75% upper bound in Q1'26 after cutting 75bp in late 2025; the current environment runs in Schwab's favor.

2. **TD IDA constraint (idiosyncratic).** Schwab earns 15bp on cash swept to TD's banks and must keep IDA balances between $60B and $90B through July 2034, with a floor-breach penalty. Balances were $73.6B at 3/31/26. The contract limits flexibility in migrating cash onto Schwab's higher-NII balance sheet (only $1.1B moved in Q1).

3. **Capital/regulatory (AOCI inclusion).** A March 2026 federal proposal would phase AOCI into regulatory capital over five years. Management already manages to AOCI-inclusive metrics and expects no material impact, but it is a real overhang and the swing factor for buyback pace. The 6.8% adjusted Tier-1 leverage is the binding constraint, though it is loosening as AOCI recovers.

4. **Margin-loan boom, double-edged.** +52% YoY margin growth, ~$21B of it RIA long/short leverage, is a strong NII driver but a market-sensitive exposure; a sharp dislocation could trigger margin/credit stress. Credit quality is currently pristine (negligible charge-offs).

5. **New ventures and concentration:** spot crypto introduces irreversible-transaction/custody/Paxos-reliance risk; 41% of mortgages are in California; $12.7B foreign counterparty exposure (mostly France).

I score risk 6.0 — meaningful idiosyncratic items (IDA constraint, AOCI rule, margin exposure) but with the central rate risk currently running favorably and the capital constraint self-resolving.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 46.1% versus 24.3% one-year historical vol — rich, in the 82nd percentile of the current chain, with a backwardated term structure (46.1% 1-week vs 27.6% long-term). This is a near-dated event-hedging spike (the implied 1-week move is only ±0.8%), not a structural bearish signal; the 1-month and 3-month IVs reset to ~25.5–27.6%. The put/call OI ratio rose to 1.42 from 1.15 since late May, but skew is balanced (2.7%) and put/call *volume* is light at 0.49, consistent with routine downside hedging rather than informed bearish positioning. Short interest is negligible (31,780 shares). The options market is not contradicting the fundamental thesis; the front-end vol is calendar noise.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($120-137):** The funding-cost glide path drives NII higher independent of rate direction; AOCI recovers and frees the 6.8% leverage constraint, accelerating the $12.1B remaining buyback. Core net new assets compound at $130B+/quarter, margin balances and trading stay elevated, and the multiple re-rates toward the peer 19x on a rising EPS base. 17-19x on $7.22 next-year EPS = $123-137. Forge/crypto/AI add optionality.

**Base Case (~$118):** NIM continues expanding on funding-cost improvement, EPS reaches the $7.22 next-year consensus, and the multiple re-rates partway toward peers (~16.5x). Capital return ramps as AOCI fills. ~$118, in line with the analyst median.

**Bear Case ($75-78):** A renewed inflation impulse forces a faster-than-expected rate rise, reigniting cash-sorting and wholesale-funding reliance; AOCI losses deepen, tightening the capital constraint and stalling buybacks; the margin-loan book takes credit stress in a market dislocation. This requires a macro reversal, not a fundamental break in the franchise.

The asymmetry is favorable: the low analyst target ($84) sits near the current price, the bear case ($75-78) requires a macro tail, and the base/bull cases imply 33-54% upside. The thesis is not rate-dependent on the upside, which removes the primary reason a balanced rating had previously been applied.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target:** $118 over 12 months — ~16.5x the $7.22 next-year EPS, partway re-rated toward the 19.2x peer median, supported by the $115.85 analyst mean.
- **Bull case target:** $123-137 — 17-19x on next-year EPS if the multiple converges fully to peers as AOCI clears and buybacks accelerate.
- **Bear case target:** $75-78 (~12-15% downside) — requires a renewed inflation impulse and faster rate rise.
- **Upside/downside from current ($88.70):** +33% to base, +39-54% to bull, -12-15% to bear.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with quarterly earnings (next ~July 2026) and the Fed path as the key checkpoints.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. NIM contracts QoQ for two consecutive quarters absent a rate cut, signaling the funding-cost tailwind is exhausted.
2. AOCI-inclusive Tier-1 leverage falls below 6.75% and management pauses buybacks.
3. Core net new assets fall below ~$80B/quarter or material deposit outflows resume, signaling renewed cash-sorting.

## Conclusion

Schwab is a self-healing balance-sheet recovery trading at a discount to lower-quality peers. The earnings engine — funding-cost-driven NIM expansion — does not require rising rates to persist, which makes the upside skew positive rather than balanced. Forward P/E against the peer median for a 40% ROTCE franchise, estimate revisions positive across every horizon, accelerating capital return, and a CEO open-market purchase form a coherent inflection-plus-value cluster. The lone binding constraint, the AOCI-inclusive Tier-1 leverage ratio, is directionally self-resolving as securities roll off and AOCI recovers. With the most bearish analyst target near current price and the mean implying ~31% upside, the risk/reward supports a **BUY**. The principal risk is a macro reversal in the rate path, which is a tail outcome rather than a thesis break.
