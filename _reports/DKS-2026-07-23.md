**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Dick's Sporting Goods is midway through a corporate transformation. The September 2025 acquisition of Foot Locker roughly doubled the store fleet, added ~$8B in annualized sales, extended the company into ~20 countries, and pulled consolidated gross margin down 298 bps to 32.9% in FY2025. Underneath that dilution, the core DICK'S business is performing well: organic comps ran +4.5% in FY25 and accelerated to +6.0% in Q1 FY26, and the DICK'S segment expanded gross margin +44 bps while growing segment profit +4.7% to $1.57B. The investment question is narrow and binary: can DKS fix Foot Locker's decade-long decline without impairing the core business?

The early evidence on the turnaround is better than skeptics expected. In its first full quarter under DKS ownership, Foot Locker posted a $17.5M segment profit on $1.8B of sales and pro-forma comps of +0.6% — its first positive comp since late 2024, achieved in a seasonally weak quarter that excludes back-to-school. That is a genuine positive surprise against a $60M loss in the stub period. The counterweights are real. Q1 reported EPS of $3.54 was inflated by roughly $1.45/share of non-recurring settlement income (a $150M interchange-fee settlement plus a $24.5M lease-termination gain); excluding those, underlying earnings fell to ~$2.90 from $3.37 a year earlier. Management trimmed full-year EPS guidance to $13.27–14.27, and consolidated operating margin compressed to ~7.3% from double-digit levels.

We maintain a HOLD. The stock trades at ~21x trailing EPS of $9.97 (which is itself depressed by $307M of acquisition charges), and ~15x consensus FY26 EPS of $14.29. Estimates are stable rather than rising, prior appreciation has reversed toward the middle of the 52-week range, and the defining catalyst — back-to-school proof of the Foot Locker thesis — is one to two quarters out. This is our fifth consecutive HOLD, and we see nothing in the current data that resolves the uncertainty in either direction. We would upgrade on confirmed Foot Locker segment-profit delivery within the $110–150M guide plus positive estimate revisions; we would downgrade on Foot Locker missing that guide or on core DICK'S comps decelerating below 3%.

## Company Overview

DKS now operates two reportable segments. The **DICK'S Business** (~$14.1B FY25 sales) comprises 640 core stores plus experiential formats (36 House of Sport locations and 44 Field House stores) and specialty banners (Golf Galaxy, Going Going Gone!, Public Lands), totaling 888 doors. It also owns GameChanger, a youth-sports live-streaming and scorekeeping SaaS platform, and a growing DICK'S Media Network retail-advertising business. The **Foot Locker Business** (~$3.1B partial-year FY25 sales; ~$8B annualized) spans Foot Locker, Kids Foot Locker, Champs Sports, WSS, and atmos across roughly 2,483 owned plus 256 licensed stores.

The strategic logic is consolidation of sneaker retail and increased leverage with brand partners as scale grows. Nike represents ~31% of merchandise purchases across the combined entity, so the deal magnifies an existing dependency rather than diversifying it. Management frames the thesis around the "convergence of sport and culture" — sneakers as fashion, women's sports growth, and a dense 2026–2028 sports calendar (FIFA World Cup, Olympics).

The company sits in Consumer Discretionary / Specialty Stores, a group we approach with caution. Sector-relative alpha in Consumer Discretionary has been structurally negative, so a BUY here requires company-specific outperformance evidence that clears a higher bar than a favorable narrative.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue scale up, profitability down.** FY2025 revenue rose 28.1% to $17.22B from $13.44B, driven by ~4.5 months of Foot Locker ($3.1B) layered on +4.5% DICK'S organic comps. Operating income fell to $1,096M from $1,474M, and operating margin dropped from ~11.0% to 6.4%. Net income declined ~27% to $849M ($9.97 diluted EPS) from $1.17B ($14.05). The decline reflects $307M of net acquisition-related charges (a $218M inventory write-down, $164M merger/integration costs, bridge financing), a $60M Foot Locker net loss in the stub period, and dilution from 9.6M newly issued shares.

The consolidated margin compression is almost entirely a mix effect. Foot Locker structurally carries gross margins of ~24–28% versus DICK'S ~36%, and the stub period included inventory liquidation. Isolate the core and the picture is healthy: the DICK'S segment grew segment profit +4.7% to $1.57B at an 11.1% margin.

**Q1 FY2026 confirms the two-speed dynamic.** Net sales rose 62.7% to $5.16B, broad-based across footwear, apparel, team sports, golf, licensed, and trading cards. Foot Locker turned a 2.9% segment margin. Reported EPS overstates run-rate earnings power right now: headline EPS was flattered by the settlement gains, while underlying non-GAAP EPS fell to ~$2.90 from $3.37. This is the single most important number in the report.

**Balance sheet and cash flow.** The screen-level D/E of 3.14 and Altman Z-Score of 2.0 look alarming but are artifacts of ~$5.9B of capitalized operating leases (from doubling the store fleet) and acquisition goodwill/intangibles. Actual funded debt is modest: $1.9B of senior notes (2029/2032/2052), investment-grade rated Baa2/BBB, with a $2.0B undrawn revolver, a new $500M undrawn commercial-paper program, and ~$1.0B cash. Current ratio is 1.53. This is not a distressed credit. FCF compressed to $400M in FY25 as gross capex surged to $1.14B, and management guides ~$1.4–1.5B capex in FY26 (House of Sport expansion, the Fort Worth DC, Foot Locker "Fast Break" remodels), which will keep FCF conversion below net income through the investment phase.

**Earnings track record.** Three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with the beats generally small (0.4%, 1.5%) except the +19.1% March quarter, and the most recent quarter a narrow -1.0% miss. The beats have not been driven by clean operating outperformance — the largest were flattered by one-time items — which argues for calibrated rather than high confidence in forward consensus.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two independent engines running at different speeds. The core DICK'S business is a mid-single-digit comp compounder with margin expansion — a durable, high-quality base. FY26 guidance for the DICK'S segment is +2.5–4.0% comps and $1.60–1.68B segment profit, which we view as achievable given the Q1 print and the experiential-format traffic driver.

Foot Locker is the variable. Guidance calls for pro-forma comps of +1.5–3.0% and segment profit of $110–150M, heavily second-half weighted, with back-to-school as the decisive test. The Q1 leading indicators are encouraging, but the segment carries a poor recent record — international comps fell 8.1% in FY25 — and the turnaround depends on the "Fast Break" store overhaul (100 stores remodeled in Q1, targeting 250 by back-to-school). Cost synergies of only $100–125M are modest relative to a $2.5B deal, so the thesis rests on a revenue and margin turnaround rather than cost-out. That is a harder thing to underwrite.

Estimate revisions are the key tell, and they are neutral. Current-year EPS consensus of $14.29 is essentially flat over 30 and 90 days; next-year $16.34 has crept up +1.2% over 90 days. There is no evidence of the +30% revision velocity that would signal a fundamental change the market is underestimating. Management cut the full-year EPS guide (from $13.70–14.70) even while raising the low end of comp guidance, which tells you Foot Locker investment is pressuring the P&L faster than sales recovery is offsetting it. The tailwinds management leans on — the 2026 World Cup, 2028 Olympics, women's sports — are real but diffuse and difficult to size.

Full-year FY26 guidance targets total net sales of $22.1–22.4B. That is genuine top-line growth, but it is bought with margin dilution and heavy capex, so the earnings growth trajectory is muted until Foot Locker's margin recovers.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$210, DKS trades on trailing earnings that are depressed net of acquisition charges, so the ~15x forward figure is the relevant anchor. Against peers, the picture is mixed. The Specialty Stores peer median P/E is 12.9 (SIG 12.9, ASO 9.2, SBH 7.5, with FIVE at 25.5 and EYE at 31.8 as outliers). On a forward basis, DKS at ~15x sits modestly above the sneaker/big-box comps ASO (9.2) and SIG (12.9), which is defensible given its higher-quality core, experiential moat, and investment-grade balance sheet, but it is not cheap enough to be an obvious value.

We resist rewarding the stock for looking "cheap" on trailing EPS. A compressed-looking multiple on inflated headline earnings is a value-trap setup, not a mispricing. The bull case requires the Foot Locker turnaround to convert dilution into accretion, which would push FY27 earnings power meaningfully higher, but that is a forward option, and the estimate-revision data does not yet confirm it.

Analyst targets are wide: low $169, mean $250, median $262, high $300. On a stock with rising estimates and a sub-15x forward multiple, we would treat consensus targets as floors. Here, with estimates flat rather than rising, the wide dispersion ($169 to $300) more accurately reflects genuine uncertainty about the Foot Locker outcome than a stale, conservative consensus. We do not treat the $250 mean as a reliable base case; it prices a successful turnaround that remains unproven.

There is one piece of un-modeled optionality: the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling against IEEPA tariffs, which may generate tariff refunds to DKS. These are treated as an unrecognized gain contingency and excluded from guidance, so any recovery would be upside not in the numbers.

Net: fair value, not compelling value. The valuation score reflects a balanced setup where the multiple is reasonable but the earnings quality is currently low and the catalyst is unproven.

## Competitive Landscape

DKS competes in a highly fragmented market against big-box (Academy Sports, Hibbett/Big 5), mass merchants, department stores, and, increasingly, brand DTC channels like Nike.com. The core DICK'S competitive position is improving: it is gaining share through differentiated experiential formats (House of Sport, Field House), a growing vertical/private-brand mix (~13% of DICK'S sales at higher margins), and deep vendor relationships. Segment margin expansion is the cleanest evidence that its moat is intact and widening in the core.

The Foot Locker addition is a bet that scale deepens brand-partner leverage in sneaker retail. That logic is plausible but unproven, and it comes with new vulnerabilities: heavy mall-based real estate exposure (a first for a company built on off-mall big-box formats), declining mall traffic, and the rationalization of underperforming doors (62 Foot Locker closures in Q1 FY26 alone; WSS shrinking from 143 to ~100 stores). Jefferies has noted that competitors Academy and Hibbett could absorb the capacity DKS is shedding, meaning the downsizing is a share give-back in parts of the portfolio even as the core gains.

The Nike concentration (no long-term contracts) is a shared, magnified risk post-merger. Any shift in Nike's allocation strategy or an acceleration of its DTC push would hit both segments simultaneously.

On balance, the competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving in the core and unresolved in Foot Locker, which nets to a neutral score. Trajectory, not level, drives our assessment: a market leader consolidating a declining franchise is not the same as an unambiguously improving position.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are material and only partially priced.

1. **Foot Locker integration/turnaround execution** is the central risk. Total acquisition-related charges are guided at $500–750M ($390M taken in FY25, ~$200M expected FY26). The turnaround depends on the Fast Break overhaul and returning the segment to full-year profitability. A miss versus the segment-profit guide would directly hit the thesis.

2. **Nike/supplier concentration** with no long-term contracts is a binary-adjacent risk shared across both segments.

3. **Margin dilution and mall exposure** are new structural features. Foot Locker's lower margins and mall-heavy footprint expose DKS to a secular headwind it did not previously carry.

4. **New international/FX exposure** (euro, GBP, CAD, AUD, JPY) plus foreign-jurisdiction losses where tax benefits cannot be recognized raised the effective tax rate to 28.3% in Q1 FY26.

5. **Securities litigation.** The shareholder class action tied to 2022–2023 inventory/shrink disclosures survived a motion to dismiss in part (April 2026), with related derivative suits and Section 220 demands ongoing. Legally material to monitor.

6. **Governance discount.** Stack-family Class B shares control a majority of combined voting power (23.57M Class B vs. ~65M common), effectively takeover-proofing the company and eliminating any takeover-premium optionality for minority holders. This is a permanent, appropriate valuation drag.

7. **Earnings-quality risk.** Reported EPS is currently flattered by non-recurring items; the market could re-rate downward as run-rate earnings become visible without the settlement tailwinds.

The offsets: ample liquidity, investment-grade credit, strong core-segment cash generation, and un-modeled tariff-refund upside. The concentration of binary events (Foot Locker back-to-school, Nike allocation, ongoing litigation) with asymmetric downside justifies a below-average risk score.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is mixed and does not strongly confirm either direction. Near-term ATM IV of 39.0% is modestly rich versus 35.5% historical vol (1st percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in contango (near-term 39.0% below long-term 43.1%) — a normal near-term structure with rising uncertainty priced further out, consistent with the back-to-school catalyst sitting a couple of quarters away. The put/call OI ratio of 2.94 (up from 2.35 six weeks ago) and elevated 14.4% short interest signal meaningful institutional bearish positioning, though the put/call volume ratio of 0.29 shows no acute near-term put buying. IV has fallen 6.7 points since early June as the stock declined 5.7%, indicating resolved event uncertainty rather than a building catalyst. The 3-month expiry shows a -4.0% put skew, consistent with hedging into the back-to-school print. Net read: the market is positioned cautiously but not panicked, aligning with our balanced HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($262–300)
The Foot Locker turnaround compounds off its Q1 traction. Back-to-school (Q2/Q3 FY26) confirms the segment can hit or beat the profit guide, pro-forma comps sustain +1.5–3.0%, and the Fast Break remodels drive traffic. The core DICK'S business continues +4–6% comps with margin expansion. FY27 earnings power inflects as Foot Locker moves from dilutive to accretive, estimates revise upward, and the multiple re-rates toward the mean/high analyst targets. Tariff refunds add un-modeled upside. At $300, the market prices a fully successful integration on ~18x FY27 EPS approaching $17.

### Bear Case ($169–180)
Foot Locker's Q1 profit proves to be an early, favorable read that fades as back-to-school disappoints, international comps stay negative, and Fast Break remodels underdeliver. Consolidated margins remain compressed, additional acquisition/impairment charges accrue against the shed doors, and Nike shifts allocation or accelerates DTC, pressuring both segments. Estimates roll over, the stock de-rates toward the low analyst target and peer big-box multiples (ASO ~9x). At $169, the market treats DKS as a low-margin, mall-exposed retailer that overpaid for a declining franchise.

### Base Case (~$215–230)
The core stays healthy at mid-single-digit comps; Foot Locker delivers within but not above the guide; earnings quality normalizes and estimates hold roughly flat. The stock oscillates in the mid-52-week range as the market waits for back-to-school proof. This is where we sit today, with balanced risk/reward and no clean catalyst to break the range in the near term.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The decisive data point is back-to-school (Q2 FY26 report expected late August 2026, and Q3 FY26 in late November 2026). These quarters test whether Foot Locker's Q1 traction was real. The FY26 EPS guide and the Foot Locker segment-profit guide are the specific benchmarks to check.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Foot Locker delivers segment profit within or above the $110–150M guide at the back-to-school prints, with pro-forma comps sustaining ≥1.5%.
- Consensus FY27 EPS estimates begin revising upward (>5% over 90 days), signaling the accretion inflection.
- Consolidated operating margin recovers above ~8% on a clean, non-settlement-flattered basis.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Foot Locker misses the segment-profit guide or reverts to negative pro-forma comps.
- Core DICK'S comps decelerate below 3%, signaling the core is being impaired.
- Additional material acquisition/impairment charges beyond the ~$200M FY26 guide, or a Nike allocation shift that pressures both segments.
- FY26/FY27 estimate revisions turn decisively negative (>5% cuts over 90 days).

## Conclusion

DKS is a well-managed core retailer executing a large, unproven acquisition. The DICK'S business is a high-quality mid-single-digit comp compounder with expanding margins and an improving experiential moat, and that part of the story is working. The Foot Locker turnaround is off to a better-than-feared start, but it is one favorable quarter into a multi-year revenue-and-margin fix that carries real execution, concentration, mall-exposure, and governance risks. Reported earnings are currently flattered by one-time settlement income, estimates are stable rather than rising, and the stock trades at a fair (not cheap) ~15x forward EPS in the middle of its 52-week range.

We reiterate **HOLD** with an uncertain rationale. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and we hold the higher bar deliberately: the setup is genuinely balanced, the catalyst that would resolve it (back-to-school) is one to two quarters out, and neither the fundamentals nor the options and short-interest positioning give a decisive edge. We would rather see the Foot Locker thesis proven in the P&L before paying up, and we would not short a healthy core business generating $1.5B+ of operating cash flow with an investment-grade balance sheet. The range-bound risk/reward does not reward front-running the back-to-school prints.
