## Executive Summary

Huntington Ingalls Industries occupies a unique position in the U.S. defense industrial base as the sole builder of nuclear aircraft carriers and one of two yards capable of constructing nuclear submarines. The $53.1B backlog (4.3x trailing revenue) provides multi-year visibility that few defense contractors can match. The margin recovery from the FY2024 trough is underway: net cumulative catch-up adjustments improved from -$126M in FY2024 to -$28M in FY2025, and Q4 2025 segment margin reached 5.6%, the best quarter in two years.

The problem is price. HII has rallied 58% from its May 2025 trough near $215 to the current $363, compressing the remaining upside to consensus targets (mean $407, median $419) to roughly 12-15%. At ~24x trailing earnings, the stock trades at a meaningful premium to the defense peer median of ~17-18x for peers with comparable margin profiles, and the premium requires sustained execution on the margin recovery that management has promised but not yet fully delivered. Virginia-class submarine performance, unquantified welding remediation costs, and an incoming capex ramp to 4-5% of revenue all create downside scenarios that the current valuation does not adequately buffer against.

HII is a hold for investors who believe the margin expansion from ~5.3% operating margin today toward management's 7-8% medium-term target will materialize, converting the backlog into substantially higher earnings. Conviction to upgrade to BUY requires either a 10-15% price pullback or demonstrable progress on Newport News margins through the next two quarters.

## Company Overview

Huntington Ingalls Industries is America's largest military shipbuilder, operating through three segments. Newport News Shipbuilding ($6.5B, 52% of FY2025 revenue) is the sole U.S. yard for nuclear aircraft carriers and a critical partner on Virginia-class and Columbia-class submarine programs. Ingalls Shipbuilding ($3.1B, 25%) builds destroyers, amphibious ships, and the newly awarded FF(X) frigate. Mission Technologies ($3.0B, 24%) provides C5ISR, cyber, unmanned systems, and fleet sustainment services.

The competitive structure is effectively a monopoly (carriers) and duopoly (submarines, destroyers) created by decades of accumulated nuclear shipbuilding expertise, specialized facilities, security clearances, and regulatory approvals. The customer is almost exclusively the U.S. Department of Defense and Navy.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY2025 revenue of $12.5B grew 8.2% YoY, with shipbuilding revenues up 9.7% to $9.6B. The $53.1B backlog at year-end (up from $48.7B) with a 1.35x book-to-bill ratio provides exceptional forward visibility. FY2026 guidance implies ~$12.7-13.1B in total revenue, representing low-to-mid single digit growth.

**Margins — the critical variable:** Operating margins remain compressed at 5.3% in FY2025, reflecting legacy contracts bid on pre-COVID cost assumptions. The trajectory is improving but remains far from management's medium-term targets. Newport News margins recovered from 4.1% (FY2024) to 5.1% (FY2025) but are still below the 6.2% achieved in FY2023. Ingalls margins have plateaued at 7.6% for two consecutive years, down from 13.2% in FY2023. Mission Technologies is the bright spot at 5.0% operating margin, up from 3.7% two years ago.

Management guides FY2026 shipbuilding operating margin to 5.5-6.5%, implying ~50-150bps of improvement. The math on earnings growth depends heavily on where margins land within this range: at 5.5%, EPS stays approximately flat; at 6.5%, EPS could approach $20+.

**Cash flow:** FY2025 free cash flow of $800M recovered sharply from the $40M aberration in FY2024, demonstrating the underlying cash generation capacity. FY2026 guidance of $500-600M FCF reflects a pull-forward in Q4 2025 and an accelerating capex program (4-5% of revenue, up from 3.2%). At the midpoint of FCF guidance ($550M), the FCF yield on the current $14.3B market cap is ~3.8%.

**Balance sheet:** Long-term debt of $2.7B against total equity of approximately $1.8B produces a debt-to-equity ratio of 1.51x. Total liquidity of $2.5B ($774M cash + $1.7B undrawn revolver) is adequate. The pension plan is 126% funded on a FAS basis, generating a positive $155M FAS/CAS adjustment in FY2025 that grows to an estimated $187M by FY2030, a meaningful non-operating earnings contributor.

**Earnings surprise track record:** HII has beaten consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters, with surprise magnitudes of +28.8%, +16.6%, +8.5%, and +3.1%. The declining surprise magnitude matters: the most recent beat of 3.1% is thin, and current quarter estimates have been revised down 5.3% over the past 90 days.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story rests on three pillars:

**Backlog conversion:** ~21% of the backlog is expected to convert in FY2026, providing a revenue floor of ~$11B+ from committed contracts alone. The FF(X) frigate program ($282.9M initial award, potential 20+ ship program) and continued Virginia-class and Columbia-class submarine work add further visibility. Management's $16B+ revenue target by 2030 implies a ~6% CAGR, which is credible given the backlog trajectory.

**Margin expansion:** Revenue growth of 4-6% combined with margin expansion from 5.3% to the 7-8% range would produce EPS growth of 15-20% annually. The mechanism is straightforward: legacy contracts with unfavorable economics are progressively completing, and new contracts reflect current cost realities. Throughput improvements (14% in FY2025, targeting 15% in FY2026) amplify this by spreading fixed costs across more earned hours.

**Mission Technologies growth:** The services segment is growing at mid-single digits with expanding margins and higher returns on capital than shipbuilding. Management targets continued margin expansion toward 5%+ operating margin with EBITDA margins approaching 9-10%.

Consensus estimates reflect this story: $17.40 for FY2026 (current year) and $20.35 for FY2027, implying 17% EPS growth. Full-year and next-year estimates are essentially stable (within 1-2% over 90 days). The estimate trajectory is not deteriorating, but it is not accelerating either.

## Valuation Assessment

At $363, HII trades at approximately 24x trailing EPS of $15.39, 20.9x FY2026 consensus of $17.40, and 17.8x FY2027 consensus of $20.35. The dividend yield is 1.5%.

Against the peer set, HII's 24x trailing P/E compares to Textron at 17.5x and the defense prime median of approximately 18-20x. L3Harris at 37.7x and TransDigm at 36.8x trade at significant premiums, but both have substantially higher margins (L3Harris at ~13% operating, TransDigm at ~45% EBITDA). HII's sub-6% operating margin profile warrants a discount to higher-margin defense peers.

The valuation question reduces to: what margin level does 24x trailing earnings imply? If margins remain at 5.3%, the stock is fully valued. If margins reach the midpoint of FY2026 guidance (6.0%), EPS could approach $18-19 and the forward multiple compresses to ~19-20x, which is reasonable for the sector. If margins reach 7%+ on a sustainable basis, the stock has meaningful upside to $450+.

The consensus mean target of $407 (12% upside) and median of $419 (15% upside) are achievable if Q1-Q2 2026 results demonstrate continued margin improvement. The analyst distribution is telling: 4 Buy, 7 Hold, 1 Strong Sell. This is a cautious consensus, consistent with the view that the stock has priced in a significant portion of the recovery.

The stock hit $460 on March 2, 2026 (the 52-week high) and has since pulled back 21%, suggesting the market briefly priced in the full margin recovery scenario and then reassessed.

## Competitive Landscape

HII's competitive position is among the strongest of any public company in any industry. The sole-source position on nuclear aircraft carriers and the duopoly on nuclear submarines create barriers to entry measured in decades and billions of dollars. No competitor can build an aircraft carrier, and the only submarine competitor (General Dynamics Electric Boat) is a capacity-constrained partner, not a price competitor.

The FF(X) frigate award strengthens the position further. By leveraging the proven National Security Cutter design that Ingalls already builds, HII enters a potentially 20+ ship program with low technical risk and production learning curve advantages.

The structural challenge is that monopoly/duopoly positions in defense shipbuilding come with corresponding monopsony buyer power. The Navy is the sole customer and sets the terms of competition. Returns on capital in this industry are structurally lower than in defense electronics or aftermarket businesses precisely because the customer has perfect information about costs and significant contractual power.

The 14% throughput improvement in FY2025 and the W International acquisition suggest management is actively investing in capacity and efficiency, which would strengthen the competitive position by reducing delivery timelines and improving cost performance on future contracts.

## Risk Assessment

**Execution risk on Virginia-class submarines:** This program has generated -$154M and -$64M in cumulative catch-up adjustments at Newport News over the past two years. Any further deterioration would directly impair the margin recovery thesis. Virginia-class performance is the single most important variable for FY2026-2027 earnings.

**Welding quality remediation (unquantified):** The Q3 2024 disclosure of welding noncompliance at Newport News remains open. Management states it "cannot at this time predict or reasonably estimate the ultimate outcome." Electric Boat faced a similar issue that resulted in significant costs. Until this is quantified and resolved, it represents a potential material charge.

**Near-term estimate weakness:** While full-year estimates are stable, the near-term softness combined with management's warning that Q1 will show -$600M FCF outflow creates a vulnerable setup for the next earnings report.

**Capex acceleration compresses FCF:** FY2026 capex at 4-5% of revenue (~$500-600M) is a meaningful step-up from the 3.2% ($402M) in FY2025. Combined with Q1 working capital timing, guided FCF of $500-600M represents a ~25-38% decline from FY2025.

**Labor and CBA negotiations:** Five Ingalls collective bargaining agreements expire in March 2026. Approximately 45% of the workforce is unionized. In the current labor market with persistent skilled worker shortages, negotiations could result in meaningful cost increases.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing significant near-term uncertainty. ATM IV of 59.0% is nearly double the 1-year historical volatility of 32.2%, representing a 26.9pp premium among the richest readings in the defense sector. The term structure is in steep backwardation (59% near-term vs. 41% longer-dated), indicating the market expects a near-term catalyst or volatility event. Since the prior snapshot on April 10, IV has surged 24.8pp while the stock declined 7.9%. Put/call OI has shifted from 0.06 to 0.28, still call-heavy but with increasing put participation. The implied move of ±9.4% on the nearest expiry (~$34 move) suggests the market is pricing in a potential re-rating event. Short interest at 3.0% of float with 2.1 days to cover is low and does not create squeeze risk. The elevated IV makes protective put purchases expensive, but the backwardation suggests this richness is concentrated in the very near-term and may dissipate after the next earnings catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($450-470, +24-29%)
Margin recovery accelerates through FY2026 as legacy contracts roll off. Newport News achieves 6%+ operating margin, Ingalls margins recover toward 9-10% as frigate production ramps, and Mission Technologies sustains 5%+ margins. FY2027 EPS reaches $21-22. The defense spending environment remains supportive ($900.6B NDAA), with dual-theater conflict spending providing incremental demand for naval assets. The welding issue resolves with modest remediation costs. Stock re-rates to 22x forward earnings on improved execution.

### Bear Case ($280-300, -23-18%)
Virginia-class submarine margins deteriorate further. Welding remediation at Newport News results in a $200M+ charge. Ingalls margins remain stuck at 7.6% as frigate startup costs offset destroyer efficiencies. FY2026 EPS comes in at $15-16, below consensus. The stock de-rates to 18x forward earnings, reflecting persistent execution disappointment.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $310-320 range (~15% decline), providing adequate margin of safety at ~18x FY2026 consensus
2. Newport News segment operating margin exceeds 6.0% for a full quarter, confirmed over two consecutive quarters
3. Welding remediation costs are quantified at <$100M total

**Downgrade triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. Virginia-class catch-up adjustments exceed -$100M in any single quarter
2. Welding remediation costs are quantified at >$300M
3. FY2026 guidance for shipbuilding operating margin reduced below 5.0%

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (expected May 2026 for Q1 FY2026 results). Q1 will provide data on early progress toward FY2026 margin targets given the warned -$600M FCF outflow and the opportunity to assess margin trajectory. The five Ingalls CBA expirations in March 2026 should also be resolved by then, providing visibility into labor cost trajectory.

## Conclusion

HII's competitive position is extraordinary, and the backlog provides a degree of revenue visibility rare in any industry. The margin recovery thesis is credible and, if management delivers on the 5.5-6.5% shipbuilding margin guide for FY2026, the stock has upside to $410-420. However, after a 58% rally from May 2025 lows, the current price of $363 already embeds substantial recovery expectations. Declining near-term estimate revisions, the unquantified welding risk, and the options market's pricing of near-term stress all argue for patience. The right entry price is $310-320, not $363. HOLD, pending evidence of execution in the next two quarters.
