**Research Date: June 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CF Industries is the world's largest ammonia producer and the dominant North American nitrogen manufacturer, operating a low-cost footprint anchored to cheap North American natural gas. The company is generating the strongest earnings in years on a nitrogen pricing surge driven by a Middle East supply disruption: Q1 2026 net sales rose 19% YoY to $1,986M almost entirely on a 28% jump in average selling price ($424/ton vs $332), while volumes fell 6%. The balance sheet is a genuine strength: $2.04B cash, ~$1.2B net debt against multi-billion EBITDA potential, no near-term maturity wall, and a $1.71B remaining buyback authorization that management has deliberately throttled as the stock rallied. The trailing P/E of ~10.2x looks cheap against a sector median near 24x.

The difficulty is that the cheap multiple is a function of peak-cycle earnings, and those earnings rest on a geopolitical premium that management openly calls "currently unknown" in duration. The Strait of Hormuz disruption removed roughly 25-30% of traded ammonia and 35-40% of traded urea from the market, and Qatari LNG curtailment idled the marginal high-cost producers CF competes against. When that supply normalizes, nitrogen prices and CF's margins fall, and the low P/E re-rates the wrong way. Q1's headline was further flattered by approximately $219M of non-operating benefits: a $170M Orica litigation settlement, $25M of Yazoo insurance recovery, and $24M of 45Q tax credits, so run-rate operating earnings are below what the EPS print implies.

Two developments since the prior reports tilt the risk profile more negative: the FTC opened a broad fertilizer-pricing antitrust probe in late May 2026, and a Sherman Act class action consolidated through the JPML on May 28 names CF alongside the major fertilizer producers. A White House executive order has explicitly targeted lowering fertilizer costs for farmers. CF is earning its best margins in years as regulatory scrutiny peaks. We rate CF a HOLD. The bullish case (durability of the supply shock, fortress balance sheet, low-carbon ammonia optionality) and the bearish case (mean-reversion, political risk, earnings-quality overstatement) are roughly balanced, and the dominant variable, Hormuz reopening, is a binary event on which we hold no informational edge over the market.

## Company Overview

CF Industries Holdings produces anhydrous ammonia via the Haber-Bosch process (natural gas to hydrogen, combined with atmospheric nitrogen) and upgrades it into granular urea, UAN, ammonium nitrate (AN), and industrial products including diesel exhaust fluid. The asset base comprises six U.S. complexes (including Donaldsonville, Louisiana, the largest ammonia complex in the world), two Canadian complexes, one U.K. facility, and a 50% Trinidad JV. The structural competitive advantage is cost leadership: North American gas runs materially cheaper than the imported LNG that European and Asian producers depend on, which widens CF's margin when global marginal producers are curtailed.

Q1 2026 segment detail shows the pricing-driven nature of the quarter:
- Ammonia: $627M net sales, 36.2% gross margin
- Granular Urea: $590M, 43.2%
- UAN: $583M, 42.9%
- AN: $58M, **(19.0)%** — a loss driven by the idled Yazoo City complex
- Other: $128M, 19.5%

The emerging strategic pillar is low-carbon ammonia via carbon capture and sequestration. The Donaldsonville CCS project came online in July 2025, enabling up to ~1.9M tons/year of low-carbon ammonia and generating 45Q tax credits ($24M in Q1). The Blue Point JV (CF 40%, JERA 35%, Mitsui 25%) is a ~$3.7B greenfield ATR ammonia plant targeted for 2029, aimed at policy-driven premium demand from Japanese power and EU carbon-border compliance.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 results.** The 28% price increase drove the revenue gain; volume fell to 4.7M tons on the Yazoo idle and product-mix shift. Gross margin expanded to $746M / 37.6% from $572M / 34.4%, with pricing adding $401M, partially offset by a 24% rise in natural gas cost ($4.57/MMBtu, a $76M drag) and lower volume ($34M). Net earnings to common rose 97% to $615M; diluted EPS of $3.98 was up 115%, aided by an 8% lower share count. Adjusted EBITDA was $983M.

**Earnings quality.** The headline overstates the run-rate. Roughly $219M of pre-tax benefits in Q1 were non-operating or one-time, with the Orica settlement received April 30. The underlying operating improvement (gross margin up ~30%) is real, but investors anchoring to the $3.98 print should discount it for these items.

**Balance sheet.** This is the clearest strength. Cash of $2.04B against total debt of $3.25B (all unsecured senior notes laddered 2034–2044 at ~5%) leaves net debt near $1.2B, modest for the cash generation. The $750M revolver is fully undrawn. Management termed out maturities by issuing $1B of 5.30% notes due 2035 in November 2025 and prepaying $750M of 2026 secured notes in December. The Altman Z-Score "distress" reading flagged in some screens is a false positive: it is distorted by the consolidation of noncontrolling-interest-heavy structures (CFN, Blue Point) inflating the balance sheet relative to attributable EBIT, not genuine solvency stress. With an investment-grade profile and no near-term wall, refinancing risk is negligible.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow was $496M in Q1, down from $586M on a working-capital build, lower customer advances, and a ~$95M Canadian tax deposit. Buybacks fell from $278M in Q4 2025 to just $15M in Q1 2026 as the stock rallied; management does not view ~$110+ as cheap. The $2B authorization runs through 2029. The dividend is flat at $0.50/quarter (~1.8% yield), well covered. 2026 capex is guided to ~$1.3B consolidated, of which ~$600M is Blue Point (60% funded by JV partners).

**Earnings surprise track record.** Three beats and one miss over the last four quarters (Q1 2026 +8.2%, Q4 2025 +24.9%, Q3 2025 +0.7%, Q2 2025 −10.3%). The pattern shows positive recent momentum but enough variability to caution against treating consensus as locked.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is dominated by commodity pricing, not volume or structural expansion. The entire revenue and earnings increase came from price. This is the central interpretive issue for the estimate revision data, which is striking on its face:

- Current quarter EPS: $5.91, up +48.5% (30d) and +173.6% (90d)
- Next quarter: $4.75, up +92.0% (30d) and +286.1% (90d)
- Current year: $18.23, up +18.0% (30d) and +115.2% (90d)
- Next year: $11.16, essentially flat at −0.2% (30d) but +66.0% (90d)

Revisions of this magnitude would normally constitute a powerful forward signal. Here they require careful framing. The near-term estimate explosion reflects analysts marking to the Middle East nitrogen premium, not a durable demand inflection. The most telling number is next-year EPS at $11.16, roughly 39% below the current-year $18.23 estimate, and flat over 30 days. Consensus is itself modeling normalization: analysts expect the premium to compress materially in 2027. CF is a cyclical at a peak, with earnings spiking on a transient shock, rather than an inflection stock with earnings sustainably recovering from a trough. The revision velocity is real but reflects a price spike with a finite tail, not a structural re-rating.

Durable growth optionality does exist in low-carbon ammonia. CBAM and Japan's Hydrogen Society Promotion Act are creating genuine policy-driven premium demand, and CF's CCS investments (Donaldsonville online, Blue Point targeting 2029) are first-mover positions. This is a multi-year story, not a 12-month catalyst, and Blue Point carries execution, tariff, and offtake-development risk. We score growth at 5.5: the headline momentum is offset by its transient nature and the consensus's own expectation of 2027 normalization.

## Valuation Assessment

CF trades at ~10.2x trailing earnings and ~10.2x forward (on the current-year $18.23 estimate, the multiple is closer to 6x; on the next-year $11.16 estimate, ~9.9x). Against a Materials peer median P/E of 24.2x, the stock looks cheap. The peer set is heterogeneous and not a clean comp: IP and ALB show negative earnings, RGLD is a royalty company, AMCR and IFF are packaging/flavors names, so the median is not a reliable anchor for a nitrogen producer at a cyclical peak.

The valuation question reduces to which earnings number is the right denominator. On peak 2026 earnings, CF is genuinely cheap; on normalized mid-cycle earnings, it is fair to slightly rich. CF's mid-cycle EPS power, stripping out both the geopolitical premium and the one-time items, is plausibly in the $7-9 range, consistent with the analyst next-year estimate (which still embeds a partial premium). At ~$110 on $8-9 normalized EPS, that is ~12-14x mid-cycle, which is fair value for a high-quality, low-cost cyclical with a fortress balance sheet, not a bargain.

The market is not mispricing CF in an obvious way. The low trailing multiple correctly reflects that the earnings are not sustainable. Analyst price targets (low $100, mean ~$128, median $130, high $150) bracket a base case modestly above spot, but the sentiment trend is degrading, and at least one analyst has downgraded explicitly on the "Iran premium" being a double-edged sword. We do not treat these targets as floors here; this is a peak-cycle producer where the targets reflect the same premium-duration uncertainty the price does. Valuation scores 6.0: cheap on trailing optics, fair on normalized power, with the asymmetry roughly balanced.

## Competitive Landscape

CF's competitive position within nitrogen is strong and improving at the margin. The sector is bifurcated: nitrogen-focused producers (CF, Nutrien's N business) are capturing the Middle East supply shock, while phosphate/potash-heavy peers like Mosaic face headwinds. CF's North American gas-cost advantage is structurally amplified whenever global marginal producers (India, Pakistan, Bangladesh on imported LNG; Egypt, Iran, Russia) are curtailed. Scale (Donaldsonville is the world's largest ammonia complex) and an owned distribution network reinforce the moat.

The low-carbon ammonia pivot is a genuine forward differentiator. CF's CCS investments target policy-driven premium demand that most competitors are not positioned for. The trajectory of the core franchise is stable-to-improving, but the level of advantage is partly cyclical: the current outsized margin is borrowed from the supply shock, not earned competitively. We score competitive position 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the weakest element of the case and the reason for the cautious overall score.

1. **Binary commodity exposure to Hormuz.** The entire earnings premium rests on the durability of the Middle East supply disruption. The world view here treats Hormuz reopening as a high-probability path (multiple unverified diplomatic signals, transit-fee frameworks under discussion, demand softening in China and India capping the upper oil tail). A verified, sustained reopening would compress nitrogen prices and CF's margin sharply. This is a binary event on which we hold no edge; the clean exit signal is physical confirmation of sustained transit.

2. **Antitrust and political risk (new and escalating).** The FTC opened a broad fertilizer-pricing probe in late May 2026. A Sherman Act class action filed March 7 (N.D. Ill.) names CF alongside Mosaic, Nutrien, Koch, Yara, and Canpotex, with JPML consolidation hearing scheduled May 28. A December 2025 executive order created food-supply-chain task forces explicitly targeting fertilizer affordability. The administration's stated intent to lower farmer input costs is a direct threat to the pricing that is driving earnings. This is unusually live political risk for a commodity producer.

3. **Natural gas input.** Feedstock is ~39% of production cost. A Henry Hub spike compresses margin directly.

4. **Operational concentration.** The November 2025 Yazoo City incident idled the entire complex; AN won't resume until late Q4 2026 at the earliest.

5. **Leadership transition.** A CEO handoff (Will → Bohn, confirmed by the 10-Q signature), a CFO departure-and-replacement (Cameron departed January 12 → interim Hoker → Scribner elected May 5), and an unusually high cadence of Item 5.02 filings across 2025-2026 all cluster in a once-in-a-cycle earnings window. The appointments appear internal and orderly, but the churn warrants monitoring for strategic continuity.

6. **Blue Point and Trinidad.** Blue Point's build carries tariff exposure (~1/3 imported materials, Section 232 unaffected by the Feb 2026 IEEPA ruling) and offtake-development risk. The PLNL Trinidad gas contract runs only to January 1, 2027, with renegotiation and impairment risk on the $39M investment.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 53.8% against 41.8% one-year historical vol (rich by 12 points, 81st percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (53.8% near-term vs 45.7% at six months). The nearest expiry implies a ±4.6% move. Skew is modestly call-heavy (−3.1%), and the put/call OI ratio rose from 0.69 to 0.96 over four sessions as the stock fell 6.1% to ~$109.50, with ATM IV simultaneously easing from 60.4% to 53.8%. The reading is consistent with event-hedging into a near-term catalyst (broader macro vol and ongoing Hormuz headline risk) rather than a directional informed bet. Short interest is moderate at 7.0% of float with 2.8 days to cover, neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup. The elevated near-term IV argues against treating any current price as a clean entry; the binary nature of the underlying premium is being priced.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The Middle East supply disruption persists longer than the market expects, Qatari LNG recovery is a multi-year timeline, and CF captures peak nitrogen margins through 2026 and into 2027. The fortress balance sheet lets management repurchase aggressively if the stock pulls back, and the low-carbon ammonia franchise (Donaldsonville CCS, Blue Point) builds a durable premium-demand annuity. On sustained pricing, EPS holds well above $12-14 and the stock re-rates toward the analyst high of $150 (~12x on elevated but persistent earnings).

### Bear Case
Hormuz reopens and sustained commercial transit resumes; nitrogen prices and margins mean-revert within two to three quarters. The FTC probe and antitrust class action result in pricing remedies or settlements that cap the upside, and the political pressure to lower farmer costs bites. Normalized EPS settles near $7-9, the one-time Q1 benefits do not recur, and the stock de-rates toward $90 (~11-13x normalized), roughly 18-20% below current levels, with the downside contained near $90-100 by the balance sheet and the low-carbon optionality.

The two cases are roughly balanced, and the swing factor is a geopolitical event with no informational edge available. We rate the stock a hold.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** The bull and bear cases are close to symmetric, and the dominant variable is binary and exogenous.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Physical confirmation that the Hormuz disruption is structurally durable (extended Qatari LNG outage timeline, sustained supply curtailment) such that elevated nitrogen pricing extends through 2027, AND the stock pulls back toward $90-100 to improve the entry.
- FTC probe and antitrust litigation resolving without pricing remedies or material settlements.
- Evidence that low-carbon ammonia premium demand (Blue Point offtake firming, CBAM/Japan policy execution) is converting to contracted revenue ahead of the 2029 timeline.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Verified sustained Hormuz reopening (72+ hours uninterrupted commercial transit) confirming nitrogen-price normalization is underway.
- An adverse FTC or antitrust development imposing pricing constraints or material liability.
- A guidance cut, gas-cost spike compressing margins below mid-cycle, or further operational disruption beyond Yazoo.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, early August), or sooner on a material Hormuz development, the JPML/FTC proceedings, or a Yazoo rebuild update. The score trajectory has been declining (7.0 in May to 5.9 now), and the current data confirms rather than reverses that drift: the FTC probe is a genuine new negative and the next-year estimate signaling normalization validates the lower growth and valuation scores.

## Conclusion

CF Industries is a high-quality, low-cost producer with a fortress balance sheet earning the best margins in years on a transient geopolitical supply shock. The trailing multiple of ~10x is cheap only because the earnings are not sustainable; on normalized mid-cycle power the stock is fairly valued near $110. The constructive elements (the balance sheet, the buyback discipline at higher prices, the low-carbon ammonia optionality, and the structural North American gas advantage) are offset by a peak-cycle earnings base, the one-time benefits flattering Q1, an escalating FTC/antitrust overhang that directly targets the pricing tailwind, and a leadership transition during a critical window. The decisive variable is a Hormuz reopening on which neither we nor the market hold a durable edge. Hold; the clean exit is physical confirmation of sustained chokepoint transit, and the upgrade is a pullback toward $90-100 combined with evidence the supply premium is durable.
