**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Allegion is a global pure-play maker of security and access-control products (mechanical and electronic locks, exit devices, door closers, credentials, and access-control software), anchored by a dominant North American non-residential franchise (Schlage, Von Duprin, LCN). The business is high-quality on almost every static measure: 21.1% FY2025 operating margin, 31.1% ROE, ~$686M free cash flow on ~$4.07B revenue (16.9% FCF margin), light capex (~2.4% of revenue), and an investment-grade balance sheet with 90% fixed-rate debt. The Americas segment earns a 27.9% operating margin, a level that reflects a genuine specification moat: architects and spec-writers embed Allegion's brands into building designs, and fire/life-safety codes create switching friction.

The problem is what sits beneath the headline growth. FY2025 revenue grew 7.8%, but the composition was pricing +3.1%, acquisitions +3.1%, FX +0.6%, and volume only +1.0%. Q1 2026 was worse: revenue +9.7%, but volume was −2.0%, operating margin compressed 200 bps to 18.9%, and EPS fell to $1.59 from $1.71. International segment margin nearly halved to 3.7%. The pricing that props up the top line is tariff pass-through on a Mexico-heavy cost base (~20-25% of COGS), and it is actively suppressing price-elastic residential volume. The company is defending revenue with price and M&A while units shrink and integration costs eat into margins. This is the crux of the story and the reason the stock has lagged its capital-goods peer group by roughly 70 points of cumulative TSR over five years despite pristine absolute fundamentals.

The stock has de-rated about 25% from its February 2026 high of $183 to $137, and it now trades at roughly 15.6x current-year consensus EPS ($8.79) and ~14.3x next-year ($9.58), a discount to its own history and to industrial peers. That compression is warranted: it reflects a real slowdown in organic volume and margin, not a mispricing. With estimate revisions flat, no dated positive catalyst, and integration costs still flowing through, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate ALLE a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Allegion was spun out of Ingersoll Rand in December 2013 and is incorporated in Ireland. It operates through two segments:

- **Allegion Americas** — $3,218.8M revenue (79% of total), 27.9% segment operating margin in FY2025. This is the core value driver: a dominant North American non-residential specification business with real pricing power.
- **Allegion International** — $848.5M revenue (21%), 9.0% margin in FY2025, collapsing to 3.7% in Q1 2026. Europe/Asia/Oceania, structurally lower-margin and volume-sensitive, carrying $573.6M of accumulated historical goodwill impairment.

The portfolio spans 40+ brands with deep heritage: Schlage (cylindrical lock, 1920), Von Duprin (exit device, 1908), LCN (door closer, 1926), CISA, SimonsVoss, Interflex, Stanley Access Technologies, and the recently acquired ELATEC. Principal global competitors are Assa Abloy and dormakaba; Fortune Brands Innovations competes in North American residential; private-label brands are a growing threat at the low end. The strategic direction is toward electronic access and mobile credentials: Schlage integrations with Apple and Google Wallet, XE360 multifamily locks, SimonsVoss battery-less cylinders, and the Zentra SaaS platform.

FCF conversion is strong: $685.7M FCF against $643.8M net income implies ~106% conversion, a sign of high-quality earnings and low capital intensity. ROE of 31.1%, ROA of 12.3%, and a 15.8% net margin round out an efficient P&L.

The balance sheet supports the quality thesis. Total debt is ~$2.0B, roughly 90% fixed-rate senior notes maturing 2027-2034 at coupons between 3.50% and 5.60%. D/E is 1.53, current ratio 1.84, and pension plans are overfunded at 103%. The Altman Z of 2.42 ("grey zone") reflects deliberate financial leverage, not distress: for a stable, cash-generative business with fixed-rate debt and comfortable covenant headroom, this is a capital-structure choice. In December 2025 the company upsized its revolver to $1.0B (extended to 2030) and repaid the term loan.

The Q1 2026 print is where the story turns. The revenue bridge was acquisitions +4.8%, pricing +4.6%, FX +2.3%, and volume −2.0%. Operating margin compressed on unfavorable volume/mix and higher acquisition/integration/restructuring costs. Americas margin slipped to 26.6% from 27.9%; International margin fell to 3.7% from 6.3% with segment operating income down 29%. The effective tax rate rose to 19.5% from 15.4% on jurisdictional mix and prior-year discrete items. The mechanism is clear: tariffs on a Mexico-heavy cost base force price increases; price increases suppress volume in price-elastic residential; M&A fills the revenue hole but carries integration cost drag.

The earnings track record confirms deteriorating visibility. Over the last four quarters, Allegion has posted two beats and two misses, with the two most recent quarters missing consensus by −4.2% (Q4'25) and −6.9% (Q1'26). A company that was reliably meeting estimates is now missing them, which is consistent with the volume/margin narrative rather than a one-off.

## Growth Analysis

The headline growth is real but low-quality. Both FY2025 (+1.0% volume) and Q1 2026 (−2.0% volume) show that reported growth is almost entirely price plus acquisitions plus FX. Pricing here is a tariff pass-through mechanism, not evidence of pricing power in the demand-elasticity sense, and it is actively suppressing unit demand.

Estimate revisions are flat, which matters for scoring. Current-year consensus of $8.79 is essentially unchanged over 30 days (+0.1%) and down slightly over 90 days (−0.2%). Next-year consensus of $9.58 is up marginally (+0.6% over 90 days). Current-quarter estimates fell 3.1% over 90 days. There is no positive revision velocity: the strong-BUY signal (estimate revisions exceeding +30% over 90 days) that would justify an inflection call is absent. This is a low-single-digit organic grower where consensus expects EPS to rise from $8.79 to $9.58 (roughly 9%), much of which depends on integration costs rolling off and M&A contribution rather than volume recovery.

The electronic-access transition is the genuine long-term growth vector. Electronic products grew low-double-digits in FY2025, decelerating to mid-single-digits in Q1'26. Mobile credentials, IoT-connected access, and multifamily electronic locks are secular tailwinds, and the ELATEC acquisition (Germany RFID readers, closed July 2025) deepens the electronics portfolio. But this mix shift has not yet been large enough to offset the mechanical volume drag or the International margin erosion, and its Q1 deceleration is a caution flag rather than a confirmation.

## Valuation Assessment

At $137, ALLE trades at roughly 15.6x current-year consensus EPS and 14.3x next-year. The reported trailing P/E of ~18.7x is on FY2025 EPS of $7.44. The dividend yield is ~1.6%, recently raised to $0.55/quarter.

This is a discount to the company's own history and to broader capital-goods peers, but the discount is justified. A compressed multiple that reflects genuine deceleration is a value trap. The reasons the market is paying less are real: negative organic volume, 200 bps of margin compression, two consecutive earnings misses, and a growth model increasingly reliant on M&A that dilutes margin. The 5-year TSR gap (146.95 vs. peer 219.77) is the market's cumulative verdict on the low-single-digit organic profile, and nothing in the recent data reverses it.

Cycle-adjusted valuation does not rescue the case. This is not a trough-earnings inflection: EPS is not collapsing and about to triple; it is grinding modestly higher on price and deals. There are no estimate revisions of the magnitude (>30% over 90 days) that would signal the market is systematically underestimating a fundamental turn. Analyst targets (mean $165, median $165, high $189, low $142) sit meaningfully above the current price, but for a stock with flat-to-negative revisions and negative organic volume, those targets are more likely to be revised toward price than the reverse; the low target of $142 is barely above spot. Applying a 16x multiple to $9.58 next-year EPS yields roughly $153; a bear-case 13x on $8.50 (if volume weakness persists and International stays impaired) yields ~$110. The distribution is roughly symmetric around current levels, which is a HOLD, not a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Allegion's durable edge is the North American specification model. Spec-writers embed Schlage, Von Duprin, and LCN into architectural plans, and stringent fire/life-safety codes create switching friction and pricing power, visible directly in the 28% Americas margin. This is a genuine moat and the reason the franchise deserves a premium to a generic industrial.

The trajectory is mixed rather than deteriorating. The Americas franchise remains dominant and the electronic-access pivot positions the company for the mechanical-to-electronic convergence. But that same convergence invites well-capitalized non-traditional entrants and elevates private-label pressure at the low end. Assa Abloy is the scale global competitor; Fortune Brands competes in residential. The International segment is structurally challenged (single-digit-to-low margins, negative volume, and prior impairments) and Europe demand is characterized as "mixed." Net, the moat in the core is intact and the electronics mix shift is a positive vector, but negative unit volumes across the portfolio and International margin collapse mean the competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding at the consolidated level, not improving.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff and supply-chain concentration (most acute near-term).** Roughly 20-25% of COGS is sourced from Mexico under the IMMEX/Maquiladora program, with <5% from China. Allegion has offset tariff inflation with pricing, which is why price-elastic residential volume is falling. The February 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating certain IEEPA tariffs, and the Court of International Trade refund ruling, create an unquantified potential refund tailwind, but Allegion has booked none pending clarity, and new tariffs under other statutes have since been imposed. Tariff policy is a live, two-sided swing factor.

**M&A treadmill and goodwill risk.** Goodwill is ~$1.93B on ~$5.3B assets. The company deployed ~$779M in 2024-2025 (ELATEC $631.6M dominant) plus DCI (~$70M) in Q1'26. With organic volume negative, Allegion increasingly needs deals to grow, a lower-quality model that raises integration and impairment risk. International already carries accumulated impairment.

**Margin compression is present, not hypothetical.** Q1'26 operating margin fell on integration costs and negative mix. The M&A propping up revenue is diluting margins simultaneously.

**End-market cyclicality.** Demand hinges on institutional, commercial, and residential construction and remodeling, sensitive to rates and confidence. In a higher-for-longer rate regime with a soft residential outlook, volume recovery is not assured.

**Irish incorporation / tax.** The OECD 15% global minimum tax is enacted; the effective rate is already climbing (16.2% FY25, 19.5% Q1'26), a direct headwind to net income.

Against these, the business is financially resilient: overfunded pensions, fixed-rate debt, comfortable covenants, and ~$686M FCF give ample flexibility. The risks are real but largely known and partly priced, which supports a middling risk score rather than a low one.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 36.5% versus 25.6% one-year historical vol, rich, with the term structure in backwardation (36.5% near-term vs. 28.3% at six months). The market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with an approaching earnings date and the two-quarter miss streak. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±9.1%. Put/call skew is balanced (1.2% near-term) and the put/call OI ratio is low at 0.24, so there is no pronounced directional bearish positioning; the elevated IV reflects event uncertainty rather than a one-sided bet. IV rose from 30.4% to 36.5% since early June as the price recovered ~5.8%, which reads as the market pricing a wider distribution into the next print. For a HOLD, this argues against fresh positioning ahead of earnings.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The Americas specification moat is durable and the electronic-access transition is a multi-year growth vector the market is under-crediting. Tariff refunds from the SCOTUS/CIT rulings materialize and provide an unbooked earnings tailwind. Integration costs roll off, ELATEC and DCI contribute accretively, and consolidated margins recover toward the 21% FY2025 level. Organic volume stabilizes as rate pressure eases and residential demand normalizes. On a recovery to 17x $9.58, the stock reaches ~$163, roughly the analyst mean. Bull case ~$185 (18x on higher normalized EPS plus refund optionality), ~35% above current.

**Bear case.** Tariff-driven price increases continue to suppress volume; organic units stay negative; International margins remain depressed; and the M&A treadmill accelerates goodwill and impairment risk. Consensus EPS stalls near $8.50 and multiple compression continues to ~13x, taking the stock to ~$110, roughly 20% below current. A third consecutive earnings miss would validate this path.

The two cases are roughly balanced. The deciding variable is whether integration costs roll off and organic volume stabilizes, neither of which is confirmed in the current data, and neither of which has a dated catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July). This is the near-term binary the options market is pricing, and it will confirm whether organic volume and margin are stabilizing or deteriorating further.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic volume turns positive (from −2.0% in Q1'26) for two consecutive quarters, confirming the tariff-price/volume tradeoff is resolving.
- Consolidated operating margin recovers toward 20%+ as integration costs roll off, with International margin back above 6%.
- Estimate revisions turn meaningfully positive (>5% upward over 90 days) or a booked tariff refund adds quantifiable EPS.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A third consecutive earnings miss with further margin compression below 18%.
- Goodwill impairment charge (International segment the most exposed, given $573.6M accumulated).
- Organic volume decline widens beyond −3% or Americas margin falls below 25%.

## Conclusion

Allegion is a high-quality franchise trading at a justified discount. The static financials are excellent (21% operating margins, 31% ROE, 106% FCF conversion, a fortress balance sheet) and the Americas specification moat is genuine. But the growth engine is running on price and acquisitions while unit volumes shrink, margins compressed in the most recent quarter, and the company has missed estimates two quarters running. Estimate revisions are flat, and there is no dated catalyst to force a re-rating.

We rate ALLE a **HOLD**. The quality and free-cash-flow profile justify owning it through the cycle for shareholders already positioned, but the balanced risk/reward and absence of a positive volume or revision catalyst do not warrant fresh capital at $137. The next earnings print is the decision point.
