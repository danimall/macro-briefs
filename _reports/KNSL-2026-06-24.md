**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kinsale Capital Group is the only U.S. publicly traded insurer focused exclusively on excess & surplus (E&S) lines, and it has built a structural cost advantage: a 20.8% expense ratio against industry peers typically above 25%, supported by a 17-year-old proprietary technology platform with no legacy software. The financials remain best-in-class: a 75.9% combined ratio in 2025, 29.3% GAAP ROE (26.4% operating), $504M net income (+21.4%), and $990M of free cash flow on a 52.8% FCF margin. The stock has fallen roughly 36% from its July 2025 high of $512.76 to ~$304, marking 2025 as KNSL's first down year since its 2016 IPO despite rising earnings.

The decline is a multiple reset driven by a growth deceleration, not an underwriting problem. Gross written premiums grew only 5.7% in 2025 — a sharp slowdown from the ~30-40% compound rates of KNSL's history — and declined 0.5% in Q1 2026. The drag is concentrated in Commercial Property (the largest division, down 17.9% in 2025) as rates softened and standard carriers re-entered the segment. Stripping out Commercial Property, GWP still grew 13.3% in 2025, and net written premium is growing faster than gross (9.4% in 2025) because management raised reinsurance retentions on lines it favors and deliberately shed low-margin large property accounts. Q1 2026 operating EPS rose 37.7% YoY to $5.11, four straight quarters have beaten consensus, and operating ROE held near 24%.

We rate KNSL a BUY with disciplined sizing. At ~13.4x trailing earnings on a growing earnings base, the valuation has compressed to a level that overcorrects for a cyclical, partly deliberate growth slowdown. The central debate is binary: whether the slowdown is cyclical (property re-softening, intentional mix shift) and reverses, or whether elite ROEs structurally compress as capital floods E&S casualty. We lean toward the cyclical interpretation given the intact cost moat and disciplined small-account focus, but the broadening of soft-market competition into long-tail casualty — 70%+ of the book — is the genuine risk that keeps this from being a maximum-conviction call.

## Company Overview

Founded in 2009 and public since July 2016 (IPO at $16/share), Kinsale writes hard-to-place, small-to-medium commercial risks through a network of roughly 227 independent wholesale brokers across all 50 states. Average premium per policy was about $13,400 in 2025, falling to ~$12,200 in Q1 2026 as the company shifted toward smaller accounts. It operates as a single reportable segment.

The 2025 business mix (GWP of $1.98B) was 70.7% casualty / 29.3% property, and 97% commercial. The largest divisions were Commercial Property (18.9% but shrinking), Excess Casualty (14.0%), General Casualty (10.5%), and Small Business Casualty (10.2%). Geographic exposure is concentrated: California (19.7%), Florida (15.3%), and Texas (14.0%) account for about 49% of premium, all catastrophe-exposed. Broker concentration is meaningful, with the top three brokers (Ryan Specialty, AmWINS, CRC) representing 47.6% of GWP.

The competitive model rests on four pillars: a structural cost advantage from a purpose-built technology stack; full vertical control (KNSL never delegates underwriting or claims authority to MGAs, with roughly 340 underwriters individually pricing risks); a contrarian small-account focus that avoids the most competitive large accounts; and an A.M. Best "A" (Excellent) rating. KNSL holds roughly 1.5% of a ~$130B U.S. E&S market that has grown direct premiums written about 10%/year versus 4.7% for total P&C from 2001-2024, providing a long runway if the structural E&S growth trend persists.

## Financial Analysis

Underwriting and earnings quality remain elite; the deceleration is on the top line.

| Combined ratio | — | 76.4% | 75.9% |
| ROE (GAAP) | — | 32.3% | 29.3% |

The 2025 loss ratio of 55.1% included $30.4M of Palisades Fire catastrophe losses and was struck after $62.8M of favorable prior-year reserve development, extending a 17-year track record of reserves developing favorably. Net investment income rose 27.9% to $192M on float growth (to $3.3B at Q1 2026) and new-money yields near 5%. The net margin was 26.9%, and operating cash flow was $1.04B.

The balance sheet is conservative. Stockholders' equity grew to $1.96B from $1.48B, debt is modest at $224M (D/E 0.68), and the $5.2B investment portfolio is 83.6% fixed maturities at an average AA- rating and 4.0-year duration. One atypical feature: roughly 12% (~$626M) sits in equities, including ~$417M of individually selected common stocks. This is aggressive for a P&C insurer and adds earnings volatility: 2025 mark-to-market gains of $58.8M flowed through GAAP net income, which is why GAAP ROE exceeds operating ROE. The equity book has lagged the S&P since 2022 given its value tilt.

The Altman Z-score of 2.6 screens as a "grey zone." That model was designed for industrials and misreads insurance balance sheets, where investment float inflates assets. It is a false signal here; the underlying ratios (FCF margin 52.8%, D/E 0.68, A.M. Best "A") confirm genuine financial strength. The financial health score of 8.5 reflects best-in-class cash generation, low leverage for the model, and a reliable favorable-development history; it is held below 9 because the equity-heavy portfolio introduces volatility a more conservative insurer would avoid.

Capital allocation has shifted appropriately as growth slowed. The company exhausted a $100M buyback in October 2025, authorized $250M in December 2025, and raised the quarterly dividend from $0.17 to $0.25. These moves acknowledge that capital cannot all be redeployed at historical growth rates, and they return cash at a depressed share price.

## Growth Analysis

This is the dimension that has deteriorated, and it is the reason the score sits at 5.7 (down from 6.8 in March). The ~30%+ compounding that built the franchise has slowed sharply. The growth-prospects dimension is the most reliable predictor of forward returns in our framework, so the deceleration warrants a genuine markdown rather than a reflexive defense of the prior thesis.

The decomposition matters. The 2025 drag was almost entirely Commercial Property, which fell 17.9% as rates declined and standard carriers re-entered. Excluding Commercial Property, GWP grew 13.3% in 2025, with General Casualty +22.9%, Excess Casualty +13.0%, Small Business Property +33.4%, and Entertainment +27.0%. In Q1 2026, GWP ex-Commercial Property grew about 6%, net written premium grew 5.6%, and submissions, quotes, and binders ex-property were up high-single to low-double digits. The franchise is still generating new business; the headline is depressed by a deliberate exit from low-margin large property accounts (average premium in that division fell from $50K+ toward $30-40K) and by raised reinsurance retentions that lift net economics.

Estimate revisions confirm a flattening rather than a deterioration. Current-year EPS consensus sits at $20.81, revised +2.2% over 90 days but essentially flat (-0.1%) over 30 days. Next-year consensus of $21.68 has slipped -2.5% over 90 days, reflecting the soft-market caution. This is a "stable, no significant directional shift" pattern, not the falling-estimate profile that would flag accelerating deterioration. It does not clear the strong inflection BUY threshold (revisions are not +30% and revenue is not accelerating), so the bull case rests on cyclical normalization and re-rating, not on a near-term earnings explosion.

Management has reset expectations candidly, anchoring guidance to "low 20s ROE or greater" versus the high-20s/low-30s historically, and conceding "no good news to report" on commercial property pricing. That honesty is a positive governance signal even as it confirms the era of 30%+ ROEs may be paused. The long-run growth runway of 1.5% of a structurally faster-growing E&S market remains real, which is why the score sits in the mid-5s rather than lower.

## Valuation Assessment

KNSL trades at ~13.4x trailing earnings (EPS $21.65) with current-year consensus near 14.6x forward. This is a substantial de-rating from the 25-35x multiples the stock commanded in its high-growth years.

The peer set complicates the comparison. The same-sector P&C median P/E is 9.9 (FAF 10.0, THG 9.9, MCY 6.5, SIGI 12.6, ORI 9.4). On a pure multiple basis KNSL is not cheap relative to these names. But the comparison must be quality- and return-adjusted: KNSL generates a mid-20s operating ROE, while these peers operate at single-digit-to-low-teens ROEs and far higher combined ratios. A high-20s-ROE compounder at 13.4x is structurally different from a 10% ROE insurer at 10x. The relevant historical comp is KNSL's own multiple, which has compressed by more than half while earnings continued to rise.

Our framework treats a low multiple as a value-trap warning absent an independent catalyst, and treats prior price appreciation as irrelevant to current valuation. Both cautions apply here. The de-rating's magnitude requires identifying what the market is wrong about, not assuming volatility. Our thesis is specific and falsifiable: the market has priced the growth slowdown as if it is structural and as if elite ROEs are gone, when the evidence points to a substantially cyclical/deliberate slowdown (property re-softening, intentional account shedding) layered on a still-growing net premium base and intact cost moat. The catalysts that close the gap are the continuation of mid-20s operating ROE in subsequent prints, a stabilization of commercial property pricing, and the $250M buyback retiring shares into the weakness.

Analyst targets bracket the case: low $267, mean $345, median $355, high $405 — all but the low above the current price. We score valuation 7.0: the asymmetry favors the long side given the quality-adjusted discount and identified catalysts, but we stop short of a higher score because the bear interpretation (structural ROE compression) is not yet disproven, and the peer P/E floor means the downside multiple is not negligible.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive position is strong in level but the trajectory has flattened, which is why the score is 6.8 rather than higher. The cost moat is the differentiator: a 20.8% expense ratio built on proprietary technology with no legacy systems, full vertical control over underwriting and claims, and a small-account focus that larger competitors find uneconomic to replicate. CEO Kehoe describes the small-account book as "a great safe harbor" that lets KNSL stay disciplined through soft markets without chasing volume, and the Q1 2026 ex-property new-business momentum supports that claim.

The cyclical wind has turned against the company. The 2021-2023 hard market that supercharged growth is fading: standard carriers are recapturing commercial property, and capital is flooding into casualty E&S via fronts, MGAs, and new entrants. The thread to watch is the Q1 2026 commentary flagging aggressive new competition in construction and long-tail casualty over the prior roughly five months. Casualty is 70%+ of the book and long-tail; if competition compresses casualty pricing while social/loss-cost inflation persists, that is a more meaningful threat to the loss ratio than the property softening already in the numbers. Whether larger, AI-investing competitors can replicate KNSL's small-account efficiency remains unresolved; management argues firmly that the absence of legacy systems makes this structurally hard, and the multi-decade cost gap supports them.

## Risk Assessment

The risk score of 5.5 reflects real, identifiable risks that are partly but not fully priced after the de-rating.

1. **Soft-market deceleration broadening from property into casualty.** This is the dominant near-term risk. E&S cyclicality exceeds the admitted market, and business that flooded in during the hard market can flow back out, exacerbating rate declines. Commercial Property is the live example; the new concern is casualty.

2. **Reserve adequacy in long-tail casualty.** 91.2% of net reserves are IBNR and 84.2% of casualty reserves are long-tail "occurrence." A 5% change in net IBNR equals roughly $114M, or about 5.8% of equity. KNSL noted adverse development in construction-liability accident years 2016-2019 and inflation-uncertainty adjustments for construction-defect exposure. As the casualty book grows as a share of mix, this risk grows with it.

3. **Catastrophe and geographic concentration.** About 49% of premium is in CA/FL/TX. Retention was raised to $75M per cat event (cover $250M xs $75M, max aggregate recovery $500M), which boosts net economics but increases earnings volatility.

4. **Broker concentration.** Top three brokers at 47.6% of GWP; loss of a major relationship would materially dent premiums.

5. **A.M. Best rating dependency.** A downgrade below "A-" would impair competitiveness and trip credit-agreement covenants.

6. **Expanded tech-related risk language in the FY2025 10-K** around AI (competitor capability, generative-AI hallucination/bias) and open-source software licensing exposure — a double edge of the technology moat.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 33.0% sits essentially in line with one-year historical volatility of 32.6% (a 0.4pp spread), with IV in only the 4th percentile of its own chain — options are fairly priced. Put/call skew is balanced (-1.2%) and put/call volume and OI ratios are low (0.03 and 0.25), indicating no acute bearish positioning in the near term. The term structure is in contango (33.0% near vs 42.1% at six months), a normal/complacent shape, though the rise into the 3-month tenor captures the August earnings event. Short interest is 12.7% of float with 8.2 days to cover, signaling institutional skepticism on the structural-deceleration thesis, while the options market is calm. The combination of fairly priced near-term vol, balanced skew, and meaningful short interest implies a stock where bears have positioned via the borrow rather than puts, leaving squeeze potential if subsequent prints confirm the cyclical interpretation.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($405)
The growth slowdown proves cyclical and partly deliberate. Commercial property pricing stabilizes, the intentional shed of low-margin accounts laps, and casualty ex-property continues compounding double digits. Net written premium growth (running ahead of gross) sustains earnings even with flat headline GWP. Operating ROE holds in the low-to-mid 20s, four-quarter beat streaks continue, and the market re-rates the multiple back toward 18-19x as the structural-impairment fear fades. The $250M buyback retires shares near a multi-year-low valuation. At ~19x a $21+ EPS base, the stock reaches the $405 high target, roughly +33% from current levels.

### Bear Case ($255-265)
The casualty competition flagged in Q1 2026 broadens and deepens, compressing pricing across 70% of the book while social inflation pushes loss costs higher. Reserve development turns less favorable or adverse as long-tail accident years season, and operating ROE drifts toward the high teens. The market concludes the elite-ROE era has structurally ended and anchors the multiple toward the P&C peer median (~10x), implying roughly $210-265 depending on the earnings base. The low analyst target of $267 sits at the optimistic edge of this scenario, roughly -12% to -16% from current levels.

### Base Case ($345)
The cyclical interpretation largely holds, with growth staying subdued near term but ROE remaining in the low 20s. Earnings grow modestly, the multiple recovers partway toward the high-teens, and capital returns support per-share metrics. This aligns with the analyst mean target of $345.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $345.** Roughly 16.5x a ~$21 forward EPS base, reflecting a partial multiple recovery as the cyclical-vs-structural debate resolves toward cyclical while growth stays subdued. This is +13% from the current ~$304.

**Bull case target: $405** (+33%) — ~19x a $21+ EPS base on a re-rating back toward quality-adjusted levels as ROE durability is confirmed.

**Bear case target: $260** (-15%) — multiple compression toward the P&C peer median on confirmation of structural ROE erosion.

**Timeframe: 12-18 months.** The thesis turns on a sequence of quarterly prints (next reportable ~late July 2026) confirming whether casualty pricing holds and ROE stays in the low 20s.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
- Operating ROE falls below 20% on a trailing-four-quarter basis, signaling structural rather than cyclical compression.
- Prior-year reserve development turns net adverse for two consecutive quarters, particularly in long-tail casualty.
- Casualty (ex-property) GWP growth decelerates below mid-single digits, indicating the soft market has fully reached the core book.

## Conclusion

Kinsale is a high-quality, founder-led E&S compounder undergoing a cyclical growth slowdown and a valuation reset that has overshot the underlying fundamentals. Underwriting, profitability, cash generation, and a durable cost moat all remain intact, while the share price has fallen on a re-rating tied to decelerating top-line growth — much of which is deliberate (shedding low-margin property, raising retentions) or cyclical (standard carriers recapturing commercial property). Four consecutive earnings beats, stable-to-rising current-year estimates, a stepped-up buyback into weakness, a candid management reset, and a small open-market insider purchase by the CAO all support the cyclical interpretation.

The recommendation is BUY with disciplined sizing. The chief unresolved risk — soft-market competition broadening into the long-tail casualty book — is real and keeps the conviction below maximum; it is also the precise variable to monitor. The quality-adjusted discount, identified re-rating catalysts, and fairly priced options tilt the 12-18 month risk/reward favorably, with roughly +13% to base case and +33% to bull against roughly -15% to bear. We note the sector caution that Financials BUYs have historically underperformed the market on a relative basis, so this call requires the company-specific catalyst (ROE durability proving the structural-impairment fear wrong) to play out rather than relying on sector beta.
