**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

OFG Bancorp is the holding company for Oriental Bank, the second- or third-largest bank in Puerto Rico's three-bank oligopoly. It generates profitability metrics that few mainland regional banks can match: Q1'26 net interest margin of 5.36%, return on assets of 1.78%, return on tangible common equity of 16.43%, and a 50.97% efficiency ratio. Earnings have compounded steadily ($166M in 2022 to $205M in 2025), Q1'26 EPS of $1.26 beat consensus by 25.4%, and current-year EPS estimates have been revised up 10.1% over 90 days. Capital is strong (CET1 13.75%, tangible book value $30.14/share), and management is returning capital through a 17% dividend increase and $193.6M of remaining buyback authorization.

The valuation is the obvious hook: at $46.66, OFG trades at roughly 9.6x trailing and forward earnings against a regional-bank peer median of 15.6x. A 16%+ ROTCE bank at sub-10x earnings looks like a mispricing on paper. The complication is that the discount is structural and durable rather than a temporary dislocation. Substantially all of OFG's business is in Puerto Rico, an economy exposed to hurricanes, earthquakes, federal-funding dependence, and government fiscal stress. The elite NIM is partly earned by lending into high-loss segments: auto net charge-offs run 1.52% and consumer/personal NCOs run roughly 4.40%, structurally above mainland norms. Management guides NIM down to 5.10–5.20% for 2026, so the rate-cycle tailwind that drove the margin is fading.

We rate OFG a HOLD with a growth bias. The business is high-quality and the capital-return program supports per-share growth, but the stock has run 14.5% to within striking distance of its 52-week high with no confirmed catalyst to close the peer discount. The single most intriguing item, repeated disclosure of "capital markets readiness and registration" costs, is unconfirmed and cannot be priced. We would upgrade to BUY on evidence that NIM is stabilizing above the guidance range or on confirmation of a value-creating strategic action.

## Company Overview

OFG operates through three segments. **Banking** is the profit engine, contributing roughly $27M of $54M Q1'26 net income through 42 Puerto Rico branches plus two in the USVI, spanning commercial, auto, residential mortgage, and consumer lending. **Wealth Management** (Oriental Financial Services broker-dealer/RIA, Oriental Insurance, OFG Reinsurance, and trust services) administers about $5.4B of client assets. **Treasury** manages the $2.79B investment portfolio, which is almost entirely agency MBS.

The company also operates tax-advantaged subsidiaries that exploit Puerto Rico's structure: OIB and Oriental Overseas (International Banking Entities), OFG USA (a Delaware lender expanding mainland middle-market participations), and a PR-incentivized private equity fund. Total assets were $12.05B at Q1'26. CEO and Chairman José Rafael Fernández and CFO Maritza Arizmendi lead the company. The competitive differentiator is a "digital bank with personal connections" strategy (Libre/Elite retail, My Biz commercial) that has built a low-cost core deposit base.

## Financial Analysis

Profitability is the central feature of this story. Net income has risen every year ($166M in 2022, $182M in 2023, $198M in 2024, $205M in 2025), and Q1'26 net income of $53.9M was up 18% YoY. Diluted EPS of $1.26 grew 26% YoY, amplified by buybacks that cut the diluted share count from 48.4M in 2022 to roughly 43M. The headline ratios sit at the top of the regional-bank universe.

The revenue base is diversified for a bank this size: net interest income of $153.8M plus roughly $32M of non-interest income (banking fees, wealth management, mortgage banking) in Q1'26, consistent with total core revenue of $185.8M. Interest expense rose sharply through the rate cycle, from $33M in 2022 to $172M in 2025, but the path of least resistance is now lower as the Fed eases and the funding mix improves; deposit cost is just 1.40%.

The balance sheet is conservative. Loans held-for-investment total $8.24B gross: Commercial 43% ($3.55B, including $872M of Commercial US), Auto 32% ($2.63B), Mortgage 17%, Consumer 8%. Deposits stand at $9.85B; uninsured deposits are elevated at ~52% but are heavily collateralized public funds rather than flight-prone retail balances. CET1 is 13.75%, Total RBC 15.01%, leverage 10.88%, TCE/TA 10.66%. The AOCI loss is a manageable –$33.6M and HTM unrealized losses are ~$45M, so OFG carries none of the unrealized-bond-loss risk that toppled some 2023 banks.

Earnings quality is supported by the beat history: three beats in four quarters, with the Q1'26 beat at 25.4% and the prior quarter at 9.3%. The one miss (Q3'25, –1.6%) was minor. Current-year EPS was revised to $4.70 from $4.26 ninety days ago (+10.3%), with the current-quarter estimate up 8.1% in 30 days.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is solid rather than explosive, the appropriate frame for a regional bank. Per-share earnings growth is being manufactured by two levers: modest underlying net-income growth (low-to-mid single digits organically) and a buyback that retired $44.5M of stock at an average $40.51 in Q1'26 alone. With $193.6M of authorization remaining against a $2.0B market cap, buybacks can support a meaningful annual share-count reduction at current prices, a primary driver of the double-digit EPS growth.

The estimate revision velocity is positive but does not reach the threshold that signals a true earnings inflection. Current-year consensus rose 10.3% over 90 days, partly a function of the Q1 beat flowing through, but next-year estimates are essentially flat at $4.70, implying the Street sees 2026 as a peak-earnings year rather than the start of an acceleration. That flat next-year line is consistent with management's own NIM guidance of 5.10–5.20% for the full year. Lower margin offsets loan growth.

The off-island expansion through OFG USA is the clearest organic growth lever: the Commercial US book has grown to 10.6% of loans and reduces the structural PR concentration. It also imports mainland credit and competition risk into a portfolio that has been the source of OFG's premium returns. Wealth management and treasury provide fee diversification but are not large enough to move the consolidated growth rate materially.

## Valuation Assessment

OFG trades at 9.6x trailing and forward earnings, a roughly 38% discount to the regional-bank peer median of 15.6x. Against the specific comparison set (FCF 12.9x, STEL 18.7x, NBHC 16.2x, NWBI 15.6x, CHCO 14.2x), OFG is the cheapest by a wide margin despite carrying the highest profitability (16.4% ROTCE versus typical mid-teens for the group). On a price-to-tangible-book basis, the stock trades at roughly 1.55x ($46.66 vs. $30.14 TBVPS), which is reasonable but not screaming-cheap for a 16% ROTCE business.

The discount is real, but the more important question is whether it closes. I treat cheap multiples on Puerto Rico banks with caution: the discount has persisted for years because the market assigns a permanent risk premium for geographic concentration, hurricane/earthquake exposure, federal-funding dependence, and a high-loss consumer book. A low P/E that reflects structural risk is a value characteristic, not a catalyst. To score valuation as a strong buy signal, I need a specific reason the gap narrows from here, and the candidates are weak. The NIM is guided lower, not higher; next-year EPS is flat; and the most interesting potential catalyst (the "capital markets readiness" line) is unconfirmed.

What does support the stock is absolute earnings power and yield. At 9.6x with a 16% ROTCE plus a 2.9% dividend and ongoing buybacks, OFG can compound book value and EPS even if the multiple never re-rates. That is a respectable total-return profile, but it is a compounding case at a fair price, not an asymmetric mispricing. Analyst targets ($45 low, $48 median, $50 high) bracket the current price tightly, implying the sell side sees the stock as roughly fairly valued near $47–48. That is about 3–7% to the median/high, which is limited headroom.

## Competitive Landscape

Puerto Rico banking is an oligopoly dominated by Popular, First BanCorp, and OFG. The consolidated structure supports pricing power and explains why OFG's NIM and ROA run well above mainland regional banks. OFG's digital-plus-personal model has driven low-cost core deposit growth, and its top-three position is stable. The PR macro backdrop is currently stable-to-positive: the Economic Activity Index rose 0.3% YoY in January 2026, and non-farm payrolls sit near 951,600, supported by federal reconstruction funds, private investment, and onshoring.

The competitive trajectory is stable rather than clearly improving. Within PR, OFG holds its position but faces a mature, slow-growth market, which is precisely why capital cannot be fully deployed organically and is being returned to shareholders. The mainland push via OFG USA is the growth vector that could improve the long-term competitive picture, but it places OFG into a far more competitive arena where it has no scale advantage. I score competitive position at the middle of the range: a strong incumbent in a defensible but slow-growing home market, expanding into a market where its moat does not travel.

## Risk Assessment

The risks here are specific and concentrated:

- **Puerto Rico concentration** is the dominant risk. Substantially all revenue is generated on an island exposed to hurricanes (Maria 2017, Fiona 2022), earthquakes (2020), government fiscal distress, and the PREPA Title III restructuring. A regional shock hits originations, NPAs, and collateral values simultaneously.
- **Policy/macro overhang.** The latest 10-Q newly emphasizes tariffs, federal spending cuts, the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act," and a potential government shutdown, each of which disproportionately affects PR's federally-supported economy.
- **Credit-cost structure.** The premium NIM is partly compensation for risk: auto NCOs of 1.52% and consumer NCOs near 4.40% drive total NCOs of ~1.05% of average loans, structurally above mainland peers. As the rate tailwind fades, credit normalization could compress earnings faster than headline figures suggest.
- **Single-name commercial exposure.** A $44.1M PR telecommunications loan has been on non-accrual since 2025; Commercial US loans showed ~$3.9M of specific reserves/charge-offs in Q1'26. ACL coverage of NPLs is a healthy 168.6%, which contains the downside, but the telecom name bears watching.
- **Deposit normalization.** A $1.2B PR government inflow from December 2023 is being drawn down; $500M was reclassified to a wealth-management advisory account in January 2026, cutting PR government deposits to $1.26B from $1.68B. Core retail/commercial deposits are growing, so this is transition rather than deterioration, but it does pressure the low-cost funding base that underpins the NIM.

Credit quality is currently improving (NPL ratio down to 1.47% from 1.59%), which moderates the near-term risk, and the capital buffer is large enough to absorb a meaningful credit cycle.

### Options Market Signal

The options data carry a caveat: the delta snapshot shows ATM IV reading 798.3%, which is a corrupted thin-options artifact and non-informative. The cleaner near-term reading is a 35.5% ATM IV against 25.8% trailing realized volatility, rich by about 9.7 points, with the term structure in backwardation (35.5% near-term vs. 26.9% at six months). Backwardation indicates the market is pricing some near-term event risk, consistent with an upcoming earnings cycle or the unconfirmed capital-markets process. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±10.4%. Short interest is modest at 4.5% of float (5.3 days to cover), so there is no squeeze dynamic and no crowded bearish positioning. The market is paying up for near-term protection without taking a strong directional view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** OFG is a 16%+ ROTCE bank trading at 9.6x earnings, roughly 40% below peers, with strong capital (CET1 13.75%), an improving credit trend, and a 2.9% dividend plus ongoing buybacks that compound per-share value regardless of multiple re-rating. If the unconfirmed "capital markets readiness" disclosure resolves into a value-creating M&A transaction or capital action, or if NIM holds above the 5.10–5.20% guidance, the peer discount could narrow toward 12–13x, supporting a price near $55–58. Bull case ~$55.

**Bear Case.** The peer discount is structural and stays in place. NIM steps down as guided, consumer and auto credit normalize into a higher-charge-off cycle, the PR government deposit drawdown pressures funding, and a policy shock (federal spending cuts, tariffs) hits the PR economy. EPS flattens near $4.70, the multiple compresses toward 8x, and the stock retraces toward the low-$40s. Bear case ~$40 (–14% from current).

The base case sits between: low-double-digit total return driven by buybacks and the dividend, with the multiple roughly stable, supporting a price near $50 over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-oriented), 12-month horizon.**

This is a growth hold rather than a buy because the upside to fair value is limited and there is no confirmed catalyst to force a re-rating. The stock has run 14.5% to within ~4% of its 52-week high, analyst targets cluster at $45–50 (implying 3–7% upside to the median/high), and the dominant valuation thesis (peer discount) is offset by the structural reasons that discount exists. The specific, falsifiable conditions that prevent a BUY: (1) NIM is guided lower (5.36% → 5.10–5.20%), removing the margin tailwind that drove the earnings beat; (2) next-year consensus EPS is flat at $4.70, signaling the Street sees 2026 as peak earnings, not the start of an inflection; and (3) the only differentiated catalyst, the "capital markets readiness/registration" disclosure, is unconfirmed, with no S-1/S-3 or 8-K to substantiate it. Absent one of these resolving favorably, the risk/reward is roughly symmetric near current levels.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- NIM stabilizes above 5.20% for two consecutive quarters, indicating the margin step-down is not materializing and 2026 is not peak earnings.
- Confirmation via S-3/S-1/8-K of a value-creating strategic action (accretive M&A, a capital structure move) tied to the "capital markets readiness" process.
- Stock pulls back to the low-$40s (toward ~1.3x tangible book), restoring asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Total NCOs exceed ~1.5% of average loans, signaling the high-yield consumer/auto book is normalizing faster than NIM can absorb.
- The $44M PR telecom non-accrual moves to charge-off without offsetting reserve release, or a second large single-name credit emerges.
- A material PR macro/policy shock (federal funding cut, tariff action) that impairs the originations and collateral base.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (mid-to-late July 2026), with the NIM trajectory and any capital-markets disclosure as the key watch items.

## Conclusion

OFG is a genuinely high-quality regional bank (elite profitability, strong capital, improving credit, and shareholder-friendly capital return) trading at a deep discount to peers. The reason to own it is the compounding: double-digit ROTCE plus a buyback and a 2.9% dividend can deliver low-double-digit total returns even without multiple re-rating. The reason not to chase it here is that the discount is structural, the NIM tailwind is fading by management's own guidance, next-year EPS is flat, and the stock has already run to within a few percent of its 52-week high and the analyst target band. The asymmetry that would justify a BUY is not present at $46.66 absent a NIM surprise or confirmation of the unexplained capital-markets process. HOLD, with a clear upgrade path on either.
