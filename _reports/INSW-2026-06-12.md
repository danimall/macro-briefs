**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

International Seaways is one of the largest publicly traded crude and product tanker operators in the world, and it is currently printing the best results in its history. Q1 2026 net income was $286M ($5.75 diluted EPS, $3.90 adjusted), versus $50M a year earlier, driven by VLCC spot TCE rates more than doubling to roughly $87,000/day on the disruption of Strait of Hormuz traffic. The company has converted that windfall into a record $4.55/share combined dividend, raised its payout ratio to 85% of adjusted net income, and continued to de-lever to a net loan-to-value below 7% with ~$918M of liquidity. On almost every fundamental dimension the business is in excellent shape.

The central problem for the equity is that these earnings are a war premium, not a run-rate. Management itself frames the Strait situation as temporary and warns that a *prolonged* closure becomes a global demand shock that would hurt the company. With ~82% of TCE revenue tied to the spot market and an 85% payout on adjusted earnings, both the income statement and the dividend mechanically shrink as rates normalize. The stock is the cleanest two-sided expression of the Hormuz binary in the tanker complex: it benefits from a genuine reopening (relief to the broader macro, but compression of the rate spike that produced these numbers) and from prolonged disruption (higher rates, but rising tail risk of a demand shock). A swelling VLCC and Suezmax orderbook delivering into 2026-2028 caps the upside as the geopolitical premium fades.

We rate INSW a HOLD. The fundamentals are strong and the capital-return discipline is real, but the earnings base is at or near a cyclical peak engineered by a geopolitical event that is itself unresolved, the trailing P/E of ~7.5x is meaningless against mean-reverting peak earnings, and a refreshed poison pill alongside CMB.Tech proximity introduces a governance/takeover dynamic that could be either a catalyst or an overhang. The score trajectory has been declining (7.1 in mid-May to 5.9 now), and current data confirms that pattern: the price has come off its $92.66 high while the rate spike that justified the rally is the thing most likely to reverse.

## Company Overview

International Seaways operates exclusively in the International Flag trade across two segments. **Crude Tankers** (52% of FY2025 TCE revenue) comprise VLCCs (the workhorse, ~3.0M dwt), Suezmaxes, Aframaxes, and an asset-light Crude Tankers Lightering business handling ship-to-ship transfers in the U.S. Gulf, U.S. Pacific, Grand Bahama, and Panama. **Product Carriers** (48%) include MRs, LR1s, and one LR2, with LR1s also employed in crude trades via the Panamax International pool.

As of Q1 2026, the fleet stood at 67 vessels (down from 74), reflecting an aggressive fleet-renewal program, with three LR1 newbuilds still to deliver. INSW runs a hybrid operating model (in-house commercial/technical expertise plus third-party managers) and deploys the majority of vessels in the spot market via commercial pools. A meaningful strategic shift occurred in January 2026: INSW acquired CMB.Tech's remaining 50% of Tankers (UK) Agencies Ltd. (TUKA), giving it 100% ownership of the commercial manager of the Tankers International VLCC pool and a newly formed Suezmax pool. This moves the company from pool participant to pool owner, enhancing cargo access, scale, and fee capture.

The current market capitalization is approximately $3.9B at $78.13, against a 52-week range of $36.03 to $92.66. The stock has roughly doubled over the past year, almost entirely on the strength of the tanker-rate cycle.

## Financial Analysis

The balance sheet and cash generation are the strongest part of the story. Altman Z-Score is 3.79 (safe zone), current ratio 3.7x, debt/equity 0.32, ROE 15.3%, net margin 36.7%, operating margin 41%. Net debt/total capitalization fell to 16.5% at year-end 2025. Total liquidity was ~$918M at 3/31/26 ($377M cash and short-term investments plus $541M undrawn revolver), with 31+ unencumbered vessels and a well-laddered maturity profile (2030-2037).

Revenue is cyclical and currently at a cyclical high. The trajectory illustrates the swing: FY2023 revenue $1,072M / net income $556M (prior peak), FY2024 $952M / $417M, FY2025 $843M / $309M as rates softened (Product Carriers fell 20% on a daily rate decline to $24,787 from $31,846, the swing factor in the YoY decline while crude held up). Then Q1 2026 broke higher: adjusted net income $194M, adjusted EBITDA $244M, on VLCC spot TCE of $86,693/day (vs. $33,531 a year earlier) and fixed VLCC rates of $128,264/day.

The earnings-surprise record is uniformly positive (four consecutive beats: +47.6%, +25.4%, +21.6%, +5.6%), but the acceleration of the beats tracks the acceleration of spot rates rather than improving execution against a stable demand backdrop. Estimate revisions have been extreme: current-year consensus EPS is now $13.90, up +40.1% in 30 days and +138.7% in 90 days; the current quarter sits at $5.68, up +352.6% in 90 days. This is precisely the magnitude of revision velocity that normally signals a durable inflection. Here the revisions are tracking a geopolitical rate spike, not a structural demand reset, which is why we do not treat them as a clean buy signal. Next-year EPS consensus is $6.42, less than half the current-year figure, so the consensus itself prices the mean reversion.

Capital allocation is the equity story. INSW has returned over $1B to shareholders since 2020. It pays a small fixed quarterly dividend ($0.12) plus large variable supplemental dividends tied to earnings: FY2025 dividends totaled $2.93/share ($144.6M), and March 2026 added $2.15. The model is explicitly cyclical: buy and sell vessels opportunistically, de-lever, and distribute the cash. FY2025 included $246M of vessel-sale proceeds ($42.5M gains); Q1 2026 added $216M ($88M gains) from selling older ships while taking modern LR1 newbuilds. The mechanical consequence is that the dividend shrinks as rates normalize, so the headline yield (~0.58% on the fixed component per Phase 1 data, far higher including supplementals) is not a stable-yield signal.

## Growth Analysis

There is no secular growth thesis here, and the data does not support scoring one. Revenue has declined for two consecutive fiscal years (FY2023 $1,072M to FY2025 $843M) before the Q1 2026 rate spike. The current "growth" is a rate event: VLCC spot TCE jumped because Strait of Hormuz traffic fell ~90-95% after active hostilities began February 28, 2026. That is a price effect on a fixed-asset base, not volume expansion or share gains compounding over time.

The forward picture is mean-reverting by management's own framing. Next-year consensus EPS sits well below the current year, embedding normalization. The supply side argues the same way: the 10-K flags the tanker orderbook up 23.6M dwt YoY, with the VLCC orderbook up 19.0M dwt and Suezmax up 6.4M dwt. Heavy crude-tanker deliveries in 2026-2028 will add capacity into the back end of the current up-cycle, pressuring rates as the geopolitical premium fades.

The one genuinely strategic, forward-positive development is the TUKA buyout and Tankers International full ownership, which moves INSW up the value chain into commercial pool management and adds a Suezmax pool. This improves long-run cargo access and fee capture, but it does not change the fundamental driver of the company's earnings, which remains spot tanker rates.

We score Growth Prospects 5.0, consistent with the declining trajectory (7.0 to 5.0 over recent reports). New data confirms the decline: the apparent earnings explosion is the very thing the company and consensus expect to reverse.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing/forward P/E of ~7.5x is the most misleading number in the file. Cycle-adjusted valuation guidance argues for using forward or normalized earnings at inflection points, but the relevant adjustment here runs the *opposite* direction from a typical trough story: INSW is at a cyclical *peak*, so trailing earnings overstate normalized earning power. Capitalizing $13.90 current-year EPS at any multiple produces a low headline P/E precisely because the market does not believe those earnings are durable. On next-year consensus of $6.42, the stock trades at ~12.2x, and on a trailing EPS basis of ~$6.23, the multiple is similar. Neither is cheap for a deeply cyclical, spot-exposed asset at the top of its cycle.

Against peers in Oil & Gas Storage & Transportation, the comparison is imperfect because most names (KNTK, AM, DTM, VNOM) are fee-based midstream businesses, not spot-rate cyclicals. The cleanest comp is Dorian LPG (LPG) at 9.2x with a 7.1% yield, another shipping cyclical, and INSW screens in line with that cohort rather than at a discount. The peer median P/E of 25.0x reflects the midstream names' stable cash flows and is not an appropriate anchor for a tanker operator whose earnings can swing from a $135M net loss (FY2021) to $556M net income (FY2023).

Analyst price targets span $56 to $120 with a $91.5 median, a 64-point range that itself captures the binary nature of the situation. The wide dispersion is honest: the upside target assumes sustained elevated rates; the downside assumes normalization. We do not treat the median as a floor here because the elevated estimates underpinning bullish targets are extrapolating a war premium, exactly the guardrail case where rising estimates do not justify a re-rating.

The fortress balance sheet provides genuine downside support: net LTV <7% and 31+ unencumbered vessels mean book value and liquidation value floor the equity even if rates collapse. That argues against a low valuation score. But there is no specific, identifiable mispricing to the upside at current levels that does not depend on the Hormuz disruption persisting. We score Valuation 5.5.

## Competitive Landscape

The tanker market is highly fragmented and competitive, with independent owners, oil majors, state fleets, and traders. In spot trades, differentiation comes down to price, vessel quality and age, and operational reliability. INSW's competitive position is improving at the margin through the Tankers International consolidation, which positions it as an aggregator of commercial scale, and through disciplined fleet renewal that lowers the average age of its ships. The hybrid operating model and deep pool relationships are durable advantages.

The industry backdrop is structurally tight but volatile. Ton-mile demand has been inflated by multiple overlapping disruptions: Russia-Ukraine sanctions lengthening trade routes, Red Sea re-routing around the Cape of Good Hope, Venezuela sanctions enforcement, and the Strait of Hormuz. Counterbalancing this is the swelling orderbook delivering into 2026-2028. INSW's competitive position relative to peers is solid and modestly improving, but it is not a structural share-gainer in a growing market; it is a well-run participant in a commoditized, capacity-cyclical industry. We score Competitive Position 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is spot-rate cyclicality. With ~82% spot exposure, earnings and the variable dividend can collapse in a down-cycle, as they did in FY2021 (net loss of $135M). The current results reflect a war-driven rate spike that will not persist indefinitely.

The Strait of Hormuz situation is both the source of the windfall and the largest discrete risk. Roughly 20% of global oil and ~20M bbl/day move through the Strait. The Q1 2026 traffic collapse came with >30 merchant vessels attacked, a partial Iranian blockade, and possible mining. This is genuinely two-sided. A verified, sustained reopening would compress the rate spike that produced these earnings while relieving broader macro stress. A prolonged closure pushes rates higher in the near term but raises the probability of a global oil demand shock that management explicitly warns "would place considerable strain on global markets." INSW currently has no vessels in the Arabian Gulf, which limits direct asset risk but not rate risk. The diplomatic picture remains unresolved per management's disclosure, so the binary should not be assumed to resolve in either direction.

Supply overhang is a slower-moving but reliable headwind, with deliveries concentrated in 2026-2028. Tax structure is a watch item: INSW relies on the Section 883 exemption and redomiciled vessel-owning subsidiaries to Bermuda (completed December 2025) to navigate OECD Pillar Two; any change in interpretation of the Bermuda shipping exclusion would threaten the near-zero effective tax rate. Vessel-value/impairment risk exists (nine MRs carried ~$43M above appraised market value, and one VLCC ~$1.7M above), though it is modest relative to the balance sheet.

The least-priced idiosyncratic item is governance. On April 9, 2026, INSW adopted a Second Amended and Restated Rights Agreement (a refreshed poison pill), months after buying out CMB.Tech's 50% of the Tankers International manager. CMB.Tech, controlled by the Saverys family, is a noted tanker consolidator. The timing suggests defense against creeping stake accumulation, plausibly by a strategic acquirer, though the specific trigger is not confirmed in a primary source. This cuts both ways: it creates takeover-premium optionality and a governance overhang. It is the single development most likely to move the stock independently of tanker rates.

We score Risk Factors 4.5, reflecting the combination of peak-cycle earnings exposure, the unresolved Hormuz binary, a known supply overhang, and an unresolved governance dynamic, partially offset by the fortress balance sheet that floors downside.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated, two-sided uncertainty rather than directional fear. Near-term ATM IV is 58.4% against 1-year historical volatility of 40.1%, a rich spread of ~18.4pp, and IV sits at the 65th percentile within the current chain. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.5%. The put/call skew is *negative* at -4.7% (call-heavy), and the term structure is flat (58.4% near vs. 59.2% far), so the market is not pricing a one-sided downside crash; it is pricing a binary that could resolve either way. Since the prior snapshot (June 8 to June 12), ATM IV fell sharply from 70.0% to 58.4% as the price declined 3.9%, while the P/C OI ratio rose from 0.42 to 0.81. The IV compression alongside a modest price decline reads as some resolution of near-term event risk, consistent with the broader oil-market de-escalation repricing. Short interest is low at 5.3% of float (2.9 days to cover), so this is not a crowded short and there is no significant squeeze dynamic. The richness of IV argues against buying premium here and is consistent with our view that the stock is a held position into an unresolved binary rather than a directional entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Hormuz disruption persists or escalates short of a full demand shock, keeping VLCC rates elevated through 2026. The 85% payout delivers another year of outsized dividends. The Tankers International consolidation compounds commercial scale and fee capture. A strategic acquirer (potentially CMB.Tech) builds a stake and forces a takeover premium, with the refreshed poison pill serving as leverage for the board to extract value. Fleet-renewal gains continue to pad results. In this scenario the stock revisits and exceeds its $92.66 high, toward the $120 high analyst target.

**Bear Case.** A verified, sustained Hormuz reopening collapses the rate spike, or a prolonged closure tips into a global oil demand shock; either way TCE rates normalize toward the FY2025 base. Next-year EPS of ~$6.42 (or lower) becomes the run-rate, the variable dividend shrinks mechanically, and the swelling orderbook pressures rates further. The stock de-rates toward the $56 low analyst target as the income story fades and the cyclicality reasserts itself. The poison-pill/takeover optionality fails to materialize.

The realistic base case is somewhere between: rates fade gradually from the war peak over 12 months, supplemental dividends decline but remain meaningful, and the balance sheet keeps downside contained. The stock drifts in a wide range as the Hormuz binary resolves and the orderbook supply builds.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Reassessment timeline: the next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release (which will reveal whether VLCC rates held above ~$70K/day or began normalizing), any verified Strait of Hormuz reopening (72+ hours of sustained commercial transit), and any 13D/13G filing or 8-K clarifying the poison-pill trigger.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A confirmed, durable disruption regime keeps VLCC spot TCE above ~$70K/day into Q3 2026 while next-year EPS estimates stabilize above ~$8.00 (i.e., the elevated rate environment proves stickier than consensus assumes), AND the stock trades at a discount to ~6x those normalized earnings.
- A confirmed strategic stake accumulation (13D filing) that puts the company genuinely in play, creating a takeover-premium floor under the equity.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A verified Hormuz reopening combined with Q2 VLCC TCE collapsing back below ~$40K/day, signaling the earnings base has reset and the dividend will contract sharply.
- A material impairment of vessel values or a Section 883 / Bermuda tax-status adverse development that threatens the near-zero effective tax rate.
- Evidence the orderbook overhang is accelerating rate compression (newbuild deliveries running ahead of demolitions with spot rates falling through prior-cycle support).

Existing holders should retain the position for its balance-sheet quality and the genuine, if mean-reverting, capital return, while recognizing that the entry point here is poor (rich IV, peak-cycle earnings, price below recent highs on a fading catalyst). We would not add at current levels and would not establish a new position until either the rate environment proves more durable than consensus or the price reflects a clearer margin of safety against normalized earnings.

## Conclusion

International Seaways is a financially pristine, well-managed tanker operator at or near a cyclical earnings peak engineered by a geopolitical shock. The fortress balance sheet, disciplined buy-low/sell-high fleet strategy, and aggressive variable dividend are genuinely attractive and provide real downside protection. But the Q1 2026 results are a war premium, not a run-rate; the 85% payout means the dividend shrinks mechanically as rates normalize; a swelling VLCC orderbook looms into 2026-2028; and the company sits at the center of an unresolved Strait of Hormuz binary that could resolve in either direction. The refreshed poison pill alongside CMB.Tech proximity adds a governance/takeover wildcard that could be a catalyst or an overhang.

The declining score trajectory (7.1 in mid-May to 5.9 now) reflects accumulating evidence that the rally was rate-driven and the rate spike is the thing most likely to reverse. The current data confirms that read. We rate INSW a HOLD with an overall score of 5.9, classified as an uncertain hold given the genuinely two-sided setup. The quality is real, but the entry is unfavorable and the dominant catalyst is unresolved.
