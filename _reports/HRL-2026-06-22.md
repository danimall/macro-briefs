**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hormel Foods sits at the intersection of a genuine operating recovery and an unusually fragile governance setup. FY2025 was a trough year: GAAP net income fell 41% to $478M and gross margin compressed ~140bps to 15.6% on pork-belly, beef, and nut cost inflation, compounded by ~$234M of largely non-cash impairments (Garudafood $164M, Planters trade name $59M) plus divestiture losses. The headline collapse overstates the deterioration: adjusted EPS fell a far milder 13% to $1.37, and the Q2 FY2026 print (reported May 28) confirmed a recovery, with a sixth consecutive quarter of organic growth, gross margin back to 17.4%, all three segments growing profit, and adjusted EPS up 14% YoY to $0.40. The stock at ~$24, down ~24% from its 52-week high, prices the trough rather than the recovery.

The recovery is shallow and the offsetting risks are concrete. Revenue is structurally flat-to-low-growth (FY25 +1.6% to $12.11B), near-quarter estimates are still being cut (current-quarter EPS revised -5.6% over 90 days), and the company is being run by an interim CEO (returning former CEO Jeffrey Ettinger), an interim CFO (Controller Paul Kuehneman, after CFO Jacinth Smiley separated in February), and a former CEO on a consulting contract, all while executing two overlapping restructuring programs and an ERP migration. The dividend (60th consecutive annual increase, ~4.8% yield) is the genuine anchor, but it was not covered by GAAP EPS in FY25 ($1.16 paid vs $0.87 earned), and the 10-K's explicit reference to potentially "modifying its approach to dividend distributions" is an uncharacteristic signal for a Dividend King.

We rate HRL a HOLD on income grounds. The yield is well-supported by adjusted earnings and ~$845M of operating cash flow, the balance sheet is investment-grade and debt-light, and trade-down to affordable protein (SPAM, Jennie-O) is a modest tailwind in a strained-consumer environment. There is no appreciation catalyst we can underwrite with confidence: flat revenue, falling near-term estimates, a leadership vacuum, a live second Garudafood impairment, and zero open-market insider buying at multi-year lows. Own this stock for the dividend, not for re-rating.

## Company Overview

Hormel Foods is a ~$12.1B-revenue branded packaged-foods and protein company, incorporated in Delaware and public since 1928. It operates three reportable segments:

- **Retail (~62% of sales, $7.46B FY25):** Branded center-store and refrigerated products (SPAM, Jennie-O turkey, Applegate, Planters, Hormel Black Label bacon, Herdez/Mexican foods). Houses the MegaMex (50%) and Joy Topco/Justin's (49%) equity-method investments.
- **Foodservice (~33%, $3.94B FY25):** Customized solutions, branded pepperoni, premium prepared proteins (Austin Blues, Fontanini, Natural Choice) sold to restaurants, healthcare, K-12, hospitality, and c-stores via a direct-selling organization. This is the structurally higher-margin growth engine.
- **International (~6%, $709M FY25):** SPAM exports, China (Jiaxing facility), Brazil, and JVs (Purefoods-Philippines 40%, Garudafood-Indonesia 30%, Okinawa Hormel 26%).

Hormel is a category leader in shelf-stable meats (SPAM), turkey (Jennie-O), and natural/organic meats (Applegate). Customer concentration is meaningful: Walmart is ~16% of gross sales, top five customers ~38%. The competitive set spans national brands, private label (a structural trade-down pressure management names explicitly), and alternative proteins.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is flat; the story is margins, one-time charges, and cash flow quality.**

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 12.46 | 12.11 | 11.92 | 12.11 (+1.6%) |
| Operating income ($M) | 1,313 | 1,072 | 1,068 | 719 (−33%) |
| Net income ($M) | 1,000 | 794 | 805 | 478 (−41%) |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | 1.47 | 0.87 |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | — | — | 1.58 | 1.37 |
| Gross margin | 17.4% | 16.5% | 17.0% | 15.6% |

The FY2025 GAAP decline was driven by two forces. First, commodity inflation (pork belly, beef, nuts) compressed gross margin ~140bps; adjusted operating income fell a more modest 11%. Second, ~$234M of Q4 non-cash impairments (the $164M Garudafood minority-investment writedown plus $71M of trade-name impairments: Planters $59M, Chi-Chi's $3M) drove the net income decline and a 28% effective tax rate, as the impairments were largely non-deductible.

**Q2 FY2026 confirms an operating recovery.** Net sales rose 2.5% (organic +3.3%). Gross margin recovered to 17.4% from 16.7%, with all three segments improving. Adjusted operating income rose 11%; adjusted EPS reached $0.40, with Foodservice segment profit +11%, International +20%, and Retail +13.5%. GAAP EPS fell to $0.29 (−12%) solely because of a $61M loss on the whole-bird turkey divestiture, a portfolio-pruning item rather than operating deterioration. H1 FY26 operating cash flow surged 44% to $528M (from $366M), largely lapping a prior-year inventory build; cash rose to $827M.

**Balance sheet and cash flow.** Hormel carries $2.85B of fixed-rate senior notes (1.7%–4.8% coupons; maturities 2027/2028/2030/2051), with $500M (4.8%, due March 2027) now in current maturities. The $750M revolver is undrawn, the company is in covenant compliance, and low coupons leave debt fair value below carrying value. FY25 FCF was $534M (4.4% margin) on $311M capex, guided down to $260–290M in FY26. Net debt/equity is a modest ~0.36x, current ratio 2.47x. The Altman Z-score of 1.26 ("distress") is a data artifact: equity-to-assets inputs read zero, but equity is actually $7.9B (over half of assets); the Piotroski 5/5 better reflects reality. Hormel is not financially distressed.

The dividend is both the centerpiece and a watch item: $633M paid in FY25, the 60th consecutive annual increase (Dividend King). GAAP EPS ($0.87) did not cover the dividend ($1.16) in FY25; only adjusted EPS and cash flow did. No buybacks in FY25 or H1 FY26. Our financial-health score of 5.5 reflects a debt-light, cash-generative, investment-grade balance sheet against the reality that the GAAP payout ratio above 100% and falling near-term earnings reduce the cushion.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the weakest part of the thesis and the dimension that most reliably sorts winners from losers. Hormel's top line has been flat for four years ($12.46B in FY22 to $12.11B in FY25). FY26 guidance is organic net sales +1–4% and adjusted EPS $1.43–1.51, a recovery off the trough rather than structural acceleration.

The estimate-revision picture is mixed and leans negative on the near term. Current-quarter consensus EPS has been cut to $0.35 from $0.37 ninety days ago (−5.6%), and next-quarter is down 2.3%. The longer-dated numbers are flat-to-marginally-positive (current-year +0.8%, next-year −0.3%). This is the opposite of the sharp upward revision velocity that signals a genuine recovery; consensus is grinding lower on the near term while crediting only a modest improvement. The earnings track record is similarly mixed: three beats in the last four quarters, but the one miss (−15.3% in the September 2025 quarter) was the commodity-driven trough.

What growth exists is concentrated in Foodservice, which is structurally higher-margin, less commoditized, and benefits from a direct-selling advantage. International grew segment profit 20% in Q2 despite its small base. Retail is being deliberately pruned (exiting low-margin private-label snack-nut SKUs), which depresses reported volume but improves mix. The strained-consumer trade-down toward affordable protein and convenience favors SPAM and Jennie-O ground turkey, a real if modest tailwind. A yellow flag: FY25 advertising was cut 9% to $148M (and down ~6–7% in H1 FY26) to protect near-term earnings, which risks long-term volume on leading brands even though management says it intends to re-invest in FY26. We score growth 4.5: the recovery is real but shallow, and near-term estimate momentum is negative.

## Valuation Assessment

HRL trades at ~28.5x trailing GAAP EPS and ~16.5x the midpoint of FY26 adjusted EPS guidance (~$1.47). On forward adjusted earnings the multiple is reasonable, but the trailing optics are distorted by the impairment-laden FY25 base, so the adjusted/forward number is the right anchor.

Peer comparison cuts against the bull case. The Packaged Foods & Meats peer median P/E is ~8.3x (GIS 8.3x, MKC 8.1x, PPC 8.0x), with peers yielding 3.8% median and trading at 38–41% discounts to their 52-week highs (GIS −38%, MKC −40%, PPC −41%). Hormel's ~16.5x forward adjusted multiple is a substantial premium to that group, and its ~4.8% yield is above the peer median but below GIS's 7.2%. The premium is partly justified by brand quality and the Dividend King status, but it is not the kind of mispriced-cheap setup that supports a high valuation score. In this regime a low headline multiple is anti-predictive (names that screen cheap have underperformed), and Hormel does not even screen cheap against its peer cohort.

Analyst targets bracket a narrow range: low $25, mean $26.5, median $26.0, high $30, against a current ~$24. That is roughly 9% upside to the mean and ~24% to the high. We do not treat these as floors: with near-term estimates falling, consensus targets are more likely to drift down than up. The valuation supports the dividend and modest mean-reversion, not a re-rating. We score valuation 4.5: fairly-to-slightly-richly valued versus a flat-growth peer group, with no specific mispricing catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

Hormel's moat rests on brand identification and breadth in shelf-stable meats (SPAM), turkey (Jennie-O), and natural/organic (Applegate). The trajectory, which matters more than the level, is roughly stable with pockets of erosion. The defining industry dynamic is consumer trade-down to private label and value, which Hormel is meeting by leaning into affordable protein and convenience while exiting lower-margin SKUs. Foodservice shows pricing power and broad-based organic growth via its direct-selling organization, and is structurally less exposed to private-label substitution than Retail.

Portfolio simplification is rational and ongoing: selling 51% of Justin's ($77.3M, $23.5M gain), exiting whole-bird turkey while keeping the Jennie-O brand and value-add ($61.2M proceeds, $61M loss), and shedding sow operations. These moves shift mix toward higher-margin branded products. Offsetting this, the 9% advertising cut on leading brands is a competitive negative if sustained, and GLP-1 weight-loss drugs are a named sector-wide overhang on packaged-food demand, partially mitigated by Hormel's protein-forward, convenience-tilted mix. We score competitive position 4.5: a category leader with a defensible but not widening moat, facing structural trade-down pressure and under-investing in brand support near-term.

## Risk Assessment

**Leadership vacuum (the single largest qualitative risk).** Hormel is running on an interim CEO, an interim CFO, and a former CEO (Jim Snee) on a consulting agreement to April 2027. President John Ghingo is the apparent heir (large February 2026 equity grant), but a permanent CEO appointment was not confirmed in available filings. The 8-K stream shows roughly a dozen Item 5.02 leadership-change filings since 2023, unusual churn for a company historically prized for continuity, and occurring alongside two restructuring programs and an ERP migration.

**Second Garudafood impairment is live.** After the $164M FY25 writedown, the remaining carrying value ($247M as of April 2026) still sits above its market value ($224M). Management declined a further OTTI on strategic-intent grounds but explicitly flagged the possibility. International goodwill ($259M) and $683M of indefinite-lived intangibles (Justin's, remaining Planters) are flagged at heightened impairment risk.

**Commodity input volatility** (pork belly, beef, nuts, feed grains) remains the dominant earnings driver; market-based pricing lags cost moves. FY26 assumes beef/nuts stay elevated with pork moderating, a partial, uneven tailwind.

**Customer concentration:** Walmart ~16%, top five ~38% of gross sales, one customer >10% of receivables.

**Operational and biological:** HPAI detected in the turkey supply chain in FY24 and FY25 (directly relevant to Jennie-O); a Q4 FY25 Little Rock peanut-butter plant fire and a Q4 FY25 Class 1 chicken recall both hit results; Austin, MN is a single point of failure.

**Legal:** In re Turkey Antitrust Litigation, first trial (direct purchasers) set for October 8, 2026; no liability recorded, but prior settlements occurred in FY24–FY25. Brazilian ICMS disputes outstanding.

**Dividend signal:** GAAP EPS did not cover the dividend in FY25, and the 10-K's explicit reference to potentially modifying the dividend approach is uncharacteristically direct for a 60-year increaser.

Several of these risks are partly priced into a ~$24 stock down 24% from its high; the leadership transition and the Garudafood second-shoe are the items least reflected. We score risk 5.0: a stable, defensive business model offset by a genuine governance overhang and a live impairment catalyst.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 26.8% sits modestly below 1-year historical volatility of 29.9% (cheap, −3.1% spread), with IV in the 25th percentile of its own chain. The put/call volume ratio is low at 0.22 and OI ratio 0.35, suggesting no aggressive bearish positioning. Near-dated skew shows mild elevated put demand (6.4%), but the 6-month tenor inverts to −4.6% (call-leaning), consistent with a market hedging near-term noise (the "noisy" Q3 management flagged on fuel/logistics/commodity headwinds) while pricing modest longer-term upside. The flat term structure (near 26.8% ≈ far 25.1%) indicates uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated on a single binary. The options market is not pricing a major dislocation in either direction, consistent with our HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($28–30).** The Q2 operating inflection is durable: gross margin holds near 17.4%, Foodservice and International keep compounding segment profit, pork costs moderate as guided, and FY26 adjusted EPS lands at the top of the $1.43–1.51 range. A permanent CEO is named (Ghingo), removing the governance overhang, and the dividend streak continues uninterrupted. On normalizing earnings and a re-rating toward the high analyst target, the stock reaches $28–30, roughly 16–24% upside, with the ~4.8% yield on top.

**Bear Case ($19–20).** A second Garudafood impairment lands, beef/nut costs stay elevated longer than guided, trade-down accelerates and pressures Retail volume more than mix improvement can offset, and the leadership transition drags on with continued C-suite churn. Near-term estimates keep falling, the FY26 guide is trimmed, and the dividend-coverage question forces management to slow the dividend-growth cadence. The stock retests the 52-week low near $19.70, roughly 18% downside, with the income partially cushioning total return.

**Base Case ($25–26).** Modest organic growth and margin recovery deliver FY26 adjusted EPS near the midpoint, the dividend rises again, but flat revenue and the leadership/Garudafood overhangs cap re-rating. The stock drifts toward the analyst mean of ~$26, ~9% upside, with most of the total return coming from the dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD on income grounds. The thesis is dividend yield (~4.8%) plus stable cash flow, not capital appreciation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A permanent CEO appointment (Ghingo or otherwise) that removes the governance overhang, combined with current-quarter estimate revisions turning positive.
- Two consecutive quarters of gross margin sustained at/above 17.5% with organic growth at the high end of the 1–4% guide, confirming the recovery is durable rather than a single-quarter lap effect.
- Open-market insider buying by C-suite executives at these levels, currently conspicuously absent.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second Garudafood impairment, or impairment of the International goodwill / indefinite-lived intangibles, signaling the portfolio quality is worse than priced.
- A dividend cut or a break in the 60-year increase streak (the 10-K's "modifying its approach" language is the early warning).
- FY26 adjusted EPS guidance cut below $1.43, or organic growth turning negative as trade-down overwhelms mix.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly print (Q3 FY26, management already flagged as "noisy") and the October 8, 2026 Turkey Antitrust trial are the natural checkpoints. The leadership transition resolution is the key non-scheduled catalyst to monitor.

## Conclusion

Hormel is a high-quality, defensive franchise emerging from a commodity-and-impairment-driven trough, with an operating recovery now in evidence. The ~4.8% dividend yield, backed by an investment-grade, debt-light balance sheet and ~$845M of operating cash flow, is the durable reason to own the stock. But the recovery is shallow against flat revenue, near-term estimates are still being cut, the company is being run on interim leadership with no insider conviction at multi-year lows, the GAAP payout ratio exceeds 100%, and a second Garudafood impairment is a live near-term risk. The trajectory of our prior assessments (declining scores, oscillating between HOLD and AVOID) reflects this push-pull; the Q2 operating data modestly improves the picture relative to the May AVOID reads, justifying a HOLD rather than further deterioration, but it does not establish an appreciation case we can underwrite. We recommend HOLD with an income rationale: own it for the yield and the trough-pricing cushion, not for re-rating, and reassess on a permanent CEO appointment and positive estimate-revision momentum.
