## Executive Summary

Unilever is delivering its best organic growth in more than a decade while its shares trade 16.2% below the February 2026 high of $74.98. Underlying sales growth accelerated from 3.5% in FY2025 to 3.8% in Q1 2026 to 5.8% in Q2 2026, H1 2026 turnover reached €25.6bn, and management raised FY2026 guidance to 4-6% from a prior 3-5% frame on 28 July. The shares rose roughly 9% on that print and gave the entire move back within eight sessions, closing at $62.86 on 10 August versus $61.37 the day before results. That retracement is the central fact to explain, and it is not explained by the operating numbers.

The market is discounting the 31 March 2026 agreement to combine Unilever's Foods business with McCormick. Per McCormick's Form 10-Q for the quarter ended 31 May 2026, Unilever PLC receives a one-time $15.7bn cash payment and up to ~9.9% of the combined company subject to a one-year lock-up; Unilever's *shareholders* receive ~55.1% directly via distribution, and McCormick's existing holders retain ~35.0%. Press coverage describing "Unilever shareholders retaining 65%" sums those two distinct items. Only the cash and the ≤9.9% stake accrue to Unilever PLC as an entity. The 55.1% is a distribution of a levered food equity in exchange for a division that generated €12.9bn of FY2025 turnover, and McCormick is funding the cash consideration with a $15.7bn bridge facility plus a $2.0bn three-year term loan against $7.6bn of total equity. Unilever holders are being asked to accept execution and de-leveraging risk at the counterparty.

Valuation offers no compelling margin of safety on either side. On the reported $3.04 per-ADR earnings basis the stock trades near 20.7x; on the aggregator's FY2025 IFRS EPS of €4.32 it appears to trade at 12.4x, but that number is not usable. FY2025 net income of €9,469m *exceeds* FY2025 operating income of €9,037m, and roughly 95% of the year-on-year earnings jump (€3,725m of a €3,933m swing) came from below the operating line, almost certainly tied to the December 2025 Magnum Ice Cream demerger. Cash-flow multiples are the safe anchor: EV/FCF ≈ 19.5x and P/FCF ≈ 16x on TTM free cash flow of €7,389m, with a 3.30% dividend yield covered roughly two-to-one by free cash flow. That is a fair price for a 4-6% organic grower with a 20.0% underlying operating margin, not a mispricing. We rate the shares **HOLD** on an income basis: hold for the covered yield and improving underlying trajectory, and wait for the deal perimeter, the use of proceeds, and the CMA outcome to resolve before committing new capital.

## Company Overview

Unilever is a UK-domiciled consumer packaged-goods group operating four reported business groups. FY2025 turnover of €50,503m breaks down as Personal Care €13,161m, Foods €12,929m, Beauty & Wellbeing €12,848m and Home Care €11,565m. The segment build ties exactly to reported turnover, which provides reasonable confidence in the underlying data.

Two structural transactions define the equity story. The Magnum Ice Cream Company was demerged in December 2025 and listed in London, Amsterdam and New York, with Unilever retaining an unquantified residual stake. On 31 March 2026 Unilever agreed to combine Foods with McCormick, excluding its foods businesses in India, Nepal and Portugal, plus Lifestyle & Nutrition, Buavita, and Lipton Ready-to-Drink. Those assets stay with Unilever and are materially under-reported. The RemainCo is therefore not €37.6bn of pure HPC (50,503 − 12,929); it is that figure plus a retained foods tail whose revenue Unilever has not disclosed at the perimeter level. The exact carve-out economics are unknown to us.

Unilever is a foreign private issuer filing Form 20-F and Form 6-K. There is no 10-K, no 10-Q, no proxy statement and no Form 4 insider data. The FY2025 20-F (filed 12 March 2026) was not retrievable, so Unilever's own risk factors, MD&A and financial-statement notes are unread. All Unilever financials in this report come from a third-party aggregation of the IFRS accounts. The most decision-relevant legal detail on the Foods transaction comes from McCormick's own SEC filings, which is a primary source for the deal structure but a counterparty view of it.

The revenue series contains a comparability break. FY2022's €60,073m appears to include Ice Cream while FY2023-25 look restated to continuing operations excluding it; Foods remains in the FY2025 segments, consistent with the McCormick deal being announced only in March 2026 and not yet closed. Multi-year revenue CAGRs across this series are meaningless. The usable growth signal is management's underlying measure.

**Earnings quality.** Net income rose €3,725m year-on-year while operating income rose €208m. Approximately 95% of the earnings improvement therefore originated below the operating line, and the leading explanation is a gain or discontinued-operations profit associated with the December 2025 Magnum demerger. Alternative explanations we cannot exclude include a large one-off tax item, gains on other brand disposals, or pension remeasurement. Any screen or valuation anchored on FY2025 IFRS EPS of €4.32 materially overstates cheapness. A non-cash gain of that character does not distort revenue, operating cash flow, free cash flow, or the balance sheet, so the cash-flow-based valuation and leverage read below stand independently.

**Margins.** Reported operating margin improved from 16.82% (FY2024) to 17.89% (FY2025) to 18.10% (TTM June 2026). Management's underlying operating margin was 20.0% in FY2025, up 60bps, with gross margin 46.9%, up 20bps. The ~210bp gap between reported and underlying is presumably restructuring, separation and amortisation; we could not read the reconciliation, so its composition is unverified. The direction of both measures is favourable.

**Cash generation.** Free cash flow has held in a €5.8-8.2bn band for five years and reached €7,389m on a TTM basis with a 14.6% FCF margin. Capex is light at €1.2-1.4bn, or 2.5-2.8% of turnover. This is the most reliable part of the financial picture and it did not deteriorate while reported net income spiked, which is itself consistent with the spike being non-cash.

**Leverage.** Net debt rose from €23,216m at 31 December 2025 to €26,062m at 30 June 2026, an increase of €2,846m in six months: gross debt up €3,832m, cash up €986m. Against TTM FCF of €7,389m that is net debt/FCF of 3.5x. We do not have reported EBITDA and decline to present a net debt/EBITDA ratio; an operating-income-plus-estimated-D&A proxy would be speculative. Net debt/FCF of 3.5x is manageable for a global staples business, but the group is adding leverage while raising the dividend and executing two carve-outs. Plausible drivers include pre-positioning liquidity ahead of the Foods separation, FX translation on non-euro debt, seasonal working capital, or buyback execution. We could not identify them from filings, and we have not annualised H1 cash flow.

**Dividend.** There is an unresolved conflict in the data. The quoted yield is 3.30%, implying roughly $2.08 per ADR annually and a payout of about 52% of TTM free cash flow. The aggregator's TTM dividend per share of €2.201 converts to roughly $2.58 at 1.17 USD/EUR, a 4.1% yield and a 65% FCF payout. We use the quoted 3.30% for return math as the more conservative figure and flag the discrepancy. On either basis the dividend is comfortably covered and was raised 10.5% in FY2025 (€1.790 to €1.977).

**Earnings-surprise record.** The supplied beat/miss table covers 2009-2010 only (2 beats, 2 misses). It provides no usable calibration for current consensus and we place no weight on it.

**Financial Health: 6.0.** Robust and stable free cash flow with light capex and a well-covered dividend, offset by €26.1bn of net debt that grew €2.85bn in six months, undisclosed separation and dis-synergy costs ahead, and the fact that every figure above is third-party aggregated rather than primary-filing verified.

## Growth Analysis

The underlying trajectory is the strongest element of the case. Underlying sales growth of 3.5% in FY2025 (volume +1.5%, price +2.0%) accelerated to 3.8% in Q1 2026 against a company-compiled consensus of 3.6%, then to 5.8% in Q2 2026, described across multiple outlets as the best quarterly performance in over a decade. Power Brands grew 4.3% in FY2025, ahead of the group, indicating the concentration strategy is working at the brand level.

Three qualifications matter.

First, reported and underlying results diverge sharply. Q1 2026 turnover of €12.6bn fell 3.3% at reported rates while underlying growth was +3.8%. H1 2026 turnover of €25.6bn against FY2025's €50,503m implies reported euro turnover roughly flat to slightly down year-on-year. A dollar-based ADR holder earns the translated reported result, not the constant-currency underlying figure. The gap between a 4-6% underlying guide and flat reported euros is a persistent drag on headline per-ADR earnings.

Growth quality is partly cost-recovery rather than share gain. Unilever told Reuters in April it would raise prices "in small doses" to offset higher-than-expected costs driven by the Iran conflict, and McCormick's Q2 FY2026 10-Q confirms higher freight and commodity costs from the same source. We could not locate Unilever's Q2 2026 volume/price decomposition, so we cannot quantify how much of the 5.8% is price. Coverage does describe H1 growth as volume-led in emerging markets, so both effects are operating, but the absence of the split is a real limitation on judging growth durability.

Growth is also concentrated in emerging markets, explicitly cited as the driver in both Q1 and Q2. That is a genuine structural advantage; Unilever's EM distribution depth is difficult to replicate, but it imports currency, pricing-power and political risk that the underlying measure deliberately strips out. It is also the segment most exposed to the dollar and EM funding conditions.

Post-transaction, the growth mix improves on paper: HPC categories grow faster and carry higher margins than packaged food, and McCormick's own Q2 FY2026 Consumer volume/mix of −1.9% with cited price elasticity in the Americas illustrates the demand environment Unilever is stepping away from. Against that, the RemainCo is a smaller company (~€37.6bn plus the retained foods tail versus €50.5bn) with less absorption of central cost.

**Growth Prospects: 6.0.** Accelerating underlying growth with raised guidance is real and confirmed by two consecutive prints. It is capped by FX-driven flat reported revenue, an unquantified price component, and EM concentration.

## Valuation Assessment

Derived market capitalisation: net debt of €26,062m divided by net-debt-per-share of −€11.92 implies approximately 2,187m shares. At $62.86 that is roughly $137.5bn, cross-checked against the aggregator's P/S of 2.33 on €50,620m TTM turnover (€117.9bn ≈ $138bn at 1.17). Enterprise value is therefore approximately €144bn.

| P/FCF (TTM FCF €7,389m) | ~16x |
| EV/FCF | ~19.5x |

| Net debt/FCF | 3.5x |

Cross-checking the $3.04 EPS: applying the FY2025 underlying operating margin of 20.0% to €50,503m of turnover gives €10.1bn of underlying operating profit; deducting roughly €1.0-1.1bn of net finance costs, 25-26% tax and €0.7-0.9bn of minority interests (largely Hindustan Unilever) gives approximately €2.70 per share, or about $3.16. The reported per-ADR figure is therefore consistent with underlying attributable earnings, and 20-21x is the right multiple to work from.

Against staples peers that places Unilever roughly mid-pack. [unverified — no peer benchmark data available in Phase 1] There is no growth-adjusted discount to exploit. A 4-6% organic grower with a 20% underlying margin at 20-21x underlying earnings and 16x free cash flow is priced approximately correctly.

The transaction creates the only genuine optionality. Foods is valued at $44.8bn including debt, against a total Unilever enterprise value of roughly $169bn ($137.5bn market cap plus ~€26.1bn/~$30.5bn net debt), so approximately 26% of EV for a division representing 25.6% of turnover at below-group margins. That is a full price for the asset. Aggregating what flows to Unilever holders: $15.7bn cash to Unilever PLC (11.4% of market cap), up to ~9.9% of the combined company (perhaps $4-5bn depending on post-deal leverage), and 55.1% of the combined equity distributed directly to shareholders (perhaps $24-25bn, assuming combined equity value of roughly $45bn after $21bn of net debt against the $65.8bn including-debt figure). That aggregate of roughly $45bn compares to Foods contributing perhaps 20-22% of group profit, arithmetic that looks accretive on paper.

Three things block converting that arithmetic into a valuation upgrade. The MKC equity component is not cash and its quality is questionable: McCormick's 5.2m outstanding options at a $75.16 weighted-average strike were entirely underwater at 31 May 2026, and 2026 RSU and LTPP grant-date fair values fell to $63.37 and $65.02 from $74.76 and $86.92 respectively. The acquirer's stock has de-rated materially, and post-close leverage on roughly $20bn of combined revenue looks high for a food company. Second, Unilever has disclosed no intended use for the $15.7bn, which arrives only at closing, expected mid-2027 on press reporting rather than a filed date. Applied to debt, net debt falls to roughly €12.7bn; applied to buybacks at current prices it retires roughly 11% of shares. Those are very different per-share outcomes and we have no basis to assume either. Third, separation and dis-synergy costs for a carve-out of this scale will be substantial and are undisclosed.

Consensus price targets run $69.81 low, $73.70 mean, $77.00 high; the entire range sits above spot, implying 11% to 22% upside. On a staples name at 20-21x underlying earnings, analyst targets are not independent confirmation; they and the price reflect the same set of assumptions about the transaction closing on current terms. Coverage sentiment is also degrading (one strong buy moved to hold; hold count rose from 1 to 2).

**Valuation: 5.5.** Fair, with unquantifiable transaction optionality on one side and undisclosed separation costs plus counterparty-equity quality risk on the other.

## Competitive Landscape

Unilever's advantages are brand scale in high-frequency personal-care categories and emerging-market distribution depth. Power Brands growing 4.3% against 3.5% group underlying growth is evidence that concentration is working. Gross margin of 46.9% and underlying operating margin of 20.0%, both expanding, indicate pricing power is holding through an input-cost shock, a meaningful test given the freight and commodity inflation following the February 2026 escalation in the Middle East.

FY2025 reported segment revenue declined in every business group relative to FY2024 (Beauty & Wellbeing €13,157m→€12,848m, −2.3%; Personal Care €13,618m→€13,161m, −3.4%; Home Care €12,352m→€11,565m, −6.4%; Foods €13,352m→€12,929m, −3.2%), which is largely currency and perimeter rather than share loss, but it does mean the reported franchise is not growing in euro terms.

The structural context is a sector-wide break-up wave: Mars/Kellanova, Ferrero/WK Kellogg, the Kraft Heinz split, and Unilever's two separations. Investors have stopped rewarding these announcements automatically; Unilever's shares fell £3.29 within 24 hours of the McCormick announcement and had not recovered by mid-May. The conglomerate-discount argument no longer earns a reflexive re-rating; the burden has shifted to demonstrated post-separation margin delivery. The Magnum precedent cuts both ways: standalone Magnum delivered H1 2026 core earnings of €880m, +3.2%, above consensus on post-spin cost cuts, suggesting margin improvement Unilever ownership had not delivered, while simultaneously de-rating enough to draw reported early-stage private-equity interest from Blackstone and CD&R within six months of listing.

**Competitive Position: 6.0.** Improving underlying trajectory and demonstrated pricing power, offset by reported segment contraction and the fact that the strongest evidence of the moat comes from management's own non-GAAP framing, which we could not corroborate from the 20-F.

## Risk Assessment

**Deal-completion risk is live and asymmetric.** Closing requires McCormick shareholder approval of both a share issuance and a charter amendment, HSR expiry, other antitrust and foreign-investment clearances, and S-4 effectiveness. The UK CMA opened a third-party consultation around 22 July 2026, unresolved and relevant given overlapping condiments and sauces positions. McCormick's own risk factors disclose that the stockholders' agreement, transition services agreement, tax matters agreement and certain IP and asset purchase agreements remain unnegotiated and may differ materially from current descriptions. A deal whose ancillary agreements are unwritten roughly a year before expected close carries real re-cut risk. The $420m break fee flows from McCormick to Unilever, but at approximately 0.3% of market cap it is not meaningful compensation for a collapse.

**Counterparty execution risk.** Because Unilever holders receive 55.1% of the combined company and Unilever PLC up to 9.9%, most of the delivered value depends on McCormick integrating and de-levering. McCormick carried $1,326.6m of short-term borrowings and $3,597.4m of long-term debt on $7,573.3m of equity at 31 May 2026 before adding $15.7bn of acquisition debt. Its own filings flag the risk of failing to manage that debt.

**Input-cost shock.** McCormick's Q2 FY2026 10-Q added a new risk factor covering the 28 February 2026 US/Israel strikes on Iran and the resulting energy, freight and commodity cost cascade, and reported higher freight and commodity costs partially offsetting gross-margin gains. Unilever is directly exposed through the same channels. This is sector-wide, limiting relative damage but pressuring absolute margins.

**Governance and strategic-commitment risk.** CEO Hein Schumacher departed after 18 months and, per reporting of Terry Smith's investor letter, had intended to retain Foods. Fundsmith sold its entire Unilever holding and in a 9 July 2026 letter alleged that management had confirmed after the ice cream demerger that disposals were finished. Unilever's response emphasised that the decision was unanimous and that under UK rules Board approval sufficed and no shareholder vote was required. Nelson Peltz is both a major shareholder and a sitting director. We do not infer motive and we treat Smith's account of private conversations as a disputed allegation from a former holder with an incentive to justify an exit. A board that can restructure the portfolio twice in 18 months without a shareholder ballot makes the long-term compounding case harder to underwrite, which is plausibly part of why the shares de-rated on announcement and have not recovered.

**Verification gaps that constrain confidence.** Unilever's 20-F is unread, so no Unilever-specific risk factor changes can be identified. No Altman Z-Score, Piotroski F-Score, or SEC XBRL series exists for this issuer. There is no insider-transaction data of any kind, neither buying nor selling, and its absence should not be read as a signal in either direction. The retained Magnum stake is unquantified. H1 2026 profit-and-loss detail below turnover is unavailable.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 23.1% sits essentially at the 24.1% one-year realised level (spread −1.0%), with an IV percentile of 34% within the current chain, so options are neither rich nor cheap. The term structure is in backwardation (23.1% at one week versus 19.7% at six months), which typically signals a near-dated event, though the nearest expiry is two days out and the implied move is only ±1.9%. Near-term put/call skew of 3.4% indicates elevated put demand, but the six-month tenor shows −2.7% skew and put/call open interest of 0.78 is call-tilted overall. Put/call volume of 0.47 is unremarkable. Short interest is negligible at 1.96m shares and 0.3 days to cover.

The composite read: the options market is not pricing a transaction break or a fundamental dislocation. It shows mild near-dated hedging against a longer-dated tilt toward upside participation. That is consistent with our HOLD: no informed positioning contradicts the fundamental view in either direction, and the absence of a bearish options signal argues against treating the post-earnings retracement as evidence of something the market knows and we do not.

**Risk Factors: 4.8.** The deal completion binary, the unnegotiated ancillary agreements, the live CMA review, and the counterparty-leverage exposure are material and only partly reflected in the current multiple. The verification gaps compound the uncertainty.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $76 (+20.9%)

Underlying growth sustains at the upper half of the 4-6% guide as volume-led emerging-market momentum continues and price contribution normalises. FY2026 underlying operating margin extends past 20.0% as Power Brand mix and post-separation cost discipline take hold. The McCormick transaction closes mid-2027 on current terms; Unilever directs a majority of the $15.7bn to buybacks, retiring a meaningful share count at a depressed multiple; and the RemainCo HPC business re-rates toward 23-24x on the higher-quality mix. McCormick's equity recovers from its de-rated level, making the 55.1% distribution worth more than the market currently implies. Reported euro revenue turns positive as FX headwinds abate. That combination takes the ADR back toward the February high and the consensus high target of $77.

### Bear Case — $52 (−17.3%)

The CMA or another regulator forces a perimeter re-cut, or McCormick shareholders balk at the charter amendment and share issuance, leaving Unilever with a $420m break fee and a Foods business that has been marketed, destabilised and separated internally for 15 months. Emerging-market currency weakness compounds already-negative reported revenue translation. The Iran-driven input-cost shock persists, forcing pricing that erodes volume as the underlying growth mix shifts from volume to price and then decelerates toward 2-3%. The multiple compresses to 16-17x on roughly $3.10 of underlying EPS. Net debt continues to build ahead of a delayed cash inflow. At $52 the shares would be near the 52-week low of $54.75, with the dividend yield near 4% providing the floor.

### Base Case — $68 (+8.2%)

Approximately 20x roughly $3.40 of FY2027 underlying per-ADR earnings, reflecting 4-5% underlying growth, modest margin expansion, and continued FX drag on the reported line. The transaction closes broadly on current terms with proceeds split between deleveraging and returns, delivering neither the buyback windfall of the bull case nor the disruption of the bear case. Total return of roughly 11.5% including the 3.30% dividend.

Probability-weighting base at 50%, bull at 25% and bear at 25% gives approximately +5% expected price return plus the 3.30% yield, or roughly 8% expected total return over twelve months. That is an approximately fair risk-adjusted outcome, adequate for existing holders and insufficient asymmetry for new capital given the unresolved deal binary, the impaired management-credibility read, and consumer staples' historically thin selection edge.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income basis). Reassessment horizon: 12 months, with a hard checkpoint at the Q3 2026 trading update (late October 2026).**

- **Base case target: $68** — 20x approximately $3.40 FY2027 underlying per-ADR EPS. Upside 8.2% from $62.86; total return roughly 11.5% including dividends.
- **Bull case target: $76** — upside 20.9%.
- **Bear case target: $52** — downside 17.3%.
- **Income support:** 3.30% quoted yield, covered at roughly 52% of TTM free cash flow (65% on the higher aggregator dividend figure). Dividend was raised 10.5% in FY2025.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Unilever publicly commits a majority of the $15.7bn to buybacks or a special distribution, quantifying the per-share effect. At current prices, an $8bn+ buyback commitment would be worth roughly 6% of shares and would materially change the base case.
2. Q3 2026 underlying sales growth of 4.5% or better with a *volume* contribution above 2%, confirming the Q2 acceleration is share-driven rather than price pass-through, alongside a reaffirmed or raised 4-6% guide.
3. Price weakness to $55-57 (near the 52-week low of $54.75) with fundamentals intact, which would put the shares at roughly 17-18x underlying earnings and a 3.7-3.8% yield.
4. CMA clearance plus execution of the outstanding stockholders', tax matters and transition services agreements, removing the largest re-cut risk.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Deal termination, or a re-cut that reduces the $15.7bn cash consideration or expands the excluded perimeter in Unilever's disfavour.
2. Underlying sales growth decelerating below 3.5% in any quarter, or the FY2026 4-6% guide being lowered.
3. Net debt rising above €28bn at the 31 December 2026 balance-sheet date without an identified operating or transactional cause, which would push net debt/FCF near 4x while the cash inflow remains a year away.
4. Underlying operating margin declining year-on-year in the FY2026 result, indicating the Iran-driven cost shock is no longer recoverable through price.

**Next natural reassessment points:** the Q3 2026 trading update (late October 2026); McCormick's Form S-4 filing and the CMA's Phase 1 decision, either of which will materially reduce the deal-uncertainty discount; and the FY2026 results in February 2027, which should carry the first RemainCo perimeter and separation-cost disclosure.

## Conclusion

Unilever presents an unusually clean separation between operating performance and equity outcome. The operations are improving on every underlying measure the company reports: underlying sales growth accelerated to 5.8% in Q2 2026, guidance was raised to 4-6%, underlying operating margin reached 20.0% and expanded 60bps, and free cash flow of €7,389m covers a 3.30% dividend roughly twice over. Yet the shares surrendered the entire 9% post-results gain within eight sessions and sit 16.2% below the February high.

The reason is structural rather than operational. Investors are being asked to accept a portfolio outcome (55.1% of a levered mid-cap food company whose own equity has de-rated, plus a $15.7bn cash payment arriving mid-2027 with no disclosed use, plus up to 9.9% of the combined entity locked up for a year) in exchange for a Foods division that was priced fully at $44.8bn including debt. Layered on that is a governance record of two portfolio restructurings in 18 months, a CEO change that coincided with a strategic reversal, the exit of a marquee long-only holder alleging reversed assurances, and no shareholder vote at any point. Those factors weigh on our view of management quality despite genuinely good operating delivery, and are why we cannot underwrite the re-rating case that the analyst target range of $69.81-$77.00 implies.

At approximately 20-21x underlying earnings, 16x free cash flow and 19.5x EV/FCF, the price is fair. Probability-weighted expected total return of roughly 8% over twelve months justifies holding for the yield and the improving underlying trajectory, but not committing new capital ahead of the CMA decision, the S-4, and a stated use of proceeds. **HOLD**, with $55-57 the price at which the risk/reward would warrant adding.
