**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Globalstar stopped being a fundamental equity story on April 13, 2026, when it signed a definitive Agreement and Plan of Merger to be acquired by Amazon.com for $90.00 per share in cash, or Amazon stock at holder election subject to a 40% cash-cap proration, valuing the company at roughly $11.6 billion. The same day, Thermo, the controlling shareholder holding 57.6% of the common, delivered a written consent adopting the merger, so shareholder approval is already locked. This converts GSAT from a niche satellite operator into a merger-arbitrage instrument. The relevant analysis is no longer about subscriber growth, satellite economics, or the Apple relationship in isolation; it is about the probability and timing of deal completion.

At $78.93, the stock trades roughly 14% below the $90 headline offer. That gross spread reflects three discrete risks: (a) regulatory-approval risk across HSR antitrust, foreign-investment review, and satellite/telecom clearances in multiple jurisdictions on a timeline that extends to April 2027 and can be pushed to April 2028; (b) an up-to-$110 million (~$0.85/share) downward price adjustment triggered if Globalstar misses amended operational milestones tied to the HIBLEO-4 satellite launches; and (c) under 40% cash proration, most non-electing holders will receive Amazon stock rather than the full $90 cash unless they win the cash election. Amazon owes a $592.1 million reverse termination fee if the deal is killed on regulatory grounds versus a $419.8 million fee GSAT would owe in certain break scenarios; the asymmetry signals Amazon's commitment.

The underlying business does not support the price on standalone fundamentals. GSAT generated a GAAP net loss of $8.7 million in 2025 on $273 million of revenue, carries a $2.15 billion accumulated deficit, screens at an Altman Z-score of 0.6 (distress zone) and 5.54x debt/equity, and derives 63% of revenue from a single customer (Apple). If the deal breaks, the stock would likely re-rate well below current levels toward pre-announcement pricing. Our recommendation is HOLD with an uncertain rationale: this is a defined-outcome arbitrage suited to dedicated arb capital that can underwrite the regulatory path, not a conventional buy-and-hold position. The spread offers a positive expected return only if you assign high confidence to close, and the downside on a break is severe.

## Company Overview

Globalstar operates a Low-Earth-Orbit (LEO) Mobile Satellite Services network delivering voice and data connectivity in areas terrestrial networks do not reach. It serves approximately 797,000 MSS subscribers as of Q1 2026. The company was transformed by its relationship with Apple, which since November 2022 has used Globalstar's network to power the iPhone's Emergency SOS and satellite-messaging features under a wholesale capacity arrangement.

FY2025 revenue of $273.0 million breaks down as follows: wholesale capacity services (Apple) $172.7 million (63% of revenue, rising to 66% in Q1 2026); SPOT consumer devices $37.3 million (declining under competitive pressure from Garmin inReach and others); commercial IoT $27.3 million (growing, with record activations and a new RM200M two-way module); Duplex $15.2 million (being wound down); and government/other approximately $5 million (growing off a small base).

Key assets include globally harmonized ITU spectrum (L/S/C-band), Band 53/n53 terrestrial spectrum (11.5 MHz across roughly 12 billion MHz-POPs in 12 countries), and the XCOM/Virewirx wireless IP (RAN and peer-to-peer technology) acquired in January 2026. The competitive moat rests on that spectrum position plus the established Apple relationship. Competition is intensifying from SpaceX Starlink direct-to-cell, AST SpaceMobile, Amazon's own Leo constellation, Iridium, Viasat/Inmarsat, and ORBCOMM.

The corporate governance structure is a controlled company. Thermo, controlled by Executive Chairman James Monroe III, holds 57.6% of the common and $136.7 million of the Series A Preferred. CEO Dr. Paul E. Jacobs, formerly Qualcomm's CEO, also controlled XCOM/Virewirx, the IP vendor Globalstar licensed and then acquired, a related-party dynamic policed by a Strategic Review Committee. These governance features matter far less now that the deal is locked by Thermo's written consent.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue growth has been strong and Apple-driven: $148.5 million (2022), $223.8 million (2023), $250.3 million (2024), $273.0 million (2025, +9% YoY), with Q1 2026 up 17% to $70.1 million. The problem is that top-line growth has not translated into GAAP profitability. Net loss was $8.7 million in 2025 and $63.2 million in 2024, with a Q1 2026 net loss of $17.4 million. Operating income was only $7.4 million in 2025, an operating margin of roughly 2.7%. The accumulated deficit stands at $2.15 billion, the residue of decades of losses.

The headline cash flow figure is a financing artifact, not operating strength. Operating cash flow of $621.7 million in 2025 and $439.2 million in 2024 is dominated by Apple's Infrastructure Prepayment ($430.6 million received in 2025) recorded as deferred revenue. This is customer prepayment for a satellite buildout, not recurring operating cash. It flows straight through to capex: investing outflows were negative $550.4 million in 2025, and total deferred revenue reached $891.7 million in Q1 2026. Any naive screen showing "OCF/revenue above 200%" badly misreads the situation; underlying operations still lose money.

The balance sheet drives the distressed screening scores. The Altman Z-score of 0.6 and debt/equity of 5.54 reflect the special-purpose-entity/prepayment structure and the accumulated deficit rather than imminent bankruptcy risk. Apple's prepayments and the guaranteed funding pipeline fund the capex program. Debt principal was $403.8 million in Q1 2026, comprising the 2024 Debt Repayment ($221.6 million) and 2023 Funding Agreement ($182.1 million); most is repaid through offsets against Apple service fees rather than cash outlays. Cash was $358.4 million in Q1 2026, down from $447.5 million as prepayment timing is lumpy. The current ratio is a healthy 2.42. A $149.4 million Series A Preferred issue carries a 7% dividend, with Thermo holding $136.7 million of it.

A massive capex cycle is underway. PP&E jumped from $674 million (2024) to $1.31 billion (2025) to $1.43 billion (Q1 2026). The Extended MSS Network program is projected at roughly $2.0 billion in total, of which only about $0.7 billion has been incurred. Satellite contracts include MDA Space ($329.3 million for 17 replacement HIBLEO-4 satellites plus $775 million for 50-plus third-gen C-3 satellites) and SpaceX for launches. In a standalone world, the financing of this program would be the central question. Inside the merger context, it matters mainly through the milestone-based price-adjustment mechanism.

The earnings-surprise and estimate-revision data underscore why fundamentals are not the driver here. The company missed EPS by 68% in Q1 2026 and by 439% in Q4 2025, and current-quarter/next-quarter estimates have collapsed (current quarter revised from $0.10 90 days ago to negative $0.09). These would be alarming for a standalone equity but are largely irrelevant to a fixed-price cash arb, except insofar as operational shortfalls could trip the milestone adjustment.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone growth prospects are secondary to the deal, but worth framing because they define the break-case value. The secular tailwind is real: Counterpoint Research projects satellite-capable smartphones will reach 46% of shipments by 2030, with Apple leading. Whoever supplies the network benefits. Commercial IoT is growing off a small base with record activations, while SPOT and Duplex are in decline.

The central growth dependency is Apple. Apple can terminate the Updated Services Agreements on advance notice and is free to use another satellite network. Post-merger, Amazon's own Leo constellation is reported (Ars Technica) to become the iPhone's primary satellite provider, while Apple retains its Globalstar relationship and its 20% ownership of the Globalstar SPE, which Amazon will acquire. The strategic logic of the acquisition is that Amazon's capital and constellation solve Globalstar's structural disadvantages versus Starlink: an older, smaller LEO fleet and narrower spectrum. As a standalone company, Globalstar's growth would remain hostage to Apple's platform decisions and to a heavy, self-funded capex cycle. As an Amazon subsidiary, that risk is transferred to a far stronger balance sheet.

For the arb, growth matters only through the milestone mechanism: the first HIBLEO-4 replacement launch was scheduled for May 2026, and hitting the amended operational milestones determines whether the full $90 or a reduced figure (down to roughly $89.15) is paid at close.

## Valuation Assessment

Conventional valuation metrics are meaningless here. GSAT screens at a negative trailing and forward P/E because it is GAAP-unprofitable, so the peer-multiple comparison against Communication Services names (median P/E 17.9, per SiriusXM, News Corp, Fox, and Pinterest) does not apply. The only valuation anchor that matters is the $90 deal price and the probability distribution around it.

At $78.93, the gross spread to the $90 cash headline is 14.0% (about $11.07 per share). That is a wide spread for a signed deal with locked shareholder approval, which tells you the market is pricing meaningful regulatory and timing risk. Decompose the $90:

- The downward adjustment equals roughly $0.85/share, so a realistic worst-case consideration on milestone failure is about $89.15.
- The timeline is long. The outside date is April 13, 2027, extendable to October 2027 and April 2028. If close occurs in, say, mid-2027, an annualized return on a ~14% gross spread over roughly one year is attractive versus arb benchmarks; if it slips to 2028, the annualized return compresses materially.
- Consideration mix matters. With 40% cash proration, most non-electing holders receive Amazon stock, introducing AMZN price risk between signing and close for anyone who does not win the cash election. A pure cash return of $90 is not guaranteed to the marginal holder.

The break-case anchor sits well below the current price. Given pre-announcement fundamentals (unprofitable, heavily levered on paper, single-customer-dependent), a deal collapse would likely re-rate the stock toward pre-announcement levels. The stock traded in the $22–$68 range during the second half of 2025 before running further on merger speculation and the announcement. A break could plausibly send shares to the $40s or lower, partially cushioned by the reverse termination fee (roughly $4.60/share). The risk/reward is therefore asymmetric: roughly $11 of upside to a full-cash close against $30-plus of potential downside on a break. That asymmetry is why this is a HOLD rather than a BUY for a generalist: the expected value is positive only if you assign a high probability to close, which requires dedicated diligence on the multi-jurisdiction regulatory path.

We do not treat the "cheap" negative-multiple screen as a buy signal; a compressed or negative multiple here reflects a business that would be structurally challenged absent the deal. The valuation score of 5.5 reflects a balanced, defined-outcome situation, not an identified mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Direct-to-cellular satellite is the industry's key battleground. The Amazon acquisition is explicitly framed as Amazon building Amazon Leo into a Starlink competitor while securing Apple as an anchor customer. Ars Technica reports Apple chose Amazon's satellites for the iPhone years after rejecting a Starlink offer, underscoring that the Globalstar/Apple/Amazon axis is a deliberate counterweight to SpaceX.

Standalone, Globalstar's competitive position is deteriorating relative to better-capitalized rivals: Starlink's larger, newer constellation and broader spectrum; AST SpaceMobile's direct-to-device ambitions; and Amazon's own Leo constellation, which now becomes an owner rather than a competitor. The company's durable edge is its globally harmonized ITU spectrum and the Band 53/n53 terrestrial holdings, plus the incumbency of the Apple relationship. Post-merger, that spectrum plus Amazon's capital and satellites is the strategic rationale. The competitive-position score of 5.5 reflects a defensible-but-eroding standalone franchise whose trajectory is now subsumed into Amazon's strategy.

## Risk Assessment

**Deal-completion risk is now dominant.** Closing requires HSR antitrust clearance plus foreign-investment, satellite, and telecom regulatory approvals across multiple jurisdictions and an FCC-related S-4 process. A break would likely send shares materially lower toward pre-announcement levels. The reverse termination fee structure signals Amazon's confidence but does not eliminate regulatory or antitrust risk given Amazon's scale and the strategic sensitivity of satellite spectrum.

**Milestone-based downward adjustment.** Triggered if Globalstar misses amended operational milestones tied to the HIBLEO-4 launches (first launch scheduled May 2026). This is a near-term overhang on the exact price received, compounded by the company's poor recent execution record (MDA delivery delays, a Q1 2025 satellite power-control anomaly causing a $7.0 million uninsured write-off).

**Customer concentration (Apple).** Apple can terminate on advance notice and use another network. This is a structural fundamental risk that becomes acute only in a break scenario; inside the deal it is Amazon's problem.

**Consideration mix and AMZN stock risk.** Under 40% cash proration, holders who do not secure the cash election receive Amazon stock, introducing equity-price risk between signing and close.

**Governance.** Thermo's control and unilateral written-consent approval meant minority holders had no vote; the Jacobs/XCOM related-party IP purchase and the Thermo-affiliate HQ lease are pre-existing red flags now largely moot given the locked deal.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term options are pricing minimal movement, consistent with a deal-pending, low-drift arb regime. ATM IV is 14.3% versus a 1-year historical volatility of 67.2%; implied vol is deeply cheap at the 7th percentile within the chain, with an implied move of only ±3.0% at the nearest expiry. The term structure is in contango (14.3% near-term rising to 21.3% at six months), reflecting expectations of a long, quiet period punctuated by eventual deal resolution. A modest 4.9% put skew signals some hedging against a break, and ATM IV ticked up 3.1 points (from 11.3% to 14.3%) as the price drifted down 3.3% since early June, a mild tell that the market is nudging up its assessment of downside/timing risk. The near-zero put/call volume and OI ratios reflect the low-activity nature of a stock that has become a rate-and-timing instrument. Short interest is unremarkable at 6.3% of float and 2.2 days to cover, so squeeze mechanics are not a factor.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (close at full cash, ~$90):** Regulatory approvals clear on the faster end of the timeline (close in 2027), Globalstar hits the HIBLEO-4 milestones avoiding the price adjustment, and cash-electing holders receive $90. Upside of roughly 14%, annualized to something attractive if resolved within 12–18 months. The large reverse termination fee and Amazon's strategic motivation (anchoring Apple, competing with Starlink) support high close probability.

**Bear case (deal breaks):** Antitrust or multi-jurisdiction regulatory objection kills the transaction, or the milestone failure plus a stock-consideration outcome erodes the realized value. On a break, the stock re-rates toward pre-announcement levels ($40s or lower), cushioned partially by the reverse fee. Downside of 40%-plus. Standalone fundamentals — GAAP losses, the accumulated deficit, Apple concentration, a self-funded $2 billion capex program, and intensifying competition from far larger players — do not support anything near the current price without the deal.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to a BUY for arb-oriented capital):**
- The spread widens materially (toward $70 or below) on timing-driven weakness rather than genuine regulatory deterioration, improving the annualized return for a deal you still judge likely to close.
- HSR clearance and initial foreign-investment/telecom approvals are obtained, de-risking the regulatory path and shortening the expected timeline.
- HIBLEO-4 launch milestones are met, removing the downward-adjustment overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any second-request, formal antitrust challenge, or regulatory objection in a material jurisdiction that raises break probability.
- Extension to the October 2027 or April 2028 outside date without visible progress, which would compress the annualized return below arb-hurdle levels.
- Milestone failure confirmed alongside signs of Amazon seeking further price/term concessions.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the HIBLEO-4 launch cadence (first launch scheduled May 2026 and subsequent milestones), the HSR review clock, and quarterly deal-status disclosures. The transaction is expected to close in 2027, so the position should be reassessed at each regulatory milestone rather than on operating results.

## Conclusion

Globalstar is a merger-arbitrage instrument, not a fundamental equity. The $90 cash offer from Amazon, with controlling-shareholder approval already secured via Thermo's written consent, defines the outcome. The gross spread reflects a genuine, multi-jurisdiction regulatory-approval process running to 2027 (extendable to 2028), a milestone-based price adjustment, and consideration-mix uncertainty under 40% cash proration. The risk/reward is asymmetric: roughly $11 of upside to a full-cash close against $30-plus of downside on a break, partially cushioned by the reverse termination fee.

For dedicated arbitrage capital able to underwrite the regulatory path, the setup can offer an acceptable annualized return if close occurs within 12–18 months. For a generalist portfolio, the asymmetry and the long timeline argue against fresh capital. We rate GSAT a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the outcome is binary, the fundamentals do not support the price absent the deal, and the expected value hinges entirely on a regulatory judgment we cannot make with high confidence. Holders positioned in the arb should manage it as a defined-outcome trade with the exit criteria above; new investors have limited edge at the current spread.
