## Executive Summary

ServiceNow's operating results have not broken. Q2 2026 subscription revenue grew 24.5% (+23% constant currency) to $3,877M, the company beat the high end of its own subscription guide by 150bps and its cRPO guide by 200bps, raised FY2026 subscription revenue guidance to $15,760-15,780M, and disclosed that ServiceNow AI crossed $1B in annual contract value. The renewal rate has held at 98% of ACV for three consecutive years, and customers with over $5M in ACV reached 658 at 6/30/26 from 533 a year earlier. Remaining performance obligations stand at $29.0B, up 21%.

What has changed is the shape of the growth and the quality of the cash. Current RPO growth has stepped down from +25% at the FY25 exit to +21% in Q2 and a guided +19.5% for Q3, the first sub-20% GAAP cRPO guide in the recent record, with only about 25bps of that step-down explained by the FX headwind management quantified. Roughly 1.2 points of Q2's 24.5% subscription growth came from incremental upfront self-hosted revenue ($149M versus $109M), which management explicitly attributed to U.S. federal demand pulled out of Q3. Reported FY2026 growth also includes Moveworks, Veza and Armis, with no organic split disclosed. On the cash line, 1H26 operating cash flow fell 6% to $2,257M; operating cash flow less capex was $2,002M versus $1,998M, essentially flat against 23% revenue growth, with the reported 14% "free cash flow" growth carried by a $283M increase in the acquisition-cost addback and a 35% capex reduction. Subscription gross margin fell from 80% (FY25) to 73.5% GAAP; stripping the $153M year-over-year increase in cost-of-revenue amortization from both periods still leaves roughly a 2.7-point organic decline, driven by third-party cloud costs (+$63M in the quarter) and AI delivery.

At $117.22 the stock is 39.8% below its 52-week high of $194.73 and 44% above the April low of $81.24. It trades at 23.4x next-year consensus EPS of $5.01 and 28.8x current-year $4.07, against a peer-median trailing P/E of 56.2 (a median distorted by loss-making and hyper-multiple comparables). That is a fair multiple for a business growing subscription revenue in the low-20s decelerating toward management's own implied ~17.4% CAGR to 2030, with a structurally lower gross-margin ceiling, positive net debt for the first time, and an unresolved federal procurement contingency. This report continues the HOLD at a score of 5.94, down from a prior sequence of BUY then three HOLDs at 7.2 declining to 6.2. The reduction reflects three specific new items from the Q2 filings rather than price (the stock is up 27% since the June assessment): the sub-20% cRPO guide, the organic gross-margin compression, and the flat unadjusted cash generation.

## Company Overview

ServiceNow sells a single multi-tenant platform, now marketed as the ServiceNow AI Platform, across four disclosed application groupings: Technology; CRM and Industry; Core Business; and Creator and Other. The original wedge was IT service and operations management built on a configuration management database that inventories enterprise technology assets; the platform expanded laterally into HR, customer service, security operations and low-code development.

FY2025 revenue was $13,278M, of which subscription $12,883M (97%) and professional services $395M. The company served approximately 8,700 customers with 29,187 employees at year-end. Revenue is 63% North America (95% of that U.S.), 25% EMEA and 12% Asia-Pacific and other. The direct sales organization represents 75-76% of 2026 revenue, though ServiceNow's definition folds systems integrators and managed service providers into "direct," so the disclosed figure overstates how much revenue arrives without a partner intermediary.

The strategic repositioning is toward AI governance: the platform's single data fabric marketed as the substrate on which enterprises govern, secure and manage AI estates. The three 2026 acquisitions map onto that pitch directly: Armis (cyber-asset and device visibility, $7.6B cash, closed 4/20/26), Veza (identity and entitlement governance, ~$1.2B, 3/2/26) and Moveworks (AI assistant, $2,407M, 12/15/25). Partner relationships deserve literal reading: AWS, Google and Microsoft are cloud infrastructure suppliers that ServiceNow pays, and simultaneously distribution channels; NVIDIA is a technology partner (AI Control Tower integrated into NVIDIA's Enterprise AI Factory Validated Design); Anthropic is the first design partner for Action Fabric, connecting Anthropic's Claude to ServiceNow workflows. Leidos is disclosed as a customer.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** Q2 2026 total revenue was $3,987M (+24%). Non-GAAP operating income was $1,173M (29.5% margin, +23%); GAAP operating income was $162M (4.1%), down 55%. The $1,011M bridge comprises stock-based compensation $655M, amortization of purchased intangibles $219M, business-combination costs $75M and severance $62M. Amortization jumped from $25M to $219M in one quarter as a mechanical consequence of Armis ($2,530M of intangibles) and Veza ($356M); the disclosed schedule runs $391M for the remainder of 2026, then $776M, $726M, $700M and $648M through 2030. That is non-cash and irrelevant to free cash flow, but it suppresses GAAP earnings for about six years and permanently widens the non-GAAP adjustment.

Stock-based compensation is a real cost: $1,955M in FY2025 (14.7% of revenue) and $1,213M in 1H26 (15.6%). Management committed at its May 4, 2026 analyst day to bring SBC below 10% of revenue by 2029, which is an acknowledgment that the current level is elevated.

The gross-margin trajectory is the most economically consequential line. GAAP subscription gross margin fell from 80% to 73.5%; non-GAAP was 80.5% with FY26 guided at 81%. Adjusting both periods for the $153M year-over-year increase in cost-of-revenue amortization yields roughly 77.9% versus 80.6%, a ~2.7-point organic decline. Named drivers are third-party cloud service costs (+$63M in the quarter, +$104M in 1H) and infrastructure depreciation and software (+$63M). Management guides cost of subscription revenue higher as a percentage of revenue in FY2026 and attributes it to hyperscaler partnerships and "an acceleration of customer AI adoption." Capex fell 35% in 1H26 to $255M while cloud costs rose: the business is shifting cost from its own capex line into cost of revenue. Committed non-cancellable cloud spend escalates to $324M for the remainder of 2026, then $401M, $573M, $704M and $2.9B in 2030, plus $1.4B owed to an IT equipment provider by FY2028. The 2030 figure converts a variable input into a large fixed obligation and implies management expects materially higher AI consumption.

Professional services gross loss widened from −2% to −26.5%, with third-party partner cost at 42% of professional services revenue versus 33%. On $110M of quarterly revenue the drag is ~$29M, immaterial to consolidated margin, but evidence that ServiceNow is paying for implementation velocity to convert AI bookings into referenceable production deployments.

**Cash flow.** 1H26 operating cash flow was $2,257M (−6%) against capex of $255M, giving $2,002M versus $1,998M in 1H25. Company-defined non-GAAP FCF was $2,299M (+14%), including a $297M addback for business-combination and related costs (up from $14M). The addback is disclosed and consistently applied, and per-transaction acquisition costs genuinely are non-recurring, but the FY26 35% FCF margin guide and the "Rule of 56" framing both rest on a measure that adds back cash the company spent. Seasonality is severe: Q2 FCF was $634M (16% of revenue), implying ~$1,665M in Q1 (~44%). Meeting 35% for the year requires roughly $3.4B of 2H FCF on ~$8.4B of revenue, consistent with FY2025's 34.9% outcome but leaving little slack for working-capital slippage.

**Balance sheet.** Cash and marketable securities fell from $10,055M to $6,707M while debt rose from $1,491M to $7,517M ($2,082M commercial paper at a 3.98% weighted-average rate on an 81-day average term, plus $5.5B of notes including five May 2026 tranches, 4.25% 2028 through 6.30% 2056). Net debt is roughly $0.9B against FY26E non-GAAP operating income near $5.1B: gross leverage ~1.5x, net leverage negligible. A $3.0B revolver is undrawn to April 2031, and the $4.0B April term loan drawn for Armis was repaid with note proceeds.

Two consequences are permanent. Interest expense went from $6M to $66M per quarter and rises further in 2H; interest income fell 40% to $70M as the portfolio was liquidated. The crossover is a $150-200M annualized pre-tax EPS headwind versus 2025. Second, the 6.30% thirty-year coupon against the legacy 1.40% 2030 notes marks a materially higher cost of capital.

The current ratio of 0.70 is not a distress signal: $8,057M of $12,153M in current liabilities is deferred revenue, settled by delivering software already in operation. Excluding deferred revenue, current assets cover current liabilities 2.08x. The genuine new item is $2.1B of commercial paper rolling on an 81-day average term, covered roughly 3x by cash and securities and backstopped by the undrawn revolver, but refinancing exposure that did not exist twelve months ago. Goodwill plus intangibles of $13,618M now exceeds total equity of $12,516M. Because ServiceNow reports as a single operating segment with the CEO as chief operating decision maker, goodwill is tested against a reporting unit whose fair value dwarfs carrying equity, so accounting impairment risk is low; the real exposure is that $9.8B of shareholder capital has been converted into revenue synergies that will be judged commercially.

**Earnings quality.** Strategic investments rose to $2,073M from $1,542M, with net upward adjustments of $273M in Q2 and $360M in 1H under the measurement alternative, classified Level 3, versus immaterial amounts in 2025. Against $767M of 1H GAAP net income, that is a substantial share of GAAP pre-tax income arising from observable-price-change marks on illiquid private securities. The marks are symmetric and require an observed transaction, and non-GAAP net income excludes them, so on this specific dimension the non-GAAP figure is the more conservative one. It does mean trailing GAAP EPS of $1.67, and the 70x trailing multiple built on it, should not anchor any valuation work.

Separately, the Q2 effective tax rate rose to 32% from 18%, attributed to stock-based compensation shortfalls (book expense exceeding realized deductions when the vesting price sits below grant-date expense). A depressed share price therefore reduces GAAP EPS twice, and reverses if the stock recovers.

Three qualifications matter for anyone modeling forward momentum.

First, the cRPO deceleration is real and only marginally FX-driven. Management attributes ~$35M of the Q3 cRPO headwind to dollar strengthening since March 31, which on a $13.2B base is roughly 25bps against a 150bp step-down.

Second, the Q2 beat borrowed from Q3, as management stated. Upfront self-hosted revenue was $149M versus $109M; holding it flat, subscription growth would have been 23.3% rather than 24.5%. FY2025 upfront self-hosted revenue was $492M (3.8% of subscription revenue), the same share as Q2 2026, so this is a persistent feature rather than a new one, but it is concentrated in federal and regulated buyers, is excluded from RPO, and raises the probability of a clean-beat-messy-next-quarter pattern.

Third, reported FY2026 growth includes acquired revenue from three deals. No organic split was disclosed, and Armis's standalone revenue is not verifiable from the filings; the 10-Q discloses only $137M of acquired current deferred revenue and $54M of order-backlog intangible, neither of which is a revenue proxy. The constant-currency subscription guide of +21% is flat against FY25's GAAP +21%. That reported acceleration from 21% to 22.5% is at least partly purchased.

Against those qualifications, the demand evidence is genuine. AI ACV crossed $1B, customers running agentic AI in production grew ninefold over nine months, 123 transactions exceeded $1M in net new ACV in Q2 (up nearly 40%), and the >$5M ACV cohort has compounded 420 → 502 → 603 → 658. Management's 2030 targets ($30B+ subscription revenue, 30% of ACV from AI, "Rule of 60+") imply a ~17.4% four-year CAGR from the FY26 base. Management is guiding the market toward gradual deceleration, not sustained 20%+.

**Estimate revisions.** Consensus is drifting down: current quarter $1.03 from $1.09 (−6.0% over 30 and 90 days), next quarter −3.2%, current year $4.07 from $4.12 (−1.3%), next year $5.01 essentially flat (+0.1% over 90 days). The near-term cuts are consistent with the Q3 pull-forward and the cRPO guide; the out-year is intact. There is no positive revision signal here, and the strong-revision buy setup (>+30% over 90 days) is absent. The earnings track record is a modest three beats and one in-line over four quarters, with surprise magnitudes of 4.3%, −0.3%, 3.2% and 11.8%, enough to support confidence in consensus, not enough to imply systematic conservatism.

## Valuation Assessment

Derived inputs at $117.22:

- Market capitalization: 1,033.9M × $117.22 = **$121.2B**
- Enterprise value: $121.2B + $7.6B debt − $6.7B cash and securities = **~$122.1B** (excludes $2,073M of Level 3 strategic investments)
- FY26E revenue ~$16.2B → **EV/Revenue ~7.5x**
- FY26E company-defined FCF at 35% margin ~$5.67B → **EV/FCF ~21.5x**; on an unadjusted basis (removing an assumed ~$450M full-year acquisition addback) ~$5.2B → **~23.5x**
- **28.8x** current-year consensus EPS of $4.07; **23.4x** next-year consensus of $5.01
- On the brief's independently derived FY26E non-GAAP EPS of ~$3.91: **30.0x**
- Trailing GAAP EPS $1.67 → 70x, a figure inflated by Level 3 marks and not usable

Consensus EPS growth from $4.07 to $5.01 is 23%, putting the forward PEG at roughly 1.0. That is not expensive for the quality of the revenue base: 98% renewal, $29.0B of RPO, 46% of it current. The peer table shows a median trailing P/E of 56.2, but the comparables are heterogeneous and largely unusable: CrowdStrike's P/E is negative, Twilio's is 315x, Gen Digital's is 15.2x. The more relevant reference set is large-cap enterprise software after the 2026 derating, where multiples in the low-to-mid 20s on forward earnings are now common.

Two internal anchors frame the drawdown. The 10-Q values Moveworks stock consideration at ServiceNow's 12/15/25 close of $153.04 and Logik.io consideration at the 5/30/25 close of $202.22 (both split-adjusted). Form 4 tax-withholding prices show $93.59-$95.07 in early-to-mid May 2026, and the Q1 accelerated repurchase settled at a $107.97 average. So the current price sits 26-28% above the May trough, most of that gain arriving after July 22.

The drawdown is 39.8% from the 52-week high, which means the burden of proof sits on the bull. What are the sellers pricing? Terminal value: the argument that agentic AI collapses per-seat application software economics, both because agents replace seats and because AI-native tooling can rebuild workflow logic cheaply. NOW fell roughly 36% in 1H26 while growing revenue in the low-20s, which is a repricing of the out-years rather than the current period.

My read is that the derating is directionally justified and the current multiple is closer to fair than to cheap. Three pieces of company-specific evidence support the sellers rather than the buyers: subscription gross margin is compressing ~2.7 points organically because AI delivery and hyperscaler migration carry lower incremental margin, which lowers the long-run margin ceiling even as operating margin expands on S&M and G&A leverage; management's own 2030 target implies deceleration to ~17%; and unadjusted cash generation was flat in 1H while revenue grew 23%. Set against that, the $1B AI ACV sits at roughly 7-8% of the ACV base implied by $13.2B of cRPO, so reaching the 2030 goal of 30% requires that ratio to roughly quadruple, and management's own product chief describes existing customers as the biggest driver of AI growth, an installed-base upsell rather than demonstrated new-logo displacement.

Consensus mean and median targets are $140.25 and $140.00, with a $248 high and $72 low, and the analyst distribution is 44 buy/strong-buy against 3 holds, 1 sell and 1 new strong-sell. On a stock at 23x forward with decelerating cRPO, I do not treat proximity to consensus as either a floor or a ceiling; the target dispersion of $72-$248 is itself a statement that the terminal-value question is unresolved.

Valuation scores 6.0: the multiple no longer embeds a growth premium and offers modest upside if cRPO stabilizes, but a compressed multiple on a franchise facing a genuine structural question typically fails to pay without an identified catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K names Microsoft, Oracle, SAP, Salesforce and Workday alongside AI-native entrants. The defensible moat rests on the configuration management database and data fabric as a system of record that competitors would need to rebuild, a 98% ACV renewal rate sustained through three years of macro and AI turbulence, and an installed base buying more per customer (658 accounts above $5M ACV, up from 533 a year earlier). Gartner Magic Quadrant leader placements in AI Governance Platforms, Workplace Experience Applications and SaaS Management Platforms are cited in the company's own release; the underlying reports were not independently reviewed.

The trajectory is the question, and it is mixed. Positive: the security and identity build (ServiceNow SecOps plus Armis asset intelligence plus Veza entitlement governance) makes the AI-governance pitch structurally more credible, since governing agents requires knowing every device, every identity and every request. Management claims the security business is "well over $1 billion" and the fastest-growing among top-ten vendors, but ServiceNow reports as a single segment and does not disclose security revenue, so that figure is an issuer statement.

Negative: gross margin is compressing, which is what a lower-margin delivery mix looks like; professional services has become a paid loss leader to buy implementation velocity; and Microsoft is simultaneously an infrastructure supplier, a distribution channel and the single most capable competitor at exactly the layer ServiceNow is claiming, given that Agent 365 is itself an agent-management plane. ServiceNow's governance moat is being constructed partly on top of, and partly against, Microsoft's agent platform. That is the central strategic ambiguity, and it is not resolvable from current disclosure.

Competitive position scores 6.3: a strong installed-base position with an unresolved question about whether the governance layer is defensible against the platform vendor that also hosts it.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Federal concentration entangled with an unresolved DOJ matter.** The Q2 10-Q discloses one customer, a U.S. federal channel partner and systems integrator, at 13% of total revenue for both the three and six months ended 6/30/26, up from 11%, and 12% of accounts receivable. The contractual counterparty is the reseller; the ultimate consumers are federal agencies, so the exposure is conditional on both the partner relationship and federal budget and procurement conditions. Separately, Note 17 discloses that an internal investigation examined the March 2023 hiring of the U.S. Army's CIO as ServiceNow's Head of Global Public Sector; the board determined that the then-President/COO and the hired individual violated conflict-of-interest policy; the COO resigned effective immediately on July 24, 2024. ServiceNow informed the DOJ, the DoD Office of Inspector General and the Army Suspension and Debarment Office, and states that DOJ commenced its own investigation and required document production, with timing and outcome unpredictable. No accrual is recorded and management does not believe resolution is likely to result in a material loss. These are separately disclosed facts sharing a common exposure to federal procurement; notification of a debarment office is frequently a self-reporting compliance step rather than evidence that debarment is contemplated, and the matter has been outstanding since 2024 without disclosed escalation. But an adverse procurement sanction would strike the largest single concentration, which is also the fastest-growing and the source of the on-premise revenue timing swings.

**2. Structural gross-margin compression.** Guided down again in FY2026 for reasons management names as persistent: hyperscaler migration to serve regulated markets and data-residency rules, and accelerating customer AI adoption. The $2.9B 2030 cloud commitment suggests the AI cost curve steepens before it flattens. The cash impact is smaller than the P&L optics because capex falls in parallel.

**3. Revenue-recognition variability.** Upfront self-hosted recognition ($149M in Q2; $492M in FY2025) makes quarters lumpy, is concentrated in federal buyers, and is excluded from RPO. Management has flagged federal on-premise timing as a swing factor twice.

**4. Integration and capital-allocation risk on ~$11.2B of M&A in seven months.** 1H26 carried $101M of acquisition costs plus $162M of deferred compensation for key acquired employees amortized into R&D, a persistent retention cost. Armis added 2.3M assumed options and 5.9M RSUs, so part of the purchase price is retention equity. Armis's standalone revenue is unverified, so the purchase multiple and value accretion are unresolved.

**5. Dilution and its asymmetry.** 46.8M RSUs/PRSUs outstanding with $4.9B of unrecognized compensation over ~3 years, plus 7.3M options, roughly 5.2% of existing shares, or ~4.9% post-issuance ownership dilution if all vest, contingent on service and, for 2.7M PRSUs, on performance with 0-250% payouts. Plan capacity of 144.9M is authorization, not obligation. Only 2,983K securities were dilutive in Q2 versus 10,789K a year earlier, with 46,730K excluded as anti-dilutive. If the stock recovers, previously anti-dilutive awards re-enter the denominator, creating a mechanical EPS headwind on good news.

**6. Commercial paper rollover.** $2.1B on an 81-day average term is new refinancing exposure, covered roughly 3x by liquidity and backstopped by an undrawn $3.0B revolver, but it did not exist a year ago. [unverified — no macro rate context (30-year yield level, Fed cadence/dissents) appears in the research brief or Phase 1 data]

**7. FX reversal.** Management quantified a $138M favorable FX effect on 1H26 subscription revenue and a ~$35M year-over-year headwind to Q3 cRPO. The tailwind has turned.

The Q2 10-Q states there were no material changes to risk factors from the FY2025 10-K; the genuinely new disclosures are in the market-risk and debt sections.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The renewal rate has held at 98% through three years of macro and AI turbulence, which is the single hardest datum for the terminal-value bear to explain. RPO of $29.0B and 658 customers above $5M in ACV establish that the installed base is consolidating spend onto the platform rather than fragmenting it. AI ACV crossed $1B with production agentic deployments up ninefold in nine months, and the Armis/Veza/Moveworks build gives the AI-governance pitch a defensible technical basis, since you cannot govern agents without knowing every device, identity and request. If value in the AI stack migrates from model weights to proprietary business context and controls, ServiceNow's data fabric is the asset. At 23.4x next-year consensus with a forward PEG near 1.0 and net debt of roughly $0.9B, the multiple no longer requires heroic assumptions. If cRPO stabilizes at or above 20% constant currency for two quarters and non-GAAP subscription gross margin holds at the guided 81%, a re-rating to 30-34x forward is available. Bull case $168 (33.5x FY27E $5.01), +43% from $117.22.

### Bear Case

Subscription gross margin is compressing 2.7 points organically because AI delivery and hyperscaler migration carry structurally lower incremental margin, which lowers the long-run margin ceiling regardless of operating leverage below the gross line. cRPO has stepped down 25% → 21% → 19.5% guided, and management's own 2030 target implies ~17.4% CAGR. Unadjusted cash generation was flat in 1H while revenue grew 23%; the reported 14% FCF growth is an addback and a capex cut. Reported growth acceleration is partly purchased with $11.2B of M&A at an unverifiable multiple, funded by liquidating the securities portfolio and adding $7.6B of debt at a cost of capital marked by a 6.30% thirty-year coupon. AI ACV is an upsell into the installed base at 7-8% of the ACV pool, and Microsoft is building an agent-management layer while hosting ServiceNow's workloads. A federal channel partner at 13% of revenue sits alongside an unresolved DOJ investigation and a debarment-office notification. If cRPO breaks into the mid-teens or the federal channel is impaired, 23x forward is still too high and the stock revisits the April low. Bear case $82 (16.4x FY27E $5.01, near the 52-week low of $81.24), −30%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (fourth consecutive; overall score 5.94, continuing the declining trajectory from 6.2). The trajectory decline is driven by the Q2 filings, not by price: the stock is 27% above its June level, but cRPO guidance broke below 20%, subscription gross margin compressed organically, unadjusted cash generation went flat, and the balance sheet moved from net-cash to modestly levered with a permanent $150-200M annualized interest headwind.

**Base case: $128** — 25.5x next-year consensus EPS of $5.01, a modest premium to large-cap software after the 2026 derating, justified by 98% renewal and $29.0B of RPO but capped by decelerating cRPO and a lower gross-margin ceiling. **+9.2% from $117.22.**

**Bull case: $168** (33.5x FY27E $5.01), **+43%.** Requires two consecutive quarters of cRPO at or above 20% constant currency, non-GAAP subscription gross margin holding at 81%, and AI ACV progressing toward the externally reported $1.5B target.

**Bear case: $82** (16.4x FY27E $5.01), **−30%.**

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint at the Q3 2026 print in late October, the quarter management pre-announced as harder because federal on-premise revenue was pulled into Q2.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. cRPO growth at or above 20% constant currency for two consecutive quarters, confirming the Q3 guide-down was pull-forward timing rather than demand deceleration.
2. Non-GAAP subscription gross margin at or above 81% with GAAP subscription gross margin stabilizing above 74%, plus operating cash flow less capex growing at or above 15% year-over-year without addbacks.
3. A price in the low $90s with the above fundamentals intact, which would put the stock near 18x FY27E and restore genuine asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. cRPO growth guided or reported below 17% constant currency, or the >$5M ACV customer count growing below 15% year-over-year.
2. Any disclosed adverse development in the DOJ/Army matter: a suspension, debarment proceeding, accrual, or loss of the 13%-of-revenue federal channel partner.
3. Non-GAAP subscription gross margin guided below 80% for FY2027, indicating the AI-delivery cost curve is steepening rather than flattening.
4. FY2026 free cash flow margin materially below the guided 35% on the company definition, which would confirm that 2H collections cannot carry the annual figure.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 earnings (late October) is the natural checkpoint. Two nearer-dated items also matter: the September 30, 2026 expiry of the 2021 CEO performance award performance period, with four of eight tranches unvested and share-price conditions that the current price makes difficult, and any 8-K addressing the two unread Item 5.02 filings from December 2025 and February 2026.

## Conclusion

ServiceNow remains a high-quality recurring-revenue franchise: 98% ACV renewal for three straight years, $29.0B of RPO, 658 customers above $5M in ACV (up from 533 a year earlier), and AI ACV past $1B with production agentic deployments up ninefold. The 39.8% drawdown from the 52-week high reflects a market judgment on terminal value rather than on current results, and at 23.4x next-year consensus EPS with a forward PEG near 1.0 and negligible net leverage, the price no longer embeds a growth premium.

I am not prepared to treat that as a buy signal, because three items in the Q2 filings support the sellers. Subscription gross margin is compressing roughly 2.7 points organically as AI delivery and hyperscaler migration shift cost into cost of revenue, and management guides that further; current RPO growth has stepped down to a guided +19.5% with FX explaining only a fraction; and unadjusted cash generation was flat in 1H while revenue grew 23%, with the reported 14% FCF growth carried by a $283M addback increase and a 35% capex cut. Layer on a 13%-of-revenue federal channel partner entangled with an unresolved DOJ investigation and a Suspension and Debarment Office notification, $11.2B of M&A at an unverifiable multiple funded with new debt at a 6.30% long-bond coupon, no insider open-market buying through a 40% drawdown, and CEO grant-date compensation up 37% in a year when indexed TSR fell from 193 to 139 against a peer group at 227.

The result is a fair-to-slightly-cheap multiple on a franchise with a genuine unresolved structural question, an option distribution the market prices at ±26% over six months, and base-case upside of 9% against 30% bear-case downside. HOLD, classified as uncertain. The evidence that would change the call is specific and dated: cRPO at or above 20% constant currency for two quarters and subscription gross margin stabilizing. Absent that, wait for either the data or a price in the low $90s.
