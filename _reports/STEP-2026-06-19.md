**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

StepStone is a global private-markets investment firm running ~$885B of total capital responsibility ($233B AUM + $652B AUA) across private equity, infrastructure, private debt, and real estate. The operating franchise grew strongly in fiscal 2026 (year ended March 31, 2026): management and advisory fees rose 21% to $926.5M, fee-related earnings (FRE) grew 14% to $354.4M, and fee-earning AUM (FEAUM) rose 19% to $144.0B. The forward fee pipeline is unusually visible: undeployed fee-earning capital (UFEC) jumped 63% to $40.1B, which is committed capital not yet generating fees. By every operating metric this was a good year, capped by what management called the "best quarter ever" for FRE in Q4.

The reported GAAP figures contradict that picture, and understanding why is the crux of the analysis. StepStone reported a GAAP net loss of $(535.8)M, or $(6.78)/share, driven almost entirely by $1.74B of non-cash equity-based compensation, the bulk of which is mark-to-market remeasurement of the private-wealth (SPW) profits-interest liability. Mechanically, the better the private-wealth business performs, the larger the GAAP loss appears, because the value of the management team's profits interest rises and flows through compensation expense. The relevant earnings measure is adjusted net income (ANI) of $2.16/share, up 8% YoY. On that basis the stock at $45.20 trades around 21x trailing ANI, having fallen ~42% from its $77.80 52-week high.

The same private-wealth success that inflates the GAAP loss also creates the dominant bear case: the SPW profits-interest holders can put their stake to StepStone beginning June 30, 2026, with a disclosed settlement range of $564M to $2.26B. Against $213M of cash and a $3.6B market cap, even the low end is roughly 16% of equity value, and the high end exceeds half of it. StepStone can pay up to 75% in stock or units, but the remainder likely requires cash and therefore financing. This is a dated, binary, partly-dilutive obligation whose size grows with the firm's own success. We hold. The fundamentals/price gap is genuine and insiders are buying at the lows, but the put overhang is too large and too imminent to underwrite confidently until its magnitude and funding mechanism are known.

## Company Overview

Founded in 2007, StepStone occupies the space between allocator and manager. It builds customized solutions for institutional and, increasingly, private-wealth clients through three synergistic strategies: primaries (fund commitments), secondaries (buying existing LP stakes), and co-investments (direct deals alongside sponsors). It deploys client capital into third-party funds rather than primarily manufacturing its own products. The firm employs ~1,310 people across 31 cities and 19 countries, with ~420 investment professionals and 117 partners averaging more than 20 years of experience.

The structural differentiators are concrete. SPI by StepStone is a proprietary research platform holding data on 19,000+ fund managers, 52,000 funds, 139,000 companies, and 293,000 investments, a hard-to-replicate asset that improves manager selection, powers the secondaries pricing engine, and is increasingly monetized directly (partnerships with Kroll, FTSE Russell, and a June 2026 deal-level benchmarking integration with PitchBook). The "global-and-local" model places 45% of investment professionals outside the US, and more than half of fees come from non-US clients. A cross-sell flywheel is evident: 34% of advisory clients also carry an AUM relationship and 34% use more than one asset class.

FY26 management and advisory fees split roughly 59% commingled funds, 32% SMAs, 8% advisory/data, 1% reimbursements. Client concentration is low: no single client exceeds 5% of fees, and the top-10 clients account for 17%.

## Financial Analysis

The reported numbers require translation. The headline GAAP loss is an accounting artifact, not operating deterioration. The $1.74B equity-based compensation charge (up from $669M) is dominated by mark-to-market remeasurement of the SPW profits-interest liability. As the private-wealth business expanded — FRE attributable to SPW profits interests rose from $12.0M to $52.5M, and net income attributable to those interests was $136.2M in FY26 — the value of the team's profits interest rose, flowing through GAAP compensation as a non-cash expense.

The operating picture, which is the reliable forward indicator for a fee-based manager:

| Metric (FY26) | Value | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| Management & advisory fees, net | $926.5M | +21% |
| Fee-Related Earnings (FRE) | $354.4M | +14% |
| FRE margin | 38% | vs 41% (FY25) |
| Adjusted Net Income (ANI) | $264.6M | +8% |
| ANI per share | $2.16 | +5% |
| Performance Fee-Related Earnings (PRE) | $196.0M | +88% |
| Accrued carried interest (backlog) | $2,037M | +36% |

Operating momentum is the strongest part of the case: AUM $233.3B (+23%) and AUA $652B (+25%). UFEC is the most important forward number — committed capital that will begin earning fees as it deploys, giving near-term fee visibility independent of new fundraising. Asset-class growth was broad (PE FEAUM +16%, infrastructure +29%, private debt +27%) with real estate the sole soft spot at −1%.

FRE margin compressed on private-wealth build-out and headcount, but Q4 recovered to 40% ($105.3M FRE), and management guides to continued FRE growth in FY27. The margin decline reflects investment, not pricing pressure.

The balance sheet warrants careful reading. Cash is $213M; corporate debt is $271M including $175M of Series A senior notes due October 2029, which is modest corporate leverage. The $931M "debt of Consolidated Funds" on the balance sheet is non-recourse fund-level debt and not a StepStone obligation. SSG stockholders' equity is negative at $(414)M, but this is structural: a function of the Up-C holding-company structure, Tax Receivable Agreements, non-controlling interests, and the accumulated deficit from the non-cash SPW charge. Standard solvency screens are not meaningful here.

Capital return signals confidence: a $0.28 quarterly dividend plus a $0.55 supplemental dividend (paid 6/30/2026), FY26 quarterly dividends up 16% to $1.08, and a new $100M buyback authorized in March 2026. The 2.4% yield is below the peer median 2.7% but supported by adjusted earnings, not GAAP.

The earnings surprise record is solid: three beats and one miss across the last four quarters, with the most recent (Mar-2026) ANI of $0.57 a 9.6% beat against $0.52 consensus.

## Growth Analysis

The structural growth drivers are real and broad-based across the alternatives industry. Institutional allocations to private markets continue rising, and investors are consolidating to fewer multi-asset managers, which favors StepStone's breadth. The standout vector is private wealth (SPW), the fastest-growing channel, posting record organic subscriptions. The entire alts complex — Blackstone, Apollo, Blue Owl, Ares — is pushing into this channel, and StepStone's 1099-reporting, single-investment, periodic-liquidity product structures position it competitively.

Two specific catalysts could accelerate the trajectory. The DOL 401(k) safe-harbor proposal (3/30/2026) could open defined-contribution plans to private assets across the entire private-wealth/DC channel. Separately, the $40.1B of UFEC provides an embedded, near-term fee tailwind as capital deploys.

The growth case has a counterweight in the estimate revisions, which are broadly negative: current-quarter EPS revised −2.8% over 30 and 90 days, next-quarter −3.1%/−4.0%, and current-year −4.9% over 90 days. Only the next-year estimate shows a positive 90-day revision (+7.0% to $3.31, though −1.6% over 30 days). This is the key reason this is not a clean inflection-stock BUY signal: a clear positive revision velocity is absent, and momentum is mildly negative. The strong operating metrics and falling near-term EPS estimates are not contradictory — the metrics reflect committed capital and balance-sheet growth, while EPS estimates reflect near-term margin investment and a sector-wide caution about fundraising velocity in a higher-for-longer rate regime.

## Valuation Assessment

On GAAP, the stock screens as nonsensical (negative trailing P/E from the non-cash loss). On the relevant adjusted basis, $45.20 against $2.16 trailing ANI is ~21x, and against the $2.50 current-year and $3.31 next-year ANI estimates it is ~18x and ~13.7x forward. That places it below the peer median P/E of 25.2 and roughly in line with the most directly comparable name, Hamilton Lane (HLNE, 13.3x), the closest analog as a private-markets allocator with a recurring-fee base.

The 13.7x next-year multiple on a franchise growing FRE mid-teens and FEAUM ~19% would normally support a higher valuation score. Three considerations cap it. First, the SPW put is a real liability of uncertain size that the market is rationally discounting; the cheapness partly reflects a genuine claim against equity value, not pure mispricing. Second, near-term estimate revisions are negative, removing the catalyst that would otherwise justify rewarding cheapness; a low multiple without a positive earnings-momentum catalyst is the classic value-trap profile. Third, both STEP and HLNE de-rated alongside the broader private-asset complex this year on rate fears and redemption-gate concerns at evergreen/wealth vehicles, a re-rating that may persist while rates stay higher-for-longer.

The disconnect between operating momentum and price is the bull's strongest argument, and analyst targets ($60 low, $72.5 mean, $92 high) sit 33% to 104% above the current price. On a stock carrying a $564M–$2.26B dated contingent liability, I treat those targets as scenario-dependent rather than as floors; they assume the put resolves benignly. A valuation score of 6.0 reflects a genuinely below-peer multiple offset by a real claim against equity and absent near-term EPS momentum.

## Competitive Landscape

StepStone's competitive position is improving on trajectory. The combination of multi-asset scale, the SPI data platform, and a solutions/customization model is durable, and the PitchBook integration extends data monetization from an internal advantage to an external revenue stream. The trend toward client consolidation among fewer, broader managers structurally favors the firm, and the cross-sell metrics demonstrate the flywheel is functioning.

The SPI data moat is the clearest differentiator versus pure product manufacturers and consulting-firm advisors: 293,000 underlying investments is a dataset competitors cannot easily replicate, and it directly improves both manager selection and secondaries pricing.

The counter-currents are sector-wide rather than company-specific: SEC scrutiny of the retailization of private markets and private credit, redemption-request risk in semi-liquid wealth vehicles during stress, and a broad 2026 de-rating of private-asset equities. Within that environment, StepStone is gaining share in the fastest-growing channel and broadening its data franchise, which supports a 7.0 — improving position, with the caveat that the wealth-channel regulatory exposure is the same engine driving its growth.

## Risk Assessment

**SPW put liability — the dominant overhang.** The SPW management team can put its profits interest to StepStone beginning June 30, 2026. The low end is ~16% of equity value and the high end exceeds half. Up to 75% can be paid in stock or units (dilutive), but the remainder likely requires cash and therefore new debt or an equity raise. The liability grows with private-wealth success — a "success tax." Timing and magnitude of resolution is the single largest swing factor over the next 12 months, and it drives the depressed risk score.

**Carry/valuation sensitivity.** The $2.04B accrued carry is a hypothetical-liquidation estimate based on lagged, manager-reported NAVs of illiquid assets; ~59% is from 2020-or-prior vintages. The 10-K notes prior periods of significant unrealized carry losses, so this backlog can swing with rates and markets.

**Client termination risk.** SMAs and advisory mandates are often terminable on 30–90 days' notice; commingled-fund LPs can remove StepStone as GP (sometimes by simple majority, without cause). Government/pension advisory contracts are RFP-renewal based.

**Governance transition.** As of September 18, 2025, StepStone is no longer a "controlled company" under Nasdaq rules but does not yet have a majority-independent board or fully independent comp committee; it must comply by September 18, 2026. Class A holders lack standard governance protections in the interim. Completion should reduce a long-standing governance discount.

**Structural complexity.** Up-C dual-class structure, Tax Receivable Agreements (potentially accelerable on change-of-control, possibly exceeding actual tax savings), and staged buy-ins of asset-class entities (SRA, SRE, SPD via ~5%/yr exchanges through 2024–2034+) create ongoing dilution and minority-interest complexity.

**Macro.** Higher-for-longer rates pressure fundraising, exits, and carry realization, and the broader private-asset complex is being repriced on redemption-gate fears at evergreen/wealth vehicles.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress, consistent with the June 30 put date. Near-term ATM IV is 66.0% versus 1-year historical vol of 44.7% — rich by 21.4 points and in the 68th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (66.0% near vs 59.4% longer-dated), the signature of a market pricing a dated catalyst. Put/call skew is elevated at 11.8%, the put/call volume ratio is 6.67, and the P/C OI ratio rose to 1.30 from 0.79 a month ago as the price fell 14%. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±15.4%. The market is positioned defensively into the SPW put resolution, and the divergence between heavy put demand and the strong underlying operating metrics is the unresolved tension that keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Disregard the GAAP loss as non-cash accounting; ANI of $2.16 (+8%) and FRE of $354.4M (+14%) are the real earnings, growing mid-teens. FEAUM +19%, UFEC +63%, and record fundraising give strong forward fee visibility. The stock fell from $77.80 to the low $40s on GAAP optics and a sector de-rating, not operating deterioration. Insiders are buying the dip — Head of Strategy McCabe purchased ~$5.0M on the open market at ~$42 in June 2026 (having sold at ~$66 in December), and a director bought ~$565K at ~$44 in February. Secular tailwinds (private-wealth democratization, the DOL 401(k) safe-harbor proposal) are real and broad. At ~13.7x next-year ANI versus a 25x peer median, with analyst targets of $60–$92, a benign SPW resolution and continued FRE growth support a re-rating toward $65–$75.

**Bear case.** The SPW put becomes exercisable June 30, 2026 at $564M–$2.26B — a dated, partly-cash obligation against $213M cash, forcing either dilution or new leverage at an inopportune point in the rate cycle. The liability grows with the firm's own success. Near-term estimates are falling (current-year −4.9% over 90 days). The private-wealth engine driving growth is also the largest emerging regulatory exposure as the SEC scrutinizes retailization, and semi-liquid wealth products face redemption risk in stress. Layered on top: Up-C complexity, TRAs, staged entity buy-ins, an incomplete governance transition, and a sector-wide private-asset de-rating that may persist while rates stay high. The carry backlog is a lagged, illiquid-NAV estimate that can reverse. A worst-case put settlement plus continued de-rating takes the stock toward $32–$38.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an uncertain HOLD, not a growth or income hold. The franchise quality and below-peer multiple argue for the upside, but a binary, dated, large contingent liability prevents a confident directional call until it resolves.

**Reassessment timeline:** Near-term — the SPW put becomes exercisable June 30, 2026, and any exercise notice plus the negotiated settlement terms (cash/stock mix, total size) is the decisive catalyst. Next natural fundamental checkpoint is the FQ1 2027 earnings release (expected August 2026).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- SPW put resolves at or near the low end (~$564M) with majority stock/unit settlement and minimal cash dilution, removing the overhang.
- Estimate-revision trend turns positive (current-year ANI revisions inflect upward two consecutive months) alongside continued FRE growth and FEAUM/UFEC momentum.
- Governance transition completes ahead of the September 18, 2026 deadline (majority-independent board, independent comp committee), closing the governance discount.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- SPW put settles toward the high end ($1.5B+) requiring a sizable equity raise or covenant-stressing debt.
- FRE margin continues compressing below ~36% with FRE growth stalling, indicating the private-wealth investment is not converting to operating leverage.
- A material client/GP termination or a redemption-gate event in the wealth vehicles, or accrued carry marked down materially on falling private-asset NAVs.

## Conclusion

StepStone is a high-quality, data-advantaged private-markets franchise with strong recurring-fee momentum trading at a depressed adjusted multiple after a ~42% drawdown driven largely by GAAP optics and a sector-wide de-rating. The operating metrics — FEAUM +19%, UFEC +63%, FRE +14%, ANI $2.16 — are unambiguously good, and insider buying at the lows is a credible conviction signal. The reason this is not a BUY is the SPW put: a $564M–$2.26B contingent liability against $213M cash, exercisable June 30, 2026, that the options market is actively pricing (66% IV in backwardation, 6.67 put/call volume) and that near-term estimate revisions reinforce as a live concern rather than a tail risk.

A benign put settlement plus continued FRE growth supports a move toward the $60–$72 analyst range; a high-end settlement plus continued de-rating takes it to the low $30s. With the binary event weeks away and its outcome unknown, the disciplined position is to hold, monitor the put resolution and the cash/stock mix closely, and upgrade if the liability clears near the low end with limited dilution.

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain). Overall score 6.0/10.**
