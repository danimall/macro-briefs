## Executive Summary

Quaker Houghton's second quarter of 2026 delivered the operating improvement that had been absent from this story since 2023. Net sales rose 10% to $532.6M with volumes up 7% and all three geographic segments growing — the first quarter in this cycle where volume, rather than acquisition contribution or price, drove the result. Adjusted EBITDA reached a record $85.2M at a 16.0% margin, non-GAAP EPS was $2.19 against $1.71 a year earlier, and consensus estimates have moved accordingly: current-year EPS is up 9.9% over 90 days to $7.62 and next year up 4.5% to $9.30. The April 2026 credit facility amendment replaced roughly $824.6M of 2027 maturities with term loans and an $800M revolver running to April 2031, converting what had been the dominant balance-sheet risk into a non-issue for five years.

The problem is entry price. The stock closed 2025 at $137.31, management repurchased shares in May and June 2026 at approximately $141-143, and it now trades at $169.95 — up 34.5% over twelve months and 7.1% below the 52-week high of $183.01. On my numbers that is 22.3x current-year consensus, 18.3x next-year consensus, 11.7x LTM EV/adjusted EBITDA of $312.4M, and a 2.8% free cash flow yield on FY2025 FCF of $80.6M. Seven of seven covering analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy with a mean target of $187.57 — roughly 10% above spot. There is no identifiable mispricing here; the recovery has been priced, and the multiple now embeds delivery of management's stated 18%-plus EBITDA margin target from 16.0% today.

Three specific items keep this from being an ACCUMULATE. First, Americas segment operating earnings fell to $57.2M from $59.0M in Q2 and to $111.2M from $117.4M in H1, while volume grew — implying price/mix erosion in the largest and historically highest-margin region, and Americas is where the remaining $1,375M of goodwill and intangibles is most exposed after EMEA goodwill was fully written off in 2025. Second, H1 2026 operating cash flow of $29M against H1 adjusted EBITDA of $157.7M is 18% conversion versus 45.6% for FY2025, and the roughly $38M of H1 buybacks and dividends was partly debt-funded (net debt rose $29.6M to $721.0M). Third, the Q2 EBITDA margin expansion of 40bps coexisted with non-GAAP gross margin contracting 50bps and non-GAAP operating margin contracting 10bps — the improvement sits below the gross-profit line, in higher depreciation and amortization that gets added back and in equity income from non-consolidated affiliates. My probability-weighted twelve-month return is approximately 6% including the 1.25% dividend, against a 4.63% ten-year Treasury. That is fair, not compelling.

## Company Overview

Quaker Chemical Corporation, operating as Quaker Houghton, is the largest global supplier of industrial process fluids by revenue, formed by the August 2019 combination of the number-one and number-two players in the category. The business sells consumable chemistry — rolling lubricants, corrosion preventives, hydraulic fluids, quenchants, metal finishing and surface treatment chemistry, cleaners, greases and coatings — into steel, aluminum, automotive, aerospace, offshore, container, mining and general metalworking end markets, across more than 25 countries with approximately 4,700 employees.

A large share of revenue is delivered through on-site chemical management arrangements in which Quaker Houghton personnel operate inside customer plants. This is the source of the moat: the fluid is a small fraction of the customer's input cost but a material determinant of throughput, scrap rate and tool life, qualification cycles are long, and the service model embeds company engineers in the customer's process. Top five customers represent approximately 11% of net sales with the largest at roughly 3%, unusually diversified for an industrial supplier levered to autos and steel.

Two structural features matter for how the financials behave. Demand is derived from customers' **production volumes**, not their capital spending; Quaker Houghton sells per ton rolled and per vehicle built. That explains why 2023-2025 was painful even as broader industrial capex held: global steel and Western auto output were soft. A meaningful share of the book runs on index-based contracts that reset selling prices against published raw-material indices. This dampens margin volatility in both directions but with a lag, so gross margin tends to peak after input costs trough. Gross margin ran 31.5% (2022), 36.1% (2023), 37.3% (2024), 36.0% (2025), 36.1% (H1 2026), which is exactly that pattern.

Ownership is concentrated. Gulf Hungary Holding Kft. and its subsidiary QH Hungary Holdings Limited, affiliated with Gulf Oil International and the Hinduja family and the former Houghton owners, hold 3,640,129 shares, or 21.0%. Sanjay Hinduja, Chairman of Gulf Oil International, sits on the board.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** Net sales were $1,943.6M (2022), $1,953.3M (2023), $1,839.7M (2024, −5.8%), $1,888.6M (2025, +2.7%), and $1,013.0M in H1 2026 (+9.4%). The FY2025 increase was purchased rather than earned: the disclosed build was acquisitions +4%, FX +1%, price/mix −2%, with organic volumes roughly flat. H1 2026 was volume-led and broad-based across segments.

**The FY2025 GAAP loss is a balance-sheet event.** Reported operating income of $53.0M and a net loss of $2.5M (−$0.14 diluted EPS) against $194.7M and $116.7M respectively in FY2024 reconcile cleanly: gross profit $679.4M less SG&A $502.4M gives $177.0M, less an $88.8M EMEA goodwill impairment and $35.1M of restructuring gives $53.1M. Non-GAAP EPS was $7.02 versus $7.44, and adjusted EBITDA $299.2M versus $310.9M, a 3.8% decline. The profile field showing trailing and forward P/E both at 29.70 against GAAP EPS of −$0.14 is internally inconsistent and should be disregarded; the multiples below are derived from disclosed non-GAAP earnings and consensus.

**Cost structure is the actual problem.** SG&A as a percentage of sales moved 23.4% → 24.8% → 26.4% → 26.6% from 2022 to 2025, roughly 320bps of structural cost added against flat revenue while gross margin improved. The 2023-2025 earnings decline was operating deleverage on a cost base sized for a larger company, not a pricing failure. It has not stopped: non-operating and administrative expenses rose 18.4% in Q2 and 13.5% in H1, both faster than the 9.4% H1 sales growth. The 2026 Global Business Transformation Program targets $20-30M of annualized savings with completion in 2028; on $1.89B of sales, $25M is roughly 130bps of margin, which makes the 18%-plus target arithmetically reachable but execution-dependent over two more years.

There is a legitimate counter-reading. In a service-intensive on-site model, commercial and technical headcount sits in SG&A rather than cost of sales, so part of the build may be productive investment. The company's own Net New Business Wins metric came in at 4.6% for 2025 against a 2.9% target, which is consistent with paying for share gains. If the spend is productive, the transformation savings are incremental; if it is bloat, the savings merely restore prior margins. The evidence does not yet distinguish these.

**Q2 2026 margin quality deserves scrutiny.** Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 40bps to 16.0%, but non-GAAP gross margin contracted 50bps to 35.5% and non-GAAP operating margin contracted 10bps to 10.4%. D&A rose $1.7M to $25.6M (Dipsol and the new headquarters) and is added back. Separately, of the roughly $6.9M gap between after-tax income before equity items and net income attributable, approximately $3.8M is equity income from non-consolidated affiliates that stays inside adjusted EBITDA versus roughly $2.8M a year earlier — about $1.1M, or 11%, of the $9.7M year-over-year adjusted EBITDA increase came from entities the company does not consolidate. Management appropriately excludes $3.0M of captive-insurance equity income. None of this is improper, but the headline "record adjusted EBITDA" overstates the underlying operating improvement.

**Cash flow is the weakest link.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $136.5M against capex of $55.9M for $80.6M of free cash flow, 45.6% conversion of adjusted EBITDA, modest for specialty chemicals. H1 2026 operating cash flow was $29M, of which $33M came in Q2, implying Q1 was approximately negative $4M. Against H1 adjusted EBITDA of $157.7M that is 18% conversion. H1 capital returns — approximately $20.6M of buybacks and $17.5M of dividends — totaled roughly $38M against $29M of operating cash flow before capex, and net debt rose from $691.4M to $721.0M.

I am not annualizing H1 cash flow. Q1 is structurally the weakest working-capital quarter (incentive payouts, restructuring cash costs), and a 7% volume acceleration necessarily consumes receivables and inventory. The honest framing is that H1 conversion ran well below the full-year FY2025 rate, shareholder returns were partly borrowed, and H2 conversion in the Q3 10-Q is the single most important verification item. H1 2025 operating cash flow was not obtainable, so the seasonal baseline is incomplete.

**Balance sheet: refinanced, levered, intangible-heavy.** The April 2026 fourth amendment established a €250M euro term loan, a $550M dollar term loan and an $800M revolver maturing April 2031, with approximately $727.6M undrawn at June 30. $400M notional of swaps fixes a 3.58% base. Derived LTM adjusted EBITDA is $312.4M ($299.2M less H1 2025 $144.5M plus H1 2026 $157.7M), giving 2.31x net leverage; bank-defined net leverage was approximately 2.1x against a 4.25x covenant. Goodwill of $501.7M plus other intangibles of $873.5M equals $1,375.2M, or 49.2% of total assets, against equity of $1,373.1M, so tangible book value is effectively zero. An Altman Z-Score of approximately 2.50 on GAAP EBIT (2.66 substituting normalized operating income) sits in the grey zone, which reads as limited cushion for a second large impairment or a leveraged acquisition rather than distress. Direct solvency evidence — covenant headroom, undrawn revolver, a 2031 wall, positive FCF every year including 2025 — points the other way.

**Earnings-data conflict, flagged.** The supplied quarterly surprise table shows a June 2026 actual of $1.63 against a $1.65 estimate (−1.2%), which reconciles to neither the disclosed GAAP EPS of $1.55 nor the non-GAAP $2.19, and shows identical estimate/actual pairs ($1.79/$1.65) for both the December 2025 and March 2026 quarters. That feed appears to be mixed-basis or duplicated and I do not rely on it. The estimate revision data — current quarter +10.4% over 30 days, current year +8.7% over 30 days — corroborates a beat rather than a miss, and I weight the filing figures and revisions accordingly.

## Growth Analysis

The bull growth case has three legs, and two of them are verified.

**Volume recovery (verified, one quarter).** Volumes up 7% with all three segments growing, following roughly flat organic volumes in 2025, is credible evidence that a cyclical trough passed. Management framed Q2 as the fourth consecutive quarter of year-over-year profitability growth.

**Share gains (verified through the compensation process).** Net New Business Wins of 4.6% against a 2.9% target and a 3.2% maximum is management's own audited-adjacent measure of business won net of business lost. It carried the entire 2025 bonus payout. The caveat is arithmetic: 4.6% net wins alongside flat organic volumes implies underlying end-market volumes declined by a comparable amount.

**Cost savings (not yet delivered).** The $20-30M transformation program completes in 2028. This is the primary bridge from 16.0% to the 18%-plus margin target, and it is the leg with the least evidence.

Consensus reflects the first two legs: current year $7.62 (from $6.93 ninety days ago), next year $9.30 (from $8.90). The revision magnitude — roughly +10% on the current year — is genuine positive earnings momentum, though below the threshold where revision velocity alone dominates a valuation judgment. FY2026 at $7.62 is barely above FY2024's $7.44 non-GAAP EPS. This is recovery to a prior peak. FY2027 at $9.30 would be a genuine new high, +25% above 2024, and requires the margin program to land.

## Competitive Landscape

The industrial process fluids market is fragmented, technically specified and service-intensive. Quaker Houghton is the largest player by revenue; named competitors include Fuchs Petrolub, Henkel, BASF/Chemetall, Element Solutions and MKS/Atotech in surface treatment, plus a long tail of regional suppliers. Customer consolidation in steel and automotive is a disclosed risk, since merged customers gain purchasing leverage.

The trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. Volume share is being won. But Americas segment operating earnings declined in both Q2 and H1 while Americas volume grew, alongside EMEA earnings up approximately 31% and Asia/Pacific up approximately 27%. Growing volume while losing earnings in the largest region implies price/mix compression, unfavorable cost absorption, or competitive pricing. A mix shift toward lower-margin high-volume accounts would be consistent with aggressive new-business capture — winning share by conceding price is a legitimate strategy but a lower-quality one than the headline suggests.

The five-year total shareholder return figure is the harshest evidence that the market treats Quaker Houghton's problems as at least partly company-specific: $100 invested at year-end 2020 was worth $57.15 at year-end 2025, against $119.03 for the S&P Composite 1500 Chemicals Index, roughly 62 points of underperformance. The SG&A data supports the company-specific reading.

## Risk Assessment

**Americas segment erosion (highest-value unpriced item).** Two consecutive periods of declining Americas operating earnings on rising volume. Americas carries the largest remaining goodwill and intangible exposure now that EMEA goodwill is fully written off. A third consecutive quarter of decline converts this from a timing question into a structural one.

**Cash conversion.** H1 2026 conversion of 18% is sustainable at 2.1x bank leverage with $727.6M undrawn, but it is the mechanism by which an 8.6% buyback authorization coexists with a 2.8% free cash flow yield.

**Intangible concentration.** Tangible book is approximately negative $2.1M. The FY2025 $88.8M charge demonstrates management will write down when triggered. There is no book-value floor under this equity.

**Shareholder overhang.** Essentially the entire Gulf/QH Hungary 21.0% block is pledged — 2,100,000 shares to Citigroup Global Markets as custodian, 1,137,630 to Citibank N.A., 397,482 to Royal Bank of Canada. Variable prepaid forward sale contracts dated May 26, 2026 cover 341,612 shares, or 1.97% of shares outstanding. Pledging and prepaid forwards are ordinary liquidity tools for concentrated family holdings and imply nothing about Gulf's view of the business; prepaid forwards typically retain capped upside participation and should be treated as a contingent future supply event with undisclosed settlement terms, not as shares sold. The practical consequence for minority holders is a persistent identifiable supply overhang against a float of roughly 13.7M shares, plus contingent collateral-call risk if pledged values fall.

**Input-cost and geopolitical transmission.** Base oils are the primary raw material; the filings flag Middle East conflict, including Strait of Hormuz shipping disruption and base-oil/energy input costs, as a transmission channel, with index contracts passing costs through on a lag that compresses margin first.

**Governance and management-change items.** CEO transition November 2024 (predecessor served under three years), CTO retirement September 2025, Chief Global Operations Officer terminated March 2026, a separation agreement dated April 2026, and Item 5.02 8-Ks dated May 15 and July 7, 2026 whose contents were not read. The departures concentrate in operations and technology — the functions responsible for delivering the savings program. Separately: the Governance Committee Chair received $100,000 in 2025 under a consulting agreement advising the CEO, just below the $120,000 NYSE independence bright line (disclosed, board-reviewed, and defensible on the merits given his operating background, but structurally awkward); PwC audit-related fees rose from $52K to $1,735K with no itemization; the pay-versus-performance benchmark index was switched in a way that reduces measured relative underperformance by roughly 33 points and applies to live PSU awards; and the CEO's 2026 LTIP target grant rose 43.5% to $3.3M following a GAAP loss year. Offsetting these, the Compensation Committee excluded Dipsol's approximately $8.6M EBITDA contribution to produce a 0% payout on the 65%-weighted adjusted EBITDA metric — discretion exercised against management — the 2023-2025 relative-TSR PSU tranche paid 0%, all outstanding options were underwater at year-end, and the CEO pay ratio is 74:1.

**Insider activity.** No open-market purchases by the CEO, CFO or other C-suite officers were observed. A director sold 600 shares at $168.31 on August 4, 2026, small in absolute terms but transacted near the current price. Executives face genuine ownership multiples (CEO 5x salary) and were compliant, so the absence of buying reflects retained equity rather than absent ownership. Nobody has committed cash at these levels.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $230 (+35%)

The trough is behind. Volumes inflected 7% across all three segments in Q2 2026, the first volume-led quarter of the recovery, and net new business wins of 4.6% versus a 2.9% target confirm share capture through the down cycle. The April 2026 refinancing eliminated $824.6M of 2027 maturities and pushed the wall to 2031, leaving 2.1x bank leverage against a 4.25x covenant and $727.6M of undrawn revolver. On $1.89B of sales, the $20-30M transformation program is roughly 130bps of margin; combined with volume leverage, the 18%-plus EBITDA margin target implies roughly $378M of EBITDA and $10.50 of EPS, versus $312.4M and $7.55 today. Consensus is chasing: current-year EPS up 9.9% in 90 days, next-year up 4.5%. At 12x forward EBITDA that is $229. Top five customers at 11% of sales and a service model embedded in customer plants make the revenue base unusually durable for a Materials name, and a $229.4M buyback authorization against a $2.9B cap plus 15.8% short interest provides mechanical support.

### Bear Case — $120 (−29%)

The improvement is one quarter old, volume-led but margin-negative where it matters: non-GAAP gross margin contracted 50bps and non-GAAP operating margin 10bps in the same quarter that produced "record" EBITDA, with roughly 11% of the EBITDA increase coming from equity income in non-consolidated affiliates and part of the rest from a larger D&A add-back. Americas — the largest and historically highest-margin segment — lost earnings in both Q2 and H1 while growing volume, which is what winning share by conceding price looks like. SG&A has added 320bps against flat revenue since 2022 and is still growing faster than sales; the fix does not complete until 2028. H1 cash conversion of 18% funded $38M of shareholder returns with $29M of operations plus borrowing. Demand is derived from customer production volumes into a backdrop of tariff friction the company's own filings cite as having pressured Americas and EMEA, while base-oil inflation from Middle East disruption risks compressing gross margin ahead of index-contract resets. With tangible book at approximately zero and $1,375M of intangibles against $1,373M of equity, a further impairment has no cushion. At 15x a reverted $7.00 of EPS the stock is $105; the 52-week low of $112.18 printed in March 2026.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Quaker Houghton has done the two things it needed to do: it refinanced out of its 2027 maturity wall into an April 2031 structure with 2.1x bank leverage and $727.6M of undrawn capacity, and it produced a volume-led quarter with 7% growth across all three segments after three years of stagnation. Consensus is revising upward, the franchise is genuinely defensible — top five customers at 11% of sales, on-site service model, number-one global share — and the sell side is unanimously constructive.

Both improvements are in the price. At $169.95 the stock trades at 22.3x current-year and 18.3x next-year consensus, 11.7x LTM EV/EBITDA and a 2.8% free cash flow yield, roughly 24% above the year-end 2025 close and 19-20% above management's own May-June repurchase execution at $141-143. My base case of $182 offers 7% upside against a $120 bear case that costs 29%, for a probability-weighted total return near 6%, inadequate compensation for the specific unresolved items: Americas segment earnings falling on rising volume, 18% H1 cash conversion funding debt-financed shareholder returns, SG&A still outgrowing sales with the fix two years out, negative tangible book against $1,375M of intangibles, and a fully pledged 21% shareholder block now partially monetized through prepaid forwards.

This is the fourth consecutive HOLD on this name, and the composition of that HOLD has shifted: financial health and risk have improved on the refinancing while valuation has compressed as the price re-rated. The call is a judgment about entry, not about the business. Existing holders have a defensible position with modest expected appreciation and a 1.25% yield. New capital should wait for either the Q3 10-Q to resolve the Americas and cash-conversion questions, or a price near $145 that restores the margin of safety. I score this 5.31 overall with a hold rationale of uncertain; the two disclosures that would move me are both scheduled for October.
