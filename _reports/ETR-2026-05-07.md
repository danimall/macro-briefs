## Executive Summary

Entergy Corporation is undergoing a structural transformation from a standard regulated utility into the primary power supplier for hyperscaler data centers in the Gulf South. The company raised its 4-year capital expenditure plan by 33% to $57 billion in April 2026, driven by contracted demand from Meta Platforms and Amazon Web Services, though key agreements remain subject to regulatory approval. Q1 2026 industrial electric sales grew 15% YoY, with Mississippi industrial volumes nearly doubling — confirming that the demand thesis is materializing in physical volumes, not just contracts.

The investment case rests on a specific asymmetry: Entergy trades at ~29x trailing EPS of $3.91, which appears expensive against utility peers (median 19.6x). However, trailing earnings represent the pre-transformation baseline. On FY2027E consensus EPS of approximately $5.02 (per sell-side estimates, source/date unverified), the forward P/E compresses to ~22.5x — a premium to peers but potentially justified by an 8.5% retail sales CAGR target that is among the highest in the U.S. utility sector. The primary risks are regulatory approval for the Lightning Initiative (December 2026), ~13% equity dilution through 2029, and customer concentration in AI/data center demand. These risks are real but quantifiable, and the "Fair Share Plus" contractual protections provide meaningful — though untested — downside mitigation against stranded asset scenarios.

## Company Overview

Entergy is a vertically integrated electric utility serving approximately 3 million customers across Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi, Texas, and New Orleans. It owns and operates a diversified generation fleet including five nuclear reactors (Arkansas Nuclear One Units 1&2, River Bend, Waterford 3, and Grand Gulf), gas-fired generation, and a rapidly expanding solar portfolio.

The defining characteristic today is Entergy's geographic positioning at the center of hyperscaler data center buildout. Meta Platforms is constructing what may become the largest data center complex in the world at Richland Parish, Louisiana, potentially scaling to 5 GW of power demand with reported investment growing from $10B to potentially $50B. AWS is building multiple campuses across Mississippi. To serve this demand, Entergy has filed with the LPSC for approval of seven new combined cycle gas plants totaling 5,278 MW (at approximately $12.9 billion) specifically linked to Meta's second data center, in addition to other generation projects already under construction or approved across its service territory.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** Q1 2026 revenue grew 12% YoY to $3.19B (vs. $2.85B). Operating income for full year 2025 reached $3.2B, up from $2.7B in 2024. The growth trajectory is accelerating as data center loads come online.

**Balance Sheet:** Total assets reached $75.8B as of March 2026 (up from $71.9B at year-end). Long-term debt stands at $31.15B, with a debt-to-capital ratio of approximately 66%. This is high but normal for a regulated utility in aggressive build mode, and the company maintains compliance with its consolidated debt ratio covenant (calculated differently than GAAP debt-to-capital). The parent $3B credit facility ($2.997B available after $3M in letters of credit) expires June 2030.

**Cash Flow:** Q1 2026 operating cash flow of $829M (up from $536M) vs. investing outflows of $2.4B demonstrates the funding gap inherent in the growth strategy. Financing activities of +$3.2B filled this gap via debt and equity issuance.

**Capital Program:** The $57.2B four-year plan (2026-2029) breaks down as: $38.2B generation, $9.2B transmission, $8.5B distribution, $1.4B support. Funding sources include $6.6B equity issuance, $3B junior subordinated debentures, customer advance payments from Meta/AWS, and subsidiary-level mortgage bonds.

**Dividend:** $2.56 annualized (2.3% yield at current price). While below peer median of 3.3%, the lower payout reflects reinvestment capacity in high-return rate base growth.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is quantifiable and already materializing:

1. **Industrial sales +15% YoY in Q1 2026.** Mississippi industrial sales nearly doubled (+91%). Arkansas industrial +20%. This is confirmed physical demand, not forecasts.

2. **8.5% retail sales CAGR target through 2029** — among the highest in the U.S. utility sector. This translates to rate base growth of approximately 10-12% annually given the capital intensity.

3. **EPS trajectory:** From $3.91 (2025) to company-guided midpoint of $4.35 (2026) to sell-side consensus of approximately $5.02 (2027E, source/date unverified) = estimated 12-14% EPS growth annually. This growth rate is exceptional for a regulated utility and is underpinned by contracted demand with investment-grade counterparties, though subject to regulatory approvals.

4. **Regulatory visibility:** Meta's "Fair Share Plus" agreements are structured so that the customer bears incremental costs of service, provides parent guarantees, and covers early termination obligations. These protections appear meaningful, though the exact scope of stranded-asset insulation depends on contract terms and regulatory treatment that have not been publicly tested.

5. **Equipment supply locked in:** Exclusivity agreement with a major vendor for 24+ power island equipment sets provides first-mover advantage in a supply-constrained turbine market.

The primary growth risk is timing and regulatory approval: the LPSC's Lightning Initiative decision in December 2026 gates ~$12.9B of the new generation capacity. A 4-1 preliminary vote in April 2026 suggests probable approval, but final regulatory terms (allowed ROE, cost caps) will determine the actual earnings contribution. The second Meta/Evest electric service agreement is itself conditional — obligations commence only after construction of needed transmission facilities and the agreement's effectiveness is conditioned on required governmental approvals, including LPSC approval.

## Competitive Landscape

Entergy occupies a structurally advantaged position for several reasons:

1. **Geography:** Louisiana and Mississippi offer low land costs, supportive industrial policy, and political willingness to fast-track approvals. The Lightning Initiative legislation was purpose-built to attract data center investment.

2. **Vertical integration:** Regulated utilities can guarantee long-term power reliability — a requirement for hyperscaler campuses targeting 99.999% uptime. Merchant generation or third-party PPAs carry counterparty and availability risks that hyperscalers have shown they want to avoid.

3. **First-mover equipment access:** The exclusivity agreement for 24+ turbine sets locks in supply in a market where delivery timelines are extending to 2-3 years. Competing utilities that haven't secured equipment face structural delays.

4. **Nuclear fleet:** Five operating nuclear reactors provide zero-carbon baseload generation with Inflation Reduction Act production tax credits. The second Meta agreement includes provisions that could support future nuclear-related initiatives, though no concrete nuclear expansion plan has been publicly disclosed.

5. **Incumbent relationships:** Meta's first data center is already under construction, creating switching costs for the second campus. AWS has multiple Mississippi sites already operational.

Entergy's cumulative TSR of 222.6 vs. peer group 153.0 reflects the market's recognition of this positioning advantage.

## Risk Assessment

**Regulatory Risk (High):** The December 2026 LPSC decision on 7 new gas plants ($12.9B) is binary. The April 2026 preliminary vote was 4-1 favorable, and bipartisan political support exists, but final terms could impose cost caps below projected costs (as Arkansas did with Jefferson Power Station, setting benchmarks $90M below Entergy's estimates). A denial or significant delay would remove the largest single growth driver. The second Meta/Evest agreement itself is conditional on these regulatory approvals — it is contracted but not yet effective.

**Customer Concentration (High):** Meta and AWS represent a transformational proportion of future load growth. If AI demand disappoints — whether from efficiency improvements, regulatory constraints on AI, or hyperscaler capital discipline — Entergy faces stranded asset risk on generation purpose-built for specific customers. Mitigant: Fair Share Plus contractual protections and parent guarantees, though these are untested in a demand-shortfall scenario.

**Equity Dilution (Moderate):** $6.6B equity issuance on a $51.6B market cap = ~13% dilution through 2029. EPS growth must outpace dilution for shareholders to benefit. Consensus estimates incorporating this dilution still show 12-14% EPS growth, suggesting the rate base expansion more than offsets share count increases.

**Leverage and Refinancing (Moderate):** At approximately 66% debt-to-capital approaching covenant limits, Entergy requires continuous capital market access at reasonable rates. Each $1B of new debt adds meaningful annual interest expense in the current rate environment. Credit downgrade risk would meaningfully increase borrowing costs across the $57B program.

**Construction Execution (Moderate):** Multiple new gas plants, solar facilities, major transmission lines, and hundreds of miles of distribution upgrades — all simultaneously — creates project management complexity. Supply chain disruptions, tariff risks, and weather events (Winter Storm Fern cost ~$480M) add uncertainty.

**Nuclear Regulatory (Low-Moderate):** Grand Gulf's preliminary "white" NRC finding on the emergency diesel generator could result in increased scrutiny if finalized.

## Conclusion

Entergy represents a regulated utility with contracted, high-visibility growth at a valuation premium justified by fundamentals. The estimated 12-14% EPS growth trajectory is 2-3x the utility peer group, supported by physical demand already materializing (Q1 industrial +15% YoY, Mississippi +91%), contractual protections from investment-grade hyperscalers, and first-mover equipment supply advantages.

The stock is not cheap at ~22.5x FY2027E, and the risks — regulatory (December 2026 LPSC), concentration (Meta/AWS), leverage (~66%), and dilution (13%) — are genuine. The second Meta/Evest agreement remains conditional on regulatory approvals, and the base-case risk/reward ratio reflects meaningful downside in an adverse scenario. These factors prevent a higher-conviction call. But the structural demand from AI infrastructure buildout is among the most confirmed secular themes in the current market, Entergy is the primary beneficiary in its geography, and the forward growth rate compensates for the premium multiple. BUY with 2-3% position sizing, with the understanding that the December 2026 LPSC decision represents the key binary event.
