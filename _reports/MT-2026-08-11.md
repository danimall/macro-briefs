## Executive Summary

ArcelorMittal is in the middle of a genuine operating inflection that the equity has largely already discounted. First-half 2026 company-defined EBITDA of $3,743m rose 8.8% year over year on shipments that fell 4.4% — unit EBITDA of $143/t versus $125/t — and the second quarter accelerated to $2,064m ($155/t), up 22.9% sequentially. The mechanism is European price-cost recovery: Europe's segment EBITDA per tonne rose from $70.5 in 1Q26 to $97.6 in 2Q26, closing the gap to North America ($172.4/t) from 48% to 57% of that level. Critically, that improvement arrived *before* the EU's new tariff-rate-quota regime took effect on July 1, 2026 (quotas for close trade partners cut 33%, 50% duty above quota, layered on CBAM). If management is right that order books "have inflected positively following the implementation of the TRQ," the policy benefit is additive to what is already in the numbers.

Against that, the price has done the work. The stock is up roughly 120% over twelve months and trades 3.1% below its 52-week high, at 1.01x attributable book value ($72.70/share derived) versus roughly 0.3x book in FY2024, and at approximately 9.0x annualized 1H26 EBITDA on an enterprise value of about $67.4bn. Consensus mean price target is $74.03 — the market price. More troubling, current-year consensus EPS has been cut 10.3% over 90 days (to $4.48 from $5.00) and next-quarter estimates cut 5.5%, while the shares rallied. Q2 EPS of $0.89-0.90 missed the $1.06-1.09 consensus by roughly 16%, the second miss in four quarters. First-half free cash flow was negative $1,493m, net debt rose from $7,931m at December 31 to $9,500m at June 30, and the bridge back down depends on releasing a $2.0bn seasonal working-capital build out of a $19,283m inventory balance (~124 days) at the same time three restarted blast furnaces are refilling the supply chain.

The result is a business improving on the metrics that matter for mid-cycle earnings power, held in an equity where fair value and market price have converged. We rate MT HOLD. Existing owners are paid to wait through a catalyst-rich second half — TRQ pass-through, guided sequential volume growth in every segment, working-capital release, and a policy-formula buyback that mechanically scales with 2H free cash flow. New capital has no margin of safety at 1.0x book with a [unverified — beta N/A in Phase 1 data] beta and near-term estimates falling; we would deploy at $60-64.

## Company Overview

ArcelorMittal is a Luxembourg-domiciled integrated steel and mining group, structured across six reported segments plus an equity-accounted India/JV bucket. Europe remains the largest revenue block (FY2025 segment revenue ~$28.8bn of $61.4bn gross) and delivered $697m of 2Q26 EBITDA on 7,138 kt of shipments; North America contributed $488m on 2,831 kt; Brazil $401m on 3,568 kt; Mining (AMMC Canada plus Liberia) $179m on 9.4 Mt of iron ore; Sustainable Solutions — European downstream, tubular, and Indian renewables — $142m. Ukraine and South Africa sit in "Others."

Two structural features distinguish MT from a regional steelmaker. First, its fastest-growing asset is not consolidated: AMNS India is a 60% equity interest generating $452m of 1H26 EBITDA on a 100% basis, up 50.2% from $301m, and reaching group results only through the $406m "income from associates, joint ventures and other investments" line. Group EBITDA therefore understates MT's economic exposure to Indian steel demand — while Indian cash and capex remain at the JV level rather than at the parent's disposal. Second, MT is vertically integrated into iron ore: 26.4 Mt produced in 1H26 (+11.9%), of which AMMC and Liberia contributed 19.8 Mt, largely sold to third parties. That integration was a tailwind in 2021 and is a headwind now, with Mining EBITDA down 17.9% year over year despite 8.4% higher shipments.

The company is a foreign private issuer filing Form 20-F rather than 10-K, and files no proxy statement. The FY2025 20-F (filed March 6, 2026) was not retrievable for this work, so risk-factor changes, debt covenant terms, major-shareholder detail, executive compensation structure, and audited FY2025 statements are unverified here. There is also no Form 4 insider-transaction record, as foreign private issuers are generally exempt from Section 16 reporting. Both gaps are reflected in the Management Quality and Risk scores below.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and mix.** Revenue fell from $79.8bn in FY2022 to $61.4bn in FY2025 — a 23% decline driven by price, not share. The inflection is visible in 1H26: sales of $32,218m, up 4.9%, on 10.2% higher average steel selling prices partially offset by 4.4% lower shipments (26.2 Mt versus 27.4 Mt) and 5.5% lower crude production. This is a price-led, volume-negative recovery; management guides volume recovery as the 2H event, with shipments "expected to exceed 1H 2026 across all segments" and Europe 3Q "stable to slightly higher" against a typical high-single-digit seasonal decline.

**Margin trajectory is the thesis.** Sequential segment unit-margin moves (segment EBITDA ÷ segment shipments): Europe $70.5 → $97.6/t (+$27.1), North America $146.0 → $172.4 (+$26.4), Brazil $97.3 → $112.4 (+$15.1), Mining $29.9 → $19.0 per tonne of ore shipped (−$10.9). Three of four steel-facing segments expanded materially; the mining offset is the only deterioration, and it reflects a mix of genuine unit-cost inflation (freight, Liberia concentrator ramp, sinter-feed mix) and timing effects (1H25 benefited from AMMC shipping accumulated Port-Cartier inventory; 2Q26 was hit by Liberian rainfall and port constraints).

**Earnings quality — the EBITDA/EPS gap.** EBITDA rose 22.9% sequentially and 11.0% year over year, yet EPS missed by ~16%. The bridge is entirely below EBITDA: FX and other net financing losses of $366m in 1H26 versus $123m of income in 1H25 (a $489m swing on a 3.0% USD appreciation against the euro, following a 12.8% depreciation in the prior-year period); net interest of $269m versus $121m, on a larger debt base post-Calvert consolidation and the loss of Kazakhstan-receivable interest income; and depreciation of $1,529m versus $1,353m. Two important framings need correction in opposite directions. The widely reported "net income down 62%" compares against a 2Q25 base containing $1,162m of net exceptional gains, chiefly the $1,742m Calvert bargain-purchase gain; on adjusted-to-adjusted terms the decline is 32%. But the FX and interest drags are not one-offs to be excluded indefinitely — a USD-reporting company with a euro-heavy cost base will keep producing translation swings, and the higher interest expense is a permanent consequence of debt-funded consolidation. FY2026 net interest is guided at ~$550m and depreciation at ~$3.0bn.

**Cash flow is where the stress sits.** 1H26 operating cash flow of $952m (versus $1,062m) absorbed a $1,981m working-capital investment; capex of $2,373m (up from $1,853m) produced reported free cash flow of negative $1,493m against negative $837m a year earlier. Management's "$0.5bn of underlying free cash flow" adds back the entire working-capital draw and treats $0.8bn of strategic capex as discretionary; it is a non-GAAP construction and should be read as one. Inventories of $19,283m are 60.3% of current assets and have risen from $18,589m at December 31 — roughly 124 days on FY2025 cost of sales. The 2H release is a real and meaningful cash event, but it is not contractually assured, and the simultaneous restart of Fos, Dąbrowa, and Asturias is precisely the condition under which inventory can stay elevated.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $99,150m, liabilities $42,327m, equity $56,823m (parent $54,820m). Gross debt $14,421m against $4,898m cash gives net debt of $9,500m — 1.27x annualized 1H26 EBITDA and 16.7% of equity. Liquidity is $10.4bn, average maturity 7.6 years, the $5.5bn RCF now runs to May 29, 2031, and only $2.2bn matures in the balance of 2026 and $1.9bn in 2027. There is no refinancing cliff. The legitimate concern is direction rather than level: net debt has risen for three consecutive years through the earnings trough while capex ran at 1.5-1.7x depreciation and $0.7bn was returned to shareholders in 1H alone. Third-party Altman Z of 2.07 sits below the 2.99 "safe" line, which correctly flags thin earnings against a $99bn asset base rather than near-term insolvency risk. A separate item deserves flagging: deferred tax assets of $8,561m (8.6% of assets, declining sequentially) support a 1H26 effective tax rate of 19.7% and a TTM effective rate of 10.4%; 2Q25 contained a ~$0.2bn DTA reversal as precedent, and normalization toward a blended statutory rate would compress EPS in a way the forward multiple does not obviously reflect.

**Financial Health: 6.0.** Low leverage, long maturities, ample liquidity, and no covenant cliff we can see (though covenant terms are unverified) against negative first-half FCF, three years of rising net debt, a heavy inventory position, and capex well above depreciation.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is cyclical and policy-driven; medium-term growth is project-driven and back-loaded.

The cyclical leg has three identifiable drivers into 2H26. First, the TRQ regime: quotas for close trade partners cut 33%, half of quota volume reserved for FTA partners, and a 50% duty above quota, effective July 1 — independently confirmed by Reuters, Bloomberg, WSJ, and Eurofer. Second, restarted European capacity: Fos restarted end-July after 34 months idle, Dąbrowa on April 28, with European crude production already up 10.5% sequentially. Third, guided sequential shipment growth across every segment against a seasonal pattern that normally declines.

The project leg is the ~$1.8bn incremental EBITDA pipeline, and its timing matters. Gijón's 1.1 Mt EAF ($50m) and Hazira Phase 1 complete in 2H26; Mardyck ($100m) and Calvert NOES ($230m) in 2H27; Indian renewables ($150m combined) in 1H28; Dunkerque's 2 Mtpa EAF ($100m) not until 2030. Usefully, the pipeline is benchmarked to 2015-2020 average prices and spreads — a mid-cycle-to-weak base, which makes the estimate more defensible than a spot-based figure — but it is not a 2026 or 2027 earnings number. India optionality (Hazira to 24 Mtpa, an 8.2 Mtpa Andhra Pradesh greenfield, a "pathway to 40 Mt") is explicitly conditioned on regulatory approvals and a long-term NMDC iron ore supply agreement that is not disclosed as obtained.

Consensus captures both legs awkwardly. Current-year EPS is $4.48, cut 9.5% in 30 days and 10.3% in 90 days; current-quarter and next-quarter estimates are down 3.4% and 5.5%. Next-year consensus is $7.30, *raised* 4.2% — implying roughly 63% earnings growth off a base that is itself being marked down. That split is the honest summary of the growth picture: the market and the sell side believe the 2027 policy-plus-volume story while marking down 2026 delivery. With two misses in the last four quarters (−14.8% and −15.9%) against a consensus the company itself publishes ahead of results, we discount the out-year figure rather than anchor on it.

**Growth Prospects: 6.0.** Real cyclical and policy-driven acceleration in unit margins, a credible and conservatively benchmarked project pipeline, and a structurally under-counted 60% Indian JV growing EBITDA 50% — offset by falling near-term estimates, volume declines still in the trailing data, and project economics that mostly land in 2027-2030.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation inputs conflict and need reconciling. The profile field pairs FY2025 EPS of $4.11 with a trailing P/E of 30.88, which is internally inconsistent ($73.29 ÷ $4.11 = 17.8x); the 30.9x figure reconciles to TTM EPS of approximately $2.36 ($73.29 ÷ $2.36 = 31.1x). We therefore treat trailing P/E as ~31x on depressed TTM earnings and use consensus for forward work.

On consensus: 16.4x current-year EPS of $4.48 and 10.0x next-year EPS of $7.30. On assets: 1.01x attributable book value of $72.70/share, versus roughly 0.3x book in FY2024 and 0.89x sales against 0.26-0.34x in FY2022-FY2024. On enterprise value: market cap of ~$55.9bn plus $9.5bn net debt plus $2.0bn NCI gives ~$67.4bn, or approximately 9.0x annualized 1H26 EBITDA of $7.49bn and roughly 8.5x an FY26 run-rate of $7.8-8.0bn if 2Q26 margins hold.

That last metric is the one we weight most, and it is not cheap. Integrated steel has historically cleared at 5-7x mid-cycle EV/EBITDA; 8.5x forward requires believing the TRQ/CBAM regime permanently re-rates European steel margins. Cross-checking against book value: at 1.1x a forward book of ~$78 the stock is worth ~$86; at 0.8x it is worth ~$62. The dispersion is wide because the multiple, not the earnings, is doing the work.

Peers frame the discount. Nucor carries a similar $62.1bn market cap on far lower revenue at 21.9x earnings; Steel Dynamics 23.6x; Ternium 15.1x; Gerdau 11.8x; ThyssenKrupp 10.2x. MT's 0.89x sales versus the US minimills' richer multiples is the market's price for a 9.2% TTM EBITDA margin, 4.1-4.3% operating margin, a European and emerging-market asset base, and a controlled Luxembourg structure. The discount is real; it is also mostly rational, and it has already narrowed substantially.

Sell-side positioning offers no support. Mean target $74.03 is 1.0% above the close; median $77.50 (+5.7%); high $82.50 (+12.6%); low $56.40 (−23.0%). Ratings are 1 strong buy, 3 buy, 3 hold from seven analysts, unchanged for four months. The analyst-targets-as-floors heuristic applies to inflection stocks with rising estimates and cheap multiples; here the current-year estimate is falling 10% per quarter of revision cycle and the enterprise multiple is above mid-cycle norms, so we treat the mean target as informative rather than stale.

**Valuation: 5.0.** Genuinely inexpensive on 2027 consensus if delivered (10x), fairly-to-fully valued on EV/EBITDA and book, with the entire case resting on consensus earnings reaching roughly 3x TTM levels. That is a fair price with two-sided risk, not asymmetric upside.

## Competitive Landscape

MT's competitive trajectory is improving on the dimension that matters most — European unit profitability — from a structurally weak absolute level. Europe went from $70.5/t to $97.6/t sequentially, still the lowest of the three steel segments but closing. The blast-furnace restarts are the strongest evidence in the release precisely because they are costly and hard to reverse: committing capital and labor to restart Fos after 34 months idle is a more informative signal of management's demand conviction than any guidance sentence. The counter-reading is that restarts also claim quota-protected share ahead of competitors and defend customer relationships, and they raise the fixed-cost base such that a demand disappointment hurts more.

Versus European peers, MT is not a European pure-play: 2Q26 non-Europe steel EBITDA of $889m (North America $488m plus Brazil $401m) exceeded Europe's $697m, before $179m of Mining and $229m of JV income. ThyssenKrupp, Salzgitter, and SSAB have no equivalent Brazilian position, no Liberian iron ore, and no 60% stake in the fastest-growing large steel market. Versus US peers, MT lacks the scrap-based cost structure and domestic tariff shelter and carries far more geopolitical exposure.

Product positioning is being deliberately tilted toward electrification: 155 kt/yr of non-grain-oriented electrical steel at Mardyck (125 kt automotive) and up to 150 kt/yr at Calvert, rails and wire rod from the new Gijón EAF, insulated panels from a new US Building Solutions plant. Management's framing of steel demand from "AI led infrastructure build out" should be read as thematic — the linkage runs through grid, transmission, and transformer steels, with no disclosed contract, customer, or quantified revenue attached.

**Competitive Position: 6.0.** Improving trajectory in the weakest region, real geographic diversification, and vertical ore integration, against persistently thin group margins versus US EAF peers and a mining franchise whose unit economics are deteriorating as volumes ramp.

## Risk Assessment

**The Europe thesis is a policy trade with a disclosed loophole.** Management itself notes that "Melt & Pour" requirements are not yet in place and "could further strengthen the integrity and effectiveness of the system" — the company is publicly identifying an active circumvention channel whereby non-EU steel can be minimally processed in a quota-favored jurisdiction. Trade-partner reaction is already visible (South Korea convening industry over a ~510,000 t quota cut), raising retaliation and WTO-challenge risk, and half of quota volume is reserved for FTA partners, so protection is partial.

**Restart operating leverage is symmetric.** If the guided 2H volume recovery does not materialize, fixed-cost absorption reverses on a base that now includes three reactivated European furnaces that cannot be cheaply re-idled.

**Working capital and net debt.** Net debt rose $1,569m in six months while $0.7bn was returned and $2,373m spent on capex. The path to lower net debt runs through releasing $2.0bn from a $19.3bn inventory position while restarted capacity refills the chain.

**Deferred tax assets and the tax rate.** $8,561m of DTAs (8.6% of assets, declining) support a 10.4% TTM effective rate, with a documented ~$0.2bn reversal precedent in 2Q25. Normalization is an EPS risk not evident in the multiple.

**Associate carrying values.** $9,937m of investments in associates and JVs, down from $10,494m, including a $0.3bn Baffinland impairment in 2Q26. Because the India growth story is equity-accounted, a large slice of value sits in a line with limited disclosure and demonstrated impairment sensitivity.

**Geopolitical and FX.** "Others" contains Ukraine and South Africa; Liberia carries weather and sovereign-contract risk (a $200m payment to the Government of Liberia in 1Q26 extended the Mineral Development Agreement to 2050); Argentina exposure is acknowledged through USD-linked instruments held to preserve cash value. FX translation produced a ~$490m year-over-year swing on a $1.26bn net income base.

**Fixed outflows.** The Votorantim settlement leaves ~$0.1bn due in each of 2027 and 2028.

**Disclosure gaps.** Risk factors, covenants, major shareholders, board independence, related-party transactions, and compensation structure are unverified for want of 20-F access. Roughly 45.4% of shares sit outside the free float (411.99m of 754.04m), consistent with a large controlling block, and there is no insider-transaction record available at all.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 50.2% against 42.8% one-year realized is rich by 7.4 points, with a flat term structure (49.2% at six months) — the market is pricing elevated but evenly distributed uncertainty rather than a single dated event. Positioning is put-heavy: put/call volume ratio 2.68 overall, 3.11 at the one-week tenor and 10.65 at three months, with open-interest ratio 1.62. Skew is balanced near-term (−1.1%) but steepens to −3.8% at six months. Short interest is negligible at 0.6% of float and 1.3 days to cover, at 2,316,809 shares.

Read together: there is no bearish equity positioning left to unwind and no squeeze fuel, so further gains require incremental fundamental buyers, while option buyers are paying above-realized volatility for downside protection into the autumn. That combination is consistent with our view that the risk/reward has flattened at this price rather than with either a breakout or an imminent break.

**Risk Factors: 4.2.** Multiple specific, dated, and partly unpriced downside catalysts — TRQ durability and the Melt & Pour gap, the working-capital release, DTA recoverability, associate impairment sensitivity — compounded by high implied volatility and material disclosure gaps.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $95 (+29.6%)

The TRQ regime proves durable and Melt & Pour rules are added, closing the circumvention channel. European utilization rises with the three restarts absorbing fixed cost, and Europe's unit EBITDA advances from $97.6/t toward $130/t. Volumes recover sequentially in every segment as guided, releasing the full $2.0bn working-capital build; FY26 free cash flow lands positive $1.4-1.9bn and year-end net debt falls to ~$7.0-7.5bn. Mining unit margins recover as Liberia ramp costs anniversary and AMMC port constraints clear. AMNS India compounds from a $452m 1H26 base as Hazira Phase 1 completes. FY2027 EPS reaches the $7.30 consensus; the 50%-of-post-dividend-FCF buyback formula scales, and the market applies 13x. Cross-check: FY27 EBITDA of ~$10bn at 8x EV/EBITDA less $9bn net debt and $2bn NCI supports approximately $91/share before buyback accretion.

### Bear Case — $52 (−29.0%)

The 2Q26 European price-cost gain proves to have been pre-buying ahead of July 1 rather than the leading edge of policy benefit, and quota allocations are absorbed within two quarters while the Melt & Pour gap channels re-routed tonnage into the EU. Restarted capacity meets a soft order book; inventories stay near $19bn and the working-capital release slips into 2027, leaving FY26 FCF negative and net debt above $9.5bn. Mining unit margins do not recover. The effective tax rate normalizes toward statutory, compressing EPS independent of operations. FY2027 EPS settles at $4.00-4.50 and the multiple compresses to 12x, or equivalently the stock returns to 0.7x book — both routes converge near $52. The 52-week low of $31.93 is only twelve months behind, and the low sell-side target is $56.40.

### Base Case — $78 (+6.4%)

TRQ delivers a partial, real benefit; 2H volumes rise but less than the full guided ramp; roughly three-quarters of the working-capital build releases, taking year-end net debt to $8.0-8.5bn. FY2027 EPS of approximately $7.10 (a modest haircut to consensus for the two-miss track record and tax normalization) at 11x supports $78. Adding the ~0.8% dividend and ~1.7% buyback yield gives a total expected return of roughly 9% over twelve months.

Probability-weighting 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear yields roughly +2% price return plus ~2.5% shareholder yield. That is approximately fair — the definition of HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE, and not weak enough for REDUCE given 1.27x leverage, $10.4bn liquidity, no maturity cliff, and a book-value floor near the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth_hold).** There is a specific, dated 12-month positive thesis — TRQ pass-through, guided sequential volume growth, and the working-capital release — but at $73.29 the price already reflects it, and the entry offers no margin of safety.

**Targets from $73.29:** base $78 (+6.4%), bull $95 (+29.6%), bear $52 (−29.0%). Timeframe: 12 months, anchored on the 3Q26 release (late October 2026) and the FY2026 release plus 20-F (February/March 2027).

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY if:**
1. Price reaches $60-64 (roughly 0.83-0.88x book, ~13-14x current-year consensus) with the operating trend intact — that restores 20-25% upside to base case.
2. 3Q26 European shipments are flat or higher sequentially against the normal high-single-digit seasonal decline, **and** Europe unit EBITDA exceeds $110/t — confirming the TRQ benefit is additive rather than pre-bought.
3. 3Q26 reported free cash flow turns positive with net debt declining below $9.0bn, evidencing the working-capital release.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if:**
1. 3Q26 Europe shipments decline sequentially in line with normal seasonality, or Europe unit EBITDA falls back below $85/t.
2. Inventories remain above $19.0bn at September 30 with net debt above $9.5bn — the release slipping to 2027 while capex runs at $4.5-5.0bn.
3. Current-year consensus EPS is cut a further 10%+ (below ~$4.00) or a third consecutive quarter misses by more than 10%.
4. A DTA write-down or a step-change in the effective tax rate toward statutory is disclosed.

**Downgrade to AVOID if** the European quota regime is materially weakened by retaliation, WTO challenge, or unaddressed Melt & Pour circumvention, removing the policy leg of the thesis while the restarted fixed-cost base remains in place.

**Next reassessment:** the 3Q26 results release, which is the first quarter fully inside the TRQ regime and the first test of both the volume guide and the working-capital release.

## Conclusion

The operating case for ArcelorMittal is stronger than the reported earnings suggest: unit EBITDA rose 14.4% year over year on 4.4% lower volumes, three of four steel-facing segments expanded margins sequentially, the entire EPS shortfall sits in translation FX, higher post-Calvert interest, and depreciation, and the July 1 European tariff-quota regime had not yet touched the quarter's numbers. Restarting a furnace idled for 34 months is expensive, publicly observable, and hard to reverse — the most credible signal in the release.

The investment case is weaker, because the equity has already moved from roughly 0.3x book to 1.01x book and from 0.28x sales to 0.89x sales, leaving consensus mean target at the market price, an enterprise value near 8.5-9.0x forward EBITDA against a 5-7x mid-cycle norm for integrated steel, negative first-half free cash flow, net debt up $1.57bn in six months, current-year estimates down 10.3% in 90 days, and put-heavy option positioning paying above-realized volatility for protection. The 2027 consensus of $7.30 makes the stock look cheap at 10x; that number requires earnings roughly triple the trailing base, from a company that has missed twice in four quarters against a consensus it publishes itself.

We rate MT **HOLD**. Own it through a catalyst-dense second half in which the TRQ benefit, the volume ramp, the working-capital release, and a formula-driven buyback can all land — but do not pay 1.0x book for a steelmaker whose near-term estimates are still being cut. The price we want is $60-64.
