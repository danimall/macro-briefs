## Executive Summary

Cytek Biosciences sells spectral flow cytometers, cell sorters, reagents and service contracts, differentiated by Full Spectrum Profiling, which captures the entire emission spectrum across multiple lasers and computationally unmixes signatures rather than assigning one detector per dye. The technology wedge is real: 3,500-plus peer-reviewed publications reference FSP products since the 2017 Aurora launch, and the installed base reached 3,933 instruments at June 30, 2026, adding 142 units in Q2. The commercial result has not followed. FY2025 revenue grew 0.5% (−1.1% on the company's own constant-currency basis), spectral and imaging unit volumes fell 6.0%, and product gross margin has compressed from 61.6% in FY2022 to roughly 52% today.

Two facts dominate the current setup. First, the valuation is genuinely depressed: cash and marketable securities of $262.0M against $9.7M of debt leaves $252.3M of net cash, or $1.94 per share on 129.98M shares, 46% of the share price. Enterprise value of approximately $291M against TTM revenue of $206.7M is 1.41x sales, and 1.39x the raised FY2026 guidance midpoint of $209.5M. For a tools company with 52% gross margins and recurring revenue at 35% of trailing sales (up from 32%), that multiple prices structural doubt rather than a growth business.

Second, that doubt has a specific and imminent test. Beckman Coulter, a Danaher subsidiary and named competitor, goes to jury trial in the District of Delaware on August 17, 2026, asserting three patents against the Aurora, Aurora CS, Northern Lights and Northern Lights-CLC. Those products generate essentially all of Cytek's product revenue. No loss reserve is disclosed in either the FY2025 10-K or the Q2 2026 10-Q, and no damages claim, royalty demand or injunction request is quantified anywhere in the filings. The docket pattern reads net-favorable to Cytek: the court ordered Beckman to narrow claims, Beckman dropped Patent 12,174,106 entirely, and Cytek's Daubert motions were granted in July 2026. But Cytek also lost its own summary-judgment motions, meaning the court found triable issues on infringement and validity. A jury verdict on a bet-the-product-line case, arriving within weeks, with no reserve and no quantified exposure, cannot be underwritten from the outside. HOLD, with an explicit acknowledgment that the recommendation is a statement of low confidence rather than of balance.

## Company Overview

Cytek operates one reportable segment, with the CEO as chief operating decision maker using net income as the segment profit measure. Product families: the Aurora flagship analyzer (2017), the lower-cost Northern Lights (2018, with the CLC variant CE-marked under IVDD and registered under EU IVDR and as a Class II IVD in China), the Aurora CS sorter (2021), Aurora Evo (2025, with expanded automation configurations added in Q2 2026), and the newly launched Borealis, a 7-laser FSP platform marketed for 60-color panels with proprietary reagents. The Amnis and Guava imaging and conventional cytometry lines were acquired from Luminex in February 2023 for $44.9M cash.

One scope limitation deserves emphasis because it is frequently glossed over: in the United States, Cytek's products are Research Use Only. Clinical marketing is limited to the Northern Lights-CLC and certain reagents, and only in China and the EU. The "clinical expansion" narrative has no FDA-cleared U.S. vehicle today.

Manufacturing sits in Fremont and San Diego (reagents), Wuxi China, and Singapore, with 702 full-time employees at year-end 2025. Named competitors in the 10-K include Danaher/Beckman Coulter, Thermo Fisher, Becton Dickinson, Agilent, Sony Biotechnology, Bio-Rad, Miltenyi and Standard BioTools, several of them 50 to 500 times Cytek's size. The patent estate is thinner than the technology story implies: 34 issued U.S. utility patents plus small foreign portfolios and 70 pending applications.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue: reacceleration of low quality

| ($M) | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product revenue | 156.7 | 153.3 | 144.2 |
| Service revenue | 36.3 | 47.2 | 57.3 |
| **Total** | **193.0** | **200.5** | **201.5** |
| Gross margin | 56.7% | 55.4% | 51.8% |
| Operating income | (27.8) | (20.5) | (40.4) |
| Net income | (12.1) | (6.0) | (66.5) |

FY2025's flat top line masked a 5.9% product-revenue decline and a 6.0% drop in spectral/imaging unit volumes; service revenue grew 21.3% and carried the consolidated number. Service is a valuable installed-base annuity, but it lags rather than leads demand.

H1 2026 revenue of $92.3M (+6.0%) and Q2 of $48.1M (+5.6%) are a genuine improvement, and both concentrations matter. Geographically, U.S. revenue rose 24.0% to $52.6M while EMEA fell 7.6%, APAC fell 6.5% and other regions fell 51.6%; the entire $10.2M of H1 growth came from the U.S. against $5.0M of decline elsewhere. By channel, Q2 direct revenue was $33.2M versus $33.5M a year earlier (−0.8%), while distributor revenue rose 23.1% to $14.9M, lifting distributor share from 26.6% to 31.0%. Cytek's own MD&A states distributors "typically receive discounted prices, thus resulting in lower gross margins."

So all consolidated growth ran through the lower-margin channel while the direct channel was flat. Two consequences: a built-in gross-margin headwind, and reduced visibility into end demand, since distributor sell-in is not sell-through. The apparent customer-mix improvement, with academia/government down 12.1% to 39.7% of revenue and biopharma/distributor/CRO up 21.7%, is partly a reclassification artifact, because the 10-Q footnotes that distributors sell to end customers in the other categories. Direct customer concentration is separately reassuring and stable: no customer above 10% of revenue in FY2023, FY2024, FY2025 or H1 2026.

### The Q2 margin expansion was a tariff refund

Reported Q2 2026 GAAP gross margin was 58.9% versus 52.3%. Nearly all of the improvement is a one-time $2.8M reduction in cost of goods sold from tariff refunds received after the February 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling.

| Q2 | 2026 reported | 2026 ex-refund | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total gross margin | 58.9% | 53.1% | 52.3% |
| Product gross margin | 60.4% | 51.8% | 52.5% |
| Service gross margin | 55.6% | 55.6% | 52.0% |

Ex-refund product gross margin declined about 70bps year over year. The entire durable improvement came from service (+365bps on lower service material costs). Applying the same adjustment down the P&L: Q2 operating loss of $11.4M becomes −$14.2M ex-refund versus −$10.6M a year earlier, a 34% wider loss on 5.6% more revenue. Adjusted EBITDA of −$1.5M becomes roughly −$4.3M ex-refund versus +$1.3M in Q2 2025. Any model extrapolating the 59% headline will be materially wrong, and the tariff line reverses: the 10-Q notes U.S. tariffs on certain Singapore and China imports rose from 10% to 12.5%.

### The cost problem is litigation-driven G&A

| G&A | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reported ($M) | 43.1 | 58.9 | 26.4 | 35.3 |
| Ex-stock comp ($M) | 31.9 | 48.0 (+50.4%) | 20.6 | 30.2 (+46.1%) |
| % of revenue | 21.5% | 29.2% | 30.4% | 38.2% |

Stock compensation within G&A was roughly flat ($11.2M → $11.0M), so the increase is cash. The 10-K attributes it primarily to patent litigation; the 10-Q adds consulting, bad-debt reserves, severance and personnel. Meanwhile R&D was managed down from $44.2M (FY2023) to $36.5M (FY2025) before rising 4.3% in H1 2026, and marketing ex-stock comp collapsed from $19.3M to $10.8M over the same period. The company does not break out litigation spend, so the widely assumed post-trial cost reversal is a hypothesis. If $10-15M annualized is truly litigation-related, its removal would move adjusted EBITDA from roughly breakeven to solidly positive. If a larger share is permanent public-company and compliance cost added after the FY2024 material-weakness remediation, far less reverses. The filings do not permit a determination.

### The $36.7M tax charge is mechanics, not cash

FY2025 net loss of $66.5M (EPS −$0.52) against a pretax loss of only $29.8M reflects a $36.7M tax expense, of which $33.6M was deferred tax expense from establishing a valuation allowance against all deferred tax assets (allowance rose from $2.8M to $38.6M). Cash income taxes paid were $2.2M net. The geography explains it: a $44.5M domestic pretax loss against $14.7M of international pretax income, so Cytek pays cash tax abroad while accumulating unusable U.S. losses. The write-off destroyed no cash and does not impair liquidity or solvency screens. It represents a formal management assertion that near-term U.S. taxable income is not more likely than not, and it imposes an ongoing cost: Q2 2026 shows a $0.5M tax expense against a pretax loss versus a $1.2M benefit a year earlier, a $1.7M swing. Management notes quarterly reassessment, so a partial release would be a one-time non-cash benefit if U.S. profitability returns.

All investments mature within a year (Treasuries $144.6M, commercial paper $25.7M, corporate bonds $17.8M), so credit and duration risk are minimal. Net cash of $252.3M equals $1.94/share; gross cash and securities of $2.02/share is 48% of the price. Altman Z-Score of 3.71 on an FY2025 basis sits in the conventional safe zone; current ratio 5.04; total liabilities/equity 0.35. Bankruptcy risk is not the issue.

Cash generation is. Operating cash flow went from +$25.4M (FY2024) to −$4.7M (FY2025) to −$1.4M in H1 2026, with free cash flow of +$21.9M, −$8.8M and −$5.8M respectively. Both the FY2024 positive and the H1 2026 near-breakeven were flattered by working-capital releases: a $14.8M inventory release in FY2024 and an $11.6M receivables release in H1 2026, the latter explicitly seasonal and reversing in H2. A normalized sensitivity: H1 net loss of $31.0M less roughly $16.5M of non-cash charges implies about $14.5M of cash loss before working capital, or ~$29M annualized, plus ~$8.9M of capex, roughly $38M/year of cash need before working-capital swings. That is years of runway against $252M, not quarters, but it is real capital destruction, and it does not reconcile the H2 receivables rebuild, the non-recurring tariff refund, or any post-trial litigation-cost reduction.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance was raised $1M to $207-212M, a midpoint of $209.5M against consensus near $208.8M, a modest, essentially in-line raise implying 3-5% growth. The building blocks are mixed.

Supporting growth: recurring revenue (service plus reagents) reached $18.5M in the quarter, 35% of trailing-twelve-month revenue versus 32% a year earlier. Service gross margin improved 365bps. Borealis and the expanded Aurora Evo automation configurations give the sales force new product to sell into an installed base with demonstrated pull-through. U.S. revenue growth of 24% in H1 is substantial in the region where Cytek's direct coverage is deepest.

Working against it: instrument unit volumes fell 6% in FY2025, which is the input to the recurring-revenue annuity. Academic and government demand, still 39.7% of Q2 revenue, declined 12.1%, with the 10-Q stating that NIH grant policy changes "have impacted and may continue to impact" that revenue. Small-cap biotech customers face a difficult fundraising environment and high rates, lengthening sales cycles. EMEA and APAC both declined. R&D was cut 17% from FY2023 to FY2025. And the January 15, 2025 U.S. export controls apply license requirements to high-parameter and spectral flow cytometers and cell sorters shipped to certain countries including China, precisely Cytek's product definition, in a market that was roughly 10.2% of FY2025 revenue and where Cytek also manufactures.

Estimate revisions are unhelpful and thin. A single contributing analyst has cut the current-year estimate from −$0.04 to −$0.12 over 30 days and next year from $0.19 to $0.07, while raising the current quarter from −$0.06 to −$0.04. Ninety-day revisions are marginally positive. With one contributor, these are close to noise, but the direction of the 30-day moves is negative and consistent with the ex-refund operating deterioration. The earnings track record is 2 beats, 2 misses over four quarters, with Q2's non-GAAP loss of $0.05 missing a $0.02 consensus while revenue beat.

Growth Prospects: 4.3. Revenue is growing at the low end of the tools sector, the growth is channel-mix-driven, the instrument input is declining, and the two largest end markets are policy- and funding-constrained.

## Valuation Assessment

The arithmetic from the frozen $4.18 basis:

- Market capitalization: 129,982,753 × $4.18 = **$543.3M**
- Net cash: $262.0M securities and cash less $9.7M debt = **$252.3M ($1.94/share)**
- Enterprise value: $543.3M + $9.7M − $262.0M = **$291.0M**
- TTM revenue: $201.5M − $87.1M + $92.3M = **$206.7M** → **EV/revenue 1.41x**
- Against the $209.5M FY2026 guidance midpoint: **1.39x**
- Deducting the $8.7M BD royalty liability from net cash gives $1.87/share

Peer comparison is of limited use because the cohort is heterogeneous and largely unprofitable: Tandem Diabetes at −11.3x, Staar Surgical at −68.3x, Artivion at 85.5x, Conmed at 19.4x, Embecta at 1.6x. Median P/E of 19.4x cannot be applied to a company with negative earnings. Revenue multiples are the workable comparison, and healthy life-science tools businesses with 50%+ gross margins and mid-30s recurring mix trade well above 1.4x sales. On that basis the market is applying a substantial discount.

The discount has identifiable causes rather than being an oversight. In order of weight: (1) a jury trial in ten days that could produce past damages, an ongoing royalty, or injunctive relief against the products generating essentially all product revenue, with no reserve; (2) product gross margin still compressing on a comparable basis, with a distributor mix shift structurally pushing the same direction; (3) roughly $38M/year of cash consumption before working capital, which erodes the cash cushion that provides the floor; (4) gross equity grants of 5,045,310 shares (3.88% of shares outstanding) in six months with no buyback offset; and (5) governance provisions (classified board, two-thirds supermajority, blank-check preferred, 38.5% insider/5%-holder ownership) plus an undisclosed change-of-control payment owed to Becton Dickinson under the 2020 settlement, which together block the strategic or activist path that a 46%-cash balance sheet would otherwise invite.

That last point deserves attention because it is the mechanism by which cheapness fails to convert. A business trading at 1.4x sales with $252M net cash is an obvious target. The BD agreement obligates Cytek to make "a specified payment upon the closing of a change of control transaction, if any," amount undisclosed, payable to a large competitor. Layered on the classified board and supermajority provisions, the practical barriers to a bid are high, and the discount cannot be readily arbitraged.

Scenario framework (all per share, from $4.18):

**Bear — $2.75 (−34%).** Adverse verdict with meaningful damages plus a compulsory ongoing royalty. Illustratively, $40M of cash damages reduces net cash to ~$212M; a 4% ongoing royalty on ~$150M of product revenue is $6M/year, and multiple compression to roughly 0.6-1.0x revenue on $210M gives $126-210M of EV. Equity lands in a $2.55-3.20 range; injunction or redesign requirements push toward the low end.

**Base — $4.60 (+10%).** Defense verdict or a manageable money judgment; litigation spend abates materially; revenue reaches ~$218M in FY2027 at 1.7x EV/revenue = $371M EV, plus roughly $240M net cash after ~$20M of net burn over 18 months, on ~133M shares.

**Bull — $6.30 (+51%).** Clean win, litigation expense reversal drives adjusted EBITDA solidly positive, Borealis lifts placements, and the multiple re-rates to 2.6x on $225M of revenue = $585M EV plus $250M net cash on ~133M shares. Partial valuation-allowance release would add a non-cash earnings benefit.

Weighting the trial at roughly 30% adverse / 45% manageable / 25% clean-win, a judgment informed by the favorable procedural pattern but tempered by the denial of Cytek's own summary-judgment motions and the variance of Delaware jury outcomes, gives an expected value near $4.47, about +7%. That is a fair-value setup with wide dispersion, not an asymmetric opportunity.

Valuation: 5.6. Cheap on absolute multiples with a hard net-cash floor, discounted for identified structural impairments and a governance structure that impedes value realization.

## Competitive Landscape

FSP is a defensible wedge in a defined niche. The publication and installed-base assets are switching-cost assets that cannot be bought quickly, and spectral cytometry has been the growing part of the category. Three dynamics visible in the filings cut against the position.

Incumbents are competing through litigation rather than price. Cytek has faced two IP assaults from category leaders: BD's 2018 trade-secret and copyright suit, settled in 2020 with a ten-year low-single-digit royalty, a $2.0M upfront, a $6.0M milestone, 2,087,545 shares issued to BD, and a covenant not to challenge the validity, enforceability or scope of the applicable BD patent claims; and Beckman's 2024 patent suit against the entire core product line. The 10-Q's own framing is candid: defending third-party infringement claims "is very expensive, particularly for a company of our size." The effect is already in the P&L: G&A ex-stock comp up 50% in FY2025 and 46% in H1 2026 while R&D and marketing were cut. Litigation as a competitive weapon scales with balance sheet, and Danaher has vastly more of one.

The end markets Cytek historically owned are constrained. NIH indirect-cost policy changes pressure academic demand; small-cap biotech funding is tight. Academic and government revenue fell 12.1% year over year. The proxy's peer-group TSR shows $100 invested at the IPO growing to $113.99 by December 31, 2025 while Cytek's fell to $26.92, roughly 87 percentage points of underperformance, worse every single year. That gap indicates company-specific problems, not sector collapse.

The January 2025 export controls are category-specific rather than generic. Restricting high-parameter and spectral cytometers and sorters to China hits Cytek's exact differentiated products, and Cytek is unusual among Western tools companies in manufacturing in Wuxi while selling into China.

Offsetting: the recurring-revenue pivot is working, with service revenue up 21.3% in FY2025 and 12% in H1 2026, service margin up 365bps, and recurring mix at 35% TTM. The constraint is that instrument placements feed that annuity, and unit volumes fell 6% in FY2025 with direct-channel revenue flat in H1 2026.

Competitive Position: 4.3.

## Risk Assessment

**Beckman Coulter trial, August 17, 2026 — dominant.** Beckman asserts U.S. Patents 10,330,582, 11,703,443 and 12,174,107 against the Aurora, Aurora CS, Northern Lights and Northern Lights-CLC. The 10-Q separately states Cytek is "highly dependent on a limited number of product offerings," naming those same products. Procedural history through August 4, 2026: the court ordered Beckman to narrow claims; Beckman dropped Patent 12,174,106 entirely; Cytek's Daubert motions were granted and one of Beckman's denied; both sides' summary-judgment motions were denied; Cytek's false-marking counterclaim was dismissed without prejudice by stipulation in March 2026. The pattern is net-favorable, and a defendant winning Daubert motions frequently constrains a plaintiff's damages case, but the filings do not state which experts or opinions were affected, so that reading is an inference. The denial of Cytek's own summary-judgment motions means the court found genuine disputes on infringement and/or validity. No damages claim, royalty demand or injunction request is quantified in any filing, and no loss accrual is disclosed, which is consistent with ASC 450 treatment but leaves no cushion. With $252M of net cash Cytek can likely absorb a money judgment; the asymmetric exposure is injunctive relief or a compulsory royalty on the whole line.

**Governance and capital allocation.** Cumulative buybacks total 13,877,992 shares for ~$80.8M at roughly $5.80 average, above today's price. The pace went $44.2M (2023) → $21.6M (2024) → $15.1M (2025) → zero in H1 2026, with $35.0M of the December 2024 authorization expiring unused on December 31, 2025 and no successor program disclosed. The $150M ATM was never drawn, the shelf expired in August 2025, and the agreement was terminated. Meanwhile Cytek drew $5.1M net on Chinese bank lines, including a February 2026 China Construction Bank loan at 2.5% explicitly "used to pay operating expenses." The most coherent reading is deliberate liquidity preservation ahead of an unquantified trial contingency, which is defensible risk management; the RMB facilities at 2.5-3.7% are also plausibly local working-capital financing that avoids cross-border repatriation friction. I do not infer distress from the borrowing. What is factual is that the company currently has neither an active return-of-capital program nor an active issuance facility, and the Bank of China Wuxi facility (RMB 37M, essentially fully drawn) expires August 14, 2026 with renewal unaddressed in the 10-Q.

**Dilution.** Gross grants in H1 2026 were 1,427,496 options plus 3,617,814 RSUs = 5,045,310 shares, 3.88% of shares outstanding in six months, including 2,488,737 shares to four executives in March. Actual share count rose 1.11% in H1 (128,550,136 → 129,982,753). Potential dilution from outstanding awards is 10,054,110 options at a $7.54 weighted-average strike plus 6,522,881 unvested RSUs = 16,576,991 shares, 12.75% of shares outstanding, equal to 11.3% ownership dilution post-issuance. Most options are deep underwater; aggregate intrinsic value of all outstanding options is just $4.2M versus $28.9M for the RSUs, so near-term economic dilution is RSU-driven. Plan capacity of 31.6M additional shares (24.3%) with 4%+1% annual evergreens through 2031 grows even as the stock falls, and the buyback that previously absorbed grant issuance is gone. Reported stock compensation is falling ($24.6M FY2025, $10.2M H1 2026 versus $13.4M H1 2025), so the P&L cost declines even as unit counts rise.

**Supply chain and China.** Certain lasers and semiconductors are single- or sole-sourced; the only long-term agreement is with Coherent NA, non-exclusive with non-binding rolling forecasts; Cytek states it is "not a major customer to most of our suppliers" and requalification takes 12-24 months. In 2026 Cytek pre-bought plastics on a longer horizon than typical and "incurred, and may continue to incur, higher procurement costs," visible in inventories rising to $52.3M, with work-in-process having jumped from $6.5M to $16.5M during FY2025. Excess and obsolete inventory was Deloitte's Critical Audit Matter for FY2025, the highest-judgment estimate in the audit.

**Control history.** FY2024 material weaknesses spanned control environment, control activities including IT general controls, and policies and procedures, with prior filings made under Rule 12b-25. The 10-K states remediation during fiscal 2025, and Deloitte issued an unqualified ICFR opinion as of December 31, 2025. Disclosure controls were effective at June 30, 2026. This appears resolved but is recent. A November 2025 security incident affecting employee personal information is separately disclosed.

**Personnel.** Three Item 5.02 8-Ks were filed on June 2, June 25 and July 15, 2026. Q2 10-Q Exhibit 10.1 is a severance and consulting agreement with Valerie Barnett, Chief Legal Officer, dated July 9-10, 2026, roughly five weeks before the trial. The simultaneous consulting arrangement suggests the company arranged continuity through the trial, arguing against an acrimonious exit, but the 8-K text is not in evidence and no reason can be attributed.

### Options and Positioning Signal

No implied-volatility, skew or open-interest data was available for this name; the only options-related datapoint is the underlying price move from $4.61 to $4.20 between snapshots. Positioning is better read from short interest: 5,029,578 shares short, 4.9% of float, 2.3 days to cover. That is uncrowded. Ten days before a jury verdict on a suit against the entire product line, the market is not expressing a directional bearish view through short positioning, consistent either with the docket being read as favorable, or with the event simply being under-followed in a $543M-cap name. It removes squeeze risk as a consideration but provides no confirming signal in either direction.

Risk Factors: 3.2. The trial alone would justify a low score; combined with unquantified exposure, no reserve, a facility expiring within a week, category-specific export controls, single-source components and a 12.75% award overhang against a discontinued buyback, the idiosyncratic risk load is heavy relative to a $291M enterprise value.

### Management Quality

Compensation actually paid to the CEO rose from $1.93M (2022) to $3.64M (2025), peaking at $4.40M in 2024, while IPO-indexed TSR fell every year to $26.92 against a peer group at $113.99. Disclosed performance measures are Revenue and Adjusted EBITDA; FY2025 revenue was flat and adjusted EBITDA deteriorated sharply. Across all Form 4s reviewed from February to June 2026, there were no open-market purchases; every acquisition was a grant, RSU settlement or option exercise, every disposition tax withholding or a gift. With the stock near multi-year lows and $2.02/share of cash and securities on the balance sheet, no officer or director appears to have bought a share. CTO Ming Yan gifted 1,176,000 shares in March and 135,892 in June, reducing direct holdings roughly 21%; gifts generate no proceeds and are not sales, but the concentration reduction is material and recipients can sell without further Section 16 reporting.

Credits: the material-weakness remediation was completed and audited clean; cash and securities were essentially flat in Q2 ($262.2M → $262.0M); guidance was raised modestly; and preserving liquidity ahead of an unquantified trial contingency is a defensible reason to stand down from buybacks. Those credits do not offset the pay-versus-performance record, the absence of any insider conviction purchase, or the dilution running at roughly 4x the net share-count increase in gross grant terms.

Management Quality: 3.7.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($6.30, +51%).** Cytek wins or substantially limits the Beckman case. Litigation-driven G&A, plausibly $10-15M annualized though undisclosed, reverses, moving adjusted EBITDA from roughly breakeven to solidly positive without any revenue improvement. Borealis and Aurora Evo automation lift instrument placements, restarting the installed-base flywheel that drives 35%-and-rising recurring revenue at 56% adjusted service gross margin. U.S. momentum (+24% in H1) broadens. The Singapore manufacturing transition that inflated FY2025 overhead becomes a tailwind. A partial valuation-allowance release produces a one-time non-cash earnings benefit. At 2.6x revenue on $225M plus $250M net cash, the equity is worth $6.30. A resumed buyback at these prices, using cash that covers 46% of the market value, would be highly accretive.

**Bear case ($2.75, −34%).** An adverse verdict brings past damages plus an ongoing royalty on products representing essentially all product revenue, with no reserve to absorb it, and injunction or redesign risk in the tail. Underlying economics continue deteriorating independently: product gross margin at ~52% and still compressing as the distributor channel takes share, the tariff refund reverses into a 12.5% tariff headwind, plastics pre-buys raise procurement costs, and academic and EMEA/APAC demand keeps declining. Roughly $38M/year of cash consumption plus ~4% annual gross equity issuance erodes the cash floor, while the classified board, supermajority provisions and the undisclosed BD change-of-control payment prevent anyone from arbitraging the discount. The stock compounds downward at 1.0x or less on revenue.

**Base case ($4.60, +10%).** The trial resolves without existential damage, either a defense verdict or a manageable money judgment. Litigation spend abates partially, revenue grows 3-5% to roughly $218M in FY2027, product margin stabilizes near 52% as service mix rises, and adjusted EBITDA hovers near breakeven. The multiple stays depressed at roughly 1.7x revenue because growth remains mid-single-digit and no capital-return program exists. Net cash declines toward $240M.

Probability-weighted, this is a modest positive expected return with a wide, near-dated distribution. The specific reason not to buy is timing: a jury verdict on unquantified exposure lands within weeks, and the position taken today is a wager on that outcome rather than on the operating business. The specific reason not to sell is the floor: $1.94/share of net cash, a flat quarterly cash burn, and a 1.4x revenue multiple that already discounts a great deal.

## Conclusion

Cytek presents two facts that are both true and largely independent. The valuation is depressed on any conventional tools-industry measure: 1.41x TTM EV/revenue, 46% of the share price in net cash, 52% product gross margins, and a 3,933-unit installed base generating recurring revenue at 35% of trailing sales and rising. And the operating business is not currently working: FY2025 revenue was flat and down 1.1% in constant currency, unit volumes fell 6%, the Q2 gross-margin expansion was a $2.8M tariff refund with ex-refund product margin down 70bps and the ex-refund operating loss 34% wider, and the entire H1 revenue reacceleration came through the lower-margin distributor channel while direct sales were flat.

Standing between those facts is the Delaware jury trial on August 17, 2026 against Danaher's Beckman Coulter, targeting the products that produce essentially all of Cytek's revenue, with no disclosed reserve and no quantified damages claim. The docket pattern favors Cytek (narrowed claims, a dropped patent, granted Daubert motions), but Cytek also failed to win summary judgment, and the exposure cannot be sized from the filings. Probability-weighted upside of roughly 7% against a −34% adverse leg resolving within weeks, in a company where no insider has bought a share in the open market at these prices and the buyback was halted precisely where net cash made it most accretive, is a fair-value proposition rather than an opportunity.

The score declines modestly from 4.7 to 4.6, with the reduction concentrated in risk (the trial is now days rather than months away, with no reserve and no quantified exposure) and management (dilution continuing against a discontinued buyback, no insider conviction, pay rising against 87 points of peer TSR underperformance), partially offset by a lower entry price and a preserved cash position. HOLD, and treat the verdict as the reassessment point rather than trying to anticipate it.
