## Executive Summary

Evercore delivered a record Q1 2026 with net revenues of ~$1.4B (up 100% YoY) and adjusted EPS of $7.53, beating consensus by 47%. This followed a record FY2025 where advisory fees grew 34% and operating margins expanded (comp ratio declining 140bps to 64.9%). The stock currently trades at $325.42, representing ~17x the $19.19 FY2026 consensus EPS estimate — a figure that has been revised upward 6.9% over the past 90 days at the full-year level despite near-term quarterly softness expectations.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) Evercore's market-leading position in independent advisory continues to take share from both bulge brackets and smaller independents, with revenue per SMD at $19.1M; (2) the combination of a 9.4% FCF yield and $1.6B buyback authorization creates a structural EPS growth tailwind independent of M&A volumes; and (3) consensus appears to be underestimating normalized earnings power by anchoring to management's cautious Q2 guidance while ignoring the demonstrated earnings leverage when deal volumes normalize above trough. The primary risk is M&A cycle reversal driven by trade policy uncertainty and rising rates, which could compress advisory fees 20-30% from peak levels.

## Company Overview

Evercore is the world's largest independent investment bank by dollar volume of announced M&A transactions advised over the past five years. The firm operates primarily through its Investment Banking & Equities segment (98% of revenue), advising on strategic M&A, restructuring, equity capital markets, and private capital. With 171 Investment Banking Senior Managing Directors across 33 cities globally, and following the 2025 acquisition of Robey Warshaw (elite UK advisory boutique), Evercore has built the broadest independent platform in the industry.

The remaining 2% of revenue comes from Investment Management (Evercore Wealth Management, $15.5B AUM). The firm employs approximately 2,570 people and generated $3.88B in total revenue in FY2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** FY2025 revenues of $3.88B grew 29.5% YoY, following 22.7% growth in FY2024. Advisory fees specifically grew 34% to $3.27B, with 806 advisory and underwriting client fees earned (up from 748 in 2024) and 529 fees of at least $1M (up from 457). Q1 2026 continued at an accelerated pace with ~$1.4B in net revenues, though management guided Q2 "closer to last year's record" — implying some normalization.

**Profitability:** Net margin of 17.0% in FY2025 with ROE of 31.5% — exceptional for a financial services business. The compensation ratio compressed from 66.3% to 64.9%, demonstrating the operating leverage inherent in the advisory model once revenues exceed a threshold. Non-compensation expenses grew 20% but slower than the 29.5% revenue growth, amplifying the margin expansion.

**Cash generation:** Operating cash flow of $1.26B and free cash flow of $1.18B in FY2025 translate to a 9.4% FCF yield on the current $12.6B market cap. Cash and investment securities totaled $3.0B at year-end against $588M in total debt. No borrowings on either the $225M or $75M credit facilities. Current ratio of 2.16x.

**Capital return:** $660.6M in share repurchases in FY2025 (2.4M shares at average price of $275.42). Board authorized up to $1.6B in total repurchases (April 2025). Quarterly dividend increased to $0.89 (from $0.84), yielding ~1.1%.

**Earnings quality consideration:** The 2025 effective tax rate of 19.3% benefited from $78.5M in excess tax benefits from stock appreciation upon vesting. At a more normalized 24-25% rate, net income would be modestly lower. This is a variable tied to stock price performance — a headwind if shares stagnate.

## Growth Analysis

The four-quarter earnings surprise record is remarkable: +34.5%, +5.9%, +25.7%, +46.6%. This pattern of accelerating beat magnitude typically indicates consensus is systematically underestimating the business's earning power during an upcycle.

Full-year FY2026 estimates have revised upward from $17.94 to $19.19 over 90 days (+6.9%). FY2027 consensus sits at $23.02. The near-term quarterly estimates show downward revisions (current quarter from $3.75 to $2.50, next quarter from $4.70 to $3.69), which reflects seasonal M&A patterns and management's cautious Q2 guidance rather than structural deterioration. The full-year upward revision despite quarterly softness indicates analysts expect H2 2026 acceleration.

Growth drivers: (1) 18 new IB SMDs added in 2025 plus 4 committed for 2026 and 11 internal promotions — each SMD generates ~$19M in advisory fees at full productivity (typically achieved 12-18 months after joining); (2) Robey Warshaw adds elite EMEA capability at a time when cross-border M&A is resurging; (3) continued secular shift toward independent advice as boards seek conflict-free counsel; (4) private capital advisory and restructuring provide counter-cyclical revenue diversification.

The risk to growth is clear: current trade policy uncertainty (tariffs, geopolitical tensions) could freeze boardroom decision-making and delay M&A transactions. The 2023 downturn demonstrated this — revenues fell 12% when the macro environment deteriorated. However, the current cycle appears earlier in its trajectory than late-2021, the pipeline entering Q2 is described as strong, and restructuring activity provides partial offset.

## Valuation Assessment

At $325.42 with FY2026 consensus EPS of $19.19, the stock trades at 17.0x forward earnings. Against the peer median P/E of 20.0x (SF 14.4x, SNEX 19.6x, HLI 24.4x, PIPR 20.0x, MC 21.5x), Evercore trades at a 15% discount despite being the largest, fastest-growing, and highest-margin firm in the group.

The discount to Houlihan Lokey (24.4x) stands out given Evercore's superior revenue growth (29.5% vs HLI's mid-teens), higher revenue per SMD, and comparable operating margins. The discount to Moelis (21.5x) is even less justified given MC's narrower service offering and smaller scale.

On a FCF yield basis, 9.4% is compelling for a business growing revenue 20-30% with expanding margins. The $1.6B buyback authorization (13% of market cap) provides structural support.

The specific market mispricing: analysts are anchoring to Q2 seasonal weakness guidance while the full-year estimate trajectory is moving higher. If H2 2026 momentum resembles H2 2025 (which drove the record Q1 pipeline), actual FY2026 EPS could approach $21-22, placing the effective multiple at 15-16x. Analyst consensus target of $373.80 implies 15% upside; the high target of $435 implies 34% upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Evercore's competitive position is improving on multiple dimensions. The 34% advisory fee growth in FY2025 significantly outpaced industry M&A volumes (which grew roughly 15-20%), confirming market share gains. Revenue per SMD of $19.1M is industry-leading and reflects the firm's ability to win large, transformative mandates generating $5-10M+ fees.

The independence model continues to gain traction as corporate boards face increasing scrutiny over advisor conflicts. Evercore's lack of commercial banking, proprietary trading, or large PE fund management means it can advise on transactions where bulge brackets face disqualifying conflicts. This structural advantage compounds over time as corporate relationships deepen.

The Robey Warshaw acquisition represents a meaningful competitive upgrade in EMEA, adding one of the UK's most prestigious advisory practices. Combined with new offices in Stockholm and Milan, Evercore's European footprint now competes credibly with Lazard and Rothschild on mega-cap cross-border transactions.

Weaknesses: Evercore cannot provide acquisition financing, which occasionally loses mandates to full-service banks. The firm also lacks Lazard's asset management scale or PJT's restructuring depth. However, the firm's breadth across M&A, capital markets, private capital advisory, and restructuring provides multiple revenue vectors that most boutiques lack.

## Risk Assessment

**Cyclical risk (moderate-high):** With 91% of core revenue from advisory fees tied to M&A volumes, the business is inherently cyclical. The current trade policy environment (tariffs, geopolitical uncertainty referenced specifically in the 10-K) could freeze deal pipelines. Revenue fell 12% in the 2023 downturn. However, the current cycle appears earlier in its trajectory than late-2021, the pipeline entering Q2 is described as strong, and restructuring activity provides partial offset.

**Key person concentration (moderate):** 171 SMDs generating $3.27B in advisory fees means the top quartile of bankers likely produces a disproportionate share. Loss of even 3-5 top producers could materially impact revenues. The Robey Warshaw earn-out structure mitigates near-term departure risk for those specific bankers.

**Near-term earnings normalization (moderate):** Q1 2026's 100% YoY growth partly reflects timing (transactions from H2 2025 pipeline closing in Q1). Management's explicit guidance that Q2 will be "closer to last year's record" signals normalization. The current quarter consensus at $2.50 (down from $3.75 ninety days ago) prices significant sequential deceleration. If actual Q2 results come in closer to $3.00-3.50, the stock would likely rally; if below $2.50, the cyclical bear case gains traction.

**Rising rate environment (low-moderate):** Higher long-term interest rates complicate leveraged buyout financing and reduce strategic M&A activity. However, this also increases restructuring mandates and activism (boards under pressure to demonstrate value creation). The net effect is more ambiguous than for pure LBO-dependent franchises.

**Tariff-driven M&A freeze (moderate):** The current environment with trade uncertainty could delay boardroom decisions. However, activist pressure, private equity dry powder, and corporate portfolio simplification create offsetting demand drivers.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($400-435, +23-34%):** M&A cycle continues through 2026-2027 as private equity deploys dry powder and corporate portfolio activity accelerates. Full-year 2026 EPS reaches $21-22 (above current $19.19 consensus) as Q3/Q4 pipeline strength mirrors H2 2025 patterns. Operating leverage continues compressing the comp ratio toward 63-64%. Share count reduction from the $1.6B buyback adds 2-3% annual EPS growth mechanically. Multiple re-rates to peer average of 20x on the higher earnings base: 20x × $22 = $440.

**Bear Case ($250-275, -16-23%):** Trade policy uncertainty freezes M&A decisions through H2 2026. Advisory fees plateau or decline 10-15% from 2025 levels as deal timelines extend. Comp ratio reverts toward 66-67% as the firm maintains headcount during the downturn. FY2026 EPS comes in at $15-16 (below consensus). Multiple compresses to 16-17x reflecting cycle peak concerns: 16x × $16 = $256.

## Conclusion

Evercore represents the highest-quality franchise in independent advisory, demonstrating exceptional operating leverage during the current M&A upcycle. The stock trades at 17x forward FY2026 consensus — a 15% discount to the peer median P/E — despite leading the sector in revenue growth, margins, and market position. The systematic pattern of accelerating earnings beats suggests consensus remains too conservative on normalized earnings power. The $1.6B buyback authorization and dividend increases provide downside protection, while the Robey Warshaw integration and 22 new/committed SMDs provide visible growth vectors into 2027.

The primary risk is M&A cyclicality in a stagflationary macro environment. Near-term quarterly estimates reflect this concern (current quarter revised down 33%). However, the full-year upward revision trajectory and the depth of Q1's beat ($2.40 above consensus) indicate the pipeline remains substantially healthier than quarterly volatility implies. At a 15% discount to peers with superior fundamentals and a 1.8:1 base-case risk/reward ratio, the stock offers asymmetric upside on continued cycle execution.
