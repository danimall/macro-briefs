## Executive Summary

YETI Holdings is an asset-light premium outdoor brand trading at $36.36, approximately 29% below its 52-week high of $51.29 and roughly flat with the average price at which management repurchased $298M of stock in FY2025. The company generates $212M in free cash flow on $1.87B of revenue (11.4% FCF margin), carries minimal debt ($74M term loan vs. $188M cash), and has reduced its share count 14% over two years through buybacks.

The fundamental challenge is that YETI's core U.S. business declined 1% in FY2025, drinkware (58% of sales) fell 1% for the full year amid promotional competition from Stanley and Owala, and tariffs erased 230 bps of gross margin. FY2025 EPS of $2.03 was flat-to-down versus FY2024's $2.05. Near-term EPS estimates are falling sharply: the current quarter estimate has dropped 38.5% over 90 days, from $0.30 to $0.19. International growth (+16% YoY, now 21% of sales) and a Q4 drinkware inflection (+6%) provide offsetting positives, but not enough to overcome the headwinds facing a premium consumer discretionary brand in a stagflationary macro environment with oil above $100 and consumer confidence declining.

At ~18x forward earnings and ~13x trailing FCF, the valuation is not expensive for a brand business with this cash generation profile. But the calibration data is explicit: Consumer Discretionary BUYs have averaged -1.34% across 12 calls, and the sector suspension remains in force. Business quality is real; the near-term setup is hostile. Recommendation is HOLD.

## Company Overview

YETI designs and distributes premium outdoor products across two primary segments: Drinkware (58% of FY2025 sales) and Coolers & Equipment (40%), with 2% from apparel and accessories. The company operates an asset-light model with all manufacturing outsourced to third-party contract manufacturers in China, Thailand, Malaysia, Vietnam, Philippines, Mexico, and Poland.

The channel mix is 60% DTC (YETI.com, Amazon Marketplace, corporate sales, 27 owned retail stores) and 40% wholesale (~5,300 retail partners including Dick's Sporting Goods, REI, Bass Pro Shops, Academy Sports). No single customer exceeds 10% of gross sales. Geographically, the U.S. accounts for 79% of revenue, with international at 21% ($394M) and growing 16% YoY. Japan launched in Q2 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** $1.869B in FY2025, +2.1% YoY — the slowest growth rate in the available history. U.S. revenue actually declined from $1.491B to $1.474B (-1.1%). Growth was entirely driven by international (+16%) and Coolers & Equipment (+7%). The growth deceleration from 10.3% in FY2024 to 2.1% in FY2025 is material.

**Margins:** Gross margin of 57.4% in FY2025 was down 70 bps from FY2024's 58.1%, with tariffs accounting for a 230 bps headwind partially offset by lower product costs (+90 bps) and price increases (+40 bps). Operating margin declined to 11.4% from 13.4%, driven by both gross margin compression and SG&A deleveraging (SG&A rose to 46.0% of sales from 44.7%). Operating income fell 13% despite revenue growing 2%.

**Cash flow:** Operating cash flow of $255M and free cash flow of $212M remain strong. CapEx of $43M is light for a $1.9B revenue business, reflecting the asset-light model. FCF conversion is high quality: $212M FCF vs. $166M net income represents a 128% FCF/NI ratio.

**Balance sheet:** Net cash position: $188M cash minus $74M term loan = $114M net cash. Debt-to-EBITDA is approximately 0.3x. $300M revolver is fully undrawn. The balance sheet is a clear strength.

**Capital allocation:** $298M in buybacks at $36.49 average price in FY2025. Share count declined from 86.9M to 75.0M over two years. No dividends. $152M remaining on the buyback authorization plus $212M in annual FCF provides substantial ongoing buyback capacity.

**Earnings trajectory:** FY2025 diluted EPS of $2.03 was essentially flat with FY2024's $2.05. Adjusted EPS fell 9% from $2.73 to $2.48. Current consensus for FY2026 is $2.82, implying 39% growth, but near-term estimates are falling rapidly. The current quarter (Q1 FY2026) estimate has dropped from $0.30 to $0.19 over 90 days — a 38.5% decline. The next quarter estimate fell from $0.75 to $0.58 over the same period (-21.9%).

## Growth Analysis

**Positive factors:** (1) International expansion has clear runway — 21% of sales with 16% growth, Japan recently launched, and the brand is early in penetration of most non-U.S. markets. (2) Q4 FY2025 showed a drinkware inflection (+6% after three quarters of declines), potentially signaling stabilization as supply chain disruptions from the China manufacturing exit ease. (3) The M&A-driven innovation pipeline is capital-efficient: Helimix was acquired and converted to a YETI product in one quarter; the $32.5M powered cooling technology patents point to a future powered cooler platform. (4) Wholesale sell-through outpacing sell-in suggests retailers are drawing down YETI inventory, creating potential for a restocking tailwind.

**Negative factors:** (1) U.S. growth has stalled — the core domestic market declined 1% in FY2025. With the U.S. at 79% of revenue, international growth alone cannot fully offset domestic weakness. (2) FY2025 revenue growth of 2.1% was the slowest on record for the company. (3) Near-term estimate revisions are uniformly negative and accelerating downward, particularly for the current and next quarter. (4) Consumer confidence is declining (-12.5% YoY per the world model), and premium discretionary purchases are early casualties in spending pullbacks. (5) The competitive environment in drinkware has structurally shifted — Stanley, Owala, and HydroJug have established market positions that YETI must compete against on an ongoing basis.

Consensus expects $2.82 EPS for FY2026 and $3.26 for FY2027. Near-term estimate cuts may precede full-year revisions. Four consecutive quarters of beats (averaging +9%) provides some buffer, but the beats have been modest in absolute dollar terms ($0.01-0.10 per quarter).

## Valuation Assessment

At $36.36, YETI trades at approximately 17.9x forward P/E (FY2026 consensus of $2.82, assuming that estimate holds), 12.9x trailing FCF ($212M / $2.75B market cap), and 11.1x FY2027 estimates ($3.26). The peer median P/E is 19.9x (THO Industries), though comparability is limited — Polaris and Brunswick are cyclical industrial manufacturers with negative earnings, while Mattel and Acushnet are different end markets.

Relative to its own history, YETI has traded between roughly 15x and 35x forward earnings over the past several years. At 17.9x, the stock is in the lower quartile of its historical range. The analyst consensus price target of $50.60 (median $53.00) implies approximately 40% upside from current levels, with a low target of $37.00 — essentially the current price — and a high of $70.00.

The valuation is reasonable for a brand business generating 57%+ gross margins and 11%+ FCF margins with minimal debt. The risk is that the forward estimate ($2.82) declines further given the near-term revision trajectory and macro headwinds. If FY2026 EPS comes in at $2.50 (tariff headwinds persisting, U.S. growth remaining negative), the stock trades at 14.5x — not cheap enough for an AVOID, not compelling enough for a BUY.

## Competitive Landscape

YETI operates in a highly fragmented market with low barriers to entry. The company acknowledges this directly in its 10-K, naming Stanley, Owala, HydroJug, SharkNinja, Igloo, and Coleman as competitors. The drinkware category has become "highly promotional," which is a structural challenge for a premium-priced brand.

Stanley's viral social media-driven growth represents the most significant competitive disruption YETI has faced. Stanley's success demonstrated that brand loyalty in drinkware is more elastic than YETI's premium pricing strategy assumed. YETI's response has been to accelerate product innovation (ceramic mugs, shaker bottles, food jars) and expand into adjacent categories.

YETI's moat rests on: (1) brand equity built through a decade of ambassador programs and outdoor community engagement, (2) product quality and engineering, (3) controlled distribution preventing excessive discounting, and (4) a growing DTC capability at 60% of sales. These advantages are real but narrower than they appeared before the Stanley disruption. The brand retains pricing power (57%+ gross margins confirm this), but the competitive environment is structurally more intense.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff exposure (Critical):** Tariffs erased 230 bps of gross margin in FY2025 and 320 bps in Q3 alone. The SCOTUS ruling invalidating IEEPA tariffs provided temporary relief, but new tariffs under alternative authorities were immediately imposed. YETI's supply chain diversification out of China is ongoing and itself caused FY2025 disruptions. With trade tensions elevated and tariff authorities being contested across multiple legal frameworks, there is no visibility on when this headwind abates.

**Consumer spending contraction:** The world model documents active consumer spending contraction: consumer sentiment -12.5% YoY, retail sales -0.2% in January, mortgage refi demand down 40%. YETI's products are explicitly discretionary. Management acknowledged this sensitivity in the 10-K. With oil above $100 and gas above $4/gallon, consumers are trading down on discretionary purchases.

**U.S. growth stagnation:** The U.S. business declined 1% in FY2025. This is the largest single risk because the U.S. is 79% of revenue. International at +16% growth is meaningful but insufficient to drive consolidated growth materially above low single digits if the U.S. remains flat or negative.

**Estimate trajectory:** Current quarter estimates down 38.5% over 90 days. This is a strong negative momentum signal. Four consecutive earnings beats provide some cushion, but the beat magnitudes have been small.

**Short interest:** At 13.4% of float with 4.5 days to cover, YETI has elevated institutional bearish positioning. This increases potential for both downside pressure and short-squeeze volatility.

**CFO transition:** Scott Bomar replaced Michael McMullen as CFO between Q3 and the 10-K filing. CFO transitions during periods of significant supply chain restructuring and tariff uncertainty add execution risk.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
- International growth at 16% continues and eventually reaches 30%+ of revenue, providing a structural growth driver independent of U.S. consumer weakness.
- Q4 drinkware inflection (+6%) proves sustainable as supply chain normalization from the China manufacturing exit enables full product availability.
- Wholesale restocking cycle begins as sell-through continues to outpace sell-in, creating a one-time revenue tailwind.
- Powered cooling technology patents lead to a new product category with high margins and limited competition.
- Aggressive buybacks at ~$36.49 average price continue to compound per-share value. At $212M annual FCF and current price, YETI can retire ~7-8% of its float annually.
- If tariffs stabilize or decline (SCOTUS pathway), gross margin recovers 200+ bps, pushing EPS toward $3.00+ and the stock toward 18-20x on that base ($54-60).

### Bear Case
- U.S. drinkware decline persists as Stanley, Owala, and promotional competition structurally erode YETI's pricing power.
- Stagflationary macro environment (oil >$100, rates >3.6%, consumer confidence declining) compresses demand for $40+ water bottles and $300+ coolers.
- Tariff headwinds persist or worsen under new authorities, keeping gross margin 200+ bps below normalized levels.
- Near-term estimate cuts accelerate into full-year revisions, driving the stock toward $27-30 (the April 2025 lows at $26.61).
- International expansion faces execution risk in new markets (Japan launch early days) and requires SG&A investment that further deleverages margins.
- SG&A growing 5% while revenue grows 2% is an unsustainable pattern that compresses operating leverage.

## Conclusion

YETI has 57% gross margins, 11% FCF margins, a net cash balance sheet, and aggressive buybacks, but it is operating against U.S. growth stagnation, permanently intensified competition, tariffs erasing 200+ bps of margin annually, and sharply falling near-term EPS estimates. At ~18x forward earnings, the stock is not expensive relative to its cash generation, but risk-reward is roughly balanced at current prices.

The calibration document's mandatory Consumer Discretionary BUY suspension applies here regardless of other considerations. Beyond the procedural constraint, the fundamental case supports HOLD: business quality prevents AVOID (strong FCF, clean balance sheet, real brand equity), but macro headwinds, estimate trajectory, elevated short interest, and heavy put positioning prevent BUY. The Q4 drinkware inflection and international growth are genuine positives worth monitoring — if FY2026 Q1/Q2 results show U.S. drinkware returning to sustained positive growth and tariff headwinds stabilizing, the entry would be more attractive. Recommendation is HOLD with an "uncertain" classification reflecting insufficient evidence for a directional call.
