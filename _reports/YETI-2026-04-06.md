## Executive Summary

YETI Holdings trades at $36.36 (~$37.12 recent), representing a ~29% discount from its January 2026 high of $51.29. The company generates $212M in annual free cash flow on $1.87B in revenue, carries minimal debt ($74M term loan), and has been buying back shares aggressively ($298M in FY2025 at an average price of $36.49 — essentially the current stock price). Financial health metrics are solid: 1.98x current ratio, 25.4% ROE, and an Altman Z-Score of 3.8.

The earnings trajectory is deteriorating. Near-term EPS estimates have collapsed: the current quarter estimate is down 38.5% over 90 days (from $0.30 to $0.19), and next quarter is down 21.9%. This reflects cumulative tariff headwinds of ~430 bps on gross margins across FY2025-FY2026, a declining U.S. drinkware business (-1% in FY2025, which is 58% of revenue), and a CFO transition announced alongside below-consensus FY2026 EPS guidance. International growth (+16% YoY, 21% of sales) and wholesale restocking potential offer second-half recovery paths, but near-term visibility is poor in a stagflationary macro environment where consumer discretionary spending is contracting.

At 17.9x forward earnings, YETI trades near its peer median P/E of 19.9x — neither cheap enough to compensate for the risk nor expensive enough to warrant shorting given the brand's demonstrated staying power. The bull case (H2 2026 margin recovery, international scaling, buyback support) and bear case (tariff escalation, U.S. consumer weakness, competitive erosion) are roughly balanced. HOLD.

## Company Overview

YETI designs and distributes premium outdoor products across two segments: Drinkware (58% of FY2025 revenue) and Coolers & Equipment (40%), with a small apparel/accessories category (2%). Distribution is 60% DTC (websites, Amazon, corporate sales, ~25 owned retail stores) and 40% wholesale (Dick's Sporting Goods, REI, Academy Sports, Bass Pro, Ace Hardware, Tractor Supply).

The company is headquartered in Austin, Texas. FY2025 revenue was $1.87B, with the U.S. generating 79% ($1.47B) and international markets 21% ($394M). Key international markets include Canada, Australia, UK, Europe, and Japan (launched Q2 2025).

Recent strategic moves include the $38M Helimix shaker bottle acquisition (Q3 2025) and the Mystery Ranch backpack acquisition (early 2024), both aimed at expanding YETI's addressable market beyond traditional outdoor coolers and drinkware.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY2025 revenue grew just 2% YoY to $1.87B, a sharp deceleration from 10% in FY2024. U.S. sales declined 1%, meaning all growth came from international (+16%). Within product lines, Coolers & Equipment grew 7% while Drinkware declined 1% domestically. Q4 was the strongest quarter (+7% overall, +25% international), offering some sequential momentum into FY2026. Management guides FY2026 revenue of $1.98-2.02B (6-8% growth), above consensus at time of guidance.

**Margins:** Gross margin compressed 70 bps to 57.4% in FY2025, with tariffs responsible for a -230 bps headwind partially offset by lower product costs and selective price increases. Operating margin fell more sharply, from 13.4% to 11.4%, as SG&A grew 5% on 2% revenue growth — reflecting investments in technology, facilities, headcount, and marketing that did not lever against the revenue base.

For FY2026, management guided an additional 200 bps tariff headwind on gross margins, concentrated in H1. First-half gross margins will be down ~300 bps YoY, with Q1 being the trough. The H2 2026 margin recovery depends on tariff stabilization — a highly uncertain assumption given the current geopolitical and policy environment.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow was $254.7M, free cash flow $212.1M (11.3% FCF margin). Capex was $42.7M, stepping up to a guided $60-70M in FY2026 for tech and supply chain investment. FCF conversion relative to net income ($165.4M) exceeds 100%, indicating high-quality earnings.

**Balance Sheet:** Net debt is effectively negative: $188.3M cash against $73.8M in term loan debt, with a $300M undrawn revolver. Current ratio is 1.98x. Debt/equity of 0.9x reflects cumulative buyback impact on equity base rather than leverage concerns. The balance sheet provides genuine downside protection.

**Share Repurchases:** YETI spent $298M on buybacks in FY2025 at $36.49 average price — exceeding FCF and consuming all net income plus additional cash. The Board authorized $450M total (with a $350M increase in Q1 2025), representing ~16% of the current market cap. This is unusually aggressive capital return for a company simultaneously guiding down margins.

**EPS:** FY2025 diluted EPS was $2.03, roughly flat vs. FY2024's $2.05 despite a 6% decline in net income ($165.4M from $175.7M), supported by a ~5% reduction in share count. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $2.77-$2.83 implies ~37% growth, but this came in below the $2.87 consensus at the time of guidance, triggering the post-earnings selloff.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term:** Current-quarter EPS estimates have fallen 38.5% in 90 days ($0.30 → $0.19). Next quarter is down 21.9% ($0.75 → $0.58). Full-year FY2026 estimates are holding better at $2.82 (down 1.4% over 90 days), suggesting analysts believe the tariff headwind is concentrated in H1 and recoverable. But 15 analysts have converged on a $0.19 current-quarter estimate — this is a deeply depressed earnings quarter.

**International:** The strongest growth vector. International sales grew 16% to $394M in FY2025, and management guides 18-20% international growth in FY2026. Japan launched in Q2 2025 and represents a large addressable market. If YETI can sustain high-teens international growth, it can offset low-single-digit U.S. trends for several years.

**Category expansion:** The Helimix acquisition ($38M) extends YETI into sport/health/wellness (shaker bottles). Mystery Ranch adds technical backpacks. Coolers & Equipment growth (+7%) outpaced the declining drinkware segment, suggesting category diversification is working. Cookware, outdoor furniture (Hondo Beach Chair), and cargo systems are additional adjacencies.

**Wholesale restocking:** Throughout FY2025, sell-through outpaced sell-in at wholesale accounts, depleting retail channel inventory. Management expects wholesale to grow faster than DTC in FY2026. If retailer inventory normalization occurs, it provides a potential demand uplift that doesn't require incremental consumer spending growth.

**Structural concern:** U.S. drinkware (approximately 46% of total revenue) declined in FY2025. This category has low barriers to entry, is trend-driven, and faces competition from Stanley, Hydro Flask, and private-label alternatives. YETI's premiumization strategy works when consumer confidence is healthy; it is vulnerable to trade-down behavior in a stagflationary environment with diesel at $5.29/gallon and consumer sentiment down 12.5% YoY.

The forward multiple is cheap only if estimates hold, and the near-term estimate trajectory is deteriorating. The FCF yield of 5.9% provides a reasonable floor, and the aggressive buyback at these prices suggests management shares this view.

## Competitive Landscape

YETI's competitive moat is its brand, which commands 57%+ gross margins in a category where most competitors operate at significantly lower margins. The brand has proven durable through the Stanley craze, the 2023 product recall, and COVID-era supply disruptions. The moat is narrow, however:

**Barriers to entry are low.** The 10-K explicitly states this. RTIC, Ozark Trail (Walmart private label), and numerous DTC brands offer functionally similar products at 30-50% lower prices. YETI's differentiation relies on perceived quality, design, and lifestyle association — all susceptible to shifting consumer preferences.

**Drinkware brand loyalty is less sticky than cooler brand loyalty.** The Stanley episode showed that a single TikTok trend can redirect hundreds of millions in spending within the category. YETI's drinkware underperformance in FY2025 is partly a normalization from this competitive disruption.

**Category expansion** is YETI's most promising competitive response. Moving into bags (Mystery Ranch), shaker bottles (Helimix), cookware, and outdoor furniture diversifies the revenue base and creates cross-selling opportunities within the installed base. Each new category also reinforces the lifestyle brand positioning, which is harder to replicate than any single product.

**International markets** offer a longer runway because YETI brand awareness is lower and competitive dynamics are less promotional than in the U.S.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariff exposure (Critical):** The 430 bps cumulative gross margin headwind from tariffs is the defining risk. Manufacturing is concentrated in China (declining share), Thailand, and Vietnam — all subject to active or threatened tariffs. The February 2026 SCOTUS ruling invalidated IEEPA tariffs, but new tariffs under alternative authorities were immediately initiated. The range of outcomes is wide: tariff refunds could boost margins by 100+ bps; tariff escalation could compress margins by an additional 100-200 bps.

**Consumer spending contraction (High):** Per the world model, consumer sentiment is -12.5% YoY, retail sales declined 0.2% in January, and the economy is in confirmed stagflation. YETI sells $30-$2,000 products to consumers who are simultaneously facing $5.29/gallon diesel, 6.5%+ mortgage rates, and rising food prices. Trade-down risk is real. The 30.5% of car trade-ins underwater (+4.2pp YoY) indicates the consumer balance sheet is weakening.

**U.S. market maturation (Medium-High):** U.S. sales declined 1% in FY2025 and represent 79% of revenue. If this is a cyclical dip, recovery is plausible. If it reflects saturation — most interested consumers already own YETI products — the growth algorithm breaks.

**CFO transition (Medium):** McMullen's departure after 10 years removes institutional knowledge during peak tariff uncertainty. Bomar (from Home Depot) brings relevant skills but is unproven at YETI.

**Short interest (Elevated):** At 13.4% of float with 4.5 days to cover, short sellers have a meaningful position against YETI. This reflects institutional skepticism about the earnings trajectory and tariff exposure.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
- FY2026 revenue guidance of 6-8% growth is above consensus and backed by international momentum (+18-20% guided), wholesale restocking, and new product launches
- H2 2026 margins should expand YoY as tariff headwinds are fully lapped, creating positive earnings revision potential in Q3/Q4
- Management is buying back stock aggressively at current prices ($298M in FY2025 at $36.49 avg), providing EPS support and signaling confidence
- FCF yield of 5.9% on a fortress balance sheet ($188M cash, $300M undrawn revolver) limits permanent capital impairment
- At 12.9x FY2026E EPS, the stock is pricing in significant pessimism. If tariffs stabilize and U.S. consumer sentiment recovers, re-rating to 15-16x on a $2.80+ EPS base yields $42-$45

### Bear Case
- Current-quarter EPS estimates down 38.5% in 90 days — earnings deterioration is accelerating
- U.S. drinkware (46% of revenue) faces structural competitive pressure with low barriers to entry
- Tariff environment could worsen rather than stabilize: new Section 232 tariffs, reciprocal tariffs, or supply chain disruption from Hormuz/energy crisis could add further margin pressure
- Consumer spending is in active contraction phase per multiple macro indicators — premium discretionary products are first to see demand destruction
- 13.4% short interest and put-heavy options positioning reflect institutional bearishness
- CFO departure during peak uncertainty reduces execution confidence
- Buybacks at $298M exceed FCF of $212M — the company is borrowing from future financial flexibility to support the stock

## Conclusion

YETI is a high-quality consumer brand with a clean balance sheet, strong FCF generation, and management putting significant capital behind its conviction in the stock. Financial health metrics are strong: sub-1x leverage, 57%+ gross margins, 11%+ FCF margins, and a track record of 4 consecutive earnings beats.

Offsetting factors: near-term estimates are falling rapidly, tariff headwinds create wide margin uncertainty, the U.S. business declined in FY2025, and the macro environment is hostile to premium consumer discretionary companies. The 13.4% short interest and 8.73 put/call OI ratio reflect informed skepticism.

This is the third consecutive HOLD rating. The score trajectory shows modest improvement in financial health (+0.7 since March) offset by competitive position erosion (-1.0). The fundamental picture has not changed enough to break the HOLD: the stock is neither cheap enough to compensate for near-term earnings risk (which would merit BUY) nor deteriorating fast enough to warrant AVOID given the balance sheet strength and FCF generation. The H2 2026 margin inflection is the potential catalyst — if tariffs stabilize and Q3/Q4 estimates begin revising upward, that would create a BUY setup. Absent that evidence, HOLD.
