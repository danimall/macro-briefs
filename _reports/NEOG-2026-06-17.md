**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Neogen is a food-safety and animal-safety diagnostics company working through the aftermath of its 2022 Reverse Morris Trust merger with 3M's Food Safety Division, a ~$5.3B transaction that roughly doubled revenue and share count but proved badly indigestible. The consequences run through every line of the current financials: a $1.06B goodwill impairment in FY2025 that produced a -$1.09B net loss and wiped out retained earnings, gross margins that have compressed from 50.2% (FY2024) to 46.6% (9M FY2026), flat-to-declining revenue, and a still-incomplete in-house Petrifilm manufacturing transition that is currently running duplicate cost. Layered on top are unremediated material weaknesses in internal controls, a securities-fraud class action naming former executives, and a near-total C-suite turnover.

The case against owning the stock is real but largely diffuse and untimed, and the data argue it is substantially in the price. The shares have rallied roughly 65% off the June 2025 trough near $4.56, the company has returned to thin profitability with three of its last four quarters beating consensus (by 53% to 96%), near-term estimates are rising (+6.3% current quarter, +11.1% next quarter over 30 days), and every sell-side target sits above the current price ($11-13 vs ~$9.31) with zero sell ratings. The balance sheet is not acute: covenant-compliant, no Term Loan principal due until Q2 FY2028, current ratio 3.3x, $160M cash, positive operating cash flow, and active deleveraging ($902M → ~$800M debt with ~$280M of divestiture proceeds inbound).

This is a balanced, low-conviction setup. The genuine idiosyncratic risks (unremediated material weaknesses, securities litigation, an unproven Petrifilm in-source, and structurally lost market share) are offset by a credible turnaround program, distant maturities, healthy liquidity, organic Food Safety re-acceleration, and constructive consensus. We rate NEOG **HOLD**. The valuation (~25x forward / ~31x next-year EPS on flat revenue and compressing margins) is not cheap enough to support a BUY, and the downside is too contained, with too constructive a consensus, to support an active AVOID. We classify this as an uncertain hold: the turnaround could work or falter, and the evidence does not yet support a stronger call in either direction.

## Company Overview

Neogen operates two reportable segments:

- **Food Safety (~74% of revenue, $638M in FY2025):** Immunoassay and DNA-based diagnostic test kits and consumables to detect foodborne pathogens, allergens, toxins, drug/pesticide residues, and spoilage organisms, plus culture media, indicator testing, and the **Petrifilm** product line acquired from 3M. The largest and structurally healthiest category is Indicator Testing & Culture Media.
- **Animal Safety (~26%, $257M in FY2025):** Veterinary instruments, rodent/insect control, and animal genomics testing. The biosecurity (cleaners/disinfectants) line has been divested, and the genomics business is held for sale to Zoetis.

The defining corporate event is the 2022 merger with 3M's Food Safety Division, which lifted revenue from $527M (FY2022) to $822M (FY2023) and share count from ~108M to ~217M today. Neogen owns the former 3M food-safety products, including Petrifilm, outright; 3M is now a former supplier and transition-service provider, not a current owner. The integration of ERP systems, manufacturing relocation, and Petrifilm in-sourcing has been the central operational challenge and the source of the impairment.

## Financial Analysis

The financials describe a company in the trough of a botched integration, now stabilizing operationally but still thinly profitable and over-leveraged relative to its earnings base.

**Revenue (flat to declining):**
- FY2023 $822M → FY2024 $924M → FY2025 $894.7M (-3.2%), with most of the FY2025 decline from FX (-$24.3M); organic was roughly flat.
- 9M FY2026 $645.1M vs $669.2M (-3.6% YoY), driven by the Cleaners & Disinfectants divestiture (-$41.5M) against +$7.6M of organic growth. Q3 FY2026 Food Safety grew +2.6% with positive organic growth, a tentative turn.

**Profitability:**
- The FY2025 -$1,092M net loss was driven by the goodwill impairment of the Food Safety reporting unit ($461M Q2 + ~$598M Q4). Goodwill fell from $2,136M to $1,065M. This was a one-time, non-cash write-down, not cash burn, a critical distinction for the investment case. An AVOID thesis built solely on "loss-making" misreads the impairment.
- Underlying operations have returned to thin profitability. 9M FY2026 net income was +$3.4M, though that was driven by a $76.4M gain on the C&D sale; operating performance remains barely above breakeven, with Q3 standalone operating profit of only ~$3.3M reversed by FX and interest to a -$17M net loss.
- Gross margin compressed from 50.2% (FY2024) to 47.1% (FY2025) to 46.6% (9M FY2026) on volume deleverage, inventory write-offs, sample-collection production inefficiencies, and duplicative Petrifilm manufacturing costs. Margin has not yet inflected upward, the single most important unproven element of the recovery.

**Earnings momentum:** The beat track record is genuinely positive — Q3 FY2026 +53.3%, Q2 +78.3%, Q1 +96.1%, with one miss in Q4 FY2025 (-34.6%). These are off small absolute bases ($0.06-0.10 quarterly EPS), but the direction and consistency are real, and consensus is rising on the front end (+6.3% current quarter, +11.1% next quarter over 30 days). The longer-dated picture is mixed: next-year EPS estimates fell 7.2% over 90 days to $0.30, indicating the Street is not extrapolating a sharp recovery.

**Balance sheet / leverage:**
- Total debt ~$800M (Feb 2026), down from $902M after $100M of divestiture-funded repayment. Components: $405M Term Loan, $346.5M of 8.625% Senior Notes due 2030, $48.5M revolver.
- Equity $2,102M but dominated by intangibles (goodwill $1,048M + amortizable intangibles $1,342M = $2.39B of $3.36B total assets); tangible book is negative.
- Annual interest expense ~$72M against ~$58M annual operating cash flow is the genuine financial pressure point — coverage is tight, and this constrains how much operational slippage the company can absorb.
- The Altman Z-Score of 1.13 ("distress") is distorted by impairment-driven negative EBIT, not liquidity. Current ratio is 3.32, D/E ~0.66, $160M cash, covenant-compliant as of Feb 2026, and no Term Loan principal due until Q2 FY2028. The leverage is not acute; the constraint is operational/earnings, namely covering interest on a thin-margin base.

**Cash flow:** Operating cash flow is positive (~$58M FY2025; $53M 9M FY2026), but FY2025 FCF was -$46M on elevated capex ($105M) for the new Lansing, MI Petrifilm facility. Capex normalizes to ~$50M in FY2026 as Lansing completes, which should drive FCF recovery into FY2027 if duplicate Petrifilm costs roll off.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the weakest pillar of the bull case and the most reliable dimension for sorting outcomes, so it deserves weight. The picture is a slow stabilization rather than a sharp turn:

- Organic revenue is barely positive (+$7.6M for 9M FY2026), with Q3 Food Safety +2.6% organically the cleanest evidence of a turn.
- This does not meet the profile of a high-conviction earnings inflection — there is no >40% revenue acceleration and no >30% estimate-revision velocity. Near-term estimates are rising but next-year estimates are falling, which argues against a sharp re-rating.
- The durable franchise is the consumables/razor-blade model in Food Safety diagnostics (indicators, pathogens, sample collection), where recurring volumes support a recovery. R&D spend is low (~$21M, ~2.4% of revenue), consistent with a consumables model rather than a high-innovation pipeline, which caps the structural growth ceiling.
- The growth thesis depends heavily on regaining share lost during the integration stumbles and on completing the Petrifilm transition to restore margins. Both are achievable but unproven.

Calendar-2026 food-safety market growth is expected by management, and end markets (food production volumes, animal safety) are characterized as at or near cyclical lows, providing a low base from which to re-accelerate. The growth case is plausible but not yet confirmed.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$9.31 against FY2026 consensus EPS of $0.37 and next-year $0.30, NEOG trades at ~25x forward / ~31x next-year earnings. On flat-to-declining revenue with compressing gross margins, this is not a cheap stock; it is reasonably priced for a credible turnaround and would look expensive if the recovery stalls.

The peer set is heterogeneous and largely unprofitable, which makes the reported peer-median P/E of 38.9 a poor anchor:

Four of five comps carry negative trailing P/Es, reflecting a sector in which several names are also working through impairment and restructuring. The only clean profitable comp (HAE at 38.9x) is a faster, more stable grower. Against that backdrop, NEOG's ~25x forward is neither a clear discount nor a clear excess.

Three observations bound the valuation:
1. **Catalyst support exists but is unproven.** The path to value creation — Petrifilm in-sourcing completion, duplicate cost roll-off, margin recovery toward 50%, FCF inflection as Lansing capex ends — is identifiable and dated to FY2027. This keeps the name out of value-trap territory.
2. **Consensus is constructive, not impaired.** Every sell-side target ($11 low, $12 mean/median, $13 high) sits 18-40% above the current price, with one strong buy, one buy, two holds, and zero sells. A genuinely impaired-equity thesis would have to explain why every covering analyst sees upside. We do not have that explanation; the residual risks are real but diffuse and largely disclosed.
3. **The valuation does not justify a BUY.** A 25x forward multiple on a company with flat revenue, margins that have not yet inflected, negative tangible book, and a serious governance overhang is full enough to remove asymmetric upside. We will pay up for proof, not promise.

We score valuation 5.0 — neither stretched nor cheap, with modest asymmetry given targets above price offset by an unproven recovery.

## Competitive Landscape

Neogen competes in food-safety diagnostics against larger, better-capitalized players (Thermo Fisher, bioMérieux, Hygiena, Merck/Sigma-Aldrich) and in animal genomics against Zoetis, which is now buying its genomics unit. The 10-K states that competitors have "greater financial, technical, manufacturing, marketing, R&D and management resources."

The competitive trajectory is the more important read, and it has been negative. The ERP/SAP implementation and manufacturing relocation caused shipment delays and backlog that the company describes as having driven permanent share loss to competitors who capitalized on its fulfillment problems. Organic growth must now claw that back, which is harder than holding ground. The Petrifilm franchise is a genuine moat — a consumables product with installed-base lock-in — but the in-house manufacturing transition introduces execution risk to a previously stable revenue stream. Low R&D intensity reinforces the consumables profile but limits the company's ability to out-innovate larger rivals. The Q3 organic re-acceleration in Food Safety is the first evidence that the share bleed may be stabilizing, but it is one quarter.

## Risk Assessment

The company-specific risks are real, idiosyncratic, and in several cases newly elevated:

1. **Unremediated material weaknesses in internal controls.** Disclosed in the FY2025 10-K and still not remediated as of the Q3 FY2026 10-Q (Feb 2026). Disclosure controls were deemed "not effective," tied to management-review controls over goodwill valuation. A new Director of Internal Controls has been hired. For a company that just took a $1B impairment, this is a serious governance red flag and the most binary near-term risk — remediation removes a discount; continued failure deepens it.
2. **Securities litigation cluster.** A Section 10(b) class action (class period Jan 5, 2023–June 3, 2025, re: integration disclosures) naming the company and former CEO Adent and former CFO Naemura; a 1933 Act suit over the 2022 merger offering materials; and a December 2025 derivative suit. All early-stage with no loss estimable.
3. **Petrifilm manufacturing transition.** The largest single operational catalyst and risk. Duplicate costs currently depress margins; a successful Lansing in-source is essential to the margin thesis, and failure risks further impairment of the intangible base.
4. **Vet HyCoat recall (Jan 2026).** Voluntary recall of ~133,000 units after adverse events in horses. Neogen was distributor, not manufacturer, and asserts defenses, but demand letters and at least one lawsuit exist; loss is "probable" but not estimable.
5. **Further goodwill/intangible impairment risk** given $2.39B of intangibles remain on the books.
6. **Tariffs** raising input costs and dampening demand; new Q3 FY2026 third-party supplier quality problems in Animal Safety, some tariff-related, are expected to bleed into early FY2027.

The critical judgment is how much of this is already priced. The impairment, integration failure, and litigation cluster were all public during the decline to the June 2025 trough and the subsequent recovery as new management began executing. The residual risks are genuine but largely untimed and unquantified, and the constructive consensus indicates the market is not treating the equity as structurally impaired. We do not have a specific, dated downside catalyst that the market is missing, which is why this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID.

### Options Market Signal

The options screen shows a reported jump in ATM implied volatility from 49.6% to 518.2% alongside a put/call open-interest ratio falling from 0.99 to 0.18. The 518% IV reading is almost certainly a low-liquidity data artifact rather than a genuine market signal and should not be relied upon. The more interpretable shift — open interest moving sharply toward calls (P/C 0.99 → 0.18) — is mildly constructive positioning, consistent with the rising-estimate, recovering-fundamentals picture, though thin options liquidity limits how much weight it can carry.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
New management is executing the right playbook: divesting non-core, low-margin businesses (Cleaners & Disinfectants to Kersia for $121.7M net; Genomics to Zoetis for $160M, expected to close 1H FY2027), using proceeds to deleverage, and refocusing on the core Food Safety franchise. The heavy Lansing capex cycle is ending, which should flip FCF positive into FY2027. If the Petrifilm in-source completes and duplicate costs roll off, gross margin can recover toward 50%, and on a refocused, deleveraged base the company can compound earnings off the current ~$0.37 EPS run-rate. Food Safety has returned to organic growth. Three of four quarters have beaten, near-term estimates are rising, and every analyst target is above the current price. A clean margin inflection plus material-weakness remediation could re-rate the stock toward the $12-13 consensus targets.

### Bear Case
Margins are still compressing (46.6% in 9M FY2026), and the Petrifilm transition could overrun, deepening duplicate costs or triggering further intangible impairment. Revenue is flat-to-declining with permanent lost share that organic growth has not yet recovered. Interest expense consumes most of operating cash flow, leaving little margin for execution error. Unremediated material weaknesses and active securities litigation are unresolved overhangs that could surprise negatively. At ~25x forward earnings on this profile, any slip in the recovery would compress the multiple meaningfully. The bear case needs only the margin recovery to stall.

The two cases are close to balanced, which is the signature of a hold rather than a strong directional view.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This is not a confident growth thesis — the recovery is real but unproven and saddled with governance overhangs. It is also not an income story (no dividend). The honest classification is uncertain: the turnaround could work or falter, and the evidence does not yet support a stronger call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Gross margin expands sequentially for two consecutive quarters back above ~49%, confirming the Petrifilm duplicate costs are rolling off.
2. Material weaknesses formally remediated (disclosure controls deemed effective) AND securities litigation moves toward resolution or quantification at a manageable level.
3. Free cash flow turns sustainably positive (consistent with normalized ~$50M capex post-Lansing), and the Zoetis genomics sale closes on terms, with proceeds applied to further debt reduction.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A new goodwill/intangible impairment, or gross margin falling below ~45%, signaling the Petrifilm transition is failing.
2. A covenant breach, downgraded refinancing terms, or operating cash flow turning negative such that interest coverage becomes a near-term concern.
3. Material adverse development in the securities litigation, or evidence the material weaknesses cannot be remediated, indicating deeper governance/control failure.
4. Organic Food Safety growth turning negative again, confirming continued structural share loss.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, with particular attention to the gross-margin trajectory and any update on material-weakness remediation; and the expected 1H FY2027 close of the Zoetis genomics sale.

## Conclusion

Neogen is a self-inflicted turnaround. The 3M Food Safety merger nearly doubled the business but was executed poorly enough to produce a goodwill write-off, lost share, and margin compression. The new management team is doing the right defensive things — divesting non-core units, deleveraging, refocusing on core diagnostics — and the operational picture has stabilized, with Food Safety back to organic growth and three of four quarters beating consensus. The balance sheet is not acute, with distant maturities, ample liquidity, positive operating cash flow, and an active deleveraging program.

The bearish case rests on real but diffuse and untimed risks: unremediated material weaknesses, securities litigation, an unproven Petrifilm in-source, and structurally lost share. These are largely already in the price: the stock recovered ~65% off the trough, trades below every analyst target, and carries zero sell ratings. We cannot point to a specific, dated downside catalyst the market is missing, and the valuation at ~25x forward is not so stretched as to invite an active short or so cheap as to be a value trap. That combination — genuine idiosyncratic risk balanced against a credible recovery program, distant maturities, and constructive consensus — defines a balanced, low-conviction position.

We rate NEOG **HOLD**. We would turn constructive on confirmed margin recovery and control remediation, and turn negative on a further impairment, a margin relapse below ~45%, or material litigation deterioration. Until the Petrifilm transition proves out, the right posture is to monitor, not to commit capital in either direction.
