**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CNH Industrial is a global pure-play agriculture and construction equipment manufacturer (Case IH, New Holland, STEYR, Raven precision-ag) currently in the trough of a severe agricultural-machinery downcycle. Revenue has fallen ~31% in two years, from $22.1B in 2023 to $15.3B in 2025, and Q1 2026 confirmed the bottom is not yet behind the company: total revenue was flat YoY at $3.83B, but Industrial Activities swung to an Adjusted EBIT loss of $45M, Agriculture EBIT margin collapsed to 1.0% (from 5.4%), Construction ran at –4.9%, and the entire $10M of consolidated net income came from the Financial Services arm while Industrial Activities lost $64M.

The valuation, properly measured, is undemanding rather than stretched. The widely-cited 33x P/E is the trailing multiple on trough EPS of $0.41; it is not a forward number. On consensus next-year EPS of $0.73 (16 analysts), CNH trades at roughly 14x forward earnings, below the peer median of 21x and consistent with a cyclically depressed name priced for a recovery that has not yet arrived. The stock is down ~27% from its 52-week high of $14.27 and sits near its 52-week low ($10.46 vs. $9.00), so much of the bear case is already in the price. The bear scenario ($8–9) implies –14% to –23% downside against a recovery case that, on mid-cycle earnings, implies +34% to +53%.

The investment question is purely one of cycle timing: is early 2026 the bottom? Two recent developments lean constructive: the June 1, 2026 Section 232 tariff relief, which reverses the single most-cited 2026 cost headwind and is "slightly more material" for CNH because it imports more finished goods than Deere; and EMEA Agriculture growing ~20% in Q1 while management continues to invest through the trough (R&D rose to 7.9% of Ag sales). Pulling the other way: South American credit deterioration in the captive finance book, a lossmaking construction segment, and consensus estimates that are still drifting lower. With the thesis genuinely balanced and insufficient evidence to call the bottom, the appropriate stance is HOLD.

## Company Overview

CNH operates three segments. **Agriculture (~82% of net sales)** is the profit engine, anchored by the global Case IH and New Holland brands plus STEYR (Europe) and the Raven/Hemisphere/Augmenta precision-ag and autonomy stack (the "Iron + Tech" strategy). **Construction (~18% of net sales)** sells CASE and New Holland Construction equipment but is subscale versus Caterpillar, Komatsu, and Deere, and is currently lossmaking. **Financial Services (CNH Capital / Banco CNH in Brazil)** is the captive finance arm providing retail and dealer floorplan financing; it is both the steadiest earner and a critical demand-support mechanism through the cycle.

Distribution runs through ~2,300 independent ag dealers (5,000+ locations) and ~400 construction dealers. The competitive moat rests on the dual-brand dealer networks, the aftermarket parts/service annuity (equipment lives 20+ years), and increasingly the precision-tech integration the company is acquiring its way into.

A structural fact frames the entire investment: CNH is a **controlled company**. EXOR N.V., the Agnelli family holding company, holds roughly 45.6% of voting power via a loyalty/special-voting-share program. This entrenches control, can deter takeovers, and reduces effective float and liquidity. CNH is also tightly bound to the broader EXOR ecosystem: its off-road engines are supplied primarily by FPT Industrial (Iveco-controlled) under a 10-year supply agreement, and CEO Gerrit Marx came from sister-company Iveco.

With Q1 2026 revenue flat YoY, COGS rose to 82.2% of sales (from 81.0%) on a combination of tariffs and low-volume fixed-cost deleverage. That 100% of Q1 consolidated profit came from Financial Services is the clearest signal that the operating businesses are running at or below breakeven at current volumes.

**The balance sheet looks alarming on a naive screen and benign on disaggregation.** The Altman Z-Score of 0.75 flags "distress," but it is structurally unsuited to a captive-finance business. Of $26.8B gross debt at YE2025, $22.9B sits in Financial Services, matched against $22.6B of financing receivables and largely funded via non-recourse asset-backed securitizations. Net debt of Industrial Activities is only ~$2.5B, and consolidated D/E of 4.49 is normal for this model. Liquidity is solid at ~$8.9B (cash, restricted cash, undrawn facilities), the revolver is extended to 2031, and the company is in compliance with all covenants. CNH is cyclically depressed, not financially impaired.

Capital allocation is conservative and appropriate for the cycle. A $500M buyback authorized in February 2024 had ~$224M remaining at Q1 2026 (~2.4M shares repurchased in Q1); the dividend is small (~0.95% yield). The pension is modestly underfunded (–$327M). Cash preservation is the stated priority.

**Earnings track record and estimate momentum.** The four-quarter record is mixed (2 beats, 1 in-line, 1 miss), with a sharp –40.4% miss in Q3 2025. Estimate revisions are negative across the board: current-quarter EPS revised down 7.2% over 90 days, full-year down 2.2%, next-year down 2.2%. This negative earnings momentum is the principal quantitative argument against turning constructive now; the Street has not yet confirmed the trough.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is negative; the case is for a recovery, not for current momentum. Consensus full-year 2026 EPS sits at $0.41 (flat-to-trough) with next-year at $0.73, implying analysts expect a ~78% earnings recovery into 2027 as volumes normalize and tariff/cost pressures ease.

The geographic picture is more nuanced than the headline trough suggests. In Q1 2026, EMEA Agriculture grew ~19.8% while South America Agriculture fell ~27.8% and North America Ag was down 3.3%. Europe is recovering; Brazil is the problem child, hit simultaneously by soft commodity prices, weather, and farmer-solvency stress that flows directly into CNH's loan book. This divergence matters: a North American replacement-demand recovery (the largest large-ag market) is the swing factor for the consolidated thesis and has not yet inflected.

The structural growth thesis is intact: global protein demand, finite arable land, and precision-tech adoption support multi-year equipment and software demand. CNH is investing through the trough rather than cutting, which pressures near-term margins but supports the recovery. The risk is that CNH is the #2/#3 player playing catch-up to Deere in high-margin precision-ag software, where Deere is pivoting hard toward recurring revenue and raising the bar.

The +30% estimate-revision BUY signal is explicitly **absent** here: revisions are negative, not positive. This keeps the growth score below the midpoint despite the longer-term recovery optionality.

The genuine forward multiple, on consensus next-year EPS of $0.73, is approximately 14.3x, a discount to the peer median of 21x.

On a trough-earnings basis the trailing multiple is uninformative, the textbook trap of valuing a cyclical at the bottom. On normalized/forward earnings, CNH at ~14x forward sits below the peer median and roughly in line with AGCO (10.7x), its closest pure-ag comparable, which is also depressed. The consensus price target mean of $13.31 (median $12.00) implies 27–46% upside, with a high of $20.50 reflecting the mid-cycle earnings-power case. Barclays carries an Overweight with a $12 PT (raised from $11 in March 2026).

The valuation does not offer the asymmetric, catalyst-driven mispricing that would justify a high score and a BUY: the recovery is not yet visible in the numbers, and the cheapness reflects genuine cyclical and credit risk rather than an identifiable market error. But it is reasonable rather than stretched, which removes valuation as a reason to AVOID. The score sits at the midpoint: cheap-on-forward, but with the recovery unconfirmed and a history (across this system) of "cheap" cyclicals sorting as value traps until a catalyst is in hand.

## Competitive Landscape

CNH is the more cyclically and operationally levered, lower-margin alternative to Deere. In agriculture it competes with Deere (the dominant, highest-margin leader), AGCO, Kubota, Claas, and Mahindra; in construction with Caterpillar, Komatsu, Volvo, Hitachi, SANY, and Bobcat, where it is clearly subscale and lossmaking. The dual-brand dealer network and aftermarket annuity are durable moats, and the April 2026 30% stake in Abilene Machine (U.S. aftermarket parts) is consistent with building the higher-margin parts business.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-eroding rather than improving. Deere's lead in precision-ag software is widening the margin gap, and CNH is responding from the #2/#3 position. The relative-performance evidence is stark: cumulative TSR of 93 (indexed) versus the peer group's 191 since the 2021 base; CNH has roughly halved relative to peers and sits below the starting index level. EMEA share gains in Q1 are a genuine positive, but they do not offset the structural catch-up dynamic in tech-enabled ag. This supports a score near the midpoint.

## Risk Assessment

**Agricultural cyclicality (active).** Management explicitly expects soft conditions to persist into FY2026. Low farmer income, high used-equipment inventories, and elevated input costs are suppressing replacement demand. Q1 finished-goods inventory built to $5.23B (from $4.65B), a leading indicator of potential discounting if a 2H 2026 recovery does not materialize.

**Tariffs — now partially relieved.** The 10-K (Feb 2026) treated tariffs as a worsening 2026 headwind, with CNH more exposed than Deere because it imports more finished goods. The Section 232 reduction reverses this direction mid-cycle and is "slightly more material" for CNH and AGCO than Deere (per Citi). This is the most timely actionable positive, even if the magnitude is not yet quantified.

**Brazil / South America credit deterioration.** Retail credit reserves rose $131M YoY for Brazil; consolidated 30+ day delinquencies climbed to 3.5% (from 2.3%); South America non-performing receivables are $190M of a $3.8B book; CNH is refinancing $130M of distressed installments. Because Financial Services is currently carrying all consolidated profit, stress in this segment is the most dangerous idiosyncratic risk.

**Iveco/FPT dependence.** A single-point engine-supply dependency within the same EXOR umbrella, plus residual exposure to the historical FPT emissions matter and EU truck-cartel follow-on claims (with recourse to Iveco, deemed remote).

**Governance / EXOR control.** The loyalty-voting stake entrenches control, depresses float, and can deter a takeover premium. Tax-residency complexity (Netherlands incorporation, U.K. tax residency, Italian branch) adds PFIC and withholding uncertainty for U.S. holders.

**New litigation.** Poland antitrust decision (Dec 2025), ~€57M fine on CNH Polska and dealers; CNH is appealing with no reserve taken.

Most of these risks are well-disclosed and at least partially priced given the stock's position near multi-year lows. The risk score is mid-range: real and active, but not dominated by an unpriced binary catalyst.

### Options Market Signal

The put/call open-interest ratio collapsed from 4.58 to 0.91 over the month to June 18 while the price was roughly flat (+1.7%). The unwind of heavy put positioning is consistent with the post-tariff-relief stabilization and removes a prior bearish skew, though it is not by itself a bullish confirmation. Short interest is moderate at 7.1% of float with 4.2 days to cover: not a crowded short, and not a setup pointing to a squeeze.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Early 2026 is the cycle trough. North American replacement demand inflects in 2H 2026 as used-equipment inventories clear; the tariff relief flows through COGS and restores Ag margins toward mid-cycle (8–11%); EMEA momentum broadens; Brazil credit stabilizes as commodity prices recover. On normalized mid-cycle earnings, a peer-median multiple supports $14–16, and the analyst high of $20.50 reflects a full mid-cycle re-rating. Upside of +34% to +53% to the recovery range.

**Bear case.** The trough deepens into 2027. Brazil credit losses accelerate in the Financial Services book that is currently carrying all the profit; finished-goods inventory forces discounting that further compresses Ag margins; construction stays lossmaking; tariff relief proves smaller than hoped. EPS stays near $0.40 and the stock retests the 52-week low. Downside to $8–9, or –14% to –23%.

The asymmetry favors the upside (+34%/+53% reward vs. –14%/–23% risk), which is why this is not a short. The recovery is unconfirmed: estimates are still falling, Industrial Activities are lossmaking, and the captive-finance cushion is showing stress. That combination of favorable asymmetry and unconfirmed timing supports a hold rather than a buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an uncertain HOLD on a genuinely balanced cycle-timing question, not a directional view that appreciation or further decline is more likely from here.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive EPS estimate revisions (reversing the current –2.2% 90-day trend), confirming the Street sees the trough.
- North America Agriculture revenue inflecting to flat-or-positive YoY, signaling replacement-demand recovery in the key large-ag market.
- Industrial Activities Adjusted EBIT returning to positive, with Ag margin recovering toward mid-single digits.
- Stabilization in Brazil/South America delinquencies (the 30+ day figure declining from 3.5%).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Acceleration of South American credit losses materially impairing Financial Services profit.
- Forced finished-goods discounting that drives Ag margin below breakeven for a second consecutive quarter.
- A covenant or liquidity deterioration at the Industrial level (currently benign at ~$2.5B net debt / ~$8.9B liquidity).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly results (Q2 2026, expected late July/August), which should clarify whether the tariff relief and EMEA strength are offsetting Brazil and inventory pressure. The cycle-bottom thesis should be confirmable or refutable within 6–9 months.

## Conclusion

CNH is a well-financed (at the industrial level) #2 global ag-equipment maker at or near a cyclical trough, trading at ~14x forward consensus EPS, below the peer median and reasonable for the recovery profile, with much of the bear case already reflected in a price near multi-year lows. The positive offsets are concrete: the Section 232 tariff relief reverses the most-cited 2026 headwind, EMEA Ag is growing, the balance sheet is sound, analyst sentiment is improving with consensus targets above the current price, and management is investing through the trough. The negative offsets are equally concrete: Industrial Activities are lossmaking, all profit is coming from a Financial Services book now showing Brazil credit stress, construction is a drag, and estimates are still falling.

The recommendation is **HOLD**. The asymmetry favors the upside, which rules out shorting, but the recovery is unconfirmed and the timing is genuinely two-sided. I would wait for confirmation that early 2026 is the bottom — positive estimate revisions, North America Ag inflecting, or Industrial EBIT returning to positive — before upgrading to BUY.
