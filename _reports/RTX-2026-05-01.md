## Executive Summary

RTX Corporation reported Q1 2026 results on April 21 that beat consensus on both revenue ($22.1B) and adjusted EPS ($1.78 vs. $1.53 estimate, a 16.1% surprise), extending a perfect four-quarter beat streak. Management raised full-year 2026 guidance to $92.5–93.5B in sales and $6.70–6.90 in adjusted EPS, implying 5–6% topline growth and ~21% adjusted EPS growth. Despite the beat-and-raise, shares dropped ~10% from the pre-earnings $196 level to $176, creating what appears to be a dislocation between fundamental performance and price.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) a record $268B backlog providing ~3x revenue visibility across defense and commercial aerospace, (2) proven margin expansion from 8.1% operating margin in 2024 to 10.5% in 2025 with further runway as Pratt & Whitney recovers from the Powder Metal Matter charge, and (3) structural defense demand driven by dual-theater geopolitical conflict and the Golden Dome missile defense initiative. These pillars are partially offset by the Airbus arbitration over GTF engine delivery delays (filed March 2026, unknown magnitude), three simultaneous external compliance monitors from DOJ/SEC/State Department, and a valuation that at ~25.5x FY2026E adjusted EPS sits above the defense peer median of ~23x.

The 10% post-earnings decline, combined with a consensus median price target of $222.50 (26% upside from current levels), suggests the market is overweighting near-term execution uncertainty relative to the multi-year earnings trajectory. RTX remains a BUY with a base case target of $210, though position sizing should reflect the elevated risk profile from the Airbus dispute and regulatory overhang.

## Company Overview

RTX Corporation is the world's largest aerospace and defense company by revenue, formed from the 2020 merger of United Technologies' aerospace businesses with Raytheon Company. It operates through three segments:

**Collins Aerospace** ($30.2B revenue, 2025): The largest supplier of aerospace and defense subsystems globally, including avionics, power controls, aerostructures, interiors, and mechanical systems. Operating margins reached 16.3% in 2025, up from 14.6% in 2024.

**Pratt & Whitney** ($32.9B revenue, 2025): Aircraft engines across commercial (GTF for A320neo family), military (F135 for F-35), business jet, and general aviation. Operating margins recovered to 7.9% from negative territory in 2023 following the $5.4B Powder Metal charge. This segment is the primary growth engine but also the primary risk vector.

**Raytheon** ($28.0B revenue, 2025): Integrated defense systems — Patriot, NASAMS, LTAMDS air defense; SPY-6 radar; AMRAAM, Tomahawk, Stinger precision weapons. Operating margins of 11.5%, up from 9.7%. The direct beneficiary of surging global munitions demand.

Revenue mix in 2025: 46% U.S. and foreign government defense, 48% commercial aerospace, 6% other commercial. Total backlog of $268B ($161B commercial, $107B defense) provides approximately three years of forward revenue visibility.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $67.1B (2022) → $68.9B (2023) → $80.7B (2024) → $88.6B (2025). Organic growth in 2025 was $8.9B, with P&W contributing $4.8B, Collins $2.6B, and Raytheon $1.7B. FY2026 guidance of $92.5–93.5B implies ~5% growth on the headline but 10% organic growth per Q1 commentary.

**Margin expansion** is the defining financial story. Operating margins expanded 240bps in 2025 (10.5% vs. 8.1%) as fixed-price contract losses narrowed ($386M in 2025 vs. $648M in 2023) and Pratt & Whitney recovered. The path to 12–13% consolidated operating margins over the next 2–3 years is credible: Collins is already at 16.3%, Raytheon at 11.5% and improving, and P&W's 7.9% has significant recovery runway as Powder Metal costs normalize and GTF aftermarket scales.

**Cash flow quality is exceptional.** Operating cash flow surged 48% to $10.6B in 2025. Free cash flow of $7.9B implies a 9.0% FCF margin and a 3.4% FCF yield on the current ~$235B market cap. FCF substantially exceeded net income ($6.7B), indicating high-quality earnings with limited accrual distortion.

**Balance sheet is improving.** Long-term debt declined from $42.4B (2023 peak, post-$10B ASR) to $34.3B at end of 2025, with $3.35B repaid in 2025 alone. Debt maturities are well-laddered: $3.4B in 2026, $2.9B in 2027, $3.5B in 2028. Cash of $7.4B plus a $5B undrawn revolver provides ample liquidity. Both Moody's and S&P revised outlooks from negative to stable in 2025, reflecting the deleveraging trajectory. Net debt/EBITDA is approximately 2.9x based on net debt of ~$30.5B ($37.9B total debt less $7.4B cash) and 2025 operating profit of $9.3B plus depreciation/amortization.

**Capital allocation priorities:** Dividends ($2.72/share, ~1.6% yield) are well-covered at 39% of adjusted EPS. Share repurchases were minimal in 2024–2025 ($494M combined) after the $12.9B ASR in 2023. Portfolio pruning through divestitures ($3.7B in aggregate proceeds) demonstrates disciplined capital management.

**R&D investment:** Company-funded R&D of $2.8B (3.2% of sales) plus $4.9B in customer-funded R&D totals $7.7B (8.7% of revenue). This level of investment sustains technology moats across all three segments.

## Growth Analysis

RTX's growth profile is supported by three distinct demand drivers:

**1. Defense demand (46% of revenue):** Defense bookings were $61B in both 2024 and 2025, up from $51B in 2023. The $107B defense backlog reflects sustained international demand for air defense systems (Patriot, NASAMS, LTAMDS), precision munitions (AMRAAM, Tomahawk), and naval systems (SPY-6). The Golden Dome missile defense initiative, backed by $24.4B in supplementary DoW funding and $25.4B for munitions/supply chain resiliency, provides a domestic demand floor through 2028+. Current global conflict structurally elevates defense spending across NATO and allied nations.

**2. Commercial aftermarket (growing share of the 48% commercial mix):** P&W's installed GTF base generates recurring MRO revenue as these engines age. Collins' aftermarket revenue grows with air traffic recovery above pre-COVID levels. Aftermarket carries significantly higher margins than OEM, making this mix shift the primary margin expansion lever.

**3. GTF Advantage certification (April 2026):** EASA certification of the next-generation GTF Advantage engine for the A320neo family opens a new OEM revenue stream and positions P&W for the next production cycle. This variant addresses durability concerns that drove the Powder Metal charge.

Consensus estimates reflect steady growth: FY2026E adjusted EPS of $6.90 (+0.3% from $6.88 in 2025 on an adjusted basis, but management guidance of $6.70–6.90 implies substantial growth from the $4.96 GAAP EPS base). FY2027E adjusted EPS of $7.55 implies 9.4% growth. Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current-year estimates rose 1.2% over 30 days and 1.7% over 90 days. Next-quarter estimates declined 2.5%, which warrants monitoring but appears seasonal.

## Competitive Landscape

**Dual commercial-defense diversification** is unique among defense primes. LMT and NOC are >90% defense; GE Aerospace is predominantly commercial. RTX's roughly 50/50 split provides counter-cyclical balance. In the current environment, both cycles are simultaneously favorable.

**Backlog depth** provides approximately three years of revenue visibility. Defense bookings of $61B in 2025 represent a strong book-to-bill ratio for the Raytheon segment, indicating continued pipeline growth.

**Technology moats are deep.** P&W's GTF engine family is the sole power plant for certain aircraft variants; Collins' integrated avionics suites have multi-decade program lives; Raytheon's air defense systems (Patriot, LTAMDS) have no direct commercial substitutes at scale. Combined company-funded and customer-funded R&D of $7.7B sustains these positions.

**Competitive trajectory is improving.** Collins margins expanded 170bps YoY to 16.3%. Raytheon margins expanded 180bps to 11.5%. P&W is recovering from the Powder Metal trough. All three segments are gaining margin simultaneously for the first time since the merger.

The primary competitive risk is Airbus's potential shift toward alternative engine suppliers (CFM/LEAP) for future narrowbody programs if the P&W relationship deteriorates further. This risk is real but contained: the GTF installed base creates multi-decade aftermarket lock-in regardless of new engine selection for future programs.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Airbus Arbitration (HIGH).** The March 2026 arbitration filing is the most material near-term risk. Airbus alleges P&W is prioritizing MRO over new engine deliveries, forcing production ramp softening. Financial damages are unspecified but could range from hundreds of millions to low single-digit billions depending on how lost production capacity and contractual penalties are calculated. P&W's remaining Powder Metal accrued liability of $0.7B may prove insufficient if arbitration awards are added. This risk is partially mitigated by structural interdependence: Airbus needs P&W engines for its A320neo backlog as much as P&W needs Airbus orders.

**2. Triple Compliance Monitor Overlay (MODERATE).** Three simultaneous external monitors (DOJ DPA, SEC Administrative Order, State Department SCO) create operational friction, cost, and strategic constraint. The 3-year DPA term extends through late 2027. Any violation could trigger criminal prosecution. Transaction-related requirements may delay future divestitures or M&A.

**3. Executive Order on Defense Contractor Capital Returns (MODERATE).** The January 2026 EO could restrict buybacks, dividends, and executive compensation during periods of perceived underperformance. RTX's minimal current buyback program limits immediate impact, but the precedent constrains future capital return capacity as deleveraging completes.

**4. Fixed-Price Contract Losses (MODERATE, IMPROVING).** The trajectory is positive but the risk persists, particularly as the Department of War increases use of fixed-price development contracts and OTA agreements.

**5. China Sanctions (LOW-MODERATE).** China has sanctioned Raytheon over Taiwan arms sales and expanded restrictions to a Collins joint venture. Continued Taiwan defense sales create ongoing retaliatory risk for commercial businesses in the Chinese market.

**6. Tariff Exposure (LOW-MODERATE).** RTX cut its 2025 profit outlook due to steel/aluminum tariffs. Fixed-price defense contracts cannot easily pass through cost increases, though commercial contracts have more flexibility.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($230–242; 31–38% upside)
- FY2026 adjusted EPS comes in at the high end of guidance ($6.90) or above, driven by continued margin expansion across all three segments
- P&W Powder Metal costs normalize below remaining accrual, GTF Advantage ramp accelerates new engine deliveries
- Airbus arbitration settles for modest damages ($200–500M) that don't impair the relationship or future order flow
- Defense backlog converts at accelerating rates as Golden Dome and munitions resiliency programs enter procurement phase
- Multiple re-rates to 30x+ as the market prices RTX as a growth compounder rather than a legacy defense prime (comparable to GE Aerospace at 35x)

### Bear Case ($145–155; 12–18% downside)
- Airbus arbitration results in >$1B damages plus structural shift of future engine orders toward CFM
- P&W engine delivery delays persist through 2027, compressing production ramp and aftermarket growth
- Fixed-price contract losses reverse their improving trend; DOJ compliance monitors flag additional issues
- Defense spending growth decelerates as fiscal pressure mounts
- Multiple compresses to 21–22x (in line with GD/LMT) as growth premium evaporates: 22x × $6.90 = $152

## Conclusion

RTX Corporation offers a differentiated growth profile within aerospace and defense, underpinned by proven margin expansion and structural tailwinds from both commercial aftermarket recovery and elevated global defense spending. The 10% post-earnings decline despite a 16% EPS beat creates an entry point below consensus fair value.

The stock carries real risks: the Airbus arbitration is the most material near-term uncertainty, and the triple compliance monitor overlay constrains management flexibility. These risks justify maintaining position sizing at 2–3% rather than full conviction. At 25.5x FY2026E adjusted EPS, the valuation is above defense peer medians, reflecting a growth premium that must be earned through continued execution.

The overall score of 6.37 sits in a range where fundamental quality (strong cash flows, competitive moats, secular growth) competes with valuation fullness and specific identifiable risks. The balance tips toward BUY because: (1) the post-earnings selloff creates a specific, measurable dislocation from consensus targets, (2) the four-quarter beat streak provides confidence that consensus estimates are conservative, and (3) the dual-theater defense environment and commercial aftermarket cycle create a multi-year growth runway that supports the current multiple. Accumulate in the $170–176 zone with a 12-month base case of $210.
