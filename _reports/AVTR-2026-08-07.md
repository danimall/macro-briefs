## Executive Summary

Avantor's July 29 second-quarter report was the first result in two years that materially changed the operating picture. Organic revenue declined 0.4% in Q2 against 2.2% for the first half, implying a roughly 4% organic decline in Q1 and a genuine sequential improvement. The VWR Distribution & Services segment returned to positive organic growth of 1.7%, adjusted EPS of $0.21 beat the $0.19 consensus, and management raised both organic growth and adjusted EPS guidance for FY2026 to $0.80–$0.83. The stock rose 17% on the print and now sits 81% above its March low of $7.27.

The improvement is real but narrow. Adjusted EBITDA continues to contract: $1,198.8M in FY2024, $1,069.4M in FY2025, and approximately $993.8M on a trailing twelve-month basis through June 30, 2026. Q2 revenue growth came from controlled-environment consumables and specialty procurement (pass-through volume tied to customer procurement services) while core lab consumables declined and the higher-margin Bioscience & Medtech Products segment fell 5.6% organically. Proprietary product revenue declined 1.5% while third-party revenue grew 2.8%, taking proprietary share of sales from 53.1% to 52.0%. Both segments' adjusted operating margins compressed year over year (VWR Distribution & Services to 10.2% from 11.7%; Bioscience & Medtech Products to 26.0% from 27.6%). Revenue stabilization is not yet converting into EBITDA stabilization, and the mechanism matters: a distributor that grows by adding third-party and procurement pass-through volume grows gross profit dollars more slowly than sales.

At $13.18 the shares trade 16.2x the guided FY2026 adjusted EPS midpoint of $0.815, 15.0x the $0.88 next-year consensus, and 12.4x trailing EV/Adjusted EBITDA on an enterprise value of $12.3B. FY2025 free cash flow of $496.4M is a 5.6% yield on market capitalization. That is not expensive for a business with more than 85% recurring revenue and no customer above 5% of sales, but it is no longer distressed pricing either. With 3.43x net leverage, a VWR Distribution goodwill cushion of roughly 5.5% at March 31, an interim CFO, and active securities litigation contesting the two items central to the thesis (competitive position and goodwill), the equity is a levered claim on EBITDA stabilization that the market has already partially rewarded. Base case $14.00, bull $17.50, bear $9.50 — a probability-weighted expected return of roughly +4%. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Avantor operates as both distributor and manufacturer of laboratory and bioproduction consumables, chemicals, equipment and services. The distribution channel, inherited from the 2017 VWR acquisition, reaches more than 300,000 customer locations in approximately 180 countries, carries millions of third-party SKUs, runs a private-label program spanning more than 5,000 suppliers, and embeds roughly 2,000 associates inside customer sites performing procurement, stockroom management, media prep and cleanroom control. Roughly 80% of transactions originate digitally.

The proprietary side comprises J.T. Baker high-purity bioprocessing chemicals, Masterflex peristaltic pumps and tubing, and NuSil implant- and aerospace-grade silicone. These carry higher margins and higher qualification barriers, which is why Bioscience & Medtech Products' organic decline is the more consequential of the two segment datapoints.

Three structural disclosures support the durability argument: more than 85% of net sales are recurring, no single end customer exceeds 5% of net sales, and approximately 45% of FY2025 sales came from customers with relationships of 15 years or more. Customer concentration risk here is genuinely low.

The offsetting structural fact is balance-sheet composition inherited from the New Mountain Capital era and the NuSil/VWR roll-up. At June 30, 2026, goodwill of $4,935.4M plus other intangibles of $3,014.2M totaled $7,949.6M, or approximately 69% of total assets of $11,595.9M. Tangible book equity is negative $2,319.4M. Purchase-accounting amortization runs roughly $300M annually, which is why guided adjusted EPS of $0.815 sits against first-half GAAP EPS of $0.12.

Segment definitions have changed twice in three years — three segments to two effective January 2024 (Laboratory Solutions and Bioscience Production), then to Bioscience & Medtech Products and VWR Distribution & Services effective January 2026, with goodwill reporting units simultaneously redrawn. Multi-year trend analysis at the segment level is compromised as a result.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Four consecutive annual declines: $7,512.4M (FY2022), $6,967.2M (FY2023), $6,783.6M (FY2024), $6,552.2M (FY2025) — a cumulative $960.2M, or 12.8%. FY2025's 3.4% decline decomposed as +1.6% FX, −2.2% Clinical Services divestiture, and −2.8% organic. First-half 2026 net sales of $3,273.7M were up 0.3% reported but down 2.2% organic, with $80.0M (2.5 points) of FX tailwind masking the underlying trend. Q2 was +0.5% reported, −0.4% organic.

**Margins.** Gross margin has fallen from 34.6% in FY2022 to 33.6% (FY2024), 32.7% (FY2025) and 31.7% in both the first half and Q2 of 2026 — approximately 295 basis points of cumulative erosion. Adjusted EBITDA margin went 17.7% (FY2024) to 16.3% (FY2025) to 14.5% in H1 2026, with Q2 at 15.0%.

**The central financial observation.** Avantor has been running a cost program targeting approximately $300M of run-rate gross savings by end-2026, expanded to approximately $400M by end-2027 per the FY2025 10-K, and Adjusted EBITDA has nonetheless fallen roughly $205M (17%) from FY2024 to the trailing twelve months. Savings have been fully absorbed by gross-margin erosion, volume decline and inflation. The Q2 10-Q describes the prior cost initiative as "former… concluded in 2025" with zero transformation expense in the quarter (versus $20.4M a year earlier), while press coverage credits a newly named "Revival" program and a Chief Transformation Officer role created in April 2026. Whether the $400M savings target survives the leadership change cannot be resolved from filings, and it is decision-relevant against a $994M EBITDA base. Restructuring and severance charges are re-accelerating: $29.8M in FY2025 versus $39.1M in the first half of 2026 alone (up 52% from $25.8M in H1 2025).

**The FY2025 loss.** Reported operating loss of $246.2M and net loss of $530.2M reflect a $785.0M non-cash, non-deductible goodwill impairment of the Distribution reporting unit taken in Q3 2025, the absence of the prior year's $446.6M Clinical Services gain, and $139.9M of lower gross profit. Excluding the impairment, FY2025 operating margin was +8.2%. The $88.9M of tax expense recorded on a $441.3M pre-tax loss is explained by the impairment carrying no tax shield.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow fell from $840.8M (FY2024) to $623.8M (FY2025); free cash flow from $768.3M to $496.4M. First-half 2026 FCF was $168.0M versus $207.5M a year earlier. Q2 FCF was reported at $143M, implying roughly $25M in Q1 — the business is heavily back-half and working-capital-timing weighted, and the quarter should not be annualized. FY2025 also benefited from approximately $43.0M of reduced current cash tax obligations under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, a partly non-recurring tailwind. Capital intensity is low: FY2025 capex of $128.8M was 2.0% of sales, and the $71.1M of first-half capex broadly covers the roughly $59.0M depreciation component of $210.6M total D&A, so the D&A/capex gap reflects purchase accounting rather than underinvestment.

**Capital structure.** Gross debt of $3,715.4M at June 30 against $306.8M cash gives net debt of $3,408.6M and net leverage of 3.43x on LTM Adjusted EBITDA. Liquidity is $1,686.6M including a $1,379.8M undrawn revolver maturing October 2030. Required term-loan amortization over the next twelve months is $30.8M. The company is covenant-compliant, and debt marked at 98.8% of par at quarter-end — credit markets are not pricing distress. The July 14 repricing of the €427.1M Term Loan B-6 into a B-7 tranche at EURIBOR+200 from +250 saves roughly $2.1M annually and confirms lender willingness to tighten. Management targets adjusted net leverage below 3x on a credit-agreement-defined basis that may include pro forma savings addbacks and is not interchangeable with the 3.43x calculation above.

Capital allocation pivoted decisively. Of a $500M authorization approved in October 2025, $75.0M was repurchased in November at approximately $11.47 per share, and nothing in Q1 or Q2 2026. First-half debt repayments totaled $217.5M including a $102.5M voluntary Euro Term Loan B-6 prepayment, against $38.1M in the prior-year half. This is prudent given the live goodwill triggering event, though the company declined to buy stock at $8 while four of its own directors were purchasing.

## Growth Analysis

The forward case rests on three assertions: the life sciences tools end market is recovering, Avantor's organic decline is nearly over, and cost actions plus deleveraging convert flat revenue into EPS growth.

Evidence for the first is external and directional. Thermo Fisher guided FY2026 revenue of $46.3–47.2B with 6–8% EPS growth, and sell-side commentary describes the sector as improving. That guidance is not a clean organic comparison because it incorporates acquisitions, so it establishes direction rather than a precise delta. Avantor's own filings show tariff language softening between the 10-K and the 10-Q.

Evidence for the second is the Q2 sequence itself: organic decline narrowing from roughly −4% to −0.4%, VWR Distribution & Services at +1.7% organic, and management raising organic growth guidance. Consensus estimates corroborate at the margin — current-year EPS revised +2.9% over 30 days and +2.8% over 90, next-year +3.6% and +3.4%. Those are positive but ordinary revisions, an order of magnitude below the +30% velocity that signals a genuine earnings acceleration. Two beats, one in-line and one miss over four quarters is a middling delivery record.

Evidence against the third is the composition of the growth. Bioscience & Medtech Products, which carries a 26% adjusted operating margin, declined 5.6% organically on lower Fluid Handling and NuSil volumes. VWR Distribution & Services, at a 10.2% margin, grew 1.7% organically but its adjusted operating income fell 10.7% (11.7% currency-adjusted). Proprietary revenue declined while third-party grew. If the mix shift persists, each incremental revenue dollar carries less gross profit than the historical average, and revenue stabilization at 2024 mix-adjusted margins is not the same outcome as revenue stabilization at current mix.

The FY2025 10-K attributes the Laboratory Solutions organic decline to "the uncertainty around funding and increased competitive intensity." That is management naming a company-specific share-loss component alongside a market one. Against a sector peers describe as improving, the more likely reading is that a meaningful portion of Avantor's underperformance is competitive. The alternative — that Avantor's academic/government-heavy exposure plus Fluid Handling and NuSil simply lag a broader recovery — is plausible and is what raised guidance implies. One quarter does not settle it.

Growth Prospects: 4.5, up from 4.0. The direction of travel improved; the quality of the growth did not.

Peer comparison is close to useless on P/E. The median of the supplied comparables is 46.9x, but the set includes two negative multiples (CRL at −50.0x, AZTA at −9.3x) and two premium names with different business models (TECH 77.8x, BIO 46.9x). MEDP at 29.9x is a CRO. None is a hybrid distributor-manufacturer with 3.4x leverage and a four-year revenue decline. The more relevant benchmarks are Avantor's own cash economics and its EV/EBITDA relative to distribution multiples generally — 12.4x for a business with contracting EBITDA is a full multiple, not a discounted one.

The GAAP-versus-adjusted gap must be held constant in any comparison. Guided adjusted EPS of $0.815 implies roughly $550M of adjusted net income on 677M shares, against first-half GAAP EPS of $0.12. The bridge is dominated by approximately $300M annually of acquired-intangible amortization, permanent for years because it stems from the 2016–2017 roll-up. Free cash flow is the cleaner anchor, and at 5.6% on market capitalization it provides support without being compelling, particularly since FCF is declining and FY2025 included roughly $43M of one-time tax relief. A further GAAP distortion arrives in Q3: a post-quarter internal reorganization is expected to generate a roughly $142.0M deferred tax benefit, adding about $0.21 to GAAP EPS as a non-cash item, with adjusted-EPS treatment undisclosed.

**Scenario construction.**

- **Bear $9.50 (−28%):** organic decline resumes, mix continues to dilute, Adjusted EBITDA drifts to roughly $900M. At 10.8x EV/EBITDA less $3.4B net debt, equity is approximately $6.4B, or $9.50 per share. A VWR Distribution goodwill charge accompanies this path — non-cash, non-deductible, covenant-neutral, FCF-neutral, but a signal that forecast cash flows in half the business have been reduced.
- **Base $14.00 (+6.2%):** organic revenue flattens through 2027, Adjusted EBITDA holds near $1,000M, net debt falls toward $3.25B on continued paydown. 12.5x EV/EBITDA gives equity of roughly $9.25B, or $13.66; crediting a further year of deleveraging supports $14.00. This is consistent with 15.9x the $0.88 FY2027 consensus and sits between the analyst median of $14.20 and the current price.
- **Bull $17.50 (+32.8%):** core lab consumables recover, Bioscience & Medtech Products returns to growth, mix normalizes and Revival savings reach EBITDA. Adjusted EBITDA of $1,100M at 13.5x, net debt at $3.0B, gives $11.85B of equity, or $17.50. That is 19.9x FY2027 consensus, achievable only if the market re-rates on evidence of durable organic growth.

Weighting base 50%, bull 25%, bear 25% gives an expected return of approximately +4%. Analyst targets bracket this: mean $14.56 (+10.5%), median $14.20 (+7.7%), high $19.00 (+44%), low $12.00 (−9.0%). The consensus mix has improved — two sell ratings went to zero over the past month, leaving three buys, twelve holds and one strong sell.

Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation argument, and the 81% move from the low was driven by genuine fundamental improvement. That improvement is now in the price: at 12.4x EV/EBITDA and 16.2x forward adjusted earnings, the market is paying a stabilization multiple for a business that has not yet demonstrated stabilization in EBITDA dollars. Valuation: 4.8.

## Competitive Landscape

Avantor's moat is commercial rather than technological. The 10-K states that most of its intellectual property is "know-how and asset configurations that we treat as trade secrets," and that no individual patent or license is material apart from the Avantor, VWR, J.T. Baker, NuSil and Masterflex trademarks. The defenses are channel breadth (300,000+ locations, ~180 countries), 5,000+ private-label supplier relationships many exceeding twenty years, roughly 2,000 associates embedded in customer facilities, spec-in position in regulated cGMP platforms, and 80% digital transaction penetration. Embedded on-site services and qualification requirements are genuine switching costs.

The threats named in the company's own risk factors are specific and structurally credible for a distributor: group purchasing organizations and online auction sites that "increase competition for, and reduce spending on, laboratory products"; biopharma consolidation producing "fewer customer accounts… resulting in increased pricing pressures"; new entrants in low-cost manufacturing locations; and customers "purchasing the products that we supply directly from our suppliers." That last item is direct disintermediation of the VWR Distribution channel, the same reporting unit now carrying approximately $2.8B of goodwill with a 5.5% valuation cushion.

The trajectory evidence is mixed and net negative. Management has admitted competitive intensity as a cause of decline. Proprietary revenue, where the moat is strongest, contracted 1.5% in Q2 while third-party grew 2.8%. Bioscience & Medtech Products declined 5.6% organically. The one positive datapoint, VWR Distribution & Services organic growth of +1.7%, came with a 154 basis point margin decline in the same segment. A securities class action, with a motion to dismiss filed July 9, alleges misstatements specifically about "Avantor's competitive position, goodwill, and various aspects of the Company's business." Those are allegations, not findings, but they contest precisely the two items on which this thesis turns.

Competitive Position: 3.8, essentially unchanged from the prior 3.5 with a modest upward adjustment for the VWR Distribution volume inflection.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill impairment at VWR Distribution.** The most concrete quantified risk, and one the company elevated itself with a new standalone risk factor in the Q2 10-Q that was not present in the FY2025 10-K. At March 31, 2026, VWR Distribution's fair value exceeded carrying value by only approximately 5.5%, with roughly $2,800M of allocated goodwill; the filing states that "a modest increase in discount rates or a reduction in forecasted cash flows could eliminate this excess." No triggering event was identified in Q2, and the share-price recovery from roughly $8 to $13 mechanically relieved near-term pressure — the fair value estimate is anchored partly to market capitalization, which creates a reflexive loop in both directions. An impairment would be non-cash, non-deductible, covenant-neutral and FCF-neutral; its significance is as a signal about forecast cash flows in half the business, and as a further reduction to already-negative tangible book. Because the unit's total carrying value is undisclosed, dollar sizing of any charge is illustrative rather than calculable.

**Leverage on a declining base.** At $3,408.6M net debt, each $100M of Adjusted EBITDA lost adds roughly 0.35x of leverage. There is no covenant problem today, near-term amortization is $30.8M, and liquidity is $1.69B. But the maturity schedule of the three unsecured note tranches — $800M 3.875% USD, €400M 3.875% Euro, $1,550M 4.625% USD, together 76% of gross debt — is not disclosed in the reviewed filings, so the maturity wall cannot be assessed. Refinancing $2.8B of coupons struck at 3.875%–4.625% at higher prevailing rates would raise interest expense materially. Cash interest paid was $80.9M in the first half; the reported net interest expense of $39.4M in Q2 is net of $10.3M of interest income primarily from cross-currency swaps and understates gross debt service.

**Governance and continuity.** Approximately thirteen Item 5.02 8-K filings between January 2025 and April 2026. The CEO was replaced in 2025 with $6.7M of disclosed severance and transition costs. CFO R. Brent Jones gave notice on March 26, 2026, and the Q2 10-Q was signed by the Chief Accounting Officer as Interim CFO on July 29 — the seat remains unfilled. A COO role was created and filled in November 2025 and the Bioscience & Medtech Products head replaced in April 2026. Cumulative TSR in the proxy stands at $40.71 against a peer group at $148.36. Executing a turnaround without a permanent CFO while defending securities and derivative litigation is a real execution risk.

**Tax volatility.** The Q2 effective tax rate spiked to 53.1% from 20.8% on a change in estimate of uncertain tax positions, against a $106.9M reserve at year-end 2025 and a $190.1M valuation allowance. The Q3 deferred tax benefit runs the other way. Reported GAAP earnings will remain noisy in both directions.

**End-market policy.** NIH-dependent academic and government customers face unpredictable appropriations, and the Q2 forward-looking factors explicitly cite U.S. government shutdown impacts. Tariff pressure has eased per the company's own language.

Risk Factors: 4.2. Material, quantified, partly priced.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 45.6% sits below one-year realized volatility of 53.2%, a −7.6 point spread, and has collapsed 21.1 points from 66.7% since July 19 as the earnings event resolved. Term structure is in contango (45.6% at one month, 49.8% at six months), which reads as near-term complacency with the market pricing more uncertainty further out — consistent with a Q3 print and a goodwill-test cycle as the next decision points. Put/call skew is mildly elevated at 3.2% on the front tenor but turns to −1.1% at six months, and open interest is overwhelmingly call-weighted at a 0.01 put/call ratio. The nearest expiry prices a ±7.9% move; the six-month tenor prices ±20.6%.

Short interest of 10.1% of float with 6.6 days to cover is elevated and represents genuine institutional bearishness. Combined with call-heavy open interest, positioning is two-sided: bears are short the equity while options participants hold upside exposure. Cheap near-term volatility makes protective structures inexpensive for holders, and the contango argues against paying up for long-dated optionality. The options data is consistent with a HOLD and suggests the market has stopped pricing an imminent binary.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($17.50, +33%).** Q2 marks the organic trough. Life sciences end markets recover through 2027, VWR Distribution & Services holds its growth as academic and government budgets normalize, and Fluid Handling and NuSil recover from destocking. Revival savings reach EBITDA rather than being absorbed by mix, taking Adjusted EBITDA back toward $1,100M. Deleveraging to below 3x on the credit-agreement basis reduces the equity risk premium, and the $425M remaining repurchase authorization is reactivated. Four directors bought 310,000 shares for approximately $2.69M at a weighted average of $8.66 between February and May, including Gregory Lucier, a former Life Technologies CEO — informed industry buyers taking cash risk at the low. Management's new option strike near $10.69 aligns their payoff with a re-rating. At 13.5x EV/EBITDA on $1,100M and $3.0B net debt, the equity is worth $17.50.

**Bear case ($9.50, −28%).** The Q2 inflection was mix-driven and temporary. Third-party and procurement pass-through volume grows while proprietary revenue and core lab consumables continue to decline, so gross profit dollars keep falling even as reported revenue flattens. Group purchasing organizations, biopharma consolidation and direct supplier purchasing structurally compress the VWR Distribution channel, validating management's own admission of "increased competitive intensity." Adjusted EBITDA drifts toward $900M, taking net leverage above 3.7x. A VWR Distribution impairment of several hundred million dollars follows, driving tangible book further negative and confirming reduced cash-flow forecasts. Refinancing $2.8B of unsecured notes at higher market rates raises interest expense. At 10.8x on $900M less $3.4B net debt, the equity is worth $9.50.

**Base case ($14.00, +6%).** Organic revenue flattens near zero through 2027 without meaningful reacceleration. Cost actions offset mix dilution roughly one-for-one, holding Adjusted EBITDA near $1,000M. Deleveraging continues at $200–250M annually. No impairment, because the share price holds above the level that triggers interim tests. Adjusted EPS reaches the $0.88 consensus in FY2027 and the multiple stays near 16x. Fair value $14.00.

The rating trajectory here has moved 3.8 → 3.6 → 4.3 → 4.4 → 4.45, with AVOID twice and now a third HOLD. The Q2 print validated the earlier upgrade: the improvement thesis that justified moving off AVOID in June has been confirmed in reported organic revenue, guidance and estimate revisions. What changed alongside it is the price. In June the shares were roughly $9.50 with the same forward earnings power; the value gap has been consumed by an 81% rally. The rating is unchanged for a different reason than before: previously cheap with unresolved risk, now fairly priced with improving but still unresolved risk.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (expected late October 2026), which will disclose whether an interim goodwill test was triggered, whether VWR Distribution & Services organic growth persisted, and whether Bioscience & Medtech Products' decline narrowed. Secondary checkpoint: appointment of a permanent CFO.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Adjusted EBITDA dollars grow year over year in a reported quarter — the single cleanest confirmation that cost savings are reaching the P&L rather than being absorbed by mix.
2. Bioscience & Medtech Products organic revenue returns to positive, or proprietary product revenue grows faster than third-party for two consecutive quarters, reversing the mix dilution.
3. Filing-level confirmation that the Revival program carries forward a quantified run-rate savings target of $300M or more, together with disclosure of the unsecured note maturity schedule showing no maturity before 2029.
4. A price below approximately $11.00 on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore roughly 27% upside to base case and re-establish margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. A goodwill impairment at VWR Distribution, or disclosure of a further narrowing of the fair-value cushion below the 5.5% level reported at March 31 — either would confirm reduced internal cash-flow forecasts for roughly half the business.
2. Organic revenue decline re-widening beyond −2% in any quarter, or a reduction in FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance below $0.80.
3. Net leverage rising above 3.75x on the calculated basis, or a note refinancing priced materially above the existing 3.875%–4.625% coupons.
4. Denial of the securities-litigation motion to dismiss combined with a first loss accrual, which would move the matter from allegation to quantified liability.

**What would not change the view:** a non-cash tax benefit in Q3, FX-driven reported revenue improvement without organic follow-through, or further director purchases absent officer buying.

## Conclusion

Avantor has stopped getting worse. Organic revenue decline narrowed to 0.4% in Q2 from roughly 4% in Q1, VWR Distribution & Services returned to growth, adjusted EPS beat consensus, guidance was raised, consensus estimates have been revised up 2.8–3.6%, two sell ratings were withdrawn, and four directors put approximately $2.69M of personal capital into the stock at an average of $8.66. The balance sheet has room: $1.69B of liquidity, $30.8M of required amortization over twelve months, covenant compliance, debt at 98.8% of par, and a July repricing 50 basis points tighter.

What has not yet happened is EBITDA stabilization. Trailing Adjusted EBITDA of $993.8M is $205M below FY2024 despite a large cost program, gross margin has eroded 295 basis points since FY2022, both segments' operating margins compressed year over year, and the revenue that returned came at lower margin than the revenue that left. Net leverage of 3.43x on a declining base leaves limited room for error, the VWR Distribution goodwill cushion of 5.5% remains thin, the CFO seat is vacant nine months into a turnaround, and the status of the $400M savings target is unresolved in the filings.

At $13.18 the shares offer roughly 6% upside to a $14.00 base case, 33% to a $17.50 bull case, and 28% downside to a $9.50 bear case — a probability-weighted expected return near +4% against 45–50% implied volatility. That is fair value, not asymmetry. Existing holders have adequate reason to stay through the Q3 print, which is the next event capable of confirming or breaking the EBITDA-stabilization case. New capital should wait for either evidence that Adjusted EBITDA dollars are growing or a price near $11.00. **HOLD.**
