## Executive Summary

S&P Global has declined 26% from its 52-week high of $579, now trading at ~$426, compressing from 30x to 21.5x forward earnings. The primary driver appears to be market anxiety over AI disruption of the Market Intelligence segment, which accounts for roughly 32% of revenue but only ~20% operating margins. The market appears to be applying a blanket disruption discount across the entire company, ignoring that the highest-margin segments (Ratings at 64% margins, Indices at 69%) are protected by regulatory recognition requirements, network effects, and $5.5T in benchmarked ETF assets that cannot practically migrate to AI alternatives.

The financial profile remains exceptional: $5.1B in free cash flow on $15.3B revenue, 79% recurring/predictable revenue, and a capital-light model enabling $15.1B in shareholder returns over three years ($11.6B buybacks + $3.5B dividends). The CEO purchased nearly $1M in stock and the Indices segment CEO bought $1.08M, both within days of the Q1 earnings release at prices near current levels. Management is putting personal capital against the AI narrative precisely when fear is most acute.

The Mobility spin-off (expected mid-2026) will simplify the business and create a cleaner capital/commodity markets data company. At 21.5x guided EPS of $19.40-$19.65 versus a historical range of approximately 25-30x, the stock prices in substantial disruption that would need to materialize across multiple independently-moated business lines to justify the current discount.

## Company Overview

S&P Global operates five segments serving capital markets, energy/commodity markets, and automotive markets:

- **Market Intelligence** ($4.9B revenue, 20% operating margin): Capital IQ Pro platform, data analytics, workflow solutions. Most exposed to AI disruption competition.
- **Ratings** ($4.7B, 64% margin): Credit ratings with duopoly market position alongside Moody's. NRSRO regulatory designation creates structural barriers.
- **Energy** ($2.3B, 41% margin): Platts commodity price assessments, CERAWeek conferences. Physical commodity price reporting requires human networks.
- **Indices** ($1.85B, 69% margin): S&P 500 and related indices. $5.48T in ETF AUM benchmarked to S&P DJI products (+25% YoY).
- **Mobility** ($1.75B, 22% margin): Automotive data. Scheduled for tax-free spin-off mid-2026.

Revenue quality is high: 51% subscription, 13% non-transaction recurring, 8% asset-linked = approximately 79% recurring or highly predictable.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term organic growth is guided at 6-8% for FY2026, with EPS growing faster (7-8% on adjusted basis) due to margin expansion and buybacks. The growth drivers by segment:

- **Indices (+17% Q1):** Asset-linked fees grow with ETF AUM, which benefits from both market appreciation and net inflows. The structural shift from active to passive investing remains early-innings globally.
- **Ratings (+13% Q1):** Transaction revenue tied to bond issuance volumes. High-yield spreads were tight through early 2026, supporting issuance. However, management guides only 4% Ratings growth for the full year (tough comps from 2025's $4.3T billed issuance).
- **Market Intelligence:** Growth driven by price increases and cross-sell of analytics tools. AI investment (ProntoNLP, GenAI in Capital IQ Pro) positions for retention.
- **Energy:** Reported revenue growth guidance was reduced by approximately 30 basis points, primarily due to lower expected tailwinds from FX, while organic constant-currency revenue guidance remained unchanged at 6-8%. Platts price assessments and commodity derivatives licensing remain solid structural growers.

Consensus projects $22.19 in EPS for FY2027 (13% growth over FY2026 guidance midpoint). Estimate revisions have been modestly negative — current quarter down 0.5% over 30 days and 1.2% over 90 days. This reflects the general headwind from macro uncertainty rather than fundamental deterioration.

Medium-term growth catalysts: (1) Mobility spin-off releasing a cleaner margin profile, (2) private markets data expansion via With Intelligence acquisition ($1.8B), (3) AI product monetization within Capital IQ Pro, (4) continued passive investing penetration driving Indices fee growth.

## Competitive Landscape

**Ratings (duopoly, improving):** S&P and Moody's together command ~80% global market share. The NRSRO regulatory designation, Basel III risk-weighting requirements referencing external ratings, and legal liability framework (rated entities bear liability) create barriers no AI model can replicate. Private credit growth is a modest headwind (some loans remain unrated), but SPGI is pursuing private market rating opportunities as these markets mature and require independent assessment.

**Indices (monopoly-like, stable-to-improving):** The S&P 500 index has $5.48T in benchmarked ETF AUM. Network effects are insurmountable — the index is embedded in trillions of dollars of derivatives contracts, pension mandates, and investment policies. Licensing fees grow with AUM and new product creation. Competitors cannot replicate this installed base.

**Energy (dominant, stable):** Platts price assessments are contractually referenced in physical commodity trading and exchange-traded derivatives. Replacement would require coordinating thousands of counterparties simultaneously.

**Market Intelligence (competitive, deteriorating trajectory):** Bloomberg, FactSet, and LSEG/Refinitiv compete directly. AI models potentially lower barriers to data aggregation and analysis. However, Capital IQ's deep data lineage, compliance workflows, and integration with Ratings/Indices create cross-sell advantages that standalone AI tools lack. The $1.8B With Intelligence acquisition adds private markets coverage — a rapidly growing segment where data scarcity creates value.

The competitive position is stable-to-improving across 68% of revenue (Ratings, Indices, Energy) and under modest pressure in 32% (Market Intelligence). The blended trajectory favors SPGI given the margin-weighting — the three most insulated segments generate approximately 79-81% of operating income.

## Risk Assessment

**1. AI Disruption of Market Intelligence (Medium-High Probability, Medium Impact):** The 10-K explicitly acknowledges that competitors could use AI to make data collection "relatively inexpensive or free." The bull case is that workflow integration, compliance requirements, and switching costs protect revenue for 3-5+ years, giving SPGI time to embed AI capabilities into its own offerings. The bear case is that disruption hits faster than expected and Market Intelligence faces 15-20% revenue erosion within 3 years.

**2. Bond Issuance Cyclicality (Medium Probability, Medium Impact):** Ratings transaction revenue (52% of Ratings, ~16% of total revenue) depends on capital markets activity. Widening spreads given current macro conditions could suppress issuance in H2 2026. Management's conservative 4% Ratings growth guidance for FY2026 partially buffers this.

**3. Goodwill Impairment Risk (Low Probability, High Impact):** $52.7B in goodwill/intangibles. If any segment (particularly Market Intelligence) faces sustained revenue decline, impairment charges could be material. The annual qualitative assessment passed in 2025.

**4. Mobility Spin-Off Execution (Medium Probability, Low-Medium Impact):** Tax-free separation requires Form 10, regulatory approvals, Board final approval. Delay or cancellation would disappoint investors expecting re-rating.

**5. Macro Sensitivity:** Elevated vol environment and potential credit stress could suppress Ratings transaction revenue. Conversely, vol benefits Indices through derivatives trading volumes and data demand.

## Conclusion

S&P Global represents a high-quality franchise trading at a discount to most premium peers, driven by AI disruption fears that disproportionately target one segment (~32% of revenue, ~15% of operating income). The Ratings and Indices businesses — which together generate roughly 61-63% of operating income — are protected by regulatory moats, network effects, and embedded benchmark status that AI cannot replicate within any reasonable investment horizon. Including Energy, the three most insulated segments account for approximately 79-81% of operating income.

The insider buying cluster (over $2M from CEO and Indices CEO at ~$430) provides a conviction signal from those with the most visibility into competitive dynamics. At 21.7x forward earnings with 7-8% EPS growth, 114% FCF conversion, and a catalyst calendar (Mobility spin-off, continued AI product launches, passive investing tailwinds), the stock offers asymmetric upside if the market's worst fears fail to materialize within 12-18 months.

BUY with 2-3% position sizing. The primary risk is credit cycle deterioration suppressing Ratings revenue, which current guidance already partially reflects. The AI concern is real for Market Intelligence but is being applied as a blanket discount across structurally insulated segments.
