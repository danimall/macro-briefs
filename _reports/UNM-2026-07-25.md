**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Unum Group is a scale leader in U.S. and U.K. workplace benefits (group disability, group life, and voluntary/supplemental products sold through employers) with a healthy, growing core franchise attached to a shrinking legacy long-term care (LTC) block that has depressed its valuation multiple for years. The investment case has always been a look-through story: reported GAAP earnings (net income down ~58% to $738.5M in FY2025) understate franchise health because the decline was driven by deliberate de-risking (the Fortitude Re reinsurance transactions) and reserve trueups on the legacy block, not by any erosion in the core.

The July 6, 2026 announcement of a second, larger Fortitude Re deal is the most important development in the story. Unum agreed to cede $3.8B of individual LTC statutory reserves (~$4.5B best-estimate, ~50,000 policies, 100% of remaining individual LTC previously reinsured to its Fairwind captive). Combined with the 2025 deal, cumulative LTC statutory reserve reduction reaches ~40%, cutting the retained block to ~$11B skewed toward simpler group LTC benefits. This directly shrinks the tail risk that the market has priced as a structural discount. Analysts reset fair value toward $95-100 following the deal, above the current $86.22 price.

This remains a HOLD rather than a BUY for these reasons: consensus EPS estimates are flat-to-slightly-negative over 90 days (next-year revised down 0.8% over 30 days), Unum's per-share growth is being manufactured as much by an ~8% annual share-count reduction as by operating profit growth (adjusted operating income rose only ~1% in Q1 2026 while adjusted operating EPS rose ~10%), the retained $11B LTC block still carries a 97.6% net premium ratio with almost no reserve cushion, and Unum UK underwriting deteriorated sharply (benefit ratio 72.9%, operating income −31% YoY). The de-risking is real and the valuation gap is genuine, but the earnings momentum and the tail-risk reduction are not yet decisive enough to justify a maximum-conviction call.

## Company Overview

Unum has been in the workplace-benefits business for over 175 years, marketing primarily through employers and brokers. Its product suite spans group and individual disability, group life & AD&D, voluntary/supplemental benefits (accident, critical illness, cancer), dental and vision, and leave/absence-management services. The company reports through five segments:

- **Unum US** (~66% of premium, Q1 2026 premium $1,840.5M) — the core franchise: group disability, group life & AD&D, and supplemental/voluntary lines.
- **Colonial Life** (~17%, $472.7M) — worksite accident/sickness/disability, life, and cancer/critical illness sold through independent agents.
- **Unum International** (~10%, $286.7M) — Unum UK and Unum Poland; premium growing fastest at +16% YoY.
- **Closed Block** — run-off legacy LTC and other discontinued lines; the strategic overhang.
- **Corporate** — holding-company items.

FY2025 revenue was ~$13.1B and the company paid $8.3B in benefits. Financial strength ratings are solid and stable across all four agencies (A / A / A2 / A for the main U.S. insurers; senior debt BBB/Baa2/bbb+). The defining feature of the investment story is the bifurcation between a healthy, growing core and a capital-intensive legacy LTC block being aggressively de-risked through reinsurance.

## Financial Analysis

**Reported earnings volatility masks a resilient core.** The FY2025 net income decline was driven almost entirely by non-operating legacy items — realized investment losses and cost of reinsurance from the first Fortitude Re LTC transaction, plus a large Q3 2025 LTC reserve assumption update that raised the LTC net premium ratio. Management's preferred metric, after-tax adjusted operating income (which excludes investment gains/losses, reserve assumption updates, and the Closed Block), was far more stable: adjusted operating EPS was $8.13 in 2025 versus $8.44 in 2024, roughly flat.

**Q1 2026 confirms core strength.** Net income was $232.0M ($1.41 diluted EPS) versus $189.1M ($1.06) a year earlier, up 23%, helped by a much smaller net investment loss ($4.0M after-tax versus $163.4M, which reflected the Fortitude Re securities transfer). After-tax adjusted operating income excluding the Closed Block was $352.5M ($2.14/share) versus $348.8M ($1.95/share) — modest income growth but +10% per-share growth from buybacks. Total revenue was $3,355.2M (+8.5%); premium income was $2,794.0M (+3.4%), growing across all three core segments.

**Segment trends (Q1 2026 vs Q1 2025):**
- Unum US adjusted operating income $337.9M (+2.7%), benefit ratio improving to 59.5%, sales up a standout 20.8%; group life benefit ratio improved sharply (61.8% vs 69.3%).
- Colonial Life $127.8M (+10.5%), benefit ratio improving to 46.0%.
- Unum International $30.9M (−20.2%), the soft spot: Unum UK operating income fell ~31% on an elevated 72.9% benefit ratio (higher LTD claim size/incidence), though UK sales rose 15%.
- Closed Block swung to a $145.3M adjusted operating loss (from +$8.0M), as the LTC net premium ratio rose to 97.6% (from 94.7%). This is precisely why management now excludes the Closed Block from its headline operating metric.

**Balance sheet and capital.** Total assets are $62.7B, equity $10.9B, and the RBC ratio is ~460%, well above action levels. Holding-company liquidity was $1.73B at 3/31/26. The net unrealized loss on the $33.3B fixed-maturity portfolio was $2.2B at 3/31/26 (up from $1.7B at YE2025) on higher Treasury rates and wider spreads, but the portfolio is 97.7% investment grade, only 2.9% below-investment-grade, with management intending to hold to recovery. Commercial mortgage loans ($2.07B) are conservatively underwritten (82.5% at LTV ≤65%, only 13.7% office, no delinquencies). This is an AOCI mark-to-market matter rather than a realized credit problem, though it will keep book value volatile with rates. Operating cash flow was steady at ~$341M in Q1 2026.

**Capital allocation is central to the thesis.** Q1 2026 buybacks totaled $402.4M (5.4M shares); a new $1.0B December 2025 authorization had $601M remaining at 3/31/26; the dividend was raised 9.5% to $0.46/quarter. Diluted share count fell from 178.9M to 164.4M year-over-year, an ~8% reduction. This is high-quality return funded by strong statutory earnings and freed-up Closed Block capital, but it means per-share growth currently outpaces underlying profit growth.

The financial-health picture is genuinely solid on the metrics that matter for an insurer — RBC ~460%, IG-heavy portfolio, ample holdco liquidity, matched durations — which is why the Altman Z-Score of ~0.6 that a naive screen would flag is meaningless here. I score financial health 6.5, unchanged from the prior report: strong capital and cash generation, tempered by the AOCI volatility and the earnings drag from LTC cohorts where net premiums exceed gross premiums ($49.1M pretax cost in Q1 2026 alone).

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is best split between the core franchise and the consolidated per-share figure. Core-franchise growth is healthy: premium income +3.4% across all three core segments, Unum US sales +20.8%, Colonial Life operating income +10.5%, and Unum International premium +16% YoY (led by UK sales +15%). The demand backdrop for workplace protection products remains favorable, with strong persistency across most lines suggesting share stability-to-gains.

The consolidated growth signal is more muted. Adjusted operating income rose only ~1% in Q1 2026; the ~10% per-share growth was driven by the ~8% share-count reduction. Consensus estimates confirm the modest operating trajectory: current-year EPS of $8.79 (revised up only 0.9% over 90 days), next-year $9.67 (revised down 0.8% over 30 days, down 0.2% over 90 days). This does not clear the +30% estimate-revision threshold that would flag an earnings inflection; the revision trend is flat-to-slightly-negative, which caps the growth score.

The Unum UK deterioration is the growth wildcard to monitor — if the elevated LTD incidence/claim size persists rather than normalizing, it will drag International, which had been the fastest-growing premium segment. I score growth prospects 5.5. The core is growing, but consolidated operating growth is low-single-digit, estimate momentum is flat, and per-share growth is heavily buyback-dependent.

## Valuation Assessment

At $86.22 the stock trades at ~9.8x current-year adjusted operating EPS ($8.79) and ~18.75x trailing GAAP EPS ($4.27), the trailing figure being distorted by the LTC/reinsurance charges and therefore not the right anchor. On the adjusted operating basis, Unum is inexpensive against its Life & Health peer set (median P/E 15.7x): Globe Life 11.4x, Primerica 11.7x, CNO 20.0x, Principal 15.7x. Even accounting for the fact that GL and PRI carry their own idiosyncratic discounts, Unum's ~10x forward operating multiple sits at the low end of the group.

The specific reason the market is discounting Unum has been the legacy LTC tail. The July 2026 Fortitude Re deal is the catalyst that partially removes that discount, given the retained book skewed to simpler group LTC. Analysts reset fair value toward $95-100 (Morningstar ~$100, Seeking Alpha ~$95) after the deal, above the current price. The analyst target range of $87 (low) to $123 (high), mean $102, median $101, all sits above spot, and on a sub-15x name with a genuine de-risking catalyst, consensus targets are more likely to be floors than ceilings.

The offsetting considerations keep this from a high valuation score. The 97.6% LTC net premium ratio leaves almost no cushion on the retained $11B block, so future reserve trueups remain a live risk that justifies part of the discount. Estimate revisions are not rising, so there is no earnings-momentum tailwind pulling the multiple higher. And the discount is partly structural rather than purely a mispricing — the market has legitimately priced the LTC tail as a recurring source of lumpy charges. I score valuation 6.0. There is a real gap between price and reset fair value with an identifiable catalyst, but this is a value-with-a-catalyst situation rather than a deeply asymmetric one, and I remain wary of cheapness that reflects genuine residual risk.

## Competitive Landscape

Unum competes in U.S. group and voluntary benefits against MetLife, The Hartford, Guardian, Lincoln, Sun Life, and Principal, plus worksite players like Aflac. Its edge is scale in disability, integrated leave/absence-management services, broker/HCM connectivity, and worksite distribution. Q1 2026 sales momentum and strong persistency point to share stability-to-gains in the core.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving in the core and challenged in one segment. Group disability and voluntary benefits show healthy demand and improving benefit ratios. Colonial Life's benefit ratio and income growth confirm worksite strength. The one clear negative is Unum UK, where the elevated benefit ratio is eroding profitability even as top-line grows — worth watching for whether it reflects a competitive/underwriting problem or a claims-cycle blip.

On the legacy side, Unum's back-to-back Fortitude Re deals put it at the forefront of reinsurance-driven LTC de-risking, a template peers with legacy blocks (Genworth, CNO) are watching. Management is executing a credible runoff strategy that peers have struggled to match. I score competitive position 6.0 — a scale leader with a stable-to-improving core, offset by the UK soft spot and the reality that workplace benefits is a mature, competitive market rather than a share-gaining growth arena.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant company-specific risk remains legacy LTC reserve adequacy. Even after the July deal, Unum retains ~$11B of LTC statutory reserves (~70% group LTC). LTC is sensitive to morbidity, mortality, claim-resolution rates, interest rates, and the pace of state-approved rate increases; reserve assumption updates produce large, lumpy charges (the Q3 2025 update being the recent example). With the LTC net premium ratio at 97.6% and cohorts already running net premiums above gross, there is minimal cushion before the next trueup hits earnings.

Secondary risks include reinsurance counterparty exposure to Fortitude Re (recoverables of $11.3B at 3/31/26, mitigated by collateral trusts, Fortitude's A rating from AM Best, and biometric retrocession to a highly rated global reinsurer); interest-rate sensitivity in both directions (falling rates raise reserves; rising rates deepen the AOCI unrealized loss); UK group LTD claims volatility; and PE-partnership NAV volatility reported on a one-quarter lag. Governance is clean — CEO comp ($19.4M) is tied to adjusted operating EPS, premium, sales, and relative TSR, with a 5-year TSR of 398.5 versus peer 201.0 showing strong pay-for-performance alignment.

My risk score has fallen from 7.0 to 5.0 over the past several reports as the LTC reserve trueups and AOCI marks surfaced. The July deal partially reverses that — cutting the block ~40% reduces the magnitude of any future trueup — but the 97.6% net premium ratio on the retained book means the risk is reduced, not eliminated. I score risk factors 5.5, a modest improvement from 5.0, reflecting the meaningful but incomplete de-risking.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 33.9% versus 1-year historical volatility of 25.1% — options are pricing elevated near-term uncertainty (spread +8.8pp, rich). The term structure is in backwardation (33.9% near-term vs 27.7% at six months), signaling the market expects an imminent event, consistent with the pending regulatory approval and close of the July reinsurance deal plus the upcoming quarterly print. IV has collapsed from 50.2% to 33.9% since mid-June as the deal news was digested, and the put/call skew is essentially balanced (0.1% near-term). The six-month tenor shows a −5.3% put skew, indicating modest longer-dated downside hedging but nothing extreme. Short interest is negligible at 2.5% of float (1.8 days to cover). The options picture is consistent with a de-risking event that has removed uncertainty rather than a market bracing for a fundamental shock, supporting the constructive-but-not-euphoric read.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($110-123).** The July 2026 Fortitude Re deal durably improves how the market values Unum. With the retained book skewed to simpler group LTC, the structural discount that held the stock at ~10x operating EPS narrows toward the peer median (15.7x). The core franchise keeps compounding — Unum US sales +20.8%, Colonial +10.5%, persistency strong — while ~8% annual buybacks and a rising dividend drive double-digit per-share growth. On ~$9.67 next-year operating EPS at 12-13x, the stock reaches $116-126, and analyst fair-value resets toward $100 prove to be floors as consensus catches up.

**Bear case ($68-75).** The retained $11B LTC block delivers another lumpy reserve trueup — the 97.6% net premium ratio leaves no cushion — reminding the market why the discount existed. Unum UK's 72.9% benefit ratio proves to be a persistent underwriting problem rather than a blip, dragging International. Rates stay higher-for-longer, deepening the AOCI loss and keeping book value volatile. Estimate revisions, already flat-to-negative, roll over, and the multiple re-compresses toward its lows. At ~9x a modestly reduced operating EPS, the stock returns to the low-$70s (the 52-week low is $68.28).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $98.** This applies ~10x to next-year adjusted operating EPS of ~$9.67, a partial re-rating from the current ~10x toward the peer median but not all the way, reflecting the residual LTC risk. This sits between the bear ($68-75) and bull ($110-123) cases and is consistent with the analyst mean target of $102 and the reset fair-value range of $95-100. Upside to base is ~14%; the ~2.3% dividend yield adds to total return.

**Bull case target: $116-123.** Peer-median multiple applied to rising operating EPS as the de-risking removes the structural discount.

**Bear case target: $68-75.** A ~13-21% decline from current, driven by a fresh LTC reserve trueup and/or persistent UK deterioration.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to the close of the July reinsurance deal (expected in 2026, pending regulatory approval), the next two quarterly prints, and any Q3 LTC reserve assumption update (the timing that produced the 2025 charge).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The July Fortitude Re deal closes on the guided terms (YE2026 holdco liquidity $1.5-2.0B, leverage ~25%, RBC 400-425%) with no negative reserve surprises, and next-year EPS estimates turn positive.
- Unum UK benefit ratio recovers below ~65%, confirming the Q1 spike was transitory.
- The stock pulls back toward the high-$70s while fundamentals hold, restoring clear asymmetry to reset fair value.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material LTC reserve assumption update on the retained $11B block that exceeds ~$150M pretax.
- Persistent UK deterioration or a broadening of claims pressure into Unum US group disability.
- A ratings action or RBC deterioration below ~400%, or a shift in capital-return posture that signals capital strain.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and the formal close of the reinsurance transaction, whichever comes first.

## Conclusion

Unum is a well-capitalized, cash-generative workplace-benefits leader executing a credible strategy to shrink the legacy LTC tail that has long suppressed its multiple. The July 2026 Fortitude Re deal is a genuine catalyst that moves the retained book toward simpler group LTC, and analysts have reset fair value to $95-100 against an $86 price. The core franchise is growing, capital return is aggressive and high-quality, and financial strength is solid (RBC ~460%).

Three factors hold this at HOLD rather than BUY: consensus operating EPS estimates are flat-to-slightly-negative rather than inflecting, per-share growth is heavily buyback-dependent while underlying operating profit grew only ~1% in Q1 2026, and the retained $11B LTC block still carries a 97.6% net premium ratio with essentially no cushion against the next trueup. My scoring declined from 6.6 to 5.9 over recent reports on surfacing LTC and AOCI risk and is partially arrested by the July deal, which is why risk and overall tick up modestly here. This is a constructive growth hold with a real re-rating path and downside protection from the dividend and buyback, but I want to see the deal close cleanly, the UK stabilize, and estimates turn before upgrading. Financials-sector selection carries a structural alpha headwind, and Unum's cheapness is partly earned by genuine residual risk rather than pure mispricing, so I hold rather than chase.
