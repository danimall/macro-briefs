## Executive Summary

Corpay is a $20B corporate payments company undergoing an aggressive strategic transformation from legacy fuel card provider to global cross-border/AP automation platform. The company spent ~$3.5B on corporate payments acquisitions in 2025 (Alpha Group at $2.4B, AvidXchange at $578M, Mastercard partnership at $300M) while divesting $700M of non-core vehicle assets. Corporate Payments now represents 36% of revenue (up from 31% a year ago) and is growing organically at 17%.

The stock trades at $303.68, representing ~11.7x FY2026 guided adjusted EPS of $26 and a 16% discount to its 52-week high of $362. Consensus analyst targets suggest an average in the $350-$385 range. The discount reflects: (1) $10B of debt from the acquisition spree, (2) investor uncertainty around near-term execution risk, and (3) macro uncertainty around cross-border volumes and FX sensitivity. However, FY2026 estimates have risen +5.4% over 90 days, the revenue growth profile has materially improved (16% total, 10% organic guided), and the upcoming Cross-Border Teach-In (May 13) provides a near-term catalyst to re-rate the stock.

The primary risk preventing higher conviction is the leverage profile: $10B in debt with credit facilities partially maturing in June 2027, interest expense consuming 8.9% of revenue, and a complex AvidXchange guarantee structure. In a rising-rate environment with potential credit stress, this balance sheet commands a discount. But at 11.7x forward earnings for a business growing EPS 22% with 57% EBITDA margins and $1.3B in annual FCF, the risk/reward skews positive.

## Company Overview

Corpay (formerly FleetCor Technologies, rebranded March 2024) provides payment and spend management solutions to businesses across 200+ countries. The company operates through three segments:

- **Vehicle Payments (47% of revenue, $2.14B):** Fuel cards, electronic toll (Sem Parar in Brazil with 7.5M tagholders), fleet management. Growing organically at 9%.
- **Corporate Payments (36%, $1.64B):** AP automation, virtual cards, cross-border FX. Growing organically at 17%. Spend volumes up 31% YoY.
- **Lodging Payments (10%, $470M):** Workforce travel/insurance displacement housing. Declining (room nights -6% YoY).

Geographic mix: U.S. 49%, Brazil 16%, U.K. 14%, Other 21%. The company has executed 100+ acquisitions since 2002 under founder-CEO Ron Clarke (age 70, $700M+ in stock ownership).

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 revenue grew 13.9% to $4.53B (10% organic), accelerating from 5.8% in FY2024. FY2026 guidance calls for $5.265B (+16%, 10% organic). The acceleration is driven by the Alpha Group acquisition contributing inorganically plus continued organic momentum in Corporate Payments.

Adjusted EBITDA margins held at 56.6% ($2.57B) despite integration costs. GAAP operating income grew to $1.99B (44% margin). Net income of $1.07B yields GAAP EPS of $15.03.

FY2025 operating cash flow was $1.50B (down from $1.94B due to acquisition-related working capital). FCF of $1.30B represents a 28.7% FCF margin. Management's FY2026 guidance assumptions indicate free cash flow will be directed toward debt reduction, which may limit near-term buyback capacity relative to FY2025's $783M in repurchases.

Total debt of ~$10B against $2.57B EBITDA yields ~3.0x net leverage (using $7.6B net debt). The credit facility includes Term Loan A and revolving facilities maturing June 2027, with the Term Loan B maturing April 2028; additionally, Corpay added a new $900M seven-year Term Loan B in connection with the Alpha acquisition, which extends part of the maturity profile. Interest expense of $404M (8.9% of revenue) is manageable but would increase materially if refinanced at higher rates. The current weighted average cost of debt is 5.2-6.0%.

$8.6B in cumulative share repurchases (outstanding shares have declined from ~85M to ~66M over five years). The $1.5B remaining authorization through December 2026 provides potential support, though management's near-term priority appears to be deleveraging. No dividend.

## Growth Analysis

Corpay's growth story rests on three pillars:

1. **Corporate Payments secular adoption (17% organic growth).** B2B payment digitization represents a $145T addressable market still majority paper-based. Corpay's proprietary virtual card acceptance network and the Alpha Group cross-border platform position it as one of the few scaled independent providers. The 31% spend volume growth validates adoption momentum.

2. **Vehicle Payments steady compounding (9% organic).** Sem Parar in Brazil (100% toll road coverage, 7.5M tagholders) is a leading scaled platform with strong network effects. The U.K./European fuel card operations benefit from fleet complexity (mixed ICE/EV fleets require more management, not less). This segment provides stable cash flow to fund the Corporate Payments transformation.

3. **M&A multiplication.** Alpha Group, acquired for approximately $2.4B enterprise value, adds significant cross-border revenue and is expected to be EPS-accretive in 2026. The integration thesis: cross-sell Alpha's corporate FX clients into Corpay's broader payment suite and vice versa.

**FY2026 guide of 22% EPS growth ($26 adjusted)** implies margin expansion as Alpha Group scales and integration costs fade. FY2027 consensus of $29.86 (+15%) suggests the Street expects sustained momentum.

**Hidden concern: Revenue yield compression.** Revenue per Corporate Payments spend dollar fell 11% YoY (0.71% → 0.63%). This suggests new enterprise clients negotiate lower take rates. If volume growth slows while yields continue compressing, revenue growth decelerates faster than volume growth implies.

## Valuation Assessment

At $303.68, CPAY trades at:
- 11.7x FY2026E adjusted EPS ($26.07 consensus)
- 10.2x FY2027E adjusted EPS ($29.86 consensus)
- 20.2x trailing GAAP P/E (on $15.03 reported EPS)
- ~10.8x EV/EBITDA on FY2025 ($2.57B)

Against peer medians of 22.1x P/E, CPAY appears significantly undervalued on forward metrics. The discount is attributable to (a) leverage concerns, (b) high absolute debt levels, and (c) execution risk on the portfolio transformation. Peer comparisons:

- **GPN (16x)** has lower growth (single-digit) and larger merchant exposure.
- **JKHY (22x)** is a bank tech provider with mid-single-digit growth.
- **Block (33x)** has higher growth volatility and lower margins.

CPAY growing EPS at 22% with 57% EBITDA margins trading at 11.7x forward earnings represents a PEG ratio of ~0.5x. Even assigning a 15x multiple (conservative for a 20%+ EPS grower) to $26 EPS yields $390 — 28% above current price.

## Competitive Landscape

Corpay's competitive position is improving through deliberate portfolio rotation:

- **Cross-border FX:** Post-Alpha acquisition, Corpay becomes one of the largest independent cross-border payment providers, competing with banks (who are slow/expensive) and fintechs (who lack breadth). The Mastercard partnership ($300M for 2.8% equity in the cross-border business) validates the platform.
- **AP automation:** The AvidXchange partnership positions Corpay in the SMB AP market without requiring full integration. This complements the existing enterprise AP solution.
- **Vehicle/Toll:** Sem Parar's 100% Brazilian toll coverage creates a strong network effect. The 7.5M tagholder base is growing and increasingly monetizable (add-on services, compliance, EV charging).

The strategic concern is that the cross-border FX business faces potential disruption from stablecoins and blockchain-based settlement. The 10-K explicitly acknowledges Corpay's "exposure to these dynamics may be greater than that of certain competitors," and management has indicated awareness of the need to adapt settlement infrastructure, though the specific timing and partnerships for blockchain-based settlement initiatives remain to be confirmed. The risk is structural and longer-term.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Leverage and refinancing (HIGH).** A significant portion of debt requires refinancing within two years. Every 100bp increase on the full stack = ~$100M additional annual interest expense.

**2. Integration execution (MODERATE-HIGH).** Three major 2025 acquisitions (Alpha, AvidXchange, Gringo) require simultaneous integration. Alpha Group alone is the largest deal in company history. Cross-border businesses historically have complex middle/back office operations.

**3. AvidXchange guarantee structure (MODERATE).** The minimum return guarantee of 1.6x invested capital to TPG partners creates a contingent liability if Corpay doesn't exercise its call option and valuations decline. The exact maximum exposure depends on the interpretation of "partners," "invested capital," and applicable limitations in the agreement, but could be material.

**4. CEO succession (MODERATE).** Ron Clarke is 70 with no disclosed succession plan. His departure would remove the singular decision-maker who has driven 100+ acquisitions and the current strategic transformation.

**5. Earnings expectations (LOW-MODERATE).** While Q4 2025 results came in ahead of expectations, investor sentiment has been cautious given the rapid pace of transformation and leverage buildup. Estimates are now rising, suggesting the Street is gaining confidence.

- Alpha Group integrates smoothly, Corporate Payments organic growth sustains 15%+
- FY2026 EPS delivers $26+ as guided; FY2027 consensus rises toward $31+
- The "portfolio rotation" narrative crystallizes after Cross-Border Teach-In (May 13)

- Cross-border volumes decelerate on global economic slowdown (51% non-USD revenue exposure)

- CEO departure without clear succession creates uncertainty premium

## Conclusion

CPAY at $303.68 is a BUY. Investor caution around the rapid acquisition pace and $10B leverage profile has suppressed the multiple to 11.7x forward on a business growing EPS 22% with 57% EBITDA margins, creating a PEG of ~0.5x.

The primary risk is balance sheet leverage in a credit-uncertain environment. This warrants position sizing at 2-3% rather than a high-conviction overweight. But FCF generation ($1.3B annually), active de-leveraging through divestitures ($450M PayByPhone pending), management's stated priority to pay down debt, and accelerating organic growth provide a credible path to re-rating within 12 months.

The Cross-Border Teach-In (May 13) is an imminent catalyst that should provide greater visibility into the combined Alpha Group/Corpay platform economics and growth trajectory.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The corrected EV/EBITDA multiple (~10.8x, not 7.7x as originally stated) materially reduces the degree of apparent undervaluation on an enterprise-value basis, though the forward P/E-based thesis and PEG analysis remain intact.
