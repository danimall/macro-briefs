## Executive Summary

Ameris Bancorp is a $27.5 billion-asset regional bank based in Atlanta delivering top-quartile profitability (1.60% ROA, ~15% ROTCE) within a Southeast franchise that benefits from population growth tailwinds. EPS of $6.00 in FY2025 grew 16% YoY, and Q1 2026's $1.63 beat consensus by 5.8%, extending an earnings surprise streak. At 12.9x forward earnings — a modest discount to the peer median — the market is not yet pricing in ABCB's superior return profile or its differentiated business mix (insurance premium finance, $4.5B mortgage platform, growing equipment finance).

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) NIM that expanded 23bp in 2025 and should remain elevated even with 5-10bp guided compression, (2) deliberate de-risking of the construction loan portfolio (-26.5% in 2025) while growing C&I (+11.4%) and premium finance (+13%), and (3) positive estimate revision momentum in recent months. The primary risks are CRE concentration (particularly $1.26B in office), uninsured deposit exposure at 47.7%, and elevated near-term implied volatility that suggests the options market is pricing event uncertainty disproportionate to fundamental risk.

## Company Overview

Ameris Bancorp operates 163 full-service branches across Georgia, Alabama, Florida, North Carolina, and South Carolina through its primary subsidiary Ameris Bank. The company differentiates from typical community banks through four distinct revenue platforms: (1) traditional community banking providing 77.6% of revenue via net interest income, (2) a $4.5B retail mortgage origination platform, (3) a $1.3B national insurance premium finance business, and (4) growing SBA and equipment finance verticals.

As of December 31, 2025, total assets stood at $27.5B with $22.1B in loans and $22.4B in deposits. By March 31, 2026, total assets had grown to $28.1B with $22.6B in deposits. The bank employed 2,642 FTEs as of Q1 2026, down from 2,765 in 2023, reflecting efficiency gains while growing the balance sheet.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Acceleration:**
Net income grew from $269M (2023) to $359M (2024) to $412M (2025), with Q1 2026 annualizing to approximately $442M. Diluted EPS rose from $3.89 to $5.19 to $6.00 over this period, with share repurchases ($77.1M in 2025) contributing approximately 1 percentage point of the 16% EPS growth in 2025.

**Net Interest Margin:**
The bank's NIM expanded from 3.56% to 3.79% in 2025, driven by deposit costs falling 51bp on interest-bearing deposits while asset yields declined only 14bp. Q4 net interest income of $245.3M compared to Q1 2025's $221.8M demonstrates the margin expansion is flowing through to dollars. Management guides 5-10bp compression ahead — a headwind, but one that starts from a strong base.

**Credit Quality:**
Non-performing assets at 0.44% of total assets and net charge-offs at 0.18% are both well within normal ranges. The allowance for credit losses covers nonaccrual loans at 319%. Provision for credit losses on loans dropped from $69.8M to $47.4M year-over-year; however, total provision for credit losses (including unfunded commitments) increased from $58.8M to $70.2M in 2025, as provision for unfunded commitments swung to a $22.8M charge from an $11.0M release in 2024. This reflects conservatism in reserving rather than credit deterioration, but it modestly offset the loan-loss provision benefit at the net income level.

**Capital:**
CET1 at 13.17% provides 617bp of excess above the 7.00% regulatory minimum plus capital conservation buffer. This supports continued buybacks and dividend growth. Tangible book value per share grew 14.5% to $44.18.

**Free Cash Flow:**
Approximately $370M in free cash flow (operating cash flow of $390M less ~$21M in capital expenditures) in 2025. The bank generates significant excess capital that management is deploying through repurchases (~$77M), dividends (~$54M), and organic loan growth.

## Growth Analysis

Forward estimates paint a constructive picture:

- Current quarter (Q2 2026): $1.66
- Next quarter (Q3 2026): $1.73
- FY2026: $6.77 (+12.8% YoY)
- FY2027: $7.15 (+5.6% YoY)

Estimates have been revised upward in recent months, reflecting the Q1 beat and management's confidence in mid-single-digit loan growth. Revenue growth of 9.9% YoY in Q1 2026 demonstrates that NIM expansion and loan growth are both contributing.

Key growth vectors:
1. **C&I lending** (+11.4% in 2025): Higher-velocity, relationship-deepening assets replacing construction runoff
2. **Insurance premium finance** (+13% in 2025): Short-duration, low-risk national platform with inherent demand growth tied to premium inflation
3. **Equipment finance** (activity income +41% YoY): Still small but scaling rapidly
4. **Share repurchases**: With CET1 at 13.17% and a 13% payout ratio, there's capacity to retire 1-2% of shares annually

Growth headwinds include: NIM compression (5-10bp), potential mortgage volume sensitivity to rate movements, and natural deceleration as the deposit repricing benefit becomes fully reflected.

## Valuation Assessment

At $87.31, ABCB trades at:
- 14.6x trailing EPS ($6.00)
- 12.9x FY2026E EPS ($6.77)
- 12.2x FY2027E EPS ($7.15)
- 1.98x tangible book value
- ~0.9% dividend yield

Versus the peer median forward P/E of approximately 13.6x, ABCB trades at a slight discount on forward earnings, which understates ABCB's value proposition:
- ABCB's ROA (1.54-1.60%) exceeds typical peers by 20-40bp
- ROTCE at ~15% vs. peer averages of 10-12%
- 5-year total shareholder return of 208% vs. peer group 153%
- Diversified fee income (mortgage, premium finance, equipment) reduces earnings volatility relative to pure NII-dependent peers

On a forward P/E of 12.9x with 12.8% EPS growth, PEG is approximately 1.0x — reasonable for a bank with this profitability profile. A 14x multiple on $6.77 FY2026E yields ~$95; a 14.5x multiple on $7.15 FY2027E yields ~$104.

## Competitive Landscape

Ameris occupies a productive niche: large enough to support differentiated lending platforms (premium finance, warehouse lending, SBA) but small enough to maintain a decentralized community banking model with local market authority. This allows it to compete against mega-banks on relationship quality while offering product breadth beyond typical community banks.

The Southeast franchise benefits from structural population migration. Georgia, Florida, and the Carolinas have consistently ranked among the highest in-migration states — a secular tailwind for loan demand and deposit growth independent of the economic cycle.

The insurance premium finance business at $1.3B with 13% growth and minimal credit risk (0.13% charge-offs) is effectively a diversified national lending platform that produces stable returns regardless of rate cycle or CRE conditions. It is underappreciated in the market's valuation of the bank.

Construction & development loan reduction (-26.5% to $1.47B) while CRE ratios remain below regulatory triggers (261.9% vs. 300% threshold) shows proactive risk management that peers often lack.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE Concentration (Primary Risk):** The $9.3B CRE portfolio represents 43% of loans. Within this, $1.26B in office loans constitutes the most vulnerable segment. National office vacancy rates remain elevated at ~18-19%, and ABCB's 10-K explicitly acknowledges risk from "levels of interest rates above those at origination for loan renewals." While current past-due metrics are benign (8bp of CRE), office deterioration could produce elevated losses in 2026-2027 as loans mature and refinance at higher rates.

**Uninsured Deposits (47.7%):** While $3.76B is collateralized municipal deposits (reducing effective uninsured to ~31%), the headline number creates vulnerability to confidence-driven outflows. The 2023 bank run episode demonstrated how quickly uninsured depositors can move.

**NIM Compression:** The 5-10bp guided compression is modest but directionally negative. If the Fed holds rates higher for longer, deposit competition could intensify faster than guided.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** The bank is asset-sensitive (positive gap). Falling rates compress margins; rising rates benefit them. In the current environment where rates are likely to stay elevated, this is neutral-to-positive.

**Mortgage Banking Cyclicality:** At $147M (12% of revenue), the mortgage platform creates earnings volatility. Gain-on-sale spreads already compressed from 2.37% to 2.20% in 2025. Further rate increases would reduce origination volumes.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term implied volatility at 51.1% is elevated versus 24.6% historical volatility — a 26.5pp premium. The term structure appears in steep backwardation (51.1% near-term vs. 26.3% at 6 months), indicating the market may be pricing an imminent event that should resolve relatively quickly. However, these observations should be treated as indicative only given very low trading activity (approximately 53 contracts across 4 expirations) and wide bid-ask spreads that can distort implied volatility readings in small-cap names. The IV spike is more likely a liquidity artifact than a signal of genuine institutional concern.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Bull case target:** $105 — 14.5x FY2027E EPS of $7.15, reflecting full recognition of the bank's return premium and diversified fee income platform.

**Bear case target:** $72 — 10.5x a reduced FY2026E of $6.50 (reflecting incremental CRE provision and NIM headwinds). This represents 17.5% downside.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Non-performing assets exceed 0.75% of total assets, indicating broad credit deterioration beyond office CRE

## Conclusion

Ameris Bancorp offers above-peer profitability, diversified revenue streams, accelerating EPS growth with positive estimate revisions, and a reasonable valuation at 12.9x forward earnings. The bank's deliberate de-risking of construction loans, growing C&I and premium finance portfolios, and strong capital position provide both offense and defense. The primary risk is CRE office exposure ($1.26B), but current credit metrics remain benign overall with mixed small movements quarter to quarter — NPAs excluding GNMA-guaranteed loans improved to 0.33% from 0.35%, while total NPAs ticked up to 0.45% from 0.44% — and the bank has substantial reserve cushion (319% coverage of nonaccruals).

At $87.31, the stock trades near the 52-week high, which requires acknowledging that the easy money from the $59 low has been made. However, with positive estimate revision momentum, consistent quarterly beats, and a forward multiple still below the peer median despite substantially higher returns, the risk/reward remains favorable. BUY with a base case of $95 and 12-18 month horizon.
