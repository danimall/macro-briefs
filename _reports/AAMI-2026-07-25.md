**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Acadian Asset Management is a holding company whose sole operating business is Acadian Asset Management LLC, a ~40-year-old systematic (quantitative) equity manager that positions itself as the only pure-play, publicly-traded systematic manager. The company is the former BrightSphere Investment Group, which wound down its multi-boutique affiliates and rebranded as Acadian effective January 1, 2025. The past four quarters have been exceptional: AUM rose 61% year-over-year to $195.7B, Q1 2026 net client cash flows hit a record +$21.4B (12% of beginning AUM), ENI operating margin expanded ~978 bps to 38.1%, and ENI diluted EPS grew 94% to $1.05. The stock has roughly tripled from the mid-$20s in early 2025 to ~$81, reflecting most of this operating turnaround.

The core tension for a forward view is that AUM growth and revenue growth are diverging. The blended average fee rate fell to 34.1 bps in Q1 2026 from 37.9 bps a year earlier, driven by the rapid growth of lower-fee Enhanced Equity (now 29.5% of AUM, up from 22.5% at year-end). The CFO flagged a further fee-rate headwind next quarter as the $16B St. James's Place Enhanced mandate — which funded late in Q1 and drove the flow surge — reaches its full lower-fee run-rate. That same mandate creates single-client flow concentration. Layered on top is a persistent supply overhang: Paulson & Co. has sold ~3.1M shares over the past year (Sep 2025 and June 2026) and continues to distribute into strength, holding ~5.84M shares down from a 21.8% stake.

Estimate revisions are the strongest offsetting signal — current-year and next-year EPS consensus rose +10.9% and +12.6% respectively over 90 days, with the next-quarter line up +11.9%. This is genuine positive earnings momentum on a business trading at ~16x forward ENI EPS, which is not demanding for a manager with 38%+ margins and a net-cash-adjacent balance sheet. The balance is close: high-quality, cash-generative, capital-returning franchise with rising estimates, set against a re-rated price, fee-rate compression, and a technical overhang. We rate AAMI a HOLD (growth_hold), with a clear upgrade path if fee-rate compression stabilizes and the Paulson overhang clears.

## Company Overview

Acadian operates a single reporting segment, Quant & Solutions, through its majority-owned subsidiary Acadian Asset Management LLC. Product lines span Emerging Markets Equity, Non-U.S. Equity, Global Equity, Small Cap Equity, Enhanced Equity, Equity Extensions (130/30-type), Systematic Credit, and Alternatives/Managed Volatility. The client base is predominantly institutional (~75% of AUM), with sub-advisory (~8%) and a fast-growing wealth channel (~17% after Q1 2026). Roughly 70% of AUM is non-USD denominated, making reported results sensitive to the dollar.

The economics run through a profit-share structure. Acadian LLC key employees hold direct equity/profit interests in the LLC; variable compensation is a percentage of pre-bonus profits, with remaining profits split between public holders (AAMI) and LLC key-employee equity holders on a tiered basis. Approximately 44% of the ENI cost base is variable, which cushions downside in a market drawdown but caps some of the operating leverage in an up market. Employee equity is repurchasable at a fixed multiple of trailing earnings and recycled into future compensation, which produces large non-cash revaluation swings in GAAP compensation expense, the primary reason management steers by ENI rather than GAAP.

## Financial Analysis

The AUM trajectory is the central driver. AUM moved from $117.3B at the start of 2025 to $177.5B at year-end 2025 and $195.7B at March 31, 2026, up 61% year-over-year. Net client cash flows were +$21.4B in Q1 2026, a quarterly record, versus +$3.8B in Q1 2025, marking nine consecutive quarters of positive net flows.

Q1 2026 results relative to the prior-year quarter:
- GAAP revenue $167.0M (+39%); management fees $159.3M (+41%).
- GAAP net income to controlling interests $24.3M (+21%); GAAP diluted EPS $0.68 (+26%).
- ENI $37.6M (+85%); ENI diluted EPS $1.05 (+94%).
- ENI operating margin 38.1%, up ~978 bps from 28.3% — operating leverage as fees scaled against a largely fixed cost base.
- Variable comp ratio improved to 39.4% from 47.6%; FY2026 guided to 40–43%.

The key nuance for forward modeling is fee-rate compression via mix shift. The average fee rate was ~35.9 bps for FY2025. Revenue should not be extrapolated linearly from AUM. Performance fees are minor and volatile: only ~$22B (11%) of AUM carries performance-fee features, with TTM performance fees ~$32M.

Consolidated GAAP operating cash flow appears negative, but this reflects the seasonal Q1 payout of prior-year accrued incentive comp (accrued incentive comp fell from $129.9M to $47.4M in the quarter) and the consolidation of seed-capital funds, not underlying deterioration. On management's economics, ENI is a reasonable free-cash-flow proxy and is strongly positive; the business is genuinely cash-generative.

The balance sheet is healthy. Cash was $129M, with seed capital ~$97M. Debt consists of a $200M term loan plus an $85M revolver (a seasonal Q1 draw expected to be repaid by year-end). In December 2025, the company redeemed $275M of 4.80% senior notes and replaced them with cheaper variable-rate facilities, cutting Q1 interest expense 29% YoY. The leverage ratio is 1.0x with interest coverage of 46.2x, against covenants of max 2.5x leverage and min 4.0x coverage — ample headroom. Net debt/adjusted EBITDA is ~0.7x, and goodwill is modest at $20.3M, limiting impairment risk relative to peers. The company has reduced diluted share count ~58% since 4Q2019 (86M to 35.8M) and returned ~$1.4B via buybacks and dividends, raising the quarterly dividend 10x from $0.01 to $0.10.

The earnings surprise record over four quarters is two beats, one in-line, one miss — Q1 2026 beat by 16.1% ($1.05 vs $0.90 estimate), following a -6.9% Q4 2025 miss. The pattern is decent but not a clean, consistent beat streak, which tempers confidence in extrapolating forward estimates aggressively.

## Growth Analysis

The primary growth engine is net flows into a differentiated systematic franchise, reflecting genuine demand. Excluding the single St. James's Place mandate, net flows were still a robust ~$4B, roughly half from extension strategies, so the underlying flow trend is not entirely dependent on one client.

Estimate revisions are the strongest forward indicator here and are broadly positive:
- Current quarter: $1.03, +5.1% (30d), +3.9% (90d).
- Next quarter: $1.19, +11.7% (30d), +11.9% (90d).
- Current year: $5.04, +5.0% (30d), +10.9% (90d).
- Next year: $6.32, +7.9% (30d), +12.6% (90d).

Revision velocity of +10–13% over 90 days on the annual lines is a meaningful positive signal — consensus is catching up to the flow and margin inflection. It falls short of the +30% threshold that would flag a decisive inflection buy, and the mix-shift fee headwind is a legitimate reason revenue growth will trail AUM growth. The direction is unambiguous: estimates are rising, not falling, and the stock's pullback from its ~$85.73 high to ~$81 has occurred against improving fundamentals.

Two structural growth legs sit beyond flows. First, the systematic credit platform: three strategies approaching 3-year track records in late 2026/early 2027 could unlock a new institutional growth leg by taking share from both traditional active credit and, prospectively, private credit. Second, the wealth channel, now ~17% of AUM after the SJP win, is a faster-growing distribution vector than the institutional core. Management held its inaugural Investor Forum on May 19, 2026 to detail its addressable market and capital-allocation framework, a catalyst for the growth narrative.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$81, AAMI carries a trailing P/E of ~34x on GAAP EPS of $2.21. That trailing figure is not the right anchor. The GAAP number is distorted by non-cash compensation revaluation swings under the profit-share structure, and management (and the compensation plan) steer by ENI. On forward ENI EPS, the picture is more reasonable: consensus current-year ENI EPS of $5.04 implies ~16x, and next-year $6.32 implies ~12.8x. For a manager compounding AUM at double-digit rates, generating 38%+ ENI margins, and returning cash aggressively, low-to-mid teens forward earnings is not stretched.

Peer context is mixed and highlights the heterogeneity of the group. Median peer P/E is 25.2x with a 3.1% yield. Within the set, the dispersion is wide: WisdomTree at 40.7x, Cohen & Steers at 25.2x, Hamilton Lane at 13.3x, Artisan Partners at 8.9x (with an 8.9% yield), and StepStone at a negative reported multiple. Acadian's ~34x trailing sits above the median, but its ~16x forward ENI is below it — the comparison depends entirely on whether one uses distorted trailing GAAP or the operating metric. Adjusting for the growth differential (Acadian's flows and margin expansion outpace most of the traditional-active peers), the forward multiple is defensible rather than cheap.

The reason this is a 5 rather than a 7 on valuation: the stock has already re-rated to reflect the turnaround, the fee-rate compression is a real drag on the revenue-per-dollar-of-AUM that the market is currently extrapolating, and the analyst price target band is tight and centered near the current price (low $72, high $84, mean/median $78 — i.e., the mean sits ~3.6% below spot). On an inflection stock with rising estimates I would treat targets as floors, but here the multiple is already re-rated and the fee headwind is a specific, management-flagged reason for near-term revenue disappointment, so the asymmetry is not compelling. A high valuation score requires a specific reason the market is mispricing the stock; the clearest such reason (Paulson overhang resolution removing a discount) is real but untimed. A low headline multiple is not itself a buy signal, but here the issue is the opposite: the operating multiple is fair, not a bargain.

## Competitive Landscape

Acadian's moat rests on performance and its niche positioning. It describes itself as the only pure-play public systematic manager with a ~40-year track record, differentiated from both fundamental active managers and passive/ETF providers. The performance data support the moat: 96% of strategies by revenue (92% by assets) outperformed their benchmarks over 3/5/10-year periods, with a 5-year revenue-weighted excess return of +4.1%. That track record is the direct cause of nine straight quarters of net inflows and is the franchise's core competitive asset.

The competitive trajectory is improving at the flow level — the firm is taking share within active equity and building an adjacent systematic credit platform — but two headwinds temper the score. First, sector-wide fee pressure is amplified here by Acadian's own success in lower-fee Enhanced product; growing AUM into a lower-fee channel is share gain that dilutes revenue quality. Second, the AI question is a genuine open item. Management frames AI as an extension of its existing ML-driven process and is investing in it for research, coding, and productivity, but the same tools that help Acadian could lower barriers for new systematic entrants over time. This is not an immediate threat, but it is a legitimate long-term watch item for a business whose entire edge is quantitative. The competitive position is solid and improving on flows, with a structural fee headwind and a longer-dated technology risk that keep it below the top tier.

## Risk Assessment

**Fee compression / adverse mix shift (materializing now).** This is the single most important forward risk: rising AUM can mask flat-to-declining revenue-per-dollar as the $16B low-fee Enhanced mandate reaches full run-rate.

**Single-mandate flow concentration.** The record Q1 net-flow number is heavily dependent on one $16B client (St. James's Place). Top-5 clients represent ~14% of run-rate fees. A large redemption would be visibly damaging to both flows and revenue.

**Strategy concentration.** As of 12/31/25, $81B (46%) of AUM sat in just five strategies (EM Equity ~12%, Enhanced Global ~10%, ACWI ex-US ~10%, Non-US Small Cap ~8%, Non-US Equity ~6%). Underperformance in a flagship could disproportionately hit asset-weighted performance and flows.

**FX / non-U.S. exposure.** ~70% of AUM is non-USD; a 10% FX move is ~$54M annual management-fee swing / ~$21M post-tax ENI. A stronger dollar mechanically shrinks reported AUM and fees.

**Paulson overhang / change-of-control mechanics.** Paulson continues to distribute into strength (1.90M shares in June 2026 at ~$77.25, ~$147M). This is both a technical supply pressure and a stated 10-K risk: a deemed transfer of a controlling block of Acadian LLC voting securities could trigger client-consent requirements under the Advisers Act / Investment Company Act. Resolution could remove a persistent discount, but timing is uncertain.

**Market beta.** A 10% market decline is ~$26M reduction in annual post-tax ENI — revenue is directly levered to equity markets.

**Single-business dependence and key-personnel risk.** All cash flow depends on distributions from Acadian LLC, and the systematic edge plus the equity-recycling comp model depend on retaining quant staff.

**Governance note.** A June 11, 2026 8-K carried Item 5.02 (director/officer departure) alongside the annual meeting; the CEO handoff from Suren Rana to Kelly Ann Young appears complete and was smoothly executed given record results, but the board-level change is worth monitoring.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 42.7% against 1-year historical volatility of 42.8% — options are priced fairly, with no unusual richness or cheapness. Put/call skew is balanced at 1.5%, and IV sits at the 37th percentile within the current chain, so there is no elevated put demand contradicting or confirming a directional view. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 42.7% vs 6-month 36.0%), with an implied ±9.6% move over the next month, indicating the market is pricing an imminent event — most plausibly the next earnings print and any commentary on the flagged fee-rate headwind. IV has come down from 46.5% to 42.7% over the past month even as the stock rose ~15%, consistent with resolving uncertainty rather than building stress. Options positioning is neutral and does not challenge the fundamental HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($95–100+).** Net flows remain strong beyond the SJP mandate (~$4B organic in Q1), operating leverage continues as fees scale against a largely fixed cost base, and the systematic credit platform crosses its 3-year track-record threshold in late 2026/early 2027, opening a new institutional growth leg. Estimate revisions (+10–13% on annual lines over 90 days) continue rising, and the Paulson overhang clears, removing a persistent discount. On next-year ENI EPS of $6.32, a modest re-rating to ~15x supports ~$95, with upside toward $100+ if credit scales and the fee mix stabilizes. Continued buybacks below intrinsic value amplify per-share economics.

**Bear Case ($55–62).** The fee-rate headwind proves worse than a one-quarter reset as Enhanced Equity keeps diluting the blend, revenue growth decouples sharply from AUM growth, and a market drawdown compresses AUM (10% market decline ~$26M post-tax ENI) with FX adding a second leg down. A flagship-strategy stumble (EM Equity is ~12% of AUM) slows flows, and continued Paulson distribution caps the stock. On current-year ENI EPS of ~$5.04 and a de-rating to ~11–12x, the stock falls to $55–62, roughly 24–32% below current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD (growth_hold): a high-quality, cash-generative franchise with genuine earnings momentum, held back from a BUY by a re-rated multiple, an active fee-rate headwind, and a technical overhang.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (roughly late July 2026, following the April 30 Q1 print cadence), which should reveal whether the CFO-flagged fee-rate headwind is a one-quarter reset or a deeper trend, and whether organic (ex-SJP) flows are sustained.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Blended fee rate stabilizes in the 33–34 bps range (i.e., the SJP full-run-rate impact is absorbed without further compression) while ENI margin holds at/above ~38%.
- Paulson reduces its position to a de minimis level, clearing the supply overhang, or the stock pulls back to ~$65–70 restoring a clear discount to ~12–13x next-year ENI EPS.
- Organic net flows (excluding one-off mega-mandates) sustain at ~$4B+/quarter across two consecutive quarters, plus positive momentum in the systematic credit strategies.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Net client cash flows turn negative for two consecutive quarters, or a large SJP-scale redemption is disclosed.
- Blended fee rate falls below ~32 bps with no offsetting margin gains, indicating structural revenue-quality erosion.
- ENI margin compresses back below ~30% (i.e., operating leverage reverses), or estimate revisions turn negative across the annual lines.

## Conclusion

Acadian is a well-run, differentiated systematic equity manager delivering record flows, strong operating leverage, and disciplined capital return, with a clean balance sheet (1.0x leverage, 46x coverage) and rising consensus estimates. The turnaround is real, and the forward ENI multiple of ~16x current-year / ~12.8x next-year is fair rather than expensive for the growth and quality on offer. The reasons this is a HOLD and not a BUY are specific and current: the stock has already tripled and re-rated, the blended fee rate is compressing with a management-flagged further headwind ahead, the record flow number leans on a single $16B mandate, and Paulson continues to sell into strength. The analyst target band (low $72, high $84, mean $78) brackets the current price tightly, reinforcing balanced near-term risk/reward.

We would become constructive on a fee-rate stabilization, a clearing of the Paulson overhang, or a pullback toward the mid-$60s that restores a clear discount to forward earnings. Until then, own it for the earnings-momentum and capital-return story, but recognize the asymmetry is balanced at ~$81. **Recommendation: HOLD.**
