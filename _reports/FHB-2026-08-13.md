## Executive Summary

First Hawaiian is a high-quality, low-loss deposit franchise with a structural growth problem it has now chosen to solve through a large, all-stock acquisition. The quality is verifiable: CET1 of 13.27% at 6/30/26, nonperforming assets of 0.29% of loans, net charge-offs of 0.13% annualized in 1H26, an "Outstanding" CRA rating, and a net interest margin that has expanded from 2.92% (FY2023) to 3.15% (FY2025) to 3.25% in Q2 2026. The problem is equally verifiable and comes from management's own disclosures: audited net income of $266M (2021), $266M (2022), $235M (2023), $230M (2024) and $276M (2025) — roughly a 0.9% compound annual rate — a total asset base that has shrunk from $23,955M at 12/31/25 to $23,646M at 6/30/26, average noninterest-bearing deposits down $1.31 billion (16%) over two years, and cumulative total shareholder return below the company's own selected peer group in every one of the five years disclosed in the March 2026 proxy. The binding constraint is stated in the 10-K: FHB holds more than 30% of Hawaii deposits and is therefore legally barred from acquiring another Hawaii bank, in a state with documented out-migration and Japanese visitor arrivals still below pre-pandemic levels.

The July 12, 2026 agreement to acquire TriCo Bancshares is the strategic answer, and it is a genuinely two-sided transaction. It converts each TriCo share into 2.095 FHB shares — approximately 67.7 million new shares on TriCo's 12/31/25 count of 32.335M, a 55.6% increase in share count that leaves existing FHB holders with roughly 64% of the company (consistent with the disclosed ~65/35 split). At the $63.12 announced value the price is ~1.97x TriCo tangible equity of $1,019.1M and ~16.5x TriCo's FY2025 net income of $121.6M. FHB is therefore issuing stock trading at 12.0x its own trailing earnings and 1.84x its own tangible book to buy a franchise at 16.5x and 1.97x. On static FY2025 earnings the combination produces $397.9M of net income on ~189.4M shares, or $2.10 per share against FHB's standalone $2.20 — 4.5% dilutive. Adding the full 25% cost-save target against TriCo's $241.0M expense base ($60.3M pre-tax, roughly $44.6M after tax) lifts pro forma EPS to $2.34, or 6.4% accretive. Purchase accounting accretion adds to that figure and core deposit intangible amortization plus one-time charges subtract, but the direction of the sensitivity is unambiguous: the deal's per-share arithmetic rests almost entirely on synergy delivery, and management has publicly committed to no branch closures and retention of TriCo leadership.

What FHB acquires is a better margin (TriCo FY2025 NIM 3.89% FTE versus FHB's 3.15%), a better funding profile on uninsured deposits (~34% versus FHB's 48%), higher capital (TCE/TA 10.71% versus ~7.7%), growing loans (+5.1% in 2025 against FHB's contraction), and an opposite-signed rate profile that is a real hedge — FHB gains 6.6% of net interest income on a +200bp shock while TriCo loses 3.5%. It also acquires a 68.3% commercial real estate concentration and four consecutive years of credit deterioration: nonperforming loans/total loans of 0.33% → 0.47% → 0.65% → 0.90%, reserve coverage of NPLs collapsing from 516% to 196%, net charge-offs rising from $2.6M to $9.9M, with $31.6M of the $64.2M NPL balance in farmland and C&I net charge-offs at 1.93% in 2025. Central Valley agricultural credit is a category in which FHB has no stated operating history. Rating: **HOLD**, with the return case anchored on a covered 3.73% dividend rather than on an identified mispricing.

## Company Overview

First Hawaiian, Inc. is a Delaware bank holding company headquartered in Honolulu whose sole material subsidiary is First Hawaiian Bank, founded in 1858. It operates 49 branches — 45 in Hawaii, three in Guam, one in Saipan — with more than 2,000 employees averaging 11.7 years of tenure, and delivers wealth management through Bishop Street Capital Management. It is the largest bank headquartered in Hawaii by loans and by net income.

Two legacy items from the BNP Paribas era shape the current financials. First, the $995.5 million of goodwill on the balance sheet was pushed down from BNPP's 2001 acquisition of the BancWest minority rather than arising from any FHB acquisition; it is roughly 35% of stated common equity and explains why tangible book value per share is $15.04 against stated book value of $23.22. Second, the April 2016 pre-IPO reorganization left contingent tax liabilities and a tax-sharing agreement with BNPP that are still moving cash in 2026; the July 2026 California Franchise Tax Board settlement will route approximately $90.2 million through FHB to BNPP, producing a net ~$6.7 million benefit to FHB's Q3 2026 net income (a $24.3M expense increase offset by a $31.0M tax provision decrease) and essentially nothing for shareholders economically.

Segments were reduced from three to two in Q4 2025 (Treasury folded into Corporate/Other). FY2025 segment net income was Retail $250.5M (up ~10%), Commercial $130.0M (down ~9%), Corporate/Other –$104.3M. The direction of the commercial line should be tracked against the 10-K's newly emphasized competitive category — private credit and direct lenders — though the filing does not attribute the decline to any specific cause.

## Financial Analysis

**The FY2025 recovery was liability-driven, not asset-driven.** Net interest income rose to $663.7M from $622.7M, NIM to 3.15% from 2.95%, ROA to 1.16% from 0.96%, ROTCE to 16.27% from 14.74%, and efficiency improved to 56.43% from 61.57%. The mechanism was deposit and borrowing repricing: earning-asset yield actually *fell* to 4.51% from 4.63% and loan yield fell to 5.45% from 5.65%, while the rate paid on interest-bearing deposits dropped 45bp to 2.06% and borrowing costs fell from $20.0M to $7.4M as a $250M FHLB advance matured unreplaced. Interest rate spread widened 37bp to 2.42%. This is a rate-cut beneficiary profile on the funding side against an asset-sensitive net position — a combination that works when the curve steepens and short funding reprices faster than assets.

Expense flatness was partly non-recurring. Noninterest expense held at $499.3M despite salaries and benefits rising $10.3M, with offsets including a 37% drop in regulatory assessments (a $2.6M FDIC special-assessment credit) and $3.8M of favorable Reorganization Transaction liability adjustments. The underlying run-rate is higher than the reported flat line implies, and Q2 2026 expense of $130.4M (+4.4% year over year) confirms that.

**Q2 2026 shows margin momentum with stalled net income.** Net interest income of $171.0M (+4.5%), NIM 3.25% (+6bp sequentially), cost of deposits 1.20%, noninterest income $60.3M (+11.7%), noninterest expense $130.4M (+4.4%), pre-tax income $95.2M versus $88.1M, and net income of $73.4M versus $73.2M, flat, entirely because the effective tax rate rose to 22.95% from 16.86%. First-half 2026 is the cleaner read: net income $141.2M versus $132.5M (+6.5%) and diluted EPS $1.15 versus $1.05 (+9.5%). Two Q2 revenue items should not be annualized: $7.1M of BOLI income (versus $4.7M) and $6.0M of "other" noninterest income (versus $3.0M).

Trailing-twelve-month EPS reconstructs to $2.30 (FY2025 $2.20 + 1H26 $1.15 – 1H25 $1.05), putting the stock at 12.0x trailing at $27.63.

**The balance sheet is the standalone problem.** Total assets fell to $23,646M at 6/30/26 from $23,955M at year-end. Loans declined $95.7M during FY2025 to $14,312.5M before recovering $264.8M in 1H26 to $14,577.3M — a genuine positive. Deposits, however, fell $361.5M (–1.8%) in the first half to $20,154.2M. The composition matters: $223.2M of that (–27%) was public deposits, which are fully collateralized and low-margin, so the economic loss is smaller than the headline. The more serious trend is the free-funding base: average noninterest-bearing demand deposits went $8,126.4M (FY2023) → $6,994.5M (FY2024) → $6,814.4M (FY2025). That $1.31 billion erosion is the largest structural drag on FHB's margin relative to peers and it has not stabilized.

**A $376 million unrecognized securities loss sits between regulatory capital and tangible equity.** Held-to-maturity securities carry $3,411.7M of amortized cost against $3,035.5M of fair value at 6/30/26 — a $376.2M unrecognized loss that *widened* from $344.5M at year-end, on a 1.67% book yield and 7.3-year weighted average life. AOCI is –$359.8M. FHB has elected the AOCI opt-out, which is why CET1 reads 13.27% while TCE/TA is roughly 7.7%. The amortization of previously transferred HTM losses added $9.7M to income in Q2 and $23.6M in 1H26, a mechanical margin tailwind that will persist for years, but it is accretion of a prior loss rather than franchise improvement, and it should be stripped out when judging margin quality. Liquidity ($3.6B FHLB capacity, a $3.3B undrawn FRB line, $10.67B of pledged collateral) makes forced sales unlikely, so the economic character is opportunity cost rather than impairment.

**Credit is excellent in absolute terms and deteriorating at the margin.** Nonaccruals doubled from $20.7M (12/31/24) to $41.0M (12/31/25) and eased to $39.5M at 6/30/26; NPAs/loans went 0.14% → 0.29%. Of the current balance, $30.1M is residential/HELOC where the FY2025 10-K discloses a 52% weighted-average current LTV on 59 nonaccrual first mortgages and attributes the balance to Hawaii's judicial foreclosure timeline — a timing artifact with minimal loss content. The real loss center is consumer, running 1.41% annualized net charge-offs in Q2 against a 4.60% reserve on a $1,046.5M book. The item to monitor is CRE risk grading: special mention rose from $146.2M to $183.7M between 12/31/25 and 6/30/26 while substandard fell from $83.1M to $66.0M, and the reserve for unfunded construction commitments rose from $9.8M to $12.1M. That pattern is consistent with a proactive downgrade cycle; it is equally consistent with a handful of large maturing loans facing refinancing stress at current rates. The filings do not disaggregate enough to distinguish. Total ACL is $168.1M, or 1.15% of loans, down from 1.18%, with nonaccrual coverage falling from 5.87x to 4.25x year over year.

**Capital and returns.** CET1 13.17% → 13.27%, total capital 14.42% → 14.52%, Tier 1 leverage 9.27% → 9.46% over the six months. FY2025 equity reconciled +$276.3M earnings, +$95.9M OCI recovery, –$129.9M dividends, –$100.0M buybacks (4,020,554 shares at ~$24.87). The January 2026 authorization of $250.0M — 7.4% of market capitalization — has seen only $32.0M deployed through 6/30/26 (1,307,738 shares at $24.47). Curtailment ahead of a share-issuance transaction is standard practice and the most plausible reading, though the filings do not state it. The quarterly dividend has been $0.26 for twelve consecutive quarters, with a 47.3% FY2025 payout ratio.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone, there is no organic growth engine. The 30%+ Hawaii deposit share is a regulatory ceiling; the state's economics — 2.2% unemployment reflecting in part out-migration, a $1,139,000 Oahu median single-family price, Japanese arrivals still below pre-pandemic levels — do not support volume growth. Per-share earnings growth over FY2024–FY2025 came substantially from share count reduction (diluted weighted average 128.3M → 125.5M).

The credible growth vectors are therefore three, in descending order of reliability:

1. **Margin, not volume.** NIM has expanded 33bp from the FY2023 trough to Q2 2026's 3.25%, and roughly $23.6M of the 1H26 contribution is HTM loss amortization that continues mechanically as the portfolio runs off. Management has hedged the downside: interest rate floors went from three contracts/$300M notional at 12/31/25 to nine contracts/$900M notional at 6/30/26, with $3.8M of premium paid on 3.00% strikes maturing 2029. That is a cash-committed action, though $900M against ~$21B of earning assets is modest and could equally reflect routine ALM policy.
2. **Loan growth resumption.** 1H26 added $264.8M, led by C&I (+7.8% to $2,339.9M) and CRE (+4.2% to $4,783.1M), against declines in construction (–9.5%) and residential (–1.3%). The mix is shifting commercial — better yielding, more cyclical.
3. **The TriCo transaction.** This adds ~$7.1B of California loans, ~$8.3B of deposits, 64 branches and a 3.89% NIM, and moves the loan geography materially off its 69% Hawaii concentration. Consensus reflects this only partially: current-year EPS of $2.35 (up 1.1% over 90 days) and next-year $2.53 (up 2.8% over 90 days), implying 7.7% growth. The near-quarter estimate has been trimmed 2.5% in 30 days, which likely reflects merger-related charge timing rather than operating deterioration.

The estimate revision picture is mildly positive on annual figures and mildly negative on the near quarter. It does not resemble a profile of 30%+ ninety-day revisions with accelerating revenue that would justify overriding a middling valuation. FHB is a low-single-digit earnings grinder.

## Valuation Assessment

At $27.63:

| Metric | FHB | Peer median |
|---|---|---|
| P/E (TTM $2.30) | 12.0x | 15.0x |
| P/E (FY26E $2.35) | 11.8x | — |
| P/E (FY27E $2.53) | 10.9x | — |
| Price/stated book ($23.22) | 1.19x | — |
| Price/tangible book ($15.04) | 1.84x | — |
| Dividend yield | 3.76% | 3.0% |

The apparent 20% P/E discount to the peer median is the weakest part of the bull case, for two reasons. First, the peer median is distorted (SFNC prints –9.6x on a loss). Second, and more importantly, the discount is earned: a bank compounding net income at 0.9% annually, shrinking its balance sheet, and underperforming its own selected peer group for five straight years should not trade at a peer multiple.

The more informative anchor is price to tangible book against return on tangible common equity. FHB earns roughly 16% ROTCE. A standard warranted-multiple frame — (ROTCE – g)/(COE – g) with ROTCE 16%, cost of equity 10%, growth 3% — supports approximately 1.86x tangible book. The stock trades at 1.84x. On the metric that matters for a goodwill-heavy bank, FHB is fairly valued, not cheap. The low P/E is an artifact of $995.5M of pushed-down goodwill inflating stated book, not evidence of mispricing.

Pro forma the transaction, the picture tightens rather than loosens. Mid-case tangible book dilution of roughly 5% (assuming ~$135M of after-tax marks, one-time charges and CECL day-two provision) takes pro forma TBVPS to about $14.24, so $27.63 is 1.94x pro forma tangible book, a slight premium to the warranted multiple, before the deal has demonstrated its synergies. If pro forma ROTCE settles at 14% rather than 16% because the tangible equity base grows faster than earnings, the warranted multiple falls to roughly 1.57x, or about $22.40 per share. That asymmetry — modest upside if synergies land, meaningful downside if returns dilute — is the core valuation judgment.

Analyst positioning is unusually dispersed: 1 buy, 6 hold, 2 sell, 2 strong sell, with targets from $28 to $34 (mean $30.78, median $30.00). Stephens upgraded to Overweight with a $32 target; TD Cowen holds with a target *lowered* to $31; J.P. Morgan carries a Sell. A distribution containing both an upgrade and an outright sell tells you the pro forma math is contested, which is the correct conclusion from the arithmetic. The consensus target *low* of $28 sits above the current price — the sell-side floor is where the stock trades, so there is no target-versus-price gap to exploit. Analyst targets do not act as stale floors here; the stock is neither at a premium multiple with rising estimates nor at a compressed multiple with an accelerating fundamental.

Valuation score: 5.0. The stock is inexpensive on earnings and fair on tangible book, with no identified mispricing and an ambiguous-signed catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

FHB is the largest bank headquartered in Hawaii, with an "Outstanding" CRA rating and specified conservative underwriting (CRE generally at or below 75% LTV with 1.20x minimum debt service coverage; average residential first mortgage of ~$391,000). Its scale within Hawaii has become a ceiling rather than an advantage.

Three competitive dynamics are moving against it. The funding franchise is eroding — $1.31 billion of average noninterest-bearing deposits lost over two years is share loss in the deposit category that matters most for margin. The Commercial Banking segment's net income fell 9% in FY2025 while Retail rose 10%, at the same time the 10-K newly emphasized private credit and direct lenders as competitors; middle-market commercial lending is increasingly contested by vehicles not subject to bank capital rules, and while the filing draws no causal link, the coincidence should be monitored. And the home market itself cannot generate volume.

The merger genuinely reduces geographic concentration: Hawaii falls from 69% of loans toward roughly half, with California added. It does not establish diversification in the risk sense. It substitutes Hawaii residential and tourism exposure for California CRE (68.3% of TriCo's book) and Central Valley agriculture. Two concentrations reduce aggregate risk only if their loss drivers are uncorrelated, and neither company's disclosures analyze that.

The one clear structural gain is the rate hedge. FHB's Q2 10-Q shows +200bp raising net interest income 6.6% and –100bp reducing it 3.0%; TriCo's 10-K shows +200bp *reducing* net interest income 3.5% (+300bp: –5.2%) and –300bp raising it 2.0%. Combining opposite-signed rate sensitivities materially reduces pro forma earnings volatility to the largest single swing factor in regional bank earnings. Neither company's announcement materials emphasized this. The caveat is real: these are static simulations from different measurement dates using different deposit-beta assumptions, so they are not strictly additive.

Competitive position score: 5.5 — a strong incumbent position in a market that cannot grow, with funding share eroding and a merger that trades one concentration for another.

## Risk Assessment

**Deal execution is the dominant risk.** The 25% cost-save target — an externally reported management statement from the July 13 call, not a figure verified in a filing — must be extracted from TriCo's $241.0M expense base, of which $149.8M is salaries and benefits across 1,164 average FTEs, while management has committed to no branch closings and retention of TriCo leadership including CEO Rick Smith as senior advisor. That forces virtually all savings into back office, systems, duplicate public-company costs, data processing ($20.2M) and professional fees ($6.4M). Most bank cost synergies do come from those categories rather than branch closures, so 25% is achievable, but it is demanding under the stated constraints, and the static arithmetic gives no cushion: without synergies the deal is 4.5% EPS dilutive.

**Imported credit risk at a top-of-market price.** FHB pays ~1.97x tangible book — above recent comparables per contemporaneous reporting — for a franchise with four straight years of credit deterioration and 49% of its NPLs in farmland. The mitigant is structural: acquisition accounting eliminates and re-establishes TriCo's 1.77% allowance, so the credit gets priced into goodwill and tangible book dilution at closing rather than surfacing as future provisions. Whether the mark is adequate is what the S-4 and closing disclosures will reveal, and TriCo's Q1/Q2 2026 credit migration is unobserved in this analysis. Farmland charge-offs were net recoveries (–0.41%) in 2025, which argues collateral is holding.

**Fixed exchange ratio with no disclosed collar.** At $27.63, TriCo holders receive 2.095 × $27.63 = $57.88 per share versus the $63.12 announced — an 8.3% erosion borne by the seller. That lowers FHB's effective purchase price (favorable) while raising the risk of TriCo shareholder dissent at the vote (unfavorable). The $80 million reciprocal termination fee is the only disclosed backstop.

**Rate path, two ways.** FHB standalone benefits from a hike (+6.6% NII on +200bp) and is hurt by cuts (–3.0% on –100bp); the $900M of 3.00%-strike floors partially covers the downside. Higher-for-longer supports the margin but pressures CRE refinancing in both books — most acutely TriCo's 68.3% concentration — and, in Hawaii, a 30-year mortgage near 7% against a $1.14M median Oahu price compresses residential origination and consumer capacity (consumer NCOs already at 1.41% annualized).

**Other identified exposures:** the $376.2M HTM unrecognized loss (invisible in CET1, widening); $211.2M of unrecognized tax benefit liabilities from the BNPP separation against $2.83B of equity; natural disaster concentration across $4.04B of Hawaii/Guam/Saipan residential and $1.18B of HELOC, with California wildfire exposure added post-merger; regulatory approval at a new $34B asset threshold requiring California DFPI sign-off; and One Big Beautiful Bill Act changes that the 10-K says "may impact the Company's ability to originate solar leases," relevant to a $450.5M lease book.

Risk score: 4.5 — several dated, specific binaries (S-4 disclosure, two shareholder votes, regulatory approvals, synergy delivery, credit mark adequacy) against substantial capital and liquidity buffers.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility jumped from 37.5% to 83.3% between the July 21 and August 13 snapshots while the price fell 3.4%, with put/call open interest at 0.50 (call-tilted). An 83% ATM reading on a $3.4B regional bank is implausible as a fundamental risk price and most likely reflects thin quoting in a low-liquidity options chain; it should be treated as a data-quality caution rather than a signal. To the extent any of it is real, the combination of sharply higher implied volatility with call-tilted open interest reads as event positioning around the S-4, the shareholder votes and a year-end close, not as directional bearishness. It does not change the fundamental view.

### Short Interest

Short interest is 14.6% of float (11.46M shares, 4.0 days to cover) — elevated for a regional bank. A full merger-arbitrage book (long TriCo, short FHB at 2.095) would require roughly 67.7M FHB shares short, so 11.46M is consistent with partial arbitrage positioning rather than a purely fundamental bear case. This is an inference, not a disclosed fact. Two implications follow if it is correct: the post-announcement price weakness is partly mechanical arb supply rather than a fundamental verdict, and there is limited squeeze fuel, because arbitrage shorts close by delivering the shares they receive at closing rather than buying in the market. The overhang dissipates at close without generating covering demand.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $34 (+23.1%)

The S-4 discloses a credible synergy path with an earnback under three years (consistent with TD Cowen's reported view), both shareholder votes pass, regulators approve without conditions, and the deal closes by year-end 2026. Pro forma EPS lands near $2.34–2.60 in 2027 as full cost saves phase in alongside purchase-accounting accretion, TriCo's 3.89% margin and asset-quality mark prove conservative, and the combined rate profile is visibly less volatile than either standalone bank. Merger-arbitrage supply clears at close, the $250M repurchase authorization resumes against a larger share count, and the stock re-rates from 1.94x to roughly 2.1x pro forma tangible book on evidence that ROTCE has been sustained in the mid-teens. $34 matches the high end of published targets and requires roughly 13x a $2.60 pro forma EPS.

### Bear Case — $22.50 (–18.6%)

Cost saves under-deliver against the no-branch-closure commitment; the credit mark on TriCo's farmland and CRE proves inadequate and provisions surface in 2027; FHB's noninterest-bearing deposit erosion continues while the Fed cuts, hitting an asset-sensitive book that only $900M of floors protects; and CRE special mention keeps migrating upward at both banks. Pro forma ROTCE settles near 14% on a larger tangible equity base, warranting roughly 1.57x pro forma tangible book of $14.24, or about $22.40. A vote failure is a variant of the same outcome — FHB reverts to a standalone franchise with 0.9% earnings growth, a regulatory acquisition ceiling and five years of documented relative underperformance, with the $80M termination fee providing no offset to the strategic dead end.

### Base Case — $29.50 (+6.8%, +10.6% with dividend)

The deal closes on schedule with partial synergy delivery. Pro forma 2027 EPS lands between $2.20 and $2.45; tangible book dilutes ~5%; the margin holds near 3.25% as HTM accretion and the acquired TriCo yield offset continued noninterest-bearing deposit attrition. Fair value of roughly 11.7x $2.53 or 1.27x stated book supports $29.50. Probability-weighting base 50% / bull 25% / bear 25% gives roughly +4.5% price return plus a 3.76% dividend, or about +8% total, which is a fair risk-adjusted outcome consistent with a hold.

**Rating: HOLD (income-oriented).** Existing holders should keep the position for the covered 3.76% dividend and mid-teens ROTCE while merger execution resolves. New capital has no urgent entry signal at 1.84x tangible book and 1.94x pro forma tangible book, with the consensus target floor at the current price.

- **Base case target:** $29.50 — 11.7x FY2027 consensus EPS of $2.53, cross-checked at 1.27x stated book value of $23.22. Upside 6.8% plus 3.8% yield.
- **Bull case target:** $34.00 (+23.1%) — 13x a $2.60 pro forma EPS with full synergy delivery and multiple re-rating to ~2.1x pro forma tangible book.
- **Bear case target:** $22.50 (–18.6%) — 1.57x pro forma tangible book of $14.24 on 14% pro forma ROTCE, or 10x $2.20.
- **Timeframe:** 12–18 months, tied to S-4 effectiveness (expected Q3 2026), both shareholder votes, Federal Reserve/FDIC/Hawaii DFI/California DFPI approvals, and a targeted year-end 2026 close. First reassessment: the Q3 2026 print in October, which will carry the ~$6.7M net BNPP tax benefit and the first post-announcement view of CRE risk grading.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. The S-4 or investor materials disclose issuer-quantified EPS accretion, tangible book dilution and earnback of under three years, together with a gross credit mark on TriCo's farmland and CRE book that is at least as conservative as TriCo's existing 1.77% allowance.
2. Average noninterest-bearing deposits stabilize or grow for two consecutive quarters, ending the $1.31 billion two-year erosion, with NIM holding at or above 3.25%.
3. Price weakness toward $24.00–24.50 — the level at which management itself repurchased shares across 2025 and 1H26 — which would put the stock near 1.6x tangible book against a mid-teens ROTCE, restoring genuine asymmetry.
4. Resumption of the $250M repurchase authorization post-close at prices below 1.9x pro forma tangible book.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. CRE special mention at FHB rising above roughly $220M (from $183.7M), or total ACL/loans falling below 1.10% while nonaccruals rise — an indication that reserve build is lagging grade migration.
2. Disclosed cost savings materially below 25%, an earnback extending beyond four years, or tangible book dilution above 8%.
3. TriCo credit deterioration continuing in its 2026 filings — NPLs/loans above roughly 1.10% or reserve coverage of NPLs below 175% — which would make the 1.97x tangible book price look like a mark-adequacy problem rather than a synergy problem.
4. Deal termination, or approval conditioned in a way that impairs the synergy or capital plan.
5. A dividend held flat while payout rises above ~60% on diluted pro forma earnings, which would remove the income underpinning of this rating.

## Conclusion

The score declines to 5.3 from 5.7, and the reason is the August 3 Form 10-Q rather than the stock's 4% pullback since the prior review. A lower price on unchanged fundamentals improves risk/reward; what the 10-Q added was new fundamental information, and it leaned negative: deposits down $361.5M in the first half, the HTM unrecognized loss widening to $376.2M from $344.5M, CRE special mention rising to $183.7M, ACL/loans slipping to 1.15%, nonaccrual coverage down to 4.25x from 5.87x, and only $32.0M of the $250M repurchase authorization deployed. Against that, the filing showed loans returning to growth (+$264.8M), NIM up 6bp sequentially to 3.25%, 1H26 EPS +9.5%, and a tripling of interest-rate-floor protection to $900M notional. The net is modestly worse than the prior read, and the specific binaries around the TriCo transaction are now clearer and larger than they appeared at announcement.

FHB is fairly valued on the metric that matters for a goodwill-heavy bank — 1.84x tangible book against ~16% ROTCE, versus a warranted ~1.86x — with the low headline P/E an artifact of pushed-down goodwill rather than evidence of mispricing. The transaction that is supposed to solve a documented five-year growth and returns problem is 4.5% EPS dilutive on static numbers and dilutes tangible book roughly 5%, with essentially all accretion contingent on a 25% cost-save target pursued under a public commitment to close no branches, in exchange for a franchise whose credit has deteriorated for four consecutive years in a loan category FHB has never underwritten. The genuine positives — a real rate hedge, a materially better acquired margin, improved uninsured-deposit mix, higher acquired capital, and pre-deal management action to buy rate-decline insurance — are worth something but are not sized to override the per-share arithmetic.

Hold for the 3.73% dividend, covered at a 47% payout and unchanged for twelve quarters, and for the option that the S-4 resolves the synergy and credit-mark questions favorably. Do not pay up ahead of that disclosure. The place to add is $24.00–24.50, where management itself has repeatedly bought.
