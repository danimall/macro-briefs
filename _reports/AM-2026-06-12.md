**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Antero Midstream is a pure-play Appalachian Basin midstream operator structured as a captive affiliate of Antero Resources (AR). Roughly 99% of revenue comes from AR under long-term, fee-based, CPI-escalating contracts with acreage dedication running to 2038 (gathering) and 2035 (water). This produces highly visible, commodity-insensitive cash flow: Q1 2026 revenue of $314.2M (+8% YoY), operating cash flow of $238.6M (+20% YoY), and a $0.225/quarter dividend yielding about 4.1% at the current $21.36. The defining strategic event of the year was the simultaneous closing of the $1.1B HG Midstream acquisition and the $400M Utica divestiture in February 2026, a portfolio high-grade toward the core Marcellus that added gathering throughput and on-pad compression economics while raising net debt by roughly $440M.

The investment case has improved at the margin, but not enough to change the call. The single most important variable for AM is the health of its sole customer, and that variable is moving favorably: AR has guided FY2026 production to ~4.1 Bcfe/d and targets ~1x leverage by mid-2026, materially de-risking the concentration that has historically capped AM's multiple. Throughput grew 14% to 3,805 MMcf/d in Q1, aided by HG assets and 72 new well connections. Estimate revisions are modestly positive (next-year EPS +6.0% over 90 days), the dividend is well-covered, and a partial tax-free return-of-capital treatment through 2028 enhances the after-tax yield.

Against that, the stock trades at ~24.9x trailing and ~16.7x forward earnings, roughly in line with or slightly above the peer-median P/E of 18.4, while offering a lower dividend yield (4.1%) than the income-oriented peers in the group (KNTK 7.2%, LPG 7.1%, VNOM 6.0%). Total debt rose to ~$3.69B, the dividend has been flat since early 2025, and there is no open-market insider buying. The setup is a covered, growing-cash-flow income vehicle priced near fair value with limited asymmetry. We rate AM a HOLD (income).

## Company Overview

Antero Midstream owns and operates gathering pipelines, centralized and on-pad compressor stations, equity interests in processing and fractionation, and an integrated water-handling system across West Virginia, Ohio, and Pennsylvania. It reports two segments: Gathering & Processing (~80% of revenue) and Water Handling (~20%).

The structural feature that determines everything about AM is its captive relationship with Antero Resources. AR dedicates substantially all current and future acreage to AM under a 2019 gathering and compression agreement (initial term to 2038) and a water services agreement (to 2035). Fees escalate with CPI, and newly invested capital earns a cost-of-service return targeted at 13%. AR also owns roughly 29% of AM and shares its officers and several directors. AM additionally holds a 50% processing/fractionation JV with MPLX (MarkWest) and a 15% interest in Stonewall Gas Gathering, which together generated about $30M of equity earnings in Q1 2026.

The structure provides contracted, predictable cash flow with minimal direct commodity exposure. The cost is total dependence: AM has no independent revenue engine and no control over AR's development and completion pace. Both the moat and the central risk derive from the same fact.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 was a clean quarter operationally. G&P revenue rose 9% to $250M and Water 3% to $64M. Fresh-water delivery fell 20% on the timing of AR completions, offset by a 61% increase in other water handling. Net income was $118.3M ($0.25/share diluted), roughly flat YoY, depressed by $9M of HG transaction expense and higher interest cost. That produced a small headline EPS miss versus the $0.29 consensus, the third miss or in-line print in four quarters. Operating cash flow is the more important number for a contracted-cash-flow entity.

The balance sheet is the central financial question. Total debt rose to ~$3.69B at Q1 2026 (from $3.25B at YE2025), comprising $442M on the revolver plus five tranches of senior notes (5.375%–6.625%, maturities 2028–2034), with $808M of revolver capacity remaining. Stockholders' equity is $1.94B against $6.4B of total assets. Interest expense climbed to $54M/quarter from $48M after issuing the 2033 and 2034 notes. The company was in compliance with all covenants (maximum total leverage 5.00x). The Altman Z-Score of ~1.4 nominally signals "distress," but that is a false positive for asset-heavy contracted infrastructure; the model penalizes the debt/asset ratio that is normal for this business. The relevant credit metric is leverage against contracted EBITDA, which is within covenants and trending toward management's deleveraging target.

Capital allocation is the income thesis. The dividend is $0.90/year (~$439M paid in 2025), held flat since early 2025 as capital was directed to the HG deal and buybacks. A $500M buyback program (authorized Feb 2024) has ~$318M remaining; ~1M shares were repurchased in Q1 ($18M), slowly shrinking the count from 479M to 475M. The 2026 capex budget is a modest, maintenance-oriented $190–220M. AM expects no material federal or state cash taxes through 2026 due to NOLs, and a portion of distributions through 2028 is a tax-free return of capital, which lifts the effective after-tax yield for taxable holders.

H2 2025 debt management termed out the maturity wall: AM redeemed the 2027 notes at par (Sept 2025) and issued $650M of 2033 notes and $600M of 2034 notes. Refinancing risk now concentrates on the 2028/2029 tranches, manageable given revolver capacity and free cash flow.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is real but bounded, driven by HG integration, well connections, and CPI escalators. Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current-quarter EPS +4.1% over 30 days, next-quarter +9.8% over 90 days, and next-year +6.0% over 90 days, consistent with positive but unspectacular earnings momentum. Consensus FY EPS sits at $1.30 (current year) and $1.49 (next year), implying mid-teens forward growth off the trailing $0.86 base.

These revisions do not approach the +30% / 90-day inflection threshold that would flag a structural re-rating, and AM is not a depressed cyclical recovering off a trough. It is a stable infrastructure compounder whose growth ceiling is set by AR's development pace. AR's guidance and the broader secular tailwind from U.S. LNG and data-center gas demand support a multi-year throughput trajectory, but Appalachian basis remains structurally discounted to Henry Hub due to takeaway constraints, which caps AR's incentive to accelerate. The HG/Utica swap shifts the asset mix toward higher-return Marcellus core gathering and introduces on-pad compression at 13% cost-of-service returns, modestly accretive over time.

A latent upside item is the Veolia litigation: AM (via Antero Treatment) won a ~$280M judgment plus ~$19M in fees over the Clearwater facility, affirmed on appeal, with the Colorado Supreme Court oral argument set for May 2026. A favorable final outcome would be a one-time cash inflow not reflected in operating estimates. The other watch item is dividend growth resumption once leverage targets are met, which would be a clean positive catalyst for an income vehicle that has plateaued its payout.

## Valuation Assessment

AM trades at ~24.9x trailing earnings and ~16.7x forward earnings (on the $1.30 FY consensus). Against the peer-median P/E of 18.4, the forward multiple is roughly in line, while the trailing multiple is a premium. The more telling comparison is yield: at 4.1%, AM offers less income than KNTK (7.2%), LPG (7.1%), VNOM (6.0%), and the 6.0% peer-median yield. DTM, the closest structural comparable (gas-focused midstream C-corp), trades at 31.6x with a 2.5% yield, so AM is cheaper than DTM but on a lower-yield, higher-leverage profile than the income peers.

The cycle-adjusted valuation guidance does not apply here. AM is not at a cyclical trough, estimate revisions are well below the +30% inflection bar, and revenue growth is high-single-digit rather than the 40%+ that would justify discarding trailing multiples. Forward earnings power of $1.30–$1.49 is the right anchor, and at ~16.7x forward the stock is fairly valued rather than mispriced.

The case for upside rests on the AR de-risking story compressing AM's historical concentration discount, plus the underappreciated tax shield and a potential Veolia windfall. The case against rests on the higher leverage versus peers, a flat dividend, and a yield that lags the income-focused names. Analyst targets bracket $20–$26 (mean $23.29, median $23.00), implying roughly 9% upside to the mean from $21.36. That is consistent with a fairly-valued income holding, not an asymmetric long. The current consensus targets are reasonable anchors here, since AM is neither cheap relative to growth nor at a premium multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

AM does not compete in the conventional sense for its dedicated acreage; its volumes are locked to AR through 2038. Competition matters only for growth beyond AR, where MPLX/MarkWest (also AM's JV partner), Williams, and EQT/Equitrans dominate Appalachian midstream. AM's differentiated niche is the integrated water-plus-gathering offering tied to a single producer.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving rather than expanding. The HG acquisition modestly enlarged the AR-dedicated footprint and added on-pad compression economics; the Utica exit shed lower-return assets at an $87M impairment. The regulatory backdrop is currently favorable (NEPA streamlining, methane fee delayed to 2034 under OBBB, OOOOb/c reconsideration), reducing near-term compliance cost. None of this widens the moat in a way the market is mispricing; it preserves a strong but narrow position whose ceiling is AR's drilling cadence and Appalachian takeaway capacity.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-customer concentration is paramount.** ~99% of revenue is AR. Any deterioration in AR's production, balance sheet, credit, or drilling cadence flows directly to AM, which is explicitly "indirectly subject to the business risks of Antero Resources." Under certain conditions AR can dispose of dedicated acreage free of dedication without AM's consent. The mitigant this cycle is genuine: AR's deleveraging and production guidance put the customer in its strongest financial position in years, which lowers the probability of the tail scenario even though the structural exposure is unchanged.

**Leverage and interest-rate exposure.** Floating-rate revolver exposure on the $442M drawn balance; refinancing risk on the 2028/2029 notes; covenant constraints on distributions and buybacks. Interest expense is rising. Within covenants today, but leverage is higher than the income peers.

**HG integration risk** (new in the FY2025 10-K). The deal relied on rep-and-warranty insurance rather than seller indemnity, so undisclosed liabilities are a live exposure, and purchase-price allocation is still preliminary.

**Geographic concentration** — 100% Appalachian, structurally discounted regional gas pricing.

**Related-party governance** — shared officers and directors owe fiduciary duties to AR, which is under no obligation to favor AM.

These are largely known and priced; the concentration and leverage are the reasons AM trades at a midstream multiple rather than a premium one. The incremental new risk is HG integration. The net risk picture is roughly stable, with the concentration risk improving via AR's deleveraging.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and complacent. Near-term ATM IV is 24.4% versus 20.7% trailing realized vol (a modest 3.6pp rich), but IV percentile within the chain is just 4%, the put/call volume ratio is 0.06, and the put/call OI ratio is 0.26 — all skewed toward calls, with no demand for downside protection. Skew is balanced (2.6%) and the term structure is in normal contango (24.4% near vs 29.8% at three months). IV fell 2.7pp into the recent price rise. There is no options-market signal of informed bearish positioning, and nothing that challenges the fundamental view. The implied one-week move of ±2.6% is small, consistent with a low-volatility contracted-cash-flow name.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** AR's deleveraging and production growth de-risk AM's dominant concentration risk, allowing the market to compress AM's historical discount. CPI-escalating fees plus HG throughput and on-pad compression drive mid-teens forward EPS growth toward $1.49 next year. The dividend resumes growth once leverage targets are hit, the tax shield enhances after-tax yield, and a favorable Veolia ruling delivers a ~$280M+ one-time cash windfall. Re-rating toward the high end of the analyst range (~$26) on a sustained 4.5%+ covered, growing yield.

**Bear case.** Appalachian basis weakness or a softer-than-guided AR program slows throughput growth; rising interest expense and the higher post-HG debt load constrain distribution growth and buybacks; an HG integration liability surfaces; and the yield disadvantage versus 6–7% income peers caps the multiple. The flat dividend signals capital constraint rather than discipline. Stock drifts back toward the low-$20s/high-teens (the $20 analyst low / 52-week range $16.77–$23.84).

The probability-weighted center sits near the current price. The fundamental improvement (AR de-risking, positive revisions, covered yield) is real but already substantially reflected in a stock that has risen 17.7% over the trailing year and trades near consensus targets.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release (testing AR's mid-2026 ~1x leverage target and HG run-rate contribution) and the Colorado Supreme Court Veolia outcome.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- AR confirms it reached ~1x leverage with maintained ~4.1 Bcfe/d production, and AM resumes dividend growth (payout above $0.225/quarter), pushing the covered yield toward 4.5%+.
- A favorable final Veolia judgment delivers the ~$280M+ cash inflow, materially accelerating deleveraging and creating buyback/dividend capacity.
- Forward P/E compresses below ~14x on continued throughput growth (price weakness on unchanged fundamentals), restoring asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- AR materially cuts its FY2026 development program or its credit profile deteriorates, threatening AM throughput.
- AM breaches or approaches leverage covenant pressure that forces a dividend cut or buyback suspension.
- A material undisclosed HG liability surfaces, or quarterly throughput growth turns negative on a sustained basis.

## Conclusion

Antero Midstream is a well-run, contracted Appalachian midstream affiliate generating predictable, growing, fee-based cash flow with a covered ~4.1% dividend and a useful tax shield. The key risk — total dependence on Antero Resources — is improving as AR deleverages and grows production, which is the most important positive development in the story and supports the modest upward drift in our composite score. But the stock is fairly valued at ~16.7x forward earnings, roughly in line with the peer-median multiple while offering a lower yield than the income-focused names in the group, carries higher leverage post-HG, has a flat dividend, and shows no insider buying. The risk/reward is balanced near consensus targets, with ~9% to the mean target and comparable downside to the low end.

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on AM, and the bar for continuing it unchanged is appropriately high. The new data (Q1 throughput, AR's deleveraging guidance, positive but sub-inflection estimate revisions) confirms the slow-improving trajectory without producing the catalyst or valuation asymmetry that would justify a BUY. We rate AM a HOLD on an income basis — own it for the covered, modestly growing, tax-advantaged yield, and revisit on dividend-growth resumption, a Veolia windfall, or a meaningful pullback.
