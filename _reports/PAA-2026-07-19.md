**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Plains All American is one of North America's largest crude oil midstream operators, built around the largest Permian Basin crude gathering and long-haul network, storage, terminalling, and a merchant/marketing function. The 2025–2026 story is a deliberate transformation into a crude-oil pure-play: PAA sold its Canadian NGL business to Keyera for roughly C$5.15–5.3B (~$3.3B net proceeds), acquired the EPIC Crude long-haul system (now "Cactus III," >600 Mbpd Permian-to-Corpus Christi) for ~$2.9B including assumed debt, and bolted on several smaller Permian/Eagle Ford assets. The intended result is a more focused, fee-heavy operator with a cleaner balance sheet.

The investment case rests on two mechanical drivers rather than growth. First, the ~$3.3B Keyera proceeds are being directed to debt paydown, taking pro forma leverage from ~4.1x at Q1 2026 back toward ~3.5x by mid-year and the low end of the 3.25x–3.75x target by year-end. Second, the ~$1.67 annualized distribution (~7% yield at the current price) is well covered by ~$2.06B of distributable cash flow against roughly $2.83B of attributable EBITDA. Both are real and largely under management's control. What is missing is near-term growth: 2026 guidance assumes flat Permian production, the durable volume upside is a 2027-and-beyond story tied to gas-takeaway relief and higher recontracting rates, and current-year EPS estimates have been drifting lower.

The stock has appreciated 27% over the trailing twelve months and now trades at ~21.5x trailing earnings with a low-3% net margin (a pass-through artifact of merchant revenue) and near its 52-week high. On forward earnings the multiple looks more reasonable, but the earnings-momentum signals are negative: two consecutive small misses and a current-year estimate cut of ~4.7% over 30 days. We rate PAA a HOLD on an income basis. The ~7% covered yield plus deleveraging is a defensible total-return package, but there is no identified catalyst for meaningful multiple re-rating over the next twelve months, and the unresolved Competition Tribunal challenge to the Keyera close is a live, if low-probability, tail.

## Company Overview

PAA is structured as a master limited partnership managed by its general partner (ultimately Plains GP Holdings, PAGP). The core business is crude oil transportation, storage, terminalling, and merchant/marketing, anchored in the Permian Basin where PAA operates the largest crude gathering and long-haul network. The asset base historically included 20,000+ miles of crude pipelines, ~76 million barrels of storage, and interests in numerous joint ventures (BridgeTex, Cactus, Cheyenne, Saddlehorn).

The strategic pivot in 2025–2026 sharpens PAA into a crude pure-play:
- **Keyera NGL divestiture** — sold the Canadian NGL segment, exiting the more seasonal, commodity-sensitive NGL business. Classified as discontinued operations.
- **EPIC Crude / Cactus III acquisition** — a Permian-to-Corpus Christi long-haul system (plus an earnout), deepening long-haul reach and Gulf Coast export positioning.
- Bolt-ons: Ironwood Midstream (Eagle Ford), Black Knight, additional BridgeTex/Cheyenne interests, and the Wildhorse terminal at Cushing.

The end product is a fee-oriented crude midstream operator levered to Permian production trends and Gulf Coast crude export demand, with a marketing function that monetizes location, quality, and time spreads.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue** tracks crude prices more than volumes because it includes low-margin commodity pass-through from merchant activities: FY2022 ~$57.3B → FY2023 ~$48.7B → FY2024 ~$50.1B → FY2025 ~$44.3B. The decline is a price effect, not a volume story.

**Profitability and cash flow** — the metrics that matter for an MLP:
- FY2025 net income attributable to PAA ~$1.44B, up from ~$772M in 2024 (which carried a ~$345M Line 901 charge plus NGL impairments).
- FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA ~$3.37B (roughly flat vs. ~$3.33B in 2024); attributable EBITDA ~$2.83B.
- Operating cash flow ~$2.94B; implied DCF ~$2.06B, comfortably above the distribution.
- 2026 guidance (raised on the Q1 call): Adjusted EBITDA midpoint $2.88B (up $130M), Adjusted FCF ~$1.85B ex-divestiture proceeds.

**Balance sheet and leverage** are the swing factor. Total debt is ~$11.3B, largely fixed-rate and investment-grade across S&P/Moody's/Fitch. The EPIC/Cactus III acquisition (~$2.9B including ~$1.1B assumed debt) pushed pro forma leverage above the target range. Management is applying the Keyera net proceeds to retire term loan, commercial paper, and a $750M note maturing later in 2026. This deleveraging is the cleanest, most controllable lever in the story.

**Capital allocation** — quarterly distribution of $0.4175/unit. Priority framework: maintain/grow the distribution, fund organic + M&A growth, opportunistically retire Series A preferreds when leverage is at/below the low end, then opportunistic unit buybacks. PAA repurchased ~$333M of Series A preferred in January 2025.

On the financial-health screen, an Altman Z-Score in the distress zone should be discounted: it is largely an MLP-structure artifact, since partners' capital classification drives the equity/asset ratio near zero. The sub-1.0 current ratio and small working-capital deficit are normal for a midstream operator, and the low net margin (~3–4%) reflects pass-through merchant revenue, not operational weakness. The business is investment-grade, generates ~$2.9B of operating cash flow, and covers its distribution. A financial-health score of 6.0 reflects genuine cash generation and IG ratings, tempered by leverage still above target until the paydown completes and the structural leverage a midstream MLP carries.

## Growth Analysis

This is the softest part of the story, and the reason the recommendation is a hold rather than a buy. Management's 2026 guidance explicitly assumes **flat Permian crude production**. The durable volume upside — the ~200–300 Mbpd of Permian production management describes as "behind pipe" awaiting gas-takeaway relief — is skewed to 2027 and beyond, contingent on Waha gas pricing normalizing and the back of the WTI curve firming toward ~$75+. Phased Cactus III/EPIC expansion, contracted at higher rates, is a real but deferred organic lever.

Estimate revisions confirm negative near-term momentum. Current-quarter EPS was revised to $0.40 from $0.42 over 30 days (-6.8%); current-year estimates fell to $1.71 from $1.79 over 30 days (-4.7%) and are essentially flat over 90 days (-0.2%). The one bright spot is next-year: $2.01 current vs. $1.70 ninety days ago (+18.3%), reflecting the annualized benefit of the pure-play mix and Cactus III optimization. The earnings-surprise record is mixed — two beats, two misses over four quarters, with the two most recent quarters both small misses (-5.1% and -3.3%). Near-term consensus is drifting down, not up.

The growth thesis, such as it is, is a 2027+ volume flush plus higher recontracting, layered on the mechanical deleveraging benefit as interest expense falls. That is a legitimate multi-year case, but for a twelve-month horizon it does not carry a growth-hold classification. Growth prospects score 5.2.

## Valuation Assessment

At $23.87 (and near the $24.26 52-week high), PAA trades at a premium to the energy peer median P/E of 19.2 in the supplied comp set (PR 22.4, OVV 19.2, DINO 10.6, DTM 31.1, EXE 6.5). On forward earnings — using the ~$1.71 current-year and ~$2.01 next-year consensus — the multiple compresses to roughly 12x on next-year EPS, which is more defensible for a fee-heavy midstream operator. The dividend yield is well above the peer median of 2.6%, though that comparison is imperfect given the mix of upstream E&Ps and midstream in the peer set. Against the more relevant midstream comp DTM (31.1x P/E, 2.5% yield), PAA's yield support is clearly superior.

The core valuation question is whether the current price offers asymmetric upside. It does not obviously. Analyst targets bracket the current price tightly — low $20.00, mean $24.06, median $24.00, high $27.00 — with the stock already sitting essentially at the mean. On a stock with falling current-year estimates and flat 2026 volumes, consensus targets are not "floors" that lag reality; they reflect a market that broadly appreciates the pure-play pivot and prices the yield-plus-deleveraging package fairly. Estimate revisions of the magnitude that would justify treating targets as stale are absent: the current-year revision is negative, not accelerating upward.

I resist over-crediting the yield as evidence of cheapness. In this analytical framework, a low headline multiple or high yield is a warning to check for a value trap rather than a buy signal on its own. Here the yield is well-covered and not a distress signal, but the absence of a re-rating catalyst and the negative near-term estimate trend argue against a high valuation score. The upside is total-return via the covered distribution and gradual multiple support as leverage falls; the downside is a de-rating if Permian recontracting disappoints or merchant/optimization gains (which management flagged as partly transient) roll off. Valuation scores 5.3 — fairly priced, with the yield providing a floor and limited multiple expansion available.

## Competitive Landscape

PAA competes with Enterprise Products, Energy Transfer, Enbridge, and MPLX in Permian gathering/long-haul and Gulf Coast export. Its durable advantage is scale and integration in the Permian — the ability to capture location, quality, and time spreads across an integrated gathering-to-dock system, augmented by a marketing/trading function. The Cactus III addition extends reach to Corpus Christi export docks, positioning PAA for rising U.S. crude export volumes and improving the security-of-supply thesis management articulated on the Q1 call.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving rather than deteriorating. Management describes "structurally full-pipe" conditions and intent to recontract Permian long-haul at higher rates, which reverses the prior downward tariff-reset pressure if it holds. The residual structural risk is basin egress overbuild forcing recontracting below prior levels. PAA is levered to crude volumes and export demand, and the constructive medium-term crude backdrop management described (SPR restocking, tighter OPEC spare capacity, North America as a security-of-supply source) supports the volume-recovery optionality. Competitive position scores 6.0, holding the prior read after the June decline; the pure-play pivot and Cactus III integration are neutral-to-positive for the moat, though execution on the $100M cost-efficiency program and higher recontracting is unproven.

## Risk Assessment

**Keyera divestiture regulatory overhang (most distinctive current risk).** The deal closed in mid-June 2026 despite the Canadian Competition Bureau's Section 92 application at the Competition Tribunal (filed May 4, 2026) seeking to block it. Closing under active litigation is unusual. Tribunal proceedings were ongoing as of mid-July 2026. For PAA, the residual risk is proceeds/transaction certainty and potential indemnification exposure if the Tribunal orders remedies. Low probability of a material adverse outcome, but genuinely unresolved.

**Permian recontracting / tariff reset.** Legacy long-haul contracts rolling to market have pressured rates. Management is now more optimistic on higher recontracting, but a below-prior reset remains a structural risk if egress capacity overbuilds.

**Volume dependence on flat/uncertain Permian production.** The upside case is deferred to 2027+ and depends on producers re-engaging as WTI back-end firms and gas takeaway constraints (frequently negative Waha pricing) ease.

**Merchant/commodity volatility.** Marketing margins, PLA, and optimization gains fluctuate with price levels and backwardation. Management explicitly cautioned that part of the Q1 EBITDA raise came from transient optimization/frac-spread gains that may not repeat.

**Integration and cost-program execution** on Cactus III/EPIC and the $100M efficiency target ($50M by 2026, $50M by 2027).

**MLP structural and concentration risks.** GP conflicts and inability to remove the GP, K-1 tax reporting, Series A preferred preferences, and customer concentration — ExxonMobil historically ~31% of revenues. The Line 901 legacy spill ($345M 2024 charge) is largely resolved but a reminder of operational/environmental tail risk.

Risk factors score 5.4: the leverage above target (mitigating toward mid-year), the live Tribunal proceeding, ExxonMobil concentration, and the transient nature of some earnings upside collectively exceed a benign profile, but none is a high-probability catastrophic catalyst.

The put/call skew is -6.5% (call-heavy) and the put/call volume ratio is 0.05, both of which lean modestly bullish/neutral. The term structure is flat (22.1% near vs. 21.8% far). The options market does not challenge the income-hold view and is consistent with a low-drama, yield-driven holding.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$27, matching the high analyst target).** Deleveraging completes ahead of schedule, taking leverage to the low end of range and freeing capacity for Series A preferred takeouts and unit buybacks. Permian recontracting lands at higher rates on "full-pipe" conditions, and 2027 volume optionality (the ~200–300 Mbpd behind pipe) begins to materialize as gas takeaway eases and WTI firms. Cactus III phased expansion is contracted accretively. Next-year EPS estimates (already +18.3% over 90 days) prove durable, and the yield plus modest multiple support drives a low-double-digit total return. The Tribunal challenge resolves without remedies.

**Bear Case (~$20, matching the low analyst target, ~16% downside).** Merchant/optimization gains that flattered Q1 roll off, current-year estimates keep drifting lower, and Permian volumes stay flat into 2027 as producers remain disciplined at sub-$75 WTI. Recontracting comes below prior rates amid egress overbuild. The Competition Tribunal orders remedies affecting proceeds/indemnification, complicating the deleveraging path. The stock de-rates from its 52-week-high perch toward the low $20s, with the yield providing the effective floor.

**Base Case (~$24–25).** Leverage normalizes to ~3.5x by mid-year and toward the low end by year-end; the distribution stays well-covered; 2026 volumes are flat as guided; and total return is carried by the yield plus low-single-digit growth from cost efficiencies and next-year mix benefit. The stock trades roughly sideways-to-modestly-higher around consensus targets.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-year and current-quarter EPS estimate revisions turn positive for two consecutive periods, confirming that the negative momentum has reversed rather than the Q1 optimization gains simply rolling off.
- Confirmed leverage reduction to the low end of the 3.25x–3.75x target with explicit commencement of Series A preferred takeouts or unit buybacks, signaling that free cash is being deployed accretively.
- Evidence of higher-rate Permian recontracting materializing in reported segment results, or Cactus III phased expansion contracted at accretive rates.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Competition Tribunal orders structural or behavioral remedies materially affecting the Keyera proceeds or creating indemnification exposure that disrupts the deleveraging plan.
- Distribution coverage deteriorates below ~1.1x DCF/distribution, or management signals a distribution cut.
- Permian recontracting resets below prior levels and next-year EPS estimates reverse the +18.3% 90-day gain.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026) is the primary checkpoint — watch for leverage progress against the mid-year ~3.5x target, distribution coverage, and any update on the Tribunal proceeding. The natural longer-horizon reassessment is year-end 2026, when the deleveraging path should be complete and the 2027 volume-recovery thesis becomes testable against actual guidance.

## Conclusion

PAA is a well-run, investment-grade crude midstream operator delivering a defensible income proposition: a ~7% distribution covered by ~$2.06B of DCF, backed by a scale Permian franchise and a pure-play pivot funded by the Keyera divestiture. The deleveraging from ~4.1x toward ~3.5x is the cleanest lever in the story and is largely under management's control. That combination justifies holding for the yield and balance-sheet repair.

It does not justify a buy at the current price near the 52-week high. Near-term earnings momentum is negative — falling current-year estimates and two consecutive small misses — 2026 assumes flat Permian volumes, and the genuine growth optionality is a 2027-and-beyond volume flush that is not the base case for the next twelve months. The valuation is fair rather than asymmetric, with the stock already sitting at the consensus mean target and no identified catalyst for meaningful re-rating. The unresolved Competition Tribunal challenge to a deal that closed under litigation is a low-probability but live tail. The durable return here is the covered distribution plus gradual deleveraging, which supports an income-oriented HOLD rather than a growth-driven buy. This continues the prior HOLD but the score has softened modestly, consistent with the recent trajectory, as the negative estimate revisions and full valuation offset the improving management-alignment read.

**Recommendation: HOLD (income). Overall score 5.6.**
