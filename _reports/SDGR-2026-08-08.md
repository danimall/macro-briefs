## Executive Summary

Schrödinger reported Q2 2026 net income of $6.0M against a loss from operations of $41.5M. The gap is a $42.1M non-cash fair-value gain on its equity stake in Ajax Therapeutics, acquired by Eli Lilly in June 2026 for consideration of up to $2.3bn, from which Schrödinger received approximately $57.2M in cash ($47.2M for equity on a $4.7M cost basis, plus a $10.0M collaboration milestone). The reported EPS of $0.08 against a $(0.49) consensus is therefore not evidence of operating improvement. The stock has risen 19.9% since July 23 ($15.14 to $18.16), with most of the move occurring in the three sessions around the August 5 print.

The operating picture underneath is ambiguous, and the ambiguity is unresolvable before February 2027. On the constructive side, Q2 annual contract value grew 27% to $29.6M (a filing fact), the on-premise-to-hosted migration mechanically suppresses recognized revenue at unchanged economics, hosted revenue grew 23.8% in H1, and Bunsen — the agentic-AI product launched in early access in July — is being deployed by Bristol-Myers Squibb. On the disconfirming side, FY2025 full-year ACV grew only 4.0% to $198.5M, net dollar retention fell from 113% to 100%, customers above $1M ACV fell from 29 to 27, software remaining performance obligations fell 17.0% in six months ($105.1M to $87.2M), conversion of opening-balance software deferred revenue fell 6.6% year over year, and software gross margin compressed 800bp to 70% on rising cloud costs. Roughly three-quarters of annual ACV lands in H2, concentrated in Q4, so two strong quarters cover about a quarter of the year's billings.

At $18.16 the market pays for the constructive reading. Using the full 74.8M share count including the 9.16M limited common shares (identical economics, 1:1 convertible, no director vote), equity value is $1.36bn — roughly 12% above the $1.19bn quoted on the listed class alone. Net of $418.8M of liquidity and $39.1M of equity investments, core enterprise value is approximately $900M, or 3.5x FY2025 revenue and about 4.0x the $218-228M implied by FY2026 ACV guidance. That is a fair-to-full multiple for a business whose GAAP software line is shrinking, whose drug discovery revenue is the amortization of cash collected in January 2025, and which consumes $100-105M of cash per year. Base case is $16.50; bull $25.00; bear $12.00. Probability-weighted expected return is modestly negative. Trim.

## Company Overview

Schrödinger sells physics-based computational chemistry (free-energy perturbation, molecular dynamics, quantum mechanics) that predicts binding affinity, selectivity and, increasingly, off-target toxicity before compounds are synthesized. Two reportable segments run on that one platform.

**Software** (58% of H1 2026 revenue, $68.1M) licenses to biopharma, industrial/materials companies, academia and government labs. Licensing is capacity-based rather than seat-based: customers buy the right to run simultaneous calculations, so expansion tracks computational throughput rather than headcount. Revenue arrives in four recognition patterns: on-premise licenses upfront, hosted subscriptions ratably, maintenance ratably (and only attached to on-premise), and professional services as consumed. This mix is the source of the current reporting distortion.

**Drug Discovery** (39%, $45.9M) comprises funded collaborations with Bristol-Myers Squibb (Nov 2020), Eli Lilly (Sept 2022) and Novartis (Nov 2024), plus proprietary programs now sharply curtailed. SGR-1505 (MALT1) and SGR-3515 (Wee1/Myt1) will be completed through Phase 1 dose escalation and then partnered; SGR-2921 was discontinued in August 2025; the Q2 10-Q states the company does not intend to initiate additional trials or advance other preclinical programs independently.

A third value stream is not a segment but drives reported earnings: equity stakes in biotechs Schrödinger co-founded or served, taken partly in lieu of cash. Nimbus produced FY2023's $40.7M net income; Ajax produced Q2 2026's. Remaining holdings are Structure Therapeutics ($36.5M) and Nimbus ($2.4M), down from $73.6M in aggregate at December 31, 2025.

The market data classifies SDGR under Health Care Services. Peer medians drawn from AMN, ADUS, NEO, MD and PGNY (median P/E 17.2x) have no bearing on a loss-making 70%-gross-margin software and discovery-services business, and I do not use them.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2021-25: $137.9M, $181.0M, $216.7M, $207.5M, $255.9M. FY2025's +23.3% was not organic software strength: management stated software grew 11% while total grew 23%, the difference being drug discovery recognition against the Novartis upfront.

H1 2026 versus H1 2025 ($M):

| Line | 2026 | 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| On-premise software | 23.5 | 41.2 | −43.0% |
| Hosted software | 27.2 | 22.0 | +23.8% |
| Maintenance | 11.5 | 13.6 | −15.0% |
| Professional services | 5.9 | 4.3 | +37.4% |
| **Software products & services** | **68.1** | **81.0** | **−15.9%** |
| Drug discovery | 45.9 | 24.2 | +89.7% |
| Contribution (Gates) | 3.5 | 9.1 | −61.6% |
| **Total** | **117.5** | **114.3** | **+2.8%** |

The mechanism behind the software decline is legitimate: a customer shifting a $10M on-premise license (recognized at once) to a $10M hosted subscription (recognized ratably) reports roughly half the revenue in the transition year at identical economics, and maintenance falls because it attaches only to on-premise. Hosted +23.8% against on-premise −43.0% is consistent with that. What weakens the pure-artifact reading is that software RPO (backlog plus deferred revenue) fell 17.0% over the same six months. A migration to ratable delivery should build contracted balances. Some of that decline is seasonal (December 31 is the peak billing balance and the filings do not provide a June 30, 2025 comparative), so I treat it as a partial rather than decisive contradiction, but it is the single strongest disconfirming datapoint available.

**Segment gross profit is the number that matters.** H1 software gross profit fell $14.6M to $48.2M (−23.3%), with margin down from 78% to 70% on a $1.9M increase in cloud computing cost of revenues. Drug discovery gross profit swung from −$5.5M to +$13.8M, but that swing is powered by the $10.0M Ajax milestone and $20.5M of Novartis research services recognized against a $150M upfront collected in January 2025. Software gross profit funds platform R&D, and it is shrinking.

Operating expenses fell only 5.4% ($152.3M from $161.1M) despite the May 2025 restructuring and the SGR-2921 discontinuation. External program costs fell 23% to $18.9M, but "all other research and development" (platform investment) rose 2% to $47.2M. The roughly $70M of announced savings is not yet visible in aggregate opex.

**Cash flow, corrected.** FY2025 reported operating cash flow of +$13.9M against ~$1.4M capex looks like breakeven. It is not. Changes in operating assets and liabilities contributed +$118.2M, principally collection of the Novartis upfront billed in December 2024 (receivables fell from $235.7M to $83.0M). Pre-working-capital operating cash flow was approximately −$104.3M. H1 2026 operating cash flow was −$48.9M. Cross-checking against the balance sheet: liquidity rose $16.5M in H1 ($402.3M to $418.8M), but $67.0M of that came from equity-investment proceeds and $1.2M from option exercises, implying operations plus capex consumed roughly $51.7M in six months. Two independent periods triangulate to $100-105M of annual cash consumption.

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $640.4M, liabilities $311.4M, equity $329.0M, accumulated deficit $682.9M, current ratio 2.71. There are **no borrowings**. A screen showing "debt/equity ~0.99" is measuring total liabilities to equity; of the $311.4M, $149.9M is deferred revenue (discharged by delivering software and research, not cash) and $108.0M is operating lease liabilities. Liquidity of $418.8M alone exceeds every liability including leases. The Altman Z-score of ~1.44 is arithmetically correct but misspecified here: the retained-earnings and EBIT terms push the composite below 1.81 regardless of the absence of debt. What it legitimately flags is inability to self-fund; what it overstates is insolvency risk for a debt-free issuer with roughly four years of runway. The Leerink ATM has $241.1M available and has been used for only $8.7M net since inception, with nothing drawn in H1 2026 or H1 2025.

**Commitments.** $155.5M of lease obligations through December 2037 (New York City $121.8M; Hyderabad discovery facility $21.8M) plus an $82.0M three-year cloud compute minimum signed in December 2025 — roughly $259M total, about $27M/yr of cloud spend committed as software revenue turned negative and cloud cost drove the 800bp margin compression. This is a purchase commitment, not indebtedness: no covenants, no maturity wall. It is also the clearest fixed cost that becomes stranded if hosted adoption and Bunsen monetization disappoint.

**Earnings surprise record, interpreted.** Three beats in four quarters ($0.08 vs $(0.49); $0.45 vs $(0.19); $(0.45) vs $(0.72)) with one miss ($(0.81) vs $(0.65)). The two large beats are investment-mark driven and unforecastable by management's own admission. This record should not raise confidence in forward operating estimates.

## Growth Analysis

The bull growth case rests on ACV. Q2 2026 ACV of $29.6M versus $23.3M (+27%) is a filing fact; Q1's $28.4M (+12%) and FY2026 guidance of 10-15% ACV growth come from secondary reporting of the August 6 call and are unverified against a primary release. Taking them at face value, H1 ACV was approximately $58.0M versus $48.7M, or +19.2%. Against FY2025 ACV of $198.5M, H1 2025 represented only about 24.5% of the annual figure, so roughly three-quarters of billings land in H2 and concentrate in Q4 with calendar-year enterprise renewals. Two good quarters cover a quarter of the year. The 10-Q itself warns ACV "can be impacted by contract execution start and end dates and renewal rates," and with two customers at 25% and 17% of Q2 revenue, one large multi-year renewal can move the metric materially.

Against that: FY2025 ACV grew 4.0%, net dollar retention fell to 100% from 113%, and the count of customers above $1M ACV fell from 29 to 27. Those are same-basis annual measures and they are weak. My reading is that demand deflated in 2025 under pharma R&D budget pressure and is recovering in 2026, with the GAAP decline layered on top as a recognition artifact. Both can be true; neither is proven at the required scale.

Drug discovery growth is the amortization of cash already received. Of H1's $45.9M, $10.0M was the one-time Ajax milestone and $20.5M was Novartis research services against the January 2025 upfront. Lilly's March 2026 termination of the February 2025 added target *increased* recognized revenue via accelerated completion of obligations. Meanwhile drug discovery deferred revenue fell from $117.6M to $89.2M, drug discovery RPO fell 27.4% to $97.8M, and Novartis deferred revenue net of contract assets fell from $100.5M to $80.0M. At H1's $20.5M-per-half recognition rate the remaining balance supports roughly two more years, a sensitivity rather than a forecast, since recognition is cost-based and lumpy and only about half of drug discovery RPO converts within twelve months. Absent a new large upfront, this segment steps down in 2027-28.

New product surface area is real but unmonetized: Bunsen in early access since July 2026 with a full commercial release expected by year-end, and predictive toxicology (~50 representative kinases plus anti-targets) launched commercially in 2026 off $24.5M of Gates Foundation funding. The Gates relationship is philanthropic funding accounted for under ASC 958, not commercial demand, and contribution revenue fell 61.6% in H1 as allocated funds depleted.

Contingent milestones total approximately $3.174bn gross (Novartis $2.272bn, BMS $482M, Lilly $420M). None were assessed as probable at June 30, 2026. Twenty months into Novartis, zero milestone revenue has been recognized; BMS has produced $32.0M over 5.5 years. Any credit must be heavily probability- and time-discounted.

Consensus revisions are mixed and thin: current-year EPS improved to $(1.83) from $(2.23) 90 days ago and next-year to $(1.35) from $(1.56), but both are single-analyst estimates. The four-analyst quarterly estimates moved the other way: current quarter cut 14.2% in 30 days to $(0.72), next quarter cut sharply to $(0.24). There is no +30% revision inflection signal here on a revenue or operating basis.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is real in its niche. Free-energy perturbation predicts affinity from thermodynamics rather than from training data, which matters most in novel chemical space where data is sparse. Capacity-based licensing is structurally superior to per-seat SaaS because a customer doubling computational throughput doubles license need. The customer roster (Novartis, BMS, Lilly at minimum) validates enterprise-grade adoption, and predictive toxicology extends the platform beyond potency prediction.

Trajectory is the concern. Net dollar retention of 100% means the capacity-expansion model delivered no net expansion in FY2025. Two seven-figure accounts were lost. Collaboration surface area has contracted in one direction: BMS went from five initial targets plus a sixth added in December 2022 to one remaining neurology target; Lilly terminated its February 2025 added target in March 2026 for strategic reasons. Partner discontinuations are normal in discovery, but each permanently removes milestone-bearing surface area and the direction has been uniformly negative. Novartis is now the sole large intact collaboration. Management said in February 2026 that "the drug discovery AI landscape is expanding rapidly," and the filings carry a dedicated AI/ML risk factor covering the technology as both product and threat. Bunsen combines physics with agentic AI, and BMS deploying it is a real commercial datapoint, but it is a product deployment by a customer, not shared ownership or a revenue commitment of disclosed size.

Geographic mix in H1 2026: US $68.9M (+8.2%), EMEA $34.6M (−5.7%), APAC $13.4M (+2.5%). EMEA weakness bears tracking given European pharma's exposure to the tariff regime described in the filings (Proclamation 11020's default 100% ad valorem duty on covered patented pharmaceuticals, staged effective July 31 and September 29, 2026), which compresses the R&D budget pool from which software licenses are funded.

## Risk Assessment

**Concentration is worsening on both sides of the ledger.** Revenue concentration moved from one customer at 19% (H1 2025) to two at 21% and 20% (H1 2026); Q2 2026 shows two at 25% and 17%. Receivables went from one customer at 10% (12/31/25) to two at 27% and 23% (6/30/26), roughly half of receivables in two names. Contract assets show three customers at 25%/15%/11%. The revenue *mix* shift toward drug discovery does not constitute diversification; the direct concentration measures are worse than a year ago.

**Reported profitability depends on non-operating gains, and the buffer has halved.** Q2's net income sat against an operating loss. Equity investments fell from $73.6M to $39.1M, including a $10.8M markdown on the retained Structure position. H1's $67.0M of investment proceeds is the only reason liquidity rose despite operating cash use. Management describes future distributions as difficult to predict. Two realizations in three years (Nimbus 2023, Ajax 2026) argue this is repeatable but not modellable.

**Fixed commitments against a shrinking gross profit line.** $259M non-cancelable, including the $82.0M cloud minimum, against software gross profit that fell $14.6M year over year in six months.

**Governance and turnover.** Five Item 5.02 8-Ks in ten months (2025-09-08, 2026-03-12, 2026-05-20, 2026-06-08, 2026-06-23), of which one is confirmed as a Transition, Separation and Release of Claims Agreement dated June 5, 2026 with Mannix Aklian. The roles and reasons behind the others are unresolved in the documents reviewed. A director receives consulting fees of $330K in H1 2026, up from $218K — financially immaterial, an independence consideration. Insiders and >5% holders own 39.9% of common, 47.3% including limited common. Core IP depends on maintaining Columbia University licenses where co-founder and director Richard Friesner is the inventor.

**Timeline risk.** The decisive datapoint, Q4 2026 ACV, is not reported until February 2027. Nothing between now and the November Q3 print resolves the central question.

**On the other side:** no borrowings, no covenants, no material legal proceedings, disclosure controls concluded effective at 6/30/2026, the FY2022 material weakness remediated in 2023 with no recurrence, and roughly four years of liquidity.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is thin (32 contracts across four expirations, put/call open-interest ratio 0.01), so this is corroboration at best. Near-term ATM IV collapsed from 77.8% (July 23) to 45.9% post-print, is 6.5 points below one-year realized volatility of 52.3%, and sits in the 6th percentile of the current chain. Term structure is in contango (45.9% at one month rising to 61.2% at six months), and the nearest expiry prices a ±8.0% move. Skew is call-heavy at the one-month and six-month tenors (with slightly put-leaning skew at three months), and the put/call volume ratio is 0.08. Positioning is directionally optimistic and short-dated protection is inexpensive relative to realized volatility. For holders who want to keep exposure through the November print rather than sell outright, that combination favors buying protection over paying up for it. The absence of put demand is not independent confirmation of the bull case, particularly with 25.3% of float short and 7.5 days to cover; the call-heavy tilt is at least as consistent with squeeze positioning as with informed conviction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($25.00, +37.7%).** The GAAP software decline is a recognition artifact and nothing more. Q4 2026 ACV confirms mid-teens growth on the full-year base, validating that the hosted transition has been building a higher-quality ratable revenue stream; software RPO turns up as hosted contracts accumulate; software gross margin recovers as cloud spend scales against a larger hosted base. Bunsen's full commercial release converts the BMS deployment into a repeatable expansion motion, and predictive toxicology adds a second monetizable module. A Novartis milestone lands from the $2.272bn gross pool, and the equity-stake flywheel produces another realization. The market re-rates a debt-free platform with $5.60/share of liquidity from 3.9x to 6x forward revenue.

**Bear case ($12.00, −33.9%).** FY2025's 4% ACV growth, 100% net dollar retention and loss of two seven-figure accounts were the true signal, and the 2026 ACV re-acceleration reflects the timing of a small number of large multi-year contracts rather than broad demand recovery. Q4 ACV disappoints. Software RPO's 17% six-month decline proves to be real contraction rather than seasonality. The Novartis deferred balance runs down through 2027-28 with no replacement upfront, drug discovery revenue steps down, and the $82M cloud minimum becomes stranded cost against declining software gross profit. Pharma budget pressure from tariffs and MFN pricing keeps net retention at or below 100%. With the investment portfolio down to $39.1M, no non-cash gains mask the operating loss, and the market values the operating business at 2.5x.

**What tips the balance to trim.** The fundamentals have not deteriorated materially since the July report, but the price is 20% higher, and the specific new disclosures cut the wrong way: concentration worse on revenue and receivables, total RPO down 21.2% in six months, five executive-transition filings in ten months, and the monetizable investment buffer halved. The GAAP profit that drove the rally was a realization event. Base case sits 9% below spot, and the probability-weighted return is negative.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength rather than around a dated catalyst: the next scheduled event (Q3, November 2026) is not decisive, and the decisive one (Q4 ACV, February 2027) is six months out with the stock already pricing the constructive reading. For holders who want to retain a position through the Bunsen commercial release, reduce to a starter weight and use the cheap near-term optionality noted above (45.9% IV versus 52.3% realized) rather than paying full exposure. Do not short: 25.3% short interest with 7.5 days to cover, a debt-free balance sheet, four years of runway, and $3.17bn of gross milestone optionality make the upside tail too fat for a borrow-carrying position.

**Targets.** Base $16.50 (−9.1%); bull $25.00 (+37.7%); bear $12.00 (−33.9%). Timeframe 12-18 months, anchored on the February 2027 FY2026 disclosure of full-year ACV, net dollar retention and the count of customers above $1M ACV.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better.**
1. Q4 2026 / FY2026 ACV growth of 12% or better with net dollar retention back above 105% and the >$1M customer count at 29 or higher.
2. Software RPO stabilizing or rising sequentially at December 31, 2026 versus the $87.2M at June 30, alongside software gross margin recovering above 74%.
3. A price in the low teens ($12-13) that restores the margin of safety against the base case; at $13, base case upside is 27%.
4. A disclosed Novartis milestone assessed as probable, or a new collaboration upfront of $75M+ that replaces the amortizing Novartis balance.

**Avoid triggers (move to AVOID).**
1. FY2026 ACV growth below 8% with net dollar retention below 100%, confirming the 2025 deflation was structural.
2. Material ATM utilization (more than $75M of the $241.1M available) at prices below $15, indicating funding need ahead of the transition inflection.
3. Loss of Novartis as an active collaboration, or a further reduction in the BMS/Lilly target count to zero active programs.
4. Operating expense reduction failing to appear by Q4 2026 — i.e., FY2026 opex within 3% of FY2025's [unverified — actual: N/A] full-year opex base — which would push burn above $110M and shorten the runway inside three years.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q3 2026 results in November 2026 for opex discipline, software gross margin and any Bunsen commercial detail; the full-year report in February 2027 for the decisive ACV, retention and >$1M customer disclosures.

## Conclusion

Schrödinger is a debt-free platform company with genuine technical differentiation, $5.60 per share of liquidity, roughly four years of runway, and an unusual venture-style equity flywheel that has now produced two realizations. It is also a business whose GAAP software revenue fell 15.9% in the first half, whose contracted balances fell 17-27% in six months, whose customer concentration worsened on both the revenue and receivable side, whose reported profit came from a non-cash investment mark, and which consumes $100-105M of cash annually with no disclosed path to operating breakeven.

The central question — whether the revenue decline is a recognition artifact of the hosted migration or the visible edge of real demand contraction — is open, and it does not resolve until Q4 2026 ACV is reported in February 2027. At $15.14 three weeks ago that ambiguity was reasonably priced. At $18.16, with core enterprise value at roughly 3.5x FY2025 revenue and 4.0x forward ACV, the market pays for the favorable resolution in advance. Base case of $16.50 is below the current price, the probability-weighted return is modestly negative, and the score declines to 4.77 from 5.0 on risk/reward rather than on any claim that the platform is broken. **REDUCE.** Revisit at $12-13, or earlier on evidence that ACV growth and software RPO are inflecting together.
