## Executive Summary

Texas Capital Bancshares has executed a genuine two-year operating turnaround. Between FY2023 and FY2025, net interest income rose 12.5%, non-interest income rose 40.7%, expenses grew 1.5%, and the efficiency ratio improved 920 basis points from 70.4% to 61.2%. Investment banking and advisory fees stepped up from roughly $24M per quarter in mid-2025 to a $30-34M range that has now held for four consecutive quarters. The company paid its first common dividend since operations began in 1998 and has returned 71.6% of first-half net income to common shareholders while holding CET1 essentially flat. None of that is in dispute.

The problem is what the market is paying for it, and what the most recent quarter says about the trajectory. At $99.39 the stock trades at 1.291x tangible book value of $76.98 against a Q2 2026 adjusted return on average common equity of 9.80%, and with negligible intangibles ROTCE is effectively identical. A zero-growth Gordon solve implies a cost of equity of 7.6% to justify that multiple; at a more conventional 10% cost of equity, 9.8% ROTCE supports a price-to-tangible-book below 1.0x. The current price therefore embeds continued ROTCE expansion. The Q2 print pushed in the opposite direction: net interest margin fell 15bp to 3.28%, incremental balance-sheet growth is being priced at roughly a 1.4% net spread, the provision of $18.0M barely exceeded $16.1M of net charge-offs while criticized loans increased, and total capital fell 144bp to 14.68%. Consensus has responded: current-quarter EPS estimates are down 7.5% over 90 days, current-year down 3.0% to $7.40, next-year down 1.5% to $8.23.

Three further items shift the risk/reward. First, the entire first-half deposit build was wholesale: brokered deposits went from 2.8% to 11.6% of total deposits (+$2.63B) while customer deposits declined $163.6M, with most of the proceeds parked at the Federal Reserve at 3.64% against a 3.99% marginal cost on time deposits. Second, roughly 5.8 of the 15.3 percentage points of year-over-year adjusted EPS growth, about 38%, came from a 4.66% reduction in diluted share count, and those repurchases at a $97.01 average against ~$76 tangible book were dilutive to TBV per share by roughly $0.49 in the half. Third, the CEO sold 98,600 shares for approximately $10.0M across two June sessions at $101-102, cutting reported direct holdings 37%, with no offsetting open-market purchase by any insider. Base case of $105 offers 5.6% upside; the bear case at 1.0x tangible book is $79, or 20.5% downside. That asymmetry, with negative revision momentum and a 0.81% dividend against a 2.9% peer median, warrants trimming.

## Company Overview

Texas Capital Bancshares is the Dallas-based holding company for Texas Capital Bank, a Texas state-chartered institution, and TCBI Securities (Texas Capital Securities), a registered broker-dealer. Assets were $31.5B at 12/31/2025 and $33.9B at 6/30/2026, with 1,785 employees.

The defining structural feature is a branch-lite commercial model with no acquisition history. Banking offices are limited to Austin, Dallas, Fort Worth, Houston and San Antonio. Goodwill and intangibles total $1.496 million on a $33.9B balance sheet, which makes book value and tangible book value functionally identical ($77.01 vs. $76.98 per share) and eliminates goodwill-impairment risk. The 10-K acknowledges that the branch-lite model makes Community Reinvestment Act compliance "more challenging," which matters because CRA ratings gate acquisition approvals.

Business lines: core commercial banking in five Texas metros plus national verticals; mortgage finance (warehouse lending to originators, $6.383B or 25.6% of loans held for investment at 6/30/26, 100% pass-graded, ~20-day average holding period, with H1 2026 originations of $51.43B against payoffs of $51.11B); homebuilder finance; corporate and investment banking with sales and trading; Bask Bank, a digital deposit brand paying American Airlines AAdvantage miles in lieu of cash interest, where the bank is the counterparty purchasing miles rather than a partner in the loyalty program; and wealth management and trust.

Effective September 19, 2025, the Bank became a Federal Reserve member, replacing the FDIC as primary federal regulator. The filing does not state the rationale, and I have no basis for preferring any interpretation.

Management's stated strategy is to become the leading full-service financial services firm in Texas by selling capital-markets, treasury and wealth products into commercial relationships. The 10-K concedes competitors have greater financial resources, lending limits, technology and branch networks. Legal lending limit is approximately $592.7M; internal house limits generally run $20-60M.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2024 is not a usable comparison base.** It contained a $179.6M loss on sale of available-for-sale securities from a Q3 2024 repositioning, which collapsed non-interest income to $31.0M and diluted EPS to $1.28. Growth rates anchored on 2024 overstate underlying improvement. The clean read is 2023 → 2025: net income to common $171.9M → $313.0M, diluted EPS $3.54 → $6.79, ROA 0.64% → 1.04%, ROE 6.15% → 9.59%, efficiency 70.4% → 61.2%.

**On the reported "miss."** The tabulated surprise data shows Q2 2026 EPS of $1.83 against a $1.90 estimate (-3.7%). This is a basis mismatch. Adjusted (non-GAAP) EPS was $1.88, and one outlet reported that as 1.3% above consensus, implying a non-GAAP consensus near $1.86. Comparing GAAP $1.83 to a non-GAAP-basis estimate produces a phantom miss. On a like-for-like non-GAAP basis the quarter was a marginal beat. The four-quarter surprise record does show real deceleration: +20.5%, +17.7%, +9.0%, then roughly flat. The era of 15-20% beats has ended.

**Earnings composition.** Adjusted EPS rose 15.3% year over year ($1.63 → $1.88) while adjusted net income to common rose 9.5% ($75.5M → $82.7M). Diluted shares fell 4.66% (46.215M → 44.062M). Roughly 5.8 percentage points of the 15.3%, about 38%, came from share count reduction. This is legitimate value creation only if the repurchase price is attractive relative to intrinsic value; at $97.01 average against ~$76 tangible book, that is a stretch.

**Margin decomposition.** The 15bp sequential NIM decline was mix, not spread erosion on the core book. Loans held for investment excluding mortgage finance yielded 6.62%, down only 2bp sequentially. Compression came from $1.071B of average mortgage finance growth at a 4.06% yield and $0.358B of average interest-bearing cash growth at 3.64%. Taxable-equivalent net interest income rose $5.608M (+2.2%) while average earning assets rose $1,652M (+5.5%); annualizing the increment ($22.4M) over the incremental assets implies a marginal net spread near 1.36%, roughly 200bp below portfolio NIM. That calculation attributes the whole quarterly NII change to the increment and ignores repricing on the existing book, so treat it as directional. Growth is arriving at thin spreads in low-risk-weight assets: NIM-dilutive, not necessarily ROE-dilutive, but not the kind of growth that expands ROTCE toward the level the current multiple requires.

Year over year the mix is less benign: core loan yields fell 20bp (6.82% → 6.62%) and mortgage finance yields fell 18bp, offset by a 31bp reduction in total deposit cost (2.71% → 2.40%). The deposit-cost tailwind is finite; asset-yield erosion is not.

**Rate sensitivity is asymmetric.** 95.7% of loans ($23.873B of $24.947B) are variable rate. Modeled annualized NII change: +200bp → +5.1% (down from +7.4% a year earlier); +100bp → +2.6%; -100bp → -5.8%; -200bp → -10.8%. A -100bp scenario against ~$1.04B of annualized NII is roughly -$60M pre-tax, about -$1.05 per share after tax at a 24% rate over 43.47M shares. Upside sensitivity has been materially reduced (partly by the February swap on the new senior notes); downside sensitivity has barely improved. The 10-K notes three Fed cuts in 2024 and three in 2025; the earnings asymmetry runs against the shareholder if easing continues.

**Fee income: a real level shift with one opaque line.** Investment banking and advisory fees by quarter: $24.1M → $34.0M → $30.0M → $32.0M → $31.5M. H1 2026 of $63.5M compares to $40.6M (+56.5%). Four quarters of persistence make this a platform change rather than a spike. Two cautions. First, terminology: management's "investment banking fees of $42.8 million, up 34%" reconciles exactly to GAAP investment banking and advisory of $31.522M plus trading income of $11.313M, an aggregate metric, not the GAAP line. Three different "fee income" figures circulate for the same quarter ($56.8M on the company's footnoted definition, $60.5M in the IR script, $42.8M for the IB aggregate). Second, of the $21.049M year-over-year increase in non-interest income, $6.551M (31%) came from an undisaggregated "Other" line ($16.191M vs. $9.640M), more than trading and wealth management combined. That is a quality-of-earnings question requiring an answer, not an allegation.

**Funding is the most consequential finding.**

| | 12/31/2025 | 6/30/2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Total deposits | $26,448.8M | $28,911.4M |
| — Customer | $25,719.6M (97.2%) | $25,556.0M (88.4%) |
| — Brokered | $729.2M (2.8%) | ~$3,355.4M (11.6%) |
| Interest-bearing cash | $1,897.8M | $3,488.8M |

All of the deposit growth, and more, came from brokered channels; customer deposits fell $163.6M. Average time deposits rose from $2,020.8M to $3,172.4M sequentially at a 3.99% cost. Loans grew $907.0M while interest-bearing cash grew $1,591.0M; the marginal dollar was raised at ~4% and deployed at 3.64%. Two readings are consistent with the disclosure: pre-funding liquidity insurance against mortgage-finance month-end spikes (period-end balances swung from $6.962B on 3/31/26 to $6.383B on 6/30/26 against a Q1 average of only $5.239B, and the 10-K explicitly warns these spikes could push capital ratios below internal targets), or core deposit softness papered over with wholesale funding. Neither is verified.

There is a measurement trap: estimated uninsured deposits fell from 42% to 37% of total. That is very likely mechanical, because brokered deposits are typically placed in insured increments, and should not be read as improved funding durability. The company's own risk factors state brokered deposits are "more sensitive to interest rates, with a higher risk of withdrawal."

Longer-term, average non-interest-bearing deposits have declined from $9.815B (FY2023) to $9.013B (FY2024) to $8.220B (FY2025) to $7.776B (H1 2026), a 20.8% erosion in the cheapest funding source over two and a half years. Contingent liquidity remains ample: $6.843B net FHLB capacity, $1.557B unused fed funds lines, $10.801B unused Federal Reserve capacity.

**Capital was deliberately reduced.** CET1 12.07% (vs. 12.13%), Tier 1 13.51% (13.60%), total capital 14.68% (16.12%), leverage 11.65% (unchanged), TCE/tangible assets 9.9% (10.6%). The 144bp total-capital decline reflects liability management: $134.5M of Bank 5.25% subordinated notes matured in Q1; $400M of 5.301% fixed-to-floating senior notes were issued February 27, 2026 and swapped to floating; $372.66M of 4.00% Company subordinated notes due 2031 were redeemed in Q2 with a $2.157M extinguishment charge. Implied RWA of $28,542M (CET1 capital $3,445.1M ÷ 12.07%) makes the redeemed notes 1.31% of RWA, bracketing the observed decline. The trade replaced Tier 2 capital with non-capital senior debt at a higher coupon; average long-term debt cost rose from 4.90% to 5.60%, roughly $4.2M annualized pre-tax. A coherent explanation is that the 2031 notes were approaching the five-year Tier 2 phase-out and were becoming expensive non-capital; an equally coherent one is a preference for holding-company liquidity flexibility with CET1 unaffected. I am not inferring motive.

Bank-level capital fell more than consolidated (Bank CET1 13.01% → 12.35%; Bank CET1 dollars -$114.5M while consolidated CET1 dollars rose $50.6M), consistent with dividends upstreamed to fund buybacks, the new common dividend and the note redemption. Magnitude unverified.

**Held-to-maturity marks.** HTM amortized cost $692.3M against $616.8M fair value, a $75.5M unrecognized loss (10.9% below carrying value) outside AOCI and capital. AFS gross unrealized losses are $97.6M. The company elects to exclude AOCI from regulatory capital. Marking both portfolios ($173.1M pre-tax, $131.6M after tax) against $28,542M of RWA is 46bp, implying a mark-inclusive CET1 near 11.6% versus 12.07% reported. This is a partial adjustment, because AOCI also holds hedge marks that offset asset repricing, but it is the right direction for an economic capital read.

**Tangible book value deceleration.** Management highlighted TBV/share of $76.98, up 10% year over year and a ninth consecutive record. Both claims are accurate; the composition is less flattering. Tangible common equity grew only $16.3M in H1 2026 ($3,329.9M → $3,346.2M) despite $150.1M of net income to common: +$150.1M earnings, -$8.7M dividends, -$98.7M buybacks, -$34.3M AOCI deterioration, +~$7.9M issuance and stock compensation. AOCI moved from -$64.6M to -$98.9M, including $24.7M of unrealized cash flow hedge losses, partially reversing the $48.3M AOCI improvement that flattered H2 2025. Repurchasing at $97.01 against ~$76 tangible book consumed roughly $21M of tangible equity above book, about $0.49 per share of TBV dilution. Net: TBV per share grew 2.3% in the half (roughly 4.7% annualized) versus 9.75% over the trailing year. Separately, approximately $10.4M is expected to reclassify out of AOCI over the next twelve months as a decrease to interest income.

**On the health screen.** An Altman Z-Score in the distress range and 7.7x debt-to-equity are structurally inapplicable to a depository institution; the formula relies on working capital and sales-to-assets and penalizes the leverage that constitutes the business model. Every solvent US bank screens as distressed. The regulatory ratios and TCE/TTA of 9.9% are the correct solvency measures. The screen's ROA (1.03%), ROE (9.56%), operating-cash-flow-to-net-income and margin components remain informative.

## Growth Analysis

Three distinguishable growth engines, with different durability.

**Fee platform (durable, cyclical).** Non-interest income reached 22.4% of revenue versus 17.6% a year earlier. Wealth management is up 37.7% year over year for a fourth consecutive quarter, but at roughly $5.1M quarterly (~$20M annualized) it is not yet material. The four-quarter persistence of the IB step-up is real evidence of platform build. The offset: FY2024 to FY2025 investment banking was flat ($105.0M → $104.6M) after a 65% FY2024 surge, and the 10-K states plainly that investment banking revenue "is directly related to general economic conditions." Management's framing of these revenues as providing "revenue stability through economic cycles" is half correct: capital-light, yes; stable, no.

**Balance sheet (growing, low-return).** Loans held for investment grew $907.0M in the half, but at an implied marginal net spread near 1.4%, concentrated in mortgage finance at 4.06% and Fed cash at 3.64%. Volume growth of this composition does not lift ROTCE toward 12%.

**Buybacks (finite).** $102.04M remains on the $200M authorization expiring 12/31/2026, roughly 1.03M shares or 2.4% of shares outstanding at current prices. This supports perhaps 2-3 points of 2026 EPS growth and then requires re-authorization.

**Consensus is going the wrong way.** Current-quarter estimates: $2.09 (90d ago) → $2.07 (30d) → $1.93, a 7.5% cut. Current year: $7.63 → $7.57 → $7.40 (-3.0%). Next year: $8.36 → $8.33 → $8.23 (-1.5%). Twelve analysts, all four horizons negative, with the near end deteriorating fastest. Next-year consensus of $8.23 against trailing four-quarter GAAP of $7.69 implies about 7% growth, of which a meaningful share is share count. This is the mirror image of the inflection pattern that would justify paying above tangible book for a single-digit ROTCE.

## Valuation Assessment

**Multiples on the $99.39 basis:**

- P/TBV: $99.39 ÷ $76.98 = **1.291x** (P/B identical given $1.5M of intangibles)
- Trailing four-quarter GAAP EPS $7.69 → **12.9x**; adjusted $7.72 → **12.9x**
- Current-year consensus $7.40 → **13.4x**
- Next-year consensus $8.23 → **12.1x**
- Dividend: $0.80 annualized → **0.81% yield**

**Peer context.** Regional bank peers of similar market cap trade at a median 12.6x P/E with a 2.9% median yield: UCB 12.6x/2.9%, FBP 11.1x/3.2%, CATY 12.3x/2.6%, INDB 16.2x/3.1%, RNST 17.6x/2.3%. TCBI at 12.9x trailing carries no discount, and its yield is roughly one-quarter of the peer median. The apparent cheapness relative to INDB and RNST is a composition artifact, because those two carry acquisition-related intangibles and different asset mixes.

**The binding relationship is return on tangible equity against price-to-tangible-book.** Q2 ROACE was 9.56% GAAP, 9.80% adjusted, and with negligible intangibles ROTCE is essentially the same. A zero-growth solve on 1.291x P/TBV requires a cost of equity of 7.6% (9.80% ÷ 1.291). At a 10% cost of equity with zero growth, 9.80% ROTCE supports P/TBV of 0.98x. The gap between 0.98x and 1.29x is the market pricing continued ROTCE expansion: fee income compounding, efficiency holding in the low 60s or better, and credit staying benign. The Q2 evidence weakens each leg: NIM down 15bp with marginal spread at 1.4%, adjusted efficiency at 60.4% rather than the 56% run-rate of H2 2025, and provision only $1.9M above net charge-offs while criticized loans increased.

**Scenario framework (all off $99.39):**

- **Bear $79 (-20.5%):** credit migration continues into 2027, requiring reserve build; Fed delivers 100bp of cuts (-$1.05/share after tax); EPS to roughly $6.50; multiple compresses to 1.0x tangible book of about $79. This is the "9.8% ROTCE deserves book value" outcome.
- **Base $105 (+5.6%):** 12.75x next-year consensus $8.23 = $105, cross-checked against 1.30x mid-2027 tangible book of roughly $80.6 (current TBV accreting at the observed 4.7% annualized pace) = $105. Two methods converge.
- **Bull $118 (+18.7%):** ROTCE expands to 11-12% as fee income compounds and the brokered-funding drag unwinds into loan growth; efficiency returns to the mid-50s; credit stabilizes; 1.40x tangible book of roughly $84 with faster accretion from AOCI recovery and reduced buyback drag.

Probability-weighting at 25% bull / 50% base / 25% bear gives an expected price return near +2.4%, plus 0.8% dividend, approximately 3% total against a cost of equity in the 9-10% range. At a bear weight of 30%, expected return is roughly flat.

**On analyst targets.** The published range is $100 low / $115 high, mean $109.31, median $110, implying 0.6% to 15.7% upside. Several of those actions predate the July 22 release (BofA $115 from $116 on ~July 12; TD Cowen $106 from $109 on ~July 11; Citi $107 from $105 on ~July 8), and the direction of the two most recent revisions was downward. The recommendation distribution is 2 strong buy, 2 buy, 8 hold, and 1 strong sell. Analyst targets function as floors on inflection names with rising estimates and cheap multiples; here estimates are falling and the stock trades above the level its current returns support on a conventional cost of equity, so the targets are better read as consensus optimism than independent confirmation.

Valuation score: 4.5. Not egregiously expensive, but the multiple requires an ROTCE improvement that current data contradicts, with no yield support.

## Competitive Landscape

**Genuine advantages.** Office CRE is $259.5M, 4.8% of a $5.128B CRE book and roughly 1% of total loans, far below the regional bank average that drove the 2023-2025 CRE derating. Zero goodwill removes an impairment channel and makes reported book value economically meaningful. The capital-markets business inside a $34B bank charter is unusual and organically built; very few banks this size run advisory and sales-and-trading at $42.8M per quarter. Regulatory capital sits comfortably above well-capitalized thresholds with $19B of contingent liquidity capacity.

**Deteriorating positions.** The deposit franchise is the weak link and is getting weaker: non-interest-bearing average balances down 20.8% since FY2023, customer deposits down in absolute terms in H1 2026, and $2.6B of growth sourced from brokered channels at ~4%. A branch-lite commercial bank has no low-cost retail base to fall back on. Bask Bank is the intended answer, but competing on rate (or miles) does not produce cheap funding. The 10-K names two specific structural threats to the treasury-services economics management promotes as differentiators: real-time payments (FedNow/RTP), which will "likely reduce float benefits" and require more cash liquidity reserves, and stablecoin issuers under the 2025 GENIUS Act framework. Both attack transaction-balance economics directly.

Credit-book composition is also drifting the wrong way. Shared national credits were $6.2B at 12/31/25 (~26% of gross loans), of which only $1.2B is TCBI-agented, an 81% participant book with limited control over workout timing and amendment terms. $55.8M of SNCs were on non-accrual, roughly 48% of the $116.9M total. Relationships of $30M or more grew from 373 to 449 in one year (+20%), with committed exposure up 19% to $24.03B. The largest industry concentration is "financials excluding banks" at $3.764B, 15.5% of loans (fund finance and non-depository lending), a category the 10-K identifies as under active regulatory and market scrutiny. CRE composition trades one problem for another: office at 4.8% is a relative positive, but apartment/condominium is 40.4% ($2.185B), and the single new non-accrual in H1 2026 was an $18.5M apartment building, consistent with the Sun Belt multifamily supply and rate-cap-expiry cycle.

Texas concentration is less severe than the name implies: more than 50% of both loan exposure and deposits are sourced outside Texas, though CRE is 52.4% Texas and energy is 7.5% of loans skewed toward production reserve-based lending.

Trajectory: fee platform improving, funding franchise and credit composition deteriorating. Score 5.3.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit migration with thin incremental reserving.** Q2 provision of $18.0M was attributed "primarily from an increase in criticized loans and $16.1 million in net charge-offs," a net allowance build of roughly $1.9M against a loan book that grew $907.0M in the half and is migrating. The supporting computation for a declining-coverage claim is truncated in the material I have, so I treat that claim as partially verified. What is verifiable, that provision barely exceeded charge-offs while criticized assets rise on a growing book, is directionally consistent with flat-to-falling coverage. Total allowance was $268.3M at 12/31/25 against average large-relationship outstandings of $35.1M, meaning a handful of simultaneous failures would be consequential. The CECL severe-downside scenario would add approximately $91.4M to the quantitative allowance at 6/30/26 (down from $108.7M at 12/31/25).

**Funding.** Brokered deposits at 11.6% of total with a 3.99% marginal cost, customer deposits declining, and the improvement in the uninsured-deposit ratio being mechanical rather than substantive. The 10-K separately warns that commercial non-interest-bearing concentrations "could withdraw their deposits quickly… based on market rumors," and that proposed FHLB system changes could impair access to the $6.843B of net FHLB capacity.

**Rate direction.** -100bp costs roughly $1.05 per share after tax with 95.7% of loans variable-rate. Asset sensitivity to the upside has been reduced (+200bp now +5.1% vs. +7.4%), while downside sensitivity has barely improved.

**Mortgage finance operational risk.** Month-end closing spikes could, per the 10-K, push capital ratios below internally targeted levels "or even levels that could cause the Bank to not be well capitalized." Mitigation via participation sales or book reduction "might significantly damage… important mortgage finance relationships."

**Governance and disclosure gaps.** Chairman and CEO roles are combined, with no confirmation of an independent lead director. On April 28, 2026, nine directors received 2026 RSU grants after eleven had 2025 awards vest a week earlier; the three excluded (Stallings, Hyle, Ragusa) plausibly left at the annual meeting, consistent with an Item 5.02 in the April 23 8-K. Stallings held 294,961 shares, the largest individual insider position observed. The 8-K text was not retrievable, so this remains unverified. The $75M holding-company revolver matures February 8, 2027 and requires renewal. Cash-settled liability-classified stock units are growing quickly ($4.853M Q2 expense vs. $2.707M; $33.372M unrecognized), injecting share-price-driven volatility into compensation expense.

Score 4.3. These are specific, quantifiable, and in several cases not yet reflected in a 1.29x-book multiple.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 31.9% sits 3.8 points above 28.2% one-year realized, with the term structure in backwardation (31.9% at 13 days, 23.2% at 41 days, 25.8% at 132 days) and an implied move of ±4.9% on the August 21 expiry. Next earnings are not until mid-October, so the near-term premium most plausibly reflects macro event risk (payrolls and the September FOMC) rather than a company-specific catalyst. That matters for a bank with -5.8% NII sensitivity to a 100bp cut.

Positioning does not corroborate a bearish options view: put/call open interest is 0.33, call-tilted, and has declined from 0.46 since mid-June. Skew values are reported as 25.0% at one month and -1.6% at six months, but the sign convention is not defined in the supplied data, so I draw no directional inference from them; the headline characterization is balanced. The prior snapshot's 153.3% ATM IV reading is almost certainly an illiquid-chain artifact for a $4B regional bank and should not be read as a volatility collapse.

The more meaningful market-structure fact is short interest at 13.1% of float with 7.1 days to cover. That is elevated for a regional bank and confirms institutional skepticism consistent with the 8-hold, 1-strong-sell distribution. It also caps the case for anything more aggressive than trimming: at 13.1% SI with an analyst low target above spot, adverse squeeze risk on good news is real.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($118, +18.7%)

The two-year operating turnaround continues. Investment banking and advisory holds its quarterly run rate and grows with a recovering capital-markets calendar; wealth management compounds off a small base; the adjusted efficiency ratio returns to the mid-50s achieved in H2 2025 as Q1 seasonal payroll normalizes. The $3.5B cash buffer proves to be deliberate pre-funding and is redeployed into loans at 6.6% rather than 3.64%, lifting NIM back above 3.40% and adding roughly $70-90M of annualized pre-tax NII. ### Bear Case ($79, -20.5%)

## Conclusion

Texas Capital Bancshares has done the hard work of turning a subscale, expense-heavy commercial bank into one generating a 1.04% ROA with a genuine capital-markets business, an efficiency ratio 920bp better than 2023, and a clean balance sheet with negligible intangibles and 1% of loans in office CRE. The score trajectory across prior reviews (6.8 in April, 7.0 in May, 6.0 in June) reflected that progress and then the beginning of its qualification. The Q2 print and the subsequent revision cycle continue in the same direction, which is why the rating moves down a notch rather than reverting.

The specific disconfirming evidence: net interest margin down 15bp with incremental balance-sheet growth priced near a 1.4% net spread; the entire first-half deposit build sourced from brokered channels at 3.99% while customer deposits declined $163.6M and the proceeds sat at the Fed earning 3.64%; consensus EPS cut across all four forecast horizons with the near end down 7.5% in 90 days; roughly 38% of Q2 adjusted EPS growth delivered by a 4.66% share count reduction executed at $97.01 against $76 tangible book; total capital down 144bp with a mark-inclusive CET1 near 11.6%; tangible book accretion decelerating from 9.75% trailing-twelve-month to 4.7% annualized; and a provision that exceeded net charge-offs by only $1.9M in a quarter management itself attributed to rising criticized loans. Against that, the CEO sold $10.0M of stock in two June sessions at $101-102, cutting reported direct holdings 37%, with no offsetting open-market purchase by any insider.

At 1.291x tangible book on a 9.80% adjusted return on tangible common equity, the price requires an ROTCE expansion the data is not currently delivering. Base case $105 offers 5.6% upside; the bear case at book value is 20.5% below spot; the 0.81% dividend provides no cushion against a 2.9% peer median. Trim on strength above $104-106 and revisit at $84-86 or on demonstrated ROTCE above 11%.
