## Executive Summary

CoStar Group has declined 57% from its 52-week high of $97.43 to $33.59, driven by the market's repricing of a multi-year investment cycle that crushed GAAP profitability (operating income collapsed from $451M in 2022 to -$72M in 2025). The decline coincided with an activist campaign from Third Point that introduced strategic uncertainty. Both headwinds are now resolving: Third Point exited its entire position in April 2026, and the company's Q1 2026 results show Adjusted EBITDA guidance for full-year 2026 of $780-820M versus $170M in 2025 — a 4-5x improvement as marketing spend moderates and operating leverage materializes.

The core question is whether Homes.com can achieve sufficient scale to justify the billions invested. At $106M annual run-rate revenue, 35,175 agent subscribers (up 205% YoY), and demonstrated 11x ROI for subscribers, early evidence is positive. The residential segment is expected to turn profitable in Q2 2026. However, the current macro environment — with 30Y Treasury yields above 5.1%, commercial real estate under stress (CMBS distress at 12% all-time high), and a stagflationary backdrop suppressing transaction volumes — creates a hostile operating context for a company dependent on real estate activity levels [unverified — macro claims not sourced in research brief].

At ~17x 2026 guided Adj. EBITDA and 3.6x revenue, the stock is not expensive relative to its growth rate, but the >30% drawdown demands a specific explanation of what the market is missing. The bull case centers on the EBITDA inflection materializing as guided and Homes.com subscriber growth sustaining through the rate cycle. The bear case is that Homes.com stalls as agents cut spending into a real estate downturn, the EBITDA inflection disappoints, and the premium multiple evaporates entirely.

## Company Overview

CoStar Group operates two segments: Commercial Real Estate (55% of 2025 revenue, $1.79B) and Residential Real Estate (45%, $1.46B). The CRE segment houses CoStar's core information/analytics platform — the industry-standard commercial property database built over 35 years — alongside LoopNet, STR (hospitality benchmarking), Matterport (3D digital twins, acquired February 2025), and Ten-X. The Residential segment houses Apartments.com (the leading multifamily advertising platform), Homes.com (the company's aggressive push into for-sale residential), Domain (Australia, acquired August 2025), and OnTheMarket (UK).

Revenue is approximately 93% subscription-based with 89% contract renewal rates, providing substantial recurring revenue visibility. The company employs over 8,400 people across 20 countries and has completed three major acquisitions since early 2025: Matterport ($1.6B+), Domain ($1.3B+), and Visual Lease.

**Revenue trajectory remains strong:** Q1 2026 revenue of $897M represents 23% YoY growth — the company's 60th consecutive quarter of double-digit revenue growth. Full-year 2026 guidance is $3.78-3.82B (+17% growth). Annualized net new bookings reached $308M in 2025, up from $250M in 2024.

Q1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $132M exceeded guidance by 26%, and full-year guidance of $780-820M implies sequential acceleration throughout 2026.

**Balance sheet is adequate but no longer a fortress:** Cash declined from $4.7B (end-2024) to $1.7B (current) due to acquisition spending ($2.8B in 2025) and buybacks ($500M in 2025, $505M in Q1 2026). Current ratio of 2.84 provides comfortable liquidity.

**Capital return accelerating:** $1.5B buyback authorization with $700M planned for 2026. At current prices, this retires approximately 5% of shares outstanding — meaningful given the near-trough valuation.

**Declining interest income:** The company earned $110M in interest income in 2025 (down from $213M in 2024) as cash was deployed. This non-operating buffer is shrinking, increasing dependence on operational profitability.

## Growth Analysis

**CRE segment (9% growth, high margins):** This is the cash-generation engine. The 35-year database moat, high switching costs, and 89% renewal rates provide predictable, profitable growth. The addition of Matterport's 3D capabilities creates cross-sell opportunities into the existing CRE client base.

**Residential segment (the transformative bet):** Homes.com metrics as of Q1 2026:
- Revenue: $26M quarterly ($106M annual run-rate), up 92% YoY
- Agent subscribers: 35,175, up 205% YoY
- Agent ROI: 11x ($36,400 incremental commissions vs. $3,400 annual subscription cost)
- Organic traffic: up 119% YoY
- AI engagement: Homes AI users spend 18 minutes on-site vs. 4.5 minutes for non-AI users (4x)
- Price increase announced effective May 1, 2026 for new subscribers

The residential segment is expected to turn Adj. EBITDA positive in Q2 2026 — a critical milestone that would validate the investment thesis. The "Your listing, your lead" model (agents get leads from their own listings, unlike Zillow's model of selling leads to competing agents) provides structural alignment with agent interests that could drive adoption.

**Acquisition-driven optionality:** Domain (Australia) and OnTheMarket (UK) extend the residential marketplace model internationally, each with established market positions in their geographies.

**Growth risk:** The current rate environment suppresses residential transaction volumes. If agent economics deteriorate as closings decline, subscription growth could stall regardless of product quality.

## Competitive Landscape

**CRE (dominant, wide moat):** CoStar's commercial real estate database has no credible substitute for institutional users. 35 years of accumulated property-level data, 100+ patents, and deep integration into institutional workflows create switching costs that competitors cannot easily replicate. This segment generates reliable 9% organic growth with high margins.

**Residential (challenger, moat under construction):** Competing against Zillow (dominant traffic, brand), Realtor.com (agent advertising model), and Redfin (brokerage). CoStar's differentiation — agent-aligned lead model, AI-powered search (4x engagement), and willingness to spend aggressively — has produced early traction. But Zillow's traffic advantages and brand recognition remain formidable. The NAR settlement's restructuring of commission practices creates both opportunity (agents need to justify their value, making lead generation more important) and uncertainty (potential compression of agent economics).

**Matterport:** Adds unique capability (3D digital twins) that competitors lack at scale. Cross-sell into both CRE and residential creates differentiation. CoStar explicitly acknowledged in its 10-K that it has no prior experience in 3D digital technology or camera manufacturing — an honest risk disclosure.

**AI as competitive weapon:** If Homes AI metrics sustain (4x time-on-site improvement), this represents a genuine lead over Zillow's traditional search interface. Early-mover advantage in AI-powered real estate search could compound as training data accumulates.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Macro/Rate Environment (HIGH):** With elevated long-term rates and the CRE market under stress, CoStar operates in a challenging macro backdrop [unverified — specific yield and CMBS distress figures not sourced in research brief]. Reduced transaction volumes pressure agent economics (making Homes.com subscriptions harder to justify), suppress commercial leasing activity, and could trigger rating downgrades of CRE-exposed clients.

**2. Homes.com Execution Risk (HIGH):** At $106M annual run-rate against cumulative investment likely exceeding $1.5B+, the payback period remains uncertain. Subscriber growth must continue at ~100%+ YoY for the economics to justify the capital deployed. Any material deceleration would validate bear-case concerns about capital misallocation.

**3. Key Person Dependence (MODERATE):** The 10-K explicitly flags that loss of founder-CEO Andrew Florance "could have a material adverse impact on our operations." Florance has driven strategy for 35+ years. Multiple 8-K executive departure filings in early 2026 warrant monitoring.

**4. Acquisition Integration (MODERATE):** Three major acquisitions in 12 months (Matterport, Domain, Visual Lease) create execution burden and management distraction. Goodwill + intangibles of $6.7B represents substantial balance sheet risk if any acquisition underperforms.

**5. AI/Search Engine Disruption (MODERATE-LOW):** Google could expand directly into real estate marketplaces. AI platforms could disintermediate the traditional listing-search-lead funnel. CoStar's own AI investments partially offset this.

**6. Antitrust Litigation (LOW-MODERATE):** STR price-fixing class action and broader algorithmic pricing scrutiny in multifamily. Potential liability is uncertain but unlikely to be existential.

## Conclusion

CoStar presents a compelling setup on paper: a company with a dominant CRE franchise (wide moat, 89% renewals) that has depressed its profitability through deliberate investment in residential market share, now showing clear evidence of operating leverage recovery. At 17x guided 2026 EBITDA with 4-5x EBITDA growth, the valuation is reasonable if guidance is achieved.

The reason for HOLD rather than BUY is the magnitude of remaining execution risk combined with the macro headwind. The stock is down >55% and requires a specific explanation of what sellers are missing. My answer: they're pricing a failed residential strategy and permanent margin compression, while the data (205% subscriber growth, 11x agent ROI, pricing power, Q2 profitability turn) suggests the inflection is real. However, one quarter of improved EBITDA amid aggressive investment moderation is insufficient to declare victory. The next quarter's results — particularly residential profitability confirmation — will determine whether this becomes a high-conviction BUY or whether execution risks justify the discount.

The CEO's $2.5M open-market purchase at $35 provides alignment confirmation, and the resolution of the Third Point activist campaign removes a strategic overhang. But in an environment where elevated long-term rates actively suppress real estate activity, and the stock has a 554x trailing P/E (reflecting the investment-phase trough earnings), patience until the profitability inflection is confirmed by at least one additional quarter of data is warranted.
