**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Xcel Energy is a pure-play, four-state regulated electric and gas utility positioned in the most credible demand story in the utility sector: data-center large-load growth. Management is targeting roughly 6 GW of data-center load by year-end 2027, has signed a 15-year electric service agreement for a ~750 MW Google campus in Minnesota (with Google funding its own infrastructure and the deal marketed as saving other Minnesota ratepayers up to $1.5B), and has raised its 2026-2030 base capital plan ~33% to $60B. That capital program, recovered through rate base at allowed ROEs of roughly 9.25-10.3%, is the entire earnings engine, and it supports a 6-8% long-term EPS growth target off a $3.80 (2025 ongoing) base, with 2026 ongoing guidance of $4.04-$4.16.

The thesis carries real risk. Xcel admitted that its equipment sparked the Smokehouse Creek fire, the largest wildfire in Texas history, and the liability is not yet fully reserved. The Texas Attorney General is suing for damages, a June 2026 court injunction now mandates accelerated pole replacement, and excess-liability insurance has become both more expensive (premiums rose from ~$40M to ~$130-135M) and thinner (coverage cut from $600M to $450M). The Marshall Fire is settled at ~$299M; Smokehouse Creek is the open tail. The financing model is structurally free-cash-flow negative — 2025 operating cash flow fell to $4.08B against $10.9B of capex — requiring ~$7B of new equity and ~$22.8B of new debt through 2030. Interest expense has climbed from $925M (2022) to $1.34B (2025) and is guided up another ~$300M+ in 2026, and share count rose from ~563M to ~624M in a single year.

The stock has appreciated ~18% over the past twelve months but sits ~8% below its 52-week high of $84.23, trading at ~18.8x forward earnings — roughly a 4% discount to the multi-utility peer median of 19.7x. That discount is defensible rather than mispriced: the wildfire tail, the dilution drag, and the higher-for-longer rate environment all justify a haircut to the peer multiple. We rate XEL a HOLD (growth_hold). The load-growth narrative is durable, but the asymmetry needed for a BUY is not present at the current price, and the wildfire liability caps the risk score.

## Company Overview

Xcel Energy is a holding company that operates four rate-regulated utility subsidiaries across eight states:

- **NSP-Minnesota** (Minnesota, the Dakotas) — includes two nuclear plants (Monticello, Prairie Island) and the legacy Sherco/King coal fleet being retired and replaced with wind and solar.
- **PSCo (Public Service Co. of Colorado)** — the largest by capex; electric, gas, and steam serving the Colorado Front Range.
- **SPS (Southwestern Public Service)** — Texas Panhandle and New Mexico, where the oil and gas sector is a large commercial/industrial customer base.
- **NSP-Wisconsin** — Wisconsin and Michigan.

Earnings are driven by rate base growth, allowed ROEs, and a web of cost-recovery riders and trackers that pass through fuel, transmission, conservation, and wildfire-mitigation costs. The competitive moat is the regulated monopoly franchise. The central operational tension is recovering a very large capital program from regulators and ratepayers without triggering affordability backlash. FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS was $3.42 ($3.80 ongoing), with ongoing ROE of 10.38%.

Xcel is one of the more renewables-forward large utilities, with 5,000+ MW of new wind, solar, and storage RFPs across Minnesota, Colorado, and New Mexico replacing retiring coal. That posture is a competitive advantage with hyperscalers seeking clean power and a structural exposure to the July 2025 tax-law change that rolls back wind/solar credits after 2027.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory is steady and modest.** Net income grew from $1.74B (2022) to $1.77B (2023) to $1.94B (2024) to $2.02B (2025). Ongoing EPS rose from $3.50 (2024) to $3.80 (2025), driven by infrastructure rider recovery and ~2% weather-normalized electric sales growth, partially offset by higher interest, depreciation, and O&M. A meaningful portion of the 2025 EPS lift came from AFUDC (+$0.27 of the bridge), a non-cash accounting benefit on construction-in-progress, and from rider recovery rather than core operating leverage. The earnings are recoverable, but the quality is "growth-by-investment" rather than organic operating expansion.

**Capex is the defining feature.** PP&E grew from $48.3B (2022) to $65.6B (2025). 2025 capex was $10.9B, up $3.5B year-over-year. The 2026-2030 base plan is $60B, front-loaded into renewables, transmission, and generation, and it excludes incremental data-center-driven generation, so the realized number is likely higher.

**The funding model is the central financial risk.** Management plans roughly 40% equity / 60% debt funding. Share count already rose from ~563M (2024) to ~624M (Dec 2025), meaningful ongoing dilution that mechanically caps per-share growth even as rate base compounds.

**Cash flow is the honest tell.** Operating cash flow fell from $4.64B to $4.08B in 2025, partly on the Marshall wildfire settlement payment and recovery timing. The company is markedly free-cash-flow negative and dependent on continuous capital-market access. The Altman Z-Score of 0.8 and current ratio of 0.71 look alarming on a screen but are structurally normal for a capital-intensive regulated utility funded with long-dated debt; the genuine financial risk is credit-rating sensitivity, not bankruptcy. Xcel targets senior secured ratings "in the A range," and a downgrade would raise the cost of an enormous debt issuance pipeline. The total debt load is ~$30B+, with long-term debt principal plus interest obligations totaling $57.7B.

**Dividend.** Raised 4.0% (9¢) in February 2026; target payout 45-55%. The current yield is ~3.0%, slightly below the peer median of 3.2%. Dividend coverage is adequate but funded out of a capital structure that requires continuous external financing, so the dividend depends on constructive regulatory and credit conditions rather than self-funded cash generation.

The earnings surprise record is mixed: across the last four reported quarters, one beat (Q2 2025, +14.7%), one in-line (Q1 2026), and two misses (Q3 2025 -7.0%, Q4 2025 -2.2%). This is typical of weather-sensitive utility quarterly volatility and does not indicate deteriorating visibility, but it argues against assuming consistent upside to consensus.

## Growth Analysis

Xcel's growth case rests on the regulated-utility data-center demand cycle, now confirmed across the broader sector in hard load-growth data, not just hyperscaler capex commentary. Xcel's specific positioning is among the cleaner expressions:

- **Google large-load agreement (Q1 2026):** The agreement's cost-causation structure de-risks the affordability narrative while adding rate base. A filing for ~1,900 MW of supporting renewable generation was expected by April 2026.
- **6 GW data-center load target by year-end 2027** — the demand catalyst underpinning the raised capital plan and Colorado's resource-plan load forecast of ~3% CAGR through 2031.
- **Geographic mix** in growth regions: the Colorado Front Range, the Texas/New Mexico energy corridor, and the Upper Midwest, with large-load demand from both data centers and the oil and gas sector.

Estimate revisions are modestly positive. Current-quarter consensus moved +6.0% over 90 days, but full-year and next-year estimates are essentially flat (Current Year $4.11, -0.1% over 90 days; Next Year $4.53, +0.1%). This is a steady, mid-single-digit compounder, not a stock undergoing an earnings re-rating. The growth is durable because it is anchored in approved and pending rate base and a confirmed sector-wide demand shift, but the per-share growth rate is structurally capped by the equity issuance through 2030.

The principal structural growth headwind is the July 2025 tax law (OBBB), which eliminates wind/solar PTC/ITC for facilities placed in service after 2027. Management argues safe-harbor construction starts protect the 2026-2030 plan, but it is a real overhang on the renewables-heavy strategy beyond 2027, and Xcel is more exposed here than gas-heavy peers.

## Valuation Assessment

XEL trades at ~18.8x forward earnings (consensus FY2026 ~$4.11; current price $77.46), against a multi-utility peer median P/E of 19.7x. The peer set spans a wide range — ED 17.9x, PCG 13.0x, D 19.7x, DTE 24.0x, SRE 31.1x — so the cohort is heterogeneous, with multiples reflecting differing growth, regulatory jurisdictions, and risk.

The ~4% discount to the peer median is the central valuation question. We read it as roughly fair rather than a mispricing, for three specific reasons:

1. **Wildfire tail.** The Smokehouse Creek liability is uncapped and not fully reserved, with insurance coverage now at $450M against a fire that burned 1M+ acres with multiple deaths. The PG&E, Edison, and Hawaiian Electric precedents show that wildfire exposure can compress utility multiples by far more than the current discount. PCG's own 13.0x multiple in this peer set illustrates the discount the market applies to confirmed wildfire-liability names.
2. **Dilution drag.** The ~$7B of equity issuance through 2030 caps per-share earnings growth below the rate-base growth rate, structurally limiting the multiple a rational buyer should pay.
3. **Rate path.** A higher-for-longer rate environment directly pressures financing-heavy, negative-FCF utilities and compresses utility equity multiples broadly.

Analyst targets (mean $91.39, median $92, range $74-$99) imply substantial upside (~18% to the mean), but consensus utility targets tend to lag the rate environment, and the wide low-to-high spread reflects genuine disagreement about the wildfire tail and dilution rather than a stale-target floor on a re-rating stock. We do not treat the $91 mean as a near-term floor; the stock is not at a cyclical trough nor undergoing an earnings re-rating, so the cycle-adjusted valuation logic that would elevate a depressed-multiple name does not apply here.

There is no specific, identifiable catalyst that the market is mispricing. The data-center thesis is well understood and largely priced; the discount to peers is explained by company-specific risk. This is a fair-value situation, which earns a mid-range valuation score rather than a high one.

## Competitive Landscape

The franchise is a regulated monopoly across eight states, which is a durable moat by construction. The relevant competitive question is trajectory: is Xcel's rate base growing faster than peers, and is it converting that growth efficiently?

On the first, Xcel is among the better-positioned large utilities — its growth-region footprint and renewables-forward posture make it a preferred partner for hyperscalers, and the data-center pipeline and the Google deal demonstrate it is winning incremental load. The Google agreement's cost-causation structure is a genuine competitive advantage in navigating affordability scrutiny, the binding constraint on regulated-utility growth.

On the second, the dilution and negative-FCF profile mean per-share conversion of that load growth is less efficient than at utilities with stronger internal cash generation. The differentiated competitive risk versus peers is the Western-US wildfire exposure — Colorado plus the Texas Panhandle — a category that has repeatedly destroyed valuations elsewhere. On balance the competitive trajectory is improving on the demand side (load wins, hyperscaler partnerships) and constrained on the capital-efficiency side, which nets to a moderately favorable score.

## Risk Assessment

**Wildfire is the asymmetric, company-specific risk that is not fully priced.** Marshall (Colorado, 2021) drove the $0.38 GAAP-to-ongoing EPS gap in 2025. Smokehouse Creek (Texas, February 2024) is the open question: the Texas AG sued for damages in December 2025, and a June 2026 court injunction now compels ≥35,000 pole inspections per year. Roughly 19,000 poles have been replaced and ~$111M spent so far. The financial exposure is not capped, insurance coverage could be exceeded in an extreme outcome, and the premium spike is itself a margin drag pending deferred regulatory recovery. This is the key downside tail.

**Regulatory recovery and lag.** The entire equity story depends on regulators granting timely rate increases at adequate ROEs. Multiple large rate cases are pending in 2026 (CO electric +$356M, CO gas +$190M, NM +$175M, MN +$365M), with intervenors pushing ROEs toward 9.25%. Affordability pushback is explicitly flagged in the filings.

**Financing and rate risk.** ~$30B of new capital over five years; rising rates and continued equity issuance pressure both EPS and credit metrics. A credit downgrade from the targeted "A range" would raise the cost of the entire debt pipeline.

**OBBB tax overhang.** A 2028+ structural headwind to the renewables-centric strategy; safe-harbor construction starts protect the current plan but not the next one.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and offers no contradiction to the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV is 23.0% versus 18.9% one-year historical volatility — modestly rich, with IV in only the 14th percentile of its own chain, implying the market is not pricing an imminent binary event. Put/call skew is balanced at 2.7%, and the term structure is flat (1m 23.0% vs 3m 23.3% vs 6m 23.3%), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a near-term catalyst. The implied ±4.7% move on the nearest expiry is typical for a low-beta utility. The put/call OI ratio of 1.63 reflects modest hedging interest but the put/call volume ratio of just 0.04 shows no active bearish flow. Note: the reported ATM IV jump to "446.2%" in the change snapshot is a thin-options data artifact and should be disregarded. Short interest at 6.7% of float (6.5 days to cover) is uncrowded and consistent with a HOLD — no informed bearish consensus, but also no squeeze setup.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($92-$99)
Data-center load materializes on or ahead of the 6 GW-by-2027 target, the capital plan proves conservative as data-center generation is added, rate cases resolve at constructive ROEs without affordability blowback (helped by the Google deal's ratepayer-savings framing), Smokehouse Creek settles within insurance and reserves, and a peaking rate cycle re-rates the multiple back toward the peer median of 19.7x. On ~$4.53 FY2027 EPS at 21-22x, the stock reaches the analyst high of ~$92-$99, roughly +19% to +28% from current.

### Bear Case ($60-$65)
Smokehouse Creek damages exceed reserves and insurance, forcing an incremental charge and raising the prospect of a credit downgrade; rate cases come in at the low end (9.25% ROE) under affordability pressure; a higher-for-longer rate path compresses the multiple further while the equity issuance dilutes per-share growth; OBBB undermines the post-2027 renewables pipeline. The multiple compresses toward PG&E-style wildfire-discount territory (13-15x on ~$4.30 EPS), taking the stock to ~$60-$65, roughly -16% to -22% from current.

### Base Case ($82-$84)
Steady mid-single-digit EPS growth toward the lower-to-middle of the 6-8% target, rate cases resolve constructively but unspectacularly, Smokehouse Creek remains an overhang without a resolving event in the next 12 months, and the multiple holds near the current ~18.8x. On ~$4.40 blended forward EPS at ~18.7-19x, fair value is ~$82-$84, roughly +6% to +9% from current plus a ~3% dividend, for a high-single-digit total return that does not clear the bar for a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD on a growth basis (growth_hold) — a durable mid-single-digit compounder with a credible load-growth catalyst, held back from BUY by an uncapped wildfire tail, dilution, and a fair-rather-than-cheap valuation.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the 2026 rate-case decisions (CO, MN, NM), Smokehouse Creek litigation milestones, and the next two earnings reports (Q2 and Q3 2026). The data-center progress update through 2027 is the medium-term catalyst clock.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Smokehouse Creek liability resolved or capped within insurance/reserves, removing the asymmetric tail.
- 2026 rate cases approved at ROEs ≥9.7% with constructive equity treatment, confirming timely recovery on the capital plan.
- A pullback to the low-$70s (toward the 52-week low of $65.21 / analyst low of $74) that pushes forward P/E meaningfully below the peer median and widens the discount beyond what the wildfire tail justifies.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A Smokehouse Creek judgment or settlement materially exceeding the $450M insurance ceiling, or a credit-rating downgrade below the targeted "A range."
- Rate cases settled at or below 9.25% ROE with affordability-driven disallowances, signaling regulatory recovery is impaired.
- Evidence the data-center load pipeline is slipping, undercutting the capital-plan growth thesis.

## Conclusion

Xcel Energy offers a genuine, sector-confirmed growth catalyst in data-center load, anchored by a structurally favorable Google agreement and a raised capital plan, supporting a credible 6-8% EPS growth target and a ~3% dividend. The trajectory data shows scores drifting down (6.3 in February to 5.9 in May) on financial-health and management-quality concerns; the current read confirms those concerns are valid (negative FCF, dilution, the open Smokehouse Creek liability) without reversing the underlying growth thesis. The modest pullback from the 52-week high on largely intact fundamentals is not a thesis deterioration, but it also does not create the asymmetric setup a BUY requires — the ~4% discount to peers is roughly accounted for by the wildfire tail, the dilution drag, and the rate environment.

We rate XEL a **HOLD**. The base case offers high-single-digit total return; the bull case requires a benign wildfire resolution and a peaking rate cycle that are not yet visible; the bear case is a real wildfire-driven derating. We would become more constructive on a Smokehouse Creek resolution within insurance, constructive 2026 rate-case outcomes, or a pullback toward the low-$70s.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: XEL's discount to the peer-median P/E was overstated as ~22% but is actually only ~4% (18.8x vs 19.7x); the "discount is roughly fair" valuation conclusion now rests on a much smaller gap, which strengthens rather than weakens the HOLD but should be reviewed.
