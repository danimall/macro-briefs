## Executive Summary

IAC Inc. (soon to be renamed "People Incorporated") is a holding company in active transformation, trading at $42.03 with a $2.9B market cap. The investment case centers on a quantifiable sum-of-the-parts discount: the company's liquid and semi-liquid assets — a 26.1% stake in MGM Resorts ($2.5B), $797M in unencumbered holdco cash, and a ~$405M stake in Turo — total approximately $3.7B, exceeding the entire market capitalization. This arithmetic implies the core People Inc. digital/print publishing operation (generating ~$200M+ annual Adjusted EBITDA) is valued at negative equity after accounting for its $1.12B net debt.

The fundamental challenge is that the core publishing business faces an existential headwind from Google AI Overviews, which drove a 17% decline in Core Sessions in Q1 2026. The company is diversifying — non-session-based digital revenue grew 24% YoY — but the race between traffic erosion and revenue diversification creates genuine uncertainty about the medium-term trajectory. Estimate revisions are sharply negative (current-year EPS revised from +$0.49 to -$0.54 over 90 days), earnings are highly volatile (two beats and two large misses over the last four quarters), and institutional short interest sits at 17.8% of float.

The corporate simplification (merging parent into People Inc., saving ~$40M annually in overhead) and the Google antitrust litigation provide identifiable catalysts for discount narrowing. But without clearer evidence that digital revenue growth can sustainably offset session-based declines, the discount may persist or widen if the next several quarters confirm accelerating traffic losses.

## Company Overview

IAC is completing a multi-year transformation from a diversified internet conglomerate into a focused digital/print publisher with a large strategic equity investment. The company has spun off Angi Inc. (March 2025), sold Care.com ($296M net proceeds, March 2026), and is shutting down its Search segment (Google services agreement expired April 2026). What remains:

**People Inc. (formerly Dotdash Meredith):** Operates 40+ brands including PEOPLE, Better Homes & Gardens, Investopedia, Allrecipes, and Food & Wine. Two segments: Digital ($253.2M Q1 revenue, +8% YoY) and Print ($137.8M, -16% YoY). Proprietary D/Cipher advertising technology enables contextual targeting without third-party cookies.

**MGM Resorts stake:** 66.8M shares (~26.1% ownership), carried at $2.47B as of March 31, 2026. Barry Diller holds an MGM board seat.

**Turo:** ~33% stake in the car-sharing platform (private, ~$405M carrying value).

**Emerging & Other:** Vivian Health, The Daily Beast, IAC Films (~$20M quarterly revenue).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** Consolidated continuing operations revenue was $422.9M in Q1 2026, down from $481.7M a year prior. The decline is primarily structural (Search segment winding down, Print secular decline) rather than indicative of organic deterioration at the digital core. The Search segment contributed only $17.1M (vs. $72.1M prior year) before its elimination.

**People Inc. Digital — the growth engine:** Revenue of $253.2M grew 8% YoY despite the 17% Core Session decline. Non-session-based revenue reached $102.7M (+24% YoY), now representing 41% of digital revenue vs. 33% a year ago. Digital Adjusted EBITDA was $49.9M (+20% YoY). This margin expansion demonstrates operating leverage in the licensing and performance marketing channels.

**Profitability volatility:** GAAP earnings are highly distorted by the MGM mark-to-market (a $2/share move in MGM equals ~$134M unrealized gain/loss), impairments, restructuring charges, and discontinued operations. Q1 2026 reported -$0.94 EPS vs. -$0.33 estimate, a -182% miss. But Q4 2025 delivered $1.68 vs. $0.78 estimate (+115% beat). This noise makes P/E analysis nearly meaningless.

**Balance sheet:** IAC holdco carries $797M in unencumbered cash. People Inc. has $1.435B in debt at rates between 5.67% and 7.63%, generating ~$100M+ annual interest expense. Total net cash position at the parent level is strong; subsidiary leverage is elevated but manageable against the EBITDA base (approximately 4.5-5.5x net debt/EBITDA at People Inc., though seasonality makes quarterly ratios misleading — ~50% of annual EBITDA falls in Q4).

**Cash flow:** People Inc. generated $55.7M operating cash flow in Q1 2026. Corporate overhead consumed $45.7M. The $40M annual OpEx savings from consolidation will materially improve free cash flow at the consolidated level starting H2 2026.

**Capital allocation:** $123.8M in share repurchases at $37.71 average in Q1 2026 (3.3M shares). At current prices, this represents a profitable trade. Authorization is nearly exhausted (2.5M shares remaining as of May 1), which may prompt a new program.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is bifurcated:

**Positive:** Digital non-session revenue (+24% YoY) is the critical metric. AI content licensing (partnerships with Apple News+, Meta, and others), performance marketing, and direct-sold advertising are growing rapidly. D/Cipher+ technology positions People Inc. to monetize its premium content without relying on search referral traffic. The AI licensing channel is early-stage but structurally promising — publishers with authoritative content libraries have bargaining power as LLM providers need training/reference data.

**Negative:** Core Sessions declined 17% YoY in Q1 2026, and management explicitly guided for continued negative impact from Google AI Overviews. Session-based programmatic advertising ($150.5M in Q1) remains the single largest revenue line and is under direct threat. Print revenue declined 16% and will continue its secular contraction.

**Net assessment:** The company needs non-session digital growth of 20-25%+ annually to offset session-based and print declines. Q1 2026 showed this is achievable (+24% non-session, supporting overall +8% digital growth). But the traffic headwind may accelerate as Google expands AI Overviews, creating a narrowing runway. Forward estimates reflect this uncertainty — current-year EPS consensus has collapsed from +$0.49 to -$0.54 over 90 days.

## Valuation Assessment

The sum-of-the-parts math is the core valuation argument:

| Component | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| MGM stake (66.8M shares × $37.01 at 3/31/26) | $2.47B |
| IAC holdco cash | $797M |
| Turo stake (~33%) | ~$405M |
| **Total liquid/semi-liquid assets** | **~$3.67B** |
| Market cap at $42.03 | $2.88B |
| **Implied value of People Inc. equity** | **-$790M** |
| People Inc. net debt | $1.12B |
| **Implied People Inc. EV** | **~$330M** |
| People Inc. annual Adjusted EBITDA (estimated) | ~$200-240M |
| **Implied EV/EBITDA** | **~1.4-1.7x** |

A standalone digital/print publisher generating $200M+ EBITDA would trade at 6-10x EV/EBITDA. Applying even a conservative 5x yields ~$1.0-1.2B People Inc. EV, which — working backwards through the SOTP — implies $48-52/share for IAC. This aligns with the analyst median target of $50.50.

The discount exists for identifiable reasons: (1) conglomerate structure opacity, (2) Google traffic erosion uncertainty, (3) MGM stake illiquidity (selling 26% of a public company takes time and depresses the price), (4) historical value destruction (Care.com loss of $75.6M). The corporate simplification addresses reason #1. The others persist.

**Peer comparison:** At 24x trailing P/E (per the profile data, though this figure is unreliable given GAAP noise), IAC looks expensive vs. WLY (14.6x) and SCHL (16.4x) but cheaper than NYT (31.9x) and NWS (38.2x). The comparison is imperfect — IAC's asset-heavy structure makes it unlike a pure publisher. The SOTP framework is more appropriate than P/E for this company.

## Competitive Landscape

People Inc. operates one of the largest US digital publishing portfolios by audience (~175M monthly visitors). Its competitive advantages include:

1. **Brand authority:** Decades-old brands (PEOPLE, Better Homes & Gardens, Investopedia) with established trust signals that help maintain rankings even as Google's algorithm evolves.

2. **D/Cipher technology:** Proprietary contextual advertising platform that doesn't rely on third-party cookies, positioning the company for a post-cookie advertising environment.

3. **Content licensing leverage:** Large, high-quality content libraries are increasingly valuable to LLM providers needing authoritative training/reference data.

**Competitive threats:** The Google AI Overviews headwind affects all publishers, but IAC's heavy reliance on search-referral traffic (Core Sessions) makes it more exposed than subscription-first publishers like NYT. The diversification toward non-session revenue is the strategic response, but competitors (Condé Nast, Hearst, Ziff Davis) are pursuing similar strategies. The competitive position is stable but not improving in aggregate — digital diversification gains are offset by traffic losses.

## Risk Assessment

**Google AI Overviews (HIGH — specific, quantifiable, accelerating):** If the pace of Core Session declines accelerates to -25-30% annually, even 24% non-session growth may be insufficient to maintain total digital revenue. Management explicitly warned the headwind will continue.

**Estimate revisions (HIGH — directionally negative):** Next-quarter estimates collapsed from +$0.01 to -$0.17. This negative momentum typically precedes further deterioration before stabilizing.

**People Inc. leverage:** $1.44B in debt at 5.7-7.6% interest rates in a rising-rate environment. Covenants restrict cash distribution to the parent. If EBITDA declines, leverage ratios could force further operational constraints.

**MGM concentration:** The $2.47B stake represents ~36% of total assets. IAC's book value and reported earnings swing dramatically with MGM's stock price. MGM itself faces gaming/hospitality cyclical risks and macro headwinds from consumer weakness.

**Short interest (17.8% of float, 8.0 days to cover):** Elevated institutional bearish positioning suggests informed skepticism about the SOTP narrowing thesis. High days-to-cover creates potential squeeze dynamics but also signals the market has identified specific reasons the discount persists.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV at 29.3% sits below 1-year historical volatility of 32.6%, placing IV at the 6th percentile within the current chain — unusually cheap. The put/call OI ratio of 6.33 indicates substantial put open interest, consistent with the elevated short interest. The term structure is in normal contango (29.3% near-term → 34.3% at 6 months), suggesting no specific near-term catalyst is being priced beyond normal uncertainty. The combination of cheap options and heavy put positioning suggests institutional hedging activity rather than aggressive directional speculation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($52-56, +24-33%):** Corporate simplification successfully closes the conglomerate discount over 6-12 months. Non-session digital revenue growth accelerates to 30%+ as AI licensing partnerships scale. Google antitrust damages provide material cash recovery (potentially $500M-$1B+, transformative at current market cap). New buyback authorization at current prices further reduces share count. MGM remains stable or appreciates.

**Bear Case ($30-33, -21-29%):** Google AI Overviews accelerates traffic erosion beyond -20% annually, overwhelming diversification efforts. Session-based advertising revenue enters rapid decline, compressing People Inc. EBITDA to $150M or below. MGM stock declines on macro/gaming weakness, reducing asset backing. The conglomerate discount persists or widens as investors conclude the core business is in structural decline.

**Base Case ($48-52, +14-24%):** Partial discount narrowing as corporate simplification takes effect. Digital non-session growth of 20-25% partially offsets traffic declines. People Inc. EBITDA stabilizes at $200-220M. MGM remains roughly flat. Market gradually assigns 5-6x EV/EBITDA to People Inc. as the standalone story clarifies.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of People Inc. Digital revenue growth ≥10% with Core Session declines ≤-15% (demonstrating the diversification is winning the race)
- Share price decline to $36-37 without fundamental deterioration (compresses SOTP discount further)
- New share repurchase authorization of ≥$300M at current prices
- Material progress on Google antitrust litigation (damages phase begins, settlement discussions emerge)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- People Inc. Digital revenue declines in any quarter (indicating diversification has failed to offset traffic losses)
- Core Sessions decline accelerating beyond -25% for two consecutive quarters
- People Inc. debt covenant breach or refinancing difficulties
- MGM stake decline >25% from current levels without IAC share price adjusting proportionally

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (expected August 2026, covering Q2 2026 — the first full quarter without the Search segment). This will provide the cleanest view of the continuing business trajectory.

## Conclusion

IAC presents a conglomerate discount situation with quantifiable asset backing exceeding its market capitalization. People Inc.'s digital publishing business trades at an implied 1.4-1.7x EV/EBITDA, a fraction of what standalone digital publishers command. The corporate simplification, aggressive buybacks, and Google antitrust litigation provide specific catalysts for discount narrowing.

However, the negative estimate revision cycle, volatile earnings track record, accelerating Google AI Overviews headwind, and 17.8% short interest collectively argue against high conviction. The SOTP discount has existed for years and may persist if the core business enters sustained decline. Waiting for evidence that Q2/Q3 2026 digital revenue growth can sustain ≥8% — confirming the diversification thesis — is the prudent approach before upgrading conviction. HOLD, with the asset backing providing meaningful downside support near $35-37 and identifiable catalysts that could unlock a move toward the $50-52 analyst median target over 12 months.
