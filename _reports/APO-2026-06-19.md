**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Apollo is a hybrid alternative-asset manager and retirement-services company, structurally distinct from pure-play peers because it pairs a credit-origination engine with a captive insurance balance sheet (Athene) that supplies permanent, low-cost capital. Apollo manufactures spread by matching Athene's long-dated annuity liabilities against self-originated investment-grade credit, then layers third-party fee revenue on top. Total AUM crossed $1.03 trillion in Q1 2026, with fee-earning AUM growing roughly 44% year-over-year, far ahead of Blackstone's ~11%.

The investment debate reduces to one divergence. Fee Related Earnings (FRE), the recurring management-fee engine, is accelerating, hitting a record $728M in Q1 2026, +30% YoY, and grew +22.5% for full-year 2025. Spread Related Earnings (SRE) from Athene, which is roughly half of segment income, has stalled at +4% as cost of funds (3.69%) rises faster than the net earned rate (5.25%), compressing net investment spread to 1.61% from 1.78% in 2024 and 1.93% in 2023. GAAP net income fell ~24% in 2025, but that is mark-to-market noise on the Athene portfolio and largely irrelevant to how the company is valued; adjusted net income grew to $5,195M from $4,565M.

The stock has recovered roughly 38% off its $99.56 March low to ~$138, and at ~16x forward adjusted EPS it trades at a discount to KKR (33x) and Blackstone (31x). That discount is partly justified: the SRE compression caps the larger half of earnings, a $1.4B mandatory convertible preferred converts July 31, 2026 (diluting per-share metrics), and the entire listed-alternatives complex has derated on rate fears and redemption-gate concerns. We rate APO a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the FRE compounder is real and underpriced, but there is no asymmetric entry until SRE stabilizes or the rate path eases.

## Company Overview

Apollo operates three reportable segments:

1. **Asset Management** — a credit-led alternatives franchise. AUM moved from $751B at YE2023 to $938B at YE2025 to $1.03T at Q1 2026. Credit (~$749B) dominates equity/PE (~$189B). The calling card is private/direct origination of investment-grade and high-grade credit, increasingly distributed to both institutions and the wealth channel.

2. **Retirement Services (Athene)** — a fixed-annuity and pension-risk-transfer insurer/reinsurer with net invested assets of ~$292B. Athene is the liability engine. RBC ratios are strong (US 436%, Bermuda 454%), and the business generated $47.9B of net flows in 2025.

3. **Principal Investing** — balance-sheet investing and performance fees ($338M in 2025, recovering from $83M in 2023).

The September 2025 all-stock acquisition of Bridge Investment Group added ~$34B of equity AUM and real-estate/property-management capability, a deliberate push to diversify beyond credit. CEO Marc Rowan continues to steer the franchise toward fee growth and the retail/wealth channel, including a pledge to provide daily pricing on credit funds by end of September 2026.

## Financial Analysis

The GAAP-versus-segment divergence must be addressed first, because any screen on GAAP net income or a bankruptcy-style health score will misread this company. The consolidated balance sheet is dominated by Athene's ~$300B insurance portfolio and policy reserves, so traditional current-ratio or Z-score metrics are artifacts of insurance accounting, not signals of distress.

The segment metrics carry the real information:

| Metric (non-GAAP) | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FRE | $1,768M | $2,063M | $2,528M | +22.5% |
| SRE (Athene) | $3,108M | $3,224M | $3,361M | +4.2% |
| Principal Investing | $83M | $271M | $338M | recovering |
| Adjusted Net Income | $4,082M | $4,565M | $5,195M | growing |

Management's selected compensation metric is FRE; the wealth-channel push plus the daily-pricing pledge are the operational expression of that strategy.

SRE is the soft spot. Cost of funds is rising faster (3.69% vs 3.29%) than the net earned rate (5.25% vs 5.03%). In a higher-for-longer rate environment, even modest spread erosion materially caps consolidated earnings growth because SRE is roughly half of segment income. Offsetting positives include the strong Athene net flows and a portfolio-alternatives yield that rose to 10.01% from 8.03%.

Balance sheet and cash generation are robust. Operating cash flow was ~$7.25B in 2025, FCF ~$7.3B, with ~$18.3B unrestricted cash and ~$73B of dry powder. Holdco debt-to-equity is modest (~0.3x). The dividend was raised to $0.5625/quarter in Q1 2026 from $0.51, with the current yield ~1.6%, in line with the peer median but below BX's 3.9%.

Two items deserve specific treatment. First, the Q1 2026 $1.7B Bermuda deferred-tax-asset write-off, triggered by Apollo revoking ACRA's Bermuda Corporate Income Tax election after the OECD's January 2026 substance-based safe harbor exempted US-parented groups from Pillar Two. This is a one-time equity reduction that management frames as net-favorable long-term (lower cash taxes); it should not be extrapolated, but it underscores how tax-structure-dependent the model is. Second, the $1.4B of 6.75% mandatory convertible preferred (issued August 2023) converts to common on July 31, 2026, adding shares and diluting per-share metrics, a dated and mechanical headwind.

The earnings-surprise record is solid but no longer pristine: three beats and one miss over four quarters, with the most recent (Q1 2026) a hairline miss at $1.94 vs $1.95 estimate, after two large beats (+19.3%, +12.4%). The miss was driven by the SRE/spread softness, not a fee problem.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is genuinely two-track. Fee-earning AUM growth of ~44% is among the strongest in the large-cap alternatives complex, and FRE +30% in Q1 confirms that the management-fee base, tied to committed and fee-earning capital, is compounding fast. This is the durable, recurring component, and it is the part of Apollo's earnings most insulated from the redemption-gate risk currently pressuring the alternatives complex.

Against that, SRE deceleration is the binding constraint. The bull resolution is that spread compression is cyclical, reverses as Athene reprices its asset book at higher net earned rates and crediting rates normalize, and that strong net flows eventually translate to renewed spread-income growth at scale. The bear resolution is that in a structurally higher-rate regime, cost of funds stays elevated and the spread stays compressed, capping the larger half of earnings indefinitely.

Estimate revisions reflect this tension rather than resolving it. The 90-day trend is negative across the board (current year EPS revised down 4.0% to $8.88, next year down 2.4% to $10.61), but the 30-day trend has flattened to slightly positive (current quarter +1.5%, current year +0.2%). This is a stabilizing, not accelerating, revision picture; it does not clear the bar for the strong inflection-driven BUY signal (which requires +30% revisions and accelerating revenue). Growth prospects are real but no longer the explosive trajectory implied by the prior 8.5 dimension score; we mark this dimension at 6.5, consistent with the trajectory's downward reset and the SRE drag.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$138, APO trades at roughly 16x forward adjusted EPS (consensus current-year $8.88, next-year $10.61). On the headline trailing P/E of 87x the stock looks absurd, but that figure is computed off GAAP EPS distorted by Athene mark-to-market and the one-time Bermuda DTA charge; it is meaningless and should be disregarded.

Against peers, the discount is real:

| Ticker | P/E | Div Yield | 52w Discount |
|--------|-----|-----------|--------------|
| KKR | 33.2 | 0.8% | -37% |
| BX | 31.0 | 3.9% | -35% |
| AMP | 11.2 | 1.5% | -14% |
| Peer median | 16.9 | 1.6% | — |

On a forward basis Apollo (~16x) sits below BX (~19.5x) and KKR, while its fee-earning AUM growth outruns both. Third-party comparisons put APO at ~2.4x P/S vs BX's ~7.3x, though the P/S gap is partly an artifact of Athene's insurance revenue inflating Apollo's reported top line.

The valuation question is whether the discount is mispricing or earned. We judge it largely earned, for three reasons. First, the SRE compression structurally caps half the earnings base in a higher-for-longer regime, a risk BX/KKR carry less of. Second, the July 2026 mandatory-preferred conversion is a known per-share dilution event. Third, the broader alternatives-equity complex is derating on rate sensitivity and redemption-gate concerns, a sector-wide move that APO participates in. A "cheap" multiple in this cohort has repeatedly proven to be a value indicator that sorts the wrong way absent an independent catalyst, and APO's near-term catalysts (SRE stabilization, preferred conversion clearing) are not yet in hand.

The discount is not extreme enough to flag a value trap with structural impairment. The FRE engine is genuinely underpriced relative to BX. We score valuation at 5.5, modestly cheap, but without the specific, dated catalyst that would justify treating the cheapness as asymmetric upside. The mean analyst target of $150.375 (high $173, low $125) implies ~9% upside to the mean and is best read as a floor that lags the FRE trajectory, but the proximity of spot to several targets and the negative 90-day revision trend argue against treating consensus as a strong upside signal here.

## Competitive Landscape

Apollo sits in a three-way contest with Blackstone (~$1.3T AUM, real-estate/PE-led) and KKR/Brookfield. Its structural differentiation is the credit-plus-insurance flywheel: Athene's permanent capital lets Apollo originate and hold investment-grade credit at scale, manufacturing spread that pure-play managers cannot replicate. The fee-earning AUM growth gap versus BX is the clearest evidence the flywheel is working and that the franchise is taking share in private credit as the asset class goes mainstream.

The trajectory is favorable but moderating. The competitive position dimension was previously scored 8.0 and has stepped down to 6.8 in recent reports, which we maintain. Two forces temper the moat: the industry-wide private-credit "land grab" that risks compressing fees across all managers as capital floods in, and the embedded conflicts-of-interest structure (affiliated broker-dealer, cross-fund investing, no permanent information barriers) that is both a competitive advantage in origination and a perennial regulatory/reputational liability. The daily-pricing pledge is a smart competitive move to win the wealth channel, but it also exposes Apollo to the same redemption-gate and liquidity-mismatch risk that is currently gating retail vehicles across the complex.

One distinction matters: the redemption-gate risk attaches to evergreen/semi-liquid wealth products, not to the committed-capital management-fee base that drives FRE. Apollo's recurring fee engine is structurally more insulated than the headlines about gated private-credit funds imply.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Spread compression at Athene (most material).** SRE is ~half of segment income; in a higher-for-longer regime, continued compression caps consolidated growth. This is the single biggest swing factor and the primary reason the stock trades at a discount.

2. **July 31, 2026 mandatory preferred conversion.** Dated, mechanical per-share dilution.

3. **Athene credit-rating sensitivity.** A financial-strength downgrade would impair annuity sales, raise required crediting rates, and increase funding costs. Ratings are the lifeblood of the retirement business.

4. **PRT/ERISA litigation overhang.** Class actions against Athene's pension-risk-transfer plan-sponsor customers (since March 2024) have already dampened PRT inflows per management, and could spread industry-wide. Athene is not a named defendant but is portrayed negatively.

5. **Athora concentration.** Apollo committed an additional $2.0B (July 2025) to its largest single-issuer concentration, raising European insurance/regulatory exposure.

6. **Conflicts of interest and valuation opacity.** A large share of fund investments are model-valued, not market-valued, while the retail/wealth push raises suitability and liquidity-gate risk. Both are newly emphasized in the 10-K.

7. **Bermuda DTA / tax-structure dependence.** The one-time write-off signals how exposed the model is to evolving global tax rules.

The risk dimension scores 5.0, below neutral, because the SRE compression and preferred conversion are specific, near-dated downside catalysts not fully resolved, and the PRT litigation is a slow-burn reputational tail.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is constructive-to-complacent. ATM IV at 30.8% sits below 1-year historical volatility of 35.5% (cheap, -4.7% spread), in the 3rd IV percentile, with a contango term structure (near-term 30.8% < long-term 33.5%): the market is pricing no near-term catalyst and modest complacency. ATM IV collapsed from 53.0% to 30.8% since June 3 as the price rose 10.9% and the P/C OI ratio fell from 1.34 to 0.65, indicating earnings/event uncertainty resolved with bullish repositioning. The one caveat is a modest near-term put/call skew (4.6% at 1-week, 7.0% at 1-month), consistent with some hedging into the July preferred conversion window. Short interest is benign at 5.7% of float (8.8 days to cover). The net read: no options-driven warning against owning the stock, and cheap IV offers favorable entry timing for anyone establishing a position.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($165–173):** SRE spread compression proves cyclical and stabilizes as Athene reprices its book; FRE continues compounding at 20%+ on the fee-earning-AUM growth; the rate path eases and re-rates the entire alternatives complex; the preferred conversion clears as a non-event. At ~16–17x next-year EPS of $10.61, the stock reaches the analyst high of $173. This realizes the view that the market under-credits FRE growth and over-penalizes temporary SRE softness.

**Bear case ($108–115):** Higher-for-longer rates keep Athene's cost of funds elevated, net spread compresses further toward 1.5%, and SRE growth turns negative, dragging consolidated adjusted EPS below consensus; the redemption-gate stress in private credit metastasizes into fee pressure across the wealth channel; the preferred conversion plus a soft revision cycle caps the multiple. The stock retests the low-$110s, in line with the recent February-March drawdown.

**Base case ($148):** FRE keeps compounding, SRE stabilizes but does not re-accelerate, and the stock re-rates modestly toward ~16x next-year EPS as the preferred conversion clears and revision trends flatten. This is roughly the analyst median ($146) and ~7% above current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** The FRE compounder is the reason to own APO; the SRE drag and the preferred conversion are the reasons not to add aggressively at current levels.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- SRE/net investment spread stabilizes or expands for two consecutive quarters (net spread holding ≥1.61% or rising), removing the cap on the larger earnings half.
- 90-day EPS revision trend turns clearly positive (current-year estimate revised up >3%) alongside the FRE engine sustaining +25% YoY.
- A confirmed easing of the rate path that re-rates the alternatives complex.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An Athene financial-strength rating downgrade, or net spread compressing below ~1.5%.
- A flagship evergreen/semi-liquid vehicle gating redemptions, validating the wealth-channel liquidity risk.
- FRE growth decelerating below ~15% YoY, which would undercut the core bull pillar.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the July 31, 2026 preferred conversion (per-share clarity), the Q2 2026 earnings release (SRE trajectory and FRE durability), and the September 2026 daily-pricing rollout. Reassess at Q2 results.

## Conclusion

Apollo is a high-quality, structurally differentiated franchise trading at a discount to its large-cap peers, and the discount is partly an opportunity and partly earned. The FRE engine is the underpriced asset; the SRE spread compression is the binding constraint that justifies a meaningful slice of the peer-multiple gap in a higher-for-longer rate regime. With the mandatory-preferred conversion ahead, a negative-to-flattening 90-day revision trend, and the broader alternatives complex derating on rate and redemption concerns, there is no asymmetric entry here today. The recurring fee base is genuinely insulated from the redemption-gate risk pressuring the sector, which keeps this a hold rather than a sell, and supports owning APO over the redemption-exposed flagship managers. We maintain HOLD at ~$138 with a base-case target of $148, and would upgrade on confirmed SRE stabilization or an easing rate path.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the stock's forward adjusted EPS multiple was understated (~14x corrected to ~16x using current-year consensus of $8.88 against ~$138), narrowing the headline valuation discount to KKR/BX that partly underpins the "real discount / FRE underpriced" framing.
