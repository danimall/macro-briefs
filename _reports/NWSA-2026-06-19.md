**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

News Corp is a materially better business than it was 18 months ago. The April 2025 divestiture of Foxtel to DAZN removed the capital-intensive, structurally challenged Subscription Video Services segment, leaving a five-segment portfolio anchored by high-margin, recurring-revenue assets: Dow Jones (professional data plus the WSJ/Barron's franchise), a 61.6% stake in ASX-listed REA Group, and HarperCollins. Continuing-operations revenue grew 5% to $6,691M over 9M FY2026, total segment EBITDA rose 10% to $1,204M, and the company sits on $2.17B cash against ~$2.0B debt, effectively net-cash. Free cash flow of $535M (9M) funded $456M of buybacks, roughly four times the prior-year pace, signaling management's own read that the stock is undervalued.

The valuation case rests on a persistent sum-of-the-parts discount. At a ~$9.1B market cap, News Corp's 61.6% REA stake alone represents a large fraction of enterprise value, leaving Dow Jones, HarperCollins, and the news assets at a depressed implied multiple. The Murdoch succession dispute was substantially resolved in the September 2025 family settlement that consolidated control under Lachlan Murdoch and the LGC trusts at 44% of Class B voting power. AI presents simultaneous threat (content disintermediation) and opportunity (OpenAI content licensing, proprietary Dow Jones Risk & Compliance and OPIS energy datasets as paid AI inputs).

The reasons to hold rather than buy are concrete and falsifiable. Estimate revisions have turned negative on the near-term horizon: the next-quarter consensus fell 10.7% over 90 days and current-quarter slipped 2.8% over 30 days, even as the out-year estimate rose modestly. Roughly 5 points of Q3 revenue growth and nearly all of News Media's "growth" came from a weak US dollar, which reverses if the dollar strengthens against the AUD/GBP-heavy revenue base. A June 12, 2026 auditor change (8-K Item 4.01) is unexplained and warrants resolution before underwriting the cleaner story at a higher multiple. The OPIS/Dow Jones antitrust matter carries an open DOJ grand-jury subpoena and a newly filed Canadian class action despite preliminary US settlement approval. These items, against a trailing P/E of 32x and a stock 10% off its prior report price, define a balanced risk/reward rather than an asymmetric buy.

## Company Overview

News Corp now operates five reportable segments after exiting Subscription Video Services:

- **Dow Jones (~28% of 9M revenue, highest margin).** Consumer brands (WSJ, Barron's, MarketWatch, Investor's Business Daily) plus a fast-growing professional information business — Risk & Compliance, Dow Jones Energy (OPIS commodity pricing), Factiva, and Newswires. 84% of segment revenue is digital; segment EBITDA grew 10–11% YoY. WSJ digital-only subscriptions rose 11% to 4.3M.
- **Digital Real Estate Services (~22%).** The 61.6% interest in REA Group (realestate.com.au, Housing.com in India) plus 80% of Move (Realtor.com). Highest-growth segment, EBITDA +16–25% YoY.
- **Book Publishing (~26%).** HarperCollins, the world's #2 consumer book publisher, ~120 imprints, ~62% backlist sales — an annuity-like asset base.
- **News Media (~24%).** News Corp Australia, News UK (The Times, The Sun), New York Post, talkSPORT, Sky News Australia. The structurally challenged legacy print business, ~7% EBITDA margin.
- **Other.** Corporate overhead and UK Newspaper Matters costs.

The portfolio's center of gravity has shifted toward recurring digital subscription and proprietary-data revenue with pricing power, offset by a shrinking legacy newspaper tail. Murdoch family Class B super-voting shares control the company; public NWSA Class A holders have no meaningful vote. Fiscal year ends June 30, so "FY2026 Q3" is the quarter ended March 31, 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability (continuing ops, 9M FY2026 vs 9M FY2025):**
- Total revenue $6,691M, +5% YoY, of which roughly 1 point was FX tailwind. Q3 revenue +9% to $2,185M, with FX contributing ~5 points.
- Growth led by the high-quality segments: Digital Real Estate +10%, Dow Jones +7%, Book Publishing +4%, News Media +2% (almost entirely FX; underlying print advertising still declining ~2%).
- Total Segment EBITDA $1,204M, +10% YoY (9M); EBITDA margin ~18%. Dow Jones and Digital Real Estate drove essentially all of the growth.
- Net income from continuing ops was $513M (9M), down 9% YoY, but the prior-year period included an $87M one-time gain on REA's sale of PropertyGuru. Underlying operations improved.

The reported FY2025 net income swing (~$1.34B per the proxy pay-vs-performance table vs ~$354M in FY2024) reflects Foxtel reclassification to discontinued operations, not a step-change in continuing-ops run-rate. Continuing-ops trends are the cleaner signal.

**Cash flow and balance sheet:**
- 9M operating cash flow $815M (+$26M YoY); free cash flow $535M. Management guided to strong full-year FCF growth.
- Cash $2.17B against total debt ~$2.0B — effectively net-cash. Caveat: $245M of cash sits at REA and is not freely accessible without an REA dividend declaration; $822M total is held by foreign subsidiaries.
- The March 2026 amended credit agreement extended maturities to 2031, upsized the revolver to $1.0B (fully undrawn), and added a $500M term loan A. Net-leverage covenant ≤3.5x leaves ample headroom. REA's debt is non-recourse to the parent.
- $456M of buybacks in 9M FY2026 vs $114M prior year; completed the 2021 $1B program and began a new $1B program (July 2025) with ~$851M remaining. Semi-annual dividend $0.10/share (~0.77% yield). REA separately launched an A$200M buyback (Feb 2026).

The earnings track record supports near-term confidence: three beats and one in-line over four quarters, with surprises of 12.1%, 14.2%, and 17.0% in the three beats. The balance sheet is strong, FCF conversion is healthy, and capital returns are accelerating. Financial health is the company's most reliable dimension.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has two layers. Structurally, the mix shift toward Dow Jones professional data and REA is genuinely attractive — both segments compound at double-digit EBITDA growth with pricing power and recurring revenue. Dow Jones Risk & Compliance (sanctions/AML screening) and OPIS energy pricing occupy differentiated niches against Bloomberg, LSEG, and S&P. REA is the dominant #1 Australian residential portal. These are the assets that justify a re-rating over a multi-year horizon.

The near-term picture is softer than the headline 5% revenue growth suggests. Organic growth, while real, is concentrated in two segments; News Media's +2% is almost entirely currency. The estimate-revision data confirms the deceleration in forward visibility, with the offsets being the current-year estimate (+2.1% over 90 days) and next-year (+3.9% over 90 days), so the back-end is firming while the front-end softens. This is a mixed, modestly negative near-term revision trend layered on a durable structural mix shift.

AI optionality could add a growth vector the market is not pricing. The OpenAI content-licensing arrangement (press-reported in the low hundreds of millions over five years, unconfirmed) and the broader value of proprietary structured data as AI grounding inputs are real but unquantified. HarperCollins is a class member in the preliminarily-approved Anthropic copyright settlement, a small unrecognized gain. These support the longer-term thesis without anchoring near-term estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E of 32x overstates expense because it sits against earnings depressed by the prior-year comparison structure and a heavy minority-interest leakage (~$119M of 9M net income accrued to noncontrolling interests, chiefly REA). The cleaner valuation lens is sum-of-the-parts: leaving Dow Jones (the highest-margin, double-digit-EBITDA-growth crown jewel), HarperCollins, and the news assets at a heavily discounted implied multiple after accounting for the REA stake. The accelerated buyback is management's explicit signal that it views the stock as undervalued.

Against publishing peers, NWSA's reported P/E of ~32x sits above the peer median of 24.5x but below NWS Class B (38.2x) and roughly in line with NYT (31.8x). The headline multiple is misleading given the minority leakage and the SOTP setup; the relevant comparison is the discount to the look-through value of a listed REA stake plus a fast-growing data business.

The analyst consensus reinforces the gap: a low target of $29.40 and a high of $43.00, with a mean of $35.18 and median of $35.00 — all meaningfully above the $25.28 current price, implying ~16% upside to the low target and ~39% to the mean. The discount is persistent rather than transient, which is the core tension: holding-company and dual-class discounts do not close without a catalyst (REA spin/monetization, a tender, or a clear organic re-acceleration). I score valuation moderately positive — the SOTP value is real and the consensus floor sits above the price — but stop short of a high score because the discount has historically been sticky and the cheapness alone, absent a catalyst, has repeatedly failed to close.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive trajectory is mixed-to-improving. Dow Jones occupies defensible niches with pricing power and double-digit growth; the WSJ digital subscription base is growing with strong renewal economics. REA is the clear #1 Australian portal and is pursuing further property-portal M&A (the withdrawn 2024 Rightmove bid signaled appetite). HarperCollins benefits from a stable industry structure after the failed Penguin Random House–Simon & Schuster merger, with backlist annuity stability.

The structural headwind is AI-driven content disintermediation: generative answer engines threaten referral traffic and subscription substitution. News Corp's response is unusually assertive — licensing content to OpenAI while Dow Jones and the NY Post sue Perplexity for copyright infringement. The proprietary Risk & Compliance and OPIS datasets are the kind of authoritative, structured data that becomes more valuable in an AI world, partially offsetting the threat to consumer-facing journalism. News Media's legacy print remains in secular decline, masked by FX in FY2026. The competitive position is improving where the value concentrates (data, portals) and eroding where the value is shrinking (print), a net stable-to-slightly-improving trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Negative near-term estimate momentum.** The deceleration in forward visibility is the most concrete reason the BUY case is not yet clean.
2. **FX reversal.** A dollar rebound against the AUD/GBP-heavy revenue base would pressure reported results and could amplify the negative revision trend.
3. **Unexplained auditor change (8-K Item 4.01, June 12, 2026).** Disclosure controls were reported effective and there was no change in internal control over financial reporting, which is reassuring, but an unexplained certifying-accountant change warrants resolution before underwriting a higher multiple. This is a specific, datable item to monitor.
4. **OPIS/Dow Jones antitrust.** Multiple PVC-pipe-pricing class actions, a January 2025 DOJ grand-jury subpoena, and a state AG civil investigative demand. US settlement received preliminary approval (July 2025) but a Canadian class action was newly filed (September 2025) — the matter remains open with unquantified criminal exposure.
5. **Dual-class governance.** Related-party dynamics with FOX (Lachlan Murdoch runs both) persist. The September 2025 settlement substantially de-risked the succession overhang, with James Murdoch exiting to build separate ventures.
6. **REA cash ring-fencing and minority leakage.** REA's cash is not fully fungible to the parent, which constrains how much SOTP value translates into per-share returns.
7. **UK Newspaper Matters.** Open-ended phone-hacking civil claims (~$16M accrued, FOX-indemnified via a ~$24M receivable); new claims can still be filed.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is benign and does not corroborate any acute downside. Near-term ATM IV is 29.7% vs 24.6% historical vol — modestly rich, with IV in the 20th percentile of its own chain. Put/call skew is -12.4% (call-heavy), and the put/call volume ratio is a low 0.14, indicating no defensive positioning ahead of a feared catalyst. ATM IV collapsed from 50.9% to 29.7% since the prior snapshot as the post-earnings event premium bled off. The flat term structure (near 29.7% ≈ far 29.1%) shows uncertainty evenly distributed rather than concentrated on a binary event. The 3-month tenor's 54.4% reading is a thin, illiquid artifact and not informative. The options market is not pricing the negative estimate revisions or the auditor item as imminent threats, which supports a HOLD rather than an AVOID.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($33–43).** The SOTP discount closes as the market recognizes the post-Foxtel portfolio's quality. Dow Jones professional data and REA continue compounding at double-digit EBITDA growth; AI licensing (OpenAI and successors) plus proprietary-data monetization adds a re-rating vector the market has not priced. The $1B buyback program (~$851M remaining) retires shares at a discount to intrinsic value. A potential REA monetization, the resolved succession overhang, and a strengthening organic (FX-neutral) growth profile drive a re-rating toward the consensus mean of $35 and the high target of $43.

**Bear Case ($21–23).** The negative near-term estimate trend extends as a USD rebound reverses the FX tailwind, exposing thin organic growth. The auditor change signals a deeper issue. The OPIS/DOJ matter escalates with criminal exposure. AI disintermediation accelerates faster than licensing offsets, pressuring the consumer subscription base. The holding-company and dual-class discounts prove permanent, and the stock drifts toward its 52-week low of $22.20.

The current setup is balanced. The structural quality and SOTP value are real and the consensus floor sits ~16% above the price, but the negative near-term revisions, FX dependency, unexplained auditor change, and open antitrust matter prevent the asymmetry from favoring a confident long.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (hold_rationale: uncertain).** The score sits at 6.3, below the threshold for a high-conviction BUY. The specific items preventing an upgrade are falsifiable and monitorable: a negative near-term estimate revision trend, an unexplained June 2026 auditor change, FX-flattered reported growth, and an open DOJ/OPIS antitrust matter. Each is a discrete data point checkable against future filings.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The June 2026 auditor change is confirmed as a routine rotation with no disagreement, removing the governance question mark.
- FX-neutral organic revenue growth re-accelerates above ~5% in the next two quarters, or next-quarter/current-quarter estimate revisions turn positive, reversing the current negative trend.
- A concrete SOTP-unlock catalyst emerges (REA stake monetization, accelerated tender, or quantified AI-licensing revenue contribution).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The OPIS/DOJ grand-jury matter escalates to indictment or a materially larger settlement than the preliminarily-approved US figure.
- Current-year estimate revisions turn negative alongside a confirmed USD-driven reported revenue miss.
- The auditor change is revealed to stem from a disagreement or internal-control concern.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next fiscal Q4/full-year FY2026 earnings (expected early-to-mid August 2026), which will resolve the FX-vs-organic growth question and clarify full-year FCF delivery. The auditor item should resolve sooner via subsequent 8-K disclosure.

## Conclusion

News Corp is a financially strong, structurally improved business trading at a persistent sum-of-the-parts discount, with the historically dominant Murdoch succession overhang largely resolved and accelerating buybacks signaling management's own view of undervaluation. These support continued ownership. The reasons not to add conviction to a BUY are specific: negative near-term estimate revisions, reported growth materially flattered by a weak dollar, an unexplained auditor change, and an open DOJ/OPIS antitrust matter. Until at least one of these clears — most cleanly, the auditor question and a turn in organic growth/revisions — the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. HOLD, with a constructive longer-term lean and a defined path to upgrade.
