## Executive Summary

Alphabet's Q2 2026 report contained the strongest operating evidence of the last three years and the clearest fundamental deterioration in the same document. Revenue grew 24.2% to $119.80B, the fastest in years at that scale; Search and other revenue accelerated to +16.8% with paid clicks +13% versus +6% for all of FY2025, which is the most direct refutation yet of the thesis that AI answer surfaces cannibalize search monetization; Google Cloud grew 81.8% to $24.77B with segment margin at 35.6% versus 20.7% a year earlier; and operating margin expanded to 34.0% despite absorbing a $1.5B legal charge. On the operating line, this is a business getting better.

The same filing shows H1 2026 free cash flow of $4.26B against $24.25B a year earlier (an 82% decline), with Q2 free cash flow of roughly negative $5.8B (operating cash flow $39.1B against capex $44.9B). Share repurchases were zero in both Q1 and Q2 2026 against $28.3B in H1 2025 and $45.4B for FY2025, and the risk factors now concede "there can be no assurances that we will make repurchases in the near term or at all." Alphabet raised $49.6B of net equity and preferred proceeds in June 2026 plus roughly $51.8B of face-value debt across six currencies, established a $40B at-the-market equity program, and still saw its net liquid position (cash plus marketable debt securities less debt) fall from roughly $104.7B to $55.9B in six months. Purchase commitments and other contractual obligations rose from $149.1B to **$811.0B** in that same period, with $200.7B due within a year, alongside $85.2B of leases not yet commenced and roughly $75.5B of contingent credit support extended to third-party data center and energy counterparties.

We are downgrading GOOG from BUY to HOLD (growth_hold). This reflects a change in entry arithmetic and in capital-allocation quality, not a rejection of the AI thesis. Reported GAAP EPS of $9.11 for Q2 is not usable: $99.03B of $138.75B pre-tax income was gains on equity securities (SpaceX and an unnamed private company widely reported as Anthropic) of which only $278M was realized. Derived core diluted EPS is approximately $2.68, or $2.82 excluding the PriceRunner charge, which reconciles closely with the $2.85 adjusted actual against a $2.98 consensus, a 4.4% miss and the second consecutive quarterly shortfall. On core earnings the stock trades near 32x 2026 and 25x 2027 consensus of $14.73. That is a defensible multiple for 24% revenue growth, but it is not a mispricing, and the $122.8B of not-yet-in-service property implies roughly $20B of incremental annual depreciation landing in 2027 before H2 2026 additions.

## Company Overview

Alphabet reports two segments (Google Services and Google Cloud) plus Other Bets and a fourth line, "Alphabet-level activities," which the FY2025 10-K describes as housing centralized frontier-model AI research. That structure separates the cost center building the models from the segments monetizing them, and the cost center is growing fast: its quarterly loss rose 72% year over year to $5.79B ($11.2B in H1 2026).

Google Services (FY2025 revenue $342.7B, 85% of total) covers Search, YouTube, the Google Network, Android, Chrome, Play, devices and subscriptions. All fifteen of Alphabet's half-billion-user products now run Gemini models. Google Cloud (FY2025 $58.7B) sells AI-optimized infrastructure built on seventh-generation Ironwood TPUs plus GPUs, the Vertex AI platform, cybersecurity (now including Wiz, closed March 11, 2026 for $29.5B), and agent products.

A new business line appeared in Q2 2026. The 10-Q redefines Cloud revenue to include "product sales, primarily the sale of TPU systems," disclosing a limited number of agreements to supply TPU systems to customers requiring or providing on-premises infrastructure, with the significant majority of revenue to be recognized in 2027. Inventory rose from $2.44B to $9.99B, described as primarily TPU-system hardware and devices. Alphabet is moving from renting its silicon advantage to selling it, which puts it in direct competition with Nvidia and creates a channel-conflict question against its own consumption business.

Headcount rose from 190,820 at 12/31/25 to 198,933 at 6/30/26. Total shares outstanding at 6/30/26 were 12,230M (Class A 5,868M, Class B 835M, Class C 5,527M), giving a market capitalization of approximately **$4.62T** at $372.47. The $2.088T figure in the supplied profile reflects Class C shares alone; any multiple built on it understates the company by roughly 2x. Class C carries zero votes, and Page and Brin hold approximately 52.7% of total voting power.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and margin: genuinely accelerating

Q2 composition versus Q2 2025: Search and other $63.27B (+16.8%); YouTube ads $11.06B (+12.9%); Google Network $7.30B (−0.7%, impressions −12%); subscriptions, platforms and devices $12.91B (+15.2%); Cloud $24.77B (+81.8%). Traffic acquisition costs fell to 19.8% of advertising revenue from 20.6%, and total cost of revenue fell to 38% from 41%.

Segment operating income: Google Services $39.54B (41.8% margin), Cloud $8.81B (35.6% versus 20.7%), Other Bets −$1.80B, Alphabet-level activities −$5.79B.

The Search mix shift is the most important operating datapoint in the file. FY2025 growth was CPC-led (clicks +6%, CPC +7%); Q2 2026 was volume-led (clicks +13%, CPC +3%). Volume-led growth at accelerating revenue is consistent with AI Overviews and AI Mode generating incremental query volume that monetizes at a somewhat lower rate per click. Two alternative explanations cannot be excluded from disclosure: US revenue share rose from 48% to 51%, and format mix may have shifted. The filing offers no attribution.

### Earnings quality: GAAP is unusable this year

Of Q2's $138.75B pre-tax income, $99.03B was gains on equity securities: $77.54B of unrealized gains on non-marketable measurement-alternative holdings, $21.40B on marketable equity securities, and $278M realized. For H1, $135.95B of $216.17B pre-tax income, against cumulative net gains on positions actually sold of $992M. Roughly 99% of recognized gains are marks.

Two positions drive it. SpaceX now appears as approximately $94.1B ($80.0B in marketable securities subject to short-term sale restrictions plus $14.1B restricted through Q3 2027) versus total marketable equity securities of $6.31B at 12/31/25. The unnamed private company's carrying value rose from $64.09B to $124.26B, with $87.9B remeasured in Q2; multiple secondary sources identify it as Anthropic, which the filing does not confirm.

Core earnings, reproducible: Q2 pre-tax $138,753M less equity gains $99,031M = $39,722M; at Alphabet's FY2025 effective rate of ~17%, core net income ≈ $32,970M; on the 12,306M diluted shares implied by reported EPS, **core diluted EPS ≈ $2.68**, or ≈$2.82 excluding the $2.1B PriceRunner charge. H1 on the same method ≈ $5.43. This reconciles with the $2.85 adjusted actual in the surprise record. The deferred tax consequence is real: deferred income taxes contributed +$27,538M to H1 operating cash flow and the balance sheet swung from a net deferred tax asset of $8.19B to a net deferred tax liability of $21.37B.

FY2025 FCF grew 0.6% on 15.1% revenue growth. H1 2026 FCF fell 82.4%, and Q2 was approximately −$5.8B. Two adjustments make the underlying picture worse than the table. First, property and equipment sitting in accrued liabilities and accounts payable rose to $29.11B at 6/30/26 from $10.64B a year earlier, so cash capex understates economic additions. Second, of the $84.86B H1 operating cash flow, $27.54B came from the deferred tax line, a large portion of which relates to unrealized equity gains rather than operations. Stripping that contribution entirely gives roughly $57B of operating cash flow against $80.6B of cash capex, approximately −$23B. This is a labeled sensitivity: the prior-period deferred tax contribution is not disclosed in the data available, so the year-over-year comparison on that adjusted basis is not clean.

### Balance sheet and the June capital raise

Total assets rose from $595.3B to $922.0B in six months; equity from $415.3B to $640.5B; long-term debt from $46.5B to $98.2B; PP&E net from $246.6B to $321.2B, of which **$122.8B is not yet in service** (from $78.6B).

The $242.5B "cash and marketable securities" headline includes $87.1B of restricted, SpaceX-heavy equity. Truly liquid resources are cash $55.9B plus marketable debt securities $99.5B = $155.4B; against $98.2B of long-term debt and $1.3B drawn, net liquid surplus is approximately $55.9B, down from approximately $104.7B at 12/31/25. That decline of roughly $49B occurred despite raising $49.6B of net equity/preferred proceeds and roughly $51.0B of net debt.

June 2026 transactions (filing facts): $20.5B net from a public offering of 29M Class A and 29M Class C; $10.0B net from a private placement to National Indemnity Company, a Berkshire Hathaway affiliate, at the offering price; $19.0B net from 385M depositary shares representing 19M shares of 6.25% mandatory convertible preferred, converting on or about May 15, 2029 and not redeemable at the company's election; $1.0B of capped-call premium with caps at $532.67 (A) and $527.80 (C), a 50% premium; and a $40.0B ATM program established June 1 with $0 sold as of 6/30/26, stated purpose being employee equity tax obligations.

Dilution stated correctly: 86M common shares issued equal 0.71% of the pre-existing count and 0.70% of shares outstanding at 6/30/26. Maximum preferred conversion adds roughly 54M shares (~0.44%). A fully drawn ATM at current prices adds roughly 0.9%. Combined maximum is about 2%. The dollar amounts are historically large; the ownership transfer is small because the denominator is a ~$4.62T market cap. The preferred coupon is a new cash claim of approximately $1.19B per year.

Solvency is not in question: the Altman Z-Score on FY2025 data was 3.96, current ratio 2.01, debt/equity 0.43, and re-running the public-firm formula on the 6/30/26 balance sheet with market equity produces a figure near 12. Capital productivity is the question: asset turnover fell from 0.68x (FY2025) to 0.48x on a trailing basis, and operating income to total assets fell from 21.7% to 16.0%, or 18.5% excluding assets not yet in service.

## Growth Analysis

The revenue growth case is strong and confirmed in hard data across three vectors. Search re-accelerated on volume. Cloud grew 81.8%, ahead of the roughly 37% at AWS and roughly 40% at Azure in the same period, though the comparison is not clean, because Q2 Cloud now includes first-quarter-ever TPU system product sales and a full quarter of Wiz. The organic consumption rate is very likely above 50% but cannot be decomposed from disclosure. Remaining performance obligations of $519.5B ($513.9B Cloud) look enormous, but the definition changed in Q1 2026 to include contracts with original expected terms of one year or less, so the figure is not comparable to prior disclosures and is not described by the company as total commercial backlog. Deferred revenue, a separate measure, was $10.1B at 6/30/26.

The earnings growth path is where visibility deteriorates. The quarterly estimate revision picture is flat, not rising: current quarter $2.99 (−0.2% over 30 days), next quarter $3.33 (+1.5%), next year $14.73 (+1.6% / +4.1%). The headline "current year +44.6% in 30 days" is a mechanical consequence of the Anthropic and SpaceX marks flowing into GAAP consensus, not earnings momentum, and should not be read as an acceleration signal. On the adjusted basis, Alphabet has now missed two consecutive quarters (−3.1%, −4.4%) after a +29.4% and +4.2% beat streak, and the cause is identifiable: the frontier-AI cost center's loss up 72% year over year, plus rising depreciation.

The depreciation arithmetic is committed. FY2025 D&A was $21.14B. Placing $122.8B of currently idle PP&E into service over a six-year server life adds roughly $20.5B of annual depreciation, approximately doubling FY2025 D&A, equal to about $1.39 per diluted share after tax, before any of the H2 2026 additions. Timing depends on placement-in-service schedules and useful-life elections, and if Cloud revenue continues compounding near current rates the incremental depreciation can be absorbed. But margin expansion to 34.0% is happening before this cost lands, which weakens the case for extrapolating current margins into 2027.

Capex guidance of $195-205B for 2026 is supported by four independent secondary sources following the July 22 print but was not verified against a company release or transcript; the 10-K states only that capex will "significantly increase relative to 2025" ($91.4B actual). H1 capex of $80.6B implies $115-125B in H2 if the guidance is accurate.

## Valuation Assessment

Start by discarding the supplied multiples. The 18.7x figure corresponds to current-year GAAP consensus of roughly $20 per share, which is dominated by non-cash marks. The peer table is also internally inconsistent — it shows GOOGL at 27.3x and GOOG at 18.7x for the same underlying company — so peer medians derived from it carry little weight beyond noting that Meta trades near 20.7x and sits 30% below its high.

On usable bases at $372.47:

- **2026 core EPS build**: Q1 $2.62 + Q2 $2.85 + Q3E $2.99 + Q4E $3.33 = **$11.79** → **31.6x**
- **2027 consensus adjusted EPS $14.73** → **25.3x**, on implied EPS growth of ~25% → PEG ≈ 1.0
- **Depreciation-adjusted 2027**: if consensus underweights the step-up by the full $1.39/share, EPS becomes $13.34 → **27.9x**
- **Enterprise value**: $4.62T equity + $98.2B debt + ~$18.0B preferred − $155.4B liquid ≈ **$4.58T**; excluding the ~$218B of SpaceX and private-company stakes, ≈ **$4.36T**. Against FY2026E revenue near $498B that is roughly 9.2x revenue and roughly 28x FY2026E operating income.

At 25x forward with 24% revenue growth and 35% Cloud margins, Alphabet is not expensive relative to its own growth. It is also not obviously mispriced. The specific reasons a high valuation score would be warranted — an identified catalyst, a peer discount adjusted for quality, or absolute yield support — are weak here. The dividend yield is 0.25%. Buybacks, which were the actual shareholder yield (roughly 1% of market cap), are suspended with no assurance of resumption. Free cash flow, the traditional anchor for Alphabet's multiple, is approximately zero and will be negative for FY2026 if capex guidance holds. That leaves earnings alone supporting a 25-32x multiple at the same moment a $20B depreciation charge is queued.

Analyst consensus is 13 strong buy, 44 buy, 6 hold, 0 sell, with a mean target of $421.79, median $425, low $340, high $475, 13% above spot at the mean. Targets were trimmed after the capex hike while ratings were maintained. Because Alphabet is not at a premium multiple, these targets are reasonable reference points rather than ceilings, but a 13% consensus gap is not the kind of dislocation that supports adding capital.

**Our base case is $405**, 27.5x the 2027 consensus of $14.73, a modest premium to the current multiple reflecting revenue acceleration, discounted for the depreciation step-up and zero cash return. That is 8.7% above spot. Bull case $470 (32x $14.73, +26.2%); bear case $280 (21x a depreciation-haircut $13.34, −24.8%). Roughly 1:3 payoff skew to the downside against the base case is the arithmetic reason this is a HOLD.

## Competitive Landscape

Alphabet's position on trajectory is mostly improving. Search monetization accelerated with AI surfaces integrated, which is the opposite of what the disruption thesis predicted. Cloud is taking share against both hyperscaler comparables. Third-party app-share estimates (Sensor Tower–sourced) show ChatGPT's AI-assistant share falling below 50% for the first time in March 2026 with Gemini near 27.7%; these are unaudited estimates but consistently directional. Full-stack ownership — silicon, data centers, models, distribution, and fifteen half-billion-user products — remains a structural advantage no competitor replicates.

Three offsets are real. First, distribution is under active legal attack: the December 2025 final judgment in the US Search case requires sharing certain search data with and offering syndication to competitors, and the EU DMA risk language now adds a requirement for "interoperable access to the Android operating system by third-party AI companies," which directly threatens on-device Gemini distribution. Second, the Google Network segment continues to shrink (−0.7% with impressions −12%). Third, senior AI research attrition to Anthropic is reported across several outlets — four senior departures in weeks, mostly London-based DeepMind staff, and the *FT*-sourced report that the AlphaFold team was dismantled after Nobel laureate John Jumper left. All of this is secondary and unconfirmed by filings, and Alphabet has absorbed high-profile AI departures before without losing model competitiveness.

The Anthropic relationship deserves precision: Alphabet is a large minority investor, Anthropic is a compute purchaser and a competitor in assistants and enterprise AI, and Anthropic's models are its own property. Alphabet's reported profit growth is being flattered by the appreciation of a competitor that is also recruiting its scientists. Both facts are simultaneously true.

The TPU merchant-silicon move opens a hardware revenue pool currently dominated by Nvidia, and it creates channel conflict, working-capital intensity ($10.0B inventory), and, per the new risk factor, "increased costs and operational complexity."

## Risk Assessment

**Ad tech remedies — the largest unresolved binary.** The April 2025 decision found Google's publisher tools unlawfully exclusionary. The remedies trial closed with November 2025 arguments, and as of the July 23, 2026 10-Q final judgment remained unissued more than eight months later. The filing notes DOJ's proposal includes structural remedies that "could have a material adverse effect," and Google cannot estimate a possible loss. A Texas state case is queued behind it. Timing is unknowable and the outcome is not priced in either direction.

**Escalating monetary judgments.** Accrued fines and settlements stood at $17.36B. The ECJ denied Google's Android appeal in July 2026, finalizing the €4.1B fine, and Google paid $5.2B in cash. The EC ad tech €3.0B fine ($3.5B charge in Q3 2025) is on appeal with bank guarantees posted. PriceRunner, a Klarna subsidiary, won approximately $2.1B in Stockholm in July 2026 ($1.5B principal plus interest and costs), recognized in Q2 and under appeal. Whether that establishes a template for further private follow-on damages claims is plausible but unproven.

**Committed spending removes optionality.** $811.0B of purchase commitments (from $149.1B), with $200.7B short-term — the near-term slice alone exceeds FY2025 operating cash flow. $707.0B relates to terms beyond one year, mostly long-term technical-infrastructure and inventory-component supply generally fulfilled through 2030, plus energy agreements running to 2054 with take-or-pay minimums and substantive termination fees. An undisclosed portion may be component purchases against contracted TPU deliveries rather than internal capex, which the filing does not split. Either way, the assumption that Alphabet can simply slow spending if AI demand disappoints is partially foreclosed.

**Contingent credit support to third parties.** $43.79B of credit derivative notional (from $16.94B) backstopping data center payment obligations with terms up to 15 years; $7.6B of financial guarantees for counterparties' long-lead-time power equipment; an agreed approximately $24.1B of additional future backstops; $20.0B of milestone-contingent forward funding to a private company (a $457M loss recognized in Q2); and $85.2B of leases not yet commenced. In these structures Google is the guarantor and the counterparties are third-party data center developers and energy suppliers, not customers; exposure is contingent on their default, at which point Google may assume the underlying leases.

**Mark-to-model concentration.** Approximately $218B, roughly 4.7% of market capitalization, sits in SpaceX (restricted, $14.1B through Q3 2027) and one predominantly private holding, carried at fair value with essentially no realization. A downward revaluation reverses through the income statement, which will produce GAAP earnings volatility that has nothing to do with operations.

**Governance.** Class C carries zero votes; the largest equity raise in company history was structured with Series B preferred converting into non-voting Class C, so founder voting control is undiluted at 52.7%. The proxy's sole company-selected performance measure is one-year TSR relative to the S&P 100; no return-on-invested-capital, free-cash-flow, or capital-efficiency measure appears in the pay-versus-performance disclosure at the moment capex is being guided to roughly half of revenue. Qualitative proxy sections not reviewed may disclose additional metrics.

IV rose 5.0 points over the two sessions in which the stock rose 5.2%, and put/call open interest fell from 0.85 to 0.72 — options were bid up alongside the rally rather than fading it. The more informative feature is the term structure, which the data labels FLAT overall (37.8% near, 38.1% far) but which dips to 31.5% at the September tenor and rises back to 38.1% at the November 20 expiry, where the put/call skew reads −4.6% and put/call volume rises to 0.69 from 0.32. Near-term skew is characterized as balanced at −0.7%. The pattern of cheap intermediate volatility and expensive, more put-weighted six-month volatility is consistent with hedging demand concentrated around a longer-dated catalyst window rather than a near-term event, which fits the pending ad tech remedy judgment and the 2027 guidance cycle. Short interest of 58.5M shares at 2.5 days to cover is negligible; there is no crowded bearish position here.

The options market is not challenging the fundamental view. It is pricing near-term calm and paying up for protection roughly a quarter out, which aligns with our read that the binary risks sit in the second half.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case — $470 (+26.2%)

Search monetization is accelerating with AI integrated, which removes the existential overhang that has capped Alphabet's multiple for two years. Cloud compounds above 50% organically with a 35.6% margin and takes share from AWS and Azure. TPU system sales, with the significant majority of contracted revenue recognizing in 2027, open a hardware pool currently dominated by Nvidia and turn Alphabet's silicon advantage into external revenue. Depreciation from the current build is absorbed by revenue ramping on the same assets, and operating margin holds above 33%. The ad tech remedy resolves behaviorally rather than structurally. 2027 EPS reaches or exceeds $14.73 and the multiple expands to 32x as free cash flow inflects positive in 2028 once capex growth normalizes. Berkshire's willingness to place $10B at the June offering price supports the view that long-duration capital finds this valuation attractive.

### Bear case — $280 (−24.8%)

The $811B of pre-committed supply and $195-205B of 2026 capex land as roughly $20-25B of incremental annual depreciation starting in 2027 against decelerating incremental revenue, compressing operating margin from 34% toward 29-30%. 2027 EPS comes in near $13.30 rather than $14.73, and the multiple compresses to 21x as the market reprices Alphabet from a capital-light monopoly to a capital-intensive infrastructure operator — asset turnover has already fallen from 0.68x to 0.48x and operating income to assets from 21.7% to 16.0%. Buybacks stay suspended, the $40B ATM is drawn, and the preferred converts in 2029. The ad tech judgment imposes structural divestiture of the publisher stack. DMA-mandated Android interoperability erodes on-device Gemini distribution. A reversal in the Anthropic or SpaceX marks produces headline GAAP losses that, however non-economic, trigger mechanical selling from screens keyed to net income.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** There is a credible 12-month appreciation path — TPU revenue recognition beginning in 2027, continued Cloud share gains, and potential buyback resumption — but the payoff distribution at $372.47 does not support adding capital.

- **Base case: $405** (27.5x 2027 consensus EPS of $14.73, discounted for the depreciation step-up and zero cash return) = **+8.7%**
- **Bull case: $470** (32x $14.73) = **+26.2%**
- **Bear case: $280** (21x a depreciation-adjusted $13.34) = **−24.8%**
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the Q3 print (late October 2026) and the 2027 capex/depreciation framing as the decisive checkpoints.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Buyback resumption at meaningful scale against the $69.5B remaining authorization, with the ATM undrawn — this would restore per-share compounding and signal management confidence in cash coverage.
2. Free cash flow inflecting positive on a trailing-two-quarter basis with capex guidance held flat, or explicit disclosure of TPU-system product revenue and margin allowing the Cloud growth rate to be decomposed organically.
3. Price near **$310-320** (roughly 21-22x 2027 consensus, the post-earnings low), which would restore a 25%+ gap to base case without any change in fundamentals.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. A structural remedy in the ad tech judgment requiring divestiture of the publisher ad server or exchange.
2. Operating margin falling below 30% in any quarter as depreciation lands, or 2027 consensus EPS revised below $13.00.
3. The $40B ATM being drawn in size alongside continued zero buybacks and capex guidance raised again above $205B — the combination would confirm that external equity is funding recurring investment.
4. Cloud revenue growth decelerating below 40% once TPU hardware and Wiz annualize, indicating the consumption business is weaker than the reported 82% suggests.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 earnings (late October), which should provide the first clean quarter of TPU inventory conversion commentary and initial 2027 capex framing. The ad tech remedy judgment is a separate, undated trigger that could arrive at any time.

## Conclusion

Score history for GOOG ran 6.4 → 7.0 with four consecutive BUY calls, and the declining velocity across competitive position (−1.2), management quality (−1.2), and financial health (−1.0) was already flagging the direction. Q2 2026 confirms that trend rather than reversing it. The operating business is better than at any prior reading — 24.2% revenue growth at $120B quarterly scale, Search re-accelerating on volume, Cloud margin at 35.6% — but the terms on which shareholders participate have changed. Free cash flow went to approximately zero and was negative in Q2. Buybacks stopped, with the risk factors declining to promise resumption. Net liquid resources fell $49B in six months despite $100B of external capital raised. Purchase commitments rose 5.4x to $811B, converting a discretionary spending program into a contractual one. And $122.8B of idle property implies roughly $20B of annual depreciation arriving in 2027 against a consensus EPS estimate that has moved only 4.1% in ninety days.

At 25x 2027 consensus and 32x 2026 core earnings, with a 0.25% dividend and no buyback, the multiple is fair for the growth but offers no margin of safety against a live structural antitrust remedy and an under-modeled depreciation step-up. Reported GAAP EPS of $9.11 makes the stock look half as cheap as the operating business supports; investors relying on screens will misprice this security for at least the next three quarters in both directions.

**HOLD.** Existing positions should be retained; the franchise, the AI position and the revenue trajectory all justify ownership. New capital should wait for either the buyback to return, free cash flow to inflect, or a price near $310-320.
