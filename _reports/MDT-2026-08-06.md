## Executive Summary

Medtronic fixed its top line in FY2026 and did not fix its earnings. Revenue grew 8.4% to $36,364M — management's "highest annual revenue growth in 10 years" — with organic growth accelerating through the year (+4.8% in Q1, +5.5% in Q2, +6.6% in Q4). Non-GAAP diluted EPS grew 0.7%, from $5.49 to $5.53. Non-GAAP pre-tax margin compressed 163 basis points, from 25.44% to 23.81%. FY2027 guidance of 6.75%–7.25% organic growth and $5.90–$6.00 non-GAAP EPS implies EPS growth of 6.7%–8.5%, roughly matching revenue growth, which is to say another year of growth without operating leverage. Consensus sits at $5.95, precisely the guide midpoint, with no cushion.

The valuation reflects this. At $86.34 Medtronic trades at 14.5x the FY2027 guide midpoint, 13.5x the FY2028 consensus of $6.41, a 3.29% dividend yield and a 4.9% free cash flow yield ($5,426M FY2026 FCF). Against a Health Care Equipment peer set with a median trailing P/E of 41.0x, the discount is wide; it is also earned. Five-year cumulative total return through FY2025 was −2.2% per $100 invested versus +48.6% for the company's own peer group, a 50.8-point shortfall that the market has treated as company-specific rather than sector-wide.

Three concentrated risks keep this from being a buy at this price. First, the February 5, 2026 jury verdict awarding Applied Medical $382M — automatically trebled to roughly $1,146M, or about $0.89 per share pre-tax — carries no recorded liability, and the theory of liability (bundled discounting across the portfolio through group purchasing organization contracts) attacks how Medtronic monetizes breadth in Medical Surgical. Applied has also sought injunctive relief. Second, the Medical Surgical reporting unit carries $20.0B of goodwill with only ~12% fair-value headroom, falling to ~3% on a 100bp higher discount rate and ~7% on a 5% cash flow cut — and it is the same segment the antitrust case targets. Third, the MiniMed separation is 10% executed (IPO March 9, 2026; 90.03% retained and still consolidated), the stub's price has declined per the 10-K, $284M–$484M of approved restructuring charges remain, and the CFO said on June 3 there is "no set deadline." With the stock up 17.8% from its June 2 low of $73.31 and consensus at the guide, the remaining discount is thinner than it was a month ago.

## Company Overview

Medtronic is an Ireland-domiciled medical device manufacturer reporting three continuing segments plus Diabetes, which ceased to be a reportable segment in Q4 FY2026 as separation proceeded.

| Segment (FY2026) | Revenue | YoY | Key drivers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cardiovascular | $13,976M | +12% | Cardiac Rhythm & Heart Failure $7,504M (+17%); Structural Heart & Aortic $3,817M (+7%); Coronary & Peripheral $2,656M (+5%) |
| Neuroscience | $10,287M | +4% | Cranial & Spinal $5,222M (+5%); Specialty Therapies $2,997M (+2%); Neuromodulation $2,068M (+7%) |
| Medical Surgical | $8,815M | +5% | Surgical & Endoscopy $6,764M (+4%); Acute Care & Monitoring $2,051M (+7%) |
| Diabetes (deconsolidating) | $3,112M | +13% | MiniMed 780G, Simplera/Guardian 4 |
| Other/adjustments | $174M | — | Exited businesses; Italian payback |
| **Total** | **$36,364M** | **+8%** | |

Geography split nearly evenly for the first time: U.S. $18,103M (+5%) versus International $18,261M (+12%), with international outgrowing the U.S. in every segment.

Growth in FY2026 was narrow and identifiable. Cardiac Rhythm & Heart Failure alone added $1,112M, roughly 39% of the company's entire $2,827M revenue increase, driven by pulsed field ablation (PulseSelect, Affera with Sphere-9), Micra leadless pacing, Aurora EV-ICD and the SelectSecure 3830 lead — while cryoablation declined. Against that, the 10-K names declining lines: cryoablation, coronary stents, Advanced Stapling ("shifts to robotic surgery and bariatric procedure declines"), Pelvic Health and Neurovascular (Pipeline Vantage recall).

Competitive advantage rests on installed-base attach (the AiBLE spine ecosystem bundling implants with StealthStation, O-arm, Mazor and UNiD; consumables against implanted cardiac populations), breadth across ~70 conditions supporting cross-portfolio GPO contracting, and $2.9B of R&D (7.98% of sales), partially de-risked through Blackstone Life Sciences funding arrangements.

## Financial Analysis

**The conversion problem, quantified.**

| Metric ($M) | FY2025 | FY2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 33,537 | 36,364 | +8.4% |
| Non-GAAP pre-tax income | 8,533 | 8,656 | +1.4% |
| Non-GAAP diluted EPS | $5.49 | $5.53 | +0.7% |
| GAAP diluted EPS | $3.61 | $3.73 | +3.3% |
| GAAP effective tax rate | 16.6% | 21.2% | +460bp |
| Operating cash flow | 7,044 | 7,330 | +4.1% |
| Free cash flow | 5,185 | 5,426 | +4.7% |

The 163bp of non-GAAP pre-tax margin compression decomposes into disclosed items: $185M of tariffs and duties in COGS; $84M of asset write-offs from the December 2025 termination of a third-party Diabetes manufacturing agreement (part of a $118M charge); a $235M year-over-year adverse swing in currency remeasurement and hedging (net loss of $238M versus $3M); a $157M charge for a future minimum royalty obligation under a Blackstone funding arrangement; SG&A rising 25bp to 32.45% of sales on Diabetes separation support; gross margin down ~33bp to 65.08%; and $93M lower interest income.

The mix matters for FY2028. Separation costs end with the separation, and the $84M write-off and $157M royalty charge are non-recurring. Tariffs step up to a guided $250M in FY2027, and Pillar Two is structural — the non-GAAP tax rate rose 16.7% to 17.3%, and each additional point costs roughly $87M. FY2026's tax line also carried $148M of non-recurring operational benefits (prior-year resolutions, statute lapses) against $260M of certain tax adjustments. The exogenous drag partly persists; management's own FY2027 guide of flat margins is the cleanest read on how much.

**Segment-level negative operating leverage is real, not an artifact.** Using the recast nine-month segment data (corporate expense, stock comp and centralized distribution allocated from Q1 FY2026, prior periods restated):

| Segment operating margin | 9M FY2025 | 9M FY2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cardiovascular | 26.12% | 25.58% | −54bp |
| Neuroscience | 30.07% | 28.97% | −110bp |
| Medical Surgical | 24.15% | 23.69% | −46bp |
| Diabetes | 5.33% | 5.98% | +65bp |

In Q3 FY2026, Neuroscience operating profit fell 3.3% on +4.1% revenue and Medical Surgical fell 2.5% on +4.9% revenue. Consolidated GAAP operating profit fell 11% on +9% revenue. This is why analysts cut forecasts in February after a Q3 adjusted EPS beat ($1.36 versus $1.33).

**Balance sheet.** Total assets $93,028M; equity $49,463M (including $609M noncontrolling interests); current ratio 2.13x; total debt $28,000M against $9.2B of cash and current investments for net debt of ~$18,800M. Interest coverage 9.0x ($6,467M operating income / $715M interest). Net debt/EBITDA approximately 2.0x and EV/EBITDA approximately 13.7x, using an estimated ~$9,419M EBITDA that annualizes nine-month depreciation and should be treated as approximate. Ratings unchanged at S&P A / Moody's A3; $3.5B revolver undrawn to December 2030; no commercial paper outstanding. FCF conversion of 113% of GAAP net income indicates high earnings quality. A screening output showing an Altman Z of 1.98 rests on a mis-tagged negative EBIT; recomputed with 10-K inputs and market equity, Z is approximately 2.80. The ratings, coverage and leverage are the more reliable solvency evidence, and none of it indicates distress.

One genuine structural fragility: goodwill of $41.9B plus net intangibles of $10.3B, roughly $52B against $49.5B of equity, leaves tangible book equity at approximately negative $2.7B. Equity is, in accounting substance, capitalized acquisition history.

**Capital allocation shifted visibly.** Buybacks fell 71%, from $3,168M (38M shares at $83.36 average) in FY2025 to $932M (10M shares at $93.25) in FY2026, with $1.2B remaining on the March 2024 authorization and no repurchases between November 22, 2025 and January 23, 2026. Diluted share count is now roughly flat (1,288.2M versus 1,290.6M nine-month), so buybacks are offsetting equity-comp dilution rather than driving EPS. The dividend was raised 1.4% to $0.71 quarterly ($2.84 annualized, 3.29% yield), consuming about $3,638M or 67% of FY2026 FCF; dividends plus buybacks equal 84% of FCF, 51% of non-GAAP EPS and 76% of GAAP EPS. Simultaneously, roughly $1.7B of up-front tuck-in M&A closed or was announced around fiscal year-end (CathWorks $525M, Scientia Vascular ~$550M, SPR Therapeutics ~$650M) plus up to ~$490M contingent. Buying fewer shares at a higher average price is defensible price discipline; preserving capacity ahead of a possible Applied Medical surety bond is equally consistent with the evidence. Neither reading requires inferring financial stress.

**Working capital.** Inventories rose 15.2% to $6,309M by January 23, 2026 against 7.9% nine-month revenue growth, implying ~181 days on FY2026 COGS and an $803M nine-month cash use versus $478M prior year. Pre-tariff and launch-support stocking is the most plausible explanation (Affera Sphere-360, OmniaSecure, Hugo, Stealth AXiS); slowing turns in the declining lines is the alternative. The filings do not attribute it, and roughly $800M of inventory investment is the difference between 4.7% FCF growth and something better.

## Growth Analysis

The top line genuinely improved. Organic growth of 6.6% in Q4 and an FY2027 guide of 6.75%–7.25% organic are the strongest figures Medtronic has posted in a decade, and the driver is identifiable rather than diffuse: pulsed field ablation share gains in a large, fast-growing electrophysiology market, with cryoablation cannibalized in the process. Renal denervation received a finalized CMS National Coverage Determination in October 2025, opening U.S. reimbursement for Symplicity Spyral. The regulatory cadence during FY2026 was heavy — Altaviva tibial neuromodulation (September 2025), Hugo RAS urologic clearance (December 2025), Affera Sphere-360 CE Mark (January 2026), Stealth AXiS spine and cranial/ENT approvals (February–June 2026), OmniaSecure CE Mark (March 2026).

Three qualifications temper the growth score. First, the gap between +8.4% reported and roughly 5.5%–6% estimated organic growth is FX, acquisitions and a $39M Italian payback benefit; the company's audited full-year organic figure was not available to me, so the interpolation should not be read as precise. Second, deconsolidating Diabetes removes the fastest-growing unit (+13%) and simultaneously raises reported margin, since Diabetes ran at ~6.0% segment operating margin. FY2027 and FY2028 comparisons will shift basis, and investors must be careful which basis guidance is on. Third, estimate revisions are flat to slightly negative: current-year consensus of $5.95 has not moved in 30 days and matches the guide midpoint exactly, while the next-quarter estimate was cut 5.8% over 90 days to $1.35. There is no positive revision momentum here, which removes the strongest forward growth signal from consideration.

Guidance credibility is adequate but not generous: three beats and one narrow miss over four quarters, with surprise magnitudes of −0.4% to +2.4%. This is a company that guides tightly and delivers close to the line, which cuts both ways — limited upside surprise, limited downside cushion.

The peer set (SYK 35.4x, ISRG 50.2x, BSX 19.7x, EW 46.5x, IDXX 41.0x; median 41.0x trailing) makes Medtronic look deeply discounted, but those are trailing GAAP multiples on faster-growing, higher-margin businesses with cleaner balance sheets. On a like-for-like forward non-GAAP basis the gap narrows substantially, and the residual discount is attributable to identifiable facts: 0.7% non-GAAP EPS growth on 8.4% revenue growth, a five-year TSR shortfall of 50.8 points versus the company's own peer group, roughly $1.15B of un-provisioned antitrust exposure, $2.9B of gross unrecognized tax benefits, negative tangible book equity, and $20.0B of goodwill sitting on 12% headroom.

Consensus targets run $78 low, $98.44 mean, $100.00 median, $121 high, with the recommendation mix improving modestly (3 strong buy / 16 buy / 10 hold, up from 2/15/13 two months ago). The mean implies about 14% upside. I would not treat those targets as floors here — the estimate-revision signal that makes analyst targets stale is absent, since current-year consensus has been flat for 30 days and sits exactly at the guide.

What would make this cheap rather than fairly priced is evidence that margins can expand. Two mechanical catalysts exist. MiniMed deconsolidation removes a ~6%-margin business from a ~24% consolidated margin structure. And the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA tariff authority is unconstitutional, followed by CBP's April 20, 2026 refund portal, creates an unquantified refund option that management explicitly excluded from the $250M FY2027 net tariff estimate. Neither is modellable from the filings — the IEEPA-specific share of Medtronic's $185M FY2026 tariff cost is not disclosed, and the separation has no firm date. A 14.5x multiple with a 3.3% yield is a low-expectations price for ~7% organic growth; it is not a mispricing I can prove, because the earnings conversion that would justify a higher multiple has not appeared in a single reported quarter yet.

Valuation score: 5.9. Cheap in absolute terms with genuine yield support, discount substantially earned, catalysts present but unquantifiable.

## Competitive Landscape

The trajectory is bifurcated, which is why this dimension does not deserve a premium despite Medtronic's scale.

**Improving:** electrophysiology. CRHF +17% on PFA adoption against entrenched large-cap competition is the clearest evidence in years that Medtronic can take share in a large growth category, and the willingness to cannibalize cryoablation indicates platform-transition management rather than legacy defense. Spine is stabilizing through enabling technology rather than implants — CST +5% attributed to the AiBLE ecosystem, extended by the Stealth AXiS approvals — which is the right structure in a commoditizing implant category.

**Deteriorating:** Medical Surgical. Advanced Stapling is declining because of "continued shifts to robotic surgery," meaning Medtronic is losing manual-instrument revenue to a modality where Hugo RAS holds only a U.S. urologic clearance (December 2025), with general surgery and gynecology still pipeline. LigaSure RAS vessel-sealing has CE Mark but the U.S. indication gap is the binding constraint. Separately, the 10-K names GLP-1 producers as competitors and the MD&A attributes part of the stapling decline to U.S. bariatric procedure pressure "from pharmaceuticals" — a device company conceding pharmaceutical substitution inside a segment P&L. Neuroscience posted the worst margin trend (−110bp over nine months, Q3 operating profit down 3.3% on rising revenue) and changed segment leadership in May 2026 (Brett Wall to Kweli Thompson, per Form 4 titles and the May 18 Item 5.02 8-K); no cause was disclosed and none should be inferred.

The GPO bundling model is simultaneously an advantage and now a liability. Applied Medical's successful theory was monopoly maintenance in advanced bipolar devices through discounted cross-portfolio bundles. If injunctive relief constrains that practice, the mechanism by which portfolio breadth converts into Medical Surgical share is degraded, and the effect is structural rather than one-time.

## Risk Assessment

**Applied Medical antitrust (~$1,146M, un-provisioned).** Jury verdict February 5, 2026: $382M, automatically trebled. Applied is expected to seek attorneys' fees ("an estimate of which is not available") and injunctive relief; Medtronic plans to post a surety bond on final judgment and has recorded no liability, concluding a loss is not probable and no range estimable. Scale: 1.04% of market cap, 21.1% of FY2026 FCF, ~$0.89/share pre-tax. FY2026 certain litigation charges of $113M less the $89M nine-month figure leaves only $24M in the post-verdict fourth quarter, consistent with no material year-end accrual — an inference from the charge run-rate, since Note 18 was not directly available. A single tier-3 report (Orange County Business Journal, July 6, 2026) states a judge denied Medtronic's motion to overturn; this is uncorroborated by any filing or company statement, and the procedural posture — whether final judgment is entered, whether trebling is formally applied, whether injunctive relief is granted, denied or pending — is unknown. Treble antitrust awards are frequently remitted, settled or reversed; Medtronic's own Colibri matter saw a $106M verdict vacated entirely in July 2025. The cash exposure is survivable against $9.2B of liquidity. The contracting-model exposure is the part that is not merely financial.

**Medical Surgical goodwill.** $20.0B of goodwill with fair value exceeding carrying value by ~12% at the Q3 FY2026 annual test, falling to ~3% on a +100bp discount rate and ~7% on a −5% cash flow scenario. Two things make this live rather than academic. The segment facing the antitrust remedy is the same one with almost no cushion, while its operating profit already declined 2.5% year over year in Q3; and a +100bp shift in the discount rate is precisely the sensitivity the 10-K discloses as taking headroom to ~3%. An impairment would be non-cash, would not affect FCF, covenants, ratings, non-GAAP EPS or the dividend — but against $49.5B of GAAP equity and already-negative tangible equity it would be large, and it would confirm the Covidien purchase price is not being earned. Headroom has been stable at ~12% across two tests, so this is chronic rather than newly deteriorating.

**MiniMed separation.** IPO completed March 9, 2026; 252,813,348 shares (90.03%) retained and still consolidated; $538M proceeds; NCI up to $609M from $232M. The 10-K states plainly that MiniMed's stock price declined post-IPO, that fair value still exceeds carrying value, and flags future impairment risk — without quantifying the decline. Approved restructuring of $300M–$500M pre-tax has only $16M recognized, leaving $284M–$484M running through FY2029, with the Transition Services Agreement concluding no later than ~March 2028. The May 2025 "within 18 months" commitment and the 10-K's "within the next fiscal year" now sit alongside the CFO's June 3 statement of no set deadline (Reuters summary, transcript not obtained). Delay defers the margin benefit of removing a ~6%-margin business and keeps an impairment trigger tied to a market price Medtronic does not control.

**Un-provisioned litigation and tax.** Against ~$0.2B of accrued litigation: hernia mesh with ~10,100 individual plaintiffs and more signalled; the Mallinckrodt Bankruptcy Trust's intentional fraudulent transfer and alter-ego claims surviving summary judgment in August 2025 and proceeding to merits discovery; IRS/Puerto Rico transfer pricing remanded to the Tax Court again in September 2025 with $2.9B of gross unrecognized tax benefits ($2.7B rate-affecting, $2.0B noncurrent); Italian payback years 2019 onward unresolved; ongoing SEC/DOJ anti-corruption discussions.

**Tariffs and tax rate.** $185M FY2026 rising to a guided $250M net in FY2027, against an unquantified IEEPA refund option deliberately excluded from guidance. GAAP effective rate up 460bp to 21.2%, with Pillar Two structural.

### Options Market Signal

The 40.0% near-term ATM IV is expiry mechanics — the nearest tenor is a one-day contract implying only a ±1.8% move. The informative structure is the middle of the curve: 26.0% at one month, 28.9% at three months (a hump spanning the September 1 Q1 FY2027 report) and 26.5% at six months, against 23.5% one-year realized volatility. That is a modest premium for a defensive large-cap with a known catalyst date, not a stress signal. Two details are worth carrying: put/call open interest rose from 0.40 to 0.63 since July 19, and the six-month tenor carries a put/call volume ratio of 3.48 — the only meaningfully put-skewed point on the curve, consistent with hedging the separation and litigation window into late FY2027 rather than near-term downside conviction. Short interest of 1.5% of float and 2.0 days to cover show no crowded bearish position. Nothing in the options data contradicts a HOLD; the six-month put activity mildly corroborates the view that the identified binaries sit further out than the September print.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $115 (+33%)

Organic growth of 6.75%–7.25% proves durable on PFA, renal denervation and the Stealth AXiS/AiBLE attach. MiniMed deconsolidates during FY2027, removing a ~6%-margin business and roughly $284M–$484M of remaining separation charges over time, so FY2028 shows genuine operating leverage for the first time in years. IEEPA refunds materialize against the $185M already paid, offsetting part of the $250M FY2027 tariff step-up. Applied Medical settles or is remitted below $500M with no injunctive relief touching GPO bundling, removing both the cash overhang and the Medical Surgical goodwill trigger. On $6.41 FY2028 consensus at 18x — the low end of where a mid-single-digit-growth medtech with restored leverage trades — that is $115, close to the $121 high analyst target, plus a 3.3% dividend along the way.

### Bear Case — $73 (−15%)

Final judgment enters at ~$1.15B plus fees, with injunctive relief constraining cross-portfolio bundled discounting. Medical Surgical projected cash flows fall more than 5%, headroom goes negative, and a large non-cash goodwill write-down against already-negative tangible equity forces a broader question about the Covidien-era capital base. The MiniMed separation slips past FY2027 with continued stub price weakness and an impairment on the retained 90% stake. Tariffs, Pillar Two and FX prevent any margin recovery, FY2027 EPS lands at or below the $5.90 low end, and the multiple compresses to 12.5x on ~$5.80 — roughly $73, the June 2026 low. A more adverse combination of these takes the stock toward $69–70.

### Base Case — $95 (+10%)

Medtronic delivers the FY2027 guide with organic growth near 7% and non-GAAP EPS of ~$5.95, absorbing the tariff step-up with flat margins. Applied Medical resolves over 12–24 months at a cost that is material to a single quarter but not to the equity story, with no structural remedy. MiniMed separates late in FY2027 or early FY2028. On a blended forward EPS of ~$6.05 by mid-2027 at 15.7x — a modest re-rating from 14.5x as the separation completes and the litigation overhang clarifies — that is $95, or 14.8x the FY2028 consensus of $6.41. Total return of roughly 13% including the dividend. This sits below the $98.44 consensus mean and $100 median, which I regard as assuming more margin recovery than management has guided to.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The return case is a covered 3.29% dividend plus mid-single-digit EPS growth, with re-rating optionality that depends on events I cannot yet verify. Own it for the yield and the balance sheet; do not add for the growth.

**Reassessment timeline:** September 1, 2026 (Q1 FY2027, consensus $1.39). This is the first clean read on whether the earnings conversion problem is transitional. The next annual goodwill test falls in Q3 FY2027.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Q1 or Q2 FY2027 non-GAAP EPS growth exceeding organic revenue growth — i.e., demonstrated margin expansion rather than the flat margins guided — combined with FY2027 EPS guidance raised above $6.00.
2. Applied Medical resolved by settlement or remittitur below roughly $500M **with no injunctive relief** affecting cross-portfolio GPO bundling, which simultaneously removes the Medical Surgical cash-flow-sensitivity trigger.
3. Price at or below $78 (approximately 13.1x the FY2027 guide midpoint, the low analyst target, and a ~3.6% dividend yield), which would restore adequate margin of safety against the bear case even with the overhangs unresolved.
4. A firm, dated completion plan for the MiniMed separation with quantified deconsolidation margin accretion.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Injunctive relief granted that restricts bundled discounting across the Medical Surgical portfolio.
2. A Medical Surgical goodwill impairment, or disclosed headroom below ~5% at the next annual test.
3. FY2027 organic growth guidance cut below 6%, or non-GAAP EPS guidance cut below $5.90.
4. Dividend increase skipped, or dividends-plus-buybacks exceeding 100% of free cash flow while inventory continues building faster than revenue.
5. Confirmation that the MiniMed separation slips beyond FY2028 alongside an impairment on the retained 90.03% stake.

## Conclusion

This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the score stabilizes at 5.68 after four quarters of decline rather than deteriorating further. The reason is that the fundamental deterioration priced into the June selloff stopped: Q4 organic growth of 6.6% and an FY2027 guide of 6.75%–7.25% organic are the best figures in a decade, and the PFA franchise is taking share in a large contested market. What has not changed is that 8.4% revenue growth produced 0.7% non-GAAP EPS growth, that management is guiding to flat margins rather than recovery, that consensus sits exactly at the guide with no cushion, and that three specific, largely un-provisioned exposures — a trebled $1.15B verdict targeting the contracting model, $20.0B of goodwill on 12% headroom in the same segment, and an open-ended separation — remain live.

The 17.8% rally from the $73.31 June low has consumed part of the discount that made the risk/reward tolerable a month ago. At $86.34, base-case upside of roughly 10% plus a 3.3% dividend against bear-case downside of roughly 15% is not the asymmetry that justifies new capital, particularly with no positive estimate-revision momentum to lean on. Existing holders are compensated by a covered dividend, an A-rated balance sheet with 9.0x interest coverage, and 113% FCF conversion. New capital should wait for either the September 1 print to demonstrate margin expansion, a resolution of the Applied Medical matter without a structural remedy, or a price in the high $70s.
