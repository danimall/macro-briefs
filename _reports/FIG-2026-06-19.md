**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Figma is a profitable, fast-growing collaborative design platform that has been treated by the market as an AI casualty. The stock trades at $18.88, roughly 43% below its $33.00 IPO price (August 2025) and 87% below its $142.92 first-day high, even as the underlying business is accelerating: Q1 2026 revenue grew 46% YoY to $333.4M (up from ~40% the prior quarter), net dollar retention rose to 139% from 132%, customers above $100K ARR grew 48%, and management beat consensus and raised full-year guidance. The company has now beaten earnings estimates four consecutive quarters, and consensus EPS estimates have been revised up 18-20% over the past 90 days. This is not the profile of a deteriorating business.

The disconnect between fundamentals and price is a referendum on whether AI is a tailwind or an existential threat to Figma's seat-based model. The bear case has specific, disclosed support: gross margin compressed from 91% to 79% YoY as AI inference and hosting costs rose $33.7M, management explicitly warns that AI may reduce the number of design seats customers need, and Anthropic launched "Claude Design," a direct competitor, on April 17, 2026, from one of the foundation-model suppliers Figma depends on. Figma's March 2026 attempt to enforce AI credit limits produced disclosed customer dissatisfaction and reduced usage. These are real risks.

The prior recommendation history on this name was AVOID, premised on AI disruption and de-rating. That framing no longer holds up against the data. The decline is multiple compression (from roughly 10x sales at IPO to ~5.9x today), not fundamental deterioration; the seat metrics the bears fear are eroding are the ones accelerating. The structural AI-margin and seat-model risks are genuine and unresolved, and a heavy technical overhang (36.8% short float, tranche lock-up releases running through August 2026) caps near-term conviction. With every sell-side target ($25 low, $34 median, $60 high) sitting 30%+ above the current price and short interest indicating bearish positioning, the stock is neither a confident long nor a defensible short. The call is HOLD.

## Company Overview

Figma operates a cloud-based, collaborative design and product-development platform. Its foundational product, Figma Design (launched 2015), pioneered browser-based, multiplayer interface design using WebGL, displacing desktop incumbents and uniting designers, developers, and product managers in a single shared workspace. The company has expanded from a point design tool into an end-to-end platform spanning FigJam (whiteboarding), Dev Mode (developer hand-off), Figma Slides, and a 2025 wave of products including Figma Make (prompt-to-prototype), Figma Sites (design-and-publish), Figma Buzz, Figma Draw, and Figma Weave (multi-model AI canvas, from the Weavy acquisition). In March 2026 it launched an MCP server allowing third-party AI agents to read and modify Figma files.

The business is seat-based SaaS, tiered from a free Starter plan through Professional, Organization, and Enterprise, recognized ratably. Growth is heavily product-led and organic (land-and-expand within organizations), increasingly supplemented by enterprise sales. The company runs a single operating segment, is hosted entirely on AWS, and is led by co-founder Dylan Field, who controls roughly 72.3% of voting power through super-voting Class B stock and an irrevocable proxy over co-founder Evan Wallace's shares.

## Financial Analysis

The headline GAAP result is misleading and must be addressed first. Q1 2026 showed a GAAP operating loss of $(137.4)M versus +$39.7M operating income in Q1 2025. This swing is almost entirely an artifact of $169M of stock-based compensation recognized since the IPO (R&D $79M, G&A $64M, S&M $21M, COGS $5M), against negligible SBC ($0.2M) in the pre-IPO comparable period. Any screen relying on GAAP net income will badly misclassify this company.

On a non-GAAP basis the business is solidly profitable:
- **Revenue $333.4M, +46% YoY**, accelerating from ~40% the prior quarter, beating consensus ~$316M.
- **Non-GAAP operating income $52.1M, 16% margin** (vs. $40M / 18% prior year).
- **Operating cash flow $97.3M** (roughly flat YoY); **free cash flow $88.6M, 27% FCF margin** (down from 41%, partly due to a $56.1M annual bonus payment timing item).
- **Net loss $(142.4)M; GAAP EPS $(0.27)** — the SBC distortion.

The single most important financial trend is gross margin compression to 79% from 91%, driven by a $33.7M increase in AI inference and hosting costs (cost of revenue +253% YoY). Figma is absorbing AI compute costs faster than it is monetizing AI features. Management guides explicitly that AI investment "will negatively impact our gross margins and operating margins," with long-term effects "currently unknown."

SaaS metrics are strong and improving across the board:
- Net dollar retention 139% (up from 132%).
- Customers above $10K ARR: 15,218 (+37%); above $100K ARR: 1,525 (+48%).
- Deferred revenue $627.7M; RPO $682.3M (mostly recognized within 12 months).
- International revenue ($178M) now exceeds U.S. revenue ($155M).

The balance sheet is a fortress: ~$1.65B total liquidity ($405.7M cash + $1.23B marketable securities + ~$69M in USDC/Bitcoin/Bitcoin ETF), no drawn debt, a $500M undrawn revolver maturing 2030, and total equity of $1.46B. The Bitcoin and Bitcoin-ETF holdings on a SaaS balance sheet are a minor governance curiosity that adds modest other-income volatility. Offsetting the liquidity strength: $465.9M of non-cancelable hosting/AI purchase commitments (with another $50M added in May 2026), which lock in compute spend regardless of monetization progress.

FY2026 guidance was raised at Q1: revenue $1.422–$1.428B, non-GAAP operating income raised to $125–$135M, Q2 revenue $348–$350M.

## Growth Analysis

The growth signal is the strongest dimension here and warrants the most weight. Four data points reinforce it: revenue accelerating to 46%, NDR rising to 139%, the >$100K ARR cohort growing 48%, and consensus estimates revised up 18-20% over 90 days against four consecutive beats (the latest a 55.8% surprise). On the standard inflection framework, an estimate-revision velocity of +18-20% over 90 days combined with >40% revenue growth is a meaningful positive signal, and the stock is not at a premium multiple that would neutralize it: on non-GAAP profitability and ~5.9x sales, FIG is not a >50x name where rising estimates merely price in continued beats.

The growth question is durability, not current momentum. Figma's expansion path is twofold: continued seat/enterprise expansion (visible in NDR and ARR cohorts) and AI monetization. Citi's June 17 Buy/High-Risk initiation at $36 framed the latter as "the AI monetization story taking shape." The tension is that AI is simultaneously the growth lever and the threat to the underlying seat-count model. Management's own framing — growth from "seat expansion, retention, enterprise adoption, new users, and AI" — is genuine but does not resolve whether AI ultimately expands or compresses the addressable seat base.

For now, the data argue tailwind: a 139% NDR and 48% growth in large customers are not what a disrupted business looks like. The market, pricing the stock below its IPO, is betting the headwind dominates over a multi-year horizon. I cannot resolve that with current evidence, which is why growth scores well but does not by itself justify a BUY.

## Valuation Assessment

At $18.88 and a market cap of ~$8.4B, FIG trades at roughly 5.9x the midpoint of FY2026 revenue guidance ($1.425B). Trailing and forward GAAP P/E are negative and uninformative given the SBC distortion. On non-GAAP operating income guidance of $125–$135M, the stock trades at roughly 65x non-GAAP operating income — high in absolute terms, but the company is reinvesting heavily and growing 46%, so an EV/sales lens is more appropriate.

The de-rating from ~10x sales at IPO to ~5.9x reflects the market repricing the durability and quality of the revenue, not a decline in the revenue itself. This is the crux of why the call is HOLD rather than AVOID: the decline is valuation compression, and there is no evidence in the operating data that the durability has actually broken. A "cheap" multiple is not, by itself, a reason to buy; cheapness frequently marks structural impairment rather than opportunity, and here a portion of the multiple compression may correctly anticipate AI-driven margin and seat pressure that is not yet visible in trailing metrics.

The independent context that prevents an AVOID: every sell-side target sits above the current price — low $25 (+32%), median $34 (+80%), high $60 (+218%). When no analyst sees downside from current levels and the company is beating and raising, an AVOID rating requires demonstrating that the bearish view is not already over-discounted. It is not over-discounted enough to be a confident BUY either; the structural AI risks remain unresolved and the compute-cost trajectory is genuinely uncertain. The valuation is asymmetric to the upside only if seat-model durability holds; that conditionality is what keeps the score moderate rather than high.

## Competitive Landscape

Figma is the category leader in collaborative interface design, with real network effects (multiplayer collaboration), a large free-user funnel, deep enterprise workflow entrenchment, and a developer/plugin ecosystem. The current operating data indicate the competitive position is holding or improving in the present.

The trajectory question is what the market is pricing. Three competitive vectors are live. First, Anthropic's Claude Design (launched April 17, 2026) is a direct, AI-first competitor built by one of Figma's own foundation-model suppliers, embodying the platform's stated fear that model providers could intermediate end-user relationships and erode the value of software built on top of them. Second, a broader wave of "design-to-code" and "prompt-to-product" entrants threatens to compress Figma's workflow. Third, Adobe — which abandoned its $20B Figma acquisition in 2023 on regulatory grounds — remains a large incumbent, and weak Adobe results have fed a "SaaSpocalypse" narrative that AI will compress creative-software demand broadly.

Figma's MCP server is a double-edged tool: it extends the platform to third-party AI agents but also potentially enables those agents to circumvent per-seat pricing. The competitive position is strong today and structurally contested forward; the deciding evidence will be visible in NDR and seat trends over the next several quarters.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is unusually concentrated around AI as both opportunity and threat, and Figma's disclosures are candid:

1. **AI margin compression (already happening).** Gross margin fell 12 points YoY on inference costs; management warns of ongoing pressure with "currently unknown" long-term effects.
2. **Seat-model erosion (the core bear case).** Figma openly warns AI could reduce the number of seats needed, that third-party agents could circumvent per-seat pricing, and that the seat model "may become less aligned with customer value creation as AI automates work."
3. **AI monetization friction.** March 2026 AI-credit enforcement produced disclosed "elevated customer support volume, instances of customer dissatisfaction... and reduced usage by certain customers."
4. **Foundation-model dependency.** Reliance on a limited number of model providers, with $465.9M of non-cancelable commitments and no control over model pricing or availability.
5. **Anthropic/government risk.** The U.S. federal government identified Anthropic as a supply-chain risk and directed agencies to cease using Claude — the model underpinning Figma's FedRAMP/government AI features. Litigation is ongoing.
6. **OFAC voluntary self-disclosure.** Self-reported potential sanctions violations under review; no loss accrued (range not yet estimable).
7. **Share overhang.** Tranche lock-up releases (Nov 2025; Feb 2026; ~61.1M shares May 18 2026; remainder by Q2 earnings / Aug 31 2026) plus ~88M shares underlying options/RSUs create persistent supply pressure.
8. **Dual-class control.** Field controls ~72.3% of voting power; public holders have minimal governance influence, and the structure may exclude FIG from some indices.

Several of these are specific and not fully priced: the AI-credit friction, the foundation-model commitments, the government-channel risk. Others (the de-rating, the short interest) are well-known. The risk score is low because the magnitude of unresolved, company-specific binary risk is above average for a profitable, growing SaaS name.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is balanced-to-constructive, not bearish. Near-term ATM IV is 79.8% versus 88.0% trailing realized volatility, so implied vol is cheap relative to history (-8.2% spread). Put/call skew is essentially flat (-1.4%), put/call volume ratio is low at 0.21, and the term structure is in contango (near 79.8% < long 86.8%) — a normal, non-panicked structure. The most notable flow is a near-dated $19 call buyer at 5.1x volume/OI, a mildly bullish print. Despite 36.8% short interest, the options market is not pricing imminent collapse, which is consistent with the read that fresh downside is limited and the bearish thesis is already crowded.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (target $34–$60)
Fundamentals are accelerating, not deteriorating: 46% revenue growth, 139% NDR, 48% growth in $100K+ customers, four straight beats, and a beat-and-raise. AI monetization is beginning to take shape (Citi's thesis), and as credit-based pricing matures the gross-margin compression reverses into margin expansion. The seat-model fear proves wrong — more software built means more design and collaboration, expanding rather than compressing the seat base. With every sell-side target above the current price and 36.8% short interest, sustained evidence of durable growth triggers both estimate upgrades and a short squeeze. Citi's $36 (+90%) and the $60 high target frame the upside if durability holds.

### Bear Case (target $13–$15, roughly -25% to -30%)
AI structurally erodes the per-seat model: customers need fewer designers as AI automates output, and third-party agents circumvent per-seat pricing. Claude Design and design-to-code entrants compress Figma's workflow and addressable market. Gross margin stays under pressure as compute costs (locked in by $465.9M of commitments) outrun monetization, and the AI-credit friction recurs. Lock-up releases through August 2026 add persistent supply. The stock de-rates further toward 4x sales as the market concludes the revenue quality does not justify even the current multiple. The Goldman PT cut on a beat captures this durability skepticism.

The two cases are unusually wide and both rest on real evidence. The bull case is supported by everything in the trailing and current data; the bear case is supported by the disclosed forward risks and the gross-margin trajectory. I cannot confidently assign dominance to either over a 12-month horizon, which is the defining feature of this HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings report (guided to $348–$350M revenue) and the completion of lock-up releases by ~August 31, 2026. The Q2 print is the most important near-term test of whether the AI-credit monetization is improving gross margin or continuing to compress it.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- Gross margin stabilizes or inflects upward (above ~80%) on two consecutive quarters, demonstrating AI costs are being monetized rather than absorbed.
- NDR holds above 130% and the $100K+ ARR cohort continues to grow 35%+, confirming seat/enterprise durability through the lock-up overhang.
- A constructive entry would emerge in the low-to-mid teens ($13–$16) on a non-fundamental, overhang-driven selloff, given analyst targets cluster well above and downside there approaches the bear case.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- NDR falls below ~120% or sequential revenue growth decelerates sharply below ~30% YoY, indicating the seat-model erosion thesis is materializing.
- Gross margin compresses further below ~75% with no monetization offset, signaling the AI cost structure is structurally impaired.
- AI-credit enforcement produces measurable churn or a guidance cut, confirming the monetization transition has failed.

## Conclusion

The recommendation is **HOLD**, with an "uncertain" rationale. The prior AVOID was premised on AI disruption and de-rating, but the operating data show the opposite of deterioration: revenue accelerating to 46%, NDR rising to 139%, four straight beats, and a beat-and-raise. The decline from the IPO is multiple compression (10x sales to ~5.9x), and the seat metrics the bears fear are the ones accelerating. With every sell-side target 30%+ above the current price and 36.8% short interest creating squeeze risk, the stock is not a defensible short, and the balanced setup — accelerating present results against unresolved structural AI-margin and seat-model risk plus a heavy lock-up overhang — does not support an AVOID.

It also does not support a confident BUY. The gross-margin compression from 91% to 79% is real, the AI-credit monetization is producing disclosed customer friction, Claude Design is a credible direct competitor built by a key supplier, and the durability of the seat model under AI is genuinely unknown. The setup is a profitable, fast-growing franchise whose 12-month direction hinges on an unresolvable-today question about whether AI expands or compresses its model. Hold for the growth optionality, monitor gross margin and NDR as the deciding variables, and look to add on overhang-driven weakness toward the low-to-mid teens or upgrade on evidence the AI-margin trajectory has inflected.
