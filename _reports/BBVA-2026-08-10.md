## Executive Summary

BBVA is the most profitable large bank in Europe, and the market has priced it accordingly. H1 2026 net attributable profit of €6.05bn (+11.1% current FX) delivered a 22.2% ROTE and a 37.8% efficiency ratio, with Q2 profit of €3.06bn beating consensus of €2.96bn and management raising the 2026 ROTE target to approximately 21% from "above 20%." Guidance was upgraded across four separate lines: group lending income to high-single-digit growth, South America gross income to high-teens, Mexico cost-of-risk to below 335bps, and Türkiye 2026 profit to around €1bn. A new €2bn extraordinary buyback was announced with the first €1bn tranche commencing 5 August 2026. Consensus EPS for the current year has been revised up 7.3% in thirty days. The operating record is unambiguous: four consecutive beats, the last three by 5.3%, 5.5% and 12.7%.

The difficulty is what that costs. At $28.50 the ADR equates to roughly €24.89 per ordinary share and a market capitalisation near €142bn. Against a company-disclosed tangible book value plus dividends of €11.06, that is 2.25x, and stripping the added-back dividend from the denominator implies true price-to-tangible-book of roughly 2.4x. Solving a Gordon relationship at 21% ROTE and 5% growth, 2.4x implies a cost of equity of about 11.7%. That is defensible, but it means the shares are priced for sustained emerging-market ROTE rather than offering a discount for the risk of not achieving it. Mexico contributed 50.1% of reported group profit in FY2025 and 49.3% in H1 2026, earned at a 3.26% cost of risk. Underneath the improving headline, constant-currency profit growth decelerated from +19.2% in FY2025 to +10.0% in H1 2026; the headline improvement from +4.5% to +11.1% is largely a currency-translation effect that reversed sign.

Three specific frictions keep this at HOLD rather than higher. First, H1 impairments rose 24.2% against loan growth of 17.7%, with Q2 provisions of €1.68bn nearly doubling in Türkiye and rising more than five-fold in Argentina. Second, Spain — 32% of segment profit and the group's low-risk cash generator — decelerated from +11.3% profit growth in FY2025 to +2.3% in H1 2026 and −3% in Q2, because the FY2025 base was flattered by costs falling 0.7% and impairments falling 5.1%. Third, the ADR trades 16% above the consensus mean target of $24.53 and essentially at the high target of $28.58, with an options chain showing put/call open interest of 2.22 and near-term implied volatility of 46.6% against 33.4% realised. The distribution stream — roughly 6.5% trailing on my derived market capitalisation — supports existing positions. New capital should wait.

## Company Overview

BBVA operates five reporting segments plus a Corporate Center. On FY2025 segment profit of €11.60bn (summed from disclosed segment figures), the composition was Mexico €5.26bn (45.4%), Spain €4.18bn (36.0%), Türkiye €805m, South America €726m, and Rest of Business — principally corporate and investment banking booked in the US, Europe and Asia — €627m. The residual between the €11.60bn segment sum and €10.5bn reported group profit is a drag of approximately €1.10bn, roughly 10% of group earnings; H1 2026 shows the same pattern (€6.75bn segments versus €6.05bn reported, a −€0.70bn gap). The Corporate Center attribution is standard for BBVA's structure, but the label, not the arithmetic, is inference; the decomposition into funding costs, ALCO results and central overheads sits in the Form 20-F notes.

The practical description of the business is a high-return Mexican consumer and SME bank, a mature and unusually efficient Spanish bank, three higher-beta franchises in Türkiye, South America and CIB, and nascent digital banks in Italy and Germany whose contribution neither earnings release quantifies. Spain and Mexico together were 81.4% of FY2025 segment profit, so this is not a diversified global universal bank.

BBVA is a foreign private issuer and files Form 20-F annually (FY2025 filed 20 February 2026) plus a high-frequency stream of Form 6-K reports. It files no 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, DEF 14A, or Section 16 Forms 4. There is therefore no US-filed compensation table, no say-on-pay record, and no insider open-market transaction record for this issuer. The absence of insider buying carries no signal here, and standard corporate distress screens (Altman Z, Piotroski F) are inapplicable to a bank balance sheet in any case. Direct retrieval of the 20-F document was blocked, so risk-factor changes, legal-proceedings detail, total assets, liquidity ratios, MREL and the AT1 stack are not verifiable in this analysis. Capital commentary below is limited to CET1, derived RWA, NPL and coverage.

The ADR-to-ordinary-share ratio can be resolved by cross-checking, and every multiple scales with it. FY2025 EPS of €10.5bn / 5,678m dividend-bearing shares = €1.849, which converts to $2.12 — against a reported trailing ADR EPS of $2.067. The 2-3% residual is consistent with FX timing and a trailing-four-quarter versus fiscal-year basis. A 1:1 ratio reconciles; a 2:1 or 1:2 ratio would produce a 100% discrepancy. I proceed on 1 ADS = 1 ordinary share.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (issuer disclosure, 5 February 2026).** Net interest income €26.28bn (+13.9% constant FX), fees €8.22bn (+14.6%), core revenues €34.50bn (+14.1%), net trading income €2.66bn (−23.7%), gross income €36.93bn (+16.3%). Operating expenses of €14.33bn (+10.5%) produced operating income of €22.60bn (+20.4%) and an efficiency ratio of 38.80% (€14.33bn / €36.93bn ✓). Impairments of €6.07bn (+15.5%) left €16.53bn pre-tax-and-minorities against €10.5bn attributable, implying roughly €6.0bn of tax, minority interests and other provisions — plausible given BBVA does not own 100% of Garanti BBVA or its Mexican structures, and given the Spanish sector levy. ROTE 19.3%, cost of risk 1.39%, NPL 2.7% at 85% coverage, CET1 12.70%.

**H1 2026 (issuer disclosure, 30 July 2026).** NII €15.16bn (+18.8% constant), fees €4.57bn (+15.8%), gross income €21.16bn (+16.9%), operating expenses €8.00bn (+17.9% headline, +14.5% excluding named non-recurring items), operating income €13.16bn (+16.2%), net attributable profit €6.05bn. ROTE 22.2% versus 21.7% at March, efficiency 37.81% (€8.00bn / €21.16bn ✓), cost of risk 1.43%, NPL 2.6% at 85% coverage, CET1 12.90% (12.41% pro-forma the new buyback).

Four mechanics matter more than the headline.

**Translation, not acceleration.** FY2025 profit grew +19.2% constant FX but only +4.5% current FX, a 14.7pp translation drag. H1 2026 inverted to +11.1% current versus +10.0% constant, a ~1.1pp tailwind. The headline trajectory improved from +4.5% to +11.1% while underlying constant-currency growth decelerated from +19.2% to +10.0%. Any model built on reported euro momentum is substantially a Mexican peso and Turkish lira view. The counter-reading is that FY2025's constant figure was flattered by an easy base (recovering hyperinflation drags in Argentina and Türkiye, and the €285m Spanish levy booked entirely in 1Q24), making +10.0% the truer run-rate, in which case this is normalisation rather than deterioration. I lean toward that reading, but it still caps forward growth at roughly the level consensus now models.

**Operating leverage requires adjustment.** Headline H1 jaws were negative: revenue +16.9% against costs +17.9%, or −1.0pp. BBVA's disclosed +2.4pp positive jaws uses adjusted cost growth of +14.5%, excluding VAT adjustments in both 2Q25 and 2Q26 plus 2026 voluntary-redundancy charges. The two-sided nature of the adjustment (stripping items from the base period as well as the current one) makes it defensible, and the 37.8% efficiency ratio is calculated on unadjusted costs, so the efficiency claim stands unaided. But redundancy payments are real cash outflows, and the adjusted growth rate flatters near-term economics.

**Credit costs are rising faster than the book, and the increase is concentrated.** H1 impairments +24.2% against loans +17.7% and revenue +16.9%. Segment cost of risk moved: Spain 0.34% → 0.31%, Mexico 3.31% → 3.26%, Rest of Business 0.16% → 0.14% (all improving), against Türkiye 1.94% → 2.36% (+42bps) and South America 2.50% → 2.69% (+19bps). Türkiye NPL rose 3.9% → 4.1%. Q2 provisions of €1.68bn were +21.8% YoY, with Türkiye nearly doubling and Argentina up more than five-fold. The benign interpretation is mechanical: under IFRS 9, a book growing 41.6% in local currency generates large Stage 1/2 provisions without realised deterioration, and coverage of 75% in Türkiye and 89% in South America is consistent with prudence rather than reaction. The point that survives either reading is gearing: group cost of risk of 1.43% is four to five times a Spanish- or German-domiciled peer's, and Mexico's 3.26% consumer/card book means group earnings carry far more provisioning sensitivity to a downturn than the 22.2% ROTE headline implies.

**Spain has become a cash cow, not a growth engine.** Profit went +11.3% (FY2025) → +2.3% (H1 2026) → −3% (Q2 2026). This is not an NII problem: Spanish NII growth actually improved from +3.2% to +4.1%, lending grew +7.4% with market share reaching 14.15%, and asset quality improved (NPL 3.1% → 2.9%, coverage 67% → 71%). The FY2025 base was manufactured by costs falling 0.7% and impairments falling 5.1% on top of only +3.2% NII. Guidance for Spanish NII in 2026 is low-to-mid single digit. Spanish credit costs at 0.31% are at or near cyclical bests, which means the realistic direction from here is worse, not better.

**Capital and distributions.** CET1 built 20bps organically over H1 to 12.90%, against a stated target range of 11.5–12.0% and an explicit policy of distributing excess above the upper bound. If the €2bn buyback consumes the 49bps implied by the 12.41% pro-forma figure, RWA is approximately €408bn (€2.0bn / 0.0049), leaving roughly €1.67bn of further distributable capital above the 12.0% ceiling before additional organic generation, assuming the pro-forma reflects only the new programme. FY2025 remuneration exceeded €9.2bn: a €0.92 cash dividend (€0.92 × 5,678m = €5,224m ✓, +31% YoY, the highest in BBVA's history) plus a €3.96bn buyback. Share capital was 5,709m shares with 5,678m dividend-bearing as of 30 January 2026.

The discipline to note is the announced-versus-executed gap. As of 30 January 2026 only 42.8% of the first €1.5bn tranche of the December 2025 programme had been executed, and the €2.5bn residual of that programme plus the second €1bn of the July 2026 programme are both expressly conditional on corporate-body approval, with ECB authorisation a real gating condition for European bank buybacks. Share-count reduction should be modelled on executed amounts.

## Growth Analysis

The honest forward growth rate is mid-single-digit EPS, assisted by share-count reduction. Consensus (thin — two to three contributors on the ADR line) has current-year EPS at $2.39 and next year at $2.48, implying 3.8% growth. Revisions are positive but not transformational: +7.3% for the current year over thirty days, +1.8% over ninety; +3.6% and +2.9% respectively for next year. This is constructive earnings momentum, not the kind of estimate inflection that reliably precedes re-rating.

Component-level growth deserves the same discount the brief applies. Group performing loans grew +17.7% in constant euros, but the two economically meaningful franchises grew +7.4% (Spain) and +9.9% (Mexico). The group figure is lifted by Türkiye at +41.6% in local currency — a nominal number in a high-inflation economy — and Rest of Business at +52.5%. Constant-currency conversion neutralises exchange rates; it does not neutralise domestic inflation. The same caveat applies to the "nearly 60% loan growth since end-2020" framing and to the "fastest-growing bank in Europe" claim, which is directionally supportable at the Spanish level (+7.4% with share gains, in a low-nominal-growth market) but overstated in magnitude at group level.

The genuine structural growth assets are the customer-acquisition engine and the Mexican franchise economics. BBVA added 11.5 million customers in 2025, 66% digitally, and discloses that Spanish revenue per customer multiplies 3.7x over the first five years and that 75% of new Mexican credit cards sold in 2025 went to customers acquired in the prior five years. That is a compounding annuity if retention holds. Mexican loan market share reached 26.17% by May 2026 and Spanish share 14.15%, with FY2025 share gains of +20bps in Spain, +29bps in Mexico and +36bps in Türkiye.

The AI programme — eight initiatives under a framework called "The Frame," including a digital advisor ("Blue") and a banker assistant, deployed using externally supplied OpenAI technology — has no disclosed contract value, exclusivity, duration or quantified P&L contribution in either release. BBVA is the deploying party and commercial counterparty; the products are BBVA's. Treat it as strategic narrative with zero verified earnings impact until a cost-save or revenue number appears.

The unresolved growth question is Banco Sabadell. Sabadell's board rejected BBVA's bid on 12 September 2025, and neither the FY2025 nor the H1 2026 release mentions Sabadell at all, while BBVA actively distributes excess capital rather than conserving it. The inference that the acquisition did not complete is circumstantial and unverified; the outcome, date, and any break costs or capital consequences are not established in the sources. A renewed domestic consolidation attempt would consume the buyback capacity that currently underpins the shareholder-yield case.

Working from the canonical $28.4996 basis:

The reported 4.05% dividend yield sits above my €0.92-based 3.70% calculation; the difference is most likely a trailing-paid-distribution and FX-timing basis. I use 3.7% on the declared FY2025 dividend for scenario work and treat the ~6.5% total distribution yield as the more relevant return component, with the caveat that roughly a third of the buyback component is conditional on approvals.

The forward earnings multiple looks undemanding at 11.5x; the tangible book multiple does not. At ~2.4x TBV on 21% ROTE and 5% assumed growth, the implied cost of equity is 11.7% (5% + 16%/2.4). For a bank earning half its profit in Mexico with meaningful Türkiye and Argentina exposure, 11.7% is a reasonable required return, which is precisely the problem. The shares are fairly priced for sustained delivery, not discounted for the possibility of mean reversion. The sensitivity is stark and dominates any single quarter of earnings: at a 13% cost of equity the fair multiple falls to 2.0x, roughly 17% below spot; at 10.5% it rises to 2.9x, roughly 21% above. The valuation is a bet on the market's emerging-market risk premium, not on the next beat.

Two market-based cross-checks are consistent with fully valued. The ADR trades 16.2% above the consensus mean target of $24.53 and 18.7% above the median of $24.00, essentially at the high target of $28.58, and the recommendation distribution has degraded to zero buys, one hold and two strong sells. I weight this lightly, since the ADR analyst set is very small and localised sell-side coverage of the Madrid line is far deeper, but analyst targets act as floors for inflection names trading cheaply against accelerating growth. With next-year consensus growth at 3.8%, the implied PEG is close to 3.0; targets here are informative rather than stale. Separately, the earnings yield of roughly 7.4% on the trailing ADR EPS of $2.067 is a thin equity risk premium against prevailing sovereign yields for a book with a 1.43% group cost of risk.

Peer context: European banks broadly cluster in the low-to-mid teens on ROTE and around 1.0–1.5x tangible book. BBVA's 22.2% ROTE genuinely earns a premium multiple, and 2.4x is internally consistent with the return. What it does not offer is margin of safety. I score valuation 4.5: not structurally impaired, but with no identified mispricing and a multiple that requires continued 21% ROTE simply to hold flat.

## Competitive Landscape

Trajectory is the relevant test, and BBVA's is improving. It is gaining share in all three of its core lending markets simultaneously, its 37.8% cost-to-income ratio is at or near the best in European banking (Mexico's segment efficiency is around 30%), and Reuters characterises it as the second-largest euro-zone lender by market value. The customer-acquisition machine — 11.5 million adds, two-thirds digital, with disclosed revenue-per-customer ramp economics — is a genuine widening of the moat rather than a cyclical artefact.

Two qualifications. BBVA explicitly calculates Mexican card share against a set that includes major fintechs rather than only traditional banks. That is a conservative disclosure choice, and it identifies where the competitive threat is: digital disintermediation of a 26.17%-share card book earning a 3.26% cost of risk. Second, Rest of Business grew loans +37.8% in FY2025 and +52.5% in H1 2026 while reporting a 0.2–0.3% NPL ratio and 0.14–0.16% cost of risk, with fees up 56% on debt primary-market issuance. A book compounding at 40–50% with near-zero realised loss is by construction unseasoned: project and corporate finance loss experience lags origination by years, and issuance-linked fee income is cyclical. This segment rose from 5.4% to 7.5% of segment profit without being tested.

The structural read is that BBVA is the opposite trade to a domestic European bank: materially higher growth and ROTE in exchange for emerging-market credit and currency risk a Northern European peer does not carry. Europe is where the deposit-repricing tailwind has been exhausted (Spanish NII +4.1%, guided low-to-mid single digit) and where credit costs can only normalise upward from 0.31%. Emerging markets are where 8–34% profit growth is being generated at 2.4–3.3% costs of risk with provisioning accelerating.

## Risk Assessment

**Mexico concentration.** A Mexican macro shock, peso devaluation, banking-tax change, or US trade/tariff disruption transmits close to one-for-one into half of group earnings. The mix shift toward Türkiye, South America and CIB (18.6% → 23.7% of segment profit) reduces single-country dependence marginally but raises aggregate emerging-market risk, since the segments taking share are higher-risk.

**FX translation.** The 14.7pp gap between constant- and current-currency FY2025 profit growth is the empirical demonstration. Reported euro earnings can swing double digits on peso and lira moves independent of operating performance.

**Hyperinflation accounting.** BBVA attributes improvement in "other income and expenses" and part of Türkiye's +31.8% profit growth to a lower negative hyperinflation impact in Argentina and Türkiye, confirming a material and volatile line item that can move group profit in either direction for reasons unconnected to the franchise. The euro magnitude of the IAS 29 monetary-loss adjustment sits in the unreadable 20-F.

**Provisioning sequencing.** Nominal revenue growth in inflationary economies is currently outrunning credit costs. The specific risk is that this reverses: revenue growth normalises with disinflation while the credit costs of the preceding lending boom persist. Türkiye provisions nearly doubling and Argentina rising more than five-fold in a single quarter is the leading edge to monitor.

**Spanish levy.** €318m charged in FY2025 (€94m in 4Q25) from the tax on net interest income and fees, recorded inside the income-tax line, so it depresses net profit without appearing in the revenue or cost growth rates management discusses. A prior €285m extraordinary levy hit 1Q24. Politically determined and recurring.

**Governance items pending resolution.** A Q2 2026 call transcript listing indicates this was CFO Luisa Gómez Bravo's last results presentation; successor, effective date and reason are unconfirmed and I found no corroborating issuer release. A CFO change at a bank executing a multi-year, multi-billion distribution programme is material for continuity. Separately, BBVA's corporate website carries a dedicated top-level navigation item labelled "Cenyt Case." Permanent site architecture allocated to a single named matter implies legal and reputational significance; substance, status and exposure are entirely unverified and should be resolved from the 20-F legal-proceedings section.

**Valuation risk.** At ~2.4x tangible book there is limited multiple support if emerging-market ROTE mean-reverts. This is the risk that binds the others together.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin — 26 contracts across four expirations — so read it directionally rather than precisely. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 46.6% against 33.4% one-year realised is rich (13.2pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (46.6% at ten days versus 33.4% at six months), which typically indicates near-term event or stress pricing. There is no scheduled company catalyst in that window, since earnings were 30 July, so this most plausibly reflects macro event risk (payrolls, the September FOMC, EM currency stress) transmitted through a euro-area bank with half its earnings in Mexico. Put/call open interest of 2.22 and a reported put skew of 10.7% at the near tenor and 13.4% at six months indicate elevated put demand held in size. Short interest is effectively nil (0.0% of float, 0.6 days to cover), so this is hedging by holders rather than directional bearish positioning.

Options positioning corroborates the fundamental read that downside protection is being bought at the top of the multiple range, and it does not corroborate a near-term upside catalyst. For an existing holder, richly priced near-dated calls make covered-call overwriting a reasonable way to monetise the volatility premium while the buyback executes.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($34.50, +21% price, +25% total return).** Mexico delivers ROTE above 20% with cost of risk beating the improved sub-335bps guidance, Türkiye reaches the ~€1bn 2026 profit expectation, Spanish share gains offset the exhausted repricing tailwind, and the four-year distribution plan converts the full announced buyback capacity plus the derived ~€1.67bn of remaining excess capital into share-count reduction. FY2027 EPS reaches $2.65 against $2.48 consensus on a four-beat record, and the multiple holds at 13x as the market accepts 21% ROTE as structural. The COE sensitivity works in this direction: 10.5% required return supports 2.9x tangible book.

**Base case ($30.00, +5.3% price, +9.0% total return with dividend).** 12.0x FY2027 EPS of $2.50 — consensus $2.48 plus a modest upgrade consistent with the beat record. Constant-currency profit growth settles near +10%, Spain contributes low single digits, emerging-market provisioning rises broadly in line with nominal loan growth without a step-change in realised losses, and the multiple holds roughly flat at ~2.4x tangible book. Return is dominated by the ~3.7–4.0% cash dividend plus 1.0–1.4% of executed buyback, not by re-rating.

**Bear case ($20.50, −28% price, −24% total return).** A Mexican peso devaluation or Mexican employment downturn raises cost of risk toward 4%, Türkiye and Argentina provisioning outruns disinflating nominal revenue, European recession pushes Spanish cost of risk off its 0.31% cyclical low, and the market's required return on an EM-weighted bank rises toward 13%. EPS falls to $2.15 and the multiple compresses to 9.5x / ~1.7x tangible book. The 52-week low of $17.77 shows how quickly this multiple can travel.

Probability-weighting base 50%, bull 25%, bear 25% gives an expected total return of roughly +5%. That is a positive number, but for a business with a 1.43% group cost of risk and half its earnings in a single emerging market, it is well short of the compensation a full position requires, and it is achieved with a 24% left tail.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The holding case rests on the distribution stream — approximately 3.7–4.0% cash dividend plus an executing €1bn buyback tranche representing ~0.7% of shares, against an explicit policy of distributing capital above the 12.0% CET1 ceiling — and on the fact that a 22.2% ROTE franchise gaining share in three markets should not be sold on multiple discomfort alone. Prospective buyers have no entry signal at $28.50, which sits above every published ADR target but one.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price at $24.00–25.00 (approximately 10.0x next-year consensus $2.48 and ~2.0x tangible book) with FY2026 ROTE guidance of ~21% intact. That is a ~13–16% pullback and restores the margin of safety without requiring any fundamental improvement.
2. Two consecutive quarters in which group impairment growth falls below performing-loan growth, with Türkiye cost of risk stabilising at or below 2.36% and South America at or below 2.69%. This would convert the provisioning surge from a deterioration signal into the IFRS 9 volume artefact management describes.
3. Board and ECB approval plus execution of the second €1bn buyback tranche and the €2.5bn December-2025 residual, taking cumulative executed reduction above 3% of share count, alongside confirmation that no renewed Spanish consolidation bid will absorb the capital.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Mexican cost of risk exceeding 3.50% in any quarter, or Mexican segment profit growth turning negative in constant euros. Mexico is half of group profit; this is the single dominant variable.
2. A renewed large domestic acquisition attempt that suspends or cancels the buyback programme, removing the distribution component that currently supports the multiple.
3. Group CET1 falling below 12.0% without a corresponding suspension of distributions, or the FY2026 20-F disclosing a quantified Cenyt-related contingency material relative to annual profit.
4. Spanish net profit declining year-on-year for two consecutive quarters with cost of risk rising above 0.40%, which would signal that Spain has moved from mature cash cow to cyclical drag while EM provisioning is still building.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q3 2026 release, expected late October 2026, is the natural decision point: it tests whether the 22.2% ROTE holds against a Türkiye/Argentina provisioning trajectory that steepened in Q2, and whether the second buyback tranche has been approved. Between now and then, the checkable items are the 6-K confirming the CFO succession, the ECB rate path, and Mexican peso stability.

## Conclusion

BBVA's operating performance is the strongest in European banking and the disclosure quality supporting it is above median: the releases give constant- and current-currency growth for every major line, name the specific items excluded from adjusted cost growth on both sides of the comparison, and footnote which distributions remain conditional. Four consecutive earnings beats, a ROTE target raised mid-year, four separate guidance upgrades, a 37.8% efficiency ratio and 20bps of organic capital build in a half all point the same direction.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a purchase is entirely in the price and the composition of the risk. At ~2.4x tangible book, the shares require sustained 21% ROTE to stand still, and that ROTE is earned at a 1.43% group cost of risk with 49% of profit sourced from Mexico and a further quarter from Türkiye, Argentina and an unseasoned CIB book compounding above 40%. Underneath the improving headline, constant-currency profit growth halved, Spain slowed to +2.3%, headline jaws turned negative, and impairments grew 24.2% against 17.7% loan growth. Consensus targets sit 16% below spot, and the options market is paying 46.6% near-term volatility against 33.4% realised to hold puts at a 2.22 open-interest ratio.

Own it for the roughly 6.5% trailing distribution stream and the franchise trajectory; do not add here. New capital gets a materially better entry at $24–25, and there is nothing in the current data that requires paying up for it.
