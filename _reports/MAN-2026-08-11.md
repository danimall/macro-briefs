## Executive Summary

ManpowerGroup has passed through the worst year in its modern history and is visibly recovering. FY2025 produced a GAAP net loss of $13.3M on $17,957.1M of revenue, a 0.8% operating margin, an effective tax rate of 114.2%, and operating cash flow of negative $104.1M. Six months later, Q2 2026 delivered revenue of $4,860.2M (+7.5% reported, +6.1% organic constant currency), operating profit of $112.0M, adjusted EPS of $0.99 against a $0.95 consensus, and a fourth consecutive earnings beat. Covenant leverage improved from 2.78x to 2.51x against a 3.5x maximum, and fixed-charge coverage rose from 2.72x to 2.98x. The distress case that justified prior avoidance has been retired.

What replaces it is a fair-value problem. Two adjustments matter. First, the headline GAAP swing from a $25.3M Q2 2025 operating loss to a $112.0M Q2 2026 profit is inflated at both ends: the prior-year quarter carried an $88.7M impairment and the current quarter carries a $30.0M gain on the Jefferson Wells U.S. divestiture booked inside S&A. Stripping both, operating profit moved from roughly $63.4M (1.40% margin) to roughly $82.0M (1.69%), genuine progress of about 29%, and consistent with management guiding only ~10bp of year-over-year adjusted EBITDA margin improvement for Q3. Second, the recovery is being manufactured through cost control rather than demand: revenue grew 6.1% organic constant currency while gross profit grew 1%, with gross margin down 80bp to 16.1%. Ex-items S&A was flat in reported dollars ($700.4M to $698.3M) and down roughly 2% in constant currency. Almost all of the earnings improvement came from holding expense flat.

At $56.09 the stock trades 15.5x FY2026 consensus EPS of $3.62 and 11.6x FY2027 consensus of $4.85, 0.185x EV/sales, and 8.3x annualized adjusted EBITDA. It sits 3.2% below its 52-week high, roughly 123% above its 52-week low of $25.15, and essentially at the $57.33 consensus mean target. Near-term estimates are being cut — current quarter down 5.1% and next quarter down 4.5% over 30 days — while FY2027 rose 1.3%. Our base case is $61 (+8.8%), bull $78 (+39%), bear $34 (-39%). That distribution is roughly flat on a probability-weighted basis and does not support new capital. **HOLD**, with no urgency for prospective buyers and a specific re-entry level in the mid-$40s.

## Company Overview

ManpowerGroup places temporary and permanent workers and sells adjacent workforce services in more than 70 countries through roughly 2,100 offices with approximately 25,400 internal employees. Revenue is transactional and short-duration. In Q2 2026 the service-line split was Staffing & Interim $4,314.8M (88.8%), Outcome-Based Solutions & Consulting $306.3M (6.3%), Permanent Recruitment $120.6M (2.5%), and Other $123.5M (2.5%).

Three brands run on divergent trajectories. **Manpower** (commercial and light-industrial temporary staffing) grew 8% in constant currency, its fifth consecutive quarter of growth, and now represents roughly 65% of consolidated gross profit. **Experis** (professional and IT staffing) saw revenue fall 2%, an improvement from -9% in Q1, but gross profit fell 6% organic constant currency, with U.S. Experis interim revenue down $62.8M in H1 and Northern Europe Experis interim down $19.7M in Q2 alone. **Talent Solutions** (RPO, MSP, Right Management) posted flat revenue with gross profit down 4% organic constant currency.

Europe is approximately 65% of consolidated revenue and roughly 85% of revenue is non-U.S. France alone is 51% of Southern Europe; Japan is 57% of APME. This is a European industrial-cycle business with a U.S. listing and reporting currency.

Two disclosure points matter for how the business should be modeled. There is **no meaningful contracted backlog**. Deferred revenue was $37.7M at June 30, 2026, down from $45.6M, and the 10-Q states the company does not disclose unsatisfied performance obligations for contracts with original expected duration of one year or less or where revenue equals the amount it has the right to invoice; other remaining obligations are not material. Forward visibility comes from weekly assignment volumes. Any model treating deferred revenue as backlog would be misapplying the disclosure. Separately, the 10-Q states no single client represents a significant portion of revenue, a genuinely low customer concentration that is a real positive.

## Financial Analysis

### The trough and its cash cost

FY2025 revenue of $17,957.1M rose 0.6% reported but fell roughly 2.1% in constant currency; operating profit fell 50.9% to $150.1M. That 25:1 ratio of profit decline to revenue decline is the central fact about this business. H1 2026 gross profit of $1,503.3M against S&A of $1,393.0M (ex-gain) means **S&A absorbs 92.7% of gross profit**. A 5% increase in gross profit (~$75M) at flat S&A would lift operating profit roughly 68%; the same arithmetic in reverse explains FY2025.

FY2025 cash generation was worse than earnings. Operating cash flow of -$104.1M less $57.3M capex produces approximately -$161M free cash flow, against roughly $66.7M of dividends and $37.5M of buybacks, funded from the balance sheet and a December 2025 bond issue. The dividend was reset from $3.08/share declared in 2024 to $1.44 in 2025 by shifting to a $0.72 semi-annual rate, re-declared May 8, 2026. Cumulative impairments now stand at $807.4M of goodwill, $139.5M of tradenames and $30.6M of reacquired franchise rights, a long record of acquisitions failing to earn their cost of capital.

H1 operating cash outflow of $129.0M included a $230.5M working-capital use. We do not annualize this: staffing working capital consumes cash as volumes build and releases in Q4, MAN's H1 is structurally the heaviest draw, and H1 2025 was substantially worse. Q2 2026 free cash flow of roughly -$9M versus -$207M a year earlier is a genuine sequential improvement. But on an H1 basis the dividend was not covered by operations — operating cash flow -$129.0M, capex $14.8M, dividends paid $33.5M, bridged by $87.5M of Jefferson Wells proceeds. A divestiture funding a distribution is tolerable for two quarters and not a durable structure.

Roughly half the H1 free-cash-flow improvement came from capex falling from $31.3M to $14.8M on lower capitalized software, which helps cash today but is in tension with a strategy premised on technology and AI transformation.

Gross debt fell $634M and debt/capitalization fell from 44.9% to 33.1%, but this is a refinancing artifact: €500M of 3.750% notes issued December 15, 2025 pre-funded the January 2026 redemption of €500M of 2018 notes due June 2026, temporarily inflating both cash and gross debt at year-end. On the correct measure, **net debt rose $57M in H1 2026 despite $87.5M of divestiture proceeds**; absent the sale it would have risen roughly $145M. A widely circulated "debt-to-equity of 4.45" reconciles exactly to total assets divided by equity ($9,160.1M ÷ $2,059.6M), which is an equity multiplier. True interest-bearing debt/equity was 0.50x at June 30.

Covenant headroom is real: at 2.51x against a 3.5x maximum, covenant EBITDA could fall roughly 28% at constant net debt before breach. The $600M revolver (December 2030) is fully undrawn. The FY2025 MD&A indicates the facility restricts dividends and repurchases above a specified leverage level, which we recorded as 3.0x; at 2.78x on December 31, 2025 the company sat approximately 0.22x from that restriction. We flag this as characterized from MD&A rather than verified against the credit agreement.

The next refinancing event is €400M ($455.9M) due June 2027, now in current maturities. Credit ratings per the FY2025 10-K appear as Moody's Baa1 stable and S&P BBB– negative. A three-notch split is anomalous and may be a transcription issue; if BBB–/negative is accurate, MAN refinances one notch from high yield.

Three further quality flags. The H1 provision for credit losses rose to $5.4M from $1.9M, with the allowance up to $76.3M and DSO up to 56 days from 55 — small in absolute terms, directionally worth monitoring. Receivables factoring is disclosed but **not sized**, with the 10-Q noting capacity "can be reduced or eliminated at any time," an unquantifiable off-balance-sheet liquidity dependency. And goodwill of $1,483.4M equals roughly 70% of $2,106.3M book equity, leaving tangible book of approximately $207.2M, or 12.6x price/tangible book.

## Growth Analysis

The revenue line is genuinely accelerating and geographically two-speed. Q2 2026 constant-currency country data: Latin America strong (Colombia +29.7%, Chile +29.9%, Argentina +36.2%), Spain +17.7%, Poland +15.0%, Italy +7.0%, the U.K. returning to growth at +1.9%, France flat at 0.0%, Germany -2.6% in Q2 and -8.4% in H1, Netherlands -5.9%. Germany's continued contraction is the clearest read on European industrial weakness and is where MAN's manufacturing exposure concentrates. Against a European backdrop of ECB tightening to 2.25%, weak German industrial orders, and a materially elevated regional recession probability, MAN's growth is running somewhat counter to macro, which raises rather than lowers the risk that the current trajectory is not the durable one.

The problem is conversion. Revenue +6.1% organic constant currency producing gross profit +1% is a mix downgrade. The growing lines are the lowest-margin ones (light industrial/commercial, Latin America); the declining lines are the highest-margin ones (Experis professional/IT, permanent recruitment, Right Management outplacement). Management attributed roughly 60bp of the staffing gross-margin decline mainly to business mix and the Jefferson Wells divestiture, with permanent recruitment and other services each adding ~10bp of pressure. Part of the drag is therefore a deliberate portfolio choice rather than demand erosion, which cuts in MAN's favor on the interpretation question but does not change the arithmetic.

The earnings growth case rests on three stacked levers:

1. **$200M of permanent cost savings targeted by 2028.** Sized against annualized revenue of roughly $18.74B, this is ~107bp of revenue and ~7.2% of annualized ex-gain S&A of ~$2,786M. Full realization and retention would roughly double the adjusted EBITA margin from FY2025's 1.88% toward 2.9-3.0%, still short of the 3.51% achieved in 2022. The 7.2%-of-S&A framing makes it operationally plausible. The open question is retention: in a price-competitive intermediary business, savings can be competed away through lower bill rates rather than banked as margin.
2. **Operating leverage.** With S&A at 92.7% of gross profit, modest gross-profit inflection produces large percentage earnings moves. Consensus embeds this: FY2027 EPS of $4.85 versus FY2026 $3.62 is +34% growth on high-single-digit revenue growth.
3. **Tax normalization.** ETR was 114.2% in FY2025, 48.0% in H1 2026, 42.0% in Q2, guided ~44% for Q3. On roughly $200M of annualized pretax income, moving from 44% to 32% adds approximately $24M of net income, or ~$0.51/share, with no operational improvement. But the unlock requires Northern Europe to return to sustained profitability so valuation allowances can release, and Northern Europe was still loss-making at the operating-unit level in H1 2026 (-$6.2M). France's corporate rate moves the wrong way, to 36.125%, and the U.S. Work Opportunity Tax Credit was not renewed for 2026.

Management's AI initiatives — targeting AI embedded across ~70% of revenues by year-end 2026, a 67% reduction in time-to-fill, $50-100M of partnership revenue this year — should be calibrated. $50-100M on $18.7B is 0.3-0.5% of revenue, immaterial as a revenue line. The productivity angle is the plausible value driver, and none of these metrics is verifiable from filings.

Estimate revisions do not support chasing. Current quarter consensus fell 5.1% and next quarter 4.5% over 30 days; FY2026 fell 2.1%; only FY2027 rose, by 1.3%. This is nowhere near the +30% ninety-day revision velocity that flags a genuine consensus-lagging inflection, and the near-term cuts came after the July 16 beat.

The valuation splits along the classic trough-cyclical seam. On sales, 0.185x is at the low end of staffing's historical 0.2-0.4x range. On earnings and EBITDA, 15.5x current-year and 8.3x adjusted EBITDA are unremarkable. Both are true because margins are at a trough. The EBITDA figure also flatters: adjusted EBITDA excludes $40-60M per year of restructuring and transformation charges that are cash costs, so a cash-adjusted EV/EBITDA is closer to 9.4x.

The strongest bull argument is the PEG. At 11.6x FY2027 against 34% expected FY2026-to-FY2027 EPS growth, the multiple looks undemanding. The correct discount to apply is that this growth is one-year operating-leverage recovery from a trough, not a durable rate — the five-year adjusted EBITA margin series (3.10% → 3.51% → 2.62% → 2.26% → 1.88%) shows secular de-rating, not cyclical oscillation around a stable mean. A low PEG on a single-year recovery step is not the same signal as a low PEG on a compounding franchise.

Peer comparison is not clean. The stated peer median P/E of 37.2x is distorted: NSP prints -119x on losses, FA 165x, RHI 37.2x on depressed earnings. Korn Ferry at 16.1x is the most usable comparable, and MAN at 15.5x current-year sits essentially in line with a business that carries a higher-margin consulting mix. That is fair, not cheap.

The re-rating has already happened without a corresponding estimate inflection. Form 4 evidence places the stock at $29.73 on January 1, 2026 and $28.66-$31.01 in mid-February. The move to $56.09 is roughly +80% to +95% in six months, and +123% off the $25.15 52-week low. Over the same window FY2027 consensus rose 1.3%. Multiple expansion did essentially all of the work. The specific mispricing that existed in February — distress pricing on a business with 28% covenant headroom — has been corrected, and no replacement mispricing is identifiable at 15.5x/11.6x.

Analyst positioning corroborates: 1 strong buy, 3 buy, 8 hold, stable for three months; targets low $47, mean $57.33, median $57, high $72. The stock trades 2.2% below the mean. On a stock at a mid-teens multiple with falling near-term estimates, we do not treat the consensus target as a floor to be revised upward; that treatment applies when growth is accelerating and estimates are rising, and here the near-term estimates are being cut.

Our base case is $61: 12.5x FY2027 consensus of $4.85, a multiple consistent with mid-cycle staffing and with MAN's structural quality relative to Korn Ferry. Bull $78 approximates the recovery math (3.0% adjusted EBITA margin on ~$19B revenue, ~$27M intangible amortization, ~$65M net interest, 32% tax rate → roughly $6.90 EPS) at ~11x, discounted for the 2028+ timing and execution risk. Bear $34 reflects European volumes rolling over, gross profit -5% against sticky S&A, operating profit approaching zero, a goodwill charge against the $1,483.4M balance, and multiple compression back toward the Q1 2026 trading band.

## Competitive Landscape

MAN competes with Randstad and Adecco globally, with Robert Half and Hays in the Experis niche, and increasingly with digital labour marketplaces and AI-enabled sourcing. Barriers are modest in commercial staffing and meaningfully higher in licensed, heavily regulated markets — France, Italy, Japan, Germany, the Netherlands — where compliance infrastructure and operating licenses have genuine value. A U.S. franchise network generated $5.1M of H1 fees, with system-wide Q2 revenue of $5.3B against $4.9B consolidated.

The competitive economics disclose the limits plainly. Consolidated gross margin is ~16% and S&A consumes ~93% of it. There is no proprietary technology moat and switching costs in transactional staffing are low. Temporary staffing gross margin is bill-rate minus pay-rate; in loose labour markets clients push bill rates down. MAN is currently experiencing that while mix shifts toward the lowest-margin line.

The unresolved question that matters most for the thesis is whether the Experis decline is cyclical or structural. The AI-displacement reading is that permanent recruitment ($120.6M of Q2 revenue) and professional/IT contracting are the functions most exposed to automated matching and clients' internal AI-assisted sourcing, and those are exactly the lines declining, while physical-workforce sourcing with payroll and co-employment risk absorption is growing. The 10-K explicitly identifies the risk that clients use AI in place of contingent labour. The cyclical reading is that enterprise IT and professional hiring ordinarily lag the industrial cycle and will revert, supported by Experis's sequential improvement from -9% to -2%. We cannot resolve this from available evidence, and no Randstad, Adecco, Robert Half or Hays comparison data was obtainable to establish whether the gross-margin compression is industry-wide or company-specific. That missing cross-check is the single largest gap in the analysis and is a direct reason to keep the competitive-position score low.

Trajectory, which matters more than level: gross margin down 60bp in FY2025 and a further 80-100bp in 2026 while revenue accelerated, adjusted EBITA margin down in four of five years, and $807.4M of cumulative goodwill impairment. The competitive position is deteriorating on economics even as volumes recover.

## Risk Assessment

**Cyclicality with no backlog and European concentration.** ~65% European revenue, ~85% non-U.S., no contracted backlog. FY2025 quantified the sensitivity: -2.1% constant-currency revenue produced -50.9% operating profit.

**Japan Fair Trade Commission investigation (new).** Disclosed in the Q2 2026 10-Q and absent from the FY2025 10-K: on June 2, 2026 the JFTC inspected offices associated with MAN's Japanese subsidiary as part of an investigation into Japan's temporary staffing industry. Japan is ~57% of APME revenue, roughly $586M in H1 or ~6.3% of group revenue. MAN states it cannot predict scope, duration, outcome or materiality; no provision has been recorded. An inspection is an investigative step, not a finding, and because the inquiry is industry-wide MAN may be one of several parties. It is nonetheless the largest unquantifiable contingency in the filings.

**Goodwill.** $1,483.4M against $2,106.3M of equity and $207.2M of tangible book. No triggering event was identified for an interim Q2 test, while the filing warns that further deterioration "may result in the recognition of goodwill impairments."

**Refinancing and rating.** €400M due June 2027, now in current maturities, into a possible BBB–/negative S&P profile. Available liquidity fell from $1,620.6M to $930.2M over six months.

**Tax as a persistent suppressant.** ~44% guided ETR, France at 36.125%, WOTC not renewed for 2026, valuation allowances tied to Northern European losses.

**Labour-law change.** France, Italy, Germany, the Netherlands and Japan are named exposures. Mexico's 2021 outsourcing law is cited in the 10-K as having materially adversely affected that business, a concrete precedent for permanent legislative revenue loss.

**Cyber.** The FY2025 10-K discloses that an external penetration-testing firm penetrated the company's defenses and identified vulnerabilities that could facilitate loss of personal data, against millions of candidate records under GDPR and comparable regimes. That specificity is unusual among issuers.

**Off-balance-sheet.** Guarantees and standby letters of credit of $684.9M at June 30, up from $626.1M; unsized receivables factoring; unrecognized tax benefits of $41.0M with audits open in eight jurisdictions.

**Unresolved event.** An 8-K was filed August 11, 2026 carrying Items 5.02 and 9.01 with an EX-99.1 press release (accession 0001193125-26-343480). Item 5.02 covers departure or election of directors and certain officers, and the accompanying press release suggests a senior appointment or departure rather than a routine matter. The document could not be retrieved and its subject is unverified. A CEO or CFO transition would materially affect confidence in transformation execution and guidance, and this alone argues against deploying new capital at today's price.

Two risks that are frequently overstated deserve correction. Solvency risk is low: 28% covenant EBITDA headroom, a fully undrawn $600M revolver, 0.50x debt/equity, 2.98x fixed-charge coverage. And Altman Z-style screens misfire here, penalizing a retained-earnings/assets term depressed by decades of buybacks in a 72.9M-share treasury account and an EBIT/assets term measured at a cyclical trough.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 50.6% sits below 58.3% realized one-year volatility (a -7.7% spread, characterized as cheap) at the 12th percentile of the current chain, with a flat term structure (50.6% near versus 52.3% far) and a nearest-expiry implied move of ±6.9%. Skew is characterized as call-heavy at -3.0%, put/call open interest is 0.45, and since the May 28 snapshot ATM IV fell from 56.7% to 50.6% while the price rose 88.0% and put/call OI declined from 0.68. Options positioning is therefore not corroborating a bearish view; if anything the structure leans constructive and prices no near-term dislocation. That matters against 18.4% short interest with 4.5 days to cover: part of the move off February lows was plausibly short covering, and remaining short exposure creates upward squeeze mechanics that argue against an aggressive negative call. Cheap IV also means option-based downside protection is comparatively inexpensive for holders unwilling to sell into the move.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (target $78, +39%)

Q2 was a large beat against management's own guidance — roughly 6% organic days-adjusted constant-currency growth against a ~3% guided midpoint — and four consecutive beats support consensus. Manpower has now grown five straight quarters at 8% constant currency and represents ~65% of gross profit; Experis has improved from -9% to -2% and management expects a return to growth. Europe is bottoming: the U.K. returned to growth, Italy is +7.0%, Spain +17.7%. With S&A at 92.7% of gross profit, a 5% gross-profit inflection lifts operating profit ~68%. Stack the $200M cost program (7.2% of S&A, operationally plausible), gross-profit inflection, and tax normalization from 44% toward 32% (+$0.51/share), and the recovery math supports roughly $6.90 of EPS at a 3.0% adjusted EBITA margin — still below the 3.51% achieved in 2022. Covenant leverage is improving on rising EBITDA, not on debt reduction. At 11.6x FY2027 consensus, 0.185x EV/sales, and a $2.6B market cap on $18B+ of revenue in a Wisconsin-incorporated company with anti-takeover protections, the equity carries embedded strategic optionality. Options positioning and 18.4% short interest add near-term upside mechanics.

### Bear Case (target $34, -39%)

The recovery is cost-led and cost programs are finite. Revenue +6.1% organic converting to +1% gross profit means the core economics have not improved; ex-items margin gained 29bp, and Q3 guidance implies only ~10bp of year-over-year adjusted EBITDA margin improvement. The mix shift may be structurally AI-driven: the declining lines (Experis professional/IT, permanent recruitment, outplacement) are precisely the most automatable, and the growing lines are the lowest-margin. Germany is -8.4% in H1, France flat with margin down to 2.4%, the Netherlands -5.9%, and Northern Europe still loss-making after multiple restructuring rounds — into ECB tightening and an elevated European recession probability. Trailing cash generation is negative: FY2025 free cash flow ~-$161M, H1 2026 operating cash flow -$129.0M, net debt up $57M despite $87.5M of divestiture proceeds, H1 dividends funded by asset sales. Extreme operating leverage reverses violently. Goodwill of $1,483.4M against $207.2M of tangible book means a downturn produces both an earnings collapse and an impairment. The €400M June 2027 maturity may price wide. The JFTC inquiry is unquantified. Five-year TSR of -60.4% against a peer group's +21.7% is not a one-year stumble, and no insider bought a share in the open market — including when the stock traded at $28-31 in February. Near-term estimates are already falling 5%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case: $61 (+8.8% from $56.09).** 12.5x FY2027 consensus EPS of $4.85. Multiple anchored to Korn Ferry's 16.1x adjusted downward for MAN's lower-margin mix, weaker cash conversion and goodwill-heavy balance sheet, and cross-checked against the $57.33 consensus mean and $57.00 median.

**Bull case: $78 (+39%).** Approximately 11x the ~$6.90 EPS implied by a 3.0% adjusted EBITA margin on ~$19B revenue with tax normalized to 32%, discounted for 2028+ timing. Consistent with the $72 analyst high, above it because analyst targets are set on FY2027 rather than the full program.

**Bear case: $34 (-39%).** Gross profit -5% with sticky S&A drives operating profit toward zero, a goodwill charge follows, and the multiple compresses toward the Q1 2026 band. The 52-week low is $25.15.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, with the Q3 2026 print (late October) as the first decisive checkpoint against guidance of $0.96-$1.06 adjusted EPS, ~6% organic constant-currency growth, ~16% gross margin and ~10bp of adjusted EBITDA margin improvement.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Gross profit growth of 4%+ in organic constant currency for two consecutive quarters, closing the gap to revenue growth — this is the single piece of evidence that would convert the cost-led recovery into a demand-led one.
2. Positive full-year 2026 free cash flow with the dividend covered by operations rather than divestiture proceeds, plus net debt declining sequentially.
3. Price in the mid-$40s (roughly 9-10x FY2027 consensus, near the $47 analyst low) on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore a margin of safety without requiring any thesis change.
4. Germany and France returning to positive constant-currency growth, plus Northern Europe posting positive operating-unit profit for two consecutive quarters (which would also open the valuation-allowance release and the tax lever).

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. A Q3 2026 miss against the $0.96-$1.06 range, or FY2027 consensus falling below $4.40.
2. Gross margin declining a further 50bp or more year over year in Q3 or Q4 without a divestiture explanation, indicating structural rather than mix-driven compression.
3. Covenant leverage rising above 2.9x, approaching the distribution-restriction level characterized from the MD&A, or a goodwill impairment charge.
4. The August 11 Item 5.02 8-K disclosing the departure of the CEO or CFO, which would put the $200M program's execution in question.
5. Confirmed JFTC action against MAN with a quantified provision.

**Reassessment timeline:** Immediately upon retrieval of the August 11, 2026 8-K; formally at the Q3 2026 report in late October.

## Conclusion

Three prior reports carried an AVOID rating, and on the evidence available then — a GAAP loss, a 114% tax rate, negative free cash flow and an $88.7M impairment — that was a defensible read of a company at the bottom of its cycle. It was also wrong on outcome: the stock has roughly doubled. The current evidence no longer supports AVOID.
