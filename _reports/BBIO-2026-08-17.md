## Executive Summary

BridgeBio has completed the hardest part of the transition from development-stage to commercial biopharma. Acoramidis — Attruby in the U.S., Beyonttra elsewhere — generated $222.4M of U.S. net product revenue in Q2 2026, up 211% year over year and 23.2% sequentially, at a 95.3% product gross margin. Royalty revenue from Bayer (EU) and Alexion (Japan) rose from $1.6M to $15.4M. Total revenue has gone $9.3M (FY2023) → $221.9M (FY2024) → $502.1M (FY2025) → $438.2M in the first half of 2026 alone. Three additional NDAs are on file, two with Priority Review and assigned PDUFA dates (BBP-418 on November 27, 2026; encaleret on May 8, 2027). The competitive backdrop improved materially in the June quarter: AstraZeneca/Ionis' eplontersen failed CARDIO-TTRansform in ATTR-CM in a trial where 57% of patients in each arm were already on a stabilizer, and Alnylam cut FY2026 TTR guidance to $4.2–4.5B and lost roughly a quarter of its market value on July 30. Management describes a "stabilizer-first" market growing 19% in the quarter and has deferred an upward revision to its 30–40% peak-share target until after full CARDIO-TTRansform data at ESC in late August.

The reasons this is not a purchase at $79.86 are arithmetic rather than narrative. First, none of the 95% gross margin is reaching the bottom line: from Q1 to Q2 revenue rose $49.2M while operating expense rose $50.3M, and the operating loss was flat at roughly $106–107M. Second, consensus has responded by marking losses up, not down — FY2026 EPS went from -$2.15 to -$2.68 in 30 days and FY2027 from $0.59 to $0.15, a 74% cut, and the company has missed EPS in each of the last four quarters (Q2: -$0.78 versus -$0.60 expected) while beating on revenue. Third, the capital structure absorbs a large share of commercial success: $2,505M of convertible notes, ~$912M of deferred royalty obligations at 22.4% and 10.7% effective rates, and a Funding Agreement that takes 5% of *global* acoramidis net sales with a documented ability to step to 10% in 2027 on sales performance. Better commercial results accelerate that cash claim.

Adding notes ($2,505M), royalty obligations (~$912M) and the July preferred (~$934M) to the $15.61B equity value and subtracting roughly $1.6B of pro forma cash produces an enterprise value near $17.9B, about 18.3x annualized Q2 revenue and roughly 4.5x the company's own >$4B peak-sales ambition. That price requires successful execution on three simultaneous launches with no margin of safety. We rate the shares HOLD with a $88 base case (+10.2%), $125 bull (+56.5%) and $52 bear (-34.9%).

## Company Overview

BridgeBio operates a decentralized "hub-and-spoke" model in genetic disease, founded 2015, headquartered in Palo Alto, and reports as a single operating segment. Revenue flows through four distinct channels that behave very differently:

- **U.S. Attruby**: sold directly to specialty pharmacies and specialty distributors, booked as net product revenue. WAC rose to $19,790 per 28-day supply in January 2026 (~$258k annualized gross; realized net is materially lower).
- **EU/EPO**: licensed to Bayer Consumer Care, which sells Beyonttra; BridgeBio earns royalties "starting in the low-thirties percent" plus milestones.
- **Japan**: licensed to Alexion (acoramidis, low-teens royalty) and Kyowa Kirin (infigratinib, up to mid-twenties).
- **Royalty monetization counterparties** (LSI Financing/Blue Owl and CPPIB Credit Europe; Acoramidis Royalty SPV/HealthCare Royalty): these *receive* cash from acoramidis sales and are accounted for as debt.

The late-stage pipeline is the second half of the equity story: BBP-418 for LGMD2I/R9 (NDA accepted, Priority Review, PDUFA November 27, 2026, no advisory committee, approval resting on a 12-month interim analysis of FORTIFY); encaleret for ADH1 (Priority Review, PDUFA May 8, 2027, MAA filed in the EU); and oral infigratinib for achondroplasia (NDA submitted, PDUFA unassigned, U.S. launch targeted mid-2027). The largest long-dated option is RECLAIM-HP, a Phase 3 of encaleret in chronic hypoparathyroidism — a population the company sizes at ~200,000 U.S./EU patients versus the far smaller ADH1 base — with topline expected late 2027 or early 2028.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and mix.** Q2 2026 total revenue was $243.7M versus $110.6M a year earlier. Product revenue of $222.4M (+211%) and royalty revenue of $15.4M (from $1.6M) more than offset a $31.6M decline in license and services revenue, which contained a $30.0M Alexion milestone in the prior-year quarter. That mix shift is a quality improvement: recurring product and royalty revenue replaced lumpy milestones. Deriving Q1 by subtraction (H1 less Q2), total revenue grew 25.3% sequentially ($194.5M → $243.7M) and product revenue 23.2% ($180.6M → $222.4M). Geographic mix in Q2 was U.S. 91.2%, EMEA 8.1%, APAC 0.7%.

**Gross margin and the absence of sequential operating leverage.** Product gross margin was 95.3% ($222.4M revenue less $10.5M cost of goods sold). That should mean explosive incremental profitability. It does not, because operating expense is rising in lockstep. Q2 operating expense was $350.8M (R&D $149.4M, +34%; SG&A $186.3M, +44%) against Q1's $300.5M. Revenue rose $49.2M; opex rose $50.3M; loss from operations moved from -$105.96M to -$107.08M. Year over year the operating loss narrowed (Q2 2025: -$134.3M), so the trend is not deteriorating — but breakeven now depends on a future decision to stop spending rather than on the current trajectory. SG&A exceeding R&D reflects a company staffing three pre-approval launches simultaneously, including deployed neuromuscular field teams for BBP-418 ahead of the November PDUFA.

**Below the line.** Q2 net loss was -$155.9M (-$0.78 per share on 195.8M weighted shares); H1 -$322.4M. Total other expense of -$48.8M is dominated by $41.3M of *noncash* interest on deferred royalty obligations ($81.2M in H1, up $31.2M), more than triple the $13.3M of cash interest on $2.5B of convertible notes. This asymmetry deserves emphasis: the converts are cheap (0.75%–2.50% coupons, 1.0%–2.8% effective) and the royalty monetizations are expensive (22.4% and 10.7% effective). Because the royalty obligations amortize on an effective-interest method driven by management's own forecasts of acoramidis sales, rising noncash interest is evidence that internal sales projections are increasing — the 22.2% → 22.4% move should be read as a sign of commercial strength, not deterioration. It also makes GAAP net loss a poor economic proxy in either direction.

**Cash flow quality.** H1 operating cash flow was -$268.4M, modestly better than -$279.9M a year earlier. The item to watch is working capital: accounts receivable consumed $115.0M — 26% of H1 revenue — rising from $139.4M to $254.5M. Against Q2 revenue that is roughly 95 days ($254.5M / $243.7M × 91), high for a specialty-pharmacy channel where 30–45 days is typical. There are benign explanations (a steep intra-quarter ramp loads period-end receivables; the balance includes $17.6M of Bayer partner receivables; no credit losses have been recorded), but the direction has been one-way. Inventory absorbed a further $28.2M. Note the 10-Q presents cash flow only on a six-month basis, so a standalone Q2 burn cannot be isolated; doubling H1 to -$537M annualized embeds a continuing receivable build, while excluding the AR and inventory build gives roughly -$250M annualized. The 2026 outcome likely sits between, nearer the reported figure while revenue compounds.

**Balance sheet.** Cash and marketable securities were $720.2M at June 30 against total liabilities of $3,723.2M and a stockholders' deficit of -$2,504.9M. The negative equity is a construction artifact — $4.14B of accumulated deficit, $2.5B of converts with no equity component, $533.3M of treasury stock, and royalty proceeds booked as debt — and mechanical distress screens will misread it. The meaningful questions are the maturity ladder and liquidity:

Pro forma liquidity of roughly $1.65B ($720.2M plus ~$933.9M of July preferred proceeds, before fees and Q3 burn) comfortably covers the $550M March 2027 maturity, and management has stated it intends to apply remaining 2033 Note proceeds to that obligation. The 2027 and 2031 Notes became convertible for the July 1 – September 30, 2026 window on satisfaction of the stock-price condition. Capped calls hedge only the 2027 ($62.12 cap) and 2029 ($131.58) series; the 2031 series, struck at $49.81 and deep in the money, is unhedged dilution. Base conversion across all four series is 37.85M shares — 19.4% of existing shares, or 16.2% post-issuance ownership dilution. A separate 54.1M-share figure applies only on specified corporate events triggering make-whole conversion-rate increases and is a conditional ceiling, not current diluted capitalization.

**Financings.** The July 1 preferred issuance — 933,900 shares of Series A cumulative convertible participating preferred to Sixth Street and HealthCare Royalty (a KKR business), initially convertible into 6,777,704 common shares at a derived $137.79 — raised roughly $934M at a >100% premium with only 3.47% of existing shares (3.35% post-issuance ownership dilution) of conversion overhang. That is an unusually shareholder-friendly structure for a pre-profitability biotech. The cost has been shifted into a senior, cumulative, participating dividend payable in cash or in kind at the company's election, and **the dividend rate is not disclosed in the available filing text**. Buying stock at $66.96 has proven well-timed against $79.86; buying it at all, given negative equity and three unlaunched products, is debatable. Both readings are consistent with the disclosed facts.

## Growth Analysis

The revenue trajectory is the strongest element of the file and is not in dispute. Four sequential quarters of product growth, +23.2% in the most recent, with the U.S. launch still concentrated in treatment-naïve patients and ex-U.S. royalties only beginning to scale. Jefferies expects acoramidis to exceed $1B in global net sales in 2026 across BridgeBio-booked U.S. sales plus Bayer and Alexion territories. Annualizing Q2 gives $974.7M of BridgeBio revenue.

Three pipeline launches are the second growth vector, and their timing is unusually tight: BBP-418 approval decision in November 2026, encaleret in May 2027, infigratinib launch mid-2027. Priority Review with no advisory committee on the first two is generally favorable. The pediatric ADH1 cohort work, an infigratinib hypochondroplasia Phase 2 update in 2H 2026, and RECLAIM-HP in chronic hypoparathyroidism extend the runway well past 2028.

The countervailing evidence sits in earnings rather than revenue. Consensus FY2026 EPS moved from -$2.25 (90 days ago) to -$2.15 (30 days) to -$2.68 now — a 24.8% deterioration in a month, almost certainly the Street marking up launch spending after the Q2 print. FY2027 went from $0.56 to $0.15, a 72–74% cut, pushing the expected crossover to profitability out by at least a year. Four consecutive EPS misses (-31.1%, -21.4%, -32.1%, -4.8%) with concurrent revenue beats describe a company that is converting demand into topline faster than the Street models and converting topline into spending faster still. The strong-buy-signal pattern this system looks for — estimate revisions above +30% over 90 days — is absent here in inverted form: revisions are sharply negative. Revenue momentum and earnings momentum are pointing in opposite directions, and the valuation is set on the latter's eventual arrival.

## Valuation Assessment

Conventional multiples are unusable: trailing and forward P/E are both negative (-22.6x on -$3.785 trailing EPS), and next-year consensus of $0.15 implies a 532x multiple that carries no information. Peer P/Es (NBIX 21.4x, ASND 18.2x, EXEL 15.3x, BMRN 180.3x, ARWR -38.4x; median 21.4x) describe companies at different points of the profitability curve and provide no direct anchor.

**Enterprise value.** $15.61B equity + $2,505M notes + ~$912M royalty obligations + ~$934M preferred - ~$1.6B pro forma cash ≈ **$17.9B**. Excluding the preferred (which may sit in mezzanine rather than debt) gives $17.0B. Against annualized Q2 revenue of $974.7M that is 17.4x–18.3x. Against the company's own >$4B peak-sales ambition it is roughly 4.5x — before accounting for the Funding Agreement's claim on 5–10% of global net sales and the preferred's senior participating dividend.

**Forward revenue check.** A reasonable FY2027 build — U.S. Attruby $1.35–1.5B, royalties $100–130M, an initial BBP-418 contribution of $50–100M — gives roughly $1.6–1.7B. At $79.86 the shares imply roughly 12x forward revenue for a company still one to two years from GAAP profitability. Commercial-stage biotech comparables with 40%+ growth typically clear 5–9x; 12x embeds both an upward peak-share revision and pipeline success.

**Peak-earnings check.** Assume global acoramidis net sales of $4B by 2031: BridgeBio books ~$2.6B of U.S. product revenue at 95% gross margin plus ~$350M of ex-U.S. royalties, roughly $2.95B of revenue. With a mature four-product operating expense base of $1.3–1.6B, operating income lands at $1.2–1.5B and net income at $1.0–1.2B after tax. On 235–240M fully diluted shares (existing plus base note conversion plus preferred), that is $4.25–5.10 of EPS. At 20x and discounted five years at 10%, acoramidis alone supports roughly $53–63 per share. The gap to $79.86 is the market's capitalization of BBP-418, encaleret (including hypoparathyroidism) and infigratinib. That gap is defensible — three approvals in genetic disease with orphan pricing can justify $3–6B of risk-adjusted value — but it is optionality, and it is already in the price.

**Sell-side context.** Mean target $108.43, median $108.00, high $157.00, low $80.00. Note that the low target equals spot, and the rating distribution has degraded from 6 strong buy / 15 buy / 2 hold three months ago to 5 strong buy / 14 buy / 3 hold. Recent target moves reported at headline level: JPMorgan to $111 from $100, Truist to $107 from $102, Citi to $94 from $93 while maintaining Hold. Consensus is positive but drifting less so. Because BridgeBio is not a cheap stock on any forward metric, analyst targets here do not function as floors — they reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price.

Valuation scores 4.5. The multiple is not indefensible, but it requires three sequential launch successes plus an upward peak-share revision to be earned, and no identifiable mispricing supports paying it today.

## Competitive Landscape

The ATTR-CM competitive structure shifted in BridgeBio's favor during the June quarter, largely through a competitor's failure. Eplontersen (AstraZeneca/Ionis) failed Phase 3 CARDIO-TTRansform. The design detail matters more than the headline: 57% of patients in each arm were on a stabilizer at baseline and another 24% added one mid-trial. If the read that silencer-on-top-of-stabilizer adds no meaningful benefit holds, the failure removes a competitor and simultaneously undercuts the silencer class's combination-expansion path. Separately, Alnylam reported Amvuttra revenue of $1.01B for the quarter (+106%, ~4% below consensus) and cut FY2026 TTR guidance to $4.2–4.5B citing second-line demand normalization; the shares fell 25–29%.

BridgeBio's differentiation is biochemical (near-complete ≥90% TTR stabilization versus Pfizer's tafamidis) and increasingly supported by comparative outcome data: a 41% reduction in outpatient worsening heart failure versus placebo in ATTRibute-CM; post-hoc kidney findings (chronic eGFR slope +2.47 mL/min/1.73m²/year, 13.7% UACR reduction through Month 30); and three independent real-world datasets versus tafamidis including a propensity-matched TriNetX analysis of 286 pairs showing a 37% reduction in composite cardiovascular events (HR 0.63, p=0.002). Two cautions travel with that evidence. The kidney findings are post-hoc, which is why the company initiated ASCEND-ATTR, a 36-month Phase 4 imaging study. And propensity-matched real-world comparisons are observational and vulnerable to channeling bias; three converging datasets strengthen the case but do not substitute for a head-to-head randomized trial, of which none exists.

Scale should be stated honestly. Amvuttra generated $1.01B in the quarter against Attruby's $222M; Alnylam's miss was against elevated expectations on a much larger base and it attributed the shortfall to second-line normalization, not to first-line losses. Both companies flagged weak second-line uptake, which locates the real contest among treatment-naïve patients — where BridgeBio says its growth is concentrated and where management claims a 2–3 point front-line share gain against a market growing 19% in the quarter. That share figure is a management estimate the CEO himself qualified with reference to Pfizer Vyndamax inventory dynamics.

Competitive trajectory is clearly improving, which supports a 7.0 score. The full CARDIO-TTRansform dataset at ESC in late August can either confirm or complicate the stabilizer-first read, and management has explicitly tied its peak-share revision to that disclosure.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-molecule and counterparty concentration.** Essentially all revenue derives from acoramidis. Five customers each exceeded 10% of Q2 gross revenue at 28.8%, 18.8%, 18.0%, 15.0% and 13.2% — approximately 93.8% combined. Gross accounts-receivable concentration across the top five was ~93.0% at June 30, 2026 versus ~96.3% at year-end 2025. The *composition* changed (Bayer and Alexion fell below 10% as milestones rolled off, replaced by specialty distributors), but direct counterparty concentration is essentially unchanged. This is a channel and working-capital risk rather than end-demand risk, and it should not be read as diversification.

**The royalty step-up.** The Funding Agreement takes 5% of global acoramidis net sales, "may adjust to a maximum rate of 10% in 2027" on undisclosed sales-performance conditions, is capped at $950M on $500M of gross proceeds, and carries a 22.4% effective rate. At the current $890M annualized product run rate, the difference between 5% and 10% is roughly $44.5M of annual cash outflow. The trigger thresholds are not quantified in the disclosure, so whether current performance activates them is unknown. Both royalty agreements are secured by acoramidis-specific assets and contain change-of-control buy-out provisions, which raises the cost of any acquisition of BridgeBio and, combined with a classified board, blank-check preferred, and a 25% threshold for special meetings, makes a takeover approach expensive.

**Pricing and policy.** Accrued rebates nearly doubled from $45.9M to $80.0M in six months as gross-to-net builds toward steady state. Q2 risk factors specifically flag reference and most-favored-nation pricing proposals, Medicaid Drug Rebate compliance, and pharmaceutical-specific tariffs scheduled to take effect mid-2026 — the tariff risk factor was rewritten after the Supreme Court invalidated reciprocal tariffs on February 20, 2026. BridgeBio depends entirely on third-party CMOs with single-source supply for certain clinical candidates and $158.0M of noncancellable purchase commitments.

**Regulatory binaries.** BBP-418's November 27 decision rests on a 12-month interim analysis of FORTIFY. Encaleret follows in May 2027. Infigratinib has no PDUFA assigned. Launch organizations for all three are being staffed and expensed now against contingent revenue.

**New risk factor added in Q2.** The preferred stock's seniority on dividends and liquidation, its participating feature, its blocking effect on common dividends, and the obligation to seek stockholder approval to lift the 19.9% conversion cap now appear as a dedicated risk factor.

**Governance and related parties.** No open-market insider purchases were recorded in 2026 through July 30. The CEO sold repeatedly between March and June at $65.7–$75.6, reducing directly held shares from ~675,000 to ~528,000 under a 10b5-1 plan adopted May 13, 2026 covering 100% of net RSU/PSU vesting plus up to 120,000 trust shares. Directors were more aggressive: Jennifer Cook sold 112,422 shares at $90.00 and 36,167 at $84.00 on July 9, plus 88,737 at $66.62 and 37,167 at $70.00 earlier, leaving roughly 8,400 shares. Much of the director activity is exercise-and-sell on options struck as low as $8.45, which is diversification behavior; director Charles Homcy retains 919,876 shares and insiders plus beneficial holders own 40.9%. The honest read is no positive insider signal with negative texture, not an exodus. Separately, KKR simultaneously occupies four positions: large common holder (selling 5,000,000 shares in the August secondary at a reported $78.00, with no proceeds to the company and no new shares issued), majority owner of a secured royalty creditor ($207.7M of related-party deferred royalty carrying value), participant in the July preferred via HealthCare Royalty, and joint bookrunner on its own sale. All disclosed; none improper on its face; it reduces the informational simplicity of the equity, and the size of KKR's residual stake is unverified, leaving potential further supply.

**Short interest.** 13.3% of float, 23.3M shares, roughly 7 days to cover. Meaningful bearish institutional positioning that also creates upside mechanics on positive ESC or PDUFA outcomes.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 43.7% sits below one-year realized volatility of 47.0% and in the 14th percentile of the current chain, with a flat term structure (43.7% near, 43.0% far). Since the July 30 snapshot, ATM IV fell 6.8 points from 50.5% and the put/call open-interest ratio collapsed from 0.78 to 0.19 on an unchanged price. Hedging demand has drained out of the name into a calendar containing full CARDIO-TTRansform data at ESC, an October 8 Commercial Day, and a November 27 PDUFA. Positioning is call-tilted (P/C volume and OI both 0.19), and the 48.1% skew flagged as elevated put demand attaches to a 3-day expiry with a 0.09 put/call volume ratio, so it carries little information. Worth noting for anyone sizing exposure: the November 20 expiry (±19.2% implied) falls one week *before* the BBP-418 decision, so the approval binary is priced in the January 2027 tenor (±23.7%), not the 3-month. With realized vol running above implied and options in the 14th percentile, protection or call-replacement of stock is unusually inexpensive here relative to the event calendar. The complacency in positioning argues against the market having pre-discounted a bad ESC outcome.

Risk factors score 4.0: the concentration, the royalty step-up, the undisclosed preferred dividend, the DSO trend, and two dated regulatory binaries are specific and not obviously reflected in an 18x-revenue multiple.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $125 (+56.5%)

Full CARDIO-TTRansform data at ESC confirms that adding a silencer to a stabilizer provides no additive benefit, and BridgeBio raises peak share above the 30–40% band toward $5B+ of peak acoramidis sales. Attruby sustains 15–20% sequential growth through 2027 as treatment-naïve share compounds and Bayer/Alexion royalties scale. BBP-418 is approved on November 27 and encaleret in May 2027, converting three staffed launch organizations from cost to revenue; RECLAIM-HP data in late 2027 opens a ~200,000-patient hypoparathyroidism market. Opex growth decelerates below revenue growth in Q4 2026 or Q1 2027, and the operating loss inflects toward breakeven during 2027. The 13.3% short base covers into approval news. On $1.9–2.1B of 2027 revenue at 13–14x EV/sales, equity supports roughly $120–130, below the $157 sell-side high.

### Bear Case — $52 (-34.9%)

ESC data complicates the stabilizer-first read — for example, HELIOS-B-style evidence that silencers add value in stabilizer-treated patients gains traction — and the promised peak-share upgrade does not arrive. BBP-418 receives a CRL on the 12-month interim analysis, stranding a deployed neuromuscular sales force. Most-favored-nation or reference pricing, plus mid-2026 pharmaceutical tariffs, compress realized net pricing on a $258k list-price drug with Medicare-heavy exposure. DSO continues extending past 95 days, revealing that reported growth is converting to cash more slowly than it appears. Opex continues outrunning revenue, the Funding Agreement steps to 10% in 2027, and the company returns to the ATM or another structured raise on worse terms. The 52-week low is $46.81; $52 represents roughly 8x a $1.6B 2027 revenue base with the pipeline option value largely written off.

### Base Case — $88 (+10.2%)

Attruby compounds at a decelerating but healthy 12–18% sequential rate, FY2026 revenue lands near $1.0B and FY2027 near $1.6–1.7B, and BBP-418 is approved with a modest 2027 contribution. Management raises peak share after ESC but within the range already discussed. Operating losses narrow year over year without a clean sequential inflection until mid-2027. The shares hold roughly 12–13x forward revenue.

Probability-weighted at 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear gives approximately $86.45, an expected return of +8.2% against 44% implied volatility. That is not adequate compensation for the downside asymmetry, which is why the rating is HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** Existing owners have a legitimate 12-month appreciation thesis anchored on dated catalysts. New capital has no urgent entry signal at $79.86.

**Reassessment timeline:** three sequential decision points — ESC Congress (late August 2026, full CARDIO-TTRansform plus management's deferred peak-share update), Commercial Day (October 8, 2026, launch-readiness and spending framework across three products), and the BBP-418 PDUFA (November 27, 2026). Q3 results in early November are the first clean test of operating leverage.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Operating leverage appears.** Q3 or Q4 2026 sequential revenue growth exceeding sequential operating expense growth by more than $25M, i.e., an operating loss narrowing below roughly $85M sequentially. This is the single most important missing datapoint.
2. **ESC confirms and peak share is raised.** Management raises peak acoramidis share above 40% or peak sales above $5B with full CARDIO-TTRansform data supporting the no-additive-benefit read.
3. **Price toward $62–66** with product revenue growth still above 15% sequentially. At $64 the acoramidis-alone valuation floor is met and the pipeline becomes free optionality, which would clear the BUY bar.
4. **BBP-418 approval on November 27** combined with disclosure of the preferred dividend rate at 8% or below.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. **BBP-418 CRL or delay**, particularly on the FORTIFY interim analysis, given the pre-deployed field force.
2. **DSO above 110 days** at Q3, or H2 2026 operating cash burn exceeding $325M — either would indicate reported growth is not converting to cash.
3. **Sequential product revenue growth below 8%** in any quarter through mid-2027, which would signal the treatment-naïve share gain has stalled.
4. **A common-equity raise under the ATM at or below $75**, or activation of the Funding Agreement step-up to 10% without a corresponding upgrade to peak-sales guidance.
5. **FY2027 consensus falling below breakeven again** after the current $0.15, indicating the profitability crossover has been pushed past 2028.

**Position management for holders:** with implied volatility in the 14th percentile of its own chain and below realized, buying January 2027 protection or converting stock into calls ahead of ESC and the November PDUFA is inexpensive relative to the event density.

## Conclusion

BridgeBio's score trajectory — 5.5 in June, 5.8, 6.2 with a single BUY in mid-July, then 5.6 at end-July — reflects a genuine tension that the Q2 print has now clarified rather than resolved. The financial and management dimensions have not improved. Opex grew faster than revenue sequentially, the Street cut FY2027 EPS 74% in a month, EPS has missed four quarters running, receivables absorbed a quarter of first-half revenue, and the capital structure charges 22.4% on the royalty tranche with a step-up that penalizes success. Insiders have bought nothing in 2026 while selling steadily, and a single sponsor sits on four sides of the capital structure.

Price is essentially unchanged from the prior review at $79.86, so risk/reward has not improved through weakness — the mix of evidence has simply become more informative in both directions. At roughly 18x annualized revenue and 12x forward 2027 revenue, with an acoramidis-only floor near $53–63 and pipeline optionality supplying the rest, the shares require three launches to work as planned. Our base case of $88 offers 10.2% upside against 34.9% downside to $52, and a probability-weighted expected return near +8% does not compensate for that skew. Hold existing positions through the ESC and PDUFA sequence; deploy new capital either on demonstrated operating leverage in the Q3 or Q4 print, or at $62–66 where the pipeline comes free.
