**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

BioMarin is a rare-disease biotech in the middle of a deliberate strategic reset. Revenue has compounded from $2.10B in 2022 to $3.22B in 2025, and FY26 guidance of $3.825–3.925B implies roughly 20% growth, boosted by the April 27, 2026 close of the $4.8B all-cash Amicus Therapeutics acquisition. The pipeline is delivering on the most important variable: VOXZOGO's Phase 3 in hypochondroplasia met its primary endpoint on May 20, 2026 (+2.33 cm/year vs. placebo), with submissions expected in 2H26 and a potential first-in-class launch in 2027. On the revenue line, this is a growth story.

The complication is that revenue growth has not converted into earnings growth. Net income fell to $349M in 2025 from $427M in 2024 despite the higher top line, dragged by a $240M ROCTAVIAN wind-down (including a $119M inventory write-off), a $221M Inozyme IPR&D charge, and R&D rising from $747M to $922M. Q1 2026 non-GAAP EPS of $0.76 missed the $0.94 consensus and fell 43% YoY on GAAP, and management lowered FY26 non-GAAP EPS guidance to $4.85–5.05 even as it raised revenue guidance; the Amicus deal is slightly dilutive in year one. The balance sheet, pristine pre-deal (Altman Z 4.65, D/E 0.25), now carries roughly $3.7B of new non-convertible debt, and interest expense is a fresh headwind in both GAAP and non-GAAP results.

The valuation reflects this tension. At $55.70 the stock trades at ~8.6x forward earnings versus a biotech peer median around 25x, and analyst targets ($50 low, $87.84 mean, $120 high) sit well above the current price. The forward multiple is genuinely low, but it discounts real issues: management's unproven integration capability, VOXZOGO orphan-exclusivity step-downs beginning 2028, active Ascendis patent litigation, and an open DOJ subpoena. The setup is asymmetric to the upside, but the burden of proof on management execution keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY. The 2027 launch cadence and Amicus synergy realization are the catalysts that would force an upgrade.

## Company Overview

BioMarin (founded 1997, San Rafael, CA, ~3,221 employees) develops and commercializes therapies for ultra-rare, genetically defined diseases. The model rests on small patient populations, very high per-patient pricing, and protection via orphan-drug exclusivity and patents rather than volume. As of FY25 the company had eight commercial products:

- **VOXZOGO** (vosoritide, achondroplasia): $926.9M in FY25, ~29% of revenue, the primary growth engine.
- **Enzyme-replacement therapies:** VIMIZIM $792M (MPS IVA), NAGLAZYME $485M (MPS VI), PALYNZIQ $433M (PKU), ALDURAZYME $208M (Sanofi-marketed, 39.5–50% royalty), BRINEURA $186M (Batten), KUVAN $99.6M (PKU, declining on generics).
- **ROCTAVIAN** (hemophilia A gene therapy): $35.6M, in voluntary market withdrawal as of December 2025.

The April 2026 Amicus close added **GALAFOLD** (Fabry disease) and **POMBILITI + OPFOLDA** (late-onset Pompe), diversifying the commercial base. Customer concentration is meaningful (three customers account for 37% of revenue), and roughly half of sales are non-USD, creating FX and government-order timing volatility.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $2.10B (2022) → $2.42B (2023) → $2.85B (2024) → $3.22B (2025), ~53% over three years. VOXZOGO is the swing factor.

**Earnings deterioration is the key tension.** Operating income dropped from $484M to $409M. The mechanism:
- **ROCTAVIAN restructuring of $240M**, including a $119M inventory write-off that compressed gross margin from 79.7% to 77.7%.
- **R&D up $747M → $922M**, driven by a $221M Inozyme IPR&D charge (BMN 401, ENPP1 deficiency) and VOXZOGO hypochondroplasia spend.
- SG&A up to $1.15B.

**Balance sheet — before and after Amicus.** Pre-deal (YE25): $2.05B cash/investments, only $600M of 1.25% convertible 2027 Notes, current ratio 5.21, Altman Z 4.65 (safe zone). Post-Amicus the profile shifts materially: new debt of $850M 5.5% 2034 notes plus a ~$2.8B term loan. Q1'26 long-term debt already rose to $1.43B with $850M escrowed. The cushion is thinner and interest expense is now a recurring drag.

**Cash flow quality is solid.** FY25 operating cash flow $828M, FCF $725M (22.5% margin), ~7% FCF yield on the current ~$10.8B market cap. Q1'26 OCF was $221M. The cash generation supports the leverage taken on for Amicus, but free cash flow will absorb higher interest going forward.

**Q1 2026 (pre-Amicus close):** Revenue $766M (+3% YoY), GAAP net income $106M. The non-GAAP EPS miss reflected a $31M charge for a failed NAGLAZYME process-qualification campaign (−$0.12 EPS) and Amicus pre-close/interest costs (−$0.07). VOXZOGO grew only +3% as large Q4'25 government orders pulled demand forward, though the patient base grew ~20% YoY — a timing artifact rather than a demand problem.

**Guidance divergence:** raising revenue while cutting EPS is the cleanest summary of the equity's current state.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has two legs: the acquired commercial base and the organic pipeline.

**Acquired growth** comes from Amicus (Galafold, Pombiliti/Opfolda). This is real revenue but unproven in BioMarin's hands; the FY26 growth figure is mechanically driven by consolidating Amicus, not by underlying acceleration of the legacy book.

**Organic pipeline catalysts are clustering in 2026–2027:**
- **VOXZOGO hypochondroplasia Phase 3** — submissions 2H26, potential first-in-class launch 2027. This expands the addressable market beyond achondroplasia and is the single most important positive development.
- **BMN 401 (ENPP1 deficiency) Phase 3 topline due Q2 2026,** potential first-in-disease launch 2027.
- **BMN 333** (long-acting CNP) Phase 2/3 started, data 2027; **BMN 351** (DMD oligonucleotide) showing dose-dependent dystrophin increases.
- PALYNZIQ approved for adolescents 12+ (Feb 2026); VOXZOGO sNDA for full (vs. accelerated) approval submitted April 2026.

**Estimate revisions are mixed and require care.** The current-quarter +1162% revision is a swing from a −$0.09 estimate to +$0.96 — arithmetically dramatic but not a clean signal. The more durable reads: current-year estimates are down 7.5% over 90 days (to $4.94), next-year down 4.0% (to $6.46), and only the near quarters show modest upward drift (+4.8% next quarter, +3.3% current quarter over 90 days). This is a revenue-growth story with full-year EPS estimates being trimmed, not the +30% revenue-plus-rising-estimates profile that signals a fundamental turn. The growth thesis is genuine on the top line and the pipeline; the earnings translation remains the open question.

## Valuation Assessment

At $55.70, the stock trades at **~8.6x forward earnings** (on ~$6.46 next-year consensus) versus a **biotechnology peer median P/E of 25.2** (EXEL 17.4x, HALO 25.2x, KRYS 40.3x; ARWR and CYTK are loss-making). On trailing earnings the multiple is ~40x, but that figure is distorted by the ROCTAVIAN write-offs and IPR&D charges and is not the right anchor. The forward number is the relevant one, and it is conspicuously cheap relative to peers and to the company's own ~20% headline revenue growth.

The discount is not unjustified. Three things explain it: (1) the gap between revenue growth and EPS growth, with FY26 non-GAAP EPS guided lower; (2) leverage and integration risk from a debt-funded acquisition this management has not executed before; and (3) binary VOXZOGO risk (orphan-exclusivity step-downs from 2028, active Ascendis litigation). A low multiple here reflects skepticism that the revenue growth converts to durable per-share earnings, not pure mispricing.

The asymmetry is favorable. Analyst targets range $50–$120 with an $87.84 mean and $85.50 median — the median implies ~54% upside, and even the low target sits only ~10% below current price. The recommendation distribution has shifted positively (6 strong-buy / 14 buy / 6 hold currently, versus 4 strong-buy three months ago). If the 2027 launches land and Amicus synergies convert revenue to EPS, a re-rating toward even a discounted 12–14x forward multiple on $6.50+ EPS supports an $80–90 stock. The reason this is not a higher valuation score: the re-rating depends on execution milestones not yet delivered, and full-year estimates are currently being trimmed, not raised.

## Competitive Landscape

BioMarin's orphan franchises are defensible but narrow — each carries an identifiable challenger:
- **Achondroplasia/growth disorders (the most strategically important battleground):** Ascendis Pharma (active multi-jurisdiction patent litigation with BioMarin), BridgeBio/QED (infigratinib), Tyra Biosciences. VOXZOGO's label-expansion ambitions into hypochondroplasia, ISS, Turner, and Noonan are the upside, but each expansion invites competition and can also jeopardize orphan-exclusivity status under the IRA if a drug gains a second indication.
- **PKU:** PTC Therapeutics vs. KUVAN/PALYNZIQ; KUVAN faces generics.
- **MPS disorders:** RegenxBio and others pursuing gene-therapy approaches that could, long-term, displace enzyme-replacement therapy.

The competitive trajectory is roughly stable-to-slightly-improving on the strength of the hypochondroplasia readout and Amicus diversification, offset by intensifying drug-pricing reform (IRA negotiation, the MFN executive order and Dec 2025 proposed CMS demonstration models, and EU pharma-package exclusivity cuts). The ROCTAVIAN commercial failure shows that even well-funded launches in this space can miss.

## Risk Assessment

**Company-specific risks weigh on the score:**

1. **VOXZOGO concentration plus patent litigation.** The single largest product (~29% of revenue) is in multi-jurisdiction litigation against Ascendis, faces direct competitive threats, and sees U.S. achondroplasia orphan exclusivity step down beginning 2028 — relatively near-term for a stock whose thesis depends on this franchise.
2. **Amicus integration and leverage.** First large debt-funded M&A under a relatively new management team; ~$3.7B new debt, Hatch-Waxman/Galafold litigation exposure inherited, and assumption of unknown liabilities. Synergy realization is unproven.
3. **Open DOJ subpoena** (first disclosed FY2023 10-K, still open) regarding sponsored testing programs for VIMIZIM and NAGLAZYME — potential Anti-Kickback/False Claims exposure, under-discussed.
4. **Drug-pricing policy:** IRA negotiation (with the orphan-exemption-loss risk tied directly to VOXZOGO's label-expansion strategy), MFN executive order, CMS demonstration models.
5. **Manufacturing concentration:** Novato, CA (seismic zone) is the sole site for several products; single-source raw materials. The Q1'26 NAGLAZYME qualification failure shows the execution risk is live.
6. **Revenue lumpiness and FX:** ~50% non-USD; periodic large government orders create quarter-to-quarter volatility (as VOXZOGO's soft Q1'26 demonstrated).

**Options Market Signal:** No options data was provided for this report. The stock's beta of 0.24 indicates returns are driven by idiosyncratic binary catalysts (trial readouts, litigation outcomes, integration milestones) rather than market direction. Short interest is moderate at 6.0% of float with 4.8 days to cover, indicating no crowded short and no meaningful squeeze dynamic.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($85–120):** Amicus integration delivers on synergies, the 2027 VOXZOGO hypochondroplasia and BMN 401 launches arrive on schedule, and the legacy enzyme-therapy book holds. Revenue compounds toward $4.5B+ and the EPS gap closes as one-time charges roll off and interest is digested. A re-rating from 8.6x toward a still-discounted 13–14x forward on $6.50+ EPS supports $85–90, with the high analyst target of $120 reflecting fuller pipeline credit. The discount to peers and to its own growth is the source of the upside.

**Bear Case ($45–50):** Amicus integration disappoints or proves dilutive longer than guided, the Ascendis litigation goes against BioMarin or competitive entry erodes VOXZOGO, the DOJ subpoena produces a material settlement, and full-year EPS estimates continue to drift lower. The market keeps the multiple compressed, and leverage limits flexibility. Downside to the low analyst target of $50 (~10% below current) or modestly worse.

The fulcrum is whether revenue growth converts to EPS growth. Management is executing a coherent reset — Elliott involvement in 2023–24, new CEO/strategy in 2024, the ROCTAVIAN exit, Inozyme and Amicus M&A, cost transformation — but has not yet demonstrated that the debt-funded acquisition produces durable per-share earnings.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is constructive — a cheap forward multiple, real pipeline catalysts, and an asymmetric setup — but the earnings-translation and integration questions keep it below BUY-grade.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of non-GAAP EPS at or above guidance, demonstrating Amicus is converting revenue to earnings; and full-year consensus EPS estimates turning positive (currently −7.5% over 90 days).
- BMN 401 Phase 3 topline (Q2 2026) positive and VOXZOGO hypochondroplasia submission accepted in 2H26 with a clean regulatory path.
- Evidence of Amicus synergy realization quantified on an earnings call.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse Ascendis litigation ruling or material DOJ settlement.
- VOXZOGO demand (patient adds, not order timing) decelerating below ~10% YoY, indicating competitive erosion ahead of the 2028 exclusivity step-down.
- FY26 non-GAAP EPS guidance cut a second time, or leverage metrics deteriorating beyond the deal plan.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 results (BMN 401 readout window), the 2H26 VOXZOGO submission, and the next two quarterly prints that show whether Amicus is accretive on the timeline management has guided.

## Conclusion

BioMarin is a revenue-growth and pipeline story trading at a forward multiple that prices in deep skepticism about earnings durability. The skepticism is partly earned — net income fell in 2025, Q1'26 EPS missed, full-year EPS guidance was cut, and a debt-funded acquisition rests in the hands of management that has not yet proven it can integrate. But the VOXZOGO hypochondroplasia Phase 3 success is a genuine positive, the analyst target distribution implies meaningful upside, and the balance sheet, while levered, remains sound with ~$725M FCF. The management-quality score is the binding constraint: TSR has badly lagged peers (67.77 vs. 124.75 indexed), there has been no open-market insider buying, and recurring leadership turnover sits alongside the strategic pivot.

The risk/reward is asymmetric to the upside, but the catalysts that would justify a BUY — EPS conversion, synergy proof, the 2027 launch cadence — have not yet been delivered. **HOLD**, with a clear path to upgrade on the next two earnings prints and the BMN 401 readout.
