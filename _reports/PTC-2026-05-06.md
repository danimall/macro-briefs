## Executive Summary

PTC Inc. is a high-quality subscription software business trading at a substantial discount to both its own history and its application software peers. At ~$137.55 (as of early May 2026), the stock sits 37% below its 52-week high of $219.69, yet fundamental performance has improved: Q2 FY26 (March quarter) reportedly delivered $2.69 non-GAAP EPS vs. $2.13 consensus, revenue of $774M that exceeded estimates, and management raised full-year guidance. The company generates 31%+ FCF margins on a capital-light model with only ~$11M in annual capex, producing $857M in FCF in FY25.

The disconnect between price and fundamentals stems from three factors: (1) macro concerns about manufacturing customer spending amid tariffs, (2) CEO/CFO turnover within 18 months creating execution uncertainty, and (3) the market's difficulty valuing PTC's hybrid on-premises subscription model where reported revenue growth (19-21%) significantly overstates underlying ARR momentum (8-9% CC). At approximately 19-20x forward FCF and ~20x forward P/E, the valuation embeds a level of pessimism inconsistent with the company's earnings trajectory, $1.1-1.3B annual buyback, and sticky 95% recurring revenue base.

The primary thesis: PTC's combination of high-quality recurring revenue, exceptional FCF generation, aggressive capital return, and portfolio simplification under new management should drive 15-20%+ EPS growth over the next 12-18 months. The current valuation offers meaningful margin of safety even if organic growth moderates toward the low end of guidance.

## Company Overview

PTC serves 30,000+ discrete manufacturers globally with mission-critical product lifecycle management (PLM) and computer-aided design (CAD) software. The business splits roughly 62% PLM (Windchill, Codebeamer, ServiceMax) and 38% CAD (Creo, Onshape). Key verticals include industrials, aerospace/defense, electronics, automotive, and medical devices.

The business model generates 95% recurring revenue, including on-premises subscription, perpetual support, SaaS, and hosting services. On-premises licenses are recognized upfront under ASC 606 while SaaS/support revenue is recognized ratably, creating quarter-to-quarter lumpiness in reported results. Annual recurring revenue (ARR) of $2.49B as of Q1 FY26 is the truest measure of underlying business health; however, following the March 2026 Kepware/ThingWorx divestiture, the continuing-company ARR (excluding divested IoT) was approximately $2.34B growing at 9.0% in constant currency as of Q1 FY26.

Under CEO Neil Barua (since February 2024), PTC is executing a portfolio simplification strategy: divesting the underperforming IoT segment (Kepware/ThingWorx sold to TPG for $523M in March 2026), shifting professional services to partners, and concentrating R&D on core PLM/CAD. This has driven non-GAAP operating margins from 38.9% (FY24) to 47.5% (FY25).

## Growth Analysis

PTC's growth profile is best understood as a compound of three vectors:

1. **Organic ARR growth (7.5-9.5% CC ex-IoT, per FY26 guidance):** Driven by seat expansion within existing accounts, Codebeamer cross-sell into the installed base, and ServiceMax upsell. This is steady-state growth underpinned by mission-critical stickiness.

2. **SaaS transition acceleration:** Windchill+ and Creo+ represent the next growth leg as customers migrate from on-premises to cloud-native versions. This transition is early-stage — meaningful ARR impact likely FY27+, but it provides a multi-year growth runway and potential for re-rating toward SaaS multiples.

3. **Share count reduction (~7% annually at current pace):** Mechanical EPS growth from buybacks at depressed prices. At $137/share with $1.2B in annual buybacks, PTC can retire 8.7M+ shares annually.

**Growth Risks:**
- Manufacturing cyclicality amid tariffs could slow new bookings (though renewal rates remain high given switching costs)
- Q2 FY26's reported revenue beat mitigates near-term demand concerns
- FY26 CC ARR guidance of 7.5-9.5% is a relatively wide range, suggesting management acknowledges macro uncertainty

**Estimate Revisions:**
Current quarter estimates have risen 7% over 90 days. Full-year FY26 is roughly flat (-1.9% over 90 days) but rising over 30 days (+0.6%). The Q2 beat should catalyze upward revisions. This is not a high-velocity revision story, but the direction is positive following the reported beat-and-raise.

## Valuation Assessment

PTC's current valuation at $137.55 (approximately 119.5M shares outstanding, ~$16.4B equity market cap):
- Forward P/E: ~20x (Phase 1 data: 20.24x forward P/E)
- Forward P/E on FY27E: ~16.4x ($8.36 consensus)
- Trailing FCF yield: ~5.2% ($857M / ~$16.4B market cap)
- Forward FCF multiple: ~19x on post-divestiture FY26 guided FCF of ~$850M

**Peer Comparison:**
Among application software peers, PTC's ~20x forward P/E represents a meaningful discount. The selected peer set (Tyler 49.4x, Okta 58.1x, Guidewire 64.2x, FICO 33.1x, Dynatrace 60.4x) trades at significantly higher multiples, though these are not direct PLM/CAD competitors. PTC's direct competitors (Dassault Systèmes, Siemens Digital Industries, Autodesk) also generally trade at higher forward multiples.

This discount is partially justified: PTC grows ARR at 8-9% CC vs. many software peers growing revenue 15-25%+. But PTC's 31%+ FCF margin and aggressive buyback create per-share value growth of 15-20% annually, comparable to faster-growing but less profitable peers. The discount appears too wide.

**What is the market pricing in?** At 20x earnings with consensus EPS of $7.71 (FY26) and $8.36 (FY27), the current price implies either: (a) material downside risk to estimates, (b) a terminal multiple well below software norms, or (c) structural concern about growth deceleration. Given the reported Q2 beat-and-raise, (a) seems unfounded near-term. The stock hit $219 just three months ago on essentially the same fundamental picture.

**The market is mispricing:** PTC's decline from $219 to $137 as if it reflected fundamental deterioration, when Q2 earnings demonstrate the opposite. The decline reflects macro fear about tariff impacts on manufacturers, not demonstrated weakness in PTC's bookings.

## Competitive Landscape

PTC occupies a defensible position in PLM/CAD with exceptionally high switching costs. Key competitive dynamics:

**Windchill (PLM):** A leading platform for large discrete manufacturers, particularly in aerospace/defense. Competitors Siemens Teamcenter and Dassault ENOVIA target overlapping customers, but migrations take years and cost millions.

**Creo (CAD):** Strong in parametric CAD for complex mechanical engineering. Siemens NX and Dassault CATIA are primary competitors. Creo's installed base provides a persistent revenue stream with high renewal rates.

**Codebeamer (ALM):** Growth driver in the software-defined vehicle/product space. Requirements management and systems engineering for products with embedded software — a structural growth area as everything from cars to medical devices becomes software-intensive.

**ServiceMax (Field Service):** Unique asset among PLM/CAD companies, providing field service management capabilities that create cross-sell opportunities within the manufacturing customer base.

**Structural tailwinds:**
- Software-defined products require integrated PLM+ALM → benefits Codebeamer/Windchill combination
- AI-enabled product development requires clean product data → Windchill's data platform is foundational
- Regulatory complexity (FDA, AS9100) creates compliance lock-in for PTC customers

The competitive position appears stable-to-improving, with Codebeamer competing effectively in the ALM space and the portfolio simplification (IoT divestiture) allowing concentrated investment in core strengths.

## Risk Assessment

**Manufacturing Cyclicality & Tariffs (MODERATE):**
PLM/CAD software is deeply embedded in engineering workflows — historically, manufacturers cut discretionary spending before mission-critical software licenses. Q2 results suggest demand resilience so far.

**C-Suite Turnover (MODERATE):**
CEO change (Feb 2024) and CFO change (Jan 2026) within 18 months during a strategic transition creates execution risk. New CFO Jennifer DiRico brings public-company CFO experience from Commvault and senior finance leadership roles at Toast and Nuance, but is new to PTC during a critical strategic transition period.

**ASC 606 Lumpiness (LOW-MODERATE):**
Revenue volatility from contract duration mix makes quarter-to-quarter results unpredictable. A shorter-duration quarter could trigger a "miss" even if underlying ARR is on track.

**SaaS Transition Timing (LOW-MODERATE):**
If Windchill+/Creo+ adoption accelerates faster than expected, near-term revenue could be impacted by the shift from upfront license to ratable recognition (the "SaaS transition penalty"). Conversely, slower adoption means PTC retains current revenue mix longer.

## Conclusion

PTC represents a compelling risk/reward at $137.55. The stock trades at ~20x forward earnings with a meaningful discount to application software peers, despite generating superior FCF margins (31%+), executing an aggressive buyback ($1.1-1.3B/year), and demonstrating demand resilience through a reported Q2 beat-and-raise. The 37% drawdown from highs reflects macro fear about manufacturing cyclicality that PTC's own results appear to contradict.

The score trajectory shows improvement from the March low (6.5 overall) back to 7.0, driven by the valuation compression creating better entry points while fundamentals remained intact. The prior Risk Factors decline (from 6.5 to 5.5) reflected justified tariff concerns; the Q2 beat partially addresses this, supporting a recovery to 6.0.

The combination of 8-9% organic ARR growth, 7% annual share count reduction, and potential multiple re-rating from 20x toward 22-25x creates a path to 30-50%+ returns over 12 months with limited structural downside given the subscription model's resilience.
