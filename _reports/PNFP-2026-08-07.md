## Executive Summary

Pinnacle Financial Partners as it trades today came into existence on January 1, 2026. Legacy Pinnacle (Tennessee, CIK 1115055) and Synovus Financial each merged simultaneously into a newly formed Georgia entity that was then renamed Pinnacle Financial Partners (CIK 2082866), with Legacy Pinnacle designated the accounting acquirer and Pinnacle Bank surviving as a Federal Reserve state member bank. The result is a $129.1 billion-asset Southeast regional with $88.1B of loans, $100.9B of deposits, 388 branches and 8,433 FTEs, roughly 2.2x the asset base of the predecessor. Any screen built on the aggregated PNFP annual series is unreliable: the structured data for FY2025 blends predecessors (the $8.07 diluted EPS is Legacy Pinnacle standalone, while some aggregated net income and dividend figures are Synovus's), and the supplied 15.58x P/E does not reconcile with the supplied $8.07 EPS at the current price ($106.45 ÷ $8.07 = 13.2x). All multiples in this report are computed off consensus forward estimates and disclosed 10-Q figures.

On the operating record, two quarters of integration have gone well. Q2 2026 produced $956M of net interest income at a 3.44% NIM (versus Legacy Pinnacle's 3.23% in Q2 2025), $328M of net income, GAAP diluted EPS of $2.07 and adjusted diluted EPS of $2.50 after roughly $51M of merger expense and $29M of securities losses. Adjusted ROTCE was 17.70% and the adjusted tangible efficiency ratio 49.8%, the latter exactly matching the full-year guidance implied by ($2,725M adjusted expense − $185M intangible amortization) ÷ $5,100M adjusted revenue. Net charge-offs of 22-23bp sit inside the 20-25bp guide, ACL/loans is 1.17% and ACL/NPL coverage 248%. Management has disclosed that only about 40% of expected net cost savings land in 2026, which means the majority of the synergy benefit is a 2027 event and the current ~$10 annualized adjusted EPS run rate understates the post-integration earnings base.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than an ACCUMULATE is the arithmetic of entry, not a deteriorating thesis. Since the last review at $94.16 in early June the stock has advanced 13% to within 3.5% of its 52-week high, while consensus has moved the other way: 2026 EPS from $10.32 to $10.19 (-1.3% over 90 days) and 2027 from $11.84 to $11.45 (-3.3%). Three specific, quantified risks have surfaced or sharpened in the Q2 filing: interest-rate sensitivity has flipped sign (−200bp now costs 2.8% of NII, where the predecessor gained 1.7%) while 2026 guidance assumes no FOMC action; a $4.3B senior housing vertical acquired from Synovus already produced the quarter's NPA increase via two relationships; and the "finance and insurance" NAICS share of the C&I book roughly tripled to ~17%, the exact non-depository-financial-institution category the market has been scrutinizing since 2025's non-bank credit accidents. Base case $118 (+10.9%), bull $131 (+23.1%), bear $84 (−21.1%). That distribution, plus a 1.88% dividend, produces a positive but unremarkable expected return.

## Company Overview

Pinnacle now spans Tennessee, Georgia, North Carolina, South Carolina, Virginia, Alabama, Kentucky, Florida and Washington D.C., reporting in two segments, Banking and Wealth. Corporate domicile and headquarters moved to Atlanta; Pinnacle Bank remains headquartered in Nashville. Exchange ratios were 1.0 New Pinnacle share per Legacy Pinnacle share and 0.5237 per Synovus share, producing an ownership split of roughly 51% Legacy Pinnacle / 49% Synovus. Share count nearly doubled; legacy Pinnacle holders' ownership fell from 100% to about 51%.

The business model is the equity story, and the FY2025 10-K states it without euphemism: much of recent organic loan growth resulted "not of strong loan demand but rather of our ability to attract experienced financial services professionals who have been able to attract customers from other financial institutions." Pinnacle monetizes hired relationship bankers' existing books. That engine is running through the integration rather than pausing for it: net 50 revenue-producing hires in Q1 and 74 in Q2, 124 year to date against a 225-250 full-year goal.

Loan mix on the cleanest disclosed composition (Legacy Pinnacle at 12/31/25): C&I 41.8%, non-owner-occupied CRE 20.8%, consumer real estate mortgage 14.1%, owner-occupied CRE 13.3%, construction and land development 8.3%. Roughly 39% of CRE is owner-occupied, which the company treats as cash-flow lending. Distinctive concentrations above 25% of bank total risk-based capital include lessors of nonresidential buildings ($6.42B exposure), lessors of residential buildings ($2.54B), for-sale housing builders ($1.41B) and music publishers ($1.59B, up 43% year over year), the last a Nashville franchise essentially no other bank holds.

The 49% equity-method stake in Bankers Healthcare Group is the largest swing factor in fee income and deserves precision about roles: Pinnacle Bank is a non-controlling 49% owner with 2 of 5 board seats; an originator that sold $2.6B of its own originations to BHG in 2025 for a per-loan fee; a lender to BHG via a $125M participation in a $525M secured revolver plus a $50M unsecured line; and historically a purchaser of BHG paper ($103.5M retained).

## Financial Analysis

**Legacy Pinnacle standalone baseline (FY2025).** Net interest income $1,548M (+13.4%) on 10.3% loan growth and 8bp of NIM expansion as funding costs fell 48bp against a 38bp decline in earning-asset yields. Noninterest income $507M (+36.5%), of which the majority was BHG — equity-method income +87.3% to $118.3M — plus a smaller securities repositioning loss ($16.6M vs $71.9M). Expenses grew 12.8%, led by $100.4M more salaries and benefits with cash incentives paid at 125% of target (plan maximum) versus 98% in 2024. Net income $642M, diluted EPS $8.07, ROA 1.15%, ROE 9.35%, CET1 10.88%, NCOs 21bp, TBVPS $63.71.

**Post-merger run rate (Q2 2026).** NII $956M, NIM 3.44%; H1 NII $1,889M at 3.48% NIM. Net income $328M; net income to common $313M; GAAP EPS $2.07; adjusted EPS $2.50. H1 GAAP EPS $2.96, adjusted $4.90. Adjusted ROTCE 17.70%, adjusted tangible efficiency 49.8% versus 54.9% for the predecessor a year earlier. CET1 9.93%, TCE/tangible assets 7.65%, TBVPS $63.02. Loan/deposit 87.3%. NPLs 0.47% of loans, NPAs $444M, criticized/classified $1,812M or 2.1% of loans.

The balance sheet reconciles cleanly. Intangibles at 6/30/26 total $4,589M (goodwill $3,479M, core deposit intangible $848M, wealth/customer intangible $262M). Tangible assets $124,511M; tangible common equity of $63.02 × ~151.0M shares = $9,516M; ratio 7.64% against the reported 7.65%. Purchase accounting: consideration $7,575M less net assets acquired $5,945M equals $1,630M of new goodwill.

**Tangible book value requires care.** TBVPS moved from $63.71 (Legacy, 12/31/25) to $63.02 (combined, 6/30/26), only −1.1%, but the dates are six months apart and the change blends purchase-accounting dilution with two quarters of retention and AOCI. H1 net income to common of roughly $447M less about $151M of common dividends leaves ~$296M retained, or about $1.96/share of accretion. Backing that out implies TBVPS near close of roughly $61, i.e. low single-digit initial dilution already earned back. Good for a transaction this size, but not zero.

**Capital and funding are the soft spots.** CET1 at 9.93% is a level statement about a new balance sheet, not a decline in a continuous series, but it leaves less headroom than the predecessor's 10.88%, consistent with $400M of buyback authorization and zero repurchases in H1 2026. FHLB advances and other borrowings stand at $10.25B versus $2.2B for Legacy Pinnacle, of which the merger contributed roughly $2.5B; management attributed the Q2 step-up to deposit seasonality. The company issued $750M of 5.596% fixed-to-floating senior notes due 2032 on May 19. Average total deposit cost was 2.14% in Q2 (interest-bearing 2.69%) against a 3.50-3.75% fed funds target. Reported core funding of 84.0% relies on classifying the entire IntraFi reciprocal portfolio as core, where regulatory guidance treats amounts above $5.0B as non-core for a bank this size; on the regulatory cut, core funding would be roughly seven points lower. The rationale (no observed behavioral difference) is defensible, but the metric is not peer-comparable.

Two smaller items worth pricing. The company runs synthetic credit-risk transfer to reduce risk-weighted assets: a CDS referencing a $1.7B consumer mortgage pool with ~$87M of notional protection at a 7.95% annual fee, plus a December 2025 CDS on commercial subscription lines with $80M of notional at 4.94%. The 7.95% fee times $87M is ~$6.9M annually, against the $6.4M actually expensed in 2025; the disclosures reconcile. Combined annual cost is roughly $10-11M. That is expensive first-loss protection and, alongside a paused buyback at 9.93% CET1, is consistent with capital being the binding constraint rather than a preference.

**Earnings visibility is moderate, not high.** The last four quarters produced two beats and two misses (+8.7%, −2.2%, +3.8%, −0.3%), with the most recent print essentially in line on the vendor consensus supplied here and a ~1.7% beat on the non-GAAP consensus reported elsewhere. Third-party revenue-versus-consensus claims for Q2 directly contradict one another ($1.20B "miss" versus $1.24B, versus a Zacks revenue beat); no weight belongs on any of them.

## Growth Analysis

2026 guidance: period-end loan growth 9-11%, deposit growth 8-10%, adjusted revenue $5.0-5.2B, adjusted noninterest expense $2.675-2.775B, NCOs 0.20-0.25%, tax rate 20-21%, assumed NIM 3.44-3.47%, ~$185M of intangible amortization, and approximately 40% of expected net cost savings realized. Implied adjusted pre-provision net revenue is $2.23-2.53B. Anchoring on the midpoint: PPNR ~$2.375B less ~$200M provision at a 20.5% tax rate is ~$1.73B, less ~$60M of preferred dividends leaves ~$1.67B, or roughly $11.05 per share of adjusted earnings capacity on 151M shares. The Q2 annualized figure of $10.00 sits below that precisely because H1 has not captured a full-year synergy contribution.

That sequencing is the growth thesis, and it is disclosed rather than speculative: if only ~40% of net cost savings hit 2026, the majority is a 2027 event. Consensus reflects it — 2027 EPS of $11.45 versus 2026 of $10.19, +12.4%. The offset is that the absolute synergy dollar target could not be verified from a primary source, so the 60% remainder cannot be sized independently, and the 10-K warns savings "may not be realized in full, may take longer than anticipated to realize, or may not be realized at all."

Three secondary growth engines are real. Banker hiring is running at 124 net year to date against 225-250, an unusual posture during an integration and the clearest expression of the strategy. Wealth is scaling: brokerage assets $16.0B at 12/31/25 versus $13.1B a year earlier, trust assets $8.5B managed plus $4.0B custodied. Niche verticals — music publishing, franchise finance, equipment leasing — carry pricing power that a generalist middle-market lender does not have.

Two headwinds temper the score. First, estimate revisions are negative across every horizon: current quarter −1.7% over 90 days, next quarter −2.3%, current year −1.3%, next year −3.3%, with the 30-day moves in the same direction. That is modest in magnitude but consistent, and it is the opposite of the pattern that supports paying up. Second, BHG is decelerating. Q2 equity-method income fell 8% to $24M on an "intentional shift in placement strategy," and cash distributions to Pinnacle Bank were $70M in H1 2026 versus $102M in H1 2025. FY2025's 87% surge in BHG income was driven by a doubling of loan-sale volume under gain-on-sale accounting while reserves against those loans rose sharply — BHG's substitution/prepayment liability to $708.8M (8.6% of $8.3B of sold loans outstanding, from 7.1%) and its own ACL to $376.1M from $240.3M. The accounting is required and disclosed transparently, and rising reserves on a fast-growing sold book can simply reflect volume and prudence. What is fair to conclude is that BHG's contribution has low visibility, is not controlled by Pinnacle, and is unlikely to repeat 2025's growth rate.

## Valuation Assessment

At $106.45:

- 10.4x 2026 consensus EPS of $10.19; 9.3x 2027 consensus of $11.45
- 1.69x tangible book value of $63.02
- 1.88% dividend yield ($0.50 quarterly, $2.00 annualized), a ~20% payout on 2026 consensus
- Adjusted ROTCE 17.70%

Peer medians in the supplied regional bank set are 12.6x P/E and 2.2% yield (EWBC 13.0x, WBS 12.0x, FHN 12.6x, UMBF 11.8x, WTFC 13.1x), on trailing bases. On forward earnings PNFP screens in line to modestly cheap against that group; on tangible book it screens at a premium. The premium is earned rather than assumed: a 17.7% adjusted ROTCE against 1.69x TBV implies a 10.5% earnings yield on price, arithmetically consistent with the ~9.5x forward multiple. The market is paying above book for the growth engine, not for the balance sheet.

The check that matters is whether the 2027 synergy bridge is already in the price. The stock bottomed at $81.08 in late March and has recovered roughly 31% to $106.45, and the proxy's own pay-versus-performance data shows PNFP returned −15.8% in calendar 2025 against peers +6.5%, a 22-point relative markdown in the merger-announcement year, typical treatment for the share-issuing party in a large all-stock merger of equals. Two quarters of on-guidance delivery repaired most of that gap. What remains is the 2027 earnings step-up, and 9.3x on a consensus number that has been cut 3.3% in 90 days is not a distressed multiple for a bank with 9.93% CET1, a paused buyback and asset sensitivity into an ambiguous rate path.

**Scenario framework.**

- **Base $118 (+10.9%).** $11.30 of 2027 adjusted EPS (a small haircut to consensus for the revision drift) at 10.5x = $118.6. Cross-check: 1.75x forward TBV of ~$67.5 = $118. This is essentially the sell-side mean of $118.9, and consensus targets deserve to be treated as central estimates rather than floors: PNFP is a mid-teens-ROTCE bank with falling estimates.
- **Bull $131 (+23.1%).** Full synergy capture plus no Fed easing plus clean credit yields $12.20 of 2027 EPS at 10.7x = $131, at the high end of the sell-side target range ($131 high). Cross-check: 1.91x forward TBV of $68.5 = $131.
- **Bear $84 (−21.1%).** A 100-200bp easing cycle compressing NII 1.5-2.8%, senior housing and NDFI losses lifting NCOs toward 40bp (roughly $350M versus ~$200M guided), and slipped synergies produce ~$9.50 of 2027 EPS at 8.8x = $84. Cross-check: 1.28x forward TBV of $65.5 = $84, near the $81.08 52-week low.

Probability-weighting 55% base / 20% bull / 25% bear gives about +5.0% price return, roughly +6.9% with the dividend, over twelve months. That is a fair-value outcome and does not clear an ACCUMULATE bar, which would require either a wider gap to base case or lower bear-case downside.

## Competitive Landscape

The merger created the fourth-largest bank headquartered in the Southeast and the density to compete for middle-market relationships that previously defaulted to JPMorgan, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, Truist and Regions, while retaining a model the 10-K describes as "designed to promote relationship banking rather than transactional banking." Trajectory on the metrics that matter is positive: net banker hires accelerated from 50 to 74 quarter over quarter, the adjusted tangible efficiency ratio improved from 54.9% to 49.8% year over year, NIM expanded 21bp versus the predecessor's Q2 2025, and wealth AUM is compounding.

The filings are candid about the structural weakness. A "business model that focuses principally on serving small to medium-sized businesses and their owners rather than a broad retail distribution strategy" has at times limited core deposit gathering and forced greater non-core funding reliance, which showed up again in Q2's step-up to $10.25B of FHLB and other borrowings. Legacy total funding cost of 2.63% with 16% non-core funding at 4.37% is not a low-cost deposit franchise. Competition comes from three directions the 10-K names: larger banks with cheaper retail deposits; credit unions, tax-exempt and CRA-exempt, underpricing on both sides of the balance sheet across the Southeast; and non-depository lenders and private credit operating outside the prudential perimeter. The 10-K also adds stablecoin and cryptocurrency disintermediation as a named deposit risk, which is newer and more specific language than most peers use.

The strategy's own risk is embedded in it. The 10-K warns that in an elevated-rate environment it may be harder for newly hired bankers to move customers, "particularly those with existing loans that are priced below current market rates," and that an older, more experienced workforce may push compensation and healthcare costs above peers while creating succession risk. Management is also visibly trading synergy for retention, leasing roughly 31,000 additional square feet at Synovus Place in Columbus, Georgia rather than consolidating the acquired hub. That is defensible for a franchise built on people, and it is one reason to treat the 2027 synergy number as a range rather than a point.

## Risk Assessment

**Rate-cut asymmetry.** The Q2 rate-shock table shows +200bp → +4.3% NII and −200bp → −2.8%. Legacy Pinnacle at 12/31/25 modeled the opposite tilt (+0.3% / +1.7%). Guidance assumes no FOMC action, in a filing that simultaneously states short rates "are expected to decline further in 2026." An easing cycle triggered by labor deterioration would hit NII and credit simultaneously. These are instantaneous static shocks the filings themselves call more severe than a gradual shift, and fair-value hedges have already cut the securities portfolio's effective duration from 5.48% to 2.02%, so modeled sensitivity is not destiny.

**Senior housing.** The Q2 10-Q discloses roughly $4.3B of senior housing loans (~4.9% of the book) versus roughly $521M for Legacy Pinnacle — essentially an entirely Synovus-originated vertical — and states NPAs "were largely impacted during the quarter by two senior housing relationships." Two borrowers moving the needle on a $129B balance sheet implies large individual exposures. Purchase accounting means some of the higher NPL and criticized ratios versus the predecessor are definitional rather than fresh deterioration, and NCOs remain inside guidance with 248% NPL coverage. Still, this is an acquired concentration producing problem assets within two quarters of ownership.

**NDFI exposure.** The finance-and-insurance NAICS share of the C&I book rose from roughly 7% to roughly 17%. (The dollar figures recorded in the underlying work do not reconcile against reported C&I totals, so the percentage is the reliable datum.) Lending to funds, specialty finance companies and BDCs is the most scrutinized bank credit category after 2025's non-bank accidents. A near-tripling in portfolio share deserves direct management questioning.

**Goodwill and capital.** Goodwill plus intangibles of ~$4.59B equals roughly 48% of tangible common equity. The 10-K names "a significant and sustained decline in our stock price and market capitalization below book value" as an impairment trigger; the annual test date is September 30 and is currently qualitative. Crossing $100B in assets brings Category IV supervisory stress testing, resolution planning and enhanced prudential standards, a permanent cost step-up and a constraint on capital return. The March 19, 2026 Basel III re-proposal, under which Category IV firms would not adopt the expanded risk-based approach, is favorable but the 10-K cautions stricter requirements could still arrive through supervisory oversight.

**Concentration and integration.** Construction and land development stood at 57.7% of bank total risk-based capital against an internal target below 70%; non-owner-occupied CRE plus multifamily including construction at 221.1% against an internal ceiling of 225% and regulatory guidance of 300% — the CRE ratio is close to the company's own limit. Highly leveraged transactions and loans to private-equity portfolio companies are called out with the warning that deterioration "may occur quickly." The 10-K added a dedicated integration risk section and modified the key-personnel factor to include retention of Synovus employees, which for a company whose growth engine is people is not boilerplate.

Reasonably possible legal losses in excess of accruals were estimated at zero to $10 million as of 12/31/2025, immaterial.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 31.5% against 27.8% one-year realized is rich by 3.7 points, and the term structure is in backwardation (31.5% at 13 days versus 27.2% at six months), which prices event risk rather than a regime change. The 3-month put/call skew of 8.6% is characterized by the data source as elevated put demand; the 6-month reading of −2.1% is not. Open-interest put/call of 0.17 is low and has fallen from 0.35 in early June; the standing book is call-tilted, so there is no large accumulated hedge behind the near-term skew. Implied moves are ±5.1% at 13 days and ±7.3% at 41 days. Since the June snapshot, ATM IV compressed 6.6 points while the price rose 12.7%, the signature of resolved uncertainty (Q2 delivered on guidance) rather than building conviction in either direction. Short interest is a modest 4.4% of float at 4.5 days to cover. The options market is neither confirming nor contradicting the fundamental view; it is pricing a normal-sized catalyst window with no crowded directional bet.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($131, +23.1%)

Roughly 60% of net cost savings remain to be realized after 2026, and the disclosed guidance framework already reconciles internally: the Q2 adjusted tangible efficiency ratio of 49.8% matches the full-year implied figure exactly, so the run rate is on plan. Adjusted earnings capacity from the guidance midpoint is roughly $11.05 per share for 2026, above the $10.00 Q2 annualization, before the 2027 synergy step. Banker hiring at 124 net year to date against a 225-250 target sustains 9-11% loan growth that most regional peers cannot match organically, and the Southeast MSAs where that footprint sits remain among the country's fastest-growing. Adjusted ROTCE of 17.7% supports a higher book multiple than the 1.69x currently paid; at 1.91x forward TBV of $68.5 the stock is $131. If the Fed holds, the flip to asset sensitivity is a non-event or a modest positive. The Chief Banking Officer bought $1.0M of stock on the open market two days after the Q2 release at $99.90.

### Bear Case ($84, −21.1%)

The asset-sensitivity flip becomes the binding constraint. If labor deterioration forces an easing cycle, a 200bp move costs 2.8% of net interest income against guidance that assumes no FOMC action, and it arrives alongside credit normalization rather than in isolation. The two acquired concentrations are where that normalization would show first: $4.3B of senior housing already producing NPAs through two relationships, and a finance-and-insurance C&I share that tripled to ~17% into the most scrutinized credit category in the market. NCOs moving from 22bp to 40bp costs roughly $150M pre-tax, about $0.79 per share. Synergies are being partially traded away for retention through the Columbus expansion, the hiring spree and the 125%-of-target incentive payout culture, and the 10-K warns savings may not be realized at all. Capital offers little cushion: CET1 at 9.93%, buybacks authorized but untouched, 7.95% paid for synthetic RWA relief, and $4.59B of intangibles equal to 48% of tangible common equity with a September 30 qualitative goodwill test. Consensus is already cutting 2027 by 3.3% per quarter of drift. At 1.28x TBV the stock is $84, near the March low of $81.08.

### Which Way the Evidence Leans

The operating evidence is better than the price evidence. Two quarters of on-guidance delivery, a 21bp NIM improvement versus the predecessor, a 500bp efficiency improvement, credit inside guidance and an insider buying with his own money all argue the integration is working. Against that, the stock has recovered 31% from its March low and sits 3.5% below its 52-week high while every consensus horizon is being marked down, and the specific risks that surfaced in the Q2 filing — rate-sensitivity reversal, senior housing, NDFI — are each capable of consuming a year of synergy benefit. Fair risk-adjusted return from here, with a real catalyst in the 2027 earnings bridge that argues against trimming.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold). Existing owners maintain exposure; new capital waits.**

**Base case $118 (+10.9% from $106.45), twelve months.** $11.30 of 2027 adjusted EPS at 10.5x; cross-checked at 1.75x forward TBV of ~$67.50. Total return with the $2.00 dividend ~12.7%.

**Bull case $131 (+23.1%).** $12.20 of 2027 EPS at 10.7x, requiring full synergy capture, no Fed easing and NCOs at or below 25bp.

**Bear case $84 (−21.1%).** $9.50 of 2027 EPS at 8.8x, or 1.28x forward TBV, on an easing cycle plus NCOs toward 40bp.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. A price of $92-96 or below with 2027 consensus holding at or above $11.20 — that combination restores roughly 25% upside to base case against a bear case near the March low, which clears the ACCUMULATE bar.
2. Management quantifying the total cost-savings target with a dated 2027 realization schedule, plus the 30-day estimate revision trend turning positive on 2027 EPS. The revision drift is the single cleanest signal to monitor.
3. Resumption of buybacks against the $400M authorization with CET1 at or above 10.25%, evidencing capital generation rather than capital constraint.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. Senior housing or NDFI-driven NCOs printing above 30bp annualized in any quarter, or criticized/classified rising above 2.5% of loans on a like-for-like basis.
2. A Fed easing cycle beginning while management reaffirms a NIM guide built on no FOMC action — the gap between the 3.44-3.47% assumption and delivered NIM is the measurable test.
3. 2027 consensus falling below $10.75 (a further 6% cut), which would place the stock at 9.9x on deteriorating rather than improving numbers.
4. Evidence of producer attrition: net revenue-producer hires tracking below roughly 175 for the full year against the 225-250 goal, or disclosure of Synovus-side banker departures.

**Reassessment timeline.** The Q3 2026 release (late October) is the next natural decision point, and it carries two specific items: the September 30 goodwill test date passes before it, and Q3 will show whether Q2's wholesale funding step-up was in fact deposit seasonality as management stated. The September FOMC meeting is the other dated input, given the sensitivity flip. Two verification gaps should be closed before adding capital at any price: the absolute synergy dollar target, and the contents of the July 31, 2026 8-K covering both an officer/director change (Item 5.02) and a bylaws amendment (Item 5.03) nine days after earnings.

## Conclusion

Pinnacle has executed the first two quarters of one of the largest mergers in Southeast banking history without visible operational damage: 3.44% NIM, 17.70% adjusted ROTCE, a 49.8% adjusted tangible efficiency ratio matching the full-year guidance construct, charge-offs inside the guide, and a hiring engine that added 74 net revenue producers in Q2 while integrating. Roughly 60% of net cost savings remain ahead in 2027, which is a disclosed rather than speculative source of earnings growth, and the company's compensation plan is anchored on tangible book value accretion, the right metric for a serial acquirer. A $1.0M open-market purchase by the Chief Banking Officer two days after the Q2 print, with no offsetting open-market sales, is a constructive signal from the executive closest to loan pipelines and banker retention.

The rating is constrained by entry arithmetic and by three quantified risks that the Q2 filing newly made visible. At $106.45 the stock is 10.4x 2026 and 9.3x 2027 consensus and 1.69x tangible book, having advanced 13% since early June and 31% from the March low while every consensus horizon was marked down 1-3%. The rate posture has reversed sign, so a cutting cycle now costs 2.8% of net interest income against guidance that assumes the Fed does nothing; a $4.3B senior housing vertical acquired six months ago has already driven the quarter's NPA increase through two relationships; and the finance-and-insurance share of the C&I book roughly tripled to 17% into the market's most scrutinized credit category. With base case $118, bear case $84 and a 1.88% yield, expected return is positive but the margin of safety is thin. Own it; add at $92-96 or on confirmation that the 2027 synergy bridge is being quantified and estimates have stopped falling.
