## Executive Summary

La-Z-Boy closed fiscal 2026 (ended April 25, 2026) with a strong fourth quarter that the market rewarded with a 17.7% single-day move, and the stock has held that level. Q4 GAAP operating income rose 39.6% to $41.2M on essentially flat revenue of $570.3M, lifting operating margin 206bps to 7.23%, and adjusted EPS of $1.26 beat the $0.82 consensus by 52.8%. That is a real operating result, confirmable from the filings: reconstructing Q4 adjusted operating income from the 10-K and the Q3 10-Q produces 9.82%, within eight basis points of the company's reported 9.9%, and excludes the $7.6M sale-leaseback gain.

The full year tells a less flattering story. Consolidated sales grew 0.8% to $2,126.6M, but GAAP operating margin fell from 6.44% to 6.08%, and on an adjusted basis (stripping the $20.0M Joybird goodwill impairment, $5.8M UK closure charges, $3.1M Casegoods inventory write-down, and $11.5M of real-estate gains) operating margin declined roughly 68bps to ~6.89% from ~7.57%. Retail written same-store sales fell 3%. Joybird delivered sales fell 10.5%. Within Wholesale, product-and-delivery revenue declined $34.8M (-2.5%) while "Other" revenue, which Note 16 defines as primarily tariff and other surcharges, rose $37.2M (+53%). Revenue was flat because of pricing, surcharges, dealer acquisitions ($86.4M spent) and new stores, not because consumers bought more furniture. Against the FY2022 peak, sales are -9.8% and net income -32.0%.

The balance sheet is the strongest part of the case. Funded debt is effectively nil ($2.2M of finance leases), cash and investments total $321M, the $200M revolver is undrawn to July 2030 with confirmed covenant compliance, and the board authorized a new $300M repurchase program in May 2026 — 17.8% of the current market capitalization, fundable from cash and free cash flow without borrowing. At 14.2x FY2027 consensus EPS of $2.94 and ~7.0x EV/adjusted EBITDA, the stock is not expensive. It is also not obviously mispriced: the shares have moved from roughly $35 pre-print to $41.665 on unchanged estimates, compressing the multiple discount to the 16.4x peer median, base-case upside is roughly 7%, and the Q1 FY2027 report lands in mid-to-late August with the options market pricing ±11.5%. This is a third consecutive HOLD, at a modestly lower score, and the classification reflects honest uncertainty rather than a growth or income thesis.

## Company Overview

La-Z-Boy is a vertically integrated manufacturer and retailer of residential upholstered furniture, describing itself as the leading global producer of reclining chairs and the second-largest single-branded furniture retailer in the United States. Three reporting units:

| Segment | FY26 sales | FY26 operating income | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wholesale (incl. $443.4M intersegment) | $1,482.2M | $110.2M | 7.4% |
| Retail (230 company-owned stores) | $950.7M | $108.5M | 11.4% |
| Corporate & Other (Joybird, HK trading, royalties) | $143.8M | $(89.5)M | n/a |
| Eliminations | $(450.0)M | — | |
| **Consolidated** | **$2,126.6M** | **$129.2M** | **6.08%** |

The economics rest on dual-margin capture: manufacturing profit on furniture sold to company-owned stores is booked in Wholesale, and the retail markup is booked in Retail. The branded network totals 378 stores, of which 230 (60.8%) are company-owned, up from roughly 45% five years ago, against a stated 450-store target and roughly ten openings per year. Beyond the branded stores, product moves through Comfort Studios, England Custom Comfort Centers and over 1,000 independent dealers. No single customer concentration is disclosed; 91% of FY26 sales were domestic.

The portfolio has been actively narrowed: UK manufacturing ceased at the end of FY2026, the Casegoods headquarters and upholstery operation were sold, the Kincaid and American Drew wholesale businesses were sold on May 29, 2026, and two Mexican plants (San Luis Rio Colorado upholstery assembly and the Tijuana Joybird plant) are closing with production moving to U.S. facilities by the end of FY2027. What remains is La-Z-Boy branded upholstery, owned retail, Joybird and Hammary.

## Financial Analysis

**Three-year income statement (10-K Item 8):**

| ($M except EPS) | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sales | 2,047.0 | 2,109.2 | 2,126.6 |
| Gross margin | 43.07% | 43.92% | 44.04% |
| SG&A % of sales | 35.70% | 36.51% | 37.03% |
| Goodwill impairment | — | 20.6 | 20.0 |
| Operating margin | 7.37% | 6.44% | 6.08% |
| Net income attributable | 122.6 | 99.6 | 102.0 |
| Diluted EPS | $2.83 | $2.35 | $2.47 |
| Diluted shares (M) | 43.280 | 42.345 | 41.341 |

Gross margin improved only 10bps despite a 50bp favorable mix benefit from Retail growth, because higher distribution costs tied to the home-delivery transformation absorbed it. Within Wholesale, gross margin fell 50bps: -70bps from distribution transformation, -50bps from higher manufacturing overhead, +80bps from lower input costs (ocean freight, sourcing). SG&A rose 40bps consolidated on Retail mix and fixed-cost deleverage from lower delivered same-store sales.

Two items materially flattered reported segment margins. A $7.6M gain on the Q4 sale-leaseback of four retail buildings is quantified in MD&A as an 80bp SG&A benefit to Retail; ex-gain, Retail operating margin was 10.61% versus 11.73% in FY2025, a 112bp decline rather than the 30bp GAAP decline. A $3.9M gain on the Casegoods headquarters sale sits in Wholesale. Adjusting both segments for gains and charges, Wholesale margin improved ~61bps to 7.77%, of which MD&A attributes 50bps to the warranty policy change, leaving underlying wholesale margin roughly flat.

The warranty change deserves specific treatment. Effective Q2 FY2026, dealers receive an upfront service allowance for labor and delivery costs previously claimed under the wholesale warranty, producing a $5.6M reduction in the warranty liability (from $29.9M to $24.7M). The commercial logic is that dealers perform the service in-market, but the catch-up release relates to previously sold product and should not be extrapolated, and because ongoing costs now flow through SG&A as allowances rather than through the warranty accrual, FY25/FY26 gross-margin and SG&A comparability is impaired.

|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | 158.1 | 187.3 | 204.1 |
| Capex | (53.6) | (74.3) | (76.3) |
| Free cash flow | 104.6 | 113.0 | 127.8 |
| Acquisitions | (39.4) | (29.5) | (86.4) |
| Buybacks | (52.8) | (77.9) | (47.3) |
| Dividends | (32.7) | (35.0) | (37.9) |

FY26 free cash flow of $127.8M is 6.0% of sales, $3.09 per diluted share, and a 7.6% yield on the current market capitalization. Two adjustments matter. First, $26.3M of operating cash flow came from a 14.4% inventory drawdown that MD&A explicitly attributes to aligning production with incoming order trends — a one-directional lever that partly reflects weak order intake; on that basis free cash flow was ~$101.5M, a 6.0% yield. Second, FY2027 capex is guided to $90–110M, up 18–44%, for the distribution transformation, manufacturing and stores. Holding operating cash flow flat at $204M implies FY27 free cash flow of $94–114M; on the ex-inventory-release base of ~$178M, $68–88M. These are arithmetic sensitivities, not forecasts, and they bracket the funding capacity available for buybacks.

Balance sheet at 4/25/2026: cash $303.2M, investments $18.0M, inventories $218.4M, goodwill $243.3M (Retail $207.8M, Joybird $35.5M), operating lease liabilities $562.1M, finance leases $2.2M, revolver borrowings zero, shareholders' equity $1,050.0M. Current ratio 1.80x. Net cash of roughly $319M equals 19% of market capitalization, though $62.3M sits offshore and is largely deemed permanently reinvested. Cash interest paid was $0.4M in each of the last three years. PwC (auditor since 1968) issued unqualified opinions on both the financial statements and internal control, with no material weakness and no restatement; the single critical audit matter is the Joybird goodwill test.

Leases are the genuine fixed-cost commitment: $657.8M undiscounted payments, $111.1M due within twelve months, 6.9-year weighted-average term at a 4.6% discount rate, plus $37.2M relating to leases not yet commenced. FY26 total lease cost was $108.7M and new right-of-use assets added $151.9M (versus $81.9M in FY25), $58.8M of which came with the Southeast dealer acquisition. Any screen that capitalizes the $562.1M of operating leases will show leverage roughly 0.5x higher than a funded-debt view; both framings are legitimate, but default risk is not a live question here.

Capital returns over FY2024–FY2026 totaled $283.6M ($178.0M buybacks, $105.6M dividends), taking shares outstanding from 43.318M to 40.349M (-6.9%). FY26 repurchases were 1.253M shares at an average ~$37.7. Dividends declared rose from $0.763 to $0.84 to $0.924, a 37% payout of GAAP EPS.

Financial health scores 7.0, unchanged from the prior review. Nothing deteriorated and the 10-K confirmed the clean structure. What holds the score below 8 is that free cash flow quality is flattered by inventory release and lower cash taxes ($30.4M versus $43.8M), operating margin is compressing, and FY27 capex plus unquantified Mexico closure costs compete for the same cash that funds the buyback.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the weakest dimension and the reason this is not a BUY.

Underlying demand contracted in FY2026. Retail written same-store sales were -3%, which MD&A attributes to "lower consumer demand as a result of the current macroeconomic environment." Delivered same-store sales also declined, causing explicit fixed-cost deleverage. Joybird delivered sales fell 10.5% to $130.8M with written sales -7%, described as a "particularly volatile" consumer segment. Retail's $52.3M of sales growth decomposes to +$60.1M from acquisitions and +$31.7M from net new stores, partly offset by the delivered same-store decline. One cannot simply subtract $91.8M to derive organic growth, because acquiring a dealer converts external wholesale revenue into intersegment revenue — the consolidated increment is the retail markup, not the full retail sale. The direction is unambiguous.

Within Wholesale, the composition of flat revenue is the most useful single disclosure in the filing. Upholstery plus casegoods plus delivery fell from $1,409.7M to $1,374.9M (-2.5%), while "Other" — defined in Note 16 as primarily tariff and other surcharges, plus advertising reimbursements, royalties, parts and rebates — rose from $70.1M to $107.3M (+53%). MD&A independently attributes wholesale growth to "strategic pricing and surcharge actions." The mixed nature of the "Other" bucket means the full $37.2M cannot be attributed to tariffs, and the warranty/service-allowance change may have shifted amounts between lines. What the data establishes is that unit volume declined while price and surcharge revenue offset it. That is durable only while the tariff and cost regime justifying the surcharges persists and dealers and consumers absorb the price.

Forward estimates are stable rather than rising: FY2027 consensus $2.94 (revised -1.1% over 90 days), FY2028 $3.30 (+0.4%), on three analysts. Current-quarter consensus of $0.49 is essentially unchanged over 30 days. There is no estimate-revision inflection signal here — the +30%/90-day pattern that would argue for a re-rating is absent. Management guided Q1 FY2027 sales of $490–510M against a street figure near $495.4M, with the midpoint modestly above, and roughly ten new stores planned.

The credible growth path is capital deployment rather than demand: converting independents toward the 450-store target at ~10 per year, plus margin recovery from portfolio simplification (Casegoods and Kincaid/American Drew exits remove loss-making revenue; UK and Mexico plant closures consolidate manufacturing into U.S. facilities). Both are real. Neither converts negative same-store sales into positive.

The macro backdrop is adverse. Mortgage rates have reached a one-year high with total application demand now below year-ago levels, and existing home sales at roughly 4.09M annualized. Furniture purchases track household formation and moves. A 30-year at ~6.99% and a bond market pricing a Fed with three hawkish dissents does not support a demand inflection in the next two quarters. Growth prospects: 4.7.

Peer medians in Home Furnishings sit at 16.4x P/E and 2.0% yield (LEG 6.6x, ETD 14.0x, MHK 16.4x, SGI 29.4x — wide dispersion, so the median is a weak anchor). On trailing GAAP, LZB is roughly at the median; on FY27 consensus it is 14% below. Adjusting for the balance sheet, an enterprise value of ~$1,360M against ~$194M of adjusted EBITDA is undemanding for a business generating 44% gross margins with a national brand.

The case against paying up at $41.665 is threefold. First, the discount has already narrowed. Secondary reporting places the pre-print price near $35, implying the stock has moved +19% while FY2027 consensus was cut 1.1% — the multiple expanded from ~11.9x to ~14.2x on the same estimate. Second, the earnings base being capitalized is ambiguous. FY2026 contains a $20.0M impairment, $5.8M UK closure charge, $3.1M inventory write-down, $11.5M of asset-sale gains, a $5.6M warranty release, a $5.9M discrete UK tax benefit, and a mid-year acquisition. My reconstruction of adjusted EPS is $2.90, or $2.76 if the discrete tax benefit is also removed, meaning consensus FY2027 of $2.94 implies only 1.4%–6.5% adjusted growth, most of which comes from portfolio exits and a full year of the Southeast acquisition rather than demand. Third, the Q4 inflection is partly composition: Q4 was the first full quarter consolidating the higher-margin 15-store acquisition, the first without the loss-making Casegoods upholstery business, and the first with the warranty benefit fully in the base.

The genuine catalyst is the $300M repurchase authorization established May 14, 2026 with no expiration — 17.8% of market capitalization, or ~7.2M shares at the current price, fundable from cash and free cash flow. An authorization is capacity, not a schedule, and the FY26 pace of $47.3M was 39% below FY25's $77.9M while acquisition spend nearly tripled to $86.4M. If management shifts the mix from dealer acquisitions toward repurchases, an unlevered 14x business retiring 5–8% of its shares annually compounds respectably even on flat revenue. That optionality supports a mid-range valuation score rather than a low one.

Base-case fair value: 15.0x FY2027 consensus $2.94 = **$44.10**, rounded to **$44.50** to reflect the buyback capacity and net cash, roughly 7% above the current price. The single analyst target in the survey is $46.00 (+10.4%). Valuation: 5.7 — cheap enough to hold, not cheap enough to demand new capital, and the sector's history of cheapness marking value traps argues for requiring an actual demand inflection before paying up.

## Competitive Landscape

Vertical integration is the structural differentiator. Owning 61% of a 378-store single-brand network plus domestic manufacturing means La-Z-Boy captures two margin layers and controls its shelf space, which is why consolidated gross margin of 44.0% sits far above a pure wholesaler's while consolidated operating margin of 6.1% sits below a pure retailer's. Competitors split into import-led wholesalers (thin margin, tariff-exposed), pure retailers (no manufacturing margin), and DTC players (customer-acquisition-cost exposed). La-Z-Boy straddles all three.

Manufacturing localization is being deepened rather than reversed: UK manufacturing closed, two Mexican plants closing with production transferring to U.S. plants by the end of FY2027. In a tariff-elevated regime this is a relative advantage competitors sourcing predominantly from Asia cannot replicate quickly. It also cuts the other way: the 53% growth in wholesale "Other" revenue suggests a meaningful slice of recent top-line stability is surcharge-driven and would be competitively unsustainable if tariffs loosened.

The store-conversion runway (378 toward 450, against over 1,000 independent dealers) is the stated growth story, but the purchase accounting is intangible-heavy and the returns are so far unimpressive. The October 2025 Southeast acquisition: $90.2M consideration, $32.5M identifiable net assets, $57.6M goodwill plus $28.3M reacquired rights — 95.4% intangibles, with $58.8M of right-of-use assets exactly offset by assumed lease liabilities. Retail identifiable assets rose 24.3% to $903.7M while Retail operating income rose 2.9% to $108.5M (or fell 4.3% ex-gain). Return on Retail identifiable assets fell from 14.5% to ~11.2% ex-gain. Half of that is the mid-year timing of the acquisition against a full year-end asset base and will partially reverse in FY2027, and right-of-use assets inflate the denominator for a leased-store operator. But the direction — capital added faster than profit — is the right thing to watch, particularly since Retail goodwill has grown from $138.9M to $207.8M in two years and is tested at an 8.5% discount rate against negative same-store sales.

Competitive position: 5.3. The moat is real in a defined niche and the localization advantage is genuine; the trajectory is flat-to-slightly-negative given volume declines and rising capital intensity.

## Risk Assessment

**Demand.** Written same-store sales -3%, delivered SSS down, Joybird written -7%. The company is adding ~10 stores a year into a shrinking same-store base, and Retail's ex-gain margin of 10.6% carries $562.1M of lease liabilities against $1,050.0M of equity plus $37.2M of committed-but-not-commenced leases. Sale-leasebacks monetize real estate but add fixed obligations. This is the channel through which a further consumer deterioration would hit hardest.

**Joybird.** PwC's sole critical audit matter. Goodwill was written down $19.967M to $35.5M, valued at a 17.5% discount rate versus 8.5% for Retail — the company's own inputs signal it views Joybird's standalone cash flows as high-risk. The 10-K explicitly warns that failure to meet forecasts could increase the possibility of further impairment. The Joybird trade name will be fully amortized in Q1 FY2027, and the Tijuana plant transfer adds unquantified FY27 restructuring cost.

**Comparability.** FY26 contains at least six identifiable non-recurring items plus a mid-year acquisition. The risk is not fraud — the audit is clean and the balance sheet ties — it is that reported "improvement" gets misread in either direction. My own adjusted figures are reconstructions and may differ from the company's non-GAAP definitions in scope.

**Tariffs, two-sided.** Tightening raises input costs; loosening removes the justification for surcharge revenue that offset the wholesale volume decline.

**Governance.** The Chair, President and CEO roles are combined. Disclosed performance measures are Sales, Operating Margin, Operating Cash Flow, TSR and stock price, with Sales as the company-selected measure and no return-on-capital metric — a design that pays out on dealer acquisitions that mechanically consolidate third-party revenue. Operating Margin, Operating Cash Flow and a 50% relative-TSR weighting on performance shares partially offset this, and pay-for-performance mechanics do function: CEO "compensation actually paid" fell 52% to $4.812M while Summary Compensation Table total rose 9.5% to $7.296M. Maximum contingent equity (740K performance shares at max, 600K restricted, 555K options) totals 1.895M shares, 4.70% of shares outstanding and 4.49% post-issuance dilution — but these require multi-year attainment and service, and FY26 buybacks of 1.253M shares more than offset gross issuance.

**Five-year returns.** The proxy pay-versus-performance table shows $100 invested at April 2021 worth $91.68 at April 2026 versus $93.72 for the company's selected peer group. FY2026 alone was the sharpest year of relative underperformance in the series (LZB 98.46 → 91.68 while peers 76.97 → 93.72). The measurement window starts near the pandemic furniture peak, and the endpoint predates the post-earnings move, so the current relative position is better than the table shows. Still, five years of negative absolute TSR alongside $283.6M of buybacks and dividends is the strongest disconfirming evidence against the capital-allocation case.

**Insiders.** Review of the most recent 25 Form 4 transactions found predominantly routine activity — tax withholding on vesting, option exercises, grants — with no open-market C-suite purchases identified. In a limited sample this is neutral-to-mildly-unsupportive rather than bearish. Two Item 5.02 8-Ks (approximately June 20, 2025 and December 9, 2025) were not read; secondary reporting identifies Taylor Luebke as SVP & CFO on the Q4 call, so a CFO transition is plausible but unverified.

Risk factors: 5.2. Solvency risk is absent, but there is a dated near-term binary, two goodwill balances tested on generous assumptions, and unquantified FY27 restructuring.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin (31 contracts across four expirations), so read it directionally rather than precisely. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 67.2% against 40.3% one-year realized — a 26.9-point premium at the 84th percentile of the current chain — in backwardation to 50.1% at three months and 42.5% at six. The August 21 tenor prices an implied move of ±11.5%. This is earnings positioning: the company announced on August 4 that Q1 FY2027 results (period ended July 25, 2026) will be released after the close on a Tuesday in August, which the August 21 expiry captures. The put/call skew of 5.3% is characterized by the data source as elevated put demand, while open interest is heavily call-tilted at 0.12 and put volume was nil — the two readings do not corroborate each other, and the small contract count argues against drawing a directional conclusion from either.

The change since June is more informative: ATM IV has collapsed from 196.1% to 67.2% and the open-interest put/call ratio from 1.16 to 0.12 as the price rose 18.7%. Pre-print hedging demand has unwound and positioning has rotated toward calls. Short interest is 4.4% of float with 1.8 days to cover — uncrowded, with no squeeze dynamic in either direction. The practical implication for a holder: options are expensive, so covered-call writing into the print is better compensated than buying protection, and anyone waiting to add has a reasonable probability of a lower entry within three weeks.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($54, +30%).** Q4's 9.8% adjusted operating margin proves to be the new run rate rather than composition. Portfolio simplification (Casegoods, Kincaid/American Drew, UK, two Mexican plants) removes loss-making revenue and consolidates manufacturing into U.S. plants at lower unit cost. The distribution and home-delivery transformation, which cost 70bps of wholesale gross margin in FY26, begins delivering benefit in FY27–28. Housing turnover improves as mortgage rates ease, turning written same-store sales positive. Management redirects capital from intangible-heavy dealer acquisitions to the $300M authorization, retiring 6–8% of shares annually. FY2028 EPS reaches $3.30 and the market pays 16.5x for an unlevered branded compounder: $54.

**Bear case ($30, -28%).** Mortgage rates stay near one-year highs, existing-home turnover stays near 4.1M, and written same-store sales deteriorate from -3% toward -6%. Retail fixed-cost deleverage compounds as ten new stores open into a shrinking comp base, and the $111.1M of annual lease payments becomes the binding constraint. Tariff surcharge revenue cannot be repeated at the same magnitude. Joybird misses forecast again and the remaining $35.5M of goodwill is written down; Mexico closure charges land in FY27 unquantified. Capex at $90–110M against ex-inventory operating cash flow near $178M leaves $68–88M of free cash flow, throttling the buyback. FY27 EPS lands near $2.40 and the multiple compresses to 12x: $28.80, near the 52-week low of $29.03, with the ~$8/share of net cash providing the floor.

**Base case ($44.50, +6.8%).** FY2027 EPS approximates consensus $2.94 as portfolio exits and a full year of the Southeast acquisition offset continued low-single-digit organic decline. Margin recovers partially but not to the Q4 run rate. Buyback runs at $60–80M, retiring 3.5–5% of shares. 15x on $2.94 plus the balance sheet gives $44.50, plus ~2.2% dividend for a ~9% total return over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (third consecutive; overall score 5.55, down from 5.9). The prior trajectory was declining, and the FY2026 10-K partly confirms and partly contradicts it. Confirming: full-year adjusted margin fell ~68bps, written same-store sales are -3%, five-year TSR is negative, and Retail capital intensity is rising. Contradicting: Q4 margin inflected genuinely, the audit is clean, portfolio pruning executed at or above carrying value, and the $300M authorization is new optionality. The score decline is driven by valuation (the multiple expanded from ~11.9x to ~14.2x on unchanged estimates) and a fuller reading of the proxy on capital allocation, not by fresh operational deterioration.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q1 FY2027 release, expected on a Tuesday in August 2026 after the close, against guidance of $490–510M in sales. This is the first clean read on whether written same-store sales have inflected and whether Q4's margin was run rate or composition.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following three:**
1. Written same-store sales positive, or better than -1%, for two consecutive quarters — the single most important data point in the release.
2. Adjusted operating margin at or above 7.5% for two consecutive quarters, with the distribution-transformation drag quantified as diminishing rather than persisting.
3. Repurchase pace annualizing above $100M (roughly 6% of shares) against the $300M authorization, with dealer-acquisition spend below FY26's $86.4M.

A price near $36–37 — approximately 12.5x FY2027 consensus, with the ~19% net-cash cushion intact — would also improve the risk/reward enough to warrant a BUY even absent a demand inflection.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. A further Joybird goodwill impairment against the remaining $35.5M, combined with Joybird written sales worse than -10%.
2. FY2027 free cash flow tracking below $80M (capex at the $110M high end against operating cash flow at or below $190M), which would functionally suspend the buyback.
3. Retail written same-store sales below -6%, which would put the $207.8M Retail goodwill tested at an 8.5% discount rate into genuine question.
4. Any credible sign of dividend or buyback funding stress, or drawing on the revolver to fund returns.

**Position management:** existing holders should sit through the August print rather than pay 67% implied volatility to hedge a name with $8/share of net cash and no funded debt; writing covered calls into the event is the better-compensated expression. New capital should wait for either the demand data or a lower price.

## Conclusion

La-Z-Boy is a well-financed, competently run business in a demand environment that has not yet turned. The balance sheet is close to pristine — zero funded debt, $321M of cash and investments, an undrawn $200M revolver to 2030, and $319M of net cash equal to 19% of the market capitalization — and the new $300M authorization gives management the capacity to retire nearly a fifth of the shares without borrowing. The fourth quarter was genuinely good: operating margin +206bps to 7.23% and adjusted EPS of $1.26 against $0.82 consensus, with the reported 9.9% adjusted operating margin reproducible from the filings and demonstrably excluding the real-estate gain.

The full year shows adjusted operating margin falling roughly 68bps, retail written same-store sales at -3%, Joybird delivered sales -10.5%, and flat wholesale revenue whose product-and-delivery component actually declined 2.5% with the gap filled by tariff surcharges. Revenue and net income remain 9.8% and 32.0% below the FY2022 peak.
