**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

On April 22, 2026 Helix Energy Solutions signed an all-stock merger with Hornbeck Offshore Services under which each Hornbeck share converts to 10.27167 Helix shares, leaving legacy Helix holders with roughly 45% of a combined entity that will be renamed Hornbeck Offshore Services and reincorporated from Minnesota to Delaware. Helix shareholders become the minority party, ceding both control and the corporate name. At $9.57, the stock trades on the deal's completion probability and on Hornbeck's value as much as on Helix's own subsea intervention and decommissioning fundamentals. Buybacks were suspended the same day the deal was signed, freezing the capital-return optionality that had underpinned part of the prior bull case.

The standalone franchise has two clear strengths and one clear problem. The strengths are a net-cash balance sheet (net debt of roughly –$198M at Q1 2026, with ~$501M cash against ~$310M of long-dated debt) and a durable, regulation-driven position in Gulf of America decommissioning and well intervention, segments tied to non-discretionary plug-and-abandonment obligations rather than discretionary drilling spend. The problem is that 2025 earnings power fell hard: gross margin compressed to 12% from 16%, net income dropped to $30.8M from $55.6M, and Well Intervention gross profit collapsed roughly 63% as the Seawell was warm-stacked all year, regulatory dockings consumed ~131 vessel-days, and 2024's one-time cancellation fees rolled off. Q1 2026 was a seasonal trough with a –$13.4M net loss and 3% gross margin.

The trailing P/E of ~99x is meaningless here; it reflects trough earnings, not a stretched multiple, and in any case the standalone earnings stream is being subsumed into a merger. The correct lens is the arbitrage/control-transfer setup: what the combined Helix-Hornbeck platform is worth, what the completion probability is, and whether the 45% minority stake at the fixed ratio offers asymmetric upside. On the available primary-source evidence, there is not enough visibility into Hornbeck's economics or the definitive deal terms to underwrite that with conviction. This is an uncertain HOLD, consistent with the four prior HOLD calls, and the burden now sits with the S-4/proxy and closing disclosures.

## Company Overview

Helix (Houston-based, incorporated in Minnesota, formerly Cal Dive International) provides specialized offshore energy services weighted toward the back end of the well lifecycle. It operates four segments:

- **Well Intervention (~56% of revenue, the profit engine):** purpose-built vessels (Q4000, Q5000, Q7000, Seawell, Well Enhancer, plus chartered Sea Helix 1 and Siem Helix 2 in Brazil) performing workovers, production enhancement, and plug-and-abandonment across the Gulf of America, North Sea, Brazil, and West Africa.
- **Robotics (~25%):** ROVs, trenchers, and chartered vessels supporting subsea construction, cable/pipeline trenching, UXO identification, and boulder relocation, roughly half its revenue tied to offshore wind (renewables ~49% of segment revenue).
- **Shallow Water Abandonment (Helix Alliance):** Gulf of America shelf decommissioning; Helix positions itself as the only provider able to perform all facets of shelf decommissioning.
- **Production Facilities:** the Helix Producer I FPU plus owned mature fields (Thunder Hawk, Droshky).

The strategic identity is decommissioning and intervention (Q1 2026 revenue split: decommissioning 56%, production maximization 28%, renewables 13%), a late-life and energy-transition orientation that is more durable than exploration-driven service work. Customers are blue-chip: Shell (~18% of 2025 revenue), Petrobras (~10%), plus BP, Oxy, Talos, and Murphy.

Hornbeck is a Gulf-focused offshore support vessel operator; combining OSV supply with Helix's intervention/robotics/decommissioning creates a larger, more integrated Gulf platform, but also concentrates Gulf of America cyclicality and puts Hornbeck's shareholders and likely governance in the senior seat.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue** recovered through 2024 and then stalled: $873M (2022) → $1,290M (2023) → $1,359M (2024) → $1,291M (2025, –5% YoY). Q1 2026 revenue was $288M (+4% YoY).

**Profitability deteriorated in 2025.** Gross profit fell ~28% to ~$159M. Adjusted EBITDA was $272M versus $303M. The decline was concentrated: Well Intervention gross profit fell roughly 63% (from ~$110M to ~$41M) as the Seawell warm-stacked for the full year on North Sea weakness, ~131 regulatory docking days hit the Q4000/Q5000/Q7000, and ~$14M of 2024 one-time contract-cancellation fees did not recur. WI utilization dropped to 72% from 90%. A $18.1M non-cash Thunder Hawk impairment hit Q4 2025.

**Q1 2026** was weak but seasonally the trough: Adjusted EBITDA $32M (versus $52M in Q1'25). Production Facilities posted a –$7.5M gross loss on Thunder Hawk workover costs. WI utilization recovered to 82% as the Seawell reactivated in February and Brazil ran near 100%.

**The balance sheet is the clearest strength.** Helix is net-cash positive: net debt of –$137M at year-end 2025 and –$198M at Q1 2026 ($501M cash versus $310M funded debt). Debt is simple and long-dated: $300M senior notes due 2029 (an expensive 9.75% coupon) plus ~$10M MARAD debt (4.93%, matures early 2027), with no significant maturities before 2029. Liquidity is ~$612M, current ratio ~2.75, D/E ~0.66. Altman Z is ~2.58 (grey zone) and Piotroski ~5/9, reflecting thin returns (ROE ~2%, ROA ~1.2%) rather than any distress.

**Cash flow and capital allocation:** FCF was $120M in 2025 (down from $163M). The company repurchased $30.2M of stock in 2025 with $128M remaining on the authorization, then suspended buybacks effective April 22, 2026 in connection with the merger. 2026 guidance is revenue $1.2–1.4B, Adjusted EBITDA $230–290M, FCF $100–160M, and capital additions $70–80M.

The earnings-surprise record is choppy: 1 beat and 3 misses over the last four quarters, with a –16.6% miss in Q1 2026 and a +761% beat in Q4 2025 that reflects the low base and lumpy contract timing rather than reliable visibility. Consensus visibility on this business is genuinely low.

## Growth Analysis

The standalone growth story is muted and now largely irrelevant to the equity. Revenue declined 5% in 2025 and 2026 guidance brackets flat-to-modestly-lower (midpoint ~$1.3B). The recovery levers are real but incremental: WI utilization rebounding from 72% toward the low-80s, the Seawell back to work, the North Sea Enabler on a new two-year charter from July, and Brazil running full. Against those are explicit management flags of second-half 2026 schedule gaps on the Q4000 and Q7000 and continued North Sea fiscal drag from the UK Energy Profits Levy.

The renewables/robotics line is two-sided. The January 2025 US federal action pausing offshore wind leasing pressures East Coast trenching work, but Helix has redeployed assets to European and APAC wind (North Sea Enabler, Trym, Patriot, Grand Canyon III), so the segment is being repositioned rather than impaired.

The durable structural tailwind is decommissioning: plug-and-abandonment obligations are regulatory, not price-elastic, and the Gulf of America shelf carries a large, aging inventory of wells that must eventually be abandoned. Helix's full-service shelf offering is a genuine franchise here. But this is steady, not high-growth, and the merger changes the entire growth calculus by combining with an OSV operator whose forward economics are not disclosed in Helix's filings.

I do not see an estimate-revision or revenue-acceleration signal that would trigger the high-conviction growth setup. Growth prospects score in the mid-4s.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing and forward P/E of ~99x are not usable metrics; they reflect trough 2025 earnings depressed by one-time impairments and warm-stacking, not a genuinely expensive multiple. On normalized Adjusted EBITDA of ~$260M (midpoint of guidance) against an enterprise value of roughly $1.2B (market cap ~$1.41B less ~$198M net cash), the standalone name trades around 4.6x EV/EBITDA, reasonable for an offshore services business at a mid-cycle point, and cheap relative to the peer median trailing P/E of 38.2x (RES, VTOL, INVX, AESI, CLB), though the peer set is heterogeneous and mostly not comparable in business mix.

The controlling consideration is that valuation now runs through the merger, not the standalone DCF. At the fixed 10.27167x exchange ratio, HLX holders end up with ~45% of a combined entity renamed Hornbeck. The stock's value therefore depends on (a) Hornbeck's standalone value and forward earnings, which Helix's filings do not disclose, and (b) the probability and timing of close. Without the S-4 pro forma financials, there is no defensible basis to conclude the current price offers asymmetric upside; the information needed to make that call is not yet public.

I would resist scoring cheapness highly. A low headline multiple in a stalled, low-return offshore services business without a specific catalyst that the market is missing is precisely the value-trap profile that tends to sort the wrong way. The catalyst here, the merger, is known and priced, and its terms hand control to the other party. Valuation scores 4.5.

## Competitive Landscape

Helix occupies a defensible niche. The purpose-built intervention fleet is hard to replicate, the SLB Subsea Services Alliance embeds it in a major service ecosystem, and the full-service Gulf shelf decommissioning offering is differentiated. The tilt toward non-discretionary decommissioning and mature-field maintenance is structurally more durable than drilling-levered peers.

The trajectory is stable-to-slightly-soft on a standalone basis: WI utilization fell sharply in 2025 before partially recovering, North Sea share is under fiscal pressure, and the US wind pause forced a redeployment of robotics assets. Five-year TSR of 149.3 trailed the peer group's 181.7, which is the credible strategic rationale for the deal.

The combined platform gains cross-selling potential but concentrates Gulf of America exposure and cyclicality, and the renaming plus majority Hornbeck ownership signal that the combined company will be run on Hornbeck's terms. Net, the competitive position is neither clearly improving nor clearly deteriorating from the shareholder's seat; it is being transformed into something whose contours are not fully disclosed. Competitive position scores 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

- **Merger execution and control-transfer risk (now dominant):** The Q1 2026 10-Q added new risk factors covering deal-completion uncertainty, integration, litigation, a termination fee, management-attention diversion, and the ~45% minority position with loss of control and naming rights. A break would return HLX to a stalled standalone with suspended buybacks; a completion converts holders into minority owners of a Hornbeck-controlled entity.
- **Customer concentration:** Top six customers ≈ 82% of backlog; Shell and Petrobras together ~28% of revenue. Loss or delay from either is material.
- **Well Intervention utilization/spot exposure:** 2025 showed how fast WI economics swing on stacking, docking, and North Sea spot softness; management explicitly flags second-half 2026 schedule gaps on the Q4000 and Q7000.
- **US offshore wind policy:** the 2025 federal leasing pause pressures US robotics work, partially mitigated by European/APAC redeployment.
- **North Sea fiscal drag:** the UK Energy Profits Levy weighs on customer spending.
- **Owned production exposure:** the Thunder Hawk impairment illustrates the commodity risk in owned mature fields.
- **Expensive debt:** the 9.75% 2029 notes are a high cost of capital for a net-cash company, a post-merger refinancing lever, but a drag until then.

The overriding idiosyncratic risk is the binary merger outcome, which is not fully priced because the pro forma terms are not yet public. Oil-price volatility remains a swing factor for customer activity broadly, though Helix's decommissioning-weighted book is less directly exposed than drilling-levered peers. Risk factors score 4.5.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 56.1% sits rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 46.7% (a 9.4-point spread), consistent with a market pricing elevated uncertainty around the deal and second-half schedule. The term structure is in contango (56.1% near vs 58.9% at six months), a normal-to-complacent shape, and the put/call skew is call-heavy (–3.9%) with a low put/call OI ratio of 0.29; positioning is not defensively tilted despite the binary merger overhang. IV has eased 2.4 points since late June as the stock rose ~9.7%, suggesting some resolution of near-term anxiety rather than accumulating hedging pressure. Short interest is modest at 6.2% of float (4.4 days to cover), so this is not a crowded bear setup. On balance, the options market is neither confirming nor contradicting a strong directional view, appropriate for an arbitrage-like situation whose outcome hinges on a documented, on-track process.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The merger closes on schedule in 2H 2026, the combined Helix-Hornbeck platform delivers cross-selling and cost synergies across a larger Gulf offshore services franchise, and the ~45% minority stake proves to be worth more than the current implied standalone value. Standalone, WI utilization continues recovering from 72% toward the high-70s/low-80s, the Seawell and North Sea Enabler add durable charter revenue, decommissioning demand grows structurally, and the net-cash balance sheet funds a post-merger refinancing of the 9.75% notes. In this path the stock re-rates toward $12–13 as pro forma economics become visible.

**Bear case.** The merger faces antitrust or shareholder friction, or closes on terms that dilute legacy Helix holders' economic interest more than the market assumes; buybacks stay suspended and capital-return optionality remains frozen; second-half 2026 WI schedule gaps and North Sea fiscal drag keep margins thin; and Gulf concentration in the combined entity amplifies cyclicality. Standalone, a deal break returns HLX to a low-return (~2% ROE), stalled-revenue business worth roughly $7–8 on ~4x EV/EBITDA. Downside to ~$7 is ~27% from current levels.

**Base case.** The transaction advances toward a 2H 2026 close (the 8-K sequence — definitive agreement 4/24, additional agreements 5/5, shareholder-vote submission 5/13, June 12 update — is consistent with an on-track process), and HLX trades in a $9–10 range as the market waits for the S-4 pro forma and closing terms. The standalone fundamentals stabilize at the seasonal trough and improve modestly into the Q2/Q3 peak.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD, classified **uncertain**: the equity has been transformed into a control-transfer/merger situation whose ultimate value depends on disclosures not yet public (Hornbeck's pro forma economics and definitive closing terms), so neither a confident growth thesis nor a clean value case can be underwritten today.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The S-4/definitive proxy discloses combined pro forma financials that show the ~45% minority stake trading at a clear discount to sum-of-parts intrinsic value, or synergy guidance materially above expectations.
- WI utilization sustains ≥82% across two consecutive quarters with gross margin recovering back toward the mid-teens, confirming 2025 was a trough rather than structural decay.
- Post-close, refinancing of the 9.75% 2029 notes at a materially lower rate, adding EPS and freeing capital.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Regulatory or shareholder developments that materially raise the probability of a deal break, or a break itself that returns HLX to a stalled standalone with no buyback.
- Loss or significant delay of a Shell or Petrobras contract (together ~28% of revenue).
- 2H 2026 WI schedule gaps that push Adjusted EBITDA below the ~$230M guidance floor, or a further impairment on owned production.

**Reassessment timeline:** the next natural checkpoints are the S-4/definitive proxy filing, the merger closing 8-K (expected 2H 2026), and the Q2 2026 earnings release, which should confirm whether the seasonal recovery in WI utilization is holding.

## Conclusion

Helix is a fundamentally sound but low-return offshore services business: genuine net-cash balance sheet, a durable decommissioning/well-intervention franchise, blue-chip customers, and management whose "compensation actually paid" turned negative in 2025 in credible alignment with shareholders. The 2025 profit collapse was concentrated and partly temporary (Seawell warm-stacking, docking calendar, one-time-fee roll-off), and Q1 2026 marked a seasonal trough with WI utilization already recovering.

The central fact is the pending all-stock merger with Hornbeck at a fixed 10.27x ratio that converts HLX holders into a ~45% minority of a renamed, Delaware-domiciled combined company, with buybacks suspended and control ceded. The stock is priced on deal-completion odds and Hornbeck's value, neither of which can be underwritten from Helix's own filings. Until the S-4 pro forma and definitive terms are public, the correct posture is to hold: the recovery in the standalone business supports the floor, the balance sheet limits downside, but the asymmetry needed for a BUY is not yet demonstrable and the concentrated, binary deal risk holds the risk score down. This marks the fifth consecutive HOLD; the bar to continue it is met only because the merger suspends the standalone analytical framework, and the resolution catalysts (proxy, close, Q2 print) are dated and near.
