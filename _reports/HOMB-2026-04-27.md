## Executive Summary

Home BancShares is a $23B-asset (pro forma for Mountain Commerce) regional bank holding company operating through Centennial Bank across 226 branches in Arkansas, Florida, Texas, Tennessee, and Alabama. The company delivered record 2025 results: net income of $475M (+18% YoY), a 4.51% net interest margin, and a 40.88% efficiency ratio that ranks in the top decile of U.S. banks. Q1 2026 sustained this trajectory with $118M in net income, 2.09% ROA, and record $22.15 book value per share.

The investment case centers on three elements: (1) an operational efficiency advantage that generates structurally higher returns on equity and assets than peers, (2) deliberate balance sheet de-risking (private credit cut 80%+, $160M sub debt retired, hurricane reserves proactively established), and (3) a $2.7M open-market stock purchase by CEO John Allison on April 17, 2026, at ~$27/share. Allison made this purchase the same week a $110M Texas loan was disclosed as non-accrual, implying genuine confidence in resolution.

The principal risks are concentrated CRE exposure (53%+ of loans), the unresolved $110M Texas credit, negative estimate revision momentum (-2% to -3% across all forward periods), and the integration of Mountain Commerce Bancorp adding $7-7.5M/quarter in pre-synergy expenses. At ~10.9x forward earnings and a ~3.2% dividend yield, HOMB trades at a discount to the 11.8x peer median P/E, reasonable given the bank's materially superior profitability metrics.

## Company Overview

Home BancShares operates as a disciplined, acquisition-driven community banking franchise. Since 1999, the company has completed 23 acquisitions, building a Sun Belt footprint concentrated in Arkansas (75 branches), Florida (78), Texas (59), and most recently Tennessee (8 branches via the April 2026 Mountain Commerce deal). The bank generates 82% of revenue from net interest income, with supplemental fee income from service charges, mortgage banking, trust services, and its Centennial Commercial Finance Group (CFG).

Three specialty platforms differentiate HOMB from generic community banks: Shore Premier Finance (high-end marine lending, ~$1.4B portfolio), the CFG national commercial lending platform (~$2.0B in loans), and an increasingly de-risked private credit book (now $87M, down from a $500M peak in 2022). The company's stated acquisition philosophy requires deals to be "triple accretive" — to EPS, book value, and returns — which has historically prevented overpaying for targets.

## Financial Analysis

### Profitability

HOMB's profitability metrics are consistently superior to regional banking peers:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1 2026 Ann. |
|--------|------|------|------|-------------|
| Net Income ($M) | $392.9 | $402.2 | $475.4 | $472.8 |
| Diluted EPS | $1.94 | $2.01 | $2.41 | $2.40 |
| ROA | 1.77% | 1.77% | 2.10% | 2.09% |
| ROE | 10.82% | 10.43% | 11.61% | ~11.5% |
| NIM (FTE) | 4.25% | 4.27% | 4.51% | 4.51% |
| Efficiency Ratio | 46.21% | 42.74% | 40.88% | 41.6% |

The 2025 breakout was driven by a $64.5M decline in interest expense (-14.3%) as deposit costs repriced lower faster than asset yields, combined with continued expense discipline. The NIM at 4.51% exceeds the industry average of ~3.3-3.5% by approximately 100-120 bps, a structural advantage reflecting HOMB's low-cost deposit franchise and above-average loan pricing.

The 40.88% efficiency ratio means the bank spends less than 41 cents for every dollar of revenue. Most regional peers operate at 55-65%. This advantage compounds over time: lower costs generate higher retained earnings, stronger capital, and capacity for accretive deployment.

### Balance Sheet & Capital

HOMB is significantly overcapitalized:
- CET1 ratio: 16.30% (well-capitalized minimum: 6.5%)
- Total risk-based capital: 19.06%
- Equity-to-assets: 18.78%
- Tangible book value: $14.60/share (current price = 1.81x TBV)

Liquidity stands at $5.96B in net available sources, well exceeding $4.56B in net uninsured deposits. The company retired $160M in subordinated debt during 2025 and paid off a $700M BTFP advance.

Unrealized losses on the AFS securities portfolio improved to $216.9M from $335.4M in 2024, reducing a source of concern that weighed on bank valuations broadly in 2023-2024.

### Credit Quality

Net charge-offs collapsed to 0.02% of average loans in 2025, down from 0.41% in 2024 (which included a deliberate $60.8M cleanup of Texas credits). Non-performing loans improved to 0.54% of total loans. The allowance for credit losses stands at 1.90% of loans, providing 350% coverage of NPLs.

The $110M Texas non-accrual credit disclosed in Q1 2026 is the dominant near-term credit risk. Management has monitored this relationship for 1.5-2 years and expects resolution via payoff or collateral liquidation in Q2-Q3 2026 without additional loss. The forbearance agreement includes legal protections. The bank generates $150-160M in quarterly pre-provision net revenue and carries ~$300M in reserves, providing substantial absorption capacity even if the credit deteriorates.

### Capital Allocation

Dividend payout ratio is conservative at 33.4% ($0.805/share in 2025), yielding approximately 3.0% at current prices. The company repurchased 2.9M shares ($81.4M) in 2025 and 507K shares ($13.9M) in Q1 2026. The board has authorized 17.1M shares remaining under the buyback program, and management has indicated intent to repurchase the ~5.5M shares issued for the Mountain Commerce deal.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are moderate. Several crosscurrents affect the near-term outlook:

**Positives:**
- Mountain Commerce adds ~$1.4B in loans and $1.56B in deposits, expanding into high-growth Tennessee markets (Knoxville, Nashville)
- Sun Belt demographics (population growth, in-migration) support underlying franchise expansion
- The elevated payoff environment ($1B+ projected in each of Q2 and Q3 2026) is partially a function of falling rates enabling borrower refinancing; this is cyclical, and new origination should fill the gap over time

**Negatives:**
- Analyst estimates are declining: current-year EPS revised down 1.9% over 90 days (from $2.51 to $2.47), next-year down 2.0% (from $2.64 to $2.59)
- Mountain Commerce integration expenses ($7-7.5M/quarter) will weigh on efficiency through the November 2026 system conversion
- If rates continue declining, the NIM advantage will narrow as asset yields reprice lower; HOMB's 60.8% floating-rate loan book is a headwind in a declining rate environment
- Organic loan growth may be flat or slightly negative near-term given the elevated payoff cycle

Consensus projects $2.47 EPS for FY2026 and $2.59 for FY2027, implying 2.5% and 4.9% growth respectively. These are unimpressive growth rates, offset by the bank's yield and capital return profile. Mountain Commerce synergies post-November conversion could provide upside to 2027 estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

At $26.46, HOMB trades at:
- 10.7x forward P/E (FY2026E $2.47)
- 10.2x FY2027E P/E ($2.59)
- 1.81x tangible book value
- 1.19x book value ($22.15)
- ~3.2% dividend yield

Peer median P/E is 11.8x. Direct peers like Bank OZK (7.7x) and Associated Banc-Corp (9.9x) trade cheaper, while Atlantic Union (18.6x) trades much higher. HOMB's premium to the cheapest peers is justified by its 2.09% ROA (vs. industry ~1.0-1.2%), top-decile efficiency, and overcapitalized balance sheet.

The TBV multiple of 1.81x is above average for the peer group but below levels typical for banks generating sustained 2%+ ROA. Banks with comparable profitability metrics (e.g., Glacier Bancorp, Cullen/Frost) have historically traded at 2.0-2.5x TBV.

The sell-side consensus target of $31.29 (median $31.00, range $29-$33) implies approximately 18-25% upside from current levels. This target range assumes NIM stability, successful Mountain Commerce integration, and resolution of the Texas credit without material loss.

The stock's pullback from its February 2026 high of $30.83 to the current $26.46 (-14.2%) reflects three factors: (1) the Q1 2026 $110M Texas non-accrual disclosure, (2) broader regional bank sector weakness from rising recession fears and rate uncertainty, and (3) negative estimate revisions. The CEO's $2.7M purchase at $26.96 suggests insider assessment that these factors are transitory or already discounted.

## Competitive Landscape

1. **Efficiency moat:** The 40.88% efficiency ratio is a genuine competitive advantage that most peers cannot replicate. This reflects decades of operational discipline and scale benefits from acquisitions. Each successive acquisition gets integrated onto the same platform at lower incremental cost.

2. **NIM advantage:** The 4.51% NIM exceeds peers by 100-120 bps, driven by a low-cost deposit franchise ($4B in non-interest-bearing deposits, 22.5% of total) and disciplined loan pricing.

3. **Acquisition pipeline:** The "triple accretive" discipline positions HOMB as a consolidator in a sector with 4,000+ potential targets. Management has explicitly stated they won't be the highest bidder, which limits deal flow but ensures each acquisition improves returns.

4. **Geographic positioning:** The Sun Belt footprint (FL, TX, AR, TN) benefits from long-term demographic trends. Tennessee entry via Mountain Commerce diversifies revenue away from hurricane-exposed Florida.

5. **Private credit retreat:** The deliberate drawdown of the CFG private credit book from $500M to $87M was well-timed. Management recognized that yield compression and looser underwriting by new entrants were degrading the risk/return profile and exited before conditions deteriorated.

Risks to competitive position include potential CRE stress in a recession scenario and the challenge of maintaining the efficiency advantage as the franchise grows larger and more complex.

## Risk Assessment

### Concentrated CRE Portfolio (HIGH)
74.1% of loans are secured by real estate, with CRE at 53.2%. Hotel exposure totals $1.16B (7.4% of loans). Construction/land development is $2.73B (17.4%). In a recession accompanied by commercial real estate stress — particularly in Sun Belt markets experiencing supply absorption challenges — this concentration could produce outsized credit losses. The rapid growth in multifamily from $496M to $1.14B deserves monitoring given national multifamily vacancy trends.

### $110M Texas Non-Accrual (ELEVATED, TIME-BOUND)
If the credit is resolved without material loss, this converts from risk to positive catalyst. If it deteriorates, the impact is manageable (~4-5 bps of NIM, well within reserve and PPNR absorption capacity) but would damage market confidence.

### Interest Rate Sensitivity (MODERATE)
With 60.8% floating-rate loan exposure, continued rate declines would compress asset yields. The deposit cost offset is real but has a floor — non-interest-bearing deposits are already at 22.5% of total and can't reprice lower. If the Fed cuts further (currently 3.50-3.75%), NIM could compress 10-20 bps over 12 months. Conversely, the current environment where the Fed is holding or potentially hiking would benefit HOMB's asset-sensitive balance sheet.

### Natural Disaster Exposure (MODERATE)
Florida (78 branches) and Gulf Coast operations carry hurricane risk. The $33.4M reserve established for 2024 storms demonstrates the real cost. Each active hurricane season introduces volatility to credit quality and property valuations in the core franchise area.

### Short Interest
At 6.3% of float with 7.1 days to cover, short interest is modestly elevated but not extreme. The 7-day cover ratio suggests shorts are somewhat crowded relative to daily liquidity. This creates potential for upside pressure on positive news (Texas credit resolution, Mountain Commerce synergies) but is not itself a catalyst.

## Conclusion

HOMB is a well-run regional bank trading at a reasonable valuation with identifiable near-term catalysts for re-rating. The efficiency ratio, ROA, and NIM represent genuine competitive advantages that most peers cannot replicate. The CEO's $2.7M open-market purchase, made the same week the $110M problem credit was disclosed, carries significant weight — the person with the most information about the bank's asset quality is buying at current levels.

The risks are real: CRE concentration is above average, estimate revisions are negative, and the Texas credit remains unresolved. The Mountain Commerce integration adds execution risk and near-term expense pressure. These risks are reflected in a stock trading 14% below its 52-week high and at a discount to its recent trading range.

At 10.7x forward earnings with a ~3.2% dividend yield, supported by top-decile profitability and insider buying, HOMB offers an asymmetric risk/reward profile: 13-25% upside in the base-to-bull case against 15% downside in the bear case, with the dividend providing a meaningful floor. BUY with a 2-3% position size, appropriate for the idiosyncratic credit risk in the near term.
