## Executive Summary

Albany International operates two distinct businesses: a dominant Machine Clothing (MC) segment generating consistent 22% operating margins and a growing Albany Engineered Composites (AEC) segment with proprietary 3D-woven technology for aerospace/defense applications. The company's recent financial results are distorted by a $155.9M charge on the CH-53K helicopter structures program, a fixed-price contract suffering from labor overruns and inflation at the Salt Lake City facility. Strip out CH-53K, and the core business produces roughly $200-220M in adjusted EBITDA on ~$1.05B of revenue, implying 8-9x EV/EBITDA on a market-leading industrial franchise with aerospace growth optionality.

Q1 2026 results showed meaningful recovery: $311M revenue (+7.8% YoY), $0.60 adjusted EPS beating consensus of $0.52, and broad-based AEC program ramps (LEAP, F-35, JASSM). The strategic review of the problematic Salt Lake City operations is progressing with Guggenheim advising. However, the stock at $61.63 already reflects partial recovery from its $41.15 trough, and execution risks remain: the CH-53K divestiture could yield unfavorable terms, China-facing MC demand continues to weaken, and the options market is pricing significant near-term uncertainty (74.7% near-term IV vs. 43.9% historical vol). The thesis is constructive but requires further validation.

## Company Overview

Albany International, founded 130 years ago, operates through two segments. Machine Clothing (~60% of revenue) produces custom-engineered polymer fabrics and process belts for paper, tissue, and packaging manufacturing. The business holds clear global market leadership following the 2023 Heimbach acquisition. Albany Engineered Composites (~40% of revenue) manufactures advanced composite structures for commercial aerospace (LEAP engine fan blades/cases via exclusive Safran partnership) and defense (CH-53K, F-35, JASSM missiles, Boeing 787). AEC's proprietary 3D-woven composite technology — lightweight alternatives to titanium — is the company's primary growth driver.

The company is headquartered in Portsmouth, NH with approximately 5,700 employees across 12+ countries. New CEO Gunnar Kleveland (joined 2023) has assembled a refreshed management team including a Boeing-veteran CFO and a former Lockheed Martin supply chain executive leading AEC.

The 2025 result is almost entirely explained by the CH-53K program. AEC went from a -$12M operating loss in 2024 to -$145M in 2025, with $155.9M in negative cumulative catch-up adjustments. MC remained profitable at $156M operating income (22.1% margin), though down from $184M the prior year on Asia weakness and integration disruption.

**Cash Flow Quality:**

Despite the GAAP net loss of -$57M, operating cash flow was $152M in 2025 because the contract loss provision is a non-cash charge. Free cash flow of $83M (7% margin) demonstrates continued cash generation through the crisis. Capex of $70M ran below $88M depreciation, meaning the company was not reinvesting aggressively while digesting the CH-53K issue.

**Balance Sheet:**

Total debt of $456M against a ~$1.8B market cap and ~$457M of liquidity (cash + revolver capacity). Leverage ratio of 1.66x is manageable. No principal payments due until 2028 ($331M), providing a multi-year runway to resolve the CH-53K situation. Altman Z-score of 1.99 sits in the "grey zone" — not distressed but not comfortably healthy.

**Q1 2026 — Recovery Beginning:**

Revenue of $311M (+7.8% YoY) with adjusted EBIT of $48M (15.5% margin). AEC revenue of $145M versus $114M prior year, driven by LEAP volume, F-35, JASSM, and 787 ramps. CH-53K is now booked at 0% margin (losses fully reserved). Q2 guidance of $335-345M revenue and $0.70-$0.80 adjusted EPS implies accelerating recovery. Full-year 2026 consensus of $2.80 EPS would put the stock at ~22x forward earnings.

## Growth Analysis

**AEC Growth Trajectory (ex-CH-53K):**

The core AEC franchise is positioned on the right programs. LEAP engine production is ramping toward pre-pandemic rates (Safran/CFM targeting 2,000+ engines/year). JASSM/LRASM missile production is being maximized at Lockheed Martin's request including overtime. The new Pratt & Whitney Geared Turbofan contract expands composites revenue beyond the LEAP concentration. F-35 sustainment volumes are increasing.

If the Salt Lake City divestiture proceeds cleanly, remaining AEC is primarily high-value composites work with technology barriers — a structurally higher-margin business than the structural assembly work that caused the CH-53K crisis.

**Machine Clothing:**

MC growth is modest — publication grades are in secular decline, offset by packaging and tissue growth. The Heimbach integration should deliver cost synergies (multiple facility closures underway across South Korea, Switzerland, UK, Italy, France). Revenue growth of 0-3% is realistic with margin recovery toward 24-25% as integration matures.

**Combined Path to Normalized Earnings:**

MC at $700-720M revenue, 24% operating margin = $168-173M operating income. AEC (ex-CH-53K) at $350-400M revenue, 10-12% margin = $35-48M. Combined operating income potential of $203-221M, or roughly $4.50-5.50 adjusted EPS on 29M shares (after share repurchases). This represents substantial upside from the current $2.80 consensus but requires 12-18 months of execution.

## Competitive Landscape

**Machine Clothing:** Albany holds clear global leadership post-Heimbach. The business benefits from: (1) custom engineering requirements specific to each paper machine position, (2) long customer relationships with high switching costs (product failure = machine downtime at $10K+/hour), (3) 130 years of institutional knowledge. Threats include Chinese domestic supplier growth via government preference policies and Voith's ability to bundle MC with machine sales. The Heimbach acquisition eliminated the #2 player, consolidating the competitive landscape further.

**AEC:** The 3D-woven composite technology is genuinely differentiated — replacing titanium with lighter, stronger composite structures at scale. The LEAP exclusivity (via Safran JV) creates a structural moat for the most popular narrowbody engine program globally. However, competition from Spirit AeroSystems, Hexcel, GKN, and Toray exists in the broader composites market. AEC's competitive position is technology-driven (difficult to replicate the 3D weaving know-how) rather than cost-driven, which provides durable advantages on programs where the technology matters.

The CH-53K program revealed AEC's vulnerability when operating outside its technology moat — structural assembly work under fixed pricing where the company has no differentiated capability to absorb labor and inflation overruns.

## Risk Assessment

**1. CH-53K Strategic Review Outcome (HIGH):** The divestiture of Salt Lake City operations is in progress (Guggenheim advising, PwC analysis completed, marketing materials being finalized). Outcome uncertainty is severe: the facility generates ~$130M revenue at a loss, limiting buyer appeal. If no buyer materializes, Albany must either continue absorbing losses (the $155.9M charge assumed full program life), exit with contract termination costs, or negotiate with Sikorsky for different terms. Each path carries impairment risk.

**2. Fixed-Price Contract Structural Risk:** The CH-53K debacle was not isolated — 2025 also saw negative adjustments on Boeing waste tank, F-35, and JSF programs totaling $165.8M across all affected contracts. Long-term fixed-price contracts with complex manufacturing are inherently risky when labor markets tighten or materials inflate. This risk persists in remaining AEC programs.

**3. China/Asia MC Demand:** Chinese paper overcapacity and preference for domestic suppliers creates structural market share risk. Management has "limited visibility" beyond near-term. Asia contributed materially to the 2024-2025 MC revenue decline.

**4. Safran/LEAP Concentration:** Safran represents ~15% of consolidated revenue and ~37% of AEC revenue. The relationship is strong (exclusive supply, joint venture) but concentration risk is real. Any disruption to narrowbody production rates (supply chain issues, MAX grounding, demand reduction) directly impacts AIN.

**5. Tariff Exposure:** Over $100M imported to the U.S. in 2024 (45% Mexico, 29% Canada). Current tariff regime could significantly impact cost structure, though customer-directed raw materials may allow contractual pass-through.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($80-90, 30-45% upside):**
- Salt Lake City operations divested at acceptable terms by late 2026
- Remaining AEC business grows 10-15% annually on LEAP, F-35, JASSM, and new Pratt & Whitney ramps
- MC stabilizes and delivers Heimbach integration synergies (margin recovery to 24-25%)
- Normalized EPS of $4.50-5.00 by FY2027-28 at 18-20x multiple = $80-100
- Defense spending growth supports AEC diversification away from commercial aerospace concentration

**Bear Case ($42-48, 23-32% downside):**
- CH-53K strategic review yields no buyer; additional charges required
- LEAP production ramp disappoints (supply chain issues, rate delays)
- China MC demand deterioration accelerates; further write-downs on Heimbach-related assets
- Tariffs compound cost pressure across both segments
- Stock re-tests November 2025 lows

**Base Case ($68-72, 10-17% upside):**
- Gradual improvement through 2026 as AEC ramps ex-CH-53K
- Salt Lake City resolution in late 2026/early 2027 removes overhang
- FY2026 delivers near consensus ($2.80 EPS); FY2027 starts approaching $3.50-4.00
- Multiple expands moderately as execution de-risks the story
- 22-24x forward P/E on $3.00 = $66-72

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Definitive agreement announced for Salt Lake City divestiture at reasonable terms (no additional material charge)
2. Two consecutive quarters of AEC operating margin >10% (ex-CH-53K) demonstrating sustainable profitability
3. Stock pullback to $52-54 range (14-15x normalized earnings) providing better entry with unchanged fundamentals

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Additional CH-53K or other AEC program charges exceeding $50M
2. MC organic revenue declining >5% for two consecutive quarters (structural deterioration beyond cyclical weakness)
3. LEAP production rate delays or Safran relationship deterioration signaled by purchase order reductions

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 results (expected late July 2026) and any CH-53K strategic review announcement. The divestiture process should produce actionable news within 3-6 months based on management's "on schedule" commentary.

## Conclusion

Albany International is a good business obscured by one bad contract. The Machine Clothing franchise is a global leader with durable competitive advantages. The AEC composites business holds proprietary technology positioned on the right aerospace and defense programs. The CH-53K crisis, while severe, appears containable with the losses now fully reserved and a strategic review underway.

At $61.63, the stock prices in meaningful recovery but also discounts considerable ongoing uncertainty — reflected in the 4.09 put/call OI ratio and steep IV backwardation. CEO open-market purchases at $42 (Nov 2025) and $60 (Aug 2025) provide genuine insider conviction. The Q1 2026 beat and improving guidance trajectory support the recovery thesis.

The HOLD recommendation reflects two factors: (1) the stock has already recovered 50% from its trough, partially pricing in the recovery, and (2) the CH-53K strategic review represents a genuine binary event whose outcome range is wide enough to warrant patience. The analyst consensus (3 Holds, 1 Strong Sell, mean target $56.25 below current price) confirms market skepticism. A cleaner entry would come either at lower prices ($52-54) or after the Salt Lake City resolution provides clarity. For existing holders, the trajectory is constructive enough to maintain positions.
