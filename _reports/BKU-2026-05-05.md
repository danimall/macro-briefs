## Executive Summary

BankUnited is a $35 billion-asset regional bank executing a credible multi-year balance sheet transformation — shifting from residential mortgage concentration and wholesale funding dependence toward relationship-driven commercial banking with core deposit funding. The results are quantifiable: NIM expanded 22bps in FY2025 to 2.95%, non-interest-bearing deposits grew 20%, FHLB advances declined $2.2B, and the loan-to-deposit ratio improved to 82.7%. Management guides NIM toward 3.20% by year-end 2026, implying meaningful incremental earnings power.

However, the transformation coexists with deteriorating credit quality that warrants caution. Non-performing loans surged 49% YoY to $373M, net charge-offs doubled to 0.30%, and the allowance coverage ratio relative to NPLs compressed to 59%. The Q1 2026 EPS miss of $0.83 vs. $0.97 consensus was the first miss in four quarters and broke a streak of beats. With $1.4B in CRE office exposure (33% maturing within 12 months in a 5%+ rate environment), the credit trajectory over the next two quarters will determine whether the NIM expansion thesis dominates or gets overwhelmed by provision charges.

At $46.90, BKU trades at 1.17x tangible book value and ~13x forward earnings — a slight premium to regional bank peer medians (11.7x P/E) that is partially justified by the above-average NIM trajectory but fully captures current consensus. The stock has appreciated 37% over the past year, and the options market is pricing elevated near-term stress (54.3% IV vs. 29.8% HV, backwardation structure). The risk/reward is balanced at current levels.

## Company Overview

BankUnited, Inc. operates through its wholly-owned subsidiary BankUnited, N.A., providing commercial and consumer banking services primarily in Florida with secondary presence in the New York Tri-State area, Dallas, Atlanta, and Charlotte. The bank manages approximately $22.0B in loans (71.2% commercial, 28.8% residential), $26.5B in deposits, and a $9.3B investment portfolio.

The strategic model centers on specialized deposit verticals — title insurance (~$4.4B), homeowners associations (~$2.3B), and municipal/government accounts — that generate low-cost relationship deposits. Commercial lending focuses on C&I ($7.0B), non-owner-occupied CRE ($6.1B), and construction/mortgage warehouse ($1.4B combined). The bank deliberately de-emphasizes residential lending and is winding down legacy franchise/equipment finance portfolios.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Recovery from 2023 Trough:** Net income recovered from $178.7M (FY2023) to $268.4M (FY2025), a 50% improvement driven primarily by NIM expansion and funding cost reduction rather than balance sheet growth. Pre-provision net revenue (PPNR) reached $429.7M, up 16% YoY.

**NIM Expansion Mechanics:** The core earnings driver is measurable:
- Average cost of interest-bearing deposits: 4.10% → 3.39% (-71bps YoY)
- Spot APY of total deposits at year-end: 2.10%
- Non-interest-bearing deposits: grew $1.5B (+20%) to 31% of total deposits
- FHLB advances: declined from $3.8B average to $1.56B year-end

This produces a NIM path from 2.73% → 2.95% in FY2025, with management guiding to 3.20% by December 2026. Each 10bps of NIM on a $35B asset base translates to approximately $35M of incremental NII ($0.46/share pre-tax).

**Capital Position:** CET1 at 12.3% provides substantial buffer. Tangible book value grew 10% to $40.14/share. The $200M buyback authorization (January 2026) represents ~5.8% of shares outstanding at current prices. Dividend yield of 2.8% ($1.32 annualized) was increased 6% in January 2026.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $358.6M comfortably exceeds net income, reflecting clean earnings quality. Available liquidity covers 138% of uninsured/uncollateralized deposits.

**Q1 2026 Miss:** The $0.83 EPS vs. $0.97 estimate was attributed to seasonal softness in deposits and loan production. Revenue of $273.7M missed the $284M estimate by 3.6%. This interrupts a beat streak and raises questions about whether the NIM trajectory can be achieved linearly.

## Growth Analysis

**Organic Growth Levers:**
1. Continued NIDDA growth from title, HOA, and commercial verticals (target: further share of deposits from interest-bearing to non-interest-bearing)
2. C&I loan growth (~5-7% annual target based on recent trajectory)
3. NIM expansion from 2.95% toward 3.20%+ as remaining wholesale funding reprices lower
4. Fee income growth from treasury management and commercial banking services

**Growth Constraints:**
- Balance sheet is not growing (deliberate strategy — total assets stable at $35B)
- Residential loan portfolio is in planned runoff
- Franchise/equipment finance being wound down
- Geographic expansion limited (Florida + NYC are mature, competitive markets)

**Estimate Revisions:** Current-year estimates declined 2.1-2.5% over 90 days (reflecting the Q1 miss), but next-year estimates rose 2.8% over the same period. The market is looking through Q1 seasonal weakness toward continued NIM expansion. Current-quarter and next-quarter estimates are rising modestly (+0.9% and +3.0% over 30 days).

**Earnings Growth Path:** FY2025 EPS of $3.53, current-year estimate of $4.12 (+17% YoY), next-year $4.57 (+11%). If NIM reaches 3.20% with stable credit costs, the $4.57 next-year estimate is achievable.

## Valuation Assessment

At $46.90, BKU trades at:
- 12.9x trailing EPS ($3.53)
- 11.4x current-year estimate ($4.12)
- 10.3x next-year estimate ($4.57)
- 1.17x tangible book value ($40.14)
- 2.8% dividend yield

**Peer Comparison:** Regional bank peers trade at median 11.7x P/E with 3.3% dividend yield. BKU's slight premium (12.9x trailing, but 11.4x forward) is partially justified by above-peer NIM expansion trajectory and improving profitability. However, the premium is modest and already reflects the transformation thesis at consensus.

**TBV Multiple Context:** At 9% ROE, 1.17x TBV is fair. If ROE expands to 10-11% (consistent with 3.20% NIM), a 1.3-1.4x multiple is supportable — implying $52-56 per share. The analyst consensus mean target of ~$52 (median $53) reflects this scenario.

**Valuation Gap Assessment:** The stock has already re-rated from ~$33 (May 2025) to $46.90 — a 42% move that has largely priced in the NIM expansion story. Remaining upside to consensus targets ($52-53) is 11-13%, roughly equivalent to a year of earnings plus dividend yield. There is no clear mispricing at current levels; the market is pricing the transformation thesis at approximately fair value.

## Competitive Landscape

BKU operates in the highly competitive Florida and NYC regional banking markets.

**Strengths:**
- Specialized deposit verticals (title insurance, HOA) provide sticky, low-cost funding that most competitors cannot replicate easily
- Strong commercial lending relationships in middle-market segment
- Short-duration investment portfolio (1.73 years, 69% floating) provides balance sheet optionality
- Digital banking capabilities for commercial clients

**Weaknesses:**
- No dominant market share in any single geography
- Competes against both larger nationals (with scale advantages) and smaller community banks (with relationship advantages)
- Private credit funds increasingly compete for middle-market lending
- No significant proprietary technology or platform advantage

**Trajectory:** Competitive position is stable to slightly improving. The NIDDA growth and commercial deposit vertical execution demonstrate the franchise is gaining share in targeted segments. However, the competitive moat is narrow — these verticals are replicable by well-resourced competitors over time.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit Quality — Primary Concern:**
- NPLs: $373M (up 49% YoY), NPA ratio 1.08%
- C&I non-accruals: $187M (doubled from $93M)
- Net charge-offs: 0.30% (doubled from 0.16%)
- ACL/NPL ratio: 59% (down from 89%) — thin coverage
- CRE office exposure: $1.4B with $468M (33%) maturing within 12 months
- Downside scenario would require $111M additional ACL (50% increase)

The credit deterioration is the single largest risk to the investment thesis. If the CRE office maturities trigger elevated charge-offs or if C&I credit quality continues deteriorating, provision expense could offset NIM expansion entirely. With long-term rates elevated, CRE refinancing risk has intensified — borrowers face rates substantially above their origination levels.

**Geographic Concentration:** Heavy Florida exposure subjects the bank to hurricane risk, insurance cost escalation (explicitly flagged as impacting borrower cash flows), and climate-related property value risk.

**Uninsured Deposits:** 52% uninsured, though management maintains 138% liquidity coverage. The 2023 banking crisis demonstrated how quickly confidence in deposit stability can erode for mid-size banks.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:** Currently benefits from higher rates (asset-sensitive), but $4.68B in derivatives (mostly maturing 2026) creates transition risk. A rapid 200bps decline would reduce NII 4.7% in Year 1.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is signaling elevated near-term stress. Near-term IV of 54.3% vs. historical volatility of 29.8% represents a 24.5% spread — substantially rich. The term structure is in backwardation (54.3% near-term vs. 32.1% at 6 months), which typically indicates the market is pricing an imminent catalyst or heightened uncertainty. Put skew of 16.5% confirms elevated demand for downside protection.

This signal is consistent with the post-earnings-miss environment and credit quality concerns. It suggests institutional investors are hedging against further credit deterioration disclosure in coming weeks (potentially related to CRE marks or stress tests). The 7.9% implied near-term move (~$3.70) indicates the market sees meaningful binary risk around next data points.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($52-56, 11-19% upside):**
- NIM reaches 3.20% by year-end 2026 as guided, driving EPS toward $4.50+
- Credit quality stabilizes — Q1 miss proves seasonal, NPL formation decelerates
- ROE expands to 10-11%, supporting 1.3-1.4x TBV re-rating
- $200M buyback reduces share count by 3-4% annually
- Potential M&A premium if approached by larger acquirer

**Bear Case ($37-40, 15-21% downside):**
- CRE office maturities generate elevated charge-offs ($50-100M incremental provision)
- Credit deterioration spreads beyond office to broader C&I
- NIM expansion stalls as deposit competition intensifies or rate cuts compress loan yields
- Florida economic slowdown (hurricane, insurance costs, tariff impacts)
- Stock reverts to 1.0x TBV (~$40) or lower P/E reflecting elevated credit costs

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q2 2026 earnings beat with NPL formation decelerating (NPLs flat or declining QoQ)
2. Pullback to $40-42 (1.0-1.05x TBV), providing margin of safety against credit risk
3. NIM demonstrably trending toward 3.20% guidance in reported quarterly data

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. NPLs exceed $450M or NCOs exceed 0.40% in any quarter
2. ACL/NPL ratio drops below 50% without corresponding collateral improvement evidence
3. CRE office losses exceed $50M in a single quarter
4. Management abandons or materially reduces NIM guidance

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (~July 2026) is the next natural assessment point. Credit quality trajectory over Q2-Q3 2026 will determine whether the transformation thesis dominates or the credit cycle overwhelms it.

## Conclusion

BankUnited is executing a sound balance sheet transformation strategy that is delivering measurable results — NIM expansion, funding cost reduction, core deposit growth, and improving profitability. The strategic direction is clear and the near-term earnings trajectory (consensus $4.12 FY2026, $4.57 FY2027) reflects continued progress.

However, three factors prevent a BUY recommendation at current levels: (1) the stock has already appreciated 37% over the past year, pricing in much of the transformation thesis; (2) credit quality is deteriorating meaningfully, with NPLs up 49% and coverage ratios thinning, creating a genuine risk that provision expense absorbs NIM gains; and (3) the macro environment (elevated long-term rates, CRE refinancing stress, potential recession) represents a significant headwind for a bank with $1.4B in CRE office exposure and heavy Florida concentration.

The stock is fairly valued at approximately 1.17x TBV for a 9% ROE bank with improving trajectory. I would become more constructive either at a lower price ($40-42) that provides margin of safety for credit losses, or after evidence that credit quality has stabilized. Until then, the balanced risk/reward profile warrants a HOLD with a growth thesis contingent on continued NIM expansion and credit stabilization.
