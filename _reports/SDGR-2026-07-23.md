**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Schrödinger sells a physics-based computational chemistry platform (FEP+/LiveDesign) to essentially every large pharmaceutical company and runs a parallel drug-discovery operation that generates revenue three ways: collaboration milestones, equity stakes in co-founded biotechs, and (until recently) a proprietary clinical pipeline. The company reported $255.9M of FY2025 revenue (+23% YoY) with $406.4M of net cash and no funded debt. The growth is not coming from the part of the business the market pays for. Q1 2026 total revenue fell 2% YoY, and software revenue (78% of the business) declined 21% as on-premise license recognition (front-loaded) gives way to ratable hosted contracts. Management explicitly warns reported revenue may decline in the near term. Net dollar retention has slipped to roughly 100% from 113% a year prior, and the count of >$1M ACV customers declined.

The investment case has narrowed. In 2025 Schrödinger discontinued SGR-2921 after two patient deaths the drug was considered to have contributed to, and it is exiting independent clinical development: finishing the Phase 1 trials of SGR-1505 and SGR-3515 and then partnering or out-licensing. That de-risks cash burn (a ~7% headcount reduction and clinical wind-down target ~$70M of savings) but caps the blue-sky "we own an approved drug" scenario. What remains is a platform-plus-royalty engine whose reported profitability is dominated by non-operating items: the 2023 net profit was a Nimbus/Takeda monetization, and 2025's improved net loss partly reflects Structure Therapeutics mark-to-market gains. The underlying operations still burn cash; Q1 2026 operating cash flow was –$14.8M once the January 2025 Novartis upfront washes out of the comparison.

The stock is down roughly 34% over the trailing twelve months to ~$15, near the middle of its $10.95–$23.75 range, and short interest sits at 25.1% of float. We rate SDGR HOLD. The balance sheet removes solvency risk, the Ajax/Lilly transaction validates the equity-stake flywheel, and pipeline readouts have been clean, but consensus estimates are falling across every window, retention is softening, and the core software line is contracting. A fundamental investor cannot underwrite a directional edge here with confidence, and the setup is better characterized as a binary-event stock than a compounder.

## Company Overview

Schrödinger operates two reporting segments. **Software** (~$199.5M, 78% of FY2025 revenue) licenses computational chemistry and enterprise informatics tools to biopharma, materials-science, academic, and government customers. All top-20 pharma companies are customers. Revenue is a mix of on-premise licenses, hosted subscriptions, maintenance, and professional services, and the company is in the middle of a deliberate shift from on-premise (recognized largely upfront) to hosted (recognized ratably). **Drug Discovery** (~$56.4M, 22%, +107% YoY on the Novartis collaboration that began November 2024) comprises collaboration research and milestone revenue (BMS, Lilly, Novartis, Otsuka), equity stakes in co-founded biotechs (Nimbus, Ajax, Structure Therapeutics), and a proprietary pipeline now being wound down to Phase 1 completion.

The differentiation thesis is that physics-first methods augmented by machine learning generalize better and more accurately than pure-AI/ML approaches, and that industry-wide adoption of the platform validates that claim. The counter-risk is direct: if AI-native drug-discovery methods prove good enough, both platform demand and Schrödinger's value as a collaboration partner erode.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and mix.** Software grew only 11%, while Drug Discovery more than doubled on Novartis collaboration services. The Q1 2026 print is the more important signal: on-premise revenue dropped 44% (–$11.2M) while hosted rose 11%. Software gross margin compressed to 69% from 80% on higher cloud costs. Annual contract value grew to $28.4M from $25.4M, which is the metric management wants investors to track through the transition, and it guides 10–15% annual ACV growth through 2028.

**Profitability.** FY2025 net loss narrowed to –$103.3M from –$187.1M, with operating loss around –$167M. Consolidated results are heavily distorted by Structure Therapeutics mark-to-market swings and lumpy collaboration/equity monetizations: 2023 posted +$40.7M net income entirely on the Nimbus/Takeda deal, and Q1 2026 carried a $13.5M unrealized loss on the Structure stake. Trailing EPS is –$1.41 and the forward P/E is meaningless at –10.7x. Any valuation work has to separate the cash-burning operating business from the episodic equity/milestone optionality.

**Balance sheet and liquidity.** Cash, equivalents, restricted cash and marketable securities totaled $406.4M at 3/31/2026, with management guiding to >24 months of runway and no funded debt. The Altman Z-Score reads "distress" (~1.4), but that is a false signal driven by negative EBIT and a $155.8M NYC HQ operating-lease liability running through December 2037, not by leverage. Deferred revenue of $162.1M is inflated by the $150M Novartis upfront. Accumulated deficit is –$688.8M, with ~$208.5M of federal NOLs. A new $82M three-year cloud-compute commitment (December 2025) adds a real fixed cost as the business shifts to hosted delivery.

**Cash-flow quality.** FY2025 operating cash flow was roughly +$14M, but that is almost entirely the Novartis working-capital swing. Q1 2026 operating cash flow of –$14.8M reveals the underlying burn. This is a structurally cash-consuming operating business bridged by a strong balance sheet and periodic monetizations.

**Earnings track record and estimate revisions.** The quarterly beat/miss record is noisy: three beats and one miss over four quarters, but the beats (+339.7% in Q4 2025, +37.6% in Q3, +24.7% in Q2) reflect equity gains and monetizations rather than operating strength, and the most recent quarter missed by 25.2%. Consensus estimates are falling across every window: current-year EPS revised –11.7% over 90 days and next-year –20.3%. This is negative earnings momentum, the opposite of the estimate-revision signal that would support a re-rating.

## Growth Analysis

The headline 23% FY2025 revenue growth overstates the durable trajectory. Strip out the Novartis-driven Drug Discovery doubling and the core software business grew 11%, and that figure is now going backwards on a reported basis (–21% in Q1 2026) during the hosted transition. Management's own guidance frames the near term as one where reported revenue may decline while ACV compounds at 10–15%. That is a coherent story if you believe the transition mechanically depresses recognized revenue while the underlying book of business grows, but two facts complicate it: net dollar retention fell to ~100% from 113%, and the >$1M ACV customer count slipped. Ratable-revenue models are supposed to show durable expansion; retention at 100% in a pressured life-sciences funding environment suggests demand softness beyond an accounting artifact.

The genuine growth optionality sits in the equity-stake and milestone portfolio. Potential milestones total up to $2.272B (Novartis), $482M (BMS remaining), and $420M (Lilly), but none of it is recognized, all of it is collaborator-controlled, and it is years away if it materializes at all. The Ajax/Lilly transaction (April 2026, up to $2.3B; Schrödinger holds ~5.8% as co-founder) is the clearest recent proof the flywheel works, echoing Nimbus/Takeda (~$147M, 2023) and Morphic/Lilly (~$47.6M, 2024). But these are lumpy, unpredictable, and make earnings episodic rather than compounding. R&D investment continues to fund the platform and residual pipeline, and Phase 1 data has been supportive (SGR-1505 Fast Track for Waldenström; SGR-3515 stable disease in 64.7% of evaluable patients ≥100mg, no Hy's Law cases), but the strategic pivot means these become out-licensing assets, not owned products.

Growth prospects score 4.5. The reliable core is contracting in reported terms with softening retention, and the exciting optionality is unforecastable and non-recurring.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$15 and a $993M market cap, SDGR is difficult to value on earnings because it has none, and the –10.7x forward P/E is a non-signal. Backing out $406M net cash leaves an enterprise value near $587M against ~$256M of revenue, or ~2.3x EV/sales, which is not obviously cheap for a business whose 78% software segment is shrinking on a reported basis at negative operating margins. Peer-group P/E medians (17.2x, dragged by profitable services names like ADUS and MD) are not comparable, since SDGR is a loss-making platform, not a services operator.

The equity-lessons evidence here is directly relevant: a "cheap"-looking multiple has been reliably anti-predictive, and cheapness by itself is a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal. SDGR is not even cheap on operating fundamentals; the value case rests on optionality (unrecognized milestones, equity stakes, a possible Ajax cash event) that is impossible to size with a defensible range. Analyst targets span $13 to $30 with a $20.6 mean and $20 median, which brackets almost the entire plausible outcome set and offers little precision. Because the estimate-revision trend is negative and the stock is a loss-maker with contracting core revenue, we do not treat consensus targets as a floor here; the "targets are floors" logic applies to inflection stocks with rising estimates and reasonable multiples, which SDGR is not.

Fair value depends almost entirely on which scenario you underwrite. On the operating business alone, the current price looks full given the burn and revenue decline. Layering in a probability-weighted monetization stream justifies something around the current price but not a clear discount. Valuation scores 4.5: no identifiable mispricing with a catalyst that a fundamental investor can reliably capture, and the "value" is really an unhedgeable bet on lumpy events.

## Competitive Landscape

Schrödinger's software moat is real but under pressure from two directions. On the incumbent side it competes with BIOVIA (Dassault), Certara, Dotmatics, Chemical Computing Group, Cresset, and Simulations Plus. On the disruptive side, a growing wave of AI/ML drug-discovery entrants (including XtalPi and others) threatens the premise that physics-based methods are structurally superior. Management's thesis that physics plus ML generalizes better than pure AI is credible and supported by industry-wide adoption, but it is precisely the kind of claim that erodes quietly if AI approaches improve. The world model's broader read is that AI is displacing application-layer and seat-based software; Schrödinger sits closer to specialized infrastructure than commodity SaaS, which offers some insulation, but the retention slip to 100% and declining >$1M customer count are the numbers to watch.

In the pipeline, MALT1 competitors include AbbVie, HotSpot, and Recursion; Wee1/Myt1 competitors include Zentalis, Debiopharm, and Acrivon. The strategic retreat from clinical development means these are increasingly out-licensing assets whose competitive standing matters mainly to partner economics rather than to SDGR directly.

A structural industry risk flagged in the filings deserves weight: the IRA's small-molecule vs. biologics disparity (9-year vs. 13-year negotiation timelines). Schrödinger's platform is strongest in small molecules; a durable industry shift toward biologics would pressure both program value and software demand. Competitive position scores 5.0: a differentiated, widely adopted platform whose trajectory is stable-to-softening rather than clearly improving.

## Risk Assessment

**Hosted-transition revenue headwind (near-term, concrete).** Reported software revenue is declining as recognition shifts from upfront to ratable. Management flags this explicitly in both the 10-K and Q1 10-Q. The risk is that softness attributed to accounting is partly genuine demand weakness, which the retention data supports.

**Retention deterioration.** Net dollar retention at ~100% and a lower >$1M ACV customer count in a pressured life-sciences funding environment raise renewal and expansion risk.

**Milestone/royalty revenue is entirely unrecognized.** Contractual maximums are collaborator-controlled and years out.

**Clinical safety and strategic retreat.** The August 2025 discontinuation of SGR-2921 after two contributory deaths, and the exit from independent development, narrow the upside and are a reminder that platform validation in silico does not eliminate clinical risk.

**Equity-stake volatility.** Structure Therapeutics mark-to-market swings inject non-operational earnings noise; carrying value of the remaining stake is ~$32.8M after a ~$20.3M Q1 monetization.

**Customer concentration.** Two customers were 40% of Q1 2026 revenue (22% + 18%).

**Governance.** Insiders and >5% holders control ~39–47% of votes, with a classified board and anti-takeover provisions. Multiple director/officer change 8-Ks across 2025–2026 coincide with the restructuring and warrant monitoring; a prior FY2022 material weakness was remediated by end of 2023.

Risk factors score 4.5. Several specific, not-fully-priced downside catalysts exist (continued software decline, retention deterioration, milestone slippage), partly offset by the fortress balance sheet.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 77.8% versus 51.9% one-year historical volatility, rich by ~26 points, at the 100th percentile of the current chain, with a ±17.7% implied move on the nearest expiry and a backwardated term structure (77.8% near vs 64.5% at six months). Put/call skew is elevated at 5.5% near-term, reflecting hedging demand. Combined with 25.1% short interest and 6.8 days to cover, the setup reads as a market bracing for a binary event or continued fundamental deterioration rather than expressing directional conviction. The rich IV argues against buying premium here and cautions against sizing any position as though volatility is cheap.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$24–30).** The hosted transition proves to be an accounting drag that reverses as ratable revenue compounds; ACV grows at the guided 10–15% and software re-accelerates on a reported basis in 2027. The Ajax/Lilly transaction closes and delivers a material cash monetization, and one or more of the SGR-1505/SGR-3515 assets is out-licensed on attractive terms, re-validating the equity-stake flywheel. Retention stabilizes, the $70M cost program lifts the path to operating breakeven, and the market re-rates the platform toward a growth multiple. Short covering (25% of float) amplifies the move.

**Bear case (~$10–11).** Software revenue keeps declining, retention drifts below 100%, and the transition reveals genuine demand erosion from AI competition and life-sciences funding pressure. Milestones remain unrecognized, the Structure stake marks lower, and operating burn persists at ~$15M+ per quarter, drawing down the cash cushion. The equity model proves too lumpy to underwrite, estimates keep falling, and the stock retests its 52-week low near $11.

The distribution is genuinely two-sided, with the tails driven by events (Ajax closing, milestone recognition, out-licensing) that are hard to handicap. The base case is a stock that grinds in the $13–18 range while the transition plays out and the market waits for a monetization catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This is not a growth hold, since the core business is contracting in reported terms and estimates are falling, nor an income hold (no dividend). It is a hold because the balance sheet removes solvency risk and the optionality is real but unquantifiable, leaving no defensible directional edge.

**Upgrade to BUY if:** (1) software revenue returns to positive YoY growth on a reported basis for two consecutive quarters and net dollar retention stabilizes above 105%; (2) the Ajax/Lilly transaction closes with a disclosed, material cash monetization to Schrödinger; or (3) estimate revisions turn positive (current-year and next-year EPS revised upward) alongside confirmed ACV growth at or above the 10–15% guide.

**Downgrade to AVOID if:** (1) software revenue decline accelerates beyond the –21% Q1 pace with retention falling below 95%; (2) operating cash burn worsens toward ~$20M+ per quarter, compressing the runway below 18 months without a monetization; or (3) a Novartis/BMS/Lilly collaboration is terminated or materially descoped, removing a milestone leg.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (August 2026) is the natural checkpoint for software-transition trajectory, ACV, retention, and any Ajax-closing update. The near-term options backwardation implies the market expects a move around that window.

## Conclusion

Schrödinger is a well-capitalized, unlevered platform company with a genuinely differentiated technology and a self-reinforcing equity-stake model that has produced periodic large monetizations. But the segment the market pays for (software, 78% of revenue) is contracting on a reported basis, retention has weakened to ~100%, and consensus estimates are falling across every window. The excitement (Ajax/Lilly, Novartis milestones, pipeline out-licensing) is real but lumpy, collaborator-controlled, and impossible to size with confidence. The stock's ~34% trailing decline reflects those crosscurrents rather than a clean mispricing; at ~2.3x EV/sales on a shrinking, cash-burning core, it is neither cheap on operations nor obviously expensive against its optionality.

The trajectory has improved from prior reviews (AVOID → HOLD, driven by the strengthened balance sheet, cost discipline, and Ajax validation), and we maintain HOLD. The bar for BUY should stay high given three prior looks and a still-uncertain thesis: we need to see the core software line inflect positive or a material monetization land before underwriting appreciation. Until then, the short interest and rich near-term IV signal a market positioned for a catalyst, not a compounding story. We would revisit on the August print.
