## Executive Summary

Goodyear Tire & Rubber is a highly leveraged legacy manufacturer executing a multi-year turnaround ("Goodyear Forward") while simultaneously facing intensifying headwinds from tariffs (~$300M annualized), declining unit volumes, lower-tier import competition, and a critical labor contract expiration in July 2026. The company posted a GAAP net loss of $(1,721M) in 2025, driven by a $1.4B valuation allowance on U.S. deferred tax assets and $674M in goodwill impairment — signals that management itself lacks confidence in near-term domestic profitability.

At $7.03 per share (~$2.0B market cap), the stock trades at 0.63x book value and 0.11x revenue, reflecting the market's view that the $6.2B debt load (4.58x D/E) may prevent equity holders from capturing turnaround value. The Altman Z-Score of 1.36 places the company firmly in the distress zone. While $2.2B in divestiture proceeds have been used to reduce debt and the company maintains adequate liquidity ($5.2B including unused credit), 2026 represents the critical year with $1.17B in debt maturities, peak rationalization payments, and multiple binary risks (labor, tariffs, refinancing) that could each individually destabilize the business.

The risk-reward is unattractive: upside requires flawless execution across all turnaround vectors simultaneously, while downside scenarios are numerous and concentrated in the near term. Tepper has already reduced exposure by 50%.

## Company Overview

Goodyear is the world's third-largest tire manufacturer behind Bridgestone and Michelin, operating 49 facilities across 19 countries. The company generates approximately $18.3B in annual revenue through three segments: Americas (59%), EMEA (30%), and Asia Pacific (11%). Following the 2021 Cooper Tire acquisition, Goodyear operates two primary brands: Goodyear (premium) and Cooper (mid-tier).

The "Goodyear Forward" transformation plan, largely completed in 2025, involved divesting three non-core businesses for over $2.2B in gross proceeds (OTR tires to Yokohama for $905M, Dunlop brand to Sumitomo for $526M, chemicals business for $650M), closing high-cost manufacturing facilities in Germany, South Africa, the UK, and Virginia, and targeting $1.5B in annual run-rate cost savings.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory is negative:**
- 2022: $20.8B → 2023: $20.1B → 2024: $18.9B → 2025: $18.3B
- Total replacement tire units fell 6.3% globally in 2025; Americas replacement volume declined 4.9%
- Divestitures account for a portion of the decline, but organic volume losses are real

**Profitability is severely impaired:**
- Segment operating income: $1,057M (2025), down 18.8% from $1,302M (2024)
- Segment operating margin: 5.8% (2025) vs. 6.9% (2024)
- GAAP net loss: $(1,721M), or $(5.99)/share
- The loss includes $1.4B valuation allowance, $674M goodwill impairment, and $201M pension charges, partially offset by $816M in asset sale gains

**Balance sheet is under severe strain:**
- Total debt: ~$6.2B (including finance leases)
- Debt-to-equity: 4.58x
- Interest expense: $445M (2025); guided to $400-425M (2026)
- Altman Z-Score: 1.36 (distress zone, below 1.81 threshold)
- Net debt reduction of $1.76B in 2025 from divestiture proceeds

**Liquidity provides a buffer, not a solution:**
- Cash: $801M + $4,421M unused credit facility = $5.2B total liquidity
- Operating cash flow: $796M (includes $486M of deferred revenue from divestitures — a temporary boost)
- Capex: $826M (capital-intensive maintenance requirements)
- 2026 debt maturities: $1.17B; 2027: $1.03B

**Free cash flow generation is questionable:**
- Adjusted for the $486M deferred revenue benefit, underlying operating cash flow was approximately $310M
- Against $826M capex, the company is FCF negative on an organic basis
- Rationalization payments of ~$225M in 2026 add further cash drain

**2026 puts and takes:**
- Goodyear Forward savings: +$300M
- Raw material benefit: +$300M
- Tariff headwind: -$300M
- Lost divestiture income: -$185M
- Net operational improvement: approximately +$115M before volume and pricing effects

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are limited. Revenue has declined each year since 2022. Management provides "limited visibility to global tire unit volumes for 2026" due to macroeconomic and regulatory uncertainty.

Structural headwinds include:
1. **Import competition** in the replacement tire market, where lower-cost Asian producers continue gaining share
2. **EV transition** creating longer tire replacement cycles (less wear) though partially offset by larger/more expensive tire sizes
3. **Vehicle miles traveled** declining in some markets post-pandemic
4. **Tariff-induced demand destruction** as higher costs get passed to consumers

The Goodyear Forward plan's $1.5B in annualized savings is meaningful but largely complete — the incremental benefit in 2026 is $300M, which is exactly offset by tariff costs. There is no revenue growth catalyst visible in the current environment.

The premium/large-rim-diameter tire strategy is sound in theory (higher ASPs, better margins), but execution requires successfully closing plants, retraining workforce, and winning OE fitments while defending market share against well-capitalized competitors (Michelin, Continental) pursuing identical strategies.

## Valuation Assessment

At $7.03/share:
- Market cap: ~$2.0B against $18.3B revenue (0.11x sales)
- P/B: 0.63x ($11.20 book value per share)
- Forward P/E: Negative (consensus expects losses to continue)
- EV: approximately $8.2B ($2.0B equity + $6.2B debt)
- EV/Revenue: 0.45x
- EV/Segment EBIT: approximately 7.8x ($1,057M)

The stock is optically cheap on revenue and book multiples, but this is a classic value trap configuration for highly leveraged companies. The equity is effectively a deeply out-of-the-money call option on the turnaround succeeding while creditors maintain confidence. Book value has deteriorated from $18.60/share in 2022 to $11.20 today, and further impairments are possible (Cooper intangibles have fair values that only "approximate" carrying values).

Analyst consensus target is $8.73 (median $9.00), with a range of $6.60-$10.00. The distribution (1 Strong Buy, 3 Buy, 5 Hold, 1 Sell) reflects appropriate skepticism. Relative to peers, the negative P/E and 0x dividend yield compare unfavorably to the sector median P/E of 20.2x and 0-1.8% yields.

The valuation does not offer sufficient margin of safety given the leverage and execution risk. A successful turnaround could justify $10-12, but a failed one (labor disruption, tariff escalation, volume collapse) could take the equity to $2-4 given the debt overhang.

## Competitive Landscape

Goodyear occupies a challenged position in the global tire industry:

**Strengths:**
- #3 global scale provides procurement and distribution advantages
- Goodyear brand recognition in premium tier
- Cooper brand provides mid-tier presence
- TireHub JV offers distribution control (though it's losing money)

**Weaknesses:**
- Management explicitly acknowledges being "less cost-competitive" than Bridgestone and Michelin
- Manufacturing footprint still includes high-cost facilities during transition
- Cooper brand facing impairment risk from import competition in its core mid-tier segment
- Limited pricing power in replacement market where consumers increasingly choose lower-tier options

**Competitive trajectory: deteriorating.** Market share losses in Americas replacement (4.9% unit decline vs. industry) and specific acknowledgment of lower-tier import pressure suggest the competitive moat is eroding from below. The premium strategy may succeed long-term, but the mid-tier position (Cooper) is under direct structural assault.

## Risk Assessment

**Near-term concentrated risks (2026):**
1. **USW labor contract (July 2026):** ~4,300 U.S. associates + ~19,000 international workers with contracts expiring in 2026. A strike during this critical transition year could be fatal to the turnaround narrative.
2. **$1.17B debt maturity wall:** Must refinance or repay in a high-rate environment. While credit availability exists, terms may tighten if operational results disappoint.
3. **Tariff escalation:** Current $300M estimate assumes existing rates. Escalation could add materially to costs given the geopolitical environment.
4. **IRS $1.5B proposed adjustment:** Contingent liability that could eliminate $315M in DTA utility if lost.
5. **Quarterly earnings track record:** 3 misses in 4 quarters, including a -346% miss in Q2 2025 and -20% miss in Q4 2025. Limited visibility into near-term profitability.

**Structural risks:**
- Off-balance sheet financing ($551M supplier programs + $892M factored receivables) adds hidden leverage
- Pension actuarial losses of $1,951M remain on balance sheet
- TireHub JV is losing money ($28M equity loss in 9 months)
- Cooper intangible assets ($435M) at risk of further impairment

### Options Market Signal

The term structure is in steep backwardation (89.1% near-term vs. 52.4% at 6-month), characteristic of markets expecting an imminent binary event or material deterioration. Near-term IV of 89.1% vs. historical vol of 46.5% represents an extreme 42.7pp richness. This divergence, combined with 12.8% short interest and 3.6 days to cover, confirms institutional bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($9.50-$11.00, +35-56%)
- Goodyear Forward delivers on remaining $300M savings in 2026
- Raw material tailwinds offset tariff costs
- USW contract renews without disruption
- Premium/large-rim strategy gains traction, stabilizing margins at 7-8%
- Debt reduction continues, leverage falls below 3.5x by 2027
- Stock re-rates toward 1.0x book value

### Bear Case ($3.00-$5.00, -30-57%)
- Labor disruption in July 2026 halts production at U.S. plants
- Tariffs escalate beyond $300M; volumes decline further
- Refinancing of 2026 maturities requires punitive terms
- Further goodwill/intangible impairments erode book value
- Covenant breach triggers credit facility restrictions

The bear case has more identifiable pathways (5+ independent triggers) with concentrated timing (Q2-Q3 2026), while the bull case requires simultaneous absence of problems across all vectors.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** USW labor contract expiration in July 2026, combined with Q2 2026 earnings (likely May/early June 2026). If labor negotiations deteriorate or another earnings miss occurs, the stock could break through the $6.14 52-week low. Any tariff escalation announcement would create immediate downside.

**Timing window:** 2-4 months. The labor contract is the most defined catalyst (July 2026). Q2 earnings provide an interim check.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 12.8% with 3.6 days to cover is elevated but not at squeeze-risk levels (typically >25%). Institutional ownership provides lending supply. No meaningful call OI buildup, no meme characteristics, no imminent positive catalyst.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options confirm the bearish thesis with steep backwardation. The P/C OI ratio of 0.60 (slightly call-heavy) suggests some speculative long positioning exists that could unwind. Cost-to-borrow is likely elevated given SI%, reducing but not eliminating short attractiveness.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Target $5.00-5.50 on labor disruption or earnings miss (25-30% downside). Upside risk to $9.00 on positive USW resolution + earnings beat (28% upside). Risk/reward approximately 1.0:1 to 1.2:1, falling below the 2:1 threshold.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE** — The thesis is correct (deteriorating fundamentals, concentrated risk), but the risk/reward is below 2:1 with the stock already 42% off its 52-week high and near multi-year lows. The 89.1% IV means put options are expensive, reducing the attractiveness of defined-risk bearish expression. This is a name to avoid owning, not actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:**
1. Successful USW contract renewal without material cost escalation (would remove the largest binary risk)
2. Two consecutive quarters of positive EPS and segment margins above 7%
3. Net leverage (debt/EBITDA) below 4.0x with clear trajectory to 3.0x
4. Stock trades below $5.00 (at that point, risk/reward may become favorable as a turnaround option)

**Timeline:** Headwinds persist through at least Q3 2026 (labor negotiation, tariff impacts, rationalization payments peak). Earliest reassessment: post-Q3 2026 earnings (approximately November 2026) if USW contract resolves favorably.

## Conclusion

Goodyear is attempting to execute a complex turnaround while carrying dangerous leverage and facing multiple simultaneous headwinds. The Goodyear Forward plan has rational strategic logic — divest non-core assets, close high-cost plants, focus on premium products — but the financial execution window is extremely narrow. The $300M in tariff costs essentially zeroed out the 2026 incremental savings. Management's decision to book a full valuation allowance on U.S. deferred tax assets is the most telling signal: the people with the best information about this business cannot forecast with confidence when it will be profitable domestically.

At $7.03, the stock is priced for distress, and the distress indicators (Z-Score 1.36, 4.58x D/E, 3 of 4 earnings misses, options in steep backwardation) confirm that pricing is appropriate rather than creating a mispricing opportunity. Tepper's 50% position reduction in Q4 2025 reinforces that even deep-value investors who saw turnaround potential are losing conviction.

The number of things that must go right simultaneously (labor, tariffs, volumes, refinancing, execution) against the number of things that can go wrong independently makes this an unfavorable bet at current prices. Avoid.
