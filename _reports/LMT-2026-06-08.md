**Research Date: June 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Lockheed Martin is the world's largest pure-play defense prime, with FY2025 revenue of $75.0B, a record $193.6B backlog, and irreplaceable franchises in tactical aircraft (F-35), missile defense (PAC-3, THAAD), and submarine/naval combat systems. The secular demand environment is as strong as it has been in a decade: regional conflicts on three fronts, stockpile replenishment, and an Administration FY2027 budget request of $1.5T with $760B for procurement/modernization that maps directly onto LMT's core programs. The demand bull case is real.

The problem is that demand is not flowing cleanly to the bottom line. Net income has fallen for two consecutive years ($6.92B in 2023 → $5.34B in 2024 → $5.02B in 2025), driven almost entirely by reach-forward losses on a widening set of fixed-price development programs: ~$1.8B cumulative on an Aeronautics classified program, ~$1.46B on an MFC classified program, ~$670M on the Canadian Maritime Helicopter Program, ~$130M on Türkiye TUHP, plus fresh Q1 2026 charges on F-16 ($125M) and C-130 ($85M). This is a recurring estimation/execution problem across at least five distinct programs, not a one-off. Q1 2026 marked a clear negative turn: operating cash flow collapsed to $220M from $1.41B, free cash flow turned negative at –$291M, the company halted buybacks (zero vs. $750M in Q1 2025), and a securities class action now explicitly ties the classified-program losses to alleged disclosure failures.

We rate LMT a HOLD. The stock has fallen ~25% from its $692 high to ~$520, and at ~17.4x consensus FY2026 EPS of $29.89 it is no longer expensive against history. But we cannot construct a confident 12-month appreciation thesis when EPS is declining, estimates are flat-to-negative, free cash flow just went negative, and management has suspended repurchases. The holding case is the 2.6% dividend backed by genuine underlying cash generation ($6.9B FY2025 FCF) and downside support from the record backlog, not a re-rating catalyst. We would upgrade on confirmed margin stabilization and would downgrade if the classified-program losses recur in 2026.

## Company Overview

Lockheed Martin operates four segments:

- **Aeronautics ($30.3B, 40% of sales):** Dominated by the F-35 Lightning II, which is 27% of total company revenue and 67% of the segment. Also F-16, F-22, C-130, and classified Skunk Works development. 1,293 production F-35s delivered through 2025; backlog of 368 aircraft; Lots 18/19 (296 aircraft) definitized in September 2025.
- **Missiles & Fire Control ($14.5B, 19%):** Highest-margin segment (13.8% in 2025). PAC-3, THAAD, JASSM, LRASM, GMLRS, PrSM, HIMARS, Javelin, hypersonics. Backlog rose to $46.7B from $38.8B on munitions-replenishment demand.
- **Rotary & Mission Systems ($17.3B, 23%):** Sikorsky (Black Hawk, Seahawk, CH-53K), Aegis, radars, combat systems. Restructured into SEMS and MIC2 effective January 2026.
- **Space ($13.0B, 17%):** Trident II FBM, Next Gen OPIR, NGI, Orion, GPS III, SDA satellites, plus a ~50% equity stake in United Launch Alliance.

Roughly 72% of revenue comes from the U.S. Government (63% DoD), with 28% international (most of which is Foreign Military Sales routed through the U.S. Government). Total backlog declined to $186.4B by Q1 2026. Primary competitors are Boeing, General Dynamics, L3Harris, Northrop Grumman, and RTX.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue growth is solid but profitability is deteriorating.

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $66.0B | $67.6B | $71.0B | $75.0B |
| Operating profit | $8.35B | $8.51B | $7.01B | $7.73B |
| Net income | $5.73B | $6.92B | $5.34B | $5.02B |
| Diluted EPS | — | $27.55 | $22.31 | $21.49 |
| Free cash flow | — | $6.23B | $5.29B | $6.91B |

Revenue grew 5.6% in 2025, but net income fell ~28% from the 2023 peak and EPS declined for two straight years. The cause is reach-forward losses (recognized in full when a contract's estimated total cost exceeds its price), not weak demand. FY2025 FCF looks healthy but was flattered by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (immediate R&D/capex expensing reduced cash taxes) and working-capital timing, rather than operational improvement.

Q1 2026 sales were flat at $18.0B, net earnings fell to $1.49B ($6.44 EPS) from $1.71B ($7.28), and cash fell from $4.1B to $1.9B. Operating margins compressed across three of four segments (Aeronautics 8.9%, RMS 10.6%, Space 8.2%). The company repaid $1.0B of debt.

On the balance sheet, the headline Altman Z-Score of 1.53 ("distress zone") is misleading. It is driven by thin book equity ($6.7B against $59.8B of assets), an artifact of years of aggressive buybacks (D/E ~7.9x, ROE 74.6%). This reflects financial engineering, not insolvency risk. Total debt is ~$21.7B against $8.6B FY2025 operating cash flow; the company remains investment-grade and in covenant compliance. The real concern is earnings quality and program execution, not solvency. The 2.6% dividend (~$3.2B paid in 2025) is comfortably covered by underlying cash generation even after the Q1 air pocket.

## Growth Analysis

The demand side is genuinely strong. MFC backlog surged ~$8B on munitions replenishment, and the FY2027 request (including increased F-35 purchases, Golden Dome missile defense, and PAC-3/THAAD ramp) maps onto LMT's core franchises. If enacted, this supports mid-single-digit top-line growth and a multi-year tail of munitions demand.

The conversion to earnings is where the growth thesis breaks down. Consensus EPS estimates are flat-to-slightly-negative: current year $29.89 (+0.1% over 90 days), next year $32.11 (+0.5%), current quarter $7.19 (–0.2%). There is no positive estimate-revision momentum to underpin a growth re-rating. The positive-revision signal that would flag a buy is absent — estimates are stable at best, and the recent earnings record shows a Q1 2026 miss (–5.2%) breaking a three-quarter beat streak. Revenue growth of ~5-6% is respectable but not the kind of acceleration that, on its own, drives equity appreciation, particularly when each new fixed-price program carries reach-forward-loss risk that can erase the operating leverage. The FY2027 budget upside is also contingent on a second reconciliation bill passing Congress, so it is a probabilistic tailwind, not a committed one.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$520, LMT trades at ~17.4x consensus FY2026 EPS and ~16.2x FY2027 $32.11. That is a discount to the defense-and-aerospace peer median P/E of 38.7x, but the peer set is heterogeneous and skewed by high-multiple names (HWM 58.5x, BA 86.3x, TDG 38.7x). Against the cleaner pure-play primes, the comparison is less flattering: General Dynamics trades at 21.8x with cleaner execution, and Northrop at 17.0x after a deeper drawdown. LMT's discount to GD reflects two years of declining net income, recurring program losses, negative Q1 FCF, and a fresh securities suit.

The cycle-adjusted argument does not rescue the multiple here. This is not a trough-earnings setup where trailing P/E understates normalized power; estimates are flat, and the earnings decline is structural (chronic fixed-price losses), not cyclical. The 5-year cumulative TSR of 156 badly trails the peer group's 230, confirming the market has been pricing execution risk over demand for some time.

The dividend provides absolute-yield support and a partial floor. On the upside, if program losses cease and the FY2027 budget converts, a re-rating toward 20x on $32 of EPS implies the low-$600s, consistent with the $625 mean analyst target. But that requires demonstrated stabilization that has not yet appeared. At current levels the valuation is fair, not asymmetrically cheap; the discount is earned.

## Competitive Landscape

LMT's competitive position is strong in absolute terms (sole or duopoly producer in F-35-class tactical aircraft, submarine-class combat systems, and key missile-defense interceptors) but its trajectory is mixed. The filings articulate the structural tension clearly: the DoD is shifting procurement toward fixed-price development contracts, OTAs, progress payments (rather than performance-based payments), and new entrants/startups — the contract structures generating LMT's reach-forward losses. The defense "supercycle" is real, but it is rewarding cleaner-execution and munitions-pure players over LMT's mix of legacy fixed-price program risk, which is why LMT has lagged the sector rally. F-35 concentration at 27% of revenue is both a moat and a single-point-of-failure exposure to budget, TR-3/Block 4 software, and sustainment-cost risk. We score competitive position moderately, reflecting an irreplaceable installed base offset by share-of-wallet pressure in the new-program environment.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Recurring reach-forward losses (the defining LMT-specific risk).** Across FY2024–Q1 2026, LMT booked losses on at least five distinct programs: Aeronautics classified (with management warning "we may need to record additional losses in future periods"), MFC classified, CMHP (with ~$605M of contract assets still at risk and a Canada restructuring tied to U.S.-Canada trade relations), TUHP (possible further $115M on export-license failure), and fresh F-16/C-130 charges. The breadth signals a systemic estimation problem.

**2. Securities/derivative litigation (new, Q1 2026).** A class action (filed 7/28/2025, S.D.N.Y., class period Jan 23, 2024–July 21, 2025) and a derivative suit allege the classified-program/F-35 disclosures were misstated and "revealed to be false" when the losses were announced, overlapping the period of management's optimistic commentary.

**3. U.S. budget and policy risk.** 72% U.S. Government dependence amid continuing-resolution and shutdown risk, plus an Executive Order that could restrict dividends/buybacks/executive comp for contractors failing performance requirements, implemented via a new DFARS clause. The shift to progress payments and fixed-price structures threatens cash-flow timing and margins.

**4. F-35 concentration** at 27% of revenue.

**5. Pension volatility.** A noncash $479M settlement charge in 2025 (from a $943M group-annuity transfer) drove non-service FAS pension expense to $874M (vs. $62M income in 2024); further de-risking could trigger additional charges.

**6. Supply chain / rare earths / tariffs** (~$485M cash-flow impact in 2025, mostly expected recoverable) and ULA development exposure (Vulcan), where equity earnings were "not significant" in 2025 (vs. $45M in 2024) and further support may be required.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing modest, near-dated caution rather than acute stress. Near-term ATM IV is 33.5% versus 27.0% one-year HV — rich by ~6.5pp, with the term structure in backwardation (33.5% near-term vs. 27.9% at six months), consistent with hedging into the near-dated catalysts. Skew is balanced (2.5% near-term, turning mildly negative at longer tenors), the put/call OI ratio is benign at 0.61, and short interest is negligible at 1.3% of float (1.6 days to cover). There is no informed bearish positioning crowding the name and no squeeze setup. The implied near-term move of ±2.8% indicates the market does not anticipate an imminent fundamental shock, which fits a HOLD posture: the risks here are slow-burn execution issues, not a binary event.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$620-660).** The FY2027 budget converts via reconciliation, F-35/PAC-3/THAAD/Golden Dome volumes ramp, the classified-program losses prove terminal rather than recurring, FCF normalizes back above $6.5B, buybacks resume against the $8.3B authorization, and the stock re-rates toward 20x on $32 FY2027 EPS. The record backlog underwrites the revenue.

**Bear Case (~$430-450, ~16% downside).** Reach-forward losses recur on the Aeronautics classified program (management has explicitly flagged this), FCF stays weak as the shift to progress payments and fixed-price structures compresses cash conversion, the securities suit overhangs sentiment, and the multiple compresses toward 14x trough earnings as the market concludes the supercycle is bypassing LMT's bottom line.

The base case sits between: a stabilizing but uninspiring year where demand offsets continued execution friction, EPS recovers modestly to consensus ~$29.90, and the stock trades in a $510-600 band anchored by the dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The holding rationale is the 2.6% dividend supported by genuine underlying cash generation, combined with downside support from a record backlog and a multiple that has already de-rated ~25% from its high. We do not see a confident 12-month appreciation catalyst given flat estimates, the Q1 FCF reversal, and recurring program risk, so this is not a growth hold.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters with no new reach-forward losses and FCF restored to a positive run-rate above ~$1.5B/quarter, confirming the execution problem has been contained.
- Resumption of buybacks against the $8.3B authorization, signaling management confidence in cash flow.
- FY2027 reconciliation bill passes with the F-35/missile-defense procurement increases intact, converting the budget tailwind from probabilistic to committed.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A new material reach-forward loss on the Aeronautics or MFC classified programs in any 2026 quarter, confirming the estimation problem is unresolved.
- FY2026 free cash flow tracking materially below the ~$6B level, or a dividend-coverage concern emerging.
- Adverse development in the securities class action, or expansion of the alleged misstatement period.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July), where FCF recovery and the absence/presence of new program charges will be decisive. Secondary checkpoint is Congressional action on the FY2027 reconciliation bill.

## Conclusion

Lockheed Martin presents an unusually clean bull/bear setup. The demand is real and record-breaking; the conversion to earnings and cash is broken. Two straight years of declining net income, a Q1 2026 free-cash-flow reversal, halted buybacks, a fifth-program reach-forward loss, and a fresh securities suit have left the market correctly skeptical that the defense supercycle is reaching LMT's bottom line. The ~25% drawdown and the ~17.4x forward multiple have removed the valuation premium, and the 2.6% dividend provides a floor, but there is no estimate-revision momentum or near-term catalyst to drive appreciation. We hold for the yield and the backlog-backed downside support while we wait for evidence that management has stabilized program execution. We would need clean Q2 results, restored FCF, and resumed buybacks before this becomes a buy; we would exit if the classified-program losses recur.
