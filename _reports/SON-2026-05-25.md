## Executive Summary

Sonoco Products is a $4.9 billion packaging manufacturer that has undergone radical portfolio transformation over the past 18 months, acquiring Eviosys (Europe's largest food can maker) for $3.8 billion, then divesting $2.5 billion in non-core assets to partially de-lever. The resulting business is a focused two-segment operation (Consumer Packaging ~65% revenue, Industrial Paper ~31%) generating approximately $1.3 billion in adjusted EBITDA across ~265 facilities in 37 countries.

At $49.37, the stock trades at 8.1x forward earnings and roughly 3.7-4.0x EV/EBITDA, a meaningful discount to packaging peers. The 4.4% dividend yield (43 consecutive annual increases, 100+ years of consecutive payments) provides a floor, and two senior executives making $740K in combined open-market purchases within days of Q1 results adds credibility to the value thesis. However, the balance sheet carries $4.3-4.7 billion in debt (net debt/EBITDA ~3.0x), goodwill of $1.4 billion in the Metal Packaging EMEA unit passes its impairment test by only 3.1%, and estimate revisions are uniformly negative (-2.3% to -5.8% across all periods). The stock is cheap for identifiable reasons, and the path from cheap to re-rated requires debt reduction and volume recovery, neither of which is guaranteed in the current macro environment.

## Company Overview

Sonoco Products, headquartered in Hartsville, South Carolina, operates two primary segments following its 2024-2025 portfolio transformation:

**Consumer Packaging (~65% of revenue):** Metal food cans, ends and closures, rigid paper containers (composite cans — e.g., Pringles containers), and aerosol packaging. The December 2024 Eviosys acquisition added $2.2 billion in annual revenue, 44 manufacturing facilities across 17 countries, making Sonoco Europe's largest metal food can producer.

**Industrial Paper Packaging (~31% of revenue):** Tubes, cores, cones, reels, and recycled paperboard for industrial applications globally. This segment includes a growing niche in nailed wood reels for wire and cable serving AI data center construction.

Geographic exposure shifted materially post-Eviosys: approximately 52% of revenue is now international (up from ~38% pre-acquisition), with 43% of sales in EMEA. The customer base consists primarily of major food and consumer brands.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth prospects are modest. Management's 2026 guidance of $7.25-7.75B in revenue implies roughly flat to down 3% from 2025's $7.5B base (reflecting completed divestitures net of volume trends). EPS guidance of $5.80-6.20, with management pointing to the low end, represents a range of +2% to +9% from 2025's $5.71 adjusted.

**Growth Drivers:**
1. **Profitability Performance Plan:** $32M in 2026 savings (on track after $8M in Q1), targeting $150-200M over three years. This is the primary near-term earnings growth engine, representing 12-16% of current adjusted operating profit if fully achieved.
2. **Eviosys synergy realization:** Integration of the largest acquisition in company history remains in early innings.
3. **Thailand paper can facility:** New plant in Nong Yai (200M+ units annually) addressing stacked chip market growth in Asia.
4. **AI data center wire/cable reels:** $20M investment adding 15% reel capacity, a niche but genuine structural demand driver.
5. **Adhesives/sealants packaging:** $30M across three U.S. rigid paper can facilities.

**Growth Headwinds:**
- Industrial Paper Packaging experiencing ongoing demand softness
- Consumer volume/mix declines in Q1 2026 across both segments
- Estimate revisions uniformly negative: current quarter -5.2% (30-day), current year -2.3%, next year -2.2%
- Tariff impacts (50% Section 232 on steel/aluminum inputs) creating demand destruction risk despite contractual pass-through ability

The growth story is fundamentally a cost-out story layered on a defensive, low-growth base business. Organic volume growth is absent. The $150-200M savings program is credible (multiple levers across a complex global footprint post-transformation) but execution risk exists.

## Competitive Landscape

Sonoco holds strong positions in its two core markets:
- **#1 globally in rigid paper containers** (composite cans) — long-standing relationships with major food brands
- **#1 in European metal food cans** (post-Eviosys) — scale advantage across 44 facilities
- **Top-tier in global tubes/cores/reels** — serving paper, textile, and wire/cable industries

Competitive dynamics are relatively stable in both segments. Packaging is a low-growth, relationship-driven industry where scale, proximity to customers, and manufacturing efficiency determine competitive outcomes. Barriers to entry are moderate (capital-intensive manufacturing, qualification requirements with food brands, recycling infrastructure).

Key competitors: Crown Holdings (CCK) in metal cans, Silgan in food/specialty containers, Ball Corp in beverage cans, and various regional players in industrial packaging.

The AI data center opportunity is differentiated: Sonoco is one of few manufacturers with capacity to produce the specialized nailed wood reels required for heavy-gauge power cable installation at scale. This positions the industrial segment for above-market growth in a niche sub-category.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Goodwill Impairment — Metal Packaging EMEA ($1.4B, cushion only 3.1%):**
This is the single largest risk. A discount rate increase from 12.0% to 12.7% (70bp), or a 3.0% reduction in projected EBITDA, would trigger impairment. With 30-year yields above 5%, discount rate pressure is a live risk. A write-down would not affect cash flows or covenants but would devastate reported equity and could trigger sentiment-driven selling.

**2. Leverage and Refinancing Execution:**
The $538M maturing in 2026 appears addressed (March 2026 8-K indicates new financial obligation). But $1.4B matures in 2027-2028. Refinancing at current rates adds ~$50-70M in annual interest expense. If volumes deteriorate further, the leverage ratchet could bind covenant tests.

**3. Tariff Pass-Through and Demand Destruction:**
50% tariffs on steel/aluminum inputs are contractually passable to customers, but the timing lag creates quarterly volatility, and price increases at the consumer level risk demand destruction. In a stagflationary environment where food prices are already elevated, additional packaging cost pass-throughs may reduce volumes.

**4. Seasonal Cash Flow Volatility:**
The metal packaging business creates massive Q1 cash outflows (building inventory ahead of canning season). The $(368M) operating cash flow in Q1 2026 pushed debt from $4.3B to $4.7B. This creates recurring periods of balance sheet stress that could concern creditors.

**5. Russia Exposure:**
~1% of sales from inherited Eviosys Russia operations. Seizure risk exists (Russian government has seized other Western companies' assets). Ongoing compliance costs consume management bandwidth.

## Investment Thesis

- Debt reduction proceeds as planned; net leverage reaches 2.5x by YE2027, enabling multiple re-rating toward 10x P/E

## Conclusion

Sonoco offers a 4.4% dividend yield and 8.1x forward earnings in a defensive packaging business with a credible $150-200M cost savings pathway. The insider buying signal is strong: the CFO and a segment president committed $740K in open-market purchases at essentially the current price within days of reporting results.

However, I cannot recommend BUY given: (1) the Materials sector prohibition in my framework reflects persistent inability to generate alpha in cyclical commodity-adjacent businesses during this macro regime; (2) the goodwill impairment knife-edge (3.1% cushion) creates binary downside risk not adequately compensable at current valuations; (3) uniformly negative estimate revisions signal deteriorating near-term fundamentals; and (4) net debt/EBITDA of 3.0x in a rising-rate environment with $1.4B in 2027-2028 maturities creates refinancing risk.

The income thesis is compelling, and the cost-reduction program provides earnings growth independent of volume recovery. I rate this an income-oriented HOLD with a clear path to upgrade if leverage reduction materializes and volume stabilizes.
