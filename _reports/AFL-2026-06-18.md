**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Aflac is a supplemental health and life insurer whose earnings are dominated by its Japan segment (53% of adjusted revenue, 76% of total assets), where it holds the leading position in third-sector cancer and medical insurance. The investment case rests on three legs: a durable moat in Japan distribution (the Japan Post, Dai-ichi, and Daido alliances are difficult to replicate), exceptional capital strength (Aflac Japan ESR 227%/243% with USP, U.S. combined RBC ~560%), and an aggressive capital-return program that returned $4.7B in 2025 and reduced the share count ~5%. That buyback engine is the primary driver of EPS growth, because the underlying in-force premium base in Japan is in managed decline as legacy limited-pay policies reach paid-up status.

The headline 2025 numbers look alarming and are misleading. GAAP net income fell 33% to $3.65B and diluted EPS dropped to $6.82 from $9.63, but that decline is almost entirely a non-operating investment-mark and FX swing: a roughly $1.85B move from $1.3B net investment gains in 2024 to $572M net investment losses in 2025. On the operating measure management uses, adjusted EPS rose to $7.49 from $7.21, and adjusted earnings were roughly flat near $4.0B. Q1 2026 confirmed the operating stability: adjusted EPS up 6.6% ex-FX to $1.77, Japan pretax margin a strong 35.0% (up 320bp), U.S. pretax margin 20.4%. A scoring model anchored to GAAP net income or the Altman Z-Score (0.71, which is mechanically meaningless for a leveraged insurance balance sheet) would badly misread the business.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is the absence of asymmetric upside at the current valuation. The stock trades around 15-16x forward earnings, in line with its quality but offering no clear mispricing catalyst. Three idiosyncratic overhangs are not fully resolved: a June 2025 cyber breach affecting ~22.65M individuals whose litigation and regulatory cycle has not yet played out, a yen/JGB concentration that makes AFL effectively a yen-exposed equity with a tail risk around parent-company hedge collateral, and Japan Post Holdings steadily trimming its strategic stake. The franchise quality supports holding for the dividend and capital return; the lack of a re-rating catalyst against priced-in risks argues against adding.

## Company Overview

Aflac sells supplemental "third sector" products (cancer, medical, accident, disability, critical illness, hospital indemnity) that pay cash benefits directly to policyholders regardless of other coverage. These products are deliberately less interest-rate-sensitive than traditional life and annuity business and carry strong, stable margins. Profitability depends on pricing above the cost of benefits and administration, plus net investment income on a ~$98B portfolio.

The company operates two reporting segments. **Aflac Japan** pioneered cancer insurance in 1974 and is the country's #1 cancer insurer, with more than 22M individual policies in force (~14M cancer policies). Distribution spans ~6,300 sales agencies (~112,000 licensed associates) plus affiliated channels: Japan Post Group (~20,000 postal outlets), Dai-ichi Life (~37,000 reps), and Daido Life, alongside agreements with roughly 90% of Japan's banks. **Aflac U.S.** sells worksite and voluntary supplemental insurance through ~5,300 weekly-producing agents and brokers, increasingly via the broker channel for mid- and large-employer accounts. A Corporate & Other segment houses the parent, asset management, and the Aflac Re Bermuda reinsurance entity.

The structural demand backdrop in Japan is favorable: an aging population, a "100-year lifespan" framing, and rising out-of-pocket healthcare costs not covered by national insurance support continued demand for third-sector products. The competitive counterweight is post-2001 deregulation, which has intensified competition, and in the U.S. the entry of major medical carriers into supplemental lines via bundling and discounting.

The 2025 GAAP decline is largely optical. Revenue fell 9.3% chiefly on the net investment swing detailed in the Executive Summary. Adjusted EPS, the operating measure, rose to $7.49 from $7.21, with adjusted earnings roughly flat near $4.0B. The roughly 5% reduction in average share count (562M in 2024 to 533M in 2025) is the dominant EPS tailwind, because operating earnings themselves were flat.

**Segment profitability (2025):**
- **Aflac Japan:** pretax adjusted earnings ¥514B (~$3.44B), pretax margin 36.7% (up from 36.0%), helped by favorable cancer/hospitalization morbidity and a Q3 reserve remeasurement gain. Premium persistency 93.1%. Net earned premiums declined on internal cancer reinsurance to Aflac Re (~¥21B), limited-pay policies reaching paid-up status (~¥15B), and deferred-profit-liability remeasurement. Annualized premiums in force fell to ¥1.18T from ¥1.21T.
- **Aflac U.S.:** pretax adjusted earnings $1.42B (essentially flat), pretax margin 20.6%. Net earned premiums grew 2.9% on improved sales; annualized premiums in force up 4.9% to $6.7B. Persistency a lower 79.2%, reflecting worksite product churn.

**Capital allocation.** The defining financial feature is aggressive return of capital: 2025 returned $4.7B ($3.5B buybacks / 33M shares + $1.2B dividends) vs. $3.9B in 2024. Q1 2026 added $1.0B buyback + $315M dividends. The quarterly dividend was raised 5.2% to $0.61, continuing a long growth track record, with 114.3M shares still authorized.

Operating cash flow ($2.56B in 2025) was below dividends plus buybacks. For a mature insurer this is normal: return of capital is funded partly by investment portfolio cash flows and subsidiary dividends (Japan remitted ¥396.7B / ~$2.68B to the parent in 2025), not solely operating cash flow. The Piotroski signal that OCF does not exceed net income reflects insurance reserve accounting, not weakness. The genuine constraint is that buyback pace is ultimately gated by Japan FSA, Nebraska, and Bermuda regulatory dividend capacity, which would tighten if capital markets stressed those ratios.

**Balance sheet and capital.** Total assets $116.5B; shareholders' equity $29.5B ($56.85/share). Solvency is exceptional: Aflac Japan SMR 995% (down from 1,221%), new ESR 227% / 243% with USP (above the 170-230% target), Aflac U.S. combined RBC ~560-570% (target 350-450%), parent unencumbered liquidity $3.4B vs. a $1.0B minimum. The Altman Z-Score of 0.71 ("distress") is a false signal: the formula was built for industrial manufacturers and mechanically reads an insurer's investment-backed leverage as distress. GAAP ROE was 13.1% and adjusted-ex-FX ROE 17.6%, both healthy.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is the weakest leg of the thesis and the reason this dimension scores below average. Top-line earned premium in Japan is in structural managed decline. New annualized premium grew 16% in yen in 2025 (driven by the "Miraito" cancer product, which made up 67% of Japan NAP mix, and "Anshin Palette" medical launched December 2025), but earned premium fell as limited-pay policies reach paid-up status and as the internal Aflac Re reinsurance reduces reported net premium. The result is a business where strong new-business sales are partially masked by legacy runoff, and where the in-force annualized premium base shrank.

Q1 2026 third-party coverage cited Japan sales up 25.5%, which is a NAP/sales figure distinct from earned premium (down 3.8% in yen due to paid-up and reinsurance runoff). Investors should not conflate the two. The U.S. business is the cleaner growth engine on premium and in-force trends, but at ~20% pretax margin it is the smaller, lower-margin segment.

Where growth actually comes from is the combination of flat-to-modestly-growing adjusted operating earnings plus ~5% annual share count reduction, producing mid-single-digit adjusted EPS growth. This is a durable but unexciting algorithm: the company is engineering per-share growth out of a slowly contracting top line through buybacks funded by subsidiary dividends. Aflac Re's March 2026 assumption of a Japan Post Insurance whole-life annuity block is financially immaterial but signals an intent to build a Japan reinsurance franchise, a small optional growth lever rather than a near-term needle-mover.

## Valuation Assessment

With the third-party data feed unavailable, we rely on the analyst commentary in the research base, which puts the forward P/E near 15.7x with a constructive-but-cautious "Hold" tone. On adjusted EPS of $7.49 for 2025 and a mid-single-digit growth path, that multiple is reasonable for an insurer of this quality but not cheap. Against a yield-and-buyback total return, the stock offers a dividend yield around 2% on the $0.61 quarterly rate plus ~5% annual share count reduction — call it ~7% baseline shareholder yield, which sets a floor on total return but does not imply a re-rating.

The valuation does not present asymmetric upside. There is no identifiable catalyst that would force a multiple expansion: revenue is contracting on the reported (earned premium) line, and the growth algorithm is well understood by the market. The 15.7x forward multiple sits roughly in line with quality life/supplemental insurer peers (the cited peer set includes Unum, Globe Life), where the group trades at a discount to the broader market reflecting yen/credit/rate sensitivity. We treat the apparent "cheapness" relative to the broad market with skepticism: a moderate multiple on a structurally declining premium base, with embedded FX and JGB tail risks, is fairly priced rather than mispriced.

A reasonable base case anchors on ~15-16x forward adjusted EPS, supporting a fair value broadly in line with the recent ~$112-119 trading band. That implies the stock is close to fair value, with the buyback providing downside support and the FX/cyber tails capping near-term upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Aflac's moat in Japan third-sector is genuine and durable. Incumbency dating to 1974, the in-force policy base, brand recognition (the Duck is 25 years old in the U.S.), and the alliance distribution network are difficult for competitors to replicate. The 36.7% Japan pretax margin and 93.1% persistency are evidence the moat is intact and even widening on margin. The structural demand backdrop supports volume even as deregulation since 2001 increases competition.

The competitive trajectory is mixed rather than strictly improving. In Japan, the in-force premium base is shrinking even as new sales grow strongly, so the franchise is running to stand still on earned premium. In the U.S., the threat is more pointed: major medical carriers facing margin erosion in core lines are pushing into supplemental products and bundling/discounting to win share, and employers increasingly want a full product suite from one carrier. Aflac is responding by broadening into life, disability, dental/vision, and group, and U.S. premium in force continues to grow, but the 79.2% U.S. persistency points to real worksite churn. The net read is a strong, defensible position with no near-term displacement risk, against a backdrop where it must run hard to maintain its lead.

## Risk Assessment

The risks here are specific and idiosyncratic rather than sector truisms.

**Japan concentration and yen/dollar FX** is the defining risk. With the bulk of revenue and assets in Japan, reported results, book value, and equity swing materially with the yen. 2025 equity carried a $4.8B cumulative unrealized FX translation loss. The hedging program (~$25B notional Japan options, FX forwards, yen-denominated debt as a net-investment hedge) is described in the 10-K as "limited in scope and may not be successful." There is an underappreciated tail: a sharp yen strengthening would boost reported earnings and book value but could create cash strain at the parent via derivative collateral and settlement.

**JGB concentration and interest-rate risk.** Substantial Japan Government Bond holdings (~$32.6B amortized cost, ~$4.2B unrealized loss as the BoJ exits ultra-low rates) are the single largest unrealized loss position. A scarcity of long-dated yen assets forces reliance on USD-denominated investments hedged back to yen, creating roll-over and hedge-cost risk. A 100bp rate decline would raise the future-policy-benefit liability by $7.4B.

**Commercial real estate and below-investment-grade credit.** Transitional real estate loans and commercial mortgage loans concentrated partly in office are in a distressed market; the company foreclosed on $257M (amortized cost) in 2025, holds $902M real estate owned, and booked $24M of additional REO impairments in Q1 2026. Separately, ~$5.1B of below-IG exposure including $3.97B of largely floating-rate middle-market loans is a deliberate yield-enhancement allocation that adds credit risk in a downturn.

**Cyber breach (the key unresolved overhang).** Management states the incident is not currently expected to be material, but that stance has not been tested by the litigation and regulatory cycle, which typically lags 12-24 months. Costs for credit monitoring, call centers, legal, and higher cyber premiums are being incurred, and cyber-insurance recovery is still being assessed. This is the most significant idiosyncratic event and a genuine left-tail not fully priced.

**Holding-company dependence on subsidiary dividends**, constrained by Japan FSA / Nebraska / Bermuda capital rules, is material given the parent's heavy buyback and dividend commitments, and **key-executive succession** (CEO Amos is 74) is explicitly flagged in the filings.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$130).** Adjusted earnings stay flat-to-modestly-growing while buybacks continue to compress the share count ~5% annually, delivering mid-single-digit-plus EPS growth. Japan margins hold above 35%, the cyber-breach litigation resolves without material financial impact, and a stable-to-stronger yen lifts reported book value and earnings without triggering parent hedge strain. The market continues to reward the capital-return discipline and the multiple holds at the upper end of the peer range. Total return is then ~7% shareholder yield plus modest EPS growth and a stable multiple.

**Bear case (~$105, roughly -7% to -10% from the recent band).** The cyber-breach litigation and regulatory cycle produces a material settlement or reserve charge; CRE/below-IG credit losses accelerate in a stressed market; the yen swings sharply and creates either reported-earnings drag (weaker yen) or parent hedge-collateral cash strain (stronger yen). The in-force premium decline accelerates faster than new sales can offset, pressuring earned premium and eventually adjusted earnings. Japan Post continues trimming its stake, weighing on sentiment. The multiple compresses to the low end of the peer range.

The realistic outcome sits between these, with the buyback providing a floor and the FX/cyber/credit tails capping upside. The asymmetry is roughly balanced, which is the core reason for a HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The thesis is principally about a defensible ~7% shareholder yield (dividend plus buyback) from a wide-moat, exceptionally well-capitalized insurer, rather than expected appreciation from a re-rating. We classify this as an income hold because the return profile is dominated by capital return and dividend growth, not by a near-term growth catalyst.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Forward P/E compresses toward ~13x or below on stable adjusted EPS (a ~15%+ price decline on unchanged fundamentals), creating asymmetric value.
- Cyber-breach litigation resolves with confirmed immaterial financial impact, removing the principal idiosyncratic overhang.
- Evidence that Japan earned premium (not just NAP sales) inflects to positive growth, or U.S. persistency improves above ~82%, signaling the in-force decline is reversing.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material cyber-related settlement, reserve charge, or regulatory penalty that meaningfully impairs earnings or capital.
- A capital-ratio deterioration that forces a reduction in buyback pace (e.g., ESR falling below the 170% target floor, or RBC below ~400%).
- Acceleration of CRE/below-IG credit losses beyond the current modest run rate, or an adjusted-earnings decline (not GAAP optics) for two consecutive quarters.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release, with particular attention to cyber-breach cost accruals, any litigation disclosures, ESR/RBC trends, the yen, and the buyback pace. The first full ESR report under the new economic-value regime (March 31, 2026 reporting date) is also a structural item to monitor.

## Conclusion

Aflac is a financially very strong, highly cash-generative supplemental insurer with a wide moat in Japan third-sector products and a disciplined capital-return program that converts flat operating earnings into mid-single-digit per-share growth through buybacks. The headline 2025 GAAP decline overstates deterioration: adjusted EPS rose, capital ratios are exceptional, and the Altman Z-Score distress reading is an artifact of insurance-balance-sheet mechanics. Q1 2026 confirmed operating stability.

The franchise quality justifies owning the stock for its yield and capital return, but the current ~15-16x forward multiple offers no asymmetric upside, and the three idiosyncratic overhangs — the unresolved cyber breach with an untested litigation tail, yen/JGB concentration with a parent-hedge cash-strain tail, and Japan Post steadily trimming its stake — keep the risk/reward balanced. Our overall score of 5.9 and HOLD rating reflect a high-quality business at a fair price with priced-in but live risks. Existing holders should retain for the income and capital-return profile; new capital lacks the asymmetric entry that would warrant a BUY, and a 15%+ pullback on unchanged fundamentals or resolution of the cyber overhang would be the cleaner upgrade triggers.
