## Executive Summary

CBRE is producing the strongest results in its post-2022 recovery. Q2 2026 revenue rose 15.5% to $11.23bn, core EBITDA rose 33.5% to $836M, Advisory segment operating margin expanded to 17.5% from 15.8%, US office leasing grew 29%, and management raised FY2026 core EPS guidance for the second time this year, to $7.80–$7.90 from an original $7.30–$7.60. That is the fifth consecutive quarter of core EPS growth of at least 18%. The company also put a five-year target on the record: data center services revenue growth of roughly 25% annually and more than $1bn of infrastructure-related EBITDA by 2030, against roughly $1.2bn of infrastructure services revenue in Q2 alone.

Three things keep me from upgrading. First, earnings quality. H1 2026 core EBITDA grew 45.7%, but $306M of that came from real estate disposition gains versus $19M a year earlier, and CBRE's core EBITDA reconciliation does not remove them. Strip the gains and the $168M Telford provision from GAAP operating income and H1 growth was +17.0% — identical to revenue growth, i.e. no GAAP operating margin expansion. Second, the Telford UK fire-safety liability has gone $204M (12/24) → $321M (12/25) → $456M (6/30/26), with the latest true-up explicitly adding a *new work category* (internal fire containment) rather than just repricing existing scope. Roughly $300M of charges have been booked across six quarters against about $48M of cash spend, and no range or ceiling is disclosed. The charge is excluded from core EPS, the metric on which the stock is valued and on which management is compensated. Third, management's guidance rests on a stated conditional: the CFO tied the sales and origination pipeline to the 10-year Treasury holding in a 4.0–4.5% band. Advisory generates about 20% of revenue but roughly 42% of positive segment operating profit, so the transaction-levered part of the P&L is sensitive to the rate path relative to that assumption. [unverified — current 10-year Treasury level, 30-year level, Freddie Mac 30-year mortgage rate, and Fed meeting/dissent detail are not in the provided sources]

At ~$147, CBRE trades at 18.7x the FY2026 core EPS guidance midpoint of $7.85 and 16.2x the FY2027 consensus of $9.04 — reasonable for a business compounding core EPS in the high teens with a ~4.4% annualized buyback yield, but not the mispricing it was at $122–130 six to eight weeks ago. The stock has recovered about 20% off the June 1 low ($121.69) and roughly 4% in the past week (7/23 $135.55 to 7/30 $149.46). Consensus mean target of $180 implies 23% upside and 12 of 13 analysts are positive, but the estimate revisions supporting that are modest (+1.2% current year, +2.0% next year over 90 days) and next-quarter consensus has actually been cut 4.2%. I would own CBRE and add on weakness toward $125–132; I would not initiate at $147.

## Company Overview

CBRE is the largest commercial real estate services and investment firm globally, headquartered in Dallas, with roughly 155,000 employees and FY2025 revenue of $40.55bn. Its revenue center of gravity and its profit center of gravity sit in different places, which matters for how the stock should be analyzed.

**Building Operations & Experience (BOE)** — Q2 revenue $6,686M, +14.6%; segment operating profit $335M — is 60.6% of H1 revenue but only about 32% of segment profit, because much of it is client pass-through cost. On net revenue (revenue less pass-throughs), H1 BOE margin is roughly 10.0%. It houses facilities management ($5,311M), property management ($699M), and a newly created critical infrastructure services line ($676M, +67.7%).

**Advisory Services** — Q2 revenue $2,306M, +17.7%; SOP $449M — is leasing, investment sales, valuation, mortgage origination and loan servicing. At 19.0% H1 SOP margin it is the profit engine: about 20% of revenue, about 42% of positive segment profit.

**Project Management** — Q2 revenue $2,045M, +19.1%; SOP $147M — operates through Turner & Townsend, in which CBRE took a 70% controlling interest in January 2025.

**Real Estate Investments (REI)** — Q2 revenue $193M, −10.2% — is investment management (AUM $154.8bn) plus Trammell Crow development. Small in revenue, disproportionate in earnings because development monetization gains flow through it, and because the Telford liability sits there.

One relationship is easy to garble: in the widely-discussed Meta arrangement, Meta is the customer and CBRE is the service provider. CBRE recruits, trains and places technical data center personnel — including, per the CEO, into competitors' teams. It is a staffing and services revenue stream, not an AI technology partnership, and CBRE does not own or develop Meta's infrastructure.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and reported earnings.** H1 2026 revenue was $21.75bn, +17.0%. GAAP net income attributable to CBRE was $522M for the half. On a quarterly basis the divergence between GAAP and non-GAAP is stark: Q2 revenue +15.5% and core EBITDA +33.5%, yet **GAAP diluted EPS fell to $0.69 from $0.72** and consolidated GAAP operating income declined to $365M from $374M even as total segment operating profit rose to $835M from $628M. The bridge is the $168M pre-tax Telford provision, which pushed REI to a $137M GAAP operating loss despite $42M of REI segment operating profit. At the 21.6% Q2 effective tax rate that charge is roughly $0.45 per share on 293.86M diluted shares; if the UK provision is not deductible, the impact is nearer $0.57. Ex-Telford Q2 EPS was therefore approximately $1.14–$1.26. External descriptions of a "6% beat" referred to core EPS against non-GAAP consensus; on GAAP the quarter showed a year-over-year decline. Both are true and should not be blended.

**How much of the growth is operating leverage?** This is the most important calculation in the name.

| H1 measure | 2026 | 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core EBITDA (as reported) | $1,667M | $1,144M | +45.7% |
| Core EBITDA ex real-estate gains | $1,361M | $1,125M | +21.0% |
| GAAP operating income ex gains | $570M | $631M | −9.7% |
| GAAP operating income ex gains and ex Telford | $738M | $631M | +17.0% |

Real estate disposition gains were $306M in H1 2026 versus $19M in H1 2025, and the core EBITDA reconciliation retains them. Splitting by quarter (H1 less Q2 disclosures): Q1 2026 core EBITDA ex-gains was approximately $530M against roughly $518M in Q1 2025, about +2.3%; Q2 2026 ex-gains was roughly $831M against about $607M, roughly +37%. Underlying leverage is real and accelerated sharply in Q2 — 21% ex-gain core EBITDA growth on 17% revenue growth is genuine — but the headline 45.7% substantially overstates momentum because it carries lumpy development monetization pulled into Q1. Management disclosed the pull-forward in April and left full-year REI guidance unchanged, so the full year is not being inflated, only its intra-year distribution. The counterpoint deserves weight: Trammell Crow development is a long-standing operating business, not a financial gain, and GAAP operating income also absorbs non-cash acquisition amortization from Pearce and Industrious. Neither lens alone is sufficient, but a reader modeling 40%+ EBITDA growth as the run rate is modeling the wrong number.

**Add-back quality.** Excluding Telford, H1 2026 non-GAAP add-backs total roughly $220M against $522M of GAAP net income: integration and acquisition costs $114M, business and finance transformation $70M (vs. $28M in H1 2025 and $101M for all of FY2025), wind-down of certain businesses $30M, efficiency costs $6M. The transformation line running at roughly $140M annualized after multiple consecutive years weakens the case for treating it as non-recurring. FY2025's full GAAP-to-core bridge was approximately $763M ($1,157M GAAP net income vs. ~$1,920M core). A reasonable economic haircut of $0.50–$0.60 per share to core EPS puts FY2026 "cash economic" EPS nearer $7.25–$7.35 and the effective multiple around 20x.

**Cash flow requires definitional care.** GAAP net cash *used* in operating activities was −$687M in H1 2026 (H1 2025: −$489M); with $195M of capex, GAAP free cash flow was about −$882M. This is seasonal, not deterioration: accrued bonus fell from $1,879M to $1,147M, producing a $706M compensation outflow, and receivables/prepaids consumed a further $783M as the business grew. Mortgage-banking and warehouse flows netted to roughly +$18M, so they do not explain the negative half. Separately, management cited trailing-twelve-month free cash flow of about $1.7bn at 78% conversion, and one covering analyst referenced a ~$2bn run rate. FY2025 GAAP operating cash flow less capex was approximately $1.19bn. **I cannot reconcile the $500M+ gap from the filings.** The adjustments are presumably definitional and defensible (warehouse-line normalization, possibly certain non-core cash costs), but "free cash flow" and "conversion" as used are non-GAAP measures without a reconciliation in the 10-Q. Neither the negative half nor the strong single quarter ("nearly $1.4bn" of Q2 operating cash flow) should be annualized; CBRE's cash generation is structurally back-half weighted.

**Balance sheet.** At 6/30/26: total assets $30.47bn, liabilities $21.30bn, CBRE equity $8.40bn, noncontrolling interests $322M, mezzanine (redeemable NCI) $454M. Recourse-type debt of roughly $7.86bn (term loans $1,266M due 2028; senior notes 2029–2036 including a new $750M 5.250% issue in May 2026; commercial paper $1,575M at 4.08%; notes payable on real estate $405M, up from $197M) less $1,489M cash gives net debt of about $6.37bn. Annualizing H1 core EBITDA at 2x implies roughly 1.9x net leverage — a labeled sensitivity, not a forecast, and one that both overstates leverage (earnings skew to H2) and understates it (H1 embeds the Q1 development gains). Against a 4.25x covenant with reported compliance, an undrawn $3.5bn revolver to June 2030 and a new undrawn $1.0bn 364-day facility, near-term solvency is not the question.

The structural weakness is capitalization quality. Goodwill of $6,998M plus other intangibles of $2,844M totals $9,842M against $8,398M of equity, implying roughly **−$1.44bn of tangible equity**. Accumulated goodwill impairments already recognized total $1,120M across all four segments, so impairment is not hypothetical here; a January 1, 2026 interim test triggered by the segment realignment found none. Two facility details deserve attention without overreading: Amendment No. 3 (June 2025) removed the interest coverage covenant, leaving leverage as the sole operative financial test, and T&T's €120M revolver was terminated 6/30/26 and not replaced. Maximum warehouse capacity was also reduced from $4,750M to $4,200M, with the enlarged Fannie ASAP line not renewed and the TD Bank facility renewed in July on a two-month tenor to 9/13/26. These are non-recourse and self-liquidating, an operational capacity constraint rather than a solvency issue, but the tenor is worth monitoring.

Financial health scores 5.8: ample committed liquidity and covenant headroom offset by negative tangible book, a demonstrated impairment history, a $456M and rising Telford liability, $1.2bn of performance bonds, $48.5bn of Fannie DUS loss-sharing exposure reserved at only $83M, and non-GAAP cash flow claims I could not verify.

## Growth Analysis

The demand picture is genuinely good and the drivers are identifiable.

**Transactional recovery.** Global and US leasing each rose 24% in Q2; US office leasing rose 29%; global property sales revenue rose 20%. Advisory SOP grew 29.4% with margin expanding 170bp. Commercial mortgage origination revenue rose 24.5% in H1 on a 2.1% *decline* in origination volume — decomposed, Topic 606 in-scope placement fees grew 40.7% to $128M while GSE origination gains were flat at ~$50M. The growth is in debt brokerage rather than agency origination, which is less capital-intensive and consistent with the reduced warehouse capacity, but also more competitive.

**Infrastructure and data centers.** Q2 infrastructure services revenue rose more than 45% to nearly $1.2bn, of which data center services was about $700M, up nearly 30%. CBRE works on more than 1,300 data centers and touches them through brokerage, facilities management, T&T project delivery and Trammell Crow land development, the latter with roughly $30bn of in-process and pipeline projects and about $900M of embedded gains held with very little of CBRE's own capital. Management's targets — ~25% annual data center services growth for five years, >$1bn of infrastructure EBITDA by 2030 — are on the record. [unverified — recent hyperscaler capex prints are not in the provided sources]

**Two disciplines apply.** First, critical infrastructure growth is substantially inorganic. Pearce Services closed 11/4/25 for $1,186M of consideration, is guided to $600M+ of 2026 revenue, and is a telecom, power, cell tower, wind and solar asset services business — not a data center business. Q2 critical infrastructure revenue rose $273M year over year; a large share of that is Pearce. The CFO confirmed the organic picture directly: ex-Pearce Q1 BOE revenue growth was mid-teens versus 16% reported. Second, roughly 6–7 points of BOE's guided ~25% 2026 SOP growth is an amortization reclassification with an offsetting D&A increase and zero net income effect — management disclosed this clearly, which is a governance positive, but anyone modeling BOE margin expansion off reported SOP will overstate it.

**One soft spot.** Investment management AUM fell to $154.8bn from $155.5bn at year-end, with H1 net outflows of $1.3bn (inflows $5.4bn, outflows $6.7bn) partly offset by $0.6bn of appreciation; separate accounts declined from $75.8bn to $73.9bn. The 10-Q cites Middle East conflict slowing regional fundraising. REI revenue fell 10.2% in Q2 and development services revenue fell to $44M from $70M. A sub-1% net outflow on a $155bn base is not alarming, and the development decline coincides with gains being harvested in Q1, so timing is plausible. It is nonetheless the one recurring fee franchise that is shrinking while everything else accelerates.

**Estimate revisions.** Current year $7.76 (+1.2% 30d, +1.7% 90d), next year $9.04 (+2.0% both windows), current quarter $1.90 (+2.8% 30d). Directionally positive and consistent with two guidance raises, but the magnitude is nowhere near the >30% velocity that constitutes a strong standalone buy signal. Next-quarter consensus was cut 2.8% in 30 days and 4.2% in 90 days — most plausibly the intra-year redistribution of development gains, but a divergence worth noting rather than dismissing. The beat record is good: three beats and one in-line over four quarters, with the Q1 39.8% surprise driven largely by the pulled-forward gains and Q2 a more normal +2.6%.

Growth prospects: 6.8.

The gap is not purely cosmetic: Telford is a real cash cost spread over years, transformation spend has doubled year over year and recurred for multiple years, and Pearce/Industrious amortization reflects real capital deployed. A $0.50–$0.60 haircut to core EPS gives an effective forward multiple nearer 20x.

**Peer context is only partially usable.** The Real Estate Services median P/E of 41.5x is distorted by depressed earnings — CoStar at 545x and Kennedy-Wilson at 96x are not comparable, and Cushman & Wakefield at 41.5x and FirstService at 39.9x reflect trough or asset-light-premium situations. The one clean read is JLL at 16.0x GAAP against CBRE's ~33x GAAP. CBRE is a scale leader with a superior infrastructure franchise, so some premium is warranted, but a 2x GAAP multiple gap cannot be explained by growth differential alone; much of it is CBRE's larger GAAP-to-core bridge. I do not have JLL's adjusted EPS or growth rate, so I cannot claim JLL is cheaper on a comparable basis — only that the GAAP comparison flatters neither the peer group's usefulness nor CBRE's earnings quality.

**What has changed since the stock was attractive.** CBRE bottomed at $121.69 on June 1 and is up roughly 20% from there, including about 4% in the last week (7/23 $135.55 to 7/30 $149.46). The de-rating from the February high of $174.27 was attributed to an AI/disintermediation "scare trade" — the same trade that took CoStar down 72% from its high. CBRE's own drawdown from that high to the June low was roughly 30%, deeper than a mild dip but far short of CoStar's, suggesting the market has been discriminating rather than indiscriminate. The mispricing case was strong at $122–132 against a $7.80–7.90 guide; at 18.7x with the rate path a live sensitivity, it is a fair-value case rather than a mispricing case.

Consensus mean and median targets of $180.42 and $180.00 imply 23% upside, with a $144–$200 range. On stocks with rising estimates and sub-30x multiples, consensus targets typically lag rather than cap. I give that some weight, but the supporting revisions here are +1–2%, not the double-digit velocity that forces target upgrades.

Valuation: 5.8. Fairly priced with modest positive skew, not a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

Three advantages are supported by disclosure rather than assertion. Scale in labor-intensive service delivery: CBRE hires roughly 30,000 people annually, which is the stated reason Meta engaged it to recruit and place data center technicians. Breadth across the infrastructure value chain: brokerage, facilities and critical infrastructure services, T&T project delivery, and Trammell Crow land development, coordinated across 1,300+ data centers. And the land-entitlement franchise itself, with roughly $30bn of in-process and pipeline projects and about $900M of embedded gains held on minimal balance sheet.

Advisory margin expanding 170bp while volumes grow 20–24% is evidence of pricing power or share gain rather than just cycle. Per one analyst's framing (Bisnow, 7/29/26), CBRE reported ahead of JLL, Colliers and Cushman & Wakefield this quarter, though I have not independently verified peer results.

**The unresolved question is disintermediation, and its locus is contested.** The market's de-rating targets brokerage — the information-and-expertise arbitrage. Management's own assessment points the opposite way: the CEO identified BOE as where the risk sits, arguing brokerage is comparatively protected because value accrues to brokers' negotiation and judgment, and dismissing proptech competitors on revenue grounds. The CFO said AI-driven headcount reductions in HR, shared services, research and FP&A are anticipated "a few years from now versus immediately." If management is right, the earnings at risk are the lower-margin 60% of revenue that contributes ~32% of segment profit. If the market is right, the higher-margin 20% of revenue contributing ~42% of profit is exposed. No filing quantifies either direction; both framings are hypotheses. Management also has an obvious incentive to minimize brokerage risk, and the rebuttal offered was rhetorical rather than evidentiary. AI is also a cost line — management flagged access control ("it can get really expensive really fast") and transformation add-backs have doubled year over year.

**On diversification, one claim is evidenced and one is not.** Revenue mix has genuinely shifted toward management's defined "resilient" set, which grew 18% in Q1. That does not establish reduced *earnings* cyclicality: Advisory still contributes ~42% of positive segment profit on ~20% of revenue, and the filings provide no customer-concentration metric to test.

Competitive position: 7.0, down from prior 7.5. Trajectory is positive in infrastructure and Advisory share, offset by an unresolved disintermediation question in the largest segment and modest investment management outflows.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Telford UK fire-safety remediation.** The liability progression represents about $300M of new provisions across six quarters against roughly $48M of cash spend. The Q2 driver list includes fire engineer assessments, updated surveys, design evolution, regulatory feedback and the addition of internal fire containment work — scope expansion, not just unit-cost inflation. No range or maximum is disclosed. Telford Homes' construction self-delivery business is being wound down. Prudent modeling carries further true-ups. The offsetting reading is that the affected building portfolio is finite and each true-up moves toward a terminal number under a tightening standard; cash outflow is spread over years, so near-term liquidity impact is modest. I could not verify the UK statutory framework, the number of affected buildings, or any ceiling. The escalation pattern is a filing fact; further escalation is my inference.

**2. Rates relative to the stated guidance assumption.** Guidance explicitly assumes no material change to the macro rate environment, and the CFO tied the sales and origination pipeline to a 4.0–4.5% 10-year, saying that as long as there is no significant spike above that band CBRE does not expect slowing. Higher long rates raise cap rates, widen bid-ask spreads, and slow investment sales and refinancing — the mechanism that produced the 2023 core EPS trough of $3.84 from the 2022 $5.69. This is the most checkable near-term threat, and the conditional is a clearly stated management dependency. [unverified — the current 10-year level, 30-year level, Fed meeting/dissent detail, September-hike odds, and 30-year mortgage rate are not in the provided sources]

**3. Turner & Townsend put option.** T&T partners may require CBRE to purchase additional shares, exercisable **1/1/2027 through 3/31/2030 at fair value at exercise**. Carried at $454M in mezzanine equity, up from $433M. The obligation grows with T&T's earnings, so success raises the cost, and it competes directly with buybacks and M&A. At $454M against $4.5bn of undrawn committed capacity it would not strain the balance sheet, and partners may exercise in tranches or not at all. The €120M T&T revolver termination on 6/30/26 has a mundane explanation (redundancy) and I am not inferring a planned buyout.

**4. Negative tangible equity with impairment history.** −$1.44bn tangible equity, $1,120M of prior goodwill impairments across all four segments, $613M of new goodwill from Pearce of which only ~$106M is tax-deductible. Negative tangible book is normal for asset-light services roll-ups; the combination with a demonstrated impairment record in a cyclical end-market is a genuine downside-scenario consideration. The ~2.2 Altman Z-Score reflects this, but the metric is calibrated on manufacturers and systematically penalizes high-turnover, intangible-heavy, pass-through-revenue businesses — I do not read it as a distress signal.

**5. Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac loss sharing.** $51.0bn of DUS unpaid principal funded, of which $48.5bn carries loss sharing up to one-third, against an $83M recorded liability and $165M of letters of credit. Fannie's recourse is limited to CBRE MCI's ~$771M of assets. A severe multifamily credit cycle is the tail; structural recourse limitation caps parent exposure.

**6. FX and contingent commitments.** 43.0% of Q2 revenue was non-USD (UK 13.6%, euro 9.9%). Disclosed H1 sensitivities: a 10% USD appreciation versus GBP would have *increased* pre-tax income by $17M; versus the euro, *decreased* it by $12M. A 100bp rise on variable-rate debt reduces H1 pre-tax income by $14M. FX was a tailwind in Q1, flattering reported 2026 growth. Off-balance-sheet items: $344M letters of credit, $318M guarantees, $1.2bn performance bonds (up from $1.0bn), $241M deferred purchase consideration, $386M gross unrecognized tax benefits, plus a $115M Pearce deferred payment due 11/3/26 and an earnout of up to $115M through CY2027.

**7. Non-GAAP incentive alignment.** Four of five performance measures are non-GAAP or segment-level, with core EPS the company-selected measure. The same add-backs that remove Telford, transformation and integration from core earnings feed incentive determination. FY2025 GAAP net income of $1,157M remains below FY2021's $1,837M even as core EPS hit a record $6.38. The relative-TSR component provides an independent market check, and pay-actually-paid genuinely swings with outcomes (PEO actually-paid was $1.75M in 2022; NEO average was −$1.88M), so this is an alignment consideration rather than a ratchet.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not corroborating the AI-disintermediation fear. Near-term ATM IV of 30.5% sits essentially at 1-year historical volatility of 31.3% (spread −0.8%) and at the 1st percentile within the current chain following a post-earnings crush from 37.6% on 7/29. Put/call volume ratio is 0.21 and put/call open interest 0.17 — very light put demand, with the skew flagged call-heavy at −8.5%. Term structure is in contango (30.5% near versus 34.3% at six months), the normal, non-stressed configuration, with the longer-dated IV suggesting some interest in extended-horizon protection or upside. Short interest is 1.9% of float at 2.7 days to cover. No informed bearish positioning is visible. The reported 12.83 put/call volume ratio at the six-month tenor almost certainly reflects a single thin trade and should not be read as a directional signal.

The market is pricing CBRE as an ordinary, unstressed mid-cap with a resolved near-term event. That supports the view that the February–June de-rating was narrative-driven rather than distress-driven, and it also means there is no cheap volatility to buy as protection against the Telford or rate risks.

Risk factors: 5.2. The concentration of specific, quantified, unresolved items — an escalating liability with no ceiling, a contractual put opening in five months, a guidance assumption whose margin of safety has narrowed, negative tangible book with impairment precedent — outweighs the low short interest and benign options structure.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$205, +40%)

The AI infrastructure buildout is a multi-segment, multi-year demand event for CBRE, and 2026 results are the first evidence at scale: data center services ~$700M in Q2 growing ~30%, infrastructure services ~$1.2bn growing 45%+, a stated path to >$1bn of infrastructure EBITDA by 2030 against roughly $3bn of infrastructure revenue in FY2025. Simultaneously, the transaction cycle is recovering — US office leasing +29%, global sales +20%, Advisory margin +170bp — which levers the highest-margin segment. Core EPS compounds from $6.38 (2025) to $7.85 (2026 guide) to $9.04 (2027 consensus) and beyond, while ~$3.9bn of remaining buyback authorization removes 4%+ of shares annually at prices management calls undervalued. On $9.75 of FY2027 core EPS at 21x, the stock is $205. The AI de-rating reverses as investors distinguish CBRE's labor-and-relationship service model from pure information intermediaries like CoStar (−72% from its high versus CBRE's ~−30%).

### Bear Case (~$118, −20%)

The 10-year moves durably above the 4.0–4.5% band management flagged. Cap rates reset upward, bid-ask spreads widen, and investment sales and refinancing volumes stall — the exact mechanism that took core EPS from $5.69 to $3.84 in 2023. Advisory's ~42% profit contribution deleverages. Meanwhile Telford true-ups continue (three consecutive escalations, scope still expanding, no disclosed ceiling), the T&T put is exercised in size from January 2027, and investors conclude that the correct multiple applies to GAAP earnings — where H1 growth ex-gains ex-Telford exactly matched revenue, with no margin expansion — rather than to a core figure carrying $220M+ of semi-permanent add-backs per half. FY2027 core EPS lands flat at ~$7.85 and the multiple compresses to 15x: $118, roughly the June low. A deeper cyclical trough with core EPS at $6.00 and a 15x multiple implies $90.

### Base Case (~$168, +14%)

FY2026 core EPS delivers within guidance at $7.85–7.90; FY2027 reaches roughly $9.00 as data center services and infrastructure compound at 25%+ and the transaction recovery slows but does not reverse; the buyback removes ~4% of shares. Applying the current 18.6x forward multiple to FY2027 consensus of $9.04 gives $168. Telford absorbs another $75–150M of provisions, invisible in core EPS but visible in GAAP; the T&T put is exercised partially and funded from the revolver.

With the stock about 20% off its June low and 4% higher in a week, continuing an unchanged BUY would require evidence I do not have — specifically, either a resolution of the Telford escalation or confirmation that the rate environment remains inside management's 4.0–4.5% assumption.

**Reference price:** $146.81 (7/31 snapshot; 7/30 close $149.46; company profile lists $146.33 — I use the most clearly timestamped figure for all multiples).

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Price below $132** with FY2026 guidance intact — that is 16.8x the guide midpoint and 14.6x FY2027 consensus, restoring the asymmetry that existed at the June low. I would scale in $125–132.
2. **Telford stabilization** — a Q3 or FY2026 disclosure showing no incremental provision, or the first disclosed estimate range or ceiling. Two consecutive quarters without a true-up would materially change the earnings-quality assessment.
3. **GAAP operating margin expansion** — Q3 or Q4 GAAP operating income, excluding real estate disposition gains and Telford, growing at least 400bp faster than revenue. That would convert the core-EBITDA leverage story into a GAAP fact and justify a higher multiple on core.
4. **10-year Treasury sustained comfortably inside the 4.0–4.5% band** with leasing and property sales volumes holding, removing the principal threat to the ~42% of segment profit sitting in Advisory.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **A fourth consecutive Telford escalation of $100M+**, particularly if accompanied by further scope additions — cumulative provisions would exceed $400M with no ceiling, and the "converging on a terminal number" reading would fail.
2. **FY2026 core EPS guidance cut below $7.60**, or FY2027 initial guidance below $8.50, indicating the transaction recovery has stalled under the rate path.
3. **Advisory SOP margin contracting below 15.5%** on flat-to-down leasing or property sales revenue — the signature of cycle reversal in the highest-margin segment.
4. **A goodwill impairment charge in any segment**, which against −$1.44bn tangible equity and $1,120M of prior impairments would force a re-examination of the entire acquisition program (Pearce $1,186M, Industrious $841M).

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results in late October, with interim checkpoints at the 11/3/26 Pearce $115M deferred payment, any 8-K on Telford, and the 10-year Treasury level relative to the 4.0–4.5% band.

## Conclusion

CBRE is executing well against a favorable demand backdrop. Q2 delivered 16% revenue growth, 30% core EPS growth, a second guidance raise, Advisory margin expansion of 170bp, and data center services growing near 30% toward a stated $1bn infrastructure EBITDA target by 2030. Capital allocation has been disciplined: $945M of H1 buybacks at an average $141.55 (Q2 at $133.94), a 2.0% share reduction in six months, $3.9bn of authorization remaining, and a management team that raised guidance twice rather than once.

Three specific things hold me at HOLD. The operating leverage that would justify a re-rating is not visible in GAAP once the H1 disposition gains and the Telford charge are neutralized — growth was exactly in line with revenue. The Telford liability has more than doubled in 18 months, with new scope still being added and no disclosed ceiling, and it is excluded from the metric on which the stock is valued and management is paid. And the guidance rests on a 10-year Treasury band of 4.0–4.5% that is a clearly stated management conditional and a live sensitivity for the ~42% of segment profit in Advisory. At 18.7x FY2026 core EPS and 16.2x FY2027 — roughly 20x on a cash-economic basis after haircutting recurring add-backs — the price reflects most of the good news that has arrived.

The entry that mattered was $122–132 in late May and early June, and it may come again if the rate path bites. Hold existing positions, and add on weakness toward $125–132 or on evidence that Telford has stabilized and GAAP margins are expanding.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: multiple macro-rate claims used to support the "rate assumption already breached" pillar of the HOLD (current 10-year at 4.67%, 30-year at highest since 2007, Freddie Mac mortgage at 6.66%, Fed dissents, September-hike odds) are not traceable to the provided sources and have been tagged/removed; the thesis now rests only on the stated 4.0–4.5% conditional as a sensitivity. Recovery-from-low and week-over-week move figures were also overstated (~20% off the June low and ~4% in the past week, not 21% and 10%).
