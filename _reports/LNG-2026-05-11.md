## Executive Summary

Cheniere Energy is the largest U.S. LNG producer and second-largest globally, operating a $48B infrastructure asset base with ~90% of anticipated production contracted under long-term take-or-pay agreements totaling $290.6B in future revenue. The stock has declined 10.9% since our prior BUY recommendation on May 6, driven by a Q1 2026 GAAP net loss of $3.5B from derivative mark-to-market swings — while adjusted EPS of $4.77 beat consensus estimates, and management raised full-year EBITDA guidance to $7.25-7.75B on record cargo volumes (187 in Q1).

The fundamental thesis has strengthened since the prior report: raised EBITDA guidance, record cargo volumes, and the Strait of Hormuz disruption creating incremental demand for U.S. LNG cargoes. The company maintains $8.35B in available liquidity, investment-grade credit ratings (BBB/BBB+), and a $10B buyback program executing at ~$2.5B/year pace. The stock sits below where three C-suite insiders sold in late March — a price level the market has validated as fair-to-cheap given the operational trajectory.

The key material change since the prior report is the Q1 earnings release revealing the magnitude of derivative volatility risk, which has temporarily weighed on sentiment. Operating cash flow generation remains intact, guidance was raised, and the contracted revenue base is unchanged.

## Company Overview

Cheniere operates two LNG export terminals — Sabine Pass (Louisiana) and Corpus Christi (Texas) — with combined nameplate capacity approaching 45+ mtpa and expanding. The business model functions as toll-road infrastructure: customers pay fixed fees plus a percentage of Henry Hub gas prices under 20+ year contracts, with Cheniere retaining ~10% of capacity for spot/optimization sales. Total contracted future revenue stands at $290.6B ($107.7B fixed + $182.9B variable).

The company has exported over 4,610 cumulative cargoes (315M+ tonnes) since first shipment in 2016. Current growth pipeline includes Corpus Christi Stage 3 (7 trains, mostly operational), CCL Midscale Trains 8 & 9 (FID June 2025, $2.9B EPC), plus two larger-scale expansions (SPL ~20 mtpa, CCL ~24 mtpa) awaiting final investment decision.

## Competitive Landscape

Cheniere's competitive position is strengthening:

1. **First-mover infrastructure advantage:** Brownfield expansion leverages existing infrastructure, permitting, and utilities, providing meaningful cost advantages over greenfield competitors. CCL Midscale Trains 8 & 9 secured a $2.9B fixed-price EPC contract with Bechtel, reflecting these efficiencies.

2. **Operational track record:** 4,610+ cargoes without a force majeure. Competitors (Venture Global, Freeport LNG) have experienced startup delays and operational issues that reinforce customer preference for Cheniere.

3. **Qatari supply constraints:** Hormuz disruption has physically constrained Qatar's ability to deliver, creating structural demand diversion to U.S. Gulf Coast facilities that Cheniere is best positioned to capture.

4. **Contract portfolio depth:** ~30 customers under 20+ year take-or-pay agreements, with 90% of anticipated production locked in. New SPAs continue to add at attractive terms.

5. **Regulatory/permitting moat:** Existing operating permits at both terminals with expansion rights approved. New U.S. LNG projects face increasingly complex permitting environments.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Derivative Mark-to-Market Volatility (HIGH):** The IPM agreements create GAAP earnings that are essentially uninterpretable without adjustment. This creates sentiment risk around earnings releases and complicates investor communication.

**2. U.S.-Built Vessel Mandate (MEDIUM-LONG TERM):** The Section 301 requirement (1% by 2029, rising to 15% by 2047) presents implementation challenges given the current lack of U.S.-built LNG vessel capacity. Cheniere's 10-K acknowledges uncertainty around industry impacts. This either gets modified legislatively or creates structural cost increases for the industry. Not an immediate threat but warrants monitoring.

**3. LNG Supply Growth:** ~20 mtpa of new global supply entering in 2025-2026 is transitioning from tight to ample supply. However, with 90% contracted, Cheniere's fixed-fee revenue is largely insulated from spot price declines.

**4. China Demand Risk:** Chinese LNG imports fell 16% YoY in 2025. Management characterizes this as transient; if structural, it reduces global demand growth trajectory.

**5. Geopolitical Reversal Risk:** A diplomatic resolution of the Iran conflict would reduce the premium currently embedded in global LNG prices and potentially unwind Cheniere's near-term spot market advantage.
