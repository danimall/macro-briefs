**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ONE Gas is a 100% regulated natural gas distribution utility serving ~2.3 million customers across Oklahoma, Kansas, and Texas. It is a clean, defensive, single-segment LDC with no merchant or commodity exposure; gas costs pass through to customers at cost, and earnings are driven by rate base, allowed ROE, and recoverable operating expenses. The investment case is straightforward: a well-covered ~3.4% dividend, mid-single-digit rate-base growth funded by ~$800M/year of capex, and a constructive regulatory construct that has improved materially over the past year via legislation in two of its three states.

The offsetting reality is equally straightforward. OGS runs a persistent free-cash-flow deficit (capex plus asset removal of ~$759M in 2025 exceeds operating cash flow of ~$579M) and funds the gap with a blend of commercial paper and continuous equity issuance. Shares outstanding rose from ~55.6M in 2023 to ~62.9M in Q1 2026, roughly +13% over two years. That dilution is the tax on the growth model: per-share value compounds only if rate awards and rate-base growth outrun share count. Achieved ROE of ~7.7% chronically trails the ~9.4-9.8% authorized range, and a higher-for-longer rate environment raises the cost of the external funding this model depends on.

The stock at $80.53 trades at ~16.5x current-year consensus EPS ($4.87), roughly in line with the gas-utility peer median of 16.1x, with a yield (~3.4%) matching the peer median (3.4%) but below the highest-yielding names in the group (UGI 4.3%, SR 4.1%). Estimate revisions are quietly positive (+8.9% current-quarter, +2.2% current-year over 90 days), the two legislative tailwinds are underappreciated, and the analyst consensus target ($91.6 mean, $89 median) sits ~14% above spot. This is a fair-to-slightly-cheap defensive compounder with a decent yield and a modest re-rating catalyst path, but no asymmetric upside. We maintain HOLD (income-oriented), consistent with the prior four reports, with the score essentially flat at 5.9.

## Company Overview

ONE Gas was spun off from ONEOK in January 2014 and operates as a pure-play local gas distribution company through three divisions:

- **Oklahoma Natural Gas** — the largest LDC in Oklahoma (~89% of the state's gas distribution customers), ~939K customers, ~$2.45B rate base, authorized ROE 9.4% with an 8.9-9.9% dead-band under the streamlined PBRC mechanism.
- **Kansas Gas Service** — the largest in Kansas (~72%), ~660K customers, ~$1.47B rate base, authorized ROE ~9.6%, with the GSRS infrastructure surcharge bridging rate cases.
- **Texas Gas Service** — the third largest in Texas (~13%), ~716K customers (Austin, El Paso), ~$1.89B rate base, authorized ROE 9.8% at a 60% equity ratio, with GRIP annual capital-recovery filings. All Texas service areas consolidated into one statewide division effective January 2026.

The business is a regulated monopoly within its service territory. The only competition is fuel-source substitution (electricity, heat pumps, building electrification) decided at the point of appliance installation, which makes it a slow-burn secular risk rather than a near-term one. Revenue mix is heavily residential with substantial fixed-charge revenue (91% of Oklahoma non-gas revenue is fixed-charge, 52% Kansas, 66% Texas), and weather-normalization adjustment (WNA) mechanisms in all three states dampen weather-driven demand variability.

## Financial Analysis

Reported revenue is a noisy figure because it includes pass-through gas costs; operating income and net income are the clean gauges. FY2025 revenue rose 17% to $2.43B, almost entirely on higher gas costs recovered from customers. The relevant numbers:

- **FY2025 net income $264.2M ($4.37 diluted EPS)** vs. $222.9M ($3.91) in 2024 and $231.2M ($4.14) in 2023, a +19% earnings year.
- **Operating income grew to $457.5M from $399.0M**, driven by +$116M from new rates plus modest customer growth (~23,000 net connections/year, ~+0.6%), partly offset by higher D&A, employee costs, and ad-valorem taxes.
- **Q1 2026 net income $128.7M ($2.04 diluted EPS)** vs. $119.4M ($1.98) in Q1 2025 (+8%); adjusted EPS $2.11. New rates added +$27.3M, partly offset by weather 24.6% warmer YoY and higher employee costs.

Margins and returns: operating margin ~18.8%, net margin ~10.9%, ROE ~7.7%, ROA ~3.0%. The ROE gap versus the authorized range is the defining structural feature: regulatory lag on a heavy capex-growth utility.

On the balance sheet, the surface signals look alarming and are misleading. The Altman Z-Score of 1.14 ("distress zone") and current ratio of 0.6 are artifacts of the regulated-utility model: short-term commercial paper (~$737-760M classified as current) drives negative working capital, and high but stable leverage (D/E 2.57) is normal for an LDC. The accurate read is management's covenant metric — total debt-to-capital of 47.1% at Q1 2026, well below the 70% credit-facility covenant — and the credit ratings: **Moody's A3 / S&P A-, both Stable**. Long-term debt-to-cap is 40.3%. The $2.15B of senior notes have no maturities within the next year (nearest is $550M at 5.10% due April 2029); a $250M variable-rate term loan matures September 2026 (reset to 4.48%). Liquidity is ample: a $1.5B undrawn revolver extended to October 2030 and a $1.5B CP program.

Cash flow is where the model's tension lives. FY2025 operating cash flow was $578.8M against $707M of capex (~$759.5M including asset removal), with ~$800M of capex planned for 2026. The company funds the persistent gap with a balanced mix of CP and equity; it settled 2.63M shares for $205M in 2025 and runs an active $225M ATM program. The dividend is $0.68/quarter ($2.72 annualized), yielding ~3.4%, at a ~62% payout of 2025 EPS: sustainable and consistent with peers. A one-time $64.3M IRS refund received in April 2026 (Uri securitization tax deferral) provided a modest working-capital boost.

## Growth Analysis

The growth algorithm for a regulated LDC is: rate-base growth + closing the ROE gap, minus dilution. On rate base, OGS is investing ~$800M/year against a combined rate base of roughly $5.8B, implying mid-single-digit annual rate-base growth. Customer growth is steady but modest at ~0.6%/year, supported by Sun Belt/Plains population and economic trends.

On the ROE-gap side, two pieces of legislation directly attack the company's biggest structural drag:

- **Texas HB 4384** (enacted June 2025; RRC implementing rule adopted February 24, 2026) lets Texas gas utilities defer and recover depreciation, ad-valorem taxes, and a carrying cost on plant placed in service but not yet in rates. This shrinks the interval between spending capital and earning on it.
- **Kansas HB 2435** (signed April 2026, effective July 1, 2026) expands GSRS-eligible investments to essentially all utility plant and raises the maximum monthly residential surcharge from $0.80 to $1.35, improving Kansas capital recovery.

Both mechanisms should help narrow the achieved-vs-authorized ROE gap over the next several years, the single most important lever for per-share earnings growth given the dilution rate. Estimate revisions confirm the direction: current-year consensus is up +2.2% over 90 days to $4.87, next-year up +0.9% to $5.02, and near-term quarters have risen more sharply (+8.9% current-quarter, +10.9% next-quarter). These are moderate revisions that do not clear the +30%/90-day threshold that would signal a re-rating catalyst; read them as steady positive momentum rather than a step-change.

A modest incremental demand vector: a ~$120M, 43-mile southeast Oklahoma pipeline announced December 2025 will deliver 100+ Bcf/year, including to Western Farmers Electric Cooperative's gas-fired Hugo plant, online expected Q3 2028. Gas-fired power generation tied to grid-reliability and data-center demand is a small but real new rate-base and volume vector.

The case for OGS being modestly cheap rather than fairly valued rests on three points. First, the two legislative tailwinds are not fully reflected in consensus, which still models flat-to-negative long-term ROE-gap closure. Second, OGS has structurally underperformed its utility peer group on total shareholder return (cumulative TSR of 119.7 versus a peer group at 158.7) despite growing EPS every year, which suggests a relative discount that could partially close as the lag-reduction benefits show up in reported ROE. Third, the analyst consensus target ($91.6 mean, $89 median, $85-103 range) sits ~14% above spot, and sentiment has been improving (a hold was upgraded to buy in the most recent revision cycle).

I am not treating the ~$80 level as a value opportunity in the way I would a stock with steep systematic underestimation. The estimate revisions here are moderate, not the >30%/90-day velocity that flags such mispricing, and the multiple already prices in a normal LDC. This is a defensive compounder priced roughly fairly, with a plausible modest re-rating path if the ROE gap closes. A high valuation score here would require a specific mispricing I can defend; the honest read is fair value with mild upside optionality, which supports a middling score rather than a strong one.

## Competitive Landscape

OGS's competitive position is stable, and its regulatory footprint is a genuine relative advantage. As a pure-play LDC in Oklahoma, Kansas, and Texas, it faces far less anti-gas policy pressure than California or Northeast LDCs, and the 2025-26 legislation in Texas and Kansas represents a positive divergence from the tightening regulatory environments many coastal utilities face. It has no unregulated or merchant exposure, no single customer above 10% of revenue, and a diversified 2.3M-customer base.

The trajectory is best described as stable-to-modestly-improving rather than clearly improving. Customer growth is steady but low; the principal secular headwind — building electrification and heat-pump substitution — is slow-burn but real and explicitly flagged in the 10-K. The prior report cut competitive position from 6.5 to 6.0, reflecting the recognition that the electrification threat and the persistent ROE-under-earning are structural rather than transient. I hold that view: the moat is durable (regulated monopoly), but the trajectory is flat, and the achieved-vs-authorized ROE gap means the franchise is not fully monetizing its own rate base.

## Risk Assessment

The company-specific risks, beyond generic utility boilerplate:

1. **Regulatory lag and recovery risk.** The entire earnings model depends on regulators approving rate increases to recover the annual capex. The 2025 Texas case is illustrative: TGS requested $41.1M at 10.4% ROE but the outcome was ~$14.5M at 9.8% ROE. Achieved ROE chronically trails authorized. The HB 4384/HB 2435 tailwinds mitigate but do not eliminate this.
2. **Persistent negative free cash flow → dilution and refinancing dependence.** Sustained capex forces continuous equity and debt issuance. A higher-for-longer rate path directly raises the cost of this model, and equity issuance dilutes per-share growth. This is the crux of the bear case.
3. **Interest-rate sensitivity.** The CP balance (4.14% weighted average) and the variable-rate term loan expose earnings to rate moves. Falling short rates were a 2025-26 tailwind (the term loan reset from 4.96% to 4.48%); the reverse would pressure earnings.
4. **Electrification / fuel-switching.** A slow secular threat to customer growth and volumes, muted by the Sun Belt/Plains footprint.
5. **Weather / seasonality.** WNA mechanisms don't cover the full year (except Kansas); the warm Q1 2026 was a direct volume drag.
6. **Legacy tail-risk reminders.** Winter Storm Uri Kansas costs were securitized (KGSS-I bonds, $242.5M outstanding, non-recourse to OGS, final maturity 2032), largely resolved but a reminder of extreme-weather/gas-price tail risk. 12 former manufactured-gas-plant sites in Kansas plus one in Texas carry open-ended environmental exposure (AAO recovery cap raised to $32M in 2025; reserve ~$13.2M).

None of these are binary events with large asymmetric downside; they are chronic, priced features of the business. The risk score reflects a business with few catastrophic idiosyncratic risks but a genuine structural funding/dilution drag that caps the score from moving to the high end.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 26.8% versus 1-year historical volatility of 17.8%, a rich 9.1pp spread, and the term structure is in backwardation (26.8% at 1 month vs. 19.2% at 6 months), signaling the market is pricing imminent stress, most likely tied to the next earnings print and the rate-sensitivity of the group. Put/call skew is elevated at 5.4% (higher put demand), and ATM IV has risen from 21.5% to 26.8% since late May. This is consistent with a defensive, rate-sensitive utility ahead of an earnings date rather than a signal of company-specific deterioration; the fundamentals are intact and estimate revisions are positive. The elevated near-term IV argues for patience on any incremental purchase, as the event premium tends to crush after the print.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$95-103):** The two legislative tailwinds (Texas HB 4384, Kansas HB 2435) meaningfully close the achieved-vs-authorized ROE gap over the next 2-3 years, lifting earnings power toward the authorized range and driving EPS growth above the consensus trajectory. The relative-TSR discount to peers partially closes as reported ROE improves, re-rating the stock toward the high end of the analyst range ($103). Falling short rates reduce CP and term-loan costs, easing the funding model. The Hugo pipeline and gas-fired generation demand add incremental rate base. Investors capture ~18-28% upside plus a ~3.4% yield.

**Bear Case (~$68-72):** A higher-for-longer rate path raises the cost of the ATM/CP funding treadmill, dilution continues, and rate awards keep landing well below requests (as in the 2025 Texas case), so achieved ROE stays stuck near 7.7%. Warm winters and electrification erode volume growth. The stock de-rates toward the low end of the peer range and revisits its 52-week low ($71.7), a ~11-15% loss before dividends. This is the more probable downside given the demonstrated pattern of below-request rate awards and the sensitivity of the entire utility group to the rate path.

**Base Case (~$89):** Rate-base compounds mid-single-digits, the legislative tailwinds modestly narrow the ROE gap, dilution partially offsets the gains, and the stock re-rates toward the consensus median target of ~$89 over 12 months as sentiment improves. Roughly ~11% upside plus ~3.4% yield, a total return in the mid-teens, appropriate for a defensive utility but not asymmetric.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income-oriented).** This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the score is essentially flat at 5.9 versus 6.0 prior; the trajectory has been gently improving (growth prospects +0.8 and financial health +0.5 over the sequence, offset by competitive position -0.5). The improving legislative backdrop and positive estimate revisions confirm the modest upward drift, but nothing in the current data supports upgrading to BUY: the multiple is at the peer median, the revisions are moderate, and the dilution/ROE-gap structural drag caps the appreciation case.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Reported ROE improves toward ~8.5%+ (from ~7.7%) over two consecutive rate cycles, confirming HB 4384/HB 2435 are closing the lag gap faster than consensus models.
2. A pullback toward the low-$70s (near the 52-week low of $71.7) that pushes the yield toward ~3.8% and the forward P/E below ~14.5x on unchanged fundamentals, a clearer valuation entry.
3. A sustained decline in short rates that materially reduces the cost of the CP/term-loan funding stack and eases dilution needs.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A dividend cut or payout ratio breaching ~75%, signaling the FCF deficit is straining shareholder returns.
2. A structural step-up in dilution without commensurate rate-base or ROE gains, so per-share value stops compounding.
3. A regulatory reversal: an adverse rate order well below the below-request norm, or repeal/dilution of the Texas/Kansas lag-reduction statutes.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the near-term IV backwardation is pricing this event), and the Kansas HB 2435 first-year GSRS filings following the July 1, 2026 effective date, which will provide early evidence on whether the lag-reduction benefits are materializing in reported results.

## Conclusion

ONE Gas is a well-run, investment-grade, pure-play gas LDC with a durable regulated-monopoly franchise, a well-covered ~3.4% dividend, and an improving regulatory backdrop that is quietly narrowing its biggest structural earnings drag. The Altman Z-Score "distress" flag is a false positive; the A3/A- ratings, 47% debt-to-cap, and undrawn $1.5B revolver describe solid financial health. The offsetting reality is a capital-hungry model that runs a persistent free-cash-flow deficit, funds it with continuous equity dilution, and earns a ROE well below authorized, with a higher-for-longer rate path raising the cost of that funding.

At the peer-median multiple, the stock is fairly valued with mild re-rating optionality (consensus target ~14% above spot on the mean) and a decent yield. This is worth owning for income and steady mid-single-digit compounding, but it does not offer the asymmetric upside that would warrant a BUY. We maintain **HOLD** with an income rationale, and would turn more constructive on a pullback toward the low-$70s or on confirmed acceleration in reported ROE as the Texas and Kansas legislative tailwinds work through the rate base.
