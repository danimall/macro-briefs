**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Descartes operates one of the highest-quality business models in logistics software: the Global Logistics Network (GLN), a cloud platform connecting shippers, carriers, and logistics providers, with roughly 93% of revenue recurring, ~77-78% gross margins, and a debt-free balance sheet holding $377M in cash. FY26 (year ended January 31, 2026) delivered $729.0M revenue (+12% YoY), $163.8M net income (+14%), and $266.2M operating cash flow (+21%, exceeding net income, a hallmark of high-quality earnings). Q1 FY27 (ended April 30, 2026) extended the trend with record revenue of $193.6M (+15%), net income +34%, and a 46% adjusted EBITDA margin. This is a genuine compounder with operating leverage, a moat built on network effects and regulatory domain depth, and a self-funding serial-acquirer model.

The investment question is price versus a live, measurable headwind. The stock has fallen ~33% from its 52-week high of $115.88 to ~$77.61. At that level it trades at roughly 28x forward FY27 consensus EPS ($2.73) and ~24x FY28 ($3.24) for a business growing 12-15%, a PEG near 1.7. That multiple prices continued flawless execution and leaves little room for the central risk in the model: a meaningful share of revenue is transactional (per-shipment), and global freight volumes are softening. Descartes' own container-import index showed US imports down 5.5% in April 2026 amid trade-policy disruption. Management explicitly assumes shipment volumes hold "generally consistent with historical levels," an assumption the early data is challenging.

I rate DSGX a **HOLD** (growth_hold). The thesis prevents an upgrade to BUY for a concrete reason: the bear-case downside from a volume-driven deceleration is 20-25% (toward the high-$50s/low-$60s), which exceeds the cushion I require for a high-conviction long when the catalyst that would re-rate the stock (re-acceleration of freight volumes or a step-up in estimate revisions) is not visible within the next two quarters. The "beat but sold off" reaction to Q1 (shares fell ~4.5% on a clean beat) confirms the stock is priced for perfection, and near-term options are in backwardation with rich implied vol and elevated put skew, signaling the market is pricing imminent stress.

## Company Overview

Descartes is a Waterloo, Ontario–based logistics and supply-chain software company. It files as a Canadian foreign private issuer (Form 40-F annual, Form 6-K interim) rather than 10-K/10-Q/8-K, reports in US dollars under US GAAP, and runs a fiscal year ending January 31. Insider transactions are filed on Canada's SEDI system, so US Form 4 data is unavailable; the absence of US filings is a structural artifact, not a red flag.

The GLN connects participants across the supply chain and delivers solutions spanning customs and regulatory compliance (electronic import/export filing, sanctioned-party screening), global trade intelligence (HS classification, tariff/duty data), routing and last-mile delivery optimization, transportation rating and freight management, and, via recent acquisitions, ecommerce demand forecasting (OrderMine, March 2026) and driver safety/fleet performance (Idelic, April 2026). The moat is rooted in data density on the network, deep regulatory expertise, and embeddedness in customers' systems of record, which produces high switching costs. The strategy is a consolidation playbook: acquire niche logistics and compliance software assets and integrate them into the GLN. Eight acquisitions have closed since the start of FY25.

## Financial Analysis

Descartes is a consistent double-digit grower with expanding margins.

**Revenue and profitability:**
- FY26 revenue $729.0M, +12% YoY (FY25 $651M); services (recurring) revenue grew 15% to $677.2M, improving recurring mix
- FY26 operating income $210.0M (+16%); net income $163.8M (+14%); net margin steady at 22%
- FY26 diluted EPS $1.87 (+14% from $1.64); adjusted EBITDA $329.5M at a 45% margin
- Q1 FY27 revenue $193.6M (+15%, record), beating consensus by ~$2.25M; net income $48.5M (+34%); net margin 25%; adjusted EBITDA $89.8M at a 46% margin; operating cash flow $75.1M (+40%)

**Balance sheet and cash flow:**
- Cash $377.0M at April 30, 2026; essentially debt-free (~$0.2M/quarter interest expense)
- FY26 operating cash flow $266.2M (+21%), exceeding net income; clean earnings quality with no accrual concern
- Capex negligible (~$5.7M/yr), an asset-light model
- Total assets $1.92B, of which goodwill ($1.03B) plus intangibles ($0.33B) equal ~71%, the structural offset to the clean balance sheet, carrying impairment exposure if growth or market cap rolls over
- Stockholders' equity $1.63B; total liabilities only $0.29B

**Capital allocation:** Acquisitions are self-funded from cash flow ($151.6M deployed in FY26). A Normal Course Issuer Bid (buyback) for up to ~8.6M shares (~10% of float) was launched December 11, 2025, with ~$20.8M repurchased in Q1 FY27. No dividend.

The earnings surprise record supports confidence in near-term consensus: three beats and one small miss over the last four quarters, with beats of 4.7%, 5.5%, and 7.3% against one -1.8% miss. This is a company that delivers within or modestly above guidance.

Financial Health scores high (8.5). Debt-free, cash-rich, ~46% adjusted EBITDA margin, OCF above net income, minimal capex. The only blemish is the goodwill/intangible concentration inherent to the acquisitive model.

## Growth Analysis

Growth has two structural legs and one cyclical swing factor. The structural legs: (1) recurring revenue compounding at ~15%, faster than headline, on retention and upsell across the GLN; and (2) the serial-acquirer engine, which converts cash flow into new revenue streams (OrderMine, Idelic) at tuck-in prices. The cyclical swing factor is shipment volume.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current-year EPS +4.7% over 90 days, next-year +7.5% over 90 days, with 30-day revisions flat. This is healthy, but it is not the +30% revision velocity that signals a genuine earnings inflection. Consensus is grinding higher, not lurching. Revenue growth of 12-15% is solid for a mature SaaS compounder but does not, on its own, justify a premium re-rating from here.

The tariff/trade-policy regime is the dominant dynamic. Trade complexity, sanctions, and protectionism drive demand for customs, compliance, and trade-intelligence products, a tailwind management repeatedly highlights, and so far the winning effect (FY26 +12%, Q1 FY27 +15%). The same dynamics suppress freight volumes, which pressures the transactional portion of revenue. The April container-import decline is the first concrete sign the headwind side is gaining force. If global volumes deteriorate through 2026, transactional revenue decelerates and the structural recurring growth has to carry more of the load, which would slow the headline rate below the 12-15% the multiple assumes.

Growth Prospects score 6.5: durable structural growth, but moderate in magnitude with a live cyclical drag and revision velocity well short of an inflection signal.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$77.61, DSGX trades at roughly 28x forward FY27 EPS and ~24x FY28. On trailing FY26 EPS of $1.87 the multiple is ~41x. Against 12-15% growth, the forward PEG is roughly 1.6-1.9: full, not cheap.

The relevant peer is Manhattan Associates (MANH), a higher-multiple supply-chain software name, alongside SPS Commerce and E2open. Descartes' margin profile (~46% adjusted EBITDA) is best-in-class and supports a premium to the broader software median, but a ~28x forward multiple for low-teens growth does not embed a discount; it embeds continued execution. The 33% drawdown from the high has compressed the multiple from the high-$30s/low-$40s forward range, which improves the entry relative to mid-2025, but it has not created a clear mispricing; the multiple compressed in step with rising recognition of the volume risk.

Analyst targets sit above spot (low $82, median $95, mean $100.8, high $126), and on inflection stocks consensus targets often lag. Here, though, the estimate-revision velocity is modest and the multiple is full, so I treat these targets as a reasonable upside reference rather than a stale floor that consensus will chase. The C$126 TSX target referenced by the sell-side reflects a constructive but not aggressive view.

The case against a high valuation score is specific: there is no identifiable catalyst within 60 days (next earnings is roughly September), the freight-volume headwind is actively materializing, and the "beat but sold off" reaction to Q1 demonstrates that even clean beats are not re-rating the stock. A low trailing P/E is not present here, and a full forward multiple with a live downside risk does not offer asymmetric upside. Valuation scores 4.5.

## Competitive Landscape

Descartes' differentiation is the combination of a logistics network and regulatory/customs data depth, distinct from pure WMS/TMS functionality offered by Manhattan, SPS, E2open, and segments of Oracle and SAP. Network effects (more participants → more data → better decisioning) and embeddedness in compliance and execution workflows produce high switching costs. The current trade environment of tariffs, sanctions, reshoring, and route volatility is structurally favorable for the compliance and trade-intelligence franchise, the part of the portfolio least exposed to volume softness and most exposed to complexity. Management is positioning the GLN as the data backbone for AI-driven logistics decisioning, and the OrderMine and Idelic acquisitions extend the platform into AI-adjacent niches.

Competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving on the compliance side and stable on transportation/freight management. There is no evidence of share loss or moat compression. Competitive Position scores 7.0: a durable moat with a favorable regulatory tailwind, tempered by the fact that the transactional franchise rides the freight cycle rather than escaping it.

## Risk Assessment

**Shipment-volume sensitivity (the central tension).** A meaningful slice of revenue is per-shipment. The April container-import decline is a direct read on the risk that management's "historical volume" assumption is too optimistic for 2026. A sustained volume contraction would decelerate transactional revenue and pressure the multiple.

**Tariff/trade-policy double edge.** The same regime that sells compliance software suppresses freight volumes. The net effect is genuinely uncertain; management frames it as "uncertainty" cutting both ways.

**Acquisition and integration risk.** The model requires continuous deal sourcing, pricing, and integration. Goodwill plus intangibles at ~71% of assets create impairment exposure tied to growth and market cap; management explicitly flags this.

**Geopolitical disruption.** Management cites the Iran/Israel/US conflict and Russia-Ukraine as potential drags on volumes and demand.

**Cybersecurity and network reliability.** As a mission-critical network handling regulatory filings, a breach or outage is a top-tier operational risk.

**CFO transition.** Edward Gardner succeeded long-time CFO Allan Brett effective March 12, 2026 (Brett remains an advisor), an orderly planned succession, not an abrupt departure. FY26 operating cash flow absorbed $6.5M in personnel-departure amounts, immaterial to the cash engine but worth monitoring.

Risk Factors score 5.0: the volume sensitivity is concrete and currently materializing, and the full multiple amplifies the downside if it persists. These are real, near-term, and not fully priced at 28x forward.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$100-110).** Freight volumes stabilize, the compliance/trade-intelligence franchise continues outgrowing the transactional side, recurring revenue compounds at ~15%, and the M&A engine adds incremental growth. Estimate-revision velocity steps up toward double digits, consensus targets pull the stock toward the mean. At ~32-34x FY28 EPS, the stock reaches ~$105-110. This requires the volume headwind to prove transitory and the premium multiple to hold.

**Bear case (~$55, ~25-30% downside).** The April container-import decline broadens into a sustained 2026 volume contraction. Transactional revenue decelerates, headline growth slips toward high single digits, and the market de-rates a 12-15% grower with a cyclical swing factor from 28x to ~20x forward. At ~20x FY27 EPS, the stock falls to ~$55. A goodwill/intangible impairment, while not a base case, would compound the de-rating.

**Base case (~$80-85).** Volumes soften modestly but recurring growth holds the headline at ~11-13%, EPS lands near consensus, and the multiple stays full but stable. The stock grinds in the high-$70s to mid-$80s, roughly in line with the analyst low target of $82.

The asymmetry here is unfavorable for a high-conviction long: the bear case (~-25-30%) is larger than the realistic 12-month upside to the base case (~+5-10%), and the bull case requires the volume risk to resolve favorably with no catalyst to force that resolution before September earnings.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth orientation: a high-quality compounder I would own at a better entry, not chase at a full multiple into a live headwind. Reassessment point: Q2 FY27 earnings (~September 2026), and monthly readings of Descartes' own container-import index.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Freight-volume stabilization: Descartes' container-import index returns to flat-to-positive YoY for two consecutive monthly readings, removing the transactional-revenue overhang.
2. Estimate-revision acceleration: current-year EPS revisions step up above +10% over 90 days alongside maintained 15%+ services growth.
3. A better entry: the stock pulls back toward the low-$60s (~20x forward FY27), compressing the bear-case downside below 15% and improving risk/reward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A sustained volume contraction: container imports down >5% YoY for three-plus consecutive months, with management lowering the volume assumption.
2. Services (recurring) revenue growth decelerates below 10% YoY, signaling the structural leg can no longer offset transactional softness.
3. A goodwill or intangible impairment charge, or a materially overpriced/poorly-integrated acquisition.

## Conclusion

Descartes is a best-in-class logistics SaaS franchise: debt-free, cash-rich, ~46% adjusted EBITDA margins, 93% recurring revenue, operating cash flow above net income, and a self-funding M&A engine with a new buyback lever. None of that is in question. The recommendation rests on price against a specific, falsifiable risk: at ~28x forward FY27 EPS for 12-15% growth, the multiple prices continued flawless execution while transactional revenue tied to freight volumes is actively deteriorating. The bear-case downside is 25-30%, the realistic 12-month upside to base case is ~5-10%, and there is no catalyst within 60 days to force a favorable resolution. The "beat but sold off" Q1 reaction and the backwardated, rich-vol options structure both confirm the stock is priced for perfection. I rate DSGX a **HOLD** and would become a buyer either on freight-volume stabilization or on a pullback toward the low-$60s that restores the margin of safety.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the bear case at ~$55 implies ~25-30% downside from $77.61 (not the 20-25% repeatedly stated), which widens the unfavorable asymmetry the HOLD rests on.
