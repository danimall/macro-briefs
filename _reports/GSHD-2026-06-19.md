## Executive Summary

Goosehead Insurance is a technology-enabled personal-lines P&C brokerage that places policies through a dual-channel model (company-owned corporate agencies plus a franchise network) and earns commissions, agency fees, and franchise royalties without underwriting risk. The economics are attractive on paper: an asset-light, capex-light model with a renewal royalty step-up (20% of new-business commissions rising to 50% on renewals) that builds an annuity-like recurring revenue tail. Q1 2026 produced headline revenue of $93.1M (+23%), adjusted EBITDA of $24.4M (+57%) at a 26% margin, and operating cash flow of $22.9M (+48%). The franchise flywheel and operating leverage are real.

The stock has fallen 65% from its 52-week high of $106.84 to $36.88, and the decline coincides with three genuine fundamental yellow flags rather than a simple sentiment de-rating: operating franchises fell 13% year-over-year to 956 with onboarding nearly halved, Net Promoter Score dropped 15 points to 72, and the headline growth was meaningfully flattered by $10.7M of volatile contingent commissions (against $4.5M a year prior) that management itself describes as unpredictable and lower-quality. Stripping those out, core revenue grew 15% and total written premium grew 13% — still good, but a different growth trajectory than the +23% headline implies. On top of this sits an engineered-negative-equity balance sheet (-$204M total equity) and ~$324M of variable-rate debt from a January 2025 leveraged recap that funded special distributions and buybacks.

The single most compelling signal is the May 2026 open-market insider-buying cluster: the brand-new CEO, President/COO, new CFO, new General Counsel, and at least one director all bought in the open market near today's price, immediately after a full leadership transition. That is a rare, high-conviction tell that floors my downside view. But it is set against a falling franchise base, deteriorating client satisfaction, and a Financials-sector backdrop where broker selection has been a persistent drag. The balance of evidence supports a HOLD: the risk/reward has improved with the price decline, but the fundamental questions (franchise count, NPS, durable core growth) are unresolved, and the leverage adds a layer of fragility that a pure brokerage would not carry.

## Company Overview

Goosehead operates a personal-lines P&C distribution platform headquartered in Westlake, Texas. It does not bear underwriting risk; it places home, auto, umbrella, and flood policies with third-party carriers and collects commissions and fees. Revenue flows through two channels:

- **Corporate channel** — 17 company-owned locations staffed with 482 W-2 sales agents (+13% y/y). Higher cost, fully controlled, the channel management is now leaning into.
- **Franchise channel** — the larger engine (~82% of total written premium). Goosehead recruits independent franchisees, provides carrier appointments, Salesforce-based technology, training, and service-center support, and collects royalties (20% on new business, 50% on renewals) plus amortized initial franchise fees over a 10-year term.

The economic flywheel is the renewal step-up: new business is lower-margin and front-loaded with acquisition cost, while renewals are high-margin and predictable. As the in-force book ages, the royalty share more than doubles and renewal commissions compound. Total written premium — the leading indicator — reached $1.13B in Q1 2026 (+13% y/y), policies in force hit 2.0M (+14%), and client retention sits near 85%. The model's differentiation in a fragmented personal-lines market is genuine, but the growth engine is visibly shifting from franchise-unit expansion toward corporate-agent productivity and technology efficiency.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 2026 results (vs. Q1 2025):**
- Total revenue: $93.1M (+23%)
- Core revenue (excludes volatile contingents): $79.5M (+15%)
- Net income: $8.0M vs. $2.6M
- Adjusted EBITDA: $24.4M (+57%); margin expanded to 26% from 21%
- Diluted EPS: $0.19; Adjusted EPS: $0.37
- Operating cash flow: $22.9M (+48%)

The revenue-quality story matters here. Contingent commissions jumped to $10.7M from $4.5M, a +$6.2M swing, of which $7.6M came from prior-period estimates being released as uncertainty resolved. Management explicitly flags these as weather/underwriting-dependent and lower-quality. The durable run-rate is better captured by core revenue and total written premium. Operating leverage is real where it counts — employee compensation grew only 5% against 23% revenue growth — but G&A rose 37% ($17.6M → $24.0M) on professional services and AI/technology spend, plus a sharp increase in capitalized software development (+$2.9M). The margin expansion is genuine but should not be extrapolated linearly given the contingent-commission flattering and rising G&A.

**Balance sheet — the central nuance:**
- Total equity is -$204M (stockholders' equity -$121M, NCI -$83M). This is engineered, not distress: the Up-C holding-company structure plus a January 2025 leveraged recap (a $300M term loan funding a $175M LLC special distribution and a $5.91/share / $146M special dividend). Any automated screen reading negative book equity as "distressed" is wrong for an asset-light brokerage with strong operating cash flow.
- Debt: $323.8M total ($297.7M Term B-1 at SOFR+3.00%, maturing January 2032 with a $280.5M balloon; $26M revolver drawn). Against ~$95M+ annualized adjusted EBITDA, leverage is roughly 3x versus a 5.0x covenant max — manageable but materially higher than the company's pre-2025 profile.
- Cash of just $25.7M, down from $34.4M at year-end 2025 and $70M a year ago, after $50.3M of Q1 buybacks.

The debt is collateralized by substantially all assets, including rights to future commissions and royalties. The variable-rate structure means interest expense (~$5.5M/quarter, ~$22M+/year) is directly exposed to the higher-for-longer rate path. This is the key structural fragility distinguishing GSHD from an unlevered broker peer.

## Growth Analysis

The underlying growth is healthy but decelerating from the explosive prints that supported a $100+ stock. Policies in force, total written premium, and corporate sales agents (each +13-14%) all point to durable mid-teens compounding. Management reiterated full-year 2026 organic revenue growth guidance of 10-19% on the Q1 call, with a constructive tone.

The complication is the composition of growth. Franchise count fell 13% y/y to 956 (from 1,098), and only 20 franchises were onboarded in Q1 2026 versus 36 a year earlier. Management frames terminations and transfers positively — they accelerate franchise-fee recognition — but a shrinking unit base changes the long-term narrative for a model built on franchise expansion. The strategic pivot toward corporate-agent productivity and AI-driven efficiency is rational maturation, but it is unproven as a sustained growth substitute and carries higher fixed cost.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than inflecting. Current-year consensus rose +6.5% over 90 days to $2.20 and next-year to $2.77 (+4.1%), but near-term quarters slipped slightly (-2.0% current quarter, -2.8% next quarter over 90 days). This does not clear the high bar of a strong inflection signal (the +30% revision velocity that would force a more aggressive growth read is absent). The earnings beat record is mixed — 2 beats and 2 misses over the last four quarters, with the most recent +79% surprise itself driven by the contingent-commission swing rather than core operations. I weight core revenue and written premium over the headline and treat the growth thesis as solid but no longer accelerating.

## Valuation Assessment

At $36.88, GSHD trades at roughly 32x trailing EPS of $1.04 and about 16.8x the current-year consensus of $2.20 (13.3x next-year $2.77). The trailing multiple overstates richness — the trailing EPS is partly suppressed by the recap-driven interest expense and one-time items, and forward earnings power on consensus is the better anchor.

Against peers, the picture is mixed. Insurance-broker peer median P/E is ~20.7, with the higher-growth, higher-quality names trading well above: AJG at 35.7x, RYAN at 44.5x, BRO at 19.2x, WTW at 15.2x. On a forward basis GSHD's ~17x current-year multiple is below the premium-growth cohort and roughly in line with the median, despite a faster organic growth rate (mid-teens core) than most. On growth-adjusted terms the stock is not expensive — PEG on next-year estimates is near 1.0.

The reason I do not score this higher: the 65% drawdown is not a pure mispricing I can dismiss. Sophisticated holders sold a stock that fell from $107 to the mid-$30s, and the fundamental yellow flags (franchise count, NPS, leverage) give them a coherent reason. The analyst target range of $44 (low) to $100 (high), with a $62.50 median, implies meaningful upside if the franchise base and core growth stabilize, and the $44 low target sits ~19% above the current price. But on a stock down this much, the burden of proof is on identifying what the market is missing, and here the market is at least partly right: the growth has slowed and the balance sheet is now levered. The valuation is fair-to-attractive on forward fundamentals but does not offer the kind of asymmetric, catalyst-backed mispricing that would justify a high score. I treat the cheapness with appropriate skepticism rather than as a standalone buy trigger.

## Competitive Landscape

Goosehead competes in highly fragmented personal-lines distribution against captive agents, direct-to-consumer carriers (GEICO/Progressive-style), independent agents, insurtech entrants, and PE-backed brokerage rollups. The franchise model and renewal royalty step-up are genuine differentiators, and the brand carries referral value. But the competitive trajectory is more stable-to-eroding than improving:

- **NPS fell from 87 to 72** — a 15-point drop in a brand-and-referral-dependent business is a real signal of competitive friction or service strain, and it directly contradicts the retention story (premium retention also slipped to 89% from 90%).
- **Declining franchise count** indicates the network-expansion moat is narrowing even as policies in force grow, meaning growth is being carried by fewer, more productive units and the corporate channel.
- **Moderating P&C rate increases** are double-edged: they improve client retention but slow commission growth from rate, removing a tailwind that flattered prior years.
- **AI/digital disintermediation** is a live structural risk; the company's response (heavy internal AI spend) is driving the G&A increase, a cost the market is funding with no proven payback yet.

The company is maturing from a unit-expansion story to a productivity story. That can work, but trajectory matters more than level, and the franchise-count and NPS trends point sideways-to-down rather than up.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are above-average for a broker, which is why this scores below neutral:

1. **Engineered leverage and refinancing risk.** The variable-rate (SOFR+300bps) debt with a $280.5M balloon in January 2032 is collateralized by future commissions and royalties. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, interest expense and 2032 refinancing terms are live concerns.

2. **Falling franchise count and onboarding.** The clearest fundamental challenge underneath the headline growth.

3. **NPS deterioration.** A leading indicator for a referral-dependent model; if it persists, retention and new-business lead flow are at risk.

4. **Carrier concentration.** Two carriers represented 19% and 13% of 2025 revenue (32% combined). Catastrophe-driven carrier capacity squeezes (California wildfires, Texas floods cited) can cut placement and whipsaw contingent commissions.

5. **Housing/rate sensitivity.** Homeowner/dwelling lines are ~63% of premiums; a majority of new accounts come from home-closing referral partners. Elevated mortgage rates suppress home-purchase lead flow, a direct channel from the macro rate path into new-business growth.

6. **Up-C / TRA / founder-control structure.** A $171.9M TRA liability owed to pre-IPO members competes for cash with taxes, debt service, and dividends — all dependent on LLC distributions the credit agreement restricts. A loss of the Jones family's >50% voting control triggers a change-of-control default under the credit agreement, tying debt stability to founder ownership.

7. **Revenue quality.** The reliance on contingent commissions to flatter headline growth means reported numbers can overstate the durable trend in either direction.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 72.2% versus 1-year historical volatility of 57.0%, so options are rich (the market is pricing elevated uncertainty, implied move ±11.3% on the nearest expiry). The skew is mildly call-heavy (-4.3%) on the front month, flipping to put-heavy (+4.4%) at 3 months, with a flat term structure — uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated at a single near-term catalyst. Short interest is elevated at 10.7% of float with 4.1 days to cover, confirming meaningful institutional bearishness but not a crowded squeeze setup. The combination of rich IV, near-balanced front-month skew, and moderate short interest reads as genuine two-sided uncertainty rather than a directional informed-positioning tell, consistent with the HOLD thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (→ $62-100)

The franchise pruning is deliberate quality-over-quantity, and policies in force, written premium, and corporate agents prove the underlying engine compounds in the mid-teens. The renewal royalty step-up keeps building a high-margin recurring tail. Operating leverage drives adjusted EBITDA margin from 26% toward the 30%+ range as AI/technology investment matures and G&A normalizes. The entire new C-suite bought stock in the open market near $35-41, signaling conviction at these levels. Forward consensus implies the stock is cheap on growth-adjusted terms; as franchise count and NPS stabilize, multiple re-rating toward the premium-broker cohort takes the stock to the analyst high of $100. Base re-rating to the $62.50 median target is achievable on stabilization alone.

### Bear Case (→ $28-30, ~-20%)

The franchise-count decline accelerates rather than stabilizes, and NPS deterioration signals real service/brand erosion that pressures retention. Contingent commissions normalize lower, exposing a core growth rate that decelerates toward the low end of the 10-19% guide. Rising G&A from AI spend with no proven payback compresses the margin re-expansion. The variable-rate debt load weighs on EPS as rates stay elevated, and the 2032 refinancing looms. Carrier concentration or a catastrophe event cuts placement. The stock de-rates toward the high-$20s as the market concludes the growth story has structurally slowed and the leverage was a poorly-timed return of capital.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale — the risk/reward has genuinely improved with the price decline, but the unresolved fundamental questions and engineered leverage prevent a confident directional call in either direction.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, roughly late July) is the key checkpoint, with franchise count, NPS, and core revenue the metrics to watch.

**Upgrade triggers (→ BUY):**
- Franchise count stabilizes or returns to growth (956 holding or rising) for two consecutive quarters, confirming the pruning is complete rather than ongoing.
- NPS recovers toward the low-80s, removing the brand/retention yellow flag.
- Core revenue growth holds at ≥15% with adjusted EBITDA margin sustaining ≥26% excluding contingent-commission help, validating durable operating leverage.

**Downgrade triggers (→ AVOID):**
- Franchise count and onboarding decline accelerate, or policies-in-force growth decelerates below 10%.
- NPS falls further (below 70) alongside premium retention dropping below 88%.
- Leverage approaches 4x EBITDA, a covenant breach risk emerges, or refinancing terms on the 2032 balloon deteriorate.
- Insider buying reverses to net selling by the new management team.

## Conclusion

Goosehead is a high-quality business model — asset-light, cash-generative, with a genuine renewal annuity tail — trading at a fair-to-attractive forward multiple after a 65% drawdown. If the only question were whether the model is good and the price reasonable, this would be a buy. But the drawdown is anchored in real fundamental deceleration: the franchise-count decline, the NPS drop, headline growth flattered by volatile contingent commissions, and an engineered-negative-equity balance sheet carrying ~3x variable-rate leverage into a higher-for-longer rate path. These are coherent reasons the stock fell, and they remain unresolved.

The May 2026 open-market buying cluster by the entire new C-suite at prices near today's is the strongest counterweight and is why this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID — management is putting personal capital in at these levels right after taking their roles. That floors my conviction on the downside but does not, by itself, resolve the franchise-count and NPS questions that will determine whether the growth re-accelerates or structurally slows. The forward setup is balanced: roughly 19-171% upside to the analyst target range against ~20% downside in the bear case, but with genuine binary uncertainty on the operational metrics. I recommend HOLD and would upgrade on two quarters of franchise-count and NPS stabilization at sustained mid-teens core growth.
