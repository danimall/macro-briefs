## Executive Summary

Acuity Inc. is executing a deliberate transformation from a North American lighting manufacturer into a building technology platform. The $1.2B acquisition of QSC in January 2025 added a 40%+ growth, 59% gross margin AV/controls business that now represents ~23% of revenue and is the primary source of consolidated earnings growth. The company has repaid $400M of acquisition debt within 15 months while simultaneously repurchasing $103M of stock, demonstrating strong FCF generation (~$533M annually) and disciplined capital allocation.

The problem is the other 77% of revenue. The core ABL lighting segment declined 2.8% organically in Q2 FY2026, with direct sales falling 21% YoY. Consensus estimates are being revised downward across all forward periods (-2.5% to -2.7% in the last 90 days). At ~21x trailing P/E, the stock is reasonably valued for a company with mid-single-digit consolidated growth, but it lacks the upside asymmetry required for a BUY given the specific risks of ABL deterioration, 55% Mexico manufacturing exposure in an unstable trade policy environment, and rising input costs from the current inflationary regime.

The investment thesis hinges on whether QSC's growth can structurally overwhelm ABL's drag over the next 12-18 months. If AIS reaches 30%+ of revenue mix while maintaining current margins, consolidated operating margins should expand 100-200bps, justifying a re-rating toward 23-25x earnings. This scenario is plausible but not yet confirmed in the data — ABL must at minimum stabilize, and Q2's decline raises questions about construction cycle sensitivity.

## Company Overview

Acuity Inc. (renamed from Acuity Brands in March 2025) operates two segments from its Atlanta headquarters:

**Acuity Brands Lighting (ABL) — ~77% of revenue:** Market leader in North American commercial/institutional lighting and controls. Brands include Lithonia Lighting, Juno, Holophane, and nLight. Products distributed through independent agents and electrical distributors for new construction, renovation, and maintenance. Manufacturing concentrated in Mexico (7 facilities producing 55% of finished goods), with 6 US, 3 Canadian, and 2 European facilities. Approximately 13,800 total employees.

**Acuity Intelligent Spaces (AIS) — ~23% of revenue:** Technology-focused segment encompassing Distech Controls (building management/HVAC), Atrius (spatial intelligence data platform), and QSC (acquired January 2025 for $1.2B — cloud-managed audio, video, and control via the Q-SYS platform). QSC serves education, commercial, hospitality, government, healthcare, and entertainment verticals.

The strategic repositioning is clear: Acuity is attempting to become a building technology company that also sells lighting. The name change, R&D growth (+44% since FY2023 to $140M), and $1.2B capital deployment into QSC all signal this direction.

## Growth Analysis

**Segment-Level Growth Dynamics:**

AIS is the growth engine. Q2 FY2026 revenue of $248M (+44.7% YoY) was driven by QSC's continued momentum. QSC contributed $429M in its first 8 months, annualizing to ~$640M. At 59% gross margins and 40%+ growth, QSC alone could represent $3-4B of standalone value if this pace sustains.

ABL is the problem. Q2 FY2026 revenue of $817M (-2.8% YoY) reflected soft non-residential construction demand and project business that "did not recur." The direct sales channel declined 21% YoY in H1 — a significant deterioration in what should be a more predictable revenue stream. ABL is tied to non-residential construction starts, which face headwinds from higher financing costs.

**Estimate Revisions:**

Current quarter EPS estimates have been revised down 2.7% over 90 days ($5.31 → $5.16). Full-year FY2026 declined from $19.80 → $19.52 (-1.4%). FY2027 from $21.78 → $21.28 (-2.3%). This is negative earnings momentum — not catastrophic, but clearly indicating the street is moderating its outlook, likely reflecting ABL softness flowing through consolidated estimates.

**Forward Growth Math:**

At consensus $19.52 FY2026E and $21.28 FY2027E, implied growth is 9.0% EPS growth year-over-year. This is respectable but not exceptional for a company trading at ~21x trailing earnings. The growth is almost entirely mix-shift (AIS growing, ABL declining), which means execution risk is concentrated in sustaining QSC's trajectory.

## Competitive Landscape

**ABL Segment:** Acuity is the undisputed North American leader in commercial lighting but faces structural headwinds. The LED transition is complete, eliminating a secular growth driver. Competition from Asian imports on price and from major electrical companies (Signify, Eaton, Hubbell) on specifications. The controls platform (nLight) provides differentiation, but risk factor filings explicitly cite "non-traditional competitors with significant resources" (i.e., technology companies) entering the space.

**AIS/QSC Segment:** QSC competes with Crestron, Biamp, Extron, and others in the AV/unified communications space. The shift to cloud-managed AV (Q-SYS platform) is a meaningful differentiator — customers are migrating from hardware-centric to software-defined architectures, and QSC appears well-positioned. However, this is a market where Cisco, Microsoft, and other tech giants also participate, creating long-term competitive uncertainty.

**Trajectory Assessment:** Improving in AIS (gaining share in a growing market with a differentiated platform approach) while stagnating/declining in ABL (mature market, pricing pressure, demand sensitivity to construction cycles). Net competitive position is modestly positive given AIS's rapid growth, but ABL still dominates revenue.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **ABL Demand Deterioration (HIGH PROBABILITY, MODERATE IMPACT):** Non-residential construction financing headwinds from elevated interest rates are real. ABL revenue already declining 2.8% in Q2 — this could accelerate if the rate environment persists or worsens.

2. **Mexico Manufacturing Concentration (MODERATE PROBABILITY, HIGH IMPACT):** 55% of finished goods manufactured across 7 Mexican facilities operating under Maquiladora/IMMEX programs. Trade policy uncertainty (USMCA renegotiation, potential tariff escalation), annual union contract renewals for 6,600 workers, and currency volatility all represent structural operational risk. The IEEPA tariff Supreme Court decision provided temporary relief, but the policy environment remains volatile.

3. **QSC Integration/Growth Sustainability (LOW-MODERATE PROBABILITY, HIGH IMPACT):** QSC's 40%+ growth rate is exceptional but difficult to sustain at scale ($640M annualized). If growth decelerates to 15-20%, the mix-shift story slows materially and the multiple compression risk increases. Additionally, $714M in acquired intangibles ($24M/quarter amortization) will pressure reported earnings for 7-10 years.

4. **Input Cost Inflation (MODERATE PROBABILITY, MODERATE IMPACT):** ABL products are materials-intensive. Tariff costs (even post-IEEPA ruling) add uncertainty. Pricing power in a competitive market may be insufficient to fully offset cost escalation.

5. **Construction Cycle Timing (MODERATE PROBABILITY, MODERATE IMPACT):** IRA/CHIPS Act infrastructure spending provides tailwind, but traditional commercial construction (offices, retail) faces secular headwinds from remote work and higher financing costs. The mix of construction demand matters as much as the level.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($340-360, +23-30%):**
- QSC sustains 25-30%+ growth through FY2027, driving AIS to 30%+ of revenue
- Mix shift expands consolidated gross margins toward 50%+, operating margins toward 15%
- ABL stabilizes at flat-to-low-single-digit growth as data center and infrastructure demand offset traditional commercial weakness
- Multiple re-rates to 18-20x forward FY2027 EPS of $21+ as market recognizes technology platform transformation
- May 2026 material agreement signals another value-accretive acquisition in the intelligent buildings space
- FCF generation ($550M+) enables continued buybacks at today's depressed prices, creating EPS accretion

**Bear Case ($220-240, -15-20%):**
- ABL decline accelerates to -5% or worse as higher rates choke non-residential construction
- QSC growth decelerates to 15% as enterprise AV spending pulls back in recessionary environment
- Tariff/Mexico disruption creates margin pressure beyond management's ability to offset through pricing
- Estimates cut further (FY2026E toward $18, FY2027E toward $19-20)
- Multiple compresses to 12x on industrial cyclical concerns as recession probability rises
- $19.52 → $18 EPS at 12-13x = $216-234 bear case

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. ABL organic revenue growth returns to positive for one full quarter (demonstrates construction demand stabilization)
2. FY2027 EPS estimates stabilize or inflect upward — specifically, consensus $21.28 holding steady or rising for 30+ consecutive days
3. Stock declines below $255 (12x FY2027E), creating 25%+ upside to consensus targets with margin of safety

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. ABL organic decline exceeds -5% for two consecutive quarters (structural demand impairment)
2. QSC growth decelerates below 20% YoY (acquisition thesis challenged)
3. Mexico tariff or USMCA event creating verifiable >200bps margin headwind
4. FY2027 EPS estimates cut below $19.50 (indicating fundamental deterioration beyond cyclical softness)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings (Q3 FY2026, likely late June/early July 2026). This quarter should reveal whether Q2 ABL weakness was a one-quarter blip or a trend acceleration. The May 2026 material agreement details should also clarify strategic direction.

## Conclusion

Acuity is converting lighting market leadership into a building technology platform via QSC and intelligent spaces. The financial execution has been strong (rapid deleveraging, high FCF conversion, disciplined buybacks). At ~14.5x forward FY2026 earnings with 24-28% upside to consensus targets, the stock is not expensive.

The specific reason this remains a HOLD rather than a BUY: **negative estimate revisions combined with ABL organic decline in a worsening rate environment create near-term earnings risk that the current ~21x trailing multiple does not adequately discount.** The declining estimates (-2.5% to -2.7% over 90 days) indicate analysts are still adjusting expectations downward — buying into falling estimates historically produces negative short-term returns. The bull case requires ABL stabilization, which in turn requires either construction demand recovery or evidence that data center and infrastructure demand can offset traditional commercial weakness (not yet visible in the data).

When ABL demonstrates stabilization or the stock reaches a valuation level (~$255) that discounts further downside, the risk/reward shifts decisively. At current levels, the 12-month return profile is likely positive (consensus implies 24-28% upside) but the path is uncertain and near-term catalysts are ambiguous. HOLD with a growth thesis contingent on next-quarter confirmation.
