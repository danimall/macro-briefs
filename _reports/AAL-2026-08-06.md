## Executive Summary

American Airlines' second quarter demonstrated something genuinely important about the current airline cycle and something genuinely troubling about American specifically. The important part: with jet fuel at $4.05 per gallon versus $2.29 a year earlier, American raised yield 11.9% and TRASM 10.3%, producing record quarterly revenue of $16.735B (+16.3%). Fares of that magnitude only stick when industry capacity is disciplined. The troubling part: TRASM +10.3% against CASM +16.5% is a 620bp adverse spread, and operating margin fell from 7.89% to 2.67% in what is seasonally the company's strongest quarter. First-half GAAP result is a net loss of $(311)M against net income of $126M a year earlier.

The valuation question reduces to one variable. At $16.19 the stock trades at 6.5x the 2027 consensus of $2.48, versus a peer-group median P/E of 8.6x, and that 2027 number has been revised up 12.7% over 90 days. If fuel normalizes toward $2.50-2.75 per gallon while fares hold most of their 2026 gains, American earns something in the $2.00-2.50 range and the stock works. The disclosed sensitivity is unusually blunt: roughly $45M of annual fuel expense per one cent per gallon, so a $1.00/gal move is approximately $4.5B of annual pre-tax income against a $10.7B market capitalization. The company holds no fuel hedges and states a policy of not hedging. American has chosen the highest fuel beta among the big three while carrying, by its own 10-K admission, obligations "generally greater than the indebtedness and other obligations of our competitors."

Two things prevent an upgrade. First, capital structure: $28.565B of debt principal, $(3.972)B of stockholders' deficit, and a $7.743B 2028 principal wall that must be refinanced in the same year as roughly $3.7B of aircraft capex, from a non-investment-grade credit whose best asset — AAdvantage — is already pledged. Gross debt was flat over the first half ($28,594M to $28,565M) despite excellent liability management. Second, the near-term estimate direction is negative: current-quarter consensus was cut from $(0.04) to $(0.22) over 30 days, and current-year 2026 from $0.38 to $0.12. The strong estimate-revision buy signal (>+30% over 90 days on out-year EPS combined with accelerating volume growth) is not present here — next-year revisions are +12.7%, and revenue growth is price pass-through, not volume: RPMs rose 3.6% and load factor fell 1.5 points. HOLD, with a base case of $18.50 (+14%) against a bear case of $10.50 (-35%).

## Company Overview

American Airlines Group is the parent of American Airlines, Inc., one of three U.S. global network carriers. The group operates a nine-hub domestic system (Charlotte, Chicago O'Hare, Dallas/Fort Worth, Los Angeles, Miami, New York, Philadelphia, Phoenix, Washington), with substantially all flights touching one of those points plus partner gateways including London Heathrow. Fleet was 1,609 aircraft at June 30, 2026, up 4.5% year over year, against 143,400 full-time equivalents (+3.8%). Approximately 86% of employees are unionized and roughly 15% are covered by agreements currently amendable or becoming amendable within one year.

Two structural features define the earnings model. First, geographic mix: Q2 2026 passenger revenue was 70% domestic ($10.726B), with Latin America $1.726B, Atlantic $2.353B and Pacific only $409M (2.7%). American is the most domestically weighted and least Pacific-exposed of the big three, and the most Latin-America-levered. Second, loyalty: Q2 "Other" revenue of $1.248B was driven primarily by AAdvantage, with loyalty revenue-marketing services of $1.058B in the quarter ($2.085B for the half), plus $1.119B of mileage-redemption revenue booked inside passenger revenue. AAdvantage is also collateral — the AAdvantage financings total roughly $3.24B of term loans plus $3.58B of secured notes, and their covenants restrict amendments to the program's commercial agreements.

Regional flying runs through wholly owned subsidiaries including PSA and third-party operators under capacity purchase agreements. American holds a 20.8% stake in Republic Airways Holdings (carrying value $259M) while simultaneously paying Republic $364M under a capacity purchase agreement in the first half — American is Republic's counterparty and part-owner, not its supplier.

Operating margin: 446 ÷ 16,735 = 2.67%, versus 1,135 ÷ 14,392 = 7.89%. That is 522bp of destruction in the seasonal peak quarter.

The earnings surprise table shows $0.15 actual against $0.03 estimate for the June quarter, a 339.9% beat. That figure is on a non-GAAP basis; GAAP diluted EPS was $0.11. The distinction matters for anyone comparing to consensus, and it should not be read as a $0.15 GAAP result.

**Unit economics.** Yield 22.33¢ vs 19.96¢ (+11.9%); PRASM 18.59¢ vs 16.90¢ (+10.0%); load factor 83.2%, down 1.5 points on ASMs +5.4% and RPMs +3.6%. CASM ex-special items, fuel and profit sharing rose 2.9% in the quarter and 4.0% for the half, following +4.6% in FY2025.

Two readings of the load-factor decline are available. Fares were pushed to the point of demand resistance, or capacity growth of 5.4% outran what higher fares could fill; alternatively, mix shift toward premium and long-haul Atlantic seats (Atlantic revenue +12.8%) mechanically dilutes load factor. The filing does not disaggregate. Both effects are plausible and the distinction matters for whether 2027 fare assumptions hold.

The non-fuel cost trend is the more durable problem because fuel mean-reverts and airport costs do not. The 10-Q attributes CASM ex-fuel growth to maintenance and materials (+10.8% on engine overhaul volume), other rent and landing fees (+9.2% on "rate increases at certain airports"), selling expenses (+12.7% on renegotiated agency commissions and card fees), and other (+13.8%, including Wi-Fi). The 10-K warns that "our operating costs are expected to increase significantly at many airports at which we operate," naming capital programs at O'Hare, DFW, Charlotte, Miami and LAX, and notes such costs can be imposed without American's approval. The selling-expense line is partly the recurring cost of reversing the failed 2024 direct-distribution push: American is now paying higher agency commissions for distribution peace. Free Wi-Fi for AAdvantage members, launched in 2026, is a deliberate revenue-for-loyalty trade with unproven payback.

Current ratio 0.53x. That figure overstates distress because air traffic liability and current loyalty liability are deferred revenue settled in flying, not cash. The deficit also warrants nuance: accumulated other comprehensive loss is $(4,357)M, of which $(2,687)M is unamortized pension and retiree-medical loss on plans frozen in 2012, with $(1,667)M of related tax. A meaningful share of the book-equity hole is frozen-pension accounting rather than cumulative operating losses. That changes the interpretation without removing the consequence: negative equity constrains covenant headroom, ratings and any equity-linked financing.

Liquidity is the stronger part of the story. Total available liquidity was $11.280B at June 30 (cash $1.028B + short-term investments $6.742B + $3.510B undrawn revolvers), up from $9.233B at year-end, against a $2.0B minimum-liquidity covenant that binds "a significant portion" of the debt agreements. Management states compliance with each covenant at the most recent applicable measurement dates. Headroom today is comfortable.

**Debt: excellent liability management, zero deleveraging.** Total principal was $28,565M versus $28,594M six months earlier — down $29M. Within that window American prepaid the $1.0B 8.50% senior secured notes in full, repaid a $629M short-term term loan, repaid $914M of fuel financing obligations, prepaid $310M of EETC notes, refinanced the 2014 term loans from a January 2027 maturity out to May 2033, extended all three revolvers from June 2029 to March 2031 while upsizing to $3.1B, and cut the 2025 AAdvantage term loan margin to SOFR+2.75%. Funding came from roughly $2.7B of new EETC/equipment debt plus $703M of incremental term loans. Cost and tenor improved materially; quantum did not move. Any thesis resting on 2026 deleveraging is unsupported by the balance sheet. Six months is a short window and the remaining $1.279B of 2026 amortization could run without matching issuance, so no conclusion about management intent should be drawn from the flat balance.

### Cash flow quality

First-half operating cash flow was $4.694B versus $3.419B, which against a $(311)M GAAP loss looks powerful. It is not what it appears. The 10-Q attributes the increase primarily to working-capital growth in air traffic liability and loyalty deferred revenue, and separately discloses that first-half cash remuneration "included a one-time cash payment associated with the extension of a partner agreement announced in 2025." Cash from co-branded card and other partners was $4.7B in the half versus $3.2B, while Q2-only was $1.8B versus $1.4B — implying roughly $1.1B of the year-over-year increase landed in Q1, consistent with a large one-time payment.

Stripping an assumed ~$1.1B leaves roughly $3.6B of first-half operating cash flow against $3.419B prior year: low-single-digit underlying growth, not a 37% surge. The $1.1B is an estimate derived from disclosed partner-cash figures; the company does not disclose the amount. Free cash flow on reported figures was $4,694M − $1,633M capex = $3,061M for the half; adjusted for the one-time item, roughly $2.0B, before the second-half reversal of the seasonal air traffic liability build. The sober anchor is FY2025 free cash flow of approximately negative $680M.

The cash is real and de-risks near-term liquidity, and it will support reported loyalty revenue for years as it amortizes out of the loyalty liability. The error to avoid is counting it twice — once as cash and again as future loyalty revenue growth.

### Forward commitments

Aircraft and engine purchase commitments total $24.324B at June 30, phased $1.404B for the rest of 2026, $3.145B in 2027, $3.712B in 2028, $5.593B in 2029, $3.526B in 2030 and $6.944B thereafter, against a 373-aircraft order book. The 10-K frames 2026-2030 spend at roughly $17.5B and states the intent to finance deliveries "using cash on hand and long-term debt." In July 2026, during the fuel shock, American exercised purchase options on six additional A320-family aircraft.

Principal amortization at American: $1.279B remainder-2026, $3.448B 2027, **$7.743B 2028**, $3.775B 2029, $869M 2030. The 2028 stack includes the 2013 term loan ($960M), 7.25% senior secured notes ($750M) and the 2021 AAdvantage term loan ($2.252B in installments). The 2026 refinancings pushed paper around 2028 rather than dismantling it. Interest-rate exposure is unhedged: a 100bp rise adds roughly $150M of annual interest expense against roughly $80M of interest income, a net $70M drag per 100bp, with variable tranches carrying 5.91%-6.67% coupons.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth of 16.3% is almost entirely price. Volume grew 3.6% (RPMs) on 5.4% capacity, with load factor down. The revenue line is therefore a fuel pass-through mechanism, and its durability depends on whether industry capacity discipline persists once fuel falls. Externally reported and unconfirmed by any filing: the collapse of Spirit Airlines, which if verified removes a material block of domestic ULCC capacity and would help American disproportionately given its 70% domestic mix. That fact should be independently verified before being underwritten; it is the single most load-bearing unconfirmed input in the bull case.

Consensus tells a two-speed story:

The FY2026 "+262.7% over 90 days" is arithmetically meaningless off a near-zero base and should be ignored. The genuine signal is FY2027 rising 12.7% over 90 days to $2.48 — positive, but below the threshold at which estimate-revision momentum becomes a standalone buy case, and paired with deteriorating near-term numbers.

What does $2.48 require? On roughly 665M shares that is about $1.65B of net income. With the tax provision substantially non-cash owing to $11.9B of federal NOLs, pre-tax income needs to be in the $1.7-1.9B range, implying operating income near $3.1B after roughly $1.2-1.3B of net interest. On revenue of $56-57B that is a 5.4% operating margin, versus 2.7% delivered in FY2025 and 2.67% in the 2026 peak quarter. The path exists — hold the 11.9% yield gain, pay $1.00-1.50/gal less for fuel — but it requires the favourable half of the fuel asymmetry and no fare give-back. That is a forecast about crack spreads, not about execution.

Growth investment is real: 373 aircraft on order, 14 737 MAX and 8 E175 delivered in the first half, plus 10 engines. The loyalty franchise is compounding, with marketing-services revenue of $2.085B in the half now underpinned by a decade-length exclusive co-brand contract whose cash was partly received up front.

## Competitive Landscape

American concedes two structural disadvantages in its own 10-K: obligations "generally greater than the indebtedness and other obligations of our competitors," and a network skewed toward Latin America with lower Asia exposure. In a record-crack-spread environment, Delta's refinery ownership is a direct relative advantage — it captures on the refining side part of what it pays on the fuel side — while American, holding no hedges and the thinnest equity cushion, absorbs the full move. That combination is the clearest competitive fact in the file.

Offsetting assets are real and non-replicable: slot-constrained positions at DCA, JFK, LGA, Heathrow, Frankfurt, Paris and Tokyo, plus perimeter-rule protection at DCA and LGA; nine hubs; four joint businesses (transatlantic with British Airways/Aer Lingus/Iberia/Finnair, transpacific with Japan Airlines, and Australia/New Zealand with Qantas) and alliances with Alaska and Qatar. The CMA closed its transatlantic joint-business investigation in August 2025 by accepting binding commitments, clearing a long-running Heathrow slot-remedy overhang. Note the two-sided nature of the slot moat: those assets are pledged as collateral, and the 10-K warns that termination of slot controls would itself damage American's competitive position.

Threats named specifically in the filing go beyond generic low-cost pressure: LCCs flying long-haul international on long-range narrowbodies; premium-focused carriers pursuing growth (a squeeze from above as well as below); scheduled operators using the lighter FAA Part 135 regime, which American has formally protested to DOT and TSA; loyalty competition from banks' proprietary rewards ecosystems rather than only from SkyMiles and MileagePlus; and dependence on partners for award-redemption inventory.

Trajectory assessment: pricing power in Q2 was strong, but it was industry-wide rather than company-specific, cost position is deteriorating relative to a peer with a refinery hedge, load factor fell, and distribution economics worsened as a consequence of a self-inflicted 2024 error. Score 4.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Fuel, unhedged.** Approximately $45M of annual fuel expense per one cent per gallon, so $1.00/gal ≈ $4.5B pre-tax against FY2025 operating income of $1.467B and a $10.7B market cap. First-half 2026 spot jet fuel ranged $1.86-$4.78, exceeding the entire 2023-2025 range of $1.83-$3.82. The company confirms no hedges and a policy of not hedging.

**Capital structure and the 2028 wall.** $28.565B debt, $(3.972)B equity, non-investment-grade, "a very significant portion of our assets are pledged" including aircraft, slots, routes, receivables, intellectual property and loyalty assets. The 10-K acknowledges "substantial risk of cross-default and cross-acceleration." Refinancing $7.743B in 2028 alongside $3.712B of aircraft capex is the central financing risk.

**Loyalty regulation — the highest-convexity tail.** The 10-K states that the Credit Card Competition Act or a proposed 10% cap on credit-card interest rates "could fundamentally alter the profitability of our agreement with our co-branded credit card partner." Because AAdvantage is also collateral for roughly $6.8B of debt and carries a peak debt service coverage covenant, a material interchange or card-NIM regulation would hit revenue and collateral value simultaneously. Neither measure is law, and the CCCA has been proposed repeatedly without passage.

**Labor.** 86% unionized; roughly 15% under amendable agreements. Salaries grew 9.6% in FY2025 and 8.2% in the first half. The 10-K flags binding wage arbitration as an unquantified exposure.

**Litigation.** Flight 5342 wrongful-death actions are consolidated in D.D.C. with trial set for April 12, 2027; motions to dismiss on preemption were denied without prejudice in February 2026, and American/PSA cross-claimed against the U.S. Government on June 10, 2026 after the Government admitted liability as to helicopter-crew negligence. Management states the matter is covered by aviation insurance; limits and retentions are not disclosed. The demonstrated channel is revenue — the accident reduced Q1 2025 revenue by approximately $200M. Separately, the NEA antitrust class actions survived dismissal in September 2024, and the remaining plaintiff filed for class certification in July 2026 with opposition due by October 2026. Antitrust damages are trebled; certification is the inflection from bounded to unbounded exposure.

**Reflexive liquidity tail.** Credit-card processors may impose holdbacks "up to and including 100% of relevant advanced ticket sales" upon a material adverse change or liquidity-covenant trigger. Against $9.551B of air traffic liability, this is the mechanism by which airline stress becomes sudden. Not a base case at $11.3B of liquidity against a $2.0B floor.

**Governance.** The Tax Benefit Preservation Plan, extended to October 29, 2027, imposes substantial dilution on any non-exempt holder reaching 4.9%, layered on no action by written consent, a 20% special-meeting threshold, an 80% bylaw supermajority and exclusive-forum provisions. Four Item 5.02 director filings between March and July 2026 are established from the filing index but unexplained; the 8-K texts and 2026 proxy were not read, and the 4.9% trigger argues against an activist-settlement reading. No motive should be inferred. Executive compensation and Form 4 insider activity were not reviewed, so no inference — in either direction — is available on insider sentiment.

**Other quantified items.** Brazil tariffs on aircraft affect the sole source of new regional jets (72 E175 on order). Two consecutive airline-equity write-downs: GOL substantially lost following August 2025 plan approval, and China Southern marked from $203M to $117M in six months. The $2.417B deferred tax asset carries valuation-allowance risk if 2026 finishes at a loss.

**Not-yet-priced upside risk.** The same leverage that drives the bear case makes the equity respond violently to fuel relief. A sustained crack-spread compression while fares stay elevated is the single strongest identifiable catalyst. The reported easing of jet fuel prices from the June peak while fares remain elevated (tier-3 news, CNN 2026-06-29; CBS 2026-07-09) is unconfirmed by any filing and should be independently verified, so this is an open question rather than a base case.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 54.5% against 48.7% one-year realized, rich by 5.8 points, at the 65th percentile within the chain. The term structure is in backwardation (54.5% at one week versus 50.8% at six months), which the data source reads as pricing imminent stress. Skew is balanced across tenors (−0.9% to −2.1%), put/call volume 0.74 and put/call open interest 0.79.

The more informative reading is the change since July 19: ATM IV collapsed from 67.0% to 54.5% (−12.5 points) while the stock rose 8.1%, and put/call open interest fell from 1.29 to 0.79. Hedging demand has fallen sharply and positioning is no longer put-tilted — the post-earnings uncertainty resolved favourably. Residual near-dated backwardation with balanced skew is more consistent with macro event risk (payrolls, oil headlines) than with a company-specific catalyst. Implied moves are ±7.7% by August 21 and ±13.5% by September 18. Short interest is 9.7% of float with 0.4 days to cover — extremely liquid, no squeeze mechanics, and a modest short base that neither confirms nor contradicts the bear case.

Options positioning does not challenge the HOLD; it removes the near-term bearish tilt that existed at the prior review, which is part of why the overall score improves.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $25.00 (+54%)

Fuel normalizes to $2.40-2.60 per gallon in 2027 while fares retain most of the 11.9% yield gain, because industry capacity remains disciplined and Spirit's reported exit permanently removes domestic ULCC seats — a benefit weighted toward American's 70% domestic mix. FY2027 EPS reaches or exceeds the $2.48 consensus; at 10x, in line with a normalized network-carrier multiple and matching the analyst high, the stock reaches $25. Supporting elements: $11.28B of liquidity against a $2.0B covenant floor; maturities extended to 2031-2033 with the 8.50% tranche and fuel financing eliminated; approximately 39.1M shares of CARES-era contingent dilution retired in cash rather than stock, leaving a clean ~662M-share capitalization; a substantially non-cash tax provision meaning cash conversion of pre-tax profit exceeds what GAAP EPS suggests; and a decade-length co-brand contract whose upfront cash is already collected. Three beats in four quarters supports confidence in consensus.

### Bear Case — $10.50 (−35%)

Middle East escalation re-establishes jet fuel above $4.00, or crack spreads stay at records even as crude falls. Fares prove less sticky than 2026 suggested once competitors re-add capacity into the fare umbrella — the 1.5-point load-factor decline is early evidence of demand resistance. CASM ex-fuel continues at 3-4% on contractual airport capital programs, amendable labor agreements and elevated agency commissions, so the cost base ratchets while the revenue benefit reverses. FY2027 EPS lands below $1.00, gross debt stays flat or rises to fund $3.1-3.7B of annual aircraft capex, and the 2028 refinancing of $7.743B becomes a live credit question rather than a scheduling exercise, with rating agencies acting first. NEA class certification in late 2026 introduces unbounded trebled antitrust exposure. A full-year loss triggers a material addition to the valuation allowance against the $2.417B deferred tax asset. The stock returns toward the 52-week low of $10.09 and the analyst low of $10.30.

### Why HOLD rather than a directional call

The distribution is roughly symmetric in magnitude (+54% / −35%) but the base case offers only +14%, and the determining variable is a commodity spread that neither management nor this analysis can forecast with useful precision. Management does not hedge it. The company holds the weakest balance sheet in its peer group and has an unresolved 2028 obligation. Near-term consensus is falling even as out-year consensus rises. There is a real conditional value case here; there is not a mispricing I can name.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print (late October 2026), which will reveal realized fuel, whether load factor stabilized, and whether CASM ex-fuel decelerated. Secondary date: NEA class-certification briefing completing in October 2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of CASM ex-fuel growth at or below 2% with realized fuel below $2.75/gal, and a positive full-year free cash flow print (versus approximately −$680M in FY2025).
2. Refinancing or extension of a material 2028 tranche — the $2.252B AAdvantage term loan, the $960M 2013 term loan or the $750M 7.25% notes — beyond 2030, accompanied by a decline in gross debt principal below $28.0B.
3. A verified, sustained compression in jet crack spreads with realized fuel below $2.75/gal while yield growth remains positive year over year.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Realized fuel back above $4.00/gal for a full quarter with load factor declining another point, confirming demand resistance to fare pass-through.
2. NEA class certification granted, or disclosure that Flight 5342 exposure exceeds insurance limits.
3. Total available liquidity falling below roughly $6B, any collateral-coverage or cash-trap remedy triggered, or any credit-card processor holdback imposed.
4. FY2026 finishing in a net loss accompanied by a material addition to the valuation allowance against the $2.417B deferred tax asset, or a rating downgrade.

**Position guidance for existing holders:** hold. The 8.1% appreciation since the prior review has come with a 12.5-point collapse in implied volatility and rising 2027 estimates, which improves the quality of the holding without creating a new-capital case. New capital should wait for the October print or a price in the low $13s, where the base case would offer roughly 40% upside against the same $10.50 bear case.

## Conclusion

**HOLD. Overall score 4.55** (up from 4.4), continuing the improving trajectory from 3.2 in April. The improvement is earned rather than mechanical: a 339.9% non-GAAP beat in the June quarter, FY2027 consensus up 12.7% over 90 days to $2.48, liquidity up $2.0B to $11.28B, maturities pushed to 2031-2033, contingent dilution retired in cash, and a sharp reduction in options-market hedging demand. Fundamentals improved while the stock remains 13.8% below its June high.
