## Executive Summary

Lattice is in the middle of a genuine operating recovery. Q1 FY2026 (quarter ended April 4, 2026) revenue rose 42.2% year over year to $170.9M, gross margin expanded 80bp to 68.8%, and operating margin more than doubled to 15.3% from 5.8% as opex grew ~22% against 42% revenue growth. The Compute and Communications end market grew 86% year over year to $106.6M, driven by hyperscaler platform-control demand. Consensus non-GAAP EPS estimates have risen 16.0% for the current year and 14.5% for next year over 90 days. The operating leverage is real, and the direction of travel is favorable.

The problem is price and capital structure. At $124.27 the stock carries roughly 67.9x current-year consensus non-GAAP EPS of $1.83 and 53.6x next-year $2.32 — multiples that exclude a stock-compensation charge that ran $116.3M, or 22.2% of revenue, in FY2025. On enterprise value of roughly $18.1B (including our estimated $0.8-0.9B of post-acquisition net debt), the stock trades near 34.7x trailing revenue and about 18.1x the $1B annual run rate management targets for the end of 2026 — a target that itself requires Q4 revenue of at least $250M against $170.9M in Q1. Meanwhile the July 27 AMI acquisition ($1.0B cash plus $650M of stock) replaced a pristine balance sheet with a **364-day** senior secured term loan of up to $950M that must be refinanced by roughly late July 2027, into a credit market where AI-adjacent borrowers are already repricing wider at issuance.

The most important number in this analysis does not yet exist publicly. AMI's revenue, gross margin, EBITDA and recurring-revenue share are undisclosed; management deferred all of it to the Q2 FY2026 call after the close on **August 4, 2026**. The options market is pricing a ±19.3% move on the August 21 expiry with near-term implied volatility at 105.9% against 59.1% realized. Publishing a conviction call on either side the day before that disclosure would be an exercise in guessing. We rate LSCC HOLD with an uncertain rationale: own it if you own it, do not add here, and let the August 4 disclosure resolve the leverage and accretion math before re-underwriting.

## Company Overview

Lattice is a fabless designer of low-power small- and mid-range FPGAs, plus the software, IP cores and solution stacks around them. As of January 3, 2026 the company had 1,174 employees. The portfolio is deliberately narrow: legacy families (MachXO series, CrossLink, ECP, iCE40 UltraPlus), the Nexus platform (28nm FD-SOI at Samsung, introduced 2019), Nexus 2 (16nm at TSMC, 2024), and Avant (16nm FinFET at TSMC, 2022) — the mid-range push into 150K to 600K+ system logic cells, territory historically held by AMD (Xilinx) and Altera.

The economically important franchise is the server platform security and control socket, where Lattice devices act as the "first-on, last-off" hardware root of trust and manage power/thermal sequencing and system management. Management describes these sockets as processor-agnostic, which is the strategic point: the control-plane position persists whether the compute silicon is Intel, AMD, NVIDIA or a hyperscaler ASIC.

Manufacturing is fully outsourced — UMC/USJC, Samsung, TSMC and Seiko Epson on the front end; ASE (primary) and Amkor on assembly and test, with wafer sort in Taiwan and Malaysia. Essentially the entire supply chain sits in Taiwan, Japan, Korea and Malaysia. Distributors accounted for 84% of FY2025 revenue and foreign sales 83%, all USD-denominated.

On July 27, 2026 Lattice completed the acquisition of AMI from Thomas H. Lee Partners. AMI supplies platform firmware and baseboard-management/infrastructure-manageability software, and now operates as a business unit under long-time AMI CEO Sanjoy Maity, reporting to CEO Ford Tamer. Lattice's own release states twice that AMI firmware will continue to be developed and delivered "without preference for any silicon vendor, including Lattice." AMI is a silicon-neutral supplier to hyperscalers, OEMs, ODMs and neocloud providers — several of which buy competitors' control FPGAs.

## Financial Analysis

### GAAP earnings are currently uninformative, and compensation is why

| Fiscal year | Revenue | YoY | Gross margin | Operating income | Op. margin | Net income |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2023 | $737.2M | +11.6% | 69.8% | $212.3M | 28.8% | $259.1M |
| FY2024 | $509.4M | −30.9% | 66.8% | $34.5M | 6.8% | $61.1M |
| FY2025 (53 wks) | $523.3M | +2.7% | 68.2% | $11.2M | 2.1% | $3.1M |

FY2024 was a severe inventory correction. FY2025 stabilized revenue but operating income fell a further 67% on flat sales, and GAAP net income collapsed to $3.1M ($0.02 per diluted share on 138.2M shares). The mechanism: R&D rose 18.0% to $188.0M (35.9% of revenue) and SG&A rose 31.4% to $153.6M (29.4%), driven by stock-based compensation including payroll tax of **$116.3M versus $53.7M in FY2024** — 22.2% of revenue. Management attributes the increase to market- and performance-based awards plus a depressed prior-year base from departing-executive forfeitures. The tax line compounds it: FY2025 pretax income was $13.4M against $10.3M of tax expense, an effective rate near 77%, which the 10-K attributes principally to non-deductible stock compensation.

Cash tells a different story. FY2025 operating cash flow was $175.1M against $42.5M of capex, giving free cash flow of $132.6M — a 25.3% FCF margin against a 0.6% net margin. Management's Adjusted EBITDA was $183.0M (35.0% margin). The bridge is almost entirely the $116.3M of non-cash SBC plus $34.3M of D&A.

Both presentations mislead in opposite directions. GAAP net income of $3.1M understates cash economics; Adjusted EBITDA that adds back $116M of genuinely dilutive equity compensation overstates distributable economics. FY2025 free cash flow per diluted share was roughly $0.96, and that figure still ignores the share creation itself. For valuation purposes, neither trailing GAAP EPS nor Adjusted EBITDA is usable without adjustment, and the two must never be compared to estimates on the other basis.

Opex grew ~22% against 42% revenue growth — the definition of operating leverage. SBC remains large in absolute terms but is falling as a share of revenue (16.7% versus 22.2% for FY2025), which is the mechanism by which GAAP earnings can recover quickly if revenue holds. Q1 operating cash flow was $50.3M against $10.5M of PP&E capex plus $4.9M of capitalized software/IP, so free cash flow was $34.8M on the broader definition. We will not annualize that: accounts receivable rose $15.8M with the revenue ramp, and $13.8M of RSU tax withholding sat in financing outflows. As a labeled sensitivity only, 4x $34.8M is ~$139M; a normalized view assuming receivables continue to build with revenue would come in lower.

### Balance sheet: pristine before the deal, levered after

At April 4, 2026: cash $140.0M, receivables $118.1M (zero allowance), inventories $88.2M, current ratio 3.48x, equity $740.2M, **zero funded debt**. The revolver was undrawn and had been deliberately reduced from $350M to $200M effective December 10, 2025 to lower carrying costs. Days inventory improved from 207 to 178. Altman Z-Score at FY2025 was 4.64 — comfortably in the safe zone, though the EBIT term within it is genuinely weak.

Post-close the picture changes materially. Consideration was $1.0B cash plus $650M of stock on a cash-free/debt-free basis, financed with a committed senior secured **364-day** term loan of up to $950M plus a $200M revolver (Wells Fargo, Morgan Stanley). Assuming the cash portion was funded with roughly $950M of term loan against $140-160M of balance-sheet cash, pro forma gross debt is roughly $0.9-1.0B and net debt roughly **$0.8-0.9B**. Against standalone FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $183.0M that is 4.4-4.9x; against a sensitivity using 4x Q1 FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA ($271M annualized) it is 3.0-3.3x, and lower once AMI EBITDA is included. Because AMI's EBITDA is undisclosed, **no reliable pro forma leverage ratio can be computed today.** The exact draw, final share count and covenant terms are also unverified — the primary July 27 8-K document could not be retrieved.

The 364-day tenor is the most concrete financing risk in the story. A 364-day facility is a bridge by construction and must be termed out — most plausibly into a term loan B, secured notes or converts — by roughly late July 2027. Lattice's cash generation supports that refinancing, and issuers with 25%+ FCF margins routinely complete it uneventfully. But the takeout will happen in whatever credit market exists then, and any equity-linked takeout would layer dilution on top of the acquisition shares.

**Dilution, stated precisely.** Shares outstanding were 137,007,857 at April 30, 2026. The stock consideration is 5.2M to 6.1M shares including ~$57.3M of Lattice equity awards to AMI employees. At the low end, share count rises 3.80% and existing holders retain 96.34% (3.66% ownership dilution); at the high end, share count rises 4.45% and holders retain 95.74% (4.26% dilution). The supplied $17.26B market cap reconciles to 142-143M shares at current prices, so **post-deal shares are already reflected in the quoted capitalization**. Separately, inducement grants announced July 30 under Nasdaq Rule 5635(c)(4) represent additional, unquantified share creation and SBC beyond the consideration range; treat that as a probability-weighted future item, not part of current diluted capitalization.

Financial health scores 5.5, down from 6.0 previously. This is not a reaction to the share price — it reflects the specific, confirmed conversion of a zero-debt balance sheet into ~$0.9B of net debt on a bridge facility, partially offset by strong and improving cash conversion.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has two independent legs, and only one is currently visible.

**Leg one — compute and communications — is running.** The Q1 re-cut segmentation shows Compute and Communications at $106.6M, 62.4% of revenue, up 86% year over year. The 10-Q attributes the Asia increase primarily to hyperscaler demand. Server platform builds require a hardware root of trust and power/thermal sequencing controller per board regardless of which accelerator sits on it, and Lattice already occupies that socket. Mature-node economics help — Lattice's volume runs on 28nm FD-SOI and 40-130nm, not the leading edge, so its cost curve does not deteriorate as advanced-node wafer prices rise.

**Leg two — industrial and automotive — has only just stopped falling.** That end market declined 45.3% in FY2024 and a further 18.1% in FY2025, and grew just 2% year over year in Q1 FY2026 under the new Industrial and Embedded label. External commentary crediting Lattice with industrial/automotive recovery gains is only weakly corroborated by a +2% print. If this leg genuinely turns, growth compounds; if AI capex digests while industrial stays flat, the industrial leg alone cannot support the multiple.

**AMI is the third leg, and it is unquantified.** Management's stated target is a "$1 billion or greater annual revenue run rate by end of 2026." That arithmetic requires Q4 FY2026 revenue of at least $250M against $170.9M in Q1 — a 46% increase in three quarters. If standalone Lattice reaches $195-205M by Q4 on its current trajectory, AMI must contribute roughly $45-55M per quarter ($180-220M annualized); if standalone lands nearer $185M, AMI needs ~$65M per quarter (~$260M annualized). Against the $1.65B purchase price, those bands imply roughly 6.3x to 9.2x revenue paid — rich but not implausible for a high-margin firmware business that management says is accretive to gross margin. All of that is derived, not disclosed.

**Estimate revisions are positive but not at inflection velocity.** Current-year consensus non-GAAP EPS has risen 16.0% over 90 days to $1.83 and next year 14.5% to $2.32, with current-quarter estimates up 17.2%. Over 30 days, however, revisions are essentially flat (+0.3% to +0.5%). This is constructive momentum, below the threshold at which revision velocity alone justifies aggressive positioning, and it arrives on a stock already trading above 50x forward earnings — the regime in which beats are expected and priced, while misses are punished asymmetrically.

**The critical structural limitation: there is no contracted visibility.** The 10-K states plainly that backlog "has not been a predictor of future sales or customer demand," that purchase orders can generally be revised or cancelled up to 60 days before delivery without significant penalty, and that a meaningful portion of revenue is same-quarter turns business. Contract assets were $6.3M and contract liabilities $4.4M at April 4, 2026. There is no RPO, no material deferred revenue, and no backlog figure to lean on. AMI likely brings recurring license and support revenue with better visibility, which may be an under-appreciated part of the rationale, but its revenue model and renewal rates are undisclosed.

Growth prospects score 7.5, unchanged. The compute leg is confirmed and the operating leverage is demonstrated; the absence of contracted visibility and the unquantified AMI contribution cap it there.

The relevant question is what multiple survives a disappointment, and the June 23 episode — a 7.8% single-day decline on an SK Hynix HBM capacity headline with no direct bearing on control-FPGA demand — shows the multiple is levered to AI sentiment rather than to Lattice's own order book.

That genuinely improves entry math on the equity. It is offset by ~$0.85B of new net debt entering enterprise value, so EV-based multiples have improved only marginally (roughly 18.1x the $1B target versus about 19.6x previously). We score valuation 4.5. The score reflects the absolute multiple against forward earnings that exclude a very large real cost, plus the fact that a cheap-looking cash-flow story requires adding back $116M of dilution to work — not the share-price path.

## Competitive Landscape

Lattice's differentiation is not logic density; it is power, form factor, I/O density, instant-on behavior, and security. The strongest strategic claim in the 10-K is incumbency: Lattice devices already sit in target sockets performing control and security functions, which makes adding an adjacent far-edge inference or offload workload cheaper than displacing an incumbent. Post-quantum cryptography reinforces the case — the MachXO5-NX TDQ is positioned as the first secure control FPGA with full CNSA 2.0-compliant PQC support, and reprogrammable logic handles fast-moving cryptographic standards better than fixed-function ASICs.

The AMI logic is coherent at the platform level. Lattice silicon is the hardware root of trust and power/thermal controller; AMI supplies the firmware and manageability layer that runs on and around it. Combining them lets Lattice sell a hardware-plus-firmware secure management and control stack into the same hyperscaler, OEM, ODM and neocloud accounts, and brings roadmap visibility into next-generation platform requirements.

The constraint is embedded in the same logic. AMI's franchise rests on silicon neutrality, and it is now owned by a silicon vendor. Lattice's dual public commitment to neutrality is commercially necessary — and the emphasis suggests customers raised the question. If AMI genuinely never prefers Lattice silicon, the revenue synergy is limited to bundling convenience and account access rather than attach-rate capture. Every hyperscaler and ODM running AMI firmware alongside a competitor's control FPGA now has a supplier owned by that competitor.

Two competitive headwinds deserve weight. First, the FY2025 10-K added language about industry restructurings, divestitures and spin-offs creating more focused competitors willing to compete aggressively on price. The filing names no one; the most plausible reference is Altera's separation from Intel, which created a pure-play FPGA competitor with its own P&L discipline and every incentive to defend mid-range share — precisely where Avant is trying to win. Second, mid-range is a harder fight than small FPGAs: Lattice is the challenger there, not the incumbent, and challenger economics in mid-range logic historically involve price.

Competitive position scores 6.5 — the control-plane socket and mature-node cost position are durable, the compute segment's 86% growth evidences share capture in a growing market, and the AMI platform logic is sound; offset by a newly focused competitor in the exact segment of expansion and a synergy ceiling created by the neutrality commitment.

## Risk Assessment

**Counterparty concentration is worsening on every direct measure.** Two distributors accounted for 68.8% of FY2025 revenue, up from 64.5% in FY2024 and 52.1% in FY2023; Distributor B alone went from 20.5% to 37.9% over three years. In Q1 FY2026, distributors rose to 94% of revenue from 79%, with direct revenue falling from $25.8M to $9.5M, and two distributors held 52% and 38% of net receivables (90% combined). The attractive shift in end-market mix toward compute is a demand fact; it establishes nothing about counterparty diversification, which is going the wrong way. Mitigants: global distributors are large and well-capitalized, and Lattice reports a zero receivable allowance consistent with negligible realized credit loss. Distributor identities are undisclosed.

**Trade policy is the most specific and least-discussed risk in the filing.** The 10-K reports as fact: a January 2026 presidential proclamation imposing an immediate 25% tariff on certain advanced semiconductor articles with notice of broader action; a USTR determination that an additional Section 301 tariff on Chinese semiconductors will apply on top of the existing 50% beginning June 23, 2027; and IEEPA tariffs whose legality was pending before the Supreme Court after November 2025 argument. Overlay the geography: Greater China rose to 64% of Q1 FY2026 revenue from 48%, Asia in total to 78% from 65%, while Americas fell to 11% from 25%. All fabrication and all assembly/test sit in Taiwan, Japan, Korea and Malaysia. Revenue is assigned by ship-to location, so the filings do not resolve whether the China surge reflects ODM contract-manufacturing locations serving global hyperscalers (the 10-Q's "hyperscaler demand" attribution supports this) or genuine Chinese end demand. The two carry materially different export-control exposure, and that ambiguity is itself the risk.

**Export controls now extend to technology transfer.** Risk language covers export controls on the transfer of technology, source code and know-how for internal development outside the US. Lattice's R&D footprint spans Shanghai, Metro Manila, Pune, Penang and Montreal, so this constrains its ability to shift engineering to lower-cost jurisdictions.

**Management flags the multiple risk itself.** The 10-K names the risk of a "perceived overbuild of AI capacity," warns that front-loaded AI investment may increase the magnitude and volatility of semiconductor cycles, and lists investor expectations regarding AI and "potential strategic transactions, whether or not ultimately realized" as drivers of stock volatility. That is unusually candid drafting.

**Goodwill sits in a single reporting unit** — $315.4M pre-deal, rising substantially with AMI. Single-unit testing is protective while enterprise value is elevated and abruptly less so if it is not. Precedent exists: Mirametrix intangibles were fully impaired in Q4 FY2024 ($13.9M).

**GAAP accretion is not claimed.** Management says the deal is accretive to gross margin, free cash flow and non-GAAP EPS. The omission of GAAP EPS is expected given acquired-intangible amortization, interest on ~$950M of debt, transaction costs and inducement-grant SBC — but it means GAAP results will look worse post-close even if the deal works, and readers must compare GAAP to GAAP.

### Options Market Signal

Positioning is pricing a discrete event, not a regime change. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 105.9% against 59.1% one-year realized — a 46.9-point spread at the 88th percentile of the current chain — with an implied move of ±19.3% for the August 21 expiry. The term structure is in backwardation (105.9% at one month, 89.5% at three, 83.3% at six), the classic signature of a dated binary rather than sustained stress. Implied volatility rose 6.3 points in the four sessions to August 3 on a roughly flat price, and put/call open interest more than doubled from 0.17 to 0.37. The data source characterizes the +5.4% one-month put/call skew as elevated put demand; notably, the three- and six-month skews read −1.6% and −5.1%, so hedging demand is concentrated at the front, consistent with the August 4 print rather than a structural bearish view.

Two implications. First, options are expensive here; buying protection into the print costs a great deal, and existing holders willing to accept a capped outcome are being paid unusually well for near-dated calls. Second, the elevated near-term put demand does not contradict a HOLD — it corroborates the view that the risk is event-shaped and that the sensible action is to wait 24 hours rather than take a position on undisclosed information. Short interest of 6.9% of float with 2.5 days to cover is unremarkable and provides neither squeeze fuel nor bearish confirmation.

**Risk factors score 3.0.** The combination of a dated binary with a ±19.3% implied move, an undisclosed acquisition P&L, a 364-day bridge maturing into an uncertain 2027 credit market, 69% two-distributor revenue concentration, 64% Greater China ship-to exposure against an active tariff regime, and no contracted forward visibility beyond 60 days constitutes an unusually dense stack of idiosyncratic risk relative to what a 54-68x forward multiple can absorb.

## Management Quality

The record is mixed and, on the forward-looking measures that matter, below average.

**Positives.** FY2025 repurchases of 1.8M shares for $100.0M at roughly $55.6 average were well timed against today's $124.27. The revolver was voluntarily reduced from $350M to $200M in December 2025 to cut carrying costs. The company initiated $60M notional of FX cash-flow hedges in fiscal 2026 — a first, and a sensible response to a cost base spread across Canada, India, China, the Philippines and Malaysia against USD-denominated revenue. The Q3 2024 restructuring plan ($11.4M cumulative) was expected to be largely complete by the end of Q2 FY2026, so those add-backs should fade.

**Negatives.** Stock compensation at 22.2% of FY2025 revenue is extreme for a company of this size, and much of it is non-deductible, which is why the effective tax rate hit 77%. The incentive plan's company-selected measure is TSR percentile versus the **Russell 3000** — a broad-market index, not a semiconductor peer set. Lattice ranked at the 77th percentile in FY2025 and the first tranche of FY2025 executive grants vested at **200%**, while the same proxy table shows cumulative company TSR of 171.65 against a disclosed peer-group TSR of 278.99. Maximum payout in a year of cumulative sector underperformance is a legitimate pay-for-performance design criticism, though the same plan produced negative comp-actually-paid of $(21.72)M in FY2024, so it is directionally responsive. FY2025 CEO total compensation could not be verified (the structured extraction returned zero, almost certainly a parsing artifact).

**Insider activity provides no support.** There were no open-market purchases in the reviewed window through July 15, 2026. Executive sales by SVP R&D Desale (~$1.9M in May, more in June) and SVP GC Feanny (~$416K) ran under 10b5-1 plans adopted February 18 and March 5, 2026 — before the AMI agreement — which substantially weakens any signaling read. The CEO and CFO transacted only for tax withholding. Discretionary director sales are harder to explain by diversification: Lederer sold 6,101 shares at $153.935 and Schwarting sold 3,200 at $124.53, leaving **6,636 shares**. Director positions are small enough that this is weak evidence, but the complete absence of buying by a board and management team that watched the stock triple is the absence of a positive signal.

**Capital allocation ahead is constrained.** Q1 FY2026 buybacks were only 165,913 shares for $15.0M at an average of $90.41, leaving $235.0M authorized — while RSU tax withholding consumed $13.8M in the same quarter. The company spent nearly as much retiring shares for employee tax obligations as buying stock for shareholders. With a $950M bridge to term out, further buybacks should be assumed deprioritized until the facility is refinanced.

Management quality scores 4.5.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $172 (+38%)

Q2 confirms the trajectory: standalone revenue near $190M, gross margin at or above 69%, and the first AMI disclosure shows $50M+ of quarterly revenue at software-like gross margin with a meaningful recurring support base and pro forma net leverage below 2.5x. The $1B run-rate target is validated with a clear path. The industrial and embedded leg inflects from +2% toward double digits as the two-year destock finally clears, giving the story a second engine independent of AI capex. Management terms out the bridge in H1 2027 into fixed-rate notes at a manageable coupon, removing the refinancing overhang. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS reaches roughly $2.85 including full-year AMI accretion, and the market pays 60x for a hardware-plus-firmware secure control platform with an incumbent socket position: $172, near the $175 analyst high.

### Bear Case — $78 (−37%)

AMI arrives smaller and lower-margin than the derived $180-260M band implies, and pro forma leverage lands nearer 4x than 3x. Hyperscaler platform builds digest after two quarters of front-loaded ordering; because Lattice has no contracted backlog and orders cancel inside 60 days, the revenue line turns fast, as it did in FY2024 when revenue fell 30.9% in a single year. Two distributors representing 69% of revenue reduce inventory simultaneously. Industrial stays flat. The Section 301 China semiconductor tariff scheduled for June 23, 2027 or a Section 232 expansion raises landed cost into a 64% Greater China ship-to base. The bridge is refinanced at a materially wider spread than committed pricing, consuming FCF. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS resets toward $1.95 and the multiple compresses to 40x: $78. A goodwill test against a depressed enterprise value in a single reporting unit becomes a live issue.

### Base Case — $130 (+4.6%)

Q2 confirms mid-to-high-30s percent standalone growth and AMI lands roughly mid-range on the derived contribution band, with pro forma leverage near 3.0-3.5x and a credible term-out path. The $1B run rate is approached but the timing slips into early 2027. Industrial improves modestly without inflecting. FY2027 blended non-GAAP EPS reaches ~$2.35 including AMI accretion net of interest, and the market applies 55x — a premium to the 30-45x range typical for high-quality AI-exposed logic, justified by the 68-70% gross margin and socket incumbency but not extended further given the absence of contracted visibility. That is $129, rounded to $130.

The distribution is the problem: roughly 5% upside to base, 38% to bull, 37% downside to bear. Probability-weighted, that is close to flat unless one assigns high confidence to the bull leg — and the single variable that would justify such confidence is disclosed tomorrow.

**Reassessment timeline:** August 4, 2026 (Q2 FY2026 results and first AMI financial disclosure), then the Q3 print in early November 2026 for the first full quarter of consolidated AMI results, then H1 2027 for the bridge term-out.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **AMI disclosure at or above the derived band with software-like economics** — quarterly revenue of $50M+ at gross margin above 75%, a disclosed recurring/support revenue share above 50%, and pro forma net leverage below 2.5x. That combination would compress forward EV/sales toward 15x on the $1B run rate and give the story contracted visibility it currently lacks.
2. **Industrial and Embedded revenue growth above 10% year over year for two consecutive quarters**, converting a single-engine AI story into a two-engine one and materially reducing the dependence on hyperscaler order timing.
3. **Price toward $95-100 on unchanged fundamentals**, which would put the stock near 41-43x next-year consensus $2.32 — inside the range we would pay for this franchise, roughly 20-23% below the current basis.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **Compute and Communications revenue declining sequentially in any quarter**, given no contracted backlog and 60-day cancellable orders — the leading indicator of a repeat of the FY2024 correction.
2. **AMI disclosed at below ~$35M quarterly revenue or below 65% gross margin**, or pro forma net leverage disclosed above 4.0x, which would mean roughly 9x+ revenue paid for a business that does not shorten the path to $1B.
3. **Two-distributor concentration exceeding 75% of revenue**, or any disclosed receivable allowance against those counterparties.
4. **Bridge refinanced at a spread materially above committed pricing, or extended rather than termed out**, indicating the 2027 credit market is not accommodating this credit.

**What would not change our view:** further share-price weakness on AI-complex sentiment absent company-specific deterioration (the June 23 SK Hynix episode is the template), or continued insider selling under pre-existing 10b5-1 plans.

## Conclusion

Lattice has done the hard part. Revenue is growing 42% with 68.8% gross margins and demonstrated operating leverage, the compute and communications franchise is up 86% on hyperscaler platform demand, cash conversion is strong at a 25% FCF margin, and consensus estimates have risen 16% over 90 days. The control-plane socket in server platforms is a genuinely defensible position, and the AMI acquisition attaches a firmware layer to it with coherent platform logic.

What the company has not done is make the price attractive. At $124.27 the stock carries 67.9x current-year and 53.6x next-year consensus non-GAAP EPS — figures that exclude stock compensation running at 16.7% of revenue — and roughly 18.1x enterprise value to management's own $1B revenue target, which itself requires 46% sequential growth over three quarters. The balance sheet went from zero funded debt to roughly $0.9B of net debt in nine weeks, on a 364-day bridge that must be termed out by late July 2027. Concentration is worsening on every direct measure, 64% of revenue ships to Greater China against a live tariff regime, and the filing states plainly that backlog does not predict demand.

The scores reflect that shape: strong growth (7.5) and a solid competitive position (6.5) against a demanding valuation (4.5), a weakened capital structure (5.5), below-average forward management signals (4.5), and an unusually dense idiosyncratic risk stack (3.0), for an overall 5.4. That is down from 6.0 in June — driven by the confirmed leverage event and worsened concentration, not by the share-price decline, which on its own improved the entry math.

Our prior three reviews were HOLD, and the bar for repeating a call should rise. It does here in substance: we are lowering the score, sharpening the exit triggers, and stating explicitly that this is an uncertain hold rather than a growth hold, because the variable that determines whether the price is defensible — AMI's actual revenue, margin and recurring mix — becomes public after the close on August 4. Options are pricing a ±19.3% move on that disclosure with front-dated implied volatility at 105.9% against 59.1% realized. Taking a directional position on undisclosed information one day ahead of its release is not analysis. Hold what you own, do not add, and re-underwrite on the numbers.
