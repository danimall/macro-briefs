## Executive Summary

Maximus Inc is a $3.2B government services company trading at 7.1x FY2026 guided adjusted EPS. The stock has declined 40% from its January 2026 high of $100 due to three identifiable headwinds: (1) federal procurement uncertainty related to the government's heightened focus on efficiency, (2) a severe DSO spike from administrative payment delays on one large federal contract, and (3) non-recurring FEMA disaster revenue rolling off. Despite these revenue headwinds, operating margins expanded from 9.7% (FY2025) to 11.4% (Q2 FY2026), driven by technology deployment, cost initiatives, and the absence of lower-margin disaster recovery work.

The CEO and CFO both made open-market stock purchases in early February 2026 at approximately $75-79 per share. They would have had visibility into the H1 FY2026 payment delays at that time (the issue existed throughout H1) and chose to buy anyway. At today's $60 price, they are approximately 20-22% underwater. Senior executives deploying personal capital despite known near-term challenges indicates belief that the issues are temporary and the stock is materially undervalued. However, the dollar amounts are relatively modest relative to their total compensation and existing holdings.

The key catalyst the market appears to underweight is the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), signed July 4, 2025, which doubles Medicaid redetermination frequency for expansion adults and adds work requirements — both driving new demand for exactly the services Maximus provides. Health insurers are explicitly prohibited from managing these community engagement programs, which narrows the competitive field, though it does not eliminate competition from other government services firms. With only 2 analysts covering the stock and estimates declining modestly (-3.3% over 30 days for current quarter), this is a thinly followed name where institutional knowledge is limited.

## Company Overview

Maximus operates three segments delivering tech-enabled government services: U.S. Federal Services (56% of revenue, operating VA Medical Disability Examinations and CMS Contact Centers), U.S. Services (32%, managing Medicaid eligibility/enrollment as the nation's largest provider), and Outside the U.S. (11%, UK disability assessments and employment programs). The company serves over 100 million American citizens through contracts with federal, state, and local government agencies.

The business model features long-duration contracts (average remaining life ~5 years on $15.3B backlog, providing 2.8x revenue coverage), high switching costs due to regulatory complexity and domain expertise, and limited competition given the specialized compliance requirements of government health and human services programs.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Trajectory — Temporary Headwinds Identifiable:**

| Fiscal Year | Revenue | YoY Growth |
|---|---|---|
| FY2023 | $4.90B | +5.9% |
| FY2024 | $5.31B | +8.2% |
| FY2025 | $5.43B | +2.4% |
| FY2026E (guidance) | ~$5.28B | -2.8% |

Q2 FY2026 revenue of $1.31B declined 4.1% organically, driven by: (a) ~$65M FEMA disaster work in H1 FY2025 not recurring, (b) Medicaid redetermination wind-down in U.S. Services, and (c) divestitures of Australia/Korea and U.S. child support business. Excluding FEMA, underlying U.S. Federal growth was approximately +3%.

**Margin Expansion — The Core Thesis:**

U.S. Federal Services operating margin: 12% → 17.6% over two years. Total company operating margin: 9.7% (FY2025 full year) → 11.4% (Q2 FY2026). Adjusted EBITDA margin reached 14.4% in Q2 FY2026 versus 11.6% in FY2024. This expansion is driven by a combination of technology deployment (AI/ML in medical assessments, automation in contact centers), cost initiatives, and favorable mix effects including the absence of lower-margin short-term disaster recovery work.

Management raised FY2026 adjusted EBITDA margin guidance by 20 bps to approximately 14.2%, and indicated on the earnings call that the near-term EBITDA margin target range has been raised to 12%-15%, up from the prior 10%-13%. For the first half of FY2026, operating income grew to $294.7M from $239.8M in H1 FY2025 despite revenue declines, though Q2 operating income itself was modestly lower year-over-year ($148.5M vs. $153.0M).

**Cash Flow — Legitimate Near-Term Concern:**

H1 FY2026 operating cash flow was negative $54.9M due to DSO spiking to 78 days on a reported basis (up from 62 days at fiscal year-end), or 102 days excluding the receivables purchase agreement (up from 73 days ex-RPA at fiscal year-end). The company attributes this to "administrative delays on payments from one of our large contracts with the U.S. federal government." To compensate, Maximus sold $682M in receivables through its Wells Fargo facility (versus $377M in prior year H1) and drew $225M on its revolver.

Q2 alone generated $189.5M of operating cash flow and $179.0M of free cash flow — a significant recovery from the severely negative Q1. Management reiterated FY2026 free cash flow guidance of $450M-$500M, indicating confidence in H2 normalization.

If payment delays persist beyond H2 FY2026, the company faces working capital stress. However, net leverage remains at 1.75x versus a 4.0x covenant limit — substantial headroom exists.

**Capital Return:**

Shares outstanding declined from 61.8M (FY2022) to 52.5M (May 2026) — a 15% reduction. A new $400M buyback authorization (12.5% of market cap) was announced in May 2026. At $60/share, this could retire approximately 6.7M additional shares (another 12%+ reduction). Combined with margin expansion and eventual revenue stabilization, the mathematical path to EPS growth is clear: 3-5% revenue growth + 100-200bps margin expansion + 5-7% annual share count reduction = 12-18% annual EPS growth.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term revenue growth is negative, but the company has identified specific catalysts for recovery:

**OBBBA Legislation (Primary Catalyst):** Effective December 31, 2026, though states may request a delay of up to two years. The law requires Medicaid redeterminations every 6 months (versus annually) for expansion adults, work/community engagement requirements, and SNAP error rate provisions. Health insurers are prohibited from managing community engagement, which narrows the competitive field to specialized government services firms like Maximus, though other government services contractors could also compete for this work. The company's 10-K cautions that the nature, extent, and timing of these changes remain uncertain and that federal procurement changes could lower barriers to entry. Some states may begin acting in anticipation of the effective date, while others may delay implementation, making revenue timing difficult to predict with precision.

**Revenue Base Recovery:** Non-recurring FEMA drag ($65M semi-annually) fully laps by H1 FY2027. U.S. Services segment is expected to trough in FY2026 as Medicaid redeterminations normalize, then inflect higher as OBBBA implementation begins.

**Technology-Driven Margin Expansion:** The 12%-15% EBITDA margin target (raised from 10%-13%) represents structural improvement. If revenue recovers to $5.5-5.8B over 2-3 years with 14% EBITDA margins, that implies $770-$810M EBITDA versus approximately $700M currently guided for FY2026.

Guided FY2026 adjusted EPS of $8.25-$8.55 with consensus at $8.44. FY2027 consensus at $9.07 (+7.5% growth). With OBBBA implementation, buyback accretion, and margin expansion, $10+ EPS by FY2028 is achievable.

## Valuation Assessment

MMS trades at 7.1x FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint ($8.40) and 6.6x FY2027 consensus ($9.07). Based on the company's TSR peer group (Booz Allen Hamilton, CACI, Conduent, ICF, Leidos, SAIC), the current multiple represents a significant discount. Even applying a meaningful discount for government concentration risk and near-term revenue declines, this valuation implies the market is pricing in permanent earnings impairment that the underlying data does not support.

**Why the market is wrong:**

1. The revenue decline is attributable to specific, quantifiable, and temporary factors (FEMA non-recurrence, Medicaid redetermination wind-down, divestitures). Underlying organic Federal growth ex-FEMA is positive.
2. Margins are expanding, not contracting. The market appears to be discounting the stock on revenue while ignoring that earnings power is increasing.
3. The DSO spike from payment delays is creating a misleading cash flow picture that institutional screens flag as deterioration. Q2 showed meaningful cash recovery, and FY2026 FCF guidance remains intact.
4. Only 2 analysts cover the stock — limited institutional attention creates mispricing opportunities.

**Upside math:** At 12x FY2027 EPS of $9.07 = $109 (82% upside). At 15x (closer to peer levels) = $136 (127% upside). Even a re-rating to just 10x forward earnings = $90.7 (51% upside).

The dividend yield of 2.2% provides income while waiting for re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

Maximus holds entrenched positions in government health and human services that are protected by several structural barriers: regulatory compliance expertise accumulated over 50 years, FedRAMP-certified technology platforms, established performance track records required for contract renewal, and relationship capital with government decision-makers.

The competitive set includes General Dynamics IT, Leidos, Booz Allen Hamilton, Deloitte, and Accenture — primarily for federal technology work. In state Medicaid services, Maximus is the dominant provider with few direct competitors of equivalent scale.

The OBBBA legislation strengthens Maximus's competitive position by prohibiting health insurers from managing community engagement requirements, eliminating what would have been among the most capable potential competitors. However, other government services firms could pursue this work, and the company's own 10-K notes that federal procurement changes could lower barriers to entry and expose Maximus to greater competition over time.

The $15.3B backlog with 5-year average remaining life provides visibility that is unusual for a company trading at 7x earnings.

## Risk Assessment

**Federal Procurement/Efficiency Disruption (HIGH):** The 10-K explicitly addresses a "significant focus on efficiency" by the U.S. federal government with respect to contracting with private companies. Specific risks include procurement changes, delays or cancellations of existing contracts, changes affecting demand/funding for state programs funded federally, additional competition if barriers to entry are lowered, and disruption from changing regulations. While Maximus argues it provides efficiency to government (not bureaucracy), across-the-board procurement reviews don't distinguish between helpful and wasteful contractors. The Q2 10-Q cited "administrative delays" on one large federal contract as the cause of payment slowdowns; this could be related to the broader government efficiency focus, though the company does not explicitly attribute it to any specific initiative. This risk is binary: either the disruption is temporary (6-12 months) and Maximus emerges with intact contract positions, or it structurally reduces federal outsourcing appetite long-term.

**Customer Concentration (HIGH):** 60% of revenue from top 10 contracts, 20% from a single federal agency, 55% from U.S. federal government total. Loss of any major contract would be devastating to revenue.

**Cash Flow Normalization (MODERATE-HIGH):** If federal payment delays extend beyond H2 FY2026, the company's balance sheet comes under increasing pressure. The Q2 recovery and reiterated FY2026 FCF guidance are encouraging, but the 1.75x leverage ratio, while having headroom to 4.0x, would erode with persistent negative operating cash flow.

**MOVEit Litigation (MODERATE):** Named bellwether defendant in MDL for 2023 data breach. Discovery ongoing, reserves accrued but undisclosed. Approximately half of claims survived dismissal motions.

**OBBBA Implementation Uncertainty (MODERATE):** While the effective date is December 31, 2026, states may delay implementation by up to two years. The nature, extent, and timing of revenue impact remain uncertain per company disclosures. Revenue contribution may not materialize until FY2028 or later.

**Goodwill ($1.78B, 44% of assets):** Concentrated in the VES acquisition for VA MDE contracts. Impairment risk exists if VA contract volumes decline.

## Conclusion

Maximus presents an asymmetric opportunity: a company with expanding margins, aggressive share buybacks, and a visible legislative catalyst trading at 7.1x earnings because of temporary, identifiable headwinds. The CEO and CFO buying stock at $75-79 with full visibility into the payment delays — and now sitting approximately 20-22% underwater — reinforces the thesis, though the dollar amounts are modest relative to total compensation. The 40% drawdown from highs is attributable to federal procurement uncertainty and cash flow optics, not structural business deterioration. At current prices, the market is pricing permanent impairment that contradicts the margin expansion trajectory, backlog coverage, and incoming legislative tailwinds. Risk/reward skews to the upside for a 12-18 month holding period.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The OBBBA competitive moat is less exclusive than stated (other government services firms can compete), OBBBA timing is uncertain (states may delay up to two years), margin expansion is partially driven by mix/cost factors rather than solely AI, and the peer P/E discount of 62% is based on an unspecified peer set — all of which may affect the confidence level of the target price range.
