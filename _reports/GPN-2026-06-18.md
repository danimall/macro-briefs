**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Global Payments completed a restructuring on January 9, 2026, simultaneously acquiring Worldpay (from FIS and GTCR, ~$17.0B total consideration: ~$6.0B cash + 42.8M shares) and divesting its legacy Issuer Solutions business (the former TSYS card-issuer franchise) to FIS for ~$7.5B cash plus FIS's Worldpay stake. The company is now a pure-play merchant acquirer/commerce-solutions provider. The strategic bet that scale plus embedded/integrated software-led distribution offsets commoditizing core processing is real and unproven, and the market has rendered a skeptical preliminary verdict: the stock has de-rated from ~$130 in early 2024 to ~$66, and the proxy's own pay-vs-performance table shows five-year TSR of 37.5 against a peer-group TSR of 203.5.

The investment debate reduces to whether the de-rating has overshot. On the bull side: ~$2B annual FCF (~26% margin), adjusted EPS growth of +10% YoY (Q1'26 adjusted EPS $2.96 beat consensus by 3.7%), a ~6x EV/EBITDA multiple at the low end of a uniformly de-rated peer cohort (FIS 7.5x, Fiserv 9.0x, PayPal 7.7x P/E), covenant compliance at the loosened 4.50x leverage limit, and aggressive buybacks. On the bear side: ~$22.6B of debt and goodwill/intangibles at ~74% of total assets to acquire a slower-growing (core payments -1.7%), higher-chargeback-risk asset; rising interest expense ($239M in Q1'26 vs. $145M PY); a credit-loss allowance up 109% YoY before Worldpay even consolidated; and a ~16% GTCR equity overhang.

We rate GPN a HOLD. The genuine deterioration argues firmly against a BUY, but the depressed price, trough multiple, serviceable leverage, and substantial free cash flow argue against terminal impairment. With the analyst low target at $60 (only ~10% below spot) and a positively-skewed reward/risk profile we would not short, the honest classification is an uncertain hold: the balanced positives and negatives roughly cancel, and no near-term catalyst resolves the question in either direction.

## Company Overview

As of January 9, 2026, Global Payments operates a single continuing segment, Merchant Solutions, organized around four reporting units: Core Payments Solutions, Integrated & Embedded Solutions, Point-of-Sale & Software Solutions, and International Merchant Solutions. Revenue is overwhelmingly transaction-based (percentage-of-value or per-transaction fees), recognized over time, and distributed through direct sales, ISOs, VARs, referral partners, and payment facilitators. The company retains a 45% interest in China UnionPay Data Services plus majority stakes in subsidiaries in Greece, Chile, and Germany.

The corporate identity has changed materially. By selling the TSYS issuer-processing franchise and folding in Worldpay, GPN exited a comparatively higher-multiple, stickier business (issuer processing) and concentrated itself in merchant acquiring, where it now ranks among the largest global players alongside Fiserv (Clover), FIS, Adyen, Stripe, Block, and bank-owned acquirers. Management's stated logic is simplification into "a leading pure-play commerce solutions provider for merchants of all sizes with extensive global scale." Whether that scale translates into durable per-share value is the open question the market is pricing.

## Financial Analysis

The reported financials are heavily distorted by transaction accounting and require disaggregation.

**FY2025 (continuing operations):** Revenue $7.71B, essentially flat versus $7.74B restated 2024 (Issuer Solutions now in discontinued ops). Operating income $1.75B (22.8% margin, down from 25.5%), pressured by $737.5M of acquisition/transformation costs versus $308.5M in 2024. Net income $1.40B; operating cash flow $2.66B; FCF $2.04B (~26% FCF margin). The free-cash-flow generation is the single most important fact supporting the bull case; it funds the dividend, buybacks, and deleveraging simultaneously.

**Q1 2026 (first quarter with Worldpay):** Revenue jumped 63% to $2.97B (Worldpay added ~$1.2B). On a GAAP basis the company reported an operating loss of $15.6M and a net loss of $1.80B (-$6.59/share). That headline is misleading: it is driven by $747M of acquired-intangible amortization (now 59% of cost of service), $387M of acquisition/transformation/transaction expense, and a one-time $1.6B tax charge in discontinued operations from derecognizing non-deductible Issuer Solutions goodwill on the sale. Adjusted EPS of $2.96 grew 10% YoY and beat the ~$2.86 consensus; adjusted net revenue rose 5.5% to ~$2.86B. The operational picture is stable; the GAAP loss is an accounting artifact of the swap.

**Balance sheet — the central risk.** Total assets ballooned to $64.3B (Q1'26) from $53.3B. Goodwill stands at $27.1B and other intangibles at $20.2B, together ~74% of total assets, creating real impairment risk if growth assumptions disappoint. Total debt is ~$22.6B (long-term debt $21.0B plus settlement/CP), including $16.2B of senior notes, $3.5B of convertibles ($1.5B due 2029 at a $138.71 conversion price; $2.0B due 2031 at $156.96, both deeply out-of-the-money against a ~$66 stock), a $1.6B revolver draw, and $1.1B of commercial paper. A $1.0B term loan was added in April 2026. Interest expense rose to $239M in Q1'26 from $145M a year prior, refinancing at 4.5–5.6% coupons. Debt/equity is ~1.29.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.43 nominally sits in the "distress zone," but this is characteristic of a goodwill-heavy, debt-financed acquirer rather than genuine bankruptcy risk; revenue/asset turnover of 0.14 and EBIT/assets of 0.03 mechanically depress the score. The leverage covenant was loosened to 4.50x post-deal, and the company is in compliance. Interest coverage and covenant headroom, not the Z-score, are the relevant gauges, and both are presently adequate.

**Capital allocation carries an internal tension.** GPN completed a $550M ASR in Q1'26 at ~$76, launched another $500M ASR in May 2026, carries $1.95B of remaining buyback authorization (raised to $2.5B total in January 2026), and maintains a $0.25 quarterly dividend (~1.5% yield). Repurchasing $1B+/year of stock, including an ASR at $76 now marked at ~$66, while carrying $22B of debt and promising to deleverage is an aggressive, arguably contradictory stance. It supports EPS optically but consumes cash that could de-risk the balance sheet faster.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is decelerating, and the disaggregation matters. The growth engine, Integrated & Embedded Solutions grew 6.6% in 2025, and POS/software (ex-divestitures) grew ~7%, led by the new "Genius" platform, is real but is being diluted by the lower-growth assets. Core payments declined 1.7% as GPN exits wholesale and parts of Asia-Pacific. The market views Worldpay as a slower-growing, higher-risk asset, and nothing in the first stub quarter contradicts that read: Worldpay contributed a GAAP operating loss of $138.7M in the period (purchase-accounting amortization).

Estimate revisions are mildly negative but not collapsing. Current-year consensus is essentially flat (-0.1% over 30 days, +0.0% over 90 days) at $13.85; next-year is $16.18 (-0.3% over 90 days); next quarter is flat. Only the current quarter shows meaningful erosion (-2.2% over 90 days to $3.49). The characterization of "broadly falling estimates" overstates the case; the picture is one of stable-to-slightly-softening forward earnings, consistent with a company digesting a major deal rather than one in fundamental decline. There is no strong upward revision velocity here, so the strongest forward BUY signal is absent; equally, there is no estimate breakdown that would force an AVOID.

The synergy and transformation programs are the medium-term swing factor: management targets $600M of run-rate synergies by year-end 2028, plus a separate >$650M transformation benefit by 1H 2027. If delivered, these materially lift adjusted EPS power. The risk is execution: the company is simultaneously separating Issuer Solutions (under a TSA to FIS) and integrating Worldpay, a dual-track challenge that runs costs through 2028 and that the 10-K explicitly flags as elevating complexity.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$66, GPN trades at ~26x trailing GAAP EPS of $4.43, a meaningless figure given the transaction distortion. On adjusted forward earnings the picture is very different: current-year consensus of $13.85 puts the stock at ~4.8x forward adjusted EPS, and next-year $16.18 at ~4.1x. On enterprise value, the ~6x EV/EBITDA cited in sell-side work sits at the low end of a uniformly de-rated payments cohort.

Peer comparison underscores that the entire group has been re-rated downward: FIS trades at 7.5x P/E (53% below its 52-week high), Fiserv at 9.0x (70% below high), PayPal at 7.7x (48% below high), Jack Henry at 17.6x, Corpay at 21.0x; peer median P/E is 9.0x. GPN's compressed multiple is therefore partly sector beta, not a company-specific anomaly. The question the valuation score must answer is whether the discount within this cohort is justified. We think it is largely justified — GPN carries higher leverage (debt/equity ~1.29), a riskier acquired asset mix, and integration execution risk that FIS and Fiserv do not face to the same degree — but not punitively so.

Analyst targets frame a wide distribution: median $82 (+23% from spot), mean $94.52 (+41%), with a high of $194 and a low of $60 (-10%). Even the single most bearish covering analyst sees only ~10% downside, and the consensus skews positive (12 buy versus 18 hold, with only 1 sell and 1 strong-sell). The median +23% target and the analyst low at $60 together argue that the market has already discounted most of the bear case. We do not rely on these targets as a floor — in a depressed but uncertain situation they reflect ranges of opinion rather than confirmation — but they are a meaningful check against an overly bearish downside view.

We score valuation moderately. The trough multiple offers genuine support and asymmetric upside if synergies land, but cheapness on its own has been an unreliable signal in payments names where the de-rating reflected real structural concerns. There is no clean catalyst to force a re-rating, which keeps this from being a high valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

GPN is now one of the largest global merchant acquirers, materially larger in Merchant Solutions post-Worldpay. The competitive backdrop is intense and structurally challenging: Adyen and Stripe continue to win enterprise and platform volume with modern, developer-friendly stacks; Block and bank-owned acquirers compete at the SMB end; and Fiserv's Clover is a direct rival in integrated POS. Non-traditional fintech entrants increasingly disintermediate the traditional acquiring stack.

The bull case for competitive trajectory rests on the embedded/integrated software pivot, which carries stickier economics than commoditizing core processing. The bear case is that GPN sold its crown-jewel issuer franchise — a higher-quality, higher-multiple business — to double down on lower-multiple merchant acquiring where competition is fiercest and pricing pressure most acute. The five-year TSR gap is a stark verdict that the prior strategy destroyed relative value; the new strategy is unproven. Worldpay's high-risk "future-delivery" merchant book also adds chargeback exposure that competitors with cleaner books do not carry. On balance the competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-deteriorating, with the software mix shift the only clearly positive vector.

## Risk Assessment

**Integration and execution (elevated, new).** The dual-track separation of Issuer Solutions and integration of Worldpay is the dominant near-term risk. Synergy and transformation delivery are not yet evidenced; slippage would pressure the adjusted-EPS trajectory the entire bull case depends on.

**Leverage and credit-rating risk.** Rising interest expense refinancing in a higher-for-longer rate environment. A downgrade below investment grade would raise borrowing costs (the revolver steps up on downgrade) and restrict capital-markets access. The intangible-heavy balance sheet creates impairment risk if growth disappoints; a $33.2M Issuer Solutions goodwill impairment was already taken in 2025.

**High-risk merchant chargeback exposure (new).** The 10-K states Worldpay serves "a large portfolio of high-risk merchants who promise future delivery of goods and services," explicitly raising chargeback/credit exposure. The allowance for credit losses rose to $50.2M, a thread to watch closely in coming provisions.

**GTCR overhang.** GTCR received 42.8M shares (~16% of ~273M outstanding) with a Shareholders Agreement and Registration Rights Agreement. This is both validation (a PE firm chose equity over all-cash) and a potential future supply overhang.

**Convertibles.** The 2029 and 2031 converts are deeply out-of-the-money, so no near-term dilution; the 2029 note is a refinancing item to monitor.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 42.4% sits modestly rich to 1-year historical volatility of 38.9% (a 3.5% spread), with IV in the 11th percentile of its own chain; the market is not pricing an imminent shock. The put/call volume ratio of 1.80 and OI ratio of 1.11 lean defensive, but the put/call skew is balanced (-0.9%), and the term structure is flat (near 42.4% ≈ far 42.9%), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a dated catalyst. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±5.6%. (Note: the reported single-point ATM IV jump to 374% in the options-delta series is a thin-options data artifact and is treated as non-informative.) Net read: options positioning is mildly cautious but not signaling a binary event, consistent with the balanced HOLD framing.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$95, +43%)
The de-rating has overshot. GPN generates ~$2B of FCF at a ~26% margin, trades at ~4–5x forward adjusted EPS and ~6x EV/EBITDA at the low end of a de-rated cohort, and is delivering adjusted EPS growth. If the synergy and transformation programs land on schedule, adjusted EPS power moves materially higher, and a re-rating toward the cohort median (9x EBITDA) plus mid-single-digit earnings growth supports the analyst mean near $95. The software/embedded mix shift provides a higher-quality growth vector that the market is under-crediting. Aggressive buybacks at trough prices amplify per-share recovery.

### Bear Case (~$58, -13%)
GPN overpaid for a slower-growing, higher-risk asset and financed it with debt and an intangible-heavy balance sheet. Integration slips, synergies underdeliver, organic core growth stays negative, chargeback provisions climb, and a ratings downgrade raises financing costs. The buyback-amid-deleveraging tension proves value-destructive. GTCR begins selling its stake into a weak tape. In that scenario the stock drifts toward the analyst low (~$60) and modestly below. Even this bear path is shallow relative to the prior $130-to-$66 decline; most of the structural skepticism is already in the price.

We anchor the base case near $74 (roughly the median of recent trading and modestly above spot), reflecting stable adjusted earnings, no near-term re-rating catalyst, and continued execution uncertainty.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (early August), with particular attention to the first disclosure of organic-versus-acquired performance once Worldpay enters segment reporting, plus updated synergy/transformation progress and credit-loss provisions.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence of synergy/transformation delivery ahead of or on plan (management quantifies realized run-rate benefit toward the $600M / >$650M targets), combined with stabilization of core-payments organic growth at or above flat.
- Adjusted net revenue organic growth re-accelerating above ~6% with margin holding, and forward consensus EPS revisions turning positive (+ over both 30d and 90d windows).
- A credible deleveraging path (net leverage trending toward ~3x) with no ratings pressure.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill or intangible impairment, or a ratings-agency downgrade below investment grade.
- Credit-loss provisions accelerating materially (allowance continuing to climb beyond the 109% pace), signaling the Worldpay high-risk merchant book is deteriorating.
- Synergy targets pushed out or cut, or organic core decline worsening beyond -1.7%, while leverage stays elevated.

## Conclusion

Global Payments is a balanced, uncertain hold. The genuine fundamental deterioration — decelerating organic growth, a slower-growing and higher-risk acquired asset, ~$22.6B of debt, integration execution risk, and the five-year TSR gap against peers — rules out a BUY at this stage; we want evidence of synergy delivery and organic stabilization before paying up. At the same time, the depressed price has already discounted most of the bear case: the stock trades at a trough ~6x EV/EBITDA within a uniformly de-rated cohort, generates ~$2B of FCF, grew adjusted EPS +10% YoY, remains covenant-compliant, and faces no near-term catalyst to drive further material downside. With the most bearish covering analyst at $60 and median consensus at $82, the asymmetry is positively skewed, which is why we hold and monitor rather than short or avoid. The balanced positives and negatives roughly cancel; the prudent stance is to wait for the Q2 print and the first clean organic/synergy disclosure to break the tie.
