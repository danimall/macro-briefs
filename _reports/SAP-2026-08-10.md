## Executive Summary

SAP's ADR fell from $295 in August 2025 to a 52-week low of $144.97 on July 23, 2026 — a 51% peak-to-trough derating — and has since recovered roughly 41% in eleven trading sessions to $208. The trigger was the Q2 2026 quarterly statement, which showed current cloud backlog of €22,929m, up 27% reported and 26% in constant currency, with less than one percentage point of that contributed by the Reltio acquisition. For a franchise whose derating was driven by the thesis that AI agents will disintermediate application software, an organically accelerating multi-year cloud commitment book is the most direct available falsification test, and it passed for this quarter.

The problem is entry arithmetic. At $208.36 the ADR trades at roughly 25.5x FY2026 consensus EPS of $8.17 and 21.7x FY2027 consensus of $9.60, against a ~€10bn FY2026 free-cash-flow guide that implies a 4.7% FCF yield. That is a defensible multiple for a wide-moat, A1/A+-rated compounder guiding constant-currency operating profit growth of 13–17%, not a mispricing. Consensus mean target is $242.92, 16.6% above spot, and 13 of 16 covering analysts are positive, which after a large move reads as confirmation rather than as an independent source of upside.

Three specific items keep us at HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE. First, the FY2026 FCF guide of ~€10bn against H1 delivery of €6,250m (up only 5% year-on-year) requires an implied H2 of €3,750m versus €2,301m in H2 2025, a 63% increase. We think most of that gap is the mechanical absence of 2024-restructuring cash outflows rather than an operational stretch, but it is unverified and it is the single most back-end-loaded line in the outlook. Second, SAP's own outlook states verbatim that it "is based on the assumption of a near-term de-escalation of the conflict in the Middle East," with "materially adverse consequences" flagged if the situation persists. Q2 cloud strength was concentrated in APJ and EMEA, so the transmission channel is the region SAP is currently leaning on. Third, the July 9, 2026 EU commitment decision converts the on-premise maintenance decision from all-or-nothing into a modular, per-landscape choice on a ~€10.8bn support base already guided to decline at an accelerating rate. Management's own characterization was non-committal: "There might be an impact, but we think we can manage it."

## Company Overview

SAP is the dominant vendor of the enterprise transactional system of record — finance, procurement, HR, supply chain and customer experience — for large, process-complex organizations. It is a German-domiciled foreign private issuer that files Form 20-F and Form 6-K rather than 10-K/10-Q/8-K, and whose executives report insider transactions to German authorities under EU market-abuse rules rather than on SEC Form 4. Two consequences matter for this analysis: there is no proxy statement disclosing compensation structure or board composition, and no insider-transaction signal is observable through US channels. The absence of that signal is a disclosure architecture fact, not evidence about insider behavior.

FY2025 revenue was €36,800m. The mix is now decisively cloud-led: cloud revenue was €21.02bn (57.1% of total) in FY2025 and reached 63.6% of revenue in Q2 2026 (€6,281m of €9,878m). Software support was €2,439m in Q2 (24.7% of revenue), software licenses €131m, and services the remainder.

Growth is narrow and concentrated by design. Within cloud, the Cloud ERP Suite generated €5,525m in Q2 — 88.0% of total cloud revenue — growing 25% reported and 27% cc. The Extension Suite grew 11% (12% cc) and IaaS resale fell 23% as SAP de-emphasizes infrastructure pass-through. Practically all of SAP's growth is migration and expansion of the core ERP estate into the cloud via the RISE and GROW programs. The mix shift toward cloud is genuine and organic; it should not be described as diversification, since product-family dependence is increasing rather than decreasing. No customer-concentration data is disclosed, and we make no claim in either direction.

The 2026 strategic posture is the "Autonomous Enterprise," announced May 12, 2026 alongside partnerships with Anthropic, AWS, NVIDIA, Palantir, Accenture, n8n and Parloa. SAP is a commercial consumer of third-party frontier models in these arrangements, not the owner of them. It also bought into the data and semantic layer, closing Reltio (master data management) on May 7, Dremio (open data lakehouse) on July 6, and Prior Labs (tabular foundation models) on July 16. Consideration was not disclosed; combined FY2026 non-IFRS operating profit dilution from Dremio and Prior Labs was quantified by management as "in excess of €100 million," and was the stated reason for trimming the FY2026 operating profit range by €0.1bn at both ends.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year revenue trajectory** (20-F XBRL, EUR): FY2022 €29,520m, FY2023 €31,207m, FY2024 €34,176m, FY2025 €36,800m — a 7.6% three-year reported CAGR. Reported growth understates operating momentum because of euro strength: SAP reports FY2025 constant-currency total revenue growth of 10.6% against 7.7% reported, an FX drag near 2.9 percentage points. FY2026 currency effects are guided at −1.5pp to cloud revenue growth and −2.0pp to non-IFRS operating profit growth at June 30, 2026 rates.

**Reported profit is too noisy to trend.** IFRS profit was €1,708m (2022), €5,964m (2023, the Qualtrics disposal year), €3,150m (2024, depressed by the restructuring program) and €7,326m (2025). FY2025 net margin was 19.9% and return on average equity 16.1% (€7,326m on average equity of €45,440m). We weight constant-currency operating profit, cloud backlog and free cash flow far above any single-year net-income ratio for this issuer.

**Q2 and H1 2026.** Total revenue €9,878m, +9% (+11% cc). Cloud €6,281m, +22% (+24% cc). Support €2,439m, −8% (H1 −9%). Licenses €131m, −32%. Non-IFRS operating profit €2,743m, +7% (+9% cc), with margin of 27.8%, down 0.7pp. Free cash flow €3,002m, +27%. For H1: revenue €19,432m (+8%), non-IFRS operating profit €5,609m (+12%), margin 28.9% — up 1.0pp — and FCF €6,250m (+5%).

The margin picture requires care in both directions. Q2's 0.7pp contraction sits against H1 expansion of 1.0pp, and management attributes the sequential deceleration to four named causes: slower revenue growth, an unusually low Q1 stock-based compensation charge that flattered the comparator, accelerated R&D, and Reltio dilution. One quarter is not a trend break. But non-IFRS cloud gross margin of 74.6% was down 0.6pp reported and 0.7pp cc, attributed to one-time test costs and sovereign-cloud environment build-out. Cloud gross margin was the primary engine of group margin expansion through the transition; that engine has paused, and group leverage in H1 came from elsewhere. A second consecutive quarter of cc cloud gross margin decline would materially favor a structural reading over the transitional one.

Cost intensity is rising in a specific place. R&D spending grew roughly 14% on approximately 3% R&D headcount growth, with higher-cost AI roles and large-language-model token costs cited as the gap. SAP is simultaneously a buyer of third-party inference capacity and a seller of AI-embedded applications; if token consumption scales with agent adoption faster than pricing captures it, the 74–75% cloud gross margin is where it shows up. The falsifiable counterweight is management's formal 2027 commitment that total operating expenses grow at 80–90% of total revenue growth.

**Free cash flow is the sharpest open item.** FY2025 FCF was €8,240m with H1 2025 at €5,939m, implying H2 2025 of €2,301m. The maintained FY2026 guide of ~€10,000m against H1 2026 of €6,250m implies H2 2026 of €3,750m, or +63%. Q1 2026 FCF, derived at €3,248m, was down roughly 9% year-on-year before Q2 rebounded 27%. SAP's cash flows are heavily first-half weighted by annual support invoicing, so quarterly run-rates are not annualizable. Our view is that most of the required step-up is the mechanical absence of 2024-restructuring cash outflows that were still running through H2 2025, which we could not verify. We treat this as a monitoring sensitivity at Q3, not a forecast of a miss; a nine-month FCF figure below roughly €7.3bn would make the €10bn unreachable.

**Balance sheet.** At 12/31/25: total equity €45,073m, cash €8,220m, goodwill €29,014m. Equity declined €733m during 2025 despite €7,326m of profit, implying roughly €8.06bn of distributions (dividends plus buybacks) and negative other comprehensive income, chiefly FX translation. Goodwill fell €2,250m over the same balance dates with no major disposal disclosed; a ~13% euro appreciation against roughly €17bn of dollar-denominated goodwill from legacy US acquisitions produces approximately €2.2bn, which fits closely, but an impairment or undisclosed disposal cannot be excluded, and the goodwill note was not read. Goodwill is 64.4% of total equity, so tangible book is thin. That is normal for a serial software acquirer and directly relevant to three 2026 acquisitions that will add further goodwill and intangibles.

Credit quality is solid and independently rated: A1 (stable) by Moody's, A+ (stable) by S&P. SAP completed a €3.5bn four-tranche Eurobond on May 28, 2026, with proceeds for general corporate purposes including financing recent acquisitions. Gross debt, net debt and covenant terms were not obtained, so we quote no leverage ratio. On what is established — €8.2bn cash, ~€10bn guided FCF, IG ratings, ready capital-market access — the balance sheet comfortably funds the acquisition program and shareholder returns. Financial health scores 7.0: strong cash generation and rating quality, offset by a declining book equity base, thin tangible book and an unverified H2 cash-flow hurdle.

**A basis warning on the reported earnings surprise.** The supplied surprise table shows Q2 2026 EPS "actual" of $1.59 against a $1.79 estimate, an apparent 11.1% miss. That $1.59 corresponds exactly to SAP's disclosed non-IFRS EPS of €1.59, and the prior quarter's $1.72 corresponds exactly to the derived Q1 non-IFRS €1.72 (H1 non-IFRS EPS €3.31). Converting €1.59 at the 1.14 EUR/USD rate SAP cites for June 30, 2026 gives approximately $1.81, broadly in line with the $1.79 estimate rather than an 11% shortfall. The table appears to compare euro actuals against dollar estimates and cannot be used to calibrate confidence in forward estimates in either direction. The price action corroborates this: the ADR rose from the July 23 close of $146.38 to $206.16 by August 7, which is not how a genuine double-digit miss trades.

## Growth Analysis

The forward indicator that matters is current cloud backlog, a multi-year committed-revenue measure, whose organic acceleration against a bearish AI narrative is the strongest single data point in the quarter.

FY2026 guidance, largely maintained: cloud revenue €25.8–26.2bn cc (+23–25%), cloud and software revenue €36.3–36.8bn cc (+12–13%), non-IFRS operating profit €11.8–12.2bn cc (+13–17%, trimmed from €11.9–12.3bn on acquisition dilution), FCF ~€10bn at actual currencies, non-IFRS effective tax rate ~29%. Management guides constant-currency total revenue growth to remain similar to 2025's 10.6% in 2026 and to accelerate in 2027, with the 80–90% opex-to-revenue-growth commitment supplying the operating leverage.

Two guided headwinds are explicit rather than speculative. Constant-currency support revenue decline is guided to accelerate in coming years as customers transform to cloud; annualizing H1's €4,908m gives roughly €9.8bn for FY2026, an absolute decline near €1.0bn against guided cloud growth of €4.8–5.2bn cc — about 5x coverage. The transition is comfortably self-funding today. The second-order risk arises only if cloud growth decelerates while support decline accelerates simultaneously. Separately, current cloud backlog cc growth is guided to "slightly decelerate" from 25%, with management attributing likely year-end softness to geopolitical tension and warning that bookings timing and conversion "could become lumpier."

Estimate revisions are mixed and net-neutral: next quarter +2.9% over 30 days and +7.6% over 90 days, but current year −1.3%/−0.1% and next year −1.4%/−2.1%. There is no positive revision velocity here of the kind that supports an aggressive re-rating call; the out-year number has drifted down 2.1% over three months even as the stock rallied off its low. Growth prospects score 6.3: the backlog is genuinely strong and the 2027 operating-leverage commitment is credible and testable, but reported growth is high single digits, the legacy base is eroding by guidance, and consensus is not being revised up.

## Valuation Assessment

Using the authoritative $208.36 basis and EUR/USD 1.14, the ADR is €182.8. On approximately 1,163m shares, market capitalization is roughly $242bn (€213bn).

Our bottom-up FY2026 EPS build reconciles to consensus: guided cc non-IFRS operating profit midpoint €12.0bn, less the guided 2.0pp FX drag on growth gives reported operating profit near €11.78bn; a ~29% non-IFRS tax rate and roughly zero net financial result give net profit near €8.36bn; divided by ~1,160m weighted shares gives €7.21, or $8.22 at 1.14 — within a rounding of the $8.17 consensus. That cross-check gives us reasonable confidence in the multiple.

The 21.7x FY2027 multiple sits below where SAP traded through most of 2025. The relevant question is whether that discount is opportunity or a rational reduction in the durability premium. We think it is mostly the latter, for reasons the market identified during the derating and that remain live: the EU commitment decision structurally weakens the switching-cost moat on a ~€10.8bn revenue pool; the cloud gross margin engine has paused just as AI cost intensity arrives; euro strength suppresses reported results that are the basis for published multiples; and the AI-agent threat to application-layer value capture is unresolved rather than refuted by one strong backlog print.

Against that, 21.7x forward earnings growing 17.5% with a 4.7% FCF yield, a ~2.5% annual share-count reduction from the €10bn buyback, and IG credit is not expensive. Our base case is $230, derived as 24x FY2027 non-IFRS EPS of approximately €8.40 (€202 → $230 at 1.14). That is +10.4% from spot over twelve months, plus roughly 1.1% dividend. Consensus mean target of $242.92 sits 5.6% above our base case. We do not treat the consensus target as a floor here: SAP does not have rising estimates, and out-year consensus has been cut 2.1% in ninety days while the price rallied off its low, which is the configuration in which targets lag on the way down rather than on the way up.

Valuation scores 5.3. There is no identifiable mispricing at 25.5x current-year earnings after the recent advance; the multiple is fair for the quality, and the margin of safety that existed at €150 (roughly 20.8x FY2026E) at the July low has been spent.

## Competitive Landscape

SAP's moat is the cost and operational risk of replacing the transactional system that runs a large enterprise's books. Two pieces of evidence support this rather than assert it. Q2 named RISE wins included Airbus, Eli Lilly, Gilead Sciences, Shell, Electrolux, Ørsted, Vonovia, Samsonite, Sun Pharma and Hindustan Zinc — large, process-complex incumbents re-committing rather than replatforming. And the German-speaking user group DSAG notes that customers committing to S/4HANA obtain a maintenance commitment to 2040, a fourteen-year forward lock, against ECC mainstream support ending 2027 (extendable to 2030).

The near-term competitive front is the third-party maintenance ecosystem, and the EU commitment decision reshapes it. SAP may no longer prevent customers from splitting their landscape into parts and choosing a different maintenance provider, a different SAP support level, or no support for each part; customers gain termination rights on workforce reduction or bankruptcy; reinstatement fees are abolished and back-maintenance is capped at the lesser of six months or 50% of fees for time away. The modularization converts an all-or-nothing decision into a partial, reversible experiment, which is precisely the structure that makes Rimini Street and Spinnaker Support economically viable at the margin.

Two counterweights deserve fair weight. DSAG argues the beneficiaries are mainly customers who had already decided against S/4HANA, since committed migrators hold maintenance to 2040 regardless; on that reading SAP formalized flexibility for a cohort it had largely conceded. And SAP's CFO offered a specific reversion observation: customers who try third-party maintenance "tend to be more nervous over time about incidents happening, and then come back." Set against that, Spinnaker Support's EMEA VP argues RISE migration is "slower than SAP needs" and that large-enterprise migration business cases repeatedly conclude "the return is not there," a directly self-interested claim, contradicted by 27% cc Cloud ERP Suite growth, but corroborated in neutral form by DSAG's own guidance that customers can keep proven core systems and selectively supplement them with cloud ERP components. Partial migration is exactly the path the EU commitments now make cheaper.

The structural competitive question is where value accrues in an agentic world. SAP's answer is that owning business-process logic and governed enterprise data matters more than owning the model, and every 2026 acquisition — Reltio, Dremio, Prior Labs — buys into the data and semantic layer rather than the application layer. That is consistent with management judging the application estate alone insufficient; it is equally consistent with conventional adjacent tuck-ins. Both readings are live. Competitive position scores 6.0: the trajectory in core cloud ERP is improving and share is being gained from the on-premise estate, while the regulatory foundation of the support moat and the location of AI-era value capture are both moving against SAP.

## Risk Assessment

**EU commitment decision (most underappreciated).** On a ~€10.8bn support base at very high incremental margin, an incremental 2pp per year of decline is roughly €215m of revenue and similar operating profit — about 1.8% of FY2026E non-IFRS operating profit annually, compounding. The commitments raise the probability that the guided support-decline acceleration exceeds plan. The reported ten-year duration of the commitments is unconfirmed by any primary source.

**Guidance conditioned on a geopolitical assumption.** The transmission channel is EMEA, where Q2 cloud strength was concentrated. A named-condition caveat of this specificity materially widens the FY2026 distribution.

**FCF back-end loading.** The +63% implied H2 requirement is most likely a soft comparator, but unverified.

**Margin and AI cost inflation.** The confirming signal for a structural read is a second consecutive quarter of cc cloud gross margin decline at Q3.

**Acquisition execution.** Three deals closing within ten weeks into the same data/AI layer, with undisclosed prices and >€100m of quantified FY2026 dilution, create integration concentration risk on an already intangible-heavy balance sheet.

**Currency and reporting basis.** For a dollar-based ADR holder the euro-strength drag partially reverses at the translation line, but guidance and published multiples are euro-based.

**Disclosure gaps.** No proxy, no compensation or board-independence assessment, no observable insider signal, no gross or net debt figure, no acquisition consideration, and the goodwill decline cause is inferred. These are structural to a foreign private issuer, but they mean several conventional governance and leverage checks are simply unavailable.

Risk factors score 5.2: no single item is existential for an IG-rated franchise generating ~€10bn of FCF, but there are two dated, specific catalysts for downside at the October Q3 print (FCF trajectory, cloud gross margin) plus a regulatory erosion mechanism and a named guidance condition, and after a sharp rally, the market is priced for those resolving favorably.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 39.7% against 38.6% one-year realized is fair, with the implied-vol percentile at 26% within the current chain. The most informative observation is the collapse from 90.3% on July 24 to 39.7% now while the price rose 32.3%: event risk around the print has fully discharged, so there is no cheap-volatility entry argument and no priced near-term catalyst. Term structure is flat (39.7% near, 38.2% far), meaning uncertainty is spread evenly rather than concentrated. Put/call open interest rose from 0.39 to 0.57 as the stock advanced, indicating protection was accumulated into strength, and the data source characterizes the 3.2% near-term put/call skew as elevated put demand. The six-month tenor prices a ±19.5% move, which brackets both our bull and bear cases. Short interest is negligible at 0.8% of float and four days to cover, so none of the rally is short-covering mechanics and there is no squeeze support underneath it. Options positioning corroborates a fair-value, two-sided setup rather than a directional opportunity.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($275, +32%).** Current cloud backlog holds above 25% cc into year-end, confirming that ERP cloud migration is insulated from AI-agent disintermediation because business-process logic and governed data are the binding constraint. The cloud gross margin drags — one-time test costs and sovereign-cloud build-out — prove transitional and margin re-expands at Q3. Nine-month FCF tracks toward the €10bn guide, retiring the back-loading concern. The 2027 commitment to grow opex at 80–90% of revenue growth converts accelerating cc revenue into 20%+ EPS growth, with the remaining €7.4bn of buyback retiring ~2.5% of shares annually. On FY2027 non-IFRS EPS near €8.50, 28x gives €238, or $271 at 1.14. Below the $319 street high.

**Bear case ($165, -21%).** The Middle East guidance condition fails and EMEA bookings slow, pushing cc backlog growth toward high teens. The EU commitments accelerate support erosion beyond plan as customers split landscapes and route parts to third-party maintenance. Cloud gross margin declines a second consecutive quarter as token costs scale with agent adoption faster than pricing, and FY2026 FCF lands materially below €10bn. FY2027 EPS resets to roughly €7.60 and the multiple compresses to 19x on reduced durability: €144, or $164. The July 23 low of $144.97, three weeks ago, makes this path concrete rather than theoretical.

**Base case ($230, +10.4%).** Backlog decelerates modestly as guided, cloud gross margin stabilizes near 74–75% without re-expanding, FY2026 FCF lands in the €9.3–10.0bn range, support decline steepens by a point or two, and FY2027 EPS reaches approximately €8.40 with the multiple holding near 24x.

Probability-weighting base 50%, bull 20%, bear 30% gives an expected return near +5%, plus a 1.1% dividend. That is approximately a fair risk-adjusted return, which is the definition of HOLD. The distinction from ACCUMULATE is margin of safety: at €150–155 (roughly $171–177, and near the $177 analyst low target), the same fundamentals would offer roughly 20.5x FY2026E and 30%+ base-case upside, which would clear the bar. At 25.5x with two unresolved items reporting in October, it does not.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Horizon:** 12 months, anchored to the Q3 2026 quarterly statement in late October 2026 and the FY2026 results and FY2027 guide in January 2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Q3 cc cloud gross margin returns to year-on-year expansion** with non-IFRS operating margin also expanding — this converts the AI/sovereignty cost drag from structural to transitional and is the single most important swing factor for FY2027 EPS.
2. **Nine-month FCF at or above €8.0bn**, keeping the ~€10bn guide credible and removing the 63%-H2 arithmetic overhang.
3. **Current cloud backlog cc growth at or above 25% at Q3** together with reaffirmation of accelerating cc revenue growth in 2027 and the 80–90% opex commitment.
4. **Price at or below $177** (roughly €155, ~21x FY2026E and ~18x FY2027E) on unchanged fundamentals — this alone would restore the asymmetry and warrant staged accumulation.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. A **second consecutive quarter of cc cloud gross margin decline** combined with any further cut to the FY2026 non-IFRS operating profit range.
2. **cc software support decline steepening beyond roughly 10–11%** with management attributing it to the EU commitments, which would validate the moat-erosion mechanism ahead of schedule.
3. **FY2026 FCF guidance reduced**, or nine-month FCF below approximately €7.3bn.
4. **Current cloud backlog cc growth below 22%**, or explicit disclosure of EMEA bookings impairment from Middle East escalation.

**Reassessment date:** Q3 2026 results, late October 2026. Both live uncertainties — the FCF trajectory and the cloud gross margin direction — resolve at least one increment at that print.

## Conclusion

SAP is a high-quality franchise that just supplied the best available evidence against the AI-disintermediation thesis for application software: 26% constant-currency growth in a multi-year committed cloud backlog, essentially all organic, from the deepest-moat product category in enterprise software. The balance sheet is IG-rated with €8.2bn of cash and a ~€10bn FCF guide, and the €10bn buyback is executing at an average price of €161.16 — below the current market, and therefore accretive on the tranche completed.

None of that is disputed. What we dispute is paying 25.5x FY2026 and 21.7x FY2027 earnings for it eleven sessions after a sharp advance off the 52-week low, with out-year consensus drifting 2.1% lower over the same period, and with two specific items reporting in October: whether cloud gross margin resumes expanding, and whether free cash flow can deliver a second half large enough to reach €10bn. Layered on both is the Middle East de-escalation guidance condition and the July 9 regulatory decision that makes partial defection from a ~€10.8bn high-margin support base materially cheaper for the first time.

Our base case is $230, roughly 10% above the current $208.36, against a bear case of $165 that the July low makes tangible rather than hypothetical. That is a fair, two-sided proposition. Existing holders should stay long and let the October print resolve the two open items. New capital should wait for either $177 or lower on unchanged fundamentals, or for a Q3 that puts cloud gross margin and nine-month free cash flow back on the right side of the ledger.

**Recommendation: HOLD** (growth hold — positive 12-month appreciation path with identified catalysts, inadequate margin of safety at the current entry).
