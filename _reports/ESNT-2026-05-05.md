## Executive Summary

Essent Group is one of six GSE-approved private mortgage insurers in the United States, with $248.4 billion in insurance in force and an exceptional cash-generation profile ($849M FCF on a $5.6B market cap). The stock trades at 8.7x forward earnings, approximately 1.0x book value, and yields 2.3% with a 15% FCF yield inclusive of buybacks. The company has been returning virtually all free cash flow to shareholders through an aggressive repurchase program that reduced shares outstanding by 6% in FY2025.

The core challenge is a confirmed loss normalization cycle that is accelerating. The MI segment's loss ratio rose from 3.7% (FY2023) to 8.8% (FY2024) to 16.8% (FY2025), and management explicitly states losses will increase further as 53% of the portfolio enters years 3-6 of seasoning — the historical peak-claim period. The last two quarterly earnings missed consensus by 7-10% due to elevated provisions, and forward EPS estimates have been revised downward by 2% over the past 90 days. This specific, quantifiable, and ongoing headwind prevents a BUY recommendation despite attractive absolute valuation. The stock is worth holding for its income characteristics and capital return program, with a clear upgrade path if loss experience stabilizes or the Lloyd's diversification accelerates.

## Company Overview

Essent operates primarily as a private mortgage insurer, providing credit enhancement on residential mortgages with less than 20% down payments, enabling borrowers to qualify for GSE-backed loans. Founded in 2008 and headquartered in Bermuda (with U.S. operations in Radnor, PA), the company benefits from having no legacy pre-crisis exposure: every policy was written since May 2010.

Key metrics as of year-end 2025:
- Insurance in force: $248.4B across 807,230 policies
- Risk in force: $56.5B
- New insurance written: $46.6B (FY2025)
- Persistency: 85.7%
- Weighted average FICO: 746 (41.9% of IIF at ≥760)
- PMIERs sufficiency: 169% of required assets
- Employees: 514

The company recently expanded into Lloyd's of London P&C reinsurance (starting Q1 2026) and owns a title insurance operation acquired in 2023.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Profile:**
FY2025 revenue of $984M was essentially flat versus FY2024 ($991M). Net income declined to $690M from $729M, with the difference attributable to loss provisions nearly doubling ($149.3M vs. $81.2M). EPS of $6.90 benefited from aggressive buybacks (share count fell 6.2% YoY) partially offsetting the earnings decline.

**Cash Flow Excellence:**
Operating cash flow of $856M and free cash flow of $849M (capex only $7.4M) translate to a 15.2% FCF yield at the current market cap of $5.6B. This is among the highest FCF yields available in financial services and reflects the capital-light nature of mortgage insurance once the book is established.

**Capital Return:**
The company deployed ~$710M in share repurchases in FY2025 — the most aggressive year in company history. Combined with dividends ($1.12/share × ~103M average shares ≈ $115M), total shareholder returns consumed approximately 97% of FCF. This is enabled by substantial excess capital above regulatory minimums (PMIERs sufficiency at 169%, risk-to-capital at 9.1:1 vs. 25:1 regulatory max).

**Balance Sheet:**
Debt-to-equity of 0.29x is conservative. The only term debt is $500M of 6.25% senior notes due 2029. The investment portfolio totals $6.5B in predominantly investment-grade fixed income, generating $236.5M in annual investment income. Holding company liquidity stands at $1.3B.

**Loss Trajectory (Critical):**
| Year | MI Loss Ratio | Default Count | Reserve/Default | Claims Paid |
|------|--------------|--------------|-----------------|-------------|
| FY2023 | 3.7% | 15,826 | $16.8K | $22.3M |
| FY2024 | 8.8% | 18,439 | $16.8K | $26.5M |
| FY2025 | 16.8% | 20,210 | $21.3K | $44.9M |

Claim severity rose to 75% from 63%. The combined ratio for the MI segment moved from 22.8% to 32.9% over two years.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth is effectively zero at the top line. NIW of $46.6B in FY2025 represents approximately 15% market share in a constrained origination environment (~$2.05 trillion total originations). Growth prospects are limited by:

1. **Elevated mortgage rates suppress origination volumes.** Until 30-year mortgages fall meaningfully below 6%, the purchase market remains constrained and refinance activity is minimal.

2. **Persistency acts as a buffer but not a growth driver.** At 85.7%, the existing book is sticky, but this simply maintains IIF rather than growing it.

3. **Lloyd's expansion offers modest diversification.** Management guides $100-150M in P&C reinsurance premiums for 2026, representing 10-15% incremental revenue if achieved. Execution risk is meaningful in a competitive market where Essent lacks track record.

4. **EPS growth comes from buybacks, not operations.** With flat-to-declining net income offset by 5-6% annual share count reductions, EPS can grow 3-5% annually through financial engineering alone. Forward estimates reflect this: $7.18 current year, $7.75 next year (+8% growth driven by lower share count and modest revenue recovery).

5. **Estimate revisions are negative.** Current year EPS has been revised down 2.0% over 90 days. The pattern of missing estimates (two consecutive quarters at -7% to -10%) suggests consensus still hasn't fully absorbed the loss normalization.

## Valuation Assessment

ESNT trades at:
- 8.7x trailing and forward P/E
- ~1.03x book value
- 15.2% FCF yield
- 2.3% dividend yield
- 10.4% total shareholder yield (including buybacks)

These are attractive absolute multiples. The specific question is whether the market is correctly discounting: (a) the loss normalization trajectory, and (b) the lack of organic growth.

Relative to private MI peers (MGIC, RDN, ACT), ESNT trades at a discount to historical norms, possibly reflecting the two recent earnings misses and rising loss concerns. The peer median P/E for MI companies is typically 7-9x, placing ESNT at the midpoint.

The analyst consensus mean target of $67.81 implies 13% upside, with a range of $61-75. BofA maintains a Buy with a $68 target. The median target of $68.75 corresponds to roughly 9.5x forward earnings.

At 1.0x book with 12% ROE, the stock is fairly valued using a Gordon growth model (12% ROE - ~3% growth = 9% discount rate, P/B = ROE/r ≈ 1.3x in equilibrium). The discount to theoretical fair value exists because loss normalization is actively compressing ROE from its recent above-trend levels.

## Competitive Landscape

Essent operates in a concentrated oligopoly (six GSE-approved insurers) with meaningful barriers to entry. The competitive dynamics are stable: GSE approval processes take years, capital requirements are substantial, and pricing is partially regulated.

Key positioning advantages:
- No legacy book (all post-2010 underwriting)
- Bermuda tax structure (~5pp effective rate advantage over purely domestic competitors)
- Lean operations (514 employees vs. peers with 1,000-3,000+)
- High credit quality portfolio (746 FICO average)

The primary competitive concern is customer concentration accelerating from 39.9% → 50.2% → 59.3% (top-10 customers as % of NIW) over three years. This reflects industry consolidation among mortgage originators but creates bargaining power asymmetry that could compress pricing.

Relative performance has lagged: Essent's 5-year TSR of 186% compares unfavorably to a peer group TSR of 325%, suggesting the market has assigned a persistent discount to Essent's growth profile.

## Risk Assessment

**Loss Normalization (HIGH — 60-65% probability of further deterioration):**
The combined ratio trajectory (22.8% → 26.3% → 32.9%) could reach 40%+ within 12-18 months without a housing recovery. Each 1pp of combined ratio expansion reduces net income by approximately $8-10M annually.

**GSE Reform / Housing Finance Policy (MODERATE — tail risk):**
GSE privatization would disrupt the entire MI industry. The Trump Administration has historically favored reform but 17 years of conservatorship with no resolution makes near-term action unlikely. Basel III Endgame uncertainty adds a secondary regulatory vector — if bank capital rules don't recognize MI as a credit mitigant, demand could decline.

**Bermuda Tax Structure (MODERATE — 4 years remaining on LIP exemption):**
Loss of the LIP exemption from Bermuda's new 15% CIT would increase the effective tax rate by 3-5pp, reducing annual earnings by $40-50M (~$0.45-0.55/share).

**Lloyd's Execution Risk (MODERATE):**
Entering P&C reinsurance introduces catastrophe tail risk and requires different underwriting expertise. Long-term profitability of this line is unproven.

**30-Year Treasury at 5% — Dual Impact:**
Higher long rates support persistency and investment income (positive near-term) but freeze the housing market (negative for NIW growth) and raise mortgage costs toward levels that increase default probability on existing loans.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 66.8% is dramatically elevated versus 21.8% historical volatility — a 45pp spread indicating the market is pricing imminent stress. The term structure is in steep backwardation (66.8% 1-week vs. 23.4% 6-month), suggesting a specific near-term event is expected. The put/call skew of 5.7% confirms elevated downside hedging demand. The implied move of ±9.1% for the nearest expiry (May 15) is outsized for a stock that has traded in a $55-67 range all year. This options structure suggests either an imminent earnings event or macro catalyst is being priced — inconsistent with a confirmed next earnings date in August. The extreme backwardation warrants monitoring as it could reflect informed positioning around a credit event, housing data release, or GSE policy development.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($68-75, 13-25% upside):**
- Loss normalization peaks at 35-40% combined ratio (still highly profitable)
- Buybacks continue at 5-6% annual pace, compounding EPS growth
- Lloyd's diversification succeeds, adding $15-25M in annual earnings by FY2027
- Mortgage rates decline to 5.5-6.0%, stimulating $60B+ NIW without excessive prepayment
- Re-rates to 9.5-10x normalized EPS of $7.50+

**Bear Case ($48-52, 15-20% downside):**
- Loss ratio accelerates beyond expectations (housing downturn, claims severity increases)
- Customer concentration leads to pricing pressure from major originators
- GSE reform legislation introduced, creating policy uncertainty overhang
- Bermuda tax exemption challenged or lost
- Stock de-rates to 7x earnings on perceived secular decline

**Base Case ($63-67, 5-12% upside over 12 months):**
- Loss normalization continues but at a decelerating pace
- EPS grows 4-8% to $7.15-7.50 through buybacks offsetting loss headwinds
- Dividend provides 2.3% income
- Stock re-rates modestly from current 8.7x to 9x on stabilization

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters with loss provisions at or below prior-quarter levels, indicating peak seasoning has passed
2. Forward EPS estimates stabilizing or rising (30-day revision turns positive)
3. Price pull-back to $55-56 (7.5x forward EPS) providing margin of safety against further loss development

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. MI combined ratio exceeding 45% for two consecutive quarters
2. GSE reform legislation introduced with bipartisan support
3. Loss of Bermuda LIP tax exemption

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (likely early August 2026 for Q1 2026 results). Q1 will be the first quarter including Lloyd's P&C reinsurance results and will show whether loss normalization is decelerating.

## Conclusion

Essent is a high-quality cash generator trading at a reasonable valuation with attractive shareholder returns (15% FCF yield, 2.3% dividend). The specific factor preventing a BUY recommendation is the confirmed, management-guided, and quantifiably accelerating loss normalization cycle. The portfolio seasoning dynamic (53% entering peak-claim years) is a concrete, time-bounded risk that is partially but not fully reflected in the current price. The stock deserves to be held for its income characteristics and capital return program, with a clear path to upgrade once loss experience stabilizes.
