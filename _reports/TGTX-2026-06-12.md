**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

TG Therapeutics is a commercial-stage biotech built around a single approved product, BRIUMVI (ublituximab), an anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody for relapsing multiple sclerosis whose differentiating feature is a one-hour IV infusion given twice a year. The company has executed a fast commercial ramp — U.S. net product sales grew roughly 92% in 2025 to ~$594M, and Q1 2026 product revenue rose 69% year-over-year to $201.3M — and crossed into genuine operating profitability (Q1 2026 operating income $34.8M, ~17% margin). Over a six-week window in May–June 2026, the company delivered three positive clinical readouts: Phase 3 ENHANCE (single Day-1 infusion bioequivalence), positive Phase 1 subcutaneous BRIUMVI data, and positive Phase 1 subcutaneous ublituximab in myasthenia gravis. These materially reduce the lifecycle-extension bear thesis ahead of the pivotal subcutaneous Phase 3 readout expected YE2026/Q1 2027.

The complication is timing and entry. The stock has climbed from a $25.28 52-week low in August 2025 to $48.05, including a ~25% move in the two weeks into this report as the subcutaneous data landed. At $48-50 the shares now trade roughly at the analyst consensus mean target ($48) and near the median ($50), with the high target at $70 and a wide low at $17 reflecting the binary nature of the franchise. The bull case (subcutaneous Phase 3 success extending BRIUMVI into the self-administration segment Kesimpta owns) is increasingly well-founded, but much of the de-risking has now been priced in a rapid run.

We rate TGTX HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The franchise is real, IP-protected to ~2034 (with a patent family to 2042), and the next binary catalyst is genuine and favorable in expected value. But three issues prevent a BUY at this price: the stock has appreciated into the catalyst rather than ahead of it; operating cash flow is still marginally negative despite GAAP profitability that is heavily flattered by tax accounting; and the company carries acute, partly uncontrollable tariff and most-favored-nation pricing exposure on a single product made at an ex-U.S. contract manufacturer. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the high-$30s or on confirmation that the subcutaneous program holds through Phase 3.

## Company Overview

TG Therapeutics operates as a single reportable segment built on BRIUMVI (ublituximab-xiiy), FDA-approved in December 2022 and launched in the U.S. in January 2023 for relapsing forms of MS. The product is in-licensed from LFB Group (mid-to-high-single-digit royalty). Ex-U.S. commercialization is handled by Neuraxpharm under a 2023 agreement: $140M upfront plus a $12.5M EU launch milestone and up to $492.5M in further milestones ($645M total potential) and tiered double-digit royalties up to 30%. TG does not control ex-U.S. pricing, which becomes a structural risk under U.S. reference-pricing policy.

The commercial proposition is convenience within the anti-CD20 class: a one-hour IV infusion every 24 weeks. BRIUMVI competes against Roche's Ocrevus (the dominant IV anti-CD20, now also available subcutaneously) and Novartis's Kesimpta (self-administered monthly subcutaneous injection).

The pipeline is concentrated on lifecycle management of ublituximab plus one cell-therapy asset:
- **Subcutaneous ublituximab** (Phase 3, enrollment complete April 2026; topline YE2026/Q1 2027) — the most important growth catalyst, aimed at the self-administration segment.
- **IV single Day-1 infusion** (ENHANCE Phase 3b) — met its primary endpoint in May 2026.
- **Ublituximab in myasthenia gravis** (positive Phase 1, June 2026; moving to registration-directed Phase 2) — a new indication.
- **Azer-cel**, an allogeneic CD19 CAR-T licensed from Precision BioSciences for progressive MS (first patient dosed August 2025; up to ~$286M milestones owed).

## Financial Analysis

The revenue ramp is the clearest positive. Total revenue moved from ~$234M (2023, including the Neuraxpharm upfront) to ~$329M (2024) to ~$616M (2025). Q1 2026 total revenue was $204.9M, with product revenue of $201.3M (U.S. $194.8M, Neuraxpharm $6.5M). Net income was $19.8M versus $5.1M in Q1 2025, even after absorbing a $9.2M one-time loss on debt extinguishment from the March refinancing. Diluted EPS was $0.12.

Two financial nuances are essential to scoring this name correctly, because a standard screen misreads both.

**GAAP profitability is heavily flattered by tax accounting.** FY2025 GAAP net income (~$447M) was dominated by a one-time ~$372M deferred-tax-asset valuation-allowance release in Q3 2025, when the company concluded sustained profitability made the DTAs realizable. Underlying pre-tax income was far smaller, roughly $107M. The Q1 2026 effective tax rate of 2.9% (thanks to NOLs) confirms cash taxes remain minimal, but it also means the trailing P/E of ~16.7x is calculated against a tax-inflated denominator. Operating cash flow was still slightly negative in 2025 and in Q1 2026 (-$17.9M), driven by working-capital build (inventory and receivables on extended launch-period terms). GAAP profit does not yet equal cash generation, though the gap is narrowing.

**Gross margins are normalizing downward for a structural reason.** Reported 2024–early-2025 gross margins were flattered because pre-approval inventory had been expensed to R&D at a zero-cost basis. That inventory was fully depleted by mid-Q1 2025, so cost of revenue is now rising toward a true commercial level. COGS rose from $15.5M (Q1 2025) to $33.5M (Q1 2026) on roughly $80M more revenue, with LFB royalties (~$20.6M/quarter) and third-party manufacturing the main components. Forward models must reset the margin baseline rather than extrapolate the prior reported levels.

The balance sheet was transformed by the March 2026 refinancing. The company replaced its $250M Blue Owl term loan with a new $750M facility (maturing March 2031, ~SOFR + 4.75%, amortization deferred to Q1 2030), a net ~$500M non-dilutive raise, with up to $250M more available. Cash and investments rose to $572.8M from ~$199M at YE2025. Total liabilities are $945.7M (loan payable $745M), and the accumulated deficit stands at $1.06B. Current ratio is ~5.8x post-raise and the Altman Z-score (~3.3) sits in the safe zone, but leverage is now meaningfully higher, the debt is secured against substantially all assets, and dividends are prohibited under the facility.

Capital allocation has been aggressive. The company completed its first $100M buyback (3.5M shares at ~$28.55), raised the program to $300M in March 2026, and repurchased ~$100M in Q1 2026 at ~$30.44 (cumulative ~$200M at ~$29.28 as of 4/30/26). The simultaneous debt raise plus buyback at ~$30 is a deliberate signal that management views the equity as cheap. The flip side is that repurchases are being partly funded with secured leverage.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has two legs: continued BRIUMVI share gains in RMS, and lifecycle expansion into self-administration.

The first leg is well-supported by the data. The 69% year-over-year product-revenue gain in Q1 2026, on top of 92% U.S. growth in 2025, indicates BRIUMVI is still taking share, helped by its twice-yearly dosing convenience. Estimate revisions over 90 days are sharply positive: current-year EPS consensus rose +22.2% (to $1.54), next-year +15.1% (to $2.87), and the next two quarters +24.3% and +29.9%. That revision velocity is among the stronger forward signals available and reflects a genuine fundamental ramp.

The more recent 30-day picture has softened: current-quarter EPS was cut -6.4% (to $0.43) and next-year nudged down -0.9%. The company has also missed EPS estimates in all four of the last reported quarters, by margins ranging from roughly -23% to -61%. Some of this reflects the margin-normalization dynamic the Street has not fully modeled and the working-capital drag, rather than topline weakness — revenue is beating while EPS lags. But a four-quarter miss streak warrants caution on the precision of forward EPS and tempers the otherwise strong 90-day revision signal.

The second leg, lifecycle expansion, is where the recent newsflow concentrates. The three readouts simplify onboarding (ENHANCE single Day-1 infusion), support a quarterly self-administered autoinjector regimen (subcutaneous BRIUMVI Phase 1), and open a new indication (subcutaneous ublituximab Phase 1 in myasthenia gravis). These reduce the technical-failure risk on the subcutaneous program — viscosity, immunogenicity, PK-equivalence — that the 10-K flagged. The decisive event remains the subcutaneous Phase 3 readout, which would directly target the segment Kesimpta dominates and counter Ocrevus's subcutaneous launch. Phase 1 success de-risks but does not guarantee the Phase 3 outcome.

## Valuation Assessment

At $48-50, the picture is balanced rather than asymmetric. The trailing P/E of ~16.7x is misleading because the denominator is inflated by the DTA release; on the more relevant forward estimate of $2.87 for next year, the stock trades at roughly 17x forward EPS. Against biotechnology peers (median P/E ~40x, with HALO at 25x, LGND at 32x, KRYS at 40x, RGEN at 134x), TGTX screens cheap, and a sub-30x forward multiple against 20%+ revenue growth and rising estimates would normally argue for upside.

Two factors restrain the valuation score from the 7-plus range. First, the forward EPS base itself is uncertain given the margin-normalization reset and the four-quarter miss streak, so the apparent cheapness rests on estimates that have a poor recent delivery record. Second, the stock has already moved sharply into the catalyst, climbing ~90% off the August low and ~25% in the past two weeks specifically on the subcutaneous data, and now sits essentially at the consensus mean target and just under the median. The wide analyst dispersion (low $17, high $70) reflects the binary nature of the franchise rather than a settled view of fair value.

The price appreciation itself is not a reason to mark the stock down; it is driven by fundamental de-risking. But the relevant question is whether $48-50 still offers asymmetric upside today, and the answer is closer to neutral: the easy part of the re-rating (Phase 1 success, single-infusion data, refinancing) is largely reflected, while the larger value step (subcutaneous Phase 3) is six-to-nine months out and binary. We assess fair value in the mid-$40s to low-$50s on current fundamentals, with a clear path to the $60s on Phase 3 success and downside toward the high-$20s/low-$30s on Phase 3 failure or a pricing/tariff shock.

## Competitive Landscape

BRIUMVI's competitive position is improving but contested. Its convenience wedge of a one-hour infusion twice a year has driven share gains, but Ocrevus now has a subcutaneous version that erodes part of that edge, which is why TG's own subcutaneous program is strategically essential. Kesimpta owns the self-administered niche that TG's autoinjector ambition targets directly. Longer term, BTK inhibitors and other novel MS mechanisms could shift treatment away from anti-CD20s, though that is a multi-year risk.

The IP runway is a genuine relative strength: 12-year biologic exclusivity (BPCIA) to ~December 2034, composition-of-matter patents to ~2029–2030 (plus patent-term extension), and a newer family of patents (issued February 2024) extending protection to 2042. Biosimilar risk is therefore distant, which supports the durability of the franchise's cash flows if the clinical and commercial execution continues.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the primary reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY, and several risks are specific and not fully priced.

**Single-product, single-indication dependence.** Essentially all revenue is BRIUMVI in RMS. Any safety signal (anti-CD20s carry infection/immunosuppression concerns), label restriction, or commercial stumble is existential.

**Tariff exposure is acute and specific.** BRIUMVI's bulk drug substance is produced ex-U.S. (Samsung Biologics, Korea; secondary FUJIFILM Diosynth). The April 2026 Section 232 executive order threatens up to 100% tariffs on imported branded pharmaceuticals, and the Q1 10-Q flags tariffs as an inventoriable cost. Management states the impact "to date" has not been material, but the structural exposure is larger than for domestically-manufactured peers.

**Pricing policy with an uncontrollable feedback loop.** CMS's proposed GUARD (Part D) and GLOBE (Part B) pilots and most-favored-nation reference pricing explicitly name CNS/MS agents. Because Neuraxpharm controls ex-U.S. pricing, low European prices could feed back into U.S. MFN reference points, a risk TG cannot directly manage.

**Sole-source manufacturing with take-or-pay commitments.** Non-cancelable purchase commitments total $304M (2026–2028). If demand undershoots, TG pays anyway.

**Higher leverage.** $745M secured against substantially all assets, restrictive covenants, ~$37.5M/quarter amortization beginning 2030; a default would let the lender seize assets.

**Subcutaneous Phase 3 binary.** Phase 1 success lowers but does not eliminate the risk that the pivotal readout disappoints, which would compress the lifecycle-extension premium now in the stock.

On the positive side of the risk ledger, the IP runway is long, the balance sheet is liquid, and the recent clinical de-risking is real. But the concentration of company-specific, partly external risks (tariffs, MFN, single product, single CMO) on a stock that has already re-rated keeps the risk score below neutral.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is balanced and does not contradict the fundamental read. Near-term ATM IV is 48.8% versus 47.2% historical volatility (a fair 1.6pp spread), with the IV percentile within the current chain at a low 16%; uncertainty is not extreme. The put/call skew is essentially flat (0.9%), and the put/call volume and open-interest ratios are very low (0.11), indicating no meaningful hedging demand against the position. The term structure is flat to slightly upward (48.8% near, 54.0% at the August tenor, 50.7% at six months), with the August expiry reflecting some event premium. The implied move to the nearest expiry is ±5.1%. The P/C OI ratio fell from 0.35 to 0.11 alongside the recent price move, consistent with call-side positioning into the catalyst rather than defensive hedging.

The standout structural feature is the 26.6% short interest (28.2M shares short, 13.5 days to cover). At this level the stock is two-sided: a positive subcutaneous Phase 3 readout could trigger a squeeze that overshoots fundamentals, while disappointment would validate the bears. High short interest combined with the recent run argues for caution on chasing the move, but also caps the conviction of any short thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** BRIUMVI continues taking RMS share on its convenience advantage, the subcutaneous Phase 3 reads out positively and opens the self-administration segment Kesimpta dominates, myasthenia gravis adds a second indication, and the long IP runway protects a durable cash-flow franchise. Operating cash flow turns positive as working-capital build normalizes, the $300M buyback at ~$30 proves well-timed, and the stock re-rates toward peer multiples on a larger, multi-product earnings base. Target $62-70 on Phase 3 success plus a short squeeze.

**Bear case.** The subcutaneous Phase 3 disappoints or is delayed, removing the lifecycle premium just as Ocrevus's subcutaneous version pressures BRIUMVI's convenience edge. Margin normalization continues to drive EPS misses, MFN/GUARD/GLOBE pricing pilots and 100% pharma tariffs on ex-U.S. drug substance compress economics, and the $745M secured debt looks heavier in any commercial stumble. Target high-$20s to low-$30s.

The expected value tilts positive given the recent de-risking, but at $48-50 — essentially at the consensus mean target and after a 25% two-week move — the asymmetry has largely been captured.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a growth-hold with a 6-to-9 month reassessment horizon tied to the subcutaneous Phase 3 readout (YE2026/Q1 2027) and the intervening quarterly prints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the high-$30s on no fundamental deterioration, restoring asymmetric upside (forward P/E back toward 13-14x on $2.87 estimates).
- Positive subcutaneous Phase 3 topline confirming the self-administration franchise extension.
- Two consecutive quarters of positive operating cash flow plus a revenue beat that breaks the EPS-miss streak, demonstrating the margin baseline has stabilized.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Subcutaneous Phase 3 failure or material delay.
- A BRIUMVI safety signal, label restriction, or evidence of decelerating U.S. net sales (sequential product-revenue decline).
- A finalized MFN/Section 232 action that materially impairs U.S. BRIUMVI economics, or a covenant breach on the Blue Owl facility.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings print (Q2 2026, expected early August) for the revenue/margin trajectory; the subcutaneous Phase 3 readout as the decisive binary.

## Conclusion

TG Therapeutics has executed well: a single product scaled to over $600M of annual revenue, a turn to operating profitability, a non-dilutive balance-sheet recapitalization, and three positive clinical readouts in six weeks that meaningfully reduce the lifecycle-extension risk. The IP runway is long and biosimilar risk is distant.

The constraint is price and risk concentration. The stock has run ~90% off its low and ~25% in two weeks into the catalyst, now sitting at the consensus mean target with the larger value step still six-to-nine months out and binary. GAAP profitability is flattered by a one-time tax item, operating cash flow remains marginally negative, margins are resetting lower, and the company carries acute tariff and most-favored-nation pricing exposure on a single product made offshore that it only partly controls. The 26.6% short interest makes the name volatile in both directions.

We rate TGTX HOLD. The franchise and the next catalyst are favorable in expected value, but at $48-50 the risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling. We would move to BUY on a pullback toward the high-$30s or on a positive subcutaneous Phase 3 outcome, and to AVOID on a Phase 3 failure or a material pricing/tariff impairment.
