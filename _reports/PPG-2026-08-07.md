## Executive Summary

PPG is a well-financed, globally diversified coatings company whose earnings power has not grown for three years. FY2025 adjusted diluted EPS from continuing operations was $7.58, down 3.7% from FY2024's $7.87. Reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of $7.70-$8.10 puts the midpoint at $7.90, still below the 2024 result. The reported reacceleration in revenue (1H26 net sales +6.9%) decomposes to FX +4, price +2, volume +1: roughly 58% of the growth is currency translation, which flows through with limited P&L offset because PPG hedges net investment with €3.5B and CHF320M of local-currency debt rather than hedging translation. Underlying organic growth of about 3% is better than 2025's 2%, not a step change.

The more important number is margin. Total segment income was flat at $743M in Q2 2026 on sales of $4,495M versus $4,195M, a 118bp contraction to 16.53% from 17.71%. Two causes: Middle East-driven raw material, energy and logistics inflation, against which 2% realized price covered only about 90% of COGS inflation; and a double-digit organic decline in automotive refinish, which management identifies as the highest-margin business inside the highest-margin segment. Performance Coatings income fell 7.6% on 7.1% higher sales, a 300bp EBITDA margin contraction. Management has committed to full price/cost coverage by Q4 2026 and to refinish returning to growth in Q3, a falsifiable and dated commitment that is the crux of the near-term thesis. Q3 guidance of flat to −100bp adjusted EBITDA margin indicates management does not expect the crossover before Q4.

At 14.8x the FY2026 guidance midpoint and 13.5x FY2027 consensus of $8.68, with a 4.5% trailing free cash flow yield and a 2.42% dividend at a 37% payout, PPG is not expensive. It is also not obviously mispriced. Consensus estimates have risen only 0.1-0.6% over 90 days; the analyst mean target of $126.20 sits 7.7% above the current price with a low target of $115 below it. Against that thin upside sit two unreserved litigation binaries expected to resolve inside 2026 and a competitive event: AkzoNobel and Axalta shareholders approved their all-stock merger on August 5, 2026, creating a single competitor overlapping PPG in all three reportable segments with $600M of company-projected synergies. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD on PPG and the score continues to drift lower (6.0 → 6.0 → 5.7 → 5.4). The decline reflects specific new information rather than price action: the Q2 margin contraction, the refinish divergence versus Axalta, the buyback slowdown, and the Akzo-Axalta approval.

## Company Overview

PPG manufactures paints, coatings and specialty products, was incorporated in Pennsylvania in 1883, markets in over 50 countries, and employed approximately 43,500 people at year-end 2025. Approximately 70% of FY2025 net sales were recognized outside the United States, a share increased by the 2024 divestiture of the U.S./Canada architectural coatings business.

Three reportable segments (FY2025):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Global Architectural Coatings | $3,838M | 24.2% | $599M | 15.6% | −11.7% |
| Performance Coatings | $5,513M | 34.7% | $1,148M | 20.8% | +0.5% |
| Industrial Coatings | $6,524M | 41.1% | $875M | 13.4% | −2.0% |
| **Total** | **$15,875M** | | **$2,622M** | **16.5%** | **−3.4%** |

Architectural is now entirely ex-North America (SIGMA, JOHNSTONE'S, TIKKURILA, COMEX, RENNER, TAUBMANS). Performance Coatings carries aerospace, automotive refinish, protective & marine, and traffic solutions, the highest-margin mix. Industrial Coatings is the largest by revenue and lowest by margin: automotive OEM, general industrial, packaging, and specialty products. EMEA is 33.8% of sales, statistically tied with U.S./Canada as the largest region. There is no customer concentration disclosure in either the 10-K or the 10-Q; distribution runs through company-owned stores, retail, independent distributors and direct-to-factory. The absence of concentration disclosure is not evidence that concentration is improving; the mix shift toward aerospace and packaging does not by itself establish customer diversification.

R&D was $446M in FY2025 including depreciation of research facilities, 2.8% of sales, essentially flat for three consecutive years.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and cost structure.** The 2023-to-2024 revenue decline from $18,246M to $15,845M reflects the divestitures of U.S./Canada architectural coatings and silicas plus the Russia and Argentina exits, not organic deterioration. FY2025 was flat at $15,875M (+1 price, +1 volume, +1 FX, −3 divestitures). COGS ex-D&A was 58.7% of sales versus 58.4%; SG&A 21.7% versus 21.4%. Gross margin eroded slightly (41.32% versus 41.61%).

**GAAP versus adjusted.** GAAP EPS from continuing operations rose 21% to $6.92 in FY2025 while adjusted EPS fell 3.7% to $7.58. The GAAP improvement is dominated by the non-recurrence of 2024's $239M restructuring charge, $146M Russia impairment and $110M Argentina cumulative-translation write-off. Reading the GAAP line as operating improvement would be wrong. Underlying earnings power declined.

**1H26.** Net sales $8,425M, +6.9% (FX +4, price +2, volume +1). Segment income $1,379M versus $1,350M, +2.1%, a 76bp margin contraction to 16.37%. Adjusted diluted EPS $4.06; reported diluted EPS $3.66. The $0.40 gap comprises $53M acquisition amortization, $25M New Jersey Chrome, $18M restructuring, $12M portfolio optimization and an $11M legal settlement. Environmental and legal add-backs have recurred in every period reviewed, and for a company with PPG's legacy footprint they are arguably operating in nature.

**Q2 2026 by segment.** Architectural sales +7.9% with income +15.6% to $185M, though FX contributed 6 of the 7.9 points and management cites favorable translation as an income driver. Performance sales +7.1% with income −7.6% to $329M, margin 20.3% from 23.5%. Industrial sales +6.8% with income +0.9% to $229M. Regionally, U.S. & Canada sales grew 1.6% and segment income *fell* to $320M from $360M while every other region grew.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow from continuing operations was $1,936M against capex of $778M, giving free cash flow of $1,158M, 7.3% of revenue and a 4.45% yield on the current market capitalization. 1H26 OCF was $594M against $309M capex; do not annualize, because the 10-K states cash from operations is greatest in Q4 on architectural and traffic-solutions seasonality. A conservative sensitivity holding FY2026 OCF at the FY2025 level with capex at the guided $650-700M gives FCF of roughly $1.24-1.29B (4.8-5.0% yield). The gating variable is working capital: operating working capital rose to 18.2% of annualized sales at 6/30/26 from 17.6% at year-end, and DSO extended to 63 days from 59. In FY2025 DSO extended 8 days to 59 in a year when sales were flat, without MD&A explanation. The allowance for doubtful accounts was essentially unchanged ($22M to $21M on $3.9B gross receivables), which argues against credit deterioration and favors a channel-mix explanation following the architectural divestiture. The year-over-year comparison (6/30/26 at 18.2%/63 days versus 6/30/25 at 19.4%/64 days) actually improved, which is the more meaningful read given Q4 collection seasonality.

**Balance sheet.** At 6/30/26: gross debt $6,886M, cash and short-term investments $1,594M, net debt $5,292M, matching management's stated $5.3B, down $415M year over year. Approximate FY2025 EBITDA of $2.67-2.74B puts net debt/EBITDA at roughly 1.9x. Interest coverage (EBIT/interest expense) is 9.5x. The total-indebtedness-to-total-capitalization covenant ratio was 44% at 6/30/26 against a 60% maximum (65% for six quarters following a >$1B acquisition), leaving roughly $3B of incremental debt capacity relevant to M&A optionality in a consolidating sector. Liquidity: $2.3B undrawn revolver ($2,148M running to July 2029), a new undrawn €250M Euro revolver from May 2026, zero commercial paper. Maturities have been systematically termed out: $700M 4.375% notes due 2031, €900M 3.250% due 2032, CHF180M/CHF140M due 2030/2034 issued June 2026, Term Loan extended to January 2029, and the $700M 1.2% notes repaid at March 2026 maturity from cash.

Recomputing screening metrics from the FY2025 balance sheet gives an Altman Z of approximately 3.82 (safe zone) and a Piotroski F-Score of 6 of 9, with failures on rising long-term-debt/assets, gross-margin erosion, and flat-to-lower asset turnover, the same three issues the operating narrative flags. The Z-score is flattered by the retained-earnings term (38% of the total) for a 143-year-old company with $22.9B of accumulated retained earnings against equity reduced by $15.1B of treasury stock; direction is right, cushion is overstated. Solvency is not the investment question.

**Capital allocation.** FY2025: $790M buybacks (6.9M shares), $628M dividends, $778M capex, $1M acquisitions. Distributions of $1,418M exceeded FCF of $1,158M by 22%, funded from cash and net debt issuance. Over three years PPG repurchased roughly 12M shares at an average of about $133, above today's $117.16. FY2025 marked the 54th consecutive annual per-share dividend increase and 126th consecutive year of payments; the $2.84 annualized rate is 37% of adjusted EPS and 54% of FY2025 FCF, comfortably covered.

The decision-relevant change is the buyback slowdown: 1H26 repurchases were $175M versus $540M in 1H25, a 68% reduction, with zero shares repurchased in April and May 2026 and 639,383 shares in June at an average $117.46. Cash fell $625M, $145M went to an unidentified bolt-on acquisition, and $700M of notes were repaid. Several explanations are consistent with the facts: the March maturity repayment; a CFO transition (Jamie Beggs took office July 6, 2026) and a new shareholder-approved equity plan constraining trading windows; redeployment to aerospace capacity and M&A; or balance-sheet preservation ahead of the 2026 legal decisions. The filings do not permit distinguishing among them, and I decline to infer motive. What is observable is that the per-share support that offset flat earnings in Q2 (management's MD&A explicitly attributes flat adjusted EPS to lower share count) has been running at one-third of last year's pace.

## Growth Analysis

Three years of no adjusted earnings growth is the governing fact. $7.87 (FY2024) → $7.58 (FY2025) → $7.90 guidance midpoint (FY2026). PPG would need the top of its own guidance range merely to exceed 2024.

**Guidance decomposition.** 1H26 adjusted EPS of $4.06 against FY guidance of $7.70-$8.10 implies 2H26 of $3.64-$4.04 versus 2H25 of $3.65. The range spans flat to +11%, midpoint +5%. The low end requires no second-half progress at all, which looks conservative given that 2H26 carries realized pricing, an easier refinish comparison, roughly $50M of restructuring savings, and a full-quarter bolt-on contribution. Consensus for the current year sits at $7.88, essentially at the guidance midpoint, and next year at $8.68 (+10% on the midpoint).

**Segment-level Q3 guidance from the 10-Q:** Architectural flat to +low-single-digit; Performance +mid- to +high-single-digit; Industrial flat to +low-single-digit. Weighting by FY2025 revenue shares gives roughly +2.5-3% total organic growth. Two secondary sources conflict on a company-level figure; the segment guides are the filing-grade data, and one outlet's "mid- to high-single-digit" appears to be the Performance Coatings guide mislabeled.

**Where growth is real.** Packaging coatings organic sales grew a high-single-digit percentage in FY2025 and a double-digit percentage in Q2 2026, with volumes up over 20% on a two-year stack, and PPG attributes European share gains to tightening regional regulation favoring its non-BPA/low-migration technologies. Aerospace is capacity-constrained with a disclosed customer order backlog of approximately $315M at year-end 2025 that *increased* year over year despite improved production; PPG defines this as the aerospace business's customer order backlog only, not company-wide backlog, RPO or deferred revenue. Industrial Coatings booked $100M of share gains last year with management claiming line of sight to further auto OEM outperformance.

**Where growth is absent.** EMEA sales declined 0.3% in FY2025; architectural EMEA organic fell a low-single-digit percentage in FY2025 and was flat in 1H26; management guides European and global industrial demand to "remain challenged." Automotive refinish fell a mid-single-digit percentage organically in FY2025 and a double-digit percentage in Q2 2026. The 10-K also carries a secular threat to that business, collision-avoidance technology reducing accident frequency, and an unusually candid self-cannibalization disclosure: adoption of "sustainably-advantaged" products (43% of FY2025 sales) "could adversely impact our sales volumes as customers may require a lower quantity of our products."

Estimate revisions are positive but immaterial in magnitude: current quarter +0.4% over 90 days, next year +0.6%. This confirms stability, not acceleration, and is nowhere near the threshold that would justify overriding a valuation or competitive concern. The earnings track record over four quarters is two beats and two misses, including a 2.1% adjusted miss in Q2 2026 and a 5.5% miss in Q4 2025; visibility is ordinary, not high.

Growth Prospects score: 4.8.

Peer context is messy: LyondellBasell and Albemarle carry negative trailing P/Es, DuPont prints 147.7x on depressed earnings. The usable comparables are RPM at 20.7x and IFF at 24.0x. PPG at 15.5x trailing adjusted / 14.8x forward sits materially below both. That gap is defensible in part, since RPM's end markets are more North America-weighted and its earnings have been growing, but a 25-35% discount to the nearest coatings-adjacent comparables on forward earnings is more than a growth-differential adjustment normally requires.

The question is whether the discount is a mispricing or an accurate assessment. Three pieces of evidence argue for accuracy. First, adjusted earnings have not grown in three years and the FY2026 guidance midpoint does not clear FY2024. Second, PPG's own proxy discloses five-year cumulative TSR of 78 against a peer index of 129 (base 100 at 12/31/2020), a 51-percentage-point shortfall that opened decisively in 2024-2025. A two-year relative derating of that magnitude is usually the market repricing normalized earnings power downward, not a temporary dislocation. Third, the two largest unreserved contingencies (Brazil, ethylene oxide) resolve inside the next six months with no reserve recorded, and PPG's low forward multiple is exactly what you would expect a stock to display ahead of unquantified binary outcomes.

Arguing for mispricing: the balance sheet is genuinely sound (1.9x net leverage, 9.5x coverage, 44% covenant ratio against a 60% limit), the dividend is covered twice over by FCF with a 54-year increase record, capex guidance has come down to $650-700M from $778M which mechanically lifts the forward FCF yield toward 5%, and the Q4 2026 price/cost crossover is a mechanical margin tailwind if realized price holds. Management characterized Q2 price realization as faster than in previous cycles, and covering 90% of an exogenous cost shock within two quarters is a reasonable industry outcome.

**Scenario framework** (all from the $117.16 basis):

- **Base $128** (+9.3%): 16.2x the FY2026 adjusted midpoint of $7.90, equivalently 14.7x FY2027 consensus of $8.68. Assumes price/cost turns positive in Q4, refinish stabilizes without proving out as share loss, and the multiple recovers modestly toward its recent range. With the 2.42% dividend, roughly 11.7% total return over 12 months.
- **Bull $148** (+26.3%): 17x FY2027 consensus $8.68. Requires refinish returning to growth in Q3 and Q4 as promised, full price/cost coverage, restructuring savings landing, FX remaining favorable, and both legal matters resolving without cash cost. This is the scenario in which PPG finally clears its 2024 adjusted EPS.
- **Bear $94** (−19.8%): 12.5x a reset $7.50 of adjusted EPS, roughly the 52-week low of $93.39. Requires the refinish decline to prove structural share loss rather than a distributor-order comparison, price/cost coverage to slip as Akzo-Axalta reinvests synergies into price, and/or a Brazilian execution proceeding that consumes cash and halts repurchases.

Probability-weighting base 50%, bull 25%, bear 25% gives roughly +6% price appreciation and about +8.5% total return, approximately the cost of equity for a levered global industrial. That is a fair-value verdict, not an asymmetric one. The analyst distribution corroborates: mean $126.20 (+7.7%), median $125, high $138, low $115, and the low target is *below* the current price. Analyst targets on a name with flat estimates and a compressed multiple do not function as floors; they reflect the same low-growth arithmetic.

Valuation score: 5.5. Cheap relative to peers, but the cheapness has persisted through two years of derating and lacks a proven catalyst. A low multiple on stagnant earnings ahead of unreserved binaries is the classic setup that punishes buyers of statistical value.

## Competitive Landscape

The defining development is not in PPG's filings. Between May and August 2026, three of the four global competitors PPG names were involved in major M&A:

- **AkzoNobel and Axalta are merging** in an all-stock merger of equals. The F-4 was declared effective 6/23/2026 and **both shareholder bases approved on August 5, 2026**, with completion expected within several months. Reporting cites $600M of synergies, the merging parties' own projection.
- **Nippon Paint and Sherwin-Williams jointly bid €12.5B (~$14.6B) cash for AkzoNobel**, rejected 5/27/2026 and abandoned 6/3/2026.

The overlap is direct and unfavorable. Axalta is PPG's closest competitor in automotive refinish, the highest-margin business inside PPG's highest-margin segment, and a significant one in automotive OEM. AkzoNobel leads EMEA architectural, PPG's weakest-performing business, and competes in marine/protective and industrial. A combined entity overlaps PPG in every reportable segment with a synergy pool that can be redeployed into price, technology or distribution. The failed Nippon/Sherwin bid separately establishes that two large strategics were prepared to deploy $14.6B for European coatings assets. PPG has the covenant headroom and revolver capacity to participate and has announced nothing of scale beyond a $145M unidentified bolt-on.

**The refinish divergence is the single most important unresolved question in this analysis.** In Q2 2026, PPG's automotive refinish organic sales declined a double-digit percentage. In the same quarter, Axalta reported refinish sales up 6% with record adjusted earnings. Management's explanation for PPG's decline has two parts: a front-half-weighted 2025 distributor order pattern (a genuine PPG-specific comparison artifact, fully consistent with the divergence) and weak U.S. collision claims driven by insurance premiums rising 16-17% in each of 2024 and 2025 (an industry-demand explanation that is harder to sustain if the closest competitor grew 6% in the same market). CEO Knavish explicitly denied share loss ("we haven't really seen any, frankly none that I know of, lost business as a result of our pricing efforts") and denied trade-down from premium to value.

The two figures are not strictly like-for-like: Axalta's +6% is a reported segment figure that may include price, currency and acquisitions, versus PPG's organic decline, and Axalta's refinish mix skews more toward value brands and international markets. Axalta's organic refinish volume is not available. This remains an unresolved divergence rather than established share loss. It is precisely the kind of question that determines whether Performance Coatings' 300bp EBITDA margin contraction is transient or durable, and it is unresolvable from public information until the Q3 print.

**Structural positives.** PPG's stated advantages are grounded in the filings rather than generic: on-site resin production at certain plants and formulation work to reduce titanium dioxide content are real cost levers against its largest production input; the packaging regulatory tailwind is specific and is converting to volume; LINQ subscriptions and MOONWALK automated mixing tie refinish share to customer productivity; alliances with Kansai (Japanese OEMs in North America and Europe) and Asian Paints (India) extend reach without capital. Aerospace demand exceeds capacity.

Trajectory, which matters more than level: packaging and industrial improving, refinish and EMEA architectural deteriorating, competitive structure consolidating against PPG. Competitive Position score: 5.7, down from 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Brazilian Di Gregrio judgment — a 2026-dated liquidity and headline event.** A 2006 Manaus lawsuit alleges PPG chemical products in a ship's cargo caused a 1998 fire. PPG transferred the liability in 2012-2013 to Eagle US 2, LLC (via Eagle Spinco), which became Axiall and then Westlake. Westlake has controlled the defense since 2013, but PPG learned in 2024 that Westlake never substituted itself into the Brazilian case, leaving PPG as nominal defendant. Westlake informed PPG that with prejudgment interest, fees and costs the award "would total over $700 million," or approximately $350M on simple interest, and that it "will not post any bond, pay any judgment, or take any steps to prevent the plaintiff from attempting to execute on the judgment against PPG." PPG sued in Delaware Chancery for specific performance and uncapped indemnification; Eagle Spinco's position is that indemnification is net of PPG's insurance recoveries. Bench trial was May 2025, an additional hearing is scheduled for **August 2026**, and PPG expects the trial court's final decision **in 2026**. PPG characterizes loss risk as "remote" and records no reserve.

The economically relevant point is funding sequence, not ultimate liability. Even a full PPG win in Delaware leaves timing and collection exposure in a Brazilian execution proceeding against an indemnitor that has declined to bond. A $350-700M gross exposure against $1.6B of cash is manageable but would plausibly interrupt repurchases. PPG's "remote" characterization is an audited disclosure judgment and carries weight; the amounts are Westlake's estimates relayed to PPG rather than court-confirmed, and the award is subject to appeal.

**2. Ethylene oxide litigation — first trial October 2026.** Approximately 400 Cook County plaintiffs allege injury from ethylene oxide emissions at a Gurnee, Illinois surfactants facility PPG owned from 1986 to 1997. Damages unspecified, no reserve recorded, PPG unable to estimate potential losses. This matter appears in the commitments note and legal proceedings but is not elevated to a standalone risk factor.

**3. Environmental.** Reserves $206M (12/31/25) → $214M (6/30/26), with New Jersey Chrome rising from $56M to $75M on a $25M Q2 charge. Reasonably possible but unreserved additional losses are estimated at $100-200M. Cash outlays: $20-40M for the remainder of 2026, $20-60M annually 2027-2030.

**4. Price/cost recovery.** The Q2 2026 10-Q added a disclosure absent from the FY2025 10-K: a Middle East military conflict has raised raw material, energy, logistics and packaging costs across the coatings value chain. The FY2025 10-K, filed 2/19/2026, had said 2026 raw material costs would "remain relatively flat," a view overtaken by events within about six weeks. Price/cost was positive in Q1, slightly negative in Q2, guided positive in Q3, with 100% coverage anticipated by Q4. Complicating recovery: index-based customer contracts in Industrial Coatings pass raw-material moves through on a lag tied to indices rather than PPG's actual costs; FY2025 Industrial selling prices were −1%.

**5. FX symmetry.** Currency added 4 points to 1H26 sales and $43M to pretax income. In FY2025 FX added $137M to sales but *reduced* pretax income by $8M. The current tailwind is disclosed, not concealed, but it reverses.

**6. Governance and management alignment.** Five-year cumulative TSR of 78 versus a peer index of 129 in PPG's own pay-versus-performance disclosure. CEO summary-compensation total rose 27% in 2025 to $14.89M in a year when TSR fell 20 index points and adjusted EPS declined 3.7%. The counterweight is genuine: compensation actually paid was $6.64M, 45% of grant-date value, and the 2022 CAP figure of $0.24M shows the mechanism bites. Design changes for 2026 increase alignment: Market Stock Units paying 0-200% on absolute three-year share price appreciation (118,989 granted in 1H26), and Performance-RSU payout shifting from frequency of meeting a cash-flow-return-on-capital target to the level of CFROC *growth*. Contingent TSR awards benchmark against the entire S&P 500, a non-self-selected comparator. Five Item 5.02 8-Ks since August 2025 (including two within ten days in December 2025) indicate officer churn I could not attribute. Insider activity is neutral: reviewing 25+ Form 4 entries from June-July 2026, essentially all are phantom-stock dividend-equivalent accruals and scheduled grants; two "P"-coded entries totaled under $1,400. No meaningful open-market purchases and no open-market C-suite sales. The absence of buying at a five-year relative low and the equal absence of selling both inform the neutral read. Net: neutral signal.

**7. Equity plan dilution.** The 2026 Omnibus Incentive Plan approved 4/16/2026 authorizes 6,903,753 shares, of which 1,500,000 are newly approved, 0.68% of the ~222.2M shares outstanding. Full issuance of the entire authorization would take existing holders from 100% to approximately 96.99% ownership, about 3.01% ownership dilution. These are multi-year authorizations, not outstanding awards; reported diluted shares are 223.9M with 3.2M antidilutive options excluded, and count has fallen from 227.7M year over year.

**8. Disclosure inconsistency on restructuring savings.** The FY2025 10-K says "approximately $50 million in incremental savings in 2026" (implying ~$125M total on 2025's realized $75M); the Q2 10-Q says "total restructuring savings are expected to be approximately $50 million in 2026" (implying savings *below* 2025). These are not reconcilable from the filings. The program targets ~$175M of annualized pretax savings from a $239M charge; reserve fell from $226M to $192M in 1H26 with $80-100M of 2026 cash outlays guided.

Risk Factors score: 4.5. Two unreserved contingencies quantified in the hundreds of millions resolve within roughly six months, environmental has $100-200M of unreserved reasonably-possible exposure, and the refinish share question is unresolved. These are specific, dated, and not visibly discounted in a 14.8x multiple that also has to account for stagnant earnings.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($148, +26.3%)

Refinish returns to growth in Q3 exactly as management committed, confirming the double-digit Q2 decline as a distributor-order comparison rather than share loss. Price/cost coverage moves from 90% to 100% by Q4, and the 118bp Q2 segment-margin contraction reverses into expansion against easy 2H comparisons. Roughly $50M of restructuring savings land, the $145M bolt-on contributes a full quarter to Performance Coatings, aerospace capacity debottlenecking converts a growing $315M order backlog into revenue, and packaging continues compounding on regulatory tailwinds. ## Conclusion

PPG is a financially sound, geographically diversified coatings company trading at 14.8x the midpoint of its own reaffirmed FY2026 guidance and 13.5x FY2027 consensus, with 1.9x net leverage, 9.5x interest coverage, a 4.5% trailing free cash flow yield, and a dividend covered at 37% of adjusted earnings and 54% of free cash flow with 54 consecutive years of increases. Nothing about the balance sheet or the dividend is in question.

The earnings are the question. Adjusted EPS has not grown for three years and the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $7.90 still does not clear FY2024's $7.87. Q2 2026 delivered 7.2% sales growth and zero segment income growth, with roughly 58% of the top-line move attributable to currency. The highest-margin business inside the highest-margin segment declined double digits organically in the same quarter its closest competitor reported refinish sales up 6%, a divergence management denies is share loss and that public information cannot resolve until the Q3 print. The sector is consolidating against PPG: the Akzo-Axalta merger creates a competitor overlapping PPG in all three segments with $600M of projected synergies to deploy. Two unreserved legal matters quantified in the hundreds of millions resolve inside 2026, and the options term structure is charging visibly more for that Q4 window than for the next month.

Probability-weighted expected total return of roughly 8.5% over twelve months, against a bear case 20% below the current price, is approximately fair compensation and no better. Existing holders are paid 2.4% to wait for a Q3 print that resolves the two questions that matter — refinish volume and price/cost crossover — and for a Delaware decision that removes or confirms a funding exposure. Prospective buyers have no reason to act ahead of that information. **HOLD**, with $105 as the level at which the margin of safety would justify accumulating and a Q3 refinish decline as the trigger to reduce.
