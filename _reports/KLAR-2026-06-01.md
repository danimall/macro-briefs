**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Klarna Group PLC is a global BNPL and digital-banking platform that listed on the NYSE in September 2025 at prices near $46-52 and has since fallen roughly 60%, trading at $17.67 against a 52-week high of $57.20. The company posted a Q1 2026 swing to operating profit that beat consensus, but the beat was driven materially by a quarter-over-quarter decline in credit-loss provisions, and management paired the print with an outlook that fell short of expectations. This beat-and-soft-guide combination is the central tension in the name: a one-quarter improvement in reported earnings sitting alongside management's own signal of moderating forward momentum.

The fundamental concern is that Klarna's profitability swing rests on the most cyclically sensitive line in a consumer lender's P&L, loan-loss provisioning, at a point where its forward guidance has come in soft. Klarna is compounding this exposure by deliberately shifting toward longer-duration, big-ticket loans, which carry higher balance-sheet credit risk and capital intensity than the 30-day "Pay in 4" product that built the franchise. Lower provisions flatter near-term earnings; if the soft guide reflects management's expectation of normalizing or rising losses, Q1 profitability is not a clean run-rate.

Estimate revisions corroborate the caution. Current-quarter EPS has been revised down 29.2% over 30 days and the next-quarter estimate cut 87.8% over 30 days; the full-year estimate, while up 58.3% in 30 days, is down 62.1% over 90 days, reflecting a chaotic, low-visibility forecasting environment with only 3-8 analysts covering. Trailing EPS remains negative (-$0.79), the trailing/forward P/E is meaningless at -33.9x, and short interest sits at 10.9% of float. We see no specific catalyst that resolves the credit-quality question favorably within a 12-month window, and the asymmetry skews to the downside. We rate KLAR **AVOID**.

## Company Overview

Klarna is a Stockholm-founded, UK-domiciled (Klarna Group PLC) global payments and digital-banking company that earns merchant fees plus interest and late fees on short-term point-of-sale consumer installment financing ("Pay in 4," "Pay in 30 days"), increasingly supplemented by longer-term big-ticket financing and banking/deposit products under a Swedish banking license. Company materials cite over 118 million global active users and approximately 3.4 million transactions per day, with the firm positioning itself as an "AI-powered payments and commerce network."

The listed entity is a foreign private issuer that files F-1 (IPO registration), 20-F (annual report), and 6-K (interim) forms rather than 10-K/10-Q/8-K. This reduces the granularity of routine US disclosure relative to domestic issuers and limits the standardized financial-health diagnostics available for the name; audited XBRL data, an Altman Z-Score, and Piotroski F-Score could not be independently retrieved for this analysis, and the financial figures below are drawn from public reporting around the Q1 2026 and FY2025 results rather than from the primary filings. That disclosure gap is itself a risk consideration for a recently-listed credit-exposed institution.

The market capitalization of roughly $6.7 billion at the current price is a substantial reset from Klarna's 2021 private peak of approximately $45 billion and below its rumored $15-20 billion IPO-era valuation. The market has already repriced BNPL economics and growth durability considerably.

## Financial Analysis

Reported financials (drawn from public reporting, treated as provisional pending the 20-F/6-K):

- **Revenue:** Approximately 25% growth cited for the recent period, with revenue described as having doubled since 2022 to a base around $3.4 billion annually in FY2025.
- **Profitability:** Klarna swung to operating profit in Q1 2026, with operating profit and revenue ahead of analyst expectations. Q1 strength was driven partly by a quarter-over-quarter decline in the provision for credit losses.
- **Trailing earnings:** EPS remains negative at -$0.79 on a trailing basis, producing a meaningless -33.9x trailing/forward P/E.
- **Outlook:** Despite the Q1 beat, forward guidance fell short of expectations, the defining disconnect in the print.

The earnings-surprise record shows the volatility of this model. Q1 2026 (period ending 2026-03-31) beat at -$0.01 actual versus -$0.19 estimate (+94.7% surprise), but the prior quarter (2025-12-31) missed badly at -$0.12 actual versus -$0.02 estimate (-424% surprise), and 2025-09-30 came in essentially in line at -$0.25. One beat, one in-line, one large miss over three quarters is not a track record that supports high confidence in forward estimates. A near-breakeven quarter on a substantial favorable provision swing, sitting between a sharp miss and a soft guide, reads as low earnings durability.

The mechanism that matters: a consumer installment lender books loan-loss provisions against expected credit losses; when those provisions fall quarter-over-quarter, reported operating profit rises mechanically even if the underlying loan book is unchanged or deteriorating. Provision releases are the swing factor explicitly cited as driving Q1 profitability. The durability of the swing therefore hinges entirely on whether loss rates stay low, and management's soft outlook is consistent with the possibility that they will not.

## Growth Analysis

Top-line growth of roughly 25% with a doubling of revenue since 2022 is genuine and reflects a real franchise with global scale. The AI-efficiency narrative, that Klarna doubled revenue while reducing operating costs and expanding margins, is the central bull thesis, but it originates with management and has not been independently validated against the filings. The "mispriced behind accounting noise" framing requires verification of IFRS expected-credit-loss modeling, fair-value marks, and share-based compensation before it can be relied upon.

The estimate-revision picture is the most important forward signal and it does not support a growth-acceleration thesis:

- **Current quarter:** -$0.07 estimate, revised down 29.2% over 30 days and down 252% over 90 days.
- **Next quarter:** $0.01 estimate, revised down 87.8% over 30 days and 94.9% over 90 days.
- **Current year:** $0.23 estimate, up 58.3% over 30 days but down 62.1% over 90 days.
- **Next year:** $0.72 estimate, up 7.1% over 30 days and down 4.6% over 90 days.

This is a low-visibility, high-dispersion forecast set where near-term estimates are being cut sharply even as full-year and out-year numbers wobble in both directions. The 30-day bounce in the current-year estimate is contradicted by the deep 90-day cut and by the severe near-term-quarter reductions, the pattern you would expect if Q1's provision release pulled forward earnings that the back half will give back.

The strategic pivot to longer-duration, big-ticket lending can lift revenue per loan and interest income, but it does so by lengthening credit duration and raising the balance-sheet loss exposure on each dollar lent. Growth purchased by taking more consumer-credit risk into a softening consumer is lower-quality growth than the merchant-fee-driven, short-duration model.

## Valuation Assessment

Conventional multiples are uninformative: trailing EPS is negative and forward EPS estimates are near zero with wide dispersion. The only peer in the supplied comparable set, Blue Owl Capital (OWL), trades at 86.2x P/E with an 8.9% yield, a fundamentally different business (alternative asset manager) that provides no usable valuation anchor for a consumer-credit fintech. More relevant comparables are Affirm (AFRM), Block/Afterpay (XYZ), PayPal (PYPL), and Sezzle (SEZL), against which Klarna's pivot invites comparison on loss rates and capital adequacy rather than GMV growth alone.

The bull argument is that the ~60% decline from the September 2025 debut and the reset from a $45 billion private peak to a $6.7 billion market cap make the entry far less demanding than at peak hype. That is true as far as it goes, but the burden of proof on a name down this far is to identify what the market is specifically wrong about. The decline coincides with a downward 90-day estimate trajectory, a chaotic forecasting environment, a soft management guide, and a deliberate increase in credit-risk intensity. The price action and the fundamental signals are pointing the same direction, which argues the repricing is informed rather than an overshoot.

The analyst target distribution underscores the absence of asymmetric upside at current levels: mean $22.89, median $20.00, high $45.00, low $17.00. The current price of $17.67 sits essentially at the analyst floor, and the median implies only ~13% upside. On a credit-exposed business where the central question is whether the next several quarters of loss provisions normalize higher, we cannot assign a high valuation score. The multiple reflects a market that has correctly identified an unresolved credit-quality question. We score Valuation 4.5.

## Competitive Landscape

Klarna's differentiation rests on scale, a full banking license enabling deposits and longer-duration products, and an AI-driven cost-efficiency claim. These are real advantages relative to monoline BNPL peers. But the competitive environment is maturing and consolidating under intensifying regulatory pressure (US CFPB, UK FCA, EU), and the field is crowded with well-capitalized incumbents: Affirm, Block/Afterpay, PayPal, Apple, and traditional card issuers all compete for point-of-sale financing.

The pivot to big-ticket lending moves Klarna closer to traditional consumer lenders, where the basis of competition shifts from merchant relationships and user experience toward underwriting discipline, loss rates, and capital cost. That is a more capital-intensive, lower-margin, more cyclically exposed arena where Klarna's scale advantage matters less and its balance-sheet risk matters more. Competitive trajectory is roughly stable rather than clearly improving; the franchise is large but the move into longer-duration credit is a strategic concession that the core short-duration model has limited room to drive the profitability the market wants. We score Competitive Position 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit/loss cycle exposure** is the dominant risk. Provision for credit losses is the explicit swing factor in Q1 2026 profitability. A normalization or deterioration in loss rates would reverse the favorable provision dynamic that produced the Q1 beat. The shift to longer-duration big-ticket loans amplifies this exposure.

**Guidance/expectations risk** is live: a soft outlook alongside a Q1 beat suggests management sees moderating momentum or rising caution. The market has not yet seen this play out in reported results.

**Regulatory risk** spans BNPL-specific scrutiny (CFPB, FCA, EU) and bank prudential/capital regulation given the Swedish banking license.

**Governance/disclosure risk** from foreign-private-issuer status: reduced US disclosure obligations and likely concentrated founder control (Sebastian Siemiatkowski), with dual-class dynamics to be verified in the F-1/20-F. The absence of standardized US filings limits transparency on a credit-exposed institution.

**Funding/deposit cost risk** as a bank: net interest margin and funding mix are rate-sensitive.

We score Risk Factors 3.5 — the credit-cycle and guidance risks are specific, asymmetric, and not yet visible in trailing data.

### Options Market Signal

The options surface signals near-term stress. ATM IV is 81.5% against 1-year historical volatility of 74.6% (rich by 6.9 points, 81st percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 81.5% > long-term 75.1%), which the market typically prices when it expects an imminent catalyst or elevated event risk. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.9%. ATM IV has collapsed from 166.7% on May 11 to 81.5% as the Q1 print resolved, while the price rose 21.9% to $17.67; the immediate event premium has unwound but uncertainty remains elevated. Skew is call-heavy (-8.1% near-term, P/C OI ratio 0.40), which reflects positioning for upside continuation rather than confirming the bearish fundamental view. The call-tilted positioning into a backwardated, high-IV structure is consistent with a crowded, momentum-driven post-earnings bounce that could reverse sharply if the credit-quality question reasserts.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Klarna's AI-efficiency narrative is real, the franchise has genuine global scale, the decline from the IPO debut has compressed the entry valuation substantially from peak hype, and Q1 2026 demonstrated the model can swing to profit. If the "mispriced behind accounting noise" thesis is correct (reported results understate true economics) and if loss rates stay benign while the big-ticket pivot drives revenue per loan higher, the stock could re-rate toward the $45 high target as profitability proves durable and the next-year $0.72 EPS estimate is achieved or beaten. Bull case ~$30 (roughly 70% upside), reflecting a re-rating on confirmed durable profitability.

**Bear Case.** The Q1 profit was substantially driven by a provision decline that reverses if US consumer credit deteriorates; the soft guide is consistent with management telegraphing this. Near-term estimate cuts bleed into reported results, the big-ticket pivot adds losses if the consumer weakens, and the name retests the $12.06 52-week low or below. Bear case ~$11 (roughly 38% downside).

**Base Case.** A choppy, low-visibility year in which provisions normalize higher, profitability proves uneven, and the stock holds in a $14-18 range near the analyst floor with no durable re-rating catalyst. The asymmetry from $17.67 is unfavorable: roughly 38% downside to the bear case against an upside that requires the credit-quality question to resolve cleanly in the bulls' favor, which we cannot underwrite given the soft guide and the provision-driven nature of the beat.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The next 6-K earnings report (following the May 14 Q1 release) is the relevant dated event. The bearish thesis requires that report to show loss provisions normalizing higher and profitability reversing, validating the soft guide. There is no dated debt-maturity or regulatory-ruling catalyst with a hard timeline; the credit-normalization thesis is real but its timing is diffuse rather than pinned to a single event.

**Timing window.** The next earnings catalyst is roughly one quarter out. That is within a manageable carry window, but the thesis depends on a credit-cycle turn that may take more than one quarter to surface in reported provisions, extending the effective hold.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 10.9% of float (30.1 million shares) with 4.8 days to cover. This is elevated but below the >20% threshold that creates severe squeeze risk. The position is moderately crowded but not extreme.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options positioning works against the short: near-term skew is call-heavy with a P/C OI ratio of 0.40, and IV is in backwardation. Rich near-term IV raises the effective cost of expressing the view through options, and the call-tilted positioning means the options market is not confirming the bearish thesis. A post-earnings momentum squeeze is a real risk given the 21.9% bounce off the recent low.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target ~$11 (-38%); upside squeeze risk toward the $30 bull case (+70%). That is roughly 0.5:1 reward-to-risk against the short, well below the 2:1 threshold, because the options market and recent price momentum are running counter to the fundamental view and the credit-normalization catalyst lacks a hard date.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamental concern is valid, but there is no dated catalyst with favorable mechanics, the options market is positioned bullishly against the thesis, and the reward-to-risk is unfavorable. This is a name to avoid owning, not one to actively short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria.** We would revisit a constructive view if: (1) two consecutive 6-K reports show stable-to-improving credit-loss provisions with profitability sustained independent of provision releases (confirming the Q1 swing was durable, not a one-time release); (2) management raises forward guidance rather than guiding soft, with corroborating positive 90-day estimate revisions exceeding +20%; or (3) the 20-F discloses provisioning methodology and loan-book composition that validates the "mispriced behind accounting noise" thesis with verifiable underlying economics. Verification of the actual 20-F/F-1 filings — currently unavailable — is a precondition for any upgrade.

**Timeline.** We expect the credit-quality question to persist for at least two to three quarters before the durability of profitability can be assessed. The big-ticket pivot's loss experience will take several quarters to season.

**Downgrade trigger from current view.** A sharper deterioration (rising provisions combined with revenue deceleration below 15% or a guidance cut) would push the name toward the lower end of its range and confirm the bear case rather than the current balanced-to-negative posture.

## Conclusion

Klarna is a large, genuinely growing global payments franchise that swung to profit in Q1 2026, but the swing was substantially driven by a quarter-over-quarter decline in credit-loss provisions while management guided forward expectations below consensus. That beat-and-soft-guide dynamic, sitting on top of a deliberate pivot into longer-duration, big-ticket credit, leaves the earnings inflection unproven and the risk profile rising. Near-term estimate revisions are being cut sharply, trailing EPS remains negative, and the stock sits essentially at its analyst-target floor with a median implying only ~13% upside against ~38% downside to the bear case.

The valuation has improved as the price fell while financial health and growth signals deteriorated, a divergence that reflects a cheaper stock attached to a softening fundamental story rather than an improving risk/reward. We see no specific, dated catalyst that resolves the central credit-quality question favorably within 12 months. We rate KLAR **AVOID** and would require multiple quarters of provision-independent profitability and verified filing-level economics before reassessing.
