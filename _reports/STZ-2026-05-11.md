## Executive Summary

Constellation Brands is the owner-operator of the #1 U.S. beer brand (Modelo Especial) through a perpetual exclusive license for Modelo, Corona, Pacifico, and Victoria in the U.S. market. The stock has declined approximately 25% from its 52-week high of $196.91 to $142.45, driven by the first volume decline in years (-2.1% beer depletions in FY2026), below-consensus FY2027 guidance ($11.20-$11.90 EPS vs. Street at $12.36), and the withdrawal of FY2028 outlook entirely.

The core question is whether the volume decline is cyclical (consumer caution amid macro uncertainty, which resolves) or structural (market saturation, demographic headwinds from immigration enforcement, GLP-1 drugs). At approximately 12x forward earnings with $1.8B in free cash flow, the stock offers decent valuation support if volumes stabilize. But falling estimates (-4.4% over 90 days), $10.6B in debt, and $800M/year in capacity spending into a decelerating market create a configuration where conviction is low on either side.

The competitive moat (perpetual brand license, #1 U.S. beer brand) is undeniable, but the growth narrative that justified a premium multiple has cracked. The 2026 FIFA World Cup and potential for guidance conservatism under a new CEO provide upside catalysts, while consumer spending deterioration, immigration policy risk, and leverage provide downside risks.

## Company Overview

Constellation Brands operates two segments: Beer (91% of FY2026 net sales, $8.3B) and Wine & Spirits (9%, $824M). The Beer segment produces Modelo Especial, Corona Extra, Corona Premier, Pacifico, and Victoria — all brewed at company-owned facilities in Mexico (Nava, Obregón, with Veracruz under construction). The company holds a perpetual exclusive sub-license to import, market, and sell these brands in the U.S., creating a deeply entrenched competitive position.

Following major divestitures (SVEDKA vodka for $409M; mainstream wine brands for $846M), the Wine & Spirits portfolio is now exclusively premium/super-premium brands. This segment is effectively breakeven ($10.5M operating income in FY2026) and represents strategic optionality rather than a meaningful earnings contributor.

The company completed a CEO transition on April 13, 2026, with Nicholas Fink (former Fortune Brands Innovations CEO, prior Beam Suntory executive) replacing Bill Newlands. This brings beverage industry expertise but introduces execution uncertainty.

- Beer operating income: $3.16B (38% margin, down from 40% YoY)
- Beer depletions: -2.1%; shipments: -3.8%
- Net income: $1.7B (vs. loss of -$81M in FY2025 which included Canopy impairments)
- EPS: $9.61 (trailing)

**Cash Flow:**
- Operating cash flow: $2.67B
- Capital expenditures: ~$875M (almost entirely Mexican brewery expansion)
- Free cash flow: $1.79B (18.4% FCF margin)
- Shareholder returns: $924M buybacks + $716M dividends = $1.64B (92% of FCF)

**Balance Sheet:**
- Total debt: $10.6B
- D/E ratio: 1.67x
- $2.0B available on revolving credit facility
- Interest expense: $353M (declining from $411M, aided by $805M lower average borrowings)

The Altman Z-Score of 1.74 technically places STZ in the "distress zone," but this is a mathematical artifact of capital-intensive brewing assets ($8.5B PP&E), $5.2B goodwill, and $10.6B debt producing low asset turnover. With $2.7B operating cash flow covering interest 7.5x, there is no near-term solvency risk. The leverage is real, however, and limits flexibility — particularly if volumes continue declining while $800M/year flows into capacity expansion.

**FY2027 Guidance:**
- EPS: $11.20-$11.90 (midpoint $11.55 vs. consensus $12.36 at time of guide)
- Capex: ~$800M
- Effective tax rate: 19-21%
- FY2028 outlook: **withdrawn entirely** — highly unusual for a Consumer Staples company and signals limited management visibility

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis for STZ historically rested on Mexican import beer taking share from domestic light lagers in the U.S. market. Modelo Especial became the #1 beer brand by dollar sales, accelerated by the Bud Light controversy in 2023. That thesis has materially weakened:

**Negative signals:**
- Beer depletions declined 2.1% in FY2026 — the first decline in a decade
- Shipments fell 3.8%, implying some distributor destocking
- FY2027 guidance implies flat-to-modest EPS growth at best ($11.55 midpoint vs. $9.61 trailing, but note trailing includes the strong Q4 beat)
- Estimate revisions negative across all periods: current year -4.4%, next year -4.0% over 90 days
- Company withdrew FY2028 outlook entirely
- Wine & Spirits is effectively a non-contributor after divestitures

**Positive signals:**
- TD Cowen upgrade (April 13) argues guidance is "overly conservative" under a new CEO
- 2026 FIFA World Cup (U.S./Mexico/Canada) provides a material marketing catalyst for Mexican beer brands
- Shipment-to-depletion realignment expected in FY2027 (management claims the 3.8% shipment decline overstates organic weakness)
- $200M+ cost savings program progressing
- $2.98B remaining buyback authorization could retire ~17% of shares outstanding at current prices

**Assessment:** The growth story is paused, not definitively broken. A 2.1% depletion decline in a weak consumer environment, with a major marketing catalyst (World Cup) ahead, could prove temporary. But the market has appropriately de-rated the stock given the uncertainty. The negative estimate revision trend is a concrete warning signal that the fundamental trajectory is deteriorating.

## Valuation Assessment

At $142.45, STZ trades at:
- Forward P/E: ~12.0x FY2027 midpoint ($11.55) or ~12.1x consensus ($11.82)
- Trailing P/E: 14.8x ($9.61 trailing EPS)
- FCF yield: ~7.3% ($1.79B / $24.5B market cap)
- Dividend yield: 2.8%
- EV/EBITDA: approximately 9-10x

**Peer comparison:** The peer median P/E is 32.6x (BG, CHD, HSY), which makes STZ appear extremely cheap. However, these peers have different growth profiles — CHD and HSY trade at premium multiples for stability. The more relevant comparison is that STZ has compressed from ~20x earnings a year ago to ~12x, reflecting the market's repricing of the growth narrative.

**Valuation support:** At 12x forward earnings with a 7.3% FCF yield and 2.8% dividend yield, the stock has meaningful downside protection unless the volume decline accelerates substantially. The perpetual brand license has a replacement value likely exceeding the entire enterprise value.

**Valuation risk:** If FY2027 comes in at the low end of guidance ($11.20) and the market applies a 10-11x multiple (appropriate for a declining-volume beer company with high leverage), downside to $112-123 exists (16-21% from current). The median analyst target of $184 implies 29% upside and suggests sell-side still assumes recovery.

The valuation is supportive but requires belief that volumes stabilize. It offers neither the deep margin of safety that would justify a BUY given the uncertainty, nor the overvaluation that would suggest AVOID.

## Competitive Landscape

STZ's competitive position is anchored by the perpetual exclusive license for Modelo/Corona brands in the U.S. Key dynamics:

- Modelo Especial is the #1 U.S. beer brand by dollar sales
- 6 of the top 15 share-gaining brands in FY2026
- Vertical integration (owned breweries, JV glass plant supplying ~60% of glass bottles) provides cost control
- Premium positioning insulates somewhat from trade-down behavior

**Competitive threats:**
- AB InBev is aggressively rebuilding Bud Light market share
- Craft beer and spirits continue fragmenting the market
- GLP-1 drugs represent a novel secular headwind to alcohol consumption (explicitly flagged in STZ's risk factors)
- RTD cocktails and cannabis beverages compete for share of "occasions"

The competitive moat is wide but the growth trajectory it enables has slowed.

## Risk Assessment

**High-impact risks:**

1. **Continued volume decline:** If beer depletions fall further in FY2027, the "growth company" re-rating becomes permanent and the stock likely trades to $110-120 (8-10x a declining earnings stream).

2. **Immigration enforcement impact:** STZ's brands disproportionately index to Hispanic consumers. Aggressive immigration policy that reduces the Hispanic consumer base or dampens spending would structurally impair demand. This is hard to model and hard to hedge.

3. **Tariff risk on Mexican imports:** All beer is brewed in Mexico. While IEEPA tariffs were struck down and Section 232 aluminum tariffs on beer removed, a USMCA renegotiation or new import duty mechanism could devastate economics overnight.

4. **Capacity-into-decline mismatch:** $800M/year in brewery capex (Veracruz starting August 2026) into declining volumes risks creating a fixed-cost absorption problem and potential impairment — echoing the Mexicali cancellation.

5. **Leverage in a high-rate environment:** $10.6B debt with maturities requiring refinancing at higher rates compresses returns to equity and limits strategic flexibility.

**Moderate-impact risks:**

6. **New CEO execution risk:** Fink has relevant beverage experience but no prior tenure at STZ. Integration period creates strategic uncertainty.

7. **Sands family dynamics:** Pledged shares could create forced selling events. Board nomination rights through November 2027 influence governance.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($175-185, +23-30%):**
- FY2027 volumes stabilize as consumer confidence improves and comps ease
- World Cup drives material brand activation and volume uplift in summer 2026
- New CEO guidance proves conservative (as TD Cowen argues); actual EPS closer to $12.50
- Aggressive buyback at depressed prices ($2.98B authorization) drives EPS accretion
- Multiple re-rates to 14-15x forward = $175-185

**Bear Case ($110-120, -16-23%):**
- Volume decline accelerates (depletions -3-4% in FY2027) as consumer spending weakens further
- Immigration enforcement materially impacts Hispanic consumer segment
- Tariff risk re-emerges on Mexican imports
- Company faces impairment on Veracruz capacity built into declining demand
- Multiple compresses to 9-10x declining earnings = $110-120

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FY2027 Q1 depletions turn positive (or decline <1% with positive trajectory)
- Management reinstates FY2028 outlook with growth targets
- Stock declines to $125-130 range (10.5-11x FY2027 midpoint), creating adequate margin of safety for the uncertainty

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FY2027 Q1 depletions decline >3%
- Management cuts FY2027 guidance below $11.20
- Material tariff action on Mexican beer imports
- Net leverage exceeds 3.5x EBITDA

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (likely early July 2026 for FY2027 Q1) will provide the first critical data point on whether volumes are stabilizing. The World Cup begins June 11 — early read on brand activation/sales lift should be visible in Q1 results.

## Conclusion

Constellation Brands owns exceptional assets (perpetual #1 U.S. beer brand license) at a reasonable valuation (12x forward, 7.3% FCF yield). However, the first volume decline in a decade, negative estimate revisions, withdrawn long-term guidance, a new untested CEO, and $800M/year in capacity spending into uncertain demand create a configuration where the risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling. The prior HOLD recommendation remains appropriate. The stock deserves monitoring through FY2027 Q1 earnings for evidence of volume stabilization — that data point will determine whether this is a compelling entry point or a value trap in formation.
