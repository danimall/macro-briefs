**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals crossed from clinical-stage to commercial-stage in November 2025 with the FDA approval of REDEMPLO (plozasiran) for Familial Chylomicronemia Syndrome, the first product from its TRiM RNAi platform. The platform's defining feature is extra-hepatic reach (silencing disease-causing genes in lung, skeletal muscle, adipose, and CNS tissue, not just liver), which has attracted an unusually deep partnership roster (Sarepta, Novartis, GSK, Takeda, Amgen, Sanofi, Madrigal). That ledger functions as built-in non-dilutive financing and drove a FY2025 revenue print of $829.4M (versus $3.6M in FY2024), almost entirely collaboration/license recognition rather than product sales. The headline near-breakeven FY25 EPS is an accounting artifact of deal timing; Q2 FY2026 reverted to a $(0.93) loss on $73.7M revenue as commercial spend ramped.

The stock has run roughly 317% from its 52-week low of $14.30 to ~$73, driven by the approval, a $930M January 2026 financing that fortified the balance sheet to ~$1.78B in liquidity, and accumulating pipeline data points (obesity combination results doubling tirzepatide's weight loss and the first dual-gene-target RNAi entering the clinic). Estimate revisions are broadly positive (current-year EPS revised +7.4% over 90 days), and the launch metrics are encouraging: over 400 prescriptions, ~180 patients shipped, ~85% APOC3-naïve indicating genuine new-patient identification rather than switching.

The case for restraint at this level rests on three items the price has partly absorbed but not resolved. First, Ionis sued Arrowhead in September 2025 alleging REDEMPLO infringes U.S. Patent 9,593,333, and Arrowhead's invalidity countersuit was dismissed on December 23, 2025, a recent setback on a litigation that threatens its only approved product. Second, revenue is milestone-dependent and lumpy, with partners holding levers (Sarepta already exercised a step-in right trimming Arrowhead's economics). Third, the capital structure is costly and intricate (15% PIK plus 2.0–2.5x MOIC Sixth Street facility, a $700M convert, a Royalty Pharma liability), and the Sixth Street covenants restrict licensing of core cardiometabolic assets, constraining the strategy that has worked best. With a binary sHTG Phase 3 readout pending in roughly Q3 CY2026 and options markets in backwardation pricing imminent stress, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate ARWR HOLD with a constructive bias toward upgrade on a clean sHTG readout or a favorable resolution of the Ionis dispute.

## Company Overview

Arrowhead is a Pasadena-based RNA interference company. Its TRiM (Targeted RNAi Molecule) platform delivers siRNA conjugates that silence target genes; the differentiation is the ability to reach tissues beyond the liver, where most siRNA competitors are confined. This breadth underpins both the scientific moat and the partnership model.

The business model is hybrid: Arrowhead owns its cardiometabolic franchise (REDEMPLO, obesity, mixed dyslipidemia) and out-licenses programs in other therapeutic areas to large-cap partners. The result is a roster with substantial potential milestone value: Sarepta (~$10B across 13 programs), Novartis (up to ~$2B, including ARO-SNCA for synucleinopathies), GSK (HBV plus MASH, >$1.8B combined potential), Takeda (fazirsiran in AATD, 50/50 U.S. profit share), Amgen (olpasiran, royalties monetized via Royalty Pharma), Sanofi (Greater China FCS marketing), and a May 2026 Madrigal license for ARO-PNPLA3 in MASH ($25M upfront, up to $975M milestones).

The lead owned asset, REDEMPLO, silences APOC3 to lower triglycerides. It is approved and launching in FCS, a small rare-disease population. The materially larger opportunity is severe hypertriglyceridemia (sHTG), in Phase 3 (SHASTA/MUIR) with data expected around Q3 CY2026 and Breakthrough Therapy designation; management and the Street frame sHTG as a roughly $4B opportunity. The company also holds early obesity assets (ARO-INHBE, ARO-ALK7) and a first-in-class dual-gene-target candidate (ARO-DIMER-PA, silencing PCSK9 and APOC3 in one molecule).

## Financial Analysis

**The FY2025 revenue inflection is deal recognition, not a run-rate.** FY2025 revenue reflects collaboration/license income, primarily ~$696.8M from the Sarepta transaction plus Visirna/Sanofi ($130M) and GSK. Reported operating income was a positive $98.3M and net loss attributable to ARWR was effectively breakeven at $(1.6M), or $(0.01) per share. None of this is recurring. A naive screen reading $3.6M → $829M and near-breakeven EPS would conclude the turnaround is complete; the correct read is that the print captures lumpy upfront/milestone recognition.

**Normalized economics returned with the FY26 quarters.** Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended 3/31/26) revenue was $73.7M against $542.7M in the year-ago, Sarepta-heavy comparable, with an operating loss of $(141.3M) and net loss of $(132.7M), or $(0.93) diluted EPS. R&D rose to $173.3M from $133.1M, and G&A to $41.7M from $28.4M, the latter reflecting the REDEMPLO commercial buildout. Deferred revenue jumped to $157.2M from $2.4M at fiscal year-end, signaling partner upfronts to be recognized in future periods. The earnings surprise record is mixed: two beats and two misses over four quarters, with the most recent quarter beating ($(0.93) vs. $(1.21) estimate, +23.2%) but the prior quarter missing.

**The balance sheet is substantially fortified.** Total cash resources at 3/31/26 were ~$1.78B ($188.5M cash plus $1,595.6M in securities), up from $919M at FY25-end, funded by the January 2026 dual offering: $700M of 0.00%-coupon convertible notes due 2032 (conversion price ~$87.08, ~35% premium, capped call to $119.33) plus $230M of common/pre-funded warrants at $64.50.

**The liability stack warrants scrutiny rather than the cash balance.** The Sixth Street credit facility carries ~$199.6M net value but is economically expensive: 15% PIK interest and a 2.0–2.5x MOIC repayment penalty, with >$140M in MOIC payments already made. Its covenants require maintaining ≥$250M liquidity (when market cap >$2B) and restrict licensing and IP sales on core cardiometabolic assets. A separate Royalty Pharma liability (sold-future-royalties on Amgen's olpasiran) sits at ~$383.8M carrying value at a 9.4% effective rate. Stockholders' equity is ~$614M; the ~3x debt/equity flagged on health screens reflects this layered financial engineering, not operating distress. Altman Z of ~2.16 places the company in the grey zone, consistent with a firm straddling clinical-burn and launch economics. With ~$1.78B liquidity, near-term solvency is not the issue; the watch-item is burn cadence ($141M operating loss in a single quarter) against the timing of milestone receipts and the REDEMPLO ramp.

We score Financial Health 6.0: liquidity and the partnership-funded model are genuine strengths, offset by costly capital, covenant restrictions on the best strategy, and a burn rate that depends on lumpy milestone timing.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are the strongest element of the case, and the estimate-revision trend supports it. Current-quarter EPS estimates were revised +5.7% over 30 days and +12.2% over 90 days; current-year estimates +7.4% over 90 days. These are losses narrowing rather than profits accelerating, so the inflection-stock template (revenue >40% with expanding margins) does not cleanly apply; the revisions reflect improving visibility on a pre-profit launch, not a trough-earnings recovery. We weight them as a moderate positive, not the dominant signal.

The substantive growth drivers are catalyst-based:

- **sHTG Phase 3 readout (~Q3 CY2026)** is the key de-risking event. FCS is a small rare-disease population; sHTG is the ~$4B opportunity that justifies the franchise valuation. A clean readout materially expands REDEMPLO's addressable market.
- **REDEMPLO launch execution.** As of the May 7 release, >400 prescriptions received/in-process (>40% growth in the trailing four weeks), ~180 patients with ≥1 shipment, ~30 new written scripts per week and accelerating, with ~85% of scripts for APOC3-naïve patients. The high naïve share indicates new-patient identification rather than switching, which is the favorable read for a launch.
- **Obesity optionality.** ARO-INHBE plus tirzepatide delivered -9.4% weight loss at week 16 (~2x tirzepatide alone), with -76.7% liver fat and -23.2% visceral fat; ARO-ALK7 is the first RNAi to knock down an adipocyte-expressed gene in humans (-88% to -94% ALK7 mRNA). This positions Arrowhead as a credible combination partner in the largest therapeutic market in pharma, complementary to incretins rather than head-to-head.
- **Platform validation.** ARO-DIMER-PA, the first dual-gene-target RNAi, has begun Phase 1/2a dosing. Global approvals are stacking (Canada NOC, Australia TGA, positive EU CHMP opinion with EC decision expected Q2 CY2026, China NMPA).

The durability of the growth thesis is buttressed by the breadth of large-cap counterparty validation; few clinical-stage platforms have signed this many big-pharma deals. The offset is that the revenue these deals produce is milestone-dependent and lumpy, and partners can shift economics (the Sarepta step-in right already reduced remaining variable consideration). We score Growth Prospects 7.0.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing and forward P/E are negative and not meaningful for a pre-profit biotech; the company will lose money for the foreseeable future (current-year consensus EPS $-3.00, next-year $-4.34). Valuation here is a sum-of-the-parts and catalyst-probability exercise rather than a multiple comparison. The biotech peer median P/E of ~40 (BMRN 40.7, KRYS 40.3, HALO 25.2, EXEL 17.4) is not a usable anchor because those peers have positive earnings; the closest profile, Cytokinetics, also trades on negative earnings.

The ~$10.4B market cap embeds: (1) the FCS franchise plus the much larger sHTG opportunity (~$4B peak sales potential, pre-readout), (2) a partnership ledger with double-digit-billions of potential milestones at varying probability, and (3) obesity/dual-target optionality. Analyst targets span a wide $46–$110, with mean ~$88 and median $91.50; the dispersion itself signals a binary-outcome valuation where the sHTG readout and Ionis litigation dominate the distribution. The current ~$73 sits below mean and median, which on an inflection name would argue the consensus is a floor. We apply that principle cautiously here because the upside is gated by a binary clinical readout and a live patent suit, not by steady fundamental momentum that analysts will simply chase.

The asymmetry is real but two-sided. A clean sHTG readout plausibly drives the stock toward the high end of the target range; an Ionis adverse ruling or an sHTG disappointment can take it well below the $46 low. At ~$73 with both events unresolved, we do not see a clearly mispriced entry; the market is pricing a balanced distribution. Valuation scores 5.0: fair for the risk profile, neither cheap nor stretched given the binary set ahead.

## Competitive Landscape

Arrowhead's competitive position is improving on the strength of platform breadth. In RNAi/siRNA it competes with Alnylam (the category leader) and Ionis (antisense/siRNA, now also a litigation adversary). The TRiM platform's extra-hepatic reach (muscle, lung, adipose, CNS) is the genuine technical edge and the reason Sarepta (neuromuscular/CNS/lung) and Novartis (CNS) chose to partner rather than build internally. That partner selection is the strongest external evidence of a durable moat.

In FCS, REDEMPLO competes against Ionis's approved olezarsen (Tryngolza). Arrowhead is pricing at a premium ($45,000/year WAC) based on a clinical profile it characterizes as superior (83% median TG reduction, no adjudicated pancreatitis in the two-year open-label extension, less-frequent dosing). The premium-pricing strategy into the much larger sHTG population, under a unified "One-REDEMPLO" price, invites payer pushback and is a key market-access execution risk.

In cardiometabolic and obesity, Arrowhead is entering the largest commercial arena in pharma with differentiated, durable, infrequently-dosed mechanisms. The obesity data positioning it as an incretin add-on rather than a direct Lilly/Novo competitor is strategically sensible and could make it an attractive combination partner. We score Competitive Position 7.0: improving trajectory, defensible platform, but facing entrenched leaders and an active patent adversary.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk profile is the binding constraint on the rating.

1. **Ionis patent litigation (the central overhang).** Ionis sued on September 11, 2025. Arrowhead's Delaware declaratory-judgment countersuit seeking to invalidate the patent was dismissed on December 23, 2025. An adverse outcome could threaten royalties, damages, or commercialization of the only approved product at the moment of launch. This is asymmetric downside the screens do not capture.

2. **Milestone-dependent, lumpy revenue.** Headline FY25 revenue is non-recurring. Future revenue depends on achieving partner milestones with uncertain timing plus the REDEMPLO launch and sHTG approval. The FCS population is small until sHTG.

3. **Partner concentration and leverage.** Sarepta is the largest economic relationship and already exercised a step-in right reducing Arrowhead's variable consideration; Sarepta has had its own clinical/safety turbulence in gene therapy. Partner financial and strategic health is a real dependency.

4. **Single-source manufacturing and distribution.** REDEMPLO drug substance/product relies on single-source suppliers, and U.S. distribution runs through an exclusive specialty pharmacy: channel concentration during launch.

5. **Capital-structure constraints.** The Sixth Street facility's covenants restrict licensing of core cardiometabolic assets, limiting the most successful strategy. The convert (strike ~$87) and $500M ATM define the dilution ceiling.

6. **Governance/consolidation complexity.** Visirna VIE consolidation (ownership fell from 66.25% to 56.38% in January 2026) and the Bisirna equity-method stake (~25.3%) add non-controlling-interest noise and related-party complexity.

We score Risk Factors 4.0, consistent with the prior report's reduction: the dismissed countersuit and the pending binary readout justify keeping this dimension below midpoint.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent, elevated uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 74.8% sits rich to one-year historical volatility of 63.8% (an 11-point spread), at the 66th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (74.8% near-term versus 72.3% three-month), which typically signals the market expects a discrete near-term event. ATM IV jumped 14.4 points (60.4% → 74.8%) over the past three weeks on a flat price, indicating rising hedging demand into a catalyst. Near-term skew shows modest elevated put demand (+3.1%), and the implied one-week move is ±8.4%. Short interest is 12.2% of float (7.5 days to cover), confirming meaningful institutional bearish positioning. The signal is consistent with our fundamental read: the market is bracing for a binary outcome, and the rich, backwardated vol is a reason for patience rather than chasing the position ahead of the readout.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** REDEMPLO's launch metrics signal real new-patient demand. A clean sHTG Phase 3 readout around Q3 CY2026 unlocks the opportunity and re-rates the franchise toward the upper analyst range ($110). The partnership ledger provides ongoing non-dilutive funding and platform validation, with obesity and the dual-target ARO-DIMER-PA providing optionality not in current product revenue. A favorable Ionis resolution removes the central overhang. Bull case ~$110 (+50% from ~$73).

**Bear Case.** An adverse Ionis ruling threatens REDEMPLO's commercialization economics just as the launch scales. An sHTG miss collapses the franchise thesis and removes the primary upside catalyst. Milestone timing disappoints, burn outpaces receipts, and the company taps the ATM or convert, diluting holders. Premium pricing draws payer resistance, capping sHTG uptake even on approval. Bear case toward the $46 analyst low (-37% from ~$73).

The two scenarios are roughly symmetric around the current price, which is why we hold rather than buy or avoid.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: a positive 12-month thesis exists, but it is gated by binary events that make the current entry balanced rather than asymmetric.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A positive sHTG Phase 3 (SHASTA/MUIR) readout around Q3 CY2026 confirming efficacy/safety supporting label expansion into the large market.
- A favorable resolution or material narrowing of the Ionis patent litigation (settlement on manageable terms, or a procedural win invalidating/limiting the '333 patent).
- REDEMPLO launch trajectory sustaining >30% sequential prescription growth with naïve-patient share holding ≥80% over the next two quarters.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse Ionis ruling that injuncts or materially impairs REDEMPLO commercialization.
- An sHTG Phase 3 failure or a safety signal.
- Burn outpacing milestones such that liquidity approaches the $250M covenant floor without a clear milestone or financing path, or a large dilutive raise below the convert strike.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next fiscal quarter results and, decisively, the sHTG Phase 3 readout expected around Q3 CY2026. The EU EC decision (expected Q2 CY2026) and Ionis docket developments are intermediate checkpoints.

## Conclusion

Arrowhead has validated an extra-hepatic RNAi platform with a deep large-cap partner roster, secured its first FDA approval, fortified the balance sheet, and delivered an encouraging early launch. The growth and competitive dimensions are genuinely strong, and estimate revisions are moving the right way. What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the concentration of value-determining events still unresolved: the Ionis litigation directly threatens the only approved product, the sHTG Phase 3 readout is a binary that the franchise thesis depends on, and the capital structure is costly with covenants that restrict the company's best strategy. Options markets are pricing imminent stress in backwardation, and short interest at 12.2% confirms two-sided positioning. At ~$73, below the ~$88 mean target but with a wide dispersion reflecting the binary set ahead, the risk/reward is balanced. We would become buyers on a clean sHTG readout or a favorable Ionis resolution; for now, the call is HOLD with a constructive bias.

The score trajectory (5.7 → 5.9 → 5.6, with Risk Factors stepped down from 4.5 to 4.0) reflects a stable but slightly de-rating view. The strong launch metrics and positive estimate revisions argue against further deterioration, while the dismissed Ionis countersuit and pending readout argue against an upgrade. Our 5.8 overall is consistent with holding the existing stance while flagging the specific events that would move it.
