**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Quest Diagnostics is the second-largest independent clinical laboratory in the United States (behind LabCorp), a scaled diagnostic-information franchise that processed roughly 244 million test requisitions in 2025, serves about one-third of U.S. adults annually, and holds payer access to more than 90% of insured lives. The investment case rests on a low-cost, high-volume operating model plus a disciplined acquisition roll-up, with secular tailwinds (industry consolidation, hospital lab internalization pressure, value-based care, payer cost-shopping) funneling volume toward scaled providers. The franchise is durable, the cash flow is real ($1.36B FY2025 FCF, 12.3% FCF margin), and the company has beaten consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters.

The current setup is balanced rather than asymmetric. At $204, DGX trades at roughly 19x the FY2026 EPS consensus of $10.77 and ~17.5x the FY2027 figure of $11.67, near its 52-week high of $213.50 and up roughly 15% over the trailing year. Estimate revisions are mildly positive (+1.3% current-year, +1.6% next-year over 90 days) but nowhere near the >30% threshold that would signal a systematic re-rating. The Q1 2026 headline of 10.9% volume growth is flattered: roughly seven of those points came from two new low-acuity contracts (Corewell Collaborative Lab Solutions and Fresenius dialysis testing) that depressed revenue per requisition by 1.3%. Strip them out and underlying organic volume was +3.8% with revenue per requisition up ~2.5% on favorable mix — a healthy but not accelerating run-rate.

The trajectory shows the rating moving from HOLD to BUY across prior reports, with valuation rescored upward (5.5 to 6.5) and growth upgraded (6.5 to 7.0). New data does not fully support carrying that BUY forward. The stock has continued to climb, leaving valuation richer rather than cheaper, while the genuine organic growth picture is mid-single-digit, not double-digit. Layered against a structural PAMA reimbursement overhang, a newly disclosed Corporate Integrity Agreement with HHS-OIG, and a Project Nova IT overhaul running to 2031–2032, the appropriate posture is a growth-oriented HOLD: own it for the compounding, but recognize the current price already reflects most of the visible upside.

## Company Overview

Quest operates two reported segments. Diagnostic Information Services (DIS) is more than 95% of revenue and spans routine testing, esoteric testing, and a growing Advanced Diagnostics portfolio (oncology, cardiometabolic/endocrine, brain health/Alzheimer's, autoimmune, and women's health/genetics). Five Advanced Diagnostics clinical areas each generated over $1B in 2025 on double-digit growth. Diagnostic Solutions (~2% of revenue) includes ExamOne, the leading North American life-insurance risk-assessment business, and the Quanum healthcare-IT platform.

The competitive moat is built on scale and cost position: the broadest test menu in the industry, ~900 M.D./Ph.D. medical staff, one of the world's largest de-identified lab databases (>80 billion data points), logistics covering ~83,000 daily stops plus 18 aircraft, ~2,400 patient service centers, ~8,000 patient access points, and roughly 612,000 connected clinician and hospital accounts. The growth model combines organic test-menu expansion, Advanced Diagnostics, and a roll-up engine targeting 1–2% of revenue per year from acquisitions. Recent transactions include LifeLabs (Canada, 2024, the company's most significant international expansion at ~23 million requisitions across ~15 labs), the Spectra renal-testing assets from Fresenius (2025), and a Corewell Health joint venture in Michigan (51% Quest, consolidated, closed January 2026).

CEO James Davis has chaired the company since November 2022, with CFO Sam Samad (ex-Illumina) joining the same year. The leadership bench draws heavily on GE Healthcare alumni.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue moved from $9.88B (2022) to $9.25B (2023, the COVID-testing roll-off), back to $9.87B (2024), and to $11.04B in 2025 (+11.8% YoY). The 2024–2025 reacceleration is heavily M&A-aided (LifeLabs, Spectra), though Q1 2026 shows genuine organic momentum.

Q1 2026 results: net revenue of $2,895M (+9.2% YoY), with organic revenue growth of 9.0%; operating income of $399M at a 13.8% operating margin (up from 13.0% a year earlier); diluted EPS of $2.24 versus $1.94 (+15.5%). The cleaner underlying picture is mid-single-digit organic volume with positive pricing/mix — solid for a mature lab, but investors extrapolating double-digit volume will be disappointed since these are step-function contract adds rather than a new run-rate.

Cash generation is strong. FY2025 operating cash flow was $1,886M against $527M capex, producing $1,359M FCF (12.3% FCF margin). Net margin was 9.5%, ROE 14.6%, ROA 6.4%. DSO held steady at ~48 days, indicating clean working-capital management. The four straight beats (5.5%, 1.5%, 2.8%, 0.9%) support confidence in the forward consensus, though the modest magnitude argues against expecting large upside surprises.

On the balance sheet, total debt is ~$5.7B (long-term $5.17B), D/E ~2.26x, equity $7.17B. Goodwill and intangibles total ~$10.6B of $16.2B in assets, with goodwill rising from $7.7B (2023) to $8.9B (2025) on the acquisition cadence. The current ratio of ~1.04 is thin but normal for a high-turnover services business; the company has $1.3B undrawn credit capacity, investment-grade ratings, and covenant compliance. Interest coverage is comfortable at roughly 6x (FY2025 operating income $1.56B vs. $264M interest expense). The Altman Z-Score of 1.57 ("distress zone") is mechanically driven by the goodwill-heavy, asset-light structure depressing asset-productivity ratios; coverage, FCF, and the dividend-growth record contradict any genuine solvency concern. The $500M of 3.45% notes due June 1, 2026 were addressed via a $500M senior unsecured note issuance in May.

Capital allocation is disciplined and tilted toward the dividend and M&A over buybacks. The dividend was raised ~7.5% to $0.86/quarter in February 2026 (the 15th increase since 2012), and the board added $1B to the buyback authorization ($1.4B available). No shares were repurchased in Q1 2026 or Q1 2025, signaling cash is being prioritized for deal firepower and the dividend rather than repurchases at ~$204.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth algorithm is mid-to-high-single-digit revenue: organic test-menu growth plus Advanced Diagnostics (double-digit, five $1B+ clinical areas) plus 1–2% annual M&A contribution. FY2026 guidance was raised after Q1 to a revenue midpoint of ~$11.84B (~0.7% above prior consensus), with EPS guidance also lifted. Forward consensus implies EPS of $10.77 (FY2026) growing to $11.67 (FY2027), roughly 8% growth.

Estimate-revision momentum is modestly positive, with a slight current-quarter trim (-1.5% over 30 days). This is constructive but well short of the threshold (>30% revisions paired with >40% revenue growth) that would justify treating the stock as systematically underpriced. The growth here is steady compounding.

The genuine forward drivers worth watching: (1) Advanced Diagnostics scaling, particularly the Haystack Oncology MRD test, which received FDA Breakthrough Device Designation for early-stage colorectal cancer with trials underway at Mass General Brigham and Rutgers (contingent consideration up to $150M tied to revenue/CMS-coverage milestones through 2028); (2) value-based care, with CMS targeting all traditional Medicare beneficiaries in ACOs by 2030, which favors Quest's population-health data assets; (3) the Google Cloud collaboration deploying analytics and generative AI to streamline data management and personalize the consumer channel (an infrastructure/customer relationship, not Google building Quest's diagnostics); and (4) the M&A pipeline, where the absence of buybacks alongside a fresh authorization signals management sees better returns in deals.

The offsetting consideration is that the Q1 volume optics overstate the durable rate. Once the Corewell and Fresenius step-ups annualize, reported volume growth normalizes toward the mid-single-digit organic base.

## Valuation Assessment

At $204, DGX trades at ~19x FY2026 consensus EPS of $10.77 and ~17.5x FY2027 EPS of $11.67. Against the trailing $8.75 EPS, the trailing P/E is ~22.5x. The PEG on ~8% forward growth is roughly 2.3x.

Peer context: the Health Care Services median P/E is ~24.1x. LabCorp, the closest direct comparable, trades at ~23.5x; DaVita at ~18.5x; Chemed at ~24.1x. On a headline basis DGX looks reasonable versus the median, but the median is inflated by Brightspring (74.7x) and a negative-earnings name (Hims). On a like-for-like basis against LabCorp, DGX is roughly in line on trailing multiples while delivering similar growth, so there is no obvious relative mispricing to exploit.

This is a quality compounder fairly priced for its growth. The cycle-adjusted argument does not apply: there is no trough in earnings to normalize against, and revision velocity is well below the threshold that would justify treating consensus targets as stale floors. The stock sits near its 52-week high, and the absence of company buybacks at this level indicates management does not view ~$204 as cheap. Analyst targets bracket the price meaningfully (mean $223 / median $225, low $184, high $245), implying ~9–10% upside to the mean plus the 1.7% yield, against ~10% downside to the low. That is roughly symmetric, consistent with a HOLD rather than a BUY. The valuation score reflects a fairly priced quality name with no identified catalyst for re-rating and no compelling discount.

## Competitive Landscape

Quest is one of the two largest U.S. clinical labs alongside LabCorp (the larger of the two), with the two forming a scale duopoly in independent national testing. The primary structural competitive threat is hospital lab internalization — hospitals pulling outreach testing in-house — alongside physician-office labs, anatomic-pathology practices, and emerging point-of-care, home-testing, and digital-pathology players. Management's strategic thesis is that consolidation, payer value-shopping (e.g., the UnitedHealthcare Preferred Lab Network, which selected Quest), and price transparency all funnel volume toward the lowest-cost scaled providers.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving rather than clearly widening. Quest is gaining contracted volume (Corewell, Fresenius, UHC PLN) and investing in automation and AI (digital cytology, microbiology, pathology) plus a consumer channel (QuestHealth.com with 150+ tests; MyQuest portal with 45M+ users). The March 2025 vacating of the FDA's laboratory-developed-test (LDT) rule (not appealed) removed a near-term compliance burden, a competitive positive. Against this, payer consolidation increases bargaining leverage against labs, narrowing networks and expanding prior authorization, while the patient-pay mix (~14% of revenue) carries collection risk. The net read is a defensible, scaled franchise holding share in a fragmented market, with secular tailwinds favoring scale but no dramatic share-capture story that the market is missing.

## Risk Assessment

**Reimbursement / PAMA overhang** is the single most important structural risk. PAMA's flawed 2017 data-collection methodology over-weighted independent labs and produced excessive Medicare CLFS cuts (2018–2020). Congress has repeatedly delayed the next reporting round and cuts (through 2026), but the underlying flaw is unresolved; a reset of delayed cuts is a real tail risk. The proposed "Results Act" (introduced 2025) would create a market-based fee schedule, a potential positive catalyst if passed, but with uncertain legislative timing.

**Corporate Integrity Agreement (CIA)** is the standout new legal item. Per the 10-K and 10-Q, Quest settled a DOJ matter (District of New Jersey) concerning its relationship with the New York Giants and agreed to a CIA with HHS-OIG, imposing multi-year compliance monitoring and reporting. The financial settlement appears immaterial (total legal reserves ~$20M), but the CIA is a reputational and oversight overhang. Separately, active NY and TX Attorney General investigations into Medicaid test-panel ordering/billing are ongoing, the kind of qui tam/billing exposures endemic to the lab industry.

**Project Nova execution risk** is a decade-long, Epic-led Order-to-Cash overhaul not expected to complete until 2031–2032, flagged explicitly in the 10-K as an IT/operational-failure risk given Quest's already-complex multi-payer billing. **Acquisition integration and goodwill-impairment risk** rises with the roll-up cadence ($8.9B goodwill). **Payer concentration** is moderate (five largest plans ~20% of revenue, none ≥10%). **Hospital internalization and home/point-of-care testing** could erode volume over time. The 2019 AMCA third-party data-breach litigation remains a long tail with active class actions six years on. LifeLabs adds FX and international exposure (Q1 carried a $16M negative currency translation).

A 3/12/2026 8-K (Item 5.02) signals an officer/director change whose specific identity was not confirmable from the provided sources; the 10-K executive roster appears intact, so this may be board-level or a planned transition rather than a disruptive C-suite exit. Worth tracking but not currently weighted as material.

On balance, most of these risks are known and at least partially priced. The CIA and the unresolved PAMA structure are the two items capable of moving the stock that the market may be under-weighting, which holds the risk score at a middling level rather than higher.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Quest compounds revenue at high-single-digits via organic menu growth, Advanced Diagnostics, and tuck-in M&A, with operating margins expanding (13.0% to 13.8% YoY in Q1) on Invigorate cost programs and eventual Project Nova revenue-cycle gains. Advanced Diagnostics (five $1B+ areas, plus the Haystack MRD optionality) re-rates the multiple toward higher-growth diagnostics peers. The PAMA flaw gets fixed favorably via the Results Act. Continued beat-and-raise execution plus the 15-year dividend-growth streak and eventual buyback resumption drive the stock toward the analyst high of $245. Upside ~20%.

**Bear Case.** Reported volume growth normalizes hard as the Corewell/Fresenius step-ups annualize, exposing a mid-single-digit organic base; the market de-rates the stock that had priced in the 10%+ optics. Delayed PAMA cuts reset, compressing Medicare CLFS reimbursement. The CIA produces additional compliance cost or reputational drag, and Project Nova runs over budget/timeline with billing disruption. Payer consolidation squeezes pricing, and goodwill impairment surfaces on a soured acquisition. The stock falls toward the analyst low of $184 or below, roughly -10% to -15%.

The base case sits in between: steady high-single-digit revenue growth, ~8% EPS growth, modest multiple stability, and a total return roughly matching the ~9–10% upside-to-mean plus yield, offset by the symmetric downside that keeps this a hold rather than a high-conviction long.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month growth-oriented HOLD. The franchise quality and consistent execution justify ownership, but the current price near the 52-week high already discounts most visible upside, leaving risk/reward balanced.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the high-$170s to low-$180s (near the analyst low of $184), which would reset forward P/E toward ~16x and create genuine asymmetry on unchanged fundamentals.
- Estimate revisions accelerating meaningfully (current-year EPS revised up >5% over a quarter) on durable organic volume re-acceleration above ~5%, or a favorable PAMA resolution via the Results Act passing.
- Resumption of share repurchases at scale, signaling management views the price as attractive and removing the current capital-allocation ambiguity.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Confirmed reset of delayed PAMA cuts materially impacting Medicare CLFS reimbursement.
- Organic volume growth (ex-acquisition) decelerating below ~2% for two consecutive quarters, or operating margin contracting below ~13%.
- A material goodwill impairment, a Project Nova budget/timeline blowout with billing disruption, or an expansion of the CIA/AG investigations into a quantified material liability.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (the next print after Q1 2026's 4/21 report) is the natural checkpoint, with particular attention to the organic-volume-ex-acquisition trend and any updated PAMA legislative developments.

## Conclusion

Quest Diagnostics is a high-quality, scaled diagnostic franchise generating real free cash flow, raising its dividend for a 15th consecutive year, beating consensus consistently, and executing a disciplined M&A roll-up with genuine Advanced Diagnostics optionality. The fundamentals are sound and the trajectory of recent scores reflects legitimate operational momentum. What holds the recommendation at HOLD rather than BUY is valuation discipline: at ~19x forward EPS near a 52-week high, with the headline Q1 volume number flattered by two low-acuity contracts and underlying organic growth in the mid-single digits, the stock is fairly priced for its ~8% earnings growth, not mispriced. Analyst targets bracket the current price roughly symmetrically, the options market is neutral, management itself declined to repurchase shares at these levels, and structural overhangs (PAMA, the new CIA, decade-long Project Nova) cap the upside case. The right action is to own DGX for steady compounding and the growing dividend, while waiting for a pullback toward the low-$180s or a clearer organic re-acceleration to add. Recommendation: **HOLD** (growth hold).
