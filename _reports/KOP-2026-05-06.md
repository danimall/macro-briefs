## Executive Summary

Koppers Holdings is a vertically integrated specialty chemicals and treated wood products company with dominant positions in North American railroad crossties (#1), creosote (#1 globally to railroads), and utility poles (#2 in U.S.). The company is executing a multi-year restructuring ("Catalyst") that delivered $46M in benefits in FY2025 and targets another $20-40M in 2026. This transformation has improved operating margins from 7.1% to 8.9% despite a 10.2% revenue decline.

The critical tension in the investment case is whether restructuring can overcome the competitive loss in Performance Chemicals — the highest-margin segment (18.9%) — where volumes fell 17% due to U.S. market share loss. Combined with $928M in debt (3.3x net leverage), a CFO transition, and broadly negative estimate revisions across all forecast periods, the risk/reward at $41.35 — near the 52-week high of $42.17 — is no longer compelling for a new position. The stock has appreciated 62% in the past year, pricing in meaningful restructuring success already.

The consensus analyst target of $50 (range $44-$55) implies 7-33% upside, but estimate trends are moving in the wrong direction (current-quarter EPS revised down 69% over 90 days, current-year down 12%). Until Performance Chemicals stabilizes and Catalyst savings flow through demonstrably, this is a monitoring situation rather than an entry point.

## Company Overview

Koppers operates three segments:

- **Railroad and Utility Products and Services (RUPS):** $927M revenue (49% of total). Supplies railroad crossties to all Class I North American railroads under long-term contracts and produces utility poles. Operates 18 treating plants strategically located on rail lines.

- **Performance Chemicals (PC):** $544M revenue (29%). Develops and markets copper-based wood preservation chemicals (MicroPro®), fire retardants, and industrial solutions. Serves residential lumber treating and agricultural markets. Highest-margin segment.

- **Carbon Materials and Chemicals (CMC):** $409M revenue (22%). Manufactures creosote, carbon pitch, and naphthalene from coal tar distillation. Supplies creosote internally to RUPS — a key vertical integration advantage.

The vertical integration model provides supply security and cost advantages: CMC produces the creosote that RUPS uses for treating crossties, while PC supplies preservation chemicals internally. This closed-loop structure creates meaningful barriers to entry.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory is declining:**

| Year | Revenue | YoY Change |
|------|---------|-----------|
| 2023 | $2,154M | +8.8% |
| 2024 | $2,092M | -2.9% |
| 2025 | $1,879M | -10.2% |
| 2026E | $1,900-2,000M | +1-6% |

The decline from peak is 12.8%, driven by PC market share loss (-16.5%), CMC rationalization (phthalic anhydride exit), and lower Class I crosstie volumes. Guidance for FY2026 suggests stabilization at best, not recovery.

**Profitability improving through cost actions:**

- Adjusted EBITDA: $256.7M (13.7% margin) — second-highest on record
- Operating income: $168M vs. $148M prior year (+13.2%)
- Operating margin: 8.9% vs. 7.1%
- RUPS segment EBITDA margin expanded from 8.7% to 11.7%
- CMC margin improved from 7.4% to 11.2%

The margin improvement despite top-line weakness demonstrates the Catalyst program's effectiveness in the segments where it has been applied.

**Balance sheet is the constraint:**

- Total debt: $928M on $574M equity (D/E 2.29x)
- Net leverage: 3.3x EBITDA (covenant max: 4.75x — 1.45x cushion)
- Interest expense: $66M — exceeding net income of $56M
- Altman Z-Score: 2.26 ("grey zone")
- Available liquidity: $383M
- Current ratio: 2.94

The leverage is manageable given the current EBITDA run-rate, but it leaves virtually no margin for operational setbacks. A scenario where PC volume losses accelerate while CMC faces further coal tar supply constraints could compress EBITDA toward levels where the 4.75x covenant becomes binding.

**Cash flow generation is adequate but not exceptional:**

- Operating cash flow: $122.5M
- Capex: ~$55M
- Free cash flow: $67.5M (FCF yield: ~8.6% at current market cap)
- Net debt repayment: $12.7M in 2025

The 8.6% FCF yield is attractive in absolute terms, but most of this cash is needed for debt service, limiting capital return potential.

## Growth Analysis

**Positive vectors:**

1. Utility pole demand supported by datacenter/AI-driven grid buildout, EV infrastructure, and grid hardening. Management cites datacenter construction as "main driver for growth" in this business line.

2. Catalyst program's $20-40M incremental benefit target for 2026, on top of $46M already realized.

3. Portfolio simplification (exiting low-return businesses) should improve capital efficiency metrics.

**Negative vectors:**

1. Performance Chemicals lost 17% volume from U.S. market share shift — a competitive displacement that may not reverse organically.

2. Coal tar supply is in secular decline as global steel shifts from blast furnaces to electric arc furnaces. This constrains CMC's raw material availability long-term.

3. Carbon pitch demand is migrating to Middle East/Asia — away from Koppers' geographic strength in Western markets.

4. Florence, SC plant closure was driven by "lower future forecasted demand from facility's largest customer" — indicating a concrete railroad customer loss.

**Estimate revisions tell the story:** Current-quarter EPS estimate has fallen from $1.21 to $0.38 over 90 days (-68.9%). Current-year estimate dropped from $4.75 to $4.17 (-12.2%). These reflect analysts incorporating the weak Q1 guidance and lingering PC headwinds. The revisions are universally negative across all periods.

The valuation appears undemanding on an EV/EBITDA basis relative to the company's market position and restructuring potential. Peer Stella-Jones trades at higher multiples, reflecting Koppers' leverage discount. However, the forward P/E of 15.1x uses what appears to be a below-trend earnings estimate ($2.74), and if this reflects a genuinely weak FY2026, the valuation is less cheap than the adjusted metrics suggest.

The market is applying a leverage discount and a "show me" discount on the restructuring. Both are justified given: (a) interest expense exceeds net income, meaning incremental EBITDA dollars flow disproportionately to equity through deleveraging, and (b) the PC share loss introduced genuine uncertainty about sustainable earnings power.

At $41.35, with the stock having rallied 62% over the past year and sitting 2% below its 52-week high, the asymmetry that existed at $25-28 (where it traded Sept-Dec 2025) has largely been captured.

## Competitive Landscape

Koppers holds leading positions across its key markets:
- #1 supplier of railroad crossties to Class I railroads in North America
- #2 producer of utility poles in the U.S.
- #1 global supplier of creosote to the North American railroad industry
- Global leader in wood preservation chemicals

**Improving trajectory:** The RUPS segment's margin expansion (8.7% → 11.7%) reflects successful cost rationalization. The utility pole business benefits from structural demand growth tied to grid infrastructure buildout.

**Deteriorating trajectory:** The PC segment's 17% volume loss from competitive displacement is the most concerning signal. The filing's language — "a shift in United States market share" — indicates this is competitor-driven, not cyclical. Recovery requires winning back customers or acquiring new ones, neither of which is guaranteed.

**Structural moat components:**
- Vertical integration (CMC feeds RUPS) creates cost advantages competitors cannot replicate without similar asset bases
- Strategic plant locations on Class I rail lines reduce logistics costs
- ~75% of railroad products under long-term contracts
- Regulatory barriers around chemical manufacturing provide modest protection

The moat is real but narrow — concentrated primarily in the logistics/integration advantages rather than proprietary technology. The PC share loss demonstrates that in the chemical preservation market specifically, switching costs are lower than in the infrastructure products business.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks:**

1. **Performance Chemicals competitive displacement (HIGH).** The 17% volume loss may accelerate if competitors continue to gain share. This segment generates the highest margins and its decline disproportionately impacts consolidated profitability.

2. **Leverage amplification (HIGH).** At 3.3x net leverage, operational setbacks translate into disproportionate equity value destruction. Interest expense of $66M on $56M net income means the company is borrowing to fund shareholder returns at the margin.

3. **Coal tar secular decline (MEDIUM-TERM).** Raw material availability is structurally constrained. Loss of European supply from Russia/Ukraine sanctions accelerated a pre-existing trend.

4. **Customer concentration in RUPS.** Florence, SC closure driven by a single customer's reduced demand demonstrates concentrated exposure. Class I railroad capital spending is cyclical.

5. **Tariff exposure.** Estimated $4-8M pre-tax drag from copper tariffs (50% on semi-finished copper effective Aug 2025) and potential creosote import tariffs. Copper hedging effectiveness degraded by COMEX/LME divergence.

6. **CFO transition.** Interim CFO appointment suggests ongoing leadership search, creating uncertainty during a critical restructuring phase.

7. **Environmental liabilities.** Only $10.2M reserved against contamination history from decades of chemical manufacturing. Actual exposure may be materially higher.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($50-55, +21-33%):**
- Catalyst program delivers $30-40M in FY2026 incremental benefits, lifting adjusted EBITDA to $280-290M
- Performance Chemicals stabilizes as new customer acquisitions offset losses
- Utility pole demand accelerates on AI-driven grid buildout
- Deleveraging to <3.0x unlocks re-rating toward 7.5-8.0x EV/EBITDA
- FCF generation applied to debt reduction creates equity value accretion

**Bear Case ($28-32, -23-32%):**
- PC share losses accelerate; segment EBITDA declines further
- Coal tar supply disruptions force CMC production cuts
- Tariff costs exceed $4-8M estimate
- Q1 FY2026 weakness is worse than guided; full-year estimates get cut further
- Stock re-rates to 5.0-5.5x on lower EBITDA base, with leverage amplifying equity decline
- Recession reduces railroad capital spending and residential remodeling activity

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Performance Chemicals reports volume stabilization or growth in any quarter
2. Stock pulls back to $32-34 (EV/EBITDA ~5.5-6.0x), providing margin of safety for the restructuring thesis
3. Net leverage falls below 2.5x, materially de-risking the equity

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Performance Chemicals volumes decline another 10%+ in FY2026
2. Net leverage rises above 3.75x (approaching covenant territory)
3. Catalyst savings prove insufficient to offset revenue decline (adjusted EBITDA falls below $230M)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely May 2026 for Q1 results, given last reported 2/26/2026). The Q1 weakness is pre-guided, so the focus should be on PC volume trends and Catalyst benefit realization rate.

## Conclusion

Koppers presents a restructuring story with genuine operational merit — the Catalyst program is producing measurable results, the utility pole business has a structural growth tailwind, and the valuation at 6.5x EV/EBITDA provides some cushion. However, three factors prevent a BUY at current levels:

First, the stock has already rallied 62% from its September-December 2025 base, pricing in substantial restructuring success. The margin of safety that existed at $25-28 no longer exists at $41.

Second, estimate revisions are uniformly negative — current-quarter estimates cut 69% over 90 days, current-year down 12%. The market is marking down earnings expectations at the same time the stock is rallying, creating an unstable dynamic.

Third, the PC market share loss is a genuine competitive threat to the company's highest-margin segment, and there is no evidence yet that it has been arrested. Until that stabilizes, forward earnings visibility remains poor.

The stock deserves monitoring for an entry point on a pullback to the $32-34 range, or on evidence that PC volumes have stabilized. At current levels, the risk/reward is approximately balanced.
