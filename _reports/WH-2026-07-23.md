**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Wyndham Hotels & Resorts is the world's largest hotel franchisor by property count, with over 8,300 hotels and ~869,000 rooms across 25 brands, concentrated in the economy, midscale, and upper-midscale tiers. The business is a pure-play asset-light franchisor: it licenses brands and collects royalties (~5% of gross room revenue) plus marketing and reservation fees, generating ~50% adjusted EBITDA margins on fee revenue with minimal capital intensity (~$45M annual capex on ~$1.4B revenue). The central analytical task with this name is separating headline distortion from operating reality. FY2025 GAAP net income fell 33% to $193M and diluted EPS dropped to $2.50, but this was almost entirely driven by $160M of Revo Hospitality insolvency charges plus $18M restructuring, not operational decay. Adjusted EBITDA grew every year to a record $718M in 2025, and H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA rose 8% YoY.

The 2026 story is a demand recovery. After US RevPAR fell 4% in 2025, it turned positive at +2% in Q2 2026, led by Midwest, Texas, and Florida select-service strength. Q2 net income rose 17% to $102M, adjusted EBITDA rose 9%, and management raised full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance to $735–745M with an improved global RevPAR outlook of flat-to-+1%. The Revo charge has been de-risked to a ~$1M residual receivable after Wyndham foreclosed on two European hotels. The maturity wall was cleaned up in February 2026 with a $650M senior note issuance.

Against this, the stock has fallen ~14% since June and sits near its 52-week low of $69.21. At $75.68 the shares trade at roughly 15.8x forward EPS ($4.80 consensus), a discount to the peer median. The bull case is a cheap, high-quality cash machine with a demand recovery underway. The bear case is real: forward consensus estimates have gone flat-to-negative (next-quarter and current-year revisions are down), no insider has bought on the open market despite the pullback (management's own 2025 buybacks at $85.73 are underwater), franchisee credit exposure is expanding (doubtful-accounts allowance up from $61M to $99M in 18 months), and the core economy guest is the segment most exposed to a consumer cliff. This is a balanced setup that does not clear the bar for a BUY. We rate WH a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Wyndham operates a franchising-only model, licensing 25 brands — Super 8, Days Inn, Travelodge, La Quinta, Ramada, Baymont, Howard Johnson, and the eponymous Wyndham/Wyndham Grand — to roughly 6,200 third-party owners in ~100 countries. It does not own hotels as a matter of strategy; the two European hotels currently on the balance sheet were acquired involuntarily through the Revo foreclosure. The portfolio skews to the value tier in secondary and tertiary drive-to markets: ~80% of the US population lives within 10 miles of a Wyndham-affiliated property, and the brands represent ~19% of branded US rooms.

Structural advantages include extreme franchisee diversification (no single franchisee exceeds 2% of hotels), the Wyndham Rewards loyalty program (122M+ members, 7% growth in 2025), long-dated franchise contracts (10–20 years) that provide revenue visibility, and very low capital intensity that drives high free-cash-flow conversion. Growth vectors are extended stay (ECHO Suites, WaterWalk) and higher-FeePAR international upscale expansion. Wyndham was spun off from Wyndham Worldwide (now Travel + Leisure Co.) in May 2018 and continues to collect licensing royalties from T+L's timeshare sales.

The company was the target of an unsolicited takeover bid from Choice Hotels in 2024, which the board rejected. That episode cost ~$47M in defense expenses (a key drag on 2024 net income) and left standing activism/takeover risk that management explicitly retains as a risk factor. With the stock near 52-week lows and below the value Choice implicitly ascribed, renewed strategic interest is a genuine two-sided consideration.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Fee-related revenue has grown modestly and steadily: $1,354M (2022) → $1,384M (2023) → $1,404M (2024) → $1,429M (2025), up 2% in 2025. H1 2026 net revenue was $702M, down 2% YoY, but that decline is optical: it reflects the absence of pass-through revenue from the May 2025 global franchisee conference and Revo fee deferrals. Underlying fee growth continued, with ancillary and credit-card revenue up $18M in H1 2026.

**The GAAP-vs-adjusted tension.** Reported net income fell from $355M (2022) to $289M (2023/2024) to $193M (2025), with diluted EPS declining from $3.91 to $2.50. The 2025 decline is explained by discrete items: the Revo insolvency charges ($86M impairment + $74M bad-debt provision), restructuring, and rising interest expense ($124M → $139M). Adjusted EBITDA, the cleaner read of the franchise engine, grew every single year: $650M → $659M → $694M → $718M in 2025, with Hotel Franchising segment EBITDA rising to $781M. Anyone anchoring on the $2.50 trailing EPS or the net-income downtrend would misjudge the business.

**RevPAR.** This is the demand-sensitive line. Global RevPAR fell 3% in 2025 (US −4%), reflecting genuine domestic softness in economy lodging. It turned positive in 2026, driven by Midwest/Texas/Florida strength. International remains weak (EMEA hurt by Middle East conflict, China deflationary pricing, Mexico by lower cross-border demand), down 6% in Q2 2026. Net room growth is solid: +4% system rooms ex-Revo, with a record 261,000-room pipeline carrying a ~30% FeePAR premium to the existing system.

**Cash flow.** High quality and consistent. Operating cash flow was $367M and FCF ~$321M in 2025 (a 22.5% FCF margin). H1 2026 OCF was $133M. On a ~$5.6B market cap, trailing FCF yield is roughly 5.7%.

**Balance sheet.** The headline distress signals are false. An Altman Z-Score of 0.87 and reported D/E of 7.94 are artifacts of an asset-light model that has deliberately levered down book equity to $468M via $1.9B of cumulative buybacks (28M treasury shares retired for $1.9B in Q4 2025). The economically relevant metrics: total debt $2.68B (Q2 2026) against ~$718M adjusted EBITDA, or ~3.7x gross leverage, with first-lien net leverage of just 2.0x (down from 2.8x), well inside the 5.0x covenant. Credit ratings are Ba1/BB+/BB+. The February 2026 refinancing ($650M 5.625% notes due 2033) cleaned up the maturity wall; the nearest maturity is now $500M of notes due August 2028, with Term Loan B ($1.5B) due 2030 and ~95% swapped to fixed at a weighted ~3.58%. The franchisor is comfortably financed, though leverage limits flexibility and interest expense has risen every year.

**Capital allocation.** Aggressive shareholder returns: $266M buybacks + $127M dividends in 2025, with the dividend raised to $0.43/quarter in 2026 (5+ consecutive years of increases) and $169M remaining on authorization. The 2025 buybacks were executed at an average $85.73, above today's price, so recent repurchases are underwater.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three pillars: net room growth, a positive US RevPAR turn, and margin durability from the fee-based model.

Net room growth remains the most reliable component. The company opened a record ~18,000 rooms in Q2 2026 (7% more than the prior year) and grew system rooms +4% ex-Revo. The ~30% FeePAR premium on the pipeline means new units should be more profitable per room than the existing base as they mature. This supports mid-single-digit fee-revenue compounding regardless of the RevPAR cycle.

The RevPAR turn is the most important near-term positive. The 2025 narrative was declining domestic demand; the 2026 narrative is a faster-than-expected US select-service recovery. The earnings track record supports moderate confidence in the guide: three beats and one small miss over the last four quarters, with Q1 and Q2 2026 beating by 8.0% and 2.1% respectively.

The caution is that consensus estimates have stalled rather than accelerated. This is the decisive data point against upgrading. Current-quarter estimates rose modestly (+2.5% over 90 days), but next-quarter estimates fell 7.3% over 90 days, current-year revisions are down 0.7%, and next-year down 0.4%. There is no estimate-revision velocity here; the signal that would justify treating consensus targets as floors is absent. Full-year EPS consensus sits at ~$4.80, with next-year at ~$5.39, implying high-single-digit forward EPS growth largely delivered through buybacks rather than organic acceleration. International RevPAR weakness partially offsets the domestic turn and caps global RevPAR near flat.

Growth prospects earn a middling score: the room pipeline and margin structure are durable, but the demand recovery is fragile, international is a drag, and estimates are not confirming an acceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $75.68, WH trades at ~15.8x forward EPS (~$4.80 consensus) and ~30x trailing EPS. The trailing figure is distorted by the Revo charges and should be ignored; the forward multiple is the relevant anchor.

Against peers in Hotels, Resorts & Cruise Lines, the discount is clear. Peer median P/E is 27.8x (HGV 27.8x, MTN 31.2x, TNL 20.6x, CHH 14.5x, H negative). WH's closest structural comparable is Choice Hotels (CHH), also a value-tier, asset-light franchisor, trading at 14.5x, roughly in line with WH. Against the broader lodging complex, WH trades at a meaningful discount to the median multiple. Some of that discount is justified: WH's five-year cumulative TSR of 138.6 badly lags its peer group's 226.6, reflecting the market's persistent discount for economy-tier demand exposure and international drag versus upscale-weighted competitors.

The bull argument for the valuation is that a ~16x forward multiple with mid-single-digit organic fee growth, a demand recovery, ~50% EBITDA margins, and ~5.7% FCF yield undervalues a durable cash machine. The 5+ year dividend-growth streak (2.3% yield, well covered by FCF) provides absolute-yield support. Analyst targets — mean $100, median $100, range $87–$115 — sit 15–52% above the current price, and if the RevPAR recovery holds, those targets are more likely to prove accurate than stale.

The discipline against scoring valuation higher: cheapness in this sector, on this consumer-cycle exposure, has historically marked value traps more often than opportunities, and the estimate revisions are not confirming that the market is wrong. A cheap multiple without an accelerating catalyst is a warning as much as an opportunity. The stock has repeatedly bounced between the high-$60s and low-$90s over the past year without a durable re-rating. We assign a valuation score of 6.0: modestly attractive on absolute and relative multiples, but without the estimate-revision catalyst that would make the mispricing high-conviction.

## Competitive Landscape

Wyndham's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding, a change from the 7.0 competitive-position score assigned in earlier reports. Its structural edge is unchanged: scale and diversification in the value/economy franchise tier, a top-rated loyalty program, long-dated contracts, and the largest branded footprint by property count.

Two developments justify tempering the trajectory. First, the 10-K added specific language on large language models potentially sourcing hotel information from OTAs rather than Wyndham's direct channels, a structural, emerging threat to the direct-booking and loyalty flywheel that is the model's differentiator. This is early and unquantified but real. Second, the value-tier positioning that provides defensiveness in most cycles becomes a liability when the drive-to, lower-income economy guest is squeezed by inflation and reduced discretionary spending. WH competes with Choice, Marriott, Hilton, IHG, and Best Western for franchisees, and with OTAs, short-term rentals, and increasingly AI travel search for guests. Its niche is defensively attractive but carries structurally lower RevPAR and pricing power than upscale peers, which is the durable reason for its multiple discount.

## Risk Assessment

**Franchisee credit risk (elevated, company-specific).** The Revo insolvency was the concrete manifestation of a structural feature: Wyndham extends development advance notes ($374M gross) and loans to franchisees to win business. As it chases higher-FeePAR international growth, counterparty credit exposure rises. The allowance for doubtful accounts climbed from $61M (Jan 2025) to $99M (June 2026), a real deterioration worth monitoring even though the Revo receivable itself is now down to ~$1M residual.

**Economy-lodging demand.** The core US guest is lower-income and drive-to, the most exposed to a consumer cliff. The 2026 recovery is real but fragile, and any renewed domestic softening would hit RevPAR and, indirectly, franchisee health.

**International weakness.** EMEA (Middle East conflict), China (deflation and trade disputes), and Mexico (cross-border demand collapse) drove the −6% Q2 international RevPAR decline.

**Leverage and rates.** Interest expense has risen every year, and a portion of debt reverts to floating after swap expiries in 2027–2028. Manageable but limiting.

**Litigation.** ~80 pending matters allege sex trafficking at franchised properties; accruals are small ($3M, disclosed max exposure ~$7M above accruals), but reputational and tail risk exists. A standing FTC data-security order remains from the 2008–2010 breaches.

**Takeover/activism.** After the 2024 Choice bid, renewed strategic or activist interest is a two-sided risk at depressed levels.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is roughly balanced and does not strongly confirm or contradict the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV of 36.4% is modestly rich versus 31.1% one-year historical vol, and the term structure is flat (near 36.4% ≈ far 35.3%), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated on a single catalyst. The one-month put/call skew is a balanced −2.6%. More telling is the change since June 17: ATM IV collapsed from 62.8% to 36.4% (post-earnings vol crush) while the put/call OI ratio rose from 0.68 to 1.47. The rising put OI, combined with 10.6% short interest (4.6 days to cover), signals meaningful institutional caution, consistent with the bear case around economy-tier demand and stalled estimates. This is not extreme enough to override the fundamentals but reinforces the balanced HOLD posture.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($100–115).** The GAAP earnings decline was a one-time Revo artifact; the franchise engine grew adjusted EBITDA to a record and continues to compound. US RevPAR has turned positive, management raised guidance, and the record pipeline drives mid-single-digit fee growth at a FeePAR premium. At ~16x forward EPS with ~50% EBITDA margins, ~5.7% FCF yield, and a growing dividend, the stock is cheap versus lodging peers and its own history. A sustained domestic recovery plus international stabilization re-rates the multiple toward the peer group, and renewed M&A interest at depressed levels caps downside. This aligns with the $100 mean analyst target.

**Bear case ($62–65).** The economy guest cracks as the consumer weakens, US RevPAR rolls back over, and international weakness persists. Franchisee credit deterioration (allowance up ~62% in 18 months) produces a second Revo-style event. Forward estimates keep drifting lower, LLM/OTA disintermediation erodes the direct-booking flywheel, and the stock breaks its 52-week low toward the low-$60s on a de-rating of economy-tier lodging. Underwater buybacks and absent insider buying prove management's capital timing and conviction were misjudged.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For HOLD:**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- US RevPAR sustains above +2% for two consecutive quarters AND consensus current-year/next-year EPS revisions turn positive (>+3% over 90 days), confirming the demand recovery is durable, not a bounce.
- Open-market insider buying by 2+ C-suite executives, signaling conviction at depressed levels (currently absent, and a negative given the stock trades below management's own $85.73 buyback average).
- Pullback toward the low-$60s (near the 52-week low of $69.21 or below) on unchanged fundamentals, which would push forward P/E toward ~12–13x and create asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- US RevPAR turns negative again OR the doubtful-accounts allowance rises materially above $99M, signaling a second franchisee-credit event.
- Management cuts the raised $735–745M adjusted EBITDA guidance.
- First-lien net leverage rises back above ~3.0x, indicating buybacks are outrunning cash generation.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 2026 earnings report (late October 2026), which will test whether the US RevPAR recovery holds through the back half and whether guidance is reaffirmed. The August 2028 note maturity is not near-term but worth tracking.

## Conclusion

Wyndham is a genuinely high-quality, asset-light franchisor whose reported financials mislead in both directions: the FY2025 GAAP decline overstated deterioration, while the trailing 30x P/E overstates expense. On the numbers that matter, adjusted EBITDA is at a record, the model throws off ~$320M of FCF at ~50% margins, leverage is comfortable at 2.0x first-lien net, and US demand turned positive in Q2 2026 with raised guidance. At ~16x forward EPS the stock is cheap versus lodging peers.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the absence of confirming momentum. Forward estimates have stalled, not accelerated; the revision velocity that would flag a true recovery is nowhere in the data. The economy-tier consumer exposure is precisely the segment most vulnerable to a consumer squeeze. Franchisee credit exposure is rising. Management is not buying at depressed levels, and its 2025 buybacks are underwater. Cheapness in a demand-sensitive consumer discretionary name, without an accelerating catalyst, is as likely to mark a value trap as an opportunity. The setup is balanced, with roughly symmetric upside to the analyst targets and downside to the low-$60s. We rate WH a HOLD (growth-hold rationale), constructive on the franchise engine but requiring either a durable RevPAR/estimate confirmation or a cheaper entry near the low-$60s before upgrading.
