## Executive Summary

Palomar Holdings is a specialty P&C insurer that has grown gross written premiums from effectively zero at founding (2014) to $2.0 billion in FY2025, diversifying from a pure earthquake book into casualty, crop, inland marine, and surety. The stock has declined 37% from its 52-week high of $175.85 to $110.75, creating a meaningful valuation disconnect: the company trades at approximately 11.6x 2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $9.58, generates 26%+ adjusted ROE, and has beaten consensus in all four reported quarters with accelerating estimate revisions (+10-13% over 90 days).

The decline appears driven by three factors: (1) broad P&C sector rotation as investors rotated into other financials, (2) the loss ratio normalization that accompanies the intentional mix shift away from earthquake toward casualty/crop, and (3) elevated near-term implied volatility. None of these factors represent fundamental deterioration. Operating cash flow reached $409M in FY2025, the balance sheet remains well-capitalized despite the Gray Surety acquisition, and management approved a new $200M buyback program in late April 2026.

The primary risks are execution on three rapid acquisitions, loss ratio trajectory as the business shifts toward higher-attritional-loss lines, and catastrophe tail risk (though net retention is capped at approximately $20M per earthquake event). At current levels, the risk/reward is asymmetric: consensus targets range from approximately $151-$183 (36-65% upside), and 2026 guidance implies growth well above what the current multiple suggests.

## Company Overview

Palomar Holdings operates as a specialty insurance holding company through its subsidiaries Palomar Specialty Insurance Company (PSIC, admitted) and Palomar Excess and Surplus Insurance Company (PESIC, surplus lines). The company underwrites risks across five categories: earthquake (28% of 2025 GWP), casualty (27%), inland marine & other property (22%), crop (12%), and fronting (11%, declining). As of Q1 2026, surety & credit contributed 5% following the Gray acquisition.

The platform is technology-enabled and asset-light: no legacy IT systems, automated personal lines underwriting, and virtually zero physical capital expenditures. Distribution occurs through retail agents, wholesale brokers, program administrators (notably Arrowhead General at 20.3% of GWP), and 35+ carrier partnerships. The company holds A.M. Best "A" (Excellent) ratings across its primary subsidiaries (PSIC, PSRE, PESIC, and FIA), while PCSC/Gray Surety carries an A- (Excellent) rating.

## Growth Analysis

Palomar's growth operates on multiple vectors simultaneously:

1. **Casualty expansion** (131% GWP growth in 2025): E&S casualty, primary casualty, excess liability, and environmental. This is the primary new growth engine and benefits from the ongoing standard-market-to-E&S migration.

2. **Crop insurance** (113% GWP growth in 2025): Acquired Advanced AgProtection in April 2025. Structural demand via USDA/RMA programs with built-in government reinsurance.

3. **Surety & credit** (Gray acquisition, January 2026): Treasury-listed surety carrier covering all 50 states with 13 regional offices. $31.9M GWP in just two months of Q1 2026.

4. **Geographic diversification**: California reduced from 53% (2023) to 31% (2025) of GWP with expansion across all 50 states.

5. **Net earned premium retention**: As the book shifts toward higher-retention lines (casualty, crop), the net earned premium ratio rose from 36.5% (2024) to 51.9% (Q1 2026) — each dollar of GWP translates to more net economics.

2026 full-year guidance of $260-275M adjusted net income (per the FY2025 earnings release filed with the SEC) implies 20-27% growth over FY2025 adjusted figures. Consensus estimates for FY2027 EPS of $11.09 (up 12.7% in 90 days) suggest sustained mid-teens earnings growth beyond the current year.

Relative to specialty P&C peers such as Kinsale (~17x), RLI (~14x), Skyward (~13x), and W.R. Berkley (~16x), PLMR's trailing P/E of 15.4x is roughly in line with peer medians. However, this comparison understates PLMR on growth-adjusted terms: no peer in the comparable set is growing at 40-60% revenue rates with 26%+ ROE and a 76% combined ratio. On forward estimates, PLMR at 11.6x is trading well below what its growth and profitability profile would warrant.

The five-year average P/E for PLMR is reportedly well above current levels. A stock growing earnings 20-27% annually trading at 11-12x forward earnings has a PEG ratio below 0.5 — among the most compelling growth-at-reasonable-price profiles available in financials.

Analyst consensus targets: low approximately $151 (36% upside), mean approximately $164-166 (48% upside), high approximately $183 (65% upside). Given accelerating estimates and the stock's decline, these targets likely lag — but even the low target represents substantial upside from current levels.

**Applying a 16x multiple (conservative for this growth rate) to consensus FY2026 EPS of $9.58 yields $153. Applying 16x to FY2027 EPS of $11.09 yields $177.** Both are well above the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

Palomar's competitive position is strengthening on multiple dimensions:

- **Dual-platform advantage**: Operating both admitted and E&S carriers allows rapid response to market opportunities. When standard carriers pull back from risks, PESIC captures the flow; when markets normalize, PSIC retains relationships.
- **Technology-first underwriting**: Granular geocode-level earthquake pricing vs. competitors' broader zone approaches delivers superior loss ratios. The asset-light model enables exceptional cash flow margins impossible for legacy carriers.
- **Diversification trajectory**: The shift from a single-product earthquake insurer to a five-line specialty platform reduces correlation and creates cross-selling opportunities.
- **Reinsurance sophistication**: $1.2B in catastrophe bonds (Torrey Pines Re), plus traditional reinsurance, with net cat retention limited to approximately $20M per earthquake event — this capital efficiency allows Palomar to write significantly more premium per dollar of equity than peers with higher retentions.

The primary competitive risk is that larger players (Kinsale, W.R. Berkley) operate with similar E&S capabilities at greater scale. However, Palomar's niche specialties (earthquake, residential flood) and technology platform differentiate sufficiently to sustain 20%+ growth rates.

## Risk Assessment

**Catastrophe Risk:** While net retention is limited (approximately $20M earthquake, $11M hurricane per management disclosures), the tail risk of a truly unprecedented event exists. More practically, a major earthquake could trigger widespread policy cancellations or claims disputes, creating revenue disruption even if insured losses are contained.

**Acquisition Integration:** Three acquisitions in 12 months (FIA, AAP, Gray Surety) with workforce doubling to 439 employees creates execution risk. The Gray acquisition added approximately $300M in debt to a previously debt-free balance sheet, and the purchase price allocation (approximately $311M aggregate price) has not yet been finalized. Integration missteps could compress margins or distract management.

**Loss Ratio Trajectory:** The non-cat loss ratio rose from 20.9% (FY2024) to 33.2% (Q1 2026). Management frames this as expected and offset by higher retained premiums, but if casualty loss experience deteriorates beyond expectations (as has occurred industry-wide in prior casualty cycles), margins could compress more than guided.

**Concentration:** Arrowhead General distributes 20.3% of GWP. Loss of this relationship would create meaningful revenue disruption. CEO Mac Armstrong's prior role at Arrowhead mitigates but does not eliminate this risk.

**Reinsurance Dependency:** 52-58% cession rates mean reinsurance availability and pricing directly determine Palomar's unit economics. A hard reinsurance cycle could compress margins significantly.

## Conclusion

Palomar Holdings presents a compelling growth-at-reasonable-price opportunity in specialty insurance. The 37% decline from 52-week highs has compressed the forward P/E to ~11.6x despite 20-27% guided earnings growth, 26%+ ROE, strong operating cash generation, and accelerating estimate revisions. The loss ratio normalization that concerns the market is intentional and healthy: it reflects a strategic shift toward higher-retention, diversified premium that is growing underwriting income faster than the loss ratio is expanding.

The key question is whether Palomar can execute on three simultaneous acquisitions while maintaining underwriting discipline. Management's track record of consistent beats (4/4 quarters), favorable reserve development trends, and industry-leading combined ratios provide confidence. The $200M buyback authorization at current depressed levels is a strong capital allocation signal.

At 11.6x forward earnings with this growth profile, the market is pricing in significant execution risk that, based on available evidence, is not materializing. BUY with a 12-month base case target of $153.
