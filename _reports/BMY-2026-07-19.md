**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Bristol Myers Squibb is a $124.5B-market-cap global biopharma throwing off roughly $12.8B of free cash flow (26.7% FCF margin) against a modest ~$1.3B capex base. The company is midway through a well-telegraphed transition: its Legacy Portfolio (Eliquis, Revlimid, Pomalyst, Sprycel, Abraxane — ~45% of 2025 revenue) is eroding at −15% YoY on patent expirations and IRA price-setting, while its Growth Portfolio (Opdivo, Reblozyl, Breyanzi, Camzyos, Cobenfy — ~55% of revenue) is compounding at +17%. Total revenue was essentially flat at $48.19B in 2025 versus $48.30B in 2024, and Q1 2026 revenue grew 3% to $11.49B, indicating the crossover where growth products begin to outweigh legacy erosion is close but not yet clearly through.

The near-term picture is dominated by three mechanical headwinds that are largely independent of product execution: Eliquis (30% of revenue) faces its IRA "maximum fair price" effective January 2026, a U.S. list-price cut, free-to-Medicaid provisions under the December 2025 government agreement, and EU composition-of-matter patent expiry in November 2026; roughly $2.7B of near-pure-margin royalty income rolls off (AstraZeneca diabetes ended December 2025, Keytruda and Tecentriq both terminate December 2026); and Orencia — a $3.7B Growth Portfolio product — was selected in January 2026 for IRA negotiation beginning 2028. These converge into a 2026-2028 earnings trough.

At ~$60.74, BMY trades at roughly 9.6x the $6.34 current-year consensus EPS with a ~4.1% dividend yield that is comfortably covered by FCF. The estimate-revision trend is modestly positive (current-year EPS +1.3% over 90 days) and the company has beaten in four consecutive quarters. This is a coherent income-and-transition hold: the dividend and cash generation are durable, the valuation is undemanding, but there is no near-term catalyst to re-rate the stock until the Eliquis erosion path and the royalty cliff clear. We rate BMY HOLD (income), with an overall score of 5.5.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1887, Bristol Myers Squibb operates as a single reporting segment focused on serious diseases across oncology, hematology, immunology, cardiovascular, and neuroscience. Management frames the portfolio in two buckets, which is the central lens for the investment thesis:

- **Growth Portfolio** (~$26.4B, +17% YoY in 2025): Opdivo ($10.0B, +8%), Orencia ($3.7B), Yervoy ($2.9B, +15%), Reblozyl ($2.3B, +31%), Opdualag, Breyanzi ($1.4B, +82%), Camzyos ($1.1B, +77%), plus newer launches Cobenfy, Krazati, Sotyktu, Augtyro, and subcutaneous Opdivo Qvantig.
- **Legacy Portfolio** (~$21.8B, −15% YoY): Eliquis ($14.4B, still growing +8% but facing IRA price-setting and patent expiry) alongside eroding franchises Revlimid (−49%), Pomalyst (−23%), Sprycel (−62%), and Abraxane (−58%).

The competitive core sits in immuno-oncology (the Opdivo/Yervoy franchise), a differentiated cell-therapy platform (Breyanzi, Abecma), and first-in-class assets: Camzyos in obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, Cobenfy (the first novel schizophrenia mechanism in decades), and Sotyktu (TYK2 inhibitor). Several key products, notably Yervoy and Orencia, still face no marketed biosimilars, a source of near-term durability that will erode on defined timelines (Orencia SC biosimilar as early as February 2028 per the Dr. Reddy's agreement).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Headline revenue was flat, masking the internal rotation described above. The GAAP swing from a −$8.95B net loss in 2024 to $7.05B net income in 2025 (EPS $3.46) is an accounting artifact, not an operational one: 2024 absorbed a one-time $12.1B non-deductible in-process R&D charge for the Karuna/Cobenfy acquisition. Non-GAAP EPS rebounded to $6.15 from $1.15. The GAAP operating line screens near breakeven because it absorbs ~$3.3B of intangible amortization plus ~$3.7B of acquired IPRD; underlying operations are highly profitable, with a 14.6% net margin. 2025 is best read as a clean-up year: the Revlimid intangible fully amortized in Q4 2024 and Pomalyst in Q4 2025, dropping amortization ~$5.6B and revealing an underlying non-GAAP earnings base of roughly $12.5B.

**Cash flow — the real strength.** Operating cash flow was $14.2B in 2025 and FCF $12.8B on only ~$1.3B capex. This is the anchor of the income thesis and the source of the dividend coverage and deleveraging capacity. One item to monitor: Q1 2026 OCF fell to $1.1B from $1.95B, driven by the Eliquis U.S. list-price cut lowering customer receipts (U.S. gross-to-net fell from 51% to 38%) and higher litigation disbursements.

**Balance sheet.** The Altman Z-Score of 1.03 nominally flags "distress" but is misleading for a large-cap pharma: it is depressed by $21.8B goodwill plus $19.1B intangibles inflating the asset base and thin book equity ($18.5B) against $71.5B liabilities (D/E 3.87). BMY holds A/A2 credit ratings (stable), $10.2B cash, and net debt of $34.0B — down $4.4B in 2025 after repurchasing $8.7B of debt (taking a $356M redemption loss). Current ratio 1.26. The leverage is real and stems from the Celgene/Karuna/RayzeBio/Mirati acquisition spree; management explicitly notes further large deals could require financing and risk a downgrade, which constrains future business-development firepower.

**Capital allocation.** BMY paid ~$5.0B in dividends (yield ~4.1%; quarterly raised from $0.62 to $0.63 in 2026) and conducted no buybacks in 2025 despite $5.0B of remaining authorization, prioritizing debt reduction and BD. The ~$5.0B dividend against $12.8B FCF is a payout below 40% of FCF.

**Earnings quality.** Four consecutive beats (Q1 2026 +10.4%, Q4 2025 +11.9%, Q3 2025 +6.9%, Q2 2025 +35.0%) support confidence in near-term consensus. The estimate-revision trend is modestly positive: current-year EPS revised to $6.34 (+0.5% 30d, +1.3% 90d), next-year to $6.20 (+1.2% 90d). The current-quarter estimate has softened slightly (−3.2% 90d), consistent with the diabetes-royalty roll-off and Eliquis price compression flowing through.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is binary in structure: can the Growth Portfolio outrun the combined drag of legacy erosion, IRA price-setting, and royalty roll-off? The Q1 2026 growth-product momentum is strong — Camzyos +97%, Cobenfy +107%, Breyanzi +56%, Opdivo Qvantig >200%, Reblozyl +16%. The Growth Portfolio grew +12% in Q1 2026 while Legacy fell −6%, netting +3% total. That the total is positive and accelerating relative to 2025's flat line is the key data point supporting the eventual crossover.

Against this, the mechanical headwinds are severe over 2026-2028:

- **Eliquis** carries IRA MFP from January 2026, a U.S. list-price cut, free-to-Medicaid provisions, and EU patent expiry in November 2026 with generics already selling in the UK and Poland and a U.S. Paragraph IV challenge (Azurity) underway. The product is still growing on volume (+16% in Q1 2026) but faces price/GTN compression that is already visible.
- **Royalty cliff:** ~$2.7B of pretax income — disproportionately valuable because it is near-pure-margin — rolls off. The AstraZeneca diabetes royalty ended December 2025 (reducing Q1 2026 non-GAAP EPS), and Keytruda plus Tecentriq royalties both terminate December 31, 2026. This is a mechanical 2026-2027 headwind independent of any product performance.
- **IRA expansion into growth franchises:** Orencia's selection for negotiation beginning 2028 signals the regime now reaches BMY's growth products, capping the durability of the offset.

Pipeline execution has been mixed. Setbacks include Camzyos missing in non-obstructive HCM (ODYSSEY), milvexian's LIBREXIA-ACS Phase III stopped for futility (November 2025), Cobenfy missing the adjunctive schizophrenia endpoint (ARISE) plus trial-conduct irregularities in the ADEPT-2 Alzheimer's psychosis study requiring re-enrollment, and Phase III misses for Opdualag and Reblozyl. Offsetting: Cobenfy monotherapy switch data, Camzyos SCOUT-HCM (adolescents), mezigdomide/iberdomide in myeloma, Opdivo cHL and Sotyktu PsA approvals, and iza-bren TNBC. The milvexian and Cobenfy-Alzheimer's disappointments matter because both were "next-leg-of-growth" narratives; the base compounders (Camzyos, Reblozyl, Breyanzi, Cobenfy schizophrenia) are the durable engine.

Net, growth prospects are best characterized as flat-to-modestly-positive on a total-revenue basis through the trough, with a plausible re-acceleration as legacy and royalty drags anniversary out in 2027-2028. We score growth 5.5, reflecting the tension between strong growth-product momentum and the mechanical near-term drag. This is consistent with the declining trajectory in prior reports (6.5 → 5.5) and the current data confirms rather than reverses it.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$60.74, BMY trades at ~9.6x the $6.34 current-year consensus EPS and ~9.8x the $6.20 next-year estimate. The trailing P/E of 17.0x reflects the GAAP EPS of $3.46 that was depressed by amortization; the forward figures on non-GAAP earnings power are the relevant metric.

Against pharma peers, the picture is nuanced. Peer median P/E is 20.0x (PFE 20.0x, ZTS 13.3x), with several peers showing negative or distorted multiples (VTRS −63.9x, JAZZ 499.9x, ELAN −49.3x) that make the median a weak benchmark. On a like-for-like basis, BMY's ~9.6-10x forward multiple sits below Pfizer's 20x, but that discount is arguably warranted: BMY's revenue is flat-to-slightly-up while carrying a heavier near-term IRA/patent/royalty drag than most peers, and its D/E of 3.87 is elevated. The dividend yield of ~4.1% is above the peer median 2.6% and below Pfizer's 6.7%, offering absolute-yield support.

The analyst target set — low $40, mean $62.96, median $63.00, high $75 — puts the stock roughly at the mean, implying limited consensus upside (~4% to median). On an inflection stock with rising estimates one might treat targets as stale floors, but BMY does not fit that profile: estimate revisions are only mildly positive (+1.3% 90d, well below the +30% threshold), and the stock is not cheap relative to a rapidly rising estimate base — it is cheap relative to a flat-to-modestly-rising one that carries known 2026-2028 headwinds. That combination is closer to a value/transition profile than a mispricing.

Applying the appropriate skepticism to cheapness as a standalone signal: the low multiple is not a clean buy signal. It reflects a genuine, quantifiable earnings trough. For the valuation to work as an upside catalyst rather than a value trap, the market needs to gain confidence that Growth Portfolio compounding plus BD can bridge the trough — evidence that will accumulate over several quarters, not immediately. We score valuation 5.5: the yield and forward multiple provide downside support, but there is no identified near-term catalyst to close the gap to fair value, and the cheapness alone does not warrant a higher score.

## Competitive Landscape

BMY competes in immuno-oncology against Merck's Keytruda (the dominant PD-1, from which BMY collects a patent royalty that ends in 2026) and Roche's Tecentriq. Its defense strategy is franchise extension — subcutaneous Opdivo Qvantig to protect the Opdivo base ahead of eventual biologic exclusivity loss — and diversification into radiopharmaceuticals (RayzeBio, new Indianapolis facility), targeted protein degradation (CELMoDs: iberdomide, mezigdomide), and next-generation/in-vivo CAR-T (Orbital Therapeutics acquisition, $1.4B IPRD; BioNTech pumitamig collaboration, $1.5B upfront).

The competitive trajectory is mixed. In hematology and select oncology indications, BMY is holding or gaining with Breyanzi (+82% in 2025), Reblozyl (+31%), and Camzyos (+77%) — differentiated assets in expanding indications. In cardiovascular, the milvexian ACS failure removed a key next-generation anticoagulant that was intended to succeed Eliquis, weakening the long-term franchise defense. In neuroscience, Cobenfy's schizophrenia launch is ramping fast (+107%) but the adjunctive and Alzheimer's setbacks narrow the near-term addressable expansion.

The entire large-pharma sector faces the IRA/tariff/drug-pricing regime, but BMY is more exposed than most because its two biggest legacy assets (Eliquis, Pomalyst) were among the first IRA-selected drugs, and its December 2025 government agreement — free Eliquis to Medicaid, API donation to a strategic reserve, ~80% direct-to-patient discounts on Sotyktu/Zeposia/Orencia — is one of the first company-specific deals of its kind in exchange for U.S. tariff relief through January 2029. This trades near-term price concessions for pricing/tariff certainty, but the exemptions may be terminated or not extended. Competitive position is stable-to-slightly-declining given the milvexian setback and IRA reach into growth franchises; we score it 5.5, consistent with the prior decline from 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Patent cliff (central risk).** Revlimid generics are now unrestricted (as of January 31, 2026), Pomalyst generics entered the U.S. in March 2026, and Sprycel/Abraxane are already generic. Eliquis EU composition-of-matter patents expire November 2026 with generics already selling in the UK/Poland and a U.S. Paragraph IV challenge underway.

**IRA price-setting (accelerating).** Eliquis MFP effective January 2026 (30% of revenue), Pomalyst MFP January 2027, and Orencia newly selected for negotiation beginning 2028 — the first Growth Portfolio product to be reached. BMY lost its constitutional challenge (Third Circuit affirmed September 2025) and filed a certiorari petition in December 2025.

**Royalty roll-off (mechanical, under-appreciated).** ~$2.7B of near-pure-margin pretax royalty income rolls off across 2025-2026 (diabetes ended, Keytruda + Tecentriq end December 2026), a hard earnings headwind independent of product performance.

**Pipeline variance.** Milvexian ACS futility and Cobenfy Alzheimer's trial-conduct irregularities are genuine disappointments to the long-term growth narrative.

**Impairment risk.** $19.1B of intangibles ($7.2B IPRD) already produced $949M of 2025 impairments and a $410M Q1 2026 IPRD write-down (radiopharma + oncology).

**Leverage.** D/E 3.87 and $34B net debt constrain future BD; management flags downgrade risk from further large deals.

Most of these risks are known and at least partially priced — the discount multiple reflects them. What remains uncertain is the magnitude and pace of Eliquis erosion post-2026 and whether the Growth Portfolio can bridge the trough. Because the largest single risks (patent cliff, IRA) are well-telegraphed and mechanical rather than surprising, but the Eliquis erosion curve is a live binary and the pipeline is high-variance, we score risk factors 5.0 — moderate idiosyncratic risk, partially but not fully priced.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV of 28.9% sits essentially in line with 1-year historical volatility of 28.0% (fair, spread 1.0%), and IV percentile within the current chain is a low 4% — the options market is not pricing elevated uncertainty. Put/call skew is balanced (2.0%), and the put/call OI ratio of 0.42 is not bearish. The term structure is flat (near 28.9% ≈ far 29.8%), with a modest 1-month IV bump to 40.6% around the late-July earnings window (implied move ±6.2%). A single large put block at the $60 strike (July 31, 10.8x volume/OI) is consistent with earnings hedging rather than directional conviction. Options positioning neither confirms nor contradicts the fundamental view; it reads as calm, consistent with a low-beta income name. IV in line with HV means entry timing is neither favorable nor unfavorable on a volatility basis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The Growth Portfolio inflects total revenue meaningfully positive as legacy and royalty drags anniversary out in 2027-2028. Cobenfy scales into a multi-billion schizophrenia franchise with indication expansions, Camzyos/Reblozyl/Breyanzi keep compounding at 30%+, and BD (RayzeBio radiopharma, BioNTech pumitamig, in-vivo CAR-T) rebuilds the pipeline. FCF sustains the dividend and continued deleveraging, and the multiple re-rates from ~9.6x toward the peer 20x median as the trough clears. Bull target ~$75 (analyst high), roughly the peer-median forward multiple applied to a re-accelerating earnings base.

**Bear case.** Eliquis erodes faster than expected post-2026 EU patent loss and under IRA MFP, the royalty cliff bites in 2026-2027, and pipeline setbacks (milvexian, Cobenfy Alzheimer's) mean the Growth Portfolio cannot fully bridge the trough. Earnings drift down toward the low-$5 range, the dividend still holds but growth stalls, and the multiple stays compressed. Bear target ~$40 (analyst low), roughly −34% from current.

**Base case.** Total revenue stays flat-to-slightly-positive through 2026-2027 as growth products offset the mechanical drags, non-GAAP EPS holds in the low-to-mid $6 range, the dividend is defended, and the stock trades within its recent $54-63 band without a durable re-rate until the Eliquis erosion path is visible. Base target ~$63 (analyst median).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income).** This is a hold for the dividend and undemanding forward multiple, not for near-term appreciation. The scores have declined across four consecutive reports (6.5 → 5.5) with the recommendation held HOLD each time; the current data confirms the transition-year framing rather than offering a reason to change the call. The stock is up ~26% over the trailing twelve months and now sits mid-range against a ~$63 median target, so much of the recovery from the 2025 lows is realized.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Total revenue growth accelerates above +5% YoY for two consecutive quarters, confirming the Growth Portfolio has decisively outrun the legacy/royalty/IRA drag.
- Current-year EPS revisions turn sharply positive (>+10% over 90 days), signaling consensus is catching up to a faster inflection.
- Clarity that Eliquis erosion post-EU-patent-expiry is milder than feared (e.g., 2027 Eliquis revenue declining less than ~15%), or a major pipeline win (Cobenfy indication expansion approval, a successful next-gen anticoagulant readout) that restores a credible next-leg-of-growth narrative.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Dividend coverage deteriorates — FCF falls below ~$8B or the dividend consumes >70% of FCF, indicating the income thesis is at risk.
- Eliquis revenue declines materially faster than the ~8-15% market expectation post-2026, or a further large intangible impairment signals overpayment on the acquisition spree.
- A credit-rating downgrade below A-/A3, or a debt-funded large acquisition that stretches leverage further without a clear return path.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoint is the late-July 2026 Q2 earnings report, with the full-year 2026 print and 2027 guidance being the more decisive read on how the royalty cliff and Eliquis MFP flow through.

## Conclusion

Bristol Myers Squibb is a high-quality cash generator ($12.8B FCF, ~4.1% covered dividend, A-rated credit) trading at an undemanding ~9.6x forward earnings, but the valuation discount is earned rather than a clean mispricing. The company is navigating a well-defined 2026-2028 trough where Eliquis IRA price-setting and EU patent expiry, a ~$2.7B high-margin royalty roll-off, and IRA expansion into the Orencia growth franchise converge as mechanical headwinds. The Growth Portfolio is compounding fast enough that an eventual inflection is plausible, and the four-quarter beat streak plus modestly positive estimate revisions support near-term consensus, but pipeline setbacks (milvexian ACS futility, Cobenfy Alzheimer's irregularities) and the absence of a near-term catalyst keep this an income-and-transition story.

We rate BMY HOLD with an overall score of 5.5, classified as an income hold. The dividend and cash generation are the durable reason to own it through the trough; the path to a re-rating runs through visible evidence that growth-product compounding and business development can bridge the 2026-2028 earnings valley. Own it for yield, reassess at Q2 and the full-year 2026 print, and require accelerating total revenue growth plus milder-than-feared Eliquis erosion before upgrading.
