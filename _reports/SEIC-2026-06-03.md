**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SEI Investments is a high-return, capital-light provider of investment processing technology, fund administration, and asset management to the financial services industry. The financial profile is genuinely elite for the Financials sector: FY2025 revenue of $2.30B (+8%), operating margin expansion from 22% to 27% over two years, ROE of 29.2%, net margin of 31.2%, an Altman Z-Score of 4.8, and an essentially debt-free balance sheet. Free cash flow of roughly $585M (25% FCF margin) funds a buyback that has shrunk the diluted share count from 137M in 2022 to ~124.5M by Q1 2026, mechanically lifting EPS. The Investment Managers segment, which administers ~$1.0T of alternative assets within ~$1.5T total, is the growth engine and benefits from a multi-year shift of large alternative managers from insourcing to outsourcing administration.

The stock trades at ~14.8x trailing/forward EPS of $5.63, a clear discount to the asset-management peer median P/E of ~19.1x. Headline FY2025 EPS growth of 28% overstates organic momentum because it includes a one-time $0.58/share gain on the June 2025 divestiture of the Family Office Services business; normalized growth is closer to the ~13% Q1 2026 revenue run-rate. Estimate revisions are positive but modest (+3.2% to +3.7% across all forward periods over 90 days), well short of the velocity that would force a re-rating. The discount to peers reflects identifiable concerns rather than pure mispricing.

We rate SEIC a HOLD with a growth lean. The business is high quality and the valuation is undemanding, but three concrete overhangs prevent an upgrade to BUY: (1) an unresolved FCA Voluntary Requirement and Skilled Person governance review on the UK subsidiary (SIEL), open since mid-2024, that constrains UK product launches; (2) the Stratos acquisition, which pivots SEI into directly owning advisory firms via a structurally complex deal with redeemable minority puts/calls and is already margin-dilutive (Investment Advisors operating margin fell from 47% to 42% in Q1 2026); and (3) an options market in backwardation pricing near-term stress (P/C OI ratio jumped to 6.81, near-term IV 31.4% vs 22.1% historical vol) with no offsetting dated catalyst. The combination caps near-term asymmetry. We would upgrade on FCA resolution or evidence of Stratos margin accretion.

## Company Overview

SEI Investments, headquartered in Oaks, Pennsylvania with ~5,000 employees, manages, advises, or administers ~$1.9 trillion in assets as of March 31, 2026. Its differentiation is an integrated front-to-back model that unifies proprietary technology (the SEI Wealth Platform and legacy TRUST 3000), trust-based custody through SEI Private Trust Company, operations outsourcing, and in-house asset management. In 2025, roughly 57% of revenue came from technology and operations outsourcing and ~38% from asset management fees.

The business runs through five segments:

- **Investment Managers (~35% of revenue)** — outsourced operating platform for alternative and traditional asset managers; a recognized leader in private-credit and alternatives administration, ~39% operating margin, growing 12-15%. This is the crown jewel.
- **Private Banks (~26%)** — investment processing (SWP) and asset management for banks and trust companies; lower-margin (17-21%) and improving.
- **Investment Advisors (~25%)** — wealth technology, custody, and asset management for independent RIAs/broker-dealers; historically ~42-47% operating margin. The Stratos investment lands here.
- **Institutional Investors (~12%)** — OCIO services for pensions and nonprofits; high margin (~48%) but in secular decline as defined-benefit plans erode (~-1%/yr).
- **Investments in New Businesses (~2%)** — incubation, runs at a small loss.

SEI owns a 38.4% minority stake in LSV Asset Management, a value-equity manager accounted for under the equity method. LSV contributed ~$132M of equity earnings in 2025, roughly 14% of pretax income, while SEI does not control it and LSV has been seeing net outflows.

## Financial Analysis

SEI screens as a high-quality compounder. Revenue grew from $1.92B (2023) to $2.13B (2024, +11%) to $2.30B (2025, +8%). Operating income rose from $424.5M to $551.7M to $627.3M, lifting operating margin from 22% to 27%. Net income to SEI moved from $462M to $581M to $715M, and diluted EPS from $3.46 to $4.41 to $5.63.

The +28% FY2025 EPS jump is materially flattered by a one-time $94.4M pre-tax gain on the Family Office Services sale to Aquiline. Stripping that out, underlying EPS growth was strong but not 28%. The cleaner read is Q1 2026: revenue $622.2M (+13% YoY), operating income $189.5M (+21%), diluted EPS $1.40 (+20%; adjusted $1.44). Operating cash flow jumped to $221.6M from $146.5M a year prior. Average AUA grew 21% YoY to ~$1.3T, confirming the alternatives-administration tailwind.

Balance sheet and cash flow are excellent. ROA 20.8%, current ratio 3.29, Z-Score 4.8 (safe zone). The company was essentially debt-free through 2024; at Q1 2026 only $32.4M of acquisition-related promissory notes appear. Cash fell from $840M (2024) to $400M (Dec 2025), driven by the ~$440.8M cash outlay for Stratos plus aggressive buybacks.

Capital allocation is shareholder-return-heavy and disciplined on price. In 2025 SEI repurchased 7.5M shares for $616M (avg $82.61), paid $124M in dividends, and Q1 2026 added $208M of buybacks (2.55M shares) plus $63.7M in dividends. Approximately $495M remains under authorization. The semi-annual dividend was raised to $0.52 from $0.49. The steadily shrinking share count is a structural EPS tailwind.

Two financial-quality caveats. First, the effective tax rate is creeping higher (23.4% in Q1 2026 vs ~22%), driven by Section 162(m) non-deductibility of executive comp under SEI's option-heavy pay model — a recurring, modest drag. Second, the Stratos deal pushed goodwill from $170M to $388M and intangibles from $77M to $480M, raising amortization and future impairment risk if synergies disappoint.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth driver is the Investment Managers segment, riding the outsourcing of alternatives and private-credit administration. Large managers are shifting from insourcing to outsourcing, and the retailization of alternatives (interval funds, BDCs, CITs) expands the addressable pool. This is a multi-year, broad-based shift rather than a single-client story, which raises its durability.

The Stratos acquisition is the most consequential strategic move in years: SEI is moving from an arms-length tech/custody/asset-management partner into directly owning financial-advisory firms (~350+ advisors, ~$40B client assets, with rights exercised over 11 affiliated practices in Q1 2026). The strategic logic is to deepen distribution for SEI's OCIO, alternatives, and asset-management products and offset legacy fund-program erosion. Near-term, it is dilutive on intangible amortization. Whether it becomes accretive within 12-18 months is the central open question.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current-year consensus rose +3.2% over 90 days to $5.88, next-year +3.4% to $6.62, with current-quarter (+2.4%) and next-quarter (+2.9%) similarly constructive. The 30-day revisions (+2.6% to +4.3%) slightly outpace the 90-day, a mild acceleration. This is healthy momentum, but well short of the +30%-over-90-days velocity that would mark a trough-earnings re-rating story. SEI is a steady mid-teens grower.

The earnings track record supports moderate confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one miss over four quarters (Q1 2026 +7.1%, the large Q2 2025 +46% beat distorted by the one-time gain, Q4 2025 -2.8%). Forward EPS implies ~$6.62 next year, roughly 13% growth off the normalized base.

## Valuation Assessment

At $87.05, SEIC trades at ~14.8x trailing/forward EPS of $5.63 and ~14.8x current-year consensus of $5.88. Against the Asset Management & Custody Banks peer median P/E of 19.1x, this is a meaningful discount. Peer comps range widely: AMG 12.3x, JHG 10.2x, VCTR 19.1x, CG 30.6x, CRBG 57.1x. SEI's higher quality (29% ROE, 27% operating margin, debt-free) versus pure-play asset managers like AMG/JHG argues for a premium to those names, not a discount.

The discount is partly explained by identifiable concerns — the FCA overhang, Stratos integration complexity and dilution, LSV outflows, and secular pressure on legacy fund programs — rather than pure mispricing. On a sum-of-quality basis the stock looks cheap; adjusting for the overhangs it looks fairly valued with modest upside. Applying ~16-17x to next-year EPS of $6.62 yields a $106-112 fair value, consistent with the analyst mean ($104.86) and median ($107) targets. The analyst range is wide ($69 low, $122 high), reflecting genuine dispersion on how the FCA and Stratos issues resolve.

We do not treat prior price action as a valuation input; the stock is ~7% below its 52-week high of $93.96 and has been range-bound between $75 and $94 for a year. The modest discount to peers and the absence of strong estimate velocity place the valuation score in the moderately-favorable range rather than the asymmetric-upside zone that would support a BUY. Per the cycle-adjusted framework, this is not a trough-earnings situation; trailing and forward multiples are nearly identical, so there is no hidden cheapness to unlock.

## Competitive Landscape

SEI's moat is its integrated single-infrastructure model — few peers combine proprietary wealth technology (SWP), trust-based custody, fund administration, and asset management under one roof. The strongest structural tailwind is the outsourcing of alternatives and private-credit administration in the Investment Managers segment, where SEI is a recognized leader serving many of the world's largest alternative managers.

The competitive set is formidable and well-capitalized: SS&C, State Street, BNY, Northern Trust, and Citco in Investment Managers; FIS, FNZ, and Avaloq in Private Banks; Envestnet, Orion, Schwab, and Fidelity in Investment Advisors; Mercer, Aon, and BlackRock in Institutional. Headwinds are equally structural: fee compression, the active-to-passive migration, the mutual-fund-to-ETF shift (SEI fund-program revenue declined 4% in 2025), RIA consolidation, and DB-plan erosion pressuring the Institutional segment.

Trajectory is mixed-positive: SEI is gaining share in the high-growth, high-margin alternatives-administration market while legacy fund programs and Institutional decline. The Stratos move is a bet that owning advice and distribution deepens wallet share and offsets legacy erosion — strategically coherent but unproven. The competitive position is improving on net because the growth engine (Investment Managers, 39% margin) is outgrowing the eroding segments.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by company-specific, partly under-appreciated items:

1. **FCA Voluntary Requirement on UK subsidiary (SIEL).** Since July 2024, SEI's UK arm operates under a publicly-registered VREQ and a mandated two-stage Skilled Person review of governance and controls. The stage-two review is incomplete. This constrains UK product launches (UK revenue ~$44M/quarter) and is a reputational and cost overhang with no clear resolution date.

2. **Stratos integration and structure.** SEI owns 57.5% of SEI-Eclipse; the 42.5% minority is redeemable via put/call options at 36/54/72 months, booked as $269.9M temporary equity growing each quarter via remeasurement. Stratos management retains rights that may limit SEI's control over integration. A separate Mexico affiliate option (NSC Asesores, ~$103M) remains under due diligence. Layered earnout/clawback mechanics and a ballooning intangibles base create future cash calls and impairment risk.

3. **Market-sensitivity of fees.** A large share of revenue is asset-value-based, so market downturns flow directly to revenue. The SEI Integrated Cash sweep program ($82.9M in 2025, up from $51.5M) is exposed to rate cuts and client cash behavior.

4. **LSV dependency.** ~14% of pretax income comes from a 38.4% stake SEI does not control; LSV has been seeing net outflows, with 2025 net income down 3%.

5. **Proprietary capital at market risk.** A new program seeds SEI's own capital into LSV funds (a consolidated VIE with ~$215M assets/$136M short positions), introducing earnings volatility and conflict-of-interest exposure not present historically.

6. **Litigation cluster.** Rubicon/Scott Mason (max ~$15M), Qu v. LSV (no estimate), Hall ERISA class action over SEI's own 401(k), and Equilus custodial matters. Individually immaterial, collectively reflecting a rising regulatory/litigation backdrop.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is flashing near-term caution that diverges from the constructive fundamentals. Near-term ATM IV is 31.4% versus 22.1% historical vol (rich by 9.3 points), the term structure is in backwardation (31.4% near-term > 29.1% six-month), and the put/call OI ratio spiked from 0.29 to 6.81 over the prior week while IV rose 6 points and price fell ~3%. Put/call skew is elevated at 6.6%, and the nearest expiry prices a ±5.5% move. Backwardation plus a put-skewed OI surge typically signals positioning ahead of a specific event or a hedging build into the June 18 expiry. It does not override the fundamental view, but it argues against chasing the stock here and is consistent with our HOLD. For a fundamentally sound name with no scheduled binary catalyst before mid-summer earnings, this argues for waiting for the stress to resolve rather than adding.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$112-122):** The alternatives-administration tailwind sustains low-double-digit revenue growth, the Investment Managers segment continues to expand margins, Stratos turns accretive within 12-18 months and deepens product distribution, the FCA matter resolves cleanly, and the buyback continues compounding EPS at ~3-4%/year. Re-rating toward the peer-quality-adjusted ~18-19x on next-year EPS of $6.62 produces $112-122. Analyst high target is $122 (Piper Sandler at $109 Overweight anchors the constructive camp).

**Bear Case (~$69-72, -17% to -21%):** A market drawdown compresses asset-based fees, Stratos dilution persists or the redeemable-minority puts create unexpected cash calls, the FCA review drags or escalates, LSV outflows accelerate, and rate cuts compress the cash-sweep fee line. De-rating toward ~11-12x on flat-to-modest EPS yields the $69 analyst low.

**Base Case (~$104-107):** Steady ~13% normalized earnings growth, modestly positive estimate revisions, and the discount to peers narrowing only partially as the overhangs resolve gradually. ~16-17x on $6.62 = $106-112; we anchor to the consensus mean/median of $104-107.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month HOLD with a growth lean.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FCA Voluntary Requirement on SIEL resolved or lifted, removing the UK product-launch constraint and the reputational overhang.
- Investment Advisors segment operating margin stabilizes or recovers toward the high-40s, confirming Stratos is on a path to accretion rather than persistent dilution.
- Estimate-revision velocity accelerates meaningfully (current-year consensus moving above ~$6.10 with revenue growth holding ≥13%), or the stock pulls back toward the low-$80s on unchanged fundamentals, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill/intangible impairment on Stratos, or disclosure that the redeemable-minority puts will require a materially larger-than-modeled cash outlay.
- Sustained quarterly EPS misses (two consecutive) accompanied by negative estimate revisions.
- FCA escalation from a Voluntary Requirement to a formal enforcement action with quantified penalties.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next scheduled reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings release (mid-to-late July), which should provide an updated read on Stratos margins and FCA progress. The June 18 options expiry is a nearer interim checkpoint given the backwardation signal.

## Conclusion

SEI is a high-quality, debt-light compounder with a genuine secular tailwind in alternatives and private-credit administration, elite returns on capital (29% ROE), and a disciplined buyback that mechanically compounds EPS. At ~14.8x forward earnings versus a peer median near 19x, it is not expensive. The reason it is a HOLD rather than a BUY is concrete and falsifiable: the FCA Voluntary Requirement on the UK subsidiary remains unresolved and constrains the business; the Stratos acquisition is structurally complex, currently margin-dilutive, and carries redeemable-minority cash-call risk; and estimate revisions, while positive, are modest rather than strong. The options market is pricing near-term stress with no offsetting dated catalyst to force a re-rating before mid-summer. These are specific concerns that cap near-term asymmetry. We would move to BUY on FCA resolution or confirmed Stratos margin recovery, and accumulate on a pullback toward the low-$80s on unchanged fundamentals.
