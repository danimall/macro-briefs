## Executive Summary

InterDigital's Q2 2026 was the most consequential quarter in the company's recent history on operating substance, and the market has priced most of it. Revenue of $260.2M against $139–143M of guidance included **$110.0M from the Streaming and Cloud Services program, up from zero a year earlier** — the first revenue ever recognized from licensing streaming *service operators* rather than device makers. Annualized recurring revenue reached a record $625.7M (+13% y/y), FY2026 guidance was raised $85M at the midpoint to $775–845M of revenue and $469–529M of Adjusted EBITDA, and the Unified Patent Court enjoined Disney twice across 11 EU countries while twice finding Disney an unwilling licensee. Consensus current-year EPS rose 20.2% over 90 days to $11.55. The shares are up roughly 26% from their mid-July level.

The valuation now embeds that outcome. On the company's own non-GAAP diluted share count of ~30.6M — not the 25.8M basic count behind the quoted $8.5B market cap — equity value at $328.41 is roughly $10.0B and enterprise value roughly $9.3B after $380M of convertible principal and $1,112M of cash and short-term investments. That is **27.8x the FY2026 non-GAAP EPS midpoint of $11.83, 37.4x the GAAP midpoint of $8.79, and 18.7x EV/Adjusted EBITDA at the guidance midpoint** — paid in a year when guided revenue is ~2.9% *below* FY2025's $834.0M and guided Adjusted EBITDA is ~15% below FY2025's ~$589M, because FY2025 carried an unusually large slug of near-100%-margin catch-up revenue and FY2026 operating expenses are up 40% in the first half. Consensus 2027 EPS of $11.59 is flat against 2026's $11.55, so the multiple is not being supported by near-term earnings growth in either the company's guidance or the sell-side model.

We are maintaining HOLD, consistent with the prior review and with an overall score that continues to drift lower (7.0 → 6.1 → 6.0 across five reports). The decline is located in valuation and in the risk profile, not in business quality: the July HOLD at roughly $258 offered materially better asymmetry against the same asset. The re-rating since then is largely *earned* — a 20.2% lift in current-year consensus against a 27% price move leaves the forward multiple only modestly higher (roughly 26.8x to 28.4x on current-year consensus) — but the residual upside to our base case is now single-digit against a 30% bear case, and the two largest swing factors (the Amazon arbitration award, 18–24 months out, and Disney's litigation and antitrust posture) are binary rather than forecastable.

## Company Overview

InterDigital is a pure research-and-licensing company with no product revenue. It develops wireless, video and increasingly AI-adjacent technologies, contributes them to standards bodies (3GPP, video codec groups), patents them, and licenses the resulting standard-essential portfolio. There is essentially no cost of goods sold, which is why FY2025 operating margin was 55.3% on $834.0M of revenue and net margin 48.8%.

Three reporting programs:

- **Smartphone** — Q2 2026 revenue $122.7M. Eight of the top ten global OEMs licensed, covering roughly 85% of the market; Apple (patent license to September 30, 2029), Samsung and Xiaomi licensed through the end of the decade. Smartphone ARR was a record ~$492M in Q1 2026 and approximately flat sequentially in Q2.
- **CE, IoT and Auto** — Q2 2026 revenue $27.5M. LG Electronics (signed through the joint TV licensing program operated with Sony), Sony renewal, Buffalo Americas, KEBA (EV chargers), a fintech point-of-sale manufacturer.
- **Streaming and Cloud Services** — Q2 2026 revenue $110.0M, the first revenue the program has ever produced. Driven by the Amazon agreement announced June 11, 2026, covering both devices and services including Prime Video, resolving pending litigation and submitting valuation to binding arbitration.

The competitive assets that actually carry the story are the standards position (management cites more than 110 standards leadership roles and multiple 3GPP chair positions) and the willingness plus ability to enforce. Because InterDigital has no products, it cannot trade cross-licenses; portfolio strength and injunctive leverage are the entire negotiating toolkit. On that dimension the record is strong: six-for-six in recent injunction proceedings per management, two UPC injunctions against Disney covering 11 EU countries with unwilling-licensee findings in both, and a Brazilian injunction against Tencent on two 5G patents where the court also found InterDigital's offer fair and reasonable.

## Financial Analysis

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: cash and short-term investments $1,112.4M, accounts receivable $193.0M (from $69.8M at December 31, 2025), total assets $2,192.1M, total liabilities $989.9M, shareholders' equity $1,202.2M. Convertible principal outstanding $380.0M (from $460M), of which holders elected to convert $80.3M settling in Q3 2026 — settled in cash up to principal. Deferred revenue rose from $329.6M to $442.2M. FY2025 debt/equity was 0.87 and current ratio 1.84.

The FY2025 Altman Z-score of 2.94 sits marginally below the conventional 2.99 threshold, but the binding component is revenue-to-assets of 0.404. Altman's model was calibrated on manufacturers, where low asset turnover signals trouble; for an asset-light licensor carrying $1.1B of cash against $380M of convertible principal, low turnover is a design feature. EBIT-to-assets of 0.223 and equity-to-liabilities of 1.143 are healthy. We do not treat the grey-zone score as evidence of solvency risk. The Piotroski reading available (5 of 5 computable signals positive) is a liquidity and cash-conversion read, not a 9-point F-Score — two of the uncomputed change signals (revenue declined from $868.5M in FY2024 to $834.0M in FY2025; share count rose) would likely score negatively.

**The P&L is deteriorating on reported metrics while ARR improves.** H1 2026 versus H1 2025: research and portfolio development $112.2M vs $101.1M (+11%); licensing expense $86.8M vs $41.6M (+109%); G&A $45.0M vs $31.2M (+44%); total operating expense $244.1M vs $173.8M (+40%); income from operations $221.5M vs $337.3M (−34%); operating margin 47.6% vs 66.0%.

Two drivers of the licensing-expense doubling are identified and they carry different implications. Enforcement spend against Disney (Germany, Brazil, UPC, U.S.), TCL and Hisense (multi-jurisdictional, launched Q1 2026) and Tencent (Brazil) is cyclical but management explicitly declined to forecast a decline while those cases run. The second driver is structural: third-party revenue share on deals sourced through the joint TV licensing program with Sony, which the CFO named as the primary cause of the Q1 step-up in connection with the LG agreement. **Not all InterDigital revenue carries the same incremental margin**, and a mix shift toward joint-program sourcing compresses reported margin even as the top line grows. That is a permanent consideration for how much of gross licensing revenue reaches the EBITDA line.

**Cash conversion is the genuine watch item.** H1 2026 free cash flow of $67.6M converted only 35% of H1 net income of $191.7M, against FY2025's $528.6M of FCF on $406.6M of net income (130%). Accounts receivable rose $123.2M and other long-term assets rose $121.8M since year-end. Management attributed the Q1 build to cash due from new agreements and said Q2 collections would follow; Q2 operating cash flow of $82.5M was consistent with that, and Q3 was guided to another strong FCF quarter on scheduled payments. There is a further open item: the summary H1 2026 cash-flow statement shows non-cash adjustments of $195.5M versus $17.5M a year earlier, against only ~$73M identifiable as D&A ($38.7M) plus share-based compensation ($34.4M). The composition of the remainder is not decomposed in the summary statement and we do not speculate on it; it should be read from the full Q2 10-Q before drawing cash-quality conclusions.

Annualizing Q2 FCF of $66.6M to $266M is not decision-useful — receipts are contractually scheduled and lumpy. A normalized frame is better: FY2025 FCF was ~90% of FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of ~$589M; applying 85–90% conversion to FY2026 EBITDA guidance of $469–529M implies **FY2026 FCF of roughly $400–475M**, or a 4.0–4.7% yield on the ~$10.0B diluted equity value. That band is a sensitivity, not a company forecast, and the principal risk to it is that part of the Amazon consideration may not be collectible until the arbitration concludes.

**Earnings surprises are an artifact of guidance design.** Four consecutive beats, including a 266% Q2 surprise, reflect a convention in which quarterly guidance covers existing licenses only and explicitly excludes new agreements and enforcement outcomes — the company's principal value drivers. The Q1 2026 non-GAAP result of $2.57 against a $2.54 non-GAAP consensus, in a quarter without a landmark deal, is the more informative surprise data point. This convention is arguably the most honest way to guide a business dependent on negotiation and court timing; it is also why headline surprise magnitudes should not be read as evidence of forecasting conservatism that will repeat.

We note a conflict in the supplied market data: quoted trailing and forward P/E are both 38.09 while quoted EPS is $11.80, which is internally inconsistent at any of the observed prices. All multiples in this report are computed from FY2026 guidance and consensus against the $328.41 basis.

## Growth Analysis

ARR is the metric management steers to, defined as (quarterly revenue − quarterly catch-up revenue) × 4. It reached $625.7M in Q2 2026, +13% y/y, from $567.2M in Q1 2026. The definition reproduces from the disclosed inputs: ($260.170M − $103.7M) × 4 = $625.9M against the reported $625.7M.

The composition of that growth matters more than the growth rate. Sequential ARR rose $58.5M while smartphone ARR was approximately flat (Q2 smartphone revenue of $122.7M annualizes to ~$491M). The step-up is therefore attributable to the streaming program. Because the company does not break catch-up out by program, streaming ARR can only be bounded: if all $103.7M of Q2 catch-up sat in the streaming line, streaming ARR is ~$25M; if none of the CE/IoT or smartphone lines carried catch-up, it is ~$58M. **Plausible range: $25–60M.** William Blair's externally reported estimate of $40–80M of recurring annual revenue from the Amazon deal brackets the upper half of that range.

Against a stated 2030 target of **$300M+ of streaming ARR** and **$1B+ total ARR**, the arithmetic is demanding. Streaming must grow five to twelve times in roughly four and a half years. Total ARR must compound at roughly 10% annually from mid-2026. And the smartphone program, which supplies ~79% of current ARR, is structurally complete: 85% of the market licensed, ARR of ~$492M against a ~$500M target, top three OEMs locked through the end of the decade, and the next repricing opportunity tied to 6G (standard expected finalized ~2029, wide adoption ~2030). Part of that $492M is a contractual floor under a hybrid agreement rather than realized run-rate royalties, which is a quality positive for downside but caps volume upside.

So essentially all 2026–2029 ARR growth must come from streaming and CE/IoT/Auto, and the streaming path runs through Disney — currently litigating, with an antitrust complaint filed against InterDigital — plus other SVOD/AVOD operators with whom no agreement has been announced. Management explicitly declined to update on further litigation plans.

Offsetting this, renewal attrition is quantified and recurring: five agreements expired during 2025 without renewal representing ~$31.7M of recurring revenue, of which roughly two-thirds had been renewed as of the Q1 call; 14 further agreements representing ~$15.0M of recurring revenue expire by the end of 2026. ARR growth is net of that mechanical headwind.

**Estimate revisions cut both ways here.** Current-year consensus rose 20.2% over 90 days to $11.55 — a large positive revision that reflects the Amazon deal and the guidance raise. But next-year consensus rose only 3.1% to $11.59, meaning the sell-side models a step-change in 2026 followed by no growth in 2027. Meanwhile current-quarter consensus fell 7.7% and next-quarter fell 29.2% to $2.55, consistent with Q3 guidance of $154–158M revenue and $1.94–2.13 non-GAAP EPS from existing contracts only. The strong revision signal that ordinarily supports a buy case is present at the annual level but is a level shift from a closed transaction, not evidence of an ongoing acceleration, and the flat 2027 line is the tell.

## Competitive Landscape

The streaming licensing frontier is the substantive competitive development. Video codec royalties have historically been collected from device makers; InterDigital is extending the claim to the service operators that encode and distribute content, and two data points suggest it is winning that argument. Amazon took a license covering both devices and services including Prime Video, resolving pending litigation. The UPC twice enjoined Disney on encoding patents across 11 EU countries and twice found Disney an unwilling licensee — a finding that materially weakens a defendant's FRAND defense in subsequent proceedings.

Management cited third-party reports that certain premium services, including 4K HD content, were disrupted in European markets as a result, and that consumer-protection agency investigations were triggered or discussed. That leverage is disproportionate to any single patent's value, which is what a licensor wants. It also cuts the other way: European regulators intervening because consumers lost premium service could become a political argument for constraining SEP injunctive relief, which is InterDigital's primary source of negotiating leverage. This is externally reported and not company-verified.

Demand-side insulation is real. Roughly 94% of revenue came from fixed-fee agreements per prior-quarter disclosure, with one large agreement structured as a hybrid — guaranteed fixed fee plus royalties above a shipment threshold. That decouples revenue from smartphone unit cycles, at the cost of forfeiting volume upside.

The structural constraint is that InterDigital has no products with which to cross-license. It depends entirely on portfolio strength plus litigation willingness, which is why the 3GPP chair positions and the injunction record carry so much weight and why an adverse FRAND-methodology ruling in a major jurisdiction would be portfolio-wide rather than case-specific.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration has not improved.** FY2025 disclosed three customers above 10% of revenue — Samsung ~31%, Apple ~16%, vivo ~14%, roughly 61% from three payers. Q2 2026 disclosed a single customer at approximately 42% of quarterly revenue. Program mix has broadened; counterparty concentration has not. A third program is not diversification of the payer base.

**The Amazon revenue is recognized on an estimate subject to binding arbitration.** Management describes the recognized figure as a conservative estimate of expected consideration, states the arbitration outcome cannot be assured, and says any adjustment on the award is more likely to increase than reduce recognized revenue. That last point is management's forward judgment, not a verifiable fact, and the award is 18–24 months out. The cited Samsung precedent appears to have resolved favorably, but one precedent is not a pattern, and a downward adjustment — or simply cash deferral into 2027–2028 — is a live outcome on 42% of a quarter's revenue.

**FRAND and antitrust counter-attack is the consequential tail risk.** Disney has filed an antitrust complaint and counterparty-initiated royalty-rate-setting proceedings exist in the United Kingdom. Case status requires re-verification, but the category is the standard defensive playbook against SEP licensors, and an adverse methodology ruling would compress achievable rates across the portfolio rather than in one dispute.

**Enforcement-cost escalation is confirmed and open-ended.** Licensing expense doubled in H1 2026; management declined to forecast a decline while Disney, TCL/Hisense and Tencent cases run. Costs are incurred whether or not campaigns succeed, and resulting revenue timing is unpredictable.

**Dilution, disclosed and price-linked.** 4.07M net warrant shares at the current price (13.6% post-issuance), maturing September 2027 through April 2028. The company's own risk-factor language characterizes the dilution as substantial and warns the market could react negatively.

**Open verification items.** The composition of the $195.5M H1 non-cash cash-flow adjustment and the $121.8M increase in other long-term assets are unexplained in the summary statements. The Lenovo arbitration appears resolved from management's past-tense references, but no award amount, date or revenue impact was located. The contracted-revenue schedule read at approximately $2.1B at June 30, 2026 (from roughly $1.5B of contracted fixed-price cash payments at December 31, 2025) includes management estimates for the Lenovo and Amazon arbitrations; it is neither audited backlog nor equivalent to the $442.2M deferred revenue balance. Tax exposure includes a challenged French research tax credit and an $11M favorable tax adjustment in the FY2026 non-GAAP reconciliation that was $0 in prior guidance.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($450, +37%).** Disney settles or is licensed at scale following the UPC injunctions and unwilling-licensee findings, and one or two further major SVOD/AVOD operators follow, taking streaming ARR from ~$25–60M toward $150M+ by 2028 and making the $300M 2030 target credible. The Amazon arbitration adjusts recognized revenue upward, producing a one-time catch-up and validating the recognition methodology. Enforcement spend normalizes as cases resolve, restoring operating margin toward the high 50s. CE/IoT/Auto compounds through the joint TV program and adjacent verticals. On that path, 2028 non-GAAP EPS of ~$15 at 30x supports $450, which is where the four covering analysts' $425–488 targets sit. Cheap implied volatility makes this an efficient path to express.

**Bear case ($230, −30%).** The Amazon arbitration award lands below cumulative recognized revenue, or defers cash into 2028 while recognized revenue stands — either outcome undermines confidence in the estimate-based recognition that produced Q2's headline. Disney prevails on FRAND methodology or gains traction on antitrust, or European regulators respond to consumer disruption by constraining SEP injunctive relief, removing the leverage that made Amazon settle. Non-renewals compound against the $15.0M of 2026 expirations. Enforcement spend stays at H1's elevated run rate, streaming ARR stalls near $30M, and 2027 non-GAAP EPS lands nearer $9 on a 24x multiple. The May 2026 low of $249.14 and April–June repurchase prints at $349/$273/$261 show the market has recently been willing to pay far less for a very similar fundamental picture.

**Base case ($355, +8.1%).** Streaming ARR grows toward $80–100M by end-2027 on Amazon plus one additional operator, Disney remains unresolved but under injunctive pressure, smartphone ARR holds at ~$490M with 2029 renewals unaddressed, CE/IoT adds incrementally, and enforcement spend stays elevated but stops growing. FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA of ~$530M at 19x plus ~$700M net cash over ~30.9M shares gives ~$350–355.

Probability-weighting base 50%, bull 25%, bear 25% gives an expected return of roughly +6%. That is a positive expected value with wide dispersion around it, and the dispersion is driven by two events we cannot forecast — an arbitration award and a litigation outcome. It supports holding, not initiating at size.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment point:** Q3 2026 earnings, expected late October 2026, which tests (a) whether the guided strong FCF quarter converts the accounts-receivable build, (b) whether streaming ARR is disclosed with more granularity, and (c) whether any new streaming counterparty has signed.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE or BUY:**
1. A Disney license or settlement announced on terms that lift streaming ARR above ~$120M, or any second major SVOD/AVOD agreement — either would convert the option-value component of the multiple into contracted recurring revenue.
2. Price toward $270–285 with fundamentals intact (FY2026 guidance maintained, ARR ≥$625M), which would put the shares near 23–24x forward non-GAAP earnings and restore the margin of safety that existed in July.
3. Two consecutive quarters of FCF conversion above 80% of net income plus full decomposition of the $195.5M non-cash adjustment and the $121.8M long-term asset increase, which would remove the cash-quality overhang.

**Downgrade triggers to REDUCE or AVOID:**
1. An Amazon arbitration award below cumulative recognized revenue, or disclosure that collection is deferred beyond 2027.
2. An adverse FRAND rate-setting or antitrust ruling in the UK, EU or U.S. that constrains injunctive relief or portfolio-wide royalty rates.
3. FY2026 revenue guidance cut, or ARR declining sequentially on non-renewals, or licensing expense rising further without a corresponding revenue event.

**Existing holders:** maintain. The annuity base is high quality, the balance sheet carries $1.1B of cash explicitly framed as an enforcement war chest against Disney-, Amazon- and TCL-scale opponents, and the streaming optionality is real and improving. **New capital:** wait for $270–285 or for a named second streaming counterparty. Model per-share value on ~30.6–31.0M shares, not 25.8M.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** InterDigital did the hard operating work in Q2 2026 — first streaming revenue, record ARR, two UPC injunctions with unwilling-licensee findings, an $85M guidance raise — and the shares captured it, rising ~26% off mid-July levels. At $328.41 the stock trades at 27.8x guided non-GAAP earnings and 18.7x EV/Adjusted EBITDA in a year when both guided revenue and guided EBITDA decline against FY2025, with consensus 2027 EPS flat against 2026. The premium to licensing-peer multiples is an option premium on Disney, further streaming operators, a favorable Amazon award and the 2029-2030 6G renewal cycle. Those are coherent options with genuine leverage behind them; they are not earnings.

The overall score declines to 6.0 from 6.1, extending a five-report drift from 7.0. The decline sits in valuation and risk, not in the franchise: business quality is intact and arguably improving in the video program. Financial health remains strong (7.0) on $1.1B of liquidity against $380M of shrinking convertible principal, tempered by 35% H1 cash conversion and an unexplained non-cash adjustment. Growth prospects (5.8) reflect ARR growth of 13% offset by declining reported revenue and EBITDA, a plateaued smartphone engine, and a 2030 streaming target requiring five to twelve times the current run rate. Risk factors (5.0) reflect 42% single-counterparty quarterly revenue recognized on an estimate subject to arbitration, an antitrust counter-suit from the most important growth prospect, and 13.6% post-issuance warrant dilution maturing within two years.

Base case $355 (+8.1%), bull $450 (+37%), bear $230 (−30%). Probability-weighted expected return of roughly 6% with that dispersion, resolved by two events outside our forecasting ability, is a hold rather than an accumulate. The entry we want is $270–285, or a second named streaming licensee.
