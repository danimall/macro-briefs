## Executive Summary

InterDigital's second quarter resolved the central open question in the bull case. The Streaming & Cloud Services program, which had generated zero revenue in its history, produced $110.0M in Q2 2026 from a June license with Amazon covering both devices and services including Prime Video, and simultaneously unwound litigation across the UK High Court, São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, Munich, Mannheim, the UPC's Mannheim and Düsseldorf divisions, the ITC, Delaware, E.D. Virginia and W.D. Texas. Recurring revenue, measured as the company's ARR (quarterly revenue less catch-up, annualized), reached $626.0M, +13.1% year over year and exactly reproducible from the 10-Q. FY2026 guidance was raised to $775–845M revenue, $469–529M adjusted EBITDA and $10.85–12.81 non-GAAP EPS, an $85M revenue increase at the midpoint. The prior two reports on this name were BUY at materially lower prices; those calls were correct and the operating evidence has confirmed them.

The problem is the entry price. The stock has moved from $258.30 on July 25 to $328.41, a 27% re-rating in eight sessions, and the arithmetic no longer offers much margin of safety. On the economic share count that matters — 25.806M shares outstanding plus roughly 4.05M shares issuable on the unhedged $105.43-strike warrants settling September 2027 through April 2028 — the stock trades at about 24x FY2027 consensus non-GAAP earnings against a consensus that is essentially flat versus FY2026 (+3.1% revision to $11.59). Warrant-adjusted enterprise value of roughly $9.07B is 18.2x guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA and 14.5x ARR. Strip out catch-up revenue entirely and the implied multiple on recurring EBITDA of roughly $310–330M is closer to 28x, which the market reads as an expectation that the deal-signing machine keeps producing catch-up indefinitely.

Two features of the quarter deserve more weight than the headline suggests. First, the $118M revenue beat versus guidance traces almost entirely to one transaction whose price does not yet exist: the Amazon agreement sends final terms to binding arbitration expected to take 18–24 months, and the corresponding non-current contract asset (unbilled receivable) rose from $21.0M at year-end to $143.0M at June 30. H1 operating cash flow was $98.6M against $191.7M of net income. Second, the company that won the Samsung ICC award of $1.05B over eight years (~$131M of annual recurring revenue, a 67% step-up) is now defending it: Samsung filed a challenge in December 2025 and InterDigital filed jurisdictional objections in July 2026. Samsung remains 13% of quarterly revenue and roughly a fifth of ARR. I move to HOLD on entry arithmetic and unresolved binaries, not on business deterioration.

## Company Overview

InterDigital is a research and licensing company with approximately 460 employees generating $834.0M of FY2025 revenue — about $1.8M per employee. Subsidiaries hold more than 38,000 patents and applications expiring between 2026 and 2045, split between a wireless portfolio (2G through 5G, 6G research, IEEE 802.11 Wi-Fi 4–8) built through decades of internal R&D and a video portfolio (AVC, HEVC, VVC, HDR, immersive) acquired substantially from Technicolor in 2018 and extended by the October 2025 Deep Render acquisition for AI-based codecs. Net patents on the balance sheet are only $317.6M; the franchise value sits in standards positioning and enforcement capability.

Four programs, one reportable segment. Q2 2026 revenue: Smartphone $122.7M, CE/IoT/Auto $27.5M, Streaming & Cloud Services $110.0M, Other $0.2M. Penetration tells the growth story more precisely than revenue does: approximately 85% of the global smartphone market is licensed including 8 of the top 10 vendors (Apple, Google, Honor, Lenovo, LG, OPPO, Samsung, vivo, Xiaomi), versus roughly 60% of tablets/PCs and roughly 35% of televisions. Smartphone is a rate-and-renewal business with negligible room for unit expansion. Every incremental dollar must come from CE/IoT/Auto or from streaming services.

Two platform arrangements complicate the economics. Under the Madison program (DTV and computer monitors, with Sony), CPPIB Credit is a financing investor entitled to a percentage of future Madison revenue through September 2030, carried as debt at a ~14.5% effective rate ($10.9M), and Technicolor is entitled to 42.5% of future cash receipts from new Madison licensing efforts (currently deemed not probable, unrecorded). Under Avanci, InterDigital contributes cellular SEPs to a pool where Avanci, not InterDigital, is the licensor.

## Financial Analysis

**Reported revenue is a poor read on the business.** FY2025 revenue fell 4.0% and H1 2026 fell 8.9%, but catch-up revenue — earned value attributable to pre-execution periods — dropped from $460.1M (FY2024) to $277.4M (FY2025) and from $247.1M to $167.4M across the first halves. Revenue excluding catch-up grew 36.3% in FY2025 ($408.4M → $556.6M) and 13.0% in H1 2026 ($264.0M → $298.2M). ARR of $626.0M reconciles precisely: ($260.2M − $103.7M) × 4.

**Margins are high but the marginal economics of the growth programs are worse than consolidated margins suggest.** Q2 adjusted EBITDA of $184.1M on $260.2M revenue is a 71% margin, flattered by catch-up carrying no matching cost. FY2026 guidance implies 61.6% at midpoints ($499M / $810M). Two H1 cost lines are inflecting:

| H1 opex driver | H1 2025 | H1 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| IP enforcement | $18.9M | $42.9M | +127% |
| Revenue share | $5.2M | $28.4M | +$23.2M |
| Share-based comp | $21.3M | $34.4M | +$13.1M |
| Departmental | $90.7M | $99.6M | +$8.9M |
| **Total opex** | **$173.8M** | **$244.1M** | **+40.4%** |

Accrued legal fees doubled from $14.0M to $29.8M, confirming enforcement spend is still accelerating. The H1 pace annualizes to roughly $86M, about 10% of guided revenue. Management declined to guide it lower; the CFO said arbitration "ring-fences things… but I don't want to oversell it that expenses would come down." Revenue-share costs spiked on LG TV catch-up flowing through Madison, a structural margin tax on television deals specifically, which are also the least-penetrated market.

**Cash conversion weakened materially in H1.** FY2025 free cash flow was $528.6M on $834.0M revenue, a 63.4% margin with operating cash flow above net income. H1 2026 produced $98.6M of operating cash flow against $191.7M of net income: receivables absorbed $123.2M, non-current contract assets $122.0M and deferred charges $40.3M, offset by $107.6M of deferred revenue growth. Q2 alone was better at $82.5M. The dominant item is the unbilled contract asset tied to Amazon — $110M of revenue recognized on a conservative estimate for a contract whose price will be set by arbitration in 18–24 months. This is conventional ASC 606 treatment for constrained variable consideration and the Samsung precedent trued up upward, so the bias is plausibly favorable. It is nonetheless revenue that has been neither invoiced nor priced.

**Balance sheet is strong with one near-term item to clear.** At June 30: cash, restricted cash and short-term investments $1,121.2M; total debt $389.1M, of which $378.2M is the 2027 convertible classified current and settled in cash by June 2027; net cash approximately $732M ($28.4/share). Current ratio 1.74, or 3.30 excluding the convertible against the liquidity pool. Reported returns are excellent (FY2025 ROE 36.9%, operating margin 55.3%). Tax receivables of $152.8M are largely foreign withholding credits whose recovery depends on generating U.S. tax capacity; the French CIR challenge (~$29M) and a potential ~$22.5M foreign-tax-credit exposure sit alongside. The Altman Z of 2.94 is a modelling artifact of 0.40 asset turnover on a cash-heavy, asset-light licensor and should not be read as solvency risk.

**Financial health score: 7.0.** Net cash, high-margin recurring base, covered dividend ($2.80 annualized costs ~$72M against recurring free cash flow I estimate near $260M). Held below 7.5 by H1 conversion, the unpriced $143M unbilled asset, receivable concentration of three licensees at 83% of AR (from 55% at year-end), and tax-receivable recoverability.

## Growth Analysis

The recurring base is compounding at a rate consistent with the 2030 target. Reaching $1B+ ARR from $626M requires an 11% CAGR over 4.5 years; the company is running at 13.1% with 13 new agreements in the trailing twelve months including Amazon, Xiaomi, Honor and LG TV. Management targets $300M+ of streaming ARR by 2030 within that $1B, against a video-services addressable market it sizes at $475B growing to $675B by 2029 — the royalty ask is roughly 0.04–0.06% of industry revenue, which supports the fairness argument and also explains why implementers litigate rather than pay.

Three qualifiers keep the growth score from going higher.

First, **the installed base does not escalate on its own**. Q3 guidance of $154–158M from existing contracts brackets Q2's recurring base of $156.5M, so ARR is guided roughly flat sequentially. The 13% growth rate is a function of new agreements landing, each of which requires either a negotiation or a multi-jurisdictional enforcement campaign.

Second, **consensus sees no earnings growth in 2027**. Current-year consensus was revised +20.2% to $11.55 (in line with the guided $10.85–12.81 range); next year was revised only +3.1% to $11.59. The contracted-revenue table supports the caution: fixed-fee royalty streams for FY+1 (2027) total $572.6M versus H2 2026's $292.3M, which annualizes below FY2026's guided $810M. Total contracted revenue rose to $2,112.8M from $1,769.0M, but that figure covers only dynamic fixed-fee streams, excludes variable royalties and unsigned agreements, and explicitly embeds management estimates for the unpriced Lenovo and Amazon arbitrations. It is not backlog and not a contractual minimum.

Third, **the quarterly beat record overstates forecast visibility**. Four consecutive beats, including Q2's 266% surprise, reflect a guidance convention (existing contracts only) rather than operating outperformance. The −29.2% revision to next-quarter consensus is the same convention working in reverse. Annual guidance, not quarterly consensus, is the usable anchor.

Against those qualifiers, the Amazon deal is a genuine proof-of-concept for a new monetization theory — that streaming *services*, not only devices, require HEVC/AVC licenses — from a counterparty with every incentive and resource to fight. Management said negotiations are progressing across both SVOD and AVOD. If a second large streaming license lands at a comparable scale, the 2030 target becomes achievable rather than aspirational.

**Growth prospects score: 6.6.** Compounding recurring base and a validated new program, offset by flat sequential guidance, flat 2027 consensus and dependence on litigation-driven deal timing.

## Competitive Landscape

InterDigital does not compete for licensees — patent rights are exclusionary, so no rival holds the same inventions. It competes for share of the aggregate royalty stack that implementers argue a product can bear, which makes the competitive question legal, not commercial.

**Evidence the position is strengthening.** Two UPC injunctions against Disney (Mannheim in June 2026, Düsseldorf in July 2026), each spanning 11 EU countries, with both tribunals finding Disney an unwilling licensee. Three Munich injunctions since October 2025, with coercive fines of €550,000 in January (reduced to €50,000 on appeal) and €150,000 in July for violations. A Brazilian preliminary injunction affirmed in May. A third Düsseldorf decision expected in September 2026. The Samsung ICC award set royalties 67% above the prior rate. Amazon chose to license and arbitrate rather than continue a multi-jurisdiction defence. Standards positioning is strong — 100+ leadership positions, multiple 3GPP chairs, a senior engineer elected vice chair of a 6G working group — and R&D of $211.4M in FY2025 (+7.4%) funds 6G, Wi-Fi 8 and post-VVC codecs. Deep Render targets end-to-end AI codecs, a bet on owning the next fifteen-year royalty base.

**Evidence the model itself is under attack.** Disney's Delaware antitrust complaint (August 2025) alleges monopolization in video compression and streaming markets and seeks injunctive relief against InterDigital's licensing practices; the DOJ Antitrust Division filed a Statement of Interest in October 2025 whose position I could not verify, and the motion to dismiss filed in September 2025 remains unruled as of the Q2 10-Q. TCL filed two Shenzhen suits in July 2026, one seeking a Chinese court determination of *global* FRAND terms for the HEVC portfolio in televisions, the other alleging abuse of dominance. Hisense filed a CADE antitrust action in Brazil in April 2026. In July 2026 the UK Supreme Court reversed two lower courts and held there is an issue to be tried on Tesla's FRAND claims against InterDigital *and Avanci*, including whether pool licenses must themselves be FRAND, which extends exposure to the entire Avanci-mediated automotive channel. In March 2026 a C.D. Cal. claim-construction ruling held certain claims of a subset of asserted patents invalid for indefiniteness (reconsideration sought, appeal intended); that case was stayed in June pending reexaminations and a Dolby declaratory action.

The counter-argument is that implementer antitrust and rate-setting claims are routine defensive tactics that frequently fail, InterDigital won the Tesla jurisdiction question twice before the reversal, and the reversal establishes only that there is an issue to be tried. Multiple tribunals have found the patents valid and infringed. The coordinated character of the response — three competition-law fronts plus a Chinese global rate-setting request — targets the rate structure across the entire book rather than individual contracts.

**Competitive position score: 6.7**, roughly stable versus the prior report: enforcement leverage is demonstrably working while the legal architecture underneath it faces more simultaneous challenges than at any point in the company's recent history.

## Risk Assessment

**Samsung's challenge to its own award (most material single item).** The July 2025 ICC award set $1.05B over 1/1/2023–12/31/2030. Samsung filed a request in December 2025 to challenge the determined royalties; InterDigital filed jurisdictional objections in July 2026. Unresolved. Samsung was ~31% of FY2025 revenue, so roughly a fifth of ARR sits behind this. The mitigant is real — the 10-K notes it is generally difficult to vacate or modify arbitration awards — but the separate Samsung TV agreement expired December 31, 2025 with no renewal confirmed in filings through Q2.

**Amazon price risk is two-sided.** Arbitration is 18–24 months out. Management expects any adjustment "more likely to increase rather than reduce" recognized revenue, and the Samsung precedent supports that, though that precedent is itself under challenge.

**Lenovo arbitration, Q4 2026 hearing.** Nearest-term binary. The license runs from January 1, 2024, so a favorable award generates substantial catch-up. Estimated Lenovo revenue is *already embedded* in the $2.11B contracted-revenue table, so an adverse award creates downside to a disclosed figure rather than upside to a blank.

**Concentration.** Q2: Amazon 42%, Apple 13%, Samsung 13% — 68% from ≥10% customers versus 78% a year earlier. Program mix diversified sharply (streaming from 0% to 42% of revenue) but customer concentration is essentially flat; one whale replaced another. Receivable concentration worsened to three licensees at 83% of AR from 55%.

**Renewal mechanics.** Five agreements representing ~$31.7M of recurring revenue expired during 2025; fourteen representing ~$15.0M expire through end-2026. Each is a re-pricing event with an option to fail, and management declined to commit to which close in Q4.

**Dilution and capital allocation.** 4.05M warrant shares at the current price, 13.6% post-issuance ownership dilution, against $96.1M of remaining buyback authorization. H1 repurchases of $31.1M were smaller than $55.7M of RSU tax withholding. Neutralizing the warrants would cost roughly $1.33B.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV of 40.6% sits below one-year realized volatility of 48.6% (−7.9pp) at the 15th percentile of the current chain, and ATM IV collapsed from 56.7% to 40.6% while the price rose 33.4% — post-event vol crush. Put/call open interest fell from 1.37 to 0.40, so protection was unwound as the price rose; near-term skew is described as balanced (0.8%). The six-month tenor at 42.3% prices a ±21.5% move and spans both the September Düsseldorf decision and the Q4 Lenovo hearing. For holders, optionality is cheap relative to how this stock actually trades, which favors keeping exposure with defined risk into the arbitration calendar rather than adding naked equity. Short interest of 12.9% of float with 7.2 days to cover adds squeeze potential on favorable rulings, and adds downside velocity if the Samsung challenge advances.

**Risk factors score: 4.6.** Multiple unpriced binaries with asymmetric outcomes, high counterparty concentration, and a dilution instrument most screens ignore. Held above 4.0 because the arbitration-vacatur bar is high, several binaries are two-sided with upward bias, and net cash of $28.4/share plus a 63% FCF-margin base limits the fundamental floor.

## Management Quality

Leadership is stable through the escalation — CEO Liren Chen and CFO Richard Brezski signed the Q2 10-Q, and there were no Item 5.02 officer/director change filings in 2026. Continuity matters more here than in most companies because the enforcement strategy is bespoke and dependent on a small senior team. The operating record is strong: Adjusted EBITDA compounded from $208.0M (2021) to $588.9M (2025); the dividend was raised to $0.70/quarter, +17% year over year.

Three items hold the score at 6.0. The incentive plan is anchored on absolute Adjusted EBITDA and Revenue, neither of which is per-share, at a company facing 13.6% ownership dilution — though the alternative reading, that revenue and EBITDA are the cleanest metrics for a business where per-share figures are dominated by catch-up timing and convertible mechanics, is plausible, and the company discloses the dilution sensitivity table prominently. Buybacks of $31.1M in H1 ran below RSU tax withholding of $55.7M, with only $96.1M of authorization left; management justified this explicitly as maintaining balance-sheet strength for enforcement actions against very large companies and settling $380M of convertible principal in cash by June 2027, which is a defensible trade-off. And CEO compensation of $7.85M was large enough that its non-deductible portion moved the Q2 effective tax rate roughly 440bp (18.8% versus 14.4%). The FY2024–25 "compensation actually paid" figures of $88.8M and $57.2M are SEC mark-to-market constructs reflecting share appreciation, not cash, and should not be read as extraction.

Insider activity from June through August 2026 was sales only, but small and largely mechanical: CTO Pankaj sold 4,500 shares for ~$916K while retaining 66,516; four directors sold 365–522 shares each on June 12 two days after receiving 906-share annual grants. No Section 16 officer adopted or modified a 10b5-1 plan in Q2. The absence of open-market buying across a $260–$350 range reflects the absence of a bullish signal rather than the presence of a bearish one.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($460, +40%).** Streaming becomes a real program: a second and third large SVOD/AVOD license follow Amazon at comparable scale, the Disney injunctions force a settlement (the September Düsseldorf decision is the next pressure point), and the Amazon arbitration trues up above the conservative estimate as Samsung's did. The Lenovo award lands at or above the embedded estimate with substantial catch-up from the January 2024 effective date. ARR tracks past the 11% CAGR needed for the $1B 2030 target, and 8 of 10 top smartphone vendors renew at Samsung-like rate uplifts. On roughly $800M of ARR by 2028 with mid-50s recurring EBITDA margins plus normal catch-up, warrant-adjusted EV around 20x supports the low end of the $425–488 analyst range.

**Bear case ($225, −31%).** The Samsung ICC challenge succeeds in reopening or reducing the award, removing a fifth of ARR and the precedent that arbitration delivers upward rate resets. Disney survives dismissal and proceeds to discovery on monopolization; the Shenzhen court accepts jurisdiction to set global HEVC rates for televisions; the UK Supreme Court's Tesla reversal produces a ruling that pool licenses must be FRAND, compressing the Avanci channel. Enforcement costs stay near 10% of revenue with no offsetting deal flow, catch-up continues its $460M → $277M → ~$190M decline, and the market re-rates the stock toward recurring earnings power on 29.85M economic shares.

**Base case ($355, +8%).** ARR grinds toward $700M by end-2027 on continued mid-sized IoT/CE deals plus one additional streaming license; the Samsung challenge fails on jurisdiction or merits; Lenovo lands roughly in line with the embedded estimate; Disney settles or drags. FY2027 non-GAAP earnings approximate consensus of ~$400M in aggregate, or $13.42 per economic share, at 26x — consistent with 20–21x warrant-adjusted EV/EBITDA. Probability-weighted at 30/45/25 across bull/base/bear, expected return is roughly +8%, approximately fair compensation for the risk profile.

**Score trajectory.** Prior reports ran 7.0 → 6.9 → 6.8 → 6.8 with the last two at BUY. The move to 6.1 and HOLD is driven by valuation (5.4) and risk (4.6), not by growth or competitive deterioration — both of those held roughly flat, and the Q2 print confirmed the operating thesis those BUYs rested on. What changed is that the stock rose 27% while 2027 consensus rose 3.1%, and the litigation surface widened (TCL Shenzhen in July, the UK Supreme Court reversal in July, the Samsung jurisdictional filing in July). A price move of that magnitude on unchanged out-year estimates is a legitimate reason to lower a valuation score; it is not a reason to lower the business assessment, and I have not.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** Existing holders have a positive 12-month path with dated catalysts; new capital lacks an adequate margin of safety at $328.41.

- **Base case target: $355** (+8.1%). ~26x FY2027 consensus non-GAAP earnings of ~$400M on 29.85M economic shares ($13.42/share), equivalent to ~20.6x warrant-adjusted EV/EBITDA on ~$480M.
- **Bull case: $460** (+40.1%). Streaming scales with a second large license; Lenovo and Amazon arbitrations resolve favorably; ~20x warrant-adjusted EV/EBITDA on ~$650M.
- **Bear case: $225** (−31.5%). Samsung award reopened or reduced, one adverse antitrust/global-rate outcome, multiple compression toward recurring earnings power. Note net cash of $28.4/share and the 52-week low of $249.14 as reference points.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the September 2026 Düsseldorf Disney decision, the Q3 print in late October, and the Q4 2026 Lenovo arbitration hearing.
- **Note on price basis:** the extended-hours print of $345.88 is 5.3% above the official close used here. If that level holds, upside to base narrows to roughly 3% and the case weakens further.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price back toward **$270–285** (≈20–21x FY2027 economic EPS) with ARR growth intact — that restores the asymmetry that justified the prior BUY calls.
2. **Samsung's ICC challenge dismissed** (jurisdiction or merits) *and* the Samsung TV agreement confirmed renewed, removing the largest single-counterparty overhang.
3. **Lenovo award disclosed at or above the embedded estimate with catch-up ≥$100M**, plus a Disney settlement or a third UPC injunction that forces one.
4. A **second streaming license of ≥$50M ARR** announced, converting the Amazon deal from a single validation into a program.
5. **Repurchase authorization expanded to ≥$500M and executed**, demonstrating that per-share economics are being defended against the warrant dilution.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. The Samsung award is **vacated, modified downward, or reopened for re-determination** — a direct hit to roughly a fifth of ARR and to the arbitration-uplift precedent.
2. **Disney's motion to dismiss is denied** and the monopolization claim proceeds to discovery, particularly if the DOJ Statement of Interest is shown to support implementer theories.
3. The **Shenzhen court accepts jurisdiction to set global HEVC rates**, or a UK ruling holds Avanci pool licenses must be FRAND.
4. A **write-down of the non-current contract asset** or an Amazon arbitration outcome below the recognized estimate.
5. **ARR growth decelerating below ~8%** for two consecutive quarters, or enforcement costs exceeding 12% of revenue without corresponding new agreements.

**Reassessment:** Q3 2026 earnings (late October), with interim checks on the September Düsseldorf decision and any 8-K disclosing the Lenovo award or a Samsung ICC ruling.

## Conclusion

InterDigital has done what the prior bullish calls required. The Amazon license converted the streaming program from a theory into $110M of revenue and unwound multiple simultaneous legal fronts; ARR is compounding at 13.1%; FY2026 guidance was raised by $85M at the midpoint; the balance sheet carries $732M of net cash against a business with 63% free-cash-flow margins in a normal year. Those are the facts that support ownership.

The facts that argue against adding at $328.41 are equally concrete. On the economic share count including 4.05M warrant shares, the stock trades near 24x FY2027 consensus earnings that consensus expects to be flat, and warrant-adjusted enterprise value is 18.2x guided EBITDA and roughly 28x recurring EBITDA excluding catch-up. The largest single revenue relationship is defending an arbitration award it lost. The largest new revenue relationship has no price and sits on the balance sheet as a $143M unbilled receivable. Three competition-law actions and one Chinese global-rate-setting request target the licensing model rather than individual patents. Buybacks are running below RSU tax withholding against 13.6% pending ownership dilution.

Probability-weighted expected return of roughly +8% against a −31% bear case is approximately fair, not compelling. HOLD, with a clear path back to a stronger rating: a retreat toward $270–285, a Samsung resolution, or a second streaming license of scale would each restore the asymmetry.
