## Executive Summary

Sterling Infrastructure has converted a decade-long strategy of exiting low-margin heavy-highway work into a genuine earnings inflection. Low-bid heavy highway fell from roughly 79% of revenue in 2016 to 9% at December 31, 2025; consolidated gross margin moved from 15.5% in 2022 to 23.0% in FY2025 and 23.5% in Q1 2026; operating margin reached 16.7%. Q1 2026 revenue grew 92% to $825.7M, and the growth was mostly organic — E-Infrastructure revenue of $597.7M included $156.1M from the CEC acquisition, implying roughly $441.6M organic against $218.3M a year earlier, or about +102%. Signed backlog stood at $3.80B with $1.36B of unsigned awards, a combined $5.15B, on Q1 book-to-burn of 2.1x signed and 3.5x combined. The balance sheet carries $511.9M cash against $289.1M of funded debt — positive net cash of $222.8M — with an undrawn $150M revolver and full covenant compliance.

The stock is the volatile part of this story. It traded at $305 in January (the company's own buyback average), $888 on June 25 (a verified insider sale price), a quoted 52-week high of $1,005.68, and $604.94 now — roughly 40% below the high. The late-July move was violent and appears positioning-driven rather than news-driven: the shares fell 22% between July 27 and July 29 on heavy volume, then recovered 21% over July 30-31, with no company disclosure in between and Q2 results still pending. That drawdown has done real work on the multiple. Consensus adjusted EPS for the current year has been raised 40.5% in 90 days to $19.10 and next year 58.4% to $25.26, so at $604.94 the shares trade at 31.7x FY2026 and 23.9x FY2027 consensus, a PEG near 1.0 on the implied 32% out-year growth. That is not a demanding multiple for the growth rate.

We remain at HOLD, our fifth consecutive, but for a different reason than before: the valuation objection has largely been answered by price, and what remains is timing and earnings quality. Three specific items keep us from upgrading today. First, Q2 results are released hours after this cutoff with an implied move of ±22.4% and near-term implied volatility at 123.8% against 83.6% realized — buying a binary at 90th-percentile option pricing without an informational edge is a poor use of risk. Second, $55.8M of Q1 revenue came from favorable revisions to estimates on prior-period performance obligations, against $194.3M of quarterly gross profit; the same cost-to-cost mechanism runs in reverse, and average project size and duration are rising. Third, the forward-looking governance signals are unhelpful: the CEO sold 150,000 shares for ~$66.1M across five weeks in March-April, cutting his reported position roughly 42%, the General Counsel sold at $888 in June, and $362.1M of a $400M repurchase authorization sat unused while the stock fell 40%.

## Company Overview

Sterling is a decentralized holding company for U.S. heavy-civil and specialty contracting subsidiaries, headquartered in The Woodlands, Texas, with ~4,400 employees (~4% unionized). Three segments:

**E-Infrastructure Solutions** — 59% of FY2025 revenue and 72% of Q1 2026 revenue. Large-scale site development (mass grading, earthwork, utilities, storm water) for data centers, semiconductor fabs, advanced manufacturing and distribution, plus, since the September 1, 2025 CEC Facilities Group acquisition, mission-critical electrical and mechanical contracting. Top four customers were 27% of segment revenue in 2025 (31% in 2024).

**Transportation Solutions** — 26% of FY2025 revenue. Highways, bridges, aviation, rail and ports for state DOTs and transit authorities, concentrated in Utah, Arizona, Colorado, Nevada, Texas and the Pacific Islands. Top four state DOTs were 58% of segment revenue in 2025, up from 47%.

**Building Solutions** — 15% of FY2025 revenue. Residential and commercial concrete foundations, concentrated in Dallas-Fort Worth, Houston, Phoenix and Oklahoma. Top four customers 45% of segment revenue, up from 36%.

Management targets what it calls the mid-level market — work too large for local contractors and too small for global engineering firms — and states in its own risk disclosure that softer end markets would draw both cohorts into that niche, pressuring bids and margins. The claimed differentiator is schedule assurance on large, time-sensitive projects, which is an execution-reputation moat rather than a contractual or technological one. Substantially all backlog is fixed-unit-price or lump-sum, so Sterling bears cost-overrun risk, and substantially all of it contains termination-for-convenience clauses.

An accounting structure matters for interpretation. Effective January 1, 2025, Sterling deconsolidated Road and Highway Builders (RHB, 50%-owned) after a December 31, 2024 operating-agreement amendment removed unilateral control over disposition. This moved $235.9M of revenue and roughly $426-491M of backlog off the reported statements, produced a $91.3M non-cash gain in Q4 2024, and now routes ~$16M/year of pre-tax income through a single "other operating income" line with no matching revenue. Consequences: FY2024-to-FY2025 GAAP comparisons are not like-for-like, and consolidated margin percentages are structurally flattered by income without revenue.

Two adjustments are required to read this table correctly. FY2024 net income includes the $91.3M pre-tax deconsolidation gain, and FY2024 includes $235.9M of RHB revenue absent from FY2025. On the company's adjusted basis, EPS went from $7.09 (2024) to $10.88 (2025), +53%, versus +13.4% GAAP-to-GAAP; and RHB-adjusted revenue grew 32.5%, not the reported 17.7%. The distortion reverses by quarter: Q4 2025 GAAP EPS of $2.81 fell 22.8% year over year purely because the gain landed in Q4 2024, while Q4 adjusted EPS rose 78%. Any screen comparing GAAP to GAAP across the 2024/2025 boundary mis-ranks this company in both directions. The defensible middle ground is GAAP with the one-time gain removed; management's adjusted figure also strips ~$24.2M of 2025 stock compensation (guided ~$34M for 2026) and all intangible amortization, so it flatters economics by roughly $1.50-$2.00 per share annually.

**Segment mechanics.** E-Infrastructure FY2025 revenue rose 58.8% to $1,466.8M with operating margin at 23.6% from 22.0%. Transportation operating margin nearly doubled to 12.1% from 6.5% on improved project mix — the clearest evidence the risk-reduction strategy monetizes. Building Solutions is the offset: revenue fell 6.3% in FY2025 with margin down to 10.2% from 13.2%, and in Q1 2026 margin halved again to 6.5% from 13.4% on residential affordability weakness. Management expects that demand to stay muted.

A derived point worth naming: E-Infrastructure Q1 segment operating margin of 22.4% includes CEC's $12.3M of operating income on $156.1M of revenue, a 7.9% margin. Backing that out implies the organic site-development business ran near 28.4% ((137.8 is consolidated; segment-level: ($133.9M − $12.3M) ÷ ($597.7M − $156.1M) ≈ 27.5%) — approximately 27.5% on the organic E-Infrastructure revenue base. That strengthens the organic story and simultaneously flags mix dilution — as CEC scales (already 28.8% of total assets), it drags segment margin toward the electrical/mechanical trade norm.

**Cash flow and leverage.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $440.0M against $77.3M of capex, giving $362.7M of free cash flow and a 14.6% FCF margin, with FCF/net income conversion at 125%. Operating cash flow fell from $497.1M in 2024 because working capital ("Contract Capital") released $185.5M in 2024 and consumed $54.2M in 2025, driven by the increased size and duration of E-Infrastructure projects. That is a growth-funded build, and it is the leading indicator to watch — bigger, longer jobs tie up more capital. Q1 2026 operating cash flow of $165.6M should not be annualized: it included $32.1M of non-recurring affiliate-receivable collection and effectively zero cash taxes against $31.8M of accrued current tax expense.

The screened debt-to-equity of 1.38x is total liabilities over equity, not funded leverage. Funded debt of $289.1M against $1,195.3M of equity is 0.24x, and net cash is +$222.8M. The liability stack is dominated by $695.6M of contract liabilities — customer billings ahead of revenue — which is supplier- and customer-funded working capital and structurally favorable for an over-time contractor. The Altman Z of 2.48 ("grey zone") is dragged down by a working-capital-to-assets component of 0.005 for the same reason; with 0.24x funded leverage, an undrawn revolver, covenant compliance at both 12/31/2025 and 3/31/2026, and total contractual debt obligations of $330.2M all due by June 2028, solvency is not a live question. Two genuine offsets: $105.4M of the cash sits at consolidated construction JVs and is not freely deployable, and the negative-working-capital tailwind reverses if growth stalls — Q1 alone recognized $312.7M of revenue out of the prior year-end contract-liability balance.

Goodwill of $584.8M and intangibles of $548.0M together equal 41% of assets and about 95% of book equity, leaving tangible equity near zero. FY2025 goodwill testing was qualitative only. CEC's purchase-price allocation remains preliminary through September 1, 2026.

## Growth Analysis

The forward evidence is dense and mostly corroborating. Signed backlog rose to $3.80B at March 31 from $3.01B at year-end (itself +78% year over year, +49% excluding CEC), unsigned awards jumped from $300.7M to $1.36B, and combined backlog reached $5.15B. E-Infrastructure remaining performance obligations alone went from $1.84B to $2.71B in one quarter. Management expects ~70% of March 31 RPOs to convert to revenue within twelve months. Definitional caution: Sterling's "backlog" equals ASC 606 remaining performance obligations, unsigned awards are apparent-low-bidder positions not yet executed, and more than $1B of future phases on current projects is excluded from both. Combined backlog of $5.15B is therefore neither contracted revenue nor total opportunity.

Backlog gross margin has been flat: 17.7%, 17.8%, 18.0%, 17.8% across the four quarters of 2025. Sterling is winning very large volume at a stable rather than expanding margin — consistent with capacity being bid up without irrational pricing, and an argument against assuming further margin expansion in the model.

Consensus revisions are the strongest single signal in the file. Over 90 days, current-quarter EPS went from $3.69 to $5.18 (+40.5%), next quarter $3.96 to $5.86 (+48.2%), current year $13.59 to $19.10 (+40.5%) and next year $15.95 to $25.26 (+58.4%). Revisions of that magnitude, combined with 92% revenue growth, historically precede further re-rating rather than exhaustion. The 30-day picture is more mixed: current quarter −1.9%, next quarter +0.3%, current year +0.9%, next year −6.6%. The FY2027 trim of 6.6% is the one genuine deceleration datapoint, and it is small relative to the 90-day gain. The aggregate "estimates falling" label attached to this data is driven entirely by that out-year cut and misrepresents the overall trend.

The February 25 guidance (revenue $3.05-3.20B, GAAP EPS $11.65-12.25, adjusted EPS $13.45-14.05, adjusted EBITDA $626-659M) is almost certainly superseded — Q1 revenue of $825.7M annualizes above the top of the revenue range from what is seasonally the weakest quarter. One tier-3 source cites a $3.75B full-year revenue midpoint (+50.6%), but we could not confirm from a filing whether that is company-raised guidance or consensus, and no revised EPS or EBITDA range is verifiable. Consensus at $19.10 sits 39% above the February adjusted midpoint, which is where the multiple debate actually resides.

Two structural offsets to the growth case. IIJA authorizes transportation funding only through federal fiscal 2026, with no reauthorization visibility; the segment's top-four-DOT concentration rose to 58%. And Building Solutions, 15% of FY2025 revenue, is contracting with margins halved, removing diversification precisely as E-Infrastructure concentration peaks.

## Competitive Landscape

The CEC acquisition ($561.6M total consideration: $442.9M cash, 285,000 shares at $278.53, and $39.2M of recorded earn-out fair value against an $80M maximum) moves Sterling from doing the earthwork to also doing the electrical and mechanical fit-out. That expands scope per project and moves Sterling into the competitive space occupied by EMCOR, Comfort Systems, IES Holdings and Limbach, all of which have re-rated on data-center exposure. It also increases the share of execution risk Sterling carries on each job. The share consideration was ~0.93% of pre-deal shares, so existing holders' ownership fell to roughly 99.1% of their prior stake — immaterial dilution.

Trajectory is favorable on the metrics that matter: organic E-Infrastructure revenue roughly doubled in Q1, organic segment margin near 27.5%, Transportation margin nearly doubled, low-bid exposure down to 9% of revenue, Texas heavy-highway downsizing completing in 2026. The moat is execution reputation and scale within a niche, which is real but not contractual — and management's own convergence risk disclosure describes exactly how it erodes if end demand softens.

One attribution point deserves precision. Sterling is a contractor paid by data-center owners, developers and general contractors. It names no customer in its filings and states no individual customer exceeded 10% of consolidated 2025 revenue. Hyperscaler capital raises fund Sterling's customers' capex; they are not payments to Sterling, and no linkage to any named hyperscaler is company-disclosed. The externally reported semiconductor-fab campus award is single-sourced, unconfirmed by any filing, with customer, value and phasing unknown — directionally consistent with the $1.06B one-quarter jump in unsigned awards, but not established as connected.

## Risk Assessment

**Concentration in one discretionary end market.** E-Infrastructure is 72% of Q1 revenue, driven by hyperscaler, colocation and semiconductor capital plans, and substantially all backlog contains termination-for-convenience clauses. The valuation assumes multi-year visibility; the contracts are cancellable at customer election. Diversification is decreasing, not increasing: segment mix shifted from 44% (2024) to 59% (2025) to 72% (Q1 2026), and direct customer concentration worsened in two of three segments (DOT top-four 47%→58%, Building top-four 36%→45%).

**Contract-estimate reversal.** Favorable revisions to prior-period performance obligations added $55.8M of revenue in Q1 2026 versus $30.5M in Q1 2025, against $194.3M of quarterly gross profit. Two readings are available: genuine outperformance against conservative initial bids, or an estimating framework that will mean-revert. The 10-K warns revisions could reverse previously recognized profit. Lump-sum work rose to 65% of Q1 revenue from 52%, and average project size and duration are increasing, both of which widen the variance. This is the highest-probability source of a future negative surprise and is invisible in standardized screens.

**Unresolved disclosures.** An 8-K filed July 8, 2026 carries Items 1.01 (material definitive agreement), 2.03 (creation of a direct financial obligation) and 7.01 — the combination most often indicates a new or amended credit facility, possibly financing an acquisition. Its contents are unread. Two Item 5.02 filings (May 21 and July 10, 2026) covering officer or director changes are also unread; the May 21 filing coincides with a 40,000-share CEO grant dated May 20. We make no claim about what any of these contain, but a new financial obligation disclosed three weeks before this price date is a material open item for the leverage picture.

**Impairment and controls.** $1.13B of goodwill and intangibles against $1.19B of equity, with CEC's allocation preliminary and customer relationships valued using a 13.5% discount rate and 10% attrition. Management's Q1 disclosure-controls assessment excluded CEC (28.8% of assets, 18.9% of revenue) — a permitted accommodation, but nearly a fifth of revenue has not been through Sterling's assessed control environment.

**Other.** IIJA authorization ends in federal fiscal 2026. The 10-K states reliance on immigrant labor and flags enforcement changes as a capacity constraint for a business scaling electrical trades at 100%+ organic growth. Roughly $375M of work remains on unconsolidated JVs, of which ~$150M is Sterling's proportionate share, but Sterling is jointly and severally liable for the whole. Bonding capacity depends on capitalization and the surety market, with contract proceeds and receivables pledged.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 123.8% against 83.6% one-year realized — a 40-point spread at the 90th percentile of the current chain — and rose 11.6 points in a single session into the print. Term structure is in backwardation (123.8% at 17 days versus 89.7% at 136 days), the standard signature of a dated binary event. The August 21 expiry prices a ±22.4% move; the December expiry prices ±44.6%. Put/call skew is described as balanced at −1.8% near-term, and put/call volume of 0.58 is call-tilted while open interest at 0.92 is neutral, so the market is pricing a large move without a clear directional lean. The September 18 tenor shows a put/call volume ratio of 1.50, the one place in the chain where put activity is elevated. Short interest is 4.8% of float at 1.4 days to cover — uncrowded, with no squeeze mechanics in either direction.

The takeaway: option pricing is expensive and non-directional, which argues against paying for exposure ahead of the release and in favor of letting the event resolve. It neither confirms nor contradicts the fundamental case.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($900, +48.8%).** Q2 confirms the Q1 run rate and management raises FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance toward the high teens or above, validating the $19.10 consensus and the $25.26 FY2027 figure. Combined backlog above $5B converts at ~70% within twelve months at a stable 17.8% backlog gross margin; E-Infrastructure organic growth stays above 40% as the semiconductor-fab and data-center pipeline builds; CEC cross-selling lifts scope per project. Positive net cash and $362.1M of unused repurchase authorization fund either buybacks at compressed prices or further vertical-integration M&A. At 35.6x FY2027 consensus — inside the range at which data-center-levered specialty contractors have traded this cycle — the shares reach $900.

**Bear case ($430, −28.9%).** Q2 misses the raised $5.18 consensus, or guidance disappoints, and the mechanism is contract-estimate normalization: the favorable prior-period revisions that supplied 29% of Q1 gross profit shrink or reverse as larger, longer projects mature. Consensus FY2027 is cut 20% toward $20, and the multiple compresses to ~21x on the recognition that this is a fixed-price contractor with cancellable backlog, 72% single-end-market exposure and near-zero tangible equity. Building Solutions stays impaired, the IIJA cliff hits Transportation bid activity in FY2027, and goodwill testing turns quantitative against $1.13B of intangibles and $1.19B of equity. That path produces $430 without requiring a demand collapse.

**Base case ($700, +15.7%).** Q2 beats a demanding bar modestly, guidance is formally raised to something near the current consensus, and the shares recover part of the July de-rating without returning to a 50x forward multiple. $700 is 27.7x FY2027 consensus of $25.26 and 36.6x FY2026 of $19.10 — a discount to Dycom's 44.3x trailing and a premium to Jacobs' 36.8x, appropriate for higher growth offset by fixed-price and concentration risk.

The distribution is roughly symmetric around the print, which is what the ±22.4% implied move already tells us. That symmetry, not a deteriorating thesis, is the reason for HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade to BUY on any one of:**
1. **Q2 print confirms and guidance is raised.** Adjusted EPS at or above $5.18 with FY2026 adjusted guidance at or above $18.50 and unsigned awards or signed backlog rising again. That would put the shares below 33x a company-confirmed FY2026 number with the FY2027 consensus intact, and we would add.
2. **Price at $500-520 with estimates intact.** That is 26-27x FY2026 and ~20x FY2027 consensus, a level at which the fixed-price and concentration discounts are adequately compensated. This is our preferred accumulation zone.
3. **Evidence the earnings quality concern is unfounded** — two consecutive quarters in which favorable prior-period estimate revisions fall below ~10% of gross profit while consolidated gross margin holds above 22%, plus resumption of buyback activity at prices below $650.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any one of:**
1. **Negative contract-estimate revisions in any quarter**, or a decline in consolidated gross margin below 20% with management attributing it to project reforecasts. This is the mechanism that would justify a permanently lower multiple.
2. **Combined backlog declining sequentially** with book-to-burn below 1.0x, or disclosure of a material termination-for-convenience on an E-Infrastructure project.
3. **The July 8 8-K resolving as material new leverage** — funded debt above ~1.5x adjusted EBITDA, or an acquisition at a multiple above ~12x EBITDA that pushes goodwill meaningfully above book equity.
4. FY2027 consensus cut more than 15% from $25.26, or IIJA lapsing without reauthorization alongside Transportation backlog decline.

**Reassessment timeline.** Immediate — tonight's Q2 release and the accompanying guidance are the decisive inputs, followed by the CEC measurement-period close on September 1, 2026 and the Q3 report in late October. We expect the earnings-quality question to be answerable within two quarters.

## Conclusion

Sterling's business is performing better than its reported GAAP figures suggest and better than the price action implies. Organic E-Infrastructure revenue roughly doubled in the March quarter at a segment margin near 27.5% excluding CEC, combined backlog is $5.15B on 3.5x combined book-to-burn, the balance sheet holds net cash of $222.8M with funded leverage at 0.24x, and consensus adjusted EPS has been marked up 40-58% over 90 days. The 40% drawdown from the June high compressed the FY2027 multiple from the mid-30s to 23.9x without any reduction in the underlying estimates, which is a genuinely better setup than existed earlier this year.

What holds us at HOLD is the combination of a dated binary and an unresolved earnings-quality question. The Q2 release lands within hours at 123.8% near-term implied volatility and a ±22.4% implied move, with consensus for the quarter already raised 40% in 90 days — the bar the company must clear has moved a long way, and option pricing offers no informational edge to exploit. Underneath that, 29% of Q1 gross profit came from favorable revisions to prior-period contract estimates in a business where average project size and duration are rising and 65% of revenue is now lump-sum. The forward-looking governance evidence does not help: the CEO reduced his reported position by roughly 42% in five weeks at $408-498, the General Counsel sold at $888, and $362.1M of repurchase authorization went unused while the stock fell 40%. None of that establishes a management view of value, and none of it contradicts the operating record, but it removes the confirmation a BUY at this multiple would want.

We would own Sterling at $500-520, or above current levels once Q2 confirms the run rate and guidance is formally raised. Overall score 6.75, up from 6.7, with the improvement located entirely in valuation as the multiple compressed and the deterioration confined to management-quality signals and the risk stack.
