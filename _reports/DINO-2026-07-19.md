**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

HF Sinclair is a mid-cap independent refiner that has delivered one of the sharpest moves in the energy complex over the past year, rising from a $42.16 low to $88.59, roughly +103%. The move is a crack-spread recovery story rather than a company-specific transformation. Refining margins across the group widened on tight regional product supply and strong distillate demand, and DINO's inland geography (Mid-Continent, Southwest, Rockies/Pacific Northwest) captured that tailwind through 2025 and into 2026. Earnings recovered from a trough $0.91 diluted EPS in 2024 to $3.08 in 2025, and consensus current-year EPS has been revised +84.9% over 90 days to $10.62, with a clean four-quarter beat record.

Two facts temper the enthusiasm. First, 2025 earnings quality is inflated by a discretionary regulatory windfall: roughly $485M of pre-tax improvement came from EPA small-refinery RIN exemptions, which are being litigated and are not guaranteed to recur. Strip that out and the recovery is materially thinner. Second, DINO is running with an interim CEO (Chairman Franklin Myers) and an acting CFO (Vivek Garg) after both the prior CEO and CFO took "voluntary leaves of absence" in February 2026 in the wake of a whistleblower complaint from the CFO about the CEO's "tone at the top" around 2025 disclosure processes, followed by an Audit Committee review. The company concluded controls were effective, but a simultaneous dual C-suite exit under an audit-driven cloud is a serious idiosyncratic risk that caps the multiple the market should be willing to pay.

At $88.59 the stock trades above the $80.00 consensus median target and above every analyst price target except the $95 high. On a cyclical variable (crack spreads) that has already run to elevated levels, and with per-share earnings power flattered by a litigated one-off, we see the recovery as substantially priced. We rate DINO HOLD (uncertain). The fundamental momentum is genuine, but the valuation now sits at a cyclical peak against a governance overhang, which produces a balanced rather than asymmetric setup.

## Company Overview

HF Sinclair is a downstream-focused energy company formed from the 2022 combination of HollyFrontier and Sinclair Oil, with the December 2023 internalization of its former MLP, Holly Energy Partners. It operates across five segments:

- **Refining** (core earnings driver): seven complex refineries totaling 678,000 BPSD of crude capacity across Kansas, Oklahoma, New Mexico, Utah, Wyoming, and Washington. These are coking/cracking-complex facilities capable of running discounted heavy/sour crudes, sited in inland niche markets where distance from Gulf Coast competitors provides a logistics moat. Refinery utilization improved to 89.1% in 2025, though the West region lagged at 80.7% on turnarounds.
- **Marketing**: licenses the Sinclair brand to 1,744 branded sites plus ~350 licensed locations, a captive, higher-margin channel for its own fuel. Management targets ~10% annual site growth and formed the Green Trail Fuels JV (50% non-operating) in February 2026.
- **Lubricants & Specialties**: Petro-Canada Lubricants (Group III base oils), Sonneborn (pharma white oils), and Red Giant Oil, with sales into 80+ countries. Bolted on Industrial Oils Unlimited in January 2026 ($38M).
- **Renewables**: three renewable diesel units (~378M gal/yr) plus a pre-treatment unit.
- **Midstream**: ~2.0M BPD of pipeline/terminal throughput, mostly captive support of its own refineries post-HEP internalization, plus third-party revenue and JV pipeline stakes.

The strategic logic is a diversified non-refining base (Lubricants, Marketing, Midstream) that dampens pure crack-spread cyclicality. In practice, the Refining segment still dominates earnings and drives the stock. DINO owns no upstream production and no company-operated retail, so it cannot offset weak refining margins with upstream or retail profits the way integrated peers (Valero, Marathon, Phillips 66) can.

## Financial Analysis

The trailing four years describe a textbook cyclical trough-and-recovery:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 38,205 | 31,964 | 28,580 | 26,869 |
| Operating income | 4,054 | 2,203 | 261 | 927 |
| Net income (to DINO) | 2,923 | 1,590 | 177 | 579 |
| EBITDA | — | 2,900 | 1,133 | 1,809 |
| Diluted EPS | — | $8.29 | $0.91 | $3.08 |
| Operating cash flow | — | 2,297 | 1,110 | 1,315 |

The 2025 recovery ran on a 47% jump in adjusted refinery gross margin ($10.43 → $15.37/bbl), driven by lower crude/feedstock costs and the EPA small-refinery RIN waivers. Margins remain structurally thin — 2025 net margin was 2.2% and operating margin roughly 3.4% — which is normal for refining but underscores how violently earnings swing with crack spreads. A $417M non-cash lower-of-cost-or-market inventory write-down depressed GAAP results in 2025.

The balance sheet is solid and provides genuine downside support. Altman Z-Score of 3.33 sits in the safe zone; current ratio is 1.94; debt/equity is 0.79. Stockholders' equity of $9.2B compares to total debt of roughly $2.8B. The company refinanced in 2025, issuing $1.9B of new notes at 5.5–6.25% and tendering older paper, and replaced its revolver with a new undrawn $2.0B facility maturing 2030. Liquidity is roughly $3.0B, with no refinancing cliff and no covenant stress.

Cash flow and capital allocation are disciplined. FCF was roughly $866M (about an 11.6% FCF yield on the prior lower market cap; less compelling at the current $16.0B). The 2026 capex guide of $775M ($650M sustaining including $325M turnarounds, $125M growth) is restrained. The company returned roughly $716M to shareholders in 2025 — $376M in dividends at $0.50/quarter and $340M in buybacks — with $459M of buyback authorization remaining. Basic shares fell from 202.6M (2022) to 186.5M (2025), aided by large privately negotiated repurchases from REH Advisors, the legacy Sinclair/Holding holder that continues to sell into company buybacks.

The critical caveat on financial quality is that the 2025 earnings anchoring the trailing multiple embed roughly $485M of RIN exemption benefit. RIN compliance cost was $475M in 2025, so the SRE waivers were essentially the difference between a modest recovery and the reported number. These waivers are litigated by both refiners and biofuel groups, and their recurrence is uncertain. This is the single hardest-to-model swing factor in the forward earnings picture.

## Growth Analysis

The forward estimate revision picture is the strongest element of the bull case and warrants explicit treatment:

| Period | Current Est. | 90d Ago | 90d Rev. |
|--------|-------------|---------|----------|
| Current Quarter | $4.14 | $2.07 | +100.4% |
| Next Quarter | $4.00 | $1.89 | +111.5% |
| Current Year | $10.62 | $5.74 | +84.9% |
| Next Year | $8.23 | $5.42 | +51.7% |

Estimates are rising sharply and the 30-day revisions (+16.3% current year, +31.2% next quarter) show the momentum is still accelerating. The four-quarter beat record is unblemished, with the March 2026 quarter beating a −$0.07 estimate at $0.69 actual and prior quarters beating by 35–161%.

The complication is what is driving the revisions. This is a crack-spread recovery, and crack spreads are mean-reverting. The revisions reflect margins that Morgan Stanley characterized as having "eased from a mid-May peak but still well above normal." The next-year estimate ($8.23) sits meaningfully below the current-year estimate ($10.62), which is the market's own acknowledgment that 2026's margin environment is not expected to persist. The estimate-revision signal is therefore powerful but points at a peak, not a durable step-change in earnings power. DINO's revision surge is a leveraged read on a cyclical commodity variable that the same analysts expect to normalize.

Structural growth avenues are modest. Marketing site growth (~10%/yr) and lubricants bolt-ons add incremental earnings but are small relative to refining swings. The Renewables segment has generated negative margin-less-opex in each year 2023–2025 (−$0.16/gal in 2025) and depends entirely on the 45Z Producer's Tax Credit (extended to 2029 under OBBBA) and volatile LCFS/RIN credit prices — capital deployed below cost of capital and a candidate for future impairment.

## Valuation Assessment

At $88.59, DINO trades at a 13.1x trailing P/E on $3.08 of 2025 EPS. On the forward $10.62 current-year estimate, the multiple compresses to roughly 8.3x, and on the $8.23 next-year estimate to roughly 10.8x. On its face this looks cheap, and the estimate-revision framework argues against anchoring to a trailing multiple at a trough.

The problem is that these are not trough earnings — they are near-peak earnings. When a cyclical is priced on peak-cycle earnings at a low headline multiple, the low multiple is the market correctly refusing to capitalize an unsustainable earnings level. The current-year estimate depends on crack spreads that consensus itself expects to fade and on the recurrence of a litigated RIN benefit. Applying a normalized mid-cycle earnings power — somewhere between the 2024 trough ($0.91) and the 2025/2026 peak — the stock does not screen cheap; it screens fairly-to-fully valued.

Peer context reinforces this. The refining group median sits at a 10.8x P/E (PBF), with several peers showing negative trailing earnings (CVI, IEP, WKC) reflecting how thin and volatile the group's economics are. DINO's forward multiple is broadly in line with the group once the cyclical position is accounted for; it is not carrying a discount that implies mispricing.

The stock also trades above the sell-side. The consensus median target is $80.00 and the mean $79.53, both below the $88.59 spot; only the $95 high target sits above the current price. Morgan Stanley's June upgrade to Overweight raised its PT to $78, which the stock has since exceeded. On a high-momentum stock trading below 30x forward, analyst targets are typically stale floors, and we would normally treat the stock's move past them as consensus catch-up. Here the calculus is different: the targets lag on a variable (crack spreads) that the analysts expect to normalize, and the stock has run ahead of the fundamental variable rather than the fundamental variable running ahead of the analysts. The move past the sell-side is more likely momentum overshoot than durable underpricing.

We assign a below-average valuation score. A compressed multiple on peak-cycle, windfall-flattered earnings is a value-trap warning signal for a commodity cyclical at the top of its margin range, not a green light.

## Competitive Landscape

DINO's competitive edge is geographic. Its inland refineries in the Rockies, Mid-Continent, and Southwest are insulated from Gulf Coast oversupply, and its complex configuration lets it run discounted heavy/sour crudes. The Sinclair-brand marketing channel provides captive, higher-margin fuel sales, and the internalized midstream network supports its own refineries at low cost. These are real, durable advantages within its niche.

The structural weakness is scale and integration. DINO competes against larger, more integrated players — Valero, Marathon, Phillips 66 — that own upstream production and company-operated retail, giving them profit pools to offset weak refining margins. DINO only licenses the Sinclair brand, which leaves it more exposed to pure crack-spread cyclicality than its integrated peers. The five-year cumulative TSR figure of 209 versus a peer group at 330 confirms that shareholders have underperformed refining competitors through the cycle; the geographic moat has not translated into peer-beating returns.

Long-term secular headwinds — EV adoption, CAFE standards, LCFS programs — pressure gasoline demand, though DINO's inland markets are more insulated and the current federal administration has rolled back several EV/GHG mandates. The trajectory is stable rather than improving, which supports a middling competitive-position score.

## Risk Assessment

**Governance / disclosure-control crisis (most important, company-specific).** In January 2026, the Audit Committee launched a review after CFO Atanas Atanasov raised concerns that CEO Tim Go's actions created an unfavorable "tone at the top" around 2025 disclosure processes. The Board separately developed concerns about Go's communications. Both the CEO (Feb 17) and CFO (Feb 24) took "voluntary leaves of absence," with separation agreements expected, and a new risk factor on disclosure/internal-control adequacy was added to the 10-K. The company concluded controls were effective and there was no tone-at-the-top impairment, but that conclusion is self-serving, and a simultaneous dual C-suite exit under an audit-driven cloud is a rare and serious event. Investors should monitor for restatement, SEC inquiry, and the terms and timing of permanent CEO/CFO hires. This risk is only partly priced.

**Commodity/crack-spread volatility.** The dominant structural risk. Earnings swing violently with refining margins, and LIFO/LCM inventory swings add non-cash volatility. The current environment is near a cyclical peak, so the asymmetric near-term risk is margin normalization, not further expansion.

**RIN/RFS regulatory dependence.** The SRE windfall is litigated by both refiners and biofuel groups. Non-renewal or a court clawback would remove a material chunk of 2025's earnings base and pressure 2026.

**Renewables drag and impairment risk.** Negative margin-less-opex every year 2023–2025; a candidate for future impairment if 45Z/LCFS credit economics deteriorate.

**Environmental/regulatory overhang.** A 2025 EPA/DOJ/New Mexico Consent Decree ($35M penalty + $30M capex at Navajo), a $189M environmental remediation accrual, and HF alkylation TSCA litigation risk at three refineries using hydrogen fluoride.

**Goodwill sensitivity.** Goodwill of $2.98B (~18% of assets) sits on a thin-margin, cyclical base. Management changed its goodwill testing date from July 1 to October 1 in 2025. No impairment was taken and units cleared by >10%, but sensitivity to crack-spread assumptions is high, and a downturn plus the governance cloud could pressure this.

**Related-party overhang.** REH Advisors continues to sell into company buybacks — a persistent supply of stock and a governance consideration.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 49.0% against 37.3% one-year historical vol — rich, with the spread of 11.7pp and an 80th-percentile reading indicating the market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. The term structure is in backwardation (49.0% near-term vs 42.1% long-term), signaling imminent stress being priced, consistent with the upcoming Q2 earnings release and the leadership-transition overhang. The put/call skew is balanced (2.1%), and put/call volume (0.12) and OI (0.03) ratios are low, indicating no directional put buildup; the market is not positioned defensively despite the run-up. The P/C OI ratio collapsed from 0.75 to 0.03 as the stock rose 36.8% since the prior snapshot, showing call-heavy positioning into the move. Unusual activity shows heavy volume in the $100 August call (10.6x vol/OI), consistent with momentum chasing rather than informed hedging. The rich IV means options-implied entry timing is unfavorable for buyers, and the backwardation flags a binary Q2 event risk.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (to ~$105–110).** Crack spreads stay elevated through 2026 on tight regional product supply and strong distillate demand; the SRE waivers recur or survive litigation; the current-year estimate and continued upward revisions prove conservative; disciplined buybacks compound the shrinking share count; and a credible permanent CEO/CFO is appointed, removing the governance discount. The four-quarter beat streak continues, and the market re-rates DINO toward the top of its cyclical range on peak earnings.

**Bear case (to ~$60–65).** Crack spreads normalize from the mid-2026 peak, as consensus next-year estimates already anticipate; the RIN windfall does not recur or is clawed back; a restatement or SEC inquiry emerges from the disclosure-control episode; goodwill impairment risk crystallizes on lower crack assumptions; and the stock reverts toward the sell-side consensus in the low-$80s and below as the peak-cycle multiple compresses on falling forward estimates. The $62 analyst low target frames the downside if margins roll and governance risk materializes.

The setup is symmetric. The genuine estimate-revision momentum and solid balance sheet are offset by peak-cycle margins, windfall-flattered earnings, a stock trading above consensus targets, and an unresolved governance overhang. There is no identifiable catalyst that makes the risk/reward asymmetric to the upside at the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The fundamental recovery is real but substantially priced, and the forward variable driving earnings (crack spreads) is near a cyclical peak that consensus expects to fade.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the mid-$60s to low-$70s (toward or below the $79.53 consensus mean and the interim CEO's $69.11 buy level), restoring asymmetry on the recovery thesis.
- Appointment of a credible permanent CEO and CFO with no restatement or SEC action, removing the governance discount.
- Evidence that crack spreads and RIN economics are structurally durable into 2027 rather than peaking, with next-year estimates revised upward toward current-year levels.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A restatement, SEC inquiry, or material adverse finding from the disclosure-control episode.
- Confirmation that the SRE/RIN waivers are non-recurring or clawed back, cutting forward earnings materially.
- Sequential quarters of falling estimate revisions coincident with crack-spread normalization, signaling the cyclical peak has passed with the stock still near highs.

**Reassessment timeline:** The Q2 2026 earnings release is the near-term reassessment point; the options market is pricing a ±12.4% move into it. Beyond that, monitor RIN/SRE litigation developments and the permanent CEO/CFO search.

## Conclusion

HF Sinclair has delivered a genuine cyclical recovery: 2025 EPS more than tripled off the 2024 trough, forward estimates have been revised up sharply over 90 days, and the balance sheet is in good shape with an Altman Z of 3.33, $3.0B of liquidity, and no refinancing cliff. The estimate-revision momentum is the kind of signal that normally argues for a constructive stance.

The offsetting reality is that this is a leveraged read on crack spreads at a cyclical peak, with 2025 earnings flattered by a litigated RIN windfall and a peak-earnings valuation that has carried the stock above the consensus median target and above every analyst PT except the $95 high. Layered on top is a serious, only-partly-priced governance overhang: an interim CEO and acting CFO following a whistleblower-driven dual C-suite exit under an Audit Committee cloud, against a five-year TSR (209) that has trailed the peer group (330). The score trajectory has improved from 5.0 to a peak of 6.2, with a recent BUY, but that call was made at $64.75; at $88.59 the same fundamentals now sit against a materially higher price, which converts an attractive recovery into a fully-valued one.

We rate DINO HOLD (uncertain). The business is sound and the recovery is real, but at a cyclical top with peak-earnings valuation, a litigated earnings prop, and an unresolved leadership crisis, the risk/reward is balanced. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-$70s or on resolution of the governance overhang alongside evidence of durable, rather than peaking, margins.
