**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Wynn Resorts operates four luxury integrated-resort properties (Wynn Palace and Wynn Macau in Macau, Wynn Las Vegas/Encore, and Encore Boston Harbor) plus a 40% equity stake in the first licensed casino resort in the United Arab Emirates. The business is a premium-brand franchise: 18 Forbes Five-Star awards in 2026, Las Vegas ADR of $547 well above Strip peers, and one of only six Macau concessions. Q1 2026 marked a clear operational re-acceleration: revenue rose 9.2% to $1.857B and net income attributable jumped 65.6% to $120M (diluted EPS $1.04), led by a 23% surge at Wynn Palace and +5.9% Las Vegas.

The stock trades against the quarter. At ~$94, WYNN sits roughly 30% below its 52-week high of $134.72 (reached December 2025) and about 20% below the consensus target low of $118. That drawdown is not noise. Consensus current-year EPS has fallen from $5.03 to $4.65 (-7.5%) over 90 days, next-year from $5.82 to $5.02 (-13.8%), and the company has missed EPS in each of the last four quarters, including a 27.9% miss in Q3 2025 and a 22.2% miss in Q4 2025. The Wynn Al Marjan Island (UAE) opening, the single biggest catalyst in the equity narrative, has slipped from a 2027 target to a "modest delay" with no firm date, and Wynn Macau (the peninsula property) saw VIP collapse and EBITDAR fall 16% in Q1 even as Wynn Palace boomed.

The result is a two-sided setup. The bull case rests on UAE optionality being underappreciated, continued Macau mass-market recovery, and a low-teens forward multiple on normalized earnings power. The bear case rests on falling estimates, $10.6B of debt against negative book equity, a softening Las Vegas backdrop, and concentrated geopolitical exposure to Macau. We rate WYNN a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the evidence does not cleanly support conviction in either direction, and the falling-estimate trend is a real headwind that a cheap headline multiple does not offset.

## Company Overview

Wynn designs, builds, and operates luxury integrated resorts combining five-star hotels, high-end retail, dining and entertainment, convention space, and casinos. The segment structure:

- **Macau (~52% of revenue)** — through ~72%-owned, Hong Kong-listed Wynn Macau, Limited (WML). Wynn Palace generated $2.31B of 2025 revenue ($683M Adjusted Property EBITDAR); Wynn Macau (peninsula) $1.41B ($402M EBITDAR). Operated under a 10-year concession running 2023–2032.
- **Las Vegas ($2.57B revenue, $902M EBITDAR)** — 100%-owned Wynn Las Vegas and Encore, with a 50.1% Retail Joint Venture. The single largest profit contributor.
- **Encore Boston Harbor ($847M revenue, $237M EBITDAR)** — Massachusetts Category 1 license.
- **Wynn Al Marjan Island (UAE)** — 40% equity JV in a ~$5.1B project, the first licensed casino resort in the UAE.

Casino revenue is ~62% of the total. Gaming taxes are the largest single cost driver (Macau 35% plus 5% levies; Massachusetts 25%). The "Wynn" name is licensed perpetually from Stephen Wynn under a surname-rights agreement.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Full-year 2025 revenue was essentially flat at $7.138B (+0.1% vs. $7.128B in 2024). Net income fell 34.7% to $327M (diluted EPS $3.14), but the decline was almost entirely tax- and interest-driven: a $101M higher tax provision (partly from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act reducing foreign-tax-credit utilization) and $64M lower interest income. Operating income was roughly stable at ~$1.12B. A $46.8M derivatives gain flattered the Q1 2026 result.

**Margins and cash flow.** Operating margin was 15.7%, net margin 5.7%. Operating cash flow was $1.35B in 2025 with free cash flow of roughly $692M (a 9.7% FCF margin). This is a cash-generative business at the operating level.

**Balance sheet — the central issue.** Total debt is ~$10.63B and stockholders' equity is negative (-$275M at year-end 2025; -$942M total deficit including non-controlling interest). This produces an Altman Z-Score of 0.92, nominally in the "distress zone." That reading requires heavy caution. The negative equity is manufactured by years of buybacks and dividends exceeding retained earnings (accumulated deficit -$1.45B), not by operational or liquidity stress. Supporting evidence: current ratio 1.63, all debt covenants met as of Q1 2026, ~82% of debt fixed-rate, ~$1.46B cash plus $602M investments, and ~$2.58B of undrawn revolver capacity. The Z-Score should not be read literally for a capital-return-heavy operator.

The leverage is nonetheless real and the refinancing calendar matters. The WML Convertible Bonds are redeemable at holder option in March 2027, and multiple maturities fall in 2027–2029. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, refinancing at a leveraged, cash-flow-concentrated operator carries genuine cost. The parent depends on distributions from subsidiaries — particularly the ~72%-owned Macau entity — to service holdco obligations, which adds a structural layer of dependency.

**Capital allocation.** The company repurchased ~$380M of stock in 2025 (share count down from ~113M in 2022 to ~104M), pays a $1.00/year dividend (~1.1% yield), and had ~$401M of repurchase authority remaining at Q1 2026. It is simultaneously funding over $1B of growth capex (UAE plus the new Enclave tower). Buying back stock while carrying negative equity and funding two large projects is aggressive; the discipline question is whether the buyback price ($100+ average in 2025) proves sensible against a stock now at $94.

The financial-health score of 4.5 reflects strong operating cash generation offset by the leverage, negative equity, and 2027 refinancing calendar. Financial health is the most reliable discriminator of stock outcomes in our framework, and here it flags a real, if not acute, structural vulnerability.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term momentum is mixed and estimates are falling.** Q1 2026 revenue growth is a genuine positive, but the earnings track record and revision trend cut the other way. WYNN has missed EPS four consecutive quarters (-2.3%, -22.2%, -27.9%, -11.4%). Consensus current-quarter EPS has been cut from $1.24 to $1.00 (-19.9%) over 90 days. This is negative earnings momentum, and it is the dominant near-term signal. Estimates are moving the wrong way, the mirror image of the strong-BUY inflection pattern.

**Growth pipeline.** Two projects define the multi-year growth story:

- **Wynn Al Marjan Island (UAE):** 40% equity economics on the ~$5.1B project. Life-to-date contributions of $1.01B with $350-450M remaining, plus a completion guarantee on the $2.4B Al Marjan facility (off-balance-sheet contingency). The delay cites supply-chain/shipping disruption tied to the Middle East conflict. This is the crux of the equity thesis and the largest single uncertainty. First-mover access to a brand-new, potentially very lucrative regulated market is a genuine differentiator, but a 40% co-investor without full control faces both execution and timing risk.
- **The Enclave at Wynn Palace:** a 432-key all-suite tower, $900-950M budget, construction beginning H2 2026, ~2.5-year build, opening ~2029. Signals continued reinvestment under the Macau concession.

**Sector backdrop.** Macau GGR recovered to ~$30.9B in 2025 (from $28.4B in 2024), with visitation +14.7% and mass-market/slots carrying growth as VIP structurally shrinks post-junket crackdown. Las Vegas is softer: Strip GGR flat at $8.8B, visitation down 7.5% to 38.5M, occupancy slipping to 83.2%. Wynn's premium positioning has held ADR, but the macro consumer picture — a probable H2 2026 US consumer cliff and mid-market discretionary softening — is a headwind for the Las Vegas segment.

The growth score of 5.5 balances real multi-year optionality (UAE, Enclave, Macau mass recovery) against falling near-term estimates and a soft Las Vegas consumer backdrop. Growth prospects have declined from 7.0 in February to 5.5 today in our history, and the current data confirms rather than reverses that trend, with the UAE delay and estimate cuts as the specific drivers.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$94 with trailing EPS of $3.14, the headline trailing P/E is ~30x, but that reflects the tax-depressed 2025 result and is not the right lens. On consensus forward EPS of $4.65 (current year) and $5.02 (next year), the forward multiple is ~20x and ~19x respectively. Against the Casinos & Gaming peer median P/E of 19.9, WYNN trades roughly in line, not at a discount.

The peer set is heterogeneous and largely uninformative: MGM at 67.9x, BYD at 3.8x, CHDN at 15.9x, CZR negative, RRR at 19.9x. The dispersion means peer medians carry little signal. More useful is the absolute setup. The stock sits ~30% below its 52-week high and ~20% below the consensus target low of $118 (mean/median $134, high $145). Analyst sentiment is uniformly constructive — 20 buy/strong-buy ratings, zero holds or sells in the current period.

The gap between the ~$94 price and the $118-145 target range would ordinarily flag mispricing. Two considerations temper that. First, the estimate-revision trend is negative and accelerating; when a stock is 30% below target while estimates fall 8-14%, the price is telling you the targets are stale on the downside, not that the stock is a bargain. Analyst targets are floors only when estimates are rising; here they are falling, and consensus targets are likely to be revised down toward the price, not the reverse. Second, our framework treats a compressed multiple as a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal by default; cheapness sorts the wrong way across our sample and the deficit has been widening. The Consumer Discretionary sector carries a structurally negative alpha in our BUY history, which raises the bar for rewarding apparent cheapness here.

On normalized earnings power, a case exists: if UAE opens and Macau mass recovers, mid-cycle EPS could exceed $6, on which ~15x would imply meaningfully higher value. But that is a 2028-2029 story contingent on UAE execution, not a valuation the current data supports today. We score valuation 5.5: the multiple is not stretched, but neither is it a clean asymmetric opportunity given the falling estimates and the absence of a near-term catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

Wynn's competitive position is a bright spot and the reason the equity is not a simple AVOID. The luxury brand is genuinely differentiated: 18 Forbes Five-Star awards, top-of-market ADR, in-house design and development capability, and one of only six Macau concessions. In Macau, Wynn competes at the premium end against SJM, Galaxy, Sands, Melco, and MGM; the mass-market and slots segments are carrying growth as the structurally shrinking VIP business normalizes post-junket crackdown. The Q1 2026 divergence between Wynn Palace (Cotai, mass-oriented) and Wynn Macau (peninsula, more VIP-exposed) is instructive. The portfolio is well-positioned for the mass-market shift, though the peninsula property is the soft spot.

The UAE first-mover position is the clearest structural advantage versus peers — a licensed casino in a brand-new, wealthy, regulated market with no incumbent competition. If it opens on reasonable economics, it is a durable moat extension.

Trajectory matters more than level in our framework. Wynn's competitive position has held up but is not clearly improving: Las Vegas visitation is falling, Macau VIP is structurally shrinking, and the UAE catalyst has slipped. We score competitive position 6.5 — a strong franchise with intact premium positioning, but with the growth vector (UAE) delayed and one Macau property weakening. This is down from 7.5 in our February history, reflecting the delay and the Las Vegas softening.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and negative equity.** The 2027 convertible-bond put and multiple 2027-2029 maturities fall in a higher-for-longer rate environment. The parent depends on subsidiary distributions to service holdco debt.

**Macau concentration and geopolitics.** Roughly half of revenue and the bulk of gaming exposure sits in Macau, dependent on PRC/HK/Taiwan visitation, PRC visa policy, anti-corruption campaigns, and currency controls. The concession can be rescinded without compensation for "endangerment to national security" and is redeemable by the government from year 8 (2030). The 12% Complementary Tax exemption on gaming profits expires end-2027, a known, dated earnings headwind that the Street may under-model.

**VIP volatility and credit risk.** 73.9% of markers are owed by non-US (mostly Asian) customers where gaming debt is difficult to enforce. Wynn Macau VIP win % fell to 0.39% in Q1 2026.

**UAE execution and co-investment risk.** As a 40% co-investor without control, plus a completion guarantee on the $2.4B facility (off-balance-sheet). The project is already delayed with no new date.

**AML/regulatory history.** A September 2024 non-prosecution agreement cost Wynn Las Vegas a $130M forfeiture.

**Consumer cliff.** A probable H2 2026 US consumer slowdown and mid-market discretionary softening pressures the Las Vegas segment.

The risk score of 4.5 reflects the combination of leverage, concentrated geopolitical exposure, the 2027 tax-exemption cliff, and the delayed UAE catalyst. These are material and specific, and several (the tax-exemption expiry, the UAE timing) are not fully reflected in a headline forward multiple.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 50.7% is rich versus 34.0% one-year historical volatility (16.7pp spread, 80th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (50.7% near-term vs. 40.7% long-term), pricing an imminent event, likely the upcoming earnings print. Put/call skew is elevated at 19.9%, indicating stronger demand for downside protection. Short interest is 10.3% of float (5.9 days to cover), meaningful institutional bearish positioning. The put/call volume ratio of 0.24 is low, and IV compressed 4.2pp intraday alongside a 2.3% price bounce, suggesting some near-term hedging unwound. The options market corroborates the cautious fundamental stance: elevated put demand and backwardation align with the falling-estimate, missed-earnings backdrop rather than contradicting it. This is a divergence worth respecting for anyone tempted by the "cheap vs. target" framing.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
Q1 2026 confirmed operational re-acceleration. Macau mass-market recovery continues, Wynn Palace momentum sustains, and Las Vegas ADR holds. UAE Al Marjan opens in 2027-2028 on reasonable economics, adding a differentiated new-market profit stream at 40% economics that the market currently underappreciates. Normalized mid-cycle EPS reaches $6+, on which ~18-20x supports a $120-135 valuation, matching the analyst target range. Estimates stabilize and revise upward as UAE nears, and the stock re-rates from ~$94 toward the consensus mean of $134. Bull case target: ~$135 (+43%).

### Bear Case
UAE delays extend further, pushing the primary catalyst into 2028-2029. Macau VIP stays weak, the end-2027 tax-exemption expiry compresses Macau margins, and a US consumer slowdown hits Las Vegas visitation and ADR. Estimates continue falling, the fifth consecutive miss arrives, and refinancing in 2027-2029 proves costly. The stock re-rates toward the 52-week low. Bear case target: ~$78 (-17%), roughly the January-2026 support and consistent with a 15x multiple on cut estimates.

### Base Case
Macau mass recovery offsets VIP softness and a modestly weaker Las Vegas; UAE opens in 2027-2028 with continued schedule risk; estimates stabilize near the current $4.65-5.02 range. The stock trades in a $90-110 band as the market waits for UAE clarity and estimate stabilization. Base case target: ~$105 (+11%), roughly 21x the current-year estimate.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The evidence is genuinely two-sided, and the falling-estimate trend prevents a confident BUY while the differentiated franchise and UAE optionality prevent an AVOID.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions turn positive — current-year consensus stabilizes or rises for two consecutive months, reversing the -7.5% 90-day trend.
- A firm UAE Al Marjan opening date within 2027 is confirmed, de-risking the primary catalyst.
- Macau mass-market GGR share gains sustain and Wynn Macau (peninsula) EBITDAR inflects positive, ending the two-property divergence.
- A pullback toward the low-$80s that offers a genuinely asymmetric entry on normalized earnings power.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A fifth consecutive EPS miss with further downward guidance.
- UAE delayed again beyond 2028, or the completion guarantee triggers a material off-balance-sheet cash call.
- Refinancing difficulty on the 2027 convertible put or 2027-2029 maturities at materially higher cost.
- Las Vegas segment revenue turning negative YoY as the consumer cliff arrives.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (early August 2026) is the natural checkpoint, particularly for estimate direction and UAE commentary. The March 2027 convertible-bond put and the end-2027 Macau tax-exemption expiry are the two dated structural events to monitor over the medium term.

## Conclusion

Wynn Resorts is a high-quality luxury franchise with real UAE optionality trading at a reasonable forward multiple after a ~30% drawdown from its high. On paper, a ~20x forward P/E and a price ~20% below the analyst target floor look like a mispricing. In practice, the drawdown reflects four consecutive earnings misses, an accelerating downward estimate revision, a delayed UAE catalyst, and structural leverage on negative book equity. The options market corroborates the caution with elevated put skew and backwardated IV ahead of the next print.

This is a HOLD, not a BUY. The franchise quality and the UAE story keep it well above AVOID territory, but the falling-estimate trend, the Consumer Discretionary sector's structurally weak selection alpha, and the leverage profile mean a cheap headline multiple is not a sufficient reason to add. The path to a BUY runs through estimate stabilization and a firm UAE date; the path to an AVOID runs through a fifth miss or a further UAE slip. Until one of those resolves, the risk/reward is balanced. Our overall score of 5.25 reflects a modestly improved setup versus the April-May lows (the Q1 re-acceleration and price decline improve risk/reward at the margin) but confirms rather than reverses the multi-quarter decline in our growth and competitive-position assessments.
