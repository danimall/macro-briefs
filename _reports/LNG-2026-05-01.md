## Executive Summary

Cheniere Energy is the largest US LNG producer and exporter, operating two world-class liquefaction facilities with combined capacity exceeding 60 mtpa. The company sits at the intersection of three structural forces: the Iran conflict has removed 15-20% of global LNG supply (primarily Qatari), European regulatory action is banning Russian LNG by 2027, and Cheniere's own Corpus Christi Stage 3 expansion is adding ~9 mtpa of incremental capacity through 2026. The company is running at maximum utilization during the tightest global LNG market in decades, with contracted revenues of $290.6B providing extraordinary cash flow visibility.

At ~11x trailing and forward earnings with a $10B buyback authorization (17.7% of market cap over 5 years), the valuation remains reasonable given the growth trajectory. EPS estimates have been revised sharply higher: current-year consensus is up 11.2% over 90 days, and next-year estimates are up 23.1%. The stock has rallied from $186 to $275 since December lows, reflecting the geopolitical premium, but consensus targets of $303-$308 suggest 10-15% additional upside. The primary risks are concentrated in $23B of debt, derivative-driven earnings opacity, and the tail probability that diplomatic resolution normalizes LNG markets.

The score trajectory from 7.0 to 7.2 over the past month reflected improving competitive positioning and risk assessment. The current analysis maintains the BUY rating while modestly downgrading Management Quality (insider selling near highs) and keeping Risk Factors at a cautious level given extreme options market stress signals.

## Company Overview

Cheniere operates the Sabine Pass LNG Terminal in Louisiana and the Corpus Christi LNG Terminal in Texas, with combined nameplate liquefaction capacity exceeding 30 mtpa at each facility when all trains are operational. The company has loaded and exported over 4,610 cumulative cargoes totaling 315 million tonnes to more than 40 countries.

The business model functions as an infrastructure toll with commodity upside. Under long-term Sale and Purchase Agreements (SPAs), customers pay a fixed fee per MMBtu (inflation-adjusted annually) plus a variable fee typically equal to 115% of Henry Hub. The fixed fee represents near-guaranteed liquefaction margin regardless of commodity price movements, and customers must pay even if they cancel cargoes. Approximately 90% of total anticipated production is contracted through the mid-2030s.

The growth pipeline is substantial. Corpus Christi Stage 3 (94.1% complete as of year-end 2025, with Trains 1-4 substantially complete and Train 5 producing first LNG in February 2026) adds ~9 mtpa. Midscale Trains 8 & 9 (FID taken June 2025, 31.8% complete, targeting 2H 2028 completion) add further capacity. Two additional expansion projects at SPL and CCL are in FERC permitting and would roughly double current capacity if fully developed.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** FY2025 revenue was $20.0B, up 27.2% from FY2024's $15.7B. Operating income reached $9.1B (+48.7%), and net income attributable to Cheniere was $5.3B (+63.9%), translating to $24.13 diluted EPS. The revenue increase was driven by $2.9B from higher Henry Hub pricing, $1.2B from higher LNG volumes as Stage 3 trains ramped, and $417M in net derivative gains.

A critical analytical distinction: GAAP earnings include $3.6B in pre-tax derivative gains (primarily mark-to-market adjustments on IPM agreements). The fair value of Level 3 liquefaction supply derivatives swung by $3.7B in a single year. Adjusted EBITDA of $6.9B (up from $6.2B in FY2024) is the more reliable measure of underlying economic performance.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt stands at $23.0B, producing a debt-to-equity ratio of 4.4x. The Altman Z-Score of 1.41 technically falls in the distress zone, but this mechanically misleads: the model cannot capture $290.6B of contracted take-or-pay revenues from investment-grade counterparties. S&P upgraded Cheniere and CQP to BBB+, and Fitch upgraded to BBB in 2025, confirming the credit markets' favorable assessment. Available liquidity is $8.76B ($1.1B cash + $485M restricted cash + $7.2B credit facilities). The weighted average interest rate on senior notes is 4.65%.

The current ratio of 0.94 (below 1.0) warrants monitoring but is typical for infrastructure companies with predictable cash flows and revolving credit access. Debt maturities are manageable: $0.3B in 2026, with $11.8B due 2027-2030.

**Cash Flow and Capital Allocation:** Operating cash flow was $5.5B in FY2025. Free cash flow of $2.46B (12.3% FCF margin) reflects heavy capex for Stage 3 and Trains 8 & 9. The company repurchased $2.7B of shares in FY2025 (12.1M shares at $221.55 average), reducing the share count from 251.1M (2022) to 219.7M. The February 2026 $10B buyback authorization through 2030 represents 17.7% of the current market cap. The quarterly dividend was increased 10%+ to $0.555 ($2.22 annualized), yielding ~0.8%.

**Earnings Quality Concern:** FCF ($2.46B) is less than half of net income ($5.3B), driven by the derivative gains. Investors should anchor to Adjusted EBITDA and operating cash flow rather than GAAP EPS for valuation purposes.

## Growth Analysis

Cheniere's growth comes from three identifiable sources with distinct timelines:

**Near-term (2026):** Corpus Christi Stage 3 Trains 6-7 completing through 1H-2H 2026, adding the remaining capacity of the project. Train 5 produced first LNG in February 2026. This is largely de-risked (94.1% engineering complete, fixed-price Bechtel EPC). Revenue impact: immediate upon substantial completion of each train, with contracted SPAs already in place.

**Medium-term (2028):** Midscale Trains 8 & 9, with FID taken and 31.8% complete. The $2.9B fixed-price EPC contract with Bechtel de-risks cost overruns. Targeting 2H 2028 substantial completion.

**Long-term (2030+):** SPL Expansion (~20 mtpa) and CCL Expansion (~24 mtpa) are in FERC permitting. If fully developed, these would roughly double Cheniere's current capacity. These projects depend on securing sufficient long-term SPAs and favorable regulatory/permitting outcomes.

Estimate revisions confirm the positive trajectory. Current-year EPS consensus has risen 11.2% over 90 days (from $14.31 to $15.92), and next-year estimates are up 23.1% (from $14.65 to $18.03). The current quarter estimate rose 34.8% over 90 days, from $3.48 to $4.69. The earnings surprise record is strong: 3 beats out of 4 quarters, with the two most recent beats at +171% and +60%. The single miss (Q1 2025, -42%) preceded the geopolitical catalyst.

The geopolitical backdrop amplifies all growth vectors. The Iran conflict removed 15-20% of global LNG supply via drone strikes on Qatar's Ras Laffan complex. Asian spot LNG prices surged 140%, European benchmarks rose 50-85%, and Europe's LNG demand hit a record 125 mtpa in 2025 (up 27% YoY). The European Parliament's vote to ban Russian LNG by 2027 creates a permanent structural demand floor for Atlantic Basin LNG. CEO Fusco stated at CERAWeek that Cheniere is "already operating at maximum capacity" as Asian customers request additional cargoes.

The medium-term risk is global LNG oversupply: management's own MD&A notes ~20 mtpa of new supply in 2025 alone, and the market is transitioning from tight conditions into rapid supply growth. If the Iran conflict resolves, this oversupply risk materializes faster. China's LNG imports declined 16% YoY in 2025, adding a demand-side concern.

## Valuation Assessment

At $269.40, Cheniere trades at approximately 11.4x trailing EPS ($24.13) and 11.4x forward P/E. Against FY2026E consensus of $15.92, the P/E expands to ~16.9x, and against FY2027E of $18.03, it's ~14.9x. These consensus figures likely understate the earnings power if elevated LNG premiums persist through the forecast period.

The consensus analyst price target is $303-$308 (mean/median), implying 12-14% upside. The range spans $259-$340. The current price sits 4% above the low target and 21% below the high target. Twenty-one of 23 covering analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy, with zero sells.

The $290.6B contracted revenue backlog ($107.7B fixed fees + $182.9B variable fees) deserves specific valuation treatment. The fixed-fee component alone represents ~$512 per share in future contracted revenue, providing a floor value substantially above zero. At a conservative 15% margin on fixed fees and discounted at 8%, the fixed-fee backlog alone is worth roughly $70-80 per share in present value — approximately 25-30% of the current stock price.

The $10B buyback program through 2030 mechanically supports the stock. At $270/share, this represents ~37M shares or ~17% of the current outstanding count. Combined with the 0.8% dividend yield, total shareholder yield approaches 4%+ annually before earnings growth.

Relative to peers in the energy infrastructure space, an 11x P/E for a company with 90%+ contracted capacity, 10-15% annual earnings growth from capacity additions, and direct exposure to the most favorable LNG market in a decade appears reasonably priced. The market is applying a modest geopolitical premium but has not fully priced the capacity additions through 2028.

## Competitive Landscape

Cheniere is the largest producer and exporter of LNG in the United States and the second-largest LNG operator globally. Barriers to entry are substantial: a greenfield LNG export terminal requires 5-7 years from permitting to first gas, $10-15B in capital, FERC and DOE approvals, long-term offtake agreements, and deep-water marine infrastructure.

The Iran conflict has specifically strengthened Cheniere's positioning. Qatar, the world's largest LNG exporter, has suffered direct damage to Ras Laffan. Australian LNG faces distance penalties to European markets. Russian LNG faces regulatory bans. Mozambique LNG projects remain delayed by insurgency. US Gulf Coast LNG — and Cheniere specifically — is the most reliable marginal supplier to both European and Asian markets.

Cheniere's cost advantage is structural. Henry Hub-linked gas procurement costs $2-4/MMBtu versus $8-15+ for international alternatives. The 115% Henry Hub pass-through in SPAs means Cheniere's liquefaction margin is largely insulated from commodity swings, while the current JKM-Henry Hub spread (exceeding $15/MMBtu) creates extraordinary profitability on uncontracted volumes and IPM agreements.

The competitive trajectory is improving: Stage 3 and Trains 8 & 9 increase capacity and spread fixed costs across more volumes. The FERC applications for SPL and CCL expansions position Cheniere for the next wave of global LNG demand. No competitor has Cheniere's combination of operational scale, expansion optionality, contracted backlog, and proximity to the cheapest gas feedstock globally.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage Risk:** The complex subsidiary structure (SPL, CQP, CCH each with independent capital structures and distribution covenants requiring 1.25x debt service coverage) limits financial flexibility. A major facility outage lasting 6+ months could create cascading covenant issues. Refinancing the $11.8B due 2027-2030 in a higher-rate environment could increase interest costs by $200-400M annually.

**Geopolitical Reversal:** Diplomatic resolution of the Iran conflict (estimated 5-8% probability) would crash LNG premiums and compress spot/short-term margins. With ~10% of volumes uncontracted and significant derivative positions tied to international gas benchmarks, a normalization of JKM/TTF to pre-conflict levels ($8-12/MMBtu versus current $20+) would reduce earnings by an estimated $3-5 per share. The contracted base provides a floor, but the stock's geopolitical premium (~$30-40 per share based on the December-March move) would unwind.

**Medium-Term Oversupply:** Management acknowledges ~20 mtpa of new global supply in 2025, with additional capacity from Qatar, Mozambique, and other US terminals through 2028. If the geopolitical premium fades while new supply arrives, LNG prices could overshoot to the downside, compressing margins on future contract negotiations and reducing the economics of expansion projects.

**Derivative Volatility:** The $3.6B in derivative gains in FY2025 used internal models with significant unobservable inputs. Management explicitly warns that "material change in estimated fair value could occur in the near future." A reversal of these mark-to-market gains could produce a GAAP earnings miss even if underlying operations perform well.

**Insider Selling:** CFO Davis, CCO Feygin, CLO Markowitz, and Director Botta collectively sold ~$28.5M of shares in late March 2026 at $291-$300. While this coincided with all-time highs and is typical profit-taking, the concentration and size of sales across multiple C-suite executives warrants caution. No insider purchases were observed.

**Section 301 Vessel Requirements:** Starting April 2029, 1% of US LNG exports must be on US-built vessels, increasing to 15% by 2047. Given near-zero US LNG shipbuilding capacity, compliance will require building or contracting vessels years in advance at materially higher costs than the global fleet. This could add $0.50-1.00/MMBtu to shipping costs.

**Tax Credit Investigation:** Seven senators are probing Cheniere's $370M alternative fuel excise tax credit. While the IRS approved it after multi-year review and the amount is immaterial relative to earnings (~$1.70/share pre-tax), a clawback would be a negative headline.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility of 68.6% for the nearest expiry (May 1) vastly exceeds 1-year historical volatility of 26.4% — a 42.2pp spread. The term structure is in steep backwardation (68.6% near-term versus 31.6% at 6 months), indicating the market expects an imminent catalyst. Put/call skew of 143.2% shows elevated put demand. The OI put/call ratio of 0.50 is moderate, suggesting the elevated near-term IV may be driven by concentrated hedging activity rather than broad bearish positioning. Given the stock's 19% appreciation over the trailing period, this options structure likely reflects institutional hedging against a sudden diplomatic resolution or conflict de-escalation rather than fundamental deterioration. The signal does not override the fundamental thesis but confirms that protection is expensive and the market recognizes the binary geopolitical risk.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($320-$340)

Stage 3 Trains 6-7 complete on schedule through 1H-2H 2026. The Iran conflict persists through 2026-2027, maintaining elevated LNG premiums ($20+ JKM). European Russian LNG ban implementation in 2027 permanently redirects ~15 mtpa of demand to Atlantic Basin suppliers. FY2027 EPS reaches $20-22 as capacity additions and elevated pricing converge. The buyback reduces the share count by 8-10% annually. Multiple re-rates to 16-17x forward earnings as the market recognizes contracted cash flow duration. Price target: $320-$340 (18-26% upside).

### Bear Case ($200-$215)

Diplomatic resolution normalizes Hormuz transit and Qatari LNG exports resume within 6 months. Global LNG spot prices revert to $8-12/MMBtu. China LNG demand continues declining (-16% trend). New global supply (~60 mtpa by 2028) creates structural oversupply. Derivative positions reverse, producing $2-3B in mark-to-market losses. FY2027 EPS compresses to $12-14. The stock de-rates to 15x trough earnings. Price target: $200-$215 (21-26% downside).

## Conclusion

Cheniere is the highest-quality way to express a long US LNG thesis. Maximum utilization during a structurally constrained global LNG market, near-term capacity additions from Stage 3, and the buyback program create a favorable setup at 11x forward earnings. The competitive moat is widening as Qatari supply disruption, Russian LNG bans, and Atlantic Basin proximity advantages reinforce Cheniere's position as the most reliable marginal LNG supplier globally.

The risks are real: leverage, derivative-driven earnings opacity, concentrated insider selling near highs, and the tail risk of geopolitical normalization. The options market's extreme backwardation confirms active pricing of binary geopolitical outcomes. These risks warrant standard position sizing (2-3%) rather than an oversized allocation.

Maintaining BUY with a $310 base case target, representing ~15% upside over 12 months. The estimate revision trajectory, near-unanimous analyst Buy ratings, and structural demand tailwinds support the thesis. The 5-8% probability of diplomatic resolution is the single largest risk to the position and should be monitored closely.
