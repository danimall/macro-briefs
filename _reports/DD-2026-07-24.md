**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

DuPont has finished its multi-year transformation from a diversified conglomerate into a focused specialty-materials company. The two defining events closed within five months of each other: the November 1, 2025 spin-off of the semiconductor and interconnect business as Qnity Electronics, and the April 1, 2026 sale of the Nomex/Kevlar aramids business to Arclin for $1.8B gross. What remains operates in two segments: Healthcare & Water Technologies (H&WT), the growth engine, and Diversified Industrials, a cyclical drag anchored to US construction. The Qnity separation delivered a $4.1B cash distribution that DuPont used to cut total debt from $7.2B to $3.2B, halving interest expense and funding a $2B buyback authorization while retaining investment-grade ratings (BBB+/Baa1, stable).

A note on the numbers is required upfront. The headline trailing P/E of 144x is an artifact of a GAAP net loss driven by a $768M aramids goodwill impairment in discontinued operations. The market-context price of $137.58 appears erroneous or stale: multiple primary sources indicate DD trades in the ~$40–50 range (the Q4 2025 ASR executed at an average of $40.89, insider Form 4 transactions in May–June 2026 priced at $45.54–$48.82, and a market cap of $18.6B against ~410M shares outstanding implies ~$45/share). A reverse stock split (1-for-2 to 1-for-4) was proposed on March 18, 2026 but had **not** yet been effected as of the Q1 10-Q (filed May 5, 2026). On continuing operations and consensus estimates, DuPont trades at roughly 20x current-year EPS ($6.88) and 18x next-year ($7.58), a discount to the specialty-chemicals peer median of 24x, not a premium. Any screen relying on the raw trailing multiple or on year-over-year revenue (which collapsed on the spin) will badly misjudge this company.

The investment case is balanced. H&WT is a legitimate secular grower (+9% sales, +15% EBITDA in FY2025) tilted toward medical packaging, biopharma components, and water purification. The balance sheet is materially cleaner. Against that, consensus EPS estimates are falling across every window (current quarter -4.9% over 30 days), Building Technologies remains soft, and the PFAS liability tail — roughly 11,900 growing AFFF personal-injury cases and a stalled New Jersey consent order — remains an unquantifiable binary that management explicitly says could exceed accruals. We maintain a HOLD.

## Company Overview

The "new DuPont" is roughly half the revenue base of the prior entity. FY2025 continuing-operations revenue was $6,849M. The company reports two segments:

**Healthcare & Water Technologies** (~47% of sales, $3,233M FY25): Tyvek medical packaging, medical device and biopharma components (built out via the Donatelle and Spectrum acquisitions), Tychem protective garments, and water purification — FilmTec reverse osmosis, Amberlite ion exchange, and ultrafiltration. This segment carries the higher margin (~30% EBITDA) and the secular tailwinds: aging demographics, biopharma growth, water reuse, and, ironically, PFAS-in-drinking-water standards that drive filtration demand.

**Diversified Industrials** (~53% of sales, $3,616M FY25): Building Technologies (Tyvek housewrap, Styrofoam insulation, Corian) and Industrial Technologies (adhesives, Vespel, Molykote, Cyrel printing plates), serving construction, aerospace, automotive, and packaging. This segment is where the residual cyclicality lives, primarily through US construction exposure.

About 53% of sales are international, with China/Hong Kong around 10%. Management's stated strategy is continued portfolio optimization, deliberately pivoting away from cyclical and commodity exposure (Mobility & Materials, Delrin, Aramids all divested; Electronics spun out) toward regulated, secular-growth end markets.

## Financial Analysis

Reading DuPont's financials requires separating continuing operations from the spin/divestiture noise. On a continuing basis:

- **Revenue:** FY2025 $6,849M vs $6,719M FY2024, up ~2% on +3% volume. Q1 2026 accelerated to $1,681M, +4% YoY (+2% organic, +2% FX).
- **Segment trajectory:** In FY2025, H&WT grew sales +9% and operating EBITDA +15% to $972M, led by medical packaging, biopharma, and industrial/municipal water. Diversified Industrials fell -3% on sales and -5% on EBITDA to $800M, dragged by construction weakness. Q1 2026 showed both segments positive: H&WT EBITDA +9% to $244M, Diversified Industrials +8% to $200M.
- **Margins:** Cost of sales improved to 64% of sales in Q1'26 from 66% a year earlier. Segment EBITDA margins run ~30% (H&WT) and ~23% (DI).
- **Reported vs. continuing profitability:** The FY2025 reported net loss of $779M is dominated by the aramids goodwill impairment in discontinued operations. Continuing-operations net income was roughly $88-98M for 2025, and Q1'26 continuing net income was $150M.
- **Balance sheet:** The single most important financial development. The $4.1B Qnity distribution funded ~$4.0B of senior-note repayment, cutting total debt from $7.2B (2024) to $3.2B (2025). Interest expense fell from $366M to $313M for the full year, and Q1 interest expense halved ($83M → $40M). Current ratio 2.1x; the company remains in compliance with its 0.60 debt/cap covenant and holds investment-grade ratings.
- **Cash flow:** FY2025 continuing operating cash flow was $560M against light capex (~$333M, ~5% of sales). Q1'26 operating cash flow jumped to $232M from $77M.
- **Balance-sheet caveat:** Goodwill ($7.9B) plus intangibles ($2.9B) equal roughly half of $21.5B total assets. The Altman Z of 2.52 sits in the grey zone, but this reflects depressed transition-year EBIT and the goodwill-heavy asset base rather than distress; liquidity and leverage are healthy.

The earnings-delivery record supports confidence in near-term numbers: three beats and one in-line result over the last four quarters, with Q1'26 EPS beating by 10.4%.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is bifurcated. H&WT is a genuine secular grower with a clear path: medical packaging and biopharma benefit from healthcare volume and reshoring, and water purification is supported by regulatory pressure (drinking-water standards) and reuse economics. The +9%/+15% sales/EBITDA split is the kind of organic-plus-mix profile that justifies a higher multiple on that segment in isolation. Bolt-on M&A (Sinochem RO for $56M, Donatelle for $365M) is being deployed to extend this segment specifically.

Diversified Industrials is the counterweight. Building Technologies is guided roughly flat for 2026 on soft US construction, and while aerospace and auto pockets are strong, the segment as a whole is a cyclical anchor that mutes the blended growth rate.

The tension shows in the estimate revisions, which are the most important forward signal here and are running the wrong way. Current-quarter consensus fell -4.9% over 30 days and -5.8% over 90 days; next-quarter -4.4%/-1.4%; current-year -3.9% over 30 days (flat over 90); next-year -3.4% over 30 days. This is broad negative earnings momentum, not a one-off. Blended organic growth of ~2% is modest, and the analyst community is trimming near-term expectations. This is the central reason the growth score sits in the middle of the range rather than higher: a real growth engine (H&WT) is being diluted by cyclicality and softening estimates. A BUY signal from estimate revisions is absent here — the revision velocity is negative, not the +30% acceleration that would flag a re-rating.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation picture is far more favorable than the raw data implies, but not compelling enough for a buy given negative revisions.

Correcting for the discontinued-operations noise, DuPont trades at approximately 20x current-year consensus EPS ($6.88) and 18x next-year ($7.58). Against the specialty-chemicals peer median P/E of 24.0x (IFF 24.0, RPM 20.7, with LYB and ALB showing negative/distressed multiples and SOLS at 69x), DD sits at a modest discount. The dividend yield is 1.7% per Phase 1 data (post-spin dividend reset to $0.20 quarterly), below the peer median of 2.1%.

The bull framing would argue that a business now tilted toward secular healthcare/water end markets, with a clean balance sheet and disciplined capital return, deserves to trade in line with or above the peer median. Analyst targets sit meaningfully above the current quote: low $153, mean $170 (rounded from $169.63), high $186 (per Phase 1 consensus target data). Against the current price of $137.52, that consensus target range is roughly 11-35% above spot.

Two things temper enthusiasm. First, estimate revisions are falling, and analyst targets tend to lag both directions; a stock with declining consensus estimates is more likely to see targets trimmed than raised, so the current target spread should be treated as optimistic rather than as a floor. Second, a cheap-looking multiple in the Materials sector has historically been an unreliable buy signal; discounted valuations in cyclical chemicals frequently reflect the market correctly pricing forward earnings compression rather than a genuine mispricing. The ~18-20x forward multiple is reasonable, not a screaming discount, and there is no dated catalyst that forces the gap to close. That keeps the valuation score in the middle.

This must be reconciled before per-share valuation is finalized.

## Competitive Landscape

DuPont's repositioning is the core of the competitive story. By divesting Mobility & Materials, Delrin, and Aramids, and spinning Electronics, management has concentrated the remaining portfolio in end markets with structural demand: medical packaging and biopharma (regulated, sticky, brand-anchored via Tyvek), and water purification (FilmTec, Amberlite). These are entrenched positions with formulation know-how and switching costs, particularly in regulated medical and municipal-water applications.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving in H&WT, which is gaining scale through bolt-on M&A and posting above-market growth, but we lack clear evidence of share gains against specific competitors, which caps the competitive score. In Diversified Industrials, the position is stable but exposed to construction cyclicality where DuPont competes on brand (Tyvek housewrap, Styrofoam) rather than on a structurally widening moat. The China SAMR antitrust investigation into Tyvek (announced April 2025, since suspended) is a reminder that the brand strength that underpins pricing also attracts regulatory scrutiny in a key ~10%-of-sales market.

The Letter Agreement resetting the Minimum EBITDA covenant to $1.4B constrains how aggressively DuPont can divest further without reassigning PFAS indemnities, a modest limit on future portfolio surgery.

## Risk Assessment

**PFAS liability is the dominant, unquantifiable overhang.** DuPont shares legacy PFAS exposure under a 2021 MOU with Chemours and Corteva (Chemours 50%; DuPont+Corteva 50%; $4B cap; DuPont's portion ~$1.4B, ~$730M paid through Q1'26). The active exposures:
- The August 2025 New Jersey settlement ($875M total over 25 years, MOU-shared; DuPont's NPV share $186M accrued) awaits a Judicial Consent Order. The NJ Court delayed approval and requested more detail on the use of proceeds; the next hearing is June 24, 2026.
- The AFFF MDL now covers roughly 11,900 personal-injury cases as of Q1'26 and is rising due to a South Carolina "channeling order." DuPont never manufactured AFFF and is largely named on a fraudulent-transfer theory, but litigation outcomes are unpredictable, and management explicitly states losses could exceed accruals with a range that cannot be estimated.
- Chemours reached a separate $450M federal/multistate PFAS settlement on June 24, 2026, and the NC Attorney General called it "an insult" and vowed to keep suing, indicating the shared-liability web remains active.

**Spin-off tax and indemnity risk.** The Qnity distribution and prior N&B/IFF and Dow/Corteva distributions relied on tax opinions and PLRs; an IRS reversal would create significant tax liability. Qnity assumed 44% of legacy liabilities (DuPont 56%), but DuPont bears the shortfall if Qnity cannot pay.

**Cyclicality and goodwill.** Building Technologies remains weak on US construction. Goodwill at ~37% of assets carries impairment risk, and the aramids unit was already fully impaired, evidence the risk is real rather than theoretical.

**Governance flags to monitor.** Item 5.02 officer/director-departure 8-Ks were filed January 20 and April 15, 2026, with identities not fully confirmed (possibly routine post-spin realignment, but worth verification). CEO Koch's FY2025 comp was $16.1M, and company TSR (169) trailed the peer group (190) over the measurement period.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 40.2% is rich relative to 30.7% one-year historical volatility (a 9.5-point spread), sitting at the 84th percentile within the current chain, with an implied move of ±8.8% on the nearest expiry. Put/call skew is balanced at the front (0.2%) but turns modestly negative in the 3-month (-3.4%) and 6-month (-4.8%) tenors, indicating some downside hedging demand further out, consistent with the June-and-beyond PFAS calendar. The put/call volume ratio of 0.10 and OI ratio of 0.47 do not signal aggressive bearish positioning. The IV collapse from a prior reading of 524% (which reflected the pre-split option chain around $47.71) to 40.2% is an artifact tied to the price/share-count discrepancy rather than a sentiment signal. The rich near-term IV suggests the market is pricing event uncertainty; a fundamental entry would be better made after the front-month vol resolves. Short interest is modest at 4.9% of float (4.2 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case:** H&WT compounds high-single-digit sales and low-double-digit EBITDA growth, the market re-rates the portfolio toward or above the 24x peer median on the secular healthcare/water tilt, and the PFAS overhang resolves within the accrued ~$1.4B MOU cap (June 24 consent order approved, AFFF cases settled without material excess). The deleveraged balance sheet funds continued buybacks and bolt-on M&A. Estimate revisions turn positive as construction stabilizes. This supports the analyst high target of $186.

**Bear Case:** AFFF case count keeps climbing and settlements exceed accruals, forcing incremental charges; the NJ consent order remains stalled or is renegotiated adversely; Building Technologies stays soft into 2027; and negative estimate revisions continue, compressing the forward multiple toward the low end of peers. A goodwill impairment on the ~$7.9B balance amplifies the derating.

**Base Case:** H&WT growth roughly offsets DI cyclicality for blended low-single-digit organic growth; EPS lands near the $6.88-7.58 consensus band; and the stock re-rates modestly toward the peer median as the balance-sheet improvement is recognized, while PFAS remains a manageable but recurring overhang. On the consensus estimate base, a modest re-rating toward the peer median maps roughly to the analyst mean target of $170.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: there is a plausible 12-month appreciation path driven by H&WT growth and a modest peer-relative re-rating, but negative estimate momentum and the PFAS binary prevent a stronger call.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions turn positive (current-year and next-year consensus rising over consecutive months), confirming the H&WT growth is outrunning the DI drag.
- The New Jersey consent order is approved and AFFF exposure is resolved or capped within the accrued ~$1.4B MOU portion, removing the binary overhang.
- The forward multiple compresses toward 16x or below on unchanged fundamentals, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- AFFF settlements or a court ruling force charges materially above the ~$1.4B accrued MOU share, or the NJ consent order is renegotiated adversely.
- A goodwill impairment in either operating segment, signaling operating deterioration beyond the transition noise.
- Two or more consecutive quarters of declining organic H&WT revenue, indicating the growth engine has stalled.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next earnings report and any update on the June 24, 2026 NJ hearing are the near-term checkpoints. The full thesis should be revisited within two quarters, when the trajectory of estimate revisions and PFAS developments will determine whether this resolves toward the bull or bear case.

## Conclusion

DuPont is a cleaner, better-capitalized company than the raw financial screens suggest. The breakup has left a healthcare/water-tilted specialty portfolio with a genuine growth engine in H&WT, a balance sheet cut roughly in half in leverage terms, and a valuation at ~18-20x forward EPS that is a modest discount to peers rather than the 144x trailing figure the headline data displays. Ed Breen's portfolio surgery and the disciplined capital return (debt paydown, $2B buyback) reflect sound capital allocation.

The reasons to wait rather than buy are concrete: consensus EPS estimates are falling across every window, Building Technologies remains a cyclical drag, and the PFAS liability tail is an unquantifiable binary that management itself says could exceed accruals, with the AFFF docket growing and the New Jersey consent order stalled. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the recent score trajectory has drifted lower (6.5 → 5.5) largely on management-quality and valuation resets tied to the transition. New data neither reverses that drift nor accelerates it; the deleveraging is a positive offset to the negative revisions and PFAS overhang, leaving the balance roughly where it has been. We maintain HOLD with modest 12-month upside in the base case, and would upgrade on a PFAS resolution combined with a turn in estimate revisions.
