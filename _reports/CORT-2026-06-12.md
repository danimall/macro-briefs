**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Corcept Therapeutics is a commercial-stage biopharmaceutical company built on a single scientific platform, selective modulation of cortisol at the glucocorticoid receptor, that has transitioned from a one-product story (Korlym for Cushing's syndrome) into a two-pillar franchise following the March 25, 2026 FDA approval of Lifyorli (relacorilant) in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer. The stock has done a full round trip and then some over the past twelve months: from $91 in October 2025, down to an intraday $28.66 after the December 30, 2025 Complete Response Letter on relacorilant in Cushing's, back up to $83-84 today on the ovarian-cancer approval, a guidance raise to $950M-$1.05B, and an NDA-resubmission announcement. The 16.9% move in the trailing week alone reflects momentum that has run ahead of fundamental confirmation.

The core tension is that Corcept is simultaneously absorbing a significant regulatory setback (the Cushing's CRL, which gates the larger piece of management's "$2B Cushing's by end of decade" ambition) while executing a genuinely strong oncology launch (>200 prescribers within ~36 days, NCCN preferred-regimen status 15 days post-approval, an overall-survival benefit of HR 0.65 that clears a higher bar than competitors' PFS endpoints). The reported earnings collapse, a Q1 2026 net loss of $31.8M versus +$20.5M a year prior, is deliberate launch spend rather than franchise deterioration, with underlying Korlym demand at record highs. The estimate-revision picture is constructive: next-year consensus EPS has been revised from $1.61 to $2.50 over 90 days (+55%), and current-year revenue guidance sits roughly 6.5% above consensus.

We rate CORT a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The business is improving, the catalyst calendar through year-end 2026 is dense, and the balance sheet (no debt, $515M cash) removes solvency risk. But at $83-84 the price already discounts both a successful Lifyorli ramp and an eventual relacorilant resubmission win, neither of which is yet proven on execution. Against that priced-in optimism sits an unresolved CRL with no firm timeline, a securities class action plus derivative suit, two antitrust actions heading to a March 2027 trial, an open DOJ/HIPAA investigation, and confirmed generic erosion of Korlym after the Federal Circuit affirmed Teva's non-infringement. The trailing P/E of 207x is not the right lens given trough earnings, but even on the $2.50 next-year EPS the stock trades at ~34x, which is fair-to-full for a company carrying this many binary overhangs. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-$60s or on de-risking of the Cushing's resubmission path.

## Company Overview

Corcept's entire business rests on selective glucocorticoid receptor modulation. The company has patented five structurally distinct chemical series comprising over 1,000 compounds and is pursuing applications across endocrinology, oncology, metabolic disease (MASH), and neurology (ALS). The commercial business now stands on two pillars:

**Hypercortisolism franchise (Korlym).** Korlym (mifepristone), approved since 2012, treats hyperglycemia secondary to endogenous Cushing's syndrome in type 2 diabetic or glucose-intolerant patients who have failed or cannot undergo surgery. This is the legacy cash engine, generating roughly $761M in 2025 revenue. Corcept launched its own authorized generic in June 2024 as a partial defense against third-party generics. The strategy here is less about head-to-head share than about expanding the diagnosed population: the CATALYST study found 23.8% of difficult-to-control type 2 diabetics have hypercortisolism, and MOMENTUM found 27.3% of resistant-hypertension patients do. CATALYST was referenced in the March 2026 AACE diabetes guidance, an early sign of clinical-practice penetration.

**Oncology (Lifyorli/relacorilant).** Approved March 25, 2026, 3.5 months ahead of its July 11 PDUFA date, in combination with nab-paclitaxel for platinum-resistant ovarian cancer in patients who received 1-3 prior regimens including bevacizumab. This is Corcept's first oncology approval. Lifyorli differentiates from Korlym by having no progesterone-receptor affinity (not an abortifacient, avoids endometrial effects), no hypokalemia (a leading cause of Korlym discontinuation at 44% incidence in the pivotal trial), and no QT prolongation. In ovarian cancer it is a biomarker-agnostic, all-comer therapy requiring no companion test, which distinguishes it from competitors targeting specific receptor subsets (Merck's KEYTRUDA/PD-L1, AbbVie's Elahere/folate-alpha, AstraZeneca/Daiichi's HER2 ADC).

The company is headquartered in the US, trades on NASDAQ, employs a workforce undisclosed in the data provided, and carries a market cap of approximately $9.0B at 107.4M shares outstanding.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory is intact and accelerating into guidance.** Revenue grew from $482M (2023) to $675M (2024) to $761M (2025), a +13% YoY clip in the most recent full year. Q1 2026 revenue was $164.9M, up 4.9% YoY, a deceleration that management attributes to specific, transitory factors rather than demand erosion. The decomposition: +12.4% volume offset by -6.7% average price (mix shift toward the lower-priced authorized generic, partially offset by an August 2025 price increase). Management raised 2026 guidance to $950M-$1.05B (~$1.0B midpoint), implying roughly 25-38% growth and sitting about 6.5% above consensus.

**The earnings collapse is deliberate launch spend.** Operating income fell from ~$137M (2024) to ~$45M (2025) as SG&A surged ~60% to ~$449M, then turned to an operating loss of $49.6M in Q1 2026 versus +$3.4M a year earlier. Q1 SG&A jumped to $145.4M from $90.7M, and R&D rose to $66.3M from $60.7M. The company swung to a net loss of $31.8M ($0.30/share) from +$20.5M net income. This is front-loaded commercial investment ahead of two launches, the Lifyorli oncology build-out plus endocrinology sales expansion to capitalize on the CATALYST/MOMENTUM screening data, not a deterioration of the core franchise. Korlym demand metrics hit record highs in the quarter; reported revenue lags due to Q1 reauthorization seasonality, new patients ramping to full dose over several quarters, and a prior-authorization backlog during the Curant pharmacy transition. The mechanism matters: if management's guidance is correct, revenue inflects upward through 2026 while spend normalizes, restoring operating leverage.

**The balance sheet removes solvency risk.** No debt. Cash and marketable securities of $515M at March 31, 2026, down modestly from $532M at year-end 2025. Q1 operating cash flow was -$16.8M (versus +$4.8M prior year), reflecting the loss, but the company states it can fund operations over the next twelve months and beyond without raising capital. Retained earnings stand at $611.6M, deferred tax assets at $181.6M, and the Q1 loss generated a $13.0M income tax benefit.

**Capital allocation has pivoted from buybacks to launch funding.** Of the $200M buyback authorized in January 2024, ~$189M has been executed with only $11.4M remaining as of March 31, 2026. The pace has slowed sharply as cash is redirected to commercial investment, a defensible choice given the launch window, though it removes a prior support for per-share metrics.

**Earnings-surprise track record is mixed and recently weak.** Over the last four quarters: 2 beats, 2 misses. The recent pattern is concerning: Q4 2025 missed by 34.6% ($0.20 actual vs $0.31 estimate) and Q1 2026 missed badly ($-0.30 vs $-0.02 estimate, driven by the launch-spend overshoot). This argues for some caution on the precision of near-term consensus even as the directional revisions trend higher.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has two distinct legs, and the estimate-revision data confirms the market is repricing both.

**Estimate revisions are strongly positive at the inflection horizon.** Next-quarter consensus EPS has been revised from $0.16 to $0.25 (+56% over 30 days, +60% over 90 days). Next-year EPS moved from $1.61 to $2.50 (+55% over 30 days, +30% over 90 days). Current-year EPS rose from $0.39 to $0.49 (+24% over 90 days). The combination of rising estimates and revenue guidance implying 25-38% growth typically precedes further consensus catch-up. The one soft spot is the current-quarter estimate, which fell 29.5% over 30 days (to $0.04 from $0.05), consistent with ongoing launch spend in the near term before the back-half inflection.

I weight this revision velocity as a genuine positive but stop short of treating it as a standalone BUY trigger. The signal is strongest when a stock is cheap relative to the improving estimates; here the stock has already moved 40%+ in a month and trades at ~34x the upgraded next-year number, so the multiple has substantially absorbed the revision. The revisions support a growth-hold, not a BUY.

**Leg 1 — Lifyorli in ovarian cancer.** The early launch metrics are strong: >200 prescribers across community, gyn-onc, and academic settings within ~36 days; NCCN preferred-regimen status 15 days post-approval; record new-patient starts in March and April. The clinical case rests on an overall-survival benefit (HR 0.65, median OS 16.0 vs 11.9 months), a higher evidentiary bar than the PFS endpoints many competitors rely on, and on biomarker-agnostic positioning that addresses the full PROC population rather than a receptor-defined subset. An EU MAA was submitted in October 2025 with a decision expected Q4 2026. Management's stated ambition is >$1B in US Lifyorli revenue by end of decade and market leadership "in a relatively short time frame."

**Leg 2 — Cushing's expansion, partially gated by the CRL.** The screening studies (CATALYST, MOMENTUM) are designed to grow the diagnosed population, and management targets the Cushing's franchise reaching $2B+ by end of decade. The larger share of that ambition depends on relacorilant being approved in Cushing's, which the FDA declined on December 30, 2025. Korlym alone, facing generic erosion, does not get the franchise to $2B.

**Pipeline optionality is large and largely unpriced.** A cluster of binary readouts lands by year-end 2026: BELLA Part A (relacorilant + nab-paclitaxel + bevacizumab in PROC), MONARCH Phase 2b in MASH (175 patients, fully enrolled), plus a Phase 3 ALS start. The DAZALS ALS Phase 2 missed its functional primary endpoint but showed an 84-87% reduction in risk of death at one and two years (p<0.001); scientifically intriguing but regulatorily unconventional, since death-prevention without slowing functional decline makes the Phase 3 design and FDA acceptance a watch item. Management claims positive BELLA-type data could expand relacorilant's addressable oncology population fivefold.

Growth Prospects score 7.5, consistent with the prior report. The trajectory has improved from 5.5 (March) on the back of a confirmed approval and a credible guidance raise, and the new data does not reverse that.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E of 207x is not a usable metric: it reflects trough earnings depressed by deliberate launch spend, and applying trailing multiples to a cyclical/inflection trough overstates expensiveness. The correct lens is forward and normalized earnings.

On the upgraded next-year consensus of $2.50, CORT trades at roughly 34x forward EPS. Against the pharmaceutical peer median P/E of 38.4x (ALKS 47.1x, APLS 38.4x, AMRX 37.1x, with ELAN and PTCT loss-making), this is not stretched in absolute terms; it sits at a discount to the median. But the peer set is heterogeneous and several comparables are themselves richly valued or unprofitable, so the median is a weak anchor. The more useful framing is the PEG: at ~34x forward on a business management guides to ~25-38% near-term revenue growth, the multiple is reasonable if and only if the growth materializes and the litigation/CRL overhangs resolve benignly.

The analyst target structure tells the story of a wide outcome distribution rather than a clear mispricing: mean $88, median $88, low $50, high $135, against a current price of $84.10. The stock is essentially trading at the consensus mean. The $50-$135 spread (a 2.7x range) reflects the binary character of the situation: the low case captures a failed Cushing's resubmission and accelerating Korlym erosion, the high case captures Lifyorli exceeding expectations plus pipeline wins. UBS upgraded to Buy with a $72 PT on May 13; the stock has since run past that target. On an inflection stock that would normally suggest targets lag reality, but here the run is steep enough (40%+ in a month) that I read it as the market pulling forward optimism rather than analysts being behind the curve.

The valuation does not offer asymmetric upside at $84. The price already discounts a successful Lifyorli ramp and an eventual relacorilant resubmission. For that reason I score Valuation 5.0: fair, not cheap, not egregiously expensive. The asymmetry would improve materially on a pullback toward the low-$60s (where the stock traded in late May before the latest leg) or with CRL de-risking that narrows the outcome distribution.

## Competitive Landscape

**Hypercortisolism.** Korlym competes with Recordati's osilodrostat (label broadened in April 2025 to all etiologies) and pasireotide, plus Xeris's levoketoconazole and off-label generic ketoconazole. Corcept's defensible edge is the population-expansion strategy rather than head-to-head share. The competitive position here is under pressure on the generic side: the Federal Circuit affirmed on February 19, 2026 that Teva's generic does not infringe Corcept's '214/'800 patents (Corcept filed an en banc rehearing petition April 22), Teva has sold a generic since January 2024, and settlements allow Sun and Hikma to enter. The -6.7% average-price effect in Q1 is the visible erosion. Corcept's authorized generic is a partial defense but caps pricing.

**Platinum-resistant ovarian cancer.** This is a crowded but fragmented market. Lifyorli's competitive edge is being biomarker-agnostic with a Phase 3 overall-survival benefit, a stronger endpoint than the PFS readouts competitors rely on. Management argues that even within KEYTRUDA's PD-L1 population (~35-40% of patients) physicians may prefer the ROSELLA regimen. The early prescriber breadth and NCCN preferred status support the share-gain narrative.

On trajectory, which matters more than level, the picture is genuinely mixed. The company is gaining share in a new oncology market (improving) while losing pricing/share in its legacy franchise to generics (deteriorating). The net is a moderately improving competitive position driven by the oncology entry, which I score 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the binding constraint on this recommendation. The cluster of idiosyncratic, partly binary overhangs is unusually dense for a $9B-cap pharma, and the current valuation does not leave room for adverse resolution.

**1. Cushing's CRL — unresolved binary.** On December 30, 2025 the FDA issued a Complete Response Letter declining to approve relacorilant in hypercortisolism, demanding additional evidence of effectiveness even though it acknowledged GRACE met its primary endpoint and GRADIENT provided confirmatory evidence. Corcept met with the FDA in April 2026 and announced on May 27 it will resubmit. The path and timeline are uncertain, and this gates the largest piece of the $2B Cushing's thesis.

**2. Securities and derivative litigation.** A securities class action (Allegheny County Employees' Retirement System, filed February 20, 2026) alleges misleading statements about the relacorilant NDA over an October 31, 2024-December 30, 2025 class period. A related derivative suit (LeRiger v. Belanoff, April 10, 2026) names directors/officers and includes disgorgement-of-insider-trading-profits claims. Both are direct overhangs.

**3. Generic erosion (now court-confirmed).** Covered above; the Federal Circuit ruling crystallizes the long-term Korlym pricing threat.

**4. Antitrust litigation.** Teva's antitrust suit (filed June 2024, trial set March 2027) and a parallel payer suit (Aetna, HCSC, Humana, Molina) allege anticompetitive distribution and patient-support practices via former specialty pharmacy Optime. A long-running NJ U.S. Attorney HIPAA/Korlym-marketing investigation (open since November 2021) adds to the cluster.

**5. Specialty-pharmacy single-point dependency.** Corcept transitioned from Optime to Curant Health, which now collects >99% of hypercortisolism revenue. The prior vendor's inability to fully meet demand constrained 2025 revenue, and the Curant transition created the Q1 prior-auth backlog. A second pharmacy is only planned for Q4 2026. This concentration is both a revenue-fragility and the center of the antitrust litigation.

**6. Mifepristone political/regulatory risk.** Korlym's active ingredient is the abortion pill. HHS announced in September 2025 it will re-examine mifepristone's safety; post-Dobbs state restrictions create tail risk to distribution even though Korlym is not used for termination.

**7. Drug-pricing policy.** IRA inflation rebates and Medicare negotiation (orphan exemption currently shields the Cushing's products), the July 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act Medicaid cuts, and December 2025 CMS Most-Favored-Nation proposals are all flagged as potentially revenue-reducing in 2026+.

None of these litigation matters are accrued as losses. Collectively they represent meaningful tail risk plus management distraction. I score Risk Factors 4.0, an improvement from the 2.5 trough in March (the early-approval win removed the worst-case "no growth driver" scenario), but still firmly below midpoint given the unresolved CRL, the dense litigation slate, and the fact that the current price prices little of this in.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent, asymmetric downside risk. Near-term ATM IV is 76.6% versus 51.5% at the six-month tenor, a backwardated term structure that signals the market expects near-term stress to resolve. Put/call skew is elevated at 23.3% (with the 1-week skew at 22.9%), indicating relatively expensive downside protection. The put/call volume ratio of 5.92 confirms heavy near-term put demand. IV is actually cheap relative to one-year historical volatility of 91.1% (a -14.5% spread), reflecting how extreme realized volatility has been in this name. Implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.0%. ATM IV jumped from 50.7% to 76.6% as the stock ran +40% over the same window, consistent with the market hedging a sharp move in either direction around the next catalyst rather than pure complacency. For a BUY this skew would be a flag; for our HOLD it reinforces the view that the risk/reward is two-sided and that chasing the momentum here is poorly compensated.

Short interest sits at 10.6% of float (8.79M shares, 6.8 days to cover), elevated and consistent with institutional skepticism. This is not high enough to create meaningful squeeze fuel on its own, but it does mean the recent rally has occurred partly against a bearish base.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (toward $135)

Lifyorli becomes the standard-of-care regimen in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer faster than expected, driven by biomarker-agnostic positioning and the OS benefit, reaching a clear path toward >$1B US revenue. The relacorilant Cushing's NDA resubmission succeeds, unlocking the acceleration toward the $2B franchise target. The year-end 2026 catalysts (BELLA-A in PROC, MONARCH in MASH) read out positively, expanding the relacorilant opportunity and validating the broader pipeline. SG&A normalizes as launches mature, restoring operating leverage and pushing EPS well above the $2.50 next-year consensus. The litigation cluster resolves without material damages. In this scenario the stock re-rates on a larger, de-risked earnings base.

### Bear Case (toward $50)

The Cushing's resubmission fails or stalls again, permanently capping the franchise at a Korlym base eroding under confirmed generic competition (Teva, Sun, Hikma) and ongoing price mix shift. Lifyorli's launch decelerates after the initial prescriber surge as real-world uptake proves slower than the early metrics implied. The securities and antitrust litigation produces a material settlement or adverse ruling at the March 2027 trial. Continued launch spend keeps the company in operating losses longer than guided, and the cash position erodes toward a capital raise. The MASH/ALS pipeline disappoints. At $50 the stock would be back near where it traded in early 2026, pricing the legacy franchise plus a discounted Lifyorli.

### Base Case (toward $90)

Lifyorli ramps solidly but not explosively, revenue lands within the $950M-$1.05B guide, the Cushing's resubmission remains a live but unresolved option, and the year-end catalysts are mixed-to-positive. SG&A intensity persists through 2026 before easing in 2027. The litigation overhangs continue without resolution but without material accruals. The stock holds near the consensus mean as the market waits for the binary catalysts to clarify the distribution.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The growth thesis is real and the catalyst calendar is dense, but the stock has run too far too fast relative to the unresolved binaries to justify a fresh long here.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$60s on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore PEG below ~1.2 on the $2.50 next-year EPS and re-establish asymmetric upside.
- FDA acceptance of the relacorilant Cushing's resubmission with a defined PDUFA date, materially de-risking the $2B franchise leg and narrowing the outcome distribution.
- Two consecutive quarters of Lifyorli revenue confirming the launch trajectory (e.g., the back-half quarters showing the revenue inflection the guidance implies) alongside continued positive estimate revisions.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second adverse FDA action on relacorilant in Cushing's (resubmission rejected or requiring a new trial), which would permanently cap the larger growth leg.
- Evidence that Lifyorli uptake is stalling after the initial prescriber surge — sequential decline in new-patient starts or a guidance cut.
- A material adverse development in the securities or antitrust litigation, or a settlement that meaningfully impairs the balance sheet.
- Operating cash burn accelerating toward a level that would force a capital raise within 12 months.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (late July/early August), which should show the first full quarter of Lifyorli contribution and clarity on whether the back-half revenue inflection is materializing. The year-end 2026 catalyst cluster (BELLA-A, MONARCH, ALS Phase 3 start) and the EU MAA decision (Q4 2026) are the larger value-determining events. The March 2027 antitrust trial is the longer-dated overhang.

## Conclusion

Corcept has transformed from a single-product company into a two-pillar franchise on the strength of an early, clean ovarian-cancer approval, and the underlying Korlym demand base remains at record highs despite reported-revenue noise. The estimate revisions are constructive (+55% on next-year EPS over 90 days), the balance sheet is debt-free with $515M cash, and the year-end 2026 catalyst calendar offers substantial free optionality. These are the reasons the score trajectory has improved from 4.8 (March) to 6.0 today, and why we hold rather than avoid.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the combination of a fully-valued price and a stacked set of unresolved binaries. At $83-84 the stock trades at roughly the consensus mean, at ~34x an already-upgraded next-year EPS, having tripled from its January trough and risen 17% in the past week. That price discounts both a successful Lifyorli ramp and an eventual Cushing's resubmission win, while the options market prices imminent two-sided stress (76.6% near-term IV, 23.3% put skew, backwardated term structure) and short interest sits at 10.6%. The Cushing's CRL is unresolved with no firm timeline, Korlym generic erosion is now court-confirmed, and the litigation cluster (securities class action, derivative suit, two antitrust actions, and an open DOJ investigation) represents genuine tail risk that is not accrued and not priced. The right entry is lower, or after the resubmission path de-risks. We would revisit toward a BUY in the low-$60s or on FDA acceptance of the relacorilant resubmission.
