## Executive Summary

Travel + Leisure is the largest vacation ownership operator in the world by owner count, and the marginal profit dollar comes from consumer lending rather than hospitality. The company finances roughly half of its vacation ownership interest (VOI) sales at a 14.6% weighted-average rate and funds those receivables in the asset-backed market at 5.11% (March 2026) and 5.52% (July 2026) coupons at ~98% advance rates. That spread is the durable advantage, and it is also the structural dependency: the equity has no book-value cushion (total deficit of $(1,020)M at 6/30/2026) and relies on uninterrupted securitization access.

The operating record is better than the GAAP optics suggest. FY2025 GAAP net income fell 44% to $230M on ~$216M of inventory write-downs tied to a 17-resort optimization initiative, while adjusted diluted EPS rose 10.3% to $6.34 and adjusted EBITDA rose 6.6% to $990M. H1 2026 continued that trajectory: revenue $2,024M (+3.7%), adjusted EBITDA $494M (+9.3%), Q2 adjusted EPS $1.88 (+13.9%). Management raised FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance to $1.065-1.085B and guided approximately 20% adjusted EPS growth. Consensus has followed — current-year EPS is $7.60, up 3.7% over 90 days, and next-year is $8.86, up 4.2%. Credit, the central bear worry, has stabilized: H1 2026 provision of $241M approximately matched net write-offs of ~$244M (which is why the allowance was flat at $660M), the derived provision rate on financed sales was 20.2% versus 20.3% a year earlier, and early-stage delinquency improved ~40bps sequentially in Q2 after adjusting for normal seasonality.

Against that, three things keep this a HOLD for a fifth consecutive review rather than an upgrade. First, the Travel and Membership segment — roughly 20% of segment EBITDA — is contracting at a rate that is accelerating, not stabilizing: Q2 exchange transactions fell 13.4% and revenue per transaction 12.3%, and the 10-K states that developer-owned internal exchange networks reduce RCI usage "by design" while corporate-member concentration makes the loss of any single affiliation "more pronounced." Second, cash conversion is deteriorating in a way that is disclosed but unresolved: H1 operating cash flow was $258M versus $353M, with FY2026 inventory spend guided to $200-230M against $130M in FY2025, an ~$85M incremental drag that competes with $343M of acquisitions and a buyback management says will be "similar" to 2025's $300M. Third, the identifiable bear case runs through the same channel the macro backdrop flags as live and uncalibrated — a consumer-credit break in H2 2026 — and TNL's credit and funding exposures are correlated rather than diversifying. At $74.68 the base case offers roughly 15% upside against a bear case near $57 (-24%). That is a positive expected value with an inadequate margin of safety.

## Company Overview

Two reportable segments:

**Vacation Ownership (~80% of segment adjusted EBITDA)** develops, markets, sells and finances VOIs and manages the resulting resorts under HOA agreements. The owner base was approximately 800,000 before the July 2026 acquisitions, across 280+ resorts. Three stacked economics: high-gross-margin retail points sales; a consumer finance book (14.6% weighted-average yield, ABS-funded); and recurring property-management and club dues.

**Travel and Membership (~20%)** is the RCI exchange network (3,275,000 average exchange members in Q2 2026) plus travel clubs and subscription travel.

Counterparty structure matters and is easy to misstate. TNL owns the *Travel + Leisure* brand as applied to travel services but does not own the magazine or media properties, which People Inc. operates outside TNL's control — Item 1A flags that deterioration there could damage the brand TNL owns. TNL is a licensee, not owner, of the Club Wyndham and WorldMark marks, and accesses Wyndham Hotels' loyalty program through the "Blue Thread" marketing arrangement; Item 1A states that any cessation or adverse change in that program "could be expected to materially adversely impact our business, growth strategy and financial results." Certain TNL directors also sit on Wyndham Hotels' board, a conflict the 10-K discloses. Accor Vacation Club was acquired in 2024; Margaritaville, Sports Illustrated Resorts and Eddie Bauer Adventure Club are brand arrangements whose underlying agreements were not reviewed.

Two July 2026 transactions reshape the owner base: Yes& Vacations closed July 15 for $193M cash, and Spinnaker Resorts was signed July 14 for $150M plus up to $10M contingent, expected to close in Q3 2026. Combined: 23 resorts (6 in Hilton Head, 7 in Maui), more than 100,000 owners added (a >10% increase on the ~800,000 base), approximately 80% of whom carry no remaining loan balance. Management's stated economics are ~$340M invested, ~$80M of acquired receivables to be securitized, and ~$50M of synergized run-rate EBITDA in the first 12 months inclusive of incremental interest expense — implying roughly $260M net capital at about 5x. These are management estimates from the July 22 call, corroborated by trade press but not audited; no Item 2.01 8-K was filed, consistent with both deals falling below the significance threshold against ~$6.9B of total assets.

Adjusted diluted EPS: $3.65 (2021) → $4.52 → $5.70 → $5.75 → **$6.34 (2025)**. The FY2025 GAAP/adjusted divergence reconciles to ~$216M of inventory write-downs plus ~$8M of impairments under the resort-optimization initiative. The gap between +4.1% revenue growth and +10.3% adjusted EPS growth is the arithmetic product of modest EBITDA margin expansion (FY2025 adjusted EBITDA margin ~24.6%) and a ~4% share-count reduction.

**H1 2026:** revenue $2,024M (+3.7%); adjusted EBITDA $494M (+9.3%); GAAP diluted EPS $2.94; Q2 revenue $1,063M (+4.4%), adjusted EBITDA $269M (+7.6%), adjusted EPS $1.88 versus $1.65.

**Sales mix and the composition of growth.** FY2025 gross VOI sales were $2,486M, +8.4%, but tours grew only 2.5% while volume per guest (VPG) grew 6.1% to $3,284. Q3 2026 guidance puts VPG at $3,300-3,350 — broadly flat against the FY2025 level. Price- and close-rate-led growth is more finite than tour-flow-led growth, and VPG is the first metric that will register consumer fatigue.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $640M against $117M of capex, so free cash flow was $523M — 13.0% of revenue and 52.8% of adjusted EBITDA, consistent with management's "about half of EBITDA" framing. H1 2026 operating cash flow was $258M against $353M, a $95M decline the 10-Q attributes to ~$54M of higher inventory spend, ~$22M of prepaid build and ~$18M of higher tax payments; H1 capex was ~$44M, so H1 GAAP FCF was ~$214M.

A sensitivity, not a forecast: if H2 2026 operating cash flow simply matched H2 2025's ~$287M, FY2026 OCF would be ~$545M and, net of guided capex of $90-100M, GAAP FCF ~$450M — about 42% of guided adjusted EBITDA rather than ~50%. The identifiable reconciling driver is the disclosed inventory step-up to $200-230M from $130M. H2 is TNL's seasonally strongest period, working-capital timing and acquisition items are not reconciled here, and the definition of management's non-GAAP "adjusted free cash flow" was not verified. The honest range for FY2026 FCF is roughly $450-540M, or a 9.8-11.8% yield on the current market cap.

**Credit book — the core of the equity.** Derived provision rate on gross VOI sales net of fee-for-service: FY2024 20.1%, FY2025 20.8%, H1 2025 20.3%, H1 2026 20.2%, Q2 2026 21.2% (Q2 2025 21.3%). The allowance was $660M at 6/30/2026 versus $663M at 12/31/2025, against gross contract receivables of $3,249M and $3,301M (20.3% and 20.1%). H1 net write-offs were ~$244M versus ~$229M. The multi-year reserve build several analysts had flagged has paused, because provision approximately equaled write-offs. Underwriting inputs: FICO above 740 at origination; down payments in the mid-twenties percent and improving. There is also a structural collections gate — an owner must be current to book travel and to arrive at a resort — which is a genuine advantage over unsecured consumer lending but also means reported delinquency partly measures a policy mechanism.

FY2026 consolidated provision guidance is ~21%, above FY2025's derived 20.8%, because the acquired book carries a higher rate even as the organic rate improves. Each 100bps of eventual provision-rate reduction on ~$2.35B of financed-eligible sales is roughly $23-24M of pre-tax EBITDA — an order-of-magnitude sensitivity, and the CFO said the longer-term provision assumption may be reevaluated later this year for 2027 planning.

**Balance sheet.** Total deficit $(1,020)M at 6/30/2026. Corporate debt $3,700M, non-recourse securitization debt $2,010M, cash $282M. Covenants have headroom: first-lien leverage 3.16x against a 4.25x maximum, interest coverage 5.05x against a 2.00x minimum. The $650M 6.625% notes due July 2026 were refinanced in May 2026 with $900M of 6.25% notes due 2031 — five years of extension and ~38bps of coupon relief, at the cost of $250M of incremental gross debt. Next corporate maturity is $400M of 6.00% notes due April 2027. Ratings Ba3/BB−/BB+. Approximately $954M of undrawn revolver was reported retained after funding the acquisitions (sourced to management's call remarks via trade press, not verified against a filing). Off-balance-sheet items include $431M of maximum HOA subsidy exposure, $544M of surety bonds against $2.38B of capacity, and $1,008M of purchase commitments with $456M due within a year.

A screening Altman Z-Score near 0.86 places TNL in the "distress" zone. That is mis-specified rather than informative here: the metric penalizes negative book equity heavily when TNL's deficit is the mechanical result of repurchasing stock, and it treats $2.0B of non-recourse securitization debt — collateralized by roughly $2.3B of pledged receivables — as a general corporate claim. The covenant headroom, the cleared 2026 maturity and the revolver are the more probative solvency evidence. The score is nonetheless gesturing at something real: there is no equity buffer, and in a severe consumer shock receivables performance and ABS market access would deteriorate together.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance: adjusted EBITDA $1.065-1.085B (core, excluding acquisitions, $1.05-1.065B; acquisitions $15-20M), gross VOI sales $2.6-2.675B, provision ~21%, tax ~29%, FCF conversion ~half of EBITDA, adjusted EPS growth ~20%, year-end leverage ~3.2x. Q3: gross VOI sales $700-740M, adjusted EBITDA $275-285M.

The midpoint implies +8.6% EBITDA growth, of which roughly 1.8pp is acquisitions. That consolidated number understates Vacation Ownership momentum, because Travel and Membership is subtracting. Segment adjusted EBITDA there was $108M in H1 2026 versus $123M (−12%); on an annualized base of roughly $210-220M, a 12% decline is about a $27M headwind, or roughly 2.7pp of consolidated EBITDA. Vacation Ownership therefore has to grow closer to 10% to deliver the guided ~6.8% core growth. Segment figures do not sum to consolidated because unallocated corporate costs sit between, so treat the attribution as directional.

Estimate revisions corroborate the guide rather than signal an inflection:

| Current quarter | $2.06 | $2.03 | $1.99 | +1.4% | +3.5% |

This is steady positive momentum in the +3-9% range across nine analysts, not the +30% revision velocity that marks a genuine earnings inflection. The earnings track record is 3 beats and 1 miss in four quarters, and the miss was the most recent — Q2 adjusted EPS of $1.88 against $1.91 consensus (−1.7% per the surprise data; separately reported as −2.6% versus a $1.93 non-GAAP consensus in trade press), paired with a ~2% revenue beat and a raised guide.

Growth drivers worth crediting: the acquired owner base is the cheapest source of high-VPG upgrade sales TNL has, and roughly 80% of incoming owners have no loan balance; new brands (Margaritaville above $150M of annual VOI sales, Accor Vacation Club sales on track to nearly double in 2026, Sports Illustrated Resorts Nashville opening in Q3) now represent roughly 10% of the sales mix; the Club Wyndham app drives more than 30% of club bookings, lowering tour-sourcing cost. Drivers worth discounting: the conversion rate of acquired owners onto TNL's points platform is entirely unquantified, and the fee-for-service channel is collapsing — Q2 FFS VOI sales were $28M versus $52M and FFS commission revenue fell 58% to $11M, which shifts the business toward owned inventory and self-financed consumers. That raises gross margin per sale while consuming cash and increasing VOCR exposure. It is a mix shift, not diversification.

## Valuation Assessment

At $74.68:

- **Forward adjusted P/E: 9.8x** on FY2026 consensus of $7.60; **8.4x** on FY2027 consensus of $8.86. (The data provider's 19.69x forward and trailing figures are identical, which is a feed artifact; the trailing GAAP multiple is distorted by the FY2025 write-downs and is not the right anchor.)
- **EV/EBITDA: 7.7x.** Market cap $4,577M plus pro forma net corporate debt of ~$3,681M ($3,700M debt less $282M cash, plus $193M Yes&, plus $150M Spinnaker, less ~$80M of acquired receivables to be securitized) equals $8,258M, against the $1,075M guidance midpoint. Including the $2,010M of non-recourse debt gives 9.6x, but that double-counts against $2.3B of pledged receivables and overstates the operating multiple.
- **FCF yield 9.8-11.8%** on the $450-540M range above.
- **Dividend yield ~3.0-3.3%.** The brief's $0.56 quarterly rate annualizes to $2.24 (3.0%); H1 cash dividends of $78M on ~61.5M shares imply a higher run rate closer to $0.63 quarterly, consistent with the provider's 3.25%. Annual cost is roughly $137-155M, comfortably covered on either FCF estimate.
- Analyst targets: low $77, median $90, mean $91, high $107. Eleven buy/strong-buy against one hold, stable over four months. The low target sits 3.1% above spot.

Peer trailing multiples are a poor anchor here: median 27.0x is dragged by VAC at negative earnings, MTN at 33.6x and WH at 27.0x, while HGV — the closest comparable — trades at 25.6x trailing. On a forward adjusted basis, timeshare operators cluster in the high-single to low-double digits, and TNL at 9.8x is not conspicuously cheap against HGV once HGV's Bluegreen integration charges are normalized. The more defensible statement is that TNL trades near the low end of its own recent range: FY2025 repurchases averaged $55.52, which was 8.8x that year's adjusted EPS, and the stock has re-rated to 9.8x since.

The discount to lodging and leisure peers has identifiable causes rather than being a pure market error: a $(1.02)B deficit with no equity cushion; a structurally declining segment worth roughly 20% of segment EBITDA; consumer-credit exposure at a point in the cycle where the H2 consumer path is unresolved; 14.2% short interest with 8.4 days to cover. Cheapness alone is not a thesis, and in this sector a compressed multiple has more often signalled the market anticipating credit or demand deterioration than a mispricing.

What supports a 6.3 rather than a lower valuation score: the guide was raised, not cut; estimates are rising in every window; the acquisitions were funded from the revolver at roughly 5x post-synergy and add owners rather than just real estate; a 2027 provision-assumption reduction is a specific, non-trivial and currently unpriced earnings lever; and continued repurchase at 9.8x forward earnings is arithmetically accretive. What caps it: the FCF conversion shortfall relative to management's framing is unreconciled, and the bear case is a live macro channel rather than a hypothetical.

**Base case $86** — 9.7x FY2027 consensus of $8.86, essentially the current multiple applied to next year's earnings, +15.2% from spot, plus ~3% of dividend. **Bull $102** — 11x on $9.25 if the 2027 provision assumption comes down, acquisition conversion lands near management's $50M synergy figure, and Travel and Membership is monetized (+36.6%). **Bear $57** — 8x on ~$7.10 if the provision rate moves to 24-25% and VPG rolls over, roughly the 52-week low of $57.86 (−23.7%). Weighting base 50%, bull 20%, bear 30% gives an expected return near +8% before dividends. Positive, but not enough asymmetry at this entry point to justify a positive rating.

## Competitive Landscape

The industry is consolidating around TNL, HGV and VAC plus a long tail of independents, and the driver is funding cost: a scaled operator securitizes at ~5% against a 14.6% loan yield, which independents cannot replicate, so the independents become sellers and their owner bases become the currency. TNL's July acquisitions are the direct expression of that dynamic — more than 100,000 owners for roughly $260M of net capital.

Relative execution in the most recent quarter favors TNL. HGV posted Q2 revenue of $1.36B (+7.3%) but non-GAAP EPS of $0.89, missing consensus by 11.7%, and the stock fell. TNL missed by 1.7% on a revenue beat and raised its full-year guide. HGV grew revenue faster, though the Bluegreen integration complicates the organic comparison. That is the sector-relative evidence the bull case needs, and it is real but narrow — one quarter.

The competitive deterioration is concentrated in Travel and Membership, and it is worsening rather than stabilizing. FY2025: exchange transactions −9.0%, average members −2.9%, revenue per transaction −5.6%. Q2 2026: transactions −13.4%, members −1.6%, total revenue per transaction −12.3%. Item 1A explains the mechanism plainly — developers are building internal points-based exchange networks that "by design decrease the propensity of owners to continue their membership in and use of external vacation ownership exchange programs, such as RCI" — and cites the precedent of Marriott Vacations' Welk acquisition terminating the RCI contract. It further notes that as corporate member relationships grow, losing any single affiliation becomes "more pronounced," so concentration in this segment is increasing. The travel clubs are not offsetting: transactions grew 26.5% while revenue per transaction fell 17.3%, and the 10-K concedes the clubs "have not grown as quickly as originally anticipated."

Item 1A also frames the broader competitive set honestly: hotels, cruises, and home/apartment sharing platforms, where new supply "may create new competitors, in some cases without corresponding increases in demand," plus persistent pressure from secondary-market and private VOI resales on primary pricing.

Net trajectory: the funding-scale moat is widening and the owner base is growing through M&A; the exchange franchise is eroding at an accelerating rate with worsening counterparty concentration. That combination supports a competitive-position score below average, unchanged from the prior review.

## Risk Assessment

**Consumer credit and ABS dependency (primary).** TNL provisions ~21% of financed sales at origination. A 300-400bps deterioration in loss expectations would compress margin materially — roughly $70-95M of pre-tax EBITDA on the sensitivity above — and Item 1A specifically identifies lower down payments, reduced receivable sales, and timeshare-exit companies that "encourage defaults" as drivers of a higher allowance. The exposure is correlated: in a severe shock, receivables performance and ABS market access deteriorate simultaneously, and Item 1A flags conduit renewal risk and "portfolio performance triggers under securitization transactions which if violated may result in a disruption or loss of cash flow." With a $(1.02)B deficit there is no book-equity buffer, so the capital-return program becomes the flex item earlier than at a peer with positive equity — the Board did reduce the dividend and suspend repurchases during COVID, which Item 1A recalls without assurance it will not recur.

**Physical/climate concentration.** On insurable property values at 12/31/2025: 35% of managed properties sit in Tier I windstorm exposure areas, 22% in high-risk wildfire states, 19% in high flood-risk areas, and a 2025 assessment identified 63 managed resorts in high or extremely high water-stressed locations. The under-appreciated second-order effect, stated in Item 1A, is that insurance costs "may materially impact resort ownership unit maintenance fees to timeshare owners which could potentially make vacation ownership less attractive." The acquisitions increase rather than diversify this exposure: 6 resorts in Hilton Head and 7 in Maui.

**Further optimization charges.** The 10-K states the restructuring "may lead to additional impairments or other charges as the initiative progresses," and removing properties from timeshare regimes involves court-supervised restructuring with HOAs. GAAP earnings remain exposed through FY2027 even if the cash benefit — H1 developer obligations fell to $20M from $59M, a $40M reduction — is real.

**Blue Thread dependency.** The tour funnel depends on a loyalty program TNL does not own; Item 1A offers "no assurance that this Blue Thread marketing source will continue to generate new owner tours at the level or with the volume per guest we have experienced to date." Since growth is currently VPG-led, degradation in tour *quality* would hit the engine directly.

**Legacy contingent liabilities.** TNL remains responsible for 25% of Avis Budget/Cendant contingent liabilities incurred on or before August 23, 2006, and two-thirds of certain pre-spin Wyndham Worldwide contingent liabilities, with shortfall obligations if a co-obligor defaults. These are unquantified — a genuine tail item rather than boilerplate.

**Governance and disclosure items.** An agreement in principle to settle an SEC investigation for a $975,000 non-scienter civil penalty relating to 2019-2020-era disclosures about rescinded delinquent loans is financially immaterial (~0.02% of market cap) but not final. The company sublets aircraft from the Chairman. Four officers reduced direct holdings to zero or near-zero in 2026 at $70-78; whether they remain employed is unconfirmed (no 2026 Item 5.02 8-K exists and searches returned nothing), so it should not be read as a conviction signal in either direction — but it should not be assumed benign either.

**Macro overlay.** The July sector selloff in vacation-related names was attributed in trade press to renewed Middle East conflict lifting oil and clouding the consumer outlook, and TNL sits at the intersection of discretionary demand and subprime-adjacent consumer credit. A consumer-credit deterioration would hit financed-sale credit performance directly, and sustained oil strength compounds it through travel cost. That concern is part of why the multiple is 7.7x. [unverified — the specific mortgage-rate, housing-wealth, and payroll transmission claims are not supported by the research brief; the brief's macro attribution is limited to oil/Middle East and consumer-outlook concerns.]

### Options Market Signal

Post-earnings vol crush dominates the surface. ATM IV fell from 30.4% on July 23 to 19.4% on August 13 while the price moved −1.4%, and put/call open interest shifted from 0.94 to 0.36 — a call-tilted move in structure. The term structure is in contango: 19.4% at 7 days, 33.8% at 35 days, 32.1% at 98 days, so the "cheap versus 35.7% one-year historical vol" reading applies only to the front week, which contains no scheduled catalyst. The 31.7% put/call skew flagged as elevated put demand is confined to that same one-week tenor and inverts to −4.1% at one month and +0.8% at three months; with only 74 contracts across three expirations, the skew signal is thin and I would not weight it. The informative reading is that one- and three-month IV in the low-30s prices roughly ±8% and ±14% moves, which is consistent with a market that regards the Q3 print and the consumer-credit path as genuinely two-sided. Separately, 14.2% short interest with 8.4 days to cover means a clean Q3 print with credit metrics holding could produce an outsized upward move, and a provision surprise an outsized downward one.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($102, +36.6%)

Vacation Ownership is compounding EBITDA near 10% while the consolidated number is dragged to ~8.6% by a shrinking exchange segment. The market prices the consolidated growth rate and the negative book equity, at 9.8x current-year and 8.4x next-year adjusted earnings. Three catalysts convert that into re-rating: (1) the acquired 100,000 owners, ~80% debt-free, convert onto the points platform at management's implied economics, delivering ~$50M of synergized EBITDA on ~$260M of net capital; (2) the CFO's signalled reassessment of the longer-term provision assumption for 2027 comes down, worth roughly $23-24M of pre-tax EBITDA per 100bps, on the basis of FICO above 740, mid-twenties down payments, provision now equal to write-offs, and 40bps of seasonality-adjusted delinquency improvement; (3) some strategic action on Travel and Membership removes a structurally declining, low-multiple asset. Repurchases at 9.8x forward earnings — following FY2025's $300M at $55.52, now up 34% — compound per-share value, with a $750M authorization added and share count down ~4% year over year. At 11x $9.25 the stock is $102, still below the $107 high analyst target.

### Bear Case ($57, −23.7%)

TNL is a subprime-adjacent consumer lender with no equity cushion, entering the second half of a year in which the consumer outlook is clouded by renewed macro and oil pressure. Growth is VPG-led with tours up only 2.5% in FY2025 and Q3 VPG guided flat — the pricing lever is close to exhausted. The consolidated provision rate is already guided up to ~21% because the acquired book underwrites worse; a further 300-400bps of deterioration removes most of the EPS growth the multiple depends on, and the CECL allowance flat against a flat book means lifetime loss expectations are unchanged rather than improving. Cash conversion is already slipping — H1 OCF $258M versus $353M, FY2026 FCF plausibly ~42% of EBITDA rather than ~50% — while inventory spend steps up $85M and $343M went to acquisitions, so the buyback that supports the EPS growth rate is the flex item. Meanwhile 20% of segment EBITDA declines 12% a year with worsening counterparty concentration, further optimization impairments are explicitly flagged, and the acquisitions concentrated exposure in Maui and Hilton Head into a hardening property insurance market that feeds back into HOA maintenance fees and demand. At 8x a $7.10 earnings base the stock is $57, roughly the 52-week low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** There is a coherent 12-month appreciation thesis with dated catalysts, and the yield is covered, but the entry does not offer sufficient margin of safety. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, so the reasoning for not changing is stated explicitly: since the last review the price is essentially unchanged (−1.2% from $75.60), estimates have risen 3-5%, guidance was raised, and credit metrics held — all improvements — while the offsetting facts also strengthened (exchange-segment decline accelerated to −13.4% transactions, cash conversion slipped, consolidated provision guided up, coastal concentration increased). The net is a marginally better business at the same price with an unresolved macro overhang. The arithmetic supports holding, not adding.

- **Base case $86** — 9.7x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $8.86, the current multiple carried forward. **+15.2%** from $74.68, plus ~3% dividend.
- **Bull case $102** — 11x on ~$9.25 (provision assumption reduced, acquisition conversion delivered, T&M monetized). **+36.6%.**
- **Bear case $57** — 8x on ~$7.10 (provision to 24-25%, VPG rolls over). **−23.7%.**
- **Probability-weighted:** ~+8% before dividends at 50/20/30 base/bull/bear.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoints at the Q3 2026 print (late October) and the FY2026 guide-out for 2027 (February 2027).

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires any two of:**
1. Price at $65-67 or below on unchanged fundamentals — the level at which the CFO purchased in April 2026 and where base-case upside exceeds 28% against a bear case near $57. At that price the asymmetry works.
2. A disclosed reduction in the longer-term provision assumption for 2027, or a second consecutive quarter of seasonality-adjusted early-stage delinquency improvement with the consolidated provision rate at or below 21%.
3. FY2026 operating cash flow tracking to $545M or better with capex within the $90-100M guide, reconciling FCF conversion back toward 50% of EBITDA.
4. An announced transaction or separation for Travel and Membership at a disclosed multiple, with proceeds applied to leverage or repurchase.

**Downgrade to REDUCE on any of:**
1. Consolidated provision rate above 23% in any quarter, or the allowance rebuilding above 21% of gross receivables alongside rising net write-offs.
2. VPG below the $3,300 low end of Q3 guidance combined with flat-to-negative tour growth — the signal that the pricing lever is exhausted.
3. Repurchases suspended or materially curtailed, or the dividend cut, indicating cash is being redirected to inventory, acquisitions or credit.

## Conclusion

Travel + Leisure is a better-run business than its GAAP earnings and its screening metrics suggest. FY2025's 44% net income decline was a deliberate pruning of 17 under-performing resorts that has already reduced developer obligations by $40M in H1 2026, while adjusted EPS rose 10.3%. Credit, the central bear concern, has stabilized on every observable metric: provision equal to write-offs, flat allowance, 40bps of seasonality-adjusted delinquency improvement, FICO above 740, higher down payments. The July acquisitions buy 100,000 mostly debt-free owners — TNL's cheapest source of high-margin upgrade sales — at roughly 5x post-synergy EBITDA, funded from the revolver. Estimates are rising, guidance was raised, and the closest peer missed by 11.7% in the same quarter TNL missed by 1.7% with a revenue beat.

The price reflects a set of real constraints rather than an obvious error. Roughly 20% of segment EBITDA is contracting at an accelerating 12% annual rate with worsening counterparty concentration and no credible stabilization plan beyond a possible sale. Cash conversion is slipping against an $85M inventory step-up, and the buyback that supplies a meaningful share of the ~20% adjusted EPS growth is the flex item in a capital plan that also funded $343M of acquisitions. There is no book-equity cushion, and the funding advantage that makes the business work is rented from a securitization market that would close in the same shock that impairs the receivables. The macro path most likely to produce that shock — a clouded H2 consumer outlook amid renewed oil/geopolitical pressure — is live rather than hypothetical.

At $74.68, 7.8% below the 52-week high and 22% above the low, the base case offers about 15% plus a 3% yield against roughly 24% of downside in a credit-deterioration scenario. That combination warrants owning if already owned and waiting if not. The specific level that would change the call is $65-67, or evidence in the Q3 print that the 2027 provision assumption is coming down and cash conversion is reverting toward 50% of EBITDA.

**Rating: HOLD.** Overall score 5.7/10.
