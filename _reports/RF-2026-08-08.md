## Executive Summary

Regions Financial has done what it said it would do. Q2 2026 delivered $0.64 GAAP / $0.68 adjusted diluted EPS, a 3.66% net interest margin held essentially flat for four consecutive quarters, interest-bearing deposit costs of 1.69%, and net charge-offs of 42bps down 12bps sequentially. Business-services criticized loans fell from 7.22% to 5.01% of business loans in a year, and roughly $1.3B of office, trucking and communications exposure has left the bank. The board raised the quarterly dividend 13% to $0.30 effective October 1, taking the forward yield to 3.82% at $31.43 against a regional-bank peer median of 2.8%. On the metrics that define a deposit-funded bank — funding cost, margin, liquidity ($69B available, 181% coverage of uninsured deposits), and stress-test resilience (PPNR covering 101.4% of projected credit losses) — this is a well-run institution.

The investment question is narrower than the business question. Since our late-May review the stock has moved from $28.09 to $31.43, +11.9%, and now sits 3.2% below its 52-week high of $32.47. Over the same period the composition of earnings deteriorated in one specific way: H1 2026 net charge-offs of $232M exceeded provision of $159M by $73M, a pre-tax reserve release worth roughly $0.07 per share after tax, and the CFO stated on the July 17 call that the current 1.63% allowance ratio "is indicative of where we'd expect to be absent new information." That tailwind does not repeat. Simultaneously, fee income was guided to the low end of the +3% to +5% range (mortgage −31% y/y, capital markets flat), near-quarter consensus was cut ~2% over both 30 and 90 days, and sell-side sentiment shifted from 8 buy-side ratings against 3 sell-side three months ago to 6 against 4 today. Consensus mean target of $32.93 sits 4.8% above spot.

We keep the HOLD, for the fourth consecutive review, but on a lower overall score (5.95 from 6.4) driven by valuation (price up 12% against roughly unchanged intrinsic value), growth (loss of the reserve tailwind plus the fee guide cut), and risk (an unresolved Basel re-proposal that management itself cites as gating buybacks, plus a 2027 core deposit conversion). Base case $33.50 implies +6.6% price appreciation and +10.4% total return; the bear case at $25.75 implies −18.1%. That is fair, not compelling. The reason to own Regions here is the 3.82% dividend covered at a 46% payout ratio, supplemented by buybacks, for a realistic total shareholder yield of 7-9%. The reason not to add is that you are paying 2.28x tangible book with no identified mispricing and no near-term catalyst.

## Company Overview

Regions is a Delaware financial holding company headquartered in Birmingham, operating Regions Bank, an Alabama state-chartered Fed member bank. At December 31, 2025 it held $158.8B in assets, $131.1B in deposits and $19.0B in equity across 1,247 branches; the July release cites "$161 billion in assets" and "more than 1,200 banking offices." Three segments: Consumer Bank (Q2 segment NII ~$748M, average deposits $80.6B), Corporate Bank (~$483M NII, $40.1B deposits) and Wealth Management (~$46M NII, $7.6B deposits). The Consumer Bank funds the balance sheet; the Corporate Bank grows the assets.

Geography is the structural fact. The top five states hold 862 of 1,247 branches, 69.1% of the network — Florida 270, Tennessee 194, Alabama 184, Georgia 117, Mississippi 97. Texas, which management names in its self-description, has 85 branches, which makes it a build rather than a franchise. The 10-K carries the concentration explicitly as a risk factor.

Regulatory classification matters more here than at most banks. Regions is a Category IV firm under the Tailoring Rules: no LCR or NSFR requirement, biennial rather than annual supervisory stress testing, no 165(d) resolution plan, and — critically — eligibility to opt out of recognizing most AOCI in regulatory capital, an election Regions has taken. The stress capital buffer remains floored at 2.5% through Q3 2027 following the Federal Reserve's February 2026 decision to hold SCB requirements pending stress-test model feedback.

## Financial Analysis

**H1 2026, reconciled.** Net interest income $2,525M ($2,552M taxable-equivalent), non-interest income $1,255M, total TE revenue $3,807M, non-interest expense $2,189M, pre-tax pre-provision income $1,618M, provision $159M against net charge-offs of $232M. That yields net income of ~$1,130M against $1,129M reported, a reconciliation that closes to within $1M and gives high confidence in the component figures. H1 net income of $1,129M compares to $1,053M in H1 2025, +7.2%; H1 diluted EPS $1.26 GAAP, $1.30 adjusted.

**Margin.** NIM (FTE) of 3.66% in Q2 versus 3.67% in Q1 and 3.65% in Q2 2025. For a $161B bank this is a genuine outlier on the high side. The drivers are identifiable and durable rather than opportunistic: average non-interest-bearing deposits of $39.7B on $130.7B total, a 30.4% mix; interest-bearing deposit cost of 1.69%, down 3bps sequentially with ~$5B of CDs repriced in Q2 at a ~30bp pickup and forward maturities falling to ~$3B/quarter at roughly equivalent rates; and ~$3B per quarter of fixed-rate asset turnover repricing 75-100bps higher, plus a ~7bp hedge benefit in Q3.

**Rate positioning is the differentiated element.** The Q2 10-Q models NII sensitivity at approximately +$62M for a gradual +100bp shift and −$60M for −100bp — roughly ±1.2% of annualized NII in either direction. Near-symmetric and low-magnitude rate sensitivity removes the single largest swing factor from the earnings model. Most regionals cannot say that.

The cost shows up in AOCI, which widened to −$1,922M at June 30 from −$1,535M at year-end, a $387M six-month deterioration driven principally by $278M (net of tax) of cash-flow hedge losses. Receive-fixed hedges lose value as rates rise; the mark reverses as they roll. Held-to-maturity securities carry $6,088M amortized cost, $5,271M carrying value and $5,180M fair value, so only ~$91M of additional unrecognized loss sits outside AOCI — a materially smaller hidden mark than at several peers. Separately, the 10-Q discloses a ~$2.4B (2.5%) fair-value discount on the loan portfolio, ~$2.81/share pre-tax, not reflected in the $20.48 book value.

**Expenses and operating leverage.** Q2 non-interest expense of $1,121M was +4.5% y/y and +5.0% sequentially; efficiency 58.3% reported, 56.9% adjusted, versus 56.6% in Q1. H1 efficiency 57.5%. Headcount rose to 20,003 from 19,642 at mid-2025, consistent with 60+ commercial banker hires over 18 months and wealth advisor recruitment. Expense growth is intentional, but it is running at the top of the +1.5% to +3.5% adjusted guide, which makes the promised full-year positive operating leverage dependent on a revenue-weighted H2.

**Balance sheet: the loan/deposit divergence.** Ending loans reached $99.2B, +1.3% sequentially and +2.6% y/y, with H1 growth of ~$3.6B concentrated in C&I. Business lending grew 5.3% y/y (C&I +4.5%, investor real estate +9.6%, owner-occupied CRE +4.4%) while consumer lending declined 2.7%. Ending deposits fell to $130.7B, −0.9% sequentially and −0.2% y/y; core customer deposits grew (consumer +1.3%, wealth +1.6%) while wholesale/brokered "Other" fell 34% to $2.3B. Loans/deposits rose from 73.9% to 75.9%. The gap was funded with borrowings: short-term borrowings went from $750M at year-end to $3.0B, plus a $1.5B Regions Bank senior note issuance in Q2.

Two readings are consistent with the data. Regions may be deliberately funding investment-grade commercial growth with wholesale debt rather than bidding for deposits, protecting the 1.69% deposit cost that underpins the NIM — and the issuance did reduce the FDIC assessment 11% sequentially via the unsecured-debt adjustment. Alternatively, the deposit softness is largely seasonal (tax refunds and payments, corporate cash deployment), making the borrowing bridge funding. The observable test over the next two quarters is whether loans/deposits keeps climbing.

**Capital, and the constraint that is not the headline.** CET1 of 10.7% under the AOCI opt-out; 9.5% including AOCI; Tier 1 11.8%; TCE/tangible assets 7.55%; book value $20.48; tangible book $13.78 (+6.7% y/y despite the H1 AOCI deterioration). Management targets 9.25%-9.75% on CET1 **including** AOCI and sits at 9.5%, the midpoint. It is not running the company to the 10.7%.

Capital generation is 45-50bps of CET1 per quarter, with the raised dividend consuming ~18bps rising to ~20bps, leaving ~25-30bps discretionary. Since ~10bps corresponds to ~$125M of repurchases, implied RWA is ~$125B and theoretical buyback capacity is roughly $310-375M/quarter. Actual repurchases were $59M in Q2 and ~$460M in H1 against a $3.0B authorization running January 2026 through December 2027. The authorization should not be modeled as a commitment: the binding constraints are the self-imposed CET1-including-AOCI range, capital consumed by loan growth, and an unresolved Basel re-proposal on which management explicitly deferred ("we'll evaluate that once we kind of get better clarity").

**Earnings surprise track record.** Over four quarters: one in-line, three misses (Q4 2025 −8.2%, Q3 2025 −0.9%, Q2 2026 −0.9%). The Q2 comparison pits GAAP $0.64 against a $0.65 estimate while adjusted EPS was $0.68, so there is basis ambiguity in the tracked series. On either basis, there is no cushion of positive surprise supporting the forward estimates.

## Growth Analysis

Management's FY2026 guidance: average loans and deposits up low single digits; NII +2.5% to 4%, "progressing toward the middle," with Q3 up ~2% sequentially and NIM exiting the year near 3.70%; adjusted non-interest income +3% to 5% but trending toward the low end; adjusted expense +1.5% to 3.5% with full-year positive operating leverage; net charge-offs 40-50bps; capital markets income $90-105M/quarter at the low end in Q3; deposit costs approximately flat in H2 at a constant funds rate.

That is a low-to-mid single digit revenue franchise. Consensus carries $2.63 for the current year and $2.84 for next, +8%, and the arithmetic of that step-up depends on three things beyond revenue: continued share count reduction, no reserve build, and expense discipline. Two of those are within management's control; the reserve is not, and it no longer contributes.

**The reserve arithmetic deserves emphasis.** The allowance ratio fell from 1.80% (Q2 2025) to 1.68% (Q1 2026) to 1.63% (Q2 2026), with the dollar allowance down $34M in Q2. H1 provision was $73M below charge-offs, worth roughly $0.07/share after tax at a 21% rate. The CFO said the equivalent CECL Day 1 coverage today is ~1.62% and that 1.63% "is indicative of where we'd expect to be absent new information over the next several quarters." Held flat, that $73M benefit does not recur, so 2027 growth requires revenue to carry more load than 2026 did. The partial offset is the CFO's hint that the 40-50bp charge-off range may be revised downward for 2027 given the portfolio mix shift — if the shift toward investment-grade C&I proves durable, a structurally lower loss rate would justify a lower steady-state reserve.

**Fee income is the weak half and it is levered to long rates in the wrong direction.** Q2 non-interest income of $630M was −2.5% y/y reported ($670M adjusted, +3.7%). Wealth management set a record at $150M (+12.8%) and deposit service charges grew 10.6% to $167M, but mortgage fell 31.3% to $33M, card and ATM was +0.8%, capital markets +1.2%, and securities losses were −$41M. Two lines are carrying the whole fee story. The balance sheet benefits from elevated long rates through fixed-asset repricing while the fee businesses are hurt by them — a partial internal hedge that also caps the revenue diversification management says it wants. Management attributes the mortgage and capital markets weakness to elevated long-term rates, and there is no visible near-term relief signaled for mortgage or permanent-financing capital markets activity.

**Volume signals are constructive but management is damping them.** Loan pipelines +15% y/y, line utilization +100bps in Q2, ~40% increase in new commercial logos in H1, small business checking production +7% YTD, wealth advisors hired over three years adding ~$6B of client assets on a ~$60B base, and JD Power #1 among regional banks in online banking satisfaction. Against that, the CFO explicitly cautioned: "some of that were draws that we saw late in the quarter. So we'd be cautious to extend too much of that into the second half... I wouldn't just extrapolate out what we've seen the first half." Full-year average loan growth of low single digits implies H2 average balances roughly flat to modestly above Q2's $98.7B.

**Estimate revisions** are the cleanest independent read and they are mixed-to-negative: current quarter −2.1% over both 30 and 90 days, next quarter −2.2%/−1.8%, current year +0.6%/+0.7%, next year −0.5%/−0.6%. There is no earnings inflection here, and the modest current-year upgrade is offset by out-year drift.

Total return +23%.

Total return −14.3%. That price is roughly where the stock traded in mid-March 2026 and above the $22.70 52-week low.

Ordinarily we treat consensus targets as lagging indicators on names with rising estimates and reasonable multiples — that logic does not apply here, because estimates are drifting down for the near quarters and sell-side ratings have migrated toward the sell side over three months. The target distribution is informative rather than stale.

The valuation score of 5.3 reflects a stock that is neither expensive nor cheap. A sub-peer earnings multiple with an above-peer yield is genuinely supportive, but there is no identifiable mispricing, no catalyst, and no margin of safety 3.2% below the 52-week high with the earnings tailwind exhausted. Cheapness alone is not a thesis.

## Competitive Landscape

The deposit franchise is the argument, and it is real. At $161B in assets: 30.4% non-interest-bearing mix, 1.69% interest-bearing deposit cost, 75.9% loans/deposits, 3.66% NIM, and a full falling-rate-cycle interest-bearing beta of 37% with management guiding to mid-30s in either direction. The source is a granular branch-and-relationship consumer base across lower-competition Southeastern markets, with Florida's 270 branches alone larger than most peers' single-state footprints. The CFO's framing was direct: "This is not something that we just accidentally have."

The trajectory is where we mark the score down from 7.0 to 6.5. Total deposits were −0.2% y/y and −0.9% sequentially while loans grew 2.6%, and the funding gap was closed with wholesale borrowings. Consumer lending declined 2.7% (residential first mortgage −2.6%, other consumer −6.9%). Outside wealth management and deposit service charges, every fee line was flat or down. The franchise is high quality and the commercial-banker and wealth-advisor hiring is producing measurable pipeline and logo growth, but the aggregate balance-sheet evidence shows a bank holding its position rather than clearly gaining.

The named threat is Southeast deposit competition from banks expanding into the region — raised directly on the call by Evercore. Management's response was that the pressure "has existed for 12 to 18 months" and that Regions doesn't "need it from a funding standpoint." That is credible at a 75.9% loan/deposit ratio, but it is the single most important claim to keep testing, because the entire NIM advantage rests on it. The 10-K's competition discussion also now emphasizes DeFi platforms, cryptocurrencies and blockchain-enabled payments/lending/custody alongside fintechs and technology entrants; management's answer is digital investment, with the JD Power rankings as evidence.

Two constraints limit Regions' participation in what the 10-K candidly describes as a permissive merger-approval environment: the core deposit conversion runs through 2027, and capital is being managed to 9.25%-9.75% CET1-including-AOCI with an unresolved rule pending. Management's stated position on inorganic activity is not available in the transcript we reviewed, so we do not assign a probability to Regions as either acquirer or target. The July 1 Frazer Lanier acquisition — a municipal securities investment banking firm, framed as restoring public-sector capability lost with the 2012 Morgan Keegan sale — was not quantified as to price, revenue or headcount and should not be valued.

## Risk Assessment

**Basel III re-proposal and the AOCI opt-out (highest-consequence unpriced item).** The original 2023 proposal would have removed the AOCI opt-out for Category III and IV firms and was not enacted; a re-proposal was expected in early 2026 and appears to have been issued, again contemplating AOCI inclusion. If enacted, the 10.7% headline CET1 converges toward the 9.5% AOCI-inclusive figure as a regulatory measure, roughly 100bps above the 8.0% effective minimum, with repurchase capacity as the shock absorber. Management's explicit deferral of buyback decisions pending the final rule identifies this as the live constraint. The precise status and content of the re-proposal are not independently confirmed here and should be verified before sizing the impact.

**Pending long-term debt / clean holding company rule.** Outstanding since August 2023. If adopted as proposed, eligible long-term debt of the greatest of 6% of RWA, 2.5% of total leverage exposure, or 3.5% of average assets would imply roughly $5.6-7.5B against ~$125B of derived RWA and ~$160B of assets. Regions has begun issuing; full compliance raises structural funding cost at a bank whose entire margin advantage rests on cheap deposits.

**Investor real estate / multifamily bridge concentration.** Investor real estate grew fastest of any category at +9.6% y/y, with management attributing part of that to bridge financing for maturing multifamily credits that cannot access permanent financing at current long rates. On the same call: "a little softness in multifamily in a couple of markets," slower absorption, and impaired refinance-ability into the permanent market, characterized as "nothing to be particularly concerned about today." The structure is what matters — Regions earns the bridge spread and holds the takeout risk in a market where the takeout is impaired by the same rate level that benefits its asset repricing. The book is modest against $99.2B of loans and overall investor RE credit metrics improved in the quarter, so this is a monitored exposure rather than a demonstrated problem — but it is where new risk is being added while everything else de-risks, against a 1.63% allowance with no release cushion left.

**2027 core deposit system conversion.** The new commercial lending platform went live in Q2; core deposit transformation is in testing with a pilot later in 2026 and full conversion in 2027. Core deposit conversions are among the highest-risk projects a bank undertakes, and a poor execution damages precisely the asset — deposit relationships and service quality — that justifies the valuation premium.

**CFO transition at the function that constitutes the moat.** An 8-K filed January 12, 2026 (Items 5.02/7.01/9.01) contains an exhibit named for a CFO retirement, amended by an 8-K/A on February 9. On the July 17 call, investor relations introduced "John and Anil," and all CFO-domain commentary — NII guidance, deposit betas, capital ratios, reserve philosophy — was delivered by "Anil." The successor's full identity could not be verified from primary sources. Regions' differentiation lives in hedge construction, deposit-pricing analytics and capital planning, and the guidance the market is underwriting (3.70% exit NIM, flat deposit costs, mid-30s beta, 9.25-9.75% capital range) is a new CFO's first full forecast set. An orderly, pre-announced succession is not a red flag; continuity in this specific function warrants monitoring.

**Litigation.** Shareholder derivative litigation relating to the September 2022 CFPB consent order on deposit account fee practices remains pending, with a Special Litigation Committee formed and the stay extended to November 2, 2026 — a dated near-term calendar item.

**Concentration.** 69.1% of branches in five states, with the 10-K carrying regional economic concentration as an explicit risk. Offsetting the risk stack: $69B of available liquidity covering uninsured deposits at ~181%, a Fitch upgrade of Regions Bank long-term deposits to A+ on May 12, 2026, a small HTM unrecognized loss (~$91M), and 2026 supervisory stress-test results in which management claims PPNR fully offset projected credit losses at 101.4% coverage (an issuer statement against a company-defined peer group, not independently verified).

Short interest of 8.6% of float and 5.3 days to cover is elevated for a large regional and worth noting as a modest contrarian marker; it is well short of squeeze-relevant levels.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (→ $37.50, +19.3% price / +23.1% total return)

The deposit franchise is a structural, not cyclical, asset: 30.4% non-interest-bearing mix and 1.69% interest-bearing cost produce a 3.66% NIM that peers cannot replicate, and near-symmetric ±1.2% NII sensitivity means Regions earns through either policy outcome. Credit has genuinely normalized — criticized business loans down from 7.22% to 5.01%, NPLs from 0.80% to 0.67%, $1.3B of office/trucking/communications exposure exited — and the CFO has hinted the 2027 charge-off range may be revised downward on mix. Commercial banker and wealth advisor hiring is converting: pipelines +15%, new logos +40%, $6B of added wealth client assets. If the Basel re-proposal is softened, capital deployment accelerates from $59M in Q2 toward $375M/quarter, and shrinks the share count materially. At 13x $2.88, still a discount to CFG's current multiple, the stock is $37.50, and AOCI accretion adds to tangible book alongside earnings.

### Bear Case (→ $25.75, −18.1% price / −14.3% total return)

The 20% ROATCE headline is roughly 17% on an AOCI-neutral equity base, so the multiple rests on a return that is good rather than exceptional. H1 earnings growth was partly non-recurring: $73M of provision below charge-offs (~$0.07/share) at an allowance now at 1.63% with the CFO stating that is the steady state. Deposits are flat to down while loans grow, and the gap is being funded with wholesale debt — short-term borrowings quadrupled to $3.0B plus a $1.5B senior note — so the loan/deposit ratio is climbing through 75.9%. Fee income is −2.5% y/y reported with mortgage −31%, and the full-year guide was cut to the low end. Estimates for the next two quarters have been trimmed ~2%. New credit risk is being added exactly where softness is appearing: multifamily bridge loans to sponsors who cannot refinance into an impaired permanent market. Overlay a Basel rule that removes the AOCI opt-out — compressing regulatory CET1 to ~9.5% and freezing repurchases — plus a 2027 core deposit conversion executed by a finance organization with a new CFO, and 10x a shaved $2.575 gives $25.75.

### Adjudication

The bull case requires a regulatory outcome Regions does not control and a buyback acceleration management has explicitly conditioned on that outcome. The bear case requires a credit event in a book that is currently improving. The base case — mid-single-digit revenue growth, ~8% consensus EPS growth partly funded by share count, a 46%-payout dividend, and a stable multiple — is the modal outcome, and it produces roughly 10% total return over twelve months from a price 3.2% below the 52-week high. Financials as a group have been a negative-alpha place to add without a distinct company-specific catalyst, and we do not identify one here.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold). Horizon: 12 months, with the next natural checkpoint the Q3 2026 print in mid-October 2026.**

- **Base case target: $33.50** — 11.8x FY2027 consensus $2.84, cross-checked against 2.28x a mid-2027 tangible book of ~$14.75. Upside +6.6% price, +10.4% total return with the $1.20 dividend.
- **Bull case target: $37.50** — 13.0x $2.88. Upside +19.3% price, +23.1% total return.
- **Bear case target: $25.75** — 10.0x $2.575. Downside −18.1% price, −14.3% total return.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price in the **$27.00-28.50** range on unchanged guidance, which would put the stock at ~9.7x FY2027 consensus with a 4.2-4.4% yield and restore the margin of safety absent today.
2. Basel re-proposal withdrawn or amended to preserve the Category IV AOCI opt-out, **combined with** repurchases running at or above $250M in a single quarter — this converts the authorization from optionality into deliverable yield and lifts total shareholder yield toward 9%.
3. Loans/deposits stable or declining below 75% for two consecutive quarters with core deposit growth resuming, confirming the H1 wholesale funding was bridge rather than structural.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Allowance ratio rebuilding above 1.75% or net charge-offs exceeding the 50bp guided ceiling, particularly if driven by investor real estate — this would convert the multifamily bridge exposure from a watch item into a demonstrated problem while removing the provision cushion.
2. NIM falling below 3.55% or interest-bearing deposit cost rising more than 10bps in a quarter at an unchanged funds rate, which would indicate Southeast deposit competition has begun to bite and invalidate the core competitive premise.
3. Adverse core-deposit conversion developments — a delayed pilot, a scope reduction, or an incremental expense charge — given that the project touches the franchise asset the valuation rests on.
4. A cut or suspension of the buyback attributed to capital constraints, which would signal the Basel outcome has gone against Regions and compress the total shareholder yield toward the dividend alone.

**Monitoring calendar:** Q3 2026 earnings (mid-October) for the exit NIM trajectory toward 3.70%, the loan/deposit ratio, buyback pace against the "pick up a bit" commitment, and any change to the 1.63% allowance; November 2, 2026 for the CFPB-related derivative litigation stay expiry; ongoing for Basel re-proposal finalization and any 8-K clarifying the CFO succession.

## Conclusion

Regions is a well-capitalized bank with a genuinely differentiated funding base, a normalized credit book, and a covered and growing dividend. None of that is in dispute, and the score reduction from 6.4 to 5.95 is not a statement that the business has deteriorated — the Q2 print was solid on margin, credit and capital, and the 13% dividend increase is a real return of value.

The reduction reflects three changes in the investment arithmetic since our May review. First, the stock rose 11.9% while base-case intrinsic value did not, compressing upside to roughly 6.6% on price and leaving no margin of safety 3.2% below the 52-week high. Second, the composition of 2026 earnings growth is now explicitly non-repeating: $73M of H1 provision below charge-offs at an allowance management says is already at steady state, which means 2027 needs revenue to carry load that provisioning carried this year, against a fee guide cut to the low end and near-quarter estimates trimmed ~2%. Third, the unpriced risk stack — a Basel re-proposal that management itself cites as gating capital return, a pending long-term-debt rule that would raise structural funding cost at a bank whose whole edge is cheap funding, a 2027 core deposit conversion, and multifamily bridge lending into an impaired takeout market — now sits under a full valuation rather than a discounted one.

At 12.0x this year and 11.1x next, a 3.82% forward yield at a 46% payout, and a realistic 7-9% total shareholder yield, existing holders are adequately compensated for holding through the October print. Prospective buyers should wait for either the $27-28.50 range or resolution of the Basel question with a demonstrated buyback acceleration. **HOLD.**
