## Executive Summary

Vistra Corp. has undergone a fundamental transformation from a leveraged merchant power generator into the second-largest U.S. independent nuclear operator with contracted 20-year hyperscaler PPAs. The Meta (2,609 MW) and AWS (1,200 MW) agreements convert the majority of Vistra's 6,448 MW nuclear fleet from commodity-exposed to utility-like contracted earnings, justifying a premium to traditional utility peers. The pending Cogentrix acquisition ($5,500 MW natural gas) will push total capacity above 50,000 MW.

The fundamental thesis is compelling: AI-driven power demand growth is real, nuclear is irreplaceable for 24/7 carbon-free baseload, and Vistra's fleet is among the most valuable in North America. Consensus FY2027 EPS estimates of $11.16 (rising +5.1% over 90 days) imply the stock trades at approximately 14x forward earnings on next-year numbers — superficially cheap for the growth trajectory. However, the trailing execution record (4 consecutive quarterly EPS misses, including a -77% miss in Q4 2025), $20.7B in gross debt (7.15x D/E), and the elevated IV term structure signaling near-term uncertainty collectively prevent upgrading to BUY at this price. The stock requires clean execution over the next 1-2 quarters to rebuild credibility against estimates.

The prior declining score trajectory (7.1 → 5.8 → 5.3) reflected legitimate concerns about earnings delivery and balance sheet strain. Today's reassessment stabilizes the trajectory: the structural growth thesis has strengthened (estimate revisions positive, PPAs signed, S&P upgrade achieved), but operational delivery remains unproven against forward expectations. A confirmed quarterly beat would be the clearest upgrade trigger.

## Company Overview

Vistra Corp. operates approximately 44,000 MW of generation capacity across a diversified fleet: 62% natural gas, 20% coal, 15% nuclear (6,448 MW across 6 units), and 3% solar/battery. The company serves approximately 5 million retail electricity customers across 18 states through brands including TXU Energy and Energy Harbor. The vertical integration of generation and retail creates a natural hedge against commodity price volatility.

The company operates primarily in three geographic segments:
- **Texas (46% of capacity):** ERCOT market, including Comanche Peak nuclear (2,400 MW)
- **East (51%):** PJM, ISO-NE, MISO, NYISO — includes Perry, Davis-Besse, and Beaver Valley nuclear plants (4,048 MW acquired via Energy Harbor merger)
- **West (3%):** California natural gas and battery storage

Key recent transactions: Energy Harbor merger (March 2024, +4,048 MW nuclear), Lotus acquisition (October 2025, +2,600 MW gas), pending Cogentrix acquisition (mid-late 2026, +5,500 MW gas for ~$3.8B enterprise value).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Profitability:**
Revenue grew from $13.7B (FY2022) to $17.7B (FY2025), a 29% increase driven by the Energy Harbor merger, rising wholesale power prices, and capacity market appreciation. Adjusted EBITDA reached $5.84B in FY2025, up 5.4% YoY — a more accurate profitability measure than GAAP net income ($944M), which was depressed by $808M in unrealized mark-to-market commodity hedging losses.

FY2025 GAAP operating income of $1.9B (vs. $4.1B in FY2024) similarly reflects non-cash hedge accounting, not operating deterioration.

**Cash Flow Generation:**
Operating cash flow was $4.07B in FY2025 ($4.56B in FY2024). Capital expenditures of $2.75B yielded approximately $1.3B in free cash flow. The company has returned $5.9B to shareholders via buybacks since October 2021, reducing share count by approximately 83 million shares (from ~422M to ~339M) — a ~20% reduction that meaningfully amplifies per-share value.

**Balance Sheet — High Leverage Requires Monitoring:**
Total debt of $20.7B represents 7.15x debt/equity. Interest expense rose 31% YoY to $1.18B. The Altman Z-Score of 0.76 is a mechanical artifact of the capital-intensive utility model rather than a genuine distress signal — S&P upgraded Vistra to investment grade (BBB-) in December 2025, and the company successfully priced $4.0B in senior notes in April 2026. Available liquidity was $2.78B as of year-end 2025.

The leverage is strategic: financing irreplaceable generation assets with 20+ year contracted cash flows. However, the Cogentrix acquisition will add approximately $1.5B in assumed debt plus $2.3B in cash consideration, pushing total leverage higher before EBITDA accretion occurs.

**Earnings Delivery Problem:**
The most concerning data point is 4 consecutive quarterly EPS misses: Q1 2025 (-242.5%), Q2 2025 (-42.5%), Q3 2025 (-17.6%), Q4 2025 (-77.2%). While these misses are substantially attributable to mark-to-market accounting noise (unrealized commodity hedge gains/losses flow through GAAP EPS), they have eroded market confidence in management's ability to deliver against consensus expectations. Until Vistra demonstrates a clean beat against analyst estimates, the stock will carry a credibility discount.

## Growth Analysis

**AI/Data Center Power Demand — The Core Growth Driver:**
The Meta PPA (2,609 MW, 20-year term) and AWS PPA (1,200 MW, 20-year term with extension options) effectively contract 59% of Vistra's nuclear fleet on multi-decade terms. At implied pricing of $50-75/MWh, these contracts could generate $2-4B in annual revenue over 20 years. Delivery begins Q4 2027 (AWS) and late 2026 (Meta existing units), with full delivery by 2032-2034 including uprates.

**Capacity Market Tailwinds:**
PJM capacity prices have increased to $333.44/MW-day (2027-28 delivery year), up from $269.92 just two years prior. ISO-NE prices are rising from $2.59 to $3.58/kW-month. These market-wide price signals benefit Vistra's entire fleet regardless of bilateral contracts.

**Wholesale Power Price Appreciation:**
PJM Western Hub average prices increased 48% YoY ($50.25 vs. $33.83/MWh). ISO-NE Massachusetts Hub rose 64% ($67.86 vs. $41.47/MWh). Nuclear and coal plants capture this upside without proportional fuel cost increases, driving margin expansion.

**Estimate Revisions:**
Current quarter EPS estimates rose +46.9% over 90 days ($1.27 → $1.87). Full-year FY2026 at $8.97 (+2.4% 90-day revision). FY2027 at $11.16 (+5.1% over 90 days). The revision trajectory is positive but not at the +30% annualized threshold that signals a strong BUY — particularly given the next quarter is declining (-10.3% over 90 days), suggesting near-term uncertainty around timing of PPA revenue recognition.

**Fleet Expansion:**
Post-Cogentrix, total capacity reaches ~50,000+ MW. Nuclear uprates of 433 MW (PJM) planned through 2034 will add incremental contracted capacity for the Meta PPA.

**Why the Discount Exists (and Why It May Be Warranted):**
1. **Execution credibility:** 4 consecutive misses create a persistent discount until proven otherwise. The market is applying a haircut to forward estimates.
2. **Earnings quality:** Heavy mark-to-market volatility makes GAAP earnings unreliable quarter-to-quarter. Investors demand compensation for this unpredictability.
3. **Leverage:** 7.15x D/E is materially above utility peer norms (typically 1.5-3.0x). Higher leverage = higher equity risk premium.
4. **Dividend yield:** 0.57% vs. peer median 3.3%. Income-seeking utility investors are absent from the shareholder base, reducing a traditional valuation floor.

**Analyst Targets:**
Consensus mean target of $228 implies 44% upside from current price. Low target of $97 reflects bear case with significant leverage-related downside. High target of $323 reflects full AI power thesis priced in. The wide $97-$323 range confirms high conviction paired with high uncertainty.

**Assessment:** The forward multiple is not stretched if estimates are achievable. The 4-miss streak and execution-dependent valuation create a "prove it" dynamic rather than an obvious mispricing. Valuation is reasonable but not compellingly cheap given the risk profile. A 4.5 score reflects that the discount exists for identifiable reasons (leverage, execution, earnings quality) that are partially but not fully justified.

## Competitive Landscape

**Nuclear Scarcity Value:** Only Constellation Energy operates a comparable U.S. independent nuclear fleet. New nuclear construction is effectively impossible on relevant timescales (10-15+ year permitting and construction). Vistra's 6,448 MW of nuclear capacity is irreplaceable — hyperscalers cannot build their own nuclear plants. This creates genuine pricing power in PPA negotiations.

**Scale Advantages:** At ~50,000 MW post-Cogentrix, Vistra will have the largest competitive generation fleet in the U.S. Scale provides portfolio optimization benefits (matching generation with retail load across geographies), lower cost of capital, and negotiating leverage with fuel suppliers and offtakers.

**Integrated Model:** The retail platform (5M customers) provides a natural hedge and customer relationship that pure-play generators lack. This integration reduces cash flow volatility and creates switching costs.

**Contracted Revenue Transformation:** The shift from 100% merchant to ~60% contracted nuclear (via Meta/AWS PPAs) fundamentally de-risks the business model. Few competitors can offer hyperscalers the combination of zero-carbon power, geographic diversity, existing interconnection, and multi-GW scale.

**Capacity market position:** Rising PJM and ISO-NE capacity prices benefit the entire fleet, particularly natural gas assets that serve as intermittent-resource backup.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Earnings Delivery Risk (HIGH):**
The divergent revision pattern (current quarter +46.9%, next quarter -10.3%) suggests estimate visibility remains poor. Management credibility is on the line at Q1 2026 earnings.

**2. Balance Sheet Risk (MODERATE-HIGH):**
Interest expense of $1.18B/year consumes 29% of operating cash flow. In a scenario where power prices decline (peace deal normalizing natural gas, demand growth disappointing), the leverage becomes problematic rather than strategic. Investment-grade rating provides access to refinancing, but the margin of safety is thin. Cogentrix will add approximately $1.5B in assumed debt before EBITDA accretion occurs.

**3. Data Center Demand Growth Uncertainty:**
Management explicitly flagged this as a risk: "more energy efficient AI solutions or slow adoption of AI products and services" could undermine the growth thesis. The 20-year PPAs mitigate this for contracted nuclear capacity, but uncontracted gas assets remain exposed to demand growth assumptions.

**4. Nuclear Operational Risk:**
433 MW of planned uprates require regulatory approvals and successful engineering over 8+ years. Nuclear PTC revenue ($765M in 2024-25) depends on Treasury interpretation that could change. Russian uranium supply risk exists, though contracted through 2030.

**5. Regulatory/Environmental:**
Coal retirements (Coleto Creek 2027, Miami Fort 2028), RGGI cap tightening, CCR remediation obligations, ERCOT market design changes.

## Conclusion

Vistra Corp. possesses one of the most valuable generation portfolios in North America — irreplaceable nuclear assets contracted to creditworthy hyperscalers on 20-year terms, complemented by a growing natural gas fleet benefiting from AI-driven power demand. The competitive position is strong and improving. Forward estimates imply a reasonable valuation at ~14x FY2027E relative to 20x utility peers.

The specific factor preventing a BUY recommendation is the 4-consecutive-quarter earnings miss record combined with $20.7B in debt at 7.15x D/E. This is a measurable execution credibility deficit. The market's discount to peer multiples (14x vs. 20x) exists because investors cannot yet trust that forward estimates will be achieved. Every quarter that misses, the discount persists or widens; a single clean beat begins to close it.

With Q1 2026 earnings imminent and Reuters already reporting a "swing to profit," the upgrade trigger may arrive within days. Until confirmed, HOLD at current levels with a growth-oriented outlook. The 19 analysts at BUY/Strong Buy vs. 1 at Sell confirms the structural thesis is consensus; execution timing is the variable.
