## Executive Summary

Everus is a specialty electrical and mechanical contractor that has, over roughly two years, become a data-center construction business with a legacy utility segment attached. Management says so explicitly in its filings. The Q2 2026 print on August 4 delivered records across revenue ($1,231.5M, +33.7%), net income ($83.9M, +58.9%), diluted EPS ($1.64, +59.2%), EBITDA ($128.6M, +52.7%) and backlog ($4.55B, +52.8%), and management raised FY2026 guidance for the second time — revenue from $4.3-4.4B to $4.5-4.7B and EBITDA from $345-360M to $410-425M, an 18% raise at the midpoint that excludes the pending Epsilon acquisition.

Two things changed materially versus our prior assessment. First, backlog inflected. The prior HOLD rested substantially on a flat-to-declining order book; E&M backlog is now $4,163.2M against $2,568.1M a year ago (+62.1%), and second-quarter bookings exceeded $2.0B against $1,231.5M of revenue, a ~1.62x book-to-bill. Second, the multiple compressed. Consensus FY2026 EPS rose 19.1% in 90 days to $5.37 while the share price is roughly flat over the same window, so the forward P/E fell from the low-30s to 25.7x — against a construction-and-engineering peer median trailing P/E of 28.4x. That combination — rising estimates, rising guidance, expanding backlog, falling multiple — is an improvement in risk/reward, not merely price action.

What has not improved, and in one case worsened, is the quality of the earnings base and the concentration of the customer base. Approximately 42-43% of full-year net income in both FY2024 and FY2025 came from cumulative-catch-up revisions to prior-period cost and contract estimates under percentage-of-completion accounting — $1.09 of Q2's $1.64 diluted EPS, and $1.67 of H1's $2.78. The largest single customer rose from below the 10% disclosure threshold in FY2024 to 18.3% of H1 2026 revenue and 17.0% of total trade receivables, and neither acquisition addresses that. Off-balance-sheet guarantee exposure grew 34% in six months to $858.3M, and maximum surety exposure of $827.3M now exceeds total stockholders' equity of $773.2M. We rate the stock ACCUMULATE: buy in stages, with the majority of capital reserved for weakness toward $115-125, and reassess on the Q3 print.

## Company Overview

Everus was spun off from MDU Resources on October 31, 2024 and operates 16 wholly owned operating companies from Bismarck, North Dakota, in two reportable segments.

**Electrical & Mechanical (82.0% of Q2 2026 revenue, $1,010.3M)** performs electrical and communication wiring and infrastructure, fire suppression, renewables infrastructure and mechanical piping across commercial, industrial, institutional, service and renewables end markets.

**Transmission & Distribution (18.5%, $227.5M)** performs overhead and underground electrical, gas and communication infrastructure work plus transportation lighting, and includes Wagner Smith Equipment, a small tool manufacturer.

The model is labor- and execution-intensive rather than capital-intensive: net PP&E was $186.9M against $2,057.7M of total assets at June 30, and FY2026 gross capex is guided at $90-100M, or 1.9-2.2% of forecast revenue. The majority of contracts have original durations under one year, so backlog turns quickly — total backlog of $4.55B is roughly 0.99x guided FY2026 revenue, with 80.0% ($3,641.5M) scheduled inside 12 months.

Growth is narrow. Within E&M in Q2, commercial revenue rose 48.3% to $753.2M (data center and hospitality) and industrial rose 91.0% to $147.7M (high-tech and manufacturing), together 89% of segment revenue. Institutional fell 21.1%, renewables fell 29.5%, and T&D's transportation end market fell 18.0%. T&D revenue grew 7.1% with backlog *down* 5.3% year over year. Investors seeking a grid-capex expression should note that the utility segment is the one not growing; all backlog growth is E&M, and functionally that means data centers.

### The estimate-revision engine

This is the single most important item in Everus's financial statements. Under cost-to-cost percentage-of-completion, changes in project cost or contract-value estimates flow through the current period as a cumulative catch-up. Everus discloses the dollar impact:

| Period | Gross profit impact | EPS impact | Reported EPS | Share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q2 2026 | +$75.7M | +$1.09 | $1.64 | 66% |
| H1 2026 | +$115.2M | +$1.67 | $2.78 | 60% |
| H1 2025 | +$73.6M | +$1.05 | $1.75 | 60% |
| FY2025 | +$113.7M | +$1.64 | ~$3.95 | ~42% |
| FY2024 | +$83.4M | +$1.21 | ~$2.81 | ~43% |

Three observations follow. First, the benefit is heavily first-half weighted: subtracting H1 from FY2025 implies an H2 2025 contribution of roughly $0.59 of EPS, or ~27% of H2 EPS versus ~60% in H1. Second, on a full-year basis the ratio has been remarkably stable at ~42-43% across two years, which argues against escalating aggressiveness. Third, roughly 40-45% of Q2's gross-margin expansion traces to a higher *intensity* of favorable revisions rather than execution: the revision impact equalled 6.15% of revenue in Q2 2026 versus 5.33% a year earlier (+0.82 points) against total gross-margin expansion of 1.9 points. Stripping the revision contribution entirely gives a crude ex-revision margin of 8.75% versus 7.67%, still 1.08 points of underlying improvement. That is a sensitivity, not a normalized margin — a steady-state percentage-of-completion contractor always carries some revision benefit — but the direction is clear: the underlying improvement is real and roughly half the size of the reported improvement.

Concentration within the revisions matters too. In Q2, individual projects each contributing more than $1.0M of impact supplied +$47.0M of gross profit and +$0.68 of EPS, so a majority of the benefit came from a small number of large jobs. Unfavorable revisions already run at 1.3% of revenue, attributed to labor sequencing inefficiencies and project delays. The mechanism is symmetric.

### Cash flow: a growth-funded float

H1 2026 operating cash flow was $196.8M against $32.5M a year earlier; free cash flow $167.0M against $6.5M. Composition matters. Contract liabilities, net contributed +$98.9M of operating cash — customers billing and paying ahead of performance — moving the net contract position from a $49.3M net liability at year-end to a $128.7M net liability at June 30. Receivables simultaneously consumed $127.9M.

This is negative working capital driven by booking growth, not a durable cash characteristic. FY2025 makes the point: free cash flow of $100.0M on net income of $201.8M is 49.6% conversion, and FCF *fell* from $128.8M in FY2024 while net income rose 41%. A defensible normalized figure is LTM FCF of roughly $260.5M (FY2025 $100.0M + H1 2026 $167.0M − H1 2025 $6.5M), about 102% of LTM net income of $254.5M — but even that carries the contract-liability distortion, and H2 capex will be heavier ($90-100M guided full year against $35.6M spent in H1). Annualizing H1 FCF to $334M would be a serious error. If bookings flatten, the float stops growing and conversion reverts toward FY2025's ~50%.

### Balance sheet

At June 30: cash and restricted cash $176.7M ($157.4M unrestricted); gross debt $277.5M, entirely a term loan due 10/31/29 at a 5.73% weighted average, amortizing $15.0M annually; revolver $225.0M with zero drawn and $222.8M available; net debt $120.1M; net leverage 0.3x LTM EBITDA of $391.3M against a 3.00x covenant; stockholders' equity $773.2M; current ratio 1.59x; Altman Z 3.92. Maturities are trivial until $240.0M in 2029.

Derived returns are strong: LTM ROE ~36%, ROA ~13%, and estimated ROIC of ~30% (LTM EBIT $356.5M taxed at 25.9% against invested capital of ~$893M). The invested-capital base now includes $282.5M of goodwill and intangibles that did not exist a year ago. Note that screens reporting 1.74 "debt-to-equity" are using total liabilities to equity; actual gross debt to equity is 0.36x.

All debt is floating (Term SOFR + 2.00-2.75%). A 100bp increase raises interest expense ~$2.7M over twelve months, and the company notes that borrowing for Epsilon adds to that exposure.

## Growth Analysis

The forward evidence is the strongest part of the case, and it is management-attested rather than accounting-derived.

**Guidance raised twice.** Revenue $4.3-4.4B → $4.5-4.7B; EBITDA $345-360M → $410-425M, excluding Epsilon. The EBITDA raise is 18% at the midpoint. This is a forward operating number, not a cumulative-catch-up artifact.

**Backlog.** Total $4,551.6M versus $2,978.2M a year earlier (+52.8%) and $3,228.3M at year-end (+41% in six months). E&M $4,163.2M (+62.1%); T&D $388.4M (−5.3%). Two definitional cautions: Everus's backlog definition includes letters of intent, notices to proceed and probable change orders and claims, so it exceeds ASC 606 remaining performance obligations of $3.95B by $602M (13%); and deferred revenue of $575.7M is billings ahead of performance, not backlog. The company itself warns backlog "should not be relied upon as a standalone indicator."

**The implied H2 is conservative.** Guidance implies H2 revenue of $2,231.5-2,431.5M (+11.7% to +21.7%) and H2 EBITDA of $192.5-207.5M at an 8.53-8.63% margin — below H1's 9.6% and below H1's EBITDA dollars. The revision seasonality explains most of this: revisions supplied ~$1.05 of H1 2025 EPS versus ~$0.59 in H2 2025. Adjusting for two full quarters of SE&M (~$67M of revenue) that H2 2025 lacked, the organic H2 guide is closer to +8% to +18%. We read this as consistent with historical seasonality plus conservatism rather than as a deterioration signal, but it does mean the H1 margin trajectory is not extrapolable.

**Estimate revisions.** Current-year consensus $5.37 (+16.2% in 30 days, +19.1% in 90 days); next year $5.58 (+9.2%, +15.2%). Revision velocity of this magnitude alongside 30%+ revenue growth is normally a strong forward signal, and the usual guardrail — a premium multiple already discounting the beats — does not bind here at 25.7x forward. Two qualifications specific to this name: coverage is thin (2-3 analysts on near-term periods, 5 on next year), and the next-year figure of $5.58 implies only +3.9% growth over FY2026, which looks stale against a 53% backlog build and probably does not fully reflect Epsilon.

**Epsilon Industries (agreed 7/31/26, not yet closed).** $295M cash for a business expected to generate ~$250M of CY2026 revenue at low-double-digit EBITDA margin: 9.1-11.8x EV/EBITDA (midpoint ~10.3x) and 1.18x EV/revenue, against Everus's own ~17.1x forward EV/EBITDA. Epsilon designs and manufactures modular mechanical and electrical building infrastructure — off-site prefabrication — with U.S. and Canadian plants, 50+ engineers and 120+ skilled trades, and extends reach into Florida, Texas, the Mid-Atlantic and Northeast. Strategically this attacks the industry's binding constraint (skilled craft labor) and should improve cost-estimate reliability over time.

One modelling caution: the company describes Epsilon as "financially cash accretive," not EPS-accretive. Using SE&M as precedent (86% of purchase price landed in goodwill and intangibles, including a short-lived backlog intangible amortized over 1.8 years), first-year intangible amortization plus ~$16.8M of interest on $295M at ~5.7% could offset most or all of Epsilon's EBITDA contribution at the GAAP line, turning positive from year two as the backlog intangible rolls off. Pro-forma net leverage is ~0.99x — ample against covenant — but liquidity narrows more than leverage does: funding $295M from $157.4M of unrestricted cash plus revolver would leave roughly $12M of cash and ~$73M of revolver availability before H2 cash generation. That constrains further M&A until the balance sheet rebuilds.

The data feed reports "forward P/E" of 28.02 identical to trailing P/E; both appear computed on LTM EPS of roughly $4.92, so neither is a forward multiple. We use consensus and our own build instead.

**Peer context.** Construction & Engineering comparables show a median trailing P/E of 28.4x with wide dispersion: ACA 28.4x, FLR 22.8x, MYRG 46.8x, TTEK 16.6x, GVA 37.5x. ECG at 27.7x trailing and 25.7x forward sits at or modestly below the median while growing revenue 30%+ organically — faster than any of these five. The set is heterogeneous (TTEK is consulting-weighted, FLR is large-project E&C, GVA is heavy civil), so this is directional rather than precise. Corroborating evidence that the multiple reflects sector conditions rather than a company-specific premium: the FY2025 proxy shows Everus's TSR index at 163.0 against a selected peer index of 159.04 — the peer set re-rated with it.

**What the market may be mispricing.** The stock is 19.7% below its May 6 high of $171.58 and traded as low as $109.17 on July 29, nine days before this report. Over that same window, backlog grew 41% in six months, guidance was raised 18% on EBITDA, and current-year estimates rose 16% in a month. The compression from roughly 31-34x forward to 25.7x on rising numbers is the specific inefficiency we are underwriting. On FY2026 EPS growth of ~36% against FY2025's $3.95, the PEG is roughly 0.7.

**What argues against it.** A 17x EV/EBITDA multiple on a ~10% EBITDA-margin, short-cycle, sub-1.0x-backlog-coverage contractor is not cheap in absolute terms, and the earnings base is lower-quality than an equivalent multiple elsewhere because ~42% of it derives from management cost re-estimates. The next-year consensus implies almost no growth, and while we think that number is stale, we cannot verify what the five contributing analysts assume about revision normalization.

**Analyst targets.** Mean $182, median $180, range $172-$200 — even the lowest published target sits 24.8% above spot, and recommendation mix improved to 4 buy / 2 hold from 3/3. On a name where the multiple has compressed against rising estimates, we treat these as corroborating rather than driving the case, and note the tight clustering suggests thin, correlated coverage rather than six independent views.

**Our build.** FY2027: backlog of $4.55B with 80% converting inside twelve months plus continued bookings and a full year of Epsilon supports revenue of ~$5.7B. At a 9.5% EBITDA margin (H1 2026 actual 9.6%, guided H2 8.6%) that is $542M; less ~$70M D&A, ~$25M net interest, taxed at 25.9%, gives net income of ~$331M and EPS of ~$6.45 on 51.3M shares. At 25x — roughly the current forward multiple and below the peer median — base value is **$160**.

Valuation score of 6.0: modest positive asymmetry, with the discount to peers real but the earnings quality genuinely inferior.

## Competitive Landscape

Everus competes in what its own MD&A calls a "highly fragmented" industry with "thousands of construction services providers," on "technical expertise, service pricing, financial and operational resources, track record for safety, industry reputation and dependability." There are no patents, licenses or switching costs. The defensible advantages are bonding capacity and balance-sheet capability to be trusted with very large projects, union labor access at scale, and — increasingly — off-site prefabrication capacity.

On trajectory, which matters more than level: Everus is taking share in the fastest-growing pocket of nonresidential construction (E&M commercial +48%, industrial +91%, E&M backlog +62%) and is vertically integrating into the constraint via Epsilon. The pivot from pure field contractor to partly integrated manufacturer should raise margins (Epsilon's low-double-digit EBITDA margin exceeds Everus's 9.6% H1 consolidated) and improve estimate reliability, which is a second-order benefit given how much of reported earnings rides on those estimates.

Against that: the moat is thin, the peer group re-rated identically, T&D is not growing, and manufacturing introduces fixed plant, inventory and utilization risk that a variable craft workforce does not carry. A data-center capex pause is harder to flex out of an owned factory than out of a union labor pool. Epsilon's end markets include data center, so the acquisition deepens rather than dilutes theme concentration.

Management's stated demand thesis is "high tech reshoring, data center construction and utility infrastructure investments," and the results validate the first two while the utility leg is not delivering for Everus. Data-center construction demand is the exact exposure that would reverse fastest on any AI-capex disappointment.

Competitive position 6.3: improving trajectory in the growth pocket, no structural moat, and the sector-beta component of the re-rating is large.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-customer concentration, worsening.** Largest customer as a share of consolidated revenue: none above 10% in FY2024 → 15.5% (H1 2025) → ~17% (FY2025) → **18.3% (H1 2026)**, 17.6% in Q2. As a share of total trade receivables: 16.3% at year-end → **17.0%** at June 30. Top-10 customers went from 33% of FY2024 revenue to 43% of FY2025. The customer is not named in any filing; its identity, credit quality and capex durability are unknown. A separate T&D customer was 16.9% of that segment's Q2 revenue. Neither acquisition addresses this — SE&M and Epsilon add geography, capability and end-market access, and every direct concentration metric, including the receivables measure that is hardest to manage, moved the wrong way. Do not read end-market diversification as customer diversification.

**Data-center theme concentration, flagged by management.** The 10-Q Overview states that while management believes demand persists, "we cannot guarantee that will be the case. The loss of, or reduction in business from, these types of contracts could have a material adverse effect." Two readings are available — genuine disclosure that management does not underwrite its primary growth driver, or standard defensive drafting. Either way, roughly 89% of E&M revenue is commercial plus industrial.

**Earnings dependence on management estimates.** ~42-43% of full-year net income, ~60% of H1 EPS. Reported GAAP EPS is structurally unforecastable by outsiders, which is why the four-quarter beat record (39.2%, 77.8%, 41.1%, 42.4%) should be discounted heavily as a quality signal — those magnitudes are an artifact of the cumulative-catch-up line, not evidence of superior forecasting or deliberate sandbagging. The economically relevant test is whether the *annual* revision-to-net-income ratio holds near 42%.

**Off-balance-sheet guarantees exceeding equity.** Fixed maximum amounts guaranteed under subsidiary performance and lease guarantees rose from $641.1M to $858.3M in six months (+34%). Surety bonds outstanding $2.31B with maximum payment exposure $827.3M, against equity of $773.2M. Everus indemnifies the surety for expenses it incurs, so the residual risk is Everus's. These are theoretical maxima with no losses to date, and comparable disclosures exist at every large specialty contractor — but bonding availability is a real constraint on growth rate, as the 10-K states.

**Disputed receivable.** ~$31.3M withheld by a customer since October 2023 on a time-and-materials contract with no stated maximum price; revenue recognized, lien filed, unresolved 2.75 years later. Full write-off would be ~$0.45 per diluted share. Claim positions excluded from transaction price rose to $38.3M from $25.8M in six months, though unexecuted change orders *included* in transaction price halved to $36.0M from $69.9M and recorded loss provisions are only $2.1M.

**Auditor change, unresolved.** Item 4.01 8-Ks dated 1/21/26 and 3/18/26 could not be read. The identity of predecessor and successor firms and, critically, whether any disagreements or reportable events under Item 304(a)(1)(iv)-(v) were disclosed, are unknown. A routine post-spin auditor engagement is the most likely explanation and an 8-K/A most commonly adds the former accountant's Exhibit 16.1 letter — but those are inferences about common practice, not evidence about these filings. Given that ~42% of net income derives from contract estimates, an auditor transition accompanied by disagreements over estimate accounting would change the earnings-quality assessment materially. We treat this as an open verification gap and it is the primary reason risk factors score below 5.

**Other.** All debt floating; insurance cost inflation disclosed (wildfire-driven carrier losses, standalone post-spin coverage); skilled-labor and multiemployer pension withdrawal exposure; SE&M excluded from the internal-control assessment perimeter for its first year; fixed-price contracts now 50.9% of revenue (down from 59% in FY2024), which reduces overrun risk but caps upside.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 60.5% against 61.7% one-year realized vol is fair (−1.3pp spread), with a balanced put/call skew of −0.7% and an implied move of ±9.5% on the August 21 expiry. IV fell from 73.6% to 60.5% between July 19 and August 7 while the price rose 3.4% — a post-earnings vol crush consistent with an event resolved rather than a new one priced. Term structure is in contango (60.5% near-term versus 65.9% at six months), which the data characterizes as a normal-to-complacent structure; put/call open interest of 0.35 is call-tilted, and put/call volume is 1.00. Short interest is 4.5% of float at 2.6 days to cover — uncrowded, with no squeeze or forced-cover dynamics either way.

The takeaway: the options market prices no near-term stress and mild residual premium at longer tenors. This neither confirms nor contradicts the fundamental case, but the absence of near-term put demand means positioning is not offering a cheap hedge against the concentration risks, and the 60%+ vol level argues for staged rather than single-tranche entry.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $200 (+45.1%)

Backlog of $4.55B with 80% executing inside twelve months converts at or above guided margins as data-center bookings continue. FY2027 revenue reaches ~$6.1B including a full year of Epsilon; EBITDA margin reaches 10.2% as prefabrication scales and shop-based work displaces variable field labor, giving $622M of EBITDA and ~$7.60 of EPS. Epsilon proves the M&A engine — bought at ~10x against a 17x own multiple, with the seller's leadership retained — and Everus becomes a serial consolidator of prefab capacity at accretive multiples. Estimate revisions continue at the current pace and the multiple holds at ~26x, matching peer median. The prefab pivot also raises cost-estimate reliability, gradually reducing the earnings-quality discount investors apply.

### Bear Case — $105 (−23.8%)

Hyperscaler capex decelerates or a frontier-lab funding stumble strands commitments; bookings fall below 1.0x book-to-bill in two consecutive quarters. The contract-liability float stops expanding and reverses, taking free cash conversion back toward FY2025's 50% just as $295M of Epsilon consideration has consumed most of the revolver and cash. The revision mechanism inverts — unfavorable revisions already run at 1.3% of revenue — and FY2027 EPS lands near $4.70 on $5.0B of revenue at an 8.5% margin. The multiple compresses to ~22x on lower earnings quality and visibility. The largest customer, at 18.3% of revenue and 17.0% of receivables, reduces its program; the $31.3M dispute is written off; an owned Epsilon factory carries fixed cost through the slowdown. The stock traded at $109 on July 29 and $69.61 within the last twelve months, so this path requires no imagination.

### Base Case — $160 (+16.1%)

FY2026 delivers within guidance ($4.6B revenue, $417.5M EBITDA, ~$5.35 EPS ex-Epsilon). FY2027 revenue reaches ~$5.7B at a 9.5% EBITDA margin, giving $542M of EBITDA and ~$6.45 of EPS after ~$70M D&A and ~$25M interest at a 25.9% tax rate. Epsilon closes in Q3 2026, is roughly GAAP-neutral in year one on amortization and interest, and turns accretive in FY2027. The revision-to-net-income ratio normalizes toward the stable ~42% annual level rather than the ~60% H1 level. The multiple holds at 25x, modestly below the 28.4x peer median, reflecting the earnings-quality and concentration discount. Probability-weighted at 50% base / 20% bull / 30% bear, expected return is approximately **+10%**.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: ACCUMULATE.** Positive expected risk-adjusted return with an identified catalyst set, but the margin of safety is below the BUY bar because the earnings base is ~42% dependent on unforecastable management estimates, the bear case is −24%, and realized volatility above 60% argues for staged entry.

- **Base case target: $160** — 25x FY2027 EPS of ~$6.45 (revenue $5.7B, 9.5% EBITDA margin, $70M D&A, $25M interest, 25.9% tax, 51.3M shares). **+16.1%** from $137.86.
- **Bull case: $200** — 26x FY2027 EPS of ~$7.60. **+45.1%.** Coincides with the highest published analyst target.
- **Bear case: $105** — 22x FY2027 EPS of ~$4.72 on a data-center booking pause. **−23.8%.**
- **Timeframe:** 12-18 months, anchored on the Q3 2026 print (early November), Epsilon closing in Q3 2026, and the FY2026 10-K with FY2027 guidance (late February 2027).

**Staged-entry plan.** Establish one-third to one-half of a target position now: the backlog inflection, the 18% guidance raise and the compression to 25.7x forward are established facts, and waiting for full resolution of the earnings-quality question means waiting for a full fiscal year. Reserve the balance for the **$115-125** range, which the stock visited nine trading days ago and which corresponds to ~19-21x our FY2027 base EPS. Given ±9.5% implied moves on near-dated expiries and 61.7% realized vol, this range is a realistic rather than aspirational entry.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. The auditor-change 8-Ks are confirmed to disclose no disagreements or reportable events under Item 304(a)(1)(iv)-(v), removing the single largest unresolved earnings-quality overhang.
2. Q3 2026 shows book-to-bill above 1.2x with the largest-customer share of revenue *and* trade receivables both declining, evidencing that Epsilon and SE&M are broadening the counterparty base rather than only the geography.
3. Price at or below $125 with FY2027 consensus above $6.00.

**Downgrade to HOLD or REDUCE on any of:**
1. Book-to-bill below 1.0x for two consecutive quarters, or total backlog declining sequentially — the float that produced H1's $167M of free cash flow is a direct function of booking growth.
2. Unfavorable cumulative-catch-up revisions exceeding 2.0% of quarterly revenue, or the annual revision-to-net-income ratio exceeding ~50% (evidence of escalating dependence rather than steady state).
3. Largest-customer revenue share exceeding 22%, or disclosure of a reduction in that customer's program.
4. Post-Epsilon liquidity — unrestricted cash plus revolver availability — below $100M at year-end 2026 without a demonstrated H2 free-cash-flow recovery.
5. FY2026 EBITDA guidance reduced, or FY2027 initial guidance implying EBITDA margin below 9.0%.

**Reassessment date:** Q3 2026 earnings, expected early November 2026, with an interim check on the Epsilon closing 8-K and purchase-price allocation.

## Conclusion

Everus has delivered the operational quarter the bull case required. Revenue grew 33.7%, backlog grew 52.8% with a 1.62x book-to-bill, guidance was raised 18% on EBITDA, and the balance sheet remains genuinely conservative at 0.3x net leverage with ~30% ROIC. The multiple has compressed from the low-30s forward to 25.7x while consensus estimates rose 19% in 90 days — an improvement in risk/reward that reverses the specific negative (a flat-to-declining order book at a 31-34x multiple) that anchored our prior HOLD. Epsilon, bought at ~10x EBITDA against Everus's own ~17x and aimed at the industry's binding labor constraint, is the most credible capital-allocation decision management has made since the spin.

The reasons this is not a full-position BUY are specific and unresolved. Roughly 42% of annual net income and 60% of first-half EPS derive from cumulative-catch-up revisions to management's own project cost estimates, which means the reported margin expansion is roughly half as large as it appears and the four-quarter record of 39-78% earnings beats carries almost no information about execution quality. Single-customer dependence rose to 18.3% of revenue and 17.0% of trade receivables and neither acquisition addresses it. Off-balance-sheet guarantee exposure grew 34% in six months to a level exceeding book equity. And an auditor change whose Item 304 disclosures we could not verify sits directly on top of the one accounting line that drives the earnings.

Buy in stages. Take an initial position on the confirmed backlog and guidance inflection at a below-peer multiple, hold capital back for the $115-125 range that this stock visits regularly, and let the Q3 print and the auditor-change resolution determine whether the position becomes a full one.
