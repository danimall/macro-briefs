## Executive Summary

QuinStreet buys consumer traffic from Google, social platforms, third-party publishers and its own websites, qualifies and matches that traffic against advertiser demand, and sells clicks, leads, calls and completed customers to auto-insurance carriers and agents, card issuers, banks, personal-loan providers and home-improvement contractors. Media cost sits inside cost of revenue, which is why gross margin was 11.9% in fiscal Q3 2026 and 10.4% for the nine months. The business is a media arbitrageur with a technology and data overlay, and the operating leverage above and below media cost drives the results: a modest improvement in blended media yield flows through hard to EBITDA, and a modest deterioration does the reverse.

The FY2026 numbers are strong at the headline and much narrower underneath. Nine-month revenue of $919.8M grew 10.6% (5.9% excluding HomeBuddy), but that splits into an essentially flat first half (H1 revenue $573.7M, +2.1%; derived H1 adjusted EBITDA ~$38.7M against $39.7M a year earlier) and a Q3 that grew 28.3% headline, 13.8% organic, with adjusted EBITDA of $29.6M (+53%). Pro-forma nine-month growth including HomeBuddy in both periods was roughly 8%. Reported GAAP net income of $62.1M is 77% a non-cash valuation-allowance release; underlying pre-tax income was $14.2M on $919.8M of revenue, and cash taxes paid were $0.5M. Guidance for fiscal Q4 of $350-370M revenue and $37-43M adjusted EBITDA implies a 10.6-11.6% margin, a 200-300bp sequential jump on only 1.1-6.9% sequential revenue growth.

The score trajectory has been 4.9 (March) → 7.0 → 6.8 (late May), with two consecutive BUYs, and the last call has worked: the shares are up roughly 25% from the $12.43 reference in late May. What has changed is price and estimates, not the business. Over 90 days, FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS was cut 4.0% to $1.21, FY2027 4.8% to $1.52, and the about-to-be-reported quarter 11.1% to $0.44. EV/FY2026E adjusted EBITDA has moved from roughly 7x to 8.7x, and to 13.2x on EBITDA less the $36M annualized stock-compensation charge that both the adjusted metric and the executive compensation plan exclude. Fiscal Q4 and full-year results are imminent, the options market prices a ±15.1% move on the nearest expiry with near-term implied volatility at 83.5% against 46.2% realized, and consensus adjusted EPS of $0.44 appears to require the upper end of management's own EBITDA range. We move to HOLD: the 12-month growth thesis is intact, the entry point is not.

## Company Overview

Single reportable segment, revenue reported by client vertical: Financial Services (auto insurance dominant, plus credit cards, banking/deposits and AmOne Financial in personal loans, debt settlement and credit repair) and Home Services & Other (the Modernize brand plus HomeBuddy, acquired 1/2/2026). In fiscal Q3 2026 the mix was 67% financial services and 33% home services, versus 74%/26% a year earlier.

The cited moat is the QuinStreet Rating Platform, which integrates carrier rate data so consumers can be matched against accurate pricing; machine-learning bid and match optimization developed since 2008; proprietary client and media-source integrations; and a data asset accumulated across billions of dollars of media spend. Founded 1999, Delaware-reincorporated 2009, Douglas Valenti CEO throughout — a 27-year founder tenure disclosed as a key-person risk with no successor named. Asset-light, with offices in the U.S., India, Mexico and now Switzerland, and infrastructure at two third-party colocation facilities.

Two structural features matter more than the narrative. First, there is no backlog: contract liabilities (client deposits) were $1.2M at 3/31/26. Revenue is bid and sold continuously, so a demand air pocket arrives immediately and without contractual buffer — precisely what produced cumulative GAAP losses of $100.7M across FY2022-FY2025 when carrier loss ratios blew out. Second, the largest single client was 22% of nine-month revenue, 24% of Q3 revenue and 23% of net receivables. That client is not named in any filing.

The named competitors are LendingTree and MediaAlpha. The more consequential relationship is with the platforms: Google is simultaneously the largest media supply source and, in aggregate, a competitor for the advertiser budgets QuinStreet intermediates.

## Financial Analysis

**Nine months FY2026, as filed:** revenue $919.8M; gross profit $96.0M (10.4%); operating income $16.3M (1.8%); pre-tax income $14.2M (1.5%); income tax benefit $(47.9)M; net income $62.1M; diluted EPS $1.07.

The tax line requires care in both directions. Releasing the valuation allowance on the majority of federal and state deferred tax assets (gross release $61.2M) required management and auditors to conclude realization was more likely than not — an auditor-tested statement about sustained future taxable income, and a genuine positive signal. It also means 77% of reported nine-month net income is a one-time non-cash entry, that the trailing P/E of 13.8x quoted in market data screens is computed on approximately $1.13 of tax-inflated GAAP EPS and is not a meaningful multiple, and that the cash tax rate rises from $0.5M paid in nine months as net operating losses are consumed. That is a future free-cash-flow headwind absent from every trailing metric. (Note also that the supplied profile field showing $0.08 EPS is inconsistent with the same profile's 13.84 P/E; neither should be used.)

**Cash flow.** Nine-month operating cash flow was $78.2M against $55.1M a year earlier; capex $2.6M and capitalized software $8.1M give company-definition free cash flow of $67.4M. Two adjustments matter. Working capital contributed a net +$13.7M (payables +$25.9M, accruals +$30.4M, receivables -$44.9M); normalizing that leaves roughly $53.7M. In a business scaling media spend, payables and accruals growth flatters cash conversion and reverses when growth stalls. Receivables grew 36% (to $184.8M) against 10.6% revenue growth — partly HomeBuddy consolidation and the Q3 step-up, but the revenue reserve rose from $1.0M to $2.9M and total allowances from $1.9M to $3.8M, which is worth tracking as a lead-quality or client-credit indicator. Add back that $27.2M of the operating cash flow is non-cash stock compensation that did not reduce share count, and the underlying cash generation is good but less than the headline.

**Balance sheet transformation.** Total liabilities to equity moved from 0.77x at 6/30/25 to 1.27x at 3/31/26. The company drew $70M on a new $150M five-year MUFG revolver (SOFR + 200-275bp, maturing 1/2/2031, secured by first-priority liens on substantially all assets, $80M undrawn, in compliance at 3/31/26) and carries $85.9M of post-closing and contingent acquisition obligations. Goodwill rose to $261.8M — 84% of $310.4M book equity — and intangibles to $71.5M, leaving tangible book value at negative $22.9M. Net debt on management's definition (cash $102.0M less revolver $70.0M less seller obligations $85.9M) is $53.9M, which reconciles to the $54M cited on the call and is roughly 0.5x derived FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of ~$108M.

Solvency risk is low. Flexibility is materially lower than a year ago, and the Q3 10-Q added a new risk factor that did not exist in the FY2025 10-K, disclosing a "significant amount of debt" and limited remaining collateral for additional financing. Approximately $85.9M of the next four years' free cash flow is pre-committed to sellers: $75M of HomeBuddy Anniversary Payments in four equal $18.75M annual installments beginning January 2027 (carried at $64.9M with accretion running through interest expense), plus an uncapped AquaVida earn-out carried at $18.2M, up from roughly $2.1M at initial recognition, based on a percentage of margin over a four-year measurement period. The earn-out grows because the acquired business is outperforming, which is good news about the asset and a growing cash claim that adjusted EBITDA excludes.

Returns on capital are thin. FY2025: net margin 0.43%, ROE 1.9%, ROA 1.1%, asset turnover 2.54x. Any Altman Z or debt/equity figure sourced from the FY2025 10-K predates HomeBuddy and the $70M draw and understates current leverage.

**Financial health score: 6.0** (down from 7.0). Modest net leverage, an undrawn $80M revolver and real cash generation support the mid-range; negative tangible book, an all-asset lien, working-capital-flattered operating cash flow, a rising cash tax rate and pre-committed seller cash argue against a 7.

## Growth Analysis

The sequence is FY2025 +78% (off a depressed FY2024 base of $613.5M, driven by carriers returning to rate adequacy), H1 FY2026 +2.1%, Q3 FY2026 +28.3% headline and +13.8% organic. HomeBuddy contributed $39.1M of the $76.3M Q3 revenue increase. The acceleration is two quarters old and partly purchased.

In fairness to the bull case, H1 comparisons were brutal — Q2 FY2025 grew 130% with auto insurance up 615% — so flat H1 growth is not deterioration, and Q3's 13.8% organic against a normalized base is genuinely good. Management's Q4 guide implies +33.6% to +41.2% year over year, and the derived FY2026 full year is approximately $1,280M revenue (+17%) and ~$108M adjusted EBITDA (+33%, 8.4% margin).

Three named margin drivers underpin the guided Q4 step-up: mix normalization (auto insurance carries below-average media margin at scale, so its de-concentration lifts the blend), auto-insurance media-margin projects and proprietary media, described as worth 4-5 points since the start of the year, and operating leverage on the semi-fixed cost base below media. Q4 is seasonally strongest for home services. Against that, the $6M spread on a $40M midpoint is ±15% — management's own confidence interval is wide, and the guide requires 200-300bp of sequential margin gain on 1-7% sequential revenue growth.

For FY2027, management has guided to "strong double-digit" organic revenue and adjusted EBITDA growth with margins growing faster than revenue in most businesses, confirming twice that no acquisitions are assumed. Home services is described as supply-constrained rather than demand-constrained ("significantly greater demand than we have capacity to fill"), at an approaching-$0.5B annual run rate, which is the most attractive part of the portfolio. Offsetting this: AmOne has been deliberately shrunk to improve revenue quality (part of the $6.1M decline in other financial services), deposit-gathering clients are restrained by an unclear rate path, and Valenti describes low-income consumers as squeezed by inflation, gas prices and weak wage growth while middle and upper-income consumers remain active.

The estimate-revision record does not corroborate acceleration. Over 90 days: current quarter -11.1% to $0.44, next quarter -4.4% to $0.34, FY2026 -4.0% to $1.21, FY2027 -4.8% to $1.52. The 30-day change is zero across all periods. There is no positive revision momentum here; the pattern is a step down 90 days ago followed by stability.

**Growth prospects: 6.3** (down from 7.5). Real double-digit organic growth with a supply-constrained home-services vertical, but the underlying organic rate is 6-14% rather than 28%, the acceleration is two quarters old and partly acquired, and consensus has been trimmed rather than raised.

## Valuation Assessment

At $15.50: market capitalization $890M; enterprise value $890 + $70 revolver + $85.9 seller obligations - $102 cash = $944M.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV / FY2026E revenue (~$1,280M) | 0.74x |
| EV / FY2026E adjusted EBITDA (~$108M) | 8.7x |
| EV / (FY2026E adjusted EBITDA − SBC ~$36M) | 13.2x |
| P/E on FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS $1.21 | 12.8x |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS $1.52 | 10.2x |
| Trailing GAAP P/E (~$1.13, tax-inflated) | 13.8x — not meaningful |

The 10.2x forward multiple against 25.6% consensus adjusted EPS growth screens as a PEG near 0.4, and that is the core bull argument. The honest qualification is that adjusted EPS and adjusted EBITDA both exclude stock compensation running at $27.2M for nine months, equal to 167% of GAAP operating income and roughly 40% of adjusted EBITDA. On EBITDA less SBC the multiple is 13.2x, which is ordinary rather than cheap for a thin-margin intermediary with 24% single-client concentration and a cyclical demand pool.

A discretionary free-cash-flow cross-check: FY2027 operating cash flow of roughly $95-105M less $15M of capex and capitalized software gives $80-90M, less the $18.75M January-2027 seller installment leaves $61-71M discretionary against $890M of equity value — a 7-8% yield, before the cash tax rate normalizes. That is fair, not compelling.

The supplied peer set (GTM 6.7x, SSTK negative, CARS 22.0x, YELP 10.4x, TRIP 75.6x; median 22.0x) is too heterogeneous to anchor anything. The relevant comparable is MediaAlpha, which per third-party summaries grew 26% in the June quarter on $317M of revenue and ran a 10.1% adjusted EBITDA margin on $310M in the March quarter against QuinStreet's 8.6% on $346M. Two businesses of nearly identical scale at high-single to low-double-digit EBITDA margins. QuinStreet's guided Q4 margin of 10.6-11.6% would close and slightly invert that gap, which is why the imminent print is the number that matters.

Sell-side targets: low $15.00, median $17.00, mean $19.00, high $24.00, against a stable 1 strong buy / 4 buy / 1 hold distribution. The low target equals the current price and the median implies 9.7% upside. With estimates falling modestly, the targets are better read as a range than a floor. One third-party author carried a buy rating in June with a $15.20 FY2027 target, below spot.

Fair value on 8.25x EV/FY2027E adjusted EBITDA of $132M gives $1,089M EV, less ~$50M net debt, over ~59.5M shares = $17.46. That is our base case, roughly 13% above the current price, and it sits between the sell-side median and mean.

**Valuation: 5.5.** Modestly below fair value on adjusted metrics, at fair value on SBC-adjusted metrics and discretionary cash flow. The apparent cheapness is compensation for cyclicality, concentration and a 1.5% pre-tax margin, not an identifiable mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

The strongest element of the competitive case is that the AI shift in search has so far been additive. Management states AI Overviews now trigger on more than 50% of Google searches while QuinStreet's revenue from proprietary Google campaigns grew over 100% over the same period, and that the company is live as an advertiser on OpenAI's advertising platform in both insurance and home services with first revenues generated. The literal relationship matters: QuinStreet pays Google and OpenAI for media and placement and is paid by carriers and contractors for matched consumers. It does not own or control either platform's ad stack. Valenti's claim that LLM platforms are prioritizing marketplace providers because marketplaces supply consumer-choice content is explicitly hedged as "from what we understand and have been told," is unconfirmed by OpenAI, and he concedes carriers will eventually buy direct. The 10-K's own risk factor warns that AI-driven changes in search usage could negatively affect both owned-and-operated and third-party publisher sites. The internal AI productivity figures (+50% on QRP rate integration, +400% on ad creative) are unaudited management estimates with no disclosed methodology, and a 400% creative-generation productivity gain is not a 400% campaign-ROI gain.

Vertical diversification improved — home services from 26% to 33% of quarterly revenue — but $39.1M of the $114.3M home-services quarter came from HomeBuddy. Mix diversification purchased is a weaker fact than customer diversification grown, and it does nothing to the single-client exposure inside financial services, which was 24% of Q3 revenue versus 27% a year earlier and 23% in FY2025. Management's point that the broader client base grew faster than the largest client is consistent with genuine broadening within insurance; the top-client share has drifted down three points on a much larger base but remains the dominant idiosyncratic exposure.

On the positive side of trajectory: auto-insurance media margins up 4-5 points within the year, integration of HomeBuddy running ahead of schedule with a one-platform media approach already routing unmatched Modernize media into HomeBuddy's auction and vice versa, and a home-services vertical where the binding constraint is media supply rather than client demand. HomeBuddy's $39.1M of Q3 revenue carried a 3.0% net margin, above QuinStreet's 2.1% consolidated, and total consideration of $179.7M against roughly $156M of annualized acquired revenue is not an obviously bad price.

**Competitive position: 5.8** (down from 6.5). Improving in home services and in media-margin execution, structurally thin in moat, and dependent on platforms that could compress the intermediation layer. The reduction reflects the recognition that the mix diversification was bought while top-client concentration is unchanged.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-client concentration with no backlog.** One unnamed client at 22-24% of revenue and 23% of receivables in a business with $1.2M of contract liabilities. A budget reallocation shows up in the next month's revenue.

**Auto-insurance underwriting cycle.** The FY2022-FY2024 record — cumulative GAAP losses of $100.7M — is the risk factor, not an abstraction. Management's read is benign (carriers rate-adequate, healthy loss ratios, higher gas prices meaning less driving, fewer claims and more shopping). It is also the setup that preceded the last downturn. Investors are underwriting a mid-to-peak cyclical at a high-single-digit EBITDA margin.

**The imminent print.** Fiscal Q4 and full-year results were announced on approximately 7/23/2026 as forthcoming and are not yet filed; the most recent 8-K on EDGAR is 5/7/2026. Consensus adjusted EPS of $0.44 implies roughly $25.6M of adjusted net income; applying Q3's realized adjusted-net-income-to-adjusted-EBITDA ratio of 60% implies about $42.6M of adjusted EBITDA, at the top of the $37-43M guide. At the $40M midpoint the arithmetic points to roughly $0.41, a 7% miss. This is an inference from one quarter's conversion ratio and could be wrong if operating leverage improves the conversion, but it is why we would not add capital into the event.

**Goodwill and impairment.** Goodwill of $261.8M is 84% of book equity, intangibles a further $71.5M, tangible book negative $22.9M. The FY2023 net loss of $68.9M is the precedent for what a renewed downcycle does to carrying values. HomeBuddy goodwill is not tax-deductible.

**Regulatory.** TCPA/FCC consent rules govern the economics of multi-buyer lead marketplaces. Near-term relief exists: the FCC extended the "revoke-all" waiver to 1/31/2027. The FY2025 10-K's discussion of a one-to-one consent requirement scheduled for 1/26/2026 is absent from the Q3 10-Q; the most plausible reading is judicial resolution, but that is an inference from changed filing language, not verified fact. CFPB Circular 2024-01 on preferencing and steering in comparison-shopping tools remains directly applicable, alongside state mini-TCPA statutes.

**Consumer and rate transmission.** Deposit-gathering clients are, by management's own description, restrained by an unclear rate path. Low-income consumer stress — inflation, gas prices and weak wage growth per management — pressures AmOne regardless of the deliberate shrinkage, and home-improvement demand is exposed to housing and contractor-capacity conditions.

**Uncapped earn-out and pre-committed cash.** The AquaVida earn-out has grown roughly ninefold from initial recognition to $18.2M, is uncapped, and is excluded from adjusted EBITDA. Combined with $18.75M annual HomeBuddy installments from January 2027, $85.9M of forward free cash flow is spoken for ahead of buybacks or new deals.

**Key person.** Valenti has run the company for 27 years, is the sole voice on strategy in the transcript, and no successor is disclosed.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 83.5% against 46.2% one-year realized — a 37-point spread, 81st percentile within the current chain — with the term structure in backwardation (83.5% at 17 days versus 59.9% at six months) and an implied move of ±15.1% on the August 21 expiry. Implied volatility has risen from 53.2% to 83.5% since late May while the price rose 25.6%. That combination prices a specific imminent event, consistent with fiscal Q4 results landing within days. The near-term put/call skew of 14.3% is characterized as elevated put demand; the September tenor's skew carries the opposite sign, so the near-term put bid looks like event hedging rather than a durable directional position. Short interest of 11.3% of float with 6.5 days to cover means the move is two-sided: a clean beat with in-line FY2027 guidance could squeeze, and a margin miss has 11% of the float positioned to be right. Buying at 83.5% implied volatility into that is paying for the catalyst rather than being paid for it.

**Risk factors: 4.2.** Concentration, cyclicality, negative tangible book with 84%-goodwill equity, a dated binary within days that consensus appears to have positioned above the guide midpoint, and live regulatory exposure. These are not fully priced at 10.2x forward adjusted EPS.

### Management Quality

The compensation plan measures only adjusted EBITDA and media margin dollars. Media margin dollars is arguably the correct unit-economic metric for a media arbitrage model, and adjusted EBITDA is sector convention, so the choice is defensible as an operating measure. The problem is the absence of any per-share, return-on-capital or GAAP earnings metric in a company with negative GAAP net income in three of the last five years, and the fact that both measures exclude the $27.2M nine-month stock-compensation charge — 167% of GAAP operating income. CEO FY2025 compensation rose 29% to $7.69M; NEO average rose 51%.

Capital allocation is mixed. HomeBuddy looks like a competently priced, well-integrated deal that is self-funding if the acquired businesses perform. Against that, despite retiring 1,121,745 shares for $16.8M at an average $14.94 in nine months, shares outstanding rose from 57.16M to 57.44M — the buyback functioned as partial dilution offset, not shareholder return. The April 2022 program completed in October 2025 and zero shares had been repurchased under the new $40M authorization through 3/31/26, most plausibly because HomeBuddy consumed the budget.

Insider activity offers no support. There were no open-market purchases by executives or directors in the reviewed window through 7/29/2026. The CFO adopted a 10b5-1 plan in March 2026 for up to 108,258 shares and has sold 22,057 at $12.19 and 10,338 at $15.00 — small, pre-planned, and not a signal on its own. The CEO's gift dispositions are not sales and carry no inference. The 7/29/2026 grants of approximately 394K shares to the CEO and 158K to the CFO total roughly 0.96% of shares outstanding, about $8.6M of grant-date value; a portion is likely performance-contingent, though the conditions are not in the Form 4 detail. Governance is otherwise clean: no executive-departure 8-K since February 2023, no related-party transactions surfaced.

**Management quality: 5.2.** Competent operators with a defensible acquisition and clean disclosure, in a plan that does not measure the cost of the equity it issues, with no insider conviction visible.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $22.50 (+45%)

Fiscal Q4 lands at or above the top of guidance ($370M revenue, $43M adjusted EBITDA, 11.6% margin), validating the three named margin drivers as structural rather than seasonal. FY2027 guidance confirms strong double-digit organic revenue growth with margins expanding faster, taking revenue to roughly $1.47B and adjusted EBITDA to $140M. Home services, currently supply-constrained at an approaching-$0.5B run rate, absorbs additional media capacity through the one-platform integration. Auto-insurance carriers remain rate-adequate — MediaAlpha's 26% June-quarter growth independently corroborates an intact upcycle — and the client base continues broadening, taking top-client concentration below 20%. AI intermediation continues to favor marketplaces that supply consumer choice, and the OpenAI advertising channel scales from first revenues into a second material supply source, reducing Google dependence. At 10x EV/EBITDA on $140M less ~$40M net debt, equity is $1.36B, or roughly $22.90 per share on 59.5M shares. Call it $22.50, near the $24 high sell-side target.

### Bear Case — $10.50 (−32%)

Fiscal Q4 adjusted EBITDA lands at or below the $40M midpoint, adjusted EPS misses the $0.44 consensus that appears to require the top end, and the 200-300bp sequential margin step-up is revealed as guidance optimism. FY2027 guidance is softer than "strong double-digit," or is delivered with a mix caveat. Auto-insurance carriers begin trimming acquisition spend as loss ratios normalize, and with $1.2M of contract liabilities the revenue decline is immediate; deposit clients stay restrained while the rate path stays opaque; a squeezed low-income consumer pressures home improvement and AmOne. Cash taxes normalize as NOLs are consumed, the $18.75M January-2027 seller installment begins, working capital reverses from tailwind to headwind, and discretionary free cash flow compresses toward $45-55M. Goodwill at 84% of equity comes under review. At 6.5x EBITDA of $100M less $60M net debt, equity is roughly $590M, or $10.00 per share — near the February low of $10.29.

### Base Case — $17.50 (+13%)

FY2026 closes at approximately $1,280M revenue and $108M adjusted EBITDA. FY2027 delivers low-teens organic revenue growth to roughly $1.45B with adjusted EBITDA near $132M (9.1% margin), consistent with management's framework but below the top of it. 8.25x EV/EBITDA on $132M = $1,089M EV, less ~$50M net debt, over ~59.5M shares = $17.46. This is corroborated by a 6.5-7.5% discretionary free-cash-flow yield on $61-71M of post-seller-payment cash generation, which supports $14.80-17.10, and it sits between the sell-side median of $17.00 and mean of $19.00.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** Existing holders should own through the print; new capital should wait for it.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. Fiscal Q4 adjusted EBITDA at $41M or better (≥11.2% margin) together with FY2027 guidance confirming double-digit organic revenue growth and margin expansion — this converts the margin-expansion narrative from two quarters of evidence into a demonstrated structural trend and would justify 9-10x forward EBITDA.
2. Consensus FY2027 adjusted EPS revised up through $1.60 (reversing the 4.8% 90-day cut) with the 30-day trend positive.
3. A price of $12.50-13.00 without fundamental deterioration, which restores roughly 7x EV/forward EBITDA and a 35%+ gap to base case. The stock traded at $10.29-12.50 as recently as February through May 2026, so this is not a hypothetical level.
4. Evidence that top-client concentration has fallen below 20% of revenue on an organic basis.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. Fiscal Q4 adjusted EBITDA below $37M, or a Q4 adjusted EBITDA margin below Q3's 8.6%, which would break the sequential-margin-expansion thesis outright.
2. FY2027 guidance for single-digit organic revenue growth, or any language walking back the "no acquisitions in that plan" framing.
3. Sequential decline in auto-insurance revenue, or disclosure that the largest client's share is rising back toward 27%.
4. Goodwill impairment testing commentary, a covenant amendment request, or a revolver draw above $100M outside of a disclosed acquisition.

**Reassessment timeline:** immediate — fiscal Q4 and full-year FY2026 results are due within days of this report and were announced on approximately 7/23/2026. The next scheduled checkpoint after that is fiscal Q1 2027 (September quarter) in late October/early November, which will show whether Q4's margin is sustainable at seasonally weaker home-services volumes, and January 2027, when the first $18.75M HomeBuddy installment lands.

## Conclusion

The prior BUY has been rewarded: the shares are up roughly 25% from late May while the business delivered a record quarter and guided to another. What has changed is that the multiple absorbed the good news while consensus was trimmed 4-5% for both fiscal years and 11% for the quarter about to be reported, compressing the gap to a defensible base case of $17.50 to about 13%, against a bear case near $10.50 that the stock visited twice this calendar year.

The specific reasons to wait rather than add are concrete. Consensus adjusted EPS of $0.44 appears to require the top of management's own $37-43M EBITDA range. The guided margin step-up is 200-300bp sequentially on 1-7% sequential revenue growth. Near-term implied volatility of 83.5% against 46.2% realized in a backwardated term structure means the market is charging full price for the event, with 11.3% of the float short and a ±15% move priced.
