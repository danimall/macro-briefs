## Executive Summary

Helmerich & Payne reported its June 2026 quarter on August 5. The headline was a return to GAAP profitability — net income of $75.7M, or $0.74 per diluted share — and it should be discarded. Of that, $88.7M after tax ($0.88 per share) was the gain on selling Utica Square, a Tulsa shopping center with a $12.9M net book value, for roughly $129.0M net proceeds. On the company's own adjusted basis, Q3 FY2026 was a loss of $9.9M, or $0.11 per share, the sixth consecutive quarter without a core profit. Headline-EPS screens will misread this quarter badly.

Underneath the accounting, the operating trend genuinely improved. Adjusted EBITDA rose 32.7% sequentially to $236.1M. North America Solutions deployed ten additional rigs into private-operator demand, lifting direct margin per revenue day by more than $1,000 to $18,669 and segment operating income to $140.3M from $111.3M. The International segment's operating loss halved to $54.4M and its direct margin nearly tripled to $31.1M. Argentina produced contracts for five additional FlexRigs, three exported from the idle U.S. fleet into Vaca Muerta. Offshore renewed a four-year Norwegian contract and carries $3.6B of backlog against only four owned platform rigs plus thirty management contracts where the customer owns the asset. Fourth-quarter guidance of 145-151 average North America rigs against 142 in Q3 implies further sequential activity gains. The $400M acquisition term loan was fully retired in April 2026, and funded debt to total capitalization stands at 41.1% against a 55.0% covenant.

The problem is that none of this is being sold at a distressed price. Enterprise value of roughly $5,077M sits at 5.9x nine-month annualized adjusted EBITDA of $859M — inside, and toward the top of, the 2.5x-5.5x guideline public-company range management itself used to write off 100% of the goodwill assigned to the acquired reporting units in mid-2025. The stock trades at 1.34x book value of $25.76 per share, 1.75x tangible book of $19.69, and roughly 24.6x next-year consensus EPS of $1.40 — a consensus produced by analysts who have missed the last four quarters by margins ranging from 104% to 1,676%. Guided FY2026 cash taxes of $150-180M against $46.2M paid in the first half imply a back-loaded cash drain, and capex at roughly 41% of depreciation is harvest-mode spending that cannot persist while the company guides activity higher. This is a company whose operations are recovering into a valuation that already assumes recovery. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Helmerich & Payne is a contract driller founded in 1920 and headquartered in Tulsa. It is no longer principally a U.S. land driller. On January 16, 2025 it closed the acquisition of KCA Deutag International for approximately $2.0 billion of cash — $0.9 billion of share purchase price plus $1.1 billion to retire KCA Deutag debt, interest, premiums and fees. That transaction re-segmented the company and re-levered the balance sheet.

Four reporting units result. **North America Solutions** (202 U.S. land rigs as of August 5, 2026, down from 223 available a year earlier) is the legacy super-spec FlexRig fleet and remains the earnings engine: $287.9M of nine-month segment operating income and a 42.3% nine-month direct margin. **International Solutions** (127 rigs) combines legacy H&P international work with acquired KCA Deutag land operations across Saudi Arabia, Oman, Argentina, Europe, North Africa and Australia; it generated a $209.3M nine-month segment operating loss on a 10.1% direct margin. **Offshore Solutions** operates four owned platform rigs plus thirty management contracts on customer-owned platforms — H&P provides drilling operations and maintenance for a fee and carries none of the rig capital, which is why $534.1M of nine-month revenue produced $87.1M of direct margin on only ~11% of consolidated assets. **Other** houses BENTEC manufacturing, captive insurers and real estate.

Total fleet has been deliberately shrunk: 367 rigs at September 30, 2025 → 337 at March 31, 2026 → 333 at August 5, 2026, with 30 North America rigs and 3 offshore rigs committed to scrap in October 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Four-year path.** Revenue: $2,049.8M (FY22) → $2,862.7M (FY23) → $2,746.1M (FY24) → $3,678.7M (FY25). Operating income: $45.3M → $561.9M → $451.9M → **$3.3M**. Net income: $7.0M → $434.1M → $344.2M → **–$163.7M**. FY2025's 34% revenue growth was acquisition arithmetic — $1.0 billion of incremental revenue over 258 days of KCA Deutag ownership — while organic North America revenue *declined* 3.4% to $2,362.3M on fewer average active rigs (147 from 151) and fewer revenue days (53,523 from 55,387).

**The quarterly sequence is the more useful lens.** Nine months to June 30, 2026: revenue $2,984.2M, operating income $91.4M, net loss attributable to H&P of $79.6M. By quarter, revenue ran $1,017.0M (Q1, derived) → $932.4M (Q2) → $1,034.9M (Q3), and adjusted EBITDA $230.4M (derived) → $177.9M → $236.1M. Q2 was the trough; Q3 recovered to roughly Q1 levels with better underlying mix.

**Margin structure explains the strategic problem.** Nine-month direct margins: North America $695.2M on $1,644.1M (42.3%, down 12.8% year over year); International $71.3M on $702.7M (10.1%); Offshore $87.1M on $534.1M (16.3%). International carries ~24% of revenue and contributes ~8% of the three segments' combined direct margin. Below direct margin the segment is deeply loss-making on $231.9M of nine-month D&A (against $128.7M a year earlier), reflecting the purchase-price allocation flowing through the P&L, including $432.2M of customer-relationship intangibles amortizing over nine years, with total finite-lived amortization guided at $72.7M in each of FY2027 and FY2028. The International loss is substantially non-cash; the segment does not burn cash, it fails to earn a return on the ~$2.39 billion of assets allocated to it.

**Impairments have been sustained rather than one-off.** FY2025 recorded $194.0M, of which $192.2M was a full write-off of goodwill assigned to International Solutions and BENTEC, triggered — per the filing — "primarily by the sustained decline in our share price and market capitalization," valued using guideline public-company multiples of 2.5x-5.5x EBITDA. FY2026's nine-month $130.3M is a different animal: physical fleet decisions (30 North America rigs, 3 offshore rigs, one international rig written from $23.5M to $0.2M scrap value). Two readings of the goodwill charge coexist and the filings do not adjudicate: management overpaid for earnings power that proved weaker than underwritten, or a market-approach test at 2.5x-5.5x sector multiples would have failed for essentially any acquisition goodwill in this industry. Management simultaneously found no long-lived-asset impairment indicator, stating there had been "no material change in our long-term cash flow projections." Both readings are consistent with the disclosure; no motive is inferred.

**Cash conversion is real but declining.** Operating cash flow: $833.7M (FY23) → $684.7M (FY24) → $542.9M (FY25) → $372.7M in nine months of FY2026. Three consecutive annual declines against 34% FY2025 revenue growth is the direct consequence of buying low-margin revenue while legacy high-margin volumes and pricing eroded. Nine-month FY2026 OCF of $372.7M against a $72.6M net loss is bridged by $543.6M of D&A and $130.3M of impairments — accrual quality is sound. Nine-month simple FCF is $172.5M.

Do not annualize that to ~$230M. Three adjustments cut the other way: guided FY2026 cash taxes of $150-180M against $46.2M paid in the first half imply a heavy fourth-quarter load; guided capex of $270-310M against $200.2M spent implies $70-110M in Q4; and the $127.7M of real-estate proceeds sits in investing, not operating, and does not recur. Building up from guidance instead — Q4 direct margin of $296-335M less ~$70M SG&A less ~$25M interest supports roughly $200-240M of quarterly adjusted EBITDA, from which ~$90M capex and $60-90M of cash tax leave modest quarterly free cash. A defensible FY2026 free-cash-flow range is $180-240M, a 5.2%-7.0% yield on the $3,446.6M market capitalization, of which the $1.00 annual dividend (~$101M) consumes roughly half.

**Balance sheet.** Book debt fell from $2,063.9M (9/30/25) to $2,002.4M (3/31/26) to $1,862.1M (6/30/26). The $400M Morgan Stanley term loan drawn at closing was retired in stages and fully repaid in April 2026, confirmed in both the 10-Q subsequent-events note and the nine-month cash flow statement. Remaining debt is four fixed-rate unsecured note tranches ($350M 4.65% due Dec 2027; $350M 4.85% due Dec 2029; $550M 2.90% due Sept 2031; $550M 5.50% due Dec 2034) plus ~$79M of Omani-rial facilities. **There is no unsecured maturity before December 2027.** Liquidity: $950M undrawn revolver with zero borrowings and zero letters of credit, $775M of commitments running to November 2028, plus $420M of uncommitted bilateral guarantee facilities of which $234.4M was utilized at March 31 (up from $186.4M at fiscal year-end — a rising call on guarantee capacity consistent with more international work).

Covenant headroom is comfortable: funded debt to total capitalization of $1,862.1M ÷ ($1,862.1M + $2,670.4M) = **41.1%** against a 55.0% test. Net debt is $1,630.7M, roughly 1.9x nine-month annualized adjusted EBITDA.

The screened Altman Z of 1.54 warrants care rather than dismissal or acceptance. Two of its inputs are mechanically depressed by items already discussed — FY2025 EBIT of $3.3M absorbed $194.0M of impairments and $54.7M of transaction costs, and total assets were inflated ~16% by the acquisition. Adding back those items lifts EBIT/assets from 0.01 to roughly 0.038 — still weak, and a fair signal that returns on the enlarged asset base are inadequate. The evidence bearing directly on solvency points the other way: zero revolver drawings, 41% funded-debt/capitalization, no near maturity, and $372.7M of nine-month operating cash flow. The Z-score correctly flags weak earnings power on assets and substantially overstates near-term default risk. Separately, the screened debt/equity of 2.37 does not reconcile to the filings: total liabilities to equity is 1.35x and total debt to equity is 0.70x.

**Capital allocation.** Dividends per share fell from $1.94 (FY23) to $1.68 (FY24) to $1.00 (FY25); the $0.25 quarterly rate is being held, and the CFO's framing — "maintaining our *base* dividend" — does not signal restoration. Buybacks stopped entirely: $249.0M in FY2023, $51.6M in FY2024, nothing since. Capex fell from $495.1M to $426.4M to guided $270-310M against ~$700M of guided depreciation. Non-core monetization funded part of the deleveraging: the ADNOC Drilling stake for $193.3M net (at a $12.4M loss) and Utica Square for ~$129.0M net against $12.9M of book value.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth signal is genuine but narrow. Q4 guidance implies 145-151 average North America rigs against 142 in Q3 and 140-144 for the full year; the ten rigs added in Q3 were deployed "in response to strong demand from private operators." Argentina supplies the most attractive organic opportunity in the file — five additional FlexRig contracts including three units exported from the idle U.S. fleet, a capital-efficient redeployment into Vaca Muerta. Offshore added a four-year Norway renewal and a five-year O&M extension that lifted total backlog from $7.0B to $8.3B.

Against that, the structural ceiling is low. North America active rigs have ranged 136-150 across seven reported quarters while direct margin per day held near $18,000-19,000; management describes E&P behavior as "more stable and predictable," which is another way of saying the market does not grow. The Q2 FY2026 10-Q added pricing to the volume story for the first time — revenue fell on "lower activity levels **and per revenue day pricing levels**" — and nine-month North America direct margin fell 12.8% against only a 5.6% decline in revenue days, the signature of price. Q3's sequential $1,000/day improvement may mark a trough, but one quarter does not establish a trend. R&D is being cut from $41.0M (FY24) to $34.1M (FY25) to guided ~$28M (FY26), a 32% reduction from peak in a company whose stated differentiation is proprietary rig technology and automation.

**Estimate revisions require careful reading.** The headline is "broadly rising": next quarter $0.26 (+45.7% over 90 days), next year $1.40 (+29.8%), current quarter $0.10 (+14.8%). But the current-year figure was cut from +$0.17 to –$0.07 over the same 90 days, a large downward reset. The pattern is a deferred recovery — the near-term year written down, the out-year written up — rather than a clean inflection. (The current-year aggregate of –$0.07 does not reconcile cleanly against reported nine-month adjusted results plus a $0.10 Q4 estimate, so I read the direction of the revisions rather than the levels.)

The estimate-revision signal has to be discounted against the delivery record: four consecutive misses, at –103.9%, –245.6%, –1,675.7% and –185.3%. The June quarter's supplied comparison ($0.13 estimate against –$0.11 actual) is on the company's adjusted basis. Whatever the basis, consensus has been systematically too optimistic about this company for a year. A +29.8% out-year revision from analysts with that record does not carry the weight it would at a company that beats. Haircutting $1.40 to $1.15-1.25 is prudent, and even the unhaircut number puts the stock at 24.6x.

## Competitive Landscape

The North America franchise is the real asset. A 42.3% nine-month direct margin, a homogeneous super-spec fleet, and reactivation cost advantage are substantiated in the numbers, not just in management language. The decision to permanently scrap 30 rigs took available North America capacity from 224 (3/31/25) to 202 (6/30/26), a 9.8% reduction, and lifted reported utilization from 62.9% to 72.8%. In a market where marginal rigs suppress pricing, retiring capacity is rational. The alternative reading — that these rigs were obsolete and unmarketable, making the decision economically forced — fits the write-down to scrap value and the fact that 10 of the 30 were already decommissioned. Both are consistent with the disclosure.

The Offshore management-contract franchise is underappreciated. Thirty contracts on customer-owned rigs plus four owned platforms generated $174.4M of Q3 revenue and $29.2M of direct margin off roughly 11% of total assets. Direct margin of ~16.8% looks thin until capital intensity is accounted for. Offshore also carries $138.1M of the company's remaining $182.4M of goodwill, and at the June 2025 interim test its fair value exceeded carrying value by only ~20%, the thinnest cushion disclosed. Operating momentum since then argues the cushion has widened, but this is the identifiable next impairment exposure.

The acquired international franchise is where the competitive case weakens. A 10.1% direct margin against 42.3% in North America means the company bought a large volume of revenue at roughly a quarter of the unit margin of its legacy business. Twenty-seven Saudi rigs were suspended between the July 2024 announcement and September 2025; seven resumption notices followed, only six were expected operational in the first half of calendar 2026, and the in-country target was revised down from 24 to 23 rigs. Saudi revenue fell from $94.7M (Q2 FY25) to $65.6M (Q2 FY26). H&P also liquidated a minority stake in a growing regional driller (ADNOC Drilling, at a loss) to help fund control of a suspension-prone one.

Trajectory is mixed but improving at the margin: North America margin per day rising, Argentina winning work with redeployed U.S. iron, Offshore renewing long contracts, International losses halving. The moat narrative around technology is being defunded. Score 5.3.

## Risk Assessment

**Backlog quality is materially weaker than the headline.** Of $8.3B at March 31, 2026, only $5.4B is firm; $2.9B is optional extension periods the customer may decline. Only 13.3% converts in the remainder of FY2026, down from 22.6% a year earlier. Within the optional International bucket, $0.5B is revenue from *currently suspended* Saudi contracts, included on H&P's assumption that they will be extended for at least the suspension period — an assumption, not an entitlement. Most consequentially, acquired KCA Deutag agreements "contain provisions for optional early termination or suspension **without any associated early termination fees**," so the margin-preservation logic that protected H&P's legacy backlog does not extend to the acquired book. One early-termination notice after fiscal year-end removed $34.9M. The ASC 606 remaining-performance-obligation figure of $5.4B is the conservative anchor.

**Concentration was relocated, not reduced.** International revenue reached 41.2% of the total in Q2 FY2026 from 28.1%, with the Middle East at 16.7%. A single Saudi counterparty is 7.0% of consolidated revenue and, per the filing, "has the ability to suspend rigs." H&P has $835.6M of net PP&E located in Saudi Arabia — 21% of consolidated net PP&E — exposed to a customer with unilateral suspension rights. Geographic mix and customer concentration are separate measures; only the former improved. Argentine currency controls are separately disclosed, having cost $7.1M (FY24) and $12.2M (FY23) to repatriate $13.8M and $9.8M.

**Material weakness in internal control.** The Q2 FY2026 10-Q discloses that management concluded disclosure controls were effective because the material weakness described in Item 9A of the FY2025 10-K was "**inapplicable in the current period**" — not remediated. KCA Deutag's controls were excluded from the FY2025 ICFR assessment under the first-year accommodation and fully incorporated only from January 1, 2026. The nature, root cause, scope and remediation status of the weakness are not established from any available source, and a targeted news search returned nothing. The phrasing suggests a process specific to business-combination accounting that did not recur, which would be materially less severe than a revenue-recognition or environment-level failure. This is an open verification gap, not evidence of misstatement, and it is unpriced.

**Tax expense is decoupled from pretax income.** FY2025 recorded $85.8M of tax on a $74.1M pretax loss. Nine-month FY2026: $92.9M of tax on $20.2M of pretax income. Foreign losses carry no recognized benefit, so H&P pays cash tax in profitable jurisdictions without sheltering it elsewhere — a structural drag from international expansion. The $592.4M deferred tax liability, largely accelerated depreciation, reverses into cash taxes faster as capex falls.

**Non-operating volatility is large relative to operating earnings.** Investment securities swung from a $14.0M FY2024 gain to a $22.4M FY2025 loss to a $15.3M six-month FY2026 gain to a $16.0M Q3 loss. The Galileo convertible note — a $33.0M 2022 "cornerstone investment" — was written down $10.2M, then $29.6M, then fully written off after H&P released the counterparty from its obligation. The Tamboran position can move quarterly EPS by ±$0.13-0.20 for reasons unrelated to drilling.

**End-market visibility is genuinely low.** The FY2025 10-K warned that tariffs and OPEC+ supply increases "could lead to reduced activity in fiscal year 2026." By the following quarter the framing had inverted: the same geopolitical uncertainty "may **support** increased drilling activity." Management's directional read on its own market reversed within two quarters. Both directions cut: sustained Hormuz disruption supports activity but raises physical risk to 21% of net PP&E; verified normalization relieves that risk while removing the crude support that is driving private-operator demand in the Lower 48.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM implied volatility of 51.0% sits 8.7 points above one-year realized volatility of 42.3%, in backwardation against 41.4% at six months — the market is pricing near-term event risk, with a ±8.8% implied move over the 14 days to the August 21 expiry despite no scheduled earnings in that window. ATM IV rose 5.8 points since July 19 while the price fell 2.5%, so uncertainty is being repriced upward rather than resolved. The supplied skew reading is call-heavy (–9.6%) with a put/call volume ratio of 0.55, which sits awkwardly against 12.6% short interest and 6.4 days to cover. Read together: option buyers are positioned for upside and paying rich premium for it, while a meaningful institutional short base remains. For a fundamental HOLD this argues against paying up for near-dated optionality in either direction, and it flags a two-sided squeeze/flush dynamic around oil headlines. The elevated short interest also caps how much downside conviction a bear can express cheaply.

Risk score 4.0: the specific, quantified, unpriced items (material-weakness gap, Offshore goodwill cushion, no-termination-fee acquired backlog, single-counterparty suspension rights over 21% of net PP&E) are unusually dense for a company of this size.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $47 (+36.3%)

The June quarter marks the operating trough. North America rig count rises to the 145-151 guided range and beyond as private operators respond to sustained crude strength, with daily margins continuing the $1,000+ sequential recovery toward $20,000. The five Argentine FlexRig awards, including three U.S. exports, prove a repeatable capital-light redeployment channel from an idle fleet into a growing basin. Saudi reactivations continue, taking International direct margin toward the upper half of its $25-45M quarterly guide and turning the segment's GAAP loss positive as the acquisition intangible amortization runs its course. The second cost program delivers where the first did not, taking SG&A meaningfully below the $265-285M guided band. Deleveraging continues from $1,631M of net debt, and with no unsecured maturity before December 2027 the company builds equity value by paying down debt against a stable enterprise value. On roughly $1.0B of FY2027 adjusted EBITDA at 6.3x and net debt of ~$1,450M, equity is worth $48; the analyst high of $47 is the practical anchor.

### Bear Case — $23 (–33.3%)

Hormuz transit normalizes, crude retraces toward the $60s as OPEC+ completes its rollback of voluntary cuts, and the private-operator demand that drove Q3's ten-rig deployment reverses. North America pricing resumes the erosion visible in the Q2 filing language, with direct margin per day falling back below $18,000 on flat-to-lower activity. Saudi suspensions extend rather than resolve, and the $0.5B of suspended-contract backlog fails to convert — with no early-termination fees to preserve margin. Guided cash taxes of $150-180M land at the high end while capex must step up from 41% of depreciation to reactivate rigs, compressing free cash flow toward the dividend. The Offshore goodwill cushion of ~20% closes and the remaining $138.1M is written off. On $750M of EBITDA at 5.25x against $1,650M of net debt, equity is $23 — a 33% decline, with book value of $25.76 offering only partial support given the sector's history of trading below book in genuine downcycles.

### Base Case — $37 (+7.3%)

Activity holds near the Q4 guided range without accelerating. FY2027 adjusted EBITDA of roughly $900M is capitalized at 5.75x — the middle of the range the market currently applies — for $5,175M of enterprise value; net debt declines to roughly $1,530M as $180-240M of free cash flow funds ~$101M of dividends and modest debt reduction. Equity of $3,645M over 100.0M shares is $36.45, rounded to $37. An EPS cross-check is less generous: $1.40 haircut to $1.20 for the four-quarter miss record, at 28x, gives $34. The two approaches bracket the current price, which is the substance of the HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The overall score rises from 4.5 to 4.76 on verified fundamental improvement — adjusted EBITDA up 32.7% sequentially, ten rigs deployed, daily margins up more than $1,000, the International loss halved, the acquisition term loan fully retired, and Q4 guidance implying further activity gains. The trajectory's prior decline (management quality 5.5 → 3.9, competitive position 6.0 → 5.2) is arrested rather than reversed: management quality stays at 4.0 because there has been no insider open-market buying anywhere between $28 and $41 while two C-suite seats changed hands, and risk stays at 4.0 because the material-weakness disclosure remains unresolved. This is not an upgrade for arithmetic reasons, not sentiment — a 7% base-case return against 33% bear-case downside is not a buyable asymmetry, and the energy sector's record of delivering beta rather than selection alpha argues for requiring a wider margin before adding.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q4/FY2026 report in early-to-mid November 2026, which will carry the FY2026 10-K, FY2027 guidance, and Item 9A disclosure on the internal-control weakness.

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. **FY2027 capex guidance below $400M alongside average North America rigs guided above 150**, demonstrating that reactivation is being funded without abandoning harvest-mode discipline. Capex at 41% of depreciation is not a steady state; the market needs to see what normalized spending looks like at higher activity.
2. **Two consecutive quarters of positive adjusted net income**, ending the six-quarter core-loss streak, with International segment direct margin at or above $45M quarterly. This would break the four-quarter miss pattern and make consensus estimates usable.
3. **Price below $28** on unchanged fundamentals, which would put the stock near 5.0x EV/EBITDA on nine-month annualized figures and roughly 1.09x book — a genuine discount to the range the market currently applies.
4. **Item 9A discloses a remediated, business-combination-scoped material weakness with a clean auditor ICFR opinion**, closing the largest open verification gap.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. **North America direct margin per revenue day falling below $17,500** for two consecutive quarters, confirming that Q3's sequential improvement was a bounce inside a pricing downtrend rather than a trough.
2. **Additional Saudi suspension notices, or failure of the remaining suspended rigs to resume by mid-calendar 2027**, which would invalidate the $0.5B of suspended-contract backlog and put the acquisition's underwriting formally in question.
3. **FY2027 free cash flow guidance insufficient to cover the $101M dividend after guided cash taxes and capex**, or any indication the base dividend is at risk. The dividend was already cut 48% from FY2023 and is not covered on GAAP earnings.
4. **Impairment of the $138.1M Offshore goodwill**, which would signal that the strongest-performing segment has softened and would remove the last support for the asset-backing argument.

## Conclusion

Helmerich & Payne is a better business in August 2026 than it was in May, and the improvement is verifiable rather than narrative: adjusted EBITDA up 32.7% sequentially, ten rigs deployed into private-operator demand, North America daily margins up more than $1,000, the International operating loss halved, Argentine and Norwegian contract wins, and the acquisition term loan fully retired against 41.1% funded-debt/capitalization with no unsecured maturity until December 2027. Those facts justify raising the score.

They do not justify buying the stock at $34.49. The June quarter's GAAP profit was a shopping-center sale; on the company's own adjusted basis it was still a loss, the sixth in a row. Enterprise value at 5.9x nine-month annualized EBITDA sits in the upper half of the multiple range the market applies to the sector and above the range that forced a $192.2M goodwill write-off fourteen months ago. Next-year consensus of $1.40 puts the stock at 24.6x, and that consensus comes from analysts who have missed four consecutive quarters, in one case by a factor of seventeen. Guided cash taxes of $150-180M and capex at 41% of depreciation mean reported EBITDA overstates distributable cash. The unresolved internal-control disclosure, the absence of any insider open-market purchase across a $28-to-$41 price range while both the CEO and CFO seats changed hands, and $835.6M of net PP&E sitting under a customer with unilateral suspension rights are specific, quantified risks that the multiple does not appear to discount.

Base case $37 (+7.3%), bull $47 (+36.3%), bear $23 (–33.3%). Own it if you own it; the balance sheet is not the problem and the operating trend is improving. New capital should wait for either the November 10-K to close the internal-control gap and set FY2027 capex, or a price near $28 that restores the asymmetry. **HOLD.**
