**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Brown & Brown is one of the largest publicly traded insurance intermediaries in the world, earning commissions and fees as an agent/broker that largely does not assume underwriting risk. The stock has fallen roughly 37% from its July 2025 high of $104.25 to ~$66, a decline driven by a specific, identifiable fundamental problem: organic revenue growth collapsed from 10.4% in 2024 to 2.8% in 2025 and to 0.0% in Q1 2026, arriving at the same time the company absorbed a heavily debt-funded $9.83B acquisition (Accession, formerly Risk Strategies). The >30% drawdown reflects a real change in the fundamental trajectory, not simply market volatility. The core commission engine is levered to insurance premium rates, and property/CAT rates are now declining after years of a hard market that lifted the entire brokerage sector.

The bull case rests on quality and asymmetry: BRO carries best-in-class adjusted EBITDAC margins (~38.5%, up 40bps YoY in Q1'26), a recurring commission base (~77-81% commission-driven across segments), a 30-year record of disciplined M&A (725 acquisitions since 1993), a beat in all four of the last four quarters, insider open-market buying near the lows, and an aggressive $250M accelerated repurchase executed into the weakness. At ~14.7x consensus current-year EPS ($4.48) and ~13.6x next-year ($4.84), the stock has de-rated well below its historical premium multiple and below broker peers.

The two things needed to justify a BUY — reaccelerating organic growth and rising estimates — are both moving the wrong way. Q1'26 organic was flat only because a 126% surge in contingent commissions masked a flat-to-shrinking underlying book; Specialty Distribution organic was negative 2.0%. Consensus EPS estimates have fallen across every window over 90 days. Interest expense doubled to a ~$400M/year run-rate. Management's own 2026 guidance frames organic growth topping out at only ~2.5%. This is a good business at a fair-to-cheap price with deteriorating near-term momentum. We rate it HOLD, awaiting evidence of organic reacceleration before the risk/reward turns asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Brown & Brown, headquartered in Daytona Beach, Florida, operates as an insurance intermediary whose principal revenue is commissions (a percentage of premium) with a secondary fee stream. Following the Accession acquisition that closed August 1, 2025, the company reorganized from three segments into two:

- **Retail (~64% of revenue; Q1'26 revenue $1,210M):** P&C, employee benefits, private client, F&I (auto/RV dealer warranty), and consulting. ~77% commission-based.
- **Specialty Distribution (~36%; Q1'26 revenue $682M):** Combines the former Programs and Wholesale Brokerage units into Arrowhead Programs (MGU/programs, including the Wright National Flood carrier), Bridge Specialty Group (wholesale/E&S, Lloyd's access), and Arrowhead Specialty (affinity, captives, reinsurance, travel, warranty, life & health). ~81% commission-based.

BRO also operates ancillary captive insurance facilities (reinsurance captives, series captives, protected cells) that provide incremental underwriting capacity with limited net risk, and it runs a write-your-own NFIP flood carrier whose premiums are 100% ceded to FEMA. The historical competitive edge is a decentralized, sales-driven culture combined with disciplined acquisition integration and industry-leading margins.

Its principal competitors are Marsh McLennan, Aon, Arthur J. Gallagher, WTW, and PE-backed consolidators such as Acrisure, Hub, and USI. The Accession deal positions BRO within the industry mega-merger wave (Gallagher/AssuredPartners, Marsh/McGriff), a consolidation race in which scale and acquisition capacity matter.

## Financial Analysis

**Scale transformation via Accession.** FY2025 revenue rose ~23% to ~$5.9B (from $4.8B), and Q1 2026 revenue reached $1,901M, up 35.4% YoY. Goodwill nearly doubled to ~$15.1B and total debt roughly doubled to ~$7.8B, both driven by the $7.46B cash consideration for Accession.

**The central concern is the organic growth collapse.** BRO's commission revenue is a percentage of premium; when insurance carriers cut property/CAT rates and moderate casualty increases, the same book of business generates less commission dollars, compressing organic growth even without account attrition. The trajectory:

- FY organic growth decelerated from 10.4% (2024) → 2.8% (2025).
- Q1 2026 consolidated organic revenue growth was 0.0% (vs. +6.5% in Q1'25). Retail organic was +1.0% (vs. 4.1%); Specialty Distribution organic was −2.0% (vs. +11.3%).
- The reported "Organic Revenue with Contingents" of +2.2% was propped up almost entirely by profit-sharing contingent commissions, which surged 126% ($43M → $97M) on favorable carrier loss ratios and new-acquisition contingents. Contingents average only ~4.4% of total commissions/fees and are inherently lumpy. Stripping them out, the underlying book was flat-to-shrinking.

**Margins are holding despite the growth stall.** Q1'26 adjusted EBITDAC margin was 38.5% (up 40bps YoY), and adjusted EPS grew ~8% to $1.39. Retail EBITDAC margin declined (37.3% → 36.0%) on acquisition-revenue timing and litigation headwinds; Specialty margin rose (40.5% → 40.8%) on contingents. Margin durability is a genuine strength, but the composition — Specialty margin rising on volatile contingents while Retail margin fell — is lower quality than the headline suggests.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Q1'26 operating cash flow was $262M (up from $213M). Interest expense more than doubled YoY to $99M/quarter (~$400M/year run-rate), reflecting ~$4.2B of new senior notes (coupons 4.6%–6.25%, maturities through 2055). The Altman Z-Score screens in the "distress" zone (~1.0) and D/E is elevated (~2.4x), but this is partly an artifact of the intermediary model: large fiduciary balances (~$4.0B fiduciary liabilities, $3.9B fiduciary cash/receivables) and NFIP reinsurance flows inflate current liabilities without representing corporate debt. Real leverage rose materially post-Accession, and the higher-for-longer rate environment raises the cost of servicing and refinancing that stack. Covenants were in compliance as of 3/31/26.

**A GAAP EPS distortion to normalize.** The 4.4M escrowed shares (part of the ~$552M Accession indemnity escrow) are marked to market through the income statement. In Q1'26, BRO's falling stock produced a $64M non-cash, non-taxable gain that flattered GAAP results. If the stock recovers, this reverses into a headwind. Anchor on adjusted EPS ($1.39) and adjusted EBITDAC, not GAAP.

**Capital allocation.** Despite the debt load, management repurchased stock into the weakness: a $250M accelerated share repurchase (Feb–Mar 2026, ~3.57M shares at ~$69.93) with ~$1,150M remaining authorization, and raised the dividend 10% to $0.165/quarter. This signals management believes the stock is undervalued and leverage is manageable.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has deteriorated materially over the past two quarters, and the data confirm the trajectory rather than reverse it. Flat consolidated organic growth (and −2.0% in Specialty) is the sharpest signal, and management's guidance frames only a ~2.5% upper bound later in 2026 — a fraction of BRO's historical high-single/double-digit organic track record.

Consensus EPS has fallen across every horizon over 90 days: current quarter −2.8%, next quarter −2.5%, current year −1.1%, next year −2.7%. These are modest declines, not a collapse, but they run in the wrong direction and disqualify the strongest bullish setup in this framework — the combination of accelerating revenue and rapidly rising estimates that flags a fundamental turn. Here we have decelerating organic revenue and falling estimates, which is the mirror image.

The drivers of the deceleration are partly cyclical and partly idiosyncratic. Cyclically, the entire brokerage sector is facing softening property/CAT rates after a multi-year hard market — a sector-wide headwind that will eventually normalize and is not permanent impairment. Idiosyncratically, BRO's Specialty organic was dragged by a revenue-model change in specialty pharmacy consulting and a ~$10M competitor-poaching drag (a "start-up US broker" hiring teammates and taking accounts). BRO's numbers look weaker than best-in-class peers this cycle, which is the harder fact to dismiss.

The growth optionality that remains: Accession synergies ($30–40M target, admittedly modest), continued tuck-in M&A supported by the acquisition machine, and an eventual re-hardening of insurance pricing that would restore commission organic growth. None of these is a near-term catalyst with a specific date. The four consecutive earnings beats (Q3'25 +10.8%, others +0.8% to +3.0%) give some confidence that management guides conservatively, but the beats have been driven by margin discipline and contingents rather than organic revenue.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$66, BRO trades at ~14.7x consensus current-year EPS ($4.48) and ~13.6x next-year EPS ($4.84). The stated trailing/forward P/E of ~21x in the profile reflects GAAP EPS distorted by the escrow mark-to-market and acquisition amortization; on the adjusted earnings base the market actually watches, the multiple is materially lower.

This is a substantial de-rating from BRO's historical premium. Large-cap brokers (MMC, AJG, AON, WTW) have historically commanded 20-25x forward earnings on the strength of recurring revenue, high margins, and steady organic compounding. BRO now trades at a clear discount to that cohort, and the discount is justified: the company delivered flat organic while peers delivered positive organic, it carries meaningfully higher leverage post-Accession, and it faces ring-fenced but real 831(b) litigation exposure. The market is pricing BRO as the weakest organic grower in a high-quality peer group, which is a fair reading of the current data.

The valuation framework used here treats a "cheap" multiple as a value-trap warning absent an independent catalyst, and that caution applies directly. BRO looks inexpensive on adjusted earnings, but the cheapness reflects genuine growth deceleration, not a mispricing. There is no dated catalyst to force a re-rating; the next natural checkpoint is the Q2'26 print (late July 2026), where the swing factor is whether organic growth turns positive or continues to erode.

Analyst targets bracket the stock with limited asymmetry: low $55, mean $74.375, median $73, high $90. The current price of ~$66 sits below the mean, implying ~13% upside to consensus. But on a stock with falling estimates and no turn signal, analyst targets are not floors; they are as likely to be revised down as up if organic growth stays soft. This is not the setup (cheap relative to rising estimates) where consensus targets lag reality; it is the setup where targets track the same softening fundamentals.

We assign a valuation score of 5.5 — cheaper than peers with downside support from the recurring base and buybacks, but not asymmetric because the discount is earned and no catalyst exists to close it.

## Competitive Landscape

BRO's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-eroding on a trajectory basis, which matters more than absolute standing. The company remains a scale player with best-in-class margins and a proven M&A integration capability, and the Accession deal enlarges its Specialty footprint (Arrowhead Programs, Bridge Specialty, Lloyd's access) in a consolidating industry where scale confers carrier leverage and cross-sell breadth.

The concerning signals are on the trajectory. Organic growth lagging best-in-class peers this cycle suggests share is not being gained on a same-store basis. The competitor-poaching litigation — a start-up broker hiring away teammates and taking ~$10M of revenue — is a direct sign that BRO's decentralized, relationship-based model is vulnerable to talent flight, the core asset of any brokerage. The specialty pharmacy consulting revenue-model change points to pricing/structural pressure in a niche. None of these is existential, but the aggregate is a franchise holding position rather than extending it.

Insurance brokerage carries structural advantages that limit downside: revenue is recurring and sticky, switching costs for commercial clients are real, and the intermediary model is asset-light with limited underwriting risk. Those features protect BRO's floor. But the competitive-position score reflects trajectory, and the trajectory is flat-to-down, warranting a 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Organic growth / pricing cycle (primary risk).** Continued flat-to-negative organic growth is the single most important variable. Commission revenue is levered to premium rates; declining CAT property rates and moderating casualty increases directly compress organic growth. If the soft market persists into 2027, the growth stall extends and the multiple discount deepens.

**Accession integration and leverage.** The ~$7.5B deal was funded by $4.3B equity (43.1M shares at ~$102, well above the current price) plus ~$4.2B new debt. The doubled interest expense raises the stakes on Accession delivering both growth and its modest $30–40M synergy target. Integration missteps or synergy shortfalls would compound the organic problem.

**831(b) micro-captive / Oxford Risk Management litigation.** Accession (fka Risk Strategies) brought discontinued/run-off businesses under IRS scrutiny for 831(b) micro-captive tax structures. This is why the merger includes a ~$552M escrow (4.4M BRO shares + cash) to indemnify BRO. It is ring-fenced, but a genuine risk if claims exceed the escrow — a binary, hard-to-size contingent liability.

**Goodwill impairment.** The large goodwill balance post-Accession creates impairment risk if the acquired book underperforms or the growth environment stays weak — a non-cash but sentiment-negative tail.

**Escrow mark-to-market noise.** GAAP EPS is currently flattered by the non-cash gain from the falling share price marking down escrowed shares. A stock recovery reverses this into a GAAP headwind. Investors must normalize.

**Family/insider control and NFIP dependence.** Concentrated Brown-family voting influence (controlled-company considerations) and reliance on Congressional NFIP reauthorization are structural but longstanding, largely priced.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is mixed with a modest stress signal. Near-term ATM IV is 38.5% versus 30.8% historical vol — rich by ~7.6 points — and the term structure is in backwardation (38.5% near-term > 35.6% long-term), indicating the market is pricing imminent event stress, consistent with the upcoming Q2 print. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±8.8%, roughly the magnitude of the post-Q1 selloff. Skew is call-heavy at −5.3% (1-month) and the put/call OI ratio has fallen sharply from 0.95 to 0.35 since early June, suggesting the recent ~14% price recovery drew in bullish/call positioning rather than downside hedging. The recent price bounce has not been accompanied by heavy put demand, but the rich near-term IV and backwardation warn that the market expects a large move around earnings. Short interest at 7.3% of float (5.7 days to cover) is uncrowded and does not indicate a strong informed-short thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($85–90)
The organic deceleration is primarily a cyclical soft-market phenomenon that normalizes as insurance pricing re-hardens, restoring commission organic growth toward mid-single digits. Accession integrates cleanly, synergies land, and the enlarged Specialty platform compounds. Margins hold at ~38%+, adjusted EPS resumes double-digit growth toward $5+ in 2027, and the multiple re-rates back toward the broker-peer 20x on ~$5 earnings. Insider buying and the ASR near the lows prove prescient. Target $85–90, ~29–36% upside.

### Bear Case ($50–55)
The soft market persists, organic growth stays flat-to-negative through 2026 and into 2027, and BRO continues to lag best-in-class peers, confirming share erosion rather than a cyclical dip. Accession synergies disappoint against the interest burden, the 831(b) escrow proves inadequate, and a goodwill impairment surfaces. Estimates keep falling, the discount to peers widens, and the stock retests the ~$54 52-week low. Target $50–55, ~17–24% downside.

### Base Case ($66–72)
Organic growth stabilizes in low-single digits (~2–2.5% consistent with guidance), margins hold, and adjusted EPS grows high-single digits to ~$4.84 in the next fiscal year. The stock tracks earnings growth without meaningful multiple change, holding a ~14x forward multiple. Target ~$68–72 on next-year EPS, roughly in line to modestly above current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report (late July 2026), where the decisive variable is whether consolidated organic growth turns positive and Specialty organic recovers from −2.0%.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Consolidated organic revenue growth turns positive and accelerates above ~3% for two consecutive quarters (ex-contingents), signaling the soft-market drag is easing.
- Consensus EPS estimate revisions turn positive over a 30–90 day window, reversing the current broad-based declines.
- Evidence of clean Accession synergy capture (approaching or exceeding the $30–40M target) with leverage beginning to de-lever toward pre-deal norms, at a price still below ~$65.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Organic growth stays flat-to-negative through 2026 with management cutting the ~2.5% guidance, confirming structural rather than cyclical erosion.
- A goodwill impairment charge or an 831(b)/Oxford claim exceeding the ~$552M escrow.
- Adjusted EBITDAC margin compression below ~36% signaling loss of pricing/cost discipline, or covenant pressure from the debt stack in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

## Conclusion

Brown & Brown is a high-quality, asset-light insurance intermediary whose stock has been repriced ~37% lower for a specific and real reason: organic growth collapsed to 0.0% just as it took on a large, debt-funded acquisition. The quality attributes are intact — best-in-class adjusted EBITDAC margins, a recurring commission base, four consecutive earnings beats, disciplined capital allocation, and constructive insider buying with an aggressive buyback into the weakness. These support the downside and are why the stock is not an AVOID.

The two conditions that would make this a BUY — reaccelerating organic growth and rising estimates — are both absent. Organic growth is flat-to-negative, estimates are falling across every window, interest expense has doubled, and the valuation discount to peers is earned rather than mispriced. The 831(b) litigation is ring-fenced but hard to size, and no dated catalyst exists to force a re-rating before the Q2 print. This is a good business at a fair price with poor near-term momentum, which is a hold. We rate BRO **HOLD** with an uncertain rationale, and would move to BUY on confirmed organic reacceleration or to AVOID if the growth stall proves structural.
