**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Valvoline is now a pure-play automotive preventive-maintenance retailer, having sold its Global Products (branded lubricants) business to Aramco in March 2023. What remains is a 2,409-store quick-lube network (roughly half company-operated, half franchised) built around 15-minute stay-in-your-car oil changes and adjacent services. The operating core is strong: 19 consecutive years of system-wide same-store-sales growth, organic SSS that accelerated to 8.2% in the quarter ended March 31, 2026, and non-oil-change revenue running ~22% of sales and growing faster than the base. This is a fragmented ~$8-9B U.S. "Do-It-For-Me" market where a well-capitalized consolidator has room to run.

The complication is capital structure and reporting quality, not the store economics. The $637.4M Breeze Autocare acquisition (closed December 1, 2025) was funded with a $740M Term Loan B, pushing total debt from $1.07B at September 2025 to $1.66B by March 2026, cutting the fixed-rate share of debt roughly in half, and forcing a leverage-target reset to 1.5-2.5x net debt/EBITDA. Buybacks have been paused since Q2 FY2025 to prioritize deleveraging. Headline H1 FY2026 GAAP earnings collapsed (net income $12.0M vs. $129.2M), but that is optical: a $57.9M FTC-mandated divestiture loss, a $20M nondeductible-goodwill tax hit, and the absence of a prior-year $74.2M refranchising gain explain the entire swing while adjusted EBITDA rose 28%. Compounding the reporting concern, a material weakness in internal controls first disclosed in Q1 2024 remained unremediated as of March 31, 2026, now overlapping an acquisition-heavy period.

We maintain a HOLD with a constructive, growth-oriented lean. The stock has re-rated from the low-$30s (where insiders, including the CFO, bought on the open market in May 2026) to ~$39, leaving a rich ~22x forward multiple that already discounts continued execution. Estimate revisions are modestly positive (FY26 EPS +3.1% over 90 days, FY27 +2.9%), the SSS trajectory is intact, and the insider cluster is a coherent bullish tell. But at ~52x trailing earnings and with the balance sheet stretched, buybacks paused, and controls still in remediation, the asymmetry does not support a BUY. The prior three reports (April, May, June) each landed on HOLD; the improving trajectory (5.5 → 6.0) is confirmed by the data, but the higher price offsets the fundamental progress.

## Company Overview

Valvoline operates a single reportable segment ("Retail Services") comprising Valvoline Instant Oil Change (VIOC) in the U.S., Great Canadian Oil Change in Canada, the newly acquired Oil Changers brand (from Breeze), and 240+ Express Care licensed locations. The system had 2,409 stores (1,210 company-operated, 1,199 franchised), up from 2,180 at fiscal year-end September 2025. Franchisees, who run roughly half the network, are independent legal entities and are not consolidated.

The post-spin structure matters for how the business is analyzed. Valvoline sold Global Products in 2023 for a ~$1.57B pre-tax gain and retained the brand only for retail services; the divested entity (VGO, now Aramco-owned) owns the brand for product uses. Valvoline now buys substantially all of its lubricants from VGO under a long-term Supply Agreement. It is a customer of the brand it spun off, not a manufacturer, which reframes both its cost structure (lubricant supply is a purchased input tied to a single counterparty) and its capital intensity (asset-light, with a growing tilt toward franchise royalties via ongoing refranchising).

The competitive moat rests on brand trust (Valvoline traces its trademark to 1873), speed, the proprietary SuperPro service system, and franchise economics, rather than any low-cost or product-technology edge. Direct competitors include Take 5 (Driven Brands), Jiffy Lube (Shell), Grease Monkey, Express Oil Change, plus dealerships and independents.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 (ended September 30, 2025), continuing operations.** Revenue was $1,710M, up 5.6% YoY (FY24 $1,619M; FY23 $1,444M), driven by 6.1% system-wide SSS and 8.5% store growth, partly offset by refranchising. Gross margin was 38.5% (+30bps), operating income $389.9M (22.8% margin), and adjusted EBITDA $466.8M (+5.5%, 27.3% margin — stable). Net income was $210.7M with diluted EPS up ~2%.

Free cash flow was only $47.9M (down from $58.5M) against $259M of total capex, of which ~$193M was growth capex and ~$66M maintenance. The thin ~2.8% FCF margin is a function of deliberate growth reinvestment; maintenance-only FCF is closer to $241M. This is the crux of the financial-health debate: the bear (weak FCF, high leverage) and bull (high-ROIC growth reinvestment) cases point to the same numbers, and the swing factor is whether new-store returns materialize. That evidence will only be visible over the next several quarters as the Breeze cohort seasons.

**Q2 FY2026 (ended March 31, 2026).** Revenue was $503.8M, up 25% YoY, inflated by Breeze (331 net new stores YoY). Organic system-wide SSS of 8.2% (vs. 5.8% prior year) is the healthiest underlying signal. Gross margin slipped to 37.1% on new-store depreciation drag. Diluted EPS was $0.41 (a reported +18.1% surprise vs. the $0.35 consensus estimate) and adjusted EBITDA +28%. Six-month GAAP figures are distorted (H1 operating income $104.3M vs. $210.7M; H1 net income $12.0M vs. $129.2M), driven by the $57.9M FTC divestiture loss, the absence of a prior-year refranchising gain, and a 75.5% effective tax rate (the divestiture loss generated no tax benefit while nondeductible goodwill added ~$20M). These items are non-operational and largely one-time.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt rose from $1.07B (Sept 2025) to $1.66B (March 2026) after the $740M Term Loan B (5.67%, seven-year). Fixed-rate debt fell to ~32% from ~50%, raising rate sensitivity in a higher-for-longer environment. Altman Z-Score sits at 2.7 (grey zone) and the current ratio at 0.7. Stockholders' equity is thin ($353M against $3.4B assets), a legacy of the capital-light spin rather than distress; Piotroski is 4/5 with OCF exceeding net income. Goodwill and intangibles ballooned to $1.28B (Breeze added ~$524M goodwill), a future impairment risk if store economics disappoint. Management has paused buybacks ($325M authorized, unused) to deleverage toward the reset 1.5-2.5x target. FY2026 capex is guided to $250-280M.

The earnings-surprise record supports moderate confidence in forward numbers: three beats and one miss over the last four quarters (Q2 FY26 +18.1%, Q1 FY26 +12.3%, Q4 FY25 -4.7%, Q3 FY25 +3.4%).

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth engine is the strongest part of the story, extending a 19-year streak. Growth has three legs: franchisee expansion, opportunistic M&A, and new-store development, which together have grown the system roughly 50% over five years (+718 net stores). Premiumization and non-oil-change penetration are the margin lever — non-oil-change revenue was $110.6M of $503.8M in Q2 and growing faster than the base, lifting average ticket through mix and pricing.

Structural tailwinds are real: an aging vehicle parc (older cars require more service), rising miles driven, and a large fragmented aftermarket favorable to a consolidator. Estimate revisions are modestly positive rather than explosive — current-year EPS revised +3.1% over 90 days to $1.75, next-year +2.9% to $2.06, with next-quarter down 1.9%. This is steady positive momentum, not the +30% velocity that would flag a mispriced trough. On a two-year view, consensus implies EPS growth from ~$1.75 (FY26) to $2.06 (FY27), roughly 18%, consistent with mid-to-high single-digit SSS plus store growth and share-count stability once buybacks resume.

The principal growth risk is that the M&A leg of the strategy is now constrained. The 10-K explicitly flags limited targets remaining in the consolidation runway, and the FTC-forced Breeze divestiture (45 stores, $57.9M loss) demonstrates that antitrust friction will increasingly bind the consolidation thesis the company relies on. Secular EV adoption and lengthening OEM drain intervals are slow-moving headwinds to transaction frequency, partly offset by expanded coolant/fluid/grease opportunities and mobile service (NuWash/NuBrakes).

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$39 the stock trades at roughly 52x trailing EPS ($1.67 TTM inclusive of the depressed H1 GAAP figure) and ~22x forward FY26 consensus of $1.75, or ~19x FY27's $2.06. The trailing multiple is distorted by the FTC divestiture loss and tax hit and should be disregarded; the forward multiple is the relevant anchor. PEG on ~18% forward EPS growth is roughly 1.2 — not cheap, not egregious.

Peer comparison is complicated because the listed automotive-retail comps are franchised auto dealers and parts distributors, not quick-lube services, and trade at low multiples for structurally different reasons: GPI 12.5x, ABG 7.1x, AN 10.6x are cyclical dealership businesses; AAP at 54.1x is a distressed-earnings parts retailer; PHIN 22.9x is an aftermarket components supplier. The peer median of 12.5x is not a fair benchmark for a recurring-service retailer with a 19-year SSS streak and mid-20s EBITDA margins. Valvoline's closest true comp is Driven Brands' Take 5 franchise, which is not cleanly isolatable in the public comp set. On its own merits, ~22x forward for a high-single-digit organic grower with pricing power is a full but defensible multiple.

The multiple already discounts continued execution while several overhangs (leverage, paused buybacks, unremediated controls) argue for a discount rather than a premium; this is not a value trap. Analyst targets bracket the debate: low $35, high $48, mean $43.3, median $44 — implying modest upside to the mean but a target floor essentially at the current price. At 22x forward (below the 30x threshold where targets can be treated as stale floors, but not cheap enough to justify aggressive upside extrapolation), the consensus mean of ~$43 is a reasonable base-case anchor rather than a conservative floor. We treat cheapness skeptically in general; here the issue is the reverse — the stock is priced for success, so the valuation score reflects a balanced setup.

## Competitive Landscape

Valvoline holds a strong and improving position within a fragmented market. The 8.2% SSS acceleration, non-oil-change penetration gains, and 50% system expansion over five years all point to share capture and moat-widening within its niche. Brand trust, speed, and franchise economics are durable advantages against independents and dealerships, and the SuperPro system supports consistency across a scaling network.

Two trajectory qualifiers temper the score. First, the supplier relationship with VGO/Aramco creates a single-counterparty dependence for substantially all lubricant supply — a structural vulnerability distinct from the operating moat. Second, the FTC action signals that regulators view Valvoline's regional density as anticompetitive enough in specific markets to force divestitures, which will progressively raise the friction and cost of the M&A-driven expansion the company depends on. Take 5 (Driven Brands) remains an aggressive, well-funded consolidator competing for the same sites and franchisees. On balance the position is improving, but the consolidation runway is narrowing and the input-supply concentration is a genuine dependency.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks not fully reflected in a smooth-looking operating story are the reason the risk score is below neutral:

1. **Unremediated material weakness in internal controls (two-year saga).** First disclosed in Q1 2024 following the post-spin ERP implementation, disclosure controls were still "not effective" as of March 31, 2026 due to business-process control design deficiencies. Remediation is targeted for FY2026 but not guaranteed, and Breeze was excluded from the ICFR assessment for a year. Persistent control weakness during an acquisition-heavy period elevates financial-reporting risk beyond the "no material misstatements yet" language.

2. **Leverage and rate sensitivity.** Only ~32% of debt is fixed-rate; the current ratio is 0.7 and the Altman Z is 2.7 (grey). A higher-for-longer rate path raises the cost of the variable-rate tranche and the deleveraging math.

3. **Supplier concentration.** Substantially all lubricants come from VGO/Aramco under a long-term supply agreement; any interruption, quality issue, or brand-use dispute hits the retail business directly.

4. **Integration and consolidation-runway risk.** Breeze is the largest deal since the spin; the FTC divestiture already trimmed the acquired base and demonstrated antitrust constraints. Goodwill/intangibles create impairment exposure if store returns disappoint.

5. **Governance turnover.** Frequent Item 5.02 filings through 2026 (January, March, a July 23, 2026 departure) plus a new CAO signal ongoing management churn worth monitoring, though nothing in filings indicates it is adverse.

6. **Underfunded pension** (~$71.3M underfunded qualified; $151.7M net unfunded total) with mark-to-market accounting that produced a $26.6M remeasurement loss in FY2025 — a source of earnings volatility.

7. **Secular EV/drain-interval pressure** on transaction frequency, partly offset by fluid-service expansion.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV is 61.8% against 1-year historical vol of 30.7% — rich, in the 83rd percentile of the current chain — with the term structure in backwardation (near-term 61.8% > long-term 51.6%), which typically flags an imminent catalyst (the FY26 earnings print). Put/call skew of 7.1% shows elevated put demand, consistent with the 12.3% short interest and 6.4 days to cover. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±13.4%. ATM IV has already compressed from 100.1% on June 17 to 68.0% on July 23, so some event premium has bled off. The combination of elevated put skew and double-digit short interest is a divergence to flag against a constructive fundamental lean: institutions are positioned defensively into the print, and the rich IV means the market is discounting elevated volatility rather than complacency.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Organic SSS is accelerating and the streak is intact, non-oil-change penetration is expanding margins, and the system continues to grow. The Breeze integration seasons well, new-store ROICs prove out, deleveraging progresses toward target, buybacks resume in FY26-27 on the $325M authorization, and the material weakness is remediated. Insiders — including the CFO's $318K open-market purchase at $31.80 and two directors — bought the dip near lows, and JP Morgan reversed its Underweight to Neutral. Forward EPS reaches ~$2.06 in FY27, and a 22-24x multiple supports a $48-50 price.

**Bear case.** Leverage constrains flexibility, the variable-rate tranche bites as rates stay high, and the FTC-limited M&A runway caps the growth algorithm. New-store returns disappoint, goodwill impairment surfaces, and the material weakness persists into a fourth year, denting reporting confidence. EV/drain-interval headwinds erode transaction frequency faster than fluid-service growth offsets. A consumer discretionary slowdown pressures ticket and volume. The stock derates toward 15-16x forward, ~$31-33, back to the insider-buy zone.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a 12-month horizon, classified as a growth hold — the thesis is a positive-appreciation case gated by valuation and balance-sheet risk rather than an income story (no dividend, buybacks paused).

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) material weakness formally remediated in the FY2026 10-K; (2) net debt/EBITDA confirmed inside the 1.5-2.5x target with buybacks resumed on the $325M authorization; (3) a pullback toward the low-$30s (insider-buy zone) with SSS holding ≥6%, restoring asymmetric upside; or (4) FY26 EPS estimate-revision velocity accelerating above +8% on continued SSS beats.
- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) organic SSS decelerating below ~4% YoY; (2) a goodwill/intangible impairment charge on the Breeze cohort; (3) the material weakness persisting past FY2026 with any restatement or misstatement; or (4) a leverage-driven covenant issue or a downgrade in credit posture.
- **Reassessment timeline:** Next fiscal-year-end results and the FY2026 10-K (control-remediation status), plus each quarterly SSS print. The elevated near-term IV and backwardation point to the upcoming earnings report as the next event.

## Conclusion

Valvoline's operating business is one of the more durable in consumer retail: recurring, needs-based demand, 19 years of SSS growth, accelerating comps, and pricing power in a fragmented market. The trajectory of our own scoring (5.5 → 6.0 across three reports) is confirmed by the data, and the May 2026 insider-buying cluster near the lows is a coherent bullish signal. But the equity today is priced for continued success at ~22x forward while carrying a stretched, more rate-sensitive balance sheet, paused buybacks, a narrowing M&A runway, and a two-year unremediated material weakness. The stock has re-rated ~25% off its lows since insiders bought, which offsets the fundamental progress and removes the asymmetric entry.

We maintain HOLD. The path to a BUY is monitorable: finish the controls remediation, prove the Breeze economics, deleverage, and resume capital return — or get a better entry price. Until then, the risk/reward is balanced. Base case ~$43 (mean target, ~22x FY27 $2.06 with modest re-rating), bull ~$48-50, bear ~$31-33.
