**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Constellation Brands is now effectively a single-product company: Mexican import beer accounts for roughly 91% of net sales and essentially all operating income, anchored by Modelo Especial (the #1 U.S. beer brand by dollar sales), Corona, and Pacifico, sold under an exclusive perpetual license. The stock has fallen ~13% over the past twelve months to ~$139, sits ~22% below its 52-week high, and trades at ~14x forward earnings — well under half the Consumer Staples peer median P/E of ~32x. The market is pricing genuine deterioration, and the central question is whether that deterioration is fundamental and durable or a cyclical demand trough that has overshot.

The bear case is real: beer shipments fell 5.0% and depletions fell 2.7% over the first nine months of FY2026, consensus EPS estimates have fallen ~4.5% over 90 days for both the current and next fiscal year, management cut FY2027 operating margin guidance to 37–38% from 39–40%, and the company is bringing on the new Veracruz brewery (raising fixed costs) into a declining volume base. Secular pressure on alcohol consumption (moderation, GLP-1 adoption, younger-cohort abstention) compounds a near-term Hispanic-consumer demand softness tied to value-seeking behavior and immigration-enforcement effects on social occasions.

Two facts cut against an outright negative call. First, the two most recent reported quarters beat consensus by 9.5% (Q4 FY2026) and 15.0% (the prior quarter), with a clean track record of two beats and two in-line prints and zero misses over the available window. A thesis premised on the company "catching down" to reality is materially weakened when the company has out-earned its own lowered estimates twice in a row; the estimates may have over-corrected. Second, at ~14x forward earnings with ~$2.1B of nine-month operating cash flow and an investment-grade balance sheet, much of the deterioration is already in the price. I rate STZ a HOLD with an honest "uncertain" classification: the evidence does not support a confident appreciation thesis (estimates are still falling, not stabilizing), nor does it support shorting a cash-generative franchise near trough valuation with a recent beat record and a Buy-skewed Street.

## Company Overview

Constellation reports in three segments — Beer, Wine & Spirits, and Corporate Operations & Other — but the franchise is the beer business. Over the first nine months of FY2026, Beer generated $6.59B of $7.22B consolidated net sales (~91%) and comparable operating income of $2.59B, against a Wine & Spirits unit running near break-even and Corporate as a cost center.

The beer portfolio is a legacy of the 2013 Anheuser-Busch InBev/Grupo Modelo antitrust divestiture, which granted Constellation an exclusive, perpetual license to produce the Mexican beer portfolio in Mexico and import, market, and sell it in the United States. Production is concentrated at the Nava and Obregón breweries, with the new Veracruz facility under construction. Constellation is the #2 U.S. beer company and the #1 dollar-share gainer in the overall U.S. beer market, driven by premiumization and pricing.

The Wine & Spirits segment has been deliberately shrunk into a smaller "exclusively higher-end" portfolio (Robert Mondavi, Kim Crawford, The Prisoner, High West, Casa Noble, Mi CAMPO, Meiomi, Sea Smoke). Mainstream wine brands and SVEDKA vodka were divested in 2024–2025. The FY2025 $2.74B Wine & Spirits goodwill impairment wrote that reporting unit's goodwill to zero, which dominates the year-over-year GAAP comparison.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is contracting, with deliberate and organic components.** Consolidated net sales fell ~10% YoY in both Q3 and the nine months of FY2026 (9M: $7.22B vs $8.04B). The majority of the decline is the Wine & Spirits divestitures (~$459M of lost 9M sales), but beer net sales also fell ~4% on the shipment and depletion declines, only partly offset by pricing. Full-year FY2026 revenue was ~$9.76B, down ~11% from ~$10.96B in FY2025.

**The GAAP earnings rebound is an accounting artifact, not an operating inflection.** FY2026 GAAP net income of ~$1.69B compares to a FY2025 net loss of ~$81M purely because the wine impairment did not repeat. Nine-month net income to CBI was $1.48B vs $294M, almost entirely the absence of that impairment. Underlying beer operating income was down ~7% over the nine months. Investors should not mistake the headline rebound for fundamental improvement.

**Core beer margins are healthy but compressing.** Beer comparable operating margin was ~39.3% over the nine months, down from 40.5%. The pressure comes from aluminum tariffs ($43.1M in 9M, ~$70M full-year beer impact), unfavorable fixed-cost absorption from lower production, and FX, partly offset by more than $145M of procurement and logistics savings. The margin guidance cut reflects Veracruz fixed-cost absorption, higher SG&A, and incremental marketing (~9% of beer sales).

**Cash flow remains strong; leverage is the headline balance-sheet concern.** Nine-month operating cash flow was $2.11B (down from $2.56B), with capex of $656M (down from $931M). Total debt is ~$10.66B, almost entirely fixed-rate senior notes, against a 4.0x net leverage covenant ceiling the company complies with. The Altman Z-Score of ~1.7 sits in the technical distress zone, but that reading is depressed by the leverage and a shrunken post-buyback/impairment equity base; it is mitigated by stable high-margin beer cash flows, investment-grade access (commercial paper at ~4.2%), and $1.86B of revolver headroom. The company issued $500M 4.80% notes (May 2025) and $500M 4.95% notes (Oct 2025) while redeeming $1.4B of older notes; net debt is down ~7% since February 2025.

**Capital return is aggressive relative to a shrinking top line.** Nine-month buybacks were $824M and dividends $539M (~$1.36B combined), funded partly by debt issuance and ~$846M of wine-divestiture proceeds. A new $4.0B authorization (April 2025, through FY2028) has ~$3.18B remaining; the dividend is $1.02/quarter (~2.9% yield). Returning capital faster than debt reduction, with a distress-zone Z-Score, is a defensible-but-watchable allocation choice, particularly with a new CEO who may reset priorities.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth picture is the weakest part of the story. Consensus estimates are broadly falling: current-year EPS revised from $12.37 (90 days ago) to $11.79 (-4.6%), and next-year from $12.99 to $12.41 (-4.5%), with consistent if modest 30-day declines. This is negative earnings momentum, the opposite of the signal that would warrant a cyclical re-rate.

The volume picture is the crux. Beer depletions and shipments reflect a mix of secular and cyclical pressure. Secularly, total U.S. alcohol consumption faces moderation trends, GLP-1 adoption, and lower younger-cohort participation; wine is in structural decline (the rationale for the mainstream-wine exit). Cyclically, management attributes "suppressed" beer demand to socioeconomic factors among value-seeking and Hispanic consumers, the latter affected by immigration-enforcement effects on out-of-home social occasions.

The bull growth case rests on a volume reacceleration that the data does not yet confirm but that several factors could support: easier comparisons in FY2027, World Cup marketing tailwinds in 2026, premiumization continuing to deliver dollar-share gains even in a flat-to-declining category, and the >$200M net annualized restructuring savings targeted by FY2028. TD Cowen's April 2026 upgrade argues FY2027 beer guidance is overly conservative for these reasons.

I weight this as a low-single-digit grower at best over the medium term, with genuine downside if volume does not stabilize. The growth score of 4.0 reflects falling estimates and declining volumes; it is not lower because dollar-share gains, pricing power, and a credible cost program prevent the franchise from being in outright structural collapse.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$139 and ~14x forward EPS, STZ trades at a steep discount to the Consumer Staples peer median P/E of ~32x (Hershey 33.7x, Church & Dwight 32.0x, Bunge 33.0x). Even the cheaper discount-retail peers (Dollar General 16.2x, Dollar Tree 18.5x) trade above STZ. The 2.9% dividend yield exceeds the 2.1% peer median.

The discount is partly warranted: STZ is shrinking while several peers are growing, and it carries higher leverage and a single-category concentration that the staples peer set does not. But a low multiple alone is a value-trap warning, not a buy signal, and I am explicit about not rewarding cheapness in the absence of a stabilization catalyst. The key valuation question is whether 14x reflects over-correction.

Here the recent beat record matters and deserves direct engagement. The beats are against repeatedly lowered bars, which is the textbook setup for upward target revisions if a single quarter shows volume stabilization. The Street agrees on direction: the consensus mean target is ~$175.59 and median ~$177.50 (low $117, high $209), implying ~26% upside to the mean, with a Buy-skewed distribution (3 strong buy, 11 buy, 7 hold, 2 strong sell, 0 sell).

What keeps me from scoring valuation higher (and from upgrading to BUY) is that the beat history co-exists with still-falling forward estimates. Consensus continues to mark down both FY2027 and FY2028. Margin guidance was cut, not raised. A re-rate toward the consensus target requires the volume line to turn, and the data does not yet show that turn. I assign valuation a 6.0 — above neutral because the discount to both peers and targets is large and the beat record argues the estimates may be conservative, but not a high-conviction mispricing call because the catalyst (a confirmed depletion turn) has not arrived.

## Competitive Landscape

Constellation's competitive position within beer is strong and arguably still improving on a relative basis: it is the #1 dollar-share gainer in U.S. beer and out-executes ABI and Molson Coors in the high-end import lane, with pricing power and a perpetual, defensible license. Modelo Especial's #1 dollar-share position is a durable asset. The peer contrast is favorable on the spirits side: Diageo rebased its dividend in early 2026, signaling distress among spirits-heavy peers, while Constellation's beer-led model has held up comparatively better operationally.

The deterioration is at the category and demographic level, not in Constellation's relative standing. The company is gaining share of a flat-to-declining pool. That is a meaningfully different (and better) position than a market leader losing share, but it caps the upside: dollar-share gains in a shrinking category produce low single-digit growth at best. I score competitive position 6.0 — a stable-to-improving relative moat offset by a category that is structurally challenged and a concentration that leaves no diversification cushion.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that matter for this specific company:

1. **Mexican beer and tariff/trade concentration.** Production is concentrated in Mexico under a single perpetual license, and ~$83M of FY2026 tariff costs (aluminum plus Mexican/EU/NZ inputs) are a structural margin tax that escalates if U.S.-Mexico friction worsens. This is a first-order, asymmetric risk that is only partly priced.

2. **Hispanic-consumer and secular demand softness.** A large share of Modelo/Corona/Pacifico volume is tied to Hispanic consumers, and immigration enforcement plus value-seeking behavior have suppressed demand. Layered on secular moderation/GLP-1 trends, this is the central debate and the main source of estimate cuts.

3. **Beer-focused securities litigation.** The Meza putative class action (W.D.N.Y., §10(b)/§20(a)) amended its complaint in July 2025 to pivot from wine to allege misleading statements about beer prospects, the segment that is 91% of sales. A motion to dismiss is pending; related derivative suits (Silva, Mason) are stayed. The pivot to beer makes this more relevant to the core thesis than typical staples litigation.

4. **Veracruz stranded-capacity risk.** Bringing on capacity into a declining volume base pressures FY2027 margins and creates impairment risk if volume does not recover, echoing the wine playbook.

5. **Capital-allocation and leverage.** ~$10.7B debt with ~$1.8B maturing in FY2028, a distress-zone Z-Score, and buybacks outpacing debt reduction. A new CEO may reprioritize.

6. **Management transition and weak alignment signal.** New CEO Nicholas Fink arrived ~April 2026 with a large sign-on package; five-year cumulative TSR of 80.3 trails the peer group's 166.9 by roughly 2:1, while outgoing CEO Newlands' FY2026 comp was $14.2M. No insiders have bought on the open market at depressed prices; recent Form 4 activity is routine grants/tax-withholding plus minor director/exec sales.

The risk score of 4.5 reflects that several of these risks (tariff escalation, the beer-focused litigation outcome, Veracruz stranding) are binary and not fully priced, partly offset by the franchise's cash generation and the fact that the demand debate is at least partly in the multiple.

### Options Market Signal

The headline 112.5% near-term ATM IV is misleading and should be discounted. It sits on the 1-week tenor with one day to expiry, while IV is 43.6% / 34.6% / 33.4% at the 1-month / 3-month / 6-month tenors, a normal, slightly elevated structure. The apparent backwardation is end-of-contract pin/expiry noise, not a structural signal: the last earnings print was April 8, 2026, and no earnings catalyst is imminent as of the data date. The cleaner reads are the 1.29 put/call open-interest ratio and the modest term-structure put skew (-2 to -3% at 3–6 months), which indicate mildly defensive positioning consistent with a beaten-down name, not a market pricing an acute event. I do not treat the options data as confirming a bearish thesis; it is roughly neutral-to-mildly-cautious.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$175–209):** Beer volume stabilizes and reaccelerates on easier FY2027 comps, World Cup marketing, and easing Hispanic-consumer pressure. The two consecutive beats prove estimates over-corrected, and consensus revises targets upward toward the $175 mean / $209 high. The restructuring savings and premiumization restore margins above the 37–38% guide. At a re-rate toward ~16–18x on recovering ~$12+ EPS, the stock returns to the mid-$170s+, with the high target requiring a clean volume turn plus multiple normalization toward the staples cohort.

**Bear Case (~$117):** Hispanic-consumer and secular alcohol declines persist, beer depletions stay negative, Veracruz fixed costs strand and pressure margins below guidance, and a tariff escalation against Mexico hits COGS. Estimates continue falling, the Meza suit survives its motion to dismiss, and the multiple compresses further toward the analyst low of $117 (~10x on declining earnings). This is roughly -16% from the current price.

**Base Case (~$148):** Volumes stabilize but do not meaningfully reaccelerate; the company continues to beat conservatively-set bars while top line stays roughly flat-to-down low single digits; margins land near the 37–38% guide. EPS holds near ~$11.80–12.00, and the multiple drifts modestly higher from a depressed ~14x toward ~12.5x as the worst fears ease, producing a low-single-digit return plus the ~2.9% dividend.

The reason this is a HOLD and not an AVOID: an AVOID at ~14x forward, near a 52-week low, ~26% below the consensus mean, with a Buy-skewed Street and a recent two-quarter beat record, is the textbook setup for being wrong on a depressed name that subsequently rallies. The bearish "catch-down" argument requires evidence that FY2027 numbers remain too high given the beats, and the beats argue the opposite, that estimates may have over-corrected. I do not have falsifiable evidence that the deterioration is fundamental-and-unpriced beyond secular handwaving. The reason it is not a BUY: estimates are still falling, margin guidance was cut, the volume turn is unconfirmed, and there is no insider conviction buying to corroborate the value-trap-versus-value debate.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly print (Q1 FY2027, expected ~late June/early July 2026) is the key data point for the volume-stabilization debate, followed by the pending Meza motion-to-dismiss ruling.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Beer depletions turn positive (or improve sequentially to flat) for two consecutive quarters, confirming a volume bottom.
- Consensus EPS estimates cease falling and begin to be revised upward, particularly for the current and next fiscal years.
- Open-market insider buying by the new CEO or directors at current depressed levels, signaling internal conviction.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A reported earnings miss (breaking the beat streak) accompanied by a further FY2027 guidance cut.
- Tariff escalation specifically targeting Mexican beer imports, materially raising beer COGS beyond the current ~$70M run-rate.
- The Meza securities suit surviving its motion to dismiss with the beer-focused allegations intact, or evidence of a Veracruz impairment.
- Net leverage breaching toward the 4.0x covenant ceiling, or a buyback pace that forces a dividend or capital-allocation strain.

## Conclusion

Constellation is a high-quality, cash-generative premium-import beer franchise trading at a steep discount to its Consumer Staples peers and to Street targets, fighting genuine volume declines, falling forward estimates, tariff exposure, and a beer-focused securities suit. The recent two-quarter beat record (9.5% and 15.0%) is the single most important fact arguing the estimates may have over-corrected, and it is the principal reason I do not issue an AVOID on a depressed, Buy-skewed name near its lows. The absence of a confirmed volume turn, continued downward estimate revisions, a cut to margin guidance, and no insider buying prevent a confident BUY. The call is HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the risk/reward is roughly balanced around a base case near current levels, and the next one to two quarters of depletion data will resolve whether this is a cyclical trough that has overshot or the early innings of a structural derate. I would become constructive on a confirmed volume stabilization plus an end to estimate cuts; I would turn negative on a guidance cut plus a broken beat streak.
