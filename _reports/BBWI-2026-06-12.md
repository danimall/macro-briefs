**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Bath & Body Works is a single-segment specialty retailer of personal care and home fragrance — body care, Wallflowers and home fragrance, soaps and sanitizers — running 1,923 company-operated stores plus 579 partner-operated international locations. The business sits between two opposing forces: a secularly declining store base losing both traffic and market share, and a balance sheet that throws off roughly $865M of free cash flow (11.9% FCF margin) while paying a ~4.4% dividend. The stock is down about 28% over the trailing year and roughly 45% from its July 2025 high of $33.96, and now trades near $18.82 at approximately 6–7x earnings.

The operating trend is genuinely deteriorating. FY2026 guidance calls for sales down 4.5% to 2.5% and adjusted EPS of $2.40–$2.65, a step-down of roughly 21% from FY2025's $3.21. Q1 FY2026 sales fell 3.2%, store productivity dropped 5.7%, and the reported $0.90 EPS was flattered by $150M of one-offs (an $88M interchange-litigation settlement plus a $62M tax benefit); stripping those out, adjusted EPS was $0.32, down 35% year-over-year, with adjusted operating income off 28%. A new CEO (ex-Nike Daniel Heaf) is running an unproven multi-year "Consumer First Formula" turnaround, and lost his CFO mid-transformation in May 2026.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID is that the bear thesis is substantially in the price, and three offsetting signals point away from terminal impairment. First, the November 2025 single-day collapse (–25% on 41M shares versus a ~6M average) shows the structural concerns were already violently repriced. Second, six directors bought stock on the open market at $14.40–$15.58 in November 2025 — the strongest form of insider signal — and the current price is only ~20–30% above their cost. Third, full-year consensus has firmed (current-year EPS +1.3% over 90 days, next-year EPS $2.87, +2.2%), implying recovery off a FY2026 reinvestment trough rather than a falling-knife revision profile. With the sell-side carrying zero sell ratings and a median target of $23, the balance of evidence describes a depressed name where positive and negative roughly cancel.

## Company Overview

BBWI was created by the 2021 spin-off of Victoria's Secret from L Brands and retains that separation's capital structure: heavy debt and negative shareholders' equity. Its competitive moat historically rested on a vertically integrated, fast-turn supply chain (third-party manufacturers clustered near its Columbus, Ohio HQ for speed and agility), a large loyalty program, dominant U.S. fragrance positioning, and a brand built on "affordable indulgence, gifting and self-care." More than half its stores are now off-mall, the product of a deliberate multi-year real-estate shift.

The central story is a turnaround. CEO Daniel Heaf joined in Q2 FY2025 and launched the Consumer First Formula in Q3 FY2025: (1) drive innovation in hero categories while exiting laggards (hair, men's grooming); (2) reignite the brand through creators and marketing; (3) expand distribution onto Amazon (~7% of store assortment today) and into college bookstores; and (4) cut $250M of costs over two years ($175M in FY2026) to fund reinvestment. Management has been explicit that FY2026 is a dilutive reinvestment year and that the payoff is not expected to be visible until late FY2026/FY2027.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is in slow secular decline.** Net sales went $7.56B (FY2023) → $7.43B (FY2024) → $7.31B (FY2025) → $7.29B (FY2026, –0.2%). Q1 FY2026 sales fell 3.2% to $1.378B, driven by fewer transactions partially offset by higher average order size. Store productivity is eroding faster than headline sales: sales per average store –1% in FY2025 and –5.7% in Q1 FY2026; sales per square foot –5.8% in Q1. That gap between aggregate sales and per-store productivity is the clearest operating warning in the file.

**Margins are compressing, primarily on tariffs.** Operating margin fell from 17.3% (FY2024) to 15.4% (FY2025); gross margin 44.3% → 43.7%, explicitly attributed to tariffs. Q1 FY2026 gross margin dropped to 42.6% from 45.4%, citing tariffs, inflation, and crude oil (a fragrance/packaging input) plus deleverage on lower sales.

**Earnings quality requires care.** FY2025 GAAP net income was $649M ($3.11 diluted EPS), down from $798M; adjusted EPS $3.21 versus $3.29. The Q1 FY2026 reported $0.90 is misleading — it includes the $88M pre-tax interchange settlement gain (income to BBWI as a merchant-plaintiff, not operating revenue) and a $62M tax benefit. Adjusted for those, EPS was $0.32 versus $0.49 (–35%) and adjusted operating income fell from $209M to $151M (–28%). The "beat" was real against lowered guidance, but underlying profitability deteriorated.

**Cash flow remains genuinely strong.** FY2026 operating cash flow was $1.102B and FCF $865M (11.9% FCF margin), up from $660M. The business is highly cash-generative even while shrinking, which is the core support under the dividend and the downside.

**The balance sheet is the headline risk, but it is mischaracterized by screening tools.** Total debt is ~$3.6B (after redeeming the $284M 2027 Notes in Q1), all fixed-rate at a ~7% average coupon, laddered across 2028–2037 with no near-term wall. Stockholders' equity is negative (–$1.13B at Q1 FY2026), a legacy of the spin-off and years of buybacks rather than operating losses. That negative equity single-handedly pushes the Altman Z-Score to 1.77 ("distress zone"), but the signal is a false positive on solvency: liquidity is sound ($820M cash, $544M undrawn ABL), debt leverage is 2.7x EBITDAR, the current ratio is 1.27, ratings are BB+/Ba2 (stable), the fixed-charge covenant is not currently in effect, and the Piotroski F-score is healthy (5/5 of measured signals). Negative equity does constrain financial flexibility, but near-term default risk is low.

**Capital allocation is pivoting from buybacks toward deleveraging and reinvestment.** FY2026 repurchases were $400M, but Q1 FY2026 saw zero buybacks as cash was redirected to retiring the 2027 Notes. The dividend was held flat at $0.20/quarter ($0.80/year, ~4.4% yield); $117M remains on the authorization. The supplier-finance program balance grew to $154M, a working-capital lever worth monitoring.

## Growth Analysis

There is no credible near-term revenue growth thesis. Guidance is for sales to decline 2.5–4.5% in FY2026, and the company underperformed its own sector in FY2025 by management's own admission, which means share loss layered on top of a soft category. Transactions are falling, and the reliance on physical retail (more than half off-mall) leaves BBWI more exposed to traffic erosion than online-native competitors.

The growth case, such as it is, rests on self-help and mix rather than the existing store base. The Amazon channel (~7% of assortment) shows early strength in men's, aromatherapy, and Wallflowers — categories that underperform in BBWI's own stores — suggesting incremental reach to younger and lapsed consumers. College bookstores broaden the funnel. The $250M cost program ($175M in FY2026) is intended to fund reinvestment and protect margins against tariffs. None of this is proven, and management has explicitly told shareholders the payoff is a FY2027 story.

The estimate-revision picture is more constructive than the operating headlines suggest, and it matters for framing. Current-year consensus EPS sits at $2.64, up 0.5% over 30 days and 1.3% over 90 days; next-year EPS is $2.87, up 2.2% over 90 days. The current-quarter estimate was revised up 20.5% over 30 days. This is stable-to-rising, not a falling-knife profile, and next-year EPS of $2.87 implies recovery off the FY2026 reinvestment trough. The single soft spot is next-quarter EPS, revised down 10.5% (30d) and 16.2% (90d), consistent with front-loaded reinvestment. The combination of trough adjusted earnings this year with firm full-year and next-year consensus is the signature of a reinvestment cycle, not terminal decline, though it does not yet validate the turnaround.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$18.82 the stock trades at roughly 6–7x trailing/forward earnings. Against the Other Specialty Retail peer set, that is a deep discount: peer median P/E is 31.3, with the closer fragrance/specialty comps ULTA at 17.9x and TSCO at 15.4x. BBWI's discount to even the cheaper comps is wide. The ~4.4% dividend yield is well above the peer-median 1.0% and is covered multiple times over by FCF.

The discount is not unearned. A secularly declining, share-losing, brick-and-mortar-heavy retailer with negative book equity and an unproven turnaround deserves to trade below growing specialty peers. The relevant question is whether the discount is now excessive relative to the deterioration, and the evidence suggests it is roughly fair-to-slightly-cheap rather than a clear mispricing. On the bull side, the FCF yield is ~22% on the equity, the dividend is secure, and consensus implies recovery to $2.87 next year, at which point even a modest 8–9x multiple would put the stock above current levels. On the bear side, the multiple has been low for a reason and could stay low if traffic keeps eroding and the tariff drag persists, because a low P/E on declining earnings is a value trap.

The November 2025 –25% single-day gap (from $21.04 to $15.82) is important context. The stock has roundtripped from those lows back into the high teens, which is evidence that the worst-case scenario is no longer the consensus. On balance the valuation is supportive enough to anchor the downside but not so dislocated that it forces a BUY without operating confirmation. I score valuation moderately above neutral.

Note on analyst targets: the price-target distribution is wide and stale. Median is $23 (~19% above current), mean $25.57, with a $54 high that reflects an outlier turnaround-success scenario. There are zero sell ratings across all periods (4 buy / 13 hold / 0 sell). The mean and median sit above the current price, which is inconsistent with an at-no-price avoidance thesis.

## Competitive Landscape

BBWI competes in a fragmented personal-care and home-fragrance market against specialty, department, discount, and online retailers. Management argues its categories are healthy and that value-seeking consumers still spend on "small emotionally rewarding indulgences." The problem is that BBWI underperformed its own sector in FY2025, which converts a soft-category narrative into a share-loss reality. The –5.7% Q1 store productivity decline is the cleanest evidence the competitive position is weakening, not stabilizing.

The Amazon and college-bookstore pushes are sensible attempts to reach consumers where they already shop, and the early Amazon strength in men's, aromatherapy, and Wallflowers suggests untapped demand outside the legacy store footprint. But heavy brick-and-mortar reliance remains a structural disadvantage as traffic shifts online, and pushing into third-party channels risks cannibalizing owned channels and diluting brand positioning. The trajectory is deteriorating, which I weight more heavily than the still-leading absolute position, so the score sits below neutral.

## Risk Assessment

- **Execution risk on the Consumer First Formula** is a newly added/expanded risk factor: a new CEO, an unproven multi-year strategy, near-term EPS dilution, and the risk that exiting categories and pushing onto Amazon cannibalize owned channels.
- **Leadership instability is escalating.** Beyond the FY2025 CEO transition, CFO Eva Boratto announced her departure (to Cencora) on 5/27/2026, effective 6/12/2026, with a long-tenured insider (Tom Javitch) named interim. Multiple Item 5.02 filings across 2024–2026 indicate sustained C-suite churn during a critical turnaround.
- **Tariffs are the dominant, two-sided margin driver.** They are the named cause of gross-margin erosion, but the February 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA decision invalidated certain tariffs, creating a potential refund tailwind that BBWI has not recognized (timing/amount uncertain). The government re-imposed tariffs under alternative authorities, so net exposure continues. This is the single most important unquantified swing factor for FY2026/27.
- **Pressured consumer / declining traffic** in a discretionary category, with falling transactions and heavy physical-retail exposure.
- **Supply-chain concentration** in central Ohio with a limited vendor base (top vendor ~12%, top five ~40%) and China-sourcing UFLPA compliance risk.
- **Negative equity / leverage** constrains flexibility and depresses headline solvency scores even though near-term default risk is low.

Many of these risks are at least partly priced after the ~45% drawdown from highs, which is why I score risk near neutral rather than low. The genuinely unpriced items are the tariff swing (two-sided) and the CFO-transition execution risk.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 53.5% against 1-year historical vol of 57.8%, so options are modestly cheap (–4.3% spread) with IV in only the 12th percentile of the current chain — the market is not pricing acute near-term event risk. The term structure is in contango (53.5% near vs 63.5% at six months), a normal/complacent shape. Put/call skew of 18.0% on the front month shows elevated put demand, but the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.24 and OI ratio 0.89, so this is moderate hedging rather than aggressive bearish positioning. The clearest change is the collapse in put/call OI ratio from 3.58 to 0.89 alongside a +13.3% price move since mid-May; bearish positioning has unwound materially as the stock recovered off the lows. Net, the options market is not corroborating a strong directional bear case; it reads as two-sided with mild downside hedging.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** A deeply cash-generative ($865M FCF, ~22% FCF yield on equity), dividend-paying (~4.4%, well covered) franchise trades at 6–7x earnings versus a high-teens-to-30s peer set. Six directors deployed personal capital on the open market at $14.40–$15.58 in November 2025, roughly 20–30% below the current price, the strongest insider signal available. Consensus has firmed (full-year +1.3% over 90 days, next-year EPS $2.87 implying recovery). Self-help levers — $250M cost program, Amazon/college distribution, continued deleveraging — give management room to inflect adjusted operating income in FY2027. If traffic stabilizes and a tariff refund materializes, the stock re-rates toward the mid-$20s on the existing earnings base alone, with the $54 outlier target reflecting full turnaround success. Bull case ~$30 (roughly the prior 52-week-high zone, ~15x normalized $2.00–$2.20 adjusted EPS), +59% from current.

**Bear Case.** The Consumer First Formula is an unproven strategy run by a new CEO who just lost his CFO mid-transformation. Store productivity is falling 5.7%, the company is losing share within its own sector, tariffs continue to compress gross margin, and FY2026 adjusted EPS is guided down ~21%. A low multiple on declining earnings is a value trap; if traffic erosion accelerates and the cost program fails to offset tariff drag, adjusted EPS keeps sliding and the multiple stays depressed or compresses further. Bear case ~$14–15 (back toward the November 2025 lows and the directors' purchase zone), –20% to –26% from current. The dividend and FCF provide a soft floor near those levels.
