**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

BOK Financial is a ~$52 billion-asset super-regional bank holding company headquartered in Tulsa, operating an 8-state Southwest footprint anchored by a dominant Oklahoma franchise (14% statewide deposit share, 33% in Tulsa). It earned $578.0M in net income and $9.17 diluted EPS in 2025, up from $8.14 in 2024, with net interest margin expanding to 2.87% from 2.65% on a deliberate funding-mix shift from wholesale borrowings into interest-bearing deposits. The franchise carries two structural features that distinguish it from peer regionals: an unusually high 38% fee-income mix (fiduciary/asset management AUM/AUA of $126.6B, the TransFund EFT network, and an agency-MBS trading/mortgage-finance niche), and a controlling 63% ownership stake held by George Kaiser.

The fundamentals are improving and consensus estimates are rising. Current-year EPS estimates moved up 4.6% over 30 days and 4.8% over 90 days to $10.13; next-year to $10.58. The company beat estimates in three of the last four quarters (Q4 2025 by 20.1%, Q1 2026 by 8.5%). Credit quality is excellent — net charge-offs of 0.03% of average loans, NPAs 0.26% — and capital is solid (CET1 12.90%, TCE 9.46%, loan/deposit ratio of just 65%). The AOCI loss narrowed by $293M as rates fell, a tailwind to tangible book.

The reasons this is a HOLD rather than a BUY are specific and falsifiable. First, the Q1 2026 print confirmed the asset-sensitive balance sheet is now feeling Fed cuts: net interest income missed expectations even as EPS beat, and the stock fell ~5.3% on the report. NII remains the largest revenue line, and management's own base case assumes two more cuts in 2026. Second, the Kaiser stake removes any takeover premium, suppresses float and liquidity (avg daily volume ~289K shares), and concentrates governance — a permanent structural discount the market reasonably applies. Third, at ~13x trailing and ~12.4x forward EPS the stock is reasonably valued but not mispriced, and it sits below the $144 consensus mean target without a near-term catalyst to force a re-rate. A live margin headwind, a structural governance discount, and a full-but-not-cheap multiple keep this at a constructive HOLD.

## Company Overview

BOK Financial (incorporated in Oklahoma, founded 1990) operates through its national bank subsidiary BOKF, NA, under regional brands including Bank of Oklahoma, Bank of Texas, and Bank of Albuquerque. It reports three segments:

- **Commercial Banking** (~72% of segment pretax income, ~$584M of $813M): C&I lending, treasury/cash management, customer derivatives, and the TransFund EFT/ATM network (2,909 ATMs).
- **Consumer Banking** (~$76M pretax): retail deposits/lending and all mortgage origination/servicing ($22.5B serviced).
- **Wealth Management** (~$153M pretax): fiduciary/asset management ($126.6B AUM/AUA), private banking, brokerage, municipal underwriting, and agency-MBS trading.

The competitive structure is bifurcated. In Oklahoma, BOKF is the clear leader (14% state share, 33% Tulsa, 13% OKC), a genuine local moat with sticky low-cost deposits. Outside Oklahoma it is a small-share challenger (≈1% in Dallas-Fort Worth, Houston, San Antonio, Phoenix, Kansas City; 13% Albuquerque; 3% Denver) competing against money-center and larger regional banks. Its differentiators are deep energy-sector lending expertise (a "hallmark since founding"), the elevated fee-income mix, and the agency-MBS trading franchise within Wealth Management.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings and returns.** 2025 net income of $578.0M ($9.17 EPS) rose from $523.6M ($8.14) in 2024 and $530.7M ($8.02) in 2023. ROA was 1.12%, ROE 9.89%, ROTCE 12.15%. The efficiency ratio of 65.1% deteriorated modestly on personnel investment. Q4 2025 was strong at $177.3M net income ($2.89 EPS), helped by a $23.5M pre-tax gain on a merchant banking investment sale.

**Net interest income.** NII was $1.33B in 2025, up $116.6M YoY (core NIM ex-trading 3.14%). The improvement came from shifting funding away from expensive wholesale borrowings into interest-bearing deposits and from improving AFS securities yields. The balance sheet is structurally asset-sensitive — roughly 84% of commercial/CRE loans are variable or reprice within one year — which was a tailwind during high rates but is now a headwind as the Fed cuts. The 2025 NIM expansion was funding-mix driven; the Q1 2026 NII miss shows the asset-sensitivity is starting to bite.

**Fee income.** Total fees/commissions of $800.7M were roughly flat YoY and represent ~38% of net revenue. Fiduciary/asset management rose 11% to $257.2M on AUM/AUA growth of $12B. The headline "brokerage & trading −27% to $159.7M" overstates weakness — trading revenue migrated into net interest income on trading securities (trading-activity NII rose from $7.6M to $58.8M), so the underlying franchise is stable.

**Balance sheet and capital.** Loans grew 6% to $25.7B (+$1.5B, broad-based); deposits rose to $39.4B (+$1.2B). The 65% loan/deposit ratio leaves substantial on-balance-sheet liquidity. CET1 was 12.90%, total capital 14.77% (lifted by a $400M sub-debt issuance at 6.108% on 11/6/2025), and TCE 9.46%. The AOCI loss narrowed to −$166M from −$503M as AFS unrealized losses fell $293M with declining rates.

**Cash flow and credit.** Operating cash flow was $740M. Net charge-offs were 0.03% of average loans (down from 0.05%), combined ACL 1.28%, NPAs 0.26% ex-agency. Nonaccruals rose off a 2024 historic low (to $66M ex-agency from $42M) on a few healthcare and commercial credits — low in absolute terms but worth monitoring.

A note on screening artifacts: the reported Altman Z-Score of 0.24 and D/E of 7.83 are not meaningful for a bank, where deposits are counted as liabilities. The relevant signals — profitability, positive operating cash flow, strong regulatory capital, low loan/deposit ratio — are all sound.

## Growth Analysis

Growth visibility is moderate and improving. Management reaffirmed ~10% loan growth and mid-single-digit revenue growth for 2026, leaning on fee income (loan syndications, transaction cards) to offset margin pressure. Q1 2026 revenue was $556.4M, up 10.2% YoY.

Estimate revisions are positive across all horizons: current quarter +2.1% (30d) / +2.5% (90d), current year +4.6% / +4.8% to $10.13, next year +3.0% / +3.3% to $10.58. This is a steady upward drift rather than the >30% velocity that signals a trough recovery. The earnings track record supports confidence in forward estimates — three beats in four quarters, including a 20.1% Q4 2025 surprise.

The structural growth lever is the diversified revenue mix. With 38% of revenue from fees and a 65% loan/deposit ratio, BOKF can fund continued loan growth from existing deposits while fee income provides an NII offset that a typical regional lacks. AUM/AUA growth of $12B and 11% fiduciary fee growth give the Wealth segment a real compounding engine. The constraint is that the dominant earnings driver — NII — faces a known headwind from two assumed 2026 Fed cuts against an asset-sensitive book, which caps the upside on consensus revenue growth.

## Valuation Assessment

At $125.39 (recent ~$127), BOKF trades at ~13.7x trailing EPS of $9.17 and ~12.4x current-year consensus of $10.13. The dividend yield is ~2.0% on a conservative 25% payout, leaving ample room for buybacks (the company repurchased $390M of stock in 2025 at $105.72 average, with a fresh 5M-share authorization).

Relative to super-regional peers, BOKF's low-double-digit forward multiple is in line to slightly cheap for a bank generating ~10% ROE / ~12% ROTCE with excellent credit and a fee-heavy mix. The consensus price target distribution sits above spot: mean and median $144, low $130, high $165. Third-party fair-value estimates cluster around $144–$153.

The case for the stock being undervalued rests on the fee mix justifying a premium to NII-dependent peers and on rising estimates eventually pulling the multiple up. The case against a high valuation score is concrete: the ~13x multiple already reflects a quality bank, the next-year EPS estimate ($10.58) implies only ~4% growth over the current-year $10.13 consensus, and the Kaiser stake permanently removes the takeover-premium optionality that supports many regional-bank valuations. A controlled company with no realistic path to a control sale and thin float trades at a structural discount the market will not close. The result is a fair, modestly attractive valuation rather than an asymmetric one: ~13-15% upside to consensus mean against a reasonable bear case in the low-$100s if margin pressure and energy reserving both turn.

## Competitive Landscape

BOKF's moat is concentrated and real where it exists. The Oklahoma franchise generates sticky, low-cost deposits that fund expansion. Outside the home market, BOKF is a sub-scale challenger competing for share against far larger institutions, which limits pricing power in Texas, Colorado, and Arizona.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving. Loan growth of 6% with AUM/AUA up $12B suggests the bank is holding or gaining ground in its growth metros. The industry backdrop is hyper-competitive deposit and lending pricing with margin compression as the central risk, and here BOKF's fee mix is a structural advantage that insulates it more than a typical regional. The energy-lending expertise is a durable niche, though the book is shrinking by cycle/consolidation rather than expanding. Credit quality remains best-in-class (0.03% NCOs), evidence of disciplined underwriting through the cycle. Net assessment: a leader in one state and a competent challenger elsewhere, with fee diversification widening the effective moat at the franchise level.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Controlling shareholder (the defining equity risk).** George Kaiser owns ~63%. He can elect the entire board, control any vote, block any takeover (no control premium available), and could sell large blocks under Rule 144 or a registration statement. The float is small and liquidity low (~289K avg daily shares), amplifying volatility. This structurally caps the multiple and removes the M&A optionality that supports peer valuations.

2. **Net-interest-income / rate sensitivity (live risk).** NII is the largest revenue line; the asset-sensitive book (84% of commercial/CRE variable or repricing within a year) is now a headwind as the Fed cuts. The Q1 2026 NII miss confirmed this is no longer theoretical. Management assumes two more 2026 cuts; further cuts pressure NII directly.

3. **Energy concentration.** 11% of loans ($2.9B) are to energy borrowers, and the home economies (OK, TX, CO) are themselves energy-levered. The base-case CECL forecast assumes WTI ~$52.83/bbl; a downside scenario models $44.65 with recession, and a 100% downside weighting would add $189M to reserves. The book is shrinking by choice (consolidation/payoffs, not charge-offs), and the conservative first-lien reserve-based structure is the lowest-risk form of energy lending, but the macro backdrop keeps this a genuine tail.

4. **Geographic concentration.** Texas ~33%, Oklahoma ~15%, Colorado ~11% of loans by collateral location.

5. **MSR / agency-MBS sensitivity.** Large MSR ($22.5B serviced) and agency-MBS holdings swing with rates (a 50bp rate decline cuts MSR fair value ~$17.8M); hedging may not fully offset.

6. **Operational / vendor concentration.** The 10-K explicitly flags heavy reliance on a single core-systems vendor and prior outages with its largest service provider (immaterial to date).

7. **$50B asset-threshold crossing.** BOKF has exceeded $50B, triggering OCC "heightened standards" and higher compliance cost — though a proposed rule could lift the threshold to $700B and relieve this.

8. **Uninsured deposits.** $21.2B (54% of deposits) uninsured; $15.6B (40%) uninsured/uncollateralized — relevant in any renewed regional-bank confidence shock.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 33.9% versus 23.5% historical volatility — rich, with the term structure in backwardation (1-month 33.9% > 6-month 23.7%), signaling the market is pricing near-term event risk into the June 18 expiry (implied move ±5.6%). Put/call skew is balanced (0.2%), so positioning is two-sided rather than bearish-tilted. Short interest is moderate at 8.9% of float with 6.3 days to cover — not a crowded short, and combined with the thin float, the elevated near-term IV likely reflects rate-path uncertainty (the June FOMC sits in this window) more than a company-specific bearish bet. The benign skew is consistent with the fundamental view: a quality franchise with a live but not acute risk profile.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$165, +30%)
The fee-income mix proves durable enough to offset NII pressure, loan growth hits the guided ~10%, and credit stays pristine. Estimates continue their upward drift, EPS reaches the high end of the $10.58 next-year consensus and beyond, and the stock re-rates toward 15-16x on demonstrated through-cycle resilience. Buybacks at the current ~12x multiple accrete EPS. The AOCI tailwind continues if rates fall, lifting tangible book. This matches the high analyst target of $165.

### Bear Case (~$100-105, -16% to -20%)
Fed cuts compress NIM faster than fee growth and loan volume can offset; the Q1 2026 NII miss becomes a trend. An oil decline toward the $44.65 downside CECL scenario drives a reserve build (up to +$189M) and pressures the energy-levered home economies, lifting nonaccruals beyond the few healthcare/commercial credits already flagged. The multiple compresses to ~10-11x on a regional-bank de-rate, and the Kaiser overhang caps any recovery. This is roughly the low-$100s.

### Base Case (~$144, +13-15%)
Steady mid-single-digit revenue growth, ~10% loan growth, fee income offsetting most of the NII drag, and continued buybacks deliver EPS near consensus. The multiple holds around 13-14x as estimates drift higher, pulling the stock toward the $144 consensus mean over twelve months. Credit stays benign; energy reserving holds at base-case oil. This is the most-likely outcome and sits between the bull and bear scenarios.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to quarterly NII/NIM trajectory and the energy-credit picture, with the June and subsequent FOMC decisions as the key macro catalysts for the rate-sensitive earnings line.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 prints show NII stabilizing or NIM holding flat-to-up despite Fed cuts, confirming fee income and loan volume are offsetting margin pressure on a sustained basis.
- The stock pulls back toward $110-115 on no fundamental deterioration, widening the discount to the $144 consensus and improving risk/reward to clearly asymmetric.
- Continued upward estimate revisions of similar or accelerating magnitude (current-year EPS revised toward $10.50+) alongside maintained ~10% loan growth.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A sustained NII decline trend (two consecutive quarterly NII misses with NIM compressing) without fee-income offset.
- A material energy-driven reserve build (toward the $189M downside scenario) or a step-up in nonaccruals beyond the current 0.26% NPA level.
- A large Kaiser block sale or registration that overhangs the thin float.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, ~July) for the NII/NIM read, and the June FOMC for the rate path that drives the dominant earnings line.

## Conclusion

BOK Financial is a well-run, well-capitalized super-regional with a genuine Oklahoma deposit moat, a differentiated fee-income mix that structurally cushions margin compression, excellent credit, and rising consensus estimates after three beats in four quarters. Those attributes justify a constructive stance and underpin the favorable bias in the score.

The reasons it falls short of a BUY are specific and falsifiable. The Q1 2026 NII miss confirmed the asset-sensitive book is a live headwind into a Fed-cutting cycle, with the dominant earnings line under pressure precisely as management assumes two more cuts. The Kaiser stake permanently removes takeover optionality and suppresses float and liquidity, applying a structural discount the market is correct to maintain. And at ~12-13x forward on ~4% projected EPS growth, the valuation is fair-to-modestly-cheap but not asymmetric — roughly 13-15% to the consensus mean against a credible low-$100s bear case if margin pressure and energy reserving both turn. The result is a growth-oriented HOLD: own it for the steady compounding and capital return, accumulate on a pullback toward $110-115 or on confirmed NIM stabilization, and reassess at the Q2 print.
