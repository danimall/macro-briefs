## Executive Summary

Itaú Unibanco is the highest-return large bank in Latin America and, on the operating evidence available, is executing well. Q2 2026 recurring net income of R$12.4bn was up 7.8% year over year, consolidated ROE was 24.3% (25.7% in Brazil), CET1 rose 30bp to 12.3%, the cost of credit held at 2.7% of the portfolio — flat since Q1 2025 — and the Brazil efficiency ratio was 35.5% with commercial and administrative expenses *down* 0.5% year over year in an economy running a 14.00% policy rate. Loans grew 9.9% year over year to R$1.522 trillion. Management has been explicit that it will forgo volume rather than lend below its cost of capital, and the numbers corroborate the claim: the mortgage book (R$152bn, +13.3% YoY, a stated 55% share of private-sector bank origination) now exceeds the credit card book, private payroll lending grew 90.1% year over year, and risk-adjusted NIM rose 10bp to 6.2%. That mix shift into secured and payroll-deducted credit is what has kept credit costs flat through six quarters of restrictive rates.

Three things stop this from being a purchase at $7.94. First, the valuation is coherent rather than cheap in local-currency terms: at 2.08x book on a 21.5% accounting ROE (24.3% recurring), a Gordon-growth solve at 8% nominal BRL growth implies a cost of equity of 14.5-15.8% against a 14.00% Selic — an equity risk premium of roughly one to two points over the local risk-free rate. The upside leg therefore depends on Brazilian rates falling materially rather than on any re-rating at unchanged rates. Second, forward earnings lean on a 12.34% trailing effective tax rate. Management cut full-year commissions, fees and insurance growth guidance to +2-5% from +5-9% and stated the bottom line is effectively unchanged *if the effective tax rate lands at the lower end of its range*. A move to a 25% rate would cut EPS from roughly $0.81 to $0.70 and take the multiple from 9.8x to 11.3x, which removes the apparent discount. Third, the October 2026 Brazilian general election sits inside a 12-month horizon at a moment when the central bank has flagged fiscal stimulus as a new inflation risk.

A probability-weighted total return of roughly 12-14% (base case $8.75, bull $10.00, bear $6.25, plus a ~6.7% trailing distribution) is approximately the return the implied cost of equity says an owner should demand. That is the definition of fair value. Existing holders are being paid a well-covered distribution to wait through the election; new capital has no urgent entry signal. **HOLD.**

A material qualification on confidence: as a foreign private issuer, Itaú files Form 20-F and Form 6-K, and no primary filing text was accessible for this analysis — SEC archive documents returned HTTP 403 and the XBRL company-facts dataset for CIK 1132597 is effectively empty. Every financial figure below is either EDGAR filing metadata, third-party aggregated data, or secondary reporting of management's own Q2 2026 statements. The 20-F filing record itself is clean (ten consecutive punctual April filings through April 29, 2026), and the key figures cross-check internally, but nothing here is traced to audited text. That justifies a confidence discount, not a solvency concern.

## Company Overview

Itaú Unibanco Holding S.A. is a São Paulo-based universal bank formed by the 2008 merger of Banco Itaú and Unibanco, and is the largest private-sector bank in Brazil and the largest bank in Latin America by assets. It is dual-listed on B3 (ITUB3 voting, ITUB4 preferred) and on the NYSE via the ADR analyzed here. Employment is 92,470, of which 82,693 (89.4%) sit in Brazil; named subsidiaries include Itaú Chile, Itaú Paraguay, Itaú Uruguay, the merchant acquirer Rede, Hiper and Credicard.

Three reporting segments carry the business. On third-party segment revenue (gross of eliminations, so useful only for mix), Retail Banking has moved from 60.1% of the segment sum in FY2021 to 61.9% on a trailing-twelve-month basis, Wholesale from 30.4% to 33.1%, and Activities with the Market + Corporation has fallen from 9.5% to 5.0%. On the most recent comparison — TTM versus FY2025 — Retail grew 4.3% while Wholesale fell 0.2% and Market + Corporation fell 2.1%. Growth is now a retail story.

A structural fact for the ADR holder: shares outstanding of 11.02bn against a float of 5.78bn (52.4%) leaves roughly 5.24bn shares non-floating, consistent with a controlling block in the ordinary/voting class held through the corporate parent Itaúsa, with the NYSE ADR representing the preferred class. Under Brazilian corporate law preferred shares typically carry limited or no voting rights. That reading is directionally supported by the two-class B3 listing and the non-float percentage, but the ADR-to-share-class mapping and the exact voting percentages could not be verified against a filing. Treat it as probable, not established.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings and margin trajectory (BRL, third-party aggregated).** Net income has compounded from R$29,207mn (FY2022) to R$44,857mn (FY2025), a 15.4% three-year CAGR, with FY2024 up 24.1% and FY2025 up 9.2%. Trailing-twelve-month net income to June 2026 is R$46,828mn. Profit margin expanded 21.7% (FY2023) → 24.9% (FY2024) → 27.1% (FY2025) → 27.4% (TTM); pretax margin 25.5% → 31.2%. EPS of R$4.07 (FY2025) and R$4.25 (TTM) both reconcile to the 11.02bn share count, which validates the series internally.

One data caution that changes how growth must be measured: the same aggregator reports two irreconcilable FY2025 revenue figures — R$169,197mn (+0.06%) in the financials table and R$138.95bn (+2.36%) on the overview page — while net income agrees at R$44.86bn across both. Bank top line can be defined as gross interest income, net interest income, or NII plus fees, and the definitions are evidently being mixed. "Revenue" is not a usable metric for this issuer from third-party sources, and the "FY2025 revenue was flat" reading should be discarded. The analysis therefore anchors on net income, EPS, and the disclosed NII/loan/expense components.

**The Q2 2026 decomposition.** Client NII was R$32.6bn, up 3.3% sequentially and 5% year over year, with core client NII up R$0.8bn (+2.9%) sequentially and product mix broadly neutral to margin. Risk-adjusted NIM was 6.2% consolidated (+10bp QoQ) and 6.7% in Brazil. Market NII was only R$0.9bn, depressed by market volatility and the cost of the bank's capital-index hedge. Credit costs were R$10.1bn (2.7% of the portfolio). Efficiency was 35.5% in Brazil and 37.4% consolidated, with Brazil non-interest expenses up 3.1% year over year.

**Capital and leverage arithmetic.** CET1 finished June at 12.3%, with earnings retention contributing 0.8 percentage points in the quarter against dividend/interest-on-capital provisions and RWA growth. Gross capital generation therefore runs roughly 3.2pp of CET1 annually against approximately 1.2pp of net accretion — about 2.0pp per year absorbed by distributions and by RWA growth off a loan book compounding near 10%. Management consistently reports ROE "adjusted to an 11.5% CET1 ratio," which implies 11.5% is the internal reference and that roughly 80bp of surplus capital exists today. Converting that to currency requires an RWA input that was not disclosed; on an assumed R$1.3-1.6tn RWA range it is roughly R$10-13bn. That is an order-of-magnitude estimate built on an assumption, not a disclosure.

Three independently sourced balance-sheet figures cross-check: equity of $44.01bn against approximately $559.3bn of assets gives 7.9% equity/assets, or 12.7x leverage; ROA of 1.58% × 12.7 = 20.1%, close to the reported 21.48% ROE. The internal consistency raises confidence in all three despite weak provenance on the asset figure.

**Cash flow — a metric to discard, not interpret.** Reported operating cash flow of R$54,306 / 129,633 / 77,493 / 7,069 / 34,466mn across FY2021-FY2025 produces "FCF margins" ranging from 4.18% to 90.71%. For a bank, operating cash flow is dominated by period changes in trading assets, deposits, interbank balances and loan origination — the business itself, not working-capital noise around it. Derived metrics such as "Debt/FCF 25.63" or "P/FCF 10.79" carry no information here and are not used. The same applies to the Piotroski F-Score of 3: current ratio, change in leverage and asset turnover all penalize an institution expanding its loan book 9.9%, which is precisely what Itaú is doing. Altman Z returned n/a, which is correct — it was never designed for financial institutions.

**Distributions — the mechanics matter.** Itaú pays a token monthly dividend of roughly $0.0027-$0.0030 per ADR plus large, lumpy complementary dividend and interest-on-capital payments. Summing all sixteen payments with ex-dates between August 20, 2025 and August 4, 2026 gives $0.5331 per ADR, reconciling to the reported $0.53 annual figure — a 6.68% trailing yield at $7.94. One widely distributed data feed reports the ITUB dividend yield as 0.40%, which captures only the monthly token payment and is wrong by more than an order of magnitude.

The large complementary payment escalated from $0.17484 (ex-Feb 2024) to $0.25531 (ex-Feb 2025) to $0.34510 (ex-Dec 2025), with no payment of comparable size before February 2024 (2023's largest was $0.03571). The timing also shifted from February to December, so calendar-2026 has not yet seen a large payment and the trailing window captures exactly one. Payout is roughly 65-73% depending on whether measured on USD or BRL EPS. Two qualifications: a supplemental distribution is discretionary by construction, and a material portion of Brazilian bank distributions is paid as *juros sobre capital próprio*, subject to Brazilian withholding for non-residents — the gross figures above are not what an ADR holder nets, and the applicable rate could not be verified.

**Financial health: 7.0.** Capital above the internal reference, six quarters of flat credit costs through a restrictive-rate period, best-in-class efficiency, and 12.7x leverage that is unremarkable for a universal bank. The score is capped below 7.5 by the complete absence of audited verification, by a 12.34% effective tax rate that functions as an earnings support rather than a durable structural feature, and by market NII volatility.

## Growth Analysis

Volume growth is solid and broad. Total loans of R$1.522tn grew 2.7% sequentially and 9.9% year over year (Brazil +9.6%), with large companies +10%, MSMEs +11.6%, payroll lending +11.7% (private payroll +90.1% off an unsized base), and mortgages +13.3%. Management expects loan growth to remain above the guidance midpoint while moderating.

Earnings growth is slower and increasingly composed of items other than volume. H1 2026 recurring net income of R$24.7bn (R$12.3bn + R$12.4bn) is about 8.9% above the implied H1 2025 figure derived from the disclosed +10% and +7.8% year-over-year growth rates. Holding that rate gives roughly R$48.9bn for FY2026; a naïve 2x annualization gives R$49.4bn, or +10.1%. Both land in an R$48-49bn range, and that range should be treated as a range: Brazilian banks typically carry heavier Q4 expenses and year-end provisioning, the Colombia retail disposal removes approximately R$10bn of loans from the perimeter beginning Q3 2026, and the fee guidance cut is not yet in the run rate.

Two negatives deserve weight. The fee guidance cut, on slower activity and moderating card revenues, is a downgrade in the highest-quality, least capital-intensive revenue line, and it was offset in management's own framing by the tax rate rather than by another operating line. Second, three-year net income growth of 15.4% against slower growth on any revenue definition means the earnings compounding has been margin- and credit-cost-driven. That is a legitimate source of value creation, but it is finite: the cost of credit cannot fall from 2.7% indefinitely, and the efficiency ratio at 35.5% has less room than it did at 45%.

USD estimate revisions are close to flat: current-year consensus of $0.89 is down 0.5% over 30 days but up 1.1% over 90; next year of $1.00 is up 0.5% and 1.2%. On only two to three contributing estimates, the signal is thin either way. There is no estimate-revision inflection here, positive or negative — the "broadly falling" characterization overstates a marginal near-quarter trim against slightly higher annual figures.

The earnings-surprise series requires care. The reported table shows four consecutive negative prints (-4.5%, -17.1%, -8.3%, -5.7%) on per-share figures of $0.95-$1.11, which are BRL-scaled (R$1.11 × 11.02bn ≈ R$12.2bn, near the R$12.4bn recurring figure). The Q1 2026 actual of R$0.95 implies roughly R$10.5bn against management's R$12.3bn managerial figure, so the series appears to compare accounting results against recurring/managerial estimates. That is a basis mismatch and no beat/miss conclusion can be drawn from it. What can be said is that on a consistently aggregated series, delivered results have come in below consensus four quarters running, which argues against assuming upside to the forward numbers.

**Growth prospects: 5.3.** Roughly 9-10% nominal BRL earnings growth against Brazilian inflation, a confirmed fee-guidance cut, a perimeter reduction from the Colombia disposal, and growth composition shifting toward margin and tax rather than volume.

*A reconciliation note: one feed reports EPS of $0.73195 alongside a trailing P/E of 9.775, which is arithmetically inconsistent at any of the captured prices ($7.94/$0.73195 = 10.85x). The 9.775x figure reconciles to the $0.81 aggregated TTM EPS used above. The absolute multiple therefore sits somewhere in a 9.8x-10.9x band on trailing earnings; the conclusion is unchanged.*

The absolute multiples look inexpensive, and the internal valuation stack is coherent: earnings yield 9.97% × P/B 2.06 = 20.5%, reconciling to reported ROE within rounding. Solving the implied cost of equity is the more informative exercise. Gordon growth with P/B = (ROE − g)/(CoE − g), at 8% nominal BRL growth, gives CoE = 8% + (21.5 − 8)/2.08 = **14.5%** on accounting ROE, or 8% + (24.3 − 8)/2.08 = **15.8%** on recurring ROE. At 6% growth the results are 13.5% and 14.9%. Against a 14.00% Selic, the market is embedding an equity risk premium of roughly one to two points over the local risk-free rate. The shares are not obviously mispriced in BRL; they are priced for a high ROE to persist.

That framing changes what the bull case requires. Multiple expansion at unchanged Brazilian rates is hard to argue when the implied premium is already thin. The upside leg is a lower discount rate — the central bank has cut 25bp at four consecutive meetings to 14.00% and has left September in play. Each 100bp of easing that is credibly permanent both lowers the local discount rate and supports loan growth and credit quality. That is an identifiable catalyst, and it is the main reason cheapness here should not simply be dismissed as a chronic EM-bank condition.

Two published inputs for this ADR are actively misleading and should not be used. A five-year beta measured against a US index reflects low correlation to US equities, not low risk — the 52-week ADR range of $6.58-$9.60 puts the high 46% above the low. Feeding a very low beta into CAPM produces the sub-2% WACC one vendor reports, which is not credible for a Brazilian bank. Separately, 11.02bn shares × $7.94 = $87.5bn, not the $90.68bn market cap reported alongside a $7.88 price, indicating blended snapshots.

The tax-rate sensitivity is the single largest quantified threat to the multiple. On TTM pretax income of $10.56bn with roughly $0.22bn of non-controlling interests, moving the effective rate from the actual 12.34% to 25% / 30% / 35% reduces net income to parent by 14.8% / 20.7% / 26.5%, taking EPS to $0.70 / $0.65 / $0.60 and the multiple at $7.94 to 11.3x / 12.2x / 13.2x. The forward P/E near 8-9x implicitly assumes the low rate persists. Interest-on-capital deductibility is the standard, legal, disclosed Brazilian mechanism and a variable tax rate in bank guidance is transparency rather than obfuscation, but it ties the earnings estimate to a tax-policy variable that could not be verified, including the legislative status of proposals to limit JCP deductibility.

**Valuation: 6.0.** Genuinely inexpensive on absolute multiples with a real catalyst in the easing cycle, offset by an implied cost of equity that leaves little margin over the local risk-free rate and by a forward multiple contingent on a 12% tax rate.

## Competitive Landscape

Itaú's strongest documented position is in mortgages: a R$152bn book, R$36bn of trailing-twelve-month origination, and a stated 55% share among Brazilian private-sector banks. That share excludes Caixa Econômica Federal and Banco do Brasil, which dominate Brazilian housing credit overall, so it describes leadership within the private cohort rather than the market. A 35.5% Brazil efficiency ratio with commercial and administrative expenses falling 0.5% year over year in a double-digit nominal-rate economy is the clearest evidence of durable scale advantage in the numbers. Itaú carries a premium to Brazilian bank peers on book value; the 24.3% recurring ROE against peers running mid-to-high teens is the justification, and the premium is proportionate rather than excessive. No peer benchmark set was supplied, so this comparison is qualitative.

Two trajectory signals cut the other way. First, the credit card book is now smaller than the R$152bn mortgage book, and management cited moderating card revenues when cutting fee guidance while stating a preference for private payroll products over traditional unsecured personal lending on "pricing and repayment-priority advantages." The favorable reading is a deliberate trade of gross yield for risk-adjusted yield, which reconciles the flat 2.7% cost of credit, the rising 6.2% risk-adjusted NIM and the fee cut into one coherent strategy. The less favorable reading is that the retreat from cards is defensive — a response to Brazilian regulation of revolving card rates and to fintech competition in payments. The available evidence does not distinguish these.

Second, large-company loans grew 10% year over year while third-party Wholesale segment revenue was down 0.2% TTM versus FY2025, consistent with corporate spread compression as Brazilian large corporates fund through local capital markets against competition from BTG Pactual, Santander Brasil and Banco do Brasil. Segment-definition differences could also explain it, and the 20-F segment note would be needed to resolve.

A gap worth naming: no direct evidence was obtainable on Itaú's deposit or payments share, on the competitive effect of Nubank and Mercado Pago, or on the impact of Pix and Open Finance on deposit costs and interchange. The absence of a documented fintech-disruption assessment is a gap in the evidence, not a conclusion that the threat is small. The fee-guidance cut is consistent with payments pressure without establishing it.

**Competitive position: 6.0.** Dominant in secured retail lending with best-in-class cost efficiency; eroding at the margin in cards, payments-linked fees and corporate spread.

## Risk Assessment

**Brazil concentration and FX.** With 89.4% of employees in Brazil and Brazil ROE (25.7%) above consolidated (24.3%), this is functionally a levered claim on Brazil. The ADR's 52-week return of roughly +15% (from a $6.86 close on August 11, 2025 to the $7.94 official close) exceeded BRL EPS growth of about 10% while the trailing multiple barely moved, implying a meaningful share of the dollar return came from BRL appreciation rather than operating performance. The mechanism is symmetric. Marginal ITUB demand is substantially index- and ETF-driven (ILF, EWZ), so EM allocation shifts move the ADR independent of fundamentals.

**The October 2026 election.** A national election inside this quarter's window puts pre-election fiscal stimulus and bank-directed policy — rate caps, mandated lending, sector taxation — into the live discussion. Reuters reporting confirms the central bank has flagged fiscal stimulus as a new inflation risk and described a worsening inflation outlook even while cutting to 14.00%, with the finance ministry publicly arguing there is "still room" for cuts. Monetary and fiscal policy pulling against each other is the configuration in which the easing leg of the bull case stalls. No verifiable evidence was obtainable either way on pending bank-specific tax legislation, and the election calendar itself could not be sourced in any document reviewed; treat it as unverified background context.

**Tax structure.** Quantified above: a normalization to a 25% effective rate removes roughly 15% of EPS. This is the largest single sensitivity identified and it is not visible in the headline multiple.

**Credit normalization.** Management expects the SME early-delinquency indicator to rise about 10bp to roughly 2.1% next quarter and then stabilize, attributing it to expiring grace periods on government-backed programs and characterizing it as mechanical. The government-guaranteed MSME portion grew 7.2% sequentially with originations up 47.3%. Individual delinquency was described as stable near 3.0%, though whether on the same 15-90 day basis is ambiguous in the source — it should not be read as a 90+ day NPL ratio. The CEO cautioned that household indebtedness remains elevated, rates are restrictive, and market-wide credit has expanded. The Desenrola renegotiation program is quantified and immaterial: R$60mn added to quarterly credit costs against R$10.1bn total.

**Governance.** No proxy statement exists for a foreign private issuer, so no compensation-alignment, pay-versus-performance or related-party assessment is possible. The probable non-voting status of the ADR class means economic participation without governance influence over board composition, related-party transactions or capital allocation. Reported insider sales surfaced through third-party aggregators (names appearing garbled or reversed) carry essentially no evidentiary weight, since ITUB officers are generally exempt from Section 16 reporting and none could be verified against EDGAR — these should not be read as an insider signal in either direction.

**Disclosure gaps.** The Colombia retail disposal — Banco Itaú Colombia sold its retail banking business to Banco de Bogotá around July 31, 2026, retaining Colombian corporate banking — had no disclosed consideration, gain or loss, or goodwill effect, which leaves open the possibility of a non-recurring Q3 2026 item. ITUB's current ownership percentage of the separately listed Itaú Chile could not be verified, which determines how much of the disposal economics accrues to ITUB holders versus Itaú Chile minorities.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 31.8% sits essentially on top of 31.3% one-year realized (spread 0.5%), at the 15th percentile within the current chain, with a balanced 2.8% put/call skew and a ±4.4% implied move on the nine-day tenor. The options market is pricing no near-term event. Open-interest put/call of 1.74 against a volume ratio of 0.13 suggests standing protection rather than fresh directional buying. The December tenor quotes at 75% IV (±41.2% implied move), which across a 32-contract chain is more plausibly a stale or illiquid quote than a genuine market view; if genuine, it would price a large event before mid-December, which the October election would satisfy. Low confidence either way. Short interest of 0.5% of float and 1.2 days to cover means positioning is not a factor in either direction — there is neither a crowded bearish view to fade nor a squeeze dynamic to exploit.

**Risk factors: 4.8.** Two specific, dated, quantifiable binaries — the tax rate and the election — sit inside the horizon and are not visibly priced in either the multiple or the options chain, alongside a governance structure and disclosure regime that cannot be independently verified.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case — $10.00 (+25.9% price, +32.6% total return).** Brazil's central bank continues cutting, taking the Selic toward 11.5-12.0% by mid-2027 with inflation expectations anchored. The local discount rate falls, the BRL holds or firms, loan growth stays above the guidance midpoint, and the cost of credit begins declining from 2.7% as affordability improves. The mix shift into mortgages and payroll lending proves to be a structural improvement in loss content rather than a defensive retreat, sustaining risk-adjusted NIM above 6.2%. The effective tax rate remains at the low end of management's range, and CET1 surplus funds another escalation in the December complementary distribution. Ten times FY2027 consensus EPS of $1.00 gives $10.00, matching the high analyst target.

**Bear case — $6.25 (-21.3% price, -14.6% total return).** Pre-election fiscal stimulus reignites inflation, the easing cycle stalls or reverses, and the BRL depreciates 10-15%. Fee income undershoots even the reduced +2-5% guidance as card revenues keep moderating and payments competition intensifies. The effective tax rate normalizes toward 25%, taking USD EPS to roughly $0.70. SME delinquency does not stabilize at 2.1% as management expects, and the cost of credit moves through 3.0%. A post-election policy turn toward bank taxation or directed lending compresses the multiple toward 1.5x book. Seven times a $0.90 EPS, or 1.6x book, gives roughly $6.25 — near the low analyst target of $6.10.

**Base case — $8.75 (+10.2% price, +16.9% total return).** Easing continues but slowly, with the Selic near 12.5-13.0% a year out. Earnings deliver the R$48-49bn range for FY2026, roughly 8-9% growth, with the fee shortfall offset by the tax line as management indicated. USD EPS reaches approximately $0.95-1.00 by FY2027 on modest BRL stability. The multiple holds near 8.75x forward and 2.1x book, and the December complementary distribution repeats at or slightly above the $0.345 level. That is +10% in price and roughly +17% with the distribution.

Weighting 30% bull / 45% base / 25% bear gives an expected total return of approximately +13.6%; shifting the bear weight to 30% and bull to 25% gives +11.3%. Against an implied cost of equity of 14.5-15.8%, an expected return in the 11-14% range is at or slightly below the return the market's own pricing says an owner should require. That is a fair-value conclusion, not an opportunity.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income hold). Horizon: 12 months, with the natural reassessment points at the September Copom decision, the October general election, the Q3 2026 results package in early November — which should disclose the Colombia perimeter effect and any disposal gain — and the December complementary dividend declaration.**

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price into **$6.75-7.00** without fundamental deterioration. That is P/B near 1.75-1.85x on a 24% recurring ROE and a trailing distribution yield of 7.6-7.9%, which restores a margin of safety adequate for a name with this confidence profile.
2. **Selic below 12.5% with 12-month inflation expectations anchored** and no post-election fiscal impulse. A credible 150bp of further easing directly lowers the discount rate that currently limits the upside case.
3. **Confirmation from the FY2026 20-F tax note that the sub-15% effective rate is structural**, together with resolution of pending JCP legislation, plus fee and insurance growth returning to +5% or better. That removes the tax dependency that currently caps the valuation score.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. **Cost of credit above 3.0% of the portfolio**, or SME early delinquency exceeding 2.3% without stabilizing — either would break the six-quarter flat-credit-cost record that underpins the earnings quality argument.
2. **Effective tax rate above 20% for two consecutive quarters**, or legislation curtailing JCP deductibility. On the sensitivity above this alone re-rates the stock from 8.9x to 11x+ forward on unchanged operations.
3. **CET1 below 11.5%**, a reduction or omission of the December complementary distribution, or loan growth falling below the guidance range — the first two would remove the income rationale for holding.
4. A **post-election policy program including bank sector taxation, rate caps or mandated lending**, or a resumption of Selic hikes.

**Position guidance for existing holders:** maintain. The 6.7% trailing distribution is supported by capital generation of roughly 3.2pp of CET1 annually against a 12.3% ratio versus an 11.5% internal reference, and the base case pays a mid-teens total return for waiting through the election. Do not model the complementary payment as an annuity — it is discretionary and would be the first line to flex if RWA growth accelerated or capital rules tightened.

## Conclusion

Itaú is operating at the high end of what a large EM bank can deliver: 24.3% recurring ROE, 12.3% CET1 with genuine surplus, a cost of credit flat for six quarters through a 14-15% policy-rate regime, a 35.5% Brazil efficiency ratio achieved with nominal cost declines, and 9.9% loan growth concentrated in secured and payroll-deducted products. The distribution mechanics are well documented and the escalation from $0.175 to $0.345 per ADR over three years is funded by capital surplus rather than payout stretch.

The problem is price, and the constraint is verifiability. At 2.08x book on 21.5-24.3% ROE, the implied cost of equity of 14.5-15.8% against a 14.00% Selic leaves a one-to-two-point risk premium — the market already capitalizes the high ROE, so the upside depends on Brazilian rates falling rather than on any re-rating at current rates.
