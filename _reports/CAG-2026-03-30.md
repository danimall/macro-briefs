## Executive Summary

Conagra Brands is a packaged food company in structural decline, still absorbing the consequences of its $10.9B Pinnacle Foods acquisition in 2019. Goodwill of $9.73B exceeds the company's $7.5B market cap — the market prices these acquired assets at a clear discount to book. The Refrigerated & Frozen segment, which represents ~40% of revenue, has zero excess fair value over its carrying amount after a $771.3M goodwill impairment in Q2 FY2026, meaning any further operational deterioration triggers additional write-downs against $4.15B of remaining segment goodwill.

Revenue has declined for two consecutive fiscal years and fell another 6.8% YoY in Q2 FY2026. Every operating segment is deteriorating: Refrigerated & Frozen operating profit down 32% in H1 FY2026, Grocery & Snacks down 18%, Foodservice down 17%. H1 FY2026 free cash flow of approximately $112M annualizes to ~$225M, which does not cover the $669M annual dividend obligation. The 8.9% yield reflects market skepticism about sustainability.

In the current stagflationary macro environment — oil at $116, consumer sentiment down 12.5% YoY, 30.5% of auto trade-ins underwater — Conagra faces simultaneous headwinds: input cost inflation (commodities, freight, tariffs), consumer trade-down to private label, and GLP-1 medication adoption reducing demand for calorie-dense frozen meals. The stock has fallen 41% over the past year and we see no catalyst for reversal.

## Company Overview

Conagra Brands operates four segments: Grocery & Snacks (~42% of FY2025 revenue), Refrigerated & Frozen (~40%), International (~8%), and Foodservice (~9%). Key brands include Slim Jim, Birds Eye, Marie Callender's, Healthy Choice, Banquet, Duncan Hines, and Reddi-wip. The company also holds a 44% stake in Ardent Mills, a flour milling joint venture that contributed $182M in earnings in FY2025.

Walmart accounts for approximately 29% of consolidated net sales — a concentration risk that gives the retailer significant negotiating power over pricing, shelf placement, and promotional terms.

The company is headquartered in Chicago with approximately 18,300 employees and 38 domestic manufacturing facilities.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue: Two-Year Decline Accelerating

| Fiscal Year | Revenue ($B) | YoY Growth |
|-------------|-------------|-----------|
| FY2022 | 11.54 | +3.2% |
| FY2023 | 12.28 | +6.4% |
| FY2024 | 12.05 | -1.8% |
| FY2025 | 11.61 | -3.6% |
| H1 FY2026 | 5.79 (annualized ~$11.6B) | -6.3% |

Revenue peaked in FY2023 and has declined every quarter since. The most recent quarter (Q2 FY2026, ending November 2025) showed revenue of $2.98B, down 6.8% YoY. Organic volume declines are broad-based. The Chef Boyardee divestiture ($607M in proceeds) removes roughly $400-500M in annual revenue, making the underlying trajectory even weaker than the headline figures suggest once adjusted.

### Profitability: Severely Impaired

FY2025 reported EPS of $2.40 was inflated by $253.5M in tax benefits from an IRS settlement and release of deferred tax asset valuation allowances. Normalized EPS was approximately $1.87. The trailing P/E of -84.9x (negative) reflects the Q2 FY2026 net loss of $663.6M driven by $968.3M in combined goodwill and intangible impairments.

Gross margins have compressed from 28.4% in FY2021 to 25.9% in FY2025 and are under further pressure in FY2026 from input cost inflation and tariffs. Segment operating profit declined 22% YoY in H1 FY2026, with every segment contributing to the decline.

### Balance Sheet: Goodwill Exceeds Market Cap

| Item | Nov 2025 |
|------|----------|
| Goodwill | $9.73B |
| Intangible Assets (net) | $2.20B |
| Total Debt | $7.24B |
| Cash | $47M |
| Total Equity | $8.09B |
| Market Cap | ~$7.5B |

Goodwill of $9.73B against a market cap of $7.5B is the central balance sheet concern. The market prices the economic value of Conagra's acquired businesses substantially below what the company paid. Since FY2023, cumulative goodwill and intangible impairments have exceeded $2.5B. The Refrigerated & Frozen segment, carrying $4.15B in goodwill, is at zero cushion — any deterioration in volumes, margins, or discount rates triggers additional write-downs.

Total debt of $7.24B is approximately 4.8x the segment operating profit run-rate. Interest expense consumed $416.7M in FY2025. The company has been deleveraging (down from $8.8B in FY2022) partly through divestiture proceeds, but the debt burden remains heavy.

### Cash Flow: Dividend Coverage Eroding

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 FY2026 |
|--------|--------|--------|-----------|
| Operating Cash Flow | $2,016M | $1,692M | $331M |
| Capex | $388M | $389M | $219M |
| Free Cash Flow | $1,628M | $1,303M | $112M |
| Dividends Paid | $659M | $669M | $335M |

H1 FY2026 FCF of $112M covers only one-third of the ~$335M H1 dividend obligation. Management typically guides to back-half weighted cash flow due to seasonal patterns, but the magnitude of the shortfall is concerning. FY2024's elevated FCF was partly driven by working capital improvements that are not repeatable. At $669M annually, the dividend requires approximately $850-900M in FCF to maintain a reasonable payout ratio — and the trajectory is negative.

The 8.9% current dividend yield prices in substantial probability of a cut or suspension.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are poor across every measurable dimension:

**Volume:** Organic volumes are declining in all four segments. Consumer trade-down to private label, inflation fatigue, and competitive pressure from retailers' store brands are structural headwinds with no visible reversal catalyst.

**Pricing:** The company has limited remaining pricing power. After aggressive price increases in FY2022-2023, consumers have responded with volume reductions. Further price increases risk accelerating volume losses.

**Innovation:** Management is investing in snack-oriented acquisitions (FATTY meat sticks, $179M) and divesting legacy brands (Chef Boyardee, frozen fish). This portfolio reshaping is directionally sensible but small relative to the overall revenue base and unlikely to offset the core business decline within a 12-month horizon.

**GLP-1 risk:** The 10-K explicitly cites weight loss medication adoption as a risk factor. Conagra's frozen meal portfolio (Healthy Choice, Marie Callender's, Banquet) is directly exposed to reduced caloric consumption among GLP-1 users. A slow-moving but potentially material secular headwind.

**Guidance disconnect:** Management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of flat to +1% organic net sales and 11.0-11.5% adjusted operating margin at the February 2026 CAGNY presentation. H1 FY2026 showed a 6.3% revenue decline and meaningful margin compression. Achieving flat to +1% organic growth in H2 requires a sharp reversal that is difficult to justify given current consumer and macro trends.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing P/E is negative due to impairment charges. On a forward basis, the profile shows a forward P/E of -84.9x, reflecting analyst estimates that incorporate continued losses or extremely depressed earnings.

Against peer medians (P/E 23.0x, yield 4.5%), Conagra's 8.9% yield is roughly double the peer median — a spread consistent with distress pricing. Campbell's (CPB) trades at 15.2x with a 7.4% yield in a similar situation, suggesting CAG's valuation is in the same distressed cohort rather than unfairly discounted.

Using normalized FY2025 EPS of ~$1.87, the stock trades at 8.3x — optically cheap, but appropriate given declining revenue, eroding margins, $7.24B in debt, and ongoing impairment risk. A 10-12x multiple on deteriorating earnings does not constitute a margin of safety.

Analyst consensus target is $18.40 (median $18.00), with a range of $14.00 to $23.00. Of 17 analysts, 11 are Hold, 3 Strong Buy, 2 Sell, and 1 Strong Sell. The consensus has been moving downward (JPMorgan cut to $17 in March 2026).

The current price of $15.62 sits close to the analyst low target of $14.00, suggesting limited downside cushion in the consensus view. The consensus may be stale relative to the March 2026 macro deterioration (oil $116, consumer contraction accelerating).

## Competitive Landscape

Conagra competes against General Mills, Campbell's, Kraft Heinz, Hormel, and Tyson in various categories. Its competitive position has weakened:

- **Brand erosion:** Birds Eye has suffered >$750M in cumulative impairments since FY2023, identified as a Critical Audit Matter by KPMG. The frozen vegetable category faces structural pressure from private label alternatives.
- **Scale disadvantage:** At ~$11.6B in revenue, Conagra is smaller than General Mills (~$19B), Kraft Heinz (~$25B), and faces scale disadvantages in procurement, marketing, and distribution.
- **Customer concentration:** 29% of sales through Walmart gives the retailer outsized leverage over pricing and terms.
- **Licensed brands:** Reliance on licensed trademarks (P.F. Chang's, Wendy's, Dolly Parton) creates margin-sharing and renewal risk.
- **Portfolio quality:** The repeated need for impairments signals that the brand portfolio assembled through the Pinnacle acquisition lacks the pricing power and consumer loyalty that management assumed at acquisition.

The 44% Ardent Mills stake ($182M in FY2025 equity earnings) is a meaningful contributor and provides some diversification, but it cannot offset the core branded foods deterioration.

## Risk Assessment

### Critical Risks

1. **Goodwill impairment cascade:** $4.15B remaining in Refrigerated & Frozen at zero cushion. Any increase in discount rates (currently driven higher by oil-driven inflation and war risk), further volume declines, or margin compression triggers additional write-downs. In the current stagflationary environment with the Fed potentially hiking rates (35-40% probability), discount rates are likely to increase.

2. **Dividend cut:** H1 FY2026 FCF of $112M vs. $335M in dividends paid. Coverage is inadequate. Management has not signaled a cut, but the math is deteriorating. A dividend cut would remove the income thesis, likely triggering another 15-25% selloff as yield-seeking holders exit.

3. **Consumer contraction:** The macro environment is actively hostile to packaged food companies dependent on consumer spending. Oil at $116 increases transportation and packaging costs. Consumer sentiment down 12.5% YoY accelerates private label substitution.

4. **Tariff and input cost inflation:** Management acknowledges tariff-driven cost pressures in FY2026. Commodity inflation (corn, soy, wheat, poultry) is compounding. With limited pricing power, margin compression continues.

5. **Cooking spray litigation:** $185M in remaining settlement payments through Q1 FY2027, partially offset by $82M in insurance receivables. A net ~$100M cash drain.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 72.0% against 27.3% historical volatility represents a 44.6pp spread — the market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty. The term structure is in steep backwardation (72.0% near-term vs. 37.3% at 6 months), indicating the market expects a specific stress event in the very near term. Put/call OI ratio of 1.62 and put/call volume ratio of 1.22 confirm elevated bearish positioning. The 16.3% put/call skew in the nearest expiry reflects demand for downside protection. This options profile is consistent with institutional positioning for further deterioration, likely around the next earnings catalyst or potential dividend action.

Short interest at 10.7% (39.5M shares, 2.8 days to cover) adds to the bearish institutional positioning picture.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
- Normalized earnings of ~$1.87/share at 10-12x would imply $19-22 (21-42% upside).
- Portfolio reshaping (divesting legacy brands, acquiring snack-oriented businesses) could accelerate growth in higher-margin categories.
- The 8.9% dividend yield provides income support if maintained.
- Ardent Mills JV provides stable, diversified earnings.
- Back-half FY2026 cash flow recovery (seasonal pattern) could restore dividend coverage.
- A consumer spending recovery or de-escalation in the Iran conflict reduces input cost pressure.

### Bear Case
- Revenue decline is structural: private label competition, GLP-1 adoption, and consumer trade-down have no visible reversal catalyst.
- Refrigerated & Frozen goodwill at zero cushion means additional impairments are likely in an environment of rising discount rates.
- Dividend coverage is broken at current cash flow levels. A cut removes the income thesis and triggers forced selling.
- $7.24B in debt at ~5% average cost consumes a disproportionate share of deteriorating operating cash flow.
- Management's FY2026 guidance (flat to +1% organic growth) appears disconnected from H1 results (-6.3%) and macro conditions.
- No insider buying despite a 41% decline signals that management lacks conviction at current prices.
- Stagflationary macro (oil $116, inflation >3.5%, potential rate hikes) pressures both consumer demand and input costs simultaneously.

## Conclusion

Conagra Brands is a deteriorating business burdened by a legacy acquisition that has destroyed significant shareholder value. The core financial metrics — declining revenue, eroding margins, compressed free cash flow, $9.73B in goodwill against a $7.5B market cap, and a dividend not covered by current FCF — are uniformly negative. The 8.9% yield has the profile of a value trap: it requires the company to reverse declining cash flow trends in a stagflationary environment that is actively hostile to its business model.

The options market, short sellers (10.7% short interest), and the stock's 41% decline over the past year are consistent with the fundamental deterioration thesis. No insider buying at these levels is an additional negative signal. With goodwill impairment risk ongoing, a potential dividend cut, and no growth catalysts on a 12-month horizon, the downside risk materially outweighs any recovery scenario.

**Recommendation: AVOID.** The stock is cheap for structural reasons, and those reasons are getting worse.
