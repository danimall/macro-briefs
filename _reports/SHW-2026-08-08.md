## Executive Summary

Sherwin-Williams is executing well against a weak end-market. Q2 2026 net sales rose 7.5% to $6,789.3M, adjusted diluted EPS rose 9.5% to $3.70 (about 5.1% above non-GAAP consensus), adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 60bp to 21.5%, and management raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $11.80-$12.20 from a range it had left unchanged at Q1. All three segments beat internal plan. That is the fourth consecutive earnings beat, and consensus estimates have risen 2.9% (current year) and 2.6% (next year) over 90 days.

The composition of the growth is what governs the rating. Decomposing Q2, Suvinil contributed roughly 2.05pp of consolidated growth and favorable FX translation roughly 0.78pp, leaving organic constant-currency growth near 4.7%, of which Paint Stores Group price was mid-single-digit and Performance Coatings price low-single-digit. Company volume is therefore running at approximately +1% to +2%. Paint Stores Group segment margin *fell* 10bp in H1 2026 (21.85% versus 21.95%) and consolidated gross margin fell 20bp in Q2 to 49.2%, despite mid-single-digit price. Price is covering cost inflation, not exceeding it. That is the mechanical reason the company announced an 8% Paint Stores price increase effective September 1, into a market management itself describes as showing "no meaningful improvement in demand" with second-half leading indicators pointing to "continued demand softness."

At $369.73 — 2.6% below the 52-week high and 15.3% above the $320.79 level of our mid-June review — the shares trade at 30.8x the FY2026 adjusted guidance midpoint, 33.2x the GAAP midpoint, 27.2x next-year consensus of $13.59, 21.3x TTM EBITDA on an enterprise value of about $101.0B, and a 3.60% free cash flow yield. Since our last review, price has advanced roughly five times faster than estimates. Our base case of $385 implies 4.1% upside; the bear case of $298 implies 19.4% downside. Probability-weighted total return including the 0.87% dividend is approximately 1.5% over twelve months, below fair compensation for the risk, with unfavorable asymmetry. We move from HOLD to REDUCE. This is a de-rating call on entry price and near-term margin arithmetic, not a judgment that the franchise is impaired.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1866 and headquartered in Cleveland, Sherwin-Williams manufactures and distributes paint and coatings through three segments plus an Administrative function.

**Paint Stores Group (PSG)** generated FY2025 net sales of $13,605.9M (+3.2%) and segment profit of $3,061.5M at a 22.5% margin. PSG is 57.7% of consolidated revenue but 67.8% of pre-Administrative segment profit. It sells Sherwin-branded architectural, industrial and protective coatings exclusively through a company-operated network — 4,853 stores at 12/31/2025, 4,841 at 6/30/2026.

**Performance Coatings Group (PCG)** generated $6,795.2M (flat) and $942.7M of segment profit at 13.9% reported (17.3% adjusted, down from 18.0%), serving general industrial, automotive refinish, packaging, industrial wood, coil and protective/marine markets in over 120 countries through 317 branches and third-party distributors.

**Consumer Brands Group (CBG)** generated $3,166.4M (+1.9%) and $509.6M at 16.1% reported (19.3% adjusted). CBG sells Valspar, Dutch Boy, Krylon, Minwax, Thompson's WaterSeal, Cabot and, since October 2025, Suvinil and Glasu! through mass merchandisers, home centers and dealers, plus 308 company stores in Latin America. CBG also manufactures for PSG, with intersegment transfers eliminated in consolidation.

The structural advantage is channel control rather than formulation. Sherwin-branded product reaches the professional contractor through stores Sherwin-Williams owns and staffs; Behr reaches the same contractor through Home Depot's aisle, Benjamin Moore through independent dealers. This is why PSG earns a 22-25% segment margin against PCG's 14%, and why the company can announce an 8% list increase without negotiating it through a retail intermediary. Foreign subsidiaries produced $4,615M of FY2025 sales (19.6% of total); roughly 80% of revenue is North American.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2022-FY2025 revenue compounded at 2.10% ($22,148.9M to $23,574.3M), three years of nominal-GDP-or-worse growth in a business priced as a compounder. H1 2026 broke the plateau at +7.2% ($12,456.2M), with Q2 at +7.5%. Stripping Suvinil (~$129.5M in Q2) and FX translation (~$49M) leaves roughly 4.7% organic constant-currency, of which price is the large majority. PSG same-store sales accelerated to +4.2% in Q2 from +0.8% a year earlier, with volume turning from low-single-digit declines in FY2025 to low-single-digit growth.

**Margins.** The margin engine changed direction in 2026. FY2025 showed gross margin expansion (48.85% versus 48.5%) with SG&A deleverage (32.64% versus 32.1%). Q2 2026 inverted that: gross margin 49.2%, down 20bp, with SG&A down 90bp to 31.0% and pretax margin up to 16.4% from 15.6%. Management quantified Q2 restructuring at approximately $17M of annual savings, 0.07% of TTM revenue, so the SG&A leverage is predominantly price and volume flow-through, not structural cost removal. Segment margins H1 2026 versus H1 2025: PSG 21.85% versus 21.95% (down 10bp), CBG 21.68% versus 18.84% (up 284bp), PCG 13.97% versus 13.45% (up 52bp). The Administrative loss widened to -$639.9M from -$572.9M on new-headquarters costs and interest.

The highest-return segment in the company is not expanding margin while realizing mid-single-digit price. Raw materials, energy, logistics and packaging are inflating faster than realized price in the segment that carries two-thirds of segment profit.

**Earnings.** FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS fell 2.7% to $10.26; adjusted EPS rose 0.9% to $11.43. H1 2026 GAAP EPS was $5.58 (+11.6%) and adjusted $6.05 (+7.7%). FY2026 guidance midpoints imply +8.4% GAAP and +5.0% adjusted growth. The buyback contribution matters: Q2 net income rose 11.8% against 14.3% GAAP EPS growth, and on the adjusted line, implied net income growth was roughly 7.2% against 9.5% adjusted EPS growth. Approximately 2.3-2.5pp of headline EPS growth is share count, not operations.

**Cash flow.** TTM operating cash flow of $3,886.7M against TTM net income of $2,688.2M is a 1.45x conversion — high-quality earnings. TTM capex of $673.5M leaves TTM free cash flow of $3,213.2M, a 13.2% margin on TTM revenue of $24,410.3M. H1 2026 operating cash was $1,486.6M against $1,051.5M, but H1 is the seasonal working-capital build for a paint company (H1 2025 was only 30.5% of the FY2025 total), so H1 strength should not be annualized. A defensible FY2026 operating-cash range is $3.9-4.5B, and we would underwrite nearer the low end given raw-material inflation and receivables extension.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt rose to $12,072.1M at 6/30/26 from $10,871.3M at year-end; net debt is $11,778.6M. Against TTM EBITDA of $4,734.6M, net leverage is 2.49x — at the top of the stated 2.0-2.5x target. Including the $813.0M real estate financing obligation from the failed headquarters sale-leaseback, adjusted net obligations reach roughly $12.59B, or 2.66x. Interest coverage remains comfortable at approximately 7.8x TTM EBIT to interest, and covenant headroom to the 3.75x consolidated leverage test implies EBITDA could fall roughly a third.

Two balance-sheet items warrant attention. Short-term borrowings reached $2,246.4M (including $1,460.9M of domestic commercial paper) against $1,706.7M a year earlier, while **unused credit capacity fell from $3,649M to $1,969M in six months** — a 46% reduction in the backstop supporting the CP program. Separately, the carrying value of public debt was $9,325.7M against a fair value of $8,421.4M, a $904.3M (9.7%) discount confirming below-market embedded coupons; the January 2026 repayment of $350.0M of 3.95% notes with commercial paper is the template for how cash interest steps up as maturities roll. FY2025 interest expense was $465.0M; the 10-K guided to roughly +$85M in 2026, and H1 actual of $267.5M versus $216.2M is running slightly ahead of that.

A note on screens: a standardized Altman Z-Score of 1.22 ("distress") for this company is a definitional artifact — it zeroed the EBIT/assets term and substituted book equity for market capitalization. Recalculated properly, Z is 3.68-3.90, safely above 2.99. Retained earnings fell $6.2B in FY2025 because the Q4 retirement of 29.5M treasury shares reduced retained earnings by $7,996M with zero cash effect. Reported book D/E of 5.63x and ROE of 55.9% measure an accounting election. Solvency is not the issue; capital-structure optionality is.

**Capital allocation.** H1 2026 returns were $1,837.0M of buybacks (5.6M shares at an average $328.04) plus $394.6M of dividends, totaling $2,231.6M against H1 FCF of $1,239.9M — 180% of H1 free cash flow, with total debt up $1,200.8M. H1 buybacks alone exceeded the entire FY2025 repurchase of $1,656M. Shares outstanding fell 2.6% year-over-year to 242.8M. The $3.20 annualized dividend (47th consecutive annual increase) is a 31% payout of FY2025 GAAP EPS and yields 0.87%. The average repurchase price of $328.04 is 12.7% below today's close, so price execution was good. The funding source — debt, at the leverage ceiling, with the liquidity backstop halved — is the offset.

## Growth Analysis

The end-market detail from Q2 is specific. In PSG: protective and marine up a double-digit percentage, commercial up high-single-digit, residential repaint up mid-single-digit, new residential down low-single-digit in H1. In PCG: general industrial and automotive refinish each up high-single-digit; packaging, industrial wood and coil each up mid-single-digit.

Growth is coming from non-residential, industrial and protective demand plus price. The protective/marine and commercial strength is consistent with an industrial construction cycle in these end markets, a durable-looking demand pocket for coatings.

Residential is the problem. Residential repaint is PSG's largest end market and it correlates with existing-home turnover, which remains depressed, and new residential is still contracting. A housing-turnover recovery is an un-cashed option in these shares rather than something being earned.

Two other growth qualifiers. First, PSG net store count was **-6 in Q2 and -12 in H1 2026**, against +20 and +38 in the comparable 2025 periods and a 10-K plan for 80-100 new stores in 2026. The most likely reading is deliberate network optimization with gross openings back-half weighted, which is consistent with same-store sales accelerating as volume concentrates into surviving stores and with the RONAE metrics in the compensation plan. Management commentary reconciling the plan to the H1 count was not obtainable, so this is unresolved. If unit growth runs below the historical ~2% per year, price and share-of-wallet must do more of the work.

Second, estimate revisions are positive but modest: +2.9% current year and +2.6% next year over 90 days, +3.4% and +3.9% for the current and next quarter. This confirms steady execution, not the +30% revision velocity that signals a genuine earnings inflection. Next-year consensus of $13.59 implies 12.6% growth off $12.07 — respectable, and dependent on the September price increase sticking.

## Valuation Assessment

| Metric | Value at $369.73 |
|---|---|
| P/E, TTM diluted EPS ($10.84) | 34.1x |
| P/E, FY2026 GAAP guide midpoint ($11.12) | 33.2x |
| P/E, FY2026 adjusted guide midpoint ($12.00) | 30.8x |
| P/E, next-year consensus ($13.59) | 27.2x |
| Enterprise value | ~$101.0B ($101.8B incl. real estate financing) |
| EV / TTM EBITDA ($4,734.6M) | 21.3x |
| EV / TTM sales | 4.14x |
| FCF yield on market cap | 3.60% |
| Dividend yield | 0.87% |

Peer context is heterogeneous and requires judgment. The sector median P/E of 35.7 is distorted by DuPont at 147.7x and LyondellBasell at negative earnings. The two informative comparables are Ecolab at 35.7x — a similar-quality, similar-market-cap compounder — and PPG at 16.9x, the closest direct business comparable in coatings, with a 2.5% dividend yield. SHW at 30.8x forward adjusted trades at roughly 1.8x PPG's multiple. Much of that premium is deserved: PSG's owned-store channel produces margins PPG cannot match through distribution. But the premium leaves no margin of safety, and it is being paid for approximately 5% adjusted EPS growth in the current year.

Growth-adjusted, the arithmetic is unfavorable. On FY2026 adjusted guidance growth of 5.0%, the forward PEG is above 6. On next-year consensus growth of 12.6%, PEG is 2.4. Neither reading supports treating analyst targets as stale floors; that heuristic applies when a stock looks cheap relative to rising estimates, which is not the case here. Consensus mean of $390.10 sits 5.5% above spot and median $395 sits 6.8% above; the low target of $340 is 8.0% below. The distribution of 25 covering analysts (3 strong buy, 11 buy, 11 hold, 0 sell) is consistent with a fully-valued high-quality name.

The absolute yield test also fails. A 3.60% free cash flow yield and a 0.87% dividend yield mean the equity offers limited income support and requires either multiple expansion or earnings acceleration to generate return. With net leverage at 2.49x and interest expense guided up roughly $85M, financial leverage is working against per-share earnings rather than for them.

We are not penalizing the stock for having appreciated 15.3% since June. The appreciation was multiple expansion — from roughly 26.5x FY2026 adjusted guidance in mid-June to 30.8x now — against estimate revisions of 2.9%. The discount that made the June entry defensible has been consumed.

**Scenario framework:**
- **Bear $298 (-19.4%):** the 8% increase is only partially realized, contractors trade down or defer, raw-material and logistics inflation continues (oil-linked resins, solvents, TiO₂, containers), gross margin compresses, FY2027 adjusted EPS lands near $12.40 and the multiple compresses to 24x — roughly PPG-plus-a-premium rather than a full compounder multiple.
- **Base $385 (+4.1%):** 28.5x on FY2027 adjusted EPS of $13.50, slightly below consensus to reflect partial price realization. Volumes hold at +1-3%, industrial and protective demand offsets flat residential, buyback continues to remove 2-2.5% of shares annually.
- **Bull $450 (+21.7%):** 31.5x on $14.30. The 8% sticks with minimal volume loss, gross margin recovers 100bp+, existing-home turnover improves in 2027, and PSG margin resumes expansion toward 25%.

Weighting base 50%, bull 20%, bear 30% gives an expected price return of approximately +0.6%, or roughly +1.5% including the dividend. That is less than fair compensation.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is specific: 4,841 company-operated stores in North America give Sherwin-Williams direct pricing authority over the professional contractor plus a service model (job-site delivery, color matching, contractor credit) that a big-box aisle cannot replicate. That mechanism is what makes an 8% list increase announceable at all. The competitive set — PPG, AkzoNobel, RPM, Benjamin Moore (Berkshire, dealer channel), Behr (Masco, Home Depot) and Nippon — cannot match the combination of scale and owned distribution.

Trajectory is more mixed than the moat description implies. PSG segment margin declined 10bp in H1 despite mid-single-digit price, meaning cost inflation is currently outrunning the pricing mechanism in the crown-jewel segment. Management attributes the Q2 beat to "new account wins and increased share of wallet" and claims outperformance of the market; that assertion could not be independently verified against third-party architectural coatings share data and should be treated as management's characterization. The supporting circumstantial evidence — same-store sales accelerating from +0.8% to +4.2% while the market is described as flat — is real but flattered by price and, potentially, by volume consolidating out of the 12 net-closed stores.

Suvinil integration looks constructive. CBG adjusted segment margin rose 210bp to 24.5% in Q2, reversing FY2025's purchase-accounting drag. Part of that improvement is not operational: the company cited "benefits from foreign currency transaction gains and losses," and the consolidated FX transaction line swung $12.8M favorably in Q2 and $28.6M in H1. If the full Q2 swing were attributed to CBG it would represent up to 21% of CBG's $60.0M adjusted profit increase, though the exact allocation is not disclosed.

The Administrative drag is growing. Corporate costs plus interest consumed roughly 26% of pre-Administrative segment profit in H1, with the loss widening $67.0M on new-headquarters depreciation (Q2 depreciation +24.2% to $98.5M) and interest. Capitalized interest of $37.2M in 2025 is now ending as the asset enters service, moving cost from the balance sheet into the income statement.

## Risk Assessment

**1. September 1 price realization (the pivotal near-term binary).** An 8% PSG increase is being pushed into a market with "no meaningful improvement in demand" and volumes at +1-2%. No elasticity data is disclosed. Full realization would step FY2027 EPS up materially; partial realization with volume loss trades margin for share.

**2. Raw material and logistics inflation.** After flat FY2025 costs, management raised the raw-material inflation outlook to low-to-mid single digits at Q1 and described "broad-based cost inflation across raw materials, energy, logistics and packaging" at Q2. Key inputs — propylene-derived resins, latex, solvents, TiO₂, containers — are oil-linked, making this a live 2027 variable rather than a receding one.

**3. Firetex fire-protection coatings — unquantified.** Per the Q2 10-Q, a third-party certifier changed the listing for Firetex FX9502 intumescent structural-steel fire protection in July 2024; the company has received claims including from a competitor; it is investigating potential inaccuracies in the certifier's test results for *other* Firetex intumescent products; and it is investigating the Firetex Design Estimator software, which recommended dry film thicknesses that were under-estimated or exceeded published maximums. Carboline Global filed a Lanham Act false-advertising suit on 9/2/2025. **No accrual or estimable range is disclosed.** Liability may rest substantially with the certifier and scope may prove narrow; we assert only that the company has left an unquantified investigation standing across multiple filings, and that no valuation currently reflects it.

**4. Lead pigment litigation carried at zero accrual.** California Proceedings under a 2019 agreement plus Wisconsin risk-contribution cases involving four individuals (*Beal* at summary judgment, *Valoe* stayed 4/6/2026, *Gibson* discovery closing 12/15/2026). Four plaintiffs is a small number; Wisconsin's doctrine, which shifts burden without product identification, is the precedent risk.

**5. Contractor credit deterioration.** Accounts receivable rose 14.8% to $3,571.2M against H1 sales growth of 7.2%; DSO extended roughly 3 days to 47.9; H1 bad debt expense rose 34.8% to $39.5M with write-offs of $20.3M versus $13.0M and the allowance up from $62.5M to $81.7M. Two non-exclusive readings: credit stress among small painting contractors in a soft residential market, and a Brazilian receivables book from Suvinil with longer local terms. Segment-level AR is not disclosed, so the two cannot be separated. Absolute amounts remain small against $1,378.3M of H1 net income — a monitorable, not a thesis-breaker.

**6. Financing and rate exposure.** $2,246.4M of short-term borrowings, $1,969M of remaining credit capacity, a $904.3M fair-value discount on public debt guaranteeing higher cash interest on refinancing, and roughly $0.8B of real estate financing outside the debt line. Higher rates are a direct per-share earnings headwind.

**7. Other legal and environmental.** Environmental accruals of $264.3M with $73.9M of reasonably possible additional exposure; Gibbsboro NJ (NPL since 1999) is the majority, with the NJDEP natural resource damages trial adjourned and no new date. Texas AG petition regarding the Garland facility (amended 1/30/2026), no accrual disclosed. Major Sites were reduced from four to three in Q2, a favorable development.

**8. Governance and disclosure flags.** The proxy's "Adjusted EPS" series ($10.71 for 2025) does not reconcile to MD&A adjusted diluted EPS ($11.43), so the compensation definition differs from the investor-reporting definition and should not be used to model earnings. The FY2026 GAAP-to-adjusted gap of roughly $0.88 is substantially Valspar amortization ($0.81/share), a 2017 acquisition still being adjusted out in 2026.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 26.9% against 27.4% one-year realized vol is fair (spread -0.5%), sitting at the 26th percentile of the current chain. The nearest expiry prices a ±4.1% move; the September tenor prices ±7.0%, which brackets the first market read on the price increase. Term structure is mildly backwardated (26.9% near versus 24.8% at six months), the near-term put/call skew is balanced at 1.1%, and put/call open interest is 0.42 with volume ratio 0.45 — call-tilted positioning. Short interest is 2.8% of float at 2.5 days to cover. ATM IV has collapsed from 141.5% on 6/18 to 26.9% as the earnings event resolved. The options market is not corroborating a bearish thesis. Our REDUCE rests on valuation and margin arithmetic, and the absence of options confirmation is a genuine divergence; it argues for trimming rather than aggressively exiting.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The owned-store network is the most durable distribution asset in coatings, and the September 1 8% increase is the clearest demonstration of that pricing authority in years. If contractors absorb it — as they have absorbed prior increases — gross margin recovers the 20bp lost in Q2 and more, PSG margin resumes its path toward 25%, and FY2027 adjusted EPS clears $14.00. Volumes have already turned positive after two years of decline. Industrial, protective and commercial end markets are growing high-single to double-digit on the non-residential construction cycle. Residential repaint and new residential are the two weakest markets and both are options, not liabilities: any easing in mortgage rates unlocks existing-home turnover, which drives PSG's largest end market. Free cash flow of $3.2B TTM funds a buyback removing 2.5%+ of shares annually, executed at an average $328.04 in H1, 13% below today's price. Four consecutive beats and rising estimates support 31-32x on $14.30, or $450.

**Bear case.** The 2026 reacceleration is price, FX and an acquisition; volume is +1-2% and PSG margin is down. Pushing 8% into flat demand risks trade-down to Behr, Benjamin Moore and PPG precisely when small contractors are showing credit strain — bad debt expense +34.8%, DSO +3 days, receivables growing at twice the rate of sales. Raw materials, energy, logistics and packaging are inflating, and PSG could not expand margin on mid-single-digit price. Residential turnover is unlikely to recover while mortgage rates remain elevated. Interest expense is guided up roughly $85M with the existing debt stack carrying a $904M below-market fair-value discount, so every refinancing raises cash cost. Leverage is at the 2.5x ceiling and unused credit capacity fell 46% in six months, financing buybacks at 180% of H1 free cash flow. Store count is contracting against a plan for 80-100 openings. Firetex is an unquantified structural-fireproofing exposure with a competitor already litigating. On $12.40 of FY2027 adjusted EPS at 24x, the stock is $298.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: REDUCE.** Horizon: 6-12 months, with the decisive checkpoint at the October Q3 print.

**Scenario targets from $369.73:**
- Base case **$385** (+4.1%): 28.5x FY2027 adjusted EPS of $13.50, modestly below consensus $13.59 to reflect partial price realization.
- Bull case **$450** (+21.7%): 31.5x on $14.30.
- Bear case **$298** (-19.4%): 24x on $12.40.
- Probability-weighted (50/20/30): approximately +0.6% price return, +1.5% total return including the 0.87% dividend.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength; the shares are 2.6% below the 52-week high and 27.6% above the 52-week low, and the multiple has expanded from roughly 26.5x to 30.8x FY2026 adjusted guidance in seven weeks while estimates rose 2.9%. Reduce toward a partial position ahead of the October print rather than exiting entirely; the franchise quality and the un-cashed housing option justify residual exposure. Do not add here.

**Triggers that would restore HOLD or better:**
1. Q3 (October) results showing consolidated gross margin *up* year-over-year with PSG segment margin expanding, evidencing that the September 1 increase is being realized without volume loss. Two consecutive quarters of PSG margin expansion on positive volume would support an upgrade to ACCUMULATE.
2. Price back toward $320-330 — the H1 repurchase average of $328.04 — which restores roughly 27x forward adjusted and re-opens the asymmetry.
3. Net leverage returning below 2.25x with unused credit capacity rebuilt above $3.0B, removing the balance-sheet constraint on buyback capacity.

**Triggers that would move us to AVOID:**
1. PSG volume turning negative in Q3 or Q4 alongside gross margin compression — the trade-down scenario confirmed.
2. Disclosure of an accrual or estimable range on Firetex, or an adverse ruling on the Carboline motion that expands the matter beyond a competitor's commercial claim.
3. Bad debt expense continuing to grow at 2x+ the rate of sales with DSO extending beyond 50 days, indicating contractor-credit deterioration rather than Suvinil mix.

**Reassessment date:** late October 2026 (Q3 earnings), which will contain the first two months of realization data on the price increase and the resolution of the store-count question.

## Conclusion

Sherwin-Williams remains one of the better-positioned businesses in Materials: owned distribution to the professional contractor, 22.5% margins in the segment that produces two-thirds of profit, $3.2B of TTM free cash flow at 1.45x net income conversion, and a management team that repurchased 5.6M shares at an average 13% below today's price. Four consecutive beats and rising estimates confirm competent execution.

The problem is the price. At 30.8x FY2026 adjusted guidance and 27.2x next-year consensus, with a 3.60% free cash flow yield, the shares require the 8% September price increase to be fully realized into a market management describes as showing no demand improvement — while the last two quarters demonstrate that mid-single-digit price was not enough to expand PSG margin. Volumes are +1-2%, receivables and bad debt are deteriorating faster than sales, leverage sits at the top of the stated target with the liquidity backstop halved, interest expense is rising, and the residential recovery that would validate the multiple is not visible.

Base-case upside of 4% against 19% bear-case downside, with an expected total return near 1.5%, does not compensate for that configuration. Trim into strength; revisit at $320-330 or on evidence in October that the September increase is sticking with gross margin expanding.

**Analyst consensus:** low $340, mean $390.10, median $395, high $420 (25 covering analysts: 3 strong buy, 11 buy, 11 hold, 0 sell).
