## Executive Summary

United Airlines is trading at $93.96, down ~24% YTD and ~21% from its 52-week high of $119.21, with a forward P/E of 8.7x against January 2026 guidance of $12.00-$14.00 adjusted EPS. That guidance was issued before oil breached $119/bbl and before the IEA declared the current Middle East situation the "largest supply disruption in history of the global oil market." UAL does not hedge its fuel exposure, sources a disproportionate share of fuel from the West Coast (where refining margins are elevated), and has committed to $12B+ in 2026 capex for fleet expansion. Every dollar increase in jet fuel prices costs UAL approximately $180-200M annually.

The stock's decline reflects rational repricing of an unhedged, operationally leveraged business model in the worst fuel cost environment since the 2008 oil spike. Management's JPMorgan conference commentary emphasized brand loyalty and demand resilience, but demand strength cannot offset margin compression when input costs are rising 30-50% above the assumptions embedded in January guidance. The prior score trajectory (6.9 → 5.0, BUY → AVOID) correctly captured this deterioration, and conditions have worsened since the March 18 assessment. The AVOID recommendation is maintained.

## Company Overview

United Airlines Holdings operates the third-largest US airline by revenue, with major hubs at Newark, Houston, Denver, Chicago O'Hare, San Francisco, Washington Dulles, and Los Angeles. The company is executing an aggressive fleet expansion, growing from 1,096 mainline aircraft in Q1 2026 to a planned 1,180 by year-end — an 84-aircraft net addition (+7.7%). The fleet plan includes 58 additional B737 MAX deliveries and 27 additional A321neo/XLR aircraft during 2026, representing a significant capital commitment during a period of acute operational uncertainty.

The company's market cap stands at $30.4B with a beta of 1.37, confirming its high sensitivity to broad market moves. No dividend is paid.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Guidance (Pre-Crisis):** UAL guided Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.00-$1.50 and FY2026 adjusted EPS of $12.00-$14.00 on January 20, 2026. At the midpoint ($13.00), this implies ~$4.2B in net income on approximately 323M diluted shares.

**Fuel Cost Sensitivity:** Airlines typically derive 25-35% of operating expenses from fuel. UAL's lack of a hedging program means 100% exposure to spot prices. With jet fuel cracking at approximately $30-35/bbl above crude, $119 crude implies jet fuel around $150-155/bbl. UAL consumes approximately 4.2-4.5B gallons annually. A $0.10/gallon increase costs approximately $420-450M pre-tax. The move from the ~$80 crude assumed in January guidance to $119 implies a fuel cost headwind of approximately $3.5-5.0B annually, which would reduce EPS by $8-12 per share on a pre-tax basis. Even with partial demand-driven fare increases, this magnitude of cost increase makes the $12-14 guidance range unreachable.

**Capital Expenditure:** The company guided <$8.0B in adjusted total capex for 2026. This is a fixed commitment driven by aircraft delivery schedules that cannot be easily deferred. Combined with elevated fuel costs, free cash flow generation is likely to be severely pressured or turn negative.

**Profit Sharing:** The $45-75M Q1 profit sharing accrual signals management expected healthy profitability. This assumption now appears aggressive given fuel costs.

**Balance Sheet:** Financial statements were not provided in the data, but UAL historically carries $25-30B in total debt including lease obligations. The airline industry's high fixed-cost structure means small revenue or margin changes produce large swings in earnings and cash flow. With beta at 1.37 and no hedging, the financial profile is highly pro-cyclical in an environment turning hostile.

## Growth Analysis

**Fleet Expansion:** UAL is adding 84 mainline aircraft in 2026, growing capacity approximately 7-8%. In a normal demand environment, this drives revenue growth and unit cost dilution. In a stagflationary environment with $119 oil, it locks in elevated fuel consumption against potentially weakening demand.

**Hub Investments:** The Dulles airport renovation (noted in recent news) improves long-term hub economics. These are multi-year structural positives, but they do not offset the current fuel cost crisis.

**Demand Trends:** Management emphasized "strong demand trends" at the JPMorgan conference and positioned UAL as one of two "brand loyal airlines" that will outperform in a downturn. This framing has merit — UAL's premium product (Polaris, United Club) and domestic hub dominance provide pricing power relative to ULCCs. However, brand loyalty provides relative outperformance within the airline sector, not absolute protection against a $40/bbl increase in crude.

**Capacity Discipline Risk:** UAL's aggressive capacity growth occurs while the broader economy shows recession probability at 40-45%. If demand weakens while capacity grows 7-8%, revenue per available seat mile (RASM) will decline, compounding the margin pressure from fuel.

## Valuation Assessment

At $93.96 with forward P/E of 8.7x on $12-14 guidance, UAL appears statistically cheap. This is misleading for two reasons:

1. **Guidance is stale.** The $12-14 EPS range assumed crude oil prices roughly $35-40/bbl below current levels. Adjusting for the fuel cost headwind described above, realistic 2026 EPS is likely $2-6 depending on demand elasticity, fare passthrough, and oil's path. At $4 EPS, UAL trades at ~23.5x — expensive for an airline.

2. **Cyclical troughs compress multiples.** Airlines historically trade at low P/E ratios near earnings peaks and high P/E ratios near troughs. The 8.7x multiple reflects peak-earnings expectations that are now impaired.

Wall Street consensus target of $138.56 (per 24/7 Wall St.) was set before the current oil spike intensified. Redburn already cut its target to $110 "amid fuel cost concerns" on March 5, when oil was lower than today. Further target cuts are likely.

Analyst distribution: 7 strong buy, 19 buy, 4 hold, 0 sell. This consensus bullishness reflects pre-crisis positioning and typical sell-side lag. The absence of sell ratings on an unhedged airline with $119 oil is a contrarian indicator of stale consensus.

## Competitive Landscape

UAL operates in a consolidated US airline oligopoly (DAL, UAL, AAL, LUV control ~80% of domestic capacity). This structure provides pricing power in normal conditions. UAL specifically benefits from:

- **Hub dominance:** Newark (near-monopoly), Denver, Houston are fortress hubs with limited competitive overlap
- **Premium product:** Polaris business class, United Club renovations, and loyalty program improvements drive higher-yield traffic
- **International network:** Pacific and Atlantic exposure provides revenue diversification

However, competitive advantages do not insulate UAL from the fundamental problem: fuel represents a pass-through cost that exceeds the airline's ability to raise fares proportionally, particularly in a weakening consumer environment. DAL, unlike UAL, maintains a fuel hedging program, creating a competitive disadvantage for UAL specifically in this environment.

## Risk Assessment

**Fuel Cost (CRITICAL):** Oil at $119 with IEA projecting a 6-month supply restoration timeline. Central range $112-122 with 25-30% probability of $130-150. UAL's zero-hedging policy means full spot exposure. Every $10/bbl move in crude costs UAL approximately $1.3-1.5B annually. This single factor can reduce EPS by $4-5 per share.

**Recession/Demand Destruction:** Recession probability 40-45%. Consumer spending already weakening (January retail sales -0.2%). Business travel is more resilient but not immune to corporate cost-cutting in a stagflationary environment.

**Capex Inflexibility:** $8B+ in committed capex for fleet deliveries that cannot easily be cancelled or deferred. This creates a cash burn scenario if revenues disappoint.

**West Coast Fuel Premium:** UAL's San Francisco and Los Angeles hubs face structurally higher fuel costs due to California's unique refining constraints and environmental regulations. Seeking Alpha specifically flagged UAL's "exposure to West Coast fuel price spikes."

**Hormuz Closure Duration:** The world model estimates 40% probability of conflict extending beyond 6 weeks. Extended closure increases the probability of airline-specific operational disruptions (route cancellations, overflying costs, reduced international demand).

**Stagflation Macro:** The Fed discussed rate hikes. Higher rates increase UAL's debt service costs on floating-rate obligations and depress consumer spending on discretionary travel.

## Conclusion

UAL is an AVOID. The stock's 24% YTD decline has not fully priced the earnings impairment from $119 oil with no hedging. January guidance of $12-14 EPS is unachievable at current fuel prices; realistic 2026 EPS is likely $2-6, making the apparent 8.7x P/E illusory. The company's aggressive fleet expansion commits $8B+ in capex during the worst fuel cost environment in nearly two decades, while the macro regime (stagflation, 40-45% recession probability, Fed discussing rate hikes) compounds demand risk. Per the calibration framework, Consumer Discretionary (airlines included) BUY calls have averaged -1.54% returns, and this environment — $119 oil, zero hedging, aggressive capex — represents the maximal expression of the risks that have historically made airline BUY calls destructive in this system's track record.

Short interest at 4.5% is moderate and does not suggest a squeeze setup. The options market's backwardated term structure and elevated put skew corroborate the fundamental bear case. Investors seeking cheap cyclicals should look at sectors with input cost tailwinds rather than headwinds.
