## Executive Summary

Koppers is a levered, three-segment industrial materials converter in the middle of the largest restructuring program in its recent history. The operating story of the last four quarters is genuinely mixed: adjusted EBITDA held essentially flat at $256.7M in FY2025 on a $212.8M (-10.2%) revenue decline, Performance Chemicals volumes have recovered +11% in Q2 and +12% in H1 2026 after a 17% share-loss-driven collapse in FY2025, and management has removed the maturity wall — 98% of $928.3M of debt now matures in 2030, with net leverage at 3.4x against a 4.75x covenant and ~$390M of liquidity at 6/30/26.

What has changed since our last review is the price against the earnings. In June the stock was $43.73; it reached $53.00 on August 6 and trades at $45.00 today. Over the same period the company guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA to $240-250M (externally reported from the August 6 call, not filing-confirmed), below FY2025's $256.7M, and took a $215.8M impairment, restructuring and plant-closure charge in Q2 alone — roughly four times the previously disclosed Stickney program — producing a $(147.5)M quarterly GAAP net loss. On our estimates that charge takes tangible book equity from $137.9M at 12/31/25 to approximately zero, since goodwill ($329.4M) and intangibles ($106.7M) were not the assets written down. Enterprise value of roughly $1.73B against guided EBITDA of $245M is 7.1x, the top of the 4.4-7.5x market-multiple range disclosed in the company's own October 2025 goodwill test.

Three specific, dated items sit between here and the consensus 2027 EPS of $4.90 (+20.7% over the $4.06 current-year figure): copper hedges that Note 5 states run only "through the end of 2026," after which a portion of PC's 22.4% Q2 margin lapses; $400M of interest-rate swaps fixing SOFR at 3.97% that expire in April 2027, re-exposing roughly $900M of floating debt; and CMC, whose H1 EBITDA fell to $8.5M from $26.7M, a ~$29M full-year headwind that PC's $14.7M H1 gain does not cover. The next-quarter consensus has already been cut 46.2% in thirty days. We rate the shares REDUCE: base case $50 (+11%), bull $68 (+51%), bear $29 (-36%), probability-weighted expected return of roughly +7% before a 0.8% dividend, on a business where each 1x of EV/EBITDA multiple equals about 30% of the equity.

## Company Overview

Koppers Holdings is a holding company whose operations sit at Koppers Inc.; upstream dividends are restricted by the Koppers Inc. credit agreement. FY2025 net sales of $1,879.3M split across three segments:

- **Railroad and Utility Products and Services (RUPS)** — $926.8M (49%), adjusted EBITDA $108.1M. Largest supplier of crossties to North American Class I railroads, second-largest US utility pole producer, largest Australian pole supplier. 18 wood treating plants. Product lines: railroad treated $551.5M, utility poles $304.6M, rail infrastructure services $70.7M. Approximately 75% of North American Railroad Products sales are under long-term contract; serves 8 of the top 10 US utilities and ~630 short-line railroads. Named competitor: Stella-Jones.
- **Performance Chemicals (PC)** — $543.8M (29%), adjusted EBITDA $102.7M (18.9% margin, the highest of the three). Wood preservatives to 10 of the 11 largest US lumber treaters. Vertically integrated in copper: ~30M pounds of scrap purchased annually.
- **Carbon Materials and Chemicals (CMC)** — $408.7M (22%), adjusted EBITDA $45.9M. Coal tar distillation into creosote, carbon pitch, naphthalene and carbon black feedstock. Three distillation facilities at 12/31/25, being reduced further. Named competitor: Rain Carbon.

The stated vertical-integration advantage is that CMC supplies 100% of North American RUPS creosote requirements, and that all crosstie treating plants sit on the rail lines of the largest railroad customers. Customer concentration is genuinely low: no customer above 6% of FY2025 sales, top ten approximately 36%. Item 1 states explicitly that Koppers does not manufacture against a backlog, so there is no order book to use as a forward indicator; contract assets were $1.8M at 12/31/25. Non-US operations generated ~29% of sales but 73% of FY2025 pretax income ($59.2M foreign versus $22.0M domestic).

## Financial Analysis

**Five years of flat earnings on a swinging revenue base.** Adjusted EBITDA: $223.5M (2021), $228.1M (2022), $256.4M (2023), $261.6M (2024), $256.7M (2025) — corroborated by the pay-versus-performance table in the proxy. Revenue over the same window moved from roughly $1.68B to $2.15B and back to $1.88B. FY2025 net margin was 3.0%. The business converts commodity inputs at low single-digit net margins and has not demonstrated the ability to expand absolute earnings across a cycle.

**FY2025 GAAP operating profit was flattered.** Operating profit rose to $167.8M from $148.2M despite the sales decline, but management itself removed $34.2M of mark-to-market commodity hedging gains (20.4% of reported operating profit) and an $11.0M LIFO benefit from adjusted EBITDA. Per Note 5, the Q3 2025 hedge-effectiveness test failed for copper swaps, cash-flow hedge accounting was prospectively discontinued, and $15.0M of cumulative pre-tax unrealized gain went straight to earnings; total FY2025 unrealized copper gain was $19.1M versus a $(7.7)M loss in 2024. All 25.6M pounds of outstanding swaps were undesignated at year-end, carried as a $31.4M net asset versus an $(8.9)M liability a year earlier. The reversal has begun: H1 2026 shows $6.9M of MTM losses versus $9.8M of gains in H1 2025, a $16.7M year-over-year swing against GAAP. Copper price moves now flow into cost of sales without hedge-accounting smoothing, permanently raising GAAP earnings volatility.

**Q2 2026: operations up, GAAP down.** Sales $520.1M (+3.0%; +5.1% underlying after $16.3M of portfolio effects and $6.7M of favorable FX). Adjusted EBITDA $71.0M (-7.9%). Segment margins: PC $37.7M at 22.4% (from 19.0%), RUPS $25.7M at 10.5% (from 12.6%), CMC $7.6M at 7.2% (from 16.2%). GAAP net loss $(147.5)M, approximately $(7.60) per share, on $215.8M of impairment/restructuring and a $(32.5)M tax benefit. The charge covers exiting distillation and chemical operations at Stickney, Illinois plus idling two RUPS plants and transformation consulting.

**Balance sheet.** At 12/31/25: assets $1,886.8M, liabilities $1,312.5M, Koppers equity $574.0M, current ratio 2.94, Altman Z 2.26 (grey zone). Debt $928.3M — revolver $448.0M at 5.65%, Term Loan B $480.3M at 6.22% — with $4.9M amortizing in each of 2026-2029 and $916.7M in 2030. The June 2025 amendment set the total net leverage covenant at 4.75x for the life of the facility. Covenant metrics: 3.4x net leverage and 4.4x cash interest coverage at 6/30/26. Holding net debt constant, covenant EBITDA can fall 28.4% before a breach (3.4 ÷ 4.75 = 0.716). That is adequate, not generous, for a company whose CMC segment EBITDA fell 68% year over year in H1.

All debt is floating. $400M notional of swaps fix SOFR at a weighted average 3.97%, and all expire in April 2027, leaving roughly $900M of unhedged floating exposure absent re-hedging. Interest expense has been falling ($76.2M FY2024 → $66.1M FY2025 → ~$60M annualized off Q2's $15.0M); the April 2027 roll-off is a dated risk to that trend.

**Tangible book.** At 12/31/25, equity $574.0M less goodwill $329.4M less intangibles $106.7M = $137.9M, or $7.09/share. Deducting the H1 2026 net loss of $(140.4)M plus ~$15M of Q2 buybacks and dividends, and given that the impairment hit PP&E rather than goodwill, tangible book equity is now approximately zero. Partially offsetting: a $104.7M LIFO reserve ($5.45/share pre-tax, ~$4.10 after tax) representing inventory carried below current cost, monetizable only on liquidation of LIFO layers.

**Cash flow.** OCF $146.1M (2023) → $119.4M (2024) → $122.5M (2025); capex $120.5M → $77.4M → $55.0M; FCF $25.6M → $42.0M → $67.5M. FCF improved because capex fell 54% from the 2023 peak, not because operations improved. FY2025 OCF absorbed a $57.2M payables outflow as the company brought aging from 85% current/15% past due to 95%/5%. That reversed in 2026: at 6/30/26 aging was back to 85%/15%. H1 2026 OCF of $96.3M (versus $27.8M) therefore reflects re-lengthened payables and inventory reduction against a prior year depressed by ~$14M of pension funding. We do not annualize it. As a labeled sensitivity, 2 × $96.3M = $192.6M; the defensible normalized figure is closer to FY2025's $122.5M, and the business is explicitly seasonal with Q1/Q4 materially weaker than Q2/Q3.

Capital returns: $100M authorization from February 2025 with $29.6M remaining at 6/30/26 (~$70M deployed in 16 months), including 358,232 shares in Q2 2026 at $41.27 and $42.55 — below the current price. Share count fell from 20.86M (12/31/23) to ~19.23M today, -7.8%. The dividend was raised to $0.09 quarterly, $0.36 annualized, 0.80% yield, ~$6.9M annual cost, comfortably covered.

## Growth Analysis

There is no revenue growth thesis here, and management does not claim one. The strategy is explicit portfolio contraction: "growing the more profitable businesses while continuing to selectively scale back our lower margin, capital intensive business," with the target date drifting from "the next three years" in the 10-K to "by the end of 2028" in the 10-Q. Capex guidance of $55M for 2026 against $73.6M of D&A confirms a shrinking asset base; CMC took $18.9M of FY2025 capex against $24.0M of its own D&A.

**Segment-by-segment forward drivers:**

- **RUPS.** The Railway Tie Association 2026 crosstie estimate was revised from 19.9M ties (13.3M Class I) in the 10-K to 19.8M (13.2M) in the 10-Q, now "slightly below" 2025. Pricing has already turned: FY2025 delivered $11.0M of crosstie-led price increases; by Q2 2026 the MD&A cites "price decreases across multiple markets, particularly for crossties." The Florence, South Carolina plant is being idled "due to lower overall future forecasted demand from the facility's largest customer." Utility poles remain the bright spot, with data center construction for AI development named as the primary growth driver alongside grid hardening and electrification — a $304.6M revenue line with a real structural tailwind, augmented by the $20.7M Greenhill Reload acquisition (Douglas fir transmission poles, western US).
- **PC.** Volumes +11% in Q2 and +12% in H1, margin to 22.4%. But FY2025 sales of $543.8M remain $127.8M below 2023's $671.6M, so this is partial recovery. The demand driver was downgraded materially between filings: the LIRA remodeling forecast moved from +2.9% easing to +1.6% (10-K) to +0.5% year-over-year by Q1 2027 (10-Q), "less than overall inflation." Part of the margin gain is copper-hedge vintage — the Q2 MD&A attributes lower raw material costs to "the benefit realized from our copper-hedging program, net of increased scrap copper costs," and Note 5 says copper is hedged only through end-2026. The alternative explanation, fixed-cost absorption on +11% volume, is durable; both are likely contributing and disclosure does not permit apportionment.
- **CMC.** Structurally shrinking by design and by raw-material availability. The MD&A links coal tar supply to metallurgical coke production, which is falling outside Asia; European supply was already damaged by the loss of Russian and Ukrainian sourcing. Aluminum smelting is migrating to low-energy-cost regions where Koppers has little presence, and carbon pitch prices fell ~6% globally in FY2025 and ~6% again in H1 2026.

**Guidance arithmetic.** H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA was $120.3M; guidance of $240-250M implies H2 of $120-130M against H2 2025's $124.1M — roughly flat, achievable but requiring CMC to stop deteriorating. Doubling CMC's H1 $8.5M gives ~$17M for FY2026 against $45.9M in FY2025, a $29M headwind that PC's $14.7M H1 gain does not cover. Consensus reflects the strain: current-quarter estimate $1.35 (-1.2% in 30 days), next quarter $0.56 (-46.2% in 30 days, versus $0.70 actual in Q4 2025), current year $4.06 (-0.7%), next year $4.90 (+4.2%). The near-term revision profile is negative; the out-year is up modestly and, in our view, does not yet incorporate the copper-hedge lapse or the swap roll-off.

Growth prospects score 4.0: three years of revenue decline, five years of flat EBITDA, guided EBITDA down year over year, one segment in structural terminal decline, and a single genuine growth line (utility poles) inside a 49%-of-revenue segment with falling pricing.

The peer table supplied is not usable for relative valuation: median P/E of 111.4 across a set including three negative-earnings names (SXC, OI, TWI) and one at 111x (MTUS) provides no signal. A more informative internal benchmark exists in the filings themselves. The October 31, 2025 goodwill test disclosed market multiples of 4.4-7.5x adjusted EBITDA and discount rates of 12.5-20.0%. Koppers currently trades at 7.1x forward EBITDA, the top of the range its own valuation exercise assigns to businesses of this type. The same test showed reporting-unit fair values exceeding carrying values by 24-58%, compressed from 22-103% a year earlier.

In early November 2025 the stock traded at $25-27, implying roughly 5.5x EV/EBITDA on then-current $256.7M. Today it is 7.1x on lower guided EBITDA. That is a ~30% multiple expansion coinciding with a ~5% reduction in forward earnings expectations. Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation argument, and we do not treat it as one, but the combination of an expanded multiple and reduced forward earnings is a valuation argument, because it means the transformation targeted for 2028 is already partly in the price.

The leverage structure sharpens both directions. Equity is roughly 50% of enterprise value, so a 1x change in EV/EBITDA moves equity value by about $257M, or 30% of market cap. We treat a cheap-looking 7.1x with caution: the multiple is not low, the earnings base has not grown in five years, and the equity is a stub.

Consensus targets are $54 low, $62 high, $57 mean, $55 median, all above spot, from three analysts. On stocks with rising estimates and reasonable multiples, targets often lag reality. That condition is not met here: near-term estimates are falling, and the out-year figure carries two identifiable unpriced headwinds. We treat these targets as anchored on non-GAAP 2027 EPS that we believe is at risk, not as independent confirmation.

## Competitive Landscape

Item 1A states the position plainly: "we believe that the most significant competitive factor for our products is selling price." That framing is more diagnostic than the market-share statistics.

**Crossties** are a duopoly-plus-fringe versus Stella-Jones in a mature market — 18-22M ties annually against a ~450M installed base, with Class I railroads taking ~70% of production. Koppers' advantages are physical: plants sited on customer rail lines, and internal creosote. The test of whether that constitutes a wide moat came quickly. FY2025 produced $11.0M of crosstie-led price increases; four quarters later the MD&A reports price decreases, particularly for crossties, with unfavorable mix. Pricing power in this segment is episodic.

There is also a second-order issue in the integration story. CMC supplies 100% of North American RUPS creosote requirements, and CMC is the segment whose raw material base is in structural decline and whose distillation footprint goes from three facilities to two with the Stickney exit. The vertical integration cited as RUPS' advantage runs through the business being harvested. The filings offer petroleum-blended substitutes as mitigation and note that Denmark-sourced creosote, with an explicit US-Denmark/Greenland trade-tension risk factor, supplies part of the requirement. This is a competitive dependency worth monitoring rather than a resolved advantage.

**Performance Chemicals** is the most differentiated business, with patents and know-how flagged as important in Item 1A, supplying 10 of the 11 largest US lumber treaters. Concentrated customer bases cut both ways: FY2025's 17% volume decline came from "a shift in United States market share," meaning a single account change produced a double-digit volume event and a $40.0M segment EBITDA decline. The +11-12% 2026 recovery is real improvement in trajectory; sales remain $127.8M below 2023.

**CMC** is in managed retreat, and the filings are candid that the industry "has operated in an excess capacity mode." Aluminum smelting migration away from mature geographies where Koppers holds share, plus falling ex-Asia met coke output, gives a shrinking market on both sides of the ledger.

Net trajectory: PC improving from a self-inflicted trough, RUPS stable in volume with deteriorating price and one customer-driven plant idling, CMC structurally declining. Competitive position 4.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Environmental exposure is the largest unquantified item.** Total environmental reserves were $10.2M at 12/31/25. Against that: one Koppers-owned NPL site; Portland Harbor, where EPA's 2017 ROD estimated the remedy at ~$1.1B NPV / ~$1.7B undiscounted with the 10-K stating costs "will likely increase," and where Koppers believes it is a de minimis contributor among 80+ parties in a private allocation with $3.9M accrued for Portland Harbor plus Newark Bay combined and an explicit caveat that actual cost "could be materially higher." The Beazer East indemnity (December 1988 asset purchase, guaranteed by Beazer Limited, no monetary cap) has performed in all material respects, but the third-party-claim indemnification window closed July 14, 2019 and Beazer East "may now tender certain of such claims to Koppers Inc." Item 1A states that indemnitor failure "could result in us having a significant negative net worth." Separately, certain conditional asset retirement obligations are unrecorded because they cannot be estimated, with the filing noting that "such amounts may be material" — a live capitalization question as plant closures accelerate.

**Stickney enforcement.** The Illinois AG received an enforcement referral from the Illinois EPA in June 2024 over alleged air emissions violations at Stickney. No reserve has been provided. The company is now closing distillation there by September 30, 2026, which may reduce forward exposure but does not resolve historical claims.

**Copper basis and tariffs on the highest-margin segment.** Hedges are LME-indexed while most purchases price off COMEX; a 50% US tariff on semi-finished copper effective 8/1/25 has caused significant COMEX premiums, and a Commerce recommendation on refined copper is expected by mid-2026. Hedge accounting has already been discontinued, so this now flows through cost of sales. Total tariff exposure is estimated at $4-8M of pre-tax profit over twelve months if unmitigated, against $100-120M of imports and $90-110M of exports.

**Rate exposure.** All debt floating; $400M of swaps at 3.97% expire April 2027.

**Goodwill.** $329.4M, sitting only in RUPS ($156.4M) and PC ($173.0M); CMC carries none. Cushion narrowed to 24-58% from 22-103%, with Utility Products ($116.7M) designated the sole critical audit matter.

**Known unrecognized charge.** Note 14 discloses an expected ~$20M pre-tax settlement loss on converting the UK buy-in annuity to a buy-out, timing delayed by pending UK remedial legislation.

**Governance and insider activity.** CFO turnover was near-complete inside five months: Jimmi Sue Smith retired effective January 5, 2026; CAO Bradley Pearce served as interim; Eric Brenner (ex-Nova Chemicals) started May 26, 2026. KPMG issued unqualified opinions on FY2025 financials and internal control, there is no restatement or material weakness, and the Q2 10-Q reports no change in internal control, so we read this as succession under strain rather than an accounting flag, though it coincides with the largest restructuring in years. Insider transactions run one way: President and CTO James Sullivan sold 80,000 shares at $50.01-$51.22 on August 7, one day after earnings, roughly 47% of his pre-sale position, leaving 90,398 shares; CAO Pearce sold 2,000 at $51.01 on August 10; the CEO sold ~10,200 shares across three May-June transactions (~2% of his ~440,000-share position). No open-market purchases by any officer or director appear in the window. The Q2 10-Q states no director or officer adopted, modified or terminated a Rule 10b5-1 arrangement during Q2, which does not tell us whether the August sales executed under a pre-existing plan. What is verifiable is size, timing relative to the print, and price — roughly 11% above today's basis, with the stock down about 12% from the August 6 high within three sessions.

Compensation alignment is mechanically sound (CEO compensation actually paid swung from $17.57M in 2023 to $(3.00)M in 2024 to $1.08M in 2025) but the underlying record is poor: indexed TSR of 89.66 versus a peer group at 154.36 from a 12/31/2020 base — roughly 64 percentage points of five-year relative underperformance. Note 8 also discloses that 137,226 market-condition PSUs and 31,167 RSUs were issued in lieu of part of the 2025 cash incentive, saving ~$5M of 2025 expense and deferring recognition into later years; combined with short-term incentive expense falling to $6.6M from $13.7M and stock compensation to $13.8M from $20.8M, a meaningful share of the FY2025 SG&A improvement is incentive-cost related. The alternative reading — that a formulaic plan simply paid out less on missed targets — is equally consistent with the disclosure.

Risk factors score 3.5: multiple specific, dated, quantified exposures, several carrying no reserve, against approximately zero tangible book equity.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($68, +51%).** The transformation is real and the market is paying only 7.1x for its first year. Exiting Stickney distillation and phthalic anhydride removes a structurally declining, capital-intensive, low-margin business; two RUPS plant idlings consolidate volume into Kennedy, Alabama and Guthrie, Kentucky at higher utilization. Capex of $55M against $73.6M of D&A funds a rising FCF profile from a shrinking asset base. PC volumes are up 11-12% with margin at 22.4% and $128M of revenue still to recover toward 2023 levels. Utility poles — $304.6M and growing — sit directly in front of data-center-driven grid investment, aging-infrastructure replacement and storm hardening, one of the more durable demand themes available in Materials. There is no maturity wall before 2030, covenants have 28% EBITDA headroom, and roughly 8% of shares have been retired in 2.5 years at prices below today's. If FY2028 EBITDA reaches $285M with net debt in the $770M range, the equity is worth about $71 at 7.5x.

**Bear case ($29, -36%).** Adjusted EBITDA has not moved in five years, and 2026 guidance is below 2025. Three identified 2027 headwinds are not in consensus: copper hedges expire at end-2026 and remove part of PC's margin uplift; $400M of swaps roll off in April 2027, re-exposing ~$900M of floating debt; and CMC's contribution is heading toward zero, a ~$29M annual gap PC's gains do not close. Demand drivers were downgraded between February and August — LIRA remodeling to +0.5% by Q1 2027, RTA crosstie estimate below 2025 — and crosstie pricing has already turned negative. The next-quarter consensus was cut 46% in thirty days. Tangible book is approximately zero after the Q2 charge, environmental exposures of potentially material size carry $10.2M of reserves plus an indemnity whose third-party window has closed, and the equity is a 50%-of-EV stub where one turn of multiple is 30% of market cap. At 6.5x on $220M the stock is $29.

The bull case turns on the durability of the utility-pole line and the cash-flow effect of a genuinely smaller asset base. The bear case turns on the fact that the two most visible 2026 margin tailwinds, copper hedge vintages and lower incentive compensation, are dated and finite, while the two most visible 2027 cost items, swap expiry and CMC stranded overhead, are dated and rising.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria
