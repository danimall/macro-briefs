## Executive Summary

SouthState Bank Corporation completed its transformative acquisition of Independent Financial Group in January 2025, growing from a $46B to a $67B institution. The integration is now fully complete, and the financial results confirm meaningful accretion: FY2025 EPS of $7.87 (+12.9% YoY) despite 33% share dilution, NIM expansion of 52bps to 3.95%, and a 380bps improvement in efficiency ratio to 53.1%. Management has signaled confidence through an aggressive share repurchase authorization and a dividend increase to $2.40 annualized.

At approximately 10.3x forward earnings and ~1.06x GAAP book value ($91.38/share), SSB appears to trade at a discount to peer multiples on a P/E basis. However, on a price-to-tangible book value basis, SSB trades at approximately 1.71x TBV ($56.27/share as of Dec. 31, 2025), reflecting the substantial goodwill and intangibles accumulated through acquisitions. The 4-quarter earnings beat streak (avg. surprise +9.8%) and management's guidance for continued operating leverage suggest the market may be undervaluing normalized earnings power for a franchise with premium Southeastern/Texas markets, granular deposits, and proven acquisition execution.

The primary concerns include the Q1 2026 NIM compression to 3.79% (from FY2025's 3.95%), multifamily CRE stress (22.6% substandard), and the inevitable accretion headwind as the Independent portfolio seasons. However, the fundamental trajectory remains positive and the franchise quality supports a constructive outlook.

## Company Overview

SouthState Bank Corporation is a $67.2B-asset financial holding company headquartered in Winter Haven, Florida, operating across 8 states (FL, SC, TX, GA, CO, NC, AL, VA). The company serves as a traditional relationship-focused community/regional bank with complementary businesses in correspondent banking and capital markets, wealth management, and corporate billing (factoring/invoicing).

The January 2025 closing of the Independent Financial acquisition added $13.1B in loans, $15.2B in deposits, and a significant Texas/Colorado presence. This transaction represented SSB's fourth major acquisition in recent years (following CenterState, Atlantic Capital, and smaller deals), establishing a pattern of disciplined M&A execution with rapid integration timelines. Systems integration was completed by May 2025 — a 5-month timeline for a deal of this magnitude is notably efficient.

The resulting franchise operates in high-growth Sun Belt markets with favorable demographic tailwinds: population migration to Florida, Texas, Georgia, and the Carolinas supports organic loan and deposit growth independent of rate cycle dynamics.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability Metrics (FY2025):**
- Net Income: $798.7M (GAAP); Adjusted PPNR: $1,273M
- EPS: $7.87 (diluted); ROAA: 1.22%; ROAE: 9.13%
- NIM (TE): 3.95% (vs. 3.43% in FY2024)
- Efficiency Ratio: 53.1% (vs. 56.9% in FY2024)

**Q1 2026 Update:**
- Diluted EPS: $2.28
- NIM (TE): 3.79% (down from FY2025's 3.95%)
- Net charge-offs: 0.09% annualized
- Book value per share: $92.21; Tangible book value per share: $56.90

The 52bps NIM expansion in FY2025 was the headline story. Drivers included: (1) acquired loan accretion of $258.6M generating a 7.46% yield on Independent portfolio loans versus 5.70% on legacy loans, (2) controlled deposit costs as rate cuts reduced funding pressure, and (3) the securities repositioning that shortened AFS duration from 6.47 to 4.22 years while improving yield. The Q1 2026 NIM compression to 3.79% confirms that accretion decay is underway, making the trajectory of organic NIM the critical analytical question going forward.

**Balance Sheet Quality:**
- Total loans: $48.6B; loan-to-deposit ratio: 88%
- CET1: 11.36% (well above 7.0% minimum + 2.5% buffer)
- ACL/loans: 1.20% (1.94x NPL coverage)
- Net charge-offs: 0.23% FY2025 (0.11% excluding day-1 PCD charge-offs); 0.09% in Q1 2026
- Unrealized AFS losses: $382.8M (5.7% of portfolio), improved from $808.6M (15.8%)

Capital adequacy is strong. The 11.36% CET1 ratio, while down from pre-acquisition levels, provides ample buffer for organic growth, continued buybacks, and potential credit losses. The securities repositioning materially reduced unrealized loss exposure, improving balance sheet resilience.

**Valuation Context — Book Value vs. Tangible Book Value:**
- GAAP book value per share: $91.38 (Dec. 31, 2025); $92.21 (Mar. 31, 2026)
- Tangible book value per share: $56.27 (Dec. 31, 2025); $56.90 (Mar. 31, 2026)
- At $97.36, P/B is approximately 1.06x; P/TBV is approximately 1.71x
- The spread between book and tangible book reflects ~$3.48B in goodwill and intangible assets from the Independent and prior acquisitions

**Credit Quality Concerns:**
NCOs at 0.23% in FY2025 remain below the ~0.35-0.50% range typical for elevated stress environments, and Q1 2026's 0.09% annualized rate suggests stabilization. However, the multifamily segment requires attention: NCOs spiked from 0.01% to 0.71% in 2025, and 22.6% of the $2.8B multifamily portfolio is classified as substandard/accruing. One large C&I charge-off ($21.5M) in Q3 2025 also suggests idiosyncratic concentration risk. The granular nature of the broader portfolio (avg. CRE loan <$2.5M) is a meaningful mitigant against systemic losses.

## Growth Analysis

**Earnings Trajectory:**
- FY2025 EPS: $7.87
- Q1 2026 EPS: $2.28 (annualized ~$9.12)
- Management guidance: ~4% expense growth, mid-to-upper single-digit loan growth

The EPS growth expected in FY2026 reflects the first full-year run-rate of Independent synergies, with merger costs ($117.8M in 2025) rolling off and efficiency gains realized. Management's guidance for ~4% expense growth and mid-to-upper single-digit loan growth implies continued positive operating leverage.

**Revenue Diversification:**
The correspondent banking/capital markets business grew 121% YoY to $72M, benefiting from rate volatility and the addition of Independent's client relationships. Wealth management revenue ($58M, +28%) adds higher-margin recurring fee income. These non-spread businesses reduce earnings sensitivity to NIM compression.

**Organic Growth Potential:**
Sun Belt markets provide structural tailwinds. Texas represents a meaningful new growth market with above-average business formation and population growth. Management's guidance for mid-to-upper single-digit loan growth is conservative given market demographics and implies disciplined underwriting rather than aggressive expansion.

**Headwind: Accretion Decay:**
The $258.6M in acquired loan accretion will decline over 2-3 years as the Independent portfolio seasons. The Q1 2026 NIM compression to 3.79% already reflects this dynamic. Using a rough 30% decay rate annually, this implies a $75-80M headwind to net interest income in 2026, partially offset by organic volume growth and deposit repricing. A faster-than-expected decay could pressure NIM toward 3.50-3.60% over time.

- P/TBV: ~1.71x (tangible book value $56.27/share)

**Valuation Context:**
SSB's P/B of ~1.06x reflects a stock trading near GAAP book value, which is modestly supportive of downside protection. However, the more relevant metric for acquisition-heavy banks is P/TBV, where SSB trades at approximately 1.71x — a level that reflects the market's recognition of franchise value above tangible assets but does not represent a deep discount. The P/E multiple of approximately 10-11x on an annualized Q1 2026 basis appears more attractive relative to peers, though the exact peer discount is difficult to quantify precisely without a contemporaneous peer comparison.

**Fundamental Quality vs. Valuation:**
SSB's superior operating metrics (NIM of 3.79% in Q1 2026 vs. estimated peer average of ~3.3-3.5%, efficiency ratio of 53.1%, ROAA of 1.22%) suggest the franchise deserves at least a peer-level multiple. If the market assigns a multiple consistent with these operating fundamentals as integration concerns fade, meaningful upside exists from current levels.

## Competitive Landscape

SouthState occupies an advantageous position in the community/regional bank landscape. Key competitive advantages include:

**Franchise Quality:** 24% noninterest-bearing deposits, 1.4M accounts at an average $39K balance, and 134.5% liquidity coverage of uninsured deposits. This granular, relationship-based deposit franchise provides stable, low-cost funding that is difficult to replicate.

**Market Position:** The combination of Florida, Texas, Georgia, and Carolinas creates one of the most demographically favorable footprints among banks its size. Population and business formation growth in these markets exceeds national averages by 2-4x.

**M&A Capability:** Four successful integrations in recent years demonstrates institutional competence in acquiring, integrating, and extracting value from community banks. This creates optionality as the M&A cycle potentially accelerates under favorable regulatory conditions.

**Fee Diversification:** The correspondent banking platform and wealth management business provide earnings streams that are less rate-sensitive than traditional spread income, and both are growing at double-digit rates.

**Competitive Threats:** Larger regionals (Truist, Regions) compete for the same customer base in many markets; fintech disintermediation of small business lending; potential for rate-driven deposit flight to money market funds in certain environments.

## Risk Assessment

**1. CRE Concentration (Moderate-High):**
At 271.8% of risk-based capital as of Dec. 31, 2025 (rising to 275.8% at Mar. 31, 2026), CRE exposure is elevated and approaching the 300% supervisory concentration indicator (a regulatory threshold that triggers heightened scrutiny, though not a hard limit). The multifamily segment ($2.8B) shows early stress with 22.6% substandard classification. While the granular nature of the portfolio (avg. loan <$2.5M) mitigates catastrophic loss scenarios, a broader Sun Belt multifamily correction could generate $50-100M in incremental losses over 2-3 years.

**2. Accretion Decay (Medium-High):**
The $258.6M in FY2025 accretion income will decline each year, as confirmed by Q1 2026's NIM compression to 3.79%. If organic growth and deposit repricing cannot offset this decline, NIM could compress further toward 3.50-3.60% over 2-3 years, creating EPS headwinds.

**3. Interest Rate Sensitivity:**
Slightly asset-sensitive (+100bps = +1.4% NII; -100bps = -1.9% NII). In the current environment with elevated policy uncertainty, rate volatility creates earnings volatility. The securities repositioning (shorter duration) reduces this risk but doesn't eliminate it.

**4. Regulatory Uncertainty:**
At $67B in assets, SSB falls under heightened regulatory standards for institutions >$50B. The OCC proposed raising this threshold to $700B in December 2025, which if finalized would reduce the regulatory burden. However, as a proposal rather than a final rule, the outcome remains uncertain. Should current standards persist, additional compliance investment may be required.

**5. Cybersecurity Litigation:**
Class action from February 2024 incident remains pending. The company received a $3.6M insurance reimbursement for related costs, suggesting direct expenses were relatively contained, but the ultimate litigation outcome remains uncertain.

## Conclusion

SouthState represents a well-managed regional bank franchise trading at approximately 1.06x GAAP book value and an estimated 10-11x forward earnings, which appears attractive relative to its superior operating metrics. The 4-quarter earnings beat streak, successful integration of a transformative acquisition, and active capital return program support a constructive thesis. The Q1 2026 NIM compression to 3.79% and multifamily CRE stress warrant monitoring, but Q1 credit metrics (0.09% NCOs) and continued organic growth provide reassurance.

The key monitoring item over the next two quarters is whether organic loan growth and deposit repricing can offset the natural decay of acquired loan accretion. If Q2/Q3 results demonstrate NIM stability above 3.70% with NCOs below 0.30%, the stock should converge toward $110+ as integration discount fades. The 2.5% dividend yield provides income support during the holding period.
