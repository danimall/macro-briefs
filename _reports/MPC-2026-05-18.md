## Executive Summary

Marathon Petroleum is the largest U.S. refiner by throughput capacity (2,986 mbpcd), currently trading at approximately $252 with a forward P/E of approximately 9.6x on FY2026 consensus EPS of approximately $26.30. The stock has appreciated 54% over the past year, driven by strong earnings: Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $1.65 significantly beat consensus (various sources cite estimates of $0.72–$1.09), crack spreads have widened materially due to Hormuz supply disruptions, and sell-side estimates have been revised materially upward following the beat.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) structurally elevated refining margins driven by Middle East conflict and U.S. crude cost advantages, (2) aggressive capital return reducing share count at approximately 10-11% annually, and (3) MPLX's $37B midstream value providing stable cash flow that partially insulates total enterprise value from crack spread volatility. The primary risk is geopolitical de-escalation compressing margins — but even under normalized crack spreads of $13-15/bbl, MPC's mid-cycle earnings power of $15-18/share supports a valuation above $200.

## Company Overview

Marathon Petroleum operates three segments: Refining & Marketing (the earnings driver, generating $6.1B adjusted EBITDA in FY2025 and tracking well above that in FY2026), Midstream (operated through ~64%-owned MPLX LP, generating $6.75B EBITDA with mid-single-digit growth), and Renewable Diesel (marginally profitable at best, immaterial to the thesis). MPC's refining system spans refineries across the Gulf Coast, Mid-Continent, and West Coast, with complexity that allows processing of discounted sour crude at a $2.76-$4.84/bbl advantage.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Dynamics:**
FY2024 EPS of $10.08 represented a cyclical trough as crack spreads compressed. FY2025 recovered to $13.22. Q1 2026 alone generated $1.65 adjusted EPS on a $15.34/bbl blended crack spread (vs. $11.31 in Q1 2025), with FY2026 consensus now approximately $26.30, implying substantial margin expansion through the driving season.

The Q1 2026 beat was large: adjusted EPS of $1.65 vs. consensus estimates in the $0.72–$1.09 range indicates sell-side analysts systematically under-modeled the impact of widened crack spreads. Post-earnings upward estimate revisions reflect analysts incorporating physical market realities that had outpaced their models.

**Balance Sheet:**
Consolidated debt of $33.3B looks concerning in isolation, but structural analysis matters: ~$26B sits at MPLX (generally non-recourse to MPC parent, with limited exceptions for certain guarantees, IG-rated, self-servicing from LP cash flows). MPC-parent debt is ~$7.3B against $5.6B liquidity. The Altman Z-Score of 2.44 is a methodological artifact of consolidating MPLX — the parent entity is comfortably investment-grade with manageable maturities.

Rising interest expense ($525M → $839M → $1.276B over FY2023-2025) reflects MPLX's acquisition strategy, not deteriorating credit quality. MPLX's new assets (Northwind, BANGL, Whiptail) are accretive and expanding capacity into the Permian-to-Gulf-Coast corridor.

**Capital Return:**
Since 2012, MPC has repurchased $56.4B through buybacks — approximately 74% of current market cap. Share count has fallen from 512M (2022) to 292M (Q1 2026), a 43% reduction. The new $5B authorization (May 5, 2026) provides ~$8.6B total capacity, representing approximately 12% of current market cap. At recent prices, this implies another 33M+ shares could be retired, continuing the compounding effect on per-share metrics.

MPLX distributions to MPC run ~$2.56B annually, funding a significant portion of the buyback program with stable, growing midstream cash flow.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-Term (FY2026-2027):**
Current-year consensus of approximately $26.30 EPS reflects elevated crack spreads driven by Hormuz disruptions. The estimate revision trajectory following Q1's beat has been strongly positive, with analysts revising FY2026 and FY2027 estimates materially higher as they incorporate the elevated crack spread environment.

**Structural Supports:**
- U.S. refining enjoys structural crude cost advantages (WTI/MEH discount to international crude) that persist regardless of geopolitics
- Global refining capacity rationalization through end of decade supports utilization rates above 90%
- MPLX targets 12.5% annual distribution growth to MPC in 2026 and 2027, growing MPC's stable cash flow base
- Refined product export sales volumes (~362 mbpd in Q1 2026) allow MPC to capture international product premiums

**Growth Headwinds:**
- Long-term gasoline demand decline from EV penetration (MPC's own risk factors cite alternative-fuel vehicles and mandates as risks, though the most material impact is likely multi-year rather than near-term)
- Renewable Diesel segment remains marginally profitable ($38M EBITDA in Q1 2026)
- Rising RIN/compliance costs ($2.4B annualized in Q1 2026 vs. $2.07B in 2023)

## Valuation Assessment

At approximately $252 with FY2026 consensus of approximately $26.30 EPS, MPC trades at roughly 9.6x forward earnings. The refining sector broadly trades at depressed forward multiples reflecting market skepticism about earnings sustainability.

This skepticism is partially warranted — crack spreads are geopolitically elevated. However, even using a "normalized" crack spread scenario of $13-15/bbl (vs. current $15.34+), mid-cycle EPS power is $15-18/share, implying the stock trades at 14-17x normalized earnings. This discount appears excessive given MPC's superior scale, capital return program, and integrated midstream.

**Sum-of-Parts:**
- MPLX: 647M units × $57 = $37B value to MPC
- MPC-parent debt: ~$7.3B
- Implied refining value: ~$71.5B market cap + $7.3B debt - $37B MPLX = ~$42B
- Refining EBITDA (FY2025): $6.1B → ~6.9x implied refining multiple
- On FY2026 projected Refining EBITDA (if cracks sustain near $15/bbl): $8-10B → 4.2-5.3x

The refining business is cheap relative to the elevated earnings environment, and the MPLX holding provides a valuation anchor even if refining reverts to trough.

**Analyst targets:** Low $186, high $335, mean $259. The mean target at $259 sits near current levels, but given the velocity of upward estimate revisions post-Q1, these targets are likely stale. Historical pattern with refiners during crack spread expansions is that targets lag price by 4-8 weeks.

## Competitive Landscape

MPC's competitive advantages are quantifiable and durable within the refining cycle:
- **Scale:** 2,986 mbpcd capacity — largest U.S. footprint, providing operational flexibility to optimize crude sourcing and product yields across refineries
- **Complexity:** Nelson Complexity Index allows processing of discounted heavy/sour crudes at a $2.76-4.84/bbl advantage
- **Utilization:** 94% in FY2025; Q1 2026 crude capacity utilization was 89% (with management guiding to 94% for Q2 2026), reflecting seasonal maintenance patterns and strong overall reliability
- **Integration:** MPLX provides captive midstream logistics, reducing third-party costs and ensuring crude supply security
- **Geographic diversity:** Gulf Coast, Mid-Continent, West Coast exposure diversifies crack spread risk across regions

Against peers, MPC's integrated model (refining + midstream) most closely parallels PSX but with larger refining scale and better capital return execution. The 5-year TSR of 448% vs. 329% for the peer group validates the operational and capital allocation edge.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risk — Geopolitical De-Escalation (HIGH IMPACT):**
The Iran conflict is among the primary drivers of current elevated crack spreads, alongside stable demand and higher global product prices. A ceasefire or de-escalation could compress blended cracks from $15-17/bbl toward $11-13/bbl within weeks. At $11/bbl cracks, quarterly EPS would decline to $2-4/share range (annualized $8-16), creating significant downside from current levels.

We assign low probability to full geopolitical resolution within the next 6 months and modest probability to partial sanctions relief. Multiple diplomatic attempts have failed, and Bloomberg reporting as of mid-May 2026 indicates the U.S. and Iran remain far apart. These probability estimates reflect analyst judgment, not quantifiable odds, and should be treated as scenario assumptions. This risk does not dominate the near-term probability distribution in our base case.

**Secondary Risks:**
- California regulatory/labor (10-15% probability of material disruption)
- Rising RIN costs compressing margins by $1-2/bbl annually
- Derivative losses creating earnings volatility ($909M in Q1 2026)
- Long-term demand decline from EVs (multi-year timeframe, per MPC's own risk factor disclosures)

**Options Market Signal:**
Market-implied volatility and positioning data suggest elevated uncertainty post-earnings, with call-heavy positioning indicating bullish skew. Short interest at 2.2% is minimal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($300-335, +19-33% upside):**
Hormuz disruptions persist through driving season, crack spreads expand to $17-20/bbl blended. FY2026 EPS reaches $30-35. MPC continues buying back 10%+ of float annually. Market re-rates refining from "cyclical at peak" to "structural beneficiary of multi-year supply dislocation." 11x forward on $30 EPS = $330.

**Bear Case ($185-200, -21-27% downside):**
Geopolitical resolution or partial diplomatic progress normalizes crude supply, cracks compress to $11-12/bbl by H2 2026. FY2026 EPS realized at $18-20. Market applies trough multiple of 10-11x. Even this scenario implies moderate downside from current levels, with the buyback providing a floor.

**Base Case ($280-300, +11-19% upside):**
Cracks remain elevated ($14-16/bbl) through 2026 given structural supply deficits and ongoing geopolitical disruption. FY2026 EPS of $25-28. Multiple of 10.5-11x. Buyback eliminates ~10% of shares. Share price reaches $280-300 over 12 months.

## Conclusion

MPC warrants a BUY at ~$252. The stock trades at approximately 9.6x forward earnings during a period of strong earnings momentum following a significant Q1 beat. The geopolitical backdrop driving elevated crack spreads shows limited credible path to resolution in the near term based on current reporting. The capital return program (~12% of market cap in buyback capacity, 43% share reduction over 3 years) provides a structural per-share earnings growth mechanism even if absolute margins eventually normalize. The MPLX asset ($37B value) provides stable cash flow underpinning the capital return. Even with an appropriate cyclical discount, $280-300 is achievable over 12 months given the earnings trajectory and buyback velocity.

The primary risk is a geopolitical resolution that compresses crack spreads — an outcome we assess as low probability in the near term given the conflict's multi-party complexity, though we acknowledge this is subjective judgment rather than a quantifiable probability. Position sizing should reflect the commodity cyclicality: 3-4% allocation appropriate for most portfolios, with tolerance for 23% drawdown in a normalization scenario.
