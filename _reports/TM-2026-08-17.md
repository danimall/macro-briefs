## Executive Summary

Toyota reported fiscal Q1 2027 (April–June 2026) on August 4 with a headline that flattered a deteriorating operating picture. Revenue of ¥13.53tn beat the ¥13.06tn Bloomberg consensus, net income of ¥1.48tn beat a ¥1.04tn consensus by 42% and rose 76% year over year, and management raised full-year operating income guidance 13% to ¥3.4tn. Underneath: operating income fell 8.8% to ¥1.06tn — the fifth consecutive quarterly decline — with operating margin down 160bp to 7.9% and global units down 0.8% to 2.39 million. Toyota disclosed that currency added ¥345bn to that quarter's operating profit. Removing it leaves roughly ¥0.715tn against a derived ¥1.162tn prior-year base, a decline near 38%. The net income beat came, by the company's own attribution, from "other income and a much weaker yen."

The guidance raise is lower quality than the 13% headline suggests. Its two named drivers are a yen assumption moved from 150 to 160 per dollar and improved Middle East logistics that cut the estimated Iran-war impact from ¥670bn to ¥510bn. The revised ¥3.4tn still sits 10% below FY2026's derived ~¥3.78tn and below the ~¥3.75tn Toyota was guiding before May's 20% cut. It excludes any impact from the magnitude-7.1 Kumamoto earthquake of July 28, which halted Kyushu-region production and the Tahara plant in Aichi and which the company says it is still assessing. And the currency assumption is already stale in the adverse direction: after the joint US-Japan yen-buying intervention in late July, spot sat near 157 on the print date versus lows near 164 in July.

Against this, the ADR has risen from $176.55 on July 23 to $191.11, while single-analyst current-quarter estimates were cut 46.5% over 90 days and the three-analyst next-year figure fell 19.0% in 30 days. Price up, estimates down, operating profit down for five quarters. At ~11.3x consensus current-year EPS of $16.91 and roughly 1.0x book, Toyota is not expensive, but cheapness here lacks a catalyst that is independent of the yen. We move from HOLD to REDUCE. This is our fifth review of a declining score series (7.4 in February to 5.2 in July), and the August print confirms rather than reverses that trajectory.

## Company Overview

Toyota is the world's largest automaker by unit volume, retaining the global top spot in the first half of calendar 2026. FY2026 (ended March 31, 2026) delivered revenue of ¥50.7tn, net income of ¥3.85tn, and 9.595 million vehicles. FY2027 guidance calls for ¥54tn revenue, ¥3.4tn operating income, ¥3.25tn net income, and 9.7 million units.

The operative structural fact is that Toyota is a hybrid-electric franchise. Management guided hybrid sales up 10% to 5 million units this fiscal year — approximately 52% of the 9.7 million unit target — and is expanding hybrid battery capacity while shifting chemistry from nickel-metal-hydride to lithium-ion, which the company says will improve performance while lowering costs by "several tens of thousands of yen per vehicle." Chief Officer Takanori Azuma cited strong US demand for Camry and RAV4, plus India (Urban Cruiser, Innova Hycross), Thailand and Europe (Yaris), and said hybrid and hybrid-battery production will be boosted through 2030.

Two reporting caveats govern this analysis. Toyota is a foreign private issuer filing a Form 20-F (264 pages for FY2026, per the company's own SEC filings library), not a 10-K. That filing was not machine-readable in this cycle, so Item 3.D risk factors, the audited statements, segment data and the notes were not read. There is no Section 16 insider tape and no DEF 14A. Separately, the supplied quote fields are internally inconsistent — trailing and forward P/E are both listed at 8.56 against EPS of $1.86, which cannot reconcile with a $191.11 price. We therefore build multiples from reported yen results and a derived share count rather than from those fields, and flag the resulting precision limits.

## Financial Analysis

**Q1 FY2027 (per company disclosures relayed August 4, 2026):**

| Metric | Q1 FY2027 | Q1 FY2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | ¥13.53tn | ~¥12.3tn | +10% |
| Operating income | ¥1.06tn | ¥1.162tn (derived) | −8.8% |
| Operating margin | 7.9% | 9.5% | −160bp |
| Net income | ¥1.48tn | ¥0.841tn | +76% |
| Global units | 2.39m | 2.41m | −0.8% |
| ¥/$ average | ~160 | ~145 | ~10% weaker yen |

*Prior-year operating income derived as ¥1.06tn ÷ (1 − 0.088).*

**The FX adjustment.** With a disclosed ¥345bn currency benefit to operating income, ex-FX operating profit was approximately ¥0.715tn versus ¥1.162tn, roughly −38%. Two limits on that figure: the ¥345bn is a company-disclosed effect on the operating line, not a constant-currency restatement — it does not isolate translation from transaction, does not deflate the FX-inflated revenue denominator, and does not net hedging or the pricing latitude a weak yen enables. Separately, a substantial part of the non-FX decline is separately quantified as episodic: the ¥510bn full-year Iran-war impact covers higher raw-material costs (aluminium named), delivery delays, lower volumes and supplier support payments. Both readings should be carried: underlying earnings power is materially weaker than the reported 7.9% margin implies, but not permanently 38% weaker.

**Earnings quality.** Toyota beat on revenue and net income and came in "somewhat below expectations" on operating income. Management attributed the net income figure to "other income and a much weaker yen." We could not decompose "other income," and the prior-year ¥841bn base was itself depressed, so part of the 76% increase is a base effect. A structural observation supports the non-operating point independently: FY2026 net income of ¥3.85tn exceeded derived FY2026 operating income of ~¥3.78tn, a 7.6% net margin above a 7.5% operating margin. For an industrial company that confirms equity-method affiliate income (Denso, Aisin and others), FX revaluation and listed-portfolio marks are structurally large contributors, not a one-quarter artifact. The four-quarter beat record — +43.8%, +12.0%, +27.5%, +24.3% — is therefore lower-signal than it looks, because the line being beaten is heavily FX- and affiliate-driven.

**Full-year composition.**

| | FY2026 actual | FY2027 guided |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | ¥50.7tn | ¥54.0tn (+6.5%) |
| Operating income | ~¥3.78tn (derived) | ¥3.40tn (−10%) |
| Operating margin | ~7.46% | 6.30% |
| Net income | ¥3.85tn | ¥3.25tn (−15.6%) |
| Units | 9.595m | 9.700m (+1.1%) |

Revenue is guided +6.5% on volume +1.1%. Roughly 5.4 points of the top-line increase comes from price/mix, FX translation and financial-services revenue rather than units, against a 116bp margin contraction. The ¥54tn revenue guide is not evidence of commercial momentum.

The ¥510bn Iran-war charge equals 15% of guided FY2027 operating income — an unusually large single-event concentration, and one that has already moved ¥160bn in a single quarter, in both directions.

**Balance sheet and capital allocation.** No audited balance-sheet data was obtainable this cycle. The only quantified claim available is Macquarie's estimate of ¥15tn net cash, relayed by Reuters, alongside the observation that the stock trades below book value. Both are analyst statements, and the net-cash figure almost certainly refers to the industrial balance sheet excluding Toyota Financial Services, whose finance-receivable funding dominates consolidated gross debt. No verified total debt, equity, operating cash flow, capex or free cash flow figure exists in this analysis. That opacity is the reason financial health scores 6.5 rather than 7.5 despite substantial apparent capacity: the company funded a ¥1tn authorization and a $3.6bn US capex program simultaneously, which is consistent with strength, but coverage cannot be verified.

The buyback arithmetic warrants correction. The ¥1tn authorization was described as "equivalent to as much as 4.22% of outstanding stock," plus cancellation of 200 million shares. Working from the FY2020 20-F cover count of 2,766,153,000 shares, adjusted for the October 2021 five-for-one split and reduced by subsequent repurchase, we assume ~13.0bn shares outstanding (unverified, and the weakest link below). At $191.11 per ADS, a 1:10 ADR ratio and ¥157/$, the implied ordinary price is ~¥3,000 and market capitalization ~¥39.0tn (~$248bn). On that basis ¥1tn is **2.56% of market capitalization**, not 4.22%. The 4.22% share-count cap would require ~549m shares at ¥1,822 apiece, roughly 39% below the current price. The yen value cap binds first. That reconciles with Macquarie's on-the-record disappointment — the ¥15tn net cash and sub-book valuation "had created expectations of bigger shareholder returns" — and with the shares closing down 1.5% on the print.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth thesis in the current data set, only a currency thesis and a hybrid cost thesis.

Volume: units guided +1.1% for FY2027 after Toyota and Lexus combined delivered 5.01 million units in H1 calendar 2026, down 2.9% — the first half-year decline in two years. (A separate July 31 report cites 5.39 million for the same period; the gap most plausibly reflects inclusion of Daihatsu and Hino, and neither figure should be treated as definitive.)

Regional: China sales fell 28% in the fiscal quarter; Middle East fell about one-third; the US, Toyota's largest market, grew just 1%, lagging Ford, GM and Stellantis, which are benefiting from high-margin pickup demand. GM and Ford both raised full-year guidance in the same window, so Toyota's raise is not differentiating.

Estimate revisions are the sharpest negative signal in the file:

| Period | Current | 30d ago | 90d ago | 30d rev. | 90d rev. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current quarter | $2.73 | $4.19 | $5.12 | −34.7% | −46.5% |
| Next quarter | $3.23 | $4.66 | $5.74 | −30.7% | −43.7% |
| Current year | $16.91 | $17.51 | $22.16 | −3.4% | −23.7% |
| Next year | $20.50 | $25.31 | $24.87 | −19.0% | −17.6% |

Coverage is thin (one to three analysts on the ADR), which inflates the noise in any single line, but the direction is uniform across four periods and both windows, and the 30-day next-year cut of 19% came *after* a guidance raise. Falling estimates into a rising price is the configuration that most reliably precedes multiple compression.

The one genuine self-funded growth lever is the hybrid cost curve. At ~5 million units, per-vehicle savings of ¥30,000–50,000 from the NiMH-to-lithium-ion transition would be a ¥150–250bn annual gross benefit against ¥3.4tn of operating income. Management did not attach a total value, the phrase "several tens of thousands of yen" is imprecise, the transition requires capex, and savings phase in with capacity over years. Treat that range as a sensitivity, not a forecast — but it is the only identified earnings driver that is independent of the yen.

Growth prospects score 3.5, down from 4.0. Operating income −10%, net income −15.6%, margin −116bp, units +1.1%, estimates falling 18–47% depending on horizon.

## Valuation Assessment

All multiples below inherit the ~13.0bn share-count assumption and the 1:10 ADR ratio.

- Market capitalization ≈ ¥39.0tn (~$248bn) at $191.11 and ¥157/$
- Trailing P/E on FY2026 net income (¥3.85tn): **10.1x**
- Forward P/E on guided FY2027 net income (¥3.25tn): **12.0x**
- P/E on consensus current-year ADR EPS $16.91: **11.3x**
- P/E on consensus next-year ADR EPS $20.50: **9.3x**
- Dividend yield: **2.97%**; buyback ≈ 2.6% of market cap; combined shareholder yield ≈ **5.6%**
- Price/book: approximately 1.0x on Macquarie's below-book observation (unverified)
- Macquarie's ¥15tn net cash ≈ 38% of implied market capitalization (unverified)

The critical observation is what the "cheap" next-year multiple contains. Consensus next-year ADR EPS of $20.50 implies net income near ¥4.18tn — roughly 29% above the ¥3.25tn Toyota just guided for FY2027. The 9.3x is therefore not a multiple on current earnings power; it is a multiple on an assumed FY2028 recovery that has already been cut 19% in 30 days. Consensus current-year EPS of $16.91 sits about 6% above the guidance-implied ~$15.9, so even the near multiple carries some optimism.

Peer context: GM and Ford both raised guidance in the same window and trade in a comparable high-single-digit to low-double-digit forward range. Toyota's traditional premium to Detroit — earned on volume leadership, hybrid mix and balance-sheet strength — has largely compressed, which is itself informative about how the market is discounting the yen dependence.

Analyst targets are mean $231.58, median $233.50, high $239.31, low $220.00 — every published target above spot, implying 15–25% upside from four analysts. We treat this set as stale rather than as an independent floor. Targets function as floors when a stock is cheap against *rising* estimates; here estimates are falling across every horizon, and target revisions typically follow estimate revisions with a lag. The gap between a $220 low target and a $191 price with next-year EPS cut 19% in a month is more likely to close downward than upward.

Valuation scores 4.8. The absolute multiple is undemanding and the ~5.6% shareholder yield provides real support, but the cheapness is a function of a falling denominator and lacks a catalyst independent of the currency. There is no identified specific mispricing — the market is discounting exactly what the disclosures show: currency-manufactured margin resilience, a knowingly incomplete guide, and a capital return that management chose to keep modest relative to claimed net cash.

## Competitive Landscape

Toyota's competitive position is high in level and negative in trajectory, which is the configuration that matters.

**What is working.** The hybrid franchise is being validated in the US, India, Thailand and Europe, where higher fuel prices from the Iran war are pushing buyers toward hybrids rather than BEVs. Toyota's supply-chain execution is genuinely differentiated: it rerouted Middle East exports overland around a Strait of Hormuz that is now effectively closed, cutting expected regional export disruption from an initial 50% full-year estimate to 25% from September. The $3.6bn San Antonio second assembly line — relocating Tacoma production from Mexico to Texas over four years and making San Antonio the exclusive US home of Tundra, Sequoia and Tacoma — is a pre-emptive tariff hedge announced against a White House statement that USMCA would not be renewed.

**What is deteriorating.** In China, the same fuel-price shock accelerates substitution toward domestic BEV brands, away from Toyota. Toyota's China sales fell 28% in the fiscal quarter against a total market down 20.2% in H1 2026 (CPCA, which cut its 2026 forecast to −14%). Toyota's own H1 calendar decline of 17% was shallower than the market's 20.2%, so the picture is genuinely mixed across windows — but the most recent quarter is materially worse than the market, consistent with accelerating share loss. These are non-comparable series and we do not treat any share conclusion as established.

The larger competitive consequence sits outside China. Chinese overcapacity is being exported into Toyota's profitable third markets: BYD's founder stated in July that BYD intends to overtake Toyota as the world's largest carmaker without entering the US, and in Australia — a market Toyota has dominated for decades — BYD sold only 243 fewer vehicles than Toyota in a reported period. That structure partially insulates near-term US-concentrated profit while eroding the volume base that funds Toyota's cost advantage.

On BEVs, the reported cancellation of the next-generation Lexus LF-ZC (Yomiuri, Nikkei Asia, Automotive News; company-unconfirmed) is one instance of an industry-wide retrenchment on weakening demand and fading US subsidies, with the underlying platform, battery and gigacasting work reportedly retained and a successor SUV green-lit. The relative competitive penalty is limited because peers are retreating too — but that also means the hybrid window Toyota is exploiting is one every incumbent is now crowding into.

Competitive position scores 5.5, down from 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Yen assumption (most mechanically identifiable).** Guidance assumes ¥160/$ for the full year; spot was ~157 on the print date after the joint US-Japan yen-buying intervention in late July, which established the yen level as a policy variable. At an assumed sensitivity of roughly ¥50bn of operating income per ¥1 of depreciation (unverified), the 150-to-160 assumption change alone is worth ~¥500bn — more than the ¥400bn raise. A sustained move back toward 145–150 removes most of it. Working the same arithmetic forward: the ~¥660bn of identified positives (FX plus the ¥160bn Iran revision) exceed the ¥400bn raise, implying roughly ¥250–260bn of offsetting deterioration elsewhere in China, volume, tariffs and costs. Treat that as a directional sensitivity, not a bridge.

**Guidance is knowingly incomplete.** The July 28 Kumamoto earthquake halted Kyushu-region and Tahara production and is explicitly excluded. No party — including Toyota — has quantified it.

**Trade policy.** Toyota's own yen tariff-cost disclosure was not obtainable. A tier-3 aggregation claiming ~$28bn of tariff costs absorbed across Japan's automakers with Toyota worst affected is unconfirmed and should not be relied on. USMCA non-renewal would be a first-order risk to a North American footprint that San Antonio only partly hedges.

**Iran war.** ¥510bn, or 15% of guided operating income, with the estimate having already moved ¥160bn in one quarter. Mitigation is working; the exposure is live and volatile in both directions.

**Governance.** The Toyota Industries take-private closed with Toyota Fudosan becoming parent in March 2026, following a ¥4.7tn (~$33bn) transaction at a ¥16,300 tender price backed by a vehicle in which Toyota Motor and chairman Akio Toyoda were co-investors. Toyota Industries' shares fell as much as 13% on announcement; global investors publicly criticized the valuation as "opaque" in October 2025; antitrust delays pushed the tender; Elliott built a ~5% stake in opposition. Toyota Industries reportedly held 9.1% of Toyota Motor. Two readings coexist — cross-shareholding simplification consistent with Japanese governance reform, or consolidation of group control at a contested price — and the evidence establishes contested pricing rather than intent. We could not verify the final price, Toyota Motor's cash contribution, the disposition of the 9.1% stake, or any gain recognized. If Toyota Motor deployed material cash here, it competed directly with a buyback analysts already called too small; that is a hypothesis, not a finding.

**Evidence opacity as its own risk.** The 20-F was unread; there is no Form 4 tape, no DEF 14A, and no usable XBRL series. Altman Z and Piotroski F are not computable, and would be poor tools regardless because consolidated leverage and working-capital ratios are dominated by finance-receivable funding at Toyota Financial Services. Absence of insider data is neither positive nor neutral — it is absence.

**ADR currency mechanics.** A stronger yen raises the USD translation of a given yen share price while reducing Toyota's yen earnings. For an exporter with disclosed FX-driven operating leverage of this magnitude, the earnings effect has historically dominated, so yen strength is net negative for the ADR rather than offsetting.

Risk factors score 4.5.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 22.9% sits below one-year realized volatility of 26.3% (spread −3.4%) at the 11th percentile of the current chain, with a normal contango term structure rising to 27.6% at six months. The market is pricing no near-term event — implied move on the nearest expiry is ±1.9%. Two details cut against complacency. Put/call open interest rose from 1.17 to 1.77 while the price gained 7.2% between snapshots, and put/call skew is 5.3% with elevated put demand noted; protection accumulated into the rally rather than against a decline. Short interest is negligible at 0.1% of float and 1.8 days to cover, so there is no crowded-short mechanic in either direction. For holders, cheap six-month protection at 27.6% IV against a stock whose guidance hinges on a currency assumption already contradicted by spot is the most efficient way to hold the position without selling it. (The 32.67 three-month put/call volume ratio reflects thin volume and should not be read directionally.)

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $232 (+21% from $191.11)

The yen resumes weakening past 160, delivering the ¥500bn-plus of FX operating profit that guidance assumes and more. Hormuz transit normalizes, the ¥510bn Iran charge falls further from its already-reduced level, and Middle East volume recovers from a one-third decline. The hybrid cost-down program delivers toward the upper end of a ¥150–250bn annual benefit as lithium-ion capacity phases in, and 5 million hybrid units at 52% of mix hold pricing in the US, India, Thailand and Europe. Management follows the ¥1tn authorization with a second tranche, moving shareholder yield above 7% and forcing the sub-book valuation to close. On a recovered ~$18.50 of ADR EPS at 12.5x, the stock reaches $232 — the low end of the published analyst range.

### Bear Case — $152 (−20% from $191.11)

Joint intervention and a BOJ hike hold the yen at 148–152, removing most of the ¥400bn guidance raise and taking FY2027 operating income back toward ¥3.0tn. The Kumamoto earthquake lands as a quantified charge in the September quarter. USMCA is not renewed, forcing a further unbudgeted North American reconfiguration on top of San Antonio. China volume continues falling faster than a market already guided to −14%, and Chinese overcapacity compresses pricing in Australia, Southeast Asia and Europe. Consensus next-year ADR EPS falls from $20.50 toward $16–17 as the assumed 29% FY2028 recovery is written down, and published targets follow. At 9.5x $16, the ADR trades to $152, still above the June low of $166.10 only if the yen stays near 157.

### Base Case — $195 (+2% price, ~5% total return)

The yen settles in a 155–160 range, delivering most but not all of the guidance raise. The earthquake produces a modest, absorbable charge. Hybrid cost-down and Middle East rerouting offset continued China erosion and flat-to-slightly-up US volume. FY2027 net income lands near the ¥3.25tn guide, and consensus current-year EPS converges down from $16.91 toward $16.00–16.50. At 11.5x a normalized ~$17 of ADR earnings power, roughly 1.0x book, supported by a 5.6% shareholder yield, fair value is $195. Probability-weighting bear 30% / base 45% / bull 25% gives roughly flat price return plus a 3.0% dividend — approximately 3% total expected return over twelve months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: REDUCE.** Expected return of roughly 3% including the dividend, against 20% bear-case downside and 21% bull-case upside, is below fair compensation for the identified risks. The asymmetry has worsened since our July review specifically because the price rose 8.2% while every consensus horizon was cut.

**De-risking plan.** Trim on strength rather than at any single date. The ADR has recovered 15% off the June 25 low of $166.10 and sits 23% below the February 13 high of $248.90; the $190–200 band is where we would reduce. Holders unwilling to sell should buy six-month protection while IV sits at 27.6% against 26.3% realized and 22.9% near-term — the cheapest expression of the yen risk. We would retain no more than a half-weight position into the November quarter report, which is the first print that must absorb both the Kumamoto charge and any post-intervention yen level.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD (any two):**
1. Operating income growth turns positive year over year — ending the five-quarter decline streak — with margin expansion ex-FX rather than the disclosed FX contribution.
2. Consensus next-year ADR EPS stabilizes for two consecutive months at or above $20.00 (currently $20.50 after a 19% 30-day cut).
3. A second buyback authorization taking cumulative FY2027 repurchase above 4% of market capitalization, or verified disclosure of industrial net cash near ¥15tn with a stated payout framework.
4. Price at or below $165 (approximately 9.7x consensus current-year $16.91 and near the June low), which would restore adequate margin of safety on unchanged fundamentals.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE:** the above plus verified evidence that China volume decline has narrowed to at or better than the market rate for two consecutive quarters, and a yen assumption that guidance no longer depends on.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. FY2027 operating income guidance cut back to ¥3.0tn or below on a stronger yen, resuming the May sequence.
2. Kumamoto earthquake or tariff charges quantified above ¥300bn combined without offsetting guidance.
3. USMCA non-renewal confirmed with an announced North American production reconfiguration not covered by existing capex.
4. Any disclosure that Toyota Motor's cash contribution to the Toyota Industries transaction materially reduced the capital available for shareholder return, or new governance escalation involving minority shareholders.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoint is the fiscal Q2 FY2027 report in early November 2026, which must quantify the earthquake and mark guidance to the post-intervention yen. Intermediate monitorables: monthly China and US sales, the ¥/$ level relative to the 160 assumption, and Hormuz transit status, where 72-plus hours of sustained uninterrupted commercial traffic would materially reduce the ¥510bn charge.

## Conclusion

The August 4 print gave the market a revenue beat, a 42% net income beat and a 13% guidance raise, and the ADR responded by holding an 8% gain from late July. The composition of those three items is where the investment case fails. The net income beat came from "other income and a much weaker yen" by the company's own attribution, against a 5th consecutive quarterly operating-profit decline and a 160bp margin contraction. Removing the disclosed ¥345bn currency benefit puts ex-FX operating profit down roughly 38%. The raise rests on a yen assumption of 160 that spot already contradicts at 157 after coordinated intervention, and on a logistics improvement that is real but leaves ¥510bn — 15% of guided operating income — still charged against the year. The earthquake is excluded. And after all of it, guidance sits 10% below FY2026 actual.

Valuation offers support without offering a catalyst. At ~11.3x consensus current-year EPS, roughly 1.0x book, with a 2.97% dividend and a buyback worth ~2.6% of market capitalization (not the headline 4.22% — the yen value cap binds well before the share-count cap), Toyota is inexpensive. But the 9.3x "next year" multiple rests on a 29% earnings recovery above the company's own guide, and that number has already been cut 19% in thirty days. Every published analyst target sits above spot; with estimates falling across all four horizons, those targets are more likely to be revised down than met.

Score of 4.9, down from 5.2 in July and 7.4 in February, with growth prospects (3.5), risk factors (4.5) and management quality (4.8) doing the damage while financial health (6.5) holds on unverified but substantial balance-sheet capacity. Four prior HOLDs raised the bar for a fifth; the August data cleared it in the wrong direction. **REDUCE.** Trim into the $190–200 band, hedge what remains with cheap six-month volatility, and revisit at $165 or on two consecutive quarters of ex-FX operating improvement.
