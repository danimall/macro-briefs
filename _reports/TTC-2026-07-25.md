**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

The Toro Company has moved through the low point of a two-year soft patch. FY2025 was ugly — revenue fell ~1.6%, net income dropped ~24.5%, and an $81.1M non-cash Spartan trade-name impairment layered onto a Residential segment that collapsed 14% for the year with EBIT margin compressing to roughly 4.2%. The H1 FY2026 data reverses that story: net sales grew 6.4%, Q2 revenue rose 8.1%, adjusted EPS climbed 13.1% for the half, and Residential EBIT margin nearly doubled from 5.4% to 9.8% year over year. Free cash flow conversion jumped from 45% to 124% as inventory normalized (down 17.5% YoY). Management raised FY2026 guidance to 4.0%–6.5% sales growth and $4.50–$4.62 adjusted EPS on the June 4 print. This is a genuine operating inflection, and a trailing financial-health screen built on FY2025 understates where the business is today.

The problem is price. TTC has already re-rated ~25.5% over the trailing year and sits at ~$93.76, near the mid-point of its $67.64–$105.19 range, at roughly 26x trailing and ~20x forward EPS. That forward multiple sits well below the peer median (Deere 32x, CNH 33x, Lindsay 21x), but the peer set is heterogeneous and skewed by Deere/CNH cyclical-trough optics; on a more relevant normalized basis Toro is fairly valued for a company delivering mid-single-digit organic growth with a mid-teens operating margin. Estimate revisions are flat (current-year +1.6% over 90 days, next-year −0.3%), the revision-velocity signal that flags true inflection buys is absent, and analyst targets ($100–$120, mean $109) imply meaningful but not exceptional upside from here.

We rate TTC a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The operational turnaround is real and the Professional franchise (78% of sales, 20.3% EBIT margin) is high quality, but the setup is balanced: the recovery is largely priced, revision momentum has stalled, and the management picture (an orderly CEO succession, but persistent TSR underperformance versus peers at ~98 vs. ~174 and no open-market insider buying) offers no positive catalyst. This is our fourth consecutive HOLD; the new data confirms the operational thesis but does not clear the bar for an upgrade given the entry price.

## Company Overview

Toro is a Bloomington, MN–based global manufacturer of outdoor equipment, reporting on an October 31 fiscal year across two primary segments plus a small "Other."

**Professional (~78% of sales, the profit engine):** Golf course and sports-field turf equipment, turf and agricultural irrigation, landscape equipment, snow and ice management, and increasingly central to the growth story, rental, specialty, and underground construction equipment (Ditch Witch directional drills and trenchers, plus hydrovac excavation via the Tornado acquisition). Q2 FY2026 EBIT margin was 20.3%. The strategic pivot toward underground and infrastructure equipment aligns Toro with multi-year spending on broadband/fiber, data centers, grid modernization, and energy transmission, end markets management explicitly names and which are less exposed to golf and consumer cyclicality.

**Residential (~22% of sales):** Walk and riding mowers, zero-turn mowers, snow throwers, and yard products sold through mass retailers (Home Depot), dealers, and online. This is the lower-margin, weather- and consumer-sensitive business that broke the FY2025 thesis and is now healing.

Toro's competitive moat runs through an extensive dealer/distributor network plus the Red Iron floor-plan financing joint venture (45% Toro / 55% Huntington), which finances roughly $1.4B/yr of dealer inventory and locks in channel loyalty. Key competitors are Deere and Textron/Jacobsen in turf, Vermeer in underground, and Husqvarna/Stihl/Honda in residential.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** H1 FY2026 reaccelerated to $2,461.0M, with Q2 at $1,424.7M. Growth came from net price realization, higher Professional shipments (underground construction, zero-turn mowers, snow), and the Tornado acquisition (~$29.7M Q2 / $48.7M H1 contribution). Stripping out Tornado, organic growth is in the mid-single digits.

**Margins and earnings.** Q2 gross margin was 33.9% (+80 bps YoY), adjusted 34.5%. H1 operating margin was 11.5% (adjusted 12.5%). Q2 net earnings were $145.4M ($1.50 diluted EPS) versus $136.8M ($1.37); adjusted EPS was $1.60 versus $1.42 (+12.7%). H1 adjusted EPS reached $2.33 versus $2.06. The single most important line is the Residential EBIT margin recovery, driven by price, AMP productivity savings, and the non-recurrence of prior-year inventory valuation charges.

**Cash flow.** H1 operating cash flow was $293.5M versus $123.1M a year ago; free cash flow was $265.5M versus $84.7M — a 124.5% conversion rate against 44.7% prior year. The improvement is primarily working-capital-driven: inventory fell $196.4M as channel destocking from FY2025 reversed, plus payables timing. Some of this benefit is non-recurring — inventory can only normalize once — so we do not extrapolate 124% conversion forward, but it confirms the earnings are cash-backed.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt is $1,016.8M (up from $921.5M at FY-end, reflecting $210M drawn for Tornado). Cash is $180.4M; available liquidity $983.3M. Maturities are well-laddered and largely fixed-rate ($724.3M fixed), and interest expense is falling ($14.8M Q2 vs. $15.8M). Leverage is comfortable for a business with this cash generation; this is not a balance-sheet-risk story. The financial-health screens that flagged weakness are lagging the FY2025 impairment-driven print rather than describing current fundamentals, a reason we hold the Financial Health score at 6.8 rather than letting a trailing screen depress it.

**Earnings track record.** Four consecutive beats: +5.6% (Q2 FY26), +13.1% (Q1 FY26), +3.6%, +0.8%. A consistent, modest-beat cadence supports confidence in the current guide, which the raised guidance now brackets.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three legs, each with a caveat.

First, the **Residential recovery** is the highest-quality near-term driver. A segment that fell 14% in FY2025 has nearly doubled its EBIT margin in a year on price realization plus AMP savings. If sustainable, this de-risks the biggest FY2025 negative. The caveat is that part of the margin recovery is the non-recurrence of prior-year inventory charges, a one-time reset rather than a permanent step-change in earning power.

Second, the **underground/infrastructure pivot** via Tornado ($210.3M, closed December 8, 2025) levers Toro into secular tailwinds: data centers, grid, fiber. This is a higher-quality growth narrative than golf/consumer durables, and it slots alongside the existing Ditch Witch franchise. The caveat is Toro's mixed M&A record: the Spartan write-down shows integration and synergy realization are not automatic, and Tornado's purchase-price allocation (~$138M goodwill + $63.8M intangibles) is still preliminary.

Third, the **AMP productivity initiative** targets $125M run-rate savings by FY2027 (raised from $100M), with $106.8M cumulative delivered through Q2 — ahead of pace. This underpins the margin algorithm.

Against these, estimate revisions are flat. Current-quarter EPS is unchanged over 30 days ($1.30), current-year up only 1.6% over 90 days ($4.61), and next-year down 0.3% ($5.07). Next-quarter estimates were cut 6.6% over 90 days. This is not the kind of revision velocity that marks a true inflection buy; consensus has quietly acknowledged the beat-and-raise without extrapolating acceleration. The growth is real and durable at a mid-single-digit organic rate, but it is not accelerating in a way that suggests the market is underestimating the trajectory.

Applying the calibration lens: growth_prospects is a directionally useful but recently thin signal, and Industrials as a group carries negative alpha by default, so a positive raw print here is borrowed beta, not selection skill. We score growth 5.8: positive trajectory, confirmed by the print, but not a standalone edge without accelerating revisions.

## Valuation Assessment

At $93.76, TTC trades at ~26x trailing EPS ($3.17) and ~20x the $4.61 current-year adjusted estimate. Against peer medians (P/E 32.2), that looks cheap, but the peer set is heterogeneous: Deere (32x) and CNH (33x) reflect large-ag-equipment cycle dynamics distinct from Toro's turf/consumer mix, AGCO (10.7x) sits at a cyclical trough, and Lindsay (21x) is the closest analog at a similar multiple. On a like-for-like basis, Toro is fairly valued at ~20x forward for mid-single-digit organic growth and a mid-teens operating margin.

Cheapness against a stretched peer median is not, by itself, a buy signal: a "cheap" screen reliably marks value traps absent an independent catalyst, and the forward multiple already embeds the recovery. The Residential margin snapback, the Tornado accretion, and the AMP savings are known and reflected in the guide the stock is priced against. There is no identified mispricing where the market is systematically wrong.

Two pieces of unbooked optionality provide modest asymmetry. First, ~$20M of potential IEEPA tariff refunds following the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling are explicitly not recognized; management says refunds should substantially offset new tariff headwinds. Second, the beat cadence suggests the guide is achievable-to-conservative. But these are second-order, worth a few percent, not a re-rating.

Analyst targets ($100 low, $120 high, $109 mean) imply 7%–28% upside. On an inflection stock at a compressed multiple, targets would be floors that lag reality; here, with flat revisions and a ~20x forward multiple that is not distressed, the targets are a reasonable range rather than a stale floor. We score valuation 4.8: not a value trap, but no asymmetric upside at the current entry.

## Competitive Landscape

Toro's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-improving, with a clear bifurcation. The Professional business is franchise-quality: strong share in professional golf/turf (against Deere and Textron/Jacobsen) and in underground construction (Ditch Witch against Vermeer), a 20.3% EBIT margin, and a distribution moat reinforced by the Red Iron captive-financing JV. Products introduced in the last three years remain a significant portion of sales, and the innovation cadence in alternative power, autonomy, and smart-connected equipment is intact.

The Residential business is structurally weaker: lower margin, more cyclical, and dependent on mass retailers like Home Depot, exposing Toro to channel destocking risk. The FY2025 collapse demonstrated how quickly the segment can deteriorate.

The trajectory argument for the score: the underground/infrastructure pivot (Ditch Witch + Tornado) is genuinely improving the mix toward higher-quality, less-cyclical end markets. Prior reports scored competitive position as high as 7.0; it has drifted to 6.3 as the market has recognized that the moat, while real, is not widening dramatically and that the Residential exposure caps the quality of the overall franchise. We hold 6.3. Competitive_position has recently been a mildly anti-predictive dimension in our framework, so we do not lean on it as a differentiating signal; it is context, confirmed by financial health.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks are moderate and largely priced.

- **Residential cyclicality and channel inventory swings.** The Red Iron JV and inventory-repurchase obligations ($29.8M contingent) tie Toro to dealer financial health. A consumer air-pocket would hit this segment first.
- **Tariffs — two-sided.** U.S./Mexico manufacturing with commodity exposure to steel, aluminum, engines (some China-sourced, subject to AD/CVD), and resins. The SCOTUS IEEPA ruling created the unbooked refund optionality; new Q2 tariff-rule changes create offsetting cost. Management expects refunds to substantially offset new headwinds. Genuine two-sided uncertainty.
- **Golf/municipal budget concentration.** A large slice of Professional revenue depends on golf activity/renovation and municipal/infrastructure spending, both macro- and government-budget-sensitive.
- **Tornado integration.** Preliminary purchase accounting; the Spartan write-down is a reminder the M&A record is imperfect.
- **AMP execution.** The margin algorithm increasingly depends on delivering the $125M run-rate savings, though delivery is ahead of pace.
- **Governance.** Persistent TSR underperformance raises comp-alignment questions, and there are no offsetting open-market insider buys.

None of these is a binary event with imminent asymmetric downside. The 10-Q states no material change to the 10-K risk factors. We score risk_factors 6.0: moderate, priced, with the tariff refund providing a small positive skew. Risk_factors is a low-signal dimension in our framework, so we do not over-weight it.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is complacent, not defensive. Near-term ATM IV is 30.2% versus 25.2% historical volatility — a modest 5.0-point rich spread, but at only the 12th percentile within the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced (1.9%), and the term structure is in normal contango (30.2% near, 34.6% at six months). ATM IV has collapsed from 46.9% to 30.2% since June 1 as the Q2 earnings binary resolved. The 6.6% implied move for the nearest expiry reflects post-earnings calm. There is no elevated put demand challenging a constructive fundamental view, and no unusual positioning signaling an informed bearish bet. This confirms the balanced, no-imminent-catalyst read.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The Residential recovery proves durable (9.8% margin holds and expands toward historical highs), AMP delivers the full run-rate savings, Tornado integrates cleanly and levers Toro into data-center/grid/fiber spending, and the tariff refund lands as a one-time tailwind. Adjusted EPS pushes toward $5.00+ in FY2027, and the multiple holds at ~22x, driving the stock toward $110–$120. Consistent capital return (H1 returned $360.9M via buyback and dividend, share count down from ~100.6M to ~96.6M) compounds per-share value.

### Bear Case
Residential demand weakens, forcing renewed channel destocking and reversing the margin recovery. Golf/municipal budgets tighten, Professional growth decelerates, Tornado underdelivers like Spartan, and tariff costs outrun the unbooked refund. Estimates roll over, and the ~20x forward multiple compresses toward the low end of the peer range. The stock retraces toward the low-$70s, near its 52-week low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The operating turnaround is confirmed and the Professional franchise is high quality, but the recovery is priced, revisions have stalled, and there is no positive management catalyst.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 FY2026 print (early September 2026), which tests whether the Residential margin recovery holds into the seasonally strong shipping quarter, followed by the CEO transition (Funk → CEO, November 1, 2026).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-year or next-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive by +5% or more, signaling the market is beginning to underestimate the trajectory.
- A pullback toward the low-$80s, which would compress forward P/E toward ~17x and restore asymmetric upside against the $109 mean target.
- Residential EBIT margin sustains above 9% for two consecutive quarters, confirming the recovery is structural rather than a one-time inventory-charge reversal.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q3 sales growth decelerates below 2% YoY or Residential margin reverses back below 6%.
- Management cuts the FY2026 guide, or Tornado shows early impairment/integration signs.
- FCF conversion collapses back toward FY2025 levels alongside rising channel inventory (a destocking signal).

## Conclusion

Toro has done the hard work of turning around the business that broke its FY2025 thesis. Residential margins have nearly doubled, free cash flow conversion has surged, organic growth has reaccelerated to the mid-single digits, and management raised guidance on the back of four straight beats. The financial health here is genuinely better than a trailing screen suggests, and the underground/infrastructure pivot gives the growth story a higher-quality shape.

An improving business and an attractive stock are not the same thing, however. At ~$93.76, up over the year and near the middle of its 52-week range, TTC trades at ~20x forward earnings that already embed the recovery, with estimate revisions that have gone flat rather than accelerating. The management setup is orderly but uninspiring: a clean succession, persistent TSR underperformance versus peers, and no open-market insider buying to signal conviction. The options market is complacent, confirming no imminent catalyst in either direction.

We maintain HOLD with an overall score of 5.8. This is a well-run cyclical industrial at a fair price, not a mispriced one. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-$80s or on a positive revision inflection; we would step aside on evidence the Residential recovery is a one-time inventory reset rather than a structural gain.
