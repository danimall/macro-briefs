## Executive Summary

DXP Enterprises has produced one of the better five-year total-return records in industrial distribution: the proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows $100 invested at the end of 2020 growing to $487 by year-end 2025 against $157 for its peer composite. That record rests on three things: 304 basis points of gross-margin expansion (28.50% in 2022 to 31.54% in 2025), a 15.2% reduction in diluted shares since 2022, and a substantial multiple re-rating. The first two are durable achievements. The third is not repeatable from here.

The Q1 2026 print is where the model's tension becomes visible in the numbers. Consolidated revenue rose 9.5% to $521.7M, but acquisitions owned less than a year contributed $40.7M, implying organic growth of roughly 1% ($481.0M versus $476.4M). Adjusted EBITDA rose 10.1% to $57.8M; operating income rose about 5% to $42.5M; GAAP net income *fell* 2.9% to $20.0M and diluted EPS declined to $1.22 from $1.25. The wedge is the amortization and interest that the acquisitions themselves create. Service Centers, 65% of sales, grew 3.3% year over year and declined 5.1% sequentially, and the CFO acknowledged that energy-related backlog had been falling in the third and fourth quarters of fiscal 2025 before stabilizing at +2.1% sequentially in Q1.

At $167.40 the equity carries a 31.3x multiple on TTM diluted EPS of $5.34, 27.3x the current-year consensus of $6.14, and 14.0x EV/TTM adjusted EBITDA of $230.6M (a multiple range where lightly levered, dividend-paying industrial distributors trade), while DXP carries 2.74x net leverage, a non-investment-grade rating, negative tangible book equity of roughly −$78M, and generated only $54.0M of FY2025 free cash flow (a 2.1% yield). The two covering analysts have targets of $157 and $160, both below spot. That combination does not make DXP a short; the water franchise is real, covenant headroom is wide, and next-year consensus of $7.51 implies a defensible 22.3x. It does mean the burden of proof now sits with the August Q2 print rather than with the multiple.

## Company Overview

DXP is a Houston-based distributor of maintenance, repair and operating (MRO) and OEM products, combined with a fabrication/engineered-systems business and an outsourced-procurement business, operating in the U.S., Canada, Mexico and selected international markets (the proxy's pay-ratio methodology confirms employees in the UAE, India and Saudi Arabia).

**Service Centers (SC)** — approximately 65% of Q1 2026 sales — distributes rotating equipment, bearings and power transmission, industrial supplies, safety products, metal working and air compressors, plus repair and rebuild services.

**Innovative Pumping Solutions (IPS)** — $111.7M, or 21.4% of reported Q1 2026 revenue — builds custom-engineered pump packages, skids and modular process equipment. Within IPS sits **DXP Water**, which management said was 66% of IPS sales in Q1 2026 and had just recorded a fourteenth consecutive quarter of sequential growth. Water is therefore roughly $73.7M, or about 14% of consolidated quarterly sales.

**Supply Chain Services (SCS)** — approximately 12% of sales — provides integrated/on-site MRO procurement and inventory management.

Two structural features matter more than the segment map. First, DXP is a programmatic acquirer: six deals for $61.7M in FY2025, then three deals (Mid Atlantic Storage Systems, PREMIERflow, Ambiente H2O) for $126.3M of consideration in Q1 2026 alone, with all goodwill assigned to IPS. A further water/wastewater service acquisition (General Repair Service) was reported in mid-June via press release; I have not verified terms against a filing. Management described three signed LOIs and two more approaching LOI on the Q1 call. Second, the model is deliberately low-inventory: FY2025 inventories of $108.1M against $1,380.4M of cost of sales imply roughly 12.8 turns and only 5.4% of revenue, versus receivables at 19.7% of revenue. DXP is closer to a technical/engineered distributor with service labor and project fabrication than to a broad-line stocker.

No single customer exceeded 10% of FY2025 revenue. That is genuine low customer concentration, and it should be kept analytically separate from end-market concentration: roughly two-thirds of sales still sit in a cyclical Service Center business.

The gross-margin expansion is the strongest line in the model: 304bp over three years is roughly $61M of incremental gross profit on FY2025 revenue. But SG&A intensity rose about 90bp between 2023 and 2025, absorbing roughly a third of that gain, so operating margin expanded only 51bp over the same period. The acquired businesses appear to arrive with both higher gross margins and heavier operating cost structures, which is what you would expect from services and engineered content rather than resale.

One accounting nuance materially affects how FY2025 reads. Effective tax rates were 27.0% (2022), 20.8% (2023), 17.0% (2024) and 25.6% (2025). FY2024's depressed rate flattered that year, so FY2025 pre-tax income grew 40.3% while net income grew only 25.8%. Anchoring on reported net income understates the FY2025 operating improvement; extrapolating FY2024's tax rate would overstate future EPS.

### Cash conversion — the weakest link

FY2025: operating cash flow $94.3M, capex $40.3M, free cash flow $54.0M against net income of $88.7M. OCF/net income of 1.06x is poor for a business carrying depreciation, a materially larger acquisition-driven amortization charge, and stock compensation; non-cash add-backs alone should have pushed OCF well above earnings. Receivables rose 17.1% to $397.5M against revenue growth of 11.9%, with DSO extending from roughly 68.7 to 71.9 days. A portion of that balance-sheet increase arrived with acquired companies (recorded in investing, not operating, cash flow), so the organic working-capital drag is smaller than the raw delta; the direction is established, the precise organic magnitude is not.

Q1 2026 OCF was $29.8M and FCF $26.3M, but this should not be annualized: Q1 capex of $3.3M was below 1% of sales and at maintenance levels after a heavier 2025 investment year, seasonal payroll-tax and insurance timing helps Q1, and working capital rose $17.9M sequentially to $379.6M (18.4% of sales). As a labeled sensitivity rather than a forecast: if FY2026 capex reverts to roughly 1.5% of sales (~$33M) and working-capital growth halves, FY2026 FCF of $80-110M is plausible, lifting the yield to 3.1-4.2%. That sensitivity, not the FY2025 actual, is the bull case's cash-flow foundation.

Ratings are non-investment-grade (B1 area). Floating-rate leverage at 34% of operating income is a direct earnings headwind rather than an abstraction.

An Altman Z-score on FY2025 inputs computes to approximately 3.70 (nominally safe), but 1.31 points come from the market-value-of-equity term, so the comfort partly reflects the current share price; at $100 the same balance sheet yields roughly 3.18. Solvency is not the near-term issue; the point is that the screen would compress precisely when it matters.

Two beats and two misses in the last four quarters, with Q1 2026 missing by 15.2% ($1.22 versus $1.44) and Q3 2025 by 10.8%. That record argues for treating single-analyst consensus with caution rather than confidence.

## Growth Analysis

The headline growth is real but its composition has shifted decisively toward acquisition. IPS grew 37.7% and carries the water franchise; Service Centers grew 3.3% and fell 5.1% sequentially; SCS grew 2.7%.

Management's monthly average-daily-sales disclosure was unusually candid: $7.2M/day in January, $8.4M in February, $9.2M in March, $9.0M in April with April up 15% year over year. The CEO said "we are not sure why sales in January were so soft," across water, oil and gas, and general industry. That statement is informative about forward visibility in the Service Center business.

The 90-day estimate revision trend is negative — current year −9.2%, next year −3.8% — while the 30-day trend is modestly positive (+0.3% and +3.6%). Read together: estimates were cut materially after the Q1 miss and have since stabilized and ticked up. The share price has risen while the 90-day revision direction was down, which is the opposite of the configuration that supports paying up.

The durable growth vectors are: water and wastewater infrastructure (treatment upgrades, PFAS, reuse, municipal and industrial capital spending), where fourteen consecutive quarters of sequential growth is a credible operating fact; and continued consolidation of a fragmented distribution base, where DXP's institutional M&A competence is genuine (the CFO's background includes industrial-distribution banking and corporate development, and the proxy carries a dedicated acquisition-oversight section). The constraint is scale: water is roughly one-seventh of revenue, so a 30-40% growth rate there adds only 4-6 points of consolidated growth, and it has to overcome a 65% segment growing at 3%.

Energy remains the swing factor. Backlog declined sequentially in Q3 and Q4 of fiscal 2025 and only stabilized in Q1 2026. Energy operating spend and Gulf-region project capital spending is exactly the kind of decision that gets deferred under weaker end-market conditions, and the peer table is a warning: DNOW, the closest energy-facing distribution comparable, currently shows a negative P/E, meaning GAAP losses.

## Valuation Assessment

Reproducible arithmetic at $167.40:

- Market capitalization: 15,505,312 × $167.40 = **$2,596M**. On a diluted basis (16.35M shares, including the 840,000 Series B conversion shares already in reported diluted EPS): **$2,737M**.
- Enterprise value: $2,596M + $844.7M debt − $213.4M cash = **$3,227M** (basic) / **$3,368M** (diluted).
- TTM diluted EPS: $5.37 − $1.25 + $1.22 = **$5.34** → **P/E 31.3x**.
- Forward: **27.3x** current-year consensus $6.14; **22.3x** next-year $7.51.
- **EV/TTM adjusted EBITDA 14.0x** (basic) / 14.6x (diluted); 13.2x against covenant EBITDA of $243.9M.
- FY2025 FCF yield **2.1%**; on the $80-110M FY2026 sensitivity, 3.1-4.2%.
- Dividend yield: zero. No income support.

Peer context cuts both ways. Median trailing P/E across the supplied comparable set is 31.1x — MSC Industrial at 31.1x with a 3.0% yield, Watsco at 31.5x with a 3.5% yield, Core & Main at 21.2x, and DNOW and Resideo both loss-making on a GAAP basis. DXPE at 31.3x trailing is therefore *at* the median, but the two names anchoring that median are lower-leverage, dividend-paying franchises with substantially larger scale. Core & Main — arguably the closest business-mix comparable given its water-infrastructure distribution exposure — trades at 21.2x and sits 35% below its 52-week high. Adjusting for DXP's 2.74x net leverage, negative tangible book, B1-area rating and ~1% organic growth, a median multiple is not a discount.

The one genuinely supportive frame is next-year: 22.3x on $7.51 against 22% expected earnings growth is a PEG near 1.0. That is a fair multiple, not an expensive one. But it rests on a single analyst's estimate that was cut 3.8% over 90 days, at a company whose GAAP net income declined year over year last quarter and which has missed twice in four quarters. The gap between adjusted EBITDA growth (+10.1%) and GAAP EPS growth (−2.4%) is the reason I will not underwrite $7.51 as a base case.

On analyst targets: the mean of $158.50 (high $160, low $157) sits 5.3% *below* the current price. Treating targets as lagging floors applies to inflection stocks that are cheap relative to rising estimates. Here estimates have been cut over 90 days and the stock trades above every published target. That is the opposite configuration and I read it as corroborating caution rather than as a contrarian buy signal.

**Valuation score: 4.0.** The multiple is defensible on next-year consensus and stretched on trailing GAAP and free cash flow, with no identified specific mispricing and no yield support.

## Competitive Landscape

DXP occupies a middle position: too small and too levered to compete on purchasing scale with Grainger, Fastenal or Motion, but more technically capable than regional independents and, unusually for its size, vertically integrated into engineered pump packaging and fabrication. Its nearest listed analogues on mix are Applied Industrial Technologies, and on the energy side MRC Global and DNOW; in water it brushes against Core & Main and Ferguson on distribution and Xylem-type OEMs on equipment, with DXP's role being packaging and integration rather than manufacturing.

The trajectory arguments in DXP's favor: gross margin has expanded 304bp in three years, toward the higher end of the sector, driven by category mix into services and engineered content; the water franchise is compounding sequentially through fourteen quarters; customer concentration is genuinely low; and repair/rebuild capability is something pure catalog distributors cannot replicate.

The trajectory arguments against: the core 65% of the business grew 3.3% year over year and shrank sequentially last quarter; roughly nine points of last quarter's growth was purchased rather than earned; and the pricing component of the post-2021 gross-margin expansion is the part most vulnerable to a disinflationary industrial environment, since distributors defend service margins more easily than product pass-through.

I am moderating competitive position from the prior 6.8 to **6.0**. The improvement thesis — mix shifting toward secular water demand — is intact and confirmed by segment data. What does not support a 6.8 is a business where the largest segment is flat-to-down organically and where consolidated organic growth rounds to 1%. Mix improvement and share gain are not the same thing, and the filings support the former, not the latter.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Acquisition execution and goodwill against negative tangible equity.** Q1 2026 deployed $126.3M — roughly double the entire FY2025 acquisition spend — with approximately 83% of purchase price allocated to goodwill and intangibles ($56.3M + $48.5M), all assigned to IPS. IPS segment assets jumped from roughly $383M to $517M in one quarter. Goodwill of $494.6M exceeds two-thirds of equity and tangible book is negative. No deal-level revenue or EBITDA is disclosed, so whether DXP is buying well cannot be assessed from filings; the returns depend entirely on prices paid and the durability of acquired margins, neither of which is visible.

**2. IRS challenge to research-and-development credits.** The 10-K and 10-Q disclose an examination of tax year 2018 with Notices of Proposed Adjustment issued October 2024, tied to credits from a multi-year study, with cumulative credits recognized in the tens of millions against a materially smaller uncertain-tax-position reserve. Independent corroboration of materiality: PwC's tax fees of $352,451 (2025) and $493,362 (2024) are disclosed as relating to the R&D credit study — meaning the auditor produces the study behind the contested benefit, which is permissible and was pre-approved, but concentrates the documentation. FY2023-2024 net income benefited from a position under active challenge. The 10-K and 10-Q appear to carry different cumulative figures, which I could not reconcile and therefore do not state as fact.

**3. Floating-rate leverage.** Interest expense of $60.5M consumed 34% of FY2025 operating income. Each debt-funded acquisition tightens coverage from 2.59-2.92x. The December 2025 amendment also adjusted certain financial-ratio covenant compliance dates; the 10-Q does not specify which ratios or whether the change loosened or merely re-timed testing.

**4. Energy and industrial cyclicality with a narrow growth engine.** Energy backlog fell in two consecutive quarters before stabilizing; Service Centers, 65% of sales, declined sequentially. Water is 14% of sales.

**5. Working-capital intensity.** Working capital of $379.6M equals 18.4% of sales and rose $17.9M sequentially; the CFO noted project and product delivery timelines are stretching in an already long-cycle water business. This is the mechanism that produced $54M of FCF against $176.9M of operating income.

**6. Governance and succession.** A 74-year-old founder holds Chairman, President and CEO simultaneously with no lead independent director, no disclosed successor and no CEO-transition framework. His son is COO and two sons-in-law are senior executives (combined ~$3.47M of 2025 pay); $2.3M of FY2025 lease expense went to family-controlled entities (immaterial in magnitude at ~0.1% of revenue). The board has six members, four independent, all serving on all four committees, meeting four times a year, with the longest-tenured "independent" director seated in 2001 and no new director in roughly five years. Say-on-pay support was 91%. Pledging is permitted with CFO approval up to 10% of shares outstanding in aggregate, with no current pledged amounts disclosed — a forced-selling channel in a drawdown.

**7. Contingent consideration.** ~$29.1M accrued against a ~$32.5M maximum over three years — near the cap, implying management expects acquired businesses to hit earn-outs (a positive operating read) while representing near-certain cash outflow outside the debt figure.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 74.1% sits 20.4 points above 53.7% one-year realized volatility, in the 78th percentile of the current chain, with a backwardated term structure (74.1% at 16 days versus 55.9% at 107 days) and a ±13.0% implied move on the August 21 expiry. Implied volatility rose 4.3 points (69.8% to 74.1%) even as the price rose 7.4% since July 29 — volatility normally falls into a rally, and the divergence is consistent with the market pricing a binary Q2 earnings event rather than direction. Put/call open interest of 0.64 is call-tilted, and near-term skew is described as balanced (2.3%), while the six-month tenor shows −3.7%. Short interest is only 4.4% of float at 2.1 days to cover, so there is no crowded bearish position and no squeeze fuel.

The read-through: the options market agrees this is an event-driven setup with a wide distribution, and near-term positioning is not bearish. Selling premium into the print is more attractively priced than buying direction. That supports a wait-for-data posture rather than a position.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($200, +19.5% from $167.40)

Q2 confirms that April's +15% average-daily-sales momentum held through May and June; the Q1 SG&A step-up (elevated self-insured healthcare claims, acquisition-related legal and consulting, one-time fleet and equipment costs) reverses as management indicated, pushing SG&A back toward 22.8% of sales; GAAP EPS returns to year-over-year growth. Capex normalizes to roughly 1.5% of sales and working-capital growth halves, delivering FY2026 FCF of $80-110M and a 3.1-4.2% yield that begins to justify the multiple. DXP Water compounds toward 20% of consolidated sales as the acquisition pipeline (three signed LOIs plus two near-LOI) converts, and the market continues to award a water-infrastructure multiple to a business two-thirds of which is industrial MRO. On next-year consensus of $7.51 at 26.6x, that is $200 — matching the reported external bull-case target.

### Bear Case ($118, −29.5%)

Organic growth stays near zero as Service Centers remain flat-to-down and energy backlog resumes sequential decline; acquisition amortization and interest continue to consume EBITDA growth so GAAP EPS stagnates in the $5.50-6.00 range; the market re-rates a levered roll-up with a 2% cash yield toward Core & Main's 21x or below. A discrete event — an adverse IRS resolution requiring cash materially above the uncertain-tax-position reserve, a goodwill impairment against negative tangible book equity, or a covenant re-test complication — accelerates the de-rating. 19.2x on $6.14 is $118, roughly where the stock traded in January 2026, and well above the November 2025 low of $84.04, which the stock reached on a comparable earnings disappointment.

### Base Case ($166, −0.8%)

The most likely outcome is that Q2 partially confirms the SG&A normalization while organic growth stays low-single-digit, FY2026 EPS lands near consensus at $6.10-6.25, and FY2027 arrives closer to $7.00 than $7.51. Twenty-four times a blended twelve-month-forward EPS of roughly $6.90 gives $166 — essentially the current price. The stock has already re-rated to a peer-median multiple; further appreciation requires either the multiple to exceed peers (hard to justify at this leverage and cash conversion) or an earnings trajectory above what the last two quarters' delivery supports.

The distribution is roughly +19% / −30% around a flat base case. That does not justify new capital, and it is not deteriorated enough to justify a short.

## Trajectory and What Has Changed

Prior reports scored DXPE 5.8 (March), 6.1 (May) and 5.7 (July), with three consecutive HOLDs. This report scores 4.96, still HOLD, and the reductions are located in two dimensions.

Competitive position comes down from 6.8 to 6.0. The prior upgrade credited the water mix shift, which is confirmed and intact, but it does not survive the organic-growth reconstruction: about 1% organic in Q1, with the 65% segment down 5.1% sequentially. Mix improvement in a 14% sub-segment does not carry a 6.8.

Valuation comes down because the multiple has expanded without fundamental confirmation. The share price is roughly 6% higher than at the early-July report while the current-year consensus was cut 9.2% over 90 days. This is not a case of penalizing prior appreciation — price appreciation on rising estimates is neutral-to-positive. It is a case of price rising while estimates fell, which mechanically raises the multiple paid for unchanged-to-weaker forward earnings. The 30-day revision direction has since turned modestly positive (+0.3% current year, +3.6% next year), which is why this is a HOLD rather than a downgrade to AVOID.

Financial health stays at 5.0 and management quality at 5.3, essentially continuing the prior declines: cash conversion, floating-rate leverage, and an incentive structure keyed to absolute pre-tax profit and adjusted EBITDA with no return-on-capital, leverage, free-cash-flow or per-share metric. On the last point, I am describing design and observed behavior — accelerating M&A, an incremental $205M term loan, a paused buyback with ~$68M of authorization remaining — not motive. The countervailing evidence is real: insiders own 22% of the common, diluted shares are down 15.2% since 2022, and the proxy cites 39.2% FY2025 ROIC. Insider activity in the retrieved sample was dispositions only, with no open-market purchases and a founder gift of roughly 91,000 shares; the sales are small relative to remaining stakes and read as routine diversification and estate planning at a multi-year price high. That is a mild negative rather than a red flag, though it carries slightly more weight than usual here because it coincides with declining GAAP EPS and negative 90-day revisions rather than with a fundamental inflection.

## Conclusion

DXP is a competently run consolidator with a real growth asset in water/wastewater and a demonstrated ability to expand gross margin through acquisition and mix. It is also a business where, last quarter, adjusted EBITDA grew 10.1% while GAAP diluted EPS declined; where organic revenue growth rounds to 1%; where free cash flow was $54M against a $2.6B market capitalization; and where 34% of operating income goes to floating-rate interest.

At 31.3x TTM GAAP earnings, 27.3x current-year consensus and 14.0x EV/EBITDA — a peer-median multiple carried by lower-levered, dividend-paying comparables — the price already reflects the water pivot and the M&A machine. Both covering analysts have targets below spot. The August Q2 print is a genuine binary, with options implying ±13% and volatility rich by 20 points to realized, and the three questions it answers are specific: did April's +15% daily-sales momentum hold, did the "timing-related" SG&A reverse, and did GAAP EPS return to growth.

**HOLD.** Own it through the print if you own it; wait for the data if you do not. The setup that would make this a BUY is either confirmation of organic re-acceleration alongside a step-up in cash conversion, or a price in the $125-135 range that restores the asymmetry the current multiple has removed.
