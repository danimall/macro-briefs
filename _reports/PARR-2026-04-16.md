## Executive Summary

Par Pacific Holdings operates four refineries totaling ~219,000 bpd across Hawaii, Montana, Washington, and Wyoming — logistically isolated markets where transportation barriers create durable competitive advantages. The stock has appreciated ~408% from its April 2025 low of $12.23 to ~$62.20, driven by an improving crack spread environment and the geopolitical-driven oil supply compression that has pushed crude above $100/bbl.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) PARR's geographic moat translates elevated crack spreads into outsized earnings with minimal competitive erosion; (2) consensus estimates have surged — current-year EPS revised from $3.77 to $9.14 in 90 days, with the 30-day revision alone at +113.6%; and (3) valuation remains compressed at ~6.8x FY2026E despite these revisions, well below the peer median of 21.6x. The primary risks are crack spread cyclicality, the non-recurrence of a $200M SRE benefit that flattered FY2025 results, broad insider selling, and expiring union contracts. On balance, the macro environment favoring independent refiners with captive markets supports a BUY at current levels, with a base case target of $75.

## Company Overview

Par Pacific Holdings, headquartered in Houston, is a refining and logistics company serving geographically isolated markets in the western United States. The business comprises three segments:

- **Refining** (dominant): Four refineries — Hawaii (84.1 Mbpd), Montana (51.7 Mbpd), Washington (38.7 Mbpd), and Wyoming (13.3 Mbpd) — processing ~188,000 bpd of crude oil throughput combined in FY2025.
- **Logistics**: Pipelines, terminals, and storage facilities that support the refining operations and generate modest third-party revenue.
- **Retail**: Fuel stations primarily in Hawaii, capturing downstream margin.

The company also holds an equity stake in Laramie Energy (Colorado E&P) and recently formed a renewable fuels JV (Hawaii Renewables, 63.5% PARR / 36.5% Mitsubishi-ENEOS) expected to commence operations in H1 2026.

Each refinery serves a market where transportation costs make it uneconomic for Gulf Coast or Asian refiners to routinely compete. Hawaii has only two refineries (PARR and Chevron), and the inland refineries sit behind pipeline and rail bottlenecks. This geographic isolation insulates PARR from the hyper-competitive dynamics that pressure margins at coastal, large-scale refineries.

## Financial Analysis

### Earnings Volatility and FY2025 Results

PARR's financial profile exhibits the extreme cyclicality characteristic of independent refiners:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Revenue | $7.3B | $8.2B | $8.0B | $7.5B |
| Net Income | $364M | $729M | ($33M) | $369M |
| Adj. EBITDA | — | $696M | $239M | $634M |
| FCF | — | — | — | $296M |

FY2025 benefited from two key tailwinds: crack spread improvement across all four refineries (Combined Index +32% YoY) and a $199.5M Small Refinery Exemption (SRE) benefit covering compliance years 2019-2024. Stripping out the SRE, refining adjusted gross margin per barrel was $11.64, up from $9.05 in the trough year but well below FY2023's $16.01. The SRE is non-recurring — investors should not extrapolate headline FY2025 earnings as the baseline.

### Balance Sheet and Liquidity

The balance sheet has improved meaningfully. Long-term debt declined 28% in one year from $1.1B to $798M. Total liquidity stands at $914.6M ($164M cash + $751M ABL availability). Debt-to-equity of 1.51 is elevated but declining. The Altman Z-Score of 3.11 sits just above the "safe zone" threshold. The company repriced its term loan in December 2025 at 50bps lower margin, reflecting the improved credit profile.

Approximately $700M in net operating loss carryforwards provide a meaningful tax shield, reducing the effective cash tax rate over the next several years. Some NOLs expire between 2031-2037.

### Capital Allocation

Management is running a two-pronged approach: deleveraging and buybacks. In FY2025, the company repurchased $124M of stock (following $142M in FY2024), reducing share count from 60M to 51M over two years — a 15% reduction. A new $250M buyback authorization was announced in February 2025 with no end date. FY2026 capex is guided at $190-220M, down from $250M in FY2025, with spending concentrated on Hawaii and Wyoming turnarounds.

FCF yield is approximately 9.6% on the current $3.1B market cap, based on FY2025's $296M in free cash flow. If FY2026 earnings materialize near consensus, FCF should be substantially higher, potentially pushing the yield above 12%.

### Renewable Fuels Optionality

The Hawaii Renewables JV is expected to commence operations in H1 2026. Co-located with the existing Hawaii refinery and backed by Mitsubishi/ENEOS capital, this facility adds renewable diesel and sustainable aviation fuel production. If successful, it provides a less-cyclical revenue stream and generates environmental credits. PARR effectively acquired JV capital at minimal cost — Alohi contributed $100M, with $83M distributed back to PARR.

### Earnings Surprise History

The recent track record is mixed: 2 beats and 2 misses over the last four quarters. The Q2 2025 beat was massive (+96.5%), the Q4 2025 miss was modest (-9.9%). This unevenness is typical for refiners where quarterly crack spread realization drives results. The mixed beat rate warrants some discount to forward consensus but does not undermine the directional thesis.

## Competitive Landscape

PARR competes in the U.S. independent refining sector alongside PBF Energy, HF Sinclair (DINO), CVR Energy, and Delek US Holdings. The differentiation is geography. While PBF and DINO operate larger-scale facilities on the Gulf Coast and East Coast, PARR's facilities serve captive markets:

- **Hawaii**: Duopoly with Chevron. Replacement supply requires transoceanic shipping, structurally protecting local margins.
- **Montana/Wyoming/Washington**: Pipeline-constrained inland markets where transportation economics favor local refiners over Gulf Coast product imports.

This geographic positioning means PARR's margins are less correlated with the Gulf Coast 3-2-1 crack spread and more dependent on regional supply-demand dynamics. When global refining capacity is destroyed, PARR's captive markets face reduced import competition, widening the spread advantage.

The 2025 crack spreads were already recovering: Washington's 3.1.1.1 crack rose from $12.11 to $19.93, and Montana's 6.3.2.1 crack improved from $21.59 to $24.49. The geopolitical escalation since then has driven further improvement, which is captured in the estimate revisions discussed above.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**1. Crack Spread Cyclicality (Severe).** PARR swung from $729M net income to a $33M loss in one year. If a comprehensive Iran deal materializes or demand destruction offsets supply disruption, crack spreads could compress rapidly. The FY2027 estimate decline from $9.14 to $7.59 already assumes some normalization.

**2. SRE Non-Recurrence.** The 2025 compliance year determination is pending, and denial would expose the mainland refineries to full RVO cost burden.

**3. Insider Selling.** CEO Monteleone sold ~$5.9M (March 2026), Director Pate disposed of $29.4M, and multiple other executives sold. No open-market purchases were observed. This is a broad pattern across the C-suite and board.

**4. Union Contract Expiration.** Collective bargaining agreements covering 22% of the workforce expired January 31, 2026, and are under rolling 24-hour extensions. A strike at Hawaii, Washington, or Montana refineries would be materially disruptive.

**5. Wyoming Operational Risk.** The February 2025 incident idled Wyoming for 66 days. While recovered, it highlights the operational fragility of older, smaller refineries.

**6. Geopolitical Reversal.** A rapid diplomatic resolution (probability 10-15% based on current geopolitical dynamics) would compress crack spreads and reverse the estimate trajectory.

### Options Market Signal

PARR's options market is pricing significant near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 80.1% is 25.5 percentage points above the 54.6% 1-year historical vol. The term structure is in steep backwardation (80.1% near-term vs. 60.3% at 6 months), indicating the market expects an imminent catalyst or volatility event. The put/call volume ratio of 0.08 and OI ratio of 0.28 are both extremely call-heavy. The -18.3% put/call skew confirms this call-heavy bias.

The options market is simultaneously pricing high absolute uncertainty and directional bullish positioning, consistent with a momentum-driven refining trade where participants expect further crack spread expansion but acknowledge the binary risk of geopolitical resolution. The rich IV means near-term uncertainty is already priced, which does not invalidate the fundamental thesis but suggests entry timing is less favorable than it would be at depressed vol levels.

### Short Interest

Short interest at 12.4% of float (4.36M shares, 2.5 days to cover) is elevated, reflecting institutional skepticism about the sustainability of the earnings ramp. This creates squeeze potential if results continue to surprise upward, while also indicating that sophisticated investors see cyclical peak risk.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($80-85, ~30-37% upside)

Global refining capacity destruction is permanent — Jubail, SAMREF, Kuwait, and other Gulf facilities face 3-5 year rebuilds. Combined with the Hormuz blockade enforcement and dual Russian/Iranian waiver non-renewal, crack spreads remain elevated through 2026-2027. PARR's FY2026 EPS reaches $10-11 as estimates continue revising upward. The renewable fuels JV begins contributing earnings in H2 2026. Applying 8.5x to $10 EPS yields $85. Share buybacks at the current pace further compress the float.

### Bear Case ($35-40, ~36-43% downside)

A comprehensive Iran deal is reached within 3-6 months, crude falls to $80-85, and crack spreads normalize toward 2024 levels. The SRE benefit is not renewed for 2025. Union negotiations break down, causing a multi-week strike at one or more refineries. FY2026 EPS collapses toward $4-5. The stock reverts to 8x trough earnings at $32-40.

### Base Case ($75, ~21% upside)

Oil remains above $95 through most of 2026. Crack spreads stay above historical averages but moderate from peak levels. FY2026 EPS comes in at $8-9. Renewable JV starts contributing modestly. Applying 8.5x to $8.75 mid-cycle EPS yields $74. Continued buybacks provide a floor.

## Conclusion

Par Pacific Holdings offers a differentiated way to express the current refining tailwind through geographically captive markets that amplify crack spread improvements with limited competitive erosion. At 6.8x FY2026E earnings with estimates rising at triple-digit rates, valuation has not caught up to the improving fundamentals. The 60% discount to peer median P/E overstates the risks for a company with $914M in liquidity, declining leverage, and an active buyback program.

The primary offset is that this is a deeply cyclical business where crack spreads can reverse abruptly. The SRE benefit masks the underlying margin quality, broad insider selling is a yellow flag, and expiring union contracts add near-term uncertainty. These risks are real but quantifiable, and the current valuation provides meaningful margin of safety against moderate crack spread normalization. A BUY is appropriate with a $75 base case target, with tight monitoring of crack spread benchmarks and geopolitical developments as the primary exit signals.
