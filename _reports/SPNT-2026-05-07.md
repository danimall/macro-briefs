## Executive Summary

SiriusPoint has executed one of the more impressive turnarounds in specialty reinsurance over the past three years, moving from a $387M loss in 2022 to generating 22.1% ROE in 2025 (inclusive of the $222M Armada sale gain). The operational engine is functioning well: Q1 2026 delivered an 88.9% core combined ratio and 15.3% operating ROE, gross written premium grew 16% in 2025 to $3.7B, and share count has been reduced 30% through aggressive buybacks. The CM Bermuda overhang has been resolved, the capital structure simplified via Series B preference redemption, and the company is transitioning from "reshaping" to "growth" with a new four-division operating model.

However, the investment case at current prices requires careful calibration. The stock trades at approximately 9.1x forward earnings (on consensus $2.58 EPS for 2026), not the 6.4x trailing P/E that superficially appears from the most recent 12-month EPS of $3.64 — which was inflated by $1.90/share from the non-recurring Armada gain. At 9.1x forward and 1.26x book value, SPNT sits slightly above the peer median P/E of 8.4x. Normalized 12-15% ROE (management's stated target range) and modest positive estimate revisions (+6.5% over 90 days) may justify current valuation but leave limited room for further multiple expansion. With analyst sentiment degrading and the median price target of $25 implying only 6.4% upside, the near-term risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

SiriusPoint is a Bermuda-domiciled global specialty underwriter created through the 2021 merger of Third Point Reinsurance and Sirius International Insurance Group. The company operates through two reportable segments — Insurance & Services (62% of GWP) and Reinsurance (38%) — and generates earnings from three sources: underwriting results, service fee income from consolidated MGAs (primarily IMG for travel/health and Alta Signa for European professional lines), and investment income on $6.5B in invested assets.

A distinguishing feature is SPNT's distribution-first model: the majority of premium flows through MGA partnerships with delegated underwriting authority. The company launched 16 new MGA partnerships in 2025 and holds equity stakes in 18 MGA/InsurTech entities. This model provides capital-light access to diverse specialty lines but introduces delegated authority risk. As of March 2026, the company reorganized into four global divisions: Global P&C Programs, Global Reinsurance, Global Accident & Health, and London Market Specialty.

Financial strength ratings of A- (Positive outlook) from AM Best, Fitch, and S&P, with A3 (Stable) from Moody's, position the company competitively for attracting program business. A potential upgrade to "A" would be a meaningful competitive catalyst.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Power — Trailing vs. Normalized:**

The trailing 12-month EPS of $3.64 materially overstates sustainable earnings power. It includes approximately $1.90/share from the October 2025 Armada sale ($222M gain) and likely a portion of the $25M Arcadian gain in Q1 2026. Stripping these out, normalized trailing EPS approximates $1.74-$2.00/share, consistent with the forward consensus of $2.58 (FY2026) and $2.79 (FY2027).

The forward estimates imply a normalized ROE of approximately 12-14% on the current equity base ($2.47B common equity, ~117M shares). This is within management's 12-15% through-the-cycle target and represents solid performance for a specialty reinsurer but removes the "exceptional ROE at a discount multiple" argument that the 22.1% 2025 ROE superficially supported.

**Key Operating Metrics:**

|--------|--------|--------|---------|

The Q1 2026 combined ratio of 88.9% is the strongest quarterly result in recent history and suggests operational momentum is intact.

**Capital Management:**

The 30% share count reduction (166.5M → 116.5M) in 2025 was transformative for per-share economics. The $491M spent on repurchases included a $483M block purchase from CM Bermuda — effectively a single-counterparty buyout of an overhang shareholder rather than open-market repurchases at prevailing prices. The Series B preference redemption ($200M at 8% coupon) saves $16M annually and simplifies the capital structure.

BSCR ratio of 247% provides substantial regulatory buffer, and $694.7M in available dividend capacity from subsidiaries supports continued capital return. Debt-to-equity at 4.09x is optically high but standard for insurance balance sheets dominated by policyholder reserves.

**Investment Portfolio:**

$6.5B total invested assets with 3.2-year duration and average credit quality of AA-. The fixed income orientation provides stable $270M+ annual investment income at current rate levels. Duration is appropriately managed.

**Estimate Trajectory:**

The growth story is credible but moderate: mid-single-digit earnings growth ($2.58 → $2.79, or +8% YoY) supported by continued share buybacks and stable underwriting performance. This is a compounding story with mid-teens total return potential, not a high-growth situation.

This premium is partially justified by: (1) stronger recent operating performance, (2) positive rating outlooks signaling potential upgrade, (3) superior per-share growth trajectory given aggressive buybacks, and (4) optionality on the A&H/travel vertical build-out.

At 1.26x book with normalized 12-14% ROE, the stock trades approximately at fair value for a well-managed specialty reinsurer. The math: 14% ROE × 1.26x book = approximately 17.6% earnings yield on book, which at 9.1x earnings prices in steady-state execution without significant upside from here.

## Competitive Landscape

SiriusPoint occupies a distinct niche in the specialty reinsurance market as a distribution-first, MGA-focused capacity provider. This model differentiates it from larger peers (Swiss Re, Munich Re, RenaissanceRe) that primarily write direct or through traditional broker channels. The limited channel conflict with MGA partners is a genuine competitive advantage — SPNT provides capacity without competing for the same clients.

The A- (Positive) rating is adequate for current business but represents a ceiling for attracting the highest-quality program administrators. An upgrade to "A" would unlock an additional tier of MGA partnerships and could accelerate premium growth.

The reinsurance market is softening. Property catastrophe rates are declining materially due to excess capital and strong recent industry results. This pressures the Reinsurance segment specifically while the Insurance & Services segment benefits from continued E&S market growth and strong demand for specialty program capacity.

The travel/health vertical integration strategy (IMG + Assist America + World Nomads) positions SPNT in a growing, lower-volatility segment. Travel insurance demand is structurally increasing post-pandemic, and the asset-light MGA model provides operating leverage as volumes scale.

## Risk Assessment

**Catastrophe Exposure:** California wildfires cost $67.1M in 2025. A 1-in-250 year Southeast US windstorm would consume ~6% of common equity. While manageable within the retrocession program ($160M limit above $90M retention for 2026), a major loss event would compress near-term earnings and book value.

**Reinsurance Market Softening:** Property catastrophe rates declining "significantly" per management commentary. This is an industry-wide headwind that pressures the 38% of GWP from the Reinsurance segment. The shift toward Insurance & Services partially mitigates but doesn't eliminate this risk.

**Casualty Reserve Adequacy:** IBNR reserves of $2.66B (72% of total reserves) carry inherent uncertainty, particularly in casualty lines subject to social inflation and extended reporting periods. Favorable development declined from $108M to $74M in 2025; further deterioration would pressure reported earnings.

**Tariff/Inflation Impact on Loss Costs:** The 10-K explicitly flags tariff-driven increases in property replacement values, labor costs, and materials costs as potentially increasing loss severity. In the current macro environment with sustained above-target inflation, this risk is elevated.

**Bermuda Corporate Income Tax:** The newly enacted 15% CIT (effective 2025) increased tax expense from $31M to $81M. Further OECD minimum tax developments could erode the Bermuda domicile advantage that has historically benefited reinsurers.

**MGA Delegated Authority Risk:** Majority of premium produced through third-party underwriters with limited direct oversight creates potential for adverse selection, aggregation risk, or compliance failures.

**Options Market Signal:**

The options delta data shows an extraordinary spike in ATM IV from 82% to 168.4% over a single session (May 7), accompanied by a minimal price move (+0.9%). The near-zero put/call OI ratio indicates extremely limited put hedging demand. The IV spike likely reflects a specific options market dislocation (perhaps a large options trade or earnings-adjacent pricing) rather than fundamental repricing of the stock. This warrants monitoring but doesn't alter the fundamental thesis at this stage.

## Conclusion

SiriusPoint has earned its re-rating from the 2022 lows. The turnaround is real: underwriting discipline has been restored, the balance sheet simplified, capital management is shareholder-friendly, and the MGA-focused model provides differentiated growth access.

The challenge is that the re-rating is largely complete. At 9.1x forward P/E and 1.26x book, the stock trades at a modest premium to specialty reinsurance peers. Normalized ROE of 12-14% (management's own target) supports current valuation but doesn't demand significant expansion. The stock has appreciated 19% over the past year and now sits near its 52-week high. Upside from here requires either above-target execution (sustained 15%+ ROE), a rating upgrade, or meaningful acceleration in the A&H/travel vertical that isn't yet in consensus.

The appropriate stance is HOLD with a positive lean. The business quality is high and improving, per-share compounding from buybacks provides a floor on returns, and the rating upgrade optionality provides a defined catalyst path. A pullback to $20-21 (1.05-1.10x book) would create the asymmetric entry point that would warrant a BUY recommendation. At current prices, the expected return of 9-13% over 12 months is adequate but doesn't meet the threshold for high-conviction overweight.
