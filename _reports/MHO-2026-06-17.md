**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

M/I Homes is a top-10 single-family homebuilder operating 17 divisions across 10 states, with a Southern/Florida tilt (~56% of homebuilding revenue) and a captive mortgage operation that captures 93-96% of deliveries. The company is in the middle of a cyclical margin reset. Homebuilding gross margin fell 390bp to 20.8% in 2025 and a further 410bp YoY in Q1 2026 (to 19.3%), driven almost entirely by mortgage rate buydowns (+$53.3M in 2025, $52.7M in Q1'26), higher lot costs (+$64.9M), and one-time impairments ($47.7M) and warranty charges ($11.2M). Net income fell 29% in 2025 and 39% in Q1'26 even though deliveries dipped only 1%, making this a margin story rather than a volume collapse.

The competing forces define a balanced setup. On one side: backlog is down 23-36% in value, estimates are falling across every horizon (current-year EPS revised -13.5% over 90 days), and the margin weakness that was a Southern problem in 2025 is now spreading north (Q1'26 Northern margin fell 270bp to 19.5%, converging with the South's 19.1%). On the other side: the balance sheet is among the strongest in the sector (effectively negative net homebuilding debt, 18% debt-to-capital, 17-18x interest coverage, Altman Z 3.03), capital return is aggressive and self-funded (~$250M/year of buybacks at record equity), and the valuation at ~10.9x trailing EPS and ~1.15x book is in line with comparable peers rather than at a premium that invites derating.

The prior-cycle framing of this name as an unbroken deterioration overstated the case. The most recent quarter (Q1 2026) beat consensus ($2.55 vs $2.53), and the surprise trajectory has stabilized (-1.3%, -6.3%, -39.1%, +0.9% across four quarters), indicating management is now modeling the buydown drag rather than being surprised by it. The stock is +34% off its trough, and the entire covering street holds targets ($155-165) above spot. The bearish case of continued margin compression is at least partially priced. With genuine fundamental headwinds capping upside and a strong balance sheet capping downside, this is a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1976 and headquartered in Columbus, Ohio, M/I Homes has sold over 170,500 homes in its 50-year history. The business splits into three segments:

- **Northern region (~41% of homebuilding revenue):** Columbus, Cincinnati, Indianapolis, Chicago, Minneapolis/St. Paul, Detroit.
- **Southern region (~56%):** four Florida metros (Tampa, Orlando, Sarasota, Ft. Myers/Naples), four Texas metros (Austin, Dallas/Ft. Worth, Houston, San Antonio), plus Charlotte, Raleigh, Nashville.
- **Financial Services (~3% of revenue):** M/I Financial plus title agencies, high-margin and integral to delivering rate buydowns; captured 93% of 2025 deliveries, rising to 96% in Q1'26.

Average sales price was $479K in 2025, spanning ~$190K to $1.25M. The affordability-focused "Smart Series" was ~52% of homes sold. The land strategy is low-risk (options and takedowns, ~80% of lots developed internally, ~50,000 lots under control, or ~5-year supply). Inventory ("Ready Now") homes were 68% of 2025 closings, up from 60%, reflecting a shift toward spec building to keep pace with incentive-driven demand.

## Financial Analysis

The financial picture pairs deteriorating earnings with an exceptionally strong balance sheet.

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 vs Q1'25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 4.03 | 4.50 | 4.42 (-2%) | $0.92B (-6%) |
| Net income ($M) | 465 | 564 | 403 (-29%) | $67.8M (-39%) |
| Diluted EPS | — | $19.71 | $14.74 | $2.55 vs $3.98 |
| Homes delivered | 8,112 | 9,055 | 8,921 (-1%) | 1,914 (-3%) |
| Homebuilding gross margin | — | 24.7% | 20.8% (-390bp) | 19.3% (-410bp) |

**Margin drivers.** The 2025 gross margin decline decomposes into a $53.3M increase in mortgage rate buydowns, $64.9M higher lot costs, $47.7M of inventory impairments and land-deposit write-offs, and $11.2M of Florida warranty charges. The damage concentrated in the Southern region, where gross margin fell 680bp (26.6% → 19.8%) and segment operating income fell ~45% ($451M → $250M). Northern margins held in 2025 (-10bp to 22.0%). In Q1'26 the pattern shifted: Northern margin fell 270bp to 19.5%, converging with the South's 19.1%. The earnings cushion the North provided is thinning, the single most important negative development this cycle.

**Demand and backlog.** 2025 new contracts fell 4% and absorption pace slowed to 3.0/month from 3.3. Backlog dropped to 1,809 units / $990M at YE2025 from 2,531 / $1.4B (-36% in value); Q1'26 backlog was $1.20B vs $1.56B (-23%). Backlog ASP is also declining. New contracts rose 3% in Q1'26, but on the back of higher incentives and more communities, with inventory homes dominating sales; demand is being pulled forward by buydowns rather than organically strong.

**Balance sheet.** This is the offsetting strength. Equity is at a record $3.17B (book value ~$123-125/share); homebuilding debt-to-capital is 18%; Altman Z is 3.03 (safe zone). Homebuilding net debt is effectively negative: $767M cash at Q1'26 against $700M of senior notes, no revolver draw, and $812M available. The credit-facility leverage ratio is 0.01-0.02 against a 0.60 covenant; interest coverage is 17-18x. The only fixed debt is $400M of 4.95% notes due 2028 and $300M of 3.95% notes due 2030, with no near-term maturity wall.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow was $137M in 2025 (down from $180M), modest because cash is consumed by inventory growth (+$313M). The company returns capital aggressively: $202M repurchased in 2025, $50M in Q1'26, with a new $250M authorization (Nov 2025) leaving ~$170M remaining. Share count fell to ~26.7M basic in 2025 from ~27.8M in 2022. No dividend.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is negative and visibility is shrinking. Estimates are falling across every horizon: current quarter -16.9% (30d) / -9.9% (90d), next quarter -10.0% / -15.9%, current year -9.4% / -13.5%, next year -4.4% / -5.2%. The negative momentum is real and the primary reason this is not a BUY.

The structural question is whether this is cyclical or terminal. The decline is overwhelmingly a margin story; deliveries fell only 1% in 2025. If mortgage rates ease and buydown intensity normalizes, earnings power can rebound quickly, because the volume base is largely intact and the incentive drag flows directly through the P&L in both directions. The macro backdrop is unhelpful in the near term: mortgage rates remain elevated, Florida and parts of Texas are the softest, most oversupplied new-home markets, and management expects elevated rates and tepid sentiment to persist through 2026. There is no near-term catalyst for margin recovery, but neither is there evidence of structural impairment; this is a rate-cycle compression, and homebuilders have repeatedly mean-reverted out of these.

The Q1'26 beat ($2.55 vs $2.53) and the surprise trajectory (-1.3%, -6.3%, -39.1%, +0.9%) show the most recent quarter back near consensus. Management is now guiding to and meeting the buydown drag rather than being surprised by it. That does not turn growth positive, but it argues against the thesis that the drag is intensifying beyond what's modeled.

## Valuation Assessment

MHO trades at ~10.9x trailing EPS ($14.74) and ~1.15x book (~$123-125/share). Against the homebuilding peer set, the relevant comparison is to direct production builders, not the manufactured-housing names that skew the median:

| Ticker | P/E | Note |
|---|---|---|
| KBH | 10.4 | Direct comp |
| GRBK | 10.7 | Direct comp |
| **MHO** | **10.9** | — |
| TPH | 22.2 | — |
| SKY | 22.0 | Manufactured housing |
| CVCO | 25.0 | Manufactured housing |

Against KBH (10.4x) and GRBK (10.7x), MHO trades at no premium. The peer median of 22.0x is distorted by SKY and CVCO, which are different businesses. The valuation is fair, not cheap and not stretched.

A low multiple on a cyclical at peak-to-mid margins is not a buy signal on its own; it typically prices in further earnings normalization, and the falling estimates confirm the market expects exactly that. The trailing multiple is being calculated on declining earnings, so the forward multiple is higher than 10.9x as the denominator compresses. There is no specific, identifiable mispricing to lean on for a high valuation score. The downside is bounded by book value and the buyback floor; the upside requires a rate-driven margin recovery that has no near-term catalyst. That balance produces a mid-range valuation score.

The covering street's targets ($155 low / $160 mean / $165 high) sit 9-16% above spot. These reflect the same cyclical-recovery optionality and balance-sheet support that anchor the HOLD case, and are consistent with a name where downside is capped and modest upside exists if margins stabilize.

## Competitive Landscape

Homebuilding is fragmented and competitive; MHO faces national builders (D.R. Horton, Lennar, PulteGroup) with greater scale and land resources, plus regional players. The affordability squeeze and resulting buydown-driven margin compression are sector-wide, not MHO-specific.

MHO's relative advantages are durable: a low-leverage balance sheet that self-funds land and buybacks through the downturn, a captive mortgage operation that makes buydown incentives operationally efficient, and a value-oriented Smart Series mix. Long-run total shareholder return of 288.9% versus the peer group's 204.2% (per the pay-versus-performance disclosure) supports the view that this is a well-run operator gaining or holding ground over the cycle.

The competitive trajectory is roughly stable with a near-term negative tilt. The Florida/Southern concentration is a relative disadvantage right now because those are the softest markets, and the North-South margin convergence in Q1'26 removes a differentiator that held in 2025. The company is not losing share in a structural sense, but its geographic mix is exposed to the worst of the current cycle.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Margin compression from buydown dependence.** Management explicitly warns of "further margin pressure" in 2026. Every incremental incentive dollar flows straight through gross margin. This is the central P&L risk and the reason estimates keep falling.
2. **Southern/Florida concentration.** The Southern segment drove nearly all the 2025 decline. Florida saw inventory impairments and a $11.2M attic-ventilation warranty issue (remaining accrual $5.2M). Florida faces oversupply, insurance-cost, and demand-softening dynamics.
3. **Inventory impairment risk.** Impairment models use 13-16% discount rates; further price/absorption deterioration triggers more charges.
4. **Construction cost / tariffs.** The Q1'26 MD&A newly flags tariffs and retaliatory tariffs as a forward risk.
5. **Financing-arm rollover.** M/I Financial's repurchase facilities ($200M committed + $100M uncommitted) expire October 2026 and require annual renewal, with no assurance of extension. Manageable given the balance sheet, but a monitorable.
6. **CFO succession.** Veteran CFO Phillip Creek is labeled "Ex. VP & CFO" in recent Form 4s and a March 4, 2026 Item 5.02 8-K was filed, though Creek still signed the Q1'26 certifications. This appears orderly, but the successor's identity and timeline should be confirmed.

Most of these risks are at least partially priced; the stock sits at trough-cycle earnings with a compressed multiple, and the analyst targets already embed continued normalization. The balance sheet substantially caps the downside scenarios, which is why the risk score sits in the middle rather than below 4.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent, near-dated stress with a bullish lean. Near-term ATM IV is 68.7% against 34.4% one-year historical vol, rich at the 77th percentile within the chain, in steep backwardation (68.7% near-term vs 32.1% six-month). This is event-specific positioning rather than a structural-collapse signal: the 1-week put/call skew is -14.1% (call-heavy), the put/call volume ratio is just 0.15, and the put/call OI ratio collapsed to 0.02 from 0.12 a month ago as the stock rallied +15%. The positioning is for a near-term move with a call-side bias, consistent with the recovery off the lows. The backwardation will resolve as the near-dated catalyst passes; the absence of put demand argues against an imminent fundamental scare.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$165, +16%):** Mortgage rates ease in H2 2026, buydown intensity normalizes, and margins inflect back toward the low-20s. With deliveries largely intact and a 96% mortgage capture rate, earnings power rebounds faster than consensus expects. The buyback at 1.15x book compounds book value per share through the trough. Q1's beat marks the bottom of the surprise cycle, and the street revises targets upward as margins stabilize. The stock re-rates toward $165.

**Bear case (~$110-115, -19% to -23%):** Rates stay elevated through 2026, buydown drag compounds, and the North-South margin convergence continues so the earnings cushion disappears entirely. Florida oversupply triggers further impairments, and backlog continues shrinking, pulling forward earnings into a thinner pipeline. Estimates keep falling and the stock derates toward book value (~$123) and below, finding support near the buyback floor around $110-115.

**Base case (~$145-150):** Margins stabilize in the high-teens to ~20% as buydowns plateau, earnings normalize lower but predictably, and the buyback supports per-share metrics. The stock trades in a range around current levels, with the balance sheet capping downside and the absence of a margin-recovery catalyst capping upside. This is the most likely outcome over the next 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, late July) and the Fed's rate path are the key checkpoints. Margin trajectory and backlog direction are the data points to watch.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Homebuilding gross margin stabilizes or inflects upward for two consecutive quarters (i.e., Q2/Q3'26 margins flat-to-up vs Q1's 19.3%), signaling the buydown drag has peaked.
- A sustained move lower in mortgage rates that reduces incentive intensity, combined with stabilizing estimate revisions.
- The stock pulls back toward book value (~$123-125 / ~1.0x book) while fundamentals hold, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Northern margins fall below ~18% alongside continued Southern deterioration, confirming the earnings cushion has fully eroded with no offset.
- Backlog declines accelerate beyond -25% YoY in value, signaling demand is breaking rather than being incentive-supported.
- A new wave of inventory impairments materially larger than 2025's $47.7M, or evidence the Florida warranty issue is broader than two communities.

**Re-entry constructive price:** A pullback to ~$123-125 (book value) on intact fundamentals would flip the risk/reward to constructive and warrant a BUY, offering the buyback-supported floor with cyclical recovery optionality at minimal premium.

## Conclusion

M/I Homes is a well-managed, exceptionally well-capitalized homebuilder working through a cyclical, not terminal, margin reset. The fundamental headwinds are real: gross margin down 390bp in 2025 and 410bp in Q1'26, estimates falling across every horizon, backlog down 23-36%, and the North-South margin convergence removing the 2025 earnings cushion. These cap any case for buying. The offsetting strengths are equally real: negative net homebuilding debt, 17-18x interest coverage, ~$250M/year of self-funded buybacks at record equity, a valuation in line with direct peers (10.9x vs KBH 10.4x, GRBK 10.7x), and a Q1 beat that suggests the buydown drag is now modeled rather than surprising. Downside is bounded by book value and the buyback floor; the bearish margin-compression view is at least partially reflected in the compressed multiple and the +34% the stock has already recovered off its trough.

The name is too exposed to falling estimates to buy and too well-supported by its balance sheet and in-line valuation to short. We rate MHO **HOLD** with an uncertain rationale, and we would turn constructive on a pullback toward book value (~$123-125) or on two consecutive quarters of margin stabilization.
