**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Federal Realty enters the second half of 2026 with better operating momentum than at any point in the last three years and a share price that has already absorbed most of it. Q2 2026 Core FFO of $1.88 per diluted share rose 6.8% year over year, revenue grew 7.8% to $335.7M, comparable leasing volume of 819,273 square feet was an all-time quarterly record at +15% cash spreads, and management raised all four guidance metrics while lifting the dividend 3% to $1.16 per quarter — the 59th consecutive annual increase, from a payout of only 62% of guided Core FFO. Leverage improved from roughly 5.97x to 5.49x net debt to our EBITDA proxy in six months, and in April the bank group upsized the revolver from $1.25B to $1.4B, cut the spread to SOFR+72.5bp, and extended maturity to 2030. On the evidence available, this is an investment-grade balance sheet improving at the margin.

The constraint is arithmetic and well documented in the filings. Interest expense rose 13.8% in the first half ($99.1M vs. $87.1M) while total debt principal *fell* from $4,963.6M to $4,786.0M. That is pure coupon migration: FY2025 carried a 3.8% weighted average rate, the $400M 1.25% notes matured in February 2026, and $693.3M (2027) plus $1,157.5M (2028) of legacy paper still has to roll into a higher-rate market. Roughly 200bp of refinancing spread on the 2027 tower alone is about $14M of annual interest, near $0.16 per share. This is the mechanical reason a portfolio generating +17% trailing cash leasing spreads delivers only ~6% FFO-per-share growth.

At $124.58 the shares trade at 16.6x the $7.52 midpoint of 2026 Core FFO guidance, a ~5.9% implied cap rate on annualized property operating income before corporate overhead, and a ~3.7% forward yield — 3% below the 52-week high of $128.21 and 5% below the $131.14 consensus mean. We continue to rate FRT HOLD for a fifth consecutive review, but the composition of that call has shifted: the fundamental case is modestly stronger than in the spring while the price has advanced roughly 13% since April, leaving compensation for the 2027-28 refinancing overhang and unquantified office exposure thinner than it was. The overall score declines to 6.0 from 6.2, entirely on valuation.

## Company Overview

Federal Realty is an internally managed retail REIT founded in 1962, headquartered in North Bethesda, Maryland, and a member of the S&P 500. As of June 30, 2026 the Trust held 103 predominantly retail properties comprising about 28.8 million commercial square feet leased to roughly 3,700 commercial tenants, plus approximately 2,500 residential units.

The portfolio's defining characteristics are density and rent level. Average base rent is $32.79 per square foot portfolio-wide, well above typical open-air shopping center peers. Geography is deliberately concentrated: California is 24.9% of GLA, Maryland 17.7%, Virginia 16.5%, Massachusetts 7.8% and New Jersey 6.5% — 62.5% in five states, with the Northeast/Mid-Atlantic/California core near 85%. Grocery and necessity anchoring is pervasive (Whole Foods, Trader Joe's at eight-plus centers, Giant, Safeway, Harris Teeter, Kroger, Sprouts, Aldi), layered with off-price (TJX banners, Ross, Burlington, Nordstrom Rack) and fitness (Equinox, Life Time, Planet Fitness).

Four mixed-use assets carry the strategy: Santana Row in San Jose (1,521,000 commercial sf at $57.21/sf plus 554 residential units, 98% leased), Pike & Rose in North Bethesda ($47.31/sf, 100% leased), Assembly Row in Somerville ($41.61/sf, 98% leased) and Bethesda Row ($60.65/sf, 99% leased). Together roughly 4.2 million square feet, about 15% of GLA at rents far above portfolio average. Street retail extends the pattern: Greenwich Avenue at $96.19/sf, Wildwood at $114.79/sf.

Tenant diversification is genuine: no single tenant or affiliated group exceeds 2.4% of annualized base rent, and no property exceeded 10% of 2025 revenue. Leases run 3-10 years with escalators, percentage rent, and recovery of a majority of on-site operating expenses and real estate taxes. Residential leases were 8.7% of 2025 rental income.

One classification point matters for how the multiple should be set: the property schedule footnotes that some properties include office space, and Santana Row's principal tenants include Cisco Systems and NetApp. Total office GLA and office occupancy are not disclosed in the sections available, and Santana West — office-adjacent development in a San Jose market with well-documented office weakness — is excluded from comparable properties as under construction. Investors underwriting FRT as pure open-air retail are capitalizing an unquantified office and multifamily stub at a retail cap rate.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and operating income.** FY2025 revenue was $1,279.0M (+6.4%) with property operating income of $860.1M (+6.1%). Q2 2026 revenue reached $335.7M (+7.8%) and POI $232.1M (+8.9%); first-half revenue was $676.8M (+9.0%). G&A of $13.5M in Q2 remains tightly controlled at roughly 4% of revenue.

**The FFO presentation requires care.** Nareit FFO of $1.88 per diluted share in Q2 2026 compares to $1.91 a year earlier — a 1.6% decline. Core FFO of $1.88 compares to $1.76 — a 6.8% increase. The entire difference is a one-time $13.0M ($0.15 per share) New Market Tax Credit transaction income recognized in Q2 2025, whose compliance period concluded in June 2025. GAAP diluted EPS separately fell from $1.78 to $0.97 on a smaller gain on sale ($20.6M vs. $76.5M) plus the NMTC absence. First-half Core FFO of $3.75 per share is up 8.4% from $3.46.

Two data-quality warnings follow. First, the earnings-surprise series showing 155-161% "beats" compares reported FFO per share against GAAP EPS estimates — different bases. On matching bases, Q2 Core FFO of $1.88 beat the $1.85 FFO consensus by 1.6%. Second, the trailing P/E and EPS fields in the company summary are internally inconsistent ($4.679 EPS against a 21.5x multiple does not reconcile to a $124.58 price), and GAAP earnings multiples are the wrong instrument for a REIT whose net income swings on $20-115M gains on sale. We anchor valuation on Core FFO per share and implied cap rate.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt principal fell to $4,786.0M at June 30 from $4,963.6M at year-end; cash was $107.2M and shareholders' equity $3,434.6M. Our derived net debt of $4,678.8M against a TTM EBITDA proxy of roughly $852.1M (POI less G&A, adjusted for the first-half increment) gives 5.49x, versus 5.97x at December 31 — approximately half a turn of deleveraging in six months. Interest coverage on the same proxy is about 4.1x, and roughly 4.0x including preferred dividends. These are our constructions, not covenant or supplemental definitions; the turn count could differ by ±0.2x, but the direction is clear.

The mechanics: the $400M 1.25% notes matured February 15, 2026 and were retired with a $250M term loan draw plus disposition proceeds. Senior notes fell $397.3M while notes payable rose $225.6M; the balance of senior notes declined, and the term-loan draw should not be read as incremental leverage.

**Maturity ladder** at June 30: $251.7M (2026), $693.3M (2027), $1,157.5M (2028), $945.4M (2029), $686.9M (2030), $1,051.0M thereafter. The 2028 tower is the item to monitor. Variable-rate debt fell from about $1.4B to $1.1B, where the disclosed sensitivity is $10.9M of annual interest per 100bp, roughly $0.125 per share.

**Dividend durability.** The raised $4.64 annualized rate represents 61.7% of the 2026 Core FFO midpoint. Our AFFO construction — FY2025 Core FFO available to common of about $611M less roughly $133M of recurring property improvements and leasing costs — implies about $478M against $389.7M of dividends, an ~81% AFFO payout. That figure is conservative because a portion of "other property improvements" is value-add rather than maintenance. Dividend coverage is not where the risk sits.

## Growth Analysis

**Leasing economics are the strongest part of the story.** FY2025 comparable leasing of 2,340,000 sf came in at +15% cash (new +19%, renewals +12%) with tenant improvements and incentives of $23.18/sf blended, down from $26.03/sf in FY2024 when spreads were +11%. Rising spreads alongside falling concessions is the clearest available evidence of landlord pricing power, consistent with the near-absence of new open-air retail construction in the US since 2008 against expanding grocery, off-price, fitness and medtail demand. Q2 2026 extended it: 124 comparable leases across 819,273 sf at $33.68/sf versus $29.23 prior, +15% cash and +28% straight-line; trailing twelve months 2,796,064 sf at +17% cash.

**Conversion to reported growth is slower and lags meaningfully.** Comparable property POI grew only 2.8% in Q2 ($206.3M vs. $200.7M). Adjusted comparable POI, which strips straight-line rent and in-place lease amortization, grew 4.2% — higher than the unadjusted figure because both accrual contributions shrank year over year ($2.8M vs. $4.2M straight-line; $2.7M vs. $3.8M amortization). The cash engine is running ahead of the GAAP-accrual engine, the opposite of the pattern at REITs supporting growth with straight-line rent, and guidance formalizes it by expecting adjusted comparable POI to run about 75bp above comparable POI for the year.

Offsetting that, lease termination fee income rose roughly $3.1M in Q2 and $5.5M in the first half year over year, against a comparable POI increment of $5.6M. The company does not disclose how termination fees split between comparable and non-comparable pools, so the exact contribution to the 2.8% cannot be stated. Termination fees are lumpy and non-recurring, though recapturing below-market space for re-leasing at +28% straight-line new-lease spreads has real economic value.

**Occupancy is the soft spot.** Leased rate was 96.1% at both December 31, 2025 and June 30, 2026; occupancy slipped from 94.1% to 93.8%. Management frames both as flat sequentially, with occupancy +20bp and leased rate +70bp year over year. Residential comparable leased rate of 97.0% is down 20bp. The 230bp leased-to-occupied gap is contracted signed-but-not-commenced rent worth roughly $21.9M annualized on portfolio-average assumptions (28.8M sf × 2.3% × ~$33/sf), about $0.25 per share, and represents the most visible near-term earnings bridge. The 10-K is explicit that 2025-signed leases become effective over the following two years, with some not effective until 2028 or beyond, and that some tenants will not take possession. Anyone underwriting record leasing volume as near-term revenue will be early.

**Guidance and consensus.** Management raised 2026 Core FFO guidance to $7.48-$7.56 from $7.46-$7.55 and comparable POI growth to 3.25%-3.75%. The midpoint implies 5.9%-7.1% Core FFO growth over FY2025's $7.06. The GAAP EPS guidance raise was far larger — $3.94-$4.03 to $4.22-$4.30, roughly $0.28 at the midpoint against $0.02 for FFO — driven almost entirely by higher expected gains on sale, now about $1.30 per share for the year.

This matters for how the estimate revision data should be read. The current-year consensus of $4.02 is up 37.9% over 90 days; the next-year figure of $3.08 is down 3.3%. Both series are GAAP EPS, and the current-year surge reflects disposition-gain assumptions rather than operating improvement, while the next-year decline reflects fewer assumed gains. We do not treat the +37.9% revision as an earnings-inflection buy signal — it is a transaction-timing revision. On the FFO series that actually drives the stock, revisions are approximately flat to modestly positive, matching the $0.02 guidance raise.

**Capital deployment has reversed.** FY2025 was a deployment year: $752.8M of acquisitions (Del Monte in Monterey, Town Center Crossing/Plaza in Leawood Kansas, Annapolis Town Center, Village Pointe in Omaha) against $315.7M of dispositions. 2026 to date is a digestion year: roughly $205M of sales (Santana Row residential building plus Courthouse Center at $158.5M, Barcroft Plaza at $58.0M, 3131 Commodore Plaza at $8.1M) against a single $19.7M Kingstowne parcel. Construction in progress fell from $374.7M to $329.8M as Blayr at Bala Cynwyd (217 residential units, 19,000 sf retail) delivered — non-earning capital converting to earning.

The Kansas and Nebraska acquisitions broadened geography beyond the historical coastal framing, at rents ($37.14/sf in Leawood) above portfolio average and with tenant rosters including Apple, RH, Aritzia and lululemon. That is quality-consistent and geography-inconsistent. A less charitable reading is that coastal assets became too expensive to underwrite and "select underserved markets" is the language of yield-reaching. Both readings fit the facts, and the evidence does not support any claim about improving tenant diversification — the 2.4% maximum single-tenant figure has no prior-year comparison available.

## Valuation Assessment

At $124.58 against the $7.52 guidance midpoint, FRT trades at 16.56x 2026 Core FFO and 17.6x FY2025 actual. The forward yield on the raised $4.64 rate is 3.73% (the 3.58% in the summary data reflects the trailing rate). Our implied cap rate: enterprise value of roughly $15.82B (market cap $10.8B plus net debt $4.679B plus $159.8M preferred plus $183.1M redeemable non-controlling interests) against annualized Q2 POI of $928.6M gives 5.87% before corporate overhead, or roughly 5.6% after allocating G&A. Adjusting for $329.8M of non-earning construction in progress lowers the effective going-in yield modestly.

Peer context is imperfect because the comparable table carries GAAP P/E rather than P/FFO, and one constituent (MAC) has negative earnings. On the reported basis FRT's 21.5x sits below the 27.6x peer median, with a 3.6-3.7% yield against a 3.9% median. On the more relevant P/FFO basis, 16.6x places FRT modestly above lower-rent open-air peers such as Brixmor and roughly in line with Regency, and materially below the premium FRT commanded historically. The company's own compensation plan includes a "relative Nareit FFO per diluted share multiple premium" metric, and that premium has compressed — Mizuho's June 2026 upgrade to Outperform reportedly cited it as having evaporated on lower growth and weaker spreads (a secondary-source characterization we were unable to confirm against the primary note).

The five-year record frames the debate precisely. FFO per share grew from $5.57 (2021) to $7.22 (2025), about +29.6% cumulative or a 6.7% CAGR, while cumulative TSR was +34.06% against the company's own selected peer index at +54.56%. Nearly all fundamental growth was offset by multiple compression. The bull reading is mean reversion toward a restored premium. The bear reading is that the prior premium was not structurally justified once the refinancing drag and the office/residential components are properly capitalized — a sum-of-the-parts investor would apply a higher cap rate to Cisco/NetApp office space and to 8.7% residential rental income, which mechanically lowers the blended multiple without implying management failure. We weight both.

Consensus targets run $118 low, $131.14 mean, $132 median, $149 high. The distribution is unusually tight at the bottom, with the low target within 5% of spot. The analyst recommendation mix has shifted modestly positive over three months (strong buy plus buy from 11 to 13; holds from 8 to 7). Because FRT trades at a mid-teens multiple with rising estimates on the FFO line, we treat consensus as a reasonable anchor rather than a ceiling — but 5% mean upside plus a 3.7% yield is a total return in the high single digits, not asymmetric.

The macro discount rate argues against multiple expansion in the near term. Long-end rates and Fed policy remain a headwind for rate-sensitive REITs. A REIT whose earnings growth is already capped by coupon migration is a direct casualty of a steeper long end. Valuation score: 5.3 — fairly priced, with the identifiable catalyst (a credible 2028 growth framework) unverified.

## Competitive Landscape

FRT competes with Regency, Kimco, Brixmor, Phillips Edison and Kite in open-air retail, and partially with urban mixed-use owners at Santana Row, Pike & Rose and Assembly Row. Its differentiators are measurable: the highest average base rent in the peer set at $32.79/sf, the smallest asset count (about 103 versus several hundred at Kimco), and the deepest in-house entitlement and redevelopment capability, evidenced by repeated completion vintages at the same assets (Santana Row 2002, 2009, 2016, 2020; Pike & Rose 1963, 2014, 2018, 2020, 2025). Growth comes from adding density to owned land rather than from aggregating assets.

Trajectory is mixed. Improving: leasing spreads accelerated year over year (+11% to +15%) while TI per foot fell, new-lease spreads hit +28% straight-line in Q2, comparable volume set quarterly and trailing-twelve-month records, and the credit market repriced FRT tighter. Flat or eroding: occupancy has been effectively unchanged for twelve months despite record signings, residential leased rate slipped 20bp, and the relative FFO multiple premium the company explicitly manages toward has compressed for four consecutive years.

The high-rent model carries a symmetric exposure that deserves naming. Rents of $32.79/sf portfolio-wide and $57/sf at Santana Row imply high occupancy-cost ratios and thinner tenant margins than a deeper-value portfolio. If discretionary spending softens, high-rent centers can see spreads compress faster than low-rent ones, even where credit quality is superior. Score: 6.7.

## Risk Assessment

**Refinancing at higher coupons.** The clearest quantified headwind and the binding constraint on FFO growth. Cash interest paid rose to about $95.6M from $82.4M. The $1,157.5M 2028 tower is larger than the 2027 maturities and is the item to watch. This is finite and quantifiable — once legacy sub-2% paper is retired, the same operating performance drops more to the bottom line — but it caps growth through at least 2028 in the current rate regime.

**Washington-area exposure to federal spending.** Maryland, Virginia and D.C. account for 34.3% of GLA. The 10-K does not quantify federal-employment or federal-spending sensitivity, and we flag it as inference rather than a disclosed exposure — but the Bethesda/Rockville/Falls Church/Arlington/Alexandria density of the portfolio makes it a live question a national retail REIT would not face.

**Unquantified office and residential inside a retail multiple.** Cisco and NetApp are Santana Row office tenants; Santana West is office-adjacent development in a weak San Jose office market with lease-up status not disclosed in the sections reviewed; residential was 8.7% of 2025 rental income. Total office GLA is not published. This is a real valuation gap, not a hypothetical one.

**Signed-not-occupied conversion.** The 230bp gap is contracted, but the 10-K warns explicitly that some tenants will not take possession and that some leases will not commence until 2028 or later. Our $21.9M estimate applies portfolio-average rent to vacant space and should be treated as a sensitivity.

**Tenant credit concentration by category.** No tenant exceeds 2.4% of ABR, but exposure clusters in casual dining, off-price apparel, furniture, electronics (Best Buy at four-plus centers) and books (Barnes & Noble at four). Management's own framing is that multiple simultaneous filings could be significant.

**Governance and succession.** Donald Wood has led the Trust since 2003 and now holds both President and CEO titles following the former President and COO's departure (last Form 4 dated December 31, 2024). Core FFO still carries "executive transition costs" as a standing adjustment category. A Form 8-K dated November 10, 2025 under Item 5.02 could not be retrieved, so whether a successor-track executive has been named is unresolved. Compensation actually paid did fall to $8.37M from $11.34M as the stock declined, which is the pay-for-performance mechanism working correctly.

**Capital allocation tension.** A $300M repurchase authorization from April 2025 remains entirely unused through June 30, 2026, while the Trust issued 493,374 shares via ATM in Q2 at a weighted average $123.92 (about $61.1M gross, roughly $688.9M capacity remaining). Issuing equity at a ~6% implied cost to fund development yielding more is defensible; we do not infer a valuation view from the authorization's existence or non-use. What is verified is that issuance occurred and repurchase did not.

**Exchangeable notes.** $485M of 3.25% notes due January 2029 exchange at approximately $122.80 per share (1,000 ÷ 8.1436), so the stock is about 1.4% above the strike. Maximum contingent shares of 3,949,646 equal 4.5% of the 86.9M outstanding, or 4.3% post-issuance ownership dilution. Settlement is cash up to principal with cash-or-shares at FRT's election above it, and capped calls offset share dilution to $143.26 — so realistic dilution between $122.80 and $143.26 is near zero. A monitoring item, not a live valuation issue.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and offers no confirmation for either bull or bear. Near-term ATM IV of 17.0% sits essentially on top of one-year realized volatility of 17.3% (spread -0.3%), the IV percentile within the chain is 11%, put/call skew is balanced at -0.2%, and term structure is flat at 17.0% across one to six months. ATM IV compressed from 21.1% to 17.0% between July 29 and July 31, a standard post-earnings vol crush now that guidance is out. Put/call open interest of 0.23 is call-tilted, and short interest is effectively zero (22,581 shares, 0.0% of float). Total chain liquidity is thin at 56 contracts across three expirations, so read this as an absence of stress signal rather than a positive vote. The market is pricing FRT as a low-volatility income vehicle with no near-term binary event, which is consistent with our fundamental read and with an income-oriented HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($146-$149).** The refinancing drag is finite and quantifiable, and the multiple premium is recoverable. Core FFO growth reaccelerates from +4.3% (2025) to the guided 5.9%-7.1% (2026) and holds mid-to-high single digits as the 230bp signed-not-occupied gap converts (~$21.9M, ~$0.25/share) and as +17% trailing cash spreads roll through the rent roll. Leverage continues toward 5.0x, releasing the balance-sheet discount. Long rates ease, shrinking the 2027-28 coupon step-up. The Investor Day 2028 framework proves credible, sell-side follows Mizuho's June upgrade, and the multiple returns toward 18.5x on roughly $7.90 of 2027 Core FFO, or about $146 — near the $149 high target — plus a ~3.7% yield.

**Bear case ($105-$110).** The derating is structurally correct. Coupon migration keeps FFO growth near 5% through 2028 while occupancy stays flat and termination fees, which contributed a meaningful share of Q2's 2.8% comparable POI growth, normalize lower. A consumer slowdown compresses spreads at a portfolio whose $32.79/sf average rent implies elevated tenant occupancy costs. Federal spending cuts weigh on the 34.3% of GLA in the Washington block. Sum-of-the-parts pressure builds as investors capitalize the Santana Row office component and Santana West at office cap rates. The long end steepens further, and a 3.7% yielder rerates to 14x on $7.70, near $108 — roughly 13% below spot before dividends.

**Base case ($131).** 16.6x on approximately $7.90 of 2027 Core FFO (2026 midpoint $7.52 grown ~5%), which reconciles with the $131.14 consensus mean and $132 median. Total return of roughly 9% over twelve months, comprising 5% price and 3.7% yield. Adequate, unremarkable, and materially dependent on long rates not steepening further.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (late October) is the natural checkpoint, with the next FOMC and any 2027 refinancing announcement as interim inputs. The 2028 growth framework from the May 21 Investor Day should be retrieved and evaluated before any rating change in either direction.

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. Occupied rate improving to 94.5% or better with the leased rate holding at or above 96%, evidencing that the signed-not-occupied pipeline is converting to cash rent rather than stalling — worth roughly $0.25 per share of FFO.
2. A verified 2028 FFO or AFFO per-share growth target implying sustained growth of 7% or better, bridged by disclosed components (SNO conversion, development deliveries, quantified refinancing assumptions), combined with a share price at or below 15.5x forward Core FFO — approximately $118-$120.
3. Pre-funding or forward-locking of the 2027-28 maturities at a spread implying less than 150bp of coupon step-up, which would remove the principal cap on FFO growth.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Comparable POI growth falling below 2.0% for two consecutive quarters, or 2026 guidance being trimmed below $7.45, indicating the leasing engine is no longer offsetting interest migration.
2. Occupancy declining below 93.0% alongside a falling leased rate, which would signal tenant credit stress rather than commencement timing.
3. Disclosure of material Santana West office write-down, impairment, or lease-up failure, or evidence that Washington-area centers are seeing measurable tenant fallout from federal spending reductions.
4. A dividend increase announced at a payout above 75% of Core FFO, or reliance on ATM equity to fund the distribution.

## Conclusion

This is our fifth consecutive HOLD on FRT, and the composition of the call has changed even as the label has not. In February and May the argument was that a good business was working through a rate-driven derating with adequate compensation for the wait. Today the operating evidence is stronger — record leasing volume, +17% trailing cash spreads with falling concessions, half a turn of deleveraging, a tighter revolver, a 59th dividend increase from 62% coverage — while the shares have advanced roughly 13% since April to within 3% of their 52-week high. Compensation for the identified risks has narrowed.

The overall score falls to 6.0 from 6.2 on valuation alone. Financial health and competitive position are unchanged to modestly better: the balance-sheet trajectory is favorable and the credit market ratified it in April with more capacity, a tighter spread and a longer tenor. Management quality stays near 5.9 — the dividend discipline and disposition execution are genuine positives, but five years of TSR underperformance against a self-selected peer group, an unresolved succession question, no identified insider open-market buying, and equity issuance at $124 against an untouched buyback authorization together do not support a higher mark. Risk factors remain the weakest dimension at 5.5 because two material exposures are unquantified in the filings we could review: total office GLA including Santana West lease-up, and the portfolio's sensitivity to federal-employment and federal-spending trends across 34.3% of GLA.

We would own FRT for the income: a ~3.7% forward yield covered at 62% of Core FFO, backed by a 59-year increase streak and 96.1% leased high-barrier real estate, with mid-single-digit FFO growth on top. We would not press for multiple expansion at 16.6x with $1.85B of 3%-and-under paper still to roll into a higher-rate market. Add on weakness toward $115-$118, where the forward yield approaches 4% and the multiple compresses below 15.5x; trim into the high $130s absent verified evidence that the 2028 growth framework supports sustained high-single-digit per-share compounding.
