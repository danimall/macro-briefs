**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Grainger enters the second half of 2026 with the best operating momentum it has shown in three years and the most demanding valuation it has carried in that span. Q1 2026 delivered net sales of $4,742M (+10.1% reported, +12.2% daily organic constant currency), 110bp of consolidated operating-margin expansion to 16.7%, diluted EPS of $11.65 versus a $10.31 consensus (+13.0% surprise), and a full-year revenue guidance raise to roughly $19.4B at the midpoint. Consensus FY2026 EPS has moved to $45.63 from $43.65 ninety days ago (+4.5%), and FY2027 to $50.48 (+3.9%). Estimates are rising, the balance sheet was upgraded to A1 by Moody's between the 10-K and 10-Q filings, and scheduled debt principal is $126M in 2026 followed by zero from 2027 through 2030.

The offset is what the growth is made of and what it costs. High-Touch Solutions North America — 78% of revenue and essentially all of the high-margin profit pool — grew 10.5% in Q1, and the 10-Q attributes that to an "equal contribution of 5% for both volume and price." A 5-point price contribution from an MRO distributor in a market management models at down 1.5% to flat is most plausibly tariff cost recovery, which annualizes away. Underneath, FY2025 was weaker than the adjusted headlines suggest: adjusted operating margin contracted 45bp to 15.00% on 4.5% revenue growth, HTSNA adjusted operating earnings *fell* $46M, adjusted EPS growth of 1.3% came mainly from a 2.0% reduction in diluted shares, and management's own company-selected metric — adjusted ROIC — has declined three consecutive years from 42.8% to 39.1%. Free cash flow has fallen two straight years, from $1,586M in 2023 to $1,331M in 2025, while FY2025 capital returns of $1,512M ran at 114% of FCF and were part-funded by drawing cash from $1,036M to $585M.

At $1,382.22 the stock trades at 30.3x FY2026 consensus, 27.4x FY2027, ~22.7x EV/adjusted EBITDA and a 2.0-2.7% FCF yield. The sell-side mean target of $1,292 is 6.5% *below* spot, the rating mix has drifted to 3 buys / 14 holds / 2 strong sells from 4 buys / 11 holds a month ago, and Barclays raised its target to $1,171 in May while keeping an Underweight. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 32.2% against 25.6% realized, in backwardation versus 25.5% at six months, prices a ±6.4% move around an imminent Q2 report — the same event that produced a 10.3% single-day decline in August 2025. This is a high-quality compounder whose multiple has already absorbed the earnings recovery. Fourth consecutive HOLD.

## Company Overview

Grainger is a broad-line distributor of maintenance, repair and operating products, incorporated in Illinois in 1928 and now concentrated in North America and Japan following the Q4 2025 exit from the United Kingdom (Cromwell sold December 17, 2025; Zoro U.K. closed).

**High-Touch Solutions N.A.** generated $13,993M of FY2025 sales (78% of total) at a 41.7% gross margin and 16.82% operating margin. It serves mid-size and large customers through roughly 2 million products, distribution-center-led next-day and same-day fulfillment, a field sales organization, eProcurement integration into customer purchasing systems, and KeepStock vendor-managed on-site inventory. Approximately 19% of U.S. stocked product sales are private label (DAYTON, CONDOR, WESTWARD, SPEEDAIRE and others).

**Endless Assortment** generated $3,625M (20%) at a 29.9% gross margin and 9.52% reported operating margin, comprising Zoro (U.S., ~13 million SKUs) and MonotaRO (Japan, ~29 million SKUs, majority-owned, separately listed in Tokyo and consolidated on a one-month lag).

Structurally the business is diversified at the customer level and concentrated at the policy level: ~4.6 million customers with none above 10% of sales, more than 5,000 primary suppliers with none above 5% of purchases, no product category above 20% of sales, while Government is 15% of consolidated revenue and 19% of HTSNA. Manufacturing is the largest end market at 30%. Roughly 81% of FY2025 sales originated in the U.S. Service revenue is ~1% of the total; this is product distribution.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** $15,228M (2022) → $16,478M (2023) → $17,168M (2024) → $17,942M (2025) → $4,742M in Q1 2026 (+10.1%). Daily organic constant-currency growth accelerated from 4.9% in FY2025 to 12.2% in Q1 2026.

**The FY2025 result requires unpacking in both directions.** Reported net income attributable to Grainger fell 10.6% to $1,706M and diluted EPS fell 8.6% to $35.40, driven by $196M of one-time SG&A charges from the U.K. exit ($186M Cromwell divestiture loss, $10M Zoro U.K. closure) with no tax benefit on either. Excluding these, adjusted operating earnings were $2,691M (+1.4%) and adjusted EPS $39.48 (+1.3%). A GAAP screen materially overstates the deterioration.

The adjusted figures, however, are not flattering. Adjusted operating margin was 15.00% ($2,691M / $17,942M) versus 15.45% in 2024 — 45bp of contraction on 4.5% growth. HTSNA operating margin fell 56bp to 16.82% and HTSNA adjusted operating earnings declined $46M. Gross margin fell 30bp to 39.1%. Adjusted SG&A rose 5.2% against 4.5% revenue growth, with advertising expense up from $638M (2023) to $813M (2025) — 4.5% of sales versus 3.9%, a 27% two-year increase against 8.9% revenue growth. The accurate characterization: the high-margin 78% of the business shrank in operating profit on 2.6% organic growth, and adjusted EPS growth came from a 2.0% share-count reduction rather than operations.

**Q1 2026 is a real change in trajectory, with two qualifications.** Gross margin +30bp to 40.0%, SG&A down 80bp to 23.3% of sales, operating margin +110bp to 16.7%, EPS +18.2%. HTSNA segment operating margin improved ~68bp to 18.34%; that improvement is not explained by the divestiture, since Cromwell sat in "Other," so it appears operationally genuine. EA grew 19.6% reported / 21.9% organic with operating margin up to 10.61% from 8.70%. The qualifications: (a) the 10-Q states part of the SG&A improvement reflects "a benefit related to the exit from the U.K. market" — removing a unit that posted a $204M reported / $18M adjusted operating loss lifts consolidated margin arithmetically; and (b) 5 of HTSNA's 10 points of growth is price.

**Cash flow is the weakest part of the picture.** Operating cash flow: $2,031M (2023) → $2,111M (2024) → $2,015M (2025). Capex rose from $445M to $684M (2.7% → 3.8% of sales) for U.S. and MonotaRO supply-chain capacity. Free cash flow therefore fell from $1,586M to $1,331M, a 16% cumulative decline while revenue grew 8.9%; FCF margin compressed from 9.6% to 7.4%. The FY2025 OCF decline came from working capital — receivables −$190M, inventories −$147M — not the non-cash U.K. loss, which was added back. Q1 2026 OCF of $739M (+14.4%) should not be annualized: income taxes payable rose from $49M to $198M and accounts payable built $253M, both timing items, and management's own $2.1-2.3B FY2026 OCF guidance implies $1.4-1.6B across the remaining three quarters.

**Balance sheet quality is high and improving.** Total debt $2,411M at 3/31/26, of which $2,300M is fixed-rate senior notes maturing 2034-2047. Debt/total capitalization fell 42.9% → 37.5% → 35.7%. Altman Z 4.23. Current ratio 2.6-2.8. Debt agreements carry no financial performance covenants. Moody's moved corporate and senior unsecured ratings from A2 to A1 between the two filings, and a new $1.25B commercial paper program was established May 1, 2026 (a subsequent event in the 10-Q), adding cheap working-capital flexibility as receivables absorb cash. The screening debt/equity of 2.4x is inconsistent with the filed balance sheet; debt-only leverage is $2,488M / $3,736M ≈ 0.67x, and total liabilities to equity attributable to Grainger is ~1.40x. Zero scheduled principal from 2027 through 2030 limits refinancing risk this decade.

Reported ROE of 48.4% is an artifact of $12,558M of cumulative treasury stock against $3,736M of book equity and should not be read as an incremental return. The relevant series is management's adjusted ROIC: 42.8% → 41.4% → 39.1%. Still exceptional in absolute terms, but declining, and the 2023→2024 leg predates the U.K. charge, so the decline is not purely a one-off distortion.

**Earnings surprise record** is mixed: +13.0% (Q1 2026), −1.2%, +1.6%, −1.9%. Two beats, two misses in four quarters. Only the most recent quarter is a large beat, which argues for holding confidence in consensus rather than assuming systematic conservatism.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth case rests on four elements.

**First, share gain in a shrinking market.** Management's proprietary multifactor model put the 2025 U.S. MRO market at −1.5% to −0.5% and models 2026 at −1.5% to flat. HTSNA volume grew 1.4% in FY2025 — roughly 250bp of outgrowth against a stated long-term target of 400-500bp, a shortfall management has acknowledged. Q1 2026's 5% HTSNA volume growth against that same flat-to-down market is a meaningful step up and the single most encouraging data point in the file. The next two prints will show whether it holds.

**Second, Endless Assortment compounding.** EA grew 15.6% in FY2025 and 21.9% organically in Q1 2026, with MonotaRO up 18.4% in local currency and enterprise customers up 25%. MonotaRO funded DC expansion with ¥13B of 2025 term loans at 1.27% and ¥7.5B in 2026 at 1.74%, including a new Mito facility that management says will nearly double Japanese shipping capacity. Japan revenue moved from $1,893M (2024) to $2,173M (2025) to $589M in Q1 2026 (+22.7%). Sub-2% yen funding for a business growing 18%+ is high-quality growth capital.

**Third, self-funded commercial investment.** More than 85,000 net SKUs added in 2025 (described as the largest HTSNA assortment increase in nearly a decade), ~110 sellers added in 2025 and 300+ since 2022, plus a push into factory automation. These are the levers closing the outgrowth gap, and they are also the SG&A line that compressed FY2025 margins.

**Fourth, the LIFO mechanic, the most underappreciated swing factor.** Approximately 80% of inventory is on LIFO. Note 1 discloses that on FIFO, inventories would have been $939M higher at 12/31/25 versus $804M a year earlier, and net earnings $102M higher in 2025 versus $26M in 2024 and $58M in 2023. That $102M is roughly $2.13 per diluted share of FY2025 earnings suppressed relative to a FIFO presentation, and the escalation from $26M maps onto tariff-driven cost inflation. If product cost inflation moderates while the 5% price realization persists, gross margin should expand faster than the guided 40-90bp of operating-margin improvement implies. Q1's 30bp of gross-margin expansion — against management's Q4 guidance that Q1 gross margin would decline sequentially on lingering LIFO — is early evidence of the turn. The limits: a LIFO reserve is not stored profit awaiting release; it unwinds only if quantities fall or costs decline, and if tariffs escalate the drag extends.

**Estimate revisions confirm positive momentum but not a re-rating trigger.** FY2026 consensus is up 4.5% over 90 days and 0.1% over 30 days; FY2027 up 3.9% and 0.2%. Consensus FY2026 EPS of $45.63 implies 15.6% growth over the FY2025 adjusted base, and FY2027 $50.48 implies a further 10.6%. That is solid mid-teens-to-low-double-digit earnings growth. It is not the 30%+ revision velocity that signals systematic underestimation, and the 30-day flatlining matters: the multiple expanded 11.4% since late May while estimates moved 0.1-0.2%.

Against the peer set — ITW 23.6x on a 2.6% yield, DOV 27.3x, XYL 27.1x, OTIS 18.6x, IR 49.6x, median 27.1x — Grainger's 30.3x forward multiple carries a premium to the median even before adjusting for the fact that peer multiples in the table are trailing. The PEG on FY2026 growth is 30.3 / 15.6 ≈ 1.9; on FY2027 growth, 27.4 / 10.6 ≈ 2.6. Both sit above the threshold at which rising estimates alone justify paying up.

Two disciplines matter here. Prior appreciation is not a valuation input: the stock's 30% twelve-month gain is irrelevant except insofar as the multiple, not the price, has changed, and it has, from roughly 26x forward in late May to 30x now on essentially unchanged 30-day estimates. Analyst targets function as floors only when a stock looks cheap against improving estimates; at 30x forward with a mean target of $1,292 sitting 6.5% *below* spot, a median of $1,320, and a rating mix that degraded from 4 buys / 11 holds to 3 buys / 14 holds in a month, consensus is expressing skepticism about durability.

What would make the market wrong? A credible path exists: LIFO drag reversing ($2.13/share of FY2025 suppression), HTSNA operating margin reaching the guided 16.9-17.4% with the Q1 18.34% run rate as evidence, EA scaling toward 10.5%, and volume outgrowth holding at Q1's 5% pace. Stack those and FY2027 EPS of $53-54 becomes reachable, supporting $1,600 at 30x. What makes that a bull case rather than a base case is that half of current HTSNA growth is price recovery of a specific cost shock, and the company is delivering roughly half its own outgrowth ambition while return on invested capital erodes. A 30x multiple and a 2% FCF yield leave no room for that resolution to disappoint. Valuation score 4.5 — stretched relative to demonstrated durable growth, with the LIFO optionality the only identified reason the market may be underpricing near-term earnings power.

## Competitive Landscape

Ranked by defensibility as the filings support:

1. **Supply chain density.** More than 1.5 million products stocked across DCs and branches, with same-day shipping and next-day delivery described in the 10-K as integral to strategy. Capital-intensive, slow to replicate, and the reason capex rose to $684M.
2. **Procurement integration.** eProcurement wired into customer purchasing platforms plus KeepStock on-site vendor-managed inventory create non-price switching costs. This is the least visible and most durable element of the moat, and it is what allows 5% pricing to stick with enterprise accounts.
3. **Private label, ~19% of U.S. stocked product sales.** Structurally higher margin and unavailable elsewhere, but disproportionately import-sourced, and management has flagged tariff-related competitive pressure on certain private-label items. Tariffs attack the highest-margin part of the mix.
4. **Assortment breadth in EA** (13M Zoro SKUs, 29M MonotaRO SKUs). The most contestable advantage. SKU count is what large eCommerce platforms match, and the risk factors concede dependence on search-engine rankings and third-party digital-advertising practices to convert breadth into traffic, which is why advertising is now 4.5% of sales.

The bifurcated model is genuinely differentiated: a 42%-gross-margin, sales-rep-intensive enterprise business and a 30%-gross-margin, zero-sales-force eCommerce business sharing DC infrastructure, serving complex procurement and long-tail demand without cannibalization. Few competitors run both at scale. The permanent cost is consolidated margin dilution: EA's share of revenue rose from 18% (2023-24) to 20% (2025) to 21% (Q1 2026), and continued EA outperformance dilutes blended margin even when both segments improve. Management has framed the margin path as "gradual rather than rapid" for this reason.

Trajectory assessment: Grainger is gaining share, but at ~250bp against a 400-500bp target, with declining incremental returns on capital and a rising cost of customer acquisition. Score 7.0 — improving on volume in Q1, structurally sound, but not widening the moat at the rate the multiple implies.

## Risk Assessment

**Government exposure — 15% of consolidated revenue, 19% of HTSNA.** Both the FY2025 and Q1 2026 MD&A explicitly flag "shifting government budget policies and priorities at the municipal, state, and national levels" and government-shutdown impact. A dedicated risk factor notes government contracts may be terminated, reduced or modified at any time with or without cause, and Note 14 discloses potential inquiries or audits related to contract administration and pricing. No specific matter is pending. This is correlated policy risk across a large revenue block spanning civilian and municipal buyers. [unverified — the brief does not support the specific characterization of federal procurement being restructured toward defense via fixed-price mandates]

**Tariff/LIFO two-sidedness.** The same mechanic that suppressed $102M of FY2025 earnings extends if tariffs escalate. The risk factors state plainly that Grainger "may not be able to pass rising product costs to customers if those customers have product or supplier alternatives."

**Durability of the 5% price contribution.** The 10-Q states the fact and not the cause. If it is tariff pass-through, it annualizes away in 2027 and consensus FY2027 growth of 10.6% becomes hard to hit without a volume acceleration. This is the central unresolved question.

**Structural mix dilution.** EA at ~30% gross / ~10% operating margin growing at 3-4x HTSNA's rate arithmetically compresses blended margin. Rising EA mix is *not* diversification — customer concentration disclosure is unchanged and government concentration held roughly flat at 19% (FY2025) / 18% (Q1 2026).

**Cost inflation with limited pricing control.** Higher-than-anticipated healthcare expense was a named driver of Q4 2025's 120bp HTSNA margin decline, and advertising rose 27% over two years against 8.9% revenue growth.

**Tax step-up.** FY2026 guided to ~25% versus FY2025 adjusted 23.7%; Q1 confirmed 25.1%. Roughly $0.75 of EPS headwind, already in guidance.

**AI as a two-sided force.** The 10-K carries a substantive risk factor stating management's belief that AI proliferation "will have a significant impact on customer preferences and market dynamics." For a distributor whose moat partly rests on search-and-select expertise and customers' procurement friction, AI-mediated purchasing could compress the value of assortment navigation. Not sizeable today; not priced at 30x either.

**Lower-order items:** Canada goodwill ($119M) is the auditor's sole critical audit matter, immaterial at 1.3% of assets; cybersecurity risk is elevated by eProcurement lateral-access exposure but no material incident has occurred; MonotaRO's one-month reporting lag means Japanese results reach the Tokyo market before Grainger's filings.

**Governance verification gap:** a May 15, 2025 8-K (Item 3.03, material modification of securityholders' rights, with Item 5.03) and a December 12, 2025 8-K (Item 5.03) were identified but not read; Restated Articles were filed as Exhibit 3.1 to the Q1 10-Q. The nature of the change is unresolved and should be checked before any position increase.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 32.2% against 25.6% one-year realized is rich by 6.6 points and sits at the 64th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation — 32.2% at one month, 27.4% at three, 25.5% at six — characteristic of a dated event rather than a regime change. The event is the Q2 print: the nearest expiry (August 21) prices a ±6.4% move, the one-month put/call volume ratio is put-heavy at 1.29 while the aggregate volume ratio is 0.53 and OI ratio 0.46 (call-tilted), and ATM IV has risen from 23.0% to 32.2% since late May while the stock advanced 11.4% and P/C open interest ratio rose from 0.28 to 0.46. Rising IV with rising price and rising put open interest reads as hedging of gains into a binary print, not a directional bear bet.

The precedent supports caution: the August 1, 2025 session produced a 10.3% single-day decline ($1,039.54 to $932.44), and the February 12, 2026 session saw a 7.7% drop ($1,202.47 to $1,116.60). Six-month skew of −2.1% and 14.0% implied move suggest no structural fear beyond the event. Practical read: implied volatility is expensive, so this is not an attractive moment to buy premium or to add stock ahead of the print. Short interest of 3.0% of float and 3.2 days to cover means there is no crowded-short dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $1,600 (+16%)

Q1 2026 marks the exit from a genuine trough year. FY2025 was compressed by three identifiable, non-structural forces: a contracting MRO market, negative LIFO price-cost worth $102M of suppressed earnings, and an unanticipated healthcare cost spike. All three ease in 2026. HTSNA volume growth of 5% against a flat-to-down market is a step change in outgrowth, driven by 85,000 net new SKUs, 110 added sellers and the factory-automation push. EA compounds above 20% with MonotaRO's Mito DC nearly doubling Japanese capacity, funded at sub-2% yen rates. LIFO drag reverses in H2 2026 as cost inflation moderates while price realization holds, delivering gross-margin expansion above the guided 40-90bp of operating improvement. FY2027 EPS reaches $53-54; the market pays 30x for a widening-moat A1 credit with 39% ROIC, zero refinancing risk through 2030, no financial covenants, a ~$4.0B remaining buyback authorization and a dividend growing at 10%. Target $1,600.

### Bear Case — $1,120 (−19%)

The 5-point price contribution is tariff recovery, it annualizes away through 2027, and underlying volume reverts toward the 1-2% pace of FY2025 as the MRO market contracts another 1.5%. Consolidated margin then faces the full weight of EA mix dilution, 4.5%-of-sales advertising intensity, healthcare inflation and a 25% tax rate without the price offset. Adjusted ROIC continues the 42.8% → 41.4% → 39.1% descent as the $550-650M capex program depreciates into a slower growth base. FCF, already down 16% over two years to a 7.4% margin, fails to reach the guided $1.45-1.75B, forcing a choice between the ~$1B buyback pace and further cash drawdown. Government revenue at 15% of the total absorbs a municipal and federal budget squeeze. Consensus FY2027 EPS drops to $45; the multiple compresses toward ITW/DOV at 24-25x. Target $1,120. A recession scenario with MRO contracting 4-5% takes EPS toward $42 and the multiple to 20x, implying a tail near $850.

### Base Case — $1,420 (+2.7%)

28.1x FY2027 consensus EPS of $50.48. Volume outgrowth holds in the 3-5% range but price decays through 2027; margin expands to the middle of the guided 15.4-15.9% band rather than the top; LIFO provides a partial H2 tailwind; the multiple holds near current levels because credit quality and dividend growth support it but does not expand because outgrowth stays below the 400-500bp target. Total return over twelve months is roughly 3-4% including the 0.72% dividend. That expected return does not compensate for a 19% bear case.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q2 2026 report is imminent (consensus $11.28-11.30, +13% YoY), with the August 21 options chain pricing a ±6.4% move. The Q3 print in late October is the more decisive test, because it is the first quarter in which the tariff-driven price contribution begins to lap a comparable base.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following three:**
1. **HTSNA volume growth of 4%+ with a price contribution declining below 4%** in Q2 or Q3 — this separates share gain from cost recovery and is the single most important data point. Paired with HTSNA operating margin holding above 17.4% (the top of guidance).
2. **Evidence of LIFO reversal in reported gross margin** — consolidated gross margin above 40.5% with management confirming positive price-cost on a LIFO basis, which would put FY2027 EPS above $53 and reduce the forward multiple to 26x or better on unchanged price.
3. **A price of $1,150-1,200 or below on unchanged estimates**, which would place the stock at 23-24x FY2027 consensus — roughly ITW's multiple for a business with better growth and a stronger credit. The Q1 buyback was executed at $1,045-1,136 average, so management itself has been a buyer in that zone.

**Downgrade to AVOID if:**
1. **HTSNA daily constant-currency growth falls below 4%** while price contribution remains above 3% — implying volume outgrowth has collapsed back to the FY2025 pace of 1.4% and share gain is not durable.
2. **Adjusted operating margin fails to reach 15.4%** (the low end of guidance) for FY2026, confirming that FY2025's 45bp contraction was structural rather than cyclical.
3. **FY2026 free cash flow comes in below $1.3B** (a third consecutive decline) while buybacks continue at the ~$1B pace, requiring further cash drawdown or commercial paper to fund capital returns.
4. **Adjusted ROIC declines a fourth consecutive year** below ~37%, indicating the supply-chain capex is not earning its cost of capital.

**Do not add here.** Implied volatility is 6.6 points rich to realized in backwardation ahead of a print that moved the stock 10.3% a year ago; the mean sell-side target sits 6.5% below spot; and the 30-day estimate revisions are flat while the multiple expanded 11%. If owned, hold through the print; if not owned, the risk/reward improves materially in the $1,150-1,250 zone.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** The score moves from 6.8 in late May to 6.2, and the reason is valuation rather than fundamentals. Since the prior review the stock has advanced 11.4% while FY2026 and FY2027 consensus estimates moved 0.1% and 0.2% respectively — the re-rating outran the revisions, compressing forward return. Financial health remains the strongest dimension: A1-rated, 35.7% debt/capitalization, no financial covenants, zero scheduled principal 2027-2030, and a new $1.25B commercial paper facility for working-capital flexibility. Competitive position improved operationally in Q1 (HTSNA segment margin +68bp on genuine share gain, not divestiture arithmetic) but remains capped by outgrowth at roughly half the stated target and a third consecutive year of declining adjusted ROIC.

Three things must be true for the current multiple to work: the 5% HTSNA price contribution must prove durable or be replaced by volume; the LIFO drag must reverse rather than extend; and blended margin must expand despite EA mix dilution. Q1 2026 is consistent with all three and confirms none. At 30.3x FY2026 and 27.4x FY2027 consensus, a 2.0-2.7% FCF yield, and a sell-side mean target below spot, the market is paying for the resolution before it arrives. Hold the quality, collect the 0.72% dividend and the ~2.25% total shareholder yield, and let the Q2 and Q3 prints answer the price-durability question. The buy point is the one management itself has been using: the low-to-mid $1,100s.
