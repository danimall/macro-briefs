**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

DigitalOcean has executed one of the cleaner thematic re-ratings of this cycle. The stock has risen from the high-$20s in mid-2025 to roughly $173-178, a gain exceeding 530%, driven almost entirely by the company's repositioning from a developer-focused IaaS/PaaS vendor into an "AI-native / agentic inference cloud." The fundamental data underneath the move is real: Q1 CY2026 revenue grew 22.4% YoY to $257.9M, a clear reversal of the mid-teens deceleration that defined 2023-2024, and AI Customer ARR tripled to $170M from $53M a year earlier. Management raised FY2026 guidance, BofA lifted its target to $200 from $107, and the company carries a four-quarter streak of earnings beats.

The problem is that the price now embeds the success case. At ~$173 the stock trades at roughly 79x trailing EPS and ~144x consensus FY2026 EPS of $1.20, against a peer median P/E near 45 for businesses with different growth and margin profiles. The bull thesis requires AI inference demand to ramp fast enough to fill ~$1.9B of committed but not-yet-commenced data-center leases (terms running ~11 years through June 2027) at a profit, while gross margin has already compressed 500bps YoY to 56% as the company spends ahead of revenue. Q1 non-GAAP EPS declined to $0.44 from $0.56 a year earlier. The dilution stack is also fully live: a March 2026 follow-on added ~13% to the share count, the 2030 converts are deep in the money with capped-call protection only to $66.51, and the CEO's MRSU tranches are essentially all in the money at $172.

The decisive tell is insider behavior. There is no open-market buying. The largest strategic holder, Access Industries, sold over 3.7M shares in May 2026 at $147-162 (~$500M+), the CFO sold into the post-earnings spike, and management received large March equity grants before the run. Combined with a stock sitting at or near the analyst mean target ($178.77) and median ($179), the asymmetry has been wrung out. We rate DOCN HOLD. The growth thesis is genuine and the company belongs to the one sector where selection has historically added value, but at this multiple the risk/reward is balanced rather than favorable, and the entry point has already been monetized by those closest to the business.

## Company Overview

DigitalOcean operates a cloud infrastructure platform targeting what it calls "Digital Native Enterprises" (DNEs), customers spending more than $500/month whose needs are not fully served by hyperscalers. The product stack spans IaaS (Droplets, Spaces storage, networking), PaaS/SaaS (Managed Hosting via the Cloudways acquisition, Managed Databases, Kubernetes, App Platform, a 350+ app Marketplace), and an AI layer marketed as the "Gradient AI Agentic Cloud": GPU Droplets on AMD and NVIDIA silicon, Bare Metal GPUs from the Paperspace acquisition, and a serverless Gradient platform layered over third-party foundation models. The company is an infrastructure and serverless-orchestration provider, not a frontier model developer.

Scale: ~22,000 DNE customers as of Q1 2026, presence in ~190 countries, roughly two-thirds of revenue international, and 1,462 employees. It operates nine geographic regions and is expanding its US footprint (Memphis, Richmond, Kansas City planned for 2026). The addressable market it cites, IDC's sub-500-employee IaaS/PaaS segment, is $138B in 2025 growing to $251B by 2028, a 22% CAGR. It competes against AWS/Azure/GCP at the top, niche hosts (OVHcloud, Akamai/Linode, Hetzner, Vultr) at the value end, and AI-infrastructure specialists CoreWeave and Lambda Labs in the GPU segment.

The strategic bet is that DigitalOcean can capture the long tail of AI-native startups running inference and agentic workloads, which are more steady-state and margin-friendly than training clusters, by leaning on simplicity, predictable pricing, and an integrated stack rather than competing on raw capacity and price against dedicated GPU clouds.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue trajectory shows clear re-acceleration:

| Period | Revenue | YoY Growth |
|---|---|---|
| FY2022 | $576M | — |
| FY2023 | $693M | +15% |
| FY2024 | $781M | +12.7% |
| FY2025 | $901M | +15.5% |
| Q1 2026 | $257.9M | +22.4% |

The AI tailwind is the driver. AI Customer ARR reached $170M in Q1 2026, against total ARR of $1,032M. Revenue from $1M+ customers jumped to 18% of revenue ($45.7M) from 8% ($16.4M) YoY. The four-quarter beat record (62.6%, 13.3%, 7.4%, 23.6% surprises) supports confidence in near-term consensus.

Profitability requires careful reading. FY2025 GAAP net income of $259M (28.8% margin) is inflated by two non-recurring items: a $48.1M gain on convertible-note extinguishment and a $69.9M release of the US tax valuation allowance. Stripping these out, non-GAAP net income was $217M and non-GAAP diluted EPS was $2.12 (vs. $1.92 FY2024). FY2025 operating income was $157M (17.4% margin), Adjusted EBITDA was $375M (42% margin, stable), and FCF was $180.5M (20% margin).

The margin trajectory is the key near-term concern. Q1 2026 gross margin fell to 56% from 61% YoY, and operating margin fell to 14% from 18%. Management attributes this to data-center capacity built ahead of the AI revenue ramp, a deliberate investment that pressured Q1 non-GAAP EPS. The tax rate also normalized to ~36% after the valuation allowance reversed, further pressuring GAAP per-share figures.

The balance sheet was transformed by a March 2026 equity raise. Stockholders' equity flipped from -$28.7M at YE2025 to +$887.4M in Q1 2026 after a follow-on of 11.95M shares at $77.00 (net proceeds $887.9M). The prior negative equity reflected buybacks plus accumulated deficit plus the convert-heavy capital structure, not operating distress; the Altman Z-Score of 0.65 ("distress zone") is a false signal for a company generating ~$300M+ annual operating cash flow at 42% adjusted EBITDA margins. Cash stood at $741.4M. Total debt is $937.3M: $625M of 2030 Convertible Notes (0.00%) plus $312.3M of 2026 Convertible Notes (0.00%, maturing Dec 1, 2026), with the $500M term loan drawn in early 2026 fully repaid in March with offering proceeds. Management plans to settle the 2026 maturity via revolver plus cash, which is manageable given the liquidity position.

The capital-allocation story sits off the balance sheet. There was $668M of not-yet-commenced data-center leases at Q1, plus an additional ~$1.27B in leases signed after Q1 (commencing through June 2027, ~11-year terms), plus growing finance leases for servers ($134M added in Q1 alone). This ~$1.9B of future lease obligation is the real leverage story, and it will keep depreciation and lease costs elevated regardless of whether AI demand fills the capacity.

## Growth Analysis

The growth signal is strong and broad-based at the demand level. Revenue re-accelerated to 22% from low-teens; AI ARR tripled; large-customer revenue share doubled. Consensus FY2026 EPS estimates rose 26.9% over 90 days (from $0.94 to $1.20), the kind of revision velocity that typically precedes continued re-rating when paired with accelerating revenue. Next-year estimates moved up 6.3% over the last 30 days. These are meaningful positive revisions and they support the growth case.

Two caveats temper the score. First, the most recent 30-day revisions are roughly flat (-0.3% current year, -0.5% current quarter, -0.9% next quarter), suggesting the burst of upward revision following the Q1 print has stabilized rather than continued accelerating. The strongest revision momentum is now behind the stock. Second, the growth is increasingly concentrated: top-25 customers rose to 16% of revenue from 9% YoY, and these are usage-based, month-to-month relationships that can contract quickly. The net dollar retention rate of 100-101% is adequate but well below best-in-class SaaS (110%+), which means the growth is being driven by land-and-expand among a smaller set of large AI customers rather than durable expansion across the base.

The TAM is large (22% CAGR to $251B by 2028) and the AI inference/agentic thesis aligns with where infrastructure demand is genuinely broadening, as confirmed across the data-center buildout layer. But AI ARR remains only ~16% of total ARR, and the growth durability depends on demand filling ~$1.9B of committed capacity profitably, execution that is unproven at the scale where this strategy must compete.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$173, DOCN trades at ~79x trailing GAAP EPS of $2.52 (the cleaner FY2025 non-GAAP figure is $2.12) and ~144x consensus FY2026 EPS. On next-year consensus of $1.76 the multiple is ~98x; the per-share figures are depressed by gross-margin compression and a normalized tax rate, so the EPS denominator understates cash generation. On FCF of ~$180M and an $18B market cap, the FCF yield is ~1%, and on adjusted EBITDA of $375M the EV/EBITDA is ~50x. By any measure the stock is priced for sustained high growth and margin recovery.

The peer set is heterogeneous and not a clean comparison: SMCI at 16.8x, AKAM at 44.8x, OKTA at 85.1x, CDW at 15.7x, SITM negative. The peer median P/E is 44.8; DOCN trades at a steep premium to that on forward earnings despite faster top-line growth, which the growth could partly justify, but the margin compression and dilution erode the per-share quality that a premium multiple requires.

The key valuation point: the stock now sits at or near analyst targets. The mean target is $178.77, the median $179.00, with a high of $200 (BofA's "agentic AI beneficiary" call) and a low of $135. With the stock at $173-178, the consensus upside is modest to the mean and ~13% to the most bullish target, against a low target implying ~22% downside. When a high-momentum name is cheap relative to rising estimates, analyst targets behave as floors. Here the opposite holds: at a triple-digit forward multiple, the targets and the price reflect the same growth extrapolation, so proximity to target is not independent confirmation of value; it signals that the optimism is already in the price. A stock at this multiple has asymmetric earnings risk: beats are expected and earn little re-rating, while any miss or margin disappointment derates the multiple sharply.

We do not penalize the stock for having appreciated; the appreciation was fundamentally driven. We score valuation low because the current price discounts flawless AI capacity execution with no margin of safety, the FCF and EBITDA yields are thin, and the dilution stack is a real headwind to per-share value even as the business grows.

## Competitive Landscape

DigitalOcean's traditional moat is real but narrow: simplicity, predictable pricing, a large developer community, and an integrated stack serving SMB and digital-native workloads that hyperscalers under-serve and niche hosts under-equip. That positioning is defensible in its core market.

The AI pivot moves it onto more contested ground. GPU/inference workloads put it in direct competition with CoreWeave and Lambda Labs (well-capitalized specialists) and with the hyperscalers' own AI services. DigitalOcean's bet, capturing the long tail of inference and agentic workloads rather than massive training clusters, is sound in theory because inference is steadier and more margin-friendly. But it is unproven at the scale where price and capacity competition is fierce, and the company depends on a limited number of GPU suppliers. The 500bps gross-margin compression is partly evidence that competing for this business is more capital-intensive and lower-margin than the legacy IaaS base.

The trajectory is mixed: revenue share is being gained in AI, but the moat (low switching costs, month-to-month consumption contracts, 100-101% NDR) is not strengthening commensurately, and customer concentration is rising. That combination of improving growth and stable-to-eroding moat characteristics argues for a middling competitive score rather than a high one.

## Risk Assessment

**Dilution and the 2026 maturity.** The 2026 converts mature Dec 1, 2026, settleable via revolver plus cash, which is manageable. More material is that the 2030 converts are deeply in the money (~15.96M potential dilutive shares), with capped-call protection only to $66.51 against a $172 stock. Combined with the follow-on dilution and fully-vested CEO MRSU, per-share value faces a structural headwind.

**Margin compression vs. lease obligations.** The binary risk is that AI demand does not ramp fast enough to profitably fill the ~$1.9B of committed long-dated leases.

**Customer concentration.** Top-25 customers rose to 16% of revenue from 9%; $1M+ customers are 18%. These are usage-based, month-to-month, terminable relationships. The historical "diversified long tail" thesis is weakening as a few large AI customers scale.

**Executive turnover.** A multi-year pattern of Item 5.02 officer departures (most recently Nov 2025), though the current CEO/CFO team delivered the AI re-acceleration. A new CPTO was added in 2026.

**Insider distribution.** Access Industries sold ~3.7M shares ($500M+) in May at $147-162; the CFO sold into the post-earnings spike. No open-market buying. This is monetization of a sharp run, not necessarily a fundamental red flag, but the magnitude of the strategic-holder exit at these levels is consistent with insiders concluding the easy upside has been captured.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 94.5% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 76.9% (a 17.6-point spread), and the term structure is flat, so the market is pricing elevated, evenly distributed uncertainty rather than a single dated catalyst. Put/call skew is balanced (2.9%) and the implied move for the nearest expiry is ±6.5%, with longer tenors pricing larger implied moves. The intraday options-delta reading (ATM IV spiking to 445.7% with a falling P/C OI ratio) appears to be a thin-options data artifact and is treated as non-informative. Short interest is elevated at 15.7% of float with 3.3 days to cover, meaningful bearish institutional positioning that creates modest squeeze potential on a beat, but not a crowded-short setup. The net read: the market prices high uncertainty without a clear directional lean, consistent with a stock whose risk/reward has flattened after a large move.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
AI inference and agentic demand is structurally broadening, and DigitalOcean is capturing it among the long tail of AI-native startups that hyperscalers under-serve. AI ARR triples again over the next 12-18 months, the committed lease capacity fills profitably, gross margin stabilizes and recovers toward the high-50s as utilization rises, and revenue growth holds in the low-20s. On that path, FY2027 EPS approaches $2.50+, and the stock re-rates toward the $200 bull target (~13% upside) or beyond as consensus catches up. The four-quarter beat streak and 26.9% upward FY26 estimate revision support the inflection.

### Bear Case
AI demand ramps slower than the lease commitments require, leaving the ~$1.9B of long-dated obligations under-utilized. Gross margin stays compressed below 56%, customer concentration produces a lumpy revenue miss as a large month-to-month AI customer contracts, and per-share growth disappoints as dilution from the in-the-money converts and equity grants bites. At a triple-digit forward multiple, any of these triggers a sharp derating. The stock retraces toward the $135 low target (~22% downside) or lower if the AI narrative cools sector-wide.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the growth thesis is genuine but the valuation already discounts the success case, and insider behavior signals the asymmetry has been captured.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback to the low-$130s (near the analyst low target), restoring asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.
- Two consecutive quarters of gross-margin stabilization or recovery (≥57%) alongside sustained 20%+ revenue growth, demonstrating the lease capacity is filling profitably.
- Evidence of NDR moving above 105%, confirming durable expansion rather than concentration-driven growth.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Revenue growth decelerating below 15% YoY or a guidance cut, signaling AI demand is not filling the committed capacity.
- Gross margin compressing below 54%, indicating the AI buildout is structurally lower-margin than underwritten.
- A material AI customer loss or churn event given the rising concentration, or further large strategic-holder selling.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next quarterly print (Q2 CY2026, expected early August) is the key checkpoint; it will show whether the post-Q1 estimate stabilization reflects a pause or a plateau, and whether margins are inflecting. The Dec 1, 2026 convert maturity is a secondary monitoring point.

## Conclusion

DigitalOcean has earned its re-rating with a genuine AI-driven acceleration: 22% revenue growth, AI ARR up 221%, a guidance raise, and four straight beats. The business is in the one sector where this system's selection has reliably added value, and the data-center demand it is riding is confirmed broadly across the infrastructure layer. At ~$173 the stock trades at ~144x forward consensus EPS, sits at or near analyst targets, and prices in flawless execution on filling the committed lease commitments while gross margin has already compressed and per-share earnings declined YoY. The dilution stack is fully live, and insiders, including the largest strategic holder and the CFO, are uniformly selling into strength with no offsetting open-market buying. We rate DOCN HOLD and would require either a meaningful pullback toward the low-$130s or demonstrated margin recovery before turning constructive.
