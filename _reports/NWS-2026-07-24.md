**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

News Corp emerged from the April 2025 Foxtel divestiture as a structurally cleaner company: five reporting segments led by the Dow Jones B2B information franchise (28% of revenue, ~25% EBITDA margin), a 61.4% stake in ASX-listed REA Group, and HarperCollins. The headline revenue drop from $10.1B to $8.45B is a reclassification artifact; on a continuing-operations basis, revenue grew ~2% and Total Segment EBITDA rose 14% to $1.42B, with every operating segment expanding EBITDA year over year. Net income attributable to the company reached $1.18B, boosted by a $692M gain on the Foxtel sale, but the underlying continuing-ops earnings power (+71% to $648M) is what matters for forward valuation. The balance sheet is net-cash at the parent, with $2.4B cash against $1.94B long-term debt and $571M of free cash flow.

The investment case rests on a sum-of-the-parts discount that management is slowly narrowing. NWS's ~61% of REA Group, marked to the ASX, approaches or exceeds News Corp's entire equity value, implying the market assigns Dow Jones, HarperCollins, and the news assets a deep discount. Two overhangs eased in 2025: Foxtel is gone, and the multi-year Murdoch trust dispute settled in September with Lachlan Murdoch securing control and three siblings bought out for cash. A new $1B repurchase authorization was added in July 2025.

Against these positives, the near-term picture is more balanced. The stock trades at ~$29.75 on a ~$1.08 current-year and ~$1.29 next-year consensus EPS, a ~28x forward multiple that is not obviously cheap on a look-through basis, and next-quarter estimates have been cut ~11% over 90 days. Generative AI is a two-sided force for the highest-margin Dow Jones segment: paid content-licensing deals are a real new revenue avenue, but adverse fair-use rulings would undercut the monetization model. With no dated re-rating catalyst and revision momentum drifting negative, we rate NWS a HOLD (growth-hold rationale) and would accumulate on weakness rather than chase.

## Company Overview

News Corp is a diversified global media and information-services company, controlled by the Murdoch family through a dual-class structure in which Class B (this ticker, NWS) carries the votes and Class A (NWSA) is largely non-voting. Following the Foxtel sale, the business reports five segments:

- **Dow Jones** — 28% of revenue ($2.33B), $588M EBITDA at the highest segment margin (~25%). The Wall Street Journal, Barron's, MarketWatch, IBD, plus higher-growth B2B professional-information products: Risk & Compliance, Dow Jones Energy (OPIS), Factiva, and Newswires. Digital is 82% of Dow Jones revenue; total consumer digital-only subscriptions rose 9% YoY to 5.7M, with WSJ digital-only subs at 4.1M.
- **Digital Real Estate Services** — 21% of revenue ($1.80B), the largest single EBITDA contributor at $601M. Comprises the 61.4% stake in REA Group (realestate.com.au, dominant in Australia at roughly 4x the nearest competitor's traffic) and 80% of Move (Realtor.com in the U.S.; REA owns the remaining 20%).
- **Book Publishing** — 25% of revenue ($2.15B), $296M EBITDA. HarperCollins, the #2 global consumer book publisher; backlist is 64% of consumer revenue, with audiobooks (Spotify partnership) the growth vector.
- **News Media** — 26% of revenue ($2.17B), $153M EBITDA at the lowest margin (~7%). News Corp Australia, News UK (The Sun, The Times), the NY Post, talkSPORT.
- **Other** — corporate overhead (–$223M).

The prevailing framing is a sum-of-the-parts thesis: the REA stake anchors valuation, and management's portfolio simplification (Foxtel exit, buybacks) is the mechanism for narrowing the holding-company discount.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue optics.** The reported decline is entirely the Foxtel deconsolidation into discontinued operations. On continuing operations, revenue grew from roughly $8.25B, up ~2%. This is a common misread on a screen and should not be interpreted as a business in decline.

**Profitability inflected upward.** Net income attributable to NWS rose from $266M (FY24) to $1,180M (FY25), +344%. The drivers were the +71% increase in continuing-ops net income plus the Foxtel gain, augmented by a ~$111M gain on REA's disposal of its PropertyGuru stake. EBITDA growth was recorded across all operating segments. Reported net margin was 15.9%, ROE 15.3%, ROA 8.6%. The recurring, non-one-time earnings base is the continuing-ops figure, so the trailing GAAP EPS of $0.84 and the headline net income both need to be normalized down for the divestiture gains when setting a forward multiple.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow from continuing operations was $978M, with free cash flow of $571M (+6% YoY, ~6.8% FCF margin). Total debt was ~$2.0B (parent debt $1,962M plus REA's non-recourse borrowings); long-term debt fell from $2.86B to $1.94B, leaving the parent net-cash. The current ratio was 1.84. The reported D/E of 1.77 overstates leverage; it captures operating leases and other liabilities, and on a debt/EBITDA basis the company is conservatively financed.

**The Altman Z-Score of 1.87 is a false signal.** It is dragged into the grey zone by low EBIT/assets and asset turnover, both artifacts of a goodwill-heavy information/media business ($4.4B goodwill plus $1.9B intangibles, ~40% of assets). With the net-cash parent and $571M FCF, distress risk is negligible. A standardized screen flagging distress here would be materially wrong.

**Capital allocation.** FY25 buybacks totaled $150M (~$310M left on the 2021 program), and the new $1B authorization was added July 15, 2025. The dividend is modest at $0.20/share (~0.65% yield). Buybacks mechanically raise the Murdoch Family Trust's Class B ownership percentage, a governance nuance rather than a red flag.

**Earnings track record.** The company has beaten in three of the last four quarters (surprises of +12.1%, +14.2%, +17.0%, and one in-line print), supporting reasonable confidence in near-term consensus even as forward revisions soften.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth engine is Dow Jones, where the B2B professional-information products — Risk & Compliance (+15%) and Dow Jones Energy/OPIS (+11%) — carry pricing power and product velocity, and where digital already dominates segment revenue. This is the highest-quality, most-recurring revenue in the portfolio and the segment that most justifies a premium multiple.

REA Group provides structurally advantaged growth in Australian digital real estate, but it is only ~61% owned and its most direct U.S. cousin, Move/Realtor.com, is pressured by weak U.S. housing (leads –9% YoY) and by the NAR commission-rule changes that could reduce agent lead purchases. HarperCollins is a low-single-digit grower with a stable backlist and audiobook upside. News Media is managed for cash in secular print decline.

The estimate-revision picture tempers the growth case. Current-year consensus is roughly flat ($1.08, +2.1% over 90 days), next-year is modestly positive (+3.9% to $1.29), but the next-quarter estimate has been cut 10.7% over 90 days and the current quarter trimmed 2.8%. This is negative near-term momentum rather than a collapse, and it falls well short of the revision velocity that would flag a genuine earnings acceleration. Growth is real but moderate and concentrated in one segment; we do not see the broad-based acceleration that would warrant a top-tier growth score.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$29.75, NWS trades at roughly 28x current-year and ~23x next-year consensus EPS. Against the publishing peer set — median P/E 24.5, with the closest comparables NWSA (33.4x), NYT (31.8x), IAC (24.5x), WLY (15.5x), and SCHL (18.0x) — NWS sits mid-pack, richer than the legacy publishers but below the Class A shares and the New York Times. The Class B shares typically trade at a discount to Class A despite carrying the votes, a quirk of liquidity and index inclusion rather than fundamentals.

The bull case is not the earnings multiple but the sum-of-the-parts discount: the ~61% REA stake alone approaches or exceeds News Corp's ~$5.5B equity value, implying Dow Jones, HarperCollins, and the news assets are valued at a steep discount. That discount is real, but it has been persistent for years, and holding-company discounts rarely close without a hard catalyst: a spin, a sale, or a collapse of the dual-class structure. None is dated. The Foxtel exit and the succession settlement narrowed two overhangs, but the market has largely absorbed both.

We are deliberately cautious about treating the SOTP cheapness as a standalone buy signal. Compressed multiples on holding-company structures tend to be value traps absent an independent catalyst, and the family-controlled voting structure specifically limits the mechanisms by which the discount could be forced closed. The forward multiple is not stretched for a mid-single-digit organic grower with a high-quality core, but it is not cheap enough, without a re-rating trigger, to score aggressively. We assign a modestly below-neutral valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

Dow Jones competes with Reuters, RELX/LexisNexis, S&P Global, and Argus in professional information, and increasingly with AI-native entrants. Its brand strength, pricing power, and product velocity in Risk & Compliance and Energy support a widening moat there, partly offset by a Factiva customer dispute that dragged "Other information services." REA is the dominant Australian portal with a durable structural advantage; Move faces Zillow (now also a rentals partner) and CoStar's Homes.com in a weaker U.S. market and carries a latent structural risk: Realtor.com operates under a perpetual NAR license that NAR can terminate for cause. HarperCollins competes in a scale-and-backlist business against Penguin Random House and Hachette. News Media is in managed decline, dependent on platform algorithms whose changes have already hurt U.K. digital reach (notably The Sun).

The competitive trajectory is favorable at Dow Jones and REA, stable at HarperCollins, and negative at News Media. On balance, the mix is improving as the portfolio tilts toward the higher-quality subscription and information assets, which supports a modestly above-neutral competitive-position score.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Generative AI is the pivotal, two-sided swing factor for Dow Jones.** The 10-K's expanded language flags that AI chatbots and search overviews trained on NWS content without permission or compensation reduce traffic and subscriber demand, and that some lower-court fair-use rulings could undermine the content-licensing monetization model. The offset is paid licensing deals with large AI platforms plus litigation against unauthorized use. This is the single most important thread to monitor because it hits the highest-margin segment directly.
2. **Move's NAR license and U.S. housing exposure.** Realtor.com depends on depressed U.S. transaction volumes and on a perpetual NAR license terminable for cause, a single-point-of-failure risk for a segment that helps drive the most EBITDA, compounded by NAR commission-rule changes.
3. **Non-recourse, minority-owned REA cash.** The most valuable asset is only ~61% owned, and ~$280M of cash at REA is inaccessible without a dividend declaration.
4. **Secular print decline** in News Media, partly offset by cost cuts and cover-price increases.
5. **FX translation** (large AUD and GBP exposures) and newsprint cost cyclicality.
6. **Governance/control.** The Murdoch Family Trust holds ~40.6% of Class B votes (capped at 44%), and by-laws waive corporate-opportunity fiduciary duties for persons overlapping with Fox; Lachlan Murdoch chairs both. Minority voting holders have limited recourse.

Short interest at 14.3% of float (3.8 days to cover) reflects institutional caution, likely tied to the AI structural debate and the mid-cycle U.S. housing exposure, rather than an imminent catalyst.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility collapsed from 83.9% to 40.0% between early June and late July, consistent with the passing of the May earnings event and a market no longer pricing a near-term binary. The put/call open-interest ratio ticked up modestly to 0.14 but remains low, indicating no meaningful hedging pressure. The falling IV suggests resolved near-term uncertainty rather than complacency about a specific catalyst, and it makes options-based entry timing relatively inexpensive if one wanted to express a view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Post-Foxtel News Corp is a higher-margin, subscription-and-information-weighted business with a net-cash parent, strong FCF, and a $1B buyback in hand. Dow Jones B2B compounds at double-digit rates in Risk & Compliance and Energy with real pricing power; REA anchors a large, structurally advantaged real-estate franchise. The succession settlement removed years of litigation overhang and clarified control. If management continues to simplify the portfolio and buy back stock, and if AI content-licensing evolves into a durable revenue stream, the sum-of-the-parts discount narrows and the stock re-rates toward $38–43.

**Bear case.** The holding-company discount is structural and has persisted for years; the dual-class family control limits the mechanisms to force it closed. Adverse fair-use rulings erode the Dow Jones licensing model just as AI search overviews compress traffic; Move suffers from prolonged U.S. housing weakness and NAR-rule fallout; News Media decline accelerates. Near-term estimate cuts prove to be the start of a softer earnings trajectory rather than noise, and the ~28x forward multiple compresses. Downside to $21–23.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The 12-month growth thesis is intact — Dow Jones B2B, REA, portfolio simplification, buyback support — but no dated catalyst is set to force the SOTP discount closed, and near-term revision momentum is negative. This is a name to own for the compounding of the higher-quality core and to add to on weakness, not to chase near the middle of its range.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Next-quarter or FY26 estimate revisions turn positive and Dow Jones digital subscription growth reaccelerates above the current ~9% pace.
- A concrete SOTP-narrowing action (a REA/Move restructuring, a spin, or an accelerated buyback pace materially drawing down the $1B authorization) or a favorable AI-licensing framework with disclosed counterparties and economics.
- A pullback into the low-to-mid $20s that widens the discount to the REA-implied floor with fundamentals unchanged.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A definitive adverse fair-use ruling materially impairing the Dow Jones content-licensing monetization model.
- Sustained negative estimate revisions across current-year and next-year windows combined with a Dow Jones or Digital Real Estate EBITDA decline.
- Loss or material impairment of the NAR license underpinning Move.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings release and the subsequent 10-K, which should confirm the CFO transition to Lavanya Chandrashekar and provide fresh segment EBITDA and subscription data.

**Bear-case downside from current (~$29.75):** roughly –23% to –29% ($21–23). **Bull-case upside:** roughly +28% to +45% ($38–43). The risk/reward is broadly balanced, which is consistent with the HOLD.

## Conclusion

News Corp is a better business than its screen suggests: a net-cash, FCF-generative, subscription-weighted portfolio anchored by a genuine Dow Jones compounder and a dominant Australian real-estate franchise, with two multi-year overhangs (Foxtel, succession) now resolved. The persistent sum-of-the-parts discount is real, but so is the absence of a dated catalyst to close it, and near-term estimates are drifting lower even as the company beats on the print. The trailing GAAP figures are inflated by one-time divestiture gains, so the ~28x forward multiple is the right anchor, and it is fair rather than cheap for a mid-single-digit organic grower whose highest-margin segment faces a two-sided AI question. We recommend HOLD, favor accumulation on weakness toward the low-to-mid $20s, and would upgrade on a positive revision turn or a concrete discount-narrowing action.
