## Executive Summary

Jack Henry is one of four scaled providers of core account-processing systems to US community and regional banks and credit unions, serving roughly 1,670 core clients and more than 5,710 non-core clients. Nine-month FY2026 revenue grew 8.0% GAAP (7.6% on management's adjusted basis), trailing return on equity is 24.9% and NOPAT ROIC 22.8%, debt is $90M against $2.13B of equity, and management raised FY2026 guidance on May 5 to GAAP EPS of $6.78–6.87. Four consecutive quarterly beats (8.7% to 17.8%) and open-market purchases by both the CEO and CFO on 5/14/2026 at $133.42 and $134.12 support the view that the franchise is intact and that management saw the selloff as a mispricing.

This is a HOLD rather than a fifth consecutive BUY because of arithmetic, not deterioration. Subtracting nine-month actuals from full-year guidance implies Q4 FY2026 GAAP EPS of $1.37–1.46 against $1.75 a year ago (−17% to −22%), implied adjusted operating margin of roughly 20.3–21.2% versus 23.2% (200–290bp of contraction), and implied GAAP revenue growth of +0.9% to +2.8% versus +8.7% in Q3. Two mechanical items reconcile most of that gap: a roughly $17M year-over-year swing in Q4 deconversion revenue at approximately 78% incremental margin, and the reversal of an abnormally low medical-claims experience under the self-insured health plan. But the company never disclosed the split, and Q3 standalone adjusted operating margin was exactly flat year-over-year (22.9% versus 22.9%). Separately, the 46% jump in nine-month operating cash flow to $459.3M is predominantly a tax-timing event: cash income taxes paid fell to $28.9M from $94.6M following OBBBA's restoration of immediate domestic R&D expensing, with the deferred tax liability rising from $240.0M to $340.4M.

At $155.65 the shares sit 19.5% below the January high of $193.39 and 28.6% above the June 22 low of $121.04. At $121 this was 17.7x guided earnings; at $155.65 it is 22.8x, and the 12-month total-return algorithm (≈7% revenue growth, 50–90bp of margin, ~1.5% share-count reduction, 1.6% dividend) supports a base case near $165. The August 18 release, which carries the first FY2027 guidance, resolves the central open question. Near-term implied volatility of 41.4% against 27.3% realized, in backwardation, indicates the market agrees the event matters.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1976 and headquartered in Monett, Missouri, Jack Henry employs approximately 7,300 associates and serves roughly 7,400 clients. Core bank clients exceed 950, split across SilverLake (520 banks, $1B to over $55B in assets), CIF 20/20 (260 banks) and Core Director (over 170 banks); approximately 715 credit unions run the single Symitar platform. Revenue splits between Services and Support (57% of FY2025) and Processing (43%), reported across four segments: Core, Payments, Complementary, and Corporate Services.

Three structural features define durability. First, as a bank service provider Jack Henry is examined by the federal banking agencies under the Bank Service Company Act and by the CFPB, a supervisory gauntlet any new core entrant must also pass. Second, switching costs are monetized directly: deconversion fees were $33.9M in FY2025 and $33.5M in nine-month FY2026, and Q3 FY2026 deconversion revenue of $18.7M carried $14.6M of operating income, roughly a 78% incremental margin. Clients pay to leave. Third, the customer pool is shrinking in units while growing in dollars: FDIC-insured institutions fell 13% from year-end 2019 to 2024 while aggregate assets compounded 5% to $24.1 trillion; credit unions fell 15% while assets compounded 8% to $2.3 trillion. Jack Henry reports net core footprint gains in both bases from CY2023 to CY2024, with average assets per bank core client rising from $1.26B to $1.29B.

The forward technology bet is the Jack Henry Platform, a public-cloud-native, API-first componentized build now covering wire transfers, a data hub, exception item processing, general ledger, deposit servicing and entitlements. It is explicitly described as being developed into a full core alternative, meaning it is not complete.

**Margin quality.** The 270bp nine-month GAAP margin gain (190bp adjusted) decomposes into three parts: genuine leverage (cost of revenue fell from 57.8% to 56.0% of revenue; SG&A from 11.9% to 11.2%); a self-insurance windfall management disclosed twice as lower-than-normal medical claims "especially during the first and second quarters"; and a $6.8M gain on the sale of two aircraft booked as an SG&A offset. Q3 standalone adjusted operating margin was 22.9% ($141.245M on $615.929M) against 22.9% a year earlier ($131.680M on $574.242M). The most recent quarter delivered no structural margin expansion on the company's own preferred measure. Cost of revenue still improved 100bp year-over-year in Q3, so cost discipline was intact; the difference was the absence of the non-recurring help.

**Cash flow.** Nine-month operating cash flow of $459.3M versus $314.4M is a 46% increase, and the 10-Q attributes it primarily to deferred income taxes. The deferred-tax add-back swung from −$12.8M to +$100.3M; cash taxes paid fell $65.7M to $28.9M. A portion of that is a finite catch-up deduction of the previously capitalized TCJA R&D balance; the ongoing immediate expensing of domestic R&D is durable for a company spending north of $300M annually on development. The split is not disclosed, which makes forward free cash flow the largest modeling uncertainty in the file.

**Free cash flow, correctly defined.** Screens showing FY2025 free cash flow of $588.1M subtract only $53.4M of PP&E capex. Jack Henry's own definition also deducts $172.4M of capitalized software development and $5.4M of internal-use software, giving approximately $410.3M, a 17.3% FCF margin and a **3.7% FCF yield** at the current market cap. Capitalized software has run $166.1M, $167.2M and $172.4M across FY2023–FY2025, recurring and mandatory, not discretionary. The distinction changes the valuation frame materially and is the single most common error in screening this name.

**Development intensity.** FY2025 gross development spend of roughly $340.6M (R&D expense $162.8M plus $172.4M capitalized plus $5.4M internal-use) equals 14.3% of revenue, with approximately 52% capitalized, a ratio stable across FY2023 through nine-month FY2026. Reported R&D of 6.7–6.9% of revenue therefore understates true innovation spend by about half, and reported operating margin sits above what a full-expensing presentation would show. This is consistent, disclosed, and GAAP-compliant; it argues against direct margin comparison with peers on different policies rather than against earnings quality. Net capitalized software of $651.7M does carry an impairment tail tied to the Platform build.

**Balance sheet.** Altman Z-Score 3.83 (safe zone). Debt of $90M at 3/31/26 against $2,134.8M of equity, down from $170M a year earlier; a 1% rate move shifts annual interest expense by $0.9M. The March 25, 2026 credit agreement upsized the revolver to $1,000M maturing 3/25/2031, plus a separate $50M facility, leaving roughly $960M of undrawn capacity on the main revolver. Cash of $20.6M (from $102.0M) reflects $284.4M of buybacks, $127.5M of dividends and a $42.4M acquisition, not stress. Current deferred revenue falling from $290.5M to $129.0M is the predictable unwind of June-billed annual maintenance; on a like-quarter basis total deferred revenue was $208.7M versus $221.8M, a 5.9% decline consistent with the on-premise-to-cloud mix shift.

**RPO.** $8.03B at 3/31/26, roughly 3.2x guided forward revenue, with 23% expected within 12 months. The company does not define this as backlog and does not disclose treatment of cancellable arrangements or how volume-variable consideration is estimated. Treat it as directional contract coverage.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance: GAAP revenue $2,521–2,533M (+6.1% to +6.6%), adjusted revenue $2,479–2,491M (+6.6% to +7.1%), GAAP operating margin 24.7–24.9%, adjusted 23.9–24.1%, GAAP EPS $6.78–6.87 (+8.7% to +10.0%). Assumptions include $37M of deconversion revenue and no further acquisitions.

Composition matters more than the headline. Nine-month adjusted segment growth: Complementary +8.4% at a 62.1% segment margin, Core +7.4% at 60.3%, Payments +6.5% at 48.5%. Within Payments, faster payments grew 46.4% in Q3 and 50.5% over nine months as JHA PayCenter aggregates Zelle, RTP and FedNow certification for institutions that cannot absorb that work internally, the clearest instance of the model converting regulatory and integration complexity into recurring revenue, though the base is not separately sized. Against that, card revenue decelerated to +3.6% in Q3 from +6.2% over nine months. Card is a large, mature line; a sustained 3-4% growth rate there caps the consolidated algorithm.

The implied fourth quarter is the near-term issue. Full-year guidance less nine-month actuals yields Q4 GAAP revenue of $620.7–632.7M against $615.4M, adjusted revenue growth of +3.6% to +5.7%, GAAP operating income of $123.7–131.7M against $155.7M. Guidance implies only about $3.5M of Q4 deconversion revenue versus roughly $20.5M a year prior; at 78% incremental margin that swing is worth roughly $13M of operating income and explains most of the GAAP shortfall, with medical-claims normalization covering more of the remainder. The CFO said only that Q4 would show "relative weakness to the year to date in fiscal Q4 non-GAAP revenue and margins consistent with previously stated expectations." The reconciliation fits but is not a disclosure. Genuine organic softening or a Q4 investment step-up would produce identical arithmetic, and the $100–130M FY2026 facilities capex guidance against $46.6M spent in nine months implies a large Q4 spend step-up.

**Estimate revisions.** Next-quarter consensus is $1.98 (+0.3% in 30 days, +1.4% in 90 days), current year $6.57 (+0.5% / +1.9%), next year $7.10 (+0.4% / +1.5%). The one negative line is the current quarter, cut 5.3% over 90 days from $1.52 to $1.44, the Street marking Q4 to the middle of the implied range. There is a basis mismatch worth flagging: nine-month GAAP EPS of $5.41 plus the $1.44 current-quarter estimate equals $6.85, squarely inside GAAP guidance, while the "current year" $6.57 figure does not reconcile to that. The quarterly panel appears GAAP-consistent (Q1 $1.97 + Q2 $1.72 + Q3 $1.71 = $5.40); the annual figure likely reflects a normalized or differently constructed basis. Read against a cleaned FY2026 base of roughly $6.60–6.70 (excluding the $6.8M asset gain, ≈$0.07, and the medical-claims benefit), the FY2027 consensus of $7.10 implies 6-8% growth, not the 3.7% a naive comparison to reported GAAP suggests. That reframing bears on the valuation.

Revisions are positive but nowhere near the +30% velocity that would signal an acceleration. This is a steady 7% compounder with an EPS algorithm in the 9-11% range when buybacks and margin cooperate.

## Valuation Assessment

At $155.65:
- 22.8x FY2026 GAAP guidance midpoint of $6.825
- 21.9x FY2027 consensus of $7.10
- 24.9x FY2025 actual $6.24
- EV of roughly $11.13B (net debt ≈ $69M) = 4.4x FY2026 revenue, ~17.8x guided GAAP operating income
- 3.7% FCF yield on the company's own FY2025 definition ($410.3M)
- 1.57% dividend yield ($2.44 annualized), roughly 35% payout of guided EPS

Peer medians in the supplied comparable set (P/E 19.7) are of limited use: SEZL at 30.8x, FOUR at 44.0x, WEX at 15.3x and EEFT at 9.7x are merchant-acquiring, fleet-card and money-transfer businesses with different growth, cyclicality and capital intensity. JKHY's genuine comparables are the core-processing oligopoly and quality payroll/data compounders, which sit in the low-to-mid 20s. On that frame Jack Henry is fairly valued to modestly cheap: 21.9x forward for 25% ROE, 22.8% ROIC, near-zero leverage and 7% revenue growth is a defensible price rather than an obvious one.

The bull argument on valuation is the multiple history. The proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows JKHY TSR indexed at 103.95 at FY2025 year-end against a peer group at 215.90 (FY2020 = 100) — cumulative five-year shareholder return of +3.95% against peers' +115.9% — while net income grew from $311.5M in FY2021 to $455.7M in FY2025, a 46% increase. Earnings compounded; the multiple compressed from the low 30s to the low 20s.

I decline to treat that as a re-rating catalyst on its own. Five years of persistent multiple compression against rising earnings is the pattern that separates a genuine mispricing from a rational reassessment of terminal growth, and the company's own disclosures support the latter: renewal price compression is admitted in Item 1A ("certain of our renewals have resulted in price compression"), with a stated inability to fully pass through vendor cost inflation because client contracts run longer than vendor contracts; the bank pool shrinks roughly 3% annually with only six new charters in each of CY2023 and CY2024; and management describes acquisition opportunities as scarce after "nearly 50 years in business." Betting on multiple recovery here has lost money for five consecutive years.

What has changed since June is the entry price, and it moved against the buyer. At $121.04 on June 22 the shares were 17.7x guided earnings. At $155.65 that gap has largely closed, which is a multiple observation rather than a statement about prior price action. Analyst targets — low $158, mean $185.71, median $186.50, high $208 — are unusual in that the lowest target sits above spot, and the recommendation mix improved to 4 strong buy / 8 buy / 4 hold. That corroborates moderate undervaluation but is not independent evidence; on a 22x stock with 7-8% growth, targets clustered 19% above spot mostly reflect the same multiple-recovery assumption the last five years disproved.

Valuation score of 6.0: fair, with a modest discount to intrinsic value, and no identified catalyst that reliably closes it inside 12 months.

## Competitive Landscape

Named core competitors are FIS, Fiserv, Corelation and Finastra, with a fragmented complementary market. Jack Henry's stated primary advantage is client service, measured through anniversary surveys, randomized post-support surveys and single-point-of-contact coverage. The 10-K concedes the technology position candidly: "we believe our success depends upon our technical expertise more than our proprietary rights... Most of our technology is not patented."

Trajectory evidence is favorable at the margin. Net core footprint gains in both banks and credit unions from CY2023 to CY2024; rising average client assets under management; 17 competitive core wins in Q3, described by the CEO as the best third quarter for new core wins in seven years; and a pipeline management attributes to "increased technology spending and competitive uncertainty." That last phrase implies displacement of accounts from a disrupted competitor, but no independent corroboration surfaced, so it should be weighted as management characterization with a plausible mechanism.

Two offsets keep this at 6.5 rather than higher. Renewal price compression is a disclosed, ongoing condition, and with roughly six-year cloud and payment contracts a substantial cohort renews annually, which is pricing power eroding at the point of contract, the most direct measure of moat strength. And the Platform's viability as a full core replacement is unresolved: development runs at 14.3% of revenue, $651.7M of capitalized software sits on the balance sheet, and a December 23, 2025 addendum committed $450M of cloud capacity through 6/30/2033 (replacing $182M of prior obligations, a net increase of roughly $268M). That commitment converts variable hosting cost into fixed capacity, amplifying operating leverage in both directions. Success converts a legacy franchise into a modern SaaS franchise; a stall leaves fixed cost and capitalized software against better-funded rivals.

On the AI question, the FY2025 10-K carries a granular AI risk factor covering bias, explainability, unauthorized employee use and undefined future regulation, and the cybersecurity factor notes AI is accelerating the sophistication of attacks. Whether this factor was newly added or materially expanded versus the prior year is an inference from its depth, not an established fact.

## Risk Assessment

**Near-term binary (highest weight).** The Q4/full-year release lands after the close on August 18, 2026 with a call August 19, carrying the first FY2027 guidance. Consensus at $1.44 sits mid-range, so a headline miss is less likely than a guidance disappointment. FY2027 revenue guidance below roughly 6% adjusted, or flat-to-down adjusted margin, would validate the renewal-compression and fixed-cloud-cost bear case.

**Renewal price compression.** Disclosed and ongoing, with a stated asymmetry on cost pass-through. This is the most concrete margin threat in the filings.

**Concentrated, unnamed third-party commitments.** A card-processing strategic services agreement (signed FY2017, amended May 2025 to add two years and $213.1M) had $1,022.3M remaining at 6/30/25 extending to January 2038; the $450M cloud addendum runs to 6/30/2033. Total off-balance-sheet purchase obligations were $1,643.8M. Neither counterparty is named, so credit quality, renewal leverage and potential competitive conflict cannot be assessed.

**Capitalized software impairment.** $651.7M net, with software capitalization named as a critical accounting estimate where a change in useful-life or marketability judgment "could result in a material change to our results of operations." ASU 2025-06 modernizes the framework for annual periods beginning after 12/15/2027.

**Cybersecurity and settlement.** Jack Henry directs funds settlement across debit, credit, bill pay, ACH, Zelle, RTP, FedNow and check clearing, discloses that a significant portion of associates and contractors work outside company-controlled facilities, and notes it may extend short-term credit as part of settlement, creating direct counterparty loss exposure.

**Governance and disclosure gaps.** No employment agreements with any executive officer, with management described as concentrated in a small number of individuals. Two filings could not be retrieved: the 5/12/2026 8-K announcing a share repurchase increase (size unknown) and the 6/4/2026 8-K announcing Foss's retirement (terms, effective date and board-chair succession unknown). An IRS examination of the FY2023 return is open, with $25.6M of gross unrecognized tax benefits and an expected $3–9M reduction within twelve months.

**Client-base contraction.** Roughly 3% annual decline in both bank and credit-union counts. Share gains have more than offset it to date; that is not guaranteed to persist.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($195, +25%)

FY2027 guidance on August 19 lands at 7-8% adjusted revenue growth with 75–100bp of margin expansion, confirming that Q4 weakness was deconversion timing and medical-claims normalization rather than demand. Faster payments compounds above 40% off a growing base, Complementary sustains 8%+ at 62% segment margins, and the Victor acquisition scales embedded payments. The 17 competitive core wins convert to revenue on the usual 12-24 month lag, lifting FY2028 visibility. Buybacks continue at the eightfold-higher pace (nine-month FY2026 repurchases of $284.4M versus $35.1M), shrinking the count 1.5-2% annually. OBBBA's permanent domestic R&D expensing proves a durable cash-tax reduction, lifting owner earnings toward $480–500M. EPS reaches $7.40 in FY2027 and the multiple recovers to 26x as the bottom-quintile relative TSR position — the compensation plan's own 20.0% beginning percentile input — begins to close.

### Bear Case ($130, −16%)

FY2027 guidance comes in at 5-6% adjusted revenue growth with flat margins. Renewal price compression accelerates as the annual cohort reprices; card growth stays near 3-4%; and the $450M fixed cloud commitment plus the Q4 capex step-up to reach $100–130M of facilities spend absorbs the operating leverage. The medical-claims reversal proves larger than the deconversion explanation implies, revealing that underlying Q4 demand softened. Development spend at 14.3% of revenue with 52% capitalized comes under scrutiny as ASU 2025-06 approaches, and any Platform milestone slip raises impairment risk against $651.7M of net capitalized software. With a client count shrinking 3% annually and no re-rating, the multiple compresses to 19x on $6.90, a continuation of the five-year pattern that has already cost shareholders roughly 112 percentage points versus peers.

Upside of **+6.0%** from $155.65, plus a 1.6% dividend, for roughly a 7.6% one-year total return.

**Bull case target: $195** (+25.3%) — 26x $7.40 on FY2027 guidance of 7-8% revenue growth with margin expansion. Brackets the analyst mean of $185.71 and sits below the high of $208.

**Bear case target: $130** (−16.5%) — 19x $6.90 on FY2027 guidance of 5-6% revenue growth with flat margins. Above the June low of $121.04 and near where the CEO and CFO bought in May.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint on **August 18-19, 2026** (Q4/FY2026 results and initial FY2027 guidance) and confirmation at the Q1 FY2027 print in early November.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. FY2027 guidance of ≥6.5% adjusted revenue growth with ≥50bp of adjusted operating margin expansion, and Q4 adjusted margin landing at or above the implied 21.2% high end, which would establish that the nine-month expansion was not entirely non-recurring.
2. A decline to **$135–140** (roughly 19.5-20.5x normalized FY2027 EPS) on unchanged guidance, restoring the June asymmetry near where management bought.
3. Disclosure quantifying the permanent portion of the OBBBA cash-tax benefit at a level that lifts sustainable free cash flow above roughly $470M (a 4.2%+ yield on the company's own FCF definition), combined with a repurchase authorization increase sized above 4% of shares.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. FY2027 adjusted revenue guidance below 5.5% with flat-to-negative margin guidance, which would confirm renewal price compression outrunning mix and volume.
2. Card revenue growth falling below 2% for two consecutive quarters, or Complementary adjusted growth dropping below 6%.
3. Any impairment charge against the $651.7M of net capitalized software, or a disclosed adverse renegotiation of the card-processing or cloud commitments.
4. Cash taxes reverting to prior levels while capital returns continue above free cash flow, pushing drawn revolver borrowings above roughly $400M.

**Reassessment timeline:** August 19, 2026 (FY2027 guidance), then the Q1 FY2027 print in early November.

## Conclusion

Jack Henry is a high-quality, high-return franchise with a genuine moat artifact — clients pay 78%-margin deconversion fees to leave — a near-pristine balance sheet, and management that bought stock at $133–134 and stepped up repurchases eightfold. The score trajectory (6.9 → 7.1) reflected that, and nothing in the current evidence breaks the franchise thesis.

Three things moved the risk/reward to neutral. The price is 28.6% above the June low at 22.8x guided earnings, closing most of the discount that justified prior BUYs. The margin expansion that anchored the nine-month narrative was flat year-over-year in Q3 on the company's own adjusted basis, and the cash-flow surge is predominantly a finite tax-timing benefit that screens misread — properly defined FY2025 free cash flow is $410.3M, a 3.7% yield, not the $588.1M screens report. And the August 18 print carries an implied 17-22% EPS decline plus first FY2027 guidance into 41.4% near-term implied volatility, with five years of unbroken multiple compression as the base rate for what happens when this business guides conservatively.

**HOLD.** Own it for a roughly 8% base-case total return with a credible path to $195 if FY2027 guidance confirms the 7%-plus algorithm. Add at $135–140, or on FY2027 guidance that puts the margin story back on structural footing. Do not increase exposure in the twelve days before the print.
