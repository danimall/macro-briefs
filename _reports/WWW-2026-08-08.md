## Executive Summary

Wolverine World Wide has completed the operational half of a genuine turnaround. Gross margin rose from 39.0% in FY2023 to 44.3% in FY2024 to 47.3% in FY2025, operating margin went from –3.0% to 8.0%, and diluted EPS more than doubled to $1.14 from a restated $0.55. Interest expense fell $9.9M as the term loan was retired, and the capital structure is now unusually clean for a $1.9B-revenue consumer company: $550M of 4.000% notes due August 2029, $75M drawn on a $600M revolver that runs to 2030, and no maturities in 2027–2028. Q1 FY2026 continued the trend, with revenue up 11.0% to $457.6M, operating profit of $33.9M versus $21.0M, and GAAP diluted EPS of $0.24 versus $0.15. Consensus has responded, with current-year EPS estimates rising 7.3% and next-year 5.3% over 90 days.

What has not happened is a broadening of demand. Decomposing FY2025's reported 6.8% revenue growth: Active Group wholesale grew 19.9%, Active DTC fell 0.1%, Work Group wholesale fell 7.1%, and the licensing-heavy Other segment fell 17.4%. By geography, the United States — 48% of revenue — grew 0.3%; EMEA grew 13.6% and Asia Pacific 20.4%. FY2025 also contained a 53rd week, worth up to 1.9 percentage points, and translation was a tailwind (Q1 FY2026 disclosed a $15.4M FX revenue benefit, implying 7.25% constant-currency growth against the 11.0% reported). Growth is therefore concentrated in the channel with the least contractual protection — Item 1A states wholesale orders are "subject to wholesale customers' rights of cancellation and rescheduling" — and in geographies where the company has been converting to license and distribution models.

At $18.93 the shares sit 42% below the August 2025 high of $32.80 while forward estimates have risen. That combination usually signals a mispricing, and part of it is: the derating is multiple compression, not estimate reduction. The offsetting evidence is specific and dateable. Sweaty Betty's reporting unit passed its FY2025 impairment test with only 16% headroom against $161.6M of carrying value equal to 39.6% of book equity, and it is the sole Critical Audit Matter in EY's opinion. Q1 gross margin was held flat on higher prices, with the volume consequence not yet observable. Tangible book value is –$232.8M. The Q2 FY2026 report is imminent, with the near-dated option chain pricing a ±15.1% move, implied volatility at 91.5% against 53.7% realized, put/call open interest at 2.57 (from 0.91 two days earlier), and 12.2% of float short. Expected return probability-weighted across scenarios is roughly +8–10% including the dividend, against a 29% drawdown in the bear case. That is a hold, not an entry.

## Company Overview

Wolverine designs and markets footwear and apparel and owns no manufacturing. Note 17 of the FY2025 10-K states the company "sourced 100% of its footwear products and apparel and accessories from third-party suppliers, located primarily in the Asia Pacific region," and Item 1A confirms it "does not have long-term contracts with its third-party manufacturers." Capital intensity is correspondingly low: FY2025 capex of $14.5M was 0.8% of revenue.

FY2025 segment structure:

| Segment | Revenue | Op. income | Margin | Principal brands |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Active Group | $1,407.8M | $253.2M | 18.0% | Merrell, Saucony, Sweaty Betty, Chaco |
| Work Group | $422.2M | $72.7M | 17.2% | Wolverine, Cat, Bates, Harley-Davidson, HYTEST |
| Other | $44.3M | $28.6M | 64.6% | Hush Puppies, licensed Stride Rite, sourcing/royalty |
| Corporate | — | $(204.3)M | — | Unallocated overhead, environmental, impairments |
| **Total** | **$1,874.3M** | **$150.2M** | **8.0%** | |

The company is the residue of a two-year divestiture program: Keds (Feb 2023), Hush Puppies China/HK/Macau IP ($58.8M, Sept 2023), U.S. and Asia Leathers (Aug and Dec 2023), the Merrell/Saucony China JVs to Xtep ($22.0M, effective Jan 2024), and Sperry to Authentic Brands and Aldo ($97.4M, effective Jan 2024), plus two sale-leasebacks. Revenue went $2,684.8M (FY2022) → $2,242.9M (FY2023) → $1,755.0M (FY2024) → $1,874.3M (FY2025). The business is roughly 30% smaller than three years ago and resumed growth only in FY2025.

Note 17 discloses no customer exceeding 10% of consolidated revenue in any year — structural protection relative to footwear peers dependent on a handful of accounts.

## Financial Analysis

**Margin rebuild is substantially structural, and the reported year-over-year improvement understates it.** The income statement carries an "environmental and other related costs, net of recoveries" line of +$6.6M expense in FY2025 versus –$10.3M of income in FY2024, a $16.9M swing driven by legacy insurance recoveries that ended when the company's insurer lawsuit was dismissed in December 2024. Corporate segment drag ex-environmental was $(197.7)M in FY2025 versus $(198.2)M in FY2024 — essentially flat. Normalizing both years for that line, operating margin improved from 5.0% to 8.4%, +340bp rather than the +245bp reported. Underlying overhead discipline held better than the headline suggests.

The residual margin problem is scale. Both operating segments earn 17–18% margins; the consolidated 8.0% is what survives $204.3M of Corporate unallocated cost, equal to 10.9% of revenue. Consolidated SG&A is 38.9% of revenue. WWW's 47.3% gross margin sits mid-pack in footwear (below Deckers, On and Crocs at mid-50s to ~60%; above Steve Madden at ~41%), while its operating margin is bottom-quartile. The gap is a corporate cost problem, not a brand pricing problem, and with DTC flat the nearer-term lever is overhead rather than mix.

**Cash flow fell year over year and is higher quality than the FY2024 comparison implies.**

| | FY2025 | FY2024 | FY2023 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | $140.0M | $180.1M | $121.8M |
| Capex | $(14.5)M | $(20.2)M | $(14.6)M |
| Free cash flow | $125.5M | $159.9M | $107.2M |
| FCF margin | 6.7% | 9.1% | 4.8% |

FY2024's superior OCF was a one-time destocking event: inventories released $130.6M of cash in FY2024 against consuming $20.9M in FY2025, and total working-capital contribution was +$86.1M versus +$8.8M. FY2025's $140.0M reconciles to earnings: net income $101.0M + D&A $25.9M + SBC $24.4M + deferred taxes $8.0M = $159.3M, less the $14.5M environmental cash-over-expense adjustment and $12.6M other, plus $8.8M working capital. FCF conversion of approximately 124% of net income is a mark of quality.

Environmental cash outflow exceeds the P&L charge: $16.1M of remediation payments plus $3.5M of litigation payments in FY2025, roughly $19.6M against a $6.6M charge. Any FCF-based valuation should carry $15–20M per year as non-discretionary for several years.

**Balance sheet.** Derived at FY2025 year-end: EBITDA $176.1M (operating profit plus D&A); net debt $415.4M against $206.3M of cash; net leverage 2.36x, gross 3.53x; EBIT/interest 4.58x; current ratio 1.40x (from 1.31x). Tangible book value is negative $232.8M ($408.0M equity less $431.3M goodwill and $209.5M intangibles), with goodwill plus intangibles at 37.5% of total assets. The 4% notes are carried at $621.7M total debt against a $583.7M fair value, meaning the market yield exceeds the coupon — favorable to equity holders today, and a dateable headwind at the 2029 refinancing (replacing $550M at, say, 6.5% adds ~$13.75M of pre-tax interest, roughly $0.14 per share after tax).

Reproducible screens: Altman Z ≈ 3.01 using $1.552B of market equity — at the conventional 2.99 threshold and sensitive to the price input (a 20% share decline takes it to ~2.87). Piotroski F-Score 8 of 9, the single failure being share issuance.

**Taxes and share count.** The 16.9% effective rate ($20.5M on $121.5M pretax) rests on Hong Kong exemptions, a foreign withholding credit and FDII, offset by +2.8% ($3.3M) of non-deductible executive compensation — implying roughly $15.7M of pay above §162(m) limits, up sharply from a $1.4M tax effect in FY2024. Cash taxes paid were $25.4M, above the book charge, so the large carryforwards ($103.4M federal capital loss, $105.9M federal §163(j), $308.5M state NOL) are not currently reducing cash tax much. The capital-loss carryforward has value only against U.S. capital gains, i.e. only if further assets are sold.

Shares outstanding rose from 80,329,020 to 81,186,677 (+1.07%) despite $14.5M of repurchases, because 1,033,494 restricted shares plus 717,533 option exercises exceeded the 900,000 shares bought. SBC of $24.4M equals about 1.6% of market capitalization annually and rose from $19.1M and $15.2M in the prior two years. Reported diluted shares were 81.7M. Outstanding unvested awards plus options total 4,325,805 shares, 5.33% of shares outstanding or 5.06% post-issuance ownership dilution; the 2,126,047 performance awards vest only on two targets over a three-year measurement period and belong in scenarios, not a current diluted count. A ceiling case granting and vesting the entire remaining 4,790,839-unit plan reserve at maximum reaches 9,116,644 shares, 10.1% post-issuance ownership dilution — a bound, not an expectation.

## Growth Analysis

Reported FY2025 growth of 6.8% deflates to roughly 4.8% on a like-for-like 52-week basis if revenue accrued evenly across FY2025's 53 weeks (the extra week fell over the New Year holiday, a low-shipping period for wholesale, so the true drag is probably less than the full 1.9 points; management did not quantify it). Currency was additionally a tailwind — the cumulative translation adjustment in OCI swung from –$17.6M to +$23.2M.

Q1 FY2026 is the cleanest datapoint: $457.6M revenue, +11.0%, or +7.25% stripping the disclosed $15.4M FX benefit, with gross margin approximately flat at 47.6% and operating profit up 61%. The company raised its FY2026 profit outlook alongside the print. Consensus now sits at $1.56 for the current year and $1.76 for next, implying about 12.8% EPS growth, with the current-quarter estimate at $0.38 against $0.35 delivered a year ago.

Estimate revision momentum is positive but decelerating: +7.3% and +5.3% over 90 days for the current and next year, but only +0.2% and +0.1% over 30 days. This does not meet the threshold at which revision velocity becomes a standalone buy signal, and the 30-day flattening argues the easy part of the re-rating has occurred.

The brand-level engine, per secondary reporting I could not verify against the 10-K (the segment note does not disclose brand revenue), is Saucony at roughly 31% FY2025 growth, with Merrell also cited as a FY2026 driver. If approximately right, that is share capture in a running category reshaped by On and Hoka at premium price points, and it is the most important element of the equity story, because it means Saucony is carrying a portfolio in which the U.S. was flat.

Against that: Work Group revenue fell 7.3% (likely nearer 9% on a 52-week basis) while its operating margin improved 200bp to 17.2%. Two readings are consistent with the filings — disciplined price/mix in a soft industrial end market, or harvesting a declining franchise by trading volume for margin. The segment note does not distinguish them, and a $422M base eroding at high single digits is a material offset. The Other segment, which produces 19.0% of consolidated operating profit on 2.4% of revenue, saw revenue fall 17.4% and operating income 8.6%. Note 6's $29.9M of remaining fixed transaction price under license agreements through December 2028 is contractual minimum consideration with variable royalty consideration explicitly omitted; it is not backlog.

Consolidated DTC was flat in FY2025 against what Item 1A describes as a "high fixed-cost structure" with $168.9M of undiscounted operating lease payments and a 6.6-year weighted-average term. DTC is the usual source of brand pricing power, and it did not grow.

## Valuation Assessment

At $18.93 against $1.14 of FY2025 diluted EPS the trailing multiple is 16.6x; the quote feed reports a trailing and forward P/E of 15.5x. Forward on consensus: 12.1x the current-year consensus of $1.56 and 10.8x next year's $1.76. FCF yield on FY2025's $125.5M is 8.1%. The dividend is $0.40 per share, 2.1% yield, unchanged for three years, and consumed 27% of FCF; total shareholder returns of $47.8M were 38% of FCF.

The equity-level multiple understates the enterprise valuation. EV of $1.967B against $176.1M of EBITDA is 11.2x, and 13.1x EV/EBIT. The gap between a 12x P/E and an 11x EV/EBITDA is explained by two items that are favorable but not permanent: a 16.9% effective tax rate resting on Hong Kong exemptions and specific credits, and $550M of 4.000% fixed-rate debt that reprices in 2029. Investors should not treat WWW as trading at a deep-value multiple on an enterprise basis; it trades at a modest discount with the equity leveraged into it.

Peer context is heterogeneous. The Footwear median P/E of 43.0 is unusable — Steve Madden at 43.0x and Crocs at negative earnings are not comparables for a mid-cap in transition. Deckers at 16.3x is the informative reference, and it is a materially better business: higher growth, mid-50s gross margin, net cash. WWW's 12.1x forward is a 26% discount to Deckers, which is defensible given 2.36x net leverage, negative tangible book, flat U.S. revenue, a $161.6M impairment exposure and a PFAS tail. The discount is not obviously excessive.

The identifiable catalysts on the cheap side are three. First, the Supreme Court's February 20, 2026 ruling that IEEPA tariffs were unauthorized: WWW paid approximately $36M under that authority, Customs opened a refund process on April 20, 2026, and no loss-recovery asset has been recorded. That is $28.4–29.9M after tax at a 16.9–21% rate, $0.35–0.37 per diluted share, 1.8–1.9% of market cap. Not recording a gain contingency is standard GAAP conservatism and implies nothing about management's expectations; timing depends on protest and liquidation status. Ongoing tariff relief is a separate and weaker question because Sections 232, 301 and 122 remain available. Second, $135.5M of the $150M March 2024 repurchase authorization is unspent with the three-year window closing around March 2027 — 8.7% of market cap, though the FY2025 base rate of $14.5M argues against full deployment. Third, if Corporate overhead at 10.9% of revenue is reduced toward peer norms, each 100bp is roughly $18.7M pre-tax, about $0.19 per share after tax.

Scenario framework:

- **Base $21.50** — 12.2x FY2027 consensus of $1.76, consistent with the analyst median of $20.50 and mean of $21.70. Assumes mid-single-digit constant-currency revenue growth, gross margin held near 47%, no impairment charge in the operating case. **+13.6% from $18.93**, +15.7% with the dividend.
- **Bull $28.00** — 15x $1.76 plus IEEPA refund receipt and meaningful buyback execution; requires U.S. revenue returning to growth and DTC turning positive. Near the $29.00 analyst high. **+47.9%**.
- **Bear $13.50** — near the $13.47 52-week low. Assumes tariff-driven price increases cost volume, Active wholesale decelerates to low single digits, Sweaty Betty is written down, and U.S. discretionary spending weakens into H2 2026. Roughly 8x depressed EPS of $1.60–1.70. **–28.7%**.

Weighting base 50%, bull 20%, bear 30% gives approximately +7.8% price return, +9.9% total return. That is a fair-to-modestly-positive expected value with high dispersion, which supports HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE — particularly in a sector where selection has historically underperformed the index and where a binary print sits within two weeks.

**On the 42% drawdown from $32.80.** The mechanism is multiple compression, not estimate reduction: forward estimates are up 5–7% over 90 days while the stock is down 42% from its high, with the bulk of the decline occurring in a single session on November 5, 2025 (–24% on 9.6M shares, roughly six times average volume) on the Q3 FY2025 release. The stock has since recovered off the $13.47 low. What the sellers may be right about, stated so it can be checked: (a) Q1's flat gross margin was purchased with price increases, and Item 1A warns that "if the Company raises prices on products but competitors do not make similar price increases, the Company's competitive position may be materially adversely affected" — the volume consequence is not yet in reported data; (b) growth is wholesale, international, FX- and calendar-aided, and the U.S. at 48% of revenue is flat; (c) $161.6M of Sweaty Betty carrying value is one impairment test from a 40% reduction in book equity. Each is falsifiable at the next two prints.

## Competitive Landscape

Item 1A concedes the structural position directly: competitors "have larger customer and consumer bases, and/or greater financial, technical or marketing resources than the Company, particularly its competitors in the apparel and direct-to-consumer businesses." That the company singles out apparel and DTC as its weakest fronts matches the numbers — DTC flat, Sweaty Betty impaired in fiscal 2022 by $48.4M and passing its FY2025 test by 16%.

Where the position is improving: performance running, if Saucony's reported 31% FY2025 growth is approximately correct. Growing at that rate while On, Hoka, ASICS, New Balance and Brooks compete aggressively is share capture rather than category lift, which makes Saucony credible as a durable asset rather than a cyclical bounce. This framing is inference, not filing fact, and the brand figure is unverified.

Where it is deteriorating or static: Work Group (Wolverine, Cat, Bates, HYTEST) shrinking at high single digits; Other/licensing revenue down 17.4%; DTC flat against a fixed-cost lease base; Sweaty Betty competing with Lululemon, Alo and Vuori in the most promotional apparel niche, primarily in the UK. Merrell in outdoor faces Salomon, Hoka's trail line, Keen, Columbia and Timberland, and no verified brand-level figure is available.

The genuine structural assets are two brands with authentic category heritage, a royalty stream at 64.6% segment operating margin, 0.8%-of-revenue capital intensity, and no customer above 10% of revenue. The genuine structural weakness is 100% third-party sourcing concentrated in Asia Pacific with no long-term supplier contracts, distribution from "a relatively small number of locations," and growth concentrated in cancellable wholesale orders.

## Risk Assessment

**Sweaty Betty impairment — quantified and near-term.** FY2025 fair value exceeded carrying value by 16% on $105.4M of trade name plus $56.2M of goodwill, equal to 39.6% of the $408.0M stockholders' equity. A full write-off would be non-cash: covenants are leverage- and coverage-based rather than net-worth-based, and re-running the Altman Z after a $161.6M asset write-down actually produces ~3.10 versus ~3.01 today because asset turnover rises. The exposure is to book-value screens, headlines and sentiment, not to solvency. The 16% headroom can be closed or restored by a modest change in the discount rate alone, and the trade name is tested separately and could impair on its own.

**PFAS environmental and litigation.** The Rockford, Michigan tannery operated from the early 1900s to 2009 using 3M Scotchgard; Michigan tightened PFOA/PFOS drinking-water standards to 8 and 16 ppt in 2020. The remediation reserve is $26.5M (down from $39.7M and $57.9M), with $14.5M payable over up to 25 years; litigation liabilities are $8.5M; the 2020 Consent Decree caps the municipal water-extension obligation at $69.5M aggregate. The insurance-recovery suit was dismissed in December 2024, ending the recovery income stream. The Landfill Suit remains pending and a new suit was filed December 4, 2025 by a landfill owner seeking PFAS response costs. The 10-K states the company "cannot estimate a possible loss or range of loss in excess of the associated established reserves."

**Tariffs, two-sided.** Restrictive measures "may be announced with little or no advance notice." The IEEPA refund is upside; the ongoing regime is unresolved because alternative authorities remain. The pricing-response risk is the more consequential one: margin was defended with price in Q1, and the volume effect has not yet been reported.

**Consumer macro.** WWW's U.S. business is already flat, so any deterioration in U.S. discretionary spending into H2 2026 would work on top of a stalled base rather than a growing one. [unverified — specific macro indicators (mortgage rates, application demand, payrolls) are not in the provided sources]

**Governance and alignment.** FY2025 CEO compensation of approximately $10.85M against a five-year cumulative TSR index of 65.91 versus a peer index of 101.72; a classified board with one-third of directors standing annually plus Delaware anti-takeover provisions that the company acknowledges "could discourage proxy contests"; and an Item 1A risk factor now contemplating activism explicitly. Three 8-Ks in 2025 carried Item 5.02 officer/director changes whose subjects were not verified. Share count is rising 1.07% annually. There were no open-market C-suite purchases in the transaction sample reviewed, and a director adopted a 10b5-1 plan on March 11, 2026 to sell up to 125,000 shares between July 2026 and June 2027; a pre-planned sale is a weak signal in either direction.

**Refinancing.** No wall until August 2029, and $75M of revolver in 2026. This comfortable structure is the main reason financial health scores near mid-range despite negative tangible book.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is pricing an imminent event, not a directional regime shift. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 91.5% against 53.7% realized — a 37.8-point premium at the 88th percentile of the current chain — with term structure in backwardation (91.5% at 12 days versus 68.1% at six months) and a ±15.1% implied move on the August 21 expiry. Put/call volume ratio is 2.00 and open-interest ratio 2.57, the latter having risen from 0.91 in two days. Short interest is 12.2% of float at 6.4 days to cover.

The most defensible reading: the Q2 FY2026 report has not been filed and is expected within days, and participants are paying steeply for near-dated protection into it. Elevated put positioning is a caution flag on the timing of any purchase; it is not by itself a bearish fundamental signal, and the 12.2% short interest cuts both ways — a beat against this positioning could produce a sharp move higher. Two conclusions follow. Buying volatility here is expensive. And for a fundamental buyer, waiting 10–14 days to see the print costs little relative to the 15% two-sided move being priced.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($28, +47.9%).** Q2 confirms the Q1 trajectory with U.S. revenue returning to growth and DTC turning positive, demonstrating that the price increases used to defend gross margin did not cost volume. Saucony and Merrell reaccelerate as guided, and mid-single-digit constant-currency growth on a 47%+ gross margin plus flat Corporate overhead compounds operating profit at a mid-teens rate. Consensus $1.76 for FY2027 proves conservative, the $36M IEEPA refund lands, and management deploys a meaningful share of the $135.5M remaining authorization before the March 2027 expiry — at $19 that is roughly 8.7% of shares. Sweaty Betty's headroom is restored or the unit is divested, removing the 39.6%-of-equity overhang. At 15x $1.76 plus refund proceeds, $28 is reachable within 12–18 months, close to the $29 analyst high.

**Bear case ($13.50, –28.7%).** The tariff pass-through breaks: competitors do not follow on price, Active Group wholesale decelerates from 19.9% toward low single digits, and wholesale customers exercise the cancellation and rescheduling rights the filings disclose. FY2026 faces reversal of three FY2025 tailwinds — the 53rd week, favorable translation, and a year-end DSO that fell to 32.1 days partly because the extra week was a low-shipping holiday period (intra-year reliance on the receivables facility already rose, with the derecognized balance at $120.7M in Q1 FY2026 versus $102.6M a year earlier). Sweaty Betty is written down, cutting book equity ~40% and deepening negative tangible book. The U.S. consumer weakens into H2 2026 against a domestic business already flat. Work Group's high-single-digit erosion proves to be harvesting rather than discipline. Eight times $1.60–1.70 of impaired earnings power puts the stock back at the $13.47 low.

**Base case ($21.50, +13.6% plus 2.1% dividend).** Constant-currency growth settles in the mid single digits as international momentum partly offsets a flat U.S. and eroding Work Group. Gross margin holds near 47% but does not expand, because DTC mix is not growing. Corporate overhead stays near 10.9% of revenue. FY2027 EPS approximates the $1.76 consensus, the IEEPA refund arrives as a one-time item, and the buyback is used partially. Twelve times forward earnings — a discount to Deckers that reflects leverage, negative tangible book and the impairment overhang — produces $21.50, which brackets the $20.50 analyst median and $21.70 mean.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD** (fourth consecutive). Score 5.65 versus 5.6 in June — essentially flat, with a mild trim to competitive position (6.5 → 6.0) on the flat DTC line and Work Group erosion, and to management quality (5.5 → 5.2) on the absence of open-market insider buying, the 1.07% share-count increase despite repurchases, and $135.5M of authorization unused with roughly 8 months left in the window. Growth prospects come down marginally (6.5 → 6.2) on the flattening of 30-day revisions, not on any deterioration in reported results. The price appreciation since the June report has consumed part of the discount without a corresponding fundamental change, which reduces rather than increases the case for adding.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE (any two):**
1. Q2 FY2026 shows U.S. revenue growth above 2% and consolidated DTC positive, with FY2026 guidance raised again.
2. Price returns to $16.00–17.00 with estimates intact — that is 9.1–9.7x FY2027 consensus and a 9.0–9.5% FCF yield, a margin of safety the current price does not provide.
3. Sweaty Betty valuation headroom restored above 25% at the next test, or the unit divested, removing $161.6M of equity exposure.
4. Repurchases above $40M in a single quarter, evidencing intent to use the authorization before March 2027.

**Downgrade triggers to REDUCE:**
1. Gross margin down more than 150bp year over year, indicating the price increases were reversed or discounted away.
2. Active Group wholesale growth decelerating below 5% with DTC still negative — the growth engine and the margin engine failing together.
3. FY2026 guidance cut, or U.S. revenue turning negative.
4. Net leverage above 3.0x on an inventory rebuild, or a working-capital reversal that takes FY2026 FCF below $90M.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q2 FY2026 release, expected within roughly two weeks and priced by the option market at ±15.1%, is the immediate decision point. The subsequent checkpoint is the Q3 report in late October or early November — the same slot in which the November 5, 2025 24% single-day decline occurred, which makes the guidance language in that release the highest-information event of the next six months. The annual goodwill test resolves the Sweaty Betty question in the FY2026 10-K, expected February 2027.

## Conclusion

Wolverine has delivered on margin and balance sheet. Gross margin is up 830bp in two years, operating margin has moved from negative to 8.0% (8.4% normalized for the environmental line), interest expense has fallen $9.9M, maturities are clear until 2029, FCF conversion is approximately 124% of net income, and the company has beaten consensus four quarters running. At 12.1x current-year and 10.8x next-year earnings with an 8.1% FCF yield and a covered 2.1% dividend, the shares are not expensive.

The reason not to buy them today is that the demand evidence is narrower than the earnings evidence, and the near-term event risk is priced at 91.5% implied volatility. The U.S., 48% of revenue, grew 0.3%; consolidated DTC was flat; Work Group and the high-margin royalty stream are both shrinking; and FY2025's headline growth carried a 53rd week and a translation tailwind. Q1's flat gross margin on 11% revenue growth was achieved with price, and the volume consequence appears in the report that is about to be filed. Behind that sit $161.6M of Sweaty Betty carrying value at 16% headroom, a PFAS tail with new claims still arriving, negative tangible book value of $232.8M, and an enterprise multiple of 11.2x EBITDA that is unremarkable once the 16.9% tax rate and 4% fixed-rate debt are accounted for.

Probability-weighted expected return of roughly +10% total, against 29% downside in the bear case and with a binary print days away, is a fair rather than compelling proposition.
