**Research Date: July 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Charter Communications is the second-largest U.S. cable operator, marketed under the Spectrum brand, serving ~59 million passings across 41 states with broadband, mobile, video, and voice. The equity has collapsed from a July 2025 high of $402 to $130, a 68% drawdown, and now trades at roughly 3.5x trailing earnings and 4x free cash flow. The bull case rests entirely on those multiples and accelerating buybacks; the bear case rests on the core broadband profit engine shrinking, EBITDA turning negative in the most recent quarter, and consensus estimates falling across every window. We land on the bear side.

The central problem is structural, not accounting. Residential internet customers fell 393K in 2025 and 455K year-over-year through Q1 2026, with Q1 2026 alone losing 120K. This is broadband share loss to two well-capitalized competitor classes: fiber-to-the-home overbuilders (AT&T and others) and fixed-wireless access from T-Mobile and Verizon. Internet is 43% of revenue and the overwhelming majority of segment margin. When the profit engine loses subscribers while video (-9.4%) and advertising (-17.6%) decline structurally, the mobile line (+22%) cannot carry the whole company, and it is a lower-margin defensive product riding Verizon's network. Adjusted EBITDA was flat in FY2025 (+0.6%) and declined 2.2% in Q1 2026.

Layered on top is $94.6B of debt refinancing at 7%+ coupons, two simultaneous transformational mergers (Cox and Liberty Broadband) with an August 2027 backstop date and a governance handoff to Cox's CEO as chairman, and a demonstrated pattern of value-destructive capital allocation: $1.0B repurchased in Q1 2026 at $200-232 against a stock now at $130. The CEO and three directors bought stock in the open market in April-May 2026, a genuine signal, but all bought above the current price. We maintain AVOID. This is the sixth consecutive negative call and the fourth explicit AVOID; the fundamentals have deteriorated in line with that trajectory, and nothing in the current data reverses it.

## Company Overview

Charter operates a single reportable segment as a "converged broadband connectivity company." Its network is hybrid fiber-coaxial covering ~59 million passings. The balance sheet is dominated by intangibles: $67.5B of indefinite-lived franchise rights (44% of assets) and $29.7B goodwill (19%), together ~63% of the $154B asset base.

FY2025 revenue was $54.8B, down 0.6% from $55.1B, with the following product mix:
- **Internet: $23.8B (43%)** — the profit engine, +1.7% on rate despite subscriber losses
- **Mobile: $3.8B (+22%)** — the sole growth driver, an MVNO on Verizon's network with CBRS offload
- **Video: $13.7B (-9.4%)** — structural decline
- **Voice: $1.4B (-6%)**, **Commercial: $7.3B (+0.9%)**, **Advertising: $1.5B (-17.6%)**

The strategic response to cord-cutting and broadband competition is convergence — bundling owned-economics mobile with broadband to lower churn — plus a "Life Unlimited" brand relaunch, simplified pricing, and free streaming apps (Peacock, Max, Disney+) bundled into Spectrum TV. Charter is pursuing two mega-deals concurrently: the acquisition of Cox Communications' residential cable and commercial fiber (announced May 2025) and the absorption of its largest shareholder, Liberty Broadband (announced November 2024).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is stagnant to declining.** FY2025 fell 0.6%; Q1 2026 fell another 1.0% year-over-year. Connectivity growth (+4.1%) and mobile are being fully offset by video and advertising erosion.

**Profitability has stopped growing and is now contracting at the margin.** FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA was $22.71B, up just 0.6% from $22.57B. A cable operator's whole equity thesis rests on EBITDA compounding through rate and cost discipline offsetting subscriber losses. That mechanism has stalled. Net income to shareholders was $5.0B in FY2025 (net margin ~10.5%); GAAP diluted EPS was $36.21; Q1 2026 diluted EPS was $9.17.

**Free cash flow remains the genuine strength.** Operating cash flow was $16.1B and FY2025 FCF was $5.0B, up from $4.3B, aided by OBBBA bonus-depreciation tax relief and lower interest. Q1 2026 FCF fell to $1.4B from $1.6B as capex stepped up for network evolution.

**Capital intensity is high but disciplined.** Capex was $11.7B in FY2025 (~21% of revenue), including $2.2B on subsidized rural construction (483K passings activated). 2026 capex is guided to ~$11.4B ex-Cox.

**Leverage is the defining feature.** Net-debt/EBITDA is 4.15x, within the company's stated 4.0-4.5x target. The Altman Z-Score of 0.49 and D/E of 9.6x look like distress signals in isolation, but they are characteristic of a stable-cash-flow, asset-heavy cable operator running deliberate leverage with negative working capital funded by predictable subscription cash. The market does not price CHTR as a bankruptcy candidate, and it should not. Interest coverage is ~2.6x (EBIT $12.9B vs. interest $5.0B). Only ~13% of debt is floating-rate, and maturities are well-laddered into the 2060s.

The credit concern is refinancing cost, not solvency. In January 2026 Charter issued $3.0B of 7.00% 2033s and 7.375% 2036s to replace sub-6% notes. Every refinancing at these coupons is a mechanical headwind to future interest expense. The Cox transaction adds ~$12.4B of assumed net debt plus ~$4.15B of cash consideration funded by new debt, meaning the interest burden grows into an EBITDA base that is no longer growing.

**Capital allocation has been value-destructive at recent prices.** Since 2016 Charter has repurchased ~184M shares for ~$79.7B, cutting the share count from 161.5M (2022) to ~125.5M (Q1 2026 weighted), a ~22% reduction that has propped up EPS while net income stagnated. But the $1.0B repurchased in Q1 2026 was executed at $200-232 per share against a stock now at $130, a direct destruction of shareholder capital in the most recent quarter. It reflects management's conviction that the stock was oversold at levels 50-80% above the current price.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth thesis. Consolidated revenue is flat-to-down, EBITDA has turned negative in the latest quarter, and the only growing product (mobile) is a lower-margin defensive subsidy that partly cannibalizes broadband economics through convergence pricing.

The estimate revision data confirms deteriorating momentum. Every window is negative:
- Current quarter EPS: $10.07, down 2.5% over 30 days and 4.8% over 90 days
- Next quarter: $10.14, down 3.3%/30d and 7.3%/90d
- Current year: $41.61, down 1.1%/30d and 5.9%/90d
- Next year: $44.56, down 2.6%/30d and 9.6%/90d

The 90-day cuts steepen the further out you look; a 9.6% reduction to next-year consensus is a meaningful downgrade of forward earnings power. Where a positive estimate revision signal of +30% over 90 days would flag a mispriced recovery, here estimates are falling at accelerating rates across all periods, which is a warning, not an opportunity.

The earnings track record reinforces this: three misses in the last four quarters, including an 11.8% miss in Q1 2026 ($9.17 vs. $10.40 estimate) and a 12.4% miss in Q3 2025. The single beat (Q4 2025, +3.6%) is bracketed by three misses. Forward visibility is deteriorating.

The only real growth optionality is the Cox and Liberty deals delivering scale synergies and cost density that offset secular broadband share loss. That is a bet on execution of two simultaneous mega-transactions during a period of dense management turnover, with an August 31, 2027 backstop date. It is optionality, not a growth thesis, and it carries as much downside (termination fees, integration disruption, dilution) as upside.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing metrics CHTR is extraordinarily cheap: ~3.5x trailing earnings, ~4x FCF, and a 68% drawdown from its 52-week high. The instinct is to treat this as a value opportunity. We do not, for a specific reason.

The compressed multiple reflects the market's assessment that both the numerator (earnings) and the denominator quality (the durability of the broadband base) are deteriorating. A 3.5x P/E on earnings that are falling 5-10% per year across the estimate curve, on a subscriber base shrinking 400K+ per year, on an EBITDA line that just went negative, is not the same as 3.5x on stable earnings. When earnings decline and leverage is 4.15x, equity value compresses non-linearly because the debt is fixed and the equity absorbs the entire earnings shortfall. That is why the stock fell 68% while earnings fell far less.

Analyst targets illustrate the disconnect: the mean is $209.94 and the median $190 against a $130 price, with a range of $120 to $413. The estimate revisions underlying those targets are falling, which means the targets themselves are stale on the high side and will likely drift down as analysts mark to the deteriorating subscriber and EBITDA data. The $120 low is the more informative anchor because it reflects analysts who have already incorporated the secular thesis. Treating the mean as a floor would be exactly the value-trap error the compressed multiple is warning against.

A high valuation score requires identifying why the market is wrong. We cannot. The bear case (structural broadband share loss to FWA and fiber) is a coherent, falsifiable thesis that the trailing numbers actively support. Cheapness here is a warning flag, not a buy signal. We score valuation at 4.5 — above the impaired-value-trap floor because the FCF is real and leverage is not a solvency threat, but well below a level that would reward the low multiple, because there is no catalyst to re-rate the stock and estimates are still falling.

## Competitive Landscape

Charter sits at the center of a three-way broadband battle (cable vs. fiber vs. fixed wireless), and it is losing share. The 10-K explicitly names FTTH overbuilders, fixed-wireless home internet, satellite (Starlink-type), and DSL as internet competitors. Fixed-wireless access from T-Mobile and Verizon is the more disruptive threat because it undercuts on price and requires no last-mile build, and fiber overbuilders are taking the premium tier.

The strategic response is convergence: bundling owned-economics mobile (Verizon MVNO plus CBRS offload) with broadband to lower churn and raise household stickiness, plus wholesale streaming bundles to slow video losses. Mobile added 1.8M lines in 2025 to reach 12.1M total, and it is working as a retention lever. But it is a defensive subsidy: lower-margin, partly cannibalizing broadband ARPU through convergence discounts, and dependent on Verizon's wholesale terms. The trajectory of the core broadband-share moat is negative, and the level of the moat (density, scale) is not enough to reverse it. The Cox deal is a scale play to defend the base with more density, but scale has not historically reversed FWA-driven share loss in the markets where it is most acute.

Competitive position is deteriorating on the dimension that matters: the profit engine is losing subscribers to structurally advantaged entrants. This is a low score on trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**Broadband subscriber erosion** is the central operating risk and it is already materializing. If losses accelerate rather than stabilize, the FCF thesis erodes further.

**Leverage plus rising refinancing cost.** Refinancing at 7%+ into a flat EBITDA base compresses equity value with each roll. A credit downgrade could trigger a change-of-control repurchase on Cox notes at 101%.

**Two simultaneous transformational M&A transactions.** The Cox deal (~$4.15B cash, $6.0B of 6.875% convertible preferred, ~33.6M Charter Holdings units, ~$12.4B assumed debt; Cox ends at ~25.1% and its CEO Alex Taylor becomes chairman) and the Liberty Broadband combination (stock merger contingent on a GCI divestiture, outside date August 31, 2027) run in parallel. Either failing triggers termination fees and a likely stock decline; both closing creates concentrated integration and governance-handoff risk during a period of leadership churn.

**Governance and dilution.** Liberty Broadband (~29% voting) and A/N (~13%) hold board seats and preemptive rights; post-deals Cox (30% cap) and A/N (19% cap) hold outsized influence. Minority holders face preemptive-right dilution.

**Management turnover during the most sensitive window.** At least eight Item 5.02 filings since April 2025, including January, February, and May 2026. Senior hires (Nick Jeffery, ex-Frontier/Vodafone UK; Adam Ray) may signal a rebuilt bench, but the density of change ahead of integration is a red flag.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term stress. ATM implied volatility is 179.2% versus 1-year historical volatility of 53.4%, a 125.8-point spread and the 94th percentile within the current chain. IV has jumped from 53.3% to 179.2% in the month to July 21, and the term structure is in steep backwardation (179.2% near-term vs. 72.1% at six months), which the market uses to price an imminent event, almost certainly the upcoming earnings report. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±13.4%. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (0.7% near-term, though inverting to -8.6% at three months), and the put/call OI ratio is 1.21. The market expects a large move on the next print but is not decisively positioned for a crash, consistent with the two-sided debate over whether broadband losses stabilize. Rich near-term IV means options are an expensive way to express either view.

**Short interest is extreme at 43.6% of float with 5.0 days to cover.** This cuts both ways. It confirms the bearish thesis is heavily crowded, and it creates genuine squeeze risk on any positive surprise (subscriber stabilization, deal progress, or an oversold-bounce catalyst). The June 29 spike from $133 to an intraday $168 before closing at $146 on 12.7M shares shows how violently the stock can move on positioning. For a short seller, 43.6% SI with a 68% drawdown already realized is a dangerous setup.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
At ~3.5x earnings and ~4x FCF, CHTR is one of the most statistically cheap large-caps in the market. It generates $5B of FCF, covers interest 2.6x, and is aggressively shrinking its share count (down 22% since 2022). The CEO and three directors bought stock in the open market at $140-175 in April-May 2026, a credible "insiders think it's oversold" signal. CEO comp-actually-paid of -$46.1M (2025) and -$24.9M (2024) means management's paper wealth has been destroyed alongside shareholders, indicating deep negative alignment rather than extraction. If broadband losses stabilize as convergence pricing takes hold, and the Cox/Liberty deals deliver scale synergies, the equity could re-rate toward the $190 median analyst target, roughly 46% upside, with the 43.6% short interest amplifying any positive surprise into a squeeze. Bull case: $190-210.

### Bear Case
The broadband base is in secular decline to structurally advantaged FWA and fiber competitors, and no amount of convergence bundling has reversed it. EBITDA has turned negative, estimates are falling 5-10% across every window, and three of the last four quarters missed. Each refinancing at 7%+ compresses equity value against a flat-to-declining EBITDA base with 4.15x leverage. Value-destructive buybacks confirm management misjudged the trajectory. The Cox and Liberty deals add integration risk, dilution, and assumed debt during a period of dense leadership turnover. If losses accelerate and EBITDA declines further, the compressed multiple is justified, not anomalous, and the stock drifts toward the $120 analyst low or below. Bear case: $105-120.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.** This is the fourth explicit AVOID and the sixth consecutive negative-scoring read; the score has fallen from 5.5 (March) to 3.6 now, driven by declines in valuation (8.5→4.5 across the sequence), competitive position (6.5→3.0), and financial health (5.0→3.5). The current data confirms rather than reverses that trajectory: EBITDA is now contracting, estimates are still falling, and the two-mega-deal execution risk is unchanged.

**Re-entry criteria:**
- **Broadband subscriber stabilization**: two consecutive quarters of net internet losses better than -50K (vs. -120K in Q1 2026), demonstrating that competitive response is working rather than the secular thesis holding.
- **EBITDA re-inflection**: Adjusted EBITDA returning to positive year-over-year growth, confirming rate and cost discipline can offset subscriber losses.
- **Estimate revision turn**: current-year and next-year consensus stabilizing and turning positive over a 90-day window.
- **Deal clarity**: both the Cox and Liberty transactions closing cleanly with articulated, quantified synergy delivery, removing the integration/dilution/governance overhang.

**Timeline for headwinds to persist:** The secular broadband competition (FWA and fiber) is a multi-year structural pressure, not a cyclical trough. The deal integration overhang runs to the August 31, 2027 Liberty backstop date. We expect no clean resolution to the core subscriber-erosion debate before mid-2027 at the earliest. The next earnings print (options market pricing a ±13.4% move) is the near-term data point to watch; a subscriber surprise in either direction would materially move the setup.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: The next earnings report is the clearest dated catalyst. The pattern of three misses in four quarters and estimate cuts across every window raises the probability of another miss. Beyond that, no single dated event forces the stock lower; the thesis is a slow secular grind (subscriber losses, EBITDA decline, refinancing cost creep) rather than a cliff event.

**Timing window**: The earnings catalyst resolves within weeks, but the structural thesis plays out over multiple quarters to years. A slow-grind short carries for a long time with meaningful borrow cost.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Extreme. 43.6% of float short with 5.0 days to cover. This is a deeply crowded short. The June 29 intraday spike to $168 (from $133) on positioning demonstrates the squeeze mechanics. Institutional ownership is high (controlled-company structure with Liberty ~29% and A/N ~13%), which reduces true float and amplifies squeeze potential.

**Borrow and options signal**: Near-term IV is in steep backwardation, making both puts and borrow expensive. Put/call skew is only mildly negative (balanced near-term, -8.6% at three months); the options market is not confirming a decisive bearish tilt, which is a warning sign for shorts. The stock has already fallen 68%; much of the bear thesis is priced.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Downside to the $105-120 bear range is roughly 8-19% from $130. Upside squeeze risk on a subscriber-stabilization surprise or deal progress could be 30%+ given 43.6% SI (the June 29 spike was ~26% intraday). The reward:risk is worse than 1:1 against the short: limited remaining downside versus large squeeze risk.

**Verdict**: **CROWDED SHORT.** The bear thesis is correct on fundamentals, but 43.6% short interest, a 68% realized drawdown, high institutional ownership, and expensive borrow make the remaining downside small relative to squeeze risk. This is a stock to avoid owning, not to short. Do not initiate a short position.

## Conclusion

Charter is cheap for a reason. The trailing multiple of ~3.5x and ~$5B of FCF are real, and the balance sheet is not a solvency risk despite screens that flag it. But the equity thesis for a leveraged cable operator depends on EBITDA compounding, and that mechanism has broken: EBITDA declined 2.2% in Q1 2026, the broadband base is shrinking 400K+ subscribers per year to structurally advantaged FWA and fiber competitors, and consensus estimates are falling 5-10% across every forward window. Value-destructive buybacks confirm that management, however well-aligned, misjudged the trajectory. Two parallel mega-mergers add integration, dilution, and governance risk into a 2027 backstop date, during a period of dense leadership turnover.

The insider buying and the deeply negative CEO comp-actually-paid are genuine positives, but they are not a catalyst, and all the buying was above today's price. Until subscriber losses stabilize, EBITDA re-inflects, and estimate revisions turn, the compressed multiple is a warning rather than an opportunity. We maintain **AVOID**. The 43.6% short interest makes this a stock to sidestep rather than short; the squeeze risk on any positive surprise outweighs the limited remaining downside.
