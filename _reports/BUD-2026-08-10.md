## Executive Summary

AB InBev has done what it said it would do. H1 2026 delivered organic revenue growth of 5.7% to $31,927M, normalized EBITDA of $11,375M (+5.6% organic), gross margin expansion of 88bps, underlying EPS of $2.18 (+22.1% reported, +11.0% constant currency), free cash flow of $3.9bn (up $2.5bn year over year), and net debt/normalized EBITDA of 2.86x versus 3.27x a year earlier. Four consecutive quarterly EPS beats (2.3% to 8.2%) and modestly rising consensus (FY2026 $4.46, +2.1% over 90 days; FY2027 $4.97, +1.8%) confirm improving visibility. The ADR has appreciated 36.5% over twelve months.

The operating story is working; the question is what remains to be paid for. Three adjustments materially compress the apparent growth. First, roughly 11 percentage points of the ~22% H1 underlying EPS growth is dollar translation; true operating compounding is low-double-digit, and management simultaneously flags *transactional* FX as a margin drag in Mexico, Colombia, Brazil and South Africa. Currency is two-sided, not a free tailwind. Second, reported attributable profit of $6,314M in H1 (basic EPS $3.20) contains approximately $2.0bn of non-underlying items, overwhelmingly non-cash mark-to-market gains on derivatives hedging share-based obligations; underlying EPS of $2.18 is the only usable base, and the gain reverses symmetrically if the shares retrace. Third, the two largest developed profit pools are contributing nothing: US EBITDA +0.1% in Q2 (+0.2% H1) despite being the #1 share gainer in total alcohol per Circana, and European EBITDA declined low single digits. Group EBITDA growth is carried by Brazil (+16.1%), Mexico, Colombia and Ecuador, which raises the currency and political risk embedded in the multiple.

At $83.76 the stock trades at ~18.8x FY2026 and ~16.9x FY2027 consensus underlying EPS, ~22.5x trailing FY2025 underlying EPS of $3.73, and ~9.5-10.2x EV/TTM EBITDA of roughly $22.4bn. That is a reasonable price for the world's largest brewer with a genuine deleveraging tailwind and a differentiated distribution platform, but it is not a mispricing. Our base case of $93 (holding the current forward multiple and rolling forward one year of consensus EPS) implies +11%, against a $67 bear case (-20%) if the dollar strengthens and China deteriorates further. Probability-weighted, including the 1.68% dividend, expected return is high-single-digit and approximately fair. HOLD, with a defined accumulation zone in the low-to-mid $70s.

## Company Overview

AB InBev is the largest brewer globally, moving 561.1 million hectolitres in FY2025 on $59.3bn of net revenue and $21.2bn of normalized EBITDA. H1 2026 volumes were 280.4 million hL, up 0.8% organically (beer +1.2%, non-beer -1.5%).

The volume base is far less American than the brand names suggest. By H1 2026 hectolitres: Middle Americas 76.0m (+4.7% organic), South America 75.4m (+0.5%), EMEA 45.2m (+1.1%), Asia Pacific 42.1m (-2.8%), North America 41.6m (-1.8%). North America is the smallest of the five real segments at 14.8% of volume, while Middle Americas plus South America is 54%. Profit weighting differs, since the US and Mexico carry much higher revenue per hectolitre, but the earnings *growth* weighting now follows volume: Latin America is doing the work.

Strategy rests on three pillars. Category leadership is funded by $4.1bn of H1 sales-and-marketing spend, up 9% and equal to 12.8% of revenue; management claims share gained or maintained in 70% of markets. Kantar BrandZ 2026 places eight of the world's ten most valuable beer brands in the portfolio, Corona #1 and Budweiser #2. Digitization runs through BEES, the company's B2B ordering platform, live in 30 markets, serving more than 6 million customers and capturing 72% of company revenue; Q2 GMV was $15.0bn (+16%), of which third-party Marketplace GMV was approximately $1.2bn (+50%). Optimization means deleveraging: 2.86x at 30 June 2026 against 3.27x a year prior, with net interest expense down 12% year over year to $583M in Q2.

Portfolio mix is shifting deliberately: Q2 revenue growth of +2.7% in mainstream/core, +6.9% above-core beer, +13% "Balanced Choices," +27% no-alcohol, +44% Beyond Beer. Megabrand globalization is the strongest evidence of pricing power: Corona +17%, Stella Artois +19% and Michelob Ultra +21% revenue growth outside their home markets, with Corona posting double-digit volume growth in 37 markets and Michelob Ultra taking 40% of its Q2 volume growth from outside the US.

A structural note worth carrying: the "Corona outside its home market" framing appears to exclude the US Corona profit pool, which sits with Constellation Brands following the 2013 Grupo Modelo divestiture. That arrangement was not verified in the available sources and is treated here as unconfirmed background.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement quality.** Q2 2026 revenue of $16,660M grew 11.0% as reported against 5.6% organic; scope was negative on volumes (-557k hL), so the ~5.4pp gap is predominantly translation. Gross margin rose 99bps to 57.5%, but normalized EBITDA margin rose only 4bps to 35.6%, and in H1 the EBITDA margin *contracted* 5bps organically despite 88bps of gross-margin expansion. The bridge is SG&A growing 6.5% organically against 5.6% revenue growth, driven by the marketing step-up. Management is choosing share defence over near-term margin, which is a legitimate choice but means investors underwriting margin expansion as an earnings driver are underwriting the wrong variable.

Normalized EBIT grew faster than EBITDA (+8.0% versus +5.8%), which flatters operating leverage. Derived D&A was $1,334M in Q2 2026 versus $1,288M a year earlier, growth of 3.6% against 11.0% reported revenue growth. Depreciation cannot lag revenue indefinitely; the EBIT-over-EBITDA growth premium should not be extrapolated.

**Reported versus underlying earnings.** This is the single most important adjustment. Reported attributable profit was $3,751M in Q2 (basic EPS $1.90) against underlying profit of $2,390M (underlying EPS $1.21). The gap is non-underlying net finance income of +$1,402M, essentially all mark-to-market on derivatives hedging the share-based payment program, deferred share instrument and restricted shares: 90.5 million instruments at 30 June, with the Brussels share price moving from €59.72 to €72.66 during the quarter. For H1, non-underlying finance income was +$2,033M against a reported-versus-underlying profit gap of $2,000M. Roughly a third of reported H1 attributable profit is this single non-cash line.

The treatment is disclosed, ordinary and economically justified: the hedges neutralize the cash cost of settling share-based obligations, so the gain offsets a rising settlement liability. No inference about management intent is warranted. The analytical consequence is narrow but firm: reported basic EPS ($3.20 in H1) overstates underlying EPS ($2.18) by 47%, and any P/E computed on reported earnings is meaningless. It also cuts the other way: a share-price retracement will depress reported profit with no operating deterioration. This item does not impair EBITDA, EBIT, cash-flow or leverage analysis.

A useful independent cross-check: consolidated profit rose from $4,568M to $7,061M in H1 while profit attributable to non-controlling interests was flat at $747M versus $744M. The increase is concentrated in parent-level, non-operating items rather than in operating improvement at the partially owned subsidiaries.

**Cash flow and capital intensity.** H1 2026 FCF of $3.9bn (12.2% of revenue) was up $2.5bn, implying an H1 2025 base near $1.4bn. This must not be annualized: AB InBev's cash generation is H2-weighted by working-capital seasonality, and a $2.5bn half-year swing is more likely timing than a permanent step-change. Doubling H1 2026 gives ~$7.8bn, but doubling H1 2025 would have given ~$2.8bn against a full-year figure almost certainly well above that, which itself demonstrates the seasonality problem. FY2026 net capex guidance of $3.5-4.0bn against an annualized ~$64bn revenue base implies capital intensity of 5.5-6.3%, genuinely low for a global manufacturing footprint.

**Balance sheet.** Net debt/normalized EBITDA of 2.86x (30 Jun 2026), 2.87x (31 Dec 2025), 3.27x (30 Jun 2025). Deriving trailing normalized EBITDA of ~$22.4bn (FY25 $21.2bn + H1 26 $11.375bn − H1 25 $10.156bn) implies net debt of roughly $64bn, though the absolute figure is derived rather than disclosed and is itself moved by the same weak dollar that inflated EBITDA. The reliable fact is the ratio improvement. Average gross debt coupon guided at ~4% for FY2026, with accretion and pension interest of $190-220M per quarter. At 2.86x, leverage remains well above the ~2x AB InBev has historically framed as optimal, which means the deleveraging EPS tailwind has further to run.

The maturity ladder and covenant terms were not verified. For an issuer with ~$64bn of net debt at a 4% coupon, that is a genuine gap, mitigated by the fact that interest expense is currently *falling*, which is inconsistent with adverse refinancing pressure to date.

**Data conflict to flag.** The market-data profile shows trailing and forward P/E both at 18.06 with EPS of $3.39, which is internally inconsistent ($83.76/$3.39 = 24.7x). Shares outstanding are also unreconciled: three convergent issuer data points imply ~1.97-1.98bn weighted-average shares, while LSEG-sourced reporting implies ~1.787bn, giving a market cap range of $148-164bn and a ~10% band around all per-share and EV metrics. Valuation below is anchored on consensus underlying EPS, which is basis-consistent, with EV/EBITDA expressed as a range.

## Growth Analysis

Volume growth is effectively nil. Total volumes +0.8-0.9%, beer +1.1-1.2%, non-beer -1.1% to -1.5%. On a 561m hL base, the entire equity story rests on price/mix, premiumization, cost control and deleveraging.

Within that, the composition matters:

- **Working:** Brazil (Q2 EBITDA +16.1%, +230bps margin; premium/super-premium volumes up mid-twenties), Mexico (EBITDA up high single digits), Colombia (high teens), Ecuador and Colombia at record Q2 volumes, Europe share gains in five of six key markets, and Canada as #1 share gainer in beer and Beyond Beer.
- **Flat:** US revenue +2.7% in Q2 on revenue/hL +3.3% with sales-to-retailers -1.9% and EBITDA +0.1%. STWs at -0.6% versus STRs at -1.9% signals wholesaler inventory build that will reverse; management notes the two converge on a full-year basis. Europe EBITDA declined low single digits despite volume growth.
- **Deteriorating:** China Q2 volumes -9.7%, revenue -8.8%, EBITDA -16.1% (H1 EBITDA -13.9%), with management conceding it underperformed an industry itself down mid-single digits. Roughly 4-5pp is company-specific share loss. South Africa volumes down low single digits, explicitly underperforming.

The TAM-expansion response is real: Beyond Beer +44% group-wide, with Cutwater the second-largest single-brand contributor to total company revenue growth in Q2 and the #1 share gainer in the entire US spirits industry; no-alcohol +27%, led by Michelob Ultra Zero. BEES Marketplace GMV of ~$1.2bn (+50%) monetizes already-sunk logistics at high incremental margin. But GMV is not revenue: AB InBev books a take rate, undisclosed, and at a mid-single-digit rate this implies well under $100M of quarterly revenue. Strategically significant, financially immaterial today.

Consensus revisions are positive but modest: FY2026 $4.46 (+1.6% 30d, +2.1% 90d), FY2027 $4.97 (+1.4%, +1.8%). This is not the >30% revision velocity that signals a mispriced inflection; it is a well-modeled staple where consensus is being nudged up by FX and beats. FY2026 guidance was reaffirmed rather than raised after a strong H1 with a World Cup tailwind, consistent either with genuine H2 caution (China, US shipment normalization, tougher World Cup comparisons) or a preference for beatable guidance. The four-quarter beat record argues at least partly for the latter, but a reaffirmation after a beat is not a raise.

Growth prospects score 5.5: durable low-double-digit constant-currency EPS compounding with a mechanical deleveraging kicker, offset by zero developed-market EBITDA growth, structural China share loss, and category volumes that are flat at best. GLP-1 adoption, generational moderation and cannabis substitution are widely cited category headwinds whose magnitude is not established by any evidence here; the fact that management's answer is TAM expansion rather than core beer volume growth is itself informative.

## Valuation Assessment

Using consensus underlying EPS at the $83.76 basis:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| P/E on FY2026E $4.46 | 18.8x |
| P/E on FY2027E $4.97 | 16.9x |
| P/E on FY2025 underlying $3.73 | 22.5x |
| EV/TTM normalized EBITDA (~$22.4bn) | ~9.5-10.2x |
| Dividend yield | 1.68% |
| Implied EPS growth FY26→FY27 | +11.4% |
| PEG (FY2026 multiple / FY27 growth) | ~1.65 |

Peer context: global brewers cluster wide. Heineken, whose 1H 2026 margin and cash improvement is the closest read-across, trades in the mid-to-high teens; Molson Coors trades near single digits as a structurally challenged US mass-market operator; premium spirits and staples compounders sit at 18-24x. BUD at 18.8x FY2026 sits at a premium to Heineken and a large premium to Molson Coors, justified by scale economics, BEES, and the deleveraging tailwind, but it is not a discount to anything. Historically ABI has traded 8-9x EV/EBITDA in periods of stress and 10-12x in confidence; ~9.5-10.2x is mid-range.

The bull argument on valuation is that consensus targets (low $85, median $96.50, high $111) all sit above spot, and that at 18.8x with 11% constant-currency EPS growth plus deleveraging the multiple is not demanding. There is something to that: the low target above spot is unusual and the recommendation mix (3 strong buy, 6 buy, 2 hold, no sells) is stable. But analyst targets are only meaningful confirmation when a stock is cheap relative to improving estimates. Here estimates are rising 1.5-2% per quarter, not 30%, and the multiple is mid-range rather than compressed. The targets corroborate; they do not establish a mispricing.

The bear argument is more specific. Roughly half of the headline EPS growth is dollar translation, and translation reverses. If the dollar strengthens materially, reported growth compresses to the low double digits or below with no operating change, and the multiple is likely to compress with it. Transactional FX is already a named margin headwind in four markets. The earnings the market is capitalizing at 18.8x are FX-flattered and Latin America-levered, with 10.6% of consolidated profit leaking to non-controlling interests at Ambev, Budweiser APAC and AB InBev Africa.

Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation input, and the 36.5% twelve-month move was driven by real fundamental improvement: deleveraging, four beats, margin gains, FX. The issue is the level, not the path: at $83.76 the stock is 3.4% below its 52-week high on a multiple that already embeds continued execution, leaving base-case upside of roughly 11% against bear-case downside of 20%.

Valuation score 5.2: fair, with no identified mispricing and a thin margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is scale plus owned route-to-market, not brands alone. Procurement scale across malt, aluminium and glass, distribution density in Latin America, and BEES as the digital layer over that density are the structural advantages. BEES capturing 72% of company revenue across 30 markets and 6m+ customers converts sunk logistics into a two-sided platform; the $1.2bn quarterly third-party Marketplace GMV growing 50% is the incremental economics. Sub-scale competitors in Latin America cannot easily replicate owned route-to-market at that density.

Trajectory by market is mixed but net positive ex-China. Winning: #1 share gainer in US total alcohol (both Q2 and H1), with Michelob Ultra, Busch Light and Busch Light Apple the top three industry volume share gainers; Michelob Ultra Zero the #1 no-alcohol share gainer; Cutwater the #1 share gainer across US spirits; Canada; Brazil premium; Europe (five of six key markets). Losing: China, where the portfolio's premium/on-premise skew is structurally exposed to a channel shift toward in-home consumption, and the remedy of building in-home presence means competing at lower price points against entrenched local brewers; and South Africa.

The premiumization-plus-no-alcohol playbook is the industry playbook, not proprietary: Heineken is executing the same strategy with improving 1H 2026 margins and cash. That compresses the relative-value case: AB InBev's operational improvement is not differentiated enough to command a widening premium.

One measurement caution: the shift in revenue mix toward Beyond Beer, no-alcohol and premium is not a reduction in geographic or channel concentration. Middle Americas plus South America remains 54% of volumes, North America EBITDA growth remains ~0%, and China remains a double-digit-decline drag. Mix has diversified; concentration has not.

Competitive position score 6.0: a wide, durable moat with genuinely improving share in the US and Latin America, offset by clear execution failure in the second-largest beer market on earth.

## Risk Assessment

**China structural, not cyclical.** Losing 4-5pp of share inside a market contracting mid-single digits, with EBITDA down 16.1% in Q2, is the clearest execution failure in the disclosure. Management cites sequential share improvement and weather, and part of the drag sits in non-controlling interests, but a premium on-premise portfolio facing a durable channel shift is a multi-year problem.

**FX reversal.** The largest single swing factor. Approximately 11pp of H1's 22.1% underlying EPS growth is translation. A dollar rally cuts reported growth roughly in half with no operating deterioration and would likely compress the multiple concurrently.

**Latin America concentration.** 54% of volumes and the great majority of EBITDA growth. Brings currency, inflation (Argentina revenue +mid-teens on pricing while volumes decline), excise-tax and political risk. Nigeria and South Africa both soft.

**Leverage.** ~$64bn of derived net debt at 2.86x. Serviceable at a ~4% coupon with interest expense falling, but the maturity ladder, covenant terms and fixed/floating split were not verified. Refinancing at above the embedded coupon is a reasonable default assumption for any tranche rolling.

**Minority-interest leakage.** $747M of H1 consolidated profit (10.6%) accrues to others, disproportionately from the strongest-growing markets.

**Disclosure regime.** As a foreign private issuer, BUD files 20-F annually and 6-Ks interim: no 10-Q, no Form 4 insider data, no DEF 14A, IFRS with no US-GAAP reconciliation. Standard US screens return nothing. The FY2025 20-F risk-factor and legal-proceedings text was not read in this analysis, so newly added or modified risk factors and litigation contingencies (Brazilian tax disputes at Ambev are a known historical exposure) are not established.

**Reference-shareholder overhang.** E.P.S. SA, a legacy-family holding vehicle, sold ~10 million shares on 3 August 2026 for €731M (~€73.10/share), taking its stake from >67M (~3.75%) to ~57M (~3.19%), with JPMorgan as bookrunner and the stock trading lower on the print. This is a secondary sale: no dilution, no new shares, no proceeds to the company, a transfer of roughly 0.5% of shares outstanding. Holding-company sales frequently reflect liquidity, diversification or estate planning with no view on intrinsic value. But given that no Form 4 dataset exists for this issuer, it is the only insider-sentiment proxy available, and it points one way: a long-term holder monetized four days after a strong print, into a 37% year-to-date rally. Whether the remaining reference holders face lock-ups or standstills is unverified.

**Category demand.** GLP-1 adoption, generational moderation and cannabis substitution are directionally accepted headwinds of unquantified magnitude. Non-beer volumes already declining 1.5% in H1 is at least consistent with pressure.

Risk factors score 5.0: the FX-reversal and China-structural risks in particular are not obviously priced into an 18.8x multiple.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 27.3% against 22.9% trailing realized vol is rich by 4.4 points, with the term structure in backwardation (27.3% at one week, 23.9% one month, 21.5% three months, 22.8% six months). The one-week put/call skew is 4.6% with a 1.19 put/call volume ratio, but the one-month and three-month skews are flat at 0.1% and the six-month skew is -3.7% (call-tilted), while total put/call volume and open-interest ratios are 0.40 and 0.39. The reading is a short-dated, event-specific hedge (the three-day expiry prices only ±2.3%) sitting on top of a structurally call-tilted book. Short interest is negligible at 0.8% of float with 3.0 days to cover, so there is no bearish crowd. The composite signal: near-term event risk is being hedged with modest urgency (plausibly the residual float overhang from the 3 August block), but nothing in the six-month structure prices deterioration. For a HOLD, this argues against paying up here and in favor of waiting for the near-term vol premium to decay.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $105 (+25%)

The deleveraging path from 2.86x toward the historical ~2x target continues, delivering mechanical EPS accretion on top of low-double-digit constant-currency operating growth; consensus FY2027 EPS proves conservative at roughly $5.10 given the beat record and reaffirmed-then-exceeded guidance. Latin America momentum holds (Brazil premium up mid-twenties, Mexico and Colombia compounding), China stabilizes as sequential share improvement continues and the in-home build gains traction, and the US STR/STW gap closes without a shipment air pocket. Beyond Beer and no-alcohol establish that the company can grow ahead of a flat beer category, and BEES Marketplace begins to be valued as a platform rather than an ordering tool. The dollar stays soft, reported growth stays in the low twenties, and the multiple expands toward 21x FY2027 as the market re-rates a deleveraged, share-gaining global staple. The $6bn buyback (32% complete) finishes, retiring ~3.6% of shares. Consensus median of $96.50 and high of $111 bracket this outcome.

### Bear Case — $67 (-20%)

The dollar reverses, and reported EPS growth collapses from ~22% to high single digits without a single operational change; the same arithmetic that produced the 11pp translation tailwind runs backwards. China deepens rather than stabilizes as the on-premise channel shift proves structural, and the in-home pivot dilutes margin. The US STW/STR convergence takes the form of a negative shipment quarter, turning +0.1% EBITDA growth negative in the largest developed profit pool while Europe stays down low single digits. Latin America absorbs a currency or excise shock, exposing the 54%-of-volume concentration and the 10.6% minority-interest leakage. Reported profit falls sharply as the equity-derivative mark-to-market reverses on a lower share price, producing headline "earnings collapse" optics that pressure sentiment even though EBITDA is intact. The multiple compresses to 15x FY2026 $4.46 = $67. Additional reference-shareholder selling into a weakening tape amplifies the move.

### Base Case — $93 (+11%)

Constant-currency EPS compounds at 11-13%, FX contributes little either way, deleveraging continues at roughly 0.3-0.4 turns per year, and China remains a drag that stops worsening. FY2027 underlying EPS lands near consensus $4.97. Holding today's 18.8x forward multiple flat and rolling forward one year gives $93. Adding the 1.68% dividend, twelve-month total return is roughly 13%.

Probability-weighting 55% base, 20% bull, 25% bear gives an expected price return of about +6% plus 1.7% dividend: high-single-digit, approximately the cost of equity for a business of this quality. That is fair value, and it is why the rating is HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (growth hold — a positive but unexceptional 12-month appreciation thesis)

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the Q3 2026 trading update (29 October 2026) and the FY2026 release (expected February 2027).

**Base case $93** — 18.8x FY2027 consensus underlying EPS of $4.97, holding the current forward multiple flat. **+11.0%** from $83.76, or ~12.7% including the dividend.
**Bull case $105** — 20.6x FY2027 EPS of $5.10 on a soft dollar, China stabilization and completed buyback. **+25.4%**.
**Bear case $67** — 15.0x FY2026 EPS of $4.46 on FX reversal and a US shipment air pocket. **-20.0%**.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price in the **$72-76** range with fundamentals intact, which is ~15-16x FY2027 consensus, restoring roughly 20-25% base-case upside and a real margin of safety. This is the preferred entry.
2. **China EBITDA returning to growth** or volume decline narrowing to low single digits with confirmed share stabilization, for two consecutive reporting periods. This removes the clearest structural drag and would support a 20x+ multiple.
3. **US segment EBITDA growth above 3%** on positive sales-to-retailers, demonstrating that the marketing reinvestment is converting to volume rather than defending a shrinking pool.
4. **Net debt/EBITDA below 2.5x** with the FY2026 release, confirming the deleveraging pace and quantifying the interest-expense tailwind into FY2027.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. **US segment EBITDA turning negative** on the Q3 print as the STW/STR gap converges, confirming that share gains in a shrinking pool do not produce profit growth.
2. **Constant-currency underlying EPS growth falling below 8%** in any quarter, which would break the low-double-digit compounding assumption underpinning the base case.
3. **A dollar rally that turns translation into a reported-EPS headwind** while transactional FX margin pressure in Mexico, Colombia, Brazil and South Africa persists, the two-sided exposure resolving unfavorably on both sides simultaneously.
4. **Further reference-shareholder block sales** exceeding 1% of shares outstanding, or a China EBITDA decline steeper than 20% in a quarter.

**Reassessment date:** 29 October 2026 (Q3 trading update). The specific items to check are China volume and share commentary, the US STR/STW convergence, sales-and-marketing spend as a percentage of revenue, and buyback progress against the $6bn authorization.

**What would change the analysis independent of price:** disclosure of the FY2025 20-F risk factors and legal proceedings (not read here), the debt maturity ladder and covenant terms, the BEES Marketplace take rate, and reconciliation of the ~10% shares-outstanding discrepancy between issuer EPS-implied (~1.975bn) and LSEG-implied (~1.787bn) counts.

## Conclusion

AB InBev is a high-quality, wide-moat operator executing a coherent plan: reinvest gross-margin gains into share defence, expand the addressable market through Beyond Beer and no-alcohol, monetize owned distribution through BEES, and delever. The evidence that it is working is concrete: 5.7% organic H1 revenue growth, 88bps of gross-margin expansion, four consecutive EPS beats, 2.86x leverage against 3.27x a year ago, and interest expense down 12%.

The evidence that it is fully paid for is equally concrete. At $83.76 the ADR trades ~18.8x FY2026 and ~16.9x FY2027 consensus underlying EPS and ~9.5-10.2x EV/EBITDA, a premium to Heineken and mid-range against its own history, on earnings where roughly half the headline growth is dollar translation, the largest developed profit pool grows at 0%, Europe shrinks, China loses share inside a contracting market, and 10.6% of consolidated profit leaks to minorities. Reported EPS is unusable: H1 basic EPS of $3.20 overstates underlying $2.18 by 47% on non-cash equity-derivative marks that reverse if the shares fall. The only available insider-sentiment proxy, a €731M legacy-family block sale four days after a strong print, points the wrong way, and near-term implied volatility is rich and backwardated.

Base-case upside of 11% against 20% bear-case downside, with 1.7% of carry, is fair compensation and no more. Existing holders should stay. New capital should wait for the low-to-mid $70s, where 15-16x FY2027 earnings on a deleveraging global staple restores the asymmetry, or for evidence that China has stopped losing share and the US has started converting share gains into profit growth.

**HOLD.**
