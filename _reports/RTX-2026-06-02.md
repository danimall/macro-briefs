**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

RTX is one of three aerospace and defense companies with genuine exposure to both the defense spending cycle and the commercial aerospace aftermarket simultaneously. The FY2025 results confirm a clean recovery off the 2023 GTF (geared turbofan) powder-metal trough: revenue grew 9.7% to $88.6B, operating margin expanded from 5.2% (2023) to 10.5% (2025), and diluted EPS more than doubled across two years to $4.96. The Q1 2026 print continued the trend, with revenue up 8.7% to $22.1B, operating margin at 11.6%, and a 16.1% EPS beat. Backlog stands at $271B, up from $218B two years ago, providing multi-year revenue visibility with roughly a quarter converting within twelve months. The franchise is structurally sound and improving operationally.

The investment question is whether the current price compensates for the risks. At $174.48, RTX trades at roughly 25x current-year consensus EPS ($6.90) and 23x next-year ($7.56), with consensus estimates stable to slightly negative over the past 90 days (current-quarter +0.4%, next-quarter -2.7%). The stock has fallen ~19% from its 52-week high of $214.50, and the decline coincided with a "sell the news" reaction to a clean Q1 beat paired with a FY2026 revenue guide (~$93B midpoint) that came in modestly below Street expectations. That sequence indicates the market had already priced in strong execution; the valuation now requires continued upside surprises rather than mere delivery.

Three idiosyncratic overhangs cap the asymmetry: an open SEC investigation into the 2023 GTF disclosures with a securities class action on appeal, a fresh three-year DOJ Deferred Prosecution Agreement clock that started in April 2026 (compliance monitor only just engaged), and a 2025 executive order that could restrict capital returns for defense contractors deemed to underperform on government contracts. None is likely to be catastrophic, but together they reduce the probability of clean multiple expansion. We rate RTX a HOLD with a bullish lean, and would become a buyer on a pullback toward the low-$160s or on resolution of the compliance overhangs.

## Company Overview

RTX, formed from the 2020 merger of United Technologies and Raytheon, operates three segments that span both major aerospace and defense demand cycles:

- **Collins Aerospace** (FY25: $30.2B revenue, 16.3% operating margin): avionics, power systems, interiors, landing and braking, and connected aviation. Aftermarket-heavy and the highest-margin segment. Boeing and Airbus together are roughly 16% of segment sales.
- **Pratt & Whitney** ($32.9B, 7.9% margin; fastest grower at +17%): GTF commercial engines powering the A320neo/A220/E2 families, the exclusive F135 engine for the F-35, and next-generation programs (XA103/NGAP). Airbus is roughly 29% of segment sales.
- **Raytheon** ($28.0B, 11.5% margin): integrated air and missile defense: Patriot, LTAMDS, NASAMS, SM-3/SM-6, AMRAAM, AIM-9X, Tomahawk, SPY-6, and hypersonics.

The FY25 revenue mix was 38% US government, 8% foreign military sales, 7% foreign direct, and 48% commercial. International sales reached 47% of total revenue and continue to rise, driven by NATO rearmament and missile-defense demand. The dual-cycle structure is the franchise's defining feature: it captures record defense budgets and a robust commercial aftermarket recovery at the same time, which smooths the cyclicality that pure-play primes and pure-play commercial suppliers carry individually.

The fundamental trajectory is clean and improving:

Revenue growth is organic and broad-based. FY25 organic growth of approximately $8.9B was led by Pratt & Whitney aftermarket (+$4.8B), Collins (+$2.6B), and Raytheon (+$1.7B). In Q1'26 all three segments grew organically, with Raytheon bookings up 51% year over year. Margin expansion reflects recovery from the 2023 powder-metal charge and 2024 legal charges plus operating leverage, with cost of sales improving to 79.2% of revenue in Q1'26.

Cash flow quality is high. FY25 operating cash flow of $10.6B exceeded net income, free cash flow was $7.9B against light capex of roughly $2.6B, and non-service pension income of approximately $1.2B per year is a structural earnings tailwind from well-funded plans. The balance sheet is investment-grade and deleveraging: total debt $37.9B, debt-to-capital 36% and improving, roughly $3.4B repaid in 2025, $7.4B cash plus an undrawn $5B revolver. Moody's upgraded the outlook to Baa1/positive in February 2026; S&P holds BBB+/stable.

One data point that screening systems flag wrongly: the Altman Z-Score of 1.45 sits in the conventional "distress zone." This is a mechanical artifact of roughly $85B of goodwill and intangibles (54% of assets) created by the UTC–Raytheon merger accounting, not a sign of operating distress. The Piotroski score (5/5 on available signals), $7.9B FCF, investment-grade ratings, and rising margins all point to a financially healthy company. The same merger accounting drives approximately $2.0B per year of non-cash acquisition amortization (a $1.15–1.20 EPS drag), meaning cash earnings materially exceed reported GAAP EPS, which understates reported profitability.

The earnings-beat record reinforces confidence in forward estimates: four consecutive beats (16.1%, 4.6%, 19.5%, 7.0% surprises over the last four quarters). Management has demonstrated conservative guidance and reliable delivery on the bottom line.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on durable demand from both cycles. On defense, RTX is well-positioned for the "Golden Dome for America" missile-defense initiative ($24.4B in the 2025 Act) through Patriot, LTAMDS, SM-3, and NASAMS, plus $25.4B of munitions and supply-chain resiliency funding. With 45% of remaining performance obligations tied to long-cycle aftermarket maintenance contracts, the backlog provides multi-year visibility.

On commercial, Pratt & Whitney is recovering off the GTF trough, with aftermarket revenue rising sharply and GTF Advantage achieving FAA/EASA certification in 2025. Collins benefits from the same aftermarket-led recovery at the highest segment margin.

Consensus estimates frame the growth as steady rather than accelerating: current-year and next-year EPS imply roughly 10% EPS growth, with revenue guided to mid-single-digit top-line growth for FY2026. Estimate revisions are not providing a momentum tailwind. Over 90 days, the next-quarter estimate fell 2.7% and current-year rose only 1.3%; over 30 days the readings are essentially flat. This is a solid, visible growth story, but it is not the kind of estimate-revision pattern that signals the market is systematically underestimating the trajectory. Management delivered a clean Q1 beat, but the forward framing disappointed expectations that were already elevated.

The peer median P/E of 40.2 is inflated by Boeing's depressed-earnings multiple and the GE/HWM premium names. The relevant comparison set for RTX's defense-weighted, dual-cycle profile is GD (21.8x) and LMT (25.7x). RTX at ~25x sits at the upper end of that pure-defense range, which is defensible given the commercial-aftermarket exposure GD and LMT lack, but it is not a discount. On cash earnings (adjusting for the ~$1.15–1.20/share non-cash amortization drag), the multiple is more like 21x, which is more reasonable but not cheap.

The cycle-adjusted argument does not rescue the valuation here. RTX is not at a cyclical earnings trough: margins are recovering and approaching mid-cycle norms, EPS more than doubled over two years, and the powder-metal charge is resolved. The earnings base is roughly normalized, so the trailing-to-forward gap is modest, and there is no compelling case that consensus is materially understating earnings power. With estimate revisions flat-to-negative, analyst targets (mean $215, median $220) are not behaving as floors that will be revised upward; they more likely reflect optimism the market is in the process of discounting, given the stock's slide to $174.

The valuation score is a 5. The business quality is high and the multiple is not egregious, but there is no specific reason the market is mispricing the stock to the upside. A low-double-digit growth rate at ~25x forward (cash-adjusted ~21x) is roughly fairly valued, and the absence of estimate-revision momentum means the path to multiple expansion is unclear.

## Competitive Landscape

RTX's competitive position is strong and stable-to-improving. The moats are genuine: long-cycle defense programs with high switching costs, ~20-year commercial aftermarket maintenance contracts (45% of remaining performance obligations), exclusive franchises (F135 as the sole F-35 engine, SPY-6, Patriot), and the scale to invest across both cycles. Backlog growth over two years confirms share capture in a rising-demand environment.

Key competitors are GE Aerospace (engines), Lockheed Martin and Northrop Grumman (defense primes), and Honeywell (avionics). The defense competitive environment is shifting, with new entrants bringing different technology and business models, and government demands for expanded IP rights and offset obligations (~$14B notional ICIP outstanding). Pratt & Whitney's commercial position carries customer concentration (Airbus ~29% of segment sales), and the GTF reliability episode dented the franchise's reputation, though the segment is now the fastest-growing in the portfolio. Net, the trajectory is positive: RTX is gaining backlog and benefiting from structural tailwinds (NATO rearmament, missile-defense demand, commercial aftermarket recovery) that are broad-based and confirmed across multiple programs and budget lines.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the binding constraint on this rating, and the risk score has been declining across recent reviews for specific reasons:

1. **Pratt & Whitney powder-metal matter — winding down, tail risk remains.** The 2023 pre-tax charge was $2.9B. The customer-compensation accrual has fallen from a $2.8B peak to $0.5B (Q1'26), with ~$0.7B cash impact expected in 2026 and AOG (aircraft on ground) levels elevated through 2026. The financial exposure is largely behind the company; the residual is legal and reputational.

2. **SEC investigation and securities litigation.** The SEC is investigating the 2023 GTF disclosures (subpoenas issued). The securities class action was dismissed in September 2025 but is on appeal to the Second Circuit, and shareholder derivative suits are pending. Management states estimates remain "subject to variability."

3. **Compliance overhang with a fresh three-year clock.** RTX operates under two DOJ Deferred Prosecution Agreements (FCPA/AECA Middle East payments; defective-pricing major-fraud counts), an SEC Administrative Order, and a State Department ITAR Consent Agreement ($200M penalty, $100M suspended). It paid ~$964M in 2024. The independent compliance monitor was engaged only in April 2026, restarting the three-year DPA cooperation term from that date. Any DOJ-determined breach could trigger criminal prosecution; debarment, though remote, would be existential for a defense prime.

4. **Executive-order risk on capital returns.** A 2025 EO may limit distributions, repurchases, and executive compensation for defense contractors deemed to underperform on government contracts. RTX explicitly warns this "could adversely affect the market price of our common stock." This is a novel, idiosyncratic policy risk that compounds RTX's already-minimal buyback activity ($50M in 2025, ~$0.6B remaining authorization).

5. **Pratt CAS litigation.** DCMA claims contesting P&W's IR&D allocation ($1.7B + $1.1B) and collaborator-parts costing methodology total well over $4B notional plus interest. RTX won a partial ASBCA ruling, but the CAFC remanded one claim in December 2025; litigation continues.

6. **Concentration and geopolitical.** Airbus ~29% of P&W sales; US government 38% of total. China sanctions on the Raytheon business (Taiwan arms sales); Russia counter-sanctions name RTX management and board members.

The combination of an open SEC probe, a freshly-started DOJ monitor period, and the capital-return EO is what depresses the risk score to 5. None of these is a high-probability catastrophe, but each is a specific, dateable overhang that the market is appropriately wary of, and they collectively reduce the chance of clean multiple expansion over the next 12 months.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 34.3% against 23.6% one-year historical volatility, rich by 10.7 points, and the term structure is in backwardation (1-week 34.3% > 6-month 27.7%), which typically signals an imminent catalyst window. The put/call skew is elevated at 5.8% (more demand for downside protection), though the put/call volume ratio of 0.43 and OI ratio of 0.84 are not extreme. IV collapsed from 97.8% to 34.3% over the prior few sessions, indicating a specific event premium recently bled off. The signal is consistent with a market hedging against near-term disappointment (guidance, litigation headlines, or the compliance monitor process) rather than pricing systemic distress. It supports patience on entry rather than chasing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($210–230, +20% to +32%).** Dual-cycle demand proves durable: defense backlog converts faster on Golden Dome and munitions funding, commercial aftermarket accelerates as the GTF fleet returns to service, and margins continue expanding toward mid-cycle norms. The SEC probe and DPA clock resolve without material penalties, lifting the compliance discount. Cash earnings (which exceed GAAP by ~$1.15/share) get more credit, supporting a re-rating toward GE/HWM premium territory. Estimate revisions turn positive and analyst targets ($220 median) prove to be floors. The 5-year cumulative TSR of 287.6 vs. 230.5 peer group continues.

**Bear Case ($150–160, -8% to -14%).** The FY26 guide proves the leading edge of a slowdown as defense budget timing slips and commercial recovery plateaus. A DOJ-determined DPA breach or adverse SEC outcome introduces a fresh penalty or, in the tail, debarment risk. The capital-return EO constrains the dividend-and-deleveraging story. Multiple compresses toward GD's 21.8x on roughly $7 of EPS, landing the stock in the low-$150s. Estimate revisions stay negative and the stock continues to de-rate from its peak.

The base case sits in the middle: a fairly-valued, high-quality compounder where the risks are real but manageable and the upside requires catalysts that are not yet visible in estimate revisions.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (bullish lean / growth_hold).** Reassessment timeline: Q2 2026 earnings (late July) and any material development on the SEC probe or DPA monitor process.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$160s (roughly 23x current-year EPS, cash-adjusted ~20x), restoring asymmetric upside.
- Resolution of the SEC GTF investigation or the securities class-action appeal without material penalty, removing the compliance discount.
- A turn in estimate revisions: current-year and next-year EPS revised up >5% over 90 days, confirming the market is underestimating the trajectory.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A DOJ-determined DPA breach, a new criminal proceeding, or any signal of debarment risk.
- Operating margin reversing below 10% or FY26 revenue guidance cut below $90B.
- A fresh GTF-related charge, indicating the powder-metal matter is not contained.

## Conclusion

RTX is a high-quality, dual-cycle aerospace and defense franchise with a $271B backlog, expanding margins, $7.9B of free cash flow, and an improving credit profile. The operating story is genuinely good and the four-quarter beat streak supports confidence in near-term delivery. What is missing is asymmetry in the price. At ~25x forward (cash-adjusted ~21x) with flat-to-negative estimate revisions, a slightly-light FY26 guide, and three specific overhangs (the SEC GTF probe, a freshly-started DOJ monitor clock, and the capital-return executive order), the risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling. The recent slide has improved the entry point, but not yet to the level where the compliance and policy discounts are adequately compensated. We rate RTX a HOLD with a bullish lean and would accumulate on weakness toward the low-$160s or on resolution of the legal overhangs.
