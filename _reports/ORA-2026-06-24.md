**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ormat Technologies is the world's only vertically integrated geothermal company; it explores, drills, designs, manufactures (via its proprietary Ormat Energy Converter), builds, owns, and operates geothermal and recovered-energy plants. That integration, combined with six decades of reservoir data and a fleet running at an ~84% capacity factor, gives Ormat a genuine moat in baseload renewable power. The structural driver is the collision of surging data-center electricity demand with hyperscalers' need for 24/7 carbon-free supply. Intermittent solar and wind cannot meet that requirement at scale; geothermal can. This has begun to show up in the numbers: new U.S. PPAs are now pricing above $100/MWh (versus $60–80 historically), a 15-year contract of up to 150 MW will supply Google's data centers via NV Energy's Clean Transition Tariff, and a CD4 blend-and-extend repricing came in roughly +27% on existing capacity.

The tension that holds the rating at HOLD is the divergence between revenue and earnings. Revenue has climbed from roughly $830M to $989.5M over three years, yet net income has been essentially flat at $133M (2023), $131M (2024), and $124M (2025). Growth is being achieved through depreciation-heavy capex, and reported earnings are flattered by approximately $67M per year of tax-benefit monetization, a recurring line that is itself exposed to the federal tax-credit regime. Electricity gross margin compressed from ~34.6% to ~28.5% in FY2025. Free cash flow was roughly -$285M as ~$620M of capex outran ~$335M of operating cash flow. The stock trades at ~59x trailing and ~59x forward earnings, a premium to the ~24x utility peer median that the current earnings base does not support without continued execution on the pricing thesis.

At $123.30, down ~16% from the $146 high, the setup is more balanced than it was at the peak, but it is not asymmetric. The bull case rests on data-center demand re-rating Electricity margins as legacy PPAs roll to >$100/MWh pricing and on Enhanced Geothermal Systems unlocking new resource. The bear case rests on flat core profitability, recurring resource/exploration write-offs (the basis of Bernstein's Underperform initiation), and a valuation that already discounts the optionality. We hold, with a growth-hold rationale, and would upgrade on confirmed margin expansion or Nevada PUC approval of the Google tariff.

## Company Overview

Ormat operates three segments. **Electricity (~70% of revenue)** is the core: roughly 1,340 MW of geothermal and solar generation across the U.S. (Nevada-heavy), Kenya, Guatemala, Indonesia, Honduras, and Guadeloupe, sold under long-term PPAs with weighted-average terms near 14 years and ~94% fixed pricing. The capacity factor is the structural reason hyperscalers now seek geothermal: baseload, around-the-clock power versus 20–30% utilization for intermittent renewables. **Product (~22%)** is engineering and equipment manufacturing for third parties, concentrated in New Zealand and Asia, with project-driven, lumpy revenue and a ~$239M backlog as of May 2026. **Energy Storage (~8% and growing fast)** is a ~495 MW U.S. battery portfolio operating in merchant markets (PJM) and under Resource Adequacy contracts, increasingly hybridized with solar PV.

The moat is genuine and hard to replicate. Ormat is simultaneously a developer/owner and the equipment OEM, which provides cost control and a captive product channel, and it holds proprietary subsurface data accumulated over six decades. The constraint is that the addressable conventional-geothermal resource is geographically limited, which is why the growth thesis increasingly hinges on Enhanced Geothermal Systems (EGS), the "drill anywhere" expansion, and why the Sage Geosystems investment and SLB collaboration matter. Stated 2028 targets are 1.65–1.75 GW of geothermal/solar Electricity capacity and 950–1,050 MW of storage.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 revenue was $989.5M (+12.5% YoY), with Electricity at $693.9M (-1.2%, hurt by curtailments and Puna wellfield issues), Product at $216.7M (+55%), and Storage at $79.0M (+109%). Q1 2026 was a record quarter at $403.9M (+75.8% YoY), but the headline is heavily distorted by a one-time $105.1M Product-segment revenue recognition from the Topp 2 (New Zealand) sale, where a customer exercised a purchase option. Underlying Electricity grew only +0.8% in the quarter; Storage rose +153% on strong PJM merchant pricing. Management reiterated rather than raised 2026 guidance of $1,110–1,160M revenue and $615–645M Adjusted EBITDA, appropriately cautious given the one-time nature of the Q1 strength.

Profitability is the central concern. Net income has been flat despite double-digit revenue growth. Electricity gross margin fell on rising depreciation (+$20M), property tax (+$8.3M), and curtailments; Q1 2026 Electricity gross margin was 30.8% versus 33.5% a year prior. Adjusted EBITDA reached ~$582M (+5.7%) and FY2025 diluted EPS was ~$2.02. Roughly $67M of FY2025 income came from monetizing tax benefits (~$16.6M in Q1 2026 alone). Strip that out and core profitability is materially thinner than headline EPS implies, and that line is directly exposed to any contraction in tax-equity demand.

The cash-flow and balance-sheet picture is a deliberate growth-capex profile, not distress. FY2025 operating cash flow of ~$335M (down from ~$411M) against ~$620M capex produced the negative free cash flow. Total debt is ~$2.66B (~84% fixed-rate), with net debt/EBITDA at ~4.36x against a 6.0x covenant ceiling, leverage that is high for a generalist but normal for an asset-heavy, regulated-style utility. The Altman Z-Score of ~1.14 ("distress zone") is a false alarm for this asset profile; the covenant headroom is the better read. The balance sheet strengthened materially after fiscal year-end: a $1.0B convertible senior notes offering in March 2026 lifted cash to $654.6M (from $147M at YE2025), part of which repurchased the 2027 converts, triggering a $33.7M induced-conversion charge in Q1. 2026 plans call for ~$675M capex plus ~$304M of debt repayments. The dividend ($0.12/quarter, ~0.4% yield) is a token return; this is a reinvestment story.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has improved in quality, and the evidence is broad-based rather than a single-company story. Roughly 270 MW of new U.S. PPAs have been signed since YE2025, including the 67 MW Jersey Valley solar plus 67 MW/268 MWh storage project. The 15-year, up-to-150 MW Google supply contract and the 20-year, ~13 MW Switch deal from Salt Wells are top-credit, long-dated, premium-priced baseload revenue. The +27% CD4 blend-and-extend repricing demonstrates pricing power on the existing fleet, not just new builds.

The earnings beat record supports forward confidence: four straight beats, including +40.3% in Q1 2026, +5.5%, +8.9%, and +25.2% in the prior three quarters. But estimate revisions tell a more nuanced story. Near-term quarterly estimates have been cut sharply: current-quarter consensus fell -36.9% over 90 days and next-quarter -39.3%, reflecting the absence of the Topp 2 one-time benefit and normalization of storage merchant pricing. Full-year revisions are roughly flat (current year +1.5% over 30 days, -1.3% over 90 days; next year -5.2% over 90 days). The estimate picture does not clear the high bar of a +30% upward revision that would override valuation concerns; if anything, the steep near-term cuts argue against extrapolating the Q1 headline.

The longer-term growth thesis depends on two things converting: legacy PPAs rolling to >$100/MWh pricing to lift Electricity margins, and EGS proving out to expand the addressable resource. Both are on-strategy, but neither is yet visible in reported net income.

## Valuation Assessment

At $123.30, Ormat trades against a Utilities peer median P/E of ~23.6x (NFG 10.3x, UGI 12.0x, IDA 23.6x, TXNM 42.1x). The premium is steep. The cycle-adjusted argument deserves a fair hearing, given the Q1 2026 revenue growth, but that growth was one-time-driven (Topp 2 plus storage merchant spikes), and underlying Electricity grew only +0.8%. This is not a cyclical trough that makes trailing P/E meaningless; it is a structurally capital-intensive business whose reported earnings are flattered by tax-benefit monetization and held flat by margin compression and rising depreciation. The estimate revision data does not show the +30%-and-rising signature that would justify treating the multiple as cheap relative to improving estimates; near-term estimates are being cut, and full-year estimates are flat.

The analyst price-target range runs $115 low to $152 high, with a $133.75 mean and $132.50 median, putting the current price below the mean, with ~8% upside to consensus. Given the premium multiple and split sell-side view (Roth MKM Buy; Bernstein Underperform on exploration risk; JPMorgan ~$106 Neutral), we treat those targets as a balanced range rather than a floor; the guardrail on inflection stocks does not apply here because the stock trades well above 30x forward with flat earnings. The pricing thesis is the legitimate reason the market is paying up, but the premium already discounts a successful conversion that has not yet appeared in net income. We score valuation 4.5: the multiple is stretched against current fundamentals, and the historical pattern is that "cheap-looking on the thesis but expensive on the numbers" sorts the wrong way without confirmed earnings follow-through.

## Competitive Landscape

Ormat's competitive position is improving, which is the strongest part of the case. The company is the dominant pure-play geothermal operator globally, and its vertical integration (developer/owner plus equipment OEM) is structurally difficult to replicate. The defining industry dynamic favors it: surging data-center/AI electricity demand colliding with the need for 24/7 carbon-free power, which intermittent renewables cannot supply at baseload scale. This has shifted Ormat from a slow-growth utility into a company with genuine demand-side optionality, reflected in PPA prices breaking above $100/MWh and in the Google and Switch contracts.

The trajectory is positive: blend-and-extend repricing improving legacy economics, premium pricing on new capacity, and the EGS push (Sage Geosystems, SLB collaboration, a larger high-capacity OEC under development) positioning the company for resource expansion. The competitive risk is geographic limitation of conventional resource and a crowded, China-supply-chain-exposed BESS market in storage. On balance, share and structural positioning are widening, supporting a 7.5, consistent with the upward trajectory in this dimension across prior reports.

## Risk Assessment

The risks specific to this company are material and partly idiosyncratic. **Geothermal resource depletion** is the existential operational risk and it is active, not theoretical: a $7.2M Brawley impairment (2025), an $8.1M impairment in Q1 2026, ongoing ~5°F/yr temperature decline at McGinness Hills, and prior issues at Puna, Don Campbell, and Sarulla. These write-offs are individually small but persistent, the empirical basis for Bernstein's Underperform call. **Federal tax-credit/policy risk** matters because tax-benefit monetization is a recurring earnings line; geothermal fared relatively better than solar/wind under technology-neutral OBBBA credits, but FEOC rules and China battery tariffs threaten the Storage segment's supply chain and economics. **Customer concentration and sovereign collection risk** is real: SCPPA ~17.8%, NV Energy ~13.8%, KPLC/Kenya ~11.9%, with overdue receivables of ~$29.5M (KPLC) and ~$20.3M (ENEE/Honduras). **Curtailment and weather** eroded ~$4.8M of Q1 2026 revenue on Nevada heat (binary plants lose efficiency at high ambient temperatures). **Israel concentration** (core manufacturing, ~531 employees, a facility ~26 miles from Gaza) is a geopolitical tail risk. Continued **negative FCF plus ~$304M of 2026 maturities** keeps the company dependent on capital-markets access.

A near-term swing factor underappreciated by the headlines: the Google deal is contingent on Nevada PUC approval, expected H2 2026. Approval would validate the premium-pricing thesis; delay or rejection would remove a visible catalyst.

### Options Market Signal

Options pricing is balanced rather than directional. Near-term ATM IV of 38.1% sits modestly rich versus 30.9% one-year historical vol (a 7.2pp spread), but IV percentile within the current chain is only 10%, suggesting the market is not pricing an acute near-term event. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-1.5% at the front, widening to -5.0% at six months, typical hedging demand into the back half of 2026, which aligns with the PUC approval timeline). The implied move for the nearest expiry is ±7.4%. The put/call open-interest ratio jumped from 0.26 to 1.93 alongside the ~10% price decline since late May, indicating accumulated downside hedging into the pullback. Short interest at 8.9% of float (4.6 days to cover) is moderate: bearish but not crowded. The aggregate signal is consistent with a balanced HOLD: the market is hedging the back half of 2026 (PUC, estimate normalization) without pricing a binary collapse.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$152, +23%):** Data-center demand structurally tightens the market in Ormat's favor; new and repriced PPAs above $100/MWh lift Electricity-segment margins as legacy contracts roll off, reversing the gross-margin compression. Nevada PUC approves the Google tariff in H2 2026, validating the premium-pricing thesis and adding long-dated, top-credit baseload revenue. EGS progress (Sage/SLB, larger OEC) reframes the resource ceiling, justifying a sustained growth premium. Storage continues to compound off a small base. Net income finally inflects upward as pricing power flows through.

**Bear case (~$106, -14%):** Net income stays flat as margin compression, rising depreciation, and curtailments offset top-line growth; the headline Q1 strength proves one-time and full-year results normalize toward reiterated guidance. Recurring resource impairments continue, validating the exploration-risk thesis. Tax-credit/FEOC policy tightening reduces tax-equity monetization, directly cutting the ~$67M earnings contribution. The PUC delays or rejects the Google tariff. Negative FCF and refinancing needs pressure the balance sheet in a higher-for-longer rate environment. The premium multiple compresses toward the peer median as the inflection fails to appear in earnings.

The balance of evidence places the most-likely outcome between these. The pricing inflection is broad-based, which supports the competitive and growth scores; but it has not yet converted to net-income growth, which caps valuation and management-quality scores and keeps the rating at HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The competitive position and pricing-power evidence are improving, but flat core earnings and a premium multiple keep the risk/reward balanced at $123.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Nevada PUC approval of the Google Clean Transition Tariff (expected H2 2026), confirming the premium-pricing thesis.
2. Two consecutive quarters of Electricity gross margin expansion (back toward 33%+) demonstrating that >$100/MWh PPAs are flowing through to reported profitability, with full-year net income breaking above the ~$124–133M three-year plateau.
3. A pullback toward the low-$100s (peer-relative valuation compression) without fundamental deterioration, restoring asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A material resource impairment (>$25M) or evidence of accelerating wellfield decline at a top-revenue facility (e.g., McGinness Hills, Olkaria III).
2. OBBBA/FEOC policy change that visibly contracts tax-equity demand and cuts the ~$67M tax-benefit earnings contribution.
3. PUC rejection of the Google tariff combined with a downward revision to full-year guidance.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, and specifically the H2 2026 Nevada PUC decision, which is the cleanest near-term catalyst.

## Conclusion

Ormat is a differentiated asset: the only vertically integrated, pure-play geothermal operator, sitting on a structural tailwind as data-center demand reprices baseload clean power above $100/MWh. The competitive position is widening and the growth optionality is real. The reason to hold rather than buy is that none of this has yet converted to net-income growth: three years of flat earnings, ~$67M of annual earnings dependent on tax-credit monetization, recurring resource impairments, negative free cash flow, and a ~59x forward multiple that already prices the favorable conversion. The trajectory of prior assessments (HOLD held three times, scores drifting up modestly) is consistent with our read: the story is improving but the proof points (margin expansion, PUC approval, an earnings inflection) have not yet landed. We rate ORA a HOLD with a $133 base case (~8% upside, near consensus), a $152 bull case, and a $106 bear case. The path to a BUY is well-defined and monitorable; until margins expand or the Google tariff clears, the asymmetry is not there.
