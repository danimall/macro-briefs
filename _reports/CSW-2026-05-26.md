## Executive Summary

CSW Industrials is a Dallas-based diversified industrial company that has fundamentally transformed its financial profile over the past fiscal year. The company deployed approximately $1 billion in acquisitions (primarily MARS Parts at ~$650M), taking leverage from net cash to 2.55x net debt/EBITDA while more than doubling its goodwill and nearly tripling intangible assets. Revenue grew 23.3% to $1.08B in FY2026, but organic revenue declined 2.1% for the full year, with all growth attributable to acquisitions.

The Q4 FY2026 earnings report (May 26, 2026) provided a meaningful positive signal: adjusted EPS of $3.14 beat consensus of $2.43 by 29%, and the core Contractor Solutions segment returned to positive organic growth of 2.6% after declining organically for most of the year. This suggests the underlying business is recovering rather than relying solely on M&A for growth.

At $278 per share (~27x adjusted EPS of $10.38, ~17x adjusted EBITDA), the stock prices in continued execution of both integration synergies and organic growth recovery. Estimate revisions are modestly negative (current year EPS down 4.3% over 90 days), and the gap between GAAP EPS ($6.70) and adjusted EPS ($10.38) at 55% is unusually wide. The investment case requires conviction that management can simultaneously integrate transformational acquisitions, restore organic growth, and delever — a complex multi-variable execution challenge in a rising-rate environment.

## Company Overview

CSW Industrials operates through three segments:

**Contractor Solutions (~75% of revenue, ~84% of Adj. EBITDA):** Replacement parts, installation accessories, and maintenance products for HVAC/R, plumbing, and electrical contractors. Key brands include RectorSeal, Aspen Manufacturing, and MARS Parts. Contractor brand loyalty creates meaningful switching costs and pricing power.

**Specialized Reliability Solutions (~15% of revenue):** Products for mining, energy, rail, and general industrial maintenance applications. This segment showed the strongest organic momentum in Q4 (8.8% organic growth, margins expanding from 15.3% to 21.8%).

**Engineered Building Solutions (~11% of revenue):** Architecturally-specified building products. Includes the impaired Greco business (Canada) currently being exited.

The company reached $1 billion in annual revenue in its 10th year since formation, primarily through a disciplined acquisition strategy where acquired companies immediately benefit from CSW's established contractor distribution network.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

FY2026 revenue of $1,082.5M represents 23.3% growth, but the composition matters: $223M from acquisitions offset by $18M in organic decline (-2.1%). Adjusted EBITDA grew 18.3% to $269.6M, but gross margins compressed from 44.8% to 41.9% due to lower-margin acquisitions and tariff impacts.

The GAAP vs. adjusted earnings gap is substantial and widening:
- GAAP EPS: $6.70 (down 20% YoY)
- Adjusted EPS: $10.38 (up 6.9% YoY)
- Key adjustments: $3.06/share intangible amortization, $0.81 Greco impairment, plus integration/transaction costs

Interest expense increased from -$0.3M to -$22.2M, reflecting the full-year impact of $871.5M in new debt.

### Balance Sheet Transformation

- Net debt went from negative (net cash of $225.8M) to $842.7M
- Goodwill: $264.1M → $632.6M (+140%)
- Intangible assets: $357.9M → $900.1M (+151%)
- Combined goodwill + intangibles = $1.53B = 66% of total assets
- Net leverage: 2.55x (management target range: 1-3x)

With $871.5M in floating-rate debt (SOFR + 200bps), the company faces approximately $5.7M in incremental annual interest for each 100bp rate increase on unhedged portions. Only $300M is hedged at 3.416% SOFR.

### Cash Flow

Free cash flow declined from $152.1M to $132.4M despite higher EBITDA. FCF/EBITDA conversion fell from 67% to 49%. Q4 was actually negative FCF (-$6.8M), which is unusual for a profitable industrial. Recovery in cash conversion is critical for the deleveraging thesis.

### Q4 FY2026 Results

The quarter delivered:
- Revenue: $309M (+34% YoY)
- Adjusted EBITDA: $82.9M (+38.8%)
- Adjusted EPS: $3.14 (+21.1%, beating $2.43 consensus by 29%)
- Contractor Solutions organic growth: +2.6% (vs. -3.6% full-year decline)
- SRS organic growth: +8.8%

Q4 shows the acquired businesses contributing above-average EBITDA growth and organic momentum turning positive — the most important data point for forward-looking investors.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two components that must be evaluated separately:

**Inorganic growth (proven capability):** Management has a decade-long track record of identifying, acquiring, and integrating niche industrial businesses. The acquisition playbook — buying companies with good products but limited distribution and immediately plugging them into CSW's contractor network — has demonstrably worked. MARS Parts and Aspen Manufacturing fill genuine product gaps in the HVAC/R offering.

**Organic growth (currently proving up):** Full-year organic decline of 2.1% reflects genuine end-market weakness: soft residential housing, consumer preference for HVAC repair over replacement, and customer destocking. The Q4 inflection to +2.6% organic growth is encouraging but needs confirmation through FY2027.

Secular tailwinds supporting medium-term growth: aging US housing stock requiring HVAC replacement, energy efficiency mandates driving heat pump/mini-split adoption, and electrification trends. The Duckt-Strip acquisition specifically targets the mini-split market.

Estimate revisions are modestly negative: current year EPS consensus down 4.3% over 90 days, though next-year estimates ticked up 2.2% over 30 days. This mixed picture suggests analysts are uncertain about the pace of synergy realization and organic recovery.

## Valuation Assessment

At $278, CSW trades at:
- ~27x adjusted EPS ($10.38)
- ~42x GAAP EPS ($6.70)
- ~17x adjusted EBITDA ($269.6M)
- ~4.3x revenue
- Forward P/E of 36.8x (per Yahoo Finance data)

Against peer medians of 29.3x P/E (using Carlisle at 19.3x, 3M at 29.3x, Honeywell at 36.0x), CSW's adjusted P/E of 27x appears reasonable, but the GAAP P/E of 42x highlights the heavy acquisition-related adjustment burden.

The analyst consensus target is $322.71 (median $307), with a range of $280-$378. At $278, the stock sits below the low end of the target range, suggesting analysts see limited downside from current levels.

The valuation question centers on which earnings metric is "real." If adjusted EPS of ~$10.38 is the appropriate measure and grows to $11.71 this year (consensus), the stock trades at 23.8x current-year estimates — not unreasonable for a high-quality industrial compounder. But if integration proves slow, organic growth stalls, or the $51M in annual intangible amortization reflects genuine economic depreciation of acquired assets, the premium valuation becomes harder to justify.

Management's FY2027 guidance for "meaningful growth" in revenue and adjusted EBITDA, combined with the Q4 beat, provides near-term support but not enough specificity to anchor a firm fair value estimate.

## Competitive Landscape

CSW operates in highly fragmented niche markets where competitive dynamics favor incumbents with distribution relationships and brand recognition:

**Strengths:**
- Contractor brand loyalty creates real switching costs — HVAC/R technicians specify products by name
- Broad distribution network provides immediate scale benefit to acquired companies
- Niche focus on value-added (not commodity) products supports pricing power
- Comprehensive HVAC component offering post-MARS/Aspen acquisitions creates one-stop-shop advantage

**Vulnerabilities:**
- Tariff exposure on imported motors and electrical components (likely Asian sourcing)
- Housing market cyclicality affects end-demand directly
- Larger competitors (Watsco, Ferguson) could pursue similar strategies

The SRS segment's margin expansion and organic growth demonstrate that the platform model works beyond HVAC — mining, energy, and rail applications provide useful diversification.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Integration execution at unprecedented scale:** $1B deployed in one year is roughly 2x the company's prior cumulative M&A. MARS Parts alone is larger than any prior deal. If synergy realization takes 24+ months rather than 12, margin expectations will need to be revised downward.

2. **Leverage in a rising-rate environment:** At 2.55x net leverage with ~$570M in unhedged floating-rate debt, each 100bp rate increase adds ~$5.7M in annual interest expense. If rates rise further while cash conversion remains depressed, deleveraging stalls.

3. **Organic growth sustainability:** One quarter of positive organic growth after a year of decline does not constitute a trend. Housing market weakness could persist if mortgage rates remain elevated.

4. **Goodwill/intangible impairment risk:** $1.53B in intangible assets (66% of total assets) requires continued strong performance from acquired businesses. The Greco impairment ($15.6M) demonstrates this risk is real.

5. **Tariff escalation:** Explicitly called out by management as impacting margins. Further tariff increases on Chinese-sourced components could compress margins beyond current levels.

## Investment Thesis

- MARS Parts integration proceeds on schedule, driving margin expansion in Contractor Solutions through FY2027
- Organic growth sustains at 3-5% as housing activity recovers and mini-split/electrification trends accelerate
- FCF conversion recovers to 60%+, enabling deleveraging to <2x by FY2028
- Multiple re-rates as the market rewards successful execution at scale
- Math: $13.00 next-year EPS × 26x = $338; at 29x = $377

- Integration proves slower than expected, with margins on acquired businesses remaining dilutive
- Interest rate increases add $5-10M in annual expense while FCF stays depressed
- Further goodwill/intangible impairments signal overpayment for acquisitions
- Multiple compresses to 18-20x adjusted EPS ($10.38): $187-$208
- Math: $11.71 current-year EPS × 20x = $234

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. Two consecutive quarters of organic growth >3% in Contractor Solutions, confirming the Q4 inflection is a trend
2. FCF conversion recovering above 55% for two consecutive quarters
3. Stock declines to $240-250 range (20-22x forward EPS) providing valuation margin of safety

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Organic growth returns to negative in Q1 FY2027, invalidating the inflection thesis
2. Net leverage rises above 3.0x (covenant concerns) or management signals intention to pause deleveraging
3. Another material impairment charge suggesting acquired asset values are deteriorating

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely late July/early August 2026 for Q1 FY2027). This report will be the first full quarter of MARS Parts in the base, and organic growth trends will be highly informative.

## Conclusion

CSW Industrials is a quality industrial business executing an ambitious but unproven transformation to materially larger scale. The Q4 beat and organic growth inflection are genuinely encouraging, but the balance sheet has been fundamentally altered, and the valuation at ~27x adjusted EPS assumes continued successful execution across multiple dimensions simultaneously. In the current environment — elevated rates, uncertain housing, tariff headwinds — the margin for error on a levered industrial is narrower than it was a year ago.

The consensus analyst target of $307-$323 implies 10-16% upside, which is reasonable for a HOLD but insufficient to warrant a BUY given execution uncertainty, negative estimate revision trends, and the company's first-ever period at significant leverage. The stock warrants monitoring for either a valuation pullback or additional proof points confirming organic growth recovery and integration success before upgrading.
