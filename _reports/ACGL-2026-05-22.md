## Executive Summary

Arch Capital Group remains one of the highest-quality specialty insurance/reinsurance franchises globally, generating 17.1% operating ROE in 2025 on a capital base exceeding $26B. The stock trades at approximately 10x forward operating earnings and approximately 1.47x book value — a modest discount to what a 17%+ ROE compounder should command, and at a discount to peers like W.R. Berkley despite superior returns on equity. The company's three-segment structure (P&C insurance, reinsurance, mortgage) provides earnings diversification that most specialty competitors lack.

The bear case centers on competitive market softening (management signaled a ~$250M net premium headwind for 2026 with property cat rates down 10-20%), the new Bermuda 15% corporate income tax that raised the effective tax rate from ~8% to approximately 14-15%, and modestly declining consensus estimates (-3.9% for current quarter over 90 days, -1.2% for FY2026). These are real headwinds, but they reduce the growth rate of an exceptional earnings machine rather than impairing the franchise. At ~10x forward operating earnings, the stock prices in meaningful cycle softening. Book value per share grew 22.6% in 2025 and is compounding at ~20%+ annually — even at a reduced growth rate, $65 book value growing at 15% reaches $75 in 12 months, providing valuation support.

The specific catalyst for re-rating is the gap between current price ($96.15) and analyst median target (~$109), combined with substantial buyback capacity. With approximately $3.0B available under repurchase authorization as of May 1, 2026, the company has been buying back approximately 5% of its float annually at prices that generate excess returns based on 2025 activity.

## Company Overview

Arch Capital Group Ltd., incorporated in Bermuda, operates a diversified specialty insurance platform with $79.2B in total assets and $24.2B in shareholders' equity. The business operates through three segments:

**Insurance (47% of net premiums written):** Specialty P&C underwriting in North America, UK, Europe, and Australia. Lines include property & short-tail specialty, general liability, commercial auto, workers' compensation, and professional liability. The August 2024 MCE Acquisition (Allianz's U.S. MidCorp and Entertainment businesses) contributed materially to 2025 insurance premium growth.

**Reinsurance (46% of net premiums written):** Global property, casualty, specialty, and marine/aviation reinsurance through Bermuda, U.S., UK (Lloyd's), Ireland, and Denmark.

**Mortgage (6% of net premiums written):** U.S. primary mortgage insurance ($286B insurance in force), GSE credit risk transfer, and international mortgage insurance/reinsurance.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is moderating from exceptional levels, consistent with P&C cycle transition:

- Management explicitly signaled ~$250M net premium headwind for 2026
- Property catastrophe rates down 10-20% at January 1, 2026 renewals
- Reinsurance net premiums already declined 1.7% in 2025
- Insurance growth of 13.4% in 2025 was MCE acquisition-driven; organic growth decelerating

Consensus estimates reflect this moderation:
- FY2026E: $9.27 EPS (down 1.2% over 90 days)
- FY2027E: $9.90 EPS (down 2.2% over 90 days)

These are modest negative revisions, not the kind of collapse that signals structural impairment. The company is choosing discipline over volume: pulling back from commoditized property lines while growing selectively in specialty casualty, E&S, construction, and alternative markets.

The mortgage segment provides meaningful diversification from P&C cycle dynamics, though it remains subject to macroeconomic conditions including housing prices, unemployment, and credit quality. At 81.8% persistency and 2.17% delinquency rates, this segment generates $1B+ annually and is subject to PMIERs/GSE capital requirements.

Key growth vectors:
1. MCE acquisition synergy realization as integration costs decline ($75M in 2025, likely lower in 2026)
2. Specialty casualty expansion in E&S and alternative markets where pricing remains adequate
3. Investment income growth from reinvesting at higher yields (3.34-year duration portfolio turns over quickly)
4. Share count reduction (estimated ~5%/year based on 2025 buyback activity) directly compounds EPS

## Competitive Landscape

Arch's competitive position is strengthening through disciplined cycle management while competitors pursue volume. The company's willingness to signal a $250M premium reduction while peers maintain growth targets preserves underwriting margins that compound over time.

Three structural advantages:
1. **Segment diversification**: No other Bermuda specialty reinsurer has a $1B/year mortgage income stream providing meaningful earnings diversification from P&C underwriting cycles.
2. **MCE acquisition platform**: The Allianz MidCorp business provides relationship-driven middle-market access that takes competitors years to replicate.
3. **Capital flexibility**: 10.1% debt-to-capital and $6.4B available dividend capacity from Bermuda subsidiaries enable opportunistic deployment when pricing hardens.

The Bermuda 15% CIT is a structural headwind but applies equally to all Bermuda-based competitors (RNR, RE, AXS). It does not change relative positioning.

The reinsurance segment's 240bp combined ratio improvement in 2025 (to 80.8%) demonstrates that even in a transitioning market, disciplined underwriting produces exceptional results when cat losses are normal.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Casualty Reserve Uncertainty (Medium-High):**
Net reserves grew $3B to $24.5B in 2025. The 90th percentile Monte Carlo outcome implies potential $4.5B adverse development. Social inflation, nuclear verdicts, and PFAS/forever chemicals litigation represent long-tail risks. However, the company reported $600M favorable development in 2025 across all segments — reserve conservatism appears intact.

**2. Catastrophe Exposure (Medium):**
Florida Tri-County windstorm exposure: $1.9B net probable maximum loss at 1-in-250-year return period (~8.3% of tangible equity). Current geopolitical environment elevates man-made catastrophe risk (war-risk marine exposure, aviation).

**3. Cycle Softening (Medium):**
If competition intensifies beyond current levels, premium reductions could exceed guided levels. However, specialty casualty pricing remains adequate, and management has demonstrated the discipline to pull back further if needed.

**4. Bermuda CIT (Low-Medium):**
Already reflected in 2025 results (effective rate 14.7%). The rate may fluctuate based on jurisdictional income mix, catastrophe activity, and geographic tax-rate differences. Q1 2026 GAAP effective tax rate was 8.6%, while effective tax rate on pre-tax operating income was 14.8%. Substance-based tax credits partially offset the impact.

**5. Mortgage Risk (Low near-term, Medium in downturn):**
2.17% delinquency, 749 average FICO, 179% PMIER sufficiency. A severe housing downturn scenario implies $931M potential loss — manageable against the segment's $1B annual income generation. However, this segment is exposed to housing cycles, unemployment, and credit quality shifts.

## Conclusion

Arch Capital represents a high-quality compounder trading at a reasonable valuation with upside potential. At approximately 10x forward operating earnings on a 17%+ ROE business with substantial buyback capacity, the stock offers asymmetric upside. The modest negative estimate revisions reflect cycle normalization that is already well understood by management and partially priced. The stock offers 14-16% base case upside with downside well-supported by tangible book value growing at double-digits annually.

The declining score trajectory (from 8.0 to current 7.55) reflects legitimate growth moderation as the P&C cycle softens. However, the fundamental thesis — that a 17% ROE compounder at ~10x forward operating earnings with buyback support offers material upside — remains intact as the price has remained flat while book value continues to grow.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The corrected forward P/E (~10x, not 7.4x as originally stated) reduces the magnitude of the valuation discount argument; the stock is modestly rather than deeply undervalued relative to peers, and the base case relies primarily on the upper end of the P/E range and the P/B framework.
