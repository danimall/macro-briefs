**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hershey is a domestic confectionery champion working through the back end of a commodity-driven earnings trough. FY2025 net income fell roughly 60% to ~$883M from ~$2,221M, and gross margin compressed more than 1,300 basis points from ~47% to ~33-34%, including a ~$491M cocoa hedging loss. The franchise held: volumes softened modestly and pricing held, but the cocoa supercycle (West African supply constraints driving cocoa from ~$3,000/ton to peaks near $12,500/ton) hit a business with a heavy chocolate mix and ~89% North America revenue concentration harder than diversified global peers.

The recovery is already visible in the hard data. Q1 2026 gross margin rebounded to ~39.4% from ~33.7% a year earlier, and net income nearly doubled to ~$435M as cocoa futures retreated toward the ~$1.86-$2/lb area and pricing flowed through. Hershey has beaten consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters by 13.8% to 20.8%. The investment debate is the timing and terminal margin of normalization, not solvency or franchise erosion.

This is a hold rather than a buy because the recovery is largely priced and the estimate-revision picture is not a clean signal. Current-year and next-year EPS consensus ($8.44 and $9.94) have barely moved (+0.7%, +1.5% over 90 days), while near-term quarterly estimates have been cut (current quarter -11.8% over 90 days). The stock at ~20x current-year and ~17x next-year earnings is reasonable but not cheap given that analysts expect cocoa to stabilize at a structurally elevated $7,500-$8,500/ton — roughly double pre-2023 levels — which may cap the gross-margin recovery below the historical ~47%. A 3.2% dividend from a long-standing grower plus the earnings recovery supports total return on a 12-month view, justifying a hold; the absence of an undervaluation argument and the GLP-1/pricing-elasticity overhangs keep us from upgrading.

## Company Overview

Hershey is North America's leading chocolate and confectionery manufacturer, generating ~$11.7B in FY2025 revenue with roughly 20,000 employees. The business runs three segments:

- **North America Confectionery** (>81% of revenue): Hershey's, Reese's, Kit Kat (US license), Kisses, Jolly Rancher, Twizzlers. The dominant profit and revenue engine.
- **North America Salty Snacks**: SkinnyPop, Dot's Pretzels, Pirate's Booty — a deliberate diversification leg into a faster-growing, less cocoa-exposed category, now ~80%+ manufactured in-house versus 0% five years ago.
- **International**: the smallest segment.

The North America revenue concentration is a sharp contrast with global staples peers (Mondelez, Nestlé). The competitive moat rests on iconic scaled brands with leading US chocolate shelf share, a "tentpole" seasonal marketing playbook (Halloween, Easter, holidays), deep retailer relationships, and a cocoa procurement operation (Swiss trading subsidiary) management claims has outperformed peers on sourcing cost over a decade.

A defining governance feature: the Milton Hershey School Trust holds controlling Class B supervoting shares and effectively controls the company. This limits minority governance influence and has historically blocked takeovers (the 2016 Mondelez approach). The Trust is also the dominant seller in the Form 4 data — routine, programmatic endowment diversification, not a management signal.

## Financial Analysis

The defining feature of recent financials is a severe cocoa-driven margin shock followed by an early-2026 recovery.

**FY2025 trough:**
- Revenue roughly flat-to-modest at ~$11.7B, with volume softness (a "disappointing Easter season") partly offset by aggressive pricing.
- Gross-margin contraction driven almost entirely by cocoa input inflation rather than demand destruction.

**Q1 2026 recovery:**
- Cocoa futures had fallen sharply from late-2024/2025 peaks, with realized cost moving well below the 2025 average.
- Reported Q1 EPS of $2.35 beat the $2.07 estimate by 13.8%, extending a four-quarter beat streak (13.8%, 20.6%, 20.4%, 20.8%).

**Balance sheet and cash flow:** Hershey historically generates strong free cash flow on an investment-grade balance sheet. Leverage rose in 2025 to absorb the hedging hit and working-capital build from expensive cocoa inventory, plus the multi-year $1B supply-chain capex program (a new 250,000 sq ft Hershey, PA chocolate plant, 13 new production lines, 11 upgraded lines). The dividend remained intact and continued to grow, though the 2025 earnings drop pressured the payout ratio and likely slowed the repurchase pace.

The financial-health read sits in the middle: the franchise is solvent and cash-generative, but FY2025 leverage rose into a higher-for-longer rate environment, the capex program is front-loaded, and the trailing earnings base is depressed. The downside is contained but the capital structure is not at peak efficiency through the trough.

## Growth Analysis

This is a commodity-cycle earnings recovery, not a secular growth story. Revenue is roughly flat; the earnings line is the variable. The bull mechanism is straightforward: cocoa cost per pound falls → COGS falls → gross margin recovers toward historical levels → EPS rebuilds toward $9-10 power. Q1 2026 confirms the first leg of that chain is working.

The estimate-revision data tempers the enthusiasm. The strongest forward signal — EPS revisions exceeding +30% over 90 days — is absent here. Instead:
- Current quarter: $1.43, revised -11.8% over 90 days and -0.7% over 30 days.
- Next quarter: $2.29, -2.8% over 90 days.
- Current year: $8.44, +0.7% over 90 days.
- Next year: $9.94, +1.5% over 90 days.

The Street has already modeled the cocoa-normalization recovery into full-year and out-year numbers; the near-term cuts reflect the disappointing Easter and elasticity worries. The recovery is real but consensus is not systematically underestimating it. The four-quarter beat streak argues for confidence that the company will hit or modestly exceed the $8.44/$9.94 path, supporting a base case of earnings rebuilding over the next 12-18 months without a re-rating surprise.

Two structural questions cap durable growth. First, cocoa may stabilize at $7,500-$8,500/ton, roughly double pre-2023 norms, which could leave gross margin on a structurally lower plateau than the historical ~47% unless offset by pricing, salty-snacks mix, and the $1B efficiency program (targeting +$50M productivity and -$100M inventory over two years). Second, GLP-1 weight-loss drugs and the consumer shift toward reduced-sugar, higher-protein products are a long-term demand headwind on the core sugar-confectionery franchise that management itself has flagged.

## Valuation Assessment

At $171.63, HSY trades at 32.6x trailing EPS of $4.34 — meaningless at a cyclical trough. The relevant metrics are forward: ~20.3x the $8.44 current-year consensus and ~17.3x the $9.94 next-year consensus. PEG is not informative for a flat-revenue commodity recovery, but the forward multiples on recovering earnings are reasonable for a wide-moat staples leader.

Against peers in Packaged Foods & Meats, the comparison is genuinely heterogeneous:

The trailing P/E sits above the peer median, but several peers (GIS, MKC) carry low multiples because they face their own structural pressures, and KHC's is negative on impairments. On forward earnings, HSY's ~17-20x range is defensible for the highest-quality brand portfolio in the group. The 3.2% dividend yield is below the 4.8% peer median, reflecting both the elevated payout ratio at trough earnings and the market's recovery expectations.

The valuation conclusion: this is fairly valued, not mispriced. The Evercore upgrade to Outperform (May 28, 2026) cited "relatively compelling" valuation after a ~20% pullback, and the analyst consensus mean target of ~$217 (median $213.5, range $166-$255) implies meaningful upside if the recovery completes. But the absence of an estimate-revision signal and the structurally elevated cocoa plateau mean we do not see a specific reason the market is materially wrong. A moderate valuation score reflects support from forward earnings and the dividend, without a strong asymmetric-upside argument.

## Competitive Landscape

Hershey holds leading US chocolate share in a mature, oligopolistic market (Mars, Mondelez, Lindt, Ferrero). The moat — brand equity, scale, seasonal merchandising, retailer relationships, and procurement capability — is durable but the category is slow-growing. The 2024-2026 cocoa supercycle compressed margins industry-wide; Hershey's heavy chocolate mix and US concentration left it more cocoa-exposed than diversified peers like Mondelez.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. On the positive side, the salty-snacks expansion (SkinnyPop, Dot's, Pirate's Booty) is a coherent diversification into a faster-growing, less commodity-volatile category, and the in-house manufacturing build-out (0% to ~80%+ in five years) strengthens that leg. On the negative side, the disappointing Easter and analyst debate over how much further pricing can be pushed before volumes erode suggest the pricing lever is approaching its limit, and the GLP-1/health shift threatens the core franchise over a multi-year horizon. The competitive position is stable-to-modestly-eroding at the core, offset by deliberate diversification — a step down from the prior assessment when share looked unambiguously dominant, but not deteriorating sharply.

## Risk Assessment

**Cocoa price volatility — the dominant risk.** Cocoa is the single most important input. Even with prices retreating, analysts expect stabilization at an elevated $7,500-$8,500/ton. Hedging smooths but does not eliminate this, and mistimed hedges cut both ways.

**Pricing/elasticity risk.** Hershey leaned heavily on price to offset cocoa. The disappointing Easter and consumer pushback raise the question of remaining pricing headroom in a discretionary-within-staples category.

**GLP-1 / health-trend secular risk.** Management flagged GLP-1 drugs dampening appetite and the shift toward reduced-sugar/high-protein products. This is the long-term overhang on core sugar confectionery, partially addressed via reformulation and the salty-snacks pivot.

**Tariff/commodity cost stacking.** Q1 2026 commentary cited tariff-related cost pressure on top of commodities.

**Geographic concentration.** The North America revenue concentration means limited hedge against US consumer weakness.

**Management transition execution risk.** Outside CEO Kirk Tanner (ex-Wendy's, ex-PepsiCo) is reorganizing the company into a demand-creation/commercial-excellence/demand-fulfillment model, reflected in a string of Item 5.02 executive changes. The strategy is coherent but execution risk is elevated during the transition.

**Controlled-company governance.** The Milton Hershey School Trust's supervoting control limits takeover optionality and minority influence.

Most of these risks are at least partly priced — cocoa cyclicality and the dividend-grower profile are well understood. The GLP-1 secular threat and pricing-elasticity ceiling are the risks the market may be under-weighting, and they argue for a below-neutral risk score.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 46.8% versus 1-year historical volatility of 27.6% — rich by 19.2 points, in the 72nd percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 46.8% vs long-term 30.1%), signaling imminent event risk. The put/call OI ratio rose from 1.44 to 2.12 over the past six weeks as the stock fell 7%, and put/call skew of 4.7% shows elevated put demand. Heavy put positioning into a price decline is a caution flag against the recovery thesis and reinforces the hold rating. The elevated near-term IV reflects an upcoming binary (next earnings) rather than cheap entry timing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$255):** Cocoa normalizes faster and lower than the $7,500-$8,500/ton expectation, gross margin recovers toward the high-30s/low-40s, the $1B efficiency program delivers, salty snacks compound, and the four-quarter beat streak continues. EPS rebuilds toward and past the $9.94 next-year consensus, and the multiple holds at ~22-25x recovering earnings. This matches the high analyst target.

**Bear case (~$150, roughly -13% from current):** Cocoa stabilizes at the elevated end, gross margin plateaus structurally below history, pricing power exhausts as volumes erode (the disappointing Easter extends into a broader elasticity problem), GLP-1 headwinds accelerate, and the multiple compresses on a lower terminal-margin franchise. EPS recovery stalls below $9 and the dividend yield re-rates toward the peer median ~4.8%.

The base case sits between: a steady cocoa-driven earnings recovery toward ~$8.44 this year and ~$9.94 next year, the dividend continuing to grow, and the multiple holding near current forward levels, delivering a total return roughly in line with the earnings recovery plus the 3.2% yield over 12 months.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a **growth_hold** rationale: the 12-month thesis is a cocoa-cycle earnings recovery flowing through quarterly margins, supported by a 3.2% dividend, with a dated catalyst path (each 2026 quarterly print showing margin normalization).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q2 2026, expected late July/early August) is the key checkpoint — it tests whether the Q1 margin rebound sustained and whether the disappointing Easter was an isolated seasonal miss or a broadening elasticity problem.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of gross margin sustained above ~40% with volumes flat-to-positive, confirming the recovery without further pricing destruction.
- Current-year/next-year EPS consensus revised upward >5% on confirmed cocoa cost relief, indicating the Street is catching up rather than the recovery being fully priced.
- A pullback toward ~$150 (roughly 15x next-year EPS) that creates genuine asymmetric upside against the recovery.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Q2/Q3 2026 gross margin reversing back toward the low-30s on renewed cocoa inflation or hedging losses.
- Confirmation that pricing has exhausted — sequential volume declines paired with falling pricing realization.
- A dividend-coverage problem or buyback suspension signaling balance-sheet stress from the capex program plus a stalled earnings recovery.

## Conclusion

Hershey is a high-quality, wide-moat staples franchise emerging from a cocoa-driven earnings trough. The recovery is already showing in Q1 2026, and the four-quarter beat streak supports confidence in the ~$8.44/$9.94 forward path. At ~20x current-year and ~17x next-year earnings plus a 3.2% growing dividend, the stock offers a defensible 12-month total-return case tied to the cocoa-normalization catalyst.

What holds us at HOLD rather than BUY: the recovery is largely priced (full-year estimates have barely moved, and near-term estimates are being cut), the valuation is fair rather than cheap relative to peers and forward earnings, cocoa is likely to stabilize at a structurally elevated level that caps the margin recovery below historical norms, and the GLP-1/health shift plus pricing-elasticity ceiling are genuine long-term overhangs. The options market's heavy near-term put positioning and IV backwardation reinforce caution into the next print. We rate HSY a HOLD with a growth-recovery rationale, and would look to upgrade on a sustained two-quarter margin confirmation or a pullback toward $150.
