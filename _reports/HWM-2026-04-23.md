## Executive Summary

Howmet Aerospace has delivered one of the strongest operational transformations in the industrials sector since its 2020 separation from Arconic. Revenue has grown 46% since 2022, net income has tripled, and segment EBITDA margins have expanded by 650+ basis points to 30.4%. The company occupies structurally advantaged positions across jet engine castings, aerospace fasteners, and titanium structures, with qualification-based switching costs that provide durable pricing power. Free cash flow reached $1.43B in FY2025 (17.3% margin), up from $682M just two years prior, and management has returned $1.45B to shareholders through buybacks over three years while reducing gross debt by $1.3B.

The central tension is valuation. At ~$240.88, the stock trades at approximately 52x FY2026E consensus EPS of $4.67 and ~39x trailing EBITDA — a significant premium to A&D peer median P/E of 28.3x. This premium reflects the market's recognition of Howmet's superior margins, growth trajectory, and competitive positioning, but it leaves limited margin of safety. The recently completed $1.8B acquisition of Consolidated Aerospace Manufacturing adds a fastener consolidation catalyst but also introduces integration risk and incremental leverage. Estimate revisions remain strongly positive (+5% for current year over 90 days, +7.5% for next year), and the company has beaten EPS estimates in all four reported quarters by an average of 6.1%. The secular aerospace cycle — driven by record commercial aircraft backlogs, engine spares demand, and data-center-driven gas turbine orders — provides multi-year revenue visibility.

We maintain a BUY recommendation with a base case target of $280, recognizing that the valuation requires continued execution and that the risk/reward has compressed from levels seen 12 months ago. Position sizing of 2-3% reflects the elevated multiple and near-term macro uncertainty.

## Company Overview

Howmet Aerospace is a Pittsburgh-based manufacturer of advanced engineered components serving primarily the aerospace industry (~70% of revenue) with meaningful exposure to commercial transportation (15%) and industrial gas turbines (11%). The company operates through four segments: Engine Products (52% of revenue, 33.3% EBITDA margin), Fastening Systems (21%, 30.4%), Engineered Structures (14%, 21.2%), and Forged Wheels (13%, 28.5%).

The business model centers on manufacturing mission-critical components — investment castings for jet engine airfoils, seamless rolled rings, aerospace fasteners, titanium forgings — where failure is not an option and qualification cycles span years. Once qualified on an engine program, Howmet becomes effectively sole-sourced for the life of that program. RTX Corporation and GE Aerospace each account for approximately 11% of total revenue, reflecting Howmet's embedded position in the two dominant jet engine OEM platforms.

Operations span 19 countries with approximately 25,430 employees. North America generates 72% of revenue, Europe 22%. The company holds ~1,020 granted patents and ~1,590 registered trademarks. On April 6, 2026, Howmet completed the $1.8B acquisition of Consolidated Aerospace Manufacturing (CAM) from Stanley Black & Decker, consolidating its position in aerospace fasteners.

Net income growth of 30%+ per year for three consecutive years, driven by volume growth combined with pricing in excess of cost inflation.

### Margin Expansion

Margin expansion is the most compelling aspect of Howmet's financials. Consolidated operating margin expanded from 18.1% (2023) to 22.0% (2024) to 24.8% (2025) — a 670 bps improvement in two years. Expansion has been broad-based across all segments:

- Fastening Systems: +980 bps in two years (20.6% → 30.4%)
- Engineered Structures: +830 bps (12.9% → 21.2%)
- Engine Products: +610 bps (27.2% → 33.3%)

Management explicitly states in the 10-K that "product price increases are in excess of material and inflationary cost pass through to our customers." This confirms genuine pricing power, not merely cost recovery. The structural source is qualification-based switching costs: once an engine program is certified with Howmet's components, changing suppliers requires years of re-qualification and regulatory approval.

### Balance Sheet

Long-term debt declined from $4,162M (2022) to $2,859M (2025) — a $1.3B reduction. Net debt/EBITDA stands at approximately 0.8x on trailing Segment EBITDA of $2,507M. Current ratio is 2.13. The company holds $742M in cash. Credit ratings have been upgraded to BBB+/Baa1 by all three agencies.

The CAM acquisition will add approximately $1.8B in debt, pushing net leverage to roughly 1.5-1.6x EBITDA on a pro-forma basis — still conservative for an investment-grade industrial. New credit facilities ($1.0B revolver, $600M 364-day facility, $1.0B commercial paper program) provide ample liquidity.

## Growth Analysis

### Secular Tailwinds

Several structural growth drivers support a multi-year revenue expansion:

1. **Commercial aircraft backlog.** Boeing and Airbus hold combined backlogs exceeding 15,000 aircraft, representing roughly a decade of production at current rates. Boeing's 737 MAX production ramp (FAA approved rate increases in October 2025) directly benefits Howmet's Engine Products and Fastening Systems segments.

2. **Engine spares demand.** The installed commercial engine fleet continues to age, and supply chain constraints have extended maintenance intervals. Engine spares carry higher margins than OE production due to lower volume commitments and pricing leverage.

3. **Gas turbine demand.** Data center electricity requirements are driving significant investment in natural gas turbines. Howmet's Engine Products segment serves this market with the same investment casting and ring technologies used in aerospace. Management highlighted this growth vector at their March 2026 Technology and Markets Day.

### CAM Acquisition

The $1.8B CAM acquisition (closed April 6, 2026) adds an aerospace fastener portfolio that will be integrated into the Fastening Systems segment. Strategic rationale: consolidation of a fragmented fastener market where Howmet already holds leading positions. The risk is integration execution and the step-up in leverage. Fastening Systems has been Howmet's fastest-improving segment (+980 bps margin expansion in 2 years), and the combined entity should benefit from Howmet's operational playbook.

## Valuation Assessment

At $240.88 and a ~$98.9B market cap, Howmet trades at:

- **~51.6x FY2026E EPS** ($4.67)
- **~42.8x FY2027E EPS** ($5.63)
- **~39x trailing EV/EBITDA** (on $2,507M Segment Adj. EBITDA)
- **~69x trailing P/FCF** (on $1,431M FCF)

Against the A&D peer median P/E of 28.3x, Howmet commands a ~82% premium. This is the most significant risk to the investment thesis. The premium can be partially justified:

- Margins 600-800+ bps above most A&D peers
- Organic revenue growth consistently above 10%
- FCF conversion improving (FCF/Net Income ~95%)
- Sole-source competitive positioning with qualification moats
- All segments expanding margins simultaneously

However, at 50x+ forward earnings, the stock is priced for near-perfect execution over multiple years. A deceleration in revenue growth to mid-single digits or any margin compression would likely trigger meaningful multiple contraction.

Applying 45x to FY2027E EPS of $5.63 yields a 12-month target of ~$253. Applying 50x (sustained premium for execution) yields ~$282. The analyst consensus median target is $280, with a range of $214.92 to $315.00. The valuation is stretched but supported by the earnings trajectory; the base case requires the premium to sustain, which depends entirely on continued double-digit growth and margin expansion.

## Competitive Landscape

Howmet's competitive moat is among the widest in the industrials sector. Aerospace engine components must undergo years of testing, certification, and customer approval before entering production. Once qualified, switching suppliers is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming, effectively sole-sourcing Howmet for the life of an engine program.

Key competitors:

- **Precision Castparts (Berkshire Hathaway):** The closest comparable, privately held, competes primarily in investment castings and fasteners. Berkshire's ownership means limited public benchmarking.
- **ATI Inc.:** Competes in titanium and specialty alloys. Less vertically integrated than Howmet.
- **TransDigm Group (TDG):** Competes in fasteners and other components. Higher leverage, higher margins, but more acquisition-dependent growth.
- **VSMPO (Russia):** Major titanium supplier; geopolitical disruption to Russian titanium supply has benefited Howmet's Engineered Structures segment.

Howmet's claim that it can produce "more than 90% of all structural and rotating aero engine components" reflects the breadth of its capabilities. The combination of materials science IP (1,020 patents), process expertise, and installed customer relationships creates a durable competitive advantage that would take a competitor years and billions of dollars to replicate.

## Risk Assessment

### Critical Risks

**Boeing/OEM Production Rate Dependency (HIGH).** Any further delays in Boeing 737 MAX or 787 production ramps directly reduce Howmet's revenue run rate. The 10-K explicitly notes Boeing quality control issues and the 2024 labor stoppage as negative factors. With RTX and GE Aerospace each at ~11% of sales, concentration in the jet engine OEM duopoly is a structural feature that cuts both ways.

**Valuation Risk (HIGH).** At 50x+ forward earnings, the stock has minimal margin of safety. A scenario where revenue growth decelerates to 5-7% while margins plateau could trigger 20-30% multiple compression.

**Tariff & Trade Policy (HIGH).** The 10-K references 2025-2026 executive orders imposing new tariffs and retaliatory measures. Howmet's global supply chain (titanium sponge, nickel, cobalt) and 22% European revenue exposure create direct risk. While the company has demonstrated pricing power to pass through costs, tariff escalation could disrupt specific supply relationships, particularly for limited/sole-source titanium inputs.

**CAM Integration (MODERATE).** The acquisition is Howmet's largest as an independent company. Integration of fastener businesses is relatively straightforward strategically but adds leverage and management bandwidth requirements during a period of elevated organic investment.

**Executive Transition (MODERATE).** The CFO transition (Giacobbe → Winterlich) occurred at year-end 2025. The Chief Legal & Compliance Officer position remains vacant since September 2025. CEO Plant is 72 and holds the dual Executive Chairman/CEO role. The proxy states that the Board reviews succession planning; however, public disclosure of a specific succession plan for the CEO role is limited.

**Cyclicality (MODERATE).** The commercial transportation segment (15% of revenue) is already in downturn — Forged Wheels revenue declined in both 2024 and 2025. Recovery is expected H2 2026 at the earliest. A broader economic downturn affecting commercial aerospace would compound this.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 50.6% is 20.9 percentage points above 1-year historical volatility of 29.7%. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 50.6% vs. 6-month 39.3%), indicating the market expects an imminent volatility event or resolution. Put/call OI ratio of 1.10 shows modestly elevated put positioning, and 1-week skew of 23.1% suggests directional hedging demand around the near-term expiry. The rich IV reflects the stock's decline from its $267.31 high and broader macro uncertainty (oil prices, tariff policy, geopolitical escalation). For fundamental buyers, the elevated IV may present a headwind to options-based entry but does not contradict the underlying business thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($315 — analyst high target)

Revenue growth sustains above 10% through FY2027 as Boeing production ramps accelerate, gas turbine demand from data centers adds a new growth vertical, and engine spares demand remains elevated. CAM acquisition delivers $50-75M in run-rate synergies within 18 months. Margin expansion continues toward 32%+ segment EBITDA margins as operating leverage compounds. FY2027 EPS reaches $6.00+ vs. current consensus of $5.63. Defense spending tailwinds provide additional upside. The market sustains a 50-55x earnings multiple given the duration and quality of the growth trajectory.

### Bear Case ($180 — ~25% downside)

Boeing production ramps stall again due to quality or supply chain issues. Tariff escalation disrupts titanium supply or creates cost headwinds that compress margins by 100-200 bps. CAM integration absorbs management attention while adding leverage. Commercial transportation weakness extends beyond H2 2026. The market re-rates the stock toward the A&D peer median P/E of 28-35x on FY2026 EPS of $4.67, implying $130-163, though some premium would likely persist given margin superiority. A more realistic bear case with moderate multiple compression to 38-40x FY2026 EPS yields $177-187.

## Conclusion

Howmet Aerospace is a high-quality industrial compounder with best-in-class margins, durable competitive moats, and multi-year secular tailwinds from aerospace and gas turbine demand. The financial trajectory — revenue growth above 10%, margin expansion of 600+ bps over two years, FCF doubling in two years, $1.3B debt reduction — is exceptional. The CAM acquisition extends the fastener consolidation thesis. Management has delivered for shareholders: 1,453% TSR since separation vs. 177% for the S&P 500.

The primary constraint is valuation. At ~52x FY2026E earnings, the stock prices in a sustained premium that requires continued flawless execution. The risk/reward is less attractive than it was at $120 a year ago, and position sizing should reflect the compressed margin of safety. Rising estimate revisions, a clean earnings beat record, and the structural nature of the demand drivers justify maintaining a BUY with a base case target of $280. Size at 2-3% of portfolio and monitor Boeing production rates and margin trajectory as the key ongoing checkpoints.
