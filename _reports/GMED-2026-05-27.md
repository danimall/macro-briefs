**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Globus Medical has transformed from a mid-cap spine pure-play into a comprehensive musculoskeletal platform through two acquisitions: NuVasive (September 2023, all-stock) and Nevro (April 2025, all-cash). The company is now the #3-4 global spine company with an integrated robotics platform (ExcelsiusGPS) and a newly acquired neuromodulation business. Operating margins expanded from 6.6% in FY2024 to 19.8% in Q1 2026 as acquisition-related charges rolled off, and consensus estimates are rising (+11.3% for current year EPS over 90 days). The company beat EPS estimates by 19.4% in Q1 2026.

However, the stock dropped ~8% on earnings day (May 7) and has since partially recovered from ~$76 to ~$85. The selloff reflected investor disappointment with flat revenue guidance despite the Q1 beat, coupled with acknowledged "lumpiness" in Nevro's contribution and uncertainty about enabling technologies capital sales cycles. At ~18x forward FY2026 earnings ($4.72 consensus), valuation is reasonable for the growth profile but represents a premium to where it traded during the NuVasive integration period. The prior BUY recommendation issued May 6 at $89.22 has resulted in a loss, and the fundamental picture has not changed materially enough to warrant repeating that call at current levels without a clearer near-term catalyst.

## Company Overview

Globus Medical develops and commercializes solutions for musculoskeletal disorders, operating through two segments: Musculoskeletal Solutions (~80%+ of revenue) encompassing spinal implants, trauma, arthroplasty, biologics, and spinal cord stimulation; and Enabling Technologies (robotics, imaging, navigation). The company generates 80.6% of revenue domestically and sells in 64 countries.

The company is controlled by founder David C. Paul who holds 66.1% voting power through Class B shares. Headquarters are in Audubon, Pennsylvania, with manufacturing in PA, OH, TX, and Costa Rica. Market cap is approximately $9.4 billion on ~113 million shares outstanding.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** FY2025 revenue reached $2.94B (+16.7% YoY), driven by a full year of NuVasive and ~9 months of Nevro ($293.6M contributed). Q1 2026 revenue was $759.9M (+27% YoY), beating estimates by 2.7%. Management guided FY2026 revenue to $3.18-3.22B (~9% growth).

**Margin expansion is the core financial thesis.** The NuVasive acquisition created $215.4M of inventory step-up charges in FY2024 that artificially depressed margins. With those cleared:
- Gross margin: 58.9% (FY2024) → 67.4% (FY2025) → improving further in Q1 2026
- Operating margin: 6.6% (FY2024) → 16.3% (FY2025) → 19.8% (Q1 2026)
- This represents genuine structural improvement as one-time charges roll off

**Cash generation is strong:** FY2025 operating cash flow was $753.4M (up 45% YoY), with FCF of $588.8M (20% FCF margin). The company repaid $450M in 2025 Notes and repurchased $214.7M of stock.

**Balance sheet:** Debt-to-equity of 0.16x, current ratio of 4.26x, no borrowings on the $400M revolver, and an Altman Z-Score of 6.19. This balance sheet provides substantial optionality for further M&A or accelerated buybacks.

**Non-GAAP EPS guidance:** Raised to $4.70-$4.80 for FY2026, implying the stock trades at ~18x midpoint ($4.75). Consensus for FY2027 is $5.14, putting the stock at ~16.5x next year.

## Growth Analysis

Growth vectors are diversified but each carries uncertainty:

1. **Organic spine growth (mid-single digits):** U.S. spine revenue grew 9.5% in FY2025 excluding Nevro. This is above market (spine market growing ~3-4%), indicating share gains. NuVasive's complementary product line and combined sales force are driving cross-selling.

2. **Nevro/SCS integration (~$350M annualized revenue):** The Painful Diabetic Neuropathy (PDN) opportunity is meaningful but execution risk is high. Management acknowledged "near-term lumpiness" and the need to retrain sales personnel. Nevro was distressed for reasons; those fundamental challenges (reimbursement headwinds, competitive intensity from Boston Scientific/Abbott/Medtronic) don't disappear under new ownership.

3. **Enabling Technologies (robotics):** ExcelsiusGPS placements drive recurring implant pull-through. However, domestic ET revenue declined $17.3M in FY2025 as capital sales cycles lengthened and competition from Medtronic intensified. The shift toward "flexible placements" (leases, per-procedure) may improve pull-through but delays revenue recognition.

4. **International expansion:** International revenue grew from $377M (FY2024) to $432M (FY2025), a 14.6% increase with runway in 64 countries.

**Estimate revision momentum is positive:** Current year EPS estimates rose +11.3% over 90 days (from $4.24 to $4.72), and next year rose +9.9% (from $4.68 to $5.14). These revisions are meaningful but below the +30% threshold that would constitute a strong BUY signal under inflection conditions.

## Valuation Assessment

At $85.10, GMED trades at:
- ~18x FY2026 consensus EPS ($4.72)
- ~16.5x FY2027 consensus EPS ($5.14)
- ~16x FY2025 FCF ($588.8M / $9.4B market cap = 6.3% FCF yield)

**Versus peers:** The peer median P/E is 44.3x (Masimo 44.3x, Insulet 36.1x, Revvity 46.4x). GMED at 18x trades at a massive discount to this peer set. However, the peer set is imperfect — these companies have different growth profiles and end markets. More relevant spine/medtech comps (Stryker ~28x, Zimmer ~14x, Medtronic ~16x) position GMED toward the middle.

**Analyst targets:** Consensus mean of $111.08 with a range of $87-$123. The low target ($87) is only 2.2% above current price, suggesting even bears see limited downside. The median target of $115 implies 35% upside.

**Assessment:** The valuation is reasonable for a company executing structural margin expansion with high-single-digit growth. The 18x forward multiple on rising estimates is neither obviously cheap nor expensive. The discount to peer median (44x) reflects acquisition integration uncertainty and the Nevro overhang. The stock is fairly valued with moderate upside if execution continues, but the market needs proof on Nevro and robotics before re-rating higher.

## Competitive Landscape

GMED's competitive position has materially strengthened through the NuVasive combination. The company now offers:
- The broadest independent spine implant portfolio
- An integrated robotics platform (ExcelsiusGPS) with proprietary imaging
- A neuromodulation platform (HFX/Senza SCS via Nevro)
- An exclusive, trained direct sales force

**Competitive threats are real:** Medtronic has substantially greater resources and is aggressively competing in robotics. Stryker's Mako platform dominates in joints and could move more aggressively into spine. In SCS, Boston Scientific's WaveWriter Alpha and Abbott's Proclaim platforms are gaining share while Nevro has been losing it.

The ExcelsiusGPS "pull-through" model (robot drives implant sales) is strategically sound but capital equipment sales cycles lengthening in a higher-rate environment creates near-term headwinds. Management's pivot toward flexible placements (lease/per-procedure) is the right strategic response but delays revenue recognition.

**Moat assessment:** GMED has a moderate moat through its integrated platform, surgeon relationships, and patent portfolio (3,374 US patents). The moat is widening on the spine implant side through combination benefits but may be narrowing in robotics as competitors catch up.

## Risk Assessment

**Near-term risks:**
- **Nevro integration execution:** Revenue "lumpiness" acknowledged. If SCS revenue deteriorates further or PDN adoption disappoints, the bargain purchase thesis weakens.
- **FDA Warning Letter (open since July 2024):** No safety concern but could delay new product clearances for ExcelsiusGPS. The longer this remains open, the more it constrains the enabling technologies pipeline.
- **Conservative revenue guidance:** Management held FY2026 revenue guidance flat despite Q1 beat. Either they lack visibility or there are known headwinds in H2.

**Structural risks:**
- **Controlled company governance:** David Paul's 66.1% voting control means minority shareholders have essentially no influence on strategic decisions. This eliminates M&A takeout premium and limits activist pressure.
- **Reimbursement pressure:** Payors increasingly restricting lumbar fusion coverage could constrain procedure volumes.
- **Tariff exposure:** Costa Rica manufacturing (Nevro SCS) creates tariff vulnerability, though the Feb 2026 Supreme Court ruling mitigates some risk.

### Options Market Signal

Current ATM IV of 34.4% sits materially below 1-year historical volatility of 44.0% — a 9.5pp discount suggesting the options market views GMED as less risky going forward than its recent realized vol would suggest. This is modestly bullish. The P/C OI ratio of 0.21 (heavily call-biased open interest) confirms institutional positioning skews constructive. IV collapsed from 72.5% (pre-earnings May 6) to 34.4% (current), reflecting fully resolved earnings uncertainty. Put/call skew is balanced at 0.2%, indicating no directional stress.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($105-115, +23-35%):**
- Operating margin expansion continues toward 22-24% as NuVasive synergies fully materialize and Nevro integration stabilizes
- Nevro's PDN opportunity exceeds low expectations, adding $50-100M incremental revenue by FY2027
- ExcelsiusGPS gains share with new augmented reality (ExcelsiusXR) and flexible placement models
- Multiple expands toward 22-24x on $5.14 FY2027 EPS as market recognizes the margin trajectory
- Buyback acceleration at depressed levels provides EPS tailwind

**Bear Case ($65-70, -18-24%):**
- Nevro integration fails — SCS revenue declines as competitive losses accelerate, validating why the market priced Nevro at $5.85/share
- FDA Warning Letter escalates to consent decree or import restrictions affecting ExcelsiusGPS
- Robotics capital sales continue declining as Medtronic and Stryker take share
- Reimbursement cuts to lumbar fusion reduce procedure volumes 5-10%
- Stock reverts to 13-14x forward earnings on reduced visibility

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q2 2026 revenue beat of >3% AND management raises full-year revenue guidance (confirms conservatism was intentional)
2. Nevro quarterly revenue exceeds $100M (demonstrating stability)
3. Stock pulls back to $75-78 on no fundamental change (creating more attractive risk/reward at ~16x forward earnings)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Nevro quarterly revenue declines below $80M (suggesting deterioration)
2. FDA Warning Letter escalates to consent decree language affecting new product submissions
3. Operating margin reverts below 17% for any quarter

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, likely August 2026). Revenue guidance is the key variable — if management raises it, the conservatism premium unwinds. If they maintain or cut, integration concerns deepen.

## Addressing Prior BUY Thesis (-4.6% Loss)

The prior BUY issued May 6 at $89.22 was fundamentally sound — the margin expansion thesis, estimate revisions, and beat history were all correct. The stock subsequently dropped ~15% post-earnings (to $76) because:

1. Management held revenue guidance flat despite the beat, signaling limited visibility on Nevro's contribution
2. The market wanted a revenue raise and punished conservatism
3. Broader medtech de-rating in May (healthcare sector weakness)

The stock has recovered to $85, but the information content of management's conservative posture is new and relevant. It suggests management itself lacks full confidence in H2 revenue predictability. This does not kill the thesis, but it removes the clear catalyst required to repeat a BUY call. The underlying business is performing well (margins expanding, earnings beating), but integration uncertainty creates a period where HOLD is the appropriate call until revenue visibility improves.

## Conclusion

Globus Medical is executing structural margin expansion following transformative acquisitions. The balance sheet is strong, estimates are rising, and the stock trades at a meaningful discount to healthcare equipment peers. Options positioning is constructive.

However, conservative revenue guidance, Nevro integration uncertainty, the open FDA Warning Letter, and controlled-company governance collectively justify patience at current levels. The prior BUY lost money on management's own signaling of uncertainty — a legitimate reason to wait for clearer visibility. HOLD with a growth-oriented thesis pending Q2 2026 revenue confirmation.
