**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cheniere is the largest U.S. LNG exporter and second-largest globally by liquefaction capacity, operating the Sabine Pass and Corpus Christi terminals with over 60 mtpa of total expected capacity. The investment case rests on a contracted-cash-flow engine: roughly 90% of anticipated production is locked under long-term, take-or-pay-style agreements through the mid-2030s with ~30 counterparties, generating a ~$290.6B SPA backlog ($107.7B fixed fees insulated from gas price moves). Management has paired this with an aggressive capital-return program: a buyback authorization raised to ~$10B through 2030 (roughly 19-20% of the current $51.1B market cap), a dividend up >10% to $2.22 annualized, and a share count falling from 251M in 2022 to ~210M today. Credit ratings were upgraded across all three agencies in 2025-26 (Moody's lifted parent unsecured to Baa2 in February 2026), confirming the deleveraging path toward a ~4x run-rate.

Cheniere's GAAP earnings are nearly useless as a quarterly signal. Q1 2026 reported a $3.5B net loss (–$16.65/share) driven almost entirely by a $4.8B non-cash unfavorable IPM derivative remeasurement, as Middle East conflict widened the spread between global LNG prices and Henry Hub. Adjusted EPS was +$4.77, operating cash flow stayed positive at $1.08B, and management raised FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance to $7.25-7.75B. Any model keying on reported net income misreads this company; the economic business is steadier than the headline volatility suggests.

The reasons this is a HOLD rather than a BUY are specific and falsifiable. First, valuation: on raised guidance the base business trades near fair value (~13-14x forward adjusted EPS, ~20x distributable cash flow), and the upside in the merchant/uncontracted ~10% of volumes is being squeezed as ~20 mtpa of new global LNG supply arrives and Asian demand contracts. Second, governance: three of the most senior officers (CFO, CCO, CLO) sold ~$27M combined within days of each other near the March 2026 high of $290-300 in discretionary (non-10b5-1) sales, with no offsetting open-market buying, which cuts against the company's own buyback narrative. Third, the IPM derivative structure mechanically produces multi-billion-dollar GAAP swings that drive sentiment-based selloffs regardless of cash fundamentals. The risk/reward at $244 is balanced.

## Company Overview

Cheniere operates two coastal export terminals. Sabine Pass (Louisiana, >30 mtpa) is held through publicly traded subsidiary Cheniere Energy Partners (CQP), in which LNG owns the GP interest, a 48.6% LP interest, and 100% of incentive distribution rights; Blackstone, Brookfield, and the public hold the remaining 49.4%. Corpus Christi (Texas, >30 mtpa) is wholly owned. Corpus Christi Stage 3 (7 midscale trains, >10 mtpa) is ~96.5% complete with 5 of 7 trains done as of Q1 2026 and full completion expected 2H 2026. CCL Trains 8 & 9 (~5 mtpa, FID June 2025, ~$2.9B Bechtel EPC) target 2H 2028. About 8 mtpa remains under construction. Since first cargo in February 2016, Cheniere has shipped 325M+ tonnes across 4,760+ cargoes to 40+ countries.

The contract architecture is central. SPAs charge a fixed fee paid regardless of whether the customer lifts the cargo, plus a variable fee indexed to 115% of Henry Hub sized to pass through feed-gas cost, which insulates Cheniere from U.S. gas price moves. IPM agreements buy feed gas at a global JKM-linked price minus a fixed liquefaction fee, with the resulting LNG sold short-term; economically these earn a fixed margin, but they are accounted for as derivatives at fair value, the source of the GAAP volatility.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 revenue rose 27% to $20.0B, driven by higher Henry Hub pass-through (+$2.9B), higher Stage 3 ramp volumes (+$1.2B), and a $417M favorable derivative swing. Reported net income of $5.3B (diluted EPS $24.13) included $3.6B of pre-tax non-cash derivative gains. Operating cash flow was $5.54B and FCF $2.46B. The four-year revenue path ($33.4B in 2022 → $20.4B → $15.7B → $20.0B) reflects commodity pricing and contract-mix swings layered on a steadily growing volume base.

The Q1 2026 quarter is the cleanest illustration of the accounting dynamic. The Liquefaction Supply Derivative swung from a +$2.9B asset (Dec 2025) to a –$1.7B liability (Mar 2026), a ~$4.6B non-cash move in one quarter. A 10% commodity-price move shifts this fair value by ~$3.1B. Record export volumes were achieved. The earnings-surprise record reflects this noise mechanically: a –495.9% Q1 2026 GAAP "miss" sits next to +171.2%, +60.1%, and +185.7% prior-quarter beats, none of which carry signal once read against adjusted figures.

On the balance sheet, the Altman Z-Score of 1.41 ("distress zone") is a false positive. Project-financed LNG infrastructure runs deliberately high leverage: D/E ~4.4x, equity/assets ~16.5%, but EBIT/assets a healthy 0.19. Total debt is ~$23.9B at a 4.65% weighted-average rate, laddered 2027-2056 across four independent capital silos (SPL, CQP, CCH, parent). The relevant lens is debt-service coverage (SPL/CCH covenants require ≥1.25x; all in compliance) and the credit-rating trajectory, which is positive: BBB+ at S&P/Fitch and Moody's Baa2 (parent unsecured) / Baa1 (CCH secured). Liquidity was ~$8.3B at Q1 2026; the 0.94 current ratio is normal given restricted-cash mechanics. The March 2026 note issuance ($1.0B 5.20% 2036, $750M 6.00% 2056) was used to prepay $550M of CCH facility and cancel $600M of commitments, deliberately shifting financing up to the now-investment-grade parent.

## Growth Analysis

Estimate revisions are meaningfully positive: current-year EPS has risen +20.4% over 90 days (to $17.91) and next-year +31.5% (to $19.51), with smaller but still positive 30-day moves (+12.6% CY, +8.2% NY). Under normal circumstances a +31.5% 90-day revision would be a strong signal. Here it requires qualification: a large share of the revision reflects derivative-mark and commodity-spread dynamics rather than a clean volume/margin acceleration, and the volatility in the current-quarter estimate (–6.9% over 30 days) underscores how sensitive these numbers are to commodity marks. The durable growth driver is volume: Stage 3 ramping (FY2026 production guidance lifted to ~52-54M tonnes) plus the eventual Trains 8&9 (2H 2028) and the pre-FID SPL Expansion (~20 mtpa, FID target 2026/2027) and CCL Expansion (up to 24 mtpa, FID target 2027/2028). The May 2026 ~$4.69B Bechtel EPC contract for SPL Expansion Phase 1 is a concrete step toward FID and the most actionable near-term catalyst.

The secular backdrop supports volume. WoodMac forecasts global LNG demand +64% by 2040 (410→671 mtpa) and a need for ~104 mtpa of additional capacity by 2040 beyond what is built or under construction; Europe is structurally replacing Russian gas (EU vote to ban Russian LNG by 2027). The countercurrent is timing: ~20 mtpa of new global supply arrived in 2025, with U.S./Canada/Qatar capacity surging through the late 2020s. Asian demand fell ~4% in 2025 (China –16%), with Europe (+27%) the anchor. This oversupply wave pressures merchant/spot margins on the uncontracted ~10% and complicates recontracting of expiring SPAs at attractive prices over time. The volume story is durable; the per-unit-margin story softens medium-term.

## Valuation Assessment

At $243.90, Cheniere trades at ~35.9x trailing reported EPS (a meaningless figure given the derivative noise) and roughly 13-14x forward adjusted EPS (consensus ~$17.91 CY / ~$19.51 NY). On distributable cash flow, which is the right metric for this name, the multiple is closer to ~20x. Against the FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guide of $7.25-7.75B and ~$23.9B total debt, the enterprise (≈$74-75B) values the business at roughly 10x EV/EBITDA, full but not stretched for an investment-grade infrastructure compounder with a contracted backlog.

The analyst consensus (mean $302.64, median $304.50, range $259-340) sits well above the current price, implying ~24% upside to the mean. I do not treat that gap as a clean BUY confirmation. The stock peaked at $300.89 in late March 2026 and has since traded down ~19% as merchant-margin compression and the IPM-loss optics weighed; the consensus targets are partly stale relative to that move. The upside that targets extrapolate depends on flexible-cargo and recontracting economics that the confirmed oversupply wave is actively eroding. The base business is fairly valued; the optionality (merchant upside, expansion FIDs) is real but financing- and price-contingent. This warrants a mid valuation score rather than a high one. A pullback toward $200-215, where the buyback has historically been active ($202/share average in Q1 2026, $221 in 2025), would shift the asymmetry favorably.

## Competitive Landscape

Cheniere's competitive position is strong and stable-to-improving. First-mover scale, brownfield expansion economics (shared infrastructure lowers incremental cost), a Henry Hub-linked contract structure attractive to buyers, and investment-grade credit constitute a durable moat. Recent commercial wins (a second long-term SPA with CPC Taiwan (~1.2 mtpa, 2026-2050), JERA (~1 mtpa to 2050), and the Canadian Natural Resources IPM) confirm continued contracting demand even as the global market loosens. The ~15-year weighted average remaining backlog life provides revenue visibility few energy companies can match.

The competitive risk is medium-term margin compression, not displacement. As Qatari and additional U.S. capacity arrives, the marginal value of uncontracted volumes falls and recontracting expiring SPAs at premium fees becomes harder. Management itself flags the transition "from multi-year tight conditions into a period of rapid growth." Cheniere's positioning within that environment remains advantaged, with expansion capacity described as cost-competitive with new global projects, but the industry tailwind is shifting from price to volume, which trims the score modestly from prior readings.

## Risk Assessment

**IPM derivative GAAP volatility (most important, most misunderstood).** Non-cash fair-value swings of multiple billions per quarter can produce GAAP losses even when cash flow is strong. This is volatility risk, not economic risk (the marks largely offset over the contract life) but it drives sentiment-based selloffs and complicates any earnings-based valuation.

**Global LNG oversupply.** The central tension is long-term volume growth against medium-term price/margin softening, which compresses merchant margins and recontracting economics.

**Construction/EPC dependence.** Growth relies on Bechtel delivering Stage 3, Trains 8&9, and the pre-FID expansions. Fixed-price contracts mitigate but do not eliminate cost/schedule risk; routine change orders are disclosed.

**Section 301 U.S.-built-vessel mandate (genuinely idiosyncratic).** USTR rules require 1% of U.S. LNG exports on U.S.-built vessels by April 2029, rising to 15% by 2047. There are essentially no U.S.-built LNG carriers today. Implementation timeline is uncertain after the November 2025 U.S.-China deal deferred related fees, but this is a real long-dated structural overhang a generic energy screen would miss.

**Property-tax incentive roll-off.** Ad valorem incentives at Corpus Christi and Sabine Pass begin expiring in 2026 and 2027; 2025 ad valorem tax was ~$89M and the step-up is uncertain.

**Feed-gas/pipeline dependence and counterparty concentration.** Only ~70% of 2026 feed-gas needs are secured long-term (plus 8% via IPM); ~30 customers carry SPA termination rights for delivery failures or force majeure.

**Governance caution.** Cluster selling by the three most senior officers (CFO Davis $8.7M, CCO Feygin $11.8M, CLO Markowitz $6.5M) plus Director Botta near the March high, in discretionary non-10b5-1 sales, with no open-market buying observed. Common for executives with large equity comp, but the timing near the peak cuts against the buyback signal.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV is 47.6% against 27.1% one-year HV, rich (spread +20.6pp, 73rd percentile within the chain). The term structure is in backwardation (47.6% near vs 31.5% at 6 months), and IV jumped 10.4pp over the last three sessions even as the price rose 3.2%, indicating the market is pricing imminent stress, most likely around the June FOMC and the June options expiration rather than a company-specific catalyst. The 1-week put/call skew of 30% (12.2% at the 1w tenor) reflects elevated near-term put demand, but the 1-month put/call OI ratio of 0.70 and modest 0.44 volume ratio are not extreme. Longer-dated skew turns mildly negative (-4.7% at 6 months), consistent with structural downside hedging into the oversupply backdrop. The rich front-end IV is not a clean entry-timing signal; it argues for patience rather than chasing here.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($300-340):** Stage 3 completes on schedule and lifts run-rate volumes; SPL Expansion reaches FID in 2026/2027, extending the brownfield growth runway at attractive incremental economics; the ~$10B buyback retires ~19-20% of the float over five years while the dividend compounds >10%/yr; sustained geopolitical tightness keeps flexible-cargo margins elevated; and credit continues toward the ~4x target, supporting further re-rating. Consensus mean of $302.64 and high of $340 are achievable if merchant margins hold and FIDs land.

**Bear Case ($190-205):** The oversupply wave compresses merchant margins faster than expected, recontracting of expiring SPAs comes at lower fees, an IPM-driven GAAP loss recurs and triggers another sentiment-based selloff, Section 301 vessel compliance becomes a tangible cost, and property-tax incentives roll off into a softer pricing environment. The stock retests the 52-week low of $186.20.

The base case sits between: a contracted-cash machine that compounds through buybacks and volume growth while merchant upside is capped by oversupply, leaving the stock fairly valued near current levels with the next clear catalyst (SPL FID) several quarters out.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a **growth_hold**. The 12-month thesis is positive (volume growth, capital return, and credit improvement) but the entry is full and the senior-insider selling plus merchant-margin compression cap conviction at HOLD rather than BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback toward $200-215 (where the buyback is historically active, ~$202-221/share), restoring asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.
- Confirmed SPL Expansion FID with associated long-term SPA coverage, de-risking the next growth leg.
- Evidence of merchant/recontracting margins holding through the oversupply wave (two consecutive quarters of adjusted EBITDA at or above the upper guide with stable marketing margins), accompanied by a halt or reversal in senior-officer selling.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A recurring IPM GAAP loss accompanied by a guidance cut to Adjusted EBITDA below ~$7.0B.
- A Stage 3 or Trains 8&9 schedule slip or material cost overrun beyond fixed-price protections.
- Section 301 enforcement crystallizing into a quantified, near-term cost, or a covenant coverage ratio approaching the 1.25x floor.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, ~early August), the SPL Expansion FID decision window (2026/2027), and Stage 3 full completion (2H 2026).

## Conclusion

Cheniere is a high-quality, investment-grade LNG infrastructure compounder with a contracted backlog few peers can match, disciplined capital return, and a de-risked near-term growth ramp. The score trajectory has declined from 7.0 in May to 6.1 now, and the new data confirms rather than reverses that drift: the underlying business is intact and guidance was raised, but the global LNG oversupply wave is now a confirmed medium-term headwind to merchant margins, the valuation is full on forward adjusted metrics, and the cluster of senior-officer selling near the March high contradicts the buyback signal. The stock's ~19% decline from its March peak reflects real fundamental pressures rather than a simple mispricing, and the burden of proof for a BUY at $244 is not met. I rate LNG a **HOLD**, with a clear path to upgrade on a pullback toward $200-215 or a confirmed SPL Expansion FID.
