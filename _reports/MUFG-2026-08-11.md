## Executive Summary

MUFG is the primary beneficiary of the end of Japanese deflation, and the numbers confirm it. Fiscal year ended March 2026 produced ordinary income of ¥14,620.8bn (+7.3%), ordinary profits of ¥3,410.2bn (+27.7%) and net profit attributable to owners of parent of ¥2,427.2bn (+30.3%), lifting ROE from 9.3% to 11.3% while shareholders' equity grew 8.5%, so the ROE improvement is earnings-driven, not the result of a shrinking denominator. The first quarter of FY3/27 accelerated: ordinary income +20.1%, ordinary profits +57.8%, net profit ¥809.4bn (+48.2%), against a pre-print S&P Global Market Intelligence poll of ¥644.0bn on the same Japanese GAAP basis, a 25.7% beat and the fourth consecutive beat in four quarters. The mechanism is straightforward: with ¥433.9trn of assets against ¥22.7trn of shareholders' equity, roughly 19x accounting leverage, every basis point of asset repricing after the Bank of Japan's move to a 1.00% policy rate converts into a large absolute profit number. Revenue +20.1% translating into ordinary profit +57.8% is that leverage working.

The problem is that the market has already capitalized considerably more than what has been delivered. Taking the $22.49 official close, USD/JPY of 157.45 in the week of the US–Japan joint intervention, and a 1:1 ADS-to-ordinary-share ratio, the implied Tokyo price is ¥3,541 against derived 30 June 2026 book value per share of ¥2,016.7, approximately **1.76x book on an 11.3% delivered ROE**. A standard justified-P/B framework at an 8.5% cost of equity and 2% terminal growth implies that 1.76x requires sustainable ROE of about 13.1%; the delivered 11.3% supports roughly 1.42x, or an implied ADR value near $18.20, almost exactly the $18.00 low analyst target. The stock is therefore priced for a further structural step-up in Japanese spreads that has not yet occurred, which locates the downside in multiple compression rather than in an earnings miss.

Two additional facts argue for patience rather than purchase. First, management reaffirmed its FY3/27 profit **target** of ¥2,700bn on 3 August with no change, and Q1's ¥809.4bn represents 30.0% of it; the implied residual of ¥1,890.6bn for Q2–Q4 compares to ¥1,881.2bn actually earned in Q2–Q4 of FY3/26, or +0.5% growth. Second, at $22.49 the ADR trades 4.2% *above* the $21.59 mean analyst target and 3.3% below the 52-week high of $23.25 after a 53% twelve-month advance. Consensus estimates are rising (current-year ADR EPS $1.58, +7.9% over 90 days; next year $1.68, +8.4%), which supports holding, but the revision magnitude is high-single-digit rather than the sort of velocity that overwhelms a full multiple. HOLD, with a base case of $24.00 and a specific re-entry framework below.

## Company Overview

MUFG describes itself in its Consolidated Summary Report as "engaged in financial service businesses such as banking business, trust banking business, securities business and credit card/loan businesses," listed in Tokyo, Nagoya and New York (TSE 8306; NYSE ADR: MUFG). President & Group CEO is identified as Junichi Hanzawa in both the 15 May 2026 and 3 August 2026 reports.

Scale is the defining characteristic. Total assets were ¥431.73trn at March 2026 and ¥433.91trn at June 2026, roughly $2.75trn at USD/JPY 157, against total net assets of ¥24.18trn and shareholders' equity of ¥22.70trn. The company-defined equity-to-asset ratio was 5.2%, improved from 5.0%. Simple accounting leverage (assets ÷ shareholders' equity) computes to 19.4x at March and 19.1x at June. This is thinner equity-to-assets than US G-SIBs typically carry but normal for a Japanese megabank whose balance sheet holds a large low-risk-weight component; it is not a regulatory measure and is not a substitute for CET1, which is not available in the disclosure set reviewed.

FY3/26 income from investments in affiliates accounted for by the equity method was **¥845,545mn**, up 41.6% from ¥596,956mn, equal to 34.8% of net profit attributable to owners of parent. The ratio mixes a pre-tax-consolidated ordinary-profit line with a bottom-line denominator, so the precision is indicative rather than exact, but the order of magnitude holds: roughly a third of group profit is generated by entities MUFG does not control, does not consolidate, and whose risk-taking and distributions it cannot direct. MUFG's largest such holding is generally understood to be a minority stake in a US securities firm with which it also operates Japanese securities joint ventures; MUFG is a shareholder and JV counterparty there, not a parent. The identity and size of the stake could not be confirmed from a filing, so the attribution within the ¥845.5bn is inference, not established fact.

A reporting note: MUFG is a foreign private issuer filing Form 20-F (most recent 6 July 2026) and furnishing interim results on Form 6-K under Japanese GAAP. There are no Form 4 insider filings and no DEF 14A. The absence of insider-transaction data is a reporting-regime artifact and should be treated as not applicable rather than as a negative signal. MUFG itself warns that Japanese and US GAAP "differ significantly," including on consolidation basis and goodwill treatment, so the headline ROE of 11.3% is not directly comparable to a US bank peer's.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings and operating leverage.** The FY3/26 progression is clean. Ordinary profit margin (ordinary profits ÷ ordinary income) rose from 19.6% to 23.3%; in Q1 FY3/27 it reached 28.6% versus 21.8% a year earlier. Net margin on ordinary income moved from 16.8% to 20.7% over the same quarterly comparison. Revenue growth of 20.1% converting into 57.8% ordinary profit growth is roughly 38 points of positive operating leverage, the signature of a spread business repricing assets faster than deposit and operating costs.

**Returns.** FY3/26 ROE of 11.3% reconciles independently: ¥2,427,229mn ÷ average shareholders' equity of ¥21,397,158mn = 11.34%. ROA was 0.56% versus 0.45%, low in absolute terms, as expected for a balance sheet still carrying substantial low-yield assets, but improving. Book value per share rose 10.6% to ¥1,973.31 at March and a further 2.2% to a derived ¥2,016.7 at June. The share basis reconciles: ¥22,273,941mn ÷ 11,287.6mn ex-treasury shares = ¥1,973.3, matching disclosure.

**Capital returns.** Dividends per share went ¥64 → ¥86 → ¥96 forecast, with payout ratios pinned at 40.0%, 40.3% and 40.1%. Total FY3/26 dividends were ¥976,032mn. Shares outstanding including treasury fell 200mn (−1.66%) during FY3/26 through cancellation, with average outstanding shares down 2.2%; SEC cover-page share counts fell about 10.9% across the 2022–2026 cover dates. In Q1 FY3/27 treasury holdings rose 31.84mn shares, approximately 0.28% of the ex-treasury count, implying roughly 1.1% annualized retirement if sustained; the FY3/27 authorization size is not disclosed in available material, so that annualization is a sensitivity, not guidance. Combined shareholder yield is approximately 3.5–3.8% on the forecast dividend plus that buyback pace.

An equity roll-forward for Q1 FY3/27 provides a useful integrity check: equity rose ¥425.2bn while the company earned ¥809.4bn and booked roughly ¥331bn of positive comprehensive-income residual, against a ¥51/share year-end dividend of approximately ¥576bn paid 29 June and an estimated ¥110bn of repurchases. 809 + 331 − 576 − 110 = +¥454bn versus the actual +¥425bn, with the gap consistent with non-controlling interests and other equity movements. The disclosed figures hang together.

**Comprehensive income volatility.** Comprehensive income less net profit attributable to owners of parent was **−¥410.5bn in Q1 FY3/26** and **+¥331.3bn in Q1 FY3/27**, and +¥844.0bn for full-year FY3/26. These residuals include non-controlling interests' share of income and are approximations of OCI rather than OCI itself, but a ¥740bn swing in the same fiscal quarter one year apart means mark-to-market and translation items move MUFG's equity by amounts comparable to half a quarter's earnings. That is the mechanical channel through which a further BOJ hike, which helps net interest margin, simultaneously marks down the securities book and pressures regulatory capital.

**Cash flow — read with banking-specific care.** FY3/26 operating cash flow was −¥23,064,420mn against +¥6,415mn prior year; cash and equivalents ended at ¥90,045,500mn versus ¥109,095,437mn. For a deposit-taking institution, operating cash flow embeds movements in deposits, call money, repo and trading assets. This is not a free-cash-flow proxy, and the negative figure is not evidence of distress; the most plausible reading is redeployment of excess central-bank reserves into loans and securities as Japanese rates normalized, which is exactly what a 30% profit increase driven by loan margins would imply. A funding-mix shift is an equally plausible alternative. Any screen that treats bank operating cash flow as FCF will mis-rank this company.

**A data reconciliation to flag.** The market-data feed reports trailing and forward P/E of 20.90 on ADR EPS of $0.95198. Neither reconciles to the reported results at current FX: FY3/26 EPS of ¥213.17 at USD/JPY 157.45 is $1.354, or 16.6x at $22.49. The $0.95 figure is consistent with the *prior* fiscal year's ¥160.02 EPS converted at a materially weaker yen, so the feed's P/E is stale on both the earnings and FX legs. This report uses yen-denominated results and the USD consensus estimate series instead. Similarly, the price feed shows three values: official close $22.49, daily-history close $22.24, and a $22.10 extended-hours trade. All arithmetic here uses $22.49.

**Financial health score: 6.0.** Positives: improving equity-to-assets, 8.5% equity growth, disciplined and formulaic distribution, reconcilable disclosures. Constraints: CET1, the Basel leverage ratio, RWA, credit costs, NPL ratios and allowance coverage are all absent from available disclosure; an aggregated report of the May 2026 call indicates analysts questioned a decline in the capital ratio alongside the buyback size (unconfirmed secondary characterization); and quarterly OCI swings of ±¥400bn make capital genuinely state-dependent. For a 19x-levered institution, unquantified capital and credit are material limitations on how high this dimension can be scored.

## Growth Analysis

The delivered growth is emphatic and the forward growth is not. That distinction drives the rating.

**What has happened.** FY3/26 net profit +30.3%; Q1 FY3/27 +48.2%; four consecutive earnings beats of 45.4%, 6.9%, 39.7% and 18.7% versus per-share estimates. Reuters attributed the Q1 jump to "higher loan margins" and "strong loan demand linked to the end of deflation in Japan." The BOJ raised its policy rate to 1.00% in June 2026 on a 7–1 vote, the highest since 1995, and Tokyo inflation accelerated for a second consecutive month in July, which keeps further tightening on the table.

**What is guided.** Management reaffirmed a FY3/27 **target** of ¥2,700.0bn on 3 August with the explicit note that there is no change from 15 May. Q1's ¥809.4bn is 30.0% of that. The implied Q2–Q4 residual of ¥1,890.6bn compares to ¥1,881.2bn earned in Q2–Q4 of FY3/26, **+0.5%**. Two readings are defensible and I hold both. The target may be deliberately conservative, in which case consensus migrates upward through the year and management raises at the November print. Alternatively, Q1 contained outsized market-sensitive items — the 57.8% ordinary-profit jump and the ¥1,140.7bn comprehensive income both point to favorable market conditions — in which case a flat implied path realistically embeds normalization plus rising credit costs. Without segment detail and one-off disclosure I cannot separate these, and MUFG's use of a "target" rather than a forecast is itself a caution against reading the number as a base case.

**What consensus expects.** Current-year ADR EPS consensus is $1.58 (up 6.3% in 30 days, 7.9% in 90 days) and next year $1.68 (up 7.8% and 8.4%). Converting the current-year figure at 157.45 gives ¥248.8 per share, or roughly ¥2,801bn, about 4% above the company target, so consensus is already positioned for a beat. Next-year growth of $1.58 to $1.68 is **+6.3%**. That is the forward growth rate that matters for valuation, not the 30–48% trailing prints. Only one to two analysts contribute to these estimates, so the consensus is thin and can move on a single revision.

**Structural constraints on the volume side.** Reuters reported on 15 July that nearly half of Japanese firms are experiencing negative business impact from BOJ hikes, with higher borrowing costs hurting margins and discouraging capital investment. Loan spreads are widening; loan volume growth and credit quality both depend on corporate Japan absorbing 1%+ policy rates. Yen appreciation — one broker projects roughly 6% by end-2026 — would reduce yen-reported overseas earnings, including the equity-method contribution from a US affiliate.

**Growth prospects score: 5.8.** Genuine multi-year rate-normalization tailwind and confirmed operating leverage, offset by high-single-digit forward consensus growth, a reaffirmed target implying flat nine months, and credit costs that are more likely to rise than fall from here.

Cross-checking against MUFG's 2024 ADR trading range supports the 1:1 assumption, but if the ratio differs, every multiple scales proportionally.

FX sensitivity is material and works in the ADR holder's favor on the yen leg: at USD/JPY 150 the implied Tokyo price falls to ¥3,373, reducing P/B to 1.67x without any change in the New York quote; at 165 it rises to 1.85x.

The decisive calculation is the justified-P/B test. Using P/B ≈ (ROE − g)/(COE − g) at an 8.5% Japanese cost of equity and 2% terminal growth, the current 1.76x implies **sustainable ROE of about 13.1%**. Delivered FY3/26 ROE was 11.3%; the company target of ¥2,700bn against average equity of roughly ¥23.5trn implies about 11.5%. Q1 annualizes to roughly 14.3%, but that quarter contained favorable market conditions and should not be extrapolated. On the delivered 11.3%, justified P/B is 1.42x, implying an ADR value near $18.20, which coincides closely with the $18.00 low analyst target. The market is capitalizing a further structural step-up in Japanese spreads rather than the results already reported.

Two cross-checks reach the same conclusion. At $22.49 the ADR trades **4.2% above the $21.59 mean and median analyst target**; the high target of $25.18 offers 11.9% and the low $18.00 implies 20.0% downside. Analyst targets function as floors on inflection names trading cheaply against rising estimates; here the stock is *through* the mean target while the multiple already embeds ROE expansion, so the target set is better read as a constraint than as a floor. The analyst distribution is unusual and unhelpful — one buy and one strong sell — reflecting both thin ADR coverage and genuine disagreement about whether 1.76x book is defensible.

Relative context matters too. A 13.4x multiple on next-year earnings is not demanding against global bank peers. But 1.76x tangible-adjacent book on an 11.3% ROE is a full price when a US bank earning a comparable ~10% ROE trades near 1.3x. Part of the gap is the option on further BOJ tightening; part is beta to a broad, flow-driven Japanese equity re-rating that has been widely reported and is independent of MUFG's operating performance. That second component is the more fragile.

**Valuation score: 4.8.** The multiple is not egregious and the dividend plus buyback provides roughly 3.5–3.8% of support, but there is no identifiable mispricing in MUFG's favor at this price. Forward earnings are cheap only if one underwrites ROE moving toward 13%, and that is precisely what the market has already paid for.

## Competitive Landscape

The upcycle is sectoral, not idiosyncratic. Reuters framed MUFG's Q1 as "joining rivals in seeing earnings jump," tied to the end of deflation lifting earnings across the sector. Very little of the 30%/48% growth should be underwritten as competitive-advantage-driven share gain; it is the rate cycle arriving at the largest balance sheet in Japan.

Scale is the moat, and it is measurable: each basis point of NIM expansion is worth an enormous absolute amount on the ¥433.9trn asset base, which is why revenue growth became far larger ordinary profit growth. Against the other Japanese megabanks, MUFG's relative attraction rests on scale, business mix and capital-return consistency rather than on a differentiated growth engine. Peer-comparative data on capital ratios, credit costs and multiples was not available in this review, which limits how strongly the relative case can be stated.

The equity-method line adds a cross-border earnings channel that domestic-focused peers may not share to the same degree. The affiliate income, up 41.6%, means a meaningful slice of what looks like "Japanese bank normalization" is in fact US capital-markets cyclicality. That diversifies the earnings mix away from Japanese rates while adding correlation to global market conditions. It is an earnings-mix observation only; it says nothing about the concentration of underlying exposures, and no customer- or counterparty-concentration data for MUFG was available.

The demand side is the medium-term constraint. Loan spreads are widening while nearly half of Japanese corporates report negative impact from higher borrowing costs and reduced capex intentions. Widening spreads on flat-to-slowing volume with rising credit costs is a materially different trajectory from widening spreads on growing volume.

**Competitive position score: 6.0.** Stable-to-modestly-improving trajectory driven by a sector-wide tailwind on the largest asset base in the market, without evidence of company-specific share gain.

## Risk Assessment

**Rate cycle is two-sided and the leverage cuts both ways.** The same 38 points of positive operating leverage that produced Q1 reverse if the BOJ pauses or the curve retraces. A pause would compress the multiple faster than it compresses earnings, since 1.76x book is paying for further normalization.

**Regulatory capital is unquantified.** No CET1, no Basel leverage ratio, no RWA. The single window into the May 2026 call indicates analysts pressed simultaneously on a capital-ratio decline and the buyback size — mechanically the same question, whether repurchases are being sized against a thinner capital position than assumed. This is consistent with what the filings show: assets grew ¥18.6trn (+4.5%) in FY3/26 while the yen sat near multi-decade lows, inflating yen-translated foreign RWA, and OCI-type swings of ±¥400bn per quarter move capital directly. I do not infer capital inadequacy; I flag it as the largest unresolved item and note that a buyback reduction would be the visible symptom.

**Credit costs are unquantified.** No credit-cost line, NPL ratio, coverage ratio or allowance detail. With Reuters reporting widespread corporate strain from rate hikes, this is the biggest hole in the earnings-durability case. The FY3/26 report also discloses "Changes in accounting estimates: Yes" with no direction or magnitude given; in a bank the common candidates are allowance parameters or actuarial assumptions. Estimate revisions are routine, and no inference about earnings management is warranted, but the direction is unknown.

**FX is a large, non-netting lever currently in play.** The US and Japan conducted a rare joint yen-buying intervention on 1 August 2026, reported as the first coordinated operation with the US since 1998, after the yen hit 40-year lows, with USD/JPY at 157.45 on 2 August. For a dollar-based holder, yen appreciation raises the ADR value and mechanically shrinks yen-denominated foreign RWA (capital-positive), while reducing yen-reported overseas earnings including the affiliate contribution. These effects do not net to zero and do not point the same way.

**Affiliate concentration.** Roughly 35% of group profit comes from entities MUFG does not control. A US capital-markets downturn would hit that line with no offsetting management action available to MUFG.

**Disclosure and audit status.** The Q1 report states explicitly that the Japanese-language consolidated quarterly statements were not reviewed by certified public accountants or an audit firm, and the summary report is outside the scope of the external auditor's procedures. This is normal Japanese practice, but the +48% quarter is management-prepared and unreviewed. Additionally, the 20-F itself — risk factors, segment detail, legal proceedings, US GAAP reconciliation — was not accessible in this review, so I cannot identify risk factors added or modified in the 2026 filing.

**Multiple beta to a flow-driven re-rating.** Reports through May–August describe foreign investors piling into Japanese equities on AI-investment flows and the yen rescue. Part of MUFG's 1.76x book reflects that flow, which can reverse independently of MUFG's operating results.

**Positioning context.** Short interest is 0.1% of float with 2.1 days to cover: no crowded bear position, and no short-squeeze support for the bulls either. No options data was available for this security in this review.

**Risk factors score: 4.8.** The concentration of unquantified items — capital, credit, the accounting-estimate change — combined with a two-sided rate exposure and a multiple that embeds ROE improvement, means material identifiable risks are not obviously priced.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $27.50 (+22% from $22.49)

The BOJ continues tightening as Tokyo inflation persists; MUFG's asset repricing continues to outrun deposit costs and the ¥2,700bn target proves conservative, with FY3/27 landing near ¥3,000bn (approximately ¥266 EPS) and consensus migrating to the next-year $1.68 and beyond. ROE moves toward 13%, validating and extending the current multiple. Book value per share reaches roughly ¥2,200 by mid-2027. At 1.85x book that is ¥4,070, and at USD/JPY 148 following the intervention-supported yen appreciation one broker projects, that converts to $27.50. The dividend reaches the guided ¥96 with a further raise on the 40% payout formula, and buybacks continue at 1%+ annually. Q2 (November 2026) delivering a target raise is the specific catalyst.

### Bear Case — $17.50 (−22% from $22.49)

Corporate Japan cannot absorb 1%+ policy rates; loan volume growth stalls and credit costs normalize from a suppressed base, with the flagged accounting-estimate change proving to have been allowance-favorable. The BOJ pauses. Further securities mark-downs and a capital ratio the market cannot verify force a smaller buyback authorization, confirming the concern raised on the May call. The multiple compresses to the level justified by delivered 11.3% ROE — approximately 1.35–1.42x on book of ¥2,100, or ¥2,835–2,982, which at USD/JPY 160 converts to $17.72–18.64. This bear case is corroborated by the $18.00 low analyst target and by the justified-P/B arithmetic on delivered returns.

### Base Case — $24.00 (+6.7%)

Consensus is right and the company target is modestly conservative: FY3/27 lands near ¥2,800bn, next-year ADR EPS reaches $1.68, and book value per share grows to roughly ¥2,150 by mid-2027 on ~60% retention. The multiple holds near 1.70x, slightly below the current 1.76x as the flow-driven component of the Japan re-rating fades, giving ¥3,655, which at USD/JPY 152 converts to **$24.05**. That equals 14.3x next-year consensus of $1.68. Adding the ~2.7% forward dividend yield gives roughly a 9% twelve-month total return.

Probability-weighting at 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear: (0.50 × 6.7%) + (0.25 × 22.3%) + (0.25 × −22.2%) = **+3.4% price return**, or approximately **+6% total return** with the dividend. That is a fair-to-modest expected return, not an asymmetric one, and it sits well inside the range where a single BOJ or credit-cost surprise dominates the outcome.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** Existing owners should retain exposure: the earnings momentum is real, four consecutive beats support confidence in the rising consensus, and the November Q2 print is a plausible upgrade catalyst. Prospective buyers have no urgent entry signal at 4.2% above the mean analyst target and 3.3% below the 52-week high.

**Base case:** $24.00 (+6.7%), on 1.70x mid-2027 book of ~¥2,150 at USD/JPY 152, equivalent to 14.3x next-year consensus EPS of $1.68.
**Bull case:** $27.50 (+22.3%).
**Bear case:** $17.50 (−22.2%).
**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the November 2026 Q2 print (target revision), the January and March 2027 BOJ meetings, and the May 2027 FY3/27 results with the FY3/28 target and buyback authorization.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE if any two of:**
1. Price falls to **$19.00–20.00** (approximately 1.50–1.58x June-2026 book, 11.3–11.9x next-year consensus) without fundamental deterioration.
2. Management raises the FY3/27 target above ¥2,900bn at the November print, confirming the conservatism reading rather than the market-gains reading of Q1.
3. Disclosure of a CET1 ratio comfortably above requirement alongside an expanded FY3/27 buyback authorization, resolving the capital question that was raised in May.
4. Credit costs disclosed as flat or declining year over year for two consecutive quarters despite the reported corporate strain.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of:**
1. The FY3/27 target is cut, or Q2 net profit comes in below roughly ¥600bn, breaking the run-rate toward ¥2,700bn.
2. The buyback authorization is reduced or suspended, or a capital ratio is disclosed near regulatory minimums — the specific evidence that the May analyst concern was well founded.
3. The BOJ signals an extended pause and the 10-year JGB retraces materially, removing the further-normalization option the 1.76x multiple capitalizes.
4. Credit costs rise by more than approximately ¥200bn year over year on an annualized basis, or the accounting-estimate change is disclosed as having been allowance-favorable.

**Next reassessment:** the Q2 FY3/27 report in mid-November 2026, with an interim check at the September and October BOJ decisions.

## Conclusion

MUFG has delivered an authentic earnings inflection driven by Japanese rate normalization: FY3/26 net profit +30.3% to ¥2,427.2bn, ROE from 9.3% to 11.3%, Q1 FY3/27 net profit +48.2% to ¥809.4bn against a ¥644.0bn consensus poll, four beats in four quarters, and consensus ADR EPS rising 6–8% across every horizon. Capital return is disciplined and formulaic: a 40% payout with the dividend guided from ¥86 to ¥96, 200mn shares cancelled in FY3/26, and roughly 10.9% cumulative share-count reduction across four cover dates.

The obstacle is price. At the $22.49 official close the ADR trades approximately 1.76x June-2026 book value on an 11.3% delivered ROE, a multiple requiring sustainable ROE near 13.1% at a plausible Japanese cost of equity. It sits 4.2% above the $21.59 mean analyst target after a 53% twelve-month advance, and management's own reaffirmed FY3/27 target implies +0.5% earnings growth for the next nine months after a +48% quarter. Regulatory capital and credit costs, the two variables that determine whether the buyback and the earnings trajectory are sustainable, are both unquantified in available disclosure, while quarterly comprehensive-income swings of ±¥400bn make capital genuinely state-dependent. The probability-weighted twelve-month total return is roughly 6%, with symmetric ±22% scenario tails.

HOLD. The business is performing; the entry is not compelling. Buy the pullback to $19–20 if the fundamentals stay intact, or buy the November target raise if it arrives with a CET1 disclosure and an expanded authorization.
