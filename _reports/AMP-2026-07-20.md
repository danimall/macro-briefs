**Research Date: July 20, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ameriprise Financial is a diversified financial services holding company that manages, administers, or advises $1.67 trillion in assets. The investment case rests on a structural mix-shift: the Advice & Wealth Management (AWM) segment now generates the large majority of segment earnings ($951M of $1,333M in adjusted operating earnings in Q1 2026, +20% YoY at a 30.0% pretax margin), and it produces recurring, capital-light fee revenue. The market values the company at ~11.6x forward earnings, a multiple more appropriate to a rate-sensitive annuity writer than to a high-ROE advice franchise. Adjusted operating ROE (ex-AOCI) sits at 54.3%, well above management's >30% target.

The stock has recovered from a spring trough near $422 (April 2026) to $528, roughly 4% below its 52-week high of $550. The prior report sequence downgraded AMP from BUY to HOLD in June on a declining score trajectory (7.4 → 6.2), with the largest markdowns in management quality (an unresolved succession thread) and growth prospects. The new data argues for reversing part of that caution. Estimate revisions are rising across every horizon — current-year consensus up +6.9% and next-year up +7.9% over 90 days — and the company delivered a 9.2% Q1 EPS beat, extending a four-quarter beat streak. Falling estimates were not the reason for the June downgrade; the concern was governance and growth durability. On the numbers, growth is accelerating.

The reasons to stop short of maximum conviction are genuine and unchanged: earnings are highly levered to equity and rate markets (a 10% equity decline is estimated to cut adjusted pretax income by ~$369M), a legacy long-term care block reinsured partly to Genworth's GLIC carries tail risk, Columbia Threadneedle remains in net outflow, and the founder-era CEO's succession is not clearly resolved in public disclosures. At current price and with rising estimates, we rate AMP a BUY with disciplined sizing, base-case target $600.

## Company Overview

Ameriprise traces its history to 1894, was owned by American Express, and spun off in 2005. It operates four segments:

1. **Advice & Wealth Management (AWM)** — the growth engine. A captive, high-productivity advisor network serving mass-affluent and affluent retail clients through fee-based wrap accounts, brokerage, banking (Ameriprise Bank, FSB), and financial planning. Q1 2026 adjusted operating earnings $951M (+20% YoY), 30.0% pretax operating margin (up from 28.5%). Total client assets $1.15T; advisory wrap assets $658.6B.
2. **Asset Management** — Columbia Threadneedle Investments (US plus UK/European vehicles), $661.6B AUM. Q1 2026 adjusted operating earnings $273M (+13%). The structural soft spot is persistent net outflows ($5.9B in Q1 2026).
3. **Retirement & Protection Solutions (RPS)** — RiverSource Life annuities and insurance. Q1 2026 adjusted operating earnings $190M (−12% YoY), the only declining segment, driven by deliberate de-risking.
4. **Corporate & Other** — houses the closed blocks of long-term care and fixed/fixed-indexed annuities plus consolidated investment entities. Q1 2026 adjusted operating loss of ($81M), improved from ($97M).

The competitive advantage is an integrated, advice-led model in which proprietary asset management and insurance products are distributed through a captive advisor force, generating durable recurring fee revenue. Capital return is central to the equity story.

## Financial Analysis

Profitability and returns are exceptional. Q1 2026 net income was $915M (+57% YoY), diluted GAAP EPS $9.68 (vs. $5.83), and adjusted operating EPS $11.26 (+18.5%). TTM adjusted operating earnings reached $3.97B (+10% YoY). Adjusted operating ROE (ex-AOCI) of 54.3% far exceeds the company's >30% target and its 12–15% EPS-growth objective.

Revenue growth is driven by the fee engine. Total net revenues were $4.81B (+11% YoY), led by a 13% rise in management and advice fees on a 17% increase in average advisory wrap assets, a combination of market appreciation and $6.0B of wrap net inflows in the quarter. The largest swing factor in GAAP earnings was the "market impact on non-traditional long-duration products," which was a $184M expense in Q1 2026 versus $460M in Q1 2025; this line explains most of the 57% GAAP net income jump and is exactly the volatility the de-risking program aims to reduce over time.

The balance sheet requires the right lens. Total assets are ~$184B, dominated by separate account assets ($76.5B, offset by matching liabilities), policyholder balances, and a $59B investment portfolio (92% fixed maturities, ~99% investment grade, heavily agency RMBS and corporates). GAAP equity is only $6.2B, which produces an Altman Z-Score of ~0.14 and D/E of ~28x. These are not distress signals; they reflect the insurance and asset-management balance-sheet structure. AOCI is a $(1.2B) drag from unrealized losses on available-for-sale securities driven by higher rates — non-credit in nature, with no forced sales expected. Parent-company liquidity is ~$2.3B against a conservative ~$3.1B of long-term debt laddered 2026–2035.

Capital allocation is the standout. AMP repurchased $784M–$886M of stock in Q1 2026 (1.6M shares at ~$479 average), leaving $1.8B on a $4.5B authorization running through June 2027. Diluted weighted-average shares fell to ~93.3M from 100.0M a year earlier, a ~7% reduction, and the dividend was raised to $1.70/quarter in April 2026. Operating cash flow fell to $459M from $1.69B, but the driver was derivative collateral timing (counterparties shifting to securities collateral), not a deterioration in core earnings.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in forward estimates: four consecutive beats, with the most recent quarter beating by 9.2% ($11.26 vs. $10.31). That is the largest beat of the four-quarter set, and it occurred as consensus was already rising.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is driven by the AWM mix-shift and reinforced by rising estimate revisions. The segment grew 20% on a 30% margin and now dominates the earnings base, with wrap net inflows of $6.0B and a 17% increase in average advisory assets showing the fee-based flywheel is intact. Ameriprise Bank deposits grew 5% YoY to $23.8B at a deposit cost of just 0.17%, a low-cost funding source that adds spread income as it scales.

Consensus estimates are moving higher across every horizon. Current-quarter estimates are up +4.0% over 90 days, next-quarter +6.7%, current-year +6.9% (to $45.40), and next-year +7.9% (to $49.85). The 30-day revisions are positive as well (+2.1% to +4.6%), indicating the momentum is sustaining rather than fading. Rising estimates combined with an accelerating AWM segment and a shrinking share count are a favorable configuration for per-share compounding. This is not the +30% velocity that flags a deep-trough recovery, so it does not clear the strongest BUY signal bar, but for a franchise of this quality trading below 12x forward earnings, mid-to-high-single-digit upward revision velocity is a meaningful tailwind that the June downgrade did not adequately weigh.

The offsetting drag is Asset Management, which remains in net outflow. The trajectory is improving sharply — outflows were $18.3B a year earlier — and fund performance is respectable (84% of equity assets and 89% of fixed income above index over 10 years; 95 four/five-star Morningstar funds). Even so, the segment participates in the secular active-to-passive shift and should be modeled as a mature, low-growth contributor rather than a driver.

RPS earnings fell 12% by design. Management is running off living-benefit variable annuity exposure (living-benefit riders down to 45% of VA balances from 49%) and legacy fixed deferred balances (down 8%), while shifting toward structured variable annuities (VA sales +10% on SVA strength). This lowers tail risk and reduces the GAAP earnings noise from the non-traditional long-duration line, at the cost of near-term RPS earnings.

## Valuation Assessment

At $528, AMP trades at roughly 11.6x current-year consensus EPS ($45.40) and ~10.6x next-year ($49.85). The trailing P/E of ~13.1x sits on GAAP EPS depressed by the market-impact line; adjusted operating earnings put the multiple lower. The dividend yield is ~1.3%, and total shareholder return is dominated by buybacks — the ~7% annual share-count reduction is the larger component of the per-share compounding story.

For a business generating 54% adjusted operating ROE with a 30%-margin, capital-light wealth engine growing 20%, an 11–12x forward multiple is undemanding. The mispricing thesis is specific and falsifiable: the market is valuing AMP as a rate-and-market-sensitive annuity company rather than as a wealth-management franchise. As the AWM mix-shift and insurance de-risking continue, the earnings base becomes more recurring and less volatile, which supports a higher multiple. Wealth-focused peers (LPL, Raymond James) trade at richer multiples on lower ROE; asset managers trade cheaper but face structural outflow pressure. AMP's blended multiple reflects the latter cohort even though the earnings mix increasingly resembles the former.

The equity-lessons framework counsels treating "cheap" as a value-trap warning absent an independent catalyst. Here the catalyst is credible and quantifiable: rising estimate revisions plus a specific mix-shift that reduces earnings volatility. We give the valuation a 6.0 — above neutral, reflecting genuine but not extreme mispricing, tempered by the fact that a market or rate downturn would compress the fee base quickly and re-anchor the multiple lower.

Analyst targets frame the range: consensus mean $551, median $550, low $485, high $645. The stock trades below the mean, and given the sub-30x forward P/E and rising estimates, consensus targets here should be treated as lagging rather than as a ceiling. Our base case of $600 sits between the median and high target and implies ~12x next-year EPS — still below wealth-peer multiples.

## Competitive Landscape

AMP competes in wealth management (Morgan Stanley, LPL, Raymond James, Schwab, Edward Jones), asset management (BlackRock and the broadly challenged active industry), and annuities/insurance. The dominant dynamic in AMP's own numbers is the bifurcation between thriving fee-based wealth management and structurally pressured active asset management.

In wealth management, AMP's competitive position is improving: 20% earnings growth, expanding margins (28.5% to 30.0%), positive wrap net flows, and advisor-quality signals (643 advisors on Forbes' Best-in-State list). The captive-advisor distribution model is a durable moat that converts proprietary manufacturing into recurring fee revenue. In asset management, the trajectory is stabilizing but the structural position is defensive — outflows are shrinking but persistent, and the secular tide favors passive. The de-risking of the annuity book is a deliberate competitive repositioning that trades near-term earnings for lower tail risk and reduced volatility.

Net, the trajectory is favorable in the segment that matters most (AWM) and managed-decline in the segments that don't (Asset Management, legacy insurance). That supports a competitive-position score of 6.8 — improving where it counts, with a real drag from the active-management franchise.

## Risk Assessment

**Market and rate sensitivity.** Earnings are highly levered to markets. A hypothetical 10% equity decline is estimated to cut pretax income by ~$608M (~$369M adjusted operating). The Weighted Equity Index rose 17% YoY, flattering Q1 results, so a reversal would compress asset-based fees quickly. This is the single largest swing factor and the reason the multiple stays capped.

**Legacy LTC and variable-annuity tail.** The closed LTC block carries a net future policy benefit liability of ~$5.2B after reinsurance. The Q3 2025 annual assumption review already produced unfavorable LTC morbidity/mortality changes requiring a reserve increase. Much LTC risk is reinsured to Genworth's GLIC, a counterparty of weak standalone financial health. Management argues its 2016 Delaware-governed credit protections limit true net exposure, but this is a genuine low-probability, high-severity tail worth monitoring.

**Asset Management outflows.** Persistent net redemptions pressure the segment's fee base even as performance holds up.

**Regulatory intensity.** The 10-Q describes regulatory activity as "elevated" across SEC, FINRA, OCC, FCA, and state regulators, covering advice, sales practices, cash-sweep programs, and advisor supervision. As a savings-and-loan holding company, AMP is subject to the Fed's Building Block Approach capital framework.

**Succession concentration.** CEO James Cracchiolo has led since the 2005 spinoff (~20-year tenure) and CFO Walter Berman is also long-tenured. A cluster of Item 5.02 (officer departure/election) 8-Ks — Nov 12, 2025; Feb 12, 2026; and May 20, 2026 (the last paired with a Reg FD disclosure) — signals organizational change that has not been fully clarified publicly; the specifics of these filings could not be confirmed from the available sources. This is the primary reason the prior report cut management quality from 8.0 to 5.5, and it remains the most important open governance question.

**Near-term operational item.** SEC Rule 15c3-3's move to daily customer-reserve computations at broker-dealer AEIS by June 30, 2026, may add short-term liquidity variability; management expects no material impact.

Because there are identifiable, concentrated tails (LTC/Genworth, succession, market sensitivity), the risk-factors score is 5.5 — below neutral, though the risks are partly priced given the modest multiple.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 33.0% versus 25.9% one-year historical volatility, a rich 7.1-point spread, at the 61st percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (33.0% one-month vs. 27.1% six-month), pricing near-term event stress consistent with the next earnings print and the succession-clarity overhang. Put/call skew is balanced (1.3%) and the put/call open-interest ratio is a low 0.21, so there is no pronounced bearish tilt in positioning. The prior snapshot's 323.8% IV reading was a stale/corrupted artifact; the current 33.0% is the credible number. The implied one-month move is ±7.6%. Net read: the options market is pricing a normal-to-slightly-elevated event window without a directional bearish bias, which does not contradict the fundamental BUY thesis. Rich near-term IV suggests waiting for post-earnings IV crush is a reasonable entry-timing refinement rather than a reason to avoid.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($645):** The AWM mix-shift continues, wrap net inflows accelerate, and asset-management outflows turn to net inflows. Estimate revisions keep rising and the market re-rates AMP toward wealth-peer multiples (13–14x forward) as the earnings base becomes visibly more recurring and less volatile. Continued ~7% share-count reduction compounds per-share earnings. Equity markets cooperate, and the succession question resolves cleanly with continuity. At ~13x $49.85 next-year EPS plus buyback accretion, the stock reaches the high-$640s.

**Bear case ($420, ~−20%):** A market or rate downturn compresses asset-based fees, and the 10%-equity-decline sensitivity (~$369M adjusted pretax) materializes. The LTC/Genworth tail surfaces in another adverse assumption review, the succession thread proves disruptive, and the multiple re-anchors to the annuity-company cohort at ~9x on lower earnings. This roughly retests the April 2026 low near $422.

**Base case ($600, ~+14%):** Estimates continue to grind higher at mid-single-digit velocity, AWM sustains ~15–20% earnings growth, asset-management outflows keep shrinking, and buybacks retire ~7% of shares annually. The multiple expands modestly toward 12x forward as earnings quality improves. This implies ~12x next-year EPS of ~$49.85, plus buyback accretion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target: $600** — roughly 12x next-year consensus EPS of $49.85, in line with a modest re-rating toward wealth-peer multiples and consistent with the median-to-high analyst target band. Upside ~14% from $528.
- **Bull case target: $645** — ~13x forward plus faster revision velocity and asset-management flow inflection. Matches the high analyst target.
- **Bear case target: $420** — ~9x on lower earnings following a market/rate shock or tail event; retests the April 2026 low. Downside ~−20%.
- **Timeframe: 12 months**, tied to the next four earnings prints (next report following the April 23 cadence), continued buyback execution, and clarification of the succession thread.
- **Upside/downside:** +14% to base, +22% to bull, −20% to bear. Skew is roughly 1:1 to the bear but ~1.1–1.6:1 across the base/bull range, justifying disciplined rather than maximum sizing.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. AWM adjusted operating earnings growth falls below ~10% YoY or wrap net flows turn negative for two consecutive quarters.
2. Estimate revisions reverse — current-year or next-year consensus falls more than 3% over any 90-day window.
3. A material adverse LTC assumption revision or a Genworth/GLIC counterparty credit event that management cannot demonstrate is contained; or a disruptive, poorly-communicated CEO transition.

## Conclusion

Ameriprise is a financially exceptional, high-ROE, capital-return franchise whose earnings quality is improving as it shifts toward capital-light wealth management and de-risks its insurance book. The June downgrade to HOLD rested on an unresolved succession thread and concern about growth durability. The succession question remains open and rightly caps the management-quality score, but the growth concern is not supported by the data: estimates are rising across every horizon, the company beat by 9.2% last quarter and has beaten four straight, and AWM is compounding at 20% on an expanding 30% margin. The stock's recovery from $422 to $528 reflects fundamental improvement rather than multiple speculation, and it still trades below 12x forward earnings.

The risks are real and concentrated: market sensitivity, the LTC/Genworth tail, active-management outflows, and succession. Those keep this a disciplined-sizing BUY rather than a maximum-conviction one. At the current price, with rising estimates, a favorable mix-shift, and a ~7% annual share-count reduction, the risk/reward has improved relative to the June assessment. We upgrade to **BUY**, base-case target **$600** over 12 months.
