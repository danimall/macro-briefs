## Executive Summary

Fox reported a strong fiscal fourth quarter and full year on August 6. Q4 revenue was $4,212M, up $925M or 28%, with advertising up 78% on the FIFA Men's World Cup group stage; adjusted EPS of $1.79 beat the ~$1.34 consensus by roughly 34% on a like-for-like non-GAAP basis, the fourth consecutive beat and the fourth in excess of 23%. For the full year, revenue rose 5% to $17,126M, adjusted EBITDA rose 8% to $3,906M, and margin expanded from 22.2% to 22.8%. The 26% decline in reported pretax income is not operational: "non-operating other, net" swung $1,211M on the mark-to-market of the Flutter stake, and pretax income excluding that line rose 16%, from $2,623M to $3,051M.

None of that is the decision. On June 14, Fox agreed to acquire Roku for $96.00 in cash plus 0.9693 Fox Class A shares per Roku share, an externally reported ~$160/share and ~$22B enterprise value. Financing is committed: a $12.0B senior unsecured bridge plus a $1.0B term loan, with roughly $2.1-2.5B of Fox's $4.2B cash. On our arithmetic that moves net leverage from 0.62x adjusted EBITDA to roughly 3.5-3.8x, issues about 143M new Class A shares (a 34% increase in share count, equal to 25.3% ownership dilution for existing holders), and dilutes adjusted EPS from $5.42 toward roughly $3.38-3.67 before synergies — a 31-38% reduction. Fox is paying approximately 4.6x 2025 revenue and 46x 2025 free cash flow for Roku, funded roughly two-thirds with debt and one-third with its own stock trading near 6.6x EBITDA. The Class B vote required for the share issuance has already been contractually committed by LGC Holdco, which controls ~38.8% of Class B voting power and whose voting decisions rest with a managing director appointed and replaceable by the CEO. Because the consideration is Class A, family voting control is not diluted at all.

Class B fell from $58.92 on June 12 to $44.55 on June 23 (-24%) and has since recovered to $55.25 — within about 6% of its pre-announcement level. The window in which the market was paying us to underwrite deal risk has largely closed. Against a modest base-case fair value of $58, a $70 bull case and a $38 bear case, plus an unaccrued $2.7B Smartmatic claim with summary judgment argued in December 2025 and no ruling disclosed, we maintain HOLD and classify it as genuinely uncertain rather than a conviction hold. Overall score moves from 5.7 to 5.15, with the deterioration concentrated in management quality and risk factors.

## Company Overview

Fox is the narrowest large-cap portfolio in US media: no film studio, no general-entertainment SVOD, minimal international footprint. FY2026 segment results:

| Television | $9,666M | +4% | $1,438M | +52% |
| Corporate & Other | $526M | +116% | –$631M | from –$351M |

Cable holds Fox News (#1 national cable news channel in weekday primetime for 24+ consecutive years), Fox Business, FS1/FS2, Big Ten Network (~61% owned) and Fox Deportes. Television holds the broadcast network (209 affiliates, #2 in adults 18-49, and a median viewer age of 59 — the youngest of the big four against ABC 64, CBS 63, NBC 60), 29 owned stations covering 38.7% of DMA households before the UHF discount, and Tubi. Sports rights include the NFL (expanded 11-year deal with a one-time NFL termination right after the 2029 season), MLB including the exclusive World Series, Big Ten, NASCAR including the Daytona 500, and the FIFA Men's World Cup. The 10-K states the next significant renewal is the FIFA package.

Tubi streamed over 13 billion hours in FY2026, view time up 20%, at roughly 2.2% of all US TV viewing per Nielsen. Fox One, the wholly owned DTC subscription service, launched in August 2025 and is the stated cause of the $280M widening in Corporate & Other losses. **No Fox One subscriber count is disclosed anywhere in the FY2026 10-K, the Q3 10-Q, or the earnings coverage we could access.** That absence is itself informative: a product consuming $280M of incremental segment loss and no disclosed unit metric cannot be underwritten by outside investors.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement quality.** FY2026 revenue $17,126M (+5%), with distribution $8,058M (+4%), advertising $7,339M (+7%) and content/other $1,729M (+4%). Advertising absorbed the absence of Super Bowl LIX and 2024 political dollars and still grew, on World Cup, incremental NFL/MLB postseason inventory and Tubi. GAAP EPS fell to $3.84 from $4.91; adjusted EPS rose to $5.42 from $4.78. The gap is the Flutter mark — equity securities carried at fair value fell from $1,249M (June 30, 2025) to $458M (March 31, 2026) with $776M of nine-month losses, essentially all mark-to-market rather than disposals. Anyone reading the GAAP decline as operating deterioration is misreading the statement; equally, anyone treating the adjusted figure as cash is ignoring that book equity and reported tax genuinely absorbed the hit.

**Cash conversion is the real weakness.** Operating cash flow fell 41%, from $3,324M to $1,970M; capex of $502M leaves FY2026 free cash flow of $1,468M, an 8.6% FCF margin. OCF-to-adjusted-EBITDA conversion collapsed from 91.7% to 50.4%. Management attributes this to the absence of Super Bowl LIX and 2024 election receipts plus higher sports programming payments, offset by the World Cup — largely event timing and working capital rather than earnings erosion. But the structural feature underneath is real: Fox pays for sports rights on a schedule that does not match the revenue events, and firm commitments plus future debt payments stood at approximately $34B at March 31, 2026, roughly two years of revenue. We do not annualize any single Fox quarter; a normalized FCF estimate averaging FY2025 and FY2026 OCF less ~$400M capex is roughly $2.25B, and we use that as a sensitivity, not a forecast.

**Balance sheet, standalone.** Total assets $22,482M, cash ~$4.2B, long-term debt $6,606M, equity $11,628M, current ratio 3.17, undrawn $1.0B revolver to June 2028, Moody's Baa2 / S&P BBB. Debt-to-equity is 0.57x and net leverage 0.62x. Two screening artifacts are worth correcting because they mislead in opposite directions: a reported "D/E of 1.93" is the assets-to-equity multiplier ($22,482/$11,628), not leverage; and a reported Altman EBIT/assets input of –0.007 reflects the fact that Fox's income statement presents no GAAP operating income subtotal — using pretax plus net interest ($2,278M + $274M = $2,552M) gives 11.35% and lifts the Z-score from 2.08 to roughly 2.48. Standalone, Fox's near-term solvency risk is remote. The screen becomes an accurate description only pro forma.

**Capital allocation.** Fox retired roughly 31M shares for ~$1.9B in the first nine months of FY2026 at an average near $61, including a $1.5B October 2025 accelerated repurchase at $65.51 Class A / $58.83 Class B. Share count fell from 446.3M to 420.8M, a 5.7% reduction in nine months. Total FY2026 capital returned was approximately $2.3B; FY2027 dividends are expected near $245M, implying $0.58/share and a 1.05% yield at $55.25. The Roku transaction issues ~143M shares, reversing roughly six years of buyback-driven share reduction in one step. We draw no inference about intent — the Roku opportunity may simply have arisen after the repurchases — but on a mark-to-market basis the sequencing is unfavorable, and the two policies are in direct tension.

## Growth Analysis

**FY2027 has an identifiable, dateable revenue setup.** The 2026 World Cup ran June 11 to July 19, so the knockout rounds and final fall in fiscal Q1 FY2027 (September 2026 quarter). November 2026 midterm political advertising lands in fiscal Q2 and should reverse the FY2026 drag from the absence of election dollars in the owned station group. Fox One launch costs that drove Corporate & Other to –$631M anniversary during the year. Offsetting: no Super Bowl in FY2027 (Fox carried LIX in February 2025).

**The secular core is shrinking measurably.** Nielsen-estimated subscribers as of June 30: Fox News 55M from 61M (-9.8%), Fox Business 52M from 60M (-13.3%), FS1 55M from 61M (-9.8%), FS2 39M from 44M (-11.4%), Big Ten Network 38M from 42M (-9.5%), Fox Deportes 7M from 10M (-30%). Distribution revenue nevertheless grew 4%, because +$440M of rate and affiliate-fee gains outran a –$160M volume drag. Note the tension: a ~10% decline in point-in-time households produced only a ~2% revenue headwind on a $7,780M base. Candidate explanations include average (flow-weighted) subscribers falling far less than year-end counts, growth in virtual-MVPD and non-owned affiliate fees outside the cable-network count, and Fox One subscription revenue now sitting in Corporate & Other. We cannot decompose it from the disclosure — Fox reports neither average subscribers nor revenue per subscriber — and we flag it as an open question rather than a resolved one. What is not ambiguous is the arithmetic constraint: each year of ~10% volume decline requires a larger percentage rate increase to hold revenue flat, against MVPDs facing their own subscriber losses.

**Tubi is genuine scale at free-tier economics.** 13 billion hours and ~2.2% of US TV viewing recapture part of the household loss, but at ad-supported rates rather than $2-3/month affiliate fees. Tubi delivered its most-streamed and highest-revenue quarter ever in Q4.

**Roku is the growth argument, and it is credible on its own numbers.** Revenue went from $3,126.5M (2022) to $4,737.3M (2025), a 14.8% CAGR, with 2025 up 15.2%. Operating income improved from –$792.4M (2023) to –$218.2M (2024) to –$5.6M (2025), with 2025 net income of +$88.4M, operating cash flow of $483.7M against capex of just $5.3M, and $1,587.1M of cash. Capital intensity that low is the genuine bull-case ingredient. The problem is price and per-share arithmetic, addressed next.

Growth prospects score 5.5: enterprise-level growth is positive and has dateable FY2027 catalysts, but per-share growth is sharply negative near term because of the dilution, and the linear base is contracting ~10% annually.

## Valuation Assessment

The supplied profile fields are internally inconsistent and should not be used: trailing and forward P/E are both listed at 13.65 against EPS of $4.91, which implies a price near $67 — the FY2025 GAAP EPS against a stale price. We recompute.

Class B: 221.15M shares × $55.25 = $12.22B, matching the supplied market cap. Class A: 199.63M shares; the peer table implies FOXA market capitalization near $11B, or roughly $55/share, meaning the historical 11-13% Class A premium visible in the October 2025 and February/March 2026 repurchase prints has compressed to approximate parity — consistent with 143M new Class A shares in prospect. Total equity value is therefore roughly $23.2B; adding $6,606M debt and subtracting ~$4.2B cash gives EV of roughly $25.6B.

| Metric | FOX | Peer median |
|---|---|---|
| EV / FY2026 adj. EBITDA ($3,906M) | ~6.6x | n/a |
| P / FY2026 adjusted EPS ($5.42) | 10.2x | — |
| P / FY2026 GAAP EPS ($3.84) | 14.4x | 17.9x |
| Dividend yield | 1.05% | 2.5% |

On standalone metrics this is cheap: FOXA at 17.9x, NXST at 36.6x, TGNA at 16.3x, WBD negative. But cheapness in secular-decline media has been a persistent trap, and here there are two specific reasons the discount is not obviously an opportunity.

First, the forward multiple that will matter is the pro forma one. Our illustrative sketch: FY2026 adjusted net income to Fox of $2,380M plus Roku's 2025 GAAP net income of $88.4M, less incremental after-tax interest on ~$12B at an assumed 5.5% (~$500M after 24% tax), gives ~$1,968M on ~582M implied weighted shares, or roughly $3.38 pro forma adjusted EPS against $5.42 standalone (-38%). On a cash basis, normalized FCF of $2,250M plus Roku's $478M less $660M pretax interest gives $2,068M on 564M shares, or $3.67 versus $5.34 (-31%). The sketch excludes synergies, purchase-accounting amortization (which worsens GAAP), Roku's continued growth, and any refinancing at different rates; the 5.5% cost of debt is our assumption, not disclosed. At $55.25, the stock is therefore roughly 15-16x pro forma adjusted EPS on a balance sheet at 3.5-3.8x net leverage — not a value multiple.

Second, a sum-of-parts frame shows the deal's value impact directly. Each $1B by which Roku is worth less than the ~$22B paid costs Fox equity roughly $1.77/share across 564M pro forma shares. Fox is paying approximately 4.6x 2025 revenue and 46x 2025 free cash flow, funded with debt and with equity valued near 6.6x EBITDA. The 24% peak-to-trough decline in June was broadly consistent with the market assuming full overpayment plus leverage and execution risk. The recovery to $55.25 implies the market now credits meaningful synergies or Roku growth, or is simply re-rating on the FY2026 print and FY2027 setup.

A third item cuts against the historical sum-of-parts bull case. Fox holds ~4.3M Flutter shares (~2.5% of Flutter) and a call option, expiring December 2030, on 18.6% of FanDuel at a strike of approximately $4.7B. If the $458M equity-securities mark is predominantly Flutter, implied Flutter equity value is roughly $18.3B, and FanDuel alone would need to be worth ~$25.3B for the option to be at the money — more than the implied value of all of Flutter. The FanDuel option, long treated as embedded value, appears deeply out of the money. This derivation relies on the mark being predominantly Flutter and on an unverified Flutter share count, and sports-betting equities can recover before 2030, so treat it as directional rather than settled.

Valuation score 5.5. The absolute multiple is low, but the identified reason for it (dilution, leverage, litigation, secular decay) is substantive rather than sentiment, and the pro forma multiple is unremarkable.

## Competitive Landscape

Fox owns the two content categories least substitutable by on-demand streaming — live news and live sports — with no legacy exposure to the film studios and general-entertainment SVOD services that have destroyed value across the sector. The 22.8% adjusted EBITDA margin and 6.6x EV/EBITDA reflect a market treating those assets as high quality and terminally declining.

Trajectory is mixed. Deteriorating: 9-13% annual household loss across the cable networks; Fox One economics entirely negative so far with no unit disclosure; escalating sports costs against a shrinking base, with the NFL holding a termination right after the 2029 season (a cost risk as much as a rights risk, since a reopening would likely reprice higher); and the FCC's November 2025 Public Notice examining network-affiliate relationships, which bears directly on the "higher fees from Fox Network affiliates" that MD&A credits within the $440M distribution tailwind. Improving: Tubi at 2.2% of US TV viewing with a majority cord-cutter/cord-never audience; the youngest median viewer age among the big four networks; two additional NFL windows for 2026.

The August 6 FCC vote (2-1) eliminating the 39% national ownership cap in favor of case-by-case review cuts both ways for Fox, which is under-appreciated in the coverage. As an owner of 29 stations in 15 of the top 16 markets, pressed against the old cap at 38.7% reach, Fox gains real M&A optionality. As the owner of a network collecting reverse compensation from 209 affiliates, Fox faces a consolidation wave that produces fewer, larger affiliate counterparties with more bargaining power. Fox is also constrained: the dual network rule still bars acquiring ABC, CBS or NBC, and with $14B+ of cash-and-debt consideration committed to Roku, capacity to participate as a buyer is limited. Variety reports legal challenges to the repeal are expected; none is confirmed filed.

Roku would move Fox from supplying content to third-party platforms to owning the largest US smart-TV operating system, with the home screen, first-party data and advertising inventory across more than 100 million global streaming households. That is a genuine structural repositioning. It also places Fox in direct platform-layer competition with Amazon Fire TV, Google TV, Samsung Tizen, LG webOS and Vizio (Walmart-owned) — for all of which the TV operating system is a strategic loss leader inside a far larger balance sheet.

Competitive position 5.5: the franchise is strong in level terms, the trajectory is genuinely two-sided, and the Roku repositioning is unproven.

## Risk Assessment

**Smartmatic — $2.7B, unaccrued.** Filed February 2021; summary judgment motions argued December 2, 2025; no trial date set, with the 10-K stating trial is not expected before "later in 2026 at the earliest." Fox records no accrual, characterizing loss as neither probable nor reasonably estimable. Fox paid roughly $800M in April 2023 to settle Dominion and a related suit. A $2.7B claim against ~$23B of total equity value, layered on ~$18.6B of pro forma debt, is a material tail. We could not determine whether the court has ruled.

**Delaware derivative litigation.** *In re Fox Corporation Derivative Litigation* (Del. Ch. 2023-0418) survived a motion to dismiss on December 27, 2024; on April 28, 2025 the court granted defendants leave to move for summary judgment on director independence, with limited discovery ongoing. Board oversight at a controlled company is under judicial examination.

**Deal and financing risk.** Termination fees are asymmetric: ~$866M either way for specified circumstances, ~$1.2B payable by Fox for regulatory failure or a permanent antitrust restraint, up to $70M to Roku if Fox fails to obtain Class B approval for the issuance. One reading of the $1.2B versus $866M differential is that both parties saw non-trivial vertical-foreclosure risk in combining the largest US smart-TV OS with a major content owner and a leading FAST service. An equally plausible reading is that Roku, as seller under a fixed exchange ratio and long timeline, simply had the leverage to demand deal certainty. We would not infer either party's assessment from the fee structure alone. Separately, the exchange ratio is fixed with no collar: Fox has no protection if Class A falls, and it has. No S-4 has been filed, so every pro forma figure here — including the cash consideration, the exact share issuance, purchase accounting and stated synergies — is an estimate.

**Ratings.** The 10-K explicitly flags possible negative ratings actions from the incremental debt. A Baa2/BBB issuer moving to ~3.5x+ net leverage is a plausible one-notch-or-more downgrade candidate. The counterargument is that Roku's ~$478M of 2025 free cash flow on $5.3M of capex is genuinely deleveraging-friendly and agencies may look through to a defined path — but no deleveraging target or timeline was captured from the earnings call, which we could not retrieve.

**Governance.** Only Class B approval is required for a Class A issuance, and LGC Holdco plus Cruden 2 have already agreed with Roku to vote ~38.8% of Class B power in favor, with voting decisions resting solely with a managing director appointed and replaceable by the CEO. A 38.8% commitment is not a majority and other Class B holders still matter, and voting-support agreements from controlling holders are entirely standard. The substantive point stands: public shareholders bear ~25% ownership dilution while family voting control is untouched, because the class being issued is the one that does not vote on this.

**Insider activity.** Uniformly monetization at prices well above today's: Lachlan Murdoch exercised 952,384 options and sold ~1.12M Class A shares at $56.65-$60.63 across March 11-13, 2026 (>$65M gross); John Nallen ~$26.5M in the same window; Steven Tomsic ~$9.2M at $66.54 in November 2025; Adam Ciongoli ~$2.08M at $69.55; K. Rupert Murdoch sold 168,756 Class A at $65.97 (~$11.1M) on November 7, 2025 and 100,000 Class B at $59.26 (~$5.9M) on November 21, 2025 (~$17M combined across November 2025); director Paul Ryan sold to zero direct shares at $64.36. Lachlan Murdoch's March 13 matched sale-and-purchase of 175,372 shares at an identical $60.63 is consistent with a transfer between holding vehicles and should not be read as an open-market buy. No open-market purchases by any executive or director appear at post-announcement prices. Standing alone, insider selling into rising estimates is low signal — but here it is clustered across four C-suite executives plus the chairman emeritus and a director within roughly five months, immediately preceding a materially dilutive transaction, with zero offsetting buys after a 24% decline. That combination carries more weight than routine diversification, and it is the primary reason management quality scores 4.2.

**Other.** FCC C-Band reallocation (July 2026 Order requiring 160 of 200 MHz auctioned in 2027, transition by December 2030/June 2031) reduces satellite transmission capacity. FCC license impairments were ~$64M in FY2026 and ~$70M in FY2025, with disclosed sensitivities showing aggregate FCC license fair value ~$125M below carrying value on a 0.5pp higher discount rate and fair value exceeding carrying value by less than 10% in certain markets. Note that the $6.1B of disclosed performance obligations is not total backlog — it excludes contracts of one year or less, usage-based royalties and right-to-invoice arrangements — and that the 10-Q's "no customer above 10% of receivables" disclosure establishes receivables concentration only; distribution revenue remains concentrated among a handful of MVPDs and virtual MVPDs, and the mix shift toward Fox One and Tubi should not be read as reduced counterparty concentration.

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 50.0% against 33.8% one-year realized, a 16.2-point premium at the 84th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (50.0% at 14 days versus 33.0% at six months) and an ±8.1% implied move on the August 21 expiry. Positioning is decisively call-tilted: put/call volume 0.07, put/call open interest 0.17, and the supplied skew reading is characterized as call-heavy. Elevated near-dated volatility with no put demand also means downside protection is not being bid, so a bad headline is not pre-hedged. This does not change our view, but it argues against paying up here and in favor of waiting for the near-dated event to resolve.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($70, +27%).** The deal closes in 2027; Roku's 15% revenue growth continues on 43.8% gross margin and near-zero capital intensity; cross-platform synergies from placing Fox News, Fox Sports, Tubi and Fox One on the largest US smart-TV OS with first-party household data deliver $300-500M annualized; leverage falls back below 2.5x by FY2029 on combined free cash flow; FY2027 delivers World Cup knockouts plus midterm political advertising with Fox One launch costs anniversaried; Smartmatic resolves at or below the ~$800M Dominion precedent; and the FCC cap repeal lets Fox add stations at accretive multiples. Pro forma adjusted EPS reaches $4.75-5.00 by FY2028 and the market pays 14-15x. Rothschild Redburn's $71 Class A target sits in this scenario.

**Bear case ($38, -31%).** Roku's growth decelerates as Amazon, Google and Vizio subsidize the OS layer; purchase-accounting amortization plus ~$660M of pretax interest hold pro forma adjusted EPS near $3.20; the ratings agencies downgrade and the buyback is suspended during deleveraging, removing the mechanism that shrank share count 5.7% in nine months; cable household decline accelerates past 12% and the rate-versus-volume spread turns negative; Smartmatic produces a multi-hundred-million to >$1B cash outflow against zero accrual. Eleven times $3.20 is $35; add partial litigation recovery and $38 is the landing zone.

**Base case ($58, +5%).** FY2027 standalone adjusted EPS of roughly $5.60-5.80 (World Cup knockouts and midterms offsetting the absent Super Bowl) at 10.5x is $59-61 pre-close; post-close pro forma adjusted EPS of $3.40-3.70 growing with Roku's top line at 15-16x is $52-59. Blending the two, weighted toward closure given committed financing, break fees and a pre-committed Class B vote, gives $58.

The asymmetry is the problem: +5% to base, +27% to bull, -31% to bear, on a 1.05% dividend yield. In June, when Class B traded at $44.55, that distribution was attractive. At $55.25 it is not.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** We classify this as uncertain rather than a growth or income hold. The two dominant variables — Roku's closing terms and realized synergies, and the Smartmatic outcome — are unresolved binaries whose disclosure requirements (an S-4 with audited pro formas; a summary judgment ruling) have not been met. The 1.05% yield does not support an income thesis, and base-case upside of 5% does not support a growth thesis.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **S-4 pro formas showing quantified synergies of $400M+ with a stated deleveraging path to below 2.5x net leverage within 24 months of close**, alongside confirmation of bridge/term-loan pricing at or below 5.5%. That combination would move pro forma adjusted EPS toward $4.00+ and make 14x defensible against the current price.
2. **A Smartmatic resolution at or below ~$800M**, or a summary judgment ruling substantially narrowing the claim. This alone would remove the largest unpriced tail.
3. **Price near $45-47** with fundamentals intact — roughly 8.3-8.7x standalone adjusted EPS and about 5.5x EV/EBITDA, which restores the June risk/reward.
4. **Disclosure of Fox One subscribers at a level that makes the –$631M Corporate & Other loss a customer-acquisition investment rather than an open-ended subsidy**, combined with a narrowing of that segment loss.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **A ratings downgrade below investment grade, or suspension of the dividend or buyback** to fund deleveraging.
2. **An adverse Smartmatic development** — a summary judgment denial that clears the full $2.7B claim for trial, or an accrual recorded above ~$1B.
3. **Distribution revenue turning negative year over year**, i.e., the rate/volume spread inverting, or point-in-time cable household declines accelerating beyond 13%.
4. **Roku revenue growth decelerating below 8%** in the quarters between signing and close, which would invalidate the growth half of the deal rationale while the leverage half is already committed.
5. **A second request or extended antitrust review that materially delays close** while the $12B bridge accrues commitment cost, or a termination triggering the $1.2B regulatory fee (~$2.85/share on the pre-deal 420.8M share count).

**Reassessment timeline.** Three near-dated checkpoints: the August 21 options expiry that current backwardation is pricing (implied ±8.1%); the S-4 filing, which is the first document containing audited pro formas and stated synergies; and the fiscal Q1 FY2027 report in early November 2026, which will show World Cup knockout economics, the first post-launch year of Fox One, and any updated Smartmatic disclosure.

## Conclusion

Fox is executing well on the assets it has. Four consecutive earnings beats averaging above 30%, adjusted EBITDA up 8% with margin expansion, a Q4 with 28% revenue growth and record Tubi engagement, and 16% growth in pretax income excluding the Flutter mark all argue that the operating business is healthier than the GAAP headline or the -19% distance from the 52-week high implies. Standalone, at 6.6x EV/EBITDA and 10.2x adjusted EPS with 0.62x net leverage and an investment-grade rating, this would be a straightforward value holding with real FY2027 catalysts.

The Roku acquisition changes what the equity is. Fox is paying roughly 4.6x revenue and 46x free cash flow for a platform asset, funding it with debt and stock valued at 6.6x, taking net leverage from 0.62x to roughly 3.5-3.8x, diluting per-share earnings 31-38% before synergies, and doing so under a vote that the controlling holder has already committed while the class being issued carries no say in the outcome. The strategic logic — owning the operating system layer rather than renting it — is sound, and Roku's inflection from -$792M of operating losses in 2023 to $88M of net income and $478M of free cash flow in 2025 is genuine. But the price paid transfers value to Roku holders on day one, the offset depends on synergies that have not been quantified in any filed document, and the sequencing against ~$1.9B of buybacks at ~$61 is unfavorable.
