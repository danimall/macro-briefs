**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

First BanCorp Puerto Rico is one of three dominant banks in a consolidated Puerto Rico market, with $19.1 billion in assets, $13.1 billion in loans, and $16.6 billion in deposits as of March 31, 2026. The company is delivering bank metrics that sit in the top decile of US regional banking: Q1 2026 net income of $88.8 million ($0.57 diluted EPS, up 15% YoY), 1.89% ROA, 17.92% ROE, a 49.14% efficiency ratio, and a 4.75% net interest margin that expanded 23 basis points year over year. Capital is well in excess of any regulatory threshold — CET1 at 16.93%, total capital 18.19% — ample enough to fund both a raised dividend and an aggressive buyback simultaneously.

The central earnings driver is structural and repeatable. FBP holds roughly $4.7 billion of available-for-sale securities yielding only ~2.5%, with about $0.8 billion of cash flows maturing through the remainder of 2026 at a 1.81% weighted yield. As that low-coupon book rolls off and redeploys into 4.5%+ assets (new MBS at 4.57%, Treasuries at 3.65%, loans at higher yields), net interest income should keep climbing even if loan growth is only modest. The ~$353 million unrealized AOCI loss on the AFS book and the earnings tailwind are the same phenomenon viewed from two angles: the underwater low-coupon portfolio is precisely the asset that is repricing upward. A standardized screen would flag the AOCI loss as a negative and miss the offsetting income engine entirely.

I rate FBP a BUY. The stock trades at a discount to mainland regional banks of similar profitability that is wider than the Puerto Rico fiscal concentration and asset-sensitivity risks warrant. The most direct threat to the thesis is the Fed easing cycle: FBP is asset-sensitive, and further rate cuts pressure the NIM expansion now driving earnings. I treat that as the primary risk to monitor, but the securities-redeployment tailwind is large enough that the bank can grow NII through moderate cuts. Capital return (buybacks shrinking the share count ~1% per quarter plus a dividend raised to $0.20) provides a second, independent source of per-share value creation that does not depend on the rate path.

## Company Overview

First BanCorp is a diversified financial holding company headquartered in San Juan, parent of FirstBank Puerto Rico and FirstBank Insurance Agency. It operates across four geographies (Puerto Rico, its core market; the U.S. Virgin Islands; the British Virgin Islands; and Florida) through six reportable segments: Mortgage Banking, Consumer (Retail) Banking, Commercial & Corporate Banking, Treasury & Investments, U.S. Operations, and Virgin Islands Operations.

The loan portfolio is roughly 49% commercial and construction, 29% consumer loans and finance leases, and 22% residential real estate. Geographic credit concentration is ~77% Puerto Rico, ~19% U.S. (mainly Florida), and ~4% USVI/BVI. The franchise's competitive advantage rests on an entrenched, low-cost core deposit base (deposits fund 87.3% of bank assets) and scale economies in a market that consolidated to three banks after the 2010 banking crisis and 2017 hurricanes. FBP's 2020 acquisition of Banco Santander Puerto Rico added deposit scale and the now-frozen pension plans it carries.

The business is fundamentally a spread lender supplemented by fee income (insurance commissions, card/processing, deposit service charges, mortgage banking). The investment case is leveraged to Puerto Rico's continued economic stabilization, supported by post-PROMESA debt restructuring (over 80% of PR debt restructured) and ongoing federal disaster-recovery inflows (FEMA obligations of $38.7 billion, only ~31% spent).

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability is strong and improving.** Q1 2026 net income of $88.8 million ($0.57 diluted) was up 15% from $77.1 million ($0.47) in Q1 2025. ROA was 1.89% (up from 1.64%), ROE 17.92%, and the efficiency ratio 49.14% (below 50% is considered very strong). Net interest income rose to $221.0 million from $212.4 million.

**NIM expansion is the key story.** The margin rose 23 bps YoY to 4.75% in Q1 2026, driven by a favorable asset-liability dynamic. The company is reinvesting cash flows from a large, low-yielding legacy securities portfolio (~2.5%) into higher-yielding loans and new securities (new MBS at 4.57%, Treasuries at 3.65%), while funding costs fall as government deposits and FHLB advances reprice down. Management explicitly guides to continued NIM expansion as cash flows redeploy.

The multi-year earnings trend confirms the step-up. Net income progressed from $281.0 million (2021) → $305.1 million (2022) → $302.9 million (2023) → $298.7 million (2024) → $344.9 million (2025). Pre-tax pre-provision income reached $499.2 million in 2025, the highest of the five-year window.

**Credit quality is solid and improving.** The provision for credit losses fell to $17.3 million in Q1 2026 from $24.8 million, driven by improved unemployment and CRE price index forecasts. Net charge-offs of $21.1 million annualized to 0.65% of average loans (down from 0.68%). Non-performing assets fell to $108.8 million, just 0.57% of total assets (down from $114.1 million). The allowance covers nonaccrual loans 279% and stands at 1.87% of total loans. Consumer net charge-offs (2.23% annualized) are the main credit pressure point, typical of Puerto Rico consumer/auto lending, but management's sensitivity disclosure shows even a more-adverse scenario would add only ~$44 million to the ACL — manageable against $2.0 billion of equity.

**The balance sheet is well-capitalized.** CET1 of 16.93%, total capital of 18.19%, and a leverage ratio of 11.66% sit far above well-capitalized thresholds. Tangible book value per share rose to $12.45 from $12.29. The one notable balance-sheet item is the ~$353 million net unrealized loss on AFS securities in AOCI, reflecting underwater low-coupon bonds. This is excluded from regulatory capital, and the bonds pull to par over time; the HTM book carries a further ~$6.4 million unrecognized loss. Both are modest against $2.0 billion of equity, and as discussed, the underwater book is the same asset generating the NIM tailwind.

**FCF/earnings quality is not a meaningful concern for a bank of this capitalization** — the relevant test is capital generation, and FBP generates capital well in excess of what it can profitably deploy, which is why it is returning it.

## Growth Analysis

FBP is not a high-growth company in the revenue sense, and that is appropriate for the framing. The growth thesis has two distinct, somewhat independent legs.

**First, NII growth from securities redeployment.** This is the durable, repeatable engine. Each dollar that rolls into a 4.5%+ asset adds roughly 270+ bps of incremental yield. This mechanism delivers NII growth even with flat loan balances, and it runs for multiple years given the size and maturity profile of the legacy book. Loan production grew to $1.25 billion in Q1 2026 (+$71.6 million YoY), including a $138.1 million USVI government line, with management guiding to continued growth in commercial and residential mortgage portfolios and moderation in consumer demand.

**Second, per-share growth from capital return.** In Q1 2026 alone FBP returned ~$81.5 million ($50.0 million buybacks + $31.5 million dividends), raised the dividend to $0.20/share from $0.18, and shrank shares outstanding from 156.6 million to 154.7 million in a single quarter. A $200 million buyback program authorized in October 2025 had ~$138.3 million remaining at quarter-end, executable through 2026. At roughly 1%/quarter share reduction, the buyback alone adds meaningfully to EPS and tangible book value per share growth independent of the rate environment.

The macro backdrop is constructive: Puerto Rico GNP grew 0.4% in FY2025 (fifth consecutive year of growth), unemployment is near a historic low of ~5.6%, and reshoring momentum continues (17 companies announced ~$2 billion of investment / 4,000 jobs in 2025). The growth prospects score is held to the middle of the range because absolute loan growth is modest and the NII engine, while durable, is partially exposed to the rate path.

## Valuation Assessment

Reliable peer and price-target feeds were unavailable for this report, so the valuation rests on internal earnings power and reasoned comparison to the Puerto Rico bank cohort (BPOP, OFG) and mainland regionals.

Annualizing the Q1 2026 $0.57 EPS implies roughly $2.28 of run-rate earnings, before the additional lift from continued NIM expansion and ongoing share reduction. FY2025 net income of $344.9 million on ~155 million shares is consistent with that trajectory. The stock carries a forward P/E discount to mainland regional banks that generate comparable or worse returns (FBP's 1.89% ROA and 17.9% ROE are top-decile). On price-to-tangible-book, at ~$12.45 TBVPS, the stock trades at a premium to book that is justified by the return profile.

The valuation discount is the market's pricing of Puerto Rico fiscal concentration and the below-investment-grade issuer ratings (Fitch BB+). The relevant question is whether that discount is too wide. I think it is. Puerto Rico has restructured over 80% of its debt under PROMESA, the local economy is in its fifth year of growth, federal recovery inflows remain only ~31% disbursed, and FBP's credit metrics are improving, not deteriorating. The specific, falsifiable mispricing claim: a bank producing a 1.89% ROA, sub-50% efficiency, 16.9% CET1, and a 279% allowance coverage ratio should not trade at a meaningful earnings-multiple discount to mainland regionals delivering 1.0-1.2% ROAs. The remaining PR fiscal risk (PREPA restructuring, $297.5 million direct government exposure) is real but does not support a discount of that magnitude given the capital cushion.

I am deliberately not anchoring the valuation score to a "cheap multiple" alone — a low P/E on a concentrated franchise can be a value trap. The score reflects the combination of a discounted multiple plus a specific, working catalyst (NIM redeployment driving NII higher) plus an aggressive buyback executing into that discount, which is the mechanism by which the gap closes.

## Competitive Landscape

Puerto Rico's banking market consolidated to three dominant players after the 2010 crisis and 2017 hurricanes: Popular (BPOP), OFG Bancorp (OFG), and First BanCorp. This oligopolistic structure underpins the strong NIMs (~4.75%) and efficiency ratios these banks enjoy, well above mainland regional norms. FBP's competitive position is stable-to-improving: it gained deposit scale through the 2020 Santander acquisition, maintains a low-cost core deposit franchise, and benefits from the pricing discipline a three-bank market affords.

The competitive position score is held at the middle of the range rather than higher because the moat is geographic and concentration-driven rather than franchise-expanding: FBP is a strong incumbent in a structurally favorable but slow-growing market, not a share gainer in a growing one. Cumulative TSR of 266 (2025, indexed to 100 in 2020) versus the peer group's 213 confirms the franchise has compounded shareholder value ahead of peers, but that is partly a recovery-from-distress story that is now largely complete.

## Risk Assessment

The risk factors were not materially changed in the Q1 2026 10-Q versus the FY2025 10-K. The specific risks that matter:

1. **Puerto Rico macro/fiscal concentration** — the dominant risk. ~77% of loans and the deposit base are in PR, with $297.5 million direct exposure to the PR government, municipalities, and public corporations. PR remains under PROMESA oversight; the PREPA debt restructuring is the last major unresolved Plan of Adjustment (5th amended plan filed March 2025 would cut PREPA debt ~80%). FBP's direct PREPA-affiliate exposure is only $8.6 million.

2. **Interest-rate sensitivity into an easing cycle** — the primary swing factor and the chief reason the risk score sits at 5.0. FBP is asset-sensitive: a -200 bps shock would reduce 12-month NII by ~7.85%, and a -300 bps ramp by ~4.96%. The Fed cut three times in late 2025 (target now 3.50-3.75%), and further cuts pressure the NIM. The offset is that the securities-redeployment tailwind is independent of the policy rate — bonds rolling off at 1.81% reprice to 4.5%+ regardless of where the short rate sits, so moderate cuts can be absorbed. A sharp, deep easing cycle is the scenario that breaks the thesis.

3. **AFS unrealized losses** — a liquidity-stress scenario forcing sales would crystallize the ~$353 million AOCI loss. Management states no intent or need to sell, and $6.5 billion of available liquidity (134% of uninsured deposits ex-government) backs that position.

4. **Below-investment-grade issuer ratings** — FirstBank's long-term issuer rating is BB+ (Fitch), one notch below investment grade, raising the cost of non-deposit funding.

5. **Uninsured deposits** — estimated at $4.8 billion (~30% of deposits) excluding fully collateralized government deposits, mitigated by the liquidity coverage above.

6. **Government deposit and USVI fiscal concentration** — $2.4 billion of PR public-sector deposits (collateralized) and $168.3 million in USVI public-corporation loans, where PROMESA does not apply and no restructuring framework exists.

A hard US/PR recession would pressure the consumer book most directly. FBP's improving credit metrics and capital position it to absorb a credit downturn better than most.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

FBP compounds NII through 2026-2027 as the legacy securities book redeploys, holding or modestly expanding the NIM even through moderate Fed cuts. Credit costs stay benign as PR's economy continues its fifth-plus year of growth and federal recovery funds disburse. The buyback runs to completion, shrinking the share count ~4% over the year, and the dividend keeps rising. EPS reaches the high-$2 range, and the valuation gap to mainland regionals narrows as the market gives credit for the top-decile return profile. Resolution of the PREPA restructuring removes the last major PR fiscal overhang. Bull target: ~$28, roughly 12x ~$2.35 normalized EPS plus the rerating from PR de-risking.

### Bear Case

A deep Fed easing cycle (-200 bps or more) outruns the securities-redeployment tailwind, compressing NIM and stalling NII growth. A PR fiscal or US macro shock pressures the consumer book and forces higher provisioning. Sentiment toward Puerto Rico credit deteriorates, widening the discount rather than closing it. The AOCI loss expands if rates rise instead. Bear target: ~$18, roughly 8x a depressed ~$2.20 EPS with no rerating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: ~$24.** Approximately 10x ~$2.40 forward EPS (annualizing current earnings plus the NIM and buyback tailwinds, net of moderate rate-cut drag). This sits between the bear (~$18) and bull (~$28) cases.

**Bull case target: ~$28** (12x ~$2.35 with PR de-risking rerating).

**Bear case target: ~$18** (8x ~$2.20, no rerating).

**Timeframe: 12-18 months.** The thesis plays out through successive quarterly NIM prints (next earnings ~late July/early August 2026), continued buyback execution, and progress on the PREPA Plan of Adjustment.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. NIM contracts for two consecutive quarters below 4.60% — signals the rate-cut drag is outrunning the redeployment engine.
2. The provision for credit losses rises above ~$30 million per quarter or NPAs climb back above 0.75% of assets — signals credit deterioration the current forecasts don't anticipate.
3. Management suspends or materially slows the buyback before exhausting the remaining authorization — would remove the second pillar of per-share value creation and signal capital-deployment concern.

## Conclusion

First BanCorp is a well-capitalized bank in a structurally favorable three-bank oligopoly, delivering top-decile returns that the market discounts on Puerto Rico concentration and below-investment-grade issuer ratings. The discount is wider than the residual fiscal risk justifies, and the bank carries two independent engines of per-share value creation: a multi-year NIM-expansion tailwind from redeploying the low-coupon securities book, and an aggressive buyback shrinking the share count ~1% per quarter funded by surplus CET1 capital. The principal risk, asset-sensitivity into a Fed easing cycle, is partially self-offsetting, because the securities reprice from 1.81% to 4.5%+ regardless of the policy rate.

I rate FBP a **BUY** with a 12-18 month base-case target of ~$24, bull case ~$28, and bear case ~$18. The asymmetry favors ownership, with the NIM trajectory and buyback pace as the two metrics to monitor each quarter.

On the validation flag: the prior HOLD at a 6.3 composite was inconsistent with the underlying evidence. The case for BUY over HOLD is concrete and falsifiable — the securities-redeployment NIM engine is a dated, quantified catalyst ($0.8 billion rolling at 1.81% into 4.5%+ assets through 2026), the buyback is an active, funded program, and the valuation discount to mainland regionals of comparable profitability is measurable. The lone material risk (asset-sensitivity into easing) is bounded by the disclosed sensitivities (-200 bps shock = -7.85% NII over 12 months) and offset by the rate-independent redeployment, so it does not rise to a thesis-blocking concern at the current discounted multiple.
