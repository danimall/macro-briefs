**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Stryker is one of the highest-quality franchises in medical technology, compounding revenue from $18.4B in 2022 to $25.1B in 2025 (~11% CAGR) with expanding margins, high free-cash-flow conversion, and a genuine competitive moat in orthopaedic robotics (Mako). The current investment debate is narrow and specific: a material cybersecurity incident on March 11, 2026 cut Q1 2026 organic growth from a ~10% run-rate to +1.0% constant currency, compressed gross margin ~200bps on idle production, and pushed adjusted EPS down 8.5% year-over-year to $2.60 (a 13.7% miss versus the $3.01 consensus). The stock has fallen ~21% from its July 2025 high of $404.87 to $319.87, and the question is whether the disruption is a one-quarter air pocket or the start of lingering data-integrity, customer-trust, and remediation costs.

The evidence favors the transitory interpretation but does not yet confirm it. Management reports manufacturing was restored shortly after the attack and commercial/ordering/distribution systems are back online; the sell-side treats Q1 as a one-off (Leerink maintained Outperform, trimming its target to $407); and consensus forward estimates are essentially unchanged (current-year $14.98, next-year $16.72), which is the single most important signal here — analysts who follow the company closely have not marked down the earnings power. On unchanged forward estimates, the price decline is improving risk/reward rather than reflecting fundamental deterioration. The counterweight is that the internal investigation was still open as of the Q1 10-Q, so the full cost and any Q2 spillover are not yet quantified.

We rate SYK a HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 6.3. This continues a declining trajectory (7.2 in February, 7.0 in May, 6.2 in June) and reflects a genuine tension: the business quality and Mako flywheel are intact and the valuation has compressed to ~22x forward earnings (a discount to its own history and to premium peers), but three specific overhangs — the unresolved cyber investigation, the thin 12% goodwill cushion on the $3.2B Inari-related peripheral-vascular unit, and the absence of any open-market insider buying — keep this a balanced setup rather than an asymmetric buy. The Q2 print is the decisive catalyst.

## Company Overview

Stryker (incorporated in Michigan in 1946) is one of the largest pure-play medical technology companies globally, reaching ~150 million patients annually across ~61 countries with roughly 56,000 employees. It operates two reportable segments:

- **MedSurg & Neurotechnology** — 62% of 2025 sales ($15,647M, +15.7% YoY). Includes surgical instruments/navigation, Endoscopy (the 4K 1788 camera platform), Medical (LIFEPAK defibrillators, Vocera clinical communication, Sage), Vascular (stroke, venous thromboembolism, supercharged by the Inari acquisition), and Neuro Cranial.
- **Orthopaedics** — 38% of 2025 sales ($9,469M, +4.3% YoY). Includes Knees, Hips, Trauma & Extremities, and the flagship **Mako SmartRobotics** platform, which has surpassed 2 million cumulative robotic procedures across 45+ countries, with a new Mako 4 platform, Mako Shoulder, and a first-to-market robotic revision hip launched in 2025.

The moat rests on the Mako installed base (a razor/razor-blade dynamic where the robot drives implant pull-through and creates surgeon switching costs), roughly 5,600 US and 9,000 foreign patents, deep surgeon relationships, and scale. Stryker competes against Zimmer Biomet, J&J MedTech, and Smith & Nephew in orthopaedics/robotics, and against distinct competitor sets in each MedSurg line (Medtronic, Penumbra, Terumo in Vascular; Olympus, Karl Storz in Endoscopy). Management is deliberately mix-shifting toward faster-growing, lower-margin MedSurg/Neuro categories, which it has flagged as a modest long-term gross-margin headwind.

## Financial Analysis

The pre-cyberattack financial profile is that of a top-tier compounder:

- **Revenue trajectory:** $18.4B (2022) → $20.5B (2023) → $22.6B (2024) → $25.1B (2025), a ~11% CAGR, with organic constant-currency growth of ~10% at the high end of the MedTech peer group.
- **Margins:** Gross margin 64.0% in 2025 (up from 63.7% in 2023); GAAP operating margin 19.5%, adjusted operating margin ~26%. Both segments reached ~29.8–29.9% segment operating margins.
- **Profitability:** 2025 net income $3,246M, GAAP diluted EPS $8.40; **adjusted EPS $13.63, +11.8%**. GAAP was depressed by a one-time $718M IP-transfer tax charge that spiked the effective rate to 28.1% from 14.3% (adjusted rate 15.1%). ROE 14.5%, ROA 6.8%.
- **Cash flow quality:** Operating cash flow $5,044M, FCF $4,283M (17.1% FCF margin) on only $761M of capex — asset-light relative to revenue and a signal of high earnings quality.
- **Balance sheet:** Cash $4.0B, total debt ~$15.9B (LT debt $14.9B), equity $22.4B, D/E 1.13, current ratio 1.89, investment-grade rated. Goodwill of $19.3B (~40% of assets) plus $5.7B of intangibles reflect the acquisition-driven model; the Altman Z of 2.22 sits in the "grey zone" as a function of that goodwill-heavy asset base rather than genuine distress, with solid liquidity and coverage.

The March 11 cyberattack is now the dominant near-term financial variable. Q1 2026 gross margin fell ~200bps to 63.3% on idle production; adjusted operating margin contracted 180bps to 21.1%. This followed a clean track record — the prior two quarters delivered small beats (+0.7%, +0.8%), so the miss is a discrete event rather than the culmination of deteriorating visibility.

The mechanism matters for whether the earnings snap back. Idle production time is a timing effect: units not manufactured during the outage can be produced later and shipped to fill backlog, recovering the deferred revenue and absorbing the fixed-cost drag that caused the margin compression. This is the crux of the "transitory" thesis. It holds only if there is no lasting damage to customer relationships, no data-integrity remediation cost that recurs, and no regulatory or litigation tail — none of which can be confirmed while the investigation remains open.

The GAAP-to-adjusted gap warrants attention. The ~$5.23 gap between 2025 GAAP EPS ($8.40) and adjusted EPS ($13.63) is driven largely by amortization of purchased intangibles (~$1.49) and the one-time IP-transfer tax charge (~$1.71). The tax item will not recur, but the intangible amortization is a structural consequence of the roll-up model and will persist, so the forward P/E of ~22x on adjusted estimates is the correct lens rather than the ~37x trailing GAAP figure.

## Growth Analysis

The underlying growth engine is durable and multi-pronged. Orthopaedics is levered to the Mako flywheel and demographic tailwinds in knee and hip replacement, with new platforms (Mako 4, Mako Shoulder, robotic revision hip) expanding the addressable procedure set. MedSurg/Neuro is the faster-growing engine, now supercharged by the ~$4.81B Inari acquisition in peripheral vascular (VTE/stroke) and the Amplitude Vascular Systems deal (intravascular lithotripsy, ~$435M plus up to $400M in milestones) closed in May 2026, positioning Stryker against Penumbra, Medtronic, and Terumo in high-growth stroke/VTE/peripheral-arterial-disease markets.

Consensus estimates are the most telling growth data point. Forward EPS estimates are essentially flat over the past 90 days (current-year $14.98 vs. $14.96; next-year $16.72 vs. $16.74), with next-quarter estimates up +3.5% over 90 days. This stability, in the face of a 13.7% Q1 miss, is the analyst community collectively judging the disruption to be non-structural and expecting the ~10% organic run-rate to resume. Estimate revisions here do not clear the high-conviction bar (the +30%/90-day inflection threshold does not apply to a stable-growth compounder), but the absence of downward revisions after a large miss is itself a meaningful positive signal about earnings-power durability.

Growth prospects score 6.2 — solid and improving off the June low, but not high-conviction until Q2 confirms that the Q1 organic deceleration was mechanical rather than a demand signal. We weight growth heavily as the most reliable winner-selection dimension, and the underlying franchise supports a mid-to-high single-digit constant-currency growth outlook plus M&A-driven top-line acceleration; the discount to a higher score is entirely the unresolved cyber overhang.

## Valuation Assessment

Stryker trades at ~22x forward adjusted EPS on the $14.98 current-year consensus (the ~37x figure in the headline data is trailing GAAP, distorted by the one-time tax charge and structural intangible amortization, and is not the right metric). Against the Health Care Equipment peer set, this sits above the peer-median P/E of 25.1 only on the misleading trailing GAAP basis; on a forward-adjusted basis it is roughly in line with quality peers and at a meaningful discount to the premium robotics comparable Intuitive Surgical (~39–50x). Medtronic (21.5x), Abbott (25.1x), and Boston Scientific (19.7x) frame the range; SYK's forward multiple is reasonable for a business growing organic revenue at ~10% with expanding margins and best-in-class robotics.

The stock has de-rated from its own multi-year premium (it traded above 30x forward for much of its history) to ~22x. That compression, on unchanged forward estimates, improves risk/reward: the market is applying a lower multiple to the same (or slightly higher) earnings power because of the cyber overhang. Analyst targets span $315 low to $465 high, with a $386.52 mean and $385 median. On a stable-growth compounder trading below premium peers, we treat the consensus mean as a credible fair-value anchor rather than a stale floor; the ~20% gap to the mean target reflects the market's discount for unresolved cyber risk, not a mispricing the Street has failed to catch.

The valuation score is 6.0. Our calibration treats "cheap" cautiously — a compressed multiple is only a buy signal when there is a specific catalyst for re-rating, and here that catalyst (a clean Q2 confirming reversion to the organic run-rate) is genuine but not yet realized. The fundamentals support the earnings power, and the de-rating is event-driven rather than structural, so this is not a value trap. But it is not deep value either, so we score it above neutral without treating cheapness as sufficient on its own. The valuation improvement over the trajectory (5.0 in February → 6.5 in June) is real and we largely confirm it, trimming marginally to 6.0 to reflect that the re-rating catalyst is still pending.

## Competitive Landscape

Stryker's competitive position is stable-to-improving, earning a 7.2. The Mako installed base is the clearest structural advantage in orthopaedic MedTech: each placed robot drives high-margin implant pull-through and creates surgeon workflow switching costs that Zimmer, J&J, and Smith & Nephew struggle to displace. The 2025 product cadence (Mako 4, Mako Shoulder, robotic revision hip, first-to-market status in several categories) sustains the innovation lead. In vascular, the Inari and AVS deals build a credible position in high-growth stroke/VTE/peripheral markets against Penumbra, Medtronic, and Terumo.

The Q1 2026 reorganization into a new "Ortho Tech" business (combining orthopaedic instruments with Mako/enabling technologies, reallocating $518M goodwill from MedSurg to Orthopaedics with no impairment) is an organizational move to sharpen the robotics-plus-implant flywheel. The Spine divestiture (April 2025, ~$245M, after ~$800M+ in 2024 impairments and loss on sale) demonstrates portfolio discipline — a willingness to exit a structurally challenged franchise rather than defend it, which is a governance and competitive-focus positive.

Industry-wide pressures — China volume-based procurement, hospital consolidation increasing payer bargaining power, EU MDR compliance — are sector-wide and largely priced. Stryker's scale and innovation cadence are the intended defenses. One competitive risk specific to the current situation: a prolonged or repeated cyber disruption could erode surgeon and hospital trust in Stryker's ability to reliably supply, which in a razor/razor-blade model is the asset that matters most. There is no evidence of that yet, but it is the mechanism by which a "transitory" IT event could become a competitive one.

## Risk Assessment

The risk score is 5.5, reflecting specific, currently-live idiosyncratic risks that are not fully resolved:

1. **The March 2026 cyberattack (materialized, still under investigation).** Stryker filed an 8-K/A on April 9 under Item 1.05 confirming materiality to Q1 results. Manufacturing and commercial systems are reported restored, but the investigation was open as of the Q1 10-Q, leaving the full remediation cost, any data-integrity issues, potential litigation/regulatory fines, and Q2 spillover unquantified. Third-party (tier-3, unconfirmed by Stryker) reporting attributes the attack to a pro-Iranian hacktivist group; this attribution is not in Stryker's filings and should be treated as unverified.
2. **Inari/Peripheral Vascular goodwill impairment risk.** The new Peripheral Vascular reporting unit ($3.2B goodwill) exceeded carrying value by only 12% at the October 31, 2025 test — the thinnest cushion in the company. A 100bps rise in the discount rate would trigger a ~$198M impairment. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, this is a live tail risk, and it puts a premium on Inari hitting its growth plan.
3. **German tax assessment.** A ~$754M FCTO assessment (tax years 2010–2017), plus ~$11M more, is under appeal; an adverse outcome would be material.
4. **Structural sector pressures** — China VBP, hospital consolidation, EU MDR (transition through December 2028), and revised EU Product Liability/Collective Redress directives (effective December 2026). These are priced but real.
5. **Tariff/trade whipsaw** — 2025 US tariffs raised costs; a 2026 SCOTUS ruling struck some IEEPA tariffs (refund uncertain) while the administration pivoted to Section 122 authority.
6. **New commercial ERP implementation** flagged as a major operational risk, given the concurrent cyber incident and the burden on IT/systems infrastructure.

On the positive side of the risk ledger, the long-running FCPA inquiries were closed by both the DOJ (April 1, 2025) and SEC (December 16, 2025), removing a legacy overhang.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is broadly neutral-to-constructive and does not contradict the fundamental view. The put/call open-interest ratio has collapsed from 1.32 to 0.18 since early May, indicating a sharp reduction in hedging/bearish positioning as the stock recovered from its May trough (~$282) toward $320. Near-dated put/call volume is elevated (1.68 at the 1-month tenor) but longer tenors are call-heavy (0.10 at 3 months), consistent with short-term event-hedging into the next earnings print rather than a structural bearish stance. Implied move at the nearest expiry is ±9.5%, and 1-year historical volatility is a moderate 26%. Short interest is negligible at 1.9% of float (2.6 days to cover), so there is no crowded bearish position. The market is pricing Q2 as a meaningful binary but is not positioned for a disaster.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (target ~$385, ~20% upside)

The cyberattack is a one-quarter timing event. Q2 2026 shows organic constant-currency growth reverting toward the ~8–10% run-rate as deferred production and delayed orders are recovered, and margins re-expand as idle-time drag rolls off. Consensus forward EPS holds or rises, and the multiple re-rates from ~22x back toward its historical low-to-mid-20s premium as the overhang clears. The Mako flywheel and Inari/AVS vascular expansion sustain top-tier MedTech growth. At ~24x on next-year $16.72 EPS, the stock reaches the low-$400s; the consensus mean of $386.52 is the near-term base for a re-rating. Upside case toward the $465 high target if vascular integration outperforms and margins accelerate.

### Bear Case (target ~$270, ~16% downside)

The cyberattack has a longer tail than management acknowledges. Q2 shows continued disruption, remediation costs recur, and/or data-integrity or customer-trust damage erodes the reliability advantage that underpins the razor/razor-blade model. Simultaneously, the Inari peripheral-vascular unit misses its growth plan, and with only a 12% goodwill cushion, a rate-driven or performance-driven impairment materializes, denting reported results and confidence in the M&A strategy. Forward estimates begin to fall, the multiple compresses toward the sector median (~20x), and the stock retests the May 2026 low near $282, with a bear target in the high-$260s to low-$270s. The German tax assessment resolving adversely would add to the drag.

### Base Case (target ~$360)

Q2 confirms the disruption was largely mechanical, with a partial catch-up in H2 and full run-rate resumption by year-end. Forward estimates hold near current levels. The stock re-rates modestly from ~22x toward ~23–24x on $14.98–$16.72 EPS as the overhang clears, reaching roughly $360 over 12 months — approximately in line with the current $319.87 plus the earnings/multiple recovery. This ~12% upside to base is real but not asymmetric given the open investigation, which is why the call is HOLD rather than BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month growth thesis is intact and constructive, but the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric until the cyber overhang resolves. Reassessment point: the Q2 2026 earnings print, the single most important near-term catalyst.

**Upgrade to BUY triggers:**
- Q2 2026 organic constant-currency growth recovers to ≥6% (confirming the disruption was mechanical), with margins re-expanding toward the pre-attack ~26% adjusted operating level.
- Management confirms the cyber investigation is closed with no material recurring remediation cost, litigation, or regulatory penalty.
- Any pullback toward the low-$290s / high-$280s (the May 2026 trough zone) on unchanged forward estimates, which would restore genuine asymmetry at ~19–20x forward.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
- Q2 shows continued cyber-related disruption (organic growth below ~2% cc again) or management guides down full-year adjusted EPS below ~$14.50.
- A goodwill impairment on the Inari/Peripheral Vascular unit, or an adverse German tax resolution, that signals the acquisition strategy is under-earning.
- Forward EPS estimates begin falling >5% over a 90-day window.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026). Secondary monitorables: the annual goodwill test (October 31), the German tax appeal, and any 8-K amendment quantifying the full cyber cost.

## Conclusion

Stryker remains a high-quality medical-technology compounder with a durable Mako robotics moat, expanding margins, strong free-cash-flow conversion, and a credible vascular growth vector. The March 2026 cyberattack drove a ~21% drawdown from the July 2025 high, but forward consensus estimates are essentially unchanged, the sell-side treats the event as one-off, and manufacturing/commercial systems are reported restored — all consistent with a transitory disruption on a business whose earnings power is intact. On that basis, the price decline improves risk/reward.

We stop short of a BUY for three specific reasons: the cyber investigation was still open as of the Q1 10-Q, leaving the full cost and Q2 spillover unquantified; the Inari-related peripheral-vascular goodwill sits only 12% above carrying value, a live impairment tail in a higher-for-longer rate environment; and insiders provide no positive conviction signal — recent activity is entirely routine sales (including a ~$100M+ diversification sale by founder-family director Ronda Stryker) with no open-market buying. These keep the setup balanced. The base case offers ~12% upside to ~$360 over 12 months, with a bull case to ~$385–465 and a bear case to ~$270; the Q2 print will determine which path the stock takes. **HOLD** with a growth-hold rationale, overall score 6.3, and a clear upgrade path to BUY on a clean Q2 or a pullback toward the high-$280s.
