**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

First Hawaiian is the holding company for First Hawaiian Bank, the oldest (founded 1858) and one of the two largest banks headquartered in Hawaii, with roughly 50 branches across Hawaii, Guam, and Saipan. The investment case rests on a durable, low-cost deposit franchise built over 165 years in an insular market with high barriers to entry, producing a core return on average tangible common equity near 16% and a low-beta (0.71) defensive earnings profile. FY2025 delivered a clean earnings recovery: net income rose to $276M, up roughly 20% from the $230M FY2024 trough, as deposit repricing eased and asset yields caught up following the punishing 2022–2024 margin squeeze. That recovery has driven a re-rating to a fresh 52-week high, with the shares up about 18% over the trailing year and now trading at $29.97.

The re-rating has done most of its work. At $29.97 the stock sits above the analyst mean target ($28.44) and median target ($28.00) and near the high end of the $26–$31 target range. Forward P/E of 12.1x is only modestly below the regional-bank peer median of 13.4x, and consensus estimate revisions have been positive but shallow (current-year EPS up 4.0% over 90 days, next year up 3.9%), nowhere near the velocity that would justify treating consensus targets as stale floors. Forward EPS growth of roughly 6% ($2.33 to $2.47) reflects a franchise whose growth is structurally capped by its single-market footprint.

We maintain a HOLD, classified as an income hold. The 3.47% dividend yield (well above the 1.9% peer median) is well-covered at a ~45% payout and provides a real floor, and the quality of the deposit base and capital position bound the downside. With the shares above consensus targets after an 18% advance, elevated short interest at 14.9% of float, and rich options pricing signaling near-term uncertainty, the risk/reward is materially less attractive than it was near $27. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD; the higher bar for continuing the call is met by the fact that the re-rating has closed the valuation gap rather than by any change in the underlying franchise.

## Company Overview

First Hawaiian operates two reportable segments — Retail Banking and Commercial Banking — offering deposits, residential and commercial mortgages, home equity products, auto lending and leasing, small-business and middle-market C&I lending, commercial real estate and construction finance, wealth management and trust, and an unusually large (for a bank this size) card and merchant-processing business. It holds the #1 or #2 deposit share in Hawaii, competing chiefly against Bank of Hawaii and Central Pacific Financial in a concentrated, mature market.

The bank was formerly majority-owned by BNP Paribas, which fully exited its stake several years ago; FHB is now a widely held, independent public company with no controlling shareholder. Its defining structural feature is the trade-off embedded in geography: near-total isolation gives it a sticky, granular, low-cost funding base and pricing power, while tying every part of its business (loans, deposits, CRE collateral, and fee income) to the Hawaii economy and its dependence on tourism, federal/military spending, and real estate.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year net income series tells the central story. Net income ran $266M (2021), $266M (2022), $235M (2023), $230M (2024), then recovered to $276M in 2025. The three-year decline through 2024 reflected deposit costs outrunning asset yields during the Fed's hiking cycle; the 2025 rebound of roughly 20% is the margin-compression trough reversing as deposit betas normalized. Core ROATCE moved from 20.2% (2022) down to 15.9% (2025), reflecting both rate normalization and a larger equity base, but 15.9% remains a high-quality return for a regional bank and confirms the underlying franchise economics are intact.

Capital and credit quality point to a conservatively run institution. FHB is well-capitalized with CET1 comfortably above regulatory minimums, and it actively returns capital through a regular dividend and share repurchases. The dividend (roughly $1.04 annualized against $2.33 of current-year EPS) implies a payout ratio near 45% and is well-covered. The low-cost deposit franchise is the source of the profitability advantage: it is what allows FHB to earn ~16% ROATCE without reaching for credit or duration risk.

Consensus estimates have been drifting higher, consistent with the NIM recovery: current quarter $0.58 (up 5.2% over 90 days), next quarter $0.60 (up 4.7%), current year $2.33 (up 4.0%), and next year $2.47 (up 3.9%). The 30-day revisions are essentially flat, so the momentum is positive but decelerating; the bulk of the estimate uplift landed in the earlier part of the 90-day window as the FY2025 results were absorbed. Standard bank-earnings visibility is good here; there is no sign of a deteriorating estimate trend that would flag a fundamental problem, but equally there is no acceleration.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is the weakest dimension of the case and the one that most constrains the score. The FY2025 earnings recovery is a normalization off a rate-driven trough rather than a new growth vector, and it is now largely banked in both the numbers and the price. Forward EPS growth of roughly 6% is respectable for a defensive bank but not a re-rating catalyst on its own.

The structural constraint is the single-market footprint. FHB cannot readily expand its addressable market; incremental growth depends on the Hawaii economy, marginal share gains in an oligopoly where it already holds the top position, and the fee businesses (wealth management, trust, card/merchant). None of these offers the kind of scalable growth that would support a premium multiple. The modest estimate-revision velocity (mid-single-digit) confirms this is a stable compounder, not an inflection story; it does not clear the threshold where rising estimates warrant treating consensus targets as conservative floors.

## Valuation Assessment

At $29.97, FHB trades at a forward P/E of 12.1x (next-year EPS $2.47) and a trailing P/E of 13.1x, against a regional-bank peer median of 13.4x (BOKF 13.4x, BPOP 11.7x, FCNCA 12.2x, CBC 16.1x). On a P/E basis FHB is roughly in line to modestly cheap versus peers. The clearer relative-value support is the dividend: 3.47% versus the 1.9% peer median, backed by a ~45% payout, a genuine yield premium that reflects both the payout policy and the market's discount for concentration.

The complication is that the re-rating has already happened. The shares now trade above the analyst mean target of $28.44 and median of $28.00, near the top of the $26–$31 range. For a stock with only mid-single-digit estimate revisions and a capped growth market, being above consensus targets signals that the market has priced in the earnings recovery and the current environment rather than that analysts will chase the price higher. The proxy's own pay-versus-performance table showed FHB's indexed TSR (132.97) trailing its peer group (152.71) over the measurement period, which historically supported a "relatively undervalued" read; that gap has narrowed materially as the stock advanced to new highs in 2026.

We resist scoring "cheap" as a buy here. A low P/E on a slow-growth, single-market bank is more often a fair reflection of the concentration risk than a mispricing, and the specific catalyst that would force a re-rating (a large estimate inflection, a break in the growth ceiling, or an acquisition premium) is absent. The valuation is fair: supportive of ownership for yield, not compelling as an entry point.

## Competitive Landscape

FHB's competitive position is stable and durable rather than improving. Hawaii banking is an oligopoly in which FHB and Bank of Hawaii split the top of the market, with Central Pacific a distant third. Geographic isolation, entrenched multi-generational relationships, and regulatory scrutiny create high barriers to entry, which protect incumbent deposit share and funding costs. This is a real moat and the source of the ~16% ROATCE.

The trajectory, however, is the relevant question, and here the picture is flat. FHB is already at the top of its market, so there is limited share to gain, and the same isolation that protects the moat caps organic expansion. There is no evidence of share erosion (deposits are sticky and credit is conservatively managed), but neither is there a widening-moat dynamic that the market has failed to price. The card and merchant-processing franchise is a differentiated fee source but not large enough to change the growth profile. This is a stable leader in a mature market, which supports a moderate score without arguing for upside.

## Risk Assessment

The defining risk is single-market geographic concentration. FHB's loan demand, credit quality, deposits, and CRE collateral values are all levered to the Hawaii economy and its dependence on tourism, military/federal spending, and real estate. A tourism downturn (driven by a mainland recession, an Asia-Pacific slowdown reducing Japanese visitor traffic, or a travel shock) would hit multiple revenue and credit lines simultaneously, with no geographic diversification to offset. This is a structurally higher concentration than any comparably sized mainland peer carries.

Rate and NIM sensitivity is the second material risk, demonstrated by the 2022–2024 earnings decline. The current environment is favorable, but earnings remain cyclically sensitive to the shape of the curve and deposit betas. Commercial real estate exposure is a correlated risk in an insular market where CRE values track hospitality real estate. Natural-disaster and climate risk is real rather than theoretical following the 2023 Maui/Lahaina wildfire, affecting both operations and collateral. Governance is a non-issue: reasonable CEO compensation ($6.29M, 3.8x pay ratio), well-designed performance metrics (core ROATCE, ROA, asset quality, TSR), and clean insider activity. The Item 5.02 management/board transitions noted in early 2026 appear planned, but succession dynamics around a long-tenured CEO/Chairman warrant monitoring.

Most of these risks are known and structurally priced, which is why the concentration discount exists. The near-term concern is that the stock is at a 52-week high just as it becomes more, not less, exposed to any disappointment.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is flagging elevated near-term uncertainty despite the price strength. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 43.0% sits well above the 23.8% one-year historical volatility (a 19.2-point rich spread), and the put/call skew of 44.6% indicates elevated demand for downside protection. The term structure is in contango (near-term 43.0% below longer-dated 51.4%), a normal shape, and the implied move on the nearest expiry is roughly ±11.5%. Combined with short interest at 14.9% of float and 8.6 days to cover, the positioning suggests a meaningful cohort of institutions is hedged or positioned for downside into the next earnings print, which argues against paying up at the 52-week high even though it does not overturn the fundamental quality of the franchise. The elevated short interest also creates modest squeeze potential on any upside surprise.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($31).** The NIM recovery continues as the rate environment stays constructive, core ROATCE holds near 16%, and the Hawaii tourism economy remains healthy. Continued estimate revisions push the stock toward the top of the analyst range as the market awards a small premium for the quality of the deposit franchise, the low beta, and the well-covered yield. Elevated short interest amplifies any upside surprise. This reaches roughly $31, about 3% above the current price.

**Bear Case ($25–$26).** A tourism or macro downturn (mainland recession, an Asia-Pacific slowdown, or a natural-disaster shock) pressures loan demand, credit quality, and CRE collateral simultaneously. Alternatively, a renewed deposit-cost squeeze or an aggressive rate-cut path compresses asset yields faster than funding costs fall, re-running a version of the 2022–2024 margin problem. The concentration discount widens and the multiple compresses back toward 11x on flat-to-lower earnings, taking the stock to $25–$26, roughly 13–17% below current levels.

**Base Case ($28–$29).** Earnings grow at a mid-single-digit pace in line with consensus, ROATCE stays in the mid-teens, and the dividend is comfortably covered. The stock trades broadly sideways around fair value as the re-rating has already discounted the recovery, with the yield providing total-return support. This is roughly flat to modestly below the current price, consistent with the $28 median analyst target and 12.5x on ~$2.40 of normalized EPS.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the upcoming quarterly earnings release, where NIM trajectory, deposit costs, and credit quality will confirm or challenge the recovery thesis. The options market is pricing a ±11.5% move around that print.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $26s (near the analyst low target) that restores a meaningful value cushion while fundamentals remain intact, improving the entry point on an unchanged franchise.
- Estimate-revision velocity accelerating meaningfully (current-year EPS revisions moving above high-single-digits) alongside evidence of durable NIM expansion, indicating the recovery has further to run than currently priced.
- Clustered open-market insider buying, which would provide the confirming signal absent from the current neutral insider picture.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A confirmed tourism or Hawaii-macro deterioration (visitor arrivals, hospitality CRE, or credit metrics rolling over) that turns the concentration risk from theoretical to active.
- A renewed deposit-cost squeeze or credit-cost increase that reverses the NIM recovery and cuts forward EPS estimates, or any move that threatens dividend coverage.
- A multiple expansion well above peers (P/E toward 15x+) on flat earnings that would leave the stock stretched with no growth to justify it.

## Conclusion

First Hawaiian is a high-quality, defensively positioned regional bank with a genuine and durable funding advantage, mid-teens ROATCE, conservative credit, and a well-covered dividend that stands out against the peer median. The FY2025 earnings recovery was real and confirms the franchise is out of its margin-compression trough. The issue is timing and price: the stock has re-rated to a 52-week high, now trades above the analyst mean and median targets, and offers only mid-single-digit forward growth in a structurally capped single-market economy. The value gap that existed earlier in 2026 has closed, and elevated short interest plus rich options pricing signal that the market is positioned cautiously into the next print.

We rate FHB a HOLD for income. The yield and quality justify continuing to own the shares for total-return stability, but the risk/reward at $29.97 does not support adding at current levels, and there is no distinct company-specific catalyst that would override the negative alpha typically embedded in regional-bank selection. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the mid-$26s or on evidence that the NIM recovery has further, sharper upside than consensus now embeds.
