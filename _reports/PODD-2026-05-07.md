## Executive Summary

Insulet Corporation has declined significantly from its 52-week high of $354.88, driven by a combination of the FDA Class I recall of Omnipod 5 pods, broader market dislocation, tariff uncertainty, and securities fraud lawsuits. The decline has compressed the stock's valuation from ~50x forward earnings to approximately 23x on estimated 2026 consensus EPS — a multiple that substantially underprices a business growing revenue at 30%+ with ~71-72% gross margins and a razor/blade recurring revenue model.

Q1 2026 results reported the day of this analysis (May 6) showed approximately 34% reported revenue growth (30% constant currency) with guidance raised to 21-23% constant currency growth for the full year. The CEO purchased $1.03M of stock on the open market in February at $239. The recall, while serious, has not yet shown signs of systemic platform failure — evidenced by the raised guidance and continued customer acquisition. However, the recall was disclosed on March 12, after the CEO's purchase, and the situation remains under monitoring.

The primary risks are real: recall expansion, tariff materialization on Malaysian/Chinese manufacturing, and GLP-1 competition in type 2 diabetes. At current valuations on a company growing EPS at 25%+ annually with visible multi-year runway (600,000 active users growing, type 2 expansion, 19-country international presence), the market appears to be pricing these risks as certainties rather than probabilities.

## Company Overview

Insulet manufactures and sells the Omnipod platform — the only tubeless, wearable, disposable insulin delivery system. The core product, Omnipod 5, is an automated insulin delivery (AID) system that integrates with continuous glucose monitors (Dexcom G6/G7, Abbott FreeStyle Libre 2 Plus in various markets) to auto-adjust insulin dosing. Pods are replaced every 3 days, creating a highly predictable recurring revenue stream from 600,000+ active global users.

The company distributes through the pharmacy channel (unique among insulin pumps), which eliminates the upfront capital equipment cost barrier that historically limited pump adoption. Three major distributors account for ~78% of revenue. Revenue in 2025 was $2.71B (31% growth), with U.S. contributing approximately 72% and International approximately 28%.

The company currently produces products at Acton, MA and Johor, Malaysia, is investing in a third manufacturing plant in Costa Rica, and produces devices at a China facility operated by a contract manufacturer. The Drug Delivery segment ($34.1M, ~1% of revenue) supplies modified pods to Amgen through 2028.

## Growth Analysis

Insulet's growth runway is supported by multiple independent vectors:

1. **Pump penetration in Type 1:** Only ~40% of U.S. and ~25% of international T1 patients use pumps. Most new users convert from manual daily injections (MDI), not from competitor pumps.

2. **Type 2 diabetes expansion:** FDA cleared Omnipod 5 for T2 in August 2024. The EVOLUTION 2 study demonstrated 68% time in range with no boluses — a transformative result for T2 patients. This addressable population is 2-3x larger than the T1 market.

3. **International expansion:** Omnipod 5 available in 19 countries (Omnipod products broadly available in 25 countries) with 44% international growth (39% constant currency). Significant runway in Middle East and additional European markets.

4. **Next-generation products:** Omnipod 6 (STRIVE pivotal study completed), fully closed-loop T2 system, Libre 3 integration, iPhone app launch — each represents a potential re-acceleration catalyst.

5. **Margin expansion:** Management targeting ~100bps annual margin improvement as manufacturing scales. Path to 25%+ EBITDA margins is visible.

Estimated consensus: approximately $6.46 EPS for 2026 and approximately $8.08 for 2027 (based on third-party analyst consensus estimates). These imply ~25% EPS growth, consistent with the revenue growth rate plus operating leverage. Management's guidance calls for adjusted EPS growth of >25% in 2026.

## Competitive Landscape

Insulet's competitive position is structurally advantaged:

1. **Tubeless design** is unique in the market. Medtronic's tubed pumps and Tandem's t:slim require physical connections that limit convenience. No competitor has successfully replicated the tubeless form factor at scale (EOFlow attempted and lost a $452M trade secret lawsuit).

2. **Pharmacy channel distribution** dramatically lowers the adoption barrier. Insulet's design allows Pod therapy at relatively low or no upfront investment where reimbursement allows — a significant differentiator vs. traditional durable medical equipment pump distribution.

3. **Multi-CGM compatibility** (Dexcom G6/G7, Abbott Libre 2 Plus in various markets, Libre 3 in development) avoids dependence on a single partner and gives patients choice.

4. **1,000+ patents** expiring 2026-2047 with a permanent injunction against EOFlow's competing product.

**Threats to position:**
- Medtronic is spinning off its diabetes division into a standalone public company, which may create a more focused and aggressive competitor.
- GLP-1 drugs could reduce the long-term insulin-requiring T2 population, though the near-term impact is marginal (patients still progress to insulin need).
- Insulet's dependence on a single platform means any sustained quality issue has outsized impact.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving: 600,000+ active users growing, international expansion accelerating, and the T2 market entry represents share gain in a market where Insulet previously had zero presence.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical risks (partially priced):**

1. **Class I Recall (HIGH):** On March 12, 2026, Insulet disclosed a voluntary medical device correction for specific lots of Omnipod 5 pods. The company reported 18 serious adverse events and no deaths, with affected pods representing approximately 1.5% of annual global Omnipod 5 production. The scope was later expanded on April 10 to include 13 additional lots. Q1 2026 results included approximately $11.7M of estimated warranty costs related to the correction, but revenue grew ~34% and guidance was raised — suggesting limited immediate commercial impact. However, the legal/reputational overhang persists and the situation cannot be declared fully contained until additional quarters confirm no further scope expansion. Key monitoring: whether additional lots are identified or if adverse events increase.

2. **Tariff Risk (MEDIUM-HIGH):** Insulet discloses tariff risk in its filings, noting that while tariffs are not expected to significantly impact 2026 gross margins, if the exemption currently in place for certain medical devices is eliminated, tariffs would have a material impact given manufacturing in Malaysia and China. The Costa Rica plant provides partial mitigation.

3. **GLP-1 Competition (MEDIUM, long-term):** GLP-1 drugs may delay progression to insulin dependence in some T2 patients. This is a 5-10 year headwind to TAM growth, not a near-term revenue threat. Insulet's pivot into T2 with Omnipod 5 is the correct strategic response.

4. **Customer Concentration (MEDIUM):** Three distributors at 78% of revenue creates dependency.

5. **Litigation Overhang:** Securities fraud lawsuits have been filed. The probability and magnitude of outcomes are uncertain, and no material outcome can be assessed at this stage. The EOFlow jury verdict of $452M was reduced by the court to $59.4M (to avoid double recovery), with a permanent worldwide injunction entered. EOFlow has appealed and its ability to pay is uncertain — Insulet has not recorded the damages.

6. **Auditor Change + Management Transition:** New CEO, CFO, CCO, and auditor (Grant Thornton to PwC after a competitive process) within 12 months. Grant Thornton's reports for 2025 and 2024 had no adverse or qualified opinions, so the change does not imply known accounting issues — but combined with the recall, it creates governance uncertainty that warrants monitoring. The CEO's $1M+ open-market purchase partially offsets this concern.

## Conclusion

Q1 2026 results significantly reduced concern about growth deceleration, though management's Q2 guidance of 20-22% constant-currency growth (total company) indicates sequential moderation from Q1's exceptional 30% constant-currency pace. The stock now trades at approximately 26x estimated 2026 earnings for a business delivering ~34% reported revenue growth with ~71-72% gross margins and a defensible recurring revenue model.

The key question is whether the recall represents a systemic manufacturing failure or a contained lot-specific defect. The evidence to date — raised guidance, no deaths, and scope described as approximately 1.5% of annual global Omnipod 5 production — is encouraging but not conclusive. Additional quarters are needed to confirm containment.

The CEO's $1.03M open-market purchase at $239 on February 20 represents significant personal capital deployed well above today's price. This purchase occurred before the March 12 recall disclosure and therefore cannot be interpreted as a direct response to the recall, but it does signal strong conviction in the company's underlying trajectory at a price materially above current levels.

At current levels, the market is pricing PODD as if multiple severe adverse outcomes will occur simultaneously. The probability-weighted expected value — even assigning meaningful probability to recall expansion and tariff materialization — supports a BUY recommendation with a 12-month base case target of approximately $207.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The valuation section relies on estimated consensus EPS figures ($6.46/$8.08) and a stock price that could not be fully validated from primary sources; the CEO insider purchase occurred before the recall disclosure, weakening it as a post-recall signal; and the U.S./International revenue mix correction (72%/28% vs. previously stated 57%/43%) indicates somewhat lower international diversification than originally presented. These corrections do not invalidate the thesis but reduce the certainty of the specific price targets and the strength of the "recall is contained" argument.
