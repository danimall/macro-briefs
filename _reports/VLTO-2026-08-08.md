## Executive Summary

Veralto is two businesses with divergent trajectories priced as one compounder. Water Quality — 60% of FY2025 sales at a 25.4% segment operating margin — grew core sales 5.7% in Q2 2026, led by ChemTreat industrial chemical treatment at +10.7% core. Product Quality & Innovation, the other 40%, grew core sales 0.5% in H1 2026, with packaging & color down 4.4% against +3.8% core growth in FY2025. Management has raised FY2026 guidance twice-over (core sales to +4.0–4.5%, adjusted diluted EPS to $4.35–$4.43 from $4.20–$4.28) and explicitly hung the implied second-half core acceleration to roughly 5–6% on PQI recovering. That single assumption carries most of the near-term equity story.

The Q2 print was a genuine beat — $1.11 adjusted diluted EPS against $1.03 consensus, the fourth consecutive beat, with adjusted operating margin up 90bp to 24.6% — but the composition matters. Of the $0.18 year-over-year increase in adjusted EPS, roughly $0.05 came from recoveries of tariffs previously paid under IEEPA, roughly $0.044 from a 320bp lower adjusted effective tax rate (18.7% vs 21.9%), and roughly $0.018 from a 1.6% lower diluted share count. Residual operational contribution is approximately $0.068, implying underlying adjusted EPS growth in the 7–8% range rather than 19.4%. Management's own guidance corroborates this: Q3 adjusted EPS of $1.06–$1.09 is below Q2's $1.11, and second-half adjusted EPS growth is guided to roughly 9% against 16.6% delivered in H1.

The balance sheet is the strongest element and is unambiguously investment grade: net debt of $1,260M against a $1,355M FY2025 EBITDA proxy (0.93x), 12.8x H1 interest coverage, $2,119M of cash, a $1.5B undrawn revolver, zero 2027 maturities and the September 2026 $700M maturity pre-funded in June at 4.85% versus the retiring 5.50% coupon. Capital allocation turned materially more shareholder-friendly in late 2025 — the first buyback authorization in company history ($750M), of which $434M was executed in H1 2026 at a Q2 average of $86.13, roughly 12% below today's price. At 22.4x the FY2026 adjusted EPS midpoint and a 4.36% trailing FCF yield, with a base case near $108 and a bear case near the 52-week low of $80, the risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD; the rating persists because the price has advanced 17% since mid-June against consensus estimate revisions of roughly +3%, absorbing the margin of safety without a change in the underlying growth rate.

## Company Overview

Veralto was separated from Danaher on September 30, 2023 as the former Environmental & Applied Solutions segment, distributed one-for-three to Danaher holders. The lineage is operationally substantive: the Veralto Enterprise System descends from the Danaher Business System, the M&A playbook is bolt-on acquisition of niche leaders funded by balance sheet capacity, and Danaher's former CFO Daniel Comas sits on the board.

FY2025 sales were $5,503M across ~17,000 associates and ~60 principal facilities in roughly 50 countries. Geographic mix: North America 48%, Western Europe 23%, high-growth markets 27%, other developed 2%. Approximately 61% of sales are recurring — reagents, inks, process chemicals, spare parts, service and software. No customer exceeded 10% of sales in any of 2023, 2024 or 2025, a genuine absence of concentration that distinguishes Veralto from most industrial technology peers.

**Water Quality ($3,321M FY2025, 25.4% margin)** comprises Hach (analytical water instruments and reagents, 149,000+ customers, the originator of the first regulated turbidity method — regulatory embedment is the core moat), ChemTreat (industrial water treatment chemistry delivered through on-site technical service) and Trojan Technologies (UV disinfection and membrane filtration).

**Product Quality & Innovation ($2,182M FY2025, 25.2% margin)** comprises Videojet and Linx (inline marking and coding printers with proprietary ink consumables), Esko and TraceGains (packaging pre-press and compliance software), and X-Rite/Pantone (color measurement and the de facto color standard).

The 10-K is candid that Veralto "cannot accurately determine its competitive position," and states that no single patent, trademark or license is material to any segment. The economic moat is installed-base consumable attach, regulatory method approval and workflow standard-setting, not intellectual property.

FY2025 core growth of 4.7% decomposed to +1.9pp price and roughly +2.8pp volume/mix. In Q2 2026 price contributed +3.0pp of the 4.2% core, consistent with tariff pass-through — pricing is now doing roughly 70% of the organic work, up from 40%. That is a durability question for 2027 if input costs normalize.

Gross margin has expanded consistently: 57.8% (FY2023) → 59.8% → 59.9% → 60.6% H1 2026 (61.2% in Q2). Part of the Q2 step-up is the IEEPA tariff recovery. SG&A moved the other way — 34.9% of Q2 sales versus 32.2% — driven by restructuring, GlobalVision deal costs, and acquisition intangible amortization that roughly doubled to $17M from $9M following In-Situ and GlobalVision.

Reported Q2 segment margins understate the operating picture. PQI's 21.0% (from 24.5%) reflects $22M of restructuring; adding it back gives approximately 24.9%, above the prior year. Water Quality at 25.1% reported becomes roughly 25.6% ex-$4M restructuring, flat year over year. Company-reported adjusted operating margin was 24.6% versus 23.7%.

**Cash flow is the highest-quality part of the financial statements.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $1,077M (+23%) on $63M of capex produced $1,014M of FCF, an 18.4% FCF margin with capex at 1.1% of sales. FCF/net income conversion was 108%; guidance calls for >100% again in 2026. Trailing-twelve-month FCF through Q2 2026 was $1,047M on $5,696M of sales. Quarterly FCF was $258M / $291M / $170M / $328M across Q3'25–Q2'26; annualizing Q2 would overstate run-rate FCF by roughly 25%, so the TTM figure is the defensible basis.

**Balance sheet (July 3, 2026)**: cash $2,119M, total debt $3,379M ($700M current), net debt $1,260M, equity $3,098M, goodwill $3,167M, other intangibles $828M, AOCI −$960M. All debt is senior unsecured and fixed-rate; the maturity ladder is $700M in 2026 (5.50% notes, pre-funded by the June 2026 issuance of $725M at 4.85% due 2032), zero in 2027, $700M in 2028, zero 2029–2030 and $1,997M thereafter. The covenant is net leverage ≤3.75x against 0.93x actual.

Two balance-sheet features deserve flagging. First, goodwill plus intangibles of $3,995M exceeds book equity of $3,098M, so tangible book equity is approximately −$897M. For a serial acquirer this is structural rather than alarming, but it removes asset-value support in a downside scenario. Second, carrying $2.1B of cash yielding roughly 3.7% against debt at a blended ~5.0–5.1% coupon implies negative carry on the order of $28–30M pretax annually. The most straightforward reading is deliberate M&A dry powder; prudent pre-funding of the September maturity in a favorable window is an equally consistent explanation.

An Altman Z-Score of approximately 2.28 appears in screening data. The retained-earnings term is mechanically depressed because retained earnings reset to zero at the September 2023 spin ($1,744M accumulated by December 2025, 22.7% of assets). Against 0.93x net leverage, 12.8x coverage, no secured debt and $3.6B of combined cash and revolver capacity, the score is uninformative for solvency here. A separately reported debt/equity ratio of 2.48 is not reproducible from the balance sheet — long-term debt/equity is 0.64x and total liabilities/equity 1.48x on FY2025 figures.

## Growth Analysis

Product-line core growth for H1 2026 versus H1 2025 defines the investment tension precisely:

| Product line | H1 2026 core | Q2 2026 core |
|---|---|---|
| WQ chemical treatment | +7.4% | +10.7% |
| WQ analytical instrumentation | +2.7% | +2.8% |
| PQI marking & coding | +2.8% | +3.5% |
| PQI packaging & color | **−4.4%** | −1.4% |

Packaging & color grew 3.8% core in FY2025. The swing to −4.4% is roughly an eight-point deceleration in a year, attributed in the filing to "global macroeconomic conditions." Management states that digital workflow solutions within packaging and color "continue to deliver strong underlying growth." Read against an aggregate decline of 4.4%, the arithmetic implies material declines in X-Rite and Esko hardware. Veralto does not disclose the software/hardware split, so this is inference rather than disclosed fact, but it is the only reading consistent with both statements.

Two-thirds of the 2026 Cost Optimization Program is aimed at PQI — total charges of $85–105M, allocated PQI $60–68M and WQ $20–27M, substantially complete by end-2028 — against a segment that is 40% of sales. Site consolidations and functional transformation concentrated in a segment whose ex-restructuring margin is *improving* (24.9% vs 24.5%) reads as a view that the hardware-centric cost base needs to be permanently smaller. A benign alternative is opportunistic cost harvesting in a soft-demand window, with margin expansion rather than resizing as the objective. The Q3 and Q4 prints will separate these.

The second growth concern is geographic. High-growth markets — 27% of sales, ~5,000 associates, nine local plants, and the stated long-term engine — posted core sales of −0.1% in Q2, with low-single-digit declines in both China and Latin America and Water Quality high-growth core at −1.5%. FY2025 high-growth core was +5.1%. All Q2 growth was developed-market led (core +6.3%: North America +7.0%, Western Europe +4.9%). One quarter is thin evidence and municipal tender timing can distort it, but the structural growth narrative is on hold.

**Guidance mechanics.** FY2026 adjusted EPS of $4.35–$4.43 represents +11.5% to +13.6% against the FY2025 adjusted base of $3.90. H1 2026 adjusted EPS was $2.18 (Q1 $1.07, Q2 $1.11), up 16.6% on H1 2025's $1.87. The guidance midpoint of $4.39 implies H2 of $2.21, up roughly 8.9% on H2 2025's $2.03 and only 1.4% sequentially from H1. The Q3 guide of $1.06–$1.09 is below Q2's actual $1.11. So management is guiding accelerating core sales (5–6% in H2 versus 3.1% in H1) alongside decelerating adjusted EPS growth — the arithmetic consequence of the IEEPA recovery not repeating and tougher tax comparisons. Investors extrapolating Q2's 19.4% adjusted EPS growth are extrapolating something management has already told them will not persist.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current year $4.39 (+3.2% over 90 days), next year $4.77 (+2.8%), current quarter $1.09 (+1.1%). This is confirmation of guidance delivery, not the >30% revision velocity that signals a systematically underestimated inflection. The track record supports confidence in the numbers — four consecutive beats of 2.2%, 4.3%, 4.3% and 8.3% — which argues that the $4.39 will likely be exceeded modestly rather than materially.

## Competitive Landscape

Water Quality operates in a favorable demand environment with crowded supply. Secular drivers named in the 10-K are water scarcity, water safety, severe weather, tightening discharge regulation and an aging utility workforce that pulls demand toward automation and remote monitoring. Hach's regulatory embedment — approved test methods written into compliance regimes — is the most durable competitive asset in the portfolio, because switching requires method re-approval rather than just a purchasing decision. ChemTreat's +10.7% Q2 core growth is a share-gain outcome against much larger industrial water treatment incumbents, achieved through on-site technical service representatives, and is inherently a share model rather than a market-growth model. That is favorable for durability but means the growth rate depends on continued sales-force execution.

A note on attribution: third-party coverage framed the Q2 result around "data center tailwinds." The company's own press release attributes growth to industrial water treatment, water reuse and environmental monitoring, and does not use the phrase. Hyperscale cooling-water treatment is a genuine and rapidly growing industrial water application, and North America-led chemical treatment growth of 10.7% is consistent with it, but there is no company-disclosed, quantified data-center revenue exposure. It should not be capitalized into the thesis as a known driver.

PQI's competitive position is the deteriorating side. Marking and coding retains classic razor/razorblade economics with 3.5% Q2 core growth — respectable, and the more defensible half of the segment. Packaging and color is cyclically exposed to CPG and converter equipment budgets, currently contracting, and the 10-K's specific warning that "customers may introduce or switch to lower-priced consumables" is the structural tail risk to the entire installed-base model across both segments. Combined with explicit flags on "competitors based in low-cost manufacturing locations" and market consolidation, the picture is a company defending strong niche positions rather than expanding into open ground.

Net trajectory: improving in 60% of the business, deteriorating in the discretionary portion of the other 40%. Score 6.0, down from prior assessments.

## Risk Assessment

**1. PQI second-half reacceleration is the load-bearing guidance assumption.** Core growth of 5–6% in H2 versus 3.1% in H1 requires PQI to move from +0.5% to something materially better, on the strength of digital workflow adoption (GlobalVision, acquired April 2026) and new product launches. If packaging and color stays negative, FY2026 core lands at the low end and FY2027 consensus of $4.77 is exposed. The October Q3 print is the resolution point.

**2. Earnings quality on non-operational drivers.** The ~$0.05/share IEEPA tariff recovery is a refund of amounts previously paid, and the filings reviewed do not describe a claims process, residual contingency or whether further recoveries remain. The 320bp decline in the adjusted effective tax rate is partly attributed to OBBBA (potentially structural) and partly to discrete items (not), at a disclosure level that does not permit a clean 2027 forecast. Pricing at +3.0pp taken to offset tariffs faces reversal pressure if input costs normalize.

**3. Goodwill headroom concentration.** The FY2025 10-K disclosed three reporting units with fair value exceeding carrying value by 104% to 987%, compressing to 66%–781% under a hypothetical 10% fair-value reduction. The 104% unit is unnamed. It is not at imminent risk, but it is materially tighter than the others and is the first place to look if PQI software or packaging assets underperform. Goodwill and intangibles of $3,995M against $3,098M of equity means an impairment would consume book value quickly.

**4. High-growth-market fixed cost base.** Nine local plants and ~5,000 associates supporting 27% of sales that grew −0.1% core in Q2.

**5. Danaher separation tax indemnification volatility.** FY2025 operating margin was reduced roughly 20bp by a reduction in the indemnification asset, and the 19.9% effective tax rate included a $21M net discrete benefit partly from these matters. This is a bidirectional earnings-volatility source.

**6. Governance entrenchment.** Classified board, no shareholder right to call special meetings, no action by written consent, 66⅔% supermajority to amend bylaws, Delaware exclusive forum, and a charter provision exempting Danaher from DGCL Section 203. Veralto is effectively immune to a hostile approach, which removes a valuation floor that similar-quality assets sometimes carry.

**7. FX.** AOCI of −$960M; more European-currency revenue than expense, so euro strength helps operating profit while cumulative translation losses sit on the balance sheet. $979M of net investment hedge notional including the EUR 500M notes and $410M of cross-currency swaps.

**8. Unresolved disclosure items.** Two Item 5.02 8-Ks (August 7 and November 3, 2025) were not retrieved; a Water Quality leadership transition is a plausible inference but unverified. Risk factors were modified in the Q1 2026 10-Q, contents unread. A widely cited $70M-by-2028 savings target for the cost program appears only in third-party commentary, not in filings.

What is notably *absent* from the risk register: refinancing risk (zero 2027 maturities, 2026 maturity pre-funded), covenant risk (0.93x against 3.75x), customer concentration (no customer >10% for three years), and crowded short positioning (3.3% of float, 2.9 days to cover). On balance the risk profile is moderate with one clear binary at the October print. Score 6.2.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 23.6% against 22.8% one-year realized is fair value, and the IV percentile of 7% within the current chain places near-dated options at the cheap end of their own range — the market is pricing no unusual event risk into the 12-day tenor (±3.9% implied move). Term structure is in contango (23.6% at one month, 26.0% at three months, 28.8% at six months), the normal, complacent configuration; notably, the three-month tenor spanning the October Q3 print carries only 26.0% IV and a modest 6.8% implied move, which is inconsistent with treating PQI's reacceleration as a high-variance binary. Put/call skew is call-tilted at both the one-month (−4.3%) and six-month (−7.6%) tenors, offering no confirmation of downside positioning. Put/call volume and open-interest ratios read 0.00 across the chain and are unusable; the reported ATM IV change from 626.2% to 23.6% since June is a data artifact, not a volatility collapse, and no inference should be drawn from it.

The takeaway: options positioning is mildly constructive and complacent, which corroborates the fundamental read that the Q3 print is under-hedged relative to its importance. For an owner, three-month or six-month protection is not expensive here in absolute terms relative to the guidance risk being carried.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $124 (+26%)

Water Quality is a structurally advantaged franchise: 60% of sales, 61%-recurring company-wide revenue, regulatory method embedment at Hach, a share-gain service model at ChemTreat compounding at double-digit core growth, and secular drivers (scarcity, reuse, discharge regulation, utility workforce attrition) that do not depend on the economic cycle. PQI's packaging and color weakness is a CPG capex air pocket, not a structural break — marking and coding is still growing 3.5% core, PQI's ex-restructuring margin is *up* year over year at 24.9%, and GlobalVision plus new product launches deliver the guided second-half reacceleration. The 2026 Cost Optimization Program's $85–105M of charges converts to a permanently lower PQI cost base by 2028, with savings flowing into adjusted EPS while the charges sit outside it. Capital allocation is now a full Danaher-style program: 100% of FCF returned (H1 buybacks plus dividends of $498M exactly matched H1 FCF of $498M), bolt-on M&A funded by a balance sheet with roughly 2.8 turns of gross covenant headroom, and buyback execution at $86.13 that has already proved price-disciplined. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $4.90–$5.00 as PQI normalizes and cost savings land; 25x on $4.95 gives $124. Every covering analyst's target sits above the current price, with a median of $111 and a high of $136.

### Bear Case — $80 (−19%)

Forty percent of the company is growing 0.5% organically and is being resized rather than bridged — two-thirds of a company-wide restructuring program aimed at a segment that is 40% of sales is management's own revealed assessment. The stated long-term growth engine, high-growth markets at 27% of sales, went to −0.1% core with nine plants and 5,000 people carried against flat revenue. The Q2 beat's quality was low: roughly 62% of the adjusted EPS increase came from an IEEPA tariff refund, a lower tax rate and share count, and management's own H2 guide implies adjusted EPS growth decelerating to ~9% from 16.6% in H1. Pricing now supplies 70% of core growth, exposed to reversal if tariff costs normalize. If packaging and color stays negative into 2027, FY2027 EPS lands near $4.45 and the multiple compresses toward 18x on a mid-single-digit grower, giving $80 — precisely the 52-week low touched on May 28. Governance provides no offset: entrenchment provisions rule out strategic interest, CEO reported compensation rose 28% to $14.8M in a year when Veralto's TSR index declined (121.04 → 119.12) while its own selected peer group rose 19% (132.80 → 158.59), and no officer or director made an open-market purchase in the February–July 2026 window while the company itself was buying stock at $86.13. Tangible book equity is approximately −$897M, so a goodwill write-down at the 104%-headroom reporting unit would consume book value directly.

### Assessment

Base case is $108: roughly 22.5x FY2027 consensus of $4.77, splitting the difference between the current 20.6x forward multiple and the 23.7x embedded in the $113 consensus mean. That is +9.8% from $98.34, plus 0.5% dividend. Weighting base 55%, bull 20% and bear 25% produces a probability-weighted return of approximately +6% over twelve months — positive, but below what a beta-near-one industrial should require, and materially below where it stood at $84 in June. The rating reflects entry price and the concentration of near-term outcomes in one guidance assumption, not deterioration in business quality.

On the score trajectory: this is the fourth consecutive HOLD and the overall score continues to drift lower (6.6 → 6.8 → 6.3 → 6.2). The composition of that drift matters. Financial health has genuinely improved and is confirmed here at 7.5 — the June notes issuance removed the 2026 maturity, net leverage is under 1x, and FCF conversion exceeds 100%. Growth prospects and valuation have weakened: growth because PQI's deceleration extended into a second quarter and high-growth markets turned negative, valuation because the price advanced 17% since June 18 against consensus estimate revisions of roughly +3%. Management quality holds at 6.0, balancing demonstrated buyback discipline and disciplined bolt-on M&A against the pay-versus-TSR outcome and the complete absence of open-market insider buying.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (growth hold).** Existing owners retain exposure for high-single-digit EPS compounding and the option on PQI reacceleration; new capital has no urgency at 20.6x forward with the guidance binary unresolved.

**Base case: $108 (+9.8%), 12 months.** 22.5x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $4.77. Assumes FY2026 lands at or slightly above the $4.39 guidance midpoint (consistent with four consecutive beats), PQI core recovers to 2–3% rather than the guided "meaningful" acceleration, and the multiple holds.

**Bull case: $124 (+26%).** 25x FY2027 adjusted EPS of $4.95, requiring PQI core above 3%, high-growth markets returning to positive, cost-program savings visible in the margin line, and a new buyback authorization beyond the remaining $317M.

**Bear case: $80 (−18.7%).** 18x FY2027 adjusted EPS of $4.45, on packaging and color remaining negative, no repeat of the IEEPA and tax benefits, and multiple compression toward mid-single-digit-grower levels. This is the 52-week low of $80.03.

**Reassessment point: Q3 2026 earnings, late October 2026.** The single most informative data point available. Specifically: PQI segment core growth and the packaging & color line item.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE if any two of:**
- PQI core sales growth prints ≥3% in Q3 with packaging & color positive
- High-growth-market core sales return to ≥+3%
- FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance is raised again to $4.45 or above with the raise attributed to volume/mix rather than tariff recoveries or tax rate
- The share price returns to $86–$90 (the level at which the company itself was repurchasing) with FY2027 consensus intact at or above $4.75, giving roughly 22% to base case

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any two of:**
- PQI core growth remains below 1% in Q3, with FY2026 core guided to the low end of 4.0–4.5% or below
- FY2027 consensus is cut below $4.55 (a >4% reduction from $4.77)
- Adjusted operating margin expansion for FY2026 falls below the guided +25bp once the IEEPA benefit is excluded
- A goodwill impairment is recorded at any reporting unit, or the 104%-headroom unit's cushion compresses in the FY2026 10-K
- Cost Optimization Program charges are raised above the $105M high end, or the completion date extends beyond 2028

**Monitor separately:** the remaining $317M of buyback authorization (1.3% of market cap — its EPS contribution runs out within roughly two quarters at the H1 pace, so a new authorization is a meaningful signal either way); any open-market insider purchase, which would be the first in the reviewed period; and disclosure resolving the IEEPA recovery's finality.

## Conclusion

Veralto is a high-quality business at a fair price with one unresolved question that matters more than the rest. The Water Quality franchise justifies a premium industrial multiple: 61% recurring revenue, regulatory method embedment, 25%+ segment margins, 18.4% FCF margin, 30% ROE and a balance sheet carrying 0.93x net leverage with 12.8x interest coverage and no refinancing requirement until 2028. Capital allocation has improved decisively since November 2025, with $434M of buyback executed at prices roughly 12% below today's and $620M deployed into three bolt-on acquisitions that fit the segment strategy.

Against that, 40% of the company grew 0.5% organically in the first half, its most discretionary product line contracted 4.4%, the region that was supposed to supply structural growth turned negative, and roughly 62% of Q2's headline adjusted EPS growth came from a tariff refund, a lower tax rate and a smaller share count. Management's own second-half guide embeds adjusted EPS growth decelerating to roughly 9% while assuming core sales accelerate — an internally consistent set of numbers that nonetheless requires PQI to inflect.

At 22.4x the FY2026 guidance midpoint and 20.6x FY2027 consensus, with a $108 base case, a $124 bull case and an $80 bear case that maps to the 52-week low, probability-weighted return is roughly +6%.
