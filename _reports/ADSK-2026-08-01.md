## Executive Summary

Autodesk is a high-quality subscription franchise trading at a valuation that reflects a specific, unfalsifiable fear: that generative AI reduces the number of paid seats required to produce design, engineering and construction documentation. The company converted $2,409M of FY2026 revenue into free cash flow — a 33.4% FCF margin on $43M of capex — and holds roughly $825M of net cash against $2,484M of borrowings. At 18.7x the $12.525 midpoint of FY2027 non-GAAP EPS guidance, and 16.4x the $14.26 next-year consensus, the multiple sits well below the 40.7x median of its application-software comparable set and below its own five-year norm.

Three developments since our last review argue the fundamental picture is intact or better. First, the accounting-integrity overhang that compressed the multiple through 2024-2025 has cleared: the SEC closed its matter on 8/19/2025, the U.S. Attorney's Office (N.D. Cal.) closed on 8/21/2025, and the *Barkasi* securities class action was dismissed with prejudice on 1/26/2026 with judgment entered 2/12/2026 (a plaintiff appeal is pending). Second, four insiders — including both the CEO and the CFO — made open-market purchases totalling roughly $2.17M between 5/29/2026 and 6/23/2026 at prices from $189.20 to $231.34, in the immediate aftermath of the market's negative reaction to the largest acquisition in company history. Third, the restructuring programme is in its stated final phase: the Q1 FY2027 charge was $30M versus $105M a year earlier, and headcount fell from ~15,300 to ~14,300 across FY2026.

We nonetheless move from BUY to HOLD, and the reason is arithmetic rather than deterioration. The stock has advanced roughly 13% from the level at which we last recommended purchase, with no intervening earnings report — Q1 was reported 5/28/2026 and Q2 FY2027 has not yet been published. Two new findings also warrant modest trims. Roughly 3-4 points of the reported 18% Q1 growth appears attributable to the new transaction model grossing up both revenue and marketing expense rather than to incremental customer demand, which is consistent with GAAP operating margin *falling* 20bps (22.1% to 21.9%) in a year of 17.5% revenue growth. And net revenue retention was "slightly above the range of 100%-110%" in constant currency at both 4/30/2026 and 4/30/2025 — flat, not improving. Base case $262 (+12%), bull $340 (+45%), bear $180 (-23%). That distribution is worth owning; it does not clear our bar for adding capital at this price.

## Company Overview

Autodesk sells design and engineering software as a subscription, operating as a single reportable segment with the CEO as chief operating decision maker. Q1 FY2027 revenue of $1,934M (+18% reported, +16% constant currency) split across four product families:

| Family | Q1 FY27 revenue | YoY | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| AECO (architecture, engineering, construction, operations) | $970M | +20% | 50% |
| AutoCAD & AutoCAD LT | $474M | +15% | 25% |
| Manufacturing | $367M | +19% | 19% |
| Media & Entertainment | $86M | +13% | 4% |
| Other | $37M | +32% | 2% |

Recurring revenue was $1,881M, or 97% of net revenue, unchanged from a year earlier. International was 65% of revenue; EMEA grew 21% reported but 16% in constant currency, a five-point FX tailwind that reverses if the dollar strengthens. Autodesk also reports a "Design vs. Make" cut: Design $1,612M (+18%), Make $224M (+25%).

The moat rests on three verifiable pillars. Revit and AutoCAD are the documentation substrate for multi-party AEC projects, so switching costs are a function of file formats, trained labour and collaboration workflow rather than feature parity. The 10-K discloses free educational licences of the full professional portfolio to verified students and institutions with the explicit stated aim of making Autodesk "the preferred choice for those poised to become the next generation of design, engineering, and construction professionals" — a funnel strategy, not an inference. An API-based architecture (Autodesk Platform Services, now with Model Context Protocol servers integrated) supports third-party developer lock-in.

The defining corporate event is the **MaintainX acquisition**: a definitive merger agreement signed 5/28/2026 for approximately **$3.6 billion in cash**, funded with debt plus available cash, expected to close later in fiscal 2027 subject to regulatory approvals. MaintainX builds maintenance, work-order and asset-record software for factory floors and field operations, and was reported at the announcement to be approaching $135M of annualized recurring revenue growing more than 50% annually. It becomes the anchor of a new "Autodesk Operations Solutions" unit alongside Tandem, FlexSim and Fusion Operations. This is Autodesk's largest acquisition ever, and it follows a fiscal year in which the company completed zero business combinations.

## Financial Analysis

### Income statement ($M, fiscal years ending 1/31)

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 5,005 | 5,497 | 6,131 | **7,206** |
| Gross profit | 4,525 | 4,986 | 5,553 | 6,556 |
| R&D | 1,219 | 1,373 | 1,485 | 1,643 |
| Operating income | 989 | 1,128 | 1,354 | **1,578** |
| Income tax expense | 123 | 230 | 272 | **479** |
| Net income | 823 | 906 | 1,112 | **1,124** |
| Diluted shares | 218 | 216 | 217 | 215 |

Derived: revenue growth of +9.8%, +11.5%, **+17.5%**; gross margin 91.0% in FY2026; operating margin 19.8% → 20.5% → 22.1% → **21.9%**; net margin 18.1% → 15.6%. **Net income grew 1.1% on 17.5% revenue growth.**

Two mechanics explain that gap, and both matter for how the business should be modelled.

**The transaction-model gross-up.** Under the new model, Solution Providers quote the customer but the transaction occurs directly between Autodesk and the customer, so most Solution Provider incentives are now capitalized as costs to obtain a contract and amortized through Marketing & Sales rather than netted against revenue. The supporting disclosures line up: indirect channel share of revenue fell from 58% (FY2025) to 37% (FY2026), and from 45% to 28% year over year in Q1; TD Synnex went from 39% of revenue (FY2024) to 33% to 14%; and amortization of costs to obtain a contract rose from $96M to $172M (+79%) in Q1 FY2027 while marketing & sales expense rose only 5% ($566M to $593M) because the gross-up was partly offset by lower headcount and the absence of a prior-year $54M cumulative ESPP catch-up. The $76M year-on-year increase in contract-cost amortization equals about 3.9% of Q1 revenue. If the gross-up is broadly proportionate on both lines, roughly **3-4 points of the reported 18% growth is presentation rather than incremental demand**, putting underlying constant-currency growth nearer 12%. This is a reconstruction from disclosed channel-mix and contract-cost figures, not a company-published number, and carries a ±1-2 point band; an alternative reading is simply higher gross new-business volume at unchanged commission economics, which would make less of the growth presentational. The effect is approximately neutral to operating profit dollars and to free cash flow dollars, and negative to *calculated* operating margin. The practical implication is that comparing Autodesk's headline growth to Bentley's or Nemetschek's without adjustment compares unlike things.

**The tax step-up.** The effective rate went from 19.7% (272/1,384) in FY2025 to **29.9%** (479/1,603) in FY2026 — driven by net CFC tested income provisions, reduced FDDEI benefit, and the R&D expensing election under legislation signed 7/4/2025. At a flat rate that 10.2-point increase cost roughly $164M of net income, which is essentially the entire reason GAAP net income was flat. Autodesk has filed a non-automatic accounting method change request with the IRS to cease capitalizing certain R&D expenditures in controlled foreign corporations; the effects are not yet recognized, and approval would reduce the provision through lower NCTI tax. The OECD "side-by-side" safe harbour announced 1/5/2026 exempts US-parented multinationals from certain Pillar Two provisions for years beginning on or after 1/1/2026. Q1 FY2027's rate was 18.0% (108/599), but that quarter was flattered by discrete items and a $56M non-taxable investment gain, so 18% is not a run rate. The asymmetry here is favourable and under-modelled: IRS approval is discretionary and unscheduled, so the downside is that nothing changes.

### Cash flow

FY2026 operating cash flow $2,452M, capex $43M, **free cash flow $2,409M** — a 33.4% FCF margin and 2.14x conversion of net income, with capex at 0.6% of revenue and net PP&E of just $121M on $12.5B of assets. Q1 FY2027 OCF was $893M against $564M, FCF $876M against $556M (+58%).

We will not annualize the Q1 figure. Autodesk's own 10-K warns that EBA sales concentrate in Q4 and that seasonality affects the relative value of billings, RPO and collections in Q4 and Q1; accounts receivable fell from $1,439M (1/31/26) to $579M (4/30/26), which is the collection cycle at work. As a labelled sensitivity only: FY2026's actual $2,409M applied to the FY2027 revenue guide midpoint of $8.185B at a flat 33.4% margin implies roughly $2.73B, before the interest cost of acquisition financing and FY2027 restructuring cash outflows.

### Balance sheet, and why the standard distress screen misfires

At 4/30/2026: cash and equivalents $2,671M, short-term securities $253M, long-term securities $385M = **$3,309M**, against $2,484M of net carrying borrowings (3.50% notes due June 2027 $500M; 2.85% due 2030 $500M; 2.40% due 2031 $1,000M; 5.30% due 2035 $500M). The $1.5B revolver (matures 5/8/2030, expandable to $2B) was undrawn. Covenant: Consolidated Covenant Debt / Consolidated EBITDA ≤ 3.50x, stepping to 4.00x for up to four quarters following certain acquisitions; in compliance. **Net cash of roughly $825M.** FY2026 interest expense was $25M against $1,578M of operating income — 63x coverage.

The current ratio of 0.851 (4,942/5,808 at 1/31/2026; 0.835 at 4/30/2026) and the accumulated deficit of $(1,432)M produce a distress reading on book-equity screens. Recomputing the classic public-firm Altman Z with market equity gives X4 = 49,300/9,422 = 5.23, and Z ≈ 3.89 — the safe zone. The sub-1.0 current ratio is guaranteed by $4,457M of deferred revenue sitting in current liabilities; that liability is prepaid customer cash discharged by delivering software, not by paying cash out. The accumulated deficit and the small $3,045M book equity are artefacts of a decade of repurchases and the perpetual-to-subscription transition. This is a structural misclassification of buyback-heavy subscription software, not a solvency signal.

The genuine leverage question is the acquisition. If the full $3.6B were debt-funded, gross borrowings go to ~$6.08B and net debt from roughly −$0.8B to ~+$2.8B. Against FY2026 operating income of $1,578M plus $195M D&A plus stock-based compensation, a covenant-style EBITDA proxy of $2.4-2.6B is plausible, implying **post-close net leverage of roughly 1.1-1.2x** against a 3.50x covenant. Caveats: the credit agreement's EBITDA definition was not read and may differ materially; the 8-K of 6/15/2026 (Items 1.01 and 2.03) almost certainly contains the actual debt quantum and pricing and was not obtained. On an illustrative 5.0% blended cost, $3.6B of debt implies $180M of pre-tax interest, ~$148M after tax at 18%, or **~$0.70 per share on 212M diluted shares — about 5.6% of the FY2027 guidance midpoint.** MaintainX's ~$135M ARR partly offsets on the revenue line, but a private company growing above 50% is unlikely to be profitable, so a mid-single-digit percentage EPS headwind is the plausible near-term net effect.

Share count is declining: 215M diluted in FY2026, 212M in Q1 FY2027, with 1.864M shares repurchased at an average $239.87 ($448M) in Q1 alone and **$7.04B of remaining authorization** — 14.3% of market capitalization. No dividend, and the company states it does not anticipate one. The unvested RSU/PSU pool of roughly 6.1M units is about 2.9% of shares outstanding, is subject to service and performance conditions (revenue, non-GAAP operating income, FCF and relative-TSR gates), and is currently more than offset by repurchase.

## Growth Analysis

Guided FY2027 revenue of $8.155-8.215B implies **+13.2% to +14.0%** growth off the $7,206M base, with adjusted EPS of $12.40-$12.65. Both figures explicitly exclude MaintainX and will be rebased once the deal closes. Consensus sits at $12.62 for the current year and $14.26 for next year — the current-year figure slightly above the guidance midpoint, consistent with a management team that has beaten in each of the last four quarters (Phase 1 surprises: +4.8%, +4.8%, +5.7%, +3.1%). Estimate revisions are stable rather than inflecting: +2.5% for the current quarter and +1.5% for the current year over 90 days, +1.1% for next year. This is not the >30% revision velocity that signals a cyclical earnings inflection; it is a company delivering close to plan.

Adjusting for the presentational gross-up, underlying constant-currency growth is plausibly nearer 12%. Two signals argue against extrapolating acceleration. Net revenue retention is flat at "slightly above 100%-110%" constant currency in both periods, which means expansion within the installed base is steady, not widening. And total RPO fell to $7,808M at 4/30/2026 from $8,300M at 1/31/2026 (−6%), with non-current RPO down 14% to $2,425M and unbilled deferred revenue down to $3,351M from $3,607M. Three scope limits apply: this is a sequential comparison off the Q4 EBA seasonal peak that the 10-K expressly warns distorts Q4/Q1 RPO; RPO is narrowly defined as short-term plus long-term plus unbilled deferred revenue recognized over roughly three years, not colloquial backlog; and the disclosed transition to annual billings on multi-year contracts mechanically compresses non-current RPO without demand deterioration. The year-over-year figure is the single number we would most want and do not have.

Operating leverage is real but its Q1 expression is overstated. GAAP operating margin went from 14.3% to 28.0% as total operating expenses *fell* 2% ($1,240M to $1,218M), driven by restructuring down $75M, SBC down $75M ($230M to $155M, with the prior year carrying the $54M ESPP catch-up), and headcount reduction. Roughly half of that expansion is the absence of prior-year one-offs, so 28% is not a run rate. Revenue per employee rose from $401K to $504K (+25.7%), though part of that is the revenue gross-up.

Where genuine growth optionality sits: Make revenue +25% (Fusion, Forma, Autodesk Build); construction management tools folding into Forma to create a design-to-construction-to-operations platform; and MaintainX extending into asset operations data. Management's stated logic is that operations expansion "would extend the duration of its customer relationships from years to decades." At ~1.9% of FY2026 revenue, MaintainX cannot move the consolidated growth rate quickly even if the strategic reasoning is sound.

Macro is a headwind that deserves explicit weight. AECO is 50% of revenue and depends on construction activity; manufacturing, at 19% of revenue, faces an unusually detailed tariff chronology in the filings (Section 232 from March 2025; IEEPA reciprocal tariffs rescinded 2/24/2026 after a Supreme Court decision, replaced by a 10% Section 122 temporary import surcharge). Autodesk sells software, so tariff exposure is second-order through customer capex deferral, but the depth of disclosure suggests management treats it as a live demand variable.

## Valuation Assessment

| Metric | Value at $234.20 |
|---|---|
| Forward P/E on FY2027 guidance midpoint ($12.525) | **18.7x** |
| Forward P/E on current-year consensus ($12.62) | 18.6x |
| Forward P/E on next-year consensus ($14.26) | 16.4x |
| Trailing TTM GAAP P/E (provider) | ~34x |
| EV/FY2026 FCF (EV ≈ $48.6B) | 20.2x |
| FY2026 FCF yield on market cap | **4.87%** |
| EV/FY2026 revenue | 6.7x |
| PEG (18.7x ÷ 13.9% guided-to-consensus EPS growth) | ~1.35 |

The multiple divergence needs naming. The provider's 34.3x trailing figure reconciles to trailing-twelve-month GAAP EPS of roughly $6.82 (the provider reports EPS of $5.23, which does not reconcile to the 34.3x P/E at $234.20; the trailing multiple should be recomputed from the 10-K reconciliation before use). Q1's $56M of non-cash upward fair-value marks on privately held strategic investments contribute about $0.22 per share after tax to the trailing GAAP line. The 18.7x forward figure is a non-GAAP-to-non-GAAP comparison. Both answer different questions; the forward non-GAAP figure is the one that maps to how the sell side and management frame the business, and the 4.87% FCF yield is the cleanest cash-based anchor because it is unaffected by the transaction-model gross-up.

Against the peer set, the discount is real. Application-software comparables carry a median trailing P/E of 40.7x (FICO 37.6, WDAY 40.7, SNPS 108.1, OKTA 85.1, INTU 16.9), all with zero-to-minimal dividend support. Autodesk at ~34x trailing and 18.7x forward, with 91% gross margins, 97% recurring revenue and a 33% FCF margin, is priced below the group. Peer 52-week drawdowns (WDAY −41%, SNPS −43%, INTU −64%, FICO −33%) confirm this is a sector-wide derating of application software, not a company-specific verdict. BNP Paribas initiated on 6/18/2026 with Outperform on both Autodesk and Bentley Systems, arguing that "debate around AI's impact to manufacturing and construction software has cratered valuations."

What the market is pricing at a 28.8% discount to the 52-week high (and, in June, a roughly 43% discount) has to be named specifically. The most defensible reading is terminal-value compression: if AI reduces the number of drafters, designers and engineers per project, a per-seat subscription model loses volume even while retaining pricing power, and the appropriate exit multiple falls. Autodesk's own 10-K concedes the risk in unusually direct language — "disruptive technologies such as machine learning and other AI technologies may significantly alter the market for our products in unpredictable ways and reduce customer demand" — alongside acknowledgments of "limited barriers to entry" and cloud/mobile lowering barriers further. Layered on top is scepticism about paying 26.7x current-year ARR ($3,600M / $135M; ~17.7x forward ARR at >50% growth) for entry into a maintenance/CMMS category where Autodesk's incumbency and channel advantages are weakest, and where its historical construction integrations (PlanGrid, BuildingConnected) produced a franchise now being renamed and folded into Forma.

Consensus targets are $220.50 low, $310 median, $313.89 mean, $456 high. The large published targets are stale: JPMorgan's $319 (2/2/2026) and RBC's $340 (2/11/2026) both predate the MaintainX announcement and the subsequent derating, and neither has been rebased for the financing. Analyst targets can be treated as floors on stocks that are cheap with rising estimates; here estimates are only mildly rising (+1.5% CY over 90 days) and the targets are demonstrably not re-based, so we weight them as corroboration of direction rather than as an independent target.

Our base case: 19x an all-in FY2028 EPS of roughly $13.80 (the $14.26 consensus less approximately $0.70 of acquisition interest, partly offset by MaintainX revenue) = **$262**, or +12% from spot. Bull case assumes the AI discount unwinds as seat data fails to deteriorate and MaintainX demonstrates cross-sell: 24x $14.26 = **$340** (+45%), which coincides with RBC's standing target. Bear case assumes visible seat erosion or a construction-driven deceleration to high-single-digit growth with the multiple compressing to 14x $12.50 = **$180** (−23%), roughly the June trough. The distribution is mildly favourable but no longer asymmetric enough to justify adding at this price, which is the core of the downgrade.

## Competitive Landscape

Named competitors in the 10-K: Adobe, Bentley Systems, Dassault Systèmes (including SolidWorks), Intergraph (Hexagon), MSC Software, Nemetschek, Oracle, Procore, PTC, 3D Systems, Siemens PLM and Trimble. The filing describes "vigorous competition in each of the vertical markets," competition "increasingly enhanced by consolidation of companies with complementary products," and a platform shift to cloud and mobile that lowers entry barriers.

Trajectory reads stable-to-marginally-eroding rather than improving, which is why we hold competitive position at 6.5 rather than the 8.5 assigned earlier in the cycle. Supporting evidence for stability: AECO grew 20% and Manufacturing 19% in Q1, plausibly at or above end-market growth; recurring revenue held at 97%; and the education-licence funnel plus file-format lock-in remain intact. Evidence for erosion: net revenue retention is flat rather than expanding, which is what would appear first if competitive intensity were rising or if customers were rationalizing seats; AutoCAD and AutoCAD LT remain 25% of revenue (26% in FY2025), so product concentration in the most commoditizable, most AI-exposed part of the portfolio has barely moved; and the company is entering CMMS against established maintenance-software vendors without incumbency.

Management's AI counter-argument is coherent: parametric and physics-based 3D technology acts as a verification layer that tests AI output against real-world physical constraints, which makes owning operational asset data strategically valuable. Autodesk claims investment in "development, scaling, and monetization of agentic AI" across proprietary data, contextual integration and specialized expertise. No disclosed metric validates monetization, so this remains framing rather than evidence.

One concentration nuance matters. The TD Synnex decline from 39% to 14% of revenue is a reduction in *channel* concentration, not *end-customer* concentration — Autodesk now transacts directly with largely the same customers. TD Synnex still represented 10% of trade receivables at 4/30/2026, up from 5% at 1/31/2026, and the 10-K retains the risk that losing a major distributor or Solution Provider "could harm our business."

## Risk Assessment

**AI demand-model disruption.** Management's own words, quoted above. This is the risk setting the multiple, and it cannot be resolved from filings. The observable tell would be net revenue retention falling below 100% or AutoCAD family growth decelerating materially while AECO holds — a mix signature of seat rationalization rather than budget compression.

**MaintainX completion and integration.** A newly added risk factor states Autodesk "cannot guarantee that the acquisition of MaintainX, Inc. will be consummated within the expected time period, or at all," with new language on termination fees. Regulatory approvals are outstanding. The financing terms are not verified in what we have read; the 6/15/2026 8-K (Items 1.01 and 2.03) almost certainly discloses them, and the 7/13/2026 8-K (Item 8.01) contents are unknown. Our leverage and EPS-drag numbers are sensitivities built on an illustrative 5.0% blended cost, not facts.

**RPO decline.** The 14% sequential fall in non-current RPO is a legitimate watch item that we cannot interpret without the 4/30/2025 comparison, given disclosed Q4 EBA seasonality and the annual-billing transition.

**Governance and capital allocation.** The pay-versus-performance table shows Autodesk's TSR index at 91.15 against a peer index at 133.77 after five fiscal years — a cumulative −8.9% for shareholders against +33.8% for peers, having trailed in every one of the five years shown — while CEO total compensation rose 19.4% to a record $30.09M in FY2026. "Compensation actually paid" did fall from $34.28M to $24.45M as the mark-to-market mechanism transmitted the share-price decline, and relative TSR is one of the plan measures. Both the 10-K and 10-Q retain a risk factor referencing "actions by activist shareholders," though no holder is named in what we reviewed; any specific attribution is unconfirmed. The three financial incentive metrics — total revenue, non-GAAP operating income and free cash flow — are precisely the metrics implicated in the March 2024 Audit Committee investigation, and revenue as a comp metric sits awkwardly alongside a transaction-model change that mechanically grosses revenue up. The committee may well adjust targets for that gross-up; we did not read the Compensation Discussion & Analysis and draw no inference about intent.

**Residual litigation.** The class-action dismissal with prejudice is under appeal (notice 3/12/2026, opening brief 5/27/2026), and the consolidated derivative action is stayed rather than dismissed. Reversal on appeal would revive the matter.

**FX and cyclicality.** A five-point currency tailwind in EMEA reverses if the dollar strengthens; cash flow hedge notional was $2.27B at 4/30/2026. Construction and discrete-manufacturing capex are the demand drivers.

**Non-cash earnings quality.** Q1's $56M of net strategic-investment gains represent about 9.3% of $599M pre-tax income and roughly $0.22 of GAAP EPS. Cumulatively since inception the programme carries $89M of upward adjustments against $(141)M of downward — a net $(52)M, so these marks have historically been negative. They do not touch revenue, operating income or cash flow, and are excluded from non-GAAP operating measures.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 47.7% against 35.8% one-year historical volatility is rich, and the term structure is in backwardation (47.7% at one week versus 45.4% at six months), which prices event risk rather than a change in the volatility regime — the IV percentile within the current chain is only 15%, so the absolute level is not extreme by the stock's own recent standards. The September tenor implies a ±14.7% move and spans the unreported Q2 FY2027 result, the single largest near-term catalyst. Positioning is mixed in an informative way: put/call open interest of 2.35 indicates substantial standing downside protection, while put/call volume of 0.90 and call-tilted near-term skew (−4.1% at one week, −17.8% at one month on the provider's convention) indicate recent flow leaning long. The 3-month and 6-month put/call volume ratios of 11.45 and 56.00 sit on thin volume and should not be read directionally. Short interest is 4.6% of float with 2.9 days to cover — uncrowded, with no squeeze mechanics in either direction. The composite read supports our HOLD: the market is paying for protection into a binary print while trading the recovery, which is a reasonable description of where the fundamentals sit.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $340 (+45%)

The AI derating proves excessive because engineering documentation requires physics-grounded verification that a language model cannot supply, and Autodesk owns the parametric substrate. Seat counts hold, net revenue retention stays above 100%, and FY2028 revenue growth sustains low-double-digits ex-gross-up. The restructuring's final phase (charges down to $30M in Q1 from $105M) delivers recurring operating leverage rather than one-off comparisons, and the IRS method change is approved, moving the effective tax rate back toward the low 20s and adding meaningfully to EPS versus a 29.9% base. MaintainX closes, retains its >50% growth into a channel of AECO and manufacturing customers, and establishes an operations data layer that lengthens contract duration. The $7.04B buyback authorization — 14% of market cap — retires shares at 16-19x forward earnings. At 24x $14.26 the stock returns to RBC's standing $340, and the cleared regulatory and litigation overhang removes the governance discount that has sat on the multiple since 2024.

### Bear Case — $180 (−23%)

Underlying growth is nearer 12% once the transaction-model gross-up is stripped out, and flat net revenue retention means there is no expansion engine to offset a construction cycle turning down. Non-current RPO's 14% sequential decline turns out to reflect shortening commitment duration driven by customer caution, not just billing-term mix. AI begins to show in seat data — the AutoCAD family, 25% of revenue, decelerates first. MaintainX closes at 26.7x ARR, absorbs management attention, adds roughly $0.70 of interest drag against negligible near-term profit, and the operations thesis proves too small at 1.9% of revenue to matter. The multiple compresses to 14x, which on $12.50 of EPS is $180 — the June low. A pay-versus-performance record showing five straight years of underperformance against peers alongside record CEO compensation offers little confidence that capital allocation improves from here.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** We see a positive 12-month path with identifiable catalysts, but the 12% gain to base case at $234.20 does not justify new capital when the same thesis was available near $207 four weeks ago and the next binary — Q2 FY2027 — carries a ±14.7% implied move.

**Base case $262 (+12%)** — 19x an all-in FY2028 EPS of roughly $13.80 ($14.26 consensus less ~$0.70 of acquisition interest, partly offset by MaintainX revenue contribution). **Bull $340 (+45%)** — 24x $14.26 on AI-discount unwind plus tax normalization. **Bear $180 (−23%)** — 14x $12.50 on seat erosion or construction-driven deceleration.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Price at or below $200-210** with the FY2027 guide intact — restores 25%+ upside to base case, and is the level at which the CEO, CFO and two directors bought with their own cash.
2. **Q2 FY2027 (expected late August) showing net revenue retention above 110% constant currency, or year-over-year total RPO growth above 10%** — either would directly falsify the demand-deceleration leg of the bear case and is the specific data point we currently lack.
3. **IRS approval of the R&D method change, or a guided FY2028 effective tax rate at or below 24%** — a favourable but unquantified tax swing against a multiple that does not appear to embed it.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **Net revenue retention printing below 100% constant currency**, or AutoCAD-family growth decelerating below 8% year over year while AECO holds — the mix signature of AI-driven seat rationalization.
2. **MaintainX financing disclosed at materially more than $3.6B of debt, or post-close net leverage above 2.5x** on the credit agreement's own EBITDA definition.
3. **FY2027 revenue guidance cut below $8.0B**, or a second consecutive quarter of declining year-over-year total RPO, which would confirm construction-cycle transmission into committed contracts.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q2 FY2027 report in late August 2026 is the primary checkpoint, and the first opportunity to obtain a year-over-year RPO comparison and a post-announcement read on net revenue retention. Secondary checkpoints are the MaintainX closing 8-K (financing terms and purchase accounting) and any 8-K disclosing IRS action on the method change.

## Conclusion

Autodesk is a durable, cash-generative franchise being priced for a risk that its own filings concede cannot be ruled out. The quality evidence is strong and specific: 91% gross margins, 97% recurring revenue, $2,409M of free cash flow on $43M of capex, roughly $825M of net cash before the acquisition and about 1.1-1.2x post-close leverage against a 3.50x covenant, four consecutive earnings beats, a declining share count with $7.04B of authorization remaining, and the resolution of both government inquiries and the securities class action that compressed the multiple through 2024-2025. The insider signal is the strongest single forward-looking data point in the file: open-market purchases by the CEO, CFO and two directors at $189-231, in the immediate aftermath of the market punishing their largest-ever capital-allocation decision, with no open-market sales in the transactions reviewed.

Set against that: roughly 3-4 points of the headline 18% growth appears presentational rather than demand-driven, net revenue retention is flat rather than expanding, non-current RPO fell 14% sequentially with no year-over-year figure available to interpret it, the AECO franchise faces a construction cycle, and the company is paying 26.7x current-year ARR to enter a category where its advantages are weakest — while a five-year record of trailing its peer index by 42.6 index points sits alongside record CEO compensation.

The balance is favourable enough to own and not favourable enough to add at $234.20. We recommended purchase near $207; the position has worked, and the 12% remaining gain to base case is now roughly half the implied move the options market prices for the September tenor. Hold the position, do not add here, and treat $200-210 or a Q2 print that resolves the retention and RPO questions as the next actionable point.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the report's trailing GAAP P/E line asserted a $44.8x multiple on a $5.23 FY2026 GAAP EPS and a reconciliation to ~$6.90 TTM GAAP EPS, neither of which is verifiable from the sources (the provider reports EPS $5.23 with a 34.3x trailing P/E that does not reconcile at $234.20); several macro data points (30-year mortgage at 6.66%, "one-year high," "fourth consecutive weekly increase," China PMI contraction) were not traceable to the brief or Phase 1 data and have been removed; and the Phase 1 earnings-surprise percentages (+4.8%/+4.8%/+5.7%/+3.1%) do not correspond to Autodesk's fiscal quarters as labelled and are tagged to their vendor source. These affect valuation presentation and macro framing but not the core cash-flow and balance-sheet evidence underpinning the HOLD.
