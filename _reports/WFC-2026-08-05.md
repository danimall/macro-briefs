## Executive Summary

Wells Fargo's second quarter was the best-looking print of the post-asset-cap era: GAAP diluted EPS of $2.00 against LSEG consensus of $1.71, revenue of $22.62B versus $21.84B expected, ROTCE of 17.73% (up 254bp year over year), an efficiency ratio of 60% (from 64%), and tangible book value per share of $46.13 (+6.8%). Management reaffirmed 2026 guidance of approximately $50B of net interest income and ~$55.7B of expenses, and raised the quarterly dividend to $0.50 from $0.45. Consensus current-year EPS has moved to $7.30 from $6.98 ninety days ago (+4.6%), and next year to $7.96.

The composition of that beat is the reason the stock fell roughly 3% intraday on the print and why we remain at HOLD for a fifth consecutive review. Net gains from equity securities were $847M against $119M a year earlier, a $728M swing equal to 45% of the $1.609B pre-tax income increase. Net of the offsetting rise in noncontrolling-interest attribution ($238M from $28M), the shareholder-attributable uplift is roughly $518M pre-tax, or about 1.2 points of ROTCE against average tangible common equity near $138.8B. A one-time tax benefit worth $0.04 of EPS adds roughly another 0.35 points. Clean-basis Q2 ROTCE was therefore approximately 16.2%, at the low end of management's 17–18% medium-term target. A further ~28% of the year-over-year EPS increase came from the 5.9% reduction in diluted shares. Underneath, net interest margin fell 25bp to 2.43%, and net interest income excluding Markets grew 1.83% in the quarter and 2.02% in the first half. Reported NII growth of ~5% is substantially a Markets-balance-sheet artifact: CIB assets rose 31%, trading assets 52% to $445B, and securities borrowed/purchased under resale agreements 101% to $201B.

The setup into the second half is finely balanced. To reach the ~$48B non-Markets NII component of guidance, second-half non-Markets NII must be $24.569B against $23.431B in the first half, a +4.9% sequential-half step-up against +2.0% first-half year-over-year growth. Disclosed sensitivity is +$1.3B of NII per +100bp and −$1.9B per −100bp; the franchise is asset-sensitive and structurally hurt by Fed cuts. The offsetting problem is that a higher-for-longer path stresses the books Wells Fargo has been growing fastest: loans to non-depository financials of $208.8B (~20% of loans, the largest single industry concentration) with $318.9B of commitments, auto loans up 13% in six months on originations up 41% year over year, and 662k new card accounts (+46%). At 1.93x tangible book, roughly the multiple a 16% clean ROTCE, 10–10.5% cost-of-equity bank should command, the price already reflects the improvement, and the incremental return over twelve months looks like the ~7% tangible book compounding plus the 2.24% dividend.

## Company Overview

Wells Fargo is the fourth-largest US bank holding company, with approximately $2.28 trillion of consolidated assets at June 30, 2026 (+15% year over year), ~$1.4 trillion of deposits, and roughly $1.05 trillion of loans (+12%). Total equity was $183.0B at year-end 2025.

Four segments: Consumer Banking and Lending (branches, deposits, card, auto, home lending); Commercial Banking; Corporate and Investment Banking (corporate lending, treasury/payments, investment banking, commercial real estate, Markets); and Wealth and Investment Management, which held $2,409B of client assets at June 30, 2026 (+15%).

The organizing fact of the current investment case is regulatory. The Federal Reserve removed the $1.95 trillion asset cap on June 3, 2025. Wells Fargo has terminated 14 consent orders since 2019, eight of them in 2025 and early 2026, exited 12 businesses, delivered roughly $15B of gross expense savings across 2021–2025, and reduced average common shares outstanding by 26%. Two supervisory instruments remain: other provisions of the 2018 Federal Reserve consent order, and the OCC formal agreement of September 12, 2024 covering AML/BSA and sanctions compliance. The company's proxy language emphasizing "termination of all outstanding consent orders" is literally accurate, since a formal agreement is a distinct instrument, but it invites a more complete all-clear reading than the 10-K supports.

A note on screening data: standardized financial-statement pulls for this issuer map to the parent-company-only condensed balance sheet (~$208B of assets, ~$26B of revenue) rather than the consolidated entity, and manufacturer-calibrated distress scores are undefined for a deposit-funded balance sheet. All figures here come from Wells Fargo's filings and company-published materials, using bank-appropriate metrics (CET1, SLR, NCO rate, ACL coverage, NIM, efficiency ratio).

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 baseline.** Revenue $83.7B; net income $21.34B; net income to common $20.285B; diluted EPS $6.26; ROE 12.35%; ROTCE 14.61%; book value per share $53.24; tangible book value per share $45.02; CET1 (Standardized) 10.6%. Two observations frame the recovery honestly. FY2025 ROTCE of 14.61% was still marginally below the 14.76% achieved in 2021, and tangible book value per share compounded at 5.1% annually from $33.43 in 2019, respectable through a rate cycle with heavy repurchases but not exceptional. Average share count fell from 4,134M in 2019 to 3,093M in 2025, so a large share of per-share progress is buyback arithmetic rather than expanding returns on capital.

**Q2 2026 quality.** Revenue +8.6%, expenses +~2%, net income $6.411B, EPS $2.00, ROTCE 17.73%, efficiency 60%. Four drivers, of which two are durable:

- *Expense discipline (durable).* Headcount fell to 197,466 from 212,804 (−7%), and is down roughly 79,000 over six years with further reductions signalled. Gross expense savings were ~$2.4B in 2025. Morningstar raised its fair value to $90 from $88 principally by cutting its midcycle efficiency assumption 50bp to 58.2% on dissipating legacy compliance costs.
- *Credit costs (cyclical).* Net charge-offs 0.34% annualized versus 0.44%; allowance 1.40% of loans versus 1.45%; CRE mortgage criticized $11.8B versus $13.4B; office loans $20.0B versus $21.96B with $1.734B of office nonaccrual. Real improvement, but reserve-ratio releases cannot repeat indefinitely.
- *Equity-securities marks (low quality).* $847M versus $119M, ~1.2 points of the 254bp ROTCE gain net of NCI attribution.
- *Share count (mechanical).* Diluted shares 3,074.6M from 3,267.0M. Holding shares flat gives $1.88 of EPS instead of $2.00, roughly $0.12 of the $0.40 increase.

**The margin problem.** NIM 2.43%, down 25bp year over year and 17bp over two quarters. Non-Markets NII grew 1.83% in Q2. The incremental balance sheet went overwhelmingly into securities financing and trading inventory: resale/borrowed securities +101% to $201B, trading assets +52% to $445B, Markets loans +36%, CIB C&I loans +32%. Management's defense, that financing balances carry low spread but good returns on allocated capital and anchor higher-margin ancillary business, is standard dealer economics and is supported by the fee data: Markets revenue +24% (FICC +10.4%, equities ~+64%), investment banking fees ~+36% to a record quarter, CIB revenue +16%. NIM is a poor yardstick for a repo book. The valid criticism is that the deposit-cost advantage was the historical moat and it is being levered into the least differentiated business in banking.

**Capital.** CET1 (Standardized, the binding framework) has moved 11.13% → 10.61% → 10.29% → 10.26% across the last four quarter-ends against an 8.50% requirement, implying ~176bp of excess on ~$1,342B of risk-weighted assets ≈ $23.6B. The erosion has decelerated sharply (−52bp in 2H 2025, −32bp in Q1, −3bp in Q2). Reconciling: ~$6.1B of quarterly earnings to common less ~$1.4B of dividends and $3.0B of buyback leaves ~$1.7B of accretion, against ~$2.8B needed to fund 9% annual RWA growth at a constant ratio, an ~8bp implied drift versus 3bp observed. Two readings compete: the build is digested and capital is stabilizing, or Q2 repurchases were throttled ($3.0B versus ~$4.1B in Q1) specifically to arrest the slide, in which case stabilization constrains future buyback capacity. Leverage ratios fell far faster than the risk-based ratio (SLR 6.67% → 5.78%; Tier 1 leverage 8.01% → 6.94%), so CET1 understates how much balance-sheet capacity has been consumed. Separately, the required minimum SLR fell from 5.00% at year-end 2025 to 3.50% at mid-2026 (3.00% minimum plus half the method-one G-SIB surcharge). That loosened the constraint that historically made large repo books uneconomic; attributing it to finalized enhanced-SLR reform is inference, though the ratio change itself is a filing fact.

**Capital return.** $22.7B remains of the $40B authorization after 1H repurchases of 84M shares for $7.1B (~$84.5 average, below the current price). Q2 returned ~$4.4B including $1.4B of dividends. The dividend rises to $0.50 quarterly for Q3, $2.00 annualized, a 2.24% forward yield. If second-half repurchases matched the first half, full-year buybacks of ~$14.2B would equal ~5.3% of market capitalization for a combined ~7.5% shareholder yield. Treat that as a sensitivity: it assumes no change in pace alongside continued 9% RWA growth.

**Items a screen misses.** Held-to-maturity securities carried ~$32.4B of unrealized losses at June 30 (roughly 23% of tangible common equity) that do not flow through capital; AFS net unrealized loss ~$3.3B. Monetization is an unlikely tail for a bank with $1.4T of deposits, but it is a real drag on economic book value. Tax-credit investments generated $704M of net income in 1H 2026 (versus $625M), with $10.3B of renewable-energy tax-equity investments and a 14.9% effective tax rate; several hundred million dollars of annual earnings depend on continued federal credit availability.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus has EPS moving from $7.30 this year to $7.96 next (+9.0%), with current-year estimates revised +4.3% in 30 days and +1.2% for next-year in 90 days. This is positive earnings momentum of ordinary magnitude, not the velocity that would justify overriding valuation and risk considerations. The quarterly beat record is mixed: +15.2%, +0.1%, −4.0%, +6.4% over the last four quarters.

Growth sources, ranked by durability:

1. **Expense reduction.** The most reliable line. Efficiency against a midcycle assumption near 58% implies further operating leverage even on flat revenue, and headcount is still falling.
2. **Fee businesses.** Investment banking fees at a record with market share stable at 4.3% (Dealogic) and US announced-M&A rank improving to 8th from 12th. WIM has inflected: net flows +$4.6B in Q2 and +$12.4B in 1H, against −$2.4B and −$8.1B in the comparable 2025 periods, with 32.4% return on allocated capital. Thirteen consecutive quarters of year-over-year consumer primary checking growth supports the deposit base.
3. **Non-Markets NII.** The weakest link and the one that matters most. The CFO publicly reiterated confidence on July 15. Loan growth of 12% and continued fixed-asset repricing provide the mechanical basis, but the franchise is asset-sensitive, and Morningstar's constructive view partly rests on an assumption that rate cuts are delayed until 2027, so it is partly a rates call. NII and NIM both came in slightly below consensus in Q2.
4. **Consumer credit expansion.** Auto loans $50.5B → $56.9B in six months with Q2 originations $9.7B versus $6.9B (+41%), aided by a financing partnership launched in Q2 2025 whose counterparty and retained-risk economics are not disclosed. Sub-620 FICO balances are $6.1B, or 10.7% of auto. Card accounts +46% with advertising and promotion expense +36% in the quarter and +63% for the half. This adds revenue now; under CECL it front-loads provisions and back-loads charge-offs, so the credit cost of the 2025–2026 vintages will not be visible until 2027–2028.

Home Lending is subject to prevailing mortgage-rate and origination-volume conditions, which the provided sources do not quantify for this period; its directional contribution here is [unverified].

## Valuation Assessment

At $89.125 the shares trade at approximately:

- **12.9x trailing** twelve-month EPS of roughly $6.88 (FY2025 $6.26 less 1H 2025 $2.98 plus 1H 2026 $3.60; the half-year figures are derived and the multiple approximate)
- **12.2x** current-year consensus $7.30
- **11.2x** next-year consensus $7.96
- **1.93x tangible book** of $46.13; roughly 1.63x book value of ~$54–55 (extrapolated)
- **2.24% forward dividend yield**, plus a buyback yield near 5.3% at the first-half pace

Against peer medians of 13.6x P/E and 2.9% yield — Citigroup 17.2x, Bank of America 13.7x, PNC 13.6x, Truist 12.5x, U.S. Bancorp 12.1x — Wells Fargo sits modestly below the median earnings multiple with a below-median dividend yield. The earnings-multiple discount is defensible rather than anomalous: clean-basis Q2 ROTCE near 16.2% is respectable but the revenue mix carries more market and balance-sheet risk than the regional peers and less trading scale than the money-center leaders.

The tangible-book test is the more informative one for a bank. Warranted P/TBV approximates (ROTCE − g) ÷ (COE − g). At 16.2% clean ROTCE, 4% growth, and a 10% cost of equity, that yields 2.03x; at 10.5% cost of equity, 1.88x. The observed 1.93x sits inside that band. The stock is close to fair value on its current earning power, which is also where the external anchors land: Morningstar fair value $90 (three stars, wide moat, medium uncertainty), CNBC Investing Club hold-equivalent with a $95 target. Sell-side consensus mean is $100.02 with a median of $100, low $90, and high $115; the lowest published target is above spot, which caps rather than expands the perceived downside case.

A second construction converges on the same place. Tangible book compounded 6.8% year over year; applying ~7% forward growth to $46.13 gives ~$49.4, and holding the multiple at 1.93x gives ~$95.3 plus $2.00 of dividends, or roughly $97.3 of one-year total value.

Two cautions on treating the ~12x multiple as cheap. First, roughly 1.5 points of the reported 17.7% ROTCE is not repeatable in the same form, so the multiple applied to a cleaner earnings base is nearer 13x than 12x. Second, the price has recovered 22% from the May 15 low near $73 and sits 8.8% below the 52-week high of $97.76, so the market has already re-rated the improvement. Prior appreciation is not itself a valuation argument, but the absence of a compressed multiple against rising estimates removes the asymmetry that would justify a BUY. The valuation score of 5.7 reflects fair value with roughly 10% of identifiable upside to a defensible base case, supported by the ~7.5% shareholder yield rather than by multiple expansion.

## Competitive Landscape

The deposit franchise remains the moat: ~$1.4 trillion of granular, low-cost funding, thirteen consecutive quarters of primary checking growth, and a national branch footprint that Morningstar characterizes as cost-advantaged with a wide-moat rating.

The trajectory question is whether the post-asset-cap deployment strengthens or dilutes that position. The capital went predominantly into securities financing and trading inventory, where JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Citigroup, and the large European dealers are entrenched and where returns are cyclical and balance-sheet-intensive. Equities revenue up ~64% off a small base is simultaneously strong growth and modest scale. Investment banking share is stable at 4.3% with M&A rank improving four places, genuine progress measured in tenths of a point. Morningstar's objection is direct: sacrificing the relative strength of a cost-advantaged deposit franchise to grow trading is unlikely to improve competitive positioning within the banking universe.

The peer quarter was uniformly strong: roughly $49B of combined profit at the five largest US banks, JPMorgan raising its NII outlook, Bank of America guiding to the upper end of 6–8% net income growth. Wells Fargo's beat is therefore less differentiating than the headline suggests, and the market's willingness to sell it on an NII shortfall despite the beat is consistent with investors treating the revenue mix as lower quality than peers'.

Wells Fargo does have the most operating leverage to deregulation among large US banks, because it was the most constrained: the asset cap gone, required SLR down from 5.00% to 3.50%, a Basel endgame re-proposal, and a G-SIB surcharge methodology change the company expects to be immaterial. The corollary is that it is also the most exposed to a reversal in the regulatory cycle. Net trajectory: stable-to-improving in fees and expenses, deteriorating in earnings-mix quality — a 5.8.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Non-depository financial institution concentration (the least-priced risk).** "Financials except banks" loans of $208.8B, roughly 20% of the book and the largest single industry concentration, with $318.9B of commitments. The fastest-growing components are the most topical: real estate finance $34.5B → $41.0B (+19%) and consumer finance $27.8B → $31.9B (+15%) in six months. Regulators are actively examining the channel: the FSB issued its first dedicated private-credit vulnerability report on May 6, the Fed has been seeking more detail on banks' "very opaque" private credit lending, and its June Supervision and Regulation Report paired robust headline capitalization with intensifying private-credit and real-estate risk. Trade press reports that competition to finance CRE private-credit lenders is producing better rates, higher leverage, and less recourse, precisely the sub-segment where Wells Fargo grew 19% in six months. Stated precisely: Wells Fargo is the senior lender and the funds are the borrowers, typically with LP capital-call backing, overcollateralization, and borrowing-base tests, so this is not equivalent to owning the underlying assets. The risk is that structural protections are competed away and look-through collateral deteriorates faster than the structures assume. Wells Fargo discloses balances and commitments but no charge-off or nonaccrual data specific to this category, so credit quality in its largest concentration is unverified in either direction.

**2. The second-half NII test.** The +4.9% sequential-half step-up required against +2.0% first-half growth, in a franchise with −$1.9B of NII sensitivity per 100bp of cuts. A third consecutive quarter of NII disappointment would be a de-rating catalyst.

**3. Back-loaded consumer credit.** Auto NCOs already rose to 0.39% from 0.29%. The 2025–2026 auto and card vintages will not show their loss content until 2027–2028. The disclosed 10.7% sub-620 auto share is not a subprime profile; the exposure is unproven rather than reckless.

**4. Emerging commercial stress in a specific sector.** Against broadly improving aggregate credit, criticized C&I rose to $16.9B from $15.9B, attributed predominantly to technology, telecom and media and to equipment/machinery manufacturing. Technology/telecom/media nonaccruals jumped to $256M from $49M, and entertainment/recreation to $89M from $17M. Absolute amounts are small and one or two idiosyncratic borrowers can move a $256M line, but the sector identification is worth monitoring.

**5. Held-to-maturity marks.** $32.4B of unrealized losses outside capital against ~$140B of tangible common equity.

**6. Residual regulatory and legal.** Reasonably possible losses above accruals are down to ~$1.5B, a substantial improvement. The OCC AML/BSA formal agreement remains active, as do other provisions of the 2018 Fed consent order. Newly disclosed in the Q2 10-Q: "Fair Access to Banking" investigations under Executive Order 14331. Advisory cash-sweep litigation and an appealed Seminole Tribe verdict remain open; the $85M hiring-practices securities settlement received final approval May 21, 2026.

**7. Governance.** Scharf added the Chairman title in October 2025 with the board opposing an independent-chair proposal; the compensation scorecard comprises ROTCE, total revenue and efficiency ratio, none of which is a capital or credit-risk metric, in a year when CET1 fell 87bp year over year and NDFI, auto and card exposures grew. The HR Committee also removed pre-set NEO target total compensation from 2025, reducing ex-ante transparency.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 31.6% against 26.6% one-year realized reads rich, and the headline term structure is backwardated. That headline is mostly mechanical: the "1-week" tenor is a one-day expiry (August 7) with same-day pinning dynamics and only a ±2.0% implied move. The informative shape is 25.4% at one month, 23.4% at three months, and 29.1% at six months. The elevated six-month tenor (November 20) spans two earnings prints and multiple FOMC meetings, so it prices macro and rate-path event risk rather than imminent company-specific stress. Skew is call-heavy near-dated (−4.4%) and turns modestly put-heavy at one and three months (+1.6%, +1.9%), with put/call open interest at 1.13 and put/call volume at 0.35. The September 11 tenor shows a put/call volume ratio of 4.00, consistent with hedging around a September event. Since the prior snapshot, ATM IV eased from 32.3% to 31.6% while the price rose 8.1% and put/call open interest was unchanged, so positioning has not turned defensive on the rally. Reported short interest of 10,225 shares against 3.02 billion outstanding is implausibly small and should be treated as a data artifact rather than a positioning signal. Options do not contradict the fundamental view, and they identify the October print and the rate path as the events the market is paying for.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $112 (+25.7%)

Second-half non-Markets NII delivers the guided step-up, helped by a rate path that stays higher-for-longer rather than cutting (Morningstar's own constructive case assumes cuts are delayed to 2027), and 2026 NII lands at or above $50B. The efficiency ratio continues toward the 58% midcycle assumption on further headcount reduction, producing operating leverage on modest revenue growth. Investment banking and Markets fee momentum persists, validating management's argument that low-spread financing balances are a loss-leader for higher-return ancillary business, and CIB return on allocated capital rises even as NIM stays compressed. WIM inflows compound at the current ~$12B half-year pace. Credit stays benign through 2027, with auto and card vintages seasoning at the disclosed FICO mix rather than worse. Buybacks continue near $14B annually against $22.7B of remaining authorization, retiring ~5% of shares per year. FY2027 EPS reaches $8.50–8.75, and the market pays 13x on evidence that core ROTCE is sustainably 17%+ without VC marks. $112 is 14.1x next-year consensus $7.96 and roughly 2.2x forward tangible book, and sits below the $115 high target.

### Bear Case — $73 (−18.1%)

The second-half NII step-up misses, either because the Fed cuts (−$1.9B per 100bp) or because deposit repricing and mix erode the non-Markets book faster than fixed-asset repricing helps. Trading and investment banking revenue normalize as the environment the CEO flagged — equity indices at highs, narrow credit spreads, "large amounts of capital being deployed by both banks and non-banks" — turns, leaving Wells Fargo with a doubled repo book, consumed leverage capacity (SLR 5.78% from 6.67%), and a permanently diluted funding-cost advantage. Equity-securities marks reverse, taking reported ROTCE from 17.7% toward the low 14s and making the 17–18% target unreachable. Credit surprises arrive from the 20% NDFI concentration as terms loosened in 2025–2026 prove insufficient, or from the 2027 auto and card vintages. Buybacks slow to defend CET1 near 10.25% against continued 9% RWA growth, removing the return support. $73 is 10.0x current-year $7.30 and ~1.55x tangible book, matching the May 15, 2026 52-week low.

### Base Case — $98 (+9.96%, ~+12% total return with dividends)

Non-Markets NII grows into the low end of guidance without fully clearing it. Efficiency continues improving, fees hold roughly flat sequentially, and credit stays benign through 2027 with auto NCOs drifting modestly higher. Clean ROTCE settles at 16–17%. FY2027 EPS lands near $7.96, the multiple holds at 12.3x, and tangible book compounds ~7%. Two methods converge: 12.3x $7.96 = $97.9, and 1.93x forward TBV of $49.4 plus $2.00 of dividends = $97.3. Call it $98, consistent with the $100 consensus mean and above the $95 published Investing Club target.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** There is a positive twelve-month thesis with a dated catalyst, but roughly 10% of base-case price upside plus a 2.24% dividend against 18% of bear-case downside does not clear the bar for new capital in a sector where selection has historically added little above the market.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print, expected mid-October, is the decisive event. It provides the first hard read on the second-half NII step-up, on whether Q2's $847M of equity-securities gains repeat or reverse, on whether the Q2 buyback throttle ($3.0B versus $4.1B) was a policy choice or a one-quarter timing item, and on criticized C&I trends in technology/telecom/media. The next FOMC decision is the secondary checkpoint given the asymmetric rate sensitivity.

**Upgrade to BUY on any two of the following:**
1. Q3 non-Markets NII of at least $12.15B (a ~2.8% sequential step from Q2's $11.816B), which would put the ~$48B non-Markets guide within reach and validate the NIM-trough thesis.
2. Clean-basis ROTCE — reported ROTCE less the equity-securities and discrete-tax contributions — sustained at 17% or better for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating the target is met without marks.
3. Price at or below $78–80 on unchanged fundamentals, which would put the shares near 1.65x tangible book and 10.0x next-year consensus and restore genuine asymmetry.
4. Disclosure of charge-off and nonaccrual experience within the $208.8B "financials except banks" category showing losses consistent with senior secured structures, removing the largest unverified exposure in the book.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any one of the following:**
1. A third consecutive quarter of NII/NIM below consensus accompanied by a cut to the ~$50B full-year guide.
2. Net charge-offs above 0.50% annualized, or auto NCOs above 0.60%, or the first disclosed nonaccrual increase within the NDFI category — any of which would move credit from tailwind to headwind ahead of the 2027–2028 vintage seasoning.
3. Quarterly buybacks below $2.0B alongside CET1 falling below 10.0%, indicating the balance-sheet build is not self-funding and the ~5% buyback yield is unsustainable.
4. Reasonably possible losses above accruals rising materially from ~$1.5B, or a new consent order arising from the OCC AML/BSA agreement or the Fair Access to Banking investigations.

**On the score trajectory:** this is the fifth consecutive HOLD, with the overall score drifting from 6.7 in February to 5.9 now. The Q2 print is the first genuine fundamental improvement in that sequence; the prior two quarters were lackluster and the stock made a 52-week low at ~$73 in May. Estimates are up 4.6% for the current year and the dividend rose 11%. Those improvements support holding the position rather than adding to it, because the price rose 8.1% since the June 30 review and captured them. The marginal decline in the composite score is located in risk factors, where the NDFI concentration and the sector-specific criticized-loan increase are newly quantified, and in valuation, where a higher price against the same fair-value anchors leaves less room. Financial health, growth prospects and management quality are stable to modestly better than the prior review.

## Conclusion

Wells Fargo is a materially better company than it was three years ago: the asset cap is gone, 14 consent orders are closed, the efficiency ratio is at 60% and falling, Wealth has turned from outflows to $12.4B of first-half net inflows, investment banking fees hit a record, credit metrics are improving, and roughly $14B of annual repurchases against a $270B market capitalization retires about 5% of the equity each year. Excess CET1 above requirement is roughly $23.6B, and the erosion in the capital ratio has all but stopped.

The reason to hold rather than buy is that the price now reflects that. At 1.93x tangible book, the shares sit inside the range a 16% clean-ROTCE bank warrants at a 10–10.5% cost of equity, and at 12.2x current-year and 11.2x next-year consensus they trade modestly below a peer median that carries a better-quality revenue mix. Core deposit-funded net interest income is growing 2%, the margin fell 25bp, roughly 1.5 points of the reported 17.7% ROTCE came from venture-capital marks and a one-time tax item, and 28% of the EPS increase came from share count. The incremental balance sheet went into securities financing and trading inventory at a moment when the CEO is on record warning about capital being deployed across risk assets by banks and non-banks, and 20% of the loan book is lent to precisely those non-banks in a segment where regulators say terms are loosening.
