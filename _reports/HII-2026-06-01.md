**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Huntington Ingalls Industries is the largest military shipbuilder in the United States and a pure-play U.S. Navy prime contractor. Its competitive position is among the most defensible in the defense sector: it is the sole designer and builder of nuclear-powered aircraft carriers, the exclusive provider of carrier refueling and complex overhaul, and one of only two nuclear submarine builders. Demand is not the question. Total backlog stands at $53.1B (1.7x revenue), revenue grew 8.2% in 2025 to $12.48B, and the FY2026 NDAA authorizes $26B for shipbuilding including additional Columbia, Virginia, and Ford-class hulls. The constraint is supply: labor, throughput, and the supply base. The bull case is a bet on operational execution, not on whether the orders exist.

The problem is margins. Shipbuilding operating margin sits at roughly 5-6%, well below the historical 9-10% norm, dragged down by recurring unfavorable cost adjustments on aircraft carriers and Virginia-class submarines at Newport News. Q1 2026 crystallized the market's concern: revenue grew 13.4% YoY and EPS beat consensus, but segment operating margin still compressed to 5.6% from 6.3% a year earlier, and the stock fell. Throughput improved 18% YoY in the quarter, which is the leading indicator management points to, but those operational gains have not yet flowed through to reported profit. Current-quarter EPS estimates were cut 10.8% over the past 30 days.

The stock has de-rated about 25% from its spring 2026 highs (directors received equity grants at $379-393 in March/April; the shares now trade around $295-308) to roughly 17.7x forward EPS, a discount to the defense-prime group. This compression prices in much of the execution risk, and the FF(X) frigate award (won on HII's own National Security Cutter design after the competing Constellation-class program was cancelled) adds genuine medium-term growth optionality. But there is no insider open-market buying to confirm the value thesis, cash flow is volatile and timing-driven, and the margin recovery that underwrites any re-rating remains unproven. The setup is a HOLD with a growth lean: appreciation is plausible if margins turn, but the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric at current levels.

## Company Overview

HII operates three segments:

- **Newport News Shipbuilding (~52% of revenue, ~$6.5B in 2025)** is the crown jewel: the sole U.S. designer and builder of nuclear aircraft carriers (Ford-class CVN), the exclusive provider of carrier refueling and complex overhaul (RCOH), and one of two nuclear submarine builders, partnering with General Dynamics' Electric Boat on Virginia-class attack subs and serving as a subcontractor on Columbia-class ballistic-missile subs. Barriers to entry are extreme: nuclear expertise, capital intensity, and the ~550-acre James River yard.
- **Ingalls Shipbuilding (~25%, ~$3.1B)** in Pascagoula, MS, is the sole builder of large-deck amphibious assault ships (LHA) and amphibious transport docks (LPD), one of two builders of Arleigh Burke-class destroyers, and built the Coast Guard's National Security Cutters.
- **Mission Technologies (~24%, ~$3.0B)** is the capital-light services arm: C5ISR, cyber and electronic warfare, AI/ML, training, unmanned vehicles, and DoE nuclear site operations.

Approximately 81% of revenue comes directly from the U.S. Navy, and substantially all from the U.S. Government. The business runs on multi-decade program continuity. For most product lines HII is a sole-source or duopoly supplier.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** $10.68B (2022) → $11.45B (2023) → $11.54B (2024) → $12.48B (2025, +8.2%). The 2025 acceleration was broad-based across submarine, carrier, and surface-combatant volumes. Q1 2026 revenue grew 13.4% YoY to $3.10B, with Newport News up 19.3%.

**Margins are the central issue.** Operating income was $657M in 2025 (5.3% margin), recovering from $535M in 2024 but still below the $781M of 2023. Segment margins are structurally depressed: Newport News at 5.1% in 2025 (vs. 6.2% in 2023) and Ingalls at 7.6% (vs. 13.2% in 2023), both well below historical norms near 9-10%. Net income was $605M (4.8% net margin); ROE roughly 11.9%.

The cumulative catch-up adjustments document the recurring execution problem: net unfavorable adjustments of -$28M in 2025 and -$126M in 2024, driven by unfavorable performance on aircraft carriers and Virginia-class submarines at Newport News (-$64M and -$154M respectively). The 10-K is explicit that personnel "failed to achieve expected performance improvements... which resulted in unexpected inefficiency, and in some cases, required rework." Q1 2026 continued the pattern: shipbuilding margin slipped to 5.7% from 6.4% YoY.

**Cash flow is volatile and timing-driven.** Operating cash flow swung from $970M (2023) to $393M (2024) to $1,196M (2025); FCF recovered to $800M in 2025 from just $40M in 2024. That 20x jump was largely favorable working-capital timing and lower cash taxes, not a step-change in profitability. Q1 2026 FCF was -$461M (normal Q1 seasonality). The right anchor is the smoothed FY26 guide of $500-600M, not the 2025 print.

**Balance sheet:** Total assets $12.7B; long-term debt $2.7B; D/E ~1.5x; current ratio 1.13. The Altman Z-Score of 1.97 sits in the grey zone, but this is a false alarm for a defense prime: it is dragged down by thin reported EBIT (depressed by the margin issues above) and goodwill at ~21% of assets, not genuine distress. Government-backed backlog, sole-source positions, ~$1.9B liquidity, and pension plans 126% funded (FAS basis, requiring only ~$2M of 2026 contributions) put distress risk low.

**Capital allocation:** The dividend was raised again in November 2025 to $1.38/quarter, the 10th-plus consecutive annual increase ($213M paid in 2025). HII repurchased $0 of stock in 2025 versus $163M in 2024, despite a $3.8B authorization, preserving cash for capacity expansion as capex rises to 4-5% of sales. The absence of buybacks during a ~25% price decline is a missed opportunity if management believes the stock is cheap, and it weakens the "undervalued" framing.

**Earnings track record:** Four consecutive beats, but of declining magnitude: +16.6%, +8.5%, +3.1%, +0.9% across the last four quarters. The shrinking beat is consistent with consensus catching up to the margin reality and reduces the cushion in forward estimates.

## Growth Analysis

The demand backdrop is durable. The FY2026 NDAA authorizes $900.6B in national security spending, including $26B for shipbuilding: a third Columbia-class, additional Virginia-class, two Ford-class carriers (CVN 82/83), and $1.5B for the Maritime Industrial Base. Bipartisan support and the domestic-sourcing policy push directly favor U.S. shipbuilders.

Management targets ~6% revenue CAGR and $16B+ revenue by 2030. The FF(X) frigate award (April 2026, $282.9M lead-yard-support to Ingalls) is an under-appreciated structural win: it is based on HII's own NSC hull and effectively captures a program away from competitor Fincantieri Marinette Marine after the Constellation-class was cancelled. The Navy wants the first FF(X) by 2028. Management cites the frigate plus a "battleship" opportunity as upside to the 6% medium-term CAGR.

The growth question is execution-gated, not demand-gated. Throughput improved 18% YoY in Q1 2026, building on ~14% in 2025, the operational lead indicator for margin recovery. The earnings power upside is real: if shipbuilding margins recover from ~5.5% toward the historical 9-10% on a $9.8B shipbuilding revenue base, that is several hundred million dollars of incremental operating income. But it has not yet appeared in the P&L, and current-quarter estimates were cut 10.8% over 30 days, signaling near-term skepticism. Full-year estimates are stable (FY26 $17.40, FY27 $20.37), so the revision picture is mixed rather than uniformly negative.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$295-308, HII trades at roughly 19-20x trailing EPS of $15.39 and ~17x forward EPS on the FY26 consensus of $17.40. Against the defense peer group, this is a discount:

| Ticker | P/E | Div Yield |
|--------|-----|-----------|
| TXT (Textron) | 17.6 | 0.1% |
| NOC (Northrop Grumman) | 17.3 | 1.8% |
| LHX (L3Harris) | 33.6 | 1.6% |
| TDG (TransDigm) | 38.7 | 7.3% |
| **HII** | **~20.0 (TTM)** | **1.8%** |

Peer median P/E is 33.6, but that median is skewed by aftermarket/avionics names (TDG, LHX) that carry structurally higher margins and multiples than a low-margin shipbuilder. The more apt comparables are NOC and TXT, both near 17-18x, which puts HII broadly in line on a like-for-like basis rather than at a steep discount. The forward multiple of ~17x is reasonable but not obviously cheap for a business earning 4.8% net margins with an unresolved execution problem.

The bull's valuation argument is cycle-adjusted earnings power: if margins normalize, today's price is paying a low-to-mid teens multiple on mid-cycle earnings. That is a legitimate frame, but it requires conviction in a recovery that has slipped repeatedly. Analyst targets (mean $387.9, median $400) sit well above the current price, but those targets predate the Q1 margin miss and the estimate cut, so they should be treated as somewhat stale rather than as a firm floor. With a low target of $298, roughly at the current price, the analyst range itself is wide and skewed toward "value if margins recover."

The 1.8% dividend yield, well-covered and consistently growing, provides modest support. The valuation is fair, offering upside on a margin turn but lacking the asymmetric cushion that would justify a higher score given the unproven recovery and the absence of buyback support.

## Competitive Landscape

HII's moat is among the strongest in defense. It is the sole source for nuclear carriers, carrier RCOH, and large-deck amphibious ships, and one of two nuclear submarine builders. Its primary competitor, General Dynamics, operates as a managed duopoly partner via teaming agreements on Virginia and Columbia subs, simultaneously partner and competitor. Barriers to entry (nuclear expertise, capital intensity, security clearance, decades of accumulated know-how) are effectively prohibitive.

The competitive trajectory is improving at the margin: the FF(X) win demonstrates HII captured share from a rival whose program was cancelled, validating Ingalls' design pedigree. The 10-K flags two structural watch items: the possibility that a government-owned shipyard could eventually perform carrier RCOH, and strategic reassessments by the DoD that could reduce demand for its shipbuilding programs, including aircraft carrier programs, in favor of cheaper or unmanned alternatives. Both are long-dated and low-probability, but they cap the moat from being scored as fully unassailable. Competitive position remains the highest-scoring dimension because the trajectory is stable-to-improving and the structural advantages are real.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Contract execution / cost overruns (the #1 idiosyncratic risk).** 46% of revenue is fixed-price incentive; Newport News continues posting unfavorable EACs on carriers and Virginia-class subs. Margin recovery is the entire bull thesis and remains unproven. Q1 2026's margin compression keeps this risk live.

2. **Labor and skilled-workforce shortage.** Throughput is gated by labor availability; a growing share of the workforce is nearing retirement. HII hired ~8,000 in 2025 and doubled outsourced hours. All five Ingalls collective bargaining agreements expired in March 2026, though a new CBA with wage enhancements was ratified per the Q1 call; wage enhancements support retention but pressure margins.

3. **Single-customer dependency and budget risk.** ~81% Navy, ~100% U.S. Government. The 43-day government shutdown (Oct-Nov 2025) and continuing-resolution risk directly affect order flow and cash timing.

4. **Cash flow volatility.** The 20x swing in FCF from 2024 to 2025 was timing-driven; investors should anchor on the $500-600M guide, not extrapolate the 2025 print.

5. **Long-dated structural risks.** Government-owned RCOH capability and DoD strategy reassessments toward unmanned platforms are low-probability but would be material if realized.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty without a clear directional bias. Near-term ATM IV is 42.5% against 34.6% historical volatility (rich by 7.9 points), but the IV percentile within the chain is only 26%, and put/call skew is balanced at 0.3%. The implied move for the nearest expiry is ±7.3%. The term structure is in contango (near-term 42.5% < long-term 45.0%), a normal-to-complacent shape. Over the past ten days, ATM IV rose 5.6 points and the P/C OI ratio climbed from 0.95 to 1.18 as the price fell 7.1%, indicating modest defensive positioning building into the price weakness. Short interest is negligible at 2.6% of float (1.5 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short or squeeze dynamic. The options picture is consistent with a stock the market views as fairly valued and event-sensitive (next earnings) rather than as a high-conviction directional trade in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($400-435):** Throughput gains flow through to the P&L as challenged pre-COVID contracts burn down, lifting shipbuilding margins from ~5.5% back toward 8-9%. On $9.8B shipbuilding revenue, each point of margin is ~$100M of operating income. Combined with the FF(X) frigate, additional Columbia/Virginia/Ford volumes from the NDAA shipbuilding line, and a re-rating back toward the analyst median of $400, the stock approaches 20-22x normalized EPS of ~$20+. This is the path to the $435 high target.

**Bear Case ($245-260, roughly -15% to -20%):** Margin recovery stalls as labor costs from the new Ingalls CBA, supplier inflation on fixed-price work, and continued carrier/submarine EAC adjustments keep shipbuilding margins stuck near 5-6%. Estimates continue to drift lower (the current-quarter cut is the early sign), and the multiple compresses toward the low end of the defense group. Government shutdown/CR risk pressures cash timing. With no buyback support and no insider buying, the stock drifts toward 14-15x on stagnant ~$17 EPS.

The base case sits between these: revenue grows mid-to-high single digits, margins improve gradually but unevenly, and the stock re-rates modestly as the market gains incremental confidence in the recovery.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth lean), 12-month horizon.**

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
  1. Two consecutive quarters of YoY shipbuilding segment margin expansion (currently declining) confirming throughput gains are flowing to the P&L.
  2. Resumption of share repurchases at current levels, signaling management's conviction the stock is undervalued.
  3. Clustered open-market insider buying by C-suite executives, absent today despite the de-rating.

- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
  1. A further negative EAC adjustment (>$50M) on carriers or Virginia-class subs in any single quarter.
  2. FY26 FCF guidance cut below the $500-600M range, or full-year EPS estimates falling more than 5% over 90 days.
  3. A government shutdown or extended CR materially delaying contract funding and cash collection.

- **Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026), the next read on whether the throughput improvement begins to convert into margin expansion. The FY2027 NDAA appropriations cycle is a secondary checkpoint for demand visibility.

**Base case target: ~$355.** This applies ~17.4x to the FY27 consensus of $20.37, blended toward a modest re-rating as confidence in the recovery builds, roughly +15-20% from current levels. **Bull case: $435** (matching the high analyst target, ~+40-45%). **Bear case: ~$250** (~14.5x stagnant ~$17 EPS, roughly -15-20%).

## Conclusion

HII offers one of the most defensible competitive positions in defense: sole-source nuclear carrier and RCOH franchises, a $53B backlog, and a structural demand tailwind underwritten by bipartisan defense spending. The FF(X) frigate win adds real growth optionality, and the de-rating to ~17x forward EPS prices in much of the execution risk. None of that resolves the core problem: the entire bull case is a margin-recovery bet, and margins are still falling year-over-year even as throughput improves. Until the operational gains convert into reported profit, the upside remains hypothetical.

The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. There is plausible 15-20% upside to a base-case re-rating if margins inflect, against 15-20% downside if the recovery stalls and estimates keep drifting lower. The absence of buybacks or insider buying during a sharp decline, the current-quarter estimate cut, and the unproven margin trajectory argue against pressing a BUY here. This is a quality franchise to own through the recovery if and when it shows up in the numbers, but the evidence for a confident upgrade is not yet present. **HOLD**, with a growth lean and a clear set of triggers to monitor at the next earnings report.
