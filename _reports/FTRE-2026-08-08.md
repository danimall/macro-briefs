## Executive Summary

Fortrea is a mid-tier contract research organization spun out of Labcorp in June 2023, and the equity has been one of the more violent round trips in health care services: from $6.58 in early August 2025 to a $21.39 high on July 29, 2026, and back to $17.69. The company is three quarters into a credible operating recovery. H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA rose 24.1% to $105.7M on revenue that fell 3.4%, the H1 margin improved to 8.0% from 6.3%, FY2026 guidance was raised on July 29 to revenue of $2,620–2,690M and adjusted EBITDA of $205–220M, and book-to-bill has now printed above 1.0x for four consecutive quarters (1.06x in Q2, 1.12x trailing twelve months). Consensus EPS for next year has risen 16.5% over 90 days to $1.14. None of that is trivial for a business that took a $797.9M goodwill impairment in the first half of 2025.

The problem is what the price already pays for. At $17.69, enterprise value is $2,568M ($1,682M equity plus $885.7M net debt), which is 12.1x the midpoint of guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA and 13.5x if the $300M off-balance-sheet receivables facility is treated as debt-like. Large listed CROs have derated into roughly the 9–12x EV/EBITDA range through 2025–2026. Fortrea is being valued at a full peer multiple while earning roughly half the peer margin, shrinking revenue, and carrying 4.2x net leverage against contracts terminable on 30–90 days' notice. The cheap-looking metric is EV/revenue at 0.97x, and that only converts to value if margins move materially higher. Each 100bp of adjusted EBITDA margin is worth about $27M on a $2.66B revenue base; closing half the gap to peer margins would be worth roughly $11 per share at a 10x multiple. That arithmetic is the entire bull case, and the filings do not yet evidence it.

Set against a $20 base case (+13.1%), a $27 bull case (+52.6%) and a $10 bear case (-43.5%), probability-weighted expected return is roughly +7% with unusually wide dispersion; the six-month options tenor prices 89.3% implied volatility, a fair reflection of the range of outcomes. Combined with unresolved governance items (a sitting director signing the 10-Q as interim CFO, eleven Item 5.02 8-K filings since February 2025, no open-market insider purchases during a 169% twelve-month advance, and the CEO selling roughly half his first vested RSU tranche one week after raising guidance), this is a HOLD. Score moves marginally to 4.56 from 4.5, reflecting real operating improvement offset by a price that has absorbed it.

## Company Overview

Fortrea provides Phase I–IV clinical trial management, clinical pharmacology and consulting services to pharmaceutical, biotech and device sponsors. It reports one segment (Clinical Services) but maintains two goodwill reporting units, Clinical Development and Clinical Pharmacology, a distinction that turns out to be the most useful analytical lens available on the business.

Clinical Development is roughly 90% of revenue and is delivered through Full Service, Functional Service Provider (FSP, where Fortrea staff embed in sponsor operations) and hybrid models. Oncology was 40% of 2025 full-service therapeutic revenue. Clinical Pharmacology operates four clinical research units with 376 beds (Leeds, Dallas, Daytona, Madison), each with an on-site cGMP pharmacy, specializing in first-in-human and radiolabeled ADME work.

Scale: ~14,000 employees at 6/30/2026 (down from 14,300 at year-end), 62 facilities in 42 countries, FY2025 revenue $2,723.4M. The Enabling Services segment (patient access and endpoint clinical) was sold to an Arsenal Capital affiliate in Q2 2024 for a final $340.0M, with the last $25.0M milestone collected in Q3 2025.

Two framing points. First, the market-data feed classifies FTRE as Health Care / Biotechnology, and the supplied peer set (SRPT, ADMA, VIR, XNCR, HRMY) is biotech. That comparison is not usable. Fortrea is a labor-and-project services business, and any benchmark against biotech margins, R&D intensity or cash-burn norms mis-scores it. The relevant comparables are IQVIA, ICON, Parexel, Thermo Fisher's PPD unit and Medpace. Second, the reported P/E fields in the feed (-19.33 forward and trailing, against a stated EPS of -$10.8136) are internally inconsistent with each other and both derive from GAAP losses dominated by the 2025 impairment. Neither is usable; the analysis below works from adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow, and consensus adjusted EPS.

Management sizes 2026 Phase I–IV development spend at ~$145B with a ~$41B addressable slice, implying Fortrea holds ~6.6% of its own defined market. The 10-K concedes the industry has "few barriers to entering," remains "highly fragmented," and that "no single patent, technology, trademark, license, or other intellectual property asset is material to the business as a whole." The moat rests on therapeutic depth, an investigator-site network, and switching friction on multi-year trials rather than on proprietary assets.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue: still contracting

| Period | Revenue | Change |
|---|---|---|
| FY2024 | $2,696.4M | — |
| FY2025 | $2,723.4M | +1.0% |
| H1 2025 | $1,361.6M | — |
| H1 2026 | $1,314.7M | **-3.4%** (organic -4.0%) |
| Q2 2026 | $678.2M | **-4.5%** (organic -4.9%) |
| FY2026 guide | $2,620–2,690M | **-3.8% to -1.2%** |

Management attributes the 2026 decline to "lower pass through costs as well as lower demand for our functional service provider business," partly offset by clinical pharmacology growth. Composition matters in two directions. Pass-through costs (investigator fees, travel, logistics) are recovered at or near zero margin, so their decline mechanically reduces revenue *and* mechanically improves the direct-cost ratio; the improvement from 81.2% to 79.5% of revenue in Q2 is therefore partly mix rather than pure execution. The dollar split is not disclosed, which is a verification gap. What is not explicable by mix is that adjusted EBITDA *dollars* grew 6.9% in Q2 and 24.1% in H1; there is real cost improvement underneath.

The FSP weakness is the more troubling item. Fortrea's own market commentary says sponsors are reducing Full Service near-term while *increasing* FSP usage, and positions its FSP flexibility as a differentiator. FSP is where revenue is falling. Two readings exist and the filings do not distinguish them: share loss to competitors, or sponsor insourcing (Item 1A notes many service contracts "provide our customers with the opportunity to internalize the resources provided under the contract"). Either way, the pillar management points to as the industry tailwind is currently the drag.

### Margins: a genuine recovery off a trough, with limited remaining self-help

| Period | Adj. EBITDA | Margin |
|---|---|---|
| FY2024 | $203M | 7.5% |
| FY2025 | ~$190M | 7.0% |
| H1 2025 | $85.2M | 6.3% |
| H1 2026 | $105.7M | 8.0% |
| Q2 2026 | $58.7M | 8.7% |
| TTM 6/30/26 | $210.4M | 7.9% |
| FY2026 guide | $205–220M | ~7.8–8.2% |

The FY2025 ~$190M figure reconciles two ways: the proxy pay-versus-performance table reports the company-selected measure at $190M, and TTM $210.4M less H1 2026 $105.7M plus H1 2025 $85.2M gives $189.9M.

The drivers are identifiable and mostly non-recurring. SG&A fell 18.6% in FY2025 to $456.4M and another 17.8% in H1 2026 to $202.6M, driven by exiting Labcorp transition-services agreements and lower IT spend, which is real, structural, and largely already captured. Restructuring charges are tapering fast: $50.1M (FY24) → $44.1M (FY25) → $9.4M (H1 2026, -44% YoY), meaning less self-help runway remains. Headcount is down only 2.1% over six months.

The guidance decomposition is unheroic. Guidance midpoints imply H2 revenue of ~$1,340M (+1.9% sequentially) and H2 adjusted EBITDA of ~$106.8M, essentially flat against H1 at ~8.0% margin. There is no back-half hockey stick embedded. What guidance *does* require is that revenue stop declining sequentially.

From roughly 8%, further margin expansion requires revenue growth for operating leverage, because the cost levers have been pulled. That is the crux of the bull/bear disagreement.

### GAAP and the tax line

FY2025 GAAP was dominated by $797.9M of goodwill impairment, all in Clinical Development ($488.8M at 3/31/25, $309.1M at 6/30/25), producing an $872.6M operating loss and a $986.2M net loss. Q2 2026 swung to positive operating income of $14.8M with a net loss of $13.2M (-$0.14); H1 operating income $11.4M, net loss $36.8M (-$0.39).

The bridge from positive operating income to net loss is $38.4M of H1 interest expense and $23.3M of income tax expense on a *pretax loss* of $13.5M. The 10-Q attributes this to "the impact of valuation allowance primarily related to expected interest deductibility limitations." Fortrea's leverage is large enough that part of its interest expense is not currently deductible and it cannot recognize a deferred tax asset for the carryforward. FY2025 tax expense was $3.2M; H1 2026 alone was $23.3M. This is a structural cash cost of the capital structure, and it compresses the path from adjusted EBITDA to free cash flow in a way that EV/EBITDA comparisons against less-levered peers do not capture.

FY2024's $262.8M is not a run rate. The FY2025 MD&A states the $149.3M decline was "primarily due to decreases in cash received from accounts receivable, driven by the sale of receivables under the Receivables Facility during 2024." The $300M securitization launched 5/6/2024 pulled roughly $300M of collections forward into that year; $300M remained sold at both 12/31/25 and 6/30/26. FY2025's $88.3M FCF is the clean baseline, a 5.25% yield on the current market capitalization.

H1 is seasonally cash-negative (receivables and unbilled services rose $70.0M in H1 2026, $77.5M in H1 2025). Q2's $19.9M annualizes to ~$80M, which I present only as a sensitivity: cash taxes are now running materially higher than 2025, and capex stepped up to 1.3% of revenue in H1 2026 from 0.9% in FY2025 as technology spend increases. Treating FY2026 FCF as broadly similar to FY2025's $88.3M is the reasonable planning assumption.

### Balance sheet

At 6/30/2026: cash $168.6M; total debt $1,054.3M carrying value (term loans A and B plus 7.50% senior secured notes due 2030); net debt $885.7M; total equity $524.2M against goodwill and intangibles of $1,537.1M, implying tangible book equity of **-$1,012.9M**; current ratio 0.96.

Derived leverage: net debt / TTM adjusted EBITDA = 4.21x, improved from 4.62x at year-end entirely through EBITDA growth (total debt was flat over H1). Including the $300M receivables sale as debt-like: 5.64x. Interest coverage on TTM adjusted EBITDA / net interest = 2.50x, up from 2.08x.

Liquidity is better than headline solvency screens suggest. The revolver is undrawn with $447.7M available and was not touched at all in H1 2026, versus a $135.5M peak draw in H1 2025. The receivables facility was amended on 2/24/2026 to extend termination from 5/6/2027 to 2/23/2029, removing a near-term refinancing question. Fortrea also tendered $75.7M of the 2030 notes in Q4 2025 to satisfy an indenture asset-sale obligation.

On the distress screens: an Altman Z-Score of 0.38 is reproducible from the disclosed inputs, but the EBIT/assets term alone contributes -1.06 and that EBIT is the FY2025 operating loss containing $797.9M of non-cash impairment. Substituting impairment-excluded EBIT (-$74.7M) and market rather than book equity lifts the score to roughly 1.68, still below the 1.81 threshold, but a different picture. Using the retained-earnings term as designed pushes it back toward 0.68. Z-Score is unreliable for a recently spun-off, goodwill-heavy services company whose accumulated deficit was manufactured by two quarters of impairment. The decision-relevant facts are the directly computed ones: 4.2x net leverage, 2.5x coverage, $168.6M cash plus $447.7M undrawn revolver, no maturity wall before the revolver (~2028) and notes (2030). Fortrea does not face near-term bankruptcy risk. The leverage is high for a low-margin business with cancellable contracts and no cushion for a second demand shock.

**Financial Health: 4.7.** Improvement is real (leverage down 41bp, coverage up 42bp, 2027 refi risk removed, revolver untouched) but the base level is weak: 3.2% FCF-to-revenue conversion, a sub-1.0 current ratio, negative tangible equity, and tax leakage created by the leverage itself.

## Growth Analysis

The forward signal that matters most is bookings. Book-to-bill was 1.06x in Q2 and 1.12x trailing twelve months, the fourth consecutive quarter above 1.0x, with backlog at $7.8B (up from $7.7B at year-end), or ~2.9 years of coverage at annualized revenue. The internal consistency check holds: at ~1.06x on H1 revenue of $1,314.7M, H1 net new business of ~$1.39B against $1.31B recognized implies ~$80M of backlog build before FX, and observed backlog rose ~$100M. That coherence raises confidence that bookings are genuinely inflecting rather than being defined loosely.

The offsetting reality is that backlog is Fortrea's own defined measure, not a contractual order book. The majority of contracts are terminable without cause on 30–90 days' notice, awards can be cancelled before contracting, and the company states it "generally do[es] not have a contractual right to the full amount of the contract award reflected in our backlog." Balance-sheet unearned revenue was only $502.7M at 6/30/26. Cumulative catch-up *reductions* in revenue on partially satisfied obligations were $16M in 2025 and $61M in 2024, a reminder that revenue on input-method fixed-price contracts depends on cost-to-complete estimates that have proven optimistic before.

Estimate revisions are constructive but not at the threshold that flags a forced re-rating: current year $0.84 (+5.1% over 90 days), next year $1.14 (+16.5% over 90 days, +5.8% over 30 days). Next-quarter estimates edged down 4.5% over 30 days, which is worth watching. The earnings surprise record is mixed: two beats (Q1 2026 +241%, Q2 2026 +24.9%) after two misses (Q3 2025 -21.5%, Q4 2025 -44.9%), so the visibility improvement is only two quarters old.

Industry context: management cites large-biopharma R&D spend growing 4–5% CAGR through 2030, biotech funding that "began to strengthen in the second half of" 2025, and a CRO market expected to grow more slowly near-term before reaccelerating. Newly emphasized risk factors add U.S. tariff and drug-pricing policy pressure on sponsors' capital allocation and reduced FDA/NIH funding, with the pointed observation that policy uncertainty "may lead to slower decision making." For a business whose revenue depends on converting awards into started trials, decision latency is as damaging as budget cuts.

**Growth Prospects: 5.0.** Bookings and estimate revisions point up; reported revenue points down for at least four more quarters on the company's own guidance. The 2027 revenue inflection is plausible but unproven, and the segment management identifies as the structural tailwind (FSP) is the one shrinking.

**Valuation: 5.0.** Cheap on sales, full on earnings, with the mispricing case dependent on a margin outcome the company has not yet demonstrated. Prior-cycle score was 4.5; the guidance raise and rising estimates on a flat price justify a marginal improvement, not a re-rating of the score.

## Competitive Landscape

Fortrea's structural disadvantage is margin. At ~8% adjusted EBITDA it operates well below the mid-teens-to-low-20s range typical of large listed CROs, and its own filings identify the causes: sub-scale SG&A absorption post-spin (transition-services agreements with Labcorp only recently exited), a delivery mix weighted toward lower-margin FSP and pass-through-heavy work, and a $2.7B revenue base spread across 62 facilities in 42 countries.

The most useful competitive insight in the filings is the two-speed nature of the business. Clinical Pharmacology (four CRUs, 376 beds, first-in-human and radiolabeled ADME specialization) is growing, was cited as the offset to declines in both FY2025 and Q2 2026, and had a fair value that "substantially exceeded" book value at both 2025 interim impairment test dates. Clinical Development, the ~90% revenue engine, absorbed all $797.9M of the impairment and is where revenue is contracting. The equity is a bet on repairing the large damaged business, with a smaller high-quality asset providing partial cover. Clinical Pharmacology is a plausibly separable, saleable asset if the board needed to deleverage, an option the filings do not discuss.

Customer concentration is the clearest deteriorating trend. Top-20 customers were 61% of revenue in 2023, 64% in 2024, and 69% in 2025. Top 10 were ~57% of revenue and ~54% of backlog. One customer was ~18.1% of FY2025 revenue, or roughly $493M. Management frames a "Large and Diversified Customer Base" as a competitive strength in the same document that discloses this, and Item 1A warns concentration may increase further "including as a result of our entering into strategic provider relationships." The favorable reading is that concentration reflects preferred-provider status with large pharma (56% of 2025 revenue came from "leading pharmaceutical customers," and customers use three or more services on average). The unfavorable reading is fragility. The disclosure does not permit adjudication, and the trend direction, 800bp of increase in two years, is the fact.

The oncology weighting (40% of full-service therapeutic revenue, 1,200+ trials over five years) is genuine depth, but the company's own therapeutic-concentration risk factor notes that same-class trials compete for patients, academic interest and funding, and can be cancelled together on adverse class-wide data.

**Competitive Position: 4.3.** Trajectory is stabilizing rather than improving: bookings above 1.0x for four quarters is evidence of competitiveness in the market, but share within FSP is losing, concentration is worsening, margins remain structurally sub-peer, and the company concedes no material IP moat.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage plus non-deductible interest.** $1,054.3M of debt at 4.21x net (5.64x including the receivables facility), 2.50x coverage, and a tax structure in which interest deductibility limits generate real cash tax expense on pretax losses. In a business where the majority of contracts terminate on 30–90 days' notice, this is the primary structural risk.

**Single-customer dependence.** An 18.1% revenue relationship whose identity is undisclosed. Loss, or an insourcing decision, would be a single-event thesis break.

**Further goodwill impairment.** $950.3M of goodwill remains against $524.2M of book equity. The 10/1/2024 annual test showed only a ~10% cushion in Clinical Development before the 2025 write-downs; the 10/1/2025 annual test was qualitative only, so no fresh quantitative cushion is disclosed. The market-cap input has roughly tripled since the June 2025 test at $4.94, so near-term risk appears low, but the cushion is unquantified and the named triggers (booking delays, rising terminations, higher discount rates, sustained share-price declines) are all live.

**Securities class action.** *Deslande v. Fortrea Holdings*, S.D.N.Y. 1:25-cv-04630, filed 6/2/2025; amended complaint 11/10/2025; motion to dismiss filed 1/28/2026 and unresolved as of the Q2 filing. No loss estimate provided.

**Governance and finance leadership.** The Q2 2026 10-Q is signed and certified by "David Ross Smith, Interim Chief Financial Officer," and Smith filed Form 4s as a *Director* on 6/10 and 7/20/2026 before filing as Interim CFO on 8/3/2026. The prior CFO's last Form 4 in that capacity was 6/2/2026. On the available evidence a sitting board member is bridging the finance function in the middle of a leverage-reduction and margin-recovery program that constitutes the entire equity thesis. Exhibit 10.1 to the 10-Q is an offer letter effective 7/6/2026 with Jason Knoblauch, whose role is not stated, possibly the permanent successor, unverified. Eleven Item 5.02 filings between February 2025 and July 2026 is extraordinary churn for a three-year-old public company. Separately, the anti-takeover architecture is unusually fortified: a June 2025 rights plan triggering at 10% ownership (adopted roughly four months after a cooperation agreement with Starboard Value and while the stock traded near $4.94), a classified board and 75% charter-amendment supermajority through the 2028 annual meeting, no stockholder-called special meetings, and no proxy access. The defensible reading is protection against creeping accumulation at distressed prices; the less favorable reading is entrenchment at the moment Fortrea is the most obvious consolidation candidate in its industry.

**Adjusted EBITDA quality.** Bridging the TTM -$85.2M net loss to $210.4M adjusted EBITDA requires $137.5M of discretionary add-backs beyond tax, interest, FX and D&A: restructuring $42.9M, stock compensation $61.1M, disposition costs $3.4M, spin costs $5.3M, CEO transition $0.3M, and "Other" $24.5M, where "Other" explicitly includes the yield expense on the receivables securitization, an actual financing cost. Share count rose from 93.1M to 95.1M over twelve months.

Near-term skew is call-heavy (-4.7%) with a 0.10 put/call volume ratio, so short-dated positioning is not defensive. Options do not confirm a directional bull case, and they suggest the cheapest way to express any view here is through the near-dated tenor rather than the six-month.

**Risk Factors: 3.8.** Multiple specific, unpriced catalysts for material downside: an unresolved motion to dismiss, undisclosed goodwill cushion, an 18.1% customer, an interim CFO, and leverage that leaves no cushion for a second demand shock.

## Management Quality

The scorecard here is mixed and the trajectory declined in the prior cycle (5.0 → 4.0). New evidence does not restore it.

On the credit side: FY2026 guidance was raised on the Q2 print, the cost program has delivered (SG&A down 18.6% then 17.8%), the receivables facility extension to 2029 and the $75.7M note tender were sensible capital actions, and the compensation design is well-chosen for the situation: Adjusted EBITDA (company-selected), Net New Business, Revenue, Adjusted EBITDA Margin and Relative TSR. Bookings plus margin are precisely the two variables a turnaround must move, and relative TSR guards against crediting a sector rally. COO Morais received the first visible performance stock unit grant in March 2026, consistent with the new team being placed on performance-vesting structures.

On the debit side: the cumulative TSR index stood at 50.72 at FY2025 against a peer group at 120.57, so $100 invested at the spin was worth ~$50.72 versus ~$120.57 for peers. The company-selected Adjusted EBITDA measure declined across all three standalone years ($246M → $203M → $190M). The proxy's pay-versus-performance extraction shows Thomas Pike as FY2025 PEO with total compensation of $11.58M and "compensation actually paid" of $31.94M in a year of a $986M net loss and a falling TSR index; the most likely explanations are aggregation across multiple PEOs during the transition, severance, or inducement-award grant values, but this was not resolved from the proxy narrative and should not be treated as an established fact about any individual's pay.

Insider activity is the item I weight most. Across roughly six months in which the share price doubled, the retrieved Form 4 record contains no open-market purchases by any officer or director. Every cash transaction is a disposition. CEO Anshul Thakral settled 416,666 RSUs on 8/4/2026 (leaving 833,334 unvested, implying a ~1.25M-unit inducement award) and sold 211,726 shares across 8/4–8/6 at $18.06–$19.28, roughly $3.94M gross and about 51% of the vested tranche, ending with 260,915 shares. Director Machelle Sanders sold 9,700 shares at $18.01 on 8/3. The COO and former CFO made repeated small sales sized at ~40–45% of newly settled RSUs, which is the signature of routine sell-to-cover.

There is a legitimate reading in which the CEO's sale is ordinary liquidity and tax management on a first vesting tranche. Two details temper it: the transactions were coded "S" (open-market sale) rather than "F" (shares withheld for taxes), and Fortrea's plans clearly permit withholding (another insider used an "F" code in June), so the pure-tax-mechanics explanation is incomplete; and the timing came one week after raising guidance with the stock at its post-spin high. A 10b5-1 plan adopted before Q2 would also produce "S" codes, and the 10-Q's statement that no officer adopted or terminated a plan during the quarter ended 6/30/2026 does not exclude one adopted earlier. I do not assert motive. Insider selling is normally a low-signal input when contradicted by rising estimates, and here it is not selling in isolation but selling alongside eleven executive-departure filings, an interim CFO drawn from the board, and a fortified anti-takeover structure.

**Management Quality: 4.0.** Held flat. Operational delivery is improving; alignment and governance evidence is not.

## Conclusion

Fortrea has done the achievable part of its turnaround. Adjusted EBITDA margin moved from 6.3% to 8.0% in twelve months, the FY2025 trough is behind it, net leverage fell from 4.62x to 4.21x on earnings growth rather than debt paydown, the 2027 receivables-facility maturity was pushed to 2029, and bookings have exceeded revenue for four straight quarters. Guidance was raised, and the raised guidance is undemanding: it asks only that revenue stop declining sequentially, not that margins inflect further.

What remains is the harder part, and the price already pays for a good outcome. At 12.1x EV to guided FY2026 EBITDA (13.5x on a receivables-adjusted basis), Fortrea is valued at or above derated large-cap CRO peers while earning roughly half their margin on contracting revenue, with 4.2x leverage, worsening customer concentration, a structural tax drag from non-deductible interest, and a governance profile that includes a board member signing the 10-Q as interim CFO, eleven executive-departure filings in eighteen months, a 10% poison pill layered over a classified board through 2028, and no open-market insider buying during a 169% advance. The remaining cost levers are largely pulled; from here margin expansion needs revenue growth, which is what guidance does not contain.

The base case offers roughly 13% upside against a bear case that removes 44%. That is approximately fair, not compelling. Existing holders have a defensible position with clear monitoring points: book-to-bill, the 18.1% customer, the FY2027 revenue guide, and the CFO appointment. New capital should wait for either the first quarter of positive organic revenue growth, or a price in the $13–14 range that restores margin of safety.
