## Executive Summary

Installed Building Products is the nation's largest residential insulation installer operating ~250 branches across 48 states, with a proven acquisition-driven growth model that has generated 37% ROE and 10% FCF margins. The company has demonstrated exceptional pricing discipline, expanding gross margins from 31% to 34% over three years even as volumes contracted. However, the core residential end market is now in accelerating decline: Q1 2026 residential same-store sales fell 11.2%, GAAP EPS dropped 21% YoY, and the company missed consensus estimates for the first time in four quarters.

The stock trades at $299.47, having rallied 81% from its May 2025 lows of ~$151 but sitting 14% below its February 2026 peak of $349. At approximately 27x FY2026 consensus EPS of $11.12 (with estimates trending lower), the valuation offers limited margin of safety given the cyclical headwinds. Analyst consensus is overwhelmingly cautious (1 Buy, 11 Hold, 2 Strong Sell) with a mean target of $289 — below the current price. CEO insider selling of $125M+ in March 2026 near the local high adds to the cautious setup.

## Company Overview

IBP operates as a service-based installer of insulation and complementary building products (shower doors, garage doors, waterproofing, rain gutters, fire-stopping) for residential and commercial construction. Revenue is 93% installation services, with 76% residential and 17% commercial exposure. The company purchases materials directly from manufacturers (primarily four fiberglass producers: Owens Corning, Knauf, CertainTeed, Johns Manville), bypassing traditional distribution to capture margin.

The business model combines national scale with local execution, maintaining low customer concentration (largest customer at ~4% of revenue) and below-industry employee turnover (3.2% monthly vs. 4.0% industry). Since 1999, IBP has completed over 200 acquisitions, building a platform that generates substantial free cash flow with minimal capex requirements (2.4% of revenue).

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth is negative and deteriorating. Current quarter EPS estimates have fallen from $2.05 (90 days ago) to $1.96 — a -4.5% revision. Full-year FY2026 consensus of $11.12 represents a 14.5% increase over FY2025's $9.71 EPS but is inflated by a strong Q4 2025 comparison and assumes residential stabilization that has not yet materialized.

**Positive growth vectors:**
- Commercial construction: +10.7% same-store, $162.9M backlog (up from $126.3M), providing 2-3 quarters of visibility
- Acquisition pipeline: $100M+ revenue target for 2026 (achieved $53M in 2025)
- Secular tailwinds: stricter energy codes (IECC 2021), ESG-driven insulation demand, aging housing stock
- Structural housing undersupply

**Negative growth vectors:**
- Housing starts forecast to decline from 1.36M to 1.31M in 2026
- Mortgage rates above 7% constraining affordability
- Multi-family completions down 20.3% in 2025
- Elevated spec home inventory
- Tariff uncertainty reducing housing affordability at the margin

The fundamental challenge: residential (76% of revenue) is declining faster than commercial (17%) can offset. For growth to resume, either housing starts need to stabilize or the acquisition pace needs to accelerate materially.

## Competitive Landscape

IBP holds a durable competitive position as the #1 or #2 national insulation installer, with TopBuild (BLD) as its primary large-cap peer. Key structural advantages:

1. **Purchasing scale:** Direct buying from 4 consolidated fiberglass manufacturers provides cost advantages over smaller competitors
2. **National footprint:** 250 branches enable builder relationships across geographies and insulate against regional housing softness
3. **Labor retention:** 3.2% monthly turnover vs. 4.0% industry — critical in a labor-constrained trade
4. **Diversification strategy:** Reducing insulation from 74% to 58% of revenue (2013-2025) creates cross-selling opportunities and end-market buffers

The competitive position is stable to improving. Commercial market share gains (+10.7% same-store vs. broader market) demonstrate the platform's ability to extend into adjacent markets. Low customer concentration (top 10 = 14%) prevents bargaining power erosion.

However, the insulation installation market remains fragmented below the two national players. Local competitors can underbid on price during slow periods, and IBP's margin-over-volume strategy could face limits if the downturn deepens.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary risks:**

1. **Accelerating residential downturn (HIGH).** Q1 2026's -11.2% residential same-store decline may worsen if mortgage rates remain above 7% and housing affordability doesn't improve. A further 5-10% volume decline in Q2-Q3 2026 would compress earnings below current consensus.

2. **Tariff/material cost pressure (MODERATE).** 50% aluminum tariffs directly impact the CAS distribution segment. While most insulation is domestically sourced, tariff-induced construction cost inflation reduces overall housing demand — a second-order headwind to volumes.

3. **Insurance cost inflation (MODERATE-HIGH).** General liability/auto reserves surged 35% in FY2025 ($32M to $43.3M). With a 10,400-employee labor force operating on active construction sites, this trend represents a structural margin headwind.

4. **CEO monetization concentration (MODERATE).** Jeff Edwards sold $125.6M in shares plus committed forward contracts on ~$110M additional shares near the $314 local high in March 2026. His remaining ~$375M+ stake aligns interests, but the velocity and timing of selling near cycle-high valuations is a cautionary signal.

5. **Goodwill vulnerability.** Distribution segment goodwill cushion narrowed from 32.1% to 18.4% excess fair value. Further revenue/EBITDA deterioration could trigger impairment testing sensitivity.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($330-350, +10-17%):**
- Housing starts stabilize in H2 2026 as potential rate moderation improves affordability
- Commercial backlog ($163M) converts to sustained high-single-digit growth, offsetting residential weakness
- Acquisition pace accelerates toward $100M+ revenue target
- Energy code tailwinds drive insulation content per home higher, creating secular volume growth independent of starts
- Pricing discipline sustains 34%+ gross margins through the downturn
- Multiple re-rates toward quality premium as cycle stabilization becomes visible

**Bear Case ($200-230, -23% to -33%):**
- Residential contraction accelerates beyond -11% as mortgage rates remain elevated and consumer weakens
- Commercial backlog plateaus or declines as broader economic slowdown reaches non-residential construction (typically lags residential by 6-12 months)
- Insurance cost inflation and tariffs compress margins by 100-200bps
- Acquisition multiples compress buyback ROI as asset values decline
- Stock de-rates toward 18-20x on falling earnings estimates, bringing it closer to peer valuation
- FY2026 EPS of $9.50-10.50 (below current consensus of $11.12) at 20x = $190-210

**Base Case ($270-300, -10% to flat):**
- Revenue flat to slightly down in FY2026 as commercial growth partially offsets residential decline
- Margins stable at 33-34% through pricing discipline
- EPS of $10.50-11.00 (slight miss vs. consensus)
- Multiple of 25-27x sustained given quality/cash flow characteristics
- Dividend yield of 3.1% provides income while waiting

## Conclusion

IBP is a high-quality, well-managed business in a deteriorating cyclical environment. The company's competitive advantages (scale, purchasing power, labor retention, margin discipline) are real and durable. The long-term structural thesis (housing undersupply + energy code tailwinds) remains intact. However, the stock at $299 prices in a recovery that has not yet begun — trading above consensus targets with negative estimate momentum, at a 33% premium to peers, during an accelerating residential downturn.

The risk/reward is unfavorable for new positions. Downside risk of 23-33% to the bear case exceeds upside potential of 10-17% to the bull case, given that: (1) estimates are still falling, (2) the CEO sold $125M+ near the high, (3) the residential cycle has not yet troughed, and (4) the stock trades above analyst consensus. The 3.1% dividend yield provides modest compensation for waiting but is insufficient to offset potential capital losses.

HOLD for existing positions. The business quality warrants patience, but initiating here requires conviction that residential has troughed — a claim unsupported by current data.
