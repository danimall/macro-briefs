## Executive Summary

Antero Midstream is a contractually ring-fenced Appalachian gathering, compression and water-handling business whose revenue comes almost entirely from Antero Resources (AR), which in turn owns 29% of AM and shares its management team. The Q2 2026 print delivered record throughput — gathering volumes of 4,124 MMcf/d, up 19% year over year — and converted it into a 2% increase in Adjusted EBITDA to $288.8M and a 9% decline in net income to $113.5M. That gap is the central analytical question for the stock, and the answer is partly benign and partly not. Roughly $45M of Water Handling revenue carried $40M of matching cost because third-party fluid handling is billed at AM's out-of-pocket cost plus 3%, so revenue and direct operating expense inflate together at ~11% contribution margin. Realized fixed fees actually rose (gathering $0.36 → $0.37/Mcf; fresh water $4.37 → $4.44/Bbl). But fresh-water delivery volumes fell 16% (98 → 82 MBbl/d) while lower-margin other-water volumes rose 131%, and Water Handling swung from +$6.0M of segment operating income to a $2.9M loss. Fixed-fee barrels were exchanged for pass-through barrels, which is an economic event rather than a presentational one.

The balance sheet improved materially, though not through operations. On July 24, 2026 AM received approximately $371M from the six-year Veolia litigation (affirmed by the Colorado Supreme Court on June 23, 2026) and used it plus revolver borrowings to call all $650M of the 5.75% 2028 notes at par on August 8, 2026. Reconstructing from the 10-Q: net debt falls from $3,591.9M to roughly $3,221M, and leverage moves from about 3.17x to about 2.84x against a 5.00x covenant. Annual interest expense should drop roughly $2.6M at current rates, which is a modest identifiable contributor to the upward estimate revisions. The mechanism matters: absent the litigation recovery, leverage would still sit near 3.2x, and organic post-dividend free cash flow is running roughly $80M per quarter against $3.2B of debt. The 2.8x figure should not be extrapolated as a trend.

At $21.49 the shares carry an enterprise value near $13.43B, or ~11.8x LTM Adjusted EBITDA of approximately $1,134M and ~10.9x a 2027 estimate of ~$1,235M implied by management's guidance for high-single-digit EBITDA growth. The $0.225 quarterly dividend has been unchanged for seven-plus quarters and yields 4.20%, below the 6.1% peer median, covered 1.75x by Q2 pre-dividend Adjusted free cash flow. Probability-weighting the scenarios below produces roughly a high-single-digit total return over twelve months, which is approximately fair rather than compelling. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on the name; the bar for repeating it should be higher, and the Q2 print cleared it by delivering genuinely new information on both sides — cash in hand and a smaller debt stack against a loss-making water segment and a second consecutive earnings miss. The two nets to fair value.

## Company Overview

AM operates two segments in the Appalachian Basin, both serving AR under long-dated acreage dedications. Gathering & Processing (Q2 2026 revenue $257.7M, segment operating income $187.6M) comprises low-pressure gathering, centralized compression, new well-pad compression, a 50% interest in a processing and fractionation joint venture operated by MarkWest (MPLX), and a 15% interest in Stonewall Gas Gathering. Water Handling (Q2 revenue $69.5M, segment operating loss $2.9M) sources and delivers fresh water via permanent and surface pipelines and provides wastewater transfer, trucking and disposal, much of it through contracted third parties.

The contract architecture is the asset. AR has dedicated substantially all current and future acreage in West Virginia, Ohio and Pennsylvania; the governing 2019 gathering and compression agreement runs to 2038 and the water services agreement to 2035. Fees are per-unit fixed with CPI escalation (~1.5% in 2026), and certain new-build assets carry a cost-of-service mechanism entitling AM to a 13% return on invested capital over seven years — an unusually favorable construct that is now appearing in reported results ($6.6M of G&P cost-plus revenue and $76K of G&P cost-of-service revenue in Q2, up from nil).

Three transactions reshaped the asset base in 2026: the $1.1B cash acquisition of HG Energy II Midstream (closed February 3), the $400M Utica divestiture (closed February 23, ~$380M net, recorded at an $87M FY2025 loss on long-lived assets), and the Veolia recovery. AM also commenced construction of East Side Express, described by management as its first intrastate bi-directional regional pipeline, in Q2 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Compounding and margin structure.** Adjusted EBITDA grew from $876M in FY2021 to $1,120M in FY2025, a 6.35% CAGR. FY2025 revenue was $1,188.4M (net of $70.7M of customer-relationship amortization presented as a contra) with operating income of $644.7M, a 54.2% operating margin, and net income of $413.2M. Note that the XBRL revenue tag returns ~$1,259.1M gross; margins computed off that figure are understated.

**Cash flow quality is good, with three qualifiers.** 1H 2026 operating cash flow was $492.9M, up 6%. First, income taxes are essentially entirely non-cash: 1H 2026 tax expense of $78.6M was fully deferred, cash taxes paid were $0, and the deferred tax liability rose from $563.0M to $641.6M. GAAP EPS therefore understates distributable cash — the reason a 94% GAAP payout ratio in 1H ($0.45 declared against $0.48 diluted EPS) is comfortable. The NOL shield is finite and its remaining size is not disclosed in what was reviewed; when it exhausts, Adjusted free cash flow falls at flat EBITDA. Second, JV distributions exceeded equity earnings ($71.0M vs $58.5M in 1H), a positive cash spread from assets AM does not operate. Third, accrued liabilities contributed $35.6M of working-capital tailwind.

**Capital intensity and free cash flow.** 1H 2026 capex was $88.6M against a $190–220M full-year budget set on February 11, 2026 — before East Side Express construction began. Q2 Adjusted free cash flow was $186.4M before dividends and $79.6M after, the twelfth consecutive positive post-dividend quarter. Annualizing Q2 gives ~$746M pre-dividend (7.3% of equity value) and ~$318M post-dividend (3.1%), but that run rate embeds a $19.9M working-capital adjustment, zero cash taxes, and no East Side Express spending. A normalized build — ~$1,150M of 2026 EBITDA less ~$205M capex and ~$223M of post-refinancing interest — lands in the same neighborhood pre-dividend and is the figure I would underwrite.

**Debt, covenants and the refinancing.** At 6/30/2026 principal was $3,591.9M: revolver $341.9M at a 5.35% weighted average, plus five note series ($650M 5.75% 2028, $750M 5.375% 2029, $600M 6.625% 2032, $650M 5.75% 2033, $600M 5.75% 2034). Cash was $0 — AM sweeps to the revolver. Covenants require interest coverage ≥2.5x (Q2 actual ~5.2x on $288.8M of EBITDA against $55.7M of interest) and total leverage ≤5.00x (~3.17x). Available revolver capacity was $908M against $1.25B of commitments, priced SOFR + 150–250bp because AM was not in an "Investment Grade Period."

The August 8 redemption of the 2028 notes has three consequences a screen misses. It converts fixed unsecured debt into floating secured debt: the revolver draw rises to approximately $621M, leaving ~$629M of availability, and AM does not hedge revolver interest. Replacing the $650M fixed 5.75% coupon with revolver borrowings at ~5.35% floating saves roughly $2.6M annually at current rates, but each 100bp of rate movement on the ~$621M draw is now worth roughly $6.2M annually. And it commits revolver capacity to a term-debt purpose just as a multi-year build starts. Management has issued $600–650M twice in the past twelve months at 5.75% and will presumably term this out, but that refinancing is not committed. A 2029 maturity cluster remains ($750M notes in June, revolver in July).

**Earnings delivery is the weak spot.** Non-GAAP EPS has missed in three of the last four quarters: -8.9%, +13.6%, -9.2%, -8.0%. Third-party consensus reportedly looked for ~$0.33 in Q2 against $0.27 delivered on in-line revenue — the signature of pass-through mix plus higher interest and amortization rather than a demand shortfall, but it lowers confidence in point estimates. The estimate revision data shows +6.5% current-year and +8.2% next-year revisions, sourced from a single analyst. That is directionally consistent with the interest savings and does not constitute the kind of broad, high-velocity revision cycle that carries predictive weight.

**Book equity mechanics.** Equity fell from $1,972.0M to $1,945.4M in 1H despite $231.8M of net income, because $221.0M of dividends exceeded available retained earnings and $118.6M was charged to APIC. This is Delaware surplus mechanics for a maximum-payout corporation and does not indicate impairment, but it renders book-value multiples, ROE and retained-earnings-based screens (including the Altman X2 term) uninformative for AM. A properly recomputed Z-score from the FY2025 10-K is 2.19 — grey zone, driven by near-zero retained earnings and 0.20x asset turnover, neither of which reflects solvency risk against 3.17x leverage on a 5.00x covenant.

## Growth Analysis

Volume growth is genuine and sourced from a strengthening counterparty. AR's externally reported Q2 production growth of 21% and revenue growth of 29.6% to $1.56B, driven by its own HG Production acquisition, is the direct cause of AM's record throughput. HG Midstream contributed $61.2M of revenue and $12.4M of net income from February 3 to June 30, implying roughly $147M of annualized revenue on a $1.1B purchase price — a rough scaling on a five-month, seasonally unrepresentative stub.

The conversion problem is what must be monitored. Between Q2 2025 and Q2 2026, the fixed-fee share of Water Handling gross revenue fell from 56% to 44% of the half-year total ($81.2M of ~$146.0M to $66.8M of ~$151.7M), and segment operating income went from +$14.3M to -$3.1M for the half. Management attributes the fresh-water decline to "the timing and location of well completions by Antero Resources" and guides to high-single-digit EBITDA growth in 2027 from water integration projects. The competing explanation, which the 10-K's own risk factors contemplate, is that AR has durably reduced water intensity per stage or shifted to locations AM's fixed-fee pipelines do not reach, in which case the highest-margin revenue line is structurally impaired. Two more quarters of fresh-water volume data will discriminate; nothing available now settles it.

East Side Express is the first credible path to growth outside the affiliate. An east-west bi-directional intrastate line connecting supply to long-haul pipelines and local markets could, in principle, serve volumes AM does not currently touch. Third-party revenue was $0.6M in 1H 2026 (~0.1% of revenue) and zero in Q2, so the base is nil. A single secondary summary of the July 30 call put the project at $200–300M over two to three years; neither the press release nor the 10-Q contains any capital figure, and the 10-Q does not mention the project. If accurate, $67–150M annually on top of a $190–220M base budget is a 30–70% step-up in capital intensity that would compress post-dividend free cash flow toward or below $200M annualized. I treat the project as confirmed and its economics as unverified.

Growth prospects score 5.5: real volumes and a company-sourced 2027 growth target, offset by poor EBITDA conversion, an unresolved water-margin question, a nil third-party base, and growth that is entirely a derivative of one affiliate's capital program.

The reported 25.9x trailing multiple overstates expensiveness because GAAP earnings absorb ~$91M of annualized customer-relationship amortization (~$0.19 per share, non-cash) and the FY2025 base carried an $87M Utica loss. On adjusted earnings the multiple is closer to 19.5x. Reported P/E will also be distorted in the other direction for four quarters once the ~$371M Veolia gain lands in Q3 2026.

The supplied peer set is heterogeneous — VNOM is a mineral royalty vehicle and INSW and LPG are tanker owners, whose 7.5x and 9.6x multiples are unrelated to a fee-based gathering franchise. The two structurally comparable names are DT Midstream (31.1x P/E, 2.5% yield, dry-gas pipelines) and Kinetik (19.3x P/E, 6.8% yield, Permian G&P). AM at ~19.5x adjusted with a 4.2% yield sits between them on both axes, which is where a single-customer G&P with sub-investment-grade revolver pricing arguably belongs. The peer median yield of 6.1% against AM's 4.20% is the cleanest evidence that the equity is not cheap on an income basis, particularly with the dividend unchanged for seven-plus quarters.

Analyst positioning is unusual and worth stating precisely: seven covering analysts show six holds and one strong sell, yet the price target range is $23.00–$26.00 with a $24.00 median — the low target sits 7% above spot. Targets above the price with no buy ratings generally indicate stale targets or unenthusiastic modelled upside rather than independent confirmation of value. I do not treat them as a floor here; AM is not an inflection stock with accelerating estimates, so the "targets lag reality" dynamic does not apply.

On balance the shares are approximately fairly valued: ~11.8x LTM EV/EBITDA is a defensible multiple for a 2038-dedicated fee stream with 54% operating margins and 2.84x leverage, but it is not a discount, and the sub-peer yield plus the pending capital step-up removes the margin of safety. Valuation scores 5.0.

## Competitive Landscape

AM faces essentially no competitive threat to existing throughput. The acreage dedication removes the possibility of AR taking volumes elsewhere, the systems are physically integrated and uneconomic to duplicate, and fees escalate with CPI. Processing and fractionation JV assets ran at 100% utilization in Q2.

The trajectory is more mixed than the moat description suggests. First, dedication is not a volume commitment, and this is the most commonly misread feature of the business. Total minimum future lease cash flows under all gathering and compression agreements were $866.3M at 6/30/2026, declining from $231.5M in 2027 to $89.9M in 2030. Against a G&C revenue run rate of roughly $1,086M annually, the 2027 contracted minimum covers about 21%. The Marcellus MVC has had no remaining minimum payments since 1/1/2025; Mountaineer minimum compression fees have expired; and there are no MVCs at all on low-pressure gathering or fresh-water pipelines, the two largest volume lines. These figures are in-substance fixed lease payments and are not backlog. Roughly four-fifths of revenue depends on AR choosing to drill.

Second, diversification is not happening. Third-party revenue went from $0.5M in Q2 2025 to $0 in Q2 2026. The shift toward cost-plus-3% billing changes revenue mix without changing counterparty exposure — those are different measures, and only the latter reduces risk. Third, AR may dispose of dedicated acreage free of the dedication and without AM's consent, up to the cumulative net acres it has acquired since the agreements' effective dates; the HG Production purchase enlarges that basket.

Third-party commentary linking Appalachian gas to AI data-center load growth (a fund letter discussing AR's role in AI infrastructure) is not an AM disclosure, and AM's filings contain no data-center contracts. The regulatory backdrop is a genuine tailwind for a company trying to build new pipeline — narrowed NEPA scope, judicial deference following *Seven County*, proposed WOTUS narrowing, repeal of the methane charge rule — which lowers the permitting cost of East Side Express relative to 2021–2023 conditions.

Competitive position scores 5.4: an entrenched contractual position with deteriorating revenue quality and no progress on the diversification the company itself identifies as strategy.

## Risk Assessment

**Counterparty concentration.** Substantially all revenue comes from AR; receivables from AR were $135.8M at 6/30/2026, unsecured. AM's own disclosure directs investors to read AR's risk factors. AR's current strength — 21% production growth, larger hedge book, better liquidity — mitigates near-term volume volatility, and AM says as much. But the exposure is total, and a change of control at AR would simultaneously change AM's sole customer, 29% shareholder, shared management team and contract counterparty. AM's change-of-control puts at 101% are triggered by a change of control of Antero Midstream Partners, not of AR. Speculation that AR is a takeout candidate is single-source and unconfirmed; I treat it as market commentary.

**Intangible impairment exposure.** Of the $1,103.1M HG purchase price, $622.1M (56%) was allocated to a customer-relationship intangible whose counterparty is AM's own affiliate, valued by DCF at a 14%/16% WACC and amortized over 21 years. The allocation is preliminary until roughly February 2027 and adjustments were already recorded in Q2. Carrying value is directly exposed to AR's drilling decisions on that specific acreage. AM has no seller indemnification for representation breaches, relying on an insurance policy with exclusions. This is standard acquisition accounting, not evidence of overpayment; it is nonetheless the largest single balance-sheet item that could move on a change in AR's plan.

**Governance and conflicts.** All executive officers and certain directors serve both companies; personnel are concurrently employed by AR, which charged AM $6M of direct operating and $7M of G&A in Q2. AR is under no obligation to adopt a strategy favoring AM. The HG-related commercial terms being billed today (cost-plus-3%, 13% cost-of-service) are described as "agreed in principle," with the amended agreements not confirmed as executed. Charter features are shareholder-unfriendly: classified board, removal only for cause, no written consent, 66⅔% supermajority, opt-out of DGCL 203, corporate opportunity waiver. A $68,750 arrearage exists on the de minimis Series A preferred held by The Antero Foundation.

**Rate and refinancing.** Floating exposure on a ~$621M revolver draw is unhedged (~$6.2M per 100bp), secured debt is now larger and structurally senior to the notes, and the terming-out of that draw is not committed. The 2029 cluster is the next real maturity event.

**Inflation asymmetry.** Fees are fixed with ~1.5% CPI escalation while direct operating expense rose 34% year over year in Q2; steel tariffs are specifically flagged in the 10-K. Cost inflation on the pass-through book is neutral by construction, but on the fixed-fee book it is a margin drag.

**What is not a risk.** The Q2 10-Q states there have been no material changes to the risk factors in the FY2025 10-K; no risk factors were added. The 8-K record from 2024 through July 2026 shows no non-reliance filing, no auditor change and no restatement, and disclosure controls were concluded effective at 6/30/2026.

Risk factors score 4.7. Concentration itself is well known and priced; what is less priced is the intangible-impairment channel, the capital step-up compressing post-dividend free cash flow, and the possibility that the fresh-water margin loss is structural.

### Options Market Signal

The options market prices no near-term event. Near-term ATM IV is 22.4% against 19.8% one-year realized (a fair 2.6-point spread), the IV percentile within the current chain is 5%, put/call skew is balanced at 0.9%, and the term structure is flat (22.4% near, 22.6% far). Put/call volume is 0.17 and open-interest ratio 0.38, both call-tilted. Since July 19, ATM IV fell from 27.5% to 22.4% while the price declined 5.8% — falling volatility into a falling price is characteristic of orderly de-rating rather than stress, and it removes the pre-earnings uncertainty premium. Short interest is 3.0% of float with 3.9 days to cover, so the bearish view is uncrowded and no squeeze dynamic exists in either direction. The options data neither confirms nor contradicts the fundamental view; it argues that the mix and margin questions here are being treated by the market as a slow-resolving operating issue rather than a binary.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($26.50, +23% price, ~+27% total return).** Fresh-water volumes recover as AR's completion schedule normalizes, restoring Water Handling to positive segment income and validating management's high-single-digit 2027 EBITDA growth guidance toward ~$1,250M. The ~$2.6M annual interest reduction from the 2028 redemption flows through, the revolver draw is termed out at or near existing coupon levels, and leverage grinds toward 2.5x on organic cash flow. East Side Express is scoped at the lower end of the reported range with a demand-side or third-party commitment attached, converting AM's first non-affiliate revenue and justifying a 12.5x multiple. At 12.5x on $1,250M less ~$3.1B of net debt, equity is $12.53B, or $26.40 per share, plus $0.90 of dividends.

**Bear case ($17.25, -20% price, ~-16% total return).** The 16% fresh-water volume decline proves structural — AR's water intensity per stage falls further or its well locations move away from AM's fixed-fee pipelines — leaving the highest-margin line permanently smaller and Water Handling near breakeven. East Side Express is scoped at $300M with capex overrun, pushing post-dividend free cash flow below $150M annualized and stalling deleveraging near 3.2x. Consensus, already at six holds and one strong sell after three misses in four quarters, cuts 2027 estimates; the multiple compresses to 10x on flat EBITDA of ~$1,150M against $3.4B of net debt, implying $8.1B of equity, or $17.07. The 52-week low of $16.96 corroborates that this level has recent market precedent. Tail risks not in this case: a reduction to the $622M HG intangible, or AR disposing of dedicated acreage.

**Base case ($22.75, +5.9% price, ~+10% total return).** 2027 Adjusted EBITDA of ~$1,235M (the low end of management's high-single-digit guidance, reflecting a partial water recovery), net debt of ~$3.3B as East Side Express spending offsets retained cash flow, and an 11.4x multiple — unchanged from today's ~11.8x LTM basis on a higher forward number. That gives equity of ~$10.77B, or $22.70 per share, plus $0.90 of dividends.

Probability-weighting at 25% bull / 50% base / 25% bear gives an expected total return of roughly +8%. That is approximately a fair risk-adjusted outcome for a levered, single-customer infrastructure vehicle, and it sits below the threshold that would justify new capital. Energy positions in this environment have not compensated for their beta, and AM offers neither the yield support of higher-payout peers nor the volume optionality of a diversified operator.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income). Reassessment point: Q3 2026 results, late October 2026**, which will carry the ~$371M Veolia gain, the first full quarter of post-refinancing interest, fresh-water volume data, and any formal disclosure of East Side Express capital.

Existing holders are compensated with a 4.20% dividend covered 1.75x by pre-dividend Adjusted free cash flow and unchanged for seven-plus quarters. Prospective investors have no entry signal at $21.49.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires two of the following:**
1. Fresh-water delivery volumes recover to ≥95 MBbl/d for two consecutive quarters with Water Handling segment operating income back above $10M per quarter, confirming the "timing" explanation over the "structural" one.
2. East Side Express capital disclosed in a filing at ≤$300M total with at least one non-affiliate or demand-side commitment attached, and 2027 capex guidance held below $300M.
3. Price toward $19.00 — approximately 10.4x 2027E EBITDA and a 4.7% yield — which would restore a margin of safety without any change in the operating case.

**Downgrade to REDUCE on any of the following:**
1. Withdrawal or reduction of the high-single-digit 2027 Adjusted EBITDA growth guidance.
2. 2027 capex guided above $300M without corresponding cost-of-service or MVC coverage, taking post-dividend Adjusted free cash flow below ~$150M annualized.
3. Any impairment of, or reduction in the carrying value of, the $622.1M HG customer-relationship intangible; or disclosure that AR has disposed of dedicated acreage free of the dedication.
4. Net leverage back above 3.25x, or a revolver term-out priced materially above the 5.75% coupon level AM achieved in December 2025.

## Conclusion

AM's Q2 2026 report contained a real balance-sheet improvement and a real operating question, and they roughly offset. The Veolia recovery is cash in hand, the 2028 notes are gone, and leverage is below 3x, though the swap to floating revolver borrowings only reduces annual interest by roughly $2.6M at current rates while adding unhedged rate exposure. None of that is repeatable, and the mechanism was litigation rather than operations. Meanwhile record 19% volume growth produced 2% EBITDA growth, the water segment posted an operating loss, non-GAAP EPS missed for the third time in four quarters, and third-party revenue was zero. The 2027 contracted minimum of $231.5M covers only about 21% of the current gathering and compression run rate, so the acreage dedication protects against competitive displacement rather than against a slowdown in AR's drilling.

At ~11.8x LTM EV/EBITDA, ~19.5x adjusted earnings and a 4.20% yield against a 6.1% peer median, the shares are priced approximately where a single-customer Appalachian G&P with 2.84x leverage and no third-party revenue should be priced. Expected total return of roughly 8% over twelve months, with a bear case that maps to the 52-week low, does not clear the bar for new capital. HOLD for the covered distribution; revisit at $19 or on two quarters of fresh-water volume recovery.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the 2028-note redemption's interest benefit is ~$2.6M annually (fixed 5.75% replaced by ~5.35% floating revolver borrowings), not ~$22M — the report's original "~$22M of newly freed interest" and repeated references to it as a driver of estimate revisions and holder compensation overstated the balance-sheet benefit, which is materially smaller and comes with added floating-rate exposure.
