**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Eastman Chemical is a diversified specialty-materials producer working through a broad chemicals downcycle. FY2025 revenue fell 7% to $8.75B, net income dropped 48% to $474M, and adjusted diluted EPS fell to $5.42 from $7.89. The deterioration carried into Q1 2026: sales down 5%, adjusted EPS $1.09 versus $1.91 a year earlier, a 43% decline. The pressure is broad-based: volumes fell 6% in 2025, gross margin compressed from 24.4% to 21.1%, and the Chemical Intermediates segment swung to an operating loss on Asian oversupply and weak commodity conditions. Only Additives & Functional Products grew.

Management has shifted into balance-sheet-protection mode. 2026 capex is guided down to roughly $400M from $546–599M, described as maintenance plus limited growth capital; buybacks slowed to $100M in 2025 and were zero in Q1'26; and in February 2026 the company amended its revolver to temporarily loosen the maximum leverage covenant through Q2 2027. That combination of capex cut, paused repurchases, and pre-emptive covenant headroom is a defensive posture signaling management expects a prolonged trough. The dividend was nonetheless raised to $0.84/quarter, extending a 16-year growth streak at a ~5.0% yield covered by roughly 62% of adjusted EPS.

The stock has fallen 13.8% since mid-year 2025 and sits near $68.86, in the lower half of its $56.11–$83.47 52-week range. This is an income-hold. The dividend is durable and well-covered; the balance sheet is investment-grade with adequate liquidity; but the earnings trajectory is still negative, near-term estimate revisions are turning down, and there is no insider-buying conviction signal or identifiable catalyst to re-rate the multiple. The bull case requires a cyclical recovery in Fibers and CI plus scaled contribution from molecular recycling, neither of which is visible in current data.

## Company Overview

Eastman operates four segments built on integrated chemistry streams (polyester, cellulosic biopolymer/acetyl, alkylamines, olefins), with ~13,000 employees, 36 manufacturing sites, and roughly 55% of revenue generated outside the US and Canada.

- **Advanced Materials ($2.88B FY25 sales)** — the largest EBIT contributor. Saflex PVB interlayers for auto and architectural glass, performance/window films (LLumar, SunTek, V-KOOL), and Tritan specialty plastics. Exposed to building/construction and automotive demand; competes with Sekisui, Kuraray, 3M, XPEL, Covestro.
- **Additives & Functional Products ($2.88B)** — the most resilient segment. Coatings additives, care additives, functional amines, and specialty fluids (Therminol, Skydrol). Benefits from cost-pass-through contracts and growth in water treatment, medical, and electronics. Competes with BASF, Dow, Celanese.
- **Chemical Intermediates ($1.93B)** — sells excess acetyl/olefin intermediates and plasticizers. Cyclical and commodity-exposed, loss-making in 2025 and Q1'26.
- **Fibers ($1.05B)** — historically high-margin. Acetate tow for cigarette filters, Naia textile fibers, nonwovens. Structural decline plus cyclical destocking.

The strategic centerpiece is molecular recycling (methanolysis), where Eastman operates the world's largest polyester molecular recycling facility at Kingsport under the Renew brand.

## Financial Analysis

The financial picture is a chemicals company in a cyclical trough with structural soft spots.

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 | Q1'26 (YoY) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $9.38B | $8.75B (-7%) | $2.18B (-5%) |
| Adj. EBIT | $1.30B | $930M (-28%) | $200M (-36%) |
| Net income | $905M | $474M (-48%) | $107M (-41%) |
| Adj. diluted EPS | $7.89 | $5.42 | $1.09 vs $1.91 |
| Operating cash flow | $1.29B | $970M | -$137M (seasonal) |
| FCF | — | $424M | — |

Several dynamics matter for scoring:

**The revenue decline is volume-led,** driven by acetate-tow destocking (Fibers -20%), building/construction and automotive weakness (Advanced Materials), and Asian competitive price pressure (CI -10%). AFP was the sole grower at +1%. The 330bps gross margin compression is a broad margin event rather than an isolated segment issue.

**Chemical Intermediates is loss-making** — segment EBIT of -$60M in 2025 (from +$101M in 2024) and -$18M in Q1'26. The mechanism: China capacity additions in acetyl and olefin derivatives are pushing global prices below Eastman's cost recovery, and management's cost cuts cannot fully offset a simultaneous demand-and-oversupply squeeze.

**Leverage is drifting up.** Net debt rose from $4.22B (2025) to $4.55B (Q1'26) after the company issued $600M of 4.5% notes due 2031. D/E is ~0.71 and the current ratio 1.37, adequate for an investment-grade chemicals producer, but the Altman Z of 2.67 sits in the grey zone, and goodwill of $3.66B is ~25% of total assets. The February 2026 covenant amendment is the clearest quantitative tell: a company creating headroom before it needs it expects the earnings-to-leverage ratio to remain stressed.

**Cash generation is holding up better than earnings.** FCF of $424M in 2025 comfortably covers the ~$380M dividend outlay, and the 2026 capex cut protects that coverage into a further downturn. The Q1'26 operating cash outflow of $137M is seasonal working-capital timing, not a signal.

**Earnings quality caveat:** Q1'26 was flattered by a one-time $22M IEEPA tariff-refund benefit recognized in cost of sales following Supreme Court/CIT rulings, plus cost-reduction initiatives. Strip that out and underlying volume/price trends remained negative in three of four segments. The 1.7% Q1 EPS "beat" against a $1.07 estimate should be read with that in mind.

## Growth Analysis

There is no near-term organic growth thesis. Revenue has declined for two consecutive years, and Q1'26 extended the trend. Estimate revisions confirm negative momentum on the lines that matter most for the next several quarters:

- Current Quarter EPS: $1.81, up 5.7% over 90 days but flat (+0.3%) over 30 days
- Next Quarter: $1.92, down 2.2% over 30 days
- Current Year: $6.31, down 0.9% over 30 days
- Next Year: $6.94, down 1.1% over 30 days

The 90-day revisions are modestly positive because they capture the trough recovery baked into consensus, but the more recent 30-day moves are uniformly negative, and the near-term direction is down. This is the opposite of the +30% signal pattern that would justify overriding trailing multiples; growth here is decelerating from an already-depressed base, not accelerating off a trough.

The structural growth story rests on two pillars. Molecular recycling is real but small: the Kingsport methanolysis plant ran roughly 2.5x its 2024 output in 2025 and reportedly added ~$60M of incremental earnings, meaningful but modest against a ~$930M adjusted-EBIT base. The DOE termination of the Longview PET recycling grant in May 2025 removed a subsidy underpinning the second-plant expansion and exposed the strategy's policy dependence. The second pillar, the Naia textile pivot in Fibers, is meant to offset structural acetate-tow decline but is unproven at scale.

The realistic 2026 setup is a Fibers destocking normalization (cyclical, should improve) capped by acetate tow's terminal tobacco-driven decline, with CI unlikely to recover meaningfully while Chinese oversupply persists. That is a stabilization-then-modest-recovery path, not a growth story.

## Valuation Assessment

At $68.86 the stock trades at ~19.9x trailing EPS of $4.10 and ~10.9x the current-year adjusted estimate of $6.31. Against Specialty Chemicals peer medians of 19.2x P/E and 1.4% yield, EMN screens as roughly in line on headline multiples with a materially higher dividend yield (~5.0% versus 1.4%).

The peer set is heterogeneous and cyclically distorted: Westlake (-6.9x) and Celanese (-5.2x) show negative trailing earnings, Sensient trades at 36.6x, and Axalta and NewMarket cluster near 19x. This dispersion reflects a sector caught mid-downcycle. On forward adjusted earnings, EMN's ~10.9x is a discount to the cleaner peers, but that discount is warranted rather than a mispricing: three of four segments carry negative trajectories, one segment is loss-making, and an $812M goodwill balance sits on impairment watch.

The cycle-adjusted case is the strongest bull argument. If Eastman reverts toward its 2024 adjusted-EBIT level (~$1.3B) and EPS recovers toward the high-$7 range, the current price would represent roughly 9x mid-cycle earnings. But that reversion requires CI to stop losing money and Fibers destocking to fully clear, neither of which is in evidence today. Absent a specific catalyst, cheapness relative to mid-cycle earnings is a value proposition contingent on a cycle turn that has not begun.

Analyst targets frame the range: low $69.00 (essentially at spot), mean $82.73, median $82.00, high $100.00. With the low target essentially at spot and the mean implying ~20% upside, the target dispersion reflects analyst disagreement about the timing and strength of any recovery. I do not treat these as floors: the estimate-revision trend is turning down, not up, so the guardrail against reading targets as leading indicators applies here.

Materials is among the weakest sectors for stock-selection alpha historically, and cheapness alone has been an unreliable signal. A discounted multiple on a company in a genuine downcycle with structural soft spots is more likely a value trap than an asymmetric setup until a recovery catalyst appears. The valuation score reflects a fairly-priced-to-modestly-discounted stock with no identifiable near-term re-rating trigger.

## Competitive Landscape

Eastman's portfolio is a mix of genuine specialty franchises and cyclical/commodity exposure. AFP demonstrates the value of the specialty mix — it grew in 2025 on cost-pass-through contracts and secular demand in water treatment, medical, and electronics. Advanced Materials holds strong positions in auto-safety interlayers (Saflex) and specialty plastics (Tritan), but is currently pressured by building/construction and automotive softness.

The competitive trajectory is deteriorating on the weaker end of the portfolio. Chemical Intermediates is losing money because Chinese acetyl and olefin capacity additions are structurally pressuring global prices, a competitive-position erosion rather than merely a demand cycle. Fibers faces the terminal decline of acetate tow demand as tobacco consumption falls, with top-10 customer concentration at ~65% of segment revenue adding fragility. The Naia textile-fiber pivot is the intended offset but has not scaled.

Five-year cumulative TSR of 75.81 against a peer group at 119.03 quantifies the relative underperformance and is consistent with multi-year earnings stagnation. The molecular-recycling positioning gives Eastman a genuine circular-economy differentiator, but at ~$60M of current earnings contribution the strategic narrative outpaces the financial reality, and the DOE grant loss underscored its subsidy dependence. On balance the competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding, with the specialty franchises (AFP, parts of AM) offsetting structural weakness in CI and Fibers.

## Risk Assessment

**Performance-films goodwill impairment ($812M).** In the FY25 10-K this was the only reporting unit whose fair value did not "significantly exceed" carrying value. Management's ±50bp sensitivity test cleared the hurdle, but with auto-aftermarket demand soft and a German films line closed in Q1'26, this is a live impairment watch item reiterated in the Q1'26 10-Q. A write-down would be non-cash but would dent reported earnings and sentiment.

**Covenant stress signal.** The February 2026 revolver amendment is a defensive tell about the earnings/leverage path. It preserves financial flexibility but confirms management expects sustained pressure.

**Structural Fibers decline.** Acetate tow is in secular decline layered on cyclical destocking; Fibers EBIT fell 38% in 2025 and 49% in Q1'26. Customer concentration (~65% in top 10) amplifies the risk.

**CI commodity cyclicality / China oversupply.** A demand-plus-oversupply squeeze management cannot fully offset; loss-making with no clear near-term recovery.

**Molecular-recycling policy dependence.** The DOE Longview grant termination exposed subsidy risk to the expansion strategy.

**Environmental liabilities:** $282–508M undiscounted remediation range plus legacy Solutia/Monsanto tort indemnity structure.

**Tariff/trade whipsaw:** the $22M IEEPA refund flattered Q1'26 but the underlying tariff exposure runs both ways across segments.

Several of these risks are partly priced into the depressed multiple, which limits the downside from the current level, but the goodwill and covenant items are specific, identifiable overhangs that keep the risk score below neutral.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is calm and does not corroborate acute stress. Near-term ATM IV is 35.8% versus 39.9% trailing historical volatility, a -4.1% spread that makes near-dated vol cheap; IV percentile within the current chain is only 6%. Put/call skew is balanced (0.6%), the put/call OI ratio is a low 0.18, and the term structure is in normal contango. Since late May, ATM IV fell from 42.7% to 35.8% as the price declined 7.4%; falling IV alongside a lower price suggests resolved near-term uncertainty and complacency rather than fear. There is a modest uptick in the 3-month tenor (P/C vol ratio 2.35, skew -4.0%) around the summer earnings window, worth monitoring, but nothing in the options structure signals informed positioning for a sharp move. The implied ±6% one-month move is unremarkable for a cyclical chemicals name.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($90–100):** The chemicals downcycle troughs in 2026. Fibers destocking clears, CI stabilizes as Chinese oversupply moderates, and building/construction and auto demand recover. Adjusted EBIT reverts toward $1.2–1.3B and adjusted EPS toward $7+. Molecular recycling scales beyond $60M of contribution, adding a growth kicker and multiple support from the circular-economy positioning. At ~13x recovered earnings the stock re-rates toward $90–100, matching the analyst high. The ~5.0% dividend pays the investor to wait.

**Bear Case ($52–56):** The downcycle proves prolonged. CI stays loss-making on structural Chinese overcapacity, Fibers' acetate-tow decline accelerates faster than Naia can offset, and the $812M performance-films goodwill is impaired. Leverage continues drifting up, pressuring the investment-grade rating and eventually the dividend. Earnings stay in the mid-$5 range or fall further, and the stock retests the 52-week low near $56 or below.

**Base Case ($70–78):** Earnings stabilize near the current run-rate through 2026 with modest sequential improvement in the back half. The dividend is maintained and raised at a low-single-digit pace, the capex cut protects FCF coverage, and no goodwill impairment is taken. The stock grinds in a $70–78 range, delivering a mid-single-digit total return dominated by the dividend, with limited multiple expansion until a durable earnings recovery is confirmed.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold). Reassessment timeline: next earnings (Q2 2026, late July/early August).**

This is a hold for the dividend, not for appreciation. The ~5.0% yield is well-covered (~62% of adjusted EPS, $424M FCF against ~$380M dividend outlay) and backed by a 16-year growth streak, providing a return floor while the cycle plays out. There is no near-term catalyst to re-rate the multiple and the recent estimate-revision trend is negative, so the appreciation case is speculative on a cycle turn that has not started.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive segment volume growth across Fibers and CI, with CI EBIT returning to breakeven or positive.
- Current-year and next-year EPS estimate revisions turning decisively positive (>5% over 30 days) rather than the current declines.
- Evidence molecular recycling is scaling past ~$100M of annual earnings contribution, or clustered open-market C-suite buying appearing at depressed prices.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A performance-films goodwill impairment ($812M unit) confirming structural rather than cyclical demand loss.
- Any signal of dividend risk — FCF falling below dividend coverage, a further leverage-covenant amendment, or a rating downgrade to the BBB-/Baa3 edge.
- CI losses widening rather than stabilizing, indicating permanent competitive displacement by Chinese capacity.

## Conclusion

Eastman Chemical is a specialty-materials franchise caught in a genuine chemicals downcycle, with earnings roughly halved from 2024, leverage drifting up to $4.55B net debt, and management in an explicit balance-sheet-protection stance. The trajectory of the scores continues to soften; this analysis confirms rather than reverses the decline seen over the prior two reports, driven by the covenant amendment, the CI loss, negative near-term estimate revisions, and the absence of any insider-buying conviction.

The offsetting positives are real: a well-covered ~5.0% dividend with a 16-year growth streak, adequate investment-grade liquidity, FCF coverage protected by the capex cut, and a resilient AFP segment plus optionality in molecular recycling. Those are enough to hold the stock for income, but not to buy it for appreciation ahead of a confirmed cycle turn. The recommendation is HOLD on an income basis, with the next earnings report as the natural reassessment point. Investors seeking capital appreciation should wait for evidence that Fibers destocking has cleared and CI has stopped bleeding before adding; income-oriented holders are being paid a covered yield to wait.
