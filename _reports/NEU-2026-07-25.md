**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

NewMarket Corporation is a Richmond-based holding company whose economics rest on two very different businesses. The first, Petroleum Additives (Afton Chemical plus Ethyl Corporation), generates roughly 93% of revenue and sits inside a four-player global oligopoly (Afton, Lubrizol, Infineum, Chevron Oronite) protected by formulation IP, OEM co-development relationships, and switching costs. The second, Specialty Materials, is small (~7% of revenue) but strategically the reason to pay attention: a defense/aerospace platform built through the $681M AMPAC acquisition (ammonium perchlorate, the key oxidizer in solid rocket motors) and the $218M Calca Solutions acquisition (Ultra Pure high-purity hydrazine, a satellite/spacecraft monopropellant). Together these represent ~$1B of capital deployed since 2024 into two of the most supply-constrained niches in the U.S. defense and space supply chain.

The investment case is a slow re-rating story. The market prices NEU largely as a mature, flat-growth specialty chemical (~17.6x trailing earnings, ~1.6% yield), while the specialty-materials build-out — including a live +50% ammonium-perchlorate capacity expansion completing late 2026 — repositions the company toward structurally growing, high-qualification-barrier end markets. Management's capital allocation is best-in-class: a $126M buyback in Q1 2026 at ~$617 (below today's $779), a dividend raise to $3.00/quarter, leverage held near 1.3x versus a 3.75x covenant, and a CEO paid just $3.7M (a 2.3x CEO-to-NEO ratio) against a five-year TSR of 191.9 versus a peer group at 109.1. A director made an open-market purchase of 400 shares at $619 in February 2026, the highest-signal insider action.

The offset is that the core franchise is not growing. Revenue was flat-to-down over four years ($2,765M in 2022 to $2,725M in 2025), petroleum-additives volumes fell 4.9% in 2025 and 6.9% in Q1 2026, and specialty-materials operating profit halved in Q1 2026 (to $12.4M from $23.2M) on AMPAC product-mix timing despite the added Calca revenue. The specialty-materials thesis is real but lumpy, exposed to government appropriations and shutdown risk, and carries $808M of the company's $941M intangibles in a segment producing only ~$47M of annual operating profit. We rate NEU HOLD with a constructive bias, owning it for its compounding discipline and defense/space optionality rather than a dated near-term catalyst.

## Company Overview

NewMarket operates two reportable segments:

**Petroleum Additives (~93% of revenue).** Through Afton Chemical, NEU is one of the "big four" global lubricant and fuel additive suppliers. Lubricant additives are ~85% of segment sales (~$2.16B), with engine oil additives representing ~70% of that lubricant market; fuel additives are the remaining ~15% (~$378M). Ethyl Corporation supplies antiknock compounds and contract manufacturing. The competitive structure is a rational oligopoly with high technical barriers, deep relationships with OEMs and oil majors, and roughly 1,400 patents. Afton positions itself as a top supplier of EV transmission fluids, a partial hedge against the long-term erosion of ICE engine-oil demand.

**Specialty Materials (~7% of revenue).** This is the defense/aerospace pivot, assembled through two acquisitions: AMPAC (January 2024, $681M net), the leading U.S. producer of ammonium perchlorate for DoD tactical/strategic missiles, NASA, and commercial launch, where few foreign producers are qualified for U.S. programs; and Calca Solutions (October 2025, $218M), the nation's leading producer of high-purity hydrazine, which has supplied the DoD Defense Logistics Agency for 70-plus years.

The company employs roughly 2,050 people with a very low ~4.1% resignation rate, and generates about 61% of sales outside the United States. Share count is only ~9.2M (down from ~9.5M a year ago), which produces the ~$780 nominal share price and ~$44 trailing EPS. The Gottwald family controls the company, and CEO/Chairman Thomas E. Gottwald (age 65) has run it with an explicit long-term orientation.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is flat; margins did the work.** The four-year picture is a margin-recovery story followed by a modest give-back:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $2,765M | $2,698M | $2,787M | $2,725M |
| Gross profit | $640M | $773M | $886M | $857M |
| Operating income | $355M | $483M | $590M | $544M |
| Net income | $280M | $389M | $462M | $419M |

Gross margin rose from ~23% in 2022 to ~32% in 2024 as post-supply-shock raw-material costs normalized. 2025 gave some back: revenue -2.2%, net income -9.3% ($462M to $419M) on lower petroleum-additives shipments (-4.9% volume), lower selling prices, and higher operating costs (network-optimization one-time charges, higher R&D), partly offset by lower raw materials.

**Q1 2026 confirmed continued softness.** Net sales fell 4.4% YoY ($670M vs $701M), net income 6.3% ($118M vs $126M), and EPS printed $12.62 vs $13.26, with petroleum-additives volume down 6.9%. Specialty-materials operating profit was driven by AMPAC product-mix and timing volatility that management repeatedly flags as inherent to the segment.

**The balance sheet and cash flow are the strength.** Altman Z of 2.85 sits in the grey zone due to moderate leverage, not distress; current ratio is 2.53, ROE 23.5%, ROA 12%, net margin 15.4%. Revolver leverage is just 1.36x against a 3.75x covenant. 2025 operating cash flow of $569M funded the Calca purchase, $106M of dividends, $78M of capex, $77M of buybacks, and net debt reduction. Long-term debt was $940M at Q1 2026, essentially all fixed-rate except the revolver, which insulates the company from the higher-for-longer rate path.

**Capital allocation is aggressive and well-timed.** Q1 2026 repurchases covered 203,543 shares under a $500M program (January 2025–December 2027) with ~$303M remaining, and the dividend was raised to $3.00/quarter from $2.75. Buying back stock at ~$617 that now trades at $779 is a ~26% realized return on that capital in months.

**Capex is inflecting.** 2026 capex is guided to $100–150M (up from $78M in 2025), including a ~$100M AMPAC expansion adding a new ammonium-perchlorate line to complete late 2026. Rising capex here signals management's confidence in forward solid-rocket-motor demand rather than a maintenance drag.

One caveat: the U.S. pension plan is roughly $586M overfunded with an equity-heavy allocation and an aggressive 8.0% expected-return assumption, contributing ~$30M/yr of non-operating "other income." A market drawdown would reverse this quickly, which means a portion of reported earnings quality is market-dependent.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story splits cleanly by segment. Petroleum additives is a mature, low-single-digit market at best, and NEU is deliberately shedding low-margin business, which suppresses reported volume while defending the ~20–22% segment operating margin. This is a rational, margin-first posture, but it is not a growth engine. Long-term, EV adoption threatens the ~70% of the lubricant market tied to ICE engine oils, and drain-interval extension chronically offsets volume gains. Afton's EV-transmission-fluid position is a hedge, not a growth catalyst that offsets the base decline.

The growth optionality lives in specialty materials. Solid-rocket-motor and missile demand (both tactical and strategic) and satellite propulsion are structurally growing, and the U.S. faces genuine domestic supply constraints in exactly the two products NEU now dominates. The AMPAC capacity expansion is the clearest management tell that forward demand is strong. If that capacity fills at reasonable margins, specialty materials could grow from ~$47M of annual operating profit into a materially larger contributor over a multi-year horizon.

The near-term reality is that this segment is small and lumpy. The Q1 2026 operating-profit halving even with Calca added underscores that quarterly extrapolation is dangerous. There are no analyst price targets or published consensus estimates in the provided data, and no earnings-surprise track record with estimates, which limits confidence in near-term forward numbers. The 12-month growth thesis rests on the AMPAC ramp and stabilization of petroleum-additives volumes, both of which the upcoming Q2 print will begin to inform. The estimate-revision signal that would normally sharpen a growth call is absent here, so we anchor Growth Prospects at a middling 6.0: the structural optionality is real, but neither segment is delivering visible acceleration today.

## Valuation Assessment

NEU trades at ~17.6x trailing earnings (EPS $44.44, price $779.33), with a ~1.6% dividend yield and a ~$7.2B market cap. Against specialty-chemicals peers, the P/E looks inexpensive: the peer median is 33.9x (AXTA 19.1x, EMN 21.2x, CE negative, BCPC 33.9x, ESI 69.9x). On headline multiple, NEU is roughly half the peer median.

A low multiple is not an automatic buy signal: cheapness in Materials has repeatedly been a value trap absent an independent catalyst, and this sector carries a structural alpha deficit. The question is whether there is a specific reason the market is mispricing NEU rather than simply pricing a no-growth business correctly. There are two:

1. **The specialty-materials segment is likely under-valued within the whole.** The market anchors on the flat petroleum-additives franchise. The ~$1B defense/space platform, with its high qualification barriers and structurally growing end markets, arguably deserves a defense-multiple premium that the blended 17.6x does not reflect. As the AMPAC ramp converts capex into earnings, a modest re-rating is plausible.

2. **Owner-operator quality and capital-return support.** Management repurchasing below the current price and consistently compounding EPS through buybacks provides a floor that a pure no-growth chemical would not have.

Against those, the core business is genuinely flat-to-declining, so a substantial multiple premium to the true peers (AXTA/EMN at ~19–21x) is hard to justify on the legacy earnings alone. The 17.6x multiple is fair-to-slightly-attractive, not a screaming bargain. On normalized earnings power that gives partial credit to the specialty-materials optionality, we see the stock as reasonably valued with modest asymmetric upside. That supports a valuation score of 6.2 — above neutral because the segment mix is under-appreciated, but not high because the core does not grow and the peer discount is partly deserved. This marks a stabilization after the trajectory's decline from 7.0 to 6.0 across prior reports; the fundamentals justify holding the score roughly where it settled rather than continuing to mark it down.

## Competitive Landscape

Petroleum additives is a consolidated global oligopoly of four players. NEU/Afton's moat rests on formulation IP, deep OEM and oil-major co-development relationships, global manufacturing and supply-chain scale, high switching costs, and its patent portfolio. That moat is durable but the market it serves grows slowly, and NEU is competing on margin and technology rather than volume. Customer concentration is a consideration: no single customer exceeds 10% of revenue, but the principal buyers are a handful of multinational oil companies whose demand moves together.

Specialty materials places NEU into the defense/space supply chain at a favorable moment. In both ammonium perchlorate and high-purity hydrazine, qualification barriers are extremely high: few producers are certified for U.S. DoD and NASA programs, and Calca's decades-long Defense Logistics Agency supply record reinforces the position. This is a genuinely differentiated franchise, hard to replicate, at a time when U.S. re-armament and solid-rocket-motor supply-chain bottlenecks are a recognized policy priority. The competitive trajectory here is improving: NEU is expanding into constrained niches where it holds a qualified-producer edge.

Net, the company's competitive position is stable-to-improving. The legacy franchise is defended but not gaining share in a low-growth market; the specialty-materials segment is a strengthening, high-barrier position, albeit small. That blend supports a 6.8.

## Risk Assessment

**Government dependence (new, material).** A significant portion of specialty-materials sales flow to U.S. government contractors or directly to the government, exposing the segment to annual appropriations, "for convenience" program termination, and government shutdowns, a risk management explicitly flags. This risk profile arrived with the AMPAC/Calca pivot, and it is a real near-term swing factor.

**Intangibles concentration.** $808M of the $941M goodwill/intangibles balance sits in the small specialty-materials segment. Any disappointment in defense/space demand carries disproportionate impairment risk. Calca purchase accounting remains preliminary within its one-year measurement window.

**Core-business softness and secular pressure.** Petroleum-additives volumes fell in 2025 and Q1 2026, and long-term EV adoption plus drain-interval extension pressure ICE engine-oil demand. The margin-first strategy defends profitability but leaves reported revenue flat-to-down.

**Single-source facilities.** Several products are made at only one site, including AMPAC (Cedar City, UT) and Calca (Lake Charles, LA), which handle explosive/flammable materials. A disaster or disruption could halt supply.

**Trade/FX and pension.** With 61% of sales ex-U.S., tariff and trade-relations uncertainty and Middle East conflict are recurring management concerns. The overfunded, equity-heavy pension contributes non-operating income that a market drawdown could reverse.

**Governance monitoring item.** A cluster of Item 5.02 director/officer 8-Ks (November 2025, December 2025, February 2026) could not be read from the filing index. The pattern likely reflects board refreshment or succession activity around the specialty-materials build-out (the 10-Q references a new EVP, Specialty Materials), and a CFO transition occurred January 2025 via internal promotion. This is a monitor item rather than a red flag, but it should be confirmed against the next proxy and earnings call.

Risk factors net to 5.8: several specific, partially unpriced risks (government-appropriations exposure, intangibles concentration, single-site production) offset a fortress balance sheet and disciplined management.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The specialty-materials platform re-rates as the AMPAC capacity expansion fills at high margins into a structurally supply-constrained U.S. solid-rocket-motor and satellite-propulsion market. Petroleum-additives volumes stabilize while margins hold at 20–22%, and management continues compounding EPS through buybacks executed below intrinsic value. The market begins valuing the defense/space segment at a premium multiple rather than blending it into a flat-chemical valuation. Bull case ~$900 (~15% above current), reflecting a modest re-rating on specialty-materials earnings growth plus continued buyback accretion.

**Bear case.** Petroleum-additives volume erosion accelerates as EV adoption and drain-interval extension bite, specialty-materials demand disappoints or a government shutdown/appropriations disruption dents the defense franchise, and an impairment of the specialty-materials intangibles hits reported results. Pension income reverses in a market drawdown. The multiple compresses toward the true low-growth peer range (AXTA/EMN at ~19–21x on flat earnings would still be near current levels, but a genuine downgrade in the growth narrative could take the stock lower). Bear case ~$620 (~20% below current), near the 52-week low and roughly where the director purchased in February.

**Base case.** The most likely outcome is continued compounding: flat-to-modestly-declining core revenue, steady margins, growing specialty-materials contribution as AMPAC ramps, and ongoing buybacks driving mid-single-digit EPS growth. Base case ~$820 (~5% above current) over 12 months, with the dividend adding ~1.6%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release, followed by year-end 2026 as the AMPAC capacity line completes.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Specialty-materials operating profit stabilizes and grows sequentially (Q2 print above the depressed $12.4M Q1 level, with commentary confirming AMPAC ramp), and management guides to sustained defense/space demand as the new line comes online.
- Petroleum-additives volumes flatten or turn positive year-over-year, removing the core-erosion overhang.
- A pullback toward the low-$700s or high-$600s (where the director bought and where recent buybacks executed), improving the entry risk/reward on unchanged fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Evidence of a specialty-materials intangibles impairment or a government-appropriations/shutdown disruption that materially cuts the defense revenue base.
- Petroleum-additives volume decline accelerating beyond the recent ~5–7% range with margin compression below the 20% floor.
- A confirmed adverse leadership/succession event from the unread Item 5.02 8-K cluster, or a break in capital-allocation discipline (dilutive M&A, buybacks halted, dividend not covered).

## Conclusion

NewMarket is a high-quality, owner-operated compounder in transition. The petroleum-additives oligopoly throws off ~$570M of annual operating cash flow at ~20% margins, funding a disciplined capital-return program and the ~$1B defense/space platform. The market still prices NEU mostly on the flat legacy business, leaving unrecognized optionality in the specialty-materials build-out that the live AMPAC expansion signals management expects to fill.

That optionality is real but not yet visible in the numbers: core volumes are declining, specialty-materials earnings are lumpy, the intangibles concentration sits in a segment earning only ~$47M, and there is no positive estimate-revision signal or dated catalyst to force a near-term re-rating. Against those offsets, the balance sheet is fortress-grade (1.36x leverage vs 3.75x covenant), management is exceptionally aligned (buybacks below the current price, a 2.3x CEO pay ratio, a director open-market buy), and the downside is anchored by capital return. The score trajectory (6.5 → 7.1 → 6.8) reflects an improving management/capital-allocation picture partly offset by a fading valuation cushion, and the current 6.6 is consistent with that path: a stabilization, not a deterioration, with the recent price consolidation around $779 reflecting no fundamental change.

We rate NEU **HOLD** with a constructive, growth-oriented bias. Own it for the specialty-materials optionality and best-in-class compounding, add on a pullback into the low-$700s or on confirmation that the AMPAC ramp is converting to earnings, and reassess after the Q2 print.
