## Executive Summary

TSMC reported Q1 2026 revenue of $35.9 billion (+40.6% YoY), net income of $18.12 billion (+58% YoY), and gross margins of 66.2%, all records. The company raised full-year 2026 revenue guidance to >30% growth and lifted capex guidance toward the upper bound of $52-56 billion. Capacity at both 3nm and 2nm nodes is sold out through 2027 and beyond, providing multi-year revenue visibility.

The investment thesis rests on three pillars: (1) AI/HPC now accounts for 61% of revenue and is growing 20%+ sequentially, creating a structural demand tailwind that extends well beyond the current capex cycle; (2) TSMC's technological lead of 1-2 generations over Intel and Samsung in leading-edge nodes, combined with its pure-play foundry model, creates switching costs and customer lock-in that sustain 66%+ gross margins; (3) consensus estimates are rising — current-year EPS has been revised up 7.0% in the past 30 days, and next-year EPS up 7.8%, indicating positive fundamental momentum that has not yet fully repriced.

The key risks are specific and material. The Hormuz blockade has disrupted ~30% of global helium supply, an irreplaceable input for EUV lithography, creating a novel supply chain vulnerability that management's guidance may underestimate. Taiwan/China geopolitical risk remains existential, with Polymarket pricing a 16% probability of military confrontation in 2026. The massive multi-geography capex program introduces execution risk. At ~26x 2026 earnings estimates, the stock is priced for continued exceptional execution — any disruption to the growth trajectory would compress the multiple.

## Company Overview

TSMC is the world's largest dedicated semiconductor foundry, fabricating chips for over 530 customers including Apple, NVIDIA, AMD, Qualcomm, and all major cloud service providers. The company produces over 90% of the world's most advanced semiconductors (7nm and below), making it the single most critical node in the global technology supply chain.

The pure-play foundry model — TSMC designs no chips of its own — eliminates competitive conflicts with customers, a structural advantage over Intel and Samsung that both design and manufacture. This trust-based model has allowed TSMC to aggregate demand from the entire fabless semiconductor industry, creating unmatched scale that funds the industry's highest R&D and capex investment.

Revenue has undergone a fundamental mix shift. AI/High-Performance Computing now represents 61% of total revenue, up from a smartphone-dominated base just three years ago. North America accounts for approximately 74% of revenue, with China contributing only ~9%. Advanced nodes (7nm and below) generated 74% of wafer revenue in Q1 2026, with 3nm alone at 25% and 5nm at 36%.

## Growth Analysis

TSMC's growth is driven by three reinforcing dynamics:

**1. AI infrastructure buildout.** The $725+ billion aggregate Big Tech AI capex commitment (from Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta, and Apple combined) flows directly through TSMC's fabs. NVIDIA, the primary AI chip designer, has reserved the majority of TSMC's advanced packaging capacity. Every major AI chip — from NVIDIA's Blackwell/Rubin to Google's TPUs to Amazon's Trainium to Apple's silicon — is manufactured by TSMC.

**2. Technology roadmap execution.** TSMC began 2nm mass production in late 2025. Three new advanced process technologies (A13, A12, N2U) were announced at the April 2026 symposium. A14 is on track. This maintains TSMC's 1-2 generation lead over Intel and Samsung and ensures customers have no viable alternative for leading-edge production.

**3. Capacity expansion.** A record five-fab ramp is planned in 2026, with 2nm capacity projected to grow at 70% annually from 2026 to 2028. The Arizona mega-complex may eventually include up to 12 fabs and 4 advanced packaging plants. Japan 3nm production is targeted for 2028. This geographic diversification addresses the Taiwan concentration risk while expanding total addressable capacity.

Revenue growth of 40.6% in Q1 2026, following ~32% growth in FY2025, is accelerating. Analyst estimates project 35% growth in 2026 followed by ~25% in 2027. The growth runway extends beyond 2027 given that: (a) AI workload demand continues to compound, (b) capacity remains sold out years in advance, and (c) the transition to agentic AI increases processing requirements further.

## Competitive Landscape

TSMC's competitive position continues to strengthen:

**vs. Samsung Foundry:** Samsung continues to struggle with advanced node yields and has lost ground in customer acquisition. Samsung's 3nm GAA process has not achieved the yield rates needed for high-volume production of leading-edge designs. Multiple customers (including Qualcomm and NVIDIA) have consolidated orders at TSMC.

**vs. Intel Foundry:** Intel's EMIB advanced packaging technology has attracted interest — Google may use Intel Foundry's EMIB packaging for its TPUv8e chip. TSMC management acknowledged Intel as "formidable." However, Intel Foundry remains years behind in process technology (Intel's 18A/1.8nm is targeting late 2025/2026 high-volume, while TSMC is already in 2nm mass production). The packaging competition is real but narrow; it does not threaten TSMC's foundry dominance.

**Structural moat components:**
- **Technological lead:** 1-2 generations ahead in process technology. R&D co-location in Taiwan (which Arizona and Japan cannot replicate in the near term) maintains this lead.
- **Scale:** Annual capex cannot be matched by any competitor. Samsung's foundry capex is roughly one-third of TSMC's.
- **Switching costs:** Qualifying a chip design on a new foundry process takes 12-24 months and costs tens of millions of dollars. No customer switches foundries casually.
- **Customer trust:** The pure-play model means Apple, NVIDIA, and Qualcomm can share their most sensitive designs with TSMC without competitive conflict — a structural advantage neither Samsung nor Intel can replicate.

Advanced packaging (CoWoS, InFO) is an additional competitive moat. CNBC reported that even chips fabricated at TSMC's Arizona facility must take a "round trip to Taiwan" for packaging, as advanced packaging capacity in the U.S. does not yet exist at scale.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Helium Supply Chain Crisis (Near-Term, Acute)
Approximately 30% of global helium production transits the Strait of Hormuz, currently blockaded. Helium is irreplaceable in EUV lithography — the process used for all 3nm and 2nm production. TSMC's guidance assumes no material supply chain disruptions, which may prove optimistic. Even if the strait reopens, the helium shortage will likely persist for months due to storage and logistics lag. Photoresist shortages are also emerging. This risk disproportionately affects TSMC because it runs the most EUV-intensive processes at the greatest scale. If helium constraints force production slowdowns, near-term revenue could miss the raised guidance.

### 2. Taiwan/China Geopolitical Risk (Long-Term, Existential)
TSMC's primary manufacturing base is in Taiwan. Polymarket prices a 16% probability of a Taiwan-China military clash in 2026. Warren Buffett exited his TSMC position within months of initiating it, citing geopolitical concerns. The Arizona expansion (up to 12 fabs) gradually reduces this risk over a multi-year horizon, but the near-term concentration remains.

### 3. Capex Execution Risk
$52-56 billion annually across Taiwan, Arizona, Japan, and potentially Europe requires flawless execution. Non-Taiwan fabs historically incur higher costs and ramp more slowly. The second Arizona fab is complete, with volume production targeted for H2 2027. Japan 3nm is targeted for 2028. Delays would pressure both revenue and margins.

### 4. Customer Concentration
A handful of hyperscalers (Apple, NVIDIA, AMD, cloud providers) drive the majority of revenue. NVIDIA alone has reserved the majority of advanced packaging capacity. This creates revenue stability but also concentration risk — any material shift in NVIDIA's or Apple's chip strategy would have outsized impact.

### 5. Regulatory / Export Control
The bipartisan Match Act seeks to tighten semiconductor equipment exports to China, which could affect TSMC's China-based operations. However, this may paradoxically benefit TSMC by constraining Chinese foundry competitors' ability to acquire advanced equipment.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 63.3% is significantly rich versus 35.0% historical volatility — a 28.3pp spread reflecting elevated uncertainty. The term structure is in steep backwardation (63.3% near-term vs. 46.9% at 6 months), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress. Put/call volume ratio of 2.51 and near-term put skew of 19.5% confirm elevated hedging demand. This is consistent with the helium supply chain risk and general geopolitical uncertainty. The options market is pricing more risk than the fundamental thesis warrants on a 6-12 month horizon, but the near-term backwardation pattern suggests informed participants are hedging a specific near-term event (likely Hormuz/helium disruption or broader conflict escalation). The 6-month IV of 46.9% (vs 35% HV) suggests the market expects elevated volatility to persist, which is reasonable given the macro backdrop.

## Conclusion

TSMC is the most dominant company in the most strategically important industry globally. The combination of 40%+ revenue growth, 58%+ operating margins, sold-out capacity through 2027, and rising analyst estimates makes a compelling fundamental case. The AI infrastructure buildout provides structural demand visibility that extends years beyond the current cycle. At ~26x current-year earnings with estimates rising 7-9% monthly, the valuation is reasonable for the growth and quality profile.

The risks are real and specific: the helium supply chain crisis is a novel near-term threat, Taiwan geopolitical risk is ever-present, and the capex cycle demands flawless execution. The options market is pricing elevated uncertainty (63% near-term IV vs. 35% HV), and the backwardation term structure signals informed hedging of near-term stress. These risks justify position sizing discipline (2-3% of portfolio) rather than concentrated overweighting.

The score trajectory from 7.5 to 8.1 over the past two months reflects genuinely improving fundamentals — Q1 results and guidance exceeded expectations, estimate revisions are accelerating, and the competitive moat is widening. The current 7.86 overall score reflects slight moderation in the valuation dimension (the stock has rallied significantly from the March trough) while maintaining highest scores on financial health, growth, and competitive position. BUY with a 12-month base case target of $455.
