## Executive Summary

Salesforce reported its fastest headline revenue growth in years in the quarter ended 30 April 2026 — $11,133M, up 13.3% — and beat non-GAAP consensus by a reported 24%. Both numbers overstate what the underlying franchise did. Informatica contributed $444M of disclosed inorganic revenue and foreign currency added a disclosed 2 percentage points; stripping both leaves organic constant-currency growth of approximately 6.7%. On the earnings side, the non-GAAP EPS reconciliation ($2,721M GAAP pre-tax + $859M stock compensation + $561M acquired-intangible amortization + $80M restructuring, taxed at 20%, over 871M diluted shares) closes at $3.878 *without* removing $558M of net strategic-investment gains — meaning roughly $0.51 per share of the quarter's result came from marking two illiquid private positions, one producing a $268M realized exit gain and another a $268M unrealized mark-up. Excluding those gains, the beat was approximately 8%, and GAAP EPS grew 17.7% rather than the reported 52%.

What is genuine is the cash. FY2026 free cash flow was approximately $14.4B on $594M of capital expenditure, because Salesforce leases hyperscaler capacity rather than owning it (the corresponding off-balance-sheet obligation is ~$16.7B of infrastructure commitments, ~$3.7B due within twelve months). Non-GAAP operating margin expanded roughly 250bps to 34.8% in Q1, with sales and marketing falling to 33.9% of revenue from 34.9%. Attrition is stable at ~8%, no customer exceeds 10% of revenue or receivables, and remaining performance obligations stand at $67.9B. This is not a business whose cash flows are eroding.

The tension is that Salesforce levered its balance sheet materially into an unresolved competitive question. In March 2026 it issued $25.0B of notes across eight tranches (weighted coupon 5.36%) plus a $6.0B term loan, and applied the entire $25.0B of note proceeds to an uncollared accelerated share repurchase at a $198.34 reference price. Net cash of roughly $5.5B became net debt of ~$27.7B; equity fell from $59.1B to $34.2B; treasury stock now stands at $55.0B for 258M shares, a $213.29 average cost against a $192.98 price — a $5.2B mark-to-market shortfall. The cash arithmetic of that trade is defensible (7.6% FCF yield purchased with 4.1% after-tax debt); the execution price was not, and the company bought nothing in May while the stock traded near $150. At 13.7x current-year consensus with 6-7% organic growth, no dated catalyst, and the company's own 10-K warning that AI-native architectures may "displace established user interfaces," HOLD remains the appropriate call — and at $192.98 versus roughly $170 at the time of our last review, the risk/reward is modestly less attractive than it was three weeks ago.

## Company Overview

Salesforce is the largest independent CRM software vendor, reporting as a single operating segment. Revenue splits between subscription and support (95.1% of Q1 FY27 revenue) and professional services (4.9%). Within subscription revenue, term software licenses recognized at a point in time — largely from acquired assets (MuleSoft, Tableau, Informatica) — represent roughly 6% of subscription and support revenue, introducing quarter-to-quarter lumpiness into a stream investors model as ratable.

Effective Q1 FY2027, the company collapsed its product-line disclosure from five categories to two: "Agentforce Apps" ($6,910M, +9%, comprising Sales, Service, Marketing, Commerce, Agentforce Apps Flex Credits and Slack) and "Data 360, Headless Platform, and Other" ($3,683M, +25%, comprising Data 360, Flex Credits, Headless Platform, Informatica, MuleSoft, Tableau and Other). The FY2026 10-K, filed three months earlier, still showed the older detail — including Marketing & Commerce growing only 3% on a $5,428M base. Management's stated rationale (Agentforce is now embedded across every application, and the CODM already evaluates one consolidated segment) is internally coherent, and management verbally disclosed the weakness. The analytical consequence stands regardless: the two areas flagged as soft in the Q1 call — Marketing/Commerce and Tableau bookings and renewals — now sit inside aggregated buckets and can no longer be sized independently.

Geographically, Q1 revenue was Americas $7,233M (+11.8%, ~92% United States), Europe $2,754M (+17.8%), Asia Pacific $1,146M (+12.0%). Capital intensity is minimal: $145M of capex on $11.1B of revenue.

**Revenue decomposition (derived from disclosed inputs).** $11,133M ÷ $9,829M − 1 = +13.3%. Removing Informatica's disclosed $444M: ($11,133 − $444) ÷ $9,829 − 1 = +8.7%. Corroborating evidence points the same direction: current remaining performance obligations grew 13.5% to $33.6B but included ~1pp of FX plus Informatica; total RPO grew 11%; professional services grew 2% and management attributed even that to Informatica, citing "less demand for larger, multi-year transformation engagements, which may continue in the near term"; and the 10-Q states directly that "pricing was not a significant driver of the increase in revenues."

**Margins.** The operating leverage is real and is the strongest element of the story. GAAP operating margin rose 130bps year over year with R&D at 14.6% of revenue (from 14.9%) and S&M at 33.9% (from 34.9%). The non-GAAP figure reconciles independently: $2,347M GAAP operating income + $859M SBC + $561M intangible amortization + $80M restructuring = $3,847M, or 34.6% of revenue, close to the reported 34.8%. FY2027 guidance holds non-GAAP operating margin at 34.3% on revenue of $45.9–46.2B.

**Earnings quality.** Two non-operating items drove most of the reported EPS growth. Strategic-investment gains swung from −$63M to +$558M, a $621M pre-tax move; adjusting both periods at their stated effective tax rates gives ex-gain GAAP EPS of $1.93 versus $1.64, or +17.7%. Because those gains also sit inside non-GAAP EPS, ex-gain non-GAAP EPS was approximately $3.37 against an implied consensus of $3.12 ($3.88 ÷ 1.244) — an 8% operating beat. The 20% non-GAAP tax rate is inferred from the reconciliation rather than read from a company table, and the consensus is a single provider's, so the exact percentages carry some imprecision; the direction does not.

**Cash flow.** Q1 operating cash flow of $6,701M and FCF of ~$6,556M cannot be annualized. The 10-Q states Q1 "is typically our largest collections and operating cash flow quarter" with Q2 or Q3 the smallest; receivables fell $9,431M against a $3,999M reduction in unearned revenue. Cash interest paid was only $87M against $317M accrued, because the March notes pay semi-annually in arrears. A normalized FY2027 range of $14–16B — FY26 actual plus Informatica less roughly $1.4B of incremental cash interest — is a reasonable sensitivity rather than guidance.

Net debt of $27.7B against ~$15B of annual operating cash flow is 1.8x. Screening models will flag this company as distressed because $20.4B of unearned revenue sits in current liabilities (a customer prepayment) and $65.9B of goodwill and intangibles against $34.2B of equity leaves tangible book near negative $31.7B. Neither is a solvency signal for a $14B+ FCF generator with distant maturities. What was genuinely lost is optionality: the next downturn, the next large acquisition, and the ASR settlement all now have to be funded from cash flow or new debt rather than from a net-cash balance sheet.

## Growth Analysis

The gap between reported and organic growth is the crux. FY2027 guidance of $45.9–46.2B implies +10.9% reported, but that figure carries three more quarters of Informatica plus whatever FX contributes. On a like-for-like basis the franchise is growing 6-7%, and the leading indicators are flat rather than improving: attrition unchanged at ~8%, cRPO growth inflated by the same two factors as revenue, professional services demand for multi-year transformation work softening.

The offsetting case rests on Agentforce and Data 360. Agentforce ARR crossed $1B in Q1 — roughly 2.2% of the FY27 revenue guide midpoint. Even at the ~205% growth rate cited in press coverage (unconfirmed in any filing, and Salesforce has not published a definition of what the metric includes), $1B compounding cannot offset drag across a $45B base for several years. Salesforce added "Agentforce & Data 360 ARR" to its executive compensation measures, which is defensible alignment and also a reason to scrutinize the definition.

Consensus EPS estimates have risen — current year from $13.20 to $14.13 over 90 days (+7.1%), next year from $14.93 to $15.51 (+3.9%) — but 30-day revisions are flat, and the 90-day move traces largely to the Q1 beat, roughly a third of which was investment gains. The composition matters for confidence in the back half: Q1 actual $3.88 plus the Q2 guide midpoint of $3.26 gives $7.14 first-half, requiring approximately $6.99 in H2 to reach $14.13, or ~$3.50 per quarter against a Q2 guide of $3.26. Q4 is seasonally the strongest quarter, so that is achievable, but the consensus path assumes acceleration that guidance does not yet underwrite. This is not the "+30% estimate revisions plus accelerating revenue" inflection pattern that reliably precedes re-rating; it is modest upward drift on an acquisition-assisted base.

Note also that FY2027 EPS growth is substantially manufactured by share count. Shares fell from 929M to 819M, a 12% reduction. Underlying operating earnings growth on unchanged share count is high single digits.

## Valuation Assessment

At $192.98:

| Metric | Value |

| Current-year consensus P/E ($14.13) | 13.7x |
| Next-year consensus P/E ($15.51) | 12.4x |
| EV / FY27E revenue ($46.05B) | 4.0x |
| FY26 FCF yield on equity | 9.1% |
| FCF yield net of $3.5B annual SBC | 6.9% |
| Dividend yield ($1.76) | 0.91% |

The headline multiple looks inexpensive, and the peer median of 41.6x appears to support that. It does not, because the peer set is heterogeneous: Datadog at 614x, Cadence at 89.4x and AppLovin at 41.6x are different businesses with different growth profiles. The relevant comparables are the application-software franchises facing the identical AI-displacement question — Adobe at 12.8x and Intuit at 16.9x. Salesforce at 13.7x current-year sits *inside* that band, not below it. The market is pricing three legacy application vendors on the same terminal-growth doubt, and Salesforce is not the cheapest of them.

The SBC adjustment matters here. Free cash flow of $14.4B is calculated before $3.5B of annual stock compensation, of which $9,027M remains to be recognized. Net of SBC, the equity FCF yield is 6.9% and the EV yield 5.9%. For a business growing organically at 6-7% with expanding margins, that is roughly fair value, not deep value. A discounted-cash-flow framing gives a similar answer: $11B of SBC-adjusted FCF growing 5% in perpetuity at a 9% cost of capital supports roughly $275B of enterprise value, but at 3% growth it supports roughly $183B — almost exactly today's EV. The valuation is therefore a direct bet on whether terminal growth is 3% or 5%, which is precisely the question the filings leave open.

The proxy statement quantifies how much of the derating is multiple rather than earnings: net income grew from $1,444M (FY22) to $7,457M (FY26), a 5.2x increase, while the cumulative TSR index ended at 94.12 against a peer index of 256.61 — a 162-percentage-point shortfall. That is clean evidence the story is compression, though the peer group's +157% return was itself inflated by AI-driven expansion at semiconductor and hyperscaler names, and a five-year window starting near the 2021 software peak mechanically penalizes Salesforce's starting multiple. Compression can equally reflect the market correctly lowering its terminal-growth assumption.

On analyst targets: the mean is $241.72 and the median $236, implying 22-25% upside, but the dispersion is extraordinary — $160 low to $475 high. Morgan Stanley cut to Equal-weight on 21 July with a $185 target (below spot), explicitly because "strong Agentforce KPIs have yet to drive an inflection in organic growth as legacy portfolio drags persist"; KeyBanc cut to Sector Weight on 9 July calling the cheap valuation "a mirage." Forty of fifty-three analysts still carry Buy. A 3x spread in targets is a statement that the sell side does not know the terminal growth rate, which is why consensus targets should not be treated here as a floor.

**Valuation score 5.7:** the multiple genuinely discounts meaningful deceleration and the cash yield provides real support, but the discount is not present versus the correct peer cohort, and no catalyst has been identified to close the gap to consensus.

## Competitive Landscape

Salesforce's own FY2026 10-K added a new competitor class: "AI-native companies and emerging startups that leverage generative AI and large language models as the core foundation of their architecture, offering highly specialized, autonomous, or automated solutions that may bypass traditional business process workflows or displace established user interfaces." A second new risk factor states that new AI offerings "may disrupt our service offerings or transform workforce needs and negatively impact demand." Read literally, management has disclosed that the interface layer it owns can be routed around and that AI-driven headcount reduction at customers shrinks per-seat revenue. That is the bear case, filed by the company.

The strategic answer is to own the data-context layer instead of the UI: Informatica for governance, Data 360 for unification, MuleSoft for integration, and a "Headless Platform" category whose name concedes that customers will consume Salesforce data and logic without Salesforce screens. The M&A follows: Qualified.com closed April 2026 for $1.2B ($954M of goodwill), and Fin (formerly Intercom) was announced 15 June 2026 for approximately $3.6B — an AI-native customer-service agent platform, precisely the category the risk factors describe as a displacement threat. Buying it is simultaneously defensive and offensive, and it post-dates the 10-Q, so no purchase accounting or contribution figures exist yet.

Trajectory is what matters, and it is mixed with a negative tilt. Evidence of erosion: Marketing & Commerce grew 3% in FY2026; Tableau bookings and renewals are soft; professional services growth is 2%; attrition is flat rather than improving; the derating is sector-wide (ServiceNow and Oracle each down over 30% year-to-date) as enterprises shift software budgets toward AI infrastructure. Evidence of durability: no customer above 10% of revenue, ~8% attrition on a $45B base, Europe +17.8%, 250bps of annual non-GAAP margin expansion, and $67.9B of contracted RPO. The product-mix shift (Data 360/Headless/Other from 32% to 35% of subscription revenue) shows the fast bucket gaining share of the total; it does not establish reduced customer or geographic concentration, which are unchanged.

## Risk Assessment

**Strategic-investment concentration inside reported EPS.** Of $7,772M in strategic investments across 450+ companies, two privately held positions represent ~37% of the portfolio, and the top five total $3.7B. Salesforce invests "primarily in enterprise technology and artificial intelligence companies." Because marks flow through both GAAP *and* non-GAAP EPS, the reported earnings of a $158B company carry a levered exposure to two private AI-adjacent valuations that are correlated with the same sentiment cycle driving the stock's multiple. In a downturn, operating results and portfolio marks fall together. This is the least-priced risk in the file, precisely because the market has been extrapolating investment-gain-inflated beats.

**Uncollared ASR settlement, H2 FY2027.** Approximately 103M shares were delivered at $198.34 (~80% of the expected total), with 36M shares of the unsettled forward excluded from diluted EPS as anti-dilutive and $4.6B recorded against additional paid-in capital. The exhibit is titled "Uncollared," and new risk-factor language states that stock-price volatility "may obligate us to deliver shares of our common stock or make a cash payment to the counterparty upon final settlement." At a price below the reference, standard mechanics would favor Salesforce receiving additional shares, but the Master Confirmation's pricing formula was not read and the sign should be treated as unconfirmed.

**Litigation docket expanding.** The Backpage/Website Technologies sex-trafficking actions now span 244 consolidated plaintiffs in N.D. Illinois, a Harris County MDL and federal Texas actions, plus seven new N.D. Cal. and fourteen new C.D. Cal. filings in 2026. The Fifth Circuit affirmed denial of Salesforce's Section 230 summary-judgment motion in *A.B.* Management states no material adverse impact is expected and no loss range is estimable. The docket is growing.

**Goodwill.** $59,291M against $34,235M of book equity, tested annually in Q4. Acquired-intangible amortization is running at $561M per quarter with $6,650M remaining, a rising GAAP drag that non-GAAP excludes.

**Capital-structure and reporting mechanics.** Q2 non-GAAP EPS is guided to $3.25–3.27, sequentially below Q1's $3.88, for two reasons: no repeat investment gain and a full quarter of interest. Investors comparing sequential non-GAAP EPS without that context will misread the trend, which creates headline risk into the late-August print. Separately, reported diluted share count (871M versus 868M basic) is currently flattered by the depressed stock price — anti-dilutive employee awards excluded from diluted EPS jumped to 31M from 2M. A recovery raises the diluted count. Potential issuance from 34M expected-to-vest units plus 7M options is ~41M shares, 5.0% of the 819M outstanding, or 4.8% post-issuance ownership dilution; that is contingent overhang, not part of today's diluted capitalization. With 40M restricted units carried at a $230.36 weighted-average grant fair value and 7M options struck at $203.99, most outstanding equity is underwater — the more immediate consequence is retention cost at a company already experiencing senior turnover.

**Verification gaps.** An Item 5.02 8-K filed 5 August 2026 could not be retrieved and remains unread; it is the company's most recent material event. Four Item 5.02 filings were made in 2026 (3/6, 6/1, 6/2, 8/5), and the reported "five departures, six appointments" executive reshuffle rests on press coverage rather than filings. Fin's consideration mix, financing and close date are unknown.

The informative part of the curve is flatter: 45.5% at one month, 49.3% at three months (capturing the Q2 print, ±13.8% implied), 44.8% at six months. Short interest is 6.6% of float at 2.9 days to cover, uncrowded. Nothing in the options structure contradicts a HOLD; nothing in it supports adding either.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($280, +45%).** Agentforce and Data 360 ARR compound from $1B toward $3B+, Flex Credits convert consumption into visible cRPO acceleration, and organic constant-currency growth re-accelerates toward double digits as Marketing/Commerce and Tableau stabilize. Non-GAAP operating margin reaches 36-37% on continued S&M leverage. The remaining $22.9B repurchase authorization is deployed below $200, taking share count toward 760M. On $15.75 of FY2028 non-GAAP EPS at an 18x multiple — a partial reversal of the AI-displacement discount rather than a return to historical software multiples — the stock reaches $280. Wedbush's $475 target requires a full re-rating that no current data supports.

**Bear case ($140, −27%).** Organic growth drifts to 0-4% as seat-based revenue meets customer headcount reduction, AI-native vendors take the service and marketing engagement layer, and Informatica's contribution laps out of the comparison in FY2028 leaving reported growth converging on organic. Strategic-investment marks reverse, removing the mechanism that has produced four consecutive beats and cutting reported non-GAAP EPS by $0.50-2.00 annually. Interest expense of $1.83B is now fixed against decelerating revenue. A goodwill charge against $59.3B carried on $34.2B of equity becomes plausible. At 10x $14.00 of stalled EPS, the stock is $140 — below the June low of $146.32 and near the $160 low analyst target.

**Base case ($205, +6%).** Organic growth holds at 6-8%, guidance is met at 34.3% non-GAAP operating margin, the buyback resumes and reduces share count ~5%, and the multiple stays put because nothing resolves the terminal-growth question within twelve months. FY2028 non-GAAP EPS of roughly $15.00 excluding investment gains, at 13.5x, gives $202.50.

The distribution is roughly symmetric with a modest positive skew and a wide spread — which is the definition of an uncertain hold rather than a conviction position.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** Fifth consecutive HOLD; the score is unchanged at 5.2 despite a 13.5% price recovery since our late-July review, because no new fundamental information has arrived (no filing since the 28 May 10-Q, 30-day estimate revisions flat) while the price is higher. Unchanged fundamentals at a higher price is a marginally worse entry, not a better one.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Organic constant-currency subscription growth of 9%+ for two consecutive quarters, disclosed or derivable ex-Informatica and ex-FX. This is the single condition Morgan Stanley named as absent and is the only path to multiple expansion.
2. Combined Agentforce and Data 360 ARR disclosed at $2.5B+ with a defined methodology, alongside cRPO growth ex-FX ex-acquisition above 10%.
3. Resumption of repurchases from the $22.9B authorization at prices below $180, combined with any open-market insider purchase — there has been none in five months.
4. Price near $150-160, restoring 30%+ upside to the base case against a defined bear floor.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. cRPO growth ex-FX ex-Informatica falling below 8%, or FY2027 revenue guidance cut.
2. FY2027 non-GAAP operating margin guidance reduced below 34%, breaking the operating-leverage leg of the thesis.
3. Strategic-investment impairments exceeding gains for two consecutive quarters, which removes the earnings-beat mechanism and confirms correlated exposure to the same AI sentiment cycle.
4. Goodwill impairment at the Q4 FY2027 test, or a large stock-funded acquisition.
5. Adverse Section 230 or Section 1595 rulings that quantify Backpage-related exposure.

**Reassessment point:** the Q2 FY2027 print in late August/early September 2026. The three-month options tenor (18 September, ±13.8% implied) captures it. Watch three things in that release: organic constant-currency growth after the Informatica contribution is disclosed, whether the strategic-investment line contributes to non-GAAP EPS again, and whether repurchases resumed in Q2 after the disclosed May pause.

## Conclusion

Salesforce is a high-quality cash generator with a real and unresolved terminal-growth problem, priced accordingly. The operating margin expansion (250bps of non-GAAP improvement), the ~8% attrition rate, the $67.9B RPO and the absence of any customer above 10% of revenue are durable strengths. The 13.7x current-year multiple is not a mistake by the market; it is the same multiple the market assigns to Adobe (12.8x) and Intuit (16.9x) for the same reason, and Salesforce's own risk factors articulate that reason — that AI-native architectures can bypass workflows and displace the interfaces its per-seat revenue depends on.

Three specific findings prevent a more constructive call. Organic constant-currency growth of ~6.7% is roughly half the headline. A meaningful share of four consecutive earnings beats came from marking two illiquid private positions that sit inside non-GAAP EPS. And $25B of borrowed money at a 5.36% coupon was deployed in a single tranche at $198.34, leaving the cumulative buyback program $5.2B underwater and the company with $27.7B of net debt where it previously had net cash — after which repurchases stopped entirely while the stock traded near $150 with $22.9B still authorized. The cash-spread logic of the trade was sound; the price discipline was not.

Existing holders should hold: base case $205 (+6%) with a $280 bull case, $14B+ of annual free cash flow, distant maturities and a large remaining authorization make this a poor short despite only 6.6% short interest. New capital should wait for either verified organic re-acceleration or a price near $150-160, where the $140 bear case is largely absorbed.
