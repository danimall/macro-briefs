## Executive Summary

IDACORP is the holding company for Idaho Power, a regulated electric utility serving approximately 664,000 customers across southern Idaho. The company occupies a rare position in the utility sector: a regulated monopoly experiencing genuinely rapid load growth, driven by Micron Technology's semiconductor fabrication complex, Meta's data center buildout, and strong Boise-area population growth. Idaho Power's 5-year Integrated Resource Plan forecasts 8.3% annual retail sales growth, roughly 3x the national utility average, supporting a visible 6–9% EPS growth trajectory.

This growth comes at a cost. IDACORP is in the early innings of a $6.3–7.2 billion, five-year capital expenditure program — roughly double the prior cycle — that has pushed free cash flow to negative $577 million in 2025 and will require continued equity issuance (share count has already risen 8% over three years, with ~$610M in unexercised forward sale agreements outstanding). Moody's has placed a negative outlook on the credit rating. The stock trades at approximately 24.8x trailing earnings, a meaningful premium to the peer median of 20.8x.

The bull case hinges on rate base doubling to $10–12 billion by 2030, sustained regulatory support in Idaho, and data center/semiconductor load materializing at forecast levels. The bear case centers on execution risk across a historically large capital program, continued dilution, potential Moody's downgrade, and the possibility that industrial load ramp rates disappoint, leaving the company with excess infrastructure. At current prices, the growth story is substantially embedded in the valuation, and the risk/reward does not favor initiating a new position.

## Company Overview

IDACORP, Inc. is structured as a holding company with Idaho Power Company as its principal operating subsidiary. Idaho Power is regulated by the Idaho Public Utilities Commission (IPUC), the Oregon Public Utilities Commission (OPUC, though the Oregon distribution business is being sold), and FERC. The company generates over 50% of its power from low-cost hydroelectric facilities, including the Hells Canyon Complex on the Snake River, which provides 36% of total nameplate capacity.

The service territory covers southern Idaho and a small portion of eastern Oregon. Customer growth of 2.3% in 2025 was driven by population migration into the Boise metropolitan area and industrial buildout. Two anchor industrial customers — Micron Technology (semiconductor fabrication) and Meta Platforms (data center via Brisbie LLC with a 320 MW solar PPA) — are expected to ramp load significantly beginning in 2026.

In February 2026, Idaho Power entered a definitive agreement to sell its Oregon distribution business to Oregon Trail Electric Cooperative for $154 million, subject to regulatory approvals. This simplifies the regulatory structure and concentrates operations entirely within Idaho's constructive regulatory environment.

Other subsidiaries include IFS (affordable housing tax credit investments), Ida-West (small hydro), and IERCo (a joint venture interest in the coal mine supplying the Jim Bridger plant, which is transitioning to natural gas).

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Earnings

IDACORP has delivered 18 consecutive years of net income growth through 2025. Net income grew from $259 million in 2022 to $323 million in 2025, a 7.7% CAGR. Diluted EPS advanced from $5.11 to $5.90 over the same period, a 4.9% CAGR — the gap versus net income growth reflects share count dilution from equity issuance.

The 2026 earnings guidance of $6.25–$6.45 per diluted share implies 6–9% growth over 2025, supported by the $110 million Idaho rate increase (7.48%) effective January 1, 2026.

Operating margin stands at 19.6%, and net margin at 17.9%. The net margin benefits from negative income tax of approximately -$13.7 million in 2025, driven by tax credits and ADITC (Accumulated Deferred Investment Tax Credit) amortization. ROE of 9.1% approximates the authorized return on equity, consistent with typical regulatory lag.

### Earnings Consistency

The quarterly earnings track record shows 2 beats and 2 misses over the past four quarters, with narrow magnitudes (+5.6%, -0.8%, -2.0%, +3.9%). This pattern is consistent with a regulated utility where earnings are relatively predictable but weather, power costs, and timing of regulatory mechanisms create modest quarterly variability.

### Balance Sheet

Total assets have grown from $7.5 billion in 2022 to $10.2 billion in 2025 (36% increase in three years), driven by PP&E growth of 13.6% annually. Long-term debt has risen from $2.2 billion to $3.3 billion. The capital structure stands at 52% debt / 48% equity, well within the 65% leverage covenant limit.

Cash declined to $216 million from $369 million at year-end 2024, reflecting heavy capital spending. Undrawn credit facilities total $500 million ($100M at IDACORP, $400M at Idaho Power). The Altman Z-Score of 10.66 indicates very low bankruptcy risk.

The critical balance sheet item is the ~$610 million in unexercised forward sale agreements (FSAs), maturing by November 2026. These represent committed but unsettled equity funding for the capex program and will add to share count dilution when exercised.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow of $602 million against capital expenditures of $1.18 billion produced free cash flow of negative $577 million in 2025. The FCF deficit is structural and will persist through the five-year capital program:

| Year | Capex (midpoint) |
|------|-----------------|
| 2026E | $1.4B |
| 2027E | $1.5B |
| 2028-2030E | ~$1.3B/year avg |
| 5-Year Total | $6.3–7.2B |

This is approximately double the prior five-year average of $709 million/year. External financing requirements will be substantial, including additional debt issuance (Idaho Power has $500 million remaining authorized for bond sales) and equity issuance beyond the existing FSAs.

### Dividend

The quarterly dividend of $0.88/share ($3.52 annualized) yields approximately 2.4% at current prices. The payout ratio of 59.7% sits within the recently lowered target range of 50–60% (down from 60–70%), reflecting the need to retain capital. The 14-year track record of dividend increases is positive, but dividend growth will likely be restrained during the capital-intensive buildout.

## Growth Analysis

IDACORP's growth story rests on three pillars:

**1. Rate Base Expansion.** The five-year capex program should roughly double the rate base from approximately $5–6 billion to $10–12 billion by 2030. Under standard utility regulatory math (authorized ROE × rate base growth), this supports 6–8% annual earnings growth, well above the 3–5% utility sector average.

**2. Industrial Load Additions.** Micron's semiconductor fabrication complex and Meta's data center represent step-function load increases. Idaho Power's 2025 IRP forecasts 8.3% annual retail sales growth over five years, up from the 2021 IRP forecast of 2.6%. The actual ramp rate of these industrial loads is the single most important variable for the growth thesis.

**3. Transmission and Generation Investment.** The Boardman-to-Hemingway (B2H) 300-mile, 500-kV transmission line (construction started June 2025, in-service late 2027, $1.5–$1.7 billion total cost with Idaho Power's share at ~45%), the Bennett Mountain gas expansion (167 MW, expected 2028), the SWIP-N transmission line (~11% ownership), and the Valmy coal-to-gas conversion all represent rate base additions with regulatory cost recovery mechanisms.

**Estimate Revisions.** Current-quarter EPS estimates have fallen 9.3% over 90 days ($1.21 to $1.10), a negative signal. Full-year 2026 estimates of $6.38 have been stable. Next-year estimates of $6.94 are essentially flat. The revision pattern shows near-term softness — possibly weather or timing-related — without erosion in the medium-term outlook.

IDA trades at a 19% premium to the peer median P/E. The premium is justified by the higher growth profile (6–8% vs. 3–5% for typical utilities), the data center/semiconductor exposure, and the constructive Idaho regulatory environment. The key question is how much of this growth is already capitalized in the stock price.

At $145.75, the stock is 3% below its 52-week high of $149.73 and 35% above its 52-week low of $108.15. The 24% price appreciation over the past year suggests the market has already rerated the stock for the growth story.

Analyst consensus targets show a mean of $152.33 and a median of $157.00, with a range of $121 to $166. At the mean target, the implied upside is approximately 4.5%. This is thin for a stock with meaningful execution risk.

A reasonable framework: if IDACORP delivers $6.38 in 2026 EPS (consensus) and the market sustains a 23–25x multiple (reflecting above-average growth), fair value sits at $147–$160. Current price captures the midpoint of this range, leaving limited margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

IDACORP's competitive position among U.S. electric utilities is strong and improving. The company operates as a regulated monopoly in a state experiencing above-average population and economic growth, creating a natural demand tailwind that most utilities lack. Three factors differentiate IDA:

**Hydropower cost advantage.** Over 50% of company-owned generation from hydro provides structurally lower fuel costs than peers reliant on gas or coal. This translates to lower customer rates, supporting load growth and reducing regulatory friction.

**Data center and semiconductor load concentration.** The Micron fab and Meta data center make Idaho Power one of a small number of U.S. utilities positioned as direct beneficiaries of AI infrastructure buildout. These loads are contracted with special agreements and represent long-duration demand.

**Regulatory constructiveness.** Idaho Power has obtained rate increases in three consecutive years (2023, 2024, 2025), maintained power cost adjustment mechanisms, a fixed cost adjustment (FCA) decoupling mechanism, and wildfire cost recovery provisions. The state has enacted a Wildfire Standard of Care Act providing additional liability protection.

Customer growth is accelerating, industrial load commitments are secured, and transmission investments are connecting the company to broader regional markets.

## Risk Assessment

### Execution and Financing Risk (High)
Cost overruns, supply chain delays (specialty electrical equipment), tariff impacts on steel and aluminum, and labor cost inflation are all credible risks. The B2H transmission line faces pending local court challenges in Oregon. The Jackalope wind project was terminated in September 2025 due to permitting delays and federal land use policy uncertainty, demonstrating that execution risk is not theoretical.

Financing the program requires continued equity dilution and debt issuance. Moody's negative outlook (Baa2 issuer) reflects concern about credit metric deterioration during the build cycle. A downgrade would raise borrowing costs during a period of heavy issuance.

### Industrial Load Ramp Risk (Medium-High)
The entire growth narrative rests on 8.3% annual retail sales growth materializing. The 10-K explicitly flags the risk: "if the new load does not meet forecasted power usage ramp rates or amounts, the loss of load may result in excess infrastructure and stranded costs." Load could exceed forecasts, but if Micron or Meta delay or scale back, IDACORP would be left with infrastructure without revenue to justify it.

### Hydropower and Climate Variability (Medium)
Drought conditions or reduced snowpack can force reliance on more expensive market or thermal power. Regulatory mechanisms (PCA) mitigate earnings impact but not cash flow timing.

### Hells Canyon Complex Relicensing (Medium)
The relicensing process, ongoing since 2003, has consumed $536 million in construction work in progress. FERC may issue a new license in 2027+, but environmental and ESA conditions could impose costly operational changes.

### Short Interest (Elevated)
Short interest at 10.0% of float with 9.5 days to cover is elevated for a utility. This suggests institutional bearish positioning, possibly reflecting concerns about valuation and/or the capex-driven FCF deficit. This level of short interest creates both downside pressure and potential for short squeezes on positive catalysts.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 15.8% sits slightly below 1-year historical volatility of 16.4%, with options at the 3rd percentile of the current chain, indicating the options market sees minimal near-term event risk. The term structure is in normal contango. Put/call volume and OI ratios (0.00 and 0.04, respectively) show negligible put demand, suggesting hedging activity is absent. This is consistent with a utility in a low-event window between earnings dates, but the disconnect between this calm positioning and the 10% short interest is worth monitoring — short sellers appear to be expressing their view through equity rather than options.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($160–$170, +10–17%)
- Micron and Meta load ramps exceed forecasts, driving retail sales growth above 8.3%
- Rate base doubles by 2030, supporting 7–9% annual EPS growth
- Idaho regulatory environment remains fully constructive, with timely cost recovery
- B2H transmission line completed on budget and schedule, adding significant rate base
- Moody's stabilizes outlook following improved credit metrics from earnings growth
- Multiple expands to 26–27x on confirmation of data center/semiconductor utility premium

### Bear Case ($115–$125, -14–21%)
- Industrial load ramps disappoint — Micron or Meta delay or scale back, leaving excess infrastructure
- Capex overruns push financing needs higher, resulting in accelerated dilution and/or credit downgrade
- Rising interest rates increase financing costs during the program
- Hydropower variability forces expensive market power purchases
- Hells Canyon relicensing imposes costly conditions
- Multiple compresses toward peer median (~20–21x) as growth premium erodes

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
1. **Price correction to $125–$130** (20–21x forward earnings), providing sufficient margin of safety relative to execution risk
2. **Q2 or Q3 2026 earnings confirm industrial load ramp** — specifically, Idaho Power reports material incremental MWh from Micron/Meta customers
3. **Moody's revises outlook to stable**, removing the credit overhang

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
1. **Moody's downgrade from Baa2** to Baa3, significantly raising capital costs
2. **Management increases capex guidance above $7.2B** five-year total without corresponding rate case approval
3. **Two consecutive quarters of negative earnings surprises exceeding 5%**, suggesting regulatory lag is overwhelming growth

### Reassessment Timeline
Next natural checkpoint is Q1 2026 earnings (likely May 2026), which will provide the first read on rate case revenue lift and early industrial load indicators. The B2H construction progress update and Moody's next review are additional triggers through H2 2026.

## Conclusion

IDACORP is a high-quality regulated utility with a genuinely differentiated growth profile. The combination of data center and semiconductor load additions, a constructive Idaho regulatory framework, and a low-cost hydropower base positions the company well for above-peer earnings growth over the next five years.

The challenge is valuation. At 24.8x trailing earnings — a 19% premium to the peer median — the market has substantially priced in the growth acceleration. Meanwhile, the company faces a historically large capital program that produces deeply negative free cash flow, ongoing equity dilution, and a Moody's negative outlook. Current-quarter estimate revisions are negative, and short interest at 10% of float suggests material institutional skepticism.

For investors already holding the stock, the visible growth trajectory and constructive regulatory environment support continued ownership. For new capital, the risk/reward at current levels does not offer sufficient margin of safety given the execution challenges ahead. A pullback into the $125–$130 range, confirmation of industrial load ramp, or Moody's outlook stabilization would change the calculus.

**Recommendation: HOLD** — Quality growth utility, but the premium valuation leaves limited upside and inadequate compensation for execution risk.
