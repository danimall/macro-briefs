## Executive Summary

TXNM Energy is a regulated electric utility holding company serving approximately 842,000 customers through PNM (New Mexico) and TNMP (Texas). The stock is currently dominated by a single event: the pending $61.25/share all-cash acquisition by Blackstone Infrastructure, announced May 2025. At $59.02, the stock trades at a 3.8% gross discount to the bid, reflecting residual regulatory risk — specifically, the New Mexico Public Regulation Commission (NMPRC), which has paused its merger review to investigate a contested $400M stock placement to a Blackstone affiliate.

The risk/reward is unfavorable. The upside is capped at $2.23/share (3.8%) if the deal closes, while the downside in a deal-break scenario is substantial. TXNM's standalone fundamentals include four consecutive quarterly earnings misses, deeply negative free cash flow (-$611M in 2025), a ~40x trailing P/E on depressed earnings, rising interest expenses (more than doubling since 2022), and a $10.2B capital spending plan through 2030 that requires persistent external financing. New Mexico regulators blocked the prior Avangrid/PNM merger in 2023, establishing a direct precedent for deal failure. The narrow merger-arb spread does not adequately compensate for the probability of regulatory rejection.

## Company Overview

TXNM Energy operates two regulated electric utilities: PNM, a vertically integrated utility serving ~560,000 customers in New Mexico, and TNMP, a transmission-and-distribution utility serving ~282,000 customers in Texas. PNM is transitioning toward 100% carbon-free generation by 2040, owns a 7.3% stake in the Palo Verde Nuclear Generating Station, and operates 3,244 MW of renewable/storage capacity. TNMP benefits from strong load growth driven by data centers (70.5% data center load increase in 2025) and Permian Basin activity.

The company's consolidated rate base has shifted meaningfully toward TNMP and FERC-jurisdictional transmission, now exceeding 50% of total. Texas offers a more constructive regulatory environment with multiple cost-recovery riders, while New Mexico has historically been a difficult jurisdiction.

## Financial Analysis

TXNM's financial profile reflects a capital-intensive utility in heavy investment mode with deteriorating earnings and mounting leverage.

**Revenue and Earnings:** 2025 revenue was $2.17B, up from $1.97B in 2024, but net earnings attributable to TXNM fell sharply to $151.4M ($1.48/diluted share) from $242.2M ($2.67/share). PNM segment earnings declined $104.6M (-55%), driven by a $58.8M pension settlement charge, $41.4M in higher capacity arrangement costs from energy storage agreements, $23.5M in higher interest charges, $22.4M in higher depreciation, and a $14M weather impact. TNMP partially offset with a $20.8M improvement driven by rate relief and demand growth.

**Free Cash Flow:** Deeply negative at -$611M (operating cash flow of $584M less $1.2B in capex). This is structural: the $10.2B 2026-2030 capital program will sustain negative FCF for years, requiring continuous external financing.

**Leverage:** Debt-to-equity of 2.53x with $5.1B in long-term debt. While elevated leverage is normal for regulated utilities, interest expense more than doubled from $128M (2022) to $272M (2025), and the Merger Agreement's change-of-control provisions forced TNMP to refinance $1.08B in first mortgage bonds at higher rates. The current ratio is 0.55x, with $682M available on revolving credit facilities.

**Equity Dilution:** Shares outstanding increased from 90.6M to 102.4M in 2025 through ~$848M in equity issuances (including the contested $400M Blackstone placement at $50/share, a $200M placement at $55.33, and ~$248M via ATM programs).

**Earnings Trajectory:** The company missed consensus EPS estimates in all four reported quarters: -4.6% (Q1 2025), -40.5% (Q2), -11.7% (Q3), and -24.7% (Q4). This consistent underdelivery raises questions about management's visibility into cost dynamics, particularly the escalating capacity arrangement costs and interest expense.

## Growth Analysis

TXNM has genuine long-term growth drivers, but they require sustained capital spending that the company cannot self-fund.

**Data Center Demand:** TNMP's 70.5% data center load growth in 2025 is the standout metric. Meta is building a data center in PNM's territory. ERCOT planning requirements are driving West Texas transmission investment. This load growth is real, recurring, and carries favorable regulatory treatment in Texas through cost-recovery mechanisms (TCOS, DCRF, HB 5247).

**Clean Energy Transition:** PNM's 2040 carbon-free target drives investment in renewables and storage, with 1,453 MW approved but not yet in service. The Palo Verde nuclear interest provides baseload clean energy. However, the OBBBA's accelerated phase-out of certain IRA clean energy tax credits adds cost to this transition.

**Rate Base Growth:** Analyst estimates imply $2.88 EPS for the current year and $3.26 for next year, representing 95% and 13% growth respectively off the depressed 2025 base. The 2025 figure was distorted by the pension settlement charge and other one-time items. Normalized earnings power is likely in the $2.50-2.80 range based on recurring items, but achieving $3.26 requires timely rate relief across multiple jurisdictions.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current-year estimates have declined 2.3% over 30 days, while near-term quarterly estimates have risen 18-35% (though based on only one analyst). The mixed signal reflects uncertainty about the pace of rate recovery relative to cost escalation.

## Valuation Assessment

TXNM trades at 39.9x trailing earnings, nearly double the peer median P/E of 20.8x. The dividend yield of 2.86% compares to the peer median of 2.4%, with the caveat that TXNM's yield is influenced by the stock price being supported by the merger bid.

On forward estimates of $2.88 (current year) and $3.26 (next year), the stock trades at 20.5x and 18.1x respectively. These forward multiples are roughly in line with peers but depend on earnings recovery that has yet to materialize and on rate relief that remains pending.

The merger creates a hard valuation ceiling at $61.25. If the deal closes, holders earn a 3.8% gross return. If the deal breaks, the stock's fair standalone value is considerably lower. Applying the peer median 20.8x P/E to normalized standalone earnings of $2.60-2.80 yields a range of $54-58. Applying it to the depressed 2025 actual of $1.48 yields ~$31, though that figure is clearly understated by one-time items.

The analyst consensus target range is $59.00 to $61.25, which simply brackets the current price and the merger price. This provides no independent valuation insight.

## Competitive Landscape

TXNM operates in a structurally sound position as the sole regulated electric utility in its service territories. Customer concentration risk is minimal. The shift of rate base toward Texas and FERC-jurisdictional transmission assets reduces dependence on New Mexico's historically difficult regulatory environment.

Data center growth in TNMP's territory is a genuine competitive advantage that peers like Portland General Electric or Idacorp do not share to the same degree. The Palo Verde nuclear stake provides differentiated clean energy capacity in a market increasingly focused on decarbonization.

However, TXNM's competitive position is constrained by its small scale ($6.4B market cap) and the persistent overhang of merger uncertainty, which limits the company's ability to attract long-term institutional capital on a standalone basis. The TSR has lagged the peer group (113 vs. 127 indexed) over recent measurement periods.

## Risk Assessment

### Merger Regulatory Risk — Primary

The NMPRC has paused its merger review to investigate the $400M stock placement to Blackstone's affiliate. The New Mexico Attorney General has challenged the placement as an "integral part" of the merger requiring prior PRC approval. Hearings on the stock issue are scheduled for April 30, 2026, with merger hearings in May 2026.

The Avangrid precedent is directly relevant: New Mexico regulators blocked PNM's prior acquisition in 2023. Political opposition to private equity ownership of a public utility is significant, with "hundreds of opponents" testifying at public hearings. If the NMPRC blocks the deal, TXNM owes no termination fee to Blackstone, and the stock would re-rate to standalone value — likely a 5-15% decline from current levels depending on the earnings trajectory.

### Standalone Earnings Risk

Rising interest expense ($128M → $272M over three years), increasing capacity arrangement costs, and elevated capital requirements create persistent headwinds. Without timely rate relief, the company's ability to earn its authorized ROE of ~10.4% (vs. actual ROE of 5.0%) will remain impaired.

### Capital Program Execution

The $10.2B capex plan through 2030 requires substantial external financing. In a deal-break scenario, TXNM would need to access debt and equity markets as a small standalone utility with a contested regulatory history, likely at less favorable terms than Blackstone could offer.

### Wildfire Exposure

Insurance premiums increased $8.2M in 2025 due to wildfire risk across both service territories. While manageable currently, this is a growing contingent liability common to western utilities.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term implied volatility of 14.0% substantially exceeds the 8.6% historical realized volatility, indicating the options market is pricing elevated uncertainty. The term structure shows pronounced backwardation (14.0% near-term vs. 10.8% at 6 months) with a spike to 33.6% at the 3-month tenor, likely centered on the May/June NMPRC hearing window. The implied move at that tenor is ±17.7%, far exceeding the 3.8% merger spread. The options market is pricing a meaningful probability of a large move, directionally consistent with merger uncertainty rather than operational fundamentals. Only 23 contracts trade across three expirations, reflecting extremely thin liquidity that amplifies any positioning signal.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** An adverse NMPRC ruling on the stock placement could poison the broader merger proceedings. A formal merger rejection would trigger a re-rating to standalone value.

**Timing window:** 1-3 months. The April 30 hearing and May merger proceedings create a concentrated catalyst window. If the PRC signals a path to approval, the short thesis collapses. If the PRC signals hostility, the remaining spread compresses while the downside opens to $54-58.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 7.3% of float is short with 5.4 days to cover. This is moderately crowded for a merger-arb name but not at squeeze-risk levels. Institutional ownership is likely elevated given the merger-arb community's participation. The risk of a competing bid is negligible — utilities are regulated monopolies, and Blackstone's $61.25 is the only credible offer.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options liquidity is extremely thin (23 contracts total). The 33.6% IV at 3-month tenor makes put purchases expensive relative to the potential move. The backwardation structure confirms the market already prices event risk. Cost-to-borrow data is unavailable but likely manageable given the float size.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** If the deal breaks, the stock likely re-rates to $54-58. From $59.02, that is 2-8% downside on the short. If the deal closes, the short loses $2.23 (3.8%). The risk/reward is roughly 1:1 to 2:1 — marginal at best given the binary outcome.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis is correct directionally (regulatory risk is underpriced at a 3.8% spread), but the risk/reward is insufficient for an active short. The potential downside on deal-break ($1-5/share) is modest relative to the upside risk ($2.23/share if deal closes). The trade only works with leverage or options, and options liquidity is too thin to execute at scale. The appropriate position is to avoid owning the stock, not to actively short it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($61.25, +3.8%)
The NMPRC approves the Blackstone merger, potentially with additional customer concessions. The stock converges to $61.25 cash consideration in H2 2026. The Avangrid rejection precedent is overcome by Blackstone's stronger commitment framework and customer benefit package. Investors earn 3.8% plus any remaining dividends before close.

### Bear Case ($50-54, -8.5% to -15.3%)
The NMPRC blocks or materially delays the merger, citing the stock placement controversy and political opposition. The stock re-rates to standalone value at peer multiples on depressed or normalized earnings. TXNM faces continued capital market dependence, rising interest costs, and the need to rebuild investor confidence as a standalone entity. The failed Avangrid precedent weighs heavily on any future deal attempts.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:**
- NMPRC issues a formal order approving the merger with conditions acceptable to Blackstone, AND the remaining NRC approval is on track. At that point, the stock becomes a low-risk merger-arb at whatever the remaining spread is.
- Alternatively, if the deal breaks and the stock declines to $50-52, TXNM could become a standalone BUY on normalized earnings power of $2.60-2.80 at a more reasonable 18-20x multiple, provided rate relief proceedings are on track.

**Timeline:** The NMPRC proceedings should resolve within 3-6 months (April-October 2026). The stock will remain in merger-arb limbo until then. There is no compelling fundamental reason to own the stock ahead of regulatory clarity.

## Conclusion

TXNM Energy is a competent regulated utility with genuine growth drivers in data center load and clean energy investment, but the investment case today reduces entirely to a binary merger-arb trade offering 3.8% gross upside against material regulatory risk. The narrow spread does not adequately compensate for the probability of regulatory rejection given the Avangrid precedent, standalone earnings weakness, and political opposition. The options market's backwardation and elevated 3-month IV confirm the market prices meaningful event risk that the equity spread does not reflect. AVOID until NMPRC regulatory clarity emerges or the stock re-rates to standalone value in a deal-break scenario.
