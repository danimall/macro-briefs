## Executive Summary

Forward Air is an asset-light North American transportation platform whose equity has become a residual claim on a highly levered balance sheet. At $14.38 the common carries a market capitalization of roughly $455M on 31.6M shares, but the economically relevant figure is fully diluted equity value of about $579M once the 8,627k exchangeable Opco Class B units (21.5% fully diluted noncontrolling interest per the Q1 2026 10-Q) are included. Against that sits $1,770M of face debt plus $34M of finance leases less $141M of cash, or roughly $1,663M of net debt. Equity is approximately 26% of a ~$2,242M enterprise value, which means a 10% change in enterprise value moves the equity by roughly 39%. Both the 47% decline between February and March 2026 and the 19-23% single-day gain on July 21 are the arithmetic of that structure rather than evidence of a changed business.

The binding constraint is not a maturity (the term loan runs to December 2030 and the 9.50% secured notes to October 2031) but the revolver's maximum leverage covenant, which steps down 6.25x (Q1 2026) → 6.00x → 5.75x → 5.50x from Q4 2026 onward. Reported actual leverage at 3/31/26 was 5.44x. Holding net debt constant, compliance at the Q4 2026 threshold requires roughly $296-302M of credit-agreement Consolidated EBITDA against a trailing figure of roughly $300-306M: a cushion in the low single digits as a percentage of EBITDA. That EBITDA is itself 1.65x reported EBITDA ($307.1M versus $186.2M for FY2025), with $120.9M of add-backs of which $48.9M is disclosed only as "Other" and the pro-forma synergy add-back already run to zero from $22.2M. A revolver breach cross-defaults the term loan.

Two developments since our last review pull in opposite directions and net out roughly flat. The positive: on July 20 the company signed a non-binding MOU with the largest customer (~$250M of FY2025 revenue, ~12% of Q1 2026 revenue) under which it expects to retain at least 50% and potentially 75% of that business, with a two-year term extension on retained services. The negative: consensus estimates have deteriorated sharply — current-year EPS to $(0.93) from $(0.68) ninety days ago, next-year to $(0.39) from a positive $0.29 — three of the last four quarters missed, the analyst mix downgraded (buy count 3 → 2), and front-month implied volatility of 122.5% in backwardation against 91.1% realized prices imminent stress around a Q2 print that had not been reported as of the information cutoff. The stock has given back the entire July MOU move. We maintain AVOID with an overall score of 2.25, essentially unchanged from 2.3 in July: the customer MOU is real mitigation, but it caps a decline rather than restoring growth, and it does not create covenant cushion.

## Company Overview

Forward Air operates three reportable segments. **Expedited Freight** (FY2025 revenue $1,012.6M, 6.9% segment margin) is a domestic expedited LTL and truckload network reaching approximately 96% of continental U.S. zip codes from 76 locations, marketed to shipments of 800 lbs or more and sold primarily *wholesale* to freight forwarders, 3PLs and airlines rather than to beneficial cargo owners. Capacity comes chiefly from independent-contractor Leased Capacity Providers. **Omni Logistics** ($1,351.2M, 2.2% margin) is asset-light forwarding, contract logistics and customs brokerage across 125 service centers, with international volumes concentrated Asia-to-U.S. and intra-Asia air. **Intermodal** ($230.5M, 7.3% margin) provides drayage and Container Freight Station services from 26 locations concentrated in the Midwest and Southeast.

The company's current shape derives from the January 25, 2024 acquisition of Omni Newco LLC, which roughly doubled revenue, created an Up-C structure with exchangeable Opco units and a Tax Receivable Agreement, and financed itself with the debt stack described above. Within twelve months of closing, management wrote off $1,028.4M of goodwill entirely attributable to the Omni reporting unit. In 2025 the company reincorporated in Delaware and moved its headquarters from Greeneville, Tennessee to Dallas.

Item 1 of the 10-K describes the served markets as "highly competitive, very fragmented and historically have few barriers to entry," and acknowledges that certain legacy Forward customers directly compete with Omni. That framing is more useful than any moat narrative.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY2025 revenue of $2,495.1M was up 0.8% versus FY2024, but that comparison includes 24 additional days of Omni ownership; underlying same-period revenue declined. Operating income was $36.4M against interest expense of $180.7M — coverage of 0.20x. Net loss attributable to Forward was $107.8M, or $(3.51) per diluted share. Segment detail shows Expedited revenue down 9.2% with tonnage down 12.1% (margin nonetheless improving to 6.9% from 6.1%), Omni up 12.9% on a 2.2% margin, and Intermodal down 1.0% with margin compressing to 7.3% from 8.1%.

The most important operating series in the filing is the six-year LTL volume table: average weekly pounds through the network were 55.4M in 2021 and **46.4M in 2025** — back to the 2020 trough, a 14.6% year-over-year decline. This is a network volume problem, not merely a rate cycle.

**Q1 2026.** Revenue of $582.0M fell 5.1%. Operating income more than quadrupled to $20.4M from $4.8M, but the improvement is almost entirely the corporate segment loss shrinking to $1.6M from $19.8M, driven by transaction/integration fees falling to $2.8M from $13.9M and roughly $5.6M of lower health self-insurance expense. That is the absence of prior-year deal costs, not operating leverage. Underneath: Omni segment operating income of $0.7M (0.2% margin, down from 1.0%) and Intermodal of $1.2M (2.3% margin, down from 8.9% on revenue −15.0%). Net loss attributable was $34.3M, or $(1.09) per share, widened by a $16.7M charge from the TRA liability rising to $28.3M from $11.5M.

Expedited's apparent +9.4% revenue growth requires unpacking: intersegment revenue rose to $45.5M from $22.2M (including ~$20.0M of Omni ground freight and $7.2M from converting customers previously served by Omni), while external Network revenue of $188.2M fell 1.0%. Revenue per hundredweight excluding fuel was $24.50, down 1.1%, versus +2.6% for FY2025, while purchased transportation rose to roughly 52.0% of segment revenue from 48.4%. Falling yield against rising bought capacity is the standard asset-light margin squeeze, and it is masked at the segment line by intersegment absorption.

**Cash flow quality.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $44.4M and free cash flow of $17.5M mark a swing from FY2024's $(69.0)M and $(100.9)M, but the composition is weak. Q4 2025 — the company's own seasonally strongest quarter — produced operating cash flow of $(22.7)M and free cash flow of $(30.7)M. Q1 2026 operating cash flow of $45.7M was predominantly working-capital sourced: roughly $30M of payables/accruals build and $12M of receivable decline together account for about $42M, leaving only a few million dollars before working-capital changes. Annualizing Q1 gives ~$183M; annualizing the ex-working-capital figure gives roughly $13M. FY2025 actual of $44.4M sits far closer to the latter, and the Q1 payables build reverses. Capex of $29.1M against D&A of $152.6M is partly explained by an asset-light model and ~$91M of annual intangible amortization, but the fleet averages seven to eight years and the disclosure does not permit separating light asset intensity from maintenance deferral.

**Cash interest.** On stated coupons, cash interest approximates $69M on the notes (9.50% × $725M) plus roughly $85-90M on the term loan (SOFR + 4.50%, assuming SOFR near 4% — the applicable rate is not disclosed), plus revolver and LC fees: call it $160-170M. Against FY2025 reported EBITDA of $186M and capex of $29M, discretionary cash generation before operating-lease payments is close to zero. The Q1 2026 effective tax rate was negative 4.7% — the company pays cash taxes while reporting losses, because §163(j) interest disallowance sits behind a full valuation allowance.

**Balance sheet.** At 3/31/26: cash $141.0M, total assets $2,695.9M, long-term debt $1,690.3M carrying value, total equity $122.3M of which $81.0M attributable to Forward, accumulated deficit $(482.9)M. Goodwill $522.7M and net definite-lived intangibles $884.0M mean tangible book value is negative by roughly $1,326M. Liquidity of approximately $402M ($141M cash plus $261M revolver availability) and no maturities before 2029 mean this is not an imminent-liquidity story. It is a covenant story.

**The credit market moved first.** Disclosed fair value of long-term debt fell from $1,813.5M at 12/31/25 to $1,717.0M at 3/31/26 against $1,770M of face — roughly 102.5% to 97.0% of par in a single quarter. Lenders discounted deterioration before the equity capitulated in May. Part of that move could reflect general high-yield spread widening rather than idiosyncratic credit views; we could not isolate the components.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth thesis here that survives scrutiny, and the estimate revisions confirm it. Current-year consensus EPS has moved to $(0.93) from $(0.83) thirty days ago and $(0.68) ninety days ago (−36.7%); next-year consensus has moved to $(0.39) from $(0.25) and from a *positive* $0.29 ninety days ago. A 90-day swing from profit to loss in the out-year is the single most informative forward datapoint in the file. Next-quarter estimates deteriorated 12.2% in thirty days. The earnings record — one beat and three misses in four quarters, including a 203.7% miss in Q4 2025 — argues against confidence that even the reduced numbers hold.

The one genuine cycle turn is truckload. The Q1 2026 10-Q reports that "starting in late 2025 and into 2026, tightening capacity levels with relatively stabilized demand have created an increase in spot market truckload rates," and standalone Truckload revenue grew 59.3% to $62.5M. For an asset-light purchaser of capacity, however, rising spot rates raise cost unless passed through. Q1 shows the wrong combination: yield down 1.1% while purchased transportation rose 360bp as a share of segment revenue. On this quarter's evidence, the cost effect is winning.

The customer situation caps a decline rather than creating growth. The ~$250M account is 10.0% of consolidated revenue and 18.5% of Omni segment revenue; retaining 50-75% implies losing $62.5-125M, or 2.5-5.0% of consolidated revenue. Transitions begin later in 2026, with the majority in December 2026 and through 2027, so the revenue and any stranded warehouse cost land squarely across the covenant step-down window. The earnings impact is unquantified: no contribution margin, stranded-cost profile, or disentanglement-fee figures have been disclosed, and the MOU is expressly non-binding, with "our ability to negotiate a definitive agreement with the customer on acceptable terms or at all" listed as a risk.

A further complication: Omni's only growing product line in Q1 2026 was Contract Logistics, +30.0% to $104.2M, while Ground fell 21.0% and Air & Ocean 12.6%. The departing customer sits specifically in Omni's contract logistics business. The filings do not attribute that growth to this customer, so the overlap is inference from segment and concentration disclosures read together, but if correct, Omni's underlying trajectory ex-that-account is materially worse than the reported 6.5% revenue decline suggests.

## Valuation Assessment

The reported multiples are meaningless: trailing and forward P/E are both negative at −4.77x on $(3.51) of EPS, and price-to-book of 5.6x is measured against $81M of equity composed entirely of acquisition intangibles. Peer medians (P/E 26.9x across HUBG, GXO, CHRW, EXPD, FDX) provide no anchor for a company with 0.20x interest coverage on operating income.

The only defensible frame is enterprise value against credit-agreement EBITDA, because that is the number the covenant tests:

| Component | Value |
|---|---|
| Common shares (3/31/26) | 31.6M |
| Exchangeable Opco Class B units | 8.6M |
| Fully diluted shares | ~40.2M |
| Equity value at $14.38 | ~$579M |
| Net debt (face + finance leases − cash) | ~$1,663M |
| Enterprise value | ~$2,242M |
| TTM Consolidated (covenant) EBITDA | ~$302M |
| **EV / covenant EBITDA** | **~7.4x** |
| EV / reported EBITDA (~$186M) | ~12x |

Full exchange of the remaining Class B units would raise share count 27.3% and reduce existing common holders to 78.6% ownership. The exchange is economically neutral in aggregate (NCI is already consolidated at $41.3M); what it changes is the per-share claim. Any per-share valuation using only 31.6M shares overstates value by roughly 27%.

At 7.4x covenant EBITDA the equity is not obviously cheap for a business with declining volumes, sub-1% Omni margins, and a covenant test inside two quarters. Run the sensitivity: at a constant 7.0x multiple, EBITDA of $290M (modest erosion as the customer transition begins in December 2026) implies $2,030M of EV, $367M of equity, and roughly **$9.10 per fully diluted share** — 37% below spot. EBITDA of $265M at 6.5x leaves essentially no equity value. Conversely, EBITDA sustained at $310M with a successful Intermodal divestiture reducing net debt to ~$1,500M and a 7.75x multiple produces roughly $20 per share.

That is the shape of the situation: modest changes in one EBITDA number produce 40-100% equity swings in either direction. A low headline valuation on a business where interest consumes essentially all EBITDA reflects the probability-weighted distribution of outcomes, not a margin of safety.

The reported analyst target range of $18-19 (mean $18.50, five contributors) sits 25-32% above spot while the same analysts are cutting next-year EPS by 232.8% over ninety days and the buy count fell from three to two. We treat those targets as stale rather than as evidence of upside; the internal inconsistency (a low target above the high target as delivered in the data) further reduces their usefulness.

**Deleveraging math.** The board's post-review plan is to sell Intermodal and two smaller legacy Omni businesses. Intermodal generated FY2025 revenue of $230.5M and operating income of $16.9M; adding ~$17.9M of segment D&A implies roughly $35M of segment EBITDA — but Q4 2025 margin was 5.7% and Q1 2026 margin 2.3% on revenue −15.0%, so trailing EBITDA is likely well below that and falling. A divestiture only deleverages if the sale multiple exceeds the leverage ratio. Selling $30M of EBITDA at 7x for $210M takes net debt to ~$1,453M and EBITDA to ~$276M: 5.27x, an improvement of about 0.17 turns. At 6x, roughly 5.37x. That is meaningful against a covenant cushion measured in hundredths of a turn but not transformative, and the company is marketing a visibly deteriorating asset into a soft drayage market.

## Competitive Landscape

Management's own description — highly competitive, very fragmented, few barriers to entry — is the correct starting point. Expedited competes with national and regional truckload and LTL carriers; Omni against 3PL forwarders, warehousing and customs providers; Intermodal against national and regional drayage operators. The 10-K notes competitors "with substantially greater financial and other resources, including greater freight capacity."

The trajectory is deteriorating on three specific measures. First, network volume: 46.4M average weekly pounds in 2025 versus 55.4M in 2021, with external Network revenue still declining 1.0% in Q1 2026. Second, price-cost: Expedited yield ex-fuel turned negative (−1.1%) while purchased transportation rose 360bp as a share of segment revenue. Third, capacity to invest: with leverage at 5.44x and interest consuming essentially all EBITDA, the company cannot fund technology, network density, or price competition on the terms available to CHRW, EXPD or FDX. That is the operating consequence of the capital structure, and it compounds over time.

There is also a structural channel problem. Expedited sells wholesale to freight forwarders and 3PLs — intermediaries the 10-K itself flags as subject to disintermediation, including by competitors deploying AI in brokerage — while Forward's post-Omni move toward direct beneficial-cargo-owner relationships puts it in competition with those same customers. The Q1 disclosure that $7.2M of Expedited revenue came from "converting customers previously served by Omni" is the internal expression of that tension: real cost synergy, but also a signal to forwarder customers about where the company is heading.

The July MOU does not diversify the base. Retaining 50-75% of the largest account leaves that customer at roughly 5.0-7.5% of consolidated revenue and still the largest, with top-10 concentration of 26% unaddressed. A smaller mix contribution from a shrinking account is not diversification.

## Risk Assessment

**Covenant compliance (highest materiality).** Maximum permitted leverage steps to 5.50x for Q4 2026 and thereafter against a 5.44x Q1 2026 actual, a cushion of roughly 1-3% of EBITDA. A revolver breach cross-defaults the term loan. The likeliest resolution is an amendment (at a repricing cost that lands on equity) or a divestiture-funded paydown, not acceleration. But the negotiating position is poor. We reconstructed rather than read the covenant definition, and our two formulations (5.42x and 5.54x) bracket but do not reproduce the disclosed figures, so the true netting and add-back mechanics may create more cushion than estimated.

**Add-back dependence.** Covenant EBITDA of $307.1M is 1.65x reported EBITDA of $186.2M, with $120.9M of add-backs: $48.9M "Other" (unexplained in the disclosure reviewed), $31.5M transaction/integration, $19.8M abandoned software, $14.1M pro-forma savings, $5.7M severance. Pro-forma *synergies* fell to zero from $22.2M and savings to $14.1M from $35.6M. Covenant EBITDA held roughly flat in FY2025 only because "Other" grew and the software abandonment was added back. With a 1-3% cushion, compliance depends materially on the persistence and permissibility of these items. Such definitions are negotiated with lenders and were disclosed transparently; we draw no inference about intent, only about fragility.

**Goodwill.** $522.7M of goodwill remains after the FY2024 write-down, all at Omni. The June 30, 2025 test estimated Omni's fair value only ~10% above carrying value, with methodology cross-checked against market capitalization and an implied control premium. The share price has since fallen from ~$30 in February to $14.38, and $62.5-125M of contract-logistics revenue is transitioning out of exactly that reporting unit. The June 30, 2026 test result will appear in the Q2 filing. A charge would be non-cash and would not directly reduce covenant EBITDA, but it would further erode the $81M book equity and could pressure guideline-company inputs in future tests.

**Tax Receivable Agreement.** Forward owes certain Omni Holders 83.5% of realized tax benefits from basis step-ups and related attributes. The liability rose from $11.5M to $28.3M in Q1 2026 with a $16.7M charge through Other expense. On a change of control, amounts "may be accelerated" and "may be substantially in excess of the actual benefits," which the 10-K says may impair the ability to consummate change-of-control transactions or reduce value received by stockholders. The notes separately carry a 101% change-of-control repurchase right on $725M. We do not assert the TRA caused the sale process to fail — the 10-Q cites "a variety of factors" — but the friction is large relative to ~$579M of equity value.

**Other quantified exposures.** Independent-contractor classification (California AB5 plus pending wage-and-hour litigation) strikes directly at the Leased Capacity Provider cost model. Vehicle liability retention is $5.0M per occurrence with a $5.0M aggregate in the $5-20M layer, and the company carries no stop-loss insurance. Governance: sixteen Item 5.02 officer/director filings in roughly thirty months, an Item 4.01 auditor change in March 2025 in the same window as a material weakness identified at 12/31/24 (subsequently remediated), and Ancora exempt solicitations in May-June 2025 urging votes against certain directors. Item 5.02 covers routine appointments as well as departures, and refreshment is expected after a transformational merger, reincorporation and HQ move, but this density of change is a real execution risk at a company that must hit an EBITDA number within a percent or two.

**Insider signal.** Across the Form 4 record there were no open-market purchases by any officer or director, including after a 50%+ decline. Executive holdings are modest relative to compensation (CEO 144,030 shares; CFO 91,884). A 10% holder, Cetus Capital VI, bought 215,000 shares at $17.89 and 10,000 at $18.86 in October 2025 and then sold 302,163 shares at $21.42-22.66 through mid-November — a net seller of ~77,000 shares monetizing into strength. Trading windows, the strategic review and the customer negotiation may all have restricted purchases, so the absence of buying is weaker evidence than the presence of selling would be. But there is no offsetting positive signal.

**Compensation alignment** is working. Relative TSR percentile has been zero for 2023, 2024 and 2025; five-year TSR indexes at 33 versus a peer 135; CEO FY2025 compensation actually paid was $2.74M against $5.56M granted, and the PEO figure was negative $0.77M in 2023. The exception worth a governance question is that FY2025 granted target compensation rose to $5.56M from $0.92M in 2024 in a year with a $107.8M net loss, though the 2024 figure looks anomalously low and may reflect grant-cycle timing.

### Options Market Signal

The options structure corroborates the fundamental caution and points to a near-dated event. Front-month ATM implied volatility of 122.5% sits 31.4 points above 91.1% one-year realized, at the 92nd percentile within the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (122.5% at 17 days versus 105.4% at 45 days and 97.0% at 136 days). ATM IV jumped 17.3 points in a single session (105.2% → 122.5%) on a 2.1% price move — a volatility bid disproportionate to spot action. The nearest expiry prices a ±22.3% move, with a 10.1% put/call skew indicating elevated near-term put demand; the 45-day and 136-day skews are negative, so the demand for downside protection is concentrated in the front month. Open-interest put/call of 0.20 means the standing position base is call-heavy, so the put bid is recent rather than structural.

Read together: the market expects a resolving event inside the August 21 expiry — most plausibly Q2 results (not reported as of the cutoff) carrying the June 30 goodwill test outcome, an update on the definitive customer agreement, and any divestiture progress. Rich front-month volatility raises the cost of expressing either side through options and argues against buying protection here.

## Investment Thesis

### Bear Case (base case, and where we sit)

Consolidated EBITDA drifts toward $280-290M as the customer transition begins in December 2026, Omni's 0.2% Q1 margin fails to recover, Intermodal continues deteriorating (2.3% margin, revenue −15.0%), and the synergy add-backs that supported covenant EBITDA are gone. Working capital normalizes, reversing the Q1 payables build, so reported free cash flow returns toward zero or negative. The Q4 2026 covenant test requires an amendment; lenders reprice, adding cash interest to a structure already consuming $160-170M annually. A June 30, 2026 goodwill charge takes the $81M book equity toward zero. At 7.0x on $290M, equity is worth roughly $9.10 per fully diluted share; at 6.5x on $265M, essentially nothing. **Bear target $4.00 (−72%).**

### Bull Case

The definitive customer agreement lands at the top of the 50-75% retention band with meaningful disentanglement fees. Truckload spot tightening finally passes through to Expedited yield, reversing the −1.1% print, while intersegment routing of Omni volume onto the Forward network continues extracting cost. Intermodal sells at 7x to a strategic buyer, cutting net debt to ~$1,500M and buying 0.15-0.20 turns of leverage. Covenant EBITDA holds near $310M, compliance is achieved without amendment, and the equity re-rates toward 7.75x. That produces roughly **$20 (+39%)**, and the levered structure means the path there would be volatile — the July 21 session (+18.6%) is the template.

### Why the bear case dominates the probability weighting

The bull case requires four things to go right simultaneously (definitive agreement, yield inflection, divestiture at a full multiple, covenant compliance without amendment) inside two quarters, while the bear case requires only one to go wrong. Estimate revisions are moving in the bear direction at speed — next-year consensus from +$0.29 to $(0.39) in ninety days. The debt repriced from 102.5% to 97.0% of face a full quarter before the equity broke, and the credit market is generally better informed than the equity market in structures where equity is 26% of EV. Management could not sell the company after "extensive negotiations and discussions with multiple parties," a market test of enterprise value conducted with full information. And the equity has already surrendered the entire July MOU gain, which suggests the marginal buyer does not regard the announcement as resolving the situation.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** Q2 2026 results, due within weeks of the cutoff and not yet reported, carry three items: the June 30, 2026 goodwill test outcome (last measured at only ~10% cushion, with the share price halved since), the trailing covenant leverage ratio against the 6.00x Q2 threshold, and status of the definitive customer agreement. The Q4 2026 covenant step-down to 5.50x is the larger catalyst but resolves in February 2027.

**Timing window.** Front-month options imply the market expects the near-term event inside the August 21 expiry (17 days). The covenant catalyst is roughly six months out — long enough that carry matters materially.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** 11.5% of float short, 2.3M shares, 2.1 days to cover. Not crowded by the >20% standard, and days-to-cover is low, so a mechanical squeeze from position size alone is unlikely. Headline squeeze risk is the real concern: an announced Intermodal sale, a definitive customer agreement at 75% retention, or a covenant amendment on benign terms would each produce a 20-40% move in an equity that is 26% of enterprise value. The July 21 session (+18.6%) demonstrates the sensitivity.

**Borrow and options signal.** Front-month IV against realized, at the 92nd chain percentile and in backwardation, makes put expression expensive — the market has already bid the protection. Open-interest put/call of 0.20 is call-heavy, and the 45-day and 136-day skews are negative, so positioning is not confirming a bearish consensus beyond the front month. Paying 122.5% volatility for a directional view we hold at moderate conviction is poor value.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Base case $9.50 (−34%), bear $4.00 (−72%) requiring a covenant amendment plus impairment plus EBITDA erosion. Upside risk is $20 (+39%) on the four-item bull path, with a realistic single-headline pop of 20-30%. Probability-weighting a −34% base against a +39% bull with a live positive-catalyst calendar produces something close to 1.2:1, below the 2:1 bar. Add option carry or equity borrow cost and the asymmetry disappears.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The credit thesis is sound and the equity is likely worth materially less than $14.38, but the near-term calendar is populated with positive binary headlines (definitive agreement, divestiture announcement, potential covenant amendment) in a structure where each produces an outsized equity move, and options are priced for the event. This is a name to avoid owning, not to press short. Reassess if the stock rallies above $18-19 without a definitive customer agreement or a signed divestiture — that combination would restore the asymmetry.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.** Fair value on the base case is roughly $9.50 per fully diluted share (7.0x credit-agreement EBITDA of $290M, less $1,663M net debt, on 40.2M shares) — approximately 34% below the $14.38 basis. Bull case $20 (+39%); bear case $4.00 (−72%).

**How long the headwinds persist.** At minimum through the Q4 2026 covenant test reported in February 2027, and realistically through 2027, because the majority of the non-retained customer volume transitions in December 2026 and across 2027. The Cass Freight Index was still declining year-over-year as of the Q1 2026 filing.

**Re-entry criteria — any two of the following would move us to HOLD; three would justify constructive work:**

1. **Covenant cushion of one full turn or better.** Either a signed amendment resetting the maximum leverage ratio to 6.50x or higher through 2027, or reported leverage below 4.50x. The current ~1-3% EBITDA cushion is the single disqualifying fact.
2. **Definitive customer agreement executed at ≥75% retention**, with disclosed disentanglement fees and quantified stranded-cost recovery. The MOU is non-binding; execution at the top of the band with fee disclosure would materially change the EBITDA distribution.
3. **Intermodal divested at ≥7x EBITDA with proceeds applied to debt**, disclosed with terms. At 7x on $30M this buys roughly 0.17 turns — necessary but not sufficient alone.
4. **Two consecutive quarters of positive operating cash flow before working-capital changes**, demonstrating that the FY2025 free cash flow swing is durable rather than payables timing.
5. **Expedited revenue per hundredweight ex-fuel returning to positive year-over-year growth** while purchased transportation stabilizes below 50% of segment revenue — evidence the asset-light price-cost squeeze has broken.

## Conclusion

This is the fifth consecutive AVOID on Forward Air, and the bar for repeating a call should rise each time. The July 20 MOU is the first genuinely favorable development in six months: it caps the loss on the largest account at 25-50% and extends retained services by two years. That is real, and it is why the score has stopped falling. But it does not create covenant cushion, does not diversify a customer base where the top ten remain 26% of revenue, and does not address the arithmetic that matters — $180.7M of interest expense against $36.4M of operating income, a maximum-leverage test stepping to 5.50x in Q4 2026 against a 5.44x actual, and a covenant EBITDA figure that is 1.65x reported EBITDA with $48.9M of it labeled only "Other" and the synergy add-backs already exhausted.

Consensus has moved next-year EPS from a $0.29 profit to a $0.39 loss in ninety days. Three of the last four quarters missed. Omni's segment margin is 0.2% and Intermodal's fell from 8.9% to 2.3%. The debt repriced from 102.5% to 97.0% of face a quarter before the equity broke. The company tested its own enterprise value in a full sale process and received no actionable proposals. Against that, the equity trades at roughly 7.4x credit-agreement EBITDA with net debt at 2.9x fully diluted equity value.

The distribution is wide in both directions — a function of 26% equity in enterprise value — but it is skewed against the common. Base case $9.50, bear $4.00, bull $20.00. Avoid the equity; do not short it into a calendar of positive binary headlines with front-month volatility at 122.5%.
