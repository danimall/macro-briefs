**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Viasat is a global satellite-communications operator whose equity has re-rated roughly 400% over the past twelve months, from post-anomaly lows near $14 in July 2025 to a July 2026 print of $71.70 (and an intraday high near $93 on July 1). The move is a sum-of-the-parts spectrum thesis, catalyzed by the June 2026 Rocket Lab–Iridium acquisition, which reframed Viasat as one of the last independent owners of global L- and S-band spectrum and orbital slots. A single sell-side estimate puts the spectrum alone at ~$15B on a conservative basis, above the entire ~$9.7B market cap. That optionality, plus U.S. Space Force/defense contract wins, is doing the work in the share price. The operating business is not.

The fundamentals remain soft. Nine-month FY2026 revenue rose only 3% to $3.469B, growth is mix-constrained by a bandwidth shortage that forces Viasat to cannibalize its own U.S. fixed-broadband subscribers (down to ~143,000) to feed in-flight connectivity, and the company posted a 9M net loss attributable to shareholders of $92.9M. The one visibly positive quarter (Q3 FY26 EPS of $0.18) was flattered by a $152.5M interest-income recognition from the Ligado settlement; strip that out and the quarter was roughly breakeven at the pre-tax line. Consensus EPS estimates have fallen sharply, current-year down 52.9% over 90 days, and Q4 FY26 (reported May 29, 2026) missed on both lines. A $481.9M U.S. valuation allowance signals that management and auditors do not yet expect sustained U.S. taxable profitability.

The offsetting positives are real: deleveraging is on track ($7.2B to $6.4B of debt in a year with near-term maturities cleared and revolvers undrawn), operating cash flow was a strong $1.27B for the nine months, and the spectrum/defense/D2D/capacity-relief optionality genuinely stacks. But the price now embeds much of the hidden-asset case, insiders (including founder-CEO Dankberg) are net sellers into the rally with no open-market buying, and the situation is event-driven and high-dispersion rather than a compounding fundamental story. We rate the stock HOLD. This is the third consecutive read at roughly a 5.0 overall score; the recommendation moved from AVOID to HOLD as deleveraging progressed and the spectrum catalyst emerged, but the evidence does not yet support a BUY.

## Company Overview

Viasat operates two reportable segments. **Communication Services** (~72% of revenue) is anchored by aviation/in-flight connectivity, the growth engine, with IFC systems in service on ~4,460 commercial aircraft and ~2,100 business jets as of December 31, 2025 (up from ~4,030 and ~2,000 a year earlier), plus ~1,100 additional aircraft under contract; aviation services grew 15% YoY in Q3. The segment also includes government satcom, maritime (Ka-band to ~13,400 vessels, including the NexusWave managed service), and a deliberately shrinking U.S. residential broadband business (~$112 ARPU). **Defense and Advanced Technologies** (~28% of revenue) is the higher-margin segment: information security/cyber (Type 1/HAIPE encryption), space and mission systems, tactical networking via majority-owned TrellisWare, and emerging direct-to-device initiatives.

The defining corporate event is the ~$7B+ acquisition of Inmarsat, closed May 2023, which roughly doubled revenue, added the global L-band/GX Ka-band fleet, and brought the extensive L- and S-band spectrum holdings that now underpin the bull thesis. Viasat operates a complementary fleet of 23 in-service/operational satellites across Ka-, L- and S-bands. Founder Mark Dankberg serves as CEO and Chairman and is the chief operating decision-maker.

The world model classifies VSAT under Information Technology / Communications Equipment. It sits at the intersection of two active themes, defense multi-front demand (favorable) and the structural disruption of GEO satellite economics by LEO mega-constellations (unfavorable), which is why the stock behaves as a high-dispersion event situation rather than a clean thematic play.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue growth has decelerated to a crawl. Following the Inmarsat step-up (FY2023 ~$2.56B to FY2024 ~$4.28B), the trailing run-rate is ~$4.6B, but Q3 revenue of $1.157B was +3% YoY. The composition matters: aviation added +$114M (9M) and government satcom +$31M, offset by an intentional Fixed/U.S. broadband decline of -$93M as bandwidth is reallocated, plus maritime -$14M. Defense revenue grew 9% (9M), led by information security/cyber (+$65M).

Profitability is inflecting but not yet convincing. Q3 FY26 delivered net income attributable to Viasat of $25.0M ($0.18 diluted EPS), versus a ($158M) loss a year earlier, driven by the Ligado interest-income recognition; operating income was a modest $26.3M. The 9M figure remains a net loss of ($92.9M), improved from ($328.9M) prior year. Segment operating margins are Comms Services ~6% and Defense ~16% (9M). The effective tax rate is distorted (63% in Q3) by the valuation allowance, up from $430.5M, meaning Viasat cannot recognize U.S. deferred tax benefits.

Cash flow is the genuine strength. Operating cash flow for 9M FY2026 (versus $610M prior year) was aided by Ligado cash and working-capital timing, against capex of $694M. The business is heavily capital-intensive: satellites under construction sit at $2.53B, with 9M depreciation of $772M and amortization of $236M. Free cash flow was meaningfully positive and is funding debt reduction.

The balance sheet is the central risk and the central improving story simultaneously. Cash is $1.35B, total equity $4.63B, total assets $14.9B (including $7.3B PP&E/satellites, $2.06B acquired intangibles, $1.63B goodwill). The Altman Z-Score of ~1.17 sits in the distress zone and D/E is ~2.25, but the current ratio of ~2.1 and strong FCF materially offset near-term solvency concern. The maturity ladder is well-termed: 2027 Notes ($600M, 5.625%), 2028 Notes ($400M, 6.5%), Inmarsat 2029 Notes ($1.975B, 9.0%), 2031 Notes ($733M, 7.5%), and term loans maturing 2029–2030 at floating 8.9%–9.55%. The 2025 Notes ($442.6M) and the $300M Original Inmarsat term loan were repaid in FY2026, and the Viasat ($647.5M) and Inmarsat ($550M) revolvers are essentially undrawn. Interest expense of ~$281M (9M) is high but declining. The 9.0% coupon on the largest tranche means each dollar of deleveraging carries an outsized return on capital, which is the strongest single argument for the current cash-flow strategy.

Financial health scores a 4.5. The FCF generation and executed deleveraging are real and pull the score up from where the raw Z-score would put it, but a distress-zone balance sheet, high-coupon debt, negative GAAP earnings, and the valuation allowance keep this well below a passing grade. Consistent with the calibration that financial health is the most reliable discriminator, a below-5 score here is a meaningful caution flag on the name.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is bifurcated between a constrained present and an optionality-rich future. Near-term, growth is capped by a self-disclosed bandwidth shortage. Viasat has been capacity-constrained since FY2023 and is deliberately shrinking its U.S. consumer broadband franchise to feed higher-value IFC demand, an admission that GEO consumer broadband has structurally lost to Starlink. This caps consolidated revenue growth at the low single digits until ViaSat-3 F2 (launched November 13, 2025, service expected 1H FY2027) and ViaSat-3 F3 enter service.

The forward drivers are genuine but unproven in the P&L. Aviation IFC is compounding (+15% Q3, ~1,100 aircraft under contract), defense/government is benefiting from rising multi-front demand and grew 9% in 9M, and direct-to-device is an emerging call option. Capacity relief in FY2027 should unlock IFC and mobility growth that current demand already exceeds. IR&D rose 14% YoY to $123M (9M), funding a multi-orbit resource-management strategy that combines Viasat's GEO fleet with partner MEO/LEO capacity.

The estimate-revision picture is the clearest near-term warning. Current-quarter consensus fell 45.7% over 90 days to $0.10, next-quarter fell 47.1% to $0.04, current-year fell 52.9% to $0.30, and next-year fell 12.3% to $0.40. This is negative earnings momentum, the opposite of the positive-revision inflection signal that would support a BUY. The earnings-surprise record is mixed (3 beats, 1 large miss in the last four quarters, with the most recent quarter a -125.4% miss). Growth scores a 5.5: the forward optionality (capacity relief, IFC, defense, D2D) is real and multi-pronged enough to sit slightly above neutral, but the falling estimates and low-single-digit reported growth prevent anything higher. Per calibration, growth prospects has lost most of its recent predictive edge and should be used only as confirmation, not as a standalone driver.

## Valuation Assessment

Conventional multiples are meaningless here. Trailing and forward P/E are both negative (-283x on GAAP), and consensus 2-analyst forward EPS of $0.30 (current year) / $0.40 (next year) would put the stock at 180x–240x forward earnings if taken literally, figures that describe a business at an earnings trough, not a valuation anchor. Peer medians in Communications Equipment (P/E ~60x, with peers ranging from IDCC at 26x to EXTR at 259x) offer little read-across given the dispersion and the fact that VIAV, the closest market-cap peer, also trades on negative earnings.

The only coherent valuation frame is the sum-of-the-parts / hidden-asset thesis that is actually moving the stock. The Horan spectrum estimate sits comfortably above the ~$6.4B total debt. The Rocket Lab–Iridium deal validated that established spectrum and satellite assets are strategically scarce, which is the crux of the re-rating and the M&A-target framing. If one credits even a fraction of the spectrum estimate net of debt, the equity looks cheap on an asset basis.

Three problems keep this from earning a high valuation score. First, spectrum value is illiquid and monetization is uncertain and undated; a single analyst opinion is not a transaction. Second, the operating business still loses money on a GAAP basis, carries the debt load, and burns capital into satellite construction, so the asset value is encumbered by an ongoing cash-consuming enterprise. Third, the calibration record is emphatic that a "cheap" print (here, cheap-on-assets) is anti-predictive absent an independent, dated catalyst, and the catalyst here is optionality, not a scheduled event. Analyst price targets bracket the situation widely: low $49, high $140, mean $94.6, median $95, a spread that itself confirms the high-dispersion, event-driven nature of the setup. We score valuation 5.5: there is a specific, identifiable reason the market may be mispricing the assets (the spectrum SOTP), which lifts it above a value-trap 4, but the lack of a dated monetization catalyst, the negative earnings, and the leverage cap it at modestly positive.

## Competitive Landscape

Viasat operates in a satellite-communications industry undergoing violent disruption from LEO mega-constellations. Starlink and Amazon Kuiper threaten its consumer/fixed and increasingly its mobility (IFC/maritime) franchises on price and latency; AST SpaceMobile threatens D2D. Viasat has already conceded the consumer-broadband battle, shrinking that base. This is the primary structural bear case and a genuine, underappreciated erosion vector.

Against that, three moats are defensible. The global L-/S-band spectrum and orbital slots are the scarce, hard-to-replicate assets driving the takeover/valuation narrative. Safety-of-life services in aviation and maritime, Inmarsat's regulated GMDSS/aviation safety franchise, are difficult to displace on regulatory grounds regardless of LEO price competition. And the defense/government vertical (encryption, tactical networking) is insulated from consumer LEO competition and benefits from rising multi-front defense demand, consistent with the structurally confirmed defense theme.

The competitive trajectory is mixed: Viasat is losing share in consumer broadband and facing mobility-price pressure, while gaining in defense and holding a defensible safety-of-life and spectrum position. The multi-orbit strategy (combining its own GEO fleet with partner capacity) is a pragmatic response but unproven at scale. We score competitive position 5.0: a stable-to-slightly-eroding overall trajectory where defensible niches offset consumer displacement. Per calibration, competitive position has recently been anti-predictive, so we weight this dimension lightly and lean on financial health for the winner/loser read.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are unusually concentrated and largely binary:

1. **Satellite execution/anomaly risk (existential).** The ViaSat-3 F1 and Inmarsat-6 F2 anomalies in FY2024 forced a ~$1.67B carrying-value reduction (offset by ~$770M insurance). F3 and multiple GX/Inmarsat-8 satellites remain under construction. Any further anomaly would be highly damaging to both the capacity-relief thesis and the balance sheet.

2. **Capacity constraint until new satellites enter service.** Explicitly disclosed; caps near-term growth and forces the cannibalization of the consumer base.

3. **Leverage and floating rates.** High coupons and ~$2.5B of floating-rate term loans exposed to the higher-for-longer rate path the world model flags. Revolver covenants tie to leverage/interest coverage.

4. **LEO competitive displacement.** Starlink/Kuiper in broadband and mobility, AST in D2D, the primary structural threat, partly but not fully priced.

5. **Ligado counterparty risk.** The settlement's long-dated payments (through 2107, with an ~$16M quarterly stream) depend on Ligado's post-restructuring compliance; the accounting benefit already recognized flatters current results.

6. **Spectrum/regulatory risk.** The flip side of the bull thesis: value depends on retaining and monetizing spectrum rights and orbital slots.

7. **U.S. government dependence** (~16% of revenue) with DCMA/DCAA incurred-cost audits approved only through FY2022 (FY2025 under audit) and budget/shutdown exposure.

Risk factors score 4.5. Several of these are binary and material (anomaly risk, LEO displacement, leverage under rising rates), and while some are priced, the spectrum-monetization and satellite-execution outcomes are asymmetric and undated. The distress-zone Z-score, though softened by FCF, keeps this below neutral.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV of 99.7% is rich versus 1-year historical vol of 85.6% (a 14.1-point spread) and sits at the 78th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (99.7% near-term versus 90.4% at six months), a signal of a market bracing for a near-dated event. The implied move at the nearest expiry is ±22.0%. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-2.6%) and the put/call volume and OI ratios are low (0.26 and 0.05), so the elevated IV reflects two-sided uncertainty and event risk rather than a directional bearish tilt, consistent with a stock oscillating on M&A/spectrum headlines. ATM IV jumped 24.8 points (75.0% to 99.7%) alongside a 15.2% price gain since late June, confirming rising anticipation of a catalyst. The rich near-term IV argues against chasing here and favors patience on entry; it does not by itself contradict a fundamental HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$95–140).** The spectrum SOTP is real and undervalued: the conservative spectrum estimate net of debt still leaves substantial equity value above the current cap, and the Rocket Lab–Iridium deal establishes that such assets command strategic premiums. ViaSat-3 F2/F3 relieve the bandwidth constraint in FY2027, unlocking pent-up IFC and mobility demand and swinging Comms Services margins higher. Defense/government continues compounding on multi-front demand. Deleveraging continues, high-coupon debt is retired at attractive effective returns, and a strategic acquirer or spectrum-monetization transaction crystallizes the hidden-asset value. The high end of the analyst range ($140) reflects this scenario largely playing out.

**Bear case (~$40–49).** Spectrum value never gets monetized (illiquid, regulatory-gated, no confirmed approach), and the market re-anchors to the operating business: low-single-digit revenue growth, GAAP losses, the valuation allowance, and falling estimates. LEO displacement accelerates into mobility as it already has in consumer broadband. A satellite anomaly or launch failure recurs, hitting both the capacity thesis and the balance sheet. Higher-for-longer rates raise the cost on the floating-rate debt. The distress-zone Z-score becomes the operative frame. The analyst low of $49 and current-year estimates support a downside toward the low-$40s to high-$40s.

The distribution is genuinely wide and event-driven. The bull case rests on optionality without a dated trigger; the bear case rests on the operating reality that is visible today. That symmetry, combined with a ~400% run that has already priced substantial optionality, is why this is a HOLD rather than a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (rationale: the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q1 FY2027 earnings release (assess whether ViaSat-3 F2 service entry and IFC growth begin to convert to margin), any confirmed spectrum transaction or acquisition approach, and ViaSat-3 F3 launch milestones. The near-term options backwardation flags a catalyst likely within the next month.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A confirmed spectrum-monetization transaction, strategic approach, or asset sale that crystallizes value at or above the ~$15B SOTP reference, converting undated optionality into a dated, realized catalyst.
- ViaSat-3 F2 entering service on schedule (1H FY2027) with evidence of consolidated revenue growth reaccelerating above ~8% and Comms Services operating margin expanding beyond ~6%.
- A stabilization and turn upward in consensus EPS revisions after the recent 45–53% cuts, combined with continued debt reduction below ~$5.5B.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A satellite anomaly or launch failure on ViaSat-3 F3 or the GX/Inmarsat-8 fleet, or a material carrying-value write-down.
- Evidence that spectrum value is impaired or unmonetizable (adverse regulatory ruling, or the M&A narrative fading with no transaction), leaving the market to re-anchor on the loss-making operating business.
- Deterioration in the balance sheet: leverage rising, revolver draws, covenant pressure, or FCF turning negative as capex outruns operating cash flow.

**Interim posture:** Given rich near-term IV and the ±22% implied move, do not chase strength. The favorable entry, if the thesis stays intact, is on a pullback into the low-to-mid $50s, where the spectrum SOTP downside is better protected and the operating risk is more fully discounted.

## Conclusion

Viasat is a leveraged, capital-intensive satellite operator trading on a hidden-asset spectrum thesis rather than on its fundamentals. The re-rating from ~$14 to ~$72 is a legitimate response to the Rocket Lab–Iridium deal reframing global spectrum as strategically scarce, and the deleveraging, the strong operating cash flow, and the stacked optionality (spectrum, FY2027 capacity relief, defense, D2D) are genuine reasons the story is more than noise. But the operating business still posts GAAP losses, revenue growth is stuck in the low single digits under a self-imposed bandwidth constraint, consensus estimates have been cut by roughly half over 90 days, the valuation allowance signals management does not yet expect U.S. profitability, the balance sheet remains in the distress zone, and insiders (including the founder-CEO) are net sellers into the rally with no open-market buying.

The spectrum SOTP could be worth well more than the current cap, but it is illiquid, undated, and encumbered by the debt load and a cash-consuming enterprise. The analyst target spread ($49 to $140) captures the dispersion. With much of the optionality now in the price and no scheduled monetization catalyst, the risk/reward is two-sided. We rate VSAT **HOLD**, overall score 5.0, and would look to upgrade on a confirmed spectrum/M&A transaction or on-schedule capacity relief with reaccelerating growth, and downgrade on any satellite anomaly or evidence the spectrum value cannot be realized.
