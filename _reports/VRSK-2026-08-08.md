## Executive Summary

Verisk is an unusual business. It holds licensed statistical-agent status in all 50 states plus four territories, files roughly 2,000 regulatory submissions a year on behalf of insurers, maintains the policy forms (383 national and 714 state-specific homeowners endorsements alone) on which the industry writes coverage, and receives 3.6 billion detailed records annually from the same carriers it sells analytics back to. Its claims database holds over 1.9 billion records with more than 193,000 new claims arriving daily. Under its contributory arrangements Verisk owns the derivative products built from customer-supplied data. Customers fund the input, Verisk owns the output, and no single participant can replicate the pool. That structure produces ~32% ROIC on FY2025 figures and 57.5% adjusted EBITDA margins. None of that is in question.

What has changed is the earnings arithmetic and the risk stack. Organic revenue growth has stepped down from +7.2% (FY2024) to +6.9% (FY2025) to +5.7% (H1 2026), driven by the U.S. P&C premium cycle: direct written premium growth fell from 9.6% in 2024 to 5.1% through the first nine months of 2025, and management's own MD&A warns that "some carriers are not yet spending as much as they have in the past to drive new policy volume." The transactional ~16% of revenue contracted 17% on an organic constant-currency basis in Q2. Q2 GAAP EPS fell 3.3% on operating income that *rose* 2.7%, with the gap closed almost entirely by a 6.65% reduction in diluted shares. Buybacks absorbed roughly two-thirds of the earnings decline at the per-share line. Consensus next-year EPS of $8.70 versus $7.69 this year (+13%) is not achievable on ~6% revenue growth and flat margins without continued aggressive share retirement.

That is where the August 7 Delaware ruling matters. Reuters reports the court ordered Verisk "to try completing" the $2.35B AccuLynx acquisition it terminated on December 26, 2025 — roughly seven months after Verisk redeemed $1.5B of acquisition notes at a 101% premium (cost: $33.9M) and roughly six weeks after that redemption deployed $1.5B into an accelerated share repurchase, while its own declaratory-judgment action was pending. No Verisk filing in the record discloses the ruling; the remedy's scope is unresolved, and the difference between "must resume seeking FTC clearance" and "must close" is worth billions in cash exposure. On the price and volume record through August 7 (close +1.1% on 1.26M shares against a 1.81M average), the market has not visibly repriced it. With ~7% upside to our $205 base case against ~19% downside to a $155 bear, and the principal EPS support mechanism directly exposed to the outcome, we move to REDUCE from four consecutive HOLDs.

## Company Overview

Verisk operates as a single reportable segment ("Insurance") with two disclosed revenue categories: Underwriting ($2,179.9M in FY2025) and Claims ($892.8M), totaling $3,072.7M. Founded in 1971 as ISO, an industry-owned rating and advisory bureau, and public since October 2009, its economics rest on institutional privileges accumulated over five decades rather than product features.

The asset base is physical as well as digital: coverage of 143 million U.S. residential and 16.3 million commercial properties, ~500 field representatives conducting ~300,000 commercial property visits annually, fire-protection ratings for 36,000 response jurisdictions, monthly-revised unit-cost price lists for 468 North American economic areas covering ~22,000 line items (revised weekly after disasters), and catastrophe models across nine perils in 120+ countries. Roughly 250 insurance experts and lawyers monitor ~17,600 legislative actions, 22,000 regulatory actions and 2,000 court decisions per year.

Revenue quality is high: 83% of FY2025 revenue was hosted subscriptions, rising to ~84% in H1 2026. Two concentration measures must be kept distinct. Single-customer concentration is low — the Q2 10-Q states no individual customer reached 10% of consolidated revenue in any comparative period. End-market concentration is high and disclosed: approximately 70% of FY2025 revenue came from U.S. P&C primary insurers, and invoices for certain solutions are contractually linked to premium volumes. Verisk has diversified counterparties inside a single cycle. Q2 2026 geography: U.S. $651.3M (80.8%), U.K. $66.3M (8.2%), other $88.7M (11.0%).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Q2 2026 revenue of $806.3M grew 4.3% reported and 5.9% ex-M&A; H1 revenue of $1,588.9M grew 4.1% reported, 5.7% organic. The ~160bp gap reflects the December 31, 2025 sale of Verisk Marketing Solutions for $80.0M net cash (with an $18.4M loss on sale). The deceleration is concentrated in Underwriting, which grew organically +8.1% in FY2025 but only +5.9% in H1 2026, roughly 220bps of slowdown. Claims moved the other way, +4.1% to +5.3%.

Management's Q2 framing — subscriptions +~8.0% underlying, transactional −17% on an organic constant-currency basis — does not reconcile cleanly to the reported +5.8% organic total. Weighting the 10-Q's ~84%/16% split gives 0.84×8.0% + 0.16×(−17%) = +4.0%; holding the total at +5.8% implies subscription growth near 10%. The most likely explanation is that the −17% applies to a narrower sub-population than the "transactional and advisory" bucket, or that "underlying subscription growth" uses a different base. We treat the directional read (durable subscription core holding at high single digits, cyclical tail contracting sharply) as an interpretation rather than an established fact.

**Margins.** GAAP EBITDA margin fell 352bps year over year, from 57.69% ($445.7M on $772.6M) to 54.17% ($436.8M on $806.3M). Adjusted EBITDA margin was essentially flat at 57.5% versus ~57.6%. The entire divergence is the ~$27.2M widening of the GAAP-to-adjusted gap, of which the 10-Q attributes +$16.3M net to acquisition, disposition and AccuLynx-related costs (202bps of the 352bps); remaining SG&A grew only 5.7%. The operating model is not deteriorating; GAAP results are being taxed by legal and deal expense. Both readings are correct and should be held together.

**Below the line.** Q2 net income fell 9.8% to $228.6M despite operating income rising 2.7%. Net interest expense rose $17.3M to $52.8M; the effective tax rate rose 190bps to 24.6% (~$5.8M on $303.4M pretax); investment results swung ~$16.6M from a $9.1M gain to a $7.5M loss. Diluted EPS fell only 3.3% because implied diluted shares fell to 130.63M from 139.94M.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1,436.0M against $244.1M of capex (almost entirely capitalized internal-use software), producing $1,191.9M of free cash flow — a 38.8% FCF margin with OCF/net income of 1.58x. H1 2026 OCF rose 9.8% to $756.4M with FCF of $624.3M, achieved despite ~$99M of incremental cash tax ($143.6M vs $97.3M) and cash interest ($112.2M vs $59.9M). We do not annualize the Q2 figure: the +49.7% quarterly jump reflects collection and contract-liability timing (contract liabilities rose from $446.2M to $582.5M in six months). TTM FCF is $1,236.5M, a 4.96% yield on market cap.

**Balance sheet.** Total stockholders' equity flipped to a $(1,189.0)M deficit at June 30 from +$309.0M at year-end. The deficit reconciles precisely to cumulative share retirement: APIC $2,887.8M + retained earnings $8,142.7M + AOCI $82.1M − treasury stock at cost $12,301.7M. Treasury shares total 413.87M against 544.00M issued; roughly 76% of ever-issued shares have been retired. This is share retirement in excess of cumulative earnings, not accumulated losses.

Debt at June 30: $4,250.0M of senior notes plus a $250.0M drawn term loan, $4,473.5M carrying, against $551.4M of cash. Maturities are laddered across 2029–2050 with no cliff. Derived TTM EBITDA of $1,680.1M gives gross leverage of 2.66x and net leverage of 2.33x, with EBITDA/net interest coverage of 8.6x. Revolver covenants require ≥3.00x interest coverage and ≤3.75x funded leverage, with elective step-ups to 4.50x once and 4.25x once for a permitted acquisition; $1,245.1M of the $1,250M facility was available. Senior notes trade at ~96.7% of carrying value, consistent with rate moves rather than credit concern.

One correction worth making explicitly: a mechanical Altman Z-score screen returns ~1.40 ("distress") because negative book equity is substituted into inputs that call for retained earnings and market value of equity. Recomputing the standard public-company formula on FY2025 figures gives Z ≈ 5.6, the safe zone. The model is also poorly calibrated for a business with no inventory, negligible tangible assets and 54% GAAP EBITDA margins. Goodwill and intangibles of $2,172.7M are 48.3% of total assets; Verisk escalated to a full quantitative impairment test at June 30, 2026 (from qualitative a year earlier) and all reporting units passed. An impairment would deepen the book deficit and reduce GAAP EPS without touching cash, EBITDA, FCF, or the cash-flow-based covenants.

**Capital allocation.** H1 2026 buybacks of $1,827.0M plus $130.9M of dividends equal $1,957.9M against $624.3M of H1 FCF, roughly 3.1x, funded with balance-sheet cash and new borrowing ($999.0M of notes, a $500M term loan, a $750M revolver draw repaid within the quarter). Mechanics: a $1.5B ASR paid February 23 at an initial reference price of $182.50 (6,986,302 initial shares), a $200M ASR paid June 3 at $179.10, and $126.1M of open-market purchases at an average of $216.24; that last tranche is ~11.4% underwater, a ~$14.4M mark-to-market shortfall. Authorization was raised to $2.5B on February 13; $800.0M remained at June 30. Share count fell from 138,397,709 to 130,136,216 in six months, a 6.0% reduction.

## Growth Analysis

The subscription core is doing its job. Roughly 84% of revenue is hosted, recurring, and — on management's framing — growing at high single digits. Claims organic growth accelerated to +5.3% in H1 from +4.1% in FY2025. Tuck-ins show coherent strategy: Simplitium ($19.7M, April 2025) converted 300+ independent catastrophe-model vendors from competitors into inventory on a Verisk-controlled exchange; SuranceBay ($163.1M, July 2025) extends into life and annuity producer licensing; McKenzie Intelligence Services (July 2026, stated immaterial) adds real-time post-event geospatial damage assessment. None is large enough to move a $3.1B revenue base.

The cyclical drag is the issue. U.S. DWP growth of 9.7%/10.4%/9.6% in 2022–2024 fell to 5.1% through 9M 2025. Catastrophe frequency normalized (62 events in 2025 versus 74 in each of 2023 and 2024) even as severity stayed extreme — the January 2025 California wildfires alone at ~$38B. Fewer events reduce transactional claims volume regardless of severity. Carrier profitability is supported by improved investment yields (4.0% through 9M 2025 versus 3.6% at YE2024 against a 15-year average of 3.3%), which cuts both ways: healthier customers, less premium-linked revenue, less new-policy acquisition spend.

**The guidance test is specific and near.** FY2026 guidance of $3.19–3.24B revenue against H1 actual of $1,588.9M implies H2 revenue of $1,601.1–1,651.1M versus H2 2025's $1,547.1M — reported growth of +3.5% to +6.7%, midpoint +5.1%. Adding back ~160bps of divestiture drag implies organic growth of roughly 5.1%–8.3%, midpoint ~6.7%, against H1's 5.7%. The guide therefore requires organic acceleration in the second half. The CFO stated on July 29 that growth "would return to levels consistent with the company's Investor Day targets in the second half." That is the cleanest available test of whether transactional weakness is cyclical and passing or structural and spreading.

Estimate revisions do not corroborate acceleration. Current-quarter consensus fell 1.1% over 30 days and 1.3% over 90; next-quarter fell 0.2%/0.6%. Full-year figures rose marginally (+0.4%/+0.6% for FY2026, +0.3%/+0.5% for FY2027). The pattern is near-term trimming with annual figures held — consistent with analysts deferring the required growth into Q4 rather than removing it. The earnings track record is thin support: Q2 beat by 0.5%, Q1 by 2.7%, Q4 2025 by 10.9%, Q3 2025 missed by 1.0%. Beat magnitudes are compressing.

The FY2027 consensus of $8.70 (+13.1% over $7.69) is not deliverable on ~6% revenue growth, flat adjusted margins, rising interest expense and a rising tax rate without continued 5-6% annual share retirement. The estimate embeds the buyback.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is real and mostly regulatory rather than technological. Verisk's most significant competitor across much of its portfolio is not a named rival but insourcing by its own customers, and the contributory-data structure plus statistical-agent licensure make in-housing individually irrational even where collectively feasible. A single carrier that withdraws its data loses access to cross-industry benchmarks it cannot rebuild; the 10-K notes it is "rare that contributors elect not to continue providing us with all or a substantial portion of their data." Verisk serves all of the top 100 U.S. P&C insurers in the lines it covers.

Trajectory is where we mark down from the level. Three pressures are worth naming. First, share of a shrinking wallet: with carriers reducing new-policy acquisition spend and premium-linked invoicing resetting lower, Verisk's revenue per customer compresses even at constant competitive position. Second, the AI exposure is asymmetric within the portfolio. Statistical-agent licensure, 2,000 annual filings, embedded policy forms and a 38.9-billion-record pool are not reachable by a language model. The analytics and workflow layer — property estimating in particular — is more exposed, and the 10-K explicitly flags that "generic datasets enhanced by AI could compete more effectively" and that proprietary material "could be fed into AI models without our knowledge." Third, and relatedly, AccuLynx was a move into contractor-side roofing workflow, consistent with defending the estimating flank; that flank now stands undefended or will be defended at a court-compelled price.

Two structural constraints deserve mention. Data-supplier agreements are short-term and "some suppliers are also competitors," creating exposure to pricing, non-renewal, or exclusivity granted to rivals. A 1995 settlement with state Attorneys General continues to impose constraints on insurer involvement in Verisk's governance and business, a live limit on how tightly it can coordinate with the industry that supplies its data.

## Risk Assessment

**1. AccuLynx remedy scope (dominant, and plausibly unpriced).** Filing facts: agreement signed July 29, 2025, pre-funded with a $750M term loan and $1.5B of notes carrying special mandatory redemption; terminated December 26, 2025 citing FTC review delay (per Reuters, December 29); notes redeemed January 6, 2026 at 101%, costing $33.9M ($15.0M premium plus $18.9M accelerated amortization, with a further $5.8M of term-loan issuance cost amortized); declaratory judgment filed in Delaware Chancery January 7; counterclaim January 21; trial held June 23–26; post-trial briefing ongoing at the 10-Q date with "not possible to reasonably estimate the possible loss" and no accrual. Reuters then reported on August 7/8 that a Delaware judge ordered Verisk "to try completing" the acquisition. No 8-K in the record discloses it; the latest is dated July 29.

The phrasing matters. "Ordered to try completing" is most consistent with specific performance of the agreement's efforts covenant — Verisk must resume seeking FTC clearance with closing still conditional. Plausible alternatives include an order to close subject only to the regulatory condition, or performance plus reserved or awarded damages. Each implies materially different cash exposure. Appeal intent is unknown. AccuLynx's revenue and EBITDA are disclosed nowhere in Verisk's filings and we decline to back-solve them from the price.

Funding sensitivity, as a scenario rather than a forecast: fully debt-funding $2.35B takes gross debt to $6,823.5M, or 4.06x TTM EBITDA before any AccuLynx contribution — above the 3.75x covenant but within the elective 4.50x permitted-acquisition step-up, so covenant-navigable. Incremental interest at ~5.25% is ~$123M pretax, ~$93M after tax, ~$0.71 per share, partially offset by AccuLynx's own earnings and largely excluded from adjusted EPS to the extent it is intangible amortization. The second-order effect is larger: the remaining $800M authorization would very likely be paused, removing the mechanism that offset two-thirds of Q2's earnings decline at the per-share line.

The sequencing is a filing fact and it constrains flexibility: $2.25B raised in August 2025, terminated in December, $1.5B redeemed at a premium in January, $1.0B of new notes plus a $500M term loan raised in February, and $1.5B deployed into an ASR on February 23 — roughly six weeks after asking a court to confirm no obligation existed. We draw no inference about motive; management may reasonably have judged its termination sound and 2.7x leverage ample for either outcome. The effect was reduced flexibility at the moment the contingency crystallized.

**2. Telematics privacy MDL (strategically the most dangerous).** Twenty putative class actions (16 naming GM/OnStar/LexisNexis/Verisk, 4 naming Hyundai/Verisk) consolidated in N.D. Georgia (MDL No. 1:24-md-03115-TWT), alleging driver-behavior data was transmitted to Verisk and shared with insurers without consent. Motions to dismiss were granted in part and **denied in part** on April 22, 2026 — claims survive, discovery has commenced, no accrual is disclosed. Verisk moved for partial reconsideration May 20. The exposure is damages plus precedent: an adverse outcome would constrain the consent architecture underpinning consumer-data aggregation.

**3. Daniel's Law / Atlas Data Privacy (NJ).** Atlas, as assignee for ~19,640 covered persons, seeks $1,000 actual damages per violation plus punitive damages and fees — a ~$19.6M floor before multipliers. Consolidation of 111 cases into Multi-County Litigation was denied May 1, a tactical win that also forecloses efficient global settlement.

**4. Retained divestiture liabilities.** *JPMorgan Chase v. Argus/Verisk/TransUnion* (D. Del.) alleges trade-secret misappropriation; fact discovery and depositions are complete, expert discovery ongoing — trial-ready. Verisk sold the business containing Argus but remains a defendant. A separate 2023 indemnification notice concerns a Nepalese tax matter at a subsidiary divested in 2016. Divestitures have not extinguished legal tails.

**5. Cycle exposure.** Approximately 70% of revenue from U.S. P&C primary insurers, with certain invoices contractually linked to premium volume. If the soft market extends into 2027, the drag broadens from the transactional 16% into premium-linked subscription resets.

**6. Unresolved disclosure items.** An 8-K filed July 28, 2026 under Item 5.02 (departure or election of directors or officers) could not be retrieved and no coverage surfaced. The Q2 10-Q states no material change to FY2025 risk factors despite the June trial, though it was filed before the reported ruling. Separately, the FY2025 10-K risk factors state that options to purchase ~137,941,888 shares were outstanding, which is the shares-outstanding count, not options (actual options: 1,800,521 at June 30). We read that as a drafting error; a literal parse would produce a wildly wrong dilution figure. Actual maximum contingent equity awards total 2,370,082 shares — 1.8% of shares outstanding, 1.79% post-issuance ownership dilution.

The nearest expiry (August 21, 12 days) prices a ±5.3% move.

Since the May 27 snapshot, ATM IV compressed from 39.5% to 32.7% while the price rose 11.3% and P/C open interest fell from 1.00 to 0.55: hedges were lifted into the rally, and are now being re-established at cheap absolute levels. Skew is described as balanced (−2.1%), so we make no directional claim from the skew itself. The combination of cheap protection, collapsed hedge inventory, and a spike in near-term put volume around the reported Delaware order is consistent with positioning ahead of a disclosure event, and with our view that the ruling is not yet in the price.

Short interest of 5.6% of float at 2.0 days to cover is uncrowded; the bear case is not consensus.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($238, +24.1%)

The subscription franchise is compounding at ~8% and represents 84% of revenue. Adjusted EBITDA margin is flat at 57.5% — no operating deterioration, with the entire 352bp GAAP compression traceable to deal and litigation costs that end when the litigation ends. FCF of $1,236.5M is a 4.96% yield with 1.58x cash conversion, and capex is entirely capitalized software rather than physical plant. ROIC near 32% justifies a premium multiple, and the multiple has already compressed roughly 30% from the 52-week high. Catastrophe frequency reverted in 2025 from an elevated 2023–24 base and mean-reverts upward; transactional revenue troughs in H2, validating reaffirmed guidance and the CFO's stated confidence in a return to Investor Day growth. If AccuLynx ultimately does not close — the FTC review that triggered termination remains unresolved — damages prove bounded, the $800M authorization plus incremental leverage capacity resumes 5-6% annual share retirement, and FY2027 EPS reaches $8.80. At 27x, roughly the pre-derating multiple, that is $238. Sell-side mean of $237.71 sits there.

### Bear Case ($155, −19.2%)

The remedy compels the purchase. Verisk re-raises roughly the $2.35B it just returned to shareholders, gross leverage reaches ~4.06x requiring an elective covenant step-up, incremental interest costs ~$0.71 per share, and the buyback stops. Since share retirement offset two-thirds of Q2's earnings decline, its removal exposes low-single-digit underlying EPS growth. Simultaneously, the P&C soft market extends: DWP growth stays near 5%, premium-linked subscription pricing resets lower at renewal, and the transactional 16% keeps contracting. H2 fails the required organic acceleration, guidance is trimmed, and FY2027 EPS lands near $7.90. The telematics MDL proceeds through discovery toward an adverse consent-architecture precedent with no accrual booked. At 19.5x — still a premium to the 18.4x peer median — that is $155, the 52-week low.

### Our Base Case ($205, +6.9%)

The remedy most likely obliges continued efforts toward FTC clearance rather than immediate closing, meaning the overhang persists for quarters without immediate $2.35B cash outflow, and the buyback is slowed rather than halted. Organic growth improves modestly in H2 toward 6% as the divestiture drag laps and Claims strength continues, delivering the low end of reaffirmed guidance. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches ~$8.50 — a haircut to the $8.70 consensus for interrupted share retirement — at 24x, a defensible premium to peers for 32% ROIC and 84% recurring revenue. Dividend adds ~1%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: REDUCE.** Overall score has fallen across the last four reports (7.1 → 6.2 → 6.0 → 6.0 → 5.4). The change from HOLD is not driven by price action. Since the May 27 report the stock has risen 11.3% while (a) organic growth decelerated further to 5.7% in H1 from 6.9% in FY2025, (b) a Delaware court reportedly ordered performance on a $2.35B acquisition the company spent seven months and $33.9M trying to escape, and (c) near-term consensus estimates were trimmed. Price up, risk up, growth down; the asymmetry that supported HOLD at $172 does not hold at $192.

**De-risking plan.** Trim to a below-benchmark weight now, ahead of the 8-K that must disclose the Delaware remedy and before the Q3 print (expected late October) that tests whether the required H2 organic acceleration is materializing. We would not exit entirely: the franchise quality, 4.96% FCF yield, laddered maturities and 8.6x coverage make a disorderly outcome unlikely, and the bull path is live if the deal ultimately fails FTC review. Retain roughly one-third to one-half of a full position.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better:**
1. An 8-K or opinion establishing that the remedy is limited to an efforts obligation with closing contingent on FTC clearance, *and* confirmation that the $800M authorization remains active — restoring the EPS engine.
2. Q3 organic revenue growth of ≥6.5% with transactional revenue decline moderating to better than −8% year over year, evidencing a cyclical trough rather than structural erosion.
3. A price below approximately $165 (≈19x FY2027 consensus, roughly the peer-median multiple on a 32%-ROIC franchise) — at which the bear scenario is substantially discounted and expected return turns clearly positive.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. An order compelling closing plus a material damages award, or an appeal that extends the overhang beyond FY2027 while forcing a buyback suspension.
2. FY2026 guidance cut, or Q3 organic growth below 5.0% with Underwriting decelerating further — evidence the premium-cycle drag is spreading into the subscription base.

## Conclusion

Verisk is a high-quality business trading at a fair-to-full price with an unresolved, arguably unpriced legal contingency and a decelerating end market. The franchise deserves its premium: statistical-agent licensure across 50 states, a contributory pool of 38.9 billion underwriting and 1.9 billion claims records, policy forms the industry writes on, 84% hosted-subscription revenue, 57.5% adjusted EBITDA margins that have not moved, $1.24B of free cash flow, and ~32% ROIC. Leverage at 2.66x with 8.6x coverage and no maturity cliff is comfortable, and the negative book equity is a share-retirement artifact that a mechanical distress screen misreads badly.

The problem is the return arithmetic from $191.82. Organic growth has stepped down 150bps to 5.7%, the transactional 16% of revenue is contracting 17%, second-half guidance requires organic acceleration that near-term estimate revisions do not corroborate, and next-year consensus EPS growth of 13% is largely manufactured by retiring 6% of the shares in six months — the exact mechanism a compelled $2.35B acquisition would suspend. A Delaware judge reportedly ordered Verisk on August 7 to attempt completion of a deal it terminated in December; no filing in the record discloses it, the remedy's scope is unresolved, and the price and volume record through August 7 shows no reaction. Weighting bear/base/bull at 30/50/20 gives roughly a 3.5% total return over twelve months against 19% downside if the adverse path resolves.

We move to REDUCE. This is a trim on unfavorable asymmetry with an identified near-term downside catalyst, not a judgment against the franchise. A price near $165, or clarity that the remedy leaves the buyback intact, would bring us back.
