**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Oceaneering International is the global leader in work-class remotely operated vehicles (ROVs), with the largest fleet (250 units) and roughly 60% share of drill-support work on contracted floating rigs. Around 83% of 2025 revenue ($2.32B) came from its energy business; the remaining 17% ($460M) comes from Aerospace & Defense Technologies (ADTech), a counter-cyclical engineering franchise serving the U.S. Navy, NASA, and defense primes. The company carries a clean balance sheet: $689M cash, a single $500M 6.0% senior note due February 2028, an undrawn $215M revolver, and a near net-cash position. FY2025 generated $319M of operating cash flow, ~$208M of free cash flow, and record total firm backlog of $2.70B.

The investment story is in transition from offshore oil services toward diversified subsea plus defense robotics. ADTech is the designated 2026 growth engine: revenue rose 35% YoY in Q1 2026 and unfunded contracted backlog more than doubled to $658M from $279M, and the company hired a former Seventh Fleet commander to scale it. That diversification partially de-risks oil-price exposure. Against this, the energy core is softening cyclically. Q1 2026 operating income fell 21% YoY (margin 11%→8%), ROV utilization slipped to 61% from 67%, Offshore Projects Group (OPG) is guided to decline meaningfully in 2026, and the Integrity Management & Digital Solutions (IMDS) segment swung to a small operating loss on Middle East disruption. Management frames 2026 results as second-half weighted.

We maintain a HOLD. The stock has appreciated roughly 74% over the past year and trades near the upper end of its $20.21–$40.21 range, leaving no margin of safety. The headline trailing P/E near 11x is misleading: 2025 net income of $354M was inflated by a $154M deferred-tax valuation-allowance release; pre-tax income of $423M is the cleaner figure, and on normalized forward EPS the multiple is closer to 17–19x. That places the stock near analyst fair value ($35.25 mean target) with mixed forward signals: current-year estimates have firmed modestly while next-year estimates have drifted down ~8% over 90 days. We hold for the ADTech ramp and a potential second-half energy recovery, but the entry is not asymmetric at current levels.

## Company Overview

Oceaneering, organized in 1969, operates five segments across two businesses with roughly 11,100 employees in ~48 countries. About 55% of revenue ($1.5B) is foreign.

**Energy business (~83% of revenue):**
- **Subsea Robotics (31% of revenue; the profit engine):** World's largest work-class ROV fleet at 250 units, ~60% market share on the 136 contracted floating drilling rigs at year-end 2025. Highest-margin segment at 30% operating margin in 2025. Also developing autonomous/remote systems (Liberty E-ROV, Freedom hybrid AUV, Isurus high-current ROVs).
- **Manufactured Products (20%):** Subsea umbilicals, production control hardware, connectors, and valves, plus non-energy mobile-robotics/turnkey automation (warehousing, healthcare) and a theme-park ride business.
- **Offshore Projects Group / OPG (22%):** Vessel-based subsea installation/intervention, IMR, diving, and decommissioning, concentrated in the U.S. Gulf and offshore Africa. Operates 2 owned plus 5 chartered Jones Act-compliant deepwater vessels.
- **Integrity Management & Digital Solutions / IMDS (10%):** Asset integrity, inspection/NDT, and digital software (bolstered by the Oct-2024 GDi acquisition).

**Aerospace & Defense Technologies / ADTech (17%):** Engineering and manufacturing for the U.S. Navy (submarines, surface ships), NASA, and prime contractors. The U.S. Government is OII's single largest customer at 12% of consolidated revenue in 2025.

The core competitive advantages are the largest global ROV fleet and dominant drill-support share, integrated full-lifecycle subsea capability, Jones Act-compliant vessels in the U.S. Gulf, and a differentiated harsh-environment defense franchise that runs counter-cyclically to oil prices.

## Financial Analysis

The trajectory of the operating business is strong, though the headline 2025 numbers require normalization.

**Revenue and margins.** Revenue grew from $2.07B (2022) to $2.42B (2023), $2.66B (2024), and $2.78B (2025, +5% YoY), with growth in all segments except IMDS. Operating income expanded from $111M (2022) to $182M, $246M, and $305M (2025), lifting operating margin to 11%. Segment margin improvement was broad: Manufactured Products nearly doubled (8%→13%) on higher-margin backlog conversion, OPG improved 12%→16%, and ADTech rose 11%→13%.

**The 2025 earnings figure is distorted.** Reported net income jumped to $354M from $147M in 2024, but the increase is largely a $154M valuation-allowance release (mostly U.S. federal/state deferred tax assets) that produced a $68M tax *benefit* versus a $77M *provision* in 2024. Pre-tax income, the cleaner measure, roughly doubled to $423M from $225M. The release is a one-time, non-cash item; management guides cash taxes of $95–105M for 2026. Reported diluted EPS of $3.49 therefore overstates run-rate earnings power, which consensus pegs at ~$1.89 for the current year and ~$2.08 next year. The single Piotroski miss — operating cash flow below net income — is a direct consequence of this tax benefit inflating net income.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow rose to $319M (from $203M), with FCF of ~$208M (7.5% margin) on disciplined capex of ~$111M (guided $105–115M for 2026). Cash stood at $689M at year-end 2025 with net working capital of $751M. The only material debt is the $500M 6.0% senior note due February 2028; the $215M revolver is fully undrawn. On a net basis the company is nearly net-cash and well inside its 3.25x leverage covenant. The screen's reported D/E of 2.49 reflects modest book equity, not stress. Altman Z-Score of 4.33 places the company firmly in the safe zone; ROA was 23.4% and ROE 33.0%, though the latter is inflated by the tax benefit.

**Backlog.** Total firm backlog reached a record $2.70B (from $2.44B). The standout is ADTech unfunded contracted backlog jumping to $658M from $279M, the strongest leading indicator for the defense growth thesis.

**Q1 2026.** Revenue of $692M (+3% YoY) but operating income fell 21% to $58M (margin 11%→8%). The drivers were a tough comp against an unusually strong Q1 2025 in OPG (revenue -18% YoY), IMDS swinging to a small operating loss on Middle East conflict-related weakness, lower ROV utilization (61% vs. 67%), and a $6.8M ADTech contract-dispute accrual. ADTech revenue surged 35% to $131M. This is consistent with management's guidance that 2026 energy results are second-half weighted while ADTech leads.

The earnings track record shows four consecutive beats, but the surprise magnitude has compressed sharply: 64.0% (Q3 2025), 35.6% (Q4 2025), 11.6% (Q1 2026). This deceleration is consistent with the operating slowdown visible in the segment data.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is bifurcated, and that distinction matters for scoring.

**The durable growth is in ADTech.** Revenue +35% in Q1 2026, with unfunded backlog more than doubling, supported by sustained U.S. maritime-security prioritization, submarine sustainment demand, and unmanned-systems work. This segment is counter-cyclical to oil prices and less exposed to offshore E&P budgeting. The hire of a retired Seventh Fleet commander to lead ADTech signals intent to scale.

**The energy core is in a cyclical soft patch.** ROV utilization fell to 65% (2025) and 61% (Q1 2026); average contracted floating rigs declined ~6% to 137. Management expects the oil market to remain oversupplied through early 2026 and guides OPG revenue and operating income to decline significantly this year (lower U.S. Gulf and West Africa activity, partially offset by Caspian/Middle East/Brazil). IMDS is soft on Middle East disruption. Manufactured Products is the energy bright spot: the leading demand indicator — subsea tree installations — is forecast to rise to 370 in 2026 from 343 in 2025, with tree awards jumping to 306 from 190, and the January 2026 restart of a long-suspended Mozambique LNG umbilical contract is a tailwind.

**Estimate revisions are mixed.** Current-year EPS consensus has firmed modestly (+5.6% over 90 days to $1.89), but next-year estimates have fallen 8.2% over 90 days to $2.08, and next-quarter estimates are down 7.3% over 30 days. This is well short of the revision velocity that would mark a clean earnings inflection. The combination of record backlog and a soft near-term operating cadence points to growth that is real but back-half loaded, with execution and timing risk.

## Valuation Assessment

The most important valuation point is that the cheap-looking trailing multiple is an artifact. At ~$35.50, the screen shows trailing P/E of 11.1x on $3.49 EPS, but that EPS embeds the one-time tax benefit. On normalized forward earnings the picture is different: ~18.8x current-year consensus ($1.89) and ~17.1x next-year ($2.08).

Against peers, the comparison is heterogeneous. The peer median P/E of 38.2 is skewed by high-multiple pressure-pumping and completion names (WHD 55.0, INVX 38.2, LBRT 30.8) that are not close operating comps. The most comparable profile, Tidewater (TDW), an offshore vessel operator, trades at 12.4x. On normalized forward earnings OII sits at a premium to TDW, which is defensible given OII's higher-margin ROV franchise and the defense diversification, but it does not make the stock cheap. External DCF work cited in recent coverage puts fair value around $37.50, with analyst targets ranging $31–39 and a $35.25 mean, essentially at the current price.

The valuation score reflects that there is no identifiable mispricing offering asymmetric upside today. The headline-cheap multiple is a value-trap signal once the tax benefit is stripped out; the stock trades near analyst fair value at the top of its range, with no dividend support and next-year estimates drifting lower. We are not penalizing the stock for having appreciated; the issue is that current price already reflects much of the second-half recovery and ADTech ramp, leaving limited margin of safety. A high valuation score would require either a pullback toward the low-$30s or hard evidence that energy-segment margins have troughed and the ADTech backlog is converting faster than the Street models.

## Competitive Landscape

Oceaneering's moat is real but its near-term trajectory is mixed.

**Subsea Robotics** is the clear global leader by fleet size and drill-support share. Competition turns on availability, deployment capability, service quality, and price. The franchise's economics are highly sensitive to utilization and pricing, and both have softened cyclically. This is a leader with strong structural position but slipping near-term metrics.

**Manufactured Products / umbilicals** has historically been over-supplied, but competitors have cut capacity while demand rises, supporting the margin recovery already visible. Rising subsea tree awards are constructive for the medium term.

**OPG** benefits from Jones Act compliance as a structural advantage in the U.S. Gulf, but competitors can redeploy vessels relatively easily, keeping the market competitive. This is the segment under the most near-term pressure.

**ADTech** is a differentiated niche — harsh-environment engineering, submarine sustainment, unmanned maritime systems, and subsea critical-infrastructure protection — with a structural defense tailwind and improving share trajectory. This is the part of the franchise whose competitive position is clearly strengthening.

Net, the company is a market leader with a widening position in defense and a cyclically pressured position in core energy services. The trajectory is positive in the smaller, faster-growing segment and soft in the larger, cyclical one, which nets to a moderate score.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Oil & gas cyclicality (core risk).** A majority of revenue is tied to offshore E&P spending. Utilization is declining and contracted floating rigs are falling. Many ROV contracts carry 30-day termination clauses. This is largely priced for an oil-services name, but the near-term direction is unfavorable.

2. **China "Unreliable Entity List" designation (idiosyncratic, escalating).** China sanctioned Oceaneering and certain executives in December 2024 over U.S. arms sales to Taiwan, then placed OII on its Unreliable Entity List in September 2025, generally prohibiting import/export activity and new investment in China. Management does not currently expect a material impact, but this caps the China commercial opportunity and is a tail risk if U.S.-China tensions escalate. It flows directly from the defense thesis.

3. **U.S. government budget dependence (rising as ADTech grows).** With the defense business as the growth engine and the U.S. Government at 12% of revenue, the company is increasingly exposed to appropriations risk, continuing resolutions, shutdowns, and shifting acquisition priorities.

4. **OPG decline guided for 2026** and the Q1 IMDS operating loss point to near-term segment-level weakness that could pressure consolidated margins more than the back-half recovery offsets.

5. **Project-loss exposure.** Roughly 17% of revenue is fixed-price percentage-of-completion. Projected contract losses were recognized in Q1 2026 ($2.6M) and Q1 2025 ($2.9M); the $6.8M ADTech dispute accrual underscores estimate risk on government contracts.

6. **2028 refinancing.** The $500M note is the only material maturity. The nearly net-cash position and undrawn revolver give flexibility, but refinancing terms hinge on credit markets and rates.

7. **Capital-return gap.** No dividend, and token buybacks ($40M in 2025) were paused in Q1 2026 despite strong FCF. Capital return is not yet part of the story, leaving room for a future catalyst but also no yield support for the stock at its current level.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 64.8% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 42.1% (a 22.7-point spread, 80th percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (64.8% near-term vs. 48.9% six-month), which typically signals an imminent event or elevated short-term uncertainty. Put/call skew of 14.2% indicates elevated put demand, and ATM IV rose 11.1 points as the price fell 8.6% since the prior snapshot. Offsetting this, put/call open-interest ratio is low at 0.12 and volume is thin, so the signal is event hedging rather than a heavy directional bet, consistent with the broader energy-complex volatility around Middle East developments and the second-half-weighted operating cadence. For a HOLD, this argues against chasing the stock near its highs and supports waiting for the implied-volatility premium to resolve.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** ADTech compounds at 30%+ as the unfunded backlog converts, structurally lifting the consolidated growth rate and margin mix while reducing oil-price sensitivity. Energy troughs in H1 2026 and recovers in the second half, with rising subsea tree awards and the Mozambique restart driving Manufactured Products. Margins re-expand toward 12%+, normalized EPS approaches $2.20–2.40, and the stock re-rates toward 18–20x, implying ~$40–45. Strong FCF funds opportunistic refinancing of the 2028 note and a step-up in buybacks. Bull target ~$43.

**Bear case.** Offshore oversupply persists, ROV utilization stays in the low-60s, OPG declines worse than guided, and IMDS stays pressured. Normalized EPS stalls near $1.80 and the multiple compresses toward the comparable offshore-vessel peer (~12–13x), pulling the stock toward $24–28 (roughly -25% to -35% from current). A China escalation or a soft 2028 refinancing adds tail risk. Bear target ~$26.

**Base case.** Energy stabilizes and recovers modestly in H2 while ADTech carries growth. Normalized EPS reaches ~$2.00–2.05 in 2026, the multiple holds around 17–18x, and the stock trades in a $36–40 range, roughly flat to modestly higher from current levels, which is why we hold rather than buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July), which will test the second-half-recovery framing, ROV utilization, and the OPG decline trajectory. The September quarter is the more decisive read on whether the H2 weighting materializes.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$30s (≤ ~$31), restoring a margin of safety against ~$2.00 normalized EPS (≤16x), OR
- Two consecutive quarters of ROV utilization recovering back above 65% with consolidated operating margin returning toward 11%+, confirming the energy trough, OR
- ADTech backlog conversion accelerating with current-year and next-year EPS revisions both turning positive (reversing the current -8% next-year drift).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- ROV utilization falling below 58% or OPG/IMDS weakness spreading such that consolidated operating margin drops below ~7% on a sustained basis, OR
- A material China-related impairment or restriction that becomes quantified rather than "no material impact," OR
- Deterioration in 2028 refinancing prospects (credit-market stress widening OII's implied refinancing cost meaningfully above the 6.0% coupon).

## Conclusion

Oceaneering is a high-quality, financially healthy market leader with a clean balance sheet, record backlog, and an attractive counter-cyclical defense growth engine in ADTech. Those strengths justify holding rather than avoiding. But the case for buying at ~$35–37 is weak: the headline-cheap P/E is a tax artifact, normalized valuation sits near fair value and near the most comparable peer once adjusted, the energy core is in a cyclical soft patch with declining utilization and a guided-down OPG, next-year estimates are drifting lower, and the options market is pricing near-term stress. The stock has already captured much of the recovery it needs to deliver, leaving no asymmetry at current levels. We rate OII a HOLD, with a constructive 12-month watch on ADTech conversion and the second-half energy recovery, and a preference to add only on a pullback toward the low-$30s or hard confirmation the energy segments have troughed.
