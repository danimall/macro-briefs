**Research Date: June 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Matador Resources is a mid-cap, oil-weighted independent E&P concentrated almost entirely in the Delaware Basin, paired with a 51%-owned San Mateo midstream business that gives it flow assurance and a fee-based earnings stream most similarly sized peers lack. The operating business is healthy: record 2025 production of 207,070 BOE/d, $883M of free cash flow (24% FCF margin), an upgraded BB credit profile, and a balance sheet whose headline 2.07x debt/equity overstates parent leverage because $918M of San Mateo debt is non-recourse. The Q1 2026 GAAP net loss of $35.9M is an accounting artifact: a $255.5M non-cash unrealized derivative mark booked because WTI spiked above costless-collar ceilings. Underneath it, the company generated $470M of operating cash flow and $577M of adjusted EBITDA.

The stock trades at roughly 6.8x current-year and 6.0x next-year consensus EPS ($7.82 and $8.90), consensus has been revised up +75.4% and +44.3% over 90 days, the company has beaten four straight quarters (most recently by 18.8%), and a cluster of open-market insider buying by the founder-CEO and the newly promoted CFO, COO, and CAO near $53 is among the cleanest governance signals available. The stock sits below the lowest of 19 published analyst targets ($62) against a mean of $72.67, with zero sell ratings.

The reason I land on HOLD rather than BUY is specific and falsifiable. The estimate-revision surge that makes the stock screen as a cheap inflection is overwhelmingly a function of the oil price, not of company-specific demand or share gains. The current-quarter estimate jumped +108.9% over 90 days as WTI rallied toward $95-100; those forward EPS figures embed elevated crude. Matador's costless collars, which produced the Q1 mark, cap the very upside the estimates extrapolate, so the consensus likely overstates realizable earnings in a high-oil world, and the figures compress if oil reverts toward the $72.83 the company actually realized in Q1. The energy complex broadly is a range-bound, no-add tape with live reversion risk. That combination argues for owning Matador as a quality holding to accumulate on weakness rather than buying it fresh at $53-55 on commodity-driven estimate momentum. I rate it a bullish-lean HOLD with a 6.7 composite.

## Company Overview

Matador (Texas corporation, founded 2003; NYSE-listed since 2012) operates two segments:

- **Exploration & Production** — ~99% of reserves in the Delaware Basin (southeast New Mexico, west Texas). At 12/31/2025: 667.0 MMBOE proved reserves (56% oil), 212,500 net Delaware acres, ~5,295 gross identified drilling locations, and record production of 207,070 BOE/d (58% oil). The remaining Eagle Ford position was divested in Q1 2025 for $22.2M; a small Haynesville/Cotton Valley position (~2% of production) remains.
- **Midstream — San Mateo (51% owned; Five Point owns 49%)** — gathering, processing (720 MMcf/d cryogenic capacity post-2025 Marlan expansion), oil transport, and 475,000 Bbl/d of produced-water disposal. San Mateo delivered ~$63M of segment operating income and ~$32M of distributions to Matador in Q1 2026.

The competitive identity is a scaled, low-cost, oil-weighted Delaware operator with integrated midstream. Operational efficiency is genuine: average laterals of ~10,400 ft in 2025 and batch development (a 17-well John Callahan pad). It competes against far larger Permian operators (ExxonMobil/Pioneer, Chevron, Oxy, ConocoPhillips, Devon) on execution and disciplined M&A rather than scale.

## Financial Analysis

**Production and revenue growth, acquisition-fueled.** Production climbed 131,813 → 170,751 → 207,070 BOE/d (2023→2024→2025) on the 2023 Advance and September-2024 Ameredev acquisitions plus organic drilling. Revenue rose from ~$2.8B (2023) to ~$3.66B (2025). Reserves grew 9% to 667 MMBOE despite lower SEC pricing.

**Profitability compressed on price, not on operations.** Net income to shareholders declined even as revenue rose: $846M (2023) → $885M (2024) → $759M (2025), because realized oil fell from $77.88 to $64.99/Bbl and gas from $3.25 to $2.08/Mcf. FY2025 operating margin was 33.5%, net margin 23.5%, ROE 15.2%, ROA 7.4%, with $883M of free cash flow.

**Q1 2026 — read past the headline.** The GAAP net loss (–$0.29/sh) was driven by the non-cash unrealized derivative loss as WTI spiked (intraday $102.88 in late March) above collar ceilings. Adjusted EBITDA was down from $644M in Q1'25. Oil realizations rose to $72.83/Bbl. The genuine pressure point was gas: a realized $0.64/Mcf (–82% YoY) on a Waha–Henry Hub basis differential averaging –$5.99/MMBtu.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt of $3.47B at 3/31/2026 comprises the Matador revolver ($185M, down to $45M by May 6), the non-recourse San Mateo facility ($918M), and $2.4B of senior notes. The leverage covenant (debt/EBITDA ≤3.5) is comfortably met, and ~$2.0B of revolver availability provides ample liquidity. The current ratio of 0.79 and Altman Z-score of 1.6 ("distress") are sector artifacts of E&P asset intensity (asset turnover 0.31), not signs of stress; FCF, BB credit, and revolver headroom confirm a healthy company. The March 2026 refinancing — $750M 6.0% 2034 notes issued to retire $500M 6.875% 2028 notes — lowered the coupon and extended maturity at a one-time $15.6M extinguishment cost.

**Capital allocation, returns-oriented.** The dividend was raised twice in 2025 to $0.375/quarter (a 50% increase from $0.25), the $400M buyback authorized in April 2025 has been used opportunistically (~$56M in 2025, $0.7M in Q1'26), and 2026 capex of $1.35-1.44B D/C/E plus $100-110M midstream is roughly flat to down versus 2025's $1.53B, signaling discipline after the heavy 2023-2024 acquisition phase.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two distinct components, and they should be separated.

The structural component is modest. With 2026 capex flat-to-down and the M&A engine idled after Advance and Ameredev, organic production and reserve growth from here is incremental, not transformational. Net income fell over 2023-2025 even as the company grew volumes, which is the correct reminder that an oil-weighted E&P's earnings are set by the commodity, not by the drillbit.

The cyclical component is the live driver of the estimate revisions, which clear the threshold that normally flags a fundamental inflection. The complication is that they track the oil price rather than a company-specific demand shift: the spring 2026 WTI move toward $95-100 mechanically lifts forward E&P EPS across the sector. Matador's collars then cap a portion of that upside (the Q1 mark is the proof), so the consensus likely overstates the EPS Matador will actually realize if crude stays high, and the same figures compress if oil reverts toward the low-$70s already embedded in Q1 realizations. The 30-day revisions remain positive (+9.3% CY, +7.3% NY), confirming momentum has not rolled over, but the durability of the surge depends on a commodity I do not have an edge forecasting.

The one genuine, company-controllable growth lever is gas-realization recovery. The –82% collapse in realized gas is the single biggest fundamental drag, and unlike most commodity problems it has a contracted fix: 500,000 MMBtu/d of firm transport on the Hugh Brinson Pipeline (Energy Transfer's asset) expected online Q3-Q4 2026, plus new Energy Transfer gas/NGL marketing agreements signed in June 2026. If these come online on schedule and narrow the Waha basis gap, 2H 2026 gas realizations should improve materially regardless of where oil trades. This is the catalyst most worth monitoring.

## Valuation Assessment

On almost any forward metric, Matador is cheap. The peer median P/E of 33.4x is distorted (MUR at 66x, CHRD and CRC negative), but even normalizing for that, MTDR sits at the low end of the E&P complex. It carries a 2.8% dividend yield against a 2.8% peer-median yield.

The valuation score reflects genuine asymmetry, but with an explicit haircut for the commodity-extrapolation risk in the consensus EPS. The honest stress test is normalized earnings power: realized oil was $72.83/Bbl in Q1, close to a defensible mid-cycle WTI of $70-75. If I haircut the forward EPS toward what the business earns at that realized level — call it roughly $6.00-6.50 of normalized power once the gas takeaway fix is partly captured — the stock still trades at only ~8-9x. That is cheap for a low-cost operator with a fee-based midstream stream and a returns-oriented capital program. The downside is therefore better contained than a naive commodity bear would assume: realized oil is already near normalized, and collars provide partial downside protection.

What keeps this at 7.5 rather than higher is that the "cheapness" partly reflects structural commodity cyclicality that the market permanently prices, and that the analyst targets floating above the stock are themselves built on the same elevated-oil consensus that the revisions reflect. Targets that move with the oil price are not independent confirmation of intrinsic value; they re-rate downward if crude reverts. The valuation is attractive in the way the whole oil-weighted Permian cohort is attractive right now, not because of a Matador-specific mispricing the market has missed.

## Competitive Landscape

The Delaware Basin is the most economic US oil play but is structurally short gas takeaway, which is why Waha regularly prices near zero or negative. This is a basin-wide problem affecting every Permian producer, not a Matador-specific failing. Matador's response is more proactive than most: the Hugh Brinson firm transport, the new Energy Transfer marketing agreements, Houston Ship Channel exposure for much of its gas, and NGL extraction should narrow its realized-price gap as new takeaway comes online late in 2026.

The San Mateo midstream is a real differentiator. It provides flow assurance, a fee-based earnings stream, and distributions plus Five Point performance incentives that most similarly sized E&Ps cannot match. Operationally, longer laterals and batch-frac development keep Matador in the low-cost tier. Against the scale advantages of Exxon/Pioneer, Chevron, and Oxy, Matador competes on efficiency and disciplined M&A. The competitive trajectory is stable to modestly improving, with gas-realization relief the key swing factor.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Waha gas basis blowout (dominant near-term).** ~42% of production is gas. The Hugh Brinson fix is contracted but not yet flowing — execution and timing (Q3-Q4 2026) are the risk.
2. **Commodity-price reversion.** The forward estimates and analyst targets embed elevated WTI. A reversion toward $70-75 compresses the EPS that currently makes the stock look cheap. This is the swing factor for the entire bull thesis.
3. **Customer concentration.** Three purchasers accounted for ~72% of oil/gas/NGL revenue in 2025 (79% in 2024).
4. **Federal-land exposure.** ~33% of Delaware acreage is on federal land, subject to BLM permitting delays, NEPA litigation, and the enjoined 2024 BLM waste rule.
5. **Minimum-volume commitments.** ~$2.29B of deficiency exposure on third-party transport/gathering/processing/disposal plus $701M to San Mateo — take-or-pay obligations that bite if volumes fall.
6. **Acquisition appetite / re-leveraging.** The growth model has been M&A-led; renewed deals could re-lever the balance sheet, though 2026 capital allocation signals discipline.

Per the Q1 2026 10-Q, there were no material changes to risk factors since the 10-K.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term event risk. Near-term ATM IV of 46.9% sits rich to 1-year realized vol of 41.2% (a 5.6% spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (46.9% near-term vs. 43.4% at six months), which typically reflects an imminent catalyst rather than durable stress. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.1%. The put/call volume ratio of 1.44 and rising put/call OI (0.08 → 0.22 since late May) show hedging building, and the 3- and 6-month skews are negative (–4.3% and –5.3%), consistent with downside hedging into the commodity and the gas-takeaway timeline. Short interest is moderate at 8.9% of float (6.4 days to cover), not a squeeze setup. Net read: the options market is hedging the oil/gas swing factor and an upcoming print, not signaling conviction in either direction, consistent with the balanced, wait-for-confirmation stance.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Oil holds in the $90-100 range or higher on Gulf supply disruption, the Hugh Brinson transport and Energy Transfer marketing deals come online in 2H 2026 and lift gas realizations from $0.64 toward something rational, and the four-beat track record continues. The stock re-rates from 6-7x toward the analyst mean of $72.67 and beyond as consensus targets follow rising estimates. The insider-buying cluster proves prescient. Bull target ~$80 (+45-50%).

**Bear Case.** Oil reverts toward $70-75 and softening demand. Collar caps mean realized oil does not capture spot strength, while reversion compresses the elevated forward EPS and the analyst targets that float on it. Gas-takeaway relief slips into 2027. The stock de-rates toward $45 (–15% to –18%), with the dividend and FCF providing a floor.

**Base Case.** Oil stays range-bound near $100 with reversion risk live; gas relief arrives broadly on schedule in 2H 2026, lifting realizations; the company continues to beat on cash metrics while GAAP optics remain noisy from derivative marks. The stock grinds toward the low-$60s as the gas fix is confirmed. The risk/reward is favorable but commodity-dependent.

The disagreement between this HOLD and an outright BUY comes down to one judgment: whether the +75%/+44% revision surge is a company-specific inflection (which would justify BUY) or a commodity-price re-rate (which warrants owning the quality holding but not chasing it). On the evidence, it is the latter. The revisions track the spring oil spike; the collars cap the upside they extrapolate; the targets float on the same crude assumption; and realized oil is already near where a normalized estimate would sit. That is a constructive setup to accumulate on weakness, not a fresh entry at $53-55 with oil elevated and reversion risk priced into the options term structure.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (early August), where the first read on Hugh Brinson timing and gas-realization recovery will arrive, followed by the Q3 print confirming whether firm transport is flowing.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Hugh Brinson firm transport confirmed online and gas realizations recovering toward $2.00+/Mcf, demonstrating the revision durability is not purely oil-driven; OR
- A pullback toward $45-48 that widens the downside cushion and creates the asymmetry the calibration requires for an energy long; OR
- WTI confirmed sustainably above $85 with the 2026 collar book repositioned to reduce the upside cap, making the rising estimates realizable rather than collar-suppressed.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- WTI reverting decisively below $70 with 30-day estimate revisions turning negative, signaling the inflection has reversed; OR
- Gas-takeaway relief slipping materially into 2027 while Waha basis stays below –$4/MMBtu; OR
- A re-leveraging acquisition that pushes debt/EBITDA toward the 3.5x covenant.

## Conclusion

Matador is a well-run, low-cost Delaware operator with a genuine midstream differentiator, a disciplined and shareholder-friendly capital program, an upgraded credit profile, and one of the strongest governance signals available — a cluster of open-market insider buying by the founder-CEO and the newly promoted CFO, COO, and CAO near current prices. The Q1 GAAP loss is an optical illusion driven by a non-cash derivative mark, cash generation is strong, and the dominant near-term drag (gas realizations) has a contracted fix arriving in 2H 2026. The forward valuation of 6-7x is cheap even on conservative normalized earnings, and the downside is better contained than a naive commodity bear would suggest because realized oil is already near a normalized $72.83.

I hold a HOLD with a bullish lean rather than a BUY for one reason I can state precisely: the estimate-revision surge that makes the stock screen as an inflection is a commodity-price re-rate, not a company-specific demand shift, and the collars that produced the Q1 mark cap the very upside the consensus extrapolates. In an energy tape that is range-bound near $100 with live reversion risk, that is a quality name to accumulate on a pullback toward the mid-to-high $40s or on confirmed gas-takeaway relief, not a fresh chase at $53-55. The asymmetry to the analyst distribution is real and the management signal is excellent, which is why this is a constructive HOLD rather than a neutral one. **Recommendation: HOLD (bullish lean), composite 6.7.**
