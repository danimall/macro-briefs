## Executive Summary

ServisFirst Bancshares is a $4.3 billion market cap bank holding company that runs one of the most efficient banking operations among its regional banking peers. The company's 29.8% efficiency ratio in Q1 2026 is roughly half the industry norm, produced by a model that generates $17.7 billion in assets through only 33 branches and approximately 666 employees. This structural operating leverage compounds powerfully when net interest margins expand — as they have over the past five quarters, from 2.82% to 3.53%.

The stock trades at an estimated 12-13x forward EPS, a reasonable multiple for a bank delivering 18% ROE and 8%+ loan growth, but at a discount to what the franchise quality would ordinarily command. The discount is attributable to a single large nonperforming credit relationship that has lingered since Q3 2025 and appears likely to require additional quarters to resolve. If this credit resolves without material incremental charge-offs, the stock should re-rate toward $91-95; if it deteriorates further, downside to the low $70s is plausible.

The combination of continued NIM expansion potential ($2 billion in fixed-rate repricing opportunities), the Texas market entry, and earnings momentum creates an attractive risk/reward profile at current levels, moderated but not negated by the credit overhang.

## Company Overview

ServisFirst Bancshares, headquartered in Birmingham, Alabama, operates through its wholly-owned subsidiary ServisFirst Bank across seven southeastern states (Alabama, Florida, Georgia, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee, Virginia) with a new expansion into Texas (Houston) launched in December 2025. Total assets stood at $17.73 billion as of year-end 2025, with $13.70 billion in loans and $14.22 billion in deposits.

The operating model differs fundamentally from conventional regional banking. Rather than deploying hundreds of branches, SFBS uses 33 full-service offices staffed by experienced relationship bankers operating under decentralized decision-making authority. Regional CEOs make local credit and pricing decisions, supported by centralized credit administration. This produces among the lowest efficiency ratios in its regional banking peer set while maintaining strong growth — a combination that is structurally difficult for competitors to replicate without dismantling existing infrastructure.

The company also operates a correspondent banking division serving 388 banks (145 settling at the Federal Reserve), which provides low-cost deposit funding, fee income, and cross-selling opportunities rarely available to banks of this size.

## Growth Analysis

Several growth vectors are visible and quantifiable:

**1. NIM Expansion Runway.** Management identified approximately $2 billion in fixed-rate loans scheduled to reprice in 2026 at approximately 130bp higher than current rates. Combined with continued deposit cost reductions and potential further Fed rate cuts, NIM has room to move higher from 3.53%. Each 10bp of NIM expansion on the approximately $16.9 billion earning asset base translates to approximately $17 million in pre-tax income (~$0.25/share after tax).

**2. Texas Market Entry.** A 9-person team in Houston (primarily C&I) was hired in December 2025, with management budgeting more growth for Texas "than any other region" in 2026. M&A disruption among Southeastern banks creates talent acquisition opportunities. Based on the trajectory of prior market entries (Carolina, Virginia), Texas could contribute $500M-$1B in loans within 3 years, though this is a scenario projection rather than company guidance.

**3. Loan Growth Sustainability.** 8%+ loan growth reflects the competitive advantage of decentralized, fast decision-making. The pipeline benefits from competitors distracted by integration activities.

**4. Correspondent Banking.** 388 bank relationships provide a stable deposit base and cross-selling platform that scales without proportional cost increases.

Consensus estimates (per available sell-side aggregations) suggest FY2026 EPS of approximately $6.39 (+26% YoY) and FY2027 EPS of approximately $7.17 (+12% YoY), with recent estimate revision trends positive. These figures should be verified against current consensus data.

## Competitive Landscape

SFBS competes against Regions Financial, Wells Fargo, PNC, Truist, and Pinnacle Financial Partners in southeastern markets. Its competitive advantages are structural and difficult to replicate:

1. **Efficiency moat.** The 29.8% efficiency ratio reflects a fundamentally different operating architecture. Competitors would need to close hundreds of branches and restructure compensation models to approximate this structure, which they cannot do without disrupting existing customer relationships.

2. **Speed of execution.** Decentralized decision-making with regional CEO authority means loan decisions happen in hours or days versus weeks at larger institutions. This attracts relationship-oriented commercial customers who value responsiveness.

3. **Talent acquisition model.** The bank attracts experienced bankers from larger institutions by offering decision-making autonomy, meaningful equity participation, and a performance-driven culture. The Texas hire of 9 bankers from competitors demonstrates ongoing ability to recruit.

4. **Correspondent banking network.** 388 bank relationships provide cheap, stable deposits and information flow about market conditions across the Southeast.

The bank is gaining share through organic growth (8.7% FY2025 loan growth versus generally slower industry growth rates), expanding into new markets, and benefiting from competitor M&A disruption.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risk: Concentrated Nonperforming Credit**
According to SEC filings, nonaccrual loans rose to $168.4 million (1.23% of loans) at December 31, 2025, attributable to a single large real-estate-secured relationship. Based on management commentary from earnings calls, the relationship involves approximately 8 loans in a multifamily/workforce housing portfolio described as a "merchant developer," with resolution expected to be "slow" through 2026. At year-end 2025, the allowance for credit losses of $171.7 million provided approximately 102% coverage of nonaccrual loans. Management indicated NPAs increased slightly in Q1 2026. If this relationship requires material write-downs beyond current reserves (e.g., $30-50M incremental), it would reduce EPS by $0.35-0.60 and likely send the stock to the low $70s.

**Secondary Risks:**
- **CRE concentration (44.3% of loans):** Particular exposure to Florida (26.7% of CRE portfolio) and multifamily. Office exposure ($471M) is manageable at 6.4% of CRE.
- **Uninsured deposits (68% of total):** While offset by strong liquidity and no brokered deposits, this represents confidence-risk vulnerability if regional banking stress re-emerges.
- **Revenue concentration in NII (~95%):** Minimal fee income diversification makes earnings highly sensitive to NIM trajectory.
- **Interest rate sensitivity:** Liability-sensitive balance sheet benefits from rate cuts but faces headwinds if rates rise unexpectedly (partially mitigated by floors on 86% of variable loans).
