## Executive Summary

Ziff Davis has been reconstituted. On June 17, 2026 it closed the sale of its Connectivity segment to Accenture for $1,216.1 million in aggregate proceeds ($1,134.1 million net of divested cash), booking an $860.6 million pre-tax gain and a $200 million tax liability. The company now holds $1,606.1 million of cash against $872.3 million of debt principal and a $185.6 million income-tax payable, leaving $548.2 million of net cash after the tax bill — $15.68 per share on the 34,962,853 shares outstanding at June 30, or 29% of the current market capitalization. Management retired 3,813,873 shares for $168.6 million in the first half at an average near $44.20, shrinking the count 8.9% in six months, with 7,669,154 shares (21.9% of the base) still authorized through February 2036. Solvency, refinancing capacity and covenant headroom are no longer live questions, which is why we are upgrading from the prior AVOID.

The operating business behind that balance sheet is deteriorating faster than headline revenue implies. Q2 continuing-operations revenue fell 2.7% to $286.7 million, but stripping the $7.1 million contributed by 2025 and 2026 acquisitions gives organic revenue of −5.2%; the first-half figure is −5.5% organic against −2.3% reported. Acquisition spending has fallen roughly 80% year over year ($81.9 million across seven deals in FY2025 to $8.4 million across two in H1 2026), so the reported decline should converge toward the organic rate. Advertising net revenue retention is below 100% in all three advertising segments and collapsed in Gaming & Entertainment from 93.7% to 81.6%. Revenue per advertiser fell 11.0% in Technology & Shopping and 19.8% in Gaming while advertiser counts rose; the company is adding logos worth materially less. Our EBITDA proxy (operating income plus D&A, goodwill impairment and share-based compensation) fell 15.0% to $124.9 million in H1, or 340bp of margin compression on a 2.3% reported revenue decline.

At $53.90 the market already pays for a successful break-up. Our sum-of-parts and unlevered free-cash-flow work converges on roughly $51 base value, against $36 in a bear case where organic decline steepens toward −8%, and $68 in a bull case combining a Cybersecurity & Martech divestiture at a strategic multiple, continued repurchases and stabilization near −2%. Probability-weighted at 45/30/25 that is approximately −6%. The rating is REDUCE rather than HOLD because the realized catalyst is spent, estimates are falling in every window, $196.3 million of goodwill sits at a zero-cushion impairment threshold, and near-term implied volatility is in backwardation at the 82nd percentile of its own chain.

## Company Overview

Ziff Davis operates owned-and-operated digital properties in four reportable segments following the Connectivity divestiture (the count fell from five to four in Q1 2026):

| Segment | Q2'26 revenue | YoY | Q2'26 operating income | Model |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Technology & Shopping | $76.8M | −5.0% | −$3.3M | Programmatic display + affiliate commerce |
| Gaming & Entertainment | $46.6M | +0.9% | +$9.0M | Advertising + Humble Bundle subscriptions |
| Health & Wellness | $94.7M | −4.8% | −$42.3M (incl. $54.8M impairment) | Consumer/professional health media, Lose It! |
| Cybersecurity & Martech | $68.7M | +0.5% | +$13.4M | Email security, privacy, email marketing subscriptions |
| Corporate | — | — | −$21.5M | Unallocated |
| **Total** | **$286.7M** | **−2.7%** | **−$44.7M** | |

Roughly $182 million of Q2 revenue is advertising and performance marketing, $98 million subscription and licensing, $6 million other. The vast majority of advertising is recognized gross because ZD acts as principal on properties it owns; two carve-outs are net (certain third-party ad networks and servicing of client gift-card programs through TDS Gift Cards). Management states explicitly in MD&A that margins on ads placed on owned properties are significantly higher than on third-party inventory.

The asserted moat is editorial authority in specific verticals converting into organic search rank and therefore free traffic. Google owns the distribution channel that delivers that traffic, and it is being restructured around AI answers. That is the central analytical fact about this company.

A data note before valuation: the vendor profile lists trailing and forward P/E of 3.09x. That figure is an artifact of the divestiture — trailing twelve-month GAAP EPS includes the $17.16 per-share Connectivity gain recognized in Q2. It carries no valuation information and should be discarded. The same profile lists 36.84 million shares and $1.98 billion market capitalization; the June 30 10-Q count of 34,962,853 shares at $53.90 gives $1,884 million, and post-quarter repurchases would push the count lower still. We use $1,884 million throughout; substituting $1.98 billion raises every enterprise-value multiple by roughly 8%.

## Financial Analysis

**Balance sheet (6/30/26).** Cash $1,606.1M. Debt principal $872.3M: $149.1M of 1.75% converts due November 1, 2026 (current), $263.1M of 3.625% converts due March 1, 2028, $460.0M of 4.625% senior notes due October 15, 2030. Income taxes payable $185.6M. Stockholders' equity $2,237.5M, of which goodwill and intangibles are $1,584.8M (39.6% of assets). Revolver undrawn with $348.9M available, maturing June 18, 2027. Maximum 4.00x total leverage and minimum 3.00x interest coverage covenants, in compliance. Both convertible series are deeply out of the money ($106.63 and $100.00 strikes against $53.90), failed trigger conditions, and were excluded from diluted EPS as anti-dilutive; the 4.03 million underlying shares belong in a scenario requiring the stock to roughly double, not in current diluted capitalization.

Net cash is $733.8M gross, $548.2M after the tax payable, or $15.68 per share. ZD's debt is marked at $844.3M fair value against $867.6M carrying, with the 4.625% notes at roughly 93.8 cents. Credit markets apply a modest discount despite the net cash position.

**Cash flow — do not annualize the half.** H1 operating cash flow was $118.9M against $68.1M of capex. Three disclosed distortions run through that figure. First, TDS Gift Cards imposed a $78.1M working-capital drag in H1 2026 ($86.6M in H1 2025); settlement receivables fell from $313.4M to $187.1M and settlement payables from $469.1M to $261.2M between year-end and June 30, as the Q4 float unwinds in the first half and rebuilds in the second. Adding that back gives normalized H1 OCF near $197M. Second, the $185.6M Connectivity tax bill has not been paid — H1 cash taxes were only $10.7M — and will flow out of operating cash flow in H2, artificially depressing reported FY2026 OCF. Third, capex is not trivial: continuing-operations capex of $53.7M in H1 is 9.7% of continuing revenue, with Health & Wellness alone at $21.4M. EBITDA overstates cash generation here by roughly a third.

A defensible normalized frame: FY2026 EBITDA proxy of $290–310M (H1 at $124.9M with seasonally stronger H2), less approximately $110M capex, gives $180–200M of pre-tax, pre-interest free cash flow. At a 25% cash tax rate that is roughly $140–150M of unlevered post-tax free cash flow. Treat this as a sensitivity; no company-defined Adjusted EBITDA figure or full-year guidance was available for verification.

**Earnings quality.** Reported history through FY2025 (including Connectivity) shows revenue essentially flat — $1,391.0M in 2022 to $1,451.3M in 2025, +4.3% cumulative — with operating margin oscillating between 8.1% and 14.3% as goodwill impairments landed inside operating income. Net margin of 3.3% in FY2025 on high-20s EBITDA margins reflects $228.7M of D&A, most of it amortization of intangibles from a decade of acquisitions. Accumulated goodwill impairments reached $241.9M by June 30 with charges in FY2023, FY2024, FY2025 and Q2 2026.

The most important quality finding concerns the company's own selected performance measure. Adjusted diluted EPS was $6.19 (2023), $6.62 (2024), $6.63 (2025). Multiplying by diluted shares implies adjusted net income of approximately $287.6M, $294.7M and $272.5M — a 7.5% decline in 2025 despite revenue rising 3.5%. Flat adjusted EPS was produced by a 7.7% reduction in share count from FY2023 to FY2025 (46.46M to 41.10M diluted shares). This is arithmetic that works, and with 21.9% of the float still authorized it can work for several years, but the underlying earnings stream has been shrinking.

The four-quarter surprise record is one beat and three misses (Q2 2026 +15.9% on a $0.89 estimate, following −15.8%, −8.2% and −4.3%). The Q2 comparison spans the divestiture accounting change; secondary reporting characterized the $1.03 adjusted result as meeting non-GAAP consensus. GAAP Q2 continuing operations was −$1.43 per share; total net income of $624.5M (+$17.16) is almost entirely the Connectivity gain.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth to analyze on the continuing base, only rate of decline.

Organic revenue is −5.2% (Q2) and −5.5% (H1), against reported −2.7% and −2.3%. The gap of roughly 300bp is tuck-in M&A that has largely stopped. Management guided Q3 to increase sequentially while declining low-to-mid single digits year over year, which is consistent with reported and organic rates converging as the acquisition contribution laps out. Foreign currency moved against the company in H1 (a $8.7M cumulative translation loss in OCI), so true constant-currency organic decline may be modestly better than −5.5%; the filing does not disclose constant-currency revenue.

Operating metrics deteriorate on nearly every line:

| Metric | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Ad net revenue retention — Tech & Shopping | 90.0% | 95.0% |
| Ad net revenue retention — Gaming | 81.6% | 93.7% |
| Ad net revenue retention — Health & Wellness | 99.8% | 97.4% |
| Quarterly revenue per advertiser — Tech & Shopping | $113,267 | $127,299 |
| Quarterly revenue per advertiser — Gaming | $60,015 | $74,830 |
| H&W subscription customers | 1,618,000 | 1,892,000 |

Retention below 100% means the existing advertiser base is spending less year over year, so any growth must be purchased through new-customer acquisition. Health & Wellness retention above 100% was the one bright spot in FY2025 (102.3%); it is now 99.8% and the segment produced a $33.7M H1 operating loss versus $33.0M of income a year earlier, alongside the $54.8M goodwill charge and a $1.8M internal-use software impairment. Cybersecurity & Martech's flat revenue is composition, not growth: H1 gains came entirely from a $5.7M "other revenue" contribution from a 2025 acquisition, offsetting a $2.9M decline in the core subscription line with both customer count (−1.1%) and ARPU (−2.8%) falling. Gaming's growth is Humble Bundle subscriptions (+$3.8M in H1, ARPU +16.9%) offsetting a $1.8M advertising decline.

Estimate revisions confirm the direction: current quarter $1.25 (−11.8% over 30 days), current year $5.05 (−2.6% over 30 days, −1.6% over 90), next year $5.61 (−1.4% over 30 days, +5.8% over 90 — the only positive window, reflecting the post-divestiture recast rather than improving demand). Four analysts cover the current year; three cover the quarters. This is not an inflection profile, and the estimate-revision buy signal that applies to trough-earnings cyclicals is absent in the opposite direction.

The mix shift toward subscriptions is real and does reduce traffic elasticity. It is not customer diversification: retention is worsening in two of three ad segments, revenue per advertiser is falling in two of three, and subscription customer counts are falling in two of three subscription segments.

## Valuation Assessment

**Multiples.** At $53.90 and 34,962,853 shares, market capitalization is $1,884M. Enterprise value is $1,884 − $1,606 + $872 = $1,150M, or $1,336M including the $185.6M tax payable, which is a real near-term cash obligation and belongs in EV. Against the $290–310M FY2026 EBITDA proxy that is 3.7–4.6x. Against EBITDA less capex of approximately $190M it is 6.0–7.0x. Against roughly $1.18B of FY2026 continuing revenue it is about 1.0–1.1x.

**Peer context is of limited use.** The Advertising comparables show extreme dispersion: DoubleVerify at 37.5x P/E, Trade Desk 16.2x, Omnicom 55.2x, AppLovin 26.7x, Angi negative, median 37.5x. Every one of those is either growing or a diversified agency holding company; none is an owned-and-operated publisher in organic revenue decline. The relevant comparison set for ZD is other structurally challenged publishers, where 3–5x EBITDA is the norm rather than a discount.

**The forward earnings multiple.** Consensus adjusted EPS of $5.05 (current year) and $5.61 (next year) implies 10.7x and 9.6x. Deducting $15.68 per share of after-tax net cash gives 7.6x and 6.8x on the operating business. Those are low absolute numbers, and they are the core of the bull case. They are also exactly what a 5%-declining, margin-compressing business with recurring impairments should trade at.

**What the operating business is worth.** Implied operating-business value is $53.90 − $15.68 = $38.22 per share, or $1,336M, against roughly $140–150M of unlevered post-tax free cash flow. That is 8.9–9.5x, an 10.5–11.2% unlevered yield. Solve for the implied terminal decline: at a 12% required return, a 9.2x multiple corresponds to r − g ≈ 10.9%, so the market is embedding roughly −1% terminal decline. Current organic decline is −5.5% with 340bp of annual margin compression. For today's price to be justified, the decline must nearly stop.

**Sum-of-parts, the bull argument, lands at roughly spot.** The Accenture price is a hard arms-length data point: Connectivity generated approximately $226M of annualized revenue at a 35.7% operating margin and sold for $1.2 billion — roughly 5.2x revenue and 15x operating income (annualized from H1 2025; full-year Connectivity figures were not disclosed). Applying strategic multiples to what remains: Cybersecurity & Martech at $277M annualized revenue and 3.0–3.5x = $830–970M; Gaming at $186M and 2.0x = $372M; Technology & Shopping and Health & Wellness at 0.75x combined ($686M revenue) = $515M. Gross $1,717–1,857M, less capitalized corporate and stranded overhead of $350–450M, gives $1,270–1,500M for the operating assets, plus $548M net cash: $1,818–2,048M, or $52–59 per share. The midpoint sits essentially at the current price. The company disclosed in Q2 forward-looking statements that it is reviewing "potential value-creating opportunities," confirming a process exists without disclosing scope, timing or which lines.

We are not marking the stock down because it has appreciated from the February 24 low of $22.45. The pre-announcement price of roughly $28 valued the entire consolidated enterprise (including Connectivity) at a materially lower EV, implying a modest value for the stub. Today's stub EV of $1,336M represents a genuine re-rating on genuine new information: the closed transaction, the realized cash, and the disclosed strategic review. The question is whether current fundamentals support $1,336M, and our answer is that they support approximately $1,190M plus the litigation option.

**Unpriced optionality.** ZD is plaintiff in two suits central to its economics: *Ziff Davis v. OpenAI* (D. Del., April 24, 2025; consolidated into the S.D.N.Y. MDL May 14, 2025) alleging copyright infringement, DMCA violations and unjust enrichment; and *Ziff Davis v. Google LLC and Alphabet Inc.* (S.D.N.Y., February 6, 2026) alleging antitrust violations, deceptive acts, common law fraud and unjust enrichment relating to publisher ad servers and ad exchanges. GAAP prohibits recognizing gain contingencies, so this value appears nowhere in book value or any earnings multiple. It is also multi-year, unquantifiable, and carries certain near-term legal spend against uncertain recovery. We assign it $100–150M in the bull case and zero in the bear.

Valuation score of 4.8 reflects genuine statistical cheapness offset by an identified structural reason for it, a base case at or slightly below spot, and a catalyst that has been substantially realized.

## Competitive Landscape

The traffic problem is sector-wide and independently corroborated, which matters for diagnosis in both directions: ZD's decline is largely not an execution failure, and there is correspondingly no company-specific fix. Publishers were reported in May–July 2026 to be planning for sharply reduced Google referral traffic; Reddit's CEO stated publicly on July 30 that Google is delivering less traffic than in the past and that AI Overviews cannot replace conventional search links; Google's AI opt-out control keeps publishers opted in by default and withholds AI-specific performance data, making the trade-off unmeasurable; multiple major publishers were reported to be weighing blocking Google entirely.

ZD's revenue mix is unusually exposed within that dynamic. Affiliate commerce requires a user to arrive, read, click through to a merchant and transact — four steps an AI answer can short-circuit. Programmatic display is priced per impression and therefore falls one-for-one with pageviews. Technology & Shopping is concentrated in exactly those two, and the FY2025 10-K attributed a roughly 18% year-over-year Q4 segment decline to "continued search rank volatility."

Where ZD is defended: Cybersecurity & Martech (email security, privacy, email marketing subscriptions) has no traffic dependency and produced $27.1M of H1 operating income at a 9.3% year-over-year gain; Gaming's Humble Bundle subscription base grew ARPU 16.9%. Both are stable rather than growing organically. Where it is most exposed: Technology & Shopping and, newly, the professional Health & Wellness lines, which MD&A names as the primary driver of that segment's advertising decline.

Trajectory, which is what matters for this dimension, is negative across every disclosed metric. Score 2.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill with zero cushion.** Following the Q2 Health & Wellness charge and the FY2025 Cybersecurity & Martech charge, the 10-Q states there is no excess of fair value over carrying value at those two reporting units, and that any further decline in estimated fair value greater than the decline in carrying value produces an additional charge. Goodwill at those units is $196.3M. No other reporting unit has less than 10% cushion. These are non-cash and do not affect covenants, which are EBITDA-based, but they constitute management's own downward revision of forecast revenue and EBITDA at those units, and further charges are likely if current trends persist.

**Privacy litigation as a cost of the model.** Three putative class actions are pending, all stayed: *Fregosa v. Mashable* (California Invasion of Privacy Act; motion to dismiss the second amended complaint denied October 9, 2025), *Josue v. IGN Entertainment* (Video Privacy Protection Act; motion to dismiss denied July 10, 2025, stayed pending *Salazar v. Paramount Global* at the Supreme Court), and *Milito v. Ookla*. Two denied dismissal motions mean these survived initial screening. No material loss contingencies are accrued and ranges are not estimable.

**Indirect tax.** The company states it does not collect and remit sales, use or telecommunications taxes in certain jurisdictions where it believes they are inapplicable, is under audit in various jurisdictions, and that additional liabilities are reasonably possible but not estimable. Reserves for non-income taxes are $18.4M; uncertain income tax positions total $26.1M.

**Near-term cash and refinancing.** The $185.6M Connectivity tax payment flows through H2 operating cash flow. $149.1M of converts mature November 1, 2026 and will be repaid in cash. The $350M revolver matures June 18, 2027, and the company notes it may not be able to refinance at similar rates. All are covered by the cash balance; these are cost-of-capital rather than liquidity items.

**Governance and alignment.** Five-year TSR indexed to 41.39 against a peer group at 134.40 — a 93-point relative shortfall and roughly a 59% absolute loss. Compensation actually paid was negative in 2022 and 2023 and $2.89M in 2025 against $11.77M of grant-date value, so equity is genuinely at risk. Grant-date value stepped from roughly $1.5–1.7M in 2022–2023 to $12–12.7M in 2024–2025, the years of steepest relative underperformance; the disclosed three-to-eight-year CEO vesting schedule is consistent with a front-loaded multi-year cycle, and we make no motive attribution. The 2026 PSU valuation used an expected volatility input of 49.1% versus 34.5% a year earlier — the company's own model conceding a large increase in perceived business uncertainty.

**Insider activity.** Zero open-market purchases across the 25 most recent Form 4 lines. Two discretionary sales in the weeks around the June 17 closing: CFO Bret Richter sold 18,000 shares at $46.88 on June 10, leaving 29,244 (approximately 38% of holdings); General Counsel Jeremy Rossen sold 12,347 shares at $45.75–$47.00, leaving 14,462. Both occurred 13–15% below the current price. Timing is consistent with routine diversification after a large March vesting and after a value-crystallizing transaction removed information asymmetry, and no 10b5-1 adoptions or terminations were reported. It is also consistent with less conviction than the buyback implies. CEO Vivek Shah holds 348,693 shares and did not sell, which is the strongest offsetting datapoint. CEO and CFO March dispositions were Code F tax withholding.

**Dilution.** Equity awards outstanding total approximately 2.88M shares at 100% PSU achievement (7.6% ownership dilution if fully issued, leaving existing holders with 92.4%), or 3.9M at maximum PSU achievement. A further 1,350,741 shares remain available under the 2024 Plan. Unrecognized compensation cost is $51.3M (RSUs) and $26.6M (PSUs).

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 63.4% against 57.6% one-year realized — rich by 5.8 points and in the 82nd percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (63.4% at nine days versus 60.4% at 128 days), which typically signals a near-dated event rather than a directional view. Put/call open interest is 0.08 and the front-expiry skew is call-heavy at −4.8%, while the six-month tenor shows −6.2%; the September tenor prices a ±13.3% move and the nine-day expiry ±8.5%. Since the prior snapshot on June 19, ATM IV rose 8.7 points while the price rose 16.8%.

The combination of 18.2% short interest at 6.0 days to cover, call-heavy front-month open interest, and near-term backwardation is consistent with speculation on a further divestiture announcement layered on a squeeze-prone float. It cuts against a short position and argues for trimming rather than pressing a bearish view. It also means volatility is expensive to buy for protection at the front of the curve; owners who want to retain exposure to a divestiture announcement while reducing risk are better served by outright position reduction than by front-dated puts.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($68, +26%)

The Accenture transaction proves ZD's constituent assets fetch far more than the conglomerate does — 5.2x revenue and 15x operating income against roughly 1.0x and a mid-single-digit EBITDA proxy for what remains. Management has disclosed a live review of further value-creating opportunities, and Cybersecurity & Martech is the natural next candidate: $277M of annualized subscription revenue, $54M of annualized operating income, no traffic dependency. A sale near 3.5x revenue would deliver roughly $970M pre-tax against a total current enterprise value of $1,336M. The buyback is a persistent bid with 21.9% of shares authorized and $1.6 billion of cash, and repurchases at the H1 average of $44.20 were accretive. The OpenAI and Google suits are pure option value carried at zero. If organic decline stabilizes near −2% as the subscription mix rises, per-share earnings can grow on a shrinking base indefinitely. Analyst mean and median targets of $55 and $60 sit above spot, five covering analysts show one strong buy, two buys and two holds, and forecasts were raised after the Q2 print.

### Bear Case ($36, −33%)

The decline rate is accelerating, not stabilizing. Organic revenue is −5.5% with the reported figure set to converge downward as the $18.2M H1 acquisition contribution laps out and deal spending has fallen 80%. Advertising net revenue retention is below 100% in all three ad segments and fell 12 points in Gaming in a single year. Margins compressed 340bp on a 2.3% revenue decline, so the EBITDA proxy fell 15.0%. Health & Wellness, the stable segment as recently as FY2025, swung to a $33.7M H1 operating loss with a 14.5% subscription customer loss and a $54.8M impairment, and $196.3M of goodwill now carries zero cushion. Capex at 9.7% of revenue means EBITDA overstates cash by roughly a third. The buyback converts a one-time cash windfall into fewer shares at prices above the H1 average; once deployed, the declining business remains. Adjusted EPS has been flat for three years only because share count fell 7.7%, while implied adjusted net income fell 7.5% in 2025. Estimates are falling in five of six disclosed windows. If EBITDA less capex declines toward $130M and the multiple compresses to 6x on a worsening trend, the operating business is worth $780M, or $22 per share, plus $15.68 of net cash minus further cash absorption.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: REDUCE. Base case $51 (−5.4%), bull $68 (+26.2%), bear $36 (−33.2%). Probability-weighted at 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear: approximately −6%.**

Base case math: FY2026 EBITDA proxy $295M less $110M capex = $185M, taxed at 25% = $139M of unlevered post-tax free cash flow, capitalized at 8.5x = $1,182M for the operating business, plus $548M of after-tax net cash = $1,730M on approximately 34.0M shares after continued repurchase = $50.9.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength. The stock sits roughly 7% below its August 7 intraday high of $58.06 after a substantial advance from February, and the transaction that drove that advance has closed and been paid for. Reduce exposure to a residual position no larger than one-third of a full weight, sized to retain participation if a second divestiture is announced. Do not short: 18.2% short interest at 6.0 days to cover, call-heavy open interest, a persistent corporate bid with 21.9% of shares authorized, and $15.68 per share of hard net cash make the upside tail dangerous relative to the −33% bear.

**Upgrade triggers to HOLD or ACCUMULATE.** (1) A signed agreement to divest Cybersecurity & Martech or another segment at 3x revenue or better, which would put realized proceeds above $830M against a $1,336M enterprise value. (2) Organic revenue decline improving to −2% or better for two consecutive quarters with the EBITDA proxy margin holding at or above 25%, evidencing that cost actions are outrunning the traffic decline. (3) Advertising net revenue retention returning above 95% in Technology & Shopping and above 90% in Gaming. (4) A price in the low $40s absent fundamental deterioration, which would restore the operating business to roughly 6.5x unlevered post-tax free cash flow and re-open a margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.** (1) Organic decline steepening beyond −8% or FY2026 revenue guidance implying it. (2) A further goodwill impairment at the two zero-cushion units, confirming another downward revision of internal forecasts. (3) Buyback pace slowing materially while cash is redirected to M&A at prices above 2x revenue, which would convert the one clear source of per-share value into acquisition risk. (4) The strategic review concluding with no transaction, removing the sole identified re-rating catalyst.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoints are the Q3 2026 print (late October/early November, which will show the first clean post-divestiture year-over-year comparison and the $185.6M tax outflow), the November 1, 2026 convert maturity, and any 8-K disclosing a transaction from the strategic review. The September options tenor prices a ±13.3% move, so the market expects resolution of something within that window.

## Conclusion

The prior AVOID rating is no longer supportable. Ziff Davis executed a well-priced divestiture at approximately 5.2x revenue, received $1,134.1 million net cash, holds $548.2 million of net cash after the associated tax bill, is comfortably covenant-compliant with an undrawn revolver, and has retired 8.9% of its shares in six months at prices below the current quote. Those are real accomplishments and they eliminate the financing and solvency concerns that anchored the earlier view.

What replaces those concerns is a valuation question the market has already answered optimistically. The operating business is priced at roughly 9x unlevered post-tax free cash flow, embedding terminal decline near −1% against current organic decline of −5.5% with 340 basis points of annual margin compression, advertising net revenue retention below 100% in all three ad segments, and $196.3 million of goodwill at a zero-cushion impairment threshold.
