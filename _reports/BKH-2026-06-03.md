**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Black Hills Corp is a regulated, vertically integrated multi-utility serving roughly 227,000 electric and 1,138,000 gas customers across an eight-state footprint in the Rocky Mountain and Midwest regions. For the next twelve months, the standalone utility analysis matters less than two structural developments: the pending all-stock merger with NorthWestern Energy (announced August 2025, target close in 2H 2026), and a disproportionately large data-center load-growth pipeline in Wyoming relative to the company's size. Both shareholder bases approved the merger in April 2026, the HSR antitrust waiting period was satisfied, and state settlements are falling into place (Nebraska approved, Montana and South Dakota settlements reached). The primary remaining gate is FERC, with a decision expected mid-2026.

The fundamentals underneath are typical for a rate-base-heavy regulated utility: FY2025 net income to common of $291.6M (+6.8% YoY), diluted EPS of $3.98, ROE of ~7.8%, and combined utility margin of $1,493.7M driven primarily by new rates and rider recovery. Operating cash flow of $673.4M against $819.8M of capex produced negative free cash flow of -$146.4M, which is expected during a heavy build cycle (~$4.7B five-year plan) and not a distress signal. The 55th consecutive annual dividend increase, BBB+/Baa2 stable ratings affirmed after the merger announcement, and ~$929.6M of liquidity anchor the credit profile despite a 55.1% debt-to-cap ratio that triggers misleading screener flags.

We rate BKH a HOLD (growth_hold). The investment case rests on real but priced-in growth: a >3 GW data-center pipeline (including a new 1.8 GW Wyoming contract with $201M of refundable customer cash already posted) feeds future rate-base expansion, and the NorthWestern combination creates a larger, more diversified entity with greater capacity to fund capex. At ~16.6x forward EPS the stock trades at a discount to the ~21x peer median, but that discount reflects genuine merger-integration, dilution, and regulatory-conditioning risk rather than a clear mispricing. The risk/reward is balanced: neither the standalone valuation nor the merger arbitrage offers asymmetric upside at the current ~$72 level. We see the stock as a hold for income and growth optionality, to be revisited on FERC clarity and merger close.

## Company Overview

Black Hills Corp (incorporated 1941, Rapid City, South Dakota) operates two regulated segments:

- **Electric Utilities** (~227,000 customers) in Colorado, Montana, South Dakota, and Wyoming, with 1,386 MW of owned generation and ~9,478 miles of transmission and distribution lines. The generation mix is shifting from coal (25.5% of supply in 2025 vs. 35.0% in 2023) toward natural gas (29%) and purchased renewables. The company owns its own Powder River Basin coal mine (WRDC), which supplies adjacent plants at roughly $1.26/MMBtu with about 51 years of reserves, a structural cost advantage at those plants.
- **Gas Utilities** (~1,138,000 customers) across Arkansas, Colorado, Iowa, Kansas, Nebraska, and Wyoming, with ~4,581 miles of intrastate transmission pipe, ~44,840 miles of distribution mains and service lines, and seven storage sites.

The business is a classic regulated-monopoly model. Earnings are set by cost-of-service / rate-of-return regulation across eight state commissions plus FERC. Extensive cost-recovery mechanisms (fuel and gas cost adjustments, integrity riders, and weather normalization in several states) reduce regulatory lag and protect margins. The moat is the regulated franchise itself. What differentiates BKH from similarly sized peers is the scale of emerging large-load demand in Wyoming relative to a company whose electric peak runs around 400 MW per business unit.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** FY2025 revenue was $2.31B, up 8.6% from $2.13B in 2024, though the reported top line is heavily distorted by pass-through gas commodity costs. Combined utility margin (non-GAAP) is the cleaner gauge: it grew to $1,493.7M ($683.2M electric + $810.5M gas) from $1,414.8M in 2024, driven by new rates and rider recovery (+$25M electric, +$60.9M gas). Net income to common rose 6.8% to $291.6M, and diluted EPS reached $3.98, up only modestly from $3.91 in both 2024 and 2023; the gap between net-income growth and per-share growth reflects share-count dilution from continuous ATM equity issuance (diluted shares rose from 67.1M to 69.9M to 73.2M across 2023-2025).

**Segment dynamics.** The gas segment carried the year (+$49.5M operating income), while the electric segment declined ($10.5M) on unplanned coal-plant outages (Wygen III; coal availability fell to 77.7% from 89.8%), lower transmission revenue, and unfavorable weather. Operating income overall rose 6.8% to $537.5M.

**Margins and returns.** Operating margin ~23.3%, net margin ~13.0%, ROE ~7.8%, ROA ~2.8%, all typical for a capital-intensive regulated utility. The ~7.8% ROE sits below the authorized ROE levels embedded in pending rate cases (e.g., 10.5% requested in South Dakota), indicating room for earned-ROE improvement if rate cases land favorably and outages normalize.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow was $673.4M, down from $719.3M in 2024 and $944.4M in 2023, reflecting the wind-down of Winter Storm Uri regulatory-asset recovery and gas-storage working-capital effects. Against $819.8M of capex, free cash flow was -$146.4M. For a regulated utility funding a multi-year rate-base build, negative FCF is structural; the capital is being deployed into rate base that earns a regulated return. Long-term debt stood at $4.70B, debt-to-cap 55.1%, D/E ~2.84x. The Altman Z-Score of 1.11 (nominal "distress zone") is a false positive driven by the high-leverage, low-asset-turnover nature of regulated utilities; investment-grade ratings (S&P BBB+ / Moody's Baa2, both Stable, affirmed August 2025 post-announcement) and ~$929.6M of available liquidity (cash plus $750M revolver capacity) are the more relevant solvency markers.

**Capital allocation.** 2025 marked the 55th consecutive year of dividend increases, among the longest streaks in the industry. The FY25 dividend was $2.70/share; the 2026 annualized declaration is $2.812. The payout ratio rose to 68% in 2025 from 64% in 2023, now at the top of the 55-65% target, a watch item, though comfortably covered by stable regulated cash flows. The dividend funding is partly offset by ATM equity ($219M issued in 2025), which continues to dilute per-share growth.

**Q1 2026.** GAAP EPS was $1.73 / adjusted $1.79 versus $1.87 prior year, a slight miss against ~$1.86 consensus. The shortfall was almost entirely weather (a $0.18/share drag from a record-warm winter, with combined heating degree days 18-22% below normal) plus higher financing and depreciation, partly offset by new rates. Management reaffirmed 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $4.25-$4.45 (ex-merger costs) and continues to target the upper half of its 4-6% long-term EPS growth range.

**Earnings-surprise track record.** The recent record is weak: 1 beat in 4 quarters (Q3 2025 -14.8%, Q4 2025 -1.0%, Q1 2026 -3.7%, with the lone beat in Q2 2025 at +4.5%). The misses are largely weather-driven rather than structural, but they temper confidence in near-term consensus and argue for treating the guidance midpoint as the planning anchor rather than the upper end.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone growth is the regulated-utility default of 4-6% EPS, driven by rate-base expansion funded through the ~$4.7B five-year capex plan ($890M-$983M annually 2026-2030, weighted toward gas integrity work). What elevates the growth profile above the peer baseline is the data-center load pipeline in Wyoming.

The pipeline now exceeds 3 GW, of which roughly 600 MW by 2030 is already embedded in the five-year plan (driven by Microsoft expansion and Meta's new AI data center, served under Wyoming Electric's LPCS tariff). On April 22, 2026, Wyoming Electric signed an agreement with a prospective customer for a 1.8 GW data center; the customer has posted $201M in refundable contributions-in-aid-of-construction for long-lead generation equipment. That cash commitment validates intent and would be additive to current plans. Microsoft also announced intent to acquire 3,200 additional acres in Cheyenne for future expansion. Wyoming Electric set a new all-time peak of 393 MW in January 2026 (+4% YoY), marking 20 consecutive years of Cheyenne demand growth.

These are BKH utility customers buying regulated power, not BKH-owned data centers. The upside flows through rate-base growth and LPCS-tariff revenue, not merchant exposure, which lowers the risk profile of the growth while capping the magnitude. A >3 GW pipeline against an electric utility whose total peak runs in the hundreds of MW expands rate base materially if even a portion converts, and it gives a ~$5.5B-cap utility outsized rate-base optionality.

Estimate revisions are modestly constructive at the near-term level (current quarter +11.8% over 30 days, +11.0% over 90 days) but flat for the full year (current year -0.5%, next year -0.3%). The near-term upward revisions reflect rate-case timing rather than a structural change; the flat annual estimates indicate the Street already incorporates the standard growth path and the data-center embedded plan. This is not the >30% revision velocity that would signal an underestimated fundamental shift; it is steady utility growth, appropriately weighted as such.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$72 the stock trades at ~16.6x the 2026 adjusted EPS midpoint (~$4.34 consensus current-year estimate) and ~18.6x trailing EPS of $3.98. Against the Multi-Utilities peer set, BKH sits below the median P/E of 21.1x:

| NWE | 25.9 | 3.8% |
| AVA | 16.5 | 4.8% |
| OGE | 21.1 | 3.6% |
| PNW | 18.8 | 3.6% |
| **Median** | **21.1** | **3.6%** |
| **BKH** | **~16.6 (fwd)** | **3.9%** |

The discount to peers is real but explainable. Three factors weigh against a re-rate to the peer median: (1) the pending merger introduces integration, dilution, and regulatory-conditioning risk that the market discounts; (2) continuous ATM equity issuance dilutes per-share growth, so headline net-income growth overstates EPS growth; (3) the recent earnings-miss cadence and a payout ratio now at the top of the target range. The ~3.9% dividend yield, above the 3.6% peer median and backed by a 55-year increase streak, provides absolute support and is the most concrete valuation anchor.

The fixed 0.98 exchange ratio means BKH's price now partly tracks NorthWestern fundamentals, so a pure standalone DCF is incomplete. Analyst price targets (low $76, mean $83, median $86, high $87) sit 6-21% above the current price, but the analyst tally is thin (5 covering analysts) and sentiment is degrading, with one recent buy-to-hold downgrade. We do not treat the targets as floors here: BKH is a steady utility at a sub-30x multiple with flat annual estimates, where the targets likely embed merger-completion optimism. The valuation is fair rather than asymmetric at current levels, with the discount to peers offsetting the merger and dilution overhang.

## Competitive Landscape

BKH competes for capital, not customers, against regulated peers including NorthWestern, Avista, ALLETE, and other small/mid-cap utilities. The regulated franchise is a durable moat with limited retail competition. The differentiated angle is the Wyoming large-load growth, which gives BKH disproportionate rate-base optionality relative to its size in a period of AI/hyperscale buildout.

The NorthWestern merger improves competitive position structurally. The combined entity (~0.7M electric, ~1.5M gas customers) gains scale to fund capex and absorb regulatory and wildfire risk, with greater geographic diversification across the Midwest and Rocky Mountain regions. Sector tailwinds (electrification, data-center demand, favorable Western wildfire-liability legislation: Wyoming HB0192 effective July 2025, South Dakota effective July 2026, Montana protections) are offset by sector headwinds: higher-for-longer rates pressure rate-base-heavy models, and ongoing equity dilution to fund capex weighs on per-share metrics. Competitive trajectory is improving on the strength of the load-growth pipeline and post-merger scale, supporting a score above the regulated-monopoly baseline.

## Risk Assessment

The 10-K added an extensive new merger-specific risk-factor section, now the dominant risk cluster:

1. **Merger execution and regulatory risk (dominant).** The all-stock NorthWestern combination requires FERC, remaining state commission approvals, FCC consent, and integration. Conditions could be imposed; the deal could be delayed or repriced. A $100M termination fee and a force-the-vote provision limit alternatives. Integration failure, credit pressure, and management/employee attrition are live risks.

2. **Regulatory and rate risk.** Earnings depend entirely on timely, full cost recovery across eight states and FERC. Multiple large rate cases are pending, including South Dakota Electric's first review in over a decade ($51M SD + $5M WY requests at 10.5% ROE) and Arkansas Gas ($29M).

3. **Wildfire liability.** A growing risk for Western electric utilities, being mitigated via PSPS programs and wildfire mitigation plans. Recently enacted state liability protections (WY, MT, SD) are a favorable, BKH-specific development that partially offsets this.

4. **Data-center concentration risk.** Rapid large-load growth introduces forecasting and cost-recovery risk, plus customer-concentration exposure if a hyperscaler pulls back. The posted $201M contribution mitigates but does not eliminate this.

5. **Goodwill impairment.** ~$1.3B of goodwill on the books today, with substantial additional goodwill to be added in the merger (BKH is the accounting acquirer).

6. **Financing risk.** The heavy capex program depends on capital-market access; net interest expense rose to $206.9M in 2025 from $195.2M in 2024.

7. **Coal/environmental transition.** GHG rules and Colorado's Clean Energy Plan drive coal retirements and renewable capex; regulatory direction is shifting (EPA proposed Clean Power Plan 2.0 repeal in June 2025).

A leadership transition appears underway: the March 2026 proxy lists Marne M. Jones (previously SVP-Utilities) as CEO, while operational releases still cite Linn Evans. This is plausibly choreographed around the merger but adds near-term governance uncertainty pending confirmation of timing.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 29.3% against 1-year historical volatility of 20.4%: rich, with the spread reflecting event premium around the FERC decision and merger close. The term structure is in backwardation (29.3% near vs. 25.2% long), indicating the market is pricing a near-term binary catalyst. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (-2.4%) and the put/call OI ratio of 0.32 is call-tilted, so the elevated near-term IV reads as event hedging around the merger timeline rather than a directional bearish bet. IV has compressed from 36.2% to 29.0% over the past month as the price drifted down 2.8%, consistent with some uncertainty resolving. Separately, short interest is elevated at 15.9% of float with 10.3 days to cover, meaningful for a utility, and likely a mix of merger-arbitrage positioning (short BKH against long NorthWestern, or vice versa, given the fixed ratio) rather than a clean fundamental short. This elevated SI creates modest squeeze potential on a clean merger close but does not signal informed fundamental bearishness.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** FERC approves the merger on schedule in 2H 2026 with manageable conditions; the combined entity re-rates toward the peer median (~21x) as integration risk clears, and the data-center pipeline converts (the 1.8 GW Wyoming contract advances, additive to the embedded 600 MW), driving rate-base growth toward the upper end of the 4-6% EPS range. Favorable rate-case outcomes lift earned ROE from ~7.8% toward authorized levels. The stock works toward the $86-87 analyst high targets, roughly +20% from current, supported by the ~3.9% yield.

**Bear case.** FERC or remaining state commissions impose onerous conditions, delay, or block the merger, triggering a re-rating lower and potential credit pressure; goodwill from the deal becomes an impairment overhang; continued weather drags and the elevated 68% payout ratio combine with ATM dilution to keep EPS growth at the low end. Data-center commitments slip. The stock retraces toward the low-$60s (~-12 to -15%), near where it traded before the late-2025 run.

The base case is a stable, dividend-supported hold around current levels, with the merger closing on roughly the expected timeline and modest re-rating offset by dilution and integration costs.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** Reassessment timeline is the FERC decision (expected mid-2026) and the merger close (target 2H 2026), with the next earnings print as an interim checkpoint.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- FERC approval with no material adverse conditions, plus a clear post-merger EPS-accretion and rate-base trajectory that supports re-rating toward the ~21x peer median.
- The 1.8 GW Wyoming data-center contract advancing to firm rate-base inclusion, lifting the embedded growth plan beyond 600 MW by 2030.
- Earned ROE inflecting toward authorized levels (toward 9-10%) on favorable rate-case outcomes, with the payout ratio stabilizing below 65%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FERC or a remaining state commission imposing conditions that materially impair the deal economics, or the merger being delayed beyond 2026 / repriced.
- A dividend coverage deterioration (payout ratio sustained above 70% without offsetting earnings growth), or a credit-ratings outlook revision to negative.
- Data-center commitments unwinding (e.g., the $201M refundable contribution returned, hyperscaler pullback).

**Reassessment point:** FERC ruling and the next quarterly earnings release. The stock has appreciated ~23% over the trailing year and now sits ~8% below its 52-week high of $78.69; the recent ~$72 level reflects merger-timeline uncertainty and a weather-driven Q1 miss rather than fundamental deterioration, so we read the pullback as neutral rather than a deteriorating thesis.

## Conclusion

Black Hills is a fundamentally sound regulated multi-utility whose next twelve months are governed by the NorthWestern merger and the Wyoming data-center load story. Both are real and largely de-risked relative to a year ago: shareholders approved, HSR cleared, state settlements progressing, and a 1.8 GW data-center contract backed by $201M of customer cash. The credit profile is solid investment grade, the dividend has risen for 55 consecutive years, and the standalone EPS path is a dependable 4-6%.

The case for restraint is valuation and balance of risk. At ~16.6x forward EPS the discount to the ~21x peer median is explained by merger-integration, dilution, and regulatory-conditioning risk rather than a clear mispricing. The earnings cadence has been weak (3 misses in 4 quarters, weather-driven), the payout ratio sits at the top of its target range, and continuous ATM issuance dilutes per-share growth. Analyst targets imply 6-21% upside, but coverage is thin and sentiment is degrading. The merger's fixed exchange ratio also ties BKH's value to NorthWestern, making the standalone thesis incomplete. We rate BKH a HOLD for income and growth optionality, with FERC clarity and merger close as the natural points to revisit a more constructive stance.
