## Executive Summary

Fox Factory is a levered residual claim on a profitability recovery that management has guided to but not yet demonstrated. Net debt of roughly $641M sits against FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $168.4M, a figure that has fallen 47.7% from the FY2022 peak of $321.8M on essentially flat revenue, with Adjusted EBITDA margin down about 860 basis points to 11.5%. The FY2025 net loss of $544.7M is dominated by $557.3M of goodwill impairment plus $13.5M of other asset write-offs, but stripping those out does not reveal a healthy earner: ex-impairment pretax income was approximately negative $5.5M, because adjusted operating income of $47.9M failed to cover $53.7M of interest expense (EBIT/interest of 0.89x). The interest burden is the residue of the debt-funded November 2023 Marucci acquisition, and it is now the binding constraint on capital allocation.

The forward case rests on arithmetic that deserves scrutiny. Management reaffirmed FY2026 net sales of $1.328–1.416B and Adjusted EBITDA of $174–203M. Q1 delivered $35.7M of Adjusted EBITDA (9.7% margin) and Q2 is guided to $32–40M. That puts first-half Adjusted EBITDA near $72M and requires roughly $117M in the second half at the guidance midpoint, an implied H2 margin near 18% against 9.9% in H1, and above anything the company has produced since FY2022. The bridge is identifiable: roughly $40M from Phase 2 of a ~$50M cost program, plus the non-repeat of a ~$15M first-half incremental tariff headwind. Both are plausible; neither is verified, and revenue is guided down 6.5% at the midpoint. Consensus for the June quarter was cut 48.2% over ninety days (to $0.18 from $0.34), while next-year estimates rose 7.5% to $1.74. The market is marking down the near term while holding the recovery.

On multiples the stock screens inexpensive: 14.0x current-year and 11.2x next-year consensus adjusted EPS versus a 24.1x peer median, 7.8x EV/EBITDA on guidance midpoint, 1.25x book. The discount is explained rather than mispriced. Leverage of 3.8x against a covenant schedule stepping from 5.00x to 4.00x by June 2028 makes deleveraging contractually mandatory, which raises the probability of a portfolio divestiture but also means free cash flow is unavailable to shareholders. A $250M repurchase authorization is effectively frozen during the Covenant Relief Period. With a Q2 report days away, near-term implied volatility at 104.7% against 55% realized, and a defensible bear case near $11, we maintain HOLD at a materially lower conviction than the multiple alone would suggest.

## Company Overview

Fox Factory engineers and manufactures premium ride-dynamics and performance products across three segments, plus a branded sporting-goods business that shares no operating overlap with the rest.

**Powered Vehicles Group (PVG)** — FY2025 net sales $488.1M (+5.8%), segment Adjusted EBITDA $62.3M (12.8%). Suspension for side-by-sides, off-road trucks, ATVs, snowmobiles and commercial vehicles under FOX, plus Marzocchi for motorcycles. OEM relationships include BRP, Ford, Polaris, Toyota, Kawasaki, Yamaha, Stellantis and Honda.

**Aftermarket Applications Group (AAG)** — FY2025 net sales $470.0M (+11.5%), Adjusted EBITDA $55.8M (11.9%). Lift kits, wheels and truck upfitting (BDS, Zone Offroad, JKS, Ridetech, Tuscany, SCA, Method Race Wheels). Upfitting generally excludes the chassis, which the automotive dealer buys from the OEM, but the business depends on OEM chassis allocation and carries $70.6M of prepaid Stellantis chassis deposits.

**Specialty Sports Group (SSG)** — FY2025 net sales $509.2M (−0.4%), Adjusted EBITDA $107.6M (21.1%). Bike suspension and components (FOX, Race Face, Easton Cycling) sold to Specialized, Trek, Giant, Canyon and others — approximately 22% of consolidated sales — plus diamond sports (Marucci, Victus, Lizard Skins, Baum Bat), roughly 13% of sales.

Channel mix in FY2025 was 44% OEM, 49% dealer/distributor, 7% direct. Geography was 76% North America. Approximately 3,700 employees, 270 OEM relationships, more than 9,600 dealers and distributors.

One structural feature matters for forecasting: revenue is recognized substantially on shipment against purchase orders, and the company discloses no backlog, deferred revenue balance or remaining performance obligation. Forward visibility depends entirely on management guidance and channel-inventory commentary, a lower-quality evidence base than most industrial suppliers offer.

Revenue recovered 5.3% in FY2025 to roughly FY2023 levels; earnings did not follow. Three drivers are identifiable in the filings: gross margin down 300bps from FY2022 on tariffs, input costs and mix; operating expense growth outpacing sales (R&D from $56.2M to $69.4M, sales and marketing to $132.1M); and interest expense up sixfold.

### Free cash flow is the sharper concern

Free cash flow (operating cash flow less capex) fell from $131.8M in FY2023 to $87.8M in FY2024 to $26.9M in FY2025 — a 1.8% FCF margin, an ~80% decline over two years on flat revenue. Q1 FY2026 operating cash flow was negative $16.1M and FCF negative $21.5M, against negative $6.5M a year earlier.

We do not annualize that. Q1 is a working-capital build quarter: receivables rose $23.1M, inventory $7.9M, accrued expenses fell $7.0M, and capex of $5.4M ran below the company's ~2%-of-sales intent. As a labelled sensitivity rather than a forecast: if the $188.5M Adjusted EBITDA midpoint is achieved, deducting roughly $46M cash interest, $6–8M cash taxes, $27M capex and roughly $19M of cash restructuring/transformation costs leaves pre-working-capital FCF near $88M, or a ~10–11% yield on market cap. Working-capital swings of ±$30M have been routine, and FY2025 actual FCF was $26.9M, so the honest range is wide and the upper end is guidance-derived, not observed.

Other expense included a $9.993M loss on the Phoenix divestiture. GAAP net loss $15.018M, or −$0.36 per share; adjusted EPS $0.18, which beat the $0.08 consensus by 115%.

### Balance sheet and covenants — the center of the story

At April 3, 2026: total assets $1,661.8M, total liabilities $1,003.2M, equity $658.8M, down from $1,201.2M fifteen months earlier as retained earnings fell from $875.4M to $315.8M. Term loan principal $519.062M plus $176.0M drawn on a $500M revolver ($323.7M available), cash $53.9M, weighted-average rate 6.04%. Gross principal debt $695.1M; net debt approximately $641M on a principal basis, 78% of market capitalization.

The October 2025 Fifth Amendment extended maturity from April 2027 to October 2030 and back-loaded amortization ($20.2M remaining in FY2026, $26.9M annually FY2027–29, a $418.3M balloon in 2030). That removed a genuine near-term refinancing wall.

The May 2026 Sixth Amendment is the pivotal disclosure. Lenders raised the SOFR margin ceiling from 2.50% to 2.75%, redefined the Consolidated Net Leverage Ratio, modified asset-sale prepayment provisions, and tightened negative covenants on indebtedness, investments and restricted payments during a Covenant Relief Period running to the Q2 2028 compliance certificate. Maximum leverage is 5.00x through January 2027, stepping to 4.75x, 4.50x, 4.25x and 4.00x from June 30, 2028; minimum interest coverage is 2.50x. The customary +0.50x acquisition step-up is unavailable during the relief period. The company was compliant at April 3, 2026 at management-reported leverage of ~3.77x (our mechanical TTM calculation is 3.86x; the gap is likely definitional).

The near-term headroom is real: at constant net debt of ~$634M, the 5.00x test binds only if covenant EBITDA falls to about $127M, roughly 27% below the low end of FY2026 guidance. The step-downs are the constraint. At 4.00x from mid-2028, constant net debt implies a binding EBITDA of about $158.5M, only 6% below FY2025 actual. The credit agreement does not tolerate two more years at the FY2024–25 run rate; it requires either EBITDA recovery or debt reduction. A practical consequence: $250M remains of a $300M repurchase authorization (roughly 30% of market cap), no shares were repurchased in Q1 of either year, and restricted-payment covenants are tightened through mid-2028. Buybacks should not be modelled as support.

Two hedging items: $500M of variable exposure is swapped, leaving roughly $181M floating (100bp ≈ $1.8M of interest), and the largest tranche — $200M — expires December 25, 2026, after which hedged notional falls toward $300M unless replaced.

## Growth Analysis

Guidance points down on revenue and up on profit. The FY2026 midpoint of $1.372B is 6.5% below FY2025's $1,467.3M, while the $188.5M Adjusted EBITDA midpoint is 12% above FY2025 — roughly 220bps of margin expansion. The revenue contribution of the divested Phoenix businesses is not disclosed, so organic and portfolio effects cannot be separated.

The decisive question is the second-half shape. First-half Adjusted EBITDA of approximately $71.7M (Q1 $35.7M plus Q2 guidance midpoint $36M) implies second-half Adjusted EBITDA near $116.8M on implied second-half revenue of roughly $649M — an 18.0% margin against 9.9% in the first half. The identifiable bridge is roughly $40M from Phase 2 of the cost program (a mid-single-digit-million benefit was realized in Q1) plus the non-repeat of the ~$15M incremental first-half tariff headwind, which sums to slightly more than the required gap. The guide is arithmetically achievable, not fanciful. It is also entirely dependent on two items outside demonstrated results, in a business with no backlog disclosure.

Segment trajectories are out of phase. PVG at +17.4% with 390bps of margin expansion is a real cyclical upturn in powersports and off-road aftermarket. SSG bike remains in a post-COVID destocking hangover that management has now described in three consecutive filings; a −8.7% revenue quarter with 350bps of margin loss in the highest-margin segment is the single most damaging line in the quarter. AAG grew 2.6% but lost 530bps of margin on an industry-wide aluminum supply disruption that constrained F-150 Lariat and XLT chassis availability; management stated the lost first-half volume will not be recovered later in 2026, making it a permanent rather than deferred shortfall. Distribution expansion is a modest offset — more than 135 new dealers added in roughly sixty days.

Estimate revisions are mixed rather than confirming. The June-quarter estimate was cut 48.2% over ninety days to $0.18; the September quarter was raised 13.6% to $0.53; current year is flat at $1.40 and next year up 7.5% to $1.74. There is no +30% ninety-day revision signal here; the pattern is a near-term markdown against a preserved recovery, which is what the sell side would show if it believes the cost program but not the timing.

R&D of $69.4M (4.7% of sales) sustains the product pipeline, and Marzocchi launches deferred from Q2 into Q3 are a timing rather than demand issue. But the growth score has to reflect a guided revenue decline, permanent volume loss in AAG, and a bike cycle whose recovery has repeatedly been pushed out.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is brand and OEM design-in position rather than scale or cost. FOX is a spec'd brand on premium mountain bikes and side-by-sides, OEM programs are multi-year and engineering-integrated, and the company holds relationships with about 270 OEMs. Gross margin down 300bps over three years suggests that pricing power has been insufficient to offset tariffs and mix, which is the relevant trajectory test.

By segment: in bike suspension, RockShox (SRAM) competes with a broader component portfolio spanning drivetrains, brakes and wheels, a structural disadvantage for FOXF in bundled OEM negotiations. In powered vehicles the field is fragmented (Bilstein, Öhlins, KYB, Rancho, King, Icon) and the FOX brand premium is most defensible. In lift kits and wheels, Rough Country, TeraFlex, ReadyLIFT and Wheel Pros compete on price. In diamond sports the competitors are Easton/Rawlings and Wilson's DeMarini and Louisville Slugger.

Two specific dependencies: Miyaki is the exclusive producer of Kashima coating, a signature FOX finish; and the majority of bike-component manufacturing sits in Taiwan, where CBP issued a September 2025 Withhold Release Order against Giant Manufacturing on forced-labor suspicion. Giant is an OEM customer of FOXF rather than a supplier, so the exposure is enforcement spillover onto FOXF's own Taiwan operations plus potential demand disruption at a customer, not an input-supply failure.

Customer concentration has not improved. Ford was 12% of FY2025 sales (15% in FY2024), top five 26% (28%), top ten 35% (36%). Segment revenue mix rotated meaningfully in Q1, but the top-ten measure — the one that speaks to counterparty diversification — is flat.

The AAG chassis mechanics deserve emphasis as a competitive weakness: for GM and Ford, chassis are held under bailment with floor-plan financing (utilization $15.4M of a $38.3M Ford allocation, $12.3M of $49.5M for GM), while Stellantis requires refundable deposits totalling $70.6M. The upfitting business therefore depends on OEM allocation decisions Fox Factory does not control, which is exactly the mechanism behind the Q1 aluminum shortfall.

Net trajectory: one segment improving, two deteriorating, with pricing power insufficient to hold gross margin.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage against a halved earnings base.** Net debt of ~$641M against Adjusted EBITDA of $168.4M, with maximum permitted leverage stepping to 4.00x by mid-2028 — binding at approximately $158.5M of EBITDA at constant debt. This is the constraint on buybacks, M&A and the pace of any portfolio action.

**Distress-screen signal, handled carefully.** The screening Altman Z-Score of ~1.16 sits in the distress zone, but the impairment drives three of the five inputs. Adding back $570.8M of impairments to EBIT raises the 3.3 × EBIT/assets term by about 1.13, producing an adjusted Z near 2.3 — the grey zone, above the 1.81 distress line and well below the 2.99 safe threshold. The retained-earnings term is permanently impaired, so future readings stay structurally depressed regardless of operating recovery.

**Tariff cliff with an unknown post-expiry regime.** After the February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA does not authorize the tariffs, a February 24, 2026 proclamation imposed a 10% global tariff (to rise to 15%) under Section 122 of the Trade Act, which by statute lasts 150 days absent Congressional extension — approximately July 24, 2026. Section 232 steel and aluminum tariffs were raised to 50% ex-UK with derivatives included. Management secured a move from IEEPA to Section 232 treatment, taxing aluminum input value rather than full FOB price, but still guided to roughly $15M of incremental net tariff impact in H1 2026. The regime beyond July is unresolved and cuts both ways.

**Tariff refunds are two-sided and unquantified.** CBP has implemented a refund process. Because FOXF records tariff surcharges billed to customers as revenue, a government refund may carry offsetting "contractual obligations arising from such refunds." Net economics are unquantified and all refund benefit is excluded from guidance.

**A third impairment is live.** Remaining goodwill of $83.575M sits entirely in SSG, the segment declining fastest. The 10-K discloses that, assuming 100% weighting to the income approach, a 1.5-point increase in WACC would fully impair it. That would be non-cash and would not affect covenant EBITDA, but would cut book equity by up to 12.7%. The FY2025 charges were triggered substantially by the company's own share-price decline; at $19.565 versus the ~$13 low referenced in the 10-K, that specific trigger has partially reversed.

**Deferred tax asset write-down risk.** DTAs of $86.7M equal 13% of equity, including roughly $45.4M of foreign tax credits beginning to expire in 2028, with disclosure that a material valuation-allowance adjustment is "reasonably possible" within twelve months. A full FTC write-down would be a non-cash charge equal to roughly 5.5% of market cap.

**Litigation has cleared.** The securities class action alleging misstatements about demand and inventory (class period May 2021–November 2023) was dismissed with prejudice on February 10, 2026, judgment entered February 12, and no appeal was filed. Two derivative complaints were voluntarily dismissed without prejudice (granted May 1, 2026), technically leaving refiling open.

**Warranty and hedging watch items.** The Q1 warranty charge to cost of sales was $4.046M versus $1.468M a year earlier, while the ending liability fell to $13.881M from $19.113M as costs incurred exceeded the charge. The charge is a flow and the liability a balance; the nearly 3x increase could signal emerging product-quality cost or an abnormally low base quarter. The $200M swap tranche expiring December 25, 2026 increases floating exposure unless replaced.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 104.7% against 55% one-year realized (a 49.7-point spread, 76th percentile within the chain) and a backwardated term structure (104.7% at one month falling to 70.5% at six months) price imminent event stress. The nearest expiry (August 21, fifteen days) implies a ±17.8% move, consistent with a Q2 report not yet released as of this cutoff. Put/call open interest of 0.07 is extremely call-tilted, and the source characterizes the near-term skew (−8.7%) as call-heavy, while the three- and six-month tenors show the opposite sign (+10.7% and +10.6%). We do not infer direction from the sign convention beyond what the source labels; the readable content is that near-term positioning is concentrated in calls while longer-dated structure prices something different.

The practical implications: optionality is expensive, so expressing either side through short-dated options is unattractive; the market expects a binary resolution within two weeks; and short interest of 11.5% of float with 5.3 days to cover creates squeeze risk on a positive print or a strategic announcement. For a fundamental holder, this argues for waiting for the print rather than adding into it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($28, +43%)

Second-half execution delivers. Phase 2 of the ~$50M cost program contributes roughly $40M, the ~$15M first-half tariff headwind does not repeat, Marzocchi launches deferred into Q3 land, and PVG's +17.4% momentum continues, carrying FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA to the $200M high end. Leverage falls toward 3.2x, comfortably inside the step-down schedule, and the market re-rates from 7.8x to 9.0x EV/EBITDA. Separately or additionally, the board mandate produces a divestiture — diamond sports being the cleanest candidate given zero operating overlap — with proceeds applied to debt, converting a levered cyclical into a simpler, less-leveraged suspension business. At 11.2x next-year consensus of $1.74 the equity is priced for none of this, so re-rating leverage is substantial once the second-half margin is demonstrated rather than promised.

### Bear Case ($11, −44%)

The second-half ramp does not arrive. Bike destocking extends into a fourth consecutive quarter, the aluminum-driven AAG volume loss proves broader than the F-150 platform, the post-July tariff regime turns adverse, and FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA lands near $150M. Leverage rises toward 4.2x against a 4.75x test from early 2027, forcing a further amendment at higher pricing or a distressed asset sale executed on Phoenix-like terms — $5.0M of cash plus a $22.375M seller note for a business carried near $37.4M, with the head lease retained. SSG goodwill is written off, the FTC valuation allowance is taken, and book value compresses toward tangible book of $4.49. With net debt at 78% of market cap, the equity absorbs the full multiple compression.

### What tips the balance

The evidence that most constrains the bull case is the second-half margin requirement: 18.0% implied against 9.9% delivered in Q1 and 11.5% for all of FY2025. The evidence that most constrains the bear case is the near-term covenant headroom — roughly 1.1 turns, with the 5.00x test binding only if EBITDA falls about 27% below the low end of guidance — plus a 2030 maturity and $323.7M of undrawn revolver. Neither side is close to resolved, which is why this is a HOLD rather than a directional call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (hold rationale: uncertain).** There is no dividend, so this is not an income hold, and we cannot identify a growth thesis with sufficient confidence to call it a growth hold. Existing holders should carry the position through the imminent Q2 report; new capital should wait.

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. Q2 results at or above the $40M Adjusted EBITDA guidance high end **with** FY2026 guidance reaffirmed and specific quantification of realized Phase 2 cost savings. The bull case requires H2 EBITDA near $117M; evidence that Q3 is tracking above $50M would validate the ramp.
2. A definitive agreement to divest a segment with disclosed cash proceeds applied to debt sufficient to bring net leverage below 3.0x. Structure matters: cash consideration, not a seller note with retained lease liability.
3. A price near $14–15 with FY2026 guidance intact, which would put the stock near 8.0x next-year consensus adjusted EPS and roughly 6.8x guidance-midpoint EV/EBITDA — a level at which the bear case is substantially pre-funded.
4. Clustered open-market insider purchases. The current record shows only grants and tax-withholding dispositions; no open-market buying at $17–18 by a management team publicly reaffirming a margin-recovery plan.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance cut below the $174M low end, or Q2 Adjusted EBITDA below $32M. Either would push implied FY leverage toward 4.0x against the 4.75x test effective from early 2027.
2. Bike (SSG) revenue declining more than 5% for a second consecutive quarter, which would both extend the destocking narrative and raise the probability of the SSG goodwill write-off the WACC sensitivity flags.
3. Any covenant amendment beyond the May 2026 Sixth Amendment, or disclosure of a leverage ratio above 4.25x.
4. An adverse post-July tariff outcome quantified at more than $15M for the second half, which would consume roughly one-third of the required H2 EBITDA improvement.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q2 FY2026 report, expected within days of this cutoff (the August 21 option expiry implies a ±17.8% move). Secondary checkpoints are the resolution of the Section 122 tariff regime, the Q3 report for evidence of the second-half ramp, and December 25, 2026, when the $200M interest-rate swap expires.

## Conclusion

Fox Factory is inexpensive on every earnings-based multiple and expensive on the only metric that matters when net debt equals 78% of market capitalization: the probability that FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance is met. The second-half margin requirement of roughly 18%, against 9.9% delivered in Q1 and 11.5% for all of FY2025, is the load-bearing assumption. It is bridgeable — approximately $40M from Phase 2 cost savings plus approximately $15M from the non-repeat of the first-half tariff headwind roughly covers the gap — but neither element has been demonstrated, revenue is guided down 6.5%, and the business discloses no backlog against which to check the assumption.

The covenant structure has become the primary governance mechanism. Near-term headroom of roughly 1.1 turns is genuine, and the 2030 maturity removes refinancing risk from the immediate picture. The step-down to 4.00x by mid-2028, binding at approximately $158.5M of EBITDA at constant debt, makes deleveraging mandatory and raises the probability of a real divestiture. The Phoenix transaction shows what a hurried divestiture looks like at this company: $5.0M of cash, a $22.375M seller note from a buyer group including a departing employee, a $9.993M loss, a warrant valued at zero, and a retained head lease. A larger disposal executed on comparable terms would generate limited debt reduction.

We maintain HOLD with low conviction, a base case of $22 (+12%), a bull case of $28 (+43%) and a bear case of $11 (−44%). The distribution is wide and slightly negatively skewed at the current price. Existing holders should carry the position through the imminent Q2 print, which the options market prices as a ±17.8% event. New capital should wait for either the second-half margin to appear in reported results or a price near $14–15, where the bear case would be substantially pre-funded.
