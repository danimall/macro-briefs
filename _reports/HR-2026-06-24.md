**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Healthcare Realty Trust is the largest pure-play owner, operator, and developer of medical outpatient buildings (MOBs) in the United States, with roughly $10.3 billion of gross investment across 502 consolidated properties and interests in 61–64 unconsolidated JV assets, totaling ~29 million square feet across 27 states. The company is the product of the 2022 merger of legacy Healthcare Realty with Healthcare Trust of America, and the present situation is best understood as a post-merger rationalization run by a substantially reconstituted, activist-influenced leadership team. The playbook is explicit: sell non-core assets at ~6.7% cap rates, repay debt, and repurchase stock the company believes trades below NAV.

The headline numbers screen poorly and obscure the operating reality. Consolidated rental income fell 7.7% in FY2025 and 7.4% in Q1 2026, and the company has posted three straight years of GAAP net losses (-$246M in 2025, -$654M in 2024, -$278M in 2023). Both effects are largely artifacts: the revenue decline is the result of deliberate dispositions, and the losses are dominated by impairments, the $250.5M goodwill write-off in Q1 2024, and merger-related depreciation, not operating cash burn. Operating cash flow remained solidly positive at $457M in FY2025. The metric that matters for a REIT — same-store cash NOI — accelerated to +5.0% in FY2025 and +7.1% in Q1 2026, indicating the underlying core is healthier than the consolidated income statement suggests.

We rate HR a HOLD with an income-hold rationale. The score has improved across two prior reads (4.5 in April, 5.6 in May, AVOID → HOLD), and the current data confirms that trajectory rather than reversing it: same-store NOI is accelerating, the dividend was reset to a level now covered on a FAD basis, and a director made a genuine open-market purchase. What keeps this a hold rather than a buy is that the durable return here is the ~4.85% yield plus deleveraging-driven NAV stability, not per-share earnings growth. Normalized FFO per share is essentially flat ($1.61 in 2025 vs $1.57 in 2023), and the $1.3B of 2026–2027 maturities repricing higher is a structural FFO headwind that caps the appreciation case.

## Company Overview

HR is a self-managed healthcare REIT focused on outpatient real estate. Approximately 93% of the portfolio is medical office/outpatient, with the strategic core being buildings on or adjacent to acute-care hospital campuses affiliated with leading health systems — roughly 72% of 2026 lease expirations are on or near hospital campuses. The tenancy is diversified across 30+ physician specialties, with no single tenant exceeding 10% of revenue, and the company self-manages ~92–93% of its portfolio through an internal management and leasing platform of roughly 539 employees. JV and managed-property fee income is a growing ancillary line (+47% in 2025).

The single common equity class outstanding (~346.5M shares as of 3/31/2026) is labeled "Class A" only because it is the company's sole common-stock class; there is no dual-class structure or governance overhang from that nomenclature.

The structural backdrop is favorable. The secular shift of care to outpatient settings, aging demographics, and the fact that on-campus MOBs benefit from site-neutral Medicare dynamics (hospitals retain higher reimbursement on-campus, supporting demand for hospital-adjacent buildings) underpin steady demand for HR's asset class. HR notes no material site-neutral impact to date.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and NOI.** FY2025 rental income fell 7.7% (-$94.7M) to roughly $1.13B, with disposition impact of -$130.8M partly offset by +$28.3M of leasing and contractual increases and +$7.8M from developments. Q1 2026 rental income was $267.6M, down 7.4% YoY on the same dynamic (dispositions -$32.8M, leasing +$10.9M). Stripping out the intentional asset sales, same-store cash NOI grew +5.0% in FY2025 ($601.9M vs $573.1M ex-JV) and +7.1% in Q1 2026 ($153.6M vs $143.5M). A standardized revenue screen misses this operating signal.

**Profitability.** Net loss attributable to common was -$246M in 2025, -$654M in 2024, and -$278M in 2023. The losses are driven by $361M of impairments in 2025, $250M in 2024, and the full $250.5M HTA-merger goodwill write-off in Q1 2024, plus merger-related D&A. Q1 2026 was roughly breakeven (net income of $21K) as impairments subsided. The trailing P/E of -34.8x is meaningless for this business; FFO and FAD are the correct lenses.

**FFO/FAD.** Normalized FFO per share was $1.61 (2025), $1.56 (2024), and $1.57 (2023) — flat-to-declining over three years, reflecting deleveraging drag and a shrinking asset base. Q1 2026 Normalized FFO per share was $0.41 (vs $0.39). At the current $20.30 price, annualizing the Q1 run-rate ($0.41 x 4 = $1.64) implies roughly 12.4x FFO. FAD was $448M in 2025 vs $419M in 2024.

**Dividend.** The quarterly dividend was cut from $0.31 to $0.24 between Q1 2025 and Q1 2026, a ~23% reduction. This was a material negative event, and the Q1 2026 10-Q states that the quarter's dividends were not fully covered by operating cash flow and were partly funded by the revolver. At the reset level, annualized $0.96 against FAD/share of ~$1.27 implies coverage with cushion, and management projects full-year 2026 coverage. Whether full-year operating cash flow actually covers the payout is the key monitoring item.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt is ~$4.1B. The incurrence covenant debt ratio is 35.8% (limit 60%) and debt service coverage is 3.5x (minimum 1.5x), so the company sits comfortably inside its covenants. Weighted average debt cost is ~4.5% with a weighted ~3.2 years to maturity; 72.5% of principal is due after 2027. Liquidity is adequate: ~$1.2B available on the $1.5B revolver (net of commercial paper), a new $600M commercial paper program (Feb 2026, $251M drawn at quarter-end at ~4.20%), and a renewed $1.0B ATM.

**The refinancing wall.** $1.3B is due in 2026–2027, including $600M of 3.50% senior notes maturing August 2026 that will refinance at materially higher rates (~5%). Management terminated $400M of 2026–2027 swaps and entered $400M of new swaps at 3.32% maturing January 2029 to lock in lower rates further out, but the repricing of low-coupon maturing debt is a structural FFO headwind for the next two years. In the current higher-for-longer rate environment, this is the single most important constraint on the per-share case.

**Capital allocation.** In 2025 the company sold 70 properties for ~$1.1B at a 6.7% cap rate with zero acquisitions, using proceeds to repay term loans and senior notes and fund buybacks. In Q1 2026 it repurchased 5.7M shares for ~$99.9M at ~$17.38 under a $500M authorization (October 2025, $400M remaining). Buying stock at ~$17.38 while disposing of assets at a 6.7% cap rate is a coherent value-arbitrage strategy if management's NAV view is correct.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story here is bifurcated. The operating core is genuinely improving: same-store cash NOI accelerating from +5.0% to +7.1% is a strong print for an MOB REIT, and tenant demand for hospital-adjacent outpatient space remains supported by secular care-setting shifts and aging demographics. The primary internal growth lever is occupancy. At ~90.4% portfolio occupancy (same-store 92.1%, tenant retention 77–80%), HR lags best-in-class MOB peers in the mid-90s, which means there is meaningful embedded NOI upside if new management can lease up the gap.

The countervailing forces are real. Normalized FFO per share has been flat for three years because the deleveraging program shrinks the asset base and the refinancing of low-coupon debt erodes the bottom line. The cost of maintaining occupancy is also rising: leasing commissions jumped to 7.5% of cash NOI in 2025 from 4.3% in 2023, with rent abatements and first-generation TIs climbing as well. The same-store NOI growth is therefore partly being purchased with higher concession spend.

Estimate revisions are mixed and reflect the dispositions/refinancing noise. Current-year EPS estimates were revised +53.3% over 90 days and next-year +33.3%, but these are revisions on small negative GAAP numbers (current year -$0.06) that are not the right metric for this business. Near-term quarterly estimates were cut (-37.0% on the current quarter, -65.6% on the next quarter over 90 days), again reflecting GAAP-EPS noise rather than FFO direction. The earnings surprise record is similarly noisy (two beats, two misses over four quarters, with surprise percentages distorted by tiny denominators). Net, the growth thesis is steady mid-single-digit same-store NOI with occupancy optionality, offset by refinancing drag: durable but unspectacular, and not the kind of acceleration that warrants a growth-driven buy.

## Valuation Assessment

HR trades at $20.30, near its 52-week high of $20.90 and up ~31% over the trailing year, against a 52-week low of $15.17. The dividend yield is ~4.85%, below the Health Care REIT peer median of 6.1%. On run-rate Normalized FFO of ~$1.64 annualized (Q1 $0.41 x 4), the stock trades at ~12.4x FFO, a reasonable but not cheap multiple for a low-growth, mid-leveraged MOB REIT.

Peer comparison is muddied by the sector's heterogeneity. The Health Care REIT peer set spans skilled-nursing and senior-housing names (CTRE at 23.4x P/E, SBRA at 29.3x, LTC at 14.5x) and the distressed MPT at a negative P/E. GAAP P/E is uninformative for any of these given REIT accounting, so the more relevant frame is yield and FFO multiple. On yield, HR's 4.85% sits below the peer median, partly justified by the lower-risk MOB asset class and the recently reset (better-covered) payout, but it does not offer obvious income upside relative to the group.

Analyst targets bracket the current price: low $18.00, mean $21.08, median $21.00, high $24.00. With the stock at $20.30, the consensus mean implies only ~4% upside, and the stock sits roughly at median. The "analyst targets are floors" principle does not apply here because HR is not an inflection stock with rising estimates at a sub-30x growth multiple — it is a flat-FFO turnaround near the top of its 12-month range, where targets reflect a balanced view rather than stale optimism.

The valuation case rests on a NAV argument: management is repurchasing stock at ~$17.38 and selling assets at 6.7% caps, implying it views the equity as below private-market value. That gap is real but partly closed by the ~31% rally over the past year. We do not assign a high valuation score because there is no asymmetric mispricing at $20.30: the cheapness is gone, the catalyst (deleveraging) is partly priced, and the multiple sits at a fair level for the growth and risk profile. A "cheap on NAV" thesis without a fresh catalyst warrants skepticism as potential value-trap framing.

## Competitive Landscape

HR is the largest pure-play MOB owner in the US and competes with Welltower, Ventas, and Healthpeak (which absorbed Physicians Realty), as well as private equity buyers and health systems themselves. Its competitive position is improving on trajectory even though it is not best-in-class on level. The favorable elements: a self-managed internal platform that captures fee income, a diversified tenant base with no single tenant above 10% of revenue, a concentration in on-campus/hospital-adjacent assets that benefit from site-neutral Medicare dynamics, and accelerating same-store NOI that suggests the operating turnaround is taking hold.

The weak element is occupancy. At ~90.4%, HR runs below peers targeting the mid-90s, which is both a competitive gap and the largest single source of upside. New CEO Peter Scott brings large-cap REIT capital-markets discipline (prior senior-finance background in the sector, associated with Welltower), which is relevant to closing that gap and to executing the refinancing program. The rising cost of maintaining occupancy (leasing commissions up to 7.5% of cash NOI) tempers the competitive read: HR is gaining same-store NOI partly by spending more to retain and attract tenants.

Real Estate BUYs have historically generated negative alpha against the market, so the bar for a buy in this sector is higher than the raw thesis quality alone would suggest. HR's competitive trajectory is constructive but not differentiated enough to clear that bar.

## Risk Assessment

**Refinancing risk (primary).** The 2026–2027 maturities, including the $600M of 3.50% notes due August 2026, will reprice higher in a higher-for-longer rate environment, directly pressuring FFO. This is the central swing variable and is only partly priced.

**Dividend sustainability.** The reset payout's reliance on variable-rate revolver and CP for near-term funding is a vulnerability if cash flow timing or refinancing costs disappoint.

**Tenant credit.** Prospect Medical's January 2025 Chapter 11 is a named, idiosyncratic risk — small in dollars (~80,912 sq ft, of which Hartford Health took 65,477 sq ft via direct leases effective January 2026, with ~15,000 sq ft still uncertain to re-let) but emblematic of broader health-system financial stress in the tenant base.

**Occupancy and concession costs.** Occupancy is soft for the asset class; the rising cost of maintaining it (commissions, abatements, first-gen TIs) erodes the quality of same-store NOI growth.

**Management/governance continuity.** A near-complete leadership and board overhaul (10+ Item 5.02 departures across 2024–2026, repeated CFO turnover culminating in Daniel Gabbay's January 2026 appointment, ~$56M cumulative restructuring/severance) creates execution risk, and a February 2026 auditor change is a disclosure item to track even if not necessarily adverse.

**Regulatory.** Site-neutral Medicare policy, state laws scrutinizing REIT ownership of healthcare assets, and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act of 2025 (flagged as under ongoing analysis) are watch items, though on-campus MOBs could benefit from site-neutral dynamics.

**Reinvestment/purchase-option and ground-lease constraints.** ~$1.1B of properties subject to (mostly fair-value) purchase options could force reinvestment at lower returns; 168 properties (12.4M sq ft) on ground leases constrain financing/sale flexibility.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** New, activist-influenced management closes the occupancy gap from ~90% toward peer mid-90s, driving same-store cash NOI growth in the +5–7% range that compounds into FFO per share once the deleveraging drag abates. The dispose-delever-and-repurchase strategy retires stock at a discount to NAV, and the refinancing of 2026–2027 maturities proves manageable as the swap repositioning (locking $400M at 3.32% to 2029) limits the rate hit. A re-rating toward the high analyst target of $24.00, plus a covered ~4.85% yield, produces low-double-digit total return. The director's $181K open-market purchase at $18.09 signals insider conviction below current price.

**Bear Case.** Refinancing the 2026–2027 maturities higher erodes FFO faster than same-store NOI grows, keeping per-share earnings flat or declining. Occupancy stalls near 90% while concession costs keep rising, so same-store NOI growth is bought with capital that does not flow to FAD. Tenant-credit stress (Prospect Medical-type events) recurs, and the reset dividend again requires revolver funding. Management turnover and the auditor change prove disruptive. The stock drifts back toward the low end of analyst targets (~$18.00) or below.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an income-hold rationale. The durable return is the ~4.85% covered yield plus deleveraging-driven NAV stability; the per-share appreciation case is constrained by flat FFO and the refinancing wall.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Portfolio occupancy climbs toward the mid-90s (a move above ~92.5% on a portfolio basis) while same-store cash NOI growth holds at +5% or better, demonstrating the lease-up lever is working without escalating concession costs.
- The August 2026 senior-note refinancing prints at a cost meaningfully better than the feared ~5%, removing the FFO drag overhang, combined with confirmation of full-year 2026 dividend coverage from operating cash flow.
- The stock pulls back toward the high-$17s/low-$18s (where the director bought and management repurchased), restoring an NAV-discount entry with the same improving fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Same-store cash NOI growth decelerates below +3%, or occupancy slips below 89%, signaling the operating turnaround has stalled.
- The dividend requires sustained revolver/CP funding into late 2026, indicating the reset payout is still uncovered, or a second cut is signaled.
- A material tenant-credit event beyond Prospect, or refinancing terms materially worse than ~5%, that pressures FFO and covenant headroom.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings, and specifically the August 2026 senior-note refinancing, which will resolve the largest single variable in the thesis.

## Conclusion

Healthcare Realty is a credible post-merger turnaround in a structurally attractive niche, run by a reconstituted leadership team executing a disciplined and internally consistent capital-allocation plan. The operating core is improving more than the consolidated financials reveal, and the GAAP losses are non-cash optics from impairments and merger D&A rather than operating distress. A director's open-market purchase and TSR-linked compensation provide constructive alignment.

What holds this back from a buy is that the appreciation case is capped. Normalized FFO per share has been flat for three years, the 2026–2027 refinancing wall reprices low-coupon debt higher, occupancy lags peers, and the dividend was cut and is only recently covered. The stock has rallied ~31% over the past year and sits near its 52-week high and at the analyst median target, so the NAV-discount cheapness that anchored the bull case has largely closed. We rate HR a HOLD for income, with a clear path to an upgrade if occupancy lease-up accelerates and the August 2026 refinancing prints favorably, and a clear path to a downgrade if same-store NOI decelerates or dividend coverage falters.
