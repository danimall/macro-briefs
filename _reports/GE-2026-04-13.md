## Executive Summary

GE Aerospace is the world's leading jet engine manufacturer, operating a razor-and-blade model with an installed base of approximately 49,000 commercial and 29,000 military engines. The company generated $7.7B in free cash flow in FY2025, grew adjusted EPS 38% year-over-year, and carries a $190.6B remaining performance obligation (RPO) — roughly 4.2 years of current revenue. The CFM International joint venture (50/50 with Safran) commands approximately 40% of the large commercial aircraft engine market, and including GE's independent programs, the company influences over 50% of commercial engine decisions. Pratt & Whitney's ongoing GTF durability problems are actively shifting airline preferences toward LEAP-powered aircraft, extending GE's competitive advantage.

The stock trades at ~42x FY2026 guided EPS ($7.10–$7.40 midpoint), a meaningful premium to the A&D peer median of 37x. This premium is justified by superior FCF generation, an accelerating margin expansion trajectory in both segments, and the multi-decade duration of the services backlog. However, the valuation leaves limited margin for error. The legacy insurance portfolio ($23.8B in long-term care reserves) is an idiosyncratic risk that standard screens cannot capture: policyholders averaging age 80 with 63% holding lifetime benefits are entering peak claims years. We rate GE Aerospace a BUY with a base case of $355, reflecting the structural earnings power of the installed base, while acknowledging that entry timing matters at this valuation level.

## Company Overview

GE Aerospace emerged as a pure-play aerospace company following the April 2024 spinoff of GE Vernova, completing the multi-year breakup of the General Electric conglomerate. The company operates through two segments:

**Commercial Engines & Services (CES):** ~76% of revenue. This is the core compounding franchise. Equipment sales ($8.3B) are dwarfed by services revenue ($25.0B), which carries significantly higher margins. LEAP engine deliveries reached 1,802 in 2025 (+28% YoY), and internal shop visits grew 24%. The CES segment achieved 26.6% operating margins in FY2025, up 40bps YoY. Key programs include LEAP (Boeing 737 MAX and Airbus A320neo), GEnx (Boeing 787), GE9X (Boeing 777X), and the legacy CFM56 fleet requiring ongoing maintenance.

**Defense & Propulsion Technologies (DPT):** ~24% of revenue. Military engines (F110, F414, T700/T901), marine propulsion, and additive manufacturing. This segment is accelerating: margins expanded 110bps to 12.3% in FY2025, and the $5B IDIQ contract for F110 engines and $1.6B HAL F414 order demonstrate the global defense spending tailwind.

## Financial Analysis

### Income Statement Trajectory

FY2025 results demonstrate the operating leverage inherent in the services-heavy model:

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2024 | YoY Change |
|--------|--------|--------|------------|
| Total Revenue | $45.9B | $38.7B | +18% |
| Operating Profit (Non-GAAP) | $9.1B | $7.3B | +25% |
| Operating Margin | 21.4% | 20.7% | +70bps |
| Adjusted EPS | $6.37 | $4.60 | +38% |
| Free Cash Flow | $7.7B | $6.2B | +24% |

Profit growth (25%) exceeded revenue growth (18%), confirming margin expansion is real and not purely volume-driven. FCF conversion of ~90% of GAAP net income ($8.6B) is strong, and management guides "well above 100%" conversion of adjusted net income going forward.

The four-quarter earnings beat streak is consistent and substantial: surprises of +13.7%, +12.6%, +10.9%, and +6.8%. The declining beat magnitude could reflect analysts catching up to the earnings trajectory rather than slowing execution. Q4 revenue beat estimates by approximately 20%.

### Balance Sheet

Total debt stands at $20.5B against $12.4B in cash. The headline debt/equity ratio of ~5.96x is distorted by legacy insurance liabilities that inflate total liabilities and compress book equity. The operating reality is better reflected by two consecutive credit rating upgrades in early 2025 (Moody's to A3 with positive outlook, S&P to A-) and $15.4B in total liquidity including an undrawn $3B revolver. The Altman Z-Score of 0.68 is functionally meaningless for this company — it captures the insurance book, not the operating business.

### Capital Allocation

The capital return framework is aggressive and well-structured. The company repurchased $7.4B of stock in FY2025 (29.6M shares) and received a new $20B buyback authorization in December 2025. With FY2026 guided FCF of $8.0–$8.4B, the company can sustainably repurchase 2.5–2.7% of shares outstanding annually even after capex and dividends. The 25% dividend increase announced in early 2026, while modest in yield (~0.6%), signals confidence in cash flow durability.

Capex is ramping from $1.3B to accommodate LEAP MRO capacity expansion ($1B investment) and $1B in U.S. manufacturing investment. These are return-generating investments in captive aftermarket capacity, not speculative growth spending.

## Growth Analysis

### The Installed Base Flywheel

Services RPO alone is $163.0B. These are long-term service agreements (LTSAs) spanning 10-25 year engine lifecycles with an average completion rate of only 19.2%. The revenue locked in today will flow through the income statement for decades.

The growth algorithm:
1. **New engine deliveries** expand the installed base (LEAP deliveries +28% in 2025)
2. **Young fleet aging** into first major shop visits (LEAP engines sold 2016-2020 beginning to cycle in)
3. **Shop visit pricing power** from captive maintenance requirements and limited OEM alternatives
4. **Capacity investment** ($1B in MRO) captures more aftermarket internally vs. ceding to independent MROs

### Defense Tailwinds

DPT's margin expansion reflects the early stages of a multi-year defense spending cycle. Global procurement budgets are rising, particularly for allied nations. The F110 IDIQ ($5B), HAL F414 ($1.6B), and T901 Future Attack Reconnaissance Aircraft (FARA) replacement helicopter engine program provide concrete order visibility. Rising geopolitical tensions in the Middle East and globally are accelerating defense procurement timelines.

## Competitive Landscape

GE Aerospace's competitive position is among the strongest in global industrials:

**Oligopoly structure:** The commercial jet engine market is served by three manufacturers (GE/Safran CFM, Pratt & Whitney, Rolls-Royce). A new entrant would need $1-3B+ and 10-15 years of development before competing, with no guarantee of airline adoption. China's COMAC C919 uses LEAP engines — even sovereign industrial policy has not dislodged the incumbents from the engine supply chain.

**Pratt & Whitney GTF problems:** The ongoing durability and inspection issues with PW's geared turbofan engine have actively shifted airline preferences toward LEAP. CFM International holds ~76% share on A320neo vs. PW's ~24%, and this gap may widen as airlines prioritize dispatch reliability. This is a structural rather than cyclical advantage.

**Switching costs:** Airlines with LEAP-powered fleets face $100M+ costs to transition to a different engine platform, including pilot training, maintenance infrastructure, spare parts inventory, and tooling. The installed base is functionally captive for 25-30 year engine lifecycles.

**Services moat:** GE's proprietary design data, tooling, and Parts Manufacturer Approval (PMA) restrictions limit independent MRO competition on newer engine programs. The MRO capacity investment is designed to internalize more aftermarket revenue before independents can develop capabilities.

## Risk Assessment

### 1. Legacy Insurance Liabilities — HIGH and IDIOSYNCRATIC

The $23.8B in long-term care reserves (ERAC + UFLIC) is the most misunderstood risk in GE Aerospace's profile. Key parameters:
- ~201,700 policies, average age 80
- 63% carry lifetime benefit periods; 77% have inflation protection
- Sensitivity: 5% reduction in disabled life deaths, 5% increase in utilization, or zero morbidity improvement each trigger **$1.2B adverse impact**
- Statutory reserves ($29.8B) exceed GAAP reserves, and future statutory testing could require additional capital contributions
- GE Aerospace has signaled it will not bolster capital positions of its insurance subsidiaries beyond required levels, creating residual risk

This book is entering peak claims years. If actual experience exceeds assumptions across multiple dimensions simultaneously, the capital call could be several billion dollars, directly competing with buybacks and growth investment for cash allocation.

### 2. Tariff and Trade Policy Exposure — MEDIUM

The zero-for-zero aerospace tariff agreement with EU/UK/Japan/Korea mitigated the largest exposure, but broader tariff actions on Chinese goods and potential retaliatory measures affect GE's global supply chain. The 10-K notes unfavorable changes in LTSA profitability estimates "primarily from tariffs" in 2025. At current tariff escalation levels, this headwind could intensify.

### 3. Boeing Dependency — MEDIUM

GE9X revenue depends on Boeing 777X certification (repeatedly delayed). LEAP delivery growth is tied to Boeing 737 MAX production ramp success. Boeing's operational difficulties create indirect risk for GE's equipment delivery and aftermarket growth timeline.

### 4. LTSA Estimation Risk — MEDIUM

Deloitte flagged this as a Critical Audit Matter. $174B in total estimated life-of-contract billings at only 19.2% completion. A 1 percentage point change in estimated profitability impacts the balance by $400M. These estimates extend 10-25 years and involve significant judgment on utilization, costs, and market conditions.

### 5. Oil Price / Airline Economics — ELEVATED

With Brent above $100 and potential to rise further given ongoing geopolitical tensions, airline fuel costs are climbing. Extended periods of elevated jet fuel prices compress airline profitability and can reduce flight hours, directly affecting GE's services revenue (shop visit frequency and utilization-based billing). This is the most proximate macro risk to the earnings trajectory.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 35.1% sits 6.1 percentage points above 29.0% historical volatility — the market is pricing elevated uncertainty. The 1-week put/call volume ratio of 1.76 and 8.3% put skew indicate moderately elevated put demand, consistent with institutional hedging at premium valuations. The 6-month term structure shows IV declining to 33.9% with negative put skew (-3.8%), suggesting the market views near-term uncertainty (likely Q1 2026 earnings digestion and macro volatility) as transitory rather than structural. The flat term structure overall is consistent with a stock where fundamental direction is clear but timing uncertainty is elevated.

Short interest at 1.2% of float is negligible.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($380–$425)

- LEAP shop visit wave accelerates faster than expected as first-generation engines hit major overhaul milestones
- PW GTF problems worsen, pushing more airline orders toward CFM and widening GE's installed base advantage
- DPT margins reach 15%+ as defense procurement accelerates globally, adding $300-400M in incremental profit
- Insurance liabilities run off better than expected as the oldest policyholders exit the book
- Buyback acceleration under $20B authorization reduces share count 3-4% annually, compounding EPS growth
- FCF reaches $10B+ by FY2028, supporting a re-rating to 40-45x that higher earnings level → $380-425

### Bear Case ($240–$265)

- Oil sustained above $120 compresses airline profitability and reduces flight hours, slowing services revenue growth
- Insurance reserve development requires $3-5B capital contribution, consuming 6-12 months of FCF
- Supply chain constraints worsen, limiting LEAP delivery and shop visit throughput below guidance
- Boeing 737 MAX production ramp stalls or 777X certification further delayed
- Multiple compresses to 30x as market reprices growth stocks broadly in a stagflationary environment
- 30x × $7.25 FY2026E EPS = $218; adjusted for partial recovery = $240-265

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $355** — 38x FY2027E EPS of $8.62, plus approximately 2% share count reduction. This reflects the stock trading at its peer median multiple on earnings one year forward, a reasonable anchor given GE's above-average growth profile and the services backlog duration. Timeframe: 12-18 months, with the primary catalyst being FY2026 earnings reports demonstrating continued margin expansion and LTSA profit stability.

**Bull case target: $380–$425** — Requires sustained double-digit EPS growth through FY2028 and no adverse insurance reserve development.

**Bear case target: $240–$265** — Multiple compression to 30x on macro deterioration or insurance capital call.

**Upside/downside from current ($308):** +15% to base case, +23-38% to bull case, -14-22% to bear case. Risk/reward ratio of approximately 1.5:1 on base case, acceptable for a high-quality franchise.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Insurance reserve charge exceeding $2B in any single period, signaling the tail risk is materializing faster than expected
2. CES services revenue growth falling below 5% YoY for two consecutive quarters, indicating the shop visit cycle is decelerating
3. FY2026 FCF coming in below $7.5B (below the low end of guidance), which would signal execution or supply chain problems severe enough to impair the capital return framework

## Conclusion

GE Aerospace is the premier aerospace franchise globally, with structural competitive advantages that compound over multi-decade engine lifecycles. The backlog, 90%+ FCF conversion, credit upgrades, and an installed base entering its most profitable phase (young LEAP fleet aging into first major overhauls) support continued double-digit earnings growth. At ~42x forward, the stock demands strong execution, but the 4-quarter beat streak, positive estimate revision trend, and $20B buyback authorization provide tangible support.

The primary risk is the legacy insurance liability book, which is poorly understood by the market and entering peak claims years. At current funding levels, $7.7B in annual FCF provides substantial capacity to absorb adverse development, but the insurance book represents an asymmetric negative tail that cannot be hedged away.

We rate GE Aerospace **BUY** at $308 with a 12-18 month base case target of $355. The combination of a dominant competitive position, accelerating services monetization, defense tailwinds, and an aggressive capital return program outweighs the valuation premium and legacy insurance risks at current levels. Position sizing should reflect the elevated multiple — this is a high-conviction name that warrants standard rather than maximum allocation given the limited margin of safety.
