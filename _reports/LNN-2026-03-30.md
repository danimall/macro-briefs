## Executive Summary

Lindsay Corporation trades at $118.10, representing a 17.0x forward P/E on consensus FY2026 EPS of $6.42 — a 25% discount to the peer median of 22.6x and a 7.5% FCF yield on FY2025 free cash flow of ~$90M. The company's balance sheet is in net cash ($250M cash vs. $115M fixed-rate debt at 3.82%), a rare position for a small-cap cyclical industrial that provides substantial downside protection and optionality. Analyst estimates are trending upward: current-year EPS revised +4.0% over 90 days, next-year estimates up +2.4%, with accelerating 30-day momentum (+3.7% for next year).

Three identifiable catalysts support a BUY thesis: (1) the new $80M+ MENA irrigation project with ~$70M in FY2026 revenue starting Q2, providing concrete topline visibility; (2) a $150M share repurchase authorization representing 12.5% of the market cap, with management already executing aggressively at ~$129.50/share; and (3) an infrastructure segment growing 16-17% annually with 24% operating margins and limited competition. The stock has declined 22% from its 52-week high of $150.96, at a price where valuation, balance sheet quality, and near-term revenue visibility all support the thesis.

The risks are real and specific: North American irrigation demand has declined for two consecutive years and faces continued headwinds from weak farmer sentiment and elevated interest rates; MENA project concentration creates lumpy revenue risk if the pipeline does not sustain; and the current macro regime of oil at $116 raises fertilizer and input costs for farmers, potentially compressing farm margins even as USDA projects higher gross income.

## Company Overview

Lindsay Corporation (founded 1955, Omaha, NE) operates two segments. The **Irrigation segment** (~84% of FY2025 revenue) manufactures center pivot and lateral move irrigation systems under the Zimmatic brand, along with IoT solutions (Elecsys) and precision agriculture software (FieldNET). Manufacturing spans the US, Brazil, France, China, Turkey, and South Africa, with distribution through 200+ independent US dealers and international networks. The **Infrastructure segment** (~16% of revenue) produces the Road Zipper System (moveable concrete barriers with limited competition), crash cushions, specialty barriers, and road marking equipment. The company employs 1,275 people and carries a market capitalization of $1.2B.

The center pivot irrigation market is an oligopoly — four manufacturers control the substantial majority of US installations. Lindsay competes primarily with Valmont Industries (VMI), Reinke Manufacturing, and T-L Irrigation. Differentiation comes through technology integration (GPS positioning, variable rate irrigation, cloud-based scheduling) and a global manufacturing footprint that enables large-scale international project execution.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY2025 revenue recovered to $676M (+11% YoY) after two years of decline ($760M → $674M → $607M), driven by a $100M+ MENA irrigation project and 16% infrastructure growth. Q1 FY2026 revenue declined 6% to $155.8M as the initial MENA project wound down. International irrigation was 52% of segment revenue in FY2025, up from 41% in FY2024, reflecting the MENA project's scale.

**Margins:** FY2025 gross margin was 31.2%, operating margin 13.0%, net margin 10.9%. Q1 FY2026 gross margin expanded 220bps YoY to 32.2% as lower-margin MENA project revenue declined — an important data point indicating the base business earns higher margins than the large international projects. Infrastructure segment operating margin of 24.3% (FY2025) substantially exceeds irrigation at 17.1%, meaning infrastructure's growing share improves blended profitability.

**Balance Sheet:** $250.6M cash at FY2025 end, $199.6M at Q1 FY2026 (after $30.3M in buybacks). Long-term debt of $115M at 3.82% fixed due February 2030, with $0 drawn on the $50M revolver. Net cash of ~$85-135M depending on the measurement date. Current ratio ~3.7x. Debt/equity 0.22x.

**Cash Flow:** FY2025 operating cash flow of $132.9M (up 39% YoY from $95.8M). Capex of $42.5M yields ~$90.4M in free cash flow, or a 7.5% FCF yield at the current $1.2B market cap. FY2026 guided capex of $50-55M (manufacturing modernization) will modestly reduce FCF, but operating cash flow should grow with the new MENA project revenue.

**Capital Allocation:** The company returned $42M to shareholders in FY2025 via dividends ($15.7M) and buybacks ($11.5M), then accelerated to $30.3M in buybacks in Q1 FY2026 alone, exhausting the prior authorization at ~$129.50/share. The new $150M program has no expiration date. The Pessl Instruments investment ($5.8M for a 49.9% stake with a call option) represents strategic optionality in precision agriculture at a minimal capital outlay.

**Earnings Quality:** 3 beats in 4 quarters. The Q2 FY2025 miss (September quarter, -10.9%) corresponded with MENA project timing; the surrounding quarters saw substantial beats (+27.7%, +25.4%, +3.4%). FCF/Net Income conversion was ~123% in FY2025, consistent with high-quality earnings and no accrual concerns.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term visibility:** The new $80M+ MENA project with ~$70M in expected FY2026 revenue provides concrete topline support starting Q2 FY2026. Consensus estimates reflect this: FY2026 EPS of $6.42 (+$0.25 over 90 days), FY2027 EPS of $7.04 (+$0.25 over 30 days).

**Infrastructure segment:** Growing 16-17% annually with 24% operating margins and a legislative tailwind (IIJA through September 2026). Road Zipper lease revenue of $17.3M provides a recurring component. This segment's expansion improves blended margins and reduces cyclical agricultural dependence.

**North American irrigation weakness:** Declined 9% in FY2025 and 4% in Q1 FY2026. USDA's projected 41% increase in 2025 net farm income is driven by government payments, not crop receipts (which decline 3%). Management explicitly states this is not expected to lift irrigation demand meaningfully. The 100% bonus depreciation restoration (One Big Beautiful Bill Act) provides a marginal offset by improving the after-tax economics of center pivot purchases.

**Secular tailwinds:** Water scarcity, population growth, food security, and flood-to-pivot conversion provide multi-decade demand support. Globally, less than 5% of irrigated farmland uses center pivots, leaving a large addressable market for conversion. The MENA pipeline suggests food security concerns in the Middle East are producing sustained project-level demand.

**Risks to growth:** The MENA project pipeline is not guaranteed to be recurring — the 10-K explicitly flagged this as a new risk factor. If the back-to-back projects don't lead to a sustained pipeline, international irrigation could revert to normalized levels. North American recovery depends on farm income composition shifting from government support to market-driven crop receipts, which requires higher commodity prices or lower input costs.

## Competitive Landscape

Lindsay operates in an oligopolistic center pivot market where four manufacturers dominate. Switching costs are moderate (dealer relationships, parts compatibility, technology platform lock-in via FieldNET), and the dealer network of 200+ locations creates distribution advantages that are difficult to replicate.

The Road Zipper System faces "limited competition" per the 10-K — a durable competitive advantage in a niche market where barriers to entry include proprietary technology, engineering complexity, and an installed base with government customers. Infrastructure contributes disproportionate profitability (24% operating margins) relative to its revenue share (16%).

The Pessl Instruments investment positions Lindsay to deepen its precision agriculture technology offering. Pessl's weather stations and soil sensors are already integrated into FieldNET; full acquisition (call option exercisable October 2027) would expand recurring subscription revenue and strengthen the technology differentiation versus Valmont and Reinke.

**Key competitive risk:** Valmont Industries (VMI, $5B market cap) is the dominant player and benefits from greater scale. In a normalized market without MENA-type project windfalls, Lindsay competes for incremental share in a mature North American market where replacement demand, drought events, and government incentive programs drive volumes.

## Risk Assessment

**Agricultural cyclicality (HIGH):** The core business is tied to farm income, crop prices, and farmer sentiment — all of which are weak to mixed. Corn and soybean prices face pressure from strong harvests and elevated inventories. North American irrigation demand has declined sequentially for two years.

**MENA concentration risk (MEDIUM-HIGH):** FY2025 had one customer at 13% of revenue. The back-to-back project wins are encouraging but the pipeline is not contractually guaranteed beyond the current $80M project. Revenue could decline $50-70M if no follow-on project materializes.

**Macro regime interaction (MEDIUM-HIGH):** Oil at $116 directly raises fertilizer, fuel, and input costs for farmers, compressing farm margins. Diesel at $5.29/gallon increases operating costs for center pivot-irrigated farms. The China fertilizer export ban (referenced in world model) could further elevate US nitrogen prices, benefiting CF Industries but hurting Lindsay's end customers. Higher fertilizer prices support domestic nitrogen producers (CF) while suppressing equipment demand from farmers facing input cost pressure.

**CFO/executive transition (MEDIUM):** Both the CFO and President Irrigation departed in late 2025. Transitions appear orderly, but this level of C-suite change during a cyclical downturn warrants monitoring.

**X-Lite litigation (MEDIUM):** Product liability suits regarding the X-Lite end terminal are ongoing, including a Missouri DOT suit seeking punitive damages and qui tam actions in Tennessee and California. No accrual recorded; management states loss is not probable but cannot estimate the range. Material adverse resolution could be significant for a $1.2B market cap company.

**Environmental liability (LOW):** $10.6M accrued for groundwater contamination remediation at Lindsay, NE. Costs could exceed accrual pending EPA/NDEE approval, but the magnitude is manageable relative to the balance sheet.
