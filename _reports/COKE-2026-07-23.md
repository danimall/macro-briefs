**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Coca-Cola Consolidated is the largest Coca-Cola bottler in the United States, a franchised distribution and manufacturing operation covering 14 states plus the District of Columbia. It is a defensive, cash-generative regional monopoly that owns no brands. Its economics are dictated by The Coca-Cola Company (TCCC), which sets concentrate and finished-goods pricing under agreements with no contractual cap. The past twelve months have been defined by a single corporate action: in November 2025 COKE repurchased all 18,835,460 shares held by TCCC for roughly $2.4 billion, ending a century-long ownership relationship and funding the transaction largely with new debt.

The buyback consolidated control under the Harrison family and public float, removed a strategic overhang, and delivered ~$2.7 billion of total capital return in 2025. It also pushed total debt from $1.79 billion to $2.79 billion, drove stockholders' equity to negative $739.7 million, tripled quarterly interest expense (to $32.1 million in Q1 2026 from $6.9 million a year earlier), and prompted S&P to revise its outlook to negative while affirming BBB+. The negative equity is a capital-return artifact rather than a distress signal; the company earns ~$570 million in net income and ~$620 million in free cash flow annually. But the incremental leverage and variable-rate exposure are real, and they arrive in a higher-for-longer rate regime that raises the carrying cost of the financing model.

The fundamental tension is that 2025 volume grew just 0.3% while revenue rose 4.8%; the entire top-line gain came from pricing and premium mix. That pricing engine is now colliding with aluminum-tariff cost inflation that added ~$35 million of year-over-year input cost in Q1 2026 alone. At ~26x trailing earnings and a premium to beverage-staples peers, with the stock ~19% below its March 52-week high but still up ~57% year-over-year, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate COKE a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1902 and incorporated in 1980, Coca-Cola Consolidated distributes, markets, and manufactures nonalcoholic beverages to roughly 60 million consumers across the Carolinas, Mid-Atlantic, Mid-South, and Mid-West regions. It operates 10 manufacturing plants and 60 distribution centers with approximately 17,000 employees (~15% unionized), and trades on Nasdaq following a 10:1 stock split effective May 27, 2025.

Approximately 85% of bottle/can volume consists of TCCC products (Coca-Cola, Sprite, Fanta, Dasani, Powerade, BODYARMOR, smartwater, Topo Chico, fairlife). The remaining ~15% is licensed brands, chiefly Monster Energy and Keurig Dr Pepper. By sales, the mix runs ~59% Sparkling and ~35% Still; by volume, ~46% bottles and ~54% cans. The business runs in two segments: Nonalcoholic Beverages (the vast majority of revenue) and "All Other," consisting of the Red Classic trucking subsidiaries. The Data Ventures analytics unit was dissolved at year-end 2025.

COKE is a franchised bottler operating a regional monopoly protected under the U.S. Soft Drink Interbrand Competition Act, holding exclusive distribution rights within its territories. Its competitive advantage is distribution scale and direct-store-delivery execution, not brand ownership. Primary competitors are local PepsiCo and, in some regions, Dr Pepper bottlers.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability.** Revenue has compounded steadily: $6.20 billion (2022), $6.65 billion (2023), $6.90 billion (2024), and $7.23 billion (2025, +4.8%). Of the 2025 gain, roughly $215 million came from pricing and the balance from favorable mix (energy, enhanced water, protein). Reported net income declined to $571 million from $633 million, but the drop was almost entirely non-operating: a $131.9 million non-cash mark-to-market charge on the contingent-consideration liability plus higher interest expense. Adjusted net income was $668.5 million versus $678.6 million, down 1.5%. Operating income rose to $950.7 million. Gross margin was 39.7%, down 20 basis points on aluminum/tariff cost pressure and a mix shift toward lower-margin Still products.

**Cash flow quality is high.** Operating cash flow was $931.9 million in 2025, free cash flow ~$620 million (an 8.6% FCF margin), against capex of $312 million (~$300 million guided for 2026, focused on supply-chain automation). Return metrics are strong: ROA 13.3%, operating margin 13.2%, net margin 7.9%, asset turnover 1.68x.

**The balance sheet is the crux.** COKE reported negative stockholders' equity of –$739.7 million in 2025, improving to –$643.5 million in Q1 2026, a deliberate consequence of the buyback, which drove retained earnings to a –$824 million deficit. The Altman Z-Score of 2.16 (grey zone) is mechanically depressed by the negative equity term and does not reflect operational distress; the company is highly profitable, cash-generative, and in compliance with its debt covenants. Cash fell from $1.14 billion (2024) to $282 million (2025) to $233 million (Q1 2026) as the buyback was funded. Total debt has been actively reduced to $2.64 billion by Q1 2026, with the 5-year term loan repaid down to $225 million ($150 million in February, $75 million in April 2026). Management hedged rate exposure with three $150 million interest-rate swaps in February 2026; a 1% rate rise still adds ~$7.8 million in annual interest on unhedged variable balances.

**Q1 FY2026 (ended 4/3/2026).** Net sales were $1.85 billion, up 16.9% reported but 8.5% adjusted; roughly $132 million of the gain came from six extra selling days and Easter timing. Adjusted volume rose 6.4% (Sparkling +5.3%, Still +10.2%, led by low-margin Dasani casepack water). Net income was $111.6 million; gross margin was 39.4%, down 30 basis points. Interest expense jumped to $32.1 million from $6.9 million. Q4 2026 will have six fewer selling days, mechanically reversing the Q1 calendar tailwind.

Judging financial health as a combination of resilience and capital-structure efficiency: the cash-generation engine and covenant compliance support a middling-to-solid score, but the negative equity, tripled interest burden, negative S&P outlook, and depleted cash cushion offset the strength of the FCF. Score: 5.5.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is honest but modest. Underlying volume was essentially flat in 2025; revenue growth is a pricing-and-mix phenomenon. The premium Still portfolio (Monster, Powerade, BODYARMOR, Topo Chico, smartwater, fairlife) is growing faster than Sparkling, which supports the top line but pressures margin because Still products generally carry lower gross margins.

Q1 2026 adjusted volume of +6.4% is encouraging in isolation, but the strength was concentrated in low-margin Dasani casepack water and was aided by favorable calendar timing that will reverse. The durable question is whether COKE can grow volume without eroding margin, or grow price without eroding volume. With no brand ownership, COKE depends on TCCC's innovation and marketing pipeline (marketing funding was $209.5 million in 2025, not contractually guaranteed) to drive demand.

There is no thematic tailwind here. Consumer Staples is not a sector where BUY recommendations reliably generate alpha, and beverage bottling in particular is a low-growth, defensive category. Capex is directed at supply-chain automation (the new Columbus, OH automated distribution center), which supports margin efficiency more than volume expansion. Growth Prospects score: 5.0, consistent with the recent downward trajectory in this dimension and reflecting the reality that the pricing lever is now being neutralized by input-cost inflation.

## Valuation Assessment

COKE trades at ~26x trailing earnings (EPS $6.81, price $177.02), with forward and trailing multiples essentially identical given the low expected earnings growth. Against beverage-staples peers, this is a premium: the peer median P/E is 23.2x (KDP 23.2x, PEP 22.6x, FIZZ 18.5x), with faster-growing names commanding more (MNST 44.5x, CELH 64.5x). COKE's ~26x sits above the median for a business with flat volumes and a leverage overhang, though the 602% five-year TSR outperformance and disciplined capital return partly justify the premium.

The dividend yield is negligible at ~0.55% (KDP 2.9%, PEP 4.1%), so there is no absolute-yield support for the valuation. The stock is not a cyclical trough story, since earnings are stable rather than inflecting, so cycle-adjusted valuation frameworks do not apply. There is no identified catalyst that would cause the market to re-rate the shares meaningfully higher.

The calibration lesson here is directly relevant: a superficially reasonable multiple in a defensive staples name is not, by itself, a reason to expect outperformance, and low-multiple "cheapness" has historically been anti-predictive. COKE is neither cheap enough to offer asymmetric upside nor expensive enough to flag as an obvious short. It is fairly priced for a well-run, cash-generative, low-growth bottler carrying more leverage than a year ago. Valuation score: 5.0.

## Competitive Landscape

COKE's moat is real but narrow. As the exclusive franchised bottler across its territories, protected by federal interbrand-competition law, it faces no direct competition for TCCC-product distribution within its footprint. Its scale, DSD execution, and 60-center distribution network are durable advantages. The five-year TSR of 602% versus a peer group's 128% demonstrates that management has translated this position into exceptional shareholder value.

The trajectory, however, is stable-to-slightly-eroding rather than improving. The mix shift toward lower-margin Still products compresses gross margin. Volume is flat, so the company is not gaining share in absolute consumption. The November 2025 buyback changed ownership but not economics: TCCC still unilaterally sets concentrate prices with no cap and, under the Regional Manufacturing Agreement, sets finished-goods prices for sales to other bottlers. COKE gained independence in ownership while retaining full commercial dependence. Customer concentration is significant: Walmart (21% of volume / 17% of net sales) and Kroger (15% / 12%) together account for 36% of volume and 29% of net sales, with no long-term contracts.

The competitive position remains solid but the improving-trajectory case has weakened, consistent with the score's decline from 7.0 earlier in the year. Competitive Position score: 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Aluminum/tariff cost pressure (most acute near-term).** Elevated aluminum import tariffs are explicitly cited by management as the largest margin drag. A 10% rise in key commodities equates to $35–45 million of incremental cost over 12 months, directly threatening the pricing-driven margin structure.

**Structural TCCC pricing dependence.** With no cap on concentrate pricing and TCCC controlling finished-goods pricing to other bottlers, COKE has limited ability to manage its single largest cost input. This is a permanent structural feature, not a transient risk.

**Contingent-consideration volatility.** The $753 million sub-bottling liability (payable to CCR over ~40-year lives) is marked to market each quarter; a 10bp risk-free-rate move equals ~$7 million of P&L noise. Falling rates increase the liability and depress reported earnings, as the $131.9 million 2025 charge demonstrated. Annual cash payments run ~$50–80 million.

**Leverage and rate risk.** Variable-rate term loans (partly hedged) create ongoing rate sensitivity, and S&P's negative outlook signals the rating is at risk if deleveraging stalls. In a higher-for-longer rate environment, the carrying cost of this financing model is elevated.

**Governance / controlled-company structure.** CEO J. Frank Harrison III controls ~78% of voting power (potentially ~83%) via Class B stock. Minority holders have limited influence, and related-party arrangements (HQ leased from the Harrison-controlled Beacon Investment Corp., ~$1 million/quarter) persist.

These are specific, quantifiable risks that are largely under-appreciated by the multiple: the tariff drag and the negative-outlook leverage in particular are not obviously priced into a ~26x staples multiple. Risk Factors score: 4.5.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing meaningful near-term stress. ATM implied volatility of 48.7% is rich versus 36.2% one-year historical vol (a 12.5-point spread) and sits in the 68th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 48.7% > long-term 38.3%), and the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±11.2%, consistent with positioning ahead of the next earnings print. IV has risen from 33.2% to 48.7% over the past six weeks even as the price fell 5.8%, indicating the market is pricing in increasing uncertainty rather than complacency. Put/call skew is balanced (–0.1% near-term), and short interest is modest at 8.3% of float (6.5 days to cover); this is not a heavily-shorted, high-squeeze-risk name. The elevated IV argues against buying options-embedded exposure now and suggests waiting for post-print volatility compression as a better entry window.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** COKE is a best-in-class defensive franchise with a proven capital-allocation record. The TCCC buyback removed a strategic overhang, consolidated the equity base, and was executed at a sensible price. Management is deleveraging aggressively ($225 million of term-loan repayment by April 2026) and has hedged rate exposure, positioning for an S&P outlook stabilization that would remove a valuation headwind. Adjusted earnings were essentially flat in 2025 despite the noise, and ~$620 million of annual FCF underpins the dividend and continued debt reduction. If aluminum tariffs ease and premium-mix volume growth sustains without further margin erosion, the stock re-rates modestly higher toward the low-$200s.

**Bear Case.** The pricing engine that drives all of COKE's revenue growth is being consumed by aluminum-tariff inflation, and gross margin is already compressing. Volume growth is calendar-flattered and concentrated in low-margin water. The buyback added ~$1 billion of debt and tripled interest expense at exactly the wrong point in the rate cycle, and S&P's negative outlook is a live downgrade risk if deleveraging stalls. A consumer-spending cliff in H2 2026 would pressure the volume base further. At a premium staples multiple with a negligible yield and no catalyst, the downside is a de-rating toward peer median (~$150s) on continued margin compression and reversal of the Q1 calendar benefit in Q4.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction. The business is high-quality and cash-generative, but the valuation is full, the growth is flat-to-modest, the balance sheet carries a new leverage overhang, and near-term input-cost pressure is eroding the one lever that drives the top line. There is no identifiable catalyst to justify a BUY, and the fundamentals are too sound to justify an AVOID.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Aluminum-tariff cost pressure abates (evidenced by gross margin re-expanding above 40% for two consecutive quarters), OR
- S&P revises its outlook back to stable on demonstrated deleveraging (total debt below $2.2 billion), OR
- The stock de-rates toward peer median (~$150 / ~22x trailing) while adjusted earnings hold, restoring asymmetric upside.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Gross margin compresses below 38.5% on sustained input-cost inflation with no offsetting pricing power, OR
- Volume turns negative on an adjusted basis for two consecutive quarters (indicating the pricing/volume trade-off has broken), OR
- Deleveraging stalls and S&P moves to an actual downgrade below BBB+.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (approximately early August 2026), with the Q4 2026 print especially important as it will show whether underlying volume holds once the six-fewer-selling-days calendar headwind reverses the Q1 tailwind. Monitor the S&P outlook and aluminum-tariff developments in the interim.

## Conclusion

Coca-Cola Consolidated is a well-managed, defensive bottler that has rewarded shareholders over the past five years and executed a buyout of TCCC's stake in 2025. The transaction was strategically clean but left the company with negative book equity, roughly $1 billion of incremental debt, tripled interest expense, and a negative S&P outlook, offset by strong FCF and disciplined, ongoing deleveraging. The fundamental engine is pricing-driven revenue growth on flat volumes, and that engine is now under direct pressure from aluminum-tariff cost inflation.

At ~26x trailing earnings, a premium to beverage-staples peers, with a negligible yield, no near-term catalyst, and elevated implied volatility into the next print, the risk/reward is balanced. This is a hold-to-monitor situation. We would turn constructive on margin stabilization or a de-rating toward peer multiples, and more cautious on sustained margin compression or a rating downgrade. Overall score: 5.25, HOLD.
