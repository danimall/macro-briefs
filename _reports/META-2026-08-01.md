**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Meta's second quarter of 2026 separated the two halves of the business more starkly than any prior period. Revenue grew 28% year over year to $60,801 million, an acceleration from 22.2% in FY2025, driven by a 24% increase in average revenue per person and a 12% rise in average price per ad. Operating income fell 8% to $18,775 million, operating margin compressed 12.1 percentage points to 30.9%, diluted EPS declined 13% to $6.18 against a $7.36 consensus (a 16% miss after three consecutive beats), and quarterly free cash flow fell 91% to $784 million from $8,550 million. The share price fell roughly 10-11% on July 30 and now sits about 30% below the August 2025 high of $796.25.

The compression is not a demand problem. R&D rose 67% to $21,656 million, depreciation rose 40% to $6,000 million, and stock compensation reached $7,601 million (12.5% of revenue). Two identifiable items — $2,400 million of legal charges and $1,180 million of severance from the May 2026 reduction of roughly 8,000 employees — account for about 5.9 points of the 12.1-point compression; excluding both, margin would have been approximately 36.8% ($18,775M + $2,400M + $1,180M = $22,355M; ÷ $60,801M). The remaining 6.2 points is structural, driven by depreciation on an asset base where $80,345 million of construction in progress currently generates no revenue. The FY2026 capex range was narrowed upward at the floor to $130-145 billion from $125-145 billion; against H1 spending of $50,918 million (including finance lease principal), that implies $79-94 billion in the second half.

Three developments materially change the risk profile versus prior quarters. First, share repurchases were $0 in H1 2026 against $22,921 million a year earlier, with $25,030 million of authorization untouched, and shares outstanding rose from 2,530 million to 2,548 million — Meta has become a diluting equity for the first time in roughly a decade. Second, juries returned liability verdicts against Meta in two youth-safety trials in March 2026, and the risk factors were rewritten to disclose that; further trials were calendared for August 12, September 8 and October 28, 2026. Third, non-debt contractual commitments roughly tripled in six months, with leases signed but not commenced rising to $278,990 million and non-cancelable purchase commitments to $349,310 million, plus approximately $68 billion of additional data center leases entered in July 2026. The equity is inexpensive against consensus earnings for reasons that are specific and not yet resolvable. HOLD.

## Company Overview

Meta operates two segments with extreme asymmetry. Family of Apps — Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, WhatsApp and Threads — produced $60,370 million of Q2 2026 revenue (99.3% of total) and $23,394 million of segment operating income. Within that, advertising was $59,363 million and "other revenue" $1,007 million, the latter growing 73% year over year on WhatsApp paid messaging and subscriptions. Reality Labs produced $431 million of revenue against a $4,619 million operating loss, a loss-to-revenue ratio of roughly 10.7:1. FY2025 Reality Labs operating loss was $19,190 million and management guided to similar magnitude in 2026.

Family Daily Active People averaged 3.60 billion in June 2026, up 3% from 3.48 billion. Average revenue per person was $16.86, up 24%. Effectively all incremental revenue now comes from monetizing the same audience more intensively — impressions rose 14% and price per ad 12% in the quarter. Management attributes this to AI-driven ranking and recommendation improvements; the 10-Q separately names online commerce as the largest contributor to the ad revenue increase, and ad-load expansion in Reels and Threads inventory is an equally consistent explanation for the impression growth. The driver is plausible but not independently verifiable from disclosure.

Revenue by user geography grew 32% in US & Canada, 36% Rest of World, 24% Europe and 19% Asia-Pacific. By billing address, APAC contributed $16,073 million against a much smaller APAC user-revenue base, reflecting China-domiciled advertisers purchasing outbound e-commerce inventory — a concentration the filings flag without quantifying.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement.** Q2 2026 revenue $60,801M (+28%); cost of revenue $11,330M (+33%); R&D $21,656M (+67%); marketing & sales $3,431M (+15%); G&A $5,609M (+111%); total costs $42,026M (+55%). Revenue rose $13,285 million while costs rose $14,951 million. FY2025 for reference: revenue $200,966M, operating income $83,300M (41.4% margin), net income $60,458M, diluted EPS $23.49.

**Cash flow.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $64,088M, up 29% from $49,587M. Purchases of PP&E were $49,113M against $29,479M, and finance lease principal $1,805M against $1,225M, producing free cash flow on the company's definition of $13,170M versus $18,883M. The Q2 standalone figure of $784M against $8,550M is the number that moved the stock.

Two timing items distort the near-term optics in opposite directions and both matter. Cash paid for income taxes was $1,999M in H1 2026 versus $5,544M a year earlier — a $3.5 billion tailwind unrelated to earnings. Working the other way, $19,502M of property and equipment sat in accounts payable and accruals at June 30 versus $10,618M a year earlier, meaning cash capex is lagging accrual capex by a widening margin that will reverse. I do not annualize the Q2 free cash flow figure. As a labeled sensitivity: if FY2026 operating cash flow lands at $145-155 billion and capex lands at the $137.5 billion guidance midpoint, FY2026 free cash flow would be roughly break-even to $17 billion against $43,585M in FY2025.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: cash and equivalents $15,462M plus marketable securities $74,798M = $90,260M; net PP&E $225,724M including $80,345M construction in progress; total assets $449,956M; long-term debt $83,664M; equity $261,221M. Current ratio 2.23x; debt-to-equity 0.32x; net cash approximately $6.3 billion, down from roughly $22.6 billion at year-end. Debt issuance has been concentrated: $30.0 billion in November 2025 and $25.0 billion in May 2026 out of $84.0 billion face total, with the May tranche carrying coupons up to 6.45% versus 3.50% on the 2022 issue. There are no financial covenants and maturities are back-ended — $2,750M in 2027, $1,500M in 2028, $1,000M in 2029, $5,000M in 2030, $73,750M thereafter. Refinancing risk is not a 2026-2027 concern. A derived Altman Z-Score of 5.6-6.3 places the company comfortably in the safe zone.

**Two tax distortions.** FY2025 net income fell to $60,458M from $62,360M despite operating income rising, because of an approximately $15,930 million discrete non-cash tax charge in Q3 2025 on enactment of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, including roughly $14,030 million of valuation allowance. H1 2026 carried an approximately $8,030 million discrete benefit in Q1 from CAMT transitional relief, producing a negative 5% H1 effective rate. Q2's rate was a more normal 16% and management guides 15-17% for the balance of 2026. Any screen scoring change-in-net-income or change-in-ROA reads FY2025 as deteriorating and H1 2026 as improving for reasons unrelated to operations. Neither distortion touches revenue, operating income or operating cash flow.

**Normalized earnings.** TTM net income of $68,098M spans Q3 2025 through Q2 2026 and therefore contains both items, a net −$7,900 million. Adding that back gives approximately $76,000 million, or roughly $29.62 per diluted share on 2,566 million diluted shares — approximately 18.7x at $555.10. Adding back the Q2 legal charge and severance tax-effected at 16% (≈$3,007M) gives roughly $30.79 and 18.0x. I treat the legal add-back skeptically given the trial calendar.

**Returns.** TTM ROE on average equity of $239,232M is 28.5%; ROA 16.7%; asset turnover 0.56x and falling as construction in progress accumulates. That turnover decline either resolves into revenue or into impairment; there is no third outcome.

**Capital allocation.** The pivot is decisive: $0 of repurchases in H1 2026 against $22,921M a year earlier, $24,910M of net long-term debt drawn, dividend maintained at $0.525 quarterly ($2,699M paid in H1, roughly a 0.4% yield), and share count rising 0.7% in six months (about 1.4% annualized) as RSUs vest unoffset. Unvested RSUs totaled 146,464 thousand shares at June 30, equal to 5.75% of shares outstanding, or 5.43% ownership dilution if fully issued, with $79.79 billion of unrecognized RSU compensation over roughly three years.

**Market capitalization reconciliation.** The provided profile shows $1,216 billion on 2,196 million shares, which corresponds to Class A alone. Class A of 2,206 million plus Class B of 342 million equals 2,548 million shares; at $555.10 that is approximately $1,414 billion. Enterprise value is roughly $1,408 billion ($1,414B + $84.0B debt − $90.26B cash and securities), or 6.2x TTM revenue of $228,251M and 16.2x TTM EBIT of $86,950M. TTM free cash flow of approximately $37.9 billion is a 2.7% yield, and that yield is declining by construction. All valuation work below uses the 2,548 million share count.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth accelerated from 22.2% in FY2025 to roughly 30% in H1 2026 and 28% in Q2, with Q3 guidance of $61-64 billion implying 26-32%. That is unusual for a business of this size and it is the strongest element of the case. Other revenue at +73% and business messaging represent a genuine second monetization surface, albeit off a $1 billion quarterly base.

Earnings growth is the problem. Q2 operating income fell 8% and diluted EPS 13%. Consensus estimates are falling across every horizon: current quarter $6.77 (−3.8% over both 30 and 90 days), next quarter $8.23 (−3.0%), current year $32.12 (−2.3% over 30 days), next year $34.41 (−1.5%). The implied growth from FY2026 to FY2027 consensus is only 7.1%. Revenue compounding at 28% while consensus earnings grow 7% is the arithmetic of a capital-intensity transition, and consensus may still be understating the depreciation load.

The mechanics deserve explicit treatment because they determine the outcome. Of $225,724 million of net PP&E, $80,345 million sits in construction in progress and is not being depreciated. In-service assets of roughly $145 billion are producing about $24 billion of annualized depreciation. As construction in progress is placed in service and H2 capex of $79-94 billion lands, the in-service base plausibly approaches $300 billion by late 2027, implying annualized depreciation in the $45-55 billion range — an incremental $21-31 billion of pre-tax expense, or roughly $7-10 per share after tax. Against that, 28% revenue growth on a ~$240 billion FY2026 base adds roughly $67 billion of revenue; at a 60% incremental margin before depreciation that is about $40 billion of incremental gross profit. The bet, stated plainly, is that revenue growth outruns the depreciation ramp. It can, but only if growth holds near current rates — and DAP is growing 3%, so growth depends entirely on continued 20%+ ARPP gains.

Reality Labs remains a roughly $19 billion annual drag with $431 million of quarterly revenue. Headcount is now falling — 75,472 at June 30, down 1% year over year and down from 78,865 at December 31 — after growing 6% in FY2025. R&D rising 67% while headcount falls is the signature of reallocation from broad employment toward compute and a small number of very expensive researchers.

## Valuation Assessment

At $555.10 Meta trades at 17.3x current-year consensus EPS of $32.12 and 16.1x next-year $34.41, against a peer median of 27.2x (Alphabet Class C at 27.2x, Class A at 27.3x). On tax-normalized TTM earnings the multiple is approximately 18.0-18.7x. Enterprise value is 6.2x TTM revenue and 16.2x TTM EBIT. The free cash flow yield of 2.7% is the least attractive metric and is falling.

The discount to Alphabet is roughly 40% on forward earnings. Part of it is defensible on relative fundamentals: Alphabet's capex converts into third-party cloud revenue that can be measured, while Meta's compute is consumed internally to improve ranking, recommendations, Meta AI and business messaging. The return on Meta's spend appears only indirectly, as higher ad prices and engagement, and can never be isolated in a revenue line. A permanent discount is a rational market response to that verification problem, not an error.

Against that, three specific arguments support the current price being too low. First, the FTC monopolization case seeking divestiture of Instagram and WhatsApp was decided in Meta's favor on November 18, 2025 (appeal noticed January 20, 2026), removing the largest structural tail risk from the sum-of-parts floor. Second, ex-legal-charge and ex-severance operating margin of 36.8% on a business growing revenue 28% is not a broken P&L. Third, if the FY2027 capex range flattens rather than escalates again, the incremental depreciation drag becomes a one-time reset rather than a compounding one, and a 16x multiple on a franchise with 28.5% ROE and 3.6 billion users is too low.

Two arguments cut the other way. Consensus estimates are falling, not rising, so the usual pattern where analyst targets lag improving fundamentals does not apply here; I do not treat the mean target of $778.68 or the fact that the lowest target ($580) sits above spot as independent evidence. And the PEG on next-year earnings growth of 7.1% is roughly 2.3 — the absolute multiple is low, but the earnings growth it is being applied to is weak. Cheapness alone, in a business whose per-share support from buybacks has been withdrawn and whose share count is now growing 1.4% annually, does not establish mispricing.

Valuation score of 6.0: modestly attractive on absolute forward multiples and on the removed FTC tail, offset by falling estimates, a collapsing FCF yield, and the absence of any identifiable positive near-term catalyst. The catalysts on the calendar for the next 90 days are litigation dates, all of which are negative or neutral.

## Competitive Landscape

The advertising franchise is performing well on every measurable axis. Advertising revenue grew 27%, price per ad 12%, impressions 14%, ARPP 24%. The auction is healthy and the online commerce vertical is the largest single contributor. Against Alphabet, Meta's incremental revenue is growing from a smaller base at a faster rate, and the ranking-quality improvements appear to be translating into pricing power rather than only volume.

Three structural pressures qualify that. The European Commission opened an antitrust investigation in December 2025 into Meta's policy barring general-purpose AI providers from using the WhatsApp Business API for chatbots, and in June 2026 imposed an interim measure requiring WhatsApp to offer that access free of charge. Meta intends to appeal, but interim measures take effect before final rulings, and analogous investigations are open in Africa, Brazil and Turkey. This compels open access, at zero price, to the monetization surface that "other revenue" growth is built on. It is a more economically direct constraint than the structural remedy the FTC sought and lost.

Youth-access restrictions — Australia's under-16 ban, various US state laws, the EC's DSA preliminary findings of April 29 and July 10, 2026 regarding under-13 users and addictive design — attack the top of a funnel already growing only 3%. A business monetizing the same audience harder each year has limited cushion against measures that shrink that audience.

General-purpose AI assistants are now named in the risk factors as a competitive vector for time spent, alongside TikTok. Meta retains the advantage of not needing to win the frontier-model race to earn a return — it needs only enough ranking improvement to justify the spend. That is a lower bar, and it is also a bar no external observer can measure.

Competitive position score of 6.5: the trajectory of the core ad business is improving and the FTC outcome widened the strategic runway, offset by regulatory compulsion on business messaging, funnel-level youth restrictions, and Reality Labs continuing to consume $19 billion annually for $1.7 billion of annual revenue.

## Risk Assessment

**Youth-safety litigation moved from claimed to adjudicated.** The Q2 10-Q risk factors now state that juries "have returned liability verdicts against us in two trials." On March 25, 2026 a Los Angeles County jury awarded $6 million apportioned 70% Meta / 30% YouTube; on March 24, 2026 a New Mexico jury returned a $375 million civil penalty. The dollar amounts are immaterial against $200 billion of revenue; the liability finding and the apportionment percentage give plaintiffs a valuation anchor across a claim inventory that includes more than 200,000 individual Instagram arbitration demands filed since November 2024. A separate May 2026 New Mexico bench trial saw the AG seek $953 million in abatement. Trials were calendared for August 12, 2026 (MDL state AGs, four states plus a federal disgorgement claim asserted by all 29 MDL state AGs), September 8, 2026 (New Mexico privacy case, where the AG stated intent to seek up to $62.85 billion), and October 28, 2026 (two further user bellwethers). Meta's own disclosure contemplates aggregate exposure "up to an aggregate of up to hundreds of billions of dollars" that "could be material to the financial condition of the company."

Countervailing evidence deserves equal weight: the first school-district bellwether settled in May 2026, the second user bellwether was voluntarily dismissed by the plaintiff on July 27, both March verdicts are under or subject to appeal, and plaintiffs' demanded figures are advocacy positions rather than expected values. The $2,400 million Q2 charge is the first clear P&L manifestation and, with three trial dates falling immediately after the filing, has a meaningful probability of recurring in Q3 and Q4.

**Off-balance-sheet commitments.** Leases signed but not commenced rose from $103,770M to $278,990M in six months; non-cancelable contractual commitments from $131,050M to $349,310M. Roughly $53,520M is due in the remainder of 2026 and $81,650M in 2027. Summing disclosed categories plus the July data center event gives approximately $696 billion of gross undiscounted obligations outside $84 billion of funded debt. Two caveats: I have not verified the categories are mutually exclusive, and these are undiscounted obligations spanning up to 30 years rather than debt-equivalent present values — a present-value framing produces a substantially smaller figure. The defensible conclusion is narrower than the headline: debt-to-equity of 0.32x and a safe-zone Z-Score materially understate committed capital, and the relevant question is whether multi-decade obligations of this scale are serviceable out of an advertising business generating roughly $87 billion of annual EBIT.

**Unconsolidated joint ventures.** The Louisiana venture (entered October 2025) sees Meta hold a 20% interest in an entity with approximately $27 billion of estimated development cost, funded pro rata, with leases commencing 2029 and an initial commitment of approximately $12,310M. Meta concluded it is not the primary beneficiary because remarketing decisions — future tenant negotiation and individual property sales — have the most significant impact on economic performance and are not controlled by Meta. Carrying value is $2,920M, while disclosed maximum exposure to loss is $46,030M, including residual value guarantees with an approximately $28 billion aggregate threshold against which no liability is recorded. Other unconsolidated VIEs add $6,410M. A July 2026 exclusivity agreement would replicate the template in El Paso — 20% stake, approximately $2.3 billion of contributed held-for-sale assets, a roughly $1 billion one-time distribution to Meta, and RVGs with approximately $13 billion of maximum exposure. I do not infer motive from the structure: partnering with infrastructure capital secures far more capacity than Meta's own balance sheet and construction organization could deliver, transfers genuine remarketing risk, and the exposures are disclosed with specificity. The counterparty is not named in the filings and third-party attributions conflict; treat it as unidentified.

**Tax.** Gross unrecognized tax benefits rose $2.29 billion in six months to $18,740M, with $12,730M that would benefit the provision if realized and $2,970M of accrued interest and penalties. In September 2025 the IRS issued a notice for 2017-2019 asserting approximately $15.89 billion of additional tax plus interest and penalties, petitioned to Tax Court in December 2025.

**Governance.** Meta is a controlled company; Class B shares carry ten votes and Zuckerberg holds majority voting power. The pay-versus-performance disclosure lists Revenue as the sole and company-selected performance measure, with no profitability, free cash flow or return-on-capital metric. That table has a prescribed format and may not capture the full incentive scorecard, so the claim is established for that disclosure specifically. Cumulative TSR of 243.32 compares to a disclosed peer group TSR of 445.33, with the gap widening sharply in FY2025. Zuckerberg receives no equity compensation ($25.1 million total, all actually paid), so his alignment derives from an existing ownership stake — arguably purer than any metric-based plan, though it also means shareholders have no mechanism to redirect capital allocation.

Insider activity shows systematic selling with no open-market purchases: near-weekly COO sales between $600 and $662, a new 80,000-share 10b5-1 plan adopted by the Chief Product Officer on May 19, 2026, small director sales. The cadence is characteristic of pre-scheduled plans and is weak evidence in either direction. The mildly informative observation is the absence of buying with the stock 30% below its high; at mega-cap companies insiders rarely buy, so this provides no offsetting bullish evidence rather than a bearish signal. Separately, 20 million Class A options were issued in H1 2026 at a weighted-average $2,788 strike, roughly 5x the current price, with service and market conditions and only $471 million of unrecognized compensation — probability-weighted near zero for dilution purposes; the recipient is not disclosed.

Risk factors score of 4.3: the litigation calendar contains three near-dated events with wide outcome distributions, the commitment escalation is recent and rapid, the WhatsApp API interim measure is already in force, and the market does not appear to be pricing any of it.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is priced for calm. Near-term ATM IV is 31.5% against 38.3% trailing realized volatility — a −6.8 point spread and the 1st percentile within the current chain. Term structure is in contango (31.5% near, 38.8% at six months). The one-month tenor implies a 6.1% move and the three-month tenor 10.2%, which spans the August 12 and September 8 trial dates. Put/call volume of 0.62 is call-tilted and near-term skew of −1.5% is balanced. The heavy unusual activity is concentrated entirely in the one-day August 3 expiry and reads as same-day pinning rather than positioning.

The only meaningful signal is at the back end: six-month skew of −4.4% versus −1.5% near-term indicates whatever hedging demand exists is being expressed in longer-dated puts. Combined with 1.7% short interest and 1.9 days to cover, positioning is uncrowded and complacent. The practical implication for a holder is that protection across the litigation calendar is unusually cheap relative to realized volatility — favorable hedging economics, not evidence of safety.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $790 (+42%)

Revenue compounds at 25%+ through 2027 as ARPP gains continue at 20%+, driven by ranking improvements and commerce demand. FY2027 capex guidance is set flat to FY2026 rather than escalated again, capping the depreciation ramp and allowing incremental gross profit of roughly $40 billion annually to outrun a $21-31 billion incremental depreciation load. Operating margin stabilizes in the mid-30s as the 8,000-person reduction annualizes and legal charges normalize. Free cash flow inflects in 2027, the buyback resumes against $25,030 million of untouched authorization, and share count resumes shrinking. Youth-safety exposure settles in the low tens of billions, spread over years — large in absolute terms, manageable against $145-155 billion of annual operating cash flow. On $36 of FY2027 EPS at 22x, roughly a 20% discount to Alphabet's current multiple, the equity is worth about $790, consistent with the consensus mean of $778.68.

### Bear Case — $410 (−26%)

FY2027 capex is guided above $145 billion, extending rather than resetting the depreciation ramp. Revenue growth decelerates from 28% toward the mid-teens as ARPP gains mature and youth-access restrictions bite a funnel already growing 3%, breaking the arithmetic that lets revenue outrun depreciation. The EC's free-access mandate on the WhatsApp Business API caps business messaging before it scales, removing the second revenue engine. Legal charges recur across Q3 and Q4 2026, and an adverse outcome in the August MDL state AG trial or the September New Mexico privacy trial forces a reserve in the tens of billions. FY2027 EPS lands near $27-28 rather than $34.41; at 14-15x, reflecting the capital intensity and litigation overhang, the equity is worth $390-430. Dilution of roughly 1.4% annually compounds the per-share damage with no buyback offset.

### Base Case — $630 (+13%)

Revenue growth moderates to roughly 20-24% in 2027. Margin stabilizes in the low-to-mid 30s as severance annualizes and headcount reductions offset part of the depreciation ramp. FY2026 free cash flow lands in the $5-15 billion range and improves modestly in 2027 without returning to FY2025 levels; the buyback stays suspended through at least mid-2027. Litigation produces recurring charges in the low single-digit billions per quarter without a catastrophic verdict. FY2027 EPS approximates consensus at $33-34; at 18.5x — a discount to peers reflecting unverifiable capex returns and an active litigation calendar — that is roughly $620-630.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** This is the fifth consecutive hold on a declining score trajectory (7.2 in April to 5.78 now), and the Q2 print confirms rather than reverses the decline. The specific fundamental deterioration justifying the lower scores is identifiable: a 16% EPS miss ending a three-quarter beat streak, operating income down 8% on 28% revenue growth, free cash flow down 91%, the buyback halted while share count rose 0.7% in six months, non-debt commitments tripling in six months, and consensus estimates falling across all four forecast horizons. The price decline since the prior review is a rational response to those developments, not an overreaction to be bought reflexively. Equally, at 16.1x next-year earnings with 28% revenue growth, 28.5% ROE and the FTC break-up tail removed, this is not an AVOID. The classification is uncertain because the central question — whether $130-145 billion of annual capex earns its cost of capital in a business where the output is unmeasurable — cannot be resolved with available disclosure.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. FY2027 capex guidance at the Q4 2026 print (late January 2027) set at or below $145 billion, with management framing 2026 as the peak-spend year. This is the single most important variable; it converts the depreciation ramp from compounding to one-time.
2. Two consecutive quarters of operating margin at or above 34% excluding legal charges, alongside quarterly free cash flow above $10 billion — evidence that the accrual-versus-cash capex gap is closing without margin giving way further.
3. Resumption of share repurchases at any material scale, which would end the dilution and signal management's own view that free cash flow has inflected.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. An adverse verdict in the August 12 MDL state AG trial or the September 8 New Mexico privacy trial resulting in a reserve above roughly $10 billion, or legal charges exceeding $2.5 billion in each of Q3 and Q4 2026.
2. Q3 or Q4 2026 revenue growth decelerating below 20% year over year, which breaks the arithmetic that allows revenue to outrun the depreciation ramp. Q3 guidance of $61-64 billion implies 26-32%; a print at the low end with weak Q4 guidance would qualify.
3. FY2027 capex guided above $150 billion, or a further tripling-scale increase in leases signed but not commenced, without a corresponding acceleration in revenue.
4. Any material change in the WhatsApp Business API remedy that extends free general-purpose-AI access into consumer messaging or advertising surfaces.

**Reassessment timeline:** the August 12 and September 8 trial dates are the first checkpoints; the Q3 2026 earnings release in late October is the first full fundamental update, and it coincides with the October 28 user bellwether trials. The decisive event is the Q4 print and FY2027 capex guidance in late January 2027.

**For existing holders:** near-term implied volatility at the 1st percentile of its own chain, 6.8 points below realized volatility, makes downside protection across the September trial calendar unusually inexpensive relative to the binary risk in that window.

## Conclusion

Meta's advertising business is accelerating and its financial resilience is not in question — $64.1 billion of H1 operating cash flow, a 2.23x current ratio, net cash of roughly $6.3 billion, no covenants, and no meaningful maturities before 2030. What has changed is that the equity no longer converts that cash flow into shareholder returns. Free cash flow fell 91% in the quarter, the buyback stopped with authorization untouched, share count is rising for the first time in roughly a decade, and roughly $700 billion of gross undiscounted lease and purchase obligations has been committed against an advertising business generating approximately $87 billion of annual EBIT. Two juries have now found Meta liable in youth-safety cases, and three further trials were calendared for August, September and October 2026 while the options market prices a 6.1% one-month move.

The multiple already reflects a substantial portion of this: 17.3x current-year and 16.1x next-year consensus, roughly a 40% discount to Alphabet, and about 18.0-18.7x on tax-normalized trailing earnings. That discount is not obviously wrong. Meta's compute produces no external revenue line, so the return on $130-145 billion of annual spending can never be isolated and verified, which is why a permanent valuation discount is a rational market response rather than an error to arbitrage. Base case $630 (+13%), bull $790 (+42%), bear $410 (−26%). The distribution is wide, the near-term catalysts on the calendar are negative, and the decisive information arrives with FY2027 capex guidance in late January 2027. Hold what you own, hedge the litigation window cheaply, and require either flat 2027 capex guidance or a demonstrated free-cash-flow inflection before adding.
