**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Mirum Pharmaceuticals is a commercial-stage rare-disease biopharmaceutical company that has built one of the faster-growing franchises in its segment. Revenue grew from $186M (2023) to $336M (2024) to $521M (2025), a 55% year-over-year acceleration in 2025, and management raised 2026 guidance to $660-680M (roughly 27-30% growth) on the Q1 2026 print. The business turned operating cash-flow positive at $55.8M in 2025 and generated GAAP net income in Q3 2025. The headline Q1 2026 net loss of $790.2M (-$13.43 EPS) is almost entirely a non-cash $726.3M in-process R&D write-off tied to the Bluejay acquisition plus ~$34.7M one-time stock compensation; the underlying commercial business is near cash-flow breakeven and growing.

The investment debate reduces to a single fact: LIVMARLI, which carried $360M of 2025 sales (~69% of revenue), has no composition-of-matter patent. Protection rests on method-of-use and formulation patents, the weaker forms, and the company is already in active Hatch-Waxman litigation against four generic filers (Sandoz, Hetero, Biophore, Zydus) in Delaware. A 30-month stay runs through March 29, 2029, and Sandoz has filed invalidity and non-infringement counterclaims. The bull and bear cases largely hinge on the durability of this single revenue stream and the speed at which the pipeline can diversify away from it.

The pipeline has de-risked materially in 2026. Volixibat met its PSC primary endpoint (VISTAS) and previously met its PBC endpoint (VANTAGE interim); brelovitug's Phase 2b HDV portion met its primary endpoint; and zilurgisertib (FOP) arrived from Incyte with an NDA already accepted under Priority Review and a PDUFA date of September 26, 2026. This converts a single-product story into a potential multi-launch story over 2026-2027. At ~$92, after a round-trip from a $114.99 May high, the stock prices in much of the growth while leaving the patent risk live. The combination of a genuine growth acceleration and an unresolved binary patent overhang supports a HOLD with a positive growth lean rather than a conviction long.

## Company Overview

Mirum (HQ Foster City, CA; ~369 employees) operates a rare liver and rare genetic disease franchise built on a hub-and-spoke commercial model: a specialized salesforce, patient-services hub, and payer-access infrastructure aimed at small populations, paired with serial business development that buys and in-licenses late-stage and commercial assets.

Commercial portfolio:
- **LIVMARLI (maralixibat)** — oral, minimally-absorbed IBAT inhibitor for cholestatic pruritus in Alagille syndrome and PFIC. The growth engine. Approved US/EU/Canada/Japan (Japan via Takeda).
- **Bile Acid Medicines** — CHOLBAM (cholic acid) and CTEXLI (chenodiol, CTX, approved Feb 2025), acquired in the 2023 Travere asset deal. $161M of 2025 sales.

Pipeline (late-stage, multiple near-term catalysts):
- **Volixibat** — IBAT inhibitor for PSC (VISTAS, primary endpoint met) and PBC (VANTAGE, PBC primary endpoint met in 2024 interim). PSC NDA expected H2 2026.
- **Brelovitug (BJT-778)** — anti-HBsAg monoclonal antibody for chronic hepatitis delta (HDV), acquired in the Jan 2026 Bluejay deal with worldwide rights. AZURE-1 Phase 2b portion met its endpoint; AZURE-1/-4 topline H2 2026.
- **Zilurgisertib** — oral ALK2 inhibitor for fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva (FOP), in-licensed from Incyte. NDA accepted, Priority Review, PDUFA September 26, 2026.
- **MRM-3379** — PDE4D inhibitor for Fragile X; topline 2027.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue trajectory is the clearest part of the story. Sales grew $186M → $336.4M → $521.3M across 2023-2025, with Q1 2026 product sales of $159.9M (LIVMARLI +55% YoY to $113.8M; Bile Acid +20% YoY to $46.1M).

Profitability is improving. Net loss narrowed to $23.4M (2025) from $87.9M (2024) and $163.4M (2023). Operating cash flow was $55.8M in 2025 versus $10.3M in 2024. The 2025 cost base: COGS $100.2M, R&D $186.2M, SG&A $257.0M.

The Q1 2026 reported net loss is accounting noise. Accumulated deficit jumped to $1,457.7M from $667.5M for the same reason. The adjusted Q1 2026 loss of ~$0.39 beat consensus of -$0.40. The standardized -$13.43 EPS and the -6.05x trailing P/E misrepresent the company; the trailing figures should be discarded in favor of the operating run-rate.

Balance sheet: cash and investments of $420.6M at Q1 2026 (up from $391.4M), against $316.2M of 4% convertible notes maturing May 2029. The conversion trigger was met (stock above 130% of conversion price), making the notes convertible in Q1 2026, a dilution and settlement overhang. Federal NOLs of $185.9M provide future tax shielding.

Two watch items. Stock-based compensation is heavy ($57.5M in Q1 2026, ~$38M+ recurring across R&D and SG&A), which inflates both per-share dilution and the gap between GAAP and economic results. Revenue is lumpy quarter-to-quarter because of large periodic Takeda and distributor orders booked on variable-consideration estimates; this drives noisy earnings surprises (two beats and two misses over the last four quarters, with a +130% beat in Q3 2025 and large GAAP misses in Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 driven by one-time items).

## Growth Analysis

The reported earnings-estimate revisions look catastrophic on their face: current-year EPS revised to -$16.33 (from -$1.63 ninety days ago), a -902% move. This is mechanical — analysts are folding the one-time Bluejay IPR&D charge into the full-year line. The next-year EPS estimate of -$0.56 versus +$0.02 ninety days ago is more telling: even stripped of the one-time charge, consensus expects continued losses next year, reflecting heavy pipeline spend, integration costs, and stock comp rather than commercial deterioration. The revenue line, not the GAAP EPS line, is the relevant growth signal here, and revenue guidance was raised.

Growth durability rests on two pillars. First, the in-line franchise: LIVMARLI and the Bile Acid portfolio continue compounding, with the LIVMARLI EXPAND Phase 3 (additional cholestatic conditions) reading out Q4 2026 as a potential label-expansion catalyst. Second, the pipeline diversification: zilurgisertib targets an ultra-rare ~300 US-patient FOP market, in-licensed at a modest $16M upfront plus milestones and mid-to-high single-digit royalties to Incyte; volixibat PSC NDA in H2 2026 with potential H1 2027 launch; and brelovitug entering a US market with no FDA-approved HDV therapy if AZURE Phase 3 succeeds in 2026. Four catalysts cluster in the next 12-18 months, which is the genuine attraction of the name.

I do not treat the headline estimate-revision figures as a bearish signal here, because they are an artifact of one-time acquisition accounting rather than degrading commercial fundamentals. The revenue-guidance raise is the cleaner forward indicator, and it is positive.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing multiples are meaningless given the IPR&D charge, so the work must be done on revenue and forward earnings power. At ~$92 the market cap is ~$5.6B. On 2026 guidance, the stock trades at ~8.3-8.5x current-year sales. For a rare-disease franchise growing ~27-30% with operating cash flow turning positive, that is neither cheap nor stretched in isolation; it sits in the middle of the commercial rare-disease cohort, which typically commands 6-12x forward sales depending on patent runway and pipeline depth.

The valuation problem is that the multiple has to be discounted for two structural issues that pure-play rare-disease comps with composition-of-matter protection do not carry: (1) LIVMARLI's weak patent structure with a litigation cliff that becomes acute around 2029, and (2) recurring equity dilution from the serial-acquisition model (two raises tied to Bluejay, a $200M untapped ATM, an effective WKSI shelf, plus convertible-note conversion). Both compress the fair multiple relative to a clean grower.

Analyst targets are wide and well above spot: mean $144.21, median $141, low $115, high $185. With 14 analysts at strong buy or buy and none at hold or sell, the sell-side is uniformly constructive and the implied upside to even the low target is ~25%. I would not treat these targets as floors here. The guardrail applies: this is a stock priced for growth but without a clean patent runway, and the targets embed both the pipeline optionality and an assumption that the LIVMARLI patent estate survives litigation. They are an expression of the bull case, not independent confirmation. The stock has already round-tripped from $114.99 (May 13 high) to ~$92 in three weeks, and the most recent leg lower (June 1-3, from $101 to $92) came without any new fundamental news, which signals that the marginal holder is repricing the same patent/dilution concerns the targets gloss over.

Net: the valuation is reasonable for the growth but not asymmetric once the patent and dilution discounts are applied. A 5.5 reflects fair-but-not-mispriced.

## Competitive Landscape

The IBAT-inhibitor class is the battleground. **Ipsen's odevixibat (Bylvay/Kayfanda)** is the principal direct competitor in pediatric ALGS/PFIC, head-to-head with LIVMARLI. **GSK's linerixibat** is ahead of volixibat in PBC, filed with approvals expected in 2026, which threatens the PBC opportunity directly. In HDV, there are no FDA-approved US therapies (Gilead's bulevirtide is EU-approved and FDA-pending; Vir's tobevibart is in development), so brelovitug enters genuine white space if Phase 3 reads out positively. In FOP, zilurgisertib would address an ultra-rare market with no approved oral ALK2 inhibitor.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. On the positive side, the company is widening its footprint across multiple rare indications, reducing single-asset dependence, and entering at least one white-space market (HDV). On the negative side, its anchor product faces a credible IBAT competitor and a near-patent-cliff, and its lead pipeline PBC asset is behind GSK. The Bile Acid products (CHOLBAM, CTEXLI) have no patent protection (CTEXLI only orphan exclusivity), exposing $161M of revenue to compounded and generic entry over time. The moat is real in commercial infrastructure and rare-disease access, but it is not a durable molecular moat, which caps the competitive-position score at a 6.

## Risk Assessment

**LIVMARLI generic litigation — the dominant risk.** A company deriving most of its revenue from a drug with no composition-of-matter patent, already litigating against four ANDA filers, is structurally different from the clean grower the revenue line implies. Method-of-use and formulation patents are the weaker forms, with documented section viii / off-label workarounds for generics. An adverse ruling, or a settlement permitting earlier entry, would be materially damaging and is the single thing to monitor.

**No exclusivity on Bile Acid Medicines.** CHOLBAM and CTEXLI lack patent protection, exposing the Bile Acid revenue to compounded/generic competition.

**Integration and clinical risk.** Brelovitug and zilurgisertib were developed entirely by prior sponsors; key HDV Phase 3 readouts (AZURE-1/-4) are H2 2026 and confirmatory studies are needed for accelerated-approval pathways. The serial-acquisition model (Travere, Enthorin, Bluejay, Incyte) generates recurring dilution and large milestone liabilities (up to $235M Travere, $217.5M Enthorin, $200M Bluejay, plus Incyte royalties).

**Capital structure.** Convertible notes are now in-the-money for conversion; the $200M ATM and WKSI shelf signal continued willingness to issue equity, a per-share headwind even as the business scales.

**Other.** A prior FY2023 material weakness (inventory existence/valuation; intangible fair-value review), stated remediated but relevant given acquisition-heavy accounting. IRA Medicare price negotiation, MFN pricing initiatives, and pharma tariffs are sector-level overhangs.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV is 53.8% versus 1-year historical vol of 44.6% (rich by ~9.2 points), with elevated put-call skew of 21.3% at the front-month (June 18) expiry and an implied move of ±8.9%. The skew reverses to roughly flat at the 3-month and 6-month tenors (-2.4% and -3.2%), and the term structure is in contango (53.8% near vs 75.5% at six months). The market is paying up for near-term downside protection into the next event window while pricing larger absolute uncertainty further out, consistent with the H2 2026 catalyst cluster (HDV topline, PSC NDA, EXPAND readout, September PDUFA). The 6-month 37.2% implied move shows the options market is treating those catalysts as binary. Short interest is elevated at 17.6% of float with 7.3 days to cover, confirming meaningful institutional bearish positioning, which creates two-sided risk: squeeze potential on a positive PDUFA or HDV print, and confirmation of the patent/dilution bear case on disappointment.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$140-160).** Revenue compounds toward and beyond the 2026 guide; the pipeline delivers a multi-launch sequence — zilurgisertib approval (Sept 2026), volixibat PSC and PBC launches (2027), brelovitug HDV into white space (2027) — each of which dilutes LIVMARLI concentration and re-rates the multiple toward a diversified rare-disease platform. The 30-month stay holds, and a favorable or settled LIVMARLI ruling removes the patent overhang. Operating leverage drives sustained profitability. Sell-side mean of $144 and high of $185 reflect this path.

**Bear case (~$55-65).** The LIVMARLI patent estate proves as weak as its structure suggests; an adverse ruling or early-entry settlement crystallizes a 2029 revenue cliff that the market begins discounting now. Pipeline readouts disappoint (HDV Phase 3, EXPAND), competition from Ipsen, GSK, and Gilead compresses share, and continued equity issuance dilutes per-share value. At that point the stock de-rates toward 4-5x impaired sales.

**Base case (~$100-110).** Revenue meets guidance, at least one or two pipeline catalysts land positively, the patent litigation stays unresolved but on track (stay intact to 2029), and the stock grinds modestly higher from $92 as catalysts de-risk, capped by dilution and the unresolved patent question.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a positive growth lean (growth_hold). The 12-month thesis is constructive — a catalyst-rich stretch with revenue compounding — but the unresolved LIVMARLI patent litigation and recurring dilution prevent a conviction long at ~$92.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A favorable LIVMARLI litigation ruling or settlement that extends effective exclusivity meaningfully beyond 2029, removing the central overhang.
- Zilurgisertib FOP approval at the September 26, 2026 PDUFA plus a positive brelovitug AZURE Phase 3 topline (H2 2026), which together would materially reduce LIVMARLI revenue dependence and validate the diversification thesis.
- A pullback toward the high-$60s/low-$70s on no fundamental deterioration, which would restore asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse LIVMARLI patent ruling, denial of the 30-month stay benefit, or settlement permitting generic entry materially before 2029.
- A 2026 revenue-guidance cut, or LIVMARLI growth decelerating below ~20% YoY (a sign of competitive erosion ahead of generics).
- Failure of a key pipeline catalyst (HDV Phase 3 miss, zilurgisertib complete response letter) combined with another dilutive equity raise.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026) for guidance confirmation, then the H2 2026 catalyst cluster — volixibat PSC NDA filing, AZURE-1/-4 HDV topline, the September 26, 2026 zilurgisertib PDUFA, and the Q4 2026 EXPAND readout. The September PDUFA is the cleanest near-term forcing event.

## Conclusion

Mirum is a high-quality, fast-growing rare-disease franchise inflecting to operating profitability, with a meaningfully de-risked pipeline and four catalysts clustered across the next 12-18 months. That is the genuine attraction, and it is why the sell-side is uniformly bullish with targets 25-100% above spot. The offsetting reality is that nearly 70% of revenue comes from a drug with no composition-of-matter patent, in active litigation against four generic filers, with the protective stay running only to March 2029, layered on a serial-acquisition model that keeps issuing equity. At ~$92, the stock prices in much of the growth while leaving the patent question and dilution unresolved. The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric: real upside if the catalysts land and the patent estate holds, real downside if the litigation goes the other way. HOLD, with a positive growth lean and a willingness to upgrade on patent resolution, a clean pipeline win, or a pullback that restores the margin of safety.
