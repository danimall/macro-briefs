## Executive Summary

McDonald's is the world's dominant quick-service restaurant brand with 45,356 units generating $139.4B in systemwide sales. The franchise-heavy model (95% franchised) produces $10.6B annual operating cash flow at 46% operating margins, effectively functioning as a real estate and brand licensing operation with minimal direct exposure to labor and food cost inflation. The stock trades at $285 (24x forward earnings), down 16% from its 52-week high of $342, offering a 2.6% dividend yield with 50 consecutive years of increases.

The fundamental concern preventing a more constructive view is threefold: (1) U.S. comparable sales growth is driven by pricing/average check, not guest count growth — a pattern that historically reverses when consumers reach affordability thresholds; (2) estimate revisions are uniformly negative across all time horizons (-4.1% current quarter over 90 days, -0.5% full year), indicating analyst expectations are deteriorating; and (3) the current stagflationary macro environment (Core PCE 3.2%, consumer savings rate 3.6%) directly pressures the low-to-middle income demographic that constitutes McDonald's core customer base. The stock is a reasonable income vehicle with defensive characteristics, but the path to meaningful capital appreciation requires a consumer spending recovery that is unlikely in H2 2026.

## Company Overview

McDonald's operates the world's largest QSR system across 100+ countries. Revenue breaks into franchise fees (61%), company-operated sales (36%), and other revenues including technology platform fees (3%). The company owns 56% of land and 80% of buildings for consolidated restaurants, creating a real estate portfolio that underpins the franchise model's stability. Key growth drivers include unit expansion (targeting 50,000 restaurants by end-2027 from 45,356 today), digital engagement (210M active loyalty users generating $37B in systemwide sales), and delivery (41,000 restaurants enabled).

The "Accelerating the Arches" strategy centers on four pillars: Digital, Delivery, Drive Thru, and Restaurant Development. The company is simultaneously executing a major technology transformation (universal software platform across all units) and organizational restructuring ($623M in charges since 2023, expected to complete 2027).

The company-operated margin decline is the clearest signal that direct operational exposure to labor/food inflation is punitive. The franchise model is correctly insulating shareholder returns from these pressures, but it also means U.S. consumer traffic weakness is real and being partially masked by pricing actions.

## Growth Analysis

**Unit Growth:** McDonald's plans to add ~2,600 net new restaurants in 2026 (4.5% growth), targeting 50,000 by end-2027. This represents the fastest unit growth period in company history and provides a mechanical revenue tailwind of 3-4% annually independent of comparable sales.

**Comparable Sales:** Full-year 2025 global comps of +3.1% represent a recovery from -0.1% in 2024. However, the composition matters: U.S. growth was only +2.1%, driven by average check (pricing), not guest counts. The Q4 2025 acceleration (+6.8% U.S.) provides some optimism, but the nine-month 2025 U.S. comp was only +0.5%.

**Digital/Loyalty:** 210M active users generating $37B in systemwide sales (27% of total systemwide sales). The target of 250M by 2027 provides incremental marketing efficiency and transaction frequency. "Other revenues" (technology platform fees) grew 53% to $647M in 2025 from $316M in 2023, representing a new high-margin revenue stream.

**Estimate Revision Signal:** Current quarter estimates have been revised down 4.1% over 90 days, with full-year estimates down 0.5%. No period shows upward revisions. This indicates consensus expectations are deteriorating, albeit modestly. The earnings surprise record (2 beats, 2 misses over 4 quarters) provides no directional confidence.

**Growth Assessment:** The combination of 4-5% unit growth, 1-2% same-store sales growth, and margin stability produces a structural EPS growth rate of 6-8% annually. Consensus estimates reflect this: $13.20 (FY26) and $14.36 (FY27) imply 10% and 9% growth respectively. At 24x forward earnings, this growth rate is fairly compensated but not underpriced.

- Peer median P/E: 28.8x (SBUX 80.5x, CMG 28.9x, YUM 28.8x, DRI 20.7x, DPZ 19.1x)
- Dividend yield: 2.6% (vs. peer median 2.3%)
- FCF yield: ~3.5%

**Relative Positioning:** McDonald's trades at a ~17% discount to the peer median P/E of 28.8x. This discount partially reflects: (a) MCD's more mature growth profile vs. CMG/DPZ, (b) consumer perception challenges around value, and (c) the broader QSR sector facing traffic headwinds. However, MCD's operating model quality (46% margins, 95% franchised) and defensive characteristics arguably deserve a premium relative to DRI/DPZ.

**Absolute Valuation:**
- At $285, applying 22x to FY26 consensus EPS of $13.20 = $290 (fair value, within 2%)
- Bull case: consumer recovery + unit growth acceleration → 24x on $14.36 (FY27) = $345
- Bear case: consumer recession + traffic deterioration → 19x on $12.50 = $238

The stock is trading approximately at fair value on consensus estimates. Upside requires either multiple expansion (difficult in a rising-rate environment) or estimate upgrades (currently trending in the opposite direction). The 2.6% dividend yield provides some floor valuation support but is not exceptional relative to treasuries yielding above 4%.

**Analyst Consensus:** Median target $350 (23% upside), range $250-$407. The distribution of 4 Strong Buy, 15 Buy, 16 Hold, 1 Sell shows balanced sentiment without strong directional conviction.

## Competitive Landscape

McDonald's competitive position is structural and widening in several dimensions:

**Advantages expanding:**
- Digital/loyalty scale: 210M active users is 3-5x larger than any QSR competitor's loyalty program
- Delivery infrastructure: 41,000 restaurants enabled, largest QSR delivery network globally
- Drive-thru footprint: 29,000 locations creates a permanent convenience moat
- Purchasing scale: negotiating power on food/packaging with 45,000+ units
- Real estate portfolio: irreplaceable asset base in high-traffic locations globally

**Competitive threats:**
- Value wars intensifying: Burger King, Taco Bell, Arby's all launching aggressive sub-$5 deals, compressing industry margins
- Share of stomach competition from convenience stores, grocery prepared foods, and meal kit services
- GLP-1/weight-loss drug adoption potentially reducing meal frequency (explicitly acknowledged in 10-K risk factors)

**Market share trajectory:** McDonald's U.S. market share has been stable-to-slightly-growing, with the digital platform and loyalty driving frequency among engaged customers. The value perception challenge is the primary risk to share maintenance — customers citing "too expensive" for fast food is a qualitatively different threat than historical competition.

## Risk Assessment

**Consumer Spending Deterioration (Primary near-term risk):**
With savings rates at 3.6%, Core PCE at 3.2%, and gas prices elevated, McDonald's core demographic faces direct budget pressure. The company's response (value meals, $4 breakfast) compresses margins on the very transactions needed for traffic growth. This creates a fundamental tension: grow traffic via discounting and compress margins, or maintain pricing and lose traffic. Current evidence suggests the company is choosing pricing, which works until the gap between QSR and at-home food costs narrows sufficiently to shift behavior.

**Interest Rate Exposure:**
$40B in debt with interest guided to increase 4-6% in 2026. Each 100bps on the portion coming due adds ~$100-200M in annual interest expense. Coverage remains adequate (7.8x) but is declining from higher levels.

**International Political Risk:**
Ongoing Middle East boycott effects and new tariff-related anti-American sentiment risk represent persistent drags on IDL segment performance. These are difficult to quantify but have clearly impacted results.

**Technology Execution:**
The universal software platform deployment across 45,000+ restaurants is among the most complex technology transformations in the restaurant industry. Failure or delays would impair the digital growth thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($330-$345, +16-21%):**
- Q4 2025's +6.8% U.S. comp momentum carries into 2026
- Consumer resilience proves better than feared; savings rate stabilizes
- 2,600 new units provide 4-5% mechanical revenue growth
- Digital/loyalty drives guest count inflection
- Restructuring charges roll off in 2027, providing EPS tailwind
- 24x $14.36 (FY27 EPS) = $345

**Bear Case ($238-$255, -11% to -16%):**
- U.S. consumer recession materializes (55-60% probability per macro assessment)
- Guest counts decline as pricing reaches affordability ceiling
- Value wars compress margins across the system
- International boycott effects intensify
- 19x $12.50 EPS = $238

**Why Not BUY Despite 6.5+ Score:**

The specific, falsifiable reasons preventing a BUY recommendation:

1. **Negative estimate revisions across all horizons.** A BUY requires either rising estimates or a valuation discount wide enough to compensate for estimate risk. At 21.6x FY26 consensus, the discount to peers (28.8x median) partially compensates, but the peer comparison is distorted by SBUX at 80.5x — excluding SBUX, the peer median drops to ~24x, making MCD's discount only ~10%.

2. **Consumer environment directly hostile to MCD's core demographic.** The upcoming consumer earnings cluster (May 19-21: HD, TGT, WMT) may confirm deterioration that would further pressure MCD estimates. A BUY ahead of this cluster without positive estimate momentum would be premature.

3. **U.S. growth composition is pricing, not traffic.** Until there is evidence that guest counts are recovering, the sustainability of U.S. comparable sales is questionable. Pricing-driven growth in a value-sensitive category has historically reverted when the gap between QSR and at-home food costs narrows.

These concerns are falsifiable: if Q1 2026 earnings show positive U.S. guest count growth, if estimates stabilize or reverse higher, or if consumer spending data improves (May 19-21 cluster), the thesis upgrades to BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
1. U.S. comparable sales show positive guest count contribution (not just average check) for two consecutive quarters
2. Estimate revisions stabilize and turn positive (30-day revision >0% for current year)
3. Stock declines to $260-265 (20x FY26 EPS of $13.20), providing sufficient margin of safety

**Downgrade Triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of negative U.S. comparable sales
2. Franchisee financial stress indicators emerge (closure rate acceleration, development slowdown)
3. FY26 EPS consensus falls below $12.50 (>5.5% downward revision from current)

**Reassessment Timeline:**
- Next earnings report (Q1 2026, likely mid-May 2026) — primary catalyst for thesis revision
- May 19-21 consumer earnings cluster (HD, TGT, WMT) — macro read on consumer health
- Q2 2026 results (July 2026) — first read on whether value strategy is driving traffic

**Income Thesis:** The 2.6% dividend yield on a 50-year consecutive increase streak, backed by $7.2B FCF (2.4x coverage of dividend obligation), provides reliable income generation. The defensive franchise model limits downside in recession scenarios while maintaining the dividend.

## Conclusion

McDonald's is a high-quality defensive franchise with structural competitive advantages that the market is pricing approximately fairly at 21.6x FY26 EPS. The 2.6% dividend yield and declining share count provide a 4-5% total shareholder yield floor. However, negative estimate revisions, consumer spending headwinds targeting MCD's core demographic, and U.S. growth driven by pricing rather than traffic preclude a BUY recommendation. The stock is a reasonable hold for income-oriented portfolios; capital appreciation depends on consumer spending stabilization that current macro conditions do not support. The 16% discount to the 52-week high reflects real fundamental uncertainty, not a market overreaction to noise.
