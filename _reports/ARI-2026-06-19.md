**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ARI no longer functions as an operating commercial mortgage REIT. On January 27, 2026 the company signed an agreement to sell its entire commercial real estate loan portfolio to Athene Holding Ltd., an Apollo affiliate; the sale closed on April 24, 2026 for approximately $8.6 billion in cash (99.7% of total loan commitment amount), and proceeds repaid all secured facilities, the Barclays private securitization, and all corporate debt. On June 15, 2026 the Board resolved to liquidate and dissolve the company and return capital, declaring a $3.75 per share special dividend payable July 15, 2026. The standard data classification (Sector: Real Estate, Industry: Mortgage REITs, P/E 13.3x, dividend yield 9.2%) describes a company that has effectively ceased to exist in its prior form. Any analysis keyed to net interest margin, book growth, or dividend coverage misreads the situation.

The correct framework is residual net asset value versus price. Post-close book value per share was stated at $12.15 (after the $169.3 million preferred liquidation preference), essentially unchanged from $12.14 at year-end 2025 and $12.01 at March 31, 2026, confirming the loan book was sold at carrying value. Against a current price of ~$10.83, the implied discount to stated book is roughly 11%. The $3.75 special dividend is largely a return of capital, after which the stub residual consists of approximately $1.3 billion of cash, ~$0.9 billion of real estate owned net of ~$0.4 billion of REO debt, a small retained loan, and deeply-reserved impaired assets. The realizable value of three illiquid REO properties (a Washington D.C. luxury hotel, a Brooklyn multifamily development in lease-up, an Atlanta hotel) and the legacy problem loans, plus the fairness of a captive-affiliate sale, are the swing factors.

We rate ARI HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The discount to stated book is modest, the residual is concentrated in hard-to-value real estate, and the entire transaction was executed with Apollo as buyer (Athene), manager (ACREFI), and most financing counterparties. For a holder, the $3.75 distribution plus residual liquidation proceeds may exceed the current price, but the path is governed by execution timing, wind-down costs, and the fairness of related-party marks. We would not add to this position without a clearer read on residual REO realization, but it is not a clear AVOID given the discount-to-book floor and the near-term cash distribution. The honest classification is uncertain.

## Company Overview

ARI was formed in 2009 as a Maryland commercial mortgage REIT originating and acquiring senior commercial first mortgages, subordinate financings, and other CRE debt across the United States and Europe. It operated as a single segment, externally managed by ACREFI Management, LLC, an indirect subsidiary of Apollo Global Management (~$938 billion AUM). The legacy book was a floating-rate loan portfolio (93% floating at March 31, 2026) financed by secured credit facilities, a private securitization, corporate term loans, and senior secured notes.

The business as historically constituted has been dismantled in a compressed sequence. The portfolio (54 loans, weighted-average coupon 6.4%, weighted-average origination LTV 59%) was sold to Athene, the 2030 Term Loan and the 2029 Notes (called for June 15, 2026 redemption) were repaid alongside the other facilities, and the Board resolved to liquidate, dissolve, and return capital. What remains is a liquidating shell: ~$1.3 billion cash, ~$0.9 billion REO net, ~$0.4 billion REO debt, a small retained loan expected to repay, and impaired legacy assets.

## Financial Analysis

**Pre-sale snapshot (10-Q, period ended March 31, 2026):** Net income was $26.2 million; net income to common was $0.16 per diluted share, flat versus Q1 2025. Distributable Earnings, the company's non-GAAP operating metric, were $0.22 per share, down from $0.26 in Q4 2025, driven by lower index rates, a Chicago hotel loan moving to nonaccrual, and loan-modification rate reductions. Total assets were $10.09 billion, equity $1.81 billion, and debt-to-equity 4.3x (up from 4.1x at year-end 2025).

**Multi-year trajectory:** Revenue declined from $344.6 million in FY2023 to $303.7 million in FY2024 to roughly $271.6 million in FY2025. FY2024 produced a net loss of approximately $120 million, driven principally by a $127.5 million realized loss on the Massachusetts Healthcare loan following the eminent-domain seizure of St. Elizabeth's hospital. This deterioration in the legacy book is the backdrop to the strategic exit; it was event-driven, not a general portfolio collapse.

**Post-sale balance sheet:** Per the 10-Q subsequent-events note, immediately after closing the balance sheet was approximately $1.3 billion cash plus $0.9 billion REO net, less $0.4 billion REO debt, yielding stated book value per share of $12.15 after the $169.3 million Series B-1 preferred liquidation preference. Because book value per share was $12.01 at March 31 and $12.14 at year-end, the asset sale at par effectively validated the prior carrying marks, at least as struck against an affiliate buyer.

**Liquidation math.** An investor at ~$10.83 with a declared $3.75 special dividend is underwriting the residual stub: REO monetization plus remaining cash, net of wind-down costs and the preferred. Stated book of ~$12.15 against ~$10.83 implies an ~11% discount, but the residual after the $3.75 distribution sits in illiquid, Level III–valued assets. The relevant question is not earnings power; it is how much of stated book converts to cash and over what period.

**Capital allocation in the wind-down.** The company repurchased 2.87 million shares at $10.52 in Q1 and 3.94 million more at $10.72 post-quarter, with a fresh $150 million authorization on April 27, 2026. Buying stock below stated book ahead of a liquidation is rational and accretive to remaining holders if the marks hold.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth thesis. The company has sold its income-producing assets and resolved to dissolve. Forward revenue, net interest income, and book growth are not applicable. The estimate-revision data — a current-year EPS of $0.46 swinging from a negative figure 90 days ago, a +27% next-year revision — reflects analysts struggling to model a liquidating entity rather than any operating change. The +104.6% and +125.3% 90-day revisions are artifacts of moving off a negative base tied to the 2024 loss and the transaction accounting, not signals of accelerating fundamentals. The most recent quarter missed the $0.28 estimate by 20.1% ($0.22 actual), and next-quarter estimates are falling.

The growth score reflects the absence of any forward earnings engine. Distributions from here are returns of capital, not earnings.

## Valuation Assessment

The standard multiples are uninformative for a liquidating shell. The relevant valuation is sum-of-the-parts on liquidation value: stated post-close book value of ~$12.15 per share, comprising cash and REO net of REO debt and the preferred preference. The $3.75 special dividend represents roughly 35% of the current price returned in cash by July 15, 2026, leaving a residual stub of approximately $8.40 of stated book against the remaining ex-dividend price.

The peer table (Mortgage REITs, median P/E 8.5x, median yield 13.1%) is not applicable; those are going-concern operators with ongoing NIM and dividend streams. ARI should be valued on discount-to-realizable-NAV.

The case for the valuation score sitting above neutral is the ~11% discount to stated book combined with a near-term, board-declared cash distribution that crystallizes a large portion of value. The case against a higher score is that the residual is concentrated in three REO properties whose realizable values are uncertain — hotels pressured by softer international and business travel, and a Brooklyn development still in lease-up — plus deeply-reserved legacy loans, all marked under a captive-affiliate framework. The market's discount is best read as the price of residual-realization risk, related-party fairness uncertainty, and wind-down timing/cost. That discount is modest, not the asymmetric mispricing that would warrant a high valuation score. A cheap-looking discount to book in a wind-down is only a buy if the marks are conservative and realization is prompt; neither is established here.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive frame is moot. ARI has removed itself as an ongoing CRE lender by selling its entire book and resolving to dissolve. The broader commercial mortgage REIT sector remains under pressure from higher-for-longer rates, office distress, and elevated CRE credit losses. ARI's legacy book carried meaningful office (23%) and hotel (19%) exposure alongside large idiosyncratic problem loans (111 West 57th Street; a Cincinnati retail asset on nonaccrual since 2019). Rather than grind through a multi-year workout, Apollo monetized the book at par to its own affiliate and is returning capital. For a common holder this is a cleaner outcome than continued operation through a difficult credit cycle, but it ends the franchise. The competitive-position score reflects deterioration to the point of dissolution.

## Risk Assessment

**Related-party conflict on the portfolio sale.** The loan book was sold to Athene, an Apollo affiliate, while ARI is managed by an Apollo subsidiary and most financing counterparties (Atlas facilities; Apollo Global Funding/Securities as arrangers) are Apollo entities. A 99.7%-of-commitment price and a special-committee process are cited, but the fairness of price and process is the central governance risk, since the same parent sits on both sides.

**Residual REO realization risk.** Remaining value is concentrated in three foreclosed properties — the D.C. luxury hotel, the Brooklyn multifamily development still in lease-up, and the Atlanta hotel — plus impaired loans not transferred. Hotel results were pressured by lower international and business travel and tariff-related demand effects. Liquidation proceeds depend on illiquid, Level III–valued real estate.

**Impaired legacy assets and reserves.** Three risk-rated "5" loans carried $338 million of specific CECL reserve: a Cincinnati retail asset (nonaccrual since 2019), 111 West 57th Street ultra-luxury Manhattan residential (nonaccrual since 2021, $268 million reserve), and a Chicago hotel. Nonaccrual loans totaled $301 million at amortized cost. The AmBase / 111 West 57th litigation was heard at the New York Court of Appeals on April 14, 2026.

**Wind-down timing and cost.** Dissolution incurs ongoing G&A, management fees, and legal costs that erode residual distributions. The amended management agreement (base fee 0.75% of equity in shares if ROE <7.5%, stepping to 1.5% cash above an ROE milestone, plus a 20% incentive fee above an 8% hurdle) was structured for a post-sale relaunch but was overtaken by the June 15 liquidation decision within seven weeks, implying the relaunch economics did not clear the bar versus returning capital. Residual fee leakage during the wind-down remains a cost.

**Preferred overhang.** The 7.25% Series B-1 preferred ($169.3 million liquidation preference, redeemable at the company's option on or after July 15, 2026) ranks ahead of common in any liquidation distribution.

These risks are largely identifiable and partly priced by the discount to stated book, which supports a neutral risk score rather than a low one. The principal unpriced element is the gap between affiliate-struck marks and the cash that the three REO properties and reserved loans ultimately fetch.

### Options Market Signal

Options activity is thin and no detailed options sentiment data is available. The only meaningful data point is a modest price move (~–1.5%) since the prior snapshot. Short interest is moderate at 5.4% of float, 4.3 days to cover, consistent with an event-driven situation where the binary (liquidation, special dividend) is publicly known and the residual is the only open question. ## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Stated post-close book value was validated by a par sale of the loan book, and the $3.75 special dividend returns roughly 35% of the current price in cash by July 15, 2026. If the three REO properties and retained assets realize near carrying value over the next 12-18 months and wind-down costs are contained, total distributions (special dividend plus residual proceeds plus any further return of capital) could approach or exceed stated book, delivering a high-single-digit to low-double-digit return from ~$10.83 with relatively low correlation to broad equity beta. Buybacks below book are accretive. Bull case total value: ~$12.00-12.50 in distributions over the wind-down.

**Bear Case.** The affiliate sale crystallized marks that may have been generous, and the harder-to-value residual realizes well below carrying value. Hotel assets remain pressured, the Brooklyn development stalls in lease-up, and the 111 West 57th and Cincinnati situations deliver further losses. Wind-down G&A, management fees, and legal costs (including the AmBase appeal) erode the stub, and the preferred preference absorbs residual value ahead of common. In this scenario total distributions fall short of the current price, implying a loss from ~$10.83. Bear case total value: ~$9.50-10.00, roughly 8-12% below current.

The base case sits between: distributions of approximately $11.25-11.75 over the wind-down, modestly above the current price, with the $3.75 near-term dividend providing the bulk of the realized value and the residual converting at a haircut to stated book. The asymmetry is roughly balanced — a modest premium to current price if marks hold, a modest loss if they don't — which does not clear the bar for a confident BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The thesis is neither a positive 12-month appreciation case with identifiable operating catalysts (there are none — the company is dissolving) nor a stable income hold (distributions are returns of capital in a wind-down, not a durable yield). The honest classification is that the residual-versus-price calculus is too close to call without better visibility on REO realization.

**Upgrade triggers (to a constructive/accumulate stance):**
- Disclosed third-party appraisals or sale agreements on the D.C. hotel, Brooklyn development, or Atlanta hotel at or above carrying value, confirming the residual stub is realizable near stated book.
- A definitive liquidation plan with a clear distribution schedule and capped wind-down costs that, summed with the $3.75 special dividend, implies total proceeds materially above the current price.
- Evidence that the Athene sale price was struck conservatively (e.g., a fairness opinion or subsequent secondary marks above the transfer price).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A markdown or impairment on any of the three REO properties signaling residual NAV well below stated book.
- An adverse outcome or escalation in the AmBase / 111 West 57th litigation that creates a material residual liability.
- Disclosure that wind-down costs, ongoing management fees, or the preferred absorb a larger share of residual value than the current discount implies.

**Reassessment timeline:** The $3.75 special dividend pays July 15, 2026; the next natural reassessment is the post-distribution residual disclosure and any updated liquidation plan in the following quarterly filing. Re-evaluate immediately on any REO sale or appraisal disclosure.

## Conclusion

ARI is a liquidating entity, not an operating mortgage REIT. The loan book was sold to Apollo affiliate Athene at par, all corporate debt has been repaid, and the Board has resolved to dissolve and return capital. The investment question reduces to residual net asset value versus price: ~$12.15 stated book against ~$10.83, with the $3.75 near-term distribution and the realizable value of three illiquid REO properties as the swing factors, all framed by a related-party transaction structure. The discount to stated book provides a partial floor and the near-term cash return is real, but the residual is concentrated in hard-to-value real estate marked under a captive-affiliate framework, and the asymmetry from the current price is roughly balanced rather than compelling. We rate ARI HOLD (uncertain). It is not attractive enough to add to absent better visibility on residual realization, and not impaired enough to avoid outright given the discount-to-book and the imminent distribution. Holders should monitor REO appraisals/sales and the post-distribution residual closely.
