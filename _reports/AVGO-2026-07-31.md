## Executive Summary

Broadcom's fiscal Q2 2026 (quarter ended May 3, 2026) was the largest quarter in company history by every measure — revenue $22,187M (+47.9% YoY), GAAP operating income $10,788M (48.6% margin), operating cash flow $10,493M — and the stock fell 25% over the following month, from $495.00 to $360.45. It has since recovered to $387.84, still 21.7% below the high. The market's objections were specific: Q3 AI semiconductor guidance of ~$16B against consensus nearer $17B, no increase to the FY2027 ">$100 billion" AI target, an acknowledgment that Google is dual-sourcing its custom accelerators, and Q3 non-GAAP gross margin guided to ~74% from 77.1%.

Trailing GAAP P/E is ~64x on my derived LTM diluted EPS of ~$6.01, which is uninformative here. Consensus non-GAAP EPS is $11.63 for FY2026 and $19.49 for FY2027 — 33.3x and 19.9x respectively. The FY2027 figure has been revised up 7.8% in 90 days while the price is roughly flat, so the forward multiple has compressed from ~21.4x to ~19.9x on rising estimates. Adjusting FY2027 EPS downward for ~$1.80/share of annual stock compensation that non-GAAP excludes gives roughly $18.0 and ~21.5x. For a business generating a 67.3% non-GAAP operating margin and a 44% free-cash-flow margin, with $128.1B of unconditional purchase commitments contractually concentrated in FY2027-FY2028, that is not a demanding price.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than an upgrade is a cluster of risks whose magnitude is not obviously reflected in the multiple. Broadcom's largest customer — a distributor — went from 29% to 42% of net revenue in twelve months, with the distributor channel overall rising from 48% to 56%. The company has taken a **$29 billion maximum backstop** of a customer's five-year AI-rack lease obligations to an Apollo-led vehicle, converting what would have been capital-markets credit risk into a contingent liability on its own balance sheet. Management's own FY2025 10-K predicted the gross-margin compression now occurring and stated it "would adversely impact our stock price." The tax rate that flattered FY2025 (negative 1.75%) and H1 FY2026 (9.09%) is normalizing, with the 10-Q hardening its language to global minimum taxes having "materially increased" the effective rate. And the historical pattern of consistent beats has broken: the last two quarters missed consensus by 0.2% and 0.9%. The early-September Q3 print, which 41-day options price at a ±14.9% move, is the natural decision point.

## Company Overview

Broadcom operates two segments. **Semiconductor Solutions** produced $15,009M of Q2 revenue (68% of total) at a 61.8% segment operating margin, spanning custom AI accelerators (XPUs), Ethernet switching and routing silicon for AI networking, optics, wireless RF front-end modules and connectivity (the Apple franchise), broadband, storage connectivity, industrial, and IP licensing. The business model has changed materially: the FY2025 10-K and Q2 FY2026 10-Q both now describe Broadcom selling and in some cases leasing complete AI **racks and systems** built around its XPUs, not merely chips.

**Infrastructure Software** produced $7,178M (32%) at a 78.7% segment operating margin — predominantly VMware Cloud Foundation, plus mainframe software, Symantec, and Fibre Channel SAN. VMware was acquired in November 2023 for $30.8B cash plus 544M shares valued at $53.4B, with $8.25B of notes assumed.

The competitive advantage rests on four things: multi-year co-engineering design wins with a handful of the largest compute buyers in the world; merchant AI networking silicon (roughly 40% of Q2 AI revenue per call-sourced reporting, implying ~$4.3B, and the least single-customer-dependent piece of the franchise); a software annuity with 78.7% operating margins and real switching costs; and scale with TSMC, which manufactured 95% of Broadcom's contract-manufactured wafers in H1 FY2026.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and segment bifurcation

| FY2023 | $35,819M | +7.9% |
| FY2024 | $51,574M | +44.0% |
| FY2025 | $63,887M | +23.9% |
| H1 FY2026 | $41,498M | +38.7% |

The two segments have diverged sharply. Q2 semiconductor revenue grew 79% ($8,408M → $15,009M); software grew 9% ($6,596M → $7,178M). On a half-year basis semis grew 66% and software 5% — down from +26% in FY2025. Two explanations are available from the disclosure and I cannot separate them: the VMware repricing/conversion cycle is maturing, or revenue recognition timing is at work, since Note 2 recognizes license revenue **upfront** for arrangements lacking termination-for-convenience rights ($1,964M in Q2, $3,719M in H1). A light quarter for non-terminable contracts would depress reported growth without reflecting demand.

AI semiconductor revenue of $10.8B (+143%) is externally reported from the Q2 release and call, not from the filings, but it reconciles arithmetically to 48.7% of the filed $22,187M total. Guidance from the same secondary sources: FY2026 AI revenue ~$56B, Q3 AI ~$16B on total revenue ~$29.4B (+84% YoY) with $19.6B of adjusted operating income, and FY2027 AI ">$100 billion." Treat these as credible but unverified.

### Margin mechanics — the central structural issue

I can reproduce both widely quoted non-GAAP margins from the filed statements:

- Non-GAAP gross margin: ($15,415 + $1,461 intangible amortization in COGS + $10 restructuring + $223 SBC in COGS) / $22,187 = **77.1%**, versus 79.4% on the same basis a year earlier — a **230bp** decline.
- Non-GAAP operating margin: ($10,788 + $2,092 SBC + $1,967 amortization + $81 restructuring) / $22,187 = **67.3%**.

The FY2025 10-K stated that selling or leasing AI racks "will likely increase our operating margin but compress or lower future gross margin, which would adversely impact our stock price." That is happening exactly as described. Racks bundle third-party memory, power, mechanicals, and integration passed through at low markup, so gross margin percentage falls while gross profit dollars and operating margin rise. Q3 guidance of ~74% gross margin against $19.6B adjusted operating income on ~$29.4B revenue (~66.7%) implies management expects the offset to hold roughly intact. That is directly testable in early September and is the single most important number in the print.

The GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap is large: $4,140M of pre-tax excluded items in Q2, equal to 18.7% of revenue — SBC $2,092M, acquisition intangible amortization $1,967M, restructuring $81M. Only the amortization is unambiguously non-economic to a going-concern owner. LTM SBC of ~$8,785M equals ~11.6% of LTM revenue and is a real cost.

### Cash flow

| | H1 FY2026 | H1 FY2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | $18,753M | $12,668M |
| Capex | $(481)M | $(244)M |
| Free cash flow | **$18,272M** | $12,424M |
| FCF margin | **44.0%** | 41.5% |

Three qualifications. First, receivables factoring — booked in operating cash flow — actually **declined**, to $2,350M in H1 FY2026 from $3,951M, so the improvement is not factoring-driven. That is a point in favor of cash-flow quality. Second, working capital absorbed $5,743M as receivables rose $3,685M and inventory $2,058M (finished goods nearly tripled to $1,858M), tied by management to "higher expected shipments for custom AI accelerators." Third, the tax rate is normalizing: FY2025 was a **negative 1.75%** rate (a $397M benefit on $22,729M pre-tax), H1 FY2026 was 9.09%, and cash taxes paid nearly doubled to $1,881M. Singapore and Malaysia incentives reduced the FY2025 provision by $2,709M (~$0.56 of FY2025 diluted EPS); Singapore's global minimum tax adoption is effective FY2026, and the 10-Q now says global minimum taxes "have materially increased" the effective rate. Any normalized earnings model must assume a structurally higher rate than the sub-10% of the last six quarters. H1 also included a one-time $315M gain from reversal of VMware excise tax charges.

### Balance sheet

As of May 3, 2026: total assets $179,158M, liabilities $91,467M, equity $87,691M. Debt principal $66,720M against $19,628M of cash. Net debt $47,092M. My LTM EBITDA build (LTM operating income $32,746M + ~$8,014M intangible amortization + ~$603M depreciation) gives ~$41,363M, so **net debt/EBITDA ≈ 1.14x**. Maturities are well termed: $2,252M remaining in FY2026, $493M in FY2027, $47,787M beyond FY2030. The $7.5B revolver and $4.0B commercial paper program were entirely undrawn; all covenants in compliance. Debt fair value of $62,505M against $66,720M principal reflects a stock of low-coupon 2020-2021 notes now below par — an economic benefit.

Two balance-sheet cautions. Goodwill and intangibles are $126,134M of $179,158M in assets (70.4%), with goodwill of $97,801M unchanged since FY2025 year-end. And the $128,110M of unconditional purchase commitments — $55,214M in FY2027, $72,870M in FY2028 — is roughly 5.4x LTM cost of revenue of ~$23,941M, held against no reciprocal minimum-quantity commitment from TSMC ("substantially all of our manufacturing services are on a purchase order basis with no minimum quantities"). Broadcom is contractually long supply.

The Altman Z-Score of 2.16 sometimes cited for Broadcom is a measurement artifact of an acquisition-built asset base and book-equity formulation. Net leverage of 1.14x, $19.6B of cash, an undrawn $7.5B revolver, and $18.3B of half-year free cash flow are the informative solvency evidence.

### Capital allocation

Dividends of $0.65/quarter ($6,178M in H1, +11% per share) yield 0.67% — not an income case. Buybacks were $8,450M in H1 (25M shares), but the quarterly detail matters: **all** Q2 activity (2M shares at $326.05 average) occurred between February 2 and March 1, with **zero repurchases from March 2 through May 3** and $10.1B of authorization unused. Q1 bought 23M shares at roughly $341. Broadcom bought heavily in the $320-340s and stopped as the stock ran toward $495. That is consistent with price discipline; it is equally consistent with reserving liquidity for the AI working-capital ramp and the Apollo platform, or with administrative constraints. Share count is drifting up regardless — 4,741M to 4,758M over the half, as 41M RSUs vested (aggregate vesting value $13,527M) against 25M shares retired.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three verifiable anchors and one unverifiable one.

**Verifiable:** Remaining performance obligations of ~$164.6B as of May 3, 2026, explicitly including "obligations under a long-term contract for custom AI accelerators entered in the fiscal quarter ended May 3, 2026," with ~30% (≈$49.4B) expected within twelve months. This is not total backlog and should not be called that — it excludes contracts with termination-for-convenience rights, contracts of one year or less, and usage-based royalties, and the company states it is "not indicative of revenue for future periods." Contract liabilities of $14,242M are a separate concept, ~64% of which relate to contracts subject to termination for convenience. The $128.1B purchase commitment book concentrated in FY2027-28 is the hardest corroboration in the filings that the FY2027 step-up is being backed by contracted supply. And the July 8 Apple agreement — multi-source reported at more than $30B through 2031, with a $1.5B Fort Collins expansion (attribution between Apple and Broadcom unresolved in reporting) — gives the non-AI franchise a contracted multi-year floor at roughly $6B/year.

**Unverifiable but internally consistent:** FY2027 AI revenue ">$100B" against ~$56B guided for FY2026, and the six-customer multi-gigawatt roster (Google, Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta among them). These come from the earnings call via secondary sources. Consensus has partially adopted them: FY2027 EPS of $19.49 implies +67.6% growth on FY2026's $11.63, itself +70.5% on my FY2025 non-GAAP build of ~$6.82.

Revision momentum is positive but decelerating, and this matters for the recommendation. FY2027 consensus rose 7.8% over 90 days but only 0.5% over 30 days; current-quarter and current-year estimates are effectively flat (+0.0% and +2.0% over 90 days). The revision magnitude that constitutes a strong standalone buy signal — roughly +30% over 90 days on a sub-30x forward multiple — **is not present here**. What is present is a modest out-year upgrade against a flat price, which improves risk/reward without independently forcing an upgrade.

The counterweight is the earnings-delivery record. Broadcom missed consensus by 0.2% in the most recent quarter and 0.9% the quarter before, after beats of 2.7% and 0.5%. Two small misses do not establish deterioration, but they do mean forward estimates deserve less automatic confidence than they did a year ago, particularly with tax normalization and rack mix both moving against reported margins.

## Competitive Landscape

Broadcom holds roughly 70% of the merchant custom AI ASIC market by third-party estimate (externally reported, methodology unstated). Its structural role is complementary to and partly adversarial toward Nvidia: it enables hyperscalers and labs to reduce dependence on merchant GPUs. OpenAI's "Jalapeño" inference chip, shown June 24, is the clearest instance — OpenAI's chip, designed with Broadcom as ASIC and supply partner, built expressly to run inference more cheaply than on purchased GPUs. Broadcom profits from customers' desire to escape another vendor's pricing power.

The same logic that moves a customer from GPUs to a Broadcom ASIC can move it to a second ASIC partner or fully in-house. The Q2 FY2026 10-Q newly added "**customer-owned tooling**" to the list of ways customers may displace Broadcom, in two separate risk factors, a small but real signal that management views insourcing pressure as more concrete than at the FY2025 filing. Google's acknowledged dual-sourcing is the first visible instance. Dual-sourcing at a mature ASIC program is normal procurement hygiene and typically compresses price rather than eliminating volume, and Google's TPU volumes may grow fast enough that Broadcom's absolute revenue rises even as share falls. I would not size an impact from a single call remark relayed by a secondary source.

Against Marvell, Broadcom is roughly 6x larger in AI silicon ($10.8B quarterly AI revenue versus ~$1.8B of Marvell data center) at materially better margins (77.1% non-GAAP gross margin versus ~52%), yet Marvell traded up 218% year-to-date against Broadcom's +4.5% through early July and at a richer forward multiple (~68x versus ~33x). Either Broadcom is mispriced, or the market is correctly anticipating that Broadcom's share is the source of Marvell's gains.

In software, the competitive story and the reported numbers agree: +5% H1 growth against 10-Q disclosure of competition from cloud-native and AI-native vendors, public cloud providers, and open source, plus workload migration risk off-premises.

Trajectory assessment: AI semiconductor share is expanding, networking is a genuinely diversified franchise, and the Apple agreement extends the non-AI base. Against that, disclosed revenue concentration is worsening and the anchor account is dual-sourcing. Net: stable-to-modestly-improving, which supports 6.5 rather than the 8.5 assigned earlier in the year.

## Risk Assessment

**The $29B Apollo backstop.** Disclosed twice in the Q2 10-Q (Note 11 and Part II Item 5). On June 8, 2026 Broadcom arranged for Apollo — an investor partner, not the compute customer — to take on agreements to purchase AI racks and the related lease agreements with a customer. Broadcom entered a backstop of that customer's lease obligations over five-year terms, with **maximum exposure of $29 billion**, increasing as racks deploy and decreasing as the customer pays, with remedies including lease assumption or rack sale. At maximum, $29B equals 63% of net debt and would move pro-forma net leverage from 1.14x to roughly 1.84x. Bloomberg reported on July 9 a $35B financing package "for Broadcom and Anthropic's AI infrastructure expansion"; the 10-Q names only Apollo and only $29B, and does not name the customer, Blackstone, the $35B, or the 20GW target. Do not treat the counterparty identity as established. Maximum exposure is not expected exposure — it requires full deployment, immediate default, and zero recovery, and a distressed market for used AI racks would coincide precisely with when values are lowest. But the character of the risk has changed: Broadcom has taken customer credit risk onto its own books to enable a sale, and the 10-Q added backstop/guarantee language directly into the indebtedness risk factor.

**Concentration is worsening while the narrative describes diversification.** The largest customer, a distributor, went from 29% of net revenue to **42%** in twelve months. Top-five end customers went from ~40% to ~45%. Distributor channel share went from 48% of FY2025 revenue to 56% of H1 FY2026. The 10-Q states Broadcom sells "through an increasingly limited number of distributors, which exposes us to additional customer concentration and related credit risks." The six-customer gigawatt roster establishes breadth of design wins; the 42%/45%/56% figures establish that revenue and credit exposure are becoming more concentrated. Part of the distributor jump is mechanical — AI rack volume flowing through a contract-manufacturing channel that takes title in Asia Pacific, consistent with 83% of Q2 product revenue delivered there — but the top-five end-customer figure also rose.

**Gross-margin compression and the operating-margin offset.** Covered above. The company itself frames it as a stock-price risk. Q3 is the test.

**Tax normalization.** FY2025's negative rate and H1 FY2026's 9.09% are not sustainable baselines. Singapore incentives run through November 2030, the Malaysian holiday to 2028, CAMT applies with a full valuation allowance on credits.

**Supply chain.** 95% of contract-manufactured wafers from TSMC, which "could choose or be required to materially prioritize capacity for other customers." Materials concentration worsened: more than three-quarters of manufacturing materials from five suppliers in H1 FY2026, versus roughly two-thirds in the FY2025 10-K. The 10-Q newly singles out **memory chips** as a component where suppliers may allocate or raise prices, relevant because AI racks are memory-intensive and HBM/DRAM allocation is the industry's tightest current constraint. Sole-source internal fabs at Fort Collins (FBAR filters) and Breinigsville (InP wafers); China/Taiwan risk named.

**Other:** the November 2021 VMware/Dell spin-off tax risk, unaccrued and unquantified, with potential indemnification of Dell; investigations into contracting and business practices in Korea, Japan, and the EU, with no material amounts accrued; $20,106M of unrecognized stock compensation over 3.0 years.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 56.6% against 47.5% one-year historical volatility is rich by 9.1 points, at the 69th percentile within the chain, and the term structure is in **backwardation** (56.6% near versus 48.6% at six months) — the market is pricing near-term event risk rather than a change in long-run risk. The 1-week ±0.4% implied move is a one-day-expiry artifact and should be discounted; the informative tenor is the 41-day September expiry at 53.3% IV implying **±14.9%**, which brackets the Q3 print. Put/call skew is +3.8% near-dated (elevated put demand) but -3.8% at six months, so the protective bid is concentrated in the event window rather than structural. Put/call OI ratio 0.97 and volume ratio 0.68 are balanced-to-call-tilted. The most notable non-pinning flow is downside: 2,545 August 5 $355 puts against 577 open interest and 602 August 7 $352 puts against 146 — someone paying for roughly 9% downside in early August. Short interest of 1.5% of float and 2.5 days to cover means there is no crowded bearish position and no squeeze dynamic. The options market corroborates the view that the September print is the binary, not that the stock is broken.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $575 (+48.3%)

FY2027 AI semiconductor revenue exceeds the ">$100B" floor and management raises the target at the September print. The gross-margin-for-operating-margin trade holds: ~74% gross margin delivers ~66.7% adjusted operating margin as guided, proving rack pass-through content dilutes percentage margin without impairing profit dollars. The $128.1B of FY2027-28 purchase commitments converts to revenue roughly as contracted. Apple's $30B+ agreement through 2031 gives the non-AI franchise a floor and de-risks the Fort Collins single-source exposure. FY2027 EPS is revised toward $21, and the multiple re-rates to 27-28x on demonstrated FY2028 visibility. Free cash flow at a 44% margin on a $140B+ revenue base funds accelerating buybacks against the unused $10.1B authorization.

### Bear Case — $280 (-27.8%)

AI orders are deferred or cancelled — Broadcom's own FY2025 10-K warns the AI upturn "may not be sustainable" and that AI customers "may have constrained resources or capital and may be unable to pay." Broadcom is contractually long $128.1B of supply with no reciprocal TSMC minimums, so deferral lands on its inventory and commitments. Google's dual-sourcing progresses from price concession to volume loss at the largest ASIC program. Gross margin falls below 72% without the operating-margin offset as rack service and warranty obligations land in operating expense. The tax rate steps toward the mid-teens, cutting FY2027 EPS toward $15. The backstopped customer's payments come under stress and Broadcom begins accruing against the $29B guarantee. At 18x $15 the stock is $270-280, near the $281.61 52-week low.

### Base Case — $440 (+13.4%)

22.6x FY2027 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $19.49. The discount to Broadcom's historical high-20s forward multiple reflects three specific items: structural gross-margin mix compression, a tax rate normalizing from sub-10% toward the low teens, and a $29B contingent guarantee that warrants a leverage-adjusted haircut. On my SBC-adjusted FY2027 EPS of ~$18.0 this is ~24.4x — a defensible multiple for a franchise compounding at these rates with 44% FCF margins.

## Conclusion

**HOLD (growth hold), overall score 6.4/10** — a modest improvement from 6.2 in July, driven entirely by the FY2027 forward multiple compressing from ~21.4x to ~19.9x as consensus rose 7.8% against a flat price. That is improving risk/reward on unchanged fundamentals, and I am not marking the thesis down for the drawdown from $495.

Why I am not upgrading: the strong-buy estimate-revision signal (>+30% over 90 days on a sub-30x forward multiple) is not present — the 30-day revision is +0.5% and the near-term quarters are flat. The management-quality read is weak on forward indicators: one-directional insider selling (Samueli roughly $430M across two tranche programs, Brazeal in repeated 25,000-share blocks, Velaga 70,000-plus shares), a single open-market purchase of 1,000 shares by a director after a 25% drawdown, no C-suite buying at all, and $2.27 billion of "compensation actually paid" to the CEO in FY2025 against a compensation framework keyed to non-GAAP operating margin at a company excluding ~$8.8B/year of stock compensation. The offsetting evidence — repurchases concentrated in the $320-340s and then halted as the stock approached $495 — is genuine price discipline, and I weight it, but the picture is mixed rather than aligned. And the risk register contains several specific, dated, asymmetric items: a $29B contingent guarantee whose counterparty appears to be a pre-profit AI lab, a single distributor at 42% of revenue up from 29%, an anchor customer dual-sourcing, a tax rate that cannot persist, and a print 41 days out that options price at ±14.9%.

The fundamental case is intact and, on the forward numbers, better than fair. Own the position. The evidence that would move this to BUY is specific and arrives in about five weeks: Q3 gross margin at or above 74% with operating margin holding near 66.5%, and an FY2027 AI target that moves up. Absent that, adding at $388 pays full price for a guidance figure I cannot verify from a filing.
