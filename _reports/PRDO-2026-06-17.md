**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Perdoceo Education operates accredited, predominantly online for-profit postsecondary institutions through three segments: Colorado Technical University (CTU, ~55% of revenue at a 39% operating margin), the American InterContinental University System (AIUS, ~27%), and the University of St. Augustine for Health Sciences (USAHS, ~19%, acquired December 2024). The business is high-quality on cash-flow and balance-sheet metrics: FY25 revenue of $846.1M (+24.2%), operating income of $196.0M, net income of $159.9M, FCF of ~$224.9M (~26.6% margin), and roughly $680M of cash and investments against ~$6M of traditional debt. Against a ~$2.0B market cap, net cash represents roughly a third of the equity value, and management is returning capital aggressively: $120.8M of buybacks in 2025 plus a fresh $100M authorization and a dividend raised to $0.15 quarterly.

The stock trades at ~12-13x trailing EPS rather than the mid-teens-to-high-teens multiples of cleaner peers because of regulatory exposure. Roughly 76% of cash receipts (~$615M in 2025) flow from Title IV federal student aid, and the July 2025 Reconciliation Act introduces a new universal program-level "earnings-premium" accountability metric (most provisions effective ~July 1, 2026, rules still being written via AHEAD), eliminates Grad PLUS loans for new graduate students (a direct hit to USAHS's graduate-only model), and imposes new borrowing limits. Two active qui tam False Claims Act suits tied to CTU's recruiting and credit-hour practices add unquantified tail risk. The options market is signaling acute near-term uncertainty: front-week implied volatility spiked to ~150% in steep backwardation with a put/call open-interest ratio near 3.9.

We rate PRDO a HOLD. The cash-return profile and fortress balance sheet provide a credible floor, and the company has beaten estimates four straight quarters with rising full-year consensus. But organic growth is modest (CTU +4.1% in FY25, AIUS roughly flat with Trident eroding), the 2026 earnings base is inflated by the full-year consolidation of lower-margin USAHS, and the valuation discount substantially reflects genuine regulatory risk rather than a clean mispricing. We view this as a yield-and-buyback hold pending resolution of the 2026-2027 regulatory catalyst window, not a high-conviction growth call.

## Company Overview

Perdoceo, headquartered in Schaumburg, Illinois, is a for-profit operator of accredited online and campus-based postsecondary institutions serving primarily working adults. Total enrollment reached 44,400 at year-end 2025 (+7.3% YoY) and 48,740 at Q1 2026 (+1.1% YoY).

- **CTU (~$461.6M FY25 revenue, ~39% operating margin):** The core franchise. Career-focused programs in business, IT, cybersecurity, nursing, and criminal justice, delivered ~98% online via the proprietary intellipath adaptive-learning platform. CTU contributes ~70% of enrollments and the bulk of operating income.
- **AIUS (~$226.2M, ~16% operating margin):** Includes Trident University, ~97% online. The soft spot: AIUS enrollment fell 2.2% in Q1 2026 on Trident weakness, having also been hurt by the FY25 government shutdown.
- **USAHS (~$157.6M, ~2% FY25 operating margin):** Acquired December 2, 2024 for ~$138M cash. Graduate health-sciences programs (physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech-language pathology, nursing). Campus-based, higher-cost, and structurally different from the asset-light online segments. FY25 margin was depressed by $30.3M of acquisition-related D&A; the segment swung to $6.3M operating income in Q1 2026 as that D&A begins to roll off.

The competitive model is a low-cost, technology-driven online delivery platform complemented by growing corporate tuition-assistance partnerships, which management cites as a key enrollment-growth channel.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is the strongest part of the story.

- **Revenue:** ~$147.5M of the $164.8M FY25 increase came from a full year of USAHS versus one month in 2024. Organic growth was modest: CTU +4.1%, AIUS roughly flat. Q1 2026 revenue was $221.7M (+4.1%), with all three segments growing.
- **Profitability:** FY25 operating income +12.5%, diluted EPS of $2.42 (adjusted $2.61), 18.9% net margin. Operating margin compressed from 25.6% to 23.2%, driven entirely by the mix effect of consolidating lower-margin USAHS rather than core deterioration. Q1 2026 operating income rose 22% to $63.1M on improving USAHS economics.
- **Cash generation:** OCF of $225.2M in FY25, with capex around 1% of revenue on the online side. The FCF margin and high FCF/net-income conversion indicate clean, high-quality earnings.
- **Balance sheet:** $643.5M cash and investments at year-end against $6.1M of long-term traditional debt. Altman Z of 3.29, current ratio of 5.06, ROE of 16.4%, ROA of 12.8%. The reported D/E of 1.28 is misleading: it is dominated by operating/finance leases and a $57M "failed sale-leaseback" financing liability tied to the new St. Augustine campus (~$154M of future minimum payments through 2050). Adjusting for this, the company is effectively unlevered.
- **Capital allocation:** 4.1M shares repurchased in 2025 (avg $29.17), ~$91.9M remaining on the January 2026 authorization, share count down to ~62.5M from ~65.6M, and ~$36.9M of dividends paid in 2025. The current ~1.86% yield is supplemented by a buyback program that, with roughly a third of market cap in net cash, has substantial runway.

**Earnings track record:** Four consecutive beats: Q1 2026 $0.90 vs. $0.86 (5.0%), Q4 2025 $0.59 vs. $0.55 (7.1%), Q3 $0.65 vs. $0.62 (4.5%), Q2 $0.67 vs. $0.66 (1.1%). Consistent small beats support reasonable confidence in current consensus.

**2026 guidance:** Adjusted operating income of $254-263M and adjusted EPS of $3.05-3.16, on an effective tax rate of 22.5-23.5%. Consensus current-year EPS sits at $3.11, squarely within the range.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the weakest leg of the thesis when stripped of acquisition optics. The headline +24.2% revenue growth was almost entirely USAHS consolidation. Underlying organic growth is low-to-mid single digits: CTU +1.9% enrollment in Q1 2026, AIUS roughly flat-to-declining with Trident as a persistent drag. Total enrollment growth of 7.3% in FY25 partly reflects the corporate-partnership channel and USAHS additions rather than broad-based core expansion.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest. Current-year consensus EPS rose to $3.11 from $2.78 over 90 days (+11.9%) and from $3.05 over 30 days (+2.0%); next year sits at $3.34 (+2.1% over 30 days). This is genuine positive earnings momentum, but it falls well short of the +30% revision velocity that would mark a true inflection, and revenue growth is in the single digits rather than the 30-40%+ that would pair with such a signal. This is a steadily improving estimate trend, not a re-rating catalyst.

The clearest forward growth lever is USAHS margin normalization as acquisition D&A rolls off; the Q1 2026 swing to $6.3M segment operating income should lift consolidated margins through 2026 even without strong topline acceleration. The offsetting drags are AIUS/Trident erosion and the regulatory constraints on USAHS expansion (see below). Net, we view organic growth durability as moderate: enough to support the guidance, not enough to drive multiple expansion on its own.

## Valuation Assessment

PRDO trades at ~12.3x trailing EPS and ~10.2-10.5x the midpoint of 2026 adjusted EPS guidance (~$3.10). Against the Education Services peer median P/E of 15.6 (STRA 14.1, LRN 15.6, LOPE 19.1, GHC 17.1, DUOL 14.2), the stock carries a clear discount.

The case for asymmetric upside rests on two pillars. First, on an EV basis the discount is starker: with net cash against the market cap, enterprise value is roughly $1.3B, implying an EV/FCF in the high single digits on ~$225M of FCF, cheap for a business compounding cash and shrinking its share count. Second, the lone covering analyst's $44 target implies ~39% upside from current levels, and the absolute dividend yield plus buyback yield provide downside support.

The case against treating this as a clean mispricing is that the discount is at least partly earned. A low multiple in isolation is frequently a value-trap signal rather than a buy signal, and PRDO's cheapness is substantially a function of structural regulatory exposure: 76% Title IV dependence, the pending earnings-premium accountability regime, Grad PLUS elimination, and two unquantified FCA suits. The peer discount versus STRA (also for-profit, also 14.1x) is narrow once you account for STRA's cleaner regulatory posture and lower acquisition-integration overhang. The discount versus the broader median is real but partly reflects an idiosyncratic binary-risk premium the market is correctly assigning.

We score valuation at 6.3, modestly above neutral. The balance-sheet-adjusted multiple and capital-return support are genuinely attractive, but we decline to score this as a high-conviction mispricing because the cheapness coexists with identifiable, unresolved regulatory catalysts. The $44 analyst target should be treated with caution rather than as a floor; on a regulatory-overhang name with single-digit organic growth, consensus targets are not the reliable lagging indicators they are on clean high-growth inflection stocks.

## Competitive Landscape

The postsecondary market is fragmented and competitive. PRDO faces community colleges (often free or subsidized), traditional universities expanding online, and other for-profits. PRDO's differentiators are a disciplined low-cost structure, the intellipath adaptive-learning platform, and a growing corporate tuition-assistance channel.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving rather than clearly advancing. On the positive side, the 2025 Reconciliation Act's universalization of accountability metrics to all institutions (including non-profits) partially levels a field that previously disadvantaged for-profits under 90-10 and Gainful Employment; if implemented as drafted, this reduces PRDO's relative regulatory penalty. The post-2024 political environment is arguably less hostile to the sector than the prior administration.

On the negative side, several for-profit peers have converted to non-profit or OPM-style structures specifically to escape the 90-10 Rule and Gainful Employment, a regulatory-arbitrage path PRDO has chosen not to pursue, leaving it more exposed to the rules as written. Within the portfolio, CTU is gaining modest share and USAHS occupies a defensible employment-linked graduate niche, but AIUS/Trident is losing ground. We score competitive position at 5.7: the moat is real (cost discipline, tech, corporate channel) but the trajectory is mixed and the regulatory-arbitrage gap versus converted peers is a structural relative disadvantage.

## Risk Assessment

This is where PRDO earns its discount, and the risks are concentrated and partly binary.

1. **Title IV dependence (~76% of cash receipts).** Any reduction in federal aid eligibility or funding is existential. The 90-10 Rule remains a structural constraint.
2. **The 2025 Reconciliation Act.** The single most important new risk. Programs that repeatedly fail the earnings-premium metric could lose Direct Loan eligibility. The outcome is genuinely two-sided; universalization to all institutions could be neutral-to-positive on a relative basis, but the lack of final rules creates an unquantifiable 2026-2027 catalyst window.
3. **USAHS provisional certification.** The newest, highest-multiple segment operates under temporary provisional certification (up to 3 years) from the 2024 change of ownership, restricting program/location additions, a constraint on the segment with the best growth optionality.
4. **Cohort default rates.** Artificially 0% for the 2022 cohort due to the pandemic payment pause; management explicitly warns CDRs "may increase significantly" beginning with the 2024 cohort as repayment resumes.
5. **Two active qui tam FCA suits.** Both filed by former CTU employees, both with DOJ declining to intervene (a positive). Fiorisce alleges credit-hour/LMS compliance violations; Peters alleges incentive-compensation and misrepresentation violations plus retaliation, with a motion to dismiss filed March 2026. FCA exposure is treble damages plus penalties; the company has recognized no liability and cannot estimate a range. Unquantified tail risk tied to core recruiting practices.
6. **Lead-generation/outreach pressure.** FTC 2019 agreement compliance costs and call-blocking/"Spam Likely" labeling impairing prospective-student outreach.

Several offsetting de-riskers: the December 2021 Department of Education inquiry closed in January 2025 with no findings, and USAHS's $20.5M letter of credit to the Department was released in January 2026. CTU and AIUS recertification applications are due March 31, 2027.

We score risk factors at 4.8, below neutral. The combination of a specific, dated regulatory transition (July 2026 / March 2027), high Title IV concentration, and unquantified FCA exposure represents material idiosyncratic downside that is only partially priced into the discounted multiple.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
PRDO is a misunderstood capital-return story trading at a regulatory discount that proves overdone. The earnings-premium regime applies universally and lands neutral-to-favorable for PRDO relative to converted peers; the FCA suits are dismissed or settled cheaply; USAHS margins normalize as D&A rolls off, lifting consolidated margins toward CTU-like levels; and the corporate-partnership channel sustains mid-single-digit organic growth. On ~$3.30+ of 2027 EPS, a re-rating to a peer ~15x plus the net-cash optionality and ongoing buyback supports the analyst's $44 target (~39% upside). The 26% FCF margin, ~3% combined dividend-plus-buyback yield, and net cash equal to a third of market cap provide a floor while you wait.

### Bear Case
The earnings-premium metric and Grad PLUS elimination materially impair USAHS and pressure Title IV eligibility across programs; an FCA suit produces a quantified liability; CDRs rise sharply as repayment resumes; AIUS/Trident erosion accelerates; and organic growth stalls. The market re-prices the regulatory tail, and the multiple compresses toward the low end of the for-profit range or below. Downside to ~$26-27 (the 52-week low and roughly 8x guided EPS) on a regulatory adverse outcome, ~-16% from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The thesis rests on cash-flow durability, the dividend, and the buyback supported by net cash, rather than on near-term appreciation. The regulatory catalyst window (July 2026 earnings-premium rules, March 2027 recertifications) caps conviction in either direction.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Final AHEAD/earnings-premium rules published with neutral-to-favorable impact on PRDO's programs, removing the binary overhang.
- Dismissal or immaterial settlement of both qui tam FCA suits.
- USAHS provisional certification lifted early or program-expansion approvals granted, plus consolidated operating margin recovering toward 25%+ on USAHS D&A roll-off, while organic enrollment growth holds mid-single-digits.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Adverse earnings-premium determinations threatening Direct Loan eligibility for material programs, or a quantified FCA liability.
- A guidance cut, or a quarter where AIUS decline accelerates and CTU growth turns negative.
- Cohort default rates rising sharply enough to threaten Title IV participation thresholds.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (early August 2026) and the July 1, 2026 effective date for the new accountability provisions, with the March 31, 2027 recertification deadline as the longer-dated checkpoint.

## Conclusion

Perdoceo is a high-quality cash generator with a binary regulatory risk profile. The financials are excellent: ~26% FCF margins, effectively no debt, four straight beats, and a shareholder-friendly buyback-plus-dividend program. The valuation is genuinely cheap on an EV/FCF basis. But the discount to peers is at least partly earned: high Title IV dependence, a pending earnings-premium accountability regime and Grad PLUS elimination effective mid-2026, two unquantified FCA suits, and modest organic growth dominated by an acquisition that lowered consolidated margins and added a provisionally-certified segment. The options market is pricing acute near-term uncertainty, consistent with the regulatory timeline.

We rate PRDO a HOLD with an overall score of 6.0. The balance sheet and capital return provide a credible floor that limits downside to roughly the 52-week low, but the regulatory catalysts that drive the next leg are not yet resolved and could break either way. We would upgrade on favorable final rules and FCA resolution, and downgrade on adverse accountability determinations or accelerating segment erosion. For existing holders, the yield-and-buyback profile justifies retention; new money lacks an asymmetric entry until the 2026-2027 regulatory picture clarifies.
