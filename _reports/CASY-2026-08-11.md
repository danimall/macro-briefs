## Executive Summary

Casey's is one of the better-run retailers in the US public market. It operates 2,944 stores across 19 states, roughly 71% of them in communities of fewer than 20,000 people where national chains have historically not built. That footprint, combined with three company-owned distribution centers, a 500-plus tractor fleet, a company-owned fuel terminal in Waco, and full-service kitchens in nearly all stores, produces a cost and format advantage that is difficult to replicate. FY2026 (ended April 30, 2026) delivered record diluted EPS of $19.16 (+30.9%), net income of $714.4M, EBITDA of $1,483.6M (+23.6%), operating cash flow of $1,377.5M and free cash flow of $721.6M — 101% of net income. Net debt/EBITDA is 1.29x with $1.42B of liquidity and no maturity wall. On the financial-strength dimension there is no debate.

The debate is entirely about the price. At $845 the stock trades at 44.1x trailing EPS and 39.7x the $21.31 consensus for FY2027, against implied EPS growth of roughly 10-12% — a PEG of 3.5-3.8x. Against Casey's own five-year forward P/E band of roughly 19x to 44.5x (median ~23.5x), the current multiple sits at the top. More important, the earnings base carrying that multiple is flattered by a cyclically elevated input. Backing Q4 FY2026 out of the annual figures against the nine-month 10-Q, fuel margin ran 46.85 cents per gallon versus 37.60 cpg a year earlier, a 9.25 cpg expansion worth roughly $78.5M of pretax gross profit, or about $1.60 per share after tax, against a total Q4 EPS increase of approximately $1.73. Roughly 1.3 cpg of that expansion came from RIN monetization, which is set by federal Renewable Fuel Standard policy rather than by store operations. Full-year fuel margin of 42.57 cpg is described in the company's own MD&A as "historically higher than average."

The earnings model is more resilient than the multiple. Inside gross profit of $2,676.3M growing at the FY2026 pace would add $215-240M and broadly absorb a reversion to FY2025's 38.7 cpg. What would not be absorbed is a compression of a ~40x multiple that was awarded on the strength of a fuel-driven print. Layer on clustered insider selling (five of six named executive officers plus a director sold roughly $29M in the two weeks after the 10-K was filed), a FY2027 plan requiring at least 120 new stores against FY2026 net additions of 40, and a consensus Q1 bar of $6.72 versus $5.77 last year (+16.5%) reporting in early September, and the asymmetry turns against the buyer. Base case $880 (+4.1%), bull $1,010 (+19.5%), bear $630 (−25.4%); probability-weighted expected return is approximately +0.6%. Trim.

## Company Overview

Casey's generates $17.56B of revenue across three retail categories plus a small wholesale fuel arm. The economics invert between revenue and profit mix:

| Category | FY2026 revenue | Gross profit | GP margin | % of total GP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prepared food & dispensed beverage | $1,776.8M | $1,040.9M | 58.6% | 24.1% |
| Grocery & general merchandise | $4,563.6M | $1,635.4M | 35.8% | 37.8% |
| Retail fuel | $10,615.4M | $1,496.6M | 14.1% | 34.6% |
| Other (wholesale fuel, car wash, lottery) | $605.3M | $148.1M | 24.5% | 3.4% |
| **Total** | **$17,561.1M** | **$4,321.0M** | **24.6%** | **100%** |

Fuel is 60.4% of revenue but 34.6% of gross profit; the two inside categories are 36.1% of revenue and 61.9% of gross profit. Casey's describes itself as the third-largest US convenience retailer and fifth-largest pizza chain (issuer claim, June 8, 2026 release). It has made pizza since 1984 and is rolling out wings, present in roughly 850 stores at year-end. Casey's Rewards passed 10 million members. The company owns substantially all of its stores, all three distribution centers and the fuel terminal; undiscounted operating lease obligations are only $820.9M against $8.94B of assets.

Acquired stores are converted by installing a full-service kitchen and rebranding to Casey's; where the box cannot accommodate a kitchen, stores operate as GoodStop or retain the CEFCO banner. That conversion is the mechanism by which acquired fuel-and-snacks locations migrate toward the 58% food margin, and it is also a constraint — non-convertible boxes stay in a lower-margin format indefinitely.

Casey's reports one segment and does not disclose segment assets, so returns on the $1,165.8M Fikes/CEFCO acquisition (198 stores, closed November 1, 2024) cannot be computed from the filings.

## Financial Analysis

**A data note that matters.** The standard XBRL feed reports FY2026 net income of $937,023K. That figure is pretax income: $937,023K less income taxes of $222,575K equals $714,448K, which reconciles exactly to the 10-K MD&A and the proxy's pay-versus-performance table, and the same off-by-one-line relationship holds for FY2023-FY2025. Screens built on the raw feed show a 5.3% net margin and a 33.4x P/E rather than the actual 4.07% and 44.1x — a difference that inverts the conclusion on whether the stock is expensive. Separately, the feed's $9,193,910K "net PP&E" is gross; net PP&E is approximately $5.74B.

**Trajectory (filing facts):**

| ($M except EPS) | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 15,094.5 | 14,862.9 | 15,940.9 | 17,561.1 |
| Gross margin % | — | 22.5% | 23.5% | 24.6% |
| EBITDA (co. def.) | 952.5 | 1,059.4 | 1,200.0 | 1,483.6 |
| Net income | 446.7 | 502.0 | 546.5 | 714.4 |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | ~$14.64 | $19.16 |
| Operating cash flow | — | — | 1,090.9 | 1,377.5 |

Revenue grew 10.2%, but $1,034.1M of the $1,620.2M increase came from Fikes lapping in the first half — roughly 64% of reported growth was acquisition anniversary. Underlying comps were nonetheless solid: prepared food/beverage same-store sales +5.2%, grocery/GM +3.9%, same-store gallons +1.4% (the best gallon comp in three years against +0.1% in each of FY2024 and FY2025). Inside margin expanded 70bp to 42.2% ($2,676.3M ÷ $6,340.4M inside sales), sourced from waste reduction in food and mix in grocery.

**Where the earnings growth came from.** Total gross margin moved 23.5% → 24.6%. Inside margins improved modestly (food 58.2% → 58.6%; grocery 35.0% → 35.8%). The large move was fuel: 38.68 → 42.57 cpg, +10.1%. The Q4 decomposition is the sharpest version of the point: Q4 fuel gross profit of $397.4M on 848.3M gallons equals 46.85 cpg against 37.60 cpg in the prior-year quarter. Taxed at the Q3 effective rate of 24.1%, the cpg delta alone is worth roughly $1.60 per share versus a total Q4 EPS increase of about $1.73 ($4.36 vs ~$2.63). This is a single-variable attribution holding gallons constant — operating expense, D&A, interest and tax all moved concurrently — so treat it as an order-of-magnitude sensitivity rather than a completed bridge. But the direction is unambiguous: the quarter that drove a large single-day re-rating on June 10 was substantially a fuel-margin quarter.

RIN monetization, recorded as a COGS reduction, contributed $35.4M in FY2026 versus $16.7M in FY2025. Q4 RIN revenue of roughly $15.2M equals about 1.79 cpg versus 0.53 cpg a year earlier, so approximately 1.3 cpg of the 9.25 cpg Q4 expansion is federal policy pricing rather than operating performance. One unresolved oddity: Q4 fuel revenue rose ~18.3% on gallons up only ~3.6%, implying retail price per gallon up ~14% — rising wholesale prices normally compress retail margin, not expand it. The filings do not permit a determination between lagging cost pass-through, RIN capture and industry-wide firmness.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt and finance leases of $2,431.6M against $523.0M cash and an undrawn $850M revolver plus $50M bank line (~$1.42B liquidity). Gross debt/EBITDA 1.64x, net 1.29x; debt/equity 0.62x (the 1.26x figure on screens is total liabilities/equity). Fixed-rate notes of ~$1,324M at 2.85%-5.43% maturing 2028-2034, plus $200M and $796.9M floating term loans; a 100bp rate move equals ~$9.5M annualized. Maturity ladder: $137.8M inside one year. Fair value of long-term debt (~$2,244M) below carrying (~$2,326M) reflects a below-market coupon benefit. Reproducing the standard Altman formula from the supplied components confirms EBIT/assets was zero-filled and book equity substituted for market equity; corrected Z is approximately 3.44 on book equity and 6.74 on the textbook market-value definition. Piotroski, computed across four years, is 9/9 — though leverage, current ratio and asset turnover signals are mechanically flattered by the Fikes lapping distortion in the FY2025 base.

**Cash quality.** FCF of $721.6M (OCF less $655.9M P&E purchases) equals 101% of net income; $580.0M after $141.6M of acquisitions. Working capital contributed roughly +$39M, driven by accounts payable +$180.0M against inventory −$82.3M and receivables −$58.8M, attributed in the MD&A "primarily due to fuel pricing." That is a flow that reverses if fuel prices fall, which matters given the Q4 price spike. Maintenance versus growth capex is not disclosed, so owner earnings cannot be isolated. FCF yield on market cap is 2.31%.

Financial health score of 7.0 holds the improvement from prior reviews. Leverage is conservative for a retailer with owned real estate, cash conversion is high-quality, and covenant or refinancing risk is not a live issue.

## Growth Analysis

Casey's completed its June 2023 three-year plan ahead of target: 17.2% annualized EPS growth and 504 stores built or acquired against a 350 goal. ROIC of 12.7%, up 120bp, was reported by management (externally sourced; not located in the 10-K text reviewed).

FY2027 guidance, as externally reported and not confirmed against a filing we could retrieve: inside same-store sales +2% to 5%, inside margin above 42%, EBITDA +8% to 10%, operating expenses +5% to 7%, at least 120 new stores (crossing 3,000), approximately $200M of buybacks, and a 14% dividend increase to $0.65 quarterly — the 27th consecutive annual raise. Consensus is consistent: FY2027 EPS $21.31 (+11.2%) and FY2028 $23.55 (+10.5%).

Three observations qualify the growth picture.

First, **unit growth requires a step-change.** The FY2026 store roll-forward was 40 built plus 40 acquired plus 1 prior acquisition opened less 41 closed, for net additions of 40. Excluding Fikes, the prior plan added roughly 306 stores over three years, about 102 per year gross. Reaching at least 120 requires either a materially faster build program or another platform deal. Capacity exists — 1.29x net leverage, $900M undrawn, $721.6M of FCF supports a $1-2B acquisition without equity — but a deal reintroduces integration risk and adds to a goodwill balance already at $1,268.7M, or 14.2% of assets and 32.1% of book equity. The definitional caveat matters: "new stores" in the guidance may be counted differently from the 10-K roll-forward, and the 41 closures may reflect one-time CEFCO pruning.

Second, **the comp base changes in FY2027.** Casey's requires a store to be open for the entirety of both compared periods, so FY2026's +5.2% food and +3.9% grocery comps are essentially legacy-Casey's numbers excluding the 198 CEFCO stores. The Q3 expense detail shows the migration mechanically: non-same-store operating expense collapsed to $25.7M from $78.6M while same-store employee expense jumped 15.5% and same-store card fees 19.3%, even as total Q3 operating expenses rose only 4.1%. As CEFCO enters the base, its lower inside-sales mix could dilute reported comps. That offers a benign reading of guidance at +2% to 5% against +4.2% delivered. Management has not attributed the range to base composition; this is inference.

Third, **the near-term bar is demanding.** Consensus for the July 2026 quarter is $6.72 against $5.77 a year ago, +16.5%. The four-quarter beat record (+30.0%, +14.5%, +4.9%, +12.7%) supports confidence in delivery, but the largest beats coincided with the fuel-margin expansion. Estimate revisions are constructive but not of inflection magnitude: current year +6.7% over 90 days and +0.3% over 30 days; next year +5.7% and +0.2%. The 30-day figures show revisions have flattened since the June print.

Growth prospects at 6.3, a modest step down from 6.5. Nothing has broken; the organic growth rate is mid-single-digit topline with mid-teens EPS support from margin and buyback, and the unit-growth guidance carries execution risk it did not carry a year ago.

Against Consumer Staples/Food Retail peers, CASY's 39.7x forward compares to a peer median trailing P/E of 34.7x — but that median is contaminated (Albertsons at 96.1x on depressed earnings, UNFI negative). Valuation score 3.8. The multiple is stretched relative to the growth it is paired with, the earnings base includes a cyclically elevated and partly policy-driven component, and there is no identified mispricing working in the buyer's favor.

## Competitive Landscape

Casey's competitive position is improving on the metrics that matter. Inside margin expanded 70bp; store-level operating income per store rose to $566K from $496K (FY2025) and $473K (FY2024); same-store gallons turned positive at +1.4% after two years near zero, which in a structurally declining gasoline volume market implies share gains. Loyalty membership crossed 10 million. The wings rollout extends the foodservice moat into a second category.

The structural advantage is geographic and logistical. The 10-K states the case directly: "a convenience store in an area with limited population can be profitable if it stresses sales volume, quality products, and competitive prices." Most stores sit within a ~500-mile optimum efficiency range of a company DC. In low-density markets, third-party direct-store-delivery economics are poor, so self-distribution is a real cost moat rather than a slogan.

The candid risks are also in Item 1A. In larger markets, competitors "have been in existence longer and have greater financial, marketing, and other resources." On fuel, "several non-traditional retailers such as supermarkets, club stores, and mass merchants have affected the convenience store industry by entering the retail fuel business," using "more extensive promotional pricing or discounts." Club and mass fuel is a loss-leader channel Casey's cannot match on price; its relevance is concentrated in the minority of larger-market stores.

Two secular headwinds deserve naming. Tobacco and nicotine averaged approximately 9% of revenue and 9% of gross profit over three years, with an explicit dependence on manufacturer rebates: if rebates decline, "our wholesale cigarette and related product costs will increase accordingly." EV transition is slow-moving here — 282 charging stations at 64 stores, with management noting Midwest EV demand "comparatively lower than the levels along the coasts." The rural footprint is currently a shield against fuel disintermediation rather than an exposure.

Competitive position 7.0: a widening moat in food and distribution, with a slowly eroding tobacco leg and a fuel line exposed to a channel that prices below cost.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Fuel margin mean reversion (highest impact, not priced).** Item 1A: "our net income is significantly affected by changes in the margins we receive on our retail fuel sales." The MD&A concedes FY2026 cpg was "historically higher than average." Management's stated view is that margins "will remain elevated from historical levels for the foreseeable future," immediately qualified by oil prices, rates, macro conditions and oil-region conflicts. The near-term direction of pass-through is genuinely uncertain — rising wholesale costs normally compress retail cpg, which makes the Q4 anomaly (cpg up on retail prices up 14%) something to watch rather than to extrapolate.

**2. Valuation-embedded execution risk.** At ~40x on 10-12% growth, the implied PEG is ~3.5x. Item 1A warns the stock price responds to "a deviation in our results from the expectations of public market analysts." The market has already shown it will fade the print: shares fell 7.9% in the month after June earnings before recovering.

**3. Credit card interchange, disclosed at $279M for FY2026** — approximately 9.8% of operating expenses, 6.5% of gross profit and 39% of net income. Interchange scales with ticket size, so the high-fuel-price regime that lifted Q4 revenue ~18% simultaneously inflated this expense. It is a leveraged cost against the same variable driving the upside.

**4. Unit growth and M&A risk.** At least 120 stores against 40 net FY2026 additions requires acceleration or a deal. A $1-2B platform acquisition is fundable but would add to a goodwill balance already at 32.1% of book equity. Impairment charges rose to $9.8M from $4.1M alongside 41 closures — immaterial at ~1% of pretax income, but a 2.4x increase worth monitoring against acquisition cadence.

**5. Labor and classification.** Same-store employee expense contributed ~1% of the FY2026 operating expense increase, with wage inflation partly offset by reduced same-store labor hours — a trade-off with finite runway. Two FLSA store-manager misclassification suits settled for an immaterial amount pending court approval; the durable issue is precedent risk to the exempt-manager staffing model across 2,944 stores.

**6. Concentration and seasonality.** The vast majority of stores, all three DCs and the corporate office are in the Midwest (tornado, ice, flood, cold), with Florida hurricane exposure. Fiscal Q1 and Q2 (May-October) carry disproportionate profitability, so a poor summer is not recoverable later in the year.

**7. Anti-takeover posture.** Blank-check authority for up to one million preferred shares, the Iowa three-year business-combination freeze, an Iowa constituency statute, and an explicit statement that the company may adopt a rights plan "without any further vote or action by our shareholders." For a high-quality asset in a consolidating industry, this materially reduces the probability of a takeover premium ever being realized — removing an option value that some holders may implicitly assume.

**8. Consumer backdrop.** Casey's rural value positioning is comparatively defensive, but fuel gallons and inside traffic are consumer-exposed. Gallon comps turning positive argues Casey's is taking share; it does not immunize the volume base. [unverified — specific macro figures (mortgage rates, July payrolls, headline inflation) are not traceable to the research brief or Phase 1 data]

**Verification gaps.** Several items commonly cited about this company could not be confirmed against filings: the FY2027-FY2029 three-year plan targets announced June 24, 2026 (the 8-K and issuer release returned access errors); FY2027 guidance figures generally; whether the CEO also holds the Board Chair title; the S&P 500 inclusion date, which if it coincided with the re-rating would explain part of the multiple expansion on non-fundamental grounds; and remaining buyback authorization (roughly $94M against a reported ~$200M FY2027 plan) after $156.8M at January 31, 2026. These are not treated as established facts in this analysis.

Risk factors 4.8, extending the decline from 6.5 to 5.0 seen across prior reviews. The reason is not new disclosure — the Q3 10-Q reported no material changes to Item 1A — but that the same risks now sit under a much higher multiple, which converts a moderate operating risk into a material price risk.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($1,010, +19.5%).** Industry fuel margins have structurally reset higher — marginal operator exit, higher card-fee and labor cost structures forcing rational retail pricing, and consolidation — and Casey's holds cpg above 42 while inside gross profit compounds 8-9%. Inside margin exceeds 42% on food mix and wings penetration; 120-plus new stores land, including a tuck-in program funded from FCF; CEFCO conversions accelerate comps rather than dilute them as kitchens anniversary. FY2028 EPS reaches $24-24.50 and the market pays 41-43x for a scaled, index-member, low-leverage compounder with 27 years of dividend increases. That is roughly the analyst high of $1,069 discounted modestly.

**Bear case ($630, −25.4%).** Fuel cpg reverts toward the FY2025 level of 38.7 as pass-through compresses and RIN pricing normalizes, costing roughly $2.94 of EPS before offsets. Inside growth absorbs most of the earnings hit, leaving FY2027 EPS roughly flat near $19.50 — but the multiple, awarded for a 31% growth year, compresses toward 32x. Compounding: CEFCO entry dilutes reported comps into guidance already set at +2-5%; the 120-store target slips or is met through an acquisition that adds goodwill and integration cost; card fees keep scaling with fuel prices; a weak Midwest summer removes the seasonally dominant quarter. $19.50 × 32x = $624.

**Base case ($880, +4.1%).** Casey's delivers approximately consensus — FY2027 EPS $21.00-21.50, EBITDA up 8-10%, inside margin above 42% — with cpg easing modestly from 42.57 but staying above the FY2025 level. Blended next-twelve-month EPS of roughly $21.90 (75% FY2027, 25% FY2028) at a slightly compressed 40x produces $876. Earnings growth is largely offset by multiple normalization.

Probability weighting 25% bull / 50% base / 25% bear yields an expected return of approximately +0.6%, with downside magnitude (−25.4%) exceeding upside (+19.5%). There is no margin of safety at $845: the bear case is 25% below, and the base case is within the noise of the last three months' trading range.

**On the score trajectory.** Prior reviews ran 6.3 → 6.2 → 6.7 (BUY, May 11) → 6.1 (HOLD, June 8), and this review lands at 5.76. The May upgrade was made at a price in the mid-$800s; the June downgrade preceded the print. Since June 8 the stock has risen from ~$752 to $845 (+12%) through a large single-day gain on the FY2026 report and a partial fade. Fundamentals improved — record EPS, margin expansion, guidance raised. But the improvement is concentrated in a line item the company itself flags as cyclically elevated, and the price has captured it. This is a case where price and fundamentals both moved up; the multiple moved further than the durable earnings power did. The financial-health improvement (6.5 → 7.0) is confirmed and retained. The risk-factor decline is extended, and valuation is the dimension that changes the recommendation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Horizon 12 months, with the decision point in early September 2026.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into strength ahead of the Q1 FY2027 report (expected early September; the September 18 option tenor implies ±10.4%). A reasonable approach is to reduce existing exposure by one-third to one-half at or above $845, retaining a core position to preserve participation if industry fuel margins prove structurally reset. Do not deploy new capital at this price. Prospective buyers should wait for a multiple in the low-30s on forward earnings — roughly $680-710 on FY2027 consensus of $21.31 — or for two consecutive quarters demonstrating cpg durability above 42 alongside inside comps at the top of the +2-5% guided range.

**Scenario arithmetic from $845.00:** base $880 (+4.1%), bull $1,010 (+19.5%), bear $630 (−25.4%). Analyst range $795 to $1,069, mean $957.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE:**
1. Two consecutive quarters with retail fuel margin at or above 42 cpg reported alongside stable or rising RIN-adjusted margin, confirming management's "elevated for the foreseeable future" position with the policy component stripped out.
2. Inside same-store sales at or above 4% with the CEFCO stores in the comparable base, demonstrating that the acquisition converts to Casey's economics rather than diluting them.
3. Price toward $700-720 with FY2027 EPS tracking at or above $21, which would place the multiple near 33x forward and restore an adequate margin of safety.
4. Net unit additions tracking toward 120 through organic builds and tuck-ins without a large levered acquisition.

**Downgrade triggers toward AVOID:**
1. Fuel margin below 38 cpg in any quarter without offsetting inside gross-profit acceleration, which would remove the earnings support for a 40x multiple.
2. A platform acquisition above $1.5B taking net leverage above 2.5x, particularly if goodwill rises above 40% of book equity.
3. Inside same-store sales below the +2% guidance floor in two consecutive quarters, indicating traffic deterioration rather than base-composition mix.
4. A material change to manufacturer tobacco rebate structures, given the ~9% of gross profit exposure and the explicit disclosure that cost increases pass through directly.
5. Q1 FY2027 EPS materially below the $6.72 consensus with operating expenses at or above the +7% guidance ceiling.

**Reassessment timeline.** Early September 2026 (Q1 FY2027 report) is the primary checkpoint, with the key disclosures being fuel cpg, RIN revenue, inside same-store sales on the new comp base including CEFCO, and the store roll-forward against the 120-unit target. A secondary checkpoint is any 8-K quantifying the FY2027-FY2029 plan targets, which remain unverified and which materially affect the durability of growth beyond FY2027.

## Conclusion

Casey's is a high-quality business trading at a price that already reflects the quality and much of the cycle. The operating record is real: 17.2% annualized EPS growth over the completed three-year plan, 504 stores added against a 350 target, inside margin at 42.2%, ROIC of 12.7%, FCF converting at 101% of net income, net leverage at 1.29x, and 27 consecutive years of dividend increases. Nothing in the FY2026 filings suggests deterioration, and the corrected credit metrics (Altman Z of 3.44 to 6.74; Piotroski 9/9) confirm that solvency is not where the bear case lives.

The bear case lives in the composition of the earnings that support a 39.7x forward multiple. Roughly $1.60 of Q4's ~$1.73 year-over-year EPS gain traces, on a single-variable basis, to a 9.25 cpg fuel-margin expansion, about 1.3 cpg of which came from RIN pricing set by federal policy. Full-year cpg of 42.57 is characterized in the company's own MD&A as historically above average. Inside gross profit growth would broadly absorb a reversion in earnings terms; it would not defend the multiple. At the same time, five of six named executive officers plus a director sold roughly $29M into the two weeks after the 10-K filing (plan status undisclosed, so no motive is inferred, but the CEO reduced his direct holding by roughly 18% in one day), the buyback is planned at prices well above the $563 average paid in November-January, and the FY2027 unit target requires roughly tripling FY2026 net additions.

Probability-weighted expected return is approximately zero with a bear case 25% below spot against a bull case 19.5% above. That is a trim, not a purchase. Existing holders should reduce exposure into the September print while retaining a core position; prospective buyers should wait for a forward multiple in the low-30s or for two quarters of demonstrated fuel-margin durability.
