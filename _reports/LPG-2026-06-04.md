**Research Date: June 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Dorian LPG is a pure-play owner/operator of Very Large Gas Carriers (VLGCs), the largest vessel class for seaborne transport of propane and butane. The company is founder-led, owns a modern 27-vessel fleet (averaging ~10-11 years vs. the ~11.8-year global average), and runs essentially all of its revenue through the Helios LPG Pool, a 50/50 commercial pooling JV with MOL Energia (a subsidiary of Mitsui O.S.K. Lines) that operates predominantly in the spot market. The result is a high-quality but extremely cyclical business: earnings track the spot time-charter-equivalent (TCE) freight rate almost one-for-one, with net income swinging from $307M (FY24) to $90M (FY25) back to $194M (FY26).

The FY26 result (revenue $481.5M, +36%; diluted EPS $4.54; FY TCE of $52,238/day), and especially the Q4 blowout ($153.3M revenue, $1.90 EPS vs. $1.21 consensus, $63,615/day TCE), trace directly to a Middle East supply shock: a roughly 90% reduction in Strait of Hormuz VLGC transits and damage to Saudi Aramco's Juaymah NGL terminal that cut Middle East LPG exports from ~4 MMT in January to ~1.3 MMT in March. US exports rose to fill the gap, lengthening ton-miles. By May 2026, Houston-Chiba spot freight reportedly touched a record ~$290/ton with day rates near $170K. This is a supply-shock rally layered on an already-strong market: high quality of print, low durability of catalyst.

The central tension is durable franchise quality against a peak-cycle, peak-geopolitics entry point. The stock at $40.77 trades below the consensus mean target ($48.4) on the headline number, but the more telling facts are that Freedom Broker downgraded to Hold on 5/22 (with a cited ~$38 average target below the price), directors and officers other than the CEO sold into the post-earnings rally (Director Kalborg at $45.06 on 5/22), and a ~27.5%-of-fleet orderbook through 2029 looms as a structural counterweight. The +65-80% consensus EPS revisions are an artifact of a transient freight peak, not evidence of a higher sustainable earnings base. We rate LPG HOLD.

## Company Overview

Dorian operates 27 VLGCs as of 5/15/2026: 21 owned (some on bareboat/finance leases) plus 6 time-chartered-in. The fleet is technically advanced: 6 dual-fuel ECO VLGCs, 19 ECO VLGCs, 2 older standard units, most scrubber-fitted, several ammonia-capable. The company is incorporated in the Marshall Islands with offices in Stamford, Copenhagen, and Athens.

Roughly 99% of revenue flows through the Helios LPG Pool, which commercially manages ~31 VLGCs and operates mostly on spot/voyage charters. Dorian receives a pro-rata share of pooled net profits. The structure maximizes upside in strong markets and downside in weak ones, with minimal fixed-rate contract cover. End customers contracted through the pool include ExxonMobil, Chevron, Shell, Equinor, TotalEnergies, Glencore, Vitol, Gunvor, Indian Oil, SK Gas, and Astomos. The main competitors are BW LPG, NYK Line, Petredec, and Avance Gas.

The structural demand thesis is growing US LPG export capacity (shale-driven NGL supply) feeding Asian petrochemical (PDH) and heating demand, which lengthens long-haul ton-miles. Management reiterated confidence in long-term LPG demand sustainability.

## Financial Analysis

Dorian is asset-heavy and highly cyclical, with earnings tied to the spot TCE rate per available day.

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $560.7M | $353.3M | $481.5M (+36.3%) |
| Net income | $307.4M | $90.2M | $193.7M |
| Diluted EPS | — | $2.14 | $4.54 |
| Adj. EBITDA | $417M | $206M | $305.1M |
| TCE rate/day | $62,129 | $39,778 | $52,238 |

The FY24→FY25 ~70% net income collapse, then a more-than-doubling in FY26, is the defining feature: this is a price-taker on a volatile freight rate. Q4 FY26 (Jan-Mar 2026) was the apex: $153.3M revenue (+102% YoY), $81.0M net income, $1.90 diluted EPS (vs. $0.19 prior-year quarter), $63,615/day TCE (+80% YoY).

FY26 operating income was $210.2M (44% operating margin), net margin ~40%, ROE ~17%, ROA ~10%. Daily vessel operating expenses improved to $10,557 (from $11,143 in FY25), reflecting genuine cost discipline. Operating cash flow was ~$210M with meaningful FCF after ~$94M capex (largely the Areion newbuild).

The balance sheet is solid but levered in the manner normal for shipping. Cash was $327.4M; current assets $450.3M against current liabilities $168.8M (current ratio ~2.7). Total debt was $565.8M ($100.2M current, $460.2M long-term); net debt ~$238M. Shareholders' equity was $1,139M against $1,872M total assets (D/E ~0.5x). The Altman Z-Score of ~2.6 sits in the grey zone, which is typical for capital-intensive shipping and not a distress flag given liquidity and asset coverage. The key balance-sheet item is the disclosure that aggregate vessel market value exceeded carrying value by ~$600M+ as of 3/31/2026, a net-asset-value cushion that supports covenants and explains opportunistic asset sales.

On capital allocation, management uses variable ("irregular") dividends rather than a fixed payout. FY26 paid four dividends totaling $104.7M ($0.50→$0.60→$0.65→$0.70), then declared a record $1.00/share (~$42.8M) dividend on 5/7/2026. FY25 returned ~$156M, but the company also issued ~$84M of equity opportunistically. At a $1.00 quarterly run-rate the implied yield exceeds 10%, but the dividend is explicitly discretionary and rate-linked. It should not be capitalized as a stable income stream; it will compress sharply when freight normalizes.

The earnings surprise record is itself a cyclicality signal: +27.5% beat in Q4 FY26 (3/31/26), but a -7.4% miss the prior quarter and a -61.4% miss in Q1 FY26 (6/30/25). The pattern reflects rate volatility, not a steady beat cadence.

## Growth Analysis

The headline growth picture is dramatic and overstates durability. Consensus EPS revisions are extreme: current quarter +82.7% (30d) / +68.9% (90d), next quarter +101.1%, current year +64.8% (30d) / +80.5% (90d), next year +48.8% (30d) / +88.5% (90d). Under our normal framework, revisions of this magnitude alongside revenue acceleration are a strong forward signal. The guardrail applies squarely here: this is not an inflection from a depressed structural base, but a freight-rate spike driven by a geopolitical supply shock that, by nature, is temporary. The revisions are repricing a transient $63K Q4 TCE and a ~$170K/day May spot peak, not a structurally higher mid-cycle rate.

The honest growth question is what TCE rate is sustainable. FY25 ran $39,778/day; mid-cycle is plausibly in the $35-45K range. Above the ~$26,000/day all-in cash cost cited by management, cash generation is strong; the issue is rate level, not viability. The medium-term overhang is supply: ~116 VLGCs/VLACs (~10.4M cbm) on order through 2029, an orderbook ~27.5% of the existing fleet, many ammonia-capable. Unless LPG demand growth absorbs this, rates compress over the medium term. PDH margins were deeply negative (~-$155/ton) in early 2026, a demand-side caution flag even as freight spiked on the supply side.

The genuine structural growth driver, rising US export capacity lengthening ton-miles into Asia, is real and supports a higher floor over multiple years. That is a durable tailwind, but it does not justify extrapolating the current spike. Net assessment: high near-term earnings, low durability, structural orderbook headwind.

## Valuation Assessment

LPG trades at ~9x trailing/forward earnings on $4.54 FY26 EPS. The peer median P/E in Oil & Gas Storage & Transportation is 24.7, but that comparison is misleading: the midstream peers (KNTK, AM, DTM, VNOM) are toll-road, fee-based businesses with stable cash flows, not spot-exposed shipping. The relevant comparable is INSW (International Seaways) at 7.1x, which is itself a peak-cycle tanker operator the energy complex treats as fairly valued, not cheap. A single-digit multiple on peak-cycle earnings is the market correctly refusing to capitalize transient profits.

The cleaner valuation anchor is net asset value. With ~$600M+ of vessel market value above carrying value on a $1,139M equity base, NAV per share materially exceeds book, a real downside cushion. But NAV is itself elevated by the same peak freight environment that lifts charter values, so it is not a fully independent floor.

On the estimate-revision framework, the forward P/E on the rising current-year estimate ($6.29) is ~6.5x. Ordinarily that would look cheap. Here it is a trap: the $6.29 estimate embeds the geopolitical spike, and the FY27 estimate ($3.01) already implies the Street expects normalization, a ~52% earnings decline year-over-year. On that normalized number the stock trades ~13.5x, which is fair, not asymmetric, for a spot-exposed cyclical.

The analyst spread is informative. Mean target $48.4, median $51, high $55, low $36, but Freedom Broker's fresh downgrade to Hold cites a ~$38 average target below the current $40.77, and the only recent rating action was a downgrade. The wide spread reflects disagreement on how fast rates normalize, not a clean undervaluation. We do not treat the consensus target as a floor here because this is a cyclical at a rate peak, not a cheap inflection stock. Valuation is neutral: cheap on trailing/peak earnings, fair on normalized earnings, with NAV support that is itself cycle-inflated.

## Competitive Landscape

Dorian's competitive position is solid and stable-to-improving on fleet quality, though it operates in a price-taking spot market where no operator has pricing power. The VLGC market is a concentrated oligopoly (Dorian, BW LPG, Petredec, NYK, Avance Gas). Dorian's edges are a modern, fuel-efficient, scrubber/dual-fuel/ammonia-ready fleet and the scale and commercial reach of the Helios Pool with Mitsui O.S.K. The fleet is younger than the global average, and the ammonia-capable positioning is a forward hedge against the energy-transition optionality the orderbook is partly built around.

The negative trajectory is sector-wide: the orderbook through 2029 threatens to compress rates regardless of any single operator's quality. Dorian cannot moat itself against industry oversupply; its relative advantage is being a lower-cost, higher-spec operator that survives a downturn better than peers, which is why the founder frames decisions around replacement economics. Competitive position score reflects above-average fleet quality and commercial scale against a deteriorating industry supply backdrop.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Extreme spot-market concentration (~99% of revenue via Helios).** No contracted backlog cushions a freight downturn; rate reversion flows almost directly to net income, as the FY24→FY25 ~70% collapse demonstrated.

2. **Geopolitical whipsaw.** The Hormuz disruption and Aramco terminal damage that drove the FY26 upside can reverse abruptly on de-escalation. This is the dominant near-term risk: the current rate environment is supply-shock-driven, not demand-led. Physical normalization would compress rates from anomalous peaks.

3. **Orderbook overhang.** The ~27.5%-of-fleet orderbook through 2029 is the central medium-term bear case. If demand growth lags newbuild deliveries, rates compress structurally.

4. **Operating leverage.** All-in cash cost ~$26,000/day; 6 chartered-in vessels carry $61M of fixed hire that does not flex down in weak markets.

5. **Variable dividend is not a yield promise.** The escalating payout will compress sharply on rate normalization.

6. **Single-commodity, single-vessel-class exposure** with no diversification, plus Section 883/PFIC tax-structure risk and concentrated CEO ownership (~11-12%) with anti-takeover provisions.

These are largely company-specific catalysts for downside that are not fully priced because the market is anchoring on record trailing results. The risk score reflects genuine asymmetry to the downside from a rate peak.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 54.0% against 1-year HV of 41.1% (rich by 12.9 points), with the term structure in backwardation (54.0% near-term vs. 40.9% long-term). The nearest expiry implies a ±8.8% move (with the 6-month tenor implying ±18.8%). Skew is call-heavy (-19.7%) and put/call OI is very low (0.04), consistent with speculative upside positioning around freight rates rather than hedging demand; the backwardation and elevated near-term IV signal the market expects a binary resolution soon, tied to whether the Middle East freight premium holds or normalizes. For a position holder this argues for caution into the next several weeks and, given near-record IV, favors expressing any view via covered-call overwrite rather than adding outright.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$55).** The Middle East disruption persists or rerouting structurally lengthens ton-miles; US export growth and Panama Canal congestion sustain TCE in the $50K+ range; the NAV cushion and large variable dividend (~10%+ implied yield) reward holders while the founder-CEO, who bought at $17.75 (Apr 2025) and $27.30 (Jan 2026), continues to expand the fleet at attractive replacement economics. At a sustained high-rate environment, ~13x on elevated earnings supports the $55 high target.

**Bear case (~$26-30, -26% to -36%).** Hormuz normalizes, freight reverts toward the FY25 $39,778/day level or lower, the orderbook begins delivering into 2027-2029, the dividend is cut sharply, and the stock de-rates to ~$3.01 FY27 EPS at a cyclical-trough multiple. Insider selling into the post-earnings rally (Kalborg at $45.06, CCO Hansen, CFO Young, COO Hadjipateras) and the analyst downgrade signal that sophisticated holders view current levels as fair-to-rich.

**Base case (~$40-42).** Rates moderate from the May peak but stay elevated through the summer on residual disruption; the stock holds near current levels as the durable-quality bid offsets the normalization risk. This is roughly where the stock trades today, which is why the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the FQ1 FY27 earnings release (~August 2026), plus any confirmed Strait of Hormuz reopening with 72+ hours of sustained commercial transit, whichever comes first.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence that TCE rates sustain above ~$50K/day on demand-led (US export ton-mile) rather than supply-shock drivers, confirmed over two quarters.
- A pullback toward the mid-$30s (near the ~$38 consensus average and within reach of NAV-supported levels), restoring asymmetry.
- Renewed CEO open-market buying on weakness, or a structural reduction in the VLGC orderbook outlook.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Confirmed Hormuz reopening (sustained transit) with Baltic VLGC Index reverting toward Q4 CY25 levels (~$68/ton) and the dividend cut below $0.50/quarter.
- FY27 estimates rolling over below $3.00 with no offsetting demand catalyst.
- A break of the variable-dividend cadence combined with rate reversion toward the ~$26K/day cash-cost floor.

## Conclusion

Dorian is a best-in-class, founder-led VLGC operator with a modern fleet, ~$600M of asset value above book, disciplined costs, and a shareholder-friendly variable dividend. The franchise quality is genuinely high. The problem is the entry point. FY26's record earnings and the consensus revisions are powered by a geopolitical supply shock that is by nature temporary, with the orderbook waiting to compress rates over the medium term. The stock trades above the ~$38 average target that prompted a fresh downgrade, directors and officers other than the CEO sold into the post-earnings rally, and the options market is pricing imminent binary resolution. The CEO's contrarian buys near the lows are a real positive, but they were made far below the current price and do not validate adding at a rate peak.

The combination of high fundamental quality and unfavorable timing resolves to HOLD. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the mid-$30s or on confirmation that elevated rates are demand-led rather than supply-shock-driven, and would exit on a confirmed Hormuz reopening with rate reversion. This is consistent with the broader maritime-energy positioning, where vessels that monetize the disruption premium directly carry the largest two-sided binary into any genuine de-escalation.
