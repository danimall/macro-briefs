## Executive Summary

Graphic Packaging trades at $9.71, down 59% from its 52-week high of $23.76. The stock is priced at ~10.4x trailing earnings and implies a 24-28% free cash flow yield if management achieves its $700-800M adjusted FCF guidance for FY2026. The core question is whether this discount reflects permanent impairment or a transition trough: the company just completed a ~$2B+ Waco recycled paperboard facility, fired its CEO over unauthorized capital expenditures, disclosed a material weakness in internal controls, and faces multiple securities fraud lawsuits.

The bull case rests on capex normalization ($450M in 2026 vs. $922M in 2025), a new CEO executing a $60M cost reduction program, and a structural sustainability tailwind converting plastic packaging to paperboard. Four insiders — including the new CEO — purchased shares in March-May 2026 at prices 15-20% above current levels. The bear case centers on leverage risk (4.18x with a 5.00x covenant ceiling), continued EBITDA compression below the $1.05B floor of guidance, and the possibility that Waco's returns never materialize at a scale justifying its cost overruns.

Consensus estimates are declining across all periods (current year EPS down 22% in 90 days) and the analyst community is split (7 Hold, 1 Buy, 1 Sell, 1 Strong Sell). The risk/reward favors holding for investors already positioned, but falling estimates and unresolved governance issues make adding exposure premature until Q2 2026 results confirm Waco ramp progress.

## Company Overview

Graphic Packaging is a vertically integrated global producer of consumer packaging from renewable and recycled materials. The company operates 100+ facilities across 20+ countries with approximately 23,000 employees (59% unionized). It serves blue-chip CPG companies including AB InBev, PepsiCo, Coca-Cola, Kraft Heinz, General Mills, Nestlé, McDonald's, and P&G — with no single customer exceeding 10% of revenue.

The business operates through two segments: Americas Paperboard Packaging ($5.9B, 2025 revenue) and International Paperboard Packaging ($2.2B). GPK manufactures most of the paperboard it consumes domestically and designs/converts it into cartons, cups, containers, and multipack carriers.

The strategic narrative centers on its new Waco, Texas facility — a state-of-the-art recycled paperboard mill that commenced operations in Q4 2025. This facility positions GPK as a leading player in recycled content packaging, aligned with customer sustainability mandates driving conversion from plastic. Innovation sales from plastic-to-paper conversion reached $213M in 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trends:** Revenue has declined from $9.44B (2022) to $8.62B (2025), reflecting the Augusta divestiture ($711M asset sale, May 2024), open market volume weakness, and pricing pressure on bleached paperboard. Q1 2026 showed modest improvement at $2.16B (+2% YoY), beating the $2.06B consensus.

**Profitability collapse:** Operating income fell from $1,174M (2023) to $804M (2025) to just $19M annualized in Q1 2026. The Q1 quarter included $40M in write-offs (automated roll warehouses), $18M in severance (500 employees), and $13M in impairment charges. Net loss was $(43)M in Q1 2026 vs. $444M net income for full-year 2025.

**Cash flow inflection:** Operating cash flow remained stable at ~$840M in both 2024 and 2025, demonstrating that the earnings collapse is heavily driven by non-cash charges and capital allocation decisions rather than fundamental cash generation deterioration. With capex dropping from $922M (2025) to $450M (2026 guidance), free cash flow should mechanically improve by ~$470M year-over-year.

**Balance sheet stress:** Total debt stands at ~$5.76B including $549M current portion. Net leverage reached 4.18x as of Q1 2026, triggering a covenant amendment in February 2026 that raised the ceiling from 4.25x to 5.00x through December 2026. S&P downgraded from BB+ to BB. Interest expense rose to $64M/quarter (from $51M YoY) as capitalized interest ceased with Waco's completion. Approximately 31% of debt carries floating rates at 5.3-5.5%.

**Key metrics:**
- EV/EBITDA: ~6.2x on FY2025 adjusted EBITDA of $1.395B
- Forward P/E: ~10.4x on $0.85 consensus EPS estimate (current year)
- FCF yield: 24-28% on $700-800M adjusted FCF guidance
- Dividend yield: ~4.5% ($0.44/year)
- Current ratio: 1.3x

## Growth Analysis

GPK's near-term growth profile is negative. Revenue is declining, margins are compressed, and consensus estimates are falling: current-year EPS has been revised down 22% in 90 days (from $1.08 to $0.85), next-year down 14% (from $1.37 to $1.17). Current-quarter estimates fell 30% in just 30 days.

The medium-term growth thesis rests on three pillars:

1. **Waco ramp:** The new facility should improve GPK's cost position in recycled paperboard as it achieves operational efficiency. Management guides $1.05-1.25B adjusted EBITDA for 2026, implying recovery from the Q1 trough.

2. **Sustainability conversion:** The plastic-to-paper packaging conversion generated $213M in innovation revenue in 2025 and $42M in Q1 2026. Major CPGs have committed to reducing plastic packaging, and GPK is positioned as a primary beneficiary.

3. **Cost reduction:** The new CEO's 90-day review identified $60M in annual cost savings, 500 employee reductions, and cancellation of low-return projects.

However, industry fundamentals remain weak, pricing headwinds persist ($32M drag in Q1), and the guidance range is wide ($1.05B-$1.25B EBITDA implies significant uncertainty about Waco's contribution). Revenue growth will likely remain flat-to-slightly-positive (guided $8.4-8.6B for 2026 vs. $8.62B in 2025).

## Valuation Assessment

At $9.71, GPK trades at a significant discount to peers (peer median P/E: 12.2x vs. GPK's 10.4x) and its own history. The critical valuation metrics:

- **FCF yield:** If management achieves the $750M midpoint of adjusted FCF guidance, the FCF yield is ~26% on the current $2.87B market cap. This is extraordinarily high for a company with stable blue-chip customers and structural demand drivers.
- **EV/EBITDA:** At 6.2x trailing and ~7.5x on 2026 midpoint EBITDA ($1.15B), this is below packaging sector averages (CCK at 15.4x, SEE at 12.2x, SON at 7.7x).
- **Dividend yield:** 4.5% — well above peer median of 1.9%.

The valuation discount reflects three specific concerns: (1) leverage risk that could force dilutive equity issuance or dividend cut, (2) governance uncertainty from the material weakness and litigation, and (3) whether Waco can deliver returns justifying its cost overruns.

If Waco ramps successfully and EBITDA recovers to $1.2B+, a 7x EV/EBITDA multiple implies ~$14-15/share equity value (50%+ upside). If EBITDA stagnates at $1.0B and leverage becomes problematic, the equity could compress further toward $6-7/share.

The market is assigning meaningful probability to the distress scenario, but the FCF guidance (if achieved) suggests the company can delever ~0.5-0.7 turns annually, resolving the core concern within 18-24 months.

## Competitive Landscape

GPK is a leading player in consumer paperboard packaging globally, competing primarily with Smurfit WestRock (formed from merger). Key competitive dynamics:

**Structural advantages:** Vertical integration (makes its own paperboard), multi-year contracts with blue-chip CPGs, and a state-of-the-art recycled board facility that few competitors can match. The Waco plant represents the largest investment in recycled paperboard production in the Americas.

**Sustainability moat:** Customer sustainability commitments create switching costs — once a CPG qualifies GPK's paper-based packaging for regulatory compliance and supply chain integration, switching to a competitor requires re-qualification.

**Challenges:** Smurfit WestRock's scale advantage, commodity pricing exposure on open-market board sales, and a unionized workforce (59%) that limits operational flexibility.

**Customer diversification** is excellent — no single customer exceeds 10%, and the roster represents the most stable demand base in FMCG.

The competitive position is stable-to-improving as Waco becomes operational, but weak industry pricing dynamics and the scale of the competitor post-merger temper enthusiasm.

## Risk Assessment

**Material weakness in internal controls (HIGH):** The Q1 2026 10-Q discloses that former management exceeded Board-authorized capex limits without transparency. This is the proximate cause of the CEO departure, CFO departure, securities fraud litigation, and the stock's collapse. While the company states no financial misstatement resulted, the disclosure signals deep governance failure and creates ongoing remediation costs and legal liability.

**Securities fraud litigation:** Multiple class actions cover the period Feb 4, 2025 through Feb 2, 2026, alleging false statements about inventory, demand, costs, and guidance reliability. Historical securities fraud settlements for companies of GPK's size average 2-5% of market cap, though outcomes vary widely.

**Leverage risk:** At 4.18x with a 5.00x ceiling, approximately 80bps of headroom exists. If EBITDA does not recover toward $1.1B+ in the back half of 2026, a covenant breach becomes plausible. The amendment restricts share repurchases to $65M/year and acquisitions through Sept 2027, limiting management optionality.

**Waco execution:** The plant is operational but ramping. Cost overruns during construction (acknowledged by FPA Queens Road) raise questions about whether return assumptions remain valid. Any significant maintenance or quality issues during ramp-up could delay FCF recovery.

**Goodwill impairment:** The International segment's goodwill ($525M) has only 2% excess fair value over carrying value — any European deterioration triggers a write-down.

**Short interest:** At 16.3% of float with 7 days to cover, significant institutional bearish positioning exists. This creates both continued selling pressure and potential squeeze dynamics if results improve.

**Inflation/rate environment:** With 31% of debt floating at 5.3-5.5% and interest expense already elevated, refinancing the $549M current-year maturities at acceptable rates is a real challenge. Every 100bps increase in GPK's average borrowing rate adds ~$18M in annual interest expense on the floating portion alone.

## Conclusion

GPK presents a classic turnaround situation with genuinely binary outcomes. The valuation is unambiguously cheap on management's guidance — a 26% FCF yield with a 4.5% dividend on a blue-chip customer base is anomalous. Insider buying by the new CEO and multiple directors at $11+ reinforces that informed participants see value.

However, falling consensus estimates, elevated leverage with thin covenant headroom, a material weakness disclosure, active securities fraud litigation, and a critical ramp-up phase for the company's largest-ever capital project create execution risk that exceeds normal levels. The 16.3% short interest confirms institutional skepticism.

The specific 12-month catalyst justifying a hold: GPK's capex drops by ~$470M in 2026, mechanically converting the company from a cash-burning capital project phase to a substantial FCF generator. If any portion of the $700-800M FCF guidance materializes, the stock reprices upward from delevering alone. The Q1 2026 beat ($0.09 vs. $0.06 consensus) provides early evidence that the trough may have passed. Conviction requires Q2 confirmation, making this a monitoring position rather than one to add to or exit.

**Rating: HOLD** — turnaround thesis intact but unproven; wait for Q2 2026 execution confirmation before upgrading.
