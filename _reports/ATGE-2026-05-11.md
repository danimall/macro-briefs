## Executive Summary

Adtalem Global Education, rebranded as Covista in February 2026 (NYSE: CVSA, formerly ATGE), is the largest healthcare educator in the United States, serving over 97,000 students across three segments: Chamberlain University (nursing), Walden University (online graduate programs), and Medical & Veterinary (Caribbean medical and veterinary schools). The company generates $1.79B in annual revenue growing at 13% in FY2025, produces $283M in free cash flow, and has reduced its share count by 23% since FY2022 through aggressive buybacks.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) a structural supply-demand imbalance in U.S. healthcare workers that drives persistent enrollment growth, (2) operating leverage from a primarily fixed-cost educational model that converts revenue growth into faster margin expansion and EPS growth, and (3) management's capital allocation discipline — a $750M buyback authorization (roughly 17-20% of market cap) suggests conviction in continued compounding. At approximately 14–15x forward adjusted EPS with double-digit EPS growth, the stock offers meaningful upside relative to its growth trajectory.

The principal risk is regulatory: the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA, July 2025) phases out Grad PLUS loans and introduces new aggregate borrowing caps that could affect enrollment at Walden and the medical schools. The company's healthcare concentration (with demonstrably high graduate earnings) provides natural protection against "Do No Harm" eligibility provisions, and management is proactively arranging private lending alternatives. This risk is real but quantifiable, and the current valuation appears to compensate for it.

## Company Overview

Covista operates through three reportable segments:

**Chamberlain University** (40.6% of revenue, $725.8M FY2025): The largest pre-licensure BSN program in the U.S. per AACN data, operating 23 campuses plus online. Offers BSN, MSN, DNP, Nurse Practitioner, MPH, MSW, and MPAS programs. Average total enrollment grew 9.3% in FY2025.

**Walden University** (38.8% of revenue, $693.4M FY2025): Fully online university with 50+ years of distance learning experience, offering 100+ degree programs. Serves working professionals aged 25+. Average total enrollment grew 13.5% in FY2025 — the fastest among the three segments.

**Medical and Veterinary** (20.6% of revenue, $369.1M FY2025): Comprises AUC School of Medicine (St. Maarten), Ross University School of Medicine (Barbados), and Ross University School of Veterinary Medicine (St. Kitts). Residency match rates consistently exceed 95%.

The company was incorporated in 1987, is headquartered in Chicago, and employs approximately 7,000 people.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 revenue growth accelerated.** FY2022 to FY2025 revenue compounded at 8.8% annually, but the trajectory improved: +4.6% (FY2023) → +9.2% (FY2024) → +12.9% (FY2025). Q2 FY2026 revenue of $503.4M (+12.4% YoY) indicated continued momentum; however, FY2026 guidance of approximately $1.93B–$1.945B implies high-single-digit growth (approximately 8–9% YoY), reflecting normalization from FY2025's elevated pace. Q3 FY2026 revenue of $487.0M (+4.5% YoY) confirms this deceleration to a more sustainable rate.

**Margins are expanding on operating leverage.** Adjusted EBITDA margin widened from 23.8% (FY2024) to 25.7% (FY2025). Net margin expanded from 8.6% to 13.3%. GAAP operating margin jumped from 13.7% to 19.1%, boosted by the end of Walden intangible amortization ($24.4M annual reduction) and the absence of $34.2M in integration costs. These represent a mix of operating leverage, permanent cost reductions (completed amortization), and lapping of prior-period one-time charges. Strategic advisory costs increased approximately $12M in FY2025 and have continued into FY2026, partially offsetting these benefits.

**Free cash flow generation is robust.** Operating cash flow of $333.7M and FCF of $283.4M (15.8% margin) provide ample capital for buybacks and debt reduction. The company repaid $100M on Term Loan B in January 2025, reducing net debt to approximately $358M (~0.8x adjusted EBITDA) as of FY2025.

**Balance sheet has been restructured.** As of the FY2025 10-K (June 30, 2025), total debt was $558.3M ($405M Senior Secured Notes due 2028 + $153.3M Term Loan B). On March 2, 2026, Covista completed a refinancing, entering into $510M of new term loans due March 2, 2033, and using proceeds to redeem the $404.95M 5.50% Senior Secured Notes due 2028 and refinance existing term loans. This materially extends the maturity profile and removes near-term refinancing risk. The $500M revolving facility provides additional liquidity. The current ratio of 0.82 is below 1.0 but reflects the seasonal nature of tuition collection; the unused revolving capacity eliminates any real liquidity concern.

**Share count reduction is creating powerful EPS compounding.** Diluted shares fell from 48.4M (FY2022) to 38.3M (FY2025) — a 21% reduction. The $750M new authorization, if executed at approximately $117/share (recent price), could retire another ~6.4M shares, bringing the count toward approximately 32M (34% cumulative reduction from FY2022). Combined with revenue growth and margin expansion, adjusted EPS grew 33% YoY to $6.67 in FY2025. GAAP EPS grew 82% to $6.18.

**Return metrics are solid.** ROE of 16.5% and ROA of 8.6%. Piotroski F-Score of 4/5. The Altman Z-Score of 2.4 places the company in the "grey zone," but this is driven by the $1.73B in goodwill and intangibles from acquisitions (Walden, Chamberlain), not by operational weakness.

## Growth Analysis

FY2025 revenue growth was driven by volume (enrollment) and modest pricing (2-4% tuition increases):

- Chamberlain average total enrollment +9.3% in FY2025: Driven by the persistent U.S. nursing shortage. The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects approximately 189,100 annual registered nursing job openings through 2034. Chamberlain's scale (largest BSN program nationally) creates recruiting advantages and brand recognition. The November 2025 session enrollment decreased 1.0% YoY, suggesting some near-term normalization.
- Walden average total enrollment +13.5% in FY2025: Online graduate programs for working professionals benefit from both healthcare demand and the structural shift toward remote education post-COVID. Walden's adjusted operating margin expanded from 21.9% to 26.5% in one year, making it the most profitable segment ($183.6M adjusted operating income). Walden enrollment remained up approximately 13% as of December 31, 2025.
- Medical & Veterinary enrollment +0.5%: Essentially flat, constrained by clinical rotation capacity and Caribbean campus limitations. Growth here will come from tuition pricing rather than volume.

**Forward growth vectors:**
1. Walden's enrollment momentum suggests continued demand in online healthcare-adjacent graduate programs.
2. New program launches: MPAS (Physician Assistant) at Chamberlain represents expansion into a profession with approximately $133,000 median annual salary (BLS May 2024) and projected 20% employment growth through 2034.
3. The $12M in "strategic advisory costs" suggests management is evaluating capacity expansion or acquisitions.
4. FY2026 revenue guidance of approximately $1.93B–$1.945B implies 8–9% growth, a step-down from FY2025's 12.9% but still solid for a company of this scale.

**EPS growth trajectory:** Company long-term targets presented at the February 2026 Investor Day include FY2029 revenue of $2.35B–$2.53B and adjusted EPS of $10.60–$11.80. With high-single-digit revenue growth, continued margin expansion, and 5-7% annual share count reduction from buybacks, double-digit EPS growth is achievable through FY2029.

## Valuation Assessment

Following Q3 FY2026 results, management's raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance is $7.95–$8.15. At a recent share price of approximately $117, the stock trades at approximately 14–15x forward adjusted EPS.

Using the FY2025 adjusted EPS of $6.67, the trailing multiple is approximately 17.5x. With 33% EPS growth in FY2025 and a sustainable double-digit forward growth rate supported by management's long-term targets (FY2029 adjusted EPS of $10.60–$11.80), this represents a reasonable PEG ratio.

Comparable for-profit education companies and healthcare services businesses typically trade at 15-25x forward earnings. Covista at approximately 14–15x forward is modestly discounted to account for regulatory risk, which appears appropriate given OBBBA uncertainty but potentially insufficient to reflect the company's growth and cash generation profile.

The $750M buyback authorization suggests management believes shares offer attractive value. The authorization does not obligate any specific level of repurchase and may be suspended, modified, or terminated at any time.

## Competitive Landscape

**Structural advantages:**

1. **Scale in healthcare education**: Covista holds #1 market position by enrollment in BSN, MSN, FNP, and DNP programs. In nursing education specifically, capacity is the binding constraint — clinical placement sites, faculty, and simulation labs are all scarce. Covista's existing infrastructure (23 campuses, established hospital partnerships) creates barriers to entry for new competitors.

2. **Outcome quality**: 95-98% residency match rates for Caribbean medical school graduates. Cohort default rates of 0.0-1.1%. These outcomes provide both regulatory protection (Do No Harm compliance) and enrollment demand from students.

3. **Healthcare concentration**: Unlike diversified for-profit educators (which face more regulatory scrutiny on employment outcomes in liberal arts or general business), Covista's healthcare focus means graduates enter high-demand, high-salary professions. This alignment between the company's business model and regulatory priorities is a genuine competitive moat.

4. **Full continuum**: BSN through DNP, MD, and DVM programs. Few competitors offer this breadth, enabling cross-selling and brand building across the healthcare education pipeline.

**Competitive threats:**

- Public universities expanding online nursing programs at subsidized tuition rates.
- New Caribbean medical schools attempting to replicate the AUC/RUSM model.
- State restrictions on clinical rotations for international medical graduates.
- Technology-enabled low-cost competitors targeting the Walden demographic.

The nursing shortage provides insulation: when demand for graduates exceeds supply of programs by this magnitude, all viable programs benefit. Competition would need to add substantial new capacity to meaningfully affect Covista's enrollment, and the faculty/clinical site constraints make rapid capacity expansion difficult.

## Risk Assessment

**1. OBBBA / Federal Student Aid Restructuring (HIGH):**
The Grad PLUS phase-out is the most material risk. Walden's 82% 90/10 rate and the medical schools' 87% rate indicate heavy dependence on federal financial aid. If private lending alternatives prove insufficient or more expensive, enrollment could be affected. The implementation timeline (new students after June 30, 2026; existing students grandfathered) provides a multi-year runway. Management's proactive engagement with private lenders, combined with the high earnings potential of healthcare graduates (making them creditworthy borrowers), suggests this risk may be manageable, though the outcome remains uncertain pending implementing regulations.

**2. Title IV Dependence (MODERATE-HIGH):**
77% of consolidated revenue comes from Title IV programs. Loss of eligibility for any institution would be catastrophic — the SEC risk factors state that reductions, delays, or loss of Title IV funds could have a material adverse effect on the company's operations. The provisional PPA status (requiring $179M in letters of credit, with the credit agreement allowing up to $500M) adds administrative burden and signals regulatory attention. The company's strong outcomes and financial health reduce the probability, but Title IV termination remains a tail risk. This assumption could prove incorrect under adverse regulatory conditions.

**3. 90/10 Proximity (MODERATE):**
AUC and RUSM at 87% have only 3 percentage points of headroom. Under the updated rule (effective FY2024) that includes VA benefits and other federal sources, this margin could narrow further. An institution that fails the 90% threshold for two consecutive fiscal years would lose Title IV eligibility. Management must actively diversify revenue sources (international students, employer partnerships) to maintain compliance.

**4. Caribbean Operational Risk (LOW-MODERATE):**
Hurricane exposure, political instability, and dependency on U.S. hospital clinical placement agreements. The Medical & Veterinary segment is 20.6% of revenue — material but not dominant.

**5. Goodwill Impairment (LOW):**
$961M in goodwill + $765M in intangibles. RUSM's fair value exceeds carrying value by 165%, suggesting substantial cushion. A severe regulatory shock could trigger write-downs.

**6. Concentration in a Single Regulatory Framework (STRUCTURAL):**
All three segments are ultimately dependent on the U.S. Department of Education's regulatory framework. A hostile Administration or Congressional action targeting for-profit education broadly could create systemic risk. The current political environment appears relatively favorable, but this is cyclical.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($145-165, +24-41% upside from ~$117):**
- Revenue growth sustains at 8-12% as nursing shortage persists and Walden's online enrollment remains strong.
- Margin expansion continues (26-28% EBITDA margin by FY2027) as operating leverage compounds.
- Buybacks reduce shares outstanding to ~32-33M by FY2027, driving adjusted EPS toward $10-11.
- OBBBA implementation proves manageable — private lending fills Grad PLUS gap with minimal enrollment impact.
- Multiple re-rates from 14-15x to 15-17x as the market gains confidence in regulatory outcomes.
- Fair value: 15x $10.50 FY2027E EPS = $158.

**Bear Case ($70-80, -32-40% downside):**
- OBBBA Grad PLUS phase-out causes material enrollment decline at Walden (15-20% reduction in new graduate enrollments) and medical schools.
- Private lending alternatives prove more expensive, reducing affordability and demand.
- 90/10 ratio breaches 90% at one or more institutions for two consecutive years, triggering Title IV loss.
- Revenue growth decelerates to 3-5%; margins compress as the company invests to address funding gaps.
- Multiple compresses to 8-10x on heightened regulatory risk.
- Downside: 10x $7.50 FY2027E stressed EPS = $75.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $145** (15x $9.70 FY2027E adjusted EPS, incorporating continued margin expansion and buyback-driven share reduction). This represents approximately 24% upside from ~$117.

**Bull case target: $165** (15x $11.00 FY2027E EPS assuming OBBBA proves manageable and growth targets are met).

**Bear case target: $75** (~36% downside from current levels, requires material enrollment disruption from OBBBA).

**Timeframe: 12-18 months.** Key catalysts include: OBBBA implementing regulations (clarity on Grad PLUS alternatives), FY2026 earnings progression (Q4 results), and evidence of private lending partnerships filling the Grad PLUS gap.

**Upside/downside: +24% to base case, +41% to bull case, -36% to bear case.** Risk-reward ratio of approximately 1.5:1 to base case.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. If FY2027 enrollment growth at Walden declines below 5% (from recent ~13%), suggesting OBBBA uncertainty is already affecting demand.
2. If the 90/10 ratio at AUC or RUSM exceeds 89% in any reporting period, signaling imminent compliance risk.
3. If management fails to announce private lending partnerships by Q1 FY2027 (calendar year mid-2026), indicating the Grad PLUS replacement strategy is not on track.

## Conclusion

Covista offers a compelling combination of structural growth (healthcare workforce shortage), operational execution (expanding margins), and shareholder-friendly capital allocation. At approximately 14–15x forward adjusted EPS with double-digit EPS growth potential, the stock offers attractive upside relative to its trajectory. The OBBBA regulatory risk introduces genuine uncertainty, but the company's healthcare focus, strong employment outcomes, and proactive management response suggest this risk is manageable and partially priced in at current multiples. The risk-reward skews favorably toward the bull case.
