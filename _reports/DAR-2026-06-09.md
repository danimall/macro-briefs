**Research Date: June 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Darling Ingredients is the world's largest publicly traded rendering and circularity company, converting animal by-products, used cooking oil, and food-industry residuals into feed ingredients, collagen/gelatin, and renewable-diesel feedstock. Roughly 64% of net sales come from Feed Ingredients, 26% from Food Ingredients (collagen, gelatin, casings), and 10% from Fuel. The earnings swing factor is the 50/50 Diamond Green Diesel (DGD) joint venture with Valero, accounted for under the equity method, so its profits enter the P&L as equity income rather than consolidated revenue. Reported earnings are therefore far more volatile than the underlying collection-and-processing operations.

The central event is the FY2025 earnings trough followed by a sharp Q1 2026 recovery. Net income collapsed to $62.8M in FY2025 from $278.9M in FY2024 and $647.7M in FY2023, driven by the U.S. transition from the $1.00/gal Blenders Tax Credit to the Clean Fuels Production Credit (45Z/CFPC). DGD's contribution to Darling's EBITDA fell to $6.0M in Q1 2025. Q1 2026 reversed this: net income attributable to Darling reached $134.3M ($0.83 diluted EPS) versus a $(26.2)M loss, combined adjusted EBITDA more than doubled to $406.8M, and DGD's EBITDA share leapt to $151.2M, helped by $177.7M of production-tax-credit recognition and a $48.4M favorable inventory adjustment. The core ex-DGD business also strengthened: Feed Ingredients gross margin rose 500bps to 25.3% and Feed operating income rose 270%.

Estimate revisions are running hot (current-year consensus EPS up 95.9% over 90 days), the stock has rallied 86% off its $29.15 low, and analyst targets sit well above the current price. The case for caution rests on three things: the recovery is front-loaded by credit-recognition timing that may not repeat at the same pace; leverage is 3.17x at a time when DGD is taking cash rather than distributing it; and there is no open-market insider buying to corroborate the bullish external commentary. The appropriate posture is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale, recognizing both the genuine operating improvement and the unproven durability of the policy-driven earnings beat.

## Company Overview

Darling operates 260+ facilities across 15+ countries on five continents, processes roughly 15% of the world's animal agricultural by-products, and produces about 30% of the world's collagen. The three segments serve distinct end markets:

- **Feed Ingredients (~64% of net sales):** rendered fats, proteins/meals, used cooking oil, bakery residuals, blood products, hides, and organic fertilizer (Sonac, FASA, Valley Proteins). This is the raw-material engine supplying DGD.
- **Food Ingredients (~26%):** collagen and gelatin (Rousselot, Gelnex), natural casings (CTH), and heparin/mucosa. Higher-margin with a 30–60 day processing cycle.
- **Fuel Ingredients (~10% of consolidated net sales):** the DGD JV plus European bioenergy (Ecoson, Rendac). DGD operates two plants (Norco, LA and Port Arthur, TX) with combined capacity of roughly 1.2 billion gallons/year of renewable diesel and SAF.

The competitive moat is vertical integration. Darling collects and pre-processes low-carbon-intensity feedstock (segregated used cooking oil and animal fats) and supplies it to DGD. DGD was Darling's largest single customer in FY2025 at roughly $1.2B, about 20% of net sales. Management describes DGD as "integral to how Darling operates."

## Financial Analysis

The FY2025 trough was severe, with net income down roughly 90% from the 2023 peak. The company's chosen performance metric, ROGI, fell to 8.55% from 16.4% in 2023 and 21.3% in 2022. Indexed total shareholder return of 65 against a peer-group 195 confirms the stock materially underperformed its proxy peer set during the drawdown. The root cause was the tax-credit regime change: the expiration of the Blenders Tax Credit at year-end 2024 and the messy transition to the Clean Fuels Production Credit created timing dislocations and weak renewable-diesel margins through 2025.

Q1 2026 (period ended April 4, 2026) shows a genuine recovery, with total net sales up 12% to $1.55B. The DGD swing dominated: reflecting $177.7M of production-tax-credit recognition (net of fees), 272.4M gallons sold at $1.11/gal EBITDA, a favorable inventory mark, and higher RIN values after the EPA finalized 2026–27 renewable volume obligations. Beneath the JV, the core segments improved: Feed Ingredients gross margin rose to 25.3% from 20.3% on higher fat prices and mix, lifting Feed operating income 270% to $77.8M; Food Ingredients operating income rose 23% to $50.8M.

The balance sheet is the constraint. Total debt is roughly $4.1B against only $116M of cash, with a bank-covenant leverage ratio of 3.17x at quarter-end. The capital structure includes a $900M Term A (2031), $1.0B 6% notes (2030), $500M 5.25% notes (2027), €750M 4.5% notes (2032), and roughly $806M drawn on a $2.0B revolver, refinanced in June 2025. Management describes the company as "highly leveraged" and dependent on upstream subsidiary cash and DGD distributions to service debt.

The cash-flow optics complicate the earnings recovery. Operating cash flow fell to $153.0M from $249.0M YoY even as earnings rose, because DGD took cash rather than returned it: Darling received zero DGD distributions in Q1 2026 (versus $129.5M in Q1 2025) and contributed $190.1M of capital to the JV. Distributions resumed after quarter-end ($68.7M received). The deleveraging thesis therefore depends on DGD sustaining both EBITDA and distributions. Capital allocation priority per the 2026 Investor Day is debt paydown first; the $500M buyback authorization ($460.3M remaining) saw zero repurchases in Q1.

The earnings-surprise record reinforces the volatility theme: across the last four quarters, three misses (Q2 2025 −65.9%, Q3 2025 −37.7%, Q4 2025 −13.3%) preceded the Q1 2026 +45.9% beat. Consensus visibility through the trough was poor, and the single beat does not yet establish a reliable pattern.

## Growth Analysis

Estimate revisions are the strongest forward signal in the data. Current-quarter consensus EPS rose to $1.33 from $0.66 ninety days ago (+103.6%) and from $0.98 thirty days ago (+35.5%). Current-year consensus rose to $4.72 from $2.41, and next-year to $4.97 from $3.09 (+60.6%). This is the revision velocity that typically precedes continued re-rating when it pairs with revenue acceleration. The 30-day move is roughly a third of the 90-day move on the current quarter, indicating consensus is still catching up to the Q1 print.

The structural growth drivers are credible but uneven in quality. The OBBBA, enacted July 4, 2025, extended the Clean Fuels Production Credit through 2029 but, beginning 2026, requires all eligible feedstock to be sourced from the U.S., Mexico, or Canada. This rule structurally favors Darling's North American collection network over renewable-diesel producers reliant on imported feedstock such as Chinese used cooking oil, a genuine and under-appreciated competitive advantage on the fuel side. On the ingredients side, the Tessenderlo collagen JV (NewCo Collagen LLC, Darling 85% / Tessenderlo 15%, announced December 2025) consolidates Darling's roughly 30% global collagen position into a steadier, higher-margin vertical. The UPI Bovinos acquisition in Brazil (~$119.5M for three rendering plants, expected Q2 2026 close) adds collection scale, and the agreed ~$90M divestiture of much of the U.S. grease-trap services business signals a deliberate shift toward higher-margin food/feed verticals and away from low-strategic services.

The caveat on growth durability is that a large share of the Q1 2026 strength came from credit-recognition timing, including retroactive 2025 CFPC inflation adjustments under IRS Notice 2025-37. Whether the revised estimates hold depends on whether DGD's ~$1.11/gal EBITDA is a sustainable run-rate or a recognition-driven peak. Management's decision to guide only the core ingredients business going forward (Q2 core adjusted EBITDA of $260–275M, FY2026 capex ~$400M) is a tacit acknowledgment that DGD is too policy-dependent to forecast reliably.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline trailing P/E of 42.4x on $0.39 trailing EPS is meaningless here because the trailing window captures the FY2025 trough. The relevant lens is forward earnings against rapidly rising estimates. On current-year and next-year consensus, the stock at ~$57.59 trades at roughly 12.2x and 11.6x forward EPS. Against the Consumer Staples peer median P/E of 24.5, that is a steep discount, and the discount is wider still adjusted for the growth rate implied by the revisions.

Three factors justify a substantial portion of the discount. First, a large fraction of forward EPS rides on DGD, which is policy-driven, equity-method, and lumpy; the market reasonably applies a lower multiple to earnings of that quality. Second, leverage at 3.17x and the dependence on DGD distributions for deleveraging limit the multiple. Third, the earnings stream has demonstrated 90% peak-to-trough variability within two years, which warrants a volatility discount versus stable staples peers. Valuing DAR as "stable core ingredients plus an option on DGD/biofuel policy," rather than blending a single multiple, is the more honest framework.

Analyst price targets span $70 (low) to $85 (high) with a $76.4 mean, all well above the current ~$57.59. On a cheap stock with rising estimates, consensus targets tend to lag, and the cluster of 12 Buy/Strong Buy ratings with zero Holds reflects genuine constructive sentiment. This conviction co-exists with the absence of insider buying and the genuine risk that DGD economics normalize lower. The valuation is reasonable to mildly attractive on forward numbers, but the wide gap between price and target reflects unresolved uncertainty about which forward earnings path is correct, not a clean mispricing. A mid-cycle normalized earnings power somewhere between the trough and the Q1 2026 annualized run-rate, capitalized at a mid-teens multiple to reflect quality and leverage, lands the fair value in the high-$50s to high-$60s range, close to the current price, which supports a balanced rather than directional stance.

## Competitive Landscape

Darling is the scale leader in global rendering with no pure-play public peer of comparable size, which is itself a moat: collection density and pre-processing capability are hard to replicate. The vertical integration into low-CI feedstock is a durable cost and carbon-intensity advantage precisely because the CFPC rewards low-CI domestic feedstock, and the OBBBA's North-America-only sourcing rule disadvantages import-dependent competitors. On collagen, the ~30% global share and the Tessenderlo consolidation reinforce leadership in a steadier, higher-margin vertical with health, pharma, and pet-food demand. Feed gross margin expansion signals improving pricing and mix in the core, not a deteriorating competitive position.

The trajectory is improving on the fuel-feedstock and collagen fronts and stable-to-improving in the core feed business. The principal competitive vulnerability is not displacement but the fact that the most economically significant earnings driver sits inside a JV exposed to policy levers (RVOs, LCFS, CFPC rates) that Darling does not control.

## Risk Assessment

**Biofuel policy and tax-credit dependence** is the dominant risk and the direct cause of the 2025 collapse. Earnings are extraordinarily sensitive to U.S. renewable-fuel policy, credit rates, and recognition timing. Any change, non-enforcement, or eligibility failure flows straight to margins.

**DGD concentration and equity-method opacity:** a single 50/50 JV drove the swing from a $26M loss to a $134M profit in one quarter. DGD carries its own debt, including a $400M non-recourse revolver maturing June 23, 2026 to watch for refinancing. Results are inherently lumpy from inventory marks and credit-recognition timing.

**Leverage at 3.17x** is tightly tied to DGD cash flows. The $500M 5.25% notes mature April 2027, and the repay/refinance decision is open. A renewed biofuel downturn would pressure both EBITDA and the deleveraging path.

**Commodity and FX volatility:** feed margins track Jacobsen fat/protein prices and competing oils; FX swung operating income roughly $14.4M favorably in Q1 2026, a tailwind that can reverse. **Trade/tariff exposure** spans Brazil, Canada, China, the EU, and Mexico, with added uncertainty from the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling on certain tariffs. **Animal-disease/biosecurity** (avian flu, ASF, BSE) can reduce raw-material volumes. Legal items (Lower Passaic River Superfund, multiemployer pension withdrawal liabilities) appear contained but ongoing.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 43.7% is rich relative to one-year historical volatility of 38.1% (a 5.5pp spread), and IV has fallen sharply from 58.6% on May 13, indicating event-uncertainty has subsided since the Q1 print. The term structure is flat (43.7% near vs. 42.9% far), so uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated in a single catalyst. The 1-week put/call skew of 4.3% shows modest elevated near-term put demand, but the 6-month skew is negative (−4.3%), implying longer-dated positioning leans relatively bullish. Short interest is low at 4.2% of float (2.4 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic. The options picture is consistent with a stock that has rerated on improving fundamentals, with the market pricing moderate near-term hedging against a high-beta, policy-sensitive name rather than directional bearishness.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case:** The Q1 2026 recovery is the start of a sustained trend. The OBBBA's North-America feedstock rule structurally advantages Darling's collection network, DGD's $1.11/gal EBITDA holds or improves as 45Z economics stabilize, and core Feed/Food margins continue expanding. DGD resumes regular distributions, leverage falls below 3.0x, and the company executes the Tessenderlo collagen consolidation and Brazilian expansion. On normalized EPS toward the $4.50–5.00 range and a mid-to-high-teens multiple reflecting improved durability, the stock reaches the analyst target range of $70–85, roughly 22–48% above the current price.

**Bear case:** The Q1 beat is largely a recognition-timing artifact. DGD per-gallon economics normalize lower as retroactive credits roll off, RIN values soften, or policy enforcement disappoints. Leverage stays elevated while DGD continues to call capital, constraining deleveraging and any buyback. Estimates that rose nearly 100% in 90 days reverse as the next two quarters fail to match the Q1 cadence. In that scenario the stock retraces toward the low-$40s, a roughly 25–30% decline, in line with the volatility the earnings stream has already demonstrated.

The balance of evidence places the most-likely outcome between these poles. The core ingredients improvement is real and not policy-dependent; the DGD swing is real but unproven for durability; the valuation is reasonable but the discount is earned by genuine earnings-quality and leverage concerns. The absence of insider buying during the recovery tempers conviction in the bull case.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the fundamentals have genuinely improved, but the durability of the earnings driver and the deleveraging path remain unresolved, leaving risk/reward balanced near the current price.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July/early August) is the decisive checkpoint; it tests whether DGD economics and core margins sustain post-recognition catch-up, and whether DGD distributions normalize.

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters confirming DGD EBITDA at or above ~$1.00/gal without an outsized one-time credit-recognition component, validating the revised estimates.
- Leverage falling below 3.0x with DGD resuming regular distributions, demonstrating the deleveraging path is funded organically rather than dependent on lumpy JV cash.
- Open-market insider buying by the CEO or CFO that corroborates the recovery narrative.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- DGD per-gallon EBITDA normalizing materially below ~$0.70/gal, or a negative shift in CFPC enforcement/rates, that drives current-year estimates back down by 20%+.
- Leverage rising toward or above 3.5x on continued DGD capital calls without offsetting distributions.
- Failure to refinance the DGD $400M revolver (June 2026) or the $500M 5.25% notes (April 2027) on reasonable terms.

## Conclusion

Darling Ingredients is a scale-leading, vertically integrated ingredients company whose operating businesses are healthier than the headline earnings volatility suggests, but whose reported profits are dominated by a policy-sensitive renewable-diesel JV. The FY2025 trough was a tax-policy artifact, not an operating failure, and Q1 2026 demonstrated a credible recovery alongside genuine core-margin expansion. Estimate revisions of nearly 100% over 90 days and a forward P/E around 12x against a peer median of 24.5x argue the stock is not expensive on the new earnings path.

The reasons to wait rather than buy are specific: a large portion of the Q1 strength reflects credit-recognition timing whose run-rate is unproven; leverage depends on DGD resuming distributions for deleveraging; the surprise record is three misses then one beat; and insiders are not buying in the open market. The stock has already rallied to the bottom of a wide analyst target range, which means the market has priced in much of the recovery while the durability question remains open. HOLD is the appropriate call, with the Q2 2026 print and DGD per-gallon economics as the variables that will resolve the debate in either direction.
