## Executive Summary

Northwest Natural Holding Company is a $2.1B regulated gas utility undergoing a strategic transformation from a single-state Oregon gas distributor to a diversified multi-state utility platform spanning gas, water, and storage. The company's 2025 acquisition of SiEnergy in Texas and ongoing MX3 storage expansion represent genuine growth vectors: Texas contributes a 250,000-meter customer backlog (2.8x current SiEnergy meters) while MX3 offers $300M of investment at 12.5% ROE on 25-year contracts. The 70-year consecutive dividend increase streak is an anchor for income-oriented investors.

The central tension is between this growth optionality and two offsetting forces: (1) holding company leverage that spiked to 4.18x D/E after acquisition financing created a $0.60/share interest expense drag, and (2) Oregon/Washington decarbonization policies that pose existential long-term risk to the core gas franchise. At ~17.6x forward earnings and a 3.7% dividend yield, NWN trades at a discount to peer medians (19.2x). The stock is a reasonable income hold for patient capital willing to wait 2-3 years for leverage normalization and MX3 accretion, but doesn't offer sufficient asymmetric upside to warrant a full BUY recommendation given regulatory and execution risks.

## Company Overview

NWN operates three regulated utility segments: NWN Gas Utility (810,000 meters in Oregon/Washington), SiEnergy Gas Utility (~90,000 meters in Texas, acquired January 2025), and NWN Water Utility (~81,000 connections across six states). Supplementary operations include a renewable natural gas (RNG) business, Mist underground gas storage (21.6 Bcf capacity), and asset management services.

The company's franchise history of 167 years and its 70 consecutive years of dividend increases place it among the most durable income vehicles on the NYSE. However, the business model is in active transition from pure Pacific Northwest gas distribution toward geographic diversification (Texas) and asset-type diversification (water, storage).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** Revenue grew to $1.289B in 2025 from $1.153B in 2024 (+12%), driven primarily by the SiEnergy acquisition and new Oregon rates. Net income reached $155M ($2.77 diluted EPS), with adjusted EPS of $2.93 after excluding acquisition costs. Segment contributions: NWN Gas $2.69, SiEnergy $0.33, Water $0.35, with -$0.60 from holding company interest expense.

**Capital Structure — The Key Concern:** Consolidated equity ratio dropped from 44.8% to 37.7% following acquisition financing. NW Holdings carries $2.27B in long-term debt, including $325M of junior subordinated debentures at 7.00% fixed. The regulated subsidiary (NW Natural) maintains a healthy 49.6% equity ratio, but the holding company overlay creates a structural interest expense headwind. Indebtedness/capitalization sits at 60.7% vs. a 70% covenant limit — adequate headroom but not comfortable.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow improved 34% to $269M, but capital expenditure of $467M (including $340M in acquisition spending) produced deeply negative free cash flow. 2026 capex guidance of $500-550M and a 5-year plan of $2.6-2.9B indicate this will persist. Funding relies on a combination of ATM equity issuance ($40-50M/year), debt, and retained earnings.

**ROE Gap:** Reported ROE of 7.7% sits well below the authorized 9.4-9.5% in Oregon, indicating regulatory lag. New rates effective October/November 2025 and pending Washington rate case settlements should close this gap partially, but full convergence requires successful execution of multiple regulatory filings.

**Dividend:** $1.97/share annualized on $2.93 adjusted EPS = 67% payout ratio, above the target 55-65% range. The streak is safe near-term but future increases depend on earnings growth execution.

## Growth Analysis

**Forward guidance:** Management targets $2.95-$3.15 EPS for 2026 and 4-6% long-term EPS CAGR (5-7% with MX3). Rate base growth is guided at 6-8% annually with organic customer growth of 2-3%.

**SiEnergy Texas Growth:** The 250,000-meter customer backlog is the strongest organic growth asset in NWN's portfolio. With 18% customer growth in year one and rates among the lowest of Texas LDC peers, this business has multi-year runway. Texas HB 4384 allows deferral/recovery of plant costs between rate cases, reducing regulatory lag risk versus Oregon.

**MX3 Storage Expansion:** A $300M investment at 12.5% ROE on 25-year contracts with investment-grade counterparties is highly attractive for a utility. FERC regulation with agreed terms de-risks the project. Notice to proceed expected by end of 2027 with in-service by 2029 — 3 years away from contributing meaningfully.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current quarter estimates have improved materially (+76% over 30 days, +48% over 90 days), though this reflects seasonal loss narrowing rather than structural momentum. Full-year 2026 estimates are essentially flat ($3.05, -0.1% over 30 days). No compelling estimate revision velocity exists here.

**Constraints on Growth:** Oregon meter growth slowed to 0.5% in 2025 from 0.8% in 2024. Total gas volumes declined 5% in 2025. Weather was 20% warmer than average. The core Oregon business is ex-growth absent rate relief, placing the entire growth burden on Texas, water, and storage.

At $48.66, the valuation is fair for a utility growing at 4-6% with above-average leverage and policy risk.

## Competitive Landscape

**Franchise Protection:** NWN's Oregon/Washington territories are exclusive by regulation. The Mist storage facility has limited Pacific Northwest competition and addresses a genuine regional need (9 GW capacity shortfall by 2030). The water utility platform, while small, participates in an industry with strong consolidation tailwinds.

**Texas Competitive Dynamics:** Unlike Oregon/Washington, Texas has no exclusive service territories for gas. SiEnergy competes on cost and reliability, with rates among the lowest of Texas LDC peers. The 250,000-meter backlog provides visibility but is ultimately dependent on Texas housing market conditions.

**Strategic Positioning:** NWN's multi-utility approach (gas + water + storage) is relatively unique among gas utilities and provides revenue diversification. The storage expansion specifically addresses the reliability gap created by the same electrification policies threatening the gas distribution business — a strategic hedge.

**Concerns:** The core Oregon business faces competitive pressure from electric heat pumps, building electrification mandates, and aggressive state climate goals (50% GHG reduction by 2035, 90% by 2050). Oregon's phaseout of line extension allowances by November 2027 directly limits new customer additions.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Decarbonization Policy Risk (HIGH):** Oregon CPP requires 90% GHG reduction by 2050. Washington CCA requires 95% reduction. These mandates imply substantial throughput decline over 20+ years. While NWN is diversifying geographically, Oregon/Washington still represent ~90% of gas meters. The timeline is long but directionally clear.

2. **Holding Company Leverage (MODERATE-HIGH):** 4.18x D/E at the holding company level with 7% junior subordinated notes creates earnings drag that requires 3+ years of ATM issuance and earnings growth to normalize. Ring-fencing provisions limit dividend upstreaming if NW Natural's capital structure deteriorates.

3. **Regulatory Lag (MODERATE):** Oregon's HB 3179 restricts rate case filings within 18 months of last increase. With inflation running 3-4%, inability to file timely rate cases erodes earned ROE (currently 7.7% vs. 9.4% authorized).

4. **Volume Decline (MODERATE):** Gas volumes fell 5% in 2025 and 3% in 2024. Warmer weather, conservation, and electrification all pressure throughput. Oregon meter growth of 0.5% barely offsets disconnection rates.

5. **Single Pipeline Dependency:** NW Natural relies entirely on Northwest Pipeline for gas transportation, with 60% of supply from Canada. USMCA uncertainty or pipeline disruption creates supply vulnerability mitigated partially by Mist storage.

6. **MX3 Execution Risk:** $300M project requires notice to proceed by end of 2027. Permitting, construction, and counterparty commitment risks exist. Delay would push growth accretion beyond 2029.

**Mitigants:** Regulated utility model provides downside protection through cost recovery mechanisms. Geographic diversification into Texas removes concentration risk. 70-year dividend streak creates institutional floor-setting behavior. Short interest at 3.7% suggests limited bearish conviction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($55-61, +13-25%)
- MX3 storage expansion proceeds on schedule, lifting long-term growth to 5-7% and justifying peer-multiple re-rating toward 19-20x
- SiEnergy Texas growth exceeds expectations with accelerating meter connections from 250,000-unit backlog
- Washington rate case settlement and Oregon ARM approval close the authorized ROE gap
- Leverage normalizes as ATM program and growing OCF reduce debt ratios
- Regional energy shortfalls prove that gas remains essential for Pacific NW grid reliability, moderating decarbonization policy implementation

### Bear Case ($39-42, -14-20%)
- Oregon/Washington decarbonization policies accelerate, with building electrification mandates spreading to more jurisdictions
- MX3 expansion delayed beyond 2029 or counterparties reduce commitment
- Texas housing market weakens, slowing SiEnergy meter connections below 10% annual growth
- Interest rates remain elevated, making 7% junior subordinated debt permanent rather than refinanceable
- Q1 2026 miss was signal of broader earned ROE deterioration; full-year guidance is cut

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For HOLD:**

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock pulls back to $42-44 (14x forward P/E), providing valuation cushion below peer median
- MX3 notice to proceed is confirmed, de-risking the 5-7% growth trajectory
- Holding company equity ratio recovers above 42%, demonstrating leverage normalization
- Oregon ARM is approved and earned ROE demonstrably improves toward 9% in subsequent quarter

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
- Oregon or Washington passes legislation explicitly banning new gas connections statewide (vs. current local-level restrictions)
- Management cuts 2026 guidance below $2.95 floor
- Holding company covenant (70% indebtedness/cap) approaches within 3% — i.e., above 67%
- Dividend growth suspended despite the 70-year streak (would signal fundamental cash flow deterioration)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (expected August 2026 for Q2), Washington rate case final order, MX3 notice to proceed status (check quarterly through 2027).

## Conclusion

NWN is a fairly valued income utility offering a 3.7% yield and 4-6% growth trajectory, supported by credible catalysts (Texas expansion, MX3 storage) but constrained by above-average leverage and long-term decarbonization risk to its core Oregon franchise. The stock's recent pullback from $55.99 to $48.66 reflects the Q1 2026 earnings miss, but management's reaffirmation of full-year guidance suggests this was weather-driven and temporary. At 17.6x forward earnings, the risk/reward is roughly balanced: meaningful upside exists if MX3 proceeds and Texas growth accelerates, but downside scenarios involving policy acceleration or execution failure are equally plausible. Income investors already holding should maintain positions; new capital should wait for either a pullback to $42-44 or de-risking of MX3 before committing.
