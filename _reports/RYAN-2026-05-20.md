## Executive Summary

Ryan Specialty is the second-largest U.S. P&C wholesale insurance broker and second-largest managing underwriter, operating predominantly in the Excess & Surplus (E&S) market (78% of premiums placed) without retail brokerage operations. The stock has declined approximately 52% from its highs to approximately $33.67, driven by fears of a rapidly softening property insurance cycle and organic growth deceleration from 15.4% (2023) to mid-single-digit guidance for 2026.

The market is pricing significant impairment of the organic growth engine. Q1 2026 results provide upside evidence: organic growth printed at 11.8%, the Underwriting Management specialty grew 58.5% in 2025, and the company beat revenue estimates by 3.4%. However, management subsequently cut full-year 2026 guidance from high-single-digit to mid-single-digit organic growth and guided adjusted EBITDAC margins down 100–150 bps, signaling expected deceleration over the balance of the year. At ~17x adjusted net income and approximately 13x EV/adjusted EBITDAC, the stock is trading at a discount to historical multiples for a business generating 32% EBITDAC margins, $644M of operating cash flow, and demonstrating accelerating growth in its highest-quality specialty.

The primary risk is real: property insurance rates are declining faster than any cycle Pat Ryan has seen in 60+ years. However, the stock's drawdown has already discounted severe growth deceleration. The specific catalyst for re-rating is continued organic growth prints above guidance through 2026, combined with margin improvement from the Empower Program ($80M annual savings by 2029).

## Company Overview

Ryan Specialty operates as an intermediary predominantly in the E&S insurance market, connecting 35,000+ retail brokerage firms with 350+ specialty carriers. The company earns commissions and fees as a percentage of premiums placed without assuming direct insurance risk. Founded in 2010 by Patrick G. Ryan (who previously founded and ran Aon for 41 years), the company went public in 2021.

Three specialties drive the business (reported as one operating segment):
- **Wholesale Brokerage** (53.4% of revenue, $1.60B): Open-market placement of complex risks
- **Underwriting Management** (34.2%, $1.02B): 39 MGAs/MGUs with delegated carrier authority — fastest-growing at +58.5% YoY
- **Binding Authority** (12.4%, $370M): Quick-bind smaller premium policies

The "no channel conflict" positioning — Ryan has zero retail brokerage operations — creates a structural advantage as retail brokers consolidate their wholesale panels toward partners that won't compete with them for direct business.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Growth:**

| Metric | 2025 | 2024 | 2023 |
|--------|------|------|------|
| Revenue | $3.05B | $2.52B | $2.08B |
| Growth | 21.3% | 21.1% | 20.4% |
| Organic Revenue Growth | 10.1% | 12.8% | 15.4% |
| Q1 2026 Organic Growth | 11.8% | — | — |

Total revenue growth (including acquisitions) has been consistent at ~21% annually. Organic growth has decelerated, and management cut full-year 2026 guidance after Q1 from high-single-digit to mid-single-digit organic growth with margins down 100–150 bps — indicating expected material deceleration in the remaining quarters. Q1 2026's 11.8% print exceeded full-year guidance but does not by itself guarantee sustained outperformance given management's post-Q1 outlook revision.

**Profitability:**

Adjusted EBITDAC margins are stable at 31.7% (2025), with the compensation ratio declining from 58.8% (2023) to 55.5% (2025) — evidence of genuine operating leverage. Adjusted net income was $548M ($1.96 adjusted diluted EPS). GAAP net income of $214M is lower due to partnership structure, intangible amortization ($274M), equity-based compensation, and acquisition costs.

**Cash Flow:**

Operating cash flow of $644M in 2025 (up $129M YoY) with only $68M of capex produces approximately $576M of free cash flow. This is a capital-light, high-cash-conversion business — FCF/adjusted net income exceeds 100%. Recurring structural cash uses including TRA payments ($25M), dividends ($62M), and distributions to non-controlling LLC unitholders ($27M) reduce equity-owner discretionary cash flow to approximately $460M.

**Balance Sheet:**

The Altman Z-Score of 0.55 is mechanically misleading. $4.3B of the $4.3B current liabilities are fiduciary liabilities (premiums held in trust, fully matched by fiduciary assets). Stripping these out, actual operational debt is $3.36B ($1.7B term loan, $400M 4.375% notes due 2030, $1.2B 5.875% notes due 2032). Net leverage is approximately 3.5x adjusted EBITDAC — elevated but manageable for a recurring-revenue intermediary business. Moody's upgraded the company from B1 to Ba3 in August 2025, validating improving credit quality.

Interest expense of $222M (up 40% YoY from acquisition debt) consumes approximately 23% of adjusted EBITDAC. At current rates (30Y >5%), refinancing the 2030 notes at higher rates is a medium-term headwind.

## Growth Analysis

**Organic Growth Trajectory:**

The deceleration from 15.4% → 12.8% → 10.1% → "mid-single-digit" guidance is primarily property-driven, though broader factors including direct placement by retailers and rate dynamics across lines also contribute. Pat Ryan stated the property market shifted "much faster on the way down" than any cycle in 60 years. However, several offsetting factors exist:

1. **Q1 2026 printed 11.8%** — well above the full-year guide midpoint, driven by casualty line strength across the majority of lines. Management's post-Q1 guidance revision to mid-single-digit growth implies significant expected deceleration in subsequent quarters.
2. **Underwriting Management grew 58.5%** in 2025 and is now 34.2% of revenue (up from 21.3% in 2023). This growth reflects a combination of organic expansion, recent acquisitions, transactional business, and contingent commissions. The specialty has higher barriers to entry and is more defensible than open brokerage.
3. **E&S market structural tailwinds remain**: E&S share grew from 13.5% to 25.7% of U.S. commercial premiums (2010-2024), though industry-level E&S growth dipped into single digits in 2025, with notable weakness in property lines. Elevated catastrophe losses continue to ensure complexity migration to E&S over the long term.

**Acquisition-Driven Growth:**

$747M spent on 5 acquisitions in 2025, $1.7B in 2024. The company has completed 60+ acquisitions since inception. The Underwriting Management build-out (Velocity, USQRisk, international MGUs) is strategically sound — these are higher-margin, more defensible businesses than open brokerage.

**Empower Program:**

The 3-year restructuring ($160M charges through 2028, $80M annual savings by 2029) positions the company for margin expansion when the cycle turns. This is intelligent counter-cyclical investment: use the softer growth environment to optimize operations before the next hard market.

## Valuation Assessment

At $33.67, Ryan Specialty trades at:
- ~17x adjusted net income ($548M / ~279M diluted shares = ~$1.96 adjusted EPS → ~17.2x)
- Approximately 13x EV/adjusted EBITDAC (enterprise value of ~$12.6B based on ~$9.4B equity value + ~$3.35B debt - $158M cash, divided by $967M adjusted EBITDAC)
- Approximately 16x free cash flow (~$576M FCF)

The stock traded at 30-40x adjusted earnings at its peak. The current multiple represents a compression to levels typically reserved for businesses with declining growth — which the Q1 2026 print challenges, though the post-Q1 guidance cut introduces near-term uncertainty.

The specific mispricing thesis: the market is pricing mid-single-digit organic growth as a permanent state. If organic growth stabilizes at 8-12% beyond the near-term property headwinds, with 32% margins expanding toward 34% via Empower savings, the stock deserves 22-25x adjusted earnings. On $2.10 FY26 adjusted EPS (conservative), that yields $46-$53.

Compared to insurance broker peers (Marsh & McLennan at 25-28x, Brown & Brown at 22-24x, Arthur J. Gallagher at 24-26x), Ryan's 17x multiple reflects an excessive cycle discount for a business with superior organic growth and margin profile.

## Competitive Landscape

Ryan Specialty's competitive position is strengthening despite cyclical headwinds:

1. **No channel conflict** is a genuine structural moat. As the 696-787 annual retail broker M&A transactions consolidate the industry, surviving retail brokers prefer wholesale partners that won't compete with them. Ryan is one of the few scaled pure-play wholesale platforms without retail operations.

2. **96% Producer retention rate** demonstrates the platform's value to its revenue-generating talent. Producers hired since 2016 typically exceed compensation costs by their second full year, creating a self-reinforcing talent acquisition model.

3. **Underwriting Management build-out** (39 MGAs/MGUs) creates stickier relationships with carriers than open brokerage. Delegated authority relationships take years to establish and are rarely terminated.

4. **Top 5 retail brokers represent 23.2% of revenue** (with no single retail broker exceeding 8.8%) — this is both a concentration risk and evidence of deep, institutional relationships that would be costly to replicate.

## Risk Assessment

**Insurance Cycle (Severe, Near-Term):** Property rates are declining rapidly with capacity increasing. Ryan earns percentage-based commissions — declining rates mechanically reduce revenue per policy. If casualty lines (currently hard) also soften in 2026-2027, organic growth could fall below 5%.

**Leverage ($3.36B debt, 3.5x net leverage):** In the current rate environment, refinancing the $400M 4.375% notes due 2030 could add $20-30M of annual interest expense. The company must maintain organic growth above debt cost to avoid value-destructive leverage.

**Acquisition Integration:** 60+ acquisitions create execution risk. The $274M annual amortization represents real economic cost. If acquired businesses underperform, goodwill impairment ($3.2B on balance sheet) becomes a risk.

**Dual-Class Structure and TRA:** The 77% voting control by Ryan Parties and $459M TRA liability make this less attractive to governance-sensitive institutional investors and create complexity that may sustain a valuation discount.

**Key Person Risk:** Patrick Ryan (87+) remains the cultural anchor. While CEO succession to Tim Turner is complete, Ryan's eventual full departure introduces uncertainty about relationship continuity and cultural preservation.

**What the market may be missing:** The severity of the property cycle headwind is known and priced. What's underappreciated is (a) the Underwriting Management specialty's growth providing offsetting revenue quality, (b) Q1 2026's organic growth demonstrating potential conservatism in guidance, and (c) the Empower Program creating margin upside in 12-24 months. However, the bull case may be underweighting the significance of management's own post-Q1 decision to cut guidance, which signals genuine uncertainty about the growth trajectory in the second half of 2026.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($50-55, +48-63%):**
- Organic growth stabilizes at 10-12% as Q1 suggests (casualty hardening offsets property softening)
- Empower Program delivers $80M savings ahead of schedule, expanding margins to 33-34%
- Insurance cycle catalysts (major catastrophe event, litigation nuclear verdicts) re-harden property market
- Multiple re-rates to 25x adjusted earnings on restored growth confidence
- Target: 25x × $2.10 FY26E adjusted EPS = $52.50

**Bear Case ($22-25, -26-35%):**
- Property AND casualty cycle simultaneously soften, driving organic growth below 3%
- Major retail broker internalizes wholesale operations or shifts to competitor
- Rising rates force refinancing at 7%+, adding $40M+ to interest expense
- Acquisition integrations disappoint, triggering goodwill write-downs
- Multiple compresses to 12-13x on sub-5% organic growth: 13x × $1.80 = $23.40

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $46** (22x adjusted earnings on $2.10 FY26 adjusted EPS). Timeframe: 12-18 months, driven by sequential organic growth prints demonstrating mid-single-digit guidance is conservative, plus early Empower Program savings flowing through.

**Bull case target: $53** (25x × $2.10) if property market stabilizes and organic growth sustains above 10%.

**Bear case target: $23** (13x × $1.80) if organic growth falls below 5% for two consecutive quarters with no catalyst for re-acceleration.

**Upside/downside from current ($33.67):** Base case +37%, bull case +57%, bear case -32%. Risk/reward ratio of approximately 1.7:1 on base case vs. bear case.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Two consecutive quarters of organic growth below 5% (indicating cycle damage is deeper than Q1 suggests)
2. Net leverage exceeds 4.5x adjusted EBITDAC (indicating acquisition discipline breakdown)
3. Producer retention falls below 90% (indicating competitive position erosion)

## Conclusion

Ryan Specialty represents a structurally advantaged franchise trading at cyclically depressed multiples. The drawdown has discounted severe organic growth impairment that Q1 2026's 11.8% print provides upside evidence against, though management's own post-Q1 guidance cut to mid-single-digit organic growth and 100–150 bps of margin compression signals genuine near-term headwinds. The combination of no-channel-conflict positioning, Underwriting Management growth, and the Empower Program's margin improvement potential creates a credible path to 37%+ upside over 12-18 months — contingent on organic growth stabilizing above the revised guidance. The primary risk — a simultaneous softening of both property and casualty insurance cycles — is real but lower-probability given elevated catastrophe loss trends and nuclear verdict dynamics supporting casualty hardening. At 17x adjusted earnings for a business that printed 11.8% organic growth in its most recent quarter with 32% margins, the market is pricing a worst-case cycle outcome that current results challenge but management's forward guidance does not fully dismiss. BUY with a 12-18 month horizon and $46 base case target.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The corrected EV/EBITDAC multiple (~13x, not ~9x) and management's post-Q1 guidance cut from high-single-digit to mid-single-digit organic growth with 100–150 bps margin compression reduce the magnitude of the valuation discount and weaken the "Q1 disproves the bear case" thesis underpinning the recommendation.
