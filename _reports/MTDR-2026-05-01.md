## Executive Summary

Matador Resources is a Delaware Basin-focused E&P company with 51% ownership of San Mateo Midstream, producing ~207,000 BOE/d (58% oil) from ~212,500 net acres in Southeast New Mexico and West Texas. The company is transitioning from a high-growth phase (production up 21% in FY2025, driven by the $1.83B Ameredev acquisition) to a capital efficiency phase, guiding FY2026 capex 11% lower while targeting 3% oil production growth. This should meaningfully accelerate free cash flow generation beyond the $883M produced in FY2025.

The stock trades at ~10.4x trailing EPS and 8.6x the current FY2026E consensus of $7.18, both below the peer median P/E of 17.5x. Estimate revisions are strongly positive: FY2026 consensus has risen 67% in 90 days and 34% in 30 days, reflecting improved commodity price assumptions. Four consecutive quarterly earnings beats averaging 6.8% surprise reinforce confidence in management's ability to deliver above expectations. Structural constraints on incremental US shale production create substantial upside optionality relative to the WTI strip pricing embedded in consensus estimates.

Key risks center on commodity price sensitivity (the company runs minimal oil hedging), geographic concentration in one basin, and ~$3.4B of consolidated debt post-Ameredev. However, direct leverage of ~1.1x Matador-attributed EBITDA is manageable, insider buying is clustered across four executives with zero open-market sales, and the 2026 capex reduction provides a natural deleveraging mechanism. At $62-63 per share, MTDR offers asymmetric upside if oil prices remain elevated and respectable downside protection through its low-cost Delaware Basin position even in a moderate pullback scenario.

## Company Overview

Matador Resources was founded in 2003 and is headquartered in Dallas, Texas. The company operates as a pure-play Delaware Basin E&P with a vertically integrated midstream component through its 51% ownership of San Mateo Midstream (JV with Five Point Infrastructure). San Mateo provides natural gas processing (720 MMcf/d cryogenic capacity), oil gathering and transportation, produced water disposal (475,000 Bbl/d), and natural gas gathering — serving both Matador's production and third-party volumes.

At year-end 2025, proved reserves stood at 667 million BOE (up 9% YoY), with PV-10 value of $8.24B. Approximately 61% of reserves are proved developed. The company completed the $1.83B Ameredev acquisition in September 2024, adding ~204 wells and substantially expanding its Delaware Basin footprint. FY2025 marked the first full year of Ameredev contribution.

The operational profile is weighted 58% oil, with the balance split between natural gas and NGLs. A smaller legacy position in the Haynesville/Cotton Valley plays in Northwest Louisiana generates minimal production and received no operated drilling capital in 2025.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** FY2025 revenue of $3.66B grew 5% YoY despite 21% production growth, reflecting a 14% decline in realized oil prices ($64.99/Bbl vs. $75.89 in FY2024). Net income attributable to Matador fell 14% to $759M. Adjusted EBITDA held essentially flat at $2.29B, demonstrating underlying cash flow resilience even in a lower-price environment.

**Cost Structure:** Per-BOE costs remained well-controlled. LOE of $5.50/BOE (up 6%) and DD&A of $15.82/BOE (up 1%) are within competitive range for Delaware Basin operators. G&A per BOE declined 11% to $1.81, reflecting scale benefits from the production base nearly doubling since 2023. Total cash operating costs of approximately $11/BOE provide meaningful margin at current oil prices.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $2.43B comfortably funded $1.53B in D/C/E capex, generating $883M in free cash flow (24% FCF margin). Capital allocation was balanced across dividends (~$163M), share repurchases (~$56M at an average of $41.31/share), and debt reduction (~$197M in net revolver paydowns).

**Balance Sheet:** Consolidated debt of ~$3.43B includes $2.15B in senior notes (6.25%-6.875% coupons), $398M drawn on the Matador revolver, and $883M on the non-recourse San Mateo credit facility. Excluding San Mateo's non-recourse debt, direct leverage is approximately 1.1x Matador-attributed EBITDA. The revolver has $1.80B of undrawn capacity, providing substantial liquidity. Credit ratings are BB-/Ba3/BB — sub-investment-grade, but the trajectory is toward improvement as deleveraging continues. The nearest note maturity is $500M at 6.875% due 2028.

**2026 Capital Efficiency Pivot:** Management guided FY2026 D/C/E capex at $1.35-$1.44B (down 11%) and midstream capex at $100-$110M, while targeting 3% oil production growth through efficiency gains. At current oil prices (~$63 WTI as of late April 2026), this implies FCF well in excess of $1B, accelerating both deleveraging and shareholder returns. The $400M buyback authorization has substantial remaining capacity.

## Growth Analysis

Production growth has been exceptional: from 93,120 BOE/d in FY2022 to 207,070 BOE/d in FY2025, a compound annual rate of ~30%. This was driven by aggressive Delaware Basin drilling and the Ameredev acquisition. The 2026 shift to 3% growth is a deliberate capital discipline choice, preserving FCF while maintaining production momentum.

FY2026 EPS consensus has risen from $4.30 to $7.18 over 90 days (+67%), with the 30-day revision at +34%. Next quarter estimates have more than doubled over 90 days (+115%), driven by the improving commodity price environment. If oil prices sustain at elevated levels, MTDR's realized WTI pricing would be substantially above the $65-70 strip assumptions likely embedded in these estimates.

Proved reserves of 667M BOE at a reserve-life index of approximately 8.8 years provide a long development runway. With 39% of reserves still undeveloped or non-producing, the organic drilling inventory extends well into the 2030s. San Mateo's midstream expansion (Marlan processing plant, Hugh Brinson Pipeline access) removes bottleneck risk and supports both Matador's production and growing third-party revenue streams. Midstream services revenue grew 17% in FY2025.

## Valuation Assessment

MTDR trades at ~10.4x trailing EPS ($6.09) and 8.6x FY2026E consensus ($7.18). Against a peer median P/E of 17.5x, this represents a roughly 40% discount. The dividend yield of ~2.4% ($1.50 annualized) is below the peer median of 3.0% but is supplemented by active buybacks.

On an EV/EBITDA basis, with an enterprise value of approximately $10.5B ($7.7B market cap + $3.4B debt - ~$0.6B cash equivalents/working capital adjustments) against $2.29B EBITDA, the multiple is ~4.6x — undemanding by any E&P standard.

The PV-10 of proved reserves at YE2025 was $8.24B, calculated using SEC pricing ($68.77 WTI, $2.80 Henry Hub). At current elevated oil price levels (implying WTI well above the SEC reference), the PV-10 would be materially higher. The current market cap of $7.7B sits below the SEC-price PV-10, and significantly below what a mark-to-market PV-10 would yield.

The analyst consensus target of $72 (median) implies 13% upside from the current $63.44, with a high target of $107 (+69%) and a low of $60 (-5%). The distribution is heavily skewed to the upside: 15 of 20 analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy, with zero Sell ratings.

At 8.6x FY2026E EPS and ~4.6x EBITDA, the market is pricing MTDR as though the current commodity environment is transitory. If elevated prices persist through FY2026, actual EPS could reach $8-10+, implying the stock is trading at 6-8x a more realistic earnings estimate.

## Competitive Landscape

Matador's competitive position within the Delaware Basin is strong and improving. The company is among the larger oil producers in the New Mexico Delaware Basin, with scale sufficient to attract premium service costs but small enough to benefit from continued per-BOE efficiency gains. The Ameredev acquisition was accretive to acreage quality, adding contiguous positions that improve drilling efficiency.

The San Mateo midstream ownership is a meaningful differentiator versus pure E&P peers like SM Energy, Chord, or Magnolia. Flow assurance through owned infrastructure eliminates takeaway risk that has periodically plagued Delaware Basin operators, particularly during periods of Waha basin gas pricing dislocations. The Hugh Brinson Pipeline access directly addresses Waha basis risk (($5.11)/MMBtu negative differential in February 2026) by providing a path to Gulf Coast markets.

Total shareholder return of approximately 355% over five years versus approximately 193% for the peer group demonstrates superior execution. The reserve replacement ratio — with proved reserves growing 9% year-over-year even as production rose 21% — suggests the asset base is not being depleted to fund current production.

The primary competitive weakness is geographic concentration: 98-100% of production from a single basin creates operational and regulatory concentration risk. Peers like Murphy Oil offer geographic diversification, though at the cost of higher corporate overhead and often less capital efficiency.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity Price Sensitivity (Critical):** FY2025 demonstrated the income statement impact of oil price weakness: a 14% price decline nearly offset 21% production growth in revenue terms. The company carries minimal oil hedging. At $55 WTI, FCF compresses significantly; at $45 WTI, the company would likely need to curtail the dividend and slow development. The current structural oil supply situation suggests price risk is skewed to the upside for the next 12-18 months.

**Leverage Post-Ameredev (Moderate):** $2.55B in direct debt is manageable at current EBITDA levels, but refinancing the $500M 2028 notes in a higher-rate environment could increase annual interest expense. Sub-investment-grade credit ratings constrain refinancing options. If management applies incremental FCF to debt reduction, net leverage could drop below 0.8x by year-end 2026.

**Federal Lands Exposure (Moderate-High):** 33% of Delaware Basin acreage sits on BLM federal lands. While current policy is favorable, this creates longer-term political risk. Permitting timelines are already longer for federal versus state/private lands.

**Waha Basis Differential (Moderate):** Gas production representing 42% of total BOE output is exposed to severe Waha pricing when pipeline capacity tightens. The Hugh Brinson Pipeline partially mitigates this, but negative Waha differentials can still force well shut-ins on high gas-to-oil ratio wells.

**Key-Person Risk (Moderate):** Founder-CEO Joseph Foran represents key-person risk, with the company explicitly carrying life insurance on him alone. The Co-President structure (Erman and Singleton) suggests succession planning is in place, but the transition has not been tested.

**Induced Seismicity (Low-Moderate):** New Mexico's seismicity framework could restrict produced water disposal volumes, potentially constraining production growth. San Mateo's disposal capacity would be directly affected.

Federal lands permitting tightens under policy change. The stock re-tests the $37-42 support zone established in late 2025.

## Conclusion

Matador Resources offers a compelling risk/reward profile at current levels. The stock trades at 8.6x a rapidly rising FY2026 EPS consensus, below peer multiples, during a period of structural oil supply tightness that consensus estimates have not fully absorbed. The 2026 capital efficiency pivot should drive FCF meaningfully higher, funding deleveraging and shareholder returns. Insider buying across four executives, with zero selling, signals management alignment with shareholders at these prices.

The primary risk is binary commodity price exposure, but the structural supply framework suggests elevated prices are more likely to persist than revert over the investment horizon. Geographic concentration in the Delaware Basin is a structural limitation but also a source of operational focus and cost discipline.

Position sizing of 2-3% is appropriate given the commodity price sensitivity and leverage profile. The stock warrants accumulation on pullbacks toward $55-58, with the $37-42 zone representing a high-conviction re-entry level if available.
