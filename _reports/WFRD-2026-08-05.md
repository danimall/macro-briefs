## Executive Summary

Weatherford is the fourth-largest diversified oilfield services company, with roughly 85% of revenue outside the United States. In 2026 that international weighting has worked against it in an unusual way: the Iran conflict that began in February pushed Q2 WTI to an average of $95.75 (from $64.63 a year earlier) and Brent to $103.28 (from $68.01), yet Weatherford's Middle East/North Africa/Asia revenue fell $78M year over year and the worldwide rig count declined to 1,760 from 1,777. The price signal and the activity disruption share the same cause. Prices reflect future capital plans; the conflict blocks present execution in Bahrain, Qatar, Iraq and Kuwait, where management said early recovery was interrupted by a flare-up in the ten to twelve days before the July 22 call.

The financial consequence is a second consecutive year of contraction. FY2025 revenue fell 10.8% to $4,918M; H1 2026 fell a further 5.8% to $2,257M; FY2026 guidance of $4.54-4.80B implies roughly a 5% full-year decline, and guided adjusted EBITDA of $951M-$1,046M implies a ~6.5% decline on top of FY2025's ~22.8% drop. Consensus is moving the same direction: current-quarter EPS estimates are down 10.6% in 30 days and 15.4% in 90 days, current-year down 5.8% in 90 days to $5.56, next-year down modestly to $7.27. Adjusted EBITDA margin has compressed roughly 370bps over two years to 20.2% in H1 2026.

What holds the stock up is the balance sheet and the cash conversion. Net debt was $380M at 6/30/2026 against LTM adjusted EBITDA near $1,023M (0.34x), with $1.7B of liquidity, zero revolver drawings, and 2025 upgrades from all three rating agencies. Management has raised FY2026 free cash flow conversion guidance three consecutive times to "mid- to high-40%," implying roughly $449-479M of adjusted FCF, or a 7.0-7.5% yield on the $6.41B market capitalization. At 6.8x guided 2026 EV/EBITDA and 12.3x next-year consensus EPS, the multiple is undemanding. The offsetting concern is that the cash flow improvement draws on working capital release (net working capital at 27% of revenue against a 25% target; receivables factoring collapsed to $13M in H1 2026 from $143M) and capex at the low end of the 3-5% framework, rather than on earnings. Both sources are finite. I rate this a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the balance sheet limits the downside, the multiple limits the required optimism, but with estimates falling, guidance back-end loaded, Russia concentration rising, and no verified normalization of Gulf transit, I cannot underwrite the upside with confidence.

## Company Overview

Weatherford operates in roughly 75 countries from about 302 locations with approximately 16,700 employees. It is domiciled in Ireland with operational headquarters in Houston, a structure inherited from a 2002 inversion and preserved through the 2019 Chapter 11 reorganization. That reorganization still shapes the balance sheet: a $1,022M accumulated deficit against $1,788M of shareholders' equity at 6/30/2026.

FY2025 segment detail:

| Segment | Revenue | y/y | Segment adj. EBITDA | Margin | Δ margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Well Construction & Completions | $1,875M | −5% | $515M | 27.5% | −108bps |
| Drilling & Evaluation | $1,371M | −18% | $309M | 22.5% | −523bps |
| Production & Intervention | $1,340M | −8% | $257M | 19.2% | −279bps |
| All Other | $332M | −18% | $42M | 12.7% | — |

Differentiation is product-line depth in managed pressure drilling, tubular running services and artificial lift rather than scale: at $4,918M of revenue the company is roughly one-fifth the size of SLB and one-quarter the size of Halliburton or Baker Hughes. Management stated explicitly on the Q2 call that it "will never do M&A purely for the sake of scale." The United States was 15% of FY2025 revenue and 14% of H1 2026, so the revenue base tracks national oil company budgets and sovereign credit rather than U.S. shale.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin.** Revenue went $4,331M (FY2022) → $5,135M → $5,513M → $4,918M (FY2025), with H1 2026 at $2,257M. Operating margin fell from 17.0% (FY2024) to 15.4% (FY2025) to 10.2% in H1 2026, though the H1 GAAP figure absorbs $21M of redomestication costs and $14M of M&A costs. Q2 2026 revenue was $1,105M, down 8.2% y/y and 4% sequentially, with GAAP operating income of $107M against $237M a year earlier.

On the adjusted basis that matters for the guide, Q2 adjusted EBITDA was $223M (20.2%) against $254M (21.1%) a year earlier, a ~90bp decline against a $99M revenue decline, and essentially flat sequentially. That is credible cost execution. The mechanisms, however, were cost action, capex restraint, walking away from the Saudi lump-sum turnkey contract, and a 31% reduction in R&D (H1 2026 $41M versus $59M; Q2 $20M, −33%) while SG&A rose 5% y/y to 16% of revenue on higher incentive and share-based compensation. For a company whose stated strategy is technology differentiation, that expense mix defends the current margin at some cost to the source of future pricing power. One half-year is not a trend, and management points to MPD center-of-excellence work and PetroVisor traction as evidence innovation continues, but the direction is unattractive.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $676M against $226M capex for $450M of GAAP free cash flow (9.2% of revenue). H1 2026 OCF was $311M (from $270M) against $96M capex (from $131M), giving $215M. Q2 alone produced $139M of company-defined adjusted free cash flow, a 62.3% conversion of adjusted EBITDA versus 31.1% in Q2 2025.

The composition matters more than the level. Accounts receivable factoring fell to $13M sold in H1 2026 from $143M in H1 2025 as Pemex collections normalized; Mexico fell to 25% of net receivables from 27% at year-end and the largest Mexican customer to 21% from 24%. Net working capital reached 27% of revenue against a 25% internal target. Closing that remaining 200bps on ~$4.7B of revenue is worth roughly $90M of one-time release, after which conversion should revert toward EBITDA-driven levels. Management's position is that these are structural improvements in working-capital discipline and capital intensity rather than one-time harvests; two consecutive quarters of progress lend that partial support. The discriminating test is whether conversion holds above 45% in 2027 once the ratio plateaus. I would not annualize Q2's $139M — management guided only ">$100M" for Q3.

**Balance sheet.** At 6/30/2026: cash $1,100M plus $37M restricted; total debt $1,480M ($1,182M carrying value of 6.75% 2033 notes, $234M of 8.625% 2030 notes, $64M finance leases). Net debt $380M, or $343M including restricted cash, reconciling to the stated 0.34x against ~$1,023M LTM adjusted EBITDA. Total liquidity $1.7B, revolver undrawn, facility upsized to $1.0B in September 2025 with a 2030 maturity. The October 2025 refinancing of $1.36B of 8.625% notes into $1.2B of 6.75% paper cost a $39M extinguishment loss and takes annual interest to roughly $101-103M. Current ratio 2.19; ROA 8.8%; ROE 26.9%, flattered by the thin post-bankruptcy equity base.

Two qualifications. First, cash "not immediately available for repatriation" rose from $31M at 12/31/2025 to $156M at 6/30/2026, roughly 14% of the cash balance, while Russian cash rose from $107M to $118M. The 10-Q does not itemize jurisdictions. Adjusting net debt to ~$536M gives ~0.52x, still conservative, but it changes the texture of the fortress framing and may partly explain the slow buyback pace. Second, the Altman Z-Score of 2.60 sits in the grey zone entirely because of the low equity-to-assets ratio, which is an artifact of the $1,022M accumulated deficit rather than current distress. A company at 0.34x net leverage with an undrawn revolver and three ratings upgrades is not a near-term credit risk. The frequently cited "D/E of 2.06" is total liabilities to equity; gross debt to equity is ~0.87x and net debt to equity ~0.28x.

**Earnings quality.** A material share of recent EPS came from discrete tax items: a $70M release of uncertain tax positions helped the FY2025 18% effective rate, and $44M of previously uncertain positions was recognized in Q1 2026, contributing to a 16% H1 rate against full-year guidance of "low- to mid-20%." Q2 standalone was 44%. A $1.1B valuation allowance covers 88% of gross deferred tax assets, which is the structural backdrop to the redomestication case.

**Surprise record.** Two beats and two misses over four quarters, with the pattern deteriorating: +31.2% (Q4 2025), +39.1% (Q1 2026), then −41.1% in Q2 2026. That Q2 figure compares GAAP EPS of $0.55 against a consensus that is almost certainly adjusted, and the quarter absorbed roughly $0.32 per share pre-tax of redomestication and M&A costs. The comparison is definitionally unsound and should be discarded; revenue and adjusted EBITDA came in at the upper end of internal expectations. The guidance revision — revenue narrowed and EBITDA cut ~1% at the midpoint versus April, with three named incremental pressures (Indonesia, the Norway union strike, ongoing Middle East disruption) — is the substantive read.

## Growth Analysis

There is no near-term growth. FY2026 guidance implies a fifth consecutive quarter of year-over-year revenue decline, and the estimate revision table is uniformly negative:

|---|---|---|---|
| Current quarter | $1.31 | −10.6% | −15.4% |
| Next quarter | $1.71 | −4.6% | −7.1% |
| Current year | $5.56 | −4.6% | −5.8% |
| Next year | $7.27 | −2.8% | −0.3% |

Revisions of this direction and velocity are a meaningful negative for both the growth and valuation scores, and they cut directly against reading the compressed multiple as opportunity.

**The guidance arithmetic is demanding.** FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $951M-$1,046M less H1 actual of $456M implies H2 of $495-590M, or 52-56% of the year. Q3 guidance ($1,105-1,155M revenue, $235-265M EBITDA, 22.1% midpoint margin) leaves roughly $292M of Q4 EBITDA on implied revenue of ~$1,283M — a ~14% sequential revenue increase at a ~22.8% margin. Two mitigants: Weatherford has genuine Q4 seasonality from year-end product and software sales, and ~$1,283M is roughly flat against Q4 2025 (H2 2025 revenue was $2,521M), so the ask is a return to a recently achieved level rather than a record. Management flagged Q4 MPD equipment deliveries as part of the ramp. Even so, the guide embeds Middle East normalization, the PDO Marmul ramp and Pemex stability with essentially no cushion.

**Headwinds already identified.** The Saudi Aramco LSTK contract concluded, with management saying the Saudi revenue decline "will be further visible in the second half." Corroborating evidence: Saudi Arabia was an above-10% revenue country in Q2/H1 2025 (11%) and is no longer listed in 2026, while remaining 13% of PP&E plus operating lease assets — the asset base stayed while the revenue left, which pressures utilization. The five-year, 837-well PDO Oman contract completed 14 months early; the replacement Marmul award covers 247 wells over three years, a smaller scope.

**Offsets.** Q2 produced an unusual cluster of offshore awards — Constellation Oil Services (Brazil), Ventura Offshore and Valaris (deepwater MPD), Noble (West Africa plus a global aftermarket agreement), Esso Nigeria, a five-year Chevron Australia tubular running framework, two five-year KOC contracts, PTTEP Thailand, and PDO Marmul. Several counterparties are offshore drilling contractors rather than E&P operators, so this is equipment-and-aftermarket revenue supplied to rig operators. Weatherford discloses no backlog or remaining performance obligations, and contract liabilities of $60M current plus $2M non-current are deferred revenue, not backlog; none of these awards is financially quantified in any source, so their revenue contribution cannot be estimated.

Three discrete potential contributors sit in 2027: redomestication savings of $20-30M annually beginning in 2027 (2-3% of EBITDA, worth roughly $130-200M of enterprise value at 6.5x); at least $15M of NCS Multistage cost synergies within 18 months of closing; and Venezuela, where the CEO now believes there can be "a tangible contribution to revenue and margins in 2027" against a $500M peak historical revenue base, while cautioning that the build is nonlinear "from a few million dollars to tens of millions." Consensus already embeds a 31% EPS recovery in 2027 ($5.56 → $7.27); management said 2027 "should definitely be a year of growth" without guiding. The recovery is plausible and contingent on the same Middle East normalization the market cannot yet verify.

## Valuation Assessment

At $89.42 with 71.685M shares:

- Market capitalization: **$6.41B** (at $87.20: $6.25B)
- Enterprise value: $6.41B + $1.48B debt − $1.10B cash = **$6.79B**
- EV / FY2025 adjusted EBITDA ($1,067M): **6.4x**
- EV / FY2026 guided adjusted EBITDA midpoint ($998M): **6.8x**
- P/E on FY2026 consensus $5.56: **16.1x**
- P/E on FY2027 consensus $7.27: **12.3x**
- Adjusted FCF yield on guided $449-479M: **7.0-7.5%**
- Dividend $1.10 annualized: **1.23% yield**, roughly $79M, comfortably covered

Against the supplied peer set (median P/E 30.8x, median yield 1.7%), Weatherford looks cheap, but that median is distorted by depressed earnings at KGS (88.7x) and NOV (85.3x). The more useful comparisons are Oceaneering at 11.8x and Archrock at 19.6x, which bracket Weatherford's 16.1x current-year and 12.3x next-year. On EV/EBITDA, 6.8x is at the low end of the mid-cap oilfield services range and consistent with a business whose revenue and EBITDA are both declining.

That is the core valuation question: is 6.8x a discount, or the correct multiple for a shrinking earnings stream with a rising geopolitical concentration? The evidence cuts both ways. Supporting a discount: leverage at 0.34x versus a sector norm well above that, a 7%+ FCF yield, $274M of unused buyback authorization, and every sell-side target above spot (low $93, median $114.50, mean $117.33, high $151, with UBS upgrading to Buy at $114 on July 29 after an 18% conflict-driven decline). Supporting the multiple as fair: estimates falling in every window, two years of margin compression, FCF quality reliant on a finite working-capital source, ~10% of revenue and 12% of receivables in Russia, and a guide requiring a 14% sequential Q4.

A compressed multiple with falling estimates is the classic pattern where cheapness is not a buy signal. I therefore do not treat the sell-side target range as an independent floor here — the growth trajectory is negative, not accelerating, which is the condition under which consensus targets stop lagging reality and start reflecting hope. I score valuation 5.5: supportive rather than compelling, with the FCF yield and net-cash-adjacent balance sheet doing most of the work.

My base case is $98, which is 13.5x FY2027 consensus EPS of $7.27, approximately 9.7% above the action basis. Cross-check on enterprise value: 7.0x $1,050M of FY2027 EBITDA (modest recovery plus redomestication savings and NCS synergies) gives $7,350M EV; less ~$450M of net debt (splitting the difference between reported and repatriation-adjusted) and divided by ~73.3M pro-forma shares gives $94. The two methods bracket the mid-$90s.

## Competitive Landscape

Industry activity deteriorated through the period despite the price spike. Worldwide rig count: 1,948 (FY2024) → 1,818 (FY2025) → 1,760 (Q2 2026); international 1,162 → 1,080 → 1,056. Weatherford's revenue fell faster than the rig count in the most recent quarter (−8.2% y/y versus roughly −1% on rigs), which reflects the Saudi LSTK roll-off and the Middle East disruption more than share loss, but it is not evidence of share gain either.

Within the four-player diversified group, Weatherford is the smallest and the most internationally weighted, which gives it the highest beta to NOC spending and the least insulation from a single-region shock. The Q2 result shows both sides: adjusted EBITDA margin held above 20% and essentially flat sequentially while the largest region contracted sharply. The levers used — cost action, capex at the low end, R&D down a third, exiting a low-priced LSTK — defend margin at the expense of revenue and, in R&D's case, of the differentiation that supports pricing.

On pricing, management acknowledged "pockets of pricing headwinds leading to volume declines" and expressed hope rather than evidence that "the capacity discipline of the past few years… translates into pricing discipline."

On diversification, the regional mix did shift — Europe/Sub-Saharan Africa/Russia grew to $257M from $244M while MENA/Asia fell to $446M from $524M — but that is one region shrinking, not new diversification. Direct concentration measures moved in opposite directions: Mexico receivables improved (27% → 25%; largest customer 24% → 21%) while Russia worsened on both revenue (7% → ~9.5%) and receivables (to 12%). I score competitive position 4.8: the MPD/deepwater franchise is genuinely winning work and WCC margin resilience is real, but the trajectory on revenue share, R&D spend and regional concentration is flat to deteriorating.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Iran conflict — the dominant variable.** The Q2 10-Q dates the conflict to February 2026. Management quantified the first-half profit impact within a previously guided $30-50M range and expects it "to increase over the course of the year." Saudi Arabia, UAE and Oman are recovering; Bahrain, Qatar, Iraq and Kuwait were disrupted again in the ten to twelve days before the July 22 call. The CEO said the impact "will unlikely go to zero until we get to a firm and permanent resolution" and advised that "extrapolating what we talked about is prudent." Gulf transit normalization remains unverified — improving tanker traffic is not the same as sustained uninterrupted commercial transit, and attacks on assets inside Gulf host states raise the probability of retaliation against energy infrastructure. This is the swing factor in both directions.

**2. Russia — rising, and not purely a mix effect.** Russia was 7% of revenue in FY2025 and Q2/H1 2025, 9% in H1 2026, and "just under 10%" in Q2 2026, plus 12% of net receivables and roughly $311M of net assets in country. Applying the disclosed percentages: ~$84M in Q2 2025 versus ~$99-105M in Q2 2026, i.e. absolute growth of 18-25% while total revenue fell $99M. Management attributed the share increase to declines elsewhere; the arithmetic suggests mix explains only part of it. The figure is imprecise ("just under 10%"), and ruble pricing, FX or contract timing could account for some or all of the dollar growth — I draw no inference about strategic intent. What matters for risk is the exposure itself: a sanctions escalation, nationalization or self-imposed exit could impair a low-nine-figure asset base and remove ~10% of revenue at above-average incremental margins. This is the least-priced risk in the name.

**3. Pemex / Mexico.** Improving but structurally unresolved. Mexico was 27% of net receivables at year-end 2025 against a largest customer contributing only ~5% of revenue; Latin America drove 83% of the FY2025 revenue decline. Factoring collapsed to $13M in H1 2026 from $143M and collections were described as strong, but the CFO called Pemex the biggest driver of FCF variability, Q2 Mexico activity came in below expectations with wells deferred, and surety bonds concentrated in Latin America stood at $540M. The disclosed credit default swap referencing a secured loan to the largest Mexican customer — on which Weatherford is the protection seller and received $25M upfront — has amortized to immaterial notional and terminates in September 2026, so residual risk is small; the structure documents how strained collections were in 2023-2024.

**4. Guidance back-loading.** Covered above: ~14% sequential Q4 revenue growth at a 22.8% margin, with no cushion for another regional flare-up.

**5. Redomestication vote, September 3, 2026.** The Texas proposal received more than 60% of votes cast in June but failed the 75% Irish scheme-of-arrangement threshold, at a cost of $21M in H1 charges and six months. The Delaware attempt goes to two special meetings on September 3, requiring the same 75% plus Irish High Court sanction. Delaware should be an easier institutional sell, but closing a 40% opposition gap is not trivial. Outcome is binary for $20-30M of annual savings.

**6. Cash fungibility.** $156M of the $1.1B cash balance is not immediately repatriable, up five-fold in six months, with no jurisdictional itemization.

This is consistent with a two-sided setup and does not corroborate either a strong bull or bear case.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $118 (+32%)

Gulf transit normalizes and the $30-50M conflict drag reverses through H2 2026 and 2027. Q4 delivers the guided ~$1,283M of revenue on MPD equipment deliveries and the Marmul ramp, validating the back-loaded guide and resetting the credibility of management's forecasts. Redomestication passes on September 3, adding $20-30M of annual savings from 2027; NCS closes with $15M of synergies against ~$130M of equity consideration and roughly 2% dilution; Venezuela contributes tens of millions of high-incremental-margin revenue in 2027 against sunk in-country assets. FY2027 adjusted EBITDA reaches ~$1,200M. At 7.5x, that is $9.0B of EV, or roughly $117 per share on ~73.3M pro-forma shares — consistent with the $117.33 analyst mean and below the $151 high. Free cash flow conversion holds above 45% because the improvements were structural, and management deploys the $274M of remaining authorization before it expires in June 2027.

### Bear Case — $62 (−31%)

The Middle East flare-up pattern repeats; the impact "increases over the course of the year" as management warned, and Q4 revenue lands near the Q3 level rather than 14% above it, producing an EBITDA miss against the $951M low end. Working capital reaches the 25% target, the release exhausts, and FCF conversion reverts toward 40%, cutting free cash flow toward $380-400M. R&D at two-thirds of prior levels begins to show in competitive losses as pricing discipline fails to hold. Russia exposure is impaired by sanctions escalation, removing ~10% of revenue at above-average margin and writing down part of $311M of net assets. The Delaware vote fails a second time. FY2027 EBITDA lands near $900M; at 5.5x on repatriation-adjusted net debt of $536M that is roughly $60 per share, near the 52-week low of $52.70.

The asymmetry is roughly balanced: +9.7% to base, +32% to bull, −31% to bear. That is a HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** two discrete near-term events — the special shareholder meetings on September 3, 2026 (redomestication, 75% threshold) and the Q3 2026 print in late October, which tests the guided ≥100bp sequential margin step-up and the credibility of the Q4 ramp. The NCS Multistage close in H2 2026 is a third, smaller checkpoint.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q3 adjusted EBITDA at or above the $250M midpoint with margin up at least 100bps sequentially to ~22%, plus reaffirmation of the FY2026 EBITDA range — evidence that the Saudi LSTK exit was a genuine pricing-discipline trade and that the Q4 ramp is on track. Combined with a price near or below $85, that would restore the asymmetry.
2. Consensus EPS revisions turning positive — specifically, current-year estimates stabilizing above $5.50 and next-year above $7.25 for two consecutive monthly reads. Falling estimates are the primary constraint on the valuation score.
3. Verified sustained normalization of Gulf commercial transit (72+ hours uninterrupted) accompanied by a Middle East revenue recovery in the reported segment data, not just in management commentary.
4. Resumption of buybacks at a pace consistent with using the $274M authorization before June 2027, or any open-market insider purchase — either would reconcile management's actions with the CEO's characterization of the decline as "exaggerated."

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q4 revenue tracking below ~$1,200M, or any downward revision to the FY2026 EBITDA range below $951M — the guide has no cushion and a second cut would break the forecasting credibility that supports the multiple.
2. FCF conversion falling below 40% for two consecutive quarters after net working capital reaches 25% of revenue, confirming that the cash flow strength was a finite working-capital harvest.
3. Sanctions escalation or announced exit affecting the Russian business (~10% of revenue, 12% of receivables, ~$311M of net assets), or a further material increase in non-repatriable cash beyond $156M.
4. Failure of the September 3 Delaware vote, which would remove $20-30M of 2027 savings, add further costs, and signal unresolved governance friction with institutional holders.

**Score trajectory context.** Prior scores ran 5.7 → 5.9 → 6.1 → 5.7 → 5.6 → 5.0 across four HOLDs, with growth (6.8 → 4.5), valuation (7.0 → 5.0) and management quality (6.5 → 4.5) all declining. This cycle holds at 5.0. The stock is 10.8% higher than at the July review, which on unchanged fundamentals would improve nothing about the risk/reward — but fundamentals are not unchanged. The Q2 print was constructive on margin resilience and FCF conversion (raised a third time) and negative on the EBITDA guidance cut, the Q4 back-loading, further estimate reductions, and the rise in Russia concentration. Growth stays weak at 4.0 given the additional 10.6% 30-day cut to current-quarter estimates; financial health improves modestly at 6.7 on the confirmed 0.34x leverage and third FCF conversion raise; risk stays low at 4.0 with the conflict impact guided to increase. The net is a flat score at a higher price, which is a mild deterioration in expected return and consistent with maintaining HOLD rather than upgrading on the sell-side's improving sentiment.

## Conclusion

Weatherford is a well-financed, operationally competent business caught in the wrong half of a geopolitical shock. The company held adjusted EBITDA margin above 20% while its largest region lost $78M of revenue, raised free cash flow conversion guidance three consecutive times, refinanced its debt stack down 190bps of coupon, and earned upgrades from all three agencies. At 6.8x guided EBITDA, 12.3x next-year consensus EPS, a 7.0-7.5% free cash flow yield and 0.34x net leverage, the valuation does not require heroic assumptions.

What prevents a BUY is the combination of falling estimates across every forward period, a guide that needs a 14% sequential Q4 revenue increase with no cushion for another flare-up, free cash flow strength sourced substantially from a finite working-capital release, a Russian business that appears to be growing in absolute dollars into a rising sanctions tail, and a management team that decelerated buybacks from $87M to $26M year over year while calling the share price decline exaggerated and while two directors sold stock the day after that statement. Energy services BUYs have historically required company-specific evidence of outperformance rather than a cheap multiple in a volatile commodity regime, and the evidence here points the other way on earnings momentum.

The name becomes interesting on either of two developments: a Q3 print that validates the margin step-up and Q4 ramp, or a price near $80 where the FCF yield approaches 8% and the bear case is largely discounted. Until then, hold existing positions for the cash flow and balance sheet, and wait for the September 3 vote and the October print before committing new capital. Base case $98 (+9.7%), bull $118 (+32%), bear $62 (−31%).
