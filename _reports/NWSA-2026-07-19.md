**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

News Corp emerges from its FY2025 portfolio simplification as a cleaner, faster-growing enterprise than the conglomerate it was two years ago. The April 2025 sale of Foxtel removed a capital-intensive, low-growth Australian pay-TV asset and reduced the reportable structure to five segments. The remaining businesses are led by two growth engines: Dow Jones, whose B2B professional-information franchises (Risk & Compliance +18%, Dow Jones Energy/OPIS +10%) carry recurring, enterprise-grade revenue at accretive margins; and Digital Real Estate, anchored by a 61.6% stake in ASX-listed REA Group. Nine-month FY2026 continuing-operations revenue rose 5% to $6,691M and total Segment EBITDA rose 10% to $1,204M, with the reported net-income "decline" (−9%) fully explained by a prior-year $87M one-time PropertyGuru gain. Underlying operating momentum is positive.

The investment debate has narrowed to two structural issues rather than the fundamentals of the operating businesses. First, the conglomerate/sum-of-the-parts discount: NWSA bundles a fast-growing financial-data business, a look-through stake in a listed property portal that represents a large share of the market cap, a stable publisher (HarperCollins), and a declining newspaper business. The Foxtel divestiture, the quadrupled buyback pace, and the resolved Murdoch succession all point toward continued value-crystallization, but the timing and form of any further separation are unknown. Second, the dual-class governance discount: Class A shares (NWSA) are largely non-voting, and the September 2025 New Stockholders Agreement, while resolving the succession overhang and capping family trust voting power at 44% of Class B, does not remove the structural non-voting discount.

At $28.43, NWSA trades at roughly 26x current-year consensus EPS ($1.08) and ~22x next-year ($1.29), a premium to the closest publishing peers on trailing metrics but with better growth quality than most. The stock has recovered from a February drawdown near $22.20 and now sits below its 52-week high of $31.61. We rate NWSA a HOLD (growth-hold). The self-help narrative is real and the balance sheet is close to net-cash, but the near-quarter estimate revisions have turned negative, the OPIS/DOJ antitrust thread is an active enforcement risk in the growth segment, and the price no longer offers the asymmetry it did in the low-$22s. We would become more constructive on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $20s or on a concrete separation catalyst.

## Company Overview

News Corp operates five reportable segments after the FY2025 simplification:

- **Dow Jones (~28% of revenue)** — The Wall Street Journal, Barron's, MarketWatch, Investor's Business Daily, plus high-margin B2B franchises: Dow Jones Risk & Compliance, Dow Jones Energy (OPIS), Factiva, and Newswires. Digital is now 83% of Dow Jones revenue. This is the growth-and-margin core.
- **Digital Real Estate Services (~22%)** — 61.6% of ASX-listed REA Group (realestate.com.au, Housing.com India) and 80% of Move/Realtor.com in the U.S.
- **Book Publishing (~26%)** — HarperCollins, the world's #2 consumer book publisher, with a backlist that generates roughly 62–64% of sales.
- **News Media (~24%)** — News Corp Australia, News UK (The Times, The Sun, talkSPORT), and the New York Post. Lowest margin, structurally challenged.
- **Other** — corporate overhead plus U.K. Newspaper Matters costs.

A large fraction of NWSA's value is a look-through claim on REA Group, a near-monopoly Australian property portal, layered on top of a growing financial-data franchise and a declining print business. That composition drives the persistent conglomerate discount that dominates the equity thesis.

## Financial Analysis

The continuing-operations picture is one of modest top-line growth and clearer margin expansion:

| Metric (continuing ops) | 9mo FY2026 | 9mo FY2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | $6,691M | $6,343M | +5% |
| Total Segment EBITDA | $1,204M | $1,093M | +10% |
| Net income (cont. ops) | $513M | $562M | −9%* |
| Diluted EPS (cont. ops) | $0.70 | $0.75 | −9%* |
| Free cash flow | $535M | $539M | ~flat |

*The reported net-income decline is optical. FY2025 included a +$101M "Other, net" benefit dominated by an $87M one-time gain on REA's sale of PropertyGuru. Excluding that item, underlying pre-tax income from continuing operations rose.

Q3 FY2026 (March quarter) was the strongest data point: revenue +9% to $2,185M, Segment EBITDA +18% to $343M, and net income from continuing operations +13%. Roughly 5 percentage points of Q3 revenue growth was pure FX (AUD/GBP translation), so organic growth is more modest than the headline.

Segment EBITDA trends (9-month YoY):
- Dow Jones +10% to $482M — Risk & Compliance +18%, Dow Jones Energy +10%.
- Digital Real Estate +16% to $519M — REA and Move both accelerating; Move pivoting to premium (RealPRO Select).
- Book Publishing −7% to $230M — hurt by a $16M inventory write-off and $13M distributor-receivable write-off; underlying demand solid.
- News Media −8% to $115M — structural decline plus California Post launch costs and soft News UK.

The balance sheet is a clear strength. Cash stands at $2.17B against total borrowings of ~$2.0B, leaving the parent roughly net-cash-neutral. Total equity is $9.28B; goodwill and intangibles are $6.36B. The covenant leverage limit is ≤3.5x adjusted operating income net leverage, and the company is comfortably compliant. The accumulated deficit narrowed sharply from −$1,889M (June 2024) to −$452M (March 2026) as earnings and buybacks reshaped equity. In March 2026 the company refinanced into a new $1.5B facility (a $1B revolver plus a $500M term loan), extending maturities to 2031 and removing near-term refinancing risk.

Two liquidity nuances warrant flagging: $822M of cash is held offshore, and $245M of that is effectively trapped at REA (accessible only via an REA dividend). This is a mild constraint on capital-return flexibility, not a solvency concern.

FCF conversion is roughly in line with net income ($535M FCF on continuing-ops net income of $513M), signaling reasonable earnings quality. Management guided to "strong growth in free cash flow for the full fiscal year" despite modestly higher capex.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is bifurcated and increasingly weighted toward the durable end. Dow Jones professional information services (Risk & Compliance, Energy) are recurring, enterprise-grade, data-driven revenue streams that grow high-single to low-double digits with pricing power, competing against Bloomberg, S&P Global, LSEG, and LexisNexis. Digital circulation and content-licensing revenues are cited in the 10-Q as explicit drivers of Dow Jones growth, and WSJ digital subscriptions rose 11% YoY. Digital Real Estate's segment EBITDA growth reflects REA's dominant network-effect position and Move's premium pivot.

The AI-content-licensing angle is a differentiated lever. Dow Jones is monetizing content through licensing agreements (the OpenAI arrangement, widely reported at up to ~$250M over five years — a press-reported figure not verified against primary filings), and is simultaneously litigating against unauthorized scrapers (the Dow Jones/New York Post suit against Perplexity). HarperCollins is a class member in the Anthropic AI settlement, a potential future gain not yet recognized. The 10-Q confirms content-licensing revenues as a driver of circulation-and-other revenue growth, so the theme is showing up in the numbers even where specific deal terms are press-sourced.

News Media is in secular decline — print circulation and print advertising continue to fall, held roughly flat only by FX tailwinds and cost cuts (with $50M of restructuring charges in 9 months). Book Publishing is a stable oligopoly but growth is muted.

The near-term estimate picture is a caution. Consensus current-year EPS is up modestly over 90 days (+2.1%) and next-year is up modestly (+3.9%), but the current-quarter revision is −2.8% and the next-quarter revision is −10.7%. Estimates are broadly drifting lower on a near-term basis even as the annual figures hold. This is a business growing steadily with some near-term earnings-momentum softness, not one whose revision velocity signals acceleration. The earnings track record is reassuring — three beats and one in-line over four quarters, with beats of 12–17% — which supports confidence in the annual consensus even as quarterly estimates wobble.

Against the publishing peer set, the comparison is mixed:

| SCHL | Scholastic | 18.0 | 1.8% |
| — | **Peer median** | **24.5** | **1.3%** |

NWSA's forward multiple sits modestly above the peer median but below NYT and its own Class B counterpart. That premium is defensible given the quality of the Dow Jones data franchise and the REA look-through, but it is not cheap. The dividend yield of ~0.7% offers little valuation support.

The genuine valuation case for NWSA is the sum-of-the-parts argument: the market applies a conglomerate discount to a bundle whose parts (a listed REA stake, a Bloomberg-adjacent data business, a stable publisher, a declining newspaper) would be worth more separated. This is a real potential source of upside, but it is a catalyst-dependent thesis, not a margin-of-safety statement about the current multiple. No separation of Dow Jones or REA has been announced, and the timing is speculative.

We are deliberately cautious about treating the SOTP discount as a low-risk "cheap" signal. A compressed conglomerate multiple that has persisted for years without crystallization is a value-trap pattern unless an identifiable catalyst forces the realization, and here the dual-class, controlled-company structure gives outside Class A holders limited ability to force that outcome. The valuation score reflects a stock trading at a slight premium to peers on forward earnings, with genuine but unscheduled crystallization optionality, on the low-voting share class. We would treat consensus targets (median $35, high $43) as reflecting the same optimism embedded in the SOTP thesis rather than as an independent floor, given the stock is already near the low end of that range (low $29.40) and the near-quarter estimate momentum is negative.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive picture is favorable at the top and challenged at the bottom. Dow Jones Risk & Compliance and Energy (OPIS) are the most defensible pieces — recurring, enterprise, data-driven, and margin-accretive, competing against Bloomberg, S&P Global, LSEG, and LexisNexis with differentiated proprietary content. This is the part of News Corp most consistently argued to be undervalued inside the conglomerate. REA Group holds a dominant, network-effect position in Australian residential property portals, a structurally attractive near-monopoly. HarperCollins operates in a stable publishing oligopoly with annuity-like backlist cash flows.

The competitive threat is concentrated in News Media, where generative AI risks disintermediating news traffic and search referrals, a structural challenge to the entire news industry. News Corp's mitigation, charging AI platforms for content rather than being scraped, is a credible and differentiated response that leverages the trust and depth of its brands. The net trajectory across the portfolio is stable-to-improving in the growth segments and structurally deteriorating in the print tail, which nets to a slightly-above-neutral competitive read.

## Risk Assessment

**OPIS/DOJ antitrust (the most concrete near-term risk).** Dow Jones Energy (OPIS) faces multiple PVC-pipe antitrust class actions (a settlement was preliminarily approved in July 2025 but is not final; a parallel Canadian action was filed September 2025), a DOJ Antitrust grand-jury subpoena (January 2025), and a state AG civil investigative demand (April 2025) into the PVC & Pipe Weekly Report. This enforcement thread targets the growth engine and is easy to overlook. It is the risk most capable of moving the stock in a way the market may not be fully pricing.

**Structural decline in News Media / print.** Ongoing, with continued restructuring cost. Well understood and largely priced.

**AI disintermediation (double-edged).** A threat to news traffic, partially offset by content-licensing monetization.

**FX translation.** A large share of revenue is AUD/GBP-denominated; ~5% of Q3 growth was pure FX. A reversal would pressure reported results.

**Governance / dual-class.** Class A is largely non-voting; Class B holds the votes. The September 2025 New Stockholders Agreement resolved the Murdoch succession dispute, capped family trust plus Murdoch voting at 44% of Class B, and gave the company a right of first refusal on Class B public sales — a meaningful reduction of the succession tail risk. The non-voting Class A discount persists.

**Other.** U.K. Newspaper Matters (phone-hacking) with ~$16M accrued and a ~$24M FOX Corp indemnity receivable, tail risk open-ended; the HarperCollins/Amazon e-book appeal window; an effective tax rate persistently above U.S. statutory; and the unexplained near-term negative revision drift.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 43.3% sits well above 1-year historical volatility of 25.6% (a 17.8-point rich spread), the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 43.3% vs. 6-month 31.8%), and the put/call skew is elevated at 12.7%, indicating stronger demand for downside protection. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±10.3%. This configuration typically anticipates a binary catalyst — most plausibly the next earnings report, or news flow around the OPIS/DOJ investigation or a separation decision. The low put/call volume ratio (0.14) tempers this somewhat, but the backwardation and skew argue for patience on entry rather than chasing the recent rally. Short interest is minimal at 3.2% of float (1.7 days to cover), so squeeze dynamics are not a factor.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($33–43).** Continued portfolio simplification crystallizes the conglomerate discount — a separation or spin of Dow Jones or the REA stake would re-rate the whole. Dow Jones B2B data compounds at low-double-digit EBITDA growth, Digital Real Estate sustains mid-teens growth, AI content-licensing scales into a recognized revenue line, buybacks continue at the quadrupled pace (retiring shares at ~$851M remaining authorization), and the resolved succession removes a persistent overhang. On these, the sum-of-the-parts fair value sits materially above the current market price, and analyst targets (high $43) become achievable.

**Bear Case ($21–23).** The conglomerate discount persists because the controlled, dual-class structure gives outside holders no ability to force separation. News Media decline accelerates, the OPIS/DOJ investigation escalates into a fine or reputational damage in the growth segment, FX reverses and pressures reported results, and the near-term negative estimate revisions prove to be the leading edge of a slowdown rather than noise. On these, the stock de-rates toward the mid-$20s or retests the February low near $22.

**Base Case (~$31).** Steady mid-single-digit revenue growth, continued double-digit growth in Dow Jones and Digital Real Estate offsetting News Media decline, buybacks providing per-share support, and gradual (not dramatic) narrowing of the conglomerate discount as simplification continues. This implies ~24x next-year EPS of $1.29, a modest premium to the peer median justified by growth quality.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The 12-month thesis is constructive, with a genuinely growing core, a clean balance sheet, accelerating capital return, and real crystallization optionality, but the current price no longer offers the asymmetry that supported a BUY earlier in the year, and near-term estimate momentum has turned negative. This is a hold worth owning for expected appreciation, not merely for yield (the ~0.7% dividend is immaterial).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next fiscal Q4/full-year earnings, plus any 8-K flagging a separation decision or a resolution of the OPIS/DOJ investigation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A concrete announcement of a Dow Jones or REA separation/spin, or a formal strategic review.
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $20s (restoring the risk/reward that existed in the $22–24 range) with fundamentals intact.
- Current-quarter and next-quarter estimate revisions turning positive alongside continued double-digit Dow Jones and Digital Real Estate EBITDA growth.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The OPIS/DOJ antitrust matter escalating into a material fine, indictment, or business-model impairment in Dow Jones Energy.
- Two consecutive quarters of Dow Jones or Digital Real Estate EBITDA growth decelerating below mid-single digits.
- A cut or pause in the buyback pace, signaling reduced management confidence.
- Consensus current-year EPS revisions turning materially negative (>10% over 90 days).

**Base-case target: ~$31** (24x next-year EPS of $1.29), roughly +9% from $28.43.
**Bull-case target: $33–43** (SOTP crystallization / accelerated buyback), +16% to +51%.
**Bear-case target: $21–23** (discount persists, News Media/OPIS deteriorate), −19% to −24%.

## Conclusion

News Corp is a financially healthy, low-leverage information-and-media company that has done the hard work of simplification: Foxtel is gone, the balance sheet is essentially net-cash at the parent with maturities pushed to 2031, buybacks have quadrupled, and the Murdoch succession dispute has been codified into a resolution that caps family voting power. The operating core (Dow Jones B2B data and Digital Real Estate) is growing at double-digit EBITDA rates, and the AI-content-licensing angle is a differentiated monetization lever.

The reasons to hold rather than buy are equally concrete: the stock trades at a slight forward-multiple premium to peers on the low-voting Class A share class; near-quarter estimate revisions have turned negative; the SOTP discount that anchors the bull case requires a catalyst that the controlled dual-class structure gives outside holders no power to force; and the OPIS/DOJ antitrust investigation is an active enforcement risk in the very segment driving growth. The options market is pricing near-term stress into the next catalyst. The score trajectory (6.4 → 6.3 over three months, with Financial Health improving but Valuation and Growth Prospects softening) matches this reading: an improving balance sheet against a richer price and softer near-term earnings momentum. We rate NWSA a HOLD and would upgrade on a separation catalyst, a positive turn in near-term revisions, or a pullback into the low-to-mid $20s.
