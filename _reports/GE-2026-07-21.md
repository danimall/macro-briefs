**Research Date: July 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

GE Aerospace is the pure-play commercial-engine franchise left after General Electric's three-way breakup completed with the GE Vernova spin in April 2024. The remaining business sits at the most profitable point of the aerospace value chain: it places engines at thin or negative margins, then earns high-margin service revenue for decades on an installed base of roughly 50,000 commercial and 30,000 military engines. Services remaining performance obligations reached $178.7B at Q2 2026, with 66% expected to convert within ten years. This is unusually durable, high-visibility revenue, and it is compounding: H1 2026 internal shop-visit revenue grew ~30% even as global air-travel departures were roughly flat, reflecting an aging, expanding fleet and deferred maintenance.

The fundamentals are intact and improving. H1 2026 revenue rose 23% (27% ex-insurance), free cash flow rose 31% to $4.69B, and adjusted EPS rose 24%. Q2 delivered the fourth consecutive beat ($2.02 vs $1.86), management raised FY2026 guidance across all metrics, Moody's upgraded the long-term rating to A2, and a fresh $20B buyback plus a dividend hike (to $0.47/quarter) signal balance-sheet confidence. Operating margin compressed 130bps in Q2, a mix artifact of delivering more lower-margin new engines and investing in capacity, each of which seeds a multi-decade services annuity. That is a healthy dilution, not a demand problem.

The obstacle is price. At ~$341 the stock trades near a 52-week high of $383 at roughly 40x forward EPS, a premium to a peer group that includes RTX (34.6x) and GD (22.6x). Estimate revisions are positive but modest (+4.7% current-year over 90 days, +2.3% next-year), which does not meet the threshold where rising estimates justify paying up through a high multiple. The score trajectory has drifted lower (7.0 in April to 6.4 in June) primarily on valuation, not fundamentals, and that read still holds: this is a high-quality compounder priced for continued execution, with limited margin of safety. We rate GE a HOLD (growth hold) and would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-$280s.

## Company Overview

GE Aerospace operates two reportable segments. Commercial Engines & Services (CES) is ~73% of revenue at a ~27-29% segment margin, with services representing roughly three-quarters of CES. The product line spans the LEAP narrowbody engine (via the CFM International JV), GEnx and GE90 widebody engines, the GE9X for the Boeing 777X, and the legacy CF6. Defense & Propulsion Technologies (DPT) is ~23% of revenue at a ~13-14% margin, covering the F110, F404, F414, T901 (Black Hawk), and T408 (CH-53K).

The structural feature that defines the business is CFM International, a 50/50 joint venture with Safran Aircraft Engines. LEAP and the legacy CFM56 are produced through this JV. LEAP is the sole engine on the Boeing 737 MAX and one of two options on the Airbus A320neo, giving CFM the dominant narrowbody position. The JV is both GE's greatest asset and a governance constraint: LEAP economics and strategic decisions are shared, and GE's reported segment revenue reflects only its JV share, with revenue-sharing balances ($1.6B receivable, $2.6B payable) running through the balance sheet. Any Safran production or strategic friction transmits directly to GE.

The razor-and-blades model is the core. New engines carry thin OE margins; the multi-decade, high-margin aftermarket that follows is where the economics live. The Ryanair long-term services agreement signed in H1 2026, covering the carrier's ~2,000-engine CFM56/LEAP fleet, locks in the annuity.

## Financial Analysis

Headline revenue fell from ~$73.6B (2022) to ~$45.9B (FY2025) entirely because of the HealthCare and Vernova spinoffs, not operating deterioration. On a continuing-operations basis, GE is growing quickly:

| Metric | H1 2026 | H1 2025 | Growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | $25.7B | $21.0B | +23% |
| Adjusted revenue (ex-insurance) | $24.2B | $19.2B | +27% |
| CES revenue | $18.7B | $14.3B | +30% |
| Free cash flow | $4.69B | $3.57B | +31% |
| Adjusted EPS | $3.88 | $3.14 | +24% |

The operating drivers are high quality. Total engine deliveries rose 37% and LEAP deliveries rose 41% (1,030 units). The growth is services-led — workscope, pricing, and volume on the installed base — rather than dependent on OE unit ramp alone. That distinction matters because services revenue carries the durable margin.

The margin compression is the central debate. Q2 operating margin was 21.7%, H1 was 21.8% (down 160bps), and CES segment margin fell to 27.3% from 28.9%. The drivers are mix (a higher proportion of lower-margin new-engine deliveries), elevated growth investment, and inflation. Each new LEAP installed at a thin margin becomes a decades-long, high-margin service stream, so the dilution is the mechanical consequence of a ramp that improves future economics. That logic caps near-term operating-profit surprise, which is why an otherwise strong Q2 saw the stock fall (from ~$360 to ~$346 on the print, then to $341).

Cash generation is the standout. H1 CFOA was $5.1B (+$1.2B YoY) against capex of only ~$0.67B, an asset-light model that converts to cash. FCF is management's headline compensation metric, which aligns incentives with the right lever.

The balance sheet is investment-grade despite screening as distressed. Total borrowings fell to $19.2B (from $20.5B at YE2025), with $10.3B liquidity plus an undrawn $3.0B revolver. Moody's raised the long-term rating to A2 (Feb 2026) and S&P moved to a positive outlook (April 2026). Any Altman Z-Score screen will flag GE (~0.7) and its thin ~14% equity-to-assets ratio, but that is an artifact of ~$36B in run-off insurance liabilities and matching investment securities that have nothing to do with the operating business. The operating company runs a ~46% ROE and generates $8-9B of FCF; the distress signal is a false positive.

Capital return is aggressive: a new $20B buyback authorized December 2025, $4.2B repurchased in H1 2026 (14.1M shares), and the dividend raised to $0.47/quarter. Share count is down to ~1.04B. The buyback is well-funded by FCF, though repurchasing near a 52-week high at ~40x forward is a less attractive use of capital than debt reduction at these prices.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three legs, all confirmed by the data. First, the aftermarket annuity: services RPO against an installed base that is aging and expanding, with shop-visit revenue growing while departures are flat. This is structural, not cyclical — it reflects deferred maintenance and the natural maturation of engines toward heavier workscopes. Second, the OE ramp: LEAP deliveries +41%, feeding future service volume; each unit is a multi-decade tail. Third, pricing and workscope escalation within the service book.

Estimate revisions confirm positive momentum at a moderate pace. Current-year consensus rose +4.7% over 90 days to $7.90, next-year +2.3% to $9.71. Current-quarter and next-quarter estimates ticked up 30 days but were flat-to-negative over 90 days, indicating the near-term ramp is well-modeled rather than accelerating beyond consensus. The four-quarter beat streak (5.5%, 13.0%, 6.8%, 10.9%) supports confidence in forward numbers, and FY2026 guidance was raised across all metrics (high-teens revenue growth, operating profit $10.55-10.75B).

The magnitude of revisions matters for how we weight them. Revisions above +30% over 90 days combined with 40%+ revenue growth would be a strong standalone buy signal; GE's revisions are positive but well below that bar, so they support the growth thesis without overriding the valuation concern. The LEAP durability issue is a growth wildcard: premature overhauls pull forward shop-visit revenue in the near term even as they raise warranty costs, so the growth line has some support from the very problem that is a risk elsewhere.

Growth durability is high given the multiple confirming signals across the aerospace supply chain — Boeing and Airbus rate increases, resilient maintenance demand, and the profit pool concentrating in engine OEMs and lessors. The score of 7.3 reflects strong, durable, high-quality growth tempered by the fact that revision velocity is moderate and the OE-mix drag on margins is a genuine near-term constraint on earnings leverage.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$341, GE trades at ~40x forward EPS ($7.90 current-year consensus). On next-year's $9.71 the multiple is ~35x, and on the FY26 operating-profit guidance the enterprise is fully valued for the visible ramp. Against the peer set — RTX at 34.6x, GD at 22.6x, LMT at 26.6x, with BA (88.8x) and HWM (61.4x) as outliers — GE carries a clear premium to the profitable, comparably-positioned names.

The premium is partly justified. GE sits at the most profitable node of the value chain, with an aftermarket annuity and revenue visibility that GD and LMT (primarily prime contractors on defense budgets) cannot match, and with a cleaner story than a still-recovering RTX. A ~40x multiple on a ~46% ROE franchise growing FCF ~30% is defensible in principle. The problem is the absence of asymmetry at the current price. Estimate revisions are positive but modest, so the "analyst targets are floors" logic applies weakly: consensus mean is $404 (high $455, low $347), roughly 18% above spot at the mean, but on a stock already at a premium multiple that target likely embeds the same growth extrapolation as the price rather than serving as independent confirmation.

We do not penalize GE for having appreciated ~30% over the trailing year; that move tracked fundamental improvement. But the current price offers limited margin of safety. On our numbers, the base case sits around $370-380 on ~40x $9.71 next-year EPS blended with the FCF trajectory; the bull case (aftermarket outperformance, durability derisking, margin stabilization) reaches ~$430-455; the bear case (Boeing 777X/MAX delays, an insurance reserve charge in Q3, or a demand air pocket) is ~$270-290, roughly 15-20% below spot. That is a symmetric-to-slightly-unfavorable setup near a 52-week high.

A valuation score of 4.8 reflects a stretched multiple with genuine quality behind it but no identifiable near-term catalyst to re-rate the stock higher from here. GE is expensive, and the multiple already prices continued flawless execution. We would want the low-$280s to underwrite the aftermarket annuity with adequate downside protection.

## Competitive Landscape

GE/CFM holds the strongest position in commercial propulsion. In narrowbody, LEAP competes against Pratt & Whitney's geared turbofan, which has suffered worse durability and powder-metal problems than LEAP's HPT creep and coking issues, a relative competitive tailwind for CFM. In widebody, GE holds sole-source or dominant positions on key Boeing platforms (GE9X on the 777X, GEnx on the 787/747-8), competing with Rolls-Royce and P&W elsewhere.

The moat is structural and, if anything, widening. The installed base is a captive, decades-long service annuity that cannot be dislodged by a competitor once an engine is on wing. New commercial wins in H1 2026 — United and Delta selecting GEnx for 787 orders, American and Copa selecting LEAP, the Ryanair fleet-wide services deal — extend that base. FLIGHT DECK, GE's lean operating model, is driving production and turnaround-time recovery that supports throughput as the ramp accelerates. On the technology frontier, the RISE/Open Fan program with Safran (through CFM International) and the NASA hybrid-electric demonstrator position GE for the next-generation narrowbody transition.

The main industry constraint is supply-chain throughput, not demand. The competitive-position score of 8.0 reflects a widening moat, share gains at the most profitable node, and P&W's relative stumbles, tempered only by the CFM/Safran shared-control dependency and Boeing production risk that gates OE volume.

## Risk Assessment

**LEAP durability** is the defining operational risk. HPT blade creep from ~1,650°C core temperatures forces premature overhauls in hot/dusty regions, and a separate fuel-nozzle coking issue triggered an FAA airworthiness directive. Mitigation is well advanced: the Leap-1A durability kit (on ~30% of the fleet) roughly doubles time-between-overhauls, the Leap-1B durability kit was certified in H1 2026, and a reverse-bleed anti-coking system covers ~60% of the -1A fleet. GE's GEnx and GE90 followed the same durability-improvement curve, lending credibility. The effect is two-edged: durability shortfalls raise warranty costs (product durability reserves rose in H1) but also pull forward shop-visit revenue.

**Boeing concentration** is acute. GE9X is sole engine on the still-uncertified 777X (with capitalized nonrecurring engineering costs at impairment risk), and LEAP-1B is sole engine on the 737 MAX. Boeing's production problems directly gate GE's OE volume.

**Legacy run-off liabilities** remain a drag: ~$36B in long-term-care insurance liabilities requiring statutory reserve testing (annual review due Q3 2026, a near-term binary), Bank BPH FX-mortgage litigation ($1.81B estimated losses, down from $2.33B), and ~$2.19B in environmental/worker-exposure reserves. The Q3 insurance review is the closest thing to a dated downside catalyst.

**Supply-chain constraints** persist despite $1B/yr US manufacturing investment and 5,000 new hires; the A320neo ramp to 75/month and Boeing rate hikes require sustained execution. **Tax audit** exposure (IRS 2016-2020 returns, AOF requests received March 2026) is possible but GE believes accruals are adequate and any liability is shared under tax-matters agreements. The 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling produced tariff refunds and a $0.1B charge reversal, a modest tailwind.

The risk score of 5.8 reflects a franchise with strong fundamentals but several genuine idiosyncratic risks — the Q3 insurance review, the LEAP durability arc, Boeing gating, and legacy tails — that are only partly priced at a premium multiple.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress into a call-heavy structure. Near-term ATM IV of 39.0% sits rich to 32.0% 1-year historical vol (a 7-point premium), and the term structure is in backwardation (39.0% near vs 32.8% at six months), consistent with recent-earnings digestion rather than a directional bearish bet. Put/call skew is mildly call-heavy (-5.8%), the P/C volume ratio is a benign 0.59, and the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±2.9%. IV ticked up 2.5 points and the P/C OI ratio rose from 0.81 to 0.93 into the price dip, indicating some hedging demand but nothing that contradicts the fundamental read. The rich near-term IV is not a favorable entry-timing signal; it argues for patience.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($430-455).** The aftermarket annuity compounds faster than modeled as the installed base ages and LEAP durability kits derisk the fleet while pulling forward shop visits. Margin compression stabilizes as the OE mix normalizes and services scale. Boeing 777X certifies and the MAX rate climbs, adding OE volume without diluting the service tail. Estimate revisions accelerate past the current modest pace, consensus targets ratchet toward $455, and the buyback shrinks the share count into rising FCF.

**Bear Case ($270-290).** The Q3 2026 insurance reserve review produces a material charge. Boeing 777X delays force impairment of capitalized GE9X engineering costs. LEAP durability costs run ahead of the mitigation curve, denting CES margin further, and the OE-mix drag proves stickier than the "healthy dilution" narrative implies. A macro air-travel slowdown finally pressures the aftermarket that has grown against flat departures. At ~40x forward with the growth already priced, any of these compresses the multiple toward the mid-30s on lower earnings, a 15-20% decline.

The base case ($370-380) is continued execution: high-teens revenue growth, FCF compounding ~30%, margins stabilizing, and the stock grinding toward consensus mean as the multiple holds. That is a good business at a full price, which is a HOLD rather than a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth hold).** Time horizon 12 months.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback into the low-$280s (~18% below spot), where the aftermarket annuity can be underwritten at ~30x forward with adequate downside protection.
- Estimate-revision velocity accelerating above +10% on next-year EPS over a 90-day window, signaling the aftermarket ramp is outrunning consensus and justifying the premium multiple.
- CES segment margin stabilizing or re-expanding (back toward 28-29%) as the OE mix normalizes, confirming the dilution was transitory.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material adverse Q3 2026 insurance reserve charge, or a 777X delay/impairment of capitalized GE9X costs.
- LEAP durability warranty costs materially outpacing the mitigation curve, with CES margin compressing below ~26%.
- H2 revenue growth decelerating below high-single-digits YoY alongside falling estimate revisions.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 2026 report (with the statutory insurance reserve review) and the residual separation-cost wind-down in H2 2026. Monitor Boeing 777X certification progress and the Leap-1B durability-kit fleet penetration between now and then.

## Conclusion

GE Aerospace is a genuinely superior industrial franchise: the most profitable node of the aerospace value chain, a services backlog on 50,000 engines compounding at ~30%, an investment-grade and recently-upgraded balance sheet, ~46% ROE, and management incentives correctly tied to FCF. The fundamentals are intact and improving, and the Q2 margin compression that spooked the market is the mechanical byproduct of a ramp that seeds future high-margin annuities.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is entirely price. At ~40x forward near a 52-week high, with positive-but-moderate estimate revisions and a symmetric-to-slightly-unfavorable risk/reward, the stock has already priced continued flawless execution. GE is expensive, and the peer premium is defensible on quality but leaves no margin of safety. The score drift from 7.0 to 6.4 over the past quarter has been driven by valuation, not deterioration, and that read still holds. Own it for the compounding if you already do; wait for a pullback toward the low-$280s to add.
