## Executive Summary

Humana has executed the volume side of its Medicare Advantage turnaround with unusual force. After deliberately shedding 412,500 individual MA members in 2025, it added 1,224,400 in the 2026 annual enrollment period (+23.4% year over year), grew stand-alone Part D membership 62.6% to 3,946,400, and lifted total medical membership 20.7% to 17,912,200. H1 2026 revenue of $80,515M rose 24.8%, and the operating cost ratio fell 120 basis points in Q2 as the membership surge spread fixed administrative cost. Four consecutive quarters of adjusted EPS beats and consensus revisions of +4.8% (30 days) and +8.0% (90 days) on FY2027 confirm that the operating trajectory is improving, not deteriorating.

The problem is the price. At $367.24, Humana carries roughly 40x FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $9.15 and 22.3x FY2027's $16.48, having more than doubled from the March low of $163.11. Bridging that gap requires management's stated approximately 3% pretax margin target for 2028 — a figure that appears in no filing as guidance, from a management team of which seven of ten executive officers were first elected in 2023 or later and which has no shared track record through a full MA cycle. Meanwhile the near-term earnings profile is extraordinarily fragile: on approximately $155B of 2026 premium revenue, each 10 basis points of benefit ratio is worth roughly $1.00 of annual EPS after tax, against a full-year adjusted guide near $9. H1 2026 delivered adjusted EPS of approximately $17.79 (Q1 $10.31 reported by Morningstar plus derived Q2 ~$7.56, or ~$17.87; using the derived H1 build ~$17.79), which means consensus is modeling an H2 loss of roughly $8.7–$8.8 per share versus roughly -$0.44 in H2 2025. Part of that swing is mechanical — the IRA-redesigned Part D benefit concentrates plan liability in the back half and PDP lives grew 62.6% — but the magnitude means small per-member cost errors across 1.2 million unseasoned MA members swamp the full-year result.

The most plausible driver of the 106%-plus rally off the March low is not operating improvement. Humana lost its own Star Ratings challenge on 10/14/2025 (appeal pending, Fifth Circuit). Clover Health won a separate Georgia case, after which CMS notified plans on 6/17/2026 that it would voluntarily recalculate 2027 quality bonus payment ratings — and the government appealed that ruling on 7/22/2026. Humana is a third-party beneficiary of litigation it did not win, quantifies no benefit in any filing, and the underlying ruling is on appeal. That is a legitimate call option, but it is not a foundation for a 22x forward multiple. Probability-weighting a base case of $330 (20x FY2027 consensus), a bull case of $460 and a bear case of $240 produces a negative expected return of roughly -9%. Existing holders should trim into strength.

## Company Overview

Humana is a Louisville-based managed care organization operating two reportable segments. **Insurance** comprises individual and group Medicare Advantage HMO/PPO/PFFS and D-SNP plans in all 50 states, stand-alone Part D plans including a Walmart co-brand, Medicare Supplement, specialty benefits, state-based Medicaid in ten states, an internal PBM, and the T-5 East Region TRICARE administrative contract. **CenterWell** comprises 350 primary care clinics with approximately 1,300 providers serving 491,100 patients (146 of the clinics sit inside a de-consolidated Welsh, Carson, Anderson & Stowe development partnership), CenterWell Home Health across 37 states, mail-order and specialty pharmacy, and a roughly 35% equity interest in Gentiva Hospice.

Two structural facts define the investment case. First, this is a single-program bet that is becoming more concentrated, not less: total Medicare was 82.6% of consolidated premiums and services revenue in FY2025 and approximately 86% in H1 2026, with government-funded revenue (federal plus state Medicaid) at roughly 94.6%. Florida individual MA alone is about 1.0 million members and $17.8B of premium — roughly 14% of consolidated revenue — and the February 2026 MaxHealth acquisition ($908M net cash, $800M of it goodwill) deepened that concentration.

Second, TRICARE is routinely misread. The federal government retains all health-benefit cost risk; Humana provides administrative services only and books revenue net of estimated care costs. That is why TRICARE represents 30.7% of medical membership but produced only $1,017M of services revenue in FY2025 (0.8% of consolidated). The 23.4% military membership decline in 2025 was a contract scope change, not an economic loss of similar magnitude.

CenterWell is the genuine differentiator but is overwhelmingly captive: FY2025 intersegment revenue of $17,657M against $4,816M of external services revenue, roughly 79% internal. Its economics are largely an internal transfer-pricing outcome rather than an independently validated market business, with external primary care revenue of $2,197M (+76%) the partial exception.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is at a record; earnings are near a trough.** FY2025 revenue of $129,664M grew 10.1% while GAAP net income attributable to Humana was $1,203M — a 0.93% net margin — versus $2,484M in 2023. The company's own adjusted EPS series in the proxy runs $20.64 (2021) → $25.24 (2022) → $26.09 (2023) → $16.21 (2024). This is a Medicare Advantage margin collapse absorbed against growing revenue, and the recovery thesis rests entirely on rebuilding that margin.

**Benefit ratio mechanics.** The Q2 2026 benefit ratio of 91.1% rose 140bp year over year; H1's 90.2% rose 180bp. The 10-Q attributes the deterioration to the bonus-year-2026 Star Ratings revenue headwind, new members running at higher benefit ratios, and lower favorable prior-period development. Offsetting operating leverage is real: the Q2 operating cost ratio fell 120bp to 9.8%.

**Prior-period development is fading.** Favorable development ran $872M (2023), $701M (2024) and $1,029M (2025), the last worth approximately 80bp of reported benefit ratio. In Q2 2026 it was only $53M versus $161M a year earlier; H1 was $442M versus $638M. There are two defensible readings: reserving has become less conservative, or — because membership grew 20.7% — the prior-year book on which development is measured is a much smaller share of the total, making a smaller absolute release arithmetically expected. Benefits payable rose to $12,876M at 6/30/26 from $9,967M at 12/31/25, which is consistent with the benign reading. The data does not distinguish between them, but either way roughly 80bp of reported margin support is thinning.

**Cash flow is the weakest part of the file.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $921M against $546M of capex left approximately $375M of free cash flow, below the $430M of common dividends paid. Buybacks collapsed to $151M from $817M (2024) and $1,600M (2023), with only $103M repurchased in H1 2026 at an average $182.13 against $2.7B of authorization expiring 2/15/2027. The FY2025 shortfall was substantially an IRA-related Part D receivable timing item (net CMS receivable $1.5B versus $530M), which should reverse — so this is not evidence of a structurally uncovered dividend. But H1 2026 operating cash flow of $3,220M should not be annualized: the 10-Q discloses it includes favorable timing on an approximately $1.05B Medicaid state-directed payment that settled after June 30 and therefore reverses in Q3, and it reflects a large IBNR build that is a function of membership growth rather than profitability. Removing only the disclosed timing item leaves roughly $2.17B.

**Balance sheet: adequate solvency, strained flexibility.** At 6/30/26, total assets were $57,196M, equity $19,213M, short-term debt $2,268M (including $1,319M commercial paper) and long-term debt $11,979M. Debt-to-capitalization of 42.7% sits well inside the 60% revolver covenant; the $5.0B revolver (upsized in May 2025) is fully undrawn; statutory capital of $15.5B stands against an $8.2B regulatory minimum. Ratings are BBB/Baa2. A screening output showing an Altman Z-Score in the distress zone is not usable here — it was computed on the services-revenue line rather than consolidated revenue, and the model was calibrated on manufacturers, not on a float-funded insurer with no inventory. Corrected, the score moves into the safe zone, but it carries little information in either direction.

The binding constraint is holding-company liquidity, not consolidated solvency: parent cash and short-term investments of $1.6B, FY2026 ordinary subsidiary dividend capacity of approximately $1.1B (down from actual upstreams of $1.5B in 2024 and $1.8B in 2023), against a WCAS put/call where the first two clinic cohorts are callable by Humana in 2026 for approximately $1.0–1.6B. The put liability's fair value was $1.6B at 6/30/26, up from $1.4B, and mark-to-market on it has been the largest single drag on GAAP EPS ($513M or $4.25/share in FY2025; $177M or $1.47/share in H1 2026).

Three 2026 financing actions sit alongside that: $1.0B of 6.625% junior subordinated notes due 2056 with a ten-year interest deferral option (March); a $1.5B pre-capitalized trust securities facility costing approximately $26.8M per year in fees on undrawn capacity, with mandatory exercise if consolidated net worth is believed to have fallen below $4.0B (May); and non-qualifying reinsurance under deposit accounting explicitly described as reducing capital and surplus requirements. The $4.0B trigger sits far below current equity of $19.2B, so none of this signals distress. The coherent reading is that growing 20.7% in a statutory-capital-intensive business while facing a large near-term call obligation made pre-arranged capital access a priority; an equally plausible reading is that a CFO with Morgan Stanley treasury, Fannie Mae and Evercore experience is modernizing a plain-vanilla capital structure and buying cheap optionality. I do not infer motive from structure. What is observable is that flexibility is being purchased rather than generated internally, and interest cost is heading toward roughly $720–730M annually from $631M in FY2025, on top of $7.25B notional of fixed-to-floating swaps that swung to a net liability position ($146M liabilities versus $17M assets).

Goodwill of $10,485M equals 55% of book equity, with the 10-K flagging the home solutions reporting unit and its $4.4B of goodwill as most impairment-sensitive. Indefinite-lived intangible impairments have recurred: $128M (2025), $200M (2024), $55M (2023).

**Financial health score: 4.5.** Statutory cushion and covenant headroom are genuine strengths; trailing FCF below the dividend, thin parent liquidity against a $1.0–1.6B call, three financings in five months, rising interest cost and goodwill at 55% of equity are genuine offsets.

## Growth Analysis

The volume growth is delivered and verifiable. Individual MA membership of 6,453,700 is up 1,224,400 (+23.4%); group MA +27.6%; PDP +62.6%; total Medicare +35.3%. That matched the roughly 25% individual MA growth guided in the 10-K. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $16.48 implies 80% growth off FY2026's $9.15 and has risen 8.0% over 90 days; the sell-side distribution has improved (sell ratings 2 → 1, buy ratings 7 → 11 over three months).

Three qualifications matter more than the headline.

First, the growth is arriving where the cost-control apparatus has least reach. Value-based penetration of individual MA fell from 71% (12/31/24) to 68% (12/31/25) to 64% (6/30/26), and full shared-risk arrangements covered only 37.1% of individual MA at year-end 2025. Home health geographic overlap with individual MA was 68% — measured before the surge. The absolute number of members in value-based relationships rose (roughly 3.59M to 4.12M), and value-based contracting typically lags enrollment by a year or two, so the ratio can recover mechanically. But the Q2 10-Q names new members running at higher benefit ratios as a driver of the 140bp benefit-ratio increase, so the dilution is currently showing up in the P&L.

Second, roughly 9% of that volume is being handed back. On the Q2 call management disclosed plans to exit its least profitable MA markets for 2027, affecting approximately 600,000 members, with an intention to recapture a significant portion as it did in 2025. Cutting the lower tail of profitability is disciplined, but the shed-412K / add-1,224K / exit-600K sequence across three enrollment cycles makes the earnings base genuinely hard to underwrite.

Third, the funding gap persists. Management reaffirmed a 2026 cost trend assumption of 7–8% inclusive of medical and pharmacy. CMS reportedly finalized the 2027 MA rate at +2.48% versus +0.09% proposed — a figure I could not corroborate against a primary CMS document, and whose treatment of risk-score trend (historically worth roughly two additional points of realized revenue per member) is unestablished. Even generously interpreted, the gap versus 7–8% cost trend explains the benefit-design cuts and market exits.

A modest 2027 tailwind: CMS finalized FY2027 provider updates of 2.3% for inpatient hospitals, 2.4% for SNFs, and 2.3% for IRFs and hospices, and proposed CY2027 physician fee cuts. Because a significant portion of Humana's Medicare network contracts reference Medicare reimbursement levels, sub-inflation provider updates mechanically restrain unit-cost growth.

**Growth prospects score: 5.5.** Revenue and membership growth are large and confirmed; earnings growth is trough recovery of uncertain durability, delivered at diluting integration quality, with 600K of volume scheduled to exit.

But note the revision direction: the Q3 estimate moved from -$0.06 to -$1.02 over 90 days even as the full year rose 3.7%. **Valuation score: 4.5**, up from 3.5 at the prior review. The price fell 8% while FY2027 estimates rose 4.8% in 30 days, a genuine ~13% improvement in the forward multiple. It is not enough to create a margin of safety.

## Competitive Landscape

Humana is the Medicare Advantage pure-play, and its own 10-K is candid: "Many of our competitors have a larger membership base and/or greater financial resources than our health plans in the markets in which we compete." There is no claimed scale or cost advantage over UnitedHealth/Optum. That purity magnifies both drawdown and recovery relative to UNH (Optum, commercial, international), CVS/Aetna (retail, PBM) and Elevance (commercial-weighted).

The 2026 peer divergence is informative. UnitedHealth beat and *raised* full-year guidance in both Q1 and Q2 2026, citing better cost control. Humana beat and *affirmed* in both quarters, and the stock fell on both occasions (-6.4% on 7/29). Whether the difference reflects genuine H2 exposure or floor-setting conservatism, the market has priced Humana as the less confident operator within a sector that is re-rating together.

Trajectory assessment is mixed rather than clean. Humana is gaining volume share aggressively (+23.4% individual MA, +62.6% PDP) — that is a hard-data share gain in a market where most competitors retrenched. But it is gaining share at a benefit ratio 140–180bp worse, with care-model integration diluting (68% → 64% value-based), and it is scheduled to hand back 600,000 members in 2027. CenterWell is the structural asset and the right strategic direction, but at 79% internal revenue it is not yet an externally validated business, and its FY2025 income from operations was essentially flat at $1,339M on revenue up 12.7% as the CMS v28 risk-model phase-in compressed the revenue side of value-based arrangements.

**Competitive position score: 4.5.** Volume share improving, margin share and integration quality deteriorating, no structural cost advantage claimed.

## Risk Assessment

**Star Ratings — the dominant single variable.** Humana's challenge to the 2025 Star Ratings was rejected by the N.D. Tex. court on 10/14/2025; the Fifth Circuit appeal filed 11/25/2025 is pending. The bonus-year-2026 revenue headwind is therefore fully in the P&L and is the named driver of H1's benefit-ratio deterioration. Separately, Clover Health's Georgia win prompted CMS on 6/17/2026 to notify plans it would voluntarily recalculate 2027 quality bonus payment ratings; the government appealed that ruling on 7/22/2026. Humana's potential benefit is contingent on litigation it did not win surviving an appeal, and no Humana filing quantifies any amount. KFF estimated MA insurers would receive at least $13.4B in 2026 quality bonus payments, which frames the sector stakes without sizing Humana's share.

**RADV — large, unquantified, directionally favorable but unresolved.** In *Humana v. Kennedy* the court granted Humana summary judgment on 9/25/2025 and vacated the Final RADV Rule as procedurally invalid; the government appealed on 11/21/2025. CMS has audits underway for PY2018–2021 and announced on 5/21/2025 that it would audit all eligible MA contracts annually and expedite PY2018–PY2024. Humana's certified bids through PY2027 preserved its fee-for-service adjuster position. If the vacatur is reversed and extrapolation applies retroactively, exposure spans seven payment years and is not reserved in any way visible from the disclosures.

**DOJ False Claims Act.** DOJ filed a complaint in partial intervention on 5/1/2025 in *U.S. ex rel. Shea v. eHealth et al.* concerning non-commission payments to three call-center broker partners and marketing of MA to under-65 beneficiaries. FCA damages are trebled with per-claim penalties. A separate DOJ Civil Division inquiry into Part C risk-adjustment practices is ongoing, and the *In re Humana Securities Litigation* class period (July 2022–October 2024) overlaps thematically with the broker allegations.

**Concentration.** Government revenue at approximately 94.6%; Medicare at approximately 86% of H1 premiums and rising; Florida at ~14% of consolidated revenue, deepened by MaxHealth. Claims processing is delegated for 299,900 members (299,100 individual MA) where Humana discloses "a limited view of the underlying claims experience," representing approximately $3.7B or 3.3% of total benefits expense — a blind spot precisely where new-member cost surprises would originate. Reserve estimability sensitivity: 1.0 point on the claims trend factor moves benefits payable $190M.

**Goodwill.** $10,485M is 55% of book equity; the home solutions unit's $4.4B is flagged as most sensitive; intangible impairments have occurred in each of the last three years.

Positively, the Q2 10-Q states there were no changes to the risk factors from the FY2025 10-K, and all material CMS contracts were renewed for 2026 with all 2026 product filings approved.

**Risk factors score: 3.5.** Two unquantified federal exposures (RADV, FCA) with multi-year reach, a near-term earnings print levered roughly $1.00 per 10bp to an unobservable metric, and a principal upside driver under appeal.

### Options Market Signal

Ignore the headline 146.4% near-term implied volatility — that is a one-day expiry with an implied move of only 1.4%, which is same-day pinning mechanics rather than stress. The informative structure is the 1m–6m ladder: 39.3% (13 days), 45.2% (41 days), 50.7% (104 days), against 49.5% one-year realized. Near-dated vol is actually slightly cheap to realized, while vol rises with tenor into the late-October Q3 print and the Q4/AEP season — an upward-sloping curve consistent with the market locating uncertainty in H2, exactly where consensus models an $8.86 loss. Put/call volume ratios escalate sharply with tenor (2.19 at 1m, 8.72 at 3m, 19.86 at 6m), indicating demand for longer-dated downside protection. IV sits at the 89th percentile within the chain. Open-interest put/call fell from 1.35 to 0.61 since 7/19 while the price declined 3.7%, which is ambiguous — either call-side positioning rebuilt or put open interest was closed — and I would not read direction into it. Short interest is only 4.0% of float at 2.8 days to cover, so there is neither a crowded bearish position nor squeeze fuel. Net read: the options market corroborates that the risk is dated and back-half-weighted, and near-dated protection is not expensive relative to realized volatility.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($460, +25%)

Guidance of approximately $9 is a deliberate floor set by a CFO rebuilding credibility after the April 2024 withdrawal — a pattern already visible in 2025, when nine-month adjusted EPS exceeded $21 against an "at least $17" guide. H2 2026 lands materially better than the -$8.8 consensus; the 1.2 million new members season in line rather than hot; value-based penetration recovers mechanically toward 70% as provider contracts are papered; MaxHealth and continued WCAS clinic development close the Florida integration gap. The CMS 2027 quality bonus recalculation survives appeal and delivers a revenue step-up that no filing has yet quantified, while the 2027 rate at approximately +2.48% plus roughly two points of risk-score trend narrows the funding gap against 7–8% cost trend. The 600,000-member exit removes the worst-margin cohort, and management's approximately 3% pretax margin lands in 2028 at $29–35 of EPS. Sub-inflation FY2027 provider updates restrain unit costs. At 14x the middle of that range the equity is worth $450–470, and the sell-side's $513 high target is achievable.

### Bear Case ($240, -35%)

New-member cost experience deteriorates. On 1.2 million unseasoned MA lives and 1.5 million new PDP lives, a 90bp benefit-ratio miss consumes the entire full-year adjusted guide, and 3.3% of benefits expense sits in delegated arrangements where Humana explicitly has limited visibility. The fading prior-period development (Q2: $53M versus $161M) removes roughly 80bp of reported margin support. The government's appeal of the Georgia ruling succeeds, the CMS recalculation is withdrawn, and the quality-bonus premium in the share price — the most plausible explanation for the 106% move off the March low — unwinds. The Fifth Circuit reverses the RADV vacatur, exposing seven payment years to extrapolated recoveries with no observable reserve. FY2027 guidance lands below the $16.48 consensus because the 600,000-member exit is not recaptured. Parent liquidity of $1.6B and ~$1.1B of subsidiary dividend capacity meet a $1.0–1.6B WCAS call, forcing additional issuance and further deferring the buyback. At 15x $14 of FY2027 EPS with a litigation discount, the equity is worth $210–250.

### Base Case ($330, -10%)

H2 2026 comes in messy but not catastrophic; full-year adjusted EPS clears the approximately $9 floor modestly. FY2027 lands near consensus $16.48 on the 600,000-member exit and partial recapture. The quality-bonus recalculation remains unresolved through the appeal, so the market keeps discounting it. Margin recovery progresses but on a slower path than the 2028 target implies, and the multiple compresses toward 20x forward: $330.

Probability weighting 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear yields approximately -9% expected return.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: REDUCE. De-risking plan.** Trim into the current strength rather than waiting for the Q3 print in late October. The stock is 125% above its March low and 14.4% below its 52-week high of $428.88, and the near-dated implied volatility of 39.3% is below 49.5% realized, so protection is not expensive if a holder prefers to hedge rather than sell. Reduce to a below-benchmark weight; the residual position is justified only by the quality-bonus option value and the 2028 margin path, neither of which is verifiable today. Do not add. For prospective buyers, the entry that would restore asymmetry is $270–290 — 16.5–17.6x FY2027 consensus, near the low end of the analyst range and roughly 9–10x the bottom of the 2028 scenario.

**Timeframe.** The decisive resolution window is Q3 2026 results in late October, followed by FY2027 guidance in early 2027 and the Fifth Circuit rulings on both the Star Ratings and RADV appeals.

**Triggers that would restore HOLD or better:**
1. Q3 2026 adjusted EPS meaningfully above the -$1.02 consensus with a full-year benefit ratio held at or below 90.2%, demonstrating that the 1.2 million new members are seasoning in line.
2. Any filing that quantifies a bonus-year-2027 quality-bonus benefit, or a Fifth Circuit affirmation of the RADV vacatur that removes the retroactive-extrapolation tail.
3. FY2027 guidance at or above $16.48 with the 600,000-member exit fully reflected, plus resumption of buybacks at scale (parent liquidity above roughly $2.5B after any WCAS call), which would confirm that capital flexibility has been restored internally.

**Triggers that would move this to AVOID:**
1. A full-year benefit ratio above 91.5%, or a Q4 loss materially wider than -$7.84, indicating new-member cost experience has broken from plan.
2. Reversal of the RADV vacatur on appeal, or a DOJ FCA development that quantifies exposure, given trebled damages against 120M shares.
3. Goodwill impairment in the home solutions unit ($4.4B carrying value), or a WCAS call funded by additional subordinated issuance that pushes debt-to-capitalization above 50%.

**Next reassessment:** Q3 2026 earnings, late October 2026, with an interim check at the CMS 2027 Star Ratings release and any Fifth Circuit scheduling.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory — 4.7 (Feb) → 4.6 (May) → 5.2 (Jun) → 4.7 (Jul) — with three HOLDs and one AVOID reflects genuine ambivalence about a business that is executing operationally while its equity re-rates faster than its earnings. This review moves to REDUCE at 4.6, and the reason is not the 8% price decline since the last look. Valuation actually improved: the price fell while FY2027 estimates rose 4.8% in 30 days and 8.0% in 90 days, taking the forward multiple from roughly 25x to 22.3x. Growth prospects are intact — 23.4% individual MA growth was delivered as guided, four consecutive quarters beat, and the operating cost ratio improved 120bp.

The downgrade rests on asymmetry. At $367.24 the equity is priced at roughly 40x this year's consensus earnings, and closing the gap to a defensible multiple requires an unguided 2028 margin target from a management team with no shared cycle history. Between here and there sits an H2 that consensus models as an $8.86-per-share loss, on a book where each 10 basis points of benefit ratio is worth about $1.00 of annual EPS and where 3.3% of benefits expense sits in delegated arrangements the company says it cannot see clearly. The largest identifiable upside driver is a CMS quality-bonus recalculation triggered by a competitor's lawsuit, quantified nowhere in Humana's filings, and under active government appeal since 7/22/2026. Two unquantified federal exposures — RADV across seven payment years and a DOJ False Claims Act intervention with trebled damages — sit behind that. Free cash flow did not cover the dividend in FY2025, the buyback is effectively suspended, and the company has purchased $2.5B of capital access in five months while paying roughly $27M a year for the privilege of not drawing $1.5B of it.
