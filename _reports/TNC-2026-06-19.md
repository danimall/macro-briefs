**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Tennant Company is a global leader in non-residential floor-cleaning and maintenance equipment that suffered a sharp, largely self-inflicted earnings collapse in 2025 and Q1 2026. The dominant cause was a botched November 2025 North America ERP go-live that lost three weeks of order entry and parts shipping, compounded by a two-week January 2026 manufacturing shutdown for physical inventory and the lapping of a one-time 2024 backlog-reduction benefit. Operating margin fell from a normal 8-11% range to 5.7% for full-year 2025 and to 1.6% in Q1 2026. The central investment debate is whether margins revert as management's stated mid-2026 normalization plays out. The evidence supports the transitory read: Q1 2026 organic decline was only -1.9%, the most recent quarter beat consensus by 42% ($0.58 vs $0.41), and forward estimates have begun rising again (current-year EPS +2.7% and next-year +11.4% over the past 30 days).

A live activist campaign reshapes the story. Vision One Management Partners accumulated a stake (disclosed buying ~113K shares in November 2025) and signed a Cooperation Agreement in February 2026 that added two independent directors, including James Glerum, who made a $500K open-market purchase at $61.25 in March 2026. A new COO (the company's first since 2021) starts July 1. These developments are constructive and partly explain the aggressive debt-funded buyback program.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is the entry point. Shares have rallied ~45% from the $61-63 levels where the activist and director Glerum were accumulating, to $90.75 — within 1% of the analyst mean target and near the 52-week high. Leverage jumped from 31.2% to 40.2% debt-to-capital in a single quarter as Tennant repurchased $60M of stock with revolver borrowings while EBITDA was falling. The EMEA goodwill cushion is only 7.9% ($36.8M on $172.6M carrying value), and the Oxygenator Water Technologies (OWT) litigation carries a ~$20.5M accrued liability. Recovery is real but substantially priced in; the residual upside no longer compensates for the balance-sheet and execution risk at this level.

## Company Overview

Tennant Company (founded 1870, headquartered in Eden Prairie, MN) designs, manufactures, and markets floor-cleaning and maintenance equipment for non-residential surfaces, operating as a single reportable segment across Americas, EMEA, and APAC. The business runs 11 global manufacturing sites with roughly 4,484 employees and sells direct in 21 countries plus through distributors in 100+, claiming the industry's largest direct sales and service organization. The customer base exceeds 40,000 with no material single-customer concentration, a structural positive for revenue stability.

The Q1 2026 revenue mix was approximately 60% equipment ($178M), 22% parts and consumables ($65M), and 18% service and other ($55M). The aftermarket parts/consumables and service streams (~40% combined) are higher-margin and recurring, providing ballast against equipment-sale cyclicality. Brands include Tennant, Nobles, Alfa, IPC, Gaomei, and Rongen, alongside differentiated technologies: ec-H2O NanoClean (detergent-free cleaning), IRIS asset management, ReadySpace carpet cleaning, and an expanding autonomous mobile robotics (AMR) portfolio. Tennant owns and develops its own AMR products; Brain Corp is a minority investee and autonomy-technology partner accounted for under the equity method (~12% economic / ~18% voting interest, $20.0M carrying value), not the owner of Tennant's robots.

The secular growth vector is automation and autonomous cleaning, driven by persistent labor shortages in facilities maintenance. Competition includes multinationals (Nilfisk, Kärcher, Hako) and regional players in a mature, moderately cyclical, and, per Tennant's own risk language, "very price-sensitive" market that constrains full cost pass-through.

The financial picture is one of a stable franchise enduring an acute, mostly self-inflicted disruption.

**Revenue.** Full-year 2025 organic revenue fell -7.3% (volume -8.7%, price +1.4%). Two drivers: lapping a large 2024 backlog-reduction benefit (high-margin industrial direct sales that will not repeat), and the ERP go-live disruption. Americas revenue fell -10.9%; EMEA grew +5.1% (largely FX); APAC fell -3.5%. Q1 2026 reported +2.7% was entirely FX and acquisitions; organic was -1.9%, a milder decline than full-year 2025, consistent with the disruption fading and demand holding.

**Margins.** Gross margin compressed 250 bps in 2025 (volume deleverage, ERP inefficiencies, higher material/tariff cost, unfavorable mix) and a further 330 bps YoY in Q1 2026 (ERP-recovery labor, freight, expediting, plus strategic-account mix). Management states tariffs were "fully offset by price/cost-out" in Q1. The 1.6% Q1 operating margin against a historical 8-11% range quantifies the size of the recovery available if normalization holds.

**Cash flow and balance sheet — the area to watch.** Full-year 2025 FCF was $43.3M (3.6% margin), with operating cash flow falling to $65M from $89.7M. Q1 2026 burned $31.2M in operating cash (vs. -$0.4M prior year) on a working-capital build (receivables +$25M, plus inventory). Revolver borrowings jumped from $272.5M (12/31/25) to $357.5M (3/31/26), lifting debt-to-capital from 31.2% to 40.2% in one quarter, driven primarily by $60M of debt-funded buybacks at ~$63/share. Altman Z sits at 2.62 (grey zone); current ratio is healthy at ~2.0. Covenant headroom exists (net debt/EBITDA cap 3.75x, in compliance), but leverage is rising while EBITDA is falling, which requires monitoring. Repurchasing stock with debt during depressed earnings is aggressive and reflects activist influence; it improves per-share optics in a recovery but reduces balance-sheet resilience if normalization slips.

**Earnings track record.** Tennant missed three of the last four quarters before the Q1 2026 beat (+42.4%), which followed deeply reset estimates. The beat is genuine and signals the bottom may be in, but it came off a low bar ($0.41 estimate) and does not yet establish a durable margin-recovery trend.

## Growth Analysis

The forward case is a margin-recovery story, not a top-line growth story. If management's mid-2026 ERP normalization holds, operating margin recovers from 1.6% toward the historical range, mechanically driving EPS sharply higher off the depressed base. Estimate revisions support this: current-year EPS revised +2.7% over 30 days (to $5.12) and next-year +11.4% (to $6.53), reversing the 90-day downdraft (current-year -23.8%) that accompanied the ERP disaster. The 30-day reversal to positive shows consensus beginning to price the recovery.

This does not clear the threshold for a BUY signal. The current-year and next-year revisions are recovering, but the 90-day current-year revision was downward (-23.8%), and current-quarter estimates are still falling (-10.1% over 30 days, reflecting continued Q2 ERP-recovery cost). Revenue growth is organic-negative, not the >40% acceleration that defines a true inflection setup. This is a normalization off a self-inflicted trough, not a structural demand acceleration.

The durable secular vector is AMR/autonomous cleaning, driven by labor shortages. Tennant restructured AMR contracts effective March 1, 2026 to recognize embedded autonomy software upfront with connectivity/services ratable, and its deferred-revenue book has grown to $34.4M. EMEA tuck-in distributor acquisitions (Clean Machine, Sweden, $7.7M; R4Y, Germany, $3.6M) modestly expand the direct footprint. These are incremental, not transformational. The realistic growth profile is low-single-digit organic top line with a one-time margin-recovery EPS step-up, after which the company reverts to a mid-single-digit compounder.

## Valuation Assessment

At $90.75, TNC trades at ~50x trailing EPS ($2.36), a number distorted by trough earnings and not the right lens. On forward estimates, the stock trades at ~17.7x current-year ($5.12) and ~13.9x next-year ($6.53). Against a peer median P/E of 22.1 (Enerpac 22.1, Kennametal 19.4, with Proto Labs at 73.5 and two loss-makers excluded), the forward multiple sits below the peer median.

The audit correctly flags that anchoring to the activist's $61-63 accumulation low as "fair value" would be an error. The forward math does not support an AVOID: a recovering name at ~14-18x forward, below the 22x peer median, with rising estimates and a live activist, is not structurally impaired. It is a balanced setup.

The forward math also does not support a BUY. The below-peer forward multiple is contingent on the recovery executing — next-year EPS of $6.53 assumes margins revert to roughly historical levels by 2027. That is the consensus base case, and the stock at $90.75 already discounts most of it. The market has moved ~45% off the trough to within 1% of the analyst mean target ($91.5) and inside the 52-week high range. The discount to peers compensates for: (a) the unproven margin recovery, (b) 40.2% debt-to-capital after debt-funded buybacks, (c) a 7.9% EMEA goodwill cushion vulnerable to impairment, and (d) the OWT litigation overhang. A genuine valuation entry existed in the low-to-mid $60s; at $90.75 the asymmetry has largely closed.

Analyst targets (low $85, mean $91.5, median $91, high $99) bracket the current price tightly, implying the market sees roughly symmetric, low-conviction risk/reward — the profile of a fairly-valued stock, which is what a HOLD reflects. I do not treat the consensus target as a floor here: revenue is not accelerating and the multiple already embeds the recovery, so the inflection-stock "targets-as-floors" logic does not apply.

## Competitive Landscape

Tennant's competitive position is stable-to-improving, anchored by structural moats: the industry's largest direct sales and service network, a broad installed base driving recurring parts/consumables/service revenue (~40% of sales), and brand breadth across premium and mid-tier price points and geographies. No material customer concentration limits single-counterparty risk across the customer base.

Trajectory cuts two ways. The five-year TSR underperformance (company index 112.3 vs. peer group 189.7) confirms Tennant has been a relative laggard in industrials — the classic activist trigger and evidence that capital allocation and operational execution lagged peers. The activist campaign and new COO are aimed precisely at closing that gap. On the product side, the AMR/autonomous-cleaning push positions Tennant for the labor-shortage-driven secular vector, where it leverages the Brain Corp autonomy partnership. Pricing power is the constraint: management's own risk language describes a "very price-sensitive" market, which is why 2025 price realization of +1.4% could not offset -8.7% volume. The competitive position is solid and the moat intact; the case for improvement rests on activist-driven execution that is not yet demonstrated in margins.

## Risk Assessment

1. **ERP stabilization (dominant near-term risk).** Management expects inefficiencies to persist into Q2 2026, normalizing by mid-year. The entire margin-recovery thesis hinges on this timeline holding. Slippage directly invalidates the bull case.

2. **Rising leverage into falling EBITDA.** Adjusted EBITDA fell from $208.8M (2024) to $167.4M (2025). Covenant headroom exists (3.75x cap), but the trend reduces resilience if recovery slips. A new 2.0M-share authorization signals more debt-funded repurchases.

3. **EMEA goodwill cushion of only 7.9%** ($36.8M on $172.6M carrying value as of 10/1/2025), susceptible to impairment if results or assumptions deteriorate. A non-cash but sentiment-negative tail.

4. **OWT litigation.** November 2024 jury verdict against Tennant, with September 2025 enhanced damages. $20.3M supersedeas bond posted on appeal. Management states it does not restrict product sales — manageable but real.

5. **Tariffs with a twist.** The February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling struck down IEEPA-based tariffs Tennant paid in 2025, creating an unquantified potential refund. But new 150-day tariffs under different authority (from 2/24/26) keep uncertainty live; steel, alloys, and resin are key inputs with sole-source concentration.

6. **Demand cyclicality and FX.** Equipment sales track customer capex; North American industrial weakness and China/SE Asia softness were noted in H2 2025. FX has been a tailwind (+4.1% in Q1'26) but adds volatility.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is sanguine and does not confirm a bearish thesis despite 10.4% short interest. Near-term ATM IV is 24.8% against a one-year historical volatility of 37.8% — implied vol is cheap (-13.0% spread, 4th percentile within the chain). Put/call skew is balanced at 1.7%, and the term structure is in normal contango (24.8% near-term vs. 30.4% at six months). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±5.8%. The cheap IV and balanced skew indicate the market is not pricing an imminent negative catalyst and views the recovery as largely on track. For a HOLD, this argues against any urgency; there is no options-implied dislocation to exploit in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** ERP fully stabilizes by mid-2026 as guided; operating margin reverts toward the historical range; next-year EPS reaches the $6.53 consensus or better; the activist drives capital-allocation discipline and an eventual TSR re-rating toward the peer group; the IEEPA tariff refund materializes as upside. At ~16-18x next-year EPS of $6.53-7.00, the stock reaches ~$105-115.

**Bear case.** ERP recovery slips into H2 2026; margins stay compressed through year-end; the working-capital drag and debt-funded buybacks push leverage higher into a softening industrial-capex backdrop; EMEA goodwill impairs as the cushion erodes; demand softens with the H2 industrial slowdown. EPS recovery stalls near $4.50-5.00 and the multiple de-rates toward the peer median on an unproven turnaround, taking the stock to ~$70-72 (~21-23% downside).

**Base case.** ERP normalizes roughly on schedule with some residual Q2 cost; full-year EPS lands near $5.00-5.20 and next-year near $6.00-6.50; the activist keeps modest pressure on capital allocation. The stock holds in the low-to-mid $80s to low $90s, with the recovery rally largely complete and limited further re-rating until margin recovery is demonstrated in reported results. This is a ~6-12% downside-to-flat outcome from $90.75 — symmetric with the bull case and consistent with the tight analyst target band, which is why the rating is HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings report (early August 2026), which will reveal whether ERP inefficiencies normalized by mid-year as guided. The July 1 COO transition and any further activist developments are interim inputs.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 2026 operating margin recovers above ~6% (vs. 1.6% in Q1), confirming the ERP normalization on schedule, alongside positive operating cash flow.
- A pullback to the mid-$70s or below on no fundamental deterioration, which would restore a forward multiple (sub-15x current-year) with genuine asymmetric upside to the recovery.
- Debt-to-capital stabilizes or declines below ~37% as the working-capital build reverses and buyback pace moderates.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- ERP recovery slips past mid-2026 with Q2 operating margin stuck below ~3% and management pushing normalization into 2027.
- An EMEA goodwill impairment is taken, or the EMEA cushion commentary worsens materially.
- Continued debt-funded buybacks push debt-to-capital above ~45% while EBITDA fails to recover, or a covenant-headroom warning emerges.
- Organic revenue turns sharply negative (worse than -5%), indicating the disruption was masking genuine demand erosion rather than transitory ERP effects.

## Conclusion

Tennant is a stable, moated franchise that inflicted a serious but largely transitory wound on itself through a botched ERP implementation. The recovery thesis is intact and supported by the Q1 2026 beat, the milder -1.9% organic decline, and the 30-day reversal in forward estimates. A live activist with two board seats, a $500K director open-market buy at the lows, and a new COO add credible operational catalysts. None of this constitutes structural impairment, and an AVOID would wrongly anchor fair value to the activist's trough accumulation price.

The reason to wait is price and balance sheet. At $90.75, the stock has rallied ~45% from where insiders were buying, sits within 1% of the analyst mean target, and trades at a forward multiple that, while below peers, already discounts most of the expected margin recovery. Leverage jumped to 40.2% debt-to-capital in one quarter on debt-funded buybacks against falling EBITDA, the EMEA goodwill cushion is thin, and the OWT litigation remains an overhang. The base case is a low-to-mid-$80s-to-low-$90s range with roughly symmetric risk/reward — a fairly-valued recovery that needs to demonstrate margin reversion in reported results before the next leg. Recommendation: **HOLD**. Constructive on the franchise and the turnaround; patient on the entry.
