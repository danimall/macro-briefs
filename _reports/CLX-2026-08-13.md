## Executive Summary

Clorox reported FY2026 results on August 3 and filed its 10-K on August 7. The stock rose from $98.26 to $108.25 over the following six sessions before settling at $104.69, a gain of about 9% from the $95.76 level at which we last assessed it. Over the same window, consensus FY2027 adjusted EPS fell from $6.13 to $5.81 — a 5.2% cut in 30 days and 8.0% over 90 days — and the September-quarter estimate was cut 12.7% to $1.11. The forward multiple therefore expanded from roughly 15.6x to 18.0x while forward earnings power was marked down. The stock now trades 2.9% above the consensus mean target of $101.71 and above the median of $100.00, with a sell-side distribution of one buy, fourteen holds, two sells and two strong sells, and a degrading sentiment trend.

The re-rating is not irrational. Before the print, the market was pricing a tail: reported FY2026 free cash flow of $405M against $602M of dividends paid, a headline Altman Z-score in the distress range, and 12.9% short interest. Both distortions have been resolved on the evidence. The $476M buyout of Procter & Gamble's 20% Glad interest ran through operating cash flow, so normalized FY2026 operating cash flow was approximately $1,088M and free cash flow approximately $881M, putting the dividend payout at roughly 68% of cash generation rather than uncovered. Recomputing the Altman model with actual EBIT of $921M and market rather than book equity yields approximately 2.19 — grey zone, not distress. A short base at 5.8 days to cover, colliding with the removal of a distress narrative, plausibly explains much of the move.

What is left after the de-risking is the problem. FY2027 revenue guidance of +13% to +14% decomposes into roughly 9.5 points from the GOJO/Purell consolidation, more than 3.5 points from lapping the ERP-driven retailer inventory drawdown, and therefore approximately zero to one point of genuine underlying organic growth against a long-term company target of 3% to 5%. Gross margin is guided *down* to ~42% from FY2026's 42.8% adjusted level despite four identifiable tailwinds. The GOJO acquisition cut return on invested capital from approximately 36% to approximately 15%, and the post-synergy unlevered after-tax return on the $1.92B net purchase price is roughly 6.2% — at or below a plausible cost of capital. Leverage sits near 3.3x pro forma, roughly $1.1B of short-dated borrowings roll within twelve months, and the CEO announced her resignation in May 2026 with no successor identified. At 17.9x guided adjusted EPS with a 4.79% yield, the expected total return is approximately 3%, below what Treasury bills pay. Trim.

## Company Overview

Clorox manufactures and markets household and professional cleaning, bag, litter, grilling, food, water-filtration and natural personal care products, with 84% of FY2026 net sales in the United States. The company reports four segments:

| Segment (FY2026) | Net sales ($M) | YoY | Organic | Segment adj. EBIT ($M) | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Health and Wellness | 2,697 | 0% | -8% | 678 | -19% |
| Household | 1,787 | -11% | -11% | 192 | -41% |
| Lifestyle | 1,123 | -14% | -14% | 208 | -28% |
| International | 1,113 | +5% | +2% | 113 | +3% |
| **Total** | **6,720** | **-5%** | **-8%** | **1,191** | |

Over 80% of sales come from brands holding #1 or #2 category share. Cleaning products are ~44% of consolidated sales, bags and wraps ~15%, food ~11%, cat litter ~10%. Walmart represented 26% of consolidated net sales in FY2026 (27% FY2025, 25% FY2024), and the top five customers approximately half of sales in each of the last three years.

Two structural changes closed during FY2026. First, on April 1 Clorox acquired GOJO Industries — the Purell franchise — for $2.25B headline consideration including approximately $330M of anticipated tax benefits, a net purchase price of $1.92B, funded primarily with debt. GOJO carried nearly $800M of annual sales at a 5% three-year revenue CAGR, with more than 80% of revenue through B2B distributors and approximately 20 million installed dispensers driving refill demand. Disclosed multiples were 11.9x adjusted EBITDA net of tax benefits and 9.1x net of tax benefits and at least $50M of targeted run-rate synergies. Second, the Glad venture agreement with P&G expired January 31, 2026, and Clorox paid $476M on March 2 to acquire P&G's 20% interest, retaining royalty-free licenses to the contributed intellectual property. Clorox is now the sole owner of Glad and pays no royalty, but loses P&G's R&D support.

Headcount rose to approximately 9,200 at June 30, 2026 from approximately 7,600 at March 31, primarily from GOJO. The FY2026 recordable incident rate of 0.63 excludes GOJO employees, a small but concrete indication that reporting-system integration is incomplete.

## Financial Analysis

**The FY2026 income statement is not a usable base.** A five-year, ~$580M ERP replacement went live in the U.S. in January 2026. Retailers pre-ordered ahead of the cutover, adding approximately 3.5 points of net sales and $0.90 of EPS to FY2025, then drew that inventory down in FY2026, subtracting approximately 7.5 points of sales and $0.90 of EPS — a roughly $1.80 swing in the year-over-year EPS comparison. In Q4 FY2026 the lap accounted for approximately 13.5 points of the 13% organic decline and approximately $0.90 of the $1.21 adjusted EPS decline.

|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | 7,104 | 6,720 | +13% to +14% |
| Reported gross margin | 45.2% | 42.3% | ~42% |
| Adjusted gross margin | — | 42.8% | — |
| Adjusted EBIT | 1,316 | 1,030 | — |
| Adjusted EBIT margin | 18.5% | 15.3% | — |
| Diluted EPS (GAAP) | $6.52 | $4.81 | $5.41–$5.71 |
| Adjusted diluted EPS | $7.72 | $5.53 | $5.70–$6.00 |
| Diluted shares (000) | 124,287 | 122,132 | — |

Revenue has not grown in four years: $7,389M (FY2023) → $7,093M → $7,104M → $6,720M, part of which reflects deliberate pruning (Argentina exit March 2024; Better Health VMS divested September 2024 at a $118M after-tax loss).

**Cash flow, properly adjusted, is adequate.** Reported FY2026 operating cash flow of $612M was down 38% from $981M, which management attributes primarily to the Glad termination payment. Adding back $476M gives normalized operating cash flow of $1,088M; less $207M of capex, normalized free cash flow of approximately $881M against $602M of dividends paid — a 68% payout. Anyone screening on the reported figure reaches a false conclusion about dividend sustainability. Anyone ignoring the classification overstates cash conversion. The classification itself is defensible: the P&G interest was carried in accounts payable and accrued liabilities as a mandatorily redeemable instrument, and settlement of such a liability plausibly belongs in operating activities. Note that normalized FY2026 FCF also benefited from the wind-down of digital-transformation spending and does not include a full year of GOJO's manufacturing capex, so it should not be treated as a run rate.

**The balance sheet is the constraint.** Total debt at June 30, 2026 was $5,068M ($1,086M notes and loans payable, $1M current maturities, $3,981M long-term), roughly double the $2,488M a year earlier. Net debt is $4,925M. Working capital is negative $949M for a current ratio of 0.66. Book equity attributable to Clorox is $90M against $7,794M of assets, the arithmetic residue of decades of buybacks ($1,582M treasury stock against $386M retained earnings). Goodwill of $1,945M plus trademarks of $989M plus other intangibles of $606M equals $3,540M, or 45% of assets.

Reported ROE of 668% and debt/equity of 83.8x are artifacts of the $90M equity base and convey nothing. ROIC is the right lens, and it deteriorated sharply: FY2025 NOPAT of $1,007M on invested capital of $2,803M gives approximately 35.9%; FY2026 NOPAT of $783M on invested capital of $5,177M gives approximately 15.1%. Invested capital nearly doubled while revenue fell 5%. Normalizing FY2026 EBIT for the ERP drag and annualizing GOJO lands pro-forma ROIC in the high teens to low twenties — still a third to a half below the pre-deal level.

**Pro-forma FY2027 leverage and coverage (derived).** Working back from guidance: adjusted EPS midpoint $5.85 × 122.1M shares = $714M, plus ~$14M noncontrolling interest, divided by (1 − 0.23 guided tax rate) = $945M adjusted pretax; add estimated interest expense of ~$230M less ~$8M interest income → adjusted EBIT ≈ $1,167M; add estimated D&A of ~$340M → **FY2027E adjusted EBITDA ≈ $1,500M**. That implies net debt/EBITDA of approximately 3.3x and interest coverage of approximately 6.5x against a 4.0x minimum covenant, down from 9.8x in FY2026. Headroom is adequate but has compressed by a third. The D&A step-up is an assumption; the FY2026 purchase price allocation note was not accessible.

**Near-term refinancing is the concrete catalyst.** The balance sheet shows $1,086M of short-term notes and loans payable against only $1M of current maturities of long-term debt, while the $1,250M delayed-draw term loan matures March 5, 2027 and the $1,000M 364-day revolver expires the same day. Long-term debt rose $1,497M in the quarter the acquisition closed, which is consistent with a term-out having already occurred, but the debt footnote was not retrievable. Either way, roughly $1.1B of short-dated borrowings is exposed to rate and rollover risk within twelve months. S&P lowered Clorox to BBB from BBB+ in February 2026; both agency ratings remain investment grade.

Interest expense rose from $88M in FY2025 to $130M in FY2026, with Q4 alone at $55M. Our FY2027 estimate of ~$230M consumes roughly 4 points of the FY2027 adjusted EBIT margin relative to FY2025's interest burden.

## Growth Analysis

FY2027 guidance of +13% to +14% net sales and +3.5% to +4.5% organic reads well until it is decomposed. GOJO contributes approximately 9.5 points. Management states that "more than 3.5 points" of the organic figure comes from lapping the ERP drawdown. Subtracting both leaves approximately zero to one point of genuine underlying growth. If the lap benefit is exactly 3.5 points and organic lands at the 4.5% high end, underlying growth is about one point; if the lap is larger, underlying is zero or negative. This is a bounded inference from two company-disclosed components, not a company statement, but the arithmetic does not admit a materially better answer.

Three of four segments posted double-digit or high-single-digit organic declines in FY2026. International, the only segment with positive organic growth (+2%) and positive EBIT growth (+3%), is 16% of sales — too small to offset domestic weakness. Household adjusted EBIT fell 41% for the year and 56% in Q4, and Clorox now owns Glad outright without P&G's R&D support.

The gross margin guide is the clearest negative signal. FY2027 is guided to ~42% against FY2026's 42.8% adjusted level, despite four tailwinds that should be present: a full year of GOJO with synergy capture beginning, elimination of the P&G Glad royalty previously recorded in cost of goods sold, volume recovery and fixed-cost absorption from the ERP lap, and the ongoing cost-savings program. For guided gross margin to contract 60-80bp net, inflation and adverse mix must be worth well over 100bp. Management cites "higher-than-normal inflationary headwinds" and "negative mix." One secondary source reported a $200M-plus FY2027 inflation headwind and an 11%-13% FCF-to-sales target; neither appears in the release text and both should be treated as unconfirmed.

The GOJO growth leg is genuine but modest and unproven in Clorox's hands. A 5% three-year revenue CAGR through announcement suggests the post-pandemic hand-hygiene base has normalized and re-established growth, which mitigates the obvious category-normalization concern. The bull leg is retail expansion: Purell holds #1 hand sanitizer share in retail per the company, yet more than 80% of revenue comes through B2B distributors, so the retail channel is under-penetrated relative to brand awareness. Retail gross margins exceed B2B, so successful conversion would partially reverse the negative mix. That path is also the one most exposed to the channel-conflict risk the 10-K newly discloses — distributors may resist Clorox pushing the same brand harder into retail.

Estimate momentum is unambiguously negative and accelerating on the near quarter:

| Period | Current Est. | 30d Ago | 90d Ago | 30d Rev. | 90d Rev. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 FY2027 | $1.11 | $1.27 | $1.27 | **-12.7%** | -12.6% |
| Q2 FY2027 | $1.37 | $1.43 | $1.44 | -4.1% | -5.2% |
| FY2027 | $5.81 | $6.13 | $6.31 | -5.2% | -8.0% |
| FY2028 | $6.36 | $6.71 | $6.87 | -5.1% | -7.4% |

The Q1 cut is the one to watch. Against a prior-year Q1 actual of $0.85, the $1.11 estimate still implies +31% year over year on the ERP lap, but it was cut 12.7% in a month. The four-quarter surprise record is two beats and two misses, including a fractional adjusted miss in Q4 ($1.66 versus $1.67). Visibility is mediocre, and the first quarter under a lame-duck CEO with a newly live ERP and front-loaded inflation carries above-average miss risk.

Growth Prospects: **3.5/10**.

Against a peer median of 19.2x, 18.0x looks like a discount. The peer set is heterogeneous and worth splitting: the low-growth, levered household names trade at 14.6x-19.2x (CENTA 14.6x, REYN 16.3x, ENR 19.2x), while the growth names trade at 23.5x-35.7x (IPAR, WDFC). Clorox as currently constituted — zero-to-one point underlying organic growth, 3.3x net leverage, BBB with a negative recent trajectory, ROIC halved, no buyback capacity — belongs in the first cohort, not the second. On a 16x-19x band applied to $5.85, fair value is $94-111.

The dividend provides the firmer anchor. A ~$5.01 annualized rate at a 5.0% yield implies $100; at the May low of $84.70 the yield was 5.9%. Payout is approximately 86% of guided adjusted EPS and 90% of guided GAAP EPS, but 61%-72% of the externally reported FY2027 FCF target range of $839M-$992M. The FCF measure is the right one, and it says the dividend is safe with roughly $230M-$380M left for debt reduction before any repurchase — a multi-year deleveraging path.

Two facts argue against treating the current multiple as a mispricing. First, the multiple expanded roughly 15% (15.6x to 18.0x) in three weeks while forward EPS was cut 5.2%. Multiple expansion into estimate cuts is the pattern that historically precedes disappointment, not re-rating. Second, the stock trades 2.9% above the consensus mean target with sell-side sentiment degrading and a distribution of 1 buy / 14 holds / 4 sells. The framework that treats analyst targets as stale floors applies to inflection stocks with *rising* estimates and cheap multiples; here estimates are falling and the multiple is mid-cycle, so the targets carry their ordinary informational weight.

There is no identifiable near-term catalyst that reprices Clorox upward from here. The GOJO deal is guided neutral to adjusted EPS in year one, and our independent build reconciles to that: implied GOJO EBITDA of approximately $161M, less roughly $56M of estimated acquired-asset D&A, taxed at 23%, gives approximately $81M NOPAT against approximately $83M of after-tax interest on ~$2.15B at ~5% — net zero. Full synergy delivery adds approximately $36M, or roughly $0.30 per share (+5%), matching "accretive in year two." Post-synergy unlevered after-tax return on the $1.92B net purchase price is approximately 6.2%, at or below cost of capital. The deal is close to value-neutral on management's own published numbers.

Valuation: **4.0/10**. Not stretched, not cheap; the yield floors the downside near $100 and the growth profile caps the upside near the high teens multiple already paid.

## Competitive Landscape

Clorox competes against P&G, Church & Dwight, Reckitt, SC Johnson, Kimberly-Clark and Unilever in retail, and against Ecolab, Diversey/Solenis, Kimberly-Clark Professional and facilities-management distributors in the newly acquired B2B channel. The #1/#2 share claim across 80%+ of sales is credible — bleach, Pine-Sol, Kingsford, Hidden Valley, Brita, Glad, Fresh Step — but strong share in slow-growth or declining categories is a different asset from strong share in growing ones. FY2026 organic declines of 11% in Household and 14% in Lifestyle, with EBIT down 41% and 28%, indicate the legacy portfolio is losing dollars even allowing for the ERP lap.

Customer concentration has not improved: Walmart at 26% (28% in Q3 FY2026 alone), top five approximately half, flat-to-worse over three years. The GOJO acquisition shifts revenue *mix* toward B2B distributors, which is not the same as reducing concentration — the 10-K's new risk language warns of *increased* exposure to concentration among distributors, group purchasing organizations and health systems. Only the mix change is established by the evidence.

Q4 advertising rose to $216M (11.1% of sales) from $171M (8.6%), a $45M step-up against declining sales, cited by management as a driver of the Health and Wellness EBIT decline. The most plausible reading is defensive reinvestment to stabilize share after ERP-period service disruption; an alternative is that FY2025 Q4 advertising was artificially low because the pre-buy inflated the denominator. FY2027 guidance of ~10% consolidated with >11% for retail implies the retail rate is being sustained while B2B dilutes the consolidated ratio.

The genuine moat addition is the ~20 million installed dispenser base and #1 hand sanitizer share, a razor-and-blade structure new to Clorox. It carries the classic vulnerability: a competitor winning a dispenser conversion at a large health system or facilities-management account takes years of refill revenue with it. Post-deal, health and hygiene exceeds half of net sales, and Health and Wellness generated 57% of FY2026 segment adjusted EBIT on 40% of segment sales — so the mix shift is toward the better business. But GOJO's ~20% EBITDA margin is dilutive to a segment running 31% EBIT margin, which is consistent with the guided-down FY2027 gross margin.

Trajectory: legacy deteriorating, new leg unproven in an adjacent channel where Clorox has less experience. Competitive Position: **4.5/10**.

## Risk Assessment

**Refinancing and rating.** Roughly $1.1B of short-dated borrowings and a 364-day revolver mature by March 5, 2027. A downgrade to BBB− would leave Clorox one notch above high yield and materially raise commercial paper costs. Interest coverage has compressed from 9.8x to an estimated 6.5x. This is the most concrete near-term catalyst.

**Q1 FY2027 earnings.** Consensus was cut 12.7% in 30 days to $1.11. Front-loaded inflation, a first full quarter on a new ERP, and the mechanics of the lap create two-sided risk around the late-October/early-November print.

**CEO succession.** Rendle announced her planned resignation in May 2026; Item 5.02 8-Ks were filed May 28 and June 17. No successor has been identified in anything we can verify. She presented Q4 results on August 3 as Chair and CEO. A departure seven weeks after the largest acquisition in company history and four months after a downgrade leaves integration, deleveraging and the B2B pivot without a committed long-term owner. We have no evidence on the reason and would not infer one.

**GOJO integration and channel conflict.** The FY2026 10-K adds an unusually specific cluster of new risks: adapting to a B2B operating model with different channels, margin profiles and customer requirements; increased distributor/GPO/health-system concentration; potential channel conflict with the consumer business; and expanded FDA exposure because GOJO products include cosmetics and OTC drugs. Channel conflict is the risk most directly opposed to the retail-expansion growth thesis.

**Impairment.** Goodwill of $1,945M plus trademarks of $989M is 37.6% of assets against $90M of book equity. Even a modest GOJO-related intangible write-down eliminates book equity. Such a charge would be non-cash and would not affect operating cash flow, an interest-coverage covenant, or the dividend directly, but it would hit reported EPS, book equity and every equity-based screen.

**Walmart.** At 26% of sales, any shelf reset, private-label push or terms change is a first-order earnings event.

**Environmental.** $28M recorded, principally Alameda County, California ($12M, undiscounted exposure to ~$28M over 30 years) and Dickinson County, Michigan ($10M at a 24.3% cost share but jointly and severally liable). Small relative to the enterprise; the joint-and-several exposure is the tail.

**What is already priced.** Short interest at 12.9% of float and 5.8 days to cover means a meaningful bear position exists. Options IV of 29.5% against 30.7% realized is fair, not stressed. The distress narrative has been removed. That partially offsets the risk list, which is why the score is 4.0 rather than lower.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 29.5% against 30.7% trailing realized is fair (-1.2% spread) and sits at only the 23rd percentile of the current chain — the market is not pricing a dislocation. The term structure is flat (29.5% near, 27.8% at six months), so uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated on a near catalyst. Put/call skew is balanced at the front (-1.6%) but steepens to -5.6% at the six-month tenor, consistent with hedging around the March 2027 refinancing window and the CEO transition rather than the next print. ATM IV collapsed from 38.8% to 29.5% between July 22 and August 13 while the stock rose 9.3% and put/call open interest fell from 0.87 to 0.71 — event risk was resolved, and positioning became less defensive. The one notable print is 7,709 contracts of September $100 puts against 998 open interest (7.7x), a fresh position 4.5% below spot expiring after the Q1 report. Taken together: no fundamental disconfirmation of the fundamental view, but a market that has stopped paying for protection at exactly the point where the distress discount has been removed and the growth question remains open. Cheap six-month puts are a reasonable way for holders unwilling to sell to reduce exposure.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $125 (+19%)

The FY2027 guide proves conservative because it was set during a CEO search. The ERP lap contributes more than the 3.5-point floor, the $50M of GOJO synergies land ahead of schedule, and the P&G Glad royalty elimination flows to gross margin more visibly than guidance implies, so FY2027 adjusted EPS lands at or above the $6.00 high end. Purell retail distribution expands without triggering distributor conflict, converting B2B revenue into higher-margin retail dollars and reversing the negative mix. Normalized free cash flow of $880M-$990M reduces net leverage toward 2.5x by FY2028, S&P restores the outlook, and the ~$1.1B short-dated stack is termed out at manageable spreads. A credible external CEO arrives with a cost-and-focus mandate. FY2028 EPS reaches $6.50, and a de-levered, health-and-hygiene-weighted Clorox re-rates to 19x. Add the 4.8% yield and total return approaches 24%.

### Bear Case — $82 (-22%)

The $200M-plus inflation headwind proves real and the value-seeking consumer accelerates trade-down, so gross margin misses the ~42% guide. Underlying organic growth, already near zero, turns negative as private label takes share in bleach, bags and litter, and the ERP lap flatters a deteriorating base for one year before the comparison normalizes in FY2028. A Walmart shelf reset or terms change hits the 26% concentration. Q1 FY2027 misses the already-cut $1.11 estimate and FY2027 guidance is lowered, taking consensus toward $5.30. Channel conflict stalls Purell retail expansion while a competitor wins dispenser conversions at large institutional accounts. The March 2027 refinancing prices wider, or S&P moves to BBB−, forcing a choice between the dividend streak and deleveraging. At $5.30 and 15.5x the stock is $82. If the 49-year dividend streak is broken, the de-rating is worse.

### Base Case — $103 (-1.6%)

FY2027 delivers roughly in line at $5.75-$5.90 adjusted, with the ERP lap doing the work and underlying growth near zero. Gross margin lands at ~42%. Synergy capture begins but the deal remains approximately EPS-neutral in year one. Leverage grinds down from 3.3x toward 3.0x, dividends grow ~1%, and buybacks stay minimal. The stock holds a 17.5x-18x multiple, which on $5.85 is $102-105, and a ~5% yield anchors the same range. Total return is the dividend.

Probability-weighting base 50%, bull 25%, bear 25% gives a price return of approximately -1.4%; adding the 4.79% yield gives an expected total return of approximately +3.4%. That is below cash, for equity risk with 3.3x leverage and an unresolved CEO succession.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** The prior HOLD was issued at $95.76 when the forward multiple was ~15.6x and the dividend yield was 5.2%. Since then the price rose 9.3% while FY2027 consensus fell 5.2% and FY2028 fell 5.1%, taking the multiple to 18.0x and the price 2.9% above the consensus mean target. Nothing in the FY2026 print or the 10-K improved the underlying earnings power; what improved was the market's assessment of tail risk, and that repricing is now complete.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into the current strength, targeting a reduction of roughly one-third to one-half of the position between $104 and $112. Income-focused holders with a low cost basis and a specific need for the 4.79% yield can retain a reduced core position, but should recognize the payout will grow at ~1% nominally until leverage falls below ~2.5x. Holders unwilling to sell should consider the six-month put tenor, where skew at -5.6% and IV of 27.8% price protection through the March 2027 refinancing window at reasonable levels.

**Targets:** base $103 (-1.6%), bull $125 (+19.4%), bear $82 (-21.7%). Timeframe: 12 months, with the decisive checkpoints being the Q1 FY2027 report (late October/early November 2026) and the March 5, 2027 debt maturities.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or ACCUMULATE:**
1. Price at or below $92-95 with FY2027 guidance intact, restoring a 5.3%+ yield and a ~16x forward multiple.
2. Two consecutive quarters in which organic growth excluding the ERP lap is positive, evidencing genuine underlying demand recovery rather than a mechanical comparison.
3. Confirmation that the $1,250M term loan and 364-day revolver have been termed out at spreads consistent with a stable BBB, with net leverage reported at or below 3.0x.
4. A named CEO with a credible external or internal mandate, accompanied by a stated deleveraging target and synergy timetable.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Q1 FY2027 adjusted EPS below the $1.11 consensus accompanied by a cut to the $5.70-$6.00 full-year range.
2. FY2027 gross margin tracking below 41.5% in the first two quarters, indicating the inflation and mix headwinds exceed the guide.
3. S&P or Moody's action to BBB−, or a refinancing completed at spreads implying a further ratings step.
4. Any goodwill or trademark impairment related to GOJO, or disclosure that the $50M synergy target has been reduced or deferred.
5. A dividend increase of less than 1% next July, or an outright pause — which would signal the deleveraging constraint has become binding.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q1 FY2027 report, expected late October or early November 2026, is the next natural decision point. The debt footnote in the FY2026 financial statements (Exhibit 99.1, not yet retrieved) would resolve the term-loan status and should be read as soon as available.

## Conclusion

Clorox is a genuine franchise — 80%+ of sales in #1 or #2 brands, a 49-year dividend record, normalized free cash flow of roughly $880M covering the dividend at 68% — that has spent roughly $2.15B of debt capacity to buy roughly 12% more revenue at a post-synergy unlevered after-tax return of about 6.2%, halving return on invested capital in the process. FY2027's headline +13-14% revenue growth contains approximately zero to one point of genuine underlying growth, and gross margin is guided down despite four identifiable tailwinds. Leverage of 3.3x, ~$1.1B of short-dated debt rolling by March 2027, an S&P downgrade already taken, and a CEO who announced her resignation with no successor identified are the conditions under which the company must execute its largest-ever integration and a channel pivot into B2B institutional hygiene where it competes against Ecolab and Solenis rather than P&G and Church & Dwight.

The market repaired a distress mispricing over the past two weeks, and that was the right trade. It is now finished. At 17.9x guided adjusted earnings, 2.9% above the consensus mean target, with estimates falling 5% in a month and 8% in a quarter, the expected total return of roughly 3% does not compensate for the leverage, the succession vacancy, or the refinancing window. The 4.79% yield floors the stock near $100 and makes this a trim rather than an exit. **REDUCE.**
