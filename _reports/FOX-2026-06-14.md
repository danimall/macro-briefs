**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Fox Corporation is the most narrowly focused of the legacy U.S. media companies, built around live news and sports, the content categories most resistant to cord-cutting. The investment case rests less on organic growth than on capital structure: management holds a $12B buyback authorization (roughly 40%+ of the ~$13B Class B market cap), spent $1.9B in the first nine months of FY2026 (including a $1.5B accelerated repurchase), and has cut weighted basic shares from ~457M to ~424M. With ~$3B of annual free cash flow, investment-grade ratings (Baa2/BBB, both stable), and a controlling holder who benefits as the float shrinks, Fox functions as a slowly-shrinking-share-count compounder. The five-year cumulative TSR of 224 versus a peer 157 reflects this strategy working.

FY2026 is the down leg of the biennial cycle: no Super Bowl, no national elections. Q3 FY2026 revenue fell 9% YoY to $3.99B and advertising fell ~24%, both as expected, though the print still beat consensus and adjusted EPS of $1.32 topped forecasts, prompting target hikes from Wells Fargo, Barclays, JPMorgan, and Evercore. The headline 34% drop in nine-month net income is largely an accounting artifact: a ~$785M non-cash mark-to-market loss on the Flutter equity stake, while underlying Adjusted EBITDA grew in both segments on cost discipline and distribution-rate gains. The September 2025 LGC Family Trusts stockholders agreement, which consolidated Lachlan Murdoch's control and bought out his siblings, plus the June 2026 contract extension through 2030, removes a multi-year governance overhang.

We rate FOX a HOLD. Two specific, falsifiable items keep this below a BUY. First, the Smartmatic defamation suit seeks $2.7B, is unreserved on the balance sheet ("loss neither probable nor reasonably estimable"), and could reach trial in late 2026; given the $800M Dominion settlement precedent, an adverse summary-judgment ruling or verdict is a credible source of a drawdown exceeding 15%, which fails the asymmetry test we require for a long. Second, the trailing P/E of 15.4x is computed on peak-cycle FY2025 earnings; on normalized through-cycle earnings the multiple is higher and roughly in line with peers, so there is no identifiable mispricing to exploit at the current price.

## Company Overview

Fox spun off from 21st Century Fox in March 2019, retaining live news, sports, and broadcast assets after the entertainment library was sold to Disney. It reports two segments:

- **Cable Network Programming (~44% of revenue):** FOX News Media (FOX News, FOX Business) and the sports networks FS1, FS2, Big Ten Network (~61% owned), and FOX Deportes. FOX News has led cable in primetime and total day for ten consecutive years.
- **Television (~56% of revenue):** the FOX broadcast network (NFL, MLB/World Series, college football, the 2026 men's FIFA World Cup, Super Bowl on rotation), Tubi (a leading AVOD service, ~2.2% of all U.S. TV viewing and ~11B hours streamed in FY2025), 29 owned stations covering 14 of the 15 largest DMAs, and FOX Entertainment Studios.
- **Corporate & Other:** Credible (66%-owned consumer-finance marketplace, minority put settled in FY2026), the FOX Studio Lot (Disley leases through March 2026, a known revenue cliff), and FOX One, the wholly owned DTC streaming service launched August 2025 at $19.99/month.

The moat is built on must-have live rights (an 11-year NFL deal running to ~2033, with an NFL one-time termination right after the 2029 season) and the FOX News brand. Fox is deliberately counter-positioned against larger peers: no movie studio, no money-losing general-entertainment SVOD, limited international footprint. The strategy monetizes cord-cutters through ad-supported Tubi and a narrow premium DTC rather than funding a content arms race.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 was a cyclical peak.** Revenue rose 17% to $16.3B, with advertising up 26% (~$870M from sports/Super Bowl LIX, ~$550M from political/Tubi/news). Net income to FOX stockholders rose 51% to $2.26B; Adjusted EBITDA rose 26% to $3.62B. Operating cash flow was $3.32B and free cash flow $2.99B (~18% FCF margin). Segment EBITDA: Cable $3.03B (+13%), Television $945M (+87% on Super Bowl/political).

**Q3 FY2026 reflects the down-cycle comparison without operational deterioration.** Revenue fell entirely on the absence of Super Bowl LIX and election advertising, yet beat the ~$3.80B consensus. Nine-month FY2026 revenue was $12.9B (–1%) and net income $1.03B versus $1.57B, but the GAAP decline is overwhelmingly a non-operating ~$785M mark-to-market loss on the Flutter stake (versus a $156M gain a year earlier). Underlying nine-month Adjusted EBITDA rose to $2.71B, with both segments growing segment EBITDA (Cable +4%, Television +15%) despite the cyclical absence of marquee events. Reading the headline net income line in isolation would misstate the operating trajectory.

**The balance sheet and capital allocation are the core of the story.** Cash stood at $3.6B (down from $5.4B as buybacks were funded), long-term debt was steady at ~$6.6B, and the current ratio was 2.9x with debt/equity ~1.9x. The buyback authorization was lifted to $12B in August 2025, with ~$3.5B remaining at 3/31/26. The semi-annual dividend was raised to $0.28 (~$250M/year, a 0.9% yield). The Altman Z of 1.97 ("grey zone") understates resilience: it is depressed by the asset-light, high-payables media model and optically thin operating margins, not by genuine distress; investment-grade ratings and ~$3B FCF tell the accurate story.

Fox also holds a ~2.4% direct Flutter stake plus a 10-year call option (through December 2030) to buy 18.6% of FanDuel at a ~$4.5B strike, embedded optionality on U.S. sports betting that would require major capital deployment to monetize. The direct equity stake was largely sold in FY2026 (equity securities fell from $1.25B to $458M), which is the source of the non-operating losses.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is modest and cyclical. The recurring revenue base grows in the low-to-mid single digits through affiliate-fee rate increases and Tubi/digital advertising, with biennial spikes from elections and Super Bowls. The structural headwind is MVPD subscriber attrition: FOX News network subscribers fell from 67M to 61M, FS1 from 67M to 61M, and Big Ten from 45M to 42M. Affiliate fees still grew ~5% in FY2025 only because ~$790M of rate/fee gains outran ~$460M of lost subscriber revenue, a dynamic with a finite runway.

The offsets are real but limited in scale. Tubi continues to scale free ad-supported viewing. FOX One launched in August 2025, and management reported that sports subscribers who joined for football stayed for FOX News content after the season, an early retention signal that pure-sports DTC services lack. The 2026 FIFA World Cup, carried on both FOX One and Tubi, is a ready-made subscriber-acquisition funnel arriving this summer. Cost discipline (Venu Sports discontinued, WWE rights concluded, INDYCAR/LIV Golf added at accretive returns) supports margins.

The earnings-surprise record is strong: four consecutive beats (33.9%, 61.7%, 37.4%, 25.8%), which raises confidence in near-term consensus. The dominant near-term growth driver is the buyback, not the top line: share count shrinking ~7% annually is the mechanical lever. We score Growth Prospects 5.5, reflecting solid underlying EBITDA growth and FOX One/World Cup optionality against a structurally declining linear subscriber base and a cyclical down-year in the reported numbers.

## Valuation Assessment

At $58.92, FOX trades at 15.4x trailing earnings, a modest discount to the peer median of 17.9x (FOXA 17.9x, TGNA 16.3x, NXST 36.6x, WBD negative). On free cash flow, the ~$3B FCF against the ~$27–28B total enterprise (A+B) implies a ~10–11% FCF yield, which provides genuine downside support and funds the buyback.

The key valuation nuance: the trailing multiple sits on FY2025 peak-cycle earnings (elections plus Super Bowl). FY2026 is the trough, and full-year earnings will be materially lower than FY2025's $2.26B net income. On normalized through-cycle earnings power, the effective multiple is closer to 16–18x, roughly in line with peers rather than a discount. This is a fairly valued stock, not a cheap one, and the controlled-company structure (non-voting Class A, ~44% Murdoch voting cap on Class B, corporate-opportunity waivers toward News Corp) imposes a structural discount that is unlikely to close.

We treat analyst targets with care here: the brief confirms target hikes after the Q3 beat but no specific figures were extractable, and the structured feed carries no target range (target mean/median/high/low all unavailable). Absent a specific catalyst-driven mispricing, and with the multiple fair on normalized earnings, we score Valuation 6.0. The capital-return yield supports the floor; it does not create asymmetric upside at this price.

## Competitive Landscape

Fox competes against far larger and more diversified players (Disney/ESPN, Comcast/NBCU, Paramount, Warner Bros. Discovery) and tech-scale streamers (Netflix, Amazon, Apple, YouTube) increasingly bidding for live sports. Fox's edge is focus: it owns the highest-engagement live verticals, monetizes cord-cutters via Tubi without cannibalizing the bundle, and runs a leaner cost base than peers chasing general-entertainment SVOD.

The competitive trajectory is mildly negative rather than deteriorating. The FOX News franchise is durable (ten consecutive years atop cable), and NFL rights through ~2033 anchor affiliate-fee leverage. But linear subscribers are declining, sports-rights inflation is structural (FY2025 operating expense rose ~$1B largely on NFL/Super Bowl/college football amortization), and the NFL's one-time termination right after the 2029 season is a genuine threat to the largest asset. Potential FCC deregulation of the national ownership cap could let Fox expand its already top-heavy station portfolio, a possible offset. We score Competitive Position 6.0 — strong assets undergoing a well-managed but secularly declining linear transition.

## Risk Assessment

**Smartmatic ($2.7B, unreserved) is the dominant idiosyncratic risk.** Following the $800M Dominion settlement in April 2023, the Smartmatic defamation suit remains outstanding; summary judgment was argued December 2, 2025, with trial not expected before late 2026. Fox has taken no reserve because the loss is "neither probable nor reasonably estimable," meaning an adverse outcome would be a direct, unprovisioned hit to equity. Given the Dominion precedent, the market will react sharply to the summary-judgment ruling. This binary is the principal reason bear-case downside can exceed 15%.

Secondary risks: secular MVPD decline (subscriber losses outrunning rate gains is a question of when, not if); the NFL post-2029 termination right; advertising cyclicality (the biennial swing is structural and already visible); FCC license renewals beginning June 2028 and foreign-ownership limits tied to the Murdoch structure; and a thin FCC-license impairment cushion (a 0.5pt discount-rate rise would push fair value ~$80M below carrying value, after a $70M impairment in FY2025). The FOX Studio Lot loses Disney as predominant tenant in March 2026, a modest Corporate & Other headwind.

The de-risking offset is the resolved Murdoch succession. The LGC Family Trusts stockholders agreement removed years of overhang about a post-Rupert control fight, and the contract extensions for Lachlan Murdoch and CFO Steve Tomsic settle leadership through the decade. We score Risk Factors 4.5, reflecting the material unreserved legal binary.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 42.0% is rich versus 28.1% one-year historical volatility (a 13.9pp spread), the term structure is in backwardation (42.0% near-term versus 35.6% at six months), and put/call skew is elevated at 5.2% with an implied move of ±4.8% into the nearest expiry. This pattern of front-end IV elevated, skew tilted to puts, and longer-dated calmer is consistent with hedging around a discrete near-term event (the World Cup launch window and/or Smartmatic developments) rather than a durable repricing of the franchise. The low put/call volume ratio (0.22) tempers the bearish read. We treat this as a reason for entry timing caution, not a thesis change.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$72, +22%):** FOX One scales through the 2026 World Cup with the news-driven retention surprise sticking, Tubi continues to grow ad revenue, affiliate-rate gains keep outrunning subscriber losses, and the buyback compounds per-share earnings as the float shrinks ~7% annually. Smartmatic is dismissed at summary judgment or settled well below the $2.7B claim, removing the overhang. On normalized earnings re-rating toward the peer ~18x with buyback-driven EPS growth, the stock reaches the low-$70s.

**Bear Case (~$48, –19%):** An adverse Smartmatic ruling or large settlement forces an unreserved charge and re-rates the stock on litigation risk. MVPD subscriber declines accelerate past the point where rate increases can offset them, turning affiliate revenue negative, and sports-rights inflation compresses Television-segment margins. The cyclical down-year drags reported earnings and the controlled-company discount widens. The stock retests the FY2025/early-2026 lows near $48–51.

**Base Case (~$62, +5%):** Buyback-driven EPS growth offsets the cyclical revenue dip, FOX One adds modest subscriber momentum into the World Cup, and Smartmatic remains unresolved (an overhang, not yet a realized loss). The stock grinds modestly higher on capital return, capped by full-ish normalized valuation and the unresolved legal binary.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next at FY2026 Q4/full-year results (~August 2026) and on the Smartmatic summary-judgment ruling.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Smartmatic resolved favorably — summary-judgment dismissal or a settlement materially below the $2.7B claim — removing the unreserved binary and restoring a bear case within the 15% threshold.
2. FOX One disclosing material subscriber scale with retention confirmed after the World Cup, validating DTC as a durable growth leg.
3. A pullback compressing the stock below ~13x normalized earnings, creating genuine valuation asymmetry on the FCF yield.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. An adverse Smartmatic summary-judgment ruling or a large, dated settlement.
2. Affiliate-fee revenue turning negative as subscriber declines outrun rate gains in a reported quarter.
3. Any concrete signal that the NFL will exercise its post-2029 termination right, or a sports-rights renewal at materially dilutive economics.

## Conclusion

Fox is a well-run, cash-generative live-content franchise executing a disciplined, float-shrinking capital-return strategy, with the long-standing succession overhang now legally resolved. The underlying operations grew through the FY2026 cyclical trough, the balance sheet is investment-grade with a ~10–11% FCF yield, and FOX One plus the 2026 World Cup add real optionality. We are not confusing the year-over-year price recovery or the GAAP net-income optics (a non-cash Flutter mark) with the operating reality, which is sound.

The recommendation is HOLD, with a deliberate, concrete reason it is not a BUY. The $2.7B Smartmatic suit is unreserved with a possible late-2026 trial; against the $800M Dominion settlement precedent, an adverse outcome is a credible path to a drawdown beyond 15%, which fails the asymmetry bar a long position requires. Compounding this, the 15.4x trailing multiple rests on peak-cycle FY2025 earnings, so on normalized power the stock is fairly valued in line with peers rather than mispriced. Hold for buyback-driven compounding and FOX One/World Cup upside, and reassess on the Smartmatic ruling and the August full-year print.
