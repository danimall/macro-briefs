## Executive Summary

Remitly has completed a profitability inflection. GAAP operating income went from −$114.2M in FY2023 to +$77.5M in FY2025 to +$120.4M in the first half of 2026 alone; adjusted EBITDA margin moved from 5.3% (FY2023) to 16.6% (FY2025) to 22.8% (H1 2026). The FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guide has been raised twice, from $300-320M at the December 2025 investor day to $410-415M on August 5, 2026. The balance sheet carries no net debt, $676.4M of cash, an undrawn $550M revolver maturing 2030, and a buyback that absorbed roughly 76% of gross share issuance in H1 2026. At $23.21 the enterprise trades at 10.3x the FY2026E adjusted EBITDA midpoint and 2.14x FY2026E revenue — undemanding for a business guiding 21-22% revenue growth.

The composition of that improvement is where the case weakens. From the December guide to the August guide, the revenue outlook rose roughly 2-3% while the adjusted EBITDA outlook rose 33%. Q2 2026 technology and development spending fell 4% in absolute dollars and G&A fell 6%, against 20% revenue growth; stock-based compensation dropped from 9.6% to 6.5% of revenue; H1 restructuring charges of $15.8M included $13.7M of severance tied to Israel and Ireland facility exits. Absolute expense declines against 20% revenue growth are not a repeatable input. Management's own guidance concedes this: Q3 guidance of $92-94M adjusted EBITDA on $505-507M revenue implies an 18.2-18.6% margin, 460-500bp below Q2's 23.2%, and the implied Q4 margin of roughly 19.3% sits about 70bp *below* Q4 2025.

Underneath, unit economics are drifting. Revenue per quarterly active customer was $48.55 in Q2 2026 versus $48.39 a year earlier — essentially flat. Send volume per customer rose 6.2% but was fully offset by take-rate compression from 2.229% to 2.106%. All 20% of revenue growth is customer acquisition, purchased with advertising up 30% to $83.6M and sales incentives up 34% against 20% revenue growth. The multi-product monetization story (Wallet, Card, Flex, Remitly One) is not yet visible in ARPU. We rate the shares ACCUMULATE with a $28 base case (+20.6%), $35 bull (+50.8%) and $17 bear (−26.8%), and would reserve the majority of intended capital for the $20-21 area rather than commit fully at $23.21 into a guided margin step-down.

## Company Overview

Remitly operates a digital-first cross-border money movement platform spanning more than 175 countries and, as of June 30, 2026, more than 5,700 send/receive corridors (up from 5,300 at year-end 2025). Revenue is recognized gross — the company is principal and merchant of record — and comprises transaction fees plus the foreign-exchange spread between the customer rate and Remitly's currency purchase cost. There is one operating segment, with net income as the reported segment profitability measure.

The architecture matters for the economics. On the pay-in side, Remitly accepts bank accounts, Apple Pay/Google Pay and cards; it explicitly states it "typically do[es] not have sending agents who accept cash," and therefore avoids agent commissions. On the pay-out side it reaches 5.8 billion bank accounts and mobile wallets plus approximately 490,000 cash pick-up locations, with the cash footprint flat since December 2025 while digital endpoints grew from 5.4B, consistent with the stated digital mix shift.

The defensible assets are the licensing stack (money transmitter licenses in the 49 states requiring them, plus D.C. and territories, FinCEN MSB registration, UK FCA and Central Bank of Ireland payment institution licenses, FINTRAC), direct partner integrations across 5,700 corridors, brand trust (4.9 iOS rating across ~4.5M reviewers), and proprietary fraud data. It is not intellectual property: the FY2025 10-K discloses six U.S. registered trademarks, one pending application, 135 foreign marks, and no patent count. Headcount was over 3,200 FTEs at 12/31/25; the 2026 restructuring's headcount effect is not quantified.

FY2025 send volume of $74.9B represents roughly 3.7% of the ~$2 trillion consumer-to-consumer cross-border pool cited in the 10-K. The larger $22 trillion figure in the same disclosure includes B2B categories where Remitly barely competes and should not be treated as addressable today.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and the deceleration curve.** Revenue moved $653.6M (FY2022) → $944.3M (+44.5%) → $1,264.0M (+33.9%) → $1,635.1M (+29.4%), then $452.8M in Q1 2026 (+25.2%) and $495.2M in Q2 2026 (+20.2%). The FY2026 guide of $1.978-1.988B implies +21-22%, and backing out nine-month revenue of ~$1,454M leaves roughly $529M for Q4 2026, about +19.6% against Q4 2025's $442.2M. Deceleration is orderly and from a large base, but it is continuous.

**Take rate: the central operating metric.** Revenue divided by send volume fell from 2.393% (FY2023) to 2.314% (FY2024) to 2.183% (FY2025), and from 2.229% in Q2 2025 to 2.106% in Q2 2026 — roughly 12-13bp of annual compression. Send volume has grown persistently faster than revenue (+32% versus +23% in H1 2026).

The important offset is that contribution economics held. Transaction margin dollars of $334.0M on $23.51B of Q2 send volume equal a 1.420% contribution take rate, versus 1.451% in Q2 2025 — a 3.1bp decline against 12.3bp at the revenue line. Transaction expenses fell from 35% to 33% of revenue, and reported transaction margin improved from 65% to 67%. Roughly three-quarters of gross yield compression was absorbed by transaction-cost leverage. The benign interpretation is mix shift toward larger senders, India-type corridors and digital disbursement, all of which carry proportionally lower unit cost. The alternative is that part of the yield decline is competitive price concession; the filings cannot exclude it, and incentive spending is consistent with it: incentives recorded as a revenue reduction rose to $14.2M from $11.3M, and total incentives to $23.7M from $17.6M, growth of 34% against 20% revenue growth.

**Margin inflection and its source.** Q2 2026 operating income of $66.7M was up 355% on 20% revenue growth. The expense lines show where it came from: technology and development at 15% of revenue versus 19%, with absolute dollars down 4% on a $7.4M personnel decline partly offset by $5.3M of added cloud cost; G&A at 11% versus 14%, dollars down 6% including a $3.9M decrease in provisions for processing-partner collectibility; marketing flat at 21% of revenue but with advertising up 30%; SBC down to $34.5M from $38.1M. Management attributes leverage partly to AI-driven efficiency. The arithmetic is dominated by headcount and equity-cost reduction executed through a Q1 2026 restructuring. Post-2022 over-hiring normalization is an equally consistent reading; both can be true, and neither is repeatable at this rate.

**Cash flow requires rebasing.** Beginning Q4 2025 Remitly reclassified disbursement prefunding, customer funds receivable, customer liabilities and trade settlement liability from operating to financing activities, conforming prior periods. Reported operating cash flow was $325.1M in FY2025 and $216.5M in H1 2026 (versus $135.5M in H1 2025), with company-defined free cash flow of $202.8M in H1 2026. On the old presentation, FY2024 operating cash flow would have exceeded FY2025's — the swings were driven by whether period-end fell before a long weekend (prefunding was $441.3M at 12/31/25 versus $234.6M at 6/30/26). The new treatment is defensible because customer float is pass-through, but any FCF-yield comparison spanning Q4 2025 is invalid without rebasing.

More consequentially, **H1 2026 free cash flow must not be annualized.** H1 cash taxes paid were only $5.5M against $116.7M of pretax income, aided by NOLs and by OBBBA's immediate expensing of domestic R&D. A normalized sensitivity: FY2026E adjusted EBITDA of $412.5M less ~$9M cash interest, ~$28M capex, ~$8M payroll tax on SBC and ~$50M of eventual normalized cash taxes gives roughly $318M (~13.3x EV). Burdening for the ~$130M forward annual SBC run-rate implied by $308.5M unamortized over 2.3 years gives roughly $188M (~22.5x EV). The honest answer is that range, and the SBC-burdened end is the relevant one for a company funding 6.5% of revenue in employee equity.

**Balance sheet.** Cash $676.4M (from $542.4M), long-term debt reduced to zero from $155.0M, short-term debt $3.0M, total equity $1,126.8M, accumulated deficit narrowed to −$205.4M from −$460.3M (largely the tax-asset recognition). The $550M JPMorgan-agented revolver, maturing June 2030 at Term SOFR + 1.50%, had no borrowings outstanding, $474.7M of unused capacity and $75.3M of standby letters of credit; the sole financial covenant is net leverage ≤4.50x, with compliance confirmed.

The zero period-end balance understates dependence. In H1 2026 Remitly borrowed $5.817B and repaid $5.972B under the facility at an average term of roughly four days — it is the operating engine for daily and weekend prefunding of disbursement partners, not financial leverage. Withdrawal or material tightening would be a liquidity event that a debt/EBITDA screen reads as zero risk. Certain jurisdictions also require holding eligible liquid assets equal to undisbursed customer balances, so headline cash overstates deployable liquidity.

**A note on the reported P/E.** The screen shows both trailing and forward P/E at 17.49 on EPS of $0.31; $23.21/$0.31 is 74.9x, so the field reconciles to roughly $1.33 of EPS — matching the stale 90-day-ago current-year estimate of $1.37. It is a forward estimate on a superseded number and should not be used. GAAP earnings are unusable for 2026 in any case: H1 net income of $255.0M includes a $140.6M discrete non-cash tax benefit from the U.S. valuation-allowance release.

**Earnings surprise history, corrected for basis.** Four consecutive beats are reported, including a stated 601% Q2 surprise ($0.93 actual versus $0.13 estimate). That comparison mixes bases: $0.93 GAAP diluted EPS embeds a large per-share portion of the discrete tax benefit. Excluding it, Q2 EPS was materially lower — still a substantial beat, but the headline magnitude is an accounting artifact rather than an operating result. The same correction applies to the +42.5% current-year estimate revision to $1.95: it is dominated by the tax item, which is why next-year consensus of $1.71 sits *below* current-year. Cleaner operating revisions are modestly positive (current quarter +7.0%, next year +3.9%) and one is negative (next quarter −5.9%). This does **not** qualify as the >30% inflection revision signal; it is an accounting reclassification of a single quarter's tax line.

## Growth Analysis

Active customers grew from 5,911K (Q4 2023) to 7,780K (Q4 2024) to 9,279K (Q4 2025) to 10,199K in Q2 2026, up 20% year over year. Revenue per quarterly active customer was $48.55 versus $48.39 — up 0.3%. Send volume per customer rose 6.2% to $2,306, entirely offset by take-rate decline.

That decomposition is the crux of the growth question. All revenue growth is coming from adding customers rather than monetizing existing ones, and the customer additions are being purchased: advertising intensity rose from 15.7% to 16.9% of revenue, incentives grew 34%. Remitly Flex, Wallet, Card and Remitly One are the stated path to ARPU inflection, and the Remitly Global Card launched July 30, 2026 — too recent to appear in any results. The counterargument deserves weight: MD&A states acquisition spend depresses current-period EBITDA and benefits later periods, and flat ARPU during aggressive customer intake plus a product launch is what an investment phase looks like. It is not proof the monetization thesis fails, but it is also not evidence that it works.

The December 2025 investor day set 2028 targets of $2.6-3.0B revenue and $575-600M adjusted EBITDA at a 20-22% margin, framed against a Rule of 40 objective. Those targets now look stale in one dimension and demanding in another. If FY2026 lands at $412.5M, reaching $587.5M requires only a ~19% two-year EBITDA CAGR, and the 20-22% margin band is already exceeded in H1 2026 (22.8%). But $2.6-3.0B of 2028 revenue from $1.983B requires 15-23% annual growth for two more years, which on flat ARPU means sustaining ~20% customer additions at rising acquisition cost.

Geography adds nuance. Q2 2026 U.S. revenue was $336.8M, or 68.0% of total (up from 66.1%), and grew **23.6%** — faster than the 20.2% consolidated rate and faster than rest-of-world's 17.9%. The immigration-crackdown narrative that produced a 23% single-day decline in November 2025 has not appeared in U.S. send-side revenue through June 2026. The actual soft spot is Canada: +3.4% ($43.3M versus $41.9M), falling from ~10.2% to ~8.7% of revenue, with no cause disclosed.

## Valuation Assessment

At $23.21 with 211.68M shares, equity value is $4.913B. Less $673.4M of net cash, EV is $4.240B. A more conservative EV treating $242.1M of customer liabilities plus trade settlement liability as encumbered gives ~$4.48B.

| Metric | Multiple |
|---|---|
| EV / FY2026E revenue ($1,983M mid) | 2.14x (2.26x conservative EV) |
| EV / FY2026E adj. EBITDA ($412.5M mid) | 10.3x (10.9x conservative) |
| EV / FY2025 adj. EBITDA ($272.2M) | 15.6x |
| P / next-year consensus EPS ($1.71) | 13.6x |
| P / normalized SBC-inclusive earnings (~$0.79) | ~29x |
| EV / SBC-burdened normalized FCF (~$188M) | ~22.5x |

Peer medians in transaction and payment processing sit at 18.4x P/E, but the set is heterogeneous (Shift4 at 66x, Euronet at 11.5x), which limits the comparison's force. The more useful anchors are absolute: 10.3x EV/EBITDA for a business growing revenue 21-22% with net cash and a 50%+ EBITDA increase is not a demanding multiple. Adjusting the current-year consensus for the tax benefit gives a clean 2026 operating EPS below next-year's $1.71, consistent with roughly 30% forward EPS growth on 13.6x forward. That is a supportive element of the valuation case, tempered by a three-analyst estimate base and one negative near-quarter revision.

Against that, the SBC-burdened cash-earnings multiple of ~22.5x and normalized P/E near 29x are fair rather than cheap, and SBC is real economic cost funding employee compensation. The valuation therefore offers moderate, not compelling, asymmetry. We do not treat cheapness on any single metric as a buy signal here; the multiple has to be paired with the disconfirming evidence on margin durability.

Sell-side positioning is uniformly constructive: 10 covering analysts, 2 Strong Buy and 8 Buy, zero Hold or Sell, with a $29-$35 target range and $31.89 mean. Post-Q2 raises included Monness Crespi to $36, KeyBanc to $33, Citizens to $32 and Cantor to $30. A 25-51% gap to that range is corroboration, but a slate with no Holds provides no marginal upgrade fuel and is itself a crowded-positive condition.

**Base case $28.** Applying roughly 10.8x to FY2027E adjusted EBITDA of ~$480M (17-18% revenue growth to ~$2.33B at a ~20.5% margin) gives ~$5.18B of EV; adding net cash growing to ~$800M (H2 2026 plus 2027 free cash flow net of buybacks) and dividing by ~213M shares gives ~$28. This requires no multiple expansion; the base case is multiple-neutral and rests on delivery. **Bull $35** applies 13x to $520M of FY2027E EBITDA, requiring margins to hold above 22% and ARPU to inflect from Card and Wallet; it aligns with the sell-side high. **Bear $17** assumes take-rate compression accelerates while acquisition cost rises, FY2027 EBITDA is flat near $400M, and the multiple compresses to 7.5-8x. The stock traded at $12.08 in November 2025 on a materially lower EBITDA base, so the bear is not hypothetical.

Probability-weighted at 50/25/25 (base/bull/bear), expected return is approximately +16%.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K names five competitor categories: agent-network incumbents, banks, digital-first cross-border providers, emerging wallets/neobanks/stablecoin providers, and informal channels. Two structural asymmetries are grounded in the filings rather than management framing.

First, the OBBBA remittance excise tax enacted July 4, 2025 imposes 1% on outbound **non-digital** remittances from the U.S. Remitly states it does not expect a material impact, which follows from its own disclosure that it typically has no cash-accepting sending agents. Agent-based incumbents carry the tax; Remitly does not. The residual risk is legislative extension to digital transfers.

Second, stablecoin posture is opportunistic rather than defensive. The 10-K describes stablecoins as "an emerging payment rail that may complement the traditional infrastructure," potentially letting customers receive, hold or spend in stablecoins. The unanswered economic question is whether cheaper settlement rails accrue to Remitly as margin or get competed away as lower consumer prices. The take-rate data is the only observable evidence: 12bp of annual compression at the revenue line against only 3bp at the contribution line suggests savings are currently being substantially retained.

Scale progression supports the share-gain framing: corridors 5,300 → 5,700, digital payout endpoints 5.4B → 5.8B, iOS reviewers ~4.0M → ~4.5M, cash pick-up locations flat at ~490,000, all between December 2025 and June 2026. Secondary commentary describes Western Union trading near 4.6-5x 2026 adjusted EPS with an ~11% yield on an agent-heavy model; we have not verified that against WU filings, and note that a five-times-earnings incumbent is also a potential source of irrational price competition. Competitive trajectory is improving on share and endpoint reach; the yield trend is the counterweight, which is why we hold this dimension at 6.4 rather than higher.

## Risk Assessment

**Guided near-term margin compression (most decision-relevant).** Q3 guidance implies an 18.2-18.6% adjusted EBITDA margin, 460-500bp below Q2's 23.2%; the residual implies a Q4 margin near 19.3% against Q4 2025's 20.0%. Whether this is conservatism after two large beats or planned reinvestment into Global Card launch marketing and seasonally heavy Q4 acquisition cannot be adjudicated without transcript evidence. Either way, a market extrapolating H1's 22.8% will be recalibrating in November.

**Take-rate erosion as a structural feature.** ~12-13bp annually. Transaction expenses are already down to 32-33% of revenue; if that leverage plateaus while mix shift continues, revenue growth will increasingly lag volume growth.

**U.S. send-side concentration intersecting immigration policy.** The U.S. is 68.0% of revenue and rising. The 10-K states that "recent U.S. legal and policy changes have changed the legal status of certain population groups and increased deportation activity more broadly," and the risk factors cite H-1B cost increases. Through June 2026 U.S. revenue grew 23.6%, so no impairment is visible — the U.S. is Remitly's fastest-growing send geography, not its weakest. But revenue can hold while the underlying pool erodes, particularly since the filings note that a strengthening send currency "may accelerate the timing or amount" of transactions — a pull-forward channel.

**Counterparty credit.** FY2025 G&A included a $21.5M increase in provisions related to collectibility from certain receivables and processing partners, roughly 28% of FY2025 GAAP net income. Q2 2026 showed a $3.9M year-over-year decrease in the same line, indicating partial normalization. The counterparty, resolution status and recovery expectations are undisclosed.

**Revolver dependence.** $5.8B borrowed and $6.0B repaid in H1 2026 at ~4-day average term, against a $550M commitment. Operationally essential. A June 15, 2026 Amendment No. 1 was executed whose terms we have not reviewed and whose direction is unknown.

**Governance and personnel.** Founder CEO Matthew Oppenheimer stepped down in February 2026, succeeded by Sebastian Gunningham from Santander — six months of tenure at the current print. Three additional Item 5.02 8-Ks (March 20, April 10, June 8, 2026) plus an April 2 transition agreement with the former Chief Legal Officer indicate C-suite turnover whose scope we could not determine; a broad rebuild versus isolated changes materially alters execution risk. Cumulative TSR since IPO stood at $28 indexed to $100 against a peer group at $121 — roughly 72% cumulative loss versus a 21% peer gain.

**Smaller items.** Remitly Flex 91+ days past due nearly doubled to $1.068M from $0.552M, taking the delinquent share from 1.9% to 4.0% of a $26.7M book — trivial in absolute terms, relevant only if Flex scales. Transaction losses are stable at 0.11% of send volume, and H1 2026 provisions of $45.1M were slightly below H1 2025 despite 32% volume growth. CFPB supervisory authority applies as a "larger participant," offset by the 10-K's observation that fee transparency "is not currently an area of focus," shifting risk toward a 50-state patchwork. Reliance on a single cloud provider with West Coast concentration. No dividends; classified board with supermajority and exclusive-forum provisions.

**Dilution.** Reported dilutive overhang is 10,210K shares, 4.8% above basic. Maximum contingent issuance from unvested RSUs/PSUs plus outstanding options is 26,094K shares, 12.3% of current shares, which if fully issued would leave existing holders at 89.0% — 11.0% post-issuance ownership dilution. The 1.5M market-condition PSUs had not vested at 6/30/26 and belong outside current diluted capitalization. Share count discipline improved sharply: +5.0% in FY2025 versus +0.5% in H1 2026, with $65.6M of repurchases (3.9M shares) offsetting roughly 76% of gross issuance and $110.5M remaining on the authorization. Disclosed monthly average repurchase prices were $16.45 (April), $21.73 (May), $20.04 (June).

**Insider activity.** All reviewed transactions from April through July 2026 were dispositions. Co-founder and director Joshua Hug reduced direct holdings from ~3,475,820 to 2,680,889 — roughly 795,000 shares and over $19M of proceeds, a ~23% cut, with the largest tranches at $24.93 and $25.18 in mid-July. CFO Vikas Mehta sold 25,000 at $21.07, Chief Product & Technology Officer Ankur Sinha sold 50,000 at $23.42 and 7,596 at $21.07, Chief Business Officer Pankaj Sharma sold 16,000 at $20.01 and 15,000 at $25.03, and directors Bora Chung (12,000 at $21.42) and Nigel Morris (3,572 at $24.03) smaller amounts. Three C-suite officers sold within roughly a 30-day window, which is the pattern that carries signal — though each sold small single-digit percentages of holdings during a period of record results, and the 10-Q's Item 5 disclosure that no officer or director adopted, modified or terminated a trading arrangement in Q2 means pre-existing 10b5-1 plans governed. CEO Gunningham made no discretionary sales, disposing only 22,851 shares to tax withholding while holding 764,649. The absence of any open-market purchase across the reviewed directors and officers, into a rally off the November low, is a mild negative we reflect in the management score.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 46.7% sits 9.1 points below 55.8% one-year realized volatility — options are cheap relative to how the stock has actually traded, which matters for a name that fell 23% in one session on November 6, 2025 and rose 29% in one session on February 19, 2026. Positioning is heavily call-tilted: put/call volume ratio 0.13, open-interest ratio 0.19, with skew balanced at −0.9%. Short interest is 7.6% of float at 3.8 days to cover — not crowded either way. The term structure is flat (46.7% near versus 47.4% at six months), with the December tenor pricing a ±24.0% move, which brackets the November Q3 print. The options market is not buying downside protection ahead of a quarter management has already guided 460-500bp lower on margin, and protection is available cheaply relative to realized volatility, which supports our staging recommendation.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($35)

Remitly is taking share from an agent-based incumbent complex whose economics are structurally impaired, with the OBBBA cash-remittance tax adding a legislated cost to competitors that Remitly does not bear. Revenue compounds 17-20% on 20% customer growth across 5,700 corridors, and the Global Card, Wallet and Remitly One convert flat $48.55 ARPU into ARPU growth over 2027-2028, adding a second growth vector on top of customer additions. Transaction-cost leverage continues absorbing take-rate compression at the contribution line, keeping adjusted EBITDA margin above 22%. FY2027 adjusted EBITDA reaches ~$520M against ~$2.35B of revenue; the multiple re-rates toward 13x EV/EBITDA as the market accepts durability of the margin structure, and net cash builds past $850M with buybacks continuing to absorb issuance. The three-analyst consensus for $1.71 next-year EPS proves conservative, and the December 2025 2028 targets are raised. Target $35, matching the high end of the sell-side range.

### Bear Case ($17)

Take-rate compression is competitive concession rather than mix, and the 34% growth in incentives against 20% revenue growth is the tell. Transaction expenses cannot fall below the 32-33% of revenue already achieved, so contribution take rate begins declining at closer to the 12bp gross rate. ARPU stays flat, forcing acquisition spend higher — advertising already grew 30% and ad intensity rose to 16.9% of revenue — while the cost-out lever is exhausted, since technology and G&A dollars cannot decline again off a lower base. The guided Q3/Q4 margin step-down proves structural, not conservative, and FY2027 adjusted EBITDA stalls near $400-410M on decelerating mid-teens revenue growth. Cash taxes normalize, cutting free cash flow toward the ~$318M unburdened / ~$188M SBC-burdened range and removing the FCF support story. Weakening U.S. employment and continued deportation activity finally reach the 68% U.S. revenue base with a lag. A three-analyst consensus with no Holds capitulates, the multiple compresses to 7.5-8x, and the stock returns toward $17 — still above the $12.08 November 2025 low but a 27% loss from here.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: ACCUMULATE. Staged entry.**

- **Base case target: $28** (+20.6% from $23.21). Math: ~10.8x FY2027E adjusted EBITDA of ~$480M (revenue ~$2.33B at ~20.5% margin) = ~$5.18B EV; plus ~$800M net cash = ~$5.98B equity; ÷ ~213M shares = ~$28. Requires no multiple expansion from the current 10.3x.
- **Bull case target: $35** (+50.8%). 13x FY2027E EBITDA of ~$520M with margins held above 22% and evidence of ARPU inflection.
- **Bear case target: $17** (−26.8%). 7.5-8x flat FY2027 EBITDA of ~$400M on decelerating growth and exhausted cost levers.
- **Probability-weighted expected return: approximately +16%** at 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear.
- **Timeframe: 12-18 months**, anchored to the Q3 2026 print in early November 2026 (which tests the guided 18.2-18.6% margin), the Q4/FY2026 print in February 2027 (which tests the implied ~19.6% Q4 margin and sets FY2027 guidance), and any revision to the 2028 medium-term targets.

**Staged entry plan.** Initiate at roughly one-third of intended position at current levels, given the undemanding 10.3x EV/EBITDA and net-cash balance sheet. Reserve the balance for $20.00-21.00 — the level at which the company itself repurchased in May and June 2026 ($21.73 and $20.04 average) and where the stock traded in late May and early June. Complete the position below $19.50. Do not chase toward the $27.15 high set on August 6.

**Upgrade to BUY if:** (1) the Q3 2026 print delivers an adjusted EBITDA margin above 20% versus the 18.2-18.6% guide, demonstrating the step-down was conservatism rather than structure; (2) revenue per quarterly active customer grows more than 3% year over year in any quarter, evidencing Card/Wallet monetization; or (3) the shares trade below $20 with FY2027 revenue guidance intact at 17%+ growth.

**Downgrade to HOLD if:** contribution take rate (transaction margin dollars ÷ send volume) declines more than 8bp year over year in a single quarter, indicating transaction-cost leverage has plateaued; or U.S. revenue growth decelerates below 12% year over year.

**Downgrade to REDUCE / thesis invalidation triggers:** (1) FY2027 adjusted EBITDA guided below $450M, implying margin contraction from FY2026; (2) two consecutive quarters of flat-to-negative active customer growth; (3) any material adverse change to the $550M revolver's availability, size or covenant structure — this is working-capital infrastructure, not optional leverage; (4) disclosure that the processing-partner receivable behind the FY2025 $21.5M provision has produced further losses; or (5) legislative extension of the OBBBA remittance excise tax to digital transfers, which would remove the structural advantage against agent-based competitors.

**Reassessment date: early November 2026** (Q3 print).

## Conclusion

Remitly is a real business with a real profitability inflection, no net debt, $673M of net cash, and a valuation of 10.3x forward EV/EBITDA that does not require heroic assumptions to work. The company has beaten guidance four consecutive quarters and raised its FY2026 adjusted EBITDA outlook 33% from December to August. Those facts support a positive expected return, and we estimate roughly +16% probability-weighted over 12-18 months.

They do not yet support a full position. The 2026 upside came overwhelmingly from cost removal — technology and G&A dollars declining in absolute terms against 20% revenue growth, SBC falling from 9.6% to 6.5% of revenue, $13.7M of severance — and management's own guide concedes the limit by implying Q3 margin 460-500bp below Q2 and Q4 margin below the prior year. Revenue per active customer is flat, take rate is eroding 12-13bp a year, and growth is being purchased with advertising up 30% and incentives up 34%. Directors and officers sold shares between April and July with no offsetting open-market purchase, including a co-founder cutting 23% of his stake, and the CEO is six months into the job amid C-suite turnover we could not fully scope. The headline 601% Q2 earnings surprise and +42.5% current-year estimate revision are largely the arithmetic of a $140.6M discrete tax benefit rather than operating inflection, which is why next-year consensus EPS sits below current-year.

The recommendation is ACCUMULATE with discipline: buy a partial position now, reserve the majority for $20-21 where the company's own buyback executed, and let the November Q3 print adjudicate whether the guided margin step-down is conservatism or the end of the cost story.
