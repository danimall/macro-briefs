## Executive Summary

Option Care Health, the largest independent home and alternate-site infusion services provider in the U.S., dropped 26% on April 30, 2026 after cutting FY2026 revenue guidance from $5.8-6.0B to $5.675-5.775B — just 65 days after reaffirming the higher range. The stock now trades at $20.86, a 43% discount to its 52-week high of $36.80. The core question: is this a temporary growth air pocket driven by biosimilar adoption and CID patient retention, or the beginning of structural revenue impairment in the company's highest-value therapy lines?

The case for temporary dislocation is supported by three data points: (1) EBITDA guidance was maintained at $480-505M despite the ~$200M revenue cut, indicating the lost revenue carried minimal margin; (2) four insiders — including the CEO and CFO — collectively purchased $1.88M of stock at $20-21 within days of the decline; and (3) valuation at ~9.5x EV/EBITDA is historically cheap for a business with OPCH's market position and recurring revenue characteristics. The case against is that biosimilar penetration and IRA drug price negotiations represent permanent headwinds to the CID portfolio, gross margins have compressed 350bps over three years, and management's credibility is impaired after the rapid guidance reversal.

At current levels, the risk/reward skews modestly positive but the visibility gap precludes high conviction. Monitoring Q2 results for stabilization in CID patient retention and gross margin trajectory will determine whether this is a buying opportunity or a value trap.

## Company Overview

Option Care Health operates 196 locations across 43 states, including 87 full-service pharmacies (73 with ambulatory infusion suites) and 109 stand-alone infusion suites. The company employs over 8,000 people, with 5,000+ clinicians. It serves patients requiring intravenous therapies — anti-infectives, immunoglobulin therapy, nutrition support, biologics for chronic inflammatory disorders, and specialty therapies — delivered in homes or non-hospital settings.

Revenue is concentrated in commercial payers (88% from managed care and third-party payers), with 12% from government programs. The largest single payer represents ~14% of revenue. OPCH was formed through the 2019 merger of Option Care (formerly Walgreens Infusion Services) and BioScrip, creating scale advantages in an otherwise fragmented market.

The structural thesis: site-of-care shift from hospitals to lower-cost home/ambulatory settings, growing biologics pipeline requiring infusion administration, and aging demographics increasing chronic disease prevalence. These secular drivers remain intact; the current issue is specific to therapy mix and reimbursement compression.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Trajectory:**
- FY2022: $3.94B
- FY2023: $4.30B (+9.1%)
- FY2024: $5.00B (+16.2%)
- FY2025: $5.65B (+13.0%)
- Q1 2026: $1.35B (+1.3% YoY)
- FY2026 guidance: $5.675-5.775B (~1% growth at midpoint)

The deceleration from 13% to 1% is the central financial concern. It is driven by lower CID patient retention (patients moving to subcutaneous/oral biosimilar alternatives), biosimilar reimbursement compression, and a 66% price reduction on one therapy under IRA negotiations effective January 2026. Management disclosed $25-35M in gross profit headwinds from biosimilar adoption for 2026, but the actual Q1 impact exceeded initial expectations by ~200bps.

**Margin Profile:**

- Operating margin: ~6.0% (2025)
- Net margin: 3.7% (2025), down from 6.2% in 2023

Gross margin compression of 350bps over three years reflects unfavorable therapy mix (higher-cost drugs with lower spread) and biosimilar pricing pressure. EBITDA guidance maintained at $480-505M despite the revenue cut implies the lost revenue was extremely low-margin or possibly negative-margin after service delivery costs.

At current prices, remaining buyback capacity could provide meaningful EPS support.

## Growth Analysis

Near-term growth has materially decelerated. The FY2026 guided revenue of $5.675-5.775B represents ~1% growth — a dramatic slowdown from the 9-16% compound rate of 2022-2025. The causes:

1. **Biosimilar adoption in CID:** Patients on infused biologics (e.g., Remicade, Stelara) are transitioning to subcutaneous or oral alternatives, reducing both patient volumes and per-patient reimbursement.
2. **IRA drug price negotiations:** One therapy saw a 66% list price reduction effective January 2026. Additional therapies may be negotiated in future rounds.
3. **Patient retention headwinds:** Lower CID retention indicates structural patient migration away from IV administration for certain conditions.

Consensus estimates reflect moderate optimism: current year EPS $1.94, next year $2.05 (5.7% growth). Revisions are mildly positive in the near-term (+4.4% 30-day for current quarter) but negative for next year (-3.4% 30-day). This divergence suggests analysts expect near-term stabilization but have reduced confidence in medium-term growth recovery.

The secular tailwinds (site-of-care shift, growing biologics pipeline, aging demographics) remain intact but are being offset by therapy-specific headwinds. The bull case requires new therapy launches and continued market share gains in acute care to offset CID erosion. The pipeline of infused biologics for oncology, neurology, and rare diseases is robust, but OPCH's ability to capture this volume is unproven at scale.

## Competitive Landscape

OPCH holds the #1 position among independent infusion providers. Key competitors include:
- **Optum Infusion Pharmacy (UHC):** Vertically integrated with payer, advantage in patient access
- **Coram CVS:** PBM integration, retail pharmacy network
- **Amerita/BrightSpring:** Scale through PE-backed consolidation
- **KabaFusion, Soleo Health, Vital Care:** Regional competitors

OPCH's independence is a genuine competitive advantage: health systems and non-UHC payers prefer referring to a provider that doesn't compete with them for lives. However, this independence also means OPCH lacks the captive patient flow that integrated competitors enjoy.

The 2025 "competitive landscape shift" that drove acute care growth (mid-teens) likely reflects a competitor losing contracts or exiting markets — a one-time windfall that is now normalizing. The company's 196-location national footprint creates barriers to entry for smaller competitors and operational scale advantages in procurement and staffing.

Competitive position is stable but not improving. The biosimilar headwind is industry-wide for IV-focused providers, but OPCH's CID concentration makes it disproportionately exposed.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Structural revenue impairment from biosimilar/oral alternatives (HIGH):** The migration from IV to subcutaneous/oral biologics in CID is a structural trend, not cyclical. Each biosimilar approval in OPCH's therapy portfolio creates both volume and pricing pressure. Future IRA negotiation rounds could expand the scope of affected therapies.

2. **Guidance credibility gap (MODERATE-HIGH):** Management reaffirmed guidance on February 24, then cut on April 30. Multiple securities fraud investigations have been announced. While these rarely succeed, they indicate damaged investor trust and create legal overhang.

3. **Goodwill impairment risk (MODERATE):** $1.61B goodwill (46% of assets) was accumulated at higher-growth assumptions. Permanent deceleration below low-single-digits would likely trigger impairment testing, creating a binary event for book value.

4. **Vendor concentration (MODERATE):** 68% of pharmaceutical purchases from four vendors. Loss or renegotiation of a key supply relationship could disrupt margins.

5. **Floating-rate exposure:** The September 2025 refinancing to SOFR+1.75% creates interest rate sensitivity on the $676M term loan.

## Conclusion

OPCH presents a genuine analytical dilemma. The 40% decline from highs creates valuation support at ~9x EV/EBITDA on maintained EBITDA guidance. The $1.9M insider buying cluster — CEO, CFO, and two directors purchasing simultaneously at $20-21 — is among the strongest insider signals observable and suggests the people with the best information believe the stock is significantly undervalued. The business retains its #1 independent market position, generates $200M+ FCF annually, and has $1B+ liquidity.

Against this: the growth collapse from 13% to 1% is severe, guidance credibility is impaired, biosimilar/IRA headwinds are structural rather than cyclical, and gross margins have compressed 350bps over three years. The next 1-2 quarters will determine whether this is a stabilization-and-recovery story or the early innings of secular revenue decline in the company's core CID franchise.

The current recommendation is HOLD. The insider buying and maintained EBITDA guidance tilt the probability modestly toward recovery, but the visibility gap and growth impairment prevent a high-conviction BUY call until Q2 results confirm stabilization.
