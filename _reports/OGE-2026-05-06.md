## Executive Summary

OGE Energy operates Oklahoma's largest electric utility (OG&E), serving 913,000 customers with some of the lowest electricity rates in the nation — 34% below the national average. This cost advantage underpins a credible growth story: data center operators, including Google, are choosing Oklahoma specifically because of affordable power. Commercial MWh sales rose 46% over 2023-2025, and the April 2026 announcement of three Google data centers in Muskogee and Stillwater validated the thesis with a take-or-pay contract structure that protects existing ratepayers.

The company targets 5-7% EPS growth through 2030, backed by $7.3B in planned capital expenditures and 4-6% projected load growth in 2026. Favorable legislation (Oklahoma SB 998, Arkansas Act 373) enables earlier cost recovery through CWIP inclusion, reducing the regulatory lag that typically constrains utility returns during heavy investment cycles.

The offsetting concern is balance sheet strain. Long-term debt grew 51% from $3.55B to $5.37B over three years. Both Moody's and S&P moved to negative outlooks in 2025, citing the capital program's size and emerging wildfire exposure. Free cash flow was just $83M in 2025 against $1.05B in capex. At 21.3x forward earnings (a modest premium to the 20.1x peer median) and with a 3.6% dividend yield (below the 3.7% peer median), the current price reflects much of the positive thesis without adequately compensating for execution and credit risk.

## Company Overview

OGE Energy Corp is the parent holding company of Oklahoma Gas and Electric Company, a vertically integrated regulated electric utility incorporated in 1902. The service territory spans approximately 30,000 square miles across Oklahoma (88% of revenues) and western Arkansas (7%), including Oklahoma City. FERC-regulated transmission contributes the remaining 5%.

Generation capacity totals 6,921 MW: 66% natural gas, 23% coal, 5% dual fuel, and 7% renewables (wind/solar). In 2025, actual generation mix was 57% gas, 34% coal, and 9% renewable. The coal share increased from 18% in 2024 to 34% in 2025, reflecting economic dispatch decisions under higher gas prices.

OGE completed its transformation into a pure-play regulated utility after divesting its Energy Transfer equity position in 2022. The company has no gas distribution, no midstream operations, and no unregulated generation — providing regulatory transparency but concentrating risk in a single jurisdiction.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:**
Revenue grew from $2.61B (2023) to $3.19B (2025), with operating income expanding from $650M to $799M over the same period. Diluted EPS moved from $2.08 to $2.32. The 2026 guidance midpoint of $2.43 implies approximately 5% growth, consistent with the bottom end of the 5-7% target range.

**Margins:**
Operating margin of 25.1% and net margin of 14.8% are strong for a regulated utility, reflecting both rate adequacy and cost discipline. ROE of 9.5% sits at the authorized return levels typical of Oklahoma regulatory outcomes.

**Cash Flow:**
Operating cash flow reached $1.14B in 2025, up 40% from $813M in 2024, driven primarily by fuel cost recovery timing. However, capital expenditures consumed $1.05B, leaving only $83M in free cash flow (2.6% margin). This compression will persist through the 2026-2030 capex program averaging ~$1.46B annually.

**Balance Sheet and Leverage:**
Debt/equity stands at 1.89x, reflecting the capital program's early innings. Long-term debt grew from $3.55B to $5.37B between 2022-2025. OGE raised $397M in equity in November 2025 and has $193M in pending forward share settlements, partially addressing funding needs. The Altman Z-Score of 1.19 sits in the "distress zone" nominally, though this is structurally normal for capital-intensive regulated utilities with predictable revenue recovery.

**Current ratio:** 0.78, below 1.0, indicating reliance on credit facilities for working capital. OGE has $867M in credit availability, which provides adequate near-term liquidity.

**Earnings Quality:**
The Q1 2026 quarter ($0.24 actual vs. $0.25 expected, a 5.5% miss) and Q3 2025 ($1.14 vs. $1.15, a 1.2% miss) reflect modest but persistent underperformance. The four-quarter record shows 1 beat, 1 inline, and 2 misses. This pattern suggests estimates are not systematically low — the company delivers within a narrow range but does not reliably exceed expectations.

## Growth Analysis

**Load Growth:**
Commercial MWh sales grew from 8.5M (2023) to 12.4M (2025), a 46% increase in two years. Total system sales grew 5.0% in 2025. The 4-6% projected load growth for 2026 is supported by contractual commitments from Google and additional interest from crypto mining and AI infrastructure operators.

**Google Data Center Contract (April 2026):**
Three facilities in Muskogee and Stillwater under take-or-pay terms. Google funds 100% of interconnection costs and contributes solar generation capacity. This structure protects existing ratepayers and creates margin-accretive volume growth. OGE will file a new large-load tariff with the Oklahoma Corporation Commission.

**Capital Investment Cycle:**
The $7.3B plan (2026-2030) breaks down: $3.6B distribution, $1.3B transmission, $900M generation reliability, $700M technology/fleet, $600M new generation capacity, $145M Arkansas distribution. The IRP identifies 1,096 MW additional capacity needed by 2028 and 2,592 MW by 2034.

**Legislative Tailwinds:**
Oklahoma SB 998 (effective August 2025) enables CWIP recovery for new gas generation and deferral of 90% of depreciation and return on qualified investments over 20 years. Arkansas Act 373 provides similar CWIP mechanisms. Both directly reduce the regulatory lag risk inherent in large capital programs.

**Estimate Revisions:**
Current-quarter estimates rose 10.9% over 30 days (from $0.51 to $0.57), likely reflecting the Google announcement and seasonal weather expectations. Annual estimates are essentially flat (-0.1% for FY2026 over 30 days, +0.9% for FY2027 over 90 days), indicating the market has not yet aggressively raised out-year estimates despite the data center pipeline. This represents either analyst conservatism or appropriate caution given execution uncertainty.

## Valuation Assessment

At $47.84, OGE trades at approximately 20.6x trailing EPS ($2.32) and approximately 19.7x FY2026 consensus ($2.43). The peer median P/E is 20.1x, placing OGE at a modest premium. The dividend yield of 3.6% is slightly below the 3.7% peer median.

**Upside case:** If the data center load growth accelerates estimates toward the high end of 5-7% growth ($2.60+ by 2027) and the market awards a growth utility premium of 22-23x, fair value reaches $57-60. The analyst high target of $59.00 reflects this scenario.

**Downside case:** If credit agencies downgrade (increasing borrowing costs 30-50bps on $5.4B+ debt), or if wildfire liability materializes, or if capex execution stumbles, earnings could remain at $2.40 and the multiple compress to 18x, implying $43-44 — essentially the analyst low of $42.50.

**Current valuation:** The stock sits near the analyst mean target of $49.86, suggesting consensus views it as approximately fairly valued. The median target of $51.00 offers only 7% upside from current levels.

The valuation does not screen as cheap. It reflects a utility trading at a slight premium to regulated peers, justified by above-average growth prospects but constrained by execution risk. The market is pricing the Google deal but also pricing the credit risk.

## Competitive Landscape

OGE's competitive moat centers on structural cost advantage. Residential electricity rates approximately 34% below national average and 19% below regional average provide a durable attraction for price-sensitive industrial loads. Oklahoma's regulatory environment — which now includes constructive CWIP legislation — adds a second dimension of advantage for capital-intensive growth.

Google's public selection of Oklahoma (and OGE specifically) validates the low-cost thesis and signals to other hyperscalers and large industrial users. Commercial load growth of 46% over two years suggests the pipeline of inquiries referenced in the 10-K is converting to actual connections.

Structural risks to the competitive position include: (1) other utilities cutting rates to compete for data center loads, (2) on-site power solutions (microgrids, fuel cells) enabling large users to bypass the grid, and (3) environmental compliance costs potentially eroding the rate advantage if coal fleet requires substantial investment to maintain.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit Rating Pressure (HIGH):** Dual negative outlooks from Moody's (A3) and S&P (A-) during a period requiring extensive capital market access. A single-notch downgrade on $5.4B+ debt would increase annual interest expense by $15-25M, directly reducing EPS by $0.06-0.10/share.

**Wildfire Exposure (MODERATE-HIGH):** S&P specifically cited this in its November 2025 outlook revision. Oklahoma's semi-arid climate and OGE's extensive T&D infrastructure create liability exposure analogous to (though less severe than) California utilities. No specific loss has occurred, but mitigation costs and insurance requirements are rising.

**Capital Program Execution (MODERATE):** $7.3B over 5 years against a $5.0B equity base. Requires continuous capital market access, regulatory approvals, and project management discipline. Supply chain constraints in generation equipment (turbines, transformers) represent a tangible delay risk.

**EPA Regulatory Uncertainty (LOW-MODERATE):** The Good Neighbor FIP, if implemented, would cost $2.4-2.8B. Currently stayed and under reconsideration — likely to be withdrawn or substantially modified under the current administration. This represents optionality rather than immediate risk.

**Data Center Customer Concentration (MODERATE):** As Google and potentially other hyperscalers become significant load, customer concentration increases. The take-or-pay structure protects revenues but creates volume dependence on a few large users.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV at 28.4% is nearly double the 15.6% one-year historical volatility — a 12.8pp rich spread. The term structure is in backwardation (28.4% near-term vs. 21.4% at 6 months), indicating the market prices a near-term catalyst or stress event. Put skew of 5.4% confirms modest directional concern. IV has declined from 41.7% on April 26 to 34.2% currently, suggesting post-earnings uncertainty is resolving.

The rich options pricing likely reflects the combination of recent earnings release, the Google announcement, and broader utility sector vol from rising rates. For an investor considering entry, the elevated near-term IV suggests waiting 2-3 weeks for normalization rather than selling premium immediately.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($55-59, +15-23%):**
- Google data centers are the first of multiple large-load customers attracted by low rates
- 2027-2028 load growth exceeds 6%, driving estimates toward $2.70-2.80
- Credit outlook stabilizes as equity raises and cash flow growth reduce leverage ratios
- EPA regulatory relief removes $2.4-2.8B in potential compliance costs
- Market re-rates OGE as a "growth utility" at 22-23x

**Bear Case ($42-44, -8-12%):**
- Credit downgrade increases borrowing costs during heavy capex period
- Wildfire event or mitigation requirements consume capital and compress returns
- Load growth disappoints as data center customers face their own capital constraints
- Rising long-term rates compress utility multiples sector-wide
- Coal regulatory costs materialize despite favorable political environment

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock declines to $43-44 (18x FY2026 EPS) while growth thesis remains intact — providing adequate margin of safety
2. Credit outlook revised to Stable by either Moody's or S&P — removing the financing risk overhang
3. Additional large-load customer announcements (beyond Google) that raise 2027+ estimates above $2.70

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Credit downgrade from either Moody's or S&P (actual downgrade, not outlook change)
2. FY2026 load growth comes in below 3% (materially missing 4-6% guidance range)
3. Wildfire liability materialization or significant mitigation capex announced (>$500M)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July/early August), when summer cooling season data and further Google implementation progress will be visible. Credit rating agency actions could occur at any time but most likely coincide with annual reviews in late 2026.

## Conclusion

OGE Energy presents a genuine above-peer growth story in a sector starved for organic growth catalysts. The Google data center contract, 46% commercial load growth over two years, and supportive state legislation collectively validate management's 5-7% EPS growth target. Director Ganske's $500K open-market purchase at approximately current prices adds insider conviction.

However, the current valuation at approximately 19.7x forward earnings reflects this thesis. The stock trades at a modest premium to peers, analyst consensus sees only 7% upside to median target, and the four-quarter earnings track record shows no consistent beat pattern. Dual negative credit outlooks during a $7.3B capex cycle represent a tangible execution risk not fully resolved. The 3.6% dividend yield provides income support but doesn't compensate for potential downside scenarios.

The appropriate stance is to hold for investors already positioned and monitor for either a better entry (via pullback to $43-44 or credit stabilization) or a deterioration trigger (downgrade, load miss, or wildfire event). The growth thesis is real but the price already reflects much of it.
