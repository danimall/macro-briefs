**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Union Pacific operates the largest railroad in North America, a roughly 32,000-route-mile network across the western two-thirds of the United States. By operating metrics it is the best-run Class I carrier: a 59.8% full-year 2025 operating ratio, ~16.3% adjusted ROIC, record safety and velocity statistics, and high-quality earnings driven by productivity rather than volume. The problem for the equity is that revenue has been essentially flat for four years (~$24.1–24.9B since 2022), and the operating ratio sits near a practical floor. With buybacks now paused, the two engines that drove EPS from roughly $11.09 to $11.98 in 2025, margin improvement and share-count reduction, are both running low on remaining runway organically.

The dominant fact for UNP today is the pending acquisition of Norfolk Southern, announced July 28, 2025, at 1 UNP share plus $88.82 cash per NS share: roughly $85B in headline value, ~225M new UNP shares (about 38% dilution to the current ~593M count) and ~$20B of new debt. This would create the first true transcontinental single-line railroad. It also converts UNP into a regulatory/merger-arbitrage situation. The Surface Transportation Board rejected the initial joint application as incomplete (without prejudice) in January 2026, the companies refiled an amended application on April 30, and on May 28, 2026 the STB accepted the revised application but paused and extended its review with outstanding information requests (deadline around July 27). The shares fell sharply on that news and the deal arb spread widened. Close, if it happens, is a 2027 event at the earliest, with a termination backstop of January 28, 2028.

We rate UNP a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The standalone franchise is excellent but slow-growing and fully priced at ~21.6x trailing earnings; the merger embeds genuine upside optionality but carries a binary regulatory outcome that neither we nor the options market can handicap with confidence. Until the STB review resolves, the stock will trade on deal headlines more than on quarterly fundamentals. We see no specific, falsifiable mispricing that justifies a BUY, and the franchise quality and investment-grade balance sheet rule out an AVOID.

## Company Overview

Union Pacific runs a single integrated rail network spanning 23 western states, connecting Pacific and Gulf ports, the upper Midwest, eastern interchange gateways, and Mexican and Canadian connections. Mexico cross-border traffic (~$2.9B FY25 revenue) is a franchise asset. The company reports as one operating segment but tracks revenue across three commodity groups:

- **Bulk** (~$7.6B FY25): grain and grain products, fertilizer, food/refrigerated, coal and renewables.
- **Industrial** (~$8.6B FY25): chemicals and plastics, metals and minerals, forest products, energy and specialized markets.
- **Premium** (~$7.0B FY25): finished automobiles, auto parts, and intermodal (domestic and international).

UNP is a duopolist in the West, with BNSF (Berkshire Hathaway) as its primary parallel competitor across major corridors. The moat is structural: irreplaceable rights-of-way, enormous replacement cost (net PP&E of $59.6B on a $87B gross base), and prohibitive barriers to entry. Competitive threats come from trucking (faster transit), barge (grain and bulk near waterways), pipelines, and commodity/geographic substitution (natural gas displacing coal, alternate grain sourcing).

## Financial Analysis

UNP is a highly profitable, capital-intensive, slow-growth compounder. The revenue line and the earnings line diverge.

| Operating income ($B) | 9.9 | 9.1 | 9.7 | 9.8 |
| Operating ratio | ~60.1% | 62.3% | 59.9% | 59.8% |
| Net income ($B) | 7.0 | 6.4 | 6.7 | 7.1 |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | ~$11.09 | $11.98 (+8%) |
| Operating cash flow ($B) | — | 8.4 | 9.3 | 9.3 |
| Free cash flow ($B, post-div) | — | 1.5 | 2.8 | 2.3 |

| ROE | — | 47.3% | 42.6% | 40.4% |

Revenue grew about 1% in FY25 on 1% volume growth; the EPS gain came from productivity. Management cites best-ever safety (personal injury rate −24%, derailment rate −19%), record terminal dwell (−8%), freight car velocity +8%, and 7% workforce productivity gains on a 3% headcount reduction to ~29,300. The 59.8% operating ratio leads the industry but leaves little incremental margin runway; mathematically, the lever that drove past EPS growth is approaching its floor.

Q1 2026 momentum was solid: revenue +3% to $6.2B, EPS +6% to $2.87, operating ratio 60.5%, despite international intermodal collapsing −28% YoY (lapping the 2025 tariff-driven West Coast import surge). Coal (+16% carloads) and grain (+14%) offset that drag. Management has managed mix well, lifting revenue ~3% on a ~1% carload decline through pricing and mix.

On the balance sheet, the headline Altman Z-Score of ~1.4 reads as "distress" but is a false positive. Railroads structurally run working-capital deficits (current ratio 0.91) and high leverage (D/E ~2.8) because PP&E dominates and debt is termed out. The relevant figure is adjusted debt/adjusted EBITDA of 2.5–2.7x, comfortably investment-grade, with substantial covenant headroom ($48.2B allowed vs. $32.3B actual), no variable-rate debt, and a $2B undrawn revolver. The Piotroski 2/3 is a single-filing artifact, not a deterioration signal.

Capital allocation has been disciplined: ~$3.3–3.8B annual capex (~15% of revenue, essentially all maintenance/modernization), ~$3.2B dividends, and $2.7B of buybacks in 2025, now paused for the NS deal. The July 2025 H.R.1 reinstatement of 100% bonus depreciation added ~$0.3B to operating cash flow. The board has authorized up to $20B of debt securities as the financing vehicle for the cash portion of the merger.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone, UNP's organic growth profile is muted. Four years of flat revenue is the central fact, and the productivity-and-buyback engine that produced EPS growth is constrained going forward: the operating ratio sits near ~59–60% and buybacks are suspended through the merger pendency. Estimate revisions confirm the low-growth read — consensus has been essentially stable, with current-year EPS revised +1.2% over 90 days and next-year +1.3%, short of the upward velocity that signals an earnings acceleration. Current-year consensus sits at $12.57, next year at $13.69, implying high-single-digit EPS growth that depends largely on pricing/mix and incremental productivity rather than volume.

The growth thesis has migrated almost entirely into merger optionality. UNP and NS estimate ~$3.5B in annual shipper savings and tout single-line-service efficiency. If the deal closes and synergies materialize as claimed, the combined entity would be a structurally different, higher-growth franchise. That outcome is contingent on STB approval, which remains genuinely uncertain.

Cyclical swing factors complicate the near-term picture. International intermodal whipsawed from +17% in H1 2025 (tariff pull-forward) to −24% in H2 and −28% in Q1 2026, distorting YoY optics. Coal's 2025 revenue surge (+20%, +14% carloads) is a cyclical reprieve within a structural decline, dependent on high natural-gas prices, and likely to reverse if gas prices fall. We treat both as noise around a low-single-digit organic trend, not durable growth drivers.

## Valuation Assessment

At $259.38, UNP trades at ~21.6x trailing EPS of $11.98 and ~20.6x current-year consensus of $12.57. The dividend yield is ~2.1%.

Against the listed industrials peers (ETN 39.8x, HON 37.3x, DE 30.0x, BA 89.9x; median ~37.3x), UNP looks cheap. Those comparables are a heterogeneous mix of an electrical-equipment compounder, a multi-industrial, an ag-machinery cyclical, and an aerospace turnaround — none is a rail. The relevant peer set is the Class I railroads, against which UNP normally commands a premium multiple for its lowest operating ratio, highest ROIC, and best safety record. On its own history and rail peers, ~21x earnings is roughly fair-to-slightly-rich for a franchise with ~1% revenue growth and a margin lever near its floor.

The estimate-revision picture does not support a cycle-adjusted upgrade. Revisions are stable, nowhere near the velocity that would flag a trough-earnings mispricing. This is a steady, fully-recognized earnings stream. Trailing P/E is therefore an appropriate valuation lens here, and it offers no obvious margin of safety.

Consensus targets (mean $291, median $294.5, range $239–330) imply ~12–14% upside to the mean. Those targets embed assumptions about merger value-creation that the analyst community itself cannot yet calibrate; the sentiment trend is degrading, with one recent downgrade to hold. We view the standalone fair value as roughly $245–265 on ~20x current-year EPS, with the upper end of the target range only reachable if the merger closes on favorable terms. The stock effectively prices in a partial, probability-weighted version of the deal, which is why a binary regulatory outcome makes the valuation hard to underwrite. We score valuation at 5.0: not stretched, not cheap, with no specific, falsifiable mispricing to exploit.

## Competitive Landscape

UNP's standalone competitive position is strong and stable. It is the best-run franchise in a structurally protected duopoly, with the lowest operating ratio, highest ROIC, and best safety profile among Class I carriers. The moat — physical rights-of-way and ~$60B of net PP&E that no entrant could replicate — is durable.

The industry is consolidating. A UNP–NS combination would create the first coast-to-coast single-line railroad (~50,000 miles across 43 states), eliminating Chicago and Mississippi-gateway interchange friction. That is widely expected to pressure BNSF and CSX into a defensive Western merger response, putting the entire industry structure in play. The binding constraint is the STB's post-2001 "public interest" standard, which requires demonstrated net competitive enhancement and permits shippers, competitors, and labor to intervene. Approval is uncertain and, at best, a 2027 event with appeal risk extending further.

One governance/market flag: UNP's three-year indexed total shareholder return (124) has lagged its peer group (149) despite operational leadership. The market has been pricing in either merger risk or skepticism on growth, a tension that sits uneasily against the franchise's operating quality.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by a single binary event, which is why we score risk factors at 4.5.

1. **Norfolk Southern merger / STB regulatory risk (dominant).** The FY25 10-K added an entire new risk-factor category. Specific concerns: failure to close, the $2.5B mutual termination fee (payable either way), integration complexity ("neither company has completed a transaction comparable in size"), the new debt, leverage covenants, credit-rating-downgrade risk, change-of-control triggers in NS contracts, and merger-pendency business disruption. If the deal fails, UNP pays/receives a break fee, resumes buybacks, and reverts to a slow-growth standalone story — likely a downside revaluation toward the low-$230s/$240s given the optionality currently embedded in the price. If it succeeds, the path runs through dilution and new debt before synergies arrive.
2. **International intermodal / trade-policy volatility.** The tariff pull-forward and 2026 reversal are the most active near-term cyclical swing factor and create misleading YoY optics.
3. **Coal cyclical reversal.** The 2025 surge is gas-price-dependent and not durable; lower gas prices would reverse the bulk tailwind.
4. **Hazardous-materials common-carrier liability.** UNP must transport TIH materials (chlorine, crude, ethanol) regardless of risk; a catastrophic derailment (cf. NS East Palestine) could exceed insurance.
5. **Labor and legal.** Unionized workforce, FELA personal-injury exposure ($406–523M range), recurring crew-agreement ratification charges, and environmental liabilities at 346 sites (29 government-action, 17 Superfund).
6. **Fuel-surcharge lag** (~2 months) drives quarterly earnings volatility.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent, deal-driven uncertainty. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 31.0% is rich relative to 21.2% one-year historical vol (a 9.8pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (31.0% near-term vs 25.6% six-month): the market is pricing near-term stress, consistent with the STB July information deadline. Put/call skew of 8.1% at the front week signals elevated near-term put demand, though the put/call OI ratio of 0.48 is not broadly bearish. Since early May, ATM IV rose from 26.8% to 31.0% as the price fell ~3.3% and the P/C OI ratio climbed from 0.26 to 0.48, directionally consistent with hedging into the regulatory catalyst rather than a clean directional bet. The elevated near-term IV makes long premium expensive and argues against expressing a view through near-dated options.

Short interest is modest at 5.4% of float (9.4 days to cover), so squeeze risk is not a major factor; the elevated activity is best read as deal-spread positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The merger closes in 2027 on substantially the announced terms, the STB conditions are manageable, and the claimed shipper savings translate into meaningful combined-entity synergies. UNP becomes the first transcontinental single-line railroad with a structurally higher growth profile, justifying a re-rating toward the high end of the analyst range. Standalone, the franchise continues to compound EPS in the high single digits via pricing/mix and incremental productivity, and buybacks resume if the deal fails. Bull target ~$315–330.

### Bear Case
The STB rejects or heavily conditions the merger, or the parties walk away near the January 2028 backstop. UNP pays/receives the $2.5B break fee and reverts to a slow-growth standalone story with flat revenue and a margin lever near its floor; the multiple compresses toward the lower end of its historical band. Cyclical coal and intermodal tailwinds fade. Bear target ~$239, roughly −8% from current.

The asymmetry is two-sided and the central variable (STB approval) is a regulatory judgment we cannot handicap with confidence, which warrants a hold.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The standalone franchise is high-quality but fully valued and slow-growing; the merger introduces binary regulatory risk that dominates the fundamental picture.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The STB issues a clear, favorable procedural signal (e.g., accepts the application as complete and sets a defined approval schedule with manageable conditions), reducing the binary risk such that the merger arb spread compresses meaningfully.
- The stock pulls back toward $235–245 on regulatory headline noise while standalone fundamentals (operating ratio ~60%, ROIC ~16%, EPS revisions stable/positive) remain intact — improving standalone risk/reward independent of the deal.
- Evidence of accelerating organic volume growth (sustained carload growth >3% across multiple commodity groups), shifting the standalone story off its flat-revenue baseline.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- The STB formally rejects the revised application, or the parties terminate, combined with deteriorating standalone metrics (operating ratio drifting above 62%, negative EPS revisions, fading coal/intermodal volumes).
- A credit-rating downgrade tied to merger financing that pressures the dividend or covenant headroom.
- A material safety/derailment event creating uninsured liability.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the STB information deadline around July 27, 2026, followed by the next quarterly earnings release. The deal itself is a multi-year situation running into 2027 with a January 28, 2028 termination backstop.

## Conclusion

Union Pacific is the highest-quality operator among North American railroads, with an industry-leading operating ratio, ~16% ROIC, an investment-grade balance sheet (the Z-Score "distress" reading is a false positive), and a durable structural moat. Those strengths are real and largely priced in at ~21.6x trailing earnings. The standalone growth story is constrained — flat revenue, a margin lever near its floor, and buybacks paused — leaving high-single-digit EPS growth driven by pricing/mix.

The Norfolk Southern merger has converted UNP into a regulatory/merger-arbitrage situation in which approval is uncertain and likely a 2027 event at the earliest. The options market is pricing near-term stress around the July information deadline, and the analyst-sentiment trend is degrading. The bull and bear cases are roughly symmetric and turn on a regulatory outcome we cannot confidently handicap. We maintain a HOLD. The franchise quality and balance sheet rule out an AVOID; the full standalone valuation and binary deal risk rule out a BUY. We would become more constructive on a clear favorable STB signal or a pullback toward the mid-$230s on unchanged standalone fundamentals.
