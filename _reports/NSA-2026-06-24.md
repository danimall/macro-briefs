**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

National Storage Affiliates is a merger arbitrage position rather than a fundamental equity. On March 16, 2026, the board agreed to be acquired by Public Storage (PSA) in an all-stock transaction at a fixed exchange ratio of 0.1400 PSA shares per NSA common share, an enterprise value of roughly $10.5 billion. The deal is expected to close in Q3 2026 and the price action since announcement confirms the market is treating this as a high-probability close. At $44.96, NSA functions as a synthetic position in 0.14 Public Storage shares plus the residual probability the deal completes. The implied PSA price embedded in NSA (44.96 / 0.14 ≈ $321) tracks PSA's quoted level closely, which means the arbitrage spread is thin to near-zero. The investment question collapses to two parts: do you want to own PSA at its current level, and what is the residual deal-break downside.

The standalone business is unremarkable, which is why the board chose to sell. Same-store occupancy is flat at roughly 84%, same-store revenue growth is sub-1%, tenant-insurance revenue is declining, and the company carries a 2026–2029 debt maturity ladder culminating in a $900 million wall in 2029 that would require refinancing at higher coupons. Reported Core FFO growth from $0.54 to $0.57 per unit is entirely attributable to adding back $10 million of merger costs; clean FFO actually declined to $0.49 from $0.52. A subscale operator with stagnant organic growth and a refinancing wall concluded it could create more value selling into the dominant scale player's stock than executing standalone.

The risk profile is now binary and asymmetric. Upside is capped near deal value (approximately 0.14 × PSA's price, currently ~$44–45) plus the ~5% dividend captured until close. The dominant downside is the low-probability scenario in which the deal breaks, which would return NSA toward its undisturbed ~$31 level, roughly 30% below current price. We rate NSA a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: hold through close to capture the residual spread and dividend, but recognize there is no independent appreciation thesis and the trade's payoff is now dominated by Public Storage's share price and binary deal mechanics.

## Company Overview

NSA is a self-administered, self-managed self-storage REIT organized in Maryland in 2013, with REIT election effective 2015. As of March 31, 2026, the consolidated portfolio comprised 799 properties, roughly 51.1 million rentable square feet and approximately 402,000 units across 33 states and Puerto Rico. An additional 262 properties (~18.2 million square feet) sit in four unconsolidated joint ventures partnered with Heitman Capital Management affiliates, in which NSA holds 25% interests and earns management, platform and call-center fees (4–6% of revenue) plus 50% of tenant-insurance proceeds. The total platform spans 1,061 properties, ~69.3 million square feet and ~547,000 units, operated under the iStorage, Move It, Northwest, RightSpace, SecurCare and Southern brands.

The company's most consequential structural evolution was the July 1, 2024 internalization of its original "Participating Regional Operator" (PRO) structure, a long-planned transition to a full internally-staffed management platform. Integration costs from that internalization carried through 2025 and into early 2026. NSA ranks as a distant but legitimate top-five U.S. self-storage REIT, smaller than Public Storage (3,500+ sites, 250M+ square feet), Extra Space and CubeSmart, with a stated edge in secondary and tertiary markets featuring supply barriers, supported by an off-market acquisition and JV "promoted return" model.

The acquirer, Public Storage, will be the surviving parent; NSA shareholders receive PSA stock. The transaction is structured with a "Dropdown JV" in which 313 of NSA's ~799 consolidated properties (~40%) will be contributed into a joint venture with legacy operating-partnership limited partners immediately before close, a tax-deferral vehicle for long-time contributors and a condition to closing.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 results show a business in mild contraction and optimization mode, with headline metrics flattered by deal-related accounting.

- **Revenue:** $185.4 million, down 1.6% YoY from $188.4 million, driven by selling 16 stores over the trailing twelve months and weak tenant-insurance revenue, partly offset by a modest occupancy tick-up.
- **Same-store NOI:** +2.0% ($115.8M vs $113.5M); same-store rental revenue +0.9%; revenue per occupied square foot +0.6% to $15.88. Same-store occupancy essentially flat at 84.2% vs 84.1%.
- **Net income:** $27.7 million, up from $19.5 million, but flattered by a $6.5 million gain on property sales and a swing in JV equity earnings (+$1.2M vs –$5.7M prior, largely a non-cash HLBV accounting effect on the 2024 JV).
- **Core FFO per unit:** $0.57, up from $0.54, but the entire gain came from adding back $10.0 million of merger costs. FFO before adjustments fell to $0.49 from $0.52.
- **Operating cash flow:** $87.4 million, up from $85.6 million.

The balance sheet carries ~$3.42 billion of total principal, with effective rates ranging from sub-3% on older private-placement notes to 6.7%. Roughly $417 million is variable-rate excluding swaps, with $860 million notional of swaps hedging variable exposure at a weighted-average fixed 3.08% with about two years remaining. Near-term maturities are meaningful: roughly $377 million due over the remainder of 2026 (including Term Loan D in July 2026 and the revolver maturing January 2027), ~$217 million in 2027, and a $900 million wall in 2029. NSA was in compliance with all covenants and retained $530.6 million of revolver capacity.

Distributions exceed GAAP earnings, normal for a REIT where FFO is the relevant payout metric, but the $0.57 quarterly common dividend equals roughly 100% of Core FFO per unit, leaving no FFO cushion. The dividend has been held flat at $0.57. Capital allocation reflects a company in play: NSA bought zero shares in Q1 2026 despite $350 million-plus of remaining buyback authorization, and its $400 million ATM equity program is unused, both consistent with merger covenant restrictions.

## Growth Analysis

There is no standalone organic growth thesis. Same-store revenue is growing sub-1%, occupancy is flat, and the company is a net seller of marginal assets (15 sold in 2025, three in Q1 2026 plus three more in April). The self-storage sector is in post-COVID normalization: after over-earning in 2021–2022, street-rate growth has been muted, new supply remains elevated in some Sunbelt markets, and soft housing-driven move-in demand has compressed pricing power. NSA's defensive response, selling marginal assets, internalizing the PRO platform to cut costs (property operating expenses fell 5.6% YoY), and pausing buybacks, is the behavior of a subscale operator that concluded value creation required scale it could not achieve alone.

Estimate revisions confirm this. Current-quarter consensus EPS fell 23.6% over 30 days and 38.0% over 90 days; current-year estimates are down 18.7% over 90 days. Estimates are broadly falling on weak fundamentals and the cost drag of an in-progress transaction. Growth is now an attribute of Public Storage, the acquirer, whose lower cost of capital and marketing scale are the rationale for rebranding NSA's secondary-market assets. PSA's separately announced $1.2 billion acquisition of Public Storage Canada in June 2026 is relevant because PSA stock is the currency NSA holders will own.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E of ~60x and forward P/E of ~60x are meaningless for a REIT and irrelevant for a merger-arb situation; REITs are valued on FFO and net asset value, and NSA's price is now mechanically tied to PSA's share price via the 0.14 exchange ratio. The relevant valuation framework is the arbitrage:

- The implied PSA price is ~$321, closely tracking PSA's quoted level, meaning the spread is thin to near-zero.
- The market is pricing high confidence the deal closes; otherwise the spread would be materially wider given ~30% break downside.
- Peer comparables (HIW 36.2x, LXP 35.2x, CWK 41.5x, peer median P/E 41.5x, median yield 4.8%) are not informative here; NSA is not trading on its own multiple. SL Green's negative P/E and CURB's 99.7x illustrate the heterogeneity that makes sector multiples unhelpful in this case.

The honest valuation conclusion: NSA offers no asymmetric upside at current price. Upside is capped at deal value plus the dividend captured to close, while the embedded downside to the undisturbed ~$31 level is roughly 30% if the deal breaks. Analyst targets (mean $43.06, median $41.50, high $49, low $40) cluster near and slightly below the current price, consistent with a stock pinned to deal value rather than a fundamental valuation gap; they reflect the deal-value ceiling. We score valuation at 5.0: not a value trap in the structural-impairment sense, but no mispricing to exploit and an unfavorable risk/reward skew at current price.

## Competitive Landscape

NSA's competitive position is legitimate but subscale: a top-five storage REIT meaningfully smaller than Public Storage, Extra Space and CubeSmart, with a niche in supply-constrained secondary markets. The PRO-structure internalization in July 2024 removed a structural complexity and cut operating costs, but flat occupancy and sub-1% rate growth show the platform was not generating differentiated organic results. The board's decision to sell is itself a statement that NSA could not close the cost-of-capital and marketing-scale gap versus the dominant operator. CEO Cramer's framing, that Public Storage's brand, operating platform and growth profile unlock value NSA could not achieve standalone given higher leverage and a refinancing wall, is a candid acknowledgment of competitive disadvantage. We score competitive position at 5.0: stable rather than clearly improving or deteriorating, but with the trajectory effectively resolved by the decision to merge into a stronger platform.

## Risk Assessment

The 10-Q states there were no material changes to risk factors except the addition of merger-related risks, which now dominate.

1. **Fixed exchange ratio, no collar.** NSA holders receive 0.1400 PSA shares regardless of PSA's price. NSA's value is levered directly to PSA stock; any decline in PSA between now and close reduces consideration with no protection.
2. **Deal-completion risk.** Closing requires NSA common-shareholder approval, OP unitholder approval, S-4 effectiveness, NYSE listing of PSA shares, formation of the Dropdown JV, and customary conditions. A $202 million termination fee plus no-shop provisions make a topping bid unlikely and reinforce the one-bidder, high-close-probability character of the situation.
3. **Pendency disruption.** Merger covenants restrict acquisitions, dispositions, major capex, new JVs and financings without PSA consent, alongside employee-retention and customer/vendor risks.
4. **Standalone risks if the deal breaks.** Interest-rate and refinancing risk on the 2026–2029 maturity ladder (notably the 2029 $900 million wall) in a higher-coupon environment; soft storage fundamentals; reliance on JV fee income; and concentration of JV partnerships with a single sponsor family (Heitman).
5. **Governance.** The Dropdown JV is a related-party feature: 313 properties contributed into a JV with legacy OP limited partners as a tax-deferral vehicle for insiders. It is a negotiated condition both sides agreed to, but it warrants scrutiny for fairness because public shareholders receive PSA stock while certain insiders receive a JV interest.

The risk score of 6.0 reflects that the dominant risks are now well-defined and largely priced: the market's tight spread implies high close probability, and the binary downside (~30%) is identifiable and bounded. The residual risk is the modest probability of a break combined with PSA share-price drift; neither is hidden.

### Options Market Signal

ATM implied volatility fell sharply from 51.0% to 37.7% between the May 20 and June 24 snapshots while the price rose 3.7%, consistent with diminishing uncertainty as the deal approaches close; the options market is pricing a resolving situation. The put/call open-interest ratio ticked up from 0.58 to 0.64, a mild increase in downside hedging but not an alarming skew. Short interest is negligible (0.0% of float, 4,815 shares short), confirming there is no meaningful bearish positioning and no squeeze dynamic.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$45–47)
The deal closes on schedule in Q3 2026 at the fixed 0.14 ratio. PSA's share price holds or appreciates (aided by accretive capital allocation such as the Canada acquisition), lifting the value of the consideration. Holders capture the residual arb spread plus roughly one to two quarters of the ~5% dividend before close. This is the central, high-probability path, but the payoff is modest from the current price: a few percent plus dividend.

### Bear Case (~$31, ~30% downside)
The deal breaks (regulatory, shareholder, or financing-condition failure), forcing NSA back to its undisturbed ~$31 level. Standalone, the company faces flat occupancy, sub-1% rate growth, falling estimates, a 2029 $900 million refinancing wall at higher coupons, and FFO that is flat-to-declining ex-merger-addbacks. Break probability appears low given the tight spread, the $202 million fee and no-shop provisions, but the impact is large and asymmetric.

The skew is unfavorable from $44.96: a few percent of upside against ~30% of downside, weighted by a low but non-zero break probability and the additional risk that PSA stock itself declines into close given the absence of a collar.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** This is a hold-to-close position for existing holders capturing the residual spread and dividend, not a fresh-money buy at current price given the unfavorable skew.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 expected close. The S-4 effectiveness, shareholder vote, and Dropdown JV formation are the key procedural milestones; PSA's share price is the continuous driver between now and close.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The arb spread widens materially (e.g., NSA trades 4–6%+ below 0.14 × PSA) on transient noise without a genuine deterioration in close probability, creating a favorable risk/reward to deal value.
- A confirmed topping bid emerges (low probability given the $202 million fee and no-shop), raising consideration above the current ratio.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Concrete evidence of deal jeopardy: a regulatory objection, a failed shareholder vote, an S-4 deficiency, or termination notice, any of which would expose holders to the ~30% undisturbed-price downside.
- A sustained, sharp decline in PSA's share price that reduces consideration below the current level with no offsetting spread compensation.

**For new capital:** There is no compelling entry here. The expected return from current price is dominated by the small residual spread and dividend, against a fat-tailed break risk. Investors who want the underlying economics are better served evaluating PSA directly.

## Conclusion

NSA's score trajectory has been declining (5.3 in March, 4.6 in May), with growth, valuation and risk-factor scores all marked down as the standalone weakness became visible alongside the merger. The current read modestly stabilizes that path: the risk-factor picture is now better-defined (the dominant risks are the priced, binary deal mechanics rather than diffuse fundamental deterioration), which lifts the risk score relative to the prior cut, while growth and valuation remain weak because there is no independent appreciation case. The net effect is a HOLD at an overall 5.05.

NSA is a near-completed merger arbitrage, not a fundamental storage long. The spread to deal value is nearly closed, the market is pricing high close probability, and the payoff from here is the small residual spread plus the dividend captured until a Q3 2026 close, set against ~30% downside if the deal breaks. Existing holders should hold through close and monitor the procedural milestones and PSA's share price. New capital has no edge here; the asymmetry is unfavorable and the underlying economics are better accessed through PSA directly. We classify this as an uncertain hold: the deal is likely to close, but there is no fundamental thesis that would justify a stronger directional call in either direction.
