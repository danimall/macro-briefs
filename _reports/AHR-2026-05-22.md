## Executive Summary

American Healthcare REIT is a differentiated healthcare REIT that listed in February 2024 and has delivered extraordinary operational performance: 9 consecutive quarters of double-digit same-store NOI growth, a GAAP profitability inflection (first positive net income in FY2025), and 55% NFFO growth. The integrated senior health campus model, operated exclusively by Trilogy Management Services, produces margins and occupancy metrics that exceed sector averages. The company has raised $2.7B in equity since listing at progressively higher prices, deploying capital accretively into a supply-constrained senior housing market.

However, the stock at $50.11 trades at ~24.3x forward NFFO (midpoint $2.06), a significant premium to healthcare REIT peers. EPS estimates have been cut 25% in the last 30 days, the CEO is on indefinite medical leave, 55.5% of NOI depends on a single operator (Trilogy), and elevated interest rates create fundamental headwinds for REIT valuations. The growth story is intact but the price already reflects substantial optimism, and the specific risk of growth deceleration or Trilogy disruption is not adequately compensated at current multiples.

## Company Overview

AHR operates ~337 properties across four segments: Integrated Senior Health Campuses (ISHC, 147 campuses, 55.5% of NOI), Senior Housing Operating Properties (SHOP, 97 facilities), Outpatient Medical (74 buildings), and Triple-Net Leased (19 properties). The ISHC segment is the differentiator — these campuses offer a continuum of care (independent living through skilled nursing) under one roof, managed by Trilogy Management Services. This model allows residents to age in place, improving retention and occupancy stability.

The company is geographically concentrated in Indiana (33.4%) and Ohio (11.5%), reflecting Trilogy's Midwest roots. AHR is self-managed with approximately 121 corporate employees. Total portfolio purchase price exceeds $5.4B.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Growth:** Total revenue reached $650.8M in Q1 2026, up 20.4% YoY from $541M. FY2025 resident fees were $2.09B (+11% YoY) on total revenue of $2.26B. This growth combines acquisitions ($1.05B deployed in 2025, $162.8M in Q1 2026) with organic same-store NOI gains.

**NFFO Trajectory:** Normalized FFO of $286.5M in FY2025 (+55% vs. FY2024 $184.9M). Q1 2026 NFFO/share of $0.50, up 31.6% from $0.38 a year earlier. FY2026 guidance of $2.03-$2.09/share implies continued double-digit growth.

**Margin Expansion:** SHOP operating expenses fell from 84.6% of revenue (FY2024) to 80.6% (FY2025), a 400bps expansion. ISHC improved 170bps. This reflects rate increases exceeding expense growth — a dynamic that depends on sustained occupancy improvement.

**Balance Sheet:** Net debt-to-adjusted EBITDA of 3.0x (Q1 2026) is conservative for the sector. Total debt of $1.53B at 4.34% weighted average rate, all variable debt swapped to fixed. Interest expense declined 31.5% to $86.8M in FY2025 due to equity-funded debt paydowns. Liquidity of $1.31B ($650M credit facilities + $527M unsettled forwards).

**Dilution:** Shares outstanding have tripled from ~66M (pre-IPO) to ~190M, with another ~10.5M in unsettled forward agreements. Per-share growth has been strong despite this dilution because capital is deployed at yields exceeding cost of equity, but the virtuous cycle depends on maintaining an elevated stock price.

**Free Cash Flow:** $165.9M (FY2025) — OCF $294.4M less capex $128.6M. Dividend payout ratio vs. NFFO is ~55%, well covered.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story rests on three pillars:

1. **Demographic demand:** The 80+ population is expanding rapidly while senior housing starts are at multi-decade lows. AHR's occupancy (89.5-90.0%) has runway to improve further.

2. **Same-store NOI growth:** 12.1% total portfolio in Q1 2026, with SHOP at 19.7% and ISHC at 14.5%. Guidance calls for 9-12% total portfolio growth in FY2026. This is exceptional by REIT standards but the rate of growth is decelerating from earlier quarters.

3. **Accretive external growth:** $650M+ investment pipeline expected to close by year-end 2026. The strategy of issuing equity at ~24x NFFO and acquiring properties at 7-9% cap rates is mathematically accretive but depends entirely on stock price remaining elevated.

**Estimate Revisions (Negative):** Current quarter EPS estimates have been cut 29% in the last 30 days ($0.18 → $0.13). Current year estimates fell 25% ($0.73 → $0.55). Only one analyst covers the stock for EPS estimates, creating data fragility, but the direction is unambiguously negative. This directly contradicts the growth narrative and represents the most concerning near-term signal.

## Valuation Assessment

At $50.11 with FY2026 NFFO guidance midpoint of $2.06, AHR trades at **24.3x forward P/NFFO**. The GAAP P/E of ~87x reflects non-cash depreciation charges typical for REITs and is not the appropriate metric.

**Peer comparison:** Healthcare REIT peers trade at median P/E of 32.9x on a GAAP basis, with Caretrust (CTRE) at 26.1x and Omega Healthcare (OHI) at 23.4x — both with significantly higher dividend yields (3.8% and 5.5% respectively vs. AHR's ~1.9%). The large-cap REIT sector average is ~17.8x 2026 FFO. AHR's 24.3x represents a 36% premium to the broader REIT FFO average.

**Premium justification:** Double-digit same-store NOI growth and a secular demographic tailwind support some premium. However, the ~1.9% dividend yield provides minimal income support at current prices. The premium assumes: (a) growth does not decelerate materially, (b) the accretive equity issuance machine continues functioning, and (c) Trilogy's operational excellence persists. Each of these assumptions carries specific risk.

**Analyst targets:** Consensus range of $54-$60 (mean $57.67, median $58), implying 8-20% upside. With 11 of 12 analysts at Buy/Strong Buy, the consensus is fully bullish. This leaves little room for positive surprise.

**Rate sensitivity:** At elevated long-term Treasury yields and the potential for further increases, cap rate expansion across real estate is a structural headwind. A 50bp cap rate expansion on AHR's portfolio would imply 10-15% NAV compression that is not reflected in the stock price.

## Competitive Landscape

AHR's competitive position is strong but concentrated. The Trilogy-operated ISHC model is genuinely differentiated — no other public REIT operates a comparable network of integrated continuum-of-care campuses at this scale. Welltower (WELL) and Ventas (VTR) are diversified healthcare REITs with larger portfolios but lack this specific product type.

The SHOP segment competes more directly with sector peers and outperforms on growth metrics (19.7% same-store NOI growth vs. sector averages in the low teens). Certificate of Need regulations in many of AHR's markets provide supply barriers.

The Outpatient Medical segment is being actively trimmed (84 → 74 buildings in 2025) as management concentrates on higher-growth operating segments. This strategic focus is correct.

Trilogy dependency creates both outperformance and concentration risk simultaneously.

## Risk Assessment

**Trilogy Concentration (Critical):** 147 of 337 properties depend on a single operator with no disclosed transition plan. If Trilogy experiences regulatory action, financial distress, or quality problems, AHR has no comparable replacement operator. This is a binary risk that the market may be underpricing given the stock's premium valuation.

**CEO Medical Leave (Elevated):** Danny Prosky took medical leave February 3, 2026 with "unable to predict when he may resume duties." Jeff Hanson (former CEO, Chairman) is competent — Q1 results were strong — but the indefinite nature creates governance uncertainty. Prosky is co-founder and his relationships may underpin the Trilogy partnership.

**Estimate Revisions (Negative):** Only one analyst provides EPS estimates, so the 25-29% downward revisions could reflect methodology changes rather than fundamental deterioration. But the direction is unambiguous and contradicts the growth thesis.

**Dilution Sustainability:** The equity issuance strategy works only while the stock trades at a premium to NAV. Any sustained price decline below $45 could impair the accretive issuance mechanism, removing the external growth engine.

**Rate Environment:** Elevated long-term rates and potentially rising. Cap rate expansion would compress private market values, slow transaction activity, and impair the arbitrage between public equity cost and private asset yields.

**Geographic Concentration:** 45% of portfolio in Indiana/Ohio. Medicaid funding changes at the state level would disproportionately impact AHR.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV of 28.9% vs. 23.1% HV (spread +5.8pp) indicates the market is pricing modest near-term uncertainty. The term structure is in backwardation (28.9% near vs. 25.6% 6-month), consistent with event-driven stress that resolves over time — likely reflecting the post-earnings uncertainty period and CEO situation. The put/call OI ratio of 0.03 (extreme call dominance) and -4.4% put/call skew suggest options participants are positioned bullishly. IV compressed 9pp over the past month (from 37.9% to 28.9%) while price was flat, indicating resolved near-term uncertainty following Q1 results.

Short interest at 8.6% of float (6.6 days to cover) represents moderate skepticism — higher than typical for healthcare REITs — suggesting some institutional investors share concerns about valuation or concentration risk.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($58-62):** Same-store NOI growth sustains at 10%+ through FY2027 as senior housing occupancy recovery continues. Demographic demand proves durable through economic cycles given the need-based nature of care. NFFO grows to $2.25-$2.50 by FY2027. The $650M+ acquisition pipeline closes accretively. Multiple sustains at 25x as growth REIT scarcity premium holds. CEO returns or Hanson proves a permanent effective replacement.

**Bear Case ($38-42):** Rising rates force cap rate expansion, compressing NAV 15-20%. Growth decelerates to mid-single-digits as occupancy gains plateau near 92-93%. Trilogy experiences operational difficulties (regulatory, labor) that impair ISHC performance. Stock price decline below $45 breaks the accretive issuance mechanism. NFFO multiple compresses to 18-20x as rate-of-change slows. At 20x forward NFFO on $2.06, fair value is $41.

**Base Case ($52-55):** Growth moderates from 12% to 8-10% same-store NOI as the easy occupancy gains are captured. NFFO reaches $2.10-$2.15 in FY2026. Multiple sustains at 24-25x given continued demographic tailwinds but does not expand further in the current rate environment. Stock grinds higher modestly but risk/reward is balanced.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock pulls back to $42-44 range (20-21x forward NFFO), providing margin of safety
- Estimate revisions reverse positively, with FY2026 NFFO estimates revised above $2.15
- CEO returns from medical leave, removing governance overhang
- Rates decline materially, providing fundamental REIT tailwind

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
- Same-store NOI growth decelerates below 5% for any quarter
- Trilogy experiences regulatory action, quality ratings downgrade, or disclosed financial stress
- Stock rises above $56 (27x+ forward NFFO) without corresponding estimate increases
- Company suspends or significantly reduces ATM program due to share price weakness

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected August 2026) will provide a critical read on growth trajectory sustainability and CEO status.

## Conclusion

AHR is executing exceptionally well operationally — 9 consecutive quarters of double-digit same-store NOI growth, a profitability inflection, and conservative leverage position the company as one of the strongest growth stories in REITs. The demographic tailwind is durable and the Trilogy-integrated campus model is genuinely differentiated.

At 24.3x forward NFFO with a ~1.9% dividend yield, the stock prices in continued excellence while offering limited margin of safety against Trilogy concentration, indefinite CEO medical leave, negative estimate revisions, and a structurally challenging rate environment. The analyst consensus of $54-$60 provides only 8-20% upside, insufficient compensation for the concentration and governance risks.

The combination of (1) a premium valuation with no margin of safety, (2) negative near-term estimate revisions contradicting the growth narrative, and (3) single-operator dependency representing a binary risk with no disclosed contingency plan — all occurring simultaneously with the CEO on indefinite medical leave — prevents a BUY recommendation. Growth is real but priced; the risk/reward at $50 is balanced. A pullback to the low-$40s would create an attractive entry point where the demographic thesis carries sufficient margin of safety.
