**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SLB is the world's largest oilfield services company, operating across 100+ countries with roughly 109,000 employees and five reporting divisions spanning drilling, production systems, reservoir performance, digital software, and emerging industrial adjacencies. The investment case has deteriorated materially over the past two quarters. The FY2025 10-K, filed in January 2026, declared regional headwinds "behind us" and guided to a 2026 recovery; the Q1 2026 10-Q, filed three months later, disclosed a Middle East conflict that forced SLB to demobilize operations across multiple countries, crushing margins in its largest region. Organic revenue fell ~7% in Q1 2026, Well Construction operating margin collapsed 463 bps to 15%, and free cash flow turned negative at −$23M. The narrative flipped between filings, and the more recent, more pessimistic disclosure carries the weight.

The counterweight is that SLB remains a high-quality, cash-generative franchise trading at a discount to its services peers. At $47, the stock sits ~20% below its 52-week high of $58.82, with a forward P/E near 20.7x against a peer median of 27x. The company generated $6.49B of FY2025 operating cash flow and $4.1–4.8B of free cash flow despite a 24% drop in net income, and it returned $4.0B to shareholders. The data-center pivot — modular infrastructure and power generation delivered through the newly announced Liberty Energy alliance (July 14, 2026) — grew 45% YoY in Q1 2026 and offers genuine, if still-immaterial, optionality on AI-driven demand.

The problem is that neither the bull nor the bear case has a near-term resolution. The 2026 setup is a reset year: organic revenue shrinking, EPS diluted ~9-10% by the all-stock ChampionX deal, consensus estimates falling across every window, and no confirmed catalyst until the Middle East stabilizes and ChampionX synergies materialize. Estimate revisions are negative (current-year EPS cut 6.5% over 90 days), which historically is a warning sign, and the stock's cheapness relative to peers reflects a real earnings decline rather than a mispricing. We rate SLB HOLD with an uncertain rationale; the risk/reward is genuinely balanced, and we would want either estimate stabilization or evidence of a Middle East activity recovery before turning constructive.

## Company Overview

SLB (legally renamed from Schlumberger in 2025, incorporated in Curaçao with executive offices in Paris, Houston, and The Hague) reports across five units following the Q3 2025 elevation of Digital to a standalone division:

- **Production Systems** ($13.3B FY25 revenue, ~16% margin) — now the largest division after the ChampionX acquisition; subsea (OneSubsea 70%-owned JV), artificial lift, completions, surface systems, and production chemicals.
- **Well Construction** ($11.9B, ~19% margin) — drilling measurements, fluids, bits, autonomous drilling. The most cyclical division; down 11% YoY in 2025.
- **Reservoir Performance** ($6.8B, ~18% margin) — evaluation, stimulation, intervention.
- **Digital** ($2.7B, ~28% margin) — Delfi/Lumi platforms, Petrel/Techlog applications, exploration data. Highest-margin, fastest-organic-growth core business.
- **All Other** — APS projects (Ecuador), Data Center Solutions (modular enclosures/cooling for hyperscalers), and SLB Capturi (80%-owned carbon capture JV).

The strategic thesis is de-cyclicalization: shifting revenue mix from short-cycle drilling toward higher-margin production/recovery, digital software, and industrial adjacencies. No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue, and ~82% of revenue is non-US. The company celebrates its centennial in 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** 2022 $28.1B → 2023 $33.1B → 2024 $36.3B → 2025 $35.7B (−1.6%). The headline decline understates the weakness. Excluding ~$1.5B of ChampionX revenue, organic revenue fell ~6% in 2025 on lower activity in Saudi Arabia, Mexico, and offshore Sub-Saharan Africa.

**Profitability compressed sharply in 2025.** Net income fell to $3.37B, down 24% from $4.46B in 2024; pretax income dropped to $4.29B from $5.67B. Net margin contracted to ~9.7% from ~12.3%. The drivers were activity declines in high-margin international markets plus $1.1B of charges and credits (up from $541M in 2024), including workforce reductions, ChampionX integration costs, a $210M SLB Capturi goodwill impairment, and inventory purchase-accounting amortization.

**Q1 2026 marked a negative turn.** Revenue of $8.72B was +3% YoY on the ChampionX contribution but −7% organic. Net income to SLB fell to $752M ($0.50 EPS) from $797M ($0.58 EPS). The 141M shares issued for ChampionX raised the count to ~1.50B from ~1.37B, diluting EPS meaningfully even as absolute income held roughly flat. Production Systems margin fell 240 bps. Free cash flow was −$23M versus +$103M in Q1 2025, and operating cash flow fell to $487M.

**Cash generation remains the franchise's defining strength through-cycle.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $6.49B and free cash flow held up despite the 24% earnings drop. Capital discipline is evident: ~$2.4B capex in 2025, guided to ~$2.5B for 2026. The Q1 FCF miss is partly seasonal (working-capital build), and management reaffirmed >$4B of 2026 shareholder returns, but the negative print plus a ~75% QoQ cut in buybacks (from $1.84B in Q1'25 to $451M) is the first tangible sign of pressure on the cash engine.

**Balance sheet turned intangible-heavy.** Total assets grew to $54.9B from $48.9B almost entirely from ChampionX goodwill and intangibles (goodwill +$2.2B to $16.8B; intangibles +$2.0B to $5.0B). Goodwill plus intangibles now total $21.8B, ~83% of the $26.1B stockholders' equity. Net debt is stable at ~$7.4B, D/E ~1.06, current ratio 1.33. The Altman Z-Score of 1.99 (grey zone) reflects the asset-heavy, goodwill-laden structure and depressed EBIT rather than liquidity distress; SLB holds $4.2B cash/short-term investments plus $5.0B in undrawn committed facilities. The SLB Capturi impairment, taken at a punitive 14.75% discount rate, signals further impairment risk if new-energy or JV cash flows disappoint.

**Capital returns are decelerating with the cycle.** SLB returned $4.0B in 2025 ($1.6B dividends + $2.4B buybacks), using ~$6.3B of the $10B authorization through Q1'26. The dividend was raised 3.5% to $0.295/quarter in January 2026, following 3.6% in 2025 and 10% in 2024, a decelerating growth rate consistent with the softer environment. The dividend yields ~2.5% and is well-covered by cash flow.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth picture is negative. Organic revenue is contracting ~6-7%, and consensus estimates are falling across every window: current-quarter EPS cut 10.3% over 90 days, next-quarter −9.4%, current-year −6.5%, and even next-year down 1.3%. This is the opposite of the positive estimate-revision turn that would support a constructive growth call. Falling estimates alongside contracting organic revenue is a durable negative signal, and it directly caps the growth score.

The offsets are real but slow-developing:

- **Digital** grows organically at the fastest rate of the core divisions and carries the highest margin (~28%). Agentic AI, automation, and SaaS conversion are the structural bet within the core.
- **Production/recovery pivot** — ChampionX chemicals, artificial lift, and OneSubsea address a demand shift management describes as customers prioritizing incremental barrels "at the lowest cost." This is a less-cyclical revenue base than drilling, but the integration synergies remain unproven and the deal is currently EPS-dilutive.
- **Data Center Solutions** grew +121% YoY in 2025 and +45% YoY in Q1 2026. The July 14, 2026 Liberty Energy alliance — a commercial partnership (not an acquisition; SLB divested its former Liberty stake in 2023) delivering modular data-center infrastructure and integrated power to hyperscalers — is a Tier-1-confirmed development that plugs SLB into AI-driven infrastructure demand. It remains buried in "All Other" and is not yet a material revenue driver.

Management has repackaged the 2026 reset as bullish for 2027-2028, arguing the Middle East conflict accelerates supply rebalancing and supports higher post-conflict oil prices, which would drive reserve replenishment and activity recovery. That thesis is plausible but unproven and multi-quarter in nature. The realistic base case is that 2026 is a down year with recovery timing dependent on factors outside SLB's control.

## Valuation Assessment

At $47, SLB trades at ~20.7x forward earnings and ~2.5% dividend yield, against a peer median P/E of 27.0x and yield of 1.7%. On surface metrics the stock is cheap — Halliburton at 21.9x, Baker Hughes at 20.3x, FTI at 27.0x, with NOV and KGS distorted by trough earnings at 85x+.

The discount is not a clear mispricing. SLB's forward multiple sits on falling estimates, which means the effective multiple on stabilized earnings is higher than the headline suggests. Cheapness in a sector where the earnings base is actively deteriorating, and where estimate revisions are negative, is a warning flag rather than a buy signal. Across our work, low-multiple names without an independent catalyst have systematically underperformed; SLB's cheapness reflects a real earnings decline, not a market error.

The bull argument on valuation is that the market is pricing SLB as a pure legacy oil-services company in a downcycle and giving zero credit for the emerging AI-infrastructure optionality. At $47, near the low end of the recent range, sentiment does appear skewed toward the pessimistic read. If Data Center Solutions scales and the Liberty alliance produces material revenue, SLB could earn a partial re-rating. But that optionality is not yet large enough to anchor a valuation, and awarding it a premium multiple today would be speculative.

Analyst targets frame the range: consensus mean $60.97, median $62.00, high $71.00, low $40.00. The gap between the $47 price and the $61 mean implies ~30% upside if consensus is right, but consensus estimates are being cut, and targets typically lag reality in both directions. We treat the target dispersion as reflecting the same unresolved bull/bear debate rather than as independent confirmation of upside. On balance the valuation is fair-to-slightly-cheap but offers no asymmetric edge without a catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

SLB retains the strongest competitive position in oilfield services, competing on technology, service quality, and scale against Halliburton, Baker Hughes, and Weatherford. Its differentiation strategy — Digital software at ~28% margins, the production/recovery pivot via ChampionX and OneSubsea, and industrial adjacencies through data centers — is the most credible de-cyclicalization plan in the peer group.

The trajectory, however, is softening rather than improving. Well Construction, the flagship division, is down 11% and losing margin. The competitive-position scores in prior reports declined from 8.0 to 6.8, and the current downcycle plus Middle East demobilization keeps the trajectory neutral-to-negative in the near term. The moat is intact — no peer matches SLB's technology breadth or international footprint — but "market leader in a shrinking market" describes the current position better than "gaining share into a tailwind." The Data Center pivot is a genuine cross-sector move that leverages SLB's modular-manufacturing and rapid-deployment capabilities, and it distinguishes SLB from pure services peers, but it is early. We score competitive position 6.5 — a durable moat with a flat-to-softening trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**Middle East conflict (the dominant near-term risk).** The Middle East is ~70% of SLB's largest region (Middle East & Asia), which drove the Q1 margin collapse and reversed the 10-K's optimistic outlook. Management frames it as bullish for 2027-2028, but 2026 is a confirmed headwind with resolution timing dependent on the conflict itself.

**ChampionX integration execution.** The $4.9B all-stock deal (closed July 16, 2025) is central to the production/recovery thesis. Synergy-realization risk is explicitly flagged, integration charges continued into Q1 2026 ($41M), and the deal is currently diluting EPS ~9-10% while the business shrinks organically. This is a structural EPS headwind until synergies and a cyclical recovery arrive.

**Intangible/goodwill impairment.** With $21.8B of goodwill and intangibles (~83% of equity) and a Capturi impairment already taken, further write-downs are possible if new-energy or JV cash flows disappoint.

**Cyclicality and capex dependence.** Upstream spending fell ~5% in 2025; demand hinges on oil/gas prices and OPEC+ decisions. This is permanently priced sector risk, not a novel catalyst.

**Geopolitical/sanctions exposure.** Russia remains ~4% of revenue with ~$0.7B net assets at risk; residual Iran-related banking wind-down disclosed; tax/trade/FCPA exposure across 100+ jurisdictions.

**Cash flow deterioration.** The negative Q1 FCF and buyback cut are the first signs of pressure on the cash machine. This should recover seasonally, but the trend warrants monitoring against the reaffirmed >$4B return guidance.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM implied volatility of 42.6% sits ~9 points above 1-year historical volatility of 33.4% (rich), and the term structure is in backwardation (42.6% near-term vs. 37.2% at six months), which signals the market expects an imminent catalyst — most likely the Q2 2026 print. The put/call skew is balanced (−0.9% near-term, steepening modestly to −4.0% at six months), and put/call volume (0.20) and OI (0.47) ratios are not bearishly extreme. Unusual call buying appeared at the $48 and $50 July strikes (4.7x and 7.4x volume/OI), suggesting some positioning for a post-earnings bounce. ATM IV has collapsed from 66.0% to 42.6% since mid-June as the price fell 16.7%, indicating the mid-June volatility spike (tied to the Middle East escalation) has partially resolved. The net read: elevated but not extreme uncertainty into Q2 earnings, with balanced directional positioning, consistent with our balanced fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($60-71)

The 2026 reset is cyclical, not structural. A Middle East stabilization plus higher post-conflict oil prices drive a 2027-2028 upcycle with international activity recovery. ChampionX synergies materialize, reversing the dilution and lifting Production Systems margins. Digital continues compounding at ~28% margins, and Data Center Solutions scales via the Liberty alliance into a material, re-rating-worthy revenue stream. The cash engine normalizes, buybacks resume at scale, and SLB re-rates toward the peer median of 27x on recovering earnings. Consensus mean ($61) to high ($71) captures this scenario, implying ~30-51% upside.

### Bear Case ($40)

The Middle East disruption persists into 2027, organic revenue keeps contracting, and ChampionX synergies underdeliver while the dilution stays. Falling estimates continue, FCF pressure forces further buyback cuts, and a new-energy or ChampionX goodwill impairment hits the intangible-heavy balance sheet. Oil prices stay range-bound as OPEC+ manages an oversupplied market, keeping upstream capex flat-to-down. The stock derates toward the analyst low of $40, ~15% below current price.

The base case sits between: 2026 is a down year, the recovery is real but delayed, and the stock stays range-bound near current levels until estimates stabilize.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (preview circulating July 2026) is the immediate catalyst, and the backwardated options term structure confirms the market expects that print to move the stock. Beyond that, the H2 2026 Middle East activity trajectory and 2027 guidance are the key checkpoints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Current-year and next-quarter EPS estimate revisions turn positive over a 30-day window, confirming the earnings trough is in.
- Middle East activity re-mobilization confirmed in the Q2 or Q3 10-Q, with segment margins recovering (Well Construction back above 17%).
- Data Center Solutions / Liberty alliance produces disclosed material revenue or backlog large enough to warrant a partial infrastructure re-rating, combined with a pullback toward the low-$40s that restores asymmetry.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A further leg of estimate cuts (current-year EPS revised below $2.40) alongside a Q2 organic revenue decline steeper than Q1's −7%.
- A goodwill or intangible impairment on ChampionX or the new-energy portfolio.
- A dividend or shareholder-return guidance cut, signaling the cash engine is under sustained pressure rather than seasonal strain.

## Conclusion

SLB is a high-quality franchise caught in a genuine downcycle that worsened between its last two filings. The through-cycle cash generation, discount-to-peer multiple, and data-center optionality argue against selling; the falling estimates, negative organic growth, EPS dilution, Middle East demobilization, and absence of a near-term catalyst argue against buying. The prior four reports all landed on HOLD, and the current data does not justify breaking that pattern in either direction; the setup remains balanced with no confirmed turn.

We maintain **HOLD** with an **uncertain** rationale. This is not a conviction income hold (yield is modest at 2.5% and dividend growth is decelerating) nor a confident growth hold (estimates are falling, not rising). The risk/reward is genuinely two-sided, waiting on the Q2 print and Middle East stabilization to resolve. We would turn constructive on positive estimate revisions plus margin recovery, and negative on further estimate cuts or an impairment. The overall score of 5.4 reflects a solid franchise with deteriorating near-term fundamentals and a discounted but not asymmetrically attractive valuation.
