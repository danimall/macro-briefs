## Executive Summary

SLB is the largest oilfield services company in the world, and it is currently the energy name where the geopolitical premium in crude works against the equity rather than for it. Middle East revenue fell 13% sequentially in Q2 2026 to $1.66B and 12% year on year in the first half, a roughly $0.7B shortfall that management attributes to production shut-ins and security constraints. Consolidated H1 2026 revenue rose 4% to $17,693M, but excluding ChampionX it fell 6%, the second consecutive period of mid-single-digit organic contraction. Core divisional margins absorbed the damage: Well Construction down 401bp to 15%, Reservoir Performance down 208bp to 16%, Production Systems down 178bp to 15%.

Against that, the Q2 print delivered a real sequential improvement and the market repriced it, with the stock rising roughly 10% on July 24. Pretax income rose from $956M in Q1 to $1,019M in Q2, every division except the two Middle East-exposed service lines grew sequentially, and every geography grew except MENA. Two engines are compounding through the downcycle: Digital grew 14% in H1 to $1,337M with Q2 margin at 28% (up 683bp sequentially), and Data Center Solutions grew 33% sequentially to an implied ~$186M quarter, or roughly a $740M annualized run rate, after growing 121% in FY2025. ChampionX, acquired for 141M shares valued at $4.9B, contributed $1.7B of H1 revenue at an 18.1% pretax margin, above the 15% Production Systems divisional average.

Consensus is moving down while the stock moves up. Current-quarter EPS estimates have been cut 10.5% over 90 days, with 6.7% of that in the last 30 days, and current-year estimates are down 4.4%. At 20.8x 2026 consensus of $2.51 and 16.0x 2027 consensus of $3.26, the cheapness rests entirely on a 2027 recovery whose dominant variable, Middle East activity, is exogenous, unforecastable from the filings, and was the subject of a management recovery call in January 2026 that reversed within two quarters. This is a fifth consecutive HOLD; the justification is that the risk/reward from $52.19 is roughly 9% to base case against 23% to bear case, which is not a position worth initiating.

## Company Overview

SLB (formerly Schlumberger; the legal parent renamed to SLB Limited in 2025) operates in more than 100 countries with roughly 109,000 employees. Beginning Q3 2025 it reports four divisions plus All Other, with prior periods recast; any comparison to pre-2025 third-party segment data will be mis-mapped.

FY2025 revenue and pretax operating income by division:

| Division | Revenue | Pretax Op. Income | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Digital | $2,660M | $745M | 28.0% |
| Reservoir Performance | $6,820M | $1,250M | 18.3% |
| Well Construction | $11,856M | $2,248M | 19.0% |
| Production Systems | $13,325M | $2,184M | 16.4% |
| All Other | $1,987M | $498M | 25.1% |
| Eliminations & other | $(940)M | $(351)M | — |
| **Total** | **$35,708M** | | |

The large negative eliminations line is an artifact of SLB crediting Digital Operations revenue to both the Core division and Digital to incentivize both, then eliminating it in consolidation. It is not a related-party item.

The strategic architecture matters more than the segment table. Production Systems has grown from $9,831M (29.7% of revenue) in 2023 to $13,325M (37.3%) in 2025, while Well Construction has shrunk from $13,478M (40.7%) to $11,856M (33.2%). The mechanism management describes: with project economics challenged, customers prioritize incremental barrels from existing fields over new drilling, which shifts spend toward intervention, artificial lift, production chemicals, and subsea tiebacks. Production and recovery spending is structurally less cyclical than exploration and drilling spending because brownfield optimization continues when greenfield capex is cut. ChampionX (production chemicals plus artificial lift) is the largest single expression of that pivot.

Data Center Solutions sits inside All Other and is a manufacturing business: SLB builds modular data-center enclosures, cooling systems, and critical infrastructure hardware and sells it to hyperscalers and enterprises. SLB is a supplier. It does not own, operate, or develop the data centers, and the filings name no counterparty. No single customer exceeded 10% of consolidated revenue in 2023, 2024, or 2025.

## Financial Analysis

### The FY2025 inflection was genuine

| Revenue | 28,091 | 33,135 | 36,289 | 35,708 |
| Cost of revenue | 22,930 | 26,572 | 28,829 | 29,201 |
| R&D | 634 | 711 | 749 | 709 |

| Pretax income | 4,271 | 5,282 | 5,672 | 4,291 |
| Net income (SLB) | 3,441 | 4,203 | 4,461 | 3,374 |
| Operating cash flow | — | 6,637 | 6,602 | 6,489 |

Revenue fell 1.6% with five months of ChampionX included; excluding ChampionX's $1.5B contribution, organic revenue declined 6%. Pretax income fell 24% and net income fell 24.4%. Gross margin compressed 238bp, from 20.6% to 18.2%, part mix (ChampionX products carry lower gross margin) and part price deflation in Well Construction.

Charges and credits totaled $1,107M pretax / $836M net in FY2025, against $541M / $427M in 2024 and $110M pretax in 2023. Composition: $407M of workforce reductions across all four quarters, roughly $352M of merger and integration costs, $166M of ChampionX inventory step-up amortization, a $210M SLB Capturi goodwill impairment, and two equity-method impairments of $69M and $52M, offset by a $149M gain on the Palliser APS sale and a $92M deferred-tax valuation-allowance reversal. Adding back the $836M net to reported net income gives $4,210M, or $2.93 per diluted share on 1,437M weighted shares, versus GAAP diluted EPS of $2.35. That $2.93 is a construction from SLB's disclosed charges table, not SLB's published non-GAAP measure, and should not be compared against a published non-GAAP consensus.

### Cash flow is the strongest part of the story

SLB's own free-cash-flow definition subtracts capex, APS investments, and capitalized exploration data from operating cash flow. FY2025: $6,489M − $1,694M − $428M − $252M = $4,115M. That figure has been remarkably stable at $4,038M (2023), $3,990M (2024), $4,115M (2025) while revenue swung across a $3.2B range. Operating cash flow exceeded net income in every year. Working-capital drag fell to $60M in 2025 from $1,020M in 2024. Total capital investment was $2.4B in 2025, guided to roughly $2.5B for 2026, so capital intensity is about 7% of revenue and drifting down, consistent with the mix shift toward software and manufactured product.

On $77.4B of market capitalization, FY2025 FCF is a 5.3% yield. H1 2026 FCF was $693M on $1,846M of operating cash flow, which looks alarming until seasonality is applied: H1 2025 FCF of $725M was only 17.6% of the FY2025 total, and Q4 concentrates exploration-data, software, and product sales. Repeating that conversion ratio mechanically implies roughly $3.9B for FY2026, which is a sensitivity rather than a forecast.

Net debt rose $1,303M in H1 2026 because $1,099M of buybacks and $866M of dividends were funded against $693M of seasonal FCF. In May 2026 SLB issued $500M of 4.55% 2031s, $500M of 4.80% 2033s, and $1.0B of 5.15% 2036s, retiring the $500M 5.00% 2027s. Net debt to estimated FY2025 EBITDA of roughly $7.2–7.4B (D&A is estimated, not disclosed in the materials reviewed) is about 1.2x, comfortable. Liquidity is $4.1B of cash and short-term investments plus $5.0B of fully undrawn committed facilities, with no commercial paper outstanding.

Two balance-sheet facts deserve emphasis. First, interest expense is on a mechanical upward path independent of oil prices: $503M (2023) → $512M (2024) → $558M (2025), as sub-1% euro paper (0.25% 2027s, 0.50% 2031s, 2.00% 2032s) rolls into 4.55%–5.15% dollar issuance. Roughly $5.4B matures between 2026 and 2028; a 250–300bp average coupon step-up on that alone would be on the order of $135–160M of incremental annual interest, a coarse estimate that ignores swap terminations and the possibility SLB repays rather than refinances (its disclosed contractual interest payments actually decline from $387M in 2026 to $153M in 2030, implying assumed repayment).

Second, goodwill plus intangibles of $21,877M at June 30 equals 39.4% of total assets and 84% of SLB stockholders' equity, leaving tangible book value of roughly $4.2B. SLB used the qualitative impairment screen for all four Core reporting units in 2025, concluding fair value was "significantly greater" than carrying value, and ran a quantitative test only on SLB Capturi, which failed, producing the $210M impairment at a 14.75% discount rate. A sustained downcycle that forced quantitative testing where the goodwill actually sits would be equity-consuming.

One correction worth stating: a screening Altman Z-Score of 1.99 for SLB is defective, because it zeroes the EBIT term and substitutes book for market equity in X4. Recomputing on the public-firm specification with FY2025 balances and the current market capitalization gives approximately 3.13, in the safe zone. The score is price-sensitive (at a $40B market cap it would fall to roughly 2.35), and the genuine vulnerability is the intangible concentration rather than liquidity.

## Growth Analysis

Three distinct growth vectors, with very different verification levels.

**Digital.** Revenue grew 20% in 2024 and 9% in 2025, with pretax margin expanding 710bp then 291bp, then 14% in H1 2026 to $1,337M with margin up 93bp to 25% and Q2 standalone at 28% (up 683bp sequentially). Growing revenue and expanding margin through a downcycle in which Core margins compressed 200–400bp is the single cleanest piece of evidence for differentiation in the portfolio. Delfi/Lumi platform revenue, remote operations, and the licensed seismic data library are not directly indexed to rig count.

**Data Center Solutions.** Grew 121% in FY2025 (a $251M increase, implying roughly $458M for the year and $207M in FY2024) and 33% sequentially in Q2 2026 (a $46M increase, implying roughly $186M in the quarter, or about $740M annualized). Management calls it "our fastest growing business for years to come." What is speculative is SLB's share of the end market and the economics. DCS revenue, margin, backlog, contract duration, and customers are not separately disclosed; it sits inside All Other alongside APS and Capturi, whose FY2025 results moved for unrelated reasons (Palliser divestiture, Ecuador pipeline outage, Capturi project loss). An externally reported target of a run rate above $2B by end-2027 would require roughly tripling from the implied Q2 level in six quarters; it appears in no filing or company release reviewed and should not be treated as established. Hardware supply to hyperscalers carries concentrated buyers, price deflation risk, and no disclosed switching costs, unlike Digital's recurring-revenue profile.

**Cyclical recovery in the Core.** This is where the 2027 consensus lives, and it is where visibility is worst. Consensus 2027 EPS of $3.26 implies roughly 30% growth over 2026's $2.51, which requires Middle East normalization, Well Construction margin recovery from 15%, and a full year of ChampionX. Q2 offered supporting evidence: pretax income rose sequentially, Production Systems grew 7% sequentially to $3,771M with margin up 138bp on OneSubsea and artificial lift, North America grew 4% sequentially on production chemicals and US land, and the 10-Q notes Middle East activity "began to recover in certain countries as conditions improved" even as others remained constrained.

The offsetting evidence is the revision table:

|---|---|---|---|
| Current quarter | $0.62 | −6.7% | −10.5% |
| Next quarter | $0.80 | −4.6% | −7.7% |
| Current year | $2.51 | −2.3% | −4.4% |

Two-thirds of the 90-day current-quarter cut landed in the last 30 days, so the deterioration is accelerating rather than stabilizing, and it happened through and after the Q2 print. The strongest single argument against paying up here is that the stock rose roughly 10–11% since mid-July while the numbers behind it were being marked down.

## Valuation Assessment

At $52.19:

- Trailing GAAP P/E: 22.2x on $2.35 FY2025 diluted EPS. (The supplied profile lists both trailing and forward P/E at 24.20, which cannot both hold against a 2026 consensus of $2.51; I use computed figures.)
- 2026 consensus: 20.8x on $2.51.
- 2027 consensus: 16.0x on $3.26.
- On my constructed FY2025 adjusted EPS of $2.93: 17.8x.
- FY2025 FCF yield: 5.3%. EV/EBITDA roughly 11.5–12x on estimated EBITDA.
- Dividend: $1.18 annualized, 2.26% yield, covered roughly 3.5x by FY2025 FCF.
- Position in range: 11.3% below the $58.82 52-week high, 65% above the $31.64 low.

Peer medians in Oil & Gas Equipment & Services are 21.9x P/E and 1.5% yield, with wide dispersion (WFRD 15.8x, BKR 20.3x, HAL 21.9x, FTI 27.0x, NOV 85.3x). SLB's 22.2x trailing sits at a small premium to HAL and BKR; on 2026 estimates it is roughly in line with the group, and its yield is above the median.

The bull framing is that 16.0x 2027 against 30% expected earnings growth implies a PEG near 0.5, which looks mispriced. Three things temper that. First, the 30% growth is cyclical recovery contingent on an exogenous variable, not secular compounding, and PEG is a poor tool for a cyclical trough recovery. Second, the direction of estimate revision is negative, so the denominator in that PEG is being revised downward while the numerator rises; the historical pattern in which a low forward multiple identifies opportunity requires estimates to be rising, and they are not. Third, sell-side targets (mean $62.03, median $63, low $43, high $71) sit 19–21% above spot with a stable buy-weighted rating distribution (26 of 30 ratings at buy or better), but with estimates falling, those targets carry stale earnings inputs rather than independent confirmation.

My base-case fair value is $57, built as 18.5x a haircut 2027 EPS of $3.05 (discounting consensus for the revision trend and unresolved MENA path), which cross-checks against a 5.0% FCF yield on roughly $4.0B of normalized FCF (implying a $80B enterprise-adjusted equity value, or about $54–58 per share). That is 9.2% above the price basis. The valuation is fair rather than compelling, which is what a 5.5 score reflects: there is a plausible path to modest upside, but I cannot identify a specific thing the market is getting wrong that is not simply an unforecastable geopolitical outcome.

## Competitive Landscape

SLB's own filing is unusually candid about the basis of competition: "technological innovation, quality of service, and price differentiation," with "numerous competitors, both large and small," and the explicit statement that "no particular patent or group of patents is material to SLB's business." Management does not claim patent-based exclusivity. The moat, to the extent it exists, is scale, integration, installed base, and the software/data layer.

The empirical evidence for that moat is modest but real, and it takes the form of outperforming the underlying spending environment: international revenue ex-Aker grew 7% in FY2024 while upstream investment and rig activity grew less; North America revenue ex-ChampionX fell only 2% in FY2025 against a 5% drop in North American upstream spending; and Digital grew with margin expansion through two down years. Against that, Well Construction margin fell 220bp in FY2025 and 401bp in H1 2026, which establishes that the Core has no pricing insulation when activity contracts.

Two structural points on positioning. The production/recovery mix shift is a cyclicality argument, not a diversification argument: SLB's customer concentration measure is unchanged and already benign (no customer above 10%), while the concentration that actually caused the H1 shortfall was geographic, with Middle East and Asia generating $5,257M, or 29.7% of H1 revenue. The Data Center Solutions adjacency is genuinely unusual in the sector; no direct OFS peer has disclosed a comparable business, which makes it the one place SLB could plausibly earn a multiple premium if it were disclosed separately and the margins proved out.

Relative geographic exposure versus Halliburton, Baker Hughes, TechnipFMC, Weatherford, and NOV was not examined at the financial-statement level; the peer comparison above rests on the supplied multiples only. Aker Solutions and Subsea7 are minority JV partners in OneSubsea (SLB 70%) and Aker Carbon Capture in Capturi (SLB 80%), not competitors.

Trajectory summary: improving in Digital, production-linked services, and DCS; deteriorating in drilling-linked services; net stable-to-modestly-improving, which supports a 6.0 rather than anything higher.

## Risk Assessment

**Middle East concentration, currently materializing.** The largest single geographic block, 29.7% of H1 revenue, fell 12% year on year and 13% sequentially in Q2 on production shut-ins and security constraints. The January 2026 10-K told investors "we expect rig activity in the Middle East to increase compared to today's level, and our footprint in the region puts us in a strong position to benefit from this recovery." Within two quarters that footprint became the primary drag. This argues for wide scenario bands rather than point estimates, and it explains why Q3 guidance was reportedly framed as a "base-case scenario."

**Goodwill and intangible concentration.** $21.9B against $26.1B of book equity; tangible book roughly $4.2B. The Core reporting units were tested only qualitatively in 2025.

**Rising interest expense.** Mechanical, multi-year, independent of the oil cycle, in the range of $135–160M annually on 2026–2028 maturities on a coarse coupon-differential basis.

**Russia.** Roughly 4% of FY2025 revenue and about $0.7B of net assets, with the 10-K explicitly flagging uncertainty over the ability to repatriate cash. Reported inside Europe & Africa, so not separately visible in the geographic table.

**Percentage-of-completion estimation risk, already realized once.** Over-time revenue rose from 6% (2023) to 11% (2025) as subsea and process-module work grew, and SLB disclosed a "significant loss on one particular project in SLB Capturi." As that book grows, single-project estimate revisions become a larger earnings variable.

**Capital returns at essentially 100% of FCF.** FY2025 returns of $4,016M against $4,115M of FCF; a commitment to return more than $4B in 2026; $3.03B of buyback authorization remaining at June 30. There is limited slack to fund a simultaneous downcycle and maintained returns without further borrowing.

**Conditional Capturi obligation.** After a three-year lock-up, Aker Carbon Capture may put its 20% stake to SLB at fair value with a NOK 1.0bn floor. The floor means SLB's minimum outlay for the remainder of a business it just impaired is not contingent on that business improving. Conditional, not current debt.

**Smaller items.** Legacy Iranian depository accounts at Bank Saderat and Bank Tejarat held against pre-2013 NIOC receivables (a persistent sanctions-adjacent disclosure); cybersecurity exposure that scales directly with the Digital thesis given remote wellsite operation; ChampionX integration risk added as a new risk factor in the FY2025 10-K with $110M of integration charges in H1 2026 on top of roughly $352M in 2025.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 41.9% against 34.7% one-year realized looks rich, and the term structure is nominally backwardated (41.9% near versus 31.1% at six months). Most of that near-term reading is one-day expiry mechanics: the 1-month tenor is 33.0% and the 3-month 33.3%, both close to realized vol, so the market is not pricing an imminent dislocation. Positioning is call-tilted, with a 0.32 put/call volume ratio and 0.40 put/call open-interest ratio, and it became more call-tilted through the post-earnings rally (OI ratio 0.47 to 0.40 as the price rose 11.2%, with ATM IV slightly lower at 41.9% from 42.6%). Put/call skew values are 2.4% at one week and negative at longer tenors; the sign convention is not defined in the supplied data, so I report the values without inferring direction. Short interest of 4.9% of float and 3.9 days to cover indicate an uncrowded position with no squeeze dynamic.

Options positioning corroborates the post-earnings enthusiasm rather than challenging it, and it does not flag the estimate-revision deterioration that is the central concern in this report. Positioning is not independent evidence for the thesis in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $68 (+30% from $52.19)

Middle East activity normalizes through H2 2026 and 2027, restoring roughly $1.4B of annualized revenue at Core margins and creating an easy comparison base. Well Construction margin recovers from 15% toward the 19% FY2025 level as regional volume returns and pricing stabilizes. Digital continues compounding at low-double-digit growth with margins holding near 28%, and Data Center Solutions scales toward the reported $2B run-rate ambition, at which point it would be roughly the size of today's Digital division and would justify a mix-driven multiple premium versus pure oilfield peers. ChampionX holds its 18.1% margin and contributes a full year. 2027 EPS reaches or exceeds the $3.26 consensus, and the multiple expands toward 20x on improved earnings quality and a less hydrocarbon-levered revenue mix. Sell-side targets clustered at $62–63 with a high of $71 support this framing.

### Bear Case — $40 (−23% from $52.19)

Middle East disruption persists or recurs, holding MENA revenue near the depressed $1.66B quarterly level. Organic revenue contracts for a third consecutive period, Core margins stay near 15%, and 2027 EPS lands near $2.40 rather than $3.26. At 16.5x that is roughly $40. Two amplifiers: a sustained downcycle forces quantitative goodwill testing on the Core reporting units where $17.0B of goodwill sits against $4.2B of tangible book, and the commitment to return more than $4B annually against a shortfall in FCF adds debt at 4.55%–5.15% coupons while interest expense is already rising mechanically.

### What would resolve the disagreement

A single disclosure would move this materially: MENA quarterly revenue direction over the next two prints. Secondarily, whether SLB begins to disclose Data Center Solutions economics separately. Until either arrives, the dominant variable is not forecastable from the available evidence.

## Conclusion

SLB is a high-quality, well-financed franchise trading at a fair price in a business where the primary earnings driver is currently outside management's control. The favorable evidence is concrete: $4.1B of free cash flow held stable across three years of swinging revenue, Digital compounding at 28% Q2 margins through a downcycle, ChampionX earning an 18.1% pretax margin above the divisional average, price-sensitive buyback execution (60.0M shares at an average $40.23 in 2025 slowing to 21.2M at $51.92 in H1 2026), $9.1B of liquidity, and a Data Center Solutions business at roughly a $740M annualized run rate.

The unfavorable evidence is equally concrete and more immediate: organic revenue has contracted 6% in each of the last two reporting periods; Core divisional margins fell 178–401bp in H1; consensus EPS has been cut across every horizon, with two-thirds of the 90-day current-quarter reduction arriving in the last 30 days; goodwill and intangibles equal 84% of book equity against $4.2B of tangible book with only qualitative testing on the Core units; interest expense is rising mechanically as sub-1% euro paper refinances near 5%; and distributions are running at essentially 100% of free cash flow, debt-funded at the margin in the first half. The stock has risen roughly 10–11% since mid-July while the earnings estimates supporting it were marked down.

That combination yields a fifth consecutive HOLD at a modestly higher score than the prior read, reflecting the confirmed Q2 sequential improvement and ChampionX economics, offset by worsening revisions and a less attractive entry price. Existing holders are compensated by a 2.26% dividend covered roughly 3.5x by free cash flow and a plausible path to $57. New capital should wait either for the Middle East to show two quarters of sequential improvement, for revisions to turn, or for a price in the mid-$40s.
