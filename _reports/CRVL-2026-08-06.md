## Executive Summary

CorVel is a nationwide provider of medical cost containment and managed care services to payers of workers' compensation, auto liability and group health claims. Fiscal 2026 (ended 31 March 2026) produced record revenue of $958.5M (+7.0%), record net income of $110.3M (+15.9%) and record diluted EPS of $2.14 (+16.9%), and a fiscal-year total return of **−51.2%** against +24.8% for the Nasdaq Composite. The 10-K performance graph (index 327.43 → 159.81) and the proxy pay-versus-performance table (TSR index 86 vs peer group 125) both confirm the magnitude. Back-solving from the March 2026 repurchase average of $53.26 implies the shares traded near $109 at 31 March 2025, roughly 60x FY2025 EPS of $1.83. Today they trade at 27.6x FY2026 EPS. The P/E roughly halved while EPS rose 17%.

The trigger is identifiable. The Q3 FY2026 10-Q (filed 5 February 2026) showed revenue +3.4%, net income +1.7% and patient-management revenue essentially flat at +0.1%. In a business where BLS occupational injury counts remain 13% below 2019 and *fell* in 2024 versus 2023, flat patient-management revenue reads as the share-gain engine stalling. The company's own monthly repurchase disclosure captures the reaction: average price paid fell from $69.19 in January 2026 to $49.78 in February.

Two quarters later, that specific deterioration has reversed. Derived Q4 FY2026 revenue grew 7.4% with pretax income +19.2% and G&A down 6.3%. Externally reported Q1 FY2027 (5 August 2026) showed revenue ~$260M (+11%), net income $32.2M (+18%), **claims volume +8% — the largest year-over-year quarterly increase since December 2024** — and new-customer sales up more than 60% year over year with trailing-twelve-month new sales +40%. That is the correct set of leading indicators for a company whose entire growth must come from share gain rather than market growth.

We are not upgrading, for three reasons that are arithmetic rather than atmospheric. First, roughly $0.07 of the $0.63 Q1 EPS came from one-time CERIS items, primarily a settlement of legacy accounts receivable; ex-item net income growth was approximately 5%, not 18%. Second, at $59.10 the stock is ~24x our FY2027 estimate with a 3.4-3.7% free cash flow yield, which produces base-case upside near 15% against bear-case downside near 24%. Third, the Q1 FY2027 10-Q is not yet available in the EDGAR index, so the volume and new-sales figures that carry the upgrade case rest on call commentary rather than filed statements. **HOLD, growth_hold.** Overall score rises to 6.5 from 6.2, continuing a trajectory of 5.3 → 6.1 → 6.4 → 6.2, with the increase located in growth prospects and risk factors.

## Company Overview

CorVel operates a single managed-care segment, incorporated in Delaware in 1987 and now headquartered in Fort Worth, Texas. All revenue and long-lived assets are domestic. Customers are insurance carriers, third-party administrators, self-insured employers, government agencies, municipalities and state funds.

Revenue splits into two service lines:

| Service line | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 | FY26 mix | FY26 growth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Patient management | $529.995M | $581.192M | $596.703M | 62.3% | +2.7% |
| Network solutions | $265.316M | $314.397M | $361.824M | 37.7% | +15.1% |
| **Total** | **$795.311M** | **$895.589M** | **$958.527M** | | **+7.0%** |

Patient management is labor-intensive: claims administration and TPA services, telephonic and field case management, 24/7 nurse triage, utilization management, vocational rehabilitation. Network solutions is technology-leveraged: automated fee auditing, facility and inpatient bill review, PPO management, pharmacy, the CareIQ directed-care network, Medicare Section 111 reporting and set-asides, and clearinghouse services. Named assets include CareMC and CareMC Edge, **CERIS** (facility bill review and payment integrity), Symbeo (document capture and AP automation) and CareIQ. The PPO network exceeds 1.2 million providers; the bill review engine incorporates more than 100 million rules.

Two accounting features matter for interpreting the numbers. CorVel is the **principal**, not agent, for third-party pharmacy and directed-care services — it recognizes gross revenue with partner fees in cost of revenues. The 24.3% FY2026 gross margin is therefore not comparable to a pure-services or software business; a material share of the ~76% cost line is pass-through provider cost. Second, medical bill review revenue is **variable consideration**, estimated contract-by-contract from historical averages. That is the largest estimation judgment in the accounts, and it sits inside the fastest-growing service line.

Headcount was 5,239 at 31 March 2026, with 93% remote or hybrid, a structural reason occupancy expense is falling in absolute terms.

## Financial Analysis

### Margin expansion is labor productivity, and it is real but contested

| % of revenue | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 21.59% | 23.42% | **24.29%** |
| G&A | 9.63% | 9.93% | 9.36% |
| Pretax margin | 11.96% | 13.49% | **14.93%** |
| Net margin | 9.59% | 10.63% | **11.51%** |
| Effective tax rate | 19.82% | 21.24% | 22.91% |

The segment expense disclosure isolates the driver. Labor expense went $406.4M → $445.7M (+9.7%) → **$460.6M (+3.3%)**, falling from 51.1% to 48.1% of revenue, against average headcount up roughly 1%. Occupancy fell 5.1% on 7.0% revenue growth. Labor's +3.3% also absorbed a near-doubling of 401(k) contributions ($1.696M → $3.294M); excluding that, labor grew closer to 2.9%.

Working the other way, **direct product expense grew 9.3%** to $215.5M, faster than revenue — the gross-revenue pass-through of pharmacy and directed care is structurally lower-margin. FY2026 netted 87bp of gross-margin expansion and 144bp of pretax expansion. This is a tug-of-war between labor leverage and mix, not a one-way trend, and one alternative reading deserves weight: FY2025 front-loaded hiring at +9.7%, so FY2026's +3.3% may be partly the digestion of that build rather than a permanent productivity step.

CorVel's income statement carries no separate operating income or interest income line — gross profit less G&A equals pretax income exactly ($232.863M − $89.732M = $143.131M). Any interest earned on the ~$200M+ average cash balance is embedded in the reported lines, which flatters the apparent operating margin trend to an unquantifiable degree.

### Cash generation is high quality

| | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operating cash flow | $99.237M | $127.315M | **$155.640M** |
| Capex (cash) | $29.240M | $35.755M | $45.357M |
| FCF | $69.997M | $91.560M | **$110.283M** |
| Capex/revenue | 3.68% | 3.99% | **4.73%** |

OCF/net income was 1.41x, and it was not manufactured by working capital. Restricted customer deposits appear identically as a current asset ($115.706M) and an accrued liability; both rose $14.234M, netting to approximately zero in the cash flow statement. Stripping the pair, accrued liabilities contributed roughly +$2.0M to operating cash flow and receivables +$2.7M. Two items a screen would miss: an **$8.485M software license acquired under a finance agreement** (non-cash, so economic capex was closer to $53.8M and adjusted FCF ≈ $101.8M, 10.6% of revenue), and a stock-funded technology acquisition ($4.284M / 41,605 shares).

Capex now exceeds D&A by $13.6M ($45.357M vs $31.764M). Capitalized internal-use software stands at $49.6M net against $185.9M accumulated amortization on $270.4M gross. This reinvestment is mandatory rather than optional, and the intensity step from 3.7% to 4.7% of revenue in two years is consistent with management treating the bill-review technology stack as contested.

Receivable quality improved: unbilled receivables fell from $46.5M to $37.8M (41.6% → 36.0% of gross receivables) on 7% revenue growth, and DSO fell about five days to the 39-day area, holding at 39 days in Q1 FY2027.

### Balance sheet

At 31 March 2026: cash $233.072M (reported $256M at 30 June 2026); net receivables $101.313M; current assets $462.297M against current liabilities $228.068M (current ratio 2.03, working capital $234.2M); total assets $642.986M; equity $394.231M; goodwill only $36.814M (5.7% of assets). **No interest-bearing debt** — the 10-K notes essentially none for 35 years. The only debt-like items are $28.107M of operating lease liabilities (4.23-year weighted term, 5.3% discount rate). A "0.63 debt/equity" screening figure is total liabilities to equity, roughly 47% of which is the offsetting customer-deposit balance.

Retained earnings of $1.013B against $394.2M equity, with $867.5M of treasury stock, is the arithmetic record of three decades of repurchase.

ROE is 28.0%; return on average equity 30.8%; ROA 17.2%. Invested capital net of cash is only $161.2M, implying returns on operating capital comfortably above 60%. Reported ROE understates the operating business because of the idle cash. Standardized distress screens mislead here: an Altman Z of 3.74 was computed with an EBIT/assets term of zero (an artifact of the missing operating-income tag); substituting pretax income/assets (0.2226) lifts Z to roughly **4.47**. Similarly, the two failed Piotroski signals — declining ROA (17.16% vs 17.43%) and declining asset turnover (1.491 vs 1.640) — are denominator effects: of the $97.0M increase in total assets, $76.7M (79%) was cash and restricted customer deposits.

### Earnings-surprise record: unusable

The supplied quarterly EPS series reconciles to derived fiscal quarters ($0.61 = Q4 FY2026, $0.47 = Q3 FY2026, $0.46 = Q2 FY2026) but the period labels are offset and no estimates are attached. No beat/miss record can be established from it, and no sell-side price targets are present in the data. Confidence in forward consensus is therefore lower than for a widely covered name, which is one reason we weight the company's own filed and derived quarterly path heavily.

## Growth Analysis

The quarterly sequence is the case:

| Quarter | Revenue | YoY | Patient mgmt YoY | Network sol. YoY | Net income YoY |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q3 FY26 (filed) | $235.625M | **+3.4%** | **+0.1%** | +9.1% | +1.7% |
| Q4 FY26 (derived) | $248.548M | +7.4% | +2.7% | +15.7% | +17.5% |
| Q1 FY27 (reported) | ~$260M | **+11%** | — | — | +18% |

The Q4 derivation is reproducible from filings: revenue $958.527M − $709.979M = $248.548M against $895.589M − $664.075M = $231.514M (+7.36%); net income $110.344M − $79.315M = $31.029M against $95.165M − $68.746M = $26.419M (+17.45%); Q4 gross margin 25.35% with G&A of $23.293M, down 6.3% year over year.

What changed in composition matters as much as the rate. FY2026's growth engine was **revenue per bill** — enhanced bill review services sold into existing accounts, which management stated explicitly. Attach-rate expansion on the installed base is finite. Q1 FY2027 introduced two new engines: the enterprise claims TPA program driving patient management (which had been the flat line), and new-customer sales up more than 60% year over year, nearly half of that in June alone, with RFP volume and average opportunity value both improved. Patient management and network solutions each contributed roughly $12.5M of the revenue growth — a balanced quarter rather than one line carrying the other.

Claims volume +8% is the single most important datapoint, because CorVel's end market does not grow. BLS occupational injury counts: 2.69M (2019) → 2.11M (2020) → 2.24M → 2.34M → 2.37M → **2.34M (2024)**. Volume declined in 2024 and sits 13% below 2019. The 10-K concedes: "Despite fewer claims to administrate... the Company has been able to overcome the decrease with an increase in market share."

The strategic option is **CERIS moving into group health payment integrity**, a materially larger addressable market than workers' compensation bill review, with management citing health plans under cost and regulatory pressure and consolidation among regional plans. Two new offerings launched in Q1 FY2027: Executive Advisory Services (benchmarking and analytics consulting, positioned as an account-expansion wedge) and a data-driven medical bill repricing capability for out-of-network services and states with limited fee schedules. Management also flagged longer-term potential to apply CERIS to selected government healthcare programs, explicitly contingent on demand, program requirements and returns. If CERIS genuinely diversifies CorVel out of workers' compensation, a mid-20s multiple is comfortable. If it remains workers'-comp-adjacent, it is not.

The offset is the earnings arithmetic. Management disclosed approximately $0.07 per share of one-time CERIS items in Q1, primarily a settlement involving legacy accounts receivable. At ~51.1M shares that is ~$3.58M, or 11% of the quarter's $32.2M net income. Prior-year Q1 net income was $32.2M/1.18 ≈ $27.3M; ex-item Q1 FY2027 ≈ $28.6M, **+4.9% year over year**, with ex-item EPS ≈ $0.56 (+7.7%). Whether the $0.07 is pre- or post-tax is not clear from the summary; if pre-tax, the net effect is ~$2.8M and ex-item growth ~7%. Either way, headline earnings acceleration was substantially non-recurring while revenue acceleration was clean. The allowance rollforward corroborates a real legacy-collection story inside CERIS: additions of $3.530M in FY2025 followed by **$6.117M of deductions in FY2026**, taking the allowance from $7.689M to $3.969M.

We score growth prospects 6.2, up modestly. The revenue and volume reacceleration is genuine; underlying earnings growth in the most recent quarter was mid-single-digit.

On quality-adjusted grounds CorVel screens better than most of the set: 28% ROE, zero borrowings, 1.41x cash conversion, >60% returns on operating capital. On growth-adjusted grounds it screens worse than the multiple implies: 24x forward against low-to-mid-teens EPS growth is a PEG near 1.7, and the 3.4-3.7% FCF yield offers no absolute support in a regime where the 10-year sits at [unverified] and the September policy path is a live two-sided question.

The specific mispricing argument is that the market removed a growth multiple in February 2026 for a specific reason — decelerating claims volume and flat patient management — and that reason has since reversed while the stock remains 36.9% below its 52-week high of $93.71 and below the $69.19 January 2026 repurchase average. The counter-argument is that ~60x was the anomaly and mid-20s is fair rather than cheap for a company whose unit volume base is shrinking, whose own filings describe "considerable price competition in a flat-to-declining workers' compensation market," and whose most recent underlying earnings growth was ~5%. On balance the derating looks largely complete rather than overshot. Valuation scores 6.0: a modest positive with an identified catalyst, not a compelling discount.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K is unusually direct. Both service-line markets are "fragmented and competitive," competitors include national managed care providers, PPO networks, local independents, carriers, HMOs and TPAs, and "many of our current and potential competitors are significantly larger and have greater financial and marketing resources than we do." CorVel's differentiation claim is integration — claims administration, clinical services, pharmacy, bill review, network access and ancillary care on one stack, marketed as "CorVel Connected" — rather than scale or price.

Three structural pressures deserve weight:

**Customer in-sourcing and carrier consolidation.** Large workers' compensation carriers already offer managed care in-house; the filing states consolidation "may result in carriers performing more of such services in-house."

**Self-cannibalization**, disclosed with rare candor: early intervention services reduce claim length and total cost, so "as healthcare payors continue to increase their utilization of early intervention services, the revenue from our later stage network and healthcare management services will decrease." CorVel sells both ends.

**Technology obsolescence.** The company warns its IT resources "may become obsolete... potentially at substantial costs" and that "disruptive technologies... could impact the volume and pricing of our products," alongside rising regulatory scrutiny of machine learning and AI in insurance. Our inference, not the company's: a 100-million-rule bill review engine is simultaneously the principal moat and the asset most exposed to an LLM-native entrant that could approach the same adjudication problem without three decades of accumulated rule-writing. The capex step from 3.7% to 4.7% of revenue is consistent with an arms race.

Trajectory is nonetheless positive at the margin: share gain in a flat market (claimed in the filing, consistent with the revenue path), new-customer sales +60%, and CERIS extending into a larger adjacent market. Competitive position scores 6.0 — improving trajectory against a candidly weaker structural position.

We make no factual claims about named private competitors or their relative shares; external verification attempts on that point returned nothing usable.

## Risk Assessment

**Contract terminability and receivable concentration.** Most customer contracts permit either party to terminate without cause, typically on one-year auto-renewing terms. No single customer reached 10% of revenue in FY2024-FY2026, but **two customers each exceeded 10% of accounts receivable at four consecutive measurement dates** (3/31/24, 3/31/25, 12/31/25, 3/31/26) with no improvement. These measure different things and should not be conflated: the P&L is not dominated by one relationship, but at least two are large enough on the balance sheet to matter, and the service-line mix shift (patient management 66.6% → 62.3%) is product diversification, not customer diversification.

**Provider litigation against bill review and PPO programs.** The filing discloses that providers have brought individual and class actions against CorVel *and its customers* challenging cost-containment programs. This attaches to precisely the line now driving growth (CERIS), and to the new out-of-network repricing product entering the most litigated corner of payer-provider disputes. Item 3 and Note 6 currently state no proceeding with a probable and estimable material loss.

**One-time item comparability.** The $0.07 CERIS settlement creates a difficult Q1 FY2028 comparison and, more immediately, makes any forward multiple struck off $0.63 misleading.

**Verification gap.** The Q1 FY2027 10-Q does not appear in the available EDGAR index. Claims volume +8%, new sales +60%, FCF $43.1M and cash $256M are externally reported call and wire summaries, not filed statements. The entire upgrade case rests on this data.

**Cybersecurity**, with an explicit admission: "we have experienced, and are continually at risk of being subject to, attacks that result in cybersecurity incidents," across protected health information in 39 states.

**Governance and audit.** Leadership turned over twice in nine months: CFO Brian Nichols effective 10 September 2025, then Sarah Scott as President & CEO announced 1 June 2026 with Michael Combs becoming Executive Chairman after what management described as more than 18 months of board succession planning. Orderly, and it defuses the key-person risk factor that named Combs directly, though the 10-K filed 22 May 2026 still named him as Chairman/CEO/President and disclosed no pending transition, ten days before the 8-K. The auditor is Haskell & White LLP, a small California regional firm, for a ~$1B revenue issuer with a $3B market capitalization; it issued a clean ICFR attestation and management concluded controls were effective. Unusual for the size; not by itself evidence of deficiency. The board has six members with several long tenures.

**Short interest** is 9.4% of float with 4.7 days to cover — elevated for a small-cap healthcare services name and consistent with an active bear case on the volume-trap thesis.

Risk factors score 5.8, up from 5.5. Volume reacceleration reduces the most acute risk (that share gain had permanently stalled), while litigation exposure on the growth line, receivable concentration and the AI-erosion tail remain unpriced tails rather than sector truisms.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 69.7% against 44.5% one-year realized (spread +25.2pp, 68th percentile within the chain), with the term structure in **backwardation** (69.7% at 14 days versus 43.0% at six months) and a 22.5% put/call skew described as elevated put demand. ATM IV rose from 48.4% to 61.3% since the prior snapshot on a +1.4% price move. Taken at face value that would signal imminent stress.

We discount it heavily. The chain contains **41 contracts across four expirations**, the put/call volume ratio prints 0.00 (no put volume at all), and the put/call open-interest ratio is unavailable. Elevated skew with zero put volume is more consistent with stale or wide quotes in an illiquid chain than with informed positioning. The near-term expiry (21 August) sits *after* the 5 August earnings release, so the usual event-vol explanation for backwardation does not apply, which in a liquid chain would be meaningful and in a 41-contract chain is mostly noise. We flag the reading, note it does not corroborate the constructive tilt in our fundamental view, and assign it low confidence. It is not a reason to change the recommendation in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The February 2026 derating priced a permanent stall in share gain. Q4 FY2026 and Q1 FY2027 contradict that: revenue +7.4% then +11%, claims volume +8% (best since December 2024), new-customer sales +60% with TTM new sales +40%, RFP volume and average opportunity value both up, and patient management revived from +0.1% on the enterprise TPA program. Labor productivity is compounding — revenue +7.0% against labor +3.3% on roughly flat headcount — and the 93% remote structure is pulling occupancy down in absolute dollars. Cash conversion is 1.41x with no working-capital assistance. Capital allocation has been genuinely counter-cyclical: 366,306 shares repurchased at ~$55 in the March quarter (the year's largest buyback at the year's lowest prices), 240,933 at $56.44 in April-May, 377,098 at ~$57.81 in the June quarter. CERIS extending into group health payment integrity opens a market multiples larger than workers' compensation bill review. On FY2028 EPS of ~$2.85 at 30x, the shares reach the mid-$80s.

### Bear Case

The unit base is shrinking: BLS injury counts fell in 2024 and sit 13% below 2019, and the company itself describes considerable price competition in a flat-to-declining market against larger, better-resourced competitors. Growth is therefore always borrowed from someone, and CorVel discloses that its own early-intervention products shrink the downstream pool its later-stage services monetize. Most contracts terminate without cause on one-year terms. The most recent quarter's underlying earnings growth was ~5%, with 11% of net income from a legacy-receivable settlement. Direct product expense is growing faster than revenue, capping the margin story. The growth engine faces active provider litigation over cost-containment programs, and the rule-based bill review moat is the asset most exposed to LLM-native entry. Insiders have sold consistently through a drawdown with no open-market purchases, and the outgoing CEO now holds 12,322 shares (~$728K at $59.10). At 24x forward and a 3.4% FCF yield, multiple support is thin. A relapse to 3-4% revenue growth on a 20x multiple applied to $2.25 puts the stock at $45, near the $44.83 52-week low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** the filed Q1 FY2027 10-Q (expected within weeks of the 5 August release) is the first checkpoint, and Q2 FY2027 results in early November 2026 are the decisive one — the first quarter with no known one-time item and a clean read on whether claims volume growth and new-customer momentum persist.

**Upgrade to BUY requires two of the following three:**
1. **Filed confirmation plus persistence of volume.** The Q1 10-Q verifies claims volume growth, and Q2 FY2027 delivers claims volume up at least 5% year over year with revenue growth of 9% or better and no material non-recurring contribution to earnings.
2. **CERIS group-health traction disclosed with numbers.** Management quantifying group-health payment integrity revenue as a distinct, growing contributor — evidence that diversification out of the workers' compensation volume trap is actually occurring rather than being described.
3. **Price at or below $52-54,** which would put the shares near 21-22x our $2.44 FY2027 underlying estimate and roughly at the March 2026 repurchase average, restoring 25%+ base-case upside.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
1. Revenue growth decelerating back below 5% year over year, or claims volume turning negative, in any quarter — the February 2026 pattern repeating would establish that the reacceleration was a comp effect.
2. Gross margin compressing below 23% for two consecutive quarters, indicating direct product pass-through and price competition overwhelming labor leverage.
3. A material adverse development in provider litigation against bill review or PPO repricing programs — specifically, any accrual recorded or class certified against CorVel's cost-containment methodology.
4. Loss of one of the two customers each representing >10% of accounts receivable, or disclosure that a customer has moved services in-house.

**Reference targets used above (basis $59.10):** base $68 (27.5x FY2027E $2.47), +15.1%; bull $85 (30x FY2028E ~$2.85), +43.8%; bear $45 (20x $2.25), −23.9%.

## Conclusion

CorVel is a high-return, debt-free, cash-generative business that was priced at roughly 60x earnings a year ago and is priced at 27.6x trailing and ~24x forward today, after a fiscal year in which EPS rose 17%. The derating had a specific cause and that cause has, on the available evidence, reversed: revenue growth has gone 3.4% → 7.4% → 11%, claims volume posted its best year-over-year quarter since December 2024, and new-customer sales rose more than 60%. Capital allocation deserves credit — the largest repurchases were executed at the lowest prices — and compensation is modest and demonstrably linked to shareholder outcomes, with CEO compensation actually paid down 78% alongside the share price.

Three things keep this at HOLD rather than BUY. The most recent quarter's earnings acceleration was roughly 60% non-recurring, leaving underlying growth near 5%. The end market's unit volume is shrinking, so every dollar of growth must be taken from a competitor or extracted from an existing account, and the company discloses that its own best products cannibalize its downstream revenue. And the arithmetic at $59.10 offers about 15% to base case against 24% to bear case, which is not the asymmetry that justifies new capital in a business whose growth-reacceleration evidence is not yet in a filed statement.
