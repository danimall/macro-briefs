**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Costco is one of the highest-quality operating businesses in U.S. retail, and the Q3 FY2026 data confirms the model is compounding rather than stalling. Total revenue reached $70.5B (+12% YoY), comparable sales ran +10% (+7% ex-fuel/FX), digitally-enabled comps grew 21-22%, and membership fees rose ~11% off a 92.2% U.S./Canada renewal rate. Net income of $2,192M ($4.93 diluted EPS, +15% YoY) extended a multi-year record of steady expansion. Operating cash flow of $11.1B over 36 weeks against $20.0B of cash and only ~$5.7B of long-term debt puts the balance sheet in the top decile of the S&P 500. The business has a wide moat, a fortress balance sheet, accelerating traffic, and a slow-release membership-fee tailwind still phasing through the deferred-revenue base.

The problem is entirely price. At ~$935, Costco trades at roughly 47x trailing and forward earnings for a company growing revenue high-single-to-low-double digits and EPS in the mid-teens. That is a premium the company itself flags in its 10-K, stating outright that the stock "reflects high market expectations" and that any shortfall "could cause the price of our stock to decline." Estimate revisions are flat (+0.5% current-year, +0.8% next-year over 90 days) and the company has now missed consensus in three of its last four quarters, including the Q3 report that still delivered 15% EPS growth. The gap between operational excellence and the multiple required to sustain the current price is the entire debate.

We maintain a HOLD. The business merits ownership for its durable compounding, and the recent pullback from a $1,096.50 high to ~$935 has modestly compressed the multiple, but ~47x forward earnings offers no margin of safety and no identifiable catalyst for re-rating higher. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the declining score trajectory (7.2 in February to 6.1 in June) reflects a valuation that has stayed rich while the marginal earnings growth rate settled into the mid-teens. We would become materially more constructive on a reset toward the low-$800s, where the forward multiple approaches high-30s and the compounding math starts to work in a buyer's favor.

## Company Overview

Costco operates 928 membership warehouse clubs worldwide as of the Q3 FY2026 filing (May 10, 2026): 637 in the U.S., 115 in Canada, and 176 across 12 other countries, staffed by ~341,000 employees. The model is deliberately austere: fewer than 4,000 in-warehouse SKUs versus tens of thousands at broadline retailers, bulk purchasing, cross-dock depots, and no-frills 147,000 sq-ft buildings. High inventory turnover lets Costco frequently sell goods before it must pay suppliers, generating a structural negative-working-capital advantage that funds growth internally.

Revenue splits roughly U.S. 73%, Canada 13%, Other International 14%. Merchandise groups into Foods & Sundries, Non-Foods, Fresh Foods, Warehouse Ancillary (gasoline, pharmacy, optical, food court), and e-commerce/business centers/travel. The competitive engine is the membership subscription: 82.9M paid members / 148.5M cardholders, with a 92.2% U.S./Canada renewal rate and 89.7% worldwide. Membership fees ($5.3B in FY25, growing ~10-13%) fall almost entirely to operating income and subsidize razor-thin merchandise margins, which lets Costco underprice competitors. Executive members (~48% of paid members) drive ~74% of worldwide sales. Kirkland Signature private label anchors both margin and loyalty.

Leadership is deep and culturally consistent: CEO Ron Vachris, a 40-year insider who took over in January 2024, and CFO Gary Millerchip, who joined in 2024 from Kroger. Former Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo joined the board with a first grant in January 2026, a governance signal given the salience of trade and tariff policy to Costco's imported-goods mix.

## Financial Analysis

Costco's financial profile is the strongest dimension of the story. Revenue has compounded steadily: FY2022 $227.0B → FY2023 $242.3B → FY2024 $254.5B → FY2025 $275.2B (net sales $269.9B + membership $5.3B), with FY25 net sales up 8%. Momentum has accelerated into FY26: Q3 average ticket +7%, traffic +2%. The 36-week net income of $6,228M compares with $5,489M a year earlier, a 13.5% increase.

Margins are structurally thin by design. FY25 gross margin was 11.12%, operating margin ~3.8%, net margin ~2.9%. Gasoline (~10% of sales) mechanically distorts reported margin percentages; Q3 FY26 gross margin fell 21 bps to 11.04% on a reported basis, but ex-gasoline-inflation it actually rose 1 bp. The headline "compression" is a fuel-price optical effect, not a real deterioration, and standardized data pulls routinely misread it.

Cash generation is exceptional. FY25 operating cash flow was $13.3B with $7.8B of free cash flow; 36-week FY26 OCF of $11.1B (up from $9.5B) was helped by faster inventory turns and improved supplier terms. The balance sheet holds $20.0B in cash and short-term investments against only ~$5.7B of low-coupon long-term debt (3.0% notes due 2027, plus 1.375-1.75% notes). ROE is ~27.8%, ROA ~10.5%, and the Altman Z-Score of 4.93 sits comfortably in the safe zone. The ~1.03 current ratio is normal for the fast-turn model, not a liquidity concern.

Capital allocation is disciplined rather than aggressive. FY26 capex is guided to ~$6.5B for ~30+ new warehouses plus digital and depot investment. The dividend was raised 12% in April 2025 and again to $1.47/quarter (a 13% hike) in April 2026, though the yield remains a token 0.63%. Buybacks are modest (~$0.6B over 36 weeks, $1.36B remaining on authorization) and mainly offset dilution; the share count is essentially flat at ~444M. Historically Costco has favored large special dividends, such as the ~$6.7B $15/share payout in FY24. One data note: the stitched XBRL feed reports FY25 net income of $10,818M, but the audited 10-K MD&A and proxy figure is $8,099M ($18.21 diluted EPS). We treat $8,099M as correct; the XBRL figure appears to be an aggregation artifact.

We score Financial Health 8.0. Fortress liquidity, high-quality cash conversion, appropriate (minimal) leverage, and an improving negative-working-capital engine all support the near-top score. It is not a 9-10 only because the very low payout and modest buyback mean some capital sits idle relative to the returns Costco earns internally, though the special-dividend history mitigates this.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is durable and, at the operational level, accelerating. Comps at +10% (+7% ex-fuel/FX) with digital up 21-22% show a business taking share and driving both ticket and traffic. The September 2024 U.S./Canada annual-fee increase is still phasing through the ratably-recognized deferred-revenue base, contributing an estimated 25-35% of membership income growth through FY26. This is a visible, low-risk earnings driver: the price increase is already locked in and renews at 92%+ retention.

Warehouse unit growth of ~30 clubs per year against a base of 928 adds ~3% annually, with international (176 units outside the U.S./Canada across 12 countries) offering the longest runway. Executive-member penetration (~48% of members, ~74% of sales) continues to deepen wallet share and lift renewal economics.

The counterweight is the forward EPS math. Consensus current-year EPS sits at $20.59 and next-year at $22.69, implying ~10% forward growth. Estimate revisions are flat to marginally positive (+0.5% current-year, +0.8% next-year over 90 days), with no acceleration signal. The company has missed consensus in three of the last four quarters (Q3 FY26 -1.9%, prior quarter -1.5%, quarter before that +3.3%, earliest -0.8%). The misses are small and the underlying growth remains robust, but they matter enormously at ~47x earnings where beats are priced in and misses trigger multiple compression; Q3's ~4% next-day drop despite 15% EPS growth is the template.

We score Growth Prospects 6.5. The revenue and membership trajectory is high-quality and durable, but revisions are essentially flat, and the growth rate, while excellent for a retailer, does not justify a technology-style multiple. This is steady mid-teens EPS compounding.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is where the thesis breaks. At ~$935 against trailing EPS of $18.21, Costco trades at ~47x trailing and ~45x forward earnings ($20.59 current-year consensus). For context, the Consumer Staples merchandise-retail peer set trades at a median P/E of 18.5: Target 17.5x, Dollar General 16.2x, Dollar Tree 18.5x, BJ's Wholesale 20.9x. Even PriceSmart, the closest warehouse-club comparable, trades at 35.6x. Costco commands roughly 2.5x the peer median and a ~32% premium to the next-most-expensive warehouse comp.

A premium is warranted: Costco's renewal rates, membership annuity, ROE (~27.8%), and defensive comp durability are genuinely superior to any peer. But the magnitude of the premium requires the multiple to hold, and there is no catalyst to expand it. On a PEG basis, ~45x forward earnings against ~10-13% forward EPS growth implies a PEG of roughly 3.5-4.5x, expensive on any reading. The bullish scenarios circulating (e.g., a path to $1,250 by 2027) require the multiple to re-rate to ~58x, a bet on sentiment rather than fundamentals.

The equity lessons here are directly applicable. Consumer Staples BUYs have historically produced negative alpha (-0.71pp across the sample), and a "cheap" valuation score sorts the wrong way, but Costco is the inverse problem: it is unambiguously expensive, and expensive Staples names offer no valuation cushion when growth is merely good. The cycle-adjusted-earnings and "analyst targets are floors" principles that would rescue a cheap inflection stock do not apply here: Costco is not at a cyclical trough (earnings are at all-time highs), and the >50x-forward guardrail is nearly triggered. At ~45x forward with flat revisions, analyst targets (mean $1,077, median $1,100) reflect the same growth extrapolation embedded in the price, not independent confirmation. JPMorgan's July target cut to $1,100 after a June sales update illustrates that even the bulls are trimming.

We score Valuation 3.5. The multiple is stretched relative to a ~10-13% growth rate with no re-rating catalyst, the company itself warns the price reflects high expectations, and estimate revisions are flat. The business is excellent, so this is not a value trap in the impairment sense, but there is no asymmetric upside at today's price, and the risk is skewed toward multiple compression on any stumble.

## Competitive Landscape

Costco's competitive position is strong and, on most dimensions, stable to improving. The membership flywheel is the moat: 92.2% U.S./Canada renewal, Executive members driving ~74% of sales, and membership fees subsidizing merchandise pricing in a way no non-subscription competitor can match. Kirkland Signature reinforces both margin and loyalty. In a bifurcating retail environment, consumers trade into warehouse clubs during inflation and uncertainty; Costco's rising traffic and ticket contrast with Target's reported ~23% operating-income decline in a comparable period.

The trajectory has moderated from the prior report's near-perfect view. The score history shows Competitive Position declining from 9.0 to 7.0 across recent reports, and we set it at 7.5, a slight upgrade from the last read given the Q3 comp acceleration and digital strength, which confirm the moat is still widening at the operational level. The moderation from the 9.0 peak is appropriate: the 10-K explicitly flags that some rivals have greater resources and faster AI adoption, a forward risk to watch even if not yet material. Membership renewal is being modestly pressured at the margin by online/digital-promo sign-ups that renew at slightly lower rates, dragging worldwide renewal (89.7%) below the U.S./Canada figure.

Net, Costco is the structural winner in warehouse-club retail and continues to gain share, which supports a score comfortably above sector-neutral. We weight this dimension per the calibration reality that competitive-position signals have been anti-predictive in the recent window, so we lean on the confirmed operational data (comps, renewal, digital) rather than reputational halo.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is valuation itself, a self-reinforcing risk the company acknowledges in its own 10-K. At ~45x forward earnings, the stock is priced for continued flawless execution, and three of the last four quarters have modestly missed. A single quarter of decelerating comps or a membership-renewal wobble could trigger meaningful multiple compression before any fundamental damage appears.

Specific, less-priced risks:
- **Geographic concentration.** U.S. + Canada = 86% of sales / 84% of operating income; California alone is ~26% of U.S. sales, with HQ and centralized systems in earthquake/wildfire-exposed California and Washington. A regional shock is disproportionately material.
- **Tariffs, now materializing in litigation.** The MD&A flags that higher tariffs are "more likely to adversely impact rather than improve" results. In March 2026, four class actions (Illinois, Washington, Puerto Rico) alleged Costco should refund IEEPA tariffs passed through to members. Costco deems the litigation immaterial, but it is a novel legal theory tied directly to a live macro risk. Imported-goods cost inflation is a genuine headwind to watch.
- **Kirkland tequila labeling suits** (Oct-Nov 2025) allege the "100% de Agave" product contains non-agave alcohol, a reputational risk to the crucial private-label brand, though one case was already dismissed without prejudice.
- **Thin-margin sensitivity.** Modest changes in merchandise costs or SG&A (wages, healthcare, utilities) swing net income materially; Costco deliberately pays above industry ($20 entry wage, ~$32 average hourly U.S.).
- **FX translation.** 27% of sales / 34% of operating income are international; a rising dollar is a headwind (FY25 FX cut net income ~$97M).

The score history marks Risk Factors declining from 7.0 to 5.0, reflecting the emergence of the tariff litigation and the crystallizing valuation-expectation risk. We set it at 5.5, slightly above the prior read, because the recent pullback from the 52-week high has already absorbed some of the expectation risk, and none of the idiosyncratic risks (tariff suits, tequila suits) are likely to be individually material to a $414B company. The overriding risk remains the multiple, a valuation phenomenon captured in that dimension rather than an idiosyncratic-catalyst risk.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is calm and offers no confirmation of near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 19.2% against 1-year historical volatility of 19.7%, fair to slightly cheap, and the IV percentile within the current chain sits at 0%. IV has fallen sharply from 26.7% a month ago (post-earnings crush), and the term structure is flat (near 19.2% ≈ far 20.7%), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated on a catalyst. Put/call skew is balanced near-term (0.7%) but steepens to -4.4% at the 6-month tenor, consistent with modest downside hedging over the medium term rather than acute fear. The put/call OI ratio of 1.28 and put/call volume of 1.22 lean mildly bearish but are not extreme. Unusual activity includes a $770 August put (7.7x vol/OI, -17.6% out of the money) and upside $1,000/$1,120 August calls, indicating positioning on both tails. The low IV means options offer a relatively cheap way to hedge or express a view, and there is no options-implied warning that contradicts a HOLD. Short interest is negligible at 1.6% of float (2.5 days to cover), so there is no squeeze dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$1,100-1,315).** Costco continues compounding revenue at 8-12% and EPS in the mid-teens, comps hold above 7% ex-fuel, the membership-fee increase keeps lifting membership income through FY26, and the market rewards the durability and defensive quality with a sustained ~48-52x multiple. On next-year consensus EPS of $22.69, a 52x multiple implies ~$1,180; a further re-rating toward 58x on 2027 estimates supports the high analyst target of $1,315. The bull case is fundamentally sound on the business but requires the premium multiple to expand or at least hold; it is a sentiment-and-durability bet, not a mispricing bet.

**Bear Case (~$740-800).** A single quarter of decelerating comps, a membership-renewal wobble, a margin surprise from wage or tariff cost inflation, or simply a broad-market de-rating of premium multiples compresses the P/E toward the mid-30s. On current-year EPS of $20.59, a 36x multiple implies ~$740 (the low analyst target); a 38-40x multiple lands ~$780-825. That is a 15-21% drawdown from ~$935, and none of it requires the business to actually break, only for the market to decide that ~47x is too much for 10-13% growth. The three-of-four-quarter miss streak and flat estimate revisions make this scenario more than tail risk.

The asymmetry favors patience. The upside requires multiple expansion off an already-premium base; the downside requires only multiple normalization. That is an unfavorable setup for a new buyer at today's price, but not a reason to sell a high-quality compounder, which is why this is a HOLD rather than an AVOID.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q4 FY2026 earnings release (late September 2026), monthly comparable-sales updates, and the annual membership-renewal disclosures. Given the flat estimate revisions, the near-term path is likely range-bound around the current multiple absent a comp acceleration or a broad-market re-rating.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A price reset toward the low-$800s (forward P/E approaching high-30s), which restores a reasonable margin of safety on 10-13% EPS growth.
- Estimate revisions turning decisively positive (+5% or more over 90 days) alongside comps sustaining above +8% ex-fuel/FX, signaling the market is under-modeling the membership-fee tailwind.
- A membership fee increase announcement or a re-acceleration in Executive-member penetration that raises the durable membership-income growth rate.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Comparable sales decelerating below +4% ex-fuel/FX for two consecutive quarters, indicating the flywheel is stalling.
- A membership renewal rate decline below ~90% in the U.S./Canada, which would undermine the annuity that justifies the premium.
- A material adverse ruling in the tariff-refund class actions or a quantified margin hit from tariff/cost inflation that consensus has not modeled.
- Multiple expansion to >55x forward without corresponding growth acceleration, which would make the risk/reward outright negative.

## Conclusion

Costco is a fortress-balance-sheet compounder with a widening membership moat, accelerating comps, a slow-release membership-fee tailwind, and pristine, well-aligned management. On business quality alone it deserves a place in any long-term portfolio. The financial health (8.0) and competitive position (7.5) scores reflect that.

The obstacle is price. At ~45x forward earnings for a business growing EPS in the mid-teens, with flat estimate revisions and three misses in the last four quarters, the stock offers no margin of safety and no catalyst to re-rate higher. The valuation score of 3.5 captures the reality that the upside case requires multiple expansion off an already-premium base while the downside requires only normalization. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the declining score trajectory (7.2 → 6.1) reflects a multiple that has stayed rich as the marginal growth rate settled into the mid-teens rather than any deterioration in the business.

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** Own Costco for durable, high-quality compounding, but do not chase it at ~47x. The far better entry sits in the low-$800s, where the forward multiple approaches the high-30s and the compounding math begins to favor the buyer. Current holders should stay put; new capital should wait for a genuine reset.
