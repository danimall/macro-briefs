## Executive Summary

Baxter delivered its cleanest quarter since the Kidney Care separation. Q2 2026 net sales of $2,960M grew 5% both reported and organic, every division grew organically (Advanced Surgery +12%, Care & Connectivity +5%, Infusion Therapies & Platforms +4%, Front Line Care +2%), and management raised FY2026 guidance to 2-3% organic growth and $1.95-$2.15 in adjusted EPS. Derived adjusted continuing-operations EPS of $0.56 beat a $0.37 non-GAAP consensus by 52%. The stock is up 71% from its 52-week low of $15.73 and roughly 20% since our last review at $22.40.

The quality of that beat is the crux. The 10-Q discloses approximately $75M of IEEPA tariff refunds credited to cost of goods sold in the quarter following the Supreme Court's invalidation of those duties. At the 22% first-half effective tax rate and 518M diluted shares, that is $0.113 per share, so ex-refund adjusted EPS was roughly $0.45 against $0.59 a year earlier, a 24% decline. Excluding special items, gross margin fell 210bps in Q2 and 360bps in the first half. Our derived adjusted operating margin has moved 15.0% (1H25) → 14.1% (FY25) → 12.6% (1H26). Segment margins corroborate: Medical Products & Therapies fell from 22.3% to 18.8% and Healthcare Systems & Technologies from 18.9% to 17.4% in the first half, and that is *after* a presentation change that leaves certain shared corporate costs unallocated. Sales are growing while unit economics are not. The guidance "raise" moves FY2026 up against a previously lowered bar; the $2.05 midpoint is still ~10% below our derived FY2025 adjusted EPS of ~$2.27.

At $26.93 the shares trade at 13.1x the guidance midpoint, 11.4x estimated LTM adjusted EBITDA, and a 3.1% FY2025 free-cash-flow yield, with consensus modeling 2027 adjusted EPS of $2.03, below 2026. Our base case sits at $27, essentially spot. Against a bull case of $35 and a bear case of $18.50, probability-weighted expected return is modestly negative. Layered on that: net debt/estimated LTM adjusted EBITDA of ~3.9x, BBB-/Baa3 ratings with outlooks moved back to Negative in Q1 2026, a revolver covenant accommodation that lapses after Q3 2026, an indefinite U.S./Canada shipment hold on the flagship Novum IQ large-volume pump, a December 2025 DOJ Civil Investigative Demand touching IV flexible container production, and a vacant CFO seat. We are upgrading from AVOID to REDUCE: the operating deterioration case has weakened, but the entry has become the problem.

## Company Overview

Baxter is a Delaware-incorporated (1931) manufacturer of hospital consumables, medical devices and generic injectables, operating plants in over 20 countries, selling in over 100, with approximately 37,500 employees at year-end 2025. Two divestitures reshaped the company: BioPharma Solutions (September 2023, $3.96B) and Kidney Care/Vantive to Carlyle affiliates (January 31, 2025, $3.80B, ~$3.3B net after-tax cash). Baxter applied proceeds to repay $3.81B of legacy debt in 2025.

The Kidney Care separation left three residual linkages that matter to the reported numbers. Baxter remains a contract supplier to Vantive under a Manufacturing and Supply Agreement of up to ten years; those sales flow through the "Other" line ($83M in Q2 2026 versus $98M a year earlier, and $381M in FY2025 versus $67M in FY2024). A Transition Services Agreement of up to 30 months post-closing, to roughly July 2027, generates billings recorded in "other operating income, net," which was $91M in 1H 2026 against total operating income of $283M. Baxter also carries a $43M net tax indemnity plus $52M of remaining contingent capital-expenditure reimbursement to Vantive.

Effective Q2 2026, three reportable segments became two under new CEO Andrew Hider (appointed July 2025). Pharmaceuticals folded into MPT's Infusion Therapies & Platforms division, $569M of goodwill was reallocated between reporting units (with impairment tests run on both the legacy and new structures, no impairment identified), and certain shared corporate costs are now left unallocated. Prior periods were recast, so within-series comparisons hold but segment margins are not comparable to pre-recast historicals.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2025 net sales were $11,244M, +6% reported and +3% operational, with three percentage points of the growth attributable to Kidney Care MSA sales that had no prior-year base. In 1H 2026, net sales grew 4% at actual rates but only 2% organic. U.S. sales were flat at $3,030M versus $3,026M; all reported growth came from international ($2,631M versus $2,409M, +9% actual, +4% organic). Q2 was the better quarter, +5% organic with breadth, but 1H divisional detail is thinner: Infusion Therapies & Platforms +1%, Front Line Care −1%.

Management attributes part of the Q2 Infusion improvement to "a weak prior year comparison in the U.S. IV solutions business, during which we experienced lower sales due to distributor destocking and fluid conservation practices embedded with clinical practice changes." That language recurs across both the 10-K and 10-Q. Hospital pharmacy protocols that reduce fluid usage, once embedded, do not typically reverse. We read this as a permanent reduction in addressable U.S. IV volume rather than a cyclical trough, which caps the recovery slope in Baxter's largest franchise.

**Margins.** Reported FY2025 gross margin fell to 30.1% from 37.5%; excluding special items it still fell 4.2 points. In 1H 2026, reported gross margin was flat (34.0% versus 34.1%) but ex-special-items gross margin fell 2.1 points in Q2 and 3.6 points in the first half, driven by manufacturing and supply costs including a revised estimate of indirect costs now capitalized into inventory post-separation, net of the tariff refund benefit. Our derived adjusted operating income was $712M (12.6% of sales) in 1H 2026 versus $815M (15.0%) in 1H 2025, computed by adding operating-line special items back to reported operating income.

**Earnings quality.** FY2025 GAAP was a $957M net loss (−$1.87 diluted), with continuing operations at −$1.75. The 10-K discloses $2.07B of special items ($4.02 per diluted share), producing derived adjusted continuing-operations EPS of ~$2.27. The composition is unusually weighted toward real economic loss rather than accounting noise: $485M Front Line Care goodwill impairment, $290M Welch Allyn trade-name write-down, $178M restructuring, $133M hurricane costs, $113M infusion pump product reserves, and a $513M tax charge, alongside $598M of intangible amortization.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow from continuing operations was $951M in FY2025 against capex of $513M, giving derived FCF of $438M, a 3.9% FCF margin and a 3.1% yield on today's market cap. The 1H 2026 swing to $257M FCF from −$144M is real but management attributes it to working capital: the prior-year period absorbed a $279M inventory build versus $36M this year, partly offset by a $232M accrued-expense outflow. We do not annualize it. As a labeled sensitivity, 2× $257M implies ~$514M and a 3.7% yield; the FY2025 figure is the more defensible anchor, and neither reflects the ~$98M of remaining restructuring cash payments running through 2027.

**Balance sheet and credit — the decisive constraint.** At 6/30/2026: cash $2,150M, total debt $9,459M, net debt $7,309M, total equity $6,179M. Goodwill plus intangibles of $8,959M exceed book equity, leaving tangible book value of roughly −$2.78B (normal for acquisitive medtech, and not a solvency signal on its own, but it means the rating agencies' focus is entirely cash-flow leverage). Debt/equity is 1.53x; the 2.28x figure appearing on screens is total liabilities to equity.

Our estimated LTM adjusted EBITDA of ~$1,857M (adjusted operating income plus depreciation, splicing pre- and post-recast bases) puts net leverage at ~3.9x. Management's stated target is "approximately 3.0x by the end of 2026." Reaching that on ~$1.9B of EBITDA requires net debt near $5.7B, roughly $1.6B of reduction in one half-year against a $257M half-year FCF run rate. Baxter repaid no debt in 1H 2026; total debt moved from $9,475M to $9,459M and the leverage improvement came from a $184M cash build.

Two readings are available and we cannot adjudicate between them from the filings. Credit-agreement EBITDA in investment-grade revolvers typically adds back restructuring, separation and transformation charges; adding ~$300-400M of those would put bank EBITDA near $2.3-2.4B and net leverage at 3.0-3.2x, consistent with compliance and with management describing itself as near target. Alternatively, the end-2026 target is at risk. The covenant definition, threshold and Baxter's reported ratio are not disclosed in the filings reviewed. What is disclosed: the November 2025 amendment *increased* the maximum permitted net leverage ratio for the four quarters ending 12/31/25 through 9/30/26, so the covenant tightens for the December 2026 quarter; covenant-constrained revolver draw capacity fell from $1.79B to $1.41B between year-end and 6/30/2026; ratings were cut to BBB-/Baa3 in Q4 2025 and both agencies restored Negative outlooks in Q1 2026; and the 10-K states that failure to reach the leverage target by end-2026 could prompt further downgrades.

Refinancing is a forward earnings headwind independent of the covenant question. First-half gross interest expense of $155M annualizes to roughly a 3.3% average cost on $9.46B; long-term debt carries at $8,577M against a $7,777M fair value (91% of par), which reflects market rates above legacy coupons. Each maturity refinanced at current spreads compounds against a 12.6% adjusted operating margin. The $844M now sitting in current maturities (a tranche maturing in 1H 2027, comfortably covered by cash) is the first test.

Capital allocation is fully subordinated to the credit: the quarterly dividend was cut in November 2025 from $0.17 to $0.01 (0.15% yield, dividends paid fell from $174M to $10M in the first half), no shares have been repurchased in 2024, 2025 or 1H 2026, and $1.30B of authorization sits idle. These are the right decisions for this balance sheet, and they also remove the two supports that normally cushion a value name.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth signal improved in Q2 and the breadth is genuine. Advanced Surgery hemostats and sealants grew 12% organic in Q2 (+11% 1H, +9% FY2025), and international pharmacy compounding grew 10% in FY2025 — Reuters attributed the guidance raise specifically to drug compounding. These are the two differentiated pockets in the portfolio.

Everything else is harder. Front Line Care organic sales fell 1% in the first half, exposed to hospital capital budgets that the 10-Q itself flags as at risk from macro uncertainty and elevated tariffs. Injectables supply constraints persist through 1H 2026 in both U.S. and international markets. Novum LVP contributes no meaningful revenue while the shipment and installation hold remains in effect, with the 10-Q stating the release timing "remains uncertain." Two revenue and income streams have dated step-downs: MSA sales to Vantive are already shrinking 15% year over year, and the TSA runs to approximately July 2027. On the TSA, the $91M of 1H billings is gross — the cost of providing those services sits in COGS and SG&A, Baxter receives services back under the same agreement, and $58M of first-half restructuring charges are specifically aimed at eliminating the associated stranded cost. The earnings step-down on expiry is therefore materially smaller than $91M annualized, but it is a headwind in a period that needs tailwinds.

The estimate-revision pattern is the most useful forward evidence and it cuts against the headline. Current-year consensus rose 6.7% over 90 days to $2.05, but the current quarter was cut 2.5% to $0.52 and the next quarter cut 6.5% to $0.61. Since $0.92 of first-half actual plus $0.52 plus $0.61 equals $2.05, the entire annual raise is the Q2 beat flowing through while the second-half bar was *lowered*. Next-year consensus of $2.03 sits below current-year $2.05: the street models zero earnings growth in 2027 even after the operating-model change, the restructuring, and the assumed lapping of inventory-cost drag. That is the number the bull case has to break.

The high-magnitude estimate-revision buy signal is not present here. Revisions are stable rather than accelerating, forward quarters are being trimmed, and revenue growth is mid-single-digit rather than the 30%+ that accompanies genuine inflection.

Thirteen times adjusted earnings is not demanding for a medtech in absolute terms. It is close to fair for *this* medtech: adjusted EPS declining ~10% year over year on guidance, adjusted operating margin down 240bps year over year, ~3.9x net leverage against a one-notch-above-junk rating on negative outlook, no buyback, a token dividend, an off-market flagship pump, and a DOJ inquiry into the production of its largest product line. The multiple now embeds credit for the Hider operating system before it has appeared in adjusted margins.

Consensus corroborates rather than contradicts a fair-value read: mean target $28.73 and median $28.00, only 4-7% above spot, with 11 of 13 analysts at Hold, one Buy and one Strong Buy. Analyst targets function as a floor on cheap stocks with accelerating estimates; with 2027 consensus below 2026 and forward quarters being cut, we treat the $28-ish consensus as evidence of fair value rather than a stale ceiling.

Our scenarios:

- **Base $27** — FY2027 adjusted EPS of ~$2.05 (in line with consensus; margin drag from inventory-cost roll-through and the indirect-cost reclassification annualizes out, offset by TSA step-down, Section 122/301 tariffs replacing the refund, and higher refinancing cost) at 13.2x. Cross-check: $1.95B EBITDA at 11.4x EV/EBITDA less ~$7.0B net debt = $27.3/share. Essentially spot.
- **Bull $35** — Novum LVP hold lifts with a relaunch cycle, adjusted gross margin recovers most of the 360bps, Advanced Surgery and compounding sustain low-double-digit growth, FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $2.40 and the multiple re-rates to 14.5x. Cross-check: $2.15B EBITDA at 11.5x less $6.8B net debt = $34.6/share. Deleveraging transfers value from creditors to equity in this path.
- **Bear $18.50** — margin compression persists, Front Line Care takes a further impairment (the 10-K discloses ~$160M on a 5% fair-value decline), the leverage target is missed and a downgrade to high yield raises refinancing cost, FY2027 adjusted EPS falls to ~$1.75 at 10.5x. This is above the 52-week low of $15.73 and near the low analyst target of $23.00 minus the credit-event discount.

Probability-weighting base 45% / bull 25% / bear 30% gives an expected return of roughly −2%. Fair value at spot, with the downside tail (−31%) as large as the upside (+30%), makes this an unattractive entry.

## Competitive Landscape

Baxter is two businesses with different economics. MPT sells largely commodity-adjacent hospital consumables — IV fluids, administration sets, premixes, nutrition, generic injectables, inhaled anesthetics — where management explicitly concedes purchasing decisions are driven primarily by cost-effectiveness and price, and where multi-year GPO/IDN contracts limit its ability to pass through inflation and tariffs "in a timely manner or at all." The moat is scale sterile manufacturing plus supplier-qualification friction. Hurricane Helene demonstrated that this cuts both ways: U.S. hospital dependence on the North Cove plant was acute enough to trigger a national conservation response, and that response permanently reduced Baxter's volume base. In the U.S., Cardinal Health and Medline warehouse and ship a significant portion of product; these are logistics intermediaries rather than end customers, and no named-customer concentration percentage was located in the filings reviewed. The mix shift toward international and toward Advanced Surgery does not establish reduced customer concentration.

HST (smart beds, patient monitoring, respiratory, OR integration) is where value has been destroyed. Front Line Care/Welch Allyn absorbed $425M and $485M of goodwill impairment in 2024 and 2025 plus a $290M trade-name write-down, and is running at −1% organic with $1.51B of goodwill and $390M of trade name remaining. Linet Americas is litigating antitrust claims against Hillrom in beds. Care & Connectivity Solutions is the healthier half, +5% organic on execution against a U.S. patient-support-system order backlog.

On visibility: the $7.91B of remaining performance obligations disclosed at 6/30/2026 is scoped to executed contracts of original duration over one year and *entirely within MPT*. It is not total company backlog, excludes HST equipment orders, and includes U.S. contracts with index-dependent price increases not yet known. Contract liabilities are only $166M. Recognition is ~10% over the remainder of 2026, 25% in 2027, 20% each in 2028-29.

The Novum situation deserves specific weight. FDA classified the 2025 corrections as Class I recalls — under-infusion in standby, under-infusion on bolus, misload-driven over/under-infusion, software anomalies — and Baxter halted U.S./Canada distribution in July 2025 except for medical necessity. Additional corrections are "in the early stages of verification testing," some requiring clearance. Legacy Spectrum IQ remains available, which limits share loss but forfeits the premium-platform upgrade cycle.

## Risk Assessment

**Novum LVP hold, open-ended.** No timeline disclosed; additional corrections in early verification testing. Every quarter of delay is forgone platform revenue and cedes fleet decisions to competitors.

**DOJ Civil Investigative Demand (December 2025)** on the production of Baxter's IV flexible containers and False Claims Act compliance. This touches the largest franchise and the facilities affected by the Helene disruption. Total legal and environmental reserves were $39M at 6/30/2026, which reflects the probable-and-estimable accounting threshold rather than an assessment of exposure. A CID is an information request, not an allegation; Baxter's 2022 Hillrom CID has run since October 2022 without resolution, while the ethylene oxide docket was dismissed with prejudice in June 2026 for a non-material amount.

**Securities class actions** over Novum LVP disclosures (class period February 2022 to as late as October 2025), with lead plaintiff and co-lead counsel appointed in June 2026. Two consolidated derivative suits are stayed.

**Credit and covenant.** Discussed above. The specific dated risk is the Q4 2026 covenant step-down coinciding with the end-2026 leverage target, against Negative outlooks at both agencies.

**Impairment.** $1.51B Front Line Care goodwill and $390M Welch Allyn trade name, with a disclosed ~$160M sensitivity to a 5% fair-value decline, on a DCF using a 10.0% discount rate and 3.0% terminal growth. No triggering event was identified in 1H 2026. Non-cash and covenant-neutral, but it would reset the earnings base narrative.

**Tax.** A U.S. valuation allowance against deferred tax assets was recorded in 2025 on a cumulative history of U.S. operating losses, expected to persist at least twelve months. The IRS settled 2019-2020 Costa Rica/Puerto Rico transfer pricing in Q2 2026 at amounts consistent with existing reserves, but ~$280M of reserves for 2021-2025 remain open.

**Tariffs both directions.** The $75M IEEPA refund is non-recurring; management warns results "will continue to be adversely impacted by Section 122 duties and recently announced Section 301 tariffs," with limited pass-through given contract duration.

**Governance and continuity.** The Q2 10-Q was signed by an Interim CFO; the permanent CFO is unidentified, and an August 4, 2026 8-K carrying Items 5.02 and 8.01 was not retrievable. A vacant CFO seat at a company whose central issue is covenant compliance and a leverage target is a real deficiency. The May 8, 2026 8-K carried a new Executive Severance and Change in Control Plan, an amendment to the CEO's offer letter ten months into his tenure, and a Second Amended and Restated 2021 Incentive Plan — documents not read here, and relevant to any alignment conclusion.

**Other named items:** the 2026 EU desflurane restriction, DEHP/PVC/PFAS scrutiny, proposed CMS Competitive Bidding expansion for DMEPOS, and collective bargaining agreements at one U.S. plant expiring January 2027.

**Screening context.** The FY2025 10-K produces an Altman Z of 1.43 and one of three computable Piotroski signals positive. The EBIT/assets and net-income terms are distorted by $775M of impairments plus the $513M tax charge; the current ratio (2.31), leverage and asset-turnover terms are not. The screens correctly flag a leveraged, low-return, asset-heavy business; they overstate near-term default probability given $2.15B of cash, an undrawn revolver and covered near-term maturities.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 66.8% against 44.3% one-year realized is rich, with a pronounced backwardation to 42.4% at six months; the market is paying for near-term event risk rather than a change in the long-run distribution. The one-month tenor prices a ±9.1% move and three-month ±10.2%, unusually wide for a period with no scheduled earnings until late October, which is consistent with an unresolved announcement (the unretrieved August 4 8-K, plausibly the CFO appointment, is the obvious candidate). The data source characterizes the 17.7% put/call skew as elevated put demand; skew readings differ in sign across tenors (−24.5% at one month, +4.6% at three), so we take the source's qualitative label without inferring direction from the raw values.

Positioning has flipped bullish since the print: put/call open interest fell from 0.49 to 0.18 while the price rose 20.4%, and ATM IV collapsed from 103.3% to 66.8%. Call-heavy positioning after a 71% run is a contrarian caution, not confirmation. It also means downside protection is comparatively uncrowded: for holders who want to retain exposure through the Novum resolution, buying puts is cheaper in relative crowding terms than the headline IV suggests, though the absolute vol level is expensive. Short interest of 10.0% of float and 4.9 days to cover means the name is neither crowded short nor uncontested.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($35, +30%).** The margin drag is mechanical and self-correcting: higher-cost late-2025 inventory rolling through COGS and the reclassification of indirect costs into inventory post-separation both annualize out, which is precisely what the guidance raise implies. Organic growth is broad — all four divisions positive in Q2 — with two genuinely differentiated engines in Advanced Surgery (+12%) and international compounding (+10%). Hider's Baxter Growth and Performance system, the layer elimination, the two-segment reorganization and $58M of separation-driven restructuring in the first half are the recognizable playbook of a decentralized operator, and none of it has yet shown up in adjusted margin. The Novum hold lifting would restore a platform revenue line from zero. The IRS 2019-2020 settlement at reserve levels and the Claris/Ahmedabad VAI reclassification removed two discrete tails. Thirteen times a recovering earnings stream with $2.15B of cash is undemanding if 2027 adjusted EPS reaches $2.40.

**Bear case ($18.50, −31%).** Adjusted profitability has fallen for six consecutive quarters on a rolling basis (15.0% → 12.6% adjusted operating margin), and the reasons are not all transitory: the U.S. IV volume base has structurally reset on embedded clinical conservation protocols, GPO/IDN contracts prevent tariff and inflation pass-through, Section 122 and 301 duties replace a refund that is gone, and the TSA income stream expires by mid-2027. The 52% headline beat was $0.11 of legal windfall converting a likely miss into an optical triumph; ex-refund EPS fell 24%. Consensus already models zero 2027 growth. If the end-2026 leverage target slips, a downgrade to high yield raises the cost of refinancing $9.46B of debt currently carried at ~3.3%, and each turn of refinancing cost eats a business generating $438M of annual free cash flow. Front Line Care remains a live impairment risk at −1% organic. Novum has no timeline, the DOJ is asking about IV container production with $39M of total reserves on the books, and the CFO seat is empty.

**What we think the market is pricing.** At 13.1x guidance and 11.4x EV/EBITDA, the market is paying for a 2027 margin recovery on faith in a new operating system, netted against ~3.9x leverage and binary regulatory items. That is a defensible mid-point, which is why our base case is spot. The reason to trim rather than hold is the shape of the distribution: the bear outcomes (impairment, downgrade, prolonged Novum hold, a Q4 covenant problem) are concrete and dated, while the bull outcome requires a margin inflection that has not appeared in six months of data and that consensus explicitly does not forecast.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Base case $27 (0% from $26.93), bull $35 (+30%), bear $18.50 (−31%). Probability-weighted expected return approximately −2% over twelve months.

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength rather than waiting for a catalyst. The name has moved 71% off its 52-week low and 20% since our last review while adjusted EPS declined 20% year over year in the first half; near-term implied volatility at 66.8% versus 44.3% realized means option overwrites on retained exposure are being paid well. For holders who want to keep a position through the Novum resolution, we would carry no more than a half-weight and would size it so that the $18.50 bear case is tolerable. New capital has no reason to enter here.

**Upgrade triggers (to HOLD, then ACCUMULATE).**
1. Adjusted gross margin flat or up year over year in the Q3 2026 print (late October), i.e., a genuine end to the 210-360bps compression, disclosed *excluding* tariff refunds.
2. A disclosed net leverage ratio at or below 3.2x on the company's own definition, plus confirmation that the Q4 2026 covenant step-down was met without a further amendment.
3. Novum LVP shipment hold lifted with a dated relaunch plan, plus a permanent CFO appointed with credit and cost-program experience.
Two of these three at a price at or below $24 would make the risk/reward acceptable again.

**Avoid triggers (full exit).**
1. A further Front Line Care goodwill or Welch Allyn trade-name impairment, or a triggering-event disclosure in the Q3 10-Q.
2. A downgrade to high yield at either agency, or a second revolver covenant amendment.
3. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance cut below $1.95, or a Q4 2026 covenant compliance qualification.
4. A quantified reserve established for the DOJ IV-container matter or the securities class actions above ~$150M.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next hard checkpoint is the Q3 2026 report in late October, which supplies the first clean read on adjusted gross margin without a tariff refund and the first look at 2H guidance execution. The Q4 2026 covenant step-down and the end-2026 leverage target are the second, resolving in the February 2027 10-K. The August 4, 2026 8-K (Items 5.02/8.01) should be read as soon as available.

## Conclusion

The prior AVOID rating rested on deteriorating adjusted profitability, an unresolved leverage problem, and an off-market flagship pump. Q2 2026 partially answered the first charge: organic growth was 5% with all four divisions contributing, guidance moved up, cash built to $2.15B, and two discrete tails (the 2019-2020 IRS exam and the Ahmedabad warning letter) closed. That is enough to retire the "material thesis impairment" framing.

It is not enough to justify owning the stock at $26.93. Roughly a fifth of the headline beat was a non-recurring tariff refund; ex-refund adjusted EPS fell 24% year over year; adjusted operating margin has moved from 15.0% to 12.6%; and the raised FY2026 guidance midpoint of $2.05 sits ~10% below FY2025's derived $2.27, with 2027 consensus lower still at $2.03. Consensus raised the year while cutting both remaining quarters. Against that, the shares now carry 13.1x guided adjusted earnings and 11.4x EV/EBITDA with ~3.9x net leverage, a BBB−/Baa3 rating on negative outlook, a covenant accommodation that lapses in one quarter, an open-ended Novum hold, a DOJ inquiry into the production of its largest product line, $1.9B of impairable Front Line Care assets running at negative organic growth, and no permanent CFO. Base-case fair value is at spot, the bear case is 31% lower, and the bull case requires a margin inflection that has not yet appeared in a single reported quarter.
