**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Zimmer Biomet is a global musculoskeletal medical-technology company and one of the "big four" in large-joint orthopedics, alongside Stryker, Johnson & Johnson/DePuy, and Smith+Nephew. The core problem is that ZBH has been losing relative ground for years, chiefly to Stryker's Mako robot in knees, and the financial statements now reflect that erosion directly. Net income has declined three straight periods despite rising revenue — $1,024M (2023), $904M (2024), $705M (2025) — while organic constant-currency growth runs in the low single digits against a structurally mid-single-digit end market. The company's own proxy documents 5-year cumulative TSR of 62.66 versus a peer group at 126.87, roughly half the cohort. This is a competitive and capital-allocation problem that has compounded over multiple years, not a single-quarter stumble.

The Q1 2026 print illustrates the gap between headline and substance. Adjusted EPS of $2.09 beat consensus of $1.88 by roughly 11%, and management raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $8.40–$8.55. But reported revenue growth of 9.3% was only +2.9% organic constant currency, with the difference made up by the Paragon 28 acquisition and FX. The beat was further aided by roughly $30M of one-time tariff-refund benefit (from the Supreme Court's invalidation of IEEPA tariffs, with ~$77M total expected) and by management-flagged "opportunistic end-of-quarter customer purchases" that may pull forward future demand. The stock fell roughly 6% on the beat-and-raise, reacting to the CFO departure and the unchanged organic outlook. That price reaction is the single most informative data point in the file.

This is a turnaround story whose bear case is concrete and currently dominant. ZBH is overhauling its entire U.S. sales-force model — the highest-risk change possible in a relationship-driven implant business — and the transition has already cost two large customer accounts. It is doing so with an interim CFO. A thin ~5% goodwill cushion on the Paragon 28-anchored Global Foot & Ankle reporting unit sits as a non-cash impairment trip-wire against $9.9B of total goodwill. The valuation is genuinely depressed at roughly 10x forward EPS, but the discount reflects franchise impairment, not a clean mispricing the market has missed. We move to AVOID and look for evidence of organic stabilization before reconsidering.

## Company Overview

Zimmer Biomet, headquartered in Warsaw, Indiana, sells in more than 100 countries. Its franchises center on knee and hip reconstructive implants (its historical crown jewels), supplemented by S.E.T. (Sports medicine, Extremities, Trauma), foot & ankle, dental/craniomaxillofacial, and a growing robotics/digital business (ZBEdge, the ROSA robot). The business depends heavily on elective orthopedic surgery, which benefits from demographic tailwinds (aging populations, rising activity levels) but is exposed to reimbursement pressure, hospital purchasing dynamics through group purchasing organizations, and surgeon preference.

Approximately 42% of FY2025 net sales were generated outside the U.S., with meaningful exposure to Europe, Japan, and China, the latter subject to volume-based procurement (VBP) pricing pressure. The competitive battleground has shifted to surgical robotics as a tool to lock in surgeons and pull through implant sales. Stryker's Mako leads that market and has driven knee share gains at ZBH's expense. ZBH's response is ROSA plus the July 2025 acquisition of Monogram Technologies (~$177M) to broaden its robotics platform, but it remains in catch-up mode.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue grew from roughly $6.94B in 2022 to roughly $8.0–8.2B in 2025, but the quality of that growth is the issue. The structural orthopedic market grows mid-single digits; ZBH's organic performance below that rate is direct evidence of continued share loss.

The net income trend is the principal red flag: a 31% decline over two years against rising revenue. Margin compression drivers cited across filings include excess and obsolete inventory charges, tariffs, ERP-related disruption in 2024, acquisition costs, and integration/restructuring spend. Declining absolute earnings while the top line grows signals an unresolved competitiveness and cost problem.

On the balance sheet, total indebtedness was roughly $7.5B at year-end 2025, with about $0.6B of principal due within 12 months. Goodwill of $9.9B and intangibles of $4.7B dominate the asset base, a legacy of the 2015 Biomet merger and subsequent M&A. Floating-rate (SOFR-linked) exposure adds rate sensitivity. The company generates real cash flow and the leverage is manageable for a stable-demand medtech franchise, but the asset base is goodwill-heavy and the Foot & Ankle cushion is thin.

The earnings beat track record is genuine — three beats and one in-line over four quarters, including the +11% Q1 2026 surprise — but the Q1 beat was low-quality. Stripping the one-time tariff refund, FX, the inorganic Paragon 28 contribution, and the flagged opportunistic end-of-quarter purchases, the underlying organic story is weak. Capital allocation is leaning on the raised buyback (up to $1.0B for FY2026, a $250M increase announced May 12, 2026) to support EPS, a tell that operational levers are limited near-term.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the weakest dimension of the case. Organic constant-currency growth of +2.9% in Q1 2026 trails the structural end-market growth rate, which means ZBH is ceding share even as demographics expand the addressable surgery pool. Management's 2026 organic guidance is soft (low-to-mid single digit), reflecting self-inflicted sales-force disruption rather than market weakness.

Estimate revisions confirm the absence of a turn. Current-quarter consensus is $2.01, flat over 30 days and down 1.7% over 90 days. Next-quarter estimates are down 4.9% over 90 days. Current-year and next-year estimates are essentially flat (+0.9% and +0.1% over 90 days). There is no revision velocity here. Forward consensus EPS of roughly $8.48 (2026) and $9.00 (2027) implies low single-digit earnings growth, much of which is buyback-driven rather than operational.

The growth optionality rests on the turnaround: Monogram closing the robotics gap versus Mako, Paragon 28 building out foot & ankle, and the sales-force professionalization eventually improving execution. These are multi-year, execution-dependent bets, and the sales-force overhaul is currently subtracting from growth (two large accounts already lost) rather than adding to it. Until organic growth re-accelerates toward the mid-single-digit market rate, the growth case is unproven.

## Valuation Assessment

At $83.75, ZBH trades at roughly 9.9x the FY2026 consensus EPS of $8.48 and about 9.3x the FY2027 estimate of $9.00. That is a steep discount to the Health Care Equipment peer group, where the median P/E is approximately 33.8x and comparables range from STE at 26.8x and HOLX at 31.3x to PODD at 33.8x and RVTY at 50.1x. On a headline-multiple basis ZBH looks dramatically cheap.

The discount is earned, not anomalous. The peer set trades higher because those companies are growing organically and compounding; ZBH is losing share, has declining absolute net income, and has delivered 5-year TSR roughly half its peers'. A compressed multiple on a franchise with an unresolved competitive problem is a value indication only if there is a specific, identifiable catalyst to close the gap. The available catalysts (robotics catch-up, sales-force professionalization) are years out and execution-dependent, and the near-term operational risk is rising, not falling.

Analyst targets span a wide $83 low to $130 high, with a $94 median and $98.7 mean; the high end embeds a successful turnaround that the current evidence does not support. The forward P/E of ~10x is below the implied earnings growth rate only because consensus earnings growth is itself thin and partly buyback-financed. We assign a moderately above-neutral valuation score to reflect genuine downside support from absolute cash generation, the buyback floor, and a ~1.2% dividend, but we do not treat the low multiple as a buy signal in the absence of an organic inflection. A compressed multiple that reflects structural competitive erosion is closer to a value trap than a mispricing until the share-loss trend reverses.

## Competitive Landscape

ZBH is structurally on the back foot in its core market. The orthopedic battle has migrated to surgical robotics as the mechanism for locking in surgeons and pulling through implants, and Stryker's Mako is the established leader that has taken knee share from ZBH. The persistent gap between ZBH's organic growth and end-market growth is the quantitative footprint of that share loss, and the 5-year TSR gap documents multi-year competitive deterioration rather than a transient issue.

ZBH's response — ROSA plus the Monogram acquisition — is credible strategically but leaves the company in catch-up mode against an entrenched incumbent platform with a large installed base. Industry-wide headwinds compound the company-specific problem: GPO and reimbursement pricing pressure, China VBP, "buy local" dynamics, and provider consolidation. The competitive trajectory is the central reason the stock has de-rated, and nothing in the current data set indicates it has turned.

## Risk Assessment

The risk cluster is dense and largely company-specific:

1. **U.S. sales-force transformation.** ZBH is converting large portions of its U.S. sales force from independent distributors/reps to employees while increasing category specialization. The 10-K dedicates an expanded risk factor to this, and the overhaul has already caused the loss of two large customer accounts. In a relationship-driven implant business, this is the highest-risk operational change possible, and it is actively subtracting from sales.

2. **Goodwill impairment trip-wire.** The Global Foot & Ankle reporting unit (anchored by Paragon 28) carries only a ~5% fair-value cushion over carrying value. Against $9.9B total goodwill and $4.7B intangibles, underperformance in foot & ankle could trigger a material non-cash write-off.

3. **Leadership vacuum.** CFO Suketu Upadhyay is departing, with Paul Stellato as interim CFO during the search. A CFO transition layered onto a major operational overhaul is a continuity concern, following a separate January 2026 executive departure.

4. **One-time tailwinds masking the trend.** The ~$77M expected IEEPA tariff refund is a 2026 windfall, not durable earnings power. Investors should normalize it out of the run-rate.

5. **Recurring ERP risk, independent-contractor reclassification exposure, China VBP, and distributor litigation** round out a long and active risk list.

There is no insider open-market buying to corroborate a value thesis at these levels — the only meaningful open-market transaction was a modest ~5,000-share sale by the Asia-Pacific group president at ~$82.6 in May 2026. The absence of insider conviction is meaningful for a stock that screens as statistically cheap.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 32.2% against 31.1% trailing realized — fairly priced, in the 30th percentile of the current chain. The put/call skew of 6.7% indicates elevated put demand at the front of the curve, and the term structure is essentially flat (32.2% near, 33.8% far), so uncertainty is distributed evenly rather than concentrated on an imminent binary. ATM IV rose 5.8 points (26.4% to 32.2%) since early May on a roughly flat price, suggesting the market is pricing somewhat higher uncertainty into the next earnings window. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±5.2%. The options market is mildly cautious, consistent with the fundamental read, rather than signaling either capitulation or informed bullish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
ZBH is a cash-generative incumbent in a structurally healthy, demographically supported end market, trading at roughly 10x forward earnings, a deep discount to a 30x+ peer group. If management executes the turnaround — Monogram closes the robotics gap, Paragon 28 builds foot & ankle, and the professionalized U.S. sales force stabilizes and eventually improves productivity — organic growth could re-accelerate toward the mid-single-digit market rate. A raised $1.0B buyback retires shares at depressed prices and supports EPS. In that scenario, multiple re-rating toward even the low-20s on $9+ EPS implies meaningful upside, and the $130 high analyst target reflects this path. Bull case ~$115 (roughly 13x on $9 EPS plus modest re-rating).

### Bear Case
The sales-force overhaul continues to leak accounts and depress organic growth through 2026-2027; the interim-CFO vacuum delays decisive capital and operational moves; the Foot & Ankle goodwill cushion gives way to a non-cash impairment; and the one-time tariff and pull-forward tailwinds reverse, exposing further EPS softness. Net income, already down to $705M, stalls or declines further, and the stock de-rates toward the lower end of its range as the turnaround timeline extends. Bear case ~$72 (roughly 8.5x on a normalized ~$8.40, stripping one-time items), about 14% below current.

The base case sits closer to the bear: continued sub-market organic growth, EPS supported mechanically by buybacks rather than operations, and a stock range-bound to modestly lower as the market waits for evidence of a turn. The risk/reward is skewed to the downside over the next 12 months because the catalysts are slow and execution-dependent while the risks are present and active.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: There is no single dated event likely to drive a sharp, near-term decline. The most plausible negative catalysts are (a) a Q2 or Q3 2026 earnings print revealing continued organic deceleration as one-time tailwinds roll off and pull-forward demand reverses, and (b) a goodwill impairment on the Global Foot & Ankle unit if it underperforms at the next annual test. Neither is precisely dated, and the impairment is a non-cash event the market may partly anticipate.

**Timing window**: The next earnings report (roughly late July 2026) is the nearest natural test of organic stabilization. The impairment risk is an annual-test item without a firm date. Neither offers a tight, high-conviction window.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Short interest is 6.2% of float with 3.5 days to cover — uncrowded. That leaves room to add a position but also signals that informed bears have not concentrated on the name. The buyback (up to $1.0B for 2026) actively reduces float and provides a persistent bid, which raises squeeze and carry risk for shorts.

**Borrow and options signal**: Front-end put skew of 6.7% confirms mild bearish positioning, but IV is fairly priced (32.2% vs 31.1% HV) and the term structure is flat; the options market is not pricing an imminent collapse. No unusual put activity. Borrow is likely cheap given low SI, but the buyback bid offsets that advantage.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: A bear-case target near $72 (about 14% downside) against upside risk from the buyback bid, a possible beat on the next print, or activist interest in an underperforming asset yields a reward:risk worse than 2:1. The asymmetry does not favor an active short.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE**. The thesis is a slow-bleeding underperformer with structural share loss, not an actively collapsing business with a dated trigger. The buyback provides a persistent bid, downside to a defensible target is limited relative to squeeze and carry risk, and there is no catalyst with a tight timing window. Avoid ownership; do not short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.**

**Re-entry criteria** (any would prompt re-evaluation toward HOLD or BUY):
- Organic constant-currency revenue growth re-accelerates to the mid-single-digit end-market rate (toward ~4-5%) for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating the sales-force transition is complete and additive rather than dilutive.
- 90-day forward EPS estimate revisions turn positive, signaling consensus is catching up to a genuine operational turn rather than to one-time items.
- A permanent CFO is appointed and the U.S. sales-force conversion is confirmed complete without further large-account losses.
- Net income inflects upward year-over-year, reversing the multi-year decline, confirming the margin/competitiveness problem is being resolved.
- Clustered insider open-market buying emerges, providing the insider-conviction signal currently absent.

**Timeline**: The headwinds — sales-force disruption, leadership transition, organic-growth weakness, and one-time-tailwind reversal — are likely to persist through at least the next two to three quarters and plausibly into 2027. The robotics catch-up versus Mako is a multi-year effort. We would not expect a clean turn before the second half of 2026 at the earliest, and only with hard evidence in the organic-growth and revision data.

## Conclusion

Zimmer Biomet is a financially solid but competitively impaired orthopedic incumbent. It generates real cash flow and trades at a statistically cheap ~10x forward earnings, but the discount is earned by multi-year share loss to Stryker, declining absolute net income, and organic growth that trails its own healthy end market. The Q1 2026 beat-and-raise was low-quality — propped up by a one-time tariff refund, FX, inorganic contribution, and flagged demand pull-forward — and the 6% selloff shows how sophisticated holders read the quarter. Layered on top are a high-stakes U.S. sales-force overhaul that has already cost two large accounts, an interim-CFO leadership vacuum, and a thin ~5% goodwill cushion on the Paragon 28 unit that could trigger a write-off.

The prior trajectory — scores declining from 6.0 in February to 5.4 in May, with competitive position and growth both falling — is confirmed rather than reversed by the current data. The valuation improvement noted in the trajectory is real but insufficient: a low multiple on a franchise still losing share is not asymmetric upside without a catalyst, and the available catalysts are slow and execution-dependent. With no estimate-revision turn, no insider buying, no dated near-term catalyst, and a buyback that supports the price without fixing the operations, there is no specific growth thesis that justifies holding on a 12-month horizon. We rate ZBH AVOID and would revisit only on hard evidence of organic stabilization, positive estimate revisions, and resolution of the leadership and sales-force transitions.
