## Executive Summary

Stellar Bancorp is a $10.8 billion Houston-based community bank that agreed to be acquired by Prosperity Bancshares (PB) on January 27, 2026, for approximately $39.09 per share in mixed consideration (0.3803 PB shares + $11.36 cash). The deal is expected to close in Q2 2026, creating the second-largest bank by deposits headquartered in Texas. This transaction converts STEL from a fundamental equity investment into a merger-arbitrage position where the relevant variables are deal completion probability, timeline, and Prosperity's stock price.

On standalone fundamentals, Stellar's trajectory is deteriorating: net income fell 14.6% YoY through September 2025, the loan book contracted 3.7%, nonperforming assets rose 39.3%, and the noninterest-bearing deposit ratio eroded from 39.2% to 36.4%. Capital ratios remain strong (CET1 14.43%) and net charge-offs are minimal (7 bps), but the earnings run-rate was clearly declining pre-merger. Management executed well-timed buybacks at ~$27.41/share average — roughly 30% below the merger consideration — and eliminated $70M in subordinated debt.

For most investors, STEL is a binary outcome centered on the Prosperity merger. The deal has high completion probability (unanimous board approval, no significant antitrust concerns, experienced acquirer, sound strategic logic), but the stock-heavy consideration structure means total value fluctuates with PB's share price. At current levels, the remaining upside to deal value is modest relative to the deal-break downside. This warrants a HOLD until the transaction closes or circumstances materially change.

## Company Overview

Stellar Bancorp was formed through the October 2022 merger of equals between Allegiance Bancshares and CBTX Inc. The combined entity operates 54 banking centers (37 in Houston MSA, 16 in Beaumont MSA, 1 in Dallas) focused on commercial banking services for small-to-medium-sized businesses and professionals. The bank is a pure-play community bank with revenue overwhelmingly derived from net interest income.

The loan portfolio is concentrated in commercial real estate (52.1%), commercial & industrial (18.6%), 1-4 family residential (15.9%), and CRE construction/land development (10.5%). Total assets stood at $10.81B at year-end 2025, with $7.30B in loans and $9.02B in deposits. The bank carries $497.3M in goodwill from the Allegiance/CBTX merger and prior acquisitions, representing 30.1% of shareholders' equity.

The pending acquisition by Prosperity Bancshares is the defining event for the company. The combined entity will have approximately $49B in assets and over 330 banking centers across Texas and Oklahoma.

## Financial Analysis

### Earnings Trajectory — Declining

Full-year 2025 net income was $102.9M, down 10.5% from $115.0M in 2024. Through the first nine months of 2025, diluted EPS fell 12.5% to $1.47 (from $1.68). The decline is attributable to three factors:

1. **Purchase accounting accretion runoff**: $15.5M in 9M 2025 vs. $25.4M in 9M 2024 — a $9.9M headwind that is mathematically certain to continue compressing.
2. **Loan book contraction**: Total loans fell $272M (3.7%) YTD through September 2025. Average loans dropped $506M (6.5%) YoY, directly reducing interest income.
3. **Provision swing**: The bank recorded $5.0M in provisions for 9M 2025 vs. a $3.8M reversal in the prior year — an $8.8M pre-tax earnings headwind.

Net interest income fell 2.2% to $298.2M despite a 10.3% reduction in interest expense, because interest income declined faster (down 4.9%). The tax-equivalent NIM of 4.19% compressed only 4 basis points from 4.23%, masking the underlying decline driven by purchase accounting runoff.

### Margin Quality

The reported 4.19% NIM includes purchase accounting accretion that inflates the figure. Annualizing the $15.5M in 9M accretion yields roughly $20.7M in annual benefit on an average earning asset base of approximately $9.9B, or roughly 21 basis points of NIM attributable to a non-recurring source. The "core" NIM is therefore closer to 3.98%. This accretion will continue declining as the 2022 merger marks amortize.

Cost of deposits improved to 1.94% from 2.09%, reflecting disciplined deposit pricing and lower CD rates. However, the ongoing migration of noninterest-bearing deposits (down from 39.2% to 36.4% of total deposits) will gradually offset this improvement as more balances require rate-based retention.

### Capital and Liquidity — Strong

Capital ratios are a clear strength: CET1 at 14.43% and total capital at 16.33% significantly exceed well-capitalized thresholds. The loan-to-deposit ratio of 81.3% is conservative, and contingent funding sources of $5.23B represent 59.3% of total deposits.

Management deployed capital effectively pre-merger: $64.2M in buybacks at ~$27.41/share average (vs. ~$39 merger price) and $70M in subordinated debt redemptions that simplified the capital stack and reduced interest expense.

### Credit Quality — Deteriorating But Manageable

Nonperforming assets increased 39.3% from $38.9M to $54.2M over nine months. Nonaccrual CRE loans rose from $16.5M to $25.2M. Foreclosed assets surged from $1.7M to $7.9M. Special mention loans increased from $125.0M to $133.2M, with CRE special mentions rising from $99.2M to $108.3M.

The ACL coverage ratio fell from 263% to 171% of nonperforming loans. Net charge-offs remain minimal at 7 basis points annualized, but the trajectory is unfavorable. The allowance for unfunded commitments increased from $12.4M to $15.8M alongside a $330M increase in commitments to extend credit.

For Prosperity, the credit portfolio will undergo purchase accounting re-marking at close, so the ACL levels are somewhat academic. The relevant question is whether there are embedded losses that will emerge post-merger. At 0.51% NPA/total assets and 7 bps net charge-offs, the current level of stress is well within manageable bounds.

### Free Cash Flow

Full-year 2025 free cash flow was $92.6M, roughly 90% of net income. This is a reasonable conversion rate for a bank, where capital expenditures are minimal relative to operating cash flow. The FCF comfortably covers the $21.6M in annualized dividends ($0.14/quarter × ~51.2M shares × 3 quarters reported).

## Growth Analysis

Standalone growth prospects are weak. The loan book contracted across nearly all categories, with CRE construction/land development down 10.9%, residential construction down 23.3%, and C&I down 2.2%. Only 1-4 family residential grew (+2.4%). This pattern suggests either deliberate de-risking in anticipation of the merger, competitive pressure from larger institutions with lower pricing, or weak regional loan demand.

Average deposits fell from $9.34B to $8.95B (4.2% decline), with the most pronounced runoff in public fund deposits — FHLB letters of credit pledged for public deposits dropped from $2.10B to $1.07B, a $1.03B reduction. This appears to be intentional runoff of rate-sensitive government deposits, a defensible balance sheet optimization strategy.

Organic growth catalysts are limited for a $10.8B single-state community bank competing against JPMorgan, Wells Fargo, and other well-capitalized regional banks in the Houston market. The $10B+ asset threshold brings additional regulatory burden (Durbin Amendment, stress testing considerations) without the scale to easily absorb those costs. This is precisely why the Prosperity merger makes strategic sense.

## Valuation Assessment

STEL's valuation is now almost entirely a function of the merger consideration. The deal terms are 0.3803 PB shares + $11.36 cash per STEL share. At Prosperity's announcement-date price of $72.90, this implied ~$39.09/share total consideration.

On standalone metrics through 9M 2025:
- **Price/Tangible Book Value**: Roughly 1.68x (at ~$35.50/share vs. estimated TBV of ~$21.09/share)
- **P/E on annualized 9M 2025 earnings**: Approximately 18.1x ($35.50 / ~$1.96 annualized diluted EPS)
- **Price/Book**: ~1.10x ($35.50 / ~$32.27 book value per share)

The merger consideration of ~$39.09 represents a premium of roughly 1.21x book value, modest for a well-capitalized Texas community bank. The 36.4% noninterest-bearing deposit ratio is an asset that larger acquirers value for its zero-cost funding benefit.

The stock-heavy consideration (roughly 71% stock at announcement) means the effective deal price moves with PB's share price. If PB trades at $80, the deal is worth ~$41.78; if PB trades at $65, the deal is worth ~$36.08. This introduces correlation risk that pure cash deals do not carry.

## Competitive Landscape

Stellar operates in one of the most competitive banking markets in the US. The Houston MSA is served by all major national banks and numerous strong community and regional institutions. Stellar's competitive advantages include:

1. **Deposit franchise quality**: 36.4% noninterest-bearing deposits, while declining, remains well above industry average.
2. **Local relationships**: 54 branches in Houston and Beaumont with experienced relationship bankers targeting SMB clients.
3. **Capital strength**: CET1 of 14.43% provides significant cushion and flexibility.

The competitive disadvantages that drove the merger decision are equally clear: insufficient scale to absorb $10B+ regulatory costs, inability to compete on pricing with national banks, and a shrinking loan book suggesting competitive displacement in loan origination. The combination with Prosperity addresses all three by creating a $49B institution with 330+ branches, substantially better cost absorption, and dominant Houston market presence.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risk: Merger Non-Completion

The $78M termination fee and the deal-break scenario are the dominant risks. If the merger fails, STEL would likely trade back toward the pre-deal range of $25-28, representing 25-30% downside from current levels. Mitigants: unanimous board approval, no obvious antitrust issues (combining two Texas banks below SIFi thresholds), experienced acquirer with M&A track record, and strong strategic rationale. Estimated probability of deal closure: 85-90%.

### Secondary Risk: Prosperity Stock Price Decline

With ~71% of consideration in PB stock, a significant decline in Prosperity's share price would reduce the effective deal value. Prosperity itself faces community bank challenges (NIM compression, CRE concentration, deposit competition), amplified by integration execution risk of absorbing a $10.8B acquisition.

### Tertiary Risks (Relevant Primarily if Deal Breaks)

- **CRE concentration at 62.6% of loans** exceeds typical regulatory guidance. Office exposure ($382M, 10.3% of CRE) is the most vulnerable segment.
- **Uninsured deposits at 46.0%** of total deposits create tail risk during confidence shocks in a post-SVB environment.
- **Geographic concentration** in Houston/Beaumont. Energy sector downturns, hurricane damage, or regional economic weakness would disproportionately impact the portfolio.
- **Declining earnings run-rate**: Full-year net income fell 10.5% in 2025 with further accretion runoff ahead.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The merger closes on schedule in Q2 2026 at or above the implied consideration of ~$39.09/share. Prosperity's stock price appreciates, increasing the value of the stock component above announcement levels. The remaining deal spread closes as regulatory approvals are obtained. In this scenario, STEL delivers a mid-to-high single-digit annualized return from current levels to close, with limited downside given the cash component provides a floor of $11.36/share even in extreme scenarios.

### Bear Case

The merger fails to obtain regulatory approval, or Prosperity's stock declines significantly before close. In a deal-break scenario, STEL reverts to standalone valuation in the $25-28 range. The standalone business faces a declining earnings trajectory, shrinking loan book, rising nonperforming assets, and insufficient scale to compete effectively above the $10B regulatory threshold. Management would need to find another strategic path (new merger partner, aggressive organic growth, or de-banking below $10B) in a weakened position.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (to BUY)
- If STEL trades at a spread wider than 8% to implied deal value, indicating the market is pricing excessive deal-break risk not supported by regulatory fundamentals.
- If Prosperity's stock declines to levels where the total deal consideration drops near or below standalone book value (~$32/share), creating asymmetric risk/reward with the cash component as a floor.

### Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID)
- Material regulatory opposition to the merger (formal objection, extended review period beyond Q3 2026, or public statements of concern from DOJ/OCC/FDIC).
- Prosperity announces a material credit event, capital shortfall, or management change that calls into question their ability to close.
- STEL credit quality deteriorates sharply (NPAs >1.5% of total assets, net charge-offs >50 bps annualized) in a way that could trigger material adverse change clauses.

### Reassessment Timeline
- **Next key date**: Shareholder vote and regulatory approval milestones (expected Q2 2026).
- **Reassess**: On any regulatory filing that reveals the merger review timeline, or if PB's stock moves more than ±15% from announcement levels.

## Conclusion

Stellar Bancorp is a merger-arb position. The pending Prosperity Bancshares acquisition at ~$39.09/share (mixed consideration) has high probability of closing in Q2 2026. The standalone fundamentals are deteriorating, but largely irrelevant if the deal closes on schedule.

At current prices, the remaining deal spread provides modest upside against meaningful deal-break downside. The stock-heavy consideration introduces correlation with Prosperity's share price. For investors without a specific view on PB's stock direction or merger timing, the position is a HOLD: the upside is capped, the downside in a break scenario is material, and the catalysts are binary and time-bound. Merger-arbitrage investors should evaluate the deal spread against their cost of capital and deal-break probability estimates. For fundamental equity investors seeking growth or income, STEL offers neither in its current form.
