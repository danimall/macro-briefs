## Executive Summary

Vale enters the second half of 2026 with a genuinely improved operating base and a deteriorating earnings translation. First-half revenue rose 12.8% to $19,499M and operating income rose 14.9% to $4,468M, with gross margin essentially flat at 31.9% versus 31.8% a year earlier. Second-quarter iron ore output was the highest for any second quarter since 2018 — the year before the Brumadinho dam collapse — and copper revenue rose 73.5% year over year. Against that, first-half net income fell 11.1% to $3,216M. The entire gap between operating growth and net decline sits below the operating line: Reuters attributed the Q2 net profit of $1.38bn (versus $1.85bn consensus) to "financial effects including derivatives and taxes." Adjusted EBITDA of $3.68bn was a clean miss against the $3.83bn LSEG consensus; the widely circulated $4.07bn "core" figure applies further adjustments and is not a same-basis comparison.

The decisive new information from the July 30 release was not the earnings miss but the guidance change: Vale raised 2026 iron ore all-in and production cost projections by roughly 11% at the midpoints, attributed to a stronger Brazilian real and higher oil. Vale sells in dollars and incurs a large share of costs in reais, so this compresses margin mechanically with no operational offset. Brent near $104/bbl compounds the diesel, freight and pellet-energy line. Three brokers cut targets within three weeks (Goldman and BofA both to Neutral at $16 from $18; Scotiabank $19→$16), each citing the combination of weaker iron ore fundamentals and cost-driven downward earnings revisions. Consensus current-year EPS has been cut 9.4% in 30 days to $1.92 from $2.12.

The valuation looks cheap on the headline and merely reasonable on inspection. A 7.75x multiple on $1.92 implies net income near $8.2bn on 4.26bn shares — roughly four times TTM net income of ~$2.0bn and 33% above FY2024's $6.17bn, which would be Vale's best result since 2022. Much of that gap is legitimate: FY2025 GAAP earnings absorbed roughly $4bn of unusual charges concentrated in a single quarter. But if normalized earnings power is closer to FY2024's $6.2bn, the multiple is ~10.3x. On normalized EBITDA of $13–14bn, enterprise value of ~$80bn implies 5.7–6.2x, in line with large-cap diversified miners rather than below them. With a ~6% distribution yield, an unquantified legacy dam liability, and 77% of revenue exposed to an iron ore market that three sell-side houses have just downgraded, the risk-adjusted return from $14.89 is approximately fair. We rate the shares HOLD.

## Company Overview

Vale is one of the world's largest iron ore producers and a top-tier nickel and copper producer, operating through two reported segments: Iron Ore Solutions and Vale Base Metals. It owns and operates an integrated railroad, port and maritime terminal network in Brazil, which is a structural cost advantage in seaborne ferrous rather than a standalone profit centre. Founded in 1942, roughly 65,800 employees, with operations and commercial presence across Brazil, Canada, Indonesia, Oman, Malaysia and Asian and European trading hubs.

Revenue composition is the most important structural fact about the company, and it has shifted:

| Segment (USD M) | FY2024 | FY2025 | TTM Jun'26 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Iron ore fines | 24,805 | 25,010 | 26,427 |
| Iron ore pellets | 5,921 | 4,396 | 4,429 |
| Other ferrous | 718 | 724 | 700 |
| Nickel & other | 3,762 | 4,723 | 5,043 |
| Copper | 2,852 | 3,550 | 4,638 |
| **Total** | **38,056** | **38,403** | **41,216** |

Ferrous fell from 82.6% of revenue in FY2024 to 78.5% in FY2025 and 76.6% on a TTM basis; base metals rose from 17.4% to 23.5%. This should not be read as achieved diversification. Copper revenue of $4,638M TTM reconciles almost exactly with 2026 guidance volumes of 360–380kt at the current ~$12,430/t spot price (370kt × $12,430 ≈ $4.6bn), which means the mix shift is predominantly price-driven. If copper mean-reverts, the mix reverts with it. In economic substance Vale remains an iron ore company with a large, currently very profitable base-metals option attached.

**A disclosure note that materially affects confidence.** Vale is a Brazilian foreign private issuer: it files a 20-F annually and 6-K reports for interim events, with no 10-Q, no DEF 14A and no Section 16 insider filings. The FY2025 20-F (filed March 27, 2026) was not readable in this cycle, so the business description, risk factors, MD&A, provision schedules, governance and compensation are not filing-verified here. Income-statement and balance-sheet figures below come from a third-party standardizer whose USD income statement reconciles internally to within 1.6–2.6% on FY2025 aggregates, but whose cash-flow and dividend-per-share tables mix currencies and are internally contradictory. Consequently **TTM free cash flow is unresolved and no FCF-derived conclusion is drawn.** The absence of insider-purchase data here is a regime artifact, not a negative signal.

## Financial Analysis

### The multi-year arc

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 54,502 | 43,839 | 41,784 | 38,056 | 38,403 | 41,216 |
| Gross margin | 60.1% | 45.2% | 42.4% | 36.2% | 35.0% | 34.9% |
| Operating income | 27,693 | 17,208 | 14,205 | 10,788 | 5,897 | 6,629 |
| Net income | 22,445 | 18,788 | 7,983 | 6,166 | 2,352 | ~2,000 |
| EPS | 4.47 | 4.05 | 1.83 | 1.44 | 0.55 | ~0.47 |

The ~25-point gross-margin compression from 2021 to 2024-25 reflects the unwind of the iron ore price spike plus cost inflation. Margin has stabilized rather than continued falling: H1 2026 gross margin of 31.9% versus 31.8% in H1 2025. That stabilization is the single best piece of evidence in the file for a floor on earnings power.

### The 2026 half-year: an operating result the reported number obscures

| H1 (USD M) | H1 2025 | H1 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 17,289 | 19,499 | +12.8% |
| Gross profit | 5,501 | 6,211 | +12.9% |
| Operating income | 3,888 | 4,468 | +14.9% |
| Operating margin | 22.5% | 22.9% | +40bp |
| Net income | 3,618 | 3,216 | −11.1% |

Vale's GAAP net income is currently a poor proxy for operating performance, and P/E is correspondingly a poor primary metric. That argues for EV/EBITDA and EBIT-based analysis, which is how we frame the valuation below. It does not make the below-the-line damage costless: derivative and FX losses can cash-settle, and recurring hedging losses can indicate a book positioned against the actual currency and price moves occurring. Two consecutive quarters of below-the-line damage is not yet a pattern; a third would be.

### The fourth-quarter charge pattern

Q4 2025 was the highest-revenue quarter in the visible series at $11,060M with a 38.71% gross margin — yet operating income was −$697M and net income −$3,844M. Reconstructing the stack from reported revenue and margin percentages: costs below gross profit were roughly $4,978M against a ~$1.0bn normal quarterly run-rate (Q3'25 $1,018M; Q2'26 $1,033M), implying roughly $3.9–4.0bn of unusual operating charges. Below-operating items cost a further ~$1.4bn, and tax plus minority interest cost ~$2.1bn **despite a pretax loss**. Q4 2024 carried roughly $2.0bn of comparable operating charges and also produced a net loss.

Adding back ~$4bn of Q4'25 unusual charges to FY2025 operating income of $5,897M gives ~$9.9bn, broadly in line with FY2024's reported $10,788M. That reconciliation is what explains a 31.4x trailing P/E sitting alongside a ~7.8x forward multiple. The recurrence across two consecutive fourth quarters suggests annual impairment testing and/or provision remeasurement functions as a systematic Q4 event — a durable quality-of-earnings consideration rather than a one-off. The composition is unverified; a deferred-tax valuation allowance would specifically explain the tax charge on a pretax loss, as would large non-deductible provisions under Brazilian tax law. No motive should be inferred from the accounting treatment.

Gross debt rose $7.41bn (+53.2%) and net debt $5.79bn (+56.4%) between December 2023 and March 2026, while common equity fell $2.81bn. Debt/equity moved from 0.31 to 0.58. Current ratio 1.15 and quick ratio 0.65 at FY2025 are thin for a company of this size. Net debt to normalized EBITDA of roughly 1.15–1.23x on a $13–14bn assumption means this is not a solvency question, but the direction of travel has been adverse across most of the last six reported quarter-ends. Rising gross debt alongside rising cash through the September–December 2025 period is at least partly consistent with pre-funding rather than stress; the $2.29bn sequential cash decline into March 2026 is plausibly seasonal (Brazilian rainy season plus distributions) but cannot be confirmed without the cash-flow statement.

Book value per share was $8.57 and tangible book $6.38 at March 31, 2026, putting the stock at ~1.73x book and ~2.33x tangible book.

The line item that matters most and can be verified least is "other long-term liabilities" of $16,357M at December 31, 2025, which would house Samarco/Mariana and Brumadinho provisions plus asset-retirement obligations. The remaining balance, disbursement schedule and duration are not established. Any free-cash-flow or dividend-capacity model requires that input.

### Distributions

Vale distributed $1.7bn via dividends and interest on equity in the quarter and authorized a new buyback of up to 100 million shares over 18 months. On a TTM dividend of $0.91 the yield is 6.11% at $14.89 — note that the quote feed reports 1.04%, which appears to capture a single tranche rather than the trailing twelve months; we use the TTM basis and flag the conflict. The TTM GAAP payout ratio of 194% is an artifact of the FY2025 charges rather than evidence of a broken dividend — on normalized EPS of $1.50–1.94 the payout would be 47–61% — but it is not proof of safety either, since the policy formula and free cash flow are unverified.

The buyback authorization of 100 million shares equals 2.35% of the ~4,260M outstanding; if fully executed, continuing holders' proportional ownership would rise roughly 2.40%. No shares have been repurchased under it. Vale retired roughly 15% of its shares in 2021–2023, then went nearly dormant (0.12% buyback yield in FY2025) before this restart.

## Growth Analysis

Three growth drivers are working and two are impaired.

Working: **volume recovery in ferrous.** The highest Q2 iron ore output since 2018 is real operating progress and supports unit-cost absorption. **Copper and nickel guidance low ends were each raised 10kt** (copper to 360–380kt, nickel to 185–200kt). **Base-metals realized prices** are extraordinary — copper ~$12,430/t, gold $4,713/oz, silver $75.50/oz — and the precious/PGM by-product credits reduce net nickel and copper unit costs, which is the mechanical explanation for the reported step-up in Base Metals EBITDA (a +79% year-over-year figure circulates from a single third-party source and is unconfirmed against the company release).

Impaired: **pellets.** Pellet revenue fell from $5,921M in FY2024 to $4,396M in FY2025 (−25.8%) while fines revenue was flat, with quarterly run-rate stepping from $1.40–1.50bn down to $1.00–1.19bn. Pellets are the premium, higher-margin, lower-carbon ferrous product and a core plank of the grade-premium and green-steel case. The driver is unverified — collapsed premiums (cyclical, recoverable), lower volumes, or a scope change — and the two readings have very different implications. **Estimate momentum** is the second impairment: current-quarter consensus is down 4.9% in 30 days, current-year down 9.4% to $1.92 and down 8.8% over 90 days. Only next year has edged up (+1.9%). Three of the last four quarters missed, including −32.9% in Q2 2026.

The volume story is catch-up to a 2018 baseline rather than expansion, and the price story is a commodity setup Vale does not control. The 11% cost guidance raise lands disproportionately in the second half. We do not see the estimate-revision inflection that would justify treating current consensus as a floor.

## Valuation Assessment

**Enterprise value.** Market cap ~$63.4bn + net debt $16.05bn (Mar 31, 2026) + minority interest ~$0.9bn ≈ **$80.3bn**.

**On trailing GAAP, the stock is uninformative:** 31.4x P/E, distorted by the Q4'25 charge.

**On forward consensus:** $14.89 / $1.92 = **7.75x** current year; / $2.06 = **7.23x** next year. The demanding part is what $1.92 implies: 4.26bn shares × $1.92 = **~$8.2bn of net income**, roughly 4x TTM (~$2.0bn) and 33% above FY2024's $6.17bn. Much of the gap is defensible because the ~$4bn Q4'25 charge should not recur at that scale. Getting to $8.2bn nonetheless requires copper and nickel holding near current extraordinary levels, iron ore not deteriorating, and the 11% cost increase not compounding. If normalized earnings sit closer to FY2024's $6.2bn (EPS ~$1.45), the multiple is **10.3x** — respectable for a diversified miner but not a deep-value screen. We also cannot confirm whether the consensus figure is GAAP or adjusted, which would further narrow the implied recovery.

**On EBITDA, which we prefer given the below-the-line noise:** Q2'26 further-adjusted EBITDA of $4.07bn annualizes naively to $16.3bn and 4.9x EV/EBITDA, but that overstates the year — Q1 is seasonally weakest (Q1'26 revenue $9,258M versus Q4'25 $11,060M) and the cost increase lands in H2. A normalized alternative of $13–14bn points to **5.7–6.2x EV/EBITDA** and net debt/EBITDA of ~1.15–1.23x.

That is the crux. At ~6x EV/EBITDA and 1.73x book, Vale trades in line with — not meaningfully below — large-cap seaborne peers, while carrying higher country risk, unquantified legacy provisions, a recurring Q4 charge pattern, and thinner disclosure. The ~6% distribution yield is the clearest element of absolute support. Peer comparison also cuts against the bull case on supply: BHP-controlled Escondida produced 111,400t of copper in June 2026, +45.8% year over year, indicating copper supply is responding to price.

**Sell-side.** 25 analysts, consensus label "Buy," mean target $16.80 (+12.8%), median $16.00, range $12.00–$21.00. The revision direction matters more than the label: two downgrades to Neutral in three weeks with targets cut $18→$16, plus Scotiabank $19→$16 and Wells Fargo at $15. The modal recent target is $16, ~7% above spot. Only JPMorgan sits materially above at $21, and that house's rating is ambiguous across reports. The recommendation distribution has drifted from 4 strong buy / 9 buy / 12 hold three months ago to 3 / 7 / 15 today. This is a lagging "Buy" label attached to negative revision momentum — the pattern that historically makes a low headline multiple a trap rather than an opportunity in cyclicals.

**Base case:** $13.6bn FY2026 EBITDA × 5.8x = $78.9bn EV, less ~$15.5bn year-end net debt and $0.9bn minorities = $62.5bn equity ≈ **$14.67/share**, blended with the $16.00 analyst median to **$15.50** (+4.1% price, ~10% total return with the distribution).

## Competitive Landscape

Vale's moat in iron ore is grade and logistics — high-Fe Carajás ore plus owned rail, ports and terminals. The trajectory has been repair rather than extension. It took roughly seven and a half years to return quarterly iron ore output to pre-Brumadinho levels; during that interval the seaborne market was supplied by others. The Q2 2026 record is good news for 2026 volumes and unit costs, and it also frames the cost: Vale spent most of a cycle rebuilding to a prior baseline while competitors invested in growth.

Two structural pressures on the grade franchise deserve weight. First, the pellet revenue erosion described above removes ~$1.5bn of annualized revenue from the highest-differentiation product line. Second, new high-grade West African supply — principally Rio Tinto's Simandou — is widely expected to add structural high-grade tonnage to the seaborne market, pressuring both benchmark prices and Vale's grade premium. We could not obtain ramp-rate evidence or 2026–27 price forecasts, so we treat this as a named unresolved risk rather than a quantified one, but it is the specific mechanism by which the ferrous moat could narrow.

The commodity backdrop is sharply bifurcated and this is the setup's central tension. Vale's smaller segment (23.5% of revenue) is in a price boom; its dominant segment (76.6%) faces softening prices and an 11% cost-guidance increase, with Brent at $104/bbl feeding the cost side of both. That is an unfavourable configuration for a company whose base-metals strength is price-driven rather than asset-driven: a simultaneous iron ore downcycle would not be well hedged.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Cost inflation from BRL strength and oil — company-confirmed and quantified.** The ~11% increase to 2026 iron ore all-in and production cost projections is the highest-conviction near-term negative in the file. Vale sells in USD and incurs a large share of costs in BRL; real appreciation compresses margin mechanically with no operational offset.

**2. Iron ore price and demand against ~77% ferrous concentration.** Three brokers independently flagged a weakening backdrop within three weeks. Chinese steel demand is the swing variable.

**3. Legacy dam liabilities (Brumadinho 2019, ~270 deaths; Samarco/Mariana 2015).** The obligations are real and large; the remaining provision balance and cash disbursement schedule are not established here. This is a first-order unknown for free-cash-flow and dividend modelling, and the most likely source of an unpriced negative surprise given the Q4 remeasurement pattern.

**4. Balance-sheet trajectory.** +53% gross debt and +56% net debt since December 2023, quick ratio 0.65, with buybacks restarting on top of a ~6% dividend. Sustainable if normalized EBITDA holds near $14bn; aggressive if the cost increase compounds.

**5. Recurring Q4 charges.** Two consecutive fourth quarters produced net losses despite strong revenue. Investors should expect the pattern until the composition is disclosed and explained.

**6. Below-the-line volatility.** The Q2 net-profit shortfall came entirely from derivatives, FX and tax. Vale will disappoint on reported EPS even when operations perform.

**7. Brazil sovereign, tax, royalty and licensing exposure**, plus reduced disclosure cadence inherent to foreign-private-issuer status (no 10-Q, no Form 4, 20-F filed roughly three months after year-end).

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 40.5% sits 8.6 points above 31.9% one-year realized, and the term structure is in backwardation (40.5% near versus 34.1% at six months) — nominally "imminent stress." That reading should be discounted: the 40.5% print is the two-day August 14 tenor, where expiry mechanics dominate, and the one-month tenor at 27.7% is actually *cheap* relative to realized volatility. Positioning does not corroborate a bearish thesis: put/call open interest of 0.21 is heavily call-tilted, put/call volume is 0.65, and skew is balanced at −0.4%. Short interest of 1.4% of float and 2.5 days to cover means neither a crowded short nor an informed bearish base. The six-month tenor prices a 15.3% implied move, consistent with genuine dispersion into the seasonally charge-prone fourth quarter. Net read: the options market prices event risk around the December expiry without a directional bearish tilt, which is a mild divergence from our fundamental caution and one reason we hold rather than reduce.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $20.50 (+37.7%)

Iron ore holds near current levels as Vale's volume recovery to pre-2019 run-rates lowers unit costs and lifts the grade premium; copper and nickel prices stay near $12,400/t and current nickel levels, with elevated gold, silver and PGM by-product credits continuing to suppress net base-metals cash costs; the real weakens against the dollar, reversing part of the 11% cost guidance raise; the Q4'25 charge does not repeat, and FY2026 EBITDA reaches $16bn. At 6.5x EV/EBITDA that is $104bn EV, less ~$15.5bn net debt and $0.9bn minorities, or roughly $20.50/share — consistent with the $21 high target. The 100 million-share buyback is fully executed, adding ~2.4% to proportional ownership, and the ~6% distribution continues. In this path the market is currently over-extrapolating a single quarter's cost guidance and under-crediting the base-metals earnings stream.

### Bear Case — $10.50 (−29.5%)

Chinese steel demand weakens further and Simandou tonnage begins pressuring both benchmark iron ore and the high-grade premium; the real stays strong and Brent stays above $100, so the 11% cost increase compounds rather than reverses; copper mean-reverts as supply responds (Escondida +45.8% year over year is the leading indicator); FY2026 EBITDA falls to $11.5bn. At 5.2x that is $59.8bn EV, less $17bn net debt and $0.9bn minorities, or roughly $9.85/share. A Q4 2026 provision remeasurement or impairment of the scale seen in each of the last two fourth quarters would compound the derating and could force a dividend reset, which is the specific mechanism that would break the income anchor. Bear case rounds to $10.50, below the $12.00 analyst low.

### Probability-weighted view

At 25% bull / 45% base / 30% bear the expected price is $15.25, or roughly $16.15 including twelve months of distributions — an expected total return near 8.5% with wide dispersion (+37.7% to −29.5%). That is approximately fair compensation for the risk taken, and it does not clear the bar for adding capital.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD. Horizon: 12 months, with the Q3 2026 release (late October) and the FY2026 results/20-F cycle (February–March 2027) as the decision points.**

- **Base case: $15.50** — $13.6bn FY2026 EBITDA × 5.8x EV/EBITDA = $78.9bn EV, less ~$15.5bn net debt and $0.9bn minorities = ~$14.67 equity value per share, blended with the $16.00 analyst median. **+4.1% price appreciation, ~10% total return with the ~6% distribution.**
- **Bull case: $20.50 (+37.7%)** — $16bn EBITDA at 6.5x.
- **Bear case: $10.50 (−29.5%)** — $11.5bn EBITDA at 5.2x.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Cost guidance stabilizes or reverses** — Q3 2026 confirmation that 2026 iron ore all-in cost is tracking at or below the revised midpoint, or a BRL depreciation that management explicitly credits as a margin offset.
2. **Current-year consensus EPS stops falling for two consecutive months** and the next-year figure ($2.06 today) holds or rises, ending the −9.4% 30-day revision trend.
3. **Price weakness to $11.50–12.50** without fundamental deterioration, which would put the shares at roughly 4.8–5.2x normalized EBITDA and an 7.3–7.9% distribution yield — an entry we would take.
4. **Disclosure of the Samarco/Brumadinho remaining provision balance and disbursement schedule** at a level consistent with sustaining both the dividend and the buyback.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. **A third consecutive quarter of material below-the-line damage** (derivatives/FX/tax) that converts operating growth into net decline — two quarters is noise, three is a pattern and evidence of a hedge book positioned against actual market moves.
2. **Any dividend or interest-on-equity reduction**, or execution of the buyback while net debt rises through $18bn.
3. **Iron ore realized prices falling more than ~15% from current levels**, or a further guidance increase to 2026 costs — either would push FY2026 EBITDA toward the $11.5bn bear assumption.
4. **A Q4 2026 charge of comparable scale to 2024 or 2025** without a disclosed, non-recurring explanation, which would establish provision remeasurement as an annual claim on shareholder value rather than an accounting event.

**Reassessment timeline:** late October 2026 (Q3 results and production report) for cost tracking and estimate direction; February–March 2027 (FY2026 results and the FY2026 20-F) for the provision schedule, cash-flow statement and the composition of any fourth-quarter charge. The 20-F is the filing that would resolve most of the open questions in this report and should be read before any rating change in either direction.

## Conclusion

Vale's operating business is performing better than its reported earnings suggest. Revenue grew 12.8% and operating income 14.9% in the first half on flat gross margin, iron ore volumes returned to pre-Brumadinho quarterly levels, and copper revenue grew 73.5% year over year. The company is distributing roughly 6% and has authorized a 2.35% share reduction. None of that is in dispute.

What prevents a constructive rating is the combination of a company-confirmed 11% increase to 2026 iron ore cost guidance, a 9.4% thirty-day cut to current-year consensus, three broker target reductions citing a weakening iron ore backdrop against 77% ferrous revenue concentration, base-metals strength that arithmetic shows to be price- rather than asset-driven, and a valuation that on normalized EBITDA (~6x) and book value (1.73x) sits in line with peers rather than below them. The headline 7.75x forward multiple requires consensus net income near $8.2bn — four times TTM and a third above FY2024 — at exactly the moment estimates are being cut. Layered on top are two genuinely unquantified items: the remaining Samarco and Brumadinho provisions with no disclosed disbursement schedule, and a fourth-quarter charge pattern that has produced net losses in each of the last two years despite record revenue.

Expected total return of roughly 8.5% against a −29.5% bear case and a +37.7% bull case is approximately fair compensation. Existing holders are paid ~6% to wait for cost and iron ore clarity in the October print. Prospective buyers have no urgency at $14.89; the entry we would take is $11.50–12.50, or confirmation that the cost guidance raise was a single-quarter FX and oil artifact rather than the start of a margin trend.
