## Executive Summary

MYR Group delivered a strong Q1 2026 — revenue up 20% YoY to $1.0B, EPS of $2.99 vs. $2.08 consensus (+44% beat), record backlog of $2.84B, and essentially zero net debt. The results confirm structural margin expansion (T&D at 9.7%, C&I at 8.1%) beyond the FY2024 execution trough, driven by better contract terms, prefabrication capabilities, and disciplined project selection. Management raised FY2026 organic revenue growth guidance to 12% and lifted both segment margin targets.

The demand backdrop is exceptionally strong: data center electrical construction starts up ~100% YoY, 345/765 kV transmission projects entering the pipeline through 2032, a ramping $500M Xcel MSA, and grid modernization/electrification tailwinds creating decade-long visibility. Estimate revisions of +27% for FY2026 EPS over 90 days reflect genuine fundamental acceleration.

The challenge is pricing. At $454.78, the stock trades at ~40x FY2026 consensus EPS of $11.38 and approximately 70% above peer median P/E of 29.4x. The 197% appreciation from 12 months ago and 62% from one month ago has compressed the remaining upside relative to even bull-case scenarios. While growth momentum and margin expansion are real, the current multiple embeds significant execution perfection, leaving the stock vulnerable to any quarter of estimate-change volatility — a demonstrated risk in this business model.

## Company Overview

MYR Group provides specialty electrical construction services through two segments: Transmission & Distribution (54% of revenue — high-voltage lines, substations, distribution, clean energy interconnection) and Commercial & Industrial (46% — data centers, airports, hospitals, manufacturing, primarily as a subcontractor). Headquartered in Thornton, Colorado, the company traces its operating lineage to 1891 and employs a largely unionized craft workforce (~85% covered by collective bargaining agreements).

The business model combines MSA-based recurring work (70% of T&D revenue) with fixed-price project work (57% of total revenue, 85% in C&I). Revenue is recognized on a percentage-of-completion basis, creating inherent quarter-to-quarter volatility as cost estimates are revised. The company operates through multiple subsidiary brands including L.E. Myers, Harlan Electric, and Sturgeon Electric.

## Growth Analysis

MYR Group sits at the intersection of several multi-year demand drivers:

**1. Data Center Electrical Construction:** FMI's 2026 outlook shows data center construction starts up ~100% YoY. MYRG's C&I segment posted record $459M Q1 revenue (+24% YoY), with data centers cited as the primary driver. Projects awarded across New Jersey, Arizona, California, and Colorado.

**2. Transmission Buildout (2027-2032 Visibility):** CEO Swartz confirmed active conversations about large 345 kV and 765 kV projects with construction beginning mid-2027 at the earliest. He compared this to the Texas CREZ buildout of 2013-2015 but "going across the United States over the next 10 years." This provides extraordinary long-term visibility for T&D revenue.

**3. MSA Revenue Stability:** 70% of T&D revenue under MSAs (typically 1-4 years), with backlog only capturing 90 days of MSA work. The $500M, 5-year Xcel Energy MSA ramping through 2026-2027 represents significant incremental revenue not fully reflected in the $2.84B backlog.

**4. Grid Modernization:** Aging infrastructure, wildfire hardening requirements, NERC reliability standards, and electrification (EVs, heat pumps, reshoring) all drive incremental demand.

**Estimate Revision Momentum:**
- FY2026 EPS: +27.4% over 90 days (from $8.93 to $11.38)
- FY2027 EPS: +24.4% over 90 days (from $10.19 to $12.68)
- 4 consecutive quarterly earnings beats (most recent: +44%)

This revision velocity confirms genuine fundamental acceleration. However, at ~40x current-year consensus, the stock is priced for continued strong execution.

## Competitive Landscape

MYR Group competes in a fragmented electrical construction market against larger players (Quanta Services at $50B+ market cap, MasTec, Primoris) and numerous regional contractors. Key differentiators:

1. **Financial strength and bonding capacity:** $2.7B of outstanding bonds, zero net debt, and full credit availability enable pursuit of the largest transmission projects that smaller competitors cannot bond.

2. **Centralized fleet management:** In-house maintenance and highly mobile equipment fleet provides operational flexibility and cost advantages.

3. **130+ year operating history:** Long-standing customer relationships underpin MSA renewals (70% of T&D revenue).

4. **Skilled workforce access:** Unionized craft labor relationships provide stable access to the shrinking pool of qualified linemen and electricians.

CEO noted that new entrants to data center electrical work have "not yet caused margin pressure" due to available work volume exceeding capacity. The structural labor shortage acts as a de facto barrier to entry.

**Competitive trajectory is improving:** MYRG's 5-year TSR of 363.6% has actually lagged its peer group at 517.4%, suggesting the company may have catch-up potential as it wins large transmission projects and the market recognizes its margin expansion.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Percentage-of-Completion Volatility (HIGH):** FY2024 demonstrated that negative estimate changes can wipe out 4.4% of gross margin in a single year. Even strong FY2025 had 1.4% in negative estimate changes. A single bad quarter at 40x earnings would produce severe multiple compression.

2. **Fixed-Price Contract Execution (MODERATE-HIGH):** 57% of revenue is fixed-price. Tariff-driven material cost inflation on existing fixed-price contracts is an identified risk that management flagged in Q1 2026. Trade policy uncertainty creates an unquantifiable overhang.

3. **Customer Spending Cyclicality (MODERATE):** While structural demand drivers provide a buffer, utility capex budgets can be revised downward in recession. Data center spending is ultimately dependent on hyperscaler capex decisions. Any AI spending rationalization would impact C&I growth.

4. **Labor Availability (MODERATE):** Shrinking skilled electrical workforce creates execution risk if the company cannot scale to meet demand. CEO explicitly noted labor tightness "isn't really turning into margins today" — which cuts both ways.

5. **Wildfire Liability (LOW-MODERATE):** Expanding risk factor language around wildfire exposure. Insurance costs rising. Tail risk event.
