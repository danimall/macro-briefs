## Executive Summary

InterDigital is a pure-play patent licensing company with over 38,000 patents covering wireless (3G/4G/5G/6G), video coding, and AI technologies. The stock trades at approximately $368, representing an 11% discount to its 52-week high of $413 and approximately 16% below the lowest Wall Street target of $425. Q1 2026 results exceeded guidance on all metrics, smartphone ARR hit a record $492M, and the company has achieved multiple injunction wins in its video services enforcement campaign.

The business model is structurally non-cyclical: 93% of revenue comes from fixed-fee contracts with minimum payment guarantees, insulating earnings from device volume fluctuations. Samsung's recent ICC arbitration outcome ($131M/year through 2030, a 67% rate increase) validates the pricing trajectory for upcoming renewals. The company holds $1.26B in cash and short-term investments and has approximately $1.5B in contracted future cash receipts from existing agreements.

The primary growth vector — video services licensing targeting Disney, Amazon, and other streaming platforms — remains binary and unproven. However, the DOJ's filing of a Statement of Interest in the Disney antitrust case (October 2025) and successful injunctions in Brazil and Germany suggest favorable momentum. The risk/reward profile offers moderate upside with identifiable catalysts, though forward multiples are not as compressed as they were at lower price levels.

## Company Overview

InterDigital develops foundational wireless and video technologies, contributes them to global standards bodies (3GPP, IEEE 802.11, MPEG/JVET), and licenses the resulting standards-essential patents (SEPs) to implementers. The company does not manufacture products. With approximately 460 employees generating $834M in 2025 revenue ($1.8M per employee), it operates one of the most capital-efficient business models in technology.

**Revenue Mix (2025):**
- Smartphone licensing: 81% ($679M) — covers 8 of top 10 global manufacturers (~85% of shipments)
- CE/IoT/Auto: 19% ($155M) — TVs, PCs, gaming, connected vehicles (via Avanci platform)
- Video services: 0% — entirely unmonetized despite ubiquitous use in streaming

The company's patents are declared as essential or potentially essential to implementing 4G/5G wireless standards and H.265/H.266 video coding standards. While essentiality, validity, and infringement can be disputed in individual cases, the breadth of the portfolio and decades of standards contributions create strong licensing leverage — implementers of these standards generally require licenses to InterDigital's SEPs.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-Term Revenue Drivers:**

1. **Samsung Agreement ($131M/year through 2030):** The July 2025 ICC arbitration outcome raised Samsung's annual payments 67% from the prior agreement. This alone provides 17-19% of guided 2026 revenue. This agreement excludes Samsung digital televisions and computer display monitors, which were covered by a separate agreement that expired December 31, 2025.

2. **Video Services Licensing (Binary, High-Value):** InterDigital's video coding patents (HEVC, VVC) are used by every major streaming service. No streaming company has ever taken a license. The enforcement campaign against Disney (filed Feb 2025) has achieved injunctions in Brazil and Germany, with the DOJ filing a Statement of Interest in October 2025. If even one major streaming platform licenses, this could represent $50-100M+ in annual incremental revenue from a $475B industry growing to $675B by 2029.

3. **TV/CE Licensing Gap:** Only ~35% of TVs sold globally are under license. Active enforcement against TCL and Hisense (Q1 2026) targets this gap.

4. **6G Standards Leadership:** InterDigital holds leadership positions in 3GPP 6G standardization. Commercial deployment expected 2030, creating a decade-long growth runway.

5. **AI/Generative Video:** The 10-K explicitly states that video-based AI training uses InterDigital's patented codecs. This represents an entirely new, potentially large licensing frontier.

**Revenue Guidance (2026):** $675M–$775M revenue, Adjusted EBITDA $381M–$477M, GAAP EPS $5.77–$8.51, non-GAAP EPS $8.74–$11.84. Q1 already exceeded guidance. Management typically guides conservatively — the 4-quarter earnings beat streak averaging 39% surprise confirms this pattern. FY2026 net income guidance of $202M–$298M is materially below 2025's $407M, reflecting the normalization of catch-up revenue dynamics.

## Competitive Landscape

InterDigital occupies a distinctive competitive niche. Its patents are declared essential to global technology standards — implementers generally cannot ship compliant devices without licensing them. No competitor can substitute for InterDigital's specific inventions because each SEP covers a unique technical contribution.

**Competitive advantages:**
- 38,000+ patent portfolio built over 50 years of standards participation
- Leadership positions in 6G standardization ensure relevance for the next decade
- Multiple successful injunction proceedings in recent enforcement campaigns
- Proven ability to escalate enforcement and achieve favorable outcomes (Samsung +67%)
- Lean operations ($211M R&D, ~460 employees) maintain innovation without capital intensity

**Risks to competitive position:**
- Chinese court rulings setting worldwide FRAND rates could compress pricing
- UK/EU proceedings (Amazon, Tesla) could establish unfavorable precedent
- EU SEP regulation proposals (though 2023 proposal was withdrawn)
- Implementer coordination to resist licensing (as observed with streaming companies)

The competitive trajectory is improving: Samsung's rate uplift validates pricing power, the DOJ's involvement in the Disney case signals regulatory attention to enforcement rights, and new markets (video services, AI) expand the addressable base.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer Concentration (HIGH):** Samsung (31%), Apple (16%), and vivo (14%) represent 61% of revenue. However, all three are under long-term agreements (Samsung through 2030, Apple through 2029), and the fixed-fee structure means volume fluctuations don't affect payments. Samsung's separate TV agreement expired December 31, 2025, creating uncertainty around that revenue stream.

**Video Services Binary Outcome (MODERATE-HIGH):** The video licensing campaign represents the largest growth optionality but also the largest uncertainty. Disney's antitrust counterclaim (August 2025), while not without precedent, introduces litigation risk. Failure here doesn't impair existing revenue but eliminates a significant growth vector.

**Convertible Note Dilution (MODERATE):** The 2027 warrants (6M shares at $105.67 weighted-average strike) will cause approximately 4.2M shares of dilution at $350/share when they mature in September 2027–April 2028. This represents ~16% dilution to the current ~26M share count. The impact is partially offset by continued buybacks.

**Earnings Normalization (MODERATE):** FY2026 guidance implies net income of $202M–$298M versus 2025's $407M. The 2025 figure was elevated by $277M in catch-up revenue and associated economics. Investors should not extrapolate 2025 profitability as a recurring baseline.

**Estimate Revision Momentum (MODERATE):** Quarterly estimates declining 30-38% suggests near-term earnings expectations are being reset. While likely timing-related, this creates a setup where a miss (even a timing miss) could extend selling pressure.

**Geopolitical/China (MODERATE):** Chinese licensees (vivo, Honor, Xiaomi, OPPO, Lenovo) represent substantial revenue. Trade tensions could pressure these relationships, though existing agreements are binding international contracts.

**Short Interest (MODERATE):** At 12.6% of float with 3.8 days to cover, short positioning is elevated. This reflects either informed bearish views or sets up short-covering potential on positive catalysts.
