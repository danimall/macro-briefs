## Executive Summary

Pursuit Attractions is a recently transformed pure-play owner-operator of iconic natural attractions and lodges across North America, Iceland, and Costa Rica. The company posted record Q1 2026 revenue of $51.6M (+37.4% YoY), reaffirmed 2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $123–133M, and is executing an aggressive $100M buyback program simultaneously with $103–114M in growth capex. Estimate revisions are strongly positive (+38% for the current quarter over 90 days), and the company has beaten EPS estimates in 3 of the last 4 quarters.

PRSU trades at ~38x forward P/E — roughly double its peer median of 19.8x — while generating only 2.5% FCF margin due to heavy capital investment. Michigan consumer sentiment sits at 53.3 (declining trend), the stock has appreciated 42% from its 52-week low, and the company's heavy international revenue exposure (71%) creates FX headwinds. While the growth profile and irreplaceable asset base are genuinely differentiated, current valuation embeds substantial execution risk in a deteriorating consumer environment. The investment merits holding for its growth trajectory but does not clear the evidence bar required for an active BUY in this sector.

## Company Overview

Pursuit Attractions emerged from the December 2024 transformation of Viad Corp, which sold its GES Exhibitions and Spiro segments for $535M. The remaining company owns 17 sightseeing attractions and 29 lodges across:

- **Banff Jasper Collection** (Canadian Rockies): Banff Gondola, Columbia Icefield Adventure/Skywalk, Maligne Lake Cruise, Golden Skybridge, Jasper SkyTram, ~1,220 rooms
- **Alaska Collection**: Kenai Fjords Tours, ~520 rooms near Denali and Kenai Fjords National Parks
- **Glacier Park Collection**: ~630 rooms in/around Glacier National Park, Montana
- **Sky Lagoon** (Iceland): Premium oceanfront geothermal lagoon
- **Tabacón** (Costa Rica): Eco-luxury thermal resort, acquired July 2025 for $108.6M

Revenue split: Attractions 57%, Hospitality 40%, Transportation 3%. Approximately 71% of revenue derives from international operations (Canada 41%, Iceland 27%, Costa Rica 19%).

The company is in the process of divesting its Flyover flight-motion attractions to Brogent Technologies for $78.4M (expected close May 2026), sharpening its focus on nature-based experiences.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue & Profitability

FY2025 revenue reached $452.4M, up 23.4% from $366.5M in FY2024, driven by organic same-store growth (ticket +10.1%, RevPAR +7.1%) and the Tabacón acquisition. Operating income was $55.0M (12.2% margin) vs. a loss in FY2024 that included $47.6M in impairment charges.

Q1 2026 continued the acceleration: $51.6M revenue (+37.4% YoY), with same-store effective ticket price +4.9% and RevPAR +6.5%. The operating loss narrowed to $(24.7M) from $(31.2M), consistent with the extreme seasonality profile (79% of annual revenue in Q2/Q3).

### Cash Flow & Capital Allocation

Operating cash flow was $86.2M in FY2025, but free cash flow was only $11.1M after $75M in capex — a 2.5% FCF margin. FY2026 capex guidance of $103–114M (of which $70–80M is growth projects) means FCF will remain compressed. At the current $1.13B market cap, FCF yield is approximately 1.0%, well below the 4% threshold typically required for Consumer Discretionary BUY confidence.

Despite thin FCF, management authorized $100M in buybacks and repurchased $25.2M in Q1 2026 alone (~2.2% of market cap in one quarter). This is funded through the revolving credit facility and anticipated Flyover sale proceeds.

### Balance Sheet

The GES sale transformed the balance sheet: debt/equity dropped to 0.52x, and the company carried $170M in total available liquidity as of March 2026. Long-term debt stood at $219M (up from $155M at year-end due to growth investment timing). Net leverage is approximately 1.4x 2026E EBITDA ($128M midpoint) — conservative for a hospitality business. The $300M revolver is ~53% drawn.

Altman Z-Score of 2.63 places the company in the "grey zone," driven primarily by low working capital/assets ratio (seasonal) and moderate asset turnover. The Piotroski F-Score of 4/5 reflects clean profitability signals.

## Competitive Landscape

Pursuit occupies a genuinely unique position: no other public company combines ownership of iconic national park attractions with adjacent lodging in the same vertically-integrated model. Competitors include:

- **Large hotel chains** (Marriott, Hilton) for lodging — but these lack the attractions and experiential elements
- **Theme park operators** (Disney, Six Flags) — different market segment; manufactured vs. natural experiences
- **Local independents** — fragmented, undercapitalized competitors who lack scale benefits

The company's moat derives from: (1) physical irreplaceability of locations (you cannot build another Banff Gondola or Columbia Icefield), (2) government concession/license barriers, (3) vertical integration capturing both attraction admission and lodging spend, (4) growing brand recognition as the "pure-play" in experiential natural tourism.

Same-store pricing growth of 5–10% annually over multiple years confirms demand consistently exceeds supply at these locations, particularly during peak season.

Score: 7.5/10 — Competitive position is improving through geographic diversification (Costa Rica, Iceland reducing Canadian concentration), and the structural tailwind of experiential travel outpacing general leisure remains intact.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

1. **Extreme Seasonality** (Critical): 79% of revenue in Q2/Q3 creates existential vulnerability to summer disruptions. The 2024 Jasper wildfire and 2021 Denali landslide demonstrate this is not theoretical.

2. **Currency Exposure** (High): 71% international revenue with no equity translation hedging. USD strength from trade tensions directly compresses reported results. $43.3M in cumulative unrealized FX losses already on balance sheet.

3. **Consumer Discretionary Sensitivity**: Tourism/leisure is first to contract in recessions. Michigan consumer sentiment at 53.3 (declining), gasoline prices elevated, savings rate at 3.6% — the macro backdrop for discretionary leisure spending is deteriorating.

4. **Thin Free Cash Flow**: $11.1M FCF on $452M revenue (2.5% margin) provides minimal buffer. Growth capex of $103–114M in 2026 means the company is funding buybacks with debt, not organic cash generation. If revenue disappoints during peak season, the aggressive capital allocation could become strained.

5. **Crestview Partners Overhang**: ~24–26% ownership by a PE firm creates eventual exit risk. No recent selling detected, but a block sale would pressure a stock with only 242K average daily volume.

6. **Valuation Risk at Premium Multiple**: At 38x forward P/E (2x peers), any operational miss gets punished disproportionately. The November 6, 2025 trading session (gapped up to $38.30, closed at $32.50 on 442K volume after Q4 earnings miss) demonstrates this vulnerability.

### Mitigants
- Tabacón reduces seasonality concentration
- Government park licenses create entry barriers that partially insulate from competition
- Debt/equity at 0.52x provides balance sheet flexibility
- $170M liquidity buffer adequate for seasonal cash needs

### Short Interest
Short interest at 8.2% of float with 6.6 days to cover reflects moderate skepticism — possibly related to the premium valuation and seasonal vulnerability. Not at crowded-short levels, but enough to suggest some informed capital questions the thesis.

## Consumer Discretionary BUY Gate Assessment

The calibration system requires four conditions for a Consumer Discretionary BUY:

**(a) Positive sequential same-store sales or comparable revenue growth:** YES — Same-store ticket revenue +10.1% (FY2025), +4.9% effective ticket price (Q1 2026), +6.5% RevPAR (Q1 2026). This condition is clearly met.

**(b) FCF yield >= 4%:** NO — FCF yield is approximately 1.0% ($11.1M FCF / $1.13B market cap). Even projecting FY2026 with higher EBITDA ($128M) minus capex ($108M) minus interest (~$12M) = ~$8M FCF. This condition is not met, nor close to being met. The company is in heavy investment mode, funding growth with debt.

**(c) Consumer confidence not in declining trend:** NO — Michigan consumer sentiment at 53.3 (April 2026) is in a declining trend from 57.0 (February 2026), reflecting tariff anxiety and elevated energy costs. The macro environment for discretionary leisure spending is deteriorating.

**(d) Positive estimate revisions over trailing 90 days:** YES — Current quarter +38.3%, current year +20.3%, next year +2.1% over 90 days. This condition is clearly met.

**Result: 2 of 4 conditions met.** The FCF condition (b) and consumer confidence condition (c) are not satisfied. While the growth and revision signals are strong, the valuation discipline and macro environment do not support a BUY recommendation under the Consumer Discretionary framework.

## Conclusion

Pursuit Attractions possesses genuinely differentiated assets — irreplaceable natural park attractions with demonstrated pricing power, growing visitation, and a clear strategic vision under the Refresh/Build/Buy framework. The 23% revenue growth, positive estimate revision momentum, and aggressive buyback signal management conviction. The Denali reopening in 2027 and continued Tabacón contribution provide multi-year growth visibility.

The constraint is valuation and timing. At 38x forward P/E (2x peer median), 1% FCF yield, and a deteriorating consumer confidence backdrop, the stock prices in substantial execution. The November 2025 trading action (15% single-day decline on an earnings miss) demonstrates the penalty for any shortfall at this multiple. The strong estimate revisions and same-store growth are partially offset by thin analyst coverage (1 analyst for near-term estimates, 3 for out-year), extreme seasonality risk, and 71% international revenue exposure in an uncertain trade environment.

This is a quality business in early-stage transformation that deserves monitoring for a better entry point. The thesis is positive over 12–18 months if management continues executing, but the current price/risk balance favors patience rather than initiation.

**Recommendation: HOLD (Growth Hold)** — Maintain on watchlist for BUY on pullback to $34–36 range or FCF inflection confirmation.
