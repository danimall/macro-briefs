## Executive Summary

Rockwell Automation is nine months into the best operating year it has produced since the post-COVID backlog flush. Nine-month FY2026 sales of $6,657M rose 10.5% reported and 9% organic, enterprise operating margin expanded to 21.4% from 18.0%, adjusted EPS reached $9.55 versus $7.20 (+32.6%), and free cash flow of $1,099M is up 15% year over year. As-reported incremental operating margin for the nine months computes to 53.9% ($340M of incremental operating profit on $631M of incremental sales), the fourth consecutive quarter above 40% on an organic basis. Management raised FY2026 guidance on August 4 to 7.5-9.5% sales growth, 21.5% enterprise operating margin, and $13.00-$13.30 adjusted EPS. U.S. manufacturing PMI at 53.3 in June is the highest in four years, against sub-50 readings in every month of Rockwell's FY2025 fourth quarter.

The stock fell more than 4% on the print anyway, and that reaction was analytically correct. The implied fourth quarter — revenue of ~$2,394M (+3.4% reported) and adjusted EPS of ~$3.60 (+8.1%) at the guidance midpoint — decelerates earnings growth from +22% in Q3 to single digits, with the low end of guidance implying +3.6%. Three of the four named drivers are not seasonal: memory-component inflation that the CFO described as escalating from "single-digit millions" in Q1 to a larger double-digit-million headwind in Q3 with no expected stabilization; a permanent BEPS Pillar Two tax step-up worth roughly $0.39-0.50 per share annually (adjusted ETR 19.5% guided versus 17.1% in FY2025); and organic annual recurring revenue growth of 6%, which management itself called below expectations against a long-term framework built on double-digit ARR. Only the mix headwind from seasonally peaking configured-to-order and solutions revenue reverses on its own.

Set against a three-year record that is closer to a round trip than a growth curve — FY2026E revenue of ~$9,051M is statistically indistinguishable from FY2023's $9,058M, and adjusted EPS of $13.15 is 7.3% above FY2023's $12.25, a 2.4% CAGR — the valuation is the binding constraint. At $441.80 Rockwell trades 33.6x FY2026E adjusted EPS, 29.8x FY2027 consensus of $14.81, 26.6x FY2026E enterprise operating profit, and offers a 3.0% free cash flow yield and 1.25% dividend yield. FY2027 consensus requires roughly 100 basis points of *additional* enterprise margin expansion on top of FY2026's 260bp gain, in the face of an input-cost problem management says will not resolve. That is a fair price for a high-quality franchise in the good part of its cycle. HOLD, fourth consecutive.

## Company Overview

Rockwell is the largest pure-play industrial automation company, descended from Allen-Bradley (1903) and separated from Rockwell International in 1996 when the aerospace and defense operations went to Boeing. It addresses a market it sizes at roughly $120 billion against Siemens, ABB, Schneider Electric, Emerson, Mitsubishi Electric, Honeywell and Dassault Systèmes.

FY2025 segment structure: Intelligent Devices $3,756M at 18.0% margin (drives, motion, safety, sensing, autonomous mobile robots); Software & Control $2,383M at 29.7% (the Logix control platform, FactoryTalk, Plex cloud MES, Emulate3D, Verve OT cybersecurity, network infrastructure); Lifecycle Services $2,203M at 14.5% (engineered projects, digital consulting, recurring managed services). Total segment margin was 20.4%.

The moat claim is architectural: a single integrated hardware-and-software environment spanning discrete, process, batch, safety, motion, robotics and power control, with Logix at the center, producing standardization lock-in at the plant-architecture level. Route to market is a defining structural feature and a concentration: roughly 65% of global sales flow through independent distributors, and the two largest distributors together represented ~20% of sales in FY2023, FY2024, FY2025 and the FY2026 interim periods. Geography skews to North America (FY2025: $5,270M, ~63% of sales, and the only region that grew).

Two portfolio actions define the current year. Sensia, the 53/47 oil-and-gas digital JV with SLB, was written down $224M in FY2025 ($107M attributable to the noncontrolling interest) and dissolved effective April 1, 2026. Rockwell received $40M from the partner, derecognized $94M of net assets, booked a $20M pre-tax gain, and SLB assumed its own loans. The exit removed 3 points from reported Q3 sales (~$250M annualized) and added ~40bp to enterprise margin. Separately, a new greenfield campus in New Berlin, Wisconsin, expected to be Rockwell's largest globally, is lifting capex from $186M in FY2025 to ~$270M (3% of sales) as part of a $2B five-year investment program.

## Financial Analysis

**Operating leverage is real and quantified.** Q3 FY2026 sales of $2,313M rose 8% reported and 10% organic; gross margin 49.5% (+70bp on the restated basis); enterprise operating margin 22.3% (+280bp); adjusted EPS $3.49 (+22%). SG&A rose under 1% on 8% sales growth while engineering and development rose 5% and held at ~8% of sales, expense discipline without starving the platform.

Segment detail locates the leverage and the fragility in the same place. Software & Control grew 18% organic in Q3 at a 34.8% margin, up 320bp, and generated 45.9% of nine-month segment operating earnings ($696M of $1,515M) on 31.0% of segment sales. Intelligent Devices grew 10% organic at 20.0% (+120bp). Lifecycle Services *declined* 2% organic with book-to-bill of 0.97 and margin of 15.1%, and full-year LCS revenue is guided down ~$150M.

**Cash flow is solid but the conversion guide leaves no cushion.** Nine-month FCF of $1,099M against a 100% conversion guide on ~$1,475M of FY2026E adjusted income implies ~$376M in Q4, marginally below Q4 FY2025's $405M. Achievable, and Q4 is seasonally the strongest cash quarter, but the two-year record swung from ~57% conversion in FY2024 to ~114% in FY2025, the latter flattered by the absence of an annual incentive payout in Q1 FY2025 after FY2024 targets went unearned. Treat 100% as a plan.

**Balance sheet: comfortable, not pristine.** At June 30, 2026: total debt $3,258M (including $684M of commercial paper at 3.89% with a 25-day average maturity), cash $479M, net debt ~$2,779M against attributable equity of $3,494M (0.93x). Net debt/EBITDA computes to 1.3x on FY2026E enterprise operating profit plus depreciation, 1.6x on a stricter trailing GAAP stitch. Ratings A-/A3/A. The November 2025 refinancing put a $1.5B revolver in place to November 2030, undrawn, with the only disclosed financial covenant an EBITDA/interest floor of 3.0x, not remotely binding. Contractual debt-plus-interest of $4,712M is back-end loaded, $3,590M beyond FY2030, with $102M due in each of FY2026 and FY2027. Long-term debt carries at $2,570M against a $2,285M fair value, meaning the existing coupon stack is below market: an asset today, a refinancing cost later.

Two presentation items must not be mistaken for performance. First, the apparent 950bp gross margin jump from FY2024 (38.6%) to FY2025 (48.1%) is an accounting reclassification of engineering and development out of cost of sales (Note 1 of the Q3 10-Q quantifies $170M for Q3 FY2025 and $488M for nine-month FY2025); the usable cross-year series is segment operating margin, 19.3% to 20.4%, roughly 110bp of genuine improvement. Second, the $4.2B decline in retained earnings during FY2025 was a treasury-share retirement (common stock -$40M, retained earnings -$4,489M, treasury stock +$4,529M), a within-equity reclassification with no economic effect that mechanically depresses any retained-earnings ratio.

Current ratio of 1.08x (from 1.14x) is thin on its face but includes the $684M of commercial paper inside current liabilities; with an undrawn $1.5B revolver behind it, this is a funding-mix choice rather than a liquidity constraint.

**Financial health: 7.0.** Investment-grade, comfortably covenanted, back-loaded maturities, rising FCF, and disciplined expense growth. Held back from higher by 0.93x debt/equity in a business with cyclical revenue, a conversion guide with no slack, and nine-month capital returns ($754M buybacks + $464M dividends = $1,218M) exceeding nine-month FCF of $1,099M with $162M of net commercial paper issuance at the margin.

## Growth Analysis

**Cyclical: strong and broad.** Q3 organic growth by end market: discrete +high teens (semiconductor, data center, e-commerce/warehouse all up strong double digits; e-commerce and warehouse +30%; automotive +low double digits as programs rebalance from EV toward hybrid/ICE); hybrid +mid single digits (food and beverage +mid single digits with no capital-project inflection; life sciences +10% on GLP-1 rollouts including oral solid dose); process +high single digits (LNG, midstream, offshore, and power infrastructure for data centers), with mining -mid single digits. North America +12% organic.

**AI-linked exposure is meaningful but modest and indirect.** The only company quantification: excluding data center, organic growth would have been 8% rather than 10%, roughly 2 points, or ~$43M of incremental quarterly revenue. Converting that to a revenue base requires an undisclosed growth rate; at 50-100% year-over-year growth the implied base brackets roughly 4-6% of total sales. Aggregate AI-linked demand is larger, because semiconductor capex and data-center power infrastructure sit inside the residual 8%. Rockwell's three disclosed data-center channels are modular power distribution, power controls sold to *chiller OEMs*, and Logix controllers in central utility plants where management says hyperscalers and their contractors are standardizing on Logix for its redundancy and safety characteristics versus traditional DDC units. In the largest of these, Rockwell's paying customer is the chiller manufacturer, and ~65% of product revenue reaches end users through distributors regardless of vertical.

**Structural: the record is weaker than the year.** FY2026E revenue of ~$9,051M against FY2023's $9,058M is a three-year CAGR of approximately zero; adjusted EPS of $13.15 versus $12.25 is a 2.4% CAGR. FY2026 is substantially normalization from an FY2024 destocking correction, not the emergence of a new growth plateau. Anyone underwriting 8.5% as a run rate should reconcile it against that base effect.

**The recurring-revenue leg is under-delivering.** Organic ARR grew 6% in Q3 against a long-term framework built on double-digit growth; management called it below expectations and guided full-year organic ARR to mid single digits. Software ARR grew high single digits (Plex described as "very profitable," winning new logos among automotive tier suppliers, now embedding agentic AI); the drag is recurring Lifecycle Services, which traces to food-and-beverage and process capital spending that has not inflected. This matters for the multiple specifically: a software-adjacent valuation requires a software-adjacent recurring growth rate, and 6% is not it.

**Estimate revisions are positive but not the inflection pattern.** Current quarter $3.68 (+2.9% 30d, +3.8% 90d), current year $13.21 (+1.4%/+2.0%), next year $14.81 (+1.3% 30d). Four consecutive beats, but surprise magnitude compressed sharply to 2.4% in Q3 from 12.7%, 10.5% and 13.1% in the prior three, consistent with guidance that has caught up to the operating reality and narrower forward slack. Q4 consensus of $3.68 sits above the implied guidance midpoint of $3.60; consensus is already underwriting another beat.

**Growth prospects: 6.0.** Genuine near-term acceleration with a PMI tailwind and breadth across discrete, life sciences and process; offset by a flat three-year revenue base, decelerating Q4 earnings growth, a shrinking long-cycle segment with sub-1.0 book-to-bill, and an ARR shortfall against the company's own framework.

## Competitive Landscape

The architectural argument holds up where it is testable. The data-center standardization commentary is a specific, credible share-gain mechanism: Logix displacing DDC units in central utility plants on redundancy and safety grounds is the kind of specification win that persists across build cycles. Discrete organic growth at high teens with e-commerce/warehouse +30% and North America +12% is faster than any plausible reading of the underlying market. Named engagements — Unilever expanding a managed cybersecurity program, Convergix selecting Emulate3D, an unnamed global automotive brand owner standardizing on the end-to-end portfolio, a pharma CDMO adopting integrated process control plus MES — evidence platform pull rather than component sales.

Pricing power through a two-step channel is a specific, verifiable mechanism rather than an assertion: a fixed-discount methodology with distributors enabling faster realization, more frequent price changes, and qualification of alternate material sources to introduce supplier competition. This is why management can credibly promise price/cost positive in Q4 after a negative Q3.

Three offsets keep the trajectory from being unambiguously improving. Channel concentration has not moved: two distributors at ~20% of sales for four consecutive reporting periods. Revenue *mix* is genuinely diversifying into semiconductor, data center and warehouse verticals, which reduces end-market cyclical correlation to automotive and food and beverage; it does not reduce counterparty dependence on two distributors, and these are different measures. Second, Lifecycle Services is shrinking organically with book-to-bill at 0.97, and total order backlog has not been disclosed since $2,878M at September 30, 2025 (-6.9% year over year); the $1,375M of unfulfilled performance obligations in the 10-Q is a narrower measure under practical expedients and is not a backlog substitute. Third, the 6% ARR outcome undercuts the software-attach narrative that supports the premium multiple.

**Competitive position: 6.5**, a modest step down from 6.8, reflecting the ARR shortfall and the fact that the fastest-growing channel monetizes through OEMs and contractors rather than deepening direct end-customer relationships.

## Risk Assessment

**Memory-component inflation (highest-conviction un-priced risk).** The 10-Q attributes expected continued inflation to memory-related electronic components driven by data-center demand, plus commodities, energy and freight, with PPI running 5.5% in June and accelerating. The CFO's trajectory (single-digit millions, then double-digit millions, then a higher double-digit number) combined with "not one of those that we're expecting to stabilize anytime soon," lands disproportionately on Software & Control. The same secular force is inflating Rockwell's revenue and its bill of materials, and the cost side hits the higher-margin segment.

**Q4 and the FY2027 handoff.** Implied Q4 adjusted EPS of $3.60 (+8.1%) versus consensus $3.68 means the company must beat its own midpoint to meet the Street. Management has beaten four consecutive quarters and the guide may be conservative; at the $13.30 high end Q4 grows 12.6% and the deceleration narrative weakens materially. That two-sidedness is the honest read.

**Permanent tax step-up.** Pillar Two, with Singapore the largest exposure, moved adjusted ETR to 19.2% in Q3 from 15.3% and to 19.5% guided for the year from 17.1%. Derived annual cost ~$44M, ~$0.39 per share, structural rather than timing. Already in guidance; the risk is further jurisdictional implementation.

**Legacy liabilities.** Asbestos accrual $441M against $290M of insurance receivables (net ~$151M) with claim projections through 2060. Q4 FY2025 brought a change in accounting method to accrue defense costs — a $91M charge plus a $45M indemnity increase, $136M pre-tax / $0.91 per share, with an explanatory paragraph in Deloitte's opinion. This increases the visibility of a long-tail liability; the net exposure is small relative to a $49B market cap.

**IEEPA tariff refunds are not an earnings tailwind.** Following the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling, Rockwell filed claims in July 2026 for a large portion of IEEPA tariffs paid; no asset is recognized and no guidance impact is included. The 10-Q states the company expects to implement a refund process for qualified customers; because tariff pricing was structured for EPS neutrality (~100bp of ~250bp of FY2026 price realization), a refund most likely flows back through the channel. Model it as working capital and customer goodwill.

**Macro.** North America is ~63% of sales and the growth engine; a reversal of the four-year-high PMI is the route by which the cyclical tailwind unwinds. Cyclicality itself is permanently priced; a PMI rollover from a four-year high with the stock at 30x forward is not.

**Governance.** No open-market insider purchases appear across roughly seven months of Form 4 activity (January-July 2026) during which the stock traded $385-$465; all executive activity was option exercise paired with sale, plus director grants. Post-sale holdings are small in absolute terms — several SVPs under 20,000 shares, the Chief Revenue Officer at 3,314 — so day-to-day alignment runs through unvested awards. The February 17, 2026 and October 3, 2025 Item 5.02 8-Ks remain unidentified in the available record; CEO and CFO continuity is confirmed through the August 2026 call.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 31.6% against 31.0% one-year realized is fair value, in the 21st percentile of the current chain, with term structure in contango (31.6% at one month rising to 34.3% at six months): no near-term stress premium, with the uncertainty priced further out where the FY2027 guide sits. Skew is call-heavy (-5.1% at one month) and put/call volume is 0.61, both consistent with the post-earnings absence of hedging demand. The one contrary datapoint: put/call open interest has risen from 0.83 in mid-June to 1.51, so accumulated positioning has shifted while flow and volatility have not. Short interest of 3.9% of float and 4.2 days to cover is uncrowded. The options market corroborates a fair-value HOLD rather than either an imminent dislocation or a squeeze setup, and the cheap near-term volatility makes options a reasonable substitute for pre-positioning ahead of the November print.

**Risk factors: 5.5.** No solvency or covenant risk and modest legacy exposures, offset by an accelerating input-cost problem concentrated in the profit engine, a consensus Q4 above the guidance midpoint, and an FY2027 margin assumption that has not been de-risked.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($550, +24.5%)

The cyclical recovery is early. PMI at a four-year high with automotive showing "green shoots," life sciences +10% on GLP-1 capacity, and food-and-beverage capital projects still dormant means the largest hybrid vertical has not yet contributed. Incremental margins above 40% organic for four straight quarters demonstrate a cost structure that converts volume efficiently, and the fixed-discount distributor methodology gives Rockwell faster repricing than most industrials, with price/cost turning positive in Q4 as guided and staying there. Memory costs peak in mid-FY2027 as supply responds. Data center standardization on Logix compounds through multiple build cycles, and the semiconductor and power-infrastructure channels inside the residual 8% growth are larger than the disclosed data-center vertical. FY2027 delivers $15.00+ and FY2028 approaches $16.50-17.00; at 33x that is $545-560. Management has beaten guidance four consecutive quarters and the Q4 guide proves conservative, restoring the upward revision cadence.

### Bear Case ($340, -23.0%)

The three-year revenue CAGR of approximately zero is the true underlying growth rate, and FY2026 is a comp-driven normalization now finishing. Memory inflation persists into FY2027, holding Software & Control margin flat or lower and turning the consensus +100bp of margin expansion into zero; FY2027 EPS lands near $14.00 rather than $14.81. ARR growth at 6% removes the software-attach justification for a 30x multiple, and the market reprices Rockwell toward the industrial average of 23-25x, which is $325-350. Distributor concentration at 20% remains a single point of failure. Buybacks accelerating to ~$850M annually at a ~3.0% repurchase earnings yield, funded at the margin by 3.89% commercial paper, destroy modest value and inflate reported EPS without adding intrinsic worth, and the DEF 14A's own pay-versus-performance table shows five-year cumulative TSR of 172.60 against a self-selected peer group at 226.14, meaning the recent share-price performance came from multiple expansion on falling then flat earnings and still lagged peers. If PMI rolls over from 53.3, both the earnings and the multiple compress together.

### What Would Change the Call

The decisive question is narrow and dated: does Software & Control margin hold above ~33% in the seasonally weaker first half of FY2027 while memory costs stay elevated? If yes, FY2027 consensus is achievable and $480-500 is the right area. If S&C margin compresses toward 31-32%, FY2027 EPS is closer to $14.00 and the multiple has no support.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (fourth consecutive) — growth hold.** The 12-month thesis is positive but modest: base case $480 on 32.4x FY2027 consensus $14.81, +8.6% price appreciation plus 1.25% dividend, against a $340 bear at -23.0%. Probability-weighted expected total return of roughly 5% does not clear the bar for a full initiation, and the score decline from 6.0 to 5.85 reflects the ARR shortfall and the memory-cost escalation rather than the share-price decline, which on its own improves risk/reward.

**Existing holders:** maintain. The franchise is compounding, the balance sheet is investment grade with covenant headroom, ~3.0% of market cap is returned annually, and the cyclical backdrop is the best in four years.

**New capital:** wait for $380-400 (25.7-27.0x FY2027 consensus), which would restore roughly 20-25% upside to base case and take the FCF yield above 3.6%. February and March 2026 both offered prints in the $355-385 range on materially weaker fundamentals; the volatility of this stock has repeatedly produced better entries than the post-earnings level.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q4 FY2026 print and initial FY2027 guidance, expected in early November 2026, with Automation Fair in Boston that same month as a secondary information event. Q1 FY2027 results in late January 2027 provide the first clean read on off-peak Software & Control margin.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE / BUY if:**
1. Software & Control operating margin holds at or above 33.0% in Q4 FY2026 and Q1 FY2027 with price/cost confirmed positive — evidence that the memory headwind is being offset rather than absorbed.
2. Organic ARR growth reaccelerates to 8%+ with Lifecycle Services book-to-bill back above 1.00, restoring the recurring-revenue leg of the multiple.
3. Price reaches $380-400 with FY2027 consensus intact at $14.50+, taking forward P/E below 28x.
4. Initial FY2027 guidance at or above 6% organic revenue growth with enterprise operating margin guided to 22%+ on current input-cost assumptions.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if:**
1. FY2027 initial guidance implies adjusted EPS below $14.00, confirming the margin-expansion assumption in consensus is not deliverable.
2. Software & Control margin falls below 32% for two consecutive quarters, or management quantifies the memory headwind as growing again into FY2027.
3. U.S. manufacturing PMI falls back below 50 for two consecutive months, or discrete organic growth decelerates below mid single digits.
4. Any disruption to a top-two distributor relationship, or disclosure that the ~20% concentration figure has increased.

## Conclusion

Rockwell is executing well inside the best part of its cycle. Nine-month organic growth of 9%, 340 basis points of enterprise margin expansion, 53.9% as-reported incremental margins, a cleanly completed Sensia exit at a small gain with the partner absorbing its own loans, and an investment-grade balance sheet with maturities pushed beyond FY2030 all argue for owning the franchise. Estimates are rising modestly across every forward period and the company has beaten four consecutive quarters.

The constraint is arithmetic. At $441.80 the market pays 33.4x FY2026 consensus and 29.8x FY2027 consensus — a PEG of 2.46 — for a company whose three-year revenue CAGR is approximately zero and whose recurring-revenue growth of 6% sits at half the framework rate used to justify a software-adjacent multiple. FY2027 consensus embeds roughly 100 basis points of additional margin expansion in the same quarter that management guided its most profitable segment's margin *down* on a memory-cost problem it does not expect to abate. Add a permanent ~$0.39 per share tax step-up, a long-cycle segment shrinking with sub-1.0 book-to-bill, seven months without a single open-market insider purchase, and $850M of annual repurchases at a ~3.0% earnings yield partly funded with 3.89% commercial paper, and the case for paying up here weakens considerably.

Base $480, bull $550, bear $340. Hold what you own; add toward $380-400 or on evidence that Software & Control margin can hold above 33% through the seasonally weaker first half of FY2027.
