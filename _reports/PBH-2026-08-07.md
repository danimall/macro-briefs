## Executive Summary

Prestige Consumer Healthcare is an asset-light aggregator of mature over-the-counter brands: 890 employees generating $1.09B of revenue at 55% gross margins and 1% of revenue in capital expenditure. Over eight months from December 2025 through July 2026 the company deployed approximately $1,305M on three acquisitions (Pillar5 Pharma $109.9M, the Foundation "OTC Wellness" portfolio including Breathe Right and Dimetapp for $1,045M, and LaCorium Health for ~$150M), funded almost entirely with new debt. Net debt went from roughly $936M at 3/31/2026 to approximately $2,105M post-LaCorium, moving net leverage from ~2.6x to ~4.5-4.8x adjusted EBITDA. The stock is 27.7% below its $75.31 52-week high.

The August 6 first-quarter print was a beat-and-raise: adjusted EPS of $0.98 exceeded the $0.88 non-GAAP consensus by 10.1%, adjusted EBITDA margin held at 31.6%, and full-year guidance rose to $1,290-1,315M of revenue and $4.55-4.65 of adjusted EPS from $1,100-1,121M and $4.42-4.51. The July refinancing (i.e., $400M of 6.25% notes due 2034 replacing $400M of 5.125% notes due 2028) removed the $410.5M FY2028 maturity that dominated the balance sheet, leaving essentially $10.5M per year of term-loan amortization and nothing material until 2031. That is a genuine reduction in near-term financial risk relative to our prior review.

The underlying earnings trajectory has not changed. The company's own selected performance measure, adjusted EBITDA, ran $367.7M (FY22), $378.1M (FY23), $373.9M (FY24), $374.5M (FY25), $353.8M (FY26): down 3.8% cumulatively across five years during which PBH also bought brands and retired shares. Management raised revenue guidance by $192M (+17.3%) and adjusted EPS by only $0.135 (+3.0%), because at roughly 6.3% on the term loan, financing cost consumes nearly all the acquired EBITDA in year one after purchase accounting. At 11.8x guided adjusted EPS and a ~10.5% adjusted FCF yield the shares are inexpensive in absolute terms, and the deleveraging arithmetic alone can generate low-double-digit annual equity returns if EBITDA holds. The multiple is inexpensive for identifiable reasons: flat five-year earnings power, 53% floating-rate debt with no disclosed hedge, a newly expanded disclosure that *all* sterile eye-care manufacturing sites have been inspected by health authorities, and an intangible-heavy balance sheet with negative tangible book value of -$1,977M. We keep the HOLD, and we classify it as uncertain rather than a confident growth hold.

## Company Overview

PBH buys orphaned OTC brands from large pharmaceutical and consumer companies and supports them with advertising, packaging and line extensions. Manufacturing is outsourced to 90 third-party manufacturers as of 6/30/2026 (down from 95 at 3/31/2026 and 102 at 6/30/2025); PBH owns three plants (Lynchburg VA, Victoria Australia, and the recently acquired Arnprior Ontario sterile ophthalmic facility) together producing roughly 21% of gross revenues. U.S. distribution runs through a single third-party-operated facility.

Two segments: North American OTC Healthcare at 83.9% of FY2026 revenue and International at 16.1%. The competitive structure is category-level share in small niches: PBH holds the #1 position in twelve categories per IRI data for the 52 weeks ended 3/22/2026 — analgesic powders (BC, Goody's), medicated sore throat (Chloraseptic), wart removal (Compound W), ear wax (Debrox), motion sickness (Dramamine), enemas (Fleet), Canadian upset stomach (Gaviscon), vaginal antifungal (Monistat), lice (Nix), feminine hygiene (Summer's Eve), Australian nasal saline (Fess) and oral rehydration (Hydralyte). Revenue from #1-position brands rose from 58.6% (FY24) to 61.5% (FY25) to 63.7% (FY26). Weaker positions include Clear Eyes (#3 redness relief), TheraTears (#3 dry eye), DenTek (#4), Luden's (#4).

Customer concentration is meaningful and not improving: Walmart was ~20% of FY2026 U.S. gross revenues and Amazon ~15%; in Q1 FY2027 Walmart was 19% and Amazon 16%. Combined ~35% versus ~33% in FY2025. The channel mix shift from drug/wholesale (22.8% of gross revenues FY24 → 19.2% FY26) into e-commerce changes where product sells, not the dependence on the two largest buyers.

## Financial Analysis

**Reported earnings power.** FY2026 net income was $190.3M on revenue of $1,088.7M, giving diluted EPS of $3.91 on 48.720M diluted shares. That reconciles to the trailing P/E of 13.92x at $54.43. Prior years: FY2025 $214.6M ($4.29), FY2024 $209.3M ($4.17), FY2023 a net loss of $82.3M on goodwill impairment. A widely circulated XBRL extraction reports $257.5M as FY2026 net income; that figure is pretax income ($257.5M − $67.2M tax = $190.3M, a 26.1% effective rate matching the MD&A disclosure and the proxy pay-versus-performance table). Screens built on the higher figure understate the P/E by roughly a third.

**The revenue decline was concentrated and supply-driven.** FY2026 revenue fell $49.1M (-4.3%). North American Eye & Ear Care fell 20.6% to $126.1M (a $32.7M decline) and International Eye & Ear fell 31.3% to $16.8M (~$7.7M), together ~$40.4M — approximately 82% of the total decline. MD&A attributes this to a "limited ability to supply demand for Clear Eyes." Other categories were mixed: Gastrointestinal +2.5%, Oral Care +6.2%, Dermatologicals -3.4%, Analgesics -3.5%, Women's Health -5.2%, Cough & Cold -6.9%.

Supply-constrained revenue is theoretically recoverable at high incremental margin. Closing the $40.4M gap at 55% gross margin with brand support already funded implies roughly $22.2M of incremental EBITDA and pretax income, or about $0.35 per share after tax — approximately 7.6% of guided adjusted EPS. North American Eye & Ear returned to +2.0% growth in Q1 FY2027, and management places the Clear Eyes recovery in the back half of FY2027. We treat the $0.35 as upside sensitivity, not base case: Clear Eyes is a #3 brand, eighteen-plus months of shelf gaps in a category with available branded substitutes can cost habitual purchase, and the 10-K notes the Arnprior investment program "may negatively impact production short-term."

**Margins and cash conversion.** Gross margin was 55.5% (FY24), 55.8% (FY25), 54.7% (FY26), and 51.3% GAAP / 55.0% adjusted in Q1 FY2027. Operating margin declined from 30.4% to 28.4% across FY24-FY26. Advertising and marketing fell $7.0M in dollar terms in FY2026 while holding at 13.7% of sales, then dropped to 13.1% of revenue in Q1 FY2027 from 14.0%. Whether that reflects efficiency or under-investment behind newly acquired brands is not resolvable from the filings.

Cash conversion is high quality. Operating cash flow was $248.9M (FY24), $251.5M (FY25), $257.6M (FY26); FY2026 capex was $11.2M (1.0% of revenue), giving free cash flow of $246.4M and a 22.6% FCF margin. OCF/net income of 1.35x reflects large non-cash amortization and deferred taxes rather than accrual manipulation.

FY2027 guidance implies compression. Management guides operating cash flow of $277M, capex of $26M (2.0% of revenue, double the historical rate for the Arnprior program), GAAP FCF of $251M and adjusted FCF of at least $270M (adding back ~$19M of acquisition costs paid in cash). GAAP FCF of $251M on guided revenue of $1,302.5M is a 19.3% margin, roughly 330bp below FY2026, on ~20% more revenue. The delta is interest expense. We use management's ≥$270M rather than annualizing Q1's $83.7M, which benefited from unusually low cash taxes paid ($2.0M versus $3.3M).

**Balance sheet transformed in a single quarter.** Total assets rose from $3,494.3M (3/31/2026) to $4,568.6M (6/30/2026); intangibles from $2,299.6M to $3,243.4M; goodwill from $581.1M to $650.8M. Long-term debt net went from $994.0M to $2,007.2M. Goodwill plus intangibles are 85.2% of total assets; tangible book value is -$1,977.4M; equity/total liabilities fell from 1.175 to 0.723. Book value per share is $40.46, so the stock trades at 1.35x book. There is already $214.1M of accumulated goodwill impairment on the books from FY2023.

Post-refinancing debt is approximately $2,140M of principal: $600M 3.75% notes due 2031, $400M 6.25% notes due 2034, and a $1,140M term loan due 6/12/2033 at Term SOFR + 200bp. The ABL was upsized to $225M with $0 drawn and $193.5M available. Scheduled maturities through fiscal 2031 are essentially $10.5M per year of term-loan amortization. Interest coverage on FY2027E adjusted EBITDA of ~$441M against ~$107M of interest is roughly 4.1x.

Two consequences deserve emphasis. First, near-term solvency risk is genuinely low: the maturity wall is gone, the revolver is undrawn, and the business generates a 19-23% FCF margin. An Altman Z-Score of 2.17 computed at 3/31/2026 mechanically penalizes an intangible-heavy brand aggregator whose low asset turnover and thin working capital are design features. Second, PBH is now approximately 53% floating-rate ($1,140M of $2,140M) after being 100% fixed at 3/31/2026, with no interest-rate hedge disclosed in the materials reviewed. A 100bp move in SOFR is $11.4M pretax, roughly $8.6M after tax, about $0.18 per share or 3.9% of guided adjusted EPS.

**Leverage and the deleveraging path.** Net debt of approximately $2,105M against FY2027E adjusted EBITDA of ~$441M is roughly 4.8x; against a full-year run-rate of ~$470M, roughly 4.5x. If the full ≥$270M of adjusted FCF is applied to debt with flat EBITDA and no buybacks or M&A, leverage reaches roughly 4.0x by fiscal year-end 2027, ~3.4x in FY2028 and ~2.9x in FY2029. Returning to the historical 2.6-3.0x therefore requires approximately two and a half to three years of uninterrupted discipline. Management's actions are consistent with that priority: zero shares repurchased in Q1 FY2027 despite $92.2M of remaining authorization.

**Quality-of-earnings flags.** The allowance for doubtful accounts rose from $18.2M to $19.9M in one quarter against net receivables of $187.4M — roughly 9.6% of gross AR, unusually high for a consumer-staples company whose two largest customers are Walmart and Amazon; FY2026 MD&A attributes part of an $8.2M G&A increase to a higher allowance for "one specific customer," unnamed. The obsolete and slow-moving inventory reserve rose from $6.6M to $11.5M in a single quarter. Both movements are small in dollars and could reflect purchase-accounting harmonization or ordinary conservatism. They are items to monitor, not established deterioration.

## Growth Analysis

FY2027 guidance implies ~19.6% revenue growth, with organic growth guided unchanged at +1.0% to +3.0%. Roughly 17 points of the 19.6% are acquired.

Q1 FY2027 revenue grew 6.5% to $265.7M with reported organic growth of +3.2%. The composition warrants scrutiny: of the $16.2M revenue increase, "other revenues" contributed $5.1M ($5.375M versus $0.252M, from third-party manufacturing at PBH's own facilities — effectively Pillar5's external customers) and post-close Wellness net sales contributed $5.9M. Residual branded growth is approximately $5.1M on a $249.3M base, or ~2.1%. PBH's definition of organic revenue does not appear in the reviewed materials, and the reported figure may already exclude Pillar5 while including an FX adjustment on the ~16% Australian business, so this is an alternative calculation rather than a correction. Directionally it suggests underlying branded growth closer to 2% than 3%.

The acquired portfolio's own trajectory is a question. Backing out of the 10-Q pro forma table, full-quarter Wellness revenue was approximately $37.7M versus approximately $40.2M in the comparable prior-year quarter, or -6.3%. That is one quarter measured under prior ownership and could reflect retailer destocking or order timing ahead of a known ownership change. It is not established brand erosion, but paying roughly 6-7x sales for declining brands with floating-rate debt is materially worse than paying 6x for stable ones.

Implied acquisition economics diverge by metric. Annualizing $37.7M gives ~$151M of revenue against $1,045M, or ~6.9x sales — expensive. Working backward from the guidance revision instead — approximately $8.5M of implied pretax contribution plus ~$57.2M of incremental interest plus ~$13.9M of incremental amortization — implies roughly $79.6M of partial-year acquired EBITDA, about $100M annualized, or roughly 12x EBITDA on ~$1,195M of purchase price. That is defensible for durable OTC brands. The two cannot both be right; seasonality helps reconcile them, since Dimetapp is a cough/cold brand whose season is the December quarter. Neither multiple is disclosed by the company, and both derivations depend on assumed SOFR (~4.3%) and tax (24.5%) inputs.

Consensus estimate revisions have been broadly negative: current-year EPS $4.58 (down 4.6% over 90 days), current quarter $1.08 (down 5.7% over 30 days). Current-year consensus of $4.58 now sits essentially at management's adjusted guidance midpoint of $4.60, so the 90-day decline reflects a completed reset after the March-quarter miss (-12.4% surprise) rather than ongoing deterioration. Next-year consensus of $5.04 is up 0.6% over 90 days. The four-quarter surprise record is two beats (+9.2%, +10.0%) and two misses (-1.4%, -12.4%): moderate visibility.

The five-year adjusted EBITDA flatline is the fact that governs the growth score. PBH has been sustaining earnings power rather than compounding it, with EPS growth from FY24 to FY25 driven principally by share count reduction and lower interest. FY2026 is depressed by an identifiable supply failure, and FY2023-25 absorbed post-COVID cough/cold normalization, so a flat series does not prove structural decline. It does place the burden of proof on the bull case.

Against the supplied Pharmaceuticals peer set, the median P/E is 14.3x, but that set is not a usable comparable group (SUPN at -89x, PRGO at -0.8x, INVA at 3.9x, ANIP at 20.9x, OGN at 14.3x). PBH's 13.9x trailing and 11.8x forward adjusted sit roughly at that median, so the peer screen provides no evidence of mispricing.

More instructive is the proxy peer group's total-return index, which fell from 100.09 (FY22) to 69.99 (FY26), down 30% cumulatively over five years. PBH's own index went 120.10 → 194.96 → 134.39, a 31.1% FY2026 drawdown against the peer group's 38.6%. PBH has de-rated less than its comparison set. A meaningful portion of the multiple compression is sector-wide OTC and consumer-health de-rating (Haleon, Kenvue, Opella and Reckitt all carry their own leverage and litigation overhangs), which means mean-reversion to PBH's own historical multiple is not an available argument. A re-rating requires either sector recovery or company-specific proof.

There are two defensible sources of return that do not require re-rating. First, absolute yield support: a 9.7% GAAP free-cash-flow yield on a business with 55% gross margins and 1-2% capex is real cash, and at a 9% required yield on $251M the equity is worth approximately $58.9 per share. Second, the deleveraging transfer: if enterprise value holds at ~10.6x a flat $441M of EBITDA and roughly $250M per year of free cash flow retires debt, equity value accretes about $10.50 per share over two years — roughly 9-10% annually from balance-sheet mechanics alone.

Offsetting both is the leverage-amplified downside. At 4.7x net debt, a 15% EBITDA disappointment combined with a one-turn multiple compression takes the equity from $54 to the high $30s. Guided GAAP EPS of $4.18-4.28 also deserves a haircut: the reconciliation footnote states it includes only Q1 FY2027 adjustments while future adjustments are "likely significant" but not quantifiable, and the Pillar5 remediation and integration costs are ongoing by management's own account. The adjusted $4.55-4.65 is the internally consistent figure; the 12.9x GAAP multiple is a floor-quality estimate.

Analyst targets run $55 low, $67.80 mean, $70 median, $75 high; the mean implies 24.6% upside. PBH does not have accelerating estimates, so we do not treat those targets as floors; with estimates falling and a $55 low target essentially at spot, the dispersion is the message.

The valuation is inexpensive on cash flow with a modest identified catalyst (Clear Eyes recovery worth ~$0.35 per share plus deleveraging). It does not score above the midpoint, because five years of flat earnings power and 4.7x leverage are exactly what a low multiple on a cash-generative brand portfolio is usually compensating for.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat is category-level share in niches too small to attract sustained assault from P&G, Haleon, Kenvue or Reckitt. Several brands are 90-150 years old (Fleet dates to 1869, Luden's 130+ years). That structure supports 55% gross margins and 28-30% operating margins with 890 people, and the share of revenue from #1-position brands has improved for three consecutive years. The 10-K nonetheless flags explicit private-label encroachment that management expects to intensify "during an economic downturn or periods of high inflation," relevant given the consumer pressure visible in the drug-channel decline from 22.8% to 19.2% of gross revenues.

The trajectory is mixed rather than improving. Clear Eyes (#3) has surrendered shelf availability for six-plus quarters. Women's Health, containing Monistat, fell 5.2% in FY2026 and another 5.1% in Q1 FY2027. The third-party manufacturing base shrank 12% year over year, and the 10-K discloses that certain manufacturers "have experienced cash flow shortages" such that PBH provided prepayments and short-term loans, one of which produced a $10.3M write-off in FY2026 when the supplier discontinued operations.

The structural tension is that PBH externalizes capital intensity and internalizes supply fragility. In unregulated categories (analgesic powders, wart removal, cough drops) that trade is favorable. In regulated sterile pharmaceuticals it is not: PBH could not compel a distressed contract manufacturer to fund cGMP remediation, and its remedies were to lend the supplier money (written off) and then buy a plant ($109.9M) that management subsequently described as behaving under PBH ownership much as it had under prior ownership, with expected one-week shutdowns extending to two, three and four weeks. Capex rising from 1.0% to 2.0% of revenue is the visible price.

The 10-K frames the eye-care constraint as industry-wide: heightened regulatory expectations for sterile eye care contributing to disruptions "across the industry." If true, whoever solves supply first captures disproportionate share. The Q1 10-Q's disclosure that "all of our sterile eye care manufacturing sites, including those operated by certain third-party manufacturers, have undergone inspections by health authorities" is more consistent with PBH's specific supplier base being worse positioned than the vertically integrated sterile capacity at Alcon or Bausch + Lomb.

## Risk Assessment

**Sterile eye-care regulatory escalation.** The Q1 10-Q materially expanded the 10-K language from eight weeks earlier: all sterile eye-care sites, PBH-operated and third-party, have been inspected, with PBH "actively engaging with those authorities and implementing responsive actions." Back-half FY2027 guidance depends on Clear Eyes recovery. Any adverse inspection outcome delays the single largest self-help lever in the model and would likely trigger a guidance revision. This is the risk we would monitor most closely.

**Single-supplier concentration exceeding customer concentration.** One privately owned pharmaceutical manufacturer produced products accounting for ~21% of gross revenues in FY2026 and FY2025 and 22% in Q1 FY2027, and the 10-K states PBH "accounted for a significant portion of their gross revenues over that time period." This is mutual dependence with an unnamed private counterparty, in a supply base that has already produced one failure and a $10.3M write-off.

**Leverage and unhedged floating exposure.** ~4.5-4.8x net leverage with 53% of debt floating over SOFR and no disclosed hedge; $0.18 per share per 100bp. The 10-K's cross-default language ("We presently do not have sufficient liquidity to repay these borrowings") was written when debt was ~$1.0B and now applies to ~$2.14B. A 100-200bp adverse SOFR path would meaningfully raise interest cost. The offsetting facts are real: no maturities of consequence before 2031, $193.5M undrawn ABL, ~4.1x coverage, and a springing ABL fixed-charge covenant that is not currently binding at $0 drawn.

**Monistat impairment.** At the February 28, 2026 annual test all indefinite-lived intangibles cleared a 10% fair-value cushion except Monistat, with a disclosed sensitivity of $16.6M of impairment on a 50bp WACC increase. Monistat is among the top five brands by carrying value (top five ≈ 59% of total intangible value). The dollar sensitivity is small; the concern is that it models a discount-rate move rather than a sustained cash-flow decline, and Women's Health revenue is declining ~5% annually.

**Integration execution.** Three deals in eight months, two within nineteen days, while remediating a just-purchased plant. Purchase accounting adds roughly $257M of finite-lived intangibles amortizing over ~18.8 years, and the guided GAAP-to-adjusted bridge is explicitly incomplete for the remaining quarters.

**Securities-law investigations.** Two plaintiff firms announced investigations (Levi & Korsinsky 5/15/2026, Hagens Berman 6/25/2026). Note 15 of the Q1 10-Q discloses only routine matters with no securities-litigation disclosure or reserve, and no filed complaint has been identified. One publicly circulated allegation — that management projected $245M of free cash flow — appears inconsistent with FY2026 actual GAAP FCF of $246.4M, which essentially met that figure. These should not be treated as an established liability, but a filed complaint would create a headline overhang.

**Capital-allocation optics.** Diluted shares fell from 50.178M (FY24) to 47.604M (Q1 FY27), with FY2026 repurchases up $104.8M year over year at an approximate average near $68 and a precisely disclosed Q1 FY2026 average of $84.73. Buybacks then stopped entirely at ~$55. Halting to service a $1,140M floating-rate term loan is the correct decision given the balance sheet management chose; the criticism is the sequencing that made it necessary.

### Options Market Signal

The chain shows near-term ATM implied volatility of 84.1% against 33.0% one-year historical volatility, with pronounced backwardation (84.1% at 13 days, 32.8% at 41 days, 28.5% at 160 days) and a snapshot change from 41.2% to 98.6% since July 19. We treat the near-term reading as low-confidence: the chain contains only 36 contracts across four expirations, earnings have already been reported (8/6), and no scheduled catalyst falls inside the 13-day window. On a chain that thin, one wide or stale quote dominates the ATM calculation. The 41-day and 160-day tenors at 32.8% and 28.5% sit at or below realized volatility, which is the more reliable read and prices no unusual stress. Put/call skew at the six-month tenor is -0.6%, described by the data source as balanced, and put/call volume and open-interest ratios are unavailable. Short interest of 7.9% of float at 4.5 days to cover is moderate: enough to indicate a real bear constituency, not enough to create squeeze mechanics. Options provide no independent confirmation in either direction, and longer-dated volatility around 28-33% offers reasonably priced optionality for anyone wanting to own the Clear Eyes recovery through calls rather than stock.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $73 (+34%)

Clear Eyes supply normalizes in the back half of FY2027, restoring roughly $40M of revenue at 55% gross margin with brand support already funded — approximately $0.35 per share with no incremental capital. The acquired Wellness and LaCorium portfolios become "increasingly accretive" as integration costs roll off and the tax-deductible goodwill on the $1,045M asset acquisition delivers cash-tax shields that never appear in EPS; clean second-year accretion runs well above the ~4% booked in year one. Adjusted FCF of $270M-plus retires debt, taking leverage to ~4.0x by March 2027 and ~3.4x a year later, at which point the equity re-rates toward 14x on ~$5.10 of adjusted EPS. Sector sentiment in OTC/consumer health recovers from a five-year 30% de-rating. Twelve brands hold #1 category positions in niches the majors will not attack, and 63.7% of revenue now sits in those positions.

### Bear Case — $40 (-27%)

Health-authority inspections at PBH's sterile eye-care sites produce remediation requirements that push Clear Eyes recovery past FY2028, and the back-half-weighted guidance is cut. The acquired portfolio's derived -6.3% revenue trajectory proves genuine rather than a destocking artifact, meaning PBH paid ~7x sales for declining brands with floating-rate debt. Organic growth turns negative as private label takes share in a stressed consumer environment; adjusted EBITDA lands nearer $410M than $441M. SOFR rises 100-150bp with no hedge, costing $0.18-0.27 per share. Monistat impairs. Leverage stays above 4.5x, the deleveraging story stalls, and the multiple compresses to 9x EV/EBITDA — approximately $3,690M of EV less $2,050M of net debt, or roughly $34-40 per share. The five-year adjusted EBITDA flatline is revealed as the structural condition rather than a series of transitory shocks.

### Base Case — $61 (+12%)

Organic growth runs 1-3%, adjusted EPS lands in the $4.55-4.65 guided range, and adjusted FCF of ~$270M cuts net leverage to ~4.0-4.2x by March 2027. Clear Eyes recovers partially rather than fully. Multiple holds near 10.5x EV/EBITDA and ~12.5x adjusted EPS, so the return comes mostly from debt paydown transferring enterprise value to equity: ~$250M of paydown on 47.4M shares is ~$5.30 per share, plus modest EBITDA growth. Probability-weighting 50% base / 25% bull / 25% bear gives approximately +8% expected return over twelve months against a -27% tail. That is approximately fair compensation for the risk, not compelling.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Existing holders maintain exposure; new capital has no urgent entry signal at $54.43. Horizon 12-18 months, anchored on the November 2026 (Q2 FY2027) and February 2027 (Q3 FY2027) prints, which together determine whether the back-half-weighted Clear Eyes recovery and the acquisition accretion arrive.

- **Base case $61** (+12%): 12.5x FY2028 adjusted EPS of ~$4.90, cross-checked against 10.5x EV/EBITDA on $455M with ~$250M of debt retired.
- **Bull case $73** (+34%): 14x ~$5.20 adjusted EPS with leverage below 3.5x.
- **Bear case $40** (-27%): 9x EV/EBITDA on $410M less ~$2,050M net debt.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of North American Eye & Ear growth above +8%, confirming Clear Eyes recovery rather than stabilization, combined with confirmation that health-authority engagement at the sterile sites has closed without remediation restrictions.
2. Net debt / adjusted EBITDA reported at or below 4.0x by the February 2027 print with adjusted FCF tracking at or above the ≥$270M guide, plus disclosure of an interest-rate hedge on the $1,140M floating tranche.
3. Clustered open-market insider purchases (three or more officers/directors), which are absent across the entire Form 4 record through 8/4/2026 despite a 28% drawdown.
4. A price near $46-48 without fundamental deterioration, which would put the shares near 10x guided adjusted EPS and a ~12% adjusted FCF yield.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Any FY2027 guidance reduction attributable to sterile eye-care regulatory action or Arnprior remediation timing.
2. Organic revenue growth printing negative in any quarter, or the acquired Wellness portfolio confirming continued mid-single-digit revenue decline under PBH ownership.

## Conclusion

PBH is a genuinely cash-generative brand portfolio trading at 11.8x guided adjusted EPS and a ~10% free-cash-flow yield, and the July refinancing meaningfully reduced near-term financial risk by eliminating the $410.5M FY2028 maturity and leaving nothing material due before 2031. They do not yet argue for adding capital. The company spent approximately half its market capitalization over eight months on debt-funded acquisitions that added 17.3% to revenue guidance and 3.0% to adjusted EPS guidance, taking leverage from 2.6x to roughly 4.5-4.8x with 53% of debt floating and no disclosed hedge. Adjusted EBITDA has been flat-to-down for five consecutive years. The equity is now a leveraged claim on a portfolio whose second-largest category depends on resolving regulatory scrutiny that the Q1 filing disclosed had broadened to every sterile eye-care site in the supply chain. Management has surrendered optionality (no buybacks, no further M&A) for roughly two to three years to repair the balance sheet, which is the right decision given the balance sheet it chose.

Probability-weighted return of approximately +8% against a -27% bear case, with negative near-term estimate revisions, no insider buying at a 28% drawdown, and a peer group that has de-rated 30% over five years, is approximately fair from $54.43. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and we classify it as uncertain: the decisive evidence — Clear Eyes shipment recovery, acquired-brand trajectory under PBH ownership, and the sterile-site inspection outcomes — will not be available until the November and February prints. The cheaper, cleaner entry the deleveraging thesis deserves is nearer $46-48.
