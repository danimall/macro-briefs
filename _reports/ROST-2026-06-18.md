**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ross Stores is the largest U.S. off-price apparel and home retailer (2,282 stores across Ross Dress for Less and dd's DISCOUNTS), and it is mid-way through a genuine fundamental acceleration. The Q1 FY2026 print (quarter ended May 2, 2026) showed a +17% comparable-store sales increase driven by an 11% traffic gain and 6% basket increase, sales up 21% to $6.01B, operating margin expanding ~150 bps to 13.4%, and diluted EPS of $2.02, up 37% year-over-year and roughly 15% above consensus. Management raised FY2026 comp guidance to 6-7% from the +5% just posted in FY2025. This is a step-change from the steady ~5%-comp grower the FY2025 10-K depicts, engineered by a new outsider CEO, James Conroy (ex-Boot Barn), who is modernizing the brand, refreshing stores, and reorienting the culture toward customer acquisition.

The investment problem is price, not business quality. ROST has appreciated roughly 80% over the trailing twelve months and trades at about 32.6x trailing and ~30x forward FY2026 EPS ($7.80 consensus), a ~26% premium to the apparel-retail peer median P/E of 23.8x. The off-price tailwind is real and broad-based, with TJX corroborating the same accelerating-traffic dynamic the same week as consumers trade down, but the multiple already embeds both the inflection and continued flawless execution. Management's own guidance implies Q1's +17% comp decelerates sharply to a 6-7% full-year pace, which means the Street is not underestimating the moderation; it has priced it.

We rate ROST a HOLD on a growth-hold basis. The 12-month trajectory is constructive and the franchise is best-in-class (ROE 34.7%, FCF margin 9.7%, minimal funded debt), but after an 80% run the risk/reward is symmetric rather than asymmetric. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the high-$180s/low-$190s (roughly 24-25x forward), or on evidence the comp acceleration is durable beyond the guided deceleration.

## Company Overview

Ross operates two banners as a single reportable segment. Ross Dress for Less (1,917 stores as of May 2, 2026) targets middle-income households with brand-name and designer goods at 20-60% off department/specialty prices. dd's DISCOUNTS (365 stores) targets lower-to-moderate income households at 20-70% off. Total footprint is 2,282 stores in 44 states plus DC, Guam, and Puerto Rico, with 45.1M selling square feet. The company is exclusively brick-and-mortar with no e-commerce, a deliberate strategic choice it flags in its risk factors.

The competitive model rests on opportunistic close-out and upfront buying by 800+ merchants from a fragmented vendor network; "packaway" inventory (36% of total) warehoused for later release when bargains arise; a low-cost self-service store format; and scale economies across eight highly automated distribution centers, with new DCs in Buckeye, AZ and Randleman, NC. The treasure-hunt experience and constantly-changing assortment drive repeat traffic. The primary competitor is TJX (TJ Maxx/Marshalls/HomeGoods), with Burlington, Walmart, and Target also competing for the value shopper.

## Financial Analysis

The annual data understates the current trajectory. FY2025 (ended Jan 31, 2026) showed revenue of $22.75B (+8%), a +5% comp (3% basket, 2% traffic), and EPS of $6.61, with operating margin slipping 35 bps to 11.9% on distribution deleverage from the new Buckeye DC, ~20 bps of tariff drag, and a prior-year warehouse-sale gain that did not recur. EPS absorbed ~$0.16 of unfavorable tariff impact.

Q1 FY2026 is a different business. COGS fell 145 bps as a percentage of sales (merchandise margin +85 bps, occupancy leverage +60 bps), and operating margin came in well above the company's own 11.8-12.1% plan. The growth was traffic-led, with management citing customer-count gains across income tiers, age groups, and ethnicities, a higher-quality signal than discount-driven basket inflation.

Financial health is excellent. Altman Z-Score of 3.05 sits in the safe zone; current ratio 1.58; ROE 34.7%; ROA 13.8%. The reported 2.51 debt-to-equity largely reflects ~$3.9B of capitalized operating leases (stores). Actual funded debt is small and shrinking: Senior Notes are down to ~$1.0B after repaying $700M in April 2025 and $500M in April 2026, against cash of ~$4.1-4.6B. FY2025 operating cash flow was $3.03B and FCF $2.21B (9.7% margin); Q1 FY2026 OCF jumped to $836M from $410M. Capital return is aggressive: ~$1.05B/year of buybacks under a new $2.55B two-year authorization (~$2.2B remaining), and the dividend was raised ~10% to $0.445/quarter. FY2026 capex is guided to ~$1.1B (up from $819M) for new stores and DC build-out.

The earnings track record supports confidence in forward estimates: three beats in the last four quarters, with the most recent two at +14.5% and +10.1% surprises, and only a marginal −0.4% miss in between.

## Growth Analysis

The growth drivers per management (CEO Conroy and COO Hartshorn) are brand "contemporizing," a refreshed marketing and media mix, store refreshes (half the fleet refreshed with measurable sales lift), and a cultural shift toward an entrepreneurial, offense-oriented posture. Roughly 110 new stores are planned for the year. The macro backdrop is supportive: value-seeking behavior is broadening across income cohorts amid consumer strain, a dynamic TJX confirmed independently. This gives the near-term growth thesis higher durability than a single-company story.

Estimate revisions are positive but moderate. Current-year consensus rose to $7.80 (+5.8% over 30 days, +8.2% over 90 days) and next-year to $8.58 (+5.3%/+8.4%). This is genuine positive earnings momentum, but the revision velocity is well below the threshold (+30% over 90 days) that flags systematic Street underestimation of an inflection. The reason is structural: management itself guides FY2026 comp to 6-7%, which implies that Q1's +17% decelerates materially over the balance of the year. The Street has priced both the strong Q1 and the guided moderation. Revenue growth of ~10% and forward EPS growth of ~10% are good for a mature retailer, but they do not represent an accelerating-from-a-trough inflection that warrants paying through a premium multiple.

The key strategic swing factor is Conroy's "trade-up" push into higher-priced/better-brand merchandise. Executed well, it expands basket; executed poorly, it risks alienating the core value shopper. This is the variable to watch over the next 2-3 quarters.

## Valuation Assessment

At $233 the stock trades at 32.6x trailing EPS ($6.61) and ~30x forward FY2026 consensus ($7.80). Against the apparel-retail peer median P/E of 23.8x, ROST carries a ~26% premium; it sits below BURL (35.2x) but well above GAP (8.7x), URBN (14.9x), and BOOT (23.8x). The premium is partly defensible given ROST's best-in-class ROE (34.7%), FCF generation, and balance sheet, and the off-price format's strength in this cycle, but a premium for quality is not the same as mispricing.

On a growth-adjusted basis the stock is full. PEG on ~10% forward EPS growth at a 30x multiple is roughly 3x. The cycle-adjusted argument does not rescue the case here: the Q1 surge is being explicitly guided to decelerate, so trailing earnings are not a trough being masked by a high multiple; they are close to the through-cycle earnings power the company expects. The analyst target range ($176 low / $290 high, $265 median) brackets the stock with the current price near the lower-middle of the band, consistent with a name that has run to fair value rather than one trading at a discount to rising estimates.

The concrete valuation concern that prevents a BUY: after an 80% twelve-month appreciation, the price embeds the Q1 inflection plus continued flawless execution of a new CEO's strategy, leaving no margin of safety. A 24-25x forward multiple (high-$180s/low-$190s) would offer the asymmetry currently absent.

## Competitive Landscape

Off-price is structurally advantaged versus department and specialty stores: the ability to buy late-cycle from manufacturer overruns and cancellations, no promotional/co-op allowance demands, fast inventory turns, and a self-service low-labor format. The current environment of elevated cost of living and value-seeking across income tiers is a clear tailwind, evidenced by both Ross and TJX accelerating traffic simultaneously.

Ross's competitive position is stable-to-improving on trajectory: it is gaining customer count across demographics and expanding merchandise margin, which suggests the buying organization is executing. Two caveats temper the score. First, management itself describes barriers to entry as "limited," and competition spans TJX, Burlington, mass merchants, and increasingly online players. Second, the deliberate brick-and-mortar-only posture is both a near-term cost advantage and a long-term structural question if consumer purchasing migrates further online. The trade-up strategy is a calculated bet that could strengthen or dilute the value-positioning moat.

## Risk Assessment

**China/tariff concentration** is the dominant idiosyncratic risk. More than half of goods sold originate from China while 100% of stores are U.S.-based; the company explicitly states it is "especially susceptible" to U.S.-China trade policy. Tariffs cost ~$0.16/share in FY2025 and pressured merchandise margin.

**Tariff-refund optionality** is a two-sided item. In February 2026 the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that IEEPA-based 2025 tariffs were not statutorily authorized. Ross has filed refund claims but conservatively booked no receivable or gain as of May 2, 2026. Any recognized refund is upside not in current estimates; timing and amount are uncertain.

**Geographic concentration** is meaningful: CA/TX/FL hold ~50% of stores; ~half of DC capacity, ~22% of stores, and HQ are in California (earthquake/wildfire exposure). **Merchandising/packaway execution** risk is inherent to the model — misjudged packaway forces markdowns. The **no-e-commerce** posture excludes structural online growth. There is ongoing **California wage-and-hour class-action litigation** (management deems it immaterial) and **new-market execution risk** in NY Metro and Puerto Rico.

The cyclical caveat: the trade-down tailwind currently inflating comps would moderate in a strong consumer recovery, the inverse of the H2 consumer-cliff scenario but no less real for the multiple.

### Options Market Signal

The options term structure is in steep backwardation — near-term ATM IV of 75.1% versus long-term 28.1% — pricing imminent event stress, though the 1-week implied move is only ±0.9% (the elevated 1-week IV appears to be a thin-expiry artifact; the 1-month tenor reads a more reasonable 25.6%). Near-term IV is rich relative to 1-year historical volatility of 24.2% (85th percentile within the chain). Put/call skew is balanced (1.0%), and the put/call OI ratio of 1.55 leans modestly defensive. ATM IV has fallen ~6pp since mid-May even as the stock rose ~11%, consistent with post-earnings vol normalization. The options market is not flagging a strong directional bias, but it is pricing elevated uncertainty into the next event window, appropriate given the magnitude of the Q1 beat and the guided deceleration ahead.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$290):** Conroy's brand modernization and customer-acquisition push prove durable, comps run above the 6-7% guide, the trade-up strategy lifts basket without alienating core shoppers, and a tariff refund from the IEEPA ruling delivers a one-time EPS boost. EPS reaches the high-$8s to $9 in FY2027 and the market sustains a ~32x multiple on continued momentum. This is the analyst-high scenario, ~24% above current.

**Base case (~$255):** Comps decelerate to the guided 6-7%, margins hold the Q1 gains partially, and FY2026 EPS lands near $7.80 with FY2027 toward $8.60. The market holds a ~28-30x multiple on a best-in-class franchise. This implies ~$255, roughly 9% above current; capital return (buyback + ~0.8% dividend) adds a few points. The return profile is positive but not asymmetric.

**Bear case (~$185):** The +17% Q1 comp proves to be a calendar/comparison spike rather than a sustained step-change, the trade-up strategy confuses the value proposition, tariff costs re-escalate, and the consumer trade-down dynamic moderates as macro stabilizes. The multiple compresses to ~24x on $7.70 EPS, taking the stock to the high-$180s, roughly 21% below current and back toward the lower analyst target.

The asymmetry is roughly balanced — about 9% to base case and 24% to bull against 21% downside — which does not clear the bar for a BUY in a sector where company-specific selection has historically failed to beat the index.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** Timeframe: 12 months, with the next natural reassessment at the Q2 FY2026 print (August 2026), which will test whether the +17% comp moderated in line with or above the 6-7% guide.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pullback to the high-$180s/low-$190s (~24-25x forward FY2026 EPS), restoring a margin of safety.
- Q2 comp materially above the guided 6-7% (e.g., sustained double-digit) with traffic, not just basket, leading, indicating the inflection is structural rather than a one-quarter spike.
- Recognition of a material IEEPA tariff refund, which would lift EPS estimates outside current consensus.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Comp decelerates below the FY2025 +5% baseline, signaling the acceleration was a comparison artifact.
- Merchandise margin reverses (a sign the trade-up strategy is forcing markdowns or alienating core shoppers).
- A re-escalation of China tariffs that meaningfully exceeds the FY2025 ~$0.16/share drag.

## Conclusion

Ross is a high-quality franchise delivering a genuine, traffic-led fundamental acceleration under new leadership, with an excellent balance sheet and aggressive capital return. The business case is strong. The stock case is neutral: after an 80% twelve-month run to ~30x forward earnings, a ~26% premium to apparel-retail peers, the price already reflects the Q1 inflection and management's own guided deceleration, leaving symmetric rather than asymmetric risk/reward. Company-specific selection in this sector has historically struggled to outperform the index. We hold for the constructive growth trajectory and would upgrade on a pullback toward the high-$180s/low-$190s or on Q2 evidence that the comp acceleration is durable. The late-March insider selling cluster ahead of the blowout quarter, while largely post-vest diversification, and the absence of any open-market buying, support patience rather than pursuit at this level.
