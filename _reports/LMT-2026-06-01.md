**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Lockheed Martin is the world's largest defense contractor, with FY2025 revenue of $75.0B, a record $193.6B backlog (~2.6x annual sales), and a franchise position in the F-35 program that the market has correctly identified as durable. The demand environment is the strongest in a decade: a resolved FY26 appropriation ($839.2B), a proposed $1.5T FY27 request emphasizing munitions and missile defense, and an active Middle East conflict driving stockpile replenishment that benefits the Missiles & Fire Control segment directly. The top-line and backlog story is intact.

The problem is conversion. Revenue grew from $66.0B to $75.0B between 2022 and 2025, but net income fell from $6.92B (2023) to $5.34B (2024) to $5.02B (2025), and diluted EPS declined from $27.55 to $21.49 over the same window. The cause is a cluster of reach-forward losses on classified and fixed-price development programs totaling roughly $4B cumulatively — about $1.8B on an Aeronautics classified program, $1.46B on an MFC classified program, $670M on the Canadian Maritime Helicopter Program, and $130M on the Turkish Utility Helicopter Program — with management explicitly warning of further charges. Aeronautics segment margin compressed from 10.3% in 2023 to 6.9% in 2025; RMS from 11.5% to 7.6%. Q1 2026 was the clearest negative signal yet: EPS of $6.44 missed consensus by 5.2%, free cash flow turned negative at –$291M, cash fell from $4.1B to $1.9B, and the company repurchased zero shares versus $750M in the prior-year quarter.

At $521.83, LMT trades at ~25x trailing EPS and ~25.7x forward, against a peer median P/E of 39.3 that is distorted by HWM (59.9x) and TDG (39.3x). The more relevant comparison is to GD at 21.8x and NOC at 17.6x, both of which screen cheaper. The stock has fallen ~24% from its $692 high while estimate revisions are flat and the Q1 margin trajectory deteriorated, so the drawdown reflects a genuine reset of margin expectations rather than indiscriminate selling. The investment case rests on an unproven margin recovery; we rate the stock HOLD, with the recommendation history (four consecutive HOLDs) and a declining score trajectory both arguing for patience until the program losses stabilize and FCF recovers.

## Company Overview

Lockheed Martin operates four segments. **Aeronautics** ($30.3B, 40% of sales) is anchored by the F-35 Lightning II, which alone represents 27% of total company revenue — the single largest program-level concentration. The segment also includes F-16, C-130J, and F-22. Backlog stood at 336 aircraft as of Q1'26, with Lots 18/19 (296 aircraft) definitized in September 2025.

**Missiles & Fire Control** ($14.5B) is the growth and margin leader: PAC-3, THAAD, JASSM, LRASM, GMLRS, PrSM, Javelin, and hypersonics. The segment grew 14% in 2025 and carries a $46.7B backlog. This is the franchise most directly leveraged to the current munitions and missile-defense demand cycle. **Rotary & Mission Systems** ($17.3B) houses Sikorsky helicopters (Black Hawk, Seahawk, CH-53K), Aegis, radars, and combat systems. **Space** ($13.0B) covers FBM/Trident, Orion, Next Gen OPIR, GPS III, the Next Generation Interceptor, and the ULA joint-venture stake.

The competitive moat is incumbency on multi-decade franchise programs, the monopoly position in 5th-generation Western fighters, classified Skunk Works capabilities, and a backlog at 2.6x revenue with $120.2B funded. Customer concentration is high: 72% of FY2025 sales came from the U.S. Government, 63% specifically from the Department of Defense. Principal peers are RTX, Northrop Grumman, Boeing Defense, and General Dynamics.

## Financial Analysis

The financial picture is a high-quality revenue base undermined by program execution. Revenue rose steadily ($66.0B → $67.6B → $71.0B → $75.0B from 2022 to 2025), but profitability moved the other way. Diluted EPS stepped down through the interim 2024 figure of $22.31. Consolidated operating margin sits around 10.3%, but segment-level results swing on reach-forward losses, including the Aeronautics compression through an 8.8% mark in 2024.

FY2025 free cash flow was strong at $6.9B (up from $5.3B in 2024), helped by lower cash taxes under the new tax law and working-capital timing. That makes the Q1 2026 reversal more pronounced: net income of $1.49B was down 13% YoY, EPS came in below the prior year's $7.28, FCF turned negative (versus +$955M in Q1'25), and cash declined. The quarter included new unfavorable adjustments on F-16 (–$125M) and C-130 (–$85M), which extends the margin issues beyond the headline classified programs into the broader fixed-price book.

On the balance sheet, total debt is ~$21.9B against equity of only ~$6.7–7.5B. The low equity base is a function of years of buybacks and ~$7.5B of accumulated pension OCI losses, not operating weakness, which is why the Altman Z-Score of 1.53 reads as "distress" despite a $193.6B backlog, 74.6% ROE, 8.4% ROA, and well-covered interest. The Z-Score is a capital-structure artifact, not a solvency signal. The genuine concern is the income statement, not the bankruptcy risk.

Capital allocation shifted defensively. FY2025 returned ~$3.0B in buybacks plus ~$3.2B in dividends. In Q1 2026, the company bought back zero shares and repaid $1.0B of notes, a pivot to balance-sheet conservatism amid weaker cash flow. The dividend was raised to $3.45/quarter (~2.6% yield). The dividend looks well-covered on FY2025 FCF, but the buyback halt removes a support that LMT investors have historically relied upon, and it signals management's own assessment that the cash-flow outlook warrants caution.

## Growth Analysis

The demand backdrop is the strongest part of the thesis. FY26 funding was resolved with the Consolidated Appropriations Act signed February 3, 2026, removing near-term continuing-resolution risk. The FY27 request emphasizes munitions, Golden Dome missile defense, shipbuilding, and increased F-35 purchases. The active Middle East conflict drives stockpile replenishment, which flows most directly to MFC's air-and-missile-defense franchise (PAC-3, THAAD, GMLRS). The backlog provides multi-year revenue visibility.

The constraint is that demand strength is not translating proportionally to earnings. Two structural forces are at work: legacy fixed-price contracts that absorb inflation and supply-chain cost overruns, and a U.S. Government push to shift risk onto contractors through fixed-price development, progress payments, and IP-rights provisions. The result is that revenue can grow while EPS declines, as it has for two consecutive years.

Estimate revisions confirm the stall. Current-year consensus EPS sits at $29.89, essentially flat over both 30 and 90 days. Next-year consensus is $32.11, up only 0.5% over 90 days. The current-quarter estimate has drifted down 0.1%. There is no positive revision momentum here. Consensus is modeling a gradual margin recovery off the 2025 trough, but it is not accelerating, and Q1's miss casts doubt on the timing. Growth from here depends on the troubled programs stabilizing and the new munitions volume converting to margin, neither of which is yet visible in the reported numbers.

## Valuation Assessment

At $521.83, LMT trades at ~25.7x forward EPS and ~25x trailing. The peer median P/E of 39.3 is misleading because it is pulled up by HWM (59.9x, a high-margin aerospace component maker) and TDG (39.3x, an aftermarket parts compounder). Against the more comparable pure-play primes, LMT screens richer than GD (21.8x) and NOC (17.6x). NOC trades 30% below its 52-week high, GD 8% below. LMT's premium to these two is hard to justify given that its margin trajectory is the worst of the group and its FCF just turned negative.

The cycle-adjusted argument cuts against a bullish read. This is not a trough-earnings situation where trailing P/E understates normalized power: the earnings decline is driven by recurring reach-forward losses and structural margin compression, not a one-time cyclical dip that mechanically reverses. The cumulative program losses are largely realized, but management has flagged the potential for more, and the fixed-price risk shift is a durable headwind. Applying ~24x to the consensus FY26 figure yields roughly $717; applying GD's 21.8x yields ~$652. The wide gap between those outputs reflects how much the multiple assumption drives the answer, and the market's willingness to award a premium multiple is itself contingent on the margin recovery that has not yet materialized.

Analyst targets span $511 (low) to $756 (high), with a $614 median and $625 mean. The current price sits essentially at the analyst floor. We treat the consensus targets with skepticism rather than as a floor: estimates are flat, and the targets predate full digestion of the Q1 margin reset. The dividend yield provides some absolute support but is below the peer-relevant TDG yield and is not a standalone reason to own the stock.

## Competitive Landscape

LMT's competitive position is genuinely strong at the franchise level and weakening at the execution level. The F-35 monopoly in 5th-gen Western fighters, the PAC-3/THAAD air-and-missile-defense franchise, FBM/Trident sole-source work, and Skunk Works classified capabilities are moats that competitors cannot replicate on any near-term horizon. MFC in particular is the clearest competitive beneficiary of the current demand cycle.

The trajectory is the concern. Cumulative total shareholder return of 156.4 lags the defense peer group's 230.5; the gap reflects the market's penalty for execution stumbles (the classified program, F-16/C-130 adjustments) rather than a deterioration in franchise value. RTX and Northrop face similar fixed-price and supply-chain dynamics, but LMT's specific program losses have been larger and more visible. The competitive moat protects the revenue base; it has not protected margins, and the relative-TSR underperformance suggests the market views LMT as the weaker executor among strong franchises. We score competitive position moderately positive on franchise durability, tempered by the negative margin and TSR trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant idiosyncratic risk is further reach-forward losses. The Aeronautics classified program carries ~$1.8B cumulative losses with $450M still accrued at Q1'26, and management explicitly warns of further losses as testing continues. The MFC classified program carries ~$1.46B cumulative ($1.12B still accrued). CMHP has ~$670M in losses with $605M of contract assets at risk pending a restructuring complicated by US-Canada trade tensions. TUHP carries $130M in losses with up to $115M additional possible, dependent on export licenses. These risks are quantified, specific, and ongoing.

A securities class action (filed 7/28/2025, amended 1/12/2026, SDNY) alleges misstatements about the classified programs and F-35, with a class period ending the day before the Q2'25 loss announcement. A related derivative suit names directors and management. These corroborate that the program losses surprised the market and create a governance and reputational overhang.

A newly added FY25 10-K risk factor is an Executive Order that could restrict dividends, buybacks, and executive compensation for contractors failing performance requirements, implemented via a new DFARS clause. Given LMT's recent performance misses, this is an unusually relevant capital-allocation overhang. Additional risks: F-35 concentration (27% of revenue), 72% U.S. Government dependence, the rare-earth chokepoint (China export controls; tariffs cost ~$485M of cash flow in 2025, intensified by the current Middle East conflict and Hormuz disruption), a potential shift from performance-based to progress payments (cash-flow timing headwind), pension settlement volatility (a $479M non-cash charge in Dec 2025), and ULA exposure ($544M carrying value with Vulcan Centaur performance questions).

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 33.0% sits 6.2pp above 1-year historical vol of 26.9%, and the term structure is in backwardation (33.0% near-term versus 28.4% at six months), which signals an imminent catalyst. The put/call volume ratio of 2.80 is elevated, though the put/call OI ratio of 0.74 is balanced and put/call skew of –0.6% is roughly neutral. The 3-month tenor shows a skewed put/call volume ratio (9.64) and –3.4% skew, indicating hedging demand around the summer window. ATM IV rose 1.8pp intraday as the price fell 1.6%, consistent with growing near-term caution. The signal is consistent with the fundamental view: the market is positioned for volatility, not for a clean recovery.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

LMT is a $193.6B-backlog franchise in the strongest defense demand cycle in a decade, trading at a multiple that already reflects two years of disappointment. If the classified-program losses are now fully accrued (a plausible reading given management's incremental disclosures), margins recover toward historical norms as the troubled fixed-price contracts roll off and high-margin MFC munitions volume scales. FCF returns to the ~$6.9B FY25 level, buybacks resume, and the stock re-rates toward the peer-adjusted multiple. The bull target of ~$650–660 reflects roughly 22x normalized FY26 EPS plus dividend support, around +25% from current levels.

### Bear Case

The reach-forward losses are not finished. Management's warning of "further losses possible" on the Aeronautics classified program materializes, additional fixed-price adjustments emerge (as F-16/C-130 did in Q1), and the structural risk-shift to contractors caps margins below historical norms permanently. FCF remains pressured, the buyback stays suspended, the Executive Order on capital returns becomes operative, and the securities litigation extends the overhang. The multiple compresses toward NOC/GD's 17–22x range. A de-rate to ~18x on stagnant ~$29 EPS implies ~$430–450, roughly –14% to –17% from current levels.

The asymmetry is roughly balanced: modest upside to a margin recovery that has not yet appeared in the data, against meaningful downside if the program losses continue. That balance, combined with flat estimates and no insider buying, is what keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** The franchise quality is real, but the central question — whether the margin and FCF trajectory has bottomed — is unresolved, and the data through Q1 2026 leans negative.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive, sequentially improving free cash flow with no new reach-forward loss accruals on the classified programs.
- EPS estimate revisions turning positive (+5% or more over 90 days), confirming a margin recovery the market can underwrite.
- Resumption of the buyback program at a meaningful cadence (signaling management confidence in the cash-flow outlook).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A new multi-hundred-million-dollar reach-forward loss on the Aeronautics or MFC classified programs, or a writedown of the $605M CMHP contract assets.
- The Executive Order on contractor dividends/buybacks becoming operative in a way that constrains LMT's capital returns.
- A second consecutive quarter of negative FCF without a credible timing explanation, or a guidance cut.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q2 2026, expected late July). The summer window is when the options market is pricing the most uncertainty, and it is the natural checkpoint for whether the Q1 deterioration was a timing artifact or a trend. The FY27 appropriations process is a secondary catalyst on the demand side.

## Conclusion

Lockheed Martin presents a genuine tension between an excellent franchise and poor execution conversion. The backlog, demand cycle, and competitive moats are among the best in the defense sector, and at $521.83 the stock has already absorbed a ~24% decline from its high. The decline reflects a real reset of margin expectations, not indiscriminate selling: net income has fallen three straight years, FCF turned negative in Q1, buybacks were halted, and securities litigation corroborates that the ~$4B in program losses surprised the market. Estimate revisions are flat, there is no open-market insider buying, and the multiple at ~25x is richer than the most comparable primes (GD 21.8x, NOC 17.6x) despite worse margin trajectory.

This is the fourth consecutive HOLD on LMT, and the bar for changing the call should be high in either direction. The bull case requires a margin recovery that the data does not yet show; the bear case requires further program losses that management has flagged as possible but not confirmed. With balanced risk/reward, flat estimates, and the options market pricing near-term stress, the appropriate posture is to wait for the Q2 print to clarify whether the FCF and margin trajectory has bottomed before committing capital in either direction. HOLD.
