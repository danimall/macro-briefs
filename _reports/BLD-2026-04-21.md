## Executive Summary

TopBuild Corp is currently subject to a definitive acquisition agreement by QXO Inc. at $505 per share (~45% cash, ~55% QXO stock), announced April 19, 2026, with an expected close in Q3 2026. At the current price of ~$474, the stock trades 6.2% below the offer price. This gap reflects merger-arbitrage spread capturing deal-completion risk — primarily regulatory/antitrust scrutiny given QXO's serial acquisition pace (third deal in ~12 months), the mixed consideration structure exposing shareholders to QXO equity risk, and time value of money to close.

The standalone business remains the largest U.S. insulation installer and specialty distributor, with $5.4B in 2025 revenue, $697M in free cash flow, and a leading market position in a fragmenting industry. However, 2025 showed meaningful deterioration: organic volumes declined 8.1%, margins compressed across both segments (net margin fell 210bp to 9.6%), and the balance sheet absorbed ~$1.9B in acquisition-related debt. These are relevant factors for downside scenarios if the deal fails, but secondary to the acquisition dynamics for current positioning.

The investment question reduces to a probability-weighted assessment of deal completion versus reversion to standalone value. With the board unanimously approving, QXO's experienced deal team (Brad Jacobs), and the strategic logic clear, completion probability is reasonable but not certain given antitrust considerations in building products distribution.

## Company Overview

TopBuild operates two segments: Installation Services (~59% of revenue), which installs fiberglass insulation, spray foam, commercial roofing, and related products through 200+ branches; and Specialty Distribution (~41%), which distributes building and mechanical insulation through 250+ distribution centers. Revenue splits roughly 52% residential / 48% commercial-industrial, with ~22% of revenue classified as non-cyclical (maintenance, re-roofing, repair). The company employs 14,707 people, including 6,744 insulation installers.

TopBuild was spun off from Masco Corp in 2015 and has since executed a roll-up strategy, completing seven acquisitions in 2025 alone, including the transformational Progressive Roofing (~$900M+) and SPI ($1B) deals. Customer concentration is low (top customer = 3.1% of revenue), and the company sources from four primary fiberglass suppliers (CertainTeed, Johns Manville, Knauf, Owens Corning).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** $5.41B in 2025, up 1.5% YoY. Growth was entirely acquisition-driven (+8.8%), as organic volumes declined 8.1% and pricing contributed only +0.8%. The three-year revenue trajectory shows decelerating growth: +3.7% (2023), +2.6% (2024), +1.5% (2025). Pre-deal 2026 guidance was $5.925B-$6.225B, reflecting full-year contributions from 2025 acquisitions.

**Margins compressed materially in 2025:**
- Gross margin: 29.0% (down from 30.5% in 2024), driven by volume declines, residential pricing pressure, $12.5M branch consolidation costs, and $11.4M SPI inventory step-up amortization
- Operating margin: 14.6% (down from 16.6%), further impacted by acquisition-related SG&A and amortization
- Net margin: 9.6% (down from 11.7%), hit by $30.7M higher interest expense from new debt

**Earnings declined despite revenue growth:** Net income fell 16.2% to $522M ($18.28 diluted EPS vs. $20.29 in 2024). Installation Services operating margin contracted from 19.7% to 18.5%; Specialty Distribution from 15.1% to 12.8%.

**Cash flow remains a strength:** Operating cash flow of $756M, capex of just $59M (~1.1% of revenue), yielding $697M in FCF (12.9% FCF margin). The capital-light model generates high cash conversion — FCF/Net Income of ~134%.

**Balance sheet leveraged post-acquisitions:** $1.9B spent on acquisitions funded by ~$2.0B in new debt issuance. Goodwill reached $3.05B (46% of total assets), intangibles $1.35B. Total debt-to-equity at 1.85x. Total liquidity of $1.12B (cash + revolver). Altman Z-Score of 2.17 sits in the grey zone, compressed by the heavy goodwill load typical for acquisitive services businesses.

At implied FCF yield of ~5.2% on the current market cap, cash generation is adequate even at compressed margin levels.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth has turned negative. Installation Services organic volume declined 11.2% in 2025, reflecting single-family housing weakness and multi-family starts that remain subdued. Specialty Distribution grew 7.8% but largely via the SPI acquisition.

The growth story for TopBuild as a standalone entity rests on three pillars: (1) continued M&A in a fragmenting $95B addressable market, (2) energy code tightening driving higher insulation content per home, and (3) commercial/industrial backlog strength. All three are credible over a multi-year horizon but face near-term headwinds from elevated mortgage rates (reducing residential starts), tariff-related demand uncertainty, and the fact that acquisition-driven growth at these debt levels has diminishing marginal returns on equity.

Pre-deal 2026 guidance of ~$6.1B midpoint implies ~12% revenue growth, but this is almost entirely inorganic. The real organic growth question is whether residential volumes stabilize — the company described commercial bidding as "strong" but residential remains rate-sensitive.

None of this matters much at current pricing given the pending acquisition, except as a floor-value calculation if the deal fails.

## Valuation Assessment

**Merger-arbitrage framing:** At $473.77 vs. $505 offer, the 6.2% spread annualized over ~5 months to expected Q3 2026 close equates to a ~15% annualized return if the deal closes on time with full cash election. The mixed consideration (45% cash / 55% QXO stock, subject to proration) means actual returns depend on QXO's trading value at close. This is a material complication — QXO itself is a rapidly leveraging platform with three major acquisitions in 12 months.

**Standalone valuation context:** Trailing P/E of 26.8x and forward P/E of ~26.8x trade at roughly 2.1x the peer median P/E of 12.7x. Peers Toll Brothers (9.9x), Lennar (12.7x), Taylor Morrison (7.4x), and NVR (15.4x) all trade at significant discounts. IBP (Installed Building Products, the closest comp) trades at 29.6x. The premium to most homebuilders reflects TopBuild's services/distribution model (higher margins, lower capital intensity) versus the capital-heavy homebuilders, but the premium to IBP's similar model is minimal.

If the deal fails, the stock would likely revert toward standalone value. Using a 14x forward earnings multiple (above the homebuilder median, below the current level) on ~$18 EPS suggests a standalone floor around $250-280 — a 40-47% decline from current levels. This is the key risk in the arb position.

**Deal valuation:** QXO is paying ~14.9x expected 2025 EBITDA pre-synergies, ~11.8x post-$300M synergies. This is within the range of recent building products deals (Home Depot/SRS at ~12-14x) and reflects a reasonable strategic premium.

## Competitive Landscape

TopBuild holds the #1 position in U.S. insulation installation and distribution, operating in a market characterized by high fragmentation and low barriers to entry locally but significant barriers to national scale. The dual-channel model (direct installation for large builders + distribution for smaller contractors) provides a natural hedge and procurement leverage.

The industry is in a phase of rapid consolidation. Home Depot acquired SRS Distribution for $18.25B in 2024. QXO's serial acquisitions (Beacon, Kodiak, TopBuild) are creating a ~$18B revenue platform that would rank as the #2 publicly traded building products distributor in North America. This consolidation wave validates TopBuild's strategic value — it's the kind of asset that acquirers bid for.

Customer diversification is strong (top 10 customers = 10.6% of revenue), and the four-supplier fiberglass sourcing base provides adequate bargaining position without single-source risk.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Deal Completion Risk (PRIMARY).** QXO's prior regulatory experience is mixed — TopBuild itself had a prior SPI deal terminated in April 2024 due to regulatory complications (with a $23M termination fee). QXO's rapid acquisition pace (three major deals in 12 months) may attract antitrust scrutiny from the FTC, particularly in building products distribution where the combined entity would hold dominant share in insulation. The mixed cash-stock consideration adds QXO equity risk. Deal break probability: estimated 15-25%.

**2. Residential Construction Weakness.** Organic residential volumes declined 11.2% in 2025. With mortgage rates elevated and consumer confidence at record lows, a sustained downturn in single-family starts would pressure standalone value if the deal fails. The current housing market bears multiple macro headwinds: elevated energy costs flowing through to construction materials, tariff uncertainty, and tightening credit conditions.

**3. Leverage.** Post-acquisition debt is substantially higher, with ~$2B issued in 2025. Interest expense increased $30.7M YoY. The 5.625% Senior Notes carry a meaningful coupon. If the QXO deal fails and housing weakens further, the debt load constrains financial flexibility.

**4. Labor/Immigration Risk.** 43.9% of employees self-identify as Hispanic. The 10-K explicitly flags immigration enforcement as a material operational risk. This is specific and unusual for a large-cap company to disclose at this level of detail, suggesting management views it as genuine.

**5. Goodwill Impairment.** $3.05B goodwill + $1.35B intangibles = 66.5% of total assets. The company carries $762M of historical impairment losses from 2008-2010. A significant housing downturn could trigger impairment — particularly for the Progressive Roofing unit, which became its own reporting unit for goodwill testing.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 23.3% sits at the 3rd percentile of the current chain and trades at a massive 19.9pp discount to 1-year historical volatility of 43.2%. The term structure is in steep contango (23.3% near-term vs. 37.8% 6-month), consistent with a pending acquisition compressing near-term price distribution toward the deal price while longer-dated options price the tail risk of deal failure. The put/call volume ratio of 31.7x is extreme — put volume dominates, but the OI ratio of 0.93 is balanced, suggesting the heavy put volume is recent and may reflect arb hedging rather than directional conviction. The balanced OI P/C suggests the market does not have a strong directional bet against deal completion. The cheap near-term IV is consistent with a stock trading as a deal arb, not a fundamental equity.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case:** The QXO acquisition closes on schedule in Q3 2026 at $505/share. Shareholders receive the mixed consideration. If QXO equity trades at or above its deal-implied value, total return from the current $474 price is ~6.5% over ~5 months (~15% annualized). The combination with Beacon and Kodiak creates a platform with genuine procurement synergies and cross-selling potential, potentially supporting QXO equity value. TopBuild's ~18% adjusted EBITDA margin is a high-water mark in building products distribution that enhances the combined entity.

**Bear Case:** The FTC challenges the deal on antitrust grounds, given QXO's dominant combined share in insulation and roofing distribution. Deal termination sends BLD shares back toward standalone value. With organic volumes declining, margins compressing, and the balance sheet leveraged, standalone BLD at current earnings power (~$18 EPS) trades at 12-15x, implying a stock price of $220-270 — a 43-54% decline from current levels. Additionally, if the housing market deteriorates further amid rising energy costs and weakening consumer confidence, standalone value could be lower still.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- If BLD trades at $485+ and regulatory approval signals emerge (FTC early termination granted, or second request completed without challenge), the remaining spread to $505 with reduced deal risk would warrant upgrading. Specifically, if the Hart-Scott-Rodino waiting period expires or is terminated without challenge, the risk-adjusted return improves materially.
- If BLD pulls back to $440-450 on non-deal-specific market weakness (broader housing sector selloff, not regulatory concern), the spread to $505 widens to 12-15% and the arb becomes more attractive.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FTC issues a second request with language suggesting serious competitive concerns in insulation distribution
- QXO equity declines >20% from deal announcement levels, materially reducing the value of the stock component of the consideration
- Management or board language changes suggesting deal terms may be renegotiated downward

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural reassessment point is (1) any FTC filing deadline or regulatory update, likely within 60-90 days of the April 19 announcement, and (2) BLD's next earnings report, which should provide updated 2026 guidance and standalone operating trends.

## Conclusion

TopBuild is a merger-arb situation, and the investment decision is primarily a probability-weighted bet on deal completion, not a fundamental equity analysis. The standalone business has clear strengths (market leadership, strong FCF generation, low capital intensity) and clear weaknesses (organic volume declines, margin compression, elevated leverage, housing cycle sensitivity). At ~$474, the 6.2% spread to the $505 offer price is neither compelling enough to recommend as a BUY (given the mixed cash-stock consideration, antitrust risk from QXO's serial acquisitions, and a severe downside if the deal breaks) nor negative enough to AVOID (the deal has board approval, strategic logic, and an experienced acquirer). The appropriate posture is to wait for regulatory clarity before committing capital. HOLD.
