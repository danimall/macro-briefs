**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Insight Enterprises is a $3.6B-market-cap IT solutions integrator in the middle of a deliberate transformation from a low-margin hardware/software reseller into a higher-margin services and solutions business. Its headline revenue decline (from $10.4B in 2022 to $8.25B in 2025) overstates the deterioration. Gross profit held roughly flat over that period ($1.64B → $1.76B) and gross margin expanded to a record 21.4%, because the revenue shrinkage is dominated by cloud/software migrating to net (agent) revenue recognition and principal-to-agent vendor reclassifications, not by demand loss. Roughly $4.0B of "netted" costs pass through the model each quarter, making headline revenue and cash-conversion ratios largely uninformative. The right health metrics are gross-profit dollars and services mix, both improving.

Q1 2026 delivered the clearest evidence yet that the services pivot is translating into profit: gross margin +240bps to 21.7%, gross profit +14% to $462M, operating income +19% to $71.7M, and net income roughly quadrupling to $30.0M ($0.97 diluted EPS vs $0.22). North America Adjusted EFO margin jumped to 7.3% from 5.5%. Consensus EPS estimates have risen +7% for both the current and next year over the past 90 days, and management delivered a 15.6% EPS beat in the March quarter. The board hired Jack Azagury, former Chief Group Executive at Accenture, a direct competitor on the services side, as CEO effective April 13, 2026 to accelerate the "AI-first solutions integrator" strategy.

The counterweights keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY. Debt roughly doubled in 2025 (long-term debt $531M → $1.36B, now $1.47B) to fund convertible repayment, warrant settlements, and M&A, with a variable-rate ABL revolver exposed to a higher-for-longer rate path (interest expense +46% in 2025, +51% in Q1'26). Gross profit is heavily dependent on discretionary vendor "partner funding" that management flagged as a 2025 drag, and Microsoft alone is 17% of revenue and 32% of purchases. Rising earnout liabilities from an aggressive M&A cadence will keep generating lumpy GAAP charges and cash outflows through 2027. The stock has risen ~85% off its March low (~$63.62 to $117.65) and now sits mid-range with a balanced risk/reward. I would accumulate on weakness toward the low-$100s or on a second confirming quarter of services-margin expansion.

## Company Overview

Insight (Chandler, Arizona; founded 1988, IPO 1995) operates across three geographic segments: North America (81% of 2025 sales), EMEA (16%), and APAC (3%), with roughly 14,505 teammates. Its model sits in the middle of the IT value chain: it buys hardware and software from 6,000+ partners and resells with services wrapped around it. The 2025 mix was 79% product / 21% services by revenue, but 41% product / 59% services by gross profit, so services drive the economics.

The company organizes its offering around five solution pillars: Hybrid Multicloud, Cybersecurity, Data & AI, Digital Workplace & Devices, and Intelligent Applications. Its estimated addressable market is roughly $1.3T for 2026; Insight's $8.25B represents less than 1%, in a highly fragmented industry. The competitive set is bifurcated: systems integrators and consultants (Accenture, Capgemini, Atos, TCS, Infosys, HCL) on the services side, and resellers/VARs (CDW, SHI, WWT, Presidio, Computacenter) on the product side. Insight also competes with its own suppliers (Microsoft, Dell, Cisco, HP) who increasingly sell direct.

The supply side is concentrated. The top-five suppliers (Microsoft, TD Synnex, Google, Cisco, Ingram Micro) are ~63% of purchases. The customer side, by contrast, is well diversified, with no single customer above 10% of revenue.

## Financial Analysis

The multi-year income statement makes the transformation legible once you look past the revenue line:

| 2022 | $10.43B | $1.637B | 15.7% | $413.7M | $280.6M | — |
| 2023 | $9.18B | $1.670B | 18.2% | $419.8M | $281.3M | — |
| 2024 | $8.70B | $1.766B | 20.3% | $388.6M | $249.7M | $6.55 |
| 2025 | $8.25B | $1.761B | 21.4% | $334.9M | $157.3M | $4.86 |

Revenue fell ~21% from 2022 to 2025 while gross profit rose ~8% and gross margin expanded ~570bps. The revenue decline is structural: on-premise software migrating to cloud (recorded net, as agent), vendor relationships shifting Insight from principal to agent, and a large one-time multiyear software deal in Q1'24 with no 2025 comparable. Netted (agent) costs were $4.0B in Q4'25 alone, so headline revenue and any DSO/DPO metric derived from it are distorted.

The 37% drop in 2025 net income was driven mostly by non-operational items: a $25.1M loss on warrant settlement revaluation, a $25.3M net loss on earnout revaluations (versus a $7.8M gain in 2024), a $12.6M real-estate impairment on the Santa Monica property, $37.1M of severance/restructuring, a 46% jump in interest expense ($58M → $85M), and a higher tax rate (30.3% vs 25.0%, inflated by non-deductible warrant/earnout losses). Adjusted EFO was essentially flat year-over-year ($504.0M vs $502.4M), confirming operating stability beneath the GAAP noise.

Q1 2026 revenue was +1% to $2.13B (−1% FX-neutral), helped by the absence of the prior-year warrant loss and a lower share count. This is the first quarter where the services-mix shift shows up cleanly in both margin and bottom line.

The balance sheet is where the caution sits. Long-term debt roughly doubled: $531M (2024) → $1.36B (2025) → $1.47B (Q1'26), composed of an ABL revolver (~$976M, matures 2030, variable rate) plus $500M 6.625% Senior Notes due 2032, with an additional ~$260M of inventory financing. The debt funded repayment of $333M in maturing convertible notes, $222M of cash warrant settlements, ~$285M for the Inspire11 and Sekuro acquisitions, and buybacks. The Altman Z-Score of 1.58 ("distress zone") is a false signal for a distributor: enormous receivables ($5.5B) and payables ($4.3B) inflate the balance sheet and compress equity-to-assets and EBIT-to-assets ratios. The company is covenant-compliant, generates positive free cash flow ($279M in 2025), and had roughly $1.1B undrawn on the ABL; the Piotroski F-score supports sound fundamentals. FCF fell from a very strong 2024 (OCF $633M) on lower earnings and working-capital swings inherent to the inverted cash cycle; the company pays suppliers faster than it collects.

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly. No dividend (ABL covenants restrict it), but $151M of buybacks in 2025 and $75M in Q1'26 at an average of $83.37, well below the current $117.65. A new $299M authorization (Dec 2025) leaves ~$224M remaining. Share count has fallen from ~35M (2022) to ~30.2M.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is a gross-profit and services-margin story, not a revenue-growth story. The reported top line will keep drifting as more revenue migrates to net recognition, but that is a mix effect, not deterioration. The relevant trajectory is: services now generate 59% of gross profit; North America Adjusted EFO margin expanded ~180bps year-over-year in Q1'26; and gross margin has climbed steadily to a record.

Estimate revisions corroborate the direction. Current-year consensus EPS moved from $10.70 (90 days ago) to $11.45, and next-year from $11.55 to $12.37, both +7% over 90 days and +2.4–2.5% in the last 30 days. This does not clear the +30% "strong inflection" threshold, but it is a consistent upward drift across every forward window rather than noise. The March-quarter beat and Q1'26 net income growth both reinforce confidence in the forward path. The trailing four-quarter record is 2 beats, 2 misses, so visibility is decent but not fortress-grade.

Industry tailwinds support the direction: cloud and AI adoption, cybersecurity demand, and the hybrid-work device lifecycle (DaaS). The 2025 M&A — Inspire11 (AI/data delivery, $210M) and Sekuro (APAC cybersecurity, ~$79M) — is directly aligned with the higher-margin pillars. The DRAM/NAND memory shortage entering 2026 is double-edged: it constrains product availability but can lift hardware pricing and revenue, and Insight noted higher device demand and pricing in Q1'26.

Whether the pivot compounds or plateaus depends on execution. Services margins compete against Accenture, Capgemini, and TCS on one side and CDW/SHI product economics on the other. The CEO hire signals the board intends to push the transformation harder, but execution against consultants operating at scale is unproven. I score growth as solid rather than exceptional, and I weight it alongside financial health rather than as a standalone conviction driver, consistent with recent estimate momentum being positive but not extreme.

## Competitive Landscape

Insight's position is a moving target. It is executing an up-market pivot against Accenture, Capgemini, and TCS on the services side while defending against CDW, SHI, and WWT on product. The 59%-of-gross-profit services mix and expanding North America EFO margin suggest the trajectory is improving at the margin, which is the relevant variable, given trajectory matters more than absolute level in a fragmented sub-1%-share market.

The structural risks to the moat are real. Hyperscalers and OEMs increasingly sell direct, creating disintermediation risk, and the same cloud consumption that Insight facilitates is recognized net (agent), structurally shrinking reported revenue. The company's economics depend heavily on discretionary vendor partner funding — rebates and marketing funds that flow straight through gross profit and change annually with no contractual guarantee. Management flagged that "recent changes in incentives for cloud-based solutions adversely impacted 2025 results." A Microsoft, Google, or Cisco program change can hit margins with little warning, and Microsoft's 32%-of-purchases concentration magnifies that exposure.

The Azagury hire is a credible attempt to strengthen the services flank by importing consulting DNA from the largest global integrator. Whether Insight can win services share against firms operating at far greater scale on the consulting side is the central competitive question, and it will take several quarters of segment data to answer. On balance the competitive trajectory is modestly positive but not yet proven, so I hold this dimension near neutral, consistent with competitive-position scores having offered little recent predictive edge and needing confirmation by financial results.

## Risk Assessment

**Partner-incentive dependence** is the primary idiosyncratic risk. Vendor partner funding flows directly through gross profit, changes annually, and is not contractually guaranteed. This was a documented 2025 headwind and can reverse the margin story quickly.

**Microsoft concentration** (17% of revenue, 32% of purchases) means any shift in Microsoft's channel strategy — more direct selling, incentive cuts — is material to both revenue and margin.

**Leverage into a higher-for-longer rate environment.** The ABL is variable-rate, and management expects rates higher than historical norms through 2026. This is a genuine drag that compounds if the operating inflection stalls.

**Acquisition and earnout risk.** Aggressive M&A (SADA, Amdaris, Infocenter, Inspire11, Sekuro) has created large contingent earnout liabilities (Inspire11 up to $66M; Sekuro up to AUD 122.5M; Infocenter up to $53.1M remaining). Earnout revaluations produced a $25.3M GAAP loss in 2025 and another ~$25.3M in Q1'26, and will keep generating lumpy charges and cash outflows through 2027, a recurring source of GAAP "misses" if the market fixates on reported EPS. Goodwill has grown to ~$1.17B (71% of equity).

**Memory shortage (new for 2026).** The DRAM/NAND shortage flagged in the Q1'26 10-Q can constrain supply while lifting pricing; net direction uncertain.

**Management transition.** A new CEO plus a string of officer-departure 8-Ks (Feb, Apr, Oct, Nov 2025) warrants monitoring, though much appears to be planned refresh aligned with the strategy shift.

**Public-sector and FX exposure** add second-order risk (government budget/shutdown risk; strong USD hurts EMEA translation).

None of these are binary catastrophes, but they cluster around margin volatility rather than being cleanly priced away, which keeps the risk score below neutral.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 55.6% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 47.6% (an 8.1% spread) and sits in the 77th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (55.6% near-term vs 50.1% at six months), and put/call skew is elevated at +3.4%, with an implied move of ±12.2% at the nearest expiry. The June 18-to-July 26 delta shows ATM IV collapsing from an anomalous 343.4% reading to 55.6% as price rose ~11.9%, so the prior spike appears to have been a thin-options artifact rather than signal. The current picture (rich IV, backwardation, modest put demand) indicates the market expects a meaningful move around the next catalyst (likely the next earnings print) and is hedging accordingly. For a HOLD, this argues against chasing here and in favor of waiting for the post-print IV crush to establish a better entry.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$150–165).** The Q1'26 inflection proves durable: services mix continues climbing above 59% of gross profit, North America Adjusted EFO margin holds in the 7%+ range, and gross-profit dollars grow high-single-digits while the revenue optics stabilize. Consensus catches up to the rising estimate trajectory, the memory-shortage dynamic lifts product revenue and pricing, and Azagury's consulting playbook accelerates high-margin AI/data/security services. A ~13x multiple on ~$12.40 next-year EPS gets to ~$160, with continued buybacks below intrinsic value amplifying per-share growth. GAAP noise from warrants and earnouts fades from 2027 as the earnout schedule runs off.

**Bear Case (~$80–90).** Partner-funding cuts (Microsoft/cloud incentive changes) compress gross margin back toward 20%, the higher-for-longer rate path keeps interest expense climbing against a variable-rate ABL, and earnout revaluations keep producing GAAP misses that the market punishes. Disintermediation by hyperscalers and OEMs erodes the product base faster than services can offset, revenue decline accelerates from mix to genuine demand loss, and the services pivot plateaus against better-scaled consultants. A ~10x multiple on flat-to-lower earnings, plus derating on execution doubt, takes the stock into the $80s, roughly the level it traded at through late 2025.

The base case sits between: earnings grow modestly, the multiple holds around 10–11x forward, and the stock compounds with buybacks but without a decisive rerating until a second or third quarter confirms the services margin is structural rather than a favorable-comparison artifact.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth orientation. I see a credible 12-month appreciation path contingent on the services inflection compounding, but the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric at $117.65.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A second consecutive quarter (Q2'26, expected early August) of gross-margin expansion and North America Adjusted EFO margin holding ≥7%, confirming Q1 was structural.
- Estimate revisions accelerating to +10%+ over a 90-day window on current-year EPS, indicating consensus is systematically catching up.
- A pullback toward the low-$100s or below (widening the discount to forward earnings) on unchanged fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Evidence of material partner-funding cuts (a Microsoft/cloud incentive-program change) that compresses gross margin back toward or below 20%.
- Interest expense growth outpacing operating-income growth for two consecutive quarters, signaling the leverage is eroding the operating gains.
- Revenue decline shifting from mix-driven (net recognition) to demand-driven, or a goodwill/earnout impairment materially exceeding the recurring revaluation charges.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (early August 2026) is the natural checkpoint; it tests whether Q1's margin inflection was a single favorable comparison or the start of a trend, and the elevated near-term implied volatility suggests the market is treating it as a genuine catalyst.

## Conclusion

Insight is a genuinely improving business wearing a misleading top-line. Once you strip out the agent-recognition optics and the 2025 GAAP charges, what remains is a company with a record 21.4% gross margin, flat-to-rising gross-profit dollars, an accelerating services mix (59% of gross profit), and a Q1'26 quarter that quadrupled net income and expanded margins 240bps. Rising estimate revisions, the March-quarter beat, disciplined buybacks executed at prices well below current, and a strategically pointed CEO hire from Accenture all support the transformation thesis.

The reasons to hold rather than buy are equally concrete: leverage roughly doubled into a higher-for-longer rate environment, gross profit leans on discretionary vendor incentives with heavy Microsoft concentration, earnout revaluations will keep injecting GAAP volatility through 2027, and a memory-shortage overhang cuts both ways. The stock has risen sharply off its March low and sits above the mean and median analyst targets with a degrading sentiment trend, and the options market is pricing near-term stress. At ~10.3x forward earnings the valuation is fair-to-modestly-cheap but not a compelling asymmetric entry.

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold), overall score 6.0.** The trajectory of prior scoring (5.7 → 6.3) reflects a legitimate improving thesis, and the Q1'26 data confirms rather than reverses it; I hold the score roughly flat because the operating turn is real but the balanced financing and margin-dependency risks keep it from clearing the BUY bar without a second confirming quarter. I would accumulate on a pullback toward the low-$100s or on Q2'26 confirmation that the services-margin expansion is structural.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: trailing P/E was overstated (~20.2x stated vs ~24.2x actual at $4.86 EPS), making the trailing multiple somewhat rich rather than "roughly in line" with peers — a modestly less attractive valuation picture than the report implied.
