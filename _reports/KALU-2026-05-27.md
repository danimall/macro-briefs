**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Kaiser Aluminum is experiencing a material earnings inflection driven by the completion and ramp of approximately $500M in growth capital investments (Phase VII at Trentwood, 4th coating line at Warrick). Q1 2026 delivered $129M in adjusted EBITDA — annualizing to $516M versus $310M in FY2025 — on the back of operating leverage, higher-margin coated packaging volumes, and $36M in favorable metal price lag effects. Management raised full-year 2026 EBITDA guidance to $372-403M with $140-150M in free cash flow, and the aero/defense segment shows genuine structural demand acceleration.

The challenge for new capital is that the stock at $189 has already moved 159% from its 52-week low, now sits above the highest analyst price target ($183), and the CEO/CFO/multiple executives sold $10M+ in shares at $173-178 in late April. The consensus mean price target is $165, implying ~13% downside. Additionally, $36M of Q1's $129M EBITDA came from metal price lag — a non-recurring, reversible tailwind that flatters the apparent earnings power. On an EV/EBITDA basis against management's own 2026 guidance midpoint (~$388M), the stock trades at approximately 10.8x including net debt — reasonable but not cheap for a cyclical materials business with 2.1x leverage and significant operational concentration risk.

## Company Overview

Kaiser Aluminum manufactures specialty semi-fabricated aluminum products for high-barrier-to-entry end markets: aerospace/defense (25% of revenue), packaging (44%), general engineering (23%), and automotive extrusions (8%). The company employs ~3,840 people across facilities including the critical Trentwood (WA) plate/sheet plant and Warrick (IN) packaging operation.

The core business model is conversion-based: KALU earns profit from transforming aluminum into finished semi-fabricated products, passing through underlying metal costs to customers. This makes Conversion Revenue (~$1.45B in FY2025) the relevant top-line metric rather than total net sales ($3.37B), as roughly 57% of revenue represents pass-through metal cost.

Key competitive advantages include decades of metallurgical expertise, KaiserSelect® quality differentiation, multi-year customer qualification processes (particularly in aerospace), and concentrated production at scale facilities with recent capacity expansion.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Inflection:** The financial trajectory is unambiguous. Net income grew from $47M in FY2024 to $113M in FY2025, then Q1 2026 alone delivered $62.5M ($3.71/diluted share) — nearly triple the year-ago quarter. Adjusted EBITDA rose from $241M (FY2024) to $310M (FY2025) to a $129M quarterly record in Q1 2026.

**Drivers of Improvement:**
- Phase VII Trentwood and 4th coating line at Warrick past start-up phase ($47M of one-time costs in FY2025 now behind)
- Conversion revenue per pound rose from $1.24 to $1.31 (+5.6%) in FY2025
- Metal price lag delivered $93M favorable impact in FY2025 and $36M in Q1 2026

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $1.05B in fixed-rate senior notes (5.875% due 2034 + 4.50% due 2031). No maturities until 2030 following the Q4 2025 refinancing. Net leverage improved from 3.4x to 2.8x by Q1 2026 end, with management targeting 2.0-2.5x. Liquidity of $596M (cash + revolver) is adequate.

**Cash Flow:** FY2025 FCF was negative (-$25.5M) due to $137M capex and working capital build from rising aluminum prices. Q1 2026 generated $69M in FCF. Management guides $140-150M for full-year 2026 with capex declining to $120-130M as the growth investment cycle concludes.

**Altman Z-Score:** 2.68 (grey zone) — reflecting elevated leverage and historically thin margins. Improving rapidly as EBITDA scales.

**Dividend:** $3.08/share annually (1.6% yield at $189), 19 consecutive years. Covered by current earnings and FCF generation.

## Competitive Landscape

Kaiser occupies defensible positions in its primary markets:

**Aerospace Plate:** Only a handful of global producers can manufacture heat-treated aluminum plate to aerospace specifications. Multi-year qualification processes create high switching costs. The defense demand surge ("quadrupling" on some programs) provides a countercyclical buffer against commercial aerospace volatility.

**Packaging Coil:** The Warrick facility's coating capabilities differentiate KALU from commodity can-stock producers. The 4th coating line adds capacity to a market where domestic supply is constrained by Section 232 tariffs.

**General Engineering:** Semiconductor-grade plate has limited supplier competition domestically. The reshoring wave provides structural demand that competitors cannot easily address without similar multi-year facility investments.

**Competitive Risks:** Material substitution (composites in aerospace, plastics in packaging), potential tariff policy changes, and the emergence of recycled-content competitors.

**Trajectory:** Improving. Capacity investments have widened the capability gap versus smaller competitors, and tariff protection enhances the domestic supply position.

## Risk Assessment

**Operational Concentration:** Trentwood and Warrick represent the overwhelming majority of production. Either facility experiencing extended downtime would be catastrophic and unmitigable in the near term.

**Alcoa Dependency:** KALU relies on Alcoa for essential resources at Warrick including potable water. This single-point-of-failure creates existential risk from a relationship that could be weaponized in any commercial dispute.

**Metal Price Lag Reversal:** If aluminum prices decline, the favorable lag tailwind reverses into a headwind. Management explicitly warned about this.

**Insider Selling:** CEO Harvey sold $8.7M in shares ($173-178 range) on April 27-29, 2026. CFO West sold $866K. EVP Donnan sold $489K. All sales occurred within 4 weeks of the current date at prices 7-9% below the current $189. No insider purchases have been observed. While potentially pre-planned (10b5-1), the breadth and timing — post-earnings, near all-time highs — warrants caution.

**Customer Concentration:** Top customer represents 16% of revenue; top 5 represent 56%. Loss or significant reduction from any top customer would materially impair results.

**Labor Relations:** ~65% unionized workforce with multiple contracts expiring in 2026. USW holds 40% board nomination rights through 2030 — a governance arrangement that creates tension between shareholder and labor interests.

**Inventory Accounting Change:** The switch from LIFO to weighted-average cost in a rising aluminum price environment likely boosted reported FY2025 earnings relative to what LIFO would have shown.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($220-240, +16-27%):**
Q1 2026 EBITDA run-rate ($516M annualized) is closer to sustainable reality than management's conservative $372-403M guidance. Aerospace demand accelerates beyond management's 15-20% shipment growth as defense appropriations flow through. Semiconductor plate doubles as guided. Metal lag remains favorable through 2026. EPS exceeds $12 in FY2026. At 20x = $240. FCF generation enables deleveraging to <2.0x, unlocking buyback resumption.

**Bear Case ($130-145, -23-31%):**
Metal price lag reverses as aluminum corrects. Aero destocking extends rather than concluding (management's "7th inning" call proves premature). EPS normalizes to $7-8 once lag and one-time ramp benefits fade. At 18x $7.50 = $135. Multiple contracts expire in 2026 with labor disruption risk. Alcoa disputes Warrick resource agreement.

**Base Case ($175-195, -5% to +3%):**
Management delivers on guidance. Metal lag provides moderate tailwind but normalizes in H2. The stock consolidates around current levels as the rapid appreciation is digested and analyst targets gradually revise upward. Limited upside from current price because the inflection is largely priced.

## Conclusion

Kaiser Aluminum is executing a genuine earnings inflection — the capital investments are ramping, segment demand across aerospace/defense/semiconductor is strengthening, and Section 232 tariffs provide domestic competitive protection. The 35.7% upward revision in current-year estimates over 30 days confirms fundamental momentum.

However, the stock at $189 has already discounted much of this improvement: it trades above all analyst price targets, insiders collectively sold $10M+ at $173-178 four weeks ago, and a meaningful portion of recent earnings comes from metal price lag effects that management explicitly identified as reversible. The risk/reward from current levels is approximately symmetric: upside to $220-240 if the bull case plays out, downside to $130-145 if lag reverses and aerospace disappoints. This symmetry, combined with insider selling and the stock's position above consensus, argues for holding existing positions but not initiating new ones at $189.
