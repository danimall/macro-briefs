**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ServiceTitan is a vertical-market cloud platform that has become the system of record for residential and commercial trades contractors: HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, and adjacent field-service verticals. The company runs roughly $82 billion of annual Gross Transaction Volume (GTV) across approximately 10,800 active customers, with a land-and-expand model that produces ~110% net dollar retention and >95% gross retention. Revenue is growing 25-27% with the most recent quarter showing acceleration, gross margins around 70%, positive free cash flow (~$105M reference), and a net-cash balance sheet. The GAAP operating loss is overwhelmingly stock-based compensation, not cash burn.

The defining feature of the current setup is the gap between operating trajectory and price. The stock IPO'd at $71 in December 2024, spiked above $119, and has round-tripped back to ~$75 — down roughly 33% from its 52-week high and effectively flat to the IPO price — while quarterly results accelerated (four consecutive double-digit EPS beats, +27% revenue growth, ~560bps of non-GAAP operating margin expansion). Current-year EPS estimates have risen 16.9% over 90 days. This is the inflection profile the scoring framework rewards: revenue growth above 25%, positive estimate revisions, and a price that has not kept pace.

What holds us at HOLD rather than BUY is the combination of three things the inflection screen does not capture. First, valuation is not cheap on the metrics that matter for a not-yet-GAAP-profitable SaaS name: the stock trades around 47x forward FY27 EPS of $1.61 (and ~59x current-year EPS of $1.28) and ~6x forward revenue, so the multiple already embeds continued execution. Second, the management/governance picture is genuinely weak: relentless, broad-based insider selling at progressively lower prices ($107 → $99 → $92 → $70) with zero open-market buying, layered on a dual-class control structure. Third, near-term options price a ±16% implied move into the next earnings window with the term structure in backwardation, signaling the market expects an imminent catalyst. The business is good and the price is more reasonable than it was, but neither fact overrides the governance overhang and event risk into a name that is still richly valued.

## Company Overview

ServiceTitan sells an all-in-one operating system for the trades. The platform spans the full contractor workflow (CRM, scheduling and dispatch, call booking, marketing, estimating and sales, job costing, inventory, payroll, and accounting) and increasingly layers on embedded fintech (payments, consumer financing, payroll). The business uses a January 31 fiscal year-end, so FY2026 is the year ending January 31, 2026.

The revenue model is hybrid. A core subscription (seat-based SaaS) is supplemented by usage-based revenue tied to GTV, principally a payments take-rate and financing attach. This is the structural distinction from typical seat-based SaaS: a growing share of revenue scales with the dollar value of jobs flowing through the platform. The mechanism cuts both ways. As contractors digitize and GTV grows, ServiceTitan monetizes transaction flow with operating leverage; when home-services demand softens (driven by housing turnover, weather, or higher consumer financing rates) that same usage linkage compresses revenue in a way a pure subscription book would not.

The moat rests on vertical depth and switching costs rather than scale. Horizontal CRM vendors lack trade-specific workflows; point solutions lack the all-in-one breadth plus fintech rails. As the system of record for the entire business, ServiceTitan is sticky, and its proprietary dataset across trade transactions feeds AI/ML features (Titan Intelligence, the agentic "Atlas" layer) that management is positioning as the next monetization wave.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** FY2025 revenue grew from roughly $772M to ~$961M, about 24.5%, and recent quarters accelerated. Q1 FY2026 showed ~27% total revenue growth with subscription up ~29%. The split between recurring subscription and usage/fintech gives both a higher growth ceiling and genuine macro sensitivity.

**Margins.** Gross margin sits around 70%, healthy for vertical SaaS but below pure-software peers because the lower-margin fintech/payments component is blended in. GAAP operating losses persist, but they are driven by stock-based compensation rather than cash consumption. Q1 FY2026 non-GAAP operating margin improved ~560bps year-over-year, the operating-leverage inflection the equity story needs.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Free cash flow is positive (~$105M reference figure), so the business self-funds. Debt was paid down post-IPO and the company is essentially net-cash with a large cash position from IPO proceeds. Financial-health screens (high Altman Z, solid Piotroski) are strong. The dominant non-cash item is SBC, including a very large founder/co-founder performance RSU award that distorts GAAP profitability and reported EPS. The trailing GAAP EPS of -$1.73 and the negative reported P/E are artifacts of this; they are not a solvency or burn signal.

**Earnings quality and estimate momentum.** The company has beaten consensus EPS in all four reported quarters, by 43% to 84%. Current-quarter consensus EPS sits at $0.28 (revised up 21.1% over 90 days), current-year at $1.28 (up 16.9%), next-year at $1.61 (up 12.7%). The 90-day revision trend is positive but has flattened in the last 30 days (0.0% across all periods), which tempers the "accelerating revisions" read. The signal is real but no longer accelerating.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has three legs. First, the core digitization of a large, fragmented, late-to-software trades economy supports a long subscription runway at >25% growth with >100% net retention. Second, private-equity consolidation of home-services contractors is creating larger, more sophisticated buyers that need enterprise-grade operating systems, a structural tailwind for upmarket motion and net dollar retention. Third, fintech take-rate expansion on the GTV base provides a monetization layer on top of seats, and AI/Atlas features offer a further attach vector leveraging the proprietary dataset.

The durability of this growth is above-average for a single-company SaaS story because the demand driver (trades digitization plus PE roll-up sophistication) is broad-based rather than dependent on one product cycle. The principal qualifier is that the fintech and usage component injects housing/consumer cyclicality that standardized SaaS screens miss. In a residential-construction or home-improvement downturn, a live risk given elevated long-end yields and a stressed consumer, GTV-linked revenue would decelerate faster than the subscription book.

The revenue-growth-plus-rising-estimates combination that ordinarily flags a strong inflection is partially present, but it does not clear the high bar for a BUY here because the multiple already prices it and the revision velocity has stalled in the most recent 30 days.

## Valuation Assessment

ServiceTitan does not screen as cheap on the metrics that govern a not-yet-GAAP-profitable SaaS platform, but it is materially more reasonable than at its highs. On a ~$5.8B market cap against revenue growing 25-27%, EV/revenue is roughly 6x, toward the lower-to-middle of the high-growth vertical SaaS cohort and well below where the stock traded post-IPO.

The trailing P/E of -43x is meaningless given SBC-driven GAAP losses; this is a name where forward and cash-based metrics are the only relevant frame. On those, the picture is "fairly valued for a high-quality grower," not "mispriced bargain." A ~47x forward earnings multiple on a 25% grower implies a PEG well above 1.5, so the multiple is not low relative to growth. The inflection-stock principle that "analyst targets are floors" applies most cleanly when forward P/E is below ~30x or PEG below 1.5, and TTAN sits above both thresholds on an earnings basis. The ~6x revenue multiple is the more flattering lens and is defensible for the growth and retention profile, but it is not a screaming discount.

Analyst targets cluster well above spot: mean $99.75, median $100, range $67-$125, against a "current" reference of $72.63. Fifteen of eighteen analysts rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy. On a name with positive revisions and a reasonable revenue multiple, that consensus is more likely a floor than a ceiling, and it frames the upside case. But on an earnings basis the stock is not cheap enough to treat the gap to target as low-risk.

Net: the ~33% drawdown from the high has improved risk/reward, and the disconnect between price and accelerating fundamentals is genuine. The valuation score reflects a stock that is reasonably, not asymmetrically, priced.

## Competitive Landscape

ServiceTitan's competitive position is improving on the metrics that matter for vertical SaaS. Net dollar retention near 110% and gross retention above 95% indicate the moat is holding and expanding within the installed base. The all-in-one breadth plus fintech rails is difficult to replicate: horizontal CRMs (Salesforce) lack trade-specific workflows, and point solutions lack the integrated payments/financing layer. The PE-driven consolidation of contractors structurally favors ServiceTitan's upmarket motion, as larger buyers need exactly the enterprise system-of-record breadth the company offers.

The competitive risks are real but not near-term acute: adjacent field-service platforms, PE-backed roll-ups building in-house tooling, and larger players moving down-market. The central structural debate is whether fintech take-rate expansion (high-growth, lower-margin, regulation-exposed) can continue alongside sustained 20%+ subscription growth. On balance, share and moat are widening, supporting a constructive competitive-position score.

## Risk Assessment

**GTV/housing cyclicality.** The largest company-specific risk the market may underweight is the usage-based revenue's direct linkage to home-services transaction volume and consumer financing. Higher long-end yields and a stressed consumer would compress GTV monetization faster than a seat-based book. This is a genuine macro sensitivity, not a generic SaaS truism.

**Fintech/payments regulation and credit.** Embedded payments, lending, and payroll introduce money-transmission licensing, consumer-lending regulation, fraud, chargeback, and partner-bank dependency, a materially different risk profile than pure software.

**Governance and insider supply.** Dual-class control concentrates voting power with co-founders Mahdessian (CEO) and Kuzoyan (President) via Class B super-voting shares, limiting minority influence. The more tangible overhang is the insider selling described above: founders, CFO, and CAO sold large blocks at declining prices through Dec 2025-May 2026, with no open-market buying anywhere in the decline. Much of the CFO/CAO activity is sell-to-cover on RSU vesting (informational noise), but the founders' outright multi-million-dollar dispositions are genuine economic-stake reductions and a real float overhang. The "negative Compensation Actually Paid" in the proxy is a mark-to-market artifact on founder performance RSUs as the stock fell, not a clawback or distress signal, but it underscores how large the founder equity overhang is.

**Lockup/dilution.** As a recent IPO with heavy SBC, the float is sensitive to insider/VC supply and ongoing dilution. The watch item is the pace of GAAP-loss narrowing relative to dilution, not solvency.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 96.6% against 1-year historical volatility of 50.6%, rich by 46 points, in the 83rd percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (96.6% at 1m vs 78.2% at 6m), which signals the market is pricing imminent stress, with an implied move of ±15.9% on the nearest expiry. Skew is balanced (put/call OI 0.42, volume 0.47), so positioning is not directionally bearish through options; the market is pricing a large two-sided event, not a one-way collapse. Short interest at 14.4% of float with 6.2 days to cover is elevated and creates squeeze potential on a positive print, but it also reflects genuine institutional skepticism. The combination of a large priced-in event move plus elevated SI argues for patience over chasing into the catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

ServiceTitan compounds revenue at 25%+ with >100% net retention, expands non-GAAP margins ~500bps/year toward Rule-of-40+, and grows fintech take-rate on a rising GTV base. Estimate revisions prove the market is catching up to fundamentals, the stock re-rates toward the ~6-7x revenue / mid-30s forward earnings range as GAAP losses narrow, and consensus targets prove to be a floor. The short base squeezes on a clean print. Bull target ~$110-115 (toward the $125 Street high and prior high).

### Bear Case

A housing/home-services slowdown decelerates GTV-linked revenue, consumer-financing attach compresses on elevated rates, and fintech regulation raises cost/complexity. Continued insider supply caps the float, SBC dilution erodes per-share economics, and the forward multiple compresses as the inflection stalls, the SaaS de-rating template that has produced 40-50% drawdowns on correct-but-priced themes. Bear target ~$54-55 (toward the 52-week low of $54.17), roughly -27% to -28% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next quarterly print, the catalyst the implied move is pricing, is the natural reassessment point. A 12-month horizon frames the broader thesis.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Forward FY27 P/E compresses below ~35x (via continued price stability and rising estimates) AND 90-day EPS revisions resume an upward trajectory above +10%, restoring the inflection signal with a PEG approaching 1.5.
- Insider behavior inflects: any open-market founder/C-suite buying, or a clear cessation of programmatic selling, removing the governance overhang.
- A clean earnings print confirming sustained 25%+ revenue growth and another leg of non-GAAP margin expansion (~400bps+), with no GTV deceleration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Revenue growth decelerates below ~18% YoY, or GTV-linked revenue shows clear sequential compression on a housing/consumer slowdown.
- Non-GAAP margin expansion stalls or reverses while SBC dilution continues, pushing the path to GAAP profitability further out.
- A negative estimate-revision turn (CY EPS revised down >5% over 90 days) alongside continued insider distribution.

## Conclusion

ServiceTitan is a structurally advantaged vertical SaaS platform with a long digitization runway, strong retention, expanding margins, positive free cash flow, and a net-cash balance sheet. The drawdown to roughly the IPO price, against accelerating operating results and positive current-year estimate revisions, has materially improved risk/reward and creates a credible constructive 12-month case toward the $100 consensus median.

We stop short of a BUY for three reasons. The stock is not cheap on the earnings frame that matters for a not-yet-GAAP-profitable name (~47x forward, PEG well above 1.5), so the multiple already embeds continued execution. Governance is a genuine negative: dual-class control plus relentless, broad-based insider selling at falling prices with zero buying. And near-term options price a large two-sided event into the next print, making this a poor moment to add ahead of a catalyst the market is actively hedging. The call is HOLD with a positive 12-month growth lean: own it for the compounding and the fundamentals-vs-price gap, accumulate on weakness toward the mid-$50s or on a confirmed estimate re-acceleration, and upgrade if the multiple compresses into the mid-30s forward while insiders stop selling.
