## Executive Summary

Matador is a Delaware Basin pure-play upstream operator with a materially sized, separately financed midstream arm, and it is currently the clearest example in the mid-cap E&P group of a company whose equity does not participate in the oil price it produces. At March 31, 2026 the company held 18.425 MMBbl of April–December WTI collars with a $66.36 ceiling — roughly 67,000 Bbl/d, or about 56% of Q1 oil volumes. The 10-Q disclosed WTI at $95.08 on May 6, 2026. As an illustrative sensitivity rather than a forecast, a $95 average across the remaining hedged window implies roughly $529M of cash collar settlements, about $4.26 per share pre-tax. Q1 already showed a $51.8M realized collar loss ($4.79 per Bbl produced). Simultaneously, realized two-stream gas collapsed to $0.64/Mcf from $3.56 as the Waha–Henry Hub basis widened to ($5.99)/MMBtu in Q1 and approximately ($6.57) through May 6 — worsening rather than mean-reverting. Those two mechanics explain why the stock is 29.6% below its March 30 high of $66.84 while the world's oil-price risk has been skewed upward.

The counterweight is a genuinely cheap, growing, low-recourse-leverage business. FY2025 diluted EPS was $6.09 (7.7x trailing at $47.03); consensus is $6.92 for 2026 (6.8x) and $8.78 for 2027 (5.4x), with the out-year estimate up 5.9% over 90 days. The dividend was raised 20% to $0.375 quarterly, a 3.2% yield. Recourse debt at 3/31/2026 was $2,585M against TTM Adjusted EBITDA attributable to Matador of $2,227.6M — 1.16x, and the revolver was paid down to $45M by May 6. Q2 2026 (secondary-sourced) delivered revenue of $1.19B, +32.5% year-over-year, GAAP net income of $390.7M ($3.15) and non-GAAP EPS of $2.61 against a $2.13 consensus, alongside raised full-year production guidance. Insiders bought in the open market across the CEO, CFO, COO, Chief Accounting Officer and two directors between May 27 and June 15, 2026 at $51.44–$56.29, all above today's price.

What stops this from being a buy is the combination of what the sellers are actually reacting to. Between July 22 and July 28 the stock fell 16%, bracketing the July 23 announcement of $1.275B of cash consideration for Paloma Permian plus an undisclosed Ridge Runner Woodford package; the August 5 earnings beat and guidance raise were met with a 3.6% decline. Consensus for the current quarter was cut 19.1% in 30 days despite four consecutive beats. San Mateo funded the $752M Cardinal acquisition with a new $650M term loan maturing July 2027 against a facility that restricts distributions above 4.50x debt/EBITDA and caps leverage at 5.00x, and on a grossed-up estimate its pro forma leverage sits near 5.5x. Near-term implied volatility is rich (51.5% versus 41.8% realized) and the term structure is backwardated. HOLD, growth-oriented: own it for the Hugh Brinson startup and Paloma accretion, but the risk stack does not justify new capital at this price.

## Company Overview

Matador (Dallas, founded 2003, public since 2012) operates two reported segments.

**Upstream** is concentrated in the Wolfcamp and Bone Spring of the Delaware Basin (Eddy and Lea Counties, New Mexico, and West Texas), with a small legacy Haynesville/Cotton Valley position in Louisiana. The concentration is close to absolute: per the Q1 2026 10-Q the Delaware Basin supplied 100% of daily oil and 97% of daily gas. Q1 2026 production averaged 207,594 BOE/d — 120,277 Bbl/d oil (58%) and 523.9 MMcf/d gas — up 5% and 4% year-over-year. Pro forma for Paloma and the Woodford adds, Delaware acreage reaches roughly 240,000 net acres.

**Midstream** is San Mateo Midstream, 51% Matador / 49% an affiliate of Five Point Infrastructure, consolidated with a $328.2M non-controlling interest at 3/31/2026 but financed by a credit facility explicitly non-recourse to Matador. San Mateo gathers, treats and processes gas, gathers and transports oil, and handles produced water, serving Matador under 15-year fixed-fee dedications and third parties. Matador is a *customer* of San Mateo, owing $701.2M of remaining minimum volume commitments to it. Separately, Matador holds 500,000 MMBtu/d of firm transportation on Energy Transfer's Hugh Brinson pipeline — Matador holds capacity and bears deficiency risk; Energy Transfer owns the asset. Five Point is a joint-venture co-owner that pays Matador performance incentives ($6.9M in Q1 2026, booked to additional paid-in capital rather than income).

Founder Joseph Wm. Foran remains Chairman and CEO. 2026 brought an internal executive realignment: Christopher Calvert moved from COO to CFO, Glenn Stetson from EVP–Production to COO, and G. Gregg Krug (EVP–Marketing and Midstream) transitioned to an advisor agreement effective February 28, 2026.

The margin path — net margin 30.0% (2023) → 25.4% (2024) → 20.8% (2025); operating margin 42.9% → 41.2% → 33.5% — is driven by two mechanical items. DD&A rose 22.7% to $1,195.4M as an acquisition-led asset build (total assets more than doubled since 2022) raised the full-cost depletion base. FY2025 ROE was 14.1% on average Matador equity of $5,373.6M; ROA 6.7%.

**Q1 2026 is a gas-price and hedge-accounting event, not an operating break.** Oil revenue rose 5% to $788.3M on a realized $72.83/Bbl. Gas revenue fell 81% to $30.4M as realized two-stream pricing dropped to $0.64/Mcf ($1.44 including realized hedges) against a $3.47/MMBtu Henry Hub — a basis problem, not a benchmark problem. The GAAP loss of $(35.9)M, or $(0.29) per share, was dominated by a $255.5M unrealized mark-to-market derivative loss as oil rallied through out-of-the-money collar ceilings. Adjusted EBITDA attributable to Matador fell only 10% to $577.2M; operating cash flow of $470.5M was down 35%, but excluding working-capital movements it fell 7% to $564.2M — the gap is a $188.7M receivables/prepaid build partly offset by a $113.5M payables build. No full-cost ceiling-test impairment was required.

**Q2 2026** (obtained from secondary reporting only; the release and 10-Q were not available) showed revenue of $1.19B (+32.5%), GAAP net income of $390.7M / $3.15 per share, and non-GAAP EPS of $2.61 versus $2.13 consensus, with raised full-year production guidance. GAAP exceeding non-GAAP is consistent with a partial reversal of Q1's mark-to-market loss, though the reconciliation was not obtained. Q2 production, EBITDA, June 30 debt balances and the updated hedge book are unverified; all leverage arithmetic below uses March 31, 2026 balances.

**Capital structure at 3/31/2026 (face):** $185M revolver due 2029, $918M San Mateo facility due 2029 (non-recourse), $900M 6.500% notes due 2032, $750M 6.250% notes due 2033, $750M 6.000% notes due 2034 — $3,503M total. Recourse debt was $2,585M, or $2,445M after the revolver was cut to $45M by May 6. Recourse leverage 1.16x TTM Adjusted EBITDA of $2,227.6M; consolidated roughly 1.4–1.5x. Both sit well inside the 3.5x Credit Agreement maximum, and EBITDA/interest coverage is near 10x. Liquidity was $30.5M cash plus $2,011.2M of revolver availability; San Mateo, by contrast, had only $166.6M available before Cardinal.

Two screening artifacts deserve correction. The GAAP current ratio of 0.73x is not a covenant issue: the Credit Agreement's ≥1.0x test adds unused revolver availability to current assets and excludes current maturities. And an Altman Z near 1.6 reflects 0.31x asset turnover and market equity roughly equal to total liabilities — structural features of a full-cost E&P carrying acquired properties at historical cost — not a solvency signal. What the score does correctly flag is that the balance sheet has become far more asset- and liability-heavy since 2022.

**Cash returns are light.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $2,425.0M. The widely quoted $882.8M of "free cash flow" is OCF less $1,542.3M of drilling/completion/equipping capex only; it excludes midstream capex, property acquisitions, distributions to Five Point, dividends and buybacks. Including those, FY2025 was closer to cash-flow neutral. Dividends of roughly $156M and buybacks of roughly $56M totaled about 8.7% of OCF. Buybacks were effectively dormant in Q1 2026 (17,702 shares at $39.92), with $343.4M of the $400M authorization remaining at January 31, 2026.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case is real and multi-sourced, which matters more than any single item.

**Volumes and inventory.** Q1 oil volumes +5% and gas +4% year-over-year; full-year 2026 production guidance was raised with Q2. Paloma Permian adds 16,235 net undeveloped acres in Eddy and Lea Counties, more than 156 net two-mile locations, 55 MMBOE of proved reserves and 10,600–11,600 BOE/d of Q3 2026 production (57% oil) for $1.275B cash, closing in Q4 2026. Derived acquisition metrics: 1.56x the $816M PV-10 (struck at $70.00/Bbl and $3.00/MMBtu at May 31, 2026) and roughly $23.2 per proved BOE.

**A new zone.** The Ridge Runner transaction plus organic leasing takes Matador to roughly 50,000 contiguous net Woodford acres at about $4,000/acre with more than 150 net operated locations at roughly $1.3M per location — cumulative land spend on the order of $200M, small against a $5.8B market cap. The "Rae's Creek" well in southeast Lea County tested above 2,200 BOE/d (72% oil) on June 29, 2026 and is running about 20% above the average Texas Woodford well on 60-day cumulative oil. Management targets 30–40% well-cost reduction over 12–18 months. This is inventory bought ahead of price discovery; one well is not a delineated play.

**Midstream.** Cardinal Midstream closed August 3, 2026 for $752M, adding a 320 MMcf/d designed-inlet processing complex in Loving County and roughly 145 miles of gathering. San Mateo now exceeds 1.0 Bcf/d of designed processing and 800 miles of gathering, with nine new third-party customers and roughly 100 rigs operating within ten miles. Expected contribution is "up to $110M annualized by 2028" — a target date, not a run-rate, and Cardinal's in-place EBITDA at closing was not disclosed.

**The counter-signal is near-term consensus direction.** Current-quarter EPS was cut from $1.99 to $1.61 over 30 days (-19.1%) and current-year from $7.70 to $6.92 (-10.1%), even after four consecutive beats of 5.8%, 6.2%, 18.8% and 22.5%. The most plausible mechanics are the collar settlements against a high strip, continued Waha basis drag, incremental DD&A and interest from the acquisition program, and possible gas shut-ins during negative pricing. Next-year consensus is essentially unchanged over 30 days (-0.1%) and up 5.9% over 90 days, which is the shape you would expect if analysts are marking down 2026 realizations while carrying the Hugh Brinson and Paloma benefits into 2027. That split — deteriorating near-year, improving out-year — is the honest characterization of Matador's growth profile today, and it is why I do not treat the rising out-year estimate as a standalone buy trigger.

## Valuation Assessment

At $47.03: 7.7x FY2025 diluted EPS of $6.09, 6.8x FY2026 consensus of $6.92, 5.4x FY2027 consensus of $8.78, and a 3.2% dividend on $1.50 annualized. Note a data conflict: the vendor P/E field of 12.10x does not reconcile with the supplied $6.09 EPS at any recent price; I use the EPS-derived multiples, which match the filing arithmetic ($759.2M ÷ 124.586M diluted shares).

Peer P/Es are not usable as a direct anchor — MUR at 65.8x, SM at 32.5x, CHRD negative, MGY at 15.8x, CNX at 4.4x reflect wildly different degrees of earnings depression, hedging and gas exposure. The median of 32.5x tells you the group is at trough earnings, not that Matador is 4x cheap. More useful: Matador generates 14.1% ROE and 6.7% ROA at 1.16x recourse leverage with 10x interest coverage, which is a mid-quality profile trading at a low-single-digit forward multiple.

Sell-side targets are $54 low, $68.63 mean, $93 high. Spot sits 12.9% below the *lowest* published target. The lessons of this cycle argue against treating analyst targets as independent confirmation, and one known input (BMO's $65, March 11, 2026) predates both acquisitions. But the dispersion is informative in one direction: no analyst has a target below spot, while short interest is 11.5% of float at 7.3 days to cover. Someone is wrong, and the resolution mechanism is identifiable — the Hugh Brinson in-service date and the realized gas price it produces.

The reason I score valuation at 6.2 rather than 7+ is that the earnings base supporting the multiple is compressed on both sides through 2026. If WTI stays near the levels implied by the May 6 mark, roughly 56% of oil volumes settle against a $66.36 ceiling and the cash cost is large. If crude normalizes toward the $70s, the collar drag shrinks but so does revenue, with the $52.75 floor only becoming useful well below current prices. Meanwhile the gas leg realized $0.64/Mcf in Q1 and the basis was wider still through May. Cheap multiples on hedge-capped, basis-impaired earnings are exactly the configuration that has historically trapped value buyers in energy. The asymmetry is favorable, not compelling.

A useful cross-check on management's own capital allocation: Matador is paying 1.56x PV-10 and $23.2 per proved BOE for Paloma while its own equity trades at 5.4x next-year earnings with $343M of unused buyback authorization. The company is choosing inventory over share count. That is a defensible long-horizon choice for a company with a long drilling runway, and it is also precisely what the market repriced 16% lower in late July.

## Competitive Landscape

The binding constraint in the Delaware Basin is gas egress, not rock. A realized $0.64/Mcf against a $3.47/MMBtu Henry Hub is the cleanest available statement of that, and the 10-Q attributes the widening differential to takeaway capacity concerns. Every operator producing associated Permian gas faces it; the differentiator is who contracted out of it first.

Matador's answer is two-pronged and unusual at its scale. First, 500,000 MMBtu/d of firm transport on Hugh Brinson routes gas to Henry Hub-linked and LNG-adjacent markets, and most Delaware gas already prices at Houston Ship Channel rather than Waha, which the 10-Q says has historically produced a narrower effective differential net of higher transport charges. Second, San Mateo now processes more than 1 Bcf/d and is described by the company as the largest private gas processor in the northern Delaware Basin. In a basin where flow assurance determines whether wells produce at all, owning the plant is a durable advantage — San Mateo's Q1 segment operating income of $62.7M on $135.9M of gross midstream revenue exceeded the E&P segment's $18.6M in that depressed quarter.

The vertical-integration model carries three costs: capital diverted from drilling, $701.2M of minimum volume commitments owed to Matador's own affiliate, and 49% of San Mateo's economics accruing to Five Point ($30.4M distributed to Five Point in Q1 versus $31.6M to Matador).

On sourcing, all three 2026 targets — Paloma (EnCap Investments), Ridge Runner (EnCap Investments) and Cardinal (EnCap Flatrock Midstream) — are EnCap-family entities, with a relationship Foran describes as decades long. That is a repeat-counterparty advantage in diligence speed and a concentration of counterparty risk. RBC Richardson Barr advised the sellers on Paloma/Ridge Runner, which suggests a marketed process rather than a bilateral negotiation, though I have no direct confirmation of competitive tension in the pricing.

Weaknesses in trajectory terms: three purchasers accounted for approximately 72% of FY2025 revenue, down from roughly 79% — but that improvement coincides with an 81% collapse in gas revenue and 5% growth in oil revenue. A mix shift is not customer diversification. Roughly one-third of leasehold sits on federal land, and Matador added 5,154 acres in a May 2026 federal lease sale, so BLM permitting and royalty-rule exposure is rising, not falling.

## Risk Assessment

**Waha basis (dominant near-term).** ($5.99)/MMBtu in Q1 2026, approximately ($6.57) through May 6, against roughly ($5.11) in the December 2025–February 2026 window disclosed in the 10-K. The direction is adverse. Mitigants: Houston Ship Channel pricing on much of the Delaware gas, NGL extraction reducing residue volumes exposed to Waha, Waha swaps (12.2 MMMBtu April–July 2026 at −$0.53) and basis swaps (41.25 MMMBtu April–December at −$2.52) carrying a combined +$72.4M asset at 3/31/2026. The structural fix is Hugh Brinson.

**Hugh Brinson timing and deficiency exposure.** Energy Transfer expects initial flows in Q3/Q4 2026 and full service by year-end. Matador begins paying for capacity on a third party's schedule. Aggregate non-affiliate deficiency exposure was $2.29B at 3/31/2026 if Matador ceased operations in committed areas, plus $701.2M to San Mateo; total disclosed contractual obligations were $6.93B.

**Collar ceilings.** 18.425 MMBbl of April–December 2026 WTI collars at $52.75/$66.36 (~56% of oil volumes) and 3.65 MMBbl for 2027 at $55.00/$76.00. Matador is materially less levered to an oil spike than an unhedged peer — relevant for anyone buying this as an oil-price call option.

**San Mateo leverage (unresolved).** Grossing up Q1 Adjusted EBITDA attributable to the non-controlling interest ($33.78M at a 49% interest) implies roughly $276M annualized at the San Mateo level against pro forma debt near $1.51B ($860M revolver at May 6 plus the new $650M term loan) — approximately 5.5x against a 5.00x covenant. Standard resolutions exist (pro forma EBITDA credit for acquired assets, a concurrent amendment, or higher in-place Cardinal EBITDA), and my derivation from an NCI allocation is not covenant EBITDA. This should not be treated as an established breach. What is firm: the 4.50x distribution-restriction trigger matters because Matador funded its $51M Cardinal contribution out of $92.3M of San Mateo distributions received in 2026, and the $650M term loan matures July 2027. The debt is genuinely non-recourse — Matador is not a guarantor and San Mateo is not a Restricted Subsidiary of the 2034 Notes — so the exposure is loss of distributions and equity value, not a claim on Matador.

**Full-cost ceiling test.** PV-10 fell roughly 11% to $8.24B at YE2025 from $9.23B at YE2024 despite production and reserve growth, because the SEC 12-month average deck declined. No impairment through Q1 2026. Any impairment would be non-cash, would not trip the Credit Agreement's covenants, but would reduce book equity and reset DD&A rates.

**Other.** Sub-investment-grade ratings near BB−/Ba3/BB set the 6.00%–6.50% coupons and matter as acquisition borrowings layer on. Pipeline interruptions cost roughly 3,600 BOE/d in Q4 2025, and Matador may again shut in high gas-oil-ratio wells during negative gas pricing. A "Limited Waiver and Amendment" to the Credit Agreement dated February 27, 2026 was filed as an exhibit without narrative explanation; its timing one week before the 2034 Notes closed supports a mechanical reading, and I draw no inference about motive from a document I did not read.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 51.5% against 41.8% one-year realized — rich, at the 82nd percentile of the current chain — and the term structure is backwardated (51.5% at 14 days versus 46.8% at six months), which prices near-term event risk rather than direction. Since the July 21 snapshot, ATM IV rose 6.9 points while the shares fell 13.1%, and put/call open interest moved from 0.38 to 0.44. The absolute open-interest ratio of 0.44 remains call-tilted, so positioning is mixed: the 1m tenor is described as showing elevated put demand (3.8% skew) while the 3m and 6m readings carry the opposite sign, and I do not infer direction from sign conventions that are not defined. The ±8.5% implied move on the August 21 expiry alongside 11.5% short interest at 7.3 days to cover means both hedging cost and squeeze potential are elevated. For a holder, this argues against buying downside protection at current premiums; for a buyer, it argues against paying up in front of a resolvable catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($70).** Hugh Brinson enters service on Energy Transfer's schedule by year-end 2026, Matador's 500,000 MMBtu/d of firm transport moves realized gas from $0.64/Mcf toward Gulf Coast pricing, and the single largest drag on 2026 earnings reverses in 2027. Paloma closes in Q4, adding 10,600–11,600 BOE/d at improving F&D, and roughly $1B of 2026 adjusted free cash flow plus added volumes take corporate leverage back toward 1.0x within 12–18 months as promised. San Mateo's post-Cardinal >1 Bcf/d platform converts third-party capture from a claim into contracted volumes toward the $110M 2028 EBITDA target. The Woodford delineates. On 8x $8.78 of 2027 consensus, the stock is $70, +49%.

**Bear case ($35).** The Waha differential persists through 2027 as Delaware gas growth absorbs new takeaway, Hugh Brinson slips, and Matador pays for capacity it cannot fill economically. Crude retreats toward the $60s and the $52.75 floor is not yet protective, revenue falls, and the trailing SEC deck drives a full-cost impairment that resets DD&A higher. Paloma closes on revolver borrowings at 6%+ into a higher-for-longer curve, pushing recourse leverage higher, and a San Mateo distribution block above 4.50x removes a funding source Matador is actively using. On 4x $8.78 or 5x a reduced number, the stock revisits the $37.14 52-week low area — $35, -26%.

**Base case ($57).** Gas basis improves partially and late, collar settlements consume real cash through year-end 2026, Paloma closes and is modestly accretive, and 2027 consensus proves roughly right. 6.5x $8.78 = $57, +21%, with a 3.2% dividend as carry. The base case sits between the bear and bull scenarios and below the $68.63 sell-side mean.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-oriented).** The 12-month case is appreciation toward the mid-to-high $50s on dated catalysts, with the dividend paying while the basis problem resolves. Existing holders should not sell into a 30% drawdown that reflects hedge structure and M&A funding rather than operating deterioration; new capital should wait for either a lower price or verification on Hugh Brinson and San Mateo.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. **Hugh Brinson confirmed in service with a realized gas price above $2.00/Mcf** in a reported quarter (Q4 2026 or Q1 2027 results). This is the single highest-value data point; Q1 2026's $0.64/Mcf sets the bar low.
2. **San Mateo leverage resolved** — the Q2 or Q3 2026 10-Q disclosing San Mateo debt/EBITDA below 4.50x (or a covenant amendment) plus a refinancing path for the $650M July 2027 term loan, with distributions to Matador continuing.
3. **Price toward $40–42**, which would put the stock at roughly 4.6–4.8x 2027 consensus with the insider purchase range 25%+ overhead, or a resumption of buybacks against the $343M remaining authorization at those levels.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. **A Waha differential wider than ($7.00)/MMBtu sustained into 2027** or a Hugh Brinson in-service slip past Q1 2027 disclosed by either party.
2. **A San Mateo distribution block** or an announced equity raise / asset sale to fund the Paloma closing, which would signal the RBL-plus-cash funding plan failed.
3. **A full-cost ceiling-test impairment**, or FY2027 consensus falling below $7.00 (from $8.78), which would break the multiple support that underpins the base case.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 report (late October/early November) for Hugh Brinson startup commentary and the first quarter of San Mateo post-Cardinal financials; the Paloma closing in Q4 2026; and the FY2026 10-K in late February 2027 for the ceiling test and the year-end hedge book.

## Conclusion

Matador's operating business is performing: four consecutive earnings beats of increasing magnitude, Q2 revenue +32.5%, raised production guidance, oil volumes +5%, recourse leverage at 1.16x with 10x interest coverage, and a 20% dividend increase. The equity is priced at 6.8x this year and 5.4x next, below the lowest published sell-side target, with a C-suite-and-board insider buying cluster struck 8–16% above spot.

What holds the score at 5.9 is that the bears have specific, quantified arguments rather than a sentiment complaint. Realized gas at $0.64/Mcf against a widening basis is a filing fact. Collars capping 56% of oil volumes at $66.36 while WTI last marked at $95.08 is a filing fact with a large cash consequence. $2.0B of announced cash acquisitions — one of them at 1.56x PV-10, another with undisclosed consideration, all three from a single sponsor family — funded with revolver borrowings while the company's own shares trade at 5.4x forward is a capital-allocation decision the market repriced 16% lower in four sessions and then declined to reverse on a 22.5% earnings beat. The San Mateo arithmetic needs resolving from a document I have not seen. Near-term consensus has been cut 19% in a month.

The score improves marginally from 5.7 in July because the price is 13% lower on operating fundamentals that are better, not worse, and because the out-year estimate rose 5.9% over 90 days. It does not improve enough to add, given Energy's persistent negative selection record and a risk stack that includes a basis differential moving the wrong way, a third-party pipeline schedule, a non-recourse subsidiary near its covenant with a 2027 bullet, and a hedge book that removes the one upside vector the macro environment is currently offering. Hold what you own; the checkable catalysts arrive between October and February.
