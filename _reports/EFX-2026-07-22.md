**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Equifax is one of the three dominant U.S. consumer credit bureaus, but its most valuable asset is Workforce Solutions, the segment built around The Work Number income/employment-verification database (~209 million active records, ~813 million total, fed by 4M+ employer contributors). That segment runs ~44% operating margins against a company-wide ~18%, and it is the reason EFX trades at a structural premium to the credit-bureau field. The near-term story is dominated by U.S. mortgage cyclicality: management guides 2026 U.S. mortgage activity slightly below 2025, and recent growth has leaned on price pass-through (tri-merge and FICO score royalties marked up through EFX's P&L) rather than volume recovery.

The stock has fallen roughly 33% from its September 2025 high of $271.84 to ~$173, with the sharpest leg the ~12% single-day drop after the July 21, 2026 Q2 print. The reaction reflects guidance rather than a fundamentals collapse: H1 2026 revenue accelerated to +12%, USIS Online Information Solutions grew +19% in Q2, and International margins expanded. The market repriced 2026 mortgage-volume assumptions and a Q3 guide roughly 1% below consensus. The drawdown has compressed the forward multiple from a premium ~30x to ~20x on FY26 consensus EPS of $8.59, and a free-cash-flow tailwind is building as cloud capex declines (from $601M in 2023 to $481M in 2025) while operating cash flow rose to $1.62B.

Against that improving risk/reward, three things keep this a HOLD rather than a BUY. First, estimate revisions are broadly negative across every window (current-quarter −1.7%, next-quarter −2.8% over 30 days), the opposite of the signal that would justify an upgrade. Second, litigation and regulatory pressure is concentrated on the Workforce Solutions segment — an antitrust class action and three CFPB Civil Investigative Demands on data accuracy — which is the most underappreciated structural risk in the story. Third, insider activity is plan-based selling with no open-market buying, offering no conviction tailwind. The valuation has become more interesting; the catalyst timing has not.

## Company Overview

Equifax operates three segments:

- **Workforce Solutions (WFS)** — ~41-43% of revenue and the highest-margin business (~44% operating margin FY2025). Built around The Work Number, an income/employment verification database employers contribute to (rather than being paid for), making the dataset proprietary and difficult to replicate. Splits into Verification Services (the growth engine) and Employer Services (a more cyclical BPO drag covering unemployment claims, I-9, ACA, tax credits).
- **U.S. Information Solutions (USIS)** — ~34-36% of revenue, ~23% operating margin. Traditional credit reporting/scoring, fraud/identity, mortgage tri-merge reports, and Financial Marketing Services.
- **International** — ~23% of revenue, ~13% operating margin, FX-exposed across Latin America, Europe, Asia Pacific, and Canada. Includes Boa Vista (Brazil, 2023) and the pending Círculo de Crédito (Mexico) deal.

Customer concentration is low (largest client ~3% of revenue), but end-market concentration is high through U.S. mortgage, which flows through both WFS and USIS. The multi-year cloud transformation ("single data fabric") is now largely complete, shifting the narrative from build-out to monetization and AI-enabled product velocity.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is accelerating, not decelerating.** Annual revenue moved from $5.12B (2022) to $5.27B (2023, +3%) to $5.68B (2024, +8%) to $6.07B (2025, +7%). Q2 2026 revenue was $1.70B (+11% reported, +10% constant currency), and H1 2026 reached $3.35B (+12%), driven by USIS Online Information Solutions (+19% in Q2 on mortgage pricing and volume) and WFS Verification (+7%). This top-line acceleration sits against a 33% share-price drawdown and is the crux of the valuation debate.

**Margins are under modest pressure.** FY2025 operating income was $1,095M (18.0% margin, down 0.3pt YoY); net income $660M; diluted EPS $5.32 (+10%). Segment margins: WFS 44.2%, USIS 22.9%, International 12.9%. Q2 2026 consolidated operating margin fell to 18.5% from 20.2%, partly from a one-time litigation charge (net $40M on the FCRA coding-issue settlement). Two structural pressures persist: rising royalty/revenue-share costs (notably FICO score-price increases passing through) and elevated amortization of previously capitalized cloud software.

**The free-cash-flow inflection is real.** Operating cash flow grew from $1.12B (2023) to $1.32B (2024) to $1.62B (2025). Capex declined from $601M (2023) to $511M (2024) to $481M (2025) as the cloud build winds down. The current amortization drag on margins is a non-cash headwind tied to that prior capex, and it should peak and roll off, mechanically supporting future reported margins even without revenue leverage. Management's stated $150M AI productivity-savings target through 2028 layers on top of this.

**Balance sheet is investment-grade but leveraging into a downturn.** Total debt was ~$5.1B at FY2025, rising to ~$5.49B by Q2 2026 as commercial-paper borrowings funded buybacks. Net debt/EBITDA remains well within the 3.75x covenant. Goodwill ($6.75B) plus intangibles ($1.33B) equal ~68% of assets, reflecting an acquisition-heavy strategy, and management flags the Asia Pacific reporting unit as at risk of a future goodwill impairment (cushion just over 10%). The reported Altman Z of 223 and D/E of 0.09 in ancillary data are scaling artifacts and should be disregarded; the real picture is roughly 1.1x debt/equity, not distress.

**Capital return pivot.** After years of no buybacks (cash conserved for the cloud build and 2017-breach costs), the Board authorized $3B in April 2025 and raised the dividend twice within a year (to $0.56/quarter as of Feb 2026). EFX repurchased $927M in 2025 and $560M in H1 2026, reducing diluted share count from ~125M to ~119M. Simultaneously funding a $750M acquisition, expanding credit lines to $2.0B, and buying back stock into a mortgage downturn is a confident posture that also raises financial risk if the mortgage trough deepens.

The earnings-surprise record supports estimate reliability: four consecutive beats (Q1 2026 by 8.3%, Q3 2025 by 4.5%, and two narrower beats). Delivery is consistent; the problem is the forward guide, not execution against it.

## Growth Analysis

The underlying growth engine is intact and, in the near term, accelerating. H1 2026 revenue at +12% is a step up from the ~7% annual cadence of 2024-2025, and the mix is favorable: USIS mortgage grew +19-21% and International margins expanded. Longer-term drivers are structural: the Work Number's expanding record count and lender penetration, the shift from cloud build to product velocity, and the AI/agentic capability the single-data-fabric enables.

The offsetting reality is that a meaningful chunk of recent mortgage-linked growth is price, not volume, with management guiding 2026 U.S. mortgage activity slightly below 2025. With refi dormant, the volume kicker that would turn cyclical strength into durable acceleration is absent. When rates eventually ease and volume returns, EFX has substantial operating leverage in both WFS Verification and USIS. That optionality is real but undated.

The estimate-revision picture is the clearest reason not to lean harder on growth. Consensus is falling across every window: current quarter −1.7% (30d and 90d), next quarter −2.8% (30d), current year −0.2%, next year −0.9%. This is the inverse of the revision velocity that would flag a true reacceleration. Current-year EPS of $8.59 still implies healthy growth off the $5.32 FY2025 base (the difference partly reflecting adjusted vs. GAAP framing), and next-year $10.18 continues the trajectory, but the near-term momentum is negative.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$173, EFX trades at roughly 20x FY26 consensus EPS of $8.59 and ~17x FY27 EPS of $10.18. That is a substantial de-rating from the ~30x trailing multiple and the ~30x forward the stock carried near its highs. Against peers in Research & Consulting Services, the picture is mixed: the peer median P/E is 18.4 (VRSK 27.8, TRU 18.3, BAH 11.2, FCN 18.4, EXPO 26.5). EFX at ~20x forward sits between TransUnion (its closest bureau comp) and Verisk (the closest analytics-quality comp), which is defensible given EFX's superior Work Number margin profile and reacceleration.

The relevant comparison is TransUnion at 18.3x; EFX's modest premium is justified by the WFS moat and higher-margin mix. VRSK at 27.8x shows the multiple a pure, low-cyclicality data-analytics franchise can command; EFX will not reach that until mortgage cyclicality is de-risked and the FCF inflection is visible in reported numbers. The de-rating has removed the clear overvaluation that existed near $270 without creating a compelling mispricing, because the estimate trend is negative and the near-term catalyst (mortgage-volume recovery) is undated.

Analyst targets bracket the current price: low $179, mean $211, median $210, high $245. The current price sits below even the low target, which would ordinarily be a bullish signal. But the guidance-driven cuts to consensus mean those targets are being revised down, so treating them as firm floors would be a mistake. The FCF inflection argument is the strongest bull input for valuation: as capex normalizes below $500M against ~$1.6B+ operating cash flow, FCF yield on a ~$20B market cap becomes attractive, and the software-amortization drag should ease. That case earns a moderate valuation score — better than the market's implied pessimism, but short of a high-conviction mispricing given falling estimates and the concentration of legal risk on the highest-multiple segment.

## Competitive Landscape

Equifax's competitive position is strong but faces a specific, rising challenge. The Work Number is the dominant income/employment verification asset, and its contribution model (employers give data to offload verification burden) creates a self-reinforcing dataset that is hard to replicate. In tri-merge mortgage credit, EFX cooperates with Experian and TransUnion on the report while competing on price, and all three are squeezed by FICO's score-royalty increases, which flow through as rising cost of services.

The threat is twofold and worsening. First, verification is described in filings as "highly competitive with low barriers to entry": in-house employer verification, direct-from-employer methods, and paystub-based verification are viable substitutes, and AI lowers the cost of alternative approaches. Free/low-cost data substitution is explicitly flagged as a risk. Second, the antitrust class action against Workforce Solutions (filed May 2024, alleging monopolistic conduct in income/employment verification) is a direct legal challenge to the moat, and three CFPB CIDs probe data accuracy and dispute handling at the same segment.

On balance the trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving on the business side (accelerating revenue, expanding International margins, cloud-enabled product velocity) but with a widening tail from the regulatory/legal front targeting the highest-margin segment. That nets to a competitive-position score modestly above neutral.

## Risk Assessment

**Mortgage cyclicality** is the dominant near-term swing factor and the direct cause of the post-Q2 selloff. Recent growth is price-led; if rates stay elevated and refi stays dormant, volume remains a drag through 2026.

**Data-security / breach recurrence** remains a live governance anchor from the 2017 breach. EFX operates under the Consumer Settlement (effective Jan 2022) with ongoing business-practice commitments and audits. A second material breach would be severely damaging.

**Regulatory and litigation intensity, concentrated on Workforce Solutions,** is the most underappreciated structural risk. The antitrust class action plus three CFPB CIDs strike at the highest-margin, highest-growth segment. Recent settlements — $100M (net $40M after insurance) on the FCRA coding issue in Q2 2026, and $30M on inquiry-disputes class actions in Q4 2025 — are individually manageable but confirm a persistent, FCRA-fee-shifting-driven litigation pattern.

**Balance-sheet soft spots:** Asia Pacific goodwill flagged as at risk of impairment (cushion just over 10%), and FX translation on 23% international revenue (including hyperinflationary Argentina). The pending $750M Círculo de Crédito acquisition (expected Q4 2026 close, subject to regulatory approval) adds integration and goodwill risk while leverage rises into a downturn.

**AI disintermediation** of verification demand is explicitly disclosed and, over a multi-year horizon, the most consequential structural threat to WFS economics.

Given the specific, dated legal exposure targeting the highest-margin segment plus the mortgage-volume uncertainty, risk factors score below neutral; these are live, quantifiable exposures rather than fully priced tail risks.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV at 44.1% sits above 1-year historical vol of 38.8% (rich by ~5.3 points, 70th percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (44.1% near-term vs. 40.9% at six months), which typically signals the market expects an imminent catalyst. Put/call skew is roughly balanced at −2.3%, and the put/call OI ratio rose from 0.55 to 0.85 since early June, indicating a modest tilt toward downside hedging. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±9.9%. For a prospective buyer, rich near-term IV argues against paying up for calls now and favors waiting for the vol to compress or scaling in on weakness. Short interest is modest at 6.3% of float (3.4 days to cover), so this is not a squeeze setup in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($210-245):** The mortgage trough is cyclical, not structural. When rates ease and refi volume returns, EFX's operating leverage in WFS Verification and USIS re-rates earnings sharply higher. Meanwhile the cloud-capex roll-off drives a free-cash-flow inflection, the software-amortization margin drag eases, and the $150M AI-savings target adds a margin lever. The +12% H1 revenue acceleration shows the engine is running, and the market is over-anchoring on one soft guide. At ~20x forward on rising normalized earnings, the stock re-rates toward the VRSK-style multiple its WFS margin profile deserves, reaching the $210 median and $245 high analyst targets.

**Bear Case ($130-150):** Mortgage volume stays depressed longer than expected, price pass-through decelerates as FICO royalty increases exhaust customer tolerance, and the Workforce Solutions antitrust/CFPB overhang produces either a larger settlement or a business-practice remedy that compresses the segment's margin. The Asia Pacific goodwill impairment lands as a headline negative, the Círculo integration stumbles, and leverage taken into a downturn amplifies EPS downside. Estimate revisions continue falling, the multiple compresses toward TransUnion's 18x on lower earnings, and the stock drifts into the low $150s or below (roughly −13% to −25% from current).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive — a wide-moat franchise with accelerating revenue and a genuine FCF inflection, now at a de-rated ~20x forward — but the near-term catalyst (mortgage-volume recovery) is undated, estimates are still falling, and the legal overhang on WFS is unresolved. Hold and accumulate on further weakness rather than chase at current levels.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions turn positive for two consecutive quarters (current-quarter and next-year EPS revised up rather than the current −1.7% / −0.9%).
- Evidence of mortgage-volume (not price) recovery in USIS Online Information Solutions, or a pullback toward the low-$150s where FCF-yield support becomes compelling.
- Favorable resolution or clear bounding of the Workforce Solutions antitrust case and CFPB CIDs.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material adverse ruling or large settlement in the WFS antitrust/CFPB matters that threatens the segment's margin structure.
- Consolidated operating margin sustained below ~17% ex-one-timers, signaling the amortization/royalty drag is structural rather than transitional.
- Asia Pacific goodwill impairment coupled with a second consecutive quarter of guidance cuts, or a Círculo integration that materially raises leverage without accretion.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 2026 earnings release (October), which will test whether the mortgage guide was conservative and whether estimate revisions stabilize. The Círculo close (expected Q4 2026) and any development in the CFPB investigation are the other key monitorables.

## Conclusion

Equifax is a high-quality data-analytics franchise trading at a materially better price than three months ago, with an accelerating top line and an approaching free-cash-flow inflection that the market is underweighting amid a mortgage-driven guidance scare. The drawdown from the highs has removed the clear overvaluation without producing a high-conviction mispricing, because consensus estimates are still drifting lower and the most valuable segment faces active antitrust and CFPB scrutiny. The score has been gently declining (6.1 → 6.1 → 5.6), and the current read confirms that pattern: valuation has improved as the price fell, but management-signal quality (plan-based selling, no open-market buying) and near-term growth momentum have softened.

This is a growth-oriented HOLD — a franchise worth owning through the cycle and adding to on further weakness toward the low-$150s or on a positive estimate-revision turn, but not a name that clears the bar for a fresh buy while the mortgage trough, the WFS legal overhang, and falling estimates remain unresolved. Base-case fair value sits near the $195-210 range on normalized earnings over a 12-18 month horizon, with the path there dependent on mortgage-volume stabilization and evidence that the margin drag is rolling off as capex normalizes.
