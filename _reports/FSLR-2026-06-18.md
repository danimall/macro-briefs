**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

First Solar is the largest U.S. solar module manufacturer and the only domestically headquartered firm among the world's largest, built on proprietary cadmium telluride (CdTe) thin-film technology that uses no Chinese polysilicon. That supply-chain distinction is the reason the company earns the margins it does: in a U.S. policy environment of FEOC eligibility restrictions and import tariffs, First Solar's non-Chinese product commands stable domestic pricing while global crystalline-silicon prices sit at or below cost. The balance sheet holds roughly $2.0B net cash, an undrawn $1.5B unsecured revolver, and a 47.9 GW / $14.4B contracted backlog recognizable through 2030. Q1 2026 showed 46.6% gross margins, $3.22 diluted EPS, and net sales up 23.6% YoY.

The complication is that this reported profitability is largely a policy annuity. FY2025 Section 45X tax credits of roughly $1.56–1.59B are approximately equal to FY2025 net income of $1.53B; a third-party bear analysis estimates 45X comprised 76–86% of gross margin. The credit phases down beginning 2030 and expires 2032. The bull case (GLJ's $315 target) is that OBBBA's FEOC rules, Section 232 tariffs, and a favorable TOPCon ITC ruling entrench the subsidy and the moat through 2029. The bear case is that the market is paying a quality multiple for a credit with a known expiration date, with current-year estimates already drifting lower (-4.1% over 90 days) as the OBBBA-accelerated ITC/PTC termination pressures end-customer demand.

We rate FSLR a HOLD. The stock trades at ~17.9x trailing EPS and ~14.7x FY2026 consensus of $17.39, which is not demanding on reported numbers, but the durability of those numbers is the open question. After a 77% run off the June 2025 low and a ~21% pullback from the $320.95 high, the risk/reward is balanced. Two binary events (the BP/Lightsource litigation, expected after August 2026, and the TOPCon ITC general-exclusion-order decision) and the unresolved post-2029 subsidy picture keep us from a directional call in either direction.

## Company Overview

First Solar operates as a single segment: design, manufacture, and sale of CdTe thin-film PV modules. It exited project development/EPC years ago. The manufacturing footprint spans five operating U.S. plants (a sixth coming online H2 2026), plus India, Malaysia, and Vietnam. CdTe modules use only ~2–3% of the semiconductor material of c-Si modules and rely on no Chinese polysilicon, which is the basis of the moat in a protectionist U.S. regime.

The contracted backlog of 47.9 GW / $14.4B carries up to ~$0.6B of potential technology-adjuster upside. It declined from 53.7 GW / $16.4B at September 30, 2025, driven by the BP Solar / Lightsource master-supply-agreement termination discussed below. The technology roadmap includes CuRe (copper replacement; one Ohio facility permanently converted in Q1 2026), bifacial modules, and a longer-term perovskite R&D program.

The company classifies as a "Semiconductor" name, and is the only U.S.-headquartered company among the world's largest solar manufacturers. The sector mapping is imperfect: FSLR's economics are driven by U.S. energy policy and utility-scale solar demand, not by the AI-infrastructure semiconductor cycle that drives the peer group it screens against (MTSI, AMKR, LSCC, RMBS). The peer P/E medians (160x) are not a meaningful comparison set.

## Financial Analysis

The financial profile is strong on reported numbers. FY2025 net income was $1.53B, up from $1.29B in FY2024, $831M in FY2023, and a -$44M loss in FY2022. The turnaround tracks the 45X credit ramp almost exactly. FY2025 net margin ran ~29–30%; operating margin ~30.6%.

Q1 2026 delivered net sales of $1.044B (+23.6% YoY), gross margin of 46.6% (up from 40.8%), operating income of $345M (33.1% margin), net income of $347M, and diluted EPS of $3.22 versus $1.95 prior year. Volume sold was 3.8 GW against 4.3 GW produced. Q3 2025 was a record: $1.6B net sales, 5.3 GW sold, $4.24 diluted EPS.

The balance sheet is the clearest strength. As of March 31, 2026: cash plus marketable securities of $2.43B against total debt of only $426M, leaving roughly $2.0B net cash. Total equity was $9.88B on $13.35B total assets. The February 2026 replacement of the prior $1.0B secured facility with a $1.5B unsecured five-year revolver (fully undrawn) signals an improving credit profile. Government grants receivable totaled $823M ($285M current, $538M non-current).

Two cash-flow nuances matter for quality assessment. First, operating cash flow is lumpy and seasonally negative in Q1 (-$215M in Q1 2026, -$608M in Q1 2025) on working-capital and grants-receivable build; this normalizes over the year (9M 2025 OCF was +$815M). Second, the company monetizes 45X credits by selling them to third parties at a small discount — $699.7M of 2025 credits sold for $668.2M cash — a recurring, material liquidity source. The mechanics underscore the dependence: in Q1 2026 alone, 45X reduced cost of sales by $117.9M, and income-related government grants in COGS were $418M versus $302M prior year. Strip the subsidy and reported margins compress dramatically.

2026 capex is guided to $0.8–1.0B (sixth U.S. facility ~$0.3B plus R&D and upgrades), self-funded. No dividend; no notable buyback.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is genuinely two-sided, which is why this dimension scores in the middle. On the positive side, AI/data-center electricity demand is a structural tailwind for utility-scale solar, which deploys faster than gas (~5-year lead time) or nuclear. The $14.4B backlog through 2030 provides revenue visibility few manufacturers can match. India's ALMM domestic-content rules increasingly favor First Solar's approved cell and wafer-content status.

On the negative side, the OBBBA (signed July 4, 2025) accelerated termination of the clean-electricity ITC/PTC for solar, which pressures end-customer project economics and forward demand. That headwind is visible in the estimate revisions: current-quarter consensus has been cut 33.7% over 90 days (from $4.26 to $2.82) and current-year estimates are down 4.1% (from $18.13 to $17.39). The sharply negative near-term-quarter trend is the opposite of the signal that would support a more aggressive call. Next-quarter (+5.2%) and next-year (-3.0%) revisions are mildly positive to slightly negative, so the picture is stabilizing rather than deteriorating outright, but it is not accelerating.

The backlog declined ~11% (53.7 GW → 47.9 GW) on the BP/Lightsource termination, a reminder that even contracted volume is not guaranteed. The earnings track record is mixed — two beats and two misses in the last four quarters (Q4 2025 missed by 7.9%, Q3 2025 by 2.3%, with beats in Q1 2026 and Q2 2025), so forward estimates carry only moderate confidence.

The durable growth question is what unsubsidized volumes and margins look like as 45X phases down from 2030. Until that visibility improves, we treat the headline backlog as a strong near-term anchor and a weaker long-term one.

## Valuation Assessment

At $254.80, FSLR trades at ~17.9x trailing EPS of $14.21 and ~14.7x FY2026 consensus of $17.39. On reported earnings, that is undemanding for a 30%-margin business with net cash. The complication is that those earnings are substantially a function of a credit that expires in 2032 and phases down from 2030. Adjusting for the subsidy, the unsubsidized multiple is materially higher, and the bear thesis ("priced as if subsidy never ends") rests precisely on this point.

The peer comp set (Semiconductors: MTSI 160x, LSCC 1012x, RMBS 68x, median 160x) is not informative — FSLR is a policy-driven manufacturer, not an AI-cycle chip company. The more relevant anchors are the analyst targets: consensus mean $244.70, median $243.00, low $150, high $330. The current price sits above the mean and median targets, with the high ($330) and low ($150, the subsidy-cliff case) bracketing an unusually wide $180 spread. That dispersion is the analytical signal: the Street cannot agree on the durability of the earnings base, and the stock trading above consensus mean leaves limited margin of safety on the most-likely case.

We deliberately do not credit "cheapness" here. A 14.7x forward multiple looks low, but a compressed multiple on subsidy-dependent earnings is the textbook value-trap profile when the catalyst (45X durability) is unresolved and the near-term revision trend is negative. The stock has appreciated 77% in twelve months on fundamental improvement, which we do not hold against it, but we also cannot identify a specific reason the market is mispricing the shares today. The valuation is balanced, not asymmetric.

## Competitive Landscape

First Solar's competitive position is structurally advantaged within the U.S. and India and structurally challenged everywhere else. U.S. module pricing has remained stable despite global declines, a direct function of IRA demand pull, FEOC eligibility restrictions, and import tariffs favoring domestic CdTe product. Globally, chronic c-Si overcapacity (especially China) keeps European prices at or below cost, and First Solar has deliberately concentrated where policy creates a level field.

The most underappreciated competitive lever is the IP offensive. First Solar has moved from defender to aggressor with a sweeping patent-enforcement campaign against c-Si TOPCon competitors, including a USITC Section 337 action (Inv. No. 337-TA-1494, instituted March 25, 2026) seeking a general exclusion order against JinkoSolar, Canadian Solar, JA Solar, Trina, Hanwha QCells, Adani/Mundra, and others, with Tesla and BYD America intervening as respondents. The patents have already survived multiple PTAB IPR challenges (Jinko, Mundra, Canadian Solar petitions declined). A favorable ITC outcome would impair the U.S. competitiveness of essentially every major Chinese c-Si manufacturer and would be a genuine catalyst, but it is binary and unresolved.

The offsetting concern is that TOPCon represents the high-efficiency c-Si frontier, and First Solar competes on energy yield, domestic content, supply-chain security, and price certainty rather than raw nameplate efficiency. The international footprint (India, Malaysia, Vietnam) is increasingly stranded by trade policy: the European market is "captured by Chinese modules," and management explicitly states it is "reasonably possible" these plants are reduced, paused, or ceased, which would trigger material PP&E impairments. The sixth U.S. plant is being built specifically to onshore final production from the international fleet, implicitly hedging this risk.

The trajectory has softened from prior reports: competitive position scored 8.0 in February and we mark it 6.5 now. That reflects the backlog decline, the OBBBA demand headwind, and the latent offshore impairment risk, partially offset by the ITC optionality.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is policy/subsidy dependence, and the filings make it unusually explicit. The 45X credit is the single most important variable for valuation, and it is not resolvable near-term.

Additional risks of note:
- **OBBBA/FEOC double-edge:** OBBBA accelerated ITC/PTC termination (demand-negative) while restricting 45X eligibility for FEOC-assisted products (supply-chain-positive for FSLR). Net direction is ambiguous and policy-dependent.
- **Tariff whiplash:** The February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA tariffs are unlawful was immediately replaced by Section 122 tariffs (≤15%, ≤150 days); Section 232 tariffs on steel/aluminum/copper now at 50% raise input costs. The stock fell sharply on February 25, 2026 (from ~$243 to ~$210).
- **International impairment risk:** Explicit "reasonably possible…reduce, pause, or cease" language on offshore plants.
- **BP/Lightsource litigation:** The September 30, 2025 termination triggered $384.6M in termination payments ($61M recognized, $323.6M litigated in NY Supreme Court; counterclaim of $175M + $15M). Decision not expected before August 2026.
- **Series 7 warranty:** $35–70M estimated remediation; $47M liability booked.
- **Tellurium supply:** China tightened export controls on this core CdTe input in February 2025; the U.S. is building a ~$12B critical-minerals stockpile in response.

These are largely company-specific and several are binary, which is why we score risk factors low. The balance sheet provides a wide margin of safety against any single shock, but the cluster of unpriced binary outcomes is material.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 149.5% against 1-year historical volatility of 55.3% — a 94.3-point rich spread, in the 89th IV percentile, with the term structure in steep backwardation (149.5% 1-week versus 78.8% 3-month). The market is pricing imminent stress around a near-dated event. ATM IV jumped 83 points (66.5% → 149.5%) over the past month even as the price rose 10.7%, indicating the market sees rising uncertainty rather than complacency. Skew is mildly call-heavy (-5.5%) and the put/call OI ratio is low at 0.44, so positioning is not directionally bearish; the signal is volatility, not direction. For a fundamental holder, the takeaway is that an imminent catalyst is being priced, and we would not add into this regardless of view.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($315–330)
OBBBA's FEOC restrictions plus Section 232 tariffs entrench First Solar's domestic-pricing advantage through 2029, holding U.S. module prices stable while Chinese competitors are squeezed. A favorable TOPCon ITC general exclusion order blocks infringing c-Si imports, expanding First Solar's addressable U.S. share. The 47.9 GW backlog converts at premium margins, AI/data-center demand sustains utility-scale solar deployment, and the market re-rates the durable-moat case. The balance sheet self-funds the CuRe and capacity roadmap. GLJ's $315 target reflects this entrenchment thesis.

### Bear Case ($150)
The market is paying a quality multiple for a policy annuity. 45X comprises 76–86% of gross margin and phases down from 2030; OBBBA's accelerated ITC/PTC termination erodes end-customer demand (visible in the -33.7% current-quarter estimate cut). The BP/Lightsource breach shows even blue-chip backlog can default. Offshore plants get stranded and impaired. Unsubsidized economics prove far thinner than reported, and the multiple compresses toward the low analyst target as the 2029 cliff comes into pricing range.

The two cases are roughly symmetric from the current $254.80 (+24% to bull, -41% to bear), and the resolving variables — 45X durability, the ITC ruling, the BP litigation — are largely binary and not near-term. That symmetry, combined with negative near-term revisions and a stock trading above consensus mean, is the basis for the HOLD.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is an `uncertain` HOLD: we lack sufficient evidence for a confident directional call, and the key variables resolve as binary events rather than gradual trends.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A favorable USITC general exclusion order in the TOPCon case (Inv. No. 337-TA-1494), which would materially improve U.S. competitive positioning.
- Concrete legislative or regulatory clarity extending or stabilizing 45X / FEOC economics beyond 2029, reducing the subsidy-cliff discount.
- A pullback toward the high-$190s/low-$200s (the early-2026 trading range) on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore asymmetric upside, OR a return to positive current-year estimate revisions alongside backlog re-growth above 50 GW.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse 45X/OBBBA development that accelerates the subsidy phase-down or tightens FEOC in a way that hurts FSLR.
- Material PP&E impairment on the international fleet confirming the "reduce, pause, or cease" risk.
- A loss in the BP/Lightsource litigation combined with further backlog erosion, or a guidance cut materially below the $17.39 FY2026 consensus.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release, the BP/Lightsource ruling (expected ≥August 2026), and developments in the ITC case. We would revisit the rating on any of these.

## Conclusion

First Solar is a high-quality, net-cash, U.S.-policy-advantaged manufacturer with exceptional reported margins, a $14.4B backlog, and genuine catalyst optionality in the TOPCon IP campaign. It is also a business whose GAAP profitability is overwhelmingly a function of Section 45X tax credits that phase down beginning 2030, with end-customer demand under pressure from OBBBA's accelerated ITC/PTC termination and near-term estimates drifting lower. Our own assessment has growth and competitive position softening while valuation looks more reasonable on price: the stock is cheaper on reported numbers, but the durability of those numbers is exactly what is in doubt.

After a 77% twelve-month run and a ~21% pullback from the high, with options markets pricing imminent stress, the risk/reward is balanced, and the resolving variables are binary and not near-term. We maintain HOLD, neither chasing the entrenchment narrative at above-consensus prices nor shorting a debt-free company with $2.0B of net cash and real ITC optionality. We would become more constructive on a favorable ITC ruling, durable 45X clarity, or a pullback to the high-$190s.
