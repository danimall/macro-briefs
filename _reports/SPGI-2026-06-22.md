**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

S&P Global has fallen roughly 30% from its August 2025 high of $579 to ~$403, a drawdown disproportionate to anything in its trailing fundamentals. Revenue grew 10% YoY in Q1 2026 across all five segments, free cash flow rose 13% to $919M, and diluted EPS climbed 32% to $4.69. The selling is driven by a forward-looking debate: whether generative AI commoditizes the data and analytics half of the business (Market Intelligence, Mobility) and erodes the moat the market has paid a premium for over a decade. At a >30% drawdown the burden of proof sits with the bull case, and the specific thing the sellers may be missing is that the two halves of SPGI carry very different AI exposure: the protected ratings franchise (68% margin, NRSRO-entrenched) and the index franchise (72% margin, network-effect-locked) together generate the bulk of profit and are largely AI-resistant, while the genuinely exposed pieces are the ones management is divesting or spinning off.

The mid-2026 spin-off of Mobility ("Mobility Global Inc.") concentrates the remaining business on its highest-margin, widest-moat franchises and lifts pro-forma group margins by roughly 270bps. Combined with the EDM/thinkFolio sale (STG, $172M gain), the agreed sale of Energy's geoscience software to SLB, and the OSTTRA JV exit, the company is deliberately shedding lower-margin, more cyclical, more AI-exposed assets while acquiring AI capability (Enertel AI). The single most actionable signal in the filings is open-market insider buying near the bottom: CEO Martina Cheung (~$998K at $429.93), S&P DJI CEO Catherine Clay (~$1.08M at $431.39), and a director (~$500K at $434.03), all in late April/early May 2026 after the selloff. Three insiders deploying real cash simultaneously after a sharp decline contradicts the AI-fear narrative.

We rate SPGI a BUY at ~$403 with a 12-month base-case target of $510, against a bull case of $575 and a bear case of $360. The valuation has compressed to ~18.1x next-year consensus EPS of $22.23 and ~20.6x current-year EPS of $19.63, a discount to its own history and to data peers MCO (31.7x) and MSCI (34.0x) for a comparably-moated franchise. The thesis carries risk: the AI debate is real, estimate revisions have stalled (flat over 90 days), and the options market is pricing near-term stress. The risk/reward at this price, supported by insider conviction and a structurally improving post-spin business, is favorable.

## Company Overview

S&P Global is a diversified provider of benchmarks, credit ratings, data, analytics, and workflow solutions. It runs five reportable segments (Q1 2026 revenue mix and operating margins):

- **Ratings** — $1,302M, 31% of revenue, **68% operating margin**. The crown jewel. NRSRO-status credit ratings; ~55% transaction (new-issuance-linked), ~45% non-transaction (surveillance, Crisil, royalties). Highest-margin, widest-moat franchise.
- **Market Intelligence** — $1,296M, ~31% of revenue, 34% margin. Capital IQ Pro, RatingsXpress/RatingsDirect, Enterprise/Lending Solutions. ~81% subscription. Largest by assets (~$31B, mostly IHS Markit goodwill). The most AI-exposed segment.
- **Energy** — $652M, ~16%, 44% margin. Platts price assessments and benchmarks (the renamed Commodity Insights).
- **Indices (S&P Dow Jones Indices)** — $519M, ~12%, **72% margin** (52% net of CME's 27% NCI). S&P 500, Dow Jones; asset-linked fees on $5.4T of ETF AUM (+25% YoY).
- **Mobility** — $454M, ~11%, 21% margin. Automotive data. Being spun off mid-2026.

The moat rests on irreplaceable proprietary franchises (S&P 500, Platts), NRSRO regulatory status, deep datasets, high switching costs, and recurring revenue (subscription + non-transaction + asset-linked ≈ two-thirds of total). Remaining performance obligations of $5.7B provide forward visibility. The ratings business is an effective oligopoly with Moody's and Fitch; indices are a duopoly with MSCI/FTSE Russell; Platts dominates energy benchmarks. These are among the hardest-to-replicate franchises in financial services.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and profitability — strong and broad-based.** The multi-year trajectory is ~$11.2B (2022) → ~$15.3B (2025), with net income of $4.82B in 2025, up from $4.17B (2024) and $2.89B (2023). Q1 2026 reported operating profit of $2,002M (+27%) was flattered by a $175M disposition gain; ex-items operating profit grew roughly 12-14%. GAAP operating margin was 48% (42% ex-gain). The diluted EPS gain benefited partly from the disposition gain and partly from a lower share count.

**Cash flow and capital allocation — capital-light and shareholder-friendly.** Q1 2026 free cash flow ran on minimal capex (~$27M/quarter). The company repurchased $1.0B of stock in Q1 via an ASR at $426.70 average, above the current price but consistent with management's view of fair value. A new 30M-share (~10% of float) authorization was approved in November 2025, with 29.6M shares remaining. Share count fell from 307.3M to 297.3M YoY. The dividend was raised to $0.97/quarter, extending a Dividend Aristocrat record; Q1 dividends totaled $288M and are comfortably FCF-covered.

**Balance sheet — investment-grade, lightly screened-mislabeled.** Total debt is $13.3B (long-term $10.6B), cash $1.8B, with fixed-rate notes laddered to 2062 at mostly low coupons. The sole financial covenant — debt/cash-flow ≤ 4:1 — has "never been exceeded." Equity is $31.3B, but assets are dominated by $36.4B goodwill + $16.0B intangibles ($52.4B, ~86% of total assets), the legacy of the 2022 IHS Markit merger. Traditional distress screens (such as the Altman Z-Score) flag SPGI as a measurement artifact: the low score reflects the goodwill/intangible base and the NCI structure, not leverage stress. With high FCF conversion, near-zero capex intensity, and investment-grade ratings, distress screens are not informative here.

One structural feature deserves flagging: a $4.92B **redeemable non-controlling interest**. CME Group owns 27% of S&P DJI and holds the right to put ≥20% of its stake to SPGI at fair value at any time (and fully on change-of-control). This caps full ownership of the best segment and represents a real, if remote, contingent cash claim.

**Earnings track record.** Over the last four quarters, SPGI posted three beats and one miss (Q4 2025 missed by 2.6%; Q1 2026 beat by 1.3%). The pattern supports moderate confidence in forward estimates without suggesting either conservative sandbagging or deteriorating visibility.

## Growth Analysis

The durable growth engine is the combination of asset-linked index fees, recurring MI subscriptions, and the secular tailwind of debt-market growth feeding the ratings franchise. AI shows up on both sides of the ratings ledger: investment-grade billed issuance rose 41% in Q1 2026, driven partly by AI-related corporate debt and M&A financing, while bank-loan ratings declined on AI-disruption concerns affecting software and tech-adjacent leveraged loans. AI is simultaneously a demand driver (data-center and AI-capex financing flowing through the ratings franchise) and a demand headwind (tech-credit caution), a nuance lost in the binary "AI kills SPGI" framing.

Consensus implies ~13% forward EPS growth. The estimate revision picture is the weakest part of the bull case: revisions are essentially flat (current-year -0.1% over 90 days, next-year +0.6%). This is steady, single-digit-to-low-teens organic growth with margin support, not a +30% acceleration. The Mobility spin is roughly top-line neutral but margin-accretive (~270bps), so the post-spin entity is a slightly slower-growing, materially higher-margin, more focused business. Growth prospects are solid and durable rather than accelerating, which anchors the score in the mid-6s.

## Valuation Assessment

Against the peer set, the median P/E is 26.1x. SPGI sits below the median and well below the most directly comparable franchises: Moody's at 31.7x (the closest analog, with the same protected-ratings-plus-AI-exposed-data structure) and MSCI at 34.0x (a pure index/data play). CME (22.4x) and ICE (20.2x) are exchange-leaning and less comparable.

The market is repricing SPGI's AI-exposed data segments downward while leaving the protected ratings/index franchises under-credited. If the "AI splits the ratings giants" thesis is correct that the protected franchise deserves a premium multiple and the data business a market multiple, the sum-of-the-parts on a post-spin SPGI — concentrated toward 68-72% margin franchises — supports a re-rating back toward the low-to-mid 20s on forward EPS. At 23x next-year EPS, the stock is worth ~$511; at the peer-median 26x, ~$578.

This is a quality-at-a-discount setup rather than a deep-value or cyclical one. The valuation is not a value trap: the multiple compression is sentiment-driven (AI fear) on a franchise whose forward earnings power is intact and rising, not structurally impaired. Analyst targets (low $480, mean $533, median $539, high $575) all sit above the current price, with every one of 24 covering analysts at hold-or-better and 23 of 24 bullish. On a stock at ~18.1x forward with positive (if modest) revisions and a specific re-rating catalyst (the spin), consensus targets are credible floors rather than ceilings. We score valuation a 7.0; the discount to history and to MCO/MSCI is real and identifiable, tempered by the AI overhang that justifies some of the derating.

## Competitive Landscape

SPGI's competitive position is improving in trajectory terms even as the market frets about the data segment. The ratings oligopoly (with Moody's and Fitch) is protected by NRSRO regulatory status and decades of issuer relationships — an AI model cannot self-certify as a nationally recognized rating organization. The index duopoly (with MSCI/FTSE Russell) is locked by network effects: the S&P 500 is the reference benchmark for the bulk of ETF AUM, and asset managers cannot unilaterally migrate to a competing benchmark without dislocating their products. Platts is the dominant energy price-assessment franchise. These three franchises generate the bulk of operating profit and are the most AI-resistant.

The portfolio reshaping concentrates capital on the franchises with the highest margins and deepest moats. By spinning Mobility (21% margin, cyclical auto exposure), selling EDM/thinkFolio and Energy software, and exiting OSTTRA, management narrows the business toward its strongest assets. The competitive threat is concentrated in Market Intelligence, where off-the-shelf AI models and clients building their own analytics on SPGI data could erode pricing power. The bull counter — that proprietary datasets, regulatory/compliance use-cases, and switching costs protect Capital IQ Pro, and that SPGI can monetize AI via data-licensing to model builders — is supported by the Q1 evidence that AI is a net demand driver for the larger ratings franchise.

The prior trajectory marked competitive position down from 9.0 to 8.0. We hold it at 7.5, a notch lower, reflecting the genuine and not-fully-resolved AI risk to the data segment, but well above neutral given the irreplaceable core franchises and the margin-accretive purification underway.

## Risk Assessment

**AI disruption — the central debate.** The FY2025 10-K materially expanded its AI risk factor across multiple bullets: competitors and off-the-shelf models could make data collection "free or relatively inexpensive" and reproduce SPGI content; clients could use SPGI data with their own AI to "supplant" SPGI products; and SPGI's own AI rollout requires heavy compute investment. The related disclosure — "increased availability of free or relatively inexpensive information sources may materially reduce demand" — is the core bear thesis and the reason for the derating. This risk is real and not fully priced in either direction; it is the primary reason the risk score sits at 5.5.

**Ratings cyclicality.** Transaction revenue depends on debt-issuance volumes. Q1 was strong, but the bank-loan-ratings decline on AI-driven tech-credit caution is an early, concrete sign that AI fears are already touching issuance.

**Ratings regulation.** Extensive oversight (Dodd-Frank, SEC NRSRO, EU/UK CRA regimes, ESMA, FCA), plus new ESG-ratings regulatory regimes (EU adopted Nov 2024; FCA supervision from June 2028).

**Mobility spin-off execution.** Multiple completion conditions; risk of delay, loss of tax-free status, or combined post-spin value below pre-spin. The board has approved the separation, formed an independent board (chaired by former CSX CEO Joseph Hinrichs), and Mobility Global has priced $2.0B of senior notes; execution appears well-advanced and on track for mid-2026.

**Legacy CDO litigation.** An Australian class action (filed Aug 2020) over pre-crisis (2005-07) CDO ratings remains unresolved with no assurance against a significant payment.

**Structural.** The CME put on 27% of S&P DJI ($4.92B redeemable NCI) is a contingent liquidity claim and a cap on full ownership of the best segment. AWS cloud concentration and data-supplier-as-competitor dynamics are secondary.

The prior trajectory marked risk down from 6.5 to 5.5, reflecting the expanded AI risk factor. We hold it at 5.5; the AI overhang is genuine and the litigation/regulatory/NCI items are real, but much of the AI fear is already in the price after a sharp drawdown.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV is 34.1% versus 1-year realized vol of 28.4% (rich by 5.7pp), the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 34.1% > long-term 28.3%), and put/call ratios are elevated (volume 1.42, open interest 1.81) with a 5.6% put skew on the front-week. This signals the market is hedging an imminent event, most likely the late-July earnings print and ongoing AI-narrative volatility. The 6-month implied move is ±14%. For a long position, the rich front-end IV argues against buying premium and favors patience on entry; the elevated put demand confirms bearish sentiment, consistent with our view that much of the AI fear is already discounted at this price.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($575):** AI fears prove overstated. The protected ratings and index franchises (the bulk of profit) prove AI-resistant and even AI-benefiting (data-center capex financing drives issuance). Market Intelligence holds pricing power through proprietary data and switching costs, and SPGI monetizes AI via data-licensing to model builders. The Mobility spin completes cleanly, lifting margins and prompting a sum-of-the-parts re-rating. The stock re-rates to ~26x next-year EPS, reaching ~$578.

**Bear Case ($360):** AI commoditization accelerates in Market Intelligence — clients build analytics on cheap models, off-the-shelf data erodes Capital IQ Pro pricing, and the data-segment derating deepens. Tech-credit caution spreads further into ratings issuance, pressuring the transaction-linked half of the crown jewel. A debt-issuance slowdown compounds the headwind. The multiple compresses toward 16-17x forward, taking the stock to ~$360, roughly 11% below current.

**Base Case ($510):** Organic growth continues in the high-single-to-low-teens, the Mobility spin completes on schedule and lifts group margins, and the AI threat proves manageable rather than existential. The market gradually re-credits the protected franchises as the post-spin entity demonstrates margin durability. At ~23x next-year EPS, the stock reaches ~$511.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $510.** 23x next-year consensus EPS = $511. This sits between the bear ($360) and bull ($578) scenarios and reflects a partial re-rating from the current ~18.1x as the spin completes and AI fears moderate.

**Bull case target: $575.** ~26x next-year EPS, matching the peer-median multiple and the SOTP re-rating thesis.

**Bear case target: $360.** ~16-17x forward EPS as the data-segment derating deepens and ratings issuance softens, roughly 11% below current.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, tied to the mid-2026 Mobility spin completion, the late-July Q2 2026 earnings print, and subsequent quarterly demonstration of post-spin margin durability.

**Upside/downside from current (~$403):** +26% to base case, +43% to bull case; -11% to bear case. The asymmetry favors the long.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
- Market Intelligence organic revenue growth decelerates below 5% YoY for two consecutive quarters, signaling AI commoditization is hitting the data franchise.
- Ratings transaction revenue declines YoY on broadening tech-credit caution, indicating the AI headwind has spread to the crown jewel.
- The Mobility spin fails to complete on schedule, loses tax-free status, or the combined post-spin value trades below the pre-spin level for a sustained period.

## Conclusion

SPGI is a wide-moat ratings, index, and data oligopolist trading at ~18.1x next-year EPS after a ~30% drawdown driven by an AI-commoditization debate that the Q1 data already partly refutes: investment-grade issuance rose 41% partly on AI-related financing, even as bank-loan ratings softened on tech-credit caution. The portfolio purification (Mobility spin, EDM/thinkFolio and Energy-software sales) concentrates the franchise on its 68-72% margin engines while shedding the most AI-exposed and cyclical pieces. The cluster of open-market insider buying near $430 by the CEO, a divisional CEO, and a director is the strongest forward signal in the filings and contradicts the fear-driven selloff.

The bull case carries risk: the AI threat to Market Intelligence is genuine, estimate revisions have stalled, and the options market is hedging near-term stress. At this price, with every covering analyst at hold-or-better, a re-rating catalyst in the mid-2026 spin, and a discount to comparably-moated peers MCO and MSCI, the risk/reward is favorable. We rate SPGI **BUY** with a 12-month base-case target of $510, recognizing the prior trajectory's drift from BUY to HOLD and reversing it on the improved risk/reward created by the lower price against unchanged-to-improving fundamentals.
