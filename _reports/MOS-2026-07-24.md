**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

The Mosaic Company is a leading global producer of concentrated phosphate and potash crop nutrients, currently operating deep in a cyclical trough. Revenue has fallen roughly 37% from the 2022 super-cycle peak of $19.1B to $12.05B in FY2025, and net income collapsed from $3.58B (2022) to $175M (2024) before a partial recovery to $541M last year. The stock has declined about 40% over the trailing twelve months to $22.28, sitting near the low end of its 52-week range ($19.80–$37.53).

The Q1 2026 print was weak on the surface: a net loss of $(257.6)M / $(0.81) EPS versus +$238.1M / +$0.75 a year prior, but the headline overstates operational damage. Roughly $442M of the loss was one-time Brazil restructuring (the Araxá impairment plus Patrocínio idling), which is non-cash and strategic rather than a demand collapse. The genuine operational pressure came from a raw-material cost shock: sulfur consumed rose +141% YoY and ammonia +50%, tied directly to the Strait of Hormuz disruption from Iran tensions. This turned Phosphate segment operating income to a $(48)M loss from +$139M a year earlier. Potash grew operating income to $177M on a +19% realized MOP price and tight global supply.

The investment case is genuinely two-sided, which is why we land on HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The bull thesis is a cyclical-bottom story: potash tightness, sulfur-supply normalization if Hormuz reopens, and fertilizer prices bottoming. The bear thesis is that the margin squeeze persists, FCF stays negative (FY2025 was -$535M), short-term debt keeps rising, and the antitrust/FTC/DOJ overhang plus a ~$1.4B binary IRS tax matter turn into real liabilities. Consensus estimates are falling sharply across every window (current-year EPS cut 49.1% over 90 days), which tells us the near-term earnings trajectory is still deteriorating even as the macro relief valve exists. At $22.28 with a 4.2% yield, the stock is not obviously cheap on falling estimates and not obviously expensive on normalized mid-cycle earnings, which defines a hold.

## Company Overview

Mosaic operates three segments. **Phosphate** (Q1'26 net sales $1.43B) runs Florida mines and processing plus Louisiana operations and 75% of the Miski Mayo mine in Peru; products include DAP/MAP and the premium MicroEssentials line. **Potash** (Q1'26 net sales $667M) mines in Saskatchewan and New Mexico and sells internationally through Canpotex, the Canadian potash export channel co-owned with Nutrien. It is the most profitable segment at ~29% gross margin. **Mosaic Fertilizantes** (Q1'26 net sales $937M) covers Brazilian and Paraguayan production plus a large, structurally low-margin distribution business (blending, bagging, ports) that ran a ~4% gross margin in Q1'26.

The competitive advantages are vertically integrated mine-to-market scale, the Canpotex export channel, premium/performance products (MicroEssentials, K-Mag, Aspire), and a ~$1.9B equity stake in Saudi Arabia's Ma'aden. That Ma'aden stake is marked to market through the P&L, swinging reported earnings by ±$112–120M in a single quarter, a recurring source of noise unrelated to operations. Primary competitors are Nutrien, CF Industries, OCP (Morocco), and PhosAgro (Russia).

Management is exiting the structurally low-return end of the Brazil portfolio, formalizing the Araxá impairment (~$233M) and idling Patrocínio in April 2026, while keeping optionality on niobium exploration at Patrocínio. The Carlsbad, NM potash mine was sold (closed 4/30/26) for only ~$2M cash, cleaning up the tail of the potash portfolio to concentrate on core Saskatchewan assets.

The trailing financials reflect a business at the bottom of its cycle.

| Operating cash flow | — | — | — | $825M |

FY2025 gross margin was only ~15.8%, and the year produced negative free cash flow of $535M: operating cash flow of $825M did not cover $1.36B of capex. Capex is guided to ~$1.25B for 2026, still elevated relative to depressed cash generation. The negative FCF is the central financial-health concern: the business is spending on maintenance and growth capital at a rate its trough earnings cannot fund.

The balance sheet carries total debt of ~$5.5B (ST $1.2B + LT $4.3B as of Q1'26) against $12.0B equity and only $282M cash. Net debt/EBITDA is manageable at mid-cycle earnings, but the leverage trajectory is the wrong way: short-term debt jumped from $760M (Dec'25) to $1.2B (Mar'26). Management targets investment-grade metrics and a $3.0B liquidity buffer, with roughly $0.6B uncommitted cash plus $1.7B available on the $2.5B commercial-paper/revolver backstop as of Q1'26. That liquidity is adequate but is being drawn on to fund the gap between earnings, capex, and the dividend.

Q1 2026 gross margin fell from 19% to 8%. Separating signal from noise: the one-time Brazil charges and a raw-material cost shock (sulfur consumed $379/long ton in N. America, $466 in Brazil) drove the result. The cost shock is not fully passable to customers, which is why Phosphate went to an operating loss. Management responded by curtailing phosphate production in Louisiana, Bartow (FL), and Brazil in Q2'26, a defensive move that also caps near-term volume.

On capital allocation, the $0.22 quarterly dividend (~$283M/yr, ~4.2% yield) was maintained but not covered by FY2025 FCF. No buybacks occurred in Q1'26 despite prior authorization, signaling capital preservation. The dividend appears secure near-term given the liquidity buffer, but if the trough extends into 2027 it will be funded off the balance sheet rather than out of cash flow, a sustainability question income holders must monitor.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth story here in the conventional sense; Mosaic's earnings are driven by fertilizer price cycles, input costs, and volume. The relevant question is whether the cycle is turning.

The estimate-revision data argues the near-term trajectory is still deteriorating, not inflecting up. Current-quarter EPS estimates were cut 16.3% over 30 days and 50.7% over 90 days; current-year estimates fell 10.2% over 30 days and 49.1% over 90 days; even next-year estimates dropped 24.3% over 90 days. This is broad, accelerating negative revision momentum. Under our framework, an earnings inflection requires rising estimates alongside improving fundamentals; Mosaic shows the opposite: falling estimates against a cost squeeze. The +30% positive-revision BUY signal is absent; if anything, the mirror-image negative signal is present, which is a caution.

The earnings-surprise record reinforces low visibility: three misses in the last four quarters, including -79.1% in Q1'26 and -53.3% in Q4'25. When a company misses consensus by these magnitudes repeatedly, current estimates carry lower confidence, and the risk skews toward further downward revisions before a bottom is confirmed.

The genuine growth optionality is cyclical and macro-dependent. Potash is the bright spot: tight global supply and strong international demand are lifting segment operating income. If sulfur and ammonia supply chains normalize on a Hormuz de-escalation, a scenario the broader macro backdrop treats as increasingly plausible but not yet verified, phosphate margins would recover mechanically as the cost side falls faster than prices. That is the crux of the Freedom Broker double-upgrade to Buy/$32 on 6/17/26. But this is a bet on macro reversal, not on Mosaic executing a growth strategy, and the timing is uncertain.

## Valuation Assessment

The 157x trailing P/E is meaningless: it reflects trough EPS of $1.70 distorted by one-time charges and the cost shock. The correct lens is normalized/forward earnings, but here the framework's cycle-adjustment principle cuts both ways: forward earnings power is real, yet estimates are being revised sharply lower, so the forward multiple is a moving target.

On current consensus, current-year EPS of $0.78 puts the stock at ~29x, and next-year EPS of $1.82 at ~12x. The next-year multiple looks cheap, but both figures have been cut 24–49% over 90 days, so anchoring on them requires believing the revision cycle is near its end. Against peers, the sector median P/E is 17.7x; CF Industries trades at 9.6x on healthier phosphate/nitrogen economics, while Corteva sits at 40.7x on a differentiated seeds/crop-protection model. Mosaic on next-year estimates screens roughly in line with peers, not at a compelling discount.

Analyst price targets bracket the range: low $19, mean $27.19, median $27, high $35. Sentiment is characterized as improving, and the recommendation mix has firmed modestly (four strong buys, six buys, nine holds, zero sells, one strong sell). But this is a name where analyst targets should not be treated as floors: the estimate-revision signal is negative, not positive, so the guardrail against treating consensus as a floor applies. The stock trades below the mean target, which reflects the recent trade-driven and cost-shock-driven weakness rather than a mispricing the market has failed to notice.

Our lessons-derived caution on "cheap" names is directly relevant: a compressed forward multiple in Materials, with falling estimates and no confirmed catalyst, is a classic value-trap pattern rather than a buy signal. The 4.2% yield provides some absolute support, but with the dividend uncovered by FCF, that support is contingent on the balance sheet, not the business. We score valuation as neutral-to-slightly-below-average: there is genuine cheapness on mid-cycle normalized earnings, offset by the risk that estimates have further to fall and the catalyst (Hormuz normalization) is unverified.

## Competitive Landscape

Mosaic's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding rather than structurally impaired. Its scale, Canpotex channel, and premium products remain intact, and potash pricing power is currently a tailwind. But three trajectory concerns weigh on the score.

First, the 2026 margin squeeze is largely industry-wide (the Hormuz sulfur/ammonia shock hit all phosphate producers), so it is not a company-specific competitive loss, but it does expose Mosaic's limited ability to pass input costs through, a structural feature of a commodity producer. Second, the Brazil portfolio pruning (exiting Araxá/Patrocínio) is a rational admission that Fertilizantes distribution is structurally low-margin; it shrinks the footprint and could be margin-accretive long-term, but near-term it removes revenue and signals the segment was underperforming. Third, five-year cumulative TSR of 116.3 lags the peer group's 138.9: Mosaic has underperformed its fertilizer/materials peers over the cycle, consistent with a company that has not widened its moat.

The Canpotex channel is simultaneously a competitive advantage and a regulatory focal point, since it is central to the antitrust price-fixing allegations. That dual role limits the degree to which the channel can be treated as an unambiguous positive. On balance, this is a durable but not improving competitive position.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is elevated and skews toward the downside, with several idiosyncratic items not fully reflected in a simple screen.

**Raw-material cost shock (dominant near-term risk).** The Q1'26 10-Q added a new risk factor explicitly citing the Strait of Hormuz disruption. Sulfur and ammonia costs are largely non-passable and drove Phosphate to an operating loss. This risk is two-sided: it reverses on a verified Hormuz reopening, but the resolution timing is uncertain.

**Antitrust / regulatory overhang (newly emerged, industry-wide).** Beginning March 2026, multiple putative antitrust class actions were filed against Mosaic and peers alleging fertilizer price-fixing, with a JPML consolidation hearing set for May 28, 2026. Separately, the FTC opened a broad probe (~5/28/26) and corn-grower groups are pressing DOJ, with treble damages sought. This is systemic across the sector but represents material, unquantified headline and litigation risk.

**~$1.4B binary IRS tax matter.** A single $1.4B unrecognized tax benefit tied to the Taquari mine divestiture (from the Vale acquisition) is under IRS Pre-Filing Agreement review (accepted March 2026). This magnitude is roughly 19% of the ~$7.1B market cap; an adverse outcome would hit the effective tax rate and earnings, and it is under-appreciated by standard screens.

**Environmental / ARO liabilities.** Asset retirement obligations total $2.5B (gypstack closure the bulk; undiscounted ~$2.3B). Active items include the New Wales Phase II West Stack liner-breach repairs ($55.5M reserve, grouting through summer 2026) and Florida radiation litigation. Financial-assurance requirements tie up ~$745M of capital.

**Brazil concentration and FX.** ~$542M in Brazilian labor/environmental claims and ~$783M in Brazil tax contingencies (partly Vale-indemnified), plus BRL/CAD volatility that drives large reported-earnings swings.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 60.2% is rich versus 1-year historical volatility of 45.6% (a 14.5-point spread, 74th percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (60.2% near-term versus 51.5% long-term), which signals the market is pricing imminent stress, likely around the next earnings print and the evolving Hormuz/regulatory catalysts. Put/call skew is elevated at 6.5% and the six-month tenor shows a -4.9% put skew, indicating investors are paying up for downside protection. The recorded ATM IV jump to 233.9% in the delta snapshot appears to be a thin-options data artifact and is treated as non-informative. Short interest at 12.4% of float (2.9 days to cover) reflects meaningful but not extreme bearish institutional positioning. The net read: options confirm elevated near-term uncertainty and downside-hedging demand, consistent with our two-sided fundamental view and reinforcing the case for patience rather than conviction.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The cyclical bottom is in. Fertilizer prices are near a cyclical low (echoed by Scotiabank's 6/30/26 sector upgrade), potash tightness is real, and a Hormuz de-escalation normalizes sulfur/ammonia supply, mechanically restoring phosphate margins as costs fall faster than selling prices. The Q1 loss was mostly one-time Brazil charges, so underlying operations are pressured but not collapsing. Portfolio pruning of low-margin Brazil assets is margin-accretive over time. On next-year normalized EPS of $1.82 (or higher if the cycle recovers), the stock at ~12x with a 4.2% yield offers a re-rating path toward the $35 analyst high. Bull target: ~$32.

### Bear Case
The margin squeeze persists. If Hormuz stays disrupted or resolution slips, phosphate margins remain compressed and estimates keep falling; the current 49.1% year-to-date current-year revision cut has not clearly bottomed. FCF stays negative, short-term debt keeps rising, and the dividend becomes an increasingly balance-sheet-funded liability. The antitrust class actions and FTC/DOJ probes produce quantified liabilities, and/or the $1.4B Taquari IRS matter resolves adversely. Three misses in four quarters signal poor visibility, and the market re-rates toward the $19 low target. Bear target: ~$19, roughly 15% downside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale; the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction. The macro relief valve (Hormuz normalization, potash tightness) is real but unverified, while the near-term earnings trajectory (falling estimates, negative FCF, rising short-term debt) is still deteriorating.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Verified normalization of sulfur/ammonia input costs with phosphate segment returning to positive operating income, confirmed in a quarterly print.
- EPS estimate-revision trend turning positive across current-year and next-year windows over a 60–90 day span, ending the current downward cascade.
- A favorable or dismissed resolution of the Taquari IRS matter and/or early dismissal of the antitrust class actions.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FCF remaining negative through FY2026 while short-term debt continues rising toward covenant-relevant levels, raising dividend-sustainability risk.
- A dividend cut, or the emergence of a quantified, material antitrust/FTC liability.
- Next-year EPS estimates continuing to fall below ~$1.50 with no input-cost relief, confirming the trough is deeper and longer than priced.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (which will show the Q2 production curtailments and whether input costs are easing), plus the JPML antitrust consolidation progress and any update on the Hormuz situation. A 6-month window should resolve much of the current two-sidedness.

## Conclusion

Mosaic is a well-positioned commodity producer caught at the intersection of a cyclical trough and a macro-driven input-cost shock, layered with real but early-stage regulatory and tax risks. The Q1'26 headline loss overstates operational damage, but the underlying phosphate margin squeeze and the sharply negative estimate-revision momentum are real signals that the near-term earnings picture is still worsening. Potash strength and the plausible reversal of the sulfur/ammonia shock provide a credible recovery path, but the catalyst is unverified and the timing uncertain.

At $22.28 the stock is neither compellingly cheap on falling estimates nor clearly expensive on normalized mid-cycle earnings. The 4.2% yield offers support but is uncovered by FCF. With three earnings misses in four quarters, a ~$1.4B binary tax matter, an industry-wide antitrust overhang, and estimates still being cut, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We rate MOS a **HOLD**, appropriate for investors comfortable underwriting a macro reversal. Waiting for input costs to demonstrably normalize and estimate revisions to turn is the higher-probability path to a confident BUY. We would not chase weakness here without a specific catalyst, and we would reassess at the next earnings print.
