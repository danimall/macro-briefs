## Executive Summary

MillerKnoll's fiscal 2026 (ended May 30, 2026) produced a headline recovery and an underlying deterioration, and the market has priced the headline. GAAP operating income rose from $50.5M to $198.3M and GAAP diluted EPS swung from −$0.54 to +$1.32, almost entirely because FY2025's $130.0M of goodwill and trade-name impairments plus $28.3M of Knoll integration charges did not repeat. On the company's own adjusted basis, operating earnings *fell* from $248.7M to $238.4M, operating margin contracted 60bp to 6.2%, and adjusted diluted EPS fell from $1.95 to $1.86. Gross margin was flat at 38.8%. Of the $172M revenue increase, management attributes $74M to price net of discounting and $41M to currency; volume added $57M, roughly 1.5 points of the 4.7% reported growth.

The order book is where the case breaks. FY2026 orders of $3,761.0M came in $80.7M *below* shipments (book-to-bill 0.979), organic orders declined 0.7%, and backlog fell 10.8% to $678.8M — 17.7% of revenue. Reconstructing the fourth quarter from the 10-K and the Q3 10-Q (a derivation that reconciles to reported backlog within $0.1M) shows Q4 orders down an estimated 6.3%, with North America Contract down 10.0% and International down 8.7%, and Q4 adjusted EPS of $0.55 versus $0.62 a year earlier. FY2026 revenue growth was funded in part by liquidating $82.5M of backlog. Consensus has begun to acknowledge this: the current-quarter estimate was cut 12.7% to $0.35 over the past 30 days, against $0.45 actually earned in the comparable quarter of FY2026 — a 22% implied year-over-year decline — while the full-year FY2027 figure barely moved at $2.02, meaning analysts pushed earnings into later quarters rather than removing them.

Against that, the stock has risen from $16.50 in late June to $23.605, +43%, and sits 4.5% below its 52-week high of $24.71 after touching $13.77 in the past twelve months. The re-rating took trailing adjusted P/E from roughly 8.9x to 12.7x and GAAP P/E to 17.9x. The fundamental concerns raised in prior work were validated by the 10-K while the valuation cushion that made the shares tolerable has largely closed. Base case fair value is approximately $21 (11x roughly flat FY2027 adjusted EPS of $1.90, cross-checked at 7.5x EV/EBITDA), against a bear case near $15 and a bull case near $31. Probability-weighted, the expected total return including the 3.18% dividend is modestly negative. **REDUCE** — trim into strength. This is not a balance-sheet emergency, but the risk/reward has inverted.

## Company Overview

MillerKnoll designs, manufactures and distributes interior furnishings for office, healthcare, education and residential settings, formed by the July 2021 Herman Miller–Knoll merger. The brand set is the broadest in the industry: Herman Miller, Knoll, DWR, HAY, Muuto, Holly Hunt, Geiger, Maharam, KnollTextiles, Spinneybeck, Edelman, NaughtOne, DatesWeiser and others, with trade-dress rights on canonical designs (Eames Lounge, Barcelona, Womb, Aeron, Embody).

Three segments, restructured effective March 1, 2025 with history recast:

| Segment | FY2026 sales | % of total | FY2026 adj. op. margin | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| North America Contract | $2,061.2M | 53.7% | 10.3% | 9.7% |
| International Contract | $674.0M | 17.5% | 8.6% | 11.1% |
| Global Retail | $1,106.5M | 28.8% | 3.0% | 5.0% |
| **Consolidated** | **$3,841.7M** | 100% | **6.2%** | **6.8%** |

Approximately 53.6% of sales flow through independently owned contract dealers; the remainder is direct (including GSA schedule business), owned retail (93 stores at year-end), independent retailers, catalog and eCommerce. Roughly 36% of Global Retail revenue transacts inside physical stores. Manufacturing is order-driven and largely assembly-based, with deliberate use of strategic suppliers to convert fixed cost into variable cost. Largest customer $196.8M (~5% of sales, unnamed in the filing); top ten ~16%. Named contract competitors are Haworth and HNI; retail competitors include RH, Williams-Sonoma, Arhaus, Wayfair and Crate & Barrel.

Leadership is in transition. The President and CEO departed in June 2026 and the Chief Operating Officer, Jeffrey Stutz (previously CFO), is Interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent successor. FY2026 carried $2.6M of "CEO transition costs" (severance, benefits, advisory fees), or $0.04 of adjusted EPS.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and mix.** FY2026 revenue of $3,841.7M remains 6.0% below the FY2023 peak of $4,087.1M. The composition of growth matters more than the rate: price net of discounting +$74M, FX +$41M, volume +$57M. In International Contract the price line worked in reverse — incremental discounting attributable to regional and product mix reduced net sales by approximately $16M, and the segment's reported 2.1% sales increase masked a 1.2% organic decline offset by $22M of translation. List prices are rising while realized prices in International are falling.

**Margins.** Consolidated operating expenses fell $81.6M, but the build is unfavorable: −$130M impairment and −$28M integration (both non-recurring benefits) against +$33M compensation, +$15M new-store operating cost, +$14M variable selling and royalties, +$10M FX and +$5M program spend — roughly $77M of recurring cost growth. Global Retail adjusted operating earnings fell 37% to $32.7M *on 5.9% higher sales*, with segment gross margin down 140bp to 44.5% on tariffs net of pricing and adverse FX. International adjusted operating earnings fell to $58.3M from $73.5M. North America Contract was the sole offset: adjusted margin 10.3% from 9.7%, gross margin 36.5% from 35.7%, driven by price, mix, restructuring and facility consolidation rather than volume — segment orders declined 1.0% for the year.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow of $199.9M less capex of $122.3M gives free cash flow of $77.6M, down from $101.7M. Dividends consumed approximately $51.0M (cross-checked against $38.3M of nine-month dividends paid), buybacks $16.3M — leaving roughly $10M of discretionary cash. Dividend coverage from FCF is 1.52x; the payout is 66% of FCF. Buybacks were cut 81% year over year, which reads as management already recognizing the constraint. FY2027 capex is guided to $125–135M with $82.1M already committed; at flat operating cash flow that implies FY2027 FCF of $65–75M, roughly equal to the dividend. That is a sensitivity on full-year inputs, not a forecast — a working-capital release or a realized IEEPA tariff refund would improve it, and customer deposits already declined from $102.5M to $90.3M during FY2026.

**Balance sheet.** Cash $167.7M (of which $161.7M offshore, with $119.9M intended for repatriation), total debt $1,285.7M, net debt $1,118.0M, equity $1,342.6M, revolver availability $404.0M, total liquidity $571.7M, current ratio 1.58. Using adjusted operating earnings plus D&A ($238.4M + $110.3M = $348.7M) as an EBITDA proxy, net leverage is 3.21x and gross 3.69x. That proxy sits within 1.1% of the company's own FY2025 "EBITDA, As Adjusted" when constructed identically, but it is not the credit agreement definition, and actual covenant headroom is unverified. Operating lease liabilities of approximately $481.5M sit outside these figures. Altman Z of 2.03 places the company in the grey zone, dragged there by thin EBIT relative to a goodwill-inflated $4.0B asset base rather than by illiquidity. Tangible book value is approximately negative $478M — goodwill of $1,161.3M plus indefinite-lived intangibles of $435.3M alone equal 119% of equity.

The genuine positive is maturity structure. Across ten months the company extended the revolver and Term Loan A to April 2030 (raising TLA to $400M and establishing a higher maximum first lien secured net leverage ratio with no step-downs), extended Term Loan B to August 2032 at a reduced $550M, opened a $90M receivables securitization to September 2028, and repriced TLB's margin. Cost was roughly $8.0M of extinguishment losses plus $2.2M of deferred financing fees. Near-term refinancing risk is largely removed through 2028.

**Earnings quality.** Three consecutive annual restructuring programs (FY2024 $30.8M, FY2025 $14.8M, FY2026 $13.5M) argue against treating restructuring as non-recurring; adding it back at a 25% tax rate reduces FY2026 adjusted EPS to roughly $1.71, or 13.8x the current price. Knoll integration is complete at $144.4M cumulative with no further costs expected, so the GAAP-to-adjusted gap should structurally narrow — the remaining items are $24.0M of Knoll purchased-intangible amortization and restructuring.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth case rests on assumptions the FY2026 filing does not support.

Backlog entering FY2027 is $678.8M versus $761.3M, and the company cautions that backlog at any point does not necessarily indicate net sales for a succeeding period. Even accepting that caveat, revenue in FY2026 was partly a conversion of prior-year orders; replacing it requires order intake to inflect. Q4's derived 6.3% order decline is partly a mirror image of prior-year lumpiness — Q3 FY2025 North America orders were an anomalously weak $434.0M and Q4 FY2025 an unusually strong $567.6M at 1.078 book-to-bill — so no single quarter should be over-read. The full-year organic order decline of 0.7% and the 10.8% backlog reduction are not base effects.

Consensus FY2027 adjusted EPS of $2.02 (four analysts) implies 8.6% growth over $1.86. To get there on flat-to-lower revenue, MillerKnoll must expand consolidated adjusted margin by roughly 40–50bp while absorbing a hedge roll-off worth about $0.08 of EPS and rising new-store operating costs. The near-term revision pattern is the tell: current quarter cut 12.7% to $0.35 against $0.45 earned a year ago, next quarter cut 7.3% to $0.50, and the full year essentially unchanged. Earnings were rescheduled, not removed. If the second-half recovery embedded in that shape does not appear, FY2027 lands closer to $1.75–1.85.

Two structural questions sit behind the cyclical one. First, the company's own named demand drivers are corporate profitability, service-sector employment, new office construction and office vacancy — and service-sector white-collar headcount is exposed to AI-driven role consolidation. Office furniture demand is ultimately a seat-count business. Second, Global Retail — 28.8% of revenue and the segment the strategy section identifies as the growth vehicle — is being expanded while the segment's own adjusted operating margin fell 200bp and capex rose, and the company's own risk factors describe consumer spending as having "reduced, and in the future may further reduce" demand. The filings disclose no store-level cohort economics, so whether this is a productive investment with delayed payback cannot be adjudicated; what is observable is $61.8M of incremental sales against a $19.2M decline in segment adjusted operating earnings.

There is genuine unquantified upside optionality. The 10-K newly discloses that MillerKnoll has submitted or intends to submit claims for refunds on substantially all IEEPA tariffs paid that may be eligible for recovery, following FY2026 court rulings. The company quantifies neither tariffs paid nor amounts claimed, and the U.S. government's appeal is unresolved. It is a real option on FY2027–FY2028 earnings and should not be in a base case.

## Valuation Assessment

At the frozen basis of $23.605 with 68,123,238 shares:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Market cap | $1,608M |
| Enterprise value (incl. $1,118M net debt) | $2,726M |
| P/E on FY2026 GAAP EPS $1.32 | 17.9x |
| P/E on FY2026 adjusted EPS $1.86 | 12.7x |
| P/E on restructuring-adjusted ~$1.71 | 13.8x |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus $2.02 | 11.7x |
| P/E on FY2028 consensus $2.38 | 9.9x |
| EV/adjusted EBITDA ($348.7M proxy) | 7.8x |
| FCF yield (FY2026 FCF $77.6M) | 4.83% |
| Dividend yield ($0.75) | 3.18% |
| Net debt / adj. EBITDA | 3.21x |

Peer context is imperfect. The stated Office Services & Supplies median P/E of 24.1x is distorted by HNI at 731.9x on trough earnings and WillScot at negative earnings; the cleaner comparables are Interface at 15.5x and Pitney Bowes at 14.3x. MLKN at 17.9x GAAP is above both; at 12.7x on adjusted earnings that exclude recurring restructuring it is modestly below. On EV/EBITDA, 7.8x is a reasonable mid-cycle multiple for a 3.2x-levered cyclical, not a distressed one.

The decisive point is what changed between the last assessment at $16.50 and today. Nothing improved operationally — the 10-K filed July 20 disclosed a full-year organic order decline, a 10.8% backlog drawdown, adjusted EPS decline, and goodwill cushions of 1.1% and 3.1%. What changed is a 43% price move that took the multiple from roughly 8.9x adjusted earnings to 12.7x. At 8.9x, a shrinking backlog was compensated. At 12.7x on earnings that declined year over year, with the near quarters being cut, it is not. Prior appreciation is irrelevant to intrinsic value; the multiple that appreciation produced is not.

Scenario construction:

- **Base $21.00 (−11.0%).** FY2027 adjusted EPS roughly flat at $1.90 — North America self-help and completed Knoll integration offsetting International discounting, Retail margin drag and the $0.08 hedge headwind — at 11x. Cross-check: 7.5x on $345M EBITDA gives $2,588M EV less $1,118M net debt = $1,470M equity, or $21.58 per share.
- **Bull $31.00 (+31.3%).** Office demand inflects, Global Retail margin normalizes toward 5% (+$22.6M pretax, roughly $0.24 EPS), an IEEPA refund lands, adjusted EPS reaches the FY2028 consensus $2.38, multiple 13x.
- **Bear $15.00 (−36.5%).** Orders continue declining 4–6%, revenue falls 3–4% as backlog empties, adjusted EPS $1.55–1.65, another impairment charge in International/Retail resets book value, multiple compresses to ~9.4x. The dividend becomes a live question if FCF settles at the low end of the $65–75M sensitivity.

Weighting base 50%, bull 25%, bear 25% gives an expected price return of roughly −7%, or about −4% including the dividend. Operating leverage is real in the other direction — each 100bp of consolidated adjusted margin is roughly $38M pretax, about $0.42 of EPS — which is why the bull case is wide. The asymmetry from $23.605 favors the downside.

The single published price target of $35.00 (one analyst; coverage is 1 buy, 1 hold) implies +48%. On a stock at 11.7x forward consensus with rising estimates, analyst targets would be a floor. Here the near-term estimates are being cut while the full-year is held flat, so the target reflects the second-half recovery that consensus itself is deferring rather than independent confirmation.

## Competitive Landscape

Trajectory is mixed and net negative. North America Contract is improving profitability through mix, price and facility consolidation — 80bp of segment gross margin, 60bp of operating margin — while full-year orders declined 1.0%. Margin gained without share gained. International Contract is deteriorating on both axes: organic sales −1.2%, organic orders −5.6%, adjusted margin −250bp, with the filing explicitly attributing a $16M sales headwind to incremental discounting. Global Retail is defending traffic with promotions (Q3 gross margin down 200bp partly on targeted incremental promotional actions) against RH, Williams-Sonoma, Wayfair and Arhaus, several of which the 10-K concedes have greater resources and can adopt more aggressive pricing.

The multi-channel structure — contract plus owned residential retail — is a genuine hedge neither Haworth nor HNI replicates at scale, and hybrid work is framed as an opportunity in the strategy section. FY2026 evidence is unfavorable to that framing: the residential/retail leg generated 3.0% adjusted operating margin on 28.8% of revenue while the contract legs generated 10.3% and 8.6%.

The newly added AI/agentic-commerce risk factor is not boilerplate and deserves weight. With ~53.6% of sales flowing through independent dealers whose value-add is specification, design and installation labor, the filing's own language — that AI platforms could alter how customers specify and purchase furniture and could disintermediate existing sales channels — names a structural threat to the distribution moat. It is slow-moving and unquantifiable today, and it argues against paying a recovery multiple.

One material gap: BIFMA shipment data and comparable Steelcase/HNI order data could not be obtained, so whether MLKN's order weakness is company-specific or industry-wide is unresolved. One internal datapoint leans company-specific — management raised goodwill discount rates to 13.5%–16.5% from 12.0%–15.0%, attributing the increase to higher *company-specific* risk premiums. A single self-reported valuation input is thin evidence.

## Risk Assessment

**Impairment cushions are effectively exhausted.** The March 31, 2026 quantitative test showed fair value exceeding carrying value by 45.5% (North America Contract), 8.5% (Coverings), 3.1% (International Contract) and 1.1% (Global Retail); trade-name cushions were 6.8% (Knoll) and 2.1% (Muuto). Disclosed single-variable sensitivities: a 500bp cut to forecast sales implies $322.6M of aggregate impairment (24% of equity, 20% of market cap); a 100bp operating-margin reduction implies $117.5M; a 100bp discount-rate increase implies $74.8M. Global Retail carries $408.4M of goodwill against a 1.1% cushion while its adjusted operating earnings fell 37%; International organic orders fell 5.6%. These thresholds are within one soft year of being met. Charges are non-cash and do not accelerate debt, and at roughly 1.2x book the market discounts some of this. Two mechanical concerns remain: covenant definitions that have not been read, and the reflexive link the 10-K creates by corroborating reporting-unit fair value against market capitalization while naming a sustained share-price decline as an impairment indicator. No inference is drawn about management intent; the election to test all four units quantitatively is a mark of conservatism.

**Interest-hedge roll-off.** A $575.0M swap fixed at 1.650% terminates January 29, 2027, replaced by only $200M forward-starting at 3.380%. Hedged notional falls from $950M to $575M against roughly $1,237M of floating-rate term and revolver debt plus a $70M securitization — coverage from ~77% to ~45%. Nine-month FY2026 reclassified swap gains were $14.2M (~$18.9M annualized) versus a disclosed next-twelve-months expectation of $11.6M pretax, implying roughly $7.3M of headwind, about $0.08 of EPS, with a larger step-down thereafter. This works directly against the FY2026 interest-expense improvement from $76.7M to $69.9M. If SOFR falls materially the roll-off could be neutral. [unverified — the current Fed policy rate and long-end rate path are not in the research brief.]

**Leadership.** No permanent CEO. Seven Item 5.02 8-K filings between July 2025 and June 2026 indicate elevated officer/director activity, though that count also captures routine annual director elections and cannot alone establish involuntary turnover. The circumstances of the June departure are in an 8-K that has not been read; no claim is made about them and no inference is drawn from the January 2026 10b5-1 plan or the timing of the $2.6M severance accrual.

**Other.** Tariff costs adversely affected gross margin in the first half of FY2026 and remain a live cost and demand risk. Dealer credit exposure is modest ($7.2M maximum loss on VIE notes receivable). Domestic liquidity depends materially on the revolver and securitization rather than the cash balance, 96% of which is offshore. The company is partially self-insured with no aggregate stop-loss on health-benefit and auto-liability retentions.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 65.4% sits 15.3 points above 50.1% one-year realized volatility, at the 71st percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (65.4% near versus 53.8% at three months) and the data source flagging elevated put demand at a 12.9% skew. The nearest expiry prices a ±9.3% move over eight days with no scheduled company event in that window, which in a chain of only 24 contracts across three expirations carries limited signal. Open interest tells a different story: the put/call OI ratio is 0.18, down from 0.48 on June 22 — positioning built during the rally is call-heavy. Short interest is 8.7% of float at 5.8 days to cover, moderate and consistent with some covering having contributed to the move. The composite read is that the market is paying up for near-term protection on a stock whose accumulated positioning is directionally long, an unstable configuration into a September quarter where consensus has just been cut. Rich near-dated volatility also makes covered-call overwriting a reasonable way for holders to execute a trim.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($31, +31%)

Adjusted operating margin of 6.2% is well below the levels the combined company achieved earlier in its history, and each 100bp is worth roughly $0.42 of EPS. Knoll integration is finished at $144.4M cumulative with no further costs expected, so reported and adjusted earnings converge from here. North America Contract — 53.7% of sales — is demonstrating self-help margin expansion. Maturities are pushed to 2028–2032 with covenant headroom widened and no step-downs, removing refinancing risk for three years. The stock trades at 9.9x FY2028 consensus with a 3.18% dividend covered 1.5x by free cash flow, and an unsized IEEPA tariff refund claim is a free option. If office demand inflects and Global Retail's new-store cohort matures toward 5% segment margin, FY2028 EPS of $2.38 at 13x supports $31.

### Bear Case ($15, −36%)

Twelve months of orders ran below twelve months of shipments; backlog is down 10.8% and revenue growth was 43% price and 24% currency. The demand drivers management names — service-sector employment, new office construction, office vacancy — are all named by the company as having declined over recent years with an adverse effect on furniture demand. Consensus has cut the current quarter 12.7% while holding the year flat, which will not survive a second soft order print. The January 2027 hedge roll-off removes $0.08 of EPS with more behind it. Free cash flow at guided capex roughly equals the dividend, leaving nothing for deleveraging or buybacks, which were already cut 81%. Goodwill cushions of 1.1% and 3.1% mean a soft year produces a nine-figure impairment against $1,342.6M of equity and already-negative tangible book. At $1.60 of adjusted EPS and a 9.4x multiple the shares are worth $15.

### Our View

The bear case does not require anything new to happen — it requires the trends already in the 10-K to continue for two more quarters. The bull case requires a demand inflection for which there is no evidence in the order data. At $16.50 the discount paid for that uncertainty; at $23.605 it does not.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE. Base case $21.00 (−11.0% from $23.605), bull $31.00 (+31.3%), bear $15.00 (−36.5%). Horizon 6–12 months.**

**De-risking plan.** Trim into current strength rather than waiting for the Q1 FY2027 report, which is the first hard test of a consensus figure just cut to $0.35 against $0.45 earned a year ago. Reduce position size by roughly half to two-thirds now. Holders wanting to retain the 3.18% dividend can keep a residual position and overwrite with calls, where near-dated implied volatility of 65.4% against 50.1% realized is generous. Any residual exposure should be sized for the possibility of a nine-figure non-cash impairment in International Contract or Global Retail at the FY2027 annual test.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD or better.**
1. Two consecutive quarters of positive organic order growth in North America Contract with consolidated book-to-bill above 1.0 and backlog stabilizing above $680M.
2. Global Retail adjusted operating margin recovering to 4.5%+ with new-store operating cost growth decelerating below sales growth.
3. Price returning to $18–19 (roughly 10x flat FY2027 adjusted EPS, 6.8x EV/EBITDA) with fundamentals unchanged, which restores the margin of safety that existed in June.
4. A permanent CEO appointed with a capital-allocation framework that prioritizes deleveraging over retail store count, or a quantified IEEPA refund of material size.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID.**
1. Q1 FY2027 adjusted EPS below $0.30 with orders declining again, confirming the backlog drawdown is flowing into revenue.
2. Impairment of $150M+ combined with any covenant-related disclosure or a reduction in revolver availability.
3. A dividend cut, or free cash flow tracking below $60M annualized against guided capex of $125–135M.
4. FY2027 revenue guidance implying a decline while capex stays above $125M.

**Reassessment date.** The Q1 FY2027 report, the single most informative event available, because it carries both the order print and the first management commentary on FY2027 under interim leadership. Secondary checkpoint: January 2027, when the $575M swap at 1.650% terminates.

## Conclusion

The score trajectory for this name has oscillated between 4.4 and 5.2 with recommendations alternating HOLD and AVOID, and the most recent AVOID at $16.50 was followed by a 43% rally. That call was wrong on price and right on operations: the FY2026 10-K, filed after that assessment, confirmed a full-year organic order decline, a 10.8% backlog drawdown, a 60bp adjusted margin contraction, an adjusted EPS decline, and goodwill cushions of 1.1% and 3.1%. The reason the AVOID failed was that 8.9x adjusted earnings was too low a multiple for a business generating $200M of operating cash flow with no maturities until 2028. That specific error is no longer available to make: at 12.7x adjusted, 17.9x GAAP and 7.8x EV/EBITDA, the discount has closed.

Our overall score declines to 4.1 from 4.7, and every point of that decline is traceable to new information rather than to price action — the quantified impairment cushions and full-year order data first appeared in the July 20 filing, and the 12.7% cut to the current-quarter estimate is a 30-day development. The valuation dimension falls because the price rose against unchanged fundamentals, which is what that dimension is supposed to measure.

MillerKnoll is a real business with irreplaceable design assets, a completed integration, a de-risked maturity schedule and a covered dividend. It is also a levered cyclical whose backlog is shrinking, whose growth segment earns 3.0% margins, whose interest cost steps up in January 2027, and which has no permanent chief executive, trading within 4.5% of its 52-week high while analysts cut the next two quarters. Sell into the strength and revisit at $18–19 or on two quarters of positive organic orders. **REDUCE.**

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: several unsupported macro assertions were removed (Fed policy rate/long-end path, mortgage rates at one-year highs, homes clearing below asking, AI-driven white-collar role consolidation as an active "current wave"); the dividend-coverage line already correctly states 1.52x FCF coverage / 66% payout, and the corrected macro grounding does not alter the core order-book and valuation evidence underlying REDUCE.
