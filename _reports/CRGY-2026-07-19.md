**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Crescent Energy is a KKR-affiliated, externally-managed onshore U.S. oil and gas producer built through serial acquisition. The December 2025 all-stock merger with Vital Energy stepped up its scale: Q1 2026 production reached a record 341 MBoe/d (+32% YoY), revenue $1.18B (+24%), and Adjusted EBITDAX $690M (+30%). The headline GAAP net loss of $419.8M ($(1.28) EPS) is largely a non-cash artifact: roughly $600.6M of unrealized mark-to-market derivative losses booked when the February 2026 Iran conflict pushed crude briefly above $100/bbl above the company's hedge strikes. On an adjusted basis the quarter earned $175.5M ($0.53 adj. EPS), and operating cash flow was $409M. The operating business is healthy; the reported loss overstates weakness.

The constraint is the per-share machinery around the operating engine. Crescent is effectively a public private-equity vehicle. KKR's external Manager is paid $78.5M in annual base compensation that escalates by 1.5% of net proceeds from every equity issuance, plus incentive PSUs worth up to 4.8% of Class A shares per three-year tranche. This structure rewards dilution, and the record confirms it works as designed: shares outstanding roughly doubled from ~170M in 2023 to ~330M by Q1 2026. Common shareholders do not elect directors; a KKR affiliate holds the sole non-economic Series I preferred that appoints the entire board. KKR-linked legacy holders have been steady net sellers (Rule 144 sales near $9.91, a 36.8M-share gift, a 32.6M-share foundation sale), with no offsetting open-market insider buying. This is sponsor monetization overlaid on a fee structure that fights minority shareholders.

The stock is up ~27% since its January low but has fallen from a March peak of ~$14 to ~$10.75. Near-term consensus EPS estimates are falling (current quarter -6.8% over 30 days, next quarter -16.9%), even as the full-year figure sits +10.8% higher over 90 days. Net leverage of 1.7x looks reasonable, but the Altman Z-Score of ~1.4 sits in the distress zone, reflecting the leveraged, asset-heavy build. We rate CRGY HOLD as an income vehicle: the ~4.6% dividend and $336M remaining buyback offer support, but the governance structure, dilution machine, and share-supply overhang leave no clean appreciation catalyst. We are holding for yield, not upside.

## Company Overview

Crescent Energy pursues a "returns-driven growth-through-acquisition" strategy anchored in the Eagle Ford, with expanding positions in the Permian and Uinta basins and a growing minerals and royalties business ("Crescent Royalties"). The portfolio is oil-weighted (~41% oil, ~64% liquids) with ~96% of acreage held-by-production, giving the company flexibility to defer capex.

Two structural features distinguish CRGY from operator-led E&P peers. First, it is externally managed by KKR Energy Assets Manager LLC under a Management Agreement running through December 2027 (auto-renewing). The Manager supplies the executive team (CEO David Rockecharlie, CFO Brandi Kendall) and is compensated with a large base fee that grows with equity issuance plus performance PSUs that scale with the share count. Second, governance is KKR-controlled: a KKR affiliate holds the sole non-economic Series I preferred stock, which appoints the entire board and holds approval rights over major corporate actions. Common shareholders do not vote for directors, and Crescent is a "controlled company" exempt from certain NYSE independence requirements.

The model exploits a genuine market theme, investors rewarding oil-weighted, free-cash-flow-generative producers that avoid growth-at-any-cost, but wraps it in a fee-and-governance apparatus that concentrates control and economics with the sponsor.

## Financial Analysis

The Vital merger (73.3M shares issued, ~$2.49B debt assumed) drove the Q1 2026 scale step-up. The operating comparison is strong:

| Total revenue | $1.18B | $950M | +24% |
| Adj. EBITDAX | $690M | $529M | +30% |
| Operating cash flow | $409M | $337M | +21% |
| Levered FCF | $192M | $242M | -21% |
| GAAP net income | $(419.8)M | $(2.2)M | — |
| Adjusted net income | $175.5M | $145.4M | +21% |

The GAAP loss breaks down as follows. Q1 booked a $706.6M loss on derivatives, of which ~$600.6M was unrealized mark-to-market. When crude spiked above the company's hedge strikes, the accounting value of those hedge positions fell, generating a non-cash charge. The cash business was fine, as operating cash flow of $409M shows. A naive screen flagging CRGY as unprofitable would be wrong.

The flip side is real and recurring. Hedges are capping realized oil pricing: Q1 realized oil price including derivative settlements was $63.75/bbl versus $71.00 excluding, giving up ~$7/bbl in a firm-price environment, with oil ~68% hedged for 2026. In the world model's most-probable oil scenario ($100-120 grind on the 2027 timeline) this hedge drag persists as a cash headwind, not a paper item. Natural gas realized just $2.37/Mcf (-25% YoY) on Waha basis weakness.

Balance sheet: long-term debt of $5.24B (down from $5.52B at year-end via net revolver repayment). Company-reported net LTM leverage is 1.7x on a credit-agreement basis, reasonable for E&P. D/E is ~1.4x, and the Altman Z-Score of ~1.4 sits in the distress zone, reflecting the leveraged, acquisition-built structure. Liquidity is ~$2.0B with no revolver drawn at quarter-end; borrowing base is $3.9B against a ~$2.0B elected commitment. Levered FCF fell 21% despite higher EBITDAX because capex jumped to $385M from $208M; the acquisition-and-development spend competes directly with capital return and deleveraging.

The March 2026 refinancing was a clear liability-management win: $690M of 2.75% convertible notes due 2031 issued, $500M of 9.25% 2028 notes redeemed, $39M of 2029 notes repurchased. This lowered blended interest ~50bps and extended maturities. The trade-off is that the converts (conversion price ~$14.89) add up to 46.3M potential shares to an already-inflated count.

The earnings surprise record supports forward-estimate confidence on the operating line: three beats in four quarters (Q1'26 +52.2%, Q4'25 +41.1%, Q2'25 +43.0%, one narrow Q3'25 miss of -2.0%). But estimate revisions are the more relevant forward signal here, and they are broadly negative near-term: current quarter -6.8% (30d), next quarter -16.9% (30d), current year -8.2% (30d) even though the 90-day full-year figure remains +10.8%. The 30-day trend is deteriorating, which caps the growth score.

## Growth Analysis

The absolute growth optics are strong (production +32%, revenue +24%, EBITDAX +30%) but almost entirely acquired rather than organic. Distinguishing the two matters because the acquisition mechanism is the same one that dilutes per-share value. On a per-share basis the picture is far more muted: adjusted EPS of $0.53 was down 7% YoY despite the jump in EBITDAX, because the share count inflated faster than earnings.

The organic case rests on three legs: Permian synergy capture (~$120M realized to date, "ahead of plan" on management's target), the developing minerals/royalties stream (Crescent Royalty Finance carved out with its own $1.0B RBL facility; CRF Adjusted EBITDAX $34M in Q1 at a far higher LFCF margin than the E&P segment), and the ~199.2 MMBoe of PUDs behind held-by-production acreage that provides development optionality.

Against this, the near-term estimate revision trend is negative and the 90-day full-year uplift (+10.8%) does not clear the >30% threshold that would flag a genuine earnings turn; it partly reflects the Vital scale addition already in the numbers. Growth durability at the infrastructure/demand level for oil-weighted FCF producers is intact under the $100-120 grind scenario, but a verified Hormuz reopening (pulling crude to $85-95) would compress upstream realizations and pressure the reserve/PUD economics that assume $65.34/bbl, already above the $57-61 five-year strip. Net, this is a scaled-up but per-share-diluted growth profile with two-sided commodity exposure.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is not meaningful given the non-cash GAAP loss. On forward adjusted earnings of ~$2.25 (current-year consensus), the stock trades at ~4.8x, cheap on an absolute basis and below the E&P peer median (CRK 6.2x, CNX 4.4x, MGY 15.8x; several peers show negative trailing P/E on the same hedge-mark dynamic). The ~4.6% dividend yield offers absolute support that most peers lack (peer median yield 0%).

The analyst target set is wide and biased upward: low $11.00, median $18.00, mean $16.53, high $22.00, against a $10.75 price. Taken at face value, that implies substantial upside. We treat this with skepticism for two reasons. First, "cheap" screens the wrong way in the current regime: a compressed multiple here reflects a structural discount for external-management conflicts and dilution, which is a persistent feature, not a mispricing awaiting correction. Second, in the Energy sector specifically, BUYs have historically underperformed the market on an alpha basis, so a low multiple alone does not earn a high valuation score.

The specific reason the market applies a discount is identifiable and durable: the Manager fee grows with every equity raise, incentive PSUs scale with share count, KKR controls the board, and legacy sponsor holders are steady net sellers with registration rights on large blocks. That combination structurally caps the multiple. The convertible capped-call to $22.48 signals management hopes for a higher stock but is protecting against dilution up to that cap, indicating even insiders view $14.89+ as aspirational rather than base case. We score valuation at 5.0: genuinely cheap on cash earnings and yield-supported, but the discount is warranted by governance and dilution rather than a temporary error the market will reverse. There is no independent catalyst to close the gap.

## Competitive Landscape

Crescent competes in a consolidating U.S. E&P sector against larger, better-capitalized independents. Its differentiator is the KKR-backed acquisition machine plus a diversified multi-basin footprint and a growing minerals stream: a private-equity roll-up in public form. The strategy captures a real theme, but the external-management structure and persistent dilution are atypical versus operator-led peers such as Permian pure-plays.

Competitive trajectory is the relevant question, and it is mixed-to-negative on a per-share basis. Scale is growing (a plus for cost of capital and borrowing base), but the per-share value the company delivers to minority holders is eroding through issuance. Midstream takeaway constraints in the Permian and Uinta and Waha gas basis weakness are ongoing sector pressures the company shares with peers. The minerals carve-out is the most relevant competitive development: a separately-financeable, high-margin royalty stream that could crystallize value as a future spin candidate, though it is early. On balance the moat is not widening for the common shareholder; scale accrues while per-share economics leak to the Manager and to dilution.

## Risk Assessment

**Serial equity dilution** is the primary idiosyncratic risk and it is structural. Shares roughly doubled to ~330M in two years, and the fee mechanism rewards further issuance. The 2031 converts add up to 46.3M potential shares. This is an embedded feature, not a one-time event.

**External-manager conflicts.** The KKR Manager is not fully dedicated to Crescent, allocates only ≥70% of upstream opportunities to CRGY, and the Certificate of Incorporation waives the corporate-opportunity doctrine. The Manager owes fiduciary duties to other KKR vehicles that may conflict with CRGY holders.

**Leverage and RBL dependence.** $5.24B debt with semi-annual borrowing-base redeterminations tied to reserves and commodity prices; a distress-zone Z-score; rating-downgrade exposure. Reserve PV-10 uses $65.34/bbl, above the strip, so at strip pricing, PUD economics and the borrowing base could compress. $2.4B is needed to develop the 199.2 MMBoe PUD inventory.

**Integration and accretion uncertainty.** Filings warn the Vital merger "may not be accretive, and may be dilutive, to EPS." ~$79M in restructuring costs and midstream constraints are flagged.

**Governance red flags.** Controlled-company status, common-shareholder disenfranchisement on board elections, and KKR's ability to block any takeover cap the strategic-alternatives upside. Two new convertible-note-specific risk factors were added in the Q1 10-Q, and director Bevin Brown declined reappointment in May 2026 (stated non-dispute).

**Commodity two-sidedness.** Under the Iran binary, a verified Hormuz reopening ($85-95 crude) pressures upstream realizations and reserve value; a failure spike triggers further non-cash hedge marks and forgone cash upside on the ~68% hedged 2026 book. Neither outcome is clean.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Crescent is a cheap (~4.8x forward), oil-weighted FCF producer at 1.7x net leverage, with ~$120M of captured Vital synergies running ahead of plan, a de-risked maturity profile after the March refinancing, a ~4.6% dividend, and $336M of buyback authorization remaining. The GAAP loss is non-cash; adjusted earnings are growing (+21% net income YoY). Analyst targets ($18 median, $22 high) imply 67-105% upside. The minerals/royalties carve-out is a potential value-crystallization vehicle. If oil grinds at $100-120 and management prioritizes buybacks over issuance, per-share value could re-rate toward $14-16. Bull case: **$15**.

**Bear Case.** The fee structure guarantees continued dilution, sponsor holders keep selling into any strength, and the governance structure permanently discounts the multiple. Near-term estimates are already falling (next quarter -16.9%). A verified Hormuz reopening drops crude to $85-95, compressing realizations, reserve value (booked at $65.34/bbl above strip), and the borrowing base, while the ~68% hedge book still caps upside. Capex crowds out capital return, LFCF stays pressured, and the Z-score distress signal proves prescient in a downcycle. Bear case: **$7.75** (near the 52-week low of $7.68, ~28% downside).

The asymmetry that analyst targets suggest is real on paper but the discount is durable, not transient. The bull case requires the Manager to behave against its own incentive structure, an unreliable base for a thesis.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD (income hold)**. Reassessment at Q2 2026 earnings (early August).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A demonstrated shift in capital allocation away from issuance-funded acquisition toward net share reduction — specifically, a quarter of flat-to-declining share count combined with continued buyback execution.
- Current-quarter and next-quarter EPS revisions turning positive alongside confirmed synergy realization above the current ~$120M level.
- Oil sustaining $100+ with the hedge book rolling off, restoring the gap between realized and market pricing (moving realized oil back toward the $71 unhedged level).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A borrowing-base redetermination cut, or leverage rising above 2.5x on a covenant basis in a lower-price environment.
- A dividend cut or suspension of the buyback to fund acquisition/deleveraging.
- A verified Hormuz reopening driving crude below $85 with reserve/PUD write-downs materializing.
- Accelerating sponsor block sales or a large new equity-funded acquisition that dilutes per-share metrics further.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next checkpoint is the Q2 2026 print (early August) for synergy progress, capital-return cadence, and estimate-revision direction; the fall borrowing-base redetermination is the next material leverage input.

## Conclusion

Crescent Energy runs a healthy operating business: record production, growing adjusted earnings, disciplined maturity management, and a supportive dividend. The GAAP loss is non-cash and should not drive the call. The investable question is per-share value delivered to minority holders, and here the structure works against the shareholder: a Manager fee that rewards dilution, a share count that doubled in two years, a governance regime that disenfranchises common holders, and a sponsor that is selling rather than buying. These are permanent features, not temporary headwinds, and they explain the durable discount rather than signaling a mispricing.

The trajectory of our own scoring (6.0 → 5.0 over three months) reflects the market's recognition of these structural issues, and the current data confirms rather than reverses that trend: near-term estimates are falling, and the March-to-July price decline from ~$14 to ~$10.75 tracks deteriorating near-term earnings visibility rather than a pure sentiment overshoot. The ~4.6% yield and buyback provide a floor, and the options tape does not corroborate an imminent break lower, so this is not a short. We rate CRGY **HOLD** on its income profile. Own it for the dividend and cheap cash-flow multiple; do not underwrite an appreciation thesis that requires KKR to act against its own fee incentives.
