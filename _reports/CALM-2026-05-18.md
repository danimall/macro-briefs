## Executive Summary

Cal-Maine Foods is the largest U.S. shell egg producer (~24% of domestic consumption) trading at $77.68, down 39% from its 52-week high of $126.40. The stock has declined from ~$97 a year ago as egg prices collapsed from HPAI-driven peaks. FY2025 produced $24.95 in EPS on extraordinary pricing conditions; the most recent quarter (fiscal Q3 FY2026) showed revenue down 53% YoY, confirming the cycle has turned decisively. The company holds $1.39B in cash and investments against zero debt.

The critical question is whether the DOJ antitrust investigation (expected civil lawsuit alleging price coordination) fundamentally impairs the company's pricing mechanism during future supply shortages. If the DOJ prevails, Cal-Maine's earnings upside in the next HPAI event could be structurally capped, reducing the business to a lower-multiple commodity producer permanently. If the case is dismissed or results in modest remedies, the current valuation embeds excessive pessimism given the balance sheet's cash ($29/share) and the diversification strategy's potential.

At 5.3x trailing (peak) EPS, the stock looks cheap. At 13-19x normalized EPS ($4-6/share on a mid-cycle basis), it does not. The 15.3% short interest and 6.6 days to cover indicate meaningful institutional bearish positioning that introduces squeeze potential but also reflects informed skepticism about the DOJ outcome and earnings trajectory.

## Company Overview

Cal-Maine operates as a vertically integrated egg producer across 40 U.S. states, owning ~48.3 million laying hens and manufacturing 1.9 million tons of feed annually. The company produces 90.8% of its eggs at company-owned facilities, with the remainder sourced from contract producers. Customer concentration is significant: Walmart (including Sam's Club) accounts for 33.6% of net sales, with the top three customers representing 49.2%.

The company is pursuing strategic diversification into prepared foods through acquisitions: Echo Lake Foods ($258M, June 2025), Van's Foods (May 2026), and Crepini Foods ($6.75M for 51% stake, September 2024). These collectively represent management's effort to reduce the extreme cyclicality inherent in commodity egg pricing. Prepared foods contributed 4.6% of FY2025 revenue and will grow materially with Echo Lake and Van's integrated.

The governance structure changed fundamentally in April 2025 when the founding Adams family converted all Class A shares to Common Stock, eliminating dual-class control. The family's economic ownership declined from 53% voting power to ~6% of shares outstanding.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement:** FY2025 revenue of $4.26B (+83% YoY) and net income of $1.22B represent cyclical peaks driven by HPAI-induced supply shortages pushing egg prices to record levels. Net average selling price per dozen reached $3.134 vs. $1.932 in FY2024. Operating margins hit 36.1%, compared to 13.4% in FY2024 and 8.1% in FY2022 (the trough).

The most recent quarter (Q3 FY2026, ended March 2026) showed revenue of $667M, down 53% from peak quarterly run-rates, with EPS of $1.06 (beating the $0.82 estimate by 30%). Current-year EPS estimates of $7.93 and next-year estimates of $3.60 indicate the analyst community expects continued normalization.

**Balance Sheet:** The balance sheet is exceptionally strong. Cash and investment securities total $1.39B ($499M cash + $893M in A+ rated securities). Total debt is essentially zero (the $250M credit facility is undrawn). Current ratio stands at 6.4x. Working capital is $1.7B. The Altman Z-Score of 7.8 puts the company firmly in the safe zone.

Cash per share is approximately $29.40, representing 38% of the current stock price. The effective enterprise value (market cap minus net cash) is roughly $2.3B.

**Cash Flow:** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1.22B with free cash flow of $1.06B. The company deployed $330M in dividends (variable: 1/3 of net income), $54M in buybacks, $161M in capex, and $116M in acquisitions. Cash still grew $262M despite this deployment. The $500M share repurchase authorization ($450M remaining) provides downside support.

**Feed Costs:** Feed cost per dozen declined to $0.49 from $0.55 as corn (-8%) and soybean meal (-20%) prices decreased. This is a current tailwind but also removes a barrier to industry supply expansion, which is bearish for egg prices.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are weak on a 12-month horizon and uncertain on a multi-year basis:

**Near-term (0-12 months):** Earnings are declining. Consensus estimates show current-quarter EPS at $0.63 (down from $7.04 a year ago) and next-year full-year EPS at $3.60. The 90-day revision trend for next-year EPS is -33.7%, confirming expectations are deteriorating. The HPAI supply shortage that drove FY2025's extraordinary results is easing; the U.S. layer flock (285.5M) remains 9% below the 5-year average (313M) but depopulations have slowed materially.

**Medium-term (2-3 years):** The diversification into prepared foods (Echo Lake, Van's, Crepini) is strategically logical but unproven. Management acknowledges limited experience in prepared foods. These acquisitions could eventually contribute $400-600M in less-cyclical revenue, reducing the earnings volatility that compresses the stock's multiple. However, execution risk is real, and competitors in frozen breakfast foods (Kellogg's, General Mills) are well-established.

**Structural:** Cal-Maine's core business grows at roughly the rate of population growth plus any market share gains through acquisition. Organic growth in egg volumes is low-single-digit. The company's growth profile is fundamentally cyclical rather than secular.

## Competitive Landscape

Cal-Maine's competitive position is strong and stable. As the largest U.S. egg producer by a wide margin, the company benefits from:

- **Scale economics** in feed purchasing, transportation, and processing
- **Exclusive licensing** of Eggland's Best (~50% nationwide) and Land O' Lakes (~46% nationwide) branded eggs
- **Vertical integration** reducing cost and supply chain risk
- **Geographic diversification** across 40 states
- **Acquisition track record** consistently consolidating a fragmented industry

Market share gains come primarily through acquisition (ISE America, $116M). The egg industry remains fragmented (top 10 producers = ~54% of layer hens), providing continued consolidation opportunity.

The cage-free transition ($219M in construction projects) positions Cal-Maine for regulatory compliance across the 10 states mandating cage-free, though the pace of the mandated transition and related legal challenges introduce uncertainty.

Competitive threats include: private-label expansion by retailers, potential import competition (USDA $1B HPAI plan includes exploring temporary imports), and plant-based egg alternatives (still de minimis market share but growing).

## Risk Assessment

### DOJ Antitrust Lawsuit (Critical, Unquantifiable)
As of April 2026, the DOJ is reportedly preparing a civil antitrust lawsuit against Cal-Maine and Versova alleging price coordination through information sharing. A civil case (vs. criminal) limits penalties to injunctive relief and potential treble damages in follow-on private litigation. If the DOJ establishes that Cal-Maine's "market-based pricing framework" constitutes unlawful information sharing, the remedy could fundamentally alter the company's pricing mechanism during future supply shortages — converting exceptional upside cycles (like FY2025's $25 EPS) into modest ones. The range of outcomes is extremely wide: from dismissal (stock re-rates significantly higher) to a broad behavioral remedy plus private treble-damages claims (stock re-rates lower with permanently compressed multiples).

### Earnings Cyclicality
Revenue swung from $1.78B (FY2022) to $4.26B (FY2025) and is now collapsing back. Current-quarter EPS estimates of $0.63 represent a 97% decline from the year-ago quarter. The stock's volatility around earnings cycles makes position sizing critical.

### Customer Concentration
Walmart at 33.6% of revenue creates substantial bargaining power imbalance. The company discloses that customers are pressuring pricing frameworks, which could compress margins even in future shortage periods.

### HPAI Direct Exposure
While HPAI shortages benefit Cal-Maine's pricing, the company's own flocks are vulnerable. FY2024 saw outbreaks at Kansas and Texas facilities. A major outbreak at Cal-Maine's facilities would simultaneously reduce volume and increase costs.

### Governance Transition
The founding family's exit creates M&A risk (both as acquirer and target) and removes long-term stewardship certainty. The aggressive acquisition pace ($374M deployed in FY2025-2026) outside core competency raises execution risk.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 31.5% is slightly below the 33.6% historical volatility — fair pricing with no unusual premium. The put/call volume ratio of 3.21 in the near-term expiry is elevated, suggesting active hedging. Unusual activity in June $65 puts (4,018 volume vs. 168 OI, 23.9x ratio) at 37.4% IV represents either protective hedging or a directional bet on further downside. The term structure in contango and declining IV (34.1% → 31.5% over 10 days) alongside a 2.5% price increase suggests modest de-risking.

The 15.3% short interest with 6.6 days to cover is materially elevated. This level of institutional shorting typically reflects a specific thesis (likely: earnings normalization + DOJ risk). The high days-to-cover creates potential squeeze dynamics if a positive catalyst emerges (DOJ dismissal, acquisition bid, better-than-expected pricing).

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($95-100, +22-29% upside)
- DOJ case dismissed or settled with modest remedies (behavioral changes only, no structural pricing impairment)
- HPAI persists at low levels, keeping egg prices elevated above mid-cycle for 12-18 months
- Prepared foods acquisitions (Echo Lake, Van's) demonstrate revenue stability, reducing earnings volatility and warranting a higher multiple
- Share repurchases ($450M remaining) provide meaningful per-share accretion at depressed prices
- Short squeeze catalyst if DOJ news breaks favorably
- Valuation: 15x $6.50 normalized EPS (reflecting reduced cyclicality premium from diversification) = $97

### Bear Case ($55-60, -22-29% downside)
- DOJ lawsuit succeeds, imposing behavioral remedies that cap pricing upside in future shortage cycles
- Private follow-on litigation yields treble damages on FY2025 revenues ($500M+ potential exposure)
- Egg prices collapse to FY2022 levels as flock rebuilds fully, producing EPS of $2-3
- Prepared foods acquisitions underperform, with capital destroyed in an unfamiliar category
- Market re-rates to 12-15x trough EPS ($2.50-3.00) = $30-45 operating value + $29 cash = $59-74

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. DOJ case dismissed or settled for <$100M with no structural pricing remedy
2. Stock declines to $65-68 (where EV implies <8x even depressed mid-cycle earnings)
3. Two consecutive quarters of prepared foods revenue growth >15% with positive operating margins

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. DOJ case results in structural pricing remedy (e.g., prohibition on market-based pricing formulas or mandated cost-plus)
2. Cal-Maine flock sustains a major HPAI depopulation (>5M birds) while prices are already declining
3. Next-year EPS estimates decline below $2.50 (implying deep trough conditions + no pricing power recovery)

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely July 2026) + DOJ case developments. Any pre-trial ruling or settlement communication would be the dominant catalyst.

## Conclusion

Cal-Maine Foods presents a fortress balance sheet paired with a significant legal overhang. The company's $1.39B cash hoard, zero debt, and dominant competitive position provide substantial downside protection. However, the DOJ antitrust investigation introduces a binary risk that could permanently impair the company's earnings mechanism during supply shortages — the entire source of Cal-Maine's episodic value creation.

At $77.68, the stock prices in significant earnings normalization and some DOJ risk, but does not offer sufficient margin of safety to compensate for the legal uncertainty and accelerating earnings decline. The declining estimate revisions (-33.7% for next-year EPS over 90 days) reflect institutional consensus that the cycle has further to fall. The prepared foods diversification is strategically sound but 2-3 years from proving its value.

HOLD. The score trajectory (6.5 → 6.1 → current 5.86) reflects progressive deterioration of the risk profile as the DOJ case has advanced from investigation to expected lawsuit, while growth prospects have weakened as the HPAI supply shock dissipates. A more compelling entry would emerge either through DOJ resolution (positive catalyst) or further price decline to $65-68 (sufficient discount to normalized earnings to compensate for legal risk).
