## Executive Summary

Mosaic is a vertically integrated phosphate and potash producer trading at $23.73, within 3% of its 52-week low of $23.06, after a Q4 2025 that featured a $519.5 million net loss driven by surging sulfur costs and impairment charges. The stock has been rated HOLD three consecutive times with an overall score oscillating between 5.2 and 5.4. This report upgrades MOS to BUY based on a fundamental change in the external supply environment: the global fertilizer market is experiencing its most severe supply disruption since Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine, and MOS's valuation at ~14x trailing earnings (vs. 20.7x peer median) now embeds a margin of safety sufficient to offset the company's real operational weaknesses.

The core thesis rests on three converging supply-side catalysts: (1) Iran's indefinite suspension of all petrochemical exports, which directly impacts phosphoric acid and sulfur supply chains; (2) Indian urea tender prices reaching $1,000/ton (doubled), confirming that the global fertilizer price cycle has turned upward from its 2024 trough; and (3) non-renewal of Russian oil waivers, which constrains energy inputs for Russian fertilizer producers and tightens global nutrient supply simultaneously. These are confirmed market events with direct pricing implications for Mosaic's phosphate and potash segments. However, investors must size positions with discipline: Mosaic's negative free cash flow, $2.5B in growing environmental obligations, and high capital intensity mean this is a commodity trade, not a compounding position.

## Company Overview

The Mosaic Company is one of the world's two largest integrated phosphate producers and a major potash producer, operating through three segments: Phosphate (Florida/Louisiana mines and processing), Potash (Saskatchewan and New Mexico mines), and Mosaic Fertilizantes (Brazil phosphate, potash, and distribution). The company produces ~8.7 million tonnes of concentrated phosphate and ~9.0 million tonnes of potash annually, serving agricultural markets globally. Mosaic's Brazilian distribution platform, acquired from Vale in 2018, makes it the dominant fertilizer distributor in the world's largest fertilizer import market. The company also holds a ~$1.9B equity stake in Saudi Arabia's Ma'aden, acquired via a December 2024 share swap of its MWSPC joint venture interest.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Margins:** FY2025 revenue of $12.1B represented a modest recovery from the $11.1B trough in FY2024, but remains well below the $19.1B peak in FY2022. Gross margins improved to 15.8% from 13.6%, and operating margins reached 6.8% from 5.6%. The nine-month 2025 period showed stronger trends with operating earnings of $923M (+77% YoY), but Q4 2025 erased much of that progress with a single-quarter net loss of $519.5M driven by sulfur costs that roughly doubled (from $126/long ton to $272/long ton in North America, with BofA noting spot prices reached ~$500/MT late in Q4) plus $189M in impairment charges.

**Cash Flow:** This is the weakest part of the story. FY2025 operating cash flow was $825M against capital expenditures of $1.36B, producing negative free cash flow of -$535M. The capital intensity reflects aging infrastructure (gypstack remediation, mine transitions, plant maintenance). Mosaic funded the gap through commercial paper ($774M outstanding, up from $609M). The ~$280M annual dividend adds further cash drain. Share repurchases, previously running at ~$210M/9 months, were suspended entirely in 2025.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $4.5B against equity of $12.8B yields a D/E of 0.35x, which is moderate. The $2.5B revolver (extended to 2030) provides adequate liquidity. However, the $2.54B in asset retirement obligations, up from $2.21B in 2023, is a growing off-balance-sheet-like burden. Undiscounted gypstack closure costs for Florida and Louisiana alone total ~$2.3B. The RCRA Trusts hold only $734M to offset these, leaving a substantial unfunded gap.

**Earnings Quality:** The Ma'aden share swap created ~$407M in unrealized mark-to-market gains in the first nine months of 2025, materially inflating reported earnings. Adjusting for this and the $301M foreign currency gain (vs. a $267M loss in the prior year), underlying operating improvement was approximately $400M YoY — real but smaller than headlines suggest.

## Growth Analysis

**Volume Trajectory:** Phosphate shipments were 5.86M tonnes for 9M 2025 vs. 5.65M tonnes prior year (+3.7%). Potash shipments were 6.39M tonnes vs. 6.15M tonnes (+3.9%). These are modest volume gains consistent with a mature commodity producer.

**Pricing Inflection:** Phosphate DAP selling price (fob plant) averaged $498/tonne for 9M 2025 vs. $464 (+7.3%). MOP (potash) averaged $252/tonne vs. $227 (+11%). These price increases drove the revenue and margin improvement. The geopolitical supply disruptions currently underway — Iran petrochemical halt, dual waiver non-renewals, Odesa grain export threats from Russia's largest 2026 air attack — create conditions for further price appreciation through H2 2026.

**Premium Products:** MicroEssentials and other Performance Products generated $2.0B in revenue for 9M 2025, up 24% from $1.6B, significantly outpacing commodity product growth. This demonstrates pricing power and differentiation, though the segment remains a minority of total revenue.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current-year EPS estimates have been cut 32.5% over the past 90 days (from $2.46 to $1.66), and current-quarter estimates have been slashed 62.3% (from $0.57 to $0.21). Next-year estimates are more stable ($2.44, down only 5.3% over 90 days). The sharp near-term cuts reflect the Q4 2025 cost shock and Brazil restructuring charges. The geopolitical supply disruption may reverse this trajectory in H2 2026, but the timing and magnitude remain uncertain.

**Consensus vs. Geopolitical Reality:** Consensus FY2026 EPS of $1.66 likely does not incorporate the full impact of Iran's petrochemical export halt, Indian urea at $1,000/ton, or the Russian waiver non-renewal. If phosphate prices sustain above $550/tonne (plausible given supply constraints), FY2026 EPS could reach $2.00-2.50. If sulfur costs moderate from Q4 2025 peaks (which depends partly on whether blockade conditions persist or intensify), margin recovery would amplify the earnings rebound.

## Competitive Landscape

Mosaic's competitive position is structurally strong: it is one of only two major North American phosphate producers (alongside Nutrien), operates in an industry protected by countervailing duties against Moroccan and Russian imports (16.6% and 18.2% CVD rates respectively), and holds dominant distribution infrastructure in Brazil. China's phosphate export restrictions have been a key price support; if maintained, they continue to benefit Mosaic disproportionately.

The Canpotex joint marketing arrangement for potash exports provides oligopoly pricing discipline. Mosaic's potash cost structure has improved with the Esterhazy K3 mine transition, which replaces aging solution mining with more efficient conventional methods.

Weaknesses include: (1) higher-cost Brazilian phosphate operations, which management is actively exiting (Taquari sold, Patos de Minas sold, Araxa being idled); (2) dependence on sulfur, a commodity with limited substitutes and concentrated supply sources; and (3) the gypstack environmental liability, which is unique to phosphate producers and creates a permanent capital drag with no revenue offset.

## Risk Assessment

**Sulfur Cost Inflation (HIGH):** Sulfur costs more than doubled YoY and were the primary driver of the Q4 2025 loss. If blockade conditions persist, sulfur supply from Middle Eastern sources remains constrained. This risk cuts both ways for MOS: tighter sulfur supply raises Mosaic's costs while simultaneously constraining global phosphate production and supporting phosphate prices. The net effect depends on the pass-through ratio, which has historically been ~60-70% over 2-3 quarter lags.

**Environmental Liabilities (HIGH):** The $2.54B ARO growing at ~$100M+ per year is a structural negative with no obvious ceiling. Gypstack liner failures at New Wales (two in consecutive years) suggest ongoing remediation risk. The Cruz class action (elevated radiation on reclaimed mining land) could create precedent for broader liability if successful.

**Brazil Contingencies (MODERATE):** $1.4B in maximum potential exposure from labor and tax claims. The $217M indemnified by Vale provides partial offset, but residual risk remains material.

**China Export Policy (MODERATE):** If China relaxes phosphate export restrictions, global supply would increase and prices would decline. Management explicitly identifies this as a key risk.

**Negative Free Cash Flow (HIGH):** At -$535M FCF, Mosaic is not self-funding. Capex is unlikely to decline meaningfully given the age and environmental requirements of its asset base. The dividend ($280M/yr) is funded by debt until FCF turns positive.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($35-40, +47% to +69%):** Geopolitical supply disruption intensifies through H2 2026. Iran petrochemical halt persists, Russian fertilizer output constrained by waiver non-renewal, China maintains export restrictions. Phosphate DAP prices reach $600+/tonne. Sulfur costs moderate from Q4 peaks as alternative supply sources develop. Brazil restructuring reduces cost base by $100-150M annually. FY2026 EPS reaches $2.50+. Market re-rates from 14x to 16-18x as earnings visibility improves. Ma'aden stake provides additional upside if Saudi mining sector rallies.

**Bear Case ($18-20, -16% to -24%):** Blockade resolves quickly, Iran resumes petrochemical exports, sulfur costs remain elevated while phosphate prices decline. China relaxes export restrictions. Brazil restructuring generates additional impairment charges. Environmental liabilities accelerate. FY2026 EPS comes in below $1.50. Dividend cut becomes necessary given negative FCF.

**Base Case ($30-33, +26% to +39%):** Supply disruptions persist for 3-6 months, supporting elevated fertilizer prices. Phosphate DAP prices average $530-560/tonne for FY2026. Sulfur costs moderate partially from Q4 peaks. Brazil restructuring begins generating cost savings by H2 2026. FY2026 EPS of $1.80-2.20. Multiple expands modestly to 15-16x.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**

## Conclusion

Mosaic is a below-average business at an above-average entry point during an exceptional commodity cycle turn. The 14x P/E, 3.7% yield, and 37.8% discount from 52-week highs price in the Q4 2025 loss, negative FCF, and environmental liabilities. They do not price in the strongest set of geopolitical supply catalysts for fertilizer since 2022. The three consecutive HOLD ratings reflected a period where the macro tailwind was theoretical. It has now become confirmed in physical market prices. The upgrade to BUY carries important caveats: position sizing should reflect the negative FCF, the $2.5B environmental overhang, and the binary nature of geopolitical catalysts. This is a 3-5% portfolio position, not a core holding.
