## Executive Summary

Constellation Energy is the world's largest private-sector power producer (55 GW post-Calpine) and the nation's largest nuclear fleet operator (22 GW, 25 units). The company sits at the intersection of two powerful secular forces: surging baseload electricity demand from data centers/AI and bipartisan policy support for nuclear energy. Management has delivered 734% TSR since the 2022 spin-off from Exelon, and guides to 20%+ adjusted EPS CAGR through 2029.

The fundamental challenge is valuation. At $307-321, CEG trades at 27-29x 2026 guided EPS of $11-$12 — roughly 1.5x the peer median P/E of 23.9x for traditional utilities, before accounting for newly elevated leverage ($20B+ post-Calpine). Estimate revisions are broadly negative across all forward periods. The options market prices the stock in backwardation with 60.6% near-term IV (14.6pp above realized), confirming near-term uncertainty. Capacity market reform proposals, FERC co-location rulings, and Calpine integration execution all present risks that could delay or diminish the growth trajectory the market has already priced.

The stock has corrected 22% from its October 2025 highs of $412.70 and sits below the current analyst consensus mean of ~$368. The growth thesis remains structurally sound — nuclear baseload for AI-era electricity demand is a durable competitive advantage — but at current levels the risk/reward does not favor aggressive accumulation. A more attractive entry exists at $265-280, where the multiple compresses to ~23-24x 2026E and provides adequate margin of safety for integration execution and regulatory outcomes.

## Company Overview

Constellation Energy was spun off from Exelon Corporation in February 2022, creating a pure-play competitive power generator and retail electricity supplier. The company operates in five segments: Mid-Atlantic (33% of pre-Calpine capacity), Midwest (37%), New York (10%), ERCOT (15%), and Other Power Regions (5%).

The January 2026 acquisition of Calpine Corporation for ~$22B ($4.5B cash + 50M shares) transformed CEG into a 55 GW diversified power company with both baseload nuclear and dispatchable natural gas capabilities. The combined fleet includes:
- **Nuclear:** 14 stations, 25 units, 22 GW — 94.7% capacity factor (industry-leading)
- **Natural gas:** 28+ GW (primarily from Calpine)
- **Geothermal:** 730 MW (The Geysers, via Calpine)
- **Other:** Hydro, wind, solar, battery storage

The company also operates the nation's largest competitive retail energy business, serving ~2.5 million customer accounts including 75% of Fortune 100 companies, with 77% C&I renewal rates.

**Revenue and Profitability:**

| Revenue ($B) | $24.3 | $24.9 | $23.6 | $25.5 |
| Operating Income ($B) | $0.5 | $1.6 | $4.4 | $3.1 |
| Net Income ($B) | ($0.2) | $1.6 | $3.7 | $2.3 |
| Adjusted Operating EPS | — | — | $8.67 | $9.39 |

The FY2025 GAAP decline (net income $3.7B → $2.3B) is misleading. The Nuclear Production Tax Credit (PTC) dropped $1.76B (from $2.08B to $0.32B) because higher market prices pushed gross receipts above the phase-out threshold — meaning the fleet was earning more from the market. Adjusted operating EPS actually grew 8% YoY. This PTC "collar" mechanism protects downside but caps upside, creating natural earnings stability that GAAP figures obscure.

**Cash Flow and Balance Sheet:**

Operating cash flow was $4.24B in FY2025. Capital expenditures of $2.95B yielded free cash flow of $1.29B. Post-Calpine, the balance sheet has transformed:
- Long-term debt: ~$20B (from $7.25B pre-acquisition, with $12.6B assumed)
- Cash: $3.64B
- Credit facilities: $9.5B aggregate capacity
- Ratings: BBB+ (S&P), Baa1 (Moody's) — affirmed post-acquisition
- Nuclear decommissioning ARO: $12.9B

Capital intensity is elevated: 2026 CapEx guided at ~$5.7B, 2027 at ~$4.7B. The $5.0B buyback authorization provides capital return optionality but competes with deleveraging needs. The DOE loan guarantee of up to $1.0B for the Crane restart (Treasuries + 37.5bps, maturing 2055) provides exceptionally cheap long-duration financing for that specific project.

**Earnings Composition and Quality:**

The 2-for-4 earnings surprise record (including an 83% Q1 2025 miss) raises concerns about earnings visibility. The Q1 miss was driven by mark-to-market hedging effects and PTC timing — both non-operational — but it demonstrates how GAAP volatility can create near-term stock price dislocations.

## Growth Analysis

**Demand drivers are structurally sound:**

1. **Data center load growth:** CEG has secured 20-year PPAs with Microsoft (Crane restart) and Meta (Clinton). Data centers consume 10-50x more energy per square foot than typical commercial buildings, and hyperscalers require 24/7 carbon-free baseload — exactly what nuclear provides.

2. **Capacity market tailwinds:** PJM capacity prices surged 247-445% in recent auctions ($31/MW-day → $170/MW-day in ComEd). On a 12+ GW fleet, each $10/MW-day change represents hundreds of millions in annual revenue.

3. **Nuclear uprates and restarts:** Crane (TMI-1) restart adds ~835 MW. Clinton uprate adds 30 MW. Additional uprate potential across the fleet.

4. **Calpine synergies:** $1B+ in identified cost synergies; complementary gas fleet provides dispatchable backup and additional retail supply.

**Growth quantification:** Management guides to 20%+ base EPS CAGR through 2029, implying ~$19-21 EPS by 2029 off the 2026 $11.50 midpoint. Current year consensus at $11.64 (16 analysts) and next year at $13.60 (19 analysts) embed this trajectory. However, 30-day estimate revisions are negative across most periods: current quarter +4.9% but next quarter -5.1%, current year -2.6%, next year -3.3%. The 90-day picture is similarly mixed. This negative revision trend warrants caution — growth expectations may be moderating.

**Counterpoint:** The March 2026 Investor Day guidance miss ($11-$12 vs. ~$12+ consensus) was the first signal that near-term execution may trail the bull case. No new data center deal announcements at the event disappointed investors expecting continued deal momentum.

## Competitive Landscape

CEG's competitive position is among the strongest in the US power sector:

1. **Irreplaceable assets:** Building new nuclear is economically prohibitive ($10-15B per GW, 10+ year timelines). CEG's 22 GW fleet operates at 94.7% capacity factor and holds 60-80 year operating licenses. These assets cannot be replicated at any reasonable cost.

2. **Data center positioning:** The combination of nuclear baseload (24/7, zero carbon) with the ability to offer Hourly Carbon-Free Energy (HCFE) matching puts CEG ahead of competitors who can only offer intermittent renewable supply or annual matching.

3. **Post-Calpine diversification:** The 28+ GW gas fleet provides dispatchable capacity that complements nuclear baseload. Geographic diversification into ERCOT and California reduces PJM concentration.

4. **Retail scale:** 2.5M accounts with 77% C&I renewal rates creates a stable margin pool and provides proprietary demand signals.

**Competitive threats:** Vistra (VST) operates a similar nuclear + gas model and is also pursuing data center contracts. Advanced nuclear developers (Oklo, NuScale, X-energy) are years from commercial operation but could eventually offer new-build nuclear at lower cost. Natural gas peakers face long-term displacement risk from storage.

## Risk Assessment

**1. PJM Capacity Market Reform (HIGH)**
The National Energy Dominance Council is pushing PJM to reform capacity auctions, potentially creating separate procurement mechanisms for data center load or capping administered prices. Given that capacity price surges (247-445%) are a major earnings driver, any adverse reform could compress the single largest revenue tailwind.

**2. Post-Calpine Leverage (HIGH)**
Total debt approximately doubled to $20B+. While investment-grade ratings are affirmed and OCF of $4.2B+ provides coverage, the company now carries structurally higher financial risk at a time when the rate environment remains elevated. Debt service competes with growth capex and buybacks for available cash flow.

**3. Crane Restart Execution (MODERATE-HIGH)**
A first-of-its-kind nuclear restart project subject to NRC approval, interconnection challenges, and potential cost overruns. The Microsoft PPA has specific timeline requirements — failure to meet them could trigger penalties and reputational damage.

**4. FERC Co-Location Ruling (MODERATE)**
FERC found PJM's co-location rules "unjust and unreasonable" in December 2025, creating uncertainty around behind-the-meter data center connections. This could limit the addressable market for direct nuclear-to-data-center supply arrangements.

**5. Nuclear PTC Legislative Risk (MODERATE)**
The PTC mechanism (~$15/MWh max) provides a floor when prices are low but phases out when prices are high. Changes in gross receipts thresholds, budget reconciliation clawbacks, or program expiration post-2032 would alter the risk profile.

**6. Data Center Demand Uncertainty**
The 10-K explicitly acknowledges that data center demand "may be subject to technological change, shifts in customer behavior, regulatory developments, or changes in capital investment cycles and may not grow at the pace currently anticipated by the market." The stock's premium valuation embeds continued deal momentum.

### Options Market Signal

Overall, options positioning confirms elevated uncertainty without panic-level hedging — consistent with a HOLD rather than either extreme.

## Investment Thesis

- 2026 EPS comes in above guidance at $12.50+ as Calpine integration exceeds synergy targets and capacity prices remain elevated through next PJM auction
- Additional data center PPAs announced (2-3 hyperscaler deals) validate the nuclear-for-AI thesis
- Crane restart proceeds on schedule, receiving NRC approval by mid-2027
- PJM reform proposals are watered down or delayed, preserving capacity price tailwinds

- Data center deal pipeline stalls as hyperscalers find alternative supply (SMRs, renewables + storage)
- Calpine integration hits cost overruns; 2026 EPS comes in at or below the $11 low end of guidance
- Rising rates (10Y > 5%) compress utility P/E ratios broadly, pulling CEG toward peer multiples of 20-24x
- Lock-up expiration for former Calpine shareholders creates technical selling pressure
- Stock re-rates to 20-22x 2026E → $230-265

## Conclusion

Constellation Energy owns irreplaceable nuclear assets at the right moment in energy history. The combination of data center demand growth, bipartisan policy support, and capacity market tightness creates a multi-year tailwind that few power companies can match. Management has demonstrated exceptional execution (734% TSR since spin-off).

The issue is price. At 27-29x 2026 guided EPS with negative estimate revisions, doubled leverage from Calpine, and multiple regulatory uncertainties that could compress the capacity price tailwind, the current risk/reward does not support aggressive accumulation. The stock has corrected 22% from highs — partially reflecting these concerns — but has not reached a level where the margin of safety compensates for execution risk on a 3-year growth thesis.

The score trajectory (7.1 → 6.6 → 6.1 → 6.0) reflects gradually eroding conviction without invalidation of the structural thesis. The growth thesis is intact, the competitive position is exceptional, but valuation demands near-perfect execution that regulatory and integration risks make uncertain. Maintain HOLD with a clear upgrade trigger at $265-280.
