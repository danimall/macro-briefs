## Executive Summary

Wynn reported a strong-looking second quarter — revenue $1,856.9M (+6.9%), GAAP diluted EPS $1.32 versus $0.64, non-GAAP EPS $1.24 against $0.98 consensus — and the shares rose from $97.60 to $108.12 in the two sessions around the print. The quality of that quarter does not support the headline. Adjusted Property EBITDAR, the company's own selected performance measure, grew 2.9% to $568.3M. GAAP net income attributable more than doubled because change-in-derivative-fair-value swung $44.4M favorably (HKD/USD swaps and the WML convertible option) and foreign-currency remeasurement loss narrowed $33.5M, producing $77.9M of pre-tax non-cash movement against a $73.9M increase in net income attributable. The clearest evidence is that non-GAAP EPS of $1.24 printed *below* GAAP EPS of $1.32.

The Macau recovery is largely a win-rate event. Wynn Palace mass-table hold ran 28.1% in 1H26 against 23.5% in 1H25 (29.7% vs 22.3% in Q2). Re-running the disclosed mass drop of $3,871.0M at the prior-year rate removes $177.4M of revenue; doing the same at Wynn Macau, where hold moved adversely (16.1% vs 18.0% on $3,655.4M of drop), adds back $69.7M. Net hold contribution is roughly +$108M of revenue, which at ~60% flow-through (100% less the 35% special gaming tax and up to 5% levies) is ~+$65M against a reported $70.6M Macau segment EBITDAR increase. Volumes did genuinely grow — Palace mass drop +9.1%, Wynn Macau +15.7%, slot handle +22% and +31% — and a premium-mass mix shift can structurally lift measured hold, so this is not purely luck. A reversion of Palace mass hold toward 23-24% would remove roughly $100M of annualized EBITDAR, about 4.4% of the LTM figure.

The rest of the portfolio is deteriorating. Las Vegas 1H26 revenue rose 3.3% while EBITDAR fell 2.3%; Q2 margin dropped 331bp to 33.5% *despite* favorable table hold (24.6% vs 23.1%), ADR +8.4% to $583, and occupancy down 2.0pp to 86.3% in a market where 2025 Strip visitation fell 7.5% and Strip GGR was flat. Encore Boston Harbor EBITDAR fell 12.1% with margin down 284bp, into a UNITE HERE Local 26 / Teamsters Local 25 contract covering ~1,350 employees that expired August 31, 2026 with no disclosed resolution. Macau GGR fell 8.4% year over year in July, the second consecutive monthly decline. Consensus is responding: current-quarter EPS cut 10.3% in 30 days, next-quarter cut 8.9%, next-year cut 9.6% in 90 days to $4.94. At 20.8x that falling next-year number, with $2.83B of 2027 maturities and puts, peak Macau capex, $525-650M of remaining UAE equity, and the Macau 12% Complementary Tax exemption expiring 12/31/2027, the risk/reward is unfavorable. We move from HOLD to REDUCE.

## Company Overview

Wynn operates four consolidated integrated resorts and holds a 40% unconsolidated interest in a fifth under construction:

| Property | Ownership | Scale |
|---|---|---|
| Wynn Palace (Cotai) | ~72% via Wynn Macau, Limited (WML, SEHK) | 468k sq ft casino, 305 tables, 1,706 rooms |
| Wynn Macau (peninsula) | ~72% via WML | 294k sq ft casino, 253 tables, 1,014 rooms |
| Wynn/Encore Las Vegas | 100% (ex-certain retail) | 199k sq ft casino, 4,748 rooms, 510k sq ft meeting space |
| Encore Boston Harbor | 100% | 215k sq ft casino, 671 rooms |
| Wynn Al Marjan Island (RAK, UAE) | 40% equity, unconsolidated | >1,500 rooms, 225k sq ft gaming; opening Sept 2027 |

Three structural features matter more than the asset list. First, Macau is a separately listed 72%-owned subsidiary with its own debt and dividend policy; roughly 28% of Macau economics accrue to minority holders, so consolidated EBITDAR overstates what reaches WYNN shareholders. Second, book equity is negative: a $906.7M total stockholders' deficit at 6/30/26 (WYNN portion -$169.5M), the product of $2,784.2M of cumulative treasury stock at an ~$87.25 average cost and a -$1,245.6M accumulated deficit from the 2020-22 period. Third, growth spending is contractually mandated: the 2023-2032 Macau concession obliges Wynn Macau SA to invest MOP21.03B (~$2.62B), of which MOP19.80B (~$2.47B) must be non-gaming, under annual government-approved execution plans.

Wynn has also exited online gaming entirely (Wynn Interactive wound down in 2024; corporate-and-other revenue fell from $16.6M in 2024 to $0 in 2025), leaving it without the digital growth channel that MGM and Caesars use to offset flat land-based markets.

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory.** Revenue was $6,531.9M (2023), $7,128.0M (2024), $7,137.9M (2025) — flat in the most recent year. Adjusted Property EBITDAR fell 5.9% in FY2025 to $2,224.2M with *all four* segments down (Palace -$50.8M, Wynn Macau -$39.7M, Las Vegas -$44.4M, EBH -$10.4M). Diluted EPS fell from $4.35 to $3.14, driven by tax expense rising to $105.0M from $3.7M, interest income down $63.8M, and pre-opening up $29.1M, partly offset by property charges falling to $49.7M from $215.1M (FY2024 included the $130.0M DOJ forfeiture).

**1H26.** Revenue $3,713.7M (+8.0%), operating income $580.2M (from $533.2M), Adjusted Property EBITDAR $1,130.7M (+4.2%), net income attributable $260.5M (+87%), GAAP diluted EPS $2.36. Segment EBITDAR: Palace $405.3M (+27.0%), Wynn Macau $171.1M (-8.3%), Las Vegas $447.7M (-2.3%), EBH $106.6M (-12.1%). The 1H26 derivative swing was +$120.8M ($90.1M vs -$30.7M), roughly matching the $121.5M increase in net income attributable, though a portion of that gross swing is absorbed by tax and WML minorities.

**Cash generation is real and is the strongest element of the case.** FY2025 operating cash flow $1,352.7M, capex $660.4M, FCF $692.2M (9.7% margin). 1H26 OCF $645.4M, capex $332.1M, FCF $313.3M. LTM FCF ≈ $792M, a 7.6% yield on the $10.4B equity value. That is genuine and separates Wynn from a distress narrative.

**But 1H26 already outspent it.** FCF of $313.3M funded buybacks ($165.5M), common dividends ($95.0M), NCI distributions ($15.9M) and unconsolidated-affiliate investments ($178.6M) totaling $455.0M — a ~$142M gap bridged by a $200M revolver draw. Cash still rose $110.0M with help from $140.5M of investment maturities.

**Leverage.** Debt principal $10,825.5M at 6/30/26 (up $193.1M from year-end), cash $1,573.4M plus $527.4M short-term investments, net debt $8,724.7M. Against LTM EBITDAR of $2,269.6M that is 3.84x; less ~$138M annualized corporate expense, 4.09x; also treating the ~$141M EBH triple-net ground rent as a cost, 4.38x. Because ~28% of Macau EBITDAR (LTM Macau ≈ $1,156M) belongs to WML minorities, leverage against cash flow genuinely attributable to WYNN shareholders is higher still. For an asset-heavy gaming operator, 4.0-4.4x is within norms; the constraint is not solvency but flexibility.

**The 2027 stack.** Current portion of long-term debt jumped to $1,429.0M from $9.4M. 2027 items aggregate to ~$2,830M: WML 5.5% notes $750M, Wynn Las Vegas 5.25% notes $880M, Retail Term Loan $600M, plus the WML 4.5% convertible ($600M principal, stated 2029 maturity) putable 7 March 2027. With WML at HK$5.05 (down from HK$5.94) and the embedded derivative carried at $2.0M, the conversion option is deep out of the money, making the put the likely path. Mitigants are substantial: $2,380.2M undrawn revolvers, ~81% fixed-rate debt, covenant compliance, the June 2025 WRF extension to 2030, the July 2025 Cayman II accordion to $2.50B, and three-year Nevada shelf approval. The issue is cost — the weighted-average rate already rose to 6.17% in Q2'26 from 6.03%, and $283.9M of cash interest was paid in 1H26. Refinancing $1.63B of 5.25-5.5% paper at prevailing high-yield gaming levels plausibly adds $30-50M of annual interest, roughly $0.25-0.40 per share pre-tax.

**Credit quality within the casino book is loosening.** The allowance for casino credit losses reached 18.9% of gross receivables at 6/30/26 from 14.3% at 12/31/25, with the 1H provision more than doubling to $11.0M. Net casino receivables fell to $272.9M from $309.5M. This reads either as tightening quality among Macau premium-mass credit players or as a more conservative model against a shifting geographic mix; Macau gaming tax is assessed on the face value of credit instruments regardless of collection, so bad debt there costs more than the reserve implies.

**Earnings visibility is poor.** Over four quarters: -27.9%, -22.2%, -2.3%, +9.4%. One beat, three misses, and the beat came on a bar lowered by the prior misses and on results flattered by hold.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus itself is no longer extrapolating 1H26, which is a point in the bulls' favor. Current-year consensus of $4.72 against 1H26 non-GAAP EPS of $2.49 implies 2H26 of roughly $2.23. The Q3 estimate of $0.87 is essentially flat against $0.86 actual a year earlier. Next-year $4.94 implies only ~4.7% growth. The estimates are not heroic. They are, however, moving in one direction: -10.3% for the current quarter over 30 days, -8.9% next quarter, -9.6% for next year over 90 days.

**Macau.** Market GGR was $30.9B in 2025 (+8.8%) with visitation +14.7%, and 2026 ran at a double-digit pace through May (+6.7% in May). July reversed to -8.4%, attributed to the FIFA World Cup and typhoon disruption; Citi noted the first nine days of August ran ~12% above the July daily rate and Seaport expects year-over-year growth to resume in August-September. The transitory read is plausible. What is not transitory is that Wynn's Macau growth engine now runs on mass hold: VIP tables fell to 47 from 52 at Palace and to 10 from 21 at Wynn Macau, with VIP turnover down 12.3% and 58.1%. The pivot to premium mass is strategically correct — better economics, lower credit risk — but it makes reported results more sensitive to a variable management does not control.

**Las Vegas.** The market is not growing. 2025 Strip GGR was flat at $8.8B, visitation fell 7.5% to 38.5M, occupancy fell 3.2pp to 83.2%. Wynn is defending rate (ADR $583, +8.4%) at the cost of occupancy (-2.0pp) while contractual wage escalators and premium-offering investment consume the rate gains. A 331bp Q2 margin decline achieved with favorable hold is the single most concerning operating datapoint in the filings.

**Encore Boston Harbor.** Revenue -2.4%, EBITDAR -12.1%, margin 25.7%. A 25% GGR tax rate against ~6.75% in Nevada, competition from two Connecticut tribal casinos, two Rhode Island properties and MGM Springfield, and a 10-K acknowledgement that regional competitors may pay lower taxes or offer amenities EBH cannot. The union expiry, unresolved in anything we can verify, lands on the weakest property.

**The pipeline is real but capital-hungry and slipping.** Al Marjan opening moved to September 2027 from "2027," and the Aman-operated Janu to 2029 from late 2028. Life-to-date contributions reached $1.06B, yet the *remaining* 40% share was revised *up* to $525-650M from $450-500M — a filing-derived increase of roughly $220-250M in Wynn's equity requirement over six months, consistent with the ~$600M total budget increase management described on the Q2 call (externally reported, not located in a filing). Part of that may be definitional: the 10-Q language changed to "inclusive of capitalized interest and fees." Pre-opening expense is already $21.0M in 1H26 versus $38.5M for all of FY2025. Separately, The Enclave at Wynn Palace — 432 all-suite keys, $900-950M inclusive of capitalized interest, construction starting 2H26 over ~2.5 years — pushed 2027 Macau project capex guidance to $750-800M from the 10-K's $425-475M. Neither project contributes meaningfully to earnings before 2028-2029, and the UAE gaming licence was not confirmed in the filings reviewed.

| P/E on next-year consensus $4.94 | **20.8x** | estimate cut 9.6% in 90 days |
| LTM FCF yield | 7.6% | pre-2027 capex step-up |
| Dividend yield | 0.98% | $1.00/yr, ~$103M, well covered |

The profile's stated 23.42x for both trailing and forward P/E is internally inconsistent; it approximates LTM GAAP EPS of ~$4.17 (FY25 $3.14 + 1H26 $2.36 - 1H25 $1.33), which gives 24.6x. We use consensus forward multiples above.

Two adjustments matter. First, the LTM EBITDAR denominator embeds roughly $100M of annualized hold benefit; normalizing takes EV/EBITDAR to ~8.8x. Because that benefit sits in Macau, ~72% accrues to WYNN, so capitalizing it at 8.4x implies roughly $5-8 per share of value that is not durable if hold reverts. Second, adding the market value of the ~28% WML minority to EV would raise the multiple further; we cannot size it credibly because the WML share count could not be verified.

The deeper valuation problem is what happens to the earnings base after 2027. Three items converge: the Macau 12% Complementary Tax exemption expires 12/31/2027, having contributed to a consolidated effective tax rate of just 7.3% in 1H26 (a plausible $60-85M pre-tax charge on Macau taxable gaming profit, ~$0.42-0.59 per share net to WYNN, though Macau has extended this exemption in prior cycles); refinancing the maturing notes plus the Retail Term Loan at current market levels adds ~$0.25-0.40 per share pre-tax of interest; and the UAE equity-method contribution, arriving with heavy D&A and interest on a $2.4B project facility, plausibly offsets only part of that in its first years. Paying 20.8x for ~5% EPS growth in a business facing $0.70-1.00 per share of identifiable structural headwinds in 2028 is not a mispricing in the buyer's favor.

**Peer context.** The comparable set is heterogeneous and partly unusable: BYD at 3.7x reflects non-recurring items, CZR is loss-making at -13.2x. MGM trades at 26.2x, CHDN 15.2x, RRR 21.5x, median 21.5x. Wynn's 20.8x next-year sits at the median, a fair multiple for a premium asset base rather than a discount, and it carries higher leverage and more single-jurisdiction concession risk than most of the set.

**Sell-side.** 19 buy/strong-buy versus 1 hold, mean target $132.58 (+29%), median $134, range $116-145. Targets are being trimmed (Mizuho $133→$125, Stifel $150→$140) but remain well above the price. We do not treat these as independent confirmation: analyst targets function as a floor only when a stock is cheap against *rising* estimates. Here next-year estimates have fallen 9.6% in 90 days while targets have moved less, so the gap reflects stale anchors more than identified upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Wynn's positioning is the strongest part of this file. Eighteen Forbes Travel Guide Five-Star awards in 2026 (11 in Macau), the highest ADR on the Las Vegas Strip at $583 versus a market occupancy of 83.2%, and Wynn Palace as the largest Five-Star resort globally. In Macau, Wynn is executing the correct structural pivot — mass tables +15.3% at Palace, slot handle +22%, VIP deliberately shrunk — and gaining premium-mass share.

The trajectory, which matters more than the level, is mixed to slightly negative. Las Vegas is losing occupancy while raising rate in a market with declining visitation, and margin is compressing 200-331bp with hold in its favor; that is pricing power reaching its limit against union escalators. Encore Boston is structurally disadvantaged on tax and amenity scope and is losing EBITDAR at a 12% rate. The 2024 exit from online gaming ceded a channel that competitors use to grow in flat land-based markets, and the 10-K now treats regional sports betting and iGaming as competition rather than opportunity. Longer term, potential legalization in Japan, Taiwan and Thailand would compete for the same Asian premium customer.

The most significant competitive constraint is contractual rather than commercial: the Macau concession runs only to 12/31/2032, and from year eight (2030) the government may redeem it on one year's notice with compensation set formulaically at prior-year EBITDA times remaining years. That formula caps upside and effectively truncates terminal value on Wynn's largest EBITDAR contributor absent renewal, a structural reason gaming concessionaires trade at mid-single-digit to high-single-digit EBITDAR multiples rather than lodging multiples.

## Risk Assessment

**Hold normalization.** Roughly 90% of the 1H26 Macau EBITDAR improvement traces to table win-rate variance on our reconstruction. A move back to 23-24% Palace mass hold removes ~$100M of annualized EBITDAR. Counter-argument: the mix shift toward premium mass and the "Chairman's Club" ramp can structurally raise measured hold, and the opposite hold direction at the two properties argues against a single market-wide luck event.

**2027 convergence.** Against LTM OCF of ~$1.46B, 2027 is roughly cash-flow neutral to negative before any capital returns once maturities, peak project capex, UAE equity, and the tax-exemption expiry converge. Liquidity is adequate ($2.38B undrawn, covenant compliant, demonstrated 2025 refinancing execution); the cost is flexibility and the buyback.

**Off-balance-sheet UAE exposure.** Wynn Resorts and the RAK government jointly guarantee completion to First Abu Dhabi Bank on a $2.4B delayed-draw facility borrowed by Wynn Al Marjan Island FZ-LLC (Wynn is not a borrower). The guarantee terminates on payment in full or practical completion, no later than June 30, 2028. Downside is therefore not capped at the 40% equity stake, and loss-sharing mechanics between the two guarantors were not verified. A ~$45.1M district-cooling-plant facility carries a second guarantee. The 10-K also references "contingent equity credit support."

**Labour.** The EBH agreement covering ~1,350 employees expired August 31, 2026 with a separate security-officers agreement already expired since June 2025 and under negotiation. Status unverified. A costly settlement compounds a 284bp margin decline; a stoppage hits a property already losing share.

**Governance and negative equity.** No accounting equity cushion sits ahead of creditors. The Altman Z-Score of 0.92 is mechanically depressed by that (two components are zero), and should not be read as a default probability for an operator with $792M of LTM FCF and $2.38B of undrawn commitments, but it does describe a balance sheet with no book buffer.

**Unresolved items.** An 8-K dated 2025-08-19 carrying Items 1.01 and 2.01 (material agreement plus completion of an acquisition or disposition) could not be retrieved. The UAE gaming licence status was not confirmed in the filings reviewed. Both are verification gaps, not established negatives.

**Insider and capital-allocation signals.** No open-market purchases by any officer or director in the window reviewed. Buybacks decelerated to $128.8M in 1H26 (from $380.1M in FY2025) at an average $101.42, essentially the current price, with $326.1M of authorization remaining and part of the outlay funded by a revolver draw. Compensation metrics (Adjusted Property EBITDAR, GGR market share, Forbes Five-Star awards) contain no return-on-capital or leverage measure, a gap for a company with negative book equity committing $2.5B+ to new projects. Separately, 10% holder Tilman Fertitta has run a continuous program of writing call options against his stake from March through August 2026 (roughly 8.65M option-shares gross by tally, ~$38-40M of premium at $2.64-7.48 per share). Tranches overlap and are likely rolled, so the net position is unknown, and overwriting can serve financing or tax purposes with no directional content; we do not infer motive. The observable fact is that the largest individual holder has been monetizing near-term upside rather than adding.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 33.3% against 34.3% one-year realized is fair, and IV collapsed from 50.7% on July 23 to 33.3% now as the earnings event resolved, so there is no cheap-vol entry argument and no priced near-term catalyst. The term structure is in contango, with December IV at 36.1% above the 28.9% three-month tenor: the market is pricing more uncertainty into the window that captures the Q3 print and the Macau autumn trend than into the next several weeks. Put/call volume of 1.51 with the elevated put-demand skew flagged by the data indicates active downside hedging, while put/call open interest of 0.21 is unusually call-heavy — plausibly reflecting written-call open interest of the sort the Form 4 filings describe, which would make that low ratio a poor read on directional conviction. Short interest of 9.5% of float at 5.9 days to cover is meaningful but not crowded. Options positioning corroborates a range-bound-to-lower stance rather than either a squeeze setup or an imminent repricing.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($132, +28.7%).** July's Macau GGR decline was World Cup and typhoon distortion; the August daily run-rate running ~12% above July supports a return to growth, and Wynn's mass volumes (drop +9-16%, slot handle +22-31%) are genuinely expanding. Palace's higher hold is partly structural — 38 added mass tables and a premium-mass programme ramp — so less of the 1H gain reverts than our normalization assumes. Las Vegas cost increases are deliberate premium investment with a payback. 2027 refinancing is executed at manageable spreads using $2.38B of undrawn capacity, the Macau tax exemption is extended as in prior cycles, and Al Marjan opens in September 2027 into the first regulated UAE gaming market with a scarcity premium the market is not capitalizing. EBITDAR reaches ~$2,450M and the multiple re-rates to 9.5x on visible UAE cash flow. Sell-side mean target $132.58 sits here.

**Bear case ($78, -23.9%).** Palace mass hold reverts to 23-24%, removing ~$100M of EBITDAR; Macau GGR growth stays flat to negative on a softening Chinese consumer; Las Vegas margin erosion continues as visitation declines and union escalators compound; the EBH union settles expensively or strikes. EBITDAR falls to ~$2,050M. 2027 refinancing costs 150-200bp more than the maturing paper, the WML convertible put consumes $600M, capex peaks, UAE equity calls escalate again, and the buyback is suspended to protect the balance sheet — removing the marginal bid. At 8.0x on $2,050M with net debt rising toward $9.2B, equity is ~$7.2B, or ~$70; we set the bear at $78 to account for the option value of the UAE asset and premium real estate.

**Base case ($105, +2.4%).** Hold partly normalizes while volume growth continues; Macau returns to low-single-digit growth; Las Vegas margin stabilizes in the 33-34% range; EBITDAR settles near $2,200M. 2027 refinances at moderately higher cost, the tax exemption lapses on schedule with partial offset from UAE contribution. 21x next-year consensus of $4.94 is $104; 8.75x EV/EBITDAR on $2,200M less ~$8.9B of net debt is ~$100. Call it $105 including the 1% dividend support.

**Probability-weighted:** 45% base (+2.4%), 35% bear (-23.9%), 20% bull (+28.7%) = -1.6% price return, roughly -0.6% total return including the dividend. That is below a fair risk-adjusted return for a levered, 34%-volatility consumer discretionary equity, and the distribution is left-skewed. HOLD would require an approximately fair expected return, which we do not find, and AVOID would require thesis impairment or unacceptable tail risk, which the $792M of LTM FCF, $2.38B of undrawn liquidity and covenant compliance argue against.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: REDUCE.** Horizon 12 months, with the decision points concentrated between the late-October Q3 print and the Q4/FY26 report in February 2027.

**De-risking plan.** Trim existing exposure into strength above $105-110 rather than at the current level; the shares are 23.9% below the $134.72 52-week high and only 10.8% above the $92.52 low, so a great deal of the deterioration is already reflected and forced selling into weakness is not warranted. Target residual exposure at no more than half a normal position ahead of the Q3 print, which is the first clean read on whether Palace mass hold reverts and whether July's Macau decline was distortion. Income-oriented holders should note the yield is only 0.98% and does not compensate for the downside distribution.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD (any two):**
1. Q3 2026 Palace mass hold at or above 26% *with* mass drop growth of 8%+ year over year — evidence the hold shift is mix-driven rather than variance.
2. Macau GGR returning to positive year-over-year growth for two consecutive months, and Q3/Q4 consensus revisions turning positive after the current 8-10% cuts.
3. Las Vegas EBITDAR margin stabilizing at or above 34% for two consecutive quarters.
4. EBH union agreement settled on terms that do not push segment margin below 25%.

**Upgrade triggers to ACCUMULATE:** the above plus either (a) a price in the low $80s, which would put next-year consensus at ~17x and LTM FCF yield above 9%, or (b) completed 2027 refinancing at a blended cost within 75bp of the maturing coupons together with confirmation that the Macau Complementary Tax exemption is extended beyond 12/31/2027.

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Q3 or Q4 Adjusted Property EBITDAR below $500M (versus $568.3M in Q2), signalling hold reversion compounding market softness.
2. Suspension of the buyback or a dividend cut to fund 2027 capex and the WML put.
3. A further increase in Wynn's remaining Al Marjan equity requirement above $650M, or a slip of the September 2027 opening into 2028.
4. A funded draw on the FAB completion guarantee, or a downgrade that raises refinancing cost materially above current levels.
5. Confirmation that the Macau tax exemption lapses without offset while EBH labour costs step up.

**Next reassessment:** the Q3 2026 report in late October, with interim monitoring of monthly DICJ GGR prints (August and September), the EBH labour outcome, and any 8-K addressing 2027 refinancing.

## Conclusion

Wynn owns the best physical asset base in its industry and generates roughly $792M of trailing free cash flow, a 7.6% yield. Neither fact supports the current price. The 1H26 improvement that lifted sentiment rests on Macau table hold that we can reconstruct as ~90% of the segment EBITDAR gain, plus $77.9M of non-cash derivative and FX marks that pushed GAAP EPS above the company's own adjusted figure. Beneath that, the two wholly owned segments are losing margin — Las Vegas down 331bp in Q2 with hold in its favor, Encore Boston down 284bp into an unresolved union expiry — Macau GGR turned negative in July, and consensus has cut next-year EPS 9.6% in 90 days.

Against that, 20.8x next-year earnings and ~8.4x hold-flattered EV/EBITDAR is not a discount, and the 2027 calendar concentrates $2.83B of maturities and puts, $750-800M of mandated Macau capex, $525-650M of UAE equity, and the expiry of a tax exemption worth roughly $0.42-0.59 per share into a single year in which free cash flow is already inadequate to fund existing capital returns. The buyback — decelerating, funded partly by a revolver draw, and with $326.1M of authorization left — is the swing variable, and it is the first thing to go.
