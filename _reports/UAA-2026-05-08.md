## Executive Summary

Under Armour is a $1.2B athletic apparel company in the midst of a deliberate revenue contraction strategy—pulling back from promotions to rebuild brand premium—that has produced three consecutive years of declining revenue and a reported net loss of $201M in FY2025. The stock trades at $6.39, roughly 21% below its 52-week high of $8.15, with a negative P/E (EPS -$0.47) and 28.2% short interest.

Two credible bullish catalysts exist: (1) Prem Watsa's Fairfax Financial accumulated a 10%+ stake in January 2026 at $5.60-$6.30 per share (~$75M+ invested), providing a sophisticated value investor's endorsement of the turnaround; and (2) the Supreme Court's February 2026 ruling striking down IEEPA tariffs could convert a 160bps gross margin headwind into a potential tailwind via refunds. However, these catalysts must be weighed against the company's material weakness in internal controls, the $98.5M insurance litigation overhang, the Curry Brand separation that removes a key athlete association, and a core North American business declining at double-digit rates.

The underlying operating segments produce 15.5% operating margins when stripped of restructuring charges, but the path from "restructuring completion" to "visible earnings growth" remains unclear. With the stock priced at approximately 53x FY2026E earnings of $0.12 and 28x FY2027E of $0.23, and with the options market pricing 110% near-term implied volatility in steep backwardation, the risk/reward profile does not compensate adequately for the execution risk ahead.

## Company Overview

Under Armour develops, markets, and distributes performance athletic apparel, footwear, and accessories across four geographic segments: North America (~60% of revenue), EMEA (~21%), Asia-Pacific (~15%), and Latin America (~4%). Founded by Kevin Plank in 1996, the company rose to prominence on moisture-wicking performance fabrics but has struggled to maintain brand heat against Nike, Adidas, and Lululemon over the past five years.

The business model splits roughly 58% wholesale and 42% direct-to-consumer. Under Plank's returned leadership (he reassumed the CEO role), the company is executing a strategy of pulling back from off-price and promotional channels to restore full-price brand positioning. This choice creates a deliberate near-term revenue sacrifice in pursuit of long-term margin expansion.

Critical recent strategic decisions include: separating the Curry Brand ($69.7M in non-cash termination costs), selling the MapMyFitness platform, exiting the Rialto distribution facility, and expanding the restructuring plan to $255M total (from $160M initially).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue:** FY2025 revenue of $5.16B represented a -9.4% decline from FY2024's $5.70B. Through nine months of FY2026, revenue of $3.80B is down -4.7% YoY, indicating the rate of decline is moderating but still negative. North America, the core business, declined -11.4% in FY2025 and -10.3% in Q3 FY2026. EMEA (+9.2% over nine months) is the sole bright spot.

**Margins:** Gross margin improved to 47.9% in FY2025 (from 46.1%) on lower freight costs and reduced discounting. Q1 FY2026 reached 48.2%, the highest in recent years, but Q3 FY2026 dropped to 44.4% due to 200bps of tariff impact. SG&A inflated to 50.4% of revenue in FY2025 due to restructuring charges, litigation settlements, and the new headquarters buildout.

**Operating performance:** FY2025 reported an operating loss of $(185M) versus $230M operating income in FY2024. However, the four geographic segments combined generated $589.9M in operating income over nine months of FY2026 (15.5% segment margin), suggesting the operational business is functioning; the corporate restructuring/litigation charges produce consolidated losses.

**Cash flow:** FY2025 operating cash flow was $(59M) versus $354M the prior year — a massive deterioration. Nine-month FY2026 operating cash flow recovered to $257M, indicating improvement. Free cash flow remains negative on a trailing twelve-month basis.

**Balance sheet:** Cash of $465M (down from $859M, partly due to debt defeasance). Total debt of $1.0B ($600M defeased notes due June 2026, $400M new 7.25% notes due 2030). $1.1B revolving facility undrawn. Current ratio 2.1x is adequate. Inventory of $1.07B is growing (+13%) while revenue declines — a warning signal. Altman Z-Score of 2.48 places the company in the "grey zone" of moderate financial distress risk.

**Key concern:** The defeasance of the $600M notes removed near-term maturity risk but replaced ~$20M annual interest expense with ~$49M (the new 7.25% notes plus remaining obligations), increasing the annual cash burden by approximately $30M.

## Growth Analysis

Under Armour currently has negative growth across its primary metrics. Revenue is declining. Earnings are negative. The growth thesis rests entirely on the turnaround producing: (1) revenue stabilization followed by a return to positive growth, and (2) margin expansion from the reduced promotional activity converting to earnings leverage.

**Estimate revisions provide mixed signals.** Current year (FY2026) EPS estimate has risen from $0.05 to $0.12 over 90 days (+135.3%), driven primarily by the Q3 beat ($0.09 actual vs. -$0.02 estimate). However, the current quarter estimate is -$0.02 (down from $0.01 ninety days ago), suggesting an expected loss in Q4 FY2026. Next year (FY2027) is at $0.23 with minimal revision.

The 135% current-year revision reflects a beat against an extremely low bar (consensus expected near-zero), not a genuine earnings acceleration from a high base. At $0.12 FY2026E EPS, the stock trades at ~53x forward earnings. At $0.23 FY2027E, it's ~28x. These multiples would be justified only if the trajectory demonstrates sustained, visible improvement over the next 12-18 months.

**Catalysts for growth recovery:**
- Tariff reversal could add $100M+ to gross profit annually if refunds materialize and future tariffs are eliminated
- Restructuring completion (expected by end of FY2026/March 2026) should reduce one-time charges by $80-100M annually
- EMEA growth suggests international expansion potential
- Reduced promotional activity should improve full-price selling over time

**Against:** North America's double-digit decline is accelerating in the most recent quarters. Footwear fell -14% and the Curry Brand separation removes a pillar of that strategy. The market for athletic apparel in North America is mature and intensely competitive.

## Valuation Assessment

At $6.39, UAA's market cap is approximately $1.2B. The negative trailing P/E makes traditional valuation metrics difficult to apply.

**Forward earnings approach:** FY2027E EPS of $0.23 implies 28x forward P/E — a premium for a company with declining revenue, negative trailing earnings, and a Piotroski score of 2/5. The peer median P/E is 21.2x (GIII), though the comp set is limited and many peers are also negative (CPRI at -2.0x, OXM at -24.4x).

**EV/Revenue:** Enterprise value of approximately $1.7B ($1.2B market cap + $1.0B debt - $465M cash) on ~$5.0B FY2026E revenue implies 0.34x EV/Revenue. This is genuinely cheap for a branded consumer products company, particularly one with 48% gross margins when tariffs are excluded.

**Book value:** Total equity of approximately $1.1B implies roughly 1.1x P/B — not demanding.

**The Watsa floor:** Fairfax Financial accumulated at $5.60-$6.30. At $6.39, the current price is within Watsa's purchase range, suggesting a sophisticated value investor sees fair value at or above current levels. This provides a credible valuation floor near $5.50.

**Analyst consensus:** Median target $8.00, mean $7.73, range $4.00-$13.60. The stock trades 20% below the median target, with 5 Buys, 17 Holds, and 3 Sells/Strong Sells. This distribution suggests consensus sees modest upside but low conviction.

**Assessment:** The EV/Revenue metric is attractive, and Watsa's accumulation provides conviction that the stock is cheap on a multi-year normalized earnings basis. However, at 28x FY2027E earnings for a company that has not yet demonstrated it can grow revenue, the valuation requires a leap of faith on execution.

## Competitive Landscape

Under Armour's competitive position has deteriorated meaningfully over the past five years:
- Nike (~10x UA's revenue) remains the category leader despite its own restructuring challenges
- Lululemon has captured the premium athleisure consumer that UA once targeted
- Adidas has stabilized and begun recovering under new leadership
- On Running and Hoka have captured mindshare in the performance running category

UA's North American revenue decline of -11.4% in FY2025 is a share loss problem, not primarily a market contraction problem. The company is deliberately pulling back from promotional channels, but consumers have not yet demonstrated willingness to return at full price. Q3 FY2026 revenue still declined -5.2% despite the strategy being well into its second year.

The Curry Brand separation removes Stephen Curry as a brand pillar for basketball/footwear, weakening UA's position in one of the highest-visibility athletic categories. EMEA growth of 9.2% reflects lower brand penetration (easier growth) rather than competitive repositioning.

Supply chain concentration (10 manufacturers for 69% of apparel, 9 for substantially all footwear) creates vulnerability to disruption, though this is common among apparel brands.

## Risk Assessment

**Material weakness in internal controls** is the most concerning near-term risk. Disclosure controls are deemed ineffective, errors date back to FY2021, and remediation is incomplete. This creates filing delay risk, restatement risk, and undermines confidence in reported financials.

**The $98.5M insurance litigation accrual** represents 8% of market cap. The Fourth Circuit ruled against UA, and if rehearing is denied, prejudgment interest could increase exposure. Resolution timeline is uncertain.

**28.2% short interest (6.1 days to cover)** is extremely elevated. While this creates squeeze potential, it also signals that informed investors with borrow costs are making a considered bet against the stock. The divergence between Watsa's accumulation and the short thesis warrants attention.

**Inventory growing 13% while revenue declines** is a classic warning signal for potential future markdowns, which would contradict the stated strategy of protecting full-price selling.

**Deferred tax asset valuation allowance** on all U.S. federal DTAs means management itself does not believe U.S. profitability is sufficiently certain to recognize these assets. This is an unusually bearish internal signal.

**Kevin Plank's ~65% voting control** with minority economic ownership misaligns interests. CEO compensation of $11M during a year of $201M net loss raises governance questions. The proxy discloses pay vs. performance data showing UAA total shareholder return of 28.92% vs. peer group TSR of 42.36% — significant underperformance.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 109.9% versus historical volatility of 51.7% represents a 58.2pp spread — the options market is pricing extreme near-term uncertainty. The term structure is in steep backwardation (110% near-term vs. 58% six-month), which typically indicates the market expects a specific stress event or catalyst within the next 1-2 weeks. Put skew is elevated at 16.0%, confirming directional put demand. The 94th percentile IV reading within the current chain is consistent with institutional hedging or speculative put buying ahead of a binary event.

This options profile is unusual for a stock trading within its recent range and without an imminent earnings date (last earnings were February 6, 2026). The extreme near-term vol suggests something event-driven — possibly related to the material weakness remediation timeline, a filing deadline, or institutional repositioning around the short interest.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Material weakness remediation deadline or filing delay (uncertain timing, but each quarterly filing creates risk). Consumer spending deterioration through H2 2026. Next earnings likely late May/early June 2026.

**Timing window:** 3-6 months. Consumer thesis plays out May-August 2026. Material weakness resolution uncertain.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 28.2% SI with 6.1 days to cover creates severe squeeze risk on any positive catalyst (tariff refund announcement, earnings beat, strategic announcement). The Watsa position provides a natural buyer for further dips, reducing downside momentum potential.

**Borrow and options signal:** 110% near-term IV makes defined-risk shorts via puts uneconomical. The steep backwardation suggests the market already prices near-term downside risk heavily. Cost to borrow is likely elevated given the short interest level.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case $3.50-$4.50 vs. squeeze risk to $8.50-$9.00. At best, 2:1 reward:risk on the short, but the crowded positioning and Watsa's buying make this risky.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The bearish thesis has merit (declining revenue, negative earnings, material weakness, consumer headwinds), but 28.2% short interest means the trade is extremely consensus. Squeeze risk from Watsa's continued accumulation, tariff refund news, or any earnings beat makes this unattractive as a new short. The remaining downside to $4.00-$4.50 (30-37%) is insufficient relative to squeeze risk to $9.00+ (40%+ upside on short cover).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria for potential BUY upgrade:**
1. Revenue turns positive on a quarterly YoY basis (demonstrating the promotional pullback strategy is working)
2. Material weakness remediated and disclosure controls deemed effective
3. Price below $5.00 (closer to Watsa's average cost and book value, providing better margin of safety)
4. FY2027 EPS estimate revisions rising above $0.30 with positive trajectory

**Timeline for headwinds:** 12-18 months minimum. The restructuring plan completes by March 2026, but visibility into normalized earnings requires 2-3 quarters of clean results post-restructuring. The consumer spending environment is likely to remain challenged through at least H2 2026.

**Reassessment point:** Next earnings report (likely late May/early June 2026), which will provide the first look at post-restructuring quarterly performance.

## Conclusion

Under Armour presents a genuine turnaround story with two credible catalysts (Watsa accumulation and tariff relief) that provide a floor and potential positive optionality. However, the current stock price at $6.39 already reflects much of the optimism that drove the stock from $4.17 lows in November 2025 to the current level (+53%). At 28x FY2027E earnings — which themselves require execution on an unproven turnaround in a declining market — and with active risks including a material weakness, $98.5M litigation, inventory build, and 28% short interest, the position does not offer sufficient asymmetric upside to justify the risk.

The consumer discretionary sector faces structural headwinds from declining savings rates and weakening sentiment. UA's turnaround requires consumers to return to full-price purchasing of a brand that has lost cultural relevance relative to competitors. This is possible but unproven.

The appropriate stance is to avoid the stock at current levels and revisit if: (a) the price returns to the $4.50-$5.00 range where Watsa established his position (providing better margin of safety), (b) revenue turns positive (demonstrating strategy is working), or (c) the material weakness is remediated (removing filing/restatement risk).
