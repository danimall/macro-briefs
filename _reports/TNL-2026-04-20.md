## Executive Summary

Travel + Leisure Co operates a timeshare development and consumer finance hybrid business with $4.0B in annual revenue. The company generates substantial free cash flow ($523M in 2025) and returns nearly all of it to shareholders through buybacks (~$300M/year) and dividends (~$149M/year). At 22.8x trailing earnings, TNL trades at a significant discount to the Hotels, Resorts & Cruise Lines peer median of 34.9x.

The core risk is that TNL is, in economic substance, a $3.3B subprime consumer lender. Loan loss provisions have risen 39% over two years to $484M. The 2024 origination vintage has already generated $207M in write-offs — a 15% first-year loss rate. In an environment of record-low consumer sentiment, $4+ gasoline, and rising recession probability, the trajectory of this credit book is the dominant variable in the investment case. The stock has appreciated 89% from its April 2025 low of $40.29, pricing in substantial recovery that may not materialize if consumer credit deteriorates further.

## Company Overview

TNL operates two segments: Vacation Ownership (83.6% of revenue) develops, markets, and sells timeshare interests under the Wyndham, Club Wyndham, Margaritaville, and Sports Illustrated Resorts brands, while providing consumer financing at a 14.6% weighted average rate on a $3.3B gross receivable portfolio. Travel and Membership (16.5% of revenue) operates the RCI vacation exchange network (3.3M members) and B2B white-label travel clubs.

The business model centers on generating tours through the Wyndham Hotels loyalty program ("Blue Thread" relationship), converting those tours into VOI sales ($2.49B gross in 2025), financing ~60% of purchases, then securitizing the resulting receivables through bankruptcy-remote SPEs. Property management of ~230 resorts provides recurring fee income (~$879M).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:**
Revenue grew 4.1% to $4.02B in 2025, driven by 8.4% gross VOI sales growth (2.5% tour growth + 6.1% VPG improvement). Adjusted EBITDA reached $990M (24.6% margin, expanding from 24.0% in 2024). GAAP net income declined to $230M from $411M due to $233M in resort optimization charges.

Adjusted diluted EPS of $6.34 represents consistent progression: $4.52 (2022) → $5.70 (2023) → $5.75 (2024) → $6.34 (2025). The share count reduction from 78.4M to 63.1M (19.5% over 3 years) is the primary driver of EPS growth outpacing EBITDA growth.

**Cash Flow:**
Operating cash flow of $640M represents strong conversion, though it includes $484M in non-cash loan loss provisions. Free cash flow of $523M ($117M capex) provides comfortable coverage of ~$449M in total capital returns. FCF conversion is approximately 50% of adjusted EBITDA, consistent with management guidance.

**Balance Sheet:**
Corporate debt stands at $3.47B (excluding $2.12B non-recourse securitized debt). First lien leverage of 3.06x is within the 4.25x covenant. Liquidity is adequate at $1.15B ($253M cash + $893M revolver). Negative stockholders' equity of ($981M) is structural — the result of $7.7B in cumulative buybacks — not indicative of distress.

The $667M July 2026 maturity (6.625% secured notes) requires refinancing. Management demonstrated capital markets access throughout 2025 with multiple refinancings at improving terms, including a Q4 ABS securitization at 4.78% — the lowest coupon since 2022.

**Credit Portfolio — The Central Risk:**
- Gross receivables: $3.3B at 14.6% weighted average rate
- Allowance: $663M (20.1% of gross), up from 19.0% prior year
- Provisions: $484M in 2025, up from $432M (+12% YoY) and $348M in 2023 (+39% over 2 years)
- Write-offs: $439M in 2025 vs. $392M in 2024 (+12%)
- 31+ day delinquencies: 5.6% of gross vs. 5.3% prior year
- 2024 vintage: $207M write-offs on $1.39B originated (15% first-year loss rate)

This portfolio has subprime characteristics: high rates, limited collateral value (timeshare resale markets are thin), and a borrower demographic increasingly stressed by energy costs and inflation.

## Growth Analysis

**2026 Guidance:** Adjusted EBITDA of $1.03B–$1.055B implies 4-7% growth, supported by resort optimization savings (reduced maintenance fees on unsold inventory at exited properties) and continued VOI sales momentum.

**Consensus EPS estimates:** $7.38 for FY2026 and $8.49 for FY2027, representing 16% and 15% growth respectively. Estimate revisions are flat over the past 90 days.

**Growth vectors:**
- Share count reduction at ~6% annually provides a mechanical EPS tailwind
- VPG improvement (6.1% in 2025) from owner upgrades (67% mix, up from 64%) — higher-value existing customers spending more
- New brand launches (Sports Illustrated Resorts, Accor Vacation Club) expanding addressable market
- Travel Club transactions growing 13.8%

**Growth headwinds:**
- RCI exchange membership declining 2.9% annually — secular, likely irreversible as developers build internal networks
- Travel and Membership segment EBITDA declined 9.2% to $228M
- Tour growth of 2.5% is modest and vulnerable to consumer spending contraction
- Rising delinquencies may force tighter underwriting, constraining VOI sale volumes

- ~12.4x adjusted 2025 EPS ($6.34)
- ~10.6x FY2026E adjusted EPS ($7.38)
- ~7.9x EV/EBITDA on 2026 guided midpoint ($1.04B)
- 3.1% dividend yield

**Versus peers:**
- Peer median P/E: 34.9x
- Hilton Grand Vacations (HGV): 51.7x (most direct comparable; inflated by acquisition accounting)
- Choice Hotels (CHH): 14.9x
- Marriott Vacations (VAC): Negative (losses)

On adjusted earnings, TNL appears cheap at 10.6x forward, well below the peer median. However, the direct timeshare comparables (HGV, VAC) are both dealing with integration/restructuring distortions. The discount likely reflects: (1) perceived credit risk in the VOCR portfolio, (2) the negative equity/leverage optics, and (3) the secular decline in the exchange business.

**Analyst targets:** Low $78 (essentially current price), high $107, median $86.50. The consensus 10 Buy/2 Hold with zero Sells and a median target implying ~10% upside represents a moderately constructive sell-side view.

The stock's 89% appreciation from the April 2025 low has already captured substantial recovery. At the 52-week high of $81, the stock would trade at ~10.9x FY2026E — approaching the upper end of a reasonable range for a leveraged, consumer-credit-exposed cyclical.

## Competitive Landscape

TNL operates in a consolidating timeshare industry alongside HGV (post-Diamond Resorts acquisition) and Marriott Vacations Worldwide. TNL's competitive advantages include:

1. **Securitization infrastructure** — established bankruptcy-remote SPEs with improving execution (4.78% coupon in Q4 2025)
2. **Blue Thread relationship** — Wyndham Hotels' 106M+ loyalty members provide tour generation at lower acquisition cost than cold-calling
3. **Scale** — ~230 resorts, 800K+ owners, providing a base for upgrade-driven sales (67% upgrade mix)
4. **Brand portfolio diversification** — Club Wyndham, Margaritaville, Sports Illustrated, Accor

**Structural threats:**
- Short-term rental platforms (Airbnb, Vrbo) compete directly for leisure accommodation spend
- Developer internalization of exchange functions is disintermediating RCI
- "Exit companies" actively encourage defaults and disparage the timeshare model
- Rising insurance costs in climate-exposed resort locations (Florida, Caribbean) pressuring HOA economics

## Risk Assessment

**1. Consumer Credit Deterioration (HIGH):** With consumer sentiment at record lows and energy costs driving spending cuts in discretionary categories (per March/April 2026 data), the probability of accelerating defaults is material. A 5% increase in cumulative loss rates would add approximately $165M in provisions, eliminating ~16% of adjusted EBITDA.

**2. Refinancing Risk (MODERATE):** The $667M July 2026 maturity must be addressed within months. While management has demonstrated access, current credit market stress (HY spreads widening, private credit CDS active) creates timing risk. The company's BB-/Ba3 ratings provide acceptable but not comfortable cushion.

**3. Travel and Membership Secular Decline (MODERATE):** RCI's $939M in goodwill faces impairment risk if member attrition continues at 2-3% annually. The segment's declining EBITDA contribution may require another write-down cycle.

**4. Macro Sensitivity (HIGH):** Timeshare purchases are quintessentially discretionary. In a recession scenario (50-55% probability per current macro indicators), both new tour conversion and existing owner upgrade rates would decline. Management's own risk factor disclosure acknowledges tariff-driven uncertainty.

**5. Resort Optimization Execution (MODERATE):** Two HOAs haven't yet voted on restructuring plans. Additional charges of ~$15M are expected if remaining actions proceed. The $233M charge across 17 resorts suggests a meaningful portion of legacy inventory has depreciated.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($90–$107):**
- Resort optimization savings of $40-60M begin flowing through in 2026-2027
- Consumer credit stabilizes (delinquencies plateau, vintage performance improves)
- Share count continues declining at 5-6% annually, compounding EPS growth
- Multiple expands from ~10.6x to 12-13x as the market recognizes FCF generation quality
- ABS market continues improving, lowering funding costs
- New brands (Sports Illustrated, Accor) drive incremental tour volume

**Bear Case ($50–$55):**
- Recession triggers accelerating VOCR defaults (loss rates rise 3-5pp above current assumptions)
- Securitization market tightens, restricting new originations and widening funding costs
- Tour volumes decline 10-15% as consumer discretionary spending contracts
- RCI membership attrition accelerates, triggering goodwill impairment
- Refinancing of July 2026 maturity occurs at materially wider spreads
- Multiple compresses to 8-9x on credit concerns

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- VOCR delinquencies decline below 5.0% for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating portfolio stabilization
- Price pullback to $65-68 (providing 25%+ upside to $86.50 median target at reduced risk)
- Consumer sentiment reverses above 55 with measurable discretionary spending recovery

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Loan loss provisions exceed $550M annualized (implying 14%+ of existing runoff provisions)
- 31+ day delinquencies breach 7.0% of gross receivables
- Failure to refinance July 2026 maturity by May 2026 at reasonable terms
- Two consecutive quarters of negative VPG growth

**Reassessment timeline:** Q1 2026 earnings (expected late April/early May 2026) will provide the next data point on VOCR portfolio performance, tour trends, and refinancing progress.

## Conclusion

TNL is a well-managed timeshare operator generating substantial free cash flow with an aggressive capital return program. The valuation at ~10.6x FY2026E adjusted EPS is undemanding relative to both peers and the company's own cash flow profile. However, the stock has already appreciated 89% from its 52-week low and now trades within 4% of its all-time high. The consumer credit environment is deteriorating: record-low sentiment, rising energy costs, and increasing recession probability all point toward worsening VOCR performance. The consensus view (10 Buy/2 Hold) is priced in. The risk/reward at $78 is balanced: 10-15% upside to median targets against 25-30% downside in a recession scenario affecting the credit book. HOLD until either valuation improves on a pullback or the credit portfolio demonstrates stabilization through a complete quarterly cycle.
