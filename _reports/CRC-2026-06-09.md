**Research Date: June 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

California Resources Corporation is the largest independent oil and gas producer operating in California, having consolidated the basin through two all-stock mergers (Aera Energy, closed July 2024, and Berry Corporation, closed December 2025). The company runs a low-decline, oil-weighted (~81%) conventional asset base concentrated in the San Joaquin and Los Angeles basins, plus a newly acquired Utah Uinta foothold, and a pre-revenue carbon capture business (Carbon TerraVault) developed in a 51/49 joint venture with Brookfield. The core of the equity thesis is free cash flow and capital return: 2025 free cash flow of $543M (18.7% FCF margin) on top of roughly $1.62B returned to shareholders since mid-2021, against a market cap near $5.2B.

The headline numbers are noisy and require interpretation. The reported Q1 2026 GAAP net loss of $711M is almost entirely a non-cash artifact: an $848M mark-to-market loss on oil hedges when Brent forward prices spiked roughly 40% late in the quarter on Middle East / Strait of Hormuz disruption. Adjusted net income was a positive $79M ($0.88/sh), operating cash flow was positive $99M, and management raised full-year Adjusted EBITDAX guidance 42% to a ~$1,450M midpoint in the same release. The trailing negative P/E and the distress-zone Altman Z-Score (1.27) both reflect these non-cash items rather than business deterioration. Estimate revisions are sharply positive on near-term quarters (current-year consensus EPS up 307.4% over 90 days to $6.35), driven by the oil-price move and the guidance raise.

The case for restraint is equally concrete. CRC sells nearly all of its crude in-state, and California's marketing infrastructure is in flux: Valero's Benicia refinery ceased buying crude and reportedly closed in April 2026, the San Pablo Bay Pipeline shut down, and a federally ordered Sable Offshore restart (~60,000 bbl/d) adds competing in-state supply and takeaway strain. The 65% oil hedge at a ~$65 floor protects downside cash flow but caps participation above ~$83 Brent. Canada Pension Plan Investment Board sold ~$214M of stock in March 2026, and four holders still own ~41% of the float. The stock trades roughly 18% below the low end of the analyst target range, but that discount is compensation for genuine California-specific risk rather than a clean mispricing. We rate CRC a HOLD.

## Company Overview

CRC operates two segments. The **Oil & Natural Gas** segment is the value driver: conventional, low-decline production concentrated in the San Joaquin Basin (the core, ~96 MBbl/d oil), the Los Angeles Basin (Wilmington field, ~17 MBbl/d under production-sharing contracts), the Sacramento Basin (gas), and the Uinta Basin in Utah added via Berry. Production is heavily oil-weighted, and CRC owns supporting infrastructure including the Elk Hills power plant and a cryogenic gas-processing facility. The **Carbon Management (Carbon TerraVault / CTV)** segment is a pre-revenue carbon capture and storage business preparing for California's first CO₂ injection at the Elk Hills cryogenic plant, expected spring 2026.

CRC's strategy is consolidation in a structurally supply-short, regulation-constrained market. California imports much of its crude, so in-state barrels earn premium realizations (oil at ~96-104% of Brent/WTI; in-state NGLs and gas trade at premiums to broader US markets). CalGEM permitting, CEQA, and bonding requirements keep competition out and make CRC the natural buyer of in-state assets. Aera and Berry made CRC the dominant in-state producer, lifting net production from 86 MBoe/d (2023) to 138 MBoe/d (2025) and to 154 MBoe/d in Q1 2026, with a guided 2026 exit rate near 175 MBoe/d gross.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and production.** Oil/gas/NGL sales rose from $2,155M (2023) to $2,537M (2024) to $2,910M (2025); total operating revenues reached $3,669M in 2025. The growth is M&A-driven: total assets grew from $4.0B (2023) to $7.4B (2025) as Aera and Berry were consolidated. Net production climbed sequentially across all three years and into Q1 2026.

**Cash generation.** 2025 operating cash flow was $865M, capex $322M, and free cash flow $543M (18.7% margin). This is a free-cash-flow and capital-return business that happens to be an E&P. Since mid-2021, CRC has returned roughly $1.62B to shareholders ($1.18B buybacks, $0.44B dividends), bought back 8.3M shares for $377M in 2025 alone, and carries $600M remaining on a $1.78B buyback authorization. The dividend is $0.405/quarter ($1.62 annualized, ~2.8% yield at the current price), well-covered by free cash flow.

**The Q1 2026 loss is non-cash.** The GAAP loss stems almost entirely from the derivative mark when Brent 12-month forward jumped to $83.66 from $60.30. Adjusted net income of $79M was 2.4% above the $0.86 consensus on an adjusted basis, Adjusted EBITDAX was $304M, and operating cash flow stayed positive at $99M. The hedge loss is economically offset by the higher value of unhedged production and future realizations. The mark drove equity from $3.67B (Dec 2025) to $2.92B (Mar 2026) and created a $582M net derivative liability, which is what depresses the leverage ratios and the Altman Z-Score.

**Balance sheet.** Long-term debt is ~$1.3B and well-laddered: 2029 notes (8.25%, $550M after a March 2026 partial redemption) and 2034 notes (7.00%, $750M after a follow-on). The 2026 notes were fully retired. The revolver borrowing base was reaffirmed at $1.5B in April 2026 with a 25bp pricing cut, and the company is in covenant compliance. The notable weakness is thin cash ($40M at March 31, 2026) against ~$1.28B of total liquidity (mostly revolver). FCF was a slightly negative -$32M in Q1 on capex acceleration, a function of the H2 rig ramp rather than distress.

The Altman Z of 1.27 ("distress") and the current ratio of 0.89 are misleading here. Both are dragged by the derivative liability and the Q4 2025 Sacramento gas impairment. The Piotroski signals are constructive (positive net income, ROA, OCF, and OCF > NI), and management explicitly targets low leverage. Bankruptcy is not the binding risk; commodity, hedging, and California marketing exposure are.

## Growth Analysis

Growth has two engines. The first is M&A-driven scale: Berry added oil-weighted San Joaquin reserves, the Utah Uinta assets, and well-servicing operations, with the synergy target raised 12% to $90-100M. The second is the organic ramp enabled by SB 237, which restarted Kern County well permitting in early 2026. CRC secured 66 new well permits in Q1 2026 and now holds permits for a 7-rig H2 2026 program (6 in California, 1 in Utah), supporting the guided exit rate near 175 MBoe/d gross.

Estimate revisions are strongly positive on near-term horizons: current-quarter EPS revised +12.3% (30d) and +633.6% (90d) to $1.76; current-year revised +12.0% (30d) to $6.35. These magnitudes meet the revision-velocity threshold that often precedes continued outperformance. The bulk of the 90-day revision is mechanical: it reflects the late-quarter oil-price spike feeding into realizations and the EBITDAX guidance raise, not a durable demand inflection. The signal weakens at the longer horizon: next-year EPS was revised down 10.7% over 30 days to $4.44, consistent with the market pricing oil back toward a normalized level and the hedge book capping upside above ~$83 Brent.

The 2026 Adjusted EBITDAX guidance anchors the growth case. Against capex of $520-560M, it supports continued free cash flow and capital return even with the elevated rig program. Carbon TerraVault offers long-dated optionality but contributes nothing to near-term value (segment loss ~$86M in 2025) and depends on fragile federal/state incentives.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is meaningless given the non-cash GAAP loss. On forward earnings the picture is more useful but ambiguous because consensus is split between an oil-spike year and a normalized year. At $60 against a $6.35 current-year EPS estimate, CRC trades at ~9.4x, but that estimate embeds elevated realizations. On the more normalized next-year $4.44 estimate, the multiple is ~13.5x. The peer median P/E is 16.0 (MGY 16.0, MTDR 13.8, MUR 66.1, CNX 4.6, CRGY negative). On a normalized basis CRC sits modestly below the median, which is defensible given its in-state premium pricing but also reflects the California regulatory and marketing-infrastructure overhang.

The cleaner anchor is free cash flow and capital return. A ~$5.2B market cap against $543M of 2025 FCF is a ~10% FCF yield, with a 2.8% dividend yield and an active buyback. On the raised 2026 EBITDAX midpoint, the EV/EBITDAX is roughly 4x (using ~$1.3B net debt plus the derivative liability), reasonable for a low-decline asset base.

Analyst targets (low $72, mean $82.45, high $91) sit 23-56% above the current price. The sentiment trend is flagged as degrading, and the targets are stale relative to the fast-moving California marketing developments. This is a commodity-and-regulation name: the hedge book caps upside, the differentials story is a live downside risk, and large holders are trimming. The targets are a reasonable signal of fair value in a benign scenario rather than a floor. The valuation is fair-to-modestly-attractive, supporting a hold rather than a high-conviction buy.

## Competitive Landscape

CRC's competitive position has genuinely improved, consistent with the upward trajectory in this dimension across prior reports. The Aera and Berry mergers made it the dominant in-state operator in a market where "competitors are few" and where permitting/bonding requirements create a high barrier to entry. In-state production earns premium realizations because California refiners need local barrels, and regulatory friction keeps new entrants out, a structural advantage CRC has deliberately exploited as the natural consolidator.

The near-term competitive dynamic is shifting on the demand side. Refinery and pipeline closures (Benicia/Valero, San Pablo Bay) reduce in-state crude demand, and the Sable Offshore restart adds competing supply, both of which could pressure differentials. The longer-term secular headwind is the structural decline of California oil demand (EV adoption, state climate mandates), which is why CRC is positioning Carbon TerraVault to monetize pore space, mineral rights, and power assets. The moat is real and the trajectory is positive, but the demand-side erosion of the in-state premium is the offsetting risk to watch.

## Risk Assessment

1. **California marketing infrastructure (most immediate, idiosyncratic).** The refinery/pipeline/Sable cluster could widen CRC's crude differentials. Management reports no negative pricing impact yet, but this is the variable most likely to move the stock that the market is not fully pricing.

2. **Geographic concentration and regulation.** Nearly all production is in California under a hostile regulatory regime. SB 237 restarted permitting (a real positive), but there is no assurance it continues at current levels.

3. **Hedging.** ~65% of 2026 oil is hedged at a ~$65 floor with sold calls capping upside ~$83. This protects downside cash flow but caps spike participation and drives large GAAP swings. A $10/bbl Brent increase raises 2026 settlement payments by ~$195M.

4. **Asset retirement obligations.** ARO of ~$1.03B; a 1% inflation change moves the liability ~$90M. Legacy disputes include Signal Hill (~$18M accrued, Oxy indemnity) and a $25M CalGEM Cat Canyon orphan-well dispute.

5. **Carbon management execution.** CTV is pre-revenue, depends on Class VI permits, 45Q/LCFS credits, and unresolved CO₂-pipeline rules, faces activist litigation, and shares control with Brookfield (put/call rights = $120M contingent liability).

6. **Ownership concentration and large-holder selling.** Four holders own ~41% of the float; CPPIB sold ~$214M in March 2026. Block sales pressure the stock independent of fundamentals.

7. **Thin cash.** $40M cash means reliance on the revolver and operating cash flow; little buffer for shocks.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 46.5% versus 35.7% one-year historical volatility, rich by ~10.8pp, indicating the market is pricing elevated uncertainty (consistent with the oil/Hormuz backdrop and the marketing-infrastructure cluster). The term structure is flat (near 46.5% ≈ far 45.4%), so uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a near-term catalyst. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.4%) and OI is call-heavy (P/C OI 0.14), so options positioning is not signaling directional bearishness despite the analyst sentiment downgrade. Implied move on the nearest expiry is ±6.1%. Short interest is modest at 5.0% of float (3.4 days to cover), so neither a crowded short nor meaningful squeeze fuel. The options market is pricing volatility, not a directional bet, which fits a hold.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** SB 237 permitting holds, the 7-rig program lifts production toward the exit rate, and California differentials absorb the refinery/pipeline disruptions without material widening. Oil stays firm; the 2026 EBITDAX midpoint proves conservative; free cash flow funds continued buybacks at attractive prices; Berry synergies hit the raised target; and Carbon TerraVault's first CO₂ injection validates the CCS optionality. The stock re-rates toward the analyst range. Bull target ~$85-90.

**Bear case.** The Benicia/San Pablo closures plus the Sable restart widen in-state crude differentials, compressing the realization premium that underpins the thesis. Oil normalizes back toward $60-65, capping the hedged book's value and pulling next-year estimates lower (the -10.7% 30-day revision is an early sign). Permitting stalls again; ARO and California bonding costs grind on cash; large holders continue trimming. The stock retraces toward the low-to-mid $40s, near its 52-week low. Bear target ~$44-46.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, early August), which will show the first clean read on whether the refinery/pipeline closures affected realized differentials, plus the H2 rig-program execution.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 2026 confirms in-state crude differentials held flat-to-better despite the closures, with realizations sustaining ~96-104% of benchmark.
- Production tracks toward the exit rate with the 7-rig program on schedule and free cash flow positive, supporting continued buybacks.
- Stock pulls back toward the low-$50s on unchanged fundamentals, widening the FCF-yield and capital-return cushion.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Clear evidence in Q2/Q3 that California differentials are widening materially (realizations falling below ~90% of benchmark) on the refinery/pipeline disruption.
- SB 237 permitting reverses or stalls, forcing a cut to the rig program and 2026 guidance.
- Next-year consensus EPS continues to fall (the -10.7% 30-day revision extends), or management guides EBITDAX lower, signaling the oil-spike tailwind has fully reversed without an offsetting volume story.

## Conclusion

CRC is a well-run, free-cash-flow-generative consolidator with a genuine in-state competitive advantage, a meaningful guidance raise, and a regulatory tailwind from SB 237, a rare positive in California. The fundamentals are stronger than the headline GAAP loss and distress-zone screens suggest, and the stock trades below the consensus target range. But the discount is earned: the California refinery/pipeline cluster is a live, idiosyncratic threat to the realization premium that is the heart of the thesis; the hedge book caps upside; cash is thin; and large legacy holders are reducing exposure. The near-term estimate surge is largely an oil-price artifact, and the longer-dated revision has already turned negative. The next quarter's differential data is decisive for this setup. We rate CRC a **HOLD** (growth-oriented), and would look to upgrade on either a confirmed differential hold-up or a pullback toward the low-$50s.
