**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Nasdaq has diversified from a U.S. equity venue into a financial-technology and data business, with recurring index, data, and software revenue now a majority of the mix. FY2025 was strong on every operating line: net revenue (net of transaction rebates) rose 12.9% to $5,249M, operating income rose 29.7% to $2,331M for a ~44% margin on net revenue, and non-GAAP diluted EPS grew ~23% to $3.48. The Index franchise reached a record $882B in ETP AUM on $99B of net inflows, listings won a record $1.2T of market-cap switches, and Financial Technology ARR grew 14%. Free cash flow was $1,989M on ~$266M of capex, an asset-light FCF margin that funds a growing dividend and steady deleveraging of the ~$9B Adenza/Verafin acquisition debt.

The stock has de-rated from a January 2026 high of ~$101 to ~$85, roughly 17% off the peak, and now trades at ~25x forward EPS versus a Financial Exchanges & Data peer median near 31.7x (MSCI 34, MCO 31.7, CBOE 25.3, ICE 20.2). That discount is earned. Two dated regulatory items dominate the 2026-2027 setup: the SEC access-fee rule, whose challenge Nasdaq lost in October 2025 and which takes effect after temporary relief expires in November 2026, and the vacated CAT funding model that leaves a $99M net receivable at risk. Management states the access-fee rule "may adversely impact our business and revenue," directly targeting Market Services economics. Trading share is also eroding: U.S. cash-equity matched share fell to 14.2% from 15.6%, and options share to 29.1% from 29.8%.

The result is a balanced setup. The mix-shift thesis toward higher-multiple recurring revenue is real and durable, the valuation discount to peers is genuine, and Investor AB (the Wallenberg vehicle) added shares near $86 in June 2026. Consensus estimates are stable rather than rising (current-year EPS revised only +0.8% over 90 days), the November 2026 rule is a binary revenue overhang that consensus has not fully modeled, and the prior score trajectory shows a recommendation that has oscillated between HOLD and BUY without a decisive catalyst. We rate NDAQ a HOLD with a growth bias.

## Company Overview

Nasdaq reports in three segments:

**Capital Access Platforms** (FY25 revenue $2,137M, +9.9%) houses Data & Listing Services ($804M), Index ($827M), and Workflow & Insights ($506M). The Nasdaq-100 index franchise is the crown jewel, with $882B of ETP AUM tracking Nasdaq indices ($640B in Nasdaq-100 core products) at an all-time high. The Nasdaq Stock Market lists 4,480 companies and remains #1 by U.S. IPO proceeds for the seventh consecutive year.

**Financial Technology** (FY25 revenue $1,850M, +14.1%) is the strategic growth engine, built on the 2021 Verafin (anti-financial-crime) and 2023 Adenza (AxiomSL regulatory reporting plus Calypso capital-markets/risk software) acquisitions. It comprises Financial Crime Management Technology ($331M), Regulatory Technology ($428M), and Capital Markets Technology ($1,091M), serving ~3,800 clients including all G-SIBs across 135+ marketplaces in 55+ countries. AxiomSL revenue is recognized ratably given mandatory regulatory updates, which anchors recurring, high-switching-cost relationships.

**Market Services** (FY25 gross revenue $4,214M; net $1,201M, +17.7%) runs U.S. options (largest multi-listed share), U.S. cash equities (largest single U.S. cash-equity venue), Nordic/Baltic exchanges, and clearing. This is the cyclical, price-competitive, share-eroding business the strategic pivot is designed to diversify away from.

The company's competitive moats are strongest in listings network effects, the Nasdaq-100 brand tied to secular passive/ETP flows, SRO regulatory status, proprietary market data (TotalView), and mission-critical Adenza bank software. Its weakest position is in transaction execution, where MEMX, MIAX, Cboe, and NYSE compete on price and rebates.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue growth has been consistent. Total revenue moved from $6,064M (2023) to $7,400M (2024) to $8,262M (2025); on a net-revenue basis (the cleaner metric that strips transaction rebates) the path was $3,895M → $4,649M → $5,249M.

Reported net income jumped 60.1% to $1,788M and GAAP diluted EPS reached $3.09, but that figure overstates operating momentum. It absorbed an $86M net divestiture gain (Solovis, Nordic power futures, catastrophe modeling), $83M of equity-method income (largely OCC on higher volumes), an $18M debt-extinguishment gain, and a low 16.7% effective tax rate versus 23.1% in 2024. The honest read is non-GAAP EPS of $3.48, the number management compensation targets.

Cash generation is high quality. Operating cash flow of $2,255M exceeded net income, aided by $632M of non-cash D&A (much of it acquired-intangible amortization). Free cash flow was $1,989M, a ~38% FCF margin on net revenue, on an asset-light, high-conversion model.

The balance sheet carries ~$9.0B of total debt (long-term $8,573M plus short-term $431M) against $12,227M of equity, D/E of 1.54. Goodwill of $14.4B plus intangibles of $6.5B represent ~67% of $31.1B total assets, a direct legacy of Adenza and Verafin. Deleveraging is the clear priority: Nasdaq repaid $400M of maturing 2025 notes and repurchased $444M book value of notes, all fixed-rate at a 3.7% weighted-average rate, cutting interest expense to $367M from $414M. The July 1, 2026 8-K (Items 1.01/1.02/2.03) points to a refinancing consistent with this program, though the details were not fully captured and warrant confirmation.

On the widely cited Altman Z-Score of 1.46 ("distress zone"): this is a misapplied signal. The model was built for industrial firms and penalizes an exchange/clearinghouse whose balance sheet carries large offsetting clearing default-fund and margin-deposit balances that inflate liabilities, plus a goodwill-heavy, asset-light structure. Investment-grade ratings, $1.99B FCF, and consistent profitability confirm financial health. The real leverage question is whether the ~$9B acquisition debt can be paid down while still funding dividends and buybacks, and the FY2025 cash flows indicate it can.

Earnings visibility is good: NDAQ has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters, though modestly (1.1% to 3.0%), which supports confidence in the current $3.94 FY2026 and $4.44 FY2027 estimates but does not point to a large upside surprise.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is a deliberate mix-shift toward recurring, higher-multiple revenue. Three engines drive it:

**Index** posted record $99B net inflows in 2025, with $136B of net appreciation on top. This franchise is levered to secular passive/ETP adoption and to the technology sector's weight in the Nasdaq-100. It is capital-light and among the highest-quality revenue in the company.

**Financial Technology** grew ARR 14% with recurring, sticky bank and regulator relationships. Adenza's restructuring (~$140M total charges) completed by year-end 2025 with >$160M of net synergies actioned. The forward narrative rests on Regulatory Technology and Capital Markets Technology continuing to compound as banks are forced to upgrade mandatory regulatory reporting and risk systems, with Calypso now offered as an AWS-managed service and the Eqlipse cloud-native platform advancing.

**Listings** won a record $1.2T of market-cap switches (including the largest exchange transfer on record) and led U.S. IPO proceeds for a seventh straight year, benefiting from a strong 2025 IPO market (784 new U.S. listings vs. 463 in 2024, $24B+ in proceeds). That IPO strength is partly cyclical and may not repeat, so listings growth carries more variability than index or fintech.

Two 2026 product bets could add incremental growth if approved: Global Trading Hours (a 23-hour, five-day trading day, pending SEC approval, targeted H2 2026) and tokenized equities/ETPs (SEC rule proposal filed Q3 2025). Both are optionality, not base-case revenue.

The tempering factor for the growth score is that consensus is not moving. Current-year EPS has been revised only +0.8% over 90 days, next-year +0.8%, with the current quarter down 1.5%. This is stability, not the accelerating positive revision velocity that flags acceleration. Growth is durable but priced, and the November 2026 access-fee rule creates a specific headwind to the cyclical Market Services line that partially offsets the recurring-revenue momentum. We score Growth Prospects at 6.5.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$85, NDAQ trades at ~25x its ~$3.44 trailing basis and ~21.5x the $3.94 FY2026 non-GAAP estimate (the 25.5x forward P/E in the data set reflects the GAAP $3.09 base; on the cleaner non-GAAP figure the multiple is meaningfully lower). Against the Financial Exchanges & Data peer median of 31.7x, NDAQ sits at a clear discount, cheaper than MSCI (34) and MCO (31.7), near CBOE (25.3), and above ICE (20.2). The dividend yield is ~1.6%, above the peer median of 1.0%.

The discount has a specific explanation, which is why the valuation is not a clear buy signal. NDAQ has more direct exposure than most peers to the November 2026 SEC access-fee rule (a Market Services revenue headwind), it is losing trading share, and its recurring-revenue mix, while growing, is still less complete than MSCI's (pure index/analytics) or MCO's (ratings duopoly). The market is applying a rational discount for the regulatory overhang and the cyclical trading exposure.

The discount is arguably too wide relative to fundamental quality. A business generating a ~44% operating margin, ~38% FCF margin, ~23% non-GAAP EPS growth, and record index AUM should not trade at a ~20% discount to a peer group with lower growth in several cases (ICE at 20x is the lower comp, but has a mortgage-tech drag and its own regulatory issues). If the November 2026 rule proves manageable and estimates hold, a re-rating toward the high-20s multiple is plausible. Consensus targets — mean $106, median $110, high $120, low $82 — put the stock well below the midpoint; with stable rather than rising estimates, the targets are best read as a plausible range whose realization depends on the regulatory outcome.

We treat the cheapness with the appropriate skepticism: a discount multiple on an exchange facing a known revenue rule is only a buy signal if the rule is less damaging than feared. We score Valuation at 6.6 — constructive, reflecting a genuine peer discount and quality, but capped by the identified overhang.

## Competitive Landscape

Nasdaq's competitive position is bifurcated and the trajectory matters more than the level. In listings and index, the moat is widening: the seventh consecutive year of #1 U.S. IPO proceeds, a record in switches, and record index inflows. In fintech software, Adenza's mission-critical, ratably-recognized bank software carries high switching costs, and management argues its domain-specific, proprietary-data AI differentiates against generic LLM-based entrants — an argument that is credible but unproven as competition intensifies.

In trading, the position is deteriorating on the share metrics cited above. Revenue grew on industry volume, masking relative erosion. Structural pressures compound this: SEC access-fee and market-data fee scrutiny in the U.S. and Canada, MiFID II/MiFIR systematic internalizers taking European share, and a proposed EU consolidated tape. The strategic pivot toward data, index, and software responds to a trading business that is fundamentally price-competitive and share-losing.

The company is strengthening in its highest-value segments while ceding ground in its most cyclical one. The competitive-position score of 6.3 reflects that mixed trajectory and follows the prior report's step-down from 8.0 to 7.0. The declining trading share and the regulatory pressure on the trading model justify holding this dimension below the level the index/listings wins alone would support.

## Risk Assessment

**SEC access-fee rule (November 2026, the single largest dated overhang).** The September 2024 rule cut the fees exchanges can charge for accessing quoted liquidity, which in turn cuts the rebates they can pay to attract liquidity. Nasdaq's petition to vacate was denied in October 2025; temporary exemptive relief runs only until November 2026. This is a binary, dated event that consensus estimates have not fully absorbed.

**CAT funding vacatur.** The 11th Circuit vacated the CAT funding model in July 2025; the stay expired at end-November 2025. Nasdaq carries a $99M net receivable it may be unable to recover, with additional forfeiture risk if the SEC finds late or improper technology delivery.

**Goodwill/intangible concentration.** $20.9B of goodwill plus intangibles (67% of assets) means any deterioration in Financial Technology could trigger impairment. The Adenza integration is complete with synergies actioned, which lowers but does not eliminate this risk.

**Cyber and systems reliability.** As critical global market infrastructure, Nasdaq is a prime target, and the October 2025 AWS outage that affected some (non-trading) offerings is a concrete manifestation of deepening cloud dependency.

**Volume cyclicality.** A large fixed-cost base magnifies revenue swings; 2025's elevated volumes and record IPO market are cyclical tailwinds that may not repeat, which raises the risk of a 2026 comp headwind independent of the access-fee rule.

**New/expanded risk themes.** Tokenized-securities regulation (Nasdaq flags a "less onerous" regime for competitors as a disadvantage) and a substantially expanded generative/agentic AI risk factor covering third-party model IP and competitive displacement.

The risk score of 5.5 reflects that the biggest risk is specific, dated, and not fully priced into consensus. This is the correct application of the risk dimension: scoring the magnitude of idiosyncratic risk the market has not yet reflected, rather than penalizing generic sector cyclicality. The November 2026 rule is the reason risk sits below neutral.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 21.2% against 27.1% trailing realized vol, so short-dated options are cheap (a -5.9% spread), and the term structure is in normal contango (near-term 21.2% vs. six-month 28.6%). Put/call skew is call-heavy in the near tenor (-5.4%), and IV has fallen sharply from 30.6% to 21.2% since early May even as the stock declined ~5%; the market is not pricing acute near-term stress. The offsetting caution is a put/call OI ratio that jumped from 0.17 to 1.68 and a put/call volume ratio of 1.31, indicating a build in downside hedging over the horizon that captures the November 2026 rule. Short-term positioning is complacent while longer-dated positioning shows concern, consistent with the fundamental view that the binary regulatory event sits several months out. Cheap near-term IV also makes protective puts relatively inexpensive for holders concerned about the rule.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($110-120).** The access-fee rule proves less damaging than feared (partial pass-through, offsetting volume, or further relief), estimates begin rising, and the market re-rates NDAQ toward the peer median as its recurring-revenue mix crowds out the cyclical trading discount. Global Trading Hours and tokenization add optionality. On ~$4.44 FY2027 EPS at ~27x, the stock reaches $120; a more conservative re-rate to the mid-20s on rising estimates supports $110. Investor AB's June accumulation near $86 anchors the strategic-holder conviction.

**Bear Case ($68-72).** The November 2026 rule cuts Market Services revenue materially, 2026 laps a cyclical IPO/volume peak that does not repeat, trading share erosion accelerates, and the CAT receivable is written down. Estimates fall, the multiple compresses toward ICE's ~20x on ~$3.60 realized EPS, and the stock revisits the mid-$70s lows or below, roughly 15-20% downside from current levels.

**Base Case ($92-96).** Recurring-revenue growth offsets a manageable access-fee headwind, non-GAAP EPS grows to ~$3.90-4.00 in FY2026 and ~$4.40 in FY2027, and the multiple holds at ~22-24x forward as the regulatory event resolves without a shock. That supports ~$92-96, roughly 9-13% above current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth bias). Timeframe: 12 months, keyed to the November 2026 access-fee-rule implementation and the surrounding earnings cadence.**

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The SEC access-fee rule is implemented with quantified Market Services revenue impact below ~3-4% of segment revenue, or further exemptive relief is granted, removing the binary overhang.
- Current-year or next-year consensus EPS revisions turn positive by >5% over a 90-day window, signaling the recurring-revenue mix-shift is outrunning the trading headwind.
- The stock trades below ~$78 (near the 52-week low) on unchanged fundamentals, widening the peer discount to a level where the risk/reward is clearly asymmetric.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Access-fee implementation cuts Market Services revenue materially and management guides FY2027 net revenue growth below mid-single digits.
- U.S. cash-equity matched share falls below ~12% or options share below ~27%, indicating trading erosion is structural rather than cyclical.
- A goodwill/intangible impairment charge in Financial Technology, or a CAT-receivable write-down, signals deterioration in the acquired-asset base.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 (late July) and Q3 2026 (late October) earnings calls, where management guidance on the November access-fee implementation should sharpen, and the rule's effective date itself in November 2026.

## Conclusion

Nasdaq is a high-quality, cash-generative franchise executing a credible pivot from cyclical trading toward recurring index, data, and fintech-software revenue. FY2025 delivered ~23% non-GAAP EPS growth, a ~38% FCF margin, record index AUM, and ongoing deleveraging of the Adenza debt while raising the dividend. The ~17% de-rating from the January high leaves the stock at ~21.5x forward non-GAAP EPS, a genuine discount to the ~31.7x exchange/data peer median, and Investor AB's June accumulation is a supportive strategic-holder signal.

The counterweight is specific and dated. The November 2026 SEC access-fee rule threatens Market Services economics, trading share is eroding, consensus estimates are stable rather than rising, and 2026 must lap a cyclical IPO/volume peak. The discount alone is not enough to move to BUY without a resolution of the regulatory overhang, so we rate NDAQ a HOLD with a growth bias and a base-case target of $92-96 over twelve months, with the November 2026 rule as the decisive swing factor between the bull and bear paths.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the FCF margin on net revenue was overstated as ~24% but is actually ~38% ($1,989M / $5,249M), materially strengthening the quality/valuation case; and the Investor AB purchase was characterized as "open-market accumulation," a characterization not supported by the brief (which describes a purchase but not its manner of execution).
