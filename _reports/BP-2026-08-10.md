## Executive Summary

BP reported its strongest quarter since 2022 on 4 August 2026: underlying replacement-cost profit of $5.73bn versus $2.4bn a year earlier, 12% ahead of company-compiled consensus, with operating cash flow of $10.9bn, up 73% year over year. I reconciled that growth rate against BP's Q2 2025 filing (operating cash flow of $6,271m per the 6-K filed 5 August 2025), which gives +73.8% and matches the reported figure. The quarter is real. What produced it is not BP's doing: higher crude and gas realizations, stronger fuel margins, materially better refining margins, and a modestly better oil-trading result. Over the same three months production fell from 2.3 to 2.2 million barrels of oil equivalent per day, upstream plant reliability fell 330 basis points to 92.4%, refinery throughput declined, and exploration write-offs rose. The earnings power on display belongs to the Middle East supply shock, not to the operator.

The valuation follows from that observation. Consensus FY2026 EPS of $6.24 per ADS puts the stock at 6.9x, a number that invites a value screen to fire. Consensus FY2027 is $4.21, 32.5% lower, putting the stock at 10.2x normalized-ish earnings, which is unremarkable against integrated peers. The 30-day revision to the current year was +22.0%; the revision to next year was +3.3%. That asymmetry is the tell: analysts are marking a windfall to market, not re-rating durable earnings power. Consensus for the September quarter (current-quarter estimate $1.63 per ADS) sits below Q2's per-share result, consistent with oil easing in Q3. Treating the +22% revision as an earnings-inflection buy signal would be a category error, because this is a price-driven revision in a commodity business.

What supports holding the shares is cash and yield rather than growth. FY2025 operating cash flow of $24,493m against 2026 capex guidance of $13.5–14bn implies roughly $10.5–11bn of free cash flow at last year's cash generation level. The indicated forward yield is 4.84%. Net debt fell to $22.3bn at 30 June from $25.3bn at 31 March, and the $14–18bn target was pulled forward a full year to December 2026. What argues against adding is the quality of everything behind the cash: two consecutive years of near-zero reported profit ($1,229m in FY2024, $1,295m in FY2025 on $192.5bn of revenue), total equity down $11,493m over those two years against $2,524m of cumulative reported profit, gross borrowings of $57,958m, a three-year 40.2% decline in operating cash flow from the 2022 peak, and a governance record that has produced three CEO transitions since September 2023 and a chairman removed for conduct within roughly eight months of appointment.

## Company Overview

BP is one of a small group of large integrated international oil and gas majors, with FY2025 revenue of $192,549m (Form 20-F filed 6 March 2026). Revenue scale is a poor proxy for earnings power here because a substantial share is low-margin trading and refined-product throughput.

The business shape that the available evidence establishes: upstream production of roughly 2.2 mmboe/d as of Q2 2026, a material refining and marketing arm whose margins were the principal cited driver of the Q2 beat, a significant supply-and-trading franchise, a low-carbon portfolio now being actively divested, and a UK North Sea business put up for sale on 31 July 2026 after roughly sixty years of UK Continental Shelf production.

A methodological caveat that materially affects what can be asserted: BP is a UK-domiciled foreign private issuer filing Form 20-F and 6-K rather than 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, DEF 14A or Form 4. The FY2025 20-F narrative sections — Item 4 (Business), Item 3.D (Risk Factors), Item 5 (MD&A), the impairment note and legal proceedings — could not be retrieved. Consequently: no risk-factor additions or modifications versus the prior year have been identified, no FY2025 capex or free cash flow actual is available, no Section 16 insider transaction data exists for this issuer, and no SEC proxy compensation disclosure exists. Share count could not be verified, so market capitalization, price/book and EV multiples below are presented as sensitivities over a 15.0–16.0bn ordinary-share range at the customary 1 ADS = 6 ordinary shares ratio (a long-standing structural fact not verified in a filing this session). The reported trailing and forward P/E of 120.8x in the quote feed derives from an EPS field of $0.0034 and is a data artifact; discard it.

New CEO Meg O'Neill, appointed effective 1 April 2026 and BP's first externally recruited chief executive, described the company's record in unusually blunt terms in the Q2 commentary: "Our performance over the past few years has not met our own expectations, let alone those of our shareholders. We have not delivered consistently; we have written off too much value; and our costs and liabilities are not resilient enough in a low price environment."

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue has stabilized after a three-year decline.** Revenue of $192,549m in FY2025 is down 1.1% from $194,629m and 22.6% from the 2022 peak of $248,891m. For an integrated major this tracks the commodity cycle more than company performance.

**Reported earnings have been effectively erased for two years.**

| Fiscal year | Profit (loss) for the year ($m) | Net margin |
|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 8,487 | 5.2% |
| 2022 | (1,357) | negative |
| 2023 | 15,880 | 7.5% |
| 2024 | 1,229 | 0.6% |
| 2025 | 1,295 | 0.7% |

FY2025 return on average total equity works to 1.70% (1,295 ÷ average equity of $76,159m), against 18.85% in FY2023. Splitting the year using BP's half-year tagging: H1 2025 profit was $2,911m, implying an H2 2025 loss of $1,616m, with the deterioration concentrated in the half containing December 2025's abrupt CEO change. Second-half impairment concentration is normal for oil and gas companies running annual tests against year-end price decks, and I draw no inference about timing motive.

**The cash statement says most of this is non-cash, and separately says cash conversion is weakening.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $24,493m against $1,295m of reported profit is a ratio of 18.9x; for a company with BP's depreciation base a normal range is 2–4x. The $23.2bn gap is strong evidence that reported earnings are impairment-suppressed, and any screen keyed on net income (ROE, ROA, net margin) will mis-rank BP badly. The impairment amount itself is unverified because the note could not be read.

Both halves of that sentence matter. Operating cash flow has declined every year for three years — $40,932m (2022), $32,039m (2023), $27,297m (2024), $24,493m (2025) — a cumulative 40.2% fall. Cash flow as a share of revenue was 12.7% in FY2025 versus 16.4% in FY2022. Derived from the half-year data, H2 2025 produced a $1.6bn reported loss alongside $15,388m of operating cash, the sharpest illustration of the divergence in the record.

**The balance sheet has rebuilt gross debt while equity eroded.**

| Date | Gross borrowings ($m) | Cash ($m) | Total equity ($m) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 31-Dec-2022 | 46,944 | 29,195 | 82,990 |
| 31-Dec-2023 | 51,954 | 33,030 | 85,493 |
| 31-Dec-2024 | 59,547 | 39,204 | 78,318 |
| 31-Dec-2025 | 57,958 | 36,556 | 74,000 |

Gross borrowings rose $11.0bn (+23.5%) from the end-2022 trough. Total equity fell $11,493m between end-2023 and end-2025 while cumulative reported profit across those two years was $2,524m. The most likely explanation is that distributions exceeded reported earnings with the shortfall absorbed by the balance sheet, but equity also moves on currency translation, pension remeasurement, non-controlling interests and hybrid capital, and the 2024–2025 dividend and buyback cash outflows were not verified. The equity decline is a filing fact; the attribution is inference.

**Leverage, on two different measures.** Gross borrowings/equity is 78.3%; borrowings less cash/equity is 28.9%. Gross borrowings/FY2025 operating cash flow is 2.37x; borrowings less cash is 0.87x. On cash-adjusted measures BP is not a solvency question. On gross measures it carries the heaviest balance sheet among the large majors, which is why debt reduction is the stated first priority. One definitional warning that matters: my $21,402m "borrowings less cash" is not BP's reported net debt, which is a company-defined measure adding the fair value of derivatives hedging finance debt; BP reported $22.3bn at 30 June 2026. Whether the `ifrs-full:Borrowings` tag as BP applies it captures IFRS 16 lease liabilities is unverified.

**Q2 2026 and the deleveraging arithmetic.** Externally reported (Reuters, Proactive, 4 August 2026, from a 6-K I could not retrieve directly): underlying RC profit $5.73bn, reported attributable profit $3.9bn, operating cash flow $10.9bn including a $1bn working-capital build, net debt $22.3bn, the $14–18bn target pulled forward to end-2026, $1bn of hybrid securities to be repaid this quarter, dividend raised 4% to 8.66 US cents per ordinary share, effective tax rate 33–37%. Two guidance changes went the wrong way in the same release: 2026 capex raised $0.5bn to $13.5–14bn, and 2026 disposal proceeds cut $1bn to $8–9bn, together worsening 2026 free cash flow by roughly $1.5bn.

Moving from $22.3bn to $14–18bn requires a $4.3–8.3bn reduction in six months. Rough H2 uses: capex of roughly $6.75–7bn, dividends of roughly $2.7bn (8.66c × 2 quarters × ~15.5bn shares), and $1bn of hybrid retirement — call it $10.5bn. If H2 operating cash flow repeats the Q2 run rate the target clears comfortably; if Brent retreats toward $70 and H2 operating cash flow lands nearer $12–13bn, the target depends almost entirely on disposal completions whose proceeds guidance was just reduced. A mechanical point worth holding: repaying a hybrid instrument classified within equity consumes cash without reducing reported borrowings, which all else equal raises net debt. Guiding net debt sharply lower while retiring hybrid equity tightens the arithmetic rather than loosening it.

## Growth Analysis

BP's near-term earnings growth is large and its structural growth is negative.

The cyclical leg: consensus FY2026 EPS of $6.24 per ADS against FY2025's suppressed base, with the current-year figure revised up 22.0% in thirty days and 15.3% in ninety. The structural leg: consensus FY2027 EPS of $4.21, 32.5% below FY2026, revised up only 3.3% over thirty days. The market's forward curve for BP earnings is a spike and a retreat.

Underneath, volume is contracting deliberately. Production fell to 2.2 from 2.3 mmboe/d. The UK North Sea business is for sale (press-estimated at $2.6bn by Upstream, 7 August 2026), which removes a producing asset base. The Archaea US biogas business is for sale (characterized by the FT as "$4bn"). Neither figure is a transaction price; both are press characterizations of asset value. Against those exits, BP agreed on 6 August 2026 to acquire Woodside Energy's 70% interest in the Calypso deepwater gas project in Trinidad and Tobago, taking full ownership of an early-stage discovery — Woodside is the seller receiving consideration, BP the buyer. Consideration was not disclosed in any source read. Early-stage deepwater resource does not replace near-term production.

Approximately 700 job cuts were reported in late July 2026 as part of the restructuring. Cost reduction supports margins; it does not generate growth.

The earnings surprise record gives modest support to consensus reliability — two beats, one in line, one small miss over four quarters, with beats of 8.2% and 16.6% in the two most recent — but in a commodity business the surprise is largely the price deck.

I score growth prospects 4.2. The near-term print is strong and the revisions are genuinely positive, but the sole driver is exogenous, the following year is already marked 32.5% lower, and the asset base is shrinking by management design.

## Valuation Assessment

At $42.895 per ADS:

| Metric | Value |

| P/E on FY2027 consensus $4.21 | 10.2x |

| Price/book (15.0–16.0bn share sensitivity) | ~1.45–1.55x |
| EV / FY2025 operating cash flow | ~5.3–5.6x |
| Upside to mean analyst target $47.60 | +11.0% |
| Downside to low target $37.00 | −13.7% |

The 6.9x current-year multiple is the number that looks compelling and the one most likely to mislead. It rests on earnings generated by a Middle East supply disruption that BP's own quarter attributes as the cause. The perverse sign is worth stating plainly: a de-escalation of the supply shock, which would be good news for most of the economy, compresses BP's realizations and refining margins simultaneously. Consensus already prices some of that, since the September-quarter estimate of $1.63 per ADS is below the June quarter's result.

On FY2027's $4.21, 10.2x is roughly in line with integrated peer norms and offers no discount for BP's specific deficiencies: the heaviest gross balance sheet, the lowest reported returns, a shrinking production base, and a governance record without parallel among the majors. Price/book of about 1.5x is being paid for equity that has declined $11.5bn in two years and against which management has flagged further liability resilience concerns.

The dividend does real work in the valuation. At 4.84% indicated, the yield is a floor mechanism absent a severe downcycle, though BP cut the dividend in 2020, so history does not guarantee it.

Analyst dispersion is informative and unusually wide: a low of $37.00 against a high of $64.00, with the mean at $47.60 and the recommendation distribution at 3 strong buy, 6 buy, 8 hold, 1 sell, 1 strong sell. A $27 spread on a $43 stock reflects that the answer depends on the oil price, which it does. The one recent change was an upgrade from sell to hold. With eight holds and two negative ratings out of nineteen and the mean target only 11% above spot, there is no consensus mispricing to exploit; the analyst targets here are not a stale floor, because BP is a cyclical whose out-year estimates decline rather than a cheap-versus-improving-estimates story.

I score valuation 5.2. Absolute yield support and a sub-7x headline are real; the multiple on normalized earnings is average, the discount to peers is arguably deserved, and there is no identified catalyst that reprices the shares independent of crude.

## Competitive Landscape

BP's position among the majors is the laggard's position. The evidence is internal rather than inferred: a first-time externally recruited CEO; a public admission that the company has "written off too much value"; two consecutive years of near-zero reported profit; the heaviest gross balance sheet in the group; reported activist involvement; and the sale of its founding-country production base. No well-performing major carries that combination.

Relative performance in the quarter reinforces the point. ExxonMobil and Chevron reported what one wire described as super-normal profits in the days before BP. When an entire sector rises on a geopolitical supply shock, the signal is relative operating delivery, and BP's beat came alongside falling production, falling reliability, falling throughput and rising exploration write-offs.

The one genuine differentiator is the supply and trading franchise, which monetizes volatility and was a cited contributor to Q2. That franchise lost its leader: deputy CEO and trading head Carol Howle is retiring later in 2026, with Sam Skerry taking over, announced 30 June, within three months of the new CEO's arrival. The WSJ noted the departure leaves O'Neill "without an experienced executive." Trading returns are people-dependent and difficult for outside analysts to underwrite; this is the most under-covered item in the recent flow.

The strategic reversal is the defining company-specific dynamic. BP pursued the most aggressive transition strategy of any major between 2020 and 2023 and is now dismantling it, with Archaea the clearest case. Archaea's original acquisition price could not be verified and is therefore not asserted, but if the reported ~$4bn sale valuation approximates what was paid, the nominal return over roughly four years would be poor and worse in real terms. Exiting the UK Continental Shelf reduces exposure to UK windfall taxation ahead of an autumn Budget; the more prosaic reading — that mature, capital-hungry, decommissioning-heavy assets are being high-graded away — is at least as likely, and BP's stated rationale is not established in the sources read.

Competitive position: 4.0. The trajectory is toward a smaller, more concentrated, more oil-and-gas-weighted BP with a weakened trading bench.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity reversal, with a two-sided catalyst.** If the Middle East supply shock de-escalates, BP's realizations, fuel margins and refining cracks compress together, and the FY2027 consensus of $4.21 becomes the optimistic case rather than the base. Reporting indicates crude was already easing in Q3 2026.

**Windfall taxation and political exposure, near-dated.** The US President publicly criticized oil companies for making too much money "based on a shortage" and said they would give some back. Commentators flagged the UK Budget and the US midterms this autumn as specific calendar risks. A company earning a supply-shock windfall in two jurisdictions with active political scrutiny carries genuine, dated fiscal risk.

**Deleveraging execution.** The six-month reduction requires disposals to close on a programme whose proceeds guidance was cut $1bn in the same release, while capex guidance rose $0.5bn and $1bn of hybrid equity is retired for cash.

**Undisclosed decommissioning and retained liabilities.** UK Continental Shelf assets carry substantial decommissioning obligations. Whether those transfer on sale, are retained, or are guaranteed by BP is undisclosed in every source read. Management specifically identified liabilities that are "not resilient enough in a low price environment," which makes this material and unquantified.

**Further impairment.** Equity down $11.5bn in two years plus management's "written off too much value" raises the base rate for further write-downs, particularly if the FY2025 impairment price deck assumed conditions that Q3 2026 pricing does not support.

**Governance.** Three CEO transitions since September 2023; chairman Albert Manifold removed with immediate effect on 26 May 2026 after roughly eight months, citing serious concerns over governance standards, oversight and conduct, with the shares sliding; Manifold publicly rejecting "lies" and bullying allegations; a Reuters report (two anonymous sources, unconfirmed by BP) that he met Elliott Management without directly informing fellow directors; reports that he had been preparing to move against the senior independent director. The decisive removal can be read as a board functioning properly, and that reading has merit. It does not change the execution burden: a first-time-at-BP CEO must stabilize the organization while deleveraging, cutting roughly 700 jobs and running two large disposal processes.

**Governance item requiring disclosure.** The Calypso acquisition sits close to a conflict-of-interest boundary because O'Neill was Woodside's CEO until December 2025. It is not a related-party transaction in the technical sense — she resigned months before, BP already held 30%, and consolidating a partner's stake in an asset you already hold is ordinary commercial behaviour. No improper motive is inferred. What is flagged is that the consideration was not disclosed in any source read, and neither was BP's conflict-management process.

**Structurally unavailable data.** No Section 16 insider transactions, no SEC proxy compensation disclosure, no Altman Z or Piotroski F computation (the screening tool requires a 10-K). This removes the insider-activity input that normally anchors a management-quality read and is itself a modest scoring penalty, since alignment cannot be verified in either direction.

Risk factors: 4.2. Several of these risks are partially priced through BP's discount to Exxon and Chevron, but the decommissioning treatment, further impairment exposure and disposal-execution dependence are not visible in any published number.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $56 per ADS (+30.6%)

The Middle East disruption persists through 2027, keeping Brent in the $90s and refining cracks elevated. The North Sea and Archaea disposals complete at or near the press-estimated $2.6bn and $4bn, delivering the $8–9bn 2026 proceeds guidance. Net debt reaches the low end of $14–18bn by December 2026, at which point capital allocation shifts from debt to buybacks and BP begins closing the multiple gap to Exxon and Chevron. O'Neill's operational agenda lifts plant reliability back above 95% and production stabilizes. On $4.75–5.00 of normalized EPS at 11–11.5x — a peer-average multiple for a company that has fixed its balance sheet — $56 is reachable. The analyst high of $64 requires both sustained disruption and a full re-rating.

### Bear Case — $33 per ADS (−23.1%)

The supply shock de-escalates, Brent falls toward $70, and refining margins normalize. FY2027 EPS lands nearer $3.00–3.50 rather than $4.21. Disposal processes slip or clear below press estimates, net debt stalls near $20bn, and the buyback stays suspended. A UK windfall measure in the autumn Budget or US political intervention lands on already-compressed margins. Another impairment cycle takes equity below $70bn. At 9.5–10x on $3.25 plus a modest yield discount, $33 is the floor region — roughly where BP traded before the disruption premium was priced in and near the $32.72 fifty-two-week low. The dividend probably survives, but coverage thins materially at $70 Brent alongside $13.5–14bn of capex.

### Base Case — $44.50 per ADS (+3.7%)

Partial de-escalation with intermittent disruption keeps Brent in the $80s. FY2027 EPS lands close to the $4.21 consensus. Net debt reaches roughly $17–19bn by year-end, at the high end of guidance or slightly above, with the buyback restart deferred to 2027. Production stays around 2.2 mmboe/d as disposals offset new volumes. At 10.5x $4.21 = $44.20, call it $44.50. Adding the 4.84% dividend gives roughly 8.5% total return over twelve months.

Probability-weighting 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear gives approximately +2.4% price return plus 4.84% dividend, or roughly 7% total, approximately fair for a high-beta commodity equity with this governance profile, and below the bar that would justify adding.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income). Horizon: 12 months, with a first checkpoint at the Q3 2026 result in late October 2026.**

Existing holders should retain exposure for the 4.84% yield and the deleveraging path. New capital has no urgent entry signal at $42.90; the risk/reward improves materially in the mid-to-high $30s, where the FY2027 multiple compresses toward 8.5x and the yield approaches 5.5%.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE):**
1. Net debt at or below $16bn at the Q4 2026 report **with** disposal proceeds landing inside the $8–9bn guidance and North Sea decommissioning liability treatment disclosed as transferred rather than retained or guaranteed.
2. Upstream plant reliability recovering above 95% for two consecutive quarters with production stabilized at or above 2.2 mmboe/d — evidence that the operational agenda is delivering rather than the price deck.
3. Price in the $36–38 range on unchanged FY2027 estimates, which would put the shares near 9x normalized earnings with a yield above 5.4%.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. A further impairment of $3bn or more, or FY2027 consensus cut below $3.50 per ADS, either of which would confirm that the FY2025 write-downs did not clear the deck.
2. The $14–18bn net debt target abandoned or deferred, or 2026 disposal proceeds guidance cut a second time.
3. Any UK or US windfall measure enacted that raises BP's effective tax rate above the 33–37% Q2 level on a sustained basis.
4. A fourth senior leadership departure (CFO, new trading head, or senior independent director), which would indicate the governance instability has not been contained.

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 result in late October 2026, with an interim review if crude prices move sharply, or if the UK autumn Budget introduces sector taxation.

## Conclusion

BP delivered its best quarter in four years and the market has correctly refused to pay much for it. At 6.9x current-year and 10.2x next-year consensus, the shares are cheap on windfall earnings and average on normalized earnings, a distinction that a value screen keyed on the headline multiple will miss, and the reason a sub-7x P/E does not earn a high valuation score here. The +22% thirty-day revision to FY2026 estimates alongside a +3.3% revision to FY2027 identifies the revision as a price mark rather than an earnings inflection.

The reasons to keep owning the shares are concrete: $24.5bn of FY2025 operating cash flow against $13.5–14bn of capex, an indicated 4.84% dividend, net debt falling and its target accelerated, and two disposal processes that could deliver material proceeds. The reasons not to add are equally concrete: two consecutive years of near-zero reported profit, $11.5bn of equity erosion against $2.5bn of cumulative earnings, $58bn of gross borrowings, three straight years of declining operating cash flow, a production and reliability trend moving the wrong way in the very quarter that produced the beat, undisclosed decommissioning liability treatment on the North Sea exit, and the most disrupted governance record among the majors, including the departure of the executive who ran the one franchise that genuinely differentiates BP.

The overall score of 4.55 reflects a business with real cash generation and a credible self-help programme, held back by weak capital-allocation history, governance instability, and earnings whose current strength derives from a geopolitical disruption. HOLD for the income and the deleveraging; wait for the mid-to-high $30s, verified operational improvement, or a disclosed resolution of the North Sea liability question before committing new capital.
