**Research Date: June 9, 2026**

## Executive Summary

NOV Inc. is the largest independent equipment and technology supplier to the global energy industry, structured as an "arms dealer" to oilfield-service companies rather than a direct field competitor. The investment debate is straightforward: the stock has risen 65% over the past twelve months to $20.53, near its 52-week high of $21.55, on the expectation of a renewed multi-year offshore and international capital-equipment cycle. The order data does not yet support that expectation. Book-to-bill ran 73% in Q4'25 and 80% in Q1'26, meaning NOV shipped backlog faster than it replaced it for two straight quarters, and the Energy Equipment backlog has shrunk to $4.23B from $4.41B a year earlier. With 94% of backlog destined for international markets and 58% offshore, the entire bull case rests on a capex cycle that the company's own intake metrics contradict.

The fundamentals deteriorated through FY2025 and into Q1'26. GAAP net income fell 77% to $145M, roughly half of which is tax-valuation-allowance and impairment noise: the effective tax rate jumped to 59.7% from 23.6% as NOV raised its valuation allowance to $352M. The underlying operational compression is real: Adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 11.8% from 12.5% in FY25 and to 8.6% in Q1'26 from 12.0% a year earlier. Q1'26 operating cash flow turned negative (−$26M) on a working-capital build, and the company has now missed consensus EPS in four consecutive quarters, the most recent by 68.1% ($0.05 actual vs $0.16 estimate). Current-quarter consensus has been cut 29.5% over 90 days.

The bull arguments — a conservative balance sheet, $876M of FY25 free cash flow, a 20%-raised dividend, and a falling share count — are real but do not constitute a 12-month appreciation catalyst when the stock already trades at a premium to its trough earnings and the offshore-recovery thesis remains unconfirmed by orders. After four prior AVOID ratings on this name as the price climbed, the question is whether the run has finally made the recovery a sufficiently good deal. It has not. We maintain AVOID. The trajectory of book-to-bill, not the headline EBITDA, is the metric that resolves the debate, and it is moving the wrong way.

## Company Overview

NOV (formerly National Oilwell Varco) operates with 160+ years of heritage, roughly 31,600 employees, and about 503 locations across 57 countries. It runs two segments:

- **Energy Products & Services (~$3.98B, 45% of revenue):** Shorter-cycle, transactional. Drill bits, downhole and completion tools, artificial lift, drill pipe, tubular inspection/coating, solids control, composites, and the Max digital platform. Demand is North American-weighted and tied to rig count.
- **Energy Equipment (~$4.93B, 55% of revenue):** Longer-cycle capital equipment and integrated systems: land rigs, offshore drilling packages, pressure pumping, marine and construction equipment (including offshore-wind installation vessels and cranes), process systems, subsea flexible pipe, and aftermarket. This segment carries the reported backlog.

The strategic premise is to "drive end-market fragmentation" by selling best-in-class equipment to all participants, preventing any single integrated service company (SLB, Halliburton, Baker Hughes) from controlling key technologies. The durable advantages — global procurement scale, a large installed base feeding OEM aftermarket demand, and fleet standardization lock-in — are real. The company's fortunes are levered to offshore and international capex rather than U.S. shale, where its short-cycle Products & Services business has weakened with the declining domestic rig count.

The GAAP earnings collapse is partly accounting and partly operational. About half the FY25 net-income drop traces to taxes, with the higher effective rate driven by the valuation allowance raised from $266M to $352M, layered on nondeductible goodwill impairment and an unfavorable earnings-mix shift toward higher-tax foreign jurisdictions. This is the mirror image of 2023's net-income inflation from a ~$373M valuation-allowance release. Pre-tax "Other Items" swung roughly $292M unfavorably year-over-year (FY25: +$183M of charges including $70M goodwill/long-lived impairment, $89M severance and facility closures; FY24: −$109M credit including a $130M divestiture gain).

The operational compression underneath the accounting noise is the more important signal. Adjusted operating margin fell to 7.7% from 8.6%, driven by Section 232 steel tariffs, raw-material inflation, unfavorable mix as high-margin aftermarket and short-cycle revenue declined, and weaker international rig activity. Management attributes the Q1'26 worsening heavily to Middle East conflict effects: Strait of Hormuz disruption, freight inflation, and delayed capital-equipment deliveries. Operating cash flow turned negative on a $91M inventory build.

The balance sheet is the clearest strength. Cash was $1.34B at Q1'26 against total debt of $1.72B, most of it long-dated ($1.09B 3.95% notes due 2042, $497M 3.60% notes due 2029). Debt-to-cap is roughly 24% against a 60% covenant, the current ratio is 2.4x, and the $1.5B revolver is undrawn and extended to September 2030. The Altman Z of 2.82 sits in the grey zone, but that reflects the depressed EBIT/assets ratio at the earnings trough rather than balance-sheet distress. One watch item: about $839M of the $1.34B cash sits in foreign subsidiaries, and with Q1'26 operating cash flow negative, sustained capital return at the current pace through a prolonged downturn could eventually require revolver draws.

Capital return is genuine. FY25 saw $315M of buybacks (22.8M shares) plus $190M of dividends; the quarterly dividend was raised 20% to $0.09; the basic share count has fallen from 392M to 361M; and $389M remained on the $1B authorization at Q1'26. The policy targets returning at least 50% of "Excess Free Cash Flow."

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests entirely on a renewed offshore and international capital-equipment cycle driven by years of upstream underinvestment, energy security, and natural-gas-for-power demand. The order data argues against it materializing on the timeline the stock is pricing, with two consecutive quarters of intake well below shipments and only about 40% of backlog converting in the remainder of 2026. The thesis hinges on a single end-market cycle holding up, and the company's own intake says it is not yet doing so.

The earnings-estimate trajectory corroborates the order weakness. NOV's four straight EPS misses ran −2.4%, −56.6%, −107.9%, and −68.1% most recently. Current-quarter consensus has been cut to $0.16, and full-year FY26 estimates are down 21.5% over the same window (to $0.79). Estimates are falling, not rising, and the misses are accelerating. The lone bright spot is next-year EPS, revised +1.6% to $1.27, but that figure depends on the same unconfirmed offshore recovery and sits well beyond the 12-month decision window.

The adjacency optionality — offshore-wind installation equipment, geothermal, carbon capture, and advanced-nuclear supply-chain services — is plausible but early-stage. The Renewables reporting unit already took a $40M goodwill impairment in 2025, which tells you these businesses are not yet earnings-accretive. Growth Prospects score low because the leading indicators are negative and the recovery is a forward narrative, not a current trend.

## Valuation Assessment

At $20.53, NOV trades at 85x trailing earnings (the figure is distorted by trough EPS of $0.39) and roughly 26x the FY26 consensus of $0.79, falling to about 16x the FY27 consensus of $1.27. The forward multiple is reasonable only if the FY27 estimate is achievable, and FY27 depends on the offshore recovery that current orders contradict.

The peer set in oil & gas equipment and services shows wide dispersion: Weatherford at 15.2x, Archrock 18.6x, Liberty Energy 31.3x, Cactus 53.2x, and Kodiak Gas 86.1x, for a median of 31.3x. On a forward-FY26 basis NOV at ~26x sits below the median, but that comparison flatters NOV because peers like Weatherford trade on healthier near-term earnings while NOV is being valued on a recovery that has not arrived. The cleaner frame is enterprise value against the cash-flow base: a ~$7.4B market cap (plus net debt of roughly $0.4B) against ~$1.0B Adjusted EBITDA is about 7.8x EV/EBITDA on trough EBITDA, and against $876M of FCF the equity yields about 12% — neither cheap enough to compensate for falling estimates nor expensive enough to short outright.

The analyst price-target set — low $17, mean $21.70, median $22, high $26 — places the stock essentially at the consensus mean, with the median implying about 7% upside. On a stock with falling estimates and a four-quarter miss streak, the consensus target is not a floor that analysts will revise upward; it reflects the same recovery optimism embedded in the price. The valuation does not offer asymmetric upside. The downside scenario, where book-to-bill stays below 100% and the offshore cycle slips into 2027, has the stock re-rating toward $16–17 on the lower forward multiple and the analyst-low target.

We score Valuation 4.0. The cash flow and balance sheet support a floor, but the multiple already prices a recovery that the order book does not confirm, leaving no margin of safety after a 65% run.

## Competitive Landscape

NOV's competitive position is stable, neither materially improving nor eroding. The fragmentation strategy — selling to all participants to prevent the Big 3 from monopolizing technology — remains intact, and the installed base continues to generate aftermarket pull. The moat sources (global procurement scale, fleet standardization lock-in) are durable.

The trajectory is flat-to-soft. The high-margin aftermarket and short-cycle Products & Services revenue declined in FY25, which is the mix shift driving margin compression. Five-year cumulative TSR indexed to 123.4 against a peer group at 181.7 confirms relative underperformance over the cycle. The drill-bit royalty litigation — where NOV (via Grant Prideco) is suing Halliburton, SLB, Baker Hughes, and others for unpaid patent royalties — is an attempt to monetize IP, but NOV lost at the district-court level in Sept/Oct 2025 and is appealing. Competitive position is steady, with a slow margin-mix erosion that is more cyclical than structural. We score it 5.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity and activity cyclicality** is the master risk and is largely priced — WTI averaged $65.46 in 2025 (−14.5%), worldwide rigs fell 6.6%, and management itself flags "clearer downside risk than upside" near-term as OPEC+ returns supply. The unpriced risks are more specific:

- **Order-book deterioration.** Two consecutive sub-100% book-to-bill quarters directly contradict the recovery narrative. A third would break the bull case and is the single most important variable to monitor.
- **Middle East logistics.** The dominant Q1'26 operational drag (delivery delays, freight inflation, aftermarket disruption). Given the regional concentration of both backlog and aftermarket flows, a prolonged Strait of Hormuz disruption continues to compress deliveries and margins.
- **Tax volatility.** The valuation-allowance swings make GAAP earnings erratic; further foreign losses could force additional allowances.
- **Drill-bit royalty receivable.** A $137M net receivable (gross less $78M reserve and $43M timing discount) sits against an already-lost trial. An unfavorable Federal Circuit appeal triggers further write-downs.
- **Goodwill (~$1.6B).** Renewables already took a $40M impairment; sustained downturn could trigger more.
- **Tariff refund optionality (modest upside).** The Feb 2026 Supreme Court ruling against certain tariffs, plus NOV's filed IEEPA refund claims, create a small asymmetric upside not yet in results.

We score Risk Factors 4.5 — the idiosyncratic risks (order softening, royalty receivable, tax volatility, foreign-cash concentration) are specific and not fully reflected in a stock trading near its 52-week high.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is balanced and offers no contradiction to the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV is 41.6% against 1-year historical volatility of 38.6% — fairly priced (spread 3.0%), with the IV percentile at 38%. Put/call skew is roughly flat (−1.4%), the put/call volume ratio is low at 0.23, and the term structure is flat (near 41.6% vs far 40.5%). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±5.6%. The longer-dated 6-month skew has flipped positive (+3.4% put skew), indicating some demand for downside protection further out, consistent with the cyclical uncertainty. Short interest is elevated at 14.5% of float with 5.5 days to cover, which reflects institutional bearishness already in the position but also means a sharp positive order surprise could squeeze the stock. The options data neither confirms imminent stress nor argues for an active short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($26 / +27%):** Offshore and international capex inflects in the back half of 2026, book-to-bill recovers above 100%, and backlog re-grows. Adjusted EBITDA margin recovers toward 12%+ as Middle East logistics normalize and tariff headwinds reverse (aided by IEEPA refund recoveries). FY27 EPS of $1.27 proves achievable, and the stock re-rates on a more normalized 20x forward multiple. The conservative balance sheet, 20%-raised dividend, and ongoing buyback provide a floor and accelerate per-share value as the count shrinks.

**Bear Case ($16 / −22%):** Book-to-bill stays below 100% for a third and fourth quarter, backlog continues shrinking, and the offshore cycle slips into 2027. OPEC+ supply overhang keeps rig counts soft, Middle East disruption persists, and the royalty appeal goes against NOV, forcing a write-down on the $137M receivable. FY26 estimates get cut further from the current $0.79, and the stock de-rates toward the analyst-low target on the realization that the 65% run priced a recovery that did not arrive.

The base case sits near the current price. With estimates falling, four consecutive misses, and the order book contradicting the recovery, the probability-weighted outcome offers minimal upside against a real path to −20%. The asymmetry is unfavorable.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** The cleanest catalyst is the next earnings report (Q2'26, expected late July/early August), where a third consecutive sub-100% book-to-bill print would break the recovery narrative and force further estimate cuts. The royalty appeal at the Federal Circuit is a secondary, untimed catalyst. Neither is dated with precision beyond the earnings calendar.

**Timing window:** The earnings catalyst resolves in roughly 6–8 weeks. That is a workable window for a tactical short, but the thesis (cyclical recovery failing to materialize) is a multi-quarter grind rather than a single-event collapse.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** Short interest of 14.5% of float with 5.5 days to cover is a crowded-enough position that a positive order surprise — or a sharp move in oil/offshore sentiment — could squeeze the stock. With the stock near its 52-week high and the macro energy theme (Hormuz disruption, prolonged-disruption pricing) capable of lifting the whole complex, squeeze risk is meaningful.

**Borrow and options signal:** The options market is neutral, not confirming a bearish setup — flat skew, low put/call volume (0.23), and fairly priced IV. A neutral options market alongside a 14.5% short interest is a warning sign for new shorts: the bearish case is already partly expressed, and informed positioning is not aggressively adding downside bets.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear-case downside to $16 (−22%) against squeeze/recovery upside risk to $26 (+27%) is roughly 1:1 — below the 2:1 threshold required to justify the carry and squeeze risk.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamentals are weak and the recovery is unconfirmed, but with short interest already elevated, neutral options positioning, and a macro energy backdrop that can lift the sector regardless of NOV-specific intake, the risk/reward does not support an active short. This is a name to avoid owning, not to bet against.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:** We would revisit a constructive view on confirmation that the offshore cycle is turning — specifically (1) book-to-bill back above 100% for two consecutive quarters with backlog re-growing above $4.4B, (2) Adjusted EBITDA margin recovering toward 11–12% as Middle East logistics normalize, and (3) positive 90-day EPS estimate revisions replacing the current −21.5% FY26 trend. A pullback toward the low-to-mid $16s, where the forward multiple compresses below the FY27 growth rate and the analyst-low target provides a floor, would also improve the risk/reward materially.

**Timeline:** The cyclical headwinds (soft rig count, OPEC+ supply overhang, Middle East disruption) are likely to persist for at least 2–3 quarters. The order book is the gating variable; until it inflects, the recovery thesis remains a forward narrative the stock is already paying for.

**Downgrade-to-active-bearish triggers:** A third consecutive sub-100% book-to-bill print, an adverse Federal Circuit ruling forcing a royalty-receivable write-down, or a further valuation-allowance increase compounding the tax volatility would each strengthen the bearish case toward an active short — but only if accompanied by short-interest relief below ~10% to reduce squeeze risk.

## Conclusion

NOV is a well-run, financially conservative business at a genuine earnings trough, and the bull narrative — offshore underinvestment driving a multi-year capital-equipment cycle — is plausible on a multi-year view. The problem is timing and price. The stock has run 65% to near its 52-week high, discounting a recovery that the company's own order intake contradicts: two consecutive sub-100% book-to-bill quarters, a shrinking backlog, four straight earnings misses, and a 29.5% cut to current-quarter consensus. The cash flow and balance sheet establish a floor, but at 26x forward earnings on falling estimates, with the analyst consensus target essentially at the current price, there is no margin of safety and no near-term catalyst to re-rate the multiple higher.

This is the fifth consecutive AVOID on the name, and the bar for continuing a stale call should be high. The price appreciation since the prior reports does not change the conclusion; a 65% run into deteriorating order metrics makes the risk/reward worse, not better. We maintain **AVOID**. The recovery may come, but the order book has to confirm it before the valuation is defensible.
