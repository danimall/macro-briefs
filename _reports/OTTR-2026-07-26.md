**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Otter Tail Corporation is one of the more unusual names carrying a utility label. Its rate-regulated Electric segment (Otter Tail Power) is a conventional Upper-Midwest vertically integrated utility, but the company's reported earnings are dominated by an unregulated Plastics segment that manufactures PVC pipe. In FY2025, Plastics generated $4.05 of the company's $6.55 diluted EPS — roughly 62% of profit from a business representing a small fraction of total assets. The consequence is that OTTR's headline earnings are hostage to the PVC pipe commodity cycle, which peaked in 2021–2022 and has been mean-reverting since. Management's 2026 guidance of $5.22–$5.62 (midpoint $5.42) implies a ~17% year-over-year EPS decline, essentially all of it attributable to Plastics normalizing toward pre-2021 margins.

The investment question is whether the market is correctly pricing a temporary commodity peak or wrongly extrapolating one. Management's own long-term earnings-mix target is 70% Electric / 30% non-Electric, versus a 2026 mix guided at roughly 49/51. By its own framework, the company is over-earning on Plastics today. The regulated Electric segment is the durable engine: a ~10% rate-base CAGR on a ~$1.9B capital plan, funded without external equity issuance through at least 2030, with Electric EPS guided *up* ~14% in 2026. That financing posture is genuinely shareholder-friendly. But the consolidated multiple (~14x trailing / ~17x forward) prices a blended earnings stream where roughly half is a fading cyclical commodity, not a rate-base annuity.

We rate OTTR a HOLD. The stock trades near its 52-week high at $92.34 after an 18.5% twelve-month advance, out-year estimates are drifting lower (next-quarter consensus cut 11.9% over 90 days), and the litigation tail — a DOJ criminal antitrust grand jury probe plus unresolved plaintiff classes — remains open even after a manageable $34M civil settlement. The Electric growth story is real but not mispriced at current levels; the Plastics normalization is a known headwind the market appears to be discounting. We see no asymmetric edge in either direction here.

## Company Overview

Otter Tail is a hybrid holding company headquartered in Fergus Falls, Minnesota. The parent has no operations of its own and depends on subsidiary dividends to fund its own dividend and corporate obligations. It runs three segments:

1. **Electric (Otter Tail Power / OTP).** A vertically integrated, rate-regulated utility serving ~135,000 customers across western Minnesota, eastern North Dakota, and northeastern South Dakota. Generation includes coal (Big Stone, 53.9% owned; Coyote Station, 35% owned), natural gas, and a growing renewable fleet (wind repowering, solar, battery storage). Regulated by the MPUC, NDPSC, SDPUC, and FERC.

2. **Manufacturing.** Contract metal fabrication and thermoformed plastics for recreational vehicle, construction, agriculture, and industrial end-markets. Steel costs are largely passed through.

3. **Plastics.** PVC pipe manufacturing through Northern Pipe Products and Vinyltech, sold into water/wastewater and utility-infrastructure markets.

The structural feature that defines the equity is the outsized earnings contribution from Plastics, a small-asset, high-margin business whose profitability swings with PVC resin and finished-pipe pricing. This makes OTTR behave less like a pure regulated utility and more like a regulated utility bolted to a commodity manufacturer. That distinction is central to how the stock should be valued and why it is not directly comparable to the regulated-utility peer set on multiple.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is declining by design.** Consolidated revenue fell from $1,460M (FY22) to $1,349M (FY23), $1,331M (FY24), and $1,304M (FY25). The decline is driven almost entirely by PVC pipe price normalization from the late-2022 peak rather than lost volume; pipe volumes have generally held or grown, including a ~15% capacity addition from the completed Vinyltech Phase 2 expansion. Reading the top-line contraction as demand weakness would be a mistake; it is margin/price mean-reversion.

**Earnings are past their cyclical peak.** Net income moved from $294M (FY23) to $302M (FY24) to $276M (FY25). Diluted EPS declined from $7.17 (FY24) to $6.55 (FY25). ROE remains strong at approximately 15%. Plastics is guided to $2.49–$2.71 in 2026 versus $4.05 in 2025, and Electric up ~14% to $2.61–$2.69. Management has explicitly stated Plastics earnings decline through 2027 toward pre-2021 margins.

**Q1 2026 was a clean segment story.** Reported diluted EPS of $1.73 (+7% YoY) beat consensus of $1.44 by 19.9%. Electric net income rose 42.7% on rate increases and rider recovery; Manufacturing net income rose 179% off a low base on cost discipline and RV/construction recovery; Plastics net income fell 24% (prices −19% YoY, volumes +7%). The composition confirms the thesis: the regulated and cyclical-industrial pieces are inflecting up while the commodity piece rolls over.

**Cash flow and balance sheet support the capital plan.** FY25 operating cash flow was ~$386M; capex is ramping sharply — ~$288M in FY25, with $185M in Q1'26 alone versus $58M in Q1'25. Cash was $348M–$386M with total liquidity of $659M. Long-term debt is $963M–$1,063M against equity of $1.86B–$1.91B, modest leverage for a regulated utility. The Altman Z-Score of ~1.69 is a false distress signal; the metric is miscalibrated for capital-intensive regulated utilities, and coverage and profitability profiles are healthy. The standout positive is management's stated ability to fund the entire ~$1.9B capital plan without external equity issuance through at least 2030, which removes the per-share dilution drag that plagues most rate-base growth stories.

**Capital allocation is disciplined.** The dividend was raised to $2.31 annualized ($0.5775/quarter), a ~2.5% yield, comfortably covered even on the reduced 2026 EPS base. The effective tax rate is unusually low (~14%) due to wind PTCs/ITCs. Management targets long-term EPS growth of 7–9%, TSR of 10–12%, and ~10% rate-base CAGR.

**Earnings health score: 6.5/10.** The balance sheet is genuinely strong, the no-external-equity financing posture is a real differentiator, and cash generation is robust. We hold at 6.5 (down from 7.0 in May) because a meaningful portion of the cash flow currently funding the capex plan comes from over-earning Plastics profits that management guides to fade, which introduces a modest reliance question over the 2026–2027 window.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture splits cleanly into two opposing forces.

**Electric is a durable grower.** OTP is executing a ~10% rate-base CAGR, funded internally, with three concurrent rate proceedings: North Dakota settled (+$13.1M, 10.10% ROE, effective March 2025); South Dakota settled/approved (+$5.7M, 10.80% ROE, effective April 2026); and a Minnesota case filed October 2025 requesting +$44.8M (+17.7%), 10.65% ROE, with interim +$28.6M subject to refund. The coal-to-renewables transition (wind repowering completed on budget, solar panels secured to eliminate tariff cost risk, battery storage) provides a multi-year recoverable capex runway.

**Plastics is a declining contributor from a cyclical peak.** This is the dominant force on consolidated numbers. Management explicitly guides Plastics earnings lower through 2027 as PVC pipe pricing normalizes. Because Plastics is roughly half of 2026 profit, its decline overwhelms the Electric growth on a consolidated basis, producing the ~17% EPS decline.

The estimate-revision data confirms negative near-term momentum. Current-quarter consensus was cut 1.5% and next-quarter 11.9% over 90 days; next-year (FY27) estimates fell 2.1% to $5.32, below the 2026 midpoint. The out-year decline reflects continued Plastics normalization outrunning Electric growth.

**Growth score: 4.5/10.** The underlying utility is a legitimate mid-single-digit-to-high-single-digit compounder, but the consolidated earnings trajectory is negative through at least 2027, and consensus is revising down, not up. Investors buying on trailing EPS are paying for earnings the company itself expects to fade.

## Valuation Assessment

At $92.34, OTTR trades at ~14x trailing FY25 EPS ($6.55) and ~17x the 2026 guidance midpoint ($5.42). The peer set of comparable electric utilities (MGEE 19.7x, POR 22.4x, TXNM 42.1x, UTL 15.9x, HE 17.9x; peer median 19.7x) trades meaningfully above OTTR on trailing P/E. On its face, this looks like a discount.

The discount is largely justified rather than a mispricing. A pure regulated utility earning $5.42 with a durable ~7–9% growth trajectory would arguably deserve a peer-like ~19x multiple. But roughly half of OTTR's 2026 earnings come from a commodity pipe business whose profits management guides lower through 2027. Applying a utility multiple to the Plastics contribution is inappropriate; commodity manufacturing earnings warrant a lower multiple, and the "over-earning" characterization from management's own 70/30 target framework means the trailing figure overstates normalized earnings power.

A sum-of-the-parts frame is more instructive. If the Electric/Manufacturing base ($2.61–$2.69 + Manufacturing) is worth roughly a utility-to-industrial blended multiple and Plastics ($2.49–$2.71, declining) is worth a mid-single-digit-to-low-teens commodity multiple, the blended fair value lands close to where the stock trades today. The market appears to be pricing the fade correctly rather than over- or under-discounting it.

The estimate-revision trend reinforces the caution. Cheapness accompanied by falling out-year estimates is a value-trap signature, not a catalyst. There is no identifiable positive catalyst to force a re-rating: the largest pending event (Minnesota rate case) is a positive-but-incremental outcome, and the Plastics normalization is a headwind.

**Valuation score: 4.5/10.** The trailing multiple looks cheap only because it capitalizes peak Plastics earnings; on normalized/forward numbers the stock is roughly fair. We nudge slightly below the May-to-June improvement path (5.0) because the stock is now higher, near its 52-week high, with out-year estimates still drifting lower; the risk/reward on the valuation dimension has not improved.

## Competitive Landscape

The Electric segment holds the structural moat characteristic of a regulated monopoly utility: captive service territory, cost-of-service recovery, and a constructive multi-state regulatory framework. Its competitive position is stable to slightly improving given the recoverable renewables buildout and successful rate outcomes.

The Plastics and Manufacturing segments are competitively more exposed. PVC pipe is a commodity with only four US resin suppliers concentrated on the Gulf Coast (hurricane/supply-disruption risk) and industry-wide pricing dynamics that OTTR does not control. Co-defendants and peers in the antitrust matter — Atkore, Core & Main, Ferguson — confirm this is an industry-wide pricing cycle, not company-specific share loss. OTTR's Vinyltech Phase 2 capacity addition positions it for volume growth, but into a softening price environment, which is a mixed competitive signal. Plastics customer concentration (top two ≈ 47% of segment sales) and Manufacturing concentration (top three ≈ 44%) add fragility, though none exceeds 10% of consolidated revenue.

**Competitive position score: 5.0/10.** The regulated utility is well-positioned and stable; the earnings-dominant Plastics business is a price-taker in a commodity cycle it cannot influence. The blend is neutral, held flat at the prior 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

**PVC price normalization is the central earnings risk.** Management has explicitly signaled the decline through 2027, so the direction is not a surprise, but the magnitude and timing of "normal" are hard to predict. A faster or deeper normalization would pressure both EPS and the cash flows funding the capital plan.

**Antitrust litigation is materially de-risked but not resolved.** Otter Tail's subsidiaries (Northern Pipe, Vinyltech) agreed to a $34M settlement with the Non-Converter Seller Purchaser class in the *In re: PVC Pipe Antitrust Litigation* (N.D. Ill.), with a fairness hearing set for September 15, 2026. At ~12% of annual net income, this caps one class at a digestible number and transforms an open-ended "not estimable" contingency into a quantified figure. The real tail risk remains: a DOJ Antitrust Division criminal grand jury investigation (subpoena August 2024; DOJ intervened October 2025), additional plaintiff classes, a Canadian class action, and a shareholder derivative demand. The company continues to disclose that losses could be material and are not fully estimable. A criminal outcome or additional large civil settlements are genuine, if low-probability, downside catalysts not fully priced.

**Coal retirement and regulatory recovery.** Coyote Station must exit Minnesota service by December 2031 per the MPUC IRP order; the pending Minnesota rate case seeks accelerated recovery. Coyote is a VIE via the CCMC lignite supply agreement (through 2040) with ~$35M maximum loss exposure. Disallowance risk on the large +17.7% Minnesota ask is live regulatory-lag risk.

**FERC transmission self-funding challenge** (MISO Order to Show Cause) could affect returns on transmission investment.

**Leadership transition to monitor.** Form 4 title changes (Wahlund → Nelson at CFO) combined with Item 5.02 8-Ks on December 18, 2025 and April 14, 2026 suggest a CFO/officer transition in progress. Not a red flag on current evidence, but worth confirming in the next proxy.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 31.5% against 22.0% one-year historical volatility — rich by ~9.6 points — and the term structure is in backwardation (31.5% near-term vs 22.8% at six months), indicating the market is pricing imminent event stress. The put/call skew is −7.4% (call-heavy), which is not what one would expect if the market were positioning for a sharp downside catalyst; combined with the elevated near-term IV, this reads more as event-driven positioning than directional bearishness. ATM IV collapsed from 144.6% to 31.5% since the prior snapshot; the earlier reading was almost certainly a thin-options artifact rather than genuine signal. Short interest is elevated at 18.0% of float with 18.8 days to cover, a meaningful bearish institutional position that also creates squeeze potential on any positive surprise, a two-sided consideration that reinforces the balanced HOLD.

**Risk score: 4.5/10.** The open DOJ criminal probe and unresolved antitrust classes are specific, binary tail risks not fully reflected in a 14x trailing multiple, and the earnings model has an inherent commodity-cycle swing factor. The $34M settlement de-risks one class but does not resolve the tail.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The regulated Electric segment is a legitimate ~10% rate-base compounder funded entirely internally through 2030, with no equity dilution, in contrast to most rate-base growth peers. Electric EPS grows ~14% in 2026 while the Minnesota rate case (+$44.8M requested) provides upside. The market conflates OTTR with a fading commodity story and undervalues the durable utility core; as Plastics normalizes and the earnings mix shifts back toward management's 70/30 target, the quality and predictability of earnings improve and the multiple can re-rate toward the peer median. A benign or favorable resolution of the antitrust matters removes the litigation overhang. Bull case: high-$90s to low-$100s (~19x on a stabilizing ~$5.40 blended EPS plus multiple recovery), roughly +8% to +12% from current.

### Bear Case

Plastics normalization runs deeper and faster than guided, dragging consolidated EPS below the $5.22 low end and pressuring the cash flows that fund the no-equity capital plan. Falling out-year estimates (FY27 already cut to $5.32) confirm the fade is not finished. The DOJ criminal probe produces charges or additional civil classes settle at multiples of the $34M NCSP figure, forcing a material charge and a governance/reputational overhang. Regulatory disallowance on the large Minnesota ask compresses Electric returns. In this scenario the stock derates toward the mid-$70s (near the 52-week low of $74.15), roughly −20% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** The Electric growth story is real but fairly priced, the Plastics fade is a known and appropriately-discounted headwind, and the litigation tail is a genuine but low-probability binary. We do not see asymmetric edge in either direction at $92.34, near the 52-week high.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$80s or below, where the durable Electric earnings power provides valuation support independent of Plastics.
- Consensus FY27 estimates turning positive (stabilizing above ~$5.50), signaling the Plastics fade is complete and Electric growth is dominating the consolidated trajectory.
- Favorable resolution or dismissal of the DOJ criminal investigation, removing the primary tail risk.
- Minnesota rate case approved near the requested +$44.8M / 10.65% ROE with minimal disallowance.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- DOJ criminal charges filed, or additional antitrust classes settling at figures materially above the $34M NCSP benchmark.
- 2026 guidance cut below the $5.22 low end, signaling deeper Plastics normalization.
- Deterioration in the internal-financing posture (announcement of external equity issuance to fund the capital plan), which would introduce per-share dilution.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the September 15, 2026 antitrust fairness hearing, the Minnesota rate case decision, and the Q2 2026 earnings release. Twelve-month view.

## Conclusion

Otter Tail is a well-run utility holding company with a strong balance sheet, a disciplined internally-funded ~10% rate-base growth plan, and a ~2.5% covered dividend. Those attributes anchor a HOLD. But the equity's reported earnings are dominated by a PVC pipe business that management itself guides lower through 2027, and the apparent valuation discount to utility peers exists precisely because roughly half of 2026 profit is a commodity contribution that does not merit a utility multiple. On normalized/forward earnings the stock is fair, not cheap; the trailing multiple's cheapness capitalizes peak Plastics profits that are set to fade. With out-year estimates drifting down, an open DOJ criminal antitrust probe, and the stock near its 52-week high after an 18.5% twelve-month run, we see balanced risk/reward. The trajectory of prior calls (AVOID → HOLD) reflects genuine de-risking: the $34M settlement quantified a previously open-ended contingency and the balance sheet remains sound, but the improvement is incremental and the current price does not offer the margin of safety that would justify an upgrade. We would become constructive on a pullback to the low-$80s or on evidence that consolidated earnings have troughed and the utility core is re-establishing the growth trajectory.

**Overall Score: 5.0/10 — HOLD.**
