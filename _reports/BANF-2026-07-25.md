**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

BancFirst Corporation is a $3.9 billion market-cap Oklahoma banking holding company with $14.8 billion in total assets, built through a decades-long roll-up of community and commercial banks concentrated in Oklahoma and, more recently, north Texas. The investment case rests on three pillars: a granular, low-cost core-deposit franchise (94.8% core deposits, 30.8% noninterest-bearing) that produces stable, high-quality net interest income; fortress capital and liquidity (19.88% total risk-based capital ratio, 30.3% of assets in cash/Fed balances); and a serial-acquirer flywheel funded with excess capital rather than expensive leverage or dilutive equity. Earnings have compounded steadily: net income rose from $167.6M (2021) to a record $240.6M (2025), with diluted EPS of $7.11 versus $6.44 the prior year.

The near-term trajectory is favorable in a way that distinguishes BANF from most regional banks. Because roughly 44% of the balance sheet is funded by interest-free sources and ~$4.5B sits at the Federal Reserve, the bank's margin improves as short rates fall: net interest margin expanded from 3.74% (FY25) to 3.84% by Q3 2026 as deposit costs repriced lower. Consensus estimates have risen 4-6% over 90 days across all forward windows, and the company has beaten estimates in two of its last four reported quarters (Q2 2026 EPS $1.96 vs $1.79 estimate). BANF's earnings power benefits from the current rate path rather than being threatened by it.

The reasons this remains a HOLD rather than a BUY are valuation and two identifiable soft spots. At ~15.7x trailing earnings, BANF trades at a premium to its regional-bank peer median (~13.3x) and offers a below-median 1.7% dividend yield, so the multiple already reflects the quality of the franchise. The one genuine crack in an otherwise pristine credit book, CRE and other real estate owned (OREO), is widening while the allowance ratio is falling, which is a possible under-reserving setup if office/CRE stress accelerates in the current higher-for-longer rate environment. Layered on top is heavy selling by the founder-family key insider (David E. Rainbolt, ~$6.7M+ in April-May 2026). None of these is a thesis-breaker, but together they cap the risk/reward near current levels.

## Company Overview

BancFirst operates through four bank subsidiaries: BancFirst (the Oklahoma state-chartered core franchise holding ~80% of loans, split into Metropolitan OKC/Tulsa and rural Community segments), Pegasus Bank (Dallas), Worthington Bank (Fort Worth/Arlington/Denton), and the recently acquired American Bank of Oklahoma (ABOK, closed November 2025, merged into BancFirst February 2026). It supplements banking with insurance services, trust, SBA/small-business lending, residential mortgage, brokerage, and a matched-book oil & gas derivative accommodation desk.

The competitive model is local scale and relationship banking within a single state that larger national banks underserve. Statewide branch density, a sticky low-cost deposit base, and a disciplined tuck-in acquisition playbook constitute the moat. The company is deliberately over-capitalized and over-liquid (loan/deposit ratio only ~67%), holding dry powder specifically to grow loans and acquire weaker peers through downturns.

Ownership is concentrated: directors and executives control ~31% of shares, reflecting an owner-operator culture that shows up in unusually restrained compensation (CEO total comp ~$1.01M in 2025; CEO-to-NEO ratio of just 1.3x) and disciplined capital allocation (26% dividend payout, no financial-engineering buybacks). That same control block also entrenches management and caps any acquisition-premium optionality.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings and profitability.** Net income reached a record $240.6M in 2025 (diluted EPS $7.11), continuing a steady compounding trend ($167.6M → $193.1M → $212.5M → $216.4M → $240.6M from 2021-2025). ROA of 1.70% and ROE of 13.93% are solid for a regional bank, and the 55.0% efficiency ratio is competitive. Momentum has carried into 2026: Q2 EPS of $1.96 beat the $1.79 estimate; Q3 reportedly beat earnings by ~9% and revenue by ~5%.

**Net interest income and margin.** NII was $490.5M in 2025 (+$43.6M YoY) on loan volume growth and higher earning-asset balances. NIM held stable at 3.74% (vs 3.73% in 2024, 3.79% in 2023) and then expanded to 3.84% by Q3 2026. The mechanism is important: the bank's ~$4.5B of Fed balances and its enormous interest-free funding base mean that when the Fed cuts, the cost of interest-bearing deposits falls faster than asset yields, widening margin. The interest-free-funds base contributes roughly 1.20% to margin. Roughly 64% of loans are adjustable-rate.

**Noninterest income.** $200.1M (+8.4%), spread across trust ($23.1M), service charges/NSF ($71.1M), insurance commissions ($34.9M), and cash management ($41.1M), plus a one-time $4.5M Visa B-1 stock gain. NSF/overdraft (15.8% of fee income) and debit interchange (13.6%) carry regulatory exposure now that the bank has crossed $10B in assets.

**Balance sheet.** This is the standout. Total risk-based capital ratio of 19.88% (versus a 10.5% minimum with buffer), CET1 of 17.83%, and leverage ratio of 11.60% leave the bank substantially over-capitalized. Liquidity is exceptional: cash, Fed deposits, and fed funds equal 30.3% of assets ($4.5B), with no brokered CDs, negligible borrowings, and $923M undrawn FHLB capacity. The securities portfolio ($924M, ~97% Treasury/agency) carries minimal duration risk; the net unrealized pretax loss shrank to $10.8M from $43.1M. Unlike the 2023-era regional bank casualties, BANF has essentially no bond-portfolio hole, and uninsured deposits are only ~34%. Deposits grew to $12.67B (+$951.8M); loans held for investment reached $8.53B (+6.3%).

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Operating cash flow was $285M. The $1.90/share dividend is covered at a 26.3% payout, leaving ample room to grow. No buybacks occurred in 2024-2025 (479,784 shares remain authorized); capital is being retained for organic growth and M&A, with an automatic shelf S-3 filed in August 2025 for potential equity issuance to fund deals.

One data-quality caveat that carries through prior automated screening: the standardized Altman Z-Score (0.27, "distress") and generic XBRL health fields are invalid for a bank; they misread deposits as distressed liabilities. Ground truth is a 19.88% capital ratio and 1.70% ROA. Any framework relying on those numbers should override them.

## Growth Analysis

BANF's growth is a combination of mid-single-digit organic loan and deposit compounding plus periodic accretive acquisitions. Loans HFI grew 6.3% in 2025, and the 67% loan/deposit ratio implies substantial capacity to grow the earning-asset base without straining funding. The favorable NIM inflection is a durable near-term tailwind: as the Fed continues easing, the deposit-rich, Fed-cash-heavy balance sheet keeps expanding margin, a mechanism most regional banks lack.

The acquisition flywheel is active and accelerating. ABOK ($414M assets) was a small tuck-in, and a SpiritBank acquisition is reportedly on track for a Q4 2026 close pending regulatory approval (this detail comes from a Tier-3 aggregator and warrants primary-source confirmation, but it is consistent with the company's serial-acquirer history). The shelf registration and excess capital/liquidity are being deliberately held as M&A dry powder. This engine has historically driven the earnings compounding, though it carries integration and acquired-asset-quality risk.

Consensus estimate revisions confirm the momentum: current-year EPS rose to $7.34 (+4.3% over 90 days), and next-year to $7.53 (+5.9% over 90 days, +3.0% over 30 days). Zacks upgraded BANF to Buy (#2) on July 6, 2026 on rising estimates. The revisions are positive but modest in magnitude, well below the +30% velocity that would signal a genuine earnings inflection. This is steady-compounder growth, not a re-rating catalyst, so I am weighting it as directionally supportive rather than as a strong standalone BUY signal.

## Valuation Assessment

At $116.58, BANF trades at ~15.7x trailing EPS and ~15.9x current-year consensus of $7.34. Against the regional-bank peer set — INDB (16.2x), RNST (17.6x), WSFS (13.3x), FBP (11.1x), FIBK (11.9x), with a peer median of ~13.3x — BANF trades at roughly an 18% premium to the median. That premium is defensible on quality: superior capital (CET1 ~18% vs typical peer ~11-12%), ROA well above the peer average, minimal securities duration risk, and a favorable NIM trajectory. But it is not a discount that offers asymmetric upside.

The dividend yield of 1.7% is below the peer median of ~3.1%, reflecting the low payout; the company retains capital for growth and M&A rather than returning it. This limits the yield-support floor that cheaper, higher-payout peers (FIBK at 5.2%, FBP at 3.2%) provide.

The stock has declined ~16% from its 52-week high of $138.77 and ~6.3% over the trailing period. This price action does not reflect fundamental deterioration — estimates have risen, NIM is expanding, and earnings are at records — which means the risk/reward has genuinely improved versus the prior report. The de-rating from higher levels toward the sector is a normalization, not a mispricing. Analyst price targets bracket the current price tightly (low $123, high $125, mean $124.33), implying only ~7% upside to the mean. Because BANF is a stable compounder at a modest premium rather than an inflection stock at a compressed multiple with rising estimates below 30x forward and PEG well under 1.5, I do not treat these targets as stale floors. The consensus sits close to fair value.

Cheapness relative to peers is not the story; the story is quality at a fair-to-slightly-full price. On balance, the valuation offers limited near-term upside, with downside cushioned by the fortress balance sheet and covered dividend.

## Competitive Landscape

BancFirst's moat is local scale and relationship banking within Oklahoma, anchored by a sticky, granular, low-cost deposit franchise. Against money-center and superregional competitors with higher lending limits and greater resources, BANF wins on community presence, statewide branch density, and speed in markets larger banks underserve. Against smaller community banks and credit unions, it wins on scale, capital, and product breadth (trust, insurance, SBA, treasury management).

The 2023 regional-bank crisis validated the model's defensiveness. Unlike the banks that failed, BANF has minimal securities duration risk, a granular core-deposit base with only ~34% uninsured, ~18% CET1, and $4.5B of on-balance-sheet liquidity. That positioning makes it a consolidator rather than a target of stress: it can acquire weaker peers and grow loans through downturns while others retrench.

Competitive trajectory is stable-to-improving: the bank is gaining deposit share through acquisition, NIM is expanding, and the fee businesses are growing. The main structural pressures are the >$10B regulatory threshold (Durbin debit-interchange cap, CFPB supervision, Volcker Rule — the last forced disposal of some private-equity investments in 2025) and fintech competition in payments and lending. Neither threatens the core deposit franchise near-term.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE and OREO — the genuine soft spot.** This is the highest-value item to monitor. OREO jumped ~45% to $47.9M (from $33.1M), driven by foreclosure on a single $15.6M construction & development loan; one large CRE OREO property is carried at $24.7M (written down from $28.1M). OREO writedowns doubled to $8.2M in 2025 from $4.0M, the main driver of the 9.4% rise in noninterest expense. CRE non-owner-occupied nonaccruals rose to $20.6M (from $8.9M) and owner-occupied to $15.4M (from $8.0M). Total NPAs rose to $118.4M (from $99.4M). CRE is ~71% of the loan book. Provisions and the ACL ratio *fell* in 2025 as the vintage-loss model reacted to benign recent history, a possible under-reserving setup if office/CRE stress accelerates in a higher-for-longer environment. Nonaccruals remain low in ratio terms (0.72% of loans, flat), but the trajectory is the concern.

**Single-state / energy concentration.** BANF lends almost exclusively in Oklahoma (plus Texas). Oil & gas is 6.4% of the direct portfolio, but the larger exposure is indirect: the Oklahoma/Texas economies are energy-levered. With oil at ~$61/bbl at year-end 2025 (down from ~$72), a sustained low-price environment would pressure energy borrowers and spill into CRE and consumer books. The concentration is real and warrants monitoring.

**Regulatory fee pressure.** Crossing $10B subjects BANF to Durbin, CFPB supervision, and Volcker. NSF/overdraft (15.8% of fee income) faces political pressure.

**Insider selling.** Founder-family key insider David E. Rainbolt sold ~59,000+ shares in April-May 2026 (~$6.7M+) near current price levels, reducing his direct holding to ~166,783 shares. There has been no offsetting open-market executive buying. This is likely diversification/estate planning for a founder, and against a backdrop of rising estimates and record results the estimate revisions are the dominant signal; but the size is material and directionally the opposite of an "insiders loading up" story, so it tempers the Management Quality read.

**Options Market Signal.** Near-term ATM IV of 24.4% sits slightly below 1-year historical vol of 26.9% (spread -2.4%, IV percentile 8%), so options are priced cheaply relative to realized volatility, with no elevated event premium. The put/call skew of 3.8% shows modestly elevated near-term put demand, and the 6-month tenor implies a ±13.9% move, consistent with some hedging into H2. IV compressed sharply from 35.8% to 24.4% since early May, indicating resolved post-earnings uncertainty and complacency. Nothing in the options structure contradicts the HOLD; the cheap near-term IV suggests the market is not pricing an imminent binary catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($135-145).** NIM expansion continues as the Fed eases, driving current-year EPS above $7.50 and next-year toward $8.00+. The SpiritBank acquisition closes accretively in Q4 2026 and the roll-up flywheel accelerates, adding earning assets funded by excess capital. CRE/OREO stress proves contained (the office property and construction loan resolve without further material writedowns). The stock re-rates back toward a higher premium (~18-19x) on demonstrated resilience and the falling-rates-help-margin dynamic. This path implies ~16-24% upside.

**Bear Case ($95-100).** CRE/office deterioration accelerates against a falling allowance ratio, forcing a catch-up provision cycle and OREO writedowns that pressure earnings. Oklahoma/Texas energy weakness (sustained sub-$70 oil) spills into the broader loan book. The >$10B regulatory fee drag compounds, and deposit-mix migration reasserts itself, compressing NIM. The stock de-rates toward the peer median (~13x on flat EPS), implying ~14-18% downside. The fortress balance sheet and covered dividend limit how far this goes.

**Base Case ($124-128).** Steady mid-single-digit EPS compounding to ~$7.50-7.60, NIM holding near 3.80%+, one accretive tuck-in acquisition, and CRE stress that stays contained but visible. The multiple holds around 15.5-16.5x. This is roughly in line with consensus targets and implies ~6-10% total return including the dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive — mid-single-digit earnings compounding, a favorable NIM tailwind, and an active acquisition pipeline — but the stock trades at a premium to peers with limited upside to consensus, and the CRE/reserve dynamic caps conviction. The ~16% de-rating has improved risk/reward, but not enough to clear the bar to BUY given the valuation premium and credit-quality watch item.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q4 2026, expected late January 2027) and confirmation of the SpiritBank close. Monitor the OREO/CRE trend and allowance ratio at each print.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- CRE/OREO stabilizes or declines (OREO back below ~$40M, nonaccruals flat-to-down) while the allowance ratio stops falling, removing the under-reserving concern.
- The stock pulls back toward ~$105-108 (~14x current-year EPS), restoring a clear valuation cushion with the growth thesis intact.
- NIM sustains above 3.85% into 2027 with a second accretive acquisition closing, driving next-year EPS estimates above $8.00.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A sharp step-up in CRE/office writedowns or a catch-up provision cycle that materially cuts EPS, confirming the reserve-adequacy concern.
- Sustained oil below ~$60/bbl feeding broader Oklahoma/Texas credit deterioration (rising net charge-offs beyond CRE).
- Continued heavy insider selling combined with a break in the earnings-beat streak, signaling deteriorating internal visibility.

## Conclusion

BancFirst is a conservatively run, over-capitalized Oklahoma banking franchise with record earnings, a falling-rates-help-margin dynamic, and a proven acquisition flywheel. The balance sheet is a genuine fortress, management is aligned and disciplined, and estimate revisions confirm positive earnings momentum. These qualities justify a premium to the regional-bank peer group.

But the premium is largely in the price, the 1.7% yield offers limited support, and consensus targets imply only ~7% upside. Against that, two watch items — a widening CRE/OREO problem set against a *falling* allowance ratio, and material founder-insider selling — argue for patience rather than accumulation at current levels. The favorable NIM trajectory and steady compounding make this a HOLD to own for its quality and 12-month growth path, but the setup lacks the asymmetric upside or valuation cushion required for a BUY. I would become constructive on a pullback toward the low-$100s or on evidence that CRE stress has peaked and reserves are stabilizing.
