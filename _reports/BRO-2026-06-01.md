**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Brown & Brown is the fifth/sixth-largest U.S. insurance broker, an asset-light intermediary that earns commissions and fees without assuming underwriting risk. Its model has historically combined industry-leading margins (38.5% adjusted EBITDAC) with a relentless acquisition engine (725 deals since 1993). The defining event of the past year was the ~$9.83 billion Accession (RSC Topco) acquisition, closed August 1, 2025, the largest in company history, which nearly doubled the balance sheet, lifted goodwill from $8.0B to $15.1B, and added $4.2B of new senior notes plus ~50M shares of dilution.

The market's verdict has been severe. The stock has fallen roughly 50% from its May 2025 high of ~$114 to ~$56-58, including a ~9.7% post-earnings drop on April 27, 2026. The reason is straightforward: consolidated organic revenue growth collapsed from 10.4% in 2024 to 2.8% in 2025 to 0.0% in Q1 2026, with Specialty Distribution organic running negative (−2.0%). The 35% headline revenue surge is almost entirely inorganic. Strip out a one-off +126% spike in contingent commissions and the core franchise is not growing organically. This is partly sector-wide, as property catastrophe rates are softening and casualty rate increases are slowing, compressing commission-based growth across all brokers, but BRO bought a large platform near the top of a hard market and must now integrate it into a softening rate environment.

At ~18x trailing/forward earnings (consensus current-year EPS $4.50, next-year $4.86), BRO trades at roughly half the peer median P/E of 31.7x and below large-cap peers Gallagher (32.5x) and Ryan Specialty (41.1x). The de-rating is real, and a director bought stock on the open market at $57.10 in early May. But estimates are falling (next-year EPS revised −2.3% over 90 days), the organic-growth engine has stalled, and BRO inherited two hard-to-quantify legal/tax tail risks from Accession. The combination of a reasonable multiple and a genuinely stalled core argues for a HOLD until either organic growth re-accelerates or integration synergies become visible in margins. The risk/reward is balanced, not asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Brown & Brown (founded 1939, headquartered in Daytona Beach, FL) is a diversified insurance intermediary operating across retail agency, wholesale brokerage, programs/MGU, and services. As an intermediary it earns a percentage of premium plus fees and generally does not take underwriting risk, though it runs limited captive facilities and a write-your-own flood carrier (WNFIC, 100% ceded to FEMA's NFIP).

Following the Accession close, BRO reorganized from three segments into two:

- **Retail** (~64% of revenue; $1.21B of $1.90B in Q1'26): P&C, employee benefits, private client, and dealer F&I (auto/RV warranty); ~77% commission-based.
- **Specialty Distribution** (~36%; $682M in Q1'26): the former Programs (Arrowhead) and Wholesale (Bridge Specialty) businesses plus new Arrowhead Specialty (captives, reinsurance, warranty, travel/accident); ~81% commission-based.

The competitive model rests on a decentralized, sales- and profit-oriented culture, scale in niche programs and excess-and-surplus (E&S) wholesale, and a disciplined acquisition machine. Revenue is geographically concentrated: FL 16%, MI 9%, MA 8%, CA 6%, NY 6%, GA 5%, with ~10% from the UK. The Brown family (Chairman J. Hyatt Brown, CEO J. Powell Brown, EVP P. Barrett Brown) beneficially owns ~12.6%; all insiders ~13.1%.

## Financial Analysis

**Income statement (Q1 2026 vs Q1 2025):**
- Total revenue rose 35.4% to $1.90B, almost entirely from Accession.
- Net income $426M; GAAP diluted EPS $1.06. The accounting distortion: a $64M non-cash escrow mark-to-market gain *lowered* diluted EPS because ASC 260 excludes the gain from the numerator while adding escrow shares to the denominator. Basic EPS was $1.27; non-GAAP EPS ~$1.39, beating consensus by ~0.8%.
- Adjusted EBITDAC margin held at 38.5% (vs 38.1%), still industry-leading.
- Interest expense jumped 115% to $99M, reflecting the new debt.

The earnings beat track record is consistent (four straight beats: Q1'26 +0.8%, Q4'25 +2.3%, Q3'25 +10.8%, Q2'25 +3.0%), but the magnitude of beats is shrinking and the underlying driver has shifted from organic growth to acquisition contribution and contingent commissions.

**Balance sheet and cash flow:**
- Total debt $7.82B at Q1'26 against ~$30B total assets; goodwill $15.1B; amortizable intangibles ~$4.9B.
- Q1'26 operating cash flow $262M, up 23% YoY. The Altman Z-Score (~1.05) that screens flag as distress is a false positive, standard for an asset-light broker carrying large goodwill and fiduciary liabilities. No covenant issues; in compliance.
- Capital allocation is aggressive given the leverage: a $250M accelerated share repurchase in February 2026 (avg ~$69.93), ~$1.15B remaining authorization, and a 32% dividend increase to $0.165/quarter. Management partly drew the revolver to fund buybacks while carrying $7.8B debt, a confidence signal that also reduces flexibility if integration disappoints or the soft market deepens.

The financial structure is sound for the business model, but the leverage taken on for Accession is real and the debt was added near a rate-cycle peak. With interest expense at $99M/quarter and the deal's organic contribution running negative, the deleveraging-via-cash-flow thesis depends on near-term execution.

## Growth Analysis

The headline 35.4% revenue growth is misleading. The relevant figure is organic growth, which strips out acquisitions:

- Consolidated organic revenue growth: 10.4% (2024) → 2.8% (2025) → **0.0% in Q1 2026** (vs +6.5% prior year).
- Organic revenue *with contingents* was +2.2% in Q1'26, positive only because contingent commissions spiked +126%, a volatile, lower-quality revenue source.
- By segment in Q1'26: Retail organic +1.0% (vs +4.1%); Specialty Distribution organic **−2.0%** (vs +11.3%), hurt by declining CAT property rates.

The mechanism is direct: broker commissions are a percentage of premium, so when property catastrophe rates soften and casualty rate increases slow, commission-based organic growth compresses. Baldwin Group's Q1'26 Market Pulse corroborates the "sharpest divergence between property and casualty pricing," confirming this is partly sector-wide, not purely a BRO execution problem. The harder question is whether Accession, a large platform acquired near the hard-market top, is now a drag rather than an accelerant as property rates fall.

Estimate revisions confirm the deceleration. Next-year EPS has been cut 2.3% over 90 days to $4.86; current-year cut 0.6% to $4.50; the current quarter cut 2.1%. The earnings-revision momentum is negative across every horizon.

Forward growth depends on three things converging favorably: (1) a turn in the property/casualty rate cycle, which is not in management's control; (2) realization of Accession synergies, which requires integration execution; and (3) continued M&A, which is constrained by current leverage. None has a clear near-term catalyst.

## Valuation Assessment

BRO trades at ~18.0x trailing/forward EPS ($3.16 trailing, ~$4.50 forward current-year). Against peers:

|--------|-----|-----------|-----------|
| WTW | 14.6 | 1.5% | -28% |
| ERIE | 19.5 | 2.7% | -44% |
| RYAN | 41.1 | 1.6% | -54% |
| GSHD | 31.7 | — | -67% |
| AJG | 32.5 | 1.4% | -42% |
| **BRO** | **~18.0** | **1.2%** | **-49%** |

Peer median P/E is 31.7x. BRO at ~18x is roughly in line with WTW (14.6x) and Erie (19.5x). The de-rating from a peak ~30x+ multiple to ~18x reflects a real fundamental change: a stock that has fallen ~50% on organic growth going to zero is repricing for a reason, not simply being volatile.

The valuation question is whether ~18x adequately discounts the organic stall. The argument for a low score: the discount to high-growth peers (Ryan, Goosehead, Gallagher) is justified because those names are still growing organically while BRO is not, so the comparison to a 31.7x median overstates the cheapness. The argument for a higher score: BRO's margins remain best-in-class, the franchise is durable, and at ~18x with falling-but-positive earnings and a 1.2% dividend, much of the bad news is priced. On a normalized basis, if organic growth recovers to mid-single digits as the cycle turns, $4.86 next-year EPS at even a 22-23x re-rating implies ~$107-112, near the prior high. But that re-rating requires the organic turn that is not yet visible.

I score valuation moderately positive (6.5). The multiple is cheaper than at any point in the recent past and below most peers, but the discount is partly deserved given negative estimate revisions and zero organic growth. This is a quality franchise at a fair-to-slightly-cheap price with a stalled growth engine. Analyst targets (mean $73, median $71, low $60, high $90) sit 24-58% above the current price, but these are likely stale given the organic deceleration and falling estimates; I would not treat them as floors here, because the revision trend is down, not up.

## Competitive Landscape

BRO's differentiators are durable: a high-margin Specialty Distribution/programs platform now enlarged by Accession, decentralized sales culture, and a 30-year acquisition track record. It competes with Marsh McLennan, Aon, Gallagher, WTW, and fast-growing mid-caps like Baldwin Group and Ryan Specialty.

The competitive trajectory is mixed and tilting slightly negative. Two specific pressures: first, a competitor described as a "start-up U.S. broker" is actively poaching BRO teammates and the customers they bring, costing ~$10M of Retail core commissions in Q1'26 and generating multi-jurisdiction litigation. In a decentralized, relationship-driven model this is a direct manifestation of the key-employee risk. Second, BRO's organic underperformance versus still-growing peers (Ryan, Gallagher) suggests it is losing relative ground in the current cycle, even if the level of its franchise position is intact. Five-year TSR (174) now lags the peer group (184), a reversal from prior outperformance.

The moat has not broken, but the trajectory has softened, with share of organic growth being lost to both the soft market and specific competitors. That argues for a competitive score below the historical 7.5, reflecting a leader whose relative momentum has eroded.

## Risk Assessment

**Accession integration and leverage.** ~$26M of acquisition/integration costs in Q1'26 alone; failure to realize synergies, while servicing interest, would impair the deleveraging path. The deal was struck near the hard-market top.

**Inherited captive litigation (the standout idiosyncratic risk).** Accession subsidiary Oxford Risk Management Group faces multi-jurisdiction litigation over the 2024 restructuring of financial-guarantee/final-judgment-preservation policies in segregated captive cells. BRO discloses results "would be negatively impacted if the costs of the claims… exceed the value of the cash within the captives, as well as the cash and stock held in the indemnity escrow." A $552M escrow (~$288M shares + $268M cash) secures the indemnities, but the disclosure language leaves open loss beyond the escrow. This is the single most idiosyncratic risk a standardized screen would miss.

**831(b) micro-captive / IRS exposure.** Accession's advisory business helps clients form 831(b) captives, an area where the IRS has investigated peers as "tax shelter promoters." Adverse guidance "could harm our business."

**Soft market / commission compression.** Already underway and partly sector-wide; the binding constraint on organic growth.

**Goodwill impairment.** $15B goodwill, last tested 11/30/2025 with no impairment. A sustained stock decline could trigger interim testing.

**Geographic concentration.** FL 16% concentration exposes BRO to Florida-specific catastrophe and regulatory dynamics.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 36.6% against 1-year realized volatility of 28.4%, rich by 8.3 points, indicating the market is pricing elevated uncertainty into the next move. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±7.2%. Put/call skew is 4.9% (elevated put demand) and the P/C open-interest ratio rose from 0.52 to 0.95 since early May, signaling rising hedging or bearish positioning. The term structure is flat (36.6% near ≈ 35.9% far), so uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a single dated event. For a HOLD, the rich IV and rising put OI reinforce caution: the options market does not see a clean upside catalyst and is paying up for downside protection. Short interest is moderate at 6.3% of float (4.7 days to cover), so this is not a crowded short.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Property/casualty rate softening is cyclical; as the cycle bottoms, organic growth re-accelerates toward mid-single digits, and Accession synergies plus contingent commissions lift margins above the current level. The ~18x multiple re-rates toward the low end of the historical range (22-24x) on $4.86+ EPS, implying ~$107-117. Best-in-class margins, a proven M&A machine, and aligned family ownership (13.1% insider stake, negative comp-actually-paid in 2025) support the recovery. The director open-market purchase at $57.10 marks a floor. Bull target ~$95-100 over 12-18 months.

**Bear Case.** Organic growth stays at or near zero as the soft market deepens; Accession is revealed to have been bought at the cycle top and becomes a drag; the Oxford/FG-Policies litigation produces a loss beyond the escrow; goodwill impairment testing is triggered by the depressed share price; and competitor poaching continues to bleed Retail commissions. Estimates continue falling and the multiple compresses toward WTW's 14.6x on ~$4.50 EPS, implying ~$66, with downside to ~$48-50 if organic growth turns negative and an impairment hits sentiment. Bear target ~$48-50, roughly −15% from current.

The two cases are genuinely balanced. The valuation reset has removed much of the prior froth, but there is no identifiable near-term catalyst to break the organic deceleration, and estimates are still being revised down.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** A reasonable multiple coexists with a stalled core and falling estimates, leaving balanced risk/reward.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Consolidated organic revenue growth re-accelerates to ≥4% for two consecutive quarters (excluding the contingent-commission distortion).
2. Next-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive (vs the current −2.3%/90d), confirming the deceleration has bottomed.
3. Clear evidence of Accession synergy realization lifting adjusted EBITDAC margin above ~39.5%, combined with a multiple still below ~20x forward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Consolidated organic growth turns negative for two consecutive quarters.
2. A goodwill impairment charge, or interim impairment testing disclosed in a filing.
3. An adverse development in the Oxford/FG-Policies litigation indicating loss beyond the escrow, or an unfavorable IRS ruling on 831(b) captives.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, late July 2026) is the key checkpoint; it will show whether organic growth has stabilized or continued deteriorating, and whether contingent commissions are masking further core erosion.

## Conclusion

Brown & Brown is a high-quality franchise that has been repriced for a real problem. The headline revenue growth is almost entirely inorganic; the organic engine has gone from 10.4% to 0.0% in two years, and Specialty Distribution is shrinking organically as property rates soften. The drawdown to ~18x earnings reflects this, and the discount to peers is partly deserved given negative estimate revisions. The bull case requires a rate-cycle turn and integration execution that are not yet visible; the bear case requires the soft market to deepen and the Accession bet to look like a top-of-cycle mistake. With falling estimates, no near-term catalyst, rich implied volatility, and a genuine but hard-to-quantify litigation tail from Accession, the prudent stance is to hold and watch the Q2 organic-growth print. The director's small open-market buy and best-in-class margins keep this from being an AVOID, but the absence of an organic-growth inflection keeps it from being a BUY.
