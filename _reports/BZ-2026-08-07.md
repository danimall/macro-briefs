## Executive Summary

Kanzhun operates BOSS Zhipin, China's largest online recruitment platform by average monthly active users on the company's own accounting, monetizing 7.1 million paying enterprise accounts through a direct-chat, algorithmically matched hiring model. The financial profile is unusual for a China-domiciled ADR: 85% gross margins, roughly $2.87B of net cash and liquid investments against zero interest-bearing debt, a 4.33x current ratio, and Q1 2026 operating cash flow of RMB1,190M (+18.6%) that covered adjusted net income 1.39x. At $16.09 the equity carries about $6.43 per ADS of net cash — 40% of the price — leaving an enterprise value near $4.32B against consensus FY2026 adjusted earnings of roughly $566M. Stripping the after-tax interest income embedded in those earnings (to avoid crediting the cash twice), core operating adjusted earnings of approximately $494M put the enterprise at 8.7x. That is a low bar for a marketplace with 39% adjusted operating margins.

The reasons the market has taken the stock down 36% from its September 2025 high of $25.26 are also concrete. Revenue growth decelerated from +31.9% (FY2023) to +23.6% (FY2024) to +12.4% (FY2025) to +7.6% in Q1 2026. Revenue per paying enterprise customer declined roughly 2.4% year over year on a directional derivation, with paid customers up 10.9% against enterprise revenue up 8.2% — growth is coming from a longer, cheaper tail. FY2025's doubling of operating income came from cost reduction, not from selling more: revenue rose RMB912M while operating income rose RMB1,291M, which is only arithmetically possible because absolute costs fell. Q1 2026 decomposes that cleanly: total operating costs fell 3.0%, share-based compensation fell RMB60.8M (−24.1%), and cash operating costs actually rose 1.3%. The GAAP margin expansion is real but leans on a non-cash tailwind with a finite life.

We rate BZ ACCUMULATE rather than BUY. Probability-weighted expected return of roughly +14% (base $19.50, bull $24.50, bear $12.50) is positive but not compelling given three constraints: a Q2 2026 print expected within two weeks that the options market prices at a ±19.7% move with elevated put demand and near-term implied volatility at 72.1% against 36.4% realized; China hiring demand where broad macro context could not be verified from primary sources this cycle [unverified]; and material verification gaps, including the FY2025 20-F risk-factor, VIE-structure and PCAOB disclosures, which could not be read this cycle. Buy in stages, complete on confirmation.

## Company Overview

BOSS Zhipin, launched in 2014, replaced the résumé-database job-board model with instant two-way chat between hiring decision-makers and candidates, matched by proprietary recommendation algorithms. Job seekers use the platform free; employers pay for exposure, premium candidate access, and value-added tools, and management now references a newer performance-based monetization model.

The revenue base is effectively single-line. Online recruitment services to enterprise customers were RMB2,057.8M of RMB2,068.8M total Q1 2026 revenue — 99.5%. The job-seeker value-added services line fell to RMB11.0M from RMB21.9M, a reduction the company describes as deliberate simplification since Q3 2025 to prioritize engagement. There is no offsetting segment if enterprise hiring demand softens.

Scale metrics as disclosed: 7.1 million trailing-twelve-month paying enterprise customers at 3/31/2026 (+10.9%), average Q1 MAU of 60.9 million (+5.7%), with March 2026 MAU above 72 million — a pronounced intra-quarter pattern management attributes to a later Chinese New Year shifting peak spring hiring into March and April. Each ADS represents two Class A ordinary shares; the Class A designation implies a dual-class structure whose voting mechanics could not be verified.

Structurally, this is a Cayman-incorporated foreign private issuer filing 20-F and 6-K, dual-listed on Nasdaq and HKEX (2076). It does not file 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, DEF 14A, or Section 16 insider reports. The absence of Form 4 data is a structural artifact and supports no inference in either direction about insider activity.

Three exact matches between XBRL USD figures reverse-translated at 6.9930 and the issuer's own FY2025 balance sheet (total assets RMB24,568.7M, total liabilities RMB4,486.9M, cash RMB4,104.9M) validate the reconstruction method.

**The margin composition matters more than the margin level.** Backing out selling/marketing plus G&A for FY2025 gives approximately RMB2,914M (35.3% of revenue) against RMB3,127M (42.5%) in FY2024 — an absolute decline of ~RMB213M — while R&D fell ~RMB162M. Q1 2026 shows the same pattern and permits precise attribution: revenue +7.6%, total operating cost and expenses −3.0%, share-based compensation from RMB251.8M to RMB191.0M, and cash operating costs ex-SBC from RMB1,239.4M to RMB1,255.3M (+1.3%). GAAP operating margin reached 30.1% versus 22.9%; adjusted operating margin moved a more modest +340bp to 39.4% from 36.0%. The adjusted figure is the honest read of underlying improvement.

Two readings fit these numbers. SBC decline may be the natural roll-off of pre-IPO grant cohorts, in which case retention grants for AI engineering talent would stall GAAP margin expansion without any business deterioration. Or it may reflect durable headcount rationalization in a high-incremental-margin marketplace, in which case cash margins keep improving. The evidence does not settle it, and the resolution is worth several turns of multiple.

**Earnings quality requires care.** Of RMB1,424.7M Q1 2026 pre-tax income, RMB780.9M (55%) was interest and investment income, including a RMB614.1M fair-value gain on an investee that completed an IPO in January 2026. GAAP net income rose 119.8%; adjusted net income rose 12.1% to RMB856.2M and adjusted diluted EPS per ADS rose 8.7% to RMB1.87. The accounting corroborates: long-term investments rose from RMB1,898.2M to RMB3,195.5M in the quarter and deferred tax liabilities rose RMB137.1M against the disclosed RMB153.5M tax effect. This caveat makes net-income-based ratios (P/E, ROE, net margin) noisy while revenue, operating income and cash flow are clean. It also means the company now carries a RMB3.2B equity portfolio (~13% of assets) capable of swinging quarters in both directions.

**Balance sheet.** At 3/31/2026: cash RMB3,351.8M plus short-term time deposits RMB5,953.6M plus short-term investments RMB11,192.4M = RMB19,826.5M (US$2,874.2M at 6.8980), plus RMB3,195.5M of illiquid long-term investments. Total liabilities RMB5,092.7M, of which RMB3,560.6M is deferred revenue — employer prepayments, with no borrowings line item present and line items summing to the reported total. Equity is 80% of assets. Deferred revenue should be read as a prepayment float and a coincident indicator; the company has not defined it as backlog or remaining performance obligations, and the PRC VAT law effective January 1, 2026 reduced the 3/31/26 balance by RMB45.6M versus the prior regime, breaking cross-period comparability slightly.

FY2025 ROE of 15.7% and ROA of 12.5% materially understate the operating business, because roughly 80% of the balance sheet earns treasury-like yields. That is the capital-efficiency criticism, and it is the criticism the new distribution policy is designed to answer.

**Capital intensity.** Capex fell from RMB956M (FY2023) and RMB856M (FY2024) to RMB119M (FY2025), an 86% drop, while net property/equipment/software declined sequentially from RMB1,245.0M to RMB1,143.6M and Q1 commentary cites higher cloud service fees, server depreciation, and bandwidth cost. Technology spending moved from capex to opex; free cash flow optics improved while the AI compute run-rate now compresses gross and operating margin instead. Do not read the capex collapse as reduced investment.

**Data reconciliation note.** The supplied profile shows trailing and forward P/E both at 15.47 and EPS of 0.84369, which do not reconcile with each other at any price in the file. Our derived TTM GAAP figure — FY2025 net income RMB2,735.2M less Q1 2025 RMB512.1M plus Q1 2026 RMB1,125.8M = RMB3,348.9M ≈ US$479M — gives a P/E of 15.0x at $16.09, close to the reported trailing figure; the EPS field appears stale or period-mismatched. Separately, the consensus EPS series ($2.12 current quarter, $8.73 current year, $9.80 next year) is denominated in RMB per ADS, matching the RMB1.87 Q1 actual in the surprise table. Read as USD it would imply an absurd 1.8x multiple.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is decelerating and cyclical, with a guided near-term re-acceleration that has not yet been confirmed.

The sequence is +31.9% → +23.6% → +12.4% → +7.6% (Q1 2026), with Q2 2026 guided to RMB2.38–2.42B, or +13.2% to +15.1%. That guidance range is internally consistent (both endpoints imply Q2 2025 revenue of RMB2,102.5M), and a first-half 2026 blend of ~RMB4,469M against RMB4,025.8M gives +11.0%. Management attributes the Q1/Q2 shape to Chinese New Year timing pushing peak hiring into March and April, corroborated by March MAU above 72 million versus the 60.9 million quarterly average and by wire coverage of the May 20 call. The alternative — genuine January–February softness that recovered — is not excluded, in which case the +11.0% first-half blend is the better run-rate than the Q2 guide alone.

The more important growth question is mix. Paid enterprise customers grew 10.9% while enterprise revenue grew 8.2%, implying revenue per paying customer of RMB289.8 versus RMB297.1, down ~2.4%. That ratio mixes a quarterly flow with a trailing-twelve-month count, so treat it as directional rather than as an ARPU. Three explanations compete: deliberate downmarket penetration into blue-collar and micro-enterprise accounts with structurally smaller tickets; the transition to performance-based monetization, which can recognize less revenue per account up front; or price concession in a soft hiring market. The first two are benign to good; the third means the 39% adjusted operating margin is borrowed. MAU growth of +5.7% trailing paid-customer growth of +10.9% indicates a maturing funnel, which raises the stakes on monetization per account.

Consensus implies mid-teens adjusted EPS growth: FY2026 RMB8.73 and FY2027 RMB9.80 per ADS, roughly +12% year over year, aided by buyback-driven share reduction rather than by revenue acceleration. Estimate revisions are flat — current year +1.7% over 90 days, next year −1.8%, 30-day changes zero. The strong revision signal that would support a full position is absent.

AI positioning is a stated priority — management claims its open-sourced Nanbeige 4.1-3B model was ranked jointly first among sub-4-billion-parameter models by Artificial Analysis, an independent evaluator, and describes AI assistants on both sides of the marketplace. That claim is the company's characterization of a third-party ranking and was not independently verified. It has not yet shown up as revenue per customer.

## Valuation Assessment

At $16.09, market capitalization is 447.1M ADS × $16.09 = $7,194M. Net cash and liquid investments of $2,874.2M equal $6.43 per ADS, or 40.0% of the price. Enterprise value is therefore approximately $4,320M, excluding the $463M of illiquid long-term equity investments (including them, ~$3,857M).

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV / FY2025 revenue ($1,182M) | 3.65x |
| EV / FY2025 GAAP operating income ($352M) | 12.3x |
| EV / FY2025 est. adjusted operating income (~$481M) | ~9.0x |
| P/E on FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS (RMB8.73 ≈ $1.25) | 12.9x |
| P/E on FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS (RMB9.80 ≈ $1.40) | 11.5x |
| EV / FY2026 consensus adjusted net income (~$566M) | 7.6x |
| EV / FY2026 core adjusted earnings ex-interest (~$494M) | 8.7x |
| TTM GAAP P/E | 15.0x (17.4x ex-investee gain) |

The FY2025 adjusted operating income figure rests on an interpolated ~RMB900M SBC estimate, not a disclosure, and should be treated as a sensitivity. The consensus EPS figures are denominated in RMB per ADS; USD-per-ADS equivalents shown at 6.898.

The supplied peer set is not usable for relative valuation: Zillow at 134.6x, Baidu with negative earnings, Nebius at 76.8x. These are heterogeneous businesses with unrelated capital structures. The more defensible anchor is absolute: 8.7x enterprise value on core operating adjusted earnings for a business with 85% gross margins, 39% adjusted operating margins, 7.1 million paying accounts and no customer concentration, growing adjusted EPS in the low-to-mid teens.

The argument that this is mispriced rather than a value trap rests on two verifiable items. First, the cash is being returned. The March 18, 2026 board action raised repurchase authorization to US$400M through August 28, 2027, and the company stated it expects to allocate no less than 50% of prior-year adjusted net income to combined dividends and buybacks for each of the next three years starting 2026. Disclosed 2026 repurchases reached above RMB2.06B by the July 1 release, approximately US$299M, which at observed transaction prices of RMB45–50 per ordinary share implies roughly 42–45M ordinary shares, or 4.5–4.8% of shares outstanding, retired in six months. Second, the multiple has compressed while reported profitability roughly doubled and cash built, which is a sentiment-and-multiple event rather than an earnings event.

The argument that the market is right rests on the margin composition (SBC-driven), the revenue-per-customer decline, and the possibility that the buyback support decelerates. On the last point: US$299M against a US$400M authorization is about 75% consumed in six months of a seventeen-month window. Either an increase is coming — the pattern of repeated increases suggests it might — or the pace slows sharply in the second half. The releases never disclose remaining capacity and it is unclear whether the ceiling is measured from program inception, which would make the overshoot more acute.

We score valuation 6.4. Cheapness alone is not a thesis, and this cheapness sits alongside genuine deceleration and a China regulatory discount we could not size from primary disclosure. Analyst consensus (5 strong buy, 16 buy, 1 hold; mean target $20.86, median $21.08, range $15.74–$26.25) corroborates the direction but is not independent evidence.

## Competitive Landscape

This is the weakest-evidence section. No competitor filings, market-share studies, or analyst reports were obtained; the "largest by average MAU" claim is the company's own, corroborated only by its disclosed user metrics.

What can be defended: the direct-chat, mobile-native, algorithmically matched model is a structurally different product from a résumé database, and the outcome shows in the customer base — 7.1 million paying enterprises is a mass-market long tail rather than a large-account sales book. A long tail is difficult for a competitor to attack account by account, and it eliminates customer-concentration risk by construction. Gross margin of 85.1% in FY2025 improved from 83.1%, and selling/marketing spend is now shrinking in absolute RMB while revenue grows, which is consistent with acquisition costs falling as network effects mature.

What complicates it: MAU growth of +5.7% now trails paid-customer growth of +10.9%, which in turn outpaces enterprise revenue growth of +8.2%. That ordering is the signature of a market where volume is available but pricing is not. Whether that reflects Kanzhun's own downmarket strategy or competitive pricing pressure is the single most valuable unresolved question, and it is why we score competitive position 6.0 despite an apparently strong absolute position; the trajectory of monetization is currently negative.

Also unverified: whether short-video and social platforms are taking recruitment share, blue-collar hiring cycle data, and relative share against other China recruitment platforms.

## Risk Assessment

1. **China hiring demand, with no diversification.** 99.5% of revenue is one line, tied to enterprise hiring. Management's own framing of Q1 depended on "continued improvement in recruitment demand during the peak spring recruitment season." Current Chinese macro conditions could not be verified from primary sources this cycle [unverified].

2. **Margin durability.** SBC fell 24.1% year over year in Q1 and accounted for more than all of the reported operating-cost decline while cash costs rose 1.3%. Normalization of grants for AI talent retention stops GAAP margin expansion with no change in the business.

3. **Structural tax burden rising.** Q1 income tax expense rose 293% to RMB298.9M. Beyond the RMB153.5M tied to the investment mark, the company disclosed a Pillar Two global-minimum-tax top-up of RMB15.6M and withholding tax of RMB7.6M — new recurring lines that also constrain the tax efficiency of repatriating offshore cash for buybacks.

4. **Mark-to-market earnings volatility.** RMB3,195.5M of long-term investments, up RMB1,297M in one quarter, can now swing quarters by more than operating income does.

5. **Scaling venture losses.** Net loss attributable to non-controlling interests rose from RMB6.0M to RMB30.9M, meaning income attributable to Kanzhun shareholders exceeded consolidated net income. Kanzhun's own share of these losses is undisclosed and likely larger. Ownership percentages are not disclosed, so the total drag cannot be sized.

6. **China-listing and data-security overhang, unverified.** The FY2025 20-F risk factors, VIE-structure disclosure, HFCAA/PCAOB status and any PRC data-security matters could not be read (SEC retrieval returned HTTP 403). Nothing here characterizes current 20-F language. For a platform holding tens of millions of résumés, CAC oversight is a live category of risk that we cannot size from primary sources. The HKEX listing is a partial mitigant against US-listing impairment.

7. **Governance opacity.** Founder Jonathan Peng Zhao holds both chair and CEO roles. The Class A designation implies Class B super-voting shares whose count and ratio we could not verify. The Q1 2026 results were presented by a Deputy CFO rather than a CFO, with no filing located confirming a departure — unresolved. The June 25, 2026 AGM renewed a general share-issuance mandate alongside the repurchase mandate, and weighted-average basic shares rose 6.5% year over year in Q1 (871.0M → 927.7M); the timing largely reconciles because Q1 financing outflow was only RMB359.3M and the bulk of 2026 repurchases occurred in April–May, but the source of roughly 56.7M of 2025 issuance is not fully explained.

8. **Currency translation.** All operations and cash are RMB-denominated against a USD-priced ADS; the rate moved from 6.9930 to 6.8980 in one quarter, ~1.4%, flowing directly into reported USD figures.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 72.1% against 36.4% one-year realized, a 35.7-point spread at the 75th percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (72.1% at 13 days versus 54.7% at 160 days) and the source characterizing the 18.7% put/call skew as elevated put demand. The nearest expiry prices a ±19.7% move. This is consistent with a Q2 2026 earnings print in the second half of August (a 6-K dated August 6 exists whose contents could not be retrieved). The market treats the coming print as a genuine two-sided binary and is charging a high price for protection. For a prospective buyer, expensive near-term optionality and elevated put demand argue for staging entry rather than completing it before the event. The chain is thin — 21 contracts across four expirations — and reported put/call volume and open-interest ratios are 0.00, i.e., absent, so no positioning inference should be drawn from those fields. Short interest of 5.0% of float with 5.4 days to cover is unremarkable and creates neither squeeze pressure nor crowding.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (→ $24.50, +52%)

Q2 delivers within or above the RMB2.38–2.42B guide, breaking the three-year deceleration and demonstrating that Q1 weakness was Chinese New Year timing. Paid customer growth holds near 10% while revenue per account stabilizes, confirming that the mix shift is deliberate downmarket penetration plus performance-based pricing rather than discounting. Adjusted operating margin holds near 39% as cash cost discipline persists, with SBC normalization absorbed by operating leverage. The board tops up the repurchase authorization, sustaining a material buyback pace against a 40%-net-cash balance sheet. On FY2027 core operating adjusted earnings of roughly $558M, a 14.5x enterprise multiple plus net cash supports $24.50, near the September 2025 high and inside the $26.25 top analyst target.

### Bear Case (→ $12.50, −22%)

Chinese hiring stays soft through the second half. Revenue growth reverts to high single digits after the seasonal catch-up, revenue per paying customer keeps sliding as pricing concessions defend share, and SBC normalizes upward, compressing adjusted operating margin back toward 34-36%. Core adjusted earnings flatten near $450M. The repurchase authorization is exhausted without an increase and the buyback bid disappears, at which point the equity trades on cyclical China internet sentiment at roughly 6x enterprise value plus cash, or about $12.50 — essentially the June 25, 2026 low of $12.57. A CAC or data-security action, or an adverse HFCAA development, would take it lower and is not sizeable from available disclosure.

### Base Case (→ $19.50, +21%)

The first-half blend of +11.0% revenue growth is the honest run-rate; FY2026 and FY2027 adjusted EPS land near consensus of RMB8.73 and RMB9.80 per ADS. Adjusted operating margin settles in the 37-39% band as cash-cost discipline partly offsets SBC normalization. Buybacks continue at a reduced but material pace, shrinking share count 3-5% annually. Core operating adjusted earnings of ~$558M in FY2027 at a 10.5x enterprise multiple, plus $2.87B of net cash on 447M ADS, gives $19.50 — modestly below the $20.86 consensus mean, which we discount for China macro.

Probability weighting 50% base / 20% bull / 30% bear gives an expected return of approximately +14%: (0.50 × 21%) + (0.20 × 52%) + (0.30 × −22%).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: ACCUMULATE.** Positive expected risk-adjusted return with a genuine asset-backed floor, held below the BUY bar by three factors: an unresolved binary two weeks out that options price at ±19.7%; flat estimate revisions rather than the rising-estimate confirmation that would justify a full position; and unverifiable China regulatory, structure and governance disclosures that impose a confidence discount.

**Base case target: $19.50 (+21% from $16.09).** Math: FY2027 core operating adjusted earnings ~$558M (consensus FY2027 adjusted net income ~$635M less ~$77M after-tax interest income) × 10.5x EV = $5.86B, plus $2.87B net cash = $8.73B ÷ 447.1M ADS = $19.53.

**Bull case target: $24.50 (+52%).** 14.5x on the same core earnings plus net cash.

**Bear case target: $12.50 (−22%).** 6.0x on ~$450M of compressed core earnings plus net cash, at the June 2026 low.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, with the first checkpoint at the Q2 2026 print expected in the second half of August 2026, and the decisive checkpoint at the Q3 print in November 2026, which will show whether the seasonal catch-up was timing or a durable re-acceleration.

**Staged entry plan.** Establish one-third to one-half of target position now, against the $6.43 per ADS of net cash and the continued repurchase pace. Complete the position after the Q2 print if revenue lands at or above the RMB2.40B guidance midpoint with revenue per paying customer flat or better, or on weakness below $14.50 with fundamentals intact (which would put the enterprise below 7x core adjusted earnings). Do not complete the position into the print.

**Upgrade to BUY requires:**
- Q2 revenue at or above RMB2.40B with paid enterprise customer growth ≥10% and revenue per paying customer flat or rising year over year;
- adjusted operating margin ≥39% with cash operating costs growing slower than revenue, evidencing that discipline is not purely SBC roll-off;
- a repurchase authorization increase disclosed alongside continued pace above ~RMB800M per quarter.

**Downgrade to HOLD or REDUCE on:**
- Q2 revenue below the RMB2.38B low end, or a Q3 guide implying under 8% growth;
- adjusted operating margin below 36% (the Q1 2025 level) as SBC normalizes;
- revenue per paying customer declining more than 5% year over year for two consecutive quarters;
- repurchase disclosures falling below ~RMB300M per quarter without an authorization increase;
- any CAC, data-security, or HFCAA/PCAOB development affecting the platform or the listing.

**Reassessment date:** immediately on the Q2 2026 release, and again on the FY2026 20-F, which should close the verification gaps on risk factors, dual-class voting, full-year non-GAAP figures, operating cash flow, the declared dividend, remaining repurchase capacity, and the 2025 share-count reconciliation.

## Conclusion

Kanzhun offers a rare combination in China internet: a debt-free balance sheet with 40% of market capitalization in net cash, 85% gross margins, a material repurchase pace executed at depressed prices, and an enterprise value of roughly 8.7x core operating adjusted earnings. Against that, revenue growth has decelerated for three consecutive years, monetization per paying customer is falling, the reported margin expansion is attributable in full to a finite non-cash share-based compensation roll-off rather than to lower cash costs, and the principal China regulatory and structural disclosures could not be verified from primary sources this cycle.

The score trajectory (6.5 → 6.4 → 6.5, with recommendations moving HOLD → BUY → ACCUMULATE) reflects a thesis that has been consistently valuation-led. Nothing fundamental has changed since the Q1 print: estimates are flat, the balance sheet is intact, the buyback has continued, and the price is 4% higher than at the prior review and 28% above the June low. The modest reduction in overall score to 6.3 reflects two specific items rather than price action — the confirmation that roughly 75% of the repurchase authorization was consumed in six months, which puts the second-half buyback bid in question, and the arrival of a dated binary event the options market prices at a ±19.7% move with elevated put demand.

ACCUMULATE. Buy in stages against the cash floor, hold back capacity for the print, and require Q2 revenue at the guidance midpoint with stable revenue per paying customer before completing the position.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the "8%-annualized buyback yield" claim was removed as not derivable from the disclosed cumulative repurchase figures (RMB2.06B ≈ US$299M in 1H2026 is a cumulative, not run-rate, figure and the annualization was unsupported); and the specific China macro assertions (July manufacturing PMI contraction, Politburo declining consumption stimulus) were removed as untraceable to the sources — the valuation-led thesis is unaffected, but the near-term "buyback bid" support in the base case rests on a pace assumption the sources do not confirm.
