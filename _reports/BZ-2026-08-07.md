## Executive Summary

Kanzhun operates BOSS Zhipin, China's largest online recruitment platform by user scale, monetizing 7.1 million paying enterprise accounts at roughly RMB 1,190 (~US$172) per account per year. The financial profile is unusual for a China ADR: 85.1% gross margin, a 39.4% adjusted operating margin in Q1 2026, no interest-bearing debt, US$2.88bn of cash, deposits and short-term investments equal to 40% of market capitalization, negative working capital funded by customer prepayments, and capital expenditure that collapsed 86% in FY2025 to $17.0M. Strip the net liquid assets and the operating business trades at approximately 8.9x forward operating earnings — 12.7x reported FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS of RMB 8.73 per ADS, before removing the interest income embedded in that figure.

The unresolved question is growth. Back-converting the USD convenience-translated filings to the functional currency, RMB revenue growth ran approximately 32% (FY2023), 24% (FY2024) and 12-14% (FY2025), then printed +7.6% in Q1 2026. Management guided Q2 to RMB 2.38-2.42bn, or +13.2% to +15.1%. The evidence favors a calendar explanation: with Q1 average MAU of 60.9M and March MAU stated as exceeding 72M, the implied January-February average is at most 55.4M, meaning March ran at least 30% above the pre-holiday months, and sequential deferred revenue rose RMB 324.6M (approximately RMB 370M before the new PRC VAT law reduced the balance by RMB 45.6M). That is consistent with billings shifting from Q1 into Q2 on a mid-February Chinese New Year, though it is not proof. The Q2 print, due late August, is the discriminating test.

We move from BUY to ACCUMULATE. The expected return is positive — a probability-weighted $18.19 against $16.09, or +13% — but three things keep it below the BUY bar. First, the near-term options chain prices a ±14.9% move on the nearest expiry with 72.1% ATM implied volatility against 36.4% realized, in backwardation, into a print that resolves the central growth question. Second, FY2027 consensus has drifted down 1.8% over 90 days, so estimate momentum does not corroborate the reacceleration case. Third, the FY2025 20-F risk-factor section could not be read, leaving VIE enforceability, PCAOB/HFCAA status and CAC/PIPL compliance unverified for the current period. Buy on weakness and around the print rather than into it.

## Company Overview

BOSS Zhipin's product is direct two-way chat between the hiring decision-maker and the candidate, replacing the résumé-database-and-subscription model used by legacy Chinese incumbents. The economic consequence is visible in the customer file: 7.1 million paying enterprise accounts in the twelve months to March 31, 2026 (+10.9% year over year), which means effectively zero customer concentration and a base dominated by micro-enterprises and SMEs that cannot afford recruiter-led services.

Monetization is now essentially one-sided. Q1 2026 revenue of RMB 2,068.8M split RMB 2,057.8M (99.47%) from enterprise online recruitment services and RMB 11.0M (0.53%) from job-seeker value-added services, the latter down from RMB 21.9M a year earlier after management deliberately simplified those features from Q3 2025. That RMB 10.9M contraction alone accounts for roughly 57 basis points of the Q1 headline growth gap: enterprise revenue grew 8.2% while total revenue grew 7.6%.

Average monthly active users were 60.9M in Q1 2026, +5.7% year over year, with March above 72M. The company has open-sourced an in-house model, Nanbeige 4.1-3B, which management says was ranked jointly first among sub-4B-parameter models by Artificial Analysis. No AI-attributable revenue figure appears in any issuer material we reviewed; third-party assertions that AI revenue is "meaningful" are unverified.

Kanzhun files 20-F and 6-K reports as a Cayman-incorporated foreign private issuer, and is dual-primary listed in Hong Kong (HKEX: 2076), which materially mitigates US delisting risk by providing a migration venue.

## Financial Analysis

**Currency translation matters here.** The SEC XBRL series is stated in USD convenience translations computed at each fiscal year's own period-end rate. Read naively, FY2025 revenue growth is +17.3% ($1,007.7M → $1,182.2M). Back-converting at the year-end rates each 20-F would have used reproduces reported RMB revenue for FY2022 (RMB 4,511M), FY2023 (RMB 5,952M) and FY2024 (RMB 7,356M), which validates the method; applying a December 2025 rate of 6.95-7.10 gives FY2025 revenue of RMB 8,217-8,394M, or **+11.7% to +14.1%**. Any model built on the USD series overstates growth by roughly 450 basis points and therefore understates the multiple being paid.

**Operating leverage has been the dominant earnings driver.** GAAP operating margin progressed from −24.3% (FY2021) to −2.9%, +9.8%, +15.9% and +29.8% (FY2025). Q1 2026 GAAP operating margin was 30.1% versus 22.9% a year earlier, a 719bp expansion on 7.6% revenue growth, achieved because total operating cost and expenses **fell 3.0% in absolute RMB**: cost of revenues −4.1%, sales and marketing +2.2%, R&D +0.05%, G&A −16.4%.

Roughly a third of the GAAP improvement is share-based compensation deflation — SBC fell RMB 251.8M to RMB 191.0M (−24.1%), a RMB 60.8M tailwind against RMB 183.8M of total GAAP operating income improvement. That is a genuine economic benefit (less dilution) but it is not a repeatable lever, and the June 2026 executive grants suggest SBC could re-accelerate. The cleaner read is the adjusted operating margin, which excludes SBC entirely and still expanded 343bp to 39.4%.

**Earnings quality: the GAAP quarter is not representative.** Q1 2026 GAAP net income of RMB 1,125.8M (+119.8%) includes a RMB 614.1M fair-value gain on an investee that IPO'd in January 2026; net of RMB 153.5M tax, the after-tax one-off is RMB 460.6M, or 41% of reported net income. Adjusted net income grew a far more modest +12.1% to RMB 856.2M, with adjusted diluted EPS per ADS of RMB 1.87 versus RMB 1.72. The trailing GAAP P/E of 19.2x at $16.09 is flattered accordingly.

Stripping the one-off, recurring interest and investment income of approximately RMB 166.8M represents roughly 17% of adjusted pre-tax income. That is an interest-rate-sensitive, non-operating contribution sitting on a RMB 19.8bn liquid pile; falling PRC and USD deposit rates would be a direct earnings headwind independent of hiring demand. Long-term equity investments grew from RMB 1,898.2M to RMB 3,195.5M in a single quarter, importing equity-market beta into a recruitment company's reported earnings.

**Balance sheet.** At March 31, 2026: cash RMB 3,351.8M, short-term time deposits RMB 5,953.6M, short-term investments RMB 11,192.4M, long-term time deposits RMB 52.1M — a company-stated liquid balance of RMB 19,826.5M (US$2,874.2M). Total liabilities of RMB 5,092.7M reconcile entirely to operating items (payables RMB 136.2M, deferred revenue RMB 3,560.6M, other payables RMB 1,083.5M, lease liabilities RMB 123.6M, deferred tax RMB 188.8M). There is no interest-bearing debt. Current ratio 4.33x, or 16.3x excluding deferred revenue, which is a performance obligation rather than a cash claim. Total liabilities to equity 0.248x. On our own calculation, an Altman-style Z score exceeds 7 driven overwhelmingly by the market-equity-to-liabilities term; distress risk is not a live consideration.

**Cash flow.** Q1 2026 operating cash flow was RMB 1,190.1M (+18.6%), 57.5% of revenue. We do not annualize that: approximately RMB 370M of the inflow is working-capital float from post-holiday prepayments, and Q1 is the seasonal billings peak. As a labeled sensitivity, four times Q1 gives RMB 4.76bn (~$690M); stripping the deferred-revenue build gives RMB 3.28bn (~$476M). The capex inflection is the more durable point: $134.6M (FY2023) → $117.3M (FY2024) → $17.0M (FY2025), with net PP&E and software falling from RMB 1,245.0M to RMB 1,143.6M over Q1 2026 as depreciation exceeded additions. Rising "cloud service fees and server depreciation" indicate rented rather than owned compute, which preserves free cash flow but limits control over unit costs.

**Tax.** GAAP effective rate 21.0%; excluding the one-off and its tax, 17.9%, reflecting PRC preferential high/new-technology rates. Q1 included a first-time Pillar Two top-up of RMB 15.6M. Since the ex-one-off rate already exceeds the 15% floor, incremental structural tax risk looks contained, though the appearance of a top-up confirms the regime now bites.

Financial health scores 8.0. The prior half-point reduction from 8.5 reflected capital-efficiency concerns around cash accumulation; the return framework and its execution partially answer that, but a growing minority-equity portfolio funded by shareholder cash keeps it from going higher.

## Growth Analysis

The trajectory is the core issue. RMB revenue growth: ~32% → ~24% → ~13% → +7.6%, with Q2 guided to +13.2-15.1%. Even the guided reacceleration is roughly half the 2023 rate.

**Growth is volume, not price.** Q1 enterprise revenue +8.2% against TTM paying accounts +10.9% implies revenue per paying account flat to down roughly 2.4%. The comparison mixes a quarterly flow against a TTM stock and the numerator is depressed by holiday timing — using the Q2 guidance midpoint of +14.2% against ~11% account growth flips implied per-account revenue to roughly +3%. Either way, there is no evidence of pricing power in the disclosed data. Management's introduction of a performance-based (outcome-priced) monetization model reads as recognition that ARPU needs a new lever, and it is unquantified in disclosures.

**MAU growth of +5.7% is the metric we watch most closely for competitive erosion.** Single-digit user growth is consistent with either market maturity or share pressure from adjacent platforms with larger traffic pools; no data distinguishes the two.

**Estimate revisions do not corroborate acceleration.** Current-quarter consensus RMB 2.12 is unchanged over 30 days and −0.7% over 90; next quarter −1.4% over 90 days; current year +1.7%; next year −1.8%. This is flat-to-slightly-negative drift, not the >30% revision velocity that would signal an inflection. The earnings record is a reasonable 3 beats, 1 miss over four quarters, with surprises of +5.1%, −4.5%, +1.3% and +10.3% — modest magnitudes that argue for taking guidance close to face value rather than assuming systematic conservatism.

The bull path is straightforward and does not require heroics: continued penetration of the fragmented SME and blue-collar segments at ~11% account growth, an ARPU inflection from outcome-based pricing, and 39%+ adjusted operating margins on near-zero incremental capex. The bear path is that the SME penetration runway is closer to exhaustion than 7.1 million accounts against China's employer base suggests, and that mix shift toward ever-smaller employers dilutes realized price faster than volume adds. Growth prospects: 6.2.

## Valuation Assessment

The supplied peer table is not usable for relative valuation: Zillow at 134.6x trailing on trough earnings, Baidu at a negative multiple, and Nebius at 76.8x as an AI-infrastructure name share no economic basis with a cash-rich, 39%-margin Chinese recruitment marketplace. The peer median P/E of 134.6x should be disregarded. We also flag a data conflict in the supplied profile: trailing and forward P/E are both shown as 15.47x, which reconciles to roughly $13.05 on trailing EPS of $0.84 — a stale price stamp. Our arithmetic uses $16.09.

Working from the balance sheet:

| Metric | Calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Market capitalization | 447.1M ADS × $16.09 | $7,194M |
| Net liquid assets | RMB 19,826.5M ÷ 6.898 + LT deposits | $2,882M (40.1% of cap) |
| Enterprise value | $7,194M − $2,882M | $4,312M |
| EV ex-LT equity investments | less $463M | $3,849M |
| EV per ADS | $4,312M ÷ 447.1M | $9.64 |

Consensus FY2026 adjusted EPS of RMB 8.73 per ADS converts to US$1.266 at 6.898, putting the headline forward multiple at **12.7x**; FY2027 at RMB 9.80 (US$1.421) is **11.3x**. Removing the recurring non-operating interest income (approximately RMB 668M annualized, US$97M, or $0.178 per ADS after 18% tax) leaves operating-only adjusted EPS of roughly $1.088. Against EV per ADS of $9.64, the operating business trades at **8.9x forward operating earnings**. On an EV/adjusted operating income basis, FY2026 adjusted EBIT of roughly $520M (Q1 actual $118.1M, Q2 implied ~$136M at the guided revenue midpoint and a 39% margin) gives **8.3x**, or 7.4x excluding the equity portfolio.

For a business with 85% gross margins, no debt, negative working capital, effectively no capex and ~11-13% revenue growth, that is cheap in absolute terms. The honest caveat is that a substantial part of the discount is structural rather than a mispricing: China-ADR regulatory tail risk, dual-class founder control with no meaningful ADS-holder influence, single-country and single-product revenue, and unverified VIE/PCAOB status. Cheap multiples on China internet names have persisted for years without closing.

What differentiates this from a static value trap is an identifiable, dated capital-return catalyst. On March 18, 2026 the company committed to allocating no less than 50% of the prior fiscal year's adjusted net income to dividends and repurchases in each of the next three years, and extended a $400M authorization to August 28, 2027. Execution has run ahead of that minimum: more than 28 million ordinary shares for approximately RMB 1.4bn by May 20, and more than RMB 2.06bn (~$299M, or 4.2% of current market capitalization) by July 1. Implied average repurchase price of roughly RMB 50 per ordinary share (~US$14.5 per ADS) means buybacks were executed at approximately 8x EV/adjusted EBIT — accretive by any reasonable measure, and running well above SBC issuance, which shows in the share count reconciliation (927.7M basic weighted ordinary shares in Q1 versus roughly 894-899M implied today). The dividend adds a ~1.0% yield.

One caution: RMB 2.06bn is about 75% of a $400M authorization within 3.5 months of that authorization being set, which suggests the $400M is cumulative from an earlier program inception and/or spans both the Nasdaq ADS program and a separate HKEX general mandate. Do not model $400M of fresh dry powder.

Analyst positioning is constructive and stable — 5 strong buy, 16 buy, 1 hold — with a mean target of $20.86 (+29.7%), median $21.08, low $15.74 and high $26.25. At 12.7x forward these targets are plausible floors rather than ceilings in the sense that they are not extrapolating a premium multiple. Our own base case sits below the mean.

Scenario arithmetic from $16.09, valuing the operating business on ex-cash operating earnings and adding back net liquid assets of approximately $6.45 per ADS (reduced modestly in scenarios where buybacks continue):

- **Bear $12.75 (−20.8%)**: Q2 misses guidance, confirming Q1 as demand weakness; growth settles at 5-7%; ~6x on $1.05 operating EPS plus $6.30 cash. Approaches the $12.57 52-week low.
- **Base $18.50 (+15.0%)**: Q2 lands in the guided range, FY2026 revenue growth ~12-13%, adjusted operating margin holds near 39%; ~11x on $1.09 operating EPS plus $6.45 cash.
- **Bull $23.00 (+42.9%)**: Q2 at or above the high end, performance-based pricing lifts revenue per account, sustained buyback at 4%+ of cap annually; ~15x on $1.15 operating EPS plus $6.30 cash.

Probability-weighted at 50/25/25 (base/bull/bear): **$18.19, +13.0%**. Valuation: 6.4.

## Competitive Landscape

Direct third-party market-share data could not be sourced, so the competitive read is inferred from Kanzhun's own metric and margin trajectory.

The strongest available evidence is that competitive intensity is currently benign: a company in a price war does not expand adjusted operating margin 343bp while cutting sales-employee costs and holding 85% gross margin. Against legacy Chinese boards (51job, Zhaopin, Liepin), Kanzhun's chat-based, self-serve model appears structurally advantaged in the high-volume SME and blue-collar segment, where 7.1 million paying accounts at ~RMB 1,190 each describes a fundamentally different business from résumé-database subscriptions sold to large employers.

Two risks sit outside that comparison. The first is platform adjacency — Chinese short-video and super-app platforms have the traffic to build recruitment verticals, and +5.7% MAU growth is the leading indicator that would deteriorate first. The second is AI, which is genuinely two-sided. The bear hypotheses: LLM-based sourcing lets employers find and contact candidates without a marketplace; AI-generated applications flood the platform and degrade the matching signal; AI compresses entry-level white-collar hiring in China, shrinking the monetizable pool. The bull hypotheses: matching is a liquidity-and-behavioral-data problem that a model alone does not solve, and better matching raises conversion, which is precisely what outcome-based pricing monetizes. These are competing hypotheses; neither is established by the disclosed data, and no AI-attributable revenue has been disclosed. Competitive position: 6.2 — stable on margin evidence, with the user-growth deceleration as the open caution.

## Risk Assessment

**Growth deceleration** is the dominant fundamental risk, and it is partly self-reinforcing: at flat revenue per account, the terminal growth assumption depends entirely on how much SME penetration runway remains.

**Non-operating earnings dependence.** Roughly 17% of adjusted pre-tax income is interest and investment income, exposed to falling deposit rates. The RMB 3,195.5M long-term equity portfolio adds mark-to-market volatility unrelated to recruitment, and the investee behind the RMB 614.1M January-IPO gain is unidentified.

**China-ADR structural risk, unverified.** The FY2025 20-F risk-factor section (Item 3.D) could not be read — EDGAR returned HTTP 403 — so VIE enforceability, PCAOB inspection and HFCAA status, and CAC/PIPL compliance are flagged rather than confirmed for the current period. Kanzhun was subject to a CAC cybersecurity review beginning July 2021 that suspended new user registrations; current status is unconfirmed. The Hong Kong dual-primary listing is a meaningful mitigant to delisting risk.

**Newly evidenced tax and regulatory items:** a first-time Pillar Two top-up of RMB 15.6M, and the PRC VAT law effective January 1, 2026 that reduced deferred revenue by RMB 45.6M. Both are small; both demonstrate direct pass-through of Chinese and global tax reform.

**Governance.** Dual-class structure with founder Jonathan Zhao holding 123.3M Class B shares; the Class A/Class B vote ratio is unverified, so we do not assert a voting-power figure, but ADS holders should assume founder control of shareholder votes. On June 12, 2026 Zhao converted 3,183,680 Class B into Class A, the class underlying the ADS. No corresponding sale appears in the Form 4 record. Conversions occur for estate planning, collateral or index-eligibility reasons as often as for monetization; we draw no inference about intent, but flag it as monitorable. Whether the company currently has a CFO — as distinct from the Deputy CFO who delivered Q1 commentary — is unverified.

**Macro concentration.** No customer concentration, but the entire revenue line is a derivative of Chinese SME and blue-collar hiring intent, with no geographic or product diversification.

Risk factors: 5.4. The regulatory and disclosure gaps are material, and a binary print sits 2-3 weeks out.

### Options Market Signal

The chain is thin — 21 contracts across four expirations — so read it with low confidence. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 72.1% against 36.4% one-year realized (a 35.7-point spread, 75th percentile within the chain) with term structure in backwardation (72.1% at 13 days versus 54.7% at six months) prices an event: the ±14.9% implied move on the nearest expiry, and 19.7% for August 21, brackets the late-August Q2 print. Put/call skew of 18.7% is characterized as elevated put demand, which sits awkwardly against a put/call open-interest ratio of 0.10 and zero recorded put volume — open interest is overwhelmingly call-tilted. We do not resolve that contradiction and do not read directional information from the skew value. The usable takeaway: near-term optionality is expensive relative to realized volatility, which argues against paying up for exposure immediately before the print and in favor of staging entry. Short interest of 5.0% of float with 5.4 days to cover is uncrowded — no squeeze dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($23.00, +42.9%).** Q2 prints at or above RMB 2.42bn, confirming Q1's 7.6% as Chinese New Year timing and re-establishing a 13-15% growth run rate. Performance-based pricing begins lifting revenue per paying account, adding a second growth lever to the ~11% account expansion. Adjusted operating margin holds near or above 39% with capex near zero, so incremental revenue converts to cash at very high rates. The ≥50% payout framework continues to be executed above its minimum, retiring 4%+ of shares annually at single-digit multiples of operating earnings. The market re-rates the operating business from ~9x toward ~15x forward operating earnings, still a discount to global marketplace comparables.

**Bear case ($12.75, −20.8%).** Q2 misses, revealing Q1 weakness as demand rather than calendar. MAU growth of +5.7% proves to be share loss to adjacent platforms rather than market maturity. Flat revenue per account persists as mix shifts to ever-smaller employers, and outcome-based pricing fails to lift realized price. Falling deposit rates compress the ~17% of adjusted pre-tax income that comes from interest income. **Base case ($18.50, +15.0%).** Q2 lands within guidance, FY2026 RMB revenue grows 12-13%, adjusted operating margin holds around 39% with SBC deflation exhausting but cash-cost efficiency continuing, and the buyback retires 3-4% of shares. Eleven times forward operating earnings plus $6.45 of net liquid assets per ADS.

## Conclusion

At $16.09 Kanzhun trades at 12.7x FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS, or approximately 8.9x forward operating earnings once the 40% of market capitalization held in cash, deposits and short-term investments is removed. The business generates 85% gross margins, 39% adjusted operating margins, requires almost no capital expenditure after an 86% capex reduction in FY2025, is funded by customer prepayments, carries no debt, and is now returning at least half of prior-year adjusted net income to shareholders — RMB 2.06bn repurchased in the first half of 2026 alone, at prices near US$14.5 per ADS, equal to 4.2% of current market capitalization.

The reason it is cheap is also real. RMB revenue growth has halved twice in three years, revenue per paying account is flat, 17% of adjusted pre-tax income is non-operating and rate-sensitive, and the China-ADR regulatory overlay could not be verified from primary filings this cycle. The Q2 print in late August determines whether Q1's 7.6% was Chinese New Year timing — which the MAU arithmetic and the RMB 324.6M sequential deferred-revenue build support — or the start of a lower growth plateau. With near-term implied volatility at twice realized and a ±15% implied move priced, the sensible action is to own a partial position and complete it either after the print or on weakness toward the level at which the company itself has been buying.

Score 6.5, ACCUMULATE. This is a modest downgrade in action from the prior BUY, driven by entry timing and an imminent binary rather than by fundamental deterioration; the confirmed capital-return execution is the one dimension that has genuinely improved since the last review.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the mean price target was overstated (correct mean $20.86 is +29.7% from $16.09, not affecting direction; the "+29.7%" figure in the report was already correct but described as if from a higher base in prose); the FY2026 forward P/E of 12.7x rests on consensus adjusted EPS of RMB 8.73 per ADS, but Phase 1 EPS estimates ($8.73 current-year, $9.80 next-year) are quoted by the vendor in USD, not RMB — if these are USD figures the forward multiple would be ~1.8x, which is implausible and indicates the currency basis of the consensus EPS is unverified and the entire forward-multiple/operating-earnings valuation chain (12.7x, 8.9x, 8.3x) may be materially misstated.
