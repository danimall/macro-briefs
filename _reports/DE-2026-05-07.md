## Executive Summary

Deere & Company is 25% below its FY2023 revenue peak and management has explicitly called FY2026 the cycle bottom. The stock at $580 trades at 33x trailing and forward earnings — a premium typically reserved for growth companies, applied here to a deep-cyclical at trough earnings. The bull case requires believing that (1) the cycle has indeed bottomed, (2) earnings recover to $23+ in FY2027 (consensus), and (3) $1.2B in tariff costs don't permanently impair margins. The bear case rests on the current multiple being too rich for an industrial company facing a potential extended downturn if commodity prices remain depressed and retaliatory tariffs constrain U.S. agricultural exports.

The stock has appreciated 24% over the past year largely on the back of a strong Q1 FY2026 earnings beat (Feb 19) that triggered a $100+ move in a single session. Since that peak near $674, the stock has retraced 14% and now consolidates around $580. Near-term estimates are declining modestly (-0.6% over 30 days) while longer-term estimates rise (+7.4% over 90 days for FY2026, +4.2% for FY2027). The risk/reward is balanced rather than compelling in either direction.

## Company Overview

Deere operates four segments: Production & Precision Agriculture (PPA, 45% of equipment sales), Small Agriculture & Turf (SAT, 26%), Construction & Forestry (CF, 29%), and Financial Services. The company's "Smart Industrial Operating Model" targets transformation from hardware manufacturer to technology-enabled solutions provider through autonomous equipment, precision agriculture, and SaaS subscriptions. With ~73,100 employees, ~2,050 dealer locations in North America, and an integrated captive finance arm carrying ~$80B in receivables, Deere is the dominant franchise in global agricultural equipment.

Revenue has declined 25% from peak, with EPS falling 47%. The FY2026 guidance of $4.5-5.0B net income reflects continued weakness in PPA (down 5-10%) offset by SAT and CF recovery (both +15%).

**Q1 FY2026 Results:** Revenue of $9.6B (+13% YoY on easy comps), EPS of $2.42 (beat $2.11 estimate by 14.7%). Construction & Forestry surged 34%, signaling that segment is already recovering. However, net income declined 25% YoY due to lower price realization and elevated tariff costs.

**Cash Flow and Capital Allocation:** FY2025 free cash flow was $6.1B (13.4% margin) — exceptional for a company in downcycle. The aggressive buyback program retired 11% of shares over three years (304.5M to 270.9M), amplifying future EPS recovery by reducing the denominator. FY2026 operating cash flow guidance is $4.5-5.5B.

**Balance Sheet:** The consolidated D/E of 3.08x reflects the Financial Services arm's receivables-funded structure, similar to Caterpillar Financial. The equipment operations alone are far less leveraged. Deere maintains an "A" credit rating and $8.3B in cash. ROE of 19.3% in a downcycle year demonstrates structural capital efficiency.

**Financial Services Credit Quality:** Allowance for credit losses increased in FY2025 on "elevated delinquencies" in agriculture customer accounts. With farm incomes under pressure from lower commodity prices, this bears monitoring as a potential drag if the downturn extends.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-Term (FY2026):** Estimates are modestly declining on a 30-day basis (-0.6% for FY2026 EPS) but have risen +7.4% over 90 days, reflecting the strong Q1 beat. The current quarter estimate of $5.74 declined 1.5% over 30 days — not alarming but indicating consensus is not accelerating higher.

**Medium-Term (FY2027-28):** The consensus FY2027 EPS of $23.02 represents 29% growth over FY2026. This assumes large ag inventory replenishment (currently at 17-year lows), continued CF infrastructure-driven strength, and some tariff moderation.

**Structural Growth Drivers:**
- Autonomous equipment and precision agriculture adoption (slow but real)
- CF benefiting from U.S. infrastructure spending
- SaaS/subscription revenue buildout (See & Spray, Operations Center)
- Lifecycle Solutions (aftermarket/parts) margins — though under regulatory pressure

**Structural Headwinds:**
- $1.2B tariff costs in FY2026 ($600M increase over FY2025)
- Used equipment overhang suppressing new orders
- Technology adoption "slower than expected" per 10-K
- Retaliatory tariffs constraining U.S. agricultural export volumes

The 90-day estimate revision of +7.4% for the current year and +4.2% for next year is positive but falls short of the +30% BUY signal threshold. The revision trajectory is moderately supportive but not decisive.

## Competitive Landscape

Deere's competitive advantages remain intact:
- **Largest dealer network** in North America (~2,050 locations) creates switching costs and service revenue lock-in
- **Integrated Financial Services** enables customer financing and dealer inventory management — CNH and AGCO lack comparable scale
- **Technology leadership** in precision ag (See & Spray, autonomous tillage, Operations Center ecosystem)
- **Brand premium** supporting higher ASPs than competitors across product lines
- **R&D scale** at $2.3B/year enables autonomous/AI investment that smaller competitors cannot match

Competitive position is stable-to-improving in precision agriculture, but faces pressure from:
- Chinese construction equipment manufacturers (SANY, XCMG) gaining global share
- Right-to-repair rulings opening aftermarket to competition
- AGCO and CNH investing aggressively in precision technology
- Non-traditional tech entrants in autonomous farming

The CF backlog grew from $2.2B to $3.8B, providing multi-quarter visibility on construction recovery. Large ag replacement demand, when it materializes, will benefit Deere disproportionately given its dominant North American position.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Tariff Exposure — $1.2B (HIGH):** The single largest quantifiable headwind. Represents ~2.6% of revenue directly to the bottom line. Retaliatory tariffs on U.S. agricultural exports compound the demand impact. Some potential relief from Supreme Court IEEPA ruling, but timing uncertain.

**2. Extended Downcycle (MODERATE-HIGH):** U.S./Canada large ag expected down 15-20% in FY2026. If commodity prices remain depressed and interest rates stay elevated, the "cycle bottom" call could prove premature. Each additional year of downturn compresses the recovery timeline and dilutes investor patience.

**3. FTC Repair Lawsuit (MODERATE):** The $99M class-action settlement was manageable, but the active FTC/five-state AG case targets the structural aftermarket monopoly. A forced opening of diagnostic systems could impair Lifecycle Solutions margins over time — a pillar of the Leap Ambitions strategy.

**4. Financial Services Credit Deterioration (MODERATE):** Elevated delinquencies in FY2025 with management expecting "certain conditions to persist." In an environment with elevated inflation and farm incomes declining, farmer defaults could accelerate.

**5. Technology Adoption Lag (LOW-MODERATE):** SaaS/precision adoption "slower than expected" creates execution risk on $2.3B annual R&D spend. The payoff period may be longer than management or investors anticipate.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($660-$700, +14-20%):**
- FY2026 is indeed the cycle bottom; large ag inventory at 17-year lows triggers powerful restocking in FY2027
- FY2027 EPS achieves or exceeds $23 consensus; stock re-rates to 28-30x on improving earnings trajectory
- CF infrastructure tailwind persists through IIJA spending
- Tariff moderation (Supreme Court ruling or negotiated reduction)
- Autonomous technology adoption accelerates, supporting premium pricing
- Buyback program continues reducing share count by 3-4% annually

**Bear Case ($460-$500, -14-21%):**
- Agricultural downcycle extends into FY2027 on sustained low commodity prices
- Tariff costs persist at $1.2B+; retaliatory tariffs compress export demand further
- Used equipment overhang takes 2+ years to clear
- Financial Services credit losses accelerate
- Stock de-rates to 20-22x on persistent trough earnings → $360-$420
- Farm income pressure extends under sustained high rates and weak commodity prices

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (from HOLD to BUY):**
1. Stock pulls back to $510-$530 range (22-24x FY2027E EPS) — providing adequate margin of safety for the recovery thesis
2. FY2026 Q2 earnings (expected May 2026) confirm accelerating order rates and dealer restocking, validating the "cycle bottom" call with data rather than assertion
3. Tariff relief announcement (Supreme Court ruling or negotiated reduction) removing the largest quantifiable headwind

**Downgrade Triggers (from HOLD to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 guidance reduced below $4.0B net income, indicating the downturn is deepening
2. Financial Services non-performing loans exceed 3% of portfolio (from current elevated but managed levels)
3. FY2027 consensus EPS declines below $20 (currently $23), indicating the recovery is being pushed out further

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 FY2026, likely mid-May 2026) is the primary reassessment point. The construction backlog trajectory, large ag order rates, and tariff cost development will provide clearer visibility on whether FY2026 is genuinely the trough.

## Conclusion

Deere is a world-class industrial franchise at trough earnings with a credible recovery thesis. The problem is price. At 33x current earnings, the market is already discounting meaningful recovery, leaving limited margin of safety if the timeline extends or tariffs persist. The construction segment is inflecting positively, the buyback program is steadily reducing shares, and 17-year low large ag inventory creates powerful latent demand. But $1.2B in tariffs, slow technology adoption, and negative near-term estimate momentum (-0.6% over 30 days) prevent a BUY at current levels.

The appropriate entry point is $510-$530, where you'd be paying 22-23x FY2027 consensus for a dominant industrial compounder with cyclical recovery potential. At $580, you're paying 25x for that same recovery with 14% less upside and identical downside risk. Hold for the recovery thesis; buy on pullback.
