## Executive Summary

Thermo Fisher's Q2 FY2026 result is the first quarter in three years that looks like a normalized end market rather than a recovery narrative. Revenue of $11,994M grew 10% reported and 5% organic — the strongest organic print in the available dataset — with the issuer's own decomposition (10% total, 5% acquisitions, 1% currency, 5% organic). Management's language shifted materially between the FY2025 10-K, which disclosed declining academic and government demand and declining China revenue, and the Q2 10-Q, which states all major end markets and all major geographies grew including China. Adjusted EPS of $6.03 beat the non-GAAP consensus of $5.71 by 5.6%, the fourth consecutive beat, and the annual profit forecast was raised. The stock gained 9.7% intraday on the release and has risen 7.7% since the prior review.

That is the case for reversing a declining score trajectory, and I am reversing part of it — overall moves from 5.5 to 5.83. I am not upgrading to BUY, for three reasons the print did not address. First, the price absorbed the news: at $573.365 the stock trades 22.8x FY2026 consensus EPS of $25.12 and 20.8x FY2027 consensus of $27.51, a PEG near 2.4 on ~9.5% expected earnings growth, roughly 9.4% below the $632.5 analyst median. Second, the margin improvement is narrower than the headline suggests: Analytical Instruments margin recovered 420bp year over year to 23.0%, but that is still 320bp below the FY2024 level of 26.2%, and ex-AI segment expansion in the quarter was 20bp (LSS), 70bp (SD) and 20bp (LPBS). Consolidated H1-26 adjusted operating margin of 22.3% remains below the FY2025 full-year 22.7%. Third, the balance sheet changed character in H1: cash and short-term investments fell from $10.1B to $4.06B, debt rose from $39.4B to $42.5B, and $4.0B of buybacks plus $8.87B of acquisitions were funded against $2.50B of first-half free cash flow.

Management quality, the dimension that has fallen furthest in this coverage history from 8.0 to 4.5, recovers only marginally to 4.8. CEO reported compensation rose 2.6x to $79.92M in 2025, a year in which the company's own proxy shows five-year TSR of 126.05 against a peer index of 156.63, and adjusted EPS of $22.87 below the 2021 figure of $25.13. Insider activity across March–July 2026 was sales only, all under pre-arranged Rule 10b5-1 plans, with no open-market purchases. The $3.0B of buybacks executed early in Q1-26 at an implied ~$612–621 per share sits above today's price; the $1.0B executed in Q2 at $482.66 sits well below it.

## Company Overview

Thermo Fisher operates four segments serving pharmaceutical and biotech customers, hospitals and clinical labs, academic and government research, and industrial/applied markets. Headcount was approximately 125,000 at year-end 2025, including roughly 14,000 sales personnel.

| Segment (Q2-26) | Revenue | % of total | Q2-26 margin | Q2-25 margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Laboratory Products & Biopharma Services | $6,693M | 55.8% | 14.0% | 13.8% |
| Life Sciences Solutions | $2,815M | 23.5% | 37.0% | 36.8% |
| Analytical Instruments | $1,847M | 15.4% | 23.0% | 18.8% |
| Specialty Diagnostics | $1,205M | 10.0% | 27.7% | 27.0% |
| Eliminations | ($565M) | — | — | — |

The mix matters more than the segment labels. Q2 revenue by type was services $5,171M, consumables $5,073M, instruments $1,750M. Instruments are roughly 15% of revenue; services are now the largest category. LPBS, at 56% of revenue and a 14.0% margin, houses research-and-safety distribution ($2,036M in Q2), pharma services/CDMO ($1,893M), and clinical research/CRO ($2,396M). The high-margin reagents core sits in LSS at 37.0%.

Two structural facts define the model. R&D was $1,397M in FY2025, 3.1% of revenue — below instrument-pure peers — while amortization of acquisition-related intangibles was $1,730M, larger than the entire R&D line. Growth is substantially purchased and then amortized. The 10-K is candid about the moat: no single patent group whose loss would significantly affect operations, no material single supplier, and "aggressive and able competition in virtually all of the markets we serve." Item 1A also discloses that the company competes directly with some of its own large customers and product suppliers, an inherent constraint on pushing private-label product through the Fisher Scientific channel.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue: a completed round trip, now inflecting.** FY2022 $44,915M → FY2023 $42,857M → FY2024 $42,879M → FY2025 $44,556M. FY2025 revenue remains roughly 0.8% below FY2022. FY2025 growth of 4% decomposed to +2% organic, +1% acquisitions, +1% currency. H1-26 revenue of $22,999M grew 8% reported and 3% organic, with Q2 at +10%/+5%. Geographic detail in Q2: North America $6,149M (+7.5%), Europe $3,311M (+17.0%), Asia-Pacific $2,104M (+9.6%). The European figure is not decomposable from disclosure and, against 1% company-wide currency translation, is likely dominated by Clario's European operations rather than underlying European demand.

**Margins.** FY2025 GAAP operating margin was 17.4% on $7,746M of operating income; adjusted operating margin 22.7%. Q2-26 adjusted operating margin was 22.8%, up 90bp, of which the Analytical Instruments recovery is the dominant driver. FY2025 AI margin had fallen 320bp to 23.0% on tariffs, currency and mix — roughly a $240M swing in segment income on $7,554M of segment revenue. The Q2 recovery is genuine mitigation, but it is a recovery off a depressed base rather than new operating leverage, and AI has not regained the FY2024 level.

Restructuring is recurring rather than episodic: roughly $0.3B in each of FY2024 and FY2025, $147M in H1-26, with a further ~$220M expected primarily in 2026, concentrated in LPBS and including a $60M long-lived asset impairment. Restructuring add-backs contributed $0.40 to H1-26 adjusted EPS. A charge taken every year is a cost of doing business.

**The GAAP-to-adjusted bridge.** FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS $17.74 → adjusted $22.87. Of the $5.13 bridge, $4.58 (89%) is amortization of acquisition-related intangibles. In H1-26 the amortization add-back was $2.46. Clario adds $3,796M of identified intangibles (customer relationships $2,510M/20yr, product technology $844M/7yr, backlog $423M/6yr, trade names $19M), roughly $324M per year of incremental amortization on the disclosed lives — all excluded from adjusted EPS. This is standard sector convention and improves cross-peer comparability, but the cash purchase price was permanent, and an investor paying 20.8x FY2027 adjusted earnings is paying a multiple on a number that systematically excludes the cost of the growth being acquired.

Two further items assist adjusted EPS without being operating improvement. The FY2025 adjusted tax rate of 10.4% against a 21% U.S. statutory rate contributes roughly $2.71 of the $22.87, about 12%; that rate is now guided higher (GAAP 9–11% for 2026 versus 7–9% previously; adjusted ~11.5%), so the tailwind is fading. Diluted shares fell from 394M in FY2022 to 371M in Q2-26, −5.8% cumulative. Q2-26 adjusted EPS growth of 13% therefore overstates underlying progress of +5% organic revenue and +90bp of largely AI-driven margin.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow fell 9.8% to $7,818M while GAAP net income rose, on receivables (−$0.43B), contract assets/liabilities (−$0.38B) and tax-timing-driven other items (−$1.31B). FY2025 FCF of $6,337M converts to 95% of GAAP net income but only 73% of adjusted net income of roughly $8,645M — the gap is the amortization add-back, which is the correct reading of what adjusted EPS overstates in cash terms. H1-26 FCF of $2,503M is up 69% year over year, a real improvement, but H1-25 FCF represented only 23% of that year's total, so H1 is not a base for annualization. Capex is stepping up: $1,525M in FY2025 (3.4% of revenue), guided to $1.9–2.1B for FY2026, raised from $1.8–2.0B.

**Balance sheet.** At 6/27/2026: total assets $113,174M, goodwill $54,832M, acquisition intangibles $18,606M, equity $52,686M. Goodwill plus intangibles of $73,438M is 64.9% of assets and exceeds equity by roughly $20.8B; tangible book value is around negative $21B. Debt rose to $42,549M from $39,384M while cash fell to $4,064M from $9,852M and short-term investments to zero, putting net debt near $38,485M. Against an EBITDA proxy built from H1-26 annualized adjusted operating income plus depreciation ($11,542M), that is roughly 3.3x; on a Q2-annualized basis, 3.14x. Both are elevated versus TMO's historical range and are the mechanical consequence of funding $8.87B of acquisitions and $4.0B of buybacks against $2.50B of first-half FCF.

Covenant risk is not the issue. The revolver's only disclosed financial covenant is a 3.5:1.0 minimum consolidated net interest coverage ratio; against Q2 adjusted EBITDA proxy of $3,066M and net interest expense of $194M, coverage is roughly 16x, and 26x on FY2025 figures. A screening-tool Altman Z of 1.75 circulating on this name is a computational artifact: it substitutes book-equity/assets for both the retained-earnings and market-equity terms; recomputed with retained earnings of $59,156M and market equity against total liabilities of $56,936M, Z is approximately 3.8, in the safe zone.

The interest line is a genuine headwind. Net interest expense went from $107M in Q2-25 to $194M in Q2-26, and from $206M to $314M on a half-year basis, against $426M for FY2025. With a full second half of Clario-related debt, FY2026 net interest plausibly lands near $700–800M, an incremental $275–375M pre-tax, or roughly $0.65–0.75 per adjusted share. Combined with the cost of the guided adjusted tax-rate increase, that is roughly a point of adjusted EPS growth absorbed below the operating line.

The offsetting treasury story is real and quantified. Cross-currency swaps reduced net interest expense by $283M in FY2025 and $194M in H1-26. March 2026 CHF issuance priced at 1.76%–2.11% across 2036–2056 maturities alongside February USD notes at 4.215%–5.546%. The CHF net-investment-hedge notional was raised from $5,000M to $11,800M while yen fell to $1,100M and euro to zero, and $481M of net cash proceeds from swap terminations was recorded in H1-26 investing activities, a one-time item rather than a run rate. Senior notes carry at $42,284M against a $39,604M fair value, a $2.68B discount reflecting legacy low-coupon paper in a higher-rate world, which is economically favorable to the issuer rather than a credit signal.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth case rests on four things. Organic revenue reaccelerated to +5% in Q2 with academic/government and China both returning to growth after declining in FY2025. BioProduction grew to $941M from $745M (+26%), the strongest line in the portfolio, helped by the filtration and separation acquisition. Remaining performance obligations were $29.70B at 6/27/26 with approximately 52% expected within twelve months — roughly $15.4B, about 32% of a ~$48B FY2026 revenue run rate. And consensus is drifting up: current-year EPS $25.12 (+1.1% over 30 days, +1.0% over 90 days), next-year $27.51 (+0.9%/+0.6%).

Those revisions are positive but modest, nowhere near the magnitude that signals a market systematically underestimating an inflection. Next-quarter consensus is down 0.8% over 30 days and 1.1% over 90 days, which fits a quarter where Clario contributes and the microbiology divestiture (~$166M of Q2 revenue, ~$664M annualized) subtracts. Consensus implies FY2026 adjusted EPS growth of about 9.8% and FY2027 about 9.5%, against 5% organic revenue growth; the gap is buybacks, amortization exclusion and mix.

Three qualifications. Biosciences revenue declined to $1,060M from $1,075M (−1.4%), the reagents franchise most exposed to academic and early-stage biotech budgets, so "all end markets strengthening" is not uniform at the product-line level. R&D at 3.1% of revenue means organic product velocity is unlikely to be the growth engine; Q2 launches (Orbitrap Tribrid Apex, Orbitrap Excedion, Applied Biosystems PowerFlex) are credible but incremental. And the company has closed $12.8B of acquisitions in ten months — filtration and separation at $3,865M net (September 2025) and Clario at $9,098M (March 2026) — into the two most competitive parts of the portfolio, bioprocessing and clinical research. Growth durability here depends materially on integration execution by a new President/COO and a new CFO.

## Competitive Landscape

TMO competes on four largely separate fields, which is why single-competitor comparisons mislead. In bioprocessing and reagents (LSS, 37% margin) the competitors are Danaher's Cytiva/Pall, Sartorius, MilliporeSigma and Repligen; the filtration and separation purchase was a direct move to close a stack gap, and BioProduction's +26% suggests early traction. In analytical instruments the competitors are Agilent, Waters, Bruker, Shimadzu and JEOL/Hitachi; this is the most capital-cycle-sensitive and most tariff- and export-control-exposed segment, with electron microscopy carrying semiconductor end-market exposure. In pharma services the competitors are Lonza, Catalent, Samsung Biologics and WuXi Biologics. In clinical research, PPD plus Clario face IQVIA, ICON, Fortrea and Medpace.

The differentiator is scale and breadth: 14,000 sales representatives, the Fisher Scientific channel, and the ability to sell reagents, instruments, outsourced manufacturing and trial services into the same pharma customer. The trade-off is visible in the P&L: 3.1% R&D and a 22.8% adjusted operating margin held down by a 56%-of-revenue segment earning 14%. Management asserts continued share gain, and the Q2 organic figure is consistent with that in a recovering market, but the company's own proxy shows five-year TSR trailing its selected peer group in every year from 2022 through 2025 — cumulative 126.05 versus 156.63, 4.74%/yr versus 9.39%/yr. Trajectory is improving off a trough; the structural position is stable rather than widening.

## Risk Assessment

**Tariffs and China localization** are the proven near-term earnings variable, not a hypothetical: they were among the stated causes of a 320bp FY2025 AI margin decline, roughly $240M of segment income. Mitigation appears to be working in Q2, but residual tariff cost is unquantified in disclosure.

**Government funding.** The FY2025 10-K specifically flags the October 2025 U.S. appropriations lapse and the risk of delayed or reduced federal research spending. Academic and government demand declined in FY2025 before returning to growth in Q2-26; TMO does not size the exposure.

**Goodwill impairment.** $54,832M of goodwill and $18,606M of intangibles sit against $52,686M of equity. The 10-K discloses that the new filtration and separation reporting unit was tested qualitatively and its fair value "was not substantially in excess of its carrying value" — $2.10B of goodwill against a $4.01B carrying value — with relatively small adverse changes in cash flows, peer multiples or WACC sufficient to trigger a charge. In fairness, fair value equals purchase price by construction in year one, so this is expected disclosure rather than evidence of overpayment. Any charge would be non-cash and would not affect covenants or adjusted EPS.

**Integration and execution.** $12.8B of acquisitions in ten months, landing in the two most competitive portfolio areas, with the Clario purchase price allocation explicitly preliminary and 65.2% of the price sitting in non-tax-deductible goodwill. Executing that alongside the microbiology divestiture, under a new CFO and a new President/COO, is the largest controllable risk.

**Pharma services concentration.** Item 1A discloses that TMO is the sole manufacturer of a number of pharmaceuticals for many customers. A quality or regulatory failure at a Patheon site is not a diversifiable event. A shift in revenue mix toward services does not establish customer diversification; the filings disclose no customer-concentration data, and CDMO/CRO economics rest on a limited set of large sponsors.

**Leverage and interest.** Covered above: ~3.1–3.3x with 16x coverage and no near-term covenant risk, but a cash cushion down from $10.1B to $4.06B and interest expense on track to roughly double.

**Unresolved disclosure item.** The deferred tax valuation allowance rose from $1,040M at 12/31/2024 to $3,560M at 12/31/2025, a $2.52B increase not traced to an explanation in the filings reviewed. The benign reading is that domestication transactions generated large gross deferred tax assets not more-likely-than-not realizable, with limited net P&L effect — consistent with the modest net discrete benefits actually reported. An adverse reading of realizability deterioration in specific jurisdictions cannot be ruled out. This should not be treated as established either way; it is a verification gap worth resolving at the next 10-K.

**Governance.** CEO reported compensation of $79.92M in 2025 (comp actually paid $116.09M) versus $30.45M in 2024 and an NEO average of $7.59M, in a year of adjusted EPS below the 2021 level and five-year TSR 31 points behind the peer index. The most plausible explanation is a one-time retention award tied to the leadership transition; grant-timing or pension-value effects are alternatives. The award's structure and performance conditions are unverified. What is established is the coincidence of a very large pay figure with cumulative relative underperformance.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($700, +22%).** Organic growth holds at 5% or better and broadens beyond AI normalization: LPBS margin moves through 15% as restructuring savings land, AI recovers toward the 26.2% FY2024 level, and BioProduction sustains double-digit growth as the filtration assets integrate. Clario delivers the promised clinical-research share gain and the microbiology proceeds plus rising FCF pull net leverage back below 2.75x by mid-2027. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches or exceeds $27.51 and the multiple expands to 25–26x as the market re-rates a re-accelerating compounder. Sub-2% CHF funding and a 16x-covered balance sheet keep the M&A engine available at a cost of capital smaller peers cannot match.

**Bear case ($460, −19.8%).** The Q2 organic figure proves to be a comparison effect against a weak base rather than a new run rate. Federal research appropriations tighten again, China localization pressure resumes, and academic/government reverts to decline. Analytical Instruments margin stalls near 23% as tariff mitigation runs out of room. Interest expense doubling plus the guided tax-rate increase absorbs most of the operating improvement, FY2027 estimates flatten near $26, and a goodwill charge at filtration and separation forces a conversation about the $12.8B acquisition program. At 18x FY2026 consensus of $25.12 the stock is $452.

**Base case ($630, +9.9%).** Organic growth settles in the 4–5% range, adjusted EPS reaches roughly $27.50 in FY2027 as consensus expects, and the multiple holds near 23x, the same rating the market applies today, applied to next year's earnings. That is a mid-single-digit-plus-dividend total return over twelve months, matching the analyst median of $632.5 almost exactly. The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is that 9.9% of base-case upside against 19.8% of bear-case downside is not a favorable asymmetry for a name whose earnings quality requires three adjustments to reach the multiple being paid.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 FY2026 earnings, expected late October 2026, plus the Q3 close of the microbiology divestiture and the FY2026 10-K in February 2027 (which should resolve the valuation-allowance question and the FY2026 goodwill test).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Two consecutive quarters of organic growth at 5% or better with broad-based margin expansion — specifically LPBS segment margin above 15.0% and Analytical Instruments margin above 25.0% — demonstrating that the improvement is not AI base-effect normalization.
2. FY2026 free cash flow at or above $7.0B with net debt/EBITDA back below 2.75x by the Q4-26 report, confirming the H1 leverage step-up was transaction-specific.
3. Price toward $500–515 with FY2027 consensus intact at $27.00 or better — roughly 18.5x forward, which would restore the asymmetry regardless of the operating detail.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Organic growth reverting to 2% or below, or academic/government returning to year-over-year decline.
2. A goodwill impairment at filtration and separation, or a material adverse revision to the preliminary Clario purchase price allocation accompanied by clinical-research revenue below the ~$2.4B Q2 run rate.
3. Net debt/EBITDA above 3.5x, or FY2027 consensus revisions turning negative for two consecutive months.
4. Disclosure indicating the $2.52B valuation-allowance increase reflects realizability deterioration rather than offsetting gross deferred tax assets from the domestication transactions.

**Position guidance:** hold existing positions. New capital should wait for either the broad-based-margin confirmation in the October print or a price in the low $500s.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Overall score 5.83, up from 5.5, reversing part of a six-report decline. The Q2 FY2026 result is the strongest evidence in three years that Thermo Fisher's end markets have normalized: +5% organic, all geographies growing including China, academic and government back to growth, a fourth consecutive beat, guidance raised, and a 420bp Analytical Instruments margin recovery. Those facts justify raising growth prospects to 6.4 and financial health to 6.2 on the 69% H1 free-cash-flow improvement.

They do not justify a BUY at $573.365. The 9.7% single-day re-rating consumed most of the gap to a defensible $630 base case, leaving 9.9% of upside against roughly 20% of downside. The margin improvement is concentrated in one segment recovering off a weak base and still 320bp below its FY2024 level. Roughly a third of expected adjusted EPS growth comes from share count, amortization exclusion and a tax rate now guided higher, and net interest expense will roughly double this year. Management quality remains the binding constraint at 4.8: a 2.6x jump in CEO pay against five-year peer underperformance and adjusted EPS below the 2021 level, insider activity that is sales-only, $3.0B of buybacks executed above today's price, and $12.8B of acquisitions in ten months now being integrated by a new CFO and a new President/COO.

This is a high-quality distribution-and-services franchise with a genuine cost-of-capital advantage, priced for the recovery it has just delivered. Own it for the organic inflection; add on price, not on narrative.
