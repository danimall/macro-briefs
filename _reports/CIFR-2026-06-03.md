**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cipher Digital (formerly Cipher Mining, renamed February 20, 2026) is a vertically integrated power-and-data-center developer executing a pivot from pure-play Bitcoin mining toward HPC/AI data centers leased to hyperscalers under long-term contracts. The company controls 4.2 GW of capacity across ten sites, with 600 MW under active HPC development for signed tenants including Amazon Web Services (Black Pearl, ~300 MW, 15-year lease) and a Fluidstack lease at Barber Lake backstopped by Google. A third investment-grade tenant lease was signed in Q1 2026. Management frames 2026 as "the year of execution" and points to ~$11.4B of total contracted lease revenue and ~$787M of average annualized contracted NOI from October 2026 through September 2036.

The stock has risen roughly 700% over the trailing twelve months, from a $3.08 low to ~$26, and the equity is now priced as if the contracted pipeline will energize on schedule. In under twelve months Cipher levered from a near-debt-free balance sheet to ~$4.7B of total debt, including a $2.0B 6.125% Black Pearl secured note, a $1.7B Cipher Compute secured note, and a $1.3B zero-coupon 2031 convertible. Debt service depends on lease revenue that does not begin until the data centers are physically completed. The risk factors disclose a critical asymmetry: tenant guarantees (including the Google backstop) only become effective at rent commencement, so a construction delay can let a tenant terminate without triggering the guarantee.

The reported 2025 net loss of $822.2M overstates operating distress; roughly 85% of it is non-cash, dominated by a $450.4M embedded-derivative remeasurement on the 2031 converts that reverses once authorized shares were obtained, plus impairments tied to winding down mining. The company is burning cash today (-$695.9M FCF in 2025 and -$48.2M Adjusted EBITDA in Q1 2026), but the determinative question is whether ~$4.7B of debt-financed assets energize on time. At the current price there is no margin of safety for getting that wrong, and the contracted-vs-energized gap is where the next 12-18 months of risk lives. We rate CIFR a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Cipher originated from a 2021 SPAC merger (Good Works Acquisition Corp. + Cipher Mining Technologies). Its asset base comprises 4.2 GW of total capacity across ten sites, concentrated in West Texas (ERCOT) with one Ohio site (PJM). The legacy cash engine is ~207 MW of operating Bitcoin mining at the Odessa facility, which still generated about 74% of 2025 revenue. The strategic future is HPC/AI:

- **Black Pearl (Wink, TX, ~300 MW):** 15-year lease with Amazon Web Services, signed October 2025. Bitcoin mining there ceased February 2026 to convert to HPC; phased delivery begins Q4 2026.
- **Barber Lake (Colorado City, TX, 300 MW):** Long-term lease with Fluidstack USA II, with Google backstopping certain Fluidstack obligations via a lease recognition agreement. The tenant is Fluidstack (an AI cloud/GPU provider), not Google; Google is a capped, conditional credit backstop, not the lessee. Phase I targeted by September 30, 2026.
- **Third campus lease** with an unnamed "investment-grade hyperscale tenant," signed Q1 2026.
- **~3.4 GW development pipeline** across eight additional sites (Colchis 1 GW, McLennan/Mikeska/Milsing 500 MW each, Ulysses 200 MW Ohio/PJM, plus Stingray and Reveille).

The competitive pitch is a "power-first" model: securing land plus ERCOT/AEP interconnection ahead of demand in a power-constrained market. Quanta Services is the key EPC partner. The company sits squarely in the "Bitcoin-miner-to-AI-data-center" conversion cohort (IREN, TeraWulf, Hut 8, Core Scientific, Applied Digital, Riot) and also competes against established operators (Digital Realty, Equinix, Vantage, CoreWeave).

## Financial Analysis

The headline GAAP figures are alarming but distorted by non-cash items.

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue (all BTC mining) | $126.8M | $151.3M | $223.9M | $34.8M |
| Net loss | ($25.8M) | ($44.6M) | **($822.2M)** | ($114.3M) |
| Operating cash flow | ($94.2M) | ($87.5M) | ($207.9M) | n/a |
| Free cash flow | — | — | **($695.9M)** | n/a |
| Total assets | $566M | $855M | $4.29B | $6.39B |
| Total debt | ~$14M | ~$11M | **$3.21B** | **~$4.7B+** |

**Decomposing the 2025 loss.** The largest line is a $450.4M loss on the embedded derivative in the 2031 convertible notes, a temporary accounting artifact until the company obtained sufficient authorized shares, partially offset by a $45.1M capped-call gain. Add a $96.1M miners-held-for-sale write-down, $45.3M long-lived asset impairment, $29.4M miner disposal, and ~$199M of accelerated depreciation (useful life cut from five to three years in mid-2024). Management's "Adjusted Earnings" were +$22.2M for 2025 (down from +$106.7M in 2024). A scoring model keying off GAAP net loss or the 1.5 Altman Z-score in isolation would badly misread this company; the Z-score is largely an artifact of a development-stage balance sheet (huge assets, minimal current revenue, revenue/assets of only ~0.05).

**The debt story is the central fact.** In roughly ten months Cipher went from near debt-free to one of the most heavily levered names in the sector:

- May 2025: $172.5M 1.75% convertible notes due 2030
- Sept 2025: $1,300M 0.00% convertible notes due 2031 (zero-coupon; funded capped calls)
- Nov 2025: $1,733M senior secured notes due 2030 (Cipher Compute LLC)
- Feb/Mar 2026: $2,000M 6.125% senior secured notes due 2031 (Black Pearl Compute LLC, financing the AWS facility)
- Q1 2026: new $200M corporate revolving credit facility

Debt is partially structured as project-level/non-recourse through SPEs (Cipher Compute, Black Pearl Compute), the intended risk-isolation strategy. The interest burden is now material: Q1 2026 interest expense was $59.2M versus $0.8M a year earlier, which is why management switched its preferred profitability metric from Adjusted Earnings (positive) to Adjusted EBITDA (-$48.2M in Q1). Debt/equity sits at ~4.29x and rising. The current ratio of 3.79 is healthy only because ~$3.5B of cash and restricted cash from bond proceeds sits on the balance sheet earmarked for construction.

**Dilution is significant and ongoing.** Basic shares grew from 248M (2022) to ~405M (Q1 2026). The company ran a large ATM program (33.3M shares for ~$195.5M net at an average $5.88 in 2025) plus a $50M PIPE, with a further ~$600M ATM authorized. Convertible note conversions add further overhang. Convertible holders have repurchase rights (2030 notes in May 2028; 2031 notes in October 2029).

The financial-health verdict is mixed: liquidity is adequate today because the bond war chest is funded, but solvency over the next 18 months is contingent on completing construction and retaining capital-market access. Cash generation is negative and the leverage is among the highest in the sector.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on the contracted backlog of ~$11.4B in total lease revenue and ~$787M of average annualized NOI over October 2026–September 2036, supported by 907 MW of operating plus contracted capacity, with a further ~3.4 GW pipeline behind it. Against a current revenue base of ~$35M/quarter (almost entirely Bitcoin mining), that is a step-change if energization happens on schedule.

The sector tailwind is real and broad-based. The power-constrained market favors developers who hold interconnection positions, and Cipher's signed AWS lease and the Google-backstopped Fluidstack lease are concrete, contracted demand rather than speculative pipeline.

Two cautions temper the growth score. First, the entire NOI ramp is contractually contingent on completing data centers; backlog is not revenue until facilities energize. Black Pearl phased delivery begins Q4 2026, Barber Lake Phase I targets September 30, 2026, and management reports Barber Lake "topped out" in April 2026 and construction on schedule. Second, estimate revisions are deteriorating across the near term. Consensus current-year EPS has been cut to -$0.32 from +$0.23 ninety days ago (-236.6%), and current-quarter and next-quarter estimates are falling sharply. Next-year EPS is the one bright line, revised up to +$0.67 from +$0.32 ninety days ago (+112.7%), reflecting the energization ramp; analysts expect the cash drain to flip to operating income once the leases commence. The earnings record is four straight misses, including a -267% Q4 2025 miss, which argues for discounting management's timeline rather than taking it at face value.

## Valuation Assessment

CIFR offers no clean valuation anchor. Trailing and forward P/E are negative (EPS -$2.15), so the multiple is uninformative. With no dividend, the equity is a pure call on the contracted backlog converting to NOI.

A back-of-envelope frames the situation. At ~$26 and ~409M shares, the equity is ~$10.5B. Add ~$4.7B net of the construction war chest and enterprise value is materially higher. Against ~$787M of annualized contracted NOI, the equity is being asked to support a substantial multiple on cash flows that have not yet begun. If the contracted pipeline energizes on schedule and the ~3.4 GW pipeline converts, today's price is defensible to cheap. If construction slips and tenants can walk without triggering guarantees, the equity is the most subordinated claim on ~$4.7B of debt-financed, partly-completed assets.

Analyst targets bracket a wide range: low $23, median $28, mean $31.6, high $69, with Morgan Stanley at $40.50 (Overweight) citing favorable conversion economics and Jefferies initiating Buy. The fourteen-analyst tally is 5 Strong Buy / 10 Buy / 0 Hold, uniformly bullish, which is itself a crowding signal. The current price sits near the median target and toward the lower end of that band, above the $23 floor. Treating the analyst mean as a floor would be a mistake here: this is a pre-revenue developer where near-term estimates are falling and the bull targets and the price reflect the same execution optimism. The valuation is symmetric: the upside requires flawless 2026-2027 delivery, while the downside requires only one delay in a single-tenant facility. We score valuation at the midpoint.

## Competitive Landscape

Cipher's competitive position is improving on the strength of signed leases. The 15-year AWS lease and the Google-backstopped Fluidstack lease validate the power-first sourcing model and place Cipher ahead of much of the miner-to-AI conversion cohort in contracted footprint. The Quanta EPC relationship and the held interconnection positions in ERCOT and PJM are genuine assets in a power-constrained market.

The moat is narrower than the headline suggests. All sites are single-tenant, which concentrates risk and limits the operator's pricing leverage relative to multi-tenant colocation players. The differentiator is land and interconnection timing, not proprietary technology, and the entire miner-to-AI cohort is racing for the same power and the same hyperscaler demand. New Texas regulation — SB 6 (2025), imposing interconnection requirements, minimum transmission rates, and security-type payments on large loads ≥75 MW, plus ERCOT's December 2025 "batch" interconnection process — raises costs and potential delays for the pipeline. Trajectory is positive given signed contracts, but the durability of the edge depends on execution rather than a structural barrier competitors cannot replicate.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by company-specific, partly-binary exposures that are not fully priced.

1. **Construction/completion risk is existential.** Debt service on the $2.0B Black Pearl and $1.7B Cipher Compute notes depends on lease revenue that begins only at facility completion. Tenant guarantees — including the Google backstop — become effective only at rent commencement, so a construction delay can let a tenant terminate without triggering the guarantee. The debt is contracted against revenue contingent on on-time delivery in 2026-2027.

2. **Extreme tenant concentration.** Every site is single-tenant. The thesis rests on AWS, Fluidstack/Google, and one new investment-grade tenant. Loss or default of any one is material. Fluidstack is a less-established credit than Google; the deal's strength is the capped, conditional Google backstop.

3. **Texas/ERCOT geographic and regulatory concentration.** SB 6 and the ERCOT batch interconnection process are newly elevated, company-specific headwinds.

4. **Refinancing/liquidity risk.** Solvency depends on completing construction and continued capital-market access. Convertible repurchase rights begin May 2028 / October 2029.

5. **Bitcoin legacy exposure.** Odessa drives current revenue and is exposed to BTC volatility, the next ~April 2028 halving, and the Luminant power contract (2.8¢/kWh, expiring ≥July 2027).

6. **AI-demand/technology obsolescence.** Management explicitly flags that more efficient AI models could reduce demand for high-density capacity — a DeepSeek-style risk to the whole sector.

Near-term ATM IV is 122.6% versus 1-year historical vol of 106.2% (rich by 16.4 points), in the 82nd percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (122.6% near-term > 111.4% six-month), consistent with a market expecting a near-term catalyst. The 1-week implied move is ±7.2%, the 1-month ±18.5%. Near-term skew is call-heavy (-6.3%, P/C volume 0.30), and unusual activity shows aggressive call buying ($29 calls 6/5 at 3.2x OI, $28 calls 6/12 at 9.8x OI), but the 3-month skew flips put-heavy at -21.5%. The read is short-dated speculation layered on top of medium-dated hedging, consistent with a binary energization story where the upside is a momentum chase and the downside is a delay disclosure. Short interest is 16.4% of float (54.8M shares, 2.0 days to cover), indicating investors positioned against the contracted-revenue narrative even as the sell-side stays Buy-rated.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$40-69).** Construction proceeds on schedule: Barber Lake Phase I energizes by September 2026, Black Pearl begins phased AWS delivery Q4 2026, and the third hyperscale lease comes online. The $787M annualized contracted NOI begins flowing, next-year EPS turns positive (+$0.67 consensus, revised up 112.7% over 90 days), and the ~3.4 GW pipeline converts into additional leases. The non-recourse SPE structure isolates project risk, capital-market access continues, and Cipher re-rates as a contracted data-center operator rather than a cash-burning developer. Morgan Stanley's $40.50 and the $69 high target reflect this path.

**Bear Case (~$13-15, roughly -45% to -50%).** A construction delay at any single-tenant site allows a tenant to terminate before the guarantee commences, breaking the link between the $4.7B debt load and the contracted revenue meant to service it. Capital-market access tightens, the ~$600M authorized ATM dilutes existing holders further, Bitcoin weakness pressures the legacy 74%-of-revenue Odessa engine, and the equity — the most subordinated claim on partly-completed, heavily-levered assets — compresses toward the levels it traded at before the late-2025 lease announcements. Four consecutive earnings misses and a -236% current-year estimate cut support discounting the timeline.

The two scenarios are roughly symmetric in probability, and the path between them runs almost entirely through 2026 construction milestones the company does not fully control.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline.** The binary resolves over the next 6-18 months around concrete energization dates: Barber Lake Phase I (target Sept 30, 2026), Black Pearl AWS phased delivery (begins Q4 2026), and Q2/Q3 2026 earnings updates on construction progress. The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 print.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Confirmed on-time energization of Barber Lake Phase I or Black Pearl with rent commencement, converting backlog to recognized lease revenue and activating tenant guarantees.
- Next-year EPS estimates holding or rising further alongside evidence that Adjusted EBITDA is inflecting toward positive as leases commence.
- A pullback that creates a genuine margin of safety against the contracted NOI (toward the mid-teens) without any fundamental deterioration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Any disclosed construction delay at Black Pearl or Barber Lake that pushes rent commencement past contracted dates, particularly if framed in a way that exposes termination risk before guarantee effectiveness.
- A new large dilutive equity raise or evidence of strained capital-market access (e.g., having to draw heavily on the revolver or issue equity below market to fund construction).
- A tenant credit event or lease modification, or a Bitcoin drawdown that materially impairs the Odessa cash engine while construction is mid-stream.

Given negative cash flow, four straight earnings misses, falling near-term estimates, insider selling with zero open-market buying (including a 10% holder's >12M-share forward-sale contracts), and an options market pricing imminent stress, the prudent stance is to hold pending the first hard energization data point rather than to chase the momentum or to short into a 700% run with 16.4% short interest already in place.

## Conclusion

Cipher Digital is a credible, contracted entrant in the AI data-center build-out, and the $11.4B backlog with ~$787M of annualized contracted NOI is a real step-change if it energizes on schedule. But the equity at ~$26 already prices that success, the balance sheet carries ~$4.7B of debt against revenue that has not started, and the single most important risk — tenant guarantees that do not protect against construction-delay terminations — is unresolved and outside management's full control. The reported GAAP losses overstate distress, but the leverage and timing risk are genuine and concentrated in 2026-2027. With symmetric risk/reward, deteriorating near-term estimates, four straight misses, insider selling, and no margin of safety at current levels, we rate CIFR a **HOLD** and will revisit on the first confirmed energization milestone or a meaningful pullback.
