## Executive Summary

Occidental Petroleum has undergone a meaningful transformation in the past six months. The January 2026 completion of the $9.7 billion OxyChem sale to Berkshire Hathaway reduced principal debt from ~$25 billion to ~$15 billion, and the company is within ~$700 million of its $14.3 billion target, at which point capital allocation pivots to share repurchases. Simultaneously, the oil price surge has mechanically lifted near-term earnings power — consensus FY2026 EPS estimates have been revised up ~279% over 90 days to $4.37, and OXY has beaten estimates in all four of the most recent quarters.

The problem is price. OXY has rallied ~54% from its December 2025 lows (~$39) to $60.58, absorbing much of the improved fundamental picture. At ~14x the $4.37 FY2026 consensus, the stock trades at a discount to some peers but a premium to others (EOG at 15.3x, DVN at 12.2x, EQT at 11.2x) despite carrying higher leverage, no commodity hedges, and roughly $2.3 billion in contingent tax liabilities. The bull case requires sustained elevated oil prices and successful execution of the share repurchase pivot; the bear case is that oil normalizes, compressing EPS and the stock toward the low $40s. With the stock sitting near the middle of its 52-week range ($38.72 to $67.45), the risk/reward is balanced.

## Company Overview

Occidental Petroleum is a Houston-based E&P company with dominant Permian Basin positioning (~10% of basin oil production, 786 Mboe/d), along with operations in the Rockies, deepwater Gulf of America, Oman, Algeria, and the UAE. Following the OxyChem divestiture, the company operates three segments: Oil & Gas (~97% of revenue), Midstream & Marketing (including a 43.1% interest in Western Midstream Partners), and Low-Carbon Ventures (the STRATOS direct air capture facility). Total 2025 production was 1,434 Mboe/d, up 8% year-over-year following the CrownRock acquisition. Berkshire Hathaway holds approximately 27% of shares outstanding (~265 million shares) plus $10 billion in preferred stock paying 8% annually.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Earnings:** Revenue declined from $26.7 billion (2024) to $21.6 billion (2025), primarily reflecting the reclassification of OxyChem to discontinued operations. Continuing operations revenue was essentially flat ($22.0B to $21.6B). GAAP EPS was $1.61 in 2025, down from $2.44 in 2024, reflecting the 14% decline in average WTI ($75.72 to $64.81). The Q4 2024 quarter included a significant DASS environmental charge ($925M pre-tax) that depressed reported results.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow was $10.5 billion in 2025, with capex of $6.4 billion, generating approximately $4.1 billion in free cash flow (19% FCF margin). Capex guidance for 2026 is $5.5–5.9 billion, slightly below 2025 levels.

**Balance Sheet Transformation:** This is the most important financial story. Long-term debt peaked at ~$25 billion after the CrownRock acquisition and has been reduced to ~$15 billion through a combination of operating cash flow ($4B repaid in 2025) and OxyChem proceeds ($5.4B applied post-close). The maturity profile is clean: $24M due in 2026, $48M in 2027, $14M in 2028, with the bulk not maturing until 2030+. Total liquidity is $6.15 billion ($2.0B cash + $4.15B undrawn revolver). The Altman Z-Score of 1.22 is misleading for a capital-intensive E&P; the Piotroski signals (positive net income, positive ROA, OCF exceeding net income) confirm operational health.

**Operating Efficiency:** Lease operating costs declined from $10.48/Boe (2023) to $8.94/Boe (2025), reflecting Permian scale efficiencies. This cost trajectory positions OXY to maintain margins across a wider range of oil prices.

**Dilution:** Share count has grown ~12% over 18 months (884M to 986M) from warrant exercises and CrownRock stock consideration. This dilution partially offsets per-share benefits of deleveraging and is a legitimate concern for common shareholders, particularly given the $800M annual preferred dividend flowing to Berkshire ahead of the common.

## Growth Analysis

**Production Growth:** 2025 production of 1,434 Mboe/d represented 8% growth, driven by CrownRock integration. Organic growth from the combined asset base is likely in the low single digits, consistent with Permian-focused E&Ps prioritizing returns over volume. OXY's proved reserves of 4,603 MMboe provide ~8.8 years of reserve life.

**Earnings Leverage to Oil:** With zero commodity hedges in place, OXY's earnings are a direct function of oil prices. At current elevated prices, the company's earnings power is substantially above the $1.61 reported in 2025 at $64.81 WTI. Consensus FY2026 EPS of $4.37 reflects this. However, FY2027 consensus of $3.58 implies the market expects some oil price normalization. The 30-day revision trend shows continued upward pressure (+30.2% for FY2026, +23.1% for FY2027), driven by the sustained oil price environment.

**Carbon Management Optionality:** STRATOS, the world's first commercial-scale direct air capture facility, is expected to begin CO₂ sequestration in 2026. This provides long-term optionality if carbon removal credits gain market traction, but the technology is unproven at commercial scale and the credit market is nascent. The BlackRock joint venture provides external funding, limiting OXY's direct capital exposure. This should be valued as a call option, not a core earnings driver.

**Capital Allocation Pivot:** Once debt reaches the $14.3 billion target (~$700M remaining), management has stated it will redirect free cash flow toward share repurchases ($1.2B remaining on a $3.0B authorization). At current oil prices, FCF generation could be substantial, creating meaningful buyback capacity. This specific catalyst differentiates HOLD from AVOID on a 12-month basis.

| EOG | 15.3x | 3.0% | Low |
| DVN | 12.2x | 1.9% | Moderate |
| EQT | 11.2x | 1.1% | Moderate |

The peer median P/E is 15.3x. OXY's slight discount is warranted given its higher leverage, the $800M annual preferred dividend priority, and the ~$2.3 billion Tronox contingent liability. The dividend yield of 1.7% is below the peer median of 1.9%.

At elevated oil prices, OXY is not obviously cheap. The current price implies a reasonable but not conservative oil price assumption. If oil normalizes substantially, EPS would compress materially and the stock would trade at a much higher multiple — clearly expensive at that point. The valuation is therefore a bet on sustained elevated oil prices.

The trailing P/E of 44.9x reflects the depressed 2025 earnings base ($1.35 EPS per Phase 1 data) and is not indicative of forward value.

## Competitive Landscape

OXY's competitive position within the Permian Basin is strong. The company operates approximately 10% of basin oil production with declining per-unit costs, extensive CO₂-enhanced oil recovery expertise (34 active floods, 50+ years of experience), and 8.9 million net domestic acres. The CrownRock acquisition added high-quality Midland Basin acreage that has been integrated on schedule.

Against pure Permian peers like FANG and EOG, OXY's competitive trajectory is mixed. The domestic production base is efficient and improving, but international exposure (Oman, Algeria, UAE) creates operational complexity and geopolitical risk that FANG and EOG avoid entirely. The STRATOS/DAC platform is unique among E&P companies and could become a significant differentiator if carbon markets develop, but this is a 3–5 year thesis at the earliest.

The post-OxyChem portfolio is more concentrated on oil & gas, which amplifies both the upside from elevated oil and the downside from normalization.

## Risk Assessment

**Commodity Price Concentration (Critical):** With zero hedges and the chemical business sold, OXY is effectively an unhedged oil bet. A $10/bbl decline in WTI reduces annual pre-tax income by approximately $2–3 billion based on the company's 1.4 million boe/d production. This is the dominant risk factor.

**Tronox Tax Liability ($2.3B):** The IRS disallowed Anadarko's $5.2B Tronox settlement deduction. An adverse ruling would require OXY to repay an $881M tentative cash refund plus related benefits and interest, totaling ~$2.3 billion. Trial and closing arguments are complete; the ruling is pending. This is a binary risk that could consume 6+ months of free cash flow.

**IRS Review of $2.7B Reorganization Tax Benefit:** A separate IRS review of OXY's 2022 legal entity reorganization creates additional contingent tax exposure. Combined with Tronox, the total contingent tax risk exceeds $5 billion.

**DASS Environmental Liability:** OXY retained legacy tort and environmental liabilities from OxyChem operations. The $925M Q4 2024 charge demonstrates the magnitude; further charges are likely over "many years."

**CEO Succession:** Vicki Hollub's expected retirement and replacement by COO Richard Jackson represents leadership transition risk. The succession appears orderly, but Jackson's strategic priorities may differ, and any market-unfriendly signals during transition could pressure the stock.

**Berkshire Preferred Stock:** The $10B in 8% preferred stock creates an $800M annual dividend claim senior to common shareholders, reducing per-share cash flow available for dividends and buybacks.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 53.1% is 19.5 percentage points above 1-year historical volatility of 33.7%, indicating the options market is pricing substantial near-term uncertainty. The term structure is in backwardation (53.1% near-term vs. 41.8% at 6 months), consistent with imminent stress pricing. The put/call OI ratio declined from 0.59 to 0.35 over the past two weeks as IV compressed 29 points and price rallied 12.5%, indicating put hedges were closed during the rally. The current low put/call OI (0.35) suggests limited institutional downside protection is in place. Given OXY's direct oil price exposure and the current backwardation in options, the market sees elevated near-term event risk but is not aggressively positioned for downside.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($70–73):** Oil prices sustain at elevated levels, driving FY2026 EPS toward $5.00+. Debt target achieved by Q2 2026, unlocking share repurchases (with $1.2B remaining on the $3.0B authorization, likely topped up). At $5.00 EPS and 14x multiple, fair value reaches $70. STRATOS begins operations successfully, attracting carbon credit revenue and narrowing the company's ESG discount. Tronox ruling favorable.

**Bear Case ($40–45):** Diplomatic resolution or demand destruction normalizes oil prices. FY2026 EPS compresses to $1.50–2.50. At 18–20x normalized earnings, the stock returns to the low $40s. Adverse Tronox ruling ($2.3B cash outflow) delays buyback pivot. CEO transition creates uncertainty.

**Base Case ($55–63):** Oil prices stabilize in a moderately elevated range. FY2026 EPS of $4.00–4.50 supports the current valuation. Debt target achieved, modest buyback begins. The stock trades sideways within the analyst consensus range ($48–$73).

## Conclusion

OXY has executed well on its strategic priorities: the OxyChem sale closed on time, debt has been reduced by ~$9.4 billion, and the cost structure continues to improve. The oil price environment has shifted dramatically in OXY's favor since the December 2025 lows, and consensus estimates have responded accordingly.

The stock has also responded, rallying ~54% from its lows. At $60.58, the valuation is fair but not compelling relative to peers with lower leverage, better hedging discipline, and fewer contingent liabilities. The specific catalyst sustaining a HOLD rating is the imminent debt target achievement and subsequent buyback pivot, expected within 1–2 quarters. Combined with 4 consecutive earnings beats and broad upward estimate revisions, there is a credible 12-month growth thesis tied to the capital allocation transition.

At this price, OXY is a leveraged call on sustained elevated oil with a reasonable catalyst path. The asymmetry improves meaningfully below $48–50.
