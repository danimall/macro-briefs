## Executive Summary

First Interstate BancSystem is executing a deliberate and largely correct strategic retrenchment: a $25.9 billion-asset bank shrinking its balance sheet 11% over eighteen months, exiting subscale states, selling branches above book, retiring debt, and returning capital at roughly 170% of earnings. Nine consecutive quarters of net interest margin expansion took adjusted fully-taxable-equivalent NIM from 2.95% (FY2024) to 3.42% (Q2 2026), driven by a 60 basis point decline in cost of funds against only an 11 basis point decline in earning-asset yield. Capital is genuinely fortress-grade: CET1 of 14.54%, total risk-based capital of 17.30%, zero other borrowed funds at year-end 2025, no brokered deposits, and $10.4 billion of available liquidity against $7.9 billion of uninsured deposits.

The problem is that none of this has produced earnings power growth, and the price no longer reflects that. Net interest income was $878.8M in FY2023, $821.6M in FY2024, $825.4M in FY2025, and 1H26 came in at $402.9M — down 2.3% from $412.2M in 1H25 — because average earning assets fell from $25.18 billion to $23.48 billion year-over-year. Margin gains are being consumed by volume losses. Purchase accounting accretion, a further support, decayed from $24.6M (FY2024) to $15.0M (FY2025) to $6.6M in 1H26. Approximately 19% of trailing-twelve-month net income of $324.3M came from two branch-sale gains ($62.7M pretax in October 2025, $19.5M pretax in April 2026) that are now largely exhausted — the remaining announced actions are closures, which generate expense savings rather than gains. Stripping those gains at the FY2025 effective rate of ~22.9% gives core trailing EPS of approximately $2.63, moving the P/E from 11.5x to 14.3x against a peer median of 15.9x — a small discount for a bank with declining revenue, deteriorating credit, and a 5-year total shareholder return of +12.1% versus +52.7% for its own proxy peer group.

The Q2 2026 10-Q, filed August 3, changed the credit picture materially. Non-accrual loans rose 18.7% to $158.4M (1.11% of loans) on what the company describes as a single client relationship spanning commercial and commercial real estate. Non-accruals more than 90 days past due jumped from $60.5M to $97.1M. Loans measured at fair value using Level 3 inputs within the collateral-dependent population rose from $21.5M to $53.4M, with appraisal adjustments running 5%–89% and a 31% weighted average. Internally doubtful-rated CRE went from $10.2M to $38.0M. Allowance coverage of non-performing loans fell from 141.88% to 114.09%. Countervailing evidence is real — criticized loans fell 9.3% sequentially, special-mention CRE dropped $60.7M, agricultural non-accruals declined, and the provision was a net $3.2M release — but the composition matters: broad trends improving while a handful of large exposures migrate toward loss recognition is precisely the pattern that produced FY2024 net charge-offs of 0.57% of average loans, including $59.4M of commercial charge-offs. At 14.3x core earnings with the one-time gain pipeline closed and only $170.4M of buyback authorization remaining, there is insufficient margin of safety to carry this credit uncertainty.

## Company Overview

FIBK is a single-segment community banking franchise headquartered in Billings, Montana, operating through First Interstate Bank with 271 offices in 10 states as of July 30, 2026 — down from 289 offices in 12 states at year-end 2025. Business lines are deposit gathering, commercial/CRE/agricultural/consumer lending, mortgage origination and servicing, and wealth management ($8.7 billion AUM at 12/31/25, up from $8.1 billion).

The franchise is a barbell. Per FDIC data as of June 30, 2025 disclosed in the FY2025 10-K, FIBK holds 16.6% deposit share in Montana (#2) and 13.3% in Wyoming (#1) — densities that produce genuine community-banking economics, including deposit pricing power and relationship stickiness. Everywhere else it is subscale: Idaho #9, Oregon #9, Nebraska #8, Iowa #11, Colorado #26, Washington #29, South Dakota #54, North Dakota #72, Missouri #157, Minnesota #289. Those positions carry branch cost structures without pricing power.

Management is acting on the imbalance. The Business section states the company is "exiting markets that are not aligned with our strategic plan and reallocating capital, talent and resources to expand in markets where we have meaningful market share." Executed actions: exited indirect auto/boat/RV lending (originations stopped 2/28/25, $202.7M of 2025 amortization); outsourced the consumer credit card portfolio (June 2025, $74.2M sold for a $4.3M gain); sold 12 Arizona and Kansas branches (October 2025, $641.6M deposits, $291.5M loans, $62.7M gain); sold 11 Nebraska branches (April 2026, $244.2M deposits, $64.1M loans, $19.5M gain); closed four additional Nebraska branches and exited North Dakota and Minnesota in Q1 2026; closed Iowa and Oregon branches in July 2026 with a Washington closure in Q3.

Note that the "Class A" designation in market data appears to be a vendor legacy artifact: the Q2 2026 10-Q describes a single class of common stock, 150 million authorized, one vote per share. Change-of-control defenses run through the charter — a supermajority vote requirement (the greater of a majority of all outstanding voting power or 66⅔% of voting power present), a staggered board, and blank-check preferred authority — rather than through share-class structure.

## Financial Analysis

### The margin story is real and nearly harvested

| | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1-26 | Q2-26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NIM | 3.12% | 3.02% | 3.30% | 3.41% | **3.45%** |
| Adjusted FTE NIM (ex-accretion) | 3.07% | 2.95% | 3.26% | — | **3.42%** |
| Cost of funds | — | 1.83% | 1.47% | 1.27% | **1.23%** |

Forty-seven basis points of adjusted margin expansion since FY2024, and it is not accretion-driven — the adjusted series excludes purchase accounting. The mechanism is deposit repricing: cost of funds fell 60bp while earning-asset yield fell only 11bp (4.76% Q2-25 to 4.65% Q2-26). At 1.23% cost of funds there is limited remaining room, and the bank's own sensitivity table shows net interest income falling 1.93% on a −200bp shock and 0.88% on −100bp, against a +0.97% benefit at +200bp, assuming a 29% deposit beta both ways. Critically, FIBK carries **no designated interest rate hedges** as of 6/30/26 — the $1,083.0M notional of client-facing swaps is customer accommodation, not balance sheet protection. The bank is running unhedged into a rate path its own disclosure describes as modestly adverse.

### Revenue is shrinking faster than margin is expanding

Net interest income: $878.8M (FY23) → $821.6M (FY24) → $825.4M (FY25) → $402.9M in 1H26 versus $412.2M in 1H25. Total assets fell from ~$29.14B (12/31/24) to $25.89B (6/30/26), −11.2%. Loans held for investment fell from ~$17.84B to $14.28B, −20.0%. Construction loans dropped from 10.0% of loans in 2023 to $576.1M at 6/30/26 (−31.2% in six months alone); agricultural loans fell 31.3% in six months to $357.3M.

A mechanical run-rate on Q2-26 NII ($202.2M × 4 = $808.8M) sits below FY2025's $825.4M. That is a sensitivity, not a forecast — it ignores continued deposit repricing, agricultural seasonality, and the fact that Q2-26 was the first quarter without the Nebraska branches. But it frames the problem: the bull case depends entirely on earning assets troughing. Seeking Alpha reported on July 24 that management expects Q3 2026 to be the bottom for average earning assets. That claim does not appear in any filing and should be treated as unconfirmed; it is nonetheless the pivotal variable.

### Earnings quality: gains doing ~19% of the work

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net income | $226.0M | $302.1M | $60.2M | $83.9M |
| Diluted EPS | $2.19 | $2.94 | $0.61 | $0.87 |
| ROA / ROE | 0.75% / 6.92% | 1.09% / 8.83% | — | — |
| Efficiency ratio | 62.30% | 59.19% | — | 60.2% |

Trailing-twelve-month net income to 6/30/26: $180.2M (2H25) + $144.1M (1H26) = **$324.3M**, or roughly $3.23–$3.27 per diluted share depending on share-count basis. At $37.625 that is 11.5–11.6x.

Adjusting for the two branch gains at the FY2025 effective rate of ~22.9%: 2H25 $180.2M − $48.3M = $131.9M; Q1-26 $60.2M; Q2-26 $83.9M − $15.0M = $68.9M. **Core TTM ≈ $261.0M ≈ $2.63/share → 14.3x.** Consensus corroborates the level: current-year estimate $2.71, next-year $2.85 (cut 3.3% over 90 days).

The same adjustment applies to the ratios investors cite as evidence of improvement. Q2-26 efficiency computes to 60.2% ($158.9M expense / $263.9M revenue) but 65.0% excluding the gain. FY2025 ROA of 1.09% and the 59.19% efficiency ratio are similarly gain-flattered. Q2 EPS of $0.87 was widely reported as a 35–36% beat; excluding the ~$0.15/share after-tax gain, EPS was approximately $0.72 — a modest beat at best. Whether consensus contemplated the gain cannot be resolved from available sources, so the magnitude of the true operating beat is unresolved.

Expense discipline is present but modest: noninterest expense was $656.8M (FY23) → $637.4M (FY24) → $640.3M (FY25) — essentially flat while assets fell 11%. Headcount fell 3.0% to 3,376.

### Balance sheet: overcapitalized, underlevered, with an embedded mark

Loans/deposits of **66.6%** ($14,281.4M / $21,441.3M) is the cleanest quantitative statement of the strategic problem: ample funding and capital, insufficient acceptable loan demand. The excess flows into securities, which grew to $7,985.6M at 6/30/26 (30.8% of assets) at a 2.71% portfolio yield — accretive to nothing.

Capital: CET1 14.54%, total risk-based 17.30%, leverage 9.59%. Tangible common equity = $3,322.6M equity − $1,100.9M goodwill − $46.7M other intangibles = $2,175.0M. FIBK has elected the AOCI opt-out, so the −$203.5M AOCI does not reduce regulatory capital; adding it back and deriving RWA from the reported ratio implies excess CET1 above a 7.0% minimum-plus-buffer of roughly **$1.23 billion, about 34% of market capitalization**. This figure is derived from an inferred CET1 dollar amount rather than read directly, so treat it as directionally robust and precisely approximate.

Two offsets to the capital picture. First, HTM securities carry $2,215.8M against a $2,000.3M fair value — a **$215.5M unrecognized loss not in equity**; tax-effected at 25%, adjusted TCE falls to $2,013.4M, or $21.07 per share. Second, the AFS loss picture is not improving in the way it superficially appears: the balance in a continuous unrealized-loss position beyond 12 months fell from $5,645.8M to $5,078.3M while the associated gross unrealized loss *rose* from $443.2M to $462.1M. Average loss severity deepened.

Valuation on book: book value per share $34.77 (P/B 1.08x); TBV per share $22.76 (**P/TBV 1.65x**); mark-adjusted TBV $21.07 (**1.79x**). Goodwill of $1,100.9M is 33.1% of stated equity and is tested annually on July 1 using a qualitative assessment only.

### Capital return: powerful, finite, and now less accretive

Buyback authorization went $150M (August 2025) → $300M (January 2026) → $450M (July 2026). FY2025 repurchases: 3,653,914 shares at $32.18 average ($117.6M). 1H26: 4,322,662 shares at $35.32 average ($152.7M), plus $9.3M in July. Share count fell from 101,105,745 (12/31/25) to 95,548,084 (6/30/26) — **−5.5% in six months**. Combined 1H26 return of ~$152.7M of buybacks plus ~$92M of dividends is ~170% of $144.1M net income, funded by balance sheet contraction and excess capital.

Roughly 5–6 points of year-over-year EPS growth is share-count arithmetic, not operating improvement. Two constraints now bind. First, $170.4M of authorization remained at July 30, 2026 — approximately one and a half quarters at the 1H26 pace. Second, repurchasing at 1.65x tangible book (1.79x mark-adjusted) is materially less accretive to TBV per share than FY2025 buying at $32.18 average. The Federal Reserve's policy statement on holding-company distributions, cited in the 10-K, contemplates prior consultation where distributions exceed four-quarter net income, which may bind before the authorization is exhausted.

Dividend: $0.47 quarterly, reaffirmed July 22, 2026 → $1.88 annualized = **5.0% yield** at $37.625. That is 58% of GAAP TTM EPS but **~71% of core**. Disclosed payout ratios were 75.81% (FY23), 85.84% (FY24), 63.73% (FY25) — the FY2025 improvement came substantially from the branch gain. Operating cash flow fell to $112.9M in 1H26 from $146.6M in 1H25.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth thesis, and management's incentive plan says so. The DEF 14A performance measures are Total Shareholder Return, Core Return on Average Tangible Common Equity, Adjusted PPNR per Share, Adjusted Efficiency Ratio, and Non-Performing Assets/Total Assets. No loan growth or deposit growth metric appears. That is internally consistent design for a shrink-to-profitability strategy and it removes the incentive to chase volume — a genuine positive. It also means the only paths to EPS growth are margin (nearly harvested), expenses (flat for three years), share count (authorization nearly depleted), and credit costs (currently moving the wrong way).

The risk factors concede the demand problem in the company's own words: "we have experienced weaker than anticipated loan production, including muted demand for certain commercial real estate and construction lending and softer new construction activity in several of our markets. If these conditions persist or we are unable to generate sufficient new loan production to offset ongoing runoff, amortization and payoffs, our loan balances may decline." A separate factor notes "heightened competition for certain commercial lending opportunities" and that FIBK "may determine that we are not willing to assume the same level or type of risk as certain more aggressive competitors." Read charitably that is discipline; read as a forecast it says loan growth is not returning near-term.

Working against a near-term inflection: the credit-risk framework overhaul initiated in late 2024 (immediately after the year of $104.5M net charge-offs) centralized approval authority for exposures above defined thresholds and involved "targeted paydowns and payoffs" of large criticized credits. Tighter, slower, centralized approval is the right control response and is also plausibly part of why production is weak. The remedy and the growth constraint overlap.

Estimate revisions are neutral-to-negative and do not support an inflection read: current-year consensus rose 3.5% over 90 days (to $2.71) on the gain-inflated Q2, while next-year was cut 3.3% (from $2.95 to $2.85). Wealth management AUM growth of ~7% to $8.7 billion is the only clearly expanding line, and at a fee-revenue base too small to move the aggregate.

Growth prospects score: **3.5**.

## Competitive Landscape

Montana (16.6%, #2) and Wyoming (13.3%, #1) are defensible franchises where local decision authority and brand density translate into deposit pricing power. Montana law caps any acquirer at 30% of state deposits, so FIBK's ability to consolidate its best market is real but bounded. Everywhere else, the company competes without density against larger banks with, in the 10-K's own words, "greater resources... higher lending limits," plus credit unions, fintechs and internet banks. The divestiture program is the right response — selling subscale branches above book creates value directly — and the tail is being shortened.

Two trajectory concerns qualify the improvement. First, deposit mix is eroding at the source of the franchise advantage: noninterest-bearing deposits fell from 25.9% of total (FY23) to 25.2% (FY24) to 23.9% (FY25), with the risk factors citing "select customer movement into higher-yielding deposit products." Cheap funding is the asset a dense community franchise is supposed to protect. Second, the strategy itself creates concentration: the risk factors newly disclose that divestitures "may increase our dependence on deposits and customer relationships in our remaining markets."

Sector-level: FIBK is riding the same deposit-cost tailwind as most regional banks, but with an unusual offset — most peers are not shrinking earning assets 7% year-over-year. The proxy TSR series (+12.1% five-year for FIBK versus +52.7% for its peer group, with FIBK still below its 2021 starting value at year-end 2024) indicates the market has paid for growth and clean credit, and FIBK delivered neither. Two policy items bear directly: the unfinalized Regulation II interchange reduction (FIBK is above the $10B Durbin threshold; payment services revenue was $67.9M in FY2025), and the unresolved CRA framework, where FIBK's most recent published rating is "satisfactory" rather than "outstanding" — relevant to merger and branch application approvals.

Competitive position score: **5.0** — a correct strategic response to a genuinely uneven franchise, with deposit-mix erosion and post-divestiture concentration offsetting the improvement.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit is the dominant risk and it moved adversely in Q2.** The specifics from the 10-Q: non-accruals $133.5M → $158.4M (1.11% of loans) on a single commercial/CRE client relationship; non-accruals 90+ days past due $60.5M → $97.1M; collateral-dependent loans $102.1M → $125.6M with the Level 3 fair-value subset $21.5M → $53.4M at appraisal adjustments of 5%–89% (31% weighted average); doubtful-rated CRE $10.2M → $38.0M and commercial $13.1M → $25.0M (~$70.8M combined versus ~$44.1M at year-end); modified-loan payment defaults $2.1M → $19.5M year-over-year; ACL/NPL coverage 141.88% → 114.09%. Q2 charge-offs of $14.0M included $8.5M agricultural. NPAs/assets rose to 0.64% from 0.52% — and NPAs/assets is an explicit executive pay metric, so management's own scorecard is registering this.

The offsetting evidence is genuine and should not be dismissed: criticized loans fell 9.3% sequentially, special-mention CRE dropped from $357.9M to $297.2M, agricultural non-accruals declined ($24.6M → $17.8M), the ACL ratio rose slightly to 1.28%, and the provision was a net $3.2M release. Migration to non-accrual on a well-secured credit can precede full recovery. But the appraisal write-downs embedded in the Level 3 disclosure and the coverage decline suggest resolution consumes more than a $3.2M release, and FY2024's 0.57% net charge-off year plus the separately disclosed $15.8M single-name CRE charge-off in Q4 2025 establish a pattern of concentrated rather than diffuse losses. The 10-K's own severe-scenario sensitivity is +$41.1M to the allowance.

**Concentration.** $8.1B of CRE = 69.1% of loans held for investment, with only 33.4% owner-occupied — roughly two-thirds dependent on third-party rents and refinancing markets. Uninsured deposits are $7.9B (36.9% of total), against $10.4B of available liquidity — adequate coverage.

**Balance-sheet and structural.** The $215.5M unrecognized HTM loss (~$161.6M after tax, 7.4% of TCE); goodwill at 33.1% of equity tested qualitatively only with a single reporting unit; no designated interest rate hedges into a two-sided rate path; Regulation II as an unresolved fee-income binary; and the Scott family share overhang, which the 10-K names as a risk factor and which Form 4 data confirms is active.

**Governance and personnel.** Nine Item 5.02 8-K filings between August 2025 and July 2026 — roughly one material personnel event per six weeks. A substantial portion is explained by the disclosed Q4 2025 organizational flattening (new State Presidents, completed in Q1 2026), but a named executive separation agreement in May 2026 (Kristina Robbins) and two new directors in July 2026 indicate churn continued past the announced restructuring. The content of seven of the nine filings is not verifiable from available sources, so the volume is a fact and the composition is not.

Risk factors score: **4.4** — specific, dated, quantified downside catalysts in the credit book that the current multiple does not discount.

### Options Market Signal

The options structure is unambiguously cautionary and shifted against the stock since the Q2 print. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 41.6% against 27.9% one-year realized (a 13.7-point premium, 65th percentile within the chain), the term structure is in backwardation (41.6% near versus 29.2% at six months), and the put/call open-interest ratio is 3.61 with an elevated 10.3% put skew at the front tenor. Comparing snapshots between July 23 and August 7, ATM IV rose 6.5 points while price fell 2.0% and put/call open interest rose from 3.14 to 3.61 — protection was added and repriced upward as the stock retraced. Short interest is 14.6% of float with 8.3 days to cover.

Two caveats. Only 20 contracts across three expirations are reflected, so this is a thin chain and any single position distorts the ratios; and the 8.2% implied move on the August 21 tenor with no earnings scheduled suggests hedging around macro events (payrolls, the September FOMC) as much as company-specific fear. Still, the combination of backwardation, rising put open interest, elevated short interest with meaningful days-to-cover, and no insider open-market buying is consistent with the fundamental read rather than contradicting it. Note the two-sided implication of the short interest: 14.6% with 8.3 days to cover creates real squeeze potential on any clean quarter, which is the principal risk to the REDUCE call.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case → $44 (+17%, ~+22% with dividend)

Q3 2026 marks the trough in average earning assets, as reportedly guided. From there, a 3.45% NIM applied to a stabilizing then modestly growing earning-asset base produces the net interest income growth that margin expansion has so far failed to deliver. The single-relationship non-accrual resolves with minimal loss, criticized-asset improvement (−9.3% sequentially) proves to be the leading indicator and the non-accrual spike the lagging one, and net charge-offs stay near the 0.17% 1H26 pace. The board reauthorizes buybacks beyond the current $450M, retiring another 4–5% of shares annually against $1.2B of excess CET1. Expense discipline from the flattened organization and the closed branches drops the efficiency ratio into the mid-50s. Core EPS reaches $2.90–$3.00 and the market applies 15x, a peer-median multiple, on the argument that the strategic cleanup is complete. Fifteen times $2.93 = $44.

### Bear Case → $28 (−26%, ~−21% with dividend)

The single client relationship resolves with loss content consistent with the 31% weighted-average appraisal write-down on a $53.4M Level 3 population, and the doubtful-rated migration ($44.1M → $70.8M) continues. Net charge-offs normalize toward the FY2024 pace of 0.57% — roughly $81M on the current loan book versus ~$39M annualized in Q2, a ~$43M pretax / ~$0.35 per share hit — pushing core EPS to $2.30–$2.40 and the dividend payout above 80%. Simultaneously, loan production stays weak, the earning-asset trough slips into 2027, and net interest income declines again in 2027. The buyback authorization is exhausted in Q4 2026 and either not renewed or renewed at a reduced pace under supervisory consultation as distributions exceed four-quarter earnings. Eleven times $2.55 ≈ $28, which also corresponds to roughly 1.2x tangible book and sits near the $28.57 fifty-two-week low.

### Base Case → $35 (−7%, ~−2% with dividend)

Earning assets trough roughly on schedule but net interest income stabilizes in the low-$800M range rather than growing, as continued accretion decay (~$6.6M in 1H26 and falling) and a cost of funds already at 1.23% cap further margin help. Credit costs run above 2025 but below 2024 — net charge-offs of 0.30–0.40% — as the identified large relationship generates real but contained loss. Core EPS reaches roughly $2.80 in 2027 on a 4% lower share count, consistent with the $2.85 consensus. The market applies 12.5x, below the peer median given no revenue growth and demonstrated single-name credit volatility: 12.5x × $2.80 = $35. Cross-check on book: tangible book per share of roughly $23 in twelve months (core earnings less dividend, less TBV dilution from buybacks above book) at 1.52x = $35.

Probability weighting 25% bull / 45% base / 30% bear on total return (+22% / −2% / −21%) gives an expected total return of roughly −2%. That is below the return available in short Treasuries and does not compensate for the credit uncertainty.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: REDUCE.** The distinction from HOLD rests on the base case sitting 7% below the current price and the probability-weighted expected total return being approximately zero to negative, with the adverse scenario (−21% including dividend) larger than the favorable one (+22%) after weighting. The distinction from AVOID rests on genuine downside cushions: CET1 of 14.54% with ~$1.2B of excess capital, a 5.0% dividend covered at 71% of core earnings, 1.08x stated book value, $10.4B of liquidity, and a management team executing a strategically correct retrenchment.
