**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Bristow is the world's largest provider of offshore helicopter transport and public-sector search-and-rescue (SAR) aviation, operating 214 aircraft across 15 countries. The investment case rests on a structural tightness in offshore-configured heavy and super-medium helicopters — effective utilization near 100%, 24-month-plus new-build lead times, and most recent OEM output diverted to closed markets — that is driving Bristow's legacy Offshore Energy Services (OES) contracts to reset at higher "leading-edge" rates. Two largely contractual earnings tailwinds are timed to converge in 2027: essentially all legacy OES contracts reprice by year-end 2026, and the transition costs that crushed Government Services (GS) margins to 1% in 2025 roll off as the IRCG and UKSAR2G SAR contracts complete their stand-up. Management guides GS 2026 operating income to roughly double to $70–80M and total adjusted EBITDA up ~25% to $295–325M.

That structural picture is currently obscured by two consecutive EPS misses (Q4 2025 –31.7%, Q1 2026 –56.5%) and a sharply negative estimate-revision profile. The Q1 2026 print combined a 10.9% revenue beat ($388.7M) with a GAAP EPS miss of ~57% because of accelerated non-cash S-76 depreciation (~$24M through early 2027, $6.4M booked in Q1) and lower vendor credits. The market read the miss as an operational problem and marked the stock down from a ~$50 April high to the low $40s. Consensus current-year EPS has fallen 22.5% over 90 days to $3.91, and next-year EPS is down 16.2% to $5.26. The core tension is that the fundamental setup is improving while near-term earnings visibility is deteriorating on a mix of accounting optics and genuine cost pressure (repairs/maintenance, lease costs, availability penalties tied to S92 parts delays).

We rate VTOL a HOLD with a constructive lean and a growth-hold rationale. The 2027 earnings step-change is credible and much of it is contractually locked rather than dependent on higher oil. The two-quarter miss streak, the falling estimate curve, and offshore-energy cyclicality leave the risk/reward balanced at $45 rather than asymmetric. We would add on confirmation that full-year guidance holds through the next one to two prints or on a pullback toward the high $30s.

## Company Overview

Bristow emerged in its current form from the 2020 merger of Era Group and "Old Bristow," which had filed Chapter 11 in 2019. It operates a fleet of 214 aircraft (196 helicopters, 14 fixed-wing, 4 UAS) under 12 Air Operator Certificates across three reportable segments:

- **Offshore Energy Services (OES) — 66% of 2025 revenue.** Transports personnel to and from offshore production platforms and drilling rigs in the U.S. Gulf, Brazil, Nigeria, the North Sea (UK/Norway), and Latin America. Roughly 85% of OES revenue is tied to production activity rather than exploration, which materially dampens the rig-count cyclicality that defines the broader oilfield-services sector.
- **Government Services — 26% of 2025 revenue.** Public-sector SAR under 10-year-plus contracts: UK SAR (UKSAR/UKSAR2G with the UK Department for Transport), Irish Coast Guard (IRCG), Netherlands, Dutch Caribbean, and the Falklands. High credit quality, stable, long-duration cash flows.
- **Other Services — 8% of 2025 revenue.** Airnorth regional airline in Australia, Humberside Airport (UK), and helicopter dry-leasing.

Bristow is the largest player in a consolidated field (CHC Group, NHV, Omni Helicopters, PHI). Incumbency, a strong safety record, technical qualification, and its position as the OEMs' largest customer for key rotary models create high barriers to entry in its markets.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** Revenue grew from $1.185B (2022) to $1.491B (2025), a +5.3% YoY gain in the latest year, with 2026 guidance of $1.6–1.7B (+7–14%). The company swung from net losses in 2022 (–$15.8M) and 2023 (–$6.8M) to net income of $94.8M (2024) and $129.1M (2025). FY2025 operating margin was 10.7%, net margin 8.7%, ROE 12.2%, ROA 5.6%. Pretax income jumped 48% to $151M.

The segment mix tells the real story. OES operating margin expanded from 14% to 17% (operating income +25% to $165.6M) on higher leading-edge contract rates plus $34M lower repairs and maintenance (favorable vendor credits). Government Services operating income collapsed 76% to $5.1M (margin 6%→1%) on transition and start-up costs for the new IRCG and UKSAR2G contracts (subcontractor costs +$28.2M). Management frames the GS collapse as temporary and guides 2026 GS operating income to $70–80M, roughly double 2025, as transitions complete (the final UKSAR2G base transitions in late 2026).

**Quality-of-earnings caveats.** Two items flatter the FY2025 result. First, a $22.5M FX gain in "Other, net" contributed meaningfully to the 48% pretax jump; underlying operating income growth of +19.8% to $158.8M is the cleaner metric and the FX tailwind should not be extrapolated. Second, the OES margin benefited from lower R&M vendor credits that are timing-driven — the reversal of that dynamic contributed to the weaker Q1 2026 print.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow was $198.4M in 2025 against $142M capex, yielding FCF of $56.4M (FCF margin 3.8%). Capex fell sharply from $255M in 2024 as the SAR fleet build-out wound down, aiding free cash generation. Cash was $286M and total liquidity $346.9M at year-end 2025, rising to roughly $394M by Q1 2026. Debt/equity was 1.18 with long-term debt of $643.5M. The Altman Z-Score of 2.19 sits in the grey zone, but this reflects the capital-intensive, asset-heavy model (PP&E $1.15B) rather than distress; the current ratio is 1.9. The January 2026 refinancing (6.750% Senior Secured Notes due 2033 replacing the 6.875% 2028 notes, ABL extended to 2031 at 25bps lower pricing) removed near-term maturity risk and lowered the cost of capital.

**Capital-return inflection.** In February 2026 Bristow initiated its first-ever quarterly dividend ($0.125/sh), raised to $0.25/sh declared April 30, 2026 (~$1.00 annualized, ~2.2% yield at current price). A $125M buyback authorization remains largely intact ($121M), and the company made $40M of accelerated debt paydowns in 2025 plus repurchased 123,966 shares at an average $32.43. This marks a transition from balance-sheet repair to shareholder returns and signals board confidence.

**Earnings surprise pattern.** The four-quarter record is 2 beats, 2 misses, but the sequence matters: two large beats (Q2 2025 +108%, Q3 2025 +98%) gave way to two large misses (Q4 2025 –32%, Q1 2026 –57%). The recent misses reflect the S-76 depreciation acceleration, higher R&M and lease costs, and lower vendor credits rather than a revenue problem — the Q1 top line beat. The pattern reduces confidence in the precision of forward estimates and warrants treating the 2026 EBITDA guidance as a hypothesis to be confirmed, not a given.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has three legs, two of them largely contractual.

**OES rate resets.** The largest U.S. Gulf OES contract reset at higher rates effective January 2026, and management expects essentially all legacy OES contracts to reset to leading-edge rates by year-end 2026, with the full-year benefit landing in 2027. The mechanism is structural supply tightness: after the 2014–2021 downturn, few new offshore-configured heavy/super-medium helicopters were built, utilization is near 100%, new builds carry 24-month-plus lead times, and much recent OEM production went to closed markets (China, Middle East) or non-competing militaries. This gives incumbents pricing power and better contract terms (stronger cancellation penalties, risk-sharing).

**Government Services recovery.** The 2025 GS margin collapse to 1% is a transition-cost artifact — Bristow was standing up two of the world's largest SAR contracts (IRCG, ~€670M 10-year; UKSAR2G) while running legacy operations. Guidance to roughly double GS operating income in 2026 as those costs roll off is the clearest near-term catalyst and is largely contractually locked rather than market-dependent.

**Convergence in 2027.** These two tailwinds are timed to converge: OES resets fully in effect for full-year 2027, SAR transition costs cleared by late 2026. A step-change in earnings power is plausible in 2027 even without cyclical help from higher oil. Management characterizes 2026 as a "mid-cycle plateau" with drilling and exploration expected to reaccelerate in late 2026.

**Optionality.** Advanced Air Mobility partnerships (Vertical Aerospace, Electra.aero, BETA Technologies) secure first-delivery slots and lead-operator roles with minimal capital at risk — Bristow's value is its certified operations and AOCs, not aircraft development. This is a cheap call option, not a near-term valuation driver.

The offsetting reality is estimate momentum. Current-quarter consensus fell 39.6% over 90 days (to $0.84), and the revision curve is broadly negative across every window. The bull framing is that these cuts largely reflect the non-cash S-76 depreciation and vendor-credit timing rather than lost demand, but the cuts are real, the analyst coverage is thin (1–3 analysts per line), and the direction is down. We weight the contractual catalysts as durable while acknowledging the near-term earnings trajectory is deteriorating.

## Valuation Assessment

VTOL trades at ~11.8x trailing EPS of $4.32 and, on falling forward estimates, roughly 11.6x current-year consensus and ~8.6x next-year. Against the sector-peer set (RES 76.6x, HLX 100.5x, INVX 38.2x, CLB 21.5x; peer median 76.6x), Bristow looks conspicuously cheap. That peer comparison is largely uninformative — those multiples reflect trough or distressed earnings at capital-equipment and oilfield-service names, not a clean quality read. The equity-lesson caution against treating a low multiple as a buy signal applies here: cheapness alone is a value-trap flag, not a thesis.

The independent reason the multiple could be too low is the 2027 earnings inflection. If GS operating income doubles and the OES resets carry a full-year benefit into 2027, the normalized earnings power sits well above trailing, and 8–9x forward on rising (rather than falling) estimates would be genuinely cheap. The problem is timing and confirmation: the current estimate curve is falling, so the market is not yet willing to underwrite the 2027 step-change. The analyst price targets (low $56, mean $62, high $70, all above the current $45) imply 23–54% upside, and on inflection stocks with reasonable multiples those targets often behave as floors as consensus catches up. The two-quarter miss streak and the negative revision profile mean these targets are also vulnerable to further downward revision if guidance slips.

We assign a valuation score modestly above neutral. The absolute multiple plus dividend support caps the downside, and the 2027 setup offers real asymmetry if guidance holds, but the falling estimate curve prevents scoring this as a clear mispricing today. The valuation dimension has trended down from 7.2 to 5.5 across prior reports; we hold it near the low end of the constructive range given the estimate deterioration is genuine, not merely price action.

## Competitive Landscape

Bristow is the clear leader in a consolidated global helicopter-services market. The structural offshore supply/demand imbalance favors incumbents, giving operators pricing leverage and better contract terms. Bristow's scale as the OEMs' largest customer for key models is a financing and allocation advantage in a supply-constrained market. On the government side, incumbency, safety record, technical qualification, and 10-year-plus contract durations create high switching costs and defensible positions.

The trajectory is improving on both fronts: OES margins are expanding, and the GS margin collapse is a transition artifact set to reverse toward contractual profitability. Three megatrends broaden the addressable market beyond civilian energy transport — increased NATO defense spending (outsourcing civilian Coast Guard/SAR and ISR work), energy security favoring stable offshore basins, and eventual transport electrification (AAM). The competitive picture is stable-to-improving with genuine structural tailwinds the market has not fully rewarded, supporting a competitive-position score above the midpoint.

## Risk Assessment

**Supply-chain / OEM parts dependency (most acute operational risk).** Bristow depends on a handful of OEMs (Sikorsky/Leonardo/GE) and has experienced significant S92 parts delays (S92 = ~29% of the fleet), forcing groundings and lost business. Government contracts levy availability penalties when aircraft are unavailable — a direct, recurring margin risk management flagged repeatedly.

**S-76 fleet retirement.** The Q1 2026 decision to retire the S-76 medium fleet by early 2027 (OEM support scarcity) triggers the additional non-cash depreciation. Operationally driven, but the primary near-term earnings-optics headwind and a driver of the recent misses.

**Customer and contract concentration.** Top 10 customers = 63% of revenue, top 3 = 36%. The UK SAR contract alone is ~19% of revenue and is terminable at will by the DfT (with a cancellation fee). Government appropriation risk applies to the whole GS segment.

**Offshore energy cyclicality.** Despite production-weighting, a sustained oil/gas downturn would hit OES. Heavy reliance on mature basins (North Sea, U.S. Gulf) adds a decline-rate risk. Fuel is a genuine natural hedge — pass-through in nearly all OES and most government contracts — which materially de-risks the P&L versus typical energy-services peers.

**FX exposure.** 83% of revenue is non-U.S. (GBP/EUR the biggest translation risks), and 65% of cash is held offshore with Brazil/Nigeria repatriation controls. The $22.5M FX gain that flattered FY2025 should be treated as non-recurring.

**Other.** Nigeria geopolitical/local-content risk (13% of revenue, up to 5% contract penalties); ~60% unionized workforce with several CBAs expired or expiring in 2025–2026 (strike/cost risk); a charter provision that strips voting rights from non-U.S. holders above 25% (FAA citizenship requirement), a structural overhang on liquidity and appeal. Insider-transaction and detailed proxy/comp data were not obtainable this session — a genuine gap in the management-alignment read.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 52.3% sits well above 32.6% trailing realized vol (a rich 19.7pp spread), the term structure is in backwardation (52.3% near vs. 38.2% at six months), and put/call skew is elevated at 9.7% with an implied ±12.2% move on the nearest expiry. This is consistent with hedging into the next earnings print and reflects the market's uncertainty about whether full-year guidance holds after two consecutive misses. ATM IV has fallen from 64.7% to 52.3% over the past month as the price recovered 7.7%, and the P/C OI ratio eased from 0.57 to 0.44, so some bearish positioning has unwound. Short interest is low at 4.4% of float (3.8 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic. The signal reinforces caution on entry timing rather than contradicting the fundamental thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Full-year 2026 guidance holds and GS operating income doubles as SAR transition costs clear. OES contracts fully reset to leading-edge rates by year-end 2026, carrying a full-year benefit into 2027 alongside a re-acceleration in offshore drilling. Estimate revisions turn positive as the S-76 depreciation is recognized as a one-time modeling event, and the market re-rates the stock toward 12–13x normalized 2027 earnings power. Analyst targets ($56–70) prove to be floors. Dividend growth and buyback continue. Bull target $60–70.

**Bear case.** The estimate cuts prove structural rather than optical: S92 parts delays and availability penalties compress margins, OES resets underwhelm amid a sustained offshore-energy downturn, and GS transition costs run longer or heavier than guided. FX translation and Nigeria/labor risks bite. Two more misses push consensus lower and the stock derates on lost credibility. Bear target ~$33–36 (the 52-week low zone, ~20–27% downside).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the primary thesis is a 2027 earnings step-change, and the equity is worth owning through it if guidance is confirmed, but the negative near-term estimate trajectory prevents an upgrade today.

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) Full-year 2026 guidance affirmed or raised through the next one to two prints, with current-year consensus EPS revisions turning positive; (2) GS segment operating income tracking toward the $70–80M guide in quarterly detail; (3) a pullback toward the high $30s that widens the discount to the 2027 earnings power while fundamentals stay intact.
- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) A third consecutive EPS miss driven by operating cost inflation (R&M, availability penalties) rather than the known S-76 depreciation; (2) full-year 2026 EBITDA guidance cut below $295M; (3) loss or material rate-down of the UK SAR contract (~19% of revenue) or a broad OES rate-reset disappointment.
- **Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings print is the natural checkpoint; the year-end 2026 completion of OES resets and SAR transitions is the decisive validation window for the 2027 thesis.

## Conclusion

Bristow offers a genuine, largely contractual earnings inflection into 2027 — OES rate resets and the roll-off of Government Services transition costs — backed by a structurally tight offshore-helicopter supply picture, a natural fuel hedge, a refinanced balance sheet with no near-term maturities, and a newly initiated and rising dividend. The competitive position is stable-to-improving, and the analyst target range ($56–70) implies material upside if guidance holds.

Against that, the near-term earnings momentum is negative: two consecutive large EPS misses, current-year consensus down 22.5% and next-year down 16.2% over 90 days, and options positioning that prices imminent stress. Much of the miss is attributable to non-cash S-76 depreciation and vendor-credit timing rather than demand, but the cuts are real and the offshore-energy backdrop remains cyclical. The score trajectory has declined from 6.9 to 5.8 across prior reports on exactly these growth, valuation, and management dimensions; new data neither reverses nor accelerates that trend and confirms a balanced setup awaiting confirmation. We rate VTOL a HOLD with a constructive lean, and would upgrade on guidance confirmation with positive revision turn, or on a pullback toward the high $30s.
