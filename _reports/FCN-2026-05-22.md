## Executive Summary

FTI Consulting is a high-quality expert advisory franchise trading at 16.7x forward FY26 EPS ($9.19 consensus) after a 19% decline from its April 2026 highs near $189. The business is bifurcated: Corporate Finance (41% of revenue) is firing on all cylinders with +19% Q1 growth driven by restructuring demand from private credit stress, while Economic Consulting (Compass Lexecon) posted negative segment EBITDA of -$5.9M in Q1 after losing key antitrust economists. The firm's aggressive capital return program — $859M in buybacks funded partially by $365M in new revolver debt — masks a 3.3% decline in net income with a 5.5% EPS increase.

The most significant data point supporting the stock at current levels is the coordinated insider purchase of ~$2.1M by three C-suite executives (CEO, CFO, CSTO) on May 13 at ~$144/share, days after Q1 results disappointed. This is a textbook cluster buy with informational advantage. However, estimate revisions are moving in the wrong direction (-3.1% for FY26, -7.2% for the current quarter), the balance sheet has deteriorated materially, and the Compass Lexecon recovery timeline remains multi-quarter with no visibility on stabilization. The risk/reward is attractive but insufficiently asymmetric for a high-conviction BUY given negative earnings momentum and the specific structural risk in Economic Consulting.

## Company Overview

FTI Consulting operates as a global expert advisory firm across five segments: Corporate Finance (turnaround/restructuring/transactions, 41% of revenue), Forensic & Litigation Consulting (20%), Economic Consulting (19%), Technology (10%), and Strategic Communications (10%). The firm employs 6,421 billable professionals across 104+ offices in 26+ countries. Revenue is approximately 63% U.S. and 37% international. The business model is primarily time-and-expense billing, with average bill rates ranging from $442/hr (FLC) to $583/hr (Economic Consulting).

The firm advises 95 of the Fortune 100 and works with 99 of the top 100 law firms. Its restructuring practice is the dominant global franchise, having grown 3x over the past 8 years.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Growth:** FTI grew revenue from $3.49B (2023) to $3.79B (2025), a deceleration from 15.2% to 2.4%. Q1 2026 showed acceleration to 9.5% YoY ($983M), with Corporate Finance (+19.2%) and Strategic Communications (+18.4%) driving growth while Economic Consulting (-2.3%) continued to decline. Full-year guidance of $3.94B-$4.10B implies 4-8% growth.

**Profitability:** Adjusted EBITDA of $464M (12.2% margin) in 2025 grew 14.8% YoY, but net income declined 3.3% to $271M due to a tax rate jump from 20.2% to 27.0%. Q1 2026 EPS of $1.90 missed consensus of $2.09 by 9%, breaking a 3-quarter beat streak.

**Cash Flow Deterioration:** Operating cash flow collapsed 61.5% to $152M in 2025 from $395M, driven by massive forgivable loan issuances to retain professionals. FCF was only $94M (2.5% FCF yield at current market cap). Q1 2026 showed -$310M operating cash flow (seasonal but reflecting ongoing retention spending).

**Balance Sheet Leveraging:** The firm went from net cash ($660M cash, $0 debt) in 2023 to $557M net debt by March 2026. This was entirely driven by buyback funding. Net debt/adjusted EBITDA is ~1.2x — manageable but a radical shift. The Altman Z-Score of 2.93 sits in the grey zone.

**Capital Return:** $859M repurchased in 2025 at an average of $163.07/share (above current price of $153.24). Q1 2026 added $127M at $161.11 average. Share count reduced 14.6% YoY. Remaining authorization: $365M.

## Growth Analysis

**Bull Factors:**
- Corporate Finance is the clear growth engine, benefiting from elevated interest rates stressing private credit portfolios. Covenant-light leveraged loans from the 2020-2021 era are producing failed liability management exercises that convert to restructuring mandates.
- Geographic expansion (Australia reaching critical mass, London office buildout, Middle East and Nordics development) provides runway.
- Technology segment returned to growth in Q1 (+5.3%) after a -10.5% decline in 2025.
- Bill rate increases of 4-13% across segments demonstrate pricing power.

**Bear Factors:**
- Economic Consulting remains in structural decline from talent losses. The segment posted -$5.9M adjusted EBITDA in Q1 2026 after $19M for full-year 2025 (vs. $104M in 2024). Management states recovery requires "multiple quarters."
- Reduced antitrust enforcement under the current administration diminishes demand for Compass Lexecon's core competency.
- Revenue guidance midpoint implies only 6% growth — not exceptional for a professional services firm.
- Estimate revisions are negative across all periods: current quarter -7.2%, FY26 -3.1%, FY27 -0.8%.

**Growth Score Rationale:** The restructuring tailwind is real and likely durable given elevated rates and private credit stress. However, the Compass Lexecon deterioration is structural, not cyclical, and the negative revision momentum suggests consensus has not yet finished cutting. Growth is segment-bifurcated with no visibility on when the drag resolves.

## Valuation Assessment

At $153.24, FCN trades at:
- 16.7x FY26E EPS ($9.19)
- 13.9x FY27E EPS ($11.02)
- ~10x trailing adjusted EBITDA
- 18.6x trailing P/E on $8.24 EPS

**Peer Comparison:** The peer median P/E is 26.7x (skewed by LZ at 106x). Excluding LZ, peers trade at 11.3-26.7x. FCN at 16.7x forward represents a discount to most consulting peers. Booz Allen (BAH) at 11.3x is cheaper but is a different business (government consulting with distinct dynamics).

**Valuation Support:** The discount is partially justified by negative estimate momentum and the Compass Lexecon structural issue. However, the stock trades below the average buyback price ($163 in 2025, $161 in Q1 2026), and well below insider purchase prices of ~$144 made just 8 days ago. Management reaffirmed guidance of $8.90-$9.60 EPS. At midpoint $9.25, the stock trades at 16.6x — reasonable for a business with its quality characteristics and restructuring tailwind.

**Why This Isn't Obviously Cheap:** The forward multiple of ~17x looks attractive in isolation but requires (1) Compass Lexecon to stop hemorrhaging, (2) no further estimate cuts, and (3) the restructuring cycle to persist. The first condition has no visible timeline. The second is occurring now (revisions negative). The third is probable given elevated rates but introduces cycle dependency.

## Competitive Landscape

FTI's competitive position varies dramatically by segment:

**Corporate Finance (Strong, Improving):** Market leader in global restructuring. The franchise has compounded relationships with creditor committees, debtors, and PE sponsors over decades. Network effects are real — being on more deals generates more referrals. The +19% Q1 growth rate while growing headcount suggests genuine market share gains.

**Economic Consulting (Deteriorating):** The loss of key antitrust economists to competitors (likely Analysis Group, Charles River Associates, or new boutiques) has created persistent share loss in US "routine merger clearance" work. Management acknowledged this explicitly. Non-competes offer only 6-12 months of protection. Rebuilding requires years of relationship development with law firms.

**Technology (Recovering):** Claims faster growth than competitors despite market headwinds. AI/e-discovery positioning through IQ.AI platform provides differentiation but faces longer-term disruption risk as AI commoditizes document review.

**Strategic Communications (Improving):** Moving toward higher-value crisis/event-driven work with +18.4% Q1 growth.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Compass Lexecon Structural Decline (HIGH):** No timeline for recovery. Departed professionals now actively competing for clients. The swing magnitude from 2024 peak ($104M operating income) to current run-rate is ~$110M, or approximately $2.50 of EPS.

2. **Balance Sheet Vulnerability (MEDIUM-HIGH):** If restructuring activity declines or Compass Lexecon worsens, the aggressive buyback-funded-by-debt strategy looks imprudent. Covenant headroom is adequate but narrowing.

3. **Forgivable Loan Cash Drain (MEDIUM):** Multi-year commitments to retained professionals create fixed costs that may not generate returns for years. If revenue disappoints, these become deadweight losses.

4. **Cyclicality (MEDIUM):** The "both engines firing" environment (restructuring + M&A) is temporary. A rate-cutting cycle would ease restructuring demand; continued M&A dormancy hurts Economic Consulting and Technology.

5. **AI Disruption (MEDIUM-TERM):** Document review, routine economic analysis, and data processing tasks face automation. The firm's pricing power is concentrated in senior expert testimony, which is less automatable, but volume-driven work faces compression.

**Specific Rebuttal to BUY Consideration (Score ≥ 6.5 but not BUY):**

The validation system flagged the HOLD rating. Here is why this is not a BUY despite an overall score of 6.48:

1. **Negative estimate revision momentum is the dominant signal.** FY26 estimates have fallen -3.1% over 90 days and are still declining. The Q1 miss was driven by FLC softness and SG&A ($60M headwind for 2026), suggesting consensus still needs to adjust downward. Buying into falling estimates requires a specific thesis about what the market is missing — and the restructuring demand thesis is already embedded in consensus numbers.

2. **The Compass Lexecon problem is structural and unresolved.** Management said recovery takes "multiple quarters" — H2 2027 at the earliest. Until this segment shows sequential improvement, the earnings base is impaired.

3. **The options market is pricing elevated downside risk.** Informed market participants are paying above fair value for protection, creating a divergence with a BUY thesis that demands explanation.

4. **The balance sheet leveraging creates vulnerability without buffer.** Management destroyed capital on 2025 repurchases ($163 avg vs. $153 current). This removes the margin of safety that a conservative balance sheet provides.

**Falsifiable trigger for upgrade to BUY:** If Q2 2026 Economic Consulting segment EBITDA returns to positive (>$5M), AND FY26 EPS estimates stabilize (no further cuts for 30 consecutive days), AND the stock remains below $160, the risk/reward shifts sufficiently to warrant BUY. Additionally, a pullback below $140 (where insiders bought) would represent a more compelling entry regardless of estimate trajectory.

## Conclusion

FTI Consulting is a high-quality franchise experiencing segment bifurcation that obscures underlying business strength. The restructuring practice is world-class and positioned for continued demand in the current rate environment. However, the Compass Lexecon structural issue, negative estimate momentum, aggressive balance sheet leveraging, and elevated options-implied downside risk collectively prevent a BUY recommendation despite a reasonable 16.7x forward multiple and strong insider buying.

The insider cluster buy at ~$144 on May 13 is the strongest bullish signal and suggests management believes the stock has overshot to the downside. This is credible. However, the analytical standard for BUY requires either (a) a specific non-consensus catalyst with timeline, or (b) valuation compression sufficient to create asymmetric upside. At $153, neither condition is clearly met: the catalyst is Compass Lexecon recovery which has no timeline, and the valuation discount to peers (16.7x vs. 26.7x median) is partially deserved given structural segment impairment.

HOLD with a growth orientation. Q2 2026 earnings will clarify whether Economic Consulting stabilizes or deteriorates further, providing the informational clarity needed for a directional call.
