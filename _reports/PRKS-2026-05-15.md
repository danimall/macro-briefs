## Executive Summary

United Parks & Resorts (formerly SeaWorld Entertainment) is a theme park operator running 13 parks across five U.S. states, currently trading at $36.90 with a $1.74B market cap. The company is in clear fundamental deterioration: revenue has declined for four consecutive years ($1,731M in 2022 to $1,663M in 2025), operating income has fallen 28% from peak, and the company has missed earnings estimates in all four most recent quarters by an average of -48%. The Q1 2026 report showed attendance down 5% YoY with a widening operating loss.

The balance sheet carries $2.27B in debt against negative stockholders' equity of $557M, producing an Altman Z-Score of 1.05 (distress zone). Despite this leverage, the company is conducting aggressive share buybacks (~$300M annualized pace), including drawing $80M on its revolver to fund repurchases. This strategy primarily benefits controlling shareholder Hill Path Capital (53.2% ownership). Meanwhile, the Sesame Workshop litigation seeking license termination threatens two standalone parks and themed areas in three others.

The combination of declining fundamentals, excessive leverage, management instability (interim CFO, multiple executive departures), material litigation risk, and a controlling shareholder whose interests may diverge from minority shareholders makes this an AVOID at current levels. The 39.7% short interest and options market pricing (near-term IV at 195.8% vs. 50.8% HV) confirm substantial institutional skepticism.

## Company Overview

United Parks & Resorts operates 13 theme parks including SeaWorld (3 parks), Busch Gardens (2), Aquatica (3), Sesame Place (2), Discovery Cove, Water Country USA, and Adventure Island. The company owns approximately 2,000 acres and houses one of the world's largest zoological collections. Geographic concentration is extreme: 59% of revenue from Florida, 16% from California, 13% from Virginia. Revenue splits roughly 53% admissions / 47% food, merchandise, and other in-park spending.

Hill Path Capital LP holds 53.2% of outstanding shares, making this effectively a controlled company. Hill Path's Chairman (Scott Ross) serves as Board Chairman, with two additional Hill Path affiliates on the board. The proxy explicitly states these directors are "actively involved in overseeing certain key operating activities and decisions."

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Profitability Trajectory:**

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1 2026 (annualized) |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|---------------------|
| Revenue | $1,731M | $1,727M | $1,725M | $1,663M | ~$1,112M pace |
| Operating Margin | 29.3% | 26.6% | 26.9% | 22.0% | -3.1% |
| Adj. EBITDA | $728M | $714M | $700M | $605M | $58M (Q1) |
| FCF | — | $199M | $232M | $163M | — |

Revenue has declined cumulatively 3.9% over three years while operating expenses have risen. Adjusted EBITDA has fallen 17% ($728M to $605M). The Q1 2026 quarter was particularly concerning: revenue fell 3.0% YoY while operating expenses rose 6.2%, producing an operating loss of $8.5M versus operating income of $16.9M in Q1 2025.

**Balance Sheet — Distress-Level Leverage:**

Total debt stands at $2.274B, consisting of $1.52B in Term B-3 loans (due 2031), $725M senior notes (due 2029), and ~$30M in revolver draws. Stockholders' equity is negative $557M, driven by $2.08B in cumulative treasury stock from buybacks. The Altman Z-Score of 1.05 places the company firmly in the distress zone (below 1.81 threshold). Current ratio is 0.53.

Net total leverage is 3.52x covenant-adjusted EBITDA, against a 4.25x threshold for unlimited restricted payments. The covenant-adjusted EBITDA figure includes $30M of "estimated cost savings" — pro-forma adjustments that inflate the denominator. If EBITDA continues declining, covenant headroom narrows rapidly. Annual interest expense of $134M consumes 37% of operating income.

**Capital Allocation — Aggressive and Potentially Imprudent:**

The company repurchased $160M in FY2025, $93M in Q1 2026, and an additional $65M in April-May 2026, running at a ~$300M+ annualized pace. It simultaneously drew $80M on its revolver post-year-end. Buying back stock with borrowed money while fundamentals deteriorate and the Z-Score signals distress is an aggressive capital allocation decision that primarily benefits the 53.2% controlling shareholder by increasing per-share economics without additional investment.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are weak across all measurable dimensions:

- **Attendance:** Declining. Q1 2026 down 5.0% YoY (3.22M vs. 3.39M). FY2025 down 1.8% from FY2024.
- **Revenue per capita:** Mixed. In-park spending rose 5.3% to $40.62 in Q1 2026 (record), but admissions per capita declined. Net revenue per capita fell from $80.07 (FY2024) to $78.54 (FY2025).
- **Pipeline:** Capital expenditure of $217M in FY2025. New rides/attractions in development, but these are maintenance-level investments to retain relevance against a strengthening competitive field.
- **Deferred revenue:** Up $61M sequentially to $204M in Q1 2026, suggesting strong season pass sales ahead of summer. This is the single most constructive data point for H2 2026.

The competitive environment is intensifying. Universal's Epic Universe opened in 2025, adding ~50% more capacity to Universal Orlando directly in PRKS's largest market. The Six Flags/Cedar Fair merger created a stronger regional competitor. International visitation is declining, likely due to strong-dollar headwinds and competition for the Florida tourist dollar.

Analyst estimates show positive revision momentum (+10.5% for current year over 90 days), but the starting base reflects four consecutive earnings misses. The current year estimate of $4.25 EPS implies a dramatic improvement from the recent trajectory that requires both weather cooperation and execution improvement the current management team has not demonstrated.

## Valuation Assessment

At $36.90 with ~47.1M shares outstanding (49.4M diluted), PRKS trades at:
- Market cap: ~$1.74B
- Enterprise value: ~$3.98B (adding $2.27B debt, subtracting ~$29M cash)
- EV/LTM EBITDA: ~6.6x on $605M adjusted EBITDA
- Forward P/E: ~8.7x on $4.25 FY2026 consensus

The 6.6x EV/EBITDA appears optically cheap versus the 8-12x historical theme park range. However, this discount is justified by: (1) declining EBITDA trajectory without demonstrated stabilization, (2) distress-level leverage making equity the residual claim, (3) material litigation risk, and (4) the four-quarter earnings miss streak undermining confidence in forward estimates.

Peer comparison: Six Flags (FUN) trades at negative P/E (unprofitable), Planet Fitness (PLNT) at 17.8x, OneSpaWorld (OSW) at 31.3x. These are imperfect comparisons given different business models, but PRKS's discount reflects its idiosyncratic risks rather than market mispricing.

The analyst consensus target of $44.00 (mean) implies ~19% upside, but the low target is approximately the current price at $37.00, and the median of $41.00 assumes operational improvement that remains undemonstrated. With 5 Buy / 5 Hold / 0 Sell, sentiment recently improved from 4 Buy / 5 Hold / 1 Sell — modest positive shift.

## Competitive Landscape

PRKS operates in a structurally attractive industry (high barriers, established demand) but occupies a challenging competitive position:

**Strengths:**
- Irreplaceable zoological collection creates genuine differentiation
- ~2,000 acres of owned real estate in premium tourism markets
- Florida location benefits from year-round demand
- Complementary positioning (nature/animals) vs. Disney/Universal (fantasy/IP)

**Weaknesses:**
- Lower-tier brand perception relative to Disney and Universal
- More price-sensitive customer base — first to be impacted by consumer weakness
- Epic Universe directly competes for Central Florida tourist spending and time
- Sesame Workshop litigation threatens key branded IP
- International visitation declining while competitors strengthen international marketing

The competitive trajectory is deteriorating. Epic Universe represents the most significant new competitive entry in PRKS's Florida market in over a decade. The company's response — new rides and attractions at maintenance-level capex — is insufficient to match the scale of new competition.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical Risks:**

1. **Leverage + Declining EBITDA = Covenant Risk.** Net leverage at 3.52x with $30M of pro-forma savings counted. If FY2026 EBITDA doesn't recover, covenant ratios approach thresholds. Interest expense at $134M/year is non-negotiable.

2. **Sesame Workshop Litigation.** Filed March 2026, seeking termination. Affects 2 standalone parks and themed areas in 3 others. Material to revenue and brand. No timeline for resolution disclosed.

3. **Management Instability.** Interim CFO. Multiple executive departures (6+ 8-K Item 5.02 filings in 2025-2026). CEO compensation jumped 4.5x (retention signal). Board explicitly "actively overseeing operations" — language suggesting loss of confidence.

4. **Controlling Shareholder Misalignment.** Hill Path's 53.2% position benefits disproportionately from per-share buybacks funded by debt. Minority shareholders bear asymmetric downside if fundamentals continue deteriorating and leverage becomes untenable.

5. **Consumer Environment.** Discretionary spending under pressure from inflationary headwinds, strong dollar reducing international visitation, and weakening consumer confidence. Theme park visits are directly impacted by these dynamics.

6. **Short Interest at 39.7%.** This level (8.1 days to cover) reflects deep institutional skepticism. While this creates theoretical squeeze risk, it also indicates sophisticated money believes the fundamental trajectory warrants significant negative positioning. Given 4 consecutive earnings misses and declining KPIs, the short thesis has fundamental backing.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing extreme near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 195.8% versus 50.8% historical volatility represents a massive premium. The term structure is in steep backwardation (195.8% near-term vs. 44.9% at 6 months), indicating the market expects an imminent stress event to resolve — likely related to post-earnings positioning after the May 11 report. The put/call OI ratio of 0.02 suggests minimal put protection currently outstanding (only 25 contracts across 4 expirations), making this a thinly-traded options market that provides limited informational content. The near-term IV is likely distorted by the small number of contracts and proximity to expiration.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Multiple dated catalysts exist: (1) Q2 2026 earnings (likely August 2026) — first full-quarter test of whether deferred revenue converts to attendance recovery or is merely a timing shift; (2) Sesame Workshop litigation proceedings — any preliminary ruling or settlement disclosure could materially impact valuation; (3) If H2 EBITDA disappoints, covenant leverage approaches 4.0x+ by year-end, potentially restricting further buybacks and triggering negative signaling.

**Timing window:** 3-6 months. The critical test is whether summer 2026 attendance reverses the 4-year decline. Q2 earnings (August) will provide definitive data. Sesame litigation timeline is less certain.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** 39.7% SI / 8.1 days to cover is extremely high. At this level, squeeze risk is material — any positive surprise (attendance recovery, litigation settlement) could trigger violent covering. However, the fundamental thesis supporting the short (declining operations, leverage, management instability) has strong empirical backing through 4 consecutive misses.

**Borrow and options signal:** Options market is too thin for meaningful signal (25 contracts). The extreme near-term IV reflects post-earnings positioning, not directional conviction. Cost to borrow is likely elevated given 39.7% SI.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case $22-28 represents 24-40% downside. Upside risk to $44-48 represents 19-30% upside on squeeze/recovery. Risk/reward at current entry is approximately 1.5:1 reward-to-risk for new shorts — below the 2:1 minimum threshold.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The fundamental thesis is sound (declining operations, distress-level leverage, litigation risk, management instability), but 39.7% short interest with 8.1 days to cover creates unacceptable squeeze risk for new entries. The stock has already declined ~35% from its 52-week high. Remaining downside to the bear case ($22-28) provides insufficient reward relative to squeeze potential. The short was correct at $50+; at $36.90 with this SI level, the asymmetry no longer favors new short positions.

## Conclusion

United Parks & Resorts presents a concentrated collection of risks without adequate compensation. The 6.6x EV/EBITDA appears superficially cheap but is justified by: declining revenue trajectory (4 years), declining EBITDA (17% from peak), distress-level leverage (Z-Score 1.05), material litigation risk (Sesame Workshop), management instability (interim CFO, multiple departures), controlling shareholder conducting debt-funded buybacks during operational deterioration, and an intensifying competitive environment (Epic Universe).

The single constructive datapoint — deferred revenue growth suggesting strong season pass sales — provides optionality for H2 2026 improvement. However, the last four quarters have each missed consensus by 20-94%, suggesting the market consistently overestimates this management team's ability to convert potential into results. Until demonstrated improvement materializes in reported numbers, the risk profile does not justify capital allocation. AVOID.
