**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Amgen has done the hard operational work of rotating its revenue base. Legacy franchises facing biosimilar cliffs (Prolia −34%, XGEVA −27%) and IRA Medicare price-setting (ENBREL −37% effective Jan 1, 2026) are eroding faster than the headline growth rate suggests, yet the company still delivered +4% product-sales growth and +6% total-revenue growth in Q1 2026 because newer launches — Repatha (+34%), EVENITY (+27%), TEPEZZA (+29%), UPLIZNA (>100%), IMDELLTRA (>100%), and the PAVBLU biosimilar ($276M US) — are scaling fast enough to offset the decline. The transition is functioning, and the March 2026 Sanofi/Regeneron settlement removed years of litigation overhang on Repatha just as it becomes the flagship product.

The problem is the price paid for it against the risks embedded in the structure. AMGN trades at roughly 26x trailing EPS and ~17x current-year consensus ($22.34), a premium to a Biotechnology peer group where Gilead sits at 17x and Regeneron at 15x. Estimate revisions are flat (current-year −0.1% over 90 days, next-year +0.6%), so there is no earnings inflection to justify the premium via cycle-adjusted math; trailing multiples are not misleading here on a trough-earnings basis. The premium instead reflects the market's confidence in the cardiometabolic pipeline (MariTide, olpasiran, Repatha label expansion), which is the least de-risked part of the story.

Two binary events dominate the next 12 months. First, a U.S. Tax Court decision on the IRS Puerto Rico transfer-pricing dispute (~$8.7B across 2010–2015, plus an April 2026 draft NOPA for 2016–2018 and audits opening for 2019–2022) is expected no earlier than 2H 2026; a loss would be a direct hit to both cash and Amgen's structurally low ~13% tax rate. Second, MariTide's Phase 3 MARITIME program will determine whether Amgen's distant-third obesity entry converts a monthly-dosing/adherence argument into share against Lilly and Novo, and Phase 2 GI-tolerability concerns leave that outcome uncertain. With the stock at a peer premium, ~$57B of debt against $9.2B of equity, and no open-market insider buying or buybacks in Q1, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate AMGN a HOLD.

## Company Overview

Amgen is one of the world's largest independent biotechnology companies, operating a single human-therapeutics segment across oncology/hematology, inflammation, bone health, cardiovascular/cardiometabolic, nephrology, and rare disease. FY revenue runs roughly $33B on a diversified product base. The company combines deep biologics manufacturing expertise with entrenched franchises in bone health and cardiovascular disease, a growing biosimilars unit (PAVBLU, AMJEVITA, MVASI, WEZLANA), and heavy R&D reinvestment (R&D +16% YoY to $1.72B in Q1 2026, roughly 20% of revenue).

Product mix as of Q1 2026 (quarterly sales, YoY):
- **Repatha** (PCSK9, cardiovascular): $876M, +34% — now the largest product, +35% volume
- **Prolia** (osteoporosis): $727M, −34% — RANKL patent expired Feb 2025 US
- **EVENITY**: $562M, +27%
- **TEPEZZA** (thyroid eye disease, ex-Horizon): $490M, +29%
- **Otezla**: $431M, −1% (IRA price-setting effective 2027)
- **BLINCYTO**: $415M, +12%
- **Nplate**: $412M, +32% (incl. $60M US government order)
- **XGEVA**: $411M, −27% (biosimilar erosion)
- **ENBREL**: $320M, −37% (IRA Medicare price-setting effective Jan 1, 2026)
- **UPLIZNA** ($262M) and **IMDELLTRA** ($258M): both >100% growth
- **PAVBLU** (aflibercept biosimilar): $276M US, up from $99M

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 versus Q1 2025:
- Total revenue: $8,618M, +6% (product sales $8,218M, +4%; other revenue $400M, +45%)
- Product sales driven by +9% volume, offset by −2% price and −2% inventory dynamics
- Operating income: $2,666M vs $1,178M (prior year burdened by an $800M Otezla impairment)
- Net income: $1,819M, +5%; diluted EPS $3.34, +4%
- Cost of sales fell to 31.8% of revenue from 36.4% on lower acquisition-related amortization
- Effective tax rate: 12.7%

Cash flow and balance sheet as of March 31, 2026:
- Operating cash flow: $2,189M, up from $1,391M
- Cash: $12.0B; total assets $92.5B
- Debt: current portion $5.4B + long-term $51.9B = ~$57.3B total, against $9.2B stockholders' equity — a legacy of the ~$28B Horizon acquisition
- $4.0B in new notes issued in February 2026 (4.20%–5.65%); capex guided to ~$2.6B for 2026 for US manufacturing
- Dividend: $2.52/quarter, +6% YoY; ~$6.8B buyback authorization remaining but no repurchases in Q1 2026

The earnings-quality picture is solid: operating cash flow well exceeds net income, gross margins are recovering, and interest expense declined to $657M from $723M as deleveraging progresses. The concern is the capital structure. A ~6x debt/equity ratio leaves little room for error, and the very low 12.7% effective tax rate — the primary driver of the earnings level the market is paying 26x for — rests on the same Puerto Rico structure the IRS is challenging. Financial health is adequate for a cash-generative franchise business, but the leverage and tax-rate dependency temper what would otherwise be a strong score. The absence of buybacks in Q1, despite ample authorization, signals management is prioritizing debt service rather than viewing the equity as cheap.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is a mix-rotation story. On a gross basis, roughly half the top ten products are in decline (Prolia, XGEVA, ENBREL, Aranesp, WEZLANA −97% US, Aimovig, MVASI), while Repatha, EVENITY, TEPEZZA, UPLIZNA, IMDELLTRA, and PAVBLU grow fast enough to net a +4% product-sales result. The mechanism is straightforward: biosimilar entry and IRA price-setting compress the legacy base, and newer volume-driven launches replace the lost dollars. Whether this remains a positive net over the next several years depends on pipeline execution, because the legacy erosion is contractual and predictable while the growth is contingent.

The three swing factors:

1. **Repatha label expansion.** Positive Vesalius-CV data (29% risk reduction in high-risk diabetics without prior CV events) has been submitted to regulators, with approval in a diabetes population expected in the near term. This directly supports Amgen's stated goal of becoming the global cardiometabolic-risk leader by 2030 and extends the runway on the current flagship.

2. **MariTide (maridebart cafraglutide).** The centerpiece of the offset strategy is an antibody-peptide GLP-1/GIP conjugate differentiated by monthly (or less frequent) dosing versus the weekly injectables from Lilly and Novo. Amgen's own analysis of 267,542 patients showed GLP-1 persistence falling to 49% at one year, framing infrequent dosing as an adherence edge. The broad Phase 3 MARITIME program spans obesity ±T2D plus cardiovascular, heart failure, and sleep-apnea comorbidities. The caveat is material: Phase 2 raised GI-tolerability concerns, now addressed with a 3-step, 8-week dose escalation, and no new efficacy data appeared at ADA 2026, which some investors read cautiously. Amgen is a distant third entrant into a market dominated by two incumbents, and the thesis rests on tolerability/dosing translating into real share.

3. **Olpasiran** (Lp(a) siRNA) rounds out the cardiometabolic platform.

Consensus reflects modest, non-inflecting growth: current-year EPS $22.34, next-year $23.46 (roughly +5%). Estimate revisions are flat across every window — current quarter +0.4% over 30 days but −2.2% over 90, current year essentially unchanged. This is a mature franchise with a credible but unproven growth pipeline, not a stock exhibiting the revision velocity that would flag an underappreciated inflection. The four-quarter beat streak (6–12% surprises) supports confidence in near-term consensus, but the beats have been declining in magnitude (11.6% → 10.3% → 9.6% → 6.1%), consistent with a maturing base.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$377, AMGN trades at ~26x trailing EPS ($14.23) and ~16.9x current-year consensus ($22.34). The Biotechnology peer median P/E is 17.1 (Gilead 17.1, Vertex 26.4, Regeneron 14.9), so Amgen carries a premium to the median on a group with faster-decelerating legacy exposure but also on a group without Amgen's specific pricing/regulatory overhangs. On forward earnings the gap narrows but does not disappear.

Cheapness is not a reliable buy signal in this environment, and AMGN is not cheap in the first place — it is a premium-multiple name where the premium is underwritten by pipeline optionality (MariTide, cardiometabolic leadership) that has not yet cleared its key readouts. There is no earnings-inflection argument to override the trailing multiple: revisions are flat, growth is mid-single-digit, and the tax rate that supports current EPS is itself the subject of a large pending liability.

The consensus mean target is $356.69 and the median $355.00 — below the current price of ~$377 — which signals that the sell-side does not view the stock as offering upside from here. This is the opposite of the "targets are floors" dynamic that applies to cheap, high-revision inflection names; here, the stock has run ahead of a stable consensus target on a flat-revision base. The target range is wide ($215 low to $427 high), reflecting the binary tax and pipeline outcomes. With the stock trading through the mean/median target on flat estimates, the valuation offers no asymmetric upside and carries identifiable downside catalysts. That combination warrants a below-average valuation score.

## Competitive Landscape

Amgen's competitive position is stable-to-improving on its core franchises and genuinely contested on its growth bet. The Repatha franchise strengthened materially with the March 2026 global settlement resolving PCSK9 litigation with Sanofi/Regeneron across Germany, the UPC, EPO, and Japan, removing a long-standing overhang just as Repatha becomes the largest product. TEPEZZA's subcutaneous Phase 3 (77% proptosis response, comparable to IV) protects the Horizon franchise. PAVBLU's surge to $276M US demonstrates the biosimilars unit can win share, though it is entangled in aggressive Regeneron patent/antitrust litigation (including Amgen Walker Process fraud counterclaims).

The defining competitive question is obesity. Lilly (tirzepatide) and Novo (semaglutide, plus an emerging oral field) dominate a market Amgen enters third. Amgen's differentiation — infrequent dosing addressing a documented 49%-at-one-year adherence problem — depends on the data, given Phase 2 tolerability concerns and the absence of fresh efficacy at ADA 2026. The prior report's competitive-position score of 8.0 (Feb) that stepped down to 6.0 (Jun) reflected the market's growing recognition that MariTide is a contested third-place entry rather than a certain winner. New data since then neither confirms nor reverses that reassessment, so we hold competitive position near the middle: strong incumbent franchises, a de-risked flagship, but a growth engine whose competitive outcome is unproven.

## Risk Assessment

**IRS transfer-pricing dispute (largest quantifiable risk).** The dispute seeks ~$3.6B additional tax for 2010–2012 and ~$5.1B plus ~$2.0B penalties for 2013–2015, net of repatriation credits. Because Amgen's effective tax rate is built on the very structure being challenged, an adverse ruling is a dual hit — a large cash outflow plus a potential structural increase in the go-forward tax rate. This is a binary catalyst in the next 6–12 months and is the primary reason risk factors score below 4.

**IRA / drug pricing (materializing now).** ENBREL's IRA Medicare price is driving its decline (−15% net price + higher 340B mix). Colorado's PDAB set an Upper Payment Limit on ENBREL below WAC (effective ≥Jan 2027); Washington also selected ENBREL. IRA price-setting scales toward ~100 drugs by 2031 — a worsening structural headwind, not a one-time event.

**Most-Favored-Nation pricing.** In December 2025 Amgen agreed to actions satisfying the Administration's July 2025 MFN Letter (extended MFN pricing, expanded direct-to-patient programs). The net financial impact is explicitly stated as uncertain — a live overhang.

**TAVNEOS regulatory action.** On Jan 16, 2026 the FDA requested voluntary withdrawal; Amgen refused (Jan 28). Following a March 31 safety communication on fatal liver injury/VBDS cases, CDER formally proposed to withdraw approval on Apr 27–30, 2026, alleging the original ChemoCentryx application contained untrue statements of material facts. The product is small, but the credibility/reputational dimension and parallel securities litigation are unresolved.

**Leverage.** ~$57B debt against $9.2B equity constrains flexibility and amplifies the equity's sensitivity to any pipeline or tax setback.

**Biosimilar erosion.** Management explicitly guides to accelerated erosion of Prolia and XGEVA through 2026.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 64.0% sits far above 1-year historical volatility of 26.8% (a 37.2pp spread, 88th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in steep backwardation (64.0% near-term versus 33.3% at six months), signaling the market is pricing an imminent event. The put/call volume ratio of 1.40 and a 5.5% put skew indicate elevated near-term hedging demand. The most-likely explanation is the upcoming earnings report; the 1-week implied move is only ±1.1%, but the 1-month and 3-month tenors price 6.2% and 8.8% moves respectively, consistent with hedging around the earnings-plus-guidance window and the broader tax/pipeline catalysts. For a fundamental holder, this argues against adding at current levels ahead of the event, and richer IV means any long-premium hedge is expensive here.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$427, +13%).** MariTide Phase 3 demonstrates that monthly dosing plus improved tolerability translates into meaningful obesity share, validating the cardiometabolic-by-2030 ambition. Repatha's diabetes label expansion extends the flagship runway, olpasiran adds a differentiated Lp(a) asset, and the growth franchises comfortably outpace legacy erosion. The Tax Court rules favorably or settles at a manageable figure, preserving the low tax rate. Deleveraging continues, and the multiple holds on a re-accelerating growth profile.

**Bear Case (~$215–270, −28% to −43%).** The Tax Court rules against Amgen, forcing a large cash payment and lifting the structural tax rate, compounded by the expanding 2016–2022 exposure. MariTide disappoints on efficacy or tolerability, removing the growth narrative that supports the premium multiple. IRA/MFN pricing pressure accelerates faster than expected across the base. With ~$57B of debt, a simultaneous growth-narrative and tax hit compresses the multiple toward the peer median (17x), which on a lower go-forward EPS implies material downside — the $215 analyst-low target captures this scenario.

**Base Case (~$355, roughly flat).** Mix rotation continues to deliver mid-single-digit revenue growth, Repatha's label expansion proceeds, the tax dispute remains unresolved into 2H 2026 keeping a discount on the stock, and MariTide advances without a decisive readout. The ~2.75% dividend provides the bulk of near-term return. The stock oscillates around the consensus mean/median target of $355–357.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (near-term, given the options-implied event); the U.S. Tax Court decision expected no earlier than 2H 2026; and MariTide MARITIME Phase 3 readouts as they emerge.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A favorable or manageably-settled Tax Court outcome that de-risks the tax rate, combined with the stock trading at or below the mid-$320s (a discount to consensus target rather than a premium)
- Positive MariTide Phase 3 efficacy/tolerability data that credibly establishes obesity share, converting pipeline optionality into a durable growth stream
- A turn in EPS estimate revisions to consistently positive (+5%+ over 90 days) reflecting the growth franchises re-accelerating faster than legacy erosion

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- An adverse Tax Court ruling coupled with confirmation that the go-forward tax rate structurally rises, or the 2016–2022 exposure materially expands
- MariTide Phase 3 failure or a tolerability profile that precludes competitive obesity share
- Product-sales growth turning negative as legacy erosion outpaces new launches, or an ENBREL/Otezla-style IRA hit spreading to a growth franchise

## Conclusion

Amgen is executing a competent revenue-mix rotation, has removed the Repatha litigation overhang at the right moment, and generates strong operating cash flow with a ~2.75% dividend. These support ownership. But at a premium to the Biotechnology peer median, trading through a flat consensus target of $355, and carrying two large binary catalysts (a Tax Court ruling on ~$8.7B+ of transfer-pricing exposure and an unproven MariTide obesity bet) against ~$57B of debt, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. The declining score trajectory (6.9 → 5.8 → 5.6) reflects the market's gradual recognition that the growth premium rests on pipeline outcomes that remain uncertain, and the current data neither reverses nor deepens that view materially. There is no compelling reason to add here and no fundamental deterioration that would force a sale for a long-term holder. We rate AMGN a HOLD, classified as uncertain: the two dominant catalysts resolve over the next 6–12 months, and the appropriate posture is to hold existing positions and wait for either the tax ruling or MariTide data, ideally re-engaging on a pullback toward the mid-$320s where the risk/reward would turn constructive.
