## Executive Summary

Murphy Oil is a mid-cap E&P company with a diversified asset base spanning the Gulf of America deepwater, Eagle Ford shale, Canadian natural gas, and high-impact international exploration (Vietnam, Côte d'Ivoire, Morocco). The stock has appreciated 80% over the past year, driven primarily by the rise in global oil prices, which has transformed Murphy's unhedged, oil-weighted portfolio from a liability into an earnings multiplier. Q1 2026 results demonstrated this leverage clearly: revenue rose 10.2% YoY with an EPS beat of 10.2%.

However, several structural concerns prevent a more constructive stance. Adjusted free cash flow collapsed to $12M in 2025 as capex surged to $1.22B while operating cash flow declined. GoA production fell 22% from 2023 to 2025, and DD&A per BOE is rising — both signals of asset depletion that elevated oil prices partially mask but do not resolve. The company is fully unhedged on oil, meaning any normalization toward strip pricing ($62-65 WTI by 2027) would compress earnings materially. At ~11x forward consensus EPS of $3.45, MUR carries a premium to peer median (6.7x P/E) that is partially justified by exploration optionality but also reflects the current elevated oil environment rather than sustainable through-cycle economics.

The appropriate stance is HOLD. At current prices, the risk/reward is balanced: upside exists if oil remains elevated and Vietnam/Morocco exploration delivers, but downside is meaningful if commodity prices normalize toward the forward curve and FCF remains near zero.

## Company Overview

Murphy Oil Corporation is a pure-play E&P company founded in 1950, headquartered in Houston, Texas. The company operates across four primary geographies:

- **U.S. Offshore (Gulf of America):** ~57K bbl/d of high-margin oil production across deepwater fields. 556K gross acres.
- **U.S. Onshore (Eagle Ford):** ~130K gross acres producing approximately 35% of U.S. volumes.
- **Canada Onshore (Tupper Montney/Kaybob Duvernay):** Primarily natural gas; 2.3 TCF proved reserves.
- **International Exploration:** Vietnam (7.3M gross acres, Lac Da Vang development with 2026 first oil target), Côte d'Ivoire (5 blocks), Brazil (960K acres), Morocco (4M acres acquired January 2026).

Total production in 2025 was 189K BOEPD (~55% liquids, ~45% gas on BOE basis). 2026 guidance is 173-181K BOEPD, reflecting continued GoA natural decline partially offset by Eagle Ford and Vietnam development.

## Growth Analysis

**Production growth is stagnating.** 2026 guidance of 173-181K BOEPD is flat-to-slightly-below 2025's 182K. GoA production declined 22% (73K→57K bbl/d) from 2023 to 2025 due to natural decline, subsurface issues, and impairments ($178M in write-downs over two years). Eagle Ford is mature. Canada gas is stable but low-value at current AECO pricing.

**Exploration offers genuine optionality:**
- **Vietnam (Lac Da Vang):** First oil targeted 2026. Hai Su Vang appraisal encountered 429 feet of net pay from two reservoirs. Successful development could add 15-25K BOEPD by 2028-2029.
- **GoA new discoveries:** Cello #1 and Banjo #1 (Mississippi Canyon 385) — potential new production hub.
- **Morocco:** 75% operated position in 4M+ acres; early-stage but significant acreage position.
- **Côte d'Ivoire:** Mixed results — Bubale discovery encouraging, but Civette and Caracal were dry holes.

The $320M 2026 exploration/appraisal budget represents ~25% of total capex — an unusually high allocation for a mid-cap E&P, reflecting management's conviction in exploration-driven growth. The outcomes are inherently binary.

**Estimate revisions show a complex picture.** 90-day revisions are strongly positive (+220-342%), reflecting the oil price surge from trough levels. However, 30-day revisions are negative (-9% to -13%), indicating the most recent commodity price volatility is already causing downward EPS adjustments. This divergence is characteristic of a company at peak cyclical sensitivity — rising from trough but now facing normalization pressure.

## Valuation Assessment

**Current metrics:**
- Trailing P/E: 64.5x (distorted by $0.72 GAAP EPS, which includes impairments)
- Forward P/E: ~11x on $3.45 FY2026 consensus
- Dividend yield: 3.6%
- Market cap: $5.4B
- EV/EBITDA (trailing): ~4.5-5.0x on 2025 adjusted EBITDA of $1.36B

**Peer comparison:** Peer median P/E is 6.7x (CNX at 4.9x, CRK at 6.7x, MGY at 17x). MUR at 11x forward represents a 64% premium to the median. This premium reflects: (a) oil-weighted production benefiting more from elevated prices, (b) exploration optionality that pure-play domestic peers lack, and (c) LNG/Canadian gas exposure.

**Premium justification:** Partially warranted. Vietnam first oil in 2026 and the Hai Su Vang discovery represent genuine incremental value that onshore-focused peers do not have. However, at current oil prices, MUR's forward earnings assume sustained elevated pricing. If oil normalizes toward lower levels, the forward P/E expands meaningfully — expensive relative to peers with more predictable production profiles.

**Analyst consensus:** Mean target $43.29, median $42.00, low $36.00, high $70.00. At $37.88, the stock sits 11% below median target, suggesting modest near-term upside if consensus is correct. The low target of $36 is only 5% below current price — even the most bearish analyst does not see material downside from here.

## Competitive Landscape

Murphy occupies a niche position among mid-cap E&Ps: an offshore-weighted company with active international exploration. Most domestic peers (MGY, CNX, CRK, CRGY) are onshore-focused with more predictable decline curves. Murphy's differentiation cuts both ways:

**Advantages:**
- Deepwater GoA production carries higher per-barrel margins than onshore peers
- Exploration program offers non-linear upside (Vietnam could be transformational)
- Canadian gas position provides LNG export optionality as Canadian LNG infrastructure comes online
- FPSO acquisition improves GoA operating cost control
- Regulatory tailwinds: OBBBA mandated 30+ GoA lease sales through 2040

**Disadvantages:**
- 813 employees is a lean organization but limits operational bandwidth across multiple geographies
- GoA production is declining, requiring continuous capital investment to offset
- Scale disadvantage vs. larger peers (DVN, FANG, COP) limits cost efficiencies
- Exploration spend creates earnings volatility from dry holes and impairments
- International operations carry sovereign risk (Vietnam, Côte d'Ivoire, Morocco)

The competitive position is stable but not improving on the production side. Only exploration success can offset the GoA decline over the medium term.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Commodity price exposure (CRITICAL):** Murphy is essentially unhedged on oil. Forward curve WTI at $62-65 for 2027 implies normalized earnings well below current consensus. If oil prices retreat significantly from current levels, EPS likely compresses materially from the current $3.45 estimate.

2. **GoA production decline:** 22% decline over 2 years with repeated impairments. Reservoir complexity in deepwater fields is proving more challenging than modeled. DD&A rising from $12.12/BOE (2023) to $14.06/BOE (2025) quantifies this erosion.

3. **Near-zero adjusted FCF at current spend levels:** At $1.2-1.3B guided capex plus $288M in shareholder returns (2025 level), Murphy has essentially zero margin for error. Any production shortfall or price decline forces either capex cuts (slowing exploration) or balance sheet deterioration.

4. **Exploration binary outcomes:** $320M in 2026 exploration/appraisal is ~25% of capex. Côte d'Ivoire has already produced two dry holes. Vietnam carries execution risk in a developing-market regulatory environment. Morocco is years from any cash flow.

5. **Geopolitical concentration:** The company's international exploration is concentrated in jurisdictions with non-trivial sovereign risk (Vietnam, Côte d'Ivoire, Morocco).

**Mitigating Factors:**
- Debt refinancing completed; liquidity of $1.6B with near-term maturities addressed
- Elevated oil prices provide substantial near-term buffer
- Regulatory tailwinds for GoA activity under current U.S. administration
- 4 consecutive earnings beats demonstrate operational competence

## Conclusion

Murphy Oil at $37.88 is a fairly valued mid-cap E&P that offers above-peer exploration optionality but carries elevated commodity risk and deteriorating production economics on its core GoA asset base. The stock's 80% appreciation over the past year directly reflects the surge in global oil prices from which Murphy benefits disproportionately due to its unhedged, oil-weighted portfolio.

At ~11x forward EPS, the valuation carries a 64% premium to peer median — partially justified by Vietnam/Morocco exploration upside but partially a commodity price artifact. The 30-day estimate revisions turning negative (-13%) while 90-day revisions remain strongly positive is the signature of a stock at peak commodity sensitivity: having recovered from trough but now facing normalization pressure.

The prior AVOID rating was overly bearish given: (1) the stock already trades near the lowest analyst target ($36), (2) forward valuation at 11x is not extreme for an E&P with genuine exploration optionality, (3) debt has been refinanced with ample liquidity, and (4) 4 consecutive earnings beats demonstrate operational competence in the current environment. Equally, BUY is not warranted given: (1) near-zero adjusted FCF, (2) declining GoA production, (3) negative recent estimate revisions, (4) full commodity exposure with no hedging, and (5) absence of insider buying.

HOLD is the appropriate recommendation. The position is defensible for existing holders collecting a 3.6% yield with exploration optionality, but insufficient asymmetry exists to commit new capital absent either a price pullback or a positive exploration catalyst.
