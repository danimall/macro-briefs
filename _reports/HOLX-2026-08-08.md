**Research Date: August 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hologic is no longer a publicly traded company. The merger with funds managed by Blackstone Inc. and TPG Inc. closed on or about **April 7, 2026**, and the common stock ceased trading and was delisted from Nasdaq on **April 8, 2026**. The filing record supports this sequence directly: a definitive merger proxy (DEFM14A) filed December 23, 2025, a Part III–only 10-K/A filed January 22, 2026 whose explanatory note states the company did not intend to file an annual-meeting proxy within 120 days of its September 27, 2025 fiscal year end, and 8-Ks dated April 6 and April 7, 2026. Three independent secondary sources report completion at an enterprise value of approximately **$18.3 billion**. The pricing feed used for this report returns no authoritative quote for HOLX and flags a missing market price, missing market timestamp and undeterminable currency, consistent with a deregistered security.

The practical consequence is that the investment question has changed shape. There is no equity to buy, sell, or short, and no forward participation in Hologic's cash flows for public investors. What remains is: (a) the residual claim of former holders, which appears to comprise fixed cash consideration of **$76.00 per share** plus a contingent component taking total consideration to "up to $79 per share" — roughly **$3.00/share, or about $683M on 227.573M diluted shares (3.9% of the $17.3bn cash equity value)** — whose existence, milestones, measurement dates and transferability I could not confirm from any filing; and (b) the read-through from the transaction and the sponsors' post-close behavior to how private capital is valuing low-growth, high-cash-conversion healthcare assets.

This is the fifth consecutive assessment of HOLX and the fourth consecutive AVOID (score path 5.8 → 5.6 → 5.0 → 4.8 → 4.2). The prior AVOIDs were about owning a fully valued, low-growth equity into a pending merger; this one is about the absence of a security. The score moves to **3.5** for that reason, not because the operating business deteriorated further. FY2025 produced $4,100.5M of revenue, $714.3M of operating income and $1,057.1M of operating cash flow. The name should be removed from the active coverage universe once deregistration is confirmed.

## Company Overview

Hologic is a Marlborough, Massachusetts medical technology company focused on women's health. Three distinguishable franchises are evidenced in the material available: molecular and cytology diagnostics (the source of the pandemic-era COVID assay business, expanded through tuck-in acquisitions of Biotheranostics, Diagenode and Mobidiag per external reporting); breast health imaging, a capital-equipment franchise with documented electronic-component exposure that was disrupted by the global semiconductor shortage; and a surgical unit, which Reuters, relaying the Financial Times on July 9, 2026, reports the new owners are marketing for more than $4 billion.

Item 1 (Business), Item 1A (Risk Factors) and Item 7 (MD&A) of the FY2025 10-K were not retrievable in this session; the retrieval tool resolved "most recent 10-K" to the Part III–only 10-K/A. Product-level and segment-level detail is therefore not filing-sourced here, and no segment revenue, growth or margin data was obtained. This is a genuine coverage gap and it constrains several inferences below.

What is verifiable from audited XBRL data is the financial shape of the business: **$4,100.5M FY2025 revenue, 51.6% gross margin, 17.4% operating margin, $9,014.9M total assets, $5,047.9M stockholders' equity, and goodwill plus acquired intangibles of $4,229.7M (46.9% of assets)**. The margin structure combined with low reported capital intensity is consistent with an installed-base/consumables model rather than a commodity device model. The intangible weight indicates the portfolio was assembled substantially by acquisition; the FY2025 data alone cannot distinguish franchise strength from acquisition accumulation.

Leadership changed at closing. Stephen MacMillan, CEO since 2013, retired. **José E. Almeida**, formerly chairman, president and CEO of Baxter International until February 2025, was named CEO on or about April 7, 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue: a plateau after the COVID cliff.**

| FY | Revenue ($M) | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 4,862.8 | — |
| 2023 | 4,030.4 | −17.1% |
| 2024 | 4,030.3 | −0.002% |
| 2025 | 4,100.5 | +1.74% |

The FY2022→FY2023 fall is the COVID assay unwind. The more consequential fact is the two subsequent years: essentially flat, then +1.74%. The FY2023→FY2025 CAGR is **0.87%** ((4,100.5/4,030.4)^(1/2) − 1). The company's own selected incentive metric corroborates the trajectory: the FY2024 proxy discloses Adjusted Revenue of $5,523M (FY2021), $4,915M (FY2022), $4,034M (FY2023), $4,030M (FY2024), a 27% four-year decline. Goodwill rose $197.5M during FY2025, so some part of the $70.2M revenue increase was probably acquisitive, implying organic growth near zero. That is an inference; without segment data or MD&A it cannot be established.

**Margins: a 363bp gross-margin decline whose cause is unresolved.**

| FY | Gross | Operating | Net |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 61.6% | 33.7% | 26.8% |
| 2023 | 51.4% | 16.6% | 11.3% |
| 2024 | 55.3% | 21.9% | 19.6% |
| 2025 | 51.6% | 17.4% | 13.8% |

Gross profit dollars **fell $109.9M** ($2,227.8M → $2,117.9M) while revenue rose $70.2M, implying total cost of revenue rose $180.1M. Only $39.9M of that appears in the standardized cost-of-revenue tag; roughly **$140M sits in components the tag does not capture**. The balance-sheet movement is consistent with an amortization step-up: acquired intangibles fell $255.5M ($844.6M → $589.1M) while goodwill rose $197.5M, the pattern produced when new deals add goodwill while a larger existing intangible stock runs off through the P&L. Operating cash flow of $1,057.1M at **1.87× net income of $565.7M** points the same way.

The competing explanation — real cash cost inflation from tariffs, freight, components, or a mix shift toward lower-margin capital equipment — would produce identical reported optics, and strong operating cash flow could independently reflect favorable working-capital timing. Resolving this requires the FY2025 MD&A and cost-of-revenue footnote, neither of which was retrievable. It is the single highest-value unresolved item, because a non-cash explanation is largely benign for value while a cash-cost explanation would signal franchise erosion.

**Operating income bridge FY2024→FY2025:** operating income fell $168.3M (882.6 → 714.3): gross profit −$109.9M plus operating expenses +$58.4M (1,345.2 → 1,403.6, +4.3%). The standardized SG&A tag ($449.3M, 11.0% of revenue) is implausibly low for a medtech with a direct hospital sales force; reconciling total opex against R&D ($248.2M) plus that tag leaves $706.1M unallocated, a consistent $663–716M gap in every year FY2022–FY2025. The tag almost certainly captures G&A only. No SG&A trend conclusions are drawn.

**R&D: three consecutive annual declines.** $294.3M (FY2023) → $272.8M (FY2024) → $248.2M (FY2025); intensity 7.30% → 6.77% → 6.05%. This is a clean tag with no reconciliation ambiguity: down 9.0% year over year and 15.7% from peak. For a company whose stated moat was continuous new product release, this is the most durable negative signal in the dataset. Post-COVID program rationalization, reclassification into cost of revenue, or capitalized software are alternative explanations; management's own explanation was not read, and I do not attribute the cuts to sale preparation.

**Cash generation: genuinely strong, imprecisely measured.** FY2025 OCF $1,057.1M; reported capex $58.8M implies FCF of $998.3M (24.4% of revenue). But capex of $58.8M against depreciation of $108.9M and a *rising* net PP&E balance ($481.6M FY2022 → $559.6M FY2025) is internally inconsistent, and the tool returned no capex at all for FY2022–FY2024. The tag likely excludes diagnostic instruments placed with customers and/or capitalized software. Treat $998.3M as an upper bound; a depreciation-proxy construction gives roughly **$900M**. Either figure is a high-quality result.

**Balance sheet: under-levered before the LBO.** Cash $1,959.5M against total debt $2,508.5M gives **net debt of $549.0M**. Current ratio 3.75× (3,668.1/977.2); debt/equity 0.50×; ROE 11.1% (565.7/avg equity 5,089.0); ROA 6.2%. Interest expense of $117.1M was offset by roughly $84.0M of derived other income (operating income $714.3M − interest $117.1M = $597.2M versus reported pretax $681.2M), so **net interest cost was only about $33M**; average cost of debt 4.67%. Tangible book equity is roughly $818.2M against $3,967.0M of total liabilities.

Computed screens on the pre-deal structure: **Altman Z ≈ 4.22**, above the 2.99 safe threshold, but $2.62 of that comes from the market-equity/liabilities term; substituting book equity drops Z to 2.37, the grey zone. **Piotroski F = 6/9**, with failures on ROA direction, long-term debt/assets, and gross margin. The ROA-decline signal survives normalization: at a constant FY2025 tax rate, FY2024 net income of $718.4M implies ROA of 7.85%, still above FY2025's 6.23%.

**Tax normalization matters for reading FY2025.** Effective rates: 18.02% (FY2022), 32.55% (FY2023), 8.74% (FY2024), 16.96% (FY2025). Reported net income fell $223.8M (−28.3%), but pretax fell only $183.9M. Normalizing FY2024 to 16.96% gives $718.4M, making the like-for-like decline $152.7M (−21.3%). About **$71.1M — roughly 32% — of the headline decline is tax-rate normalization**, not operating deterioration. The cause of the FY2024 8.74% rate is unverified.

**Capital allocation was buybacks only.** Retained earnings rose exactly $565.7M, matching net income and confirming zero dividends. Equity nonetheless fell $82.1M, implying roughly **$648M** of repurchase-driven reduction net of stock compensation and OCI; basic shares fell 9.567M, which at an assumed ~$68 average price implies ~$650M. Two independent derivations agreeing within $2M gives high confidence in the magnitude (±$75M for the price assumption). Diluted shares fell 10.35% since FY2022.

**Earnings delivery was unremarkable into the deal.** The last four reported quarters were $1.03 vs $1.04 estimate (−0.6%), $1.08 vs $1.07 (+0.8%), $1.13 vs $1.12 (+0.7%), and $1.04 vs $1.11 (−6.4%) — two beats, two misses, with the largest miss last. Nothing in that record supports an argument that consensus was materially understating earnings power at the time of the transaction.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case that management articulated in 2023 — that internal R&D plus tuck-in acquisitions would leave the company "geared for growth" at a structurally higher baseline after COVID revenue receded — is not validated by the subsequent three years of reported revenue: $4,030.4M, $4,030.3M, $4,100.5M. Diversification changed the revenue mix; the evidence that it changed the growth rate is absent.

Three forward considerations, all of which now sit inside a private capital structure:

1. **Base rate.** A 0.87% two-year CAGR with likely-negative organic growth in FY2025 is a low-single-digit asset at best. Medtech multiples bifurcate sharply around mid-single-digit growth.
2. **Product cycle.** R&D down 15.7% from peak and intensity down 125bp constrains the 2027–2030 pipeline. In diagnostics and imaging, underinvestment shows up in share three to five years later.
3. **Portfolio surgery.** If the FT report is accurate, the surgical unit is being marketed within three months of close. A >$4B ask on a business that is plausibly a low-double-digit percentage of revenue implies a multiple far above the 4.35× EV/revenue paid for the whole company. Two readings are live and non-exclusive: the sponsors identified a sum-of-the-parts gap, or the LBO capital structure requires asset sales to service debt. The reported "pay down debt and return cash" framing supports the second at least partially. Segment revenue was not obtained, so the implied multiple is illustrative only, and the report is single-source and unconfirmed.

None of this is accessible to public investors. Growth prospects score 3.5 on the fundamentals and on the absence of any mechanism to participate.

## Valuation Assessment

The canonical pricing basis for this report returns **no valid price**: `schwab_quote_unavailable`, `missing_authoritative_price`, `missing_market_timestamp`, `currency_not_determinable_from_us_exchange`. Consequently there is no current price against which to compute margin of safety, scenario returns, or upside/downside. No analyst price targets are available, and the recommendation feed is empty. Setting analyst target fields to null is the honest treatment; the $76 and "up to $79" figures are merger consideration reported by secondary sources, not analyst targets, and should not be presented as such.

What can be reconstructed is the valuation the transaction implied. Using FY2025 actuals and 227.573M diluted shares:

| Metric | At $76.00 | At $79.00 |
|---|---|---|
| Equity value | $17,295.5M | $17,978.3M |
| + Net debt $549.0M | $17,844.5M | $18,527.3M |
| EV/FY2025 revenue | **4.35×** | 4.52× |
| P/E on GAAP EPS $2.486 | **30.6×** | 31.8× |
| EV/derived FCF ($998.3M) | 17.9× | 18.6× |
| EV/estimated EBITDA (~$1,200M)* | ~14.9× | ~15.4× |

\*EBITDA estimated as operating income $714.3M + depreciation $108.9M + roughly $360M of intangible amortization inferred from the gross-profit reconciliation. Wide error band; not a disclosed figure.

The reported ~$18.3bn enterprise value falls between the $76 and $79 reconstructions, a useful internal consistency check across the headline number, the per-share figures, the audited share count and net debt. The 30.6× GAAP P/E overstates richness because GAAP earnings absorb heavy acquisition amortization; Hologic's non-GAAP EPS was materially higher, which would imply a high-teens multiple, but non-GAAP EPS was not verified here, so only the GAAP figure is presented.

The residual valuation question for former holders is the contingent component. The gap between the $76 announced cash price and "up to $79 per share" implies roughly $3.00/share, about $683M or 3.9% of cash equity value. That is the entire remaining claim, and **its existence, ceiling, milestone conditions, measurement dates and transferability are unverified**. The July 2026 surgical-unit process is a plausible trigger given how frequently sponsor-deal CVRs are tied to divestiture proceeds, but the $76-vs-$79 gap could alternatively reflect inconsistent EV/equity conventions between two articles or an earn-out payable to management rather than public holders. The DEFM14A filed December 23, 2025 resolves it.

A related unresolved item bears on whether the price was fair. On the October 21, 2025 announcement date the shares rose 2.9% to $73.98, implying a prior close near $71.90 and a **5.7% premium** to the $76 price — far below the 20–35% typical of large-cap LBOs. The most likely explanation is that a process was already known and the shares had re-rated (an 8-K was filed September 19, 2025); at a hypothetical unaffected price of $60 the premium would be 26.7%. Some of the residual gap may also reflect market discounting of the contingent component and closing risk. The unaffected price is unverified, so no premium figure should be treated as established. Insider transaction data around the announcement was not obtainable, which is the evidence needed given a thin apparent premium.

Valuation score 2.0: there is no instrument offering asymmetric upside, the consideration is fixed cash plus an unverified contingent claim, and forward participation is zero.

## Competitive Landscape

The transaction itself is the clearest available statement about competitive quality. Sponsors in 2025 were underwriting low-growth, high-cash-conversion, recurring-revenue healthcare assets at mid-teens estimated EBITDA multiples, in a year with more than $700bn of announced private-equity buyouts. Hologic fit that template: an installed base generating consumable and assay revenue, roughly 22–24% FCF margin, 1–2% revenue growth, and net debt of $549M against roughly $1.2bn of estimated EBITDA. The unused balance-sheet capacity was itself part of the thesis; the company was under-levered for a private owner.

Against that, the operating evidence on trajectory is soft rather than strong: flat revenue, a 363bp gross-margin decline of unresolved cause, three years of R&D reduction, and documented capital-equipment and component exposure in breast health. Cross-segment offset during the semiconductor shortage establishes that the portfolio absorbs supply shocks in one franchise, which is a real benefit; it does not establish reduced customer, payer or end-market concentration, and no customer-concentration data was obtained.

The read-through that remains actionable for a public-market portfolio is a screening one rather than a Hologic one: private capital is paying roughly 4.3–4.5× revenue and mid-teens estimated EBITDA for sub-2%-growth diagnostics and imaging assets with high cash conversion and low leverage. Listed women's-health, diagnostics and imaging mid-caps with similar characteristics — recurring consumables, under-levered balance sheets, depressed organic growth — carry an elevated take-out probability on that comparable, which is a distinct mechanism from the patent-cliff-driven pharma bid.

Competitive position scores 4.8: the franchise is real and defensible in structure, but the trajectory markers (organic growth near zero, R&D contraction, planned carve-out of a franchise) point sideways to down.

## Risk Assessment

**1. No investable instrument.** The dominant risk is definitional: there is no HOLX common equity, no authoritative quote, and no path to participate in outcomes. Any market data describing HOLX as a live listed security is stale.

**2. The residual claim is unverified and likely illiquid.** If a contingent value right exists, it is probably non-transferable, with payoff dependent on undisclosed milestones. Former holders cannot size or hedge an instrument whose terms they cannot read. This is the primary driver of the 2.5 risk score.

**3. Information blackout.** Q1 FY2026 results (10-Q filed January 29, 2026) were not retrievable, no earnings transcript exists, and no guidance will be published going forward. Item 1A of the FY2025 10-K was not read, so no claim is made about added or modified risk factors — a real coverage gap for any residual claim analysis.

**4. Post-LBO leverage (borne by creditors and sponsors, not public equity).** As a labeled sensitivity, not a forecast: if the $18.3bn transaction was funded 50–60% with debt, total debt would be roughly $9–11bn against ~$1.2bn estimated EBITDA, i.e. 7.5–9× leverage, with cash interest of roughly $650–850M annually at a 7–8% blended cost. Compare that with the pre-deal position of $549M net debt and ~$33M net interest. **The actual debt quantum, pricing, maturities and covenants are entirely unverified.** The pre-deal Altman Z of 4.22 describes a capital structure that no longer exists.

**5. Goodwill concentration.** Goodwill of $3,640.6M is 40.4% of assets and 72.1% of pre-deal equity, against roughly $818M of tangible book equity and $3,967.0M of total liabilities. Impairment at any acquired franchise would consume book equity quickly. This risk now sits inside a private structure with purchase accounting reset, but it informed the pre-deal quality assessment.

**6. Governance items not obtainable.** Insider activity around the announcement, change-in-control and golden-parachute payments, related-party transactions and board composition were not retrievable. Each is relevant to whether the board negotiated at arm's length, particularly given the thin apparent one-day premium.

**7. Margin cause unresolved.** The ~$140M unexplained cost-of-revenue increase remains the pivotal open question about underlying earnings power.

No options data was provided and none would be expected for a delisted security, so no options signal subsection is included.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (for the residual claim, not for equity ownership).** Former holders received $76.00 per share in cash — a defensible outcome at 4.35× FY2025 revenue and roughly 30.6× GAAP EPS for a business growing under 1% — and may hold a contingent claim worth up to a further $3.00 per share, roughly $683M in aggregate. If the surgical-unit process closes above $4bn and a CVR is in fact tied to divestiture proceeds, that contingent value converts. Under this reading the sale captured a full price for a low-growth asset and the residual claim adds a small, cost-free option. Supporting evidence: the pre-deal balance sheet was strong ($549M net debt, 3.75× current ratio, ~$900M–1.0bn FCF), capital allocation had been disciplined (~$650M of repurchases, 10.35% diluted share reduction since FY2022), and Hologic outperformed its proxy peer index in three of four disclosed years, ending FY2024 at TSR 125.59 versus the peer group's 96.16.

**Bear case.** The 5.7% one-day premium to a possibly already-elevated price suggests the process may have been substantially telegraphed before the formal announcement, with the board realizing less for holders than a clean, competitive auction might have produced; the evidence needed to test that (DEFM14A background section, financial-advisor opinion, pre-announcement insider trades) is unavailable. The contingent component may not exist as a public-holder claim at all, in which case total realized consideration was $76.00 and the "up to $79" framing is a reporting artifact or a management earn-out. Underlying business quality was weakening at the point of sale: organic growth near zero, R&D down 15.7% from peak, and a 363bp gross-margin decline whose cause could not be identified. The sponsors' apparent decision to market a franchise within three months of close, explicitly to pay down debt, is more consistent with a planned break-up under leverage pressure than with opportunistic value harvesting.

**Resolution.** Both cases hinge on documents that were not obtainable. In that state, the correct portfolio action is not to hold a position in an instrument whose terms are unknown and whose liquidity is absent.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Position action.** No public-market position is possible or appropriate. Former holders who received cash consideration have no ongoing exposure; those holding any contingent instrument should treat its value as unknown pending disclosure of its terms.

**Re-entry criteria — what would change the view.** The name becomes investable again only through one of three specific developments:

1. **A verified, transferable contingent instrument.** If the DEFM14A (filed December 23, 2025) or a subsequent disclosure establishes a CVR with a stated ceiling, defined milestones, measurement dates and transferability, and a market develops in it, the instrument could be underwritten against the surgical-unit process. At a $3.00 ceiling and 227.573M shares, the maximum aggregate payoff is roughly $683M; any purchase price would need to sit at a large discount to a probability-weighted payoff given zero disclosure obligations from a private issuer.
2. **A re-IPO or public debt issuance.** A future Hologic IPO or SEC-registered bond offering would restore disclosure and create an analyzable instrument. On the pre-deal base — roughly $900M–1.0bn of FCF, ~$1.2bn estimated EBITDA, 4.35× EV/revenue paid — a re-IPO would need to demonstrate that organic growth had inflected above 3% and that the gross-margin decline was non-cash before any premium to the take-private multiple would be justified.
3. **Confirmation the contingent component does not exist**, which would close the file entirely.

**Timeline.** Sponsors typically hold three to seven years. The surgical-unit process reported July 9, 2026 is the nearest identifiable event, though it is single-source and unconfirmed, with no company or sponsor acknowledgment located. There is no scheduled public disclosure that would resolve any of the open items.

**Administrative action.** Remove HOLX from the active coverage universe once Form 15 deregistration is confirmed. Retain the transaction comparables — 4.35× EV/revenue, roughly 14.9× estimated EBITDA, 30.6× GAAP EPS for a sub-2% grower with high cash conversion and low leverage — as a screening input for listed diagnostics and imaging mid-caps with similar characteristics.

**Downgrade/upgrade thresholds.** Not applicable in the conventional sense; the rating is a function of instrument availability. It would move to HOLD only on the emergence of a tradable, disclosed residual instrument at an identifiable price.

## Conclusion

**Recommendation: AVOID. Overall score 3.5/10** (from 4.2), the decline driven by valuation (2.0) and risk factors (2.5) rather than by fresh operating deterioration.

Hologic's common stock ceased to exist as an investable security on April 8, 2026 following the completion of the approximately $18.3 billion Blackstone/TPG take-private. There is no authoritative price, no analyst coverage, no options market and no borrow. The only residual claim appears to be a contingent instrument of roughly $3.00 per share — about $683M, or 3.9% of the $17.3bn cash equity value — whose existence, ceiling, milestones and transferability I could not verify from any filing. That verification gap, not a view that the business collapsed, is what makes the rating AVOID rather than HOLD.

The underlying franchise was a decent asset sold at a fair-to-full price: $4,100.5M of FY2025 revenue, 51.6% gross margin, $1,057.1M of operating cash flow at 1.87× net income, $549M of net debt, and roughly $650M of buybacks that shrank diluted shares 10.35% since FY2022. It was also decelerating: 0.87% two-year revenue CAGR, R&D down 15.7% from its FY2023 peak, and a 363bp gross-margin decline whose cause could not be identified from available disclosure. Roughly a third of the headline FY2025 net income decline was tax normalization rather than operating decay, so the deterioration was real but smaller than the reported numbers suggested.

Three items would need resolution before any residual claim could be underwritten, and all three sit in documents that were not retrievable: the merger proxy's terms on contingent consideration, the FY2025 MD&A explanation of the cost-of-revenue increase, and the unaffected pre-announcement share price. In the absence of those, the correct action is no position.
