**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

LyondellBasell sits at or near the bottom of one of the deepest petrochemical down-cycles in recent memory. Revenue fell from $49.4B in 2022 to $30.2B in 2025, operating income swung to a $420M loss, and the company reported a net loss of $738M, roughly $1.25B of which was non-cash goodwill and asset impairment. Global overcapacity in ethylene and polyolefins, driven by Middle East and Chinese additions colliding with sluggish demand, has compressed margins sector-wide. LYB's disproportionate European exposure amplified the damage: its naphtha-fed European crackers are structurally uncompetitive against ethane-advantaged Gulf Coast and Middle East producers.

The situation shows genuine sequential improvement. Q1 2026 operating income recovered to $239M from a $88M loss in Q4 2025, continuing-ops EPS reached $0.42, and O&P-Americas EBITDA rebounded to $327M on wider polyethylene spreads. Management has executed a coordinated defensive pivot: a 50% dividend cut (from $1.37 to $0.69 quarterly), a buyback suspension, capex cut to ~$1.2B for 2026 from $1.9B, an $800M cost-savings program, and completion of a European divestiture that removed four loss-making sites. These moves protect the investment-grade rating and reduce the odds of a forced deleveraging, which is the correct priority even though it was painful for income holders.

The problem for the equity case is that the near-term earnings signal has deteriorated at the same time it improved. Consensus current-year EPS rose 26.8% over 90 days but fell 11.2% over the last 30; next-year EPS is down 14.4% over 30 days. The recovery hinges on two factors LYB cannot control: the pace of global capacity rationalization and the durability of a Middle-East-disruption-driven spread windfall. Combined with a distressed balance sheet (Altman Z 1.61, D/E 3.37x) and a Materials sector that has been a persistent negative-alpha area, this is a balanced setup. We rate LYB HOLD.

## Company Overview

LyondellBasell is one of the world's largest commodity plastics and petrochemicals producers and a significant licensor of polyolefin process technology. Incorporated in the Netherlands and operating primarily from the US Gulf Coast, the company now runs through five segments after exiting refining:

- **O&P-Americas** (~$9.8B FY25 revenue) — ethylene/olefins, polyethylene, polypropylene. The profit engine, with US Gulf Coast crackers running roughly 75-80% on cheap ethane, a structural cost advantage. FY25 EBITDA $1.14B.
- **O&P-EAI** (~$10.2B) — same products in Europe/Asia, structurally disadvantaged by naphtha-based feedstock. FY25 EBITDA -$457M.
- **I&D** (~$9.1B) — propylene oxide, oxyfuels, intermediate chemicals. FY25 EBITDA $878M.
- **APS / Advanced Polymer Solutions** (~$3.5B) — engineered plastics and compounds, heavily automotive-exposed. FY25 EBITDA -$651M, driven by impairments.
- **Technology** (~$0.5B) — catalyst sales and process licensing, high-margin but small. FY25 EBITDA $180M.

The competitive moat rests on North American feedstock cost advantage and the licensing/catalyst IP inside the Technology segment. The European footprint is a structural liability the company is actively shrinking.

## Financial Analysis

The defining feature is a severe cyclical trough. Revenue has declined for four consecutive years, and 2025 marked the earnings nadir:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 49.4 | 41.1 | 40.3 | 30.2 |
| Operating income ($M) | 5,101 | 3,053 | 1,817 | (420) |
| Net income ($M) | 3,889 | 2,121 | 1,367 | (738) |
| EBITDA ($M) | ~8,700 | 4,504 | 3,460 | 1,126 |
| Stockholders' equity ($B) | 12.6 | 12.9 | 12.5 | 10.1 |

The 2025 net loss was substantially non-cash: $972M of goodwill full write-offs (O&P-EAI and APS reporting units) plus $279M of other impairments, dropping goodwill from $1.56B to $708M. Operating cash flow held at $2.26B, exceeding the net loss, but free cash flow was only $384M after $1.88B of capex, an FCF margin of 1.3%.

The balance sheet carries real stress. Total debt is roughly $12.9B, D/E is 3.37x, and the Altman Z-Score of 1.61 sits in the distress zone. That reading is distorted by cyclically negative EBIT and impairment-shrunk equity rather than a genuine solvency crisis: the company holds $3.4B cash, $4.65B of undrawn facilities, a current ratio of 1.77, and remains covenant-compliant. The September 2025 revolver amendment, raising the maximum leverage ratio through 2027, was a defensive move signaling covenant pressure, and it should be read as such rather than dismissed.

Q1 2026 confirms the turn. Operating income recovered to $239M, EBITDA reached $568M, and O&P-Americas EBITDA rebounded to $327M on wider PE margins. Management guides Q2 2026 to improve across almost all businesses, helped by Middle East supply dislocations tightening polymer spreads. Q1 used $269M of cash in operations because of a working-capital build as prices rose, so the reported operating recovery has not yet translated into consistently positive cash generation.

The earnings surprise record is volatile: 2 beats and 2 misses over four quarters, including a 140% beat in Q1 2026 ($0.49 vs $0.20 estimate) but a nearly 300% miss in Q4 2025 (-$0.26 vs $0.13). This is characteristic of a business near breakeven where small margin moves swing EPS dramatically, and it reduces confidence in point estimates.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is cyclical recovery, not secular expansion. The bull framing is that 2025 was the trough, and the sequential Q1 2026 operating recovery plus improving Q2 guidance mark the turn. On a 90-day basis, estimate revisions support this: current-quarter EPS is up 22.7%, current-year up 26.8%, and next-year up 13.0%.

The complication is the 30-day trend. Over the last month, current-year EPS fell 11.2%, next-quarter fell 23.0%, and next-year fell 14.4%. This is the opposite of the accelerating-revision pattern that signals a durable turn; the near-term momentum has reversed even as the longer-window revisions remain positive. The most likely interpretation is that the initial re-rating on the Middle-East spread windfall is being partly walked back as analysts question its durability. The strong 90-day revision figure therefore does not clear the bar for an inflection BUY signal; it is being contradicted by the more recent data.

Structurally, LYB's growth levers are constrained. The company deferred its two named growth projects, Flex-2 propylene and MoReTec-2 recycling, to preserve cash, and cut capex by roughly a third. Volume growth in commodity chemicals depends on the global supply/demand balance rebalancing through competitor plant closures, a process management explicitly ties results to but cannot control. The Middle East disruption tailwind is real but is a supply-side wild card, not a repeatable demand driver.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing metrics are meaningless here: the trailing P/E is negative (-29x) because of the net loss, and the forward figure reported in the profile data is stale. The relevant lens is forward earnings against normalized cycle power. On current-year consensus EPS of $8.41, LYB trades at roughly 7.3x; on next-year consensus of $6.45, about 9.5x. Both are below the specialty-chemicals peer median P/E of 24.0x, but that comparison overstates the discount: peers like IFF (24x), RPM (20.7x), and PPG (16.9x) are steadier, less commodity-levered businesses, while ALB (-46.8x) and DuPont (147.7x) sit in their own distorted situations. A commodity producer at a cyclical trough should trade at a low multiple on peak-ish forward estimates.

The valuation dimension carries a warning that applies squarely here. Across our recent outcome data, cheap-looking stocks have systematically underperformed; a compressed multiple more often reflects a value trap than a mispricing. LYB's low forward multiple sits on estimates that are themselves falling over 30 days, which is precisely the pattern where the multiple looks cheap but the denominator is deteriorating. The stock declined from ~$80 in March to the low $60s even as Q1 operating results improved, which the bull case reads as mean-reversion opportunity. Given a >30% drawdown from the 52-week high of $83.94, the burden is on identifying what the market is missing. The plausible answer, that 2025 was the trough and normalized earnings power is well above trailing, is real but not sufficiently asymmetric to reward, because the recovery depends on uncontrollable industry rationalization and the near-term estimate trend has turned negative.

Analyst targets frame a wide range: low $48, mean $66.71, median $65, high $95. Against the current ~$61-62 price, that implies modest upside to consensus mean (~8%) and meaningful downside to the low. The dividend, now ~4.5% at the reduced payout, provides absolute yield support but is above the peer-median yield of 2.1% on a base that was cut precisely because the prior payout was unsustainable through the trough. We assign a below-average valuation score: cheap on forward numbers, but the cheapness is the value-trap variety absent a confirmed, controllable recovery catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

LYB's competitive position is bifurcated and, on balance, deteriorating in the near term while being actively restructured toward a higher-quality remaining portfolio. On the favorable side, its US Gulf Coast crackers run predominantly on ethane, giving them a durable cost advantage that should make them the first assets to recover as the cycle turns. The Technology segment's licensing and catalyst IP is a genuinely differentiated, high-margin franchise, though small.

On the unfavorable side, the company is effectively paying to exit its worst business. The European divestiture (Berre, Münchsmünster, Carrington, Tarragona), completed May 1, 2026, required a ~$300M cash contribution to the buyer and booked a $700-800M pre-tax loss, an admission that these naphtha-based assets carry negative strategic value. APS took $782M of impairments in 2025 on automotive demand weakness. The core commodity business competes primarily on price, and the current oversupply means LYB is a price-taker in a market where new low-cost Middle East and Chinese capacity continues to pressure spreads.

The trajectory reading is that the moat around the US ethane assets is intact but that overall share and pricing power are under structural pressure from global capacity additions LYB cannot influence unilaterally. The portfolio pruning improves quality per dollar of remaining capital but shrinks the earnings base. This is a below-average competitive-position score, consistent with the recent trajectory decline from 6.0 to 5.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Petrochemical overcapacity / prolonged trough.** The single largest risk. Global additions have outpaced demand, and the 10-K explicitly makes results dependent on the pace of capacity rationalization, an industry-wide dynamic outside LYB's control. If competitors do not close uneconomic plants, the trough extends.

**Balance sheet and dividend sustainability.** Z-Score in distress territory, D/E 3.37x, a covenant amendment in September 2025, and €500M of 2026 notes within a $1.47B current-maturity balance. Liquidity is adequate, but any extension of the trough pressures the IG rating the company is defending.

**European structural disadvantage and residual impairment risk.** The divestiture removed the worst assets, but further impairments are possible if the down-cycle persists, and three of nine LIFO pools ($1.6B) were near market value at year-end 2025 (Q1 2026 price increases removed the immediate LCM risk).

**Windfall durability.** The Q2 2026 guidance improvement leans on Middle East geopolitical disruption tightening spreads. This is a supply-shock benefit that reverses if the disruption resolves.

**Regulatory / ESG deselection.** EU Single-Use Plastics Directive, packaging rules, and a 2024 Kansas class action alleging public nuisance/antitrust over recyclability create a longer-term demand-destruction tail on fossil-based PE/PP.

**Pension deficit** of $863M at year-end 2025.

Several of these are the reversible, cyclically-driven kind that markets partly price. The idiosyncratic, less-priced items are the windfall reversal, the covenant/refinancing calendar, and the residual impairment tail. On balance this is a below-average risk score: the setup contains meaningful binary and macro-dependent elements against a stretched balance sheet.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 50.8% is rich against 45.9% one-year historical volatility, and sits in the 87th percentile of the current chain, with an implied ±11.2% move on the nearest expiry. The put/call skew is roughly balanced (0.4%), and the term structure is flat, indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed across tenors rather than concentrated on a single dated catalyst. IV rose 3.4 points intraday on July 23 while the price fell 1%, and the P/C OI ratio eased from 1.60 to 1.15. The read is elevated but two-sided uncertainty: the market is not pricing a directional collapse, but it is charging up for volatility, which argues against establishing a position through options and favors patience on any equity entry until the near-term earnings binary resolves.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($80-95).** 2025 was the trough. Operating income has turned positive sequentially, the US ethane-advantaged assets lead the recovery, cost savings of $800M annualized (targeting $1.3B cumulative by 2026) drop through as volumes recover, and global capacity rationalization eventually rebalances supply. The dividend cut and buyback halt protect the IG rating, removing forced-deleveraging risk. Normalized mid-cycle EPS well above trailing supports a re-rating toward the analyst high of $95, with the ~4.5% yield paid while waiting.

**Bear Case ($42-48).** The trough extends as competitors delay plant closures, the Middle East spread windfall reverses, and estimates continue the 30-day downtrend. Working capital keeps consuming cash, the covenant amendment proves prescient, and further impairments hit the remaining European/APS book. The stock retraces toward its 52-week low of $41.58 and the low analyst target of $48, with the reduced dividend at risk if cash generation does not turn consistently positive.

The base case sits in between: a gradual, uneven recovery where operating results improve but the pace of the cycle turn is slow and the earnings power settles below peak. The 30-day negative estimate revisions and the windfall dependence keep the near-term risk/reward roughly symmetric.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale: the evidence is genuinely two-sided, and neither a confident BUY nor an AVOID is warranted.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive operating cash flow (Q1 2026 still used $269M), confirming the operating recovery converts to cash.
- Estimate revisions turning positive on both 30-day and 90-day windows simultaneously, reversing the current 30-day deterioration.
- Evidence of durable competitor capacity rationalization (announced permanent closures) that would rebalance supply independent of the transient Middle East windfall.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A further ratings-agency action or a second covenant amendment, signaling balance-sheet defense is failing.
- Reversal of the Q2 spread widening (e.g., Middle East normalization) that pulls current-year EPS below $6, combined with continued cash burn.
- Material new impairment or dividend-coverage concern implying the reduced payout is itself unsustainable.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next earnings report is the natural checkpoint, with attention to whether operating cash flow turns positive and whether management's "improve across almost all businesses" Q2 guidance materializes. The €500M 2026 notes and rising current maturities are a secondary calendar item to monitor.

This continues the HOLD from the prior two reports. The score trajectory (4.5 → 5.6 → 5.4) captured the improving cyclical picture, but the most recent data cuts against extending that upward momentum: the 30-day estimate revisions have turned negative, the balance sheet remains stressed, and the recovery's key driver is a supply-shock windfall of uncertain durability. We hold the overall score essentially flat-to-modestly-lower rather than extending the improvement, reflecting that the near-term earnings signal has weakened even as the multi-year trough thesis is intact.

## Conclusion

LyondellBasell is a legitimate deep-cyclical trough story with real sequential operating improvement, a defensibly restructured portfolio anchored by US ethane-advantaged assets, and a management team that has made the hard, correct choices to protect the balance sheet. Those choices de-risk the solvency picture even as they shrink the earnings base and remove the income appeal that once supported the shares.

The equity case falls short of a BUY for three reasons: the 30-day estimate revisions have turned sharply negative despite strong 90-day numbers, undercutting the recovery signal; the recovery leans on an uncontrollable Middle East spread windfall and industry-wide capacity rationalization rather than company-specific execution; and the low forward multiple is the value-trap variety, cheap on estimates that are themselves declining, in a Materials sector that has consistently delivered negative alpha. The ~4.5% yield and US cost advantage provide downside support, but the risk/reward is balanced, not asymmetric. We rate LYB **HOLD**. Patient investors comfortable with commodity-cycle volatility can hold for the eventual recovery and the yield, but the bar for adding is a confirmed cash-flow turn and a positive reversal in near-term estimates.
