**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

ONEOK is a large-cap midstream infrastructure company operating ~60,000 miles of pipelines across gathering/processing, NGL, natural gas pipelines, and refined products/crude segments. Following three transformative acquisitions since 2023 (Magellan, EnLink, Medallion), ONEOK has tripled in scale and now generates approximately $8.0B in adjusted EBITDA on a ~90% fee-based revenue model. The stock trades at 16.1x forward earnings and yields 4.6%, representing a substantial discount to midstream peer median P/E of 28.2x.

The discount is justified by a specific, quantifiable concern: $34 billion in total debt representing 4.2x net debt/EBITDA, with interest expense that doubled from $866M (2023) to $1.78B (2025). After accounting for $2.6B in dividends and $2.7-3.2B in annual capex, free cash flow generation is minimal until major capital projects complete in 2027. The company maintains investment-grade ratings, a $3.5B undrawn credit facility, and generated $5.6B in operating cash flow in 2025, but the balance sheet constraint limits capital return upside and creates vulnerability to a sustained volume downturn.

The investment case resolves around whether EBITDA growth can outpace the carrying cost of the debt structure. Q1 2026 results were encouraging (guidance raised to $8.25B EBITDA midpoint, volumes growing across all segments), and the completion of major projects by 2027 should meaningfully expand free cash flow. The current 4.6% yield provides adequate compensation while waiting, but estimate revisions are flat (+0.5% over 90 days for full-year), and the stock has already rallied 38% from its November 2025 low of $64.02 to $88.21. The risk/reward at current levels is balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

ONEOK operates one of North America's largest integrated midstream energy networks across four segments:

- **Natural Gas Gathering & Processing**: 22,600 miles of gathering pipelines, 7.2 Bcf/d processing capacity (Williston, Mid-Continent, Permian)
- **Natural Gas Liquids**: 10,100 miles gathering + 4,800 miles distribution, 1.2 MMBbl/d fractionation capacity
- **Natural Gas Pipelines**: 8,300 miles including Henry Hub (the NYMEX pricing point), 74 Bcf storage
- **Refined Products & Crude**: 9,800 miles of refined products pipelines, 2,100 miles crude gathering, 100 MMBbl storage

The company's competitive moat derives from irreplaceable physical infrastructure with long-term acreage dedications (~3M acres in Williston, 1M+ in Mid-Continent, 400K+ in Permian), Henry Hub ownership, and multi-basin diversification that smooths single-basin production cycle risk.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement Trajectory:**

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026E |
|--------|------|------|------|-------|
| Revenue ($B) | $17.7 | $21.7 | $33.6 | ~$35+ |
| Adj. EBITDA ($B) | $5.2 | $6.8 | $8.0 | $8.25 |
| Net Income ($B) | $2.7 | $3.0 | $3.4 | ~$3.5 |
| Diluted EPS | $5.48 | $5.17 | $5.42 | $5.72 |
| Operating Cash Flow ($B) | $4.4 | $4.9 | $5.6 | ~$5.8 |

EPS declined from $5.48 to $5.17 in 2024 due to acquisition-related share dilution, then recovered to $5.42 in 2025 as EBITDA growth outpaced the diluted share count. The 2026 consensus of $5.72 implies 5.5% growth — modest but consistent with a fee-based utility-like business.

**Balance Sheet — The Central Constraint:**

- Total debt: $34.0 billion
- Debt-to-equity: 2.96x
- Net debt/EBITDA: ~4.2x (using 2025 EBITDA)
- Cash on hand: $78 million
- Working capital: -$1.9 billion
- Undrawn credit facility: $3.5B (expires 2030)
- Interest expense: $1.78B (2025), up from $866M (2023)

The leverage is genuinely elevated. Interest expense now consumes 31% of operating income ($1.78B / $5.7B). At current rates, new debt issuance costs 5-6%. However, the $3.5B undrawn facility and $5.6B in operating cash flow provide adequate liquidity buffers. Investment-grade ratings (Baa2/BBB/BBB, all Stable) remain intact.

**Free Cash Flow:**

Operating cash flow of $5.6B minus capex of $3.2B yields ~$2.4B in FCF. Dividends consume ~$2.6B, meaning FCF does not fully cover the dividend on a trailing basis without some contribution from the balance sheet or working capital releases. This improves materially post-2027 as capex normalizes lower.

Q1 2026 demonstrated volume growth across all segments: Mid-Continent +7%, Permian +4%, NGL Rocky Mountain +11%, Gulf Coast NGL +30%, Refined Products +12%. Management raised full-year 2026 EBITDA guidance to $8.25B midpoint.

**Major Projects (Completion Timeline):**

- Denver refined products expansion — mid-2026
- Medford NGL fractionator Phase 1 (100 MBbl/d) — Q4 2026
- Bighorn processing plant (300 MMcf/d) — mid-2027
- Texas City LPG export terminal (400 MBbl/d, JV with MPLX) — early 2028
- Eiger Express Pipeline (3.7 Bcf/d, Permian to Katy) — mid-2028

**Demand Optionality:**
Management cited "advanced discussions" regarding data center natural gas demand in Oklahoma and Texas. LNG export capacity projected to double over the next decade. LPG export demand accelerating from international supply diversification. These represent upside options beyond current guidance.

**Estimate Revisions:**
Current-year EPS estimate: $5.72 (+0.5% over 90 days). Next-year estimate: $6.15 (-1.0% over 30 days). Revisions are essentially flat — no strong positive earnings momentum signal is present. This is the primary reason for maintaining HOLD rather than upgrading to BUY.

## Valuation Assessment

OKE trades at 16.1x forward P/E versus the midstream peer median of 28.2x. This appears deeply discounted, but the comparison requires adjustment:

- **Williams (WMB)**: 34.4x P/E, 2.7% yield, lower leverage (~3.5x)
- **Targa Resources (TRGP)**: 28.2x P/E, 1.8% yield, higher commodity exposure
- **Kinder Morgan (KMI)**: 22.8x P/E, 3.5% yield, lower growth

OKE's discount reflects: (1) higher leverage (4.2x vs. 3.0-3.5x for WMB/KMI), (2) integration execution risk from three major deals in <2 years, and (3) lower FCF conversion due to elevated capex through 2027.

The 4.6% dividend yield provides adequate income compensation. At $88.21, the stock trades at 10.8x EV/EBITDA (using ~$55.6B market cap + $34B debt - $78M cash = ~$89.5B EV / $8.25B 2026E EBITDA). This is reasonable for a leveraged midstream company but not compellingly cheap given the debt burden.

**Upside scenario:** If EBITDA grows to $9.0-9.5B by 2028 (projects completing, data center/LNG demand materializing) and leverage compresses to 3.5x, a re-rating toward 12-13x EV/EBITDA implies $100-110 per share.

**Downside scenario:** If producer drilling activity slows (oil below $70 sustained), volumes stagnate, and leverage remains above 4x, the stock could retest the $70-75 range where it traded in late 2025.

## Competitive Landscape

ONEOK's competitive position has improved materially through acquisitions. The company now operates one of the only bidirectional refined products pipeline systems between the Mid-Continent and Gulf Coast. Henry Hub ownership provides a unique strategic asset. Multi-basin exposure reduces the single-basin risk that smaller midstream operators face.

Against Enterprise Products Partners (the industry benchmark for financial discipline), ONEOK's balance sheet is weaker but its growth profile is stronger. Against Williams (the highest-multiple peer), ONEOK has more commodity optionality through NGL exposure but less stable cash flow quality.

The integrated value chain — from wellhead gathering through processing, fractionation, and export — allows ONEOK to capture economics at multiple points, which is difficult for competitors to replicate without similar M&A.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Leverage overhang:** A credit downgrade below investment grade would spike borrowing costs on $3.5B in commercial paper and restrict capital market access. Probability: low (ratings stable) but impact: severe.

2. **Volume sensitivity to drilling activity:** ~90% fee-based mitigates price exposure, but fee revenue depends on throughput volumes. A sustained oil price decline below $65 would reduce Williston/Permian drilling.

3. **Interest rate persistence:** With rates elevated, new debt issuance and refinancing costs remain high. Progressive turnover of $34B in debt at higher rates compresses margins.

4. **Integration execution:** Three major acquisitions in <2 years (Magellan, EnLink, Medallion) create meaningful organizational complexity. Workforce grew from ~4,500 to 6,326 employees.

5. **Limited FCF pre-2027:** Dividends slightly exceed free cash flow currently. Management cannot simultaneously deleverage, buy back stock, and grow the dividend aggressively until capex normalizes.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($100-110, 13-25% upside):**
- EBITDA grows to $9.0B+ as projects complete by 2027-2028
- Leverage naturally compresses to 3.5x via denominator expansion
- Data center/LNG demand provides upside beyond current guidance
- P/E re-rates from 16x toward 18-20x as leverage concerns dissipate
- Dividend grows 3-4% annually, providing total return of 8-9%/year

**Bear Case ($70-75, 16-20% downside):**
- Producer drilling slows due to sustained low oil prices (<$70)
- Leverage remains above 4x through 2027
- Integration synergies disappoint or project delays occur
- Rising rates force higher refinancing costs
- Stock reverts to late-2025 trading range

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
1. Net debt/EBITDA declining below 3.8x (visible in quarterly financials)
2. Stock pulling back below $80 while guidance remains unchanged (creating >5% yield + growth asymmetry)
3. EPS estimate revisions turning positive by >5% over 90 days

**Downgrade Triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. Credit rating downgrade or negative watch from any agency
2. Dividend coverage deteriorating (FCF before dividend drops below 0.9x)
3. Volume declines >5% across multiple segments simultaneously
4. Net debt/EBITDA increasing above 4.5x

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July 2026). By then, capex intensity for the year will be clearer and 2027 project completion timelines will be more concrete.

## Conclusion

ONEOK is a well-positioned midstream infrastructure company executing an aggressive growth strategy through acquisitions and organic projects. The 4.6% dividend yield and 16x forward P/E offer income and value relative to peers. However, the specific factor preventing a BUY recommendation is quantifiable: **$34 billion in debt at 4.2x leverage, combined with flat estimate revisions, means the stock lacks positive earnings momentum AND the balance sheet strength that would create asymmetric upside from current levels.** After a 38% rally from November 2025 lows, much of the acquisition-driven re-rating has been captured.

The 2027 thesis (capex declining, FCF expanding, leverage compressing) is credible and well-articulated by management. It requires 12-18 months of execution to prove out, and the current price reflects reasonable probability of that outcome without providing significant margin of safety if volumes disappoint or rates remain elevated longer than expected.

HOLD for income at 4.6% yield with total return potential of 8-10% annually if the thesis executes as planned. Upgrade to BUY at $80 or below (creating >5% yield entry with 18%+ upside to $95 target), or upon visible leverage reduction below 3.8x.
