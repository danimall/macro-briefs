## Executive Summary

Patterson-UTI Energy is a $4.6B market cap provider of drilling and completion services that has rallied 118% from its 52-week low of $5.10, driven primarily by the oil price recovery from $60/bbl (Q4 2025) to $91/bbl by late April 2026. The company generates meaningful operating cash flow ($961M in 2025) despite reporting GAAP net losses, a disconnect driven by $940M in annual depreciation on assets marked up during the 2023 NexTier/Ulterra acquisitions. The ~8% FCF yield and confirmed oil price catalyst provide a specific reason to hold rather than exit.

The core tension is between a genuine cyclical recovery (oil above $90, equipment purchase commitments tripling, estimate revisions improving 40-70% across periods) and structural concerns that limit upside: completions services operating at a loss even before depreciation, insider selling at scale (CEO sold $3M in May, a director liquidated his entire position), and 49 percentage points of TSR underperformance vs. peers since 2020. The stock is appropriately held for its oil-price-driven recovery thesis, but the insider activity and structural margin problems prevent a higher-conviction recommendation.

## Company Overview

Patterson-UTI operates the largest combined drilling and completions platform in U.S. onshore through three segments: Drilling Services (31.5% of Q1 2026 revenue, 137 Tier-1 super-spec rigs), Completion Services (60.8% of revenue, hydraulic fracturing and related services), and Drilling Products (7.1%, Ulterra drill bits sold in 30+ countries). The company was transformed by its 2023 acquisition of NexTier Oilfield Solutions and Ulterra Drilling Technologies, which approximately doubled its revenue base but also introduced the asset valuation challenges that continue to weigh on reported profitability.

The company's customer base is concentrated (top 10 = 57% of revenue, top customer = 12%) among U.S. E&P operators who have structurally shifted toward capital discipline and shareholder returns over production growth — a dynamic that creates a secular headwind for drilling service demand independent of commodity prices.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis centers on two specific catalysts:

**1. Oil Price Recovery → Activity Rebound (H2 2026):**
Oil prices recovered from $60/bbl in Q4 2025 to $91/bbl by late April 2026 amid Middle East instability (Iran conflict). Management explicitly stated in Q1 2026 earnings that elevated prices "could support increased activity in second half of 2026." Equipment purchase commitments tripling from $47.5M to $128M between December 2025 and March 2026 provides tangible evidence management is preparing for a rebound.

The business typically lags oil price by 1-2 quarters. With prices elevated since February 2026, incremental rig additions should become visible in Q3-Q4 2026 results. Each incremental rig generates approximately $50-60K/day in revenue (or ~$18-22M annually). Moving from 92 to 100 rigs would add ~$150-175M in annual revenue with high incremental margins.

**2. Estimate Revisions — Genuine Positive Momentum:**
- Current year EPS revised from -$0.33 to -$0.18 (45% improvement in 90 days)
- Next year EPS revised from -$0.21 to +$0.06 (127% improvement, crossing from loss to profit)
- Next quarter revised from -$0.06 to -$0.02 (70% improvement)

These revisions reflect analysts incorporating the oil price recovery into their models. The direction is unambiguously positive, though the absolute level remains near breakeven rather than indicating a strong earnings recovery.

**Structural Growth Constraints:**
- E&P capital discipline limits drilling activity rebound even at high oil prices (management explicitly warns "higher oil prices do not necessarily result in increased activity")
- Customer consolidation reduces addressable market and increases buyer power
- Drilling rig count at 92 (Q1 2026) vs. 131 (Q1 2023) — a 30% decline that hasn't reversed despite moderate oil prices through most of this period
- Contract backlog declined from $830M (Dec 2022) to $260M (Mar 2026) — revenue visibility is extremely low

## Valuation Assessment

At $12.12 and a $4.6B market cap:
- Forward P/E: Negative (company losing money on trailing and current-year basis)
- Next year P/E: ~202x on $0.06 consensus — essentially meaningless
- EV/EBITDA: ~6.3x on trailing adjusted EBITDA of ~$918M (2025)
- FCF yield: ~8% on 2025 FCF of $372M
- P/B: ~2.7x book value (depressed by goodwill write-downs)

**Peer Comparison:**
- HP (Helmerich & Payne): $4B market cap, negative P/E, 2.4% dividend yield — closest comp, also losing money
- VAL (Valaris): $7B market cap, 7.1x P/E — offshore exposure gives it different cycle exposure
- NE (Noble Corp): $8B market cap, 36.6x P/E — also offshore

The peer group is universally challenged, making relative comparison less informative. PTEN's 8% FCF yield provides floor support, and the stock trades at a discount to its offshore peers on EV/EBITDA despite offering more direct leverage to the U.S. oil activity recovery.

**Valuation Catalyst:** If next year's consensus ($0.06 EPS) proves conservative and earnings normalize toward $0.30-0.50/share (consistent with rig count recovery to 95-100 and completion services margin improvement), the stock's implied P/E would be 24-40x on current price — indicating the market is already pricing substantial recovery. The rally from $5.10 to $12.12 has captured much of the rerating that an activity rebound would justify.

**Counter-argument:** The stock still trades 7% below its 52-week high of $13.08 and well below pre-merger levels. If oil stays elevated and activity recovers as management expects, EBITDA could rebound to $1.0-1.1B, implying EV/EBITDA of 5.5-6.0x — reasonable for a mid-cycle oilfield services company.

## Competitive Landscape

PTEN's post-merger position as the largest combined drilling + completions platform in U.S. onshore is a structural advantage in attracting integrated contracts. The 137 Tier-1 super-spec fleet represents the industry's highest specification equipment, and no competitor has equivalent scale across both services.

However, the competitive advantage has not translated to superior returns. The company's TSR of 133% since 2020 underperforms peers' 182% by a significant margin, suggesting the market views the integration as value-dilutive rather than synergistic. The completion services segment in particular — which was the primary rationale for the NexTier acquisition — operates at a structural loss after acquisition-related depreciation.

The technology transition toward electric/gas-powered frac equipment and automation (PTEN's Vertex™ platform, eos™ digital system) represents a potential differentiator, but capital requirements are significant and competitors (Liberty Energy, ProFrac) are investing similarly.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Oil Price Reversal (HIGH):** If the Iran conflict resolves or OPEC+ increases production (they are phasing out voluntary cuts), oil could retreat to $70-80, which would eliminate the activity rebound thesis. The business lags oil by 1-2 quarters in both directions, meaning a Q3 2026 oil decline wouldn't hurt until Q1 2027 — but the stock would reprice immediately.

**2. Goodwill Impairment (MEDIUM-HIGH):** The remaining $451M in Drilling Products goodwill has only an 8% cushion above carrying value. A 100bps reduction in long-term growth assumptions would consume 7% of that cushion. Given the company's track record (already wrote off $885M in completions goodwill), another impairment is a realistic scenario.

**3. Insider Selling (MEDIUM-HIGH):** CEO sold $3M in shares on May 1 at $11.85. Director Drummond completely liquidated his position ($8.7M+) between February and May 2026. Director Jaime sold at $12.29 in May. Zero open-market purchases by any insider during the stock's recovery. This pattern is inconsistent with management's stated optimism about H2 2026 activity recovery.

**4. Structural Completion Services Losses (MEDIUM):** Even with oil above $90, pricing discipline among E&Ps may prevent the margin recovery needed to make this segment economically productive on its post-merger asset base.

**5. NOV Patent Litigation:** Ulterra won at district court, but NOV is appealing. An unfavorable reversal could have "material impact" per the 10-K. Appellate brief due June 1, 2026.

## Investment Thesis

Completion services margins improve with higher activity and pricing power. FY2027 EBITDA recovers to $1.0-1.1B, justifying an EV/EBITDA of 5.5-6.0x. Next year EPS of $0.30-0.50 proves achievable. Rig count stays below 95. Company continues operating near breakeven with no clear path to earnings growth. Stock reverts toward $7-8 (roughly 6.5x EV/trailing EBITDA of $800M).

**Base Case ($12-13):** Oil stays elevated but E&P capital discipline limits the activity response to 3-5 incremental rigs. Estimates continue to improve modestly. Stock consolidates in the $11-13 range as the market waits for evidence of the activity recovery materializing in Q3/Q4 earnings.

## Conclusion

Patterson-UTI Energy is appropriately held because of three specific factors: (1) a genuine oil price catalyst with oil at $91/bbl by late April 2026 and management preparing for H2 2026 activity recovery, (2) strong FCF generation (~8% yield) providing valuation support, and (3) estimate revisions improving 40-70% across forecast periods, crossing from loss to profit for FY2027. These catalysts have specific timelines (Q3/Q4 2026 earnings should confirm or refute the activity thesis) and are not priced in at current consensus levels.

The HOLD rather than BUY reflects three offsetting concerns: concentrated insider selling by the CEO and a director who completely liquidated his position, the demonstrated inability of completion services to earn returns on its post-merger asset base even at elevated activity levels, and the structural headwind of E&P capital discipline that may prevent the activity rebound even if oil remains elevated. The stock has already doubled, capturing much of the cyclical recovery optionality, and the insider selling pattern is difficult to reconcile with conviction in further upside.

Position sizing should be modest (1-2% maximum), with the dividend yield providing carry while waiting for Q3 earnings confirmation.
