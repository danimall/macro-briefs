## Executive Summary

Autoliv has done the hard part. Between FY2022 and FY2025 the company took gross margin from 15.79% to 19.18% (+339bps) and operating margin from 7.45% to 10.06%, on revenue up 22.3%, while retiring 11.8% of diluted shares. Diluted EPS compounded 25.3% annually to $9.56, and roughly 80% of that came from earnings growth rather than the buyback. Free cash flow of $716M on a $8.9B market cap is an 8.0% yield; net debt sits near 1.0x EBITDA on my calculation and 1.2x on management's pension-inclusive definition, comfortably inside a 0.5–1.5x policy range. This is a regulated-content oligopolist with powertrain-agnostic products, priced at 11.9x this year's consensus adjusted EPS and 10.3x next year's.

The problem is the shape of 2026. Management guided Q3 adjusted operating margin to be "similar to the first half-year level" (Q2: 9.6%) and promised a "significant step-up" in Q4. Consensus has now formalized that: the current-quarter estimate was cut 20.8% in 30 days to $2.21 while the following quarter was raised 16.4% to $3.53. Backing into it, $3.53 of Q4 EPS implies roughly a 13.5% adjusted operating margin in a single quarter against 9.6% in Q2, a level Autoliv has never sustained. On the cash side, FY2026 operating cash flow guidance of ~$1.2B against H1 actual of $359M requires ~70% of the year in H2. The seasonal shape is Autoliv's normal pattern (H2 2025 also delivered ~69% of full-year OCF), but the absolute Q4 margin required is a new high-water mark, and the levers that produce it (customer compensation negotiations and engineering income) are negotiated outcomes, not contracted ones.

The cost side is deteriorating in real time. Guidance embeds a ~$110M gross raw-material headwind that management attributed explicitly to the Persian Gulf conflict, and Autoliv buys no raw materials directly, so inflation reaches it through tier-2/3 suppliers with a lag and is recovered from OEMs component-by-component with a further lag. The cleanest quantification of the leakage is tariffs: 83% recovered in Q2, 78% year to date. The input cycle is still arriving rather than rolling off. Add China LVP guided down 5%, adverse regional mix (high-content North America organic sales fell 5% in Q2 while growth concentrated in low-content India and South America), and current-year estimates down 2.6% in 30 days, and the case for adding at $121 is weaker than the multiple suggests. HOLD, with a defined path to upgrade at the October 23 print.

## Company Overview

Autoliv is the largest pure-play automotive passive-safety supplier: airbags, seatbelts, steering wheels, plus commercial-vehicle and electrical safety products. Scale per the company's March 2026 disclosure: operations in 25 countries, 13 technical centers, ~64,000 employees, FY2025 sales of $10.8B. On the Q2 2026 call CEO Mikael Bratt characterized global passive-safety share as "around 45%." That is a management statement on a public call, not an audited disclosure, and it is uncorroborated by independent share data in this analysis — treat it as directional.

Three structural features drive the model. First, content is powertrain-agnostic: airbags and seatbelts are required regardless of ICE, hybrid or BEV architecture, which insulates Autoliv from the single largest disruption facing legacy suppliers. It does not insulate the company from volume, trim mix or regional mix. Second, growth comes from content-per-vehicle plus modest LVP growth — management frames the long-term algorithm as LVP +1–2% plus content +1–2%. Third, regional CPV dispersion is wide (high-income markets earn materially more per vehicle than India or China), which makes where volume grows as important as whether it grows. In 2026 that works against Autoliv.

Operationally this is a mature, negative-working-capital industrial: FY2025 trade payables of $2,007M exceeded inventories of $992M, and capex of $441M was only 1.08x depreciation of $407M.

The FY2023 operating-margin dip reflects capacity-alignment and antitrust charges that year, not underlying deterioration. Decomposing three-year EPS growth: net income +73.8%, share count −11.8%, so approximately 80% of EPS growth came from operations. This is not a financial-engineering story built on shrinking the denominator.

### Cash generation and balance sheet

FY2025 operating cash flow of $1,157M against net income of $735M is 1.57x conversion; management cited trailing-twelve-month conversion of 119% against an ≥80% target. FCF of $716M is a 6.6% margin and an 8.0% yield on market cap.

Q2 2026 OCF of $434M (up $157M) was a record second quarter, but the composition requires care: management disclosed a $240M positive working-capital swing (payables +$120M, net receivables +$35M, accrued severance/restructuring +$48M) explicitly described as normalization after Q1. The accrued-severance component is a cost recognized and not yet paid. On a half-year basis, H1 2026 OCF was $359M, up just $4M year over year. The quarter should not be annualized.

Leverage: FY2025 total debt $2,153M against cash $604M, net debt ~$1,549M; equity $2,572M (debt/equity 0.84x). Interest expense of $103M against $1,088M EBIT is 10.6x coverage; blended cost of debt ~4.8%. Net debt/adjusted EBITDA of ~1.02x on my calculation versus management's reported 1.2x; the difference is definitional (Autoliv includes pension liabilities). Management's 1.2x, improved from 1.3x, is the right figure for policy comparison.

The one genuine balance-sheet caveat is liquidity thinness: the current ratio was 0.959 in FY2024 and 1.045 in FY2025. That is structural for a negative-working-capital supplier, but it means Autoliv depends on continuous commercial-paper/revolver access and OEM payment discipline. In a severe LVP shock with receivable stress, that is where pressure appears first. Recomputing Altman Z from FY2025 inputs gives ~2.94 (grey zone), with the shortfall driven almost entirely by the working-capital term contributing just 0.025 of the total. I read that as a structural artifact rather than a distress signal, but it does argue against treating this as a fortress balance sheet.

### Q2 2026 in detail

Net sales ~$2.80B (+3.3%; organic +1%, FX +$62M). Adjusted operating income $270M at 9.6% margin, up 30bps. Adjusted diluted EPS $2.43 versus $2.21. Reported operating income was $192M, $78M below adjusted, mainly on capacity-alignment charges. SG&A improved 40bps to 4.9% of sales; capex/sales fell to 3.4% from 4.2%.

Two observations. First, gross margin fell 30bps to 18.2%, fully explained by ~80bps of identified items (a $13–15M supplier compensation reversal and $9M of Turkey-related impairment); underlying gross margin improved. Second, management's EPS bridge was +18¢ from operating income, +10¢ from lower share count, −7¢ from tax. Roughly 45% of adjusted EPS growth came from the buyback, a materially different mix from the 80/20 multi-year split. If operating growth stays at Q2's pace, the share count does more of the work than the historical record implies.

The Q2 result was a slight miss ($2.43 adjusted versus $2.45 consensus, −0.7% per the surprise data), breaking a three-quarter beat streak of +11.4%, +8.7% and +6.6%. Revenue beat. GAAP was clearly weak on the $78M adjusted-to-reported gap.

## Growth Analysis

FY2026 guidance (reiterated July 17, assumptions as of July 9): organic sales flat, FX translation +2.3%, adjusted operating margin 10.5–11.0%, OCF ~$1.2B, capex below 5% of sales. Against S&P Global's global LVP forecast of −2.3% and Autoliv's own −2.5% assumption, flat organic sales implies roughly 2.5 points of outperformance — the core of the "growth over market" model.

Q2 outperformance by region was substantial: global +1pp versus LVP, Asia +6pp, China +7pp, India +20pp with 36% organic growth. Front-center airbags on new Chinese launches were named as the CPV driver. Sharing, Suzuki and Nio were the largest sales-growth contributors; the Nio ES9 was the single most significant launch by sales potential.

Three things constrain how much of this converts to earnings growth.

**Regional mix is working against content.** Americas organic sales fell ~5% in Q2 while South American production grew. Bratt's own framing: growth in low-content South America "is not enough to offset what's going down in the high content markets." Global regional LVP mix was ~60bps unfavorable in Q2, and FY2026 guidance embeds only +40bps of regional mix contribution. India's +9% LVP is driven by purchase-tax reductions on smaller, lower-priced vehicles — the volume is real, the content is not.

**China volume is deteriorating faster than Autoliv's position is improving.** S&P Global has cut China LVP by 4 points since January to −5%, and management attributed the global guidance cut (−1% to −2.5%) primarily to China. Autoliv's mix response is genuine — Chinese OEMs went from 22% of its China sales in 2022 to 55% in Q2 2026 — but Chinese OEMs are ~72% of China LVP, so Autoliv remains over-weighted to the shrinking global-OEM-in-China segment. Management declined to say whether the new Chinese-OEM business is margin-accretive, deflecting to "platform program by platform program." The framework cooperation agreements identified on the call as Great Wall and XPeng carry no disclosed value, volume or duration; they are positioning, not backlog.

**Order intake is being delayed.** Management declined to disclose current intake but acknowledged OEMs are pushing decisions out and that driveline-related program reshuffling continues. Bratt's language was to "defend our market share," not expand it. With a 2–4 year award-to-revenue lag, soft 2025–26 intake is a 2028–2030 revenue question that will not appear in near-term financials.

The self-help lever remains. The Turkey exit (announced via 8-K on May 11 with Items 2.05/2.06) will shift steering-wheel, airbag and seatbelt production to Tunisia and Romania: ~$142M total charges ($90M in Q2), ~$129M cash out, ~2,200 employees, complete by H1 2028, ~$40M annual pre-tax savings starting 2027. Simple cash payback is 3.2 years and the savings equal ~36bps of margin on ~$11B of sales — solid, not transformative.

Consensus FY2027 adjusted EPS of $11.80 implies 15% growth over FY2026's $10.23, and that estimate has been revised only −0.9% in 90 days. The near-term revision picture is less comfortable: current-year down 2.6% (30d) and 2.8% (90d), with the intra-year shift from Q3 to Q4 already described. Falling near-term estimates with stable out-year estimates is consistent with a timing problem rather than a level problem, but the timing problem is exactly the risk.

Peer context is heterogeneous. Median peer P/E is 19.6, but that median is set by BorgWarner at 34.9x and Aptiv at 22.6x, both of which carry distorted trailing earnings. The genuinely comparable value names — Lear at 11.5x and Gentex at 12.7x — bracket Autoliv's 12.7x almost exactly. Autoliv trades in line with the cheap end of auto parts, not at a discount to it, while delivering a better operating margin (10.1% versus Lear's mid-single digits), a stronger share position, and a more consistent buyback. The premium ALV arguably deserves for margin structure and powertrain agnosticism is not being paid, but neither is the market pricing it as impaired.

Analyst targets: mean $134.71, median $134.00, low $118.00, high $148.00, with 12 buy-or-better and 6 holds and no change in that distribution over three months. The low target sitting near spot indicates the street sees limited downside from here, which is consistent with the yield-and-buyback floor but also means consensus is not underwriting a bear case.

Two arguments cut against rewarding the multiple more aggressively. First, cheapness here is persistent rather than newly created; Autoliv has traded in a 10–14x band through the entire margin-expansion program, so the re-rating that "should" follow 339bps of gross margin gain has not arrived and there is no identified catalyst to force it before Q4 results are reported in early 2027. Second, the forward multiple rests on an adjusted number that excludes capacity-alignment charges the company incurs more or less continuously; Turkey follows a prior multi-year EMEA program an analyst sized at ~8,000 employees on the call. Normalizing for perpetual footprint optimization, I would haircut adjusted margin by roughly 25–50bps, which pushes the effective forward multiple toward 12.5x FY2026 rather than 11.9x.

Base case $134: 11.4x FY2027E $11.80, which is mid-range for Autoliv's historical band and matches the analyst median. That is +10.5% from $121.25, or +13.4% with the dividend.

## Competitive Landscape

Passive safety is a consolidated, regulation-driven oligopoly. Principal competitors are ZF (which absorbed TRW), Joyson Safety Systems (built on Key Safety/Takata assets), Toyoda Gosei and Hyundai Mobis, plus a growing tier of Chinese domestic suppliers. Barriers are high: crash-test validation, multi-year OEM homologation, litigation liability tails, and deep patent estates. Content growth is driven by regulation (side/curtain airbags, front-center airbags, pedestrian protection) rather than consumer preference, which makes the demand for content unusually insensitive to discretionary sentiment even as the underlying volume is fully cyclical.

I could not obtain independent market-share data in this analysis; management's ~45% figure stands uncorroborated. The observable evidence on trajectory is mixed-to-positive. Positive: 339bps of gross margin expansion over three years on flat industry volume, driven by footprint consolidation, automation and pricing recovery; Q2 outperformance of LVP by 7pp in China and 20pp in India; the China domestic-OEM mix shift from 22% to 55% of China revenue in under four years. Negative: still under-indexed to Chinese domestic OEMs at 55% versus their ~72% of China production; defensive share language from the CEO; delayed OEM award decisions; unconfirmed margin profile on the new Chinese business, where price aggression is well documented.

The China mix shift is progress within one geography, not group-level customer diversification. Group concentration data was not verifiable here, so the mix shift should not be read as concentration relief.

## Risk Assessment

**The Q4 margin binary is the dominant risk.** Barclays' Dan Levy stated on the July 17 call that roughly a 15% Q4 margin is needed to hit the full-year midpoint, and management did not dispute it. My reconstruction from consensus is slightly less demanding: $3.53 of Q4 EPS on ~72.5M shares at a 30% tax rate and ~$25M of quarterly interest implies ~$391M of adjusted operating income on ~$2.85–2.9B of sales, or ~13.5%. Full-year at consensus lands near 10.4% adjusted operating margin, marginally below the guided 10.5–11.0% range, so consensus has already conceded part of the guidance. That is a useful de-risking, but a 13.5% quarter would still be an Autoliv record, and I could not verify Q4 2023/Q4 2024 quarterly adjusted margins to establish precedent. The levers are customer compensation and engineering income, both negotiated.

**Input inflation with a double lag.** Helium — used in inflator manufacture and leak testing — was named specifically as an India headwind, an exposure most supplier models miss. This headwind is still building rather than rolling off.

**Demand-side macro.** Further LVP cuts are plausible. Autoliv is classified as consumer discretionary, and vehicle affordability and household spending feed through to LVP with a lag. The offsetting note is that Q2 actual global LVP came in at −0.3%, about 160bps better than April expectations, on resilience in the Americas, Europe, India and Korea.

**Contingent liabilities I could not size.** Item 3 of the FY2025 10-K incorporates Note 19 by reference, and I was unable to re-read that note. Three threads were identified but are not verified here: NHTSA's ARC inflator investigation (where Autoliv's exposure appears to arise as a purchaser of third-party inflators incorporated into its modules, though that characterization is inference, not verified text), German civil antitrust follow-on damages claims, and a Mexican tax dispute at an early stage. One firm corroboration exists: FY2026 guidance explicitly excludes "antitrust-related matters," which no company carves out unless the item is live and potentially material. I treat this as an unquantified tail, not a modeled liability.

**Capital-return pace.** Q2 returned $264M ($200M buyback for 1.6M shares at ~$125 average, plus $64M dividend). Annualized that is ~$1.06B against guided FY FCF of ~$0.67B. Either H2 buybacks moderate or leverage drifts toward the top of policy. Leverage actually improved in Q2 (1.3x to 1.2x, net debt down ~$75M), so nothing has deteriorated, and there is capacity at 1.2x against a ~1.5x ceiling. But investors extrapolating Q2's retirement pace are underwriting something the cash guide does not fund. I could not verify the size, remaining capacity or expiry of the current repurchase authorization, so no statement about contractual runway is supportable.

**Governance.** Chairman Jan Carlson sold 19,607 shares at $130.001 on June 12, 2026 — approximately 24.6% of his reported position — retaining 60,000 shares. This occurred after the May Turkey decision and before the Q2 print, at a price ~7% above spot. A former CEO diversifying a concentrated position is routine, and I did not verify whether the sale was under a 10b5-1 plan, which would further discount the signal. I do not read a view about prospects into it. Separately: no open-market purchases by any executive, and the balance of Form 4 activity is mechanical dividend-equivalent accrual on RSUs and PSUs. The CFO transition (Monika Grama, internal EMEA finance promotion, effective April 1, 2026) is a continuity appointment, but she has no prior public-company CFO or capital-markets track record, and her first real test is the most back-loaded quarter in recent Autoliv history. Compensation is anchored on adjusted operating income, which structurally excludes the recurring restructuring charges; the metric is reasonably aligned with the margin program but insulated from a real, repeating cost.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 30.6% against 27.7% one-year realized is fair (2.9pp spread, 46th percentile within the chain). The term structure is in backwardation (30.6% at one week versus 26.7% at six months), which the data tags as pricing imminent stress, but with only 94 contracts across four expirations and no dated company catalyst inside seven days, I would not over-read a thin front-month print; the ±3.8% one-week implied move looks like weekly mechanics rather than event pricing. More informative are the structural readings: put/call open interest at 0.67 is call-tilted, put/call volume 0.11 is very light, and three- and six-month put/call skew values are small in magnitude. IV has been essentially flat since late July (30.9% → 30.6%) while price rose 1.9%. Short interest of 6.2% of float with 5.1 days to cover is moderate and uncrowded. The options and short-interest picture is balanced; it neither confirms an imminent dislocation nor contradicts the fundamental caution. Vol is not cheap enough to make hedging the Q4 binary especially attractive at the six-month tenor, where a ±13.6% move is priced.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull case — $155 (+27.8%)

Q4 delivers. Customer compensation and engineering income land as guided, FY2026 adjusted operating margin prints 10.7–11.0%, and the raw-material headwind proves recoverable at the 80%+ rate the tariff experience suggests. FY2027 then compounds off a clean base: Turkey's $40M begins to flow (36bps), the India and Chinese-OEM content wins scale, LVP stabilizes as China incentives normalize, and adjusted EPS reaches $12.25–12.50 on a share count down to ~69M. At 12.5x that is $153–156. Separately, the market finally pays for three consecutive years of margin expansion by moving Autoliv toward the middle of the broader auto-parts multiple range rather than the value end. Total return with the dividend approaches 31%.

### Bear case — $98 (−19.2%)

Q4 slips. Compensation negotiations extend into 2027, FY2026 adjusted margin lands at 10.0–10.2%, and the cash guide misses, forcing the buyback down just as leverage approaches 1.4x. Gulf-driven input inflation runs above the $110M assumption while recovery stays at ~78%, compressing FY2027 adjusted EPS to ~$10.00. China LVP is cut again toward −7%, and the Chinese-OEM mix that management would not characterize on margin proves dilutive. A US consumer slowdown pulls North American LVP negative, hitting the highest-content region. At 9.8x $10.00 the stock is $98, near the $99.16 fifty-two-week low. An adverse development on the ARC inflator or German antitrust threads would take it lower and is not quantifiable from available disclosure.

### Base case — $134 (+10.5%, +13.4% with dividend)

Q4 is strong but short of the required level; FY2026 adjusted EPS lands near consensus $10.23 with margin at the low end of guidance. FY2027 grows to roughly $11.50–11.80 on Turkey savings, continued SG&A leverage (4.9% of sales in Q2, down 40bps) and 3–4% annual share retirement, partially offset by flat-to-down LVP and continued adverse regional mix. At 11.4x FY2027E, $134, coincidentally the analyst median. Probability-weighting base 55%, bull 20%, bear 25% gives roughly +6.6% price return plus 2.9% dividend, or about 9.5% total. That is an adequate but not compelling risk-adjusted return, and it does not clear an ACCUMULATE bar given that the single largest variable resolves in early 2027 rather than at the next print.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD (income_hold).** The reason to own Autoliv now is the cash return and the valuation floor: 2.87% dividend, ~$415M of buyback capacity within guided FCF after the dividend (~4.7% of market cap), a combined shareholder yield near 7.5%, and 10.3x FY2027 consensus with out-year estimates revised only −0.9% in 90 days. The reason not to add is that the near-term earnings path is concentrated in one quarter at an unprecedented margin level, current-year estimates are falling, and the input-cost cycle driving the shortfall is still arriving.

**Reassessment date:** Q3 2026 results, October 23, 2026.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE if:**
1. Q3 adjusted operating margin prints at or above 10.0% (versus guidance of "similar to the first half"), with FY2026 guidance reaffirmed on updated raw-material assumptions rather than the July 9 basis;
2. Management quantifies customer compensation and engineering income already contracted for Q4, or raw-material headwind guidance is reduced below $110M;
3. Price approaches $105–108, which would put the stock near 9x FY2027 consensus with an unchanged 7.5% FCF yield — that entry restores the asymmetry regardless of the Q4 outcome.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if:**
1. FY2026 adjusted operating margin guidance is cut below 10.5%, or the ~$1.2B operating cash flow guide is lowered;
2. The raw-material headwind is guided above $150M, or tariff/inflation recovery falls below 75%;
3. China LVP is revised below −7%, or Americas organic sales decline exceeds 5% for a second consecutive quarter;
4. The quarterly buyback falls materially below the ~$200M Q2 pace while leverage rises above 1.4x, indicating the cash guide is not being met.

**Downgrade to AVOID if:** a quantified ARC inflator or German antitrust provision emerges at a scale material to the ~$2.57B equity base, or FY2027 consensus is cut below $10.50 on evidence that margin recovery is cyclical pass-through rather than structural.

**Position management for existing holders:** maintain. The dividend is well covered (roughly 35% of adjusted earnings), leverage is inside policy, and the low end of the analyst target range sits near spot. There is no compelling reason to trim ahead of a quarter that management has already told the market will look like H1.

## Conclusion

Autoliv is a better business than its multiple implies and a riskier one than its balance sheet implies. The margin program is real — 339bps of gross margin over three years, 10.1% operating margin, 1.57x cash conversion, four consecutive years of share-count reduction — and 80% of the EPS compounding came from operations rather than the buyback. At 11.9x FY2026E and 10.3x FY2027E with a ~7.5% FCF yield, the stock is not expensive on any measure.

What prevents an upgrade is the concentration of the 2026 outcome. Consensus now requires roughly a 13.5% adjusted operating margin in Q4 against 9.6% in Q2, delivered through negotiated customer compensation, while a Gulf-sourced $110M raw-material headwind moves through a supply chain Autoliv does not purchase from directly and recovers from OEMs component-by-component. Current-year estimates have been cut 2.6% in 30 days, Q2 broke a three-quarter beat streak, high-content Americas organic sales fell 5%, and China LVP is guided down 5% with a mix shift whose margin profile management declined to characterize. Against a $134 base case (+10.5%) and a $98 bear case (−19.2%), probability-weighted expected return of roughly 9.5% including the dividend is fair rather than attractive.

Own it for the cash return and the valuation floor. Add at $105–108, or at the October 23 print if Q3 holds 10% margin and the Q4 levers are quantified.
