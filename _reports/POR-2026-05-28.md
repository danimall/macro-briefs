**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Portland General Electric is a vertically-integrated Oregon electric utility undergoing a massive capital investment cycle ($8.6B through 2030) and attempting its first-ever geographic expansion via a $1.9B acquisition of PacifiCorp's Washington operations. The investment thesis has deteriorated materially since early 2026: Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 both missed consensus by wide margins (-26% and -35% respectively), load growth guidance was cut from 2.5-3.5% to 1.5-2.5%, and regulatory disallowances signal an increasingly adversarial relationship with Oregon's Public Utility Commission.

The stock trades at $50.78, approximately 14.8x the midpoint of 2026 adjusted EPS guidance ($3.43). While this appears discounted vs. the peer median P/E of 19.6x, the discount reflects real structural problems: persistent equity dilution (24% over three years, with 10-15% more coming), actual ROE of 7.4% vs. authorized 9.34%, and an acquisition that will not be accretive until 2028 at earliest. The 4.3% dividend yield provides some support, but is not exceptional relative to the risk profile.

Analyst sentiment is degrading: the consensus has 8 Holds, 3 Buys (including 1 Strong Buy), 1 Sell, and 1 Strong Sell, with a recent Strong Buy downgrade. The median target of $53 implies only 4.4% upside from current levels, insufficient reward given the multi-vector risk profile.

## Company Overview

Portland General Electric serves approximately 955,000 customers across a 4,000 square mile service territory in Oregon, representing roughly two-thirds of the state's commercial and industrial activity. The company operates a diversified generation fleet including thermal, wind, solar, and hydro resources totaling approximately 3,400 MW of capacity.

PGE's strategic focus centers on three imperatives: decarbonizing its power supply per Oregon's HB 2021 (80% GHG reduction by 2030, 100% by 2040), electrifying the regional economy (data centers, EVs), and modernizing grid infrastructure for resilience. The company is in the process of transforming from a single-state Oregon utility to a multi-state Pacific Northwest operator through its proposed PacifiCorp Washington acquisition.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability**

Revenue grew from $2.64B (2022) to $3.56B (2025), representing 35% cumulative growth. However, this top-line growth has failed to translate proportionally to the bottom line due to rising financing costs, regulatory lag, and dilution. GAAP EPS grew only from $2.60 to $2.77 over the same period (+7%), illustrating the dilution drag.

Operating income reached $555M in 2025 (15.6% margin), up 8% YoY. Net income was $306M, down 2% YoY. The Q1 2026 results were particularly concerning: GAAP EPS of $0.38 vs. $0.89 consensus, driven by a $15M regulatory disallowance, $17M in transformation/acquisition costs, and weak residential demand from mild weather.

**Capital Structure and Dilution**

This is the central problem. Shares outstanding grew from 89.3M to 110.5M between 2022-2025, a 24% increase. The 2026 equity plan calls for ~$550M in base issuance plus additional RFP equity, with a new $500M ATM facility authorized. At $50/share, that implies 11-17M additional shares annually — 10-15% further dilution through 2027.

Long-term debt stands at $4.66B with a debt-to-equity ratio of 2.2x. The common equity ratio is 47% vs. a 50% target. Moody's rates PGE A3/Stable (outlook upgraded to Stable from Negative in December 2025), and S&P rates BBB+/Stable. The Z-Score of 1.12 reflects structural utility leverage, not distress risk, given the regulated cash flow model.

**Cash Flow**

Operating cash flow improved to $1.12B in 2025 (from $778M in 2024), but capex of $1.19B produced negative free cash flow of -$71M. Planned capex escalates to $1.66B (2026) and $1.74B (2027), ensuring continued negative FCF and continued reliance on external capital markets.

Available liquidity stands at $954M as of Q1 2026.

## Growth Analysis

**The Growth Thesis Has Weakened**

PGE's long-term growth framework targets 5-7% EPS CAGR off a 2024 base midpoint of $3.08, implying $3.33-$3.53 for 2026 (guidance reaffirmed) and $3.50-$3.78 for 2027. This growth is entirely rate-base-driven: invest capital → earn regulated return → grow earnings. Every step in this chain is under pressure.

Industrial load growth (primarily data centers) surged 17.3% in 2025, but Q1 2026 management cut weather-adjusted load growth guidance from 2.5-3.5% to 1.5-2.5%. Oregon's new data center tariff framework (HB 3546 / POWER Act) allocates full infrastructure costs to large energy users, which could make the state less competitive for new hyperscaler commitments. PGE explicitly identifies the risk of data center customers choosing other jurisdictions.

The PacifiCorp Washington acquisition adds ~140,000 customers (15-22% expansion) and 805 MW of generation capacity. PGE diversifies out of single-state concentration, but the deal won't close until mid-2027 and won't be accretive until at least 2028 given integration costs and initial dilution from Manulife's JV equity.

**Estimate Revisions**

Near-quarter estimates are rising modestly (+6.9% over 30 days, +11.5% over 90 days for Q2), but full-year estimates are flat to slightly negative (-0.7% for 2026, -0.1% for 2027). This pattern suggests analysts are shifting quarterly timing expectations without upgrading the annual trajectory. The 90-day revision for full-year estimates is essentially zero (-0.2% for 2026, +0.3% for 2027), confirming no earnings momentum at the annual level.

## Valuation Assessment

At $50.78 and 2026 midpoint guidance of $3.43, POR trades at approximately 14.8x forward adjusted earnings. The peer median P/E is 19.6x (MGEE), with the range spanning from 13.2x (OTTR) to 43.8x (TXNM).

The discount is warranted by:
1. **Dilution trajectory**: 10-15% additional shares coming over 2 years degrades per-share value
2. **Regulatory headwinds**: Actual ROE of 7.4% vs. authorized 9.34% represents persistent under-earning
3. **Acquisition execution risk**: $1.9B deal introduces new regulatory jurisdiction and integration complexity
4. **Consecutive earnings misses**: -26% and -35% vs. consensus in Q4 2025 and Q1 2026

The consensus median target of $53.00 implies only 4.4% total appreciation. Combined with the 4.3% dividend yield, the total return expectation is approximately 8-9% — marginally adequate for a utility in a benign environment, but insufficient given the specific risk profile.

The stock's 19.2% price appreciation over the past year reflects the November 2025 rally (driven by the acquisition announcement and rate case approval), much of which has since given back following consecutive earnings misses. The current price sits at the middle of the 52-week range ($39.55-$54.62).

## Competitive Landscape

PGE operates in a period of structurally rising utility demand from data center buildout. However, Oregon's regulatory response (forcing data centers to pay full allocation costs) may limit PGE's ability to capture disproportionate share of this growth. States like Texas, Virginia, and Georgia offer more favorable cost-allocation frameworks for hyperscalers.

The company's competitive position within Oregon is secure (monopoly franchise), but the growth opportunity is constrained by regulatory philosophy. PGE's participation in the Extended Day-Ahead Market (EDAM) through CAISO should provide modest cost savings and access to regional resource diversity.

The PacifiCorp acquisition creates optionality: multi-state regulatory diversification, a FERC-jurisdictional transmission subsidiary, and access to Washington's different rate-making framework. But this optionality requires flawless execution over an 18-month timeline, and Washington's regulatory body (WUTC) may impose its own conditions.

Relative to peers, PGE has underperformed on total shareholder return (138 vs. 153 for peer group index) over the measurement period, reflecting the dilution burden and regulatory friction that other utilities have avoided.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Persistent regulatory under-earning** — The gap between actual ROE (7.4%) and authorized (9.34%) represents an annual shortfall that compounds. New HB 3179 multiyear rate plan constraints may further limit recovery.

2. **Dilution exceeding rate base growth** — If new equity issuance grows shares by 12% while rate base grows by 10%, EPS declines. This math requires precise execution and full regulatory recovery to work.

3. **Acquisition failure or delay** — FERC/OPUC/WUTC all must approve. The $35M termination fee is minimal compared to the opportunity cost and transaction expenses already incurred.

4. **IRA/OBBB repeal impact** — Loss of Renewable Energy Credits on future projects directly increases the cost of meeting HB 2021 mandates. PGE needs 3,500-4,500 MW of clean capacity; without tax credits, customer rates rise faster, creating affordability backlash.

5. **Wildfire exposure without California-style liability cap** — Oregon lacks a wildfire fund. PGE spends ~$120-130M annually on wildfire mitigation ($47-50M O&M plus $70-84M capex) but carries no liability ceiling.

6. **Portland Harbor Superfund** — Total site costs estimated at $1.9B-$3.5B across 100+ PRPs. PGE's ultimate share is unknown but potentially material.

**Options Market Signal:**

Near-term ATM IV of 21.9% sits 3.5pp above 1-year historical volatility of 18.4%, suggesting the market prices slightly elevated uncertainty related to the recent earnings miss and acquisition timeline. Put/call OI ratio of 0.33 indicates call-heavy positioning, but reflects low absolute options volume (only 23 contracts across 3 expirations). This is too illiquid for meaningful directional inference. The flat term structure indicates no specific catalyst is being priced in the near term.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($56-62)**

- Data center demand resumes acceleration despite cost-allocation tariffs
- OPUC grants favorable storm/RCE deferral outcomes, narrowing the ROE gap
- Rate base growth of 7-8% CAGR outpaces dilution, delivering 5-7% EPS growth
- Utility sector re-rates higher as economic slowdown drives demand for defensive yield

**Bear Case ($40-45)**

- Load growth stalls as data centers choose other states; guidance cut further
- Acquisition delayed or conditioned adversely by WUTC/FERC
- 30Y rates remain above 5%, making the 4.3% utility yield less competitive
- Wildfire event or Superfund allocation creates material unbudgeted liability
- Continued EPS misses erode credibility of 5-7% growth framework

Next earnings (Q2 2026, likely late July/early August) could disappoint again given lowered load growth guidance, but this is speculative. **Borrow and options signal:** Options market too illiquid (23 contracts) for meaningful signal. Cost-to-borrow likely minimal given moderate SI.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** Bear case implies $40-45 (-12 to -21% from $50.78). Upside risk to $56 (+10%). Risk/reward approximately 1.5:1 to 2:1, marginal.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE** — No specific near-term catalyst with clear timing. The thesis is "mediocre utility with compounding dilution and regulatory friction" which plays out slowly, not in a sharp decline. The 4.3% dividend creates carrying cost for shorts. This is an avoid, not an active short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria:**
- If the stock declines to $42-44 (8.2x-8.5x EV/EBITDA or ~12.5x forward earnings), the yield would approach 5%+ and the risk/reward would improve materially
- If Q2 or Q3 2026 earnings demonstrate restored execution (beat consensus by 5%+), narrowing of the actual vs. authorized ROE gap
- If OPUC grants full or substantially full recovery on storm/RCE deferrals, signaling a more constructive regulatory relationship
- If the PacifiCorp acquisition receives all regulatory approvals with clean conditions

**Timeline:** These headwinds persist for at least 12-18 months. The acquisition doesn't close until mid-2027, meaning the dilution + integration uncertainty overhang continues through that period. Reassess after Q3 2026 earnings (late October) and after OPUC deferral orders issue.

## Conclusion

Portland General Electric is executing an ambitious capital-intensive growth strategy in a regulatory environment that is increasingly difficult. The combination of consecutive earnings misses, persistent under-earning relative to authorized returns, heavy ongoing dilution, and a complex acquisition with an 18-month close timeline creates a risk profile that the current 4.3% yield and 14.8x forward multiple do not adequately compensate.

The prior HOLD recommendation is no longer supportable: there is no specific catalyst or growth thesis on a 12-month horizon that provides confidence in appreciation. The acquisition won't close or contribute until mid-2027. Load growth guidance has been cut. Regulatory relationships are strained. Estimate revisions at the annual level are flat to negative. The scoring trajectory has declined from 5.9 to 5.1 over the past month, and the underlying fundamentals confirm this deterioration.

The correct posture is to avoid new positions and wait for either a more attractive entry point ($42-44 range) or evidence of restored execution through earnings delivery and regulatory outcomes.
