## Executive Summary

Perdoceo Education Corporation operates three accredited postsecondary institutions serving primarily non-traditional adult learners, with 91% online delivery. The company generates extraordinary free cash flow ($216.7M, ~25.6% FCF margin) on virtually zero conventional financial debt, creating an approximately 10.3% FCF yield at the current $33.63 price. Guided 2026 adjusted EPS of $2.97–$3.12 places the stock at roughly 11x forward earnings — a meaningful discount to for-profit education peers — while the company returns approximately 8% of market cap annually through buybacks and dividends.

The central tension is between the quality of the underlying economics and the regulatory overhang endemic to for-profit education. The most material near-term risk is the elimination of Grad PLUS loans for USAHS programs effective July 1, 2026, which could impair approximately 19% of revenue if students cannot find alternative financing. However, the valuation already discounts substantial regulatory risk, and the balance sheet ($643.5M cash, minimal conventional debt) provides ample cushion to absorb transitional headwinds. CTU's 39.1% operating margin and 38.3% corporate engagement penetration represent a high-quality core business that generates returns well above its cost of capital regardless of USAHS outcomes.

## Company Overview

Perdoceo operates three institutions: Colorado Technical University (CTU, $462M revenue, 39.1% operating margin), American InterContinental University System (AIUS, $226M, 15.9% margin), and University of St. Augustine for Health Sciences (USAHS, $158M, 2.0% margin — acquired December 2024). Total enrollment is approximately 44,400 students. The business model is distinguished by minimal capital requirements (~1% of revenue in capex), heavy online delivery enabling scalability, and a corporate engagement program at CTU that provides 38.3% of students through employer-sponsored channels — which management believes reduces marketing costs and improves student retention.

The company participates in Title IV federal student aid programs, which generated $615M in cash receipts in 2025 (approximately 73% of total revenue on a cash-receipts basis). This dependency on federal funding creates both the regulatory risk that discounts the stock and the high barriers to entry that protect incumbent operators.

## Financial Analysis

**Income Statement:**
Revenue grew 24.2% to $846M in 2025, primarily driven by the USAHS acquisition ($147.5M contribution). Organic growth was modest: CTU +4.1%, AIUS -0.4%. Adjusted operating income reached $237.6M, with GAAP operating income of $196.5M (23.2% margin, compressed from 25.6% by USAHS integration costs and $30.3M in acquisition-related D&A). GAAP EPS of $2.42; adjusted EPS of $2.61.

**Cash Flow:**
Operating cash flow of $225.2M converted to free cash flow of approximately $216.7M (CFO minus capex of $8.6M), reflecting the asset-light model. FCF margin of ~25.6% is exceptional for any sector. Capex of $8.6M (~1% of revenue) is among the lowest capital intensity ratios in publicly traded companies of this scale.

**Balance Sheet:**
Cash and investments of $643.5M vs. long-term conventional debt of only $6.1M. The reported D/E of 1.28 includes operating lease liabilities and a $56.5M failed sale-leaseback financing obligation from USAHS campuses (with future minimum payments of $154.1M), plus finance-lease obligations. Excluding these non-traditional financing obligations, the business carries minimal conventional debt. Current ratio of 5.06x. Financial responsibility composite score of 3.0 (maximum). Altman Z-Score of 3.29 (safe zone).

**Capital Return:**
$120.8M in share repurchases in 2025 at an average of ~$29.17/share (below current price). New $100M buyback authorized January 2026. Quarterly dividend of $0.15/share ($36.9M annualized). Combined return to shareholders of ~$158M in 2025, or approximately 7.5% of current market cap.

**2026 Guidance:**
Adjusted operating income of $250M–$263M (up 5-11%). Adjusted EPS of $2.97–$3.12 (up 14-20%). Tax rate improvement to 23.5-24.5% from 26.3% provides a tailwind. Q1 2026 guided to $0.78–$0.80 GAAP EPS ($0.83–$0.85 adjusted EPS). Based on available consensus data, Q4 2025 results appeared to exceed expectations, suggesting conservative guidance patterns.

## Consumer Discretionary BUY Gate Conditions

Addressing the elevated requirements for Consumer Discretionary BUY recommendations:

**(a) Positive sequential comparable revenue growth:** CTU revenue grew 4.1% YoY organically. Total enrollment increased 7.3% YoY to 44,400 students. Bad debt expense improved from 4.9% to 3.5% of revenue, indicating better student quality and retention. Corporate engagement students grew as a proportion of CTU enrollment. Year-over-year trends confirm improving momentum across key operating metrics.

**(b) FCF yield ≥ 4%:** FCF of ~$216.7M / market cap of ~$2.11B = **approximately 10.3% FCF yield**. This exceeds the threshold by nearly 2.5x.

**(c) Consumer confidence not in declining trend:** While consumer confidence broadly is pressured (Michigan 53.3), Perdoceo's student base is predominantly working adults over 30 (68%) seeking career advancement, many sponsored by employers (38.3% of CTU). Education spending decisions by this cohort are driven by employment market conditions rather than consumer sentiment — enrollment at for-profit career-focused institutions historically increases during economic uncertainty as workers seek credentials for job security. The company's Q4 beat and enrollment growth confirm demand resilience.

**(d) Positive estimate revisions over trailing 90 days:** Current year EPS estimate revised from $2.78 to $3.05 (+9.7% over 90 days). Only one analyst covers the name, but the revision direction is unambiguously positive and aligns with management's own upward guidance trajectory.

## Growth Analysis

Organic growth is modest but positive. CTU's 4.1% revenue growth and improving enrollment metrics (7.3% total growth YoY) demonstrate the business can expand without acquisitions. The USAHS acquisition provides a structural growth lever through health sciences programs in structurally undersupplied fields (physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech-language pathology). However, the Grad PLUS elimination creates a genuine 12-month headwind to USAHS growth — management is working on private financing alternatives but visibility is limited.

Forward estimates of $3.05 (2026) and $3.27 (2027) imply 17% and 7% EPS growth respectively. The 2026 number is driven by tax rate improvement, full-year USAHS contribution, and continued buybacks. The 2027 estimate likely reflects a conservative baseline pending Grad PLUS resolution.

Corporate engagement penetration at CTU (38.3% and growing) provides a differentiated growth channel with lower customer acquisition costs and better outcomes — creating a virtuous cycle for regulatory metrics (graduation rates, employment rates, loan repayment) that protect institutional eligibility.

Peer comparisons based on available data suggest PRDO trades at a meaningful discount to for-profit education peers including STRA, LRN, and LOPE, though exact peer multiples vary by data source and date. The discount reflects regulatory risk, but the magnitude appears excessive given: (a) the company's demonstrated regulatory compliance (full recertification through June 2027), (b) a favorable political environment (Trump Administration deregulatory posture), and (c) balance sheet strength enabling management through any transition.

The board explicitly stated in January 2026 that shares are below "intrinsic value" and authorized $100M in additional repurchases — providing price support and signaling management's own view of fair value.

## Competitive Landscape

Perdoceo competes in a fragmented $796B market across ~5,600 institutions. Its competitive advantages are operational rather than brand-based:

1. **Cost structure:** 91% online delivery + 1% capex ratio creates industry-leading operating leverage.
2. **Corporate engagement:** 38.3% of CTU students through employer partnerships provides a lower-cost, higher-quality enrollment channel that most competitors cannot replicate at scale.
3. **Proprietary technology:** intellipath® personalized learning platform and AI-enhanced engagement tools create switching costs and outcome differentiation.
4. **Regulatory navigation:** Full (non-provisional) recertification and 3.0 financial responsibility score demonstrate institutional credibility.

Competitive pressures include traditional universities expanding online programs, free community college initiatives, and regulatory arbitrage by competitors converting to non-profit/servicer models. These are real but incremental — the 91% online, working-adult model serves a specific need that traditional institutions structurally underserve.

## Risk Assessment

**Grad PLUS Elimination (HIGH):** Effective July 1, 2026 for new USAHS borrowers. Annual borrowing limits of $20,500 vs. program costs significantly higher. USAHS represents ~19% of revenue. Impact bounded by: (a) existing students grandfathered, (b) private financing alternatives being developed, (c) healthcare workforce shortage supporting demand regardless. Worst case: USAHS enrollment declines 30-40% over 2-3 years, representing ~$50-60M revenue at risk (approximately $1-1.2M in operating income given current 2% margins, though contribution-margin impact could be higher if fixed costs cannot be reduced proportionally).

**Title IV Dependency (MODERATE):** Title IV cash receipts represent approximately 76% of aggregate tuition cash receipts. The company disclosed that its formal 90/10 Rule calculations differ from this percentage due to prescribed regulatory treatment of various funding types and student-level limitations. Perdoceo stated that CTU, AIUS, and USAHS were each in preliminary compliance for 2025 and that calculations improved from the prior year, but did not disclose actual 90/10 percentages. Corporate engagement provides a natural hedge against this metric tightening further, as employer tuition assistance is not classified as federal education assistance.

**False Claims Act Litigation (MODERATE):** The 2025 10-K discloses at least two active False Claims Act matters involving CTU/Perdoceo in which the DOJ declined to intervene but relators continued independently. One alleges issues related to federal financial aid credit-hour requirements; another, with an amended complaint filed January 2026, alleges incentive compensation and misrepresentation violations related to Title IV programs. While the DOJ's decision not to intervene reduces the probability of adverse outcomes, FCA cases can result in material damages if successful.

**Regulatory Whiplash (MODERATE):** Current administration favorable, but education regulation swings dramatically between parties. The 2028 election cycle introduces uncertainty beyond the current investment horizon.

**Insider Selling (LOW-MODERATE):** CEO and CFO sold shares in March-April 2026 at $34.86-$38.00, coinciding with equity vesting. No open-market purchases. Given the +9.7% estimate revisions and company guidance increase, these appear routine compensation dispositions rather than conviction sales. CEO retains ~$11.7M in shares.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($42-45):** CTU continues 4-5% organic growth, USAHS navigates Grad PLUS transition with private financing alternatives (10-15% enrollment impact vs. feared 30-40%), buybacks retire 5-6% of shares annually at below-fair-value prices, tax rate improvement flows through to $3.10+ EPS, and the stock re-rates toward peer multiples. At 14x $3.15 EPS = $44.

**Bear Case ($25-28):** Grad PLUS elimination impairs USAHS more severely than expected (40%+ enrollment decline), broader regulatory tightening under earnings-premium accountability test disqualifies some programs at CTU/AIUS, and enrollment declines accelerate across all segments. EPS contracts to $2.20-$2.40 and the multiple compresses to 11-12x. At 11.5x $2.30 = $26.50.

**Base Case ($38-42):** CTU grows mid-single digits, USAHS experiences 15-20% enrollment headwind that is partially offset by margin improvement as acquisition D&A amortizes down, buybacks retire 4-5% of float annually, and the stock trades at 12.5-13.5x on $3.05 EPS. At 12.5x $3.05 = $38; at 13.5x $3.05 = $41.

## Conclusion

Perdoceo offers a rare combination: a business generating ~25.6% FCF margins on minimal conventional debt, trading at 11x forward earnings with ~10.3% FCF yield, and actively returning capital through buybacks and dividends. The regulatory discount embedded in for-profit education names creates asymmetric opportunity when the underlying business is well-run, as PRDO's improving bad debt ratios, enrollment growth, and maximum financial responsibility score confirm.

The Grad PLUS risk to USAHS is real and bounded — at worst, it threatens ~$50-60M in revenue and modest operating income (2% margins) while the $643M cash balance and CTU's 39.1% margins provide shock absorption. The stock's re-rating toward peer multiples requires only modest resolution of this uncertainty rather than its complete elimination.

At current prices, the risk/reward skews favorably: 17-31% upside in base/bull scenarios vs. 21% downside in the bear case, with the company shrinking its float by 4-5% annually regardless of scenario. Position sizing should reflect the binary Grad PLUS catalyst (July 2026) and limited analyst coverage (single analyst), but the fundamental case supports a BUY at ~2-3% portfolio weight.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Corrected EV/EBIT (~6.2x vs. originally stated 9.4x) significantly strengthens rather than weakens the valuation case; however, the BUY gate for "positive sequential comparable revenue growth" is not clearly satisfied by the available data (Q4 revenue was slightly below Q3), which warrants investor awareness.
