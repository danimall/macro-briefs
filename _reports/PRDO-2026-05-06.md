## Executive Summary

Perdoceo Education Corporation is a for-profit postsecondary education provider generating $846M in annual revenue across three institutions: Colorado Technical University (CTU), American InterContinental University System (AIUS), and University of St. Augustine for Health Sciences (USAHS). The company's financial profile is exceptional for its sector: $643.5M in liquid assets against minimal debt, approximately 25.6% FCF margins, and a core segment (CTU) operating at 39% margins with minimal capital requirements.

At $34.75/share, the stock trades at approximately 10x free cash flow and 14x trailing earnings, with net cash representing a substantial portion of market capitalization. Enrollment grew 7.3% YoY to 44,400 students, with CTU corporate partnerships (38% of CTU enrollment) providing a low-cost, high-retention acquisition channel. The Board authorized a new $100M buyback in January 2026; the 10-K notes the Board has approved repurchase programs believing it advantageous to repurchase shares "from time to time at prices below" what it believes to be intrinsic value.

The primary risk is the Grad PLUS loan elimination effective July 1, 2026, which will constrain federal student loan availability for new USAHS graduate students. USAHS contributes only 2% operating margins (burdened by $30.3M acquisition-related D&A), but the fixed-cost structure of its physical campuses means the earnings impact of a severe enrollment decline could be materially larger than the low margin rate alone would suggest. The combination of fortress balance sheet, strong cash generation, compressed valuation, and counter-cyclical demand characteristics (enrollment rises during economic weakness) supports a BUY recommendation with a $42 base case target.

## Company Overview

Perdoceo operates three accredited institutions serving primarily non-traditional adult learners (68% over age 30) through predominantly online delivery (91% of students fully online):

- **CTU** (~55% of revenue, $461.6M): Career-oriented programs in business, nursing, healthcare management, computer science, cybersecurity. 39.1% operating margin. 38% of enrollments through employer-sponsored corporate programs.
- **AIUS** (~27% of revenue, $226.2M): Business, IT, education, behavioral sciences through AIU, Trident University, and California Southern University. 15.9% operating margin.
- **USAHS** (~19% of revenue, $157.6M): Acquired December 2024 for $137.8M. Graduate health sciences (physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech-language pathology, nursing). Physical campus locations in California, Florida, Texas. 2.0% operating margin (burdened by $30.3M acquisition D&A).

The business model is capital-light (capex ~1% of revenue), scalable through online delivery, and benefits from proprietary intellipath® adaptive learning technology. Total enrollment reached 44,400 students at year-end 2025, up 7.3% YoY.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Grad PLUS Loan Elimination (Effective July 1, 2026) — HIGH:**
The Reconciliation Act eliminates Grad PLUS loans for new borrowers starting July 1, 2026. USAHS graduate students in DPT, OTD, SLP, and nursing programs will face substantially reduced federal loan availability. For programs classified as non-professional graduate programs, new students will be capped at $20,500/year in Direct Unsubsidized loans (aggregate $100,000) — far below cost of attendance. Programs that may qualify as "professional" degrees could have higher limits ($50,000 annual / $200,000 aggregate), but USAHS's program classification under the new framework remains uncertain. USAHS is actively directing students to private loan alternatives. Enrollment impact is uncertain but potentially severe for new cohorts.

**Downside modeling:** USAHS had FY2025 revenue of $157.6M and operating income of $3.2M (2.0% margin). The low full-year margin reflects $30.3M of acquisition-related D&A and early integration costs. USAHS's fixed-cost base — including physical campus infrastructure, clinical placement obligations, faculty, and non-cash D&A — means that a severe enrollment decline would not simply reduce profit proportionally to the margin rate. A 50% revenue decline could eliminate USAHS operating income entirely and potentially generate operating losses, as fixed costs would be spread over a much smaller enrollment base. The potential for goodwill impairment ($266M consolidated goodwill, a significant portion attributable to USAHS) adds further downside risk. The total earnings impact of a worst-case USAHS scenario could be materially larger than $1.6M, though still bounded relative to CTU's $180.6M contribution.

**2. USAHS Provisional Program Participation Agreement — MODERATE:**
USAHS is operating under a temporary provisional program participation agreement following the change of ownership (December 2024 acquisition). If the Department of Education does not approve the change of ownership on a permanent basis, USAHS could face severe operational constraints including potential loss of Title IV eligibility for that institution. The letter of credit requirement was lifted in January 2026, which signals positive regulatory trajectory, but full approval remains pending.

**3. 90-10 Rule Compliance — MODERATE:**
~76-77% of institutional cash receipts come from Title IV. The expanded rule now includes VA benefits and military TA. Trident University (within AIUS) serves heavy military populations. Two consecutive years of violation = loss of Title IV eligibility, which would be catastrophic. Current compliance is achieved but proximity to thresholds warrants monitoring.

**4. Title IV Dependency — STRUCTURAL:**
$615M in Title IV funds received in 2025. Any legislative reduction, change in eligibility, or enforcement action would be existential. The new $257,500 lifetime borrowing cap adds incremental constraint. This is the fundamental risk of the for-profit education business model.

**5. Borrower Defense to Repayment — LOW-MODERATE:**
"Several thousand" pending BDR applications exist. The Reconciliation Act delayed the 2022 BDR regulations to 2035. The Sweet Settlement discharged loans for 150+ schools including AIUS and CTU without standard adjudication, creating precedent risk under future administrations.

**6. Political Regime Change — LONG-TERM:**
The current administration's regulatory posture has been favorable to for-profit education — actions include rescinding costly regulations, removing AIUS from provisional status, and releasing USAHS from letter of credit/HCM1 status. A future Democratic administration could reimpose aggressive regulation. This is a multi-year risk that periodically compresses sector multiples.

- USAHS margin normalizes to 8-12% as acquisition D&A rolls off and private loan alternatives prove viable
- Management deploys $100M+ in buybacks, reducing share count 3-5% annually

**Bear Case ($26-28):**
- Grad PLUS elimination causes 40%+ USAHS enrollment decline, triggering write-down of $266M goodwill
- USAHS provisional agreement denied, requiring divestiture at distressed value
- 90-10 Rule violation at one institution triggers broader Title IV eligibility concerns

**Probability-weighted assessment:** Given CTU's dominant contribution to profitability (92% of consolidated operating income), the bear case requires failure of the core business — not merely USAHS impairment. The USAHS downside is more substantial than the 2% margin alone suggests, given fixed costs and potential impairment. A severe USAHS scenario could reduce consolidated EPS by $0.30-0.50 (including potential impairment charges), while the upside from continued CTU momentum and capital return still exceeds this exposure by a significant margin.

## Conclusion

Perdoceo presents a compelling risk/reward at current levels. The core CTU business operates at 39% margins with growing enrollment, minimal capital needs, and a corporate partnership channel that provides structural retention advantages. The balance sheet ($525-632M net cash depending on treatment of lease/financing obligations) provides both downside protection and optionality for capital return or acquisitions.

The Grad PLUS elimination and USAHS provisional agreement represent genuine risks that are more material than the 2% segment margin alone suggests, given USAHS's fixed-cost campus infrastructure and potential goodwill impairment exposure. The market appears to be discounting the entire company for a problem concentrated in an acquisition that contributes a small fraction of consolidated profits.

BUY with a $42 base case target representing 21% upside over 12 months, supported by continued buybacks, tax rate improvement, and potential USAHS margin normalization.
