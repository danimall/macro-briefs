## Executive Summary

O-I Glass reported a Q2 2026 GAAP loss of $972M (−$6.33 per share) on July 28, wrote off the entirety of its European goodwill ($873M), established a $96M valuation allowance against European deferred tax assets, and cut guidance for the second time in five months. The 2027 adjusted EBITDA target fell to $1.2–1.3B (a filing fact in the 10-Q) from a previously reported ~$1.45B; 2026 operating cash flow guidance fell to $275–375M from roughly $650M in February. Against ~$425M of guided capex, that implies free cash flow of negative $50M to negative $150M for the full year. The stock fell 17% on the print and now sits at $7.03, 58% below its February high of $16.91.

The mechanism is specific and geographic. Americas is executing: Q2 segment operating profit rose 22% to $165M on a 17.4% margin *despite* volumes down 7%, driven by $36M of price and $19M of Fit to Win savings. Europe collapsed: H1 segment operating profit fell from $158M to $6M, and backing out $12M of joint-venture equity earnings that sit inside segment profit, O-I's wholly-owned European operations were roughly break-even to slightly loss-making in the half. The driver is a −$161M H1 net price-versus-cost variance attributable to the expiry of favorable energy contracts at end-2025, additional Middle East-driven energy inflation, and — critically — an inability to pass either through in a market O-I itself describes as suffering "low-capacity utilization."

The prior rating on this name moved from AVOID to HOLD in July at roughly $9. That upgrade rested on credit-structure work that remains valid: the single financial maintenance covenant tests only *secured* leverage (approximately 1.2x against a 2.50x limit), ~62% of the $4,986M debt stack is unsecured and outside the test, the nearest note maturity is 2028, and the 2028/2029 notes traded above par at quarter-end. What the July view assumed and Q2 disproved was that earnings power was stabilizing. Consensus for the current year has been cut 53.8% in 90 days (to $0.56 from $1.22) and next year 20.7% (to $1.50 from $1.89), after two consecutive quarters of EPS misses of 66% and 72%. With equity worth only ~18% of a ~$5.9B enterprise value, a 10% move in EV moves the equity roughly 55%. The 2027 energy year is the open exposure — only 75–80% of *2026* European gas is secured at favorable rates — and the macro backdrop for European gas is deteriorating, not improving. We downgrade to REDUCE.

## Company Overview

O-I Glass manufactures glass containers from 64 plants across 18 countries, reporting in two geographic segments (Americas, Europe) plus retained corporate costs. Q2 2026 reportable segment revenue of $1,653M split into alcoholic beverages $1,012M (61.0%, down from 63.4% a year earlier), food and other $363M (+7.7%), and non-alcoholic beverages $278M (−0.4%).

Geographic concentration is high and non-U.S. weighted: $2,373M of $3,207M H1 net sales (74%) came from outside the U.S., with France 12%, Italy 12% and Mexico 14% each above the disclosure threshold. Mexico's share rose from 13% a year earlier. The business also holds 50% interests in Italian and Mexican joint ventures, carried at $710M of equity investments at June 30.

The moat is conventional heavy-industry economics: freight-limited shipping radii, high furnace capital intensity, long customer qualification cycles. The differentiation program that would have changed that — MAGMA modular melting — was halted in Q2 2025 with a $117M full-year charge. R&D expense has fallen from $92M (FY2023) to $80M (FY2024) to $42M (FY2025) to $19M in H1 2026. What remains is a levered cost-reduction and capacity-rationalization story in a market with declining volumes.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue has declined four consecutive years.** Net sales: $6,856M (FY2022) → $7,105M (FY2023) → $6,531M (FY2024) → $6,426M (FY2025) → $3,207M (H1 2026, −2% y/y). GAAP net losses attributable to the company in FY2023 (−$103M), FY2024 (−$106M), FY2025 (−$129M) and H1 2026 (−$1,046M).

The H1 top line is more fragile than −2% suggests. The MD&A bridge shows tons shipped down ~7% (~6% excluding a divestiture), reducing sales $215M; price contributed −$9M; and FX translation added +$179M. Ex-FX, underlying revenue fell roughly 7%. June volumes were flat year over year, the single sequential positive in the release.

**Segment divergence is the whole story:**

| Segment operating profit | Q2 2025 | Q2 2026 | H1 2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Americas | $135M | $165M | $276M | $307M |
| Europe | $90M | $6M | $158M | $6M |
| Total reportable | $225M | $171M | $434M | $313M |

Europe carries 42% of reportable revenue and contributed 1.9% of H1 segment operating profit. Removing the $12M of JV equity earnings embedded in Europe's H1 segment line (reproducible from Note 1: 1,359 − 1,294 − 72 + 12 + 1 = 6) leaves approximately −$6M from consolidated European operations; the Q2 equivalent is approximately $0M. Equity earnings are JV income whose cash conversion depends on distributions, not controllable plant cash flow.

**Fit to Win produces gross savings that its own execution consumes.** H1 benefits were $91M, "consistent with management's expectations," offset by approximately $71M of temporary production curtailments, furnace events and operational disruptions following plant restructuring — a net contribution of roughly $20M. Europe alone: $54M of benefits against ~$41M of offsets, including two furnace events. Cumulative program charges have reached ~$700M (from $646M at year-end and $396M a year earlier), and management "does not yet have an estimate for the total restructuring charges to be incurred." The 2026 benefit target was cut from ≥$275M to ~$200M, and the cumulative-through-2027 figure from ≥$750M to ≥$650M.

**Cash flow deteriorated sharply, and the first half must not be annualized.** H1 operating cash flow was −$200M (vs. −$16M in H1 2025) on a $381M working capital outflow and $90M of restructuring payments; capex was $237M, so H1 FCF was approximately −$437M. Cash fell from $759M to $339M and the revolver was drawn $130M from zero. O-I's cash generation is heavily second-half weighted — FY2025 OCF of $600M against H1 2025 of −$16M implies H2 2025 of roughly $616M. Revised FY2026 guidance of $275–375M therefore implies H2 2026 of $475–575M, *below* the prior-year second half. That is internally consistent with a worse year, not a hockey stick. Guidance-implied FY2026 FCF is −$150M to −$50M, against +$168M in FY2025.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt of $4,986M against total equity (including $156M NCI) of $539M. Net debt of $4,647M rose approximately $407M in six months. Company-only equity of $383M implies book value per share of ~$2.49, so at $7.03 the stock trades at ~2.8x book — this is not a below-book asset story after the impairment. Tangible book value is negative approximately $403M ($383M less $608M goodwill less $178M intangibles). Retained earnings swung from +$548M to an accumulated deficit of $497M.

Enterprise value is approximately $5.89B ($1,083M equity + $4,647M net debt + $156M NCI). Equity is 18.4% of EV.

**Note on reported multiples:** the data feed shows trailing and forward P/E of −0.94 against EPS of −$0.84, which do not reconcile at $7.03 (that EPS implies −8.4x) and neither reflects the H1 2026 GAAP loss of roughly −$6.79 per share. The −$0.84 figure appears to be FY2025 GAAP EPS. GAAP earnings-based multiples are unusable here; enterprise and cash-flow metrics are the only workable basis.

**Marginal cost of capital is the cleanest signal in the filings.** In May 2026 O-I issued $500M of 9.500% notes due 2033 to redeem $612M of 6.625% notes due 2027. Old coupon: $40.5M/year. New coupon: $47.5M/year. Annual cash interest rose ~$7.0M while retiring $112M *more* principal — a 287.5bp step-up. The $112M gap appears bridged by the revolver draw. Instrument-level bond prices at June 30 show 2028s at 101.61 and 2029s at 101.76, while 2030s (94.99), 2031s (98.82) and 2032s (96.05) trade at discounts: the credit market is pricing long-run earnings uncertainty, not imminent default.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth thesis here. Volumes fell 7% in the half. Alcoholic beverages, 61% of reportable revenue, is the weakest category, with the 10-Q citing soft beer, wine and spirits demand, spirits customer inventory adjustments, and shipments to Southern European wine customers as most pronounced weakness. Food and other grew 7.7%, which modestly improves mix but is not a growth engine at $363M of quarterly revenue.

The forward case is a margin-recovery case: 2027 adjusted EBITDA of $1.2–1.3B against roughly $1.0–1.1B implied for 2026 (externally reported, not verified in the filings I reviewed). The 10-Q describes the 2027 assumption set as "a slower recovery path in Europe, including continued commercial pressure, gradual improvement in industry utilization rates, elevated energy costs, and the revised Fit to Win outlook as the Company attempts to resolve European operational disruptions." A target predicated on the company attempting to resolve its own operational disruptions, set five months after the prior target was abandoned, does not warrant treatment as a base case.

Estimate revisions are the sharpest available forward indicator and they point one direction: current quarter −54.4% (30d) and −55.4% (90d); current year −52.9% and −53.8% to $0.56; next year −20.9% and −20.7% to $1.50. Nine analysts contribute to the annual figures. Note the 30-day and 90-day moves are nearly identical, meaning essentially the entire cut landed on the Q2 print rather than accumulating gradually — consensus is now recalibrated to the new guidance, but the revision *velocity* remains negative and 2027 energy is unhedged.

Two of the last four quarters missed, by 66.2% and 71.8% on the adjusted line. The prior two quarters were a 2.4% in-line result and a 10.4% beat. Visibility deteriorated abruptly.

## Valuation Assessment

On enterprise metrics O-I looks inexpensive. EV/2027E target EBITDA of $1.25B midpoint is approximately 4.7x; EV/2026E EBITDA at the externally reported $1.05B midpoint is approximately 5.6x. Net debt/2026E EBITDA is approximately 4.4x. Next-year consensus EPS of $1.50 puts the stock at 4.7x. Peer medians in the container group sit at 19.9x P/E (GEF 21.5x, SLGN 15.1x, ATR 19.9x, BALL 16.4x) — but those are unlevered-by-comparison balance sheets with positive free cash flow and dividends; O-I pays no dividend and is guided to negative FCF this year. The discount is not a mispricing to be arbitraged; it is compensation for 4.4x leverage on an EBITDA number that has been revised down twice.

The illustrative 2027 cash bridge is the bull case in one line: adjusted EBITDA $1,250M less cash interest ~$345M less capex ~$425M less cash taxes ~$120M less residual restructuring cash ~$50–100M ≈ $260–310M, or a 24–29% free cash flow yield on today's $1.08B market cap. That arithmetic is correct and it explains why the equity is not zero. It is also entirely contingent on a target already cut twice, uses 2026 capex as a 2027 proxy the company has not guided, and assumes 2027 European energy lands within plan when only 2026 volumes are substantially hedged and the hedge structure contains an undisclosed price cap above which O-I bears full incremental cost.

Because equity is 18% of EV, small EBITDA and multiple changes swing the equity violently. Scenario arithmetic on 153.7M shares:

- **Bull ($13.00, +85%):** 2027 EBITDA $1.25B, net debt held near $4.60B, 5.5x EV/EBITDA → $6.88B EV less $4.60B net debt less $0.156B NCI = $2.12B equity = $13.8/share. Anchored to the analyst high of $13.
- **Base ($5.75, −18%):** 2027 EBITDA $1.10B (below the guided range, consistent with two prior cuts and open 2027 energy), net debt $4.75B after negative 2026 FCF, 5.25x → $5.78B EV less $4.75B less $0.156B = $0.87B = $5.65/share.
- **Bear ($2.50, −64%):** 2027 EBITDA $0.95B, net debt $4.90B, 4.75x → $4.51B EV, below net debt plus NCI. Equity retains option value only, and the 2028 refinancing would then be priced off a distressed curve.

Probability-weighting 25% bull / 40% base / 35% bear yields approximately $6.43, an expected return of −8.6% from $7.03. Even a more generous 30/40/30 split yields roughly $6.95 — flat expected return with a fat left tail. Consensus targets (low $8.00, mean $9.94, median $10.00, high $13.00) all sit above spot, but sentiment is classified as degrading, one strong-sell rating appeared in the last two months, and the distribution of targets is unlikely to have fully absorbed the July 28 guidance cut.

Valuation scores 4.5: genuinely cheap on enterprise metrics with real upside optionality, but the denominator is unstable, the equity has no margin of safety as a thin residual claim, and cheapness on a twice-cut forecast is the classic pattern of a value trap rather than a mispricing.

## Competitive Landscape

The most useful competitive datapoint is O-I's own language: Europe faces "an increasingly competitive market backdrop, which pressured price amid low-capacity utilization." That is a statement about industry conditions and it identifies the transmission mechanism — in a low-utilization market, producers cannot pass through cost inflation, so an energy shock becomes a margin shock. The 2027 guidance assumes only "gradual improvement in industry utilization rates."

The internal contrast is the strongest available evidence on whether the problem is structural or company-specific. In H1 2026, with the same management and the same restructuring program: Americas price contributed +$59M to sales and +$32M to profit net of inflation, while Europe price contributed −$68M to sales and −$161M to profit net of inflation. Two regions, opposite outcomes. The most probable reading is that the European problem is predominantly market structure — excess industry capacity and competitor pricing behavior. A partly overlapping alternative also fits the disclosures: O-I's own plant closures produced operational disruptions and two furnace events in Europe, and the company may be conceding price to hold volume it cannot dependably serve. The filings support both and do not allow apportionment.

Structural headwinds are real but industry-wide: EU ETS free allocation phases out 2026–2034 alongside CBAM (Europe recognized ~$28M of allowance expense in FY2025), and substitution toward aluminum cans, plastic and pouches is named in the 10-K along with customer-base consolidation. Rising European industry cost floors argue for eventual capacity rationalization, which would ultimately support pricing — but the timing of competitor closures is not something O-I controls, and no independent competitor data (Verallia, Vidrala, Ardagh) confirming European utilization or pricing was obtainable. That absence is the single most important missing corroboration for the 2027 recovery case.

One clarification worth making: the shift in end-market mix (alcoholic beverages from 63.4% to 61.0% of reportable revenue) is a mix rotation, not customer diversification. No customer-concentration percentages appear in the filings reviewed, and the one directly measurable concentration metric — country revenue — is flat to slightly worse, with Mexico rising from 13% to 14% of consolidated sales.

## Risk Assessment

**European energy in 2027 is the dominant unquantified exposure.** 2026 guidance assumes ~$150M of higher European energy costs plus $75–100M of additional Middle East-driven inflation. Approximately 75–80% of 2026 European gas requirements are secured at favorable rates, which makes 2027 the open year, and the 2027 target already "assumes elevated energy costs." The Q2 10-Q newly detailed an energy derivative in which O-I is indemnified above a price trigger but, above an undisclosed price cap, "would bear the full incremental cost." Neither level is disclosed, so residual 2027 exposure cannot be quantified. Commodity forward and collar notionals rose from 9 million to 32 million units between year-end and June 30, with maturities into 2027 and $12M of unrecognized AOCI losses to reclassify within twelve months.

The external backdrop for European energy is a live risk factor the company itself cites (Middle East conflicts driving higher expected European energy costs). The same price/cost squeeze that produced the H1 result could recur, and a third guidance revision driven by European energy is a possibility rather than a remote tail. [Note: specific claims about Hormuz transit status and ECB policy are not grounded in the provided sources and have been removed.]

**The 2028 springing maturity converts refinancing into an all-or-nothing event.** If any senior notes remain outstanding 91 days before their maturity, the term loans A and B and the revolver mature on that springing date. After the May 2026 redemption of the 2027s, the nearest note maturity is the €600M 6.25% notes due 2028 — meaning the entire ~$2.7B credit facility accelerates if those are not refinanced. The 2027 EBITDA target and the 2028 refinancing are therefore the same question. The principal U.S./Canada collective bargaining agreement expires March 31, 2028, in the same window.

**Covenant risk is materially lower than screening models imply.** The single maintenance covenant tests only secured net leverage at 2.50x. Secured debt is $1,560M ($130M revolver + $790M TLA + $640M TLB); net of $339M cash, approximately $1,221M, or roughly 1.16x against ~$1.05B of 2026E EBITDA. EBITDA would have to fall to roughly $488M to breach — a halving. Approximately 62% of the debt stack is unsecured and outside the test. An Altman Z-Score near 1.0 flags distress but is blind to covenant architecture. This mitigant is genuine and is why the rating is REDUCE rather than AVOID. Caveat: Credit-Agreement-defined Consolidated EBITDA add-backs and any cash-netting cap are undisclosed, so 1.16x is approximate.

**Unreserved and unquantified contingencies.** French authorities are investigating alleged anti-competitive conduct; no charges have been filed, the company cannot estimate any loss, and *no reserve is recorded*. France is 12% of consolidated sales. Tax examinations are open in Brazil, Canada, Colombia, Italy, Peru and Poland covering 2004–2024, with assessments received "in excess of established reserves." Remaining LatAm goodwill of $608M is explicitly described as "susceptible to future impairment," with North America and Europe already at zero. Two plaintiff firms announced investigations in early August; no complaint has been located and none is disclosed in the 10-Q.

**Operational self-harm.** Furnace events and post-restructuring disruptions cost approximately $71M in the half and constrained sales. Two furnace events in one European half-year may indicate deferred maintenance across an aging footprint rather than isolated bad luck; no maintenance-capex-by-region disclosure exists to test this, and furnace rebuilds are capital-intensive and multi-month.

**Unresolved governance item.** Three Item 5.02 officer-change 8-Ks were filed in nine months (2025-11-10, 2026-03-05, 2026-07-10). The July 10 filing landed eighteen days before the Q2 release and impairment, and its substance could not be retrieved. This is an open item, not evidence of anything.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 50.4% against 52.0% one-year realized is fair, sits in the 5th percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in contango (50.4% at 1m rising to 55.8% at 6m) — a complacent structure with no near-term dislocation priced. The nearest expiry implies ±8.2% over 12 days. Put/call open interest of 0.07 is heavily call-tilted, and the 1-month skew reads −10.1% (call-heavy per the data source); the 6-month tenor shows a put/call volume ratio of 11.25 against a 1-month reading of zero, so the volume-based signals conflict across tenors and should not be over-read. ATM IV compressed 6.2pp (56.6% → 50.4%) while the stock fell 21% since late July, consistent with event risk having passed rather than accumulating.

Two implications. First, options positioning does not corroborate a bearish thesis — if anything, open interest is positioned for upside, meaning further downside is not pre-hedged and would have to be absorbed by the tape. Second, short interest of 9.9% of float with 3.0 days to cover is not crowded, but neither is the borrow signal or options structure supportive of an active short. This is a position to trim, not to press.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($13.00, +85%)

Fit to Win's gross savings are real and verified: $91M in the half, "consistent with management's expectations." Americas proves the operating model works — a 17.4% Q2 margin and $30M of profit growth on 7% lower volumes, achieved through $36M of price and $19M of savings. If European disruption costs are genuinely transitional and industry utilization improves as EU ETS phase-out forces competitor capacity closures, 2027 EBITDA of $1.25B is achievable. That produces $260–310M of free cash flow, a 24–29% yield on today's market cap, and comfortably refinances the 2028 notes. Covenant headroom is wide (1.2x against 2.50x), $1.11B of revolver remains undrawn, and the 2028/2029 notes traded above par at quarter-end — the credit market is pricing uncertainty, not default. Five insiders bought in the open market in May with no offsetting open-market sales, and the buyback was correctly throttled to zero in Q2 rather than burning cash. At 4.7x EV to a target EBITDA, the enterprise is inexpensive, and 18% equity/EV means modest EV recovery doubles the stock.

### Bear Case ($2.50, −64%)

Europe is 42% of revenue and structurally impaired: consolidated European operations were loss-making in H1 once JV equity earnings are removed, and the $96M deferred-tax valuation allowance says management no longer expects sufficient European taxable income to use those assets. That is a narrower and more durable statement about earnings power than a goodwill charge tied partly to the share price. 2027 European gas is largely unhedged, the hedge that exists caps indemnification at an undisclosed level, and the energy backdrop is deteriorating. The 2027 target has already been cut once and rests on the company "attempting" to resolve its own operational disruptions after two furnace events. FY2026 free cash flow is guided negative, net debt rose $407M in a half-year, marginal unsecured funding clears at 9.500%, and tangible book value is negative $403M. If 2027 EBITDA lands near $0.95–1.0B, roughly $260M of the bull bridge evaporates, free cash flow approaches zero, and the 2028 refinancing — which triggers acceleration of the entire $2.7B facility if unresolved — gets priced off a distressed curve. At that point the equity is an option, not a claim.

**Which case the evidence favors:** Europe's problem is more likely market structure than management failure, which means it is recoverable in principle but not on a schedule O-I controls. The self-help engine works. The financing structure buys time. But every variable that determines the equity's value — European gas in 2027, industry utilization, furnace reliability, the 2028 credit market — sits outside management's control, and consensus has cut current-year estimates 54% in ninety days. Expected return from $7.03 is approximately flat to modestly negative with a left tail three to four times the size of any realistic dividend or carry. That is a position to reduce.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**De-risking plan.** Trim now rather than around a catalyst. There is no dated event between here and the Q3 print (late October 2026) that resolves the central uncertainty, and the interim period carries negative free cash flow, seasonal working-capital dependence and live European gas exposure. Holders willing to retain optionality should cut position size by at least half and hold no more than a starter-sized residual sized to a 60%+ drawdown, given the equity is 18% of enterprise value. No new capital.

**Base case $5.75 (−18%)** — 2027 adjusted EBITDA of $1.10B (below the guided $1.2–1.3B, consistent with two prior cuts and unhedged 2027 energy) at 5.25x EV/EBITDA against $4.75B of net debt and $156M NCI. **Bull $13.00 (+85%)** — guided $1.25B at 5.5x with net debt held at $4.60B. **Bear $2.50 (−64%)** — $0.95B at 4.75x, where enterprise value approaches net debt and the equity retains option value only.

**Upgrade triggers back to HOLD (need two of three):**
1. H2 2026 operating cash flow lands at or above the $475–575M implied by guidance, delivering FY2026 OCF ≥$350M, with net debt flat or lower versus June 30.
2. European segment operating profit recovers to $50M+ in a quarter *excluding* JV equity earnings, with disclosed disruption costs below $15M for the quarter.
3. Disclosure that ≥70% of 2027 European gas requirements are secured at rates consistent with the 2027 EBITDA target, or a quantification of the hedge price cap.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE** additionally requires a refinancing of the 2028 notes completed or committed at a coupon at or below 8%, removing the springing-maturity overhang.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
1. A third reduction to the 2027 EBITDA target, or withdrawal of it.
2. Secured leverage disclosed above 1.75x, or revolver drawings exceeding $400M.
3. Impairment of the remaining $608M LatAm goodwill, or a French competition ruling establishing a material fine.
4. FY2026 operating cash flow tracking below the $275M low end at the Q3 print.

**Reassessment timeline.** Q3 2026 results, expected late October, is the first meaningful checkpoint: it tests whether H2 cash generation is on track, whether disruption costs are declining, and whether June's flat volume comparison extended. The 2027 guidance refresh with the FY2026 10-K (February 2027) is the decisive event, since it will incorporate actual 2027 energy positions.

## Conclusion

O-I Glass is a cheap enterprise with a structurally leveraged equity claim attached to a European business that has stopped earning money and a 2027 recovery target that management has already cut once and now describes in conditional language. The credit architecture is better than screening models suggest — a secured-only 2.50x covenant at approximately 1.2x, 62% of debt unsecured, no maturity before 2028, $1.11B of undrawn revolver — and that keeps the near-term failure probability low enough to rule out AVOID and to rule out shorting a name with 9.9% short interest, call-tilted open interest and fair-priced volatility. Americas is genuinely well run.

What has changed since the last review is the earnings-power leg of the thesis. Guidance was cut twice in five months, consensus for the current year is down 54% in ninety days, free cash flow is guided negative for 2026, net debt rose $407M in six months, and the 2027 European gas exposure — the single variable that determines whether the bull bridge's $260–310M of free cash flow exists — is largely unhedged into a disrupted energy market with an undisclosed hedge cap above which O-I bears full cost. Probability-weighted expected return from $7.03 is approximately flat with a bear case implying a 64% loss at roughly a third likelihood. That asymmetry does not justify holding a full position. **REDUCE.**
