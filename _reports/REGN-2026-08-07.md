## Executive Summary

Regeneron delivered its strongest quarter since the COVID antibody period: Q2 2026 revenue of $4,290.7M (+16.7% year over year), non-GAAP diluted EPS of $14.29 (+10.9%), and H1 revenue growth of 17.8%. Consensus responded: 2026 EPS estimates rose 14.6% over 30 days to $53.00, and current-quarter estimates rose 22.7% to $16.05. The mechanism behind the step-up is disclosed and non-speculative: the Sanofi development balance, which reduced reported collaboration revenue by approximately $930M in FY2025 and $530M in H1 2026 ($253.0M in Q2 alone), was fully repaid as of June 30, 2026. From Q3 2026 forward Regeneron books its full share of Dupixent/Kevzara commercialization profits with no offset. Because the reduction carried no associated cost, the benefit flows to pre-tax income close to one-for-one. Separately, the U.S. retina franchise appears to be crossing over: applying management's own H2 guidance (low-to-mid-teens sequential demand decline for EYLEA 2mg, low-to-mid-teens sequential growth for EYLEA HD) to Q2 U.S. bases of $412.2M and $596.3M produces roughly $1,033M in Q3 and $1,079M in Q4 against $1,008.5M in Q2, the first sequential growth since the biosimilar transition began.

Against that, the structure of the business has not changed. Sanofi collaboration revenue was 50.7% of Q2 revenue, and Regeneron's net share of Sanofi commercialization profits ($2,032.6M) exceeded consolidated pre-tax income ($1,527.8M). Regeneron does not set Dupixent's price, contract with its payors, or compute its gross-to-net reserves, and it is currently suing Sanofi in the Southern District of New York over information-access and audit rights covering exactly that accounting, with Sanofi's motion to dismiss denied March 17, 2026. Part of Q2's U.S. Dupixent surge came from a favorable gross-to-net adjustment that neither company quantified. Two live DOJ False Claims Act matters on EYLEA carry treble-damages exposure with accruals described as "not material" and certain reasonably possible losses unestimable. A Dupixent T-cell lymphoma product-liability docket was consolidated into a New Jersey MDL on June 4, 2026, with a medical-monitoring class action filed July 17, 2026. Aflibercept is a Medicare Part B drug selected for IRA negotiation, with negotiated prices effective 2028.

At $772.11 the stock trades at 14.6x the $53.00 2026 consensus and 11.9x ex-cash, versus a 26.4x biotech peer median. That gap is real, and the near-term earnings direction is up. The stock has recovered 28.5% from its June 1 close of $600.66 and sits 6.0% below its 52-week high of $821.11, while consensus mean sits 7.9% above spot. Our base case of $820 implies +6.2%; our bear case of $600 implies -22.3%. We raise the overall score from 5.4 to 6.2 and stay at HOLD, with an accumulation zone in the high-$600s.

## Company Overview

Regeneron operates as a single reportable segment from Tarrytown, NY, under founder control: Leonard Schleifer (co-Chair, President and CEO) and George Yancopoulos (co-Chair, President and Chief Scientific Officer). Three structural features define the investment case.

**Most reported profit comes from a product Regeneron does not commercialize.** Regeneron discovered dupilumab (Dupixent) and sarilumab (Kevzara); Sanofi records global net product sales and Regeneron records its share of collaboration profits as revenue: 50% of U.S. profits and a sliding 35–45% of ex-U.S. profits, plus bulk drug substance supply reimbursement ($141.7M in Q2 2026). Bayer records EYLEA/EYLEA HD sales outside the U.S. on the same principle. In Q2 2026, Sanofi revenue was $2,174.3M and Sanofi plus Bayer together were 57.1% of the $4,290.7M total. Regeneron's own recorded net product sales were $1,642.4M, 38% of revenue.

**It is the sole commercial operator of the U.S. retina franchise,** booking EYLEA (aflibercept 2mg) and EYLEA HD (8mg) directly, plus global Libtayo, and U.S. Praluent, Evkeeza, Veopoz and the newly launched Lynozyfic.

**R&D intensity is extreme.** FY2025 GAAP R&D was $5,850M on $14,343M of revenue (40.8%); H1 2026 was $3,175.1M on $7,896.1M (40.2%). FY2026 GAAP R&D guidance is $6.500–6.635B across roughly 50 active clinical programs. Schleifer framed internal R&D as the highest-return use of capital in preference to large M&A on the Q2 call, adding that his objection is to overpaying rather than to M&A as such.

Product position in Q2 2026: Dupixent global net sales (recorded by Sanofi) of $5,998.2M, +38%, across nine FDA-approved indications and more than 1.5 million patients treated; EYLEA HD U.S. net sales of $596.3M, +52% and +27% sequentially, now roughly 60% of U.S. franchise sales versus 34% a year earlier, with combined EYLEA HD + EYLEA at 57% of the innovative branded anti-VEGF category; Libtayo at $489.4M globally, +30%, with 20% of new-to-brand prescriptions in first-line NSCLC.

The GAAP decline is a comparison artifact with identifiable, largely non-repeating causes. Net gains on marketable and other securities fell from $389.9M to $86.9M, a $303.0M pre-tax headwind, roughly $2.43 per diluted share after tax on 106.8M shares. The effective tax rate normalized from 9.2% (H1 2025 included release of unrecognized tax benefits on an IRS audit settlement) to 14.2%, worth another $117.9M or approximately $1.10 per share. Together those two items are worth about −$3.53 per share, implying underlying EPS improved roughly $2.46.

Two further items compressed the GAAP operating margin to 24.5% from 24.9%. Unanticipated repairs at the Limerick, Ireland bulk manufacturing site cost $176.1M in H1 2026 ($161.3M in COGS, $14.8M in cost of collaboration manufacturing; $69.4M in Q2 COGS alone), driving GAAP product gross margin to 77% from 82% while non-GAAP gross margin held at 86%. Production returned to normal by June 30, and FY2026 GAAP gross-margin guidance was *raised* to 78–79% from 77–78%. Acquired IPR&D was $228.9M versus $22.3M (Tessera $150M in Q1, Parabilis $50M upfront in Q2). Regeneron does not add acquired IPR&D back in its non-GAAP measures, which is a conservative presentation choice relative to peers.

Adding both items back gives H1 2026 operating income of approximately $2,341.4M (29.7% margin) against $1,693.5M (25.3%), roughly +38% growth and +440bp of margin. This is our derivation, not a company-reported figure, and it is generous: business-development payments will recur at some rate given management's stated appetite for external innovation.

Third, the retina crossover described in the Executive Summary. Two caveats: management guided *demand*, and the press release explicitly notes EYLEA HD's growth was partly offset by a lower net selling price; Biocon's interchangeable Yesafili launched around August 3, 2026 into a market Amgen's Pavblu already contests. ## Competitive Landscape

Anti-VEGF ophthalmology is the clearest live case study in biosimilar cliff management in large-cap biotech, and Regeneron is executing it better than the market assumed at the start of 2026. Launching a higher-dose, longer-interval version of the same molecule with the broadest label in the category (wAMD, DME, DR, RVO, with q4w through q20w dosing after the April 2026 interval extension) has converted volume ahead of biosimilar scale-up. EYLEA HD grew 52% year over year and 27% sequentially on 24% sequential physician unit-demand growth, crossed above EYLEA in net sales, and was, per management, the only innovative brand with sequential share growth. Competition is Roche's Vabysmo on the branded side and, on 2mg, Amgen's Pavblu plus a launching wave including Biocon's interchangeable Yesafili, with Sandoz, Samsung Bioepis, Formycon and Sam Chun Dang variously litigating or launched by jurisdiction. European patent protection has eroded materially: the '306 patent was revoked in November 2024 (appeal hearing October 2026), the '049 invalidated in April 2026, and only the '992 upheld in amended form and under appeal.

Type 2 inflammation is Dupixent's home market and now the most contested area in biopharma. The moat is breadth: nine indications, first-biologic status in several, with COPD, chronic spontaneous urticaria, bullous pemphigoid and allergic fungal rhinosinusitis carrying growth. The threat is oral and long-acting: JAK inhibitors, oral IL-23/TL1A agents, longer-interval biologics. Regeneron's answer is a next-generation stack: a long-acting IL-13 antibody in Phase 1, a next-generation Dupixent clinic-ready in early 2027, an IL-4/IL-13 bispecific in 2027, whose economics are unallocated, which is why the Sanofi renegotiation matters. Schleifer disclosed "productive early discussions" with Sanofi's new CEO and said the original terms need adjustments because "we were quite a different company" when they were struck. A broadened collaboration on better terms is plausible; so is one that locks Regeneron further into a structure where it lacks commercial control.

Complement may be the most underpriced area. Cemdisiran as a quarterly subcutaneous siRNA in generalized myasthenia gravis attacks a market where FcRn agents require frequent cycling, and management argues a meaningful share of FcRn-treated patients lose efficacy over time. That rests on cross-study comparison rather than head-to-head evidence. Obesity is a late-follower position with a differentiated thesis (GLP-1/GIP plus PCSK9 antibody, plus muscle preservation), where the trevogrumab result validated the mechanism narrowly while removing the simple commercial case.

Customer concentration deserves explicit correction of a common framing: two distributors were 77% of gross product revenue in Q2 2026 (Customer A 45%, B 32%) versus 76% a year earlier (A 52%, B 24%). The mix between them shifted; aggregate concentration is flat to modestly worse. A more collaboration-weighted revenue mix is not distributor diversification.

## Risk Assessment

**DOJ False Claims Act exposure on EYLEA — two live matters, no material accrual.** The June 2020 copay-foundation complaint in D. Mass. survived partial summary judgment in September 2023, the First Circuit affirmed the government's causation standard in February 2025, and DOJ filed a second partial summary-judgment motion in October 2025. Separately, the March 2024 Average Sales Price complaint (alleging EYLEA's reported ASP to CMS excluded credit-card processing fees and discounts) survived a motion to dismiss on April 29, 2025, with eleven states now intervened. A new qui tam filed January 2026 in the Central District of California was unsealed June 18, 2026 after the U.S. and states declined to intervene. FCA remedies include treble damages plus per-claim penalties against a product with more than 100 million doses administered since 2011. The 10-Q states accruals are "not material" and certain reasonably possible losses cannot be estimated. Not solvency-threatening against $15.1B of net cash; the largest unquantified item in the filings. The FY2025 10-K discloses a $60M matching donation to an independent patient-assistance organization in Q4 2025 with up to $200M planned for 2026, a real cash cost of roughly 14% of annualized H1 free cash flow. We draw no inference about intent; independent charitable patient assistance is lawful and widespread within OIG guidance.

**Dupixent T-cell lymphoma litigation.** Consolidated into an MDL in the District of New Jersey on June 4, 2026, with a putative medical-monitoring class action in the District of Maryland on July 17, 2026. MDL formation within roughly a year of first filings indicates plaintiff-side momentum, not merit. The scientific question is contested, with substantial literature arguing that apparent dupilumab–CTCL associations reflect unmasking or misdiagnosis of pre-existing disease presenting as refractory eczema. No material accrual disclosed, and the Regeneron/Sanofi indemnification split is unverified.

**IRA Part B negotiation, effective 2028.** Aflibercept is a Part B drug; Part B selection began in 2026 with negotiated prices effective 2028. A negotiated price on a fully biosimilarized 2mg market plus EYLEA HD is the most concrete medium-term valuation risk in the filings.

**Government pricing agreements, April 2026.** Regeneron committed to most-favored-nation pricing for Medicaid on wholly-owned U.S. products, MFN pricing for certain *future* medicines, participation in TrumpRx.gov for Praluent, and providing Otarmeni free of charge in the U.S., in exchange for a Section 232 pharmaceutical tariff exemption through January 2029 conditioned on continued U.S. manufacturing and R&D investment. The asymmetry: price concessions are permanent in character and extend to future launches (cemdisiran, garetosmab, the Factor XI franchise, obesity), while the tariff exemption is time-limited and conditional. Neither side is quantified.

**Partner dependency plus litigation against the partner.** Sanofi was 50.7% of Q2 revenue and its net profit share exceeded consolidated pre-tax income, while Regeneron litigates information-access and audit rights over that same accounting. Bayer may terminate its collaboration on 6 or 12 months' notice.

**Manufacturing.** Limerick cost $176.1M in H1 and demonstrated bulk-production concentration. The EYLEA HD prefilled syringe CRL (October 2025, tied to a third-party fill/finish inspection) has slipped roughly two quarters, with approval now targeted before year-end.

**Governance.** Dual-class structure with 1.8M Class A shares carrying 10 votes each and 15.2% of combined voting power; executive officers and directors hold 5.3% of economics and 17.7% of votes; 80% supermajority required to remove a director; the staggered board phases out only from the 2028 annual meeting. A July 2025 derivative suit alleges excessive non-employee director compensation. Two securities class actions are live (January 2025 on DOJ/EYLEA HD disclosures, with a June 25, 2026 MTD hearing; July 16, 2026 on fianlimab disclosures). Five-year cumulative TSR of 159.77 lags the company's chosen peer group at 200.89.

Not all contingencies are negative. Regeneron won 9 of 10 counts against Amgen in its Praluent antitrust case in May 2025, with $135.6M compensatory plus $271.2M punitive damages awarded and post-trial motions pending, and on July 7, 2026 the court dismissed Amgen's counterclaims alleging fraudulently obtained patents except for the prosecution-delay defense. That $406.8M is an unrecognized gain contingency and should not be modeled as cash.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $1,000 (+29.5%)

The Sanofi step-up lands in reported Q3 numbers, confirming roughly $250M per quarter of incremental pre-tax income with no associated cost, and the lagging 2027 consensus of $50.40 (10 analysts) is revised toward $58–60. The U.S. retina franchise inflects to sequential growth in H2 2026 exactly as management's demand guidance implies, removing the drag that has defined the equity since 2024. The Dupixent take-rate continues expanding as Sanofi generates operating leverage. Cemdisiran is approved in November as the first siRNA in generalized myasthenia gravis with quarterly subcutaneous dosing, opening a franchise against frequently cycled FcRn agents; garetosmab clears in August; PNH non-inferiority reads out positively in Q4. The Sanofi renegotiation delivers improved economics on the long-acting IL-13, next-generation Dupixent and IL-4/IL-13 bispecific, converting the partner-dependency discount into an option. FCA matters settle within the range absorbable by $15.1B of net cash. On $58 2027 EPS at 17x, the stock reaches $986; Guggenheim's $1,030 marks the ceiling of published expectations.

### Bear Case — $600 (−22.3%)

The Q2 beat proves substantially a gross-to-net true-up of undisclosed size that does not recur, and H2 Dupixent growth moderates faster than the "strong but moderating" guidance implies. The retina crossover fails to appear in net sales because biosimilar price pressure on 2mg bleeds into 8mg contracting (the press release already flags a lower EYLEA HD net selling price) while ex-U.S. continues its 32% decline with the Bayer profit-share ratio compressing further. The IRA negotiated Part B price effective 2028 is set aggressively, and the market begins discounting it in 2027. One or both DOJ FCA matters resolve with a nine-to-ten-figure settlement, and the Dupixent MDL produces adverse early rulings or a labeling change. R&D at 40% of revenue continues producing failures at the 2025–2026 rate without a Dupixent successor. On $46 2027 EPS at 12x plus a litigation discount, the stock trades to $580–600; the published low target is $641.
