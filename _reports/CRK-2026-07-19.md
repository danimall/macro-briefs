**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Comstock Resources is a pure-play Haynesville/Bossier natural gas producer in North Louisiana and East Texas, majority-controlled (~65%) by entities affiliated with Jerry Jones. The FY2025 recovery — revenue nearly doubling to $2.22B and a swing to $396M net income — was real in the sense that gas realizations rose from $1.98/Mcf to $3.17/Mcf, but the headline profit was materially flattered by a $292M gain on asset sales and a $62M unrealized derivative gain. Underneath, free cash flow was negative $450M as capex of $1.35B ran well above operating cash flow, funded by divestitures and midstream partner contributions. The core business is a levered, single-commodity, single-basin bet on sustained gas prices.

The audit correctly flags that my prior AVOID framing is inconsistent with the current price structure. At $13.34 the stock has fallen 42.6% from its 52-week high, sits just above its $12.44 low, and is bracketed by an analyst mean target of $15.58, a median of $16.00, and a third-party fair value of $13–16. That configuration describes a depressed, heavily-shorted (28.9% of float) equity where the bear case is largely discounted and the setup is two-sided, not a name with meaningful residual downside asymmetry. The earnings record is more nuanced than a simple deterioration story: three consecutive beats of 33–49.5% (Q2–Q4 2025) preceded the Q1 2026 non-GAAP miss of 42%. And current realized gas of $4.27/Mcf in Q1 2026 already sits above the $3+ level the bull thesis requires.

On balance I move the recommendation to HOLD (uncertain). The favorable near-term pricing and largely-discounted valuation offset a genuinely stressed balance sheet, a pending $440M cash redemption, chronic negative FCF, and falling forward estimates. There is no clear directional edge here at $13.34, and the honest classification is an uncertain hold pending resolution of the 1H26 liquidity events and a borrowing-base redetermination.

## Company Overview

Comstock operates ~803K net acres prospective for Haynesville/Bossier, including the emerging Western Haynesville extension, with proved reserves of 7.0 Tcfe (essentially all gas) at year-end 2025, 41% developed, and a ~16-year reserve life. The company produces ~1.1–1.2 Bcf/d, operates 99% of its reserve base, and runs an unusually lean model with only 252 employees. SEC PV-10 was $4.5B ($5.2B at the year-end strip).

Two elements differentiate CRK from a generic Haynesville producer. First, Western Haynesville, a deep (10,500–19,200 ft) high-pressure/high-temperature play where the company claims a large de-risked inventory (3,343 gross / 2,561 net locations) after 30 successful wells, and which drove most of the 2025 reserve additions. Second, a partnership with NextEra Energy Resources to supply gas for new power generation in the Western Haynesville area, framed as initial 2 GW capacity with potential expansion to 8 GW to serve hyperscaler data centers. The filings position CRK as the gas supplier and NextEra as the power developer; there is no binding offtake, guaranteed volume, or quantified economics disclosed. This is optionality that fits the data-center-power demand theme, but it is not contracted revenue and should not be capitalized into the base case.

## Financial Analysis

The dominant feature is commodity cyclicality. Revenue moved from $3.63B (2022) to $1.57B (2023) to $1.25B (2024, a $230M net loss) to $2.22B (2025, $396M net income), driven almost entirely by gas price. FY2025 operating income was $646M (29% margin), operating cash flow was $900M (+45%), and ROE was 15.9%.

The quality of that profit is the issue. Of the $396M net income, $292M was a gain on asset sales (mostly Shelby Trough) and $62M was an unrealized derivative gain. Free cash flow was negative $450M because capex of $1.35B (D&C plus $224M midstream) far exceeded operating cash flow; the gap was plugged by $429M of asset sales and $216M of midstream partner contributions. This is a growth-spending posture, not a self-funding cash machine.

Q1 2026 continued the pattern: net income of $112.5M ($0.38/sh) but operating cash flow of $272M against $405M of capex — still outspending. Realized gas of $4.27/Mcf was up 19% YoY, but the hedge book generated $80M of realized derivative losses (2026 swaps at $3.51, collars floored at $3.50), cutting the effective realized price to $3.45/Mcf. If the current ~$4 realized environment persists, CRK captures higher upstream revenue but forfeits a portion of it to hedge settlements, and the equity gets only partial participation in a gas rally through 2026–27. The favorable pricing helps, but the hedge structure caps how much of it reaches the bottom line.

The balance sheet is the constraining variable. Total debt was ~$3.0B in Q1 2026 ($1.62B 6.75% notes due 2029, $965M 5.875% notes due 2030, ~$350M drawn on the $1.5B revolver, $47M at PGS), D/E ~1.5x. The Altman Z-Score of 1.48 sits in the distress zone, and the current ratio of 0.49 flags dependence on the revolver — structurally common for E&Ps but not benign given negative FCF. Liquidity of ~$1.27B is mostly undrawn revolver plus ~$15M cash. Interest expense of ~$223M/yr is a real fixed charge; covenants (leverage <3.5x, current ratio >1.0) were in compliance. Piotroski scores 4/5, failing only on the current ratio. Applying financial health as a downside filter, this profile earns a low score: the combination of distress-zone Z, negative FCF, and a pending cash draw reliably flags risk.

The reserve narrative deserves skepticism. PUDs quadrupled from 1.0 to 4.2 Tcf in 2025, but this was price-driven — locations became economic at higher SEC gas prices — while the associated future development cost obligation ballooned from $0.9B to $4.7B. This is a reflexive, price-sensitive asset base: in 2023–24, PUDs were revised down by ~2.6 Tcf as prices fell. The runway is large but only economic at sustained higher gas, and it carries a heavy embedded capital bill.

## Growth Analysis

Production declined 15% YoY in 2025 (to 450 Bcf) and again in Q1 2026, as capital was reallocated toward Western Haynesville delineation and midstream. Near-term growth is negative on volume; the story is about future inventory conversion rather than current output.

Estimate revisions are unambiguously negative. Current-quarter EPS has been cut from $0.11 (90 days ago) to $0.01 (-87.1%); next-quarter from $0.18 to $0.11 (-42.5%); current-year from $0.88 to $0.48 (-45.2%); next-year from $1.39 to $0.93 (-32.8%), with the 30-day revisions still falling. Growth prospects carries the most weight as a winner-selection signal, and here the signal points the wrong way: this is a name with deteriorating forward earnings momentum, not one with rising estimates. That is the single strongest reason not to upgrade to BUY despite the cheap-looking multiple.

The Western Haynesville inventory and the NextEra data-center-power option provide genuine upside optionality, and the data-center gas-turbine demand theme is a real structural tailwind for Haynesville gas. But CRK's specific expression of it is unquantified and multi-year. It supports the argument that the stock is not a terminal-decline value trap; it does not support a growth-driven BUY today.

## Valuation Assessment

CRK trades at ~6.3x trailing/forward earnings versus a peer median of 15.8x. A low P/E in Energy is normally a caution flag — cheapness has been reliably anti-predictive, and the sector carries a structurally negative alpha history. Here the discount partly reflects genuine reasons: distress-zone leverage, negative FCF, a $440M cash outflow pending, no dividend (restricted by the credit facility), and a controlled-company governance discount. Peers span a wide range (CNX 4.4x, MGY 15.8x, MUR 65.8x, two with negative earnings), so the comp set is heterogeneous rather than a clean read.

The audit's central point is the correct anchor. Analyst mean ($15.58) and median ($16.00) targets sit 17–20% above the current $13.34, the target range runs $9–$21, and an independent third-party fair value of $13–16 brackets the price. This target structure implies modest upside from mean reversion with a real left tail if gas retreats or the borrowing base is cut, rather than concentrated downside. My own base case of roughly $13–16 lands squarely inside that band. Given that the bear case is largely priced (stock at -42.6%, just above its 52-week low, IV compressing from 77.1% to 60.2% as the panic bleeds off), the honest valuation read is roughly fair-to-slightly-cheap on a risk-adjusted basis, not a clear mispricing in either direction. That supports a HOLD, not an AVOID and not a BUY. I do not treat the analyst targets as a floor here because estimates are falling, which is precisely the condition under which consensus targets tend to be revised down toward, not above, the price.

## Competitive Landscape

CRK competes against larger, better-capitalized Haynesville operators — Expand Energy (former Chesapeake + Southwestern), Aethon, and EQT-adjacent players. Its claimed edge is operational: long laterals (41 wells ≥15,000 ft over 2021–25), horseshoe well designs, high-intensity completions, and MiQ methane certification for responsibly-sourced gas. Proximity to Gulf Coast LNG, Mexico exports, petrochemicals, and now in-basin data-center power is a favorable demand backdrop for the basin.

Trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving on the strategic axis (Western Haynesville consolidation, midstream control after the Pinnacle buyout) but pressured on the financial axis (scale disadvantage versus larger balance sheets in a capital-intensive play). The NextEra initiative is the most differentiated element of the story and could become a durable competitive advantage if it converts to contracted offtake, but it remains an option. I score competitive position as roughly neutral — a credible operator in a good basin, without a clear share-gaining or moat-widening trajectory the market is missing.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks that are not fully priced center on the balance sheet and 1H26 liquidity path:

- **The $440M Pinnacle/Quantum(Cactus) redemption**, expected to close 1H 2026, simultaneously removes a prior funding source (partner contributions were $216M in 2025) and requires $440M of cash from a company with ~$15M cash, ~$350M revolver draw, and negative FCF. This is the most underappreciated near-term event and directly pressures the borrowing-base redetermination cycle.
- **Gas price leverage is existential.** As ~99% gas, single-basin, no oil, every cash-flow, reserve, borrowing-base, and drilling-economics line moves directly with Henry Hub. The 2024 loss year shows the downside.
- **Reserve reflexivity** — the $4.7B future development obligation only pencils at sustained $3+ gas; a retreat evaporates PV-10 as it did in 2023–24.
- **Hedges are a drag** in the current above-$3.50 spot environment.
- **~$721M federal / ~$1.2B state NOLs** are expected to expire unused due to the 2018 change-of-control, reducing the tax shield.
- **Governance overhang** — a Jerry Jones-controlled company with pervasive related-party structures (operating Jones partnerships, marketing their gas, a $100.5M related-party equity injection in 2024). Minority holders have limited protection, and capital allocation ultimately serves the controlling shareholder.

Offsetting these, senior notes trade at 96–98% of par (no credit-market distress), 8-K flow is quiet with no restatements or executive departures, and risk factors were not materially changed in the Q1 10-Q. I score risk factors low because the $440M cash event and borrowing-base risk are concrete near-term catalysts, but not extreme, because much of the stress is already reflected in the price.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is neutral-to-mildly-constructive, which argues against an active bearish stance. ATM IV compressed from 77.1% to 60.2% over the past month while the price ticked up from $13.07 to $13.34 and the put/call OI ratio fell from 0.37 to 0.25 — the market is de-risking, not pricing an imminent collapse. Put/call skew is balanced (-1.0% near-term), IV sits at fair value versus 57.6% historical vol, and the term structure is flat. The implied ±15.2% move at the nearest expiry reflects the pending Q2 print and liquidity events, not directional panic. The 28.9% short interest with 8.4 days to cover creates real squeeze risk on any gas rally or favorable borrowing-base outcome — a further reason not to be short this name here.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$18–21):** Gas holds at $4+ into 2026–27 on LNG export ramp and data-center power demand; Western Haynesville inventory converts economically; the NextEra relationship advances toward contracted offtake; the borrowing base holds and FCF turns positive as capex intensity moderates. The short base covers into any of these, amplifying the move. This maps to the analyst high of $21.

**Bear case (~$9–11):** Gas retreats toward $3 or below; the $440M redemption plus continued outspend forces a borrowing-base cut and revolver stress; PUDs and PV-10 are revised down again; falling estimates continue. This maps to the analyst low of $9.

**Base case (~$14–16):** ~$4 gas persists but hedges cap participation; the company funds the redemption through the revolver and further high-grading, stays covenant-compliant, and grinds sideways as the market waits for FCF to inflect. This sits inside the mean/median targets and the third-party fair value.

The realistic distribution is roughly symmetric around the current price, which is why this is a hold rather than a directional call.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Reassessment timeline: the Q2 2026 print, confirmation of the Pinnacle/Quantum redemption close, and the mid-year borrowing-base redetermination are the key near-term events (next 1–2 quarters).

**Upgrade triggers (toward BUY):**
- FCF turns positive or capex guidance is cut materially below operating cash flow, removing the outspend, combined with a borrowing base held at or above the $2.0B level.
- The NextEra data-center gas supply converts to a contracted, quantified offtake with defined volumes and economics.
- Forward EPS estimate revisions stop falling and turn positive over a 90-day window with gas sustaining above $3.75/Mcf.

**Downgrade triggers (toward AVOID):**
- A borrowing-base reduction or covenant pressure following the $440M redemption, or revolver utilization climbing sharply while FCF stays negative.
- Realized gas falling back toward $3 or below, which re-triggers reserve/PV-10 markdowns and impairment risk.
- Any related-party transaction that transfers value away from minority holders on non-market terms.

## Conclusion

I am moving CRK from AVOID to HOLD (uncertain). The prior AVOID rested on downside asymmetry that the current price no longer supports: at $13.34 the stock is 42.6% off its high, just above its 52-week low, and bracketed by consensus targets, a third-party fair value, and my own base case. The bear case is substantially discounted, and current ~$4 realized gas is above the level the thesis requires — both facts I under-weighted before.

At the same time, this is not a BUY. Forward estimates are falling sharply, FCF is chronically negative, the balance sheet sits in the distress zone with a $440M cash redemption pending, and the reported profit is flattered by one-time gains. There is no clear directional edge here, and the 28.9% short interest cuts both ways — squeeze risk to the upside, structural-deterioration signal on the downside. The honest classification is an uncertain hold: own it only if you have a specific view that gas holds at $4+ and the borrowing base survives the 1H26 liquidity events, and reassess at the Q2 print and the redetermination.
