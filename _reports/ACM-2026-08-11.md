**Research Date: August 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AECOM is the world's largest architectural and engineering design firm by ENR revenue and holds the #1 ranking in water, transportation design, facilities design, environmental engineering, environmental consulting, and environmental science. That franchise is intact. What broke in FY2026 is the part of the portfolio management chose to keep after explicitly considering a sale.

On August 10, 2026 AECOM reported a Q3 FY2026 GAAP net loss of $86.7M and non-GAAP EPS of −$0.50 against a +$1.49 consensus, including a $337M pre-tax loss tied to the delayed completion of a construction management project. That charge equals roughly 33% of FY2025 income from operations ($1,026.5M). FY2026 adjusted EPS and adjusted EBITDA guidance were reset below consensus. The sequencing matters: the FY2025 10-K (November 19, 2025) disclosed a strategic review of the construction management business; the Q2 10-Q (May 12, 2026) stated the review was complete and AECOM would "continue to own and operate the business," alongside a second FY2026 guidance raise; the loss surfaced twelve weeks later. Between those events the company repurchased roughly $444M of stock at an average near $98 while generating $14.5M of first-half free cash flow, funded by drawing cash from $1,585.7M to $1,034.3M.

The offsetting evidence is real. Roughly half of AECOM's reported revenue is pass-through subcontractor cost, so headline revenue misdescribes the business: H1 FY2026 revenue fell 2.0% while net service revenue (revenue less subcontractor and other direct costs) rose 3.6% to $3,799.3M, with segment earnings before tax and amortization up 8.9% and NSR margin up 78bp to 15.87%. Americas gross margin has moved 6.1% → 7.0% → 7.3%. Backlog was reportedly up 13% to a record. Only $45.4M of debt matures in the remaining months of FY2026 with $2,627.5M due after FY2030, and the revolver was essentially undrawn at $1,495.6M available. All three of the most senior officers bought stock in the open market in May and June at $70.63–$71.12. At $67.26 the shares are 50.4% below the November 2025 high of $135.52 and trade at roughly 8.6x FY2025 operating income plus D&A.

We move to HOLD with an honest classification of uncertainty rather than to ACCUMULATE. A >50% drawdown means the burden of proof sits with the bull case, and the single most decision-relevant fact — whether the $337M loss reflects one troubled project or a broader estimation problem across a guaranteed-maximum-price book that was 37% of FY2025 revenue — cannot be established from what is currently in hand. The Q3 FY2026 10-Q is the catalyst.

## Company Overview

AECOM provides advisory, planning, design, program management and construction management services across transportation, water, environment, facilities and energy. Income derives principally from billing employee time; the firm is not primarily a self-perform contractor. Scale is roughly 51,000 employees, about 18,000 in the U.S.

Three reportable segments: Americas (which contains both Design & Consulting Services Americas and Construction Management), International (EMEA plus Asia-Australia-Pacific), and AECOM Capital, which is operationally wound down — zero revenue in H1 FY2026 — and survives as a residual equity-earnings line and a source of contingent guarantees.

Client mix has shifted toward government: 43% of revenue in FY2023 to 50% in FY2025 (U.S. federal 7%, U.S. state and local 24%, non-U.S. governments 19%). This is an end-market composition change, not a customer-concentration change; no single client has exceeded 10% of revenue in five years, and none exceeded 10% of receivables at March 31, 2026 or September 30, 2025. Contract mix in FY2025 was 38% cost-reimbursable, 37% guaranteed-maximum-price (GMP), 25% fixed-price, with H1 FY2026 shifting toward lower risk: cost-reimbursable revenue up to $3,237.7M from $2,969.3M while GMP fell to $2,514.1M from $2,871.0M.

Pass-through subcontractor revenue was $8.6B of $16.1B (53%) in FY2025 and $3.8B of $7.6B (50%) in H1 FY2026. The company itself cautions that changes in reported revenue may not indicate business trends. Any AECOM revenue figure, including the reported −14.2% Q3 decline, must be re-based before it carries information.

FY2025 was a good year on operations: revenue +0.2%, gross margin 7.5% vs 6.7%, income from operations +24.1%, operating cash flow $821.6M, capex $136.7M, free cash flow $684.9M (4.2% of revenue, 13.9% of NSR), backlog +6.1% to $39.7B with growth attributed primarily to Americas Construction Management. Two opposite signals in the same period. The fee business accelerated: NSR +3.6% (Q2 alone +4.4%), Americas gross margin 7.3% vs 6.7% on restructuring benefits, low-cost enterprise capability centers and higher-margin advisory work. Cash conversion broke. Receivables and contract assets consumed $346.6M versus a $211.1M source a year earlier, a $557.7M swing. Contract assets rose $379.1M to $2,164.2M. "Significant claims" in contract assets and other non-current assets went from approximately $400M at September 30, 2025 to approximately $680M at March 31, 2026. DSO moved from 74 days to 83 (70 at FY2024). Management attributed the shortfall to "a longer than anticipated claims resolution process."

Seasonality explains part of the level but not the change: H1 FY2025 OCF of $341.7M was 42% of that year's $821.6M, so H1 is structurally light, but that does not explain a $268M year-over-year deterioration in the same window. H1 FY2026 free cash flow of $14.5M is a sensitivity indicator, not an annualized figure.

### Balance sheet (March 31, 2026)

Cash $1,034.3M (of which $231.8M sits in consolidated joint ventures and is effectively restricted); total debt $2,747.7M ($1,450.0M credit agreement, $1,200.0M 6.000% 2033 notes, $97.7M other); goodwill $3,762.3M; AECOM stockholders' equity $2,270.6M with an accumulated deficit of $1,475.6M; shares outstanding 128,201,913, down 3,580,458 (−2.7%) in six months.

Maturity risk is close to nil: $45.4M due in the remainder of FY2026, $2,627.5M "thereafter." The March 10, 2026 Amendment No. 16 established a $1.5B revolver and $950M Term Loan A to March 2031 plus a $500M Term Loan B to April 2031, with the Term Loan B margin cut 25bp to SOFR+1.50%. Lenders extended and repriced favorably. The ≤4.00x consolidated leverage covenant was met; net debt of $1,713.4M against FY2025 operating income plus D&A of $1,202.4M implies roughly 1.4x on a GAAP proxy. The bank-defined ratio and EBITDA add-backs were not verified, and a Q3 charge flowing into covenant EBITDA would compress the calculation, but the gap from ~1.4x to 4.0x is wide enough that the direction is robust.

Book equity is thin (goodwill is 1.66x equity and total liabilities are 4.20x equity, up from 3.81x) and the driver is buybacks, not borrowing. Debt was flat. The equity roll reconciles precisely: −$1,224.8M accumulated deficit plus $254.4M net income less $80.5M dividends less $424.7M charged for repurchases equals −$1,475.6M. An Altman Z-Score of 2.12 reflects that capital structure rather than distress; revenue/assets of 1.32x and EBIT/assets of 8.4% are healthy, and near-term solvency risk is low.

Contingent exposures are large and growing: surety bonds $6.1B (up from $5.6B at FY2025 and $5.1B at FY2024), standby letters of credit $904.5M, professional liability accruals $849.0M, a guarantee of the civil-infrastructure-construction buyer's debt extended to March 2028, and an unquantified potential liability as an indemnitor on a divested business's surety bonds disclosed in February 2024 and still unsized.

### Capital allocation

H1 FY2026: free cash flow $14.5M; repurchases $441.9M cash paid; dividends $75.7M; noncontrolling distributions $28.1M — $545.7M returned, funded by a $551.5M reduction in cash. The disclosed February 2026 tranche was 1,180,876 shares at $98.33 average, marked down $36.7M at $67.26. The larger Q1 tranche (~$328M) has no disclosed average price; December 2025 insider transaction prices of $97.01–$98.85 imply a similar level, which would put the total H1 mark-to-market shortfall near $140M. That magnitude is an estimate; the direction is established. $883.9M remained authorized after the Board raised the program to $1.0B on February 4, 2026.

The dividend — $0.31 quarterly, $1.24 annualized, 1.84% at $67.26 — was 23.6% of FY2025 adjusted EPS and covered 4.3x by FY2025 free cash flow. It looks secure. The buyback is the lever that will absorb the adjustment.

## Growth Analysis

Demand conditions are the strongest part of the case, and the filings are specific. In the U.S., the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act and the One Big Beautiful Bill Act; in Canada, the federal Major Projects Office and a new sovereign wealth fund; in the UK, a 10 Year Infrastructure Strategy with £725B of long-term funding. Management identifies rapid acceleration in national defense spending as a cross-market trend contributing to revenue, backlog and pipeline. Energy and high-tech growth is attributed to electrification and data center development. Water demand is driven by drought, flooding, emerging-contaminant remediation and storage.

Segment data corroborates the mix rather than a uniform lift: H1 FY2026 Americas Transportation +$106.0M (+9.0%) and Water & Environment +$91.5M (+8.5%) against Facilities −$268.8M (−7.4%). Facilities is the soft spot, and Facilities sits closest to construction management.

Against that, earnings momentum is negative. Consensus current-year EPS fell 8.9% in 30 days to $5.46 and next-year 3.4% to $6.49. Nine-month FY2026 reported quarterly EPS sums to roughly $2.38 ($1.29 + $1.59 − $0.50), which is difficult to reconcile with a $5.46 full-year consensus; parts of the feed appear to predate the August print or sit on a different adjustment basis, and we treat the current-year figure as unreliable. Building from the disclosed quarters plus a seasonally strong Q4 in the $1.60–$1.80 range implies FY2026 adjusted EPS near $4.00–$4.20, versus $5.26 in FY2025 and the roughly $6.00 implied by May guidance. Earnings will decline this year.

The forward growth question reduces to two things: whether NSR can compound at mid-single digits with continued margin expansion — the evidence says yes, three consecutive periods of Americas gross margin gains driven by an identifiable mechanism (low-cost delivery hubs, restructuring, advisory mix) — and whether the GMP book produces further charges. Backlog reportedly up 13% to a record supports the first. It does not address the second, and reported backlog is roughly double the contractually enforceable measure: $39.7B backlog against $19.7B of remaining unsatisfied performance obligations at FY2025 ($20.1B at March 31, 2026). That gap is normal under ASC 606 and reflects definitional conservatism, but it means "record backlog" is a management-defined measure whose FY2025 growth came from the business that just produced the loss.

AI is a credible cost-side initiative with no disclosed revenue or efficiency quantification. H1 restructuring of $41.5M was described as positioning for broader AI deployment, and the FY2025 acquisitions brought a $203.0M "digital assets" intangible amortized over five years. Treat it as a margin lever, not a growth line.

Three things justify a meaningful part of it: the FY2026 earnings base is roughly 20% lower than the level the market was capitalizing in February; the free cash flow that supports the 7.9% trailing yield did not materialize in H1 FY2026; and management's forecasting credibility is impaired after two guidance raises preceded a large construction management pre-tax loss.

Cheapness alone is not a thesis. What would make the multiple work is a specific, checkable catalyst, and there are two: conversion of the ~$680M claims balance into cash, which would restore the free cash flow bridge, and a Q4 FY2026 print plus FY2027 guidance that establishes a defensible earnings base. Neither is resolved.

Scenario framework, 12 months:

- **Base $78 (+16%)**: FY2027 adjusted EPS recovers to ~$5.25 as the CM loss does not repeat and NSR margin gains continue; 15x. Cross-checks against EV/EBITDA of ~9.5x on $1,200–1,250M of operating income plus D&A less roughly $1.9B net debt, which yields $75–78.

Probability-weighted at 50/20/30 gives roughly $74, about +10% plus a 1.84% dividend. Positive expected return, modest margin of safety, low confidence.

## Competitive Landscape

AECOM competes against Jacobs, WSP, Stantec, Arcadis, Tetra Tech and Wood on technical depth, framework positions and delivery cost. Its #1 ENR rankings across water, transportation, facilities and environmental categories carry real weight in prequalification-driven procurement, and its enterprise capability centers are the specific mechanism management credits for Americas gross margin moving from 6.1% to 7.3% over three years. Customer concentration is genuinely low, with no client above 10% of revenue for five years. The revenue mix is shifting toward cost-reimbursable work, which reduces prospective contract risk.

What the filings do not support is pricing power or switching costs. This is a competitively bid, people-based business where margin comes from utilization, delivery-cost arbitrage and contract-risk selection. The Q3 charge is evidence that the third of those was mispriced on at least one large project.

The strategic tension is the more important competitive point. AECOM spent six years exiting self-perform at-risk construction — a plan approved in Q1 FY2020 — and the discontinued-operations line still cost $105.0M in FY2024, $75.4M in FY2025 and $70.1M in H1 FY2026, roughly $250M over 2.5 years. It nonetheless retained Construction Management, a GMP business, after a formal review, and that business then produced a $337M pre-tax loss while also being the source of FY2025 backlog growth. The highest-growth, lowest-earnings-quality part of the portfolio is the part management chose to keep. Facilities revenue, the end market closest to CM, is declining 7.4%.

One adjacent policy consideration: federal procurement reform pushing toward fixed-price and outcomes-based contracting would increase the industry's exposure to exactly the risk that just crystallized. AECOM's direct U.S. federal exposure is only 7% of revenue, so the mechanical impact is limited. [unverified — no procurement-reform trend is described in the research brief]

## Risk Assessment

**GMP estimation risk, now realized.** Under GMP contracts AECOM guarantees a price and bears cost overruns and delay costs. GMP was 37% of FY2025 revenue. A $337M pre-tax loss on one delayed project equals roughly 33% of FY2025 income from operations. The Q2 10-Q stated there were no material revisions to input-method contract estimates in the six months to March 31, 2026, so as of May 12 nothing broad had been disclosed. Whether the Q3 event is confined to one project is the question the 10-Q must answer.

**Unbilled claims quality.** Approximately $680M of significant claims sits in contract assets and other non-current assets, nearly double the September 2025 balance, recognized only where recovery is probable and estimable. AECOM has twice recently written down claim assets it had judged probable: a $61.8M non-cash loss in Q1 FY2026 on the DOE demolition matter (where $103M and $60.4M claims were both denied and an appeal has been pending since December 2020), and $53.0M in Q3 FY2025 on adverse post-trial interest rulings in the Montana refinery case after winning the jury verdict. That track record argues for discounting the current balance.

**Goodwill.** $3,762.3M against $2,270.6M of book equity, with $2,817.2M in Americas — the segment housing Construction Management. Market capitalization of $8.6B still exceeds book equity comfortably, so a test failure is not obviously triggered, but a CM-specific impairment would be equity-destructive and would further compress the equity-to-liabilities ratio.

**Tax credits under IRS examination.** The IRS is auditing FY2017–FY2020 returns including the methodology for calculating certain credits. Reserves total $81.4M ($47.0M FY2025 plus $34.4M in Q2 FY2026, the latter covering credits claimed through FY2026). AECOM recognized a $38.3M benefit from credits and incentives in H1 FY2026 alone, described as having a continuing effect on the effective tax rate, which ran 15.7% against a 21% statutory rate. An adverse methodology outcome is prospective as well as retrospective, implying a permanently higher tax rate on the primary compensation metric.

**Surety and bonding capacity.** $6.1B of bonds and $904.5M of letters of credit outstanding. A large project loss and a ratings-sensitive leverage profile are the conditions under which sureties tighten, which would directly constrain bidding capacity. The 10-K carries this as an explicit risk factor.

**Purchase accounting still open.** Two FY2025 acquisitions ($375.9M consideration, $146.4M in stock) had incomplete initial accounting at March 31, 2026, more than twelve months after closing, with $69.5M of goodwill adjustments already booked in H1 FY2026. The $203.0M digital-assets intangible drove amortization from $1.5M to $30.1M year over year in H1, roughly $60M annualized, excluded from adjusted EPS.

**Geographic.** International gross margin fell to 8.3% from 8.9% in Q2 on Asia and Middle East weakness; the CEO cited Middle East conflict uncertainty in FY2026 guidance. The UK Building Safety Act 2022 extends liability for historical residential defects against $849.0M of professional liability accruals. Hong Kong political risk is a named risk factor.

**Disclosure gaps.** Risk factors were stated as unchanged in the May 12 10-Q, three months before the charge; the FY2026 risk factor updates are the highest-priority forthcoming disclosure. The June 11, 2026 8-K (material agreement plus direct financial obligation) has not been read and postdates the last balance sheet. All Q3 figures used here derive from secondary reporting; the Q3 press release and 10-Q are not in hand. The split of the $337M between revenue reversal, cost accrual, liquidated damages and asset write-down — and therefore its cash character — is unknown.

**Not a solvency risk.** $45.4M of debt matures in the near term against $2,627.5M after FY2030, a nearly undrawn $1.5B revolver, roughly 1.4x net leverage on a GAAP proxy versus a 4.00x covenant, and a March 2026 refinancing that extended maturities and cut pricing. The downside here is an earnings and multiple problem.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $100 (+49%)

The professional-services business is compounding: NSR +3.6% in H1 with Q2 at +4.4%, segment earnings before tax and amortization +8.9%, NSR margin +78bp, Americas gross margin up 120bp over two years on an identifiable mechanism. Reported backlog is up 13% to a record against demand backed by IIJA and OBBBA in the U.S., the UK's £725B strategy, Canadian project financing, accelerating global defense budgets, and data center and electrification work. The $337M charge is confined to one delayed project, the ~$680M claims balance resolves substantially in AECOM's favor, and free cash flow returns toward the FY2025 level of $685M, restoring a high-single-digit yield. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches roughly $6.00 and the multiple recovers to 17x — still a discount to a peer median of 27.5x — as the buyback resumes against a share count already down 2.7% in six months. Three top officers bought at ~$71. The credit facility was extended and repriced in AECOM's favor five months ago, which is a lender assessment of the same book.

### Bear Case — $50 (−26%)

The charge is the first visible output of a systemic problem. GMP work was 37% of FY2025 revenue, the claims balance nearly doubled in six months while DSO went from 74 to 83 days, and AECOM has written down two previously probable claim assets in the last eighteen months. A second re-estimation in Q4 FY2026 or FY2027 resets the earnings base toward $4.00, and $2,817.2M of Americas goodwill against $2,270.6M of total book equity becomes an impairment question. The IRS challenge to credit methodology removes structural effective-tax-rate benefit. Surety providers tighten in response to the loss, constraining bid capacity in the very end markets that are growing. The buyback stops — it has to, since H1 distributions of $545.7M against $14.5M of free cash flow were funded by cash drawdown — removing the marginal buyer. Management credibility, having raised guidance twice and retained the business twelve weeks before the loss, no longer commands the benefit of the doubt, so the multiple compresses toward 12x rather than expanding.

### What the market may be seeing that the filings do not explain

The bulk of the derating happened before the charge. Using disclosed transaction prices as waypoints: insider sales at $97–$99 in mid-December 2025, a company buyback at $98.33 in February 2026, insider purchases at $70.63–$71.12 in May and June. Roughly 28% of the decline occurred between February and mid-May 2026, despite two guidance raises and with nothing in the filings to explain it. Either the market anticipated the project deterioration months ahead of the disclosure, or an E&C sector multiple compression was underway that the filings would not capture. Neither reading supports treating the current price as a straightforward mispricing.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Existing holders have positive expected return at $67.26 (probability-weighted ~$74, roughly +10% plus a 1.84% dividend) but the confidence interval is wide and the decisive facts are not verifiable yet. New capital should wait for the Q3 FY2026 10-Q.

**Base case $78 (+16%)** — 15x FY2027 adjusted EPS of ~$5.25, cross-checked at 9.5x normalized operating income plus D&A of $1,200–1,250M less ~$1.9B net debt. Timeframe 12 months, anchored on the Q4 FY2026 print (November 2026) and FY2027 guidance.

**Bull case $100 (+49%)** — 17x $6.00 FY2027 adjusted EPS; requires no repeat charge and material claims collection.

**Bear case $50 (−26%)** — 12x $4.00 FY2027 adjusted EPS; requires a second GMP or claims event, a goodwill impairment, or an adverse tax outcome.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires two of the following:**
1. The Q3 FY2026 10-Q identifies the charge as project-specific, with no material revisions to estimates across the remaining input-method contract portfolio and no expansion of the GMP loss provision.
2. Significant claims in contract assets fall below approximately $500M with DSO back under 78 days, and Q4 FY2026 operating cash flow lands at or above $500M for the full year.
3. FY2027 guidance sets adjusted EPS at $5.50 or better on a basis that includes normal construction management performance.

**Upgrade to BUY** additionally requires a demonstrated free cash flow inflection — FY2027 free cash flow guided at or above $600M — or a price near $55, which would put the shares below 11x a conservative $5.00 earnings base.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of the following occurs:**
1. A second GMP or claims charge above $75M in Q4 FY2026 or Q1 FY2027, or disclosure of a broad re-estimation across the construction management book.
2. Americas goodwill impairment, or book equity falling below approximately $2.0B while total liabilities to equity exceeds 4.5x.
3. Full-year FY2026 operating cash flow below $350M, or the dividend or buyback authorization being suspended for liquidity reasons.
4. An adverse IRS determination on credit methodology that raises the guided effective tax rate above 22%.

**Reassessment timeline:** immediately upon filing of the Q3 FY2026 10-Q (expected within weeks of the August 10 release), and again at the Q4 FY2026 report and FY2027 guidance in November 2026. The June 11, 2026 8-K should be read as soon as accessible.

## Conclusion

AECOM's franchise, demand backdrop and balance sheet are all better than the price implies. NSR grew 3.6% in H1 FY2026 with margin expansion, backlog is reportedly at a record, only $45.4M of debt matures near term against $2,627.5M due after FY2030, and at 10.4x a next-year consensus and 8.6x FY2025 EV/EBITDA the shares sit at roughly a 60% discount to a 27.5x peer median.

Three things prevent that from being a purchase recommendation today. The earnings base has moved: FY2026 adjusted EPS is tracking near $4.00–$4.20 against $5.26 in FY2025 and the ~$6.00 implied by May guidance.
