## Executive Summary

Deere & Company is navigating a deep agricultural equipment downcycle — revenue has declined 25% from the FY2023 peak of $61.3B to $45.7B in FY2025, and net income has been halved. The stock has rallied approximately 27% from its October 2025 trough near $433 to ~$590, driven by a strong Q1 FY2026 earnings beat ($2.42 vs. $2.11 consensus) and improving sentiment around the Construction & Forestry segment, where backlog surged 73% to $3.8B. The February 2026 earnings report sent the stock as high as $674 before it settled into the $560-$590 range.

The core tension in the DE thesis is between genuine structural strengths (dominant market position, $6.1B in FCF through the downturn, aggressive buybacks reducing share count 11% in three years, and a recovering CF segment) and severe near-term headwinds in the company's largest segment. PPA operating margins collapsed to 4.4% in Q1 FY2026 — down from 11.0% a year earlier — with $361M in quarterly tariff costs consuming roughly 41% of pre-tariff earnings. U.S./Canada large ag demand is guided down 15-20% for FY2026, and used equipment inventory overhang persists.

At ~33x trailing earnings and ~33x forward consensus, DE is priced for a recovery that has not yet arrived in its most important segment. The stock is not expensive relative to peer medians (33.8x), but the multiple does not reflect the earnings risk embedded in FY2026 guidance of $4.5-5.0B net income (implying ~$17-19 EPS vs. $18.50 trailing). The Supreme Court's invalidation of IEEPA tariffs could provide upside if previously paid tariffs are recovered, but timing and magnitude remain uncertain. We rate DE HOLD with a BUY trigger at $510-530.

## Company Overview

Deere operates four segments: Production & Precision Agriculture (PPA, 45% of equipment sales), Small Agriculture & Turf (SAT, 26%), Construction & Forestry (CF, 29%), and Financial Services. The company's competitive moat rests on three pillars: a ~2,050-location dealer network in the U.S./Canada, an integrated precision agriculture technology platform (John Deere Operations Center), and a $49B captive financing portfolio that enables equipment purchases.

The company's "Smart Industrial Operating Model" centers on precision agriculture, autonomy, and lifecycle solutions (aftermarket/SaaS). R&D spending has remained elevated at $2.3B (5.1% of revenue) even through the downcycle, signaling commitment to the technology stack. However, management has candidly acknowledged "slower than expected customer adoption" of SaaS subscription models, and SaaS revenue remains immaterial.

Deere employs approximately 73,100 people globally. The UAW contract covering U.S. manufacturing runs through November 2027, removing labor disruption risk for the near term.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Earnings

The cyclical decline is severe but well-understood by the market:

| Metric | FY2023 (Peak) | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026E (Guidance) |
|--------|---------------|--------|--------|---------------------|
| Revenue | $61.3B | $51.7B | $45.7B | ~$47-48B |
| Net Income | $10.2B | $7.1B | $5.0B | $4.5-5.0B |
| EPS | ~$34.63 | ~$25.62 | $18.50 | $17-19 |
| FCF | — | — | $6.1B | $4.5-5.5B (ops) |

The Q1 FY2026 quarter (ended February 1, 2026) showed wildly divergent segment performance. PPA revenue grew just 3% (FX-driven) while operating profit collapsed 59% to $139M on a 4.4% margin — the worst margin in the segment's recent history. SAT grew 24% with margins expanding to 9.0%. CF grew 34% with margins improving to 5.1% and operating profit more than doubling.

The tariff impact is the dominant near-term financial story. The $361M in incremental tariff costs in Q1 alone represents annualized headwinds of ~$1.4B if sustained — roughly 30% of guided FY2026 net income. As a net exporter from the U.S. (~75% of domestic sales assembled domestically), Deere faces both import cost inflation on components and retaliatory tariffs on exports.

### Balance Sheet

The consolidated D/E ratio of 3.08x and Altman Z-Score of 0.97 appear alarming but are standard artifacts of the captive finance subsidiary, which holds $49B in financing receivables funded by debt. Credit ratings remain investment grade: A+ (Fitch), A1 (Moody's), A (S&P), all with stable outlooks. Cash and equivalents stood at $6.8B at Q1 end, with $7.2B in unused credit facilities.

Financial Services credit quality is deteriorating modestly: non-performing receivables total $682M, modified financing receivables doubled YoY to $64M, and write-offs were $312M in FY2025 (up materially). The allowance for credit losses ($254M) appears thin relative to non-performing receivables, and management explicitly warns the allowance "may continue to increase in future periods." This is not yet at crisis levels but warrants monitoring.

### Capital Allocation

Deere's capital return program has been aggressive and well-timed through the downcycle. Share count has declined from 304.5M (FY2022) to 270.3M, an 11.2% reduction. The company has $7.6B remaining on its $18B buyback authorization. In Q1 FY2026 alone, $743M was returned ($302M buybacks + $441M dividends). The dividend yield at ~1.1% ($6.48 annualized) is modest but well-covered.

R&D at $2.3B annually demonstrates capital discipline in a different dimension — maintaining technology investment through the trough positions the company for the recovery but creates margin pressure during the downturn.

## Growth Analysis

### Near-Term (FY2026): Mixed Signals

The growth picture is bifurcated. PPA faces continued contraction: U.S./Canada large ag demand is guided down 15-20%, South American demand down ~5%, and EU demand flat to down 5%. Used equipment inventory overhang (combine inventories at 18% of trailing sales, up from 11%) constrains new equipment demand.

CF is the bright spot, with net sales guided up ~15% and backlog at $3.8B (up from $2.2B), driven by U.S. infrastructure spending and data center construction. SAT is also guided up ~15%, supported by dairy/livestock strength and turf recovery.

Consensus estimates reflect this tension: FY2026 EPS at $17.90 (vs. $18.50 trailing), with FY2027 at $23.02 — implying a 29% EPS recovery. The 90-day estimate revision trend is positive (+7.4% for current year, +4.2% for next year), but the 30-day trend has turned negative (-0.6% for both periods), suggesting post-earnings optimism is fading.

### Medium-Term (FY2027-2028): Recovery Thesis

The bull case rests on cyclical recovery in ag equipment — large ag is deeply depressed, and replacement demand eventually reasserts. Deere's "Leap Ambitions" target mid-cycle operating margins of 18-22% for PPA and 16-20% for SAT by 2030. If crop prices stabilize or improve, farmer cash flows recover, and used inventory normalizes, PPA could see meaningful volume and margin recovery by FY2027-2028.

The technology angle is real but unproven at scale. Precision agriculture (See & Spray, ExactApply, autonomous solutions) creates switching costs and potential recurring revenue streams. However, management's own admission that SaaS adoption has been "slower than expected" and that SaaS revenue "did not represent a significant percentage" of total revenue tempers enthusiasm. The $440M Tenna acquisition (construction tech) adds connected asset management capabilities to CF.

### Structural Growth Constraints

Global agricultural equipment demand is a mature market with GDP+ growth at best in normal cycles. Deere's growth premium must come from technology monetization, share gains, and financial engineering (buybacks). The right-to-repair movement (FTC lawsuit, $99M class action settlement) threatens the aftermarket revenue model that is central to the lifecycle solutions strategy.

## Valuation Assessment

At $590, DE trades at approximately:
- 31.9x trailing EPS ($18.50) / 32.9x forward FY2026E ($17.90)
- ~$156B market cap, ~1.1% dividend yield
- Consensus median price target: $660 (12% upside)
- Analyst range: $500-$793

Relative to industrials peers (median P/E 33.8x), DE appears fairly valued on a trailing basis. But the comparison is imprecise — UNP at 21.8x is a fundamentally different business (regulated rail), and ETN at 39.3x reflects a higher-growth electrification thesis.

DE is being priced on trough/near-trough earnings at a mid-cycle or above-mid-cycle multiple. If FY2027 estimates of $23.02 prove accurate, the stock trades at 25.6x forward — reasonable for a cyclical recovery. If the recovery disappoints (tariffs persist, crop prices remain weak, ag demand doesn't inflect), the $17-19 EPS range implies 31-35x with limited earnings growth, which is expensive for a cyclical industrial.

The stock has rallied 25%+ from its October 2025 lows and 36% from the 52-week low of $433. Much of the easily accessible upside from trough recognition has already been captured. The remaining upside depends on confirmed earnings inflection, which hasn't materialized in PPA.

## Competitive Landscape

Deere's competitive position is strong and stable. The company holds dominant share in North American large ag equipment (estimated 50-60% in row-crop tractors and combines), supported by the industry's most extensive dealer network and the only fully integrated precision agriculture technology platform among ag OEMs.

Key competitive dynamics:
- **Ag equipment:** AGCO and CNH Industrial are the primary competitors. Both face similar cyclical headwinds. Deere's technology platform (See & Spray, Operations Center, autonomous capability) provides a widening differentiation gap, though adoption rates remain the open question.
- **Construction:** Caterpillar dominates globally, and Deere holds a strong #2 position in North America with the Wirtgen roadbuilding portfolio providing incremental global reach. Chinese manufacturers (SANY, XCMG, Liugong) are gaining share internationally at lower price points.
- **Technology:** The 10-K acknowledges "non-traditional competitors, including technology-focused companies and start-up ventures" entering agricultural technology. This is the long-term competitive risk — if precision agriculture technology becomes modular and vendor-agnostic, Deere's integrated approach loses its differentiation premium.

The right-to-repair regulatory pressure is a competitive concern. If the FTC lawsuit results in mandated access to repair tools and software, the aftermarket revenue moat narrows and independent repair shops gain viability, reducing dealer channel economics.

## Risk Assessment

### Primary Risks

**1. Tariff Persistence.** At the current $361M/quarter run rate, tariffs consume ~$1.4B annually. The Supreme Court's IEEPA invalidation could provide partial relief, but timing and applicability remain uncertain. If tariffs persist through FY2026, PPA margins may not recover to the 11-13% guided range.

**2. Agricultural Cycle Extension.** If commodity prices remain depressed and farmer income doesn't recover, the large ag downcycle could extend into FY2027. PPA backlog has declined to $4.0B from $5.2B, and used inventory overhang constrains new equipment demand.

**3. Right-to-Repair Structural Risk.** The $99M class action settlement requires Deere to provide repair tools and software to farmers for 10 years. An unfavorable FTC lawsuit resolution could permanently impair aftermarket economics. Combined, these represent a medium-term threat to the lifecycle solutions revenue model.

**4. Financial Services Credit Deterioration.** Non-performing receivables at $682M, write-offs rising, and management warning of further allowance increases. In a prolonged farm income downturn, credit losses could accelerate given the $49B portfolio.

**5. Technology Monetization Uncertainty.** Despite $2.3B in annual R&D, SaaS revenue remains immaterial and adoption has been slower than expected. Precision ag may become a competitive necessity (table stakes) rather than a premium-priced differentiator.

ATM IV has surged to 77.0% (1-week tenor) versus 28.7% historical volatility — a 48.3pp spread indicating extreme near-term event risk. The term structure is in steep backwardation (77.0% near-term vs. 30.5% 6-month), consistent with an imminent catalyst. The 1-week expiry on May 1 implies a ±0.9% move, but the 3-month tenor shows P/C volume ratio at 2.33, indicating institutional put buying at longer durations.

IV rose from 35.1% to 77.0% over 10 days (+41.9pp) while the stock declined only 2.8%, suggesting the market is positioning for a significant move — likely around upcoming earnings or tariff-related policy developments. Short interest at 1.7% of float with 3.5 days to cover is low — there is no crowded short to unwind. The options signal points to informed hedging against downside risk, which aligns with the fundamental picture of stretched valuation on uncertain earnings.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($660-$700, 12-19% upside)

Tariff relief materializes through IEEPA reversal, with Deere recovering previously paid amounts and reducing forward cost burden by $1B+. Agricultural commodity prices stabilize, triggering replacement demand in FY2027. CF continues its recovery trajectory, contributing incremental earnings growth. PPA margins recover to the 11-13% guided range by Q3-Q4 FY2026 as tariff costs abate and production efficiencies improve. FY2027 EPS reaches $22-24, and the stock re-rates to 28-30x forward, yielding a $660-720 price range. Share buybacks continue reducing the denominator by 3-4% annually.

### Bear Case ($430-$480, 19-27% downside)

Tariffs persist through FY2026 without relief. Agricultural commodity prices remain depressed due to weak export demand and high global inventories. PPA margins fail to recover from the Q1 trough, and full-year PPA margins come in at 8-10% vs. 11-13% guided. Financial Services credit losses accelerate, requiring higher provisions. FY2026 EPS comes in at $15-16 (below guided range), and the stock de-rates to 28-30x trough earnings, implying $430-480. The FTC right-to-repair resolution adds a structural overhang.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

### Upgrade Triggers (HOLD → BUY)

1. **Price retracement to $510-530.** This would represent ~22-23x FY2027E EPS ($23.02), providing adequate margin of safety for a cyclical recovery thesis.

2. **Confirmed tariff relief.** Specifically: IEEPA tariff recovery proceedings yielding quantified refund amounts, or new trade policy reducing quarterly tariff impact below $150M (vs. $361M in Q1). Either would directly lift PPA margins and validate recovery timing.

3. **PPA operating margin recovery above 10% for a full quarter.** A return to double-digit margins would confirm the tariff impact is being managed and the downcycle in large ag is bottoming.

### Downgrade Triggers (HOLD → AVOID)

1. **FY2026 net income guidance cut below $4.0B.** This would signal the downcycle is deeper and more prolonged than management expects.

2. **Financial Services non-performing receivables exceed $1.0B** or write-offs exceed $500M annually. This would indicate a credit cycle developing within the finance subsidiary.

3. **Stock trades above $650 without corresponding earnings improvement** (i.e., trailing or forward P/E exceeds 37x without FY2027 estimates rising above $24). This would represent excessive speculation on unconfirmed recovery.

### Reassessment Timeline

The Q2 FY2026 earnings report (likely mid-May 2026) is the critical checkpoint. Q2 typically captures the start of the planting season — spring order patterns will provide the first real-time read on whether the large ag downturn is bottoming. Tariff policy clarity should also improve by mid-2026 as IEEPA reversal implications are resolved.

## Conclusion

Deere remains one of the highest-quality industrial franchises globally — dominant market position, strong FCF generation through the downcycle, disciplined capital return, and genuine technology optionality. The CF recovery is real and provides an earnings floor. However, at $590 the stock has already priced in substantial recovery expectations, trading at ~32x earnings while its largest segment posted 4.4% operating margins and faces $1.4B+ in annualized tariff costs.

This is the third consecutive HOLD rating, and the fundamental picture has not changed enough to warrant a different call. The valuation score has modestly declined from the prior assessment because the 25%+ rally since October has compressed the risk/reward. The stock needs confirmed tariff relief or visible PPA margin recovery to justify adding at these levels. At $510-530, the risk/reward improves materially. At current prices, the asymmetry favors patience.
