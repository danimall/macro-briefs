## Executive Summary

ScanSource is two businesses stapled together: a $3.0B low-margin specialty hardware distribution operation (STS, 96.6% of revenue, 11.0% gross margin, 2.0% operating margin) and a US-only technology-services agency business (Intelisys & Advisory, 3.4% of revenue, 98.8% gross margin, 31.3% operating margin). In the March 2026 quarter, I&A generated 24.0% of consolidated gross profit and roughly 31% of pre-corporate adjusted EBITDA. Any assessment of the equity is substantially an assessment of Intelisys, with a cyclical hardware distributor attached.

The March quarter delivered the revenue inflection investors had waited three years for: net sales of $766.8M, up 8.8% reported and 7.6% organic constant-currency, with management describing growth "across most technologies, especially networking and security" after a 19.7% cumulative revenue decline from the FY2023 peak. The stock has responded — up roughly 45% from the $36.15 average at which the company repurchased its own shares in March 2026, and up 15.5% since our late-May review. What the revenue headline conceals is that the same quarter produced a 3.1% *decline* in GAAP net income. Gross margin fell 25bp because the recovering hardware business dilutes the blended rate; adjusted EBITDA margin fell 32bp; SG&A grew 12% against 7% gross-profit growth; the tax rate stepped up roughly four points. Reported diluted EPS rose 5.4% entirely on an 8.1% reduction in share count. Across nine months, roughly two-thirds of the 13.6% EPS growth came from buybacks rather than earnings.

Meanwhile the high-margin engine is stalling. I&A net sales fell 1.5% year over year, and CEO Mike Baur said plainly on the May 7 call that new order growth "is not growing at the rate we would like to see," that a prior 12-18 month investment cycle underdelivered, and that the new Converged Communications reorganization will not show results "for anywhere from six to 18 months." The cost is in SG&A now. Layer on a 2% goodwill cushion in the segment carrying 96.6% of revenue with the annual test result due within days, consensus estimates falling across every forecast period, and near-term implied volatility of 102.8% pricing an ±11-14.5% move into the August 20 print, and the risk/reward from $52.58 is close to balanced. We maintain HOLD, but with a lower score than our prior two reviews — the first decline in this trajectory — because price rose 15.5% while forward estimates fell 1-4%.

## Company Overview

Incorporated in South Carolina in 1992 and headquartered in Greenville, ScanSource intermediates between roughly 500 suppliers and approximately 25,000 channel partners across the US, Canada and Brazil, carrying about 65,000 products with ~2,100 employees. Effective July 1, 2024 the company re-segmented by sales model.

**Specialty Technology Solutions (STS)** is the wholesale/resale business: ScanSource takes title, holds inventory and bears the receivable. Categories are mobility/barcode (AIDC), point-of-sale, payments, physical security, networking, communications and managed connectivity. Q3 FY26: $740.8M net sales, 11.0% gross margin, 3.3% adjusted EBITDA margin.

**Intelisys & Advisory (I&A)** is an agency business comprising Intelisys, Channel Exchange, RPM Software and Resourcive. The mechanics matter: the *supplier* (AT&T, Comcast Business, Lumen, RingCentral, Verizon, Zoom) bills the end user directly; ScanSource collects a commission and pays a share to the sales advisor, booking the retained commission as net sales. It holds no inventory, no end-user contract and no end-user receivable. Q3 FY26: $26.0M net sales at 98.8% gross margin and 42% adjusted EBITDA margin. A related exposure is that ScanSource extends financing to certain Intelisys advisors secured by their future commission flows, making it a creditor to its own agents.

Two structural exposures frame the risk profile. Supplier concentration is acute: Cisco and Zebra each exceeded 10% of FY2025 net sales under non-exclusive agreements terminable on 30-120 days' notice (the Q3 10-Q filed Amendment No. 24 to the Cisco distributor agreement). No single channel partner exceeded 10% of sales, so the concentration sits on the supply side. Physical concentration is equally notable: one 741,000 sq ft distribution center in Southaven, Mississippi serves most of North America, running on a single North American IT system — a configuration that produced a nine-business-day order-processing outage during the May 2023 ransomware attack.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue trajectory and the quality of the inflection

| (FY, June-end) | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | 9M FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | $3,787.7M | $3,259.8M | $3,040.8M | $2,272.9M |
| Gross margin | 11.86% | 12.24% | 13.44% | 13.97% |
| Operating income | $135.9M | $90.3M | $85.2M | $66.9M |
| Operating margin | 3.59% | 2.77% | 2.80% | 2.94% |
| Net income | $89.8M | $77.1M | $71.5M | $53.3M |
| Diluted EPS (GAAP) | — | $3.06 | $3.00 | $2.42 |
| Diluted EPS (non-GAAP) | — | $3.08 | $3.57 | $2.80 |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $179.9M | $140.7M | $144.7M | — |

Revenue fell 19.7% from the FY2023 peak through FY2025. Nine-month FY26 growth is +2.0% reported / +1.1% organic, so the +8.8% March quarter is carrying the entire recovery narrative.

The earnings decomposition of that quarter is the analytical crux. Net sales +8.8%; gross profit +7.0%; SG&A +12.0%; operating income +2.4%; adjusted EBITDA +1.7% with margin contracting 32bp to 4.65%; GAAP net income −3.1% to $16.9M; GAAP diluted EPS +5.4% to $0.78. The gap between the net income decline and the EPS gain is share count: implied diluted shares of 21.67M versus 23.57M, a reduction of 8.1% (0.969 ÷ 0.919 = 1.054, reconciling the +5.4%). The same arithmetic for nine months: net income +4.7%, shares −7.9%, EPS +13.6%.

Two identifiable drags explain the deleverage. SG&A growth of 12% reflects DataXoom's consolidated cost base plus the new Intelisys/Converged investments management flagged. And the effective tax rate stepped from FY25's 24.2% to 29.8% in the quarter, with FY26 guided to ~27.9-28.2% — roughly $3.7M, or about $0.18 per share, on ~$95M of pretax income.

### Gross margin: the multi-year uptrend has the wrong sign going forward

Consolidated gross margin rose from 11.86% (FY23) to 13.44% (FY25) to 13.97% (9M FY26), and part of that is genuine — management stated STS margin gains came from sales mix and durably normalized freight rather than one-off factors, with STS gross margin at 11.0% versus 10.9% a year earlier. But much of the FY24→FY25 rise was denominator arithmetic: STS revenue fell 7.1% while ~99%-margin I&A revenue grew, mechanically lifting the blend. In Q3 FY26 consolidated gross margin actually *fell* 25bp year over year precisely because low-margin hardware grew faster. Investors extrapolating the gross-margin uptrend into a hardware upcycle have it backwards; the recovery is margin-dilutive by construction.

The recurring-revenue share of gross profit also declined to 34.7% from 35.5%. Both facts hold simultaneously: recurring gross profit *dollars* grew roughly 4.7% (to ~$37.2M from ~$35.5M), while recurring revenue's *share* fell 80bp. Management's stated three-year goal is to grow the gross-profit contribution from recurring revenue; the ratio is currently moving against it.

### Cash flow: strong, with a large working-capital component

Nine-month operating cash flow was $125.4M (from $104.7M) and free cash flow $118.6M (from $98.9M) on capex of only $6.8M. Management raised FY26 FCF guidance to "at least $90M" from "at least $80M" while holding revenue ($3.0-3.1B) and adjusted EBITDA ($140-150M) unchanged.

Composition matters. Within nine-month operating cash flow, accounts payable was a $44.9M *source* versus a $50.4M *use* in the prior-year period — a ~$95M year-over-year swing. Balance-sheet AP rose $48.1M to $646.7M while inventory was essentially flat at $486.6M. Because inventory did not build alongside payables, this reads as a genuine extension of supplier terms rather than volume effect: derived days payable moved from roughly 77 days at June 2025 to roughly 89 days at March 2026. Excluding the AP change, nine-month FCF would be approximately $73.7M rather than $118.6M. A single-date DPO can be distorted by quarter-end purchase timing and supplier-program payment schedules, so we would not treat 12 days as a permanent structural gain.

Working against that, company-disclosed DSO rose to 74 days from 70 at June 2025, inventory turns slowed to 5.4x from 5.9x, and the provision for doubtful accounts *fell* to $5.4M for nine months from $7.7M even as the allowance grew to $28.9M (roughly 4.4% of gross receivables). Lower provisioning alongside slower collections is a mild tension; plausible benign readings include an unfavorable prior-year base carrying a specific Brazil reserve, and the mechanical inflation of period-end DSO when revenue grows 8.8%. The 10-K also states that as the company competes for business, "typical payment terms tend to get longer" — rising DSO may be a deliberate competitive choice.

On the constructive side, net working capital investment fell $52.3M to $468.4M, and the derived cash conversion cycle improved to roughly 53 days from 55. The improvement is driven by payables.

Nine-month FCF of $118.6M already exceeds the full-year "at least $90M" guide, so management is signalling a Q4 working-capital outflow. We do not annualize the Q3 figure ($69M would imply an implausible ~$276M run rate). A normalized full-year view sits closer to $110-130M reported, and a sustainable through-cycle figure closer to $95-105M (adjusted EBITDA $145M less ~$8M interest, ~$27M cash taxes, ~$10M capex) — a 9-10% yield on the current market capitalization.

### Balance sheet

At March 31, 2026: cash $120.3M against total debt of $102.0M, a net cash position of roughly $18.3M; total assets $1,805.8M; equity $906.3M. Altman Z of 3.65 sits in the safe zone. The headline D/E of 0.97 is total-liabilities-to-equity; funded debt to equity is about 0.11.

The December 18, 2025 refinancing is a credit positive: ScanSource replaced its JPMorgan-led facility (revolver plus term loan maturing September 2027) roughly 21 months early with a five-year PNC-agented $400M multicurrency revolver plus a $100M term loan, downsizing the term loan by $50M and upsizing the revolver by $50M. Covenants are unchanged at leverage ≤3.50x and interest coverage ≥3.00x, with full availability and compliance at quarter-end; the term-loan effective rate fell to 4.67% from 5.43%. The FY25 revolver average daily balance was $0.3M, confirming the revolver functions as standby capacity rather than funded leverage. Against a 3.50x covenant and ~$145M of guided EBITDA, theoretical debt capacity is ~$507M; at a conservative 2.0x net leverage the company could deploy roughly $310M.

### Capital allocation

ScanSource has never paid a dividend, and the credit agreement restricts them. All return is via repurchase, and execution has been good: share count fell from 24,243,848 (6/30/24) to 22,217,421 (6/30/25) to 20,781,041 (3/31/26) — 14.3% in 21 months. FY25 repurchases were 2,483,299 shares for $106.5M (~$42.89 average); Q3 FY26 monthly averages were $40.61, $37.16 and $36.15. $146.4M of authorization remained at March 31.

The re-rating has materially reduced that lever's remaining power. At March's $36.15 average, $146.4M would retire ~4.05M shares (19.5% of the count); at $52.58 it retires ~2.79M (13.4%). Given that roughly two-thirds of nine-month EPS growth came from share count, this matters. One accounting note: nine months of repurchases consumed the entire nine months of earnings — equity was flat at $906.3M and retained earnings declined $5.9M despite $53.3M of net income, because repurchases in excess of par and APIC are charged to retained earnings.

*A data reconciliation note:* the market-data provider reports 20,324,529 shares against the 10-Q's 20,781,041 at March 31, implying roughly 456,000 additional shares retired in the June quarter. We use the filed count for balance-sheet arithmetic and ~20.5M for forward per-share targets.

## Growth Analysis

The revenue cycle has turned in North America, and Q3's breadth — "most of our technologies showed growth," per CFO Steve Jones — is the most encouraging forward datapoint the company produced. Management explicitly linked that breadth to FY2027 setup. Networking and security led, consistent with the enterprise refresh cycle running alongside broader infrastructure spending.

Three offsets constrain how much to pay for it.

**Implied Q4 guidance is conspicuously wide.** Nine-month revenue of $2,272.9M against a $3.0-3.1B full-year guide implies Q4 of $727.1M to $827.1M, against a derived Q4 FY25 actual of $812.5M — a range of −10.5% to +1.8% year over year, immediately following a +8.8% quarter. Pressed by Northcoast to narrow it, Jones said only that management does "not want to get over our skis." Asked whether Q3 borrowed from Q4 amid component-pricing volatility, he said visibility on pull-forwards "is always hard" but that the company did not believe it saw material pull-forwards. Either management is sandbagging a range it declined to narrow, or it sees real risk of sequential deceleration.

**The profit engine is flat.** I&A net sales fell 1.5%. Intelisys net billings — a third-party volume metric representing amounts billed by suppliers to end users, not ScanSource revenue and not backlog — reached a record ~$2.88B annualized, but grew only 4.5% in FY25, and the implied retained take rate is about 3.07%. Baur's own characterization is that order growth is below where he wants it, that a year of prior investment underdelivered, and that the new Converged Communications reorganization (merging STS's Specialty Communications team with Intelisys CX under Katherine White) will take 6-18 months to show results. The strategic logic — bidirectional cross-sell, helping VARs sell cloud recurring revenue and helping agents attach hardware — is sound and unproven. Jones offered a more benign read on the quarter specifically, attributing the decline to lumpy Resourcive *services* revenue rather than Intelisys commissions.

**Consensus is moving the wrong way.** Estimates fell across every period over 90 days: current quarter −3.3% to $1.14, next quarter −4.6% to $1.04, current year −0.5% to $3.94, next year −4.3% to $4.41. Four analysts cover the name. This is the single cleanest contradiction of a growth-inflection thesis, and it has occurred while the stock rose 15.5%. The earnings track record is also uneven — two beats, one in-line, and a 22.4% miss in the December 2025 quarter.

Brazil growth is currency, not demand: nine-month revenue +0.7% reported but −5.8% constant currency; Q3 +18.0% reported but +6.2% constant currency, implying a BRL tailwind of roughly 11% in the quarter. Brazil is ~7.6% of revenue, so the consolidated impact is bounded, but a real reversal would convert it back into a visible reported drag. Q3's +6.2% constant-currency print is the first positive in the reviewed data, so the trend may be turning there too.

Acquisitions are working where they've been aimed. Advantix (August 2024) and DataXoom (October 2025, ~$31.8M including $13.4M initial contingent consideration) have lifted recurring revenue to roughly 15% of STS gross profit — approximately $12.2M of recurring gross profit inside the lowest-margin segment. That is genuine mix improvement. The associated cost: total contingent consideration of $29.04M at March 31, of which $16.4M is current (up from $1.3M at June 2025), including Resourcive's $12.7M due in fiscal 2027.

Restructuring will not help margins. Two programs targeted ~$20.5M of combined annualized SG&A savings; SG&A nonetheless rose 7.5% over nine months and 12.0% in Q3. Savings were redeployed into acquisitions and growth investment, not banked — though consolidated acquisition cost bases make organic and acquired SG&A impossible to separate from the disclosures.

## Valuation Assessment

At $52.58 on 20.78M shares, market capitalization is $1.093B. Net cash of $18.3M gives an enterprise value of $1.074B, or $1.103B including contingent consideration.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV / FY26E adjusted EBITDA ($145M guide midpoint) | 7.41x (7.61x incl. contingent consideration) |
| P/E on FY26E consensus non-GAAP EPS $3.94 | 13.3x |
| P/E on FY27E consensus non-GAAP EPS $4.41 | 11.9x |
| P/E on derived FY26E GAAP EPS ~$3.27-3.34 | 15.8-16.1x |
| FCF yield on FY26 guide (≥$90M) | 8.2% |
| FCF yield on normalized $95-105M | 8.7-9.6% |

Peer medians in Technology Distributors are 21.9x trailing P/E and 0.9% yield (CNXN 22.2, PLUS 19.2, NSIT 21.9, AVT 24.1, ARW 13.0). SCSC trades near 16x trailing GAAP. The discount is not a mispricing to arbitrage: CNXN, PLUS and NSIT carry meaningful services mixes and stronger organic growth; the closest structural analog for low-margin, supplier-concentrated distribution is Arrow at 13.0x. SCSC also pays no dividend, is roughly a twentieth the size of TD SYNNEX and Ingram Micro, and carries Brazil and single-site operational exposure. A mid-teens multiple on non-GAAP earnings with a high-single-digit EV/EBITDA is, on our read, approximately the right place for this asset — not a screaming discount.

Two things do support the valuation. First, EV/EBITDA of 7.4x is mid-range rather than expensive for distribution, and 11.9x FY27 consensus embeds no re-rating for a successful Intelisys inflection. Second, the balance sheet: net cash plus a $400M undrawn revolver plus $146.4M of remaining buyback authorization equal to 13.4% of shares gives management genuine capacity to defend per-share earnings.

What keeps the valuation score at the midpoint rather than higher is that the arithmetic to base case is thin. Anchoring on 13x FY27 consensus of $4.41 gives $57.3; 7.75x an FY27 EBITDA of roughly $152M gives $1.178B EV, $1.196B equity, and roughly $58 per share on ~20.5M shares. Blending, base fair value is approximately $57 — about 8% above spot. Analyst targets bracket this: mean $54.67, median $50.00, high $71, low $43, on a 2 buy / 2 hold split. The guidance in our framework that consensus targets are floors applies to inflection stocks with *rising* estimates; here estimates are falling, so we treat the consensus band as a reasonable fair-value range rather than a stale floor.

## Competitive Landscape

In commodity hardware distribution, scale dictates supplier economics, and ScanSource is structurally sub-scale. Its defense is specialization: an 11.0% STS gross margin materially above broadline distributor norms, reflecting concentration in AIDC/POS, payments, physical security and communications where pre-sales engineering and technical enablement are required. Direct competitors are BlueStar in AIDC/POS and ADI/Wesco in physical security; broadliners Ingram Micro and TD SYNNEX compete around the edges.

Intelisys is the real structural asset. A technology services distributor with a 42% adjusted EBITDA margin, no inventory and no end-user receivable exposure is difficult to replicate — it requires an installed base of agents and supplier agency contracts accumulated over years. Named competitors Avant and Telarus are private, limiting benchmarking. If the multiple is to expand, it will be because Intelisys new-order growth accelerates.

The trajectory reading is mixed rather than clearly improving. Managed connectivity acquisitions are genuinely reshaping STS toward recurring revenue. Against that, the agency segment's share of consolidated gross profit is falling, Intelisys net billings grow at low-to-mid single digits, and supplier concentration remains a live vulnerability: FY25's STS gross-profit walk credited "favorable supplier program recognition" as part of a $26.0M mix benefit, and margin gains sourced from supplier programs are less durable than gains from freight or mix. The 10-K also warns that stock rotation rights and price protection provisions are "becoming less standard, subject to change, limited in scope" — eroding the inventory-risk buffer that has historically protected distributor margins.

One cross-current from the broader component environment deserves naming. Memory and component cost inflation cuts both ways for a distributor pricing on percentage margins: higher ASPs can lift gross profit dollars, while simultaneously risking demand destruction and inventory-value exposure if prices reverse. Northcoast raised this on the May call; management did not endorse the framing, and this remains our inference rather than a disclosed company view.

## Risk Assessment

**The 2% goodwill cushion, with the test result days away.** At the April 30, 2025 annual testing date, STS reporting-unit fair value exceeded carrying value by only 2%, against STS goodwill of $159.8M. STS goodwill has since risen to $173.9M (DataXoom +$13.6M, FX +$0.6M), and the acquisition added intangibles and net assets to carrying value. Improved STS performance since the test date (+9.2% revenue, +6% segment adjusted EBITDA) pushes fair value up; the DataXoom additions push carrying value up. The FY26 test disclosure arrives with the 10-K around August 20-21. Precision on consequences matters: an impairment would hit GAAP net income, GAAP EPS, book equity, ROE and equity-based Z-Score terms. It would **not** affect adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow, non-GAAP EPS, or compliance with the leverage and interest-coverage covenants. A full write-off of $173.9M would be ~19% of equity, or roughly $8.37 per share pre-tax, but a partial impairment or a widened cushion are both more likely outcomes. A 2% cushion says the estimate is close, not that impairment is probable.

**Supplier concentration with short notice.** Cisco and Zebra each above 10% of net sales, non-exclusive, terminable on 30-120 days.

**Single-site distribution and demonstrated cyber fragility.** One Mississippi DC and one North American IT system, against a May 2023 ransomware event that halted order processing for nine business days with data posted to the dark web (FY25 recognized a $6.7M insurance recovery).

**Brazil.** Underlying demand contracting on a nine-month constant-currency basis, customer-specific bad debt disclosed in SG&A, increasing competition, and pre-acquisition Network1 tax contingencies recorded at $3.8M with a reasonably-possible range to $15.6M against $3.5M of available escrow. Recovery above escrow depends on seller performance, which the filings do not address.

**Structural margin thinness.** At a 2.0% STS operating margin, small moves in freight, mix or bad debt swing segment profit. Management's own Level 3 contingent-consideration models assume 60% adjusted EBITDA volatility for STS versus 45% for I&A — an encoded view that hardware earnings are highly variable.

**Governance and disclosure gaps.** An Item 4.01 auditor-change 8-K (October 16, 2025) and its amendment (November 6, 2025) could not be retrieved; the dismissed firm, engaged firm and whether any disagreements were disclosed are unknown. A routine rotation with an Exhibit 16.1 concurrence letter is the most common explanation for such an amendment, but this is unverified, and it matters more than usual alongside a 2% goodwill cushion and $29.0M of Level 3 contingent consideration. A May 29, 2026 Item 5.02 filing also remains unidentified.

**A possible re-segmentation.** The Converged Communications unit crosses the current STS/I&A boundary. If it becomes a reporting segment, goodwill would need reallocation and re-testing — which interacts directly with the thin STS cushion and would reduce year-over-year comparability. Management said nothing about changing external reporting, and leaving reporting untouched is the more common outcome; this is a hypothesis.

**Management alignment.** CEO Baur is also Chairman. FY25 total compensation of $8,231,119 was up ~40% from $5,875,799 in a year when revenue fell 6.7%, net income fell 7.2%, and the proxy's own indexed TSR declined to 174 from 184 while the peer group rose to 187 from 163. "Total compensation" is grant-date fair value, and the smaller rise in compensation-actually-paid ($6,741,272) is consistent with a redesigned or front-loaded award, so we would not infer a governance failure from the headline — but the direction versus TSR merits a question. Including EPS among performance metrics while buybacks are the sole return channel creates a mechanical assist; adjusted EBITDA, FCF conversion and ROIC are buyback-neutral offsets.

**Insider activity.** One clean positive: director Charles Alexander Mathis bought 6,000 shares for $211,230 on February 11, 2026 at $34.63-$35.32, within about a dollar of the eventual low. Against that, CEO Baur sold ~25,000 shares at ~$35.76-$36.40 on March 13-16, 2026 — the same month the company repurchased at a $36.15 average — following a September 2025 exercise-and-sell of an expiring grant (little signal) and ~20,000 shares in December at ~$40.81-$42.09. CFO Jones sold 5,020 shares at $42.30 in December. The September activity carries little information; the March sales at the lows are less flattering, though a pre-existing 10b5-1 plan, taxes or diversification are plausible, and Baur's residual 157,512 shares (~$8.3M) keep his economic alignment substantial. The honest summary: net officer selling, one modest director purchase near the bottom, no C-suite open-market buying.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing the August 20 print as a genuine binary. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 102.8% against 38.6% one-year realized — a 64-point spread, at the 81st percentile within the chain — with a pronounced backwardation (102.8% at one week, 46.6% at one month, 37.9% at six months). The nearest expiry implies a ±14.5% move; the one-month tenor implies ±12.0%. ATM IV has risen from 33.0% to 123.0% since our late-May snapshot while the price rose 15.5%. Put/call open interest sits at 0.84 and the skew is reported as call-heavy (−36.0% at the near tenor, −12.2% at one month). Short interest is a modest 7.3% of float at 5.4 days to cover.

The reading: the market is not pricing direction so much as size, and near-term positioning is tilted toward calls into an event that will resolve Q4 delivery, the goodwill test and FY27 guidance simultaneously. Call-tilted positioning into a wide-guidance print raises the risk of an unwind if the result disappoints. Volatility this rich relative to realized is not a favorable entry environment for new long stock; anyone wanting exposure through the print is better served letting the options market carry it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (to $68, +29%)

The hardware cycle turn is real and broad — Q3's "growth across most technologies" continues into FY27, and the August 20 guide validates it with a revenue number above the $3.0-3.1B FY26 base. The goodwill test comes in with a widened cushion, removing an overhang. The Converged Communications reorganization begins converting VAR relationships into Intelisys orders at the fast end of the 6-18 month window, restoring growth in the 42%-EBITDA-margin segment and re-rating the mix. SG&A growth decelerates as acquisition integration laps and the investment cycle completes, restoring operating leverage on rising gross profit. Management deploys the remaining $146.4M authorization plus revolver capacity, retiring another 13%+ of shares. FY27 non-GAAP EPS reaches $4.75 and the multiple expands toward 14.5x on a demonstrated recurring-revenue mix shift.

### Bear Case (to $41, −22%)

Q4 lands in the lower half of the implied −10.5% to +1.8% range, confirming Q3 as a timing artifact rather than a cycle turn, and FY27 guidance is conservative. The margin-dilutive mix persists: hardware grows, gross margin compresses, SG&A stays elevated on unproductive Intelisys investment, and adjusted EBITDA is flat despite revenue growth. The goodwill test produces a partial STS impairment — non-cash and covenant-irrelevant, but a sentiment event on a small-cap with four covering analysts and call-tilted positioning. The Q4 working-capital reversal management is implicitly guiding to (nine-month FCF already exceeds the full-year floor) shrinks reported FCF and exposes how much of the nine-month figure was payables extension. The BRL tailwind reverses and Brazil returns to a visible reported drag. FY27 EPS settles near $3.90 and the multiple compresses to 10.5x — roughly the February 2026 valuation, when the stock traded at $33.76-$36.

### Base Case (to $57, +8%)

The cycle recovery is genuine but modest and margin-dilutive. FY27 revenue grows mid-single digits, adjusted EBITDA reaches roughly $150-155M, and non-GAAP EPS lands near consensus $4.41 with continued buyback support. Intelisys stabilizes without inflecting inside twelve months. The goodwill cushion widens modestly on improved STS performance. The stock holds a 12-13x forward multiple and 7.5-8x EV/EBITDA.

## Conclusion

ScanSource is a well-financed, competently allocated small-cap distributor that has been re-rated on the wrong metric. Revenue inflected 8.8% in the March quarter after a three-year, 20% decline, and the market paid for it — the stock is roughly 45% above the price at which the company was buying its own shares five months ago. But that quarter produced a 3.1% decline in net income, a 25bp gross-margin contraction and a 32bp EBITDA-margin contraction, because the recovering business is the 11%-gross-margin one and the stalling business is the 99%-gross-margin one. Reported EPS growth was share count. Consensus has cut FY27 estimates 4.3% over ninety days while the stock rose.

The offsetting case is real and keeps this from being a REDUCE. Net cash, a $400M undrawn revolver refinanced 21 months early on better terms, a 9-10% sustainable free-cash-flow yield, 11.9x forward consensus, and $146.4M of remaining authorization equal to 13.4% of shares give management genuine ability to defend per-share earnings if the cycle disappoints. Intelisys, even flat, is a 42%-EBITDA-margin asset that a broadline distributor cannot easily replicate.

The decision comes down to timing. In six days the company discloses FY26 results, the goodwill test on a reporting unit whose cushion was 2% at the last measurement, and its first FY27 guidance — into implied volatility of 102.8% against 38.6% realized, with positioning tilted toward calls. Base case fair value of roughly $57 offers 8% upside against 22% downside in the bear case. That is not an entry point. **HOLD**, with a lower score than our prior reviews, and a preference for revisiting either after the print or in the mid-$40s.
