**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

The Andersons is a diversified North American agriculture and renewable-fuels company undergoing a meaningful change in earnings composition. While it is classified as a Consumer Staples food distributor, the actual driver is a renewables business that now consolidates 100% of its four ethanol plants and is ramping a federal clean-fuel tax credit (Section 45Z) from $35M in 2025 toward a guided $90-100M in 2026. Q1 2026 net income attributable to ANDE jumped to $33.2M ($0.97 diluted) from $0.3M ($0.01) a year earlier, with Renewables segment income rising to $39.6M and Agribusiness swinging back to positive pretax income. Consensus EPS estimates have risen +23.9% (current quarter) and +14.4% (current year) over 90 days, and all three covering analysts rate the stock BUY with targets of $75-90 against a current price of $69.21.

The investment debate reduces to one question: how durable and how policy-dependent is this earnings step-up? Management has guided to a run-rate exit-2026 EPS above $4.30 and a long-range target of $7 exiting 2028, against ~$2.80 GAAP EPS in 2025. The mechanics of getting there — full ethanol ownership (which eliminates the large noncontrolling-interest leakage), Skyland Grain contribution, growth capex coming online, and a normalizing ag cycle — are real and partly already visible. A substantial portion of the 2026-2028 earnings bridge rests on the 45Z credit, a single federal program that expires in 2029, sells in an immature transfer market typically at discounts to face value, and is exposed to GREET-model and prevailing-wage eligibility risk. Failure to meet wage/apprenticeship standards cuts the credit to one-fifth of value.

We rate ANDE a HOLD with a growth-oriented bias. The operational improvement is genuine and estimate momentum is strong, which supports the position. The thesis is unusually levered to politically-sensitive federal energy policy, the former CEO has been selling heavily into the run-up with no offsetting insider buying, and near-term options are pricing imminent stress (66% ATM IV vs 34% realized, in backwardation). At $69.21, below the lowest analyst target, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We would add on confirmation of the 45Z monetization at guided levels and on a pullback that resolves the rich front-end vol.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1947 and headquartered in Maumee, Ohio, The Andersons operates two segments following its January 2025 reorganization:

**Agribusiness (~75% of revenue, $8.26B FY25 sales):** Grain merchandising and logistics, terminal elevator operations (~271M bushels storage), plant nutrients/fertilizer, and a growing premium-ingredients business serving CPG customers. It earns elevation margins (basis appreciation, spreads, storage fees) plus merchandising and risk-management fees. This is a thin-margin, asset-intensive, highly seasonal physical-commodity business concentrated in the Eastern Grain Belt, with a growing Western footprint (Skyland Grain in SW Kansas/TX Panhandle, plus Nebraska, Idaho, Louisiana, and a Houston port presence).

**Renewables (~25% of revenue, $2.75B FY25 sales):** Four ethanol plants (IA, IN, MI, OH; ~405M nameplate gallons, run above nameplate; ~789M gallons shipped FY25) plus a merchandising/trade portfolio in ethanol, DDG, corn oil, and renewable feedstocks. The two segments are vertically synergistic: Agribusiness originates the corn that feeds the plants.

ANDE is a $2.3B mid-cap in a sector dominated by ADM, Bunge, Cargill, and farmer cooperatives, some of which are also customers. It positions itself as a nimble regional asset-footprint player rather than a global-scale leader.

## Financial Analysis

Profitability is solid for the business model but cyclically pressured. The Altman Z-Score of 4.04 sits in the safe zone, and Piotroski signals are positive (positive net income, OCF exceeding net income, current ratio 1.41).

**Revenue decline is misleading and should not drive the analysis.** Reported revenue fell from $17.3B (2022) to $11.0B (2025), a ~36% drop. This reflects commodity-price pass-through, not lost business. Gross profit, the better gauge for a commodity intermediary, has been resilient: $684M (2022), $745M (2023), $694M (2024), $714M (2025). Any screen keying on revenue growth will misclassify this company.

Net income attributable fell from ~$114M (2024) to ~$96M (2025). FY25 adjusted EBITDA was $337M, down from $363M, with the mix shifting: Agribusiness EBITDA fell from $218M to $187M on weak merchandising in oversupplied, low-volatility markets, while Renewables rose from $189M to $203M. Net margin runs ~1.1% and operating margin ~0.7%, normal for a commodity intermediary, offset by asset turnover near 3x.

**Cash flow and the balance sheet require seasonal context.** FY25 operating cash flow was $177M (down from $331M on working-capital swings), and FCF was -$56M as capex hit $233M. 2026 capex is guided to $200-225M (roughly half growth, half maintenance). Cash fell from $562M (YE24) to $98M (YE25), almost entirely the $425M cash purchase of Marathon's remaining TAMH stake. Long-term debt remains modest at ~$625M (LT debt/EBITDA only 1.8x vs a <2.5x target).

The Q1 2026 balance sheet looks alarming on a single-quarter snapshot but is normal seasonality: short-term debt spiked to $716.5M (from $249M at YE25) and operating cash flow was -$394M for the quarter, the standard spring inventory/working-capital build, not distress. An investor reading only the most recent quarterly balance sheet could wrongly infer leverage stress.

**Earnings surprise track record** is mixed but recently strong: +56.9% (Q1'26), +28.5% (Q4'25), +79.0% (Q3'25), -54.2% (Q2'25). The single large miss came in the trough of the ag cycle. The recent beats coincide with the 45Z ramp and full ethanol ownership.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on a composition shift toward higher-margin, more stable renewables earnings, layered on top of a normalizing ag cycle.

The two structural drivers are visible in the numbers. First, full ethanol-plant ownership: the July 2025 acquisition of Marathon's 49.9% TAMH stake was an equity transaction (TAMH was already consolidated as a VIE), so it added no net assets but eliminated noncontrolling-interest leakage. The effect is concrete: Renewables Q4 2025 pretax income attributable to ANDE tripled to $54M from $17M, and balance-sheet NCI fell from $234M (Q1'25) to $41M (Q1'26). Second, the 45Z credit ramp, with the latest proposed regulations removing the indirect-land-use-change penalty and qualifying plants for a higher credit tier.

Estimate revisions confirm positive momentum: current-quarter EPS up +23.9% over 90 days (to $1.45), current-year up +14.4% (to $5.47), next-year up +6.2% (to $5.96). The 30-day revisions are smaller (+2.8% CQ, +1.6% CY), suggesting the bulk of the upward repricing occurred earlier in the quarter and is now decelerating rather than accelerating. The magnitude of the 90-day current-year revision (+14.4%) is meaningful but falls short of the >30% velocity that signals a strong standalone inflection buy, and the deceleration in the 30-day window tempers the read.

Growth capex provides incremental volume: +30M gallons at Clymers, IN (online 2027), Ulysses, KS feedstock storage, a Port of Houston grain elevator upgrade (Q2 2026) and soybean-meal export capacity (Q3 2026), Carlsbad NM mineral processing phase 2, and a Class VI carbon-sequestration well pursuit at Clymers to lower carbon intensity and boost 45Z credits. Skyland Grain (Nov 2024) added ~$468M revenue and ~$76M gross profit in FY25, guided to $25-35M EBITDA in 2026 as it matures.

Management's targets depend heavily on 45Z monetization, RVO finalization, and year-round E15. The consensus $5.47 CY26 figure sits above 2025 GAAP and above management's $4.30+ run-rate exit target, which is consistent with the credit ramp landing as guided.

## Valuation Assessment

At $69.21 the stock trades at ~12.7x the $5.47 consensus FY26 EPS and ~11.6x the $5.96 FY27 figure. On the 18.46x trailing multiple cited in the data (against $2.79 trailing GAAP EPS, which includes a trough Agribusiness year), the stock looks fully priced; on forward estimates that capture the earnings step-up, it looks moderately cheap.

Peer comparison is awkward because the Food Distributors classification mislabels the business. The listed peers (CHEF 49.0x, PFGC 49.6x, USFD 30.6x, SYY 22.1x; peer median ~49x) are food-service distributors with entirely different economics and no ag-commodity or renewable-fuels exposure. ANDE's ~12-13x forward multiple is far below all of them, but that gap reflects business-model heterogeneity, not a clean discount. A more relevant frame is ag-merchant and ethanol comparables, which typically trade at low-double-digit forward multiples given commodity cyclicality. On that basis ANDE is fairly valued to modestly cheap.

The asymmetry argument: if management delivers anything near a $4.30+ run-rate exit-2026, the stock at 12-14x that figure supports $55-60 on the conservative end and meaningfully more if the market credits the $7 exit-2028 path. Analyst targets of $75-90 (mean $80, median $75) sit entirely above the current price, with all three analysts at BUY. On names with rising estimates trading below the lowest target, consensus targets often lag; they would likely revise higher if 45Z monetization confirms.

The offsetting risk is that a large share of the forward EPS rests on the 45Z credit, which trades in an immature transfer market at discounts to face value and is exposed to GREET-model and eligibility changes. A credit haircut or wage/apprenticeship failure (which cuts the credit to one-fifth of value) would compress the very earnings the forward multiple discounts. This is a policy-conditioned forward multiple rather than a clean cyclical-trough-multiple story. We score valuation moderately positive — the forward math is supportive and below targets — but withhold a high score because the earnings denominator is contingent on a binary policy outcome rather than durable operating power.

## Competitive Landscape

ANDE competes against far larger and better-capitalized players — ADM, Bunge, Cargill, and cooperatives — in grain merchandising and against numerous ethanol producers in renewables. Its competitive edge is a flexible regional asset footprint, integration between corn origination and its own ethanol demand, and a willingness to be nimble where global majors are not. It is not a scale leader and explicitly positions itself that way.

Competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving. The Western expansion (Skyland, Nebraska, Idaho, Houston port) diversifies a previously Eastern-Grain-Belt-concentrated footprint, and the premium-ingredients build moves part of the business up the margin curve. The renewables integration and 45Z optimization (carbon sequestration to lower carbon intensity) are credible moat-deepening moves within the ethanol fleet. None of this materially changes ANDE's standing against the majors, and the Agribusiness segment remains a price-taker on basis, spreads, and trade flows it cannot control. Eastern Grain Belt concentration is a structural vulnerability: adverse weather there hits both segments via higher corn basis, which also squeezes ethanol margins, as it did in 2025 alongside elevated natural gas costs. The competitive position is sound and modestly improving but not a source of durable pricing power.

## Risk Assessment

**45Z policy dependence (the dominant risk).** A new dedicated risk factor in the FY25 10-K. Eligibility depends on lifecycle-emissions thresholds and prevailing-wage/apprenticeship standards; credits run only through 2029; the transfer market is immature and sells at discounts; and Treasury/IRS GREET assumptions can change. A material share of 2026-2028 projected earnings rests on a single, politically-sensitive federal program. This is both the upside catalyst and the principal tail risk, and it is the reason we cap conviction.

**Commodity-cycle and basis risk.** 2025 was a cycle low — oversupply, weak prices, muted volatility compressed merchandising margins. Earnings depend on basis appreciation, spreads, and trade flows; derivatives don't hedge basis perfectly.

**Geographic concentration and operational hazards.** Eastern Grain Belt weather risk; a grain-terminal explosion in Sunray, TX contributed to $14.8M of FY25 asset impairments (partly offset by $16.8M insurance recoveries); management flags several aging assets.

**Trade/tariff policy** directly affects export-dependent grain and sorghum flows, flagged as heightened.

**Litigation.** A putative commodities/antitrust class action (agreement in principle, $5.0M accrual at Q1'26) and an unquantified receivership-related claim (likely ELEMENT).

**Seasonal liquidity.** Heavy revolver reliance (reduced from $1,550M to $1,300M in the March 2026 amendment) and futures margin-call risk in volatile markets. Total liquidity ~$1.9B mitigates this.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 66.1% against 34.2% one-year realized vol — rich by ~32 points, in the 78th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (66.1% near-term vs 35.9% three-month), which typically signals a near-dated catalyst or elevated event risk. ATM IV jumped from 36.5% to 62.1% over the past month while the price fell 4%, and the put/call OI ratio rose from 0.72 to 1.18. The nearest-expiry implied move is ±6-8%. Skew is modestly call-heavy (-3.3%), so the elevated vol is not predominantly downside hedging; it reads as two-sided event positioning. Short interest is low at 3.3% of float (2.1 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic. The market expects a sharp move but is not leaning decisively bearish; the rich front-end vol argues against chasing here and favors waiting for vol to normalize before adding.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The business-model shift is real and partly proven. Full ethanol ownership tripled the Renewables earnings flowing to shareholders, and the 45Z ramp converts a thin-margin cyclical intermediary into a more stable, higher-margin cash generator. If 45Z monetizes near guidance, management's $4.30+ exit-2026 run-rate is achievable, and the $7 exit-2028 target — supported by growth capex (Clymers, Houston, Carlsbad) coming online and a normalizing ag cycle lifting Agribusiness off a trough — would justify materially higher prices. Estimates are rising, all three analysts rate BUY with targets to $90, and the stock trades below the lowest target at ~12-13x forward earnings. Bull case: $90-100, roughly +30-45%.

### Bear Case

The 45Z credit is haircut, delayed, or politically curtailed; the transfer market discounts deepen; or eligibility standards trip up plant qualification. The ag cycle stays soft, keeping Agribusiness merchandising margins depressed. The forward EPS that justifies today's multiple fails to materialize, and the stock de-rates toward trough-earnings power. Heavy former-CEO selling proves prescient about a peak. The seasonal balance-sheet leverage compounds in a stressed commodity environment. Bear case: $48-52, roughly -25% to -30%, reflecting a return to mid-cycle ag-merchant earnings power without the full policy uplift.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** 6-12 months, anchored to the next two earnings prints (Q2 2026 in early August, Q3 in early November) and to 45Z monetization data as the credits are claimed and transferred through 2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- 45Z credits monetizing at or above the guided run-rate, with realized transfer pricing close to face value, confirmed in Q2/Q3 results.
- Current-year EPS estimates extending higher (toward or above management's $4.30+ exit run-rate) with the stock still below ~14x that figure, ideally after near-term options vol normalizes (ATM IV back toward the 35% range).
- Agribusiness merchandising margins recovering off the 2025 trough, evidenced by segment EBITDA re-expanding toward 2024 levels.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material adverse change to 45Z — eligibility failure, a credit-tier downgrade in final regulations, or transfer-market pricing collapsing well below face value.
- A guidance cut to the $4.30 exit-2026 or $7 exit-2028 targets.
- Clustered C-suite insider selling beyond the former CEO's diversification, or any signal the seasonal short-term debt is becoming structural rather than working-capital-driven.

## Conclusion

The Andersons is executing a credible shift toward a higher-margin, renewables-and-tax-credit-driven earnings core, and Q1 2026 plus rising estimates confirm the shift is underway rather than hypothetical. The forward valuation (~12-13x FY26 EPS, below the lowest analyst target) is supportive, and the operational logic — full ethanol ownership eliminating NCI leakage, plus the 45Z ramp — is sound.

What holds us at HOLD rather than BUY is the unusual degree of policy leverage. A meaningful slice of the 2026-2028 earnings that the forward multiple discounts rests on a single federal credit program expiring in 2029, with an immature transfer market and live regulatory/eligibility risk. Layer on heavy former-CEO selling with no offsetting insider buying, a rich-and-backwardated near-term options structure pricing imminent stress, and a cyclically soft Agribusiness segment, and the risk/reward at $69 is balanced rather than asymmetric. The recent price weakness off the $82 peak is more about repricing the policy-conditioned earnings than fundamental deterioration, but it does not yet warrant re-upgrading. We would become more constructive on confirmed 45Z monetization and a vol-normalized entry. HOLD, growth-oriented, with the upgrade path tied to credit-monetization evidence in the next two prints.
