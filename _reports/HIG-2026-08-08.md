## Executive Summary

The Hartford enters the back half of 2026 as a structurally stronger company than it was three years ago and a fundamentally more expensive stock. Book value per diluted share ex-AOCI has compounded to $78.91 at June 30, 2026 from $73.62 at year-end 2025, trailing core ROE is 18.7%, financial leverage sits at 18% debt-to-capitalization with 24.9x interest coverage, and all three rating agencies upgraded the company during 2025. Against that, the operating trend has turned. Q2 2026 core earnings of $945M grew 1.4% year over year despite an 8.1% revenue increase and a 22% rise in net investment income. Core EPS of $3.42 grew 5.6%, meaning roughly three-quarters of per-share growth came from share retirement rather than earnings. The headline GAAP figure — $1,293M of net income, up 31%, EPS $4.68 — contains $251M (about $0.91 per share) of one-time, non-cash deferred tax asset recognition tied to classifying Hartford Funds as held for sale.

The mechanism behind flat core earnings is visible in the segment data. The Business Insurance underlying combined ratio has worsened in each of the last three annual periods (87.8 → 87.9 → 88.5) and again in Q2 2026 to 89.3, with Middle & Large Business printing a 101.9 combined ratio — an outright underwriting loss — versus 86.6 a year earlier. Employee Benefits group disability loss ratio deteriorated 6.3 points to 74.8%, core earnings fell 15%, and the FY2025 10-K already guided the long-term net income margin down to 6–7% from 2025's realized 7.8%. Renewal pricing decelerated in every commercial line during 2025, and management's own language is that 2026 will bring "an increasingly competitive market within Business Insurance." Personal Insurance's margin repair is genuine but was purchased with a 16% two-year decline in auto policies in force, and re-growth runs through independent agents where commission load is a permanent cost.

Consensus has responded: FY2027 EPS has been cut 4.9% over 90 days to $13.70, FY2026 down 2.6% to $12.75, and the current quarter down 5.1%. The stock has moved the other way, rising roughly 12% since mid-June to within 1.9% of its 52-week high of $146.07. At 1.82x book ex-AOCI, 2.04x GAAP book, and 10.5x FY2027 consensus, the price already embeds a durable ROE in the 16–17% range at a 10% cost of equity. That is the central question, and the underlying-margin data argues the other way. Adding an unquantified arbitration in which National Indemnity has suspended payments under the now-exhausted asbestos and environmental adverse-development cover, the asymmetry from $143.28 does not support new capital. HOLD.

## Company Overview

The Hartford is a U.S.-centric property & casualty and group benefits insurer founded in 1810. The registrant is now The Hartford Insurance Group, Inc., formerly The Hartford Financial Services Group; the name change coincides with the pending divestiture of the asset-management arm, which is consistent with a narrowing toward pure-play insurance.

FY2025 segment structure and results:

| Segment | FY2025 revenue proxy | FY2025 net income | Key metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business Insurance | $13,883M earned premium | $2,780M | Combined ratio 88.3 |
| Personal Insurance | $3,725M earned premium | $447M | Combined ratio 91.9 |
| Employee Benefits | $7,140M total revenue | $557M | Net income margin 7.8% |
| Hartford Funds (now discontinued ops) | $1,113M total revenue | $213M | $154.2B AUM |
| P&C Other Operations (run-off) | — | $(103)M | Legacy A&E |
| Corporate | — | $(79)M to common | Interest expense $199M |

Business Insurance is the franchise, and Small Business is its strongest component: 1,657k policies in force at end-2025 (from 1,492k in 2023) with a Q2 2026 combined ratio of 85.9, improved 3.8 points year over year. Personal Insurance operates under an exclusive AARP licensing agreement running through December 31, 2032, a real distribution moat that the 10-K also identifies as a dependency on AARP membership growth. Employee Benefits sells group disability, group life and supplemental products, which — combined with workers' compensation at 41.7% of net P&C reserves — leaves The Hartford unusually levered to employment, wage and medical-cost trends relative to a pure commercial P&C peer.

## Financial Analysis

**Consolidated trend.** FY2025 revenue reached $28,368M (+6.9%), pre-tax income $4,760M, net income $3,836M, core earnings $3,845M, diluted EPS $13.21 (+29%). Three-year compound growth was 8.2% for revenue and 28.3% for net income — the latter reflecting recovery from a depressed 2022 base rather than a sustainable run rate. Total equity rose to $18,979M from $16,447M.

**H1 2026.** Revenue $14,204M (+7.1%), income from continuing operations $1,784M (+17.2%), core earnings $1,757M, implying H1 core EPS of roughly $6.32 on approximately 278.1M weighted diluted shares.

**Where the quality gap sits.** Q2 2026 core earnings of $945M against $932M is the operative number. Business Insurance core earnings were essentially flat ($695M vs $697M) and Employee Benefits fell 15% ($139M vs $163M). Two offsetting adjustments deserve airing. Working against the bull: the Q2 GAAP result is flattered by $251M of non-cash deferred tax recognition, and trailing net-income ROE of 23.8% against core ROE of 18.7% captures the same distortion. Working against the bear: the year-ago quarter carried $146M of favorable prior-year development versus $52M this year, a $94M pre-tax swing (~$74M after tax) that on its own would lift adjusted core growth toward 9%. The honest reading is that reported core earnings were flat and a meaningful slice of the shortfall was reserve-release timing rather than a step-change in current-accident-year economics.

**Underwriting margin direction.** This is the most important operating signal in the file:

| Business Insurance | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 | FY2025 | FY2024 | FY2023 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Combined ratio | 91.4 | 87.0 | 88.3 | 89.9 | 89.6 |
| Underlying combined ratio | 89.3 | 88.0 | 88.5 | 87.9 | 87.8 |
| Underlying loss ratio | 58.3 | 57.0 | 57.0 | 56.5 | 56.5 |
| Favorable PYD | $52M | $146M | $441M | $231M | $225M |

The ex-cat, ex-prior-year measure has now deteriorated four consecutive periods. Underwriting gain fell 29% to $316M. Within the segment, Q2 2026 earned premium growth was fastest in commercial auto (+15.6%), general liability (+9.9%), package (+9.0%) and commercial property (+7.9%), and slowest in workers' compensation (+1.7%) and professional liability (+1.0%). The company is growing fastest in the two lines with the industry's worst social-inflation record and slowest in its most reserve-rich line. CFO Beth Costello attributed the loss-ratio move to mix shift toward national accounts and commercial auto rather than a change in loss-trend assumptions, which confirms the mechanism without resolving whether the pricing on that mix is adequate.

**Investment income composition.** Q2 2026 net investment income of $800M (+22%) looks strong until decomposed: $101M of the $142M year-over-year increase, or 71%, came from limited partnerships (LP income $114M vs $13M). Excluding LPs, NII rose 6% and the ex-LP yield rose 10bp to 4.7%. The fixed-income reinvestment spread narrowed to 60bp in FY2025 (5.6% reinvestment vs 5.0% on sales/maturities) from 90bp in FY2024, and H1 2026 commercial mortgage fundings came at 5.6% versus 6.2% in FY2025. LP income is recognized on a three-month lag and is mark-driven: within H1 2026 real estate joint ventures swung to +$42M from $(22)M while private equity fell to $20M from $39M, with Q2 private equity negative $15M. The durable, spread-driven portion of NII growth is decelerating.

**Credit and balance sheet.** 95% of the $46.0B AFS fixed-maturity portfolio is investment grade, no credit losses were recorded in FY2025, and office exposure is 5.1% ($373M) of the $7.3B mortgage book at 56% weighted-average LTV. Total debt of $4,374M against ~$24.0B of total capitalization gives 18% debt-to-capitalization and 22% debt-to-equity, improved from 21%/27% at end-2024. Interest expense has been exactly $199M for three years; derived FY2025 coverage is 24.9x. Statutory capital rose to $17,282M at June 30, 2026. Holding-company liquidity is ample: ~$1.5B of investments, a $750M undrawn revolver to September 2030, a $2.0B intercompany facility with $1.85B available, and expanded FHLB pledge capacity. The U.S. qualified pension plan is fully funded with no required 2026 contribution.

A screening model returning an Altman Z-Score near 1.0 for this company should be discarded on definitional grounds: $47.1B of the ~$68.4B of liabilities are policyholder loss and benefit reserves — the product, pre-funded by premium — and insurers do not present classified balance sheets. The three 2025 rating upgrades (A.M. Best senior debt to "a", S&P core FSR to "AA-", Moody's senior unsecured to "A3") speak directly to claims-paying and debt-service capacity.

**Two cash-flow cautions.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $2,234M versus $2,276M a year earlier, down 1.8% while core earnings rose roughly 15%, with prior-year paid losses rising 8.8% to $3,486M. Paid losses accelerating faster than premium is what one expects as social-inflation-affected casualty accident years mature into settlements. One half-year is noisy — reinsurance settlements, cash-tax timing and premium seasonality all distort it — so this is a metric to track across the next two quarters rather than a conclusion. Separately, the Employee Benefits statutory entity paid $439M of dividends against $285M of statutory income in H1 2026, a 154% payout that reduced its statutory capital to $2,502M from $2,674M. That may be deliberate release of excess capital or annual dividend timing; RBC ratios are disclosed only as "in excess of minimums," so it cannot be assessed against a target.

It is also worth correcting a framing that could be over-applied: the NICO adverse-development cover limit was fully ceded as of December 31, 2024, so the FY2025 A&E charge of $165M was already fully retained. FY2025's $4,760M of pre-tax income therefore already absorbs an uncovered A&E review. The ongoing headwind is real (roughly $150–200M annually, recurring in Q4 when the review is performed, or 3–4% of pre-tax income) but it is embedded in the current base rather than a fresh step-down.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth remains respectable at 7–8%, driven by earned premium and invested-asset growth. Earnings growth is not. Core earnings grew 1.4% in Q2 2026; core EPS growth of 5.6% is predominantly share-count arithmetic. Consensus reflects this: FY2026 EPS of $12.75 sits below FY2025 core EPS of $13.32, and FY2027 of $13.70 implies only 7.5% growth off a reduced base.

The research brief's central FY2026 core EPS range of $14.6–15.7 does not reconcile with H1 actuals or with consensus, and I do not carry it. H1 2026 core EPS was $6.32; reaching $14.60 would require H2 core EPS of $8.28, or +31% sequentially, in a half that includes hurricane season. The FY2025 analogue (H2 at ~60% of the year) was distorted by $305M of net Q1 2025 California wildfire losses depressing H1. Adjusting for the company's persistent tendency to beat H2 consensus (Q3 2025 +16.4%, Q4 2025 +25.0%), a reasonable FY2026 core EPS range is **$13.0–13.6** — above the $12.75 consensus but well short of $14.60.

Segment-level growth drivers are mixed in a way that argues for margin dilution rather than accretion:

- **Small Business** is the durable growth engine: policies in force up 11% over two years with a combined ratio improving to 85.9.
- **Middle & Large Business** is growing into an underwriting loss (101.9 combined, 95.3 underlying, deteriorating 6.2 points).
- **Personal Insurance** is contracting: Q2 written premium −7%, earned −3%, auto policies in force down from 1,257k (2023) to 1,054k (2025). Auto renewal pricing has decelerated from +22.1% (FY2024) to +12.8% (FY2025) to +5.5% (Q2 2026), so rate can no longer offset volume.
- **Employee Benefits** sales jumped 31% in Q2 after two years of decline ($839M → $718M → $652M, 2023–2025) at exactly the moment disability margins deteriorated. Two readings fit: either pricing was relaxed to reaccelerate volume, or competitors retrenched under industry-wide incidence pressure and created share opportunity. The disclosures do not distinguish them.

Estimate revisions are the cleanest forward read and they are negative across every horizon: current quarter −5.1%/90d, next quarter −5.5%/90d, current year −2.6%, next year −4.9% (with −2.2% in the last 30 days alone). Negative revision momentum in a stock trading near its 52-week high is the specific combination that argues against adding.

Note a data inconsistency worth flagging: the market-data feed reports a P/E near 9.95 alongside an EPS of $13.32, which are arithmetically incompatible ($143.28 ÷ $13.32 = 10.8x). The 9.95 figure implies a trailing EPS near $14.40, consistent with a derived TTM core EPS around $14.48. I anchor on forward consensus and on book value rather than on a contested trailing figure.

Peer medians are P/E 9.6 and yield 1.9% across a heterogeneous set (ALL 4.8x, ACGL 7.0x, CINF 9.6x, WRB 14.3x, FNF 16.9x). HIG at 10.5–11.2x forward carries a modest premium to the median, defensible on ROE (18.7% core is at the high end of diversified P&C) and on balance-sheet quality, but not obviously mispriced.

The book-value framework is more informative. At 1.82x book ex-AOCI against an 18.7% core ROE, the implied cost of equity assuming zero growth and no ROE fade is 18.7 ÷ 1.82 = **10.3%**. Running the standard relationship P/B = (ROE − g) ÷ (CoE − g) at a 10% cost of equity and 3% growth:

- ROE 14.3% → 1.61x → ~$127
- ROE 16.4% → 1.75x → ~$150 (on a projected mid-2027 ex-AOCI book of ~$86)
- ROE 17.9% → 2.13x → ~$168

Projected book: FY2027 core EPS of ~$13.70 less a $2.40 dividend retains $11.30 per share, offset by roughly $2.85 of per-share book destruction from repurchasing 4.4% of shares at 1.82x book, for net BVPS ex-AOCI growth of ~$8.45 to approximately $86–87 by mid-2027. The AOCI drag of $(2,371)M ($8.63 per diluted share) reverses mechanically as bonds amortize to par, providing a slow GAAP book tailwind absent further rate increases.

So the current price embeds a sustained core ROE of roughly 16–17% at a 10% cost of equity. Every underlying operating series — Business Insurance underlying combined ratio, Employee Benefits margin guidance, renewal pricing deceleration, NII quality — points toward ROE fade rather than durability. The valuation is therefore approximately fair rather than cheap, and a low headline P/E in a softening underwriting cycle is not by itself a reason to own.

Consensus targets ($135 low, $149 median, $149.85 mean, $164 high) bracket my base case of $150. At an 11.2x forward multiple this is not a stock where analyst targets should be treated as stale floors: the growth is decelerating and estimates are being cut, which is the opposite of the inflection setup where consensus lags.

**Base case $150 (+4.7%), bull $168 (+17.3%), bear $127 (−11.4%).** Probability-weighting 55/20/25 gives roughly +3.2% price return plus a 1.67% dividend, or a mid-single-digit total return over twelve months — approximately fair, with the downside tail wider than the upside.

## Competitive Landscape

The commercial P&C cycle is past peak on rate, and The Hartford's own disclosures confirm it. FY2025 renewal written pricing decelerated in every line: small business +5.5% (from +6.5%), middle market +6.2% (from +6.8%), global specialty +4.7% (from +6.0%), with workers' comp flat in small business and only "slightly positive" in middle market. The 10-K states directly that the company expects "an increasingly competitive market within Business Insurance during 2026." This is consistent with the broader reinsurance and specialty picture, where the hard market has been rolling over for several quarters.

Competitive position is genuinely bifurcated. Small commercial is a durable, widening franchise — growing policy count with an improving 85.9 combined ratio, supported by underwriting automation. Middle & Large Business is where the position is deteriorating, with a 101.9 combined ratio driven by national-account and commercial-auto mix. Personal Insurance holds a real structural asset in AARP exclusivity through 2032, a channel most competitors cannot access, but the policy attrition occurred in that direct channel and the growth strategy now runs through independent agents via Prevail, where The Hartford competes on the same terms as everyone else and pays commission. The segment expense ratio has already risen to 26.3 from 25.1 on that mix shift; the counterargument that this is largely a denominator effect reversing as premium re-grows is reasonable but unproven.

Two industry dynamics cut specifically against The Hartford's mix. Workers' compensation — its most profitable historical line and the source of the favorable development funding casualty additions — is in a prolonged flat-to-soft pricing environment while medical and wage inflation continue. And the lines growing fastest in its book (commercial auto, general liability) are precisely where litigation funding and nuclear verdicts have proven most damaging industry-wide.

## Risk Assessment

**1. NICO arbitration and exhausted A&E cover.** The Q2 2026 10-Q discloses that National Indemnity Company, a Berkshire Hathaway subsidiary, "suspended any further payment under the A&E ADC due to a dispute that is the subject of an arbitration proceeding," with timing and outcome unknown and possible impacts to cash flows or operating results. Deferred gain amortization was $36M in Q1 2026 and $0 in Q2 2026; the balance fell to $814M from $850M. FY2025 disclosed $1,436M of ceded unpaid A&E recoverables plus $64M paid under this cover. No news or trade coverage of the arbitration was locatable. The gross recoverable less the offsetting deferred gain suggests a speculative ceiling around $600M pre-tax, but the net accounting exposure cannot be computed from the disclosures and I do not present a base case. Collectability is not the issue — this is a contract dispute with a Berkshire subsidiary. Benign resolutions are plausible: the dispute could concern claims-handling protocols, allocation methodology, or the transition of administrative responsibility contemplated around year-end 2025. The material point is that a binary, unquantified, publicly uncovered item sits against 6% of common equity in a stock trading near its high.

**2. Long-tail casualty reserve adequacy.** Net P&C reserves were $32,458M at June 30, 2026, with workers' comp at $13,116M (41.7%) and general liability at $6,381M (20.3%). The company's own sensitivities: an 8% change in GL reported development patterns is ±$800M; a 2.5% change in WC paid patterns is ±$500M. Together $1.3B, or 6.0% of common equity ex-AOCI, $4.73 per diluted share. Actual development has been directionally adverse in GL: +$211M in FY2024, zero in FY2025, +$116M in H1 2026 (Q1 for 1970s–80s sexual molestation exposures including a religious-institution bankruptcy settlement in principle; Q2 for large-loss frequency in excess casualty and umbrella), plus $26M adverse in commercial auto. Precedent exists: $787M paid in April 2023 to settle Boy Scouts claims. The pattern of transferring workers' comp redundancy into deteriorating casualty lines is standard industry practice and is legitimate, but it is finite, and it is occurring while GL and commercial auto premium grow at 9.9% and 15.6%.

**3. TRIPRA expiration December 31, 2027.** Workers' compensation policies generally contain no terrorism exclusions and private NBCR reinsurance is largely unavailable. With $13.1B of WC reserves, The Hartford is among the more exposed large carriers. This is a dated legislative catalyst roughly 17 months out.

**4. Employee Benefits margin reset.** Management's own long-term net income margin guidance of 6–7% sits below FY2025's realized 7.8%, and Q2's 7.4% core margin (from 9.2%) already reflects transition. A 10bp lower LTD discount rate adds $28M of reserves; a 1% decrease in claim termination rates adds $29M.

**5. Catastrophe program gap.** FY2025 CAY catastrophe losses were $748M, stable versus $768M and $676M in the prior two years. Two specifics: the homeowners catastrophe ratio rose to 24.8% from 20.9%, and global assumed reinsurance catastrophe losses are not covered by the aggregate property catastrophe treaty — a defined gap.

**6. Unverified disclosures.** An 8-K dated July 15, 2026 carrying Item 5.02 (departure/election of directors or officers) could not be retrieved. A board or committee change is the most likely benign explanation, but a senior executive transition cannot be excluded. Separately, RBC ratios are undisclosed.

**7. Macro sensitivity.** With workers' comp and group disability as two of its largest lines, The Hartford is more exposed than peers to employment deterioration. Falling employment reduces workers' comp exposure base and premium; rising disability incidence in a weakening labor market is a recognized pattern. ## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$168, +17%).** Small Business continues compounding at an 86 combined ratio and mid-single-digit rate; the Middle & Large deterioration proves to be a discrete mix effect that reprices over two renewal cycles rather than a loss-trend problem, exactly as management describes; Personal Insurance's expense ratio normalizes as earned premium re-grows through improved retention, validating the 86 underlying combined ratio; the Employee Benefits disability incidence step-up proves to be the planned 6–7% margin reset rather than degradation; NICO resolves with full payment after a timing delay; and the $4.2B authorization is executed near the ~$475M quarterly pace, retiring roughly 4.5% of shares annually. Core ROE holds near 18%, book ex-AOCI compounds to ~$87, and the stock re-rates toward 1.95x that book. Add 1.67% of dividend for a total return near 19%.

**Bear case (~$127, −11%).** The soft commercial market compresses pricing below loss trend for two more years while the mix shift into commercial auto and general liability matures into adverse development; workers' comp redundancy is exhausted as a funding source; the Employee Benefits margin settles at the low end of 6% rather than 7%; NII growth stalls as the reinvestment spread compresses further and LP marks reverse; the NICO arbitration resolves at a discount requiring a recoverable write-down with differential GAAP and statutory effects (GAAP net P&C reserves are ~$1.4B lower than statutory precisely because statutory records the ADC recoverable as a liability reduction); and core ROE fades toward 14%. The multiple compresses to 1.5x a slower-growing book. Buybacks executed at 1.8–2.0x book in that scenario destroy per-share value.

**Base case (~$150, +4.7%).** Core ROE fades to roughly 16.5% over 24 months as competition compresses commercial margins and Employee Benefits normalizes, partly offset by Small Business compounding, buyback accretion of 4.4% annually, and mechanical AOCI reversal. Book ex-AOCI reaches ~$86 by mid-2027; the multiple holds near 1.75x. Total return of roughly 6% including the dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Existing owners should maintain exposure; the balance sheet, the ~6.1% total shareholder yield if the authorization is executed, and a defensible 1.75x fair multiple on compounding book value support the position. New capital has no urgent entry signal 1.9% below the 52-week high with estimates falling.

- **Base case target: $150** — 1.75x projected mid-2027 book ex-AOCI of ~$86, equivalently 10.9x FY2027 consensus of $13.70. Upside +4.7% plus 1.67% dividend.
- **Bull case target: $168** — 1.95x book ex-AOCI on a sustained ~18% core ROE. Upside +17.3%.
- **Bear case target: $127** — 1.50x a slower-growing book on a ~14% core ROE. Downside −11.4%.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored on the Q3 2026 print (late October), the Q4 annual A&E reserve review (February 2027), and the Hartford Funds closing expected in Q1 2027.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Business Insurance underlying combined ratio improves sequentially for two consecutive quarters, reversing the four-period deterioration from 87.8 to 89.3, with Middle & Large Business returning below a 95 combined ratio.
2. A price near **$127–132** (roughly 1.5–1.55x book ex-AOCI) without fundamental deterioration, which restores a 15%+ base-case upside and a margin of safety consistent with a fading-ROE assumption.
3. NICO arbitration resolves with substantially full recovery and the deferred-gain amortization schedule resumes, removing the unquantified item.
4. Employee Benefits core earnings margin recovers above 8% for two consecutive quarters while the group disability loss ratio returns below 70%.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. General liability or commercial auto adverse development exceeding $150M in any single quarter, or cumulative FY2026 GL adverse development above $250M.
2. An adverse NICO outcome requiring a write-down of more than ~$300M of the A&E recoverable, or any disclosed statutory capital impact.
3. Operating cash flow declining year over year for a second consecutive half while paid losses grow faster than earned premium, which would indicate the reserve position is converting to cash faster than modeled.
4. FY2027 consensus EPS cut a further 5% or more, taking it below $13.00, or core ROE reported below 16%.
5. Renewal written pricing in middle market or global specialty falling below 4%, confirming pricing below loss trend.

**Next reassessment:** the Q3 2026 earnings release in late October 2026, with the Q4 A&E review in February 2027 as the higher-consequence checkpoint.

## Conclusion

The Hartford's score trajectory has moved 7.2 → 7.2 → 7.2 → 6.1, and this cycle continues that direction to 5.3. The reason is not the stock price — which is up roughly 12% since the June assessment — but a divergence between price and fundamentals. Consensus FY2027 EPS is down 4.9% over 90 days, Q2 core earnings grew 1.4%, the Business Insurance underlying combined ratio deteriorated for a fourth consecutive period, Employee Benefits core earnings fell 15% against management's own guidance for a lower long-term margin, and 71% of the year-over-year net investment income increase came from lag-recognized limited partnership marks. Management has told the market plainly to expect an increasingly competitive Business Insurance market in 2026.

What holds the rating at HOLD rather than REDUCE is the balance sheet and the capital return. Debt-to-capitalization of 18%, 24.9x interest coverage, three rating-agency upgrades in 2025, rising statutory capital, a fully funded pension, and roughly $2.95B of expected 2026 subsidiary dividend capacity against ~$870M of fixed holding-company needs give this company the capacity to compound book value through a soft market and repurchase 4.4% of shares annually. Small Business at an 85.9 combined ratio with growing policy count is a genuinely widening franchise. Book value ex-AOCI has compounded 7% in six months, and the $8.63-per-share AOCI drag reverses mechanically over time.

At 1.82x book ex-AOCI and 10.5x FY2027 consensus, the price requires a sustained 16–17% core ROE at a 10% cost of equity. Every underlying operating series points to fade rather than durability, and the NICO arbitration adds a binary, unquantified item that no published research appears to be pricing. Base case upside of 4.7% against bear case downside of 11.4% is unfavorable asymmetry. Own it if you own it; wait for $127–132, or for two quarters of underlying combined ratio improvement, before committing new capital.
