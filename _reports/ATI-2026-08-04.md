## Executive Summary

ATI is no longer usefully described as a diversified specialty metals producer. Aerospace and defense accounted for 67.8% of FY2025 sales ($3,111.5M of $4,587.4M) and 69% of Q1 FY2026 sales; commercial jet engines alone were 38.4% of FY2025 revenue and 41.0% of Q1. The High Performance Materials & Components segment is now roughly 93% A&D and 70% commercial jet engines. Over two years, A&D revenue grew 25.7% while all other end markets contracted 14.5%. The company is deliberately reallocating melt, forging and finishing capacity toward aero alloys, and the financial signature of that shift is unusual: FY2025 revenue rose 5.2% while gross margin expanded 136bp to 21.95%, and Q1 FY2026 revenue rose 0.6% while gross margin expanded 220bp to 22.8% and diluted EPS rose 26.9% to $0.85.

The balance sheet supports the strategy. Net debt/Adjusted EBITDA was 1.60x at March 29, 2026, interest coverage 6.5x on operating income, the current ratio 2.66x, and the June 2026 issuance of $450M of 5.875% notes due 2033 refinanced the 5.875% 2027 maturity at an identical coupon while extending tenor six years and extinguishing the 2027 leg of an ABL minimum-liquidity test. Management's capital allocation record is the strongest single piece of evidence in ATI's favor: $890.2M of repurchases from FY2023 through Q1 FY2026 retired 14.2M shares at a derived average of roughly $62.69, against today's $197.58, while the 18.8M-share convertible overhang was extinguished.

The constraint is price. At $197.58 the stock is 3.8% below its 52-week high of $205.31 and up 155% over twelve months, and it trades at 65x TTM GAAP EPS of $3.03, 44.3x the FY2026 consensus of $4.46, 36.5x the FY2027 consensus of $5.42, 27.4x guided FY2026 EV/Adjusted EBITDA, and a 1.24% FY2025 free-cash-flow yield. Consensus mean and median targets are $200.33 and $210 — 1.4% and 6.3% above spot. Estimate revisions are positive but modest at +5.5% over 90 days, well short of the velocity that historically precedes further re-rating. Meanwhile Q1 working capital moved against the thesis (inventory +$177.1M, managed working capital 34.8% of annualized sales from 32.5%, gross inventory turns -13%), FY2025 cash flow was materially assisted by receivable monetization that is nearly exhausted, and a Q2 print is imminent with near-term implied volatility at 74.1% versus 39.7% realized. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD; the reason it remains a HOLD rather than an upgrade is that since the prior review at $170.05 the stock has advanced 16% with no new quarterly data, compressing the risk/reward rather than confirming it.

## Company Overview

ATI operates two reportable segments. **HPMC** produces nickel- and cobalt-based superalloys, titanium and titanium alloys, and precision forgings, castings and machined components; FY2025 external sales were $2,441.7M (53% of total) with segment EBITDA margin of 23.6%, up from 20.2% in FY2024. **AA&S** produces flat-rolled and long specialty products — nickel alloys, stainless, titanium mill products, zirconium/hafnium/niobium, and precision rolled strip — with FY2025 external sales of $2,145.7M and segment EBITDA margin of 16.3%, up from 15.4%.

FY2025 product mix: nickel-based and specialty alloys 46%, precision forgings/castings/components 22%, titanium and titanium alloys 18%, zirconium and related alloys 9%, precision rolled strip 5%. End-market mix: commercial jet engines $1,762.9M, commercial airframes $790.9M, defense $557.7M, with $1,218.6M across conventional energy ($328.4M), specialty energy ($257.3M), automotive ($244.6M), electronics ($184.8M), medical ($139.4M), construction/mining ($145.4M) and other. International sales were 42.5% of revenue, of which $1,562.5M was exported from U.S. operations; China was the largest non-U.S. destination at $324.3M.

Two assets are easy to miss in a screen. ATI is one of a small group of integrated Western producers of nuclear-grade zirconium and hafnium (roughly $413M of sales), a franchise with no aerospace correlation. And ATI consolidates STAL, a 60%-owned precision rolled strip joint venture in China with state-owned China Baowu Steel Group holding 40% — Asian electronics and automotive access, and an embedded state-owned counterparty ($14.3M of non-controlling interest income in FY2025).

The moat is qualification-based rather than patent-based. Funded R&D was $21.3M in FY2025, 0.46% of sales; the barrier is metallurgical process know-how, capital equipment (VAR/ESR melting, isothermal presses) and multi-year customer and airframer qualification. Approximately 7,600 employees, roughly 35% under collective bargaining, predominantly USW.

Two-year revenue CAGR is 4.8%. Two-year Adjusted EBITDA CAGR is 16.4%. Essentially all of the earnings growth is mix and price, and the mix shift is visible in AA&S: Q1 FY2026 segment revenue fell 4% while segment EBITDA margin expanded 320bp to 18.1%. HPMC grew 5% with margin at 24.9% from 22.4%.

**Cash flow quality carries three caveats that a headline FCF margin of 7.3% obscures.** First, receivables monetization roughly doubled: FY2025 total of approximately $549M ($80.0M under a new $125M PNC securitization established September 19, 2025; $94.2M under a separate purchase agreement; $375M under customer-established programs) versus approximately $314M in FY2024. The disclosed direct cost was $5.3M plus $1.8M. Second, supplier-finance payables rose from $34.8M (Dec 2024) to $52.8M (Dec 2025) to $101.0M (Mar 2026). Third, cash income taxes paid were $76.7M in FY2025 versus $15.0M in FY2024, equal to 14.7% of pretax income against a 19.9% book rate; as the FY2023 valuation-allowance release is consumed, cash taxes converge on book, a roughly $27M annual headwind at FY2025 pretax levels.

The important qualification is that the operating improvement is independent of these programs: 200bp of Adjusted EBITDA margin expansion in FY2025 and 310bp in Q1 FY2026 are pricing and mix, not financing. What the programs affect is the *pace* of reported free cash flow, and their incremental contribution is largely spent — the securitization was drawn $120M of a $125M maximum at March 29, 2026, and one of the two purchase agreements was terminated.

Q1 FY2026 CFO swung to +$128.2M from −$92.5M, but customer-program receivable sales in the quarter were $131.7M versus $72.6M, the securitization absorbed another $40.0M, and supplier-finance payables rose about $48M. Inventory rose $177.1M to $1,580.3M with reserves at $100.0M from $80.4M. Q1 FCF of $73.0M should not be annualized.

**Capital intensity is understated by the capex line.** FY2025 cash capex of $280.6M plus $37.7M of unpaid capex is roughly $318M on an accrual basis (6.9% of sales), with $247.4M of PP&E commitments outstanding and a further $72.3M of right-of-use assets and liabilities to be recognized on completion of finance-lease-financed equipment ($38.0M of lender progress payments made, $34.3M scheduled).

**Leverage and coverage.** At December 28, 2025: total debt $1,749.4M, cash and short-term investments $416.7M, total equity (incl. NCI) $1,916.7M. Debt/equity 0.91x, net debt/equity 0.70x, total liabilities/equity 1.66x. Company-disclosed total debt/Adjusted EBITDA 2.05x and net debt/Adjusted EBITDA 1.60x at March 29, 2026 on TTM Adjusted EBITDA of $896.4M. Adjusted EBITDA/net interest 8.7x. ROE 22.1% on average ATI equity; ROA 8.1%.

Book equity of $1,804.5M produces a derived P/B near 15x, which is an artifact rather than a solvency signal: the 2024 convertible conversion charged $233.9M directly to retained earnings, and treasury stock is $502.7M. The same distortion depresses the retained-earnings input to any Altman calculation. That said, low book equity does mean less loss-absorbing capacity in a genuine downturn, and an Altman Z near 6.9 is 5.1 points driven by the market-value term — cut the share price in half and it falls toward 4.4.

**Debt stack and the June refinancing.** Pre-refinancing: 7.25% notes due 2030 ($425M), 5.875% due 2027 ($350M), 5.125% due 2031 ($350M), 4.875% due 2029 ($325M), $200M ABL term loan at SOFR+200, $111M finance leases and other. The $150M Allegheny Ludlum 6.95% debentures were repaid at maturity December 15, 2025. On June 3, 2026 ATI priced $450M of 5.875% notes due 2033 (closing 8-K filed June 8 under Items 1.01/2.03/8.01), with approximately $350M reported as earmarked for the 2027 notes. The derived effect: gross debt up roughly $100M with a corresponding cash increase, net leverage approximately unchanged, incremental interest of roughly $6M annually, the maturity wall pushed from Q4 FY2027 to 2029, and the 2027 leg of the June 2025-amended ABL's minimum-liquidity test eliminated. Refinancing an aerospace-cyclical credit at an unchanged coupon while extending six years is a credit-market endorsement. The ABL provides a $600M revolver and $200M term loan to June 2030, plus a $100M delayed-draw option; the revolver was drawn $75.0M at March 29, 2026 with roughly $495M of availability remaining and a springing 1.00:1.00 fixed-charge covenant.

**Financial health: 6.7.** Modest leverage for a cyclical, clean refinancing, high coverage, 8-of-9 Piotroski profile. Offset by a 1.24% FCF yield, receivable/payable tailwinds that cannot repeat, a five-point cash-tax convergence, working capital moving the wrong way, and thin loss-absorbing book equity. This is marginally better than the prior review on the refinancing, and I am holding the score effectively flat rather than raising it because the Q1 working-capital deterioration is unresolved.

## Growth Analysis

The demand engine is narrow and strong. Commercial jet engine revenue rose 32.2% over two years to $1,762.9M and 12% year over year in Q1 FY2026 to $472.0M. Defense grew from $401.9M to $557.7M (+38.8%) over two years and 9.3% in Q1 to $139.0M. Backlog of confirmed orders reached a record $4.1B at March 29, 2026 versus $4.0B a year earlier and $3.7B at December 28, 2025.

Three qualifications matter. First, the backlog definition is narrow — confirmed orders excluding variable consideration, roughly 70% expected to convert within twelve months. It is not lifetime long-term-agreement value or remaining performance obligations. Second, the December 2025 sequence showed a decline to $3.7B from $3.9B, partly mechanical (two FY2025 divestitures removed roughly $76M of combined 2024 sales), and the Q1 recovery reads as order timing rather than a step-change. Third, commercial airframes fell 9.3% in Q1 to $186.6M, diverging from engines.

Non-aerospace markets are contracting: −14.5% over two years, with Q1 declines of 31% in conventional energy, 35% in medical and 29% in electronics. The medical trajectory ($176.9M → $224.9M → $139.4M) is a spike-and-collapse that the filings do not attribute to a named program. Whether this is deliberate capacity reallocation (management's framing of "higher quality revenue") or share loss is the single most important unresolved question in the business, because it determines whether idle capacity represents optionality or stranded assets.

FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $1.01B–$1.06B implies 17.5%–23.4% growth on FY2025's $859.3M. Q1 delivered $231.7M, leaving $778M–$828M for the final three quarters, an average of $259M–$276M per quarter — a 12%–19% step-up from Q1. Applying FY2025's seasonal shape (Q1 was 22.6% of the full year) to Q1 FY2026 implies roughly $1,025M, squarely inside guidance. The guidance therefore does not require an unusual ramp, which is reassuring, but it does require the inventory build to convert.

Consensus has moved up steadily: current year $4.46 (from $4.22 ninety days ago, +5.6%), next year $5.42 (from $5.14, +5.5%), with all four reported quarters beating (surprises of 2.4%, 14.1%, 4.4%, 12.4%). The revision trend is positive and consistent, but at +5.5% over 90 days it is an order of magnitude below the velocity that signals systematic underestimation of an inflection. Growth here is a credible mid-teens-to-low-20s earnings compounder, not an inflection.

**Growth prospects: 6.2.** Durable long-cycle A&D demand with record backlog and 20%-plus EBITDA growth guided, held back by low-single-digit revenue growth, a shrinking non-aero base, and a margin-led model whose runway is finite — gross margin has already expanded 272bp in two years.

## Competitive Landscape

The Western supply base for high-temperature and high-strength aerospace metals is structurally consolidated: Carpenter Technology, Howmet, Precision Castparts (Berkshire), Acerinox/North American Stainless (which absorbed Haynes in 2024), and European producers including Aubert & Duval. Russia's VSMPO-AVISMA has been effectively removed from Western aerospace qualification since 2022; ATI terminated its Uniti titanium joint venture with VSMPO following sanctions. Because requalification of a titanium source takes years, that removal is a durable tailwind rather than a cyclical one.

The filings support a capacity-constrained, price-improving market rather than a volume boom. That is the cleanest explanation for +5.2% revenue with +136bp gross margin in FY2025 and +0.6% revenue with +220bp in Q1 FY2026. Item 1A is explicit that pricing power runs both ways: historical periods of excess capacity produced reduced prices, and "benefits of any price increases" are delayed by long lead times and existing contract terms. The same lag that currently helps will hurt on the way down.

Trajectory assessment is mixed. Within aerospace, ATI is clearly gaining — HPMC segment margin from 20.2% to 23.6%, engine revenue +32.2% over two years, defense +38.8%. Outside aerospace, the position is deteriorating or being abandoned, and the filings do not distinguish between the two. The zirconium/hafnium nuclear franchise remains a genuinely differentiated, non-correlated asset. The Q1 charge of $7.0M to rationalize certain domestic HPMC facilities affecting roughly 100 employees indicates continued footprint pruning.

One nuance deserves emphasis: the 10-K's statement that no single customer exceeded 10% of sales in any year presented establishes only that no single legal entity crosses that threshold. It measures nothing about program or platform concentration, which is structurally high when 41% of quarterly revenue is commercial jet engines dominated by two narrowbody engine families.

**Competitive position: 7.2** (from 7.8). The aerospace franchise is strengthening and the Russian-titanium displacement is durable. I am trimming because the non-aero contraction remains unexplained after two years, and commercial airframes turning −9.3% while engines run +12% raises the possibility that ATI's positional strength is narrower than the consolidated margin trend implies.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Program concentration.** 69% of Q1 revenue A&D; 41% commercial jet engines; HPMC roughly 93%/70%. Long-term contracts are, per Item 1A, "subject to renewal, renegotiation, or re-pricing at periodic intervals." An engine build-rate pause converts a margin story into an absorption problem quickly, because the fixed-cost base has been expanded to serve it.

**2. Chinese export controls on ATI's own inputs.** Item 1A names nickel, zirconium, niobium, chromium, hafnium, cobalt, vanadium and titanium sponge, and states ATI sources some from China, which "has imposed, and may in the future continue to impose, export controls." This is two-sided: China was also ATI's largest non-U.S. destination at $324.3M (7.1% of sales), and its JV partner is state-owned Baowu. This is a live rather than theoretical exposure and, in my view, among the most underpriced risks in the name.

**3. Raw material lag.** Approximately 70M lbs of nickel annually; $1.00/lb ≈ $70M. Natural gas 6–8M MMBtu/year, with roughly 60% of remaining 2026 domestic gas and 25% of 2027 hedged. A new Q1 10-Q risk factor cites the February 2026 U.S./Israel/Iran strikes for potential energy-price, supply-chain and geopolitical disruption.

**4. Trade policy.** 42.5% of sales international, $1,562.5M exported from U.S. operations. Item 1A explicitly contemplates foreign governments denying access to critical raw materials in retaliation for U.S. trade actions.

**5. Working capital and inventory quality.** Managed working capital at 34.8% of annualized sales from 32.5%; inventory +$177.1M with reserves +$19.6M and turns −13%. The benign reading is a build ahead of second-half volume consistent with guidance. The unfavorable reading is material that is not moving. One quarter is not a trend, and Q1 is seasonally the weakest, but this is the metric to watch in the Q2 print.

**6. Adjusted-EBITDA add-backs trending up.** "Restructuring and other charges" ran $31.4M / $22.1M / $48.8M in FY2023–25 and $26.4M in Q1 FY2026; TTM $69.6M, or 7.8% of TTM Adjusted EBITDA. Two components are questionable as one-time: start-up costs recur while ATI commissions capacity as a stated strategy, and losses on receivable sales ($7.8M FY2025, $2.4M Q1) are a financing cost of the liquidity programs sitting inside a line added back to EBITDA. The amounts are individually disclosed — transparency, not obfuscation — but true earnings power is somewhat below the adjusted figure.

**7. Pension and litigation.** Immediate mark-to-market recognition produces recurring Q4 charges ($26.8M / $14.1M / $18.6M). PBO $333.0M against $286.9M of assets, plus $182.2M unfunded OPEB. Two consolidated class actions in W.D. Pa. challenge the October 2023 Athene annuitization; a magistrate judge recommended dismissal of all claims for lack of standing following an August 2025 hearing, pending review — a favorable development but not final.

**8. Environmental.** PRP at 40 sites; the 10-K states the "potential loss exposure on the remaining eight sites could be material," against approximately $15M of reserves and reasonably possible excess of $16–19M. Cost-sharing arrangements are under non-disclosure agreements, limiting visibility.

**9. Multiemployer plans.** Boilermakers-Blacksmiths and IAM National funds in the "red zone" with a surcharge on the IAM fund. FY2025 contributions were only $6.7M, but red-zone status is where withdrawal liability concentrates.

Mitigants worth noting: no disclosed material weakness, no going-concern language, no restatement, ICFR effective, E&Y as auditor since 1996, and a six-year USW agreement through February 2031 covering roughly 1,100 AA&S employees.

## Management Quality

The capital allocation record is genuinely strong. Repurchases of $85.2M (FY2023, ~$42.60 average), $260.0M (FY2024, ~$49.06), $470.0M (FY2025, ~$73.44) and $75.0M (Q1 FY2026, ~$150.00) retired 14.2M shares at approximately $62.69 average, now worth roughly $2.8B. Diluted shares fell from 150.0M to 138.6M while the convertible overhang was extinguished. The board added $500M of authorization on February 18, 2026, leaving $545M available at March 29, 2026. Management also declined to dilute through the trough, which is the harder test.

Two offsets. First, incremental repurchase at $197.58 is a materially lower-return deployment than at $50–75: at a 1.24% FCF yield, buying stock returns less than retiring the 7.25% 2030 notes. I do not score continued buyback at these levels as value-accretive the way the historical program was, and the remaining $545M is only 2.0% of market cap.

Second, insider activity is heavy and one-directional. CEO Kimberly Fields sold approximately 190,900 shares for roughly $34.1M between May 11 and July 28, 2026 at $154.02–$202.48, reducing reported holdings from 316,463 to 125,564 (−60%), executing roughly 84% of a 227,022-share 10b5-1 plan adopted February 5, 2026 for stated tax and estate planning. Former Executive Chairman Wetherbee sold approximately 31,276 shares in February at $144–146 following January 5, 2026 PSU settlements. Director Morehouse sold 6,609 shares in March. No open-market purchases by any officer or director appear in the reviewed record.

The mitigating context is real: the January 5, 2026 vesting created concentrated exposure and large cash tax obligations (Fields alone had 78,251 shares withheld at $121.08, roughly $9.5M), the plan predates the run-up, and pre-scheduled diversification is well-governed behavior. The reason I do not fully discount it is that the offsetting signal — rapidly rising estimates — is only +5.5% over 90 days, not the +30% velocity that would render insider selling informationally irrelevant. A 60% reduction in the CEO's reported holdings inside eleven weeks, with the final tranche at $190.99, reads as a neutral-to-mildly-negative signal on *valuation* and tells me nothing about fundamentals.

Compensation design is a further concern. FY2025 "compensation actually paid" was $41.1M for the PEO and averaged $27.4M for other NEOs. The FY2023–25 PSU cycle vested at 195.6% on relative TSR, the FY2022 cycle at 200.0%, FY2021 at 198.5%, and the first half of the 2022 "Breakout Performance Award" at maximum. A new one-time FY2025 "Enterprise Value Acceleration" PSU measures *absolute* TSR over four years with a 0x–3x payout range (161,000 target units, $28.2M grant-date fair value). On a commodity-input-levered cyclical, absolute-TSR triggers with 3x caps can pay out on cycle beta. Against that, the relative-TSR results are peer-measured and the scorecard includes cash-flow metrics; indexed TSR of $697.38 versus a peer group at $196.36 since FY2021 is genuine outperformance, and total stock compensation expense of $29.0M is contained. The mechanical cost that recurs is tax-withholding repurchase: $81.1M in Q1 FY2026 versus $34.1M for all of FY2025.

Governance: Form 4 title changes indicate Fields moved from President/CEO/Director (January 2026) to Chair/President/CEO (June 2026), implying consolidation of the chair and CEO roles — inferred from Form 4 headers rather than read from the underlying Item 5.02 filings, and therefore not established. If correct, it reduces independent board oversight. The board itself is unusually well-matched to the business (Boeing, Pratt & Whitney, USAF and energy-economics backgrounds).

**Management quality: 6.2.** Excellent trough capital allocation and a well-executed refinancing, discounted for concentrated one-directional insider selling, buyback economics that no longer clear the bar, aggressive absolute-TSR incentive design, and an unverified chair/CEO consolidation.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $235 (+18.9%)

Commercial jet engine demand is long-cycle and supply-constrained, and ATI's qualification position in nickel superalloys, titanium and precision forgings cannot be replicated in the timeframe of this cycle — particularly with VSMPO-AVISMA removed from Western qualification. FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $1.01–1.06B implies 17.5%–23.4% growth and, on FY2025 seasonality, is achievable without an unusual ramp. Four consecutive beats (average surprise 8.3%) suggest consensus of $4.46 and $5.42 is conservative; if FY2027 lands near $5.60 and the market applies 42x — inside the range at which CW (55.5x) and BWXT (51.9x) trade on trailing earnings — the stock clears $235. The June refinancing removed the 2027 maturity and a covenant trigger at an unchanged coupon, $545M of buyback authorization remains, and the AA&S mix shift has 200–300bp of further margin runway if industrial capacity continues rotating to aero. Defense at +38.8% over two years adds a less cyclical leg. The zirconium/hafnium nuclear franchise is an unmodeled call option on Western reactor build-out.

### Bear Case — $140 (−29.1%)

The multiple, not the business, is the risk. At 36.5x FY2027 consensus and a 1.24% FCF yield, ATI requires uninterrupted margin expansion on 1–5% revenue growth. Several things can interrupt it: nickel moving $1/lb costs roughly $70M against $859M of EBITDA with contractual recovery lags; the Q1 inventory build (+$177.1M, turns −13%, reserves +$19.6M) converts to a cash and margin drag if second-half volumes disappoint; the receivable-sale and supplier-finance tailwinds are largely spent (securitization drawn $120M of $125M, one purchase agreement terminated); and cash taxes are converging on the 19.9% book rate for a roughly $27M annual drag. Non-aero revenue has fallen 14.5% in two years with the medical collapse unexplained, so the "capacity reallocation" framing may partly be share loss. Chinese export controls on nickel, zirconium, hafnium and titanium sponge represent a supply interruption that ATI itself flags twice. Airframes at −9.3% may be the leading indicator. A 30x multiple on $4.65 of achieved FY2027 earnings — hardly a crisis assumption — is $140, roughly the March 2026 price. With a 36.5x entry multiple, compression alone delivers most of that.

## Conclusion

ATI has executed well. The company has converted a diversified specialty metals portfolio into an aerospace materials franchise with 23.6% HPMC segment margins, record backlog, modest leverage at 1.60x net debt/EBITDA, a clean refinancing to 2033, and a buyback record that retired roughly 10% of the share count at roughly a third of the current price. The dimensions that historically separate winners — financial resilience and management alignment on capital deployment — both score above average here.

The reason this remains a HOLD for a fourth consecutive review is that the stock has done the work. Since the prior assessment at $170.05 the price has advanced 16% with no intervening quarterly data, taking the multiple to 65x trailing GAAP earnings, 44.3x current-year consensus, 27.4x guided EV/EBITDA and a 1.24% free-cash-flow yield, with the price 1.4% below the consensus mean target and 3.8% below the 52-week high. Estimate revisions of +5.5% over 90 days are supportive but not the kind of velocity that justifies paying up. Against that, the free-cash-flow improvement is partly financed by receivable programs that are nearly exhausted, cash taxes are converging upward by roughly five points, working capital deteriorated in the most recent quarter, the CEO has reduced her reported holdings 60% in eleven weeks with no insider on the other side, and the options market is pricing a ±12.7% event within sixteen days at the 91st percentile of implied volatility.

Existing holders should stay long and consider selling expensive upside calls into the print rather than adding. New capital should wait for either the Q2 working-capital confirmation or a price in the $160–170 range. The business is better than it was in March; the investment, at this price, is not.
