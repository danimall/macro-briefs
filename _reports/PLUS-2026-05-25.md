## Executive Summary

ePlus Inc has undergone a meaningful strategic transformation, divesting its financing business in June 2025 for $180M and emerging as a pure-play IT solutions provider experiencing a strong demand recovery. Revenue from continuing operations grew 24.6% in fiscal Q3 2026 (December quarter), with operating income more than doubling. The company trades at approximately 15x forward earnings and 9x EV/EBITDA, modest multiples for a business posting mid-20s% revenue growth.

The growth inflection is driven by identifiable structural demand: AI infrastructure buildout, telecom networking refresh (particularly Verizon), and memory chip shortage-induced pull-forward purchasing. However, two specific risks prevent a higher-conviction recommendation: (1) Verizon represents 16% of revenue and the Telecom/Media vertical grew 53% over 9 months — a pace that is inherently unsustainable and creates meaningful reversion risk, and (2) management has doubled inventory to $241M, generating -$223M in 9-month operating cash flow, creating near-term balance sheet strain if the demand cycle decelerates faster than expected.

The stock has appreciated 30% over the past year and sits 9% below its 52-week high of $93.98. With the sole analyst target at $115 (35% upside) and four consecutive earnings beats averaging 33% surprise, the setup is constructive but requires sustained execution through the next 1-2 quarters to validate the growth trajectory.

## Company Overview

ePlus is a mid-market IT solutions provider serving approximately 4,600 customers across telecom/media (29% of 9-month FY26 revenue), SLED (13%), technology (13%), healthcare (13%), and financial services (10%). Following the June 2025 divestiture of its financing business, the company operates three segments: Product (81% of revenue), Professional Services (11%), and Managed Services (8%).

Key vendor relationships include Cisco (29% of Q3 net sales, down from 44% in FY2024), NetApp, HPE, Arista, Palo Alto Networks, and NVIDIA. The August 2024 Bailiwick acquisition ($125M) added professional/managed services capabilities for retail and hospitality, broadening the company's recurring revenue base.

| Adjusted EBITDA | $53.4M | $27.0M | +97% |

The operating leverage is substantial. Revenue growth of 25% translated to 164% operating income growth, demonstrating the fixed-cost absorption characteristics of the distribution/VAR model. Product margin expanded 170bps to 23.8%.

**Balance Sheet:** Cash of $326M with minimal long-term debt (only a $133M floor plan facility). Net cash position supports the ~$2.24B market cap. Stockholders' equity of $1.06B implies approximately 2.1x P/B.

**Cash Flow Concern:** Nine-month operating cash flow was -$223M, driven entirely by working capital: accounts receivable increased $184M (revenue growth) and inventory doubled to $241M (deliberate pre-build ahead of memory chip shortage). Prior year 9-month OCF was +$187M. This is a $410M swing. While management frames it as strategic positioning, it creates genuine execution risk — if demand softens, ePlus would be carrying excess inventory purchased at elevated memory chip prices.

**Earnings Power:** Annualizing the 9-month continuing operations run rate yields approximately $131M in net income, or roughly $5.00/share. At $85, this implies 17x trailing earnings power. The forward consensus of $5.21 for FY2026 (ending March 2026) and $5.54 for FY2027 places the stock at 15-16x forward.

## Growth Analysis

The growth inflection is driven by three identifiable channels:

1. **AI infrastructure demand** — Cloud segment revenue grew 50% in Q3. Customers are deploying GPU clusters, high-bandwidth networking, and associated storage/security infrastructure. ePlus's NVIDIA partnership and Cisco networking expertise position it well.

2. **Telecom networking refresh** — The Telecom/Media/Entertainment vertical grew 40% in Q3 and 53% over 9 months. This is heavily Verizon-influenced and represents a major spending cycle that likely peaks within 2-3 quarters.

3. **Memory chip shortage pull-forward** — The 10-Q explicitly notes worldwide memory shortages due to AI demand, with rapid price increases. Some portion of Q3's 32% product revenue growth represents customers accelerating purchases ahead of further price increases. This is inherently temporary.

**Sustainability Assessment:** The AI infrastructure channel has multi-year durability. The telecom refresh is likely a 2-3 quarter phenomenon. The memory shortage pull-forward is transitory. Blended, a reasonable assumption is that revenue growth decelerates from +25% to +10-15% over the next 12 months — still above the company's historical mid-single-digit trend.

**Estimate revisions are flat**, which is notable given the strong recent results. Current quarter consensus is $0.98 (2 analysts), essentially unchanged over 90 days. Next year estimates declined 4.2% over 90 days. This suggests the market has already incorporated the Q3 beat into forward estimates and may be modeling deceleration.

## Competitive Landscape

ePlus occupies a defensible mid-market niche between massive distributors (CDW at $21B revenue, Arrow at $28B) and smaller regional players. Its advantages include:

- Deep Cisco expertise with highest-tier certifications
- Integrated product + services + managed services offering
- Mid-market focus where large VARs provide less attention
- Post-Bailiwick capabilities in retail/hospitality deployment

Threats include:
- OEMs building direct sales capabilities (Dell, HPE)
- Cloud providers (AWS, Azure) disintermediating hardware sales
- CDW's scale advantages in procurement and pricing
- HPE/Juniper merger creating vendor consolidation risk

The diversification away from Cisco (44% → 29% of sales in 18 months) is a positive strategic development, reducing single-vendor dependency while the Telecom/Media customer concentration replaces one risk with another.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risk — Verizon Concentration & Revenue Lumpiness:** Verizon on a 30-day terminable contract, combined with rapid Telecom/Media growth, creates significant reversion risk. If Verizon's refresh cycle completes or pauses, ePlus could see a mid-teens revenue headwind from this single customer.

**Inventory Risk:** If demand decelerates or memory prices correct, ePlus faces potential margin compression on excess inventory. The negative operating cash flow over 9 months cannot persist — this is a bet on continued demand strength.

**DOGE/Government Spending:** SLED revenue already declining 16% YoY. If DOGE activities intensify, this segment (13% of revenue) faces further pressure. The 10-K explicitly identifies early contract terminations as a new risk.

**Options Market Signal:** Options activity is extremely thin (59 contracts across 3 expirations), reflecting the stock's small-cap nature and limited institutional derivatives activity. The 32.9% historical volatility is consistent with a small-cap IT distributor. No meaningful directional signal available from options data given the negligible volume. The 5.4% short interest (7.1 days to cover) is elevated relative to float, suggesting some institutional skepticism about growth sustainability.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($105-115, +23-35%):** AI infrastructure spending proves durable through FY2027, Telecom/Media refresh extends longer than expected, and managed services grows toward 12-15% of revenue mix. Operating margins sustain above 7% as the model demonstrates structural operating leverage. Multiple re-rates to 18-20x forward earnings. The inventory build proves prescient, allowing ePlus to fulfill orders competitors cannot. Earnings power reaches $6.00+ in FY2027.

**Bear Case ($65-70, -18-24%):** Verizon refresh cycle ends, memory shortage eases (deflating inventory values), SLED declines accelerate, and revenue growth reverts to flat/low-single-digit. Operating margin compresses back to 5% as fixed costs are under-absorbed. The negative cash flow from inventory build forces write-downs. Stock reverts toward 13x depressed earnings of $4.50-5.00.

**Base Case ($92-100, +8-17%):** Growth decelerates to 10-15% over next 12 months as telecom spending normalizes but AI infrastructure demand provides partial offset. Margins hold at 6.5-7.0%. Stock re-rates modestly to 17-18x on $5.50 forward EPS as the market gains confidence in the post-divestiture model.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Next quarter (fiscal Q4, March 2026) revenue sustains above +15% YoY growth, validating durability beyond one-quarter spike
2. Operating cash flow turns positive as AR/inventory normalize, demonstrating the working capital build was timing-related
3. Verizon revenue contribution declines below 12% while total revenue grows, proving diversification

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Revenue growth decelerates below +5% in any quarter, suggesting the cycle has passed
2. Operating margin compresses below 5.5%, indicating pricing power erosion or inventory write-downs
3. Management signals inventory write-downs or customer destocking

**Reassessment Timeline:** Fiscal Q4 FY2026 earnings (expected late May/early June 2026) is the critical data point. The company's February 4, 2026 Q3 report already passed — the next quarterly report should reveal whether the growth trajectory is sustaining or decelerating.

## Conclusion

ePlus is a fundamentally sound business trading at a reasonable valuation with genuine growth tailwinds. The 15x forward P/E on a 25%-growth business looks optically cheap.

This remains a HOLD rather than a BUY because of: (1) Verizon concentration creating binary risk in a single customer's spending cycle, (2) negative $223M operating cash flow from an aggressive inventory build that creates genuine downside if wrong, and (3) flat estimate revisions suggesting the market has already priced the near-term acceleration. The next earnings report is the decision point — if growth sustains and cash flow normalizes, the thesis upgrades cleanly. Until then, the risk/reward favors patience at $85 given the stock has already recovered from its $62 low.
