**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Entergy is a multi-jurisdiction regulated electric utility serving ~3 million customers across Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi, Texas, and New Orleans. Following the July 2025 divestiture of its gas distribution businesses, it is now a pure-play electric utility positioned at the center of the strongest demand tailwind in the US power sector: data-center and Gulf Coast industrial load growth. Q1 2026 industrial volumes rose 15% system-wide (+91% in Mississippi), while residential fell 8% on milder weather; the growth is unambiguously large-load-driven. The company has translated this into a ~$57B 2026-2029 construction plan and $8.0B of contracted fixed-revenue agreements running through 2048.

The investment debate concerns whether state regulators allow Entergy to recover an enormous, fast-growing capex base from ratepayers without forcing hyperscalers or equity holders to absorb more, and whether the balance sheet can carry the build. Two facts frame the caution. First, debt-to-capital reached 65.9% in Q1 2026, sitting at the 65% credit-facility covenant level (calculated differently under the facility, still compliant), while the plan requires ~$3B of additional junior subordinated debt and ~$6.6B of equity through 2029. Second, the regulatory posture is tightening as capex balloons: the Arkansas commission found Entergy demonstrated need but not prudence on Jefferson Power Station, set a benchmark $90M below the company's estimate, and ordered independent cost monitors. The Louisiana Cottonwood acquisition docket tests whether data-center-driven generation costs can be socialized at all.

At $109.54 the stock trades at ~24.9x forward 2026 EPS of $4.40 and ~21.7x 2027 EPS of $5.06, a premium to the electric-utility peer median of 19.0x. The growth justifies a premium, but the multiple already embeds favorable resolution of the regulatory and financing risks. Estimate revisions are modestly positive (next-year EPS +2.8% over 90 days), and the company has compounded ~37% over the past twelve months. We rate ETR a HOLD with a growth-oriented thesis: the rate-base runway is real and the backlog provides visibility, but the asymmetry from current levels is limited until the December 2026 LPSC ruling and the fall 2026 Cottonwood decision de-risk the cost-recovery question. A pullback toward the high-$90s, or a clean regulatory win, would upgrade the case.

## Company Overview

Entergy operates a single reportable "Utility" segment through five regulated operating companies (Entergy Arkansas, Entergy Louisiana, Entergy Mississippi, Entergy New Orleans, and Entergy Texas) plus System Energy Resources, which owns 90% of the Grand Gulf nuclear unit under a Unit Power Sales Agreement. Each operating company is separately rate-regulated by its state commission (APSC, LPSC, MPSC, New Orleans City Council, PUCT), with FERC/MISO oversight at the wholesale and transmission level. The competitive moat is the regulatory franchise itself: a vertically integrated monopoly in fast-growing, business-friendly states.

The generation fleet is nuclear-heavy (Arkansas Nuclear One 1&2, River Bend, Waterford 3, Grand Gulf), all currently in NRC Action Matrix Column 1, with one preliminary "white" finding flagged at Grand Gulf in March 2026 pending Q2 resolution. The July 2025 sale of the Louisiana and New Orleans gas distribution businesses simplified the company into a focused electric utility.

Entergy has become the premier data-center-and-industrial load-growth story among US regulated utilities, anchored in Louisiana and Mississippi. Hyperscalers (Meta, via subsidiary Evest, and Amazon Web Services) are customers contracting for long-term electric service, not partners owning Entergy assets. Entergy owns and builds the generation, which enters its regulated rate base. That structure is the source of both the upside (rate-base expansion) and the central dispute (who pays for it).

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Q1 2026 total operating revenue reached $3.19B, up 12% from $2.85B a year earlier, with electric revenue of $3.17B (vs $2.76B). Net income attributable to Entergy was $384.9M (vs $360.8M), but diluted EPS rose only to $0.83 from $0.82, with earnings growth muted by share issuance. Total retail electric sales volume rose 4% and total volume 8%, driven by industrial demand. The composition is large-load-driven growth, not weather or consumer strength.

**The capex supercycle.** The updated 2026-2029 construction plan totals approximately $57B ($13.2B / $16.8B / $15.4B / $11.8B), with generation the dominant bucket at $7.7B-$11.8B per year. Q1 2026 capex was $2.25B (up from $1.66B), property, plant and equipment grew to $54.9B net, and construction work in progress jumped to $7.7B from $6.0B at year-end 2025 in a single quarter. Rate base growth on this scale supports the company's growth runway, the core of the bull thesis.

**Leverage at the covenant ceiling.** Debt-to-capital rose to 65.9% from 64.3% at year-end 2025. Total long-term debt grew to $31.2B (+$3.2B in one quarter). The company issued ~$3.7B of long-term debt in Q1 alone at coupons of 4.9%-5.75%, and plans $3B of junior subordinated debentures through 2029 ($1.3B already done in November 2025) plus ~$6.6B of equity through 2029 via an ATM/forward program (~$1.9B already contracted). Interest expense climbed to $399.9M from $348.4M. This is a high-leverage utility funding a record build into a higher-for-longer rate environment.

**Dilution.** Diluted shares rose to 462.5M from 440.6M year-over-year. The equity program will continue to dilute, which is the mechanism by which strong rate-base growth converts into more muted per-share growth, exactly the pattern visible in Q1 (12% revenue growth, ~7% net income growth, ~1% EPS growth).

**Cash flow and customer advances.** Operating cash flow was $829M (up from $536M), aided by ~$294M YoY from data-center customer prepayments (contributions in aid of construction). Total customer advances now run ~$2.8B, a meaningful funding source and credit cushion. The company is deeply FCF-negative by design, as capex far exceeds operating cash flow, funded by debt, equity, and customer advances. This is normal for a utility in a build cycle but raises execution and financing risk.

**Dividend.** Raised to $0.64/quarter from $0.60, ~$293M paid in Q1, yielding ~2.3%. Dividend growth is continuing alongside the capex ramp, though the yield is below the peer median of 3.3%.

**Earnings cadence.** The recent surprise record is mixed: Q1 2026 missed by 0.7%, Q4 2025 missed by 3.2%, but Q3 and Q2 2025 beat by 4.8% and 13.0% respectively. Two beats and two misses across four quarters argues for normal visibility, not a high-confidence beat machine.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is the strongest dimension and rests on durable, broad-based demand signals. US utilities filed for ~24 GW of gas-fired capacity in 2025, a 570% jump versus 2023, and Entergy forecasts that new-build gas plants entering service in 2030 will cost ~65% more than those coming online today. Entergy sits at the epicenter of this demand: AI/data-center load plus Gulf Coast industrial reshoring (LNG, steel, petrochemicals).

Concrete commitments anchor the runway. The March 2026 second Meta data center agreement (north Louisiana, backed by a Meta parent guaranty) accompanies an LPSC application to build seven combined-cycle gas turbines totaling 5,278 MW (~$12.9B), a 500 kV line (~$1.4B), and battery/solar, with a decision targeted for December 2026. AWS expanded its Madison County, Mississippi campuses and signed a new Hinds County agreement. The $8.0B of contracted fixed-revenue backlog through 2048 ($4.4B at Entergy Arkansas, $2.6B at Entergy Mississippi) provides multi-year visibility.

Estimate revisions confirm positive momentum without being explosive: next-quarter EPS revised +2.5% over 90 days, next-year EPS revised +2.8% over 90 days. Current-year estimates are roughly flat. This is steady upward pressure consistent with a high-single-digit grower, not the +30% velocity that would signal the market is systematically underestimating an earnings recovery. The growth here is high-quality and durable but already broadly understood.

The breadth of the data-center demand signal across the utility sector (confirmed in load data at multiple regulated peers, in transmission and electrical-equipment order books, and in power-generation backlogs) argues against the hypothesis that this is a single-company story or a speculative bubble at the infrastructure layer. The demand is real. The question for Entergy specifically is conversion of that demand into recoverable, per-share earnings.

## Valuation Assessment

Against the electric-utility peer median P/E of 19.0x (AEP 19.0x, EXC 16.6x, PEG 17.4x, WEC 22.5x), Entergy carries a clear premium. The premium is partly justified by superior load growth versus the slower-load peers. On 2027 EPS the multiple remains full.

The dividend yield sits below the peer median, so there is limited absolute yield support relative to alternatives. WEC at 22.5x offers a comparison for a high-quality, premium-multiple utility, but WEC carries less leverage and less binary regulatory risk than Entergy faces in its 2026 dockets.

Analyst targets bracket the picture: low $91, mean $121.88, median $126, high $135. The current price sits ~13% below the median target, implying modest upside if the consensus growth path holds. We do not treat these targets as floors here because ETR is not a cheap stock with rapidly rising estimates; it is a premium-multiple compounder where the targets and the price largely reflect the same favorable extrapolation of the data-center thesis. The asymmetry is more two-sided than the gap to median target suggests.

The valuation score reflects genuine ambiguity. The growth premium is defensible, but the multiple already prices in favorable resolution of the Cottonwood cost-recovery dispute, the December 2026 LPSC ruling, and continued capital-markets access at reasonable rates. A "cheap relative to growth" thesis does not hold; this is a fairly-valued, high-quality utility where the path to outperformance requires the regulatory dockets to break Entergy's way and the dilution drag to ease. Higher-for-longer rates are a direct headwind to financing-heavy regulated utilities and a compressor of utility equity multiples, weighing against multiple expansion from here.

## Competitive Landscape

Entergy's competitive position is improving on the dimension that matters most: it has converted incumbency in fast-growing states into the sector's most compelling load-growth runway. The franchise is a regulated monopoly, so the threat is not rival utilities. The risks are (a) regulators and ratepayer advocates limiting cost recovery, (b) technology shifts reducing data-center power intensity, and (c) hyperscalers pursuing behind-the-meter or self-generation.

The regulatory advantage is tangible: Louisiana under Governor Landry has offered Meta incentives and the LPSC has fast-tracked approvals, but it is being tested. The "Fair Share Plus" / LPSC "Lightning Initiative" structures (minimum bills, contributions in aid of construction, early-termination payments, a Meta parent guaranty) are designed to protect ratepayers while letting Entergy build. These protections are untested at scale, and they cut both ways: Entergy also faces performance penalties, including possible contract termination, if it fails to build on time.

The competitive position score reflects a strengthening structural tailwind tempered by a tightening regulatory posture. Commissions are getting more demanding (independent cost monitors, mandated all-source competitive solicitations, prudence pushback) as bills rise. The trajectory is positive, but the moat's durability now depends on regulatory outcomes that are not yet settled.

## Risk Assessment

**Data-center concentration and stranded-asset risk.** Filings now explicitly warn of "concentration of business and credit risk with a small number of customers in an industry based on emerging technologies, including artificial intelligence" and "the potential for stranded assets." Entergy is building ~5,278 MW of CCGTs in Louisiana largely tied to Meta, plus AWS deals in Mississippi. If AI/data-center demand slows or a customer defaults, Entergy could be left with stranded gas plants. The Meta parent guaranty mitigates counterparty credit risk but concentrates exposure in one cyclical industry.

**Regulatory cost-allocation — the signature near-term risk.** The Cottonwood acquisition is the live test. Entergy wants to buy the ~1.3 GW, 22-year-old Cottonwood plant for ~$1.4B + ~$300M upgrades = ~$1.8B. A LPSC consultant noted that private-equity owner Atlas Holdings bought Cottonwood plus other plants from Cleco for ~$600M in 2023, that the plant has had "catastrophic transformer failures," that the need exists "principally [because of] the Meta-related ramp," and recommended the PSC reject ratepayer cost recovery and have Meta pay instead. Two PSC members and Governor Landry have publicly raised concerns; Landry said Entergy promised costs would not be passed to ratepayers (~$8.65/month for the average customer). The hearing is set for September 2026 with a decision targeted for fall. A rejection or a "Meta-pays" outcome would set a precedent affecting the entire ~$57B plan and could create stranded-cost exposure (a $672M figure has been cited).

**Prudence disallowance.** The Arkansas APSC's Jefferson Power Station order (January 2026) found need but not prudence, set a benchmark $90M below Entergy's estimate, and ordered independent cost monitors plus all-source competitive solicitations, a structural tightening of recovery as capex balloons.

**Financing and leverage.** Debt-to-capital is at the covenant edge with ~$57B to fund and ~$9.6B more debt/equity planned through 2029. Any rating downgrade raises borrowing costs as issuance runs at record levels.

**Storm/catastrophe.** Winter Storm Fern (January 2026) cost ~$480M to restore (~$400M capital) and spiked January gas purchases to $483M (vs $207M). Recovery depends on securitization and regulatory approval with timing lag. Mississippi enacted securitization legislation in April 2026; Louisiana is deferring ~$142M of fuel costs.

**Nuclear and tax.** The Grand Gulf preliminary "white" NRC finding is pending Q2 resolution. PTC monetization under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act of 2025 faces uncertain IRS treatment, with customers ultimately bearing disallowance risk under LPSC/APSC orders but rate treatment unsettled.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is largely complacent and consistent with a low-volatility utility. Near-term ATM IV is 18.8% against 1-year historical volatility of 20.0% (a -1.2% spread, fairly priced), with the IV percentile at the 3rd percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in contango (near-term 18.8% < long-term 21.7%), and the put/call OI ratio collapsed from 0.22 to 0.06 since the prior snapshot. There is a modest near-term put/call skew of 5.4% (elevated put demand), but the very low absolute volume and OI suggest this is event-hedging rather than informed bearish positioning. The 3-month tenor shows a P/C volume ratio of 2.00 and an 8.1% implied move, which lines up with the September Cottonwood hearing window, worth monitoring as the docket approaches. Short interest is moderate at 5.6% of float (5.3 days to cover), neither crowded short nor a squeeze setup. The options market is not pricing acute stress, consistent with the fundamental view that the near-term risk is regulatory-binary rather than imminent.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($135+).** The LPSC ruling approves the Meta-linked build with favorable cost allocation, the Cottonwood acquisition clears with ratepayer recovery intact, and the plan converts into strong rate-base-led EPS growth. Customer advances and the Meta guaranty insulate the balance sheet, ratings hold, and the contracted backlog de-risks volume. The stock re-rates toward a sustained 26-27x forward multiple on 2027 EPS of $5.06+, reaching $135 (consensus high) and beyond as the demand tailwind broadens. This requires regulatory wins and continued favorable capital-markets access.

**Bear case ($90-95).** The Cottonwood docket forces a "Meta-pays" outcome or rejects recovery, setting a precedent that data-center generation costs cannot be socialized. Prudence disallowances spread, stranded-cost exposure crystallizes (~$672M cited), and a rating-agency action raises borrowing costs as issuance peaks. Higher-for-longer rates compress the utility multiple toward the peer median of 19x, and the equity program dilutes per-share growth. The stock falls toward the analyst low of $91, ~17% downside from current levels.

The base case sits between these: regulators approve the build but with tighter cost-sharing and monitoring, EPS compounds at a high-single-digit rate net of dilution, and the stock tracks earnings growth with limited multiple expansion given the rate headwind. This argues for a 12-month base-case fair value around $118-122.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The growth runway and contracted backlog justify ownership, but the asymmetry from $109.54 is limited until the 2026 regulatory dockets resolve.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The Cottonwood docket (decision ~fall 2026) clears with material ratepayer cost recovery intact, validating the socialization of data-center generation costs.
- The December 2026 LPSC ruling approves the 5,278 MW Meta build with favorable cost allocation and no significant disallowance.
- A pullback toward the high-$90s on no fundamental deterioration, which would compress the forward multiple toward ~22x and restore meaningful upside to consensus targets.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A "Meta-pays" or recovery-rejection outcome at Cottonwood that establishes a stranded-cost precedent across the ~$57B plan.
- A credit-rating downgrade or breach pressure on the 65% debt-to-capital covenant amid the heavy issuance schedule.
- Evidence of data-center demand softening (a hyperscaler contract cancellation, materially reduced large-load applications) that threatens the load-growth thesis.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings release (Grand Gulf NRC resolution, updated capex/financing detail), the September 2026 Cottonwood hearing, and the December 2026 LPSC decision. The next two quarters of regulatory outcomes will determine whether this HOLD upgrades or downgrades.

## Conclusion

Entergy is a high-quality regulated utility riding the most powerful demand tailwind in its sector, with a ~$57B four-year rate-base runway and $8.0B of contracted hyperscaler backlog. The growth is durable and broad-based, and the franchise advantage in fast-growing, business-friendly states is real.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is that the stock at ~24.9x forward already prices in favorable resolution of the two questions that define the thesis: whether regulators let Entergy recover this enormous capex from rate base without forcing more onto hyperscalers or equity, and whether the balance sheet can carry the build at the covenant ceiling through a higher-for-longer rate environment with ~$6.6B of dilutive equity to come. The Cottonwood docket is a clean, dated test of the first question, and a Governor and two PSC members publicly questioning the deal carries weight. The dilution drag is already visible: 12% revenue growth and ~7% net income growth produced ~1% EPS growth in Q1.

The risk/reward is roughly balanced from current levels: ~13% upside to the consensus median against ~17% downside to the bear case, with the binary tilted toward regulatory outcomes the market has not yet seen. We rate ETR a HOLD and would become more constructive on a clean Cottonwood or LPSC win, or on a pullback toward the high-$90s that restores valuation cushion.
