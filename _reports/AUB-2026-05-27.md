**Research Date: May 27, 2026**


## Executive Summary

Atlantic Union Bankshares is a $5.4B market cap regional bank that has transformed itself through two consecutive acquisitions — American National (closed Q2 2024) and Sandy Spring Bancorp (closed Q1 2025) — growing total assets from ~$20.5B to $37.6B in three years. The bank now dominates the Virginia/DC Metro regional banking market with $30.5B in deposits and strong wealth management capabilities.

The stock trades at $37.64, representing 10.9x adjusted operating EPS ($3.44) and 1.74x tangible book value, with a 3.9% dividend yield. Analyst consensus targets $44.13 (17% upside), and the stock has appreciated 27.5% over the past year from its 52-week low of $29. However, the investment case is complicated by a structural earnings headwind: $146M of temporary purchase accounting accretion inflating current NIM (3.80% reported vs. ~3.33% core), rising credit stress in CRE-heavy portfolios (NCOs tripled to 0.17% in 2025), and negative estimate revisions over the past 90 days. In the current rate environment — with potential hikes rather than further cuts — AUB's asset-sensitive positioning provides some buffer, but CRE stress could intensify.

The stock is best held for its income characteristics. The 3.9% yield is well-covered, and organic loan growth guidance of 5-8% supports steady-state earnings. But the valuation premium to peers (16.2x trailing vs. peer median 11x) already prices in integration success, and the approaching accretion cliff makes earnings growth trajectory uncertain beyond FY2026.

## Company Overview

Atlantic Union Bankshares operates through Atlantic Union Bank, the largest regional bank headquartered in Virginia. The franchise spans Virginia, Maryland, Washington D.C., North Carolina, and South Carolina with two operating segments:

- **Wholesale Banking**: $23.2B in loans, $11.3B in deposits. Serves commercial clients with CRE, C&I lending, equipment finance (nationwide), treasury management, and wealth management.
- **Consumer Banking**: $5.3B in loans, $17.8B in deposits. Retail, small business, and home lending.

The Sandy Spring acquisition (April 2025, $1.3B) added 50+ branches in the affluent DC Metro/Maryland market and expanded wealth management AUM by approximately 123%. Systems integration was completed October 2025 — a rapid 6-month timeline. Management subsequently sold $2B in Sandy Spring CRE loans to reduce concentration risk.

The GAAP/Adjusted divergence ($2.03 vs. $3.44) reflects $157M in merger-related costs and $101M in CECL Day 1 provisioning — both non-recurring. Adjusted EPS of $3.44 represents the ongoing earnings run-rate.

**The Accretion Problem:**
The critical analytical issue is that $146M in net purchase accounting accretion contributed approximately 47 bps to NIM in 2025. Core NIM ex-accretion is approximately 3.33%. This accretion will decay over 3-5 years, creating a structural headwind of roughly $0.60-0.80 per share in annual EPS that must be offset by organic growth or operating leverage to maintain current earnings levels.

**Capital Position:**
- CET1: 10.10% (well above 7.0% minimum)
- Total Capital: 13.90%
- TCE/TA: 7.85%
- Tangible Book Value/Share: ~$21.67

**Credit Quality — Deteriorating:**
- NCOs: 0.17% of average loans (up from 0.05% in 2024)
- NPAs: $117M (0.42% of LHFI, up from 0.32%)
- Multifamily nonaccruals: surged from $132K to $45.4M
- Nonaccrual additions: $145M in 2025 vs. $52M in 2024

Two large C&I charge-offs drove most of the NCO increase. The multifamily stress is concentrated in DC Metro and signals emerging pressure in a sector broadly stressed by elevated rates.

**Q1 2026 (Most Recent):**
Reported $0.89 adjusted EPS vs. $0.88 consensus (1.1% beat). Revenue grew approximately 70% YoY on the first full-year Sandy Spring contribution. Management guided to $29B-$30B year-end 2026 loan balances, implying 5-8% organic growth.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term (FY2026):** Consensus estimates $3.73 EPS, rising to $3.99 in FY2027 (+7% growth). This implies modest organic earnings growth once the first full year of Sandy Spring contribution is normalized.

**Growth Drivers:**
1. Organic loan growth: 5-8% guided for 2026
2. Wealth management expansion: Fiduciary fees grew 146% to $62.9M in 2025; this fee stream should grow organically in the affluent DC market
3. Cost synergies: Sandy Spring integration complete; further deposit optimization (exiting non-relationship deposits)
4. NIM support from asset sensitivity if rates rise

**Growth Headwinds:**
1. Accretion runoff: ~$30-40M annual NII headwind as marks amortize
2. Estimate revisions are negative: Current year EPS estimates have declined 0.9% over 90 days; next year down 1.5%
3. No near-term M&A likely given integration digestion period
4. If rates rise (hawkish scenario), CRE stress could accelerate, requiring higher provisioning

The negative estimate revision trend, while modest in magnitude (-1% to -2%), signals that analysts are beginning to price in some deceleration. This is consistent with the accretion decay thesis.

However, the 5-year TSR underperformance vs. the KBW Regional Banking Index (128% vs. 153%) suggests the market has historically discounted AUB's M&A strategy relative to organic growers. The stock is not cheap enough to generate a compelling BUY thesis — upside is limited to yield + modest multiple expansion, while CRE and accretion risks create meaningful downside scenarios.

## Competitive Landscape

AUB is now the largest Virginia-headquartered bank and has established a meaningful presence in the DC Metro market through Sandy Spring. The wealth management platform (AUM grew 123%) positions the bank to capture fee income from the affluent suburban DC population base.

The scale advantages from $37.6B in assets allow investments in technology and treasury management products that smaller community banks cannot match. The bank competes against larger nationals (Wells Fargo, Bank of America, Truist) where it differentiates on relationship banking and service, and smaller community banks where it has advantages in product breadth and lending capacity.

Key vulnerability: the 60% CRE loan concentration (vs. typical well-diversified banks at 30-40%) means AUB's credit outcomes are disproportionately tied to commercial real estate fundamentals in Virginia, Maryland, and the Carolinas. The proactive $2B loan sale demonstrates management awareness of this risk, but the remaining $16.7B CRE portfolio is substantial.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **CRE Concentration (HIGH):** 60% of total loans, with concentration/capital ratio at 275.3% (approaching the 300% regulatory attention threshold). Office exposure of $1.48B and multifamily stress ($45.4M newly nonaccrual) signal early-stage deterioration. In a recession scenario with CRE values declining 15-20%, AUB could face $200-400M in additional provisions.

2. **Accretion Cliff (MEDIUM-HIGH):** Unless organic revenue growth accelerates, EPS will face a $0.60-0.80/share headwind over 3 years as accretion decays materially by 2027-2028. The market may not fully appreciate this until H2 2026 / FY2027 results.

3. **Credit Cycle Positioning (MEDIUM):** In an environment where 30Y yields exceed 5%, CRE borrowers face refinancing stress at significantly higher rates than origination. The $27.8B loan book contains meaningful maturity walls in 2026-2027.

4. **Rate Sensitivity (MEDIUM):** Asset-sensitive in a rising rate environment (NII +7.44% in +300bp scenario), but if the Fed hikes, CRE stress could more than offset NII gains through higher provisioning.

5. **Integration Fatigue (LOW-MEDIUM):** While Sandy Spring conversion is complete, full cultural and operational integration of two major acquisitions in consecutive years creates residual risks. CFO transition during this period adds uncertainty.

**Options Market Signal:**
ATM IV collapsed from 92.5% to 47.1% between early May and late May, suggesting the market has resolved a significant uncertainty event (likely the Q1 2026 earnings report on April 21). The P/C OI ratio remains very low at 0.11 (call-dominant positioning), indicating limited demand for downside protection. This is mildly bullish from a sentiment perspective — the options market is not pricing imminent stress.

**Short Interest:** 9.2% of float with 9.6 days to cover. Moderate — not extreme enough to create squeeze risk, but sufficient to indicate informed bearish positioning, likely reflecting CRE concentration concerns.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($44-46):**
- Sandy Spring synergies exceed expectations, driving operating EPS toward $4.00+ in FY2026
- Rate environment supports NIM (if rates rise) and asset quality holds
- Wealth management fee income grows organically at 15-20% annually
- Multiple re-rates toward peer median as integration risk dissipates
- Math: 12x $3.75 = $45

**Bear Case ($28-32):**

- Accretion decay becomes visible in H2 2026 / FY2027 results
- Recession materializes, compressing loan growth and increasing NCOs to 0.40-0.50%
- Stock de-rates to 8-9x normalized EPS of ~$3.20 post-accretion = $26-29

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock declines to $32-33 (8.5-9x adjusted EPS) creating meaningful yield support at 4.5%+
- Q2/Q3 2026 earnings demonstrate core NIM stability ex-accretion above 3.40%
- CRE NPAs stabilize or decline for two consecutive quarters

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
- CRE NPAs exceed 1.0% of CRE loans (currently ~0.7%)
- NCOs exceed 0.30% of average loans for two consecutive quarters
- Management suspends dividend growth or announces dilutive capital raise
- CRE concentration ratio exceeds 300% (regulatory threshold)

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings release (likely July 2026 for Q2). Key data points: CRE credit metrics, NIM trajectory excluding accretion, loan growth pace.

## Conclusion

Atlantic Union Bankshares has executed an ambitious M&A strategy that transformed it into the largest Virginia-based regional bank with strong positioning in the wealthy DC Metro market. The integration of Sandy Spring is complete, operating ROTCE exceeds 20%, and the 3.9% dividend yield is well-supported by current earnings.

However, the stock at $37.64 does not offer compelling asymmetric upside. The trailing P/E premium to peers reflects current NIM inflated by temporary accretion income that will decay materially over 2-3 years. Credit quality is deteriorating, CRE concentration at 60% of loans creates outsized exposure to the sector under most stress in the current rate environment, and estimate revisions are trending negative.

The appropriate posture is HOLD for income. The 3.9% yield provides adequate compensation for maintaining the position, but adding exposure at current levels assumes either credit quality holds or the accretion cliff is offset by organic growth — neither of which has sufficient evidence to assign high confidence.
