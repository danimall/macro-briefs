**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Equitable Holdings has become a special-situation stock. On March 26, 2026, EQH announced an all-stock merger with Corebridge Financial (CRBG), creating a combined company valued at roughly $22 billion with about $1.5 trillion in AUM/AUA and 12 million-plus customers. Press accounts describe Corebridge shareholders holding approximately 51% and Equitable shareholders 49% of the new parent, which would operate under the Equitable brand. The definitive merger agreement dated March 26, 2026, and a Voting and Support Agreement with Nippon Life Insurance (a large Corebridge holder) are confirmed in EQH's own SEC exhibit index. Until this deal closes or breaks, EQH's price will trade primarily on deal spread, closing probability, and expected pro-forma value rather than standalone fundamentals.

The standalone franchise is healthier than a naive screen suggests. FY2025 GAAP shows a net loss of roughly $1.38 billion and negative reported stockholders' equity of about -$74 million, both alarming on the surface. These are largely artifacts of variable-annuity accounting under LDTI (market-risk-benefit and derivative remeasurement swinging with rates and equities) compounded by a wave of reinsurance-ceding transactions that optimize statutory capital. Q1 2026 confirmed the read: net income of roughly $621 million and operating EPS of about $1.62. Consensus operating EPS sits near $7.14 for FY2026 and $8.92 for FY2027, so on operating earnings the stock trades at roughly 6.7x forward, a level that reflects the market's insurance-accounting discount rather than franchise decay.

We move to HOLD (uncertain), consistent with the recent trajectory (BUY through May, HOLD in June). The core tension: the merger is a credible strategic combination that removes a direct annuity competitor and blends EQH's AllianceBernstein fee engine with Corebridge's large retirement book, but the exchange ratio, synergy targets, and pro-forma accretion are not yet defined in a filed S-4. Four consecutive quarterly earnings misses and a degrading analyst-recommendation trend argue against upgrading; the wide gap to a $60.82 mean target and $50–$68 target range argues against avoiding. The stock at $48 sits between those poles, which is where an uncertain-hold belongs until the joint proxy quantifies the deal.

## Company Overview

Equitable Holdings is a diversified financial services holding company organized around four principal businesses:

1. **Individual Retirement** — variable and fixed-index annuities. EQH finished #4 in the LIMRA 2025 U.S. annuity sales rankings, just behind Corebridge at #3.
2. **Group Retirement** — tax-deferred retirement plans, with a legacy leadership position in the K-12 educator / 403(b) channel.
3. **Protection Solutions** — life insurance and employee benefits.
4. **Asset & Wealth Management** — anchored by a ~61–65% ownership stake in publicly traded AllianceBernstein (AB), plus the Equitable Advisors retail wealth platform.

Founded in 1859 as The Equitable Life Assurance Society, formerly an AXA subsidiary, EQH IPO'd in 2018. Its competitive advantages are scale in annuities, a captive advisor distribution force, the AB asset-management engine that diversifies fee income away from pure spread/insurance risk, and deep entrenchment in the educator retirement market. Market capitalization is approximately $13.5 billion at $48.00, on 281.5 million shares outstanding.

## Financial Analysis

The single most important caveat: GAAP net income and book value are unreliable metrics for a variable-annuity writer under LDTI. Management and the sell side evaluate EQH on non-GAAP operating earnings, statutory capital, and holdco cash generation.

**Reported GAAP results.** The FY2025 net loss (versus roughly $1.3 billion of GAAP profit in FY2024) has two mechanical drivers: (a) market-risk-benefit and derivative remeasurement that swings with interest rates and equity levels, and (b) losses and reserve movements tied to a large reinsurance program, including cessions through a Bermuda-domiciled affiliate and third parties such as RGA. Negative GAAP equity for an annuity writer under LDTI is not evidence of insolvency; RBC and statutory capital are the solvency measures, and the standard bankruptcy/quality screens returned insufficient data because they are designed for industrials, not insurers.

**Operating results.** Q1 2026 confirmed that the FY2025 loss was remeasurement- and reinsurance-driven, not a collapse in earnings power. The trailing P/E of -15.7 and reported EPS of -$4.83 in the data feed are GAAP artifacts and should be disregarded for valuation.

**Earnings surprise pattern.** The company has missed consensus operating EPS in each of the last four quarters: -0.4% (Q1'26), -2.4% (Q4'25), -9.2% (Q3'25), and -15.3% (Q2'25). The misses are narrowing, and the most recent quarter was essentially in line, but the streak lowers confidence in forward estimates and is one reason we do not upgrade. This pattern feeds directly into a more cautious Financial Health and Growth read.

**Capital return.** Aggressive buybacks and a growing dividend have historically been central to the EQH thesis, funded by subsidiary distributions and AB cash flows. The dividend yield is approximately 2.46%. In a pending all-stock merger, capital-return cadence and pro-forma payout policy become uncertain, a factor the market will re-price once the S-4 details the combined entity's capital plan.

**Bottom line:** The franchise generates recurring operating earnings and AB fee income, but the statements are among the noisiest in the sector, and the near-term financial picture is dominated by deal mechanics rather than organic cash flow visibility.

## Growth Analysis

On a standalone basis, EQH is a mid-single-digit operating-earnings compounder levered to equity-market levels (AB AUM, variable-annuity fee bases) and rate levels (spread income). The consensus FY26-to-FY27 operating EPS step implies roughly 25% year-over-year growth into next year, though much of that reflects the normalization of a market-sensitive base and the tailwind of favorable equity markets rather than durable organic acceleration.

Estimate revisions are mixed and modest. Over the last 90 days, the current-quarter estimate fell 6.7% and the current-year estimate slipped 0.9%, while the next-quarter (+0.9%) and next-year (+1.1%) estimates rose slightly. The 30-day picture is marginally better — current-year +1.0%, next-year +1.4%. This is a slowly stabilizing estimate base with a soft near-term quarter, not the >30% revision velocity that would flag a strong buy signal. The four-quarter miss streak tempers reliance on even these figures.

The merger is the growth story that matters. Combining Corebridge (#3) and Equitable (#4) would top the U.S. annuity league table, and the pro-forma platform blends EQH's fee-generating AB engine and advisor distribution with Corebridge's ~$380 billion retirement book. Whether that combination is accretive to per-share operating earnings depends entirely on the exchange ratio and synergy targets, neither of which is quantified in a filed S-4 yet. Until those numbers exist, the growth case is directional rather than measurable.

## Valuation Assessment

Standard multiples are of limited use here for two reasons: GAAP earnings and book value are distorted, and the stock is a merger situation. On operating earnings, EQH trades at 5.4x FY27 consensus, a deep discount that reflects the sector's insurance-accounting skepticism and the deal overhang, not a growth premium.

Peer comparison is muddied by the same GAAP noise. The sector peer set shows a median P/E of 11.4x (RNR 4.9x, GL 11.4x, AIZ 13.3x, IVZ -19.1x on its own accounting distortion, ALLY 10.6x). EQH's reported -15.7x is meaningless; on operating earnings, EQH is cheaper than most of this cohort. The peer median dividend yield of 1.4% is well below EQH's, offering some absolute yield support.

Analyst targets frame the deal-adjusted upside: mean $60.82, median $60.00, low $50.00, high $68.00. At $48.00, the stock sits below even the low target, implying either a persistent deal-uncertainty discount or a market that is not fully pricing the pro-forma combination. We are cautious about treating "cheap" as a buy signal on its own — in the current regime, low headline multiples have frequently marked value traps rather than opportunities, and here the discount is partly a rational reflection of unresolved exchange-ratio and closing risk. The catalyst that would validate the cheapness — a filed S-4 with clear accretion math and a defined timeline — has not yet arrived. We score valuation moderately positive rather than high: the discount is real, but the mispricing thesis is not yet independently confirmable.

## Competitive Landscape

EQH's competitive position is stable-to-improving, and the merger, if completed, materially strengthens it. Corebridge and Equitable were direct rivals in the 2025 U.S. annuity market; combining removes a competitor and creates scale leadership. The pro-forma platform spans retirement, life, wealth, and asset management, a diversified fee-and-spread base that is harder for single-line competitors to match.

The AB relationship is a genuine differentiator: fee income diversifies EQH away from pure insurance/spread risk, though it also introduces market-level and net-flow sensitivity. The educator 403(b) channel remains a defensible, entrenched niche with high switching costs. The captive Equitable Advisors force provides distribution control that pure manufacturers lack.

The competitive risk is regulatory and structural rather than share loss: fiduciary/best-interest rules could pressure distribution economics, and the industry trend toward private-capital involvement in retirement balance sheets raises the competitive intensity on spread products. On trajectory, the merger tilts the position favorably.

## Risk Assessment

**Merger execution — the dominant near-term risk.** The Corebridge combination requires shareholder votes at both companies, insurance and antitrust regulatory approvals, and satisfaction of closing conditions. The shareholder-base dynamics are complex: AIG remains a major Corebridge holder, and Nippon Life has signed a Voting and Support Agreement. A deal break, delay, or unfavorable regulatory condition removes the primary catalyst and would likely send EQH back toward standalone value, which is itself hard to pin given the GAAP noise. The split skewed toward Corebridge means EQH holders are accepting slightly minority control in the new entity; the exact exchange ratio, break fees, and governance terms will only be clear in the S-4/joint proxy.

**Shareholder litigation.** Kahn Swick & Foti announced a "merger investigation" in July 2026 — routine for large M&A, worth monitoring but not itself a thesis-mover.

**Market-sensitive earnings volatility.** Variable-annuity guarantees produce large GAAP swings with equity and rate moves and affect hedging costs and statutory capital. This is a permanent feature, largely priced, but it makes any single quarter hard to forecast, consistent with the four-quarter miss streak.

**Reinsurance counterparty and structure risk.** Cessions via a Bermuda affiliate and third parties optimize capital but add counterparty and structural complexity, and drove much of the FY2025 GAAP loss.

**AB and channel concentration.** A large share of fee earnings flows through AB (market- and flow-sensitive), and the book is concentrated in annuities and the 403(b)/educator market, exposing it to fiduciary-rule changes.

We score risk factors below neutral because the outcome is genuinely binary and not fully priced: the market is neither pricing a clean close (stock below low target) nor an outright break, and the pro-forma economics remain undefined.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 40.3% is rich to 1-year historical volatility of 32.5% (a 7.7-point spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (40.3% near-term versus 36.1% at six months), signaling the market is pricing imminent stress or event risk consistent with a pending merger vote/regulatory milestones. Put/call skew of 16.6% reflects elevated put demand and a defensive tilt. ATM IV has collapsed from 66.1% on June 4 to 40.3% now while the stock rose 19.3%, and the prior extreme put/call OI ratio of 5.77 has normalized: the acute hedging pressure of early June has eased even as the deal remains open. Short interest is negligible (0.0% of float, 35,374 shares), so there is no squeeze dynamic and no crowded bearish positioning. The options signal reinforces the view that this is an event-driven name where near-term realized moves may exceed the fundamentals.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$60–68):** The S-4 confirms an exchange ratio and synergy targets that are accretive to pro-forma operating EPS, regulatory approvals proceed, and the deal closes on schedule. The combined entity becomes the #1 U.S. annuity seller with a diversified fee-plus-spread earnings base, re-rating toward the analyst mean/high targets of $60.82/$68.00. Operating EPS of ~$8.92 in FY27 on a modestly re-rated ~7–8x operating multiple supports the low-$60s to high-$60s, closing the gap between the current $48 and the entire analyst target range.

**Bear Case (~$40 or below):** The merger breaks or is delayed by regulatory conditions or a shareholder-base dispute, removing the primary catalyst. The stock reverts to a standalone value burdened by noisy GAAP, the four-quarter miss streak, uncertain capital-return cadence, and market-sensitive earnings. A downside toward the February–March 2026 lows of $35–$40 (roughly -17% to -27% from $48) is plausible in a break scenario, especially if it coincides with an equity-market drawdown that pressures AB AUM and annuity fee bases.

**Base Case (~$52):** The deal progresses but the exact accretion math and timeline keep a partial uncertainty discount in place. The stock grinds toward the low end of the target range as the S-4 is filed and the votes approach, capturing part of the spread to the $50–$60 target band without fully closing it until certainty of close improves.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an uncertain rationale — the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction until the merger's economics are quantified.

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) A filed S-4/joint proxy showing a defined exchange ratio with credible synergy targets that are accretive to pro-forma operating EPS; (2) regulatory clarity or approvals that push implied closing probability meaningfully higher; (3) a pullback toward the low-$40s on unchanged deal fundamentals, which would restore asymmetric spread-to-target upside.
- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) Deal termination or a regulatory condition that materially degrades pro-forma economics; (2) an exchange ratio and synergy disclosure that imply dilution to EQH holders' per-share operating earnings; (3) a resumption of widening earnings misses (a quarter missing by >5%) alongside deteriorating statutory capital or AB net outflows.
- **Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the S-4/joint proxy filing, the single most important document to read next, containing the exact exchange ratio, pro-forma financials, synergy targets, break fees, and fairness opinions. The next quarterly print and shareholder-vote scheduling are secondary checkpoints. Given the March 2026 announcement, a 6–12 month window to closing (or break) is the relevant horizon.

## Conclusion

EQH is a merger-driven special situation. The standalone franchise is sounder than the GAAP loss and negative book value imply, and on operating earnings the stock is cheap, below the sector peer median and the entire analyst target range. Against that, the four-quarter miss streak, a degrading recommendation trend, elevated near-term put demand and backwardated volatility, and the unresolved exchange ratio, synergy targets, and regulatory path leave the outcome genuinely two-sided.

We rate EQH **HOLD (uncertain)**. The discount to targets and the strategic logic of combining the #3 and #4 U.S. annuity sellers argue against avoiding; the binary deal risk and undefined pro-forma economics argue against buying. The right move is to wait for the S-4 to quantify the combination, then re-rate. The overall score of 5.8 sits just below the prior 5.9, reflecting continued deal uncertainty rather than fresh fundamental deterioration.
