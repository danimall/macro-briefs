## Executive Summary

The St. Joe Company controls approximately 165,000 acres of land in Northwest Florida with entitlements for over 170,000 residential units and 22 million square feet of commercial space. This represents one of the most concentrated and irreplaceable land positions in U.S. real estate. The company delivered 56% net income growth in 2025 ($115.6M) on 27% revenue growth ($513.2M), and its contracted homesite backlog surged from 1,074 units at end-2024 to 3,204 at Q1 2026, validating demand for its communities.

The fundamental quality of the business is high. Hospitality and leasing now contribute ~60% of quarterly revenue, providing recurring income. Debt is 80.8% fixed at a weighted average rate of 4.8% with 19.6-year average maturity. The PulteGroup partnership announced in March 2026 brings a top-3 national homebuilder into the ecosystem for the first time.

The core issue preventing a BUY is the intersection of valuation (33x trailing earnings, forward P/E above 500x as Margaritaville JV income decelerates), extreme geographic concentration with no diversification, and a macro backdrop where elevated long-term Treasury yields directly compress housing demand and mortgage affordability. The stock trades at $65, down 12% from its $73.54 high but still up 52% over the past year. At current levels, the market prices in substantial continued growth, leaving limited margin of safety against the identifiable risks.

## Company Overview

St. Joe operates exclusively in Northwest Florida across three segments: Residential (32% of 2025 revenue), Hospitality (43%), and Commercial/Leasing (23%). The company's strategy centers on developing its land bank into integrated communities where residential sales fund infrastructure, which drives hospitality and commercial revenue over decades.

The Bay-Walton Sector Plan provides entitlements for development that would take over 100 years to exhaust at current pace (~1,500 units/year). The company has identified 23,904 homesites in various development stages, with relationships spanning 18 homebuilders including the recent addition of PulteGroup.

The Latitude Margaritaville Watersound JV (55+ active adult community, ~3,700 homes planned, 2,190 completed) has been a significant earnings contributor but is now in a deceleration phase — Q1 2026 closings fell to 83 from 192 in the prior year period.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term drivers:**
- Contracted homesite backlog tripled from 1,074 (end-2024) to 3,204 (Q1 2026), representing ~$232M+ in expected revenue
- PulteGroup partnership for 2,653 homesites in Pigeon Creek (development beginning 2027)
- Hospitality growing organically: Q1 2026 hospitality revenue was a record $44.7M (+13% YoY)
- Commercial leasing at 96% occupancy with continued development

**Long-term runway:**
- 170,000+ entitled residential units at ~1,500/year pace = 100+ years of inventory
- Infrastructure improvements (airport, medical campus, commercial centers) creating positive feedback loops
- Florida net migration from high-cost states remains a structural tailwind

**Offsetting deceleration:**
- Latitude Margaritaville JV income declining: home sales fell from 659 (2024) to 527 (2025); Q1 2026 closings dropped to 83 from 192
- This JV contributed $32.2M equity income in 2025 — its decline is a quantifiable near-term headwind
- The forward P/E of 591x reflects this: consensus expects a material earnings reset in 2026 as JV income normalizes

The growth story is real but must now overcome a meaningful earnings headwind from the Margaritaville JV deceleration. The contracted backlog surge partially offsets this, but the transition will create earnings volatility over the next 2-3 quarters.

## Valuation Assessment

At $65 per share ($3.73B market cap):
- Trailing P/E: 33.7x on $2.00 EPS (FY2025)
- Forward P/E: 591x (reflecting expected earnings decline as JV income drops)
- Price/Book: ~3.1x (land carried at historical cost, well below replacement/entitlement value)
- Dividend yield: ~0.98%

**Peer comparison:** Real estate peer median P/E is 36.7x with a median yield of 4.0%. JOE trades at a modest discount on P/E but offers substantially less income (0.98% vs 4.0%). The comparison is imperfect — JOE is a developer, not a REIT — but it receives no valuation discount for its dramatically higher risk concentration.

**The NAV argument:** Bulls will argue the land bank is carried at historical cost and is worth multiples of book value when valued at entitlement-adjusted prices. This has merit — 165,000 acres of entitled Gulf Coast land is genuinely irreplaceable. However, this argument has been true for years and represents a permanent option value, not a near-term catalyst.

**The concern:** At 33x trailing earnings with the Margaritaville JV clearly decelerating, the stock needs the contracted backlog to convert rapidly into earnings to justify current levels. In an elevated long-term rate environment where mortgage rates remain high, the conversion rate and homebuilder demand for new homesites face headwinds. The market is pricing in a continued growth trajectory that requires favorable rate conditions the current macro regime does not provide.

## Competitive Landscape

St. Joe's competitive position is exceptional within its geography. The company holds an effective monopoly on large-scale entitled land development in Northwest Florida. No competitor can replicate the combination of:
- 165,000 contiguous acres in a coastal growth market
- Entitlements for 170,000+ units (took decades to secure)
- Integrated ecosystem capturing value across residential, hospitality, and commercial
- Exclusive membership clubs (3,594 Watersound Club members) creating community demand
- Relationships with 18 homebuilders plus PulteGroup's recent entry

The PulteGroup partnership validates the competitive moat — the third-largest U.S. homebuilder chose St. Joe for its first Northwest Florida entry, confirming the land position's attractiveness to sophisticated operators.

The vulnerability is concentration, not competition. If Northwest Florida loses attractiveness (insurance crisis, hurricane damage, migration reversal), the entire business is impaired with no fallback. This single-geography risk is amplified by the current Florida property insurance market stress documented in the 10-K.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Geographic concentration (Critical).** 100% of assets in three Northwest Florida counties. A major hurricane, insurance market collapse, or migration reversal would impair the entire business simultaneously. There is no diversification mechanism.

**2. Interest rate sensitivity.** With long-term rates elevated and mortgage rates remaining high, homesite demand faces structural headwinds. While St. Joe notes a high cash-buyer proportion, the builders purchasing homesites must ultimately sell to end buyers, many of whom require financing.

**3. Margaritaville JV deceleration.** Q1 2026 closings fell 57% YoY (83 vs 192). This JV has represented 20%+ of pre-tax income. The deceleration creates a near-term earnings cliff that the 591x forward P/E reflects.

**4. Fairholme selling.** Berkowitz sold ~$12.6M in shares (Jan-Mar 2026). While representing only ~1.1% of his 16M+ share position, systematic selling by a 33.8% holder creates persistent supply pressure. Any acceleration would significantly impact a stock with only ~$15M average daily dollar volume on ~229K shares/day.

**5. Florida insurance crisis.** The 10-K explicitly flags rising premiums, reduced coverage, and higher deductibles as deterrents to buyers. This is a slow-building structural headwind specific to JOE's geography.

**6. Tariff impact on construction costs.** Materials cost increases directly impact homebuilder margins, potentially reducing willingness to pay for homesites.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($80-85, +23-31%):** The contracted backlog converts into 2026-2027 revenue, driving earnings recovery even as Margaritaville decelerates. The PulteGroup partnership validates a new growth channel. Mortgage rates moderate as the Fed eventually eases, stimulating buyer demand. The land bank's irreplaceable value receives wider market recognition, attracting institutional sponsorship. Hospitality and leasing continue compounding at 8-13% annually.

**Bear Case ($42-48, -26-35%):** The elevated rate environment persists, mortgage rates stay high, and homebuilder demand for new homesites stalls. The Margaritaville JV deceleration deepens, taking 2026 EPS below $1.50. Fairholme accelerates selling. A major hurricane season damages infrastructure or sentiment. The stock re-rates to 25-30x a lower earnings base, approaching the $42.65 52-week low.

**Why the specific risk prevents BUY at $65:** The combination of (1) a 591x forward P/E reflecting genuine near-term earnings deceleration from the Margaritaville JV, (2) elevated long-term interest rates directly impairing mortgage affordability and thus homesite demand, and (3) extreme geographic concentration with active insurance market stress in Florida creates a risk/reward profile that is balanced, not asymmetric to the upside. The bull case requires both rate moderation and successful backlog conversion — either failing alone drops the stock 25-35%. At $50-52 (approximately 25-26x a normalized $2.00 EPS), the margin of safety would be sufficient for a BUY. At $65, the market has already priced in the PulteGroup partnership, the backlog surge, and continued Florida migration.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock pulls back to $50-52 range (approximately 25-26x normalized EPS), providing 15-20% margin of safety
- Long-term Treasury yields decline materially, improving mortgage affordability outlook
- Q2-Q3 2026 earnings demonstrate homesite revenue offsetting Margaritaville JV deceleration with EPS tracking above $1.80 annualized

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Fairholme selling accelerates (>500K shares in a quarter)
- Contracted homesite backlog declines sequentially, indicating demand softening
- A major hurricane causes material property damage in Bay/Walton/Gulf counties
- Florida insurance market deterioration forces builders to abandon communities

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July 2026) will provide first full quarter of backlog conversion data post-PulteGroup announcement and continued Margaritaville JV trajectory.

## Conclusion

St. Joe is a high-quality business with a genuinely irreplaceable asset and an operating model that is successfully transitioning toward recurring revenue. The contracted homesite backlog tripling over six months and PulteGroup's entry confirm structural demand for the communities being built.

The stock is a HOLD at $65 because the current price assumes favorable conditions (rate moderation, continued migration, successful backlog conversion) that the macro environment does not guarantee. Elevated long-term rates directly impair housing demand, the Margaritaville JV is creating a near-term earnings air pocket, and geographic concentration offers no margin for error. Investors who own JOE should continue holding for the long-duration land bank optionality, but new capital should await either a price correction to the low-$50s or evidence that the earnings transition is succeeding in the next 2-3 quarterly reports.
