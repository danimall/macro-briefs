## Executive Summary

Amentum is an 18-month-old public company assembled from Jacobs' Critical Mission Solutions segment and Legacy Amentum via a Reverse Morris Trust, carrying $3.99B of gross debt, $7.47B of goodwill and intangibles, and roughly negative $2.9B of tangible book value. On guided FY2026 numbers it is inexpensive: 10.4x the $2.35 midpoint of adjusted diluted EPS guidance, 8.5x EV/EBITDA on the $1.12B midpoint, and an 8.8-9.6% free-cash-flow yield. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $2.78 puts the stock at 8.8x, and the analyst mean target of $31.75 sits 30% above spot. Backlog is at a record $47.8B (+7% year over year), funded backlog recovered 19.6% to $6.9B after the shutdown-distorted fiscal year-end print, and book-to-bill was 1.2x in the quarter and trailing twelve months.

The offsetting evidence is specific rather than atmospheric. Reported revenue fell 2.8% in the first half of FY2026 and, adjusting for the 53rd week in FY2025, guidance implies something between -1.2% and +1.3% on a comparable-week basis. Global Engineering Solutions revenue, 61% of the total, declined 9%; international declined 17.4%; "other U.S. government agencies" declined 12.6%. Net leverage was essentially unchanged across the first half (3.23x to 3.20x) despite deleveraging being both the stated capital-allocation priority and a compensation metric, because FY2025's $722M of voluntary prepayments were substantially funded by the $360M Rapid Solutions divestiture rather than by recurring cash flow. Excluding the $71M net inflow from the MUFG receivables-purchase program, first-half operating cash flow was roughly $18M versus roughly $124M a year earlier. The full-year FCF guide of $525-575M therefore requires approximately $447-497M in the back half against $78M in the front half — about 30% better than FY2025's implied $361M back half. The six-week government shutdown that ran October 2 to November 12, 2025, entirely inside Q1, is a sufficient explanation, and Q2 free cash flow of $220M on recovered collections supports the timing read. It is not yet verified.

That verification arrives on August 11, four days after this report's information cutoff, with the options market pricing a ±10.3% move and near-dated implied volatility 20.7 points above one-year realized. The stock has already run 14% from $21.48 on July 20 to $24.485 with no new fundamental disclosure in between. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on AMTM, and the bar for repeating it should be higher — but the honest position is that the cheapness is real, the cash-conversion claim underpinning it is unproven, and the entry that made a constructive stance defensible in mid-July has partly closed. Base case $28, bull $34.50, bear $19.

## Company Overview

Amentum is a government services and engineering contractor headquartered in Chantilly, Virginia, with roughly 50,000 employees in more than 70 countries and 81% of FY2025 revenue from the U.S. federal government. It operates two segments:

- **Global Engineering Solutions (GES)** — FY2025 revenue $8,850M (61%), segment adjusted EBITDA margin 7.5%. Nuclear decommissioning and environmental remediation (Sellafield, Savannah River, Hanford, Oak Ridge, Portsmouth, Idaho), base and facilities operations, energy infrastructure, and allied defense support (UK MoD, UK NDA, Australian DoD, AUKUS-adjacent work).
- **Digital Solutions (DS)** — FY2025 revenue $5,543M (39%), 7.9% margin. Intelligence community mission support, C5ISR, cyber, space systems (NASA COMET, JETS II), and what management now labels "critical digital infrastructure," including services to hyperscalers retrofitting legacy data centers and to telecom operators on capacity engineering and fiber/wireless deployment. Amentum is the paid service provider in those relationships; it does not own or build the facilities.

The business is asset-light in the extreme: FY2025 capex of $27M was 0.19% of revenue, and first-half FY2026 capex was $11M. Contract mix has shifted from 63% cost-plus / 24% fixed-price in FY2025 to 57% / 29% in the first half of FY2026 — higher potential margin and higher overrun exposure.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Q2 FY2026 revenue was $3,478M versus $3,491M, down 0.4%; the six-month figure was $6,715M versus $6,907M, down 2.8%. DS grew 10% in the quarter and 7% in the half; GES fell 7% and 9%. Management characterizes Q2 as roughly 3% "normalized growth," and the 10-Q identifies the reconciling items: contracts transitioning from consolidated to unconsolidated joint ventures (consolidated JV revenue fell from $743M to $521M, a $222M drag that now flows through the ~$24M/quarter equity-earnings line), the Rapid Solutions divestiture, the shutdown, and legacy program ramp-downs. Adjusting FY2025's 53 weeks to a 52-week equivalent of roughly $14,122M, FY2026 guidance of $13.95-14.30B implies -1.2% to +1.3%. The defensible conclusion is a flat top line, with real declines concentrated in international (-17.4%) and non-DoD federal civilian (-12.6%) work.

**Margins.** Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 34bp to 8.01% in the half. The expansion is asymmetric: GES margin rose 100bp to 8.46% on mix, fixed-price content, execution and JV performance, while DS margin fell to 7.15% from 7.99% on the Rapid Solutions sale, new program start timing, and a tougher prior-year write-up comparison. Selling a hardware business to fund deleveraging structurally lowered DS margin — a real trade-off, not an accounting artifact.

**Earnings quality.** Two items dominate. First, net favorable estimate-at-completion adjustments contributed $0.15 of the $0.40 of first-half GAAP diluted EPS, roughly 38%. The net figure is stable year over year ($45M vs $28M), but gross swings nearly doubled on both sides ($119M favorable / $74M unfavorable versus $68M / $40M), consistent with both the fixed-price shift and simply a larger consolidated contract book. Treat net EAC as a recurring, volatile input. Second, purchase-accounting amortization was $479M in FY2025 against $480M of operating income, falling to $94M in Q2 from $120M as the acquired backlog intangible exhausts ($61M net of $661M gross); customer relationships remain $1,694M net and amortize for years. Derived TTM GAAP diluted EPS is approximately $0.60, so the stock is ~41x trailing GAAP against ~10.4x guided adjusted. Note the data feed reports trailing and forward P/E identically at 39.8 alongside EPS of $0.27 — internally inconsistent; the 39.8 corresponds to TTM GAAP EPS near $0.615, and $0.27 is the FY2025 annual figure. Neither multiple is "the" P/E: roughly $1.50-1.70 per share of the gap is non-cash amortization that declines over time, plus integration costs that should end, offset partly by stock compensation ($15M) and integration spend ($27M) that are partly cash.

**Cash flow.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $543M with $27M capex, a $516M FCF and 3.6% FCF margin — respectable for a cost-plus-weighted services business. First-half FY2026 delivered $89M and $78M respectively, of which $71M of the operating figure came from the net increase in receivables sold under the $400M MARPA facility (versus $43M a year earlier). Ex-facility, underlying first-half operating cash generation was roughly $18M against roughly $124M — a $106M deterioration, wider than the $78M headline decline, driven by payables/contract liabilities at -$250M and accrued compensation at -$88M. In fairness, the FY2025 full-year figure was not meaningfully facility-inflated (the sold balance moved from ~$177M to $180M), Q2 FCF was $220M on recovered collections, and the implied Q1 figure of roughly -$142M lines up precisely with a six-week shutdown inside that quarter. The facility is a disclosed, non-recourse working-capital tool, and drawing on it during an appropriations lapse is what it exists for. The issue is dependency and back-half concentration, not impropriety.

**Balance sheet.** At April 3, 2026: cash $428M, gross debt $3,988M, net debt $3,560M, equity $4,609M against $7,467M of goodwill plus intangibles (tangible book roughly -$2.86B). Goodwill is ~49.8% of assets and is itself a named risk factor; a $186M impairment was taken in FY2023. Current ratio improved to 1.48. The April 24, 2026 credit amendment created a $1,400M Term Loan A due April 2031 and re-tranched the existing loan into a $1,591M Term Loan B due September 2031 — $2,991M against the $2,981M it replaced, a repricing and maturity extension rather than incremental borrowing — and lifted the revolver to $1,000M (undrawn, $769M available after LCs). Management attributes roughly 50bp of weighted-average cost-of-debt reduction to this plus a December 2025 Moody's upgrade, worth about $15M pre-tax or ~$0.05 of EPS after tax on ~$3.0B of term debt. Both the upgrade and the 50bp figure come from the earnings call rather than a filing.

Derived net leverage is 3.20x on $1,112M TTM adjusted EBITDA, versus 3.23x at fiscal year-end. Term loan repayments in the half were $19M. GAAP interest coverage improved to 1.97x from 1.36x; on EBITDA against roughly $294M annualized interest it is ~3.8x. The FY2025 effective tax rate of 48.7% — inflated by a valuation allowance on interest disallowed under Section 163(j) — normalized to 31.0% in the half, and should ease further as debt falls. The Altman Z-Score of 2.42 sits in the grey zone, but the metric penalizes an asset-light services roll-up with acquisition goodwill and a $363M accumulated deficit; read it as confirming that leverage and thin GAAP returns are the binding constraints rather than as a distress signal.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus has FY2026 EPS at $2.42 and FY2027 at $2.78, implying about 15% earnings growth on a roughly flat top line. That growth is mechanical rather than commercial: declining purchase amortization, a lower effective tax rate, ~50bp of cost-of-debt savings, deleveraging, and synergy capture. It is real earnings growth, and much of it is high-confidence. It is not evidence of an expanding franchise.

Estimate revisions are effectively flat — current quarter +0.4%, next quarter -1.3%, current year -0.1%, next year +0.0%, with no usable 90-day baseline. The provided trend label reads "broadly falling"; at ±1% magnitudes, "unchanged" is the accurate characterization. There is no inflection signal here in either direction, and none of the estimate-revision thresholds that would justify a more aggressive stance are present. The company has beaten adjusted EPS four consecutive quarters by 2.1-4.4% — a modest, consistent pattern that supports confidence in the guidance range without implying upside surprise.

Forward growth vectors, ranked by credibility:

1. **Nuclear and environmental.** Multi-decade decommissioning backlogs at Sellafield, Savannah River, Hanford, Oak Ridge, Portsmouth and Idaho, plus a 14-year, ~$406M Great British Nuclear SMR support award through an Amentum-led JV. The competitor list here is short (AtkinsRéalis, Bechtel, BWXT, Honeywell, HII, Fluor, Westinghouse), which is genuinely different from the fragmented IT-services bidder set. Management expects projects to move from design toward construction in late 2026 and 2027.
2. **Defense and intelligence.** GFY2027 requests defense discretionary up $250B to $1.15T; including pending reconciliation the company cites $1.5T total, up 43%. DS grew 10% in Q2. But DoW plus IC revenue was only +1.1% in the half, meaning Amentum is not currently capturing share proportional to budget growth.
3. **Critical digital infrastructure.** Over $600M of Q2 awards tied to hyperscaler data-center retrofits and telecom deployment. The COO frames this as a decade-old capability; the award scale looks new. This is a credible adjacency that maps onto confirmed AI-infrastructure spending, though at services margins.

Working against these: NASA has issued a workforce directive to incrementally insource expertise, which management quantified as immaterial to FY2026 but roughly 1% of revenue (~$140M) in FY2027. The GFY2027 request cuts non-defense discretionary by $25B, and it is precisely the "other U.S. government agencies" line that fell 12.6% in the half. Roughly a quarter of federal revenue sits in the squeezed portion of the budget. Some DOE environmental management is funded through defense-related accounts and may be insulated, but that cannot be resolved from the filings.

Finally, backlog translation is deteriorating in quality. Marquee wins are increasingly JV-structured — NASA COSMOS went to ASCEND Aerospace & Technology (Amentum plus an SDVOSB partner under the SBA Mentor-Protégé Program, value undisclosed); the GBN work went to an Amentum-led JV; roughly 30 unconsolidated JV VIEs are held at 25-50% with $216M carrying value. JVs are a legitimate access tool and can improve margin percentages, but they convert consolidated revenue into equity-method earnings, which is a partial explanation for record backlog alongside declining reported revenue. Announced contract values are a poor proxy for Amentum revenue.

The peer table's median P/E of 21.9 is not a usable benchmark: UNF (uniform rental), OPLN (auto auctions) and CXW (corrections) share a GICS bucket but not an end market. The relevant comparables are SAIC at 12.9x and KBR at 10.4x, both of which have also derated (KBR sits 29% below its 52-week high). AMTM at 10.0-10.9x guided adjusted EPS is in line with that cohort, not at a conspicuous discount to it. The stock is 36% below its February high, worse than either peer, and the research work could not identify a company-specific negative catalyst between the May 11 guidance reaffirmation and the mid-July trough at $19.80.

That gap deserves a specific answer rather than a shrug, because a 36% drawdown means informed holders sold. The most defensible explanation is a sector-level rerating of federal services driven by procurement reform — fixed-price and outcomes-based contracting mandates plus a proposed non-defense discretionary cut — which lands disproportionately on civilian, DOE and environmental franchises. GES is 61% of Amentum's revenue and sits squarely in that category, and its fixed-price mix has already risen from 24% to 29%. On that reading the compression is partly deserved, and the cheapness is a warning about earnings composition rather than a clean mispricing.

The bull-side answer is arithmetic. If FY2026 FCF lands at the $550M midpoint and leverage reaches 3.0x, net debt falls to roughly $3.36B by fiscal year-end. Nine times EBITDA of $1.12B less that net debt equals $6.72B of equity, or $27.50 per share; 9.5x gets to $29.80. Ten times FY2027 consensus EPS is $27.80; eleven times is $30.60. An 8% FCF yield on $550M is $28.15. Those converge on a base case of roughly $28, about 14% above spot. Analyst mean and median targets ($31.75 and $30.50) sit above that, and the range is wide ($22 to $40) — appropriate for a company with two quarters of clean comparable history. At an 8.8x forward multiple with flat estimates, I treat the mean target as a plausible upper-middle outcome rather than as independent confirmation.

Downside: 7.5x EV/EBITDA on the low guidance end less unchanged net debt is $19.20; 7x FY2027 EPS is $19.46. Bear case $19, essentially the June low.

The valuation score of 5.6 reflects genuine absolute cheapness on adjusted metrics and a specific catalyst set, discounted for the fact that GAAP earnings are minimal, 38% of first-half GAAP EPS came from EAC adjustments, the free-cash-flow number underpinning the yield is unverified, and the discount is shared by the peer group. It is one tick below the prior assessment purely because 14% of the margin of safety has been consumed since July 20 with no new information.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K names Booz Allen, CACI, KBR, Leidos, ManTech, Parsons, Peraton, SAIC and V2X as direct peers, plus the primes competing for services scope, plus Babcock, BAE, Mott MacDonald, Serco and Mace in the UK. The company claims no technology moat. What it claims — scale in nuclear and environmental remediation, cleared personnel, and site incumbency at long-lived installations — is real but narrow, and 7.9% adjusted EBITDA margins on 57% cost-plus revenue describe a scale-and-execution business with limited pricing power.

Trajectory is mixed. Positive: book-to-bill 1.2x, funded backlog +19.6%, more than $20B of first-half proposal submissions against a $35B annual target with $26B awaiting award (~65% new business), DS growing 10%, and GES margin up 100bp. Negative: total revenue is flat-to-down while the addressable defense budget expands, which is share loss in the growing half of the market; international revenue fell 17.4%; commercial and international customer revenue grew only 1.4%, so the rise in commercial share from 19.2% to 20.0% is arithmetic off a shrinking denominator rather than diversification; and NASA's insourcing directive is a direct demonstration that in this model the customer can elect to do the work itself.

The one place where competitive position is clearly improving is nuclear, where the bidder set is short, the work is politically durable, and Amentum holds incumbency at most major U.S. and UK sites. That supports a 5.0 rather than something lower.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and balance-sheet composition.** $3.99B gross debt against equity that is entirely goodwill and intangibles. A goodwill impairment would be non-cash but would compress equity and could pressure covenant headroom. Leverage did not improve in the first half.

**Back-half cash-flow concentration.** The $525-575M guide requires roughly $447-497M in the back half, about 30% better than FY2025's back half, against $78M delivered and roughly $18M ex-receivables-facility. This is the single most testable claim in the guidance and the primary reason risk scores low.

**Interest-rate hedge cliff.** $1.5B notional of floating-to-fixed swaps matures through January 31, 2027. Absent re-hedging, roughly half the term-loan balance becomes unhedged floating early in FY2027; the 10-Q's own sensitivity of ~$15M per 100bp per semiannual period implies ~$30M annualized, or about $0.09 of adjusted EPS — more than offsetting the ~$0.05 benefit from the April repricing. Management will likely re-hedge; this is an exposure to size, not a forecast loss.

**Appropriations exposure.** Three funding lapses in nine months: the full shutdown October 2 to November 12, 2025; a partial lapse January 31 to February 3, 2026; and a DHS shutdown February 14 to April 30, 2026 spanning all of Q2 and roughly four weeks of Q3. DHS is a named customer. Q3 carries a modest known headwind from this.

**Fixed-price mix and EAC volatility.** Fixed-price rose from 24% to 29% of revenue while gross EAC adjustments nearly doubled in both directions. Procurement reform pushes further in this direction. A single program problem shows up here.

**Concentration.** 81% U.S. federal, with no offsetting diversification trend. Roughly a quarter of federal revenue in the budget category proposed for reduction.

**JV opacity.** Roughly 30 unconsolidated JVs, ownership splits and revenue shares undisclosed on marquee awards, equity earnings of $62M in FY2025 (5.6% of segment adjusted EBITDA).

**Governance items.** CEO FY2025 total compensation of $21.2M (compensation actually paid $30.9M) against $66M of GAAP net income is a large ratio, most plausibly driven by founding equity grants around the September 2024 separation; the next proxy will show whether it normalizes. The DSO compensation metric may be partially self-satisfying if measured after receivables sales. Offsetting these: performance measures are adjusted EBITDA, DSO, free cash flow and net debt reduction — three of four cash- and balance-sheet-oriented, which is close to the right scorecard for a levered post-spin issuer. The 8-K record since inception shows no restatements, no material weaknesses, no auditor changes and no litigation-triggered filings, and controls were reported effective through an 18-month two-company integration. Form 4 activity from November 2025 through May 2026 contains no open-market purchases and no open-market sales — an absence of signal, consistent with the Jacobs tax-matters restrictions and a mostly unvested equity base.

**Capital-allocation constraint turning into optionality.** The Jacobs tax matters agreement restricts buybacks, mergers, large asset sales and 50%-plus change of control for two years post-distribution, i.e. until approximately September 27, 2026 — coinciding with fiscal year-end and the sub-3.0x leverage target. The CFO listed capital returns among future deployment options in generic terms. The option set widens; intent is not established.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 65.2% against 44.6% one-year realized is rich by 20.7 points, sits in the 80th percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is backwardated (65.2% at 13 days versus 54.4% at six months). The August 21 expiry prices a ±10.3% move, straddling the August 11 Q3 report. One-month put/call skew of 9.3% is characterized as elevated put demand, flattening to 2.9% at three months and inverting at six. Positioning composition moved the other way during the rally: put/call open interest fell from 1.15 on July 20 to 0.67 now, and near-term put/call volume is 0.10, both call-tilted. Short interest is 8.8% of float at 6.5 days to cover — moderate.

The coherent read: the market prices a large, genuinely two-sided event around the print, with short-dated protection being bought while overall open-interest composition has shifted toward upside participation during the 14% move off the low. This is a material change from the sharply put-heavy structure observed in mid-June. It does not confirm direction, and the rich near-term volatility argues against paying up immediately ahead of the event. For an existing holder it makes near-dated protection expensive and covered-call writing comparatively attractive; for a prospective buyer it argues for waiting four days.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($34.50, +41%).** Q3 free cash flow confirms the back-half ramp, FY2026 lands at or above the $550M FCF midpoint, net leverage prints below 3.0x at fiscal year-end, and the Jacobs restriction lapses into a buyback authorization at a 9%-plus free-cash-flow yield. GES margin expansion proves durable at 8.5%-plus, DS keeps growing high single digits on intelligence and critical digital infrastructure awards, nuclear projects move into construction in FY2027, and amortization step-down plus a normalized tax rate converge GAAP toward adjusted EPS. On FY2027 EPS approaching $2.90-3.00 and a 12x multiple — still a discount to broader services — the equity is worth $34-36. Several of the analyst targets stretch to $40 on this path.

**Bear case ($19, -22%).** Second-half cash conversion disappoints, full-year FCF lands nearer $450-475M, leverage stalls above 3.1x, and the receivables facility is revealed as a recurring rather than episodic support. Non-defense discretionary cuts flow through to DOE environmental management and NASA in GFY2027, compounding the quantified 1% NASA insourcing headwind; GES revenue declines accelerate; the fixed-price shift produces an unfavorable EAC swing that removes the EAC contribution currently supporting 38% of GAAP EPS. Unhedged floating exposure from January 2027 adds ~$0.09 of EPS drag. At 7-7.5x EBITDA and 7x FY2027 EPS the stock revisits the June low. A goodwill impairment would not be a cash event but would compress equity and headline optics.

**Base case ($28, +14%).** Revenue is flat, adjusted EBITDA lands near $1.12B, FCF lands in the lower half of guidance around $500-540M, leverage reaches roughly 3.0-3.05x, and the multiple re-rates modestly toward 10x FY2027 EPS as GAAP earnings converge and buyback capacity becomes available. Probability-weighting 50% base, 20% bull, 30% bear gives roughly +9% expected return — positive, but not enough asymmetry to justify buying into a print priced for a ±10% move.

**On the score trajectory.** Scores moved 5.0 (March) → 6.1 (May) → 5.7 (June) → 5.4 (July) → 5.16 now, with valuation up 2.3 points and financial health up 1.2 across the sequence while growth prospects fell 1.2. That divergence is the story: the balance sheet and the multiple improved (maturity extension to 2031/2032, 50bp cost-of-debt reduction, tax normalization, price weakness), while the operating franchise flattened (revenue down 2.8%, GES down 9%, NASA insourcing quantified, JV structures diluting backlog read-through). This report's marginal change is not a fundamental downgrade — nothing has deteriorated since May 11 — it is the arithmetic consequence of a 14% higher price against unchanged information, plus a lower risk score reflecting an imminent binary checkpoint. A fifth consecutive HOLD is warranted only because the decisive evidence is four days away; issuing a stronger call in either direction now would be positioning ahead of data rather than on it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment date: August 11, 2026** (Q3 FY2026, quarter ended July 3). Consensus is $0.61 versus $0.56 a year ago, +8.9%. The line that matters is not EPS — four consecutive 2-4% beats make a small beat the likely outcome — but year-to-date free cash flow and the net leverage ratio.

**Targets from the $24.485 basis:**
- Base case **$28** (+14%): 10x FY2027 consensus $2.78, cross-checked at 9x FY2026E EBITDA of $1.12B less ~$3.36B year-end net debt, and an 8% yield on $550M of FCF. Timeframe 12 months.
- Bull case **$34.50** (+41%): 12x FY2027 EPS approaching $2.90 with a buyback authorized and leverage below 3.0x.
- Bear case **$19** (-22%): 7x FY2027 EPS / 7.5x low-end EBITDA on an FCF miss and accelerating GES decline.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE if any two of:**
1. Nine-month FY2026 free cash flow of $300M or more (implying the back-half ramp is on track for the guided range) with the outstanding receivables-sold balance flat or lower versus the $251M at April 3.
2. Net leverage at or below 3.05x at the Q3 report, with term-loan repayments in the quarter materially above the $19M first-half pace.
3. A buyback authorization or leverage target reaffirmed below 3.0x once the Jacobs restriction lapses after September 27, 2026, at a share price implying an 8%-plus FCF yield.
4. Price weakness to $21.00-21.50 with guidance intact — restoring roughly 9x guided FY2026 EPS and a 10%-plus FCF yield.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of:**
1. FY2026 free cash flow guidance is cut, or nine-month FCF is below $200M with no credible bridge to the range.
2. Net leverage rises above 3.25x, or the sub-3.0x fiscal year-end target is withdrawn.
3. Net unfavorable EAC adjustments in any quarter, or gross unfavorable EAC above $100M in a quarter, indicating fixed-price execution problems.
4. FY2027 revenue guidance implying a decline worse than 2% ex-JV effects, or a quantified non-defense appropriations impact above the ~1% NASA figure already disclosed.

**Thesis invalidation for owners:** a goodwill impairment charge, a covenant amendment sought for headroom rather than pricing, or evidence that the receivables facility is funding rather than smoothing operating cash flow (rising sold balance alongside falling ex-facility operating cash flow for two consecutive quarters).

## Conclusion

Amentum is cheap on adjusted numbers — 10.4x guided FY2026 EPS, 8.8x FY2027 consensus, 8.5x EV/EBITDA, an 8.8-9.6% guided free-cash-flow yield — with a record $47.8B backlog, 1.2x book-to-bill, extended maturities, a 50bp lower cost of debt, and a dated capital-allocation catalyst when the Jacobs tax restriction lapses around September 27, 2026. It is also a flat-revenue business with $3.99B of debt against negative tangible book, 38% of first-half GAAP EPS from estimate-at-completion adjustments, leverage that did not fall in the first half, first-half operating cash flow of roughly $18M excluding receivables sales, and 61% of revenue in the segment most exposed to proposed non-defense discretionary cuts and to fixed-price procurement reform.

The full-year free-cash-flow guide requires a back half roughly 30% better than FY2025's. That claim is testable on August 11, four days out, with the options market pricing a ±10.3% move and near-dated volatility 20.7 points above realized. HOLD is the correct call: fair-to-modestly-positive expected return from here (+9% probability-weighted), a base case 14% above spot against a bear case 22% below, and the decisive evidence not yet in hand. Existing holders should stay; new capital should either wait for the print or for a return toward $21.00-21.50, where the same fundamentals offer roughly 9x earnings and a double-digit free-cash-flow yield.
