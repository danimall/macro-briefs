## Executive Summary

Uber Technologies trades at $70.48, near its 52-week low of $68.34, representing a ~31% drawdown from the September 2025 high of $101.99. The stock is priced at approximately 15x forward earnings on a business that generated $9.8B in free cash flow in FY 2025 (+42% YoY), grew revenue 18% to $52B, and nearly doubled operating income to $5.6B. The peer median P/E in Industrials is 28.3x, placing Uber at a 47% discount despite superior growth characteristics.

The prior BUY recommendation at ~$75 has resulted in a ~6.6% loss. The decline is attributable to broad market weakness, consumer sentiment deterioration (Michigan survey at record lows), and elevated energy costs compressing discretionary spending expectations. Uber's fundamentals have not deteriorated. Estimates are flat over the past 30 days (after a modest 6-7% downward revision over 90 days reflecting macro caution), the new CFO made a $1.6M open-market purchase at $71.25, and the Rivian robotaxi partnership ($1.25B commitment) represents the largest AV de-risking event in the company's history. The thesis is intact at a lower price.

Key risks remain substantial: driver classification litigation across multiple jurisdictions, a $1.8B UK VAT dispute, autonomous vehicle disruption from Waymo operating its own platform, and macro sensitivity to consumer spending patterns. These risks are real but are increasingly reflected in a valuation that prices in minimal growth premium relative to the actual trajectory of the business.

## Company Overview

Uber operates a three-segment technology platform across 15,000+ cities in 70+ countries with 202 million monthly active platform consumers:

- **Mobility** (57% of revenue, $29.7B): Ridesharing, car rentals, transit partnerships. Q4 Gross Bookings of $27.4B. 15% of bookings from airport trips.
- **Delivery** (33% of revenue, $17.2B): Uber Eats meal, grocery, and retail delivery. Revenue grew 25% YoY, outpacing Mobility's 18%. Adjusted EBITDA grew 45% to $3.6B, reflecting rapid margin expansion. Advertising revenue within Delivery grew $568M YoY.
- **Freight** (10% of revenue, $5.1B): Digital freight brokerage. Revenue declined 1% YoY but improved toward breakeven (-$33M Adjusted EBITDA vs. -$74M prior year).

The revenue model is primarily agent-based: Uber takes a percentage of Gross Bookings ($193.5B in FY 2025) as its revenue ($52B), yielding a ~27% revenue margin on bookings. This structure means reported revenue significantly understates the transaction volume flowing through the platform.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Profitability

Uber's revenue growth has held at 17-18% for three consecutive years, driven by 20% trip volume growth and 18% growth in monthly active consumers. The profitability transformation is the central financial narrative:

| Metric | FY 2023 | FY 2024 | FY 2025 |
|--------|---------|---------|---------|
| Revenue ($B) | $37.3 | $44.0 | $52.0 |
| Operating Income ($B) | $1.1 | $2.8 | $5.6 |
| Operating Margin | 3.0% | 6.4% | 10.7% |
| Adjusted EBITDA ($B) | — | $6.5 | $8.7 |

Margin expansion was driven across every cost line: cost of revenue improved from 61% to 60%, operations and support from 6% to 5%, sales and marketing from 10% to 9%, and G&A from 8% to 6% (partially aided by a $549M decline in legal accruals). R&D held flat at 7% of revenue.

Net income of $10.1B in FY 2025 is misleading. It includes a $5.0B non-cash tax benefit from the release of a Netherlands deferred tax asset valuation allowance. Pre-tax income was $5.8B, up from $4.1B in FY 2024. Stripping out both years' DTA releases, normalized earnings power is approximately $4.5-5.0B pre-tax, or roughly $3.5-4.0B after-tax at a normalized rate.

### Cash Flow

Free cash flow is the most important financial metric for Uber and the strongest signal of operational quality:

| Metric | FY 2024 | FY 2025 | Growth |
|--------|---------|---------|--------|
| Operating Cash Flow | $7.1B | $10.1B | +42% |
| Capital Expenditures | ($242M) | ($336M) | — |
| Free Cash Flow | $6.9B | $9.8B | +42% |
| FCF Margin | 15.7% | 18.8% | — |

Capex of $336M on $52B of revenue (0.6% of revenue) confirms the capital-light nature of the platform. FCF of $9.8B substantially exceeds normalized net income, confirming high earnings quality. At the current market cap of ~$143B (using $70.48 price × 2.037B shares), the FCF yield is approximately 6.8%.

### Balance Sheet

Cash and equivalents of $7.1B against $10.5B in long-term debt yields net debt of ~$3.4B — manageable given FCF of $9.8B (net debt / FCF < 0.4x). Uber refinanced $2.4B of debt in 2025 while issuing $3.4B at longer maturities, reducing interest expense 16% to $440M. The $2.0B commercial paper program provides additional liquidity flexibility.

The balance sheet carries $8.9B in goodwill from prior acquisitions (Postmates, Transplace, Careem). Stockholders' equity of $27.0B includes a ($10.6B) accumulated deficit reflecting the years of heavy losses before profitability.

### Capital Return

Uber authorized $27B in total share repurchases ($7B in February 2024, $20B in July 2025) and spent $6.5B on buybacks in FY 2025. $19.2B remains authorized. At current prices, this represents approximately 13% of the current market cap.

## Growth Analysis

### Core Business Momentum

Trip growth of 20% YoY and MAPC growth of 18% YoY demonstrate that Uber's growth is volume-driven, which is more sustainable than price-driven growth. Delivery's 25% revenue growth and 45% Adjusted EBITDA growth represent the highest-quality segment trajectory, combining rapid top-line expansion with accelerating margins. Advertising revenue (grew $568M YoY) is a high-margin incremental revenue stream embedded within both Mobility and Delivery.

### Autonomous Vehicle Optionality

Uber's multi-partner AV strategy is the key long-term growth (and risk mitigation) variable:

- **Rivian:** $1.25B investment for up to 50,000 R2 robotaxis, launching San Francisco and Miami in 2028, scaling to 25 cities by 2031
- **Waymo:** Already operating on Uber's platform
- **Zoox (Amazon):** Robotaxi rides in Las Vegas and LA by mid-2027
- **Lucid/Nuro:** Test rides of Level 4 Lucid Gravity robotaxis began April 2026
- **VW:** Self-driving vans entering service
- **Nvidia:** DRIVE Hyperion architecture integration
- **Joby Aviation:** Air taxi service

This approach is capital-efficient relative to building proprietary AV technology. The risk is dependency on third parties who could bypass Uber's platform (as Waymo already does with its own app). The bull case is that Uber's demand aggregation, routing optimization, insurance infrastructure, and regulatory expertise make it the natural distribution layer for AV operators — similar to how hotels use booking platforms despite having their own websites.

### Estimate Trajectory

Consensus estimates have been revised down 6-7% over 90 days, reflecting macro caution around consumer spending. Over 30 days, estimates have stabilized (flat to slightly positive). Current consensus: FY 2026 EPS of $3.37, FY 2027 EPS of $4.35. At $70.48, the stock trades at 20.9x FY 2026 and 16.2x FY 2027.

The peer set (ETN, HON, UNP, LMT, BA) is imperfect because Uber's business characteristics are more comparable to technology platforms than traditional industrials. Against tech/platform peers, a 15-21x earnings multiple on 18% revenue growth and 42% FCF growth would typically command a higher valuation. The discount reflects: (1) driver classification existential risk, (2) AV disruption uncertainty, (3) $1.8B UK VAT contingency, (4) consumer spending macro sensitivity, and (5) the lack of a dividend.

**Base case valuation:** 22x FY 2027 EPS of $4.35 = $96. This assumes modest multiple expansion toward the lower end of the peer range as FCF consistency is demonstrated and AV partnerships advance. Alternatively, 14x FY 2027 FCF of ~$12B (assuming 20% growth) = ~$83/share on 2.04B shares.

## Competitive Landscape

Uber's competitive position rests on the largest global two-sided marketplace in mobility and delivery. 202M monthly active consumers and millions of drivers/couriers create network effects that are difficult to replicate. Cross-platform synergies (ride-hailing + delivery + membership via Uber One) increase switching costs. The airport trips concentration (15% of Mobility bookings) represents a structural advantage given the high-intent, price-insensitive nature of airport rides.

Key competitive risks: DoorDash dominates US food delivery market share. Lyft maintains a viable US ridesharing alternative. Low switching costs for both riders and drivers mean promotional spending ($1.6B in consumer discounts in FY 2025) remains necessary. International competitors (Bolt, Grab, Didi) challenge Uber in specific geographies.

The AV transition introduces a new competitive dimension. Waymo already operates an independent ridesharing service in 10+ US metros. If AV operators conclude they don't need Uber's demand aggregation, the platform's value proposition weakens. Uber's counter-positioning — offering instant access to 202M consumers, proven routing/dispatching infrastructure, insurance expertise, and regulatory relationships — is credible but unproven as a durable moat in an AV-dominated future.

## Risk Assessment

### Driver Classification (HIGH)

The most thoroughly documented risk in Uber's 10-K. Active challenges across jurisdictions: Mexico reclassified drivers as employees in December 2024; New Zealand's Supreme Court ruled four drivers are employees in November 2025; France's government seeks social security contributions despite a favorable Supreme Court ruling. California's Proposition 22 survived its court challenge, providing some US stability, but Massachusetts allowed collective bargaining in 2024. If a major market (EU-wide or US federal) mandates reclassification, the financial impact would be measured in billions annually.

### UK VAT Dispute ($1.8B+)

HMRC assessed approximately $1.8B (£1.4B) in disputed VAT for March 2022-September 2024. Uber paid the assessments to preserve appeal rights and recorded them as receivables. Additional assessments covering 2023-2025 are expected. A loss on appeal would create a material writedown and establish precedent for ongoing VAT liability.

### AV Disruption

Waymo's independent expansion represents a live competitive threat. If AV costs drop below human-driver costs and operators bypass Uber's platform, the current business model faces structural margin compression. The multi-partner strategy mitigates this but doesn't eliminate it.

### Macro Sensitivity

Consumer sentiment at record lows and gasoline prices elevated above $3.50 (likely higher given oil >$100) compress discretionary spending. Ride-hailing has a discretionary component, particularly for non-commute trips. Uber's FY 2025 results showed resilience through prior macro stress, but the current environment (potential recession probability 45-50%, blockade-driven energy costs) represents a more severe test.

### Insurance Cost Escalation

Insurance expense increased $851M in FY 2025 due to higher per-mile rates and increased miles driven. This is a structural headwind that grows with volume, partially offsetting operating leverage on other cost lines.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV of 40.7% vs. 32.5% historical volatility indicates the options market is pricing elevated uncertainty (8.2pp rich). The term structure is in backwardation (near-term 40.7% > 6-month 38.0%), suggesting imminent event or macro stress pricing. OI put/call ratio of 1.73 indicates meaningful institutional hedging. The 4.0% put skew confirms elevated downside demand. These signals are consistent with broad market stress rather than Uber-specific positioning — short interest of 2.9% is low and uncrowded. The options pricing suggests unfavorable near-term entry timing but does not indicate informed bearish positioning against the company specifically.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($110-120, +56-70%)

- Revenue growth sustains at 15-18% driven by international expansion and Delivery margin improvement
- FCF grows to $12-14B by FY 2027 as operating leverage continues
- AV partnerships (particularly Rivian 2028 launch) demonstrate that Uber can serve as the distribution layer for autonomous vehicles, eliminating the existential AV disruption risk and adding a new growth vector
- $19.2B remaining buyback authorization reduces share count by 10-13% at current prices
- Multiple re-rates from 15x to 25x+ forward earnings as the market recognizes the platform's FCF durability

### Bear Case ($50-55, -22-29%)

- Consumer recession drives trip volume decline of 5-10%, breaking the 18-20% growth streak
- Major jurisdiction (EU-wide or US federal) mandates driver reclassification, adding $3-5B in annual costs
- UK VAT ruling goes against Uber, establishing precedent for ~$3B+ cumulative liability
- AV operators (Waymo, Tesla) bypass Uber's platform entirely, undermining the long-term competitive position
- Oil above $120-130 pushes consumer transportation spending into public transit, reducing ride-hailing demand
- Multiple compresses to 10-12x forward earnings

## Prior BUY Assessment

The previous BUY at ~$75 (March 6, 2026) has lost 6.6%. What has materially changed:

1. **Lower price improves valuation.** At $70.48 vs. $75.13, the forward P/E compressed from ~22x to ~21x FY 2026 and ~16x FY 2027. FCF yield increased from 6.3% to 6.8%.
2. **CFO insider purchase.** Balaji Krishnamurthy bought 22,400 shares ($1.6M) on February 24 at $71.25 — an open-market purchase by the incoming CFO within 4% of the current price. This is the strongest insider signal type (new C-suite executive committing personal capital).
3. **Estimates stabilized.** The 6-7% downward revision over 90 days has halted. 30-day revisions are flat to slightly positive across all periods.
4. **Rivian partnership announced.** The $1.25B commitment for up to 50,000 robotaxis represents the most concrete AV de-risking event since Uber began pursuing the multi-partner strategy.
5. **Macro environment deteriorated.** Consumer sentiment hit record lows. Oil rose above $100 on the naval blockade. This is the primary source of the stock's decline and represents a genuine headwind.

The macro deterioration is real and creates near-term risk. However, Uber's business demonstrated resilience through prior stress periods (COVID recovery, 2022 inflation spike), and the valuation at $70 provides a wider margin of safety than at $75. The thesis has not been invalidated by business-specific developments.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months. Key catalysts: Q1 2026 earnings (May 2026), Rivian/Zoox deployment updates (H2 2026-2027), UK VAT appeal resolution (timing uncertain), macro stabilization.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Two consecutive quarters of negative trip growth (would signal structural demand destruction, not cyclical weakness)
2. EU-wide or US federal driver reclassification mandate with implementation timeline
3. Management reduces or pauses the buyback program (would signal deteriorating confidence in FCF sustainability)

## Conclusion

Uber at $70.48 trades at 15x trailing earnings and ~6.8% FCF yield on a business generating $9.8B in annual free cash flow with 18% revenue growth and expanding margins. The stock sits near its 52-week low, pricing in both macro headwinds (consumer recession risk, elevated energy costs) and company-specific risks (driver classification, AV disruption, UK VAT). Risk-reward at this level is favorable: 36% upside to base case against 25% downside to bear case. The CFO's recent open-market purchase and estimate stabilization support the fundamental thesis. The macro environment warrants position sizing discipline, but the valuation compensates for elevated uncertainty. BUY maintained.
