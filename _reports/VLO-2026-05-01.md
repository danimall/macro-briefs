## Executive Summary

Valero Energy is the world's largest independent petroleum refiner, operating ~3.0 million BPD across 14 refineries (after the Benicia, CA closure in April 2026). The current macro environment — Strait of Hormuz disruption tightening global product markets, OPEC+ increases widening heavy sour crude discounts, and U.S. distillate inventories at 5-year lows — is close to the optimal operating environment for Valero's complex Gulf Coast refining system. Q1 2026 demonstrated the earnings power: $4.22 adjusted EPS, beating consensus by 32.5%, with refining operating income surging to $1.8B from a $530M loss in Q1 2025.

Current-year EPS consensus has risen 91.5% in 90 days to $23.26, and next-quarter estimates are up 99.2%. At $252.58, VLO trades at ~10.9x current-year earnings, a meaningful discount to peer median P/E of 18.3x. The company's capital return program is exceptionally aggressive: $4.2B in buyback authorization, a 22% share count reduction since 2022, and a 6% dividend increase in Q1 2026.

The primary risks are the Port Arthur diesel hydrotreater outage (indefinite timeline, occurring during the strongest margin environment in years), renewable diesel segment structural headwinds from tariffs and regulatory changes, and the inherent cyclicality of refining margins that will compress if the Hormuz situation resolves. The options market is pricing 76.3% near-term IV versus 34.4% realized, suggesting traders expect a volatile catalyst window. Given the magnitude of estimate revisions and the structural supply tightness management expects to persist for 6-12 months, the risk/reward at ~11x earnings favors continued ownership.

## Company Overview

Valero operates three segments: Refining (95% of external revenue), Renewable Diesel (~2%), and Ethanol (~3%). The refining segment comprises 14 active refineries — 12 in the U.S., 1 in Canada, 1 in the U.K. — with approximately 3.0 million BPD throughput capacity after the Benicia closure. Valero's competitive distinction is its refinery complexity: Gulf Coast facilities are configured to process heavy sour crude oils purchased at discounts to light sweet benchmarks, directly converting feedstock cost advantage into wider margins.

The Renewable Diesel segment is operated through Diamond Green Diesel (DGD), a 50/50 JV with Darling Ingredients, with ~1.2 billion gallons/year capacity plus SAF capability. The Ethanol segment operates 12 dry mill plants producing ~1.7 billion gallons/year.

Valero has no upstream E&P exposure and no retail network, making it a pure-play on refining margins. When crude prices rise but product prices rise faster (as in the current Hormuz disruption), Valero captures the spread without bearing upstream production risk.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue and Earnings Trajectory

Revenue has declined from $176.4B (2022) to $122.7B (2025), a roughly 30% decline reflecting lower commodity price levels rather than volume contraction. Throughput actually increased to 2,988 thousand BPD in 2025 from 2,912 in 2024. The GAAP net income of $2.3B in 2025 includes a $1.1B impairment charge for the California refinery exit; adjusted operating income rose $615M year-over-year on stronger distillate margins and higher volumes.

Q1 2026 marked a sharp recovery: $1.3B net income ($4.22 EPS) versus a $595M loss in Q1 2025. Refining OI surged to $1.8B. The renewable diesel segment returned to profitability at $139M OI after a $156M loss for full-year 2025. Management cited U.S. product inventory draws of ~30M barrels during Q1 and distillate inventories at 5-year lows.

### Balance Sheet

Valero's financial position is strong for a refiner. Cash of $5.7B (as of Q1 2026), total liquidity of $11B, investment-grade ratings (Baa2/BBB/BBB, all stable), Altman Z-Score of 3.3 (safe zone), and current ratio of 1.65. Total debt is approximately $10.6B ($949M short-term, $9.67B long-term), resulting in a debt-to-equity ratio of 2.44 — elevated in absolute terms but consistent with refining industry norms given the asset intensity. The company pre-financed $850M in 10-year notes at a sector-record-low spread of 102 bps over Treasuries, demonstrating credit market confidence.

### Capital Allocation

This is Valero's strongest attribute. Since 2022, the company has reduced its diluted share count by 22% (395M → 309M). In 2025, it returned $4.0B to shareholders ($2.6B buybacks + $1.4B dividends) — a 170% payout ratio relative to net income, funded by operating cash flow of $5.8B. A new $2.5B buyback authorization was approved in February 2026, bringing total capacity to $4.2B. Capital investments are declining: $1.9B in 2025, guided to $1.7B in 2026 ($1.4B sustaining, $300M growth). FCF of $5.1B in 2025 represents a 4.2% FCF margin and implies a free cash flow yield of approximately 7% at the current market cap of $73.4B.

### Earnings Momentum

The estimate revision trend is the most bullish signal in the data. Current-year EPS consensus has risen from $12.15 to $23.26 over 90 days (+91.5%), with 30-day revisions accelerating (+38.9%). Next quarter estimates are up 99.2% in 90 days. The company has beaten estimates in all four reported quarters, with surprise magnitudes of 15.8% to 32.5%.

## Growth Analysis

Valero's growth drivers in the current environment are margin-based rather than volume-based:

**Near-term (6-12 months):** Management expects "tight product markets for at least six to twelve months." The combination of Hormuz disruption reducing global refining product flows, capacity closures (Valero's own Benicia plus European shutdowns), unplanned Russian refinery outages, and slow emerging-market capacity ramp-ups is creating a structural supply deficit. U.S. exports surged 470,000 BPD year-over-year in Q1. Heavy sour crude discounts ($16/bbl for Canadian heavy vs. WTI) are widening as OPEC+ increases production and Venezuela sanctions are partially removed — directly benefiting Valero's complex refineries.

**Medium-term:** Benicia closure removes the highest-cost, most regulated capacity from the portfolio, improving system-wide margins. SAF capability at Port Arthur adds a growing revenue stream (global jet fuel demand is the fastest-growing petroleum product category). Declining capex frees more cash for returns.

**Headwinds:** Renewable diesel is structurally impaired by tariffs on foreign feedstocks, the transition from blender's tax credit to 45Z clean fuel production credit (fewer eligible volumes, lower credit values, ~$675M unfavorable impact), and proposed RFS Set II changes that would reduce RIN equivalency values for hydrogenation-based producers like DGD.

Next-year EPS consensus of $18.49 implies margin normalization from the $23.26 current-year estimate — roughly a 21% earnings decline as geopolitical premiums partially fade. This is reasonable but still well above the $10.61 adjusted EPS earned in 2025, reflecting a structurally tighter product market baseline.

- **10.9x current-year consensus EPS** ($23.26)
- **13.7x next-year consensus EPS** ($18.49)
- **33.4x trailing twelve-month EPS** ($7.57 — includes the impairment-distorted Q1 2025 loss)

The peer median P/E is 18.3x (EOG 15.3x, MPC 18.3x, PSX 16.1x, KMI 21.5x, BKR 22.0x). VLO's 10.9x current-year multiple represents a 40% discount to peer median. Even on next-year estimates (which embed margin normalization), 13.7x is a 25% discount.

The discount reflects three factors: (1) refining margin cyclicality — the market rightly assigns a lower multiple to peak-cycle earnings; (2) the Port Arthur outage removing throughput during the best margin environment; (3) renewable diesel headwinds. However, the magnitude of the discount appears excessive given that Valero's refinery complexity means it benefits disproportionately from heavy sour discounts that are structurally widening, the 7% FCF yield provides downside support, and the buyback program is retiring shares rapidly enough that even normalized earnings produce attractive per-share growth.

The prior trajectory showed valuation improving from 5.0 to 6.5. The 118% price appreciation over 12 months has compressed the valuation score relative to the prior assessment. At $252.58, VLO sits just 2.3% below its 52-week high of $258.43. Analyst consensus mean is $250.44, essentially at the current price. The stock has run materially, and most of the estimate revision upside has been captured. Upside to the high target of $292 is 15.6%; downside to the low target of $181 is 28.3%.

Applying 12x to current-year EPS of $23.26 yields $279; applying 14x to next-year $18.49 yields $259. The base case fair value range is $260-280, representing 3-11% upside from current levels. The bull case (sustained Hormuz disruption with further margin expansion, quick Port Arthur repair) could push toward $300+. The bear case (diplomatic resolution compressing crack spreads) would send EPS toward $10-12 and the stock toward $150-170.

## Competitive Landscape

**Refinery complexity advantage:** Valero's Nelson Complexity Index across its Gulf Coast system is among the highest in the U.S. independent refiner universe. This enables processing of discounted heavy sour crudes (Canadian WCS, Venezuelan Merey, Saudi Arab Heavy) at wider feedstock spreads than simpler refineries. With OPEC+ increasing production (predominantly heavy grades) and Venezuela sanctions partially lifted, the volume of discounted crude available to complex refiners is expanding.

**Scale and geographic positioning:** 14 active refineries with ~3.0 million BPD, concentrated on the U.S. Gulf Coast with access to export terminals. U.S. product exports surged in Q1 2026, and Valero's infrastructure is positioned to capture this trade. The Benicia exit, while reducing capacity, removes the portfolio's weakest link.

**Capital discipline relative to peers:** MPC trades at 18.3x versus VLO's 10.9x, partly reflecting MPC's midstream/retail diversification. PSX at 16.1x reflects its chemicals and midstream businesses. Valero's pure-play refining focus amplifies margin leverage in both directions. During strong margin environments, this is a competitive advantage.

**Renewable diesel differentiation weakening:** DGD was a structural advantage through 2024, but tariff and regulatory changes have temporarily impaired the segment. The SAF upgrade provides optionality as aviation fuel demand grows, but near-term profitability is pressured.

## Risk Assessment

### Port Arthur Diesel Hydrotreater Outage
Valero's largest refinery (435,000 BPD) suffered an explosion on March 23, 2026. Crude units are restarting, but the diesel hydrotreater has "indefinite" downtime and the kerosene hydrotreater is out through Q3. During a period of multi-year-high distillate margins, lost diesel/kerosene production at the company's largest plant represents material opportunity cost. Insurance should cover physical damage, but business interruption recovery timing is uncertain. Q2 Gulf Coast throughput guided 1.69-1.74M BPD versus normal ~1.85M BPD.

### Margin Cyclicality and Geopolitical Dependency
The current earnings power is heavily dependent on the Hormuz disruption and tight product markets. A diplomatic resolution to the Iran conflict would compress crack spreads rapidly. However, even under a resolution scenario, structural tightness from capacity closures and Russian outages provides a higher floor than the 2024 trough.

### Regulatory Escalation
California's SBx 1-2 (margin monitoring), New York/Vermont cost recovery programs, Oregon tort lawsuits, California antitrust class actions, and escalating LCFS requirements represent a broadening regulatory attack surface. The Benicia exit mitigates California-specific risk, but national and EU-level regulatory momentum (CSRD/CSDDD, Pillar Two) is building.

### Renewable Diesel Structural Headwinds
RFS Set II proposals, tariffs on foreign feedstocks without corresponding tariffs on finished imports, and the blender's-to-producer credit transition collectively impaired DGD by approximately $1.6B in 2025 (combining the ~$940M feedstock tariff impact and ~$675M unfavorable credit transition impact). Recovery depends on policy clarity that may not come until 2027+.

### Texas Grid and Water
The 10-K flags growing Texas electricity grid instability from data center demand and ongoing water supply challenges at several refineries. For a company with significant Gulf Coast capacity concentration in Texas, this is a non-trivial infrastructure risk.

### Options Market Signal
Near-term ATM IV of 76.3% versus 34.4% historical volatility represents an extreme 41.8pp premium, with the term structure in steep backwardation (76.3% 1-week vs. 44.1% 6-month). This pricing reflects expectations of a near-term catalyst — likely the May 1 expiry capturing overnight news risk. The put/call OI ratio of 0.35 is call-heavy, suggesting options positioning is directionally bullish. The IV delta over the past week (+12.7pp) alongside a +4.1% price move indicates buyers are paying up for calls rather than hedging downside. While the rich IV means new options positions are expensive, the directional signal confirms institutional bullishness.

## Conclusion

Valero remains a BUY at $252.58, though the risk/reward has compressed as the stock has appreciated 118% over 12 months. The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) structurally tight global product markets driven by the Hormuz disruption and capacity closures, validated by the 90-day estimate revision trajectory; (2) a forward P/E that remains well below peer median despite superior refinery complexity and aggressive capital returns; and (3) management's demonstrated commitment to shareholder value through buyback authorization and sustained share count reduction.

The score trajectory has improved from 6.1 to 6.7 over four months, driven by rising estimates and improving fundamentals. The current assessment holds the overall score at 6.7, reflecting that strong macro tailwinds and estimate momentum are partially offset by the stock's proximity to its 52-week high and compressed upside to analyst consensus. The Port Arthur outage, renewable diesel structural headwinds, and inherent cyclicality of refining margins justify a 2-3% position sizing rather than an overweight. Investors should monitor the three invalidation triggers and be prepared to take profits if the geopolitical premium fades or Port Arthur complications emerge.
