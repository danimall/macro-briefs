**Research Date: July 4, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Griffon Corporation is completing a transformation from a diversified holding company into a building-products pure-play centered on Clopay garage and rolling-steel doors, with Hunter fans as the residual second business. On February 5, 2026, management announced the exit of essentially all of its Consumer & Professional Products (AMES/tools/lawn & garden) segment; the AMES joint venture with Onex's ONCAP platform closed June 10, 2026, leaving Griffon with $100M cash, a $161.1M second-lien loan, and a 43% retained equity stake. Continuing operations now report as a single segment guided to approximately $1.8B revenue and $458M adjusted EBITDA in FY2026, a materially higher-quality profile (~25% EBITDA margin) than the old conglomerate.

The central analytical point is that the FY2025 GAAP earnings collapse to $1.09 EPS is an artifact of a $243.6M non-cash Hunter Fan impairment ($136.6M goodwill + $107M trademark). Adjusted EPS rose to $5.65 from $5.12, and the business generated $305M of free cash flow (12.1% margin) at 2.4x net leverage. Screens keying off the 96x trailing P/E, the 26.9x reported debt/equity, or the 1.95 Altman Z-score will misread this company: the thin equity is the product of years of aggressive buybacks (share count fell from 52.1M to 45.4M over three years), not distress. On forward adjusted EPS of roughly $5.20 (current year) to $5.75 (next year), GFF trades at ~17.9x–16.1x, close to building-products peers rather than the headline 96x.

The recommendation is HOLD with a growth_hold rationale. The re-rating thesis is legitimate — a cleaner, higher-margin, FCF-rich pure-play deserves a better multiple than the old mix — but three factors argue against upgrading now: current-year estimates are drifting lower (−2.6% over 30 days), the C-suite (CEO, COO, CFO) has been selling into strength near $95 while the stock sits ~6% below its 52-week high after a +20% move since our last review, and the underlying volume trend is negative (−5–6%), with the entire top line held up by price/mix. This is a quality story facing a soft near-term earnings tape and a 2028 refinancing wall that will govern the achievable multiple.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1959 and headquartered in New York, Griffon historically operated two segments. Home & Building Products (Clopay) is the crown jewel: the largest North American manufacturer of residential garage doors (Clopay, IDEAL, Holmes) and commercial rolling steel doors (Cornell, Cookson), roughly 63% of FY25 revenue at ~31% adjusted EBITDA margins. Clopay holds #1 share in both sectional and rolling-steel doors, is the exclusive garage-door supplier to Home Depot (a 40-year relationship) and Menards (30+ years), and operates a dense quick-ship distribution network (57 distribution centers) that functions as a service moat. The majority of Clopay sales are repair/remodel, a more stable demand base than new construction.

Consumer & Professional Products (AMES) — global tools, lawn & garden, home storage (ClosetMaid), and fans (Hunter) — was ~37% of FY25 revenue but structurally lower margin (~9% EBITDA) and heavily China-sourced and consumer-discretionary-exposed. That exposure produced the Hunter impairment. Post-transformation, continuing operations consist of Clopay doors plus Hunter fans, reported as one segment. Griffon has a track record of this playbook: plastics divested in 2018, Telephonics defense in 2022, and now AMES in 2026.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 consolidated revenue was $2.52B, down 4% YoY (CPP −10%, HBP roughly flat). GAAP net income fell to $51.1M ($1.09 EPS) from $209.9M ($4.23), driven almost entirely by the $243.6M pre-tax non-cash Hunter impairment (~$4.65/share after tax). Adjusted net income was $263.6M, or $5.65 adjusted EPS, up from $5.12; the operating business grew. Free cash flow was $305M (12.1% FCF margin) on operating cash flow of $357M.

Q2 FY2026 on a continuing-operations (building-products) basis: revenue of $421.9M (−1% YoY), six-month revenue of $876M (+1%). Volume was down 5–6%, offset by price/mix up 5–6%. Gross margin was 45.5% (down from 46.5% on volume deleverage and material costs); adjusted EBITDA was $97.8M, a 23.2% margin; adjusted continuing EPS was $1.05, beating the $1.00 consensus. Management maintained FY2026 guidance.

On leverage: total debt is ~$1.41B, net debt ~$1.30B, net debt/EBITDA 2.4x per the credit agreement. The stack is $974.8M of 5.75% Senior Notes due 2028, a Term Loan B of $387M (SOFR+2.00%, ~5.66%, due 2029, with $383M cumulatively prepaid), and a $500M revolver ($50M drawn, due 2028). Stockholders' equity is only $74M (Sep-25), rising to $94M (Mar-26), which produces an optically alarming 26.9x D/E and a 1.95 Altman Z-score in the grey zone. This reflects capital-return-driven equity depletion, not distress: the current ratio is 2.66, the company is comfortably covenant-compliant, and it generates $300M+ FCF. Any credit or valuation model that treats the thin book equity as a solvency signal is making an error.

Capital allocation is heavily shareholder-return-oriented: $183M repurchased in FY25 ($310M FY24), $247M remaining on the authorization, and a dividend raised to $0.22/quarter. The AMES JV proceeds provide additional deleveraging and return fuel while preserving optionality on any AMES recovery.

## Growth Analysis

This is a re-rating story, not a volume-growth story, and the distinction should temper the Growth Prospects score. Residential volume is declining 5–6%, consistent with a soft repair/remodel environment tied to existing-home turnover, rates, and cautious consumer confidence. The top line is being held up by price/mix, which reflects pricing power on galvanized-steel doors but is not the same as underlying demand growth. Guidance represents a cleaner, higher-margin base, but it is a re-mix of the portfolio rather than an acceleration.

The earnings-momentum signals are mildly negative near-term. Current-year consensus EPS has been revised down −2.6% over 30 days and −1.0% over 90 days to $5.20; current-quarter estimates fell −0.9%/−1.8%. Next-year estimates are essentially flat ($5.75, +1.3%/30d, −0.4%/90d). This is the opposite of the >30% positive-revision inflection signal that would justify a growth-driven upgrade; estimates are broadly drifting lower, so the valuation must be justified on the quality re-rating and FCF, not on rising forward numbers. The earnings track record is solid but not exceptional: three beats and one miss over four quarters, with beats of 5.3% and 7.4% in the two most recent prints.

The durable growth angle is structural, not cyclical: aging U.S. housing stock, energy-efficiency door upgrades, and Clopay's continued share consolidation in a fragmented door market. That underpins a mid-single-digit organic trajectory over a full cycle once housing turnover normalizes, a reasonable but unspectacular growth profile.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline 96x trailing P/E is meaningless; it is inflated by the non-cash Hunter impairment that cut GAAP EPS. The relevant metrics are forward adjusted earnings and FCF. GFF trades at ~17.9x current-year adjusted EPS and ~16.1x next-year. On continuing-ops EBITDA, EV of roughly $5.6B (market cap $4.26B + net debt $1.3B) implies ~12x EV/EBITDA, and the FCF supports a ~7% FCF yield on equity.

Against Building Products peers (median P/E 19.0: UFPI 18.4, FBIN 19.0, TREX 25.8, WOR 26.1), GFF's ~16–18x forward multiple is roughly in line to slightly cheap, and the peer group is heterogeneous. The re-rating case argues that a business at ~25% EBITDA margins with #1 door share and $300M+ FCF should command a premium to the diversified-conglomerate multiple it carried historically, and closer to the higher-margin peers. The counter-case is that the multiple already reflects much of that: the stock has re-rated from the low-$70s in March to $92.86, a ~20% move since our last review, and sits only ~6% below its 52-week high of $98.37.

I score valuation 5.5 — neutral. The forward multiple is not stretched for the quality, but it is not offering asymmetric upside either. Two considerations restrain a higher score. First, the cheapness that does exist is partly a value-trap flag: the "cheap on forward P/E" argument is undermined by estimates that are falling, not rising, so the P/E may not be as low as it looks once the near-term tape is marked. Second, the analyst target range ($115 low, $135 high, $118 mean) sits meaningfully above spot, but with estimates drifting lower and the stock post-re-rating, those targets look more like a stale bull case than a floor. The easy part of the re-rating has happened; the next leg requires the 2028 refinancing to clear at reasonable spreads and volume to inflect.

## Competitive Landscape

Clopay's competitive position is the strongest single element of the thesis and is improving through the transformation. In North American garage doors it holds #1 share in both sectional and rolling-steel categories, competing against Overhead Door/Wayne Dalton and Amarr/ASSA ABLOY plus regional makers. The moat rests on brand (Clopay, IDEAL, Holmes, Cornell, Cookson), exclusive big-box relationships, proprietary dealer software (MyClopay, MyDoor), and a dense quick-ship logistics network that competitors cannot easily replicate on service. The repair/remodel skew provides a more defensible revenue base than new-construction-levered peers.

The strategic exit of AMES concentrates Griffon in its most defensible, highest-margin corner and removes the commoditized, import-exposed segment. Hunter fans remain a weaker competitive position — they face retailer house brands (Home Depot's Hampton Bay, Lowe's Harbor Breeze) and Big Ass Fans in industrial — which is precisely why Hunter took the impairment. On balance, the trajectory is improving: the company is shedding its weakest competitive exposure and doubling down on category leadership. That supports a competitive-position score of 7.5, a slight step down from the prior 8.0 given that the recent secondary-signal read on this dimension has softened market-wide and the door business still faces a cyclical volume decline that tests pricing durability.

## Risk Assessment

**Housing/renovation cyclicality.** The bulk of Clopay sales tie to existing-home turnover and rates. A deeper H2 consumer pullback would test pricing power.

**Customer concentration.** Home Depot is ~10% of revenue. Loss of either the Home Depot or Menards exclusive relationship would be materially adverse.

**2028 refinancing wall.** The Senior Notes must be addressed before December 1, 2027, or the revolver springs to mature early. AMES proceeds and strong FCF provide flexibility, but the spread/rate environment at refinancing will influence both interest cost and the equity multiple.

**Tariff / China sourcing.** Tariffs triggered the Hunter impairment and remain a live exposure for the residual fan business (~$86M CNY hedges). Clopay, by contrast (galvanized steel, ~96% U.S. revenue, domestic manufacturing), is comparatively insulated and even benefits from pricing power.

**Transaction execution and residual AMES exposure.** The retained 43% JV stake, the second-lien loan to a leveraged JV, an incomplete Australia review, and a $22.6M held-for-sale loss on US/Canada AMES all carry execution and mark risk. The UK operation was liquidated ($27.3M charges).

**Reduced diversification.** The historical rationale that diversification smooths cyclicality and seasonality is being abandoned. Continuing EBITDA is now overwhelmingly Clopay garage doors, raising single-category sensitivity.

**Environmental.** Peekskill, NY (Lightron, on the CERCLA National Priorities List, RI/FS due 2027, insurer-funded) and Memphis, TN (Hunter, possible NPL listing, liability "probable") are currently deemed immaterial but bear monitoring.

**Renewed activism.** The 2021 Voss Capital campaign shaped the board; the risk factor flags potential for renewed activism.

### Options Market Signal

Options are pricing fair uncertainty: near-term ATM IV of 38.7% sits essentially on top of 1-year historical vol of 37.1% (spread +1.6%), and IV is at only the 31st percentile within the current chain. There is no cheap-vol entry signal and no rich-vol warning. Near-term put/call skew is elevated at 21.1% (1-month), suggesting some downside hedging demand into the near term, though the skew flattens to +1.4% at 3 months and inverts to −3.2% at 6 months, so the hedging is tactical rather than a structural bearish tilt. The flat term structure (near 38.7% ≈ far 39.2%) indicates the market sees no concentrated catalyst. Short interest is modest at 4.7% of float (4.2 days to cover), so this is neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup. On balance, options positioning is consistent with a HOLD: near-term downside is being lightly hedged, but there is no informed positioning signaling a large move in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The AMES exit crystallizes a clean, ~25% EBITDA-margin, $300M+ FCF building-products pure-play with #1 door share and pricing power. As the market recognizes the higher-quality earnings base and looks past the optical GAAP/leverage screens, the multiple re-rates toward higher-margin peers. FCF and AMES proceeds fund continued buybacks and de-lever ahead of the 2028 notes, clearing the refinancing at reasonable spreads. Housing turnover normalizes, volume inflects positive, and price/mix layers on top. On ~$6.00 normalized adjusted EPS and an 18–20x multiple, the stock reaches $115–$135, aligning with the analyst range. Bull target: **$130**.

**Bear case.** The H2 consumer environment deepens, existing-home turnover stays depressed, and volume declines outrun price/mix as pricing power fatigues, pressuring the door business. Estimates continue to drift lower, and the re-rating stalls with the stock already up 20% and near its highs. The 2028 refinancing lands at wider spreads, raising interest cost and compressing the multiple. Residual AMES marks and the second-lien loan to a leveraged JV take write-downs. On ~$4.75 depressed adjusted EPS and a 14x multiple, the stock falls to ~$67–$70. Bear target: **$68** (−27% from current).

Base case: the re-rating is largely priced, estimates are soft near-term, and the stock consolidates in the high-$80s to mid-$90s until either a volume inflection or a clean refinancing provides the next catalyst. Base target: **$100** (+8%).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is constructive — a higher-quality pure-play that can re-rate — but the near-term earnings tape (falling current-year estimates), C-suite selling into strength, and a post-run price ~6% below the 52-week high argue against paying up here.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Current-year adjusted EPS estimates stabilize and turn positive (a return to rising revisions rather than the current −2.6%/30d drift), signaling the earnings tape has bottomed.
2. Clopay residential volume returns to flat-or-positive YoY (from the current −5–6%), confirming demand is inflecting rather than being propped by price/mix alone.
3. The 2028 Senior Notes are refinanced at a spread that does not materially raise blended interest cost, removing the balance-sheet overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Loss or material renegotiation of the Home Depot or Menards exclusive relationship.
2. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance cut below ~$430M, or FCF conversion breaking down.
3. A refinancing forced at punitive spreads, or leverage rising above ~3.0x net debt/EBITDA on a residual-AMES mark or a demand shock.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly print (fiscal Q3 FY2026 report, expected early August), which will show whether volume trends and estimate revisions have stabilized and provide an update on the Australia AMES review and refinancing intentions.

## Conclusion

Griffon is executing a credible transformation into a Clopay-centered building-products pure-play with attractive margins, strong free cash flow, and category leadership. The GAAP earnings collapse is a non-cash artifact, and the reported leverage screens are misleading: the equity is thin by design (buybacks), and the credit profile at 2.4x leverage with $300M+ FCF is sound. On forward adjusted earnings of ~16–18x, the stock is fairly valued against higher-margin peers rather than cheap.

The recommendation is HOLD. The re-rating that this transformation warrants has substantially played out in the +20% move to $92.86, current-year estimates are drifting lower, and management is selling into strength near $95. The competitive position (7.5) is the standout, but growth is a re-mix rather than an acceleration, and the near-term earnings momentum is negative. I would wait for a volume inflection, a stabilization in estimates, or a clean 2028 refinancing before adding, and would look for a better entry in the high-$70s to low-$80s, closer to the March re-rating base, for a more asymmetric risk/reward.
