## Executive Summary

HF Sinclair delivered its strongest quarter since 2022 — Q2 2026 GAAP EPS of $4.93 on $10,390M of revenue, adjusted refinery gross margin of $25.95/bbl and $1,510M of operating cash flow — and consensus has chased it hard. FY2026 adjusted EPS estimates have risen 68.9% in 90 days to $13.24, and FY2027 estimates by 51.4% to $9.00. At $82.60 the shares trade at 6.2x the current-year and 9.2x the next-year number, against a refining peer median trailing multiple of 10.8x. Net debt is roughly $538M against annualized first-half EBITDA (excluding a non-cash inventory reversal) near $3.7B, with $4.3B of total liquidity and no bond maturity before February 2028. On the surface this is a cheap, cash-generative, unlevered inflection.

Three things prevent that from converting into a purchase recommendation. First, the earnings inflection is a regional crack-spread event, not an improvement in the business: 1H26 West-region adjusted margin rose to $22.93/bbl from $13.80, while Mid-Continent margin *declined* to $11.24 from $11.61. Consensus itself concedes the point by modeling FY2027 EPS 32% below FY2026. When the revision engine is tracking spot spreads and the out-year already embeds normalization, a rising near-term estimate is weak evidence of durable re-rating. Second, the company is being run by a temporary CEO and an acting CFO nearly six months after both the prior CEO and CFO departed following an Audit Committee review of "tone at the top," and that interim team has just launched a 12-18 month tax-free separation of Lubricants & Specialties conditional on an IRS private letter ruling and third-party financing. Third, the options market is complacent — near-term implied volatility at the 14th percentile of the current chain, put/call volume ratio 0.15, call-heavy skew — against a stack of unresolved binaries: two-sided small-refinery-exemption litigation, an unquantified Puget Sound DOJ/EPA/state enforcement matter, and $2,978M of goodwill (28.8% of equity) last quantitatively tested at a cushion of only "more than 10%."

Our probability-weighted twelve-month value is approximately $88 (base $92, bull $115, bear $60), implying roughly 7% price appreciation plus a 2.5% dividend. That is a fair-to-modestly-positive expected return with a 0.4:1 upside-to-base versus downside-to-bear ratio and no margin of safety at spot. **HOLD.** Score of 5.56 versus 5.3 on July 19 — the improvement is entirely in the valuation dimension (the stock fell 6.8% from $88.59 while FY2026 estimates rose 32.2% in 30 days), partially offset by a modest reduction in the risk score as peak-cycle exposure has widened and volatility pricing has compressed.

## Company Overview

HF Sinclair is a Dallas-based independent refiner with 678,000 BPSD of consolidated crude capacity across seven refineries: El Dorado (KS) and Tulsa (OK) in the Mid-Continent, and Puget Sound (WA), Navajo (NM), Woods Cross (UT), Parco (WY) and Casper (WY) in the West. It has no upstream production and, as of December 31, 2025, owned and operated no retail outlets — it licenses the Sinclair brand. FY2025 crude charge was 604,350 BPD (89.1% utilization).

The regional asymmetry matters more than the aggregate. FY2025 Mid-Continent utilization was 102.7% while the West ran at 80.7% with crude charge *declining* year-over-year to 337,320 BPD from 350,430 BPD. The West is the larger system by throughput (367,460 BPD versus 284,620 BPD) and structurally under-run, which amplifies earnings when West spreads widen and leaves the system exposed when they normalize.

Five segments: Refining ($23,822M FY2025 segment revenue, the dominant driver); Lubricants & Specialties ($2,519M unaffiliated revenue, now slated for separation); Marketing ($3,142M, 1,832 branded sites at 6/30/26); Midstream ($121M unaffiliated, the former Holly Energy Partners, wholly owned since December 2023); and Renewables ($991M, three renewable diesel units).

## Financial Analysis

**Multi-year trajectory.** Revenue declined from $31,964M (2023) to $28,580M (2024) to $26,869M (2025). Income from operations collapsed from $2,203M to $261M in 2024 before recovering to $927M in 2025. Diluted EPS: $8.29 → $0.91 → $3.08. This is a high-amplitude cyclical, and 2024 was a near-breakeven trough.

**FY2025 earnings quality was poor.** MD&A discloses that small refinery RINs waivers "increased pre-tax earnings by $485 million" against total pre-tax income of $732M. Applying the disclosed 19.9% effective rate across 186.465M weighted shares, that is approximately $2.08 of the $3.08 reported EPS — roughly two-thirds of pre-tax income from a discretionary federal regulatory action. Partially offsetting, FY2025 absorbed a $417M non-cash LCM inventory charge (versus a $43M benefit in 2024), so the two items net to only +$68M. The practical implication: FY2025 is unusable as a normalization base.

**1H26 is a step-change of higher quality, but heavily flattered in the GAAP line.** Reported 1H26 EPS of $8.48 includes a $642M pre-tax LCM benefit (the reserve fell from $706M at 12/31/25 to $64M at 6/30/26, with the Refining component going to zero) and a $99M Lubricants FIFO benefit, offset by a $47M Renewables construction-in-progress impairment. Adjusting those three items against approximately $2,010M of 1H26 pre-tax income at the disclosed 23.3% effective rate and 180.032M weighted shares yields approximately **$5.53 of normalized 1H26 EPS** versus $8.48 reported.

The intra-half split is instructive and is the reason no first-half figure should be annualized. 1H26 LCM was a $642M benefit while Q2 alone carried a $30M *charge* — so Q1 held $672M of the benefit. Q1 company-defined EBITDA was $1,097M, of which $672M was non-cash reversal, leaving roughly $425M of underlying Q1 EBITDA against roughly $1,434M in Q2. A 3.4x sequential swing driven by crack spreads.

**The 2026 RIN reversal is the most encouraging fact in the file.** RINs cost was $996M in 1H26 alone — more than double the $475M FY2025 full-year figure — with only $21M of waiver benefit recognized. Open RINs credit obligations rose from $43M to $493M. The 2026 profit surge is therefore spread-driven while absorbing a very large compliance bill, which is materially better earnings quality than 2025. It also creates an unmonetized option: if EPA acts favorably on pending petitions, part of that $493M could be relieved. There is a partial internal hedge as well — Renewables swung from a $43M operating loss in 1H25 to $212M of operating income in 1H26 (including $38M of LCM benefit) as RIN generation revenue rose.

**Balance sheet.** Cash $2,262M at 6/30/26 (from $978M at 12/31/25); working capital $3,639M; debt $2,800M face, all fixed-rate unsecured, weighted average coupon approximately 5.53%, with the ladder running $500M/2028, $400M/2030, $650M/2031, $500M/2032, $750M/2035. The $2.0B revolver is fully undrawn with no letters of credit outstanding, maturing April 2030. Net debt of approximately $538M ($633M including the $95M precious-metals catalyst financing) is roughly 0.17x annualized 1H26 EBITDA ex-LCM and 0.30x FY2025 EBITDA. Leverage is effectively immaterial. Total equity $10,350M; goodwill unchanged at $2,978M.

One modelling caution: reported interest expense embeds a forward-platinum embedded derivative (62,371 troy ounces at 6/30/26). FY2025 interest expense rose to $217M from $165M primarily on unrealized derivative losses; Q2 2026 interest expense *fell* to $20M from $53M partly on a $23M derivative gain. Cash interest is better approximated at roughly $155M from face and coupon.

**Cash flow and returns.** FY2025 free cash flow was approximately $794M (OCF $1,315M less $449M PP&E less $72M precious metals). 1H26 reported FCF was $1,747M, but working capital contributed +$668M and turnaround spending was only $175M versus $284M in 1H25; normalizing the working-capital swing gives roughly $1,079M. The 2026 plan calls for $775M of total capital and turnaround cash spend, H2-weighted. Capital returns in 1H26 were $180M of dividends and $251M of repurchases (4,024,728 shares), an annualized total shareholder yield near 5.8%. The quarterly dividend was raised 5% to $0.525 on July 28 (2.54% yield at $82.60).

A caveat on buybacks: the May 2024 $1.0B authorization had $208M remaining at 6/30/26, and the August 2 related-party block from REH Advisors alone was $212.3M. An 8-K carrying an Item 1.01 classification was filed August 4, but its contents are unverified. **The buyback component of the shareholder-yield thesis currently rests on an authorization we cannot confirm.**

## Growth Analysis

Consensus revisions are extraordinary in magnitude: current quarter $5.35 (+62.7% in 30 days, +120.6% in 90), current year $13.24 (+32.2%/+68.9%), next year $9.00 (+11.7%/+51.4%). The company has beaten in four consecutive quarters, most recently by 15.3%. Mechanically, revisions of this velocity combined with 1H26 revenue growth of 33% at a 6.2x forward multiple present as a strong signal.

We are deliberately discounting it, for a specific and falsifiable reason. This revision pattern is not the signature of an inflection in earnings power; it is consensus tracking realized spot crack spreads with a lag. The tell is in the estimates themselves: **FY2027 consensus of $9.00 is 32% below FY2026's $13.24.** Analysts marking up near-term numbers while modeling a sharp out-year decline are not upgrading their view of the franchise — they are updating a spot-linked model. That distinction is what separates a durable inflection (where the out-year rises with the near-year) from a commodity print-through.

Second, the growth is regionally narrow. 1H26 West adjusted refinery gross margin was $22.93/bbl versus $13.80; Mid-Continent was $11.24 versus $11.61 — down. In Q2, West was $30.57 versus $17.15. Operating expense per throughput barrel *rose* to $7.93 from $7.32. Utilization improved to 92.4% from 90.1%, which is real but modest relative to the margin move.

Third, the structural growth pipeline is thin and in several places shrinking:

- **Renewables** delivered adjusted gross margin less operating expenses per gallon of −$0.01 (2023), −$0.06 (2024), −$0.16 (2025), with volumes falling from 255.6M gallons in 2024 to 213.7M in 2025 and FY2025 segment gross margin of −$129M. The 2026 plan allocates $6M of $775M to the segment and Q2 carried a $47M impairment of abandoned construction in progress. Management's capital allocation is the clearer statement about long-run economics than one strong half-year.
- **Marketing** grew branded sites 13% (1,540 → 1,744, 2023-2025) while gallons sold fell 7.9% (1,441.6M → 1,328.0M) — implied per-site annual volume down roughly 19% to ~762K gallons. Margin capture, not volume, drove the segment. Q2 2026 volumes did rebound 14.7% year-over-year, so the trend may be inflecting.
- **Midstream** third-party crude pipeline volumes fell to 188,347 BPD (2025) from 204,202 (2024) and continued lower in Q2 (180,580 versus 189,918); third-party product pipeline and terminal volumes both fell sharply. Segment operating income declined to $173M (1H26) from $181M (1H25). Post-HEP buy-in it is becoming captive logistics rather than a growing third-party franchise.
- **Lubricants & Specialties** is being separated, and the Mississauga base oil plant is being retired at a pre-tax cost of $405-505M (largely non-cash), with base oil to be sourced externally.

Near-term, Q3 guidance of 590,000-620,000 BPD of crude runs is below Q2's realized 639,680 BPD because of a planned El Dorado turnaround. Q3 consensus of $5.35 versus Q2's $5.31 actual therefore requires adjusted margins at or above Q2's post-2022-high $25.95/bbl on materially fewer barrels. That is the most checkable near-term risk in the model.

The forward multiples look inexpensive against a 10.8x peer median. We resist reading them as cheap, because a compressed multiple on peak commodity earnings is the archetypal refining value trap, and the mid-cycle arithmetic is close to spot.

Consider throughput sensitivity: at 604,000 BPD, one dollar per barrel of adjusted refinery gross margin is worth approximately $220M pre-tax, roughly $176M after tax, or roughly $1.00 per share. The margin history is $21.06/bbl (2023), $10.43 (2024), $15.37 (2025), $18.13 (1H26 with Q2 at $25.95). A mid-cycle assumption of $14.50-15.00/bbl implies roughly $3.30-3.60 per share less than the 1H26 run rate — i.e. normalized EPS near $7.50 rather than $11.06. At 10x that is approximately $75, below spot. At 11x, $82.50 — spot.

Refining capacity is tight from two simultaneous conflicts, Hormuz transit remains disrupted with Kharg Island idled, and product cracks rather than flat crude price are the transmission channel. That argues against calling an imminent crack peak, and it is why we are not at REDUCE. It is not, however, a forecastable input. ## Competitive Landscape

The FY2025 10-K risk factors are candid that the absence of integration is a structural disadvantage: competitors with upstream production and owned retail "may be able to offset losses from refining operations with profits from those operations." DINO's substitutes are the Sinclair licensing/marketing channel and the wholly owned Midstream system that captures tariff on its own barrels.

Genuine advantages: inland Rockies and Mid-Continent positioning where import competition is logistically limited; 678,000 BPSD of scale; a Mid-Continent system that runs above nameplate. Genuine disadvantages: no Gulf Coast export optionality, so realizations are tied to regional demand; West utilization of only 80.7% in FY2025 with declining crude charge (confounded by turnaround timing at Puget Sound and Parco, so structural competitiveness is not cleanly measurable); shrinking third-party Midstream volumes; per-site Marketing volume erosion; a renewable diesel business earning negative unit margins in three of the last three full years.

Trajectory is the relevant question, and it reads flat-to-deteriorating: the L&S separation and Mississauga retirement will leave a company that is more concentrated in Refining — a point the Q2 10-Q makes itself in the one new risk factor added versus the 10-K, warning that each resulting company will be "less diversified," "subject to more volatile results," and with diminished ability to fund capex, dividends and debt service. The 50%-owned, non-operated Green Trail Fuels retail JV in Colorado and New Mexico is the first meaningful step toward retail exposure but is too small to change the picture. **Competitive position: 4.5.**

## Risk Assessment

**Small refinery exemption / RIN litigation (two-sided, dated).** DINO subsidiaries sued in the DC Circuit in October 2025 over denials for Woods Cross, Cheyenne, Casper and Parco (compliance years 2019-2024). EPA granted Tulsa East petitions in full for 2023-2024 in November 2025; the Renewable Fuels Association sued in December 2025 to challenge those grants, with EPA's response brief due November 2026. On April 7, 2026 the DC Circuit ruled unanimously for DINO that EPA erred on Parco's 2024 eligibility; EPA did not act within 90 days of the April 24 mandate, and DINO filed an emergency motion for enforcement or mandamus on July 24, 2026. Outcomes run in both directions — favorable action could relieve part of the $493M obligation; the RFA challenge could reverse benefits already recognized.

**Puget Sound enforcement, unquantified.** HFS Puget Sound has been engaged with the Northwest Clean Air Agency, EPA and DOJ on Clean Air Act, EPCRA and Washington compliance; DINO counteroffered on injunctive relief in October 2024 and the agencies responded in June 2026. No penalty has been demanded and the company says it is too early to predict. A separate 2025 consent decree carried a $35M one-time penalty recognized in 2024. Washington's new refinery Process Safety Management Rule adds compliance cost at the same asset. The 10-K separately warns that "certain key customers" of Puget Sound contribute significantly to that facility's cash flows and that defaults or disputes "have occurred in the past."

**Goodwill.** $2,978M equals 28.8% of 6/30/26 equity and 15.7% of total assets. The July 1, 2025 quantitative test found fair values exceeding carrying values "by more than 10%" — a modest cushion for a cyclical. The company then moved its annual test date from July 1 to October 1 as a voluntary change in accounting principle and used a qualitative assessment for all reporting units at October 1, 2025, stating the change "does not delay, accelerate, or avoid an impairment charge." We attribute no motive; the decision-relevant fact is that a large goodwill balance last measured quantitatively at a sub-10% cushion is now on qualitative footing while the 10-K itself concedes "a reasonable expectation exists that sustained deterioration in our operating results or overall economic conditions could lead to goodwill and/or asset impairments."

**Leadership vacuum during a complex transaction.** The FY2025 10-K discloses an Audit Committee assessment that began in January 2026 after the CFO raised concerns that CEO actions created an unfavorable "tone at the top"; the Board developed separate concerns about the CEO's communications; a separate concern then arose about the CFO's conduct during the review. Both took voluntary leave in February and both departures became permanent via agreements dated May 11 and 13, 2026. The Audit Committee **completed** its review and concluded the actions did not create an unfavorable tone at the top; disclosure controls and ICFR were assessed effective at both 12/31/25 and 6/30/26; no restatement, material weakness or investigation is disclosed. Nonetheless, the July 8, 2026 release states Franklin Myers "will continue to serve as Chief Executive Officer on a temporary basis," and the July 30 10-Q is signed by an acting CFO. Six Item 5.02 8-Ks were filed between March 6 and July 28. 1H26 PSU and RSU forfeitures (435,851 and 214,821) exceeded grants (163,609 and 43,209). The Lubricants separation — requiring an IRS private letter ruling, registration statements, and "availability of financing for the separated business on satisfactory terms" — is being executed by this interim team, and a permanent CEO could revise it.

**Regional concentration and policy tail.** Earnings are levered to one regional spread complex. **Trade policy.** The 10-K flags a 10% levy on Canadian crude potentially subject to USMCA exemption, with USMCA itself subject to 2026 review. West slate is 44% sour plus 11% heavy sour; the company carries C$522M of notional FX forwards through Petro-Canada Lubricants.

### Options Market Signal

Positioning is notably complacent and diverges from the risk inventory above. Near-term ATM IV is 42.2% versus 37.3% one-year historical volatility — nominally rich by 4.9 points — but sits at only the **14th percentile within the current chain**, with a call-heavy skew of -6.0%, a put/call volume ratio of 0.15 and put/call open interest of 0.18. The term structure is in contango (42.2% near versus 47.4% at six months). The nearest expiry implies only ±6.5%. This is the configuration in which realized downside exceeds priced downside, and it is the main reason the risk score is 4.2 rather than 5.

Myers, as Chair and temporary CEO, made an open-market purchase of 15,000 shares at $69.11 on May 18, 2026 ($1,036,650), taking him to 177,053 shares — the highest-signal insider datapoint available. The blocks appear market-priced rather than premium-priced (Myers paid $69.11 in the open market the same day REH's block struck at $68.72, a 0.6% discount), and REH's stake is down to roughly 5.1%, so the overhang is shrinking. Insider sales include the CHRO's 8,936 shares at $84.59 on August 5 and EVP Pompa's 10,000 at $69.05 in May (roughly 19% of pre-sale holdings, seven weeks before her promotion); 10b5-1 status is not established. **Management quality: 4.5**, roughly holding the July level — the Myers purchase and credible operational hires (Ledbetter, ex-Shell Midstream CEO) offset the unresolved leadership structure rather than clearing it.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $115 (+39%)

Refining capacity remains structurally tight through 2027 as Middle East disruption persists and West-region cracks hold near 1H26 levels, delivering FY2027 adjusted EPS near $11.50; at 10x that is $115, essentially the sell-side high of $114. Layered on top: EPA acts on the pending SRE petitions, relieving a meaningful share of the $493M open obligation; the Lubricants separation completes as a tax-free spin and the market assigns the specialty business a higher multiple than it receives inside a refiner; a new repurchase authorization sustains a shareholder yield near 6% against near-zero net debt. The balance sheet — $2,262M cash, $2.0B undrawn revolver, no maturity before February 2028 — means all of this accrues to equity without refinancing risk.

### Bear Case — $60 (-27%)

Crack spreads normalize toward mid-cycle in H2 2026 and 2027. At $14.50/bbl of adjusted refinery gross margin, normalized EPS falls to roughly $7.50, and if West spreads mean-revert harder — the segment where the entire 1H26 improvement occurred — FY2027 adjusted EPS lands near $5.50. At 10x that is $55; balance-sheet support and the dividend put the floor nearer $58-62. Compounding: the El Dorado turnaround compresses Q3 volumes while consensus assumes Q2-level margins; RIN costs continue at the 1H26 run-rate of $996M per half with no waiver relief, or the RFA reverses the Tulsa East grants; the Puget Sound enforcement matter produces a penalty and injunctive capex; a sustained margin decline triggers a goodwill test against a cushion last measured above 10% only modestly; and Mississauga charges of $405-505M land into a weakening tape. The 52-week low of $42.82 sets the tail, and the sell-side low target is $67.

### Base Case — $92 (+11.4%, +13.9% with dividend)

Cracks moderate from Q2's exceptional level but stay above mid-cycle into 2027 given persistent refining tightness. FY2027 adjusted EPS lands near consensus $9.00; 10x gives $90, plus roughly $2/share of after-tax SRE optionality → $92.

Probability weighting of 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear gives $88.15, approximately +6.7% in price and +9.2% total return. Upside-to-base versus downside-to-bear is 0.42:1; bull versus bear is 1.43:1. Neither ratio supports initiating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing owners should maintain exposure and collect the 2.54% dividend; prospective buyers have no entry signal at $82.60.

**Horizon:** 12 months, with three dated checkpoints — the Q3 2026 print (late October, testing whether margins hold against 590-620 kBPD of runs), EPA's response or the DC Circuit's action on the July 24 mandamus motion, and the Lubricants Form 10 filing.

**Base case $92** (+11.4%): 10x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $9.00, plus ~$2/share of after-tax SRE optionality.
**Bull case $115** (+39.2%): 10x FY2027 adjusted EPS of $11.50 on sustained West-region cracks.
**Bear case $60** (-27.4%): 10x FY2027 adjusted EPS of $5.50 on mid-cycle margin normalization, with balance-sheet support.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Price at or below $68-70** with FY2027 consensus holding at or above $9.00 — that is roughly 7.5x forward with a genuine margin of safety, and coincides with the level at which the Chair/temporary CEO bought in size.
2. **FY2027 consensus rises above $11.00** while Mid-Continent adjusted margin improves sequentially for two consecutive quarters — evidence the earnings improvement has broadened beyond one regional spread complex.
3. **A favorable EPA decision on remand for Parco (and/or the pending petitions)** that relieves a quantified portion of the $493M open RIN obligation, plus a permanent CEO and CFO appointed.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. **Q3 2026 adjusted refinery gross margin below $18/bbl**, or FY2027 consensus cut below $7.00 — either would put normalized EPS below $6.50 and the shares above 12x mid-cycle.
2. **A goodwill impairment charge**, or a return to quantitative testing disclosing a cushion below 10%, against the $2,978M balance.
3. **The Lubricants separation abandoned or materially delayed**, or the RFA challenge reversing the Tulsa East exemption grants, or a quantified Puget Sound penalty plus injunctive capex materially above $100M.
4. **Failure to disclose a new repurchase authorization** by the Q3 10-Q, which would cut the shareholder yield from ~5.8% to the 2.54% dividend alone.

**Reassessment date:** the Q3 2026 earnings release (late October 2026), or sooner on a DC Circuit or EPA action on the mandamus motion.

## Conclusion

HF Sinclair has earned its twelve-month advance: Q2 2026 was the best quarter since 2022, cash on hand more than doubled to $2,262M against $2,800M of fixed-rate debt with nothing due before February 2028, the dividend rose 5%, and the 2026 earnings surge is being delivered while absorbing $996M of RIN compliance cost rather than harvesting the $485M regulatory benefit that carried roughly two-thirds of FY2025 pre-tax income. That is a real improvement in earnings quality and it supports a high financial-health score.

What it does not support is a purchase at $82.60. The entire margin improvement is West-region: 1H26 West adjusted margin +66% while Mid-Continent declined. Consensus has marked up FY2026 by 68.9% in 90 days while simultaneously modeling FY2027 32% lower — a spot-linked model, not an upgraded view of the franchise. On a defensible mid-cycle margin of $14.50-15.00/bbl, normalized EPS is nearer $7.50 and the shares are already at ten to eleven times. Layered on that is an interim executive team executing a 12-18 month tax-free separation contingent on an IRS ruling and third-party financing, unresolved two-way SRE litigation, an unquantified Puget Sound enforcement matter, $2,978M of goodwill on a cushion last quantitatively measured just above 10%, and an options market pricing 14th-percentile volatility and a 0.15 put/call volume ratio against all of it.

Probability-weighted twelve-month value of approximately $88 implies a fair-but-unexciting return, with 0.42:1 upside-to-base against downside-to-bear. The cheap forward multiple, the near-zero leverage, and a currently supportive crack environment argue against selling; the peak-cycle denominator, the leadership vacuum and the unpriced binaries argue against buying. **HOLD**, with the concrete accumulation point in the $68-70 range should the cycle give it back.
