**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Realty Income is the world's largest net-lease REIT, with 15,511 properties (~355M sq ft) leased to 1,761 clients across 92 industries, an 8.8-year weighted average lease term, and 98.9% occupancy. Its competitive edge is structural: A3/A- credit ratings give it among the lowest cost of capital in the REIT sector, and its integrated European net-lease platform is genuinely hard to replicate. The 5.2% dividend yield, paid monthly, sits on 31+ consecutive years of increases (133 raises since 1994) and a healthy 75.2% AFFO payout ratio. For an income investor, this is a high-quality, defensible holding.

The problem is per-share growth. Absolute FFO grew roughly 9-11% in 2025, but Normalized FFO/share grew only 3.6% and AFFO/share only 2.1%, because the diluted share count expanded from ~612M (2022) to ~911M (2025) through heavy equity issuance funding the Spirit Realty merger and an active ATM program. Same-store rent growth is structurally capped near 1.3% by fixed-escalator leases, and interest expense rose 11.6% to $1.13B as the company refinances maturing notes into higher coupons. Per-share growth therefore depends entirely on finding accretive external investment spreads, and a frequently-discounted equity currency raises the cost of that growth.

The defining strategic response is the private-capital pivot: three non-overlapping third-party vehicles (a U.S. Core Plus perpetual fund with $1.7B in pension commitments, a GIC build-to-suit JV with >$1.5B in commitments, and an Apollo programmatic venture deploying $1B into ~500 single-tenant retail properties off-balance-sheet). If executed, this monetizes the platform through recurring, capital-light fee income and re-rates O from a pure spread investor toward a hybrid operator/asset-manager. That outcome is plausible but unproven. With the stock at roughly 14x forward P/AFFO, near peer-fair, we rate Realty Income a HOLD for income.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1969 and NYSE-listed since 1994, Realty Income operates a single business segment: acquiring freestanding commercial real estate under long-term net leases, where the tenant pays taxes, insurance, and maintenance. Roughly 80.6% of leases carry embedded rent escalators.

Portfolio composition by annualized base rent ($5.31B ABR):
- **Retail 79.1%** — deliberately weighted to service, non-discretionary, low-price-point formats (~91% of retail ABR). Top industries: grocery 11.0%, convenience 9.6%, home improvement 6.4%, dollar stores 6.1%.
- **Industrial 15.4%**, **Gaming 3.1%**, **Other 2.4%** (data centers, agriculture, office).
- **International 19% of ABR** (U.K. 14.3%, rest of Europe ~5%), up sharply from 14% a year earlier; Europe was ~60% of 2025 acquisition volume.

Tenant diversification is a genuine moat: the largest client (7-Eleven) is only 3.3% of ABR, and the top 20 account for 35.8%. The company runs a proprietary predictive-analytics underwriting platform and a 544-person operating team, supporting a long-duration, well-laddered book.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Revenue grew $3.34B (2022) → $4.08B (2023) → $5.27B (2024) → $5.75B (2025), +9.1% YoY, driven largely by the January 2024 Spirit Realty merger and prior VEREIT consolidation. Net income was $1.06B in 2025 (18.6% net margin). GAAP ROE of 2.7% and the trailing P/E near 50x are artifacts of REIT depreciation accounting and should be disregarded in favor of FFO metrics.

**The key per-share metrics:** Normalized FFO/share $4.27 (+3.6% YoY); AFFO/share $4.28 (+2.1% YoY). The gap between total FFO growth and per-share growth is the entire investment debate. Q1 2026 showed improvement: AFFO/share $1.13 (+6.6% YoY), with management raising FY2026 AFFO guidance to $4.41-$4.44 and investment volume guidance to $9.5B. The beat-and-raise on AFFO sits alongside a falling consensus GAAP EPS series (current-year EPS estimate cut 4.8% over 90 days to $1.57); these measure different things, and AFFO is the operative figure for a net-lease REIT.

**Operations.** Occupancy 98.9% (only 173 of 15,511 properties vacant); rent recapture 103.9% on 2025 re-leases, meaning the company is re-leasing above prior rents. Same-store rent growth was just 1.3% for 2025 — the structural ceiling of a fixed-escalator book. 2025 investments totaled $6.3B at a 7.3% initial cash yield; dispositions were 425 properties for $744M (gain $177.6M).

**Balance sheet.** Total debt ~$29.1B; Net Debt/Annualized Pro Forma EBITDAre 5.4-5.5x, within target and normal for the model. 93% fixed-rate, weighted-average rate 3.9%, weighted-average maturity 5.5 years. Covenant headroom is comfortable: total debt 41.4% of adjusted assets (limit <60%), fixed-charge coverage 4.7x (limit >1.5x), unencumbered assets 242.7% of unsecured debt. Interest expense rose 11.6% to $1.13B, the principal per-share-growth headwind: new 2035 notes price at ~5.1-5.3% against maturing notes at ~3.9-4.6%, so each refinancing cycle compresses the investment spread.

An Altman Z-score of 1.56 ("distress zone") is a false signal here — the model penalizes deliberately-levered, asset-heavy REITs on structural ratios that have nothing to do with default risk. Investment-grade ratings, 4.7x fixed-charge coverage, and ample covenant room contradict any distress reading.

**Dividend coverage.** Monthly dividend $0.27 ($3.24 annualized), 5.2% yield, 75.2% AFFO payout. The company retained ~$939M of FFO after distributions for reinvestment. A $2.0B buyback was authorized in February 2025; $101.9M (1.8M shares) was repurchased in January 2026 alongside a convertible issuance.

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is the weakest part of the story and the reason the score sits at 5.0. Three forces constrain per-share growth:

1. **Same-store growth is capped near 1.3%** by fixed lease escalators — there is no organic lever beyond contractual bumps and 103.9% recapture on a small fraction of the book that re-leases each year.

2. **Equity dilution has historically eaten absolute FFO growth.** The share count rose ~49% from 2022 to 2025, so strong absolute FFO growth translated to only 2-4% per share. This is the mechanism behind the modest 5-year TSR of 120.5 vs. a peer group at 126.7.

3. **Rising interest expense** compresses the acquisition spread that drives external growth, exactly as the cost of equity rises when the stock trades at a discounted multiple.

The offsetting thread, and the only credible path to acceleration, is the private-capital pivot. Three vehicles are now live: the U.S. Core Plus perpetual fund, the GIC build-to-suit JV (U.S./Mexico industrial development), and the Apollo programmatic venture (with a call option capping O's equity cost at 6.875% in years 7-15). These generate recurring, capital-light fee income and let O pursue deals that don't clear public-shareholder day-one accretion hurdles, without issuing dilutive equity. Management was explicit that it will only scale this if it supports AFFO/share for public holders.

This is a real, well-structured answer to the dilution problem, and the Q1 2026 AFFO acceleration to +6.6% is an early positive data point. But the fee streams are nascent, and the magnitude that flows to per-share growth is unproven over multiple quarters. Until the platform demonstrably bends the AFFO/share curve, growth prospects remain modest.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing GAAP P/E of ~50x is not the relevant metric. On AFFO, the stock trades at roughly 14x forward P/AFFO ($60.40 against the $4.41-$4.44 FY2026 AFFO guide), at the low end of the company's historical range and consistent with a fairly-valued, slow-per-share-growth net-lease REIT.

Direct retail-REIT peer comparison is imperfect because the listed comparables (SPG, KIM, REG, FRT, BRX) are shopping-center/mall REITs with different cap structures and a median GAAP P/E of 22.5 and median yield of 3.9%. O's 5.2% yield is well above the peer median, reflecting both its higher payout discipline and the market's discount for the per-share-growth concern. Against closer net-lease comps (Agree, NNN, W.P. Carey, Essential Properties), O typically commands a modest premium for scale and credit quality, partly offset by its dilution overhang.

The yield itself provides absolute support: a 5.2% covered monthly dividend with 31+ years of growth is a meaningful floor for income buyers, and the spread to the 10-year Treasury is reasonable. But there is no specific catalyst to argue the market is mispricing the equity meaningfully to the upside. The valuation is fair, not cheap. A low multiple on a structurally slow-growth business is appropriate, not an asymmetric opportunity, which is why we resist treating the 14x P/AFFO as a deep-value signal. The realistic re-rating case requires the private-capital platform to prove it can compound AFFO/share at a higher rate, which is a 2-3 year proof, not a near-term event.

Analyst targets range from $61.50 (low) to $75.00 (high), with a mean of $68.15 and median $68.50 — implying ~13% upside to mean from the current price, most of which is total return inclusive of the dividend rather than capital appreciation.

## Competitive Landscape

Net-lease REITs are spread investors: profitability hinges on acquiring assets at cap rates above cost of capital. O's durable advantages are real:
- **Lowest-in-class cost of capital** (A3/A-), reinforced by innovative liability management (a $694M municipal-prepay term loan swapped to a 4.34% blended cost; euro debt ~100bps cheaper than USD, serving as a natural FX hedge).
- **A fully integrated European net-lease platform** that competitors cannot quickly replicate; management describes European markets as "more fragmented and less crowded," offering better duration, credit, and spread, which explains the international acquisition tilt.
- **Scale and diversification** that limit single-tenant exposure and support proprietary, data-driven underwriting.

The competitive trajectory is stable-to-slightly-improving: the private-capital ecosystem, if it scales, widens the moat by adding an asset-management dimension that pure spread investors lack. The principal competitive vulnerability is that a high-rate environment compresses the spread advantage, and a discounted equity price raises the cost of the public-market funding O has historically depended on — precisely the constraint the private-capital pivot is designed to escape.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Tenant credit concentration in stressed retail.** The most important company-specific risk. Top tenants include Walgreens (3.1% of ABR, with a going-private/store-closure overhang), Dollar General (3.2%), Family Dollar (2.6%), and CVS (1.1%). 2025 real-estate impairments rose to $434.5M (+$115.5M YoY), "primarily due to clients in bankruptcy or experiencing financial distress." Mitigants are active: 103.9% rent recapture, $48.9M of 2025 termination income (guided $45-50M for 2026), and a lowered Q1 2026 credit-loss outlook to ~40bps of rental revenue — an improving tenant-health signal worth tracking each quarter.

2. **Per-share dilution risk.** The central bear thesis. Continued heavy ATM use (~$2.4B in 2025; ~$1.4B forward equity outstanding) means absolute growth need not translate to per-share growth. The pivot to off-balance-sheet capital partly addresses this but does not eliminate the risk.

3. **Rate sensitivity / bond-proxy behavior.** As a high-yield REIT, O trades inversely to long rates. With ~$2.4B of notes refinanced annually at higher coupons, a higher-for-longer rate path is a direct, ongoing spread headwind. [unverified — the specific recent macro backdrop of elevated H2 inflation expectations and a two-sided Fed path is not supported by the research brief.]

4. **Strategy-drift / execution risk in new verticals.** Expansion into gaming, data centers (credit-to-ownership), and a near-doubling of the loans & preferred book to $3.1B (from $1.5B) carries "limited experience" risk flagged explicitly in the 10-K. The CityCenter Las Vegas position is an $800M preferred-equity stake only — Blackstone owns the common equity and MGM operates — so it is a yield instrument, not casino ownership.

5. **Private-capital/JV conflicts and complexity.** The new fund/JV ecosystem introduces deal-allocation conflicts, fee-structure scrutiny, partner-redemption risk, and added REIT-qualification complexity.

6. **Governance note.** A March 2, 2026 8-K (Item 5.02, officer departure/election) could not be retrieved; the nature of the change should be confirmed. Insider activity is otherwise routine — equity grants, tax-withholding dispositions, and small discretionary sales (CLO Bushore 7,400 sh @ $62.42; Director McLaughlin 3,275 sh @ $61.87), with no open-market purchases. Pay-for-performance alignment is reasonable, anchored on AFFO/share, relative TSR, leverage, coverage, occupancy, and dividend growth, with demonstrated downside sensitivity (PEO comp-actually-paid fell to $9.5M in the weak 2023 TSR year).

### Options Market Signal

The near-term ATM IV reading of 182.4% in the one-week tenor is a thin-options artifact (historical volatility is 16.4%) and should be treated as non-informative — the one-month and three-month tenors at 16.0% and 17.0% are consistent with normal realized vol. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.7%), and the put/call OI ratio fell from 0.55 to 0.42 since early May, a mild reduction in hedging demand. Short interest is low at 4.7% of float (6.4 days to cover), indicating no meaningful bearish positioning. The options market is not pricing imminent fundamental stress beyond ordinary rate-driven volatility.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The private-capital platform scales successfully, adding recurring fee income that re-rates O from a ~14x spread investor toward a hybrid operator/asset-manager multiple. AFFO/share growth accelerates from the 2.1% (2025) low toward the +6.6% pace shown in Q1 2026 and holds. Tenant credit-loss guidance continues to improve (40bps and falling), European spread advantages persist, and the dividend compounds. In this case the stock works toward the high analyst target of $75.00, a ~24% capital gain plus the 5.2% yield.

### Bear Case
The dilution treadmill continues: a discounted equity currency and rising interest expense compress acquisition spreads, holding per-share growth near 2%. A major tenant bankruptcy (Walgreens, dollar stores) drives impairments higher than recapture can offset. A higher-for-longer rate environment pressures the bond-proxy multiple. The private-capital fee streams prove too small to move the needle. In this case the stock derates toward the low analyst target of $61.50 and the total return is essentially the dividend, with a downside scenario toward the low-$50s if rates spike and the AFFO multiple compresses to ~12x ($53).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** Realty Income is worth owning for its covered, growing 5.2% monthly dividend and fortress credit profile, but lacks the per-share-growth catalyst or valuation asymmetry required for a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of AFFO/share growth at or above 5%, demonstrating the private-capital platform is bending the per-share curve.
- Forward P/AFFO compresses below ~12x while fundamentals hold (e.g., a rate-driven selloff to the low-$50s), restoring valuation asymmetry against the covered yield.
- Credit-loss guidance falling below 30bps with stabilization or resolution of the Walgreens overhang.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A dividend cut or AFFO payout ratio rising above ~90%.
- AFFO/share guidance cut, or per-share AFFO growth turning negative.
- A material tenant bankruptcy (top-5 client) where impairments materially exceed recapture, or net debt/EBITDAre rising durably above ~6.0x.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026), with particular attention to AFFO/share trajectory, credit-loss guidance, and any disclosed fee income from the private-capital vehicles. Confirm the nature of the March 2026 Item 5.02 officer change.

**Base case (12 months):** ~$66, applying ~15x to the $4.42 FY2026 AFFO midpoint plus the 5.2% dividend, for a total return of roughly 14-15% from $60.40 — predominantly income and re-rating to the analyst mean area. Bull case $75 (+24% capital). Bear case $53 (-12% capital), partly cushioned by the yield.

## Conclusion

Realty Income remains the gold-standard net-lease REIT — best-in-class credit, deep diversification, an irreplaceable European platform, and a covered 5.2% monthly dividend with a 31-year growth streak. The score trajectory (6.8 → 6.3) has reflected the market's growing focus on the per-share-growth squeeze, and our current 5.85 holds that view: the franchise quality is intact, but the fundamental constraint (1.3% same-store growth, rising interest expense, historical dilution) caps the appreciation case. The Q1 2026 AFFO beat-and-raise and the improving credit-loss outlook are genuine positives, and the private-capital pivot is a thoughtful, well-structured answer to the dilution problem, but it is unproven over time and not yet sufficient to justify a re-rating. At ~14x forward P/AFFO, the stock is fairly valued. Own it for the income, monitor the AFFO/share trajectory and the private-capital fee ramp, and revisit on either a meaningful acceleration in per-share growth or a rate-driven price dislocation. **HOLD.**
