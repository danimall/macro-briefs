**Research Date: June 8, 2026**

## Executive Summary

American Airlines is the largest U.S. network carrier by fleet, and the most financially fragile of the big three. The thesis here is dominated by capital structure: negative stockholders' equity of -$3.73B at year-end 2025, approximately $28.6B of total debt against a $9.0B equity market cap, and free cash flow of -$680M in 2025. The equity is, in practical terms, a levered claim on the AAdvantage loyalty franchise and the Citi co-brand cash flows that sit on top of a thin, cyclical airline operating margin (2.7% in 2025).

There is a genuine improvement to acknowledge. Q1 2026 showed a demand-led recovery — revenue up 10.8%, passenger unit revenue (PRASM) up 6.5%, yield up 5.6% — which suggests the 2024 distribution-strategy misstep and the 2025 trough (an 86.8% net income collapse to $111M) were largely transitory. Estimate revisions have turned sharply positive off that depressed base: current-quarter consensus moved from -$0.04 to +$0.01 over 30 days, and current-year from -$0.10 to -$0.06. Management reaffirmed aggressive Q2 2026 guidance (revenue +13.5–16.5% YoY, potential return to adjusted profitability), and UBS raised its target to $18 on corporate-travel strength.

I am not persuaded that this is enough to own the equity. The improvement in unit revenue collides with two specific, dated headwinds the market has reason to price negatively. First, fuel has flipped from a 2025 tailwind (-8.2% price) to a 2026 headwind (+10.7% in Q1), and with zero hedging each 1¢/gallon move costs roughly $45M annually — IATA halved its 2026 industry profit forecast to $23B citing exactly this. Second, the company's profit engine, the Citi co-brand agreement, faces interchange and loyalty-program regulation (Credit Card Competition Act) that AAL's own 10-K says could "fundamentally alter the profitability" of that agreement — and that IP is pledged as loan collateral. With trailing P/E of 44x on near-zero earnings, no dividend, no insider buying, and negative book equity, the risk/reward does not compensate for the balance-sheet fragility. I maintain AVOID.

## Company Overview

American Airlines Group operates the largest U.S. network airline by fleet (~1,580–1,594 aircraft) on a hub-and-spoke model anchored at Charlotte, Chicago O'Hare, Dallas/Fort Worth, Los Angeles, Miami, New York, Philadelphia, Phoenix, and Washington National, plus a London Heathrow gateway. Regional flying runs under "American Eagle" through wholly-owned and third-party carriers on capacity purchase agreements.

Revenue breaks into three pillars: Passenger ($49.6B in 2025, ~91%), Cargo ($839M), and Other ($4.2B). The "Other" line is the strategically critical piece because it is dominated by the AAdvantage loyalty program — co-branded credit card and partner cash remuneration was $6.2B in 2025, and these are the company's highest-margin cash flows. International reach is built through joint businesses (transatlantic with British Airways/Iberia/Aer Lingus/Finnair; transpacific with JAL; Qantas for Australia/NZ) and alliances. Relative to Delta and United, American carries greater Latin America exposure and lighter Asia exposure.

The competitive position is that of a scale operator carrying the heaviest leverage in its peer set. It competes against the lower-cost structures and stronger balance sheets of Delta and United, plus price pressure from low-cost and ultra-low-cost carriers that is increasingly reaching international long-haul.

## Financial Analysis

**2025 was a profitability trough.** Revenue was essentially flat at $54.63B versus $54.21B in 2024 (+0.8%). Operating income fell 43.9% to $1,467M, an operating margin of just 2.7%. Net income collapsed 86.8% to $111M, a net margin of 0.2%, from $846M the prior year. The drivers were structural cost resets: salaries/wages/benefits rose 9.6% (+$1.5B from 2024 labor deals) and regional expenses rose 8.1%, only partially offset by fuel falling 6.1% on an 8.2% price decline. The cost base stepped up permanently while pricing stayed flat — a margin squeeze with one transitory tailwind (fuel) that has since reversed.

**The balance sheet is the central concern.** Stockholders' equity was negative -$3.73B at year-end 2025 (improving from -$5.80B in 2022 but still a deficit), with an accumulated retained deficit of -$6.7B. Total debt is approximately $28.6B (long-term $25.25B). The Altman Z-score of 2.72 sits in the "grey zone," and the company carries a non-investment-grade rating. The current ratio of 0.5 is structurally normal for airlines given large deferred-revenue liabilities (air-traffic $7.2B plus loyalty $10.6B), so I do not over-weight it, but the absolute debt load against a $9B equity cap is the defining fact of this investment.

**Free cash flow was negative -$680M in 2025** (operating cash flow $3.1B minus capex $3.78B), with operating cash flow down $0.9B year-over-year on lower profitability. Deleveraging is the explicit strategy and is progressing — prepayment of the $937M 10.75% secured notes and $487M of EETCs in 2025, then $1.0B of 8.50% notes and a $629M short-term loan in Q1 2026, with revolver maturities pushed to 2031 and interest expense down to $1.72B from $1.93B. But the deleveraging was funded partly through new equipment financing and a one-time Citi cash payment, not from organic FCF. With $17.5B of aircraft capex committed through 2030 (to be debt-financed), net deleveraging depends entirely on a sustained earnings recovery that has not yet been demonstrated across a full year.

**Q1 2026 confirms the demand recovery is real.** Revenue rose 10.8% to $13.9B, PRASM +6.5%, yield +5.6%, load factor 81.3%. The seasonal net loss narrowed to -$382M from -$473M, and liquidity rose to $10.8B. This is the strongest evidence for the bull case and I weight it accordingly. The offsetting fact is fuel: price rose 10.7% to $2.75/gallon, and with no hedging the P&L sensitivity is unusually high.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story is an inflection off a trough rather than secular expansion, and that distinction matters for how to read the estimate revisions. Consensus has moved sharply: current-quarter EPS revised +132% over 30 days (from -$0.04 to +$0.01), next-quarter +29.6%, current-year +38.2% (from -$0.10 to -$0.06). On the surface this resembles the inflection signal that normally favors a constructive view.

I am cautious about over-reading it for three reasons. First, the 90-day revisions are catastrophically negative (current-year -103%, current-quarter -99%), which means the 30-day improvement is a recovery from a collapse, not clean upward momentum — consensus was cut hard, then nudged back up. Next-year EPS, the cleaner read on normalized power, was revised *down* 17.2% over 90 days to $2.23. Second, the recovery in unit revenue is partly a normalization from self-inflicted 2024 distribution missteps (since reversed) and one-off 2025 hits (the Flight 5342 crash, ~$200M Q1'25 revenue; the Q4'25 government shutdown, ~$325M). Recovering lost share is real but is not the same as durable above-trend growth. Third, the fuel headwind directly threatens the conversion of revenue growth into the profitability the bull case requires.

There is genuine option value in the NOLs: $11.9B of federal net operating losses make incremental profit nearly tax-free for years, which materially amplifies the equity value of any earnings recovery. This is the strongest structural argument for the bulls, and it is why the board maintains the ownership-restricting Tax Benefit Preservation Plan. But it is leverage on an earnings recovery that must first materialize against a rising fuel bill.

## Valuation Assessment

Headline trailing and forward P/E both sit at 44x, on near-zero EPS of $0.17 — a meaningless ratio at a trough. The more relevant frame is normalized earnings power. Next-year consensus of $2.23 implies a forward multiple of roughly 6.1x at $13.50, which on its face looks cheap. The peer set, however, shows why a low multiple is not sufficient: Air Lease trades at 7.0x and SkyWest at 8.1x, while the loss-making and recovering carriers (ALK 69x, JBLU and ALGT negative) carry no meaningful earnings multiple. The peer median P/E of 8.1x suggests AAL on $2.23 next-year EPS is not screaming cheap relative to its sector once you account for the fact that the sector is itself depressed.

The decisive valuation issue is enterprise value, not equity multiple. Adding ~$28.6B total debt to the $9.0B equity cap gives an enterprise value near $37–38B against 2025 operating income of $1.47B — roughly 25x EV/EBIT on trough operating profit. The equity is a thin sliver on top of a large, fixed debt claim, which means small swings in operating profit produce large swings in equity value in both directions. That is the definition of a levered call option, and it cuts against paying up here: the negative book equity means there is no asset-value floor under the stock if the earnings recovery stalls.

Analyst targets span $10 (low) to $24 (high), mean $15.53, median $15.00 — implying roughly 11–15% upside to the median from $13.50. UBS at $18 is the constructive outlier on corporate travel. I treat these as a wide, uncertain band rather than a floor, because at this leverage the distribution of outcomes is genuinely two-sided and the targets reflect that disagreement. The valuation does not offer the asymmetric upside that would justify owning the balance-sheet risk.

## Competitive Landscape

American holds scale and an irreplaceable hub network, but its competitive trajectory within the big three is the relevant question, and it is stable-to-slightly-negative. It lags Delta and United on both margin and balance-sheet strength, the two attributes that matter most in a fuel-cost-up environment. The differentiation is the AAdvantage/Citi loyalty franchise and a relatively young narrowbody fleet financed via EETCs.

Domestic pricing power returned in late 2025/early 2026 as industry capacity discipline held and corporate travel recovered — the Q1 PRASM +6.5% is evidence American is recovering corporate share it ceded during the 2024 distribution misstep. That is a real positive on trajectory. But the structural position remains that of the most-levered network carrier facing a sector-wide fuel shock, where the strongest balance sheets (Delta, United) are best positioned to absorb cost inflation and the weakest is most exposed. IATA halving its 2026 industry profit forecast to $23B (from $41B, versus ~$45B in 2025) is a sector headwind that pressures AAL disproportionately.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are specific, identifiable, and not, in my view, fully priced relative to a stock trading near the middle of its range on improving sentiment.

1. **Leverage and liquidity covenants.** ~$28.6B debt plus $17.5B of committed aircraft capex through 2030. The company must maintain ≥$2.0B unrestricted cash, the AAdvantage financing carries a debt-service coverage ratio, and there is extensive cross-default/cross-acceleration exposure. Negative book equity removes any asset-value cushion.

2. **Credit-card holdback.** If financial condition deteriorates and triggers liquidity covenants, card processors can withhold up to 100% of advance ticket sales — a mechanism that can rapidly impair liquidity in a downturn. This is a reflexive risk that worsens precisely when conditions worsen.

3. **Loyalty/interchange regulation.** The Credit Card Competition Act and proposed interchange/interest-rate caps could, per the 10-K, "fundamentally alter the profitability" of the Citi agreement — the profit engine. AAdvantage IP is pledged as collateral, so regulatory impairment of the franchise also impairs loan security. This is the single largest tail risk and it is regulatory, not operational.

4. **Zero fuel hedging into a fuel shock.** $45M annual expense per 1¢/gallon. Fuel already turned headwind in Q1 (+10.7%), and with no hedging the upside cost tail remains open. This directly threatens the Q2 profitability guidance.

5. **Supplier and litigation exposure.** Boeing/Airbus/Embraer/Bombardier concentration, tariff risk on Brazilian-built regional jets, the Flight 5342 wrongful-death suits (trial April 2027), and ongoing NEA/JetBlue antitrust class actions (motion to dismiss denied).

### Options Market Signal

The options market is roughly balanced rather than confirming acute bearishness. Near-term ATM IV is 55.2% versus 48.6% historical, modestly rich (+6.7pp), with a flat term structure (near 55.2% ≈ far 55.9%) and put/call skew near zero (0.1%). IV has fallen sharply from 71.2% to 55.2% since May 22, consistent with resolved event uncertainty rather than building stress. The put/call OI ratio of 1.26 and the 12.9% short interest (1.6 days to cover) indicate meaningful institutional bearish positioning, but the low days-to-cover and management's own disclosure of "short squeeze" volatility mean upside squeeze risk is real. The 3-month and 6-month tenors show negative skew (-1.9% and -3.9%), indicating the market does price more downside tail risk further out — consistent with the fuel and regulatory concerns. Net read: the options market is not screaming sell, which keeps me from treating this as an active short despite the AVOID rating.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The 2025 collapse was a one-time trough driven by labor-contract cost steps, the 2024 distribution misstep (since reversed), the Flight 5342 crash, and the Q4 government shutdown. Q1 2026 PRASM +6.5% proves demand and pricing power are back. If Q2 hits the reaffirmed +13.5–16.5% revenue guide and turns adjusted-profitable, normalized EPS toward $2.23 next year on a near-tax-free basis (via the $11.9B NOLs) justifies a re-rating toward the $18 UBS target, roughly +33% from $13.50. Continued deleveraging and revolver extensions to 2031 reduce refinancing risk. Bull case: $18–24.

### Bear Case
Fuel rises through 2026 with no hedging, compressing the thin margin and pushing 2026 back toward a loss; IATA's halved industry outlook proves the binding constraint. Interchange/loyalty regulation advances and impairs the Citi franchise that anchors equity value. With negative book equity and $28.6B debt, any earnings shortfall amplifies into the equity. The stock retraces toward the low-$10s (the 52-week low was $10.09 and the analyst low target is $10.00), roughly -26% from current. Bear case: $10.

The asymmetry is roughly +33% to bull versus -26% to bear, but the bull case requires both a clean fuel environment and no regulatory progress, while the bear case requires only one of two highly plausible headwinds to bite. That weighting is what keeps me negative.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The nearest dated event is Q2 2026 earnings (late July), where the aggressive +13.5–16.5% revenue guide meets a +10.7% fuel cost on zero hedging. A guide-down or a fuel-driven margin miss would pressure the stock. Further out, any legislative movement on the Credit Card Competition Act is a tail catalyst with no fixed date.

**Timing window.** Q2 earnings in roughly six to eight weeks is the cleanest window. The regulatory catalyst is open-ended and not reliably tradeable on a short timeframe.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is already 12.9% of float with only 1.6 days to cover. Management explicitly flags short-squeeze volatility in its risk factors, and the stock has demonstrated sharp upside moves (it rose ~20% over the trailing price window despite the leverage concerns). This is meaningful squeeze risk against a crowded-but-shallow short base.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options are roughly balanced (skew 0.1% near-term), IV has compressed from 71% to 55%, and the put/call volume ratio of 0.70 shows no acute bearish urgency. The options market is not confirming a high-conviction short thesis.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target ~$10 (-26%); squeeze upside risk realistically +20–30% on any positive Q2 surprise. That is roughly 1:1, not the 2:1 a short requires.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The fundamental concerns are real but the mechanics are unfavorable — low days-to-cover, documented squeeze behavior, balanced options positioning, and a recently improving revenue trajectory. This is a stock to avoid owning, not to short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (what would move me to HOLD or better):**
- Two consecutive quarters of positive free cash flow with continued debt paydown funded organically rather than via new equipment financing, demonstrating the deleveraging is self-sustaining.
- Q2/Q3 2026 results that confirm sustained adjusted profitability *with* the higher fuel cost absorbed (PRASM growth outpacing CASM-ex growth), proving the demand recovery converts to durable margin rather than being a fuel-tailwind artifact.
- Clear resolution or de-escalation of interchange/loyalty-program regulatory risk, removing the largest tail threat to the AAdvantage/Citi franchise.
- Evidence of open-market insider buying, which is currently absent — insiders have only received grants and sold for taxes.

**Timeline.** The balance-sheet and fuel-exposure headwinds are structural and likely persist through at least 2026–2027 given the $17.5B committed capex and zero-hedging policy. The regulatory overhang has no defined resolution date. I would expect to revisit at Q2 2026 earnings (late July), and again after Q3, to assess whether the demand recovery is converting to durable, fuel-adjusted profitability.

## Conclusion

American Airlines has improved since the prior several reports — the Q1 2026 demand recovery is real, estimate revisions have turned positive off a depressed base, and management's reaffirmed Q2 guidance points toward a possible return to adjusted profitability. I have raised the underlying scores modestly to reflect that the operating trough appears to be behind the company. But the equity remains a thin, levered claim on the AAdvantage/Citi franchise sitting atop negative book equity, $28.6B of debt, negative trailing free cash flow, and a zero-hedge fuel exposure into a confirmed sector-wide fuel shock that IATA expects to halve 2026 industry profits. The largest single risk, regulation of the loyalty/interchange engine, is structurally pledged as collateral and unpriced.

The forward multiple of ~6x next-year EPS looks cheap in isolation, but the enterprise-value math (~25x EV/EBIT on trough operating profit) and the absence of any asset-value floor mean the cheapness is a function of risk, not mispricing. The +33% upside to the bull target is conditional on two favorable outcomes (benign fuel and no regulatory progress); the -26% downside requires only one adverse outcome. That asymmetry, combined with no income support and no insider conviction buying, leaves the risk/reward unattractive. I maintain **AVOID**, while noting the path to a HOLD upgrade is now clearer than it was a quarter ago and runs through two quarters of fuel-adjusted profitability and self-funded deleveraging.
