**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Kaiser Aluminum has delivered a real and accelerating earnings recovery. Q1 2026 net sales rose 42% YoY to $1,107M, Adjusted EBITDA grew 75% to $128.5M, and diluted EPS nearly tripled to $3.71 from $1.31, with all four end markets growing for the first time in several quarters. The aerospace destocking that depressed FY2025 volumes appears to be ending, and the two large capacity projects (Trentwood Phase VII plate expansion and the Warrick fourth coating line) are shifting from a drag on results to a contributor. Current-year consensus EPS is up 36.4% over 90 days to $10.28, and the company has beaten EPS in three of the last four quarters, including a 97% beat in Q1 2026.

The complication is that a large share of the headline improvement is aluminum-price-driven rather than volume- or margin-driven. Kaiser operates a "metal price neutrality" model, passing through the underlying aluminum index and earning a conversion margin. Conversion Revenue, the cleaner measure of underlying business activity, was essentially flat in FY2025 ($1,453M vs $1,456M), meaning most reported revenue growth was metal-price inflation. Adjusted EBITDA also carries a favorable, non-cash "Metal Price Lag" benefit of roughly $93M in FY2025 and $36M in Q1 2026 alone, which reverses when aluminum prices fall. Normalizing for this, the underlying improvement is solid but considerably less dramatic than the headline EPS suggests.

The stock has more than doubled over the trailing year, from $77.84 in June 2025 to $190.63, and now trades above the entire published sell-side price-target range ($142–$176). Insiders sold into the rally at $173–178 in late April with no offsetting open-market buying, UBS initiated at Neutral in May citing valuation and balance-sheet constraints, and the trailing P/E of roughly 21x sits at a level where further re-rating requires the metal tailwind to persist. We rate KALU a HOLD. The business is improving, but the price has caught up to and exceeded the fundamentals, and the risk/reward is balanced.

## Company Overview

Kaiser Aluminum manufactures semi-fabricated specialty aluminum mill products (flat-rolled plate, sheet and coil, extrusions, and drawn products) for four end markets where multi-year customer qualification cycles create meaningful entry barriers:

- **Packaging (~$1,490M FY25, ~44% of sales):** Bare and coated can/food stock from the Warrick, IN mill, roughly 75% coated (higher margin). The largest and fastest-growing segment, supported by the secular substitution of recyclable aluminum for plastic and glass.
- **Aero/HS Products (~$838M, ~25%):** Heat-treated plate/sheet and hard-alloy extrusions for aerospace and defense, concentrated at Trentwood, WA. High-margin but cyclical, with long supply-chain lead times that amplify demand swings.
- **GE Products (~$759M, ~23%):** General engineering 6000-series products sold largely through service centers, tied to industrial, semiconductor, defense, and reshoring demand.
- **Automotive Extrusions (~$286M, ~8%):** Lightweighting structural components for tier-one auto suppliers.

The defining feature of the business is the metal-neutrality model. Kaiser earns conversion margin and passes the aluminum index cost through to customers, which makes Conversion Revenue and Adjusted EBITDA the relevant operating metrics; headline revenue inflates and deflates purely with aluminum prices. The company employs roughly 3,840 people, 65% unionized (primarily USW), and competes with substantially larger players: Arconic, Constellium, and Novelis in aero/packaging, Tri-Arrows in packaging, and Norsk Hydro in GE/auto.

## Financial Analysis

The earnings trajectory shows a clear recovery from the 2022 loss year:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1'25 | Q1'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | $3,087M | $3,024M | $3,373M | $777M | $1,107M (+42%) |
| Adj. EBITDA | — | $241M | $310M | $73.4M | $128.5M (+75%) |
| Net income | $47M | $47–66M* | $112.5M | $21.6M | $62.5M |
| Diluted EPS | — | — | $6.77 | $1.31 | $3.71 |

*FY2024 net income reported at $46.8M (XBRL) and $65.7M (MD&A, as-adjusted under WAC).

Three points temper the headline strength. First, the underlying volume picture in FY2025 was weak: FY25 volume fell roughly 5% on the Trentwood outage and aerospace destocking, and most of the reported sales growth was metal-price pass-through. The Q1 2026 improvement was higher quality, with all four markets growing on a volume-adjusted basis. Second, the Metal Price Lag is a non-cash benefit from rising aluminum prices that mechanically reverses if prices decline, so recent EBITDA should not be extrapolated as a clean run-rate. Third, FY2025 free cash flow was negative $25.5M (OCF of $111.4M against $136.9M capex) while the company paid roughly $51M in dividends.

The cash-flow picture improved materially in Q1 2026: OCF of $87.9M against capex of just $19.4M, no revolver borrowings (zero drawn), and total liquidity of $596.3M. With 2026 capex guided down to $120–130M from $181M in 2024 as the big expansion projects complete, FY2026 should swing to positive free cash flow if the Q1 momentum holds. Leverage remains elevated: D/E of 2.1x, ~$1.05B in fixed-rate senior notes, and an Altman Z of 2.68 in the grey zone, which is typical for a capital-intensive metals processor rather than a distress signal. The November 2025 refinancing pushed the 2028 note maturity to 2034 but raised the coupon from 4.625% to 5.875%, increasing interest cost.

One technical note for cross-period comparability: effective January 1, 2025, Kaiser switched inventory valuation from LIFO to WAC, applied retrospectively, so third-party databases may show inconsistent prior-period figures.

## Growth Analysis

The growth signal is positive and broad-based. Q1 2026 saw all four end markets grow sales: Aero/HS +34%, Packaging +59%, GE +32%, and Auto +22%, with shipments growing in two of four (Aero +9%, Packaging +13%; GE -2%, Auto -8% on shipments but up sharply on price). The end of aerospace destocking is the most important durable driver: long supply-chain lead times mean the destock that hurt FY2025 hard should reverse to Kaiser's benefit as Boeing/Airbus build rates recover. Packaging's plastic-to-aluminum substitution provides a steadier secular tailwind, and the Warrick coating line plus Trentwood Phase VII add capacity into that demand.

Estimate revisions support the recovery. Current-year consensus EPS rose 36.4% over 90 days, and next-year EPS rose 19.1% to $10.86. Near-term revisions are still positive but decelerating (current quarter +7.6% over 90 days, +1.9% over 30 days), consistent with the bulk of the metal-price re-rating having already flowed through. The earnings-surprise record is strong (Q1 2026 +97.1%, Q3 2025 +106%, Q2 2025 +55.1%), with one modest miss in Q4 2025 (-3.8%).

A meaningful slice of the revision velocity reflects higher aluminum prices and the Metal Price Lag benefit, not pure conversion-margin or volume growth. The +36% current-year revision would normally be a powerful standalone buy signal, but here it is partly a commodity-price mark rather than a clean recovery in unit economics. Growth prospects are real but should be discounted for this commodity content.

## Valuation Assessment

At $190.63, KALU trades at roughly 21x trailing EPS and roughly 18.5x consensus FY2026 EPS. On reported numbers that is not extreme. The problem is what underpins those numbers. A material portion of trailing and forward earnings rests on the Metal Price Lag tailwind and elevated aluminum prices, which are cyclical and mean-reverting. Normalizing EBITDA for the lag benefit produces a meaningfully higher effective multiple on sustainable earnings power, which is the appropriate lens for a metal-neutral processor at what looks like a favorable point in the aluminum cycle.

Peer context is mixed and not especially helpful given the sector's heterogeneity: same-cap Materials names (Ashland, Olin, Chemours) carry negative earnings; Hawkins trades at 40x; Graphic Packaging at 11.6x. The peer median P/E of 40x flatters KALU's 21x, but those comparables are not aluminum semi-fabricators and the comparison is weak. The more relevant external anchor is the published price-target range: high $176, low $142, mean $159, median $160, and the stock trades above the top of the range. UBS preferred Constellium in its May initiation. Insiders sold below the current quote.

The guardrail on treating analyst targets as floors applies in reverse here: this is not a cheap stock relative to growth where consensus lags. The stock has already exceeded the full target range, and the catalyst that drove the run (the metal tailwind plus destock recovery) is largely priced in. Forward P/E below the growth rate would justify a higher score, but the growth rate is contaminated by commodity price moves. We score valuation 4.5: the multiple is not stretched on reported earnings but is unattractive on normalized earnings, and the price sits above every published target.

## Competitive Landscape

Kaiser's competitive position is solid and arguably improving at the margin, which supports a score above the midpoint. Its moat rests on multi-year customer qualification cycles in aerospace and defense, KaiserSelect quality differentiation, and its position as one of the few remaining US-based producers of heat-treated aerospace plate. The two capacity projects expand its higher-margin coated-packaging and aerospace-plate footprint into segments with durable demand. The reshoring of industrial supply chains and US aerospace/defense build favors domestic producers.

The trajectory is positive but capped by scale disadvantage. Kaiser competes against substantially larger players plus imported heat-treat plate. Customer concentration is meaningful (top five customers at 56% of sales, largest at 16%), and the company depends on Alcoa for potable water and utilities at Warrick, a single-point operational vulnerability. The prior trajectory showed Competitive Position drifting down from 7.5 to 7.0; we hold at 6.8, reflecting genuine capacity additions into structurally favored end markets against the constraints of scale and concentration.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks not fully reflected in the price are material:

1. **Metal Price Lag reversal.** The EBITDA tailwind reverses when aluminum prices fall. This is the largest unpriced risk to forward earnings: a normalization in aluminum would compress reported EBITDA even with stable volumes.
2. **Aerospace destocking amplification.** Lead times over a year mean demand swings hit Kaiser earlier and harder than its customers. Any renewed Boeing/Airbus build-rate disruption would hit a high-margin segment.
3. **Leverage and refinancing cost.** The higher 5.875% coupon increases interest expense against negative FY25 FCF and the dividend.
4. **Labor concentration.** 65% unionized; several contracts expired or expiring within 12 months (Florence AL, Kalamazoo MI, London ON, Los Angeles). The USW holds the right to nominate 40% of the board through 2030, an unusual governance feature.
5. **Concentration and single-point dependencies.** Warrick's reliance on Alcoa for utilities; environmental contingencies (~$14.2M above accrual) and a ~$4.6M Sherman, TX multiemployer pension withdrawal in 2027.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV of 49.2% sits above one-year HV of 44.8% (rich by 4.4pp), the term structure is in backwardation (49.2% near-term vs 46.4% at six months), and the near-dated put/call skew is elevated at 5.8% with a put/call volume ratio of 1.40 and OI ratio of 1.50. The implied move for the nearest expiry is ±5.0%. ATM IV rose 5.4pp since late May (43.8% → 49.2%) even as the price ticked up 1.9%. Rising IV, backwardation, and elevated put demand together indicate the market is hedging an imminent move, consistent with the stock sitting at the top of its range. The skew flips negative at longer tenors (-3.7% at six months), suggesting the near-term hedging is event-driven rather than a structural bearish view. For a HOLD, the signal reinforces caution on near-term entry timing rather than challenging the thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($230–250):** Aerospace destocking ends decisively, all four end markets sustain volume growth, Trentwood and Warrick capacity ramp into strong demand, and aluminum prices stay elevated so the Metal Price Lag persists. Capex steps down to ~$125M, FCF swings solidly positive, leverage falls, and the market re-rates the FCF improvement. FY26 EPS exceeds the $10.28 consensus and the multiple holds at ~22x, supporting a high-$220s to $250 valuation.

**Bear Case ($120–135, -29% to -37%):** Aluminum prices normalize, the Metal Price Lag tailwind reverses and turns into a headwind, and a fresh aerospace build-rate disruption hits the high-margin Aero/HS segment. Conversion Revenue stays flat while the commodity-driven EPS reverses. The stock de-rates toward normalized earnings power and trades back toward the lower end of the analyst range and the pre-run-up congestion zone.

**Base Case ($165–185):** Underlying volumes improve modestly, the metal tailwind partially fades, FY26 FCF turns positive, and EPS lands near or slightly above consensus. The stock holds in a band near the published target range, with limited upside given it already trades above the high target and below the level where a commodity reversal would force a de-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, late July). The Q2 print will show whether the volume-led improvement in Q1 is durable and how much of the EBITDA gain persists as aluminum prices and the Metal Price Lag evolve.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-to-mid $130s with Conversion Revenue growing on volume (not metal price) for two consecutive quarters, restoring asymmetric upside.
- Confirmed FY2026 free-cash-flow inflection (positive FCF run-rate) with leverage declining, demonstrating the capex step-down is converting to cash.
- Evidence the earnings improvement is structural rather than commodity-driven, i.e., conversion-margin expansion independent of the Metal Price Lag.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A sustained decline in aluminum prices that turns the Metal Price Lag into a material headwind while Conversion Revenue stays flat.
- A renewed aerospace destocking signal (Aero/HS shipments declining QoQ) or a Boeing/Airbus build-rate cut.
- Negative FCF persisting into FY2026 alongside continued dividend payments and rising interest cost.

## Conclusion

Kaiser is executing a genuine recovery, and the Q1 2026 results were strong on multiple dimensions, including volume growth across all four end markets and a cash-flow turn. But the headline numbers overstate the underlying improvement: Conversion Revenue was flat in FY2025, much of the reported growth is aluminum-price pass-through, and a meaningful slice of EBITDA is the non-cash, reversible Metal Price Lag. The stock has more than doubled in a year and now trades above the entire published price-target range, with insiders selling into the strength below the current price, a Neutral sell-side initiation citing valuation, and options markets pricing near-term stress.

The fundamentals are improving while the price has run past them, a balanced setup rather than an asymmetric one. We rate KALU a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The next clean read comes at Q2 2026 earnings, and the path to a more constructive view runs through either a pullback that restores asymmetry or evidence that the earnings gains are structural rather than commodity-driven.
