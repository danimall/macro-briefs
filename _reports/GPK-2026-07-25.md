**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Graphic Packaging is a global leader in fiber-based consumer packaging — folding cartons, beverage multipack carriers, cups, trays, and paperboard canisters — supplying blue-chip customers including Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, AB InBev, Kraft Heinz, Nestlé, and major QSRs across 100+ facilities in 20+ countries. The franchise is durable, vertically integrated, and levered to a real secular tailwind (plastic-to-fiber substitution). It is also in the middle of an acute, largely self-inflicted crisis. Over 2025 the stock fell roughly 50% (from ~$25 to ~$11); the CEO and CFO both resigned in November 2025; the board cancelled a major capital project and took a $40M write-off; and the company disclosed a material weakness in internal controls tied to former management making capital expenditures beyond board-authorized limits. Net leverage climbed to 4.18x against a covenant that had to be temporarily loosened to 5.00x, S&P downgraded the credit to BB, and multiple securities class actions are pending.

The financial data confirms the severity. Operating income fell 28% in 2025 to $804M, and Q1 2026 operating income collapsed 91% to $19M with a net loss of $(43)M versus +$127M a year earlier. Adjusted EBITDA came in at roughly $1.395B against February 2025 guidance of $1.68–1.78B — a ~$385M shortfall that is at the heart of the litigation. Management has pre-announced a "kitchen-sink" 2026 with ~$130M of inventory-reduction EBITDA drag and ~$100M of incentive-compensation accruals, so reported 2026 earnings will be weak by design. The trailing P/E near 12x looks cheap against a peer median of 15.5x, but at a cyclical/crisis trough the trailing multiple is close to meaningless; the investable question is normalized post-reset earnings power, which is currently unknowable.

The counterweight is genuine: incoming CEO Robbert Rietbroek and several refreshed-board directors bought stock in the open market near the lows (Rietbroek ~$501K at $11.32; directors Venturelli ~$199K, Stafeil ~$200K, and Hagemann ~$154K), a credible alignment signal that contrasts with the prior regime's selling before resigning. That does not resolve the two clocks now ticking: the covenant relief steps down to 4.75x by 3/31/27 and expires 6/30/27, and the International goodwill cushion sits at only ~2%. We maintain a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. The bull and bear cases both draw on the same facts, and the resolution depends on inputs not yet visible: a permanent CFO hire, covenant headroom trajectory, and Waco mill ramp economics.

## Company Overview

Graphic Packaging produces consumer-goods packaging from renewable and recycled fiber. It is vertically integrated in the Americas, manufacturing most of the paperboard it consumes (recycled, unbleached, and bleached grades), while buying most of its International board. The company employs ~23,000 people (~66% Americas, ~34% Europe/RoW; ~59% unionized).

Following a Q1 2025 realignment, the business reports in two segments plus corporate:

- **Americas Paperboard Packaging** — ~$5.9B 2025 sales, the profit engine, with 2025 segment operating income of $818M. This is where the consolidated franchise value sits.
- **International Paperboard Packaging** — ~$2.2B 2025 sales, thin margins ($139M 2025 operating income), heavily European, across food/beverage/health & beauty.
- **Corporate & Other**, including open-market paperboard sales and the Paperboard Manufacturing operating segment.

The competitive setup is a consolidated, generally rational industry. The largest competitor is Smurfit WestRock, whose formation created a larger scaled peer. GPK differentiates on sustainability and circularity, product innovation (3,100+ patents; KeelClip, Boardio, PaperSeal), integration, price, and execution. No single customer exceeds 10% of sales. The moat is real but modest, anchored by long-term pass-through supply contracts, integration economics, and IP, and offset by commodity input exposure and substitution risk from plastics and reusables.

Revenue is contracting and margins have compressed sharply.

The 2025 revenue decline of ~2% reflects the Augusta divestiture plus weaker bleached-board pricing and volume. More damaging was the drop in operating income, driven by lower packaging price/volume, ~$47M of commodity inflation, ~$106M of labor and benefit inflation, and the absence of a $75M prior-year Augusta gain. The Q1 2026 quarter absorbed ~$71M of special charges: a $40M write-off for cancelling the Kalamazoo/Texarkana automated roll-warehouse project, $18M of severance, and $13M of held-for-sale impairment. Americas segment operating income roughly halved ($228M to $130M), and International slipped to a small loss.

The balance sheet is the pressure point. Total debt is ~$5.6–5.8B against equity of $3.25B (D/E ~1.0x). Net leverage rose to 4.18x at 3/31/26 from 3.63x at year-end 2025, against a covenant that was raised to 5.00x in the February 2026 amendment. Altman Z sits at ~2.79 (grey zone), with the current ratio at 1.3. Roughly 31% of debt is floating, a live cost in the higher-for-longer rate environment. Cash-flow quality is softer than headline OCF suggests: the company relies on ~$847M of receivables factoring plus ~$271M of supply-chain financing and a supplier-finance program, all of which flatter reported operating cash flow and working capital and should be treated as quasi-debt. S&P downgraded the credit from BB+ to BB; Moody's holds Ba1 (stable).

On capital allocation, the multi-year story has been the Waco, TX recycled paperboard mill, which drove ~$1.2B of capex in 2024 and ~$0.9B in 2025 and started up in Q4 2025. Dividends run ~$128M/year (raised to $0.11/quarter, a ~4% yield). Buybacks were $150M in 2025, but the February 2026 covenant amendment caps repurchases at $65M/year through 9/30/27, a clear signal of financial constraint. The ~$1.715B remaining authorization is effectively frozen. The financial-health score of 3.8 reflects negative Q1 free cash flow, quasi-debt-flattered reported cash flow, a credit downgrade, and a covenant tightening path, partly offset by a fundamentally cash-generative asset base and manageable near-term maturities (the April 2026 notes were repaid via a $400M delayed-draw term loan drawn 4/14/26).

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth picture is negative by design. Management has pre-announced a reset year, so reported 2026 will look weak regardless of underlying demand. The restructuring is in motion: Middletown OH closed (May 2025), East Angus, Québec closed (Dec 2025), and the Kalamazoo/Texarkana project cancelled (Q1'26).

The forward case rests on three levers. First, the Waco mill ramping profitably, which converts the 2024–25 capex bulge into earnings as it scales. Second, inventory normalization — inventories have been building since 2023 and the destocking is the immediate drag but a prerequisite for margin recovery. Third, the secular fiber-substitution tailwind, which supports mid-cycle volume growth in beverage carriers, foodservice, and consumer categories moving off plastic. The earnings-surprise record is mixed: three beats and one miss over four quarters, with the Q4 2025 miss of -20.1% ($0.29 vs $0.36 estimate) marking the deterioration, followed by a Q1 2026 "beat" of +56% against a heavily reduced $0.06 estimate — a low-bar beat that says more about depressed expectations than recovery.

The estimate-revision signal here is negative-to-uncertain, not the strong positive-inflection pattern that would warrant an upgrade. We do not see the combination of strong positive estimate revisions plus accelerating revenue that flags a durable recovery. Instead, we see a franchise resetting downward with an option on normalized recovery once Waco ramps and inventories clear. Growth prospects score 4.3, up modestly from prior reads to reflect the credible restructuring path, but capped by the reset drag and the absence of confirmed volume reacceleration.

## Valuation Assessment

At $11.02, GPK trades at ~12x trailing earnings against a peer median of 15.5x. On a naive screen it looks cheap. The equity lessons here are directly relevant: a low multiple in Materials has historically been an anti-predictive value-trap flag rather than a buy signal, and the burden is on identifying why the market is wrong.

The honest answer is that the trailing multiple is close to uninformative. Trailing EPS of $1.48 spans a period that includes the earnings collapse but predates the full weight of the 2026 reset. Forward earnings are being deliberately suppressed by ~$230M of combined inventory-reduction and comp-accrual drag, plus restructuring charges. This is neither a clean cyclical trough (where you would normalize to mid-cycle EPS and buy the dislocation) nor a stable-earnings business (where the multiple is meaningful). It is a distressed reset where normalized post-2026 earnings power is genuinely unknowable until Waco's ramp economics and inventory normalization are visible.

The peer comparison illustrates the ambiguity. GPK's 12x sits above Sonoco (8.3x), near Sealed Air (12.2x), and below Crown (15.5x), Avery Dennison (17.9x), and PKG (27.3x). But GPK carries a materially worse risk profile than any of these — 4.18x net leverage, a BB downgrade, a controls material weakness, an interim CFO, and active litigation — none of which afflicted peers to the same degree. The discount to peers is warranted, not a mispricing. On the upside, if the business normalizes toward its 2023–24 earnings power (net income $650–720M, ~$2.20–2.40 EPS on the current share count), a re-rating to a mid-teens multiple would imply a stock well into the high teens. That is the bull's arithmetic, and it is real, but it is contingent on a clean deleveraging and covenant-clearing path that has not yet been demonstrated.

We score valuation 5.0 — cheap in absolute terms with genuine upside optionality, but the cheapness reflects real distress rather than an identifiable mispricing the market has missed. No published analyst price targets are available in the data, so we do not anchor to a consensus range. The 4% dividend yield provides modest support but is a secondary consideration given the constrained capital-return posture.

## Competitive Landscape

The industry structure is favorable — consolidated and generally disciplined — and GPK's franchise position is intact. Its customer roster (Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, AB InBev, Kraft Heinz, General Mills, Nestlé, plus McDonald's, Chick-fil-A, Chipotle) is among the best in fiber packaging. Vertical integration in the Americas and 3,100+ patents in sustainable-packaging innovation support a modest but real moat. The plastic-to-fiber substitution trend is a durable, multi-year demand support.

The trajectory, however, has weakened. Smurfit WestRock's formation created a larger scaled competitor. 2024–25 volume softness and input inflation hit the sector broadly, but GPK compounded it with company-specific execution failures — elevated inventory building since 2023, demand deterioration in food/foodservice/household, and a strategic overreach on capital projects. Peers did not universally suffer a ~50% derating, a dual C-suite exit, and a controls material weakness, which makes GPK's problem partly self-inflicted rather than purely cyclical. The competitive assets remain strong; the execution and credibility deficit is what has eroded. Competitive position scores 5.2 — a strong underlying franchise with a near-term negative trajectory that management is attempting to reverse.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risk stack is unusually dense and not fully priced despite the drawdown:

1. **Internal-control material weakness (new, severe).** Disclosure controls were deemed not effective at 3/31/26 because former senior management made capital expenditures exceeding board-delegated authority without authorization. No financial misstatement resulted, but this is a governance-oversight breakdown at the top, and remediation is ongoing. It reframes the story from cyclical miss to governance failure plus strategic overreach.

2. **Leverage and covenant path.** Net leverage sits against a temporarily raised 5.0x covenant that steps down to 4.75x by 3/31/27 and expires 6/30/27. If EBITDA keeps falling, headroom compresses fast. The amendment adds a higher pricing tier at ≥4.75x and restricts buybacks, acquisitions, and non-guarantor investments through 9/30/27.

3. **Securities litigation.** Multiple class actions (lead-plaintiff deadline July 6, 2026) allege the February 2025 guidance was knowingly unreliable, citing rising inventories since 2023 and foreseeable input inflation, and highlighting former CEO Doss's ~$7M in sales and former CFO Scherger's ~$1.8M in sales before their November 2025 resignations. Litigation outcome and reputational overhang are binary and unresolved.

4. **Goodwill impairment.** The International reporting unit's fair value exceeds carrying value by only ~2% ($525M goodwill). A 100bp discount-rate rise or 1.0x multiple compression would push it underwater. Total goodwill is $2.05B; the 10-K explicitly ties this to the fallen stock price and market cap.

5. **Structural/cyclical.** Commodity and energy inflation with lagged pass-through, demand shifts toward reusables, FX exposure (31% of sales ex-US), and ~59% unionized workforce with expiring contracts.

Risk factors score 3.7 — the combination of a controls weakness, a tightening covenant clock, thin goodwill cushion, and active litigation represents concentrated, partly binary downside that the current price only partially reflects.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is quiet and offers little directional edge. ATM IV is essentially flat at 49.7% (from 49.9%), elevated in absolute terms and consistent with a distressed name, but not spiking into a specific catalyst. The put/call open-interest ratio held at a low 0.12, which does not corroborate acute bearish hedging, given the fundamental overhang. Short interest at 10.9% of float (5.4 days to cover) is elevated but not crowded; it reflects institutional skepticism rather than a squeeze setup. The muted options signal argues against an imminent binary resolution priced by the derivatives market.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** GPK is a durable, well-positioned franchise trading at a distressed valuation. The customer roster, integration, and IP are intact, and the fiber-substitution tailwind is real. Management is executing a credible kitchen-sink reset — closing high-cost mills, cancelling overreaching projects, and taking charges up front. Incoming CEO Rietbroek and refreshed-board directors have put real personal capital in near the lows, the sharpest positive tell in the file and a contrast to the prior regime's selling. If Waco ramps profitably and inventories normalize through 2026, normalized net income can return toward the $650–720M range, and a re-rating to a mid-teens multiple on that earnings power would take the stock into the high teens.

**Bear case.** Leverage sits near a temporarily loosened covenant that steps down within a year, and EBITDA is still falling. The controls material weakness signals a governance breakdown that contributed to the capex overreach and precipitated the leadership purge. The CFO seat remains unfilled. Securities litigation is unresolved, and the International goodwill cushion is one bad quarter or rate move from an impairment. Reported cash flow is flattered by ~$1.1B of quasi-debt financing arrangements. If the reset fails to arrest the EBITDA decline, covenant pressure and further downgrades could force asset sales that shrink the earnings base, with a stock revisiting the high-single digits.

Both cases draw on the same facts. The discriminating variables — a permanent CFO hire, the covenant headroom trajectory over the next two quarters, and evidence Waco is contributing profitably — are not yet observable.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

We rate GPK a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the bar for continuing it unchanged is high; the reset leaves the outcome two-sided, and the insider buying is a real if insufficient positive that keeps us from moving to AVOID.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A permanent, credible CFO appointment plus a documented remediation timeline for the material weakness, removing the governance overhang.
- Net leverage declining below ~4.0x with clear evidence of covenant headroom widening ahead of the 4.75x step-down at 3/31/27.
- Evidence the Waco mill is contributing positive incremental EBITDA and that inventory normalization is complete, pointing to normalized annual EBITDA back above ~$1.6B.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Net leverage rising toward 4.75x with shrinking covenant headroom, or a further credit downgrade.
- An International goodwill impairment charge, or a second corrective disclosure/guidance cut.
- An adverse development in the securities litigation, or continued negative operating cash flow into H2 2026.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release, which should reveal early Waco economics, inventory-destocking progress, and covenant trajectory. The July 6, 2026 lead-plaintiff deadline and any permanent CFO announcement are near-term catalysts. We would revisit the thesis in full at Q3 2026 results, by which point the reset year should be substantially observable.

## Conclusion

Graphic Packaging is a high-quality fiber-packaging franchise caught in a self-inflicted crisis of execution and credibility. The ~50% derating, dual C-suite exits, controls material weakness, loosened covenants, credit downgrade, and litigation are real and concentrated, and the cheap trailing multiple reflects that distress rather than a mispricing the market has missed. Against that, the business generates substantial normalized cash flow, holds a premier customer base, benefits from a secular substitution tailwind, and now has new leadership buying stock near the lows into a deliberate kitchen-sink reset. The investable question — normalized post-reset earnings power — cannot be answered until Waco's ramp, inventory normalization, and covenant deleveraging become visible. We maintain HOLD, uncertain. The overall score of 4.5 reflects a two-sided setup where the franchise quality and insider alignment offset, but do not yet overcome, the leverage, governance, and litigation risk stack.
