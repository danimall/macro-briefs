## Executive Summary

Terex Corporation is in the middle of the most aggressive portfolio transformation in its history. Within 16 months the company acquired ESG from Dover (~$2B in debt), merged with REV Group (nearly doubling shares outstanding to 114M), and initiated a strategic review of its largest legacy segment (Aerials/Genie®, $2.06B revenue). If all three moves execute as planned, Terex by mid-2027 will be a specialty vehicles and environmental solutions company rather than a cyclical construction equipment manufacturer, warranting a different multiple.

The stock has rallied 77% from its April 2025 low of $34.25 to ~$62, driven by the Q1 2026 earnings beat (EPS $0.98 vs. $0.78 estimate, +25%) and strong Q4 2025 bookings (145% book-to-bill). Yet consensus estimates have fallen 15–20% over the past 90 days, the Altman Z-Score sits at 2.23 (grey zone), and integration of REV remains in early innings with only $28M of $75M run-rate synergies expected in 2026. The transformation thesis is credible and provides a specific 12-month catalyst path, but execution risk, leverage, and macro headwinds (tariffs, cyclical demand weakness) make this a name to hold and monitor rather than add at current levels.

## Company Overview

Terex Corporation manufactures specialty equipment across four reportable segments following the February 2026 REV merger:

- **Environmental Solutions (ES):** $1.69B revenue, 13.8% operating margin. Refuse collection bodies (Heil®), compactors (Marathon®), digital fleet management (3rd Eye®, Soft-Pak®), and utility equipment. Market leader in refuse.
- **Materials Processing (MP):** $1.68B revenue, 13.9% operating margin. Crushers, screens, concrete mixers, material handlers (Powerscreen®, Fuchs®). Global brand franchise.
- **Aerials:** $2.06B revenue, 5.0% operating margin. MEWPs, scissor/boom lifts, telehandlers under Genie® brand. Under strategic review for potential divestiture.
- **Specialty Vehicles (SV — NEW):** Expected ~$2.2–2.5B annually. Ambulances, fire apparatus, terminal trucks, recreational vehicles (from REV Group). 2-year backlog at close.

Geographic mix is 72% North America, ~10,700 employees, headquartered in Norwalk, CT.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory:** FY2025 revenue of $5.42B grew 5.7% YoY, driven entirely by the ESG acquisition consolidation. Legacy organic sales declined 11% (Aerials -14.5%, MP -11.6%). The REV merger transforms 2026 revenue toward $7.5–8.1B guided range (+38–49% reported).

**Margin compression is the critical trend:**

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Gross margin | 22.8% | 20.8% | 19.4% |
| Operating margin | 12.4% | 10.3% | 8.8% |
| Net margin | ~10% | ~6.5% | 4.1% |
| Aerials op. margin | 13.6% | 11.2% | 5.0% |

Aerials margin collapsed 860bps over two years — driven by tariff costs (Section 232 expansion August 2025), production cuts, unfavorable customer mix, and Chinese competition. Interest expense nearly tripled from $63M (2023) to $177M (2025) on ESG acquisition debt.

**Balance sheet:** Long-term debt of $2.58B (up from $620M in 2023). Debt-to-equity 1.93x. Altman Z-Score 2.23 (grey zone). No significant maturities until 2029, which provides breathing room, but interest burden (~$150M/year) constrains flexibility. Total liquidity of $1.57B ($772M cash + $800M undrawn revolver) is adequate.

**Free cash flow:** $322–325M in FY2025 at 147% cash conversion of net income — strong execution. Working capital improved to 20.8% of annualized sales from 24.0%. Guided 80–90% conversion in 2026 due to integration costs. The company factored $702M of receivables without recourse.

**Capital allocation:** $53M buybacks, ~$45M dividends, $118M capex in FY2025. Capex rising to $185M in 2026 on REV integration. Management's ROIC-driven compensation alignment is appropriate given the M&A intensity. Management-calculated adjusted ROIC was 11.7% in FY2025.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on three pillars:

**1. REV synergies ($75M run-rate by 2028, $28M in 2026).** ESG integration exceeded targets ahead of schedule — a positive precedent. But REV is larger, more complex (4 distinct product lines vs. ESG's adjacent refuse business), and introduces entirely new end markets where Terex has no prior expertise (emergency vehicles, RVs).

**2. Aerials recovery + potential divestiture.** Q4 2025 Aerials bookings surged 46% YoY on mega-project replacement demand. Book-to-bill of 145% across all segments provides near-term revenue visibility. A divestiture would generate proceeds to de-lever and simplify the portfolio, likely re-rating the remaining business at a higher multiple.

**3. Structural end-market tailwinds.** ES benefits from non-discretionary waste collection replacement cycles. Utilities are driven by data center power demand (8–15% annual utility capex growth forecast). Municipal spending ($200B/year U.S.) supports both ES and SV segments.

**Against this:** Consensus estimates have been cut 15–20% over 90 days. Current-year EPS now at $4.79 (vs. $5.64 ninety days ago). Next-year at $5.73 (vs. $6.82). The revision trajectory is unambiguously negative. Management's own FY2026 guidance of $4.50–$5.00 EPS includes 3% dilution from the REV merger relative to standalone guidance of $4.80–$5.20.

The Q1 2026 beat ($0.98 vs. $0.78) partially offsets the revision concern, but the GAAP loss of $(93)M (driven by non-cash purchase price accounting charges) shows the near-term P&L distortion from the merger.

## Valuation Assessment

At $61.71, TEX trades at:
- 18.5x trailing EPS ($3.33)
- 12.9x FY2026E EPS ($4.79)
- 10.8x FY2027E EPS ($5.73)

Against peer medians of 21.2x P/E, TEX appears modestly discounted. The discount reflects: (a) elevated leverage (1.93x D/E vs. lower for peers), (b) integration risk from two major acquisitions in 16 months, (c) negative estimate revision momentum, and (d) the Aerials segment margin collapse dragging consolidated profitability.

The analyst consensus mean target of $75.88 (median $76) implies ~23% upside. The low target of $60 essentially says the stock is fairly valued here; the high target of $95 prices in successful transformation and Aerials divestiture.

On normalized earnings of $5.50–6.00 (if synergies deliver and Aerials stabilizes or divests at accretive terms), a 14–15x multiple would yield $77–90 — consistent with analyst targets. At current $62, you're paying 11.3x that normalized case, which is reasonable for an industrial in transformation but not cheap enough to compensate for the execution risk given the macro backdrop.

The stock trades 14% below its 52-week high of $71.50, reached immediately post-earnings on Feb 12.

## Competitive Landscape

**ES/Heil® (Refuse):** Market leader, competing with McNeilus (Oshkosh) and Labrie. Digital fleet management (3rd Eye®, Soft-Pak®) provides differentiation — management describes clear industry leadership in waste-sector digital solutions. Non-discretionary replacement demand provides revenue stability.

**Genie® (Aerials):** #2 globally behind JLG (Oshkosh). The structural threat from Chinese manufacturers (Dingli, Sinoboom, XCMG, Zoomlion) is real despite U.S. and EU anti-dumping duties. The 860bps margin compression over two years suggests competitive intensity is compressing pricing power. Management's willingness to divest implies they view this as a deteriorating competitive position relative to lower-cost alternatives.

**MP (Powerscreen®):** Strong global brand against Metso and Sandvik. European demand weakness is cyclical rather than structural. The divestiture of tower/rough terrain cranes simplifies the segment toward higher-margin processing equipment.

**SV (REV businesses):** New territory. Emergency vehicles face relatively fragmented competition (REV was the consolidator). Backlog visibility (2 years) provides near-term protection while integration proceeds.

If the Aerials divestiture completes, the remaining portfolio (ES + MP + SV) would tilt toward businesses with more recurring revenue characteristics (waste, utilities, municipal) and away from the most cyclically sensitive and competitively challenged segment.

## Risk Assessment

**Critical risks:**

1. **REV integration (HIGH).** Terex is simultaneously integrating the largest acquisition in its history while exploring divestiture of its largest legacy business. Management attention is finite. The Q1 2026 GAAP loss of $(93)M (even if non-cash-driven) signals early-stage integration costs are material. Achieving $75M synergies by 2028 requires flawless execution across entirely new product categories.

2. **Leverage in grey zone (HIGH).** No maturities until 2029 provides time, but in an environment with weakening construction demand, covenant risk increases if Aerials continues to deteriorate and synergies lag.

3. **Tariff exposure (MODERATE-HIGH).** Aerials manufactures in Mexico, Italy, and China. Section 232 tariff expansion already compressed margins. Further tariff escalation would hit both Aerials and potentially MP (global supply chains).

4. **Negative estimate revision momentum (MODERATE).** The Q1 beat provides partial offset, but the revision trend must stabilize for the stock to sustain its re-rating.

5. **Aerials divestiture uncertainty (MODERATE).** "Strong inbound interest" is encouraging but there is no definitive timeline, price, or structure disclosed. A below-expectation sale price would impair the de-leveraging thesis. Failure to divest leaves Terex overexposed to a margin-compressed, competitively challenged segment.

6. **Chinese competition (STRUCTURAL).** Anti-dumping duties may not fully offset Chinese government subsidies in MEWPs. This is a multi-year structural headwind to Genie® regardless of near-term bookings recovery.

7. **SEC injunction (LEGAL).** Terex operates under a 2009 SEC settlement injunction barring future violations of anti-fraud, books and records, reporting, and internal control provisions. Any future violation would result in severe penalties — an unusual ongoing legal constraint.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($76–85; +23–38%):**
- REV synergies track to $75M by 2028; $28M delivered in 2026.
- Aerials divestiture at 8–10x EBITDA generates proceeds to meaningfully de-lever balance sheet.
- Remaining business (ES + MP + SV) re-rates to 16–17x forward earnings (~$6 EPS normalized) = $96–102.
- Q4 2025 bookings surge converts to 2026 revenue upside.
- Utilities capex growth (8–15%) drives ES expansion.
- If synergies and divestiture execute within 18 months: $85+ achievable.

**Bear Case ($42–48; -22–32%):**
- REV integration stalls; synergies disappoint (<$50M by 2028).
- Aerials divestiture fails or executes at <6x EBITDA, insufficient to de-lever.
- Tariff escalation further compresses margins (Aerials to breakeven).
- Construction cycle weakens further; MP and Aerials organic declines extend to -15%+ in 2026.
- Estimate revisions accelerate downward; EPS falls to $3.50–4.00.
- At 12x $3.75 EPS = $45. Stock revisits Nov 2025 lows.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Aerials divestiture announced with specific terms (price >$1.5B or >8x segment EBITDA), confirming de-leveraging thesis.
2. Estimate revisions stabilize and begin inflecting upward (30-day revisions positive for 2 consecutive months).
3. Stock pulls back to $50–54 range (below 11x FY2027E), providing margin of safety for integration risk.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. FY2026 EPS guidance cut below $4.00 (would signal synergy/execution failure).
2. Aerials strategic review formally terminated with no divestiture.
3. Debt covenant breach or downgrade to sub-investment-grade credit rating.
4. Two consecutive quarters of negative book-to-bill (<1.0x) across all segments.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next meaningful data points are Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July/early August 2026) — the first full quarter with REV Group contribution — and any Aerials divestiture announcement. Reassess after Q2 results with visibility into REV integration progress and synergy run-rate trajectory.

## Conclusion

Terex presents a credible transformation thesis with identifiable catalysts: REV synergies, Aerials divestiture, and structural end-market tailwinds in waste/utilities/municipal. The specific catalysts — particularly the Aerials divestiture with "strong inbound interest" and measurable synergy targets on an explicit timeline — justify maintaining the position on a 12-month horizon despite the low overall score.

At $62 and 12.9x FY2026E earnings, with negative estimate revisions, elevated leverage, and early-stage integration of a company-sized acquisition, the risk/reward is approximately symmetric. The options market confirms this with IV ~10pp rich to realized vol. The stock has already re-rated 77% from its April 2025 low; much of the cheap-cyclical upside has been captured.

HOLD. Add on pullback to $50–54 where the risk/reward improves meaningfully, or upgrade on Aerials divestiture announcement at accretive terms.
