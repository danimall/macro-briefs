## Executive Summary

Barclays reported H1 2026 results on 28 July 2026 that beat on every headline metric: group income £16.5bn (+11% year over year), statutory pre-tax profit £6.1bn (+17%) against consensus of approximately £5.94bn, return on tangible equity of 14.8%, a CET1 ratio of 14.3%, and £2.3bn of first-half distributions including a £1bn buyback that exceeded the roughly £831m the market expected. Full-year income guidance was raised to £31.5bn from £31bn. The shares fell about 4.9% in London on the day.

The market's reaction was rational rather than perverse, and two pieces of arithmetic explain it. First, £31.5bn of guided full-year income less £16.5bn delivered in H1 implies second-half income of ~£15.0bn, roughly 9% below the first half. Second, if full-year RoTE lands at the reaffirmed ">12%" floor and H1 delivered 14.8%, the implied second-half figure is approximately 2 × 12.0 − 14.8 = ~9.2%. RoTE is not strictly additive across halves — average tangible equity shifts and UK banks book certain annual levies late in the year — and ">12%" is a floor management may intend to beat, so this is an approximation rather than a forecast. But the direction is unambiguous, and it is corroborated by independent data: consensus EPS for the next quarter has been cut 13.6% over 30 days and 12.6% over 90 days.

The composition of the beat matters as much as its size. Investment Bank Q2 income of £4.0bn (revenue +20%, equities +45%, banking fees +32%, fixed income +1%) drove the upside, while Barclays UK grew 7%, Private Bank & Wealth 5%, and the US Consumer Bank's headline +38% included roughly £225m of gain on the sale of the American Airlines co-branded card portfolio. At ~47% of divisional income, IB dependence has not fallen since FY2023 on the income measure despite a strategic plan framed around rebalancing. Barclays is a well-capitalised, improving bank whose earnings mix is more cyclical than the narrative implies, trading at a derived 1.3-1.5x tangible book after a 41% twelve-month advance. Base case $30.00 (+7.0%), bull $34.50 (+23%), bear $21.00 (−25%). Probability-weighted expected total return of roughly 4% including the cash dividend is approximately fair, not compelling. HOLD.

IB share of divisional income: 46.5% (FY2023), 47.1% (FY2024), 47.5% (FY2025), 47.4% (TTM). The franchise is a genuinely scaled transatlantic markets-and-banking business bolted onto a large UK deposit franchise and a US card book — an unusual combination among European banks, and the source of both the earnings power and the principal vulnerability.

The 2024-2026 strategic plan commits to RoTE above 12% in 2026, CET1 in a 13-14% range, and at least £10bn of distributions across the three years. All three were reaffirmed at H1. One precision is required: the plan's rebalancing target was defined in terms of risk-weighted-asset allocation, not income share. RWA mix could be shifting toward domestic businesses while income share stays flat — both statements can be true. The current IB RWA share could not be verified from primary filings, so this report treats the income-mix stability as an observation about investor exposure rather than as evidence the plan has been missed.

**A disclosure note that materially affects confidence.** Barclays is a foreign private issuer. It files a Form 20-F (most recent 10 February 2026, FY2025) and Form 6-Ks; there is no 10-K, no 10-Q, no DEF 14A, and no Section 16 insider filings. Attempts to retrieve the 20-F and the 28 July 2026 results 6-K in primary form failed. FY2025 statement figures, tangible net asset value per share, the FY2026 cost guidance, and the 20-F risk-factor changes are therefore unverified against primary source, and the operating detail below comes from secondary reporting of the results release plus a third-party financial aggregator whose series contains at least two internal contradictions (a TTM revenue growth field of −14.40% that conflicts with its own rising revenue levels, and a TTM net income ÷ TTM EPS ratio implying an implausible ~19% one-year share-count reduction). Standard screens — Altman Z, Piotroski F, current ratio, FCF margin — are not meaningful for a bank in any case; the aggregator's reported 102% TTM FCF margin is an artefact. The correct diagnostics are CET1, RoTE, cost:income, impairment rate, and price-to-tangible-book.

## Financial Analysis

**Income and earnings trajectory.** Group income (£m): FY2022 23,742 → FY2023 23,488 → FY2024 24,843 → FY2025 26,884 → TTM to Jun-26 28,233. Growth of −1.1%, +5.8%, +8.2% across FY2023-FY2025. Net income: FY2023 4,274 → FY2024 5,316 → FY2025 6,175.

**Margin trend is the cleanest evidence of operating leverage.** Pretax margin: 27.92% (FY2023) → 32.64% (FY2024) → 33.99% (FY2025) → 35.43% (TTM). Profit margin: 22.66% → 25.58% → 26.83% → 27.91%. Three consecutive years of expansion off a depressed FY2023 base, consistent with structural-hedge-driven net interest income plus a cost programme (approximately £200m of Q2 2026 efficiency savings were cited).

**H1 2026 detail** (secondary reporting of the results release): income £16.5bn (+11%); PBT £6.1bn (+17%) versus ~£5.94bn consensus on a like-for-like statutory IFRS basis; RoTE 14.8%; CET1 14.3%; Q2 standalone income £8.3bn and PBT £3.3bn (+30%+), implying Q1 income of ~£8.2bn and PBT of ~£2.8bn. Impairment charges of £1.4bn versus £1.1bn (+27%), with Q2 at £571m versus £469m — implying Q1 2026 impairment of ~£829m versus ~£631m, so the step-up was front-loaded into Q1 (+31%) rather than Q2 (+22%). Those quarterly derivations carry up to ~±£50m of rounding error given the £0.1bn precision of the half-year figures.

**Distributions.** £2.3bn for the first half, comprising a £1bn buyback and a 5.9p interim dividend. One reconciliation does not close: 5.9p on approximately 14.6bn ordinary shares is roughly £861m, which plus £1bn gives ~£1.86bn — about £440m short of £2.3bn. The gap may reflect an earlier buyback tranche or items not itemised in the coverage available. It is flagged rather than resolved. On a market capitalisation of roughly £70-75bn (14.6bn shares at a derived ~514p), a £1bn half-yearly buyback annualises to about 2.7%, giving a combined cash-plus-buyback shareholder yield in the low-to-mid single digits.

**Capital.** CET1 at 14.3% sits approximately 30bp above the top of the stated 13-14% range. This is the pivotal balance-sheet fact and it cuts both ways: it funded a buyback above consensus, and it is thin. Motor-finance redress provisioning, any regulatory RWA inflation, and further inorganic activity all compete for the same surplus. The RWA denominator was not obtainable, so the absolute sterling surplus and the buyback capacity it implies cannot be quantified.

**Funding.** Aggregator "total debt" — for a bank, typically debt securities in issue plus subordinated liabilities rather than a corporate-style leverage measure — rose from £104.3bn (FY2024) to £132.0bn (FY2025), +26.5%, with a further ~£5.9bn added by June 2026. This is consistent with funding balance-sheet growth (US card assets, the Best Egg acquisition) and MREL/TLAC issuance. An alternative reading is a mix shift away from deposits toward wholesale markets, which would modestly compress net interest margin. The two cannot be distinguished without the funding note. Neither reading is a solvency signal at 14.3% CET1.

**Earnings-surprise record.** Four consecutive beats: +7.8% (Jun-26), +10.4% (Mar-26), +34.1% (Dec-25), +18.4% (Sep-25). The magnitudes are decaying as the base rises, but the consistency supports reasonable confidence in near-term consensus. Note that the quarterly figures in that record ($0.17, $0.15, $0.11, $0.12, summing to $0.55) are on a per-ordinary-share basis and are not comparable to the $2.80/$3.40 annual estimates, which are per ADS. This basis mismatch also explains the quoted trailing and forward P/E of 43.78 in the data feed — that field pairs the ADS price with a non-ADS earnings figure and should be disregarded.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is real but decelerating, cyclically sourced, and partly non-recurring.

**What is genuine.** Income has compounded at roughly 6-8% over two years with margin expansion, which is the harder half of the equation. FY2026 income guidance was raised, not trimmed. Barclays UK's 7% Q2 growth includes a structural-hedge tailwind that remains mechanically positive as low-rate legacy hedges roll into higher yields. Next-year consensus per-ADS EPS of $3.40 versus $2.80 current year implies +21% growth, and that out-year number has been unrevised for 90 days — stable rather than deteriorating.

**What is not.** Three observations weigh against extrapolating H1.

*The implied second half.* £31.5bn guided less £16.5bn delivered leaves ~£15.0bn, approximately 9% below H1. Combined with an implied H2 RoTE near 9% at the guidance floor and Jefferies' flag on higher second-half costs, management is declining to extrapolate its own first half. Consensus agrees: next-quarter EPS is down 13.6% in 30 days.

*The non-IB divisions.* Barclays UK +7%, Private Bank & Wealth +5% (with "changing deposit preferences" diluting growth from higher client balances), UK Corporate mid-single-digit. Barclays UK's own commentary attributes the offset to retail-deposit dynamics and mortgage-margin pressure — meaning the finite structural-hedge tailwind is being partly absorbed by competition as it arrives. That weakens the domestic-RoTE-improvement leg of the rebalancing story.

*The US Consumer Bank.* Headline Q2 income +38%, but this includes roughly £225m from the American Airlines co-brand disposal plus the newly acquired Best Egg platform. Annual USCB income rose £430m from FY2025 (£2,160m) to TTM (£2,590m); excluding the ~£225m gain, underlying growth is approximately £205m, or ~9.5%, against a ~20% headline. Strategically Barclays is exiting a contractual co-brand revenue stream and redeploying into owned unsecured direct lending — trading partner-dependency risk for direct credit-underwriting risk in the same half that group impairments rose 27%. No divisional impairment breakdown was available, so attributing any of the increase to Best Egg or the US card book is inference, not fact; UK consumer normalisation and macro-scenario model updates are equally plausible drivers.

One consideration sits under the IB fee line. Investment banking fees rose 32% in the period. The durability of that fee pool depends on continued deal and issuance activity, and second-half fee normalisation is a plausible risk that neither guidance nor consensus explicitly addresses. ## Valuation Assessment

Tangible net asset value per share could not be verified, which is the single most important missing input for a bank. It can be derived indirectly.

**Price basis.** $28.035 per ADS. Using the aggregator's current P/E of 10.34 against TTM EPS of £1.99 per ADS solves to an implied 1.36 USD/GBP, giving ~£20.57 per ADS and ~514p per ordinary share.

**Earnings multiples on consensus.** Per-ADS consensus of $2.80 (FY2026) and $3.40 (FY2027) puts the stock at **10.0x current year and 8.2x next year**. FY2025 EPS of 42.3p per ordinary share against 514p gives a trailing FY2025 P/E of 12.2x.

**Derived price-to-tangible-book,** using the identity P/TNAV = P/E × RoTE:
- 10.34 × 14.8% = **1.53x** — an upper bound, because it pairs a trailing-twelve-month multiple with an annualised half-year return.
- 10.34 × 12.5-13.0% (a blended TTM RoTE combining a weaker H2 2025 with a strong H1 2026) = **1.29-1.34x**.
- Cross-check on FY2025 earnings and a low-teens FY2025 RoTE lands in the same band.

So roughly **1.3-1.5x tangible book**. If reported TNAV per share is materially above the implied ~336-400p, the stock is cheaper than estimated; that input should be verified first by anyone acting on this work.

**What the multiple history shows.** Aggregator P/E: 5.38x (FY2021), 5.41x (FY2022), 5.75x (FY2023), 7.61x (FY2024), 11.19x (FY2025), 10.34x current. Barclays traded below 1.0x TNAV through most of 2020-2024. The re-rating from sub-book to ~1.4x is the bulk of the 41% twelve-month return, and it has already discounted successful delivery of the 2024-2026 plan. Further upside requires a durably higher RoTE — mid-teens sustained, not a >12% floor — or a step-up in distributions, not a re-rating of depressed assets.

**A caution on the cheap optics.** A 10x earnings multiple invites the conclusion that the stock is inexpensive. Two things argue against treating that as identified mispricing. The earnings being capitalised include a cyclical peak in equities and banking-fee revenue from a franchise where that line is 47% of income; and the low absolute multiple is the standing structural discount applied to European banks with large markets businesses, not a temporary dislocation. Cheapness without a specific reason the market is wrong is not a valuation case.

**Analyst context.** Mean target $30.09, median $30.15, low $27.10, high $32.96 — a mean of +7.3% and a low target 3.3% *below* spot. Rating mix is 2 buy / 2 hold, with sentiment characterised as improving. The principle that analyst targets act as floors applies to inflection stocks with rising estimates and reasonable multiples; here estimates are falling near-term on a two-analyst base, so the consensus target is better read as a genuine ceiling estimate than as a stale floor. Coverage this thin also means the "consensus" carries less information than the label implies.

**Scenarios.**
- **Base $30.00.** 10.5x FY2026 consensus $2.80, or 8.8x FY2027 $3.40. +7.0% from $28.035; combined with the ~2.1% cash dividend yield, roughly +9% total return.
- **Bull $34.50.** Mid-teens RoTE sustained into 2027, FY2027 EPS ~$3.50, multiple holds near 10x on demonstrated diversification plus a distribution step-up with the next strategic plan. +23%. Slightly above the $32.96 high target.
- **Bear $21.00.** IB equities and fees mean-revert, UK/European recession lifts impairments, a motor-finance provision consumes the CET1 surplus and pauses buybacks; ~7.5x on $2.60 of depressed EPS, roughly 1.0x tangible book. −25%, against a 52-week low of $19.285 and a March 2026 low of $20.07.

Probability-weighting base 55% / bull 20% / bear 25% gives +2.2% price return, or roughly **4% total return including the dividend** — below a fair equity hurdle. That is the arithmetic behind HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE.

## Competitive Landscape

The most informative competitive datapoint of the half is relative trading performance. Barclays grew equities revenue 45% while the largest US rivals averaged 69%, and grew fixed income 1% while the top five US banks averaged 13%. Barclays participated in the volatility-driven revenue pool but captured less of it, materially so in FIC. For a franchise whose bull case rests on being the credible European challenger in global markets, underperforming the US cohort by roughly 24 points in equities and 12 in FIC in one quarter is a competitive signal. The counter-argument is real: the US cohort's percentages come off different bases and mixes (larger prime brokerage and structured-derivative books), single-quarter trading comparisons are noisy, and Barclays has been gaining equities share from a low base over several years. One quarter does not establish structural share loss — but it does not support the "gaining share" claim either.

In UK retail, the whole FTSE bank cohort has re-rated on the same structural-hedge mechanic, and the same margin-compression phase is visible across it: deposit migration into higher-rate products and mortgage price competition. Barclays' commentary is indistinguishable from the sector's.

The competitive dynamic in motor finance is worth noting. When the FCA finalised the industry redress scheme on 30 March 2026 at £9.1bn — roughly £2bn below the consultation estimate — the large diversified banks (Barclays, Lloyds, Close Brothers) and the Finance and Leasing Association declined to litigate, while the challenges came from captive auto-finance arms (VW Financial Services, Mercedes-Benz Financial Services, Crédit Agricole Auto Finance) and the consumer group Consumer Voice. The FCA's June 2026 warning that opting out risks ~£6bn of extra industry cost explains the split: banks bought certainty, captives with different economics bought optionality. Barclays' non-participation in the challenge rests on a single Sky News report and is unconfirmed by the company.

A structural point on the security itself: US holders of BCS receive semi-annual primary reporting, no proxy, and no Section 16 insider data. Relative to owning JPMorgan or Citi, the disclosure set is less frequent, less standardised, and less machine-readable. That is a permanent feature, not a new risk, but it justifies a wider confidence band on every number in this report.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Motor finance / discretionary commission redress — the largest unquantified item.** Industry cost finalised at £9.1bn on 30 March 2026, then parts of the scheme suspended on 2 July 2026 pending legal challenges, with court proceedings expected December 2026 or February 2027 and payouts potentially slipping into 2027. The FCA launched a £2m national awareness campaign in August 2026, which plausibly lifts complaint volumes and scheme take-up above provisioned assumptions. **No Barclays-specific provision figure could be located.** For calibration, Close Brothers — far smaller but far more motor-concentrated — self-estimated £430m. Barclays' exposure is generally understood to be legacy point-of-sale finance rather than a core motor book, but that is market understanding, not verified evidence, and no number is asserted here. The risk has shifted from quantum to timing and accounting: provisions must be held against a scheme under judicial review, and any top-up competes directly with the ~30bp CET1 surplus that funds buybacks.

**2. Cost trajectory.** Roughly £500m of "extra costs" was cited as weighing on the shares; Jefferies flagged higher H2 costs; a 3 August 2026 sell-side downgrade to Hold was titled around unexpected costs and revenue headwinds. **The composition of that £500m could not be itemised, and FY2026 cost guidance and the cost:income target are unverified.** This directly determines whether the >12% RoTE floor is a conservative placeholder or a real ceiling.

**3. Investment Bank concentration at a cyclical peak.** 47% of income, with the beat driven by equities +45% and fees +32%. The risk is not that trading revenue is bad; it is that ~1.4x tangible book capitalises peak trading and fee conditions. A sustained higher-volatility regime plus a recovering M&A cycle could make this a plateau rather than a peak — that is the bull rebuttal, and it is not unreasonable.

**4. Credit normalisation.** H1 impairment £1.4bn versus £1.1bn (+27%), front-loaded into Q1, alongside a deliberate expansion into US unsecured direct lending via Best Egg ($800m, completed 6 May 2026 per a single tier-3 source). A UK/US consumer deterioration would hit Barclays UK, the US card book, and Best Egg simultaneously. [unverified — the research brief does not contain the specific macro indicators (US mortgage rates, July payrolls, UK GDP, European recession probability) cited in the original draft]

**5. Rate-path sensitivity in both directions.** Higher-for-longer sustains net interest income and the structural hedge but raises impairment risk and pressures the domestic mortgage book; a rapid easing cycle would compress the hedge tailwind that has driven three years of margin expansion. Neither path is clearly favourable at this multiple.

**6. Disclosure and data risk.** FY2025 primary statements, TNAV per share, the Barclays motor provision, FY2026 cost guidance, the 20-F risk-factor changes, governance and compensation, and three post-results 6-K filings (3, 6, 7 August 2026) are all unverified or unread. A material post-results event cannot be excluded. Management quality is assessed here on capital-allocation behaviour alone because no insider or proxy data exists for this issuer.

### Options Market Signal

Positioning shows no stress and no directional conviction. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 28.5% against 30.8% one-year historical — fairly priced to slightly cheap, with an IV percentile of 2% within the current chain, meaning the market is pricing unusually little near-term event risk now that the results are past. Put/call skew is balanced at the front (1.7%) and mildly negative at six months (−3.3%); put/call volume ratio is 0.26 and open-interest ratio 0.51, both call-tilted. Term structure is flat to gently upward (28.5% near, 30.5% at six months), distributing uncertainty evenly rather than clustering it. The nearest expiry prices a ±4.1% move; December prices ±15.8%. Short interest is negligible at 4.48m shares and 0.9 days to cover.

The read: no one is paying up for downside protection, and no one is paying up for upside either. That is consistent with the fundamental conclusion — a fairly valued bank between catalysts — and it means options are neither confirming nor contradicting the thesis. The mildly cheap volatility is more interesting to a hedger than to a directional buyer; the motor-finance hearings in December 2026 or February 2027 fall in a tenor where implied volatility is not obviously rich.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $34.50 (+23%)

Barclays is delivering. Four consecutive EPS beats, three consecutive years of margin expansion (pretax margin 27.9% → 35.4%), income growth accelerating to +8.2% in FY2025 and +11% in H1 2026, RoTE of 14.8% against a >12% target, and CET1 at 14.3% above the top of its own range. Management raised income guidance and returned £2.3bn in a single half, with a buyback ~20% above consensus expectation. The equities franchise is gaining share from a low base and the banking-fee pool is in a genuine upcycle. At 8.2x next-year consensus EPS with a low-to-mid single-digit combined shareholder yield, the stock does not need multiple expansion to compound — it needs only for the >12% RoTE floor to prove conservative, which the four-quarter beat record makes plausible. A new 2027-2029 plan announced alongside FY2026 results with a higher RoTE target and an expanded distribution commitment would be the specific re-rating catalyst. Motor-finance quantum risk for large diversified lenders has narrowed following the £2bn reduction in the industry bill, and if Barclays' exposure proves immaterial, that overhang is noise.

### Bear Case — $21.00 (−25%)

The market fell 4.9% on an "unblemished" beat because the forward arithmetic is unattractive. Guidance implies H2 income ~9% below H1 and an H2 RoTE near 9%; consensus has cut the next quarter 13.6% in 30 days. The beat came from the one division whose revenue is least repeatable, while the three UK-facing businesses grew 5-7% with the structural-hedge tailwind already partly absorbed by deposit competition and mortgage pricing. A third of US Consumer Bank's TTM income growth is a one-time disposal gain, and the replacement strategy takes on direct unsecured credit risk in the same half impairments rose 27%. Barclays underperformed US peers in both trading lines. Meanwhile the stock has re-rated from 5.4x to over 10x earnings and from below book to roughly 1.4x tangible book, sits near a 52-week high, and carries an unquantified redress liability against a capital surplus of only ~30bp. If IB revenue mean-reverts into a UK/European recession while a motor-finance provision pauses the buyback, both the earnings and the multiple compress, and 1.0x tangible book on $2.60 of EPS is roughly $21.

### Which is more likely

Neither dominates, which is why this is a HOLD. The company is executing; the entry price is not. The specific missing evidence — Barclays' motor-finance provision, the composition of the £500m cost step-up, and reported TNAV per share — would resolve much of the ambiguity, and none of it is available. A low headline multiple has more often marked a structural discount for European markets-heavy banks than a mispricing.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** primary decision points are the FY2026 results in February 2027 (which should carry a new multi-year plan and full-year cost:income disclosure), the motor-finance court proceedings in December 2026 or February 2027, and any Q3 trading update disclosing the H2 cost and revenue path.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Price below $24.50** with H1 operating performance intact — approximately 8.8x FY2026 and 7.2x FY2027 consensus and near 1.1-1.2x derived tangible book, which restores a genuine margin of safety and would make the base-case return roughly 22%.
2. **FY2026 RoTE guidance raised above the >12% floor**, or an explicit H2 cost figure that reconciles the £500m to one-off items rather than a run-rate step-up. Either converts the implied ~9% H2 RoTE from a fact of guidance into an artefact of conservatism.
3. **Disclosure of a Barclays motor-finance provision that is immaterial to CET1** (a charge that leaves the ratio at or above 14.0%), removing the competition for buyback capacity.
4. **A new 2027-2029 plan with a mid-teens RoTE target and a distribution commitment above the £10bn/three-year pace**, alongside verified evidence that IB RWA share has fallen.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. **Q3/Q4 IB revenue declining year over year**, particularly a second consecutive quarter of FIC underperformance versus the US cohort — the peak-earnings bear case beginning to confirm.
2. **A motor-finance provision that takes CET1 to or below 13.5%**, or any suspension of the buyback programme.
3. **Impairment charges rising above roughly £1.7bn on a half-year basis** (versus £1.4bn in H1 2026), which would indicate the credit step-up is deterioration rather than growth-related.
4. **FY2026 RoTE reported below 12%**, breaking the plan's central commitment.
5. **Barclays UK income growth falling below 4% year over year**, signalling the structural-hedge tailwind has been fully absorbed by deposit and mortgage competition.

**Position guidance for existing holders:** maintain. The combination of a low-to-mid single-digit shareholder yield, 14.3% CET1, and four consecutive beats does not warrant trimming at a fair multiple. New capital has no urgency at $28.035; the low analyst target sits below spot and the base case offers roughly 7% price upside against 25% bear-case downside. Wait for $24-25, or for one of the upgrade triggers above.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Barclays' H1 2026 was a real operating beat — £6.1bn PBT (+17%), 14.8% RoTE, 14.3% CET1, £2.3bn returned — from a bank three years into a successful margin-improvement programme. The problem is entirely forward-looking and entirely arithmetic: raised guidance implies second-half income 9% below the first half, the reaffirmed RoTE floor implies an H2 return near 9%, next-quarter consensus EPS has been cut 13.6% in a month, and the upside came from a division that is 47% of income and the most cyclical part of the franchise. Three of four non-IB divisions grew 5-7%, and roughly £225m of US Consumer Bank growth was a one-time disposal gain.

Against that, the stock has re-rated from below tangible book to roughly 1.3-1.5x, from 5.4x earnings to over 10x, and sits within roughly 2% of a 52-week high after a 41% twelve-month advance. The recovery trade has been paid. What remains is a well-capitalised bank at a fair multiple with an unquantified regulatory liability sitting against a 30bp capital surplus, in a UK and European macro environment where credit costs are already rising 27% year over year.
