**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

RadNet is the largest operator of freestanding outpatient diagnostic imaging centers in the United States, running 435 multi-modality sites across nine states, with a smaller but rapidly growing Digital Health (DeepHealth) segment that builds and sells radiology AI software. The top line is in excellent shape: FY2025 revenue rose 11.5% to $2,040M, Q1 2026 revenue grew 22.1% to $575.6M, same-center imaging volume rose 8.9%, and advanced modalities (PET/CT +35%, MR +20%) are mixing the business toward higher-value studies. Adjusted EBITDA, the metric management and the market use to value the company, reached a record $300M in 2025 and $63.3M in Q1 2026 versus $46.4M a year prior.

The problem is below the EBITDA line. GAAP operating income fell from $104.6M (2024) to $62M (2025), operating margin compressed from 5.7% to 3.0%, and the company swung to a net loss attributable to RadNet of $18.7M in 2025 from a $2.8M profit in 2024. Q1 2026 produced a $33.5M net loss (seasonally the weakest quarter) and an adjusted loss of $0.28 that came in roughly 43% below consensus. The drivers are structural rather than transitory: the October 2024 California healthcare minimum-wage increase plus broad wage inflation (salaries +9.1%, reading fees +12.7%), stock-based compensation up 66% to $54.6M, PET/CT isotope tracer costs up 22%, and accelerating GAAP losses in Digital Health (-$30.3M in 2025, -$17.7M in Q1 2026 alone) as it absorbs acquisitions.

The stock has round-tripped from a $50.76 low (Aug 2025) to an $85.84 high (Nov 2025) and back to $58.20, a 32% drawdown from the high. The valuation, growth, and AI optionality are all real, but the path to GAAP profitability is unproven and depends on lapping wage inflation and scaling Digital Health past breakeven. Insiders sold meaningfully near the highs and did not buy the decline. This is a balanced setup that does not yet clear the bar for a conviction long. HOLD.

## Company Overview

RadNet has operated freestanding fixed-site outpatient imaging centers since 1985 and is the scale leader in a fragmented market. The center base is heavily concentrated in California, with additional density in Arizona, Florida, Maryland, New Jersey, New York, Texas, Delaware, and newly entered Indiana and Idaho. The majority of centers are multi-modality (MRI, CT, PET/CT, mammography, ultrasound, X-ray, nuclear medicine), which management cites as the core differentiator: a one-stop offering that diversifies revenue and reduces single-modality reimbursement exposure. Roughly 150 centers operate as joint ventures with hospital and health systems, including Cedars-Sinai, Dignity Health, and Providence.

The business reports in two segments. Imaging Centers generate roughly 95-96% of revenue and all of the cash flow. Digital Health / DeepHealth (4-5% of revenue, fast-growing) builds AI-powered radiology informatics: RIS/PACS via eRad, the cloud-native DeepHealth OS, and clinical AI for breast, lung, prostate, thyroid, and brain imaging. These are RadNet-owned products assembled through acquisition (iCAD, See-Mode, CIMAR, Gleamer, Kheiron), not resold third-party technology, with 22 FDA clearances and 2,890+ external customers across the US and Europe.

The legal structure relies on a Consolidated Medical Group VIE (professional corporations controlled by senior management) to navigate state corporate-practice-of-medicine prohibitions. This is standard in physician services but adds a layer of related-party and structural risk concentrated in founder-CEO Dr. Howard Berger, who controls both the operating company and the Group VIEs.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue growth is strong and broad-based.** Annual revenue moved from $1,617M (2023) to $1,830M (2024) to $2,040M (2025, +11.5%). Q1 2026 accelerated to $575.6M, up 22.1% YoY, with imaging same-center growth of 8.9% and systemwide procedure volume up 10.1%. Growth is a mix of organic volume, advanced-modality shift, and a heavy acquisition cadence.

**GAAP profitability deteriorated sharply.** Operating income fell from $104.6M (2024) to $62M (2025); operating margin compressed from 5.7% to 3.0%. Net loss attributable to RadNet was $18.7M in 2025 versus a $2.8M profit in 2024 and $3.0M in 2023. Q1 2026 net loss was $33.5M. The drivers, in order of importance: (1) California healthcare minimum-wage increase plus broad wage inflation (salaries +9.1%, professional reading fees +12.7%); (2) stock-based comp surged 66% to $54.6M total; (3) medical supplies up 22% on expensive PET/CT isotope tracers; (4) widening Digital Health GAAP losses; (5) one-time items including an $8.6M lease abandonment (7 centers closed) and a $9.8M equipment disposal loss.

**Adjusted EBITDA continues to grow.** $232M (2023) → $279M (2024) → $300M (2025); Q1 2026 $63.3M versus $46.4M. The gap between record EBITDA and a GAAP net loss is the entire investment debate. The market values RadNet on EBITDA and FCF; the bears point to GAAP losses, rising SBC, and goodwill as evidence that the EBITDA figure overstates underlying earnings power.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow was $298.8M in 2025 against $213M of capex, leaving $85.6M of free cash flow. This is a capital-intensive, high-fixed-cost model. Cash fell from $767M (YE2025) to $455M (Q1 2026) after deploying $304M on Gleamer plus imaging deals. Term debt is roughly $1,085M (Barclays $961M at ~6%, maturing 2031; Truist $124M at the NJ JV), with $323M of undrawn revolver capacity ($273M Barclays + $50M Truist) and full covenant compliance. About $959M is floating-rate SOFR debt, and the 2019 interest-rate swaps expired in 2025, so a 1% rate rise now adds roughly $9.6M of annual interest with no hedge offset. Goodwill rose to $1.09B from $908M, an impairment-risk exposure that scales with the acquisition pace. Shares outstanding grew from roughly 75M to 78.5M via acquisition stock and heavy SBC, an ongoing dilution drag.

The Altman Z-Score of 1.42 nominally flags "distress," but that reading penalizes the asset-heavy leveraged structure while ignoring $455M of cash, $323M of undrawn revolver, $300M of EBITDA, and a 2031 maturity wall. Near-term solvency risk is low. The genuine financial issue is earnings quality and margin compression, not liquidity. The financial health score of 5.0 reflects that split: healthy cash generation and adequate liquidity offset by GAAP losses, rising leverage on floating rates, dilution, and goodwill buildup.

## Growth Analysis

The growth story has two engines. The imaging core benefits from durable secular tailwinds: an aging population, rising preventive screening, and the shift to outpatient settings that are lower-cost than hospital departments. Management guided 2026 imaging revenue growth of 17-19%, with EBITDA growth exceeding revenue growth and FCF growth of 29-41%. PET/CT for prostate cancer and Alzheimer's is the fastest-growing, highest-margin advanced modality, though it is also the biggest near-term cost driver via isotope tracer inflation.

Digital Health is the optionality. ARR more than doubled YoY to $96.9M at Q1 2026, against a $140M 2026 target (from $75M at YE2025). The Gleamer acquisition ($264.1M, the largest deal in the company's history) added a French radiology-AI platform spanning X-ray, MRI, CT, and mammography, with products going live in California centers within a month of close. RadNet is positioning as a hybrid imaging-services plus health-IT company, claiming the most widely used lung-cancer-screening solution in the UK and a 21% lift in breast-screening detection. The strategic logic is sound: own the AI layer, deploy it internally to lift throughput and margins, and sell it externally.

The caveat is that this is a "spend now, monetize later" bet funded by stock and cash. Digital Health remains GAAP loss-making and is expected to continue operating at a net loss in the near term, with losses currently accelerating. The estimate revision picture is mildly negative: current-year EPS estimates are down 18.4% over 90 days to $0.52, current-quarter down 11.5%, and next-year down 6.6% to $0.94. Consensus is trimming, not raising. Revenue growth is strong, but the earnings line is moving the wrong way. The growth score of 6.8 captures genuine top-line momentum and credible AI optionality, discounted for the lack of GAAP earnings traction and the modestly falling EPS path.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is meaningless here (-308x on a net loss), so valuation must rest on EBITDA, FCF, and the growth/AI multiple. At a $4.6B market cap, and adding roughly $630M of net debt (~$1,085M term debt less $455M cash), enterprise value is roughly $5.3B against $300M of 2025 adjusted EBITDA: about 18x EV/EBITDA, and roughly 15-16x on a forward $340-360M EBITDA assuming guidance delivery. FCF against the market cap is a ~1.9% FCF yield, thin for a capital-intensive operator but understated relative to underlying cash generation given the elevated growth capex.

Against peers, the comparison is noisy because the "Health Care Services" cohort is heterogeneous (peer median P/E 38.2, ranging from Concentra at 18.5x to Veracyte at 45x to Hims at a negative multiple). RadNet does not map cleanly onto any of them, being part imaging operator and part health-IT company. On a forward earnings basis, next-year consensus of $0.94 puts the stock at roughly 62x, which looks expensive until you recognize that GAAP EPS is depressed by SBC, D&A on an asset-heavy base, and Digital Health losses that management is funding deliberately. Valuation is reasonable-to-full on EBITDA, expensive on near-term GAAP earnings, and a call option on Digital Health that the market is partly already paying for.

Analyst targets ($65 low, $93.5 median, $100 high, $89.75 mean) sit well above the current $58.20, implying meaningful upside if the AI/EBITDA narrative holds. I treat these as aspirational rather than floors: this is a high-multiple growth-and-optionality stock where the targets and the prior $85 print reflected the same consensus optimism that has since faded by 32%. The 90-day downward EPS revisions argue against treating the median target as a near-term anchor. The valuation score of 5.3 reflects a fair-to-slightly-rich EBITDA multiple with genuine optionality but no clear, falsifiable mispricing that the market is missing.

## Competitive Landscape

RadNet's competitive position is the strongest dimension and improving. As the scale leader in outpatient imaging, it competes against independent operators, PE-backed chains, regional players, and hospital imaging departments. Its edge comes from regional density, payor and referring-physician relationships, multi-modality breadth, and a structural cost advantage versus hospital-based imaging. The trajectory is favorable: same-center volume growth, share gains via aggressive M&A (Regional Radiology, Northwest Radiology, Intermountain Medical Imaging), and entry into new states.

The proprietary AI stack separates RadNet from a commodity imaging roll-up. Owning DeepHealth/Gleamer lets it improve internal throughput and detection while building an external software franchise that few competitors can match. The 22 FDA clearances and 2,890+ external customers represent a real moat-widening asset, even if it loses money on a GAAP basis today. The competitive position score of 7.0 reflects clear share gains and a widening, technology-enabled moat, tempered by the reality that the AI advantage is still pre-monetization and the core imaging business remains exposed to reimbursement and wage policy it cannot control.

## Risk Assessment

**Reimbursement and regulatory rate cuts.** About 25% of revenue is Medicare, ~3% Medicaid. The CY2026 Physician Fee Schedule could reduce reimbursement, and fee-schedule pricing prevents RadNet from passing through cost inflation. This is the single most important risk the market may underweight.

**Structural labor-cost inflation.** California healthcare minimum wage is the clearest margin headwind, and the 10-K warns the effect would compound if other states follow. Multi-year contracted payor rates limit cost recovery, so wage inflation flows straight to margin.

**Operating leverage cuts both ways.** High fixed costs mean small volume or rate declines hit margins disproportionately, the same lever that magnifies upside in good years.

**Digital Health cash burn and goodwill impairment.** The segment is expected to keep losing money near-term; goodwill at $1.09B and rising is an impairment exposure if AI deals underperform.

**Acquisition integration and dilution.** An aggressive M&A pace strains integration and dilutes equity through stock-funded deals and heavy SBC.

**Key-man and VIE structural risk.** Heavy dependence on founder-CEO Berger, who also controls the Group VIEs (a related party), plus a corporate-practice-of-medicine structure that could face legal challenge.

**Interest-rate exposure.** $959M of floating-rate debt with expired swaps.

**Cybersecurity.** Healthcare ransomware risk is explicit; RadNet has previously experienced unauthorized network access.

The risk score of 5.0 reflects that the most material risks (reimbursement, wage policy, Digital Health burn) are partly but not fully priced, with a downside skew if CMS cuts bite or margin recovery stalls, offset by low near-term solvency risk.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 53.5% sits above 1-year historical volatility of 42.6% (a 10.9pp rich spread), so the market is pricing elevated uncertainty into the near-dated tenor, with an implied move of ±5.8% on the weekly. The term structure is in contango (53.5% near vs. 58.5% at three months), indicating no acute near-term event fear but rising uncertainty into the fall. Put/call skew is balanced (1.4%) and put/call OI ratio collapsed from 2.47 to 1.04 since late May as the stock rose 8.6%, so bearish positioning has been unwinding. The 13.8% short interest with 10.9 days to cover is elevated and creates moderate squeeze potential on positive news, but is not at the extreme that would force a covering rally. The options market is neutral-to-mildly-constructive, consistent with a HOLD rather than confirming either a strong long or short thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** RadNet is the scale leader in a structurally growing market, compounding revenue at 11-22% with record EBITDA and a credible plan for EBITDA to outgrow revenue as California wage inflation laps and operating leverage reasserts. Digital Health is a genuine, owned AI franchise with ARR doubling toward $140M and a moat that no imaging competitor can replicate. As the wage step-up annualizes and Digital Health approaches breakeven, GAAP earnings inflect, FCF grows 29-41% as guided, and the stock re-rates toward the $90+ analyst targets. The bull case target is $90-100, roughly +55% to +72% from $58.20.

**Bear Case.** The market is paying a full EBITDA multiple for a business that is GAAP loss-making, diluting shareholders, and burning cash on AI acquisitions whose monetization is unproven. CMS cuts and ongoing wage inflation keep imaging margins compressed; Digital Health losses widen rather than narrow; goodwill faces impairment; floating-rate interest costs climb with no hedge. Estimates continue to drift lower (already -18% on the year), and the stock de-rates toward 13-14x EBITDA. The bear case target is $44-46, roughly -22% to -24%.

The base case sits between: guidance is largely delivered on revenue and EBITDA, GAAP losses narrow but do not turn cleanly positive, and the stock works back toward the low-to-mid $60s as EBITDA grows into the multiple. Base case target ~$64, +10% from current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a 12-month reassessment horizon, classified as uncertain — the evidence does not support a confident directional call in either direction. The growth and AI story is real, but GAAP losses, falling EPS estimates, full EBITDA multiple, and net insider selling near the highs prevent a BUY, while strong revenue momentum, a widening competitive moat, low solvency risk, and an unwinding put base prevent an AVOID.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- GAAP operating margin recovers above 5% for two consecutive quarters as California wage inflation laps and Digital Health losses narrow.
- EPS estimate revisions turn positive (current-year and next-year estimates rising rather than the current -18% / -6.6% trend), confirming the earnings inflection.
- Digital Health reaches or beats the $140M ARR target with a credible segment-breakeven timeline.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material CMS reimbursement cut that management cannot offset, driving 2026 imaging guidance below the 17-19% range.
- Goodwill impairment charge on the AI acquisitions, or Digital Health GAAP losses widening beyond ~$70M annualized.
- Covenant pressure or a forced equity raise, or net debt rising materially above current levels without commensurate EBITDA growth.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q2 2026, expected early-mid August), which will show whether margins are recovering off the seasonally weak Q1 and whether estimate revisions stabilize.

## Conclusion

RadNet is a high-quality franchise — the scale leader in outpatient imaging with a genuinely differentiated, owned AI software business — trading at a fair-to-full EBITDA multiple while carrying a real and unresolved margin-quality problem. The market is currently valuing the company on record adjusted EBITDA and the DeepHealth narrative while looking through GAAP losses, falling EPS estimates, and meaningful dilution. That can persist as long as revenue keeps beating, but it leaves the stock vulnerable if the earnings inflection management has guided to does not materialize.

The trajectory has been improving (score 5.4 in March to 6.3 in May), driven by valuation and competitive-position upgrades, and the latest data partly confirms that: revenue accelerated and the competitive moat continues to widen. But the 90-day downward EPS revisions, accelerating Digital Health losses, and the absence of any insider buying on a 32% pullback argue against extending the improvement into a buy call. Insiders sold near the highs in the high $70s/$80s and have not stepped in at $58. With balanced options positioning and no clear catalyst before Q2 earnings, the disciplined posture is to hold and watch for a confirmed margin recovery before adding. **HOLD.**

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: net debt was overstated (~$1.09B used instead of ~$630M after netting $455M cash), lowering EV/EBITDA from ~19x to ~18x (and forward from ~16-17x to ~15-16x) — i.e., valuation is modestly less rich than the report implied; an unsupported macro claim about a "40-46% rate-hike probability" was removed.
