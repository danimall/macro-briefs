## Executive Summary

Leggett & Platt is a 140-year-old diversified industrial manufacturer facing structural revenue decline across its core segments: bedding components, automotive comfort systems, and furniture/flooring products. Revenue has fallen 24% from $5.15B (2022) toward a guided $3.9B (2026), driven by import competition, customer financial distress, and EV transition headwinds. The company absorbed $1.12B in asset impairments across 2023-2024 and cut its dividend 89% after 52 consecutive years of increases.

On April 13, 2026, Somnigroup International (the combined Tempur-Sealy/Mattress Firm entity) agreed to acquire LEG in an all-stock transaction at approximately $11.25/share equity value. The deal offers minimal upside from the current $11.37 price and carries material regulatory risk given Somnigroup is already LEG's largest customer. If the deal fails, LEG is left as a declining-revenue business with $500M in debt maturing 2027, no growth catalysts, and an earnings trajectory that has missed estimates in three of four recent quarters.

The stock does not offer attractive risk/reward in either scenario: deal completion delivers roughly flat returns (merger arbitrage spread is negligible), while deal failure exposes shareholders to a company with deteriorating fundamentals, elevated short interest (11.8%), and no credible path to revenue stabilization.

## Company Overview

Leggett & Platt operates through three segments: Bedding Products (~38% of sales), Specialized Products (~20%), and Furniture, Flooring & Textile Products (~21%). The company is vertically integrated in bedding, operating a ~500,000-ton steel rod mill in Sterling, Illinois, and manufacturing components from raw steel through finished innersprings and foam. It operates 104 manufacturing facilities across 18 countries with 15,900 employees.

The pending Somnigroup acquisition would create a vertically integrated bedding entity with $11.2B in projected sales, combining LEG's component manufacturing with Somnigroup's mattress brands (Tempur-Pedic, Sealy, Stearns & Foster) and retail distribution (Mattress Firm). LEG shareholders would receive 0.1455 SGI shares per LEG share, representing approximately 9% of the combined entity.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Trajectory:** The decline is unambiguous and accelerating on a quarterly basis. Full-year revenue: $5.15B (2022) → $4.73B (2023) → $4.38B (2024) → $4.06B (2025) → ~$3.9B guided (2026). Q4 2025 annualized run rate is $3.75B, suggesting the guided $3.9B may prove optimistic.

**Profitability:** After two years of net losses ($(137M) in 2023, $(512M) in 2024 driven by $1.12B in impairments), LEG returned to profitability in 2025 at $235M net income. However, approximately $93M of this came from non-recurring items (Aerospace divestiture gain, insurance proceeds, real estate sales). Adjusted EBITDA has declined 42% from $656.6M (2022) to $383.7M (2025).

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $338M in 2025 benefited from abnormally low capex ($57M vs. normalized $100-115M). Normalized FCF is approximately $225-240M. At the current $1.55B market cap, this represents a 14.5-15.5% normalized FCF yield, optically cheap but reflecting the declining trajectory.

**Balance Sheet:** Total debt of $1.50B in three tranches ($500M each maturing 2027, 2029, 2051) at an average 3.8% rate. Net debt/EBITDA has improved to ~2.4x from restructuring and asset sales, but the $500M 2027 maturity creates urgency: if the Somnigroup deal fails, LEG must refinance with declining earnings power. Debt-to-equity at 3.46x reflects the equity destruction from impairments. Altman Z-Score of 2.06 sits in the "grey zone" of moderate distress risk.

**Earnings Quality:** Recent quarterly results show three misses and one in-line quarter (Q1 2026: -37.2%, Q4 2025: -6.3%, Q3 2025: -1.0%, Q2 2025: -2.6%). Current-year estimates of $1.08 are being revised downward (-1.1% over 30 days, -0.9% over 90 days). The EPS figure of $1.69 (trailing P/E basis) likely reflects the one-time gains in 2025; forward estimates around $1.08 imply the stock trades at ~10.5x normalized earnings rather than the optically cheap 6.7x.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth story. Revenue has declined 7-8% annually for three consecutive years. Management guided 2026 demand "flat or modestly lower than 2025." The three primary segments face distinct but uniformly negative structural forces:

- **Bedding:** Import competition (partially offset by anti-dumping duties but subject to circumvention), customer consolidation/bankruptcy, and market bifurcation between premium (Tempur-Sealy) and value/imports (online). Organic bedding volumes declined 12% in 2025.
- **Automotive:** Chinese EV manufacturers gaining share at the expense of LEG's traditional OEM customers. Semiconductor disruptions creating production schedule uncertainty. Customers actively replacing higher-cost LEG components with cheaper alternatives.
- **Furniture/Flooring:** Consumer spending weakness, housing market stagnation at elevated mortgage rates, and offshore competition.

The 2024 restructuring plan (21 facility consolidations) delivered $63M in annualized EBIT savings, but these cost reductions have been overwhelmed by volume declines. A new, smaller restructuring plan (3 facilities, ~$15M cost) initiated in February 2026 signals ongoing operational shrinkage.

## Valuation Assessment

At $11.37, LEG trades at:
- 6.7x trailing EPS ($1.69, inflated by one-time gains)
- 10.5x forward EPS ($1.08 consensus FY2026)
- ~6.4x 2025 adjusted EBITDA ($383.7M vs. ~$1.55B market cap + ~$0.91B net debt = ~$2.46B EV)
- ~7x normalized FCF ($225M)

These multiples are low in absolute terms but reflect: (1) revenue declining 7-8% annually with no stabilization visible, (2) three of four recent quarterly earnings misses, (3) $751M goodwill with thin cushions (20-34% above carrying value), (4) eliminated dividend growth story, and (5) the pending acquisition which caps near-term upside.

The Somnigroup deal at $11.25/share equity value (the approximate exchange ratio implied value) means the current $11.37 price slightly exceeds the deal value. Analyst consensus targets of $12.00-$13.00 reflect modest deal-completion premiums but offer only 6-14% upside, insufficient compensation for the material regulatory and completion risks.

The low multiple reflects genuine fundamental deterioration rather than market mispricing. The company has destroyed 69% of shareholder value over five years while peers gained 42%. The market's assessment appears rational given the earnings trajectory.

## Competitive Landscape

LEG's competitive advantages — vertical integration, scale, 1,247 patents, and trade protection — are real but eroding. The company's fundamental challenge is that its dominant market positions exist in structurally declining or commoditizing markets:

- **Bedding components:** Market leadership means dominant share of a shrinking domestic production base as imports gain ground. Anti-dumping duties provide partial protection but haven't reversed volume declines.
- **Automotive:** Share in traditional OEM seat comfort systems loses relevance as EVs (with different design architectures and Chinese suppliers) capture the growth.
- **Furniture/Flooring:** Commoditized markets with limited differentiation potential.

The Somnigroup deal implicitly acknowledges that LEG cannot reverse these trends independently. Vertical integration with the largest downstream customer/retailer represents defensive consolidation.

## Risk Assessment

**Deal Failure (Primary Risk):** The all-stock Somnigroup acquisition requires regulatory approval for vertical integration of a dominant component supplier with the dominant mattress manufacturer/retailer. Multiple law firms are investigating price adequacy. If the deal fails, LEG faces:
- $500M debt maturity in 2027 requiring refinancing at higher rates with declining earnings
- No credible standalone growth strategy
- Potential further goodwill impairments (only 20-34% cushion on remaining $751M)
- Stock likely reverts toward $8-9 range (pre-deal announcement levels)

**Continued Revenue Erosion:** Even within the Somnigroup structure, the underlying demand trends remain negative. Combined entity faces $1.7B adjusted EBITDA target against cyclical and structural headwinds.

**Chinese Tax Authority Reviews:** Intensified scrutiny with potential material negative impact, per 10-K risk factor disclosure.

**Tariff Cross-Currents:** Complex exposure — Section 232 benefits steel operations but USMCA dependency for Mexico manufacturing and counter-tariff risk for 12 Chinese facilities creates net uncertainty in the current trade environment.

**Short Interest:** At 11.8% with 4.1 days to cover, institutional bearish positioning is elevated. This reflects market consensus that deal risk is material or that deal completion at these terms is not value-creating for LEG shareholders.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst:** Deal failure announcement (regulatory rejection or shareholder vote failure). Timeline: decision likely by Q4 2026 or early 2027. Secondary catalyst: Q2/Q3 2026 earnings showing continued revenue deterioration, particularly if demand comes in below "flat or modestly lower" guidance.

**Timing window:** 6-9 months to deal resolution. Carrying cost is meaningful given extended timeline.

**Short interest and squeeze risk:** SI at 11.8% is elevated but not extreme. 4.1 days to cover indicates moderate crowding. The stock spiked from $9.99 to $12.03 on deal announcement; a squeeze in a deal-failure scenario would likely be limited given fundamental deterioration would dominate.

**Borrow and options signal:** No options data available. The 11.8% SI suggests borrow availability exists but may carry elevated cost.

**Downside target and risk/reward:** If deal fails, stock likely reverts to $8.50-9.50 (pre-announcement range), representing 17-25% downside. Upside risk is limited to deal completion premium ($12.00-12.50) or competing bid (highly unlikely given structural challenges), roughly 6-10% upside. Risk/reward approximately 2.5:1 in favor of shorts if deal-failure probability exceeds 30%.

**Verdict:** **NOT SHORTABLE** — The timing window is too extended (6-9 months), the carrying cost at 11.8% SI likely makes borrow expensive, and deal-failure probability is difficult to estimate without visibility into FTC/DOJ regulatory review. The asymmetry is theoretically favorable but the catalyst is not sufficiently timed or probable to justify active shorting over simply avoiding ownership.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($12.50-13.00):** Somnigroup deal completes on schedule by year-end 2026, regulatory approval obtained without material divestitures, and LEG shareholders benefit from combined entity synergies. ~$50M annual synergies on $1.7B combined EBITDA creates modest accretion. This represents 10-14% upside from current levels.

**Bear Case ($7.50-9.00):** Deal fails due to antitrust concerns over vertical integration, LEG left as standalone declining-revenue company facing $500M debt maturity in 2027, continued volume erosion forces further impairments on remaining $751M goodwill, and stock reverts to or below pre-announcement levels. This represents 21-34% downside.

**Base Case ($10.50-11.50):** Extended deal timeline with regulatory conditions, creating a prolonged holding period where shareholders bear ongoing earnings deterioration risk for modest deal-premium upside. Essentially a wash from current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria (conditions that would change view):**
- Deal completion with confirmed regulatory approval and clear integration synergy timeline
- Evidence of revenue stabilization (two consecutive quarters of flat or positive organic growth)
- New standalone strategic plan with credible path to >$4B revenue and margin expansion

**Timeline:** The AVOID recommendation persists until deal resolution (estimated Q4 2026) or evidence of fundamental stabilization emerges. Both outcomes require 6-12 months minimum.

## Conclusion

Leggett & Platt presents unfavorable risk/reward at $11.37. The stock offers negligible upside in the deal-completion scenario (trading roughly at deal value) while bearing material downside if the acquisition fails. The underlying business has declined 24% in revenue over four years, missed earnings in three of four recent quarters, shows negative estimate revision momentum, and faces structural competitive headwinds across all three segments. No insider has purchased shares on the open market despite the depressed valuation. The dividend cut, $1.12B in impairments, and five-year TSR of -69% vs. peers at +42% all confirm a fundamentally impaired business rather than a misunderstood value opportunity. The pending deal caps any investment case that might otherwise exist: shareholders are betting on deal completion for a few percentage points of upside against substantial downside if it fails.
