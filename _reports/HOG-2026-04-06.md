## Executive Summary

Harley-Davidson is in the middle of a severe earnings downcycle. The core HDMC segment lost $29M at the operating level in FY2025 on a 16.4% decline in shipments, and 2026 guidance calls for another operating loss of ($40M) to breakeven at best. The apparent 7.5x trailing P/E is misleading: FY2025 EPS of $2.78 was propped up by ~$490M in HDFS operating income driven by one-time gains from selling $4.1B in retail finance receivables to KKR/PIMCO. That HDFS income drops to $45M-$60M in 2026 and won't recover to even half its 2025 level until ~2029.

On a forward basis, consensus estimates for FY2026 are $0.27 EPS — implying a 77x forward P/E at $20.86. The 90-day revision trend is catastrophic: current-year estimates cut 86%, next-year estimates cut 47%. The company faces a $140M-$450M unaccrued brake recall liability, $75M-$105M in tariff costs, a strategic vacuum under a new CEO, and U.S. market share that has declined from 37.9% to 34.5% in two years. The 21.7% short interest reflects institutional conviction that fundamentals are still deteriorating.

The HDFS Transaction did meaningfully de-lever the balance sheet ($3.1B cash, $4B less debt), and three insider purchases near current levels provide a modest counterweight. But the business is structurally producing ~125K-135K units against a factory footprint built for ~300K, creating punishing fixed cost absorption. Without a credible path to volume recovery or structural cost reset, the equity is a value trap.

## Company Overview

Harley-Davidson operates through three segments: HDMC (motorcycle manufacturing, ~80% of revenue), HDFS (financial services, ~19%), and LiveWire (electric motorcycles, <1%). The company sells through 1,174 independent dealerships globally (554 in the U.S.) and commands 34.5% of the U.S. 601+cc motorcycle market.

The "Hardwire" strategic plan (2021-2025) concluded at year-end 2025. New CEO Artie Starrs (appointed October 1, 2025) is conducting a comprehensive review with a new plan expected Q2 2026. The company recently cut ~800 production jobs and initiated broader workforce reductions, explicitly acknowledging that "operating expenses are built for materially higher volumes than today's demand."

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue: Three Consecutive Years of Decline

Total revenue fell from $5.84B (FY2023) to $5.19B (FY2024) to $4.47B (FY2025) — a cumulative 23% decline. Motorcycle shipments dropped from 179,984 to 148,862 to 124,477 over the same period, a 31% reduction. Worldwide retail sales fell 12.4% in FY2025 to 132,535 units. U.S. retail sales declined 12.9%.

HDMC revenue specifically fell 13.2% YoY to $3.58B. HDFS revenue fell 16.3% to $869M, reflecting the receivables portfolio reduction from the HDFS Transaction.

### Profitability: HDMC Operating Loss

HDMC operating income went from $661M (13.6% margin) in FY2023 to $278M (6.7%) in FY2024 to **($29M)** (-0.8% margin) in FY2025. The drivers are mechanical: (1) fewer units over a high fixed cost base, (2) $67M in incremental tariff costs, (3) higher incentive spending to clear inventory. Each 10% volume decline at these production levels amplifies the margin hit because fixed costs represent a larger share of unit economics.

Consolidated operating income of $387M was almost entirely HDFS's $490M contribution, which was inflated by $191M in credit loss allowance releases tied to the receivables sale. Strip that out and normalized consolidated operating income was roughly ($100M).

Q4 2025 was particularly severe: EPS of ($2.44) vs. consensus ($1.05), a -132% miss. This was the largest quarterly miss in the dataset.

### Balance Sheet: Stronger Post-Restructuring

The HDFS Transaction created a materially improved balance sheet. Cash rose from $1.59B to $3.09B, long-term debt fell from $6.32B to $2.47B, and finance receivables dropped from $7.29B to $1.97B. Total liquidity stands at $4.3B. However, $1.3B in debt matures in 2026, and the company has paused share repurchases.

Credit ratings are investment-grade but S&P has HOG on CreditWatch Negative (BBB-). Moody's Baa3 stable, Fitch BBB+ stable.

### Cash Flow

Operating cash flow declined to $569M from $1.06B, reflecting lower volumes and tariff costs. CapEx was $154M. Free cash flow of ~$415M funded $347M in buybacks (13.1M shares). Shares outstanding declined 23% from 145.9M in 2022 to 111.7M, which has been the primary capital return mechanism.

### 2026 Guidance: Another Loss Year

Management guides: shipments of 130,000-135,000 units, HDMC operating results of ($40M) to $10M, HDFS operating income of $45M-$60M, LiveWire losses of $70M-$80M, tariff impact of $75M-$105M. This implies consolidated operating income of roughly ($65M) to ($10M). There is no path to meaningful EPS in 2026.

## Growth Analysis

There are no visible growth catalysts on any near-term horizon:

- **Volume:** U.S. industry registrations fell 6.1% in 2025. The 601+cc market is shrinking. HOG's share within that shrinking market is declining. 2026 shipment guidance of 130K-135K represents modest improvement over FY2025's 124K but is still well below 2023's 180K.
- **Pricing:** The company is increasing incentives, not raising prices. Average revenue per motorcycle is under pressure.
- **New products:** The company plans new small-displacement motorcycles and an "iconic classic cruiser" for 2026, but product cycle revenue contribution won't materialize until FY2027 at earliest.
- **LiveWire:** Shipped 653 units in 2025. Management won't invest further beyond a $75M term loan. This is a cash burn ($75M/year losses) with no strategic payoff visible.
- **HDFS:** Operating income drops to $45M-$60M and won't recover to even $135M-$180M until ~2029.
- **Cost reduction:** $150M in targeted annual savings starting 2027 is real but insufficient to offset the volume/revenue decline.

Estimate revisions confirm the deterioration: current-year EPS estimates were cut 86% over 90 days (from $2.01 to $0.27). Next-year estimates cut 47% (from $2.90 to $1.54). The 30-day trend shows slight stabilization in quarterly estimates but continued erosion in annual figures.

## Valuation Assessment

The headline 7.5x trailing P/E is misleading. FY2025 EPS of $2.78 was driven by one-time HDFS Transaction gains that will not recur.

**Normalized valuation:**
- On FY2026E consensus EPS of $0.27: implied P/E is **77x** — absurd for a cyclically declining manufacturer.
- On FY2027E consensus EPS of $1.54: implied P/E is **13.5x** — reasonable IF you believe estimates are achievable. But estimates have been cut 47% over 90 days, and the direction is still down.
- The $3.09B cash position ($27.60/share) actually exceeds the stock price on a gross basis, but $1.3B matures in 2026 and $2.47B in long-term debt remains. Net cash is roughly $620M ($5.55/share).
- Enterprise value is ~$4.8B on $4.47B in declining revenue. EV/Revenue of ~1.1x would be reasonable for a stable manufacturer but not one losing money at the operating level.
- Dividend yield of 3.6% provides some support, but dividend coverage is questionable with FY2026 EPS near zero. The annual dividend obligation is ~$86M ($0.77/share based on recent payments).

**Peer comparison:** Peer median P/E is 17.9x. HOG's 7.5x trailing looks cheap but is apples-to-oranges given the one-time income inflation. On forward estimates, HOG is dramatically more expensive than peers.

**Analyst targets:** Mean $22.00, median $23.00, low $12.00, high $32.00. The distribution (1 Strong Buy, 5 Buy, 8 Hold, 2 Sell) skews cautious, with the $12 bear case implying 42% further downside.

## Competitive Landscape

Harley-Davidson's U.S. market share has declined from 37.9% (2023) to 34.5% (2025) in the 601+cc segment. This is share loss in a declining market — the worst combination. The total addressable market is also shrinking: U.S. registrations fell from 256,710 to 237,683 over two years.

The brand retains strong emotional attachment from its core customer base. But the demographic challenge is structural: the average Harley buyer is aging, and the company has consistently failed to expand its customer base to younger or more diverse riders. Premium pricing ($20K+ MSRPs) exacerbates this in a high-rate, inflation-stressed consumer environment.

Competitors (Honda, Yamaha, Kawasaki, BMW, Indian/Polaris) generally operate with lower cost bases, broader product portfolios, and less dependence on a single market segment. Indian Motorcycle in particular has been taking share in the American cruiser space.

The LiveWire electric strategy has effectively failed: 653 units shipped in 2025, management has capped investment, and the brand is pivoting to smaller vehicles.

## Risk Assessment

### Critical Risks

1. **Brake recall liability ($140M-$450M, unaccrued).** Tier-2 supplier Proterial provided non-compliant brake hose assemblies potentially affecting motorcycles back to model year 2008. HOG has accrued zero. If NHTSA mandates a recall, the cost range is 6-19% of current market cap. This is a material off-balance-sheet liability.

2. **Tariff escalation.** $67M in FY2025, guided $75M-$105M for FY2026. The EU's 25% rebalancing tariff is suspended but could be reimposed. In the current macro environment (SCOTUS tariff ruling, Section 232 alternatives), tariff policy is highly volatile. Thailand-sourced product exposure is a specific vector.

3. **Consumer discretionary spending collapse.** With consumer sentiment at 56.6 (-12.5% YoY), mortgage assistance searches at records, and 30.5% of car trade-ins underwater, premium discretionary purchases are the first to be cut. $20K+ motorcycles are the definition of deferrable spending.

4. **Fuel cost headwinds.** Oil at ~$107, diesel at $5.29/gallon. Higher gas prices directly increase the operating cost of motorcycles and reduce discretionary spending capacity for potential buyers.

5. **Strategic vacuum.** No forward strategy until Q2 2026 announcement. Buybacks paused. Investment decisions potentially delayed.

6. **Manufacturing deleveraging loop.** Factories built for ~300K units producing ~125K creates a vicious cycle: lower volumes → higher per-unit costs → need for higher prices or more incentives → further volume pressure.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 48.3% is rich relative to 1-year historical vol of 43.7% (4.5pp spread), and the term structure is in backwardation (48.3% near-term vs. 46.0% 6-month), indicating the market is pricing near-term stress. ## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
- The stock trades at $20.86 vs. $3.09B cash ($27.60/share gross), creating apparent deep value.
- The HDFS Transaction de-risked the balance sheet. Net debt is manageable.
- New CEO Starrs made a $286K open-market purchase at $19.10. Two directors also bought. Insider alignment at these levels.
- $150M cost reduction program starting 2027 + factory rationalization could restore HDMC profitability.
- 21.7% short interest creates squeeze potential if the new strategic plan or product launches surprise positively.
- The brand has survived multiple cycles; its cultural significance creates a floor on the franchise value.

### Bear Case
- HDMC is losing money and guided to lose money again in 2026. There is no near-term earnings recovery.
- The 7.5x P/E is illusory; normalized forward P/E is 77x on $0.27 consensus EPS.
- U.S. market share declining in a declining market. Demographic headwinds are structural, not cyclical.
- $140M-$450M brake recall liability is unaccrued and binary.
- The macro environment (stagflation, $107 oil, consumer sentiment collapse, rising rates) is the worst possible backdrop for a premium discretionary manufacturer.
- Estimate revisions are catastrophically negative: -86% on current year, -47% on next year.
- Dividend coverage is questionable at FY2026 EPS of $0.27 (payout ratio >280%).
- LiveWire is a $75M annual cash burn with no strategic return.
- The stock is down 33% from its 52-week high and the decline may not be over — the $12 analyst bear case implies 42% further downside.

### Why AVOID, Not HOLD

The calibration framework requires explicit justification for HOLD scores ≤5.8, and this company scores 4.28. The trajectory is deteriorating across every operating metric: revenue, volumes, market share, margins, and EPS estimates. The apparent valuation cheapness is an artifact of non-recurring HDFS gains. The macro environment (stagflation, consumer contraction, $107 oil, elevated rates) specifically targets premium discretionary manufacturers. The unaccrued $140M-$450M brake recall is an asymmetric downside risk. Insider buying provides a modest counterweight but does not overcome the weight of negative evidence. The Consumer Discretionary BUY suspension from calibration is in effect. This stock fits the AVOID criteria: structurally deteriorating business with worsening trajectory.

## Conclusion

Harley-Davidson is a deteriorating franchise facing structural demand decline, severe manufacturing deleveraging, a hostile macro environment, and a strategic vacuum under a new CEO. The balance sheet improvement from the HDFS Transaction is real but provides a balance sheet cushion but no growth engine. The normalized forward P/E of 77x on consensus estimates — estimates that have been slashed by 86% over 90 days — offers no margin of safety. The $140M-$450M unaccrued brake recall liability adds material downside risk. The 21.7% short interest reflects informed institutional positioning against the equity.

The earliest credible catalyst is the Q2 2026 strategic plan announcement, but even optimistic scenarios require 12-18 months to manifest in financial results. At current levels, the risk/reward skews to the downside. **AVOID.**
