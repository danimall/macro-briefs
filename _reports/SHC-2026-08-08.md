## Executive Summary

Sotera Health delivered a beat-and-raise on August 6 and, in the same filing, disclosed that ethylene oxide claim counts are rising in the two jurisdictions with the worst historical outcomes. Both facts belong in the same paragraph because they define the investment problem. Operationally the business is performing: Q2 2026 revenue of $321.4M grew 9.2% (+8.0% constant currency), Adjusted EBITDA of $165.7M grew 10.0% at a 51.6% margin, FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance moved to $634-643M, net leverage reached 3.0x — the top of the company's 2.0-3.0x long-term target — and the term loan was repriced a second time in eight months to SOFR+2.25%, cutting the weighted-average cost of total debt from 7.49% to 6.49% year over year. Legally, the trend has been favorable: the Georgia trial court excluded plaintiffs' general-causation experts and entered summary judgment for defendants in all five remanded pool cases on March 30, 2026; New Mexico settled for an immaterial amount on June 24 with the Santa Teresa injunction dissolved; and the Sixth Circuit affirmed dismissal of the securities class action on February 24.

The price has responded. At $18.86 the shares are up 68% from a year ago and sit 5.0% below the 52-week high of $19.85. That leaves 11.4x EV to the FY2026E Adjusted EBITDA midpoint of $638.5M, 19.2x the $0.98 Adjusted EPS midpoint, and 17.6x the $1.07 FY2027 consensus. The problem is cash. FY2026 capex guidance was raised to $200-225M from $175-225M at the same meeting that lifted EBITDA guidance by $2.0M at the midpoint, and H1 2026 free cash flow was $25.3M against $61.8M a year earlier. A defensible FY2026 free-cash-flow range of $85-150M equates to a 1.6-2.8% yield on the current market capitalization. A 51% EBITDA margin that converts to under 3% of market value in free cash flow is the central tension in the valuation.

Against that, the risk that is not priced at a 52-week high is the same risk that was heavily priced at $11. Q2's Note 13 shows Illinois cases rising from one (with two plaintiffs) in February to approximately fifteen, in a venue that produced a $358.7M judgment and a $408.0M settlement, and California rising from ~120 to ~150 claims with initial trials scheduled for January and April 2027. EO insurance limits of $10M per occurrence and $20M aggregate are fully utilized and current and future policies exclude EO claims outright, so every future defense and settlement dollar is self-funded — visible today as $43.1M of TTM "professional services relating to EO facilities," roughly 7% of Adjusted EBITDA, permanently added back in the non-GAAP presentation. We rate the shares HOLD with a $20.75 base case (+10%), a $25.00 bull case (+33%), and a $13.50 bear case (-28%).

## Company Overview

Sotera provides outsourced terminal sterilization, cobalt-60 supply, and laboratory testing across 62 facilities, more than 50 countries, and over 3,000 employees. FY2025 revenue of $1,163.6M split across three segments:

**Sterigenics (64.9% of revenue, 69.5% of segment income, 54.6% margin)** operates 48 facilities in 13 countries — 23 gamma, 17 ethylene oxide, 8 E-beam, with an X-ray site in final qualification. Terminal sterilization is the final, frequently mandated step before a device ships. Over 2,000 customers; more than 90% of segment revenue under multi-year contracts; the 10-K claims 100% renewal among the top ten customers for more than five consecutive years. The lock-in mechanism is validation, not contract length: customers validate a specific product against a specific modality at a specific site and hold registrations tied to that arrangement, so switching requires revalidation and regulatory refiling.

**Nordion (16.1% of revenue, 18.1% of segment income, 57.3% margin)** is the leading global supplier of Co-60, which the company estimates supports roughly 30% of worldwide single-use device sterilization. Co-60 decays at approximately 12% per year, producing non-discretionary replenishment demand, and conversion of cobalt-59 targets inside a reactor takes 18 months to five years. Over 200 large-scale gamma systems are active globally; Nordion built more than 120. The Kanata Class 1B nuclear licence was renewed in September 2025 for 25 years.

**Nelson Labs (18.9% of revenue, 12.3% of segment income, 33.3% margin)** provides over 900 microbiological and analytical tests plus regulatory advisory services.

One structural nuance limits how much the vertical integration is worth: information barriers established when Nordion was acquired in 2014 — some required by Nordion customer contracts — expressly "prohibit us from managing a customer pricing strategy across our Sterigenics and Nordion segments." Integration delivers supply security and investment confidence, not cross-segment pricing coordination.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue compounded at 5.30% from FY2023's $1,049.3M to FY2025's $1,163.6M, with Adjusted EBITDA compounding 5.40% from $481.2M in 2021 to $593.8M in 2025. Growth is pricing-led: Sterigenics Q2 2026 revenue growth of 8.6% decomposed as price +4.3%, volume/mix +2.7%, FX +1.6%; for H1, price +4.4% against volume/mix +2.2%.

Three items materially affect how the reported numbers should be read.

**The GAAP earnings surge is largely non-cash.** TTM net income of $163.5M against FY2025's $77.9M looks like a step-change in profitability. Finite-lived intangible gross carrying value fell from $928.4M at 12/31/2024 to $386.0M at 12/31/2025 as fully amortized LBO-era customer relationships were written off (gross $647.0M to $167.3M). Amortization expense went $81.3M (2023) → $79.4M (2024) → $41.8M (2025) → $22.4M (TTM), with only $20-21M scheduled annually through 2030. Roughly $55-58M of annual pre-tax GAAP expense disappeared with zero change in cash flow. The trailing P/E of 46x on $0.27 of GAAP EPS and the TTM P/E of 32.9x are both artifacts of this transition; neither is a usable valuation input. The offset is that GAAP net income is now a materially better proxy for cash earnings than it was.

**Free cash flow is being squeezed as margins expand.** H1 2026 operating cash flow rose $5.0M to $117.9M while capex rose $41.5M to $92.6M, so FCF fell 59% to $25.3M. Accrued-but-unpaid capex in accounts payable was $35.4M at 6/30/2026 versus $10.3M a year earlier, so accrual-basis H1 capex was closer to $117.7M. Reported FY2025 FCF of $149.2M included a $34.0M Illinois settlement accrual not paid until February 2026, putting cash-basis FY2025 FCF nearer $115M. Construction in progress was $259.9M at year-end — capital not yet earning.

Two derivations bracket FY2026. Top-down: EBITDA midpoint $638.5M less cash interest ~$140M, cash taxes ~$52M, capex midpoint $212.5M, EO professional services ~$43M, and business optimization ~$6M gives ~$185M, less the $34.0M February settlement payment = ~$151M; charging the ~$37M of share-based compensation that Adjusted EBITDA adds back reduces it to ~$114M. Bottom-up: H1 actual $25.3M plus H2 operating cash flow of ~$180M against H2 capex of $107-132M gives $75-100M. The gap is working capital ($61.0M H1 outflow versus a $33.9M inflow a year earlier) and tax/interest timing. Treat $85-150M as a sensitivity, not a forecast: a **1.6%-2.8% FCF yield**. Roughly $51M of FY2026 capex is non-revenue-generating environmental compliance spending the 10-K says will continue.

**Leverage and interest cost are genuinely improving.** At 6/30/2026: total debt $2,234.8M (term loan $1,415.9M, 7.375% secured notes $750.0M, finance leases $95.3M), cash $356.7M, net debt $1,878.0M, net leverage 3.0x on TTM Adjusted EBITDA of $621.6M. Liquidity is ~$950M with the $600M revolver undrawn but for $8.3M of letters of credit. Amortization is 1% annually (~$14.2M) with $2.1B due in 2031. Interest coverage is 4.34x on TTM Adjusted EBITDA to net interest expense. FY2026 interest expense guidance of $135-142M compares with $155.7M in FY2025 and $164.7M in FY2024. A $400M pay-fixed swap runs to 8/31/2027; a 1% SOFR increase would add $10.2M annually.

The covenant that matters is not a leverage test. The credit agreement defaults if enforceable judgments exceed the greater of $162.6M or 30% of consolidated EBITDA — approximately $186.5M today — and are not stayed or discharged within 60 consecutive days. Risk factors separately warn that appellate bonds "may create a substantial strain on liquidity." That is the transmission mechanism from tort exposure to credit risk, and it is why leverage and litigation cannot be assessed independently here.

On the distress screens: a standardized Altman Z near 0.9 is not usable — its inputs implied EBIT of roughly $10M against actual FY2025 EBIT of ~$303.2M (9.3% of assets) and substituted book for market equity. Recomputed with 6/30/2026 data the score is 1.96, in the grey zone and improved from 1.79 at year-end. What depresses it is a $451.9M accumulated deficit (LBO-era losses plus the 2022 Illinois charge) and 0.37x asset turnover, both structural to a capital-intensive high-margin service business. Note the circularity: MVE/total liabilities contributes 1.22 of the 1.96, so a sharp share-price decline mechanically worsens the score.

Tax is a persistent drag. FY2025's 47.2% effective rate reflected a US pre-tax loss of $69.4M against foreign pre-tax income of $216.9M — debt in the US, earnings offshore — producing a $186.9M disallowed-interest deferred tax asset carrying a $172.3M valuation allowance. Canadian withholding added 10.3 points and Canada accounted for $34.6M of $60.9M of cash taxes. The rate is improving (31.3% in Q2, guided 27-28% on an adjusted basis; TTM GAAP still 38.1%) and the July 2025 OBBBA produced a $16.6M valuation-allowance release, so the mechanism works. But most of the near-term improvement is already in guidance.

## Growth Analysis

Raised FY2026 guidance implies 5.25-6.75% constant-currency revenue growth and $634-643M of Adjusted EBITDA. Working from the H1 actual of $300.4M, the midpoint implies **H2 Adjusted EBITDA of ~$338.1M against $321.2M in H2 2025 — about 5.3% growth, decelerating from H1's 10.2%**. Management is not extrapolating H1's tailwinds, and they are right not to.

Two H1 drivers do not repeat cleanly. Nordion grew 16.7% constant currency in Q2 with volume/mix of +18.1% for the half, which management attributes explicitly to "the timing of Cobalt-60 harvest schedules"; the 10-Q cautions that Nordion sales patterns "can often vary significantly from one quarter to the next" and that gamma-system sales "occur infrequently and tend to be for larger amounts." And Nelson Labs' recovery is one quarter old: H1 revenue rose 2.9% (+0.9% cc) with segment income down 4.9% and margin down 250bps to 30.4%, Q1 revenue actually fell 0.7% (-3.8% cc), and backing out disclosed price (+2.8%) and FX (+2.0%) implies roughly -1.9% volume/mix for the half. FY2025 Nelson revenue fell 3.9% with roughly $23.4M of headwind from declining expert advisory services.

The durable engine is Sterigenics pricing at roughly double volume growth. Whether that persists depends on whether NESHAP compliance costs are in fact forcing smaller EO competitors out, tightening capacity. The external bull case asserts this; no filing we reviewed corroborates it, and no market-share data is disclosed. Treat competitor exit as a plausible mechanism, not an established fact — but note that the observed pricing pattern is consistent with it.

Consensus reflects this profile honestly: current-year EPS $0.98 rising to $1.07 next year, about 9% growth. Revisions are mildly positive (current year +1.3% over 30 days, +1.0% over 90; next year +0.7%/+1.1%) while current- and next-quarter estimates drifted down 0.3-1.4%. This is nowhere near the +30% revision velocity that signals an underestimated inflection; it is a stable mid-single-digit compounder where consensus is roughly right. The earnings record supports confidence in the estimates rather than upside to them: three beats and one in-line over four quarters, with surprises of 7.6%, 2.1%, 6.2% and 17.8% — modest and driven partly by tax and interest rather than operations.

The 2027-2028 free-cash-flow inflection case is not unreasonable. The X-ray facility, the Westinghouse/PSEG Co-60 initiative, and the Darlington project are discretionary in timing and should convert to revenue and margin later, and $259.9M of construction in progress will start earning. It simply is not visible in 2026, and the capex guide moved the wrong way.

## Competitive Landscape

The structure is a high-barrier oligopoly. A new sterilization facility costs over $50M plus multi-year licensing; the validation and registration mechanism, not contract duration, is what produces 100% top-ten renewal for five-plus years. STERIS AST is the named principal global competitor; the remainder is smaller regional players and manufacturer insourcing.

Rising EO regulation cuts both ways. It imposes mandatory, non-revenue capex (~$51M in FY2026, with "continuing" spend anticipated) and litigation-adjacent reputational cost. It also raises the maintenance bar for subscale competitors, which is the most plausible explanation for pricing running at twice volume.

Nordion is the hardest asset to replicate and the most concentrated. Twelve percent annual decay creates non-discretionary replenishment; spent-source return and slug recycling extend relationships. But supply runs through three reactor operators across six sites in Canada and Russia, with approximately 20% of long-term Co-60 from Russian reactors and potentially up to ~50% in a year with outages elsewhere, and a single ocean carrier licensed to move radioactive goods from Russia to North America whose licenses renew in 2026 and 2027. CNSC import/export licenses for cobalt-59 targets expire in 2028. Off-balance-sheet minimum purchase commitments total $2,379.5M. Diversification via Westinghouse pressurized-water-reactor production, PSEG as the first US utility producer, and the Darlington project is a genuine positive and part of what the elevated capex funds. Note that site counts in the risk factors (six sites, Canada and Russia) and the business section (13 reactors, five stations, plus Argentina, China and India) are not reconcilable from the filings; the precise Russian share is not determinable.

Nelson Labs is the soft spot: no dominant position claimed, competition from national to niche laboratories, FY2025 revenue -3.9%, H1 2026 volume negative, and 33% margins against 55%+ elsewhere. It contributes 18.9% of revenue and 12.3% of segment income, diluting the group multiple.

On concentration, two disclosures establish different things and should not be conflated. At the consolidated level no customer exceeded 10% of revenue or receivables in any of the last three years. Within Nordion, two customers were 34.8% and 11.0% of external segment revenue in FY2025 versus one at 34.3% in FY2024 — concentration that is high and not improving, with the largest Nordion customer representing roughly 5.6% of consolidated revenue. A favorable mix shift toward other segments would not by itself diversify that exposure.

Finally, deferred revenue is $14.2M and no backlog or remaining-performance-obligation figure is disclosed. The ">90% multi-year" contract statistic is not a quantified backlog.

## Risk Assessment

**Ethylene oxide tort litigation.** As of 7/31/2026: Georgia (Cobb County) ~500 personal injury/wrongful death claims plus ~305 property devaluation suits, all trial-court proceedings stayed pending appeals of two defense summary judgments; California (Vernon) ~150 claims, up from ~120 in February, with initial trials scheduled for January and April 2027 and an eight-case First Plaintiff Group in discovery; Illinois (Willowbrook) approximately fifteen cases, up from one case with two plaintiffs in February. Illinois history: a 2022 verdict/judgment of $358.7M including $320M punitive, then $408.0M settling over 880 claims, $30.9M for 97 claims in April 2025, and $34.0M for 129 claims in July 2025. The company's position is that losses in remaining cases are not probable while future settlements are reasonably possible, with no range estimable, and it cautions that prior settlements "should not give rise to presumptions."

Insurance is exhausted: environmental liability limits of $10.0M per occurrence and $20.0M aggregate fully utilized, with current and future environmental and product liability policies excluding EO claims. Every future dollar is self-funded, and the run-rate cost is already visible at $43.1M TTM in EO-related professional services.

The favorable rulings are real and consecutive — expert exclusions on specific causation (10/17/2025), the appellate vacatur in Sotera's favor (10/31/2025), general-causation exclusion and defense summary judgment on remand (3/30/2026), and the immaterial New Mexico settlement with the injunction dissolved (7/7/2026). Every one of them is on appeal, and the first general-causation ruling was itself vacated once, which demonstrates that appellate reversal cuts in both directions. The net exposure is better than a year ago and is not small.

**NESHAP exemption litigation.** The EPA final rule effective 4/5/2024 requires higher control efficiencies, permanent total enclosure and continuous monitoring, generally within two years. A July 17, 2025 Clean Air Act proclamation granted a two-year exemption covering Sotera's facilities. A January 2026 complaint in the District of Columbia seeks to invalidate that exemption, which would restore the original compliance timeline and compress the spending window. Separately the January 2025 FIFRA Interim Decision imposes reduced exposure limits and facility redesign over one to ten years, and SCAQMD adopted new rules with a health risk assessment for the Ontario, CA facility.

**Other.** Single US supplier of EO to the industry, with self-supply capability licensed but uninvested. Technology substitution: FDA approved vaporized hydrogen peroxide as a Category A method in 2024; X-ray substitutes for gamma. Goodwill of $1,091.3M is 33.0% of total assets against $665.6M of total equity, and goodwill valuation was the FY2025 critical audit matter; management stated the test passed with "adequate margin" without quantifying it. The $12.0M H1 goodwill decline was entirely FX translation.

**Governance and management.** No dividend, no buyback authorization, and the largest concentrated holders are gone: Warburg Pincus and GTCR cleared ~56.8M shares (~20% of the company) at $15.168-$15.27 and hold zero. That removes a multi-year overhang and also removes the natural bid, with no repurchase program announced despite leverage reaching target. The sponsor exit price should not be read as a valuation signal in either direction — a 2011-vintage mandate and an underwritten block discount are equally consistent with the facts. Former CEO Petras sold 750,000 shares at $16.45 on 12/8/2025; there were no open-market purchases by officers or directors in the window reviewed, which for a company that delivers equity principally through grants is neutral-to-mildly-negative rather than a strong signal. A CEO transition (Alton Shader, employment offer dated 5/1/2026), a General Counsel departure inferred from a Form 4 title change between 3/2/2026 and 3/31/2026, and two new independent directors all landed within four months — leadership renewal concurrent with the sponsor exit, with the new CEO's background unverified in the sources available. The five-year pay-versus-performance table shows Adjusted EBITDA up 23.4% cumulatively while TSR indexed to 64 against a peer 148, with FY2025 compensation actually paid of $16.7M. Governance mechanics remain sponsor-legacy: classified board, 75% removal-for-cause vote, 66⅔% charter amendment threshold, blank-check preferred, no written consent.

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 19.3% against 37.0% trailing realized — a -17.7 point spread at the 10th percentile of the current chain — reflects the post-earnings vol crush (ATM IV fell from 46.1% on July 24). The practical implication for a holder: downside protection into the litigation calendar is unusually inexpensive at the front end, and rich at 3-6 months. None of this changes the fundamental view; it does argue that anyone holding through the California trial calendar can hedge cheaply now.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($25.00, +33%).** Georgia summary judgments are affirmed on appeal and the California First Plaintiff Group either settles cheaply or produces defense verdicts in early 2027, collapsing the perceived tail. Sterigenics pricing holds at 4%+ as NESHAP compliance costs push subscale EO capacity out. Growth capex rolls off after 2026 as the X-ray facility, Darlington and Westinghouse programs commission, and $259.9M of construction in progress starts earning, lifting FCF toward $250M+ by 2028. Interest expense continues falling with further repricings; the adjusted tax rate settles at 27% and additional valuation-allowance release from the $186.9M disallowed-interest DTA adds EPS. With leverage inside target and no maturities until 2031, the board authorizes a buyback and absorbs post-sponsor supply. On $700M of 2027-2028 EBITDA at 13x with deleveraging, the equity clears $25.

**Bear case ($13.50, -28%).** An adverse California verdict in January or April 2027 resets the market's estimate of aggregate exposure across ~650 pending Georgia and California claims, with insurance exhausted and prospectively excluded. Illinois, having gone from one case to fifteen, reprices toward its historical cost curve. The multiple compresses toward 9.5x forward EBITDA with a settlement reserve, and the judgment-default covenant at ~$186.5M plus appellate bonding requirements converts a tort event into a liquidity event. Independently, the DC exemption challenge succeeds, restoring the original NESHAP timeline and forcing environmental capex above the ~$51M annual run-rate into an already thin FCF profile. Nelson Labs volume stays negative and Nordion normalizes off harvest-timing benefits, pulling constant-currency growth to 3-4%. The September 2022 precedent — a single Illinois verdict removing roughly two-thirds of the equity value in short order — establishes that the left tail is fatter than $13.50.

**Base case ($20.75, +10%).** Appeals grind on without resolution inside twelve months. Constant-currency revenue growth settles at 5-6% with pricing carrying two-thirds of it; FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA reaches ~$675M; interest expense falls to ~$130M; FCF lands at $110-160M as working capital partly reverses but environmental capex persists. The multiple holds near 11.5x forward EBITDA because litigation stays unresolved. Leverage drifts to ~2.7x, and the board initiates a modest buyback.

Probability-weighting 20% bull, 50% base, 30% bear gives roughly +3% expected return over twelve months — approximately fair, with a distinctly two-sided distribution. That is a HOLD, and it is the reason we are not upgrading despite genuine fundamental progress.

**On the fifth consecutive HOLD.** The score trajectory has been 4.8 → 5.3 → 5.7 → 5.8; we mark 5.5 today. The decline is not a reaction to price strength as such. Three specific pieces of new information from the August 6 filings cut against the prior read: Illinois claim counts rose from one case to approximately fifteen and California from ~120 to ~150, which is new adverse disclosure; FY2026 capex guidance was raised $12.5M at the midpoint while EBITDA guidance rose $2.0M, which lowers the FCF base the valuation rests on; and H1 free cash flow of $25.3M ran 59% below the prior year. Financial health improved on the leverage target being achieved and the second repricing. What changed most is the relationship between an unchanged risk profile and a price 8.7% higher than at the last review with a marginally weaker cash-flow outlook — less of the tail is discounted at $18.86 than at $17.35.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Horizon:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoints being the Q3 2026 print (late October/early November), any Georgia appellate ruling, and the California First Plaintiff Group trials in January and April 2027.

**Targets from the $18.86 basis:**
- Base case **$20.75** (+10.0%) — 11.5x FY2027E Adjusted EBITDA of ~$675M less ~$1.80B net debt over 289M diluted shares; cross-checked at 19.4x the $1.07 FY2027 consensus.
- Bull case **$25.00** (+32.6%) — 13x EBITDA on litigation clarity plus a buyback and visible FCF inflection.
- Bear case **$13.50** (-28.4%) — adverse California outcome, multiple to ~9.5x with settlement reserve.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Georgia appellate affirmance of the general-causation exclusion, or a California First Plaintiff Group resolution at immaterial cost — either would justify a 12.5-13x forward EBITDA multiple.
2. Two consecutive quarters of free cash flow above $70M with FY2027 capex guided below $175M, converting the FCF yield above 3.5%.
3. Announcement of a buyback authorization of $250M or more, which would both signal confidence in the litigation reserve and absorb post-sponsor supply.
4. Price toward $16.00-16.50 on unchanged guidance, which would restore roughly 25% upside to the base case.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. An adverse jury verdict in California or Illinois, or any single unstayed judgment approaching the ~$186.5M covenant threshold.
2. Illinois claim count exceeding approximately 50, or California exceeding approximately 250, indicating plaintiff-side filing acceleration ahead of the 2027 trials.
3. FY2027 capex guided above $225M or FY2027 EBITDA guided below $665M, which would break the FCF-inflection premise the bull case depends on.
