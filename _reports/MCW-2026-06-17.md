**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

On February 18, 2026, Mister Car Wash announced a definitive merger agreement to be taken private by its controlling stockholder, Leonard Green & Partners (LGP), at $7.00 per share in cash, an enterprise value of roughly $3.1 billion. The deal was disclosed simultaneously with Q4/FY2025 earnings, and the stock immediately repriced from the low-$6s to $7-plus, where it has stayed pinned for three months. As of the latest data the stock trades at $7.07-$7.10, slightly above the $7.00 cash consideration.

This converts MCW from a fundamental story into a merger-arbitrage situation, and a poor one. There is no positive spread to capture: the market price sits above the deal price, so a buyer at current levels locks in a negative arbitrage return if the deal closes as signed. The only ways to make money from here are (a) a price bump driven by the active fiduciary-duty challenges, which is speculative and uncompensated at this entry, or (b) nothing. The downside is asymmetric: if the deal breaks, the shares would likely retrace toward the pre-announcement trading range (low-to-mid $6s, where the stock spent late 2025/early 2026, with a 52-week low of $4.61). A buyer at $7.08 is therefore risking a 12%-35% decline to capture an arbitrage return that is already negative.

The underlying operating business is sound, and that is precisely the problem the situation presents to minority holders rather than a reason to own the stock. FY2025 was a record year, with revenue above $1 billion and net income recovering to $103 million, yet LGP is buying out the minority at a price well below the 2021 IPO level and below where peers trade. Cumulative TSR since the IPO was 36 versus a peer-group TSR of 134. That value gap is what the shareholder litigation targets, but litigation outcomes are uncertain, slow, and not a thesis a public-market buyer should pay above the deal price to underwrite. We rate MCW AVOID.

## Company Overview

Mister Car Wash, headquartered in Tucson, Arizona, is the largest conveyorized ("express") car wash operator in the United States. The strategic core of the business is the Unlimited Wash Club (UWC), a monthly subscription program that converts transactional customers into recurring, high-margin members. The company grows through a combination of greenfield store builds and acquisitions of existing washes, funded substantially by debt and sale-leaseback structures. MCW went public in mid-2021 in a Leonard Green-backed IPO, and LGP has remained the controlling stockholder throughout, meaning public minority holders have always had limited governance leverage. The pending take-private is the culmination of that controlled-company dynamic.

Operating fundamentals have been steady and improving, which is the central irony of the take-out price.

Revenue has compounded at roughly 6-7% annually, crossing the $1 billion threshold in FY2025. Operating margin reached approximately 19% in FY2025, healthy for the sector, and net income rebounded from the FY2024 trough. Adjusted EBITDAR (the company's preferred metric) rose from $344 million in 2021 to $446 million in 2024.

The tension in the financials is capital intensity. Operating cash flow is strong at about $286 million, but capex consumes roughly $255 million, or 89% of OCF, leaving thin free cash flow of about $30 million. Growth is heavily reinvestment-dependent, and the mechanism matters: each new wash requires upfront capital that is recovered only over multiple years of UWC subscription revenue, so reported free cash flow stays compressed as long as the build pace continues.

The balance sheet carries $796.9 million of long-term debt against $28.5 million of cash, plus a heavy intangible load of $1.13 billion goodwill and $111 million intangibles, together roughly 39% of $3.17 billion total assets, reflecting the roll-up acquisition history. Tangible equity is thin once goodwill is stripped out, which provides little downside cushion in a deal-break scenario.

## Growth Analysis

On a standalone basis MCW shows mid-single-digit organic revenue growth supplemented by store additions, with the UWC subscription base as the recurring-revenue engine. Q4 FY2025 adjusted EPS of $0.11 beat consensus of $0.09-$0.10, and the company has beaten in three of the last four quarters.

For the purposes of this recommendation, standalone growth is moot. The merger agreement fixes the equity value at $7.00 per share regardless of how the operating business performs between signing and close. Consensus EPS estimates have been flat to modestly negative over the past 90 days (current-quarter estimate revised -3.2%, current-year +0.1%, next-year -0.6%), consistent with a name where analysts have stopped revising because the outcome is a fixed cash price, not an earnings trajectory. The growth prospects score of 4.0 reflects that the equity holder captures none of the operating growth, only the cash, and that the upside to that number is capped while the downside on a break is not.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation is set by the deal, not by the market. The take-out implies approximately 7x FY2024 Adjusted EBITDAR and roughly 30x trailing GAAP net income — a full-looking earnings multiple but a depressed enterprise multiple for a market-leading, recurring-revenue franchise. That gap is the entire substance of the shareholder litigation: operations are at a record while the price is below the IPO and below peer valuations.

The relevant valuation question for a buyer today is not whether $7.00 is fair to existing holders, which plainly looks light, but what return is available at $7.08. The answer is negative. Buying above the cash consideration locks in a loss if the deal closes as signed, and exposes the buyer to a sharp drop if it breaks. The only positive-return path is a price bump, which is speculative, undated, and not something a rational arbitrageur pays a premium to the deal price to chance. The valuation score of 3.5 reflects negative arbitrage spread combined with asymmetric downside, not a comment on the standalone business quality.

The cheapness of the take-out relative to fundamentals is real, but the only realization catalyst here — a higher bid or a successful appraisal/litigation outcome — is uncertain and not capturable at a price above the existing offer.

## Competitive Landscape

MCW is the scale leader in a fragmented, highly competitive U.S. express car-wash market that saw heavy private-equity-funded expansion and overbuilding in 2021-2023, pressuring new-store returns industry-wide. The UWC subscription model is the principal differentiator and the main defense against commoditization, and the company's national footprint and membership base are genuine assets. Competitive position is the one dimension where the company scores above neutral (6.0): the franchise is durable and improving at the operating level.

LGP's decision to take the company private rather than continue funding public-market growth signals a view that the value-creation runway is better executed away from public-market scrutiny and at a depressed valuation, a vote of confidence in the asset rather than the public equity.

## Risk Assessment

**Deal-completion risk dominates everything.** The investment now hinges entirely on whether the LGP take-private closes at $7.00. The mechanics:

- **Conflicted-transaction / controlling-stockholder squeeze-out.** Because LGP is both the controlling stockholder and the buyer, the fairness process — special committee independence, majority-of-the-minority vote, fairness opinion — is the crux. A controlling-stockholder squeeze-out carries heightened Delaware (MFW) scrutiny.
- **Active litigation.** Multiple shareholder firms (Bleichmar Fonti & Auld, Johnson Fistel, Halper Sadeh) have opened fiduciary-duty investigations alleging the board and controlling stockholder undervalued the company. The April 2026 SC 13E-3 amendments included additional SEC disclosures, indicating the SEC pushed for more fulsome process disclosure. These challenges create a small probability of a bump (positive for current holders) but also of delay or a break.
- **Break scenario.** If the deal fails, the shares would likely fall back toward the pre-announcement range. A standalone MCW would also re-confront its thin free cash flow, $797 million of debt, and the broader consumer-discretionary softening backdrop, where subscription churn is the key vulnerability if households cut discretionary spending.

The risk score of 3.5 reflects a binary outcome set — close at $7.00 (negative return from $7.08), bump (speculative upside), or break (12-35% downside) — with the asymmetry tilted against a buyer at the current price.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is thin and uninformative, consistent with a near-closed M&A name. The put/call open-interest ratio collapsed from 0.06 to 0.01 over the past month, and the price has been effectively static. There is no meaningful hedging or directional positioning to read; the options market is treating MCW as a settled cash situation rather than a volatile equity. Short interest of 8.9% of float with 3.9 days to cover is modest and most likely reflects merger-arb hedging rather than a fundamental bear thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
A successful fiduciary-duty challenge or active appraisal pressure forces LGP to raise the consideration above $7.00. A bump to, say, $7.50-$8.00 would deliver a 6-13% return from the current price. This is the only path to a positive return for a buyer at $7.08, and it is speculative, undated, and not the base case. Historically, controlling-stockholder squeeze-outs that survive MFW scrutiny close at or near the announced price; bumps occur but are the exception, not the rule.

### Bear Case
The deal breaks on regulatory process grounds, a failed minority vote, or financing/market conditions. Shares retrace toward the pre-announcement low-to-mid $6s, a roughly 12-18% loss, with a tail toward the $4.61 52-week low (-35%) if the break coincides with consumer-discretionary weakness and renewed focus on the thin free cash flow and leverage. There is no operating catalyst to cushion the fall because the stock has been trading on deal value, not fundamentals, for three months.

The base case is that the deal closes at $7.00 and a buyer at $7.08 loses approximately 1% plus carry. That is the most likely outcome and it is negative.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst**: The merger closing at $7.00 is the only dated catalyst, and it would drive the stock down only about 1% from current levels (to the cash consideration). A deal break would drive it down materially, but there is no specific dated trigger for a break — the litigation is investigative, not yet a filed action with a hearing date, and the SC 13E-3 amendment cadence suggests the transaction is progressing toward a vote and close, not collapsing.

**Timing window**: The transaction appears to be moving toward a shareholder vote and close, plausibly within the next one to three months given the April-May 2026 SC 13E-3 amendment activity. A short to capture the $7.08-to-$7.00 convergence offers roughly 1% of downside over an uncertain window.

**Short interest and squeeze risk**: Short interest is 8.9% of float, 3.9 days to cover. This is moderate and largely consistent with merger-arb hedging. The real squeeze risk for a short is a price bump from the litigation, which would force a short to cover above $7.00.

**Borrow and options signal**: Options are inert (P/C OI 0.01), offering no confirmation of a directional thesis. There is no rich put skew to ride and no cheap downside structure available.

**Downside target and risk/reward**: Shorting at $7.08 targets $7.00 on close (about 1% gain) against meaningful upside risk if LGP bumps the bid (a $7.75 bump would be a 9%+ loss for the short). Reward:risk is well below 1:1.

**Verdict**: **NOT SHORTABLE.** The convergence to $7.00 is too small to justify the carry and the asymmetric squeeze/bump risk. This is a name to avoid owning, not to short. The 1% downside-to-deal-price does not compensate for the upside tail from a bid bump.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.**

**Re-entry criteria**: This would become interesting only as a merger-arb position if the stock traded at a meaningful discount to the $7.00 cash consideration (for example, below $6.85), creating positive spread to capture into close. At a price above $7.00 there is no fundamental or arbitrage case to own it. A genuine fundamental re-entry would require the deal to break, the stock to reset to a standalone valuation in the $5-6 range, and evidence that free cash flow is inflecting higher as the build pace moderates — none of which is present today.

**Timeline**: The deal-driven headwind (no spread, asymmetric downside) persists until the transaction closes, which on current filing cadence appears likely within one to three months. After close, the equity ceases to trade.

**What would change the view**: A price discount to $7.00 (re-rate to merger-arb BUY at a positive spread), or a confirmed deal break with a standalone re-rating that restores an arbitrage-free fundamental entry.

## Conclusion

MCW is a sound operating business wrapped in an unattractive securities situation. The signed $7.00 all-cash take-private by Leonard Green caps the equity value, and the stock trades above that cap at $7.08. A buyer here captures negative arbitrage if the deal closes, a speculative and uncompensated bump if litigation forces LGP's hand, or a 12-35% loss if the deal breaks. None of those paths offers an attractive risk-adjusted return. The shareholder fiduciary-duty challenges underscore that minority holders are likely getting a light price, but that is a grievance to litigate, not a return a public-market buyer can underwrite above the offer. We rate MCW AVOID. The only condition that would change the call is a price that drops below the $7.00 consideration, restoring a positive merger-arb spread.
