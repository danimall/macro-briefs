**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Warner Bros. Discovery has ceased to be a fundamentals-driven equity. On February 27, 2026, WBD signed a definitive agreement to be acquired by Paramount Skydance (PSKY) for $31.00 per share in all cash, valuing the combined enterprise at roughly $110–111 billion. The stock closed at $26.20, leaving a gross spread of approximately 18% to the cash consideration. Everything that matters for the equity over the next two to four quarters runs through one variable: the probability and timing of deal completion. The underlying media operations, the leverage profile, and the secular decline of linear networks are now relevant chiefly as the assets and liabilities an acquirer is buying, not as drivers of a standalone valuation.

The deal has cleared two of its largest hurdles. WBD shareholders approved it on April 23, 2026, and the DOJ Antitrust Division cleared it with zero conditions on June 12, 2026. What remains is regulatory: several Democratic state attorneys general (California's Bonta, New York) preparing an antitrust suit to block; an FCC review tied to CBS local stations and foreign capital including the Saudi PIF; an EU foreign-subsidies probe with an initial deadline around July 14, 2026; and UK review. The ~18% spread is the market's pricing of these contingencies against a Q3 2026 targeted close (as early as July). A clean close returns roughly 18% plus ticking consideration over a few months; a break likely cuts the stock more than 40% toward standalone value.

The score trajectory has declined from 5.3 (February) to 3.7 (late May) on deteriorating standalone fundamentals: falling growth, governance flags, and structural linear decline. We are nudging the overall score up to 4.7 and moving the recommendation to HOLD (growth_hold), because the analytical frame has changed: at $26.20 against a $31.00 cash floor with DOJ already cleared, the risk/reward on the arbitrage has improved relative to the prior reads, even as the standalone business case remains weak. The recommendation reflects the dominant catalyst being a defined cash price with a defined timeline, not a conviction long on the media business.

## Company Overview

WBD is a global media and entertainment company assembled from the 2022 combination of Discovery with AT&T's WarnerMedia. It reports three segments:

- **Streaming** — HBO Max and discovery+, with 131.6 million global subscribers at year-end 2025 (+13% YoY). Domestic ARPU $10.79 (-9%); global ARPU $6.92 (-11%).
- **Studios** — Warner Bros. Motion Picture Group, TV production, DC Studios, Warner Bros. Games, and consumer products/themed experiences.
- **Global Linear Networks (GLN)** — domestic and international cable networks including CNN, TNT Sports, Discovery, Food Network, TLC, TBS, Adult Swim, TCM, and HBO linear.

The company holds one of the largest content libraries in media (HBO, DC, Harry Potter, the Game of Thrones franchise). In 2025 it reorganized into two operating divisions ("Streaming & Studios" and "Global Linear Networks") in preparation for a corporate separation that the PSKY merger is explicitly conditioned on WBD not completing.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability headline numbers are misleading.** FY2025 net income was +$727M against a -$11.3B loss in 2024 (which included a $9.1B non-cash GLN goodwill impairment). The FY2025 profit was flattered by a $2.96B gain on debt extinguishment from 2025 tender offers; operating income was only $738M. Adjusted EBITDA was $8.74B (-3%). Segment Adjusted EBITDA: GLN $6.41B (-21%, the shrinking cash generator), Studios $2.55B (+54%), Streaming $1.37B (up from $677M, the genuine operating improvement), and Corporate -$1.10B.

**Q1 2026 reflects the deal more than the business.** Revenue $8.89B (-1% reported); net loss -$2.92B (-$1.17/share) driven by a $2.8B Netflix break-fee expense (detailed below). Studios Adjusted EBITDA jumped to $775M (from $259M) on HBO Max international licensing; Streaming Adjusted EBITDA $438M (+29%); GLN $1.63B (-9%, hurt by the loss of NBA rights, -$133M advertising). Operating cash flow turned negative at -$208M (from +$553M); cash fell to $3.26B.

**The balance sheet is the standalone weak point.** Altman Z-Score of 1.25 sits in the distress zone. Total debt is roughly $32.6B; D/E ~1.75x. Goodwill ($25.9B) plus intangibles ($27.8B) equal ~54% of $100B in assets, and accumulated deficit was -$11.5B at year-end (-$14.4B by Q1 2026). A $15B secured bridge loan facility (JPMorgan, ~7.67% variable, secured by substantially all assets) matures June 30, 2027 and must be refinanced. S&P, Moody's, and Fitch all downgraded WBD in 2025 to junk/near-junk. A JPMorgan-led group was assembling a ~$10.2B leveraged loan in May 2026 to refinance ahead of the sale. These conditions would weigh heavily on a standalone WBD; they are largely moot if PSKY assumes the capital structure at close.

## Growth Analysis

The standalone growth picture is mixed and net-negative. The Streaming improvement is real: Adjusted EBITDA more than doubled to $1.37B in FY2025, and subscribers grew 13% to 131.6M. Studios delivered a strong 2025 slate (A Minecraft Movie, Superman, F1, Sinners, Weapons, Final Destination Bloodlines, Conjuring: Last Rites), lifting segment EBITDA 54%. But these gains are swamped by the GLN decline, where the largest profit pool ($6.4B EBITDA) is shrinking at -21% annually with no plausible reversal.

Estimate revisions are distorted by the deal mechanics and the Netflix fee. Current-year consensus swung dramatically negative (the $2.8B break-fee expense), while next-year estimates show large positive percentage swings off a near-zero base; both effects are arithmetic artifacts of one-time items rather than signals of underlying earnings momentum. The +30% revision threshold that would normally flag a fundamental shift does not apply cleanly here, because the swings are dominated by the merger accounting, not operating trends.

For investment purposes, growth is a secondary consideration. The acquirer is buying a streaming/studio franchise at an operating improvement while the linear segment was intended to be jettisoned. For WBD shareholders, the relevant "growth" is the ~18% move to the cash price, not the trajectory of segment EBITDA.

## Valuation Assessment

Conventional multiples are not informative here. The trailing and forward P/E print at -37x on negative EPS, and the dividend yield is zero (prohibited under bridge-loan covenants and the merger agreement). The peer group (FOX 16x, FOXA 17.9x, NXST 36.6x, TGNA 16.3x; median P/E 17.9x) is irrelevant to a company trading on a takeout spread.

The valuation that matters is the arbitrage. At $26.20, the stock trades at a ~18% gross spread to the $31.00 cash consideration, plus a ticking consideration of ~$0.0028/day if closing occurs after September 30, 2026 (capped at $0.25 per 90 days). With a Q3 2026 targeted close (as early as July) and DOJ already cleared, the implied annualized return on a clean close is well above 50% on a few-month horizon. The downside on a break is severe: standalone WBD traded near $10–12 in the pre-bid era, and while operating improvements since then may support a somewhat higher floor, a deal collapse would plausibly send the stock below $15, a >40% loss from current levels. The $7.0B reverse breakup fee (payable by PSKY if PSKY walks) cushions one specific failure mode but does not protect against a regulatory block, where WBD would owe a $3.0B termination fee plus reimbursements.

We assign a valuation score of 6.5. This reflects a specific, quantifiable reason the price diverges from a defined cash floor: an identified catalyst with a timeline, not a generic "cheap" multiple. A low headline multiple is anti-predictive as a standalone signal; the score here rests entirely on the arbitrage math, not on the appearance of cheapness.

## Competitive Landscape

The PSKY combination joins two legacy Hollywood studios (Paramount + Warner Bros.), two streamers (Paramount+ + HBO Max), and two news networks (CBS News + CNN). The DOJ's clearance rationale — that the relevant competitive set in a world of Netflix, Amazon Prime, Apple, and YouTube is far broader than legacy Hollywood — is the same logic that frames WBD's standalone competitive position: a strong content library and improving streaming economics, set against structural linear decline and intense scaled competition.

WBD's standalone competitive trajectory is mixed. Streaming is gaining ground (subscriber growth, EBITDA improvement, a credible content engine), and Studios is competitive on a hit-driven basis. But the company lost NBA rights, which materially pressured GLN advertising, and the linear segment is in secular retreat. The Netflix bidding war shows how contested these assets are: WBD extracted a topping all-cash bid from PSKY after agreeing to a Netflix deal, and got PSKY to pay Netflix's $2.8B break fee. We hold the competitive score at 5.0: improving in streaming/studios, deteriorating in linear, with the trajectory net-flat once the binary deal outcome is set aside.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is dominated by a single binary event.

1. **Deal-break risk (dominant).** A collapse would likely send the stock toward sub-$15 standalone value. WBD would owe PSKY a $3.0B termination fee plus reimbursements (up to $1.528B junior-lien payment + the $2.8B Netflix fee) in certain walk scenarios. The $7.0B reverse breakup fee provides downside cushion only if PSKY is the cause of failure.

2. **Regulatory gauntlet (the live variable).** DOJ cleared the deal with zero conditions, but: Democratic state AGs (California, New York) are preparing an antitrust suit to block; the FCC review covers CBS local stations and foreign capital (Saudi PIF); the EU foreign-subsidies probe has an initial deadline around July 14, 2026; and UK review is pending. The deal places CNN under Trump-allied ownership, a factor cited as smoothing federal approval while galvanizing state-level and labor opposition.

3. **Leverage and refinancing.** $32.6B debt, distress-zone Z-score, a $15B secured bridge due 2027 — alarming for a standalone WBD, largely moot if the deal closes.

4. **Secular linear decline.** Structural, permanently visible, and not a novel risk.

5. **Goodwill impairment risk.** $25.9B remaining after 2024's $9.1B write-down.

6. **Nokia patent litigation** (streaming-video SEPs) — interim payment hearing May 2026, trial late 2026, exposure not yet estimable.

We score risk factors 4.5: the binary deal outcome is the defining idiosyncratic risk, partially mitigated by DOJ clearance and the reverse breakup fee but unresolved on the state-AG, FCC, and EU fronts.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($31.00+):** The deal closes in Q3 2026 as targeted. State-AG suits fail to enjoin, the FCC clears the CBS station transfers, and the EU foreign-subsidies review concludes without blocking. Shareholders receive $31.00 cash plus ticking consideration if close slips past September 30, 2026. Upside from $26.20 is ~18% plus carry, realized over a few months.

**Bear case (<$15):** A state-AG injunction, an adverse FCC ruling, or an EU block kills or indefinitely delays the deal. The stock reverts toward standalone value, which carries $32.6B of debt, a distress-zone Z-score, a $15B bridge maturing 2027, structurally declining linear revenue, and negative Q1 operating cash flow. WBD would also owe the $3.0B termination fee plus reimbursements. Downside exceeds 40%.

The payoff magnitude is roughly symmetric (~18% up vs. ~40%+ down) but skewed in probability toward completion given DOJ clearance and shareholder approval. The market's ~18% spread implies a meaningful but minority probability of failure or material delay. That is a reasonable, if uncomfortable, balance.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD classified as a growth_hold: the holding case rests on a specific 12-month (more likely 3–6 month) catalyst — deal close at $31.00 — rather than on income or standalone appreciation.

- **Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (a) EU foreign-subsidies probe clears or is resolved without conditions by/around July 14, 2026; (b) FCC grants the CBS station-transfer approvals; (c) state-AG suits are denied injunctive relief or settled. Any one of these materially de-risks the spread and would justify pressing the arbitrage.
- **Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (a) a state-AG injunction is granted or a serious antitrust suit is filed and survives early motions; (b) the FCC signals it will block on foreign-ownership grounds; (c) the EU opens an in-depth (Phase II) probe that pushes resolution past the outside date; (d) either party signals intent to walk.
- **Reassessment timeline:** The EU initial deadline (~July 14, 2026) is the next hard checkpoint, with the Q3 2026 targeted close as the primary resolution window. Reassess at each regulatory milestone.

For investors already holding, the position is a defined-catalyst arbitrage with an asymmetric but probability-favored payoff. For investors not holding, the entry is acceptable but not compelling: the spread is fair compensation for the residual regulatory risk, not a mispricing.

## Conclusion

WBD is a merger-arbitrage position trading at $26.20 against a $31.00 all-cash offer, with DOJ cleared, shareholders approved, and a Q3 2026 targeted close. The standalone business — declining linear revenue, distress-zone leverage, a $15B bridge maturing in 2027, negative recent operating cash flow — would warrant an AVOID on its own merits, consistent with the recent score trajectory. But the dominant driver is now a defined cash price with a defined timeline, and the binary outcome is probability-skewed toward completion. We move to HOLD with an overall score of 4.7. The improvement over the prior 3.7 reflects the changed risk/reward of the arbitrage at the current spread, not any improvement in the underlying media operations. The recommendation would upgrade to BUY on clearance of the EU/FCC/state-AG hurdles and downgrade to AVOID on any credible move to block.
