**Research Date: July 21, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Elbit Systems is a fully integrated Israeli defense prime with genuine leadership in electro-optics, UAVs, electronic warfare, high-power lasers, land munitions, and night vision. FY2025 revenue reached $7.94B (up ~16% YoY), net income to shareholders rose 66% to $534.3M, and the order book hit a record $30.2B in Q1 2026 — roughly 3.8x annual revenue, with 71% booked internationally. The business is capturing two simultaneous demand cycles: European rearmament (a $1.4B modernization award and a ~$750M Greek PULS deal in April-May 2026) and Middle East conflict-driven Israeli Ministry of Defense demand (Israel revenue +34% YoY in Q1 2026 to $818M, now 37% of sales). Non-GAAP operating margin crossed 10% for the first time in Q1, and the balance sheet earned an ilAAA upgrade from S&P Maalot in May 2026.

The fundamentals are close to best-in-class among global defense names, and the growth is broad-based rather than a single-program story. The difficulty is entirely valuation. At $780.86 the stock trades near 61x trailing GAAP EPS ($11.39) and ~50x current-year consensus ($15.59), after more than doubling over the past year and appreciating ~457% over five years. Estimate revisions are stable-to-modestly-positive (+3.4% current-year over 90 days, flat over 30 days), a healthy backdrop but not the +30% revision velocity that would justify paying a premium multiple with confidence. Consensus sits at a Hold with a mean target of $846 — roughly 8% above current price — capturing the "excellent company, stretched multiple" tension that defines the debate.

Our recommendation is HOLD with a growth-hold rationale. The order book de-risks mid-teens growth for two-plus years and the margin inflection is real, so we would not short or sell quality here. But the multiple already discounts sustained execution, and the two demand drivers — Middle East war intensity and European budget follow-through — are both cyclical and volatile. The risk/reward from $780 is roughly balanced. We would upgrade on a pullback toward the low-$600s or on a genuine acceleration in estimate-revision velocity, and we would turn cautious on backlog stagnation, a margin reversal from working-capital strain, or a durable Middle East de-escalation that removes the Israeli demand premium.

## Company Overview

Elbit is headquartered in Haifa and employs more than 20,000 people across dozens of countries. It reports in five segments: Land (~$2.32B FY2025, the fastest grower at +27% YoY in Q1 2026, covering munitions, PULS artillery rockets, and armored vehicles); Aerospace (~$2.07B, UAVs and avionics); Elbit Systems of America / ESA (~$1.69B, U.S.-domiciled night vision, medical devices, and DoD programs); ISTAR & EW (~$1.53B, intelligence/surveillance, electronic warfare, and lasers, +17% YoY); and C4I & Cyber (~$0.93B, radios, command and control, cyber, +17% YoY).

The company's differentiation rests on deep integration into Israel's defense-technology ecosystem, battle-proven products (a real advantage in procurement decisions), and leadership in the specific niches the modern-warfare theme rewards: loitering munitions, EW, directed energy, and electro-optics. At a $36.5B market cap it is a mid-tier global prime, smaller than Lockheed or RTX but a category leader in electro-optics and land systems. It is a constituent of defense-tech ETFs (SHLD) that have outperformed classic aerospace baskets, reflecting investor preference for the tech-forward defense narrative.

A data note carried from the research: the SEC XBRL feed mislabeled fiscal years and reported $6.83B revenue for both FY2024 and FY2025. The audited income statement in the Q1 2026 release confirms FY2025 revenue of $7.94B (the $6.83B figure was FY2024). All figures here use the corrected $7.94B FY2025 base.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has compounded steadily and is now accelerating: $5.28B (FY2021) → $5.51B → $5.97B → $6.83B (FY2024) → $7.94B (FY2025), roughly 16% growth in the most recent year. Q1 2026 revenue of $2.19B was up 15.5% YoY, consistent with management's mid-teens growth guidance.

Profitability is inflecting positively. FY2025 GAAP gross margin was 24.4%, operating income $671M (8.5% margin), and net income to shareholders $534.3M — up 66% from FY2024's $321M. FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS was $11.39 (Non-GAAP $12.75). In Q1 2026, GAAP operating margin rose to 9.4% and Non-GAAP crossed 10% (10.1%) for the first time, with net income of $160.8M (+50% YoY) and Non-GAAP diluted EPS of $3.87. R&D is being pushed up deliberately — $517M (6.5% of sales) in FY2025, rising to 6.9% of revenue in Q1 2026 — which tempers near-term margin expansion but funds the product pipeline in the niches driving demand.

Cash generation is healthy. FY2025 operating cash flow was $778M, and Q1 2026 OCF was $281M (+53% YoY) against capex of roughly 3% of sales, so free cash flow conversion is strong. The balance sheet carries modest debt (Series B/C/D notes ~$323M plus small bank loans) against $4.26B of equity, low net leverage that earned the ilAAA upgrade in May 2026.

The one area demanding attention is working capital. This is a working-capital-heavy model: inventories of $3.24B and trade/unbilled receivables of $3.62B at Q1 2026. Rapid growth consumes cash: Q1 saw a $326M receivables build and a $112M inventory build, partly offset by a $464M jump in contract liabilities (customer advances). Reported cash fell from $635M to $155M in the quarter, but that reflects a shift into $770M of short-term bank deposits rather than burn. Scaling production to convert a $30.2B backlog into revenue will keep absorbing working capital, and the cash-conversion cadence is the operational metric to monitor.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in forward estimates: four consecutive beats, ranging from +15.7% to +23.7%, with the trailing four quarters averaging roughly +19%. Consensus has been consistently conservative, which argues that the modest published estimate revisions understate likely delivery.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is anchored on backlog. At $30.2B (first time above $30B), ~3.8x annual revenue, with 71% international and ~49% scheduled for delivery through the remainder of 2026 and 2027, revenue visibility is exceptional and the mid-teens growth guidance is well-supported. Order intake in 2026 has been strong: the ~$1.4B European modernization award (May 26), the ~$750M Greek PULS deal (April 6), a ~$212M U.S. Army ENVG-B night-vision order (May 12), ~$200M of IMOD airborne munitions (April 22), and more than $370M in U.S. Customs and Border Protection contracts (July).

The demand is structural on the European side. NATO commitments and Ukraine-war fallout have lifted European defense spending, and Elbit is capturing it directly — Europe was 27% of FY2025 revenue ($2.14B) and the source of the two largest recent wins. The binding constraint across the sector is production and delivery capacity, which is precisely what Elbit's R&D and automation investment targets. On the Israeli side, IMOD demand is elevated by active conflict, a powerful near-term tailwind but cyclical in nature.

Estimate revisions are the checkpoint on how much of this growth is being upgraded into consensus. They are stable-to-modestly-positive: current-quarter EPS +3.6% over 90 days, current-year +3.4%, next-year +4.2%, but flat over the trailing 30 days. This is a solid, durable backdrop, not the >30% revision velocity that flags a mispriced trough-earnings inflection. The distinction matters for scoring: Elbit is a high-quality grower with visible demand, not a stock the market is systematically under-modeling. The growth is real and durable, which supports a strong growth score, but it is largely recognized.

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is the crux. The stock trades at roughly 61x trailing GAAP EPS and ~61x trailing Non-GAAP ($12.75). On forward consensus it is ~50x current-year and ~43x next-year ($18.06). Against the peer set, the P/E screens near or below several comparables — AXON at 179x, ATI at 66x, CW at 56x, FTAI at 48x — but those are heterogeneous business models, and the most directly comparable large defense prime, L3Harris, trades at 33.9x with a 1.7% yield. Elbit's premium to LHX reflects its faster growth and cleaner backlog-to-revenue ratio, but 50x forward on a defense prime is a full price that requires flawless mid-teens execution to hold.

The cycle-adjustment guidance argues for evaluating inflection stocks on forward and normalized earnings rather than trailing multiples, and Elbit's four straight beats plus margin inflection warrant that treatment. On next-year consensus of $18.06, the ~43x multiple against ~15-16% growth implies a PEG near 2.7 — not a cheap growth-adjusted setup. This is not a trough-earnings story where trailing P/E is meaningless; earnings are near a cyclical high, boosted by elevated Israeli conflict demand.

The analyst-target-as-floor principle does not apply here because the stock is above the 30x forward and 1.5 PEG thresholds. At these levels, consensus targets and price both embed the same growth extrapolation, so the mean target of $846 (~8% upside) and median $862.5 (~10%) are not independent confirmation of undervaluation — they reflect the same optimism as the price. The consensus posture (one Buy, five Holds, no Sells currently, with a recent sell downgrade reversed) is a Hold with modeled upside, the classic "great company, stretched multiple" split.

We score valuation 4.5. The multiple is defensible for the quality but offers no asymmetric upside from $780, and the historical pattern that "cheap" screens underperform is not relevant here because Elbit is not cheap on any forward metric. The stock is fairly-to-fully valued, with the burden of proof on continued execution rather than on multiple expansion.

## Competitive Landscape

Elbit competes with U.S. primes (Lockheed, RTX, GD), European primes (Rheinmetall, Leonardo, BAE, Thales), and domestic rival IAI. Its competitive trajectory is improving: it is gaining share in exactly the high-growth niches the modern-warfare theme rewards, and the 71% international mix of the backlog shows it is converting the European rearmament wave into bookings rather than watching share accrue to European nationals. Segment growth is broad — Land +27%, ISTAR & EW +17%, C4I & Cyber +17% in Q1 2026 — which indicates the position is widening across the portfolio, not concentrated in one line.

The battle-proven credibility is a durable procurement advantage that European primes ramping from a standing start cannot replicate quickly. The counterweight is the Israel-sourcing overhang: some countries restrict engagement with Israeli firms, and anti-Israel activism has physically targeted facilities. This caps the addressable market at the margin and is a structural feature unique to this issuer, but it has not prevented the company from winning large European and U.S. contracts. On balance the competitive position is strong and improving, supporting a 7.5.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is the dual-edged nature of geopolitical exposure. The company's own disclosure describes an extraordinary sequence since October 2023: multiple ceasefires, a June 2025 Iran ceasefire, a joint U.S.-Israel strike on Iran in February 2026, Iranian retaliation across the Gulf, renewed Hezbollah attacks in March 2026, and fragile, violated ceasefires from April 2026. War has massively increased IMOD demand, but it also drives supply-chain disruption, higher transport and component costs (Red Sea/Houthi), reserve-duty labor call-ups, and physical attacks on facilities. The Israeli demand component is both the near-term earnings accelerant and the largest reversal risk: a durable de-escalation would remove a meaningful slice of the current earnings power that the multiple is capitalizing.

Other material risks: fixed-price long-term contract exposure, where cost inflation or execution slippage compresses margins on a large backlog; working-capital and execution risk on rapid scaling (the receivable and inventory builds are real and cash-conversion is the tell); customer/budget concentration across IMOD, the U.S. DoD via ESA, and European MoDs; and FX exposure (an $8.6M FX loss in Q1 2026 against significant NIS and euro costs). The ownership/control structure (historically associated with Federmann Enterprises) could not be verified in the source material and should be confirmed against the 20-F proxy for any related-party dynamics — we flag it as an open item rather than a scored negative.

We score risk 5.0. The idiosyncratic risks here are not sector truisms (defense cyclicality is priced) but genuine binary and concentration exposures — the Israeli conflict cycle, boycott/export-license overhang, and the fixed-price margin risk on a rapidly scaling book — that are only partially reflected in a valuation that assumes smooth execution.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($1,000-1,075).** The $30.2B backlog converts smoothly at mid-teens growth, Non-GAAP margins hold above 10% and expand as operating leverage overcomes rising R&D, and European rearmament order intake continues at the 2026 cadence. Four straight double-digit beats prove consensus is too conservative, next-year EPS pushes toward or above $18, and the multiple holds at ~55-60x on the demonstrated growth durability. The stock retests its March high near $1,016 and the top analyst target of $1,075. This case requires both demand cycles staying intact and clean execution on the production scale-up.

**Bear Case ($520-620).** A durable Middle East de-escalation removes the elevated IMOD demand, working-capital strain and fixed-price cost inflation compress the margin inflection, and European budget follow-through disappoints relative to the order-intake spike. Estimate revisions roll over, and the market applies a defense-prime multiple closer to L3Harris's 33.9x on a lower earnings base, taking the stock toward the low analyst target of $518 and the mid-$600s. A >30% drawdown from current levels is plausible if both the demand premium unwinds and the multiple derates simultaneously — the two are correlated, which is what makes the downside asymmetric relative to the ~8% consensus upside.

The balanced view: this is a genuinely excellent business at a price that requires continued excellence. The quality and backlog anchor the downside above a distressed scenario, but the entry point offers little margin of safety.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a growth-hold with a 12-month horizon tied to the quarterly backlog and margin cadence.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback toward the low-$600s (roughly 38x current-year EPS / ~33x next-year), where the growth-adjusted multiple compresses toward peer-defensible levels and offers asymmetric upside.
- Estimate-revision velocity accelerating meaningfully (current-year revisions moving above +8-10% over a 90-day window) alongside a fifth consecutive double-digit beat, indicating consensus is materially behind the earnings power.
- Backlog growth to the mid-$30B range with sustained international mix, extending revenue visibility into 2028 and de-risking the growth premium further.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Backlog stagnation or decline over two consecutive quarters, or a slowdown in large-contract intake signaling the European order spike was a one-time bulge.
- Non-GAAP operating margin reversing back below 9% on working-capital strain or fixed-price cost overruns, breaking the margin-inflection thesis.
- A durable, verified Middle East de-escalation that removes the elevated Israeli demand without offsetting European growth — the earnings base and the multiple would both compress.
- Cash-conversion deterioration where receivable/inventory builds persistently outrun contract-liability advances, signaling the scale-up is straining the balance sheet.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly release (Q2 2026, expected late August), with the backlog figure, Non-GAAP operating margin, and cash-conversion cadence as the three decisive data points.

## Conclusion

Elbit Systems is one of the highest-quality expressions of the global rearmament theme: broad-based mid-teens growth, a record $30.2B backlog that is 71% international, a margin inflection crossing 10% Non-GAAP, strong free cash flow, an ilAAA balance sheet, and four consecutive double-digit earnings beats. The competitive position is improving across every segment, and the demand is structural on the European side. Those attributes justify a premium multiple and rule out any bearish stance.

The obstacle is that the price already reflects most of this. At ~50x current-year and ~43x next-year EPS with a PEG near 2.7, after more than doubling in a year, the stock offers roughly 8-10% to consensus targets that are not independent of the same growth optimism embedded in the price. Estimate revisions are stable and modest rather than the sharp inflection that would signal the market is behind reality. The two demand engines — Israeli conflict intensity and European budget follow-through — are cyclical and volatile, creating asymmetric downside if either reverses while the multiple is at a full stretch.

We rate ESLT a HOLD (growth-hold). We would own the quality through the backlog conversion cycle but would not initiate at $780; the attractive entry is a pullback toward the low-$600s or a demonstrable acceleration in estimate revisions. Overall score 6.35.
