**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

American International Group has completed a multi-year transformation from a sprawling, bailout-era multi-line conglomerate into a focused global property & casualty insurer. The defining structural event was the June 2024 deconsolidation of Corebridge Financial, the former life & retirement business, and the steady monetization of AIG's residual stake has funded an aggressive share-repurchase program. The end-state is a cleaner pure-play P&C underwriter that, on the bull thesis, should command a higher multiple than the ~9.3x forward earnings it currently trades at.

The fundamentals support a quality-value characterization rather than a deep-value rescue. AIG has beaten consensus EPS in four consecutive quarters (most recently +10.1% in Q1 2026), current-year consensus sits at ~$8.03 with next-year at ~$8.90 (~11% implied growth), and the ~22% dividend payout ratio leaves abundant room for buybacks against a ~$39-40B market cap. Operating leverage from a multi-year hard commercial market has driven the underwriting turnaround. This is a well-run, well-capitalized franchise.

The reason I am stepping back from the prior BUY streak (three consecutive calls at a 6.7 composite while the stock fell ~11%) is specific and falsifiable. The earnings-momentum signal that would justify pressing the name has rolled over: current-quarter estimates are down 1.5% over 90 days and flat-to-negative over 30 days, even as full-year estimates inched up only modestly. The re-rating thesis depends on multiple expansion that the market has declined to grant across the prior three calls, and the live debate — whether commercial P&C pricing is decelerating, which would compress the margin tailwind AIG has been riding — has no near-term catalyst to resolve favorably. I rate AIG a HOLD for the ~2.7% dividend plus buyback-driven per-share growth, with a clear upgrade path if the hard market proves durable.

## Company Overview

AIG provides liability, financial lines, property, and specialty insurance to commercial, institutional, and individual customers across approximately 200 countries and jurisdictions. Following the September 2022 IPO of Corebridge and the June 9, 2024 deconsolidation, AIG is structurally a focused global P&C/commercial-specialty insurer. Its principal competitors are Chubb, Travelers, The Hartford, AXA, and Zurich.

Competition turns on risk-acceptance criteria, pricing, and policy terms. The company's Insurer Financial Strength (IFS) ratings are a critical competitive asset; a downgrade would directly impair AIG's ability to write and retain business and would raise financing costs. The current structure removes the conglomerate complexity that historically weighed on AIG's valuation; the remaining transitional item is the residual Corebridge stake, which AIG marks to fair value with movements flowing through net investment income.

A note on screening: standardized distress metrics such as the Altman Z-Score are not diagnostic for insurers, whose balance sheets are dominated by reserves, float, and large investment portfolios. The relevant solvency framework is RBC ratios, statutory capital, and IFS ratings, all of which are sound and underpin the capital-return program. Any quantitative screen flagging AIG as "distressed" is a false negative.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings power and quality.** Current-year consensus EPS is ~$8.03, up from $7.87 ninety days ago, with next-year at ~$8.90. Trailing EPS of $5.43 reflects the transitional drag of the Corebridge transition and non-operating marks; the forward number is the relevant measure of normalized earnings power. The earnings beat record is consistent and material: Q1 2026 +10.1% ($2.11 vs $1.92), Q4 2025 +2.1%, Q3 2025 +27.3%, Q2 2025 +12.1%. Four-for-four beats raise confidence in the forward consensus, though the magnitude has compressed from the +27% Q3 print toward the low-double-digit and single-digit range.

**Earnings-quality wildcard.** Because the residual Corebridge stake is carried at fair value through net investment income, reported quarterly results can swing on equity-market moves unrelated to underwriting. The analytical discipline here is to separate underwriting earnings (combined ratio) from investment-income volatility when reading any given quarter. This issue self-liquidates as AIG completes the sell-down, but it injects noise into near-term reported figures.

**Capital structure and return.** The ~22% payout ratio on a dividend yielding ~2.7% leaves substantial room for buybacks, which have been the centerpiece of capital allocation, funded by Corebridge monetization proceeds. With 530M shares outstanding and a buyback-heavy posture, share-count reduction is a meaningful and durable per-share earnings tailwind independent of underwriting growth. AIG Parent is a holding company dependent on regulated-subsidiary dividends, so liquidity adequacy hinges on subsidiary RBC levels and regulatory dividend capacity, a standard but real constraint.

**Financial health assessment.** Strong statutory capital, sound RBC, durable cash generation, and disciplined capital return support a 7.0. The structural dependence on subsidiary dividend capacity and the outsourcing of the majority of the investment portfolio to third-party managers (which the 10-K candidly acknowledges erodes internal expertise) keep this from scoring higher.

## Growth Analysis

AIG's growth is a function of two engines: underwriting margin expansion driven by the commercial pricing cycle, and per-share accretion from buybacks. The first is decelerating; the second is durable.

A meaningful portion of forward EPS growth is buyback-driven share-count reduction rather than organic premium and margin growth. Near-term estimate revisions have rolled over: current-quarter consensus fell 1.5% over 90 days and 0.2% over 30 days, and next-quarter estimates are flat. This is the opposite of the accelerating-revision profile that signals an earnings inflection. The strong +30%+ revision velocity that would force a high growth score is absent.

The central growth swing variable is commercial P&C pricing direction. AIG has ridden a multi-year hard market in commercial and specialty lines. The live industry debate — and the most likely explanation for the stock's de-rating — is whether pricing is softening, particularly in financial lines and parts of property. Decelerating rate increases would compress the margin tailwind that drove the turnaround. The 10-K's explicit pricing-cycle risk factor confirms management views this as a real cyclical exposure.

Growth prospects score 5.5: positive but decelerating, with the near-term revision signal pointing slightly down and the durable component (buybacks) being mechanical rather than franchise expansion.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$74.77, AIG trades at ~9.3x current-year consensus and ~8.4x next-year. Against the multi-line insurance peer median P/E of 13.3, AIG screens cheap. Peer comparables: Loews 13.6x, Assurant 13.3x, American Financial Group 12.6x, with Lincoln National (4.3x) and Genworth (17.0x) as outliers reflecting idiosyncratic situations.

The bull argument is straightforward: a cleaner, simpler pure-play P&C insurer with sound capital, a consistent beat record, and a heavy buyback should not trade at a ~30% discount to large-cap multi-line peers. If the multiple re-rates toward the peer median on stable execution, the upside is material.

The reason I do not award a high valuation score, despite the apparent cheapness, is that this discount has persisted across three prior BUY calls without resolving, and the catalyst to close it is conditional on the very thing in doubt — durable hard-market pricing. A compressed multiple that the market refuses to expand, in the presence of a real cyclical headwind (pricing deceleration) and AIG's documented history of adverse reserve development, is a value setup that requires an independent catalyst to reward. The near-term estimate revisions are not providing that catalyst; they are flat-to-down. Cheap, by itself, is not a buy signal when the discount reflects a debate the market is actively pricing.

Valuation scores 6.0: genuinely below peers with real re-rating optionality, but no near-term catalyst to force the convergence, and an unresolved pricing-cycle question that justifies part of the discount.

## Competitive Landscape

AIG's competitive position has improved through the transformation. The simplified pure-play structure, disciplined underwriting, and expense reduction have strengthened the franchise relative to its bailout-era complexity. IFS ratings remain a competitive asset, and the company competes credibly against Chubb, Travelers, and The Hartford in commercial and specialty lines.

The trajectory is the relevant signal. Competitive position improved from 6.5 to 7.0 in prior reviews, and the structural simplification supports a constructive read. The moat is bounded by the cyclical nature of P&C pricing: AIG's recent margin gains are partly a function of an industry-wide hard market rather than idiosyncratic share gains, and a softening cycle would compress that advantage across the peer set. A structural industry shift toward private-equity-backed alternative reinsurance capital is double-edged: it pressures the reliability of AIG's reinsurance protection while also presenting a strategic opportunity the company is leaning into via its investment-management partnerships.

Competitive position scores 6.5: improving and structurally cleaner, but with the gains partly cycle-dependent rather than fully company-specific.

## Risk Assessment

**Reserve adequacy on long-tail liability lines** is the core fundamental risk. AIG's casualty and financial-lines book is heavily exposed to social inflation, litigation and tort trends, and adverse judicial actions. The 10-K explicitly warns that loss-development factors may deviate and "might not be identified for an extended period of time after we record the initial loss reserve estimates." Adverse reserve development has historically been an AIG pain point and remains the single most important item to monitor. This is a specific, asymmetric downside risk that is not fully priced into a ~9x multiple.

**Catastrophe exposure and reinsurance dependence.** As a global P&C insurer, AIG faces hurricanes, wildfires, floods, and earthquakes, with climate change raising frequency and severity. It is a major buyer of third-party reinsurance and is exposed to reinsurance becoming unavailable or too expensive after catastrophe-heavy years, plus reinsurer counterparty credit risk.

**Pricing-cycle risk.** The current firm pricing could soften, pressuring rates — the live bull/bear pivot and the most plausible explanation for the stock's relative weakness.

**Transitional Corebridge exposure.** A decline in Corebridge's market value reduces AIG's investment income; the timing and pricing of further sell-downs are uncertain. This self-liquidates as the stake is monetized.

**Outsourced investment management** concentrates dependence on third-party managers and erodes internal expertise, per the 10-K.

Risk factors score 5.5: the reserve-development asymmetry and the cyclical pricing risk are specific and not fully priced, weighing against the otherwise-sound solvency profile.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** AIG is a cleanly transformed pure-play P&C insurer trading at ~9x forward earnings against a ~13.3x peer median. Underwriting discipline holds, the hard market proves durable, reserve development stays benign, and the buyback drives per-share accretion. The multiple re-rates toward peers as the Corebridge transition completes and earnings quality cleans up. On ~$8.90 next-year EPS at an 11.3x multiple, the stock reaches roughly $101 (consensus high target), ~35% above current.

**Bear case.** Commercial P&C pricing softens, compressing the margin tailwind. AIG suffers an adverse reserve-development charge on its long-tail casualty book, a recurring historical vulnerability. The Corebridge mark swings against reported investment income. Estimates, already flat-to-down on the near term, roll over further. The multiple stays compressed or de-rates. On ~$7.50 stressed EPS at ~9x, the stock falls toward $67-68, ~9-10% below current.

The base case sits between: stable-to-modestly-growing earnings, continued buyback accretion, and a partial multiple recovery toward the low-to-mid $80s (consensus mean ~$87.80, median ~$86), supported by the dividend. The asymmetry is mildly favorable, but the catalyst to force the re-rating is absent, and the prior three BUY calls demonstrate that cheap-plus-buyback has not been sufficient to move the stock.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The thesis is carry plus optionality — collect the dividend and benefit from buyback-driven per-share growth while waiting for evidence the hard market is holding. This is not a catalyst-driven appreciation call; the stock has compounded sideways-to-down for twelve months despite improving fundamentals, and the near-term earnings-momentum signal has turned slightly negative.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Evidence that commercial P&C pricing is stabilizing or re-accelerating — confirmed in management's renewal-rate commentary on the next two earnings calls, or in current-quarter/next-quarter estimate revisions turning positive (+3% or more over 30 days).
2. A clean quarter that isolates underwriting strength (improving combined ratio) from Corebridge marks, demonstrating organic margin durability rather than cycle dependence.
3. Continued buyback execution materially reducing share count (>4% annualized) alongside stable or rising full-year consensus.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A material adverse reserve-development charge on long-tail casualty lines — the core historical vulnerability.
2. Full-year consensus EPS revised down more than 5% over a 90-day window, signaling the pricing cycle has turned.
3. An IFS rating downgrade or evidence of constrained subsidiary dividend capacity impairing the buyback.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoint is Q2 2026 earnings (late July/early August), with attention to commercial renewal-rate change, combined ratio ex-catastrophe, and any reserve-development commentary.

## Conclusion

AIG is a well-run, well-capitalized, structurally simplified global P&C insurer trading at a justified-but-recoverable discount to peers, with a durable buyback and a sound dividend. The fundamentals have improved through the transformation, and the four-quarter beat record supports confidence in forward earnings. The case for continuing to own it rests on per-share accretion and re-rating optionality, both of which are real.

I am moving off the prior BUY streak to HOLD because the conditions that would justify pressing the name are not present: near-term estimate revisions have rolled over, the live cyclical risk (commercial pricing deceleration) has no near-term resolution, the reserve-development asymmetry is specific and not fully priced, and the re-rating thesis has gone unrewarded across three prior calls with the stock down ~11% over the period. Cheap-plus-buyback has been necessary but not sufficient. Hold for the income and the optionality; upgrade when the hard market visibly holds or organic underwriting margins prove they can carry the story without the cycle.
