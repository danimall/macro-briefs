**Research Date: June 2, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Range Resources is a pure-play Appalachian (Marcellus) natural gas, NGL, and condensate producer that has converted a multi-decade, low-cost inventory into a deleveraging and capital-return story. FY2025 revenue rose 27% to $2.99B and net income jumped 147% to $658M ($2.74 diluted EPS), and Q1 2026 accelerated further with net income of $341.6M ($1.44 EPS, +252% YoY). The company retired its highest-coupon debt (the $600M 8.25% 2029 notes in January 2026, following $606.5M of 4.875% notes in May 2025), cutting total debt to roughly $834M, raised the dividend 11%, and lifted its buyback authorization to $1.5B. The improved balance sheet harvests a 50+ year reserve life rather than outspending cash flow to chase volume growth.

The complication is that the earnings inflection rides on commodity prices that are cyclical, not structural. NYMEX gas averaged $4.97 in Q1'26 versus $3.66 a year earlier, and Appalachian basis swung from –$0.05 to +$0.21, a meaningful netback tailwind that drove most of the 252% earnings jump. Consensus current-year EPS has been revised up 23.8% over 90 days to $4.31, which on its face is a strong forward signal. But that estimate embeds elevated gas prices, and Range's own hedge book (swaps near $4.06, three-way collars, and sold call swaptions) caps participation in exactly the rising-price environment investors are excited about. On normalized gas, the equity is fairly valued at roughly 8.6x next-year EPS, modestly below its closest comparable (Antero at 11.6x) rather than asymmetrically cheap.

We rate RRC a HOLD with a growth lean. The deleveraging, capital-return, and inventory-quality attributes are real and improving. What keeps this from a BUY is the inseparability of the earnings beat from a transient commodity premium, insider selling clustered at $42–$48 in spring 2026 (above today's price) with zero open-market buying, an escalating statewide setback-regulation risk in Pennsylvania where ~100% of reserves sit, and a ~$5.9B firm-transportation take-or-pay obligation that amplifies downside if prices or volumes fall.

## Company Overview

Range Resources operates as a single segment, with essentially all of its 18.1 Tcfe of proved reserves (65% gas, 34% NGLs, ~1% oil; 71% proved developed) located in southwest Pennsylvania. The acreage position spans roughly 879,000 gross / 769,000 net acres at ~95% average working interest and ~100% operated, with approximately 27 million feet of remaining lateral inventory. Management cites a stated 50+ year reserve life and a low base decline rate. The company is lean, with about 564 employees for a ~2.24 Bcfe/d producer.

The structural differentiator versus dry-gas peers like EQT is the 34% NGL weighting. Range markets ethane, propane, butane, and condensate, giving it a higher-value, more diversified revenue mix and exposure to Northeast and export NGL pricing. The constraint is basin egress: moving product to premium markets requires firm transportation, which generates the company's single largest cost line (~$1.50/mcfe, ~$1.2B/yr) and its largest non-commodity liability (~$5.9B in contractual firm commitments through 2037).

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025 results.** Operating cash flow reached $1.17B against $673.8M of capex, leaving the company solidly free-cash-flow positive. Net margin was ~22%, ROA 8.9%, and ROE 15.2%. Realized natural gas rose 60% to $3.08/mcf, while NGLs (–6% to $24.15/bbl) and oil (–17% to $53.68) softened.

**Q1 2026 momentum.** Gas/NGL/oil sales rose 28% to $1.01B, operating cash flow was $619M, and production held roughly flat at ~2.21 Bcfe/d. The Q1 net income gain over the prior-year $97M ($0.40 EPS) was driven by price, not volume.

**Earnings quality and beats.** The company has beaten consensus in each of the last four reported quarters, most recently by 19.1% (Q1'26: $1.52 actual vs $1.28 estimate; note the discrepancy with the 10-Q's $1.44 diluted GAAP figure, likely an adjusted-vs-GAAP difference). A consistent beat record supports confidence in forward estimates, with the caveat that the beats coincide with a rising-gas tape.

**Balance sheet.** This is the strongest part of the story. Total debt fell from ~$1.2B at YE2025 after the January 2026 redemption of the 8.25% 2029 notes (at 101.375%, booking a $12.3M extinguishment loss). Remaining debt is $500M of 4.75% notes due 2030 plus ~$334M drawn on the bank facility. Equity stands at $4.32B (D/E 0.72). The revolver was amended in October 2025 to a $4.0B maximum / $3.0B borrowing base / $2.0B commitments across 17 banks, maturing October 2030, with the base reaffirmed in March 2026. The current ratio of 0.67 is sub-1 but typical for E&Ps, and the Altman Z of ~2.02 reflects capital intensity rather than distress.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend was raised 11% to $0.10/quarter in 2026. Buybacks accelerated to $230.6M (6.4M shares) in 2025 from $65.3M in 2024, with the authorization lifted to $1.5B (fully available) in February 2026. Diluted share count is declining (~236M in Q1'26 vs ~242M in 2024).

**Reserve value torque.** Standardized-measure PV-10 more than doubled to $11.6B pretax / $9.6B after-tax at YE2025 from $5.5B/$4.7B, on the SEC 12-month gas price rising to $3.39 from $2.13. This illustrates the equity's leverage to gas, substantial in both directions.

## Growth Analysis

Range is explicit that this is not a growth story. The 2026 plan calls for modest production growth on a capex budget of just $650–700M, with less drilling and more completions (drawing down DUC inventory). The 50+ year reserve life means the company can sustain output without outspending cash flow or making acquisitions. Growth in earnings, therefore, comes overwhelmingly from price, not volume.

Estimate revisions are positive across the board: current quarter $0.74 (+9.7%/90d), next quarter $0.89 (+16.5%/90d), current year $4.31 (+23.8%/90d), next year $4.66 (+13.7%/90d). The qualification is that these revisions track the gas-price move, not a durable change in the production or cost base. Rather than unit economics structurally improving, this is consensus catching up to a higher commodity strip that may or may not hold.

The structural bull case — rising U.S. gas demand from LNG export build-out and power load (including data centers) lifting Henry Hub and tightening Appalachian basis — has directional early validation in the Q1'26 numbers. If that demand thesis proves durable, Range's lowest-cost, longest-life inventory and NGL optionality position it well. But the hedge book caps participation in precisely that upside scenario, blunting the equity's leverage to the most bullish gas outcome.

## Valuation Assessment

At $39.91 (recently $39.78), RRC trades at ~10.5x trailing EPS and roughly 8.6x next-year consensus EPS of $4.66. Against the peer set — Antero 11.6x, Matador 13.8x, SM Energy 31.9x, with a peer-median P/E of 13.8x — Range looks optically cheaper. The relevant comparable is Antero (the closest liquids-rich Marcellus operator) at 11.6x, against which Range sits at a modest discount.

The valuation question is whether that discount represents mispricing or appropriate risk pricing. The forward multiple is built on EPS estimates that embed ~$4.97 gas. Our energy framework treats the elevated gas tape as cyclical rather than structural; multiple comparable E&Ps in our coverage show forward estimates inflated by transient commodity premium. On a normalized mid-cycle gas price (closer to $3.25–3.75 Henry Hub), Range's forward earnings power compresses and the 8.6x multiple becomes roughly fair, not cheap. The PV-10 doubling on a $1.26 SEC gas-price increase quantifies the sensitivity: the same torque that lifted reserve value can reverse it.

Analyst targets span $36 (low) to $59 (high), mean $47.32, median $46.00, with sentiment flagged as degrading and the recommendation distribution skewed to HOLD (15 of 23 ratings). The current price sits ~16% below the median target, providing some support, but the targets themselves are set against the elevated-gas backdrop.

We score valuation 6.0: cheap-looking on trailing and forward multiples, but the cheapness is inseparable from a commodity premium that the forward strip may not sustain, and the hedge book caps the upside that would justify a premium. Asymmetry is limited.

## Competitive Landscape

Range competes against EQT (larger, dry-gas focused), Antero (closest liquids-rich comp), CNX, and Comstock. Within Appalachia, Range's competitive position is improving on the metrics that matter: it has one of the lowest-cost, longest-life acreage positions in the basin, full operatorship for capital control, and NGL optionality that lifts realized prices above the dry-gas benchmark. The drilling success rate (100% on 53 net wells in 2025) and the ability to harvest DUC inventory rather than aggressively drill are signs of an asset base that compounds free cash flow rather than capital intensity.

The competitive constraint is egress, which is also the source of the transport bill. Range is effectively long Appalachian gas/NGL prices and short take-away capacity costs. Relative to peers, the NGL mix is a genuine structural edge over EQT, while Antero offers a similar profile at a modestly higher multiple. We score competitive position 7.0: improving trajectory on cost and inventory quality, but bounded by single-basin egress dependence.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-basin/single-state concentration.** With ~100% of reserves in Pennsylvania, Range is more exposed than diversified E&Ps to PA's regulatory and political regime.

**Escalating setback regulation.** The November 2024 Cecil Township ordinance (setbacks raised to 2,500 ft from structures / 5,000 ft from schools) effectively bans new pads there; Range's challenge was dismissed and is on appeal in Washington County Court. More material: on December 9, 2025, the PA Environmental Quality Board accepted a citizen petition for statewide setback rulemaking. Range deems it "unlawful" and expects no near-term action, but this elevates a local fight toward a statewide precedent risk that is genuinely company-specific and not fully priced. (Partly offset by PA's November 2025 withdrawal from the RGGI cap-and-trade program, which removes a separate overhang and modestly supports in-state gas-power demand.)

**Firm-transportation take-or-pay (~$5.9B through 2037; ~$834M in 2026).** Fixed minimum-volume payments regardless of throughput. If production or prices fall, Range pays without receiving revenue, the single biggest non-commodity downside amplifier.

**Legacy North Louisiana obligations.** A $259.9M liability at 3/31/26 for gathering/transport on divested assets, running off through 2030, providing no future benefit.

**Commodity leverage with capped upside.** Earnings swing hard with NYMEX. The hedge book (sold call swaptions on >35% of remaining 2026 gas) protects downside but caps participation in a rising-gas environment; our framework views the current elevated gas premium as cyclical, which means the downside on a reversion is the more relevant risk.

**Other items.** Sub-1 current ratio (0.67) and borrowing-base sensitivity to reserve revaluation; finite local ethane offtake; PA impact fee (~$31.6M in 2025) plus recurring severance-tax proposals; potential CAMT applicability as income scales toward $1B; no employment agreements for senior management (retention risk). OBBBA's reinstated 100% bonus depreciation (July 2025) is a cash-flow positive offset.

We score risk factors 5.0: the firm-transport take-or-pay and the escalating statewide setback petition are specific, material, and not obviously priced into an 8.6x forward multiple.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is balanced-to-complacent and offers no contrarian read against the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV of 33.7% sits essentially in line with 1-year historical vol of 32.5% (spread +1.2%, fair), at the 19th percentile of the current chain. Put/call skew is a modest 2.2% (balanced), put/call OI ratio 0.81, and the term structure is in normal contango (33.7% near vs 37.2% at six months). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±5.6%. IV has fallen 3.2 points since May 22 alongside a 2.4% price decline, resolving uncertainty rather than building it. There is no elevated put demand contradicting the rating, and no unusual activity signaling informed positioning ahead of a catalyst. The 11.1% short interest (6.2 days to cover) is elevated but not extreme, consistent with the degrading analyst sentiment and the commodity-reversion concern rather than a distress signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Structural U.S. gas demand from LNG exports and power/data-center load lifts Henry Hub and tightens Appalachian basis durably, validating the early-2026 move. Range harvests its inventory for sustained free cash flow, retires the rest of its bank debt, and executes the full $1.5B buyback into a declining share count. Forward EPS holds near $4.66, and the multiple re-rates toward the peer median (13.8x) on durable cash returns. That path supports the analyst high target of ~$59 (roughly +48% from current), with a more grounded bull around $52–55 on ~11x a sustained $4.66–5.00 EPS.

**Bear case.** Gas prices revert toward the mid-cycle $3.25–3.75 and basis softens. Forward EPS compresses toward $3.00–3.50, hedges cap the offset, the firm-transport take-or-pay drains cash on reduced netbacks, and the statewide setback rulemaking advances into a material overhang. The multiple holds at ~9–10x compressed earnings, taking the stock toward $30–33 (roughly –17% to –25%), near the analyst low of $36 and the lower half of the 2023–25 range ($22.61–$43.50).

**Base case.** Gas normalizes somewhat from the Q1'26 peak but stays above prior-cycle lows on the demand build-out; EPS settles in the low-$4 range; the stock trades roughly sideways to modestly higher as deleveraging and buybacks offset commodity reversion. Fair value around $42–45.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, likely late July) is the key checkpoint to see whether the Q1 netback strength persists and whether estimate revisions hold or roll over.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Forward EPS estimates hold or rise above $4.66 for next year after a normalization in the gas strip — i.e., the earnings base proves durable independent of the peak-gas quarter, with current-year consensus sustaining above $4.30.
2. The PA statewide setback rulemaking is formally rejected or stalled by the EQB, removing the primary company-specific structural overhang.
3. A pullback toward $33–35 (the lower half of the recent range) that restores genuine valuation asymmetry on normalized earnings.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Henry Hub reverting below $3.00 with Appalachian basis turning negative again, compressing forward EPS toward $3.00.
2. The EQB advancing statewide setback rulemaking toward a binding standard that would impair drillable inventory.
3. Borrowing-base reduction on a reserve revaluation, or a current-ratio deterioration signaling liquidity strain.

## Conclusion

Range Resources is a well-run, deleveraging Appalachian gas/NGL producer with a genuinely improved balance sheet, an accelerating capital-return program, and a multi-decade inventory that compounds free cash flow without aggressive spending. The score trajectory (6.2 → 6.8 over three months) reflects real improvement in balance sheet and capital allocation. The recent price decline from the March high of $48.31 to ~$40 has not been accompanied by fundamental deterioration — estimates are still rising and the buyback is intact — which improves the risk/reward modestly.

What prevents a BUY is specific and falsifiable: the earnings inflection that drives the rising estimates is inseparable from a natural gas price spike and a positive basis swing that the broader energy framework treats as cyclical, not structural; the hedge book caps participation in the bull-case upside while the firm-transport take-or-pay amplifies the bear-case downside; insiders sold $42–$48 in spring 2026 with no open-market buying; and the PA Environmental Quality Board's December 2025 acceptance of a statewide setback petition is an escalating, company-specific structural risk in the one basin where all of Range's reserves sit. On normalized gas, ~8.6x forward is fair versus Antero's 11.6x, not asymmetrically cheap. We rate RRC a HOLD with a growth lean, and would upgrade on confirmation that the earnings base holds independent of peak gas prices or on a pullback toward the low-$30s.
