## Executive Summary

Gartner has experienced a 65% decline from its 52-week high of $452 to approximately $145, compressing its valuation from a premium growth multiple to roughly 11x trailing free cash flow. The core question is binary: if contract value growth re-accelerates from the current 1% FX-neutral level toward mid-single digits, the stock is materially undervalued at these levels. If growth stagnates or deteriorates further, the current multiple reflects a fair assessment of a business whose competitive moat is under pressure from AI commoditization of research insights.

Q1 2026 earnings beat consensus by 11% and management raised full-year guidance, suggesting the fundamental floor is holding. Free cash flow of $371M in Q1 (+29% YoY) demonstrates the business model's cash generation remains intact. However, net contract value increase was negative $56M in Q1, wallet retention has dropped meaningfully (GTS from 102% to 96%), and sales headcount is declining — collectively signaling demand weakness that has not yet stabilized.

The investment case hinges on whether the $1.2B+ annual FCF sustains through a period of flat-to-modest organic growth while buybacks compound EPS (potentially 8-10% annually at current prices). Against this, AI disruption represents a genuine structural threat to Gartner's core research model, and 17% short interest indicates significant institutional skepticism.

## Company Overview

Gartner is the world's dominant research and advisory firm for enterprise technology and business strategy, serving 13,000+ clients across 90 countries. The business operates through three segments: Insights (78% of revenue, subscription-based research), Conferences (10%, in-person events), and Consulting (9%, project-based advisory). Revenue was $6.5B in 2025.

The company's competitive position rests on the "Gartner Magic Quadrant" brand standard, 2,400+ proprietary analysts, 510,000+ annual client interactions generating non-replicable data, and 40+ years of institutional trust. Multi-year contracts (77% of base) provide revenue visibility.

In February 2026, Gartner divested its Digital Markets business (Capterra, GetApp) for $110M after a $150M goodwill impairment — effectively conceding that the acquisition strategy failed and refocusing on the core advisory model.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Growth:**
Revenue grew from $5.48B (2022) to $6.50B (2025), representing a compound growth rate of approximately 6%. However, growth has decelerated sharply: from +8% in 2023 to +6% in 2024 to +4% in 2025. Q1 2026 adjusted revenue was -1.4% FX-neutral excluding divestitures. Contract value — the leading indicator for subscription revenue — grew just 1.0% FX-neutral in Q1 2026 (GTS +0.4%, GBS +3.2%).

Wallet retention — a critical metric measuring whether existing clients spend more or less each year — declined from 102% to 96% in GTS and from 106% to 99% in GBS. This means existing clients are spending less, which is qualitatively different from slower new client acquisition.

**Profitability:**
Normalized operating margin is approximately 18% (excluding the $150M goodwill impairment). Insights segment contributes 77% gross margin, Conferences 50%, Consulting 34%. The Insights margin has been stable; Consulting margin is compressing (from 36% to 34%) alongside the 15% revenue decline in Q1 2026.

**Cash Flow:**
Operating cash flow was $1.29B in 2025. LTM free cash flow approximates $1.26B. FCF conversion runs at approximately 170% of net income — characteristic of a subscription business with prepaid contracts. Capital expenditures are minimal at $115M annually.

**Balance Sheet:**
The Altman Z-Score of 1.3 and debt/equity of 24x are artifacts of aggressive buybacks depleting book equity, not operating distress. Net debt/Adjusted EBITDA is 0.8x (per management). Total debt is $3.0B (all fixed-rate: $800M due 2028, $600M due 2029, $800M due 2030, $350M due 2031, $450M due 2035). Cash on hand is $1.72B. Annual interest expense of ~$120M is covered 10x by operating cash flow. There is no near-term refinancing risk.

**Capital Return:**
Gartner repurchased $2.0B in shares during 2025 (7.0M shares at ~$286/share average) and $535M in Q1 2026 (3.3M shares at ~$162/share). Shares outstanding have declined from ~80M in 2022 to ~67M. At the current $145 price, the company can retire approximately 8% of float annually with FCF alone. The board added $600M to buyback authorization in April 2026.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is concerning. Contract value growth of 1% FX-neutral represents the lowest level in Gartner's modern history as a public company. The components break down unfavorably:

- GTS contract value: +0.4% FX-neutral (effectively flat)
- GBS contract value: +3.2% (decelerating)
- Consulting backlog: down 7-9%, revenue -15% in Q1
- US government contract value: less than half of the December 2024 level retained ($126M at end of 2025)

The positive side: Q1 2026 EPS of $3.32 beat consensus of $3.00 by 10.6%. Management raised full-year Adjusted EPS guidance to ≥$13.25 (from $12.30) and Adjusted EBITDA to ≥$1.545B. FCF grew 29% YoY in Q1. Estimate revisions are positive: current year EPS estimates up 4.0% over 90 days, next year up 5.9%.

The critical question is whether the +1% CV growth represents a trough or the new normal. Management is guiding to acceleration in H2 2026, but they are also cutting sales headcount (GTS -3.3%, GBS -3.7% YoY), which limits the organic growth capacity.

## Valuation Assessment

At $145 per share:
- Market cap: $9.7B
- Enterprise value: ~$11.0B (adding ~$1.3B net debt)
- EV/Adjusted EBITDA: ~7.1x (on adjusted EBITDA of ~$1.545B guided)
- P/FCF: ~7.7x (on $1.26B LTM FCF)
- Forward P/E: ~10.9x on 2026E EPS of $13.25 (management guidance), ~15.2x on trailing EPS of $9.65
- Consensus Forward P/E: ~10.6x on consensus 2026E of $13.69, ~9.4x on 2027E of $15.34
- P/E vs. peer median (13.9x): discount

The stock trades below its peer median multiple despite generating superior FCF conversion and having a more subscription-heavy revenue base. Against ACN at 13.9x, CTSH at 10.4x, and IBM at 19.4x, Gartner's forward multiple appears compressed — particularly given that its FCF yield substantially exceeds peers.

The compression is warranted by the growth deceleration (peers ACN/CTSH have steadier organic growth) and the AI disruption overhang. However, the magnitude of discount (stock down 65% from highs) prices significant permanent impairment. If contract value growth recovers to 4-5%, a re-rating to 15-18x forward earnings is plausible, implying $200-240 per share. If CV stagnates at 1-2%, the current multiple is approximately fair.

## Competitive Landscape

Gartner's primary competitive advantage — the Magic Quadrant framework and proprietary analyst interactions — remains intact in the near term. No competitor has replicated the institutional trust and data depth. Forrester Research is a distant second; IDC competes in syndicated research; McKinsey/BCG overlap in strategy consulting but not subscription research.

The emerging threat is AI-generated research content. Large language models and successors can produce surface-level technology analysis indistinguishable from junior analyst output. Gartner's 10-K explicitly acknowledges that LLMs "could reduce the need to enter our websites" and that clients loading proprietary content into LLMs "could reduce the value of our offerings."

Gartner's defense: proprietary practitioner data (not available in public training sets), the "trusted advisor" relationship model, vendor briefings with confidential information, and the AskGartner AI tool launched August 2025. The bear case: enterprises paying $100K+ annually for research subscriptions may find "80% as good" insights available from AI tools at marginal cost.

The wallet retention decline in GTS could be an early signal of this competitive pressure, though it could equally reflect macro-driven budget compression. The distinction matters enormously for long-term fair value.

## Risk Assessment

**1. AI Disruption (High — Not Fully Priced):** The 65% drawdown partially reflects this risk, but the market has not conclusively determined whether Gartner's moat survives AI commoditization. The stock is priced for "challenged growth" but not yet for "structurally declining business." If evidence emerges that enterprise research subscriptions face meaningful churn from AI alternatives, the multiple would compress further from already depressed levels.

**2. Contract Value Stagnation (Medium-High):** If negative net CV increase persists for 2-3 more quarters, revenue will begin declining rather than merely decelerating. Sales force reductions limit recovery capacity.

**3. US Government Spending (Medium):** The DOGE-driven headwind has already reduced federal CV by more than half. Additional government contract terminations could create further drag, though the remaining exposure limits absolute dollar impact.

**4. Macro Sensitivity (Medium):** Consulting (-15% Q1) is the leading indicator. In a recession, subscription budgets face 5-10% compression as clients rationalize vendor spending. Gartner's prior recession experience (2008-09) showed CV declining mid-single digits — manageable but painful when combined with current AI disruption.

**5. Short Interest (Elevated):** At 17.1% of float with 5.7 days to cover, significant institutional capital is positioned for further downside. This creates two-sided risk: correct positioning if fundamentals deteriorate further, or potential squeeze catalyst if results surprise positively.

### Options Market Signal

Options pricing confirms elevated uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV of 67.5% vs. 53.6% historical volatility indicates the market prices a ~14pp premium for near-term event risk. The term structure in steep backwardation (67.5% near-term vs. 50.6% at 6-month) suggests the market views the current period as resolution-defining rather than permanently elevated risk. Put/call skew of 16.6% at the weekly tenor reflects hedging demand, though the P/C OI ratio at 0.25 (low) suggests existing positioning is not overwhelmingly bearish. The options market is pricing a ±3.9% weekly move and ±12.2% monthly — elevated but not extreme for a stock that has already fallen 65%.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($200-240, 12-18 months):**
Contract value growth re-accelerates to 4-5% as AI advisory demand drives new subscriptions, government headwinds annualize, and macro stabilizes. Buybacks retire 8-10% of shares annually, driving double-digit EPS growth even with modest revenue growth. The stock re-rates to 15-18x forward earnings ($15.34 × 15x = $230). The AskGartner platform creates incremental subscription value and defends against AI commoditization. High FCF yield provides downside support through ongoing buybacks.

**Bear Case ($100-120, 6-12 months):**
AI-generated research progressively erodes the value of traditional analyst subscriptions. Wallet retention continues declining toward 90%, CV growth turns negative, and revenue begins contracting. Consulting segment deterioration accelerates into broader subscription weakness. Recession materializes, compressing enterprise IT budgets. The stock de-rates to 8x depressed forward earnings as the market prices structural decline rather than cyclical trough.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For current HOLD recommendation:**

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Contract value growth accelerates to ≥3% FX-neutral for two consecutive quarters, indicating organic growth recovery
2. Wallet retention stabilizes at ≥100% in GTS, confirming existing clients are not structurally downgrading
3. Stock declines to $120-130 (8x FCF), where buyback math alone provides sufficient return regardless of growth trajectory

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Contract value growth turns negative for two consecutive quarters
2. Evidence emerges of enterprise research subscription churn specifically attributed to AI alternatives (e.g., major client citations, competitive wins by AI-first platforms)
3. Management reduces or pauses buyback program, signaling loss of confidence in cash flow sustainability

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected August 2026). This will provide the second quarter of post-Digital-Markets organic data and determine whether Q1's marginal improvement was noise or the beginning of recovery.

## Conclusion

Gartner presents a genuinely uncertain investment case. The financial profile (high FCF yield, aggressive buybacks, 0.8x net leverage) is attractive for a business with Gartner's brand position and subscription economics. A 65% decline typically requires either fundamental confirmation of structural decline or represents a buying opportunity.

The evidence is ambiguous: Q1 beat estimates and FCF grew 29%, but contract value growth is near-zero and wallet retention is declining. AI disruption is real but not yet quantifiable in churn data. The 17% short interest reflects legitimate skepticism that cannot be dismissed.

At $145, the stock compensates for moderate growth impairment through buyback-driven EPS compounding. It does not compensate for permanent decline. Whether CV re-accelerates or deteriorates further will emerge over the next 2-3 quarters. The appropriate position is patience rather than conviction in either direction.
