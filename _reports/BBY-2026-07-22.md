**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Best Buy has emerged from a two-year stretch of high-single-digit comp declines into a fragile but real stabilization. FY2026 revenue rose 0.4% to $41.7 billion, comps turned positive (+0.5%), and Q1 FY2027 delivered the strongest read yet: revenue +1.9% to $8.94 billion, comps +2.0%, gross margin up to 23.5%, and adjusted EPS of $1.28 versus $1.15 a year earlier. The turnaround is a margin-mix story rather than a demand recovery in Best Buy's core hardware categories, built on higher-margin, capital-light revenue streams (Best Buy Ads retail media, Best Buy Marketplace third-party seller platform, and Services) and supplemented by a favorable console and tech-refresh cycle lifting Entertainment (+38% comps) and Computing.

The problem for the equity is timing. The stock has climbed 28% from its 52-week low ($55.10) to $85.22, now trading above the analyst mean target of $79.15 and just below the high target of $90. At roughly 13x the FY27 consensus EPS of $6.55, the shares are no longer priced for distress, but the turnaround is not proven enough to justify chasing them into a leadership vacuum. CEO Corie Barry steps down October 31, 2026, longtime CFO Matt Bilunas departs at the end of July 2026, and a broader C-suite reorganization is underway — an unusually deep management transition to run through during an unfinished operational turn.

We rate BBY a HOLD. The balance sheet is healthy (Altman Z 3.63, only ~$1.15 billion of financial debt, undrawn $1.25 billion revolver), operating cash flow funds a well-covered ~4.4% dividend, and the margin-mix thesis has directional support in the data. But the stock has already re-rated to fair value, buybacks have decelerated to zero in Q1, and the structural questions (AI-driven price transparency, vendors selling direct, discretionary-demand maturity) remain unresolved. The risk/reward at $85 is balanced rather than asymmetric.

## Company Overview

Best Buy is North America's leading omnichannel consumer electronics retailer, incorporated in Minnesota in 1966 and operating roughly 1,065 stores across two segments: Domestic (U.S., ~92% of revenue, includes Best Buy Health) and International (Canada, ~8%). Revenue spans six categories: Computing & Mobile Phones (the largest and fastest-growing at ~47% of Domestic sales), Consumer Electronics, Appliances, Entertainment, Services, and Other. Online now represents about 34% of Domestic sales.

The strategic pivot is the crux of the investment case. Management is running two plays simultaneously: defending its position as the leading omnichannel technology destination, and scaling higher-margin "new profit streams." Best Buy Ads monetizes the company's traffic through a retail media network; Best Buy Marketplace, launched Domestically in FY2026, generates commission and advertising revenue from third-party sellers without inventory investment. Both mirror the Amazon and Walmart Connect model of high-margin, capital-light revenue that can lift consolidated gross margin even on flat unit sales.

The competitive moat rests on knowledgeable service labor (Geek Squad), in-home services, integrated fulfillment across store/online/in-home channels, deep vendor partnerships (store-within-a-store concepts), and scale-driven pricing. The offsetting reality is a higher-cost service model that is a margin disadvantage against price-only competitors and a demand profile that is highly discretionary and increasingly showrooming-prone.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue: decline arrested, growth marginal.** The trajectory: FY2023 $46.3B → FY2024 $43.5B (–6.1%) → FY2025 $41.5B (–4.4%) → FY2026 $41.7B (+0.4%). Q1 FY2027's +1.9% is the clearest inflection to date, led by gaming (Domestic Entertainment comps +38% on a new console cycle), computing, and mobile phones, and dragged by large appliances (Domestic –13.6%) and home theater. Revenue remains roughly 10% below the FY23 peak; this is stabilization, not recovery.

**Margins: the real engine.** Gross margin held near 22.5% in FY26 and improved to 23.5% in Q1 FY27, aided by the Ads/Marketplace/Services mix offsetting weaker product margins. GAAP operating margin was 3.3% in FY26 (up from 3.0%), with adjusted operating margin of 4.3%. Q1 FY27 operating margin jumped to 4.1% from 2.5%, largely on lower restructuring charges as the FY26 store-optimization and Best Buy Health wind-downs completed. Net income was $1.07 billion (FY26), GAAP EPS $5.04 (adjusted $6.43).

**Cash flow and balance sheet: a genuine strength.** Operating cash flow was $1.96 billion in FY26 against ~$704 million capex (guided ~$750 million FY27), leaving comfortable free cash flow to fund the dividend and modest buybacks. The Altman Z-Score of 3.63 sits firmly in the safe zone; the current ratio is 1.11; ratings are investment grade and stable (S&P BBB+, Moody's A3). The headline D/E of 4.95 is a lease-accounting artifact — actual long-term notes total only ~$1.15 billion ($500M due 2028, $650M due 2030), and the $1.25 billion revolver is fully undrawn. The optically high ~36% ROE is flattered by a thin equity base, not by financial leverage.

**Capital return is decelerating, and that matters.** The dividend was raised to $0.96/quarter ($3.84 annualized, ~4.4% yield) and is well covered by FCF. But buybacks slowed sharply to $273 million in FY26 (from $500 million in FY25), and zero were executed in Q1 FY27 despite $3.0 billion of remaining authorization. Management signals only ~$300 million of buybacks for FY27, prioritizing balance-sheet flexibility through the tariff and leadership uncertainty. The reduced share-count tailwind removes one lever that had supported per-share metrics.

**Earnings quality and surprise track record.** The company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters (3.3% to 6.0% surprises), which lends confidence to the current FY27 estimate of $6.55. Segment reclassification in Q1 (moving credit-card and digital-content revenue into "Services") inflates the strategically important Services line cosmetically, which matters when comparing period-over-period mix.

Financial Health earns a 7.0. The cash generation, minimal financial leverage, investment-grade balance sheet, and covered dividend are the most durable elements of this story and the dimension most reliably associated with relative outperformance.

## Growth Analysis

This is a margin/mix turnaround on a flat-to-slightly-growing top line, not a growth story. The bull case rests on three legs: (1) Ads + Marketplace + Services lifting consolidated gross margin even on flat sales; (2) a tech-refresh/AI-PC upgrade cycle driving Computing; and (3) a gaming-console cycle driving Entertainment. Two of those three are cyclical rather than structural.

Estimate revisions are stable but unexciting: current-year EPS at $6.55 (+1.0% over 90 days), next-year $7.07 (+0.5% over 90 days). This falls well short of the revision velocity that would flag a genuine turn worth chasing. Consensus is confident in delivery, not scrambling to catch up to an accelerating fundamental. The strong-buy signal reserved for stocks with rapidly rising estimates plus revenue acceleration is not present.

The Marketplace/Ads flywheel is the most credible structural growth vector, following a path Amazon and Walmart have validated. If retail-media and marketplace commission revenue scale as management intends, they add high-margin dollars to consolidated gross margin over multiple years that fall through disproportionately to operating income. The risk is that this remains an unproven promise; management held, rather than raised, full-year guidance after the Q1 beat, which is why a Seeking Alpha downgrade to Hold on June 11 cited "awaiting evidence earnings growth can accelerate."

Growth Prospects score a 5.0. The margin-mix thesis is real and directionally supported, but the top line is barely growing, appliances remain in double-digit decline, and the more durable growth engine (Marketplace/Ads) is early and unquantified.

## Valuation Assessment

At $85.22, BBY trades at roughly 13x the FY27 consensus EPS of $6.55 and ~12x the FY28 estimate of $7.07, with a ~4.4% dividend yield. Against the Consumer Discretionary peer median P/E of 17.9, this looks cheap on the surface. But the discount is largely earned: Best Buy's cumulative five-year TSR of 74.07 badly trails its peer group's 164.12 — a ~90-point gap that reflects genuine multi-year underperformance rather than a temporary mispricing.

The easy re-rating has already happened. The stock trades above the analyst mean and median targets ($79.15 and $79.50), just below the high target of $90. The consensus target-as-floor logic that applies to cheap inflection stocks does not hold here: estimate revisions are essentially flat, so there is no rapidly rising estimate base pulling targets higher. When a stock trades above the mean target on stable estimates, it has re-rated toward fair value.

There is one under-appreciated piece of optionality: Best Buy paid IEEPA tariffs later ruled unauthorized by the Supreme Court on February 20, 2026, and is pursuing refunds as importer of record, but has booked $0 in Q1 FY27 given legal and administrative uncertainty. Any recovery would be a one-time tailwind not in estimates, though it is too uncertain to underwrite.

The valuation dimension is where caution is most warranted. A low P/E on a structurally challenged, low-growth retailer has historically been a value-trap signal rather than a buy signal; cheapness alone, absent a specific catalyst the market is missing, is not a reason to lean in. The turnaround catalyst is real but already partially reflected in the 28% move. We score Valuation 4.5: not egregiously overvalued, but no longer offering the asymmetric upside that existed at $55–60, and carrying genuine value-trap risk if the margin-mix thesis stalls.

## Competitive Landscape

Best Buy competes against Amazon and other e-commerce players, mass merchants (Walmart, Costco, Target), warehouse clubs, mobile carriers, and increasingly its own vendors' direct-to-consumer channels. The competitive trajectory is the more concerning half of the story.

Supplier concentration is extreme and creates leverage against Best Buy: the top-20 suppliers represent ~80% of merchandise, and just five (Apple, Samsung, HP, LG, Sony) account for ~55%. There are no long-term supply contracts, and these same vendors are expanding direct-to-consumer sales, which structurally erodes Best Buy's role as an intermediary. When Apple or Samsung can sell directly at equal or better economics, the retailer's negotiating position weakens over time.

The AI threat features prominently in the FY26 10-K, which repeatedly warns of "AI shopping bots," "agentic shopping," and AI-driven price transparency compressing pricing flexibility. For a showrooming-prone retailer whose differentiation partly rests on in-store expertise and the friction of comparison shopping, agentic commerce that instantly finds the lowest price is a genuine structural headwind. Management is responding by building agentic-shopping integrations, which is defensive.

The Marketplace and Ads land grab represents an improving competitive position in high-margin capital-light revenue, an area where Best Buy is following rather than leading, but where its traffic and vendor relationships give it a credible right to play. Best Buy Health, by contrast, has been a serial value-destroyer: $171 million of goodwill/intangible impairments in FY26, a partial component exit, and ~$298 million of remaining goodwill flagged for further impairment risk.

Weighing the deteriorating core (vendor direct, AI price transparency, appliance weakness) against the improving margin-mix initiatives, the net trajectory is modestly negative. Competitive Position scores 4.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Leadership transition (highest near-term risk).** The cluster of Item 5.02 8-K filings reflects a wholesale C-suite turnover. Barry (in the role since 2019) is succeeded by 27-year veteran Jason Bonfig; five executives were named to restructured roles in July 2026. Running a rare double CEO-plus-CFO transition through an unfinished turnaround is the single most important idiosyncratic risk, and the market has already reacted negatively to the CFO exit. Continuity is partly protected by promoting an insider rather than an outsider.

**Structural demand maturity.** Consumer electronics are highly discretionary with elongating replacement cycles. The recent comp turn leans on cyclical console and refresh cycles that will fade.

**Supplier concentration and vendor disintermediation** (detailed above): ~55% of merchandise from five suppliers with no long-term contracts and growing direct-to-consumer competition.

**AI disruption to the retail model**: expanded 10-K language on agentic shopping and price transparency represents a genuine, difficult-to-quantify structural threat.

**Best Buy Health execution failure**: serial impairments and a partial exit; ~$298 million of remaining goodwill carries explicit further-impairment risk.

**New-initiative execution risk**: Marketplace introduces third-party product liability, IP, and quality/reputation risks, with an unsettled retail-media/marketplace regulatory environment.

**Founder selling**: Richard Schulze sold well over $150 million across May–July 2026, cutting his stake from ~11.4 million to ~9.5 million shares. Given his age (80s), this is most plausibly estate/diversification rather than a fundamental signal, but it is persistent and there is no offsetting open-market insider buying.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is complacent-to-mildly-constructive, offering no confirmation of imminent downside. Near-term ATM IV of 36.9% sits essentially at 1-year historical volatility of 36.5% (spread 0.5%), IV percentile is a low 25%, and the put/call skew is –14.9% (call-heavy). The term structure is in contango (near 36.9% vs long-term 42.5%), a normal/complacent shape. ATM IV has fallen from 40.7% to 36.9% over the past month even as the stock rose 12.9%, and the put/call OI ratio ticked up modestly to 0.70. The implied near-term move is a muted ±2.2%. The options market is not pricing elevated near-term stress and is leaning slightly bullish through positioning, consistent with a stock that has re-rated toward fair value on a proven Q1 beat rather than one facing an imminent catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (to ~$95–100)
The margin-mix turnaround compounds: Ads, Marketplace, and Services scale faster than expected, lifting consolidated gross margin toward 24%+ while the AI-PC refresh and console cycle sustain positive comps in Computing and Entertainment. Restructuring drags fully roll off, adjusted operating margin pushes toward 5%, and FY28 EPS approaches or exceeds $7.50. Bonfig's insider succession proves seamless, buybacks resume at scale into 2027, and IEEPA tariff refunds provide a one-time cash windfall. At 14x on ~$7.50 EPS, the stock reaches the high-$90s to ~$100.

### Bear Case (to ~$60–65)
The comp turn proves cyclical rather than structural: the console/refresh tailwind fades, appliances stay in decline, and Marketplace/Ads growth disappoints against unsettled regulation and vendor-direct competition. AI-driven price transparency compresses margins. The double leadership transition produces execution missteps, discretionary electronics soften, and estimates roll over. A de-rating back toward 9–10x on flat-to-declining EPS returns the stock to the low-$60s, roughly the analyst low target of $60 and a ~25% loss from current levels.

### Base Case (to ~$82–88)
The turnaround holds but does not accelerate: comps stay low-single-digit positive, gross margin grinds modestly higher, and FY27 EPS lands near consensus $6.55 with FY28 near $7.00. The ~4.4% dividend anchors total return, but the multiple stays range-bound near 13x as the market waits for proof of sustained top-line growth through the leadership handoff. The stock trades sideways in the low-to-mid $80s, delivering total return roughly equal to the dividend yield.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoints are the Q2 FY27 earnings report (late August 2026, into the holiday build), the CFO departure (end of July 2026), and the CEO transition (October 31, 2026). Full thesis resolution requires two-to-three quarters of post-transition operating data.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions turn meaningfully positive (current-year EPS revised up >5% over 90 days) alongside comps sustaining >2%, confirming the margin-mix thesis is compounding rather than cyclical.
- A pullback toward the low-$70s that restores asymmetric upside to the base case while fundamentals remain intact.
- Explicit disclosure that Ads + Marketplace revenue is scaling with a quantified margin contribution, plus resumption of buybacks at $500M+ annualized.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Comps turn negative or gross margin reverses below 22.5%, signaling the turn was cyclical.
- The leadership transition produces a guidance cut or a strategic reset under new management.
- Further Best Buy Health or other goodwill impairment, or evidence AI/agentic commerce is measurably eroding pricing power.

**Reassessment point:** Q2 FY27 earnings (late August 2026), then the October CEO handoff.

## Conclusion

Best Buy has arrested a multi-year decline: comps are positive, margins are expanding on a credible high-margin mix shift, and the balance sheet is investment-grade with a well-covered ~4.4% dividend. The financial-health foundation is the strongest element of the story and warrants genuine credit. But the equity has already captured much of the good news, running 28% off its lows to trade above the analyst mean target on flat estimate revisions, while entering a rare double CEO-and-CFO transition with unresolved structural threats (vendor disintermediation, AI-driven price transparency, discretionary-demand maturity) and decelerating buybacks.

This is a HOLD, and the classification is uncertain rather than a confident conviction call: the risk/reward at $85 is balanced rather than asymmetric. The stock offers dividend-supported downside protection but no compelling appreciation catalyst until the margin-mix thesis proves durable through the leadership handoff. We would turn constructive on a pullback toward the low-$70s or on hard evidence that Marketplace/Ads is driving sustained EPS acceleration, and would turn negative on a comp reversal or a transition-driven guidance cut. The prior recommendation sequence (three AVOIDs into a HOLD) reflects a business that has improved fundamentally while the price has caught up to that improvement; the appropriate response is patience rather than a chase.
