## Executive Summary

NextEra Energy remains two businesses: Florida Power & Light, a rate-regulated monopoly serving more than 6 million customer accounts under a January 2026 rate agreement that runs through at least 2029, and NextEra Energy Resources, the largest U.S. renewables developer with 37,505 MW of net capacity, ~95% of it contracted at a weighted-average remaining PPA term of ~14 years. Operating delivery has not broken. Adjusted EPS compounded at 9.8% from 2021 to 2025 ($2.55 → $3.71), grew 9.8% per share in the first half of 2026 ($2.24 vs $2.04), and the company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters. Consensus 2026 and 2027 EPS have drifted up 0.8% and 0.7% respectively over 90 days.

The capital structure and risk profile have changed. The May 15, 2026 agreement to acquire Dominion Energy for 0.8138 NEE shares plus a pro rata share of $360M cash would issue roughly 716-740 million new shares — a 34-35% increase in share count, transferring ~25-26% of the combined company to Dominion holders — and add approximately $53B of debt to a balance sheet already carrying ~$110B against $57.1B of common equity. NEE has also committed to fund $2.25B of customer rate credits ($1.78B to Virginia) over two years post-close, roughly $0.30 per combined share after tax, and has agreed to a $6.52B general / $4.83B regulatory reverse termination fee against Dominion's $2.24B, a risk allocation that puts the cost of a state-commission failure squarely on the buyer. The exchange ratio is fixed with no disclosed collar, so NEE's 8-9% decline since announcement has reduced the consideration's value from a ~$67B headline to ~$61B without altering NEE's obligation to close.

Separately, the OBBBA "begin construction" deadline of July 5, 2026 for wind and solar PTC/ITC eligibility has now passed, and no clean-energy credits will attach to wind or solar placed in service after 2030. Management believes the current 2026-2030 pipeline qualifies. Even accepting that, the mechanism that produced a (18)% consolidated effective tax rate in 2025 and a (343)% segment rate at NEER has a legislated terminus. The 6-8% long-term growth target beyond roughly 2030 now depends on storage, nuclear, gas, transmission and regulated rate base, a different and less proven formula.

At ~21.0x consensus 2026 EPS of $4.05 and ~19.3x 2027 of $4.42, against a multi-utility peer median of 19.7x and a 2.9% forward yield versus a 3.0% peer median, NEE trades at rough parity with peers for above-peer growth. That is the strongest element of the bull case. It is not, in our view, sufficient margin of safety against a binary regulatory outcome roughly five months out, a rising-long-end funding regime, and management's demonstrated willingness to accept seller-friendly deal terms without disclosed synergy guidance.

## Company Overview

FPL had 35,963 MW of net owned generating capacity at year-end 2025 (36,616 MW including PPAs): 44 gas-capable units totaling 24,314 MW (~66% dual-fuel), 7,932 MW of solar across 108 facilities, 3,502 MW of nuclear across four units, 991 MW of batteries, and a 215 MW joint interest in a Georgia coal unit. It operates ~93,000 circuit miles of transmission and distribution under 226 municipal and county franchise agreements with terms typically running 30 years and expiring through 2055. Wholesale plus industrial customers were only ~5% of FPL operating revenues in each of 2023-2025; this is an overwhelmingly retail, rate-regulated franchise.

NEER holds 22,404 MW of wind, 10,504 MW of solar, 5,177 MW of battery storage across 18 states, 1,584 MW of gas, and merchant nuclear. Merchant exposure is small and specific: 1,878 MW total, comprising 841 MW of Seabrook nuclear (261 MW of which is contracted) and 1,032 MW of Northeast peaking capacity. NEET, the regulated transmission arm, carries a $3.2B rate base, and NEER holds ~$1.5B of equity-method interests in regulated gas pipelines with 3.8 Bcf/d of gross capacity.

FPL contributed $5,012M ($2.42 per share) of 2025 attributable net income, NEER $2,975M ($1.44), with Corporate & Other at –$1,152M (–$0.56). FPL therefore represents ~73% of segment earnings, set by a single commission in a single state.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2025.** Operating revenues $27,412M, operating income $8,280M, net income attributable to NEE $6,835M, GAAP diluted EPS $3.30, adjusted EPS $3.71. Operating cash flow was $12,485M against $24,606M of capital expenditure, investments and nuclear fuel.

Three features of these figures matter more than the headline.

First, attributable net income ($6,835M) exceeded total net income ($5,332M), meaning approximately $1,503M of book losses were allocated to noncontrolling interests, principally tax-equity (differential membership) investors. NEE received $3,276M of proceeds from those investors in 2025 and paid $516M back. The structure is disclosed and long-standing, but a meaningful share of reported EPS arises from loss allocation to partners rather than operating margin.

Second, the consolidated effective tax rate was (18)% and NEER's segment rate was (343)%, with clean-energy tax credits at NEER up ~$585M year over year. NEER's earnings are substantially a function of credit generation and monetization.

Third, Corporate & Other swung by –$1,256M, driven by roughly $1,002M of after-tax unfavorable marks on non-qualifying interest-rate hedges. NEE carried $47.3B of net notional interest-rate contracts at year-end; a hypothetical 10% decline in rates would raise the fair value of its net liabilities by ~$4,392M. These marks are excluded from adjusted earnings, so GAAP and adjusted results diverge widely and unpredictably. GAAP diluted EPS has fallen three consecutive years: $3.60 (2023), $3.37 (2024), $3.30 (2025).

**The funding gap.** FY2025 operating cash flow of $12,485M less $24,606M of capex/investments/nuclear fuel is –$12,121M; subtract $4,680M of common dividends and the external requirement was roughly $16.8B in one year. It was met with $13,047M of net new long-term debt, $2,760M of net tax equity, $1,115M of asset and interest sales, $2,038M of common stock and equity units, and $676M of commercial paper.

Long-term debt including current portion rose from $80,446M at 12/31/2024 to $93,056M at 12/31/2025, and to $104,203M at 6/30/2026, an $11.1B increase in six months. Adding ~$1,736M of commercial paper and ~$4,258M of other short-term debt gives roughly $110B of total debt against ~$57.1B of common equity and ~$11.0B of noncontrolling interests, or approximately 1.9x debt to common equity. First-half 2026 capital investment of ~$19.4B against ~$7.3B of operating cash flow means the external funding pace has accelerated, not moderated. NEE established a $4B at-the-market equity program on 12/31/2025 and issued $2,038M of stock and equity units in 2025 versus $48M in 2024; equity is now part of the funding mix in a way it was not two years ago.

**Offsets.** Liquidity is genuinely deep: $18.65B of net available liquidity at year-end 2025 across ~75 banks with none providing more than 5% of combined revolvers, upsized in February 2026 to $4.5B at FPL and $10.5B at NEECH with maturities to 2031. Ratings were Baa1/A–/A– stable at NEE and NEECH and A1/A/A at FPL as of the February 2026 10-K, with first mortgage bonds at Aa2/A+/AA–. FPL's mortgage interest coverage was 7.5x with capacity in excess of $38B of additional first mortgage bonds. No rating-downgrade acceleration triggers exist. NEECH commercial paper is rated P-2/A-2/F2 versus FPL's P-1/A-1/F1, so the growth entity has modestly weaker short-term market access than the utility.

We could not verify any rating-agency action on NEE following the merger announcement. That is a material gap for a leverage-sensitive issuer proposing to assume ~$53B of debt, and readers should treat the Baa1/A–/A– stable profile as a February 2026 datapoint rather than a current one. Agencies did move Dominion's outlook to positive in May 2026 on the announcement, which tells us they viewed being acquired by NEE as credit-improving for Dominion; it says nothing about NEE.

**H1 2026.** Revenue $14,235M, +9.9%. GAAP net income attributable of $5,326M versus $2,862M is dominated by a ~$1.3B swing in hedge marks and the absence of the prior year's XPLR charges. Reconciled adjusted earnings were $4,682M / $2.24 per share against $4,201M / $2.04, up 9.8%. Q2 alone was $2,407M / $1.15 versus $1.05, up 9.5% and 2.9% above the $1.12 consensus.

FPL's trailing-13-month earned regulatory ROE was 11.70% at 6/30/2026, with $1,335M of the new rate stabilization mechanism's ~$1.5B after-tax capacity remaining.

One qualification on FPL's earnings quality. Reserve amortization — an FPSC-authorized smoothing mechanism recorded as a reduction of depreciation expense — was $593M in 2025 versus $328M in 2024. The incremental $265M pre-tax is roughly $200M after tax against a total FPL net income increase of $469M. Roughly 40% of FPL's 2025 earnings growth is therefore attributable to increased use of the mechanism rather than to rate base growth. This is not aggressive accounting: the mechanism exists to let FPL land within an authorized ROE band, and the agreement both requires amortization to maintain the minimum and forbids amortization exceeding the maximum. The analytically useful point is narrower: FPL's earned ROE is partly a managed output, so what matters for durability is rate base growth and cost control, not the smoothness of reported ROE. The 2021 mechanism was nearly exhausted ($303M of $1.45B pre-tax remaining at year-end); the 2025 agreement replenished equivalent capacity for 2026-2029.

A note on data consistency: the supplied profile reports both trailing and forward P/E at 19.01x, which cannot be reconciled with the reported $3.30 of trailing EPS (25.8x) or with either consensus estimate. All multiples in this report are computed from the $85.10 price basis against stated earnings figures.

## Growth Analysis

**What is contracted.** The FPSC's January 2026 final order provides annualized retail base revenue increases of $945M effective January 1, 2026 and a further $705M effective January 1, 2027; a Solar and Battery Base Rate Adjustment mechanism for solar entering service 2027-2029 and batteries 2028-2029; authorized ROE of 10.95% within a 9.95%-11.95% band; a 59.6% authorized equity ratio; ~$1.5B after-tax of rate stabilization capacity; a base-rate reset right if permanent corporate tax rates change; and a large-load tariff for customers with ≥50 MW of new or incremental load at ≥85% load factor. That is roughly $1.65B of stepped base revenue plus four mechanisms covering ~73% of earnings, unusual visibility for a utility, and the most defensible support for the 6-8% adjusted EPS growth target through 2029.

The order is under an unresolved February 2026 joint motion for reconsideration filed by the Office of Public Counsel, Floridians Against Increased Rates, and a Florida Rising / ECOSWF / LULAC coalition. FPL has opposed. Reconsideration motions against approved FPSC settlements are frequently denied, so the base case is that the order stands, but a partial remand would strike at the foundation of the plan.

**Build cadence.** NEER added 6,262 MW in 2025 (1,604 MW wind, 2,859 MW solar, 1,799 MW battery). FPL added 894 MW solar and 522 MW battery in 2025, placed 596 MW of solar in service in January 2026, and expects ~298 MW solar and ~1,420 MW battery across the remainder of 2026. Remaining approved capital expenditure through 2030 is roughly $54.7B at FPL and $40.0B at NEER. The declining NEER tail figures (~$1.9B in 2029, ~$2.3B in 2030) reflect that only approved projects are included, not a forecast of collapsing investment. A renewables backlog near 35 GW has been reported secondarily but is a company-defined measure we did not verify in filings.

**Duane Arnold.** NEER holds ~70% and filed with the NRC in December 2025 to reinstate the operating license, targeting commercial operation in 2029, with a 25-year PPA for full capacity and agreements to move to 100% ownership subject to approvals. The 10-K does not name the offtaker; external attribution is unconfirmed. Nuclear retains 100% PTC / 30% ITC eligibility for facilities beginning construction through 2033, and storage retains 30% ITC through 2033 with phase-down in 2034-35, so these are the parts of the pipeline the tax legislation left intact.

**The constraint.** Estimate revisions are flat: current year +0.4% over 30 days and +0.8% over 90; next year +0.4% and +0.7%. Next quarter has been cut 4.9%. There is no earnings-inflection signal here, and the historical growth engine — wind and solar tax credits at scale — is now working through a closing window. Post-2030, growth must come from regulated rate base, storage, nuclear, gas and transmission at returns that, in Dominion's jurisdictions, are lower than FPL's.

**What the deal adds.** Dominion's "high load" class — Virginia customers including certain data centers with demand ≥25 MW and load factor ≥75% — grew from $422M to $814M of Q2 revenue year over year (+93%) and from $805M to $1,450M for the half (+80%). Dominion's total operating revenue rose 20.5% in H1 2026. Northern Virginia is the densest data-center market in the world. Buying regulated rate base attached to that load pocket, at the moment the merchant-renewables credit engine closes, is a coherent strategic rationale.

Static accretion math, using Dominion's FY2025 net income to common of ~$2,956M divided by 716M new NEE shares, gives ~$4.13 of acquired earnings per share issued versus NEE's own ~$3.97 guidance midpoint, mechanically accretive before financing costs, purchase accounting, synergies and the rate credits. Four offsets are material: Dominion's own capital program (H1 2026 plant construction of $5.8B against $2.5B of operating cash flow); the $2.25B of rate credits, roughly $0.30 per combined share after tax for two years, or ~7% of combined EPS; Virginia Power's authorized 9.80% ROE versus FPL's 10.95%; and CVOW construction risk. No management accretion or synergy guidance is available in filings.

| Forward dividend yield (~$2.49) | 2.9% | 3.0% |

Peer dispersion is wide (PCG 13.0x, ED 17.9x, D 19.7x, XEL 22.6x, SRE 31.1x), which limits the precision of any single comparison. Still, the core observation holds: NEE trades at rough parity to the multi-utility median for growth that has run roughly 300bp above typical regulated peers, with a yield in line. The premium that NEE commanded for most of the last decade is gone.

Is that mispricing? The five-year record frames the question. NEE's total shareholder return for the five years ended 12/31/2025 was +18.2% against +96.2% for the S&P 500, +59.1% for S&P 500 Utilities and +64.8% for Dow Jones U.S. Electricity; the proxy's pay-versus-performance table shows $100 invested at year-end 2020 worth $118.24 against a peer index at $146.25. Over the same window adjusted EPS rose 46% cumulatively. The underperformance was multiple compression, not an earnings-delivery failure on the adjusted series.

Two readings follow. The bull reading: compression has overshot, and at parity multiples an above-peer grower with contractual rate support is mispriced. The bear reading: compression correctly anticipated a tax-credit engine with a legislated end date, a funding model requiring ~$16.8B of annual external capital, ~$2.47B of cumulative after-tax XPLR charges representing real permanent capital loss, and GAAP EPS that declined in each of the last three years. An investor weighting GAAP rather than adjusted earnings would conclude the de-rating was better justified than the adjusted series implies.

We do not think the evidence resolves decisively in either direction, which is why the valuation score sits at 5.5 rather than higher. Parity multiples are not a margin of safety when a fixed-ratio, no-collar acquisition worth ~$61B sits between the current price and the base case. Analyst dispersion corroborates this: mean target $99, median $101, high $116, low $55. A $55 low print on a $178B regulated utility indicates at least one covering analyst models a materially impaired outcome.

Base case: 21.0x 2027 consensus $4.42 = **$93**, +9.3% from $85.10, ~12% total return with the dividend. Bull: 22.5x on ~$4.65 combined-company 2027 earnings, assuming the deal closes on schedule, rate credits are absorbed and Virginia load converts = **$105**, +23.4%. Bear: 16.5x on $4.25 = **$70**, –17.7%, essentially the 52-week low of $69.24, reflecting either a broken deal (the $4.83B regulatory fee alone is ~$2.32 per share) or an adverse FPSC reconsideration outcome or a further leg higher in long rates.

Probability-weighting 50% base / 20% bull / 30% bear gives roughly +4.0% price appreciation plus 2.9% of dividend, approximately +7% total. Against a utility cost of equity of 8-9% in the current rate environment, that is approximately fair, the definition of HOLD.

## Competitive Landscape

FPL's franchise quality is high and difficult to replicate: lowest-cost position in Florida, 226 franchise agreements running to 2055, 10.95% authorized ROE with a 59.6% equity ratio, and a new large-load tariff that captures data-center growth within the regulated construct. The 10-K itself flags the threat: improving distributed solar economics and tax incentives could erode the cost advantage that keeps self-generation uneconomic for a significant majority of customers.

NEER competes "primarily on the basis of price" in a "highly fragmented" wholesale market, differentiating on on-time delivery, creditworthiness, scale and risk management. Scale in tax-credit monetization has been a genuine advantage; the closing eligibility window narrows the arena in which that advantage compounds, while storage and nuclear (eligible through 2033) preserve part of it.

The 10-K notes that RTO/ISO rules and procedures are currently being revisited in stakeholder processes and other proceedings, including before FERC, in response to the substantial increase in power demand from data centers and other large-load customers, and that resulting changes "could in turn create new risks and opportunities." NEER operates across RTO/ISO jurisdictions plus 9,788 MW in non-RTO regions (6,121 MW WECC, 2,977 MW SERC). Whether the load-growth surplus flows primarily to regulated utilities or to asset owners and developers is unresolved in these proceedings.

On regulatory economics, the Dominion deal is a step down. Virginia Power's authorized ROE is 9.80% versus FPL's 10.95%, and Dominion disclosed $6.2B of regulatory assets ($4.1B at Virginia Power) on which it does not expect to earn a return during recovery. NEE would also inherit CVOW: 2.6 GW of offshore wind 27 miles off Virginia Beach at an estimated ~$11.7B excluding financing, with ~$0.1B of contingency remaining (roughly 7% of remaining spend), 31 of 176 turbines installed as of July 2026, completion targeted end-2027, and ~$83/MWh levelized cost. Virginia Power recorded $246M of charges for non-recoverable CVOW costs in Q2 2026 alone under the commission's cost-sharing mechanism. This is a technology and delivery profile unlike anything in NEE's onshore wind, solar and battery record.

What the deal does improve is single-regulator dependence: FPL under one Florida commission becomes FPL plus Virginia Power (Virginia SCC, North Carolina UC) plus DESC (South Carolina PSC). That is a genuine structural change in rate-setting exposure. It does little for severe-weather concentration, adding coastal Virginia and Atlantic offshore assets, and it concentrates political risk across three additional jurisdictions.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Merger regulatory approval — the dominant binary.** Filings went to FERC, the NRC and the Virginia, North Carolina and South Carolina commissions in July 2026; HSR clearance and both shareholder votes are also required. The Virginia SCC's clock runs roughly six months from the July 15 filing, implying a decision window around January 2027. The $4.83B regulatory reverse fee equals ~2.7% of market capitalization and ~63% of FY2025 adjusted earnings; the general fee is $6.52B, against $2.24B from Dominion, a ~2.9x asymmetry. NEE is "generally not required" to accept a defined "burdensome condition," but the agreement otherwise "may require NextEra Energy to accept conditions from regulators that could adversely impact NextEra Energy… without… the right to refuse to close." The definition of "burdensome condition" determines how much regulatory pain NEE must absorb, and it is not available to us. Large reverse fees are standard in utility M&A because state-commission risk is the binding constraint, so the asymmetry may be the market price of getting a deal signed rather than evidence of weakness, but NEE's shareholders bear it either way. Outside date November 2027, extendable to August 2028.

**2. Virginia affordability and political exposure.** Filing-confirmed context: Virginia Power filed in May 2026 to recover ~$2.7B of projected jurisdictional fuel expense plus a $1.1B under-recovered balance (option one implying a $1.9B increase in fuel revenues); a Rider RGGI revenue requirement of $1,179M was requested for a program effective July 2026; a Rider OSW requirement of $670M; and 2025 Biennial Review base rate increases of $566M effective January 2026 with $210M more in January 2027, that order itself under appeal to the Supreme Court of Virginia since March 2026. Reports of a gubernatorial intervention in the SCC docket in August 2026 and legislative efforts to extend the review period are externally sourced and unconfirmed by filings. The $2.25B of rate credits reads as an attempt to price consent in advance.

**3. Financing and rate sensitivity.** With ~$110B of debt, ~$95B of remaining approved capex through 2030, and ~$53B more to assume, NEE is the most rate-exposed name in the multi-utility group. Each 100bp on roughly $12-13B of annual net new debt is ~$125-130M pre-tax, or ~$0.05 per share, compounding across the program. The $47.3B interest-rate derivative notional dampens economic exposure while injecting large, unpredictable GAAP swings. Macro claims that the 30-year Treasury reached its highest level since 2007, and specifics on Fed meeting outcomes and dissents, are not supported by the provided sources and have been removed.

**4. FPSC reconsideration.** Unresolved, and it targets the economic foundation of four years of FPL earnings. The 10-K is explicit that the FPSC "does not provide assurance that any regulatory ROE will be achieved."

**5. Florida storm recovery.** FPL collected a ~$1.2B interim storm surcharge in 2025 following ~$1.3B in 2024, with amounts still subject to prudence review and possible refund. Interim recovery is now capped at $5 per 1,000 kWh on residential bills in the first 12 months, with excess deferred; recovery is likely but rate-limited in timing.

**6. OBBBA post-2030 terminus.** The August 15, 2025 IRS guidance eliminated the 5% spend safe harbor for facilities beginning construction on or after September 2, 2025, requiring physical work of a significant nature. Projects beginning construction after 12/31/2025 must also satisfy new prohibited-foreign-entity material-assistance tests. Management's assertion that the current pipeline through 2030 qualifies is the load-bearing claim for the growth target; Congress could also restore credits, which would be an upside surprise.

**7. XPLR.** NEE holds an ~52.5% noncontrolling interest. After-tax charges excluded from adjusted earnings totaled ~$2.47B across 2023-2025, including ~$0.5B of after-tax OTTI in 2025. NEE has declined to support the units with its balance sheet — $114M of a $150M authorization from 2015 remains unused. That restraint is defensible capital allocation; the cumulative loss is real.

The Altman Z-Score screen places NEE in distress territory. That screen is not diagnostic for a capital-intensive regulated utility with a $200B+ asset base and revenue/assets of ~0.13. Leverage here should be judged on ratings, coverage and covenant headroom, where NEE remains solidly investment grade.

### Options Market Signal

The headline near-term reading — 32.4% ATM IV against 22.0% one-year realized, with backwardation and a –56% put/call skew — is an artifact of a one-day expiry and a 0.5% implied move; disregard it. The informative part of the term structure is flat and unremarkable: 20.8% at one month, 20.8% at three months, 21.3% at six months, all at or below realized volatility. Open-interest put/call is 0.82, modestly call-tilted, and ATM IV has collapsed from 48.4% on July 21 to 32.4% following the earnings print. The six-month tenor prices a ±11.6% move, which spans the likely SCC decision window and brackets our bull/bear range fairly.

The takeaway is that the options market is pricing no meaningful stress into a name with a $61B fixed-ratio acquisition pending and a state-commission decision roughly five months out. One partial counter-signal: the three-month put/call volume ratio of 4.27 suggests some accumulation of September protection. Reported short interest of 68,753 shares with 0.0 days to cover is implausibly low for the acquirer in a large stock-for-stock deal — merger arbitrage requires shorting the acquirer — and we treat that figure as unreliable rather than informative.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$105, +23%)

Adjusted EPS growth of 9-10% continues through the FPL rate agreement's contracted steps ($945M in 2026, $705M more in 2027) plus SoBRA additions, and the FPSC reconsideration motion is denied. The Dominion transaction clears FERC, NRC and all three state commissions by mid-2027 with the $2.25B of rate credits proving sufficient to buy consent, and NEE emerges owning regulated rate base attached to the fastest-growing large-load pocket in the country, with Dominion's high-load revenue already up 93% year over year in Q2 2026. Static accretion of ~$4.13 of acquired earnings per share issued, against NEE's own ~$3.97, becomes real accretion once financing and synergies are netted. The multiple re-rates from peer parity back toward a modest premium as the deal risk clears, and 22.5x on ~$4.65 of combined 2027 earnings gives $105. Optionality on Duane Arnold's 2029 restart with a 25-year full-capacity PPA and on congressional restoration of clean-energy credits sits on top.

### Bear Case (~$70, –18%)

Either of two paths gets there. Path one: the Virginia SCC — with an affordability crisis already in progress ($1.9B of proposed fuel-revenue increases, $1,179M of RGGI rider, $566M plus $210M of base increases, an appeal pending at the Supreme Court of Virginia) and active political intervention — imposes conditions NEE either cannot accept or accepts at a cost that eliminates accretion. If the deal breaks on regulatory grounds, NEE pays $4.83B (~$2.32/share), has consumed 18 months of management attention, and returns to a standalone story whose wind and solar credit engine has a 2030 terminus. Path two: the deal closes and the market prices the reality of 34% more shares, ~$53B more debt at 1.9x-plus leverage into a higher-for-longer rate regime, a blended authorized ROE diluted by Virginia Power's 9.80%, $6.2B of Dominion regulatory assets earning no return, and remaining CVOW construction risk with ~$0.1B of contingency against 145 turbines still to install. Layer on an adverse FPSC reconsideration outcome and 16.5x on $4.25 gives $70.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing holders are compensated by a 2.9% dividend and roughly 9% base-case appreciation; prospective buyers have no urgent entry signal at $85.10.

- **Base case: $93** (21.0x 2027 consensus $4.42), +9.3% from $85.10, ~12% total return with the dividend.
- **Bull case: $105** (22.5x ~$4.65 combined 2027 EPS), +23.4%.
- **Bear case: $70** (16.5x $4.25), –17.7%.
- **Timeframe:** 12-18 months, anchored to the Virginia SCC decision (window roughly January 2027, six months from the July 15 filing), the FPL $705M base revenue step effective January 1, 2027, and the second-half 2027 target close.
- **Probability-weighted return:** ~+7% including the dividend at 50/20/30 base/bull/bear weights, approximately fair against a utility cost of equity of 8-9%.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Virginia SCC approval without conditions materially beyond the disclosed $2.25B of rate credits, or an NEE decision to walk away before incurring the reverse fee. Either resolves the dominant binary.
2. FPSC denial of the February 2026 reconsideration motion, confirming the 2026-2029 rate agreement, combined with reaffirmed 2026 guidance at the upper end of $3.92-$4.02 and a 2027 adjusted EPS outlook consistent with 6-8% growth off that base.
3. Price near $75 (17.0x 2027 consensus) with estimates unchanged, which would restore the margin of safety we currently find absent.
4. Post-announcement rating affirmation at Baa1/A–/A– with stable outlooks from all three agencies, which would remove the leverage-verification gap.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Virginia SCC, NCUC or SCPSC conditions that NEE accepts and that consume more than roughly $1.00 per combined share of annual earnings, or evidence NEE is negotiating toward a settlement materially larger than $2.25B.
2. A downgrade of NEE or NEECH to Baa2/BBB, or NEECH commercial paper losing tier-2 status, either of which would raise the cost of a ~$16.8B annual funding requirement.
3. An adverse FPSC ruling on reconsideration that reopens the base revenue steps or the 10.95% authorized ROE.
4. CVOW cost re-estimate above ~$12.5B or a completion date slipping beyond mid-2028, given ~$0.1B of remaining contingency.
5. Two consecutive quarters of adjusted EPS growth below 6% per share, or 2027 consensus falling below $4.20.

**Next reassessment:** the Q3 2026 print (late October 2026) for the first full read on merger financing plans and any synergy disclosure, and the Virginia SCC docket around January 2027.

**On the trajectory.** The overall score has fallen from 7.3 in February to 5.2. The 3.0% price decline since the July 21 report is not the reason. The reasons are specific and fundamental: long-term debt rose $11.1B in six months while first-half capex of ~$19.4B ran at 2.7x operating cash flow; the July 5 tax-credit begin-construction deadline passed, closing the eligibility window on the historical growth engine; estimate revisions have gone flat with the next quarter cut 4.9%; and the merger's disclosed risk allocation — a fixed ratio with no collar, a $4.83B regulatory reverse fee, $2.25B of buyer-funded rate credits, and no synergy guidance — became fully visible only with Dominion's July 31 10-Q. Operating delivery, by contrast, has been fine: 9.8% adjusted EPS growth in the half, four consecutive beats. The score reflects a business whose earnings are holding while its balance sheet, growth architecture and risk profile have all moved in the same direction.

## Conclusion

NextEra remains a high-quality operator running a capital-intensive model into an unfavorable rate regime while simultaneously attempting the largest utility acquisition in the sector's recent history. The operating case is intact: 9.8% adjusted EPS growth, a Florida rate agreement providing contractual visibility on ~73% of earnings through 2029, and four straight consensus beats. The multiple has already de-rated from premium to peer parity, which removes the most obvious source of downside.

What replaces it is a set of binaries an outside investor cannot handicap with confidence: a Virginia commission decision roughly five months away in the middle of an affordability crisis, with NEE having pre-committed $2.25B of customer credits and a $4.83B reverse break fee; an unresolved challenge to the FPSC order; 145 offshore wind turbines still to install against ~$0.1B of contingency; and a funding requirement of roughly $16.8B per year that grows with the acquisition. Roughly +7% probability-weighted total return does not compensate for that distribution.

Hold existing positions for the dividend and the base-case appreciation. New capital should wait for either the Virginia decision or a price near $75.
