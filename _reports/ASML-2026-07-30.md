**Research Date: July 30, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ASML is the sole commercial supplier of extreme-ultraviolet lithography at production scale, and the July 2026 quarter demonstrated the practical consequence: the company beat its own guidance on both revenue (€9,326m) and gross margin (54.0%), raised full-year 2026 guidance for the second time this year to €43–45bn at 54–56% gross margin (from €36–40bn at 51–53%), and announced 30% unit-capacity expansions for both low-NA EUV and DUV immersion for 2027 with a further 30% for each "under investigation" for 2028. Consensus responded with the largest estimate revisions in the mega-cap semiconductor complex: FY2026 EPS up 19.8% in 30 days to €37.90 and FY2027 up 21.0% to €50.92.

The stock went the other way. From a July 15 close of $1,815, the ADR fell to $1,551 by July 29, a three-day decline triggered by a single media report that a Shanghai-based firm has begun producing immersion DUV lithography tools. The Phase 1 price series ends at a July 29 close of $1,550.69; the research brief cites an aggregator July 30 close of $1,651.44 (+6.50%), a partial rebound. The arithmetic of those two moves is the investment case: over the July 15–30 window, price down ~9% against FY2026 estimates up ~20% compressed the forward multiple materially. At $1,651 the ADR trades at approximately 38–40x FY2026 and 28–30x FY2027 consensus EPS depending on the euro cross, against consensus FY2027 EPS growth of 34%. That is a PEG of roughly 0.85 for a monopoly asset with 54% gross margins, ~35% operating margins and a €9.3bn installed-base service annuity growing faster than the systems business.

I am upgrading to BUY from HOLD, reversing a declining score trajectory (7.6 in April to 6.5 in June), with the upgrade driven by the compression in forward multiple rather than by any change in the qualitative thesis. Two risks warrant disciplined position sizing rather than a maximum weight. First, FY2026 guidance is overwhelmingly back-half loaded: the first half grew 17.2% year-over-year, so the second half must grow roughly 50% to reach the midpoint, implying a fourth quarter about 54% above the quarter just reported. That is a shipment-timing risk, not a demand risk, and it can produce a violent drawdown at a 38x multiple. Second, a US bill containing a new country-wide restriction on ASML's DUV immersion sales remains in the legislative process, and management's own risk language contemplates restrictions on the ability to "provide services to certain customers," which would attack the annuity as well as new shipments.

## Company Overview

ASML supplies the hardware, software and services chipmakers use to pattern integrated circuits. It is headquartered in Veldhoven, Netherlands, employs more than 44,500 FTE, is listed on Euronext Amsterdam and NASDAQ, and reports in euros under US GAAP with a separate IFRS statutory report. As a Dutch foreign private issuer it files a Form 20-F annual report (most recently February 25, 2026) and Form 6-K interim reports rather than 10-K/10-Q/8-K, a filing-status distinction with two practical consequences addressed in the Risk Assessment: no Section 16 insider filings exist, and no DEF 14A compensation disclosure exists.

The product mix, using ASML's own reporting categories, shows where the business has shifted (€ millions):

| Product line | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | TTM Jun-26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EXE (High-NA EUV) | — | 465 | 1,157 | 2,072 |
| NXE (Low-NA EUV) | 9,124 | 7,856 | 10,446 | 11,561 |
| ArFi (DUV immersion) | 9,017 | 9,667 | 10,311 | 9,341 |
| KrF | 2,203 | 1,991 | 1,001 | 1,278 |
| ArF dry | 780 | 774 | 427 | 601 |
| Metrology & Inspection | 536 | 646 | 825 | 866 |
| Service & Field Option | 5,620 | 6,494 | 8,193 | 9,346 |
| **Total** | **27,559** | **28,263** | **32,667** | **35,328** |

Three structural facts follow. EUV (NXE plus EXE) reached €13.6bn TTM and has overtaken DUV immersion as the largest system category. High-NA (EXE) is compounding off a small base — €465m to €1,157m to €2,072m — but remains only ~6% of revenue. Service and Field Option reached €9,346m TTM, roughly 26% of revenue, up 66% from FY2023, and it is the fastest-growing line.

The competitive structure is unusual: ASML publishes its output capacity in units (approximately 65 low-NA EUV and 130 DUV immersion systems in 2026) and treats that capacity as the binding constraint. In an industry where suppliers normally compete on price, ASML allocates. That is the operative definition of the moat, and it is why the near-term risk is a manufacturing risk rather than a demand risk.

Three points on financial quality.

**Gross margin inflection is mechanically explainable.** Margin ran 49.65% in FY2022, 51.83% in FY2025, 52.73% TTM, 53.0% in Q1-26, 54.0% in Q2-26, and is guided to 55–57% in Q3. Two mechanisms are visible in the disclosed data: mix shift toward EUV and High-NA (the highest-value systems, growing fastest), and installed-base monetization through field options and upgrades that carry no new-system cost structure. The second is the more durable of the two, and it drove the Q2 beat. I would not treat 55–57% as a floor, because High-NA tools are early in their cost-down curve and can dilute margin during ramp quarters.

**Capacity is expanding while capex declines.** Capex fell from €2,156m in FY2023 to €1,574m in FY2025 to €1,446m TTM, roughly 4.1% of revenue, while ASML commits to 30% more low-NA EUV and 30% more DUV immersion units for 2027. The expansion is coming from cleanroom utilization, supply-chain throughput and faster shipment cadence rather than new plant. Operating leverage on a 30% unit increase with flat-to-down capex is the mechanical source of both the margin expansion and the FCF conversion.

**The cash flow line warrants monitoring.** Net income rose (FY2025 €10,213m to €10,638m TTM) while operating cash flow fell (€13,827m to €11,487m) and FCF margin declined from 37.5% to 28.4%. Balance-sheet net cash fell €5,069m in six months, from €8,931m in December 2025 to €3,862m in June 2026, while total debt declined €671m. FCF conversion against net income is 94% on a TTM basis. The most probable explanation is inventory and work-in-process staged for the second-half shipment ramp, combined with €1.1bn of Q2 buybacks, the FY2025 dividend paid in the first half, and a debt repayment, a pattern that corroborates rather than contradicts the ramp. FY2023 showed the identical signature (FCF margin 15.9%) and fully reversed the following year. I could not obtain the H1 2026 cash flow statement, so slower collections or customer-prepayment timing cannot be excluded as contributors. Balance-sheet resilience is not in question: net cash positive, total debt €3,720m against €10.6bn of TTM net income.

## Growth Analysis

**The guidance shape is the most important derived fact in this analysis.** ASML guides FY2026 net sales of €43–45bn, having previously guided €36–40bn. Reconstructing the halves: H1 2026 actual is €18,093m; TTM to June 2026 is €35,328m, implying H2 2025 of €17,235m; FY2025 of €32,667m therefore implies H1 2025 of €15,432m. So:

- H1 2026 YoY growth: 18,093 / 15,432 − 1 = **+17.2%**
- Implied H2 2026: €24,907m (low) to €26,907m (high), midpoint €25,907m
- Implied H2 2026 YoY growth: **+44.5% to +56.1%, midpoint +50.3%**
- Q3 guided €11.0–12.0bn, so implied Q4 midpoint ≈ €14,407m, roughly **+54% above the €9,326m just reported**

Full-year growth of 32–38% is therefore almost entirely a second-half claim. ASML recognizes revenue on system shipment and acceptance, so a steep fourth quarter is not structurally implausible: the company also has faster-shipment and installed-base-upside levers, and the comparison base (H2 2025 at €17,235m) was soft. But the number is a statement about execution and shipment timing, not about demand already converted to revenue. This is the single highest-leverage item to monitor at the Q3 report, expected mid-October 2026 based on the prior 6-K cadence.

**Estimate revisions are the strongest forward signal in the file.** Consensus EPS (euro-denominated; confirmed by the exact match of the reported €7.15 Q1 and ~€7.58 diluted Q2 to the "actual" column of the surprise history):

| Period | Current | 30d ago | 90d ago | 30d rev. | 90d rev. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current quarter | €10.58 | €8.30 | €8.29 | +27.5% | +27.7% |
| Next quarter | €13.14 | €9.68 | €9.57 | +35.8% | +37.3% |
| FY2026 | €37.90 | €31.63 | €31.31 | +19.8% | +21.0% |
| FY2027 | €50.92 | €42.08 | €40.71 | +21.0% | +25.1% |

Revisions exceeding +20% across every window, accelerating at the quarterly level, combined with implied second-half revenue growth near 50%, constitute the highest-conviction class of forward signal I track. The relevant guardrail is whether the stock is cheap enough for rising estimates to translate into re-rating rather than merely meeting expectations already embedded in the multiple. At 28–30x FY2027 and a PEG below 1.0, the condition is met.

Consensus has fully absorbed the guidance rather than front-running it: an independent bottom-up check using guidance midpoints (€44bn revenue, 55% gross margin, annualized guided quarterly R&D of €1.2bn and SG&A of €0.4bn, and a ~17.8% effective tax rate derived from FY2025 margin relationships on 385m shares) yields FY2026 EPS of approximately €38, within a rounding error of the €37.90 consensus.

**Multi-year runway is visible in capacity commitments rather than in a backlog number.** The 30% expansions for 2027 (implying roughly 84–85 low-NA EUV units and ~169 DUV immersion units) are stated with customer commitments attached, and a further 30% for each is under investigation for 2028. The DUV immersion expansion is proportionally as large as the EUV expansion, which matters because immersion DUV is heavily memory-levered, corroborated by Korea representing 43% of Q2 sales, ASML's largest market.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing multiple is not the relevant metric here. Trailing TTM EPS is €27.55 against FY2026 consensus of €37.90 (+38%) and FY2027 of €50.92 (+34%); the trailing period straddles a soft H2 2025. Working forward:

At $1,651 and a euro cross in the 1.10–1.16 range (the supplied data contains conflicting implied rates, so I present a range rather than false precision), the ADR trades at approximately:

- **38–40x FY2026 consensus EPS of €37.90**
- **28–30x FY2027 consensus EPS of €50.92**
- **PEG of roughly 0.82–0.88** on FY2027 versus consensus 34% EPS growth

The specific reason the market may be mispricing this stock is the two-week arithmetic. On July 15, at a close of $1,815 against pre-raise consensus of €31.63, the forward multiple was approximately 49–50x, consistent with Morningstar's contemporaneous "roughly 50x forward" characterization and its "slightly overvalued" conclusion. Since then, price fell 9.0% and FY2026 consensus rose 19.8%, compressing the forward multiple by roughly 24%. The same computation on FY2027 gives similar compression. Morningstar's own stated fair multiple was 35–40x forward, a range the stock has now entered on FY2026 estimates and moved well below on FY2027.

The catalyst for the price decline was specific and, on the available evidence, misdirected: a report that a Shanghai firm has begun producing immersion DUV tools cost roughly €55bn of market value, with Reuters' own assessment that the threat "looks overdone" because the copycat equipment "is not as technically advanced." The segment under attack is where ASML's exposure has already eroded most: KrF revenue fell from €2,203m in FY2023 to €1,001m in FY2025, ArF dry from €780m to €427m, and China fell to 14% of Q2 sales from 19% in Q1. The report does not, on reported evidence, touch EUV, which is now the largest and fastest-growing category.

Sell-side positioning corroborates without driving the case: 7 strong buy, 33 buy, 3 hold, 1 sell, with one strong-sell removed this month. Mean target $2,140.68, median $2,317.50, high $2,881.42, low $892.01. The dispersion is instructive: the $892 low is a legitimate scenario in which China exposure and the DUV franchise are structurally impaired, and I treat it as the reason to size below maximum rather than as noise.

Why not a higher valuation score than 6.0: 38–40x FY2026 is a full absolute multiple that offers little margin of safety if second-half shipments slip, and the FY2027 case rests on consensus estimates that themselves depend on the 2027 capacity expansion converting as planned. The mispricing I can demonstrate is relative and recent, not structural and deep.

## Competitive Landscape

The defensible claim is narrower than "no one can build a lithography machine": ASML is the sole supplier of leading-edge EUV at volume, with an installed base that generates a growing service and upgrade annuity, and a supply chain it must itself orchestrate to add capacity.

Evidence for improving trajectory in the strategic core: EUV overtook DUV immersion as the largest system category (€13.6bn TTM); High-NA scaled 4.5x in two years; the service annuity reached 26% of revenue and grew 66% versus FY2023; gross margin expanded 218bp from the FY2022 trough of 49.65% with guidance for another 100–300bp in Q3; and the company is rationing supply while investigating a second 30% capacity step for 2028.

Evidence for deteriorating trajectory in the tail: mature-node system revenue (KrF, ArF dry) has more than halved since FY2023, a decline plausibly driven by export controls, competitive substitution, or both, and the ambiguity between those two causes is itself the risk. A credible domestic Chinese immersion DUV supplier attacks precisely that segment. A second-order consideration cuts the other way: Chinese fabs localizing lithography still require deposition, etch and inspection tools, so localization shifts the bottleneck rather than removing it.

Demand-side corroboration is broad rather than single-source. TSMC, one of ASML's largest customers, reported a 68% jump in June sales. Korea at 43% of Q2 sales points to a memory-led leg. The company attributes demand to ongoing AI-related investments driving demand for advanced logic and memory chips, with customers accelerating capacity-expansion plans, though the broader question of AI-capital-spending sustainability is one ASML's own risk enumeration and news coverage flag as an open issue.

## Risk Assessment

**Second-half execution concentration.** The FY2026 number requires ~50% H2 YoY growth and a Q4 roughly 54% above Q2. Slippage of a handful of high-value systems across the year-end boundary moves the full year materially. Mitigant: slippage shifts revenue between periods rather than destroying it, so the FY2027 valuation anchor is more robust than the FY2026 one. At 38x forward, however, a Q3 guidance walk-back would be violent.

**Pending US DUV immersion restriction.** Reuters reported in April 2026 that a US bill, while scaled back, still contains a new country-wide restriction on ASML's DUV immersion systems; the stock fell 6% on the proposal. As of mid-July the bill "still needs to work its way through the U.S. legislative process," and I could not verify its status since. ArFi is €9,341m TTM. Management's risk language explicitly contemplates restrictions on the ability to "provide services to certain customers," which would attack the annuity. This is a genuine binary with no date attached.

**An unexplained assumption inside guidance.** China fell from 19% of Q1 sales to 14% of Q2, yet ASML continues to expect China at roughly 20% of full-year sales. Reconciling those requires material H2 re-acceleration in China that has not been separately justified. The CFO characterized China as "moving in sync with the overall behavior we see globally," which does not obviously support re-acceleration.

**Effective customer concentration.** Korea at 43% of Q2 sales, in a market with two dominant memory makers, implies significant effective concentration. This is a geographic disclosure, not a customer disclosure; a shifting geographic mix does not by itself demonstrate diversification, and without the 20-F customer-concentration table I cannot say whether top-customer reliance improved or worsened. It may have concentrated further.

**Verification gaps that a reader should weigh.** As a foreign private issuer filing 20-F and 6-K, ASML's annual report text was not accessible this session. Consequently: no audited FY2025 statements were read; the risk-factor set and year-over-year additions are unverified; customer-concentration percentages, the definition and value of backlog, and the H1 2026 cash flow statement are unavailable; and no compensation, board or related-party data exists in accessible form. No Altman Z or Piotroski F score was computed — these are unavailable rather than weak, and a net-cash balance sheet with ~30% net margins is inconsistent with distress. Separately, the Q2 press-release summary table contains no net-bookings line and no backlog figure, with order intake discussed only qualitatively ("extremely strong," "customer commitments"). I am flagging an observed absence in one document rather than an established reduction in disclosure policy, but it means investors are taking order momentum on management's characterization precisely where a disclosed number would be most useful.

**Long-term targets are stale by management's own account.** The current long-term model dates to the 2024 Investor Day, predating the AI-capex acceleration, and will not be refreshed until the Capital Markets Day on June 10, 2027, roughly eleven months away.

**FX.** For a USD-based investor the ADR embeds euro exposure on top of operating exposure. The conflicting implied euro crosses in the supplied data are themselves a reminder that translation materially affects the reported multiple.

### Options Market Signal

The headline near-term reading looks alarming and is largely an artifact of tenor. Front-expiry ATM IV of 73.0% with a 12.1% put skew sits on a one-day expiry; the term structure decays to 58.5% at one month (skew −2.6%) and 54.1% at six months (skew −2.7%), against 45.5% one-year historical volatility. Backwardation is present but shallow beyond the front week.

More informative is the change: ATM IV fell from 85.3% to 73.0% while price rose 5.2%, and the put/call open-interest ratio collapsed from 2.82 to 1.15. The defensive positioning built around the China-localization report is unwinding, which is consistent with the market re-underwriting that report as a mature-node issue rather than an EUV issue. Short interest is negligible at 0.3% of float and 0.6 days to cover: there is essentially no bearish positioning to squeeze, and no crowded bear consensus.

Net read: the options market is pricing elevated event uncertainty into the October Q3 print (six-month implied move ±25.1%) rather than an imminent directional break. Rich implied volatility at three to six months argues for scaling entry rather than a single tranche, and makes selling upside calls against a long a reasonable way to fund the position for holders comfortable with capping the bull case.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Second-half shipments convert as guided, delivering FY2026 revenue of €44–45bn at 55–56% gross margin and EPS near or above the €37.90 consensus. The 2027 capacity expansion converts into FY2027 revenue with margins holding above 55%, and the 2028 expansion moves from "under investigation" to committed. FY2027 EPS reaches €55–57 against consensus €50.92. The installed-base annuity continues compounding toward 30% of revenue, structurally raising the earnings quality and the deserved multiple. The Chinese domestic DUV entrant proves confined to mature nodes, and the US bill either fails or is narrowed. At 36–38x FY2027 EPS of €56, the ADR reaches $2,300–2,450.

**Bear case.** A handful of high-value systems slip across the year-end boundary, FY2026 lands at or below the €43bn low end, and the Q3 report reveals that the second-half ramp is a timing story rather than a demand story. The US bill passes with a country-wide DUV immersion restriction covering services, removing revenue and a portion of the annuity. China fails to re-accelerate to the assumed ~20% full-year share, and the domestic immersion tool proves capable enough to take mature-node share on a three-year view. FY2027 estimates reset toward the pre-raise €42 level and the multiple compresses to 25x, giving roughly $1,180, a 29% decline. The analyst low of $892 represents the tail in which the AI-capex cycle also unwinds simultaneously.

**Which way the evidence leans.** The demand side is corroborated across multiple independent channels; the supply side is under ASML's own control and has historically delivered; the estimate revision magnitude (+20% or more across every window) is the strongest predictive signal available; and the recent drawdown was catalyzed by a report attacking the segment where ASML's exposure has already halved. The bear case is real but concentrated in two items, Q4 shipment timing and a pending bill, the first of which shifts value between periods rather than destroying it.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $2,000** (+21% from $1,651). Derivation: 35x FY2027 consensus EPS of €50.92 = €1,782, converted at a euro cross of ~1.13 = $2,014. Thirty-five times is at the low end of ASML's post-2023 forward range and below the 38–40x it currently commands on FY2026, reflecting a discount for the unresolved DUV legislation. Add the ~0.57% dividend yield (interim €1.88 payable August 5) for a total return near 22%.

**Bull case target: $2,450** (+48%). 38x an above-consensus FY2027 EPS of €57, consistent with the pattern of upward revisions and with committed 2028 capacity. Sits below the analyst median of $2,317 on a same-window basis only because of the euro assumption; below the analyst high of $2,881.

**Bear case target: $1,180** (−29%). 25x an FY2027 EPS reset to €42, approximately the pre-raise consensus, on DUV restriction plus H2 slippage.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored on three dated catalysts: the Q3 2026 report in mid-October 2026 (which resolves the H2 ramp question and reveals whether the implied ~€14.4bn Q4 is intact), the FY2025 20-F comparison at the FY2026 filing in February 2027, and the Capital Markets Day on June 10, 2027, at which the stale 2024 long-term model is refreshed.

**Position sizing.** Below a full weight. Three-to-six-month implied volatility of 54–55% versus 45.5% realized argues for scaling entry across two or three tranches rather than a single purchase, with the October print as a natural second tranche point. The $892 analyst low is not an outlier to dismiss; it is a coherent scenario that justifies capping exposure.

**Thesis invalidation triggers** (any one of which would move me to HOLD or AVOID):
1. **Q3 2026 revenue below €11.0bn, or FY2026 guidance cut below €43bn.** Either indicates the second-half ramp is a timing fiction and breaks the €37.90 FY2026 consensus.
2. **Enactment of US legislation imposing a country-wide DUV immersion restriction that extends to service and parts provision.** ArFi is €9.3bn TTM and the annuity is €9.3bn TTM; a services-inclusive restriction attacks both.
3. **FY2027 consensus EPS revised below €45** (a 12% reduction from €50.92), which would push the forward multiple above 33x and remove the PEG support that underpins the upgrade.
4. **Gross margin guidance for Q4 2026 below 54%**, which would indicate the mix and installed-base drivers of margin expansion are not durable and that High-NA ramp dilution is larger than assumed.

**Monitoring items that are not yet triggers**: whether the FY2026 20-F discloses a backlog or net-bookings figure and whether customer concentration widened or narrowed; whether China recovers toward the assumed ~20% full-year share in H2; and whether operating cash flow reverses the TTM decline as the H2 shipments convert inventory to revenue (the FY2023-to-FY2024 precedent suggests it should).

## Conclusion

ASML is the clearest bottleneck asset in semiconductor manufacturing, and it just raised full-year guidance for the second time in 2026 while announcing 30% capacity expansions in both leading-edge product families with capex running at 4.1% of revenue. Consensus EPS rose roughly 20% in thirty days. Over the same period the ADR fell 9%, driven by a media report about a mature-node competitor that Reuters assessed as overdone. The net effect compressed the forward multiple by roughly 24%, taking the stock from approximately 50x forward — where a credible independent valuation called it slightly overvalued — to 38–40x FY2026 and 28–30x FY2027, with a PEG below 0.9.

That is a genuine improvement in risk/reward on unchanged-to-improving fundamentals, and it reverses the declining score trajectory of the past three quarters, where the HOLD calls were driven principally by a valuation dimension that fell from 5.5 to 3.5 as the price outran the estimates. The estimates have now outrun the price.

The upgrade to BUY is qualified rather than unconditional. FY2026 guidance requires the second half to grow roughly 50% after a first half that grew 17%, and a fourth quarter 54% above the quarter just reported, an execution claim that the October print will settle. A US bill restricting DUV immersion sales country-wide remains live with no timeline, and management's own language admits it could reach services. Size accordingly: a partial position, scaled across the October catalyst, with the four invalidation triggers checked against each disclosure.

**Recommendation: BUY.** Base case $2,000 (+21%), bull $2,450 (+48%), bear $1,180 (−29%), 12-month horizon.
