**Research Date: June 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

AMD has completed the transition from an Intel-share-gain story into a credible second source in AI accelerators, with a real financial inflection. FY25 revenue grew 34% to $34.6B, Q1'26 revenue accelerated to +38% ($10.3B) with Data Center up 57%, gross margin reached 53%, and consensus FY27 EPS has been revised up 21.8% over the past 90 days to $13.10. The Information Technology sector is the one area where company-level selection has historically delivered alpha, and AMD sits inside the most heavily-confirmed structural demand theme in the market — hyperscaler AI capex, corroborated across power, networking, memory, and chip layers. On the merits of the operating business, this is one of the highest-quality growth profiles available.

The equity carries two features that separate the business from the stock. First, AMD sold up to 320M shares (roughly 20% of the 1.63B share base) to OpenAI and Meta at $0.01 via warrants tied to GPU-deployment and stock-price milestones. This anchors two customers for the MI450 ramp but transfers a large slice of per-share upside to them precisely as the milestones (and the stock-price targets that trigger vesting) are hit. Any per-share valuation must be run on a diluted base materially above the headline count. Second, the stock trades at ~73x consensus FY26 EPS and ~41x FY27, with the average analyst price target ($487.90) sitting below the current $537 price. The GPU revenue that justifies these multiples is still largely a forecast carrying below-average gross margins, not realized earnings.

We maintain HOLD with a growth bias. The fundamental momentum — accelerating Data Center growth, expanding margins, +21.8% FY27 estimate revisions, three of four recent earnings beats — argues for owning the shares through the MI450 ramp. The valuation and the dilution argue against paying up at $537 for a stock already discounting the bull case. We would add aggressively on a pullback toward the low-$400s, where the forward multiple compresses toward the FY27 growth rate and the risk/reward becomes asymmetric.

## Company Overview

AMD is a fabless semiconductor designer operating three reportable segments (restructured from four in Q1 2025):

- **Data Center (~48% of FY25 revenue, $16.6B):** EPYC server CPUs, Instinct AI GPU accelerators (MI350 shipping, MI450 ramping), Pensando DPUs/AI NICs, and adaptive SoCs/FPGAs. This segment is the entire investment thesis.
- **Client and Gaming (~42%, $14.6B):** Ryzen desktop/mobile CPUs (Client $10.6B) and Radeon GPUs plus semi-custom console SoCs for Sony and Microsoft (Gaming $3.9B).
- **Embedded (~10%, $3.5B):** Largely the Xilinx FPGA/adaptive SoC franchise; the only segment that declined in FY25 (-3%).

AMD relies on TSMC for all ≤7nm CPU/GPU wafers and GlobalFoundries for larger nodes; assembly/test is concentrated in China, Malaysia, and Taiwan, partly via two joint ventures with Tongfu Microelectronics. The competitive moat rests on x86 CPU leadership over Intel, an expanding AI GPU roadmap on an annual cadence, and the ROCm software stack, which remains well behind Nvidia's CUDA.

## Financial Analysis

The financial trajectory shows accelerating growth with simultaneous margin expansion:

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q1'26 (YoY) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $22.7B | $25.8B | $34.6B (+34%) | $10.3B (+38%) |
| Gross margin (GAAP) | 46% | 49% | 50% | 53% |
| Operating income | $0.40B | $1.90B | $3.69B | $1.48B |
| Net income | $0.85B | $1.64B | $4.34B | $1.38B |
| Operating cash flow | — | $3.04B | $6.49B | $2.96B (vs $0.94B) |

**Data Center is the engine.** It grew 32% in FY25 and accelerated to +57% in Q1'26 ($5.78B) on EPYC plus Instinct MI350. Operating cash flow more than doubled to $6.49B in FY25 and reached $2.96B in Q1'26 alone, signaling high-quality earnings conversion.

**The balance sheet is strong.** $12.3B cash and short-term investments at Q1'26 against just $3.25B total debt, stockholders' equity of $64.5B, current ratio ~2.7x, and net debt negative. There are no financial-distress indicators.

Three quality caveats temper the headline numbers:

1. **Intangibles dominate the balance sheet.** Goodwill ($25.3B) plus acquisition intangibles ($16.2B) total ~$41.5B, roughly 52% of total assets, almost entirely from Xilinx/Pensando. GAAP earnings absorb ~$2.2–2.3B/year of intangible amortization running through 2031+. Retained earnings are only $8.1B.

2. **FY25 net income is flattered by a one-time tax benefit.** An $853M IRS dual-consolidated-loss relief produced a -2.5% effective rate in FY25. Q1'26 reverted to a normal ~14.8% rate. Normalized FY25 earnings power is below the $4.34B headline.

3. **GPU revenue is margin-dilutive.** Data Center operating income was nearly flat in FY25 ($3.48B → $3.60B) despite +32% revenue, as opex and the $440M China export charge weighed. The Q1'26 margin lift to 53% was mix-driven; management and analysts repeatedly flag the MI450's below-average gross margin. Whether scale fixes this is the central margin question of the next two years.

The earnings-surprise record supports confidence in forward estimates: three beats in the last four quarters (Q4'25 +14.8%, Q1'26 +4.8%), with the lone miss a small -3.2% in mid-2025.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case has the durability the world model assigns to the AI-infrastructure theme, where demand is confirmed across multiple layers rather than a single-company story. AMD benefits on two fronts.

**Estimate revisions are the strongest forward signal here.** Consensus has been revised up across every horizon: current quarter +13.7% over 90 days, current year +11.3% to $7.37, and next year +21.8% to $13.10. A +21.8% next-year revision sits squarely in the range that historically precedes continued outperformance, and it pairs with revenue growth above 38%. The caveat the playbook imposes is that this signal is strongest when the stock is cheap relative to the rising estimates. At ~41x FY27 EPS, AMD is not in the cheap-inflection bucket where rising estimates alone justify a BUY; it is in the premium-multiple bucket where beats are expected and misses are punished asymmetrically.

**The demand commitments are unusually concrete.** OpenAI (Oct 2025) and Meta (Feb 2026) each agreed to up to 6GW of AMD GPU deployments, with the first gigawatts on MI450-based systems. Citi frames roughly $15B revenue per deployed GW and models AI revenue ramping to ~$33B (2027) and ~$50.8B (2028). Management said MI450 customer forecasts already exceed internal expectations. Purchase commitments doubled quarter-over-quarter to $25.7B, with $18.3B due in the remainder of FY26, indicating AMD is locking TSMC capacity and memory.

**Client is contributing more than expected.** FY25 Client revenue grew 51% on a 15% unit increase and a 31% ASP increase, evidence of genuine pricing power against Intel, with Q1'26 Client up 26%.

The growth thesis has high durability because it is confirmed by multiple independent signals — two named hyperscaler commitments, doubling purchase commitments, broad estimate revisions, and the corroborating infrastructure-layer demand. The qualifier is that the AI GPU revenue ramp is still largely forecast, and the $25.7B in commitments converts to excess-inventory risk if demand softens.

## Valuation Assessment

AMD trades at ~179x trailing GAAP EPS, which is meaningless given the one-time tax benefit and intangible amortization. The relevant metrics are forward: ~73x consensus FY26 EPS of $7.37 and ~41x FY27 EPS of $13.10. The FY27 PEG, against a ~22% next-year growth trajectory, is roughly 1.9 — above the 1.5 threshold that would mark a clean inflection bargain.

Peer context cuts both ways. The semiconductor peer median P/E is 50.8 (TSM 36.5, MU 46.8, TXN 50.8, MRVL 97.0). AMD's ~73x forward sits above the TSM/MU/TXN cluster and below MRVL. Within a sector re-rating on AI demand, an elevated multiple is not by itself anomalous, but AMD's forward multiple already embeds the GPU ramp at margins management concedes are below average, leaving little cushion.

Two features specifically prevent treating the analyst target as a floor. First, the average price target of $487.90 sits below the current $537 price, and the median ($490) is also below; the consensus does not yet validate the current price even after the run. This is the opposite of the lagging-target dynamic that applies to cheap inflection stocks. Second, the warrant overhang means per-share value capture is capped: up to 320M shares (~20% dilution) vest to OpenAI and Meta as the GPU milestones and stock-price targets are hit, so the diluted share count that matters for intrinsic value is meaningfully above 1.63B. Models that ignore this overstate per-share value.

We assign a 4.5 valuation score. The business quality is high and the sector re-rating is real, but the multiple discounts flawless execution at sub-target margins, the consensus target is below spot, and the dilution structure mechanically reduces what shareholders capture. The wide target range — low $225, high $665 — reflects how much of the value is contingent on the GPU ramp converting to realized, scaled-margin earnings.

## Competitive Landscape

AMD's competitive trajectory is improving on the CPU side and contested-but-advancing on the GPU side.

In server CPUs, EPYC continues to take share from Intel, supported by the AI inference "CPU renaissance" and a server-CPU TAM Citi pegs near $137B by 2030. The Client pricing power confirmed in the growth section underpins this trajectory.

In AI GPUs, AMD is the clear #2 and now has two anchor hyperscaler commitments validating it as a credible Nvidia alternative. The swing factor is software: ROCm remains far behind CUDA, and that gap determines whether AMD sustains GPU share once the initial second-sourcing demand is satisfied. The 10-K flags the September 2025 Nvidia–Intel partnership as a new competitive threat, alongside growing custom ASIC designs from Google/Broadcom and Arm-based alternatives.

The ZT Systems transaction — acquiring rack-scale design IP and talent for ~$4.4B, then reselling manufacturing to Sanmina for ~$2.4B plus earn-out while retaining the design team — is a capital-light way to acquire systems capability (the Helios rack-scale platform). It improves AMD's ability to compete at the rack level where Nvidia's integrated systems have an edge.

Competitive position score: 7.0 — improving trajectory in CPUs and a strengthening but software-constrained GPU position.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are specific and material, justifying a below-average risk score:

1. **OpenAI + Meta warrant dilution (the defining risk).** The warrants cap per-share upside at the moment success materializes. None had vested as of Q1'26, and AMD accounts for them as liabilities until equity classification, creating mark-to-market volatility.

2. **China export controls (quantified, ongoing).** ~$440M net inventory charges in FY25 on MI308; a floated 15% revenue cut to the U.S. government on licensed China sales; MI325 licenses now requiring U.S. inspection plus a 25% import tariff. China revenue is structurally impaired and uncertain. International sales were 74% of Q1'26 revenue, up from 66%.

3. **Margin risk from the memory shortage.** The 10-Q flags an industry-wide memory shortage with rising prices, a direct gross-margin headwind for GPU/accelerator systems, compounding the MI450's already below-average margin profile.

4. **Off-balance-sheet customer credit exposure.** $4.1B of lease guarantees on commercial-partner data-center obligations (terms up to 15 years) plus $4.4B of leases not yet commenced. AMD is increasingly underwriting customers' infrastructure, a credit-risk shift for a chip designer.

5. **Concentration and demand-stall risk.** The $25.7B purchase commitments are a concentrated bet; if AI demand softens, non-cancellable prepays become excess inventory. Customer concentration is amplified by the OpenAI/Meta mega-deals.

6. **Competitive displacement.** The Nvidia–Intel partnership and the persistent CUDA-versus-ROCm gap remain structural threats.

### Options Market Signal

Put/call skew is balanced (-1.1%), the put/call volume ratio is a low 0.54, and the term structure is in contango (69.1% near vs 71.5% long), a normal configuration rather than a fear signal. IV compressed sharply from 83.5% to 69.1% since early June, with the P/C OI ratio rising from 0.47 to 0.67. Unusual activity is concentrated in near-dated upside calls ($550, $552, $555, $558), consistent with bullish positioning into the next leg rather than hedging. Short interest is negligible at 2.7% of float, 1.1 days to cover. The options market is not flagging a bearish divergence against the fundamentals; it confirms a constructive-but-not-euphoric near-term stance. The 6-month skew turning more negative (-4.5%) is the one note of caution further out.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($665, +24%)
MI450 ramps faster than the Street models, OpenAI and Meta deployments convert to ~$15B/GW of realized revenue, AI revenue reaches ~$33B in 2027 and ~$50.8B in 2028, and scale lifts GPU gross margins toward corporate average. EPYC continues taking Intel share. Even after warrant dilution, FY27 EPS clears $13–14 and the multiple holds at a sector-premium ~45–50x, supporting a $665 target. This requires flawless execution on a still-forecast revenue ramp.

### Bear Case ($225, -58%)
GPU revenue underwhelms or its below-average margins persist, the memory shortage compresses gross margin, China revenue is further impaired, and AI capex decelerates, leaving the $25.7B in commitments as excess inventory. Warrant dilution lands while earnings disappoint. A de-rating to ~30x a reduced FY27 EPS base produces a sub-$250 outcome, consistent with the $225 analyst low.

The base case sits between these: continued strong execution but at the margins and dilution profile management has signaled, leaving per-share earnings power below the bull's extrapolation.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth bias). Reassessment at the next two earnings reports (late July Q2'26 and Q3'26), with the MI450 ramp the decisive variable.**

This is a growth hold rather than a buy at $537 for concrete, falsifiable reasons. The validation concern asks what specifically prevents a BUY at a 6.3 composite. Three items: (1) the average analyst target of $487.90 sits ~9% *below* the current $537 price and the median ($490) confirms it — consensus does not validate the entry, the opposite of the lagging-target dynamic that supports buying cheap inflection names; (2) up to 320M warrant shares (~20% dilution) vest to OpenAI and Meta as milestones hit, mechanically capping per-share value capture exactly as the thesis succeeds, so the diluted base for any per-share target is materially above 1.63B; (3) at ~41x FY27 EPS with a ~1.9 PEG and management-acknowledged below-average MI450 margins, the multiple already prices the bull case, leaving asymmetric downside on any GPU-ramp or margin disappointment. These are specific and checkable.

**Upgrade to BUY triggers:**
- Price pulls back toward the low-$400s, compressing the FY27 multiple toward ~30x and the PEG toward ~1.4, where rising estimates create genuine asymmetry.
- Two consecutive quarters of Data Center gross margin expanding (not just mix-driven blended margin), evidencing that MI450 scale fixes the dilutive-margin problem.
- MI450 deployment revenue converts to realized, recognized numbers that beat the $15B/GW framework, de-risking the forecast.

**Downgrade to AVOID triggers:**
- Data Center revenue growth decelerates below ~25% YoY, signaling the GPU ramp is stalling.
- A guidance cut or evidence the $25.7B purchase commitments are converting to excess inventory (rising inventory days, write-downs).
- Confirmation of broad AI-capex deceleration from hyperscaler capex guides, which would reprice the entire demand thesis.

**Reassessment timeline:** Late-July Q2'26 earnings, which will test MI450 traction and the margin trajectory directly.

## Conclusion

AMD is a high-quality business executing a genuine AI-driven inflection — accelerating Data Center growth, expanding margins, a strong balance sheet, and rising FY27 estimate revisions, all inside the best-confirmed structural demand theme and the one sector where company selection has historically generated alpha. We are constructive on the trajectory and would not sell shares here.

It is a HOLD rather than a BUY at $537 because the stock already discounts the bull case at ~41x FY27 EPS on a ~1.9 PEG, the average analyst target sits below the current price, the MI450 ramp that justifies the multiple carries below-average margins and remains largely forecast, and the OpenAI/Meta warrant structure mechanically caps per-share value capture with ~20% potential dilution. The risk/reward at the current price is roughly symmetric-to-unfavorable and becomes clearly favorable in the low-$400s. Maintain the position, monitor the MI450 margin and revenue conversion at the next two prints, and add on a pullback rather than chase the high.
