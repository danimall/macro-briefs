**Research Date: June 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Worthington Steel is a North American producer-independent metals processor that has just changed shape. On June 2, 2026 the company completed its acquisition of Kloeckner & Co SE, a German-listed service center with roughly €6.4B in FY2025 sales, securing ~62% of the shares and launching a delisting tender at €11.00/share for the balance. The deal more than doubles WS's revenue base, from a ~$3.1B standalone steel processor to a ~$9B+ transatlantic platform, and was funded with a ~€1.9B bridge facility. Valuing WS today flows from this single fact, and the central problem for an investor is that the post-deal capital structure, synergy realization, and combined earnings power are not yet disclosed in primary filings.

The standalone business is financially sound but cyclically soft. FY2025 revenue fell ~10% to ~$3.09B on lower average selling prices, net income dropped ~29% to ~$110.7M, and adjusted EPS declined from $3.42 to $2.30. Estimates are broadly falling (current-year consensus revised down ~13% over 90 days), and Q3 FY2026 (reported March 26, 2026) missed by 39%. Against that, the pre-deal balance sheet carried an Altman Z-score near 3.69, the stock trades at a non-demanding ~17x forward / ~17x trailing earnings, and two directors made open-market purchases in January 2026 at $39.11 and $40.15, essentially today's price.

We rate WS a HOLD. The stock has rallied ~68% off its 52-week low of $24.23 and sits at $40.73, in the upper-middle of its $24.2–$49.2 range, so the cyclical-pricing and deal-overhang concerns are already partly reflected. The bearish elements (negative revisions, the miss, leverage opacity) are real but balanced against a coherent, EPS-accretion-targeted deal, a healthy standalone balance sheet, and constructive insider buying. The decisive missing input is pro-forma leverage and one quarter of combined results. Until those arrive, the evidence does not support either an actionable buy or a short; it supports waiting.

## Company Overview

Worthington Steel buys flat-rolled carbon steel from mills and adds value through processing; it does not make steel. Core capabilities per the 10-K Business section are carbon flat-rolled processing (pickling, galvanizing, cold reduction, configured blanking, slitting, lightweighting), electrical steel laminations for motors/transformers/EVs and grid infrastructure, and Tailor Welded Solutions (laser-welded blanks for automotive lightweighting). The company also holds joint-venture interests, notably the TWB tailor-welded JV and Serviacero in Mexico, that contribute meaningful equity earnings.

WS runs ~37 facilities across 7 states and 10 countries with ~6,000 employees, headquartered in Columbus, Ohio. It spun off from Worthington Industries (now Worthington Enterprises) on December 1, 2023, giving it a short standalone public history. CEO is Geoffrey Gilmore; John Blystone is Executive Chairman. End-market exposure is weighted heavily to automotive, its single largest market, followed by construction, agriculture/heavy equipment, and energy/electrical infrastructure. That automotive concentration is the dominant swing factor for the core business.

The acquisition of Kloeckner reorders this picture. Kloeckner is one of the largest producer-independent processors globally with ~110 locations and 6,000+ employees across North America and the DACH region, pivoting toward higher-value services and CO2-reduced ("Nexigen") products. Combined, WS becomes a transatlantic metals-processing platform with materially greater scale, but also imports European industrial-demand softness, higher energy costs, and substantial euro-denominated exposure.

## Financial Analysis

WS uses a fiscal year ending May 31; the most recent annual data is FY2025 (ended May 31, 2025).

- **Revenue**: declined from ~$3.43B (FY2024) to ~$3.09B (FY2025), roughly -10%. The decline was driven primarily by lower average selling prices (steel price deflation) rather than collapsing volume, alongside softer automotive/industrial demand.
- **Net income**: fell from ~$154.7M to ~$110.7M (~-29%). The proxy pay-vs-performance table corroborates net income of ~$170M (FY24) down to ~$119M (FY25). Margins compressed as the spread (selling price minus replacement cost of steel) normalized from elevated post-COVID levels, and inventory holding losses appeared in a declining-price environment.
- **Balance sheet (pre-acquisition)**: healthy — Altman Z-score ~3.69 (safe zone), modest leverage, solid current ratio. This is what gave the company the borrowing capacity to fund the Kloeckner deal.
- **Cash flow and capital allocation**: a $0.16/quarter dividend (declared March 2026, payable June 2026, ~1.6% yield) and continued investment in electrical-steel capacity. FCF generation has historically been solid for a service center, though working capital swings materially with steel prices.

The earnings trajectory matters for how to read the deal. The recent surprise record shows one beat and three misses across the last four quarters, culminating in the Q3 miss ($0.27 actual vs $0.44 estimate). WS is layering a large, complex cross-border integration onto a core business that is in a cyclical down-leg, which raises execution risk precisely when fundamentals are weakest.

The pre-deal Z-score will not survive the transaction. A ~€1.9B bridge financing to acquire a ~€6.4B-revenue distribution business means a step-change in net leverage and a multi-year deleveraging story. Management has cited a pro-forma net leverage ratio and deleveraging goals, but the precise figures are not yet in primary filings. This is the single most important unresolved input for the financial-health assessment, and it is the reason we cannot score this dimension above the midpoint with confidence.

## Growth Analysis

On a pre-deal basis, growth was negative, driven by steel-price deflation and soft auto/industrial demand. Estimate revisions confirm continued near-term pressure: current-quarter consensus was cut 14.1% over 30 days, current-year ~12.8% over 90 days, and next-year ~11.7%. There is no organic inflection signal here; the revision trend is uniformly negative.

The growth case is now almost entirely inorganic and structural. The Kloeckner combination broadens the product portfolio, end-market mix, and geographic footprint. Management has stated synergy and EPS-accretion targets, and continues to cite automotive market-share gains and an AI/operational-efficiency transformation as offsets to soft pricing. The earlier Sitem Group acquisition (closed ~June 2025) added European electrical-steel lamination capability and was a logical precursor to the larger push.

The durability of this growth is uncertain. Kloeckner is a distribution/service-center business with thinner margins than WS's value-added processing, and it carries European demand softness and elevated energy costs. The accretion math depends on synergy realization, the financing cost of the bridge, and the FX translation of a now-substantial euro revenue base. Until one quarter of combined results is reported, "growth" here is a balance-sheet event with a revenue multiplier attached, not a demonstrated earnings inflection. We score Growth Prospects at the midpoint: real scale expansion, but no evidence yet that it converts to per-share earnings growth, and a soft organic backdrop in the meantime.

## Valuation Assessment

WS trades at $40.73, ~17x forward and ~17x trailing earnings on $2.19 trailing EPS, with a market cap of ~$2.06B. Current-year consensus EPS is $2.15 and next-year $2.67. The dividend yield is ~1.6%.

Peer comparison is messy because the listed steel comps are largely loss-making or distorted: AMR (-63x), CLF (-6x), SXC (-12x), with HCC at 36x and a peer-median P/E of 36.0. WS's ~17x trailing therefore screens cheap against that median, but the median is inflated by depressed/negative denominators across the group. The more useful read is that WS is not expensive in absolute terms for a cyclical processor, and the multiple is not the source of risk.

The cycle-adjusted lens cuts against a "cheap" call here rather than for it. The trailing period does not represent a trough that is mechanically reverting upward — estimates are being revised down, not up, so the forward multiple is being supported by falling estimates, and the Q3 miss argues the near-term earnings power is still deteriorating. A low multiple on falling estimates with an unquantified leverage spike pending is a setup where cheapness is not a buy signal. We are explicit that "low P/E" alone does not earn a high valuation score; it requires a specific reason the market is wrong, and here the market is reasonably discounting deal opacity and cyclical softness.

The most useful valuation anchor is the insider buying. Two directors bought in the open market in January 2026 — Kelly ~$274K and Bowsher ~$100K — at essentially the current price. That informed buying at $40 is a meaningful floor signal and a reason not to treat current levels as a sell. It is not, however, sufficient to underwrite upside without knowing pro-forma leverage and combined earnings. We score Valuation at 5.5: not stretched, not a value trap, but lacking the asymmetric setup that would justify a higher mark given the missing combined-entity disclosure.

The single available analyst price target is $38.00 (low/high/mean), below the current $40.73; the sell-side, on a one-analyst sample, sees no upside at spot.

## Competitive Landscape

WS competes in the fragmented steel service center space against Steel Technologies, Reliance/Metals USA, Ryerson, and the processing arms of mills (Cleveland-Cliffs, Nucor's downstream). Its differentiation is technical, value-added processing — electrical laminations, tailor-welded blanks, lightweighting — rather than commodity slitting. This is a genuine moat relative to pure commodity processors, and the electrical-steel lamination franchise is positioned for grid/EV/transformer demand, which connects to the broader power-infrastructure buildout.

The Kloeckner combination is strategically coherent. It creates one of the larger producer-independent processors globally with a strong North American and DACH footprint and complementary higher-value-services and CO2-reduced product lines. Scale improves purchasing leverage with mills, broadens customer reach, and diversifies end-markets away from pure North American auto. The trajectory is improving on a competitive-positioning basis, which is why we lifted this dimension to 6.0, consistent with the prior report's upgrade from 5.0.

The offset is that scale is bought, not earned, and it imports European demand and energy headwinds plus integration risk across borders and cultures. The competitive position is improving in principle; whether it improves in realized returns depends on integration execution that has not yet been demonstrated.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Integration and leverage risk (dominant, new).** Cross-border integration, FX translation of a large euro revenue base, and the squeeze-out dynamic (WS held ~62% at completion, pursuing 100% via the delisting offer) all compound. Pro-forma net leverage is not yet quantified in primary filings — this is the key unresolved risk.
2. **Automotive concentration.** Auto is the largest end market; North American production downturns, EV-adoption pace, or OEM destocking hit WS disproportionately.
3. **Steel price / spread volatility.** As a processor, WS earns the spread between selling price and steel cost. Falling prices create inventory holding losses and margin compression, as seen in FY2025. This is structural and recurring.
4. **Tariff / trade policy (Section 232).** Steel tariffs (50% referenced) cut both ways and now matter more given US/EU cross-border exposure post-Kloeckner.
5. **Goodwill/intangible impairment** tied to Sitem, electrical-steel expansions, and now Kloeckner, in a soft-pricing environment with weak European demand.
6. **Short standalone history.** Spun off December 2023; limited track record as an independent capital allocator.

The risk score of 4.5 reflects genuine, partly-binary uncertainty (leverage step-change, FX, integration) that is not fully reflected in the current valuation because the magnitude is undisclosed — distinct from sector-cyclicality truisms that are already permanently priced.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 70.3% against a 1-year HV of 49.8% (rich by ~20.6pp), the term structure is in backwardation (70.3% 1m vs 52.4% 6m), and the implied move on the nearest expiry is ±15.1%. ATM IV rose from 59.5% to 70.3% over the past month while the stock fell ~3.9%. The skew is call-heavy (-21.9% 1m, P/C OI 0.45), which reads as two-sided event positioning around the deal/integration catalysts rather than one-sided downside hedging. Elevated near-term IV argues against chasing the stock here and favors waiting for the volatility-driving disclosures (combined results, leverage) to resolve.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

WS becomes a transatlantic metals-processing platform with greater scale, purchasing leverage, end-market diversification, and a strengthened electrical-steel/value-added franchise positioned for grid and EV demand. If the steel-pricing cycle stabilizes and Kloeckner synergies and EPS accretion materialize as targeted, normalized combined earnings power justifies meaningful upside from a ~17x forward base. Two directors buying at ~$40 signals informed confidence near current levels; pay-for-performance is working (CEO realized comp fell >75% as TSR softened), reducing empire-building concern. Bull target ~$52 (return to the upper end of the range on accretion confirmation and a pricing-cycle turn).

### Bear Case

Pro-forma leverage proves heavier than the market expects, deleveraging stretches multiple years, and European demand/energy headwinds plus FX translation drag combined earnings. The core spread business stays soft, estimate revisions keep falling, and the Q3 miss extends into a pattern. Integration costs and a possible goodwill write-down compound. Bear target ~$30 (back toward the lower-middle of the range, ~26% downside), consistent with the $38 analyst target proving a ceiling rather than a floor.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD pending decisive disclosure. We classify it as an uncertain hold: the positives (sound standalone balance sheet, coherent deal, insider buying at spot, non-demanding multiple) and negatives (negative revisions, the miss, unquantified leverage, FX/integration risk) genuinely offset, and the single most important input — pro-forma combined-entity economics — is not yet available.

**Reassessment timeline**: the first post-deal 8-K/10-Q with combined balance sheet and pro-forma leverage; the close of the delisting tender; and the first quarter of combined results. Next natural checkpoint is the next earnings release.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Pro-forma net leverage is reported at a manageable level (e.g., net debt/EBITDA at or below ~3x with a credible deleveraging path) and estimates stabilize or turn positive.
- One quarter of combined results demonstrates synergy capture and EPS accretion consistent with management targets.
- A turn in the steel-pricing/spread cycle (rising selling prices, easing inventory holding losses) shows up in segment data alongside continued automotive share gains.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Pro-forma leverage materially exceeds expectations with no clear deleveraging path, or a covenant/refinancing concern emerges on the bridge facility.
- A goodwill/intangible impairment is taken on Kloeckner or electrical-steel assets.
- Estimate revisions accelerate downward and the combined entity misses on its first reported quarter, signaling integration is destroying rather than creating per-share value.

## Conclusion

Worthington Steel is a financially sound standalone processor mid-way through a transformation that the market cannot yet fully price. The Kloeckner acquisition reshapes the competitive standing, but it spikes leverage, imports European demand and FX risk, and lands during a cyclical down-leg in the core spread business. The stock has already rallied off its low and trades in the upper-middle of its range, so the deal-overhang and pricing-weakness concerns are partly reflected; at the same time, two directors bought at today's price and the multiple is not demanding. The decisive variable — pro-forma leverage and one quarter of combined results — is missing, which distinguishes a defensible HOLD from a conviction call in either direction. We maintain HOLD and will revisit on the first post-deal financials.

A note on the prior framing: an earlier draft characterized WS as a depressed/trough name warranting avoidance on deal opacity. That conclusion does not hold up against the data. The opacity here is missing disclosure, not demonstrated structural impairment; the standalone balance sheet is healthy; the deal is strategically coherent and EPS-accretion-targeted; insiders bought at spot; and the stock is up over the window, not collapsing. Information insufficiency with balanced positives and negatives is the profile of a HOLD, and that is the call.
