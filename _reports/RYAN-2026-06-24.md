**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Ryan Specialty Holdings is the #2 US property & casualty wholesale broker and #1 US managing underwriter by 2024 premium volume, operating a conflict-free, retail-free intermediary model that places complex and hard-to-place risks, 78% of which sit in the Excess & Surplus (E&S) market. The business rides a genuine secular tailwind: the E&S market compounded at 10.6% from 2010-2024 versus 4.4% for the admitted market, rising from 13.5% to 25.7% of US commercial P&C premium. Ryan's structural advantages are real, and the founder-led management team is signaling conviction with a clustered set of open-market purchases into 2026 weakness.

The current debate is whether the secular share-gain story can outrun a cyclical soft patch. Organic growth has decelerated from 15.4% (2023) to 12.8% (2024) to 10.1% (2025) as property catastrophe capacity returned and rates softened, letting some business migrate back to admitted/direct channels. GAAP net income fell 6.9% in 2025 despite 21% revenue growth, squeezed by acquisition-driven amortization (+73.9%) and interest expense (+40.4%). Forward EPS estimates have been cut roughly 10-12% over the trailing 90 days. The stock is down ~50% from its 52-week high of $69.49, now trading around $34.51.

The financial-distress screening signals (Altman Z-Score 0.55, D/E 14.4x) are artifacts of an asset-light Up-C broker carrying ~$4.3B of fiduciary assets and parking most equity in non-controlling interest; real credit quality is improving (Moody's upgraded to Ba3 in August 2025). The drawdown reflects genuine fundamental deceleration, not pure sentiment, but the insider buying, the secular tailwind, and the high-growth Underwriting Management segment (+58.5% YoY) make this a balanced setup rather than a clear value trap. We rate RYAN a HOLD (growth_hold) with a base-case target of $40, pending evidence of organic re-acceleration.

## Company Overview

Ryan Specialty was founded in 2010 by Patrick G. Ryan, the founder and 41-year Chairman/CEO of Aon. The company is a pure-play specialty insurance intermediary positioned between retail brokers and carriers, placing risks that are complex, high-hazard, or otherwise hard to place through standard admitted channels. Its defining structural feature is the deliberate absence of any retail operations, which avoids channel conflict with the 35,000+ retail brokerage firms (including all top-100 retail brokers) that are its customers.

The business operates three segments. Wholesale Brokerage ("RT Specialty") generated $1,600.4M in net commissions and fees in FY2025 (53.4% of the total, +7.5% YoY), handling open-market placement of specialty risks. Binding Authority contributed $370.2M (12.4%, +15.5% YoY) on high-volume, lower-premium delegated policies. Underwriting Management — the MGA/MGU and National Programs business — generated $1,024.0M (34.2%) and grew 58.5% YoY, boosted heavily by acquisitions, making it the fastest-growing piece of the platform.

Scale metrics underscore the moat: access to 350+ carriers, 39 MGAs/MGUs, 700+ producers, 1.25M+ policies bound annually, ~6,110 employees across 129 offices, and 96% producer retention over 2020-2025. Following the 2024 succession, Timothy W. Turner (ex-President of CRC) serves as CEO; Patrick Ryan is Executive Chairman and controlling shareholder; Janice Hamilton is CFO.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue growth remains strong on a headline basis but is decelerating organically:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | $2,077.5M | $2,515.7M (+21.1%) | $3,051.1M (+21.3%) |
| Net commissions & fees | $2,026.6M | $2,455.7M | $2,994.6M (+21.9%) |
| Organic growth rate | 15.4% | 12.8% | 10.1% |
| Net income (GAAP) | $194.5M | $229.9M | $214.2M (−6.9%) |
| Adj. EBITDAC | $624.7M | $811.2M | $966.7M |
| Adj. EBITDAC margin | 30.1% | 32.2% | 31.7% |
| Adj. diluted EPS | $1.38 | $1.79 | $1.96 |

Headline growth splits roughly evenly between organic (~9.5pts) and acquisitions (~9.8pts). The organic deceleration is the central concern. The mechanism is straightforward: commissions are a percentage of premium, so softening E&S property rates (as catastrophe capacity returned in H2-2024/2025) directly compress commission dollars, and lower rates let some retailers place coverage directly through admitted markets. Casualty lines held up better, but property weakness dominated the trend.

The GAAP income decline stems from amortization rising 73.9% to $274.4M and interest expense rising 40.4% to $222.4M, both acquisition-driven, plus a one-time non-cash deferred-tax charge from the post-Velocity Common Control Reorganization. The GAAP-versus-adjusted gap is wide: GAAP diluted EPS of $0.47 versus adjusted diluted EPS of $1.96, reflecting amortization, equity-comp, and the share-count complexity of the Up-C structure (138M Class A diluted versus ~279M fully exchanged).

Operating leverage is real underneath the noise. The comp-and-benefits ratio improved to 59.1% from 63.2% (adjusted comp ratio 55.5%). Operating cash flow rose to $643.7M from ~$515M, capex is minimal given the asset-light model, and $746.5M of acquisition spend drew corporate cash from $540M to $158M.

The balance sheet distress signals are largely false alarms. Total assets of $10.6B are inflated by $1,426M fiduciary cash and $2,872.8M fiduciary receivables — client and carrier money held in trust, offset by fiduciary liabilities, that is not the company's. Reported stockholders' equity of $648M understates economic equity because most of the LLC sits as non-controlling interest under the Up-C structure. Real leverage is ~$3,356M principal debt: $400M 4.375% notes (2030), $1,200M 5.875% notes (2032), $1,683M Term Loan (SOFR+2.00%), and $73M revolver drawn against $1,400M capacity. The Moody's upgrade cut the Term Loan spread, and the company is in compliance with all covenants. This is a manageable, refinanceable profile for a stable-cash-flow broker. Financial health scores a 6.5 — solid cash generation and improving credit, tempered by genuine acquisition-funded leverage and the GAAP earnings being consumed by amortization and interest.

## Growth Analysis

The earnings-surprise record is mixed: over the trailing four quarters Ryan delivered one beat, one in-line, and two misses, including an 11.3% miss in Q4 2025. Estimate revisions confirm the cyclical pressure rather than a re-acceleration. Current-year EPS consensus has been cut 10.3% over 90 days (to $2.05), next-year cut 12.0% (to $2.35), and the current quarter cut 19.9% (to $0.61). The 30-day figures have stabilized modestly positive (+0.5% to +0.9%), suggesting the worst of the downward revision velocity may be behind us, but this is a story of estimates that fell sharply and are now flattening, not one of rising estimates that warrants a strong-buy signal.

The growth case rests on two offsets to property softness. First, the Underwriting Management / delegated-authority build-out now represents 34% of revenue; the delegated-authority/MGU channel is ~31% of E&S premium and structurally favored as carriers seek scaled, intellectual-capital-rich partners. Second, casualty lines remained healthier than property, providing diversification within the book. Layered on top is the Empower restructuring program announced in Q1 2026 — ~$160M of cumulative one-time charges through 2028 targeting ~$80M of annual savings by 2029, plus investment in data/AI (generative-AI submission intake, an underwriter workbench, proprietary tooling).

The durable secular driver — E&S premium taking share from the admitted market — remains intact, and Ryan's conflict-free positioning makes it a natural beneficiary of wholesale-panel consolidation by increasingly consolidated retail brokers (695 retail M&A deals in 2025). But the near-term cyclical headwind is genuine and management has flagged that property softness may persist into 2026. Growth Prospects score a 6.0: a real long-run tailwind partially offset by a confirmed organic deceleration and downward estimate revisions. This is not the >30% positive-revision profile that would trigger a strong buy signal — the revisions point the other way.

The relevant lens is adjusted EPS of $1.96 (FY2025) and current-year consensus of $2.05, which puts the stock at roughly 17x forward adjusted earnings at $34.51 — meaningfully below the more comparable broker peers.

We score Valuation 6.5: a reasonable forward multiple with identifiable upside if the cyclical patch passes, but not the asymmetric setup that the multiple alone would suggest, because the earnings trajectory is still deteriorating.

## Competitive Landscape

The wholesale brokerage and delegated-authority market is highly fragmented with few national-scale players. Ryan's competitive position is improving structurally even as the cyclical environment weakens. The thesis is that as E&S risks grow larger, more complex, and higher-hazard, sub-scale wholesalers offering only "market access" cannot compete, driving consolidation toward scaled specialists with genuine intellectual capital. Ryan's conflict-free, retail-free model is a durable differentiator versus Aon, Marsh, and the larger brokers that operate both channels, and it positions Ryan as the preferred partner when consolidating retail brokers rationalize their wholesale panels.

Producer retention of 96% over five years, access to all top-100 retail brokers, and a 39-MGA/MGU underwriting platform are evidence of a widening moat. The near-term cyclical headwind cuts the other way — softening property rates compress commission dollars and enable mild disintermediation as business migrates back to admitted/direct channels — but this is a rate-cycle effect, not a structural share loss. Customer concentration is moderate and rising: top-5 retail brokers at 25.2% of revenue (none above 8.8%), top-5 carriers at 20.6% (none above 6.1% ex-Lloyd's). Competitive Position scores 7.5: an improving trajectory and widening moat, with the score held below 8 by the live disintermediation risk and the carrier-contract fragility inherent in the MGA model.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile carries several specific, partially-unpriced overhangs that justify a below-average score.

**Carrier-contract fragility and disintermediation.** Underwriting Management depends on MGA/MGU and binding-authority contracts that carriers can terminate on short notice or reprice at renewal; lost capacity may not be replaceable. Disintermediation — retailers placing E&S property directly and carriers favoring internal distribution — is explicitly cited as an emerging 2025 trend and is the same force pressuring organic growth.

**Securities investigations.** Pomerantz (May 14, 2026) and Schall (June 2, 2026) announced securities-law investigations following the weak Q4/FY2025 results. These are early-stage plaintiff-firm solicitations that routinely follow any sharp post-earnings drop, not filed class actions or SEC enforcement actions. They should be treated as a litigation/reputational overhang and a sentiment risk, with the substantive question being whether property-pricing softness was adequately disclosed. Escalation into a filed complaint would be a negative catalyst.

**Acquisition dependence and goodwill.** $3.2B goodwill plus $1.6B amortizable intangibles sit on a growth strategy reliant on serial M&A (60+ deals). A sustained decline in cash flow or stock price could trigger impairment, and integration risk is ongoing.

**Up-C / Tax Receivable Agreement.** The ~$459M+ TRA liability pays 85% of realized tax savings disproportionately to the Ryan Parties, accelerates to present value on change of control, and functions as both a cash drain and a takeover deterrent. Combined with ~77% Ryan voting control (Class B at 10 votes/share), a premium takeout is unlikely, and minority Class A holders cannot influence outcomes.

**Leverage and interest.** $3.36B of debt with rising, partly floating-rate interest expense, in a higher-for-longer rate environment that raises the carrying cost of the acquisition-funded model.

Risk Factors score 5.0: the disintermediation/organic-growth pressure is partly priced after the drawdown, but the securities investigations, carrier-contract fragility, goodwill exposure, and governance/TRA structure are specific, asymmetric, and not fully discounted.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 47.2% sits slightly rich to 1-year historical volatility of 41.3% (a 5.9% spread), with IV in just the 14th percentile of the current chain — the market is not pricing acute near-term stress. The put/call skew is notably call-heavy (-18.8% at the 1-month tenor, flattening to roughly neutral at 3-6 months) and the put/call OI ratio is a benign 0.70. The term structure is in contango (47.2% near versus 50.1% long-dated). The implied ±10.1% move at the nearest expiry is moderate. Net read: options positioning is not corroborating the 11.5% short interest as an imminent-downside bet; the call-heavy near-term skew suggests some positioning for a relief move, consistent with the insider buying. This is a modest counter-signal to the bearish short-interest reading rather than a confirmation of it.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($50-55).** Property pricing stabilizes through 2026, organic growth re-accelerates back toward mid-teens as the E&S secular tailwind reasserts, and the Underwriting Management segment continues compounding above 50%. Empower delivers its targeted savings, expanding margins past 32%. The wide GAAP-to-adjusted gap narrows as M&A pace moderates and amortization rolls off. On re-accelerating adjusted EPS toward $2.50-2.70 and a re-rating to ~20x, the stock returns toward $50-55. The cluster of insider buying at $31-35 (Patrick Ryan $3.9M, plus CFO, GC, and two directors) validates this as the management-base-case zone.

**Bear Case ($28-31).** Property softness persists or deepens into 2027, disintermediation accelerates as carriers favor internal distribution, organic growth slips below 8%, and a carrier terminates a material MGA contract. Estimates continue their downward path, the securities investigation escalates into a filed complaint, and the multiple compresses toward the low end of the peer range. Goldman's $35 target and the analyst low of $31 frame this downside, roughly 10-19% below current levels.

The balance between these outcomes is genuinely two-sided. The drawdown reflects a real fundamental deceleration, not pure noise, so the burden of proof for the bull case sits on the organic re-acceleration that is not yet visible in the data. The insider buying and the structural moat are the strongest reasons to wait for that evidence rather than to avoid the name.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** Timeframe 12 months, anchored to the next two to three quarterly organic-growth prints.

- **Base case target: $40.** ~19.5x forward adjusted EPS of ~$2.05, a slight re-rating from the current ~17x as estimates stabilize and the property soft patch passes. ~16% upside from $34.51.
- **Bull case target: $52.** ~20x on re-accelerating adjusted EPS approaching $2.60. ~51% upside.
- **Bear case target: $30.** ~14.5x forward EPS on continued property softness and an escalating investigation overhang. ~13% downside.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):** (1) organic growth re-accelerates above 11% for two consecutive quarters; (2) current-year and next-year EPS estimates turn net positive on a 90-day basis after the recent ~10-12% cuts; (3) Underwriting Management sustains 40%+ organic-inclusive growth while property rates stabilize.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):** (1) organic growth falls below 8%; (2) a material carrier terminates an MGA/binding contract; (3) the securities investigation escalates into a filed class action with credible disclosure-fraud allegations; (4) a goodwill impairment charge.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings release (Q2 2026, expected late July/early August), which will show whether organic growth and forward estimates are stabilizing.

## Conclusion

Ryan Specialty is a structurally advantaged, conflict-free specialty insurance platform with a real E&S secular tailwind and a credible, founder-led management team that is buying its own stock into weakness. The financial-distress screening signals are artifacts of the Up-C structure and fiduciary balances, not genuine credit risk — credit was in fact upgraded. The drawdown is rooted in a real cyclical air pocket: property-rate softening has cut organic growth, pressured GAAP earnings, and prompted downward estimate revisions and a Goldman downgrade.

At ~17x forward adjusted earnings, RYAN is reasonably valued for a high-quality broker, but the catalyst for a re-rating — organic re-acceleration — is not yet in the data, and estimates are still drifting down rather than up. That keeps this a balanced setup rather than an asymmetric buy. We rate RYAN a HOLD with a $40 base-case target, and would upgrade on evidence of organic stabilization or downgrade on a carrier-contract loss or escalation of the securities investigation. The score has improved from 5.7 (March) to 6.8 (May) and now sits at 6.5; the modest pullback reflects the confirmed organic deceleration and the falling estimate revisions, not the price decline itself.
