## Executive Summary

STERIS sells the consumables, service labor and outsourced sterilization capacity that let hospitals, device manufacturers and biopharma plants operate sterile environments. Consumables ($1,808.4M) plus service ($2,875.8M) were $4,684.2M of FY2026 revenue, or 78.9% of the $5,935.9M total, with capital equipment only 21.1%. That mix, combined with a validated-supplier moat in contract sterilization, produced FY2026 constant-currency organic revenue growth of 7.3%, a 44.2% gross margin held 20bp higher despite an 80bp tariff drag, and $982.9M of free cash flow. Solvency is not a live question: net debt/EBITDA is roughly 0.9x, interest coverage 18.1x, debt/total capital 21.3%, ratings BBB/Baa2/BBB, and the maturity ladder puts only $396.4M due through FY2029 against $1,052.5M of undrawn revolver.

The Q1 FY2027 print (June quarter, furnished 2026-08-05) confirmed the P&L and unsettled the cash statement. Revenue rose 7.3% to $1.49B (+6% constant-currency organic), adjusted diluted EPS of $2.59 beat non-GAAP consensus of $2.49, and full-year guidance of $11.10–$11.30 adjusted EPS on 7–8% reported revenue growth was reaffirmed. Against that, operating cash flow fell 12.6% to $367.1M, free cash flow fell 14.4% to $279.6M, FY2027 capex guidance was raised 20% to ~$450M to fund a new North Carolina formulated-chemistries Center of Excellence, and FY2027 free-cash-flow guidance was cut to ~$800M from ~$850M. Applied Sterilization Technologies (AST), the 46%-margin segment, saw margin compress on increased depreciation and labor while its constant-currency organic growth decelerated to +5% from +8.5% in the first nine months of FY2026. The shares traded down roughly 3% on the release.

At $228.215 the stock is 20.4x the FY2027 guided adjusted-EPS midpoint, 18.7x next-year consensus of $12.21, ~15.0x EV/EBITDA, and a 3.6% yield on guided FY2027 free cash flow, down from 4.4% on FY2026 actuals. Consensus EPS estimates have moved +0.7% over 90 days, so there is no revision momentum to lean on. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on STERIS and the score is essentially unchanged from July (6.27 vs 6.3): the Q1 beat and Healthcare margin recovery offset the cash-flow guidance cut and AST deceleration. The bar for owning STERIS here is a view that the FY2027–FY2030 reinvestment cycle earns something close to the ~29% return on tangible invested capital the existing asset base generates rather than merely defending margin against tariffs. That evidence does not exist yet.

## Company Overview

STERIS is Irish-domiciled and NYSE-listed, with $4,333.8M (73.0%) of FY2026 revenue generated in the United States, $1,493.7M (25.2%) other foreign and $108.5M (1.8%) in Ireland. Three segments since the May 2024 Dental disposal:

- **Healthcare — $4,208.6M revenue (70.9%), $1,036.4M operating income, 24.6% margin.** Sterile-processing chemistries, sterility assurance, washers, sterilizers, endoscope reprocessors, surgical tables and OR integration, plus installation, preventive maintenance, instrument/endoscope repair and outsourced reprocessing. 12,496 of ~17,937 average employees sit here, overwhelmingly field service, the reason Healthcare service revenue grew ~12% in FY2026.
- **AST — $1,138.5M (19.2%), $524.7M operating income, 46.1% margin.** Outsourced contract sterilization and lab testing across 60-plus facilities, technology-neutral across ethylene oxide, cobalt-60 gamma and e-beam/X-ray. Capital-intensive: $829.2M of gross radioisotope assets and $586.6M of construction in progress at 12/31/25, and 64% of nine-month FY2026 capex.
- **Life Sciences — $588.8M (9.9%), $251.0M operating income, 42.6% margin.** Pharma detergents, cleanroom disinfectants, pharma-grade sterilizers, VHP decontamination and high-purity water systems.

No customer exceeded 10% of any individual segment's revenue in FY2026, though the threshold disclosure does not reveal top-ten concentration or group-purchasing-organization bargaining leverage, which the risk factors flag separately. The quality record is a commercial asset in this business: no FDA or other regulatory enforcement actions in fiscal 2026, no products in the FDA MedWatch Safety Alerts database, and no Class I recalls in fiscal 2024, 2025 or 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**FY2026 headline versus underlying.** GAAP operating income rose 27.1% to $1,101.8M and net income 27.3% to $782.3M. Roughly $90M of that improvement is the non-repeat of two FY2025 charges — $46.0M of restructuring and a $48.2M Illinois ethylene-oxide settlement charge, against $4.1M and zero in FY2026 — with a further $25.6M from lower interest expense ($60.7M versus $86.3M) after Dental proceeds retired term loans. Adjusted segment income grew 7.4% ($1,286.7M to $1,381.9M) and constant-currency organic revenue 7.3%. The charges were real cash costs now behind the company and the interest saving is permanent, but the 27% figure cannot be used as a growth rate. FY2027 guidance of 6–7% constant-currency organic revenue and roughly 9% adjusted EPS growth is consistent with the adjusted series.

**Margin mechanics.** The FY2026 gross-margin bridge: pricing +120bp, operations/lower restructuring +70bp, productivity +50bp, against tariffs −80bp, inflation −70bp, materials −30bp, mix −30bp, currency −10bp, netting +20bp to 44.2%. The Q3 FY2026 quarter was worse: gross margin 43.8% versus 44.5%, with the tariff drag widening to −120bp and product gross margin falling to 46.3% from 48.3%. Pricing is currently outrunning tariffs; the margin question for FY2027 is whether that holds as customer consolidation and GPO pushback build, and as the USMCA six-year joint review (commencing July 1, 2026) potentially changes qualification rules for Canadian and Mexican manufacturing.

**Segment margin direction reversed in Q1 FY2027.** Healthcare margin recovered to ~24.8% from ~24.2% on volume, price, productivity and mix, after falling 100bp in Q3 FY2026. AST compressed to ~48.0% from ~48.6% on increased depreciation and labor. Life Sciences compressed ~130bp on inflation and lower productivity. The AST reading is the one that matters analytically: depreciation on the multi-year capacity build is now landing in the P&L ahead of the utilization it was meant to support, while AST organic growth slowed to +5%. One quarter of a 60bp move in a mid-single-digit-growth segment is weak evidence, and FY2026 full-year AST margin expanded 130bp, but the mechanism is credible and worth monitoring at the October print.

**Cash generation and its FY2027 step-down.** FY2026 operating cash flow of $1,341.4M on net income of $782.3M is 1.71x conversion, supported by $486.5M of D&A of which $265.0M is acquired-intangible amortization. Free cash flow was $982.9M on the company definition ($972.4M on strict OCF less capex), a 4.4% yield on ~$22.2B of market capitalization. Q1 FY2027 OCF fell to $367.1M from $420.0M and FCF to $279.6M from $326.5M, attributed to a lower working-capital contribution rather than earnings quality. Q1 should not be annualized in either direction: Healthcare capital equipment is seasonally weighted to the January–March quarter, and four times Q1 FCF would be $1,118M, above guidance. The normalized anchor is management's revised ~$800M FY2027 guide, which already embeds the higher capex and implies a 3.6% yield at the current price.

**Balance sheet.** At 3/31/26: assets $10,737.2M, equity $7,183.6M, total debt $1,945.6M ($1,350.0M public notes — $675M at 2.700% due 2031, $675M at 3.750% due 2051 — plus $557.8M private placements and $37.8M revolver). Current ratio 2.09x; debt/equity 0.27x on interest-bearing debt (total liabilities/equity 0.49x — the two are frequently conflated); interest coverage 18.1x; Altman Z ≈ 5.2, though roughly 72% of that score comes from the market-equity/total-liabilities term and would fall mechanically with the share price.

**The return-on-capital picture is the central financial-quality fact.** NOPAT of ~$826M on invested capital of ~$8.7B is ROIC of ~9.5%, only modestly above a plausible cost of capital. Goodwill of $4,194.8M plus net intangibles of $1,620.0M is $5,814.8M, or 54% of assets. Excluding that acquisition accounting, return on tangible invested capital is ~28.9%. The operating businesses earn excellent returns on the physical and working capital they employ; prices paid in past M&A absorb most of that excellence at the consolidated level. The practical consequence is that organic capacity investment should create more value per dollar than further large-cap M&A, and the FY2026 pattern (only $23.4M of tuck-ins, $369.0M of capex, a new $1.0B repurchase authorization) is consistent with management reaching the same conclusion.

## Growth Analysis

The demand drivers are slow and reliable rather than exciting: aging populations entering peak healthcare consumption, rising procedure and screening volumes, and customer pressure for sterile-processing efficiency. The delivered result has been constant-currency organic revenue growth of 7.3% in FY2026, 8.2% over the first nine months of FY2026, and 6% in Q1 FY2027, with FY2027 guidance of 6–7%.

Composition in Q1 FY2027 is informative. Healthcare grew 8% reported, with service +10% and consumables +9% but capital equipment only +1%. Management characterizes capital as timing and cites solid order growth; total capital-equipment backlog supports that at the annual mark ($490.7M at 3/31/26 versus $452.9M at 3/31/25, Healthcare $392.1M from $369.2M), though the intra-year reading was down (Healthcare backlog $429.0M at 12/31/25 versus $434.9M a year earlier). Hospital capital budgets are the most cyclically exposed part of the revenue base, and in a higher-for-longer rate environment with OBBBA Medicaid funding pressure on provider budgets, the +1% capital line deserves another quarter of observation before being dismissed as timing.

AST grew 6% reported and +5% constant-currency organic, decelerating from +8.5%. AST's nine-month FY2026 organic growth of 8.5% comprised mid-single-digit pricing and only low-single-digit volume. Two readings are consistent with the disclosed data: AST is monetizing the scarcity of permitted sterilization capacity, or it is passing through energy and labor inflation. The distinction matters for whether the current capacity build converts into incremental margin. Life Sciences was the growth leader at +9% reported and +8% organic, with capital equipment +17% and backlog rising to $98.7M from $83.7M, but at 9.9% of revenue it cannot move the consolidated line.

Consensus reflects this steady profile: current-year EPS of $11.16 (revised +0.7% over 90 days) and next-year $12.21 (+0.6%). There is no estimate-revision inflection here. Growth Prospects holds at 6.0: durable, mid-to-high single digit, decelerating at the margin in the highest-margin segment.

## Competitive Landscape

**Healthcare** is fragmented and crowded. The 10-K names 3M, Baxter, Boston Scientific, Belimed, Ecolab, Fortive, Getinge, Karl Storz, Olympus, Ruhof, SteelCo, Stryker, Skytron and Wassenburg on products, and B. Braun, Crothall, Olympus and Pentax on services. No competitor spans the full offering, and the 12,496-person field service and repair workforce is the practical barrier: 10% service growth and 9% consumables growth in Q1 FY2027 are the output of installed-base density that would take a competitor years and considerable capital to replicate.

**AST** is the structural asset. Sterigenics (Sotera Health) is the only large named direct competitor. The moat is layered: contract sterilization is a validated step inside customers' own FDA device filings, so switching requires revalidation; EO facility permitting is politically difficult and third-party facilities have closed following state or local action; and the capital intensity ($829.2M of gross cobalt-60 assets, $586.6M of construction in progress) deters entry. That intensity is what produces 46% segment margins and also what compressed those margins in Q1. The offsetting structural risk is unique to this segment: AST competes with its own customers, since a device manufacturer at sufficient scale can insource sterilization.

**Life Sciences** competes with Belimed, Contec, Ecolab, Fedegari, Getinge and Stilmas into aseptic biopharmaceutical manufacturing, the best growth/margin combination in the portfolio at the smallest scale.

Trajectory assessment: stable with mild softening. Pricing power is intact and demonstrably being used (+120bp FY2026, +110bp in Q3 FY2026 against −80bp and −120bp of tariff cost). Against that, Healthcare capital equipment grew 1%, AST organic decelerated 350bp, and the Q1 FY2027 forward-risk language added pressure on manufacturing strategy to the existing pricing-strategy language. Competitive Position holds at 6.8.

## Risk Assessment

**Ethylene oxide — the defining idiosyncratic exposure.** A subsidiary operated an EO facility in Waukegan, Illinois from January 2005 to September 2008; individual plaintiffs alleged cancer from air emissions. Binding Settlement Agreements were signed October 29, 2025 covering substantially all pending Cook County personal-injury claims, for up to ~$48.2M charged in FY2025. The accrual fell to $43.2M at 12/31/25. The settlement is not final: it is subject to claims administration continuing into fiscal 2027 and to final court approval, and STERIS retains walkaway rights if a substantial number of plaintiffs decline to settle or are disqualified, or if the court does not approve. The residual accrual is small (4.9% of FY2026 FCF), so the consequential risk is not this docket but the forward one the 10-K names explicitly: publicity may increase the likelihood of additional claims, and future liability could exceed insurance and indemnification. The filing also identifies a second-order channel: because EO is the only viable modality for some devices, regulatory curtailment could produce a device shortage, fewer procedures, and reduced Healthcare demand, linking a 19%-of-revenue segment's regulatory risk to the 71%-of-revenue segment.

**Tariffs, quantified and widening.** 80bp of FY2026 gross margin and 120bp in Q3 FY2026, with the Q1 FY2027 release attributing the exposure to STERIS's corporate structure. A group parented in Ireland generating 73% of revenue in the U.S. carries cross-border cost that peers domiciled onshore do not. The company warns it may need material changes to its global production footprint, with associated capital expenditure, asset impairments and restructuring charges; the North Carolina program is plausibly part of that response, though the press release cites innovation, capacity and network optimization rather than tariffs.

**Tax.** Three named exposures: two November 2023 IRS Notices of Deficiency on 2018 deemed dividend inclusions and withholding, cumulative ~$50.0M excluding interest and penalties, **with no reserves established** and the matter in U.S. Tax Court; Pillar Two, where the January 2026 OECD "side-by-side" safe harbour lists only the United States as a qualifying jurisdiction and STERIS states it does not anticipate benefiting because its ultimate parent is Irish; and re-domestication risk. The effective rate has already moved from 23.2% to 25.0%. Each 100bp of ETR is roughly $10.5M of net income, or ~$0.11 of EPS.

**Other.** Cobalt-60 and EO are single-sourced in certain regions, and the mitigation path has stumbled once — the May 2024 restructuring plan included impairment of an internally developed X-ray accelerator. Goodwill and intangibles at 54% of assets are the mechanism by which durable growth deterioration would convert into a large non-cash charge; there is no current indication of impairment. OBBBA may reduce Medicaid reimbursement to customers. Execution risk on the new NC program is now a named forward-looking risk item.

**Evidence gaps I am not treating as resolved.** Three 8-Ks carrying Item 5.02 (2025-07-03, 2026-04-06, 2026-05-07) were not readable; the only corroborating datum is that non-executive directors went from 6 male/3 female at 3/31/25 to 6 male/2 female at 3/31/26. The $600M North Carolina figure is externally reported, not filing-verified; the $55–70M charge range and the St. Louis and Plymouth closures are filing-sourced. The Q1 FY2027 10-Q and the August 6 call transcript were unavailable, so all Q1 figures come from the 8-K press release and segment margins are derived.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $280 (+22.7%)

Healthcare margin recovery continues off the +60bp Q1 move as pricing, productivity and mix compound, and the +1% capital-equipment quarter proves to be timing, converting the $392.1M Healthcare backlog into Q4-weighted shipments. AST volume catches up to the installed capacity, so the depreciation currently compressing that segment's margin becomes the denominator of a high-incremental-margin ramp; the segment historically operated at 44–46% margins with mid-single-digit pricing and would exit the build cycle above that. The North Carolina consolidation lowers unit cost on consumables growing 7–9% while relocating production inside the 73% of revenue that is U.S.-based, converting the tariff drag from a permanent 80–120bp headwind into a one-time transition cost. Management deploys the $1.0B authorization (4.5% of market capitalization) at prices below the $259.78 average paid in the December 2025 quarter, and free cash flow re-accelerates toward $1.1B in FY2028 as capex normalizes from the ~$450M peak. On $12.21 next-year consensus, 23x gets to $281, a multiple STERIS has carried before and consistent with the $280 high target.

### Bear Case — $188 (−17.6%)

Tariff cost widens past pricing power as customer consolidation and GPO leverage cap price realization, taking gross margin below 44%. The effective tax rate continues drifting above 25.0% under Pillar Two with no offsetting safe harbour, each 100bp costing ~$0.11 of EPS. Hospital capital budgets tighten under OBBBA Medicaid pressure and a higher-for-longer rate path, so Healthcare capital equipment stays near flat and the backlog converts slowly. AST volume does not follow the capacity build, leaving fixed depreciation stranded in a segment carrying $3,586.9M of assets and pushing margins back toward the mid-40s, which in turn puts $5,814.8M of goodwill and intangibles into impairment testing range. Adjusted EPS lands near the low end of guidance and grows in the mid-single digits rather than 9%, and the multiple compresses to 16x on ~$11.75, or $188. A walkaway on the EO settlement, restarting Cook County litigation, or new EPA rulemaking on commercial sterilizer emissions would add to that.

### Where I Land

Both cases route through the same variable: whether $450M of FY2027 capex and a four-year restructuring program earn returns near the 28.9% the existing tangible asset base generates, or merely defend the P&L against trade and tax costs imposed by the corporate structure. The Q1 print gave one data point on each side, with Healthcare margin recovering and AST margin not, and management chose to reaffirm rather than raise EPS guidance after a $0.10 beat. Reaffirming a beat is a conservative posture at best and an acknowledgement of cost pressure at worst; the earnings call, which I could not review, would resolve it. Against a base case of +8.7% plus a 1.2% dividend, roughly 10% total return, and a bear case of −17.6%, the risk/reward is balanced rather than favorable.

On trajectory: this is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and scores have declined from 6.8 in February to 6.3 in July, driven by growth (7.5→6.0), competitive position (8.0→6.8) and management quality (7.0→5.6). The new data neither reverses nor extends that decline. The adjusted EPS beat, the +10.7% adjusted EPS growth and the Healthcare margin recovery argue the operating franchise is intact; the FCF guidance cut, the 20% capex increase, the AST deceleration and the absence of estimate revisions argue nothing has improved on the dimensions that were downgraded. The score holds at 6.27, and I decline to upgrade on price action: the stock is roughly flat versus the July review, which does not improve the forward return.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q2 FY2027 report in late October 2026 is the decisive checkpoint. It provides the second observation on AST margin and organic growth, the first read on whether Healthcare capital-equipment orders convert, an updated tariff run-rate, and the first evidence of repurchase pace under the $1.0B authorization. Secondary dates: USMCA joint-review developments from July 2026 forward, and final court approval of the EO settlement expected during fiscal 2027.

**Upgrade triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
1. AST constant-currency organic growth returns to 7%+ **with** segment margin flat or expanding year over year, demonstrating that the capacity build is converting depreciation into utilization rather than stranding it.
2. FY2027 free-cash-flow guidance restored toward $850M+ or nine-month FCF tracking above $650M, restoring the yield toward 4%+ at a similar price; alternatively, a price near $195–200 (17x next-year consensus of $12.21) which would restore roughly 25% upside to the $248 base case.
3. Evidence of the $1.0B authorization being executed at scale below $230 — say $250M+ of repurchases in a single quarter — which would both signal management's own valuation view and lift EPS growth above 9%.

**Downgrade triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. Gross margin falling below 43.5% for two consecutive quarters as tariff cost outruns pricing, or FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance cut below $11.10.
2. Effective tax rate guided above 27% on Pillar Two or an adverse Tax Court outcome on the unreserved ~$50M IRS deficiency notices.
3. Healthcare capital-equipment revenue negative year over year with backlog declining, indicating hospital capital budgets are contracting rather than shipments being delayed; or a goodwill impairment charge against the $4,194.8M balance.
4. Exercise of walkaway rights on the EO settlement, or a new EO claim cohort filed outside the Cook County docket.

**Scenario summary from $228.215:** base $248 (+8.7%), bull $280 (+22.7%), bear $188 (−17.6%), plus ~1.2% dividend. Consensus range $225–$280 (mean $255.4).

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** STERIS is a genuinely high-quality asset: 78.9% recurring revenue, a validated-supplier moat in contract sterilization, a clean regulatory record that is itself monetizable, pricing power currently sufficient to offset a widening tariff drag, and a balance sheet that removes solvency from the discussion (net debt/EBITDA ~0.9x, 18.1x interest coverage, BBB/Baa2/BBB, $1,052.5M of undrawn revolver). Return on tangible invested capital of ~28.9% describes the operating businesses accurately.

The problem is price against a reinvestment year. At 20.4x guided FY2027 adjusted EPS, 18.7x next-year consensus, and a 3.6% guided free-cash-flow yield — down from 4.4% actual — the stock offers roughly 9% base-case appreciation with an identifiable path to a 16x multiple on flat-to-lower earnings if tariffs, tax and hospital capital budgets all move the wrong way. Estimate revisions are flat, so there is no consensus-catching-up dynamic to exploit. Insiders sold modestly under pre-planned programs and bought nothing at $209–215, roughly 17% below the December 2025 close, and the company repurchased shares at an average of $259.78 in the December quarter. None of that is damning, and none of it is a reason to add.

Existing holders should own STERIS through the October print, collecting the ~1.2% dividend, on the basis that a 6–7% organic grower with 79% recurring revenue converting to ~9% adjusted EPS growth compounds acceptably from a 20x multiple. New capital should wait for either AST margin and growth to inflect together, or a price near $195–200 that widens the margin of safety to something worth underwriting.
