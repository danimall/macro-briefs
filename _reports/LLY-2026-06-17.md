**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Eli Lilly is the clearest single-company expression of the structural shift in metabolic medicine. Tirzepatide (Mounjaro for diabetes, Zepbound for obesity) drove FY2025 revenue to $65.2B, up 45% year-over-year, and accelerated to +56% in Q1 2026 as the incretin franchise grew to 65% of total revenue. The growth is volume-led: FY2025 was +50% volume and −6% price, and Q1 2026 was +65% volume against −13% price. Pricing concessions are accelerating, but unit demand is overwhelming them by a wide margin. Net income nearly doubled to $20.6B in FY2025 and rose 168% to $7.4B in Q1 2026.

The competitive read is unusually clean. Lilly and Novo Nordisk control more than 90% of the global GLP-1 market, and in the same quarter that Lilly posted +56% revenue and raised guidance, Novo reported a roughly 4% constant-currency sales decline and announced approximately 9,000 job cuts. The duopoly is diverging in Lilly's favor in real time, on tirzepatide's efficacy perception and a deeper next-generation pipeline (orforglipron, retatrutide, eloralintide). The April 2026 FDA approval of orforglipron (Foundayo), the first oral GLP-1 for obesity, is the structural catalyst: an oral, manufacturing-light option that can expand the addressable market well beyond the injectable base.

At ~$1,111, the stock trades near 48x trailing FY2025 EPS of $22.95, which overstates the multiple at an earnings inflection. Consensus current-year EPS of $36.21 and next-year EPS of $44.49 put the forward multiple at ~31x and ~25x respectively, against a growth rate north of 20%. That is a defensible valuation for this growth profile, but it is not cheap, and it embeds continued execution. The thesis carries two genuine threats that justify a measured rather than aggressive BUY: structural U.S. pricing compression (Most-Favored-Nation/TrumpRx framework, IRA price-setting for Trulicity and Verzenio in 2028) and the FDA's request for additional liver and cardiac safety data on orforglipron alongside reportedly slow early uptake versus Novo's oral semaglutide.

## Company Overview

Lilly is a ~150-year-old, Indianapolis-based global pharmaceutical company that reports as a single operating segment across Cardiometabolic Health, Oncology, Immunology, and Neuroscience. The defining feature today is incretin (GLP-1) dominance. Tirzepatide's dual GIP/GLP-1 mechanism underpins both Mounjaro and Zepbound, which together represented 56% of FY2025 revenue and 65% of Q1 2026 revenue. The top products, adding Verzenio, Trulicity, Taltz, and Jardiance, accounted for 82% of FY2025 revenue.

The company's competitive advantages are tirzepatide's perceived efficacy edge over Novo's semaglutide, a next-generation pipeline (orforglipron oral GLP-1, retatrutide triple agonist, eloralintide), aggressive manufacturing capex to relieve supply constraints, and a direct-to-consumer channel (LillyDirect) that captures cash-pay demand and partially bypasses PBM friction. Donanemab (Kisunla) in Alzheimer's, pirtobrutinib, imlunestrant, and mirikizumab broaden the base outside metabolic disease, but the investment case stands or falls on incretins for the foreseeable future.

## Financial Analysis

The revenue and earnings trajectory is exceptional and still accelerating:

| Year | Revenue | Net Income | Diluted EPS |
|------|---------|-----------|-------------|
| 2022 | $28.5B | $6.2B | — |
| 2023 | $34.1B | $5.2B | — |
| 2024 | $45.0B | $10.6B | $11.71 |
| 2025 | $65.2B (+45%) | $20.6B (+95%) | $22.95 |
| Q1'26 | $19.8B (+56% YoY) | $7.4B (+168%) | $8.26 |

Margins are strong and largely holding. Gross margin expanded to 83.0% in FY2025 from 81.3%, then dipped modestly to 81.9% in Q1 2026 on lower realized prices, a manageable give-back given the volume engine. Net margin is roughly 31.7%. R&D rose 21% to $13.3B and SG&A rose 29% on launch promotion, both consistent with a company in heavy reinvestment to defend and extend a franchise lead.

Operating cash flow nearly doubled to $16.8B in FY2025. Free cash flow of $9.0B is depressed only because capex jumped to $7.8B (from $5.1B) as Lilly builds out manufacturing capacity. Total debt rose to $42.5B at year-end and $43.4B by Q1 2026, against ~$5.3B cash, on an investment-grade balance sheet. ROE is 77.8% and ROA 18.4%.

A screening note worth addressing directly: a naïve Altman Z-Score reads 1.25 ("distress zone") for Lilly. This is a false signal. The score is mechanically depressed by thin equity-to-assets (23.6%), itself a product of aggressive buybacks plus debt-funded capex and M&A, and by an operating-margin component distorted by acquired in-process R&D charges. With 77.8% ROE, $16.8B operating cash flow, and investment-grade ratings, Lilly is not financially distressed. The low book equity reflects capital-return choices, not solvency risk.

Capital allocation is shareholder-aligned at the margin. The dividend was raised to $1.73/quarter ($6.92 annualized for 2026), $4.1B was repurchased in 2025 and $2.3B in Q1 2026, leaving $8.6B on the $15B authorization. The yield (~0.6%) is incidental; this is a growth-and-reinvestment story, not an income vehicle.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three pillars. First, the existing tirzepatide franchise is still compounding at 45-56% on volume despite double-digit price erosion, which means the addressable patient pool is expanding faster than realized prices are falling. Second, orforglipron's oral obesity approval opens a structurally larger market: an oral pill taken without food or water restrictions can reach patients who reject injectables and is materially cheaper to manufacture, easing the supply bottleneck that has constrained Zepbound. The Phase 3 type 2 diabetes trial met its primary endpoint in June 2026 with data reported as beating oral semaglutide, supporting a second diabetes filing. Third, the pipeline behind orforglipron — retatrutide (triple agonist, Phase 3 obesity endpoints met) and eloralintide (multiple Phase 3 starts) — provides multi-year duration to the metabolic franchise.

The earnings-revision picture confirms momentum without flagging a euphoric extrapolation. Current-year consensus rose 4.4% over 90 days to $36.21, and next-year consensus rose 5.6% to $44.49. The 30-day moves are flat, so revisions are positive but no longer accelerating; consensus has caught up to the Q1 blowout. The earnings-surprise record is unambiguous: four consecutive beats, the most recent a 27% beat ($8.55 actual vs. $6.73 estimate). This is a company that consistently outdelivers consensus, which supports confidence in the forward estimates.

The cleanest growth confirmation is the competitive divergence. When the two dominant players in a duopoly move in opposite directions, the share-gain thesis has unusually high signal.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E of ~48x is the wrong metric at an inflection where EPS is rising more than 50% annually. The appropriate anchors are forward and normalized earnings. Current-year consensus puts the multiple at ~31x; next-year consensus puts it at ~25x. Against a forward growth rate above 20%, the PEG is roughly 1.2 on next-year numbers — reasonable for a wide-moat franchise leader, not stretched.

Peer context cuts both ways. Lilly trades at a large premium to the pharmaceutical peer median P/E of 20.0 (JNJ 27.6, MRK 33.8, PFE 20.0, BMY 15.9). That premium is earned by growth: those peers grow low-to-mid single digits or face patent cliffs, while Lilly grows 45%+. The relevant question is whether ~25x forward on 20%+ growth is mispriced. It is fairly priced, with modest asymmetry to the upside if orforglipron uptake accelerates and pricing compression stays slower than volume gains.

The valuation score is moderated rather than high for two reasons that are specific to this name. The first is that, at a low-30s current-year multiple, this is not a stock where rising estimates alone re-rate the multiple higher; beats are now expected, and the asymmetry around the next print is balanced (beat = modest, miss = sharp derate). The second is the pricing/policy overhang: MFN/TrumpRx and IRA selections introduce a structural ceiling on U.S. pricing power that the bull case implicitly assumes volume will continue to outrun. The stock has done that so far; sustaining it is the central uncertainty.

Sell-side targets span $850 (low) to $1,500 (high), with a $1,251 median against the current ~$1,111. The median implies ~13% upside and is consistent with a low-30s forward multiple on next-year estimates.

## Competitive Landscape

The GLP-1 market is effectively a Lilly–Novo duopoly. Lilly is winning on three axes: efficacy perception (tirzepatide's dual mechanism), next-generation pipeline lead (oral orforglipron, retatrutide triple agonist), and execution/manufacturing investment. The Novo divergence is the strongest available evidence that share is moving toward Lilly.

The competitive picture is not one-sided, which is why the score is strong rather than maximal. Novo's oral semaglutide (Wegovy) is reportedly outselling Foundayo in early launch (per Spherix), so Lilly's oral lead is contested rather than secured. Chinese biotech plus AI-enabled discovery are cited as intensifying threats. The trajectory remains positive — share is being gained, the moat in injectables is wide, and the pipeline depth exceeds any single competitor — but the oral battleground is genuinely competitive and worth monitoring rather than assuming.

The prior trajectory marked competitive position down from 9.0 to 7.5 over recent reports. That adjustment is warranted and I am holding it: the slow oral uptake versus Novo and the widening obesity field are real, and a 9.0 implied an uncontested moat that the early Foundayo data does not support. The current 7.5 reflects a leader still gaining share but facing credible contest in the next product cycle.

## Risk Assessment

**Product concentration.** Two drugs are ~65% of revenue. Any safety signal, demand shock, channel/inventory swing, patent loss, or competitive displacement in tirzepatide would be disproportionately damaging. Management explicitly warns that incretin volume swings can have an outsized impact in any period.

**Pricing and policy — the central new risk.** Lilly finalized voluntary pricing agreements with the U.S. government in Q1 2026 (reached preliminarily in November 2025), including the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Program (discounted obesity access July 1, 2026–Dec 31, 2027) and a commitment to "more balanced pricing across developed nations," aligning with the MFN/TrumpRx framework. Management explicitly flags that expanded access may not offset pricing concessions and could pressure international pricing. Separately, the IRA has selected Trulicity and Verzenio for government-set prices effective 2028 (Jardiance already selected for 2026), with more products expected. The bear case is that price compression eventually catches volume.

**Orforglipron execution.** The FDA's approval letter requested additional safety data on potential liver and heart effects, and early launch uptake reportedly trails Novo's oral semaglutide. The single biggest catalyst carries the two clearest near-term yellow flags.

**Manufacturing.** Up to ~$10B in take-or-pay supply commitments plus heavy capex represents a large bet on sustained demand. Overbuilding (demand miss strands capacity) and underbuilding (lost sales) are both disclosed. Orforglipron's manufacturing-light profile partially de-risks this.

**Tariffs / supply chain.** Pharma is currently exempt from many tariffs, but exemptions may be terminated, and Lilly relies on China-based suppliers for chemical intermediates against a BIOSECURE Act backdrop.

**Litigation.** Active items include the Average Manufacturer Price qui tam (Seventh Circuit affirmed against Lilly; Supreme Court petition filed), insulin-pricing MDL, 340B disputes, and Mounjaro/Trulicity/Zepbound product-liability MDLs (GI injury and NAION eye claims). Lilly is predominantly self-insured. None is individually thesis-breaking, but the cluster is non-trivial.

The risk score of 5.5 reflects that several of these are specific, dated, and not fully priced: the orforglipron safety-data request, the IRA 2028 price-setting, and the MFN framework are catalysts the market is treating as manageable but which could surprise to the downside.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 39.3% against 1-year historical volatility of 38.1% — fairly priced on a spread basis, but the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 39.3% > long-term 33.6%), indicating the market is pricing imminent stress around an upcoming event. IV rose 6.1 points (33.3% → 39.3%) over the prior three days as the stock fell 1.9%. Skew is call-heavy (−13.3% put/call skew), put/call volume ratio 0.59, and put/call OI ratio 0.58 (up modestly), so positioning is not bearish, which supports the fundamental BUY rather than contradicting it. The backwardation is the one caution: the options market expects a near-term move, so new entry should be sized to tolerate event-driven volatility around the next print and any orforglipron/pricing headlines. Short interest is negligible at 1.1% of float (2.8 days to cover), so there is no crowded positioning either way.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($1,500+).** Tirzepatide volume continues to outrun price compression, orforglipron uptake inflects as the diabetes filing progresses and distribution scales via LillyDirect and WeightWatchers, retatrutide advances, and Novo continues to lose share. The oral franchise expands the obesity market structurally while easing manufacturing constraints, supporting EPS toward and beyond the high-$40s for FY26 and the $50s thereafter. At ~28-30x a $50+ number, the stock approaches $1,500. This requires the FDA safety-data request to resolve benignly and pricing compression to stay slower than volume.

**Bear case ($850).** MFN/TrumpRx and IRA selections compress U.S. pricing faster than volume can offset, the orforglipron safety review surfaces a label restriction or delay, and Novo's oral semaglutide holds the oral market. Estimates flatten, the premium multiple compresses toward the high-teens/low-20s on a stalled growth narrative, and the stock derates to ~$850 (the low sell-side target, ~−23% from current).

**Base case ($1,250).** Volume growth moderates from 50%+ toward the 25-35% range as the law of large numbers and pricing concessions bite, orforglipron contributes meaningfully but not explosively against contested oral competition, and FY26 EPS lands near the $36-40 range with FY27 toward the mid-$40s. At ~30x current-year / ~26x next-year, the stock reaches roughly $1,250, consistent with the sell-side median and implying ~13% upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $1,250** (~13% upside from ~$1,111). Math: ~28x the FY26/FY27 blended consensus of roughly $36-44, which is a defensible multiple for 20%+ growth and aligns with the $1,251 median sell-side target. Targets on this name have repeatedly proven to be floors that analysts revise up after beats, so the base case skews to the upper end if the next print beats again.

**Bull case target: $1,500** (~+35%). ~28-30x an FY27 EPS approaching the low-$50s, requiring orforglipron inflection and continued Novo share loss.

**Bear case target: $850** (~−23%). Multiple compression to high-teens/low-20s on a pricing-driven growth stall and/or an orforglipron safety setback.

**Timeframe:** 12-18 months, anchored to the next 2-3 quarterly prints (next earnings cadence following the April 30 report), the orforglipron safety-data resolution, and the July 1, 2026 start of the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Program.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
- Incretin volume growth decelerates below ~20% YoY in a quarter while price erosion exceeds −15%, signaling that pricing compression is catching volume.
- The FDA's additional safety review results in a label restriction, REMS requirement, or approval setback for orforglipron.
- Two consecutive quarters of negative current-year EPS revisions (reversing the +4.4% 90-day trend), confirming consensus is rolling over.

## Conclusion

Lilly is the dominant franchise in a bifurcating GLP-1 duopoly, compounding revenue on volume while its only peer of scale contracts. Orforglipron's oral obesity approval expands the addressable market and partially de-risks the manufacturing bet, and the deep next-generation pipeline gives the metabolic franchise multi-year duration. At ~25x next-year consensus on a 20%+ growth rate, the valuation is reasonable rather than cheap, which is appropriate for a wide-moat compounder at this stage. The genuine threats — structural U.S. pricing compression and the orforglipron safety-data request — cap conviction and keep this from being a maximum-conviction call, but they do not break the thesis at the current multiple. The risk/reward to the $1,250 base case, with meaningful upside in the bull case and a defined ~−23% downside, supports a **BUY**, sized to tolerate the near-term event volatility the options market is signaling.
