**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Hershey is a high-quality, moat-protected confectionery franchise working through the tail end of a severe cocoa-cost shock. FY2025 was a distorted trough: gross margin collapsed 1,380 basis points to 33.5%, operating profit fell 50%, and diluted EPS dropped 60% to $4.34 — almost entirely a cost problem (revenue actually grew 4.4% on ~6% price realization). The core North America Confectionery business remained profitable at a 26.3% segment margin throughout, and MULO+ retail takeaway grew, confirming demand held up even under heavy pricing. Q1 2026 shows the recovery underway: net sales +10.6%, gross margin +570bps to 39.4%, and diluted EPS +94% to $2.13, driven by cocoa futures averaging ~$1.86/lb (down ~49% from the FY2025 average of $3.64/lb) plus the July 2025 pricing action flowing through.

The investment case is a cocoa cost-cycle mean-reversion layered on a stable staples franchise. There is a real mechanical tailwind to 2026 margins: cocoa normalization, ~$261M of deferred non-hedge-accounted derivative gains scheduled to flow into segment income over the next twelve months, full-year benefit from 2025 pricing, and roughly $400M of run-rate savings from the AAA productivity program landing as costs ease. Most of this is now visible and priced. The stock trades at ~32x trailing and ~21x forward consensus of $8.42, with near-term estimate revisions falling (current-quarter EPS cut 11.3% over 90 days). The dividend consumed ~123% of trough-year net income and was raised again in 2026, a bet that the margin recovery holds. That bet looks reasonable given Q1, but it is a bet, not a certainty.

We rate HSY a HOLD. The recovery is genuine but largely mechanical rather than demand-led; the valuation already reflects it; and there is meaningful execution uncertainty under a brand-new outsider CEO amid an unusually thick cluster of officer and director changes. This continues a HOLD trajectory (5.8 → 5.2 → 5.5 → 5.3 overall), with the balance of evidence still two-sided. We would turn constructive on a pullback into the low-$150s, confirmation that pricing elasticity is not accelerating, or evidence the new leadership team can convert margin recovery into durable volume growth.

## Company Overview

Hershey is the largest chocolate producer in North America and a leading U.S. snack maker, selling 85+ brands across roughly 65 countries. Three reporting segments (FY2025 net sales):

- **North America Confectionery — $9,479.7M (81.1% of sales).** The profit engine: chocolate and non-chocolate confectionery, gum/mints, protein bars, Chocolate World retail, and licensing. FY2025 segment income $2,493.8M at a 26.3% margin. Iconic brands (Reese's, Hershey's, Kit Kat license, Jolly Rancher) plus a "seasons" business (Halloween, Christmas, Easter, Valentine's) anchor demand.
- **North America Salty Snacks — $1,271.3M (10.9%).** SkinnyPop, Dot's Homestyle Pretzels, and now LesserEvil (organic popcorn, acquired November 2025). The growth engine — +11.9% net sales and +21.3% segment income in FY25, with retail takeaway +11.3% and share gains. Q1 2026 segment income fell ~18% on higher supply-chain cost and a voluntary temporary product withdrawal.
- **International — $941.6M (8.1%).** Mexico, Brazil, India, Malaysia, plus export. The weak spot: FY25 segment income collapsed to $3.3M (0.4% margin) from $111.5M, with a $6.4M goodwill impairment in one reporting unit in Q4 2025.

The competitive moat rests on dominant U.S. confectionery shelf position, scale-driven retail relationships, and brand equity that supports pricing. MULO+ takeaway grew 5.4% in FY25 and 9.3% in Q1 2026, so demand is resilient through heavy pricing. Control sits with the Milton Hershey School Trust via 10-vote Class B shares; combined with Pennsylvania change-of-control statute, this makes HSY effectively unacquirable, removing takeover optionality but reinforcing long-term stability and dividend durability.

## Financial Analysis

**The FY2025 cocoa shock.** The gross-margin collapse from 47.3% to 33.5% was driven by +$736.6M of commodity costs, +$287.2M of supply-chain/tariff costs, and +$491M of unfavorable mark-to-market on commodity derivatives. Those hedges are not designated for hedge accounting, so MTM swings hit the P&L directly, and the reported number overstated the operating damage. Net income fell to $883M from $2,221M. The effective tax rate jumped to 27.3% from 10.2% as prior-year investment tax credits rolled off. Revenue grew, so this was a cost event, not a demand event.

**The Q1 2026 recovery.** Net sales rose to $3,104M on ~10% price realization plus ~2% from LesserEvil; operating profit rose 73.5%. Management states the cocoa supply-demand outlook has "improved substantially" — the 2024-25 season ended in surplus after three deficit years, with a large surplus forecast for 2025-26 on output growth in Côte d'Ivoire and Ghana. Four consecutive earnings beats averaging ~19% (Q1 2026 +13.8%, Q4 2025 +20.6%, Q3 2025 +20.4%, Q2 2025 +20.8%) support confidence in the near-term trajectory.

**Balance sheet and cash flow.** Total debt is ~$5.4B against ~$4.7B equity (D/E ~1.96); the Altman Z of 2.03 sits in the grey zone, which is normal-to-slightly-elevated for a levered staple rather than a distress signal. FY2025 free cash flow was $1.82B (15.6% margin) on operating cash flow of $2.28B, so cash generation held up despite the earnings collapse. Capex is stepping down to $425-475M in 2026 as the multi-year ERP build completes, freeing cash. The February 2025 issuance of $2B in notes at 4.55%-5.10% refinanced maturing debt at materially higher rates, lifting interest expense — a headwind consistent with the higher-for-longer rate environment.

**The dividend is the critical non-obvious point.** The dividend consumed $1.085B in FY2025 — roughly 123% of GAAP net income — and was covered by cash flow, not earnings. Management raised it again in 2026 ($1.452/quarter versus $1.370). That is defensible only if the margin recovery holds; Q1 supports it, but the dividend safety hinges entirely on cocoa normalization persisting. Buybacks were essentially paused in 2025 (dilution offset only), with ~$470M remaining on the December 2023 authorization.

Given the mechanical recovery is real but the dividend was uncovered by earnings in the trough and leverage is meaningful in a rising-rate environment, financial health scores 5.5 — resilient cash generation offset by the payout stretch and elevated debt cost.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth in reported earnings is a recovery, not organic acceleration. FY2026 consensus EPS of $8.42 implies roughly +94% off the $4.34 trough, but that is cocoa reversion, pricing carryover, deferred derivative gains, and AAA savings, not underlying volume expansion. Volume was actually down ~1% in FY2025 and ~2% in Q1 2026; the top line is being carried by price. This matters for scoring: the estimate-revision picture is mixed and tilting negative at the near end. Current-quarter EPS was cut 11.3% over 90 days and next-quarter 4.4%, while full-year 2026 is roughly flat (+0.3%) and 2027 is essentially unchanged (-0.1%). The strong +30% revision signal that would flag a genuine buy is absent here — the recovery is already in consensus, and the near-term direction of revisions is down.

The durable growth engine is Salty Snacks, augmented by the ~$769M LesserEvil acquisition (up to $200M earnout; $289M goodwill, $604.5M intangibles preliminary). This continues a deliberate snacking pivot (Weaver 2023, Sour Strips 2024, Fulfil 2025) in response to the 10-K's own observation that in-store activity for other snacks is pressuring confectionery category growth. The strategic logic is sound, but snacks are only ~11% of sales, and the Q1 segment-income decline on supply-chain cost and a product withdrawal shows the segment is not yet a clean margin story.

The structural overhang is GLP-1/weight-management adoption, which HSY explicitly lists as a demand risk for indulgent categories. This is a slow-moving, hard-to-quantify headwind rather than an immediate one, but it caps the terminal growth rate the market will assign to confectionery. Growth prospects score 5.5 — a real cyclical recovery and a credible snacking pivot, tempered by flat-to-negative near-term revisions, price-led (not volume-led) top-line growth, and a structural category headwind.

## Valuation Assessment

At $174.30, HSY trades at ~32x trailing EPS (trough, distorted), ~21x FY2026 consensus, and ~18x FY2027 of $9.86. The trailing multiple is meaningless at a cocoa-driven trough; the forward multiples are the right lens, and they are not cheap for a staple growing volume around zero. Against Packaged Foods peers, the picture is mixed: peer median trailing P/E is 28.8x, but the group is heterogeneous and several peers (KHC, GIS, MKC) trade at single-digit or negative multiples reflecting their own structural problems, while HRL (28.8x) and TSN (44x) sit higher. On a forward basis HSY's ~21x is a premium to most of the group, justified by its superior brand equity, pricing power, and Salty Snacks growth, but not obviously mispriced.

The 3.4% dividend yield provides absolute support and is below the peer median of 4.8%, reflecting Hershey's higher quality but also less yield cushion. Analyst targets span a wide $166-250 (mean $207.7, median $208), implying meaningful upside to consensus, but on a name that has already re-rated from the November low of $161.43 and is well off its 52-week high of $239.48. The recommendation mix has drifted modestly less bullish (a strong-buy downgrade this period, now 2 strong-buy / 6 buy / 14 hold / 1 sell).

The key valuation question is whether the mechanical 2026 margin recovery is fully priced. We think most of it is: cocoa reversion, deferred derivative gains flowing to segments, full-year pricing, and AAA savings are all known and in consensus. The reported margins in 2026 will look artificially strong for the mirror-image reason they looked weak in 2025, a reason to focus on segment income, not headline gross margin, and to avoid extrapolating the recovery quarters. The cheapness that exists is cyclical-normalization cheapness the market has already recognized, which our own framework flags as a value-trap risk when unaccompanied by a fresh catalyst and rising estimates, and here estimates are falling at the near end. Valuation scores 5.0 — fairly priced on normalized earnings, with limited asymmetry either direction.

## Competitive Landscape

Hershey's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding, a downgrade from the near-monopoly framing the position carried a few reports ago (competitive position was scored 8.0 in February, now 6.0). The evidence for durability is strong: the entire industry faced the same cocoa spike, so FY25 margin pain was sector-wide and was a cost problem, not a share-loss problem. HSY's ability to push ~6-10% price with only ~1-2% volume give-up compares favorably to peers and confirms genuine pricing power and shelf dominance. Salty Snacks is gaining share.

The offsetting evidence is real. A ~10bp Confectionery market-share decline in Q1 signals the pricing ceiling is being tested; after two years of aggressive price the elasticity math is tightening. The 10-K itself flags rising in-store activity for other snacks pressuring confectionery category growth, and GLP-1 adoption is a slow structural drag on indulgent demand. International is near break-even and vulnerable. Against private players like Mars and rising private-label pressure, HSY's U.S. moat is intact but the category tailwind behind it is fading. Competitive position scores 6.0 — a genuinely wide moat on a category with softening structural growth, and early signs the pricing lever is nearing its limit.

## Risk Assessment

**Cocoa price volatility** is the dominant risk. HSY hedges only 3-24 months and is fully exposed to the structural level of cocoa, which the 10-K notes "remains structurally elevated" even after the recent fall. The non-hedge-accounted derivatives create large reported-earnings swings in both directions; the ~$380M of cumulative unallocated MTM gains as of Q1 2026 will flow to segments as a 2026 tailwind but reverse the analytical clarity that FY25 offered. A cocoa relapse would simultaneously hit margins and pressure dividend coverage.

**Pricing elasticity** is the second-order risk: the whole recovery model depends on consumers absorbing ~6-10% price, and the ~10bp share decline shows the strategy is being tested. **Customer concentration** is meaningful — McLane (Walmart's distributor) is ~27% of consolidated net sales. **Tariffs** were called a material negative in FY25; a February 2026 IEEPA ruling struck down some tariffs and may open refunds, but net exposure remains uncertain. **Regulatory items** (FDA synthetic-dye phase-out by end-2027, EU Deforestation Regulation effective December 2026) add compliance cost, though management deems each not material individually. **International** is near break-even with FX exposure. **ERP execution** and internal-control implications post the April 2024 go-live remain a flagged risk. The Q1 Salty Snacks voluntary product withdrawal is a small reputation/quality watch item, and quality is the top-listed 10-K risk factor.

**Governance and leadership** is the risk that most affects our management scoring. Kirk Tanner (ex-Wendy's, PepsiCo beverages) is a brand-new outsider CEO, and there is an unusually thick cluster of Item 5.02 officer/director 8-Ks (June 3, May 28, April 14 2026, plus multiple 2025 filings) pointing to a broad leadership reshuffle in progress. This is both risk (strategy/execution uncertainty during transition) and potential catalyst (fresh direction, more aggressive snacking M&A). The pay-vs-performance disconnect — CEO comp actually paid of just $314,886 in FY2024 then $18.4M in FY2025, a year net income fell 60% — is driven by TSR-linked equity revaluation but warrants alignment scrutiny.

Risk factors score 5.0: cocoa and pricing-elasticity risks are partly priced, but the leadership transition and dividend-coverage dependence on cocoa are live idiosyncratic risks not fully reflected.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. ATM IV of 44.6% is rich versus 27.2% one-year historical volatility (71st percentile within the current chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (44.6% near-term versus 34.5% long-term), consistent with an imminent catalyst, most likely the upcoming earnings print given the July 31 near expiry and ±4.8% implied move. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.2%) and the put/call OI ratio fell sharply from 2.12 to 0.83 since mid-June, indicating hedging pressure has eased rather than intensified. On balance, the options market signals event risk into earnings without a strong directional bearish lean, consistent with our two-sided HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$210-230).** Cocoa stays in surplus and normalizes durably; the deferred derivative gains, full-year 2025 pricing, and AAA savings drive 2026 EPS toward or above the $8.42 consensus with upside from the four-quarter beat pattern. Salty Snacks compounds double-digits with LesserEvil accretive; the new CEO executes an aggressive snacking pivot; and the market re-rates the normalized franchise back toward its 52-week high near $239. At ~24-26x $9.00+ of normalized power, $210-230 is achievable. The high analyst target of $250 anchors this scenario.

**Bear case (~$150-155, ~-11% to -13%).** Cocoa relapses or stays structurally elevated, pricing elasticity accelerates (the ~10bp share decline widens), and the ~10% price / -2% volume mix inverts toward volume loss. The dividend comes under scrutiny if margins don't hold. Leadership churn under an outsider CEO produces strategy drift or a poorly-priced acquisition. GLP-1 adoption compresses the terminal multiple the market assigns confectionery. At ~17-18x a stalled ~$8.50 EPS with no re-rating, the stock drifts to the low-$150s. The low analyst target of $166 and the November low of $161.43 bracket the near-term downside.

The base case sits between: a real but mechanical margin recovery that is largely priced, with the stock range-bound in the $170-200 zone as consensus digests 2026 normalization against soft near-term revisions and leadership uncertainty.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings print (implied by the ±4.8% options move and July 31 near expiry), followed by the specific Item 5.02 8-Ks that will identify which executives departed and were appointed under CEO Tanner.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- A pullback into the low-$150s, which would move forward P/E toward ~18x and create genuine asymmetry with the dividend yield rising toward peer-median support.
- Confirmation that pricing elasticity is stabilizing — Confectionery market share flat-to-up and volume decline narrowing below ~1% — showing the recovery is becoming demand-supported rather than purely cost-driven.
- Positive current-quarter and full-year estimate-revision inflection (reversing the current -11.3% 90-day cut) alongside continued cocoa surplus, signaling consensus is catching up to a durable margin story.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A cocoa relapse (futures sustained back above ~$3.00/lb) that re-pressures margins and threatens dividend coverage.
- A dividend-coverage warning — payout remaining above 100% of GAAP net income into 2027 without margin recovery, or any signal of a dividend reset.
- Accelerating volume decline (below ~-3% YoY) indicating the pricing model has broken, or a value-destructive large acquisition by the new leadership team.

## Conclusion

Hershey is a high-quality franchise emerging from a cocoa-driven earnings trough, and the Q1 2026 recovery is real. But the recovery is mechanical — cocoa reversion, deferred derivative gains, pricing carryover, and AAA savings — rather than demand-led, and it is already reflected in the ~21x forward multiple and consensus $8.42 EPS. Near-term estimate revisions are falling, pricing elasticity is beginning to show at the shelf, the dividend consumed ~123% of trough-year net income and was raised again into an unproven recovery, and leadership is in flux under a new outsider CEO amid heavy officer/director turnover. Against those offsets sits a genuinely wide moat, resilient cash generation, a credible Salty Snacks growth engine, and four consecutive earnings beats.

The evidence is two-sided, and the valuation offers limited asymmetry from current levels. We maintain a HOLD (overall 5.3), consistent with the prior HOLD sequence, and would look for a lower entry, confirmation that pricing power is not eroding, or an estimate-revision inflection before turning constructive. The recovery bet is reasonable but not yet clearly favorable versus the price paid.
