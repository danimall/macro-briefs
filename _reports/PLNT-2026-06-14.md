**Research Date: June 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Planet Fitness fell roughly 55% from its 52-week high of $114.47 to ~$51, with the decisive move a ~30% single-day drop on May 7, 2026 when the company beat Q1 EPS but cut FY2026 guidance, paused its planned Black Card price increase, and withdrew the three-year growth algorithm (10-15% sales / 15-20% adjusted EPS) it had set at its 2025 Investor Day. The market looked past the headline +22% revenue growth, which was carried by a one-time Equipment-segment bulge (+123% on the re-equipment cycle), and focused on the durable metrics: same-club sales decelerated to +3.5% from +6.1% a year earlier, and new member sign-ups fell roughly 36% in January, the most important enrollment month of the year.

The investment question is whether the pricing-power and demand softness is cyclical (a cash-strapped value consumer that recovers) or structural (a budget brand that has hit its pricing ceiling, possibly compounded by GLP-1 weight-loss drugs reducing gym joins). That question is genuinely unresolved, and it determines whether the stock is a ~14-16x forward bargain on a still-growing earnings base or a value trap. The evidence does not yet break cleanly in either direction, which is the reason to hold.

On the constructive side, the bad news is substantially in the price. At $50.95 against freshly-cut consensus EPS of $3.20 (FY26) and $3.61 (FY27), the stock trades at ~15.9x and ~14.1x forward earnings on a base that is still growing year over year. Free cash flow of ~$255M is roughly a 6% yield, $450M of buyback authorization remains at prices half what the company paid in its December 2025 accelerated repurchase, and both the new CEO and a director bought stock in the open market within days of the crash. The setup is roughly symmetric: a bear case of -18% to -27% against an analyst median target ~19% above spot. That symmetry, plus a 50%+ drawdown that has already discounted the worst of the news, supports a HOLD rather than an AVOID.

## Company Overview

Planet Fitness operates and franchises one of the largest fitness-center networks in the world: ~21.5 million members across 2,909 clubs (2,617 franchised, 292 corporate-owned) as of Q1 2026, spanning all 50 U.S. states plus Puerto Rico, Canada, Panama, Mexico, Australia and Spain. The model rests on a low-cost "Judgement Free Zone" value proposition: Classic Card memberships start at $15/month, with the higher-tier PF Black Card (penetration ~66.5%) driving the bulk of dues. Average monthly dues per member run ~$19.50, and system-wide sales reached ~$5.3B in FY2025.

The business runs three reportable segments. Franchise (~$468M FY25 revenue, ~72% segment margin) is the capital-light, highest-margin engine: royalties at an average ~6.7% rate, franchise and ADA fees, placement revenue, and the National Advertising Fund. Corporate-owned clubs (~$546M revenue, ~37% margin) are owned-and-operated locations. Equipment (~$310M revenue) sells gear to franchisees for new clubs and for the contractually mandated re-equipment cycle every ~5-7 years; this segment is lumpy by design and was the source of the Q1 2026 revenue distortion.

The structural advantages are a dominant brand in the value/budget niche, asset-light franchise economics, recurring electronic-funds-transfer dues, a large captive equipment-resale stream, and a high Black Card attach rate. The current debate is whether the pricing leg of that moat is intact.

## Financial Analysis

FY2025 was a solid year on the surface: revenue of $1,324M (+12.1%), net income $219M, operating margin 29.8%, net margin 16.6%, adjusted EBITDA $551.6M, and adjusted diluted EPS of $3.07. Free cash flow of ~$255M represents a ~19% FCF margin — a genuinely profitable, cash-generative business.

Q1 2026 is where the picture turns. Reported revenue of $337.2M (+21.9%) and net income of $51.8M (+23%, EPS $0.65) beat, but the Equipment segment surged +123% to $62.1M on the re-equipment cycle, with gross cost of revenue jumping 101%. Strip that out and the durable metric — same-club sales — slowed to +3.5% (franchised, corporate and system-wide all at +3.5%), down sharply from +6.1% system-wide a year earlier. New sign-ups fell ~36% YoY in the peak January enrollment window. The headline overstated underlying health, which is why the stock dropped on a "beat."

The balance sheet looks alarming on a standardized screen and is not. Stockholders' equity is -$482.8M and the Altman Z-Score reads ~0.98 (distress zone), but both are artifacts of years of large debt-funded buybacks and the Up-C/LLC structure, not operational distress. Long-term debt of ~$2.46B is entirely fixed-rate securitized senior notes through the Planet Fitness Master Issuer, with no maturity wall until ~$923M due in 2029 and ~$397M in 2030, plus $150M of undrawn Variable Funding Notes. Cash and marketable securities of ~$571M provide ample near-term liquidity, and the company is in covenant compliance. Interest expense is rising — FY25 ~$108M, Q1'26 +25.8% YoY to $33M after the December 2025 notes issuance, with FY26 net interest guided to ~$111M — which is a real headwind to net income but not a solvency concern.

Two recurring cash drains warrant flagging. The Tax Receivable Agreement liability of $415.8M owed to pre-IPO owners (including TSG Consumer Partners affiliates) pays out 85% of realized tax benefits, with ~$55M due in 2026. And the securitized debt carries rapid-amortization triggers tied to system-wide sales and DSCR, so a prolonged demand slump (not the current trajectory) would eventually pressure the structure.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has been downgraded by management itself. The revised FY2026 guidance calls for system-wide same-club-sales growth of ~1% (down from a mid-single-digit trajectory), revenue +~7% (cut from ~9%), adjusted EBITDA +~6%, and adjusted net income to decrease ~2% (versus a prior +4-5%). Management withdrew the multi-year algorithm that had anchored the bull case, replacing a 10-15% sales / 15-20% EPS framework with a year of essentially flat earnings.

Estimate revisions confirm the deterioration: current-quarter EPS cut -8.9% (30d), next-quarter -9.5%, current-year -5.1% to $3.20, and next-year -10.5% to $3.61. This is negative earnings momentum, the opposite of the signal that would support a constructive call. The four-quarter beat streak (14.8% surprise in Q1'26, 6.1% and 6.5% in prior quarters) shows management retains some conservatism in guidance, but the direction of revisions matters more than the beats here.

Unit growth remains intact, the offsetting positive. The company still guides to 180-190 new club openings and 150-160 re-equipment deals in 2026, with ~750 contractually committed future clubs in the pipeline. Franchisees continuing to commit capital signals the unit-level economics still work, even as same-club momentum fades. Next-year consensus of $3.61 exceeding the current-year $3.20 means this remains a growing earnings base — slower than the prior algorithm promised, but not contracting.

The pivotal unknown is the 36% sign-up decline against simultaneously raised marketing spend. If the cause is marketing execution (management cited "internal headwinds" and centralized marketing by raising NAF contribution 2%→3% while cutting local marketing 7%→6%), it is fixable. If it is genuine demand erosion at the budget tier — a cash-strapped consumer plus GLP-1 substitution — it is a slower-burning structural problem. There is not yet enough data to resolve this.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing 18.4x multiple overstates the true cost. On freshly-cut consensus, the stock trades at ~15.9x FY26 and ~14.1x FY27 earnings, on a base that is still growing year over year. That is not the multiple of a structurally impaired business in terminal decline; a genuine value trap would carry single-digit forward multiples with contracting earnings. The peer set is thin and heterogeneous — OSW at 33.1x, FUN negative, PRKS at 16.6x — but PLNT's franchise-heavy, high-margin, recurring-revenue model deserves a premium to a regional-amusement-park operator, and ~14-16x is below where this business has historically traded.

Free cash flow against a ~$4.0B market cap is a ~6% FCF yield, which provides absolute support. The $450M of remaining buyback authorization, executed at ~$51 rather than the ~$108.76 average of the December 2025 ASR, is materially accretive at current prices and shrinks the share count into any recovery.

Analyst targets reinforce that the bad news is priced: median $60.5 (~19% above spot), mean $66.6 (~31%), low $45 (~12% below), high $133. Consensus remains 3 strong-buy + 9 buy versus 7 hold and zero sell, with an improving sentiment trend, even after two downgrades (BofA to Neutral, TD Cowen to Hold). I would not lean on consensus as independent confirmation given it lagged the crash, but a constructive-leaning, above-spot consensus does argue against an outright AVOID. The multiple is no longer demanding, but it cannot be called cheap until the same-club and sign-up trajectory stabilizes, because the forward EPS denominator is still being cut.

## Competitive Landscape

Planet Fitness anchors the value/high-volume-low-price gym segment, historically a share-gainer and recession-resilient niche; the budget consumer is supposed to trade into PLNT during downturns, not away from it. The current concern is that the moat is being tested from two directions simultaneously: the budget consumer pulling back at the very segment that should be defensive, and the GLP-1 drug wave potentially dampening gym joins industry-wide.

The decision to pause the Black Card price increase is the most important competitive signal. The bull case for years rested on PLNT's ability to raise Black Card pricing over time given a 66.5% attach rate. Pausing that increase while citing a cash-strapped consumer suggests the company has less pricing latitude than the attach rate implied. If a 66.5% Black Card mix cannot support a modest price increase, the pricing-power premium in the multiple is overstated. Continued franchisee commitment to ~750 new clubs is the counter-evidence that the unit-level model still works. The competitive position is more likely stable-to-slightly-eroding than collapsing, but the trajectory has turned from improving to questionable.

## Risk Assessment

The most acute idiosyncratic risk is the leadership vacuum at a critical moment. The CFO departed in Q1 2026 (Thomas Fitzgerald is interim, with a permanent search ongoing), CEO Colleen Keating is relatively new following the prior CEO transition, and multiple Item 5.02 officer-change 8-Ks hit in February-March 2026. A new CEO, an interim CFO, a withdrawn long-term guidance framework, and a post-drop securities-fraud investigation (BFA Law, focused on marketing claims) form an execution/governance risk cluster that amplifies fundamental uncertainty precisely when clarity is needed.

Other risks: the pricing-ceiling question (structural if confirmed); GLP-1 substitution (secular, sector-wide); franchisee financial health, evidenced by promissory notes to franchisees ($10M Nov 2024, $20M Jan 2026 at SOFR+5.5%); securitized-debt rapid-amortization triggers tied to system-wide sales; auto-renewal/"click-to-cancel" and indoor-tanning regulation; and the rising interest expense compressing net income. The $415.8M TRA liability is a known, modeled cash drain rather than a surprise.

The mitigants are concrete: ~$255M FCF, fixed-rate debt with no near-term wall, ample liquidity, and a 55% drawdown that has discounted much of the visible bad news.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is constructive given the fundamental concerns. Near-term ATM IV is 55.8% versus 49.5% one-year historical vol (modestly rich), with an IV percentile of 32% within the current chain — uncertainty is elevated but not extreme. The put/call skew is -9.3% (call-heavy) at the near tenor, the put/call OI ratio is a very low 0.14, and the put/call volume ratio is 0.78. The call-heavy skew and low put OI suggest bullish-to-neutral positioning, possibly reflecting recovery speculation after the crash. The term structure is flat (55.8% near, 53.9% far), indicating uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than concentrated around a near-term binary. IV rose 7.1pp since mid-May as the price fell 5.1%, consistent with ongoing uncertainty rather than resolution. Short interest of 10.9% of float with only 1.8 days to cover is elevated but not crowded — meaningful bearish institutional positioning, but easily covered, limiting both squeeze potential and the informational weight of the short thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($75-95):** The Q1 weakness is primarily cyclical (a stretched value consumer) plus self-inflicted marketing missteps that new management can fix through the centralized NAF strategy. Same-club sales re-accelerate toward mid-single digits as the consumer stabilizes and marketing execution improves; the Black Card price increase is reinstated in 2027, restoring the pricing-power premium. Unit growth continues at 180-190 clubs/year, the $450M buyback at ~$51 shrinks the share count meaningfully, and the multiple re-rates back toward 20x+ on a recovering EPS base approaching $4+. Insider buying at $46-50 marks the bottom.

**Bear Case ($37-42):** The pricing pause is structural — the budget brand has hit its ceiling, and GLP-1 drugs plus a permanently weaker value consumer cap membership growth. Same-club sales stay near +1%, adjusted net income contracts in 2026 and stagnates beyond, the withdrawn algorithm proves to have been aspirational rather than achievable, and the multiple compresses toward 12x on flat-to-declining earnings. The leadership vacuum delays any turnaround, and the securities investigation creates an overhang. This is -18% to -27% from current levels.

The base case sits in between: a year or more of decelerating-to-flat earnings while the cyclical-vs-structural question resolves, with the stock range-bound around current levels as the ~6% FCF yield and buyback provide a floor and the unresolved pricing question caps upside.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, expected early August) is the critical checkpoint — it will show whether same-club sales and sign-ups stabilized or deteriorated further, and whether the centralized marketing strategy is working.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Same-club sales re-accelerate above +3% with sign-up trends turning positive year-over-year, indicating the demand softness was cyclical/marketing-driven rather than structural.
- Management reinstates the Black Card price increase, or otherwise demonstrates restored pricing power, confirming the moat is intact.
- Estimate revisions turn positive after two consecutive quarters, removing the negative earnings-momentum overhang, ideally with the stock still below ~$55 (i.e., ≤17x forward).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second consecutive quarter of sign-up declines and same-club sales sliding toward zero or negative, confirming structural demand erosion at the budget tier.
- Evidence the securitized-debt rapid-amortization triggers are being approached as system-wide sales weaken.
- Material adverse developments in the securities investigation, or further C-suite departures signaling deeper instability.

**Reassessment point:** Q2 2026 earnings (early August). Given the unresolved cyclical-vs-structural question and roughly symmetric risk/reward, the rating is HOLD pending that data.

## Conclusion

Planet Fitness has been re-priced from a premium-growth compounder to a slower-growth, value-tier operator with a genuinely uncertain demand outlook. The 55% drawdown, the paused Black Card price increase, the withdrawn three-year algorithm, the 36% peak-season sign-up decline, and the leadership vacuum are real and keep this from being a buy. The same drawdown has compressed the stock to ~14-16x forward earnings on a base that is still growing year over year, with a ~6% FCF yield, an accretive buyback at half the price the company paid eighteen months ago, post-crash insider buying by both the CEO and a director, and a consensus that has downgraded but not capitulated, with targets above spot.

The downside is cushioned (bear case -18% to -27%) and the upside is plausible if the weakness proves cyclical (analyst median ~+19%, bull case +47% to +86%). That is a roughly symmetric setup, not an asymmetric short, and the central question — whether the pricing softness is cyclical or structural — is unresolved. The call is HOLD: the bad news is largely priced, but there is no specific catalyst or fundamental evidence to commit capital in either direction until the Q2 print clarifies the demand and pricing trajectory.
