## Executive Summary

EastGroup Properties operates a high-quality portfolio of 550 shallow-bay, last-mile distribution facilities across Sunbelt markets, occupying a distinctive niche between Prologis's big-box logistics and STAG's single-tenant secondary markets. The company delivered strong Q1 2026 results with FFO of $2.34/share (or $2.30/share excluding gain on involuntary conversion and business interruption claims), raised full-year FFO guidance to a midpoint of $9.56/share, and increased development starts guidance to $265M. With 96.5% leased occupancy as of March 31, 2026, re-leasing spreads of 36.8% (straight-line) in Q1 2026, and a balance sheet leveraged at only 3.0x Debt/EBITDAre, the fundamental operating trajectory is among the strongest in the industrial REIT sector.

The stock trades at $203, or approximately 21.2x 2026 forward P/FFO (based on the $9.56 midpoint). This premium to most industrial REIT peers is justified by EGP's superior growth consistency, lower financial risk, and a development platform that generates 150-250bps of value creation above acquisition cap rates. The Q1 beat, guidance raise, and Moody's credit upgrade (to Baa1, stable, in February 2026) provide near-term positive catalysts, while the approximately 8.2% of rent rolling during the remainder of 2026 at significant mark-to-market spreads offers embedded earnings visibility.

The primary risks are tariff-driven tenant demand hesitancy (specifically in logistics/distribution tenants dependent on trade flows), ongoing equity dilution to fund development (24% share count increase over 3 years), and the rate environment's impact on REIT valuations broadly. At current levels, the risk/reward favors accumulation, with a base case target of $225-230 (approximately 24x 2026 FFO) and downside protection from the dividend yield and fortress balance sheet.

## Company Overview

EastGroup Properties is an internally-managed industrial REIT organized in 1969, focused exclusively on shallow-bay distribution facilities (typically 20,000-100,000 sq ft) clustered near transportation infrastructure in supply-constrained Sunbelt submarkets. The company operates across 12 states with core market concentrations in Texas (Houston 9.5%, Dallas 10.9%), Florida (Orlando, Tampa, Jacksonville), Arizona (Phoenix), California (LA, SF), and North Carolina (Charlotte, Raleigh).

The portfolio comprises 65 million square feet across approximately 1,700 leases with no single tenant exceeding 1.5% of annualized base rent. This granular diversification, combined with the multi-tenant shallow-bay format, creates a high-stickiness revenue stream: small tenants face substantial relocation costs relative to rent, providing pricing power on renewals. The company manages this entire operation with just 103 employees, resulting in G&A of approximately 3.3% of revenue.

EGP's integrated development platform is a key differentiator. The company develops spec industrial buildings at projected stabilized yields of approximately 7.9% on the active pipeline (with lease-up projects at 8.3% and under-construction at 7.5%), compared to acquisition cap rates of 5-6%, generating 150-250bps of incremental value per development dollar invested. The 2026 pipeline of $265M in starts, combined with approximately $508M in active development costs, represents a meaningful growth engine beyond organic rent escalation.

## Growth Analysis

EGP's growth comes from four distinct sources, each with varying visibility:

**1. Organic rent growth (highest visibility):** With 40.1% re-leasing spreads in 2025 (36.8% straight-line in Q1 2026) and approximately 8.2% of rent rolling during the remainder of 2026, there is significant embedded revenue growth from marking existing leases to market. This growth source requires no capital investment and is visible 12-18 months ahead.

**2. Development pipeline (moderate visibility):** $265M in 2026 starts at approximately 7.9% projected stabilized yields generate meaningful incremental NOI upon stabilization (typically 12-18 months after delivery). The risk is pre-leasing — the active development/value-add pipeline was 30% leased as of April 21, 2026 (up from 18.8% at year-end 2025), meaning EGP accepts spec development risk but has been making leasing progress.

**3. Acquisitions (lower visibility):** $143M in operating property acquisitions in 2025, plus $119M in land purchases. These are opportunistic and funded via ATM equity issuance at premium-to-NAV pricing.

**4. Same-property occupancy gains (limited upside at current levels):** At 96.5% leased (Q1 2026), there is limited room for occupancy-driven growth. Any demand softening hits this metric first.

Estimate revision data shows positive momentum: current-year EPS estimates rose 12.5% over 90 days (from $5.30 to $5.84) and next-quarter estimates rose 3.1% in 30 days. While the 90-day revision on current-year estimates exceeds 10%, this partially reflects the inclusion of the Q1 beat in annual numbers. Next-year estimates are essentially flat (+1.7% 30-day, -0.2% 90-day), suggesting the market has priced in near-term outperformance but remains uncertain about 2027 trajectory. The FFO metric ($9.56 guided midpoint) is the proper measure for REITs — the EPS figures are less relevant given depreciation distortions.

The structural tailwinds are intact: population migration to Sunbelt markets, potential nearshoring/reshoring demand for domestic distribution space, limited new supply due to construction cost inflation and tighter lending. However, the pace of growth is moderating from the pandemic boom — same-property NOI guidance of 6.1% for 2026 is down from 7.0% achieved in 2025.

## Valuation Assessment

At $203 and 2026 guided FFO midpoint of $9.56/share, EGP trades at approximately 21.2x forward P/FFO. Contextualizing this:

**Peer comparison (P/FFO is the appropriate metric for REITs; GAAP P/E is distorted by depreciation):**
- FR (First Industrial): 23.8x GAAP P/E
- STAG Industrial: 29.5x GAAP P/E
- REXR (Rexford): 37.6x GAAP P/E
- Terreno: 16.6x GAAP P/E
- Peer median GAAP P/E: 27.2x

GAAP P/E figures for REITs include depreciation distortions and are less meaningful than P/FFO. On a P/FFO basis, EGP's approximately 21.2x forward multiple is at a premium to most industrial REIT peers, justified by: (a) superior same-store growth trajectory, (b) substantially lower leverage, (c) development yield spread of 150-250bps, (d) consistent multi-year FFO growth record. However, the premium is well-recognized by the market and leaves limited margin of safety.

**Dividend yield:** Approximately 3.1% ($6.20 annualized / $203) — below the 3.9% peer median, reflecting the growth premium embedded in the stock.

**NAV considerations:** With acquisition cap rates in the 5-6% range for quality industrial, and Q1 2026 annualized adjusted cash PNOI of approximately $590M, an NAV analysis suggests the stock trades in the vicinity of NAV. This is typical for high-quality REITs with active development programs that create value above market cap rates, though a precise NAV estimate requires adjustments for development pipeline value, land holdings, and projected costs that are beyond the scope of this simplified analysis.

**Valuation verdict:** EGP is fairly valued on current fundamentals but has visible catalysts for re-rating — specifically the continued re-leasing spread monetization and development deliveries. The approximately 21.2x forward multiple is not cheap by absolute standards, but relative to the quality, growth visibility, and balance sheet strength, it does not appear stretched for a REIT sector that typically commands 18-25x FFO for best-in-class operators.

## Competitive Landscape

EGP occupies a defensible niche within the industrial REIT ecosystem:

- **Prologis (PLD):** Big-box (500K+ sf), global scale, e-commerce focused. Different tenant profile and competitive dynamics.
- **First Industrial (FR):** More geographic overlap with EGP in Sunbelt, but includes larger-format and more diversified property types.
- **Rexford (REXR):** Pure-play infill Southern California. Higher rents but concentrated in a single market with regulatory headwinds.
- **STAG Industrial:** Single-tenant net lease with lower growth but simpler operations. Secondary/tertiary market focus.
- **Terreno (TRNO):** Six coastal markets only, similar shallow-bay focus but different geographic bet.

EGP's competitive advantages are compounding: (1) the shallow-bay format creates sticky tenant relationships (small tenants face disproportionate relocation costs), (2) multi-tenant diversification eliminates concentration risk, (3) the Sunbelt focus captures structural migration tailwinds, and (4) the integrated development platform creates value that external acquirers cannot replicate easily. The 103-employee lean structure means operating cost advantages compound year over year.

The key competitive vulnerability is overbuilding in Texas markets (Houston, Dallas represent 20.4% of rent combined). If speculative development saturates these markets, EGP's re-leasing spreads could narrow. However, current construction starts have moderated industry-wide due to higher capital costs, partially protecting the supply dynamic.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Tariff/Trade Policy Uncertainty (Moderate Impact):**
Management explicitly flagged tariffs as creating "a cloud of uncertainty" around leasing decisions. Many EGP tenants are logistics/distribution companies dependent on trade flows. Severe trade disruptions could reduce demand for warehousing space. Counter-thesis: nearshoring/reshoring may benefit Sunbelt markets near Mexican border and ports, though this remains a macro hypothesis rather than a company-quantified demand driver. Net assessment: modest headwind to new leasing velocity, potentially offset by reshoring tailwind.

**2. Development Spec Risk (Moderate Impact):**
The active development/value-add pipeline of approximately $508M was 30% pre-leased as of April 21, 2026. In a demand downturn, newly delivered space could sit vacant for 6-12 months longer than planned, depressing near-term FFO. Mitigant: EGP has successfully executed spec development for decades with consistent stabilized yields above acquisition cap rates; the 96.5% portfolio occupancy provides confidence in demand depth.

**3. Rate Environment (Moderate Impact):**
While EGP's existing debt is locked at 3.43%, new debt (4.1-4.2%) is modestly dilutive to returns. More importantly, elevated rates compress REIT valuations broadly through the cap rate channel. A sustained higher rate environment creates a headwind to valuation expansion.

**4. Equity Dilution (Low-Moderate Impact):**
24% share count growth over 3 years is significant. While dilution is accretive when shares are issued above NAV (they generally are at $203), a market correction that takes the stock below NAV would make equity issuance destructive, potentially stalling the development program. The $1B ATM program (approximately $880M remaining capacity as of April 21, 2026) provides ongoing issuance capacity.

**5. Concentration in Sunbelt (Low Impact):**
Geographic concentration in Texas/Florida creates exposure to regional economic cycles. However, the diversity within Sunbelt (12 states, multiple MSAs) and the multi-tenant model provide significant resilience.

## Conclusion

EastGroup Properties merits a BUY recommendation at $203. The combination of a fortress balance sheet (3.0x leverage, 14.8x interest coverage, 3.43% locked-in debt rate), consistent multi-year FFO growth, 36-40% re-leasing spreads providing embedded growth visibility, and an integrated development platform yielding approximately 7.5-8.3% creates a quality franchise that compounds value through cycles. The stock trades at approximately 21.2x forward P/FFO — a premium to peers but justified by materially superior quality and growth metrics.

The specific catalyst justifying BUY over HOLD: the Q1 2026 beat plus guidance raise demonstrates continued operational momentum, the Moody's upgrade to Baa1 reduces future borrowing costs, and approximately 8.2% of rent rolling during the remainder of 2026 at significant mark-to-market spreads provides strong earnings growth visibility through the next 12 months. The primary risks (tariff-driven demand hesitancy, rate environment headwinds to REIT valuations, occupancy moderation from 97% to 96.5%) are real but manageable given the balance sheet and don't justify remaining on the sideline with these growth drivers intact.

The approximately 3.1% dividend yield ($6.20 annualized) provides downside cushion, and the stock's proximity to its 52-week high ($203.98) after a 22.8% move from the 52-week low of $159.37 reflects the market's recognition of operational quality. With continued estimate revisions upward and visible growth catalysts, further appreciation is supported by fundamentals.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Q1 2026 occupancy has declined to 96.5% leased (from 97.0% at year-end), Q1 re-leasing spreads moderated to 36.8% (from 40.1% full-year 2025), and the current dividend yield is 3.1% (not 2.8%), which collectively soften — but do not invalidate — the growth and valuation underpinnings of the BUY recommendation.
