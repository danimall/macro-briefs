**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

COPT Defense Properties is the rare office REIT that grows through the office cycle. Its portfolio sits next to or inside U.S. Government defense installations — Fort Meade/BW Corridor (44.5% of ARR, anchored by NSA/Cyber Command), Lackland AFB, Redstone Arsenal, and Northern Virginia — and serves tenants who often must locate adjacent to their missions. The result is occupancy at or above 93% for nine consecutive years, tenant retention at or above 75% for ten consecutive years (90.8% in Q1 2026), and FFO that rose 5.8% in FY2025 to $2.72/share while conventional office landlords contend with remote-work erosion and valuation collapse. The defense-spending backdrop is favorable: FY2026 appropriations rose, and additional defense money flowed through the One Big Beautiful Bill Act in July 2025.

The investment question is not whether the business is high quality — it is, and the balance sheet confirms it (investment-grade across all three agencies, ~3.4% blended fixed-rate cost, only ~$45.6M of debt maturing in 2026, ~44.6% FFO payout). The question is whether the current price compensates for two specific near-term frictions. First, consensus FY2026 EPS estimates have been revised down ~2.8% over 30 days and current-year FFO momentum is flat-to-negative even as the company raised guidance — the Street and management are not fully aligned. Second, 19.3% of ARR expires in 2026, of which 83.3% is government leases, including large Fort Meade rolls and 100% of Lackland; management expects to renew "virtually all," but that is an execution-dependent assumption set against a year that already included a 43-day federal shutdown.

At $31.61, CDP trades at roughly 11.5x the $2.76 FFO guidance midpoint, near its 52-week high of $33.33, with a consensus mean target of $35.63 implying ~13% upside plus a 4.0% yield. That is a solid total-return profile for a defensive holder, but not an asymmetric, mispriced setup that justifies a high-conviction long. We rate CDP a HOLD with a growth-oriented rationale and would upgrade on confirmed clean execution of the 2026 government lease renewals or a pullback toward the high-$28s.

## Company Overview

COPT Defense Properties (formerly Corporate Office Properties Trust, a REIT since 1992) is a self-managed, fully-integrated REIT focused on real estate proximate to or inside key U.S. Government defense installations and missions. As of 3/31/2026 the portfolio comprises:

- 201 Defense/IT operating properties totaling 23.2M sq ft (24 in unconsolidated JVs)
- 6 non-strategic "Other" office properties (2.0M sq ft, earmarked for eventual sale)
- 7 properties under development (~1.0M sq ft) and ~980 acres of controlled land (potential ~10.8M sq ft of future development)

Defense/IT represents 92.1% of square footage and 90.3% of ARR (total ARR ~$728M). By sub-segment: Fort Meade/BW Corridor (44.5%), NoVA Defense/IT (13.7%), Lackland AFB San Antonio (10.1%), Redstone Arsenal Huntsville (8.8%), Data Center Shells in N. Virginia (8.4%), Navy Support (4.8%).

The moat is concrete rather than narrative. Properties sit adjacent to demand drivers tenants frequently require for mission execution; tenant-funded secured-space buildouts (SCIFs, anti-terrorism/force-protection hardening, critical power) create high switching costs; more than a third of the 430-person workforce holds government clearances; and security-driven in-office requirements insulate the portfolio from the remote-work demand destruction afflicting conventional office. A second growth engine is the data-center-shell business in Northern Virginia, the world's largest data-center market, which gives CDP secular exposure to AI/cloud capacity expansion.

## Financial Analysis

A data caveat must be addressed up front, because automated screens misrepresent this company. An XBRL pull labeled CDP's revenue as ~$42M; that figure is only the construction/service line. Actual FY2025 total revenue was $763.9M ($721.8M real estate + $42.1M services). The Altman Z-Score of 0.76 ("distress") is not meaningful for a leveraged, asset-heavy REIT; it penalizes the structural model. CDP is investment-grade rated by all three agencies with stable outlooks. Any screen flagging CDP as a bankruptcy risk is a false positive.

**FY2025 results:**
- Total revenue $763.9M (+1.4%); real estate revenue +6.5% to $721.8M
- Net income $159.5M (+10.8%); diluted EPS $1.34 (vs. $1.23)
- Diluted FFO/share (as adjusted) $2.72, +5.8% YoY
- Real estate NOI +6.4% to $445.6M; Same-Property NOI +3.6% (+$14.9M)
- Operating cash flow $309.9M

**Q1 2026:**
- FFO/share $0.69 (vs. $0.65; beat $0.68 consensus)
- Portfolio 94.4% occupied, 95.2% leased; Defense/IT 95.6%
- Tenant retention 90.8%
- Same-Property NOI +3.1%; Defense/IT Same-Property NOI +4.7%
- 2026 FFO/share guidance midpoint raised to $2.76; ~$290M committed to new investments
- Net income $40.1M (+10.8%)

**Balance sheet and capital structure:**
- Total debt $2.55B (down from $2.77B after repaying the $400M 2.25% notes in March 2026)
- D/E ~2.06x, normal for a REIT; weighted average effective rate ~3.4%, almost entirely fixed
- Well-laddered maturities: only ~$45.6M remaining in 2026, nothing significant until 2028 ($345M), then 2029 ($728M)
- Liquidity: ~$610M on the $800M revolver + ~$62M on the $200M development facility (3/31/26)
- FFO payout ~44.6%, leaving substantial coverage and retained development cash; dividend raised to $0.32/quarter (from $0.305)
- In compliance with all covenants

Capital allocation has been shareholder-friendly: development-led, mostly pre-leased growth funded primarily by retained cash flow and the new dedicated development facility, with the $300M ATM program fully unused as of Q1 2026, holding dilution to a minimum. Earnings quality is strong (fixed-rate debt, laddered maturities). Financial Health scores 7.0.

## Growth Analysis

The growth case rests on three pillars. First, the defense-spending tailwind: rising FY2026 appropriations plus OBBBA defense funding support demand across priority missions (ISR, cyber, missile defense, space). Second, the development pipeline: CDP placed 468K sq ft (three fully-leased properties) into service in 2025, committed $233.4M to four new developments (three fully pre-leased), and has ~10.8M sq ft of future development potential on controlled land. Third, the data-center-shell business, which rides the Northern Virginia AI/cloud buildout and has made CDP a dual defense/data-center play.

The cross-current is consensus estimate direction. Despite the guidance raise and the Q1 FFO beat, GAAP EPS estimates have softened: current-year consensus moved to $1.33 from $1.37 thirty days ago (-2.8%) and from $1.34 ninety days ago (-0.5%), and next-quarter and next-year estimates were trimmed -2.9% and -1.4% respectively over 30 days. The earnings-surprise record is mixed — one beat, two in-line, one miss across the last four quarters, an in-line operator rather than a serial beater. A ~1.5% implied FFO growth into the 2026 midpoint is steady single-digit compounding, not acceleration.

This matters for scoring discipline. The strong forward signal this system weights most heavily — estimate revisions of +30% or more combined with revenue growth above 30% — is absent here. CDP shows the opposite: low-single-digit revenue growth and modestly falling consensus. The growth thesis is durable and defensible, but it is mid-single-digit and currently facing negative revision momentum. Growth Prospects scores 6.0.

## Valuation Assessment

At $31.61, CDP trades at ~11.5x the $2.76 FFO guidance midpoint and ~23.2x trailing GAAP EPS (the latter is a poor REIT metric given depreciation). The 4.0% dividend yield is well-covered at a ~45% payout.

Peer context cuts both ways. The Office REIT cohort is heterogeneous and largely distressed — SLG, CUZ, and DEI carry negative P/Es (impairment-driven), and the peer median yield is 6.3% versus CDP's 4.0%. CDP's lower yield reflects exactly what investors should pay for: growing rather than declining cash flow, 90%+ retention, and a defense-spending backdrop uncorrelated to the office cycle. On the discount-to-52-week-high comparison, CDP sits ~5% below its high while peers trade 14% to 33% below theirs. The quality premium is real and deserved.

The problem is that the market already awards it. CDP is one of the few names in its sector trading near its high, on rising-but-modest FFO, at a forward FFO multiple that already embeds the defensive premium. The consensus mean target of $35.63 (low $33.00, high $38.00) implies ~13% price upside; total return with the dividend is ~17%. That is attractive for a defensive holder but does not represent a specific mispricing the market is missing. There is no identifiable catalyst within 60 days that re-rates the multiple, and consensus estimates are drifting down, not up — the conditions under which analyst targets act as floors (cheap valuation + rising estimates) are not met here. On forward FFO the stock is fairly valued, neither stretched nor compelling. Valuation scores 5.5.

## Competitive Landscape

CDP occupies a near-unique niche with few true public comparables, which is why bucketing it as a generic "Office REIT" misleads. Its competitive position is strengthening on trajectory: it dominates business parks adjacent to installations, controls developable land near demand drivers, and possesses secured-space construction expertise and clearance-holding staff that competitors cannot easily replicate. Defense/IT occupancy of 95.6% and 90.8% Q1 retention against a backdrop of 2025 federal workforce reductions demonstrate the moat working under stress.

The competitive vulnerability is concentrated dependency rather than displacement. A single unnamed Fortune 100 cloud customer leases 33 data center shells (6.3M sq ft, 11.3% of ARR) and is now CDP's #2 tenant. This relationship is both a growth engine, funding power and fiber infrastructure and raising exit barriers, and a concentration risk; loss of future development allocations would remove a meaningful growth and capital-recycling channel. On balance the trajectory is positive and the moat is widening through development, supporting a Competitive Position score of 7.0.

## Risk Assessment

The central thesis risk is concentration. USG is 35.4% of ARR; top-3 tenants 51.1%; top-10 64.4%; 90.3% of ARR depends on defense/national-security spending. Most USG leases are one-year terms with renewal options and Congressional-funding termination rights.

The most pressing near-term item is the 2026 lease expiration schedule. Management expects to renew "virtually all," and the model's history supports that confidence: through the 2025 federal shutdown and federal workforce reductions, rent kept flowing and occupancy rose. The renewal cadence is the key execution watch item over the next two to three quarters, and a year-on-year ARR step-down on any meaningful non-renewal would directly pressure the FFO growth thesis.

Secondary risks: single data-center customer dependency (above); geographic concentration in the Mid-Atlantic/Greater DC-Baltimore region; the "Other" segment drag (six legacy offices, 9.7% of ARR, 76.6% occupied, hard to sell at acceptable prices); new Maryland energy-performance standards affecting roughly half the portfolio with potential penalties and non-recoverable capex; and explicitly flagged cyber-target risk given the national-security tenant base. The Q1 2026 10-Q reports no material changes to risk factors versus the 10-K.

These risks are real and partly priced — the discount to a true data-center REIT multiple reflects the government dependency. The 2026 government-lease roll combined with negative estimate revisions is a specific, not-fully-resolved overhang, which holds Risk Factors at 5.5.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing rising near-term uncertainty. ATM implied volatility jumped from 46.8% to 64.8% (+18.1pp) between late May and early June while the price softened ~2.2%. Rising IV alongside a flat-to-lower price typically signals the market pricing an upcoming catalyst or increased event risk — here plausibly the 2026 lease-renewal cadence and the next earnings print. This argues against initiating new long exposure at current levels and is consistent with waiting for renewal confirmation. Short interest is moderate at 7.1% of float (6.2 days to cover), neither a crowded short nor a squeeze setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** CDP renews "virtually all" of its 2026 government leases, ARR steps up, FFO compounds at mid-single digits toward and above the midpoint, and the development pipeline (three of four new projects pre-leased) extends visible growth. Defense budgets continue rising with bipartisan support, the data-center-shell business expands with the NoVA AI buildout, and the market re-rates CDP closer to a hybrid defense/data-center multiple. Estimates inflect upward as guidance proves conservative. The stock reaches the $38 high target (~20% upside) plus a growing 4% dividend.

**Bear Case.** A federal budget impasse or workforce reduction delays or shrinks 2026 renewals; the single Fortune 100 data-center customer reduces future allocations; the "Other" segment continues to drag and cannot be sold; Maryland energy penalties add non-recoverable capex; and persistent negative estimate revisions compress the multiple toward the peer group. The stock falls toward the high-$20s, with the low analyst target of $33 proving to be more of a current-value anchor than a floor. Downside to ~$27 is roughly -15%.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth rationale, reassessed at the next earnings release.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Confirmed clean execution of 2026 government lease renewals — retention at or above the high-80s% with ARR holding or stepping up through the heavy Fort Meade/Lackland roll, reported across Q2/Q3 2026.
2. Consensus FY2026 FFO estimates turning upward (positive 30-day revisions), reversing the current -2.8% drift.
3. A pullback toward the high-$28s, which would push forward FFO yield toward ~9.5% and total-return upside to base case above ~25%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. A material non-renewal in the 2026 USG expiration schedule (Fort Meade or Lackland) that produces a year-on-year ARR decline.
2. Loss or material reduction of the Fortune 100 data-center customer relationship or its future development allocations.
3. FFO guidance cut, or dividend coverage deteriorating with payout rising toward 70%+.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026), and again at year-end once the bulk of the 2026 renewal schedule has cleared.

## Conclusion

COPT Defense Properties is a high-quality, differentiated REIT with a genuine moat, a fortress-grade balance sheet, and a defense-spending tailwind that decouples it from the structural decline plaguing conventional office. The business is doing what its thesis promises: growing FFO, raising the dividend, retaining 90%+ of tenants, and managing its maturities prudently.

The HOLD is a function of price and timing, not quality. The stock trades near its 52-week high at ~11.5x forward FFO with the market already awarding the quality premium; consensus FY estimates are drifting down; 19.3% of ARR (83% government) rolls in 2026 and has yet to clear; and rising implied volatility signals the market is pricing event risk into the renewal cadence. The consensus mean target offers ~13% upside plus a 4% yield — a respectable defensive total return, but not the asymmetric, identifiable mispricing required for a higher-conviction long at this entry. We would move to BUY on confirmed clean 2026 renewal execution with estimates inflecting upward, or on a pullback toward the high-$28s. For existing holders, the dividend is well-covered and the thesis intact.
