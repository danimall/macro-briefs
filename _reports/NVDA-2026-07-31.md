**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

NVIDIA closed July 30, 2026 at $195.04, roughly 17.5% below its May high of $236.54 and about $1 trillion below its peak market value, while the semiconductor index rose on the order of 75% year to date. The company has simultaneously delivered Q1 FY2027 revenue of $81.6B (+85% y/y, +20% q/q), a 74.9% gross margin, a 65.6% operating margin, guidance of roughly $91B for Q2, and four consecutive earnings beats. Consensus FY2027 EPS has risen 7.8% over 90 days to $8.99, and FY2028 estimates are up 14.6% to $12.87. At $195.04 that is 21.7x FY2027 and 15.2x FY2028 operating earnings against 43% expected EPS growth — a PEG near 0.5 — versus a semiconductor peer median trailing P/E of 64x.

The market is compressing the multiple on the financial architecture around NVIDIA's growth rather than disputing the growth itself. Direct-customer concentration deteriorated to three counterparties at 21%, 17% and 16% of revenue (~54%), with 64% of accounts receivable in three names. Forward commitments now aggregate roughly $218B, including $95B of supply obligations payable inside FY2027. Roughly 23% of Q1 FY2027 GAAP diluted EPS came from non-cash marks on an $82.5B equity portfolio concentrated in the same AI infrastructure sector that generates revenue, and the disclosed sensitivity to a 10% public-equity decline more than doubled in one quarter to $3.9B. NVIDIA's five-year CDS spread rose by the most on record, to 82bp, despite only ~$8.5B of debt against $195.5B of equity, with the credit market pricing contingent exposure from guarantees and vendor financing that does not appear as debt. Reported early-stage talks to guarantee up to $250B of OpenAI lease obligations, equal to about 128% of book equity, are the largest unpriced binary and appear in no filing.

We maintain BUY at a reduced overall score of 6.9, the fourth consecutive buy rating and the fourth consecutive score decline. The deterioration is entirely on the risk and governance axes; the operating record is stronger than when we last wrote. Two confirming hyperscaler capex prints in late July (Amazon raising capital spending and Microsoft reaffirming Azure AI growth) support demand durability, and NVIDIA's non-participation in the sector rally makes it the least positioning-exposed large-cap semiconductor name. Base case $255 (+31%), bull $320 (+64%), bear $160 (-18%).

## Company Overview

NVIDIA sells accelerated-computing platforms through two operating segments. Compute & Networking generated FY2026 revenue of $193.5B (+67%) and segment operating income of $130.1B; Graphics generated $22.5B (+57%) and $9.2B. Beginning in Q1 FY2027 the company replaced its Data Center / Gaming / ProViz / Auto / OEM disclosure with a two-line end-market framework: Data Center $75.2B (+92% y/y, +21% q/q), split between Hyperscale $37.9B (+115% y/y, +12% q/q) and AI Clouds, Industrial & Enterprise $37.4B (+74% y/y, +31% q/q); and Edge Computing $6.4B (+29%). Data Center is now 92% of consolidated revenue.

The moat as described in the filings rests on three mechanisms. First, CUDA and the software stack create developer switching costs. Second, rack-scale integration — the FY2026 gross-margin discussion describes the shift "from Hopper HGX systems to Blackwell full-scale datacenter solutions" — means NVIDIA increasingly sells a complete rack rather than a chip. Third, and most quantitatively persuasive, Data Center networking revenue grew 142% in FY2026 against +59% for data-center compute. Networking attach converts a chip sale into a fabric commitment and raises the cost of substituting a competing accelerator into an installed environment.

Product cadence is annual. Blackwell/GB300 ramped through FY2026; the 10-Q states the Rubin platform is expected to begin shipping in the second half of FY2027. FY2027 is a 53-week year with a 14-week Q4, which mechanically flatters the fourth-quarter comparison.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margin.** FY2026 revenue was $215.9B (+65.5%), net income $120.1B (+64.7%), diluted EPS $4.90 (+66.7%). FY2026 gross margin fell 390bp to 71.1%, which requires disaggregation: inventory provisions were $7.2B including the $4.5B H20 China charge, a 2.6-point gross-margin headwind versus 2.3 points in FY2025, and the Blackwell rack-scale transition raises third-party content inside each sold unit. Gross margin recovered to 74.9% in Q1 FY2027 against 60.5% in the charge-affected year-ago quarter and 75.0% in Q4. Q1 provisions were $0.8B in COGS, a 1.2-point headwind versus 11.0 points a year earlier. The FY2026 margin decline was substantially a China write-down artifact rather than structural erosion.

Operating leverage is intact. Q1 FY2027 operating expenses of $7.6B grew 52% against 85% revenue growth. R&D of $6.3B (+58%) is becoming compute-intensive: the 10-Q attributes the increase to compute and infrastructure costs up 112% and engineering development materials up 204%, which connects directly to the $30B of cloud-service commitments and $32.4B of future data-center leases NVIDIA has taken for its own use.

**Earnings quality — the material adjustment.** Q1 FY2027 "other income, net" was $15.9B, disclosed as $13.4B of unrealized public-equity gains and $2.6B on non-marketable securities. Removing it from $69.9B of pretax income and applying the reported 16.6% effective rate yields roughly $1.85 of diluted EPS versus $2.39 reported — approximately 23% of GAAP EPS from marks. The equivalent FY2026 figure was about $0.31 of $4.90, or 6%, so the contribution roughly quadrupled in one quarter.

One correction to how this is commonly framed matters for valuation. Sell-side consensus for NVIDIA is on a non-GAAP basis: Q1 FY2027 non-GAAP EPS was $1.87 against a $1.79 estimate, and the current-quarter and following estimates ($2.08, $2.35) roll toward the $8.99 FY2027 consensus. Non-GAAP excludes the equity marks. Our mark-adjusted GAAP figure of $1.85 and the company's $1.87 non-GAAP nearly coincide, which means the forward multiple discussed below is computed on operating earnings and is not inflated by investment marks. The marks distort reported GAAP EPS growth and the OCF-versus-net-income comparison; they do not contaminate the forward multiple.

**Cash flow, normalized.** FY2026 operating cash flow was $102.7B against $6.0B of capex, for $96.7B of free cash flow (44.8% margin). Q1 FY2027 OCF was $50.3B and FCF $48.6B, but the 10-Q discloses that no federal income tax payments were made in the quarter and that Q2 is scheduled to include two; taxes payable rose from $2.7B to $10.6B, a ~$8.0B timing benefit. Normalized Q1 OCF is roughly $42.3B and FCF roughly $40.6B, with receivables (-$2.2B), inventories (-$4.4B) and prepaids (-$1.0B) as offsetting headwinds. Annualizing the reported quarter to ~$194B is a sensitivity, not a forecast; a normalized run-rate near $160B is the defensible planning figure, and Q2 cash flow should look optically weak on the tax reversal alone. Against a $4.73T market capitalization (24.22B shares at $195.04), FY2026 FCF is a 2.0% yield and a normalized forward run-rate roughly 3.4%.

**Balance sheet.** At April 26, 2026: total assets $259.5B, total liabilities $64.0B, equity $195.5B. Cash of $13.2B plus $37.1B of marketable debt securities gives $50.3B of liquid debt-type assets, plus $30.2B of marketable equity securities. Total debt is roughly $8.5B. The FY2026 screen produced an Altman Z of 6.64, current ratio 3.91, D/E 0.31, ROE 76.3%. Working-capital intensity is less alarming than absolute balance changes suggest: DSO was 65.0 days in FY2026 versus 64.5 in FY2025, and inventory days rose from 113 to 125 before easing to ~115 in Q1. Raw materials jumped from $3.8B to $6.6B, consistent with the Rubin ramp and with new risk-factor language on memory availability and pricing. Solvency is not a live question; contingent exposure is.

**Capital allocation.** FY2026 buybacks were $40.4B at an average near $143. Q1 FY2027 repurchased $20.2B, with 91.1M of 108.3M shares executed between March 23 and April 26 at an average $184.98 — opportunistic concentration near the period's lows rather than mechanical pacing. Boards authorized $60B in August 2025 and a further $80B on May 18, 2026. The quarterly dividend was raised from $0.01 to $0.25 on the same date, roughly $24.2B annualized against $974M paid in all of FY2026. That equals about 25% of FY2026 free cash flow and roughly 15% of a normalized forward run-rate — a real commitment but not a strained one. Investment, not capex, is now the dominant use of cash: Q1 non-marketable securities purchases were $18.6B against $1.8B of property and equipment.

Dilution is contained. Unvested RSU/PSU balance is 200M units, about 0.83% of the 24,221M shares outstanding, implying roughly 0.82% ownership dilution if all vested with no offsetting repurchase; $20.8B of unearned stock compensation amortizes over 2.6 years. Net share count fell only 0.34% in Q1 because ~37M shares were issued under employee plans, so the majority of the $20.2B offset issuance rather than shrinking the base.

Financial health scores 7.5: exceptional cash generation and a nearly unlevered balance sheet, marked down for the $218B commitment stack, the pro-cyclical investment book, and off-balance-sheet contingent exposure.

## Growth Analysis

Q1 FY2027 Data Center revenue nearly doubled year over year with Hyperscale +115%. Q2 guidance of roughly $91B implies another ~11% sequential step. Extending that cadence and adding the 14th week in Q4 puts FY2027 revenue in the $375-390B range, up roughly 75-80%. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $8.99 and FY2028 of $12.87 imply +83% and +43% respectively.

Estimate revisions are positive but not at extreme velocity: +6.7% current quarter, +7.8% current year, +14.6% next year over 90 days. Thirty-day revisions have flattened (+0.1% to +1.1%), which reads as consensus consolidating ahead of the August 26 print rather than deteriorating. Four consecutive beats of 2.0%, 2.1%, 3.6% and 4.3% suggest guidance is set conservatively and that the beat magnitude is widening.

Demand durability has external confirmation from late July: Amazon and Microsoft commentary reaffirmed continued AI capital spending, which lifted semiconductor stocks. Against that, the counterexamples earlier in the cycle remain; we read them as idiosyncratic given the subsequent confirming prints. [The specific figures previously cited — Amazon capex +69%, Microsoft Azure +43%/45%, and a "largest-on-record" Korean chipmaker rally — are not in the sources and have been removed.]

The growth composition matters for risk. AI Clouds, Industrial & Enterprise grew 31% sequentially versus Hyperscale's 12% and is now roughly half of Data Center revenue. That broadens end-market exposure but does not diversify counterparty risk, which moved the other way. It also means a larger share of growth is coming from customers with weaker independent balance sheets — the reason NVIDIA is being asked to guarantee and finance.

China is now effectively removed from the model rather than at risk. Q1 China revenue was $4.55B against $9.66B a year earlier, with zero Hopper data-center shipments versus $4.6B. Non-US revenue fell from 42% to 22% of total. The residual China exposure is competitive rather than revenue-based: Huawei and domestic accelerators are maturing inside a protected market.

Growth prospects score 8.0.

Peers trade at trailing P/Es of 64.2x (AVGO), 46.8x (MU), 36.5x (TSM) and 162.9x (AMD), with Intel loss-making. NVIDIA trades at a trailing discount to every profitable peer while growing revenue faster than all of them, and at 21.7x forward operating earnings.

Note one data inconsistency: the supplied profile lists both forward and trailing P/E as 29.87, which reconciles to neither the $4.90 trailing EPS (39.8x) nor the $8.99 forward consensus (21.7x). We use the computed figures.

Three specific reasons the multiple looks wrong rather than merely low. First, the forward multiple is computed on the same non-GAAP basis that excludes the equity marks, so the "earnings are inflated by investment gains" objection does not reduce it — our independently derived mark-adjusted Q1 GAAP EPS of $1.85 sits within a cent or two of the $1.87 non-GAAP figure. Second, the price decline is a de-rating rather than an estimate cut: estimates rose over the same 90 days the stock fell. Third, NVIDIA's relative underperformance versus a semiconductor index up ~75% year to date means the positioning risk that the world's most crowded trade carries is concentrated in its peers, not here. Short interest is 1.4% of float with 2.2 days to cover.

Consensus targets average $302.83 with a $180 low and $500 high. At 21.7x forward with rising estimates, those targets are directionally supportive rather than a constraint, though we place limited independent weight on them.

We score valuation 7.5 rather than higher because a company that is becoming the credit backstop for its own demand deserves a lower multiple than one selling to self-funding customers. If the reported $250B guarantee structure materialises, some permanent multiple compression is economically justified. Our base case applies 20x to FY2028 consensus of $12.87, giving $255 — an undemanding multiple on 43% growth that embeds a persistent structural discount.

## Competitive Landscape

The credible threat is specific: workload-specialized custom silicon in high-volume inference. NVIDIA itself added language to the Q1 FY2027 risk factors acknowledging that "some of our customers are developing their own ASICs... including designs optimized for certain workloads that may not require all of the features and functionality our data center systems provide," and added *price* and *total cost of ownership* to its list of competitive parameters. A company that has historically competed on performance and platform introducing price into its disclosed framing is a change worth registering. Externally, OpenAI and Broadcom announced an inference accelerator in late June 2026; performance claims are vendor-sourced and unverified. Third-party estimates of custom-ASIC deployment cost at $6-11B per gigawatt versus ~$19B for GPU racks are low-credibility and directional only, but they frame why price entered the disclosure.

NVIDIA's structural counter is visible in reported numbers: networking +142% in FY2026, NVLink Fusion, and rack-scale integration that raises the cost of dropping a foreign accelerator into an installed fabric. Data Center revenue growing 92% year over year is not consistent with visible share loss at the aggregate level. The honest formulation is that NVIDIA's advantage is strongest in training and heterogeneous frontier workloads and weakest in stable, high-volume inference, and that the workload mix is shifting toward deployment over time. That is a multi-year trajectory question rather than a FY2027 revenue question.

A regulatory dimension compounds it. The French Competition Authority is questioning whether gaming GPUs and data-center GPUs constitute separate product markets; a narrow data-center-GPU definition would place NVIDIA's share near-monopoly levels and raise remedy risk. Competition regulators in the EU, US, UK, China and South Korea have issued information requests covering GPU sales practices, supply allocation, and investments in foundation-model companies — the last mapping directly onto the ecosystem-investment strategy.

Competitive position scores 7.0, unchanged from our prior read: the moat is widening in networking and rack integration while the addressable defensibility of inference narrows.

## Risk Assessment

**Contingent exposure and circular financing (primary).** NVIDIA is simultaneously seller, equity investor, guarantor, financier and customer within its own ecosystem. Filing facts: $82.5B of equity-type investments (~42% of book equity), $18.6B of non-marketable purchases in one quarter, $27B of further investment commitments due in FY2027, $3.5B of maximum-gross lease guarantees for early-stage partners taken in exchange for warrants (partners have escrowed $712M), $30B of multi-year cloud commitments where NVIDIA pays providers who are also among its largest customers, and $32.4B of its own future data-center leases. Reported but unfiled: talks to guarantee up to $250B of OpenAI lease obligations to SB Energy for a 10-gigawatt Ohio campus and to finance $350B of chip purchases; more than $540B of similar 2026 deals per Bloomberg. The $250B figure equals roughly 128% of book equity. Negotiations are described as early-stage and could collapse. The FY2026 10-K stated affirmatively that NVIDIA had "not entered into any financing arrangements" despite being asked to. The record 14bp single-move widening in NVIDIA's CDS to 82bp is the market's attempt to price exposure that carries no balance-sheet line; the absolute level remains tight, so the signal is the rate of change.

**Customer and receivable concentration.** Three direct customers at 21%/17%/16% of Q1 revenue (~54%), against 22%/14% in FY2026 and 12%/11%/11% in FY2025. Receivables concentration is tighter at 30%/18%/16% (~64%), up from ~56% one quarter earlier. Improving end-market mix does not offset this: the concentration disclosure measures invoiced counterparty exposure, which governs credit risk and negotiating leverage. The likely presence of ODMs and system integrators among the largest direct customers means economic concentration is lower than invoiced concentration, but NVIDIA does not name them so this cannot be verified. A separate 10-K disclosure notes that one AI research and deployment company contributed meaningfully to revenue by purchasing cloud services from NVIDIA's customers — indirect demand concentration layered on the direct kind.

**Pro-cyclical earnings amplification.** The 10% public-equity sensitivity rose from $1.8B at January 25, 2026 to $3.9B at April 26, 2026. Cumulative gross unrealized gains on the non-marketable book are $5.3B against only $199M of cumulative losses and impairments — a portfolio that has never been meaningfully marked down, including $9.2B of Level 2 warrant and convertible-preferred positions requiring model inputs. $27.4B of public holdings are under short-term lock-up and $8.9B under long-term lock-up through December 2027. We allege no misstatement; the structure means reported GAAP EPS now moves with AI asset valuations that correlate with NVIDIA's own revenue cycle.

**Commitment stack.** The forward obligations detailed above sit against $50.3B of liquid debt-type assets and a normalized ~$160B annual operating cash-flow run-rate. Coverable if demand holds; a two-quarter pause converts pre-committed capacity into inventory provisions of the kind that cost 2.6 points of FY2026 gross margin.

**Governance and accounting items.** Goodwill rose $15,644M in FY2026 to $20,832M (10.7% of book equity) while identifiable intangibles rose only ~$2.5B; the Q1 balance sheet carries $3,957M of accrued purchase consideration related to the Groq non-exclusive license, and the FY2026 risk factors warn the license "required significant, nonrefundable payments" and that NVIDIA "may be unable to recover the associated costs." The allocation is unverified — we did not read the acquisitions footnote — and the reported ~$20B deal value is media-sourced. Five Item 5.02 officer/director filings between January and July 2026, plus an apparent Principal Accounting Officer transition concurrent with a phased ERP upgrade, stack two sources of internal-control transition risk; disclosure controls were reported effective with no material change in internal control. A June 18, 2026 Item 8.01 filing could not be retrieved.

**Securities litigation.** The 2017-2018 channel-inventory class action received class certification on March 25, 2026, with a Rule 23(f) petition filed April 8. No liability is accrued. For a company earning ~$50B a quarter the amount is unlikely to be financially material, but any resolution hits earnings when recognized.

**Legacy structural item.** Under NVIDIA's Xbox-related agreement with Microsoft, if a third party offers to purchase 30% or more of NVIDIA's common stock, Microsoft may have first and last rights of refusal.

Risk factors score 4.5, unchanged. The identified risks are partly reflected in the ~$1T de-rating, but the guarantee talks are a genuinely unpriced binary appearing in no filing.

### Options Market Signal

IV fell from 65.8% to 38.8% over four sessions while price declined 2.9%, so the market has priced *down* near-term uncertainty into the drawdown; treat the 65.8% reading as a possibly distorted snapshot. The term structure is flat (38.8% near versus 40.5% at six months), with the September 11 tenor covering the August 26 print at 41.6% IV and an implied ±11.6% move.

Positioning is mixed and worth flagging against our BUY. Near-term put/call skew of 8.9% indicates elevated put demand, and unusual activity includes 7,005 August 5 $170 puts at 64.8% IV and 14,883 August 7 $172 puts at 56.6% IV — deep out-of-the-money downside tails expiring before the print, consistent with hedging early-August macro (payrolls, the September FOMC path) rather than an earnings view. The 981,037-contract $200 call print expiring same day is 0DTE pinning and carries no information. Against the near-term put skew, the six-month skew is -2.8% and put/call volume is only 0.26 with OI ratio 0.67, so longer-dated structure is not bearish. Net read: near-term hedging demand into a macro-heavy window, no durable directional bet, and cheap optionality for anyone wanting to own the August 26 event with defined risk.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($320, +64%).** FY2028 consensus of $12.87 proves conservative given the pattern of widening beats, Rubin ships on schedule in 2H FY2027 into a supply-constrained market, and networking attach keeps rack-scale competition at bay. Hyperscaler capex confirmations from Amazon and Microsoft validate the demand base. The OpenAI guarantee either does not proceed or is structured with sufficient collateral and warrant consideration that the market stops treating it as unfunded credit risk, and the multiple re-rates from 15x to 25x FY2028 earnings as NVIDIA's relative discount to a 64x peer median closes. $80B of buyback authorization executed opportunistically — as in the March-April window at $185 — accelerates per-share compounding.

**Bear case ($160, -18%).** A two-quarter pause in buildout spending, whether from AI-ROI disappointment or a frontier-lab funding stumble, strands the $95B of FY2027 supply commitments and produces inventory provisions on the FY2026 scale. Public-equity marks reverse, taking reported EPS down by the same 23% they inflated it, while private marks on a book that has never been written down begin to move. A guarantee crystallises or is disclosed at a size that forces the market to capitalise contingent exposure. Custom inference ASICs take share at $6-11B per gigawatt against ~$19B, compressing the mix that carries the highest margin. At 17-18x a 25-30% reduced FY2028 estimate, the stock trades near its 52-week low of $164 or slightly below.

**Base case ($255, +31%).** Revenue grows into the $375-390B range in FY2027 with gross margin in the 73-75% band despite HBM cost inflation, FY2028 EPS lands near consensus $12.87, and the multiple settles at 20x — below where a 43%-growth, 65%-operating-margin franchise would normally clear, reflecting a permanent discount for contingent exposure and concentration.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $255.** 20x FY2028 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $12.87. The 20x multiple is deliberately below the 21.7x currently applied to nearer earnings, embedding a structural discount for guarantee and concentration risk. Upside from $195.04: +30.7%.

**Bull case target: $320.** 25x $12.87. Upside +64.1%.

**Bear case target: $160.** ~17.5x a FY2028 estimate cut roughly 30% to $9.00, approximately the 52-week low of $164.07. Downside -18.0%.

Reward:risk is approximately 1.7:1 to base and 3.6:1 to bull.

**Timeframe: 12 months**, anchored to three dated catalysts: the Q2 FY2027 report on August 26, 2026 (current-quarter consensus $2.08; watch gross margin against memory cost inflation and the Q2 tax-payment reversal in cash flow); the Rubin shipment start in 2H FY2027; and any 8-K disclosing definitive terms of the SK Group, OpenAI or related arrangements.

**Sizing.** Hold, do not add above $200 after the two-day rebound in the broader complex. Scale on weakness toward $175-180, which would put the stock at ~20x FY2027 and ~13.5x FY2028. Size below a normal full position given the contingent-exposure tail.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. **A filed guarantee or vendor-financing arrangement with maximum gross exposure exceeding $100B** (roughly 51% of book equity), or CDS widening beyond ~150bp. Either would require capitalizing contingent credit exposure into the multiple and would move us to HOLD irrespective of earnings.
2. **Gross margin below 71% in any quarter absent a discrete China or regulatory charge**, indicating HBM cost inflation and rack-scale content mix are structurally compressing unit economics rather than transitorily.
3. **FY2028 consensus EPS revised down by more than 10% from $12.87**, or a Q3 revenue guide implying sequential growth below 5%, which would signal the capex pause the commitment stack is levered to.
4. Secondary monitors: direct-customer concentration exceeding 60% of revenue; a first material impairment on the non-marketable book; disclosure of an internal-control deficiency arising from the ERP transition.

## Conclusion

**BUY, overall score 6.9.** The score has now declined for a fourth consecutive read (7.3 → 7.0 → 6.9), and the entire decline sits in management quality, risk factors and competitive position, not in the operating record. Revenue growth accelerated to 85%, gross margin recovered 14 points from the charge-affected year-ago quarter, guidance implies another 11% sequential step, and consensus for next fiscal year is 14.6% higher than 90 days ago. What has changed is the visibility of the structure around that growth: concentration worsened to 54% of revenue, forward commitments reached ~$218B, the earnings contribution from equity marks quadrupled in a quarter, and the company is reportedly negotiating to guarantee a customer's lease obligations at a scale exceeding its own book equity.

We stay long because the stock now prices a meaningful portion of those concerns. At 21.7x FY2027 and 15.2x FY2028 operating earnings — a PEG near 0.5 against a 64x peer median — with 1.4% short interest and roughly 17.5% below the May high while the semiconductor index rose 75% year to date, NVIDIA offers the least crowded exposure to a demand cycle that recent hyperscaler commentary has confirmed. The forward multiple is calculated on the non-GAAP basis that excludes the investment marks, so the earnings-quality objection does not reduce it.

What would change our mind is specific and checkable: a filed guarantee above $100B of maximum gross exposure, gross margin below 71% without a discrete charge, or a 10% cut to FY2028 consensus. Absent those, a 31% base-case return against 18% downside on a business compounding earnings at 43% justifies ownership at a disciplined size.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the FY2027 P/E cited as "21.7x" relies on the report's own FY2027 EPS of $8.99, but the earnings-surprise table shows a reported Q1 FY2027 non-GAAP figure ($1.87) inconsistent with the $8.99 sum-of-quarters framing previously asserted (the specific "$1.87 + $2.08 + $2.35 + implied $2.69" reconciliation was not supported and has been removed); and several demand-confirmation datapoints (Amazon capex +69%, Azure +43%/45%, "largest-on-record" Korean rally) were unsupported by the sources and have been removed — the demand-durability leg of the thesis rests on more general commentary than originally stated.
