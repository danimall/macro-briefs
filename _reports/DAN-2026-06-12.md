**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Dana is a 120-year-old Tier-1 auto-parts supplier that has reshaped itself into a pure-play on-highway driveline company. The January 1, 2026 sale of its capital-heavy Off-Highway business to Allison Transmission for ~$2.66B gross is the central event: it cut revenue from continuing operations to ~$7.5B, generated a $1,191M pre-tax gain, nearly halved long-term debt (from $2,566M at December 2025 to $1,236M at March 2026), and more than doubled stockholders' equity to ~$1,955M. The headline financial-distress metrics (Altman Z of 1.33, D/E of 8.2x) are calculated off the pre-deleveraging balance sheet and materially misstate the current company, which now carries roughly 2x leverage with ~$1.6B liquidity.

The investable thesis is a "shrink-to-grow plus capital return" story. Revenue is flat-to-down, but adjusted EBITDA rose from $395M (2024) to $610M (2025) and is guided to $750–850M for 2026, almost entirely on self-help (a ~$325M annualized cost program, reduced EV engineering burn, lower freight). Gross margin moved from 4.9% (2024) to 8.0% (2025) to 9.0% in Q1 2026. The company has repurchased ~38.7M shares for ~$775M, cutting the weighted-average share count roughly 25% to ~109.9M, and holds ~$1.2B of remaining buyback authorization against a ~$3.3B market cap. That is a large, ongoing per-share tailwind.

The case against a higher rating is straightforward. Dana sells into the North American commercial-vehicle and full-frame light-truck markets, both in or near a cyclical trough — Class 8 production fell ~23–25% in 2025 and Q1 2026, and management expects further deterioration. Top-10 customers are 76% of sales. The EV transition is a present cash drain ($56M Q1 2026 impairments) rather than a content tailwind. The trailing P/E is negative because GAAP continuing operations still post small losses. The forward earnings power is real, but it is being delivered against an unfavorable consumer-discretionary and auto-demand backdrop, and the stock has already re-rated ~70% off its 52-week low. We rate DAN a HOLD with a constructive bias, classified as a growth hold.

## Company Overview

Dana (founded 1904, HQ Maumee, Ohio) supplies power-conveyance and energy-management systems to essentially every major on-highway vehicle manufacturer globally. After the Off-Highway divestiture, the company operates two segments:

- **Light Vehicle Systems (LVS)** — ~68% of sales. Axles, driveshafts and transmissions for full-frame light trucks, SUVs, CUVs and vans, weighted toward higher-content RWD/4WD/AWD vehicles.
- **Commercial Vehicle Systems (CVS)** — ~32% of sales. Driveline systems for medium/heavy-duty trucks and buses, plus the legacy aftermarket business (Spicer, Victor Reinz, Glaser, GWB, Tru-Cool, SVL brands).

Roughly 60% of sales are North American and ~43–44% are non-U.S. The competitive anchor is the Spicer axle/driveshaft franchise and the aftermarket brand portfolio, backed by decades-long OEM platform incumbency. Competitors include American Axle (AXL), the former Meritor (now within Cummins), GKN/legacy driveline peers, plus EV-component entrants and OEM in-sourcing.

The strategic pivot defines the equity. Management is executing a "focus the core, slash costs, return capital" playbook: divest the capital-intensive Off-Highway unit, deleverage, and return up to $2.0B to shareholders. An orderly CEO succession (Byron Foster, current President of Light Vehicle Drive Systems, becomes CEO effective July 1, 2026, succeeding Bruce McDonald) signals the board views the transformation phase as largely complete and is shifting toward operational execution. The leadership/board refresh cadence and the playbook itself are consistent with activist influence, though the specific activist is unconfirmed.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue (continuing operations, recast):** 2023 $7,613M → 2024 $7,734M → 2025 $7,500M (down ~3% organically, with volume declines partly offset by pricing and tariff recoveries). The 2026 guide of $7,300–7,700M is roughly flat, with $200M of backlog conversion and FX/tariff-lag normalization offsetting soft end-markets.

**Profitability — the core positive trend, driven by self-help rather than volume:**
- Gross margin: 4.9% (2024) → 8.0% (2025) → 9.0% in Q1 2026 (vs. 6.6% in Q1 2025).
- Adjusted EBITDA: $317M (2023) → $395M (2024) → $610M (2025); 2026 guide $750–850M (~10.6% margin at the midpoint, ~+250bps YoY).
- Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $171M vs. $93M (+84% YoY) is early validation of the cost program.

The improvement is structural: a ~$325M annualized cost-savings program (~$260M realized through 2025, ~$65M more in 2026), reduced engineering spend on deferred EV programs, lower freight, and material-cost recoveries. The mechanism matters because it does not require a volume recovery — EBITDA is guided to grow even as revenue stays flat-to-down.

**GAAP earnings remain noisy.** FY2025 net income attributable to the parent of $85M was flattered by $155M of discontinued-ops income and a $48M valuation-allowance release; continuing operations posted a $53M net loss. Q1 2026 net income of $1,087M is almost entirely the $1,191M pre-tax gain on the Off-Highway sale; continuing operations were a $15M loss, dampened by $56M of EV program termination/impairment charges. This is why the trailing P/E reads negative (-56x). It is not informative for this business; the forward consensus is the relevant anchor.

**Balance sheet — materially de-risked, and this is what the screening metrics miss.** $615M of short-term debt was eliminated and the revolver fully repaid. Consolidated debt of ~$1.3B sits against $477M cash plus $1,140M revolver availability. On a pro forma basis, leverage is roughly 2x EBITDA or less and the "distress zone" label is no longer representative.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** Adjusted FCF swung from –$23M (2023) to $81M (2024) to $331M (2025); the 2026 guide is $250–350M. Weighted-average shares fell from ~145.6M as the buyback ran. The buyback authorization was raised to $2.0B through 2030 (Feb 2026), with ~$1,225M remaining at Q1 2026. The $0.12/quarter dividend (~$50M/year, ~1.4% yield) is modestly covered. In May 2026 Dana also agreed to buy three currently-leased U.S. manufacturing facilities for $89M, converting operating leases to owned assets.

## Growth Analysis

Top-line growth is not the story here, and that is the central tension. Revenue is guided roughly flat. The growth that matters is in margins, free cash flow, and per-share metrics.

Estimate revisions are mixed but lean constructive on the near-term path. The current-quarter consensus rose to $0.66 (+7.9% over 30 days, +6.2% over 90 days), and next-quarter to $0.80 (+9.7%/+12.9%). Against that, full-year 2026 EPS was cut to $2.44 (–3.2% 30d, –8.5% 90d), reflecting the soft commercial-vehicle backdrop, while next-year 2027 EPS of $3.55 is roughly flat. The pattern — rising near-term quarters, trimmed current-year, stable out-year — is consistent with a cost-driven margin recovery layered on a soft demand environment, not a demand-driven inflection. This is a moderate, self-help recovery rather than the +30% revision velocity that would flag a clean earnings-inflection BUY.

Forward visibility is supported by the net new business backlog, which rose from $750M (Dec 2025) to $950M (Mar 2026) for 2026–2028. The EV content-expansion thesis (management cites a potential ~3x content per vehicle) is a multi-year option, not a 12-month driver; right now EV is a managed cash drain ($56M Q1 2026 + $36M FY2025 impairments, plus the $190M buyout of Hydro-Québec's 45% stake in the Dana TM4 JV).

The strongest growth lever is per-share leverage from the buyback. Retiring ~$1.2B of stock against a ~$3.3B cap, against guided FCF of $250–350M plus residual divestiture proceeds, mechanically lifts EPS even on flat EBITDA. The earnings track record warrants caution on near-term visibility: over the last four quarters Dana logged one beat (+124% in Q3 2025), one in-line, and two misses, including a –79% Q4 2025 miss and a –4.4% Q1 2026 miss. Consensus has been hard to forecast around the transformation, so we discount precise quarterly estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is meaningless (negative GAAP continuing-operations earnings). The relevant frame is forward and normalized. On consensus current-year EPS of $2.44, DAN trades at ~12.4x; on next-year $3.55, ~8.5x. Adjusted EBITDA against ~$1.3B net debt and a ~$3.3B equity value implies an EV of ~$4.6B and ~5.5–6.1x EV/EBITDA at the guide midpoint.

Against the automotive parts peer set, the median P/E is ~20.5x (VC 19.6x, PATK 21.8x, DORM 20.5x, LCII 11.5x, ADNT 30.4x). DAN at ~8.5x next-year sits well below that median, and below LCII, the cheapest comparable. The discount is partly justified — Dana carries more cyclical commercial-vehicle exposure, a still-loss-making GAAP continuing business, customer concentration, and EV stranded-capital risk. But the gap is wide enough that closing even half of it implies meaningful upside if the margin recovery holds and the commercial-vehicle cycle stops deteriorating.

The catalyst that supports a non-trap reading of the low multiple is concrete: the remaining buyback against the market cap, FCF generation, and a margin program already delivering. This is a specific reason the market may be under-pricing forward per-share economics, rather than a structurally impaired value trap. The stock has re-rated ~70% off its $15.31 low and now trades at $30.27, below the lower end of the $36–45 analyst target range. Prior price appreciation is not a valuation input, but the fact that the stock now sits below the entire published target band, after a sharp single-day drop on June 11 (close $30.11 from $35.47 on heavy volume), suggests the market is repricing a near-term cyclical or guidance concern that the moderate full-year estimate cuts already partly reflect. We score valuation moderately positive (6.0): cheap on forward metrics with a real per-share catalyst, but the discount is partly earned and the cyclical floor is uncertain.

## Competitive Landscape

Dana's moat is incumbency on long-cycle vehicle platforms and engineering integration with major OEMs, anchored by the Spicer franchise and a strong aftermarket brand portfolio. The trajectory of that position is the more important variable than its level, and it is mixed-to-slightly-deteriorating.

On the positive side, the backlog grew and the focus on the core driveline business should sharpen execution. On the negative side, Tier-1 driveline is a perennial pricing battleground: contracts recover commodity costs with a ~90-day lag but do not cover general labor/energy/freight inflation, which requires constant renegotiation and is a persistent margin drag. The EV transition introduces both OEM vertical-integration risk (carmakers in-sourcing e-axles) and intensified competition from new entrants. Top-10 customer concentration means platform-level losses would be material. The shift to a smaller, all-on-highway footprint reduces diversification and concentrates the cyclical exposure precisely where the cycle is weakest (North American Class 8). We score competitive position 5.0 — a stable-to-modestly-eroding incumbent rather than a share gainer, consistent with the slight downtick in this dimension from the prior report.

## Risk Assessment

**Cyclical end-market trough.** North American Class 8 production is weak and management expects continued deterioration. CVS revenue is highly sensitive. Light-truck production is more stable but flat. The margin recovery is being delivered into a weakening volume backdrop, which limits operating leverage on the way up.

**Customer concentration.** Top-10 customers are 76% of 2025 sales. Loss or volume cut on a single high-content platform would be material.

**Tariffs — a live two-way wildcard.** Tariff costs were a $116M headwind in 2025 (LVS $96M), with ~90-day recovery lag on materials and no contractual recovery for general inflation. A February 2026 Supreme Court ruling struck certain IEEPA tariffs; Dana has booked neither anticipated refunds nor related customer refund obligations, and holds $73M of unapplied Section 232 OEM tariff credits as a contract liability. Outcomes could swing both costs and obligations.

**EV stranded capital.** Program impairments continue and the $190M TM4 JV buyout adds capital to an asset that is a current drain. The content-expansion upside is real but multi-year and competitive.

**Execution dependence.** The entire margin thesis rests on delivering and sustaining the $325M cost program without sacrificing volume or talent through a CEO transition.

**Macro/consumer backdrop.** Auto demand is exposed to the broader consumer environment, which can weigh on full-frame light-truck demand, Dana's largest end-market. This is a sector-wide headwind, not idiosyncratic, but it caps the demand-recovery optionality. **FX/Argentina and pension/OPEB** are secondary recurring drags (~43–44% non-U.S. sales; highly-inflationary peso accounting; modest near-term pension cash).

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 56.0% vs. 40.4% one-year HV (rich by 15.6 points, 67th percentile within the chain), with a backwardated term structure (56.0% near-term vs. 46.3% long-term) and a 1-week implied move of ±5.9%. The P/C OI ratio jumped to 1.36 from 0.41 at the prior snapshot, and 1-week put skew is elevated at 3.1%. ATM IV collapsed from 352.5% (the May 15 earnings snapshot) to 56.0%, so the prior reading was an earnings-event artifact. The current backwardation and rising put open interest, alongside the June 11 ~15% single-day price drop, indicate the market is hedging a near-term negative catalyst — likely cyclical/guidance-related. This is consistent with the moderate full-year estimate cuts and supports a HOLD rather than a BUY at this point. The 3-month and 6-month skews are negative (–4.1% / –4.4%), suggesting the longer-dated market is less bearish, consistent with the forward per-share recovery thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The cost program is structural and durable; FY2026 adjusted EBITDA lands at or above the $850M high end. The remaining buyback retires a meaningful share of the float over the authorization window, driving EPS materially higher on flat EBITDA. The commercial-vehicle cycle troughs and begins recovering in late 2026/2027, adding volume leverage on a leaner cost base. The forward multiple re-rates from ~8.5x next-year toward the peer median, and the stock approaches the $42–45 analyst band. Bull target ~$45 (+49%).

**Bear case.** Class 8 deterioration deepens and light-truck demand softens with the consumer, pulling 2026 EBITDA toward $750M or below. Cost savings prove harder to sustain through the CEO transition. Tariff resolution produces customer refund obligations. The buyback masks soft operations and the market keeps the multiple compressed. With the June 11 break below the analyst target band, the stock retraces toward the mid-$20s. Bear target ~$24 (–21%).

**Base case.** Margins hold near the guide midpoint (~$800M EBITDA), the buyback delivers steady per-share accretion, the cycle stays soft but does not collapse, and the multiple holds at a discount to peers. The stock works modestly higher over 12 months as buyback accretion accumulates. Base target ~$36 (+19%).

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters confirming FY2026 adjusted EBITDA tracking at or above the ~$850M high end with gross margin sustained above 9%.
- Evidence the North American Class 8 cycle has troughed (production stabilizing or order intake inflecting positive), removing the largest demand overhang.
- Full-year EPS estimate revisions turning positive (currently –3.2% 30d) alongside continued buyback execution at or below current prices.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A guidance cut taking FY2026 adjusted EBITDA below ~$750M, or gross margin reversing below ~7%, indicating the cost program is not durable.
- Loss or material volume cut on a top-10 platform.
- Tariff resolution producing a material customer-refund obligation not currently booked, or a renewed leverage build that pushes net debt/EBITDA back above ~3x.

**Reassessment timeline.** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2 2026 earnings release (which will also cover the July 1 CEO transition), plus the closing of the $89M facility purchases. Monitor monthly Class 8 production data as the leading cyclical indicator.

## Conclusion

Dana has executed a genuine balance-sheet transformation that the standardized distress metrics fail to capture: leverage near 2x, ample liquidity, a margin program already delivering, and a large per-share tailwind from the remaining buyback. The forward multiple (~8.5x next-year EPS, ~5.5–6.1x EV/EBITDA) is cheap versus a ~20.5x peer median, with a concrete capital-return catalyst rather than a structural value trap.

What holds us at HOLD rather than BUY is the combination of a deepening commercial-vehicle cyclical trough, 76% customer concentration, EV stranded-capital drag, a soft full-year estimate trend (–3.2% 30d, –8.5% 90d), and an options market actively hedging near-term stress after the sharp June 11 drop below the entire analyst target band. The per-share recovery is real and the risk/reward is improving as the price falls on largely intact fundamentals, but the demand backdrop and execution dependence keep conviction short of a high-bar long. We classify this as a growth hold with a constructive bias: the path to a BUY is clear and monitorable (durable EBITDA above the guide, a cyclical trough confirmed, full-year revisions turning positive), and we would upgrade on confirmation.
