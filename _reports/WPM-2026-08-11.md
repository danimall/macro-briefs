## Executive Summary

Wheaton's H1 2026 was the best half-year in company history by every measure it reports: revenue $1,830.7M (+88.0%), net earnings $1,125.3M (+106.0%), diluted EPS $2.473 (+105.7%), operating cash flow $1,415.3M (+82.4%). Gross margin expanded from 69.0% to 75.8%, and cash cost per gold-equivalent ounce fell as a *percentage* of realized price (14.5% to 14.0% for H1) even as the absolute cost rose — the defining mechanic of a portfolio carrying fixed-dollar ongoing payments into a rising metal-price tape. Management's own framing is accurate: margin growth exceeded the appreciation in gold prices.

The composition of that growth is where the investment case gets harder. Management attributes the Q2 revenue increase to a 61% rise in the average realized gold-equivalent price and a 14% rise in ounces sold; for H1 the split is 78% price and 5% volume. Gold ounces *produced* fell 2.6% year over year in Q2 (90,434 vs 92,883) despite three brand-new gold assets entering the portfolio, because Constancia's high-grade Pampacancha pit was mined out in Q4 2025 (attributable gold: 15,396 oz in Q4'25 → 2,978 oz in Q2'26) and Salobo grades declined. Stripped of price, this is a portfolio currently replacing lost ounces rather than adding to them, with a 2030 target of 1.2M GEOs that represents a ~7.5% CAGR off the 2026 guidance midpoint of 900,000 — not the "approximately 50%" headline, which is measured off an inferred FY2025 base of ~800,000.

Two things prevent a constructive rating at $133.39. First, valuation offers no cushion: ~27x annualized H1 diluted EPS, ~25x FY2027 consensus of $5.30, 6.25x book, 0.58% dividend yield, EV/annualized-H1-EBITDA-proxy of 20.2x — full multiples struck on peak realized prices, while consensus for both the current and next year has been cut ~6% over 30 days. Second, the largest capital deployment in company history is unresolved. Wheaton committed $4.3B — roughly 35% of total assets — to a single incremental silver stream at Antamina, closing April 1, 2026, and silver subsequently corrected. Using disclosed FY2025 attributable volumes and a 20%-of-spot ongoing payment borrowed by analogy from the new Jervois agreement, the implied undiscounted payback is roughly 15.5 years at $60/oz silver and 13.2 years at $70/oz, consistent with a low single-digit real return on current reserves. The actual contract terms — ongoing payment percentage, duration, whether reserves-only or life-of-mine with expansions — are not disclosed and are the single most important unresolved fact in this name. The shares have already recovered ~29% from the July 20 low of $103.45, which consumed most of the near-term asymmetry that existed a month ago.

## Company Overview

Wheaton buys the right to purchase a fixed share of a mine's future precious-metals output for an upfront lump sum plus a pre-agreed ongoing payment per ounce. It owns no mines, employs no miners, and funds no sustaining or expansion capital at the operating level. Corporate overhead is 1.2% of revenue ($11.3M G&A against $929.2M in Q2 2026), plus $4.8M share-based compensation and $4.7M of community investment.

The portfolio covers 57 assets — 22 operating mines, 20 development projects, 15 exploration-stage. Q2 2026 revenue mix was 46% gold, 52% silver, 2% cobalt, 0.3% palladium; silver became co-equal with gold following the Antamina transaction.

Asset count overstates diversification. Applying production shares to metal revenue shares, Salobo (Vale, Brazil) contributed 68.7% of attributable Q2 gold and roughly 30% of revenue; Antamina roughly 19%; Peñasquito roughly 15%. Three mines plausibly generate 60-65% of revenue. These are estimates from production-share × metal-revenue-share rather than disclosed segment disaggregation, but the direction is not in doubt.

Wheaton is a Canadian issuer reporting under IFRS and filing with the SEC as a foreign private issuer (40-F, 6-K). It does not file 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, DEF 14A or Form 4. Insiders report on Canada's SEDI system, not Section 16, so the absence of Form 4 records is a monitoring gap rather than evidence of insider inactivity, and no US proxy compensation data exists.

**Balance sheet transformation.** Wheaton entered 2026 with zero bank debt and $1,153.6M of cash. At June 30, 2026 it carried $1,969.3M of bank debt net of issue costs and $100.2M of cash — net debt of $1,869.1M. Mineral stream interests rose from $7,397.1M to $11,731.2M. The funding reconciliation is clean and worth stating precisely, because it is the strongest management-quality evidence in the file. Sources: $1,053.4M cash drawdown + $1,972.0M net new bank debt (drawn $2,700.0M less repaid $728.0M) + $323.4M proceeds from disposing of long-term equity investments + $1,415.3M H1 operating cash flow = $4,764.1M. Uses: $4,535.2M mineral streams + $27.1M royalties + $14.6M investments + $171.3M dividends + $5.1M debt issue costs + $12.1M other/FX = $4,765.4M. **A $4.5B acquisition programme was funded with essentially zero equity dilution** — weighted-average diluted shares rose 0.07% year over year, issued capital rose $10.1M in H1, and performance share units are cash-settled liabilities ($16.6M) rather than dilutive instruments.

**Leverage is modest but the profile has changed.** Net debt/equity is 19.3%. An H1 EBITDA proxy (earnings from operations $1,341.9M plus D&D $200.1M = $1,542.0M) annualizes to ~$3,084M, giving net debt/EBITDA ≈ 0.61x — a sensitivity, since it annualizes a half-year struck at exceptional prices. The revolver was upsized $500M to $2.5B with maturity extended to June 30, 2031; a $1.5B two-year term loan was drawn April 1, 2026, implying an ~April 2028 maturity; $728M was repaid during Q2, so deleveraging began immediately. Stated available liquidity of $2.6B reconciles (~$2.03B undrawn revolver + $500M accordion + $100M cash). The 0.47x current ratio ($130.2M / $279.3M) would trip a mechanical liquidity screen; current liabilities are dominated by $247.8M of accrued income taxes against ~$2.0B of committed undrawn revolver, so that screen output is not meaningful here.

**Cash flow will not run at the annualized H1 rate, for identifiable reasons.** H1 income taxes *paid* were $109.4M against $210.9M of expense; a further ~US$243M (Cdn$346M) Pillar Two payment for FY2025 is due around March 31, 2027. Finance costs of $32.5M reflect one quarter of debt; a full year on ~$2.0B at the ~5% blended rate implied by Q2 interest paid is $100M+. Annualizing Q2 alone gives $2,600M of OCF; adjusting for the tax step-up and lower silver prices takes a defensible forward estimate meaningfully below that. I use ~$2.3B ($5.07/share) as a normalized anchor.

**Depletion is about to distort reported earnings downward.** Depletion per GEO sold rose from $410 (Q2 2025) to $586 (Q2 2026), and Q2 contained only one quarter of depletion on the newly capitalized $4.3B Antamina asset. Reported EPS will grow more slowly than cash flow from here, which matters for P/E-based comparisons but not for cash-based ones.

**Dividend.** $0.195 declared quarterly (third 2026 declaration August 6), roughly +20% year over year against an implied $0.163 in H1 2025. That is $0.78 annualized, a 0.58% yield and a 12.5-15.8% payout against annualized H1 OCF/EPS. The low payout is consistent with a formula that lags trailing four-quarter cash flow and with prioritizing term-loan repayment; I could not establish the policy mechanics from the documents available.

## Growth Analysis

**The reported growth rate is not the forward growth rate.** For H1, 78 percentage points of the price/volume decomposition came from realized price and 5 from volume. Investors buying WPM on trailing growth are buying gold and silver price levels.

**Volume growth is mid-single-digit.** The 2030 target is 1,200,000 GEOs. Against the 2026 guidance midpoint of 900,000, that is +33.3%, a 7.45% CAGR over four years. The "approximately 50%" headline implies an ~800,000 base, which corroborates as FY2025 actual rather than 2026 guidance. Solid, not extraordinary, and it depends almost entirely on 20 development projects Wheaton does not control.

**2026 guidance requires a real H2 step-up.** H1 production of 414,755 GEOs is 46.1% of the 900,000 midpoint. Reaching the midpoint requires 485,245 GEOs in H2, +17.0% sequentially; the low end requires +7.4%. Gold is furthest behind at 44.7% of its 415,000 oz midpoint, needing ~+21%. The bridge is identifiable — full quarters of Antamina at normalized grades, Peñasquito grade recovery, Salobo's pronounced Q4 skew (Q4'24 84,291 oz, Q4'25 88,907 oz), Voisey's Bay ramp, Mineral Park to 16.5Mtpa, first production at Kurmuk and Koné — but the low end of guidance looks like the comfortable outcome.

**Development slippage is documented, not hypothetical.** Rio2's Fenix missed planned tonnes and grade in Q1 2026 with commercial production pushed to Q4 2026; Platreef commercial production also Q4 2026; El Domo first commissioning July 2027; Kudz Ze Kayah FID pushed to late 2027 pending permits; Santo Domingo FID Q4 2026; Copper World sanctioning "later in 2026." Only ~3% of 2026 forecast production comes from construction/ramp assets, so near-term guidance risk from this source is low — but the 2030 number is entirely exposed.

**Estimates are moving the wrong way.** Current-quarter consensus fell 9.5% in 30 days to $1.23; next quarter -8.4% to $1.37; current year -6.0% to $4.90; next year -5.9% to $5.30. This is the standard signature of consensus marking down realized metal-price assumptions, and it is the opposite of the estimate-revision pattern that supports a re-rating. The Q2 print itself is ambiguous on basis: the surprise table shows $1.19 against a $1.25 estimate (-4.3%), while the brief cites a Zacks consensus of $1.15 (a ~3.9% beat on adjusted basis). Different consensus providers on different bases; the market's reaction (+5.9% pre-market August 7) tracked metal prices and the dividend more closely than the EPS line.

Every earnings-based multiple here rests on figures struck at peak realized prices. On the more conservative normalized cash-flow anchor (~$2.3B OCF after the Pillar Two step-up and a full year of interest), the stock trades at ~26x — which is where a high-quality streamer with mid-single-digit volume growth has historically traded, not below it.

The P/B of 6.25x is not comparable to a miner's, since stream interests are carried at historical cost less depletion and bear no relation to the present value of deliveries. It is, however, a reminder that the $4.3B added to that carrying value in Q2 will amortize through depletion regardless of whether the deal earns its cost of capital.

**Peer context.** The royalty/streaming cohort — Franco-Nevada, Royal Gold, Triple Flag, Osisko — structurally trades at premium earnings multiples because of the absence of capex, cost inflation and operating leverage to input prices. Wheaton at ~25x FY2027 is inside that cohort's normal band. I do not have current peer multiples in this data set, so I treat the cohort comparison qualitatively: Wheaton is not obviously mispriced relative to its own sector, and the sector as a whole is being valued on elevated spot metal prices. Mining Journal's coverage indicates the entire cohort posted records and raised dividends this quarter, which argues against attributing Wheaton's H1 to company-specific execution.

**On the analyst targets.** The published range is low $145, mean $172.35, median $167.50, high $215 — with all 16 recommendations at Buy or Strong Buy and unchanged for four consecutive months. The entire target range sits above spot, implying +8.7% to +61.2%. This does not function as independent confirmation. Consensus EPS was cut ~6% over 30 days while targets did not move; that combination is the signature of stale targets anchored to prior spot metal assumptions rather than of analysts leaning into an under-appreciated inflection. Streamer price targets are largely a function of the gold and silver decks embedded in them.

**The central valuation question is Antamina.** Wheaton's pre-existing 33.75% Antamina share produced 5,801koz of attributable silver in FY2025. Doubling that share implies roughly 5.8Moz/year of incremental attributable silver at FY2025 grades. At a 20%-of-spot ongoing payment — the rate explicitly disclosed for the new Jervois agreement and used here as an analogy, not as a stated Antamina term — Wheaton would net ~$48/oz at $60 silver, or ~$278M of annual cash margin on $4.3B, an undiscounted payback of ~15.5 years. At $70/oz: ~$325M, ~13.2 years. At $50/oz: ~$232M, ~18.5 years.

A mid-teens undiscounted payback on a long-life copper mine corresponds to a low-to-mid single-digit real return unless silver rises materially, mine life extends well beyond current reserves, or grades and throughput improve versus the FY2025 base. Three offsets are legitimate and unquantifiable from the disclosure: Q2 Antamina silver grades were explicitly below normal (more copper-only ore processed, plus a maintenance shutdown pulled forward from July into June), so 5.8Moz may understate normalized volume; streamers routinely capture mine-life extension and expansion as free optionality, and Peruvian expansion is live in this portfolio (Hudbay received a July 2, 2026 permit to expand Constancia's mill from 31 to 34Mtpa); and if the ongoing payment is materially below 20% of spot, or the stream is life-of-mine across resources, the economics improve substantially. The terms are not disclosed. I do not conclude that management overpaid — I conclude that the disclosed volumes plus plausible terms produce a long payback at spot silver, that this is unresolved, and that a stock at 25-27x forward earnings does not compensate an owner for carrying that uncertainty on 35% of the asset base.

## Competitive Landscape

The streaming model was tested in real time in H1 2026 and passed. Wheaton's realized GEO price rose 78% year over year while cash cost per GEO rose from $399 to $621 — but *as a share of realized price*, cost fell from 14.5% to 14.0%. Over the same period the release documents operators wrestling with grade (Salobo, Peñasquito, Antamina), sequencing (Hemlo), exhausted high-grade pits (Constancia), missed plans (Fenix), rainfall (El Domo) and community access disputes (Los Filos, restarting gradually after Equinox signed 20-year land access agreements with three host communities on June 25, 2026). Wheaton captured price upside while operators absorbed operating friction. That is the model working as designed and it is the strongest structural argument for owning the shares over a miner.

Wheaton's differentiated advantage is now cheque size. A $4.3B single transaction narrows the competitive field to Wheaton and perhaps one or two peers, and as diversified majors monetize non-core by-product precious metals for capital-management reasons, the addressable deal size grows. BHP's motivation is instructive for interpreting price: silver is an incidental copper by-product carrying no separate market re-rating for BHP, so a seller monetizing at the top of a silver cycle negotiates from strength. Scarcity of competing bidders did not visibly translate into an attractive purchase price. That is an inference about deal dynamics, not a disclosed fact.

Right-of-first-refusal accumulation is a quieter, real advantage: each of the three 2026 transactions (Jervois, Spanish Mountain, Cipango) granted ROFRs on future streams or royalties over the same assets, with the Cipango ROFR extending to nine additional Japanese properties beyond the seven covered.

Trajectory is mixed rather than clearly improving. Scale and optionality are widening; the legacy cash-generating base is degrading (Salobo grades down, Constancia gold structurally lower by an estimated 40-50koz/year versus 2024-25), and asset concentration has not improved — the revenue mix shifted toward silver, but three mines still plausibly account for 60-65% of revenue.

## Risk Assessment

**Antamina return uncertainty (highest priority).** 35% of total assets in one incremental silver stream on undisclosed terms, closed weeks before silver corrected. Silver is now ~52% of revenue.

**Single-asset and counterparty concentration.** ~30% of revenue depends on Vale's Salobo, whose attributable production fell 11% year over year in Q2 on lower grades (H1: 131,317 oz vs 140,802 oz). Wheaton has no operational control and no ability to influence mine sequencing.

**Guidance dependency on an H2 step-up.** +17.0% sequential GEO growth is required for the midpoint, +21% for gold. A miss would likely be read as a break in the volume story precisely when the price story is weakening.

**Permanent tax step-down.** Pillar Two floors the effective rate at 15%; ETR rose from 13.94% (H1 2025) to 15.78% (H1 2026). $109M was paid June 24, 2026 for FY2024, and ~US$243M is due around March 31, 2027 for FY2025 — accrued but not yet paid, which currently flatters operating cash flow relative to steady state.

**Capital structure.** The $1.5B term loan maturing ~April 2028 is comfortably repayable from retained cash flow at current prices, but it removes the debt-free optionality that was part of the equity story. Third-party screens have not caught up: an Insider Monkey piece dated May 19, 2026 still listed WPM among "debt-free gold stocks," six weeks after $2.7B was drawn.

**Emerging-market jurisdiction concentration.** Peru, Mexico, Brazil, South Africa, Ethiopia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ecuador, Colombia. The Los Filos community-access dispute illustrates how this risk manifests as production interruption rather than headline expropriation.

**Counterparty corporate instability.** Zijin Gold's agreed acquisition of Allied Gold — operator of Kurmuk, where Wheaton expects first gold within weeks — was terminated on July 29, 2026 and replaced with a ~$295M strategic investment at a premium. Wheaton is neither party to nor beneficiary of that transaction; it depends on Allied remaining adequately capitalized. Constructive and cautious readings are both defensible.

**Governance and monitoring gaps.** CEO transition completed in 2026: Haytham Hodaly (formerly SVP Corporate Development) succeeded co-founder Randy Smallwood, who moved to Board Chair. Continuity mechanism read neutrally; a constraint on new-CEO independence read critically. Insider transaction data is unavailable to US investors (SEDI, not Section 16), and no US proxy compensation disclosure exists. Share-based compensation fell to $4.8M from $10.0M and the PSU liability declined from $34.8M to $16.6M with $29.3M paid — mechanically consistent with the share-price drawdown, since cash-settled PSUs are remeasured to fair value, rather than with reduced grant activity.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 51.2% against 48.5% one-year realized is fair (2.7-point spread, 67th percentile within the chain) — the market is not paying up for protection at the index of this name. Put/call skew is balanced (-0.9%) and put/call volume and open-interest ratios of 0.88 and 0.91 show no directional tilt. Short interest is negligible at 3.39M shares and 1.5 days to cover, so there is no crowded-short setup and no squeeze fuel.

Two features deserve attention. The term structure is in backwardation (51.2% near versus 43.4% at six months), which prices near-term event risk — consistent with a two-day expiry immediately after a print and with high metal-price sensitivity, not with a company-specific catalyst I can identify. More informative is the six-month tenor: -5.1% put skew with a 3.58 put/call volume ratio, the only meaningfully put-tilted tenor in the chain. That is the profile of longer-dated downside protection being accumulated against a metal-price reversal rather than near-term positioning. It corroborates the caution in my valuation and risk scores without contradicting the near-term fundamental picture.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing owners have a high-quality, low-cost vehicle for precious-metals exposure with a supportive macro backdrop and should keep it. Prospective buyers have no entry edge at $133.39 after a ~29% three-week rally, at ~25x FY2027 consensus that is being cut, with the largest deal in company history unresolved.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results (early November 2026) is the decisive checkpoint — it will show whether the required H2 production step-up is materializing and will carry a second full quarter of Antamina at normalized grades. The FY2025 40-F risk-factor section and any Antamina term disclosure are secondary but valuable.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. **Price:** a decline to **$105-112** with fundamentals intact. That range is ~20x FY2027 consensus and ~21x the normalized OCF anchor, restoring a genuine margin of safety and roughly 35% upside to the base case. This is the most likely upgrade path.
2. **Antamina terms disclosed** at an ongoing payment materially below 20% of spot, or confirmed as life-of-mine across resources and expansions, converting the largest asset from an open question into quantifiable value.
3. **Q3 production of at least 240,000 GEOs** (implying a credible path to the guidance midpoint) combined with a stabilization or upward turn in FY2027 consensus above $5.30.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. **FY2026 guidance cut** below the 860,000 GEO low end, or Q3 production below ~215,000 GEOs, which would put even the low end out of reach.
2. **Salobo attributable gold below 60,000 oz** for two consecutive quarters, confirming grade decline as structural rather than sequencing at ~30% of revenue.
3. **FY2027 consensus cut below $4.50** (a further ~15%) without a corresponding price decline, which would push the forward multiple above 29x on deteriorating estimates.
4. **Any equity issuance** to fund a new transaction, which would reverse the single clearest capital-allocation positive in the file.

## Conclusion

Wheaton is a genuinely high-quality business: 74% gross margins, cash costs at 12.8% of realized price, no operating capex, no exposure to mine-level cost inflation, and a cost ratio that *improves* as metal prices rise. Q2 and H1 2026 were records on every measure, adjusted earnings matched IFRS earnings almost exactly, and management funded a $4.5B acquisition programme with essentially zero dilution while immediately repaying $728M of the drawdown. Those are the marks of a well-run franchise.

The rating is HOLD because the price already reflects that. Volume growth is ~7.5% CAGR to 2030, not the 50% headline; gold ounces produced fell year over year in Q2 even with three new assets contributing; 2026 guidance needs a 17% sequential step-up that the low end, not the midpoint, is the comfortable outcome for; consensus for both years has been cut ~6% in 30 days; the effective tax rate has permanently stepped up with ~US$243M of cash tax landing in Q1 2027; and 35% of the asset base sits in a silver stream whose returns, on disclosed volumes and plausible terms, imply a mid-teens undiscounted payback at spot. At ~25x FY2027 consensus, 26x normalized cash flow and a 0.58% yield, none of that is compensated. The published analyst range starting at $145 is not independent confirmation when estimates are falling and targets have not moved in four months.

Buy this franchise when the metal-price cycle hands you the entry. At $105-112, the arithmetic changes materially. At $133.39, hold what you own and wait for the November print.
