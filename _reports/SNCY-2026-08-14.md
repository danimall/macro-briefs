## Company Overview

Sun Country was a Minneapolis–St. Paul-based hybrid low-cost carrier running three revenue streams across two reportable segments (Passenger and Cargo): scheduled leisure passenger service concentrated on MSP and leisure destinations; charter air transportation under ad hoc, repeat, short-term and long-term contracts; and air cargo flown on a crew-maintenance-and-insurance (CMI) basis, in which Sun Country supplied aircraft, crew, maintenance and insurance rather than selling capacity directly. A loyalty program and ancillary products supplemented these. Fleet at December 31, 2025 was **70 Boeing 737-NG aircraft** (all-Boeing, no MAX, no Airbus) with **3,276 employees**. The company IPO'd March 17, 2021.

The distinguishing feature versus pure ultra-low-cost carriers was counter-seasonal diversification: scheduled leisure demand peaks in winter and spring (MSP snowbird and spring-break traffic), while contracted charter and CMI cargo flying utilizes aircraft in shoulder periods and is structurally less fuel-exposed where the counterparty supplies fuel. Earnings seasonality was pronounced even though revenue seasonality was not: Q4 2025 revenue of $281.0M was 24.9% of the full year, but Q4 GAAP diluted EPS of $0.15 was only about 16% of the full-year $0.96.

Two caveats on the business description. First, the FY2025 Item 1 (Business), Item 1A (Risk Factors) and Item 7 (MD&A) could not be read in this research cycle; EDGAR narrative-text retrieval failed for this issuer and the only section-accessible annual document was a 10-K/A filed April 30, 2026 containing Items 10–15 only. Second, the identity of the cargo CMI counterparty, contract tenor, revenue concentration and any associated warrant position are **unverified**. Sun Country's CMI operation has historically been publicly associated with a single large e-commerce counterparty, but that could not be confirmed from the FY2025 filings here. Treat customer concentration as neither confirmed nor ruled out.

Revenue grew 7.4% cumulatively from 2023 to 2025 while operating income fell 21.1% ($127.5M → $100.6M). Operating margin gave back 322 basis points from the 2023 peak.

The flat 2024→2025 EPS ($0.961 → $0.963) despite a $5.4M decline in operating income reconciles below the operating line: interest expense fell from $44.300M to $36.861M (−$7.44M), lifting pretax income slightly from $69.574M to $70.211M; the effective tax rate rose from 23.96% ($16.671M/$69.574M) to 24.79% ($17.402M/$70.211M); diluted shares fell 0.4% (55.056M → 54.860M). FY2025 EPS stability came from financing and share count, not from operations.

One nuance argues against reading the operating-margin decline too harshly. D&A rose from $88.2M (2023) to $98.9M (2025), a $10.7M increase that mechanically accounts for roughly 40% of the $26.9M operating-income decline over those two years. Cash margin compression was therefore milder than reported operating margin suggests. Working against that: the standardized "cost of revenue" line, which by magnitude and airline income-statement convention most plausibly maps to aircraft fuel expense, though the tag label is not confirmed, fell from $237.2M (2024) to $213.5M (2025), down 10.0% on 4.7% higher revenue. If that mapping is correct, margins fell in a year when the largest volatile input was a tailwind, which points toward labor, airport and handling cost inflation or leisure pricing pressure rather than input-cost bad luck. Deliberate growth investment (fleet induction, new stations, cargo ramp) is a possible more benign explanation that cannot be adjudicated without MD&A.

**Cash flow was the strongest part of the file, with a one-year limitation.** FY2025 operating cash flow $157.1M, capex $73.1M, free cash flow $84.0M (7.45% FCF margin). OCF/net income of 2.98x reflects $98.9M of D&A plus working-capital and deferred-tax contributions and indicates good accrual quality. Capex of $73.1M sat below D&A of $98.9M (0.74x), consistent either with harvesting cash after the 2022–2024 investment cycle or with deferred reinvestment; for a carrier that historically acquired used 737-800s at low book cost and financed aircraft partly through leases and secured debt, capex materially understates gross fleet additions, so this ratio is weak evidence either way. The XBRL pull returned cash-flow lines for FY2025 only, so no multi-year cash-flow trend can be established and FY2025 FCF should not be treated as a trajectory.

**Balance sheet: asset-heavy, customer-funded negative working capital, and one unresolved inconsistency.** At YE2025 total assets were $1,680.5M, equity $625.2M, total liabilities $1,055.3M. Net PP&E of $1,341.3M was 79.8% of assets. Goodwill of $222.2M was unchanged across 2022–2025 (no impairment) and equalled 13.2% of assets and 35.5% of equity; definite-lived intangibles were $73.3M, amortizing $4.6–5.7M annually. Cash rose to $144.7M from $83.2M (2024) and $46.3M (2023). Current assets of $368.2M against current liabilities of $451.1M gives a current ratio of 0.816, improved from 0.631; a sub-1.0 ratio is normal for a passenger airline because air traffic liability represents customer prepayments rather than a claim requiring cash outflow. Retained earnings rose $52.8M, exactly FY2025 net income, confirming no dividend.

The debt series does not reconcile cleanly. Tagged funded debt rose from $259.7M at YE2024 (short-term $20.2M + long-term $239.5M) to $316.9M at YE2025 ($61.6M + $255.3M), while interest expense fell 16.8%. A period-end balance and a period flow are not directly comparable, and the candidate explanations (inconsistent tagging of finance-lease obligations across years, a late-2025 financing draw that lifted the year-end balance without lifting average balances, non-recurring 2024 refinancing or extinguishment costs inside interest expense, or genuine refinancing at lower rates) cannot be separated without the debt footnote. **No claim of FY2025 deleveraging is made here.** Relatedly, non-current liabilities of $604.2M against tagged long-term debt of $255.3M leaves roughly $349M of other non-current liabilities, most plausibly lease obligations plus deferred taxes. Any leverage ratio built from tagged debt alone understates lease-adjusted leverage materially; the apparent 0.51x debt/equity is not the operative figure.

**Returns and coverage.** ROA 3.19% (on average assets of $1,655.3M); ROE 8.83% (on average equity of $597.8M), down from 14.03% on YE2023 equity; asset turnover 0.681x versus 0.661x; interest coverage 2.73x (2025), 2.39x (2024), 2.99x (2023). GAAP EBITDA (operating income + D&A) was $199.5M (2025), $201.0M (2024), $215.7M (2023). Management's Adjusted EBITDA, per the proxy's pay-versus-performance disclosure, was $224.8M (2023) and $207.1M (2024), a different basis but pointing the same direction: peak in 2023, flat-to-lower thereafter.

**Reconstructed screens.** A manually computed Altman Z-Score for FY2025, using the $876.4M delisting-date market capitalization, comes to approximately **1.47** (X1 −0.0493, X2 0.1190, X3 0.0598, X4 0.8304, X5 0.6705, weighted). Nominally that sits in the distress zone below 1.81, and it should be heavily discounted for this business: the model was calibrated on manufacturers, X1 is negative by construction for airlines because ticket prepayments sit in current liabilities, and X5 is depressed because ~80% of assets are aircraft. A company generating $157M of operating cash flow with 2.73x interest coverage and five consecutive profitable years was not a near-term solvency risk. The score is a structural description, and it is price-sensitive: at a pre-announcement price near $12.50, Z falls to roughly 1.36. A reconstructed Piotroski F-Score comes to approximately **6 of 9**: positive net income, positive OCF, OCF above net income, improving current ratio, improving gross margin and improving asset turnover as positives; flat-to-down ROA, higher long-term debt/assets and modest share issuance as negatives. The gross-margin criterion rests entirely on the unconfirmed cost-of-revenue-to-fuel mapping; 5/9 is defensible if that mapping is wrong.

**Earnings surprise record and the final standalone quarter.** The four-quarter record is 2 beats, 1 in line, 1 miss: Q2 2025 $0.14 vs $0.12 (+15.8%), Q3 2025 $0.07 vs $0.07 (−0.8%), Q4 2025 $0.17 vs $0.13 (+27.3%), and **Q1 2026 $0.58 vs $0.72 (−19.7%)**. The Q1 2026 result is the largest miss in the record and came in the final quarter before closing. Two basis issues apply: the Q4 2025 figure of $0.17 in this series does not match the $0.15 GAAP diluted EPS in the company's press release, so the series is most plausibly on an adjusted basis and should not be summed against GAAP annual EPS; and the derived indication from third-party TTM data (TTM net income through March 2026 of $40.38M against FY2025 $52.81M, implying Q1 2026 net income roughly $12.4M below Q1 2025's) is a GAAP-basis delta. Both point the same way, a fuel-driven year-over-year deterioration in the first quarter of 2026, without providing a verified Q1 2026 absolute.

## Growth Analysis

For any holder of SNCY common stock, forward growth participation is zero. The security was cancelled at closing; shareholders received a mix of cash and Allegiant stock, and their continuing exposure, to the extent they retained the stock component, is to ALGT.

On the underlying asset, the growth picture was already flattening before the merger. Post-COVID recovery growth ended in 2023; two subsequent years at 2.5% and 4.7% indicate a business growing roughly with fleet and market rather than taking share aggressively. D&A rising from $67.6M (2022) to $98.9M (2025) confirms fleet expansion, but that expansion was not converting into operating income, the defining feature of the last two reported years. Allegiant's post-close actions cut the same direction on the passenger side: the Star Tribune reported on August 8, 2026 additional Sun Country flight cuts at MSP as Allegiant addressed post-merger operational issues, and Allegiant's CEO said MSP growth "will depend on demand." Pruning of marginal MSP capacity by the acquirer is evidence that at least part of the legacy schedule was not earning its cost of capital.

The credible forward growth story attaches to the combination, not to Sun Country standalone: **$140M of targeted annual synergies** against roughly $199M of Sun Country GAAP EBITDA. That figure is large relative to the acquired asset's own earnings, which raises both the upside case and the execution bar, and it comes from a summary of the January M&A call rather than a document read directly. It is also not available for 18–24 months, the stated timeline to a single operating certificate.

## Competitive Landscape

The U.S. low-cost segment restructured heavily in 2025–2026. Spirit Airlines ceased operations (described as defunct in industry reporting by June 2026); Frontier was characterized as pressured, and Frontier and Avelo were reported to have sought $2.5 billion in federal assistance tied to jet-fuel costs; low-cost carrier CEOs met the U.S. Transportation Secretary to press for temporary tax relief. The industry was experiencing a jet-fuel cost shock severe enough to trigger federal lobbying, with Allegiant paying approximately $4.20 per gallon in Q2 2026 against a late-June U.S. average near $2.87.

Against that, Allegiant and Sun Country were the two profitable survivors of the leisure/low-cost segment. Allegiant reported a 14.9% Q1 2026 operating margin, cited as the highest among U.S. carriers, attributing resilience to a low-utilization, flexible-capacity model, measured pandemic-era growth, and deliberate avoidance of head-to-head competition with legacy basic-economy products. Allegiant's network executive framed the deal as combining "two profitable companies" that "don't have to change everything right away to save each other."

Network complementarity was high and explains the regulatory path: **one overlapping route** between the two carriers, Allegiant serving ~125 cities with Sun Country adding more, and complementary fleets (Sun Country all-737NG; Allegiant A320-family plus 737 MAX, with Allegiant stating it will retain a mixed fleet long-term). That profile is consistent with HSR early termination on March 16, 2026 with no evident divestiture and the DOT's April 15 exemption permitting both carriers to operate under common ownership.

Sun Country's own competitive trajectory was mixed rather than strong. The three-legged model provided counter-seasonal diversification and fuel insulation on the CMI and some charter flying, but MSP single-hub concentration, a two-year operating-margin decline through a fuel tailwind, and the acquirer's decision to retire the Sun Country brand once a single certificate is obtained all point to a franchise with limited standalone pricing power. Segment-level Passenger versus Cargo revenue and profitability were not obtained, so the relative durability of the three legs cannot be quantified here.

## Risk Assessment

**Terminal-security risk (dominant).** The stock does not exist. There is no borrow, no quote, no options market, no analyst coverage and no residual SNCY-linked instrument identified. Formal deregistration was not confirmed in this cycle; that is a housekeeping item, not an investable variable.

**Fuel shock (materialized on the acquired asset).** Allegiant disclosed paying approximately $4.20 per gallon for jet fuel in Q2 2026 against a late-June U.S. average near $2.87. The Q1 2026 SNCY adjusted-EPS miss of 19.7% and the derived ~$12.4M year-over-year GAAP decline in Q1 net income are consistent with that shock reaching Sun Country's scheduled-passenger book. Sun Country's scheduled segment bore full fuel exposure; CMI cargo and some charter arrangements were structurally insulated where the counterparty supplied fuel, but 2025 contract terms are unverified.

**Integration execution.** Both brands operate separately for 18–24 months from mid-2026; separate crews, aircraft and certificates; mixed itineraries on a single ticket not yet possible; loyalty program unresolved; single labor agreements under negotiation, with seniority-list integration historically the most contentious and cost-uncertain element of airline mergers. Synergies are largely unavailable during that window while dual-certificate costs are incurred.

**Lease-adjusted leverage and asset intensity.** Net PP&E at 79.8% of assets and roughly $349M of untagged non-current liabilities mean true adjusted leverage is materially above the tagged 0.51x debt/equity. Interest coverage of 2.73x is adequate but not comfortable for a fuel-exposed cyclical; a demand downturn coinciding with $4+ fuel would compress it quickly.

**Goodwill.** $222.2M carried unchanged since at least 2022 equals 35.5% of book equity. No impairment through FY2025, but it is a meaningful fraction of equity exposed to a sustained downturn.

**Unverified cargo/charter concentration.** Neither the presence nor the absence of material counterparty concentration was confirmed. If concentration was high, contract renewal is a first-order durability question for the asset Allegiant now owns.

**Disclosure gaps.** Item 1, Item 1A and Item 7 of the FY2025 10-K were not readable, so no statement can be made about risk factors added or modified versus FY2024. No Form 4 data was obtained, so no claim is made about insider activity ahead of the January 2026 announcement. Merger-objection announcements from Rowley Law and Halper Sadeh in January 2026 are near-universal in U.S. public M&A, carry very low information content, and no substantive surviving proceeding or injunction was found.

No options data is available for a delisted security, so no options-market signal section is included.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (does not apply to SNCY).** The asset Allegiant acquired was one of two profitable survivors in a segment where a major competitor exited, with a three-legged revenue model that partially decouples earnings from fuel, $157.1M of FY2025 operating cash flow, a rebuilt $144.7M cash balance, one overlapping route with the acquirer, and $140M of targeted annual synergies against ~$199M of standalone GAAP EBITDA. If Allegiant executes the certificate consolidation on schedule and fuel normalizes toward the $2.87 spot average rather than the $4.20 Q2 paid price, the combined franchise earns materially more than the two standalone businesses did. That case is expressible only through ALGT, which is outside the scope of this report.

**Bear case (the operative one for SNCY).** There is no security. Forward participation is zero regardless of how the combination performs. Even setting the instrument aside, the standalone record supports caution about capitalizing the asset's reported earnings: operating income fell 21.1% from 2023 to 2025 while revenue rose 7.4%; ROE halved from 14.0% to 8.8%; margins compressed through a year when the fuel line fell 10%; buyback pace collapsed in 2025 (diluted shares down only 0.4%, basic shares up 208 thousand); and the proxy's TSR index shows roughly a 60% loss from the IPO measurement baseline through 2024 against a peer index down about 41%. Five consecutive profitable years coexisted with durable value destruction for shareholders.

No such action has been announced, and Allegiant's stated plan is the opposite: retirement of the Sun Country brand after a single operating certificate is obtained in 18–24 months from mid-2026.

**Where the exposure went**: Shareholders received a mix of cash and Allegiant stock; those who retained the stock component now hold ALGT. Anyone wishing to underwrite the combined leisure franchise should evaluate ALGT directly, with the three decisive variables being (1) realized jet fuel per gallon versus the ~$4.20 Q2 2026 level, (2) progress toward a single operating certificate and single labor agreements against the stated 18–24 month timeline, and (3) delivery against the $140M annual synergy target, which is large relative to the ~$199M of GAAP EBITDA the acquired business generated in FY2025.
