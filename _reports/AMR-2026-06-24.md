**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Alpha Metallurgical Resources is the leading US pure-play metallurgical (coking) coal producer, supplying roughly 20% of domestic met-coal output to the global steel industry. The investment case rests on a recognizable late-cycle-trough setup: 2025 was the worst year of a multi-year downturn (revenue -28% to $2.12B, a $61.7M net loss, EBITDA down to $122M from $1.74B in 2022), and Q1 2026 marks a clear turn upward, with adjusted EBITDA rising 431% YoY to $30.0M and per-ton margins nearly doubling to $16.41. The balance sheet is the differentiator: essentially debt-free (LT debt ~$9-10M, all finance leases), $476M of liquidity, Altman Z of 3.51, which lets the company survive trough pricing, keep buying back stock (share count down ~27% since 2022), and avoid the distress that stresses leveraged coal peers.

Two underappreciated structural items support the bull case. First, the Section 45X refundable tax credit (added by the July 2025 reconciliation bill) grants AMR a cash benefit estimated at $30-50M/year for 2026-2029, material against $122M of trough EBITDA, and effectively a permanent reduction to the cost curve. AMR already booked $7.2M of this in Q1'26. Second, director Kenneth Courtis, a sophisticated macro investor, has been buying AMR aggressively in the open market on virtually every dip from December 2025 through June 2026, accumulating to roughly 985,000 shares, the strongest class of insider signal and unusual in both size and persistence.

The counterweight is equally concrete. AMR is a pure-play on met-coal price and global steel demand, with operating leverage that took it from a $1.45B profit (2022) to a loss (2025) in three years. The stock has fallen ~34% from its 52-week high of $253.82 (January 2026) to $166.68, and trailing earnings remain negative. There are specific binary liquidity overhangs (the $80-100M black-lung collateral requirement, the NY Climate Superfund Act) and elevated short interest (19.4% of float). On balance this is a reasonable trough holding for an investor who wants commodity-cycle upside with downside protection from the balance sheet, but the slope of the recovery is unconfirmed and the multiple offers no clear margin of safety today. We rate AMR a HOLD.

## Company Overview

AMR is the #1 or #2 US producer of metallurgical coal, operating 19 active mines (14 underground, 5 surface) and 8 prep plants/loadouts across Central Appalachia in Virginia and West Virginia. Met coal is 93% of sales volume and ~96% of coal revenue; thermal coal is a low-value byproduct. The company produced roughly 13.7M tons of met coal in 2025.

The structural advantages differentiate AMR within the basin. The export franchise generates ~73-77% of revenue internationally, with Asia the largest destination and India and Turkey each above 10% of revenue. AMR owns 65% of Dominion Terminal Associates (DTA), the Newport News export terminal, giving it blending, storage, and logistics control. It produces across the full met-coal quality spectrum (Low-Vol through High-Vol B), allowing it to blend to customer specifications and capture quality spreads. Reserves total 294.5M tons (282.8M met), supporting multi-decade mine lives at several complexes.

Met coal feeds blast-furnace steelmaking, and the near-term swing factor is the China-versus-India divergence: Chinese steel output is falling (roughly -10% YoY in late 2025/early 2026) while Indian output is rising (~+9-10%). AMR's heavy India/Asia export tilt, roughly 39% of volume to India over five years, places it on the growth side of that bifurcation.

## Financial Analysis

AMR sits at a cyclical trough. The trajectory off the 2022 super-cycle peak is the central fact:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $4.09B | $3.46B | $2.95B | $2.12B (-28%) |
| Net income | $1.45B | $722M | $188M | -$61.7M |
| Adj. EBITDA | $1.74B | $1.03B | $408M | $122M |
| Non-GAAP coal margin/ton | — | — | $30.64 | $14.85 |

FY2025 swung to a loss as realized price fell -17.9% to $117/ton and volumes fell -10.8% on weak global steel demand. Management's cost response was aggressive: idling higher-cost mines (Checkmate Powellton/Elk Run, Long Branch), reducing production at Jerry Fork/Black Eagle, implementing Q2 2025 wage reductions, suspending the 401(k) match, and cutting SG&A. Non-GAAP cost per ton fell 8.7% to $102.23.

Q1 2026 is the first quarter of the recovery. Adjusted EBITDA reached $30.0M versus $5.7M a year earlier (+431%), and the net loss narrowed to -$11.0M from -$33.9M. Per-ton margin nearly doubled to $16.41 from $8.27 as realization rose 4.9% to $124.39 on a richer product mix. The quarter was further depressed by a planned month-long DTA terminal outage, elevated repair and maintenance, and a diesel-price spike tied to the Iran conflict; management pre-announced because consensus was too high, and explicitly framed Q1 as the year's low point for shipment cadence. The earnings-surprise record reinforces the noise: the Q1'26 reported EPS of -$0.86 missed an estimate of +$0.91 by a wide margin, but the prior three quarters all beat. The combination tells you the per-share earnings line is being whipsawed by mix, timing, and trough-level operating leverage rather than by structural deterioration.

The balance sheet anchors the thesis. LT debt is essentially all finance leases (~$9-10M), D/E is 0.48, the current ratio is 4.47, and the Altman Z of 3.51 signals low bankruptcy risk. Cash of $317M plus $50M of short-term investments gives $476M of total liquidity, with an undrawn $225M ABL maturing in 2029. This near-debt-free position is unusual for a cyclical commodity producer and is the reason AMR can survive trough pricing without dilution or distress.

Capital allocation has been buyback-led and disciplined on timing. Under a $1.5B authorization in place since March 2022, AMR has repurchased ~7.0M shares for ~$1.2B, cutting share count from 17.5M (2022) to 12.75M (Q1'26), about 27%. Management suspended buybacks during the worst of the trough (March 2024 to August 2025) and resumed in August 2025, buying back stock when it had visibility rather than at the peak. About $344M remains authorized. There is no fixed dividend (ceased Q4 2023). FCF was thin in 2025 ($17.8M) given growth capex on the Kingston Wildcat mine and DTA upgrades.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is cyclical recovery layered on two durable items, set against the reality that this is a price-taking commodity producer.

The cyclical recovery is visible in the data. Met prices have recovered off late-2025 lows: US East Coast Low-Vol rose to ~$195-198/t and Australian Premium Low-Vol to ~$237-242/t by early 2026, helped by Queensland flooding that tightened seaborne supply and widened US/Australian spreads. Rystad projects roughly +70Mt of incremental Asian coal demand in 2026, driven by an LNG shortfall and tight energy markets; while that most directly benefits thermal grades, tight seaborne markets and Australian supply disruptions support met pricing as well.

The two structural items extend the recovery beyond pure price. The Section 45X refundable credit (2.5% of met-coal production costs for 2026-2029) lowers AMR's effective cost curve through the period. On a company that generated trough-year EBITDA, this is material and de-risks the downside. The Kingston Wildcat new Low-Vol underground mine is expected to begin production in Q2 2026, adding premium, higher-margin tonnage. The reinstatement of the 401(k) match in Q1 2026 is a small but real signal that management believes the worst is behind it.

The estimate-revision data is mixed and warrants caution rather than a high-conviction "earnings inflection" read. Current-year EPS estimates were revised +40.2% over the last 30 days (to $6.38) but are still down -50.8% over 90 days (from $12.96). Current-quarter estimates fell -29.3% over 30 days. The next-quarter line is +26.1% over 30 days. This is not the clean, accelerating, +30%-over-90-days revision pattern that reliably precedes re-rating; it is a base that is stabilizing off a sharp downgrade, with the 30-day move turning up while the 90-day move is still deeply negative. The "Next Year" line at $27.29 implies the market expects a return toward mid-cycle earnings, but that is two analysts modeling a full price recovery rather than a confirmed trend. We weight growth as moderately positive, because the recovery is real and the 45X credit is durable, but we do not treat this as a confirmed inflection signal.

## Valuation Assessment

Trailing P/E is meaningless here (-55.5x on -$4.75 EPS), as it always is at a cyclical trough. The relevant question is forward and normalized earnings power against peers and against the cycle.

On current-year consensus EPS of $6.38, AMR trades at ~26x; on next-year consensus of $27.29, it trades at ~6.1x. The wide gap is the entire cycle: the stock is cheap if you believe the through-cycle earnings power is closer to the next-year number and expensive if you believe the current year better represents the normalized environment. Peer context partially helps: Warrior Met Coal (HCC), the closest met-coal comparable, trades at 36x, and Cleveland-Cliffs (CLF) is loss-making at -6.0x, so AMR is not screening expensive within a sector where most names carry distorted or negative multiples. But the steel/met-coal peer set is heterogeneous and the median P/E of 36.0 reflects the same trough-earnings distortion across the group.

We are deliberately cautious on rewarding "cheapness" here. AMR is a Materials-sector commodity name, and across this analyst's track record both cheap-screening valuations and Materials/Energy-sector calls have sorted the wrong way against the market. A low forward multiple on a price-taking producer is a function of where you sit in the cycle, not an independent margin of safety. The valuation becomes genuinely attractive only if met prices hold or rise from current levels and 2026 EBITDA recovers toward the $300-400M range, a plausible but not confirmed outcome.

The analyst price-target cluster (low $194, high $195, mean $194.50) sits ~17% above the current price. We do not treat that as a firm floor here, because AMR is a cyclical with a small analyst following and a wide bull/bear earnings band. The targets are useful as a directional reference but not as confirmation of mispricing. The multiple offers neither a compelling discount nor a clear stretch; valuation is fair, scored at the midpoint.

## Competitive Landscape

AMR's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving on a relative basis, driven less by share gains than by balance-sheet resilience in a downturn that stresses peers.

The company competes domestically with Appalachian and Illinois-basin producers and internationally with Australian and Canadian met-coal suppliers. Competition turns on coal quality (volatility/coke yield), freight and logistics, and FX. AMR's full-spectrum quality range and ownership of the DTA export terminal give it blending and logistics advantages that peers without terminal control lack. Its India/Asia export tilt places it on the growth side of global steel demand.

The clearest competitive read-through is the peer comparison through the trough. Peabody reported a weak Q1 2026, and Cleveland-Cliffs is loss-making, confirming that AMR's weak 2025/early-2026 is cyclical rather than company-specific. In a downturn that pressures leveraged producers' liquidity, AMR's near-zero debt is a durable relative advantage: it can keep capex flowing into growth tonnage (Kingston Wildcat) and repurchase shares while competitors retrench. The Section 45X credit applies industry-wide, so it is not a relative moat, but it does improve through-cycle economics for the basin.

What keeps this from a higher score is that AMR is not gaining structural share or widening a moat: it holds a strong position in a shrinking, ESG-constrained industry with limited access to new capital, surety bonds, and insurance. The trajectory is steady rather than improving.

## Risk Assessment

The risks here are unusually specific for a commodity name and several are binary, which is why we score risk factors below neutral despite the strong balance sheet.

**Commodity cyclicality is the dominant risk** and is structurally priced; the 2022-to-2025 swing illustrates the operating leverage. China steel output is falling, which partially offsets the India growth tailwind. This is permanently embedded in any met-coal investment.

**Black-lung self-insurance collateral** is a specific, quantified, near-term overhang. The DOL's January 2025 Final Rule imposes a 100% minimum collateral requirement that, if enforced, would require $80-100M of additional collateral. Implementation is paused pending new DOL leadership, but it remains an unresolved liquidity claim.

**The NY Climate Change Superfund Act** could impose material, ongoing cash charges on fossil-fuel producers including AMR. The company is challenging it (alongside DOJ/EPA) as unconstitutional and believes it unlikely to be upheld, but if upheld, or copied by other states, liquidity would be materially affected. This is a low-probability, high-impact binary.

**Other quantified items:** the MSHA respirable silica rule (stayed by the 8th Circuit) would raise mining costs if enforced; legacy liabilities include $227M ARO ($498M undiscounted), ~$83-87M pension net obligation, and ~$130M black-lung obligation; customer concentration runs to 82% in the top 10, with three customers each above 10%; and DTA terminal reliability already caused the Q1'26 shipment shortfall and requires ~$21M/year of capex over five years.

**Two cybersecurity material-incident 8-Ks within roughly eight months** (September 2025 and May 2026) is an operational/IT red flag worth monitoring; financial impact is not quantified in available filings.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 65.7% against 1-year HV of 61.1% (rich by 4.6 points), in the 73rd percentile of the current chain, with the term structure in backwardation (1-month vol above 6-month): the market expects elevated near-term realized volatility, consistent with a binary commodity-cycle and earnings setup. Skew is balanced at the 1-month tenor (+1.2%) and the put/call OI ratio fell sharply from 1.16 to 0.56 over the last month, which is not the put-heavy hedging you would expect if institutions were aggressively positioning for downside. Short interest is elevated with 6.2 days to cover, meaningful bearish positioning that also creates squeeze potential on any positive price/met-coal surprise. The market is pricing a wide move in either direction rather than a directional collapse, and the implied ±13% near-term move is large enough that entry timing matters.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
AMR is a textbook trough setup with downside protection. Met prices have bottomed and are recovering; management has flagged Q1'26 as the low point; the 45X refundable credit structurally lowers the cost curve through 2029; Kingston Wildcat adds premium tonnage in Q2'26; and the near-debt-free balance sheet lets management keep buying back stock at depressed prices. Director Courtis' relentless open-market accumulation is a sophisticated insider averaging down through the trough. If met prices hold near current levels and 2026 EBITDA recovers, the stock re-rates toward and beyond the analyst targets. Bull case ~$254 (a return to the 52-week high on confirmed mid-cycle recovery), roughly +52% from current.

### Bear Case
Met-coal prices roll back over on a deeper China-led global steel slowdown; the cyclical recovery stalls; the black-lung collateral requirement is enforced or the NY Climate Superfund Act survives challenge; and customer concentration or DTA reliability produces another negative quarter. With negative trailing earnings and a balance sheet that protects against bankruptcy but not against a sustained low-price environment, the stock retraces toward its 52-week low of $97.41, roughly -42% from current. The base case sits in between: a gradual, choppy recovery where 2026 EBITDA recovers off the trough, the stock holds the $150-185 zone, and the multiple normalizes as forward earnings firm up.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD: a watch-and-accumulate-on-weakness situation rather than a confident directional call. The recovery is real but its slope is unconfirmed, the valuation offers no clear margin of safety today, and the sector carries a structurally unfavorable risk/reward against the market.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of confirmed met-price stabilization with realized price per ton holding above ~$130 and per-ton margins expanding above ~$25, signaling the recovery is durable rather than a one-quarter mix benefit.
- Resolution or formal withdrawal of the black-lung 100% collateral rule and/or a favorable ruling on the NY Climate Superfund Act, removing the binary liquidity overhangs.
- A pullback toward the high-$120s/low-$140s on unchanged or improving fundamentals, which would restore an asymmetric entry; director Courtis has been buying in the $175-201 range, so meaningful weakness below that is where the risk/reward turns clearly favorable.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second consecutive quarter of sequential EBITDA decline or realized-price deterioration, indicating the Q1'26 turn has failed.
- Enforcement of the black-lung collateral requirement combined with a material met-price decline that pressures the liquidity position.
- Evidence the China-led global steel demand contraction is deepening faster than India growth can offset.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected early-to-mid August), which should confirm or refute the recovery cadence and the Kingston Wildcat production ramp. The 45X credit accrual and buyback pace are the two cleanest read-throughs to monitor at that print.

## Conclusion

Alpha Metallurgical Resources is the best-capitalized US pure-play metallurgical coal producer, sitting at a cyclical trough with a Q1'26 turn, a durable new tax-credit tailwind, and a strong balance sheet that enables continued share repurchase at depressed prices. The director's persistent open-market buying and the 45X cash benefit are genuine positives that a generic "coal is cyclical" screen would miss. Those strengths are offset by negative trailing earnings, mixed estimate-revision signals that fall short of a confirmed inflection, specific binary liquidity overhangs, elevated short interest, and a valuation that is fair rather than cheap once you adjust for where AMR sits in the cycle.

For an investor seeking commodity-cycle upside with structural downside protection, AMR is a reasonable position to hold and to add to on weakness toward the low-$140s or below. We do not see a high-conviction entry at the current price, given the unconfirmed recovery slope and the sector's unfavorable risk/reward history. **Recommendation: HOLD.**
