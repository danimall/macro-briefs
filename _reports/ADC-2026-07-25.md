**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Agree Realty is a fully integrated net-lease retail REIT that has built one of the highest-quality portfolios in its sector: 2,756 freestanding properties, 99.7% leased, with roughly 65% of annualized base rent from investment-grade-rated tenants. Its business is straightforward and defensive: long-duration net leases (weighted-average term ~7.8 years) where tenants pay taxes, insurance, and maintenance, funded by a conservative balance sheet. Net debt to enterprise value sits at 28.5%, well below most peers, and roughly 96% of debt is fixed-rate at a 4.04% weighted-average cost, so a 100bp move in rates costs only about $4.7M in annual interest.

The central tension in the ADC story is that per-share value creation is modest despite rapid gross growth. Rental revenue rose 16% in 2025 to $718M and Q1 2026 rental income was up 19% year-over-year, but AFFO per share grew only 4-7% because the company self-funds acquisitions with continuous equity issuance. Share count has expanded roughly 30% since 2022, and at quarter-end ADC carried $1.37B of unsettled forward equity, a pre-funded pipeline that is also a latent share overhang. The entire external-growth model depends on maintaining a premium cost of equity (issuing stock above NAV to buy assets at ~7.1-7.2% cap rates at a positive spread). This makes the stock a rate-sensitive bond proxy whose growth engine works only when its multiple cooperates.

The clearest recent positive is a cluster of open-market insider purchases in May-June 2026: the CEO bought ~$1.0M at $75.41, the Executive Chairman bought ~$357K at $71.41, and a large independent director bought ~$1.49M at $74.57, all in the low-$70s, below today's ~$80. These are conviction buys, not grants, and they were well-timed. Combined with a well-covered ~3.9% dividend (71% AFFO payout) and a genuinely defensive balance sheet, ADC is a solid income holding. But with the stock recovered near its highs, a trailing/forward multiple in the low-40s on GAAP EPS (mid-teens to high-teens on AFFO), and estimate revisions essentially flat, the appreciation case is limited. We rate ADC a HOLD on an income basis.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1971 by Executive Chairman Richard Agree and NYSE-listed since 1994, ADC is run today by his son, CEO Joey Agree. It operates as a single-segment net-lease REIT focused on freestanding retail assets leased to national and regional retailers. Growth comes through three channels: acquisitions (the bulk of deployment), ground-up development, and a Developer Funding Platform (DFP) that lets the company flex to wherever spreads are most attractive.

The portfolio is diversified by tenant, sector, and geography. No tenant exceeds 10% of ABR; the top ten include Walmart (5.6%), Tractor Supply (4.9%), Dollar General (3.9%), O'Reilly (3.0%), TJX (3.0%), Best Buy (3.0%), CVS (2.9%), Kroger (2.9%), Lowe's (2.9%), and Hobby Lobby (2.9%). By sector, grocery leads at 10.3%, followed by home improvement (9.0%), convenience (7.7%), tire/auto (7.6%), auto parts (6.7%), and dollar stores (6.4%). The largest state exposure is Texas at 6.9%. Management's stated investment discipline (e-commerce resistance, recession resistance, avoidance of over-levered PE-sponsored tenants, and durable/fungible real estate) positions ADC as a quality-over-quantity consolidator competing with Realty Income, NNN REIT, and Essential Properties.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue and earnings growth have been rapid and acquisition-driven. Rental revenue climbed from $430M (2022) to $537M (2023), $617M (2024), and $718M (2025). Net income rose from $152M to $205M over the same span. AFFO per share moved from $3.95 (2023) to $4.14 (2024) to $4.33 (2025). Q1 2026 rental income was $200.7M versus $169.1M a year prior (+19%), net income was up 32% year-over-year, and AFFO per share reached $1.14 versus $1.06 (+7.5%), implying a run-rate near $4.55-$4.60.

The dilution mechanism is the whole story. Diluted shares plus OP units grew from 79.2M (2022) to 111.5M (2025), roughly 41% expansion, which is why AFFO per share compounds at only mid-single digits despite ~$1.5B/year of gross investment. The $1.37B of unsettled forward equity pre-funds the acquisition pipeline (a genuine financing-risk reducer) while representing shares priced but not yet issued. Value creation is therefore contingent on ADC continuing to trade at a premium to NAV, so the equity can be issued above the price at which assets are bought.

The balance sheet is the clearest differentiator. Total debt is ~$3.72B, nearly all fixed-rate after swaps; only ~$470M of commercial paper floats. Maturities are well-laddered with no meaningful wall: the largest near-term item is CP that rolls (backstopped by a $1.25B revolver), and only $50M of notes come due in 2027. Total liquidity is ~$2.28B. The company is in compliance with all covenants.

Operating cash flow was $504M in 2025. The monthly dividend was raised twice during the year, reaching $0.262/month ($3.144 annualized). Against AFFO per share of ~$4.33, the payout ratio is roughly 71%, well-covered with room to keep raising. One accounting note: the Altman Z-Score (2.15, "grey zone") and low ROA/ROE (2.1%/3.3%) are artifacts of applying corporate-industrial screens to a depreciation-heavy REIT and should not drive the assessment. FFO/AFFO coverage and leverage/coverage ratios are the correct lenses, and they are healthy.

## Growth Analysis

Gross growth is robust and self-financing. Q1 2026 saw 85 property acquisitions for $404M at a ~7.1% cap rate with an 11.3-year weighted-average lease term, extending the portfolio to 2,756 assets. The higher-rate environment has widened acquisition cap rates, which is favorable for a well-capitalized buyer that can still raise accretive equity. ADC's ~$10B asset base remains a fraction of Realty Income's, leaving a long runway to consolidate a fragmented market.

The problem is per-share translation. Consensus places current-year EPS at $1.92 and next-year at $1.93, and the revision trend is flat-to-slightly-negative: next-quarter estimates are down 2.7% over 90 days, and next-year estimates fell 3.4% over 90 days and 1.7% over the last 30 days. This is not the >30% positive revision velocity that flags a genuine earnings acceleration; it is a mature, mid-single-digit compounder whose consensus is drifting sideways. The earnings track record reinforces the modest visibility: three misses in the last four quarters (albeit small: -1.4%, -1.8%, -5.9%) before a 3.1% beat in Q1 2026.

On a thematic basis, ADC does not sit inside the high-conviction growth themes driving the broader market (AI infrastructure, power, memory). Its growth is a steady function of cost-of-capital arbitrage. When the multiple is high and rates cooperate, the flywheel spins; when the stock de-rates, growth slows because the accretion math breaks. That contingency is why we score growth prospects at the lower end: the mid-single-digit AFFO/share trajectory is durable but unspectacular, and the near-term revision drift removes any upside surprise from consensus.

## Valuation Assessment

On the headline metrics ADC screens expensive: trailing and forward P/E near 43x on GAAP EPS of $1.77-$1.92. That figure is distorted by REIT depreciation and is not the right anchor. On AFFO per share of ~$4.33 or ~$4.55-$4.60 run-rate, the stock trades at roughly 17-18x AFFO, a premium multiple that reflects ADC's quality (high IG mix, low leverage) but leaves little margin.

Against Retail REIT peers, the picture is nuanced. Peer median P/E is 22.4x with a 3.9% median yield; ADC's dividend yield is in line, while its earnings multiple looks elevated only because GAAP EPS understates cash flow more here than at peers with different depreciation profiles. NNN REIT, the closest net-lease comparable, trades at 22.4x P/E with a 5.2% yield, meaningfully above ADC's yield. On a yield basis ADC is a premium-priced name in its group, which is defensible given its balance-sheet quality and IG tenant concentration but is not a mispricing.

Applying the valuation lessons directly: a "cheap" screen is not present here, and even if it were, low multiples in isolation are not a buy signal. What ADC lacks is an identified catalyst for multiple expansion. The most plausible upside driver is a decline in the 10-year Treasury, which would compress the bond-proxy spread and re-rate the stock, but that is a macro bet, not a company-specific mispricing. Analyst targets (mean $84.75, high $92, low $80) sit modestly above the current ~$80, and with the stock already recovered near its 52-week high and estimates flat, we do not treat those targets as stale floors; they reflect the same modest-growth consensus as the price. The valuation score of 4.5 reflects a fairly-to-fully-valued quality name with balanced-to-slightly-unfavorable risk/reward and no clear undervaluation catalyst.

## Competitive Landscape

ADC's competitive position is stable-to-improving and among the strongest in net lease. Three attributes anchor the moat: (1) an unusually high investment-grade tenant mix (~65-67% of ABR, near the top of the sector), which lowers credit-loss risk and supports durable rent; (2) a fortress balance sheet at 28.5% net debt/EV, which gives it the ability to buy accretively when higher rates stress over-levered competitors; and (3) three sourcing channels (acquisition, development, DFP) that let it deploy where spreads are best. Acquisition cap rates around 7.1-7.2% show the company can still source at attractive yields.

The sector is described in the 10-K as "highly competitive," with public and non-traded REITs, PE firms, and institutional funds bidding for a limited pool of assets. ADC's scale disadvantage versus Realty Income is real, but it cuts the other way for the growth runway: a smaller base against a fragmented market. The main competitive vulnerability is structural exposure within its tenant categories: pharmacy (CVS 2.9%, sector 3.6%, amid Walgreens/Rite Aid stress), dollar stores (6.4%), and discretionary big-box (Best Buy 3.0%). Single-tenant build-to-suit assets carry full downtime and re-tenanting cost on any vacancy. On balance, the portfolio quality and balance-sheet advantage keep the trajectory positive, but this is a market leader in a mature niche rather than a share-gaining disruptor.

## Risk Assessment

**Cost-of-capital dependency (dominant risk).** ADC's growth requires issuing equity above NAV to fund acquisitions at accretive spreads. A material fall in the share price (from higher rates or a sector de-rating) breaks the accretion math, slows growth, and could render the unsettled forward equity dilutive relative to a lower share price. The 10-K explicitly flags that rising rates raise required acquisition yields and that equity issuance dilutes book/fair value.

**Interest-rate sensitivity of the equity.** Even with debt largely hedged, the stock trades as a bond proxy and is sensitive to the 10-year Treasury. In the current higher-for-longer regime, spread compression and multiple pressure are the live threats. This is the single largest swing factor on the stock, and it is external to the business.

**Tenant credit and concentration.** Diversification is genuine, but exposure to structurally challenged categories (pharmacy, dollar stores, discretionary big-box) means idiosyncratic credit events carry full re-tenanting cost. The Q1 2026 10-Q added new forward-looking language on the "adverse impact of tariffs" on retail tenants, and one tenant moved to cash-basis accounting: small items, but the correct early-warning indicators to monitor.

**Governance nuance.** Founder-family control and a related-party Reimbursement Agreement (Executive Chairman reimburses the company for his proportionate share of certain debt losses) are flagged as potential conflicts around the timing/pricing of sales and refinancings for tax reasons. Governance is otherwise clean: no executive departures, no restatements, effective disclosure controls, and a board with eight of ten independent directors and a Lead Independent Director.

Overall, the known risks are well-understood and largely priced, and the balance-sheet defensiveness (96% fixed-rate debt, minimal near-term maturities, 71% payout) blunts the downside. There is no binary regulatory or refinancing event on the horizon. That supports a risk score at the upper-middle of the range, consistent with the recent improvement in this dimension.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign. Near-term ATM IV is 18.0% versus 16.3% one-year historical volatility, essentially fair, in the 8th percentile of the current chain, indicating no pricing of an imminent catalyst. The term structure is flat (18.0% near, 17.1% far), and the implied one-month move is ±4.2%. The 8.3% put/call skew at the 3-month tenor shows some downside hedging demand, which is typical for a rate-sensitive REIT, but the put/call volume ratio of 0.33 is light. IV fell from 20.8% to 18.0% as the stock recovered 9.5% since the prior snapshot, consistent with resolved near-term uncertainty. Short interest is negligible (0.0% of float). The options market corroborates a low-drama, income-oriented holding rather than a stock priced for a large move.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$92, +15%).** Rates decline, ADC's bond-proxy multiple re-rates, and its premium cost of equity re-widens, restoring the accretive-acquisition flywheel. The company continues deploying ~$1.5B/year at ~7.1% cap rates against a lower cost of capital, AFFO/share reaccelerates toward the high single digits, and the dividend keeps rising off a 71% payout. The insider-buying cluster proves prescient. This path requires macro cooperation more than company execution.

**Base Case (~$85, +6%).** The status quo persists: mid-single-digit AFFO/share growth, a well-covered and slowly rising ~3.9% dividend, and a stable premium multiple. Total return is roughly the dividend plus modest per-share growth, a mid-single-digit annual return dominated by income. This matches the flat consensus and analyst mean target.

**Bear Case (~$68-70, -12 to -15%).** Higher-for-longer rates compress the spread and de-rate the multiple toward peer levels. The forward-equity overhang settles into a weaker share price, growth stalls, and tenant credit deteriorates in pharmacy/discretionary categories as tariffs pressure retailers. The 52-week low of $69.56 anchors this scenario.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: $85.** Roughly 18-19x AFFO per share of ~$4.55-$4.60 run-rate, in line with the current premium multiple and the analyst mean of $84.75. This sits between the bear and bull cases.

**Bull case target: $92** (analyst high; +15% from ~$80), contingent on a rate-driven multiple re-rating and reaccelerating AFFO/share.

**Bear case target: $68-70** (-12% to -15%), driven by rate-led multiple compression toward peers and/or tenant-credit deterioration.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with quarterly earnings (next print following the July 2026 mid-year update) and Fed rate-path developments as the natural checkpoints.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate-revision inflection — next-year AFFO/EPS estimates turning positive (>+3% over 90 days) alongside a reacceleration in AFFO/share above 7%.
- A meaningful pullback toward the low-$70s (near where insiders bought), which would restore a margin of safety and asymmetric upside.
- A durable decline in the 10-year Treasury that re-widens ADC's investment spread and supports a re-rating.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Tenant-credit deterioration spreading beyond the single cash-basis tenant — additional tenants moving to cash basis or a top-ten tenant bankruptcy.
- Evidence the premium cost of equity has broken (stock trading through NAV, forward equity settling dilutively) forcing an acquisition-pace cut and a growth stall.
- A dividend-coverage deterioration (AFFO payout rising materially above the mid-70s%).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings and any change in the Fed's rate guidance.

## Conclusion

ADC is a high-quality, defensively financed net-lease REIT with a well-covered and growing dividend, a top-decile investment-grade tenant base, and a management team that just demonstrated conviction with a cluster of open-market purchases in the low-$70s. Those are the ingredients of a durable income holding, and the risk profile (mostly-hedged debt, no maturity wall, no binary events) is genuinely defensive.

What the stock lacks is an appreciation catalyst. Per-share growth is structurally capped at mid-single digits by the dilutive equity-funded model, estimate revisions are flat-to-negative, and the multiple already reflects the quality premium. The most plausible upside, a rate-driven re-rating, is a macro bet rather than a company-specific mispricing, and against a higher-for-longer regime that bet is uncertain. Having been rated HOLD across three prior reports, the bar for a change is higher, and the current data confirms rather than reverses the range-bound thesis: growth prospects remain soft (consistent with the earlier downgrade in that dimension), while the risk picture has genuinely improved on the insider signal and defensive balance sheet. We rate ADC a HOLD on an income basis. Own it for the ~3.9% growing yield and downside resilience; look to add on a pullback toward the low-$70s where insiders themselves were buyers.
