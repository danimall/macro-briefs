**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

KeyCorp is a Cleveland-based super-regional bank with roughly $184 billion in total assets, operating a 15-state branch footprint alongside a national institutional franchise anchored by KeyBanc Capital Markets. The investment case rests on a two-year recovery arc: a deliberate ~$1.8 billion after-tax securities-repositioning loss in late 2024, enabled by a strategic equity injection from Bank of Nova Scotia, cleared the balance sheet of low-yielding underwater bonds and set up a clean 2025-2026 earnings recovery. That recovery is now delivering: Q2 2026 EPS of $0.44 was up roughly 26% year over year, net interest margin is expanding toward a 3.00-3.05% exit rate, and average loan growth is guided to 4-5%. The company beat consensus in each of the last four quarters.

The problem for new capital is that most of this is priced. At $23.11 the stock trades near its 52-week high, at roughly 13.5x trailing earnings, essentially in line with the regional-bank peer median of 13.0x, and yields about 3.6%. The +26% EPS growth is partly a low-base effect against 2024's artificial trough; consensus estimate revisions have flattened, with the current quarter marked down 1.3% over 30 days and next year edged down 0.5%. The business is getting fundamentally better, but the market has already absorbed the recovery narrative.

We rate KEY a HOLD (growth_hold). The fundamentals are sound and improving, the balance sheet is well-capitalized (CET1 11.78%), and the dividend is covered. But at a peer-median multiple with flattening estimates and specific overhangs (commercial real estate concentration, the Scotiabank buyback-linked share dynamic, and a pending capital rule that would tie regulatory ratios to rate volatility) the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. The prior BUY calls in April-May captured the re-rating from the high-$17s to the low-$20s; that move is done.

## Company Overview

KeyCorp operates through KeyBank N.A. across 940 branches and 1,120 ATMs in 15 states, organized into two segments:

- **Consumer Bank** — retail deposits, mortgage and home-equity lending, credit cards, student-loan refinancing, and wealth/investment services.
- **Commercial Bank** — a middle-market Commercial arm within the footprint plus a nationally-operating Institutional arm with sector-specialized coverage (Consumer, Energy, Healthcare, Industrial, Real Estate, Technology, Public Sector). The Institutional segment houses KeyBanc Capital Markets (KBCM), offering debt/equity underwriting, M&A advisory, syndicated finance, sales and trading, and public finance. Key is also a significant national commercial real estate lender and a third-party master/special servicer of commercial mortgages.

Key sits in the "super-regional" tier as a Category IV bank ($100-250B assets). Its capital-markets and CRE-servicing capabilities give it more fee-income diversification than a typical branch-based regional, but that same mix makes fee revenue more cyclical than a deposit-heavy peer. Christopher Gorman remains Chairman, President and CEO, a position he has held since May 2020; there is no CEO transition despite prior misreadings in this coverage.

## Financial Analysis

The central financial story is the 2024 loss-to-2025 recovery. In late 2024, management sold low-coupon underwater available-for-sale securities and crystallized the ~$1.8 billion after-tax loss, reinvesting proceeds into higher-yielding assets. That drove a GAAP net loss for 2024. The Scotiabank equity injection recapitalized the balance sheet and let management take the securities hit without impairing capital ratios. 2025 was the clean recovery: net income of approximately $1.8 billion with net interest margin expanding as the repositioned book replaced the legacy drag.

Q2 2026 (reported July 21, 2026) confirmed the trajectory: EPS was roughly 3% above the $0.43 consensus, on revenue of about $1.96 billion (+6.7% YoY). The top line slightly missed estimates; the EPS beat was carried more by NII/margin expansion and expense discipline than by revenue acceleration. Management raised parts of full-year guidance, citing stronger commercial loan growth and fee-income momentum.

Capital and liquidity are solid. At 12/31/2025: CET1 11.78%, Tier 1 13.46%, Total Capital 15.70%, leverage ratio 10.50% — all comfortably above well-capitalized minimums (stress-inclusive CET1 minimum of 7.70%). The stress capital buffer stands at 3.2%. Key is a biennial-test Category IV firm and did not test in 2025.

A note on financial-health screening: the Altman Z-Score of ~0.27 that a generic screen would flag as "distress" is structurally inapplicable to banks, whose deposit-funded models are inherently high-leverage. The correct lens is regulatory capital, which shows a well-capitalized institution. The earnings-beat track record (four consecutive beats, averaging ~5% surprise) supports confidence in near-term consensus. The caveat on quality is that the +26% YoY growth flatters against the 2024 trough; the sustainable run-rate is the mid-single-digit revenue growth the top line is actually delivering.

## Growth Analysis

Forward growth has three components. First, NIM expansion: the repositioned securities book and higher-yielding asset mix continue to lift the margin toward the guided exit rate. Second, loan growth returning at 4-5%, driven by commercial demand. Third, fee-income momentum from KBCM capital markets, which benefits from the current re-acceleration in debt and equity issuance activity.

The durability question is whether growth persists beyond the low-base recovery. Estimate revisions suggest the market thinks the easy gains are captured: current-quarter EPS was revised down 1.3% over 30 days ($0.47 to $0.46), next-quarter down 0.6%, and next-year down 0.5% ($2.15 to $2.14). Current-year did tick up modestly (+0.8% over 90 days to $1.83), but the overall pattern is flat-to-slightly-negative. This does not clear the threshold for an inflection signal (revisions are nowhere near +30%), and it argues against extrapolating the +26% YoY headline forward.

Consensus points to $1.83 current-year and $2.14 next-year EPS — roughly 17% growth off the recovery base, decelerating from the 2025-2026 snapback. That is respectable for a super-regional but not exceptional, and it is well-anchored rather than surprising to the upside. The regulatory backdrop is a modest tailwind: proposals to raise the OCC "Heightened Standards" threshold to $700B (which would exempt KeyBank), a CRA-rule rollback, and resolution-plan relief are directionally favorable for a bank Key's size.

Key trades at a slight premium to the peer median P/E and offers an above-median yield. The multiple is neither cheap nor stretched; it reflects a bank that has recovered from a self-inflicted trough and is now valued in line with its higher-quality super-regional peers. The above-median dividend yield provides absolute support, but there is no discount to arbitrage.

The valuation dimension warrants skepticism here for two reasons. First, cheapness at a peer-median multiple is not a mispricing — there is no identifiable catalyst that would force a re-rating above the group. Second, in this environment a "reasonable" multiple in Financials has historically not translated into selection alpha; the sector runs a structural alpha deficit against the broad market, so a fair multiple here is closer to fully-valued than to a bargain. The analyst mean target of $25.96 (median $25.00) implies ~12% upside to the mean; the range is wide ($22 low to $43 high), and the $43 outlier should be treated as noise rather than a base case. This is a fairly-valued bank, not a mispriced one.

## Competitive Landscape

Key's competitive edge is fee-income diversification. KBCM capital markets, commercial mortgage servicing, and wealth management reduce reliance on pure spread lending relative to a typical branch-based regional. This differentiates Key on the upside of a fee-generation cycle but also makes revenue more cyclical when issuance and advisory activity slow.

The Scotiabank relationship is a genuine strategic asset: a ~14.9%-capped anchor shareholder with two board seats, deep-pocketed capital support (demonstrated in the 2024 recapitalization), and potential US-Canada cross-border commercial referral synergies. The trade-off is a large concentrated holder and the persistent buyback-linked share dynamic (Scotiabank sells shares back to the issuer pro-rata to hold near its cap as Key repurchases stock).

Competitive-position trajectory is modestly improving: Key is regaining loan growth and margin after the deposit-flight episode of 2023, and the regulatory direction (Heightened Standards exemption at $700B) is favorable. But the structural pressures the 10-K explicitly flags — fintech and private-credit disintermediation of core lending, plus competition from larger money-center banks — cap the moat. The competitive-position improvement noted in prior reports (5.5 to 6.5) is holding but not accelerating; we mark it at 6.3.

## Risk Assessment

**Commercial real estate concentration.** Key is a significant national CRE lender and servicer in a sector under stress, particularly office. Credit quality here is the key monitoring item; the new CRO hire (Mohit Ramani, ex-Truist, January 2025) is a sensible risk-function strengthening given the exposure.

**AOCI inclusion in regulatory capital.** This is the single most Key-specific forward risk. A proposed rule would force Category IV banks to include unrealized AFS gains/losses in regulatory capital, tying Key's ratios directly to rate volatility. Given the securities hit Key already took, this rule would make future rate moves flow straight into capital adequacy. In the current higher-for-longer rate environment, this is a real, if not yet dated, overhang.

**Basel III "endgame" / capital tightening.** The broader Capital Proposal could subject Key to the supplementary leverage ratio and expanded risk-based approaches, raising required capital. Timing and scope are uncertain (agencies signaled a re-proposal), so this is a two-sided regulatory item.

**Scotiabank overhang and share dynamics.** The concentrated ~14.9% holder and buyback true-ups are not a bearish insider signal, but they do create a persistent share-supply consideration and governance concentration.

**Deposit competition and funding costs.** Post-2023 deposit stickiness and funding-cost pressure remain ongoing; FDIC special assessments related to the SVB/Signature failures wind down through early 2026.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is neutral and does not challenge the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV is 26.8% versus 23.9% one-year historical vol — a fair 2.9-point spread with no elevated fear premium. IV has fallen 4.1 points since mid-June, consistent with the earnings event having passed. The put/call skew is essentially balanced (-1.5% near-term), the term structure is flat, and the implied move is ±5.8%. The elevated put/call volume ratio (3.26) is not corroborated by open interest (P/C OI 0.43), so it reads as short-dated flow rather than a directional bet. Short interest is negligible at 0.0% of float. The options market prices KEY as a low-drama, fairly-valued name, consistent with a HOLD.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The recovery has further to run. NIM continues expanding to the exit rate and beyond into 2027 as low-coupon assets keep rolling off, loan growth reaccelerates above 5%, and a live capital-markets cycle drives KBCM fee income above expectations. The favorable regulatory direction (Heightened Standards exemption) reduces compliance burden. Estimate revisions turn positive, consensus re-rates the bank toward the upper end of the peer group, and the stock reaches $27-28 (~15x on ~$1.85-1.90 normalized EPS with multiple expansion), roughly 17-21% upside plus the 3.6% yield.

### Bear Case
CRE credit deteriorates as office and multifamily stress feeds through the servicing and lending books, forcing higher provisions. The AOCI-into-capital rule advances into a rate-volatile period, pressuring capital ratios and constraining buybacks. Loan growth disappoints, fee income proves cyclical as issuance slows, and the peer-median multiple compresses toward the group's low (RF/MTB at ~12x) on ~$1.75 EPS, taking the stock to ~$18-19, roughly 18-22% downside. The dividend remains covered but the growth premium evaporates.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the improving earnings trajectory and covered yield justify holding, but the balanced risk/reward does not support adding at a peer-median multiple near the 52-week high.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Estimate revisions turn decisively positive (current-year and next-year EPS revised up >2% over 30 days), confirming the recovery has durable legs rather than a low-base effect.
- A pullback toward the $19-20 range (roughly 11.5-12.5x forward, a discount to the peer median) that restores asymmetric upside.
- Clear evidence CRE credit is stabilizing (flat-to-declining non-performing CRE, adequate reserve coverage) removing the central bear risk.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A material CRE credit event — a sharp rise in non-performers or a reserve build that cuts EPS estimates by >10%.
- The AOCI-into-capital rule advancing to a form that meaningfully compresses CET1 and forces a buyback pause or dividend reassessment.
- Two consecutive quarters of missed EPS or declining loan balances, signaling the recovery has stalled.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 2026 earnings report (mid-October 2026), which will test whether NIM is tracking the exit guidance and whether CRE credit metrics remain contained. Watch also for regulatory clarity on the AOCI and Basel endgame re-proposals.

## Conclusion

KeyCorp has executed a clean recovery from a deliberate 2024 balance-sheet repositioning, and the fundamentals — expanding NIM, returning loan growth, capital-markets fee momentum, four straight earnings beats, and a well-capitalized balance sheet — are genuinely improving. That improvement is the reason to hold. The reason not to buy is that at a slight premium to the peer median, near the 52-week high, with flattening estimate revisions and no identifiable catalyst for a re-rating above the group, the mispricing that justified the earlier BUY calls has closed. The prior recommendation history (HOLD → BUY → BUY → HOLD) reflects this: the buy window in the high-$17s to low-$20s captured the recovery re-rating, and the return to HOLD acknowledges the stock has caught up to fair value.

The overhangs — CRE concentration, the AOCI-into-capital rule, and the Scotiabank share dynamic — are real but priced, and none is imminently binary. On balance, KEY is a sound, well-run super-regional trading at a fair price, offering a covered 3.6% yield and ~12-17% upside to consensus targets against comparable downside. That is a HOLD, worth owning for the yield and the ongoing earnings recovery, but not a position to add at current levels.
