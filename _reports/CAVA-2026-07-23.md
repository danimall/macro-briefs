**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CAVA is a company-owned fast-casual Mediterranean chain scaling at 30%+ revenue growth from a still-small base (459 restaurants across 29 states plus D.C. as of the Q1 FY26 10-Q). The investment case turns on a single operational variable: same-restaurant sales (SRS) traffic. FY24 delivered +13.4% SRS, FY25 decelerated hard to +4.0% (traffic only +1.6%), and the market repriced the stock down accordingly. Q1 FY26 reversed that trend, with SRS +9.7% of which +6.8% was traffic, and management raised full-year SRS guidance to 4.5%–6.5% with 75–77 net new openings. This traffic-led reacceleration, not price/mix, is the pivotal datapoint, because it distinguishes genuine demand recovery from menu-price inflation.

The stock has fallen roughly 31% from its 52-week high of $98.79 to $62.35, and against that price the multiple remains demanding: ~116x trailing EPS ($0.54) and roughly 113x current-year consensus EPS ($0.55). This is a growth-stage restaurant where GAAP earnings are structurally suppressed by heavy self-funded capex (capex ≈ operating cash flow) and where a FY24-to-FY25 headline net income decline (from $130.3M to $63.7M) is entirely an artifact of an $80.1M one-time deferred-tax valuation-allowance release rolling off. On a comparable adjusted basis, earnings grew. The balance sheet is clean: zero drawn debt, ~$296M cash at Q1'26, and a newly upsized $150M revolver extended to 2031, so the business self-funds its expansion without financing risk.

We rate CAVA a HOLD (growth_hold) with an overall score of 6.2, unchanged from the prior read. The trajectory has been steadily improving (5.2 in March to 6.0 in June), and the Q1 traffic recovery plus the ~31% price decline have improved the risk/reward. What holds us back from a BUY is threefold: the valuation still prices in near-perfect execution, estimate revisions have flattened over the past 30 days after their early-2026 upgrade, and CAVA sits in Consumer Discretionary/Restaurants, a sector where BUY calls have historically generated negative alpha, raising the bar for company-specific conviction. We want one more quarter confirming that Q1's traffic surge is durable rather than a promotional or weather-aided blip before upgrading.

## Company Overview

CAVA operates a fast-casual Mediterranean concept built around chef-curated, customizable bowls and pitas. The first restaurant opened in Bethesda, MD in 2011; the company incorporated in Delaware in 2015 and is headquartered in Washington, D.C. As of the Q1 FY26 filing (quarter ended April 19, 2026), it ran 459 company-owned restaurants, up from 439 at fiscal year-end 2025 and 367 a year earlier.

The defining structural feature is that CAVA is ~100% company-operated, not franchised, which is unusual for a chain expanding at 70+ units per year. The trade-off is direct: there is no franchise royalty stream and growth is capital-intensive and self-funded, but 100% of unit economics accrue to CAVA and brand/quality control is total. The 2018 Zoes Kitchen acquisition supplied the real-estate base for early expansion, but that conversion pipeline was fully exhausted in October 2023, so all future growth now depends on greenfield development into markets such as South Florida, Detroit, Indianapolis, and Pittsburgh.

There is one reportable segment (CAVA restaurants). A small CPG operation (CAVA Foods, ~$10–14M annual revenue) produces dips, spreads, and dressing bases used in restaurants and sells packaged goods in grocery, extending brand reach without material P&L contribution. The strategic positioning is explicit: to be the category-defining leader in Mediterranean fast casual, the way Chipotle defined Mexican. Key differentiators are brand, culture, digital infrastructure (~38–40% digital mix), and strong new-unit economics with AUVs around $2.9–3.0M.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Growth has been robust and reaccelerating: FY23 $728.7M → FY24 $963.7M (+32%) → FY25 $1,179.7M (+22.4%, the first year above $1B). Q1 FY26 (16 weeks) delivered $438.3M, up 32.1% YoY, a reacceleration off the FY25 rate.

**Same-restaurant sales.** This is the metric that moves the stock. FY24 +13.4% collapsed to FY25 +4.0% (only +1.6% traffic, +2.4% price/mix). Q1 FY26 recovered to +9.7% SRS, of which +6.8% was traffic and +2.9% price/mix. Traffic-led growth is higher quality than price-led growth because it signals real demand rather than the temporary lift of menu-price increases that eventually pressure value perception.

**Unit economics.** AUV of ~$2.9M in FY25 is trending toward ~$3.0M in Q1'26, with new 2025-vintage units trending above $3.0M. Restaurant-level profit margin moved 25.0% (FY24) → 24.4% (FY25) → back to 25.1% (Q1'26). The FY25 dip reflected steak and chicken shawarma input costs plus tariffs; Q1'26 recovered on favorable mix. A ~25% restaurant-level margin is competitive with best-in-class fast casual.

**Profitability and the tax distortion.** GAAP net income fell from $130.3M (FY24) to $63.7M (FY25), but this is entirely the roll-off of a one-time $80.1M deferred-tax valuation-allowance release in FY24. Adjusted net income actually rose from $50.2M to $63.7M, and adjusted EBITDA grew from $126.2M to $152.8M (12.9% margin). Operating income grew 28% to $55.3M (4.7% operating margin). The effective tax rate normalized to 10% in FY25 and 21.5% in Q1'26, which is why reported EPS optics can mislead: Q1'26 EPS of $0.20 versus $0.22 prior-year is a tax-rate effect, not operational deterioration, sitting against +32% revenue and +38% adjusted EBITDA growth.

**Balance sheet and cash flow.** Zero debt drawn. Cash of $282.9M plus $110.1M of fixed-income investments at FY25 year-end; $295.8M cash at Q1'26. In March 2026, the revolver was upsized from $75M to $150M and extended to March 2031 (undrawn, $149.1M available), a prudent liquidity backstop for accelerated growth. Operating cash flow ran $161M (FY24) → $184.8M (FY25) → $64M in Q1'26 alone (+66% YoY). Free cash flow was a modest $26M in FY25 because capex ($158.7M) consumes most of operating cash flow, which is the expected profile for a chain opening 70+ units a year and guiding capex higher in FY26. Current ratio 2.65; equity $779.7M; accumulated deficit shrinking from $253.6M to ~$230M in Q1'26.

**Financial health screens.** Altman Z of 2.85 sits in the grey zone, which is normal for a lease-heavy, growth-stage restaurant and not a distress signal given zero drawn debt. Reported D/E of 0.74 is largely operating-lease driven, not real leverage. The earnings-surprise record supports moderate confidence in forward estimates: three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with beats of 11.5% (Q1'26), 52.1% (Q4'25), and 16.3% (Q2'25), and a single 5.8% miss (Q3'25).

The financial profile is genuinely strong for the growth stage (self-funding, unlevered, cash-generative at the operating level), which is why we assign Financial Health a 7.0, consistent with the prior upgrade to that level and the most reliable dimension in our framework.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine has two levers: unit count and comps. Unit growth is guided at 75–77 net new openings in FY26 against a base of 439 at year-end 2025, roughly 17% square-footage growth, entirely greenfield now that the Zoes conversion pipeline is exhausted. New-market entries (South Florida, Detroit, Indianapolis, Pittsburgh) carry unproven-demand risk for a cuisine category that is still being established, but 2025-vintage units trending above $3.0M AUV suggest new-unit economics remain intact.

The comp lever is where the story lives. The Q1 FY26 traffic reacceleration is the single most important fundamental datapoint. It occurred while the broader restaurant industry was contracting (Zacks Retail-Restaurants industry –1.7% over six months while CAVA was +36.7% on a same-store basis), so this is company-specific execution rather than a sector tailwind. Management raised the FY26 SRS guide to 4.5%–6.5%, a meaningful step up from the FY25 outcome.

Estimate revisions temper the enthusiasm. Current-year EPS consensus of $0.55 was revised up 6.0% over 90 days but is essentially flat (-0.5%) over the trailing 30 days. Next-year consensus of $0.75 is up 4.8% over 90 days but down 0.4% over 30 days. The current-quarter estimate ticked down 0.7% over 30 days after a +10.2% 90-day gain. The pattern is one of positive momentum from earlier in 2026 that has now stalled; the estimate-revision signal is not the +30% velocity that would flag a high-conviction BUY, and the recent flattening is why we do not treat rising estimates as an unambiguous upgrade trigger yet.

We score Growth Prospects at 7.0, a modest step down from the prior 7.5. This reflects the strong Q1 print and reaccelerating revenue balanced against the fact that estimate revisions have flattened over the most recent 30 days. We treat growth as a directional positive but confirm it against the stronger financial-health signal rather than leaning on it alone.

## Valuation Assessment

At $62.35, CAVA trades at ~116x trailing EPS ($0.54) and ~113x current-year consensus EPS ($0.55). Even applying the cycle-adjusted framework, where a growth-stage restaurant's GAAP EPS is structurally suppressed by heavy capex and a normalizing tax rate, the forward multiple is demanding. Next-year consensus of $0.75 implies ~83x forward EPS. On adjusted EBITDA of ~$153M (FY25) against a ~$7.3B market cap, the EV/EBITDA multiple is ~47x on trailing and still elevated on forward.

Peer context underscores the premium. The restaurant peer median P/E is 22.0 (EAT 15.5, DPZ 18.0, CAKE 22.0, WING 38.3, BROS 102.6). CAVA and Dutch Bros are the two hyper-growth outliers trading at triple-digit multiples; the mature franchise-model peers (Domino's, Brinker) trade at 15–18x. CAVA's premium is justified in kind by its 30%+ revenue growth and traffic reacceleration, but the absolute level leaves no cushion for a comp miss. The risk profile is asymmetric, with beats largely expected and misses punished severely (the FY25 deceleration and the 31% drawdown from the high demonstrate this).

The ~31% decline from the 52-week high to $62.35 is the key valuation improvement. Prior price appreciation is not itself a reason to be bearish, and by symmetry the decline is not itself a reason to be more constructive unless fundamentals are unchanged or improving. Here fundamentals improved through Q1 (traffic reaccelerated, guidance raised), so a lower price on improving fundamentals is a better entry than it was at $91 in mid-June. That said, at a >30% drawdown the burden of proof is on identifying what the market is wrong about. The market appears to be pricing (a) continued sponsor supply overhang, with Artal having sold 3.0M shares at $90.30 in June and ~6.5M shares still to monetize; (b) the risk that Q1's traffic surge does not sustain into a tougher consumer H2; and (c) a broad de-rating of consumer-discretionary growth names. None of these is a fundamental impairment of the business, but they are legitimate reasons the multiple has compressed.

Analyst targets span $55 (low) to $110 (high), with a mean of $93.04 and median of $95.00, well above the current price. On a stock at 113x current-year EPS, however, we do not treat analyst targets as a floor; at premium multiples targets reflect the same growth extrapolation as the price and are vulnerable to any disappointment. The gap between current price and consensus targets reflects the recent drawdown that analysts have not yet fully re-cut rather than a clean mispricing signal.

We score Valuation at 5.5. This is neither cheap nor a clear value trap. Our framework treats "cheap" as anti-predictive, but CAVA is not cheap on any conventional metric; the score reflects a fair-to-slightly-rich growth-adjusted valuation where the recent pullback has restored some asymmetry without making the entry compelling.

## Competitive Landscape

CAVA is effectively creating and leading the Mediterranean fast-casual category, the analog to Chipotle in Mexican. Its restaurant-level margin (~25%) is already competitive with best-in-class fast casual, and its unit growth (30%+) outpaces mature peers. The Chipotle comparison frames the analyst debate: CAVA offers superior growth, Chipotle offers larger scale and more mature margins.

The competitive moat rests on brand, culture (repeatedly cited internally as a differentiator), a ~38–40% digital mix, and strong new-unit economics. The company is also investing in efficiency: a convertible note in Hyphen Technologies ($5M initial plus $5M milestone, marked to $10.7M) gives it exposure to automated makeline technology, and it is deploying AI video systems to track ingredient depletion. These are early-stage labor-cost/throughput plays, appropriately framed as both opportunity and operational/reputational risk.

The trajectory is improving: CAVA is gaining share in a category it is largely defining, with traffic-led comps outperforming a contracting industry. The primary competitive risk is explicitly cited in the 10-K, that competitors will mimic the business model, menu, and concept as the category proves out, along with cannibalization in dense home markets (D.C./MD/VA). We score Competitive Position at 7.0, a modest step down from 7.5 to reflect that the category is still nascent and imitation risk grows with success, but the trajectory remains clearly positive.

## Risk Assessment

The most material company-specific risks:

1. **Comp/traffic volatility.** The entire equity story rests on continued unit growth and positive comps. FY25 showed how fast traffic can decelerate. The Q1'26 recovery is encouraging but one quarter; sustainability is the crux question, and at ~113x current-year EPS any comp miss carries outsized downside.

2. **New-market execution.** Greenfield expansion into markets with unproven Mediterranean demand (South Florida, Detroit, Indianapolis, Pittsburgh) and cannibalization in dense home markets.

3. **Commodity/tariff exposure.** Heavy chicken purchases; imported olive oil, lamb, beef, rice, sugar, and paper are explicitly tariff-impacted, with single-source suppliers for specialty olive oil and steak spec.

4. **Labor-cost inflation.** California AB 1228 Fast Food Council and NYC Fair Workweek/predictive-scheduling laws; ~2% wage inflation absorbed so far.

5. **Capex intensity and drive-thru step-up.** A meaningful portion of FY26+ openings will carry drive-thru pickup, raising capex and real-estate cost. FCF stays thin as capex consumes operating cash flow, which is normal for the stage but leaves less margin for error if comps disappoint.

6. **Sponsor supply overhang.** Artal continues to monetize its pre-IPO stake (~6.5M remaining). This is expected post-lockup but is an ongoing supply headwind.

7. **Executive turnover.** A high cadence of C-suite change during a critical scaling phase: COO departure (Sept 2025), new COO onboarded (Jan 2026), and a CLO transition (May 2026). Founder key-person dependency (CEO Schulman, Chief Concept Officer Xenohristos) is flagged in the 10-K with no key-person insurance.

8. **Consumer discretionary macro.** CAVA's traffic outperformance is company-specific, but it is not immune to a genuine demand pullback if the broader consumer environment softens.

We score Risk Factors at 5.0. The binary/idiosyncratic risk set is manageable given zero debt, but the valuation itself amplifies downside on any comp miss, and the combination of executive churn, sponsor overhang, and tariff/labor exposure keeps this below neutral. Our framework treats risk-factor scores as low-signal for return prediction, so this dimension is context rather than a driver.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not confirming acute near-term downside fear. Near-term ATM IV of 57.9% sits essentially in line with 1-year historical volatility of 58.9% (spread -1.0%, IV percentile 3% within the chain), so options are fairly priced rather than signaling elevated event risk into the next print. The near-dated skew is call-heavy (-8.0%) with an implied move of only ±2.5% on the nearest expiry, and the term structure is in normal contango. The one caution: the put/call open-interest ratio jumped sharply from 0.95 to 3.25 as the price fell since mid-June, indicating institutions have built downside protection during the drawdown, consistent with the elevated 11.7% short interest. The mixed read (cheap near-term IV but heavy put OI) argues for patience on entry: the market is not pricing an imminent binary, but positioning is defensively tilted.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($95–110).** Q1 FY26 traffic reacceleration proves durable, FY26 SRS lands at the top of the guide, unit growth of 75–77 new stores executes cleanly with new units above $3.0M AUV, and restaurant-level margin holds ~25%. Adjusted EBITDA compounds 30%+, the tax rate normalizes without derailing the earnings ramp, and the market re-rates the stock back toward its prior high as consensus targets ($93 mean, $95 median) prove to be floors. Automation efficiencies from Hyphen and AI ingredient tracking begin to lift throughput and blunt labor inflation.

**Bear case ($45–50).** Q1's traffic surge fades, proving a promotional, weather, or comparison-driven blip rather than structural demand, and FY26 SRS decelerates back toward the low end of guidance or below. A tougher H2 consumer environment compounds. Tariff and input-cost inflation compress restaurant-level margin, new-market units disappoint, and continued Artal supply overhang caps rallies. At a triple-digit current-year multiple, a single comp miss triggers a 30%+ de-rating as it did in FY25, taking the stock back toward the $43–50 lows of late 2025.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q2 FY26 print confirms SRS traffic remains positive (traffic contribution above +3%), demonstrating the Q1 recovery is durable rather than a one-quarter blip.
- Current-year and next-year EPS estimate revisions resume rising (>+5% over a subsequent 90-day window) rather than the current flattening, confirming consensus is catching up to a sustained inflection.
- A further pullback toward the low-to-mid $50s (approaching the analyst low target of $55), which would restore clearly asymmetric upside on unchanged fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- FY26 SRS decelerates below +4% or traffic turns negative on the next one to two prints.
- Restaurant-level profit margin compresses below ~23% on sustained tariff/labor pressure without a mix offset.
- New-unit AUVs on 2026 vintages fall materially below the ~$3.0M trend, signaling new-market demand is not translating.
- Management cuts unit-growth or SRS guidance.

**Reassessment timeline.** The next natural checkpoint is the Q2 FY26 earnings release (following the May 20 Q1 print, expect late Q3 2026). That report is the decisive test of whether the traffic reacceleration is structural. We would also monitor the pace of remaining Artal share sales as a supply/overhang indicator.

## Conclusion

CAVA is a high-quality, self-funding growth restaurant with a clean balance sheet, best-in-class unit economics, and a traffic-led comp recovery in Q1 FY26 that reversed the FY25 deceleration scare and prompted a raised full-year guide. The ~31% drawdown from the high to $62.35 has meaningfully improved the entry point on improving fundamentals. Those are the ingredients of a genuine buy case.

We stop at HOLD (growth_hold, overall 6.2) for three reasons. First, the valuation still requires near-flawless execution: a triple-digit current-year multiple leaves asymmetric downside on any comp miss, exactly the dynamic that produced both the FY25 de-rating and the recent drawdown. Second, estimate revisions have flattened over the trailing 30 days after their early-2026 upgrade, so the +30% inflection-velocity signal that would justify overriding the valuation is absent. Third, CAVA sits in Consumer Discretionary, a sector where BUY calls have historically produced negative alpha, which raises the bar for company-specific conviction; against a plausible H2 consumer softening, we want one more quarter of traffic confirmation before upgrading. The improving score trajectory (5.2 → 6.0 → 6.2) reflects a business getting fundamentally better and a price that has come down. If the Q2 print confirms durable traffic, this becomes a BUY. Until then, hold with a bias toward accumulating on further weakness toward the mid-$50s.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the forward multiple was understated — current-year consensus EPS is $0.55, so the stock trades at ~113x current-year EPS (not ~85x), making the valuation more demanding than the report characterized; the report's "85x forward" framing recurred throughout the valuation, risk, and thesis sections and has been corrected to reflect the actual multiples.
