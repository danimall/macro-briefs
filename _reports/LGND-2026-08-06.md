## Executive Summary

Ligand is a biopharmaceutical royalty aggregator that operates with 47 employees and owns economic interests in medicines developed and sold by others. The underlying engine is compounding at a rate that justifies attention: royalty revenue went from $108.8M in FY2024 to $161.0M in FY2025 (+48%) and is guided to $225-250M in FY2026 (+40% to +55%), with Q1 2026 royalties up 56% year over year. Two assets are doing the heavy lifting — Filspari, where Ligand holds a 9% worldwide royalty and Travere reported US net product sales up 88% year over year to $105M following the April 13, 2026 FSGS approval, and Ohtuvayre, where partner sales rose 84% to $131.0M in Q1 under Merck's ownership (following its October 7, 2025 acquisition of Verona). The July 14, 2026 closing of the $739M XOMA Royalty acquisition adds Roche's Vabysmo, Servier's Ojemda and Zevra's Miplyffa royalty interests plus 120-plus additional programs, funded largely by a $700M zero-coupon convertible closed June 25.

The funding arithmetic is the cleanest part of the thesis. Ligand underwrites its financial royalty assets at disclosed fair-value discount rates of 13-35% (17% weighted average) and now funds at a 0.00% cash coupon on the 2031 notes and roughly 1.4% effective on the 2030 notes. That spread, applied to a $150-250M annual deployment target against cash G&A of roughly $58M, is the entire investment case, and it is a real one.

What keeps this a HOLD at $300.79 is price and proof. The stock has appreciated 124% over twelve months and sits 7.9% below its 52-week high of $326.63. On consensus, it trades 32.7x FY2026 adjusted EPS of $9.20 and 25.0x FY2027 of $12.05; adjusting back the roughly $2.00 per share of pretax share-based compensation excluded from adjusted figures pushes the 2026 multiple toward the mid-40s. Full-year 2026 guidance of $8.50-9.50 requires $6.87-7.87 across Q2-Q4 after Q1 delivered $1.63 and missed consensus by 12%, meaning quarterly adjusted EPS must run $2.29-2.62 for three straight quarters. Two components of that bridge — Captisol order timing and milestone/contract income — are inherently lumpy. The Q2 report scheduled for August 6, 2026 is the first real test and was not available at this information cutoff; the options market is pricing a ±8.4% move on the nearest expiry with near-term implied volatility of 49.8% against 36.2% realized. This is the fifth consecutive HOLD on the name, and I address below what specifically has and has not changed.

## Company Overview

Ligand deploys capital in four ways, per the FY2025 10-K: purchasing existing royalty streams (Qarziba from Apeiron, Tzield, Ohtuvayre from the inventors); acquiring companies with embedded royalty rights and shedding the operating infrastructure (Pharmacopeia → Filspari, Metabasis → Viking programs, Vernalis → Ohtuvayre, Pfenex → Capvaxive/Vaxneuvance/Rylaze, Novan → Zelsuvmi, Apeiron → Qarziba); project finance and synthetic royalties on development-stage assets (Castle Creek, Orchestra BioMed, Agenus, Palvella); and licensing two owned IP platforms.

Captisol, a modified cyclodextrin enabling 17 FDA-approved products, is the more material platform, backed by 400-plus issued patents with the low-chloride US patent running to 2033. It is manufactured by a single supplier, Hovione, in Portugal and Ireland.

FY2025 royalty receipts of $176.9M broke down as Kyprolis $35.5M, Qarziba $33.7M, Filspari $32.0M, Ohtuvayre $14.8M, Rylaze $13.3M, Capvaxive $10.1M, teriparatide $8.1M, Vaxneuvance $7.4M, Evomela $5.9M, Nexterone $3.1M, Pneumosil $3.0M, and 16 other products $10.0M. Following XOMA, the company states the portfolio exceeds 200 commercial, clinical and preclinical royalty assets.

A three-member board Investment Committee must unanimously approve any binding term sheet — a disclosed control on deal quality that matters in a business where capital deployment discipline is the primary determinant of returns.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and the adjustments that matter.** Reported FY2025 total revenues and income were $268.1M against $167.1M in FY2024, a 60% increase. That headline overstates the underlying trend: the 10-K discloses $53.1M of income recognized in Q3 2025 on the divestiture of LNHC (the Pelthos transaction), booked inside "contract revenue and income." Excluding it, recurring FY2025 revenue was approximately $215.0M, or +28.6% year over year. The royalty line is the correct trend indicator, and it accelerated.

**GAAP earnings quality is poor and should not anchor valuation.** FY2025 pretax income of $159.0M comprised $41.0M of operating income and $118.0M of non-operating income, of which $90.7M was a non-cash fair-value gain on Ligand's retained Pelthos common and Series A preferred stock. Combined with the $53.1M divestiture income, Pelthos-related items accounted for roughly $143.8M, or 90%, of pretax income. In Q1 2026, $49.2M of that gain reversed as Pelthos fell from $31.00 to $21.01, carrying the position from $152.8M to $103.5M and producing a $13.3M GAAP net loss despite $17.4M of operating income. Trailing GAAP diluted EPS of $6.13 is therefore not a measure of earning power in either direction. The accounting itself is defensible and symmetric — the fair-value election reflects an observable market price, and adjusted EPS removed the gain just as it added back the loss.

Note a data inconsistency worth flagging: the market-data feed reports both trailing and forward P/E as 40.1x, which cannot be reconciled with $300.79 against $6.13 of trailing GAAP EPS (49.1x). I use consensus-based forward multiples throughout.

**Operating income understates cash economics.** FY2025 R&D expense of $81.2M included $44.3M (Castle Creek D-Fi) and $17.8M (Orchestra BioMed) of royalty acquisitions expensed immediately under ASC 730-20. Adding back $62.1M puts pre-deployment operating income near $103.1M. This single accounting feature is why screening ratios — asset turnover of 0.17x, EBIT/assets of 2.6%, an Altman Z-Score of 2.91 — misread the business. The Piotroski failure on operating cash flow below net income, by contrast, is a correct signal: it is precisely the non-cash Pelthos gain.

**Cash generation: do not annualize Q1.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $49.4M, depressed by the $62.1M of royalty purchases running through operating activities; adjusted for those, roughly $111.5M, or 41.6% of revenue. Separately, the principal-return portion of receipts on ASC 310 financial royalty assets is classified in investing activities ($6.98M in Q1 2026), so headline OCF understates cash royalty inflow. Q1 2026 OCF was $48.7M, but it benefited from a $6.4M receivable release, a small inventory build, essentially no royalty deployments and cash taxes of $0.8M. Building from FY2025 disclosures — royalty receipts $176.9M, Captisol gross profit roughly $25.7M, cash G&A near $58M (G&A of $92.4M less approximately $34M of share-based compensation), modest cash interest and taxes — normalized pre-deployment cash generation is in the $130-145M range and rising with royalties. Treating Q1 as a ~$195M annual run rate would be an error.

**Capital structure was transformed after the last available balance sheet.** Total convertible principal now stands at approximately $1.16B: $460M of 0.75% 2030 notes (conversion price $194.79, principal cash-settled) and $700M of 0.00% 2031 notes closed June 25, 2026 with net proceeds of $678.2M. The 2031 raise was proposed at $550M, priced at $625M and closed at $700M including the full greenshoe — evidence of demand. Across both offerings, $118.8M of net cash went to purchasing call spreads ($45.9M and $72.9M) and $75.0M to anti-dilution buybacks, so deployable cash from the 2031 offering was $545.3M rather than $700M. The 2030 warrants at a $294.02 strike are already in the money at $300.79; the 2031 warrants at $524.34 are far out of the money.

Pro forma liquidity, derived: $779.4M of cash and short-term investments at 3/31/26, plus $678.2M net proceeds, less $72.9M net hedge cost, less $60.0M buyback, less $739.0M XOMA cash consideration, leaves roughly $586M, before Q2 operating cash flow and transaction fees. Net debt is therefore approximately $574M versus a net cash position at year-end 2025, implying enterprise value near $6.53B. The arithmetic suggests the revolver was not drawn at closing, though the July 14 8-K carries an Item 2.03 that could not be read, so this remains an inference. A qualifier on asset quality: of the $779.4M, only $115.1M was cash; $76.3M was corporate equity securities (cost basis $18.8M) in partners such as Viking, Palvella and Orchestra — liquid but volatile, and taxable on monetization.

The converts carry no maintenance covenants and the nearest maturity is October 2030. The $125M revolver requires minimum trailing consolidated EBITDA of $55M and a leverage ceiling of 2.50x (3.00x for four quarters after a material acquisition); the EBITDA test is comfortably met, though whether the converts count in the leverage ratio is not established.

Financial health scores 6.5: liquidity is ample, maturities are distant and covenant-light, and the funding cost is exceptional, but the balance sheet moved from net cash to $574M net debt in a single quarter, a meaningful slice of the asset base is mark-to-market equity, and reported operating cash flow requires several adjustments before it means anything.

## Growth Analysis

The royalty line is the thesis. Its trajectory — $108.8M, $161.0M, guided $225-250M — is a two-year compound rate above 40%, and the composition of 2026 growth is identifiable rather than speculative:

- **Filspari** is now the largest contributor. Q1 2026 royalty of $11.3M on estimated global partner sales of $125.6M at a 9% rate. Travere reported 993 record new US IgAN patient start forms and US sales +88% year over year; the April 13 FSGS approval opens a US addressable population above 30,000 with first patients treated within a week.
- **Ohtuvayre** partner sales of $131.0M in Q1, +84%, now marketed by Merck (following its October 7, 2025 acquisition of Verona), with Phase 2 work in non-CF bronchiectasis and a LAMA fixed-dose combination. Ligand's total royalty is 3%.
- **XOMA** contributes approximately $25M of partial-year royalties and a company-stated +$0.50 to 2026 and +$1.50 to 2027 adjusted EPS. The acquisition was struck at roughly a 14% premium to XOMA's 30-day VWAP, a modest control premium.
- **Pipeline optionality**: Palvella's Phase 3 SELVA hit its primary and all secondary efficacy endpoints, with an NDA planned in 2H 2026 and Ligand holding an 8.0-9.8% tiered royalty; Agenus began a ~830-patient Phase 3 (2.625% royalty, $40.4M net carrying value); Orchestra received a second Breakthrough Device Designation; Tzield's label expanded to age 1.

Offsetting these are real erosions. Kyprolis partner sales fell 6.0% to $1,529M in FY2025 while remaining the largest single royalty receipt at $35.5M, with Breckenridge's US generic launch date confidential "in 2027 or sooner." Q1's 43% Kyprolis royalty growth on 2.6% sales growth was a tiering effect (effective rate 1.9% versus 1.3%) inside a 1.5-3.0% structure, not underlying strength. Evomela generic entry became permissible June 1, 2026 after royalties already declined from $8.7M to $5.9M. Captisol guidance of $35-40M implies a decline from FY2025's $40.2M at the midpoint, against patents expiring in stages (morphology 2026, high purity 2029, low chloride 2033).

Consensus reflects the balance: current-year estimates are flat over 90 days (+1.0% over 30 days) while next-year estimates rose +4.5% over 90 days and +6.5% over 30 days to $12.05, implying 31% EPS growth in 2027. That revision pattern is positive and accelerating on the out-year, but it is well short of the magnitude that would constitute a standalone inflection signal, and the near-term quarter has been revised down 1.1%.

Growth prospects score 7.0. The rate is high, the drivers are named and partner-confirmed, and 2027 accretion from XOMA is largely contractual. What prevents a higher score is that the 2026 delivery path is back-half concentrated to an uncomfortable degree, and two of the largest legacy contributors are in decline.

## Competitive Landscape

Royalty aggregation is a cost-of-capital business. Royalty Pharma dominates cheques above $1B; DRI Healthcare, HealthCare Royalty Partners and the life-sciences arms of Blackstone, Sagard and OMERS compete across the middle.

Ligand's differentiation is structural rather than scale-based. Its disclosed deals cluster from $7M to $50M — Arecor $7M, Tolerance $20M, Orchestra $25M plus $15M, Castle Creek $50M — and it concentrates on synthetic royalties on development-stage assets where competition is thinner and required returns higher. Disclosed fair-value discount rates of 13-35% (17% weighted average) confirm high-yield underwriting relative to the mid-single to low-double-digit rates typical of de-risked commercial royalties. The acquire-a-company-and-shed-the-infrastructure playbook, executed repeatedly from Pharmacopeia through Apeiron and the Pelthos carve-out, is a sourcing channel pure royalty buyers cannot replicate.

The 2026 moves strengthened the position in two respects. Acquiring XOMA removed the closest small/mid-cap competitor in the same niche, at a 14% premium to a 30-day VWAP. And the zero-coupon convertible is an exceptionally cheap funding source for an entity underwriting at 13-35%.

Three factors argue against a higher score and support the prior report's reduction from 7.5. First, the Viking Therapeutics license termination — Ligand's April 24 notice asserting Viking failed to use commercially reasonable efforts on the TR-Beta Program, which Viking disputes — puts up to $375M of milestones and 3.5-7.5% royalties in play, but a business whose franchise rests on being biotech's preferred capital partner incurs an unquantifiable cost when it terminates a partner's license and simultaneously holds that partner's equity. The outcome and the reputational consequence are both unresolved. Second, asset-count diversification is not cash-flow diversification: top-three royalty receipts were 57.2% of the FY2025 total and 56.5% in Q1 2026, and because Filspari carries the highest major rate and is growing fastest, concentration could worsen even as the portfolio broadens past 200 assets. The composition of that concentration is improving in quality — declining, generic-exposed Kyprolis giving way to Filspari and Merck-backed Ohtuvayre — which is a different and lesser good than reduced concentration. Third, the operating-leverage claim is load-bearing: 47 employees managing roughly 100 programs must absorb 120-plus more at "substantially offset" incremental opex. Q1 G&A was already up 11% year over year on "increased headcount... in its origination and portfolio management functions." If headcount must scale with asset count, the multiple is not defensible.

Competitive position scores 6.6.

## Risk Assessment

**The back-half guidance gap is the near-term binary.** Q1 adjusted EPS of $1.63 against full-year guidance of $8.50-9.50 leaves $6.87-7.87 for Q2-Q4, or $2.29-2.62 per quarter, up 40-61% from Q1. On revenue, Q1's $51.7M against $270-310M implies $72.8-86.1M per quarter. Management's bridge is credible in composition — XOMA royalties, the Filspari FSGS launch, the Ohtuvayre ramp, Captisol order-timing recovery of $26-31M across Q2-Q4 versus $8.7M in Q1, and contract revenue of $10-20M versus $0.1M in Q1 — but Captisol timing and milestone income are lumpy, and Q1 already missed consensus by 12%.

**Investment Company Act status is a genuine structural binary.** Ligand must satisfy the 40% test with subsidiaries relying on Section 3(c)(5)(A), and states plainly that an adverse SEC staff conclusion on whether royalty interests are qualifying assets would materially and adversely affect the business. The asset mix has tilted further toward financial assets: at 3/31/26, short-term investments, financial royalty assets, the Pelthos position and other investments totaled roughly $1,061M of $1,532M total assets, and XOMA adds a large purchased-royalty book. The company did not modify this risk factor, so the shifting mix is an observation rather than a disclosure; an alternative reading is that the 55% test applies at royalty-holding subsidiary level where the mix is more favorable.

**Concentration and erosion.** Top-three at 57% of receipts; Kyprolis in volume decline with a confidential 2027-or-sooner generic date; Evomela generics live; Captisol guided down with a sole supplier manufacturing in Portugal and Ireland against a newly disclosed tariff risk factor.

**Earnings-quality volatility.** The Pelthos position ($103.5M at 3/31/26) and $76.3M of partner equities mark to market through income each quarter, producing GAAP results that will keep diverging sharply from operating performance in both directions.

**Other disclosed items.** California Financing Law reliance on a five-or-fewer commercial loan exemption narrows as deal cadence rises toward $150-250M annually. Product-liability insurance carries a $15.0M annual limit. Litigation includes the Nexterone ANDA suit (trial proposed June-September 2027) and the CyDex/Bexson Captisol dispute now in discovery. Limited analyst coverage is itself a stated risk factor.

**Short interest.** 11.5% of float, 3.3 days to cover. With $1.16B of converts outstanding, a meaningful portion is likely delta-hedging by convertible arbitrageurs; the 10-K explicitly notes noteholders may hedge. The directional content of this figure is low.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 49.8% against 36.2% one-year realized is rich (13.6 points), sits in the 81st percentile of the current chain, and the term structure is in backwardation (49.8% at 14 days versus 40.9% at 105 days). Implied volatility rose 4.7 points since July 19 on a 0.3% price change, and put/call open interest moved from 0.02 to 0.86. The nearest expiry prices a ±8.4% move. All of this points to a specific, dated event — the August 6 Q2 report and the first test of the back-half ramp — rather than to a directional view; the reported put/call skew of -1.8% is characterized as balanced, and the aggregate put/call volume ratio of 0.23 is call-heavy while the nearest-tenor row shows a put-heavy 3.67, a conflict I do not resolve into a directional claim. Practically: options are expensive here, which argues against buying premium and mildly in favor of waiting for the post-print volatility crush before adding equity exposure.

Risk factors score 5.0. The volume of identifiable, quantified, non-priced idiosyncratic risk at a 32.7x current-year multiple is high, and the guidance gap resolves within a day of this cutoff.

## Management Quality

Capital allocation deserves credit. XOMA was acquired at a 14% premium to a 30-day VWAP; the convertible market was tapped at 0.00% and 0.75% cash coupons with upsizing on demand; the acquire-and-shed playbook has been executed across at least six transactions over two decades; five-year indexed TSR reached 279.02 against a peer group at 124.75.

Forward-looking alignment signals are weaker. Across roughly 25 recent Form 4 filings from March through August 2026, no open-market purchases were observed. The Chief Legal Officer exercised 5,000 options at a $52.27 strike in each of five consecutive months and sold essentially all shares at $199-293; directors reduced holdings into strength (Haas from 11,442 to 4,981; LaMattina from 31,560 to 26,969; Aryeh 5,000 shares at $250). Much of this is pre-planned — the CFO's 10b5-1 plan and the CLO's rigid monthly cadence — and follows a near-tripling in the share price, which is routine diversification rather than a fundamental signal. The CFO retained shares from a May exercise, and CEO Todd Davis shows no sales while holding 199,513 shares worth approximately $60M, which is substantial alignment. Still, the absence of any open-market buying is a non-confirmation of insider-perceived undervaluation.

Two further items temper the score. The April 2023 $50M discretionary repurchase authorization expired in April 2026 with zero shares bought; the only buybacks executed ($15.0M at $147.01, $60.0M at $262.17) were mechanical anti-dilution components of convertible offerings. Meanwhile a $100M ATM was established February 27, 2026, with the company stating it planned to begin sales after the Q1 10-Q filing. Management treats its equity as a source of capital rather than a use, which is a defensible allocation choice when underwriting at 13-35% expected returns, but it is not the behavior of a team that believes the stock is cheap. Separately, "Revenue" as the company-selected compensation measure is awkward for an entity whose revenue line absorbed $53.1M of divestiture income in FY2025 — 19.8% of reported total revenues and income — and whether the committee normalizes for such items is not disclosed.

Management quality scores 6.0: demonstrably skilled deal execution, weak-to-neutral insider alignment signals, and an equity-issuance posture at $300.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($385, +28%).** The Q2 and Q3 prints validate the back-half ramp; FY2026 lands at or above the $9.50 guidance high; XOMA delivers the stated +$1.50 to 2027 adjusted EPS with cost synergies absorbing the new assets at minimal added opex, confirming the operating-leverage claim. Filspari compounds through the FSGS launch toward a multi-hundred-million-dollar annual royalty stream at 9%; Ohtuvayre continues at Merck-driven growth rates; Palvella files its NDA in 2H 2026. The December 8, 2026 Investor Day frames a five-year outlook that the market capitalizes at a spread-business multiple rather than a royalty-trust multiple. At 32x 2027 EPS of $12.05 the stock is $385, roughly the analyst high of $388. The mechanism is straightforward: 0-1.4% cash funding cost against 13-35% underwriting yields, applied to $545M of undeployed capacity plus $130-145M of annual pre-deployment cash generation.

**Bear case ($215, -28.5%).** Q2 or Q3 misses the implied $2.29-2.62 quarterly run rate; Captisol order timing does not recover, and the guided $35-40M declines further against a 2026 morphology patent expiry; contract revenue lands near the $10M low end. Kyprolis erosion accelerates ahead of a 2027 generic while it remains a top-three receipt. Origination headcount scales with the 200-asset portfolio, and G&A growth above 11% breaks the incremental-margin argument. The Viking dispute resolves against Ligand and raises counterparty friction on new deals. Net debt of $574M and $1.16B of converts, in place of a year-end net cash position, remove the balance-sheet cushion. A cut 2026 estimate near $8.50 at a 25x multiple, or 18x a trimmed 2027 near $11.00-11.50, produces roughly $215.

**Base case ($325, +8.0%).** Guidance is met near the midpoint at approximately $9.00-9.20 for 2026; 2027 delivers close to consensus $12.05 with XOMA accretion largely intact; concentration stays near 55%; Captisol declines modestly. At 27x 2027 EPS the stock is $325, consistent with the $327.4 consensus mean and $333.5 median.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth_hold).** The 12-month thesis is positive with dated catalysts, but the entry price offers roughly 8% to base case against 28% to bear case — insufficient asymmetry to add new capital at $300.79.

**Upgrade triggers (would move to BUY):**
1. Q2 2026 revenue of $73M or better with adjusted EPS of $2.29 or better, and FY2026 royalty guidance of $225-250M reaffirmed or raised. This validates the single largest open question in the thesis.
2. A price of $250-260 on unchanged fundamentals, which would put the stock near 21x 2027E consensus and restore a 25%-plus gap to base case.
3. Evidence the operating-leverage claim holds post-XOMA: two consecutive quarters with G&A growth below royalty revenue growth.
4. Cluster open-market insider buying (3-plus insiders within 30 days), which has been entirely absent.

**Downgrade triggers (would move to AVOID):**
1. Any cut to FY2026 revenue or adjusted EPS guidance, or a second consecutive quarterly EPS miss.
2. Captisol revenue below $8M for two consecutive quarters, confirming structural rather than timing-driven decline.
3. ATM issuance exceeding $50M without a named, disclosed deployment, or a shift toward funding deals with equity at compressed prices.
4. Any adverse Investment Company Act development, or a Kyprolis royalty decline exceeding 10% year over year as the 2027 generic date approaches.

**Reassessment timeline:** Immediate — the August 6, 2026 Q2 report resolves the guidance-gap question in one print. Then the Q3 report in early November for confirmation, and the December 8, 2026 Investor Day for the updated five-year framework. Given near-term implied volatility at 49.8% in backwardation, existing holders should carry the position through the print rather than transact into rich premium; prospective buyers should wait for the print and the subsequent volatility normalization.

## Conclusion

Ligand's royalty engine is real, accelerating, and funded at a cost of capital that few competitors can match. Royalties compounding from $108.8M to a guided $225-250M in two years, Filspari at a 9% rate with a newly expanded label, Ohtuvayre growing 84% under Merck, and $545M of deployable capacity raised at a zero cash coupon against 13-35% underwriting yields — that combination supports a premium multiple and explains the 124% twelve-month return.

The reasons this stays a HOLD for a fifth consecutive assessment are specific rather than atmospheric. At $300.79 the stock trades 32.7x a current-year consensus that requires 81-83% of the year's earnings in the three quarters after a 12% miss, and 25x a 2027 number that depends on XOMA accretion and an operating-leverage assumption already showing 11% G&A growth. Adjusted EPS excludes roughly $2.00 per share of largely non-deductible share-based compensation. Cash-flow concentration has not improved despite the asset count doubling.
