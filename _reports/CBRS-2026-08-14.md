**Research Date: August 14, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Cerebras reported a quarter that was both a beat and a miss, and the distinction matters more here than at almost any other company in the market. GAAP revenue of $180.1M fell 6.9% sequentially and short of headline estimates; core (non-GAAP) revenue of $209.9M rose 9.7% sequentially and 103% year-over-year, beating the non-GAAP consensus of roughly $194M. The entire gap is two disclosed items: $44.3M of customer-warrant amortization booked as contra-revenue, and $14.5M of essentially zero-margin pass-through data-center billings. The warrant drag runs through October 2031 and, if remaining OpenAI and AWS tranches vest, carries roughly $2.37B of additional future contra-revenue at fixed grant-date values. GAAP revenue is therefore a structurally impaired series at this company for years; core revenue is the decision-useful operating line, while the warrants represent real economic consideration transferred to customers.

The operating trajectory is strong. Core gross margin improved to 40.6% from 31.2%, core operating loss narrowed to $33.6M from $43.9M on more than double the revenue, FY2026 core revenue guidance was raised to $880-890M (+~74% over FY2025's $510.0M), and the CFO stated a plan to "more than triple revenue in 2027." Remaining performance obligations stand at $25.4B and explicitly exclude AWS and other hyperscalers, with first hyperscaler revenue expected mid-2027. Management's supply-chain argument — no HBM, no CoWoS, no 3nm — is a concrete differentiator in a year when those three inputs are the binding constraints across the accelerator complex.

The problem is price and structure, not execution. Using the 237,564,041 all-class shares on the 10-Q cover (the vendor-supplied $21.1B market cap implies ~92M shares and reconciles to no disclosed count), equity value at $231.01 is $54.88B and enterprise value approximately $47.2B. That is 53x FY2026 core revenue and ~17.8x a 2027 revenue figure that exists only as verbal management commentary with no range attached. Against that multiple sit concentration of ~75% of Q2 revenue in three counterparties, an $918.2M OpenAI secured loan that can be accelerated if Cerebras misses a data-center delivery milestone, disclosure controls concluded not effective across the four most judgment-intensive accounting areas in these financials, exclusivity provisions in both the OpenAI and AWS agreements that constrain diversification, and CEO/CTO Rule 10b5-1 plans covering ~3.37M shares adopted within a week of the IPO. Probability-weighted return from $231.01 is modestly positive with very wide dispersion. HOLD; no new capital here.

## Company Overview

Cerebras designs and manufactures AI compute systems built around the Wafer-Scale Engine, described in its filings as the only commercialized wafer-scale processor, a single chip spanning an entire silicon wafer. The company was incorporated in Delaware in April 2016, is headquartered in Sunnyvale, began generating revenue in 2019, and IPO'd on May 14-15, 2026 at $185.00 per share. It operates as one reportable segment.

The commercial thesis is latency. Cerebras sells against GPUs on inference speed rather than aggregate throughput, and management argues that once customers build products around fast inference, engineering teams reallocate away from latency optimization, making reversion to slower inference costly. That is an assertion in a filing, not a measured retention statistic.

Revenue arrives through three channels: on-premises hardware (point-in-time recognition), Cerebras Cloud (dedicated take-or-pay capacity plus on-demand tokens), and services. Distribution extends through AWS Marketplace, Microsoft Marketplace, IBM watsonx Model Gateway, Vercel, OpenRouter and Hugging Face. The mix has inverted rapidly: Q2 2026 was 70% cloud/services and 30% hardware, versus 32%/68% a year earlier, and management expects cloud to keep gaining share.

Geographic exposure is unusual for a US semiconductor company. Q2 revenue was $84.0M United States and $95.7M EMEA (53% of total), reflecting MBZUAI and G42 in the UAE. Property and equipment, net includes $169.0M in Canada against $817.8M domestically.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** Q2 GAAP revenue was $180.1M (+74% YoY) with hardware down 23% to $54.1M and cloud/services up 281% to $126.0M. GAAP gross margin fell to 14.2% from 31.1% because warrant amortization and IPO-related stock compensation land partly in cost of revenue. On the core basis — $180.1M less $14.5M pass-through plus $44.3M warrant amortization — revenue was $209.9M, up 103%, with core gross margin of 40.6% versus 31.2%. Management states that excluding these items, hardware gross margin improved five points on lower material costs and manufacturing efficiency, and cloud margin improved sixteen points on pricing and utilization. Sequentially, core revenue rose from $191.3M to $209.9M while GAAP revenue fell from $193.4M to $180.1M; investors will have to live with that crossover for five more years.

**The GAAP loss.** Net loss of $450.5M ($(2.98) diluted) is dominated by $377.0M of stock-based compensation, of which $273.6M relates to RSUs whose liquidity condition was satisfied at IPO, plus $34.2M of PRSU expense and accelerated amortization of $101.6M of executive grants, with a further $22.8M of employer payroll tax. Core net loss was $6.9M and adjusted EBITDA $(53.1)M. The GAAP figure is not evidence of operating deterioration; it is also not free, since SBC is non-cash but dilutive, and the recurring run-rate is not yet observable from a single post-IPO quarter.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: cash $6,742.2M, restricted cash $684.7M, short-duration Treasury investments $1,179.4M, for $8,606.3M of total liquidity plus an $850M undrawn revolver, against $918.2M of debt (the OpenAI working capital loan). Current ratio 5.82x; debt/equity 0.10x; net cash roughly $7.69B, about $32.4 per share on 237.6M shares. Two scope caveats: $684.7M of the cash is contractually restricted to infrastructure build-out under the OpenAI Master Relationship Agreement and is not general corporate liquidity, and stockholders' equity of $9.15B includes $1,128.4M of customer warrant assets that will be consumed as contra-revenue rather than converted to cash.

**Cash flow.** H1 operating cash flow was $(47.5)M against capex of $(548.9)M, for free cash flow of $(596.4)M, understated by $58.6M of capex still in payables. Operating cash flow was flattered by a $126.4M deferred-revenue build (including $38.6M of non-cash interest on the OpenAI financing component credited to deferred revenue) and hurt by a $111.8M customer-deposit drawdown and a $182.3M prepaid/other build. These are contract-timing items tied to one anchor customer's deployment calendar; annualizing either the operating or free cash flow line would be false precision. On liquidity alone, near-term solvency risk is very low: even at a $1.2-1.5B annual free-cash-flow deficit, unrestricted cash and investments of roughly $7.9B provide multi-year runway.

**Off-balance-sheet commitments.** Recognized operating lease liabilities are $568.8M (PV) on $690.3M of fixed payments, 5.5-year weighted term at 7.2%. Not yet on the balance sheet: approximately $1.5B of Q2-executed leases with 2026 commencement plus $753.4M executed in Q3, which MD&A aggregates to roughly $2.3B. Set against that, non-cancelable purchase commitments beyond one year total only $10.3M across 2026-2028, because manufacturing is procured on purchase orders without volume commitments. The asymmetry is the clearest structural fragility in the model: long-dated data-center leases with liquidated-damages provisions funded by inference contracts that are largely consumption-based or short-duration dedicated capacity. The OpenAI take-or-pay commitment partially closes the gap on the anchor book; it does not close it on the rest.

**Estimate revisions and surprises.** Consensus has moved modestly in the right direction — current-year core EPS from $(0.97) to $(0.94) over 30 days, next-year from $0.96 to $0.97 — and both post-IPO quarters beat core consensus (Q1 $(0.04) vs $(0.16); Q2 $(0.04) vs $(0.17)). The revision magnitudes are nowhere near the +30% threshold that signals a mispriced inflection, and 90-day history does not exist for a company public since May. Two beats build some confidence in the core forecast; they do not resolve the 2027 question.

## Growth Analysis

Reported growth is exceptional on the core basis: FY2024 $290.3M, FY2025 $510.0M (+75.7%), FY2026 guided $880-890M (+~74%), and a stated 2027 plan to more than triple. Three demand anchors support it. First, the OpenAI MRA commits 750MW of inference capacity deploying 2026-2028 with an option for an additional 1.25GW by end-2030, and Cerebras is the launch infrastructure partner for GPT-5.6 Sol on OpenAI's new "Ultrafast" tier at 750 tokens/second. Second, the AWS commercial agreement contemplates disaggregated inference on Amazon Bedrock in Q1 2027 with systems in Amazon data centers; no AWS revenue has been recognized yet because lease commencement conditions were unsatisfied at June 30. Third, the AMD collaboration pairs Helios rack-scale systems for prefill with Cerebras WSE for decode, claiming up to 5x throughput at Cerebras latency, targeted for production in Q4 2026.

Capacity is being built ahead of demand: more than 600MW live or contracted for delivery by end-2027, manufacturing capacity to rise more than 10x in 2026 across Flex, Sanmina and Rocket EMS, and management stating contracted facilities already provide 3-4x more capacity for 2027 growth. Named new logos — Cognition, Lovable, Block, Figma, AlphaSense, GSK, CrowdStrike — suggest a genuine tail forming below the 10% disclosure threshold.

Two features of the guidance deserve scrutiny. Q3 core revenue of $214-216M is only +2.4% sequential at the midpoint, decelerating from +9.7%. Backing into Q4 from the FY midpoint ($885M less H1's $401.2M less Q3's $215M) implies roughly $269M, or +25% sequential; the entire raise is concentrated in one quarter. Applying the same arithmetic to margins implies Q4 core gross margin near 42% (consistent with management calling Q3 the trough as rented-system costs give way to owned systems) but Q4 core operating margin of roughly −26%, no better than Q3's guided −24%, which implies core operating expense rising from about $119M in Q2 to about $135M in Q3 to roughly $184M in Q4. This is midpoint arithmetic across three independently set ranges and should be read as a sensitivity, not a forecast; the reasonable interpretation is that management is front-loading opex and capacity into the 2027 ramp rather than harvesting 2026 leverage.

The binding constraint has shifted from silicon to real estate. Management identified data-center space and power, not wafers or packaging, as the bottleneck. That is a favorable position relative to HBM- and CoWoS-constrained competitors, and it also means the growth ceiling is set by construction and leasing timelines Cerebras does not control, the same timelines that trigger OpenAI's termination rights.

## Valuation Assessment

Start with the share count, because it determines everything. The 10-Q cover states 112,247,109 Class A, 111,601,424 Class B and 13,715,508 Class N shares outstanding as of August 5, 2026, for 237,564,041 total. The supplied market capitalization of $21.1B implies about 92M shares and matches no disclosed count; it appears to be a Class A-only or stale artifact. At the authoritative $231.01 close:

- Equity value: 237.564M × $231.01 = **$54.88B**
- Enterprise value: $54.88B − $8.61B cash and investments + $0.92B debt = **$47.19B** (roughly $47.9B if only unrestricted cash is netted)
- EV / FY2026 core revenue ($885M mid): **53.3x**
- EV / FY2027 core revenue at the "more than triple" floor ($2.655B): **17.8x**
- EV / RPO ($25.4B): **1.86x**
- Price / next-year consensus core EPS ($0.97): **238x**

There is no cycle-adjusted trough argument available here. This is a pre-profit company whose forward multiple on a management aspiration is still high, not a company at depressed earnings whose trailing multiple misleads. Even granting the tripling, 17.8x 2027 revenue at 41-43% gross margin and negative operating margin requires either continued 100%+ growth into 2028 or a rapid margin transition that has not been demonstrated. A useful cross-check: to justify $231 on a 25x forward-earnings basis at some point in 2028-2029, Cerebras would need roughly $2.2B of net income, which on 45% gross margin implies revenue near $8-10B with substantial operating leverage, achievable only if the 1.25GW OpenAI option and the hyperscaler channels both land.

Peer context within the AI-infrastructure cohort argues for restraint rather than thematic tolerance. Peer benchmark data was not available in the supplied dataset, so specific comparable multiples are [unverified]. Directionally, Cerebras carries a strong liquidity profile and a high multiple on realized revenue relative to established accelerator names. Sector momentum is real, but a correct sector theme has never protected a premium multiple from compression, and at 53x current-year core revenue Cerebras is squarely in premium territory.

Analyst targets bracket the stock at low $209, mean $292, median $300, high $340, with 10 of 11 ratings at buy or better and no revisions in three months. At >50x forward revenue those targets should not be treated as floors, as they embed the same growth extrapolation as the price. The mean implies +26%; the low implies −10%.

Scenario values (all on ~240-250M diluted shares, netting projected net cash):

- **Bear $130 (−44%)**: 2027 revenue lands near $2.0B on a delivery slip or a concentration event, multiple compresses to 8x EV/sales. The 52-week low of $160.81 was set on June 26 without any of these developments occurring.
- **Base $250 (+8%)**: 2027 core revenue ~$2.7B, 20x EV/sales, no hyperscaler revenue yet recognized.
- **Bull $375 (+62%)**: 2027 revenue $3.2B+, AWS Bedrock live, OpenAI exercises additional capacity, 28x EV/sales.

Weighting base 45%, bull 25%, bear 30% gives roughly +6% expected return over twelve months against realized volatility of 130%. That is not compensation for the variance, and it is the arithmetic behind the HOLD.

## Competitive Landscape

The differentiation claim is architectural and verifiable in kind. By avoiding HBM, CoWoS packaging and 3nm fabrication, Cerebras sidesteps the three constraints currently rationing accelerator supply industry-wide. Its substituted constraints are TSMC wafer allocation (management says secured), contract-manufacturing line capacity (scaling >10x in 2026), and data-center shell and power. Trading a semiconductor-substrate bottleneck for a real-estate bottleneck is a genuine advantage in 2026; it is not permanent, since HBM and packaging capacity are being added across the industry through 2027-2028.

The AMD collaboration clarifies how the inference market is segmenting and cuts both ways. Splitting inference so that Helios handles compute-bound prefill and Cerebras handles bandwidth-bound decode expands the reachable workload set and validates the latency claim with a major vendor. It also positions Cerebras as a specialized co-processor for half the pipeline rather than a general-purpose GPU substitute, which caps the share it can take from NVIDIA's installed base and leaves CUDA's software moat untouched.

Distribution strategy is capital-light and sensible: embed in AWS Bedrock, Microsoft Marketplace, IBM watsonx, Vercel, OpenRouter and Hugging Face rather than build a hyperscaler-scale cloud alone. The offsetting filing fact is that the AWS agreement contains exclusivity and minimum manufacturing capacity guarantees in AWS's favor, and the OpenAI MRA contains exclusivity provisions restricting sales to certain named competitors of OpenAI. Cerebras states plainly that these restrictions "limit our ability to diversify our customer base and revenue streams." Channel breadth here comes with channel-specific lock-in, a constraint on the 2027 diversification narrative that is not widely discussed.

Trajectory is improving (new logos, hyperscaler channels, an AMD design partnership, an OpenAI launch-tier win) but from a position that remains narrow, specialized and dependent on counterparties with more bargaining power. That combination supports a competitive-position score modestly below average rather than a moat premium.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration, changed in shape rather than severity.** Q2 revenue: MBZUAI 34%, Customer B 32% (almost certainly OpenAI — MRA revenue of $56.8M is 31.5% of total), Customer C 10%, G42 9%. MBZUAI and G42 are disclosed as related parties with respect to each other, so the UAE-linked cluster was 43% of Q2 and 49% of H1 revenue; adding OpenAI, three counterparties were ~75% of the quarter. Receivables are worse: two customers were 76% of accounts receivable at June 30 (one was 78% at year-end). MBZUAI's decline from 70% to 34% of revenue reflects the arrival of one enormous new customer, not diversification. Management expects first revenue from other hyperscalers in mid-2027, and the $25.4B RPO excludes AWS entirely.

**The OpenAI relationship converts execution risk into balance-sheet risk.** OpenAI is simultaneously the largest customer, a $918.2M secured lender at 6% (first-priority interest in a lockbox account, repayable in cash or in kind, amortizing only after delivery of the final tranche of the initial 250MW), a warrant holder, and the party holding unilateral rights to terminate part or all of the MRA if Cerebras misses time-based capacity milestones or breaches service levels. Termination for reasons other than OpenAI's material uncured breach can result in OpenAI controlling the lockbox and immediate repayment of the outstanding principal plus interest. The risk that matters is a missed construction milestone rather than reduced orders, which is precisely the constraint management named as binding. Mitigants: $7.9B of unrestricted liquidity makes repayment survivable, and Cerebras affirmed compliance with no event of default at June 30. These are also standard protective provisions in large anchor-customer prepay structures; the mechanics, not any inference about intent, are what should be priced.

**Warrant dilution and value transfer.** Recorded customer warrant assets of $1,128.4M amortize as contra-revenue through October 2031. Remaining unexercised OpenAI warrant shares number 23,411,518 at a fixed grant-date fair value of $82.02, plus 2,427,010 unvested AWS shares at $185.38, for approximately $2.37B of additional future contra-revenue if all vest. Because these are equity-classified and measured at grant date, there is no mark-to-market reflexivity; but at $231.01 those 23.4M shares represent about $5.4B of market value, so the accounting understates the economic transfer by roughly 2.3x at today's price. Total contingent dilution is 76.0M shares (32.0% of shares outstanding, equivalent to 24.2% ownership dilution if all were issued), a maximum rather than an expectation, since PRSUs require $75-250B market caps and the additional OpenAI capacity tranches are assessed as not probable.

**Internal controls.** Management concluded disclosure controls were **not effective** at June 30 due to two material weaknesses: insufficient resources to timely review reconciliations and apply GAAP across revenue recognition, inventory management and costing, data center assets accounting and equity administration; and inadequate IT general controls including ineffective segregation of duties. Those four named areas map exactly onto the MRA's significant-financing-component revenue model, the $19.2M H1 next-gen inventory write-down (reserves rose from $0.9M to $20.1M as inventory fell from $63.6M to $32.7M), roughly $604M of construction in progress, and $1.13B of warrant assets. No material misstatement resulted, remediation is underway with external SOX advisors, and this profile is common three months after an IPO; the overlap raises the probability that reported core metrics are later revised, and as an emerging growth company Cerebras owes no management ICFR report until the FY2027 10-K.

**Export controls and geography.** With the UAE cluster at 43% of Q2 revenue and EMEA at 53%, the export-license risk factor applies to the largest single revenue base rather than a peripheral market. Any tightening of US rules on advanced AI compute to Gulf entities is a direct revenue event.

**Other.** An October 2025 putative class action, amended to allege copyright infringement in connection with certain large language models and datasets, with no estimable loss range, an unusual exposure for a hardware vendor. Supplier concentration (three suppliers at 13%, 12% and 10% of H1 purchases, some sole-sourced, no volume commitments). Full valuation allowance on US deferred tax assets with Section 382 risk flagged. Founder control: Class B carries 20 votes and holds ~99.2% of voting power; the board is classified, no written consent, supermajority amendment provisions. As for post-IPO share supply, the cluster of Rule 10b5-1 plans adopted in May-June 2026 is consistent with sales capacity opening in H2, though the specific lock-up expiry terms are not detailed in the provided sources [unverified].

**Management alignment.** Q1 2026 grants included $101.6M of CEO/CTO RSUs and 9,000,000 PRSUs (5.7M CEO, 3.3M CTO) settling in 20-vote Class B stock, vesting on $75B/$150B/$250B market-cap thresholds measured as a 90-trading-day average Class A price times **Class A shares outstanding only**. At 112.25M Class A shares the first tranche requires roughly $668; if all classes eventually converted to Class A, it would require roughly $316. Because Class B and N convert on transfer, the hurdle mechanically declines as insiders sell, a definitional feature worth modeling, from which I draw no inference about intent, since Class A is the only publicly traded class and expense is recognized on service regardless of achievement. More directly relevant: the CEO and CTO adopted 10b5-1 plans covering approximately 3.37M shares (including charitable gifts) within a week of the IPO, with the COO, CFO, CAO and a director adding plans in May-June. There is no offsetting open-market purchase by any insider. Post-IPO diversification after a decade of illiquidity is a normal motivation and 10b5-1 is the governance-preferred mechanism; the absence of any buying alongside a governance structure that concentrates voting control and a compensation plan whose hurdle falls as insiders sell keeps this dimension below average.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 143.3% against 129.7% one-year realized looks rich, but the nearest tenor has one day to expiry and reflects pinning mechanics; the informative readings are the 13-day tenor at 99.8% (±15.9% implied move) and the 41-day at 94.8% (±26.1%). The term structure is in backwardation (143.3% near versus 93.7% at six months), consistent with event risk clustered in the next one to two months rather than a change in the long-run risk assessment. Put/call open interest has risen from 0.76 to 1.15 since late July while the stock gained 16% and ATM IV rose 23 percentage points; protection is being accumulated into strength. The supplied skew reading of −10.9% is characterized as call-heavy, so directional positioning is not confirming a bearish thesis; the market is paying for size, not direction. Short interest of 14.0% of float with 2.3 days to cover is elevated but easily covered, so squeeze dynamics are limited in either direction. The combination of high near-dated vol, rising put OI and a ±26% three-month implied move argues against initiating a position ahead of the Q3 print and any lock-up-related supply.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($375, +62%).** Core revenue triples in 2027 to $2.7B or better as the 750MW OpenAI deployment ramps, AWS Bedrock goes live in Q1 2027, the AMD disaggregated architecture enters production in Q4 2026, and the long tail of coding and agentic customers compounds. Gross margin moves toward the mid-40s as rented systems are replaced by Cerebras-owned capacity, operating leverage arrives in 2027-2028, and the no-HBM/no-CoWoS/no-3nm supply position lets Cerebras add capacity faster than GPU-based competitors constrained by packaging. OpenAI exercises part of the 1.25GW option, RPO grows past $30B, and the market applies 25-28x forward revenue to a demonstrated second and third large customer. Liquidity of $8.6B plus an undrawn revolver funds the build without equity issuance.

**Bear case ($130, −44%).** A data-center delivery milestone slips. Revenue defers, and because the OpenAI loan cross-defaults to MRA termination, a portion of the $918.2M becomes immediately repayable, survivable in cash terms but destructive to the equity narrative. Alternatively, export policy tightens on the UAE cluster (43% of Q2 revenue), or OpenAI's own funding trajectory forces it to slow inference commitments, or the material weaknesses surface a revision in revenue recognition or data-center asset capitalization. Q4's implied +25% sequential ramp misses, the $2.3B of executed but not-yet-recognized lease obligations meet a shorter-duration revenue book, and the multiple compresses toward 8x forward revenue. The H2 share-supply event lands into that. The stock traded at $160.81 on June 26 with none of these having occurred.

**Base case ($250, +8%).** Guidance is met, 2027 revenue roughly triples, hyperscaler revenue remains a mid-2027 event, concentration improves in mix but not in dependence, and the market holds roughly 20x forward revenue. Existing holders earn something close to the cost of capital with 130% volatility.

The asymmetry is the problem. The bull case requires three independent things to work; the bear case requires one construction schedule to slip.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD.** Existing holders can maintain exposure; new capital should wait. Base case $250 (+8%), bull $375 (+62%), bear $130 (−44%), on a 12-18 month horizon anchored to the Q3 print (early November 2026), the Q4/FY print and initial 2027 guidance (February 2027), and AWS Bedrock availability (Q1 2027). Probability-weighted return of roughly +6% does not clear the ACCUMULATE bar at 130% realized volatility, and the operating trajectory plus $7.9B of unrestricted liquidity do not support REDUCE.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price in the $160-180 range with FY2026 guidance intact, which would put EV/FY2027 core revenue near 12-13x and restore a margin of safety.
2. FY2027 revenue guidance issued as a formal range at or above $2.65B, converting the verbal tripling plan into guidance, accompanied by a second non-anchor customer above 10% of revenue that is not part of the UAE cluster.
3. Recognized AWS revenue plus a stated Bedrock capacity commitment, which would validate the diversification path and add a counterparty with no termination-plus-acceleration structure.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. Any disclosure of a missed capacity-delivery milestone under the MRA, an OpenAI termination notice, or a change in lockbox control, the single event that converts an operating stumble into a balance-sheet event.
2. Q4 core revenue materially below the ~$269M implied by FY guidance (say below $245M), or FY2027 growth guided below 2x, either of which breaks the multiple.
3. Escalation of the control situation — a restatement, an auditor-identified additional material weakness, or a revision to core revenue or data-center asset capitalization.
4. New export restrictions affecting UAE-linked customers, or a step-down in MBZUAI/G42 committed capacity.

**Next reassessment:** the Q3 2026 report in early November 2026, with an interim check on any 8-K disclosure regarding the MRA, the loan, or lock-up-related registration activity.

## Conclusion

Cerebras has built something real: core revenue doubling year over year, gross margin up nine points, an anchor commitment of 750MW with an option to 2.0GW, a $25.4B RPO that excludes its hyperscaler channel entirely, and a supply-chain position that sidesteps the industry's three binding constraints. Both post-IPO quarters beat core consensus, and FY2026 guidance was raised. The score improves modestly from the prior review because the Q2 print confirmed the operating ramp and the raise, and because the liquidity position is stronger than the market's framing of a pre-profit AI name would suggest.

What has not improved is the risk-adjusted entry. Correcting the share count from a vendor artifact of ~92M to the disclosed 237.6M puts enterprise value near $47B, 53x this year's core revenue and roughly 18x a 2027 figure that exists only as management commentary. Three counterparties were 75% of the quarter, two are 76% of receivables, the largest is also a secured lender with acceleration rights tied to construction timelines, disclosure controls were concluded not effective across exactly the accounts that matter most, and both marquee agreements contain exclusivity provisions that slow the diversification the multiple assumes. Insiders have filed sale plans covering millions of shares with no offsetting purchases. At $231.01, +8% to base against −44% to bear is not a position to add to. HOLD, with a specific interest in the $160-180 zone or a formal 2027 guide.
