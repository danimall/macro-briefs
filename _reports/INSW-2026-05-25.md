## Executive Summary

International Seaways is a pure-play tanker company operating 67 vessels (7.9M dwt) with ~82% spot market exposure, positioned at the epicenter of the Strait of Hormuz disruption. Q1 2026 delivered record net income of $286M—nearly matching the entire 2025 annual figure in a single quarter—driven by VLCC spot rates of $86,693/day (up 159% YoY) and Suezmax rates of $68,027/day (up 120% YoY). The company trades at approximately 7.6x trailing P/E (using TTM EPS through Q1 2026 of approximately $11.0), with consensus EPS estimates revised substantially upward in recent months.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) management has noted that even under a Hormuz resolution scenario, tanker markets may benefit from rebalancing trade flows and replenishment of inventories, though the company has cautioned that March 2026 rate levels are unlikely to be sustainable; (2) the balance sheet is among the strongest in the sector ($918M liquidity, <7% net debt-to-value, 25 unencumbered vessels), providing both downside protection and opportunistic M&A capacity; and (3) the Tankers International acquisition gives INSW commercial management control over VLCC and Suezmax pooling, generating fee income and commercial intelligence. The primary risk is a rapid Hormuz reopening that collapses spot rates faster than the market expects. Current pricing implies the market assigns significant probability to this scenario, creating asymmetric upside if resolution takes longer than 60 days to normalize flows.

## Company Overview

International Seaways operates as a leading crude oil and refined petroleum product tanker company, incorporated in the Marshall Islands with headquarters in New York. The fleet spans the full size spectrum: 10 VLCCs, 13 Suezmaxes, 4 Aframaxes, 1 LR2, 8 LR1s, and 28 MRs, with remaining LR1 newbuilds delivering through Q3 2026.

The commercial strategy emphasizes spot market participation through six major pools: Tankers International (VLCCs—now fully owned), a new Suezmax pool under TI, Panamax International (LR1s), CPTA and Norden (MRs), and Aframax International. This structure provides portfolio-level cargo optimization while maintaining leverage to rate cycles. The company also operates a Gulf of Mexico lightering business providing stable cash flow.

This is a fortress balance sheet by tanker industry standards. The unencumbered fleet provides additional borrowing capacity if needed. During the 2015-2020 downcycle, INSW's predecessor companies with weaker balance sheets were forced into distressed asset sales; INSW's current positioning ensures it would be the buyer in such scenarios.

**Capital Returns:**
The company has returned $1.0B to shareholders since 2020. The Q1 2026 dividend of $4.55/share included an additional discretionary component beyond the standard 85% payout ratio of adjusted net income. The variable formula ensures sustainability—if rates normalize to 2025 levels ($6.24 EPS), the annual dividend would moderate to ~$5.30/share (6.3% yield), still attractive. However, the Q1 payout should not be annualized as a run-rate given its discretionary component.

**Cash Flow Quality:**

However, in Q1 2026, net cash provided by operating activities was $141.1M versus net income of $286M, reflecting the impact of vessel-sale gains and working-capital timing. Fleet sales generated $216M in Q1 2026, with $88M in recognized gains, demonstrating asset management discipline.

Press reports have discussed the logistical complexity of re-routing vessels post-resolution, which could extend the period of above-normal ton-miles, though the precise timeline remains uncertain.

## Competitive Landscape

INSW's competitive position has materially strengthened over the past year:

1. **Tankers International Ownership:** INSW acquired sole ownership of Tankers International's commercial management entity, giving it direct management control over the VLCC pool and a newly expanded Suezmax pool. This generates fee income from third-party tonnage and provides commercial intelligence across the market, though the precise competitive advantage relative to other pool structures is difficult to quantify.

2. **Fleet Scale and Diversification:** At 67 vessels across crude and product segments, INSW is one of the 3-4 largest publicly listed tanker operators. Scale provides diversification across rate cycles (crude and product rates don't always move in tandem) and reduces idiosyncratic vessel risk.

3. **Balance Sheet vs. Peers:** INSW's <7% net LTV compares favorably to Frontline (40%+) and Euronav. In a downturn, INSW's unencumbered fleet enables acquisition of distressed assets while over-leveraged peers face covenant stress.

4. **Scrubber Investment:** The 9 scrubber-fitted VLCCs generate $13.6M annual fuel cost savings, a competitive advantage that compounds as fuel prices remain elevated.

5. **Orderbook Headwind:** The sector-wide orderbook has grown to 17% of existing fleet (from 7% in 2023). These deliveries begin in 2027-2028 and will pressure rates in a normalized environment. This is a sector-level risk rather than INSW-specific.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Hormuz Resolution (HIGH — Binary)**
LNG tankers were observed transiting Hormuz in late May 2026, with reports of a deal framework being "largely negotiated." If a comprehensive deal materializes within 60 days, VLCC rates could fall from $86,000/day toward $30,000-40,000/day within 3-6 months. At $35,000/day VLCC rates, INSW's annual EPS power is approximately $5-7, supporting a stock price of $50-70. Current price of $83.52 implies partial premia above this floor, meaning 20-40% downside in a rapid resolution scenario.

Mitigating factor: management notes that rebalancing trade flows and replenishing inventories would support tanker demand during a transition period, though the duration and magnitude of this effect remain uncertain.

**2. Insider Selling**
CEO Zabrocky sold 25,000 shares on May 12 at $88.08 and 2,000 shares on May 15 at $84.23. CFO Pribor sells ~1,000 shares monthly. This is consistent with programmatic selling during a period of elevated stock compensation vesting, but the absence of any insider purchases at any price over the past 6 months warrants attention.

**3. ATM Equity Program (May 11, 2026)**
INSW filed an 8-K disclosing a $200M Equity Distribution Agreement allowing at-the-market share sales with commissions of up to 3.0%. As of the filing date, no shares had been sold under the agreement. While this provides financing optionality, it represents potential dilution of approximately 4-5% of shares outstanding at current prices if fully utilized.

**4. Orderbook/Supply Growth**
The tanker orderbook at 17% of existing fleet represents meaningful future supply. Deliveries concentrated in 2027-2028 will pressure rates even without demand destruction. However, shipyard capacity constraints (competing with LNG carriers, container ships) may delay deliveries.

**5. Tax Structure Vulnerability**
INSW currently pays near-zero income tax. The Bermuda redomiciliation is a defensive measure against OECD Pillar Two (15% minimum). Failure to satisfy substance requirements would impose 15% tax, representing ~$46M+ annually at normalized earnings (2025 level), or much more at current run-rates.

**6. China Port Fee Risk**
13 vessels built in China subject to potential U.S. port fees currently suspended for one year (from November 2025). Reinstatement could cost $1-3M per affected vessel per U.S. port call.

## Conclusion

INSW represents a qualified, event-driven opportunity at $83.52. The stock trades at approximately 8.6x current-year consensus earnings (~$9.73), with a fortress balance sheet providing meaningful downside protection. The 4-quarter earnings beat streak (47.6% most recent beat) demonstrates consistent under-estimation of earnings power in this environment. The dividend policy, while variable, provides meaningful holding return.

The binary Hormuz risk is real and acknowledged. This is a probability-weighted asymmetric opportunity, not a margin-of-safety investment. Management itself has cautioned that March 2026 rates are unlikely to be sustainable, making this inherently speculative. At 50-55% probability of continued disruption, the expected value calculation modestly favors longs, though the available consensus target of approximately $75.20 suggests the market views the stock as fairly valued or slightly rich at current levels. The stock's 129% appreciation from its 52-week low reflects fundamental improvement (EPS rising from $1.02/quarter to $5.75/quarter), though $88M of Q1 gains came from vessel sales rather than purely recurring rate-driven income.

The specific concern that prevents higher conviction is the confirmed physical Hormuz transit signal and deal framework reporting that emerged in late May, combined with the $200M ATM equity program filed May 11 which suggests management may be preparing to raise capital at current levels. Position sizing should reflect this binary: appropriate at 2-3% of portfolio, with pre-committed reduction triggers (item #2 above).

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Consensus FY2026 EPS estimate (~$9.73 per available sources) and consensus price target (~$75.20) are materially lower than figures used in the original base/bull case scenarios; management's own filing explicitly states March 2026 rates are unlikely to be sustainable; Q1 earnings included $88M in non-recurring vessel-sale gains. These corrections reduce the magnitude of apparent undervaluation and strengthen the case for treating this as a speculative/event-driven position rather than a high-conviction asymmetric long.
