**Research Date: August 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Tri Pointe Homes is no longer a public company. On February 13, 2026 it signed an Agreement and Plan of Merger with Sumitomo Forestry Co., Ltd. and Teton NewCo, Inc. under which each outstanding share converted into the right to receive **$47.00 in cash, without interest**. Shareholders approved the transaction on approximately April 16–17, 2026 with roughly 78% of outstanding shares represented and 99.99% of votes cast in favor. The Q1 2026 earnings release dated April 29, 2026 confirmed that both the stockholder-approval condition and the HSR waiting-period condition had been satisfied. A **Form 25-NSE — the exchange notification removing the security from listing and registration — was filed on May 14, 2026**, and no company filings appear after that date. Trade press reported the closing the same day.

The practical consequence for a portfolio is that there is nothing to buy, hold, or sell. The provided market data is consistent with this: no price history is retrievable, market capitalization, beta, forward P/E and analyst targets are all null, and the last two options snapshots show the stock frozen at $46.90 → $46.95 between April 26 and May 15, 2026 — the pinning behavior of a deal trading a few cents inside a fixed $47.00 cash consideration. The recommendation is therefore **AVOID**, on the narrow and decisive ground that the security does not exist rather than on any judgment about business quality or price.

Two caveats worth stating plainly. First, the *exact* effective date and confirmation that consideration was actually disbursed rest on the Form 25-NSE plus trade-press reporting; the May 14 8-K text was not obtainable and no post-merger Form 15-12B surfaced. Second, whether any appraisal-rights proceedings under DGCL §262 remain open for dissenting shares is unknown. Neither caveat changes the conclusion: even in the residual scenarios, the maximum recoverable value is anchored to $47.00 plus statutory interest, with litigation timelines measured in years and no equity upside.

The residual analytical value here is the read-through. The last reported quarter showed the housing downturn deepening, not stabilizing: home sales revenue fell 29.7% to $506.5M, homebuilding gross margin compressed 510 basis points to 18.8% with **only ~$1.1M of impairments** in the quarter (essentially flat year over year), SG&A rose to 17.9% of home sales revenue from 14.0%, and diluted EPS fell to $0.08 from $0.70. Margin compression of that composition — pricing and incentives rather than write-downs — is the cleanest confirmation available that the mortgage-rate channel is now cutting into builder economics, and it supports continued caution on the housing complex generally.

## Company Overview

Tri Pointe was founded in April 2009 and grew from a Southern California fee builder into a regionally focused national builder operating in **17 markets across twelve states and the District of Columbia**. It reported three homebuilding segments — West (AZ, CA, NV, WA), Central (CO, TX, UT), and East (D.C., FL, MD, NC, SC, VA) — plus Tri Pointe Solutions, comprising Tri Pointe Connect (mortgage), Tri Pointe Assurance (title/escrow, acting as agent for First American Title), and Tri Pointe Advantage (P&C insurance agency).

At December 31, 2025 the company operated 156 active selling communities, controlled 32,219 lots (15,954 owned, 16,265 optioned), owned 449 model homes, and employed 1,579 people. Its price band was unusually wide for its size — approximately **$190,000 to $3.2 million** base prices, 1,240 to 5,300 square feet — spanning entry-level through luxury and active adult. FY2025 average sales price of homes delivered was **$680,000**, materially above the entry-level-heavy volume builders, which is the structural reason the franchise was more rate-sensitive than peers.

Three "startup divisions" were being funded at the time of sale: Utah (launched September 2023), Orlando and the Coastal Carolinas (announced April 2024, with **no home sales commenced in either as of December 31, 2025**). No Georgia operations had begun.

FY2025 was a sharp cyclical downturn year, and Q1 2026 was worse.

Home sales revenue fell **$1.02 billion, or 23%**, and the decline was entirely volume: deliveries fell 23% to 4,947 from 6,460 while average sales price was flat at $680,000 versus $679,000. Net income to common fell 47.4%; diluted EPS fell 43.7%, the gap explained by an 8.6% reduction in share count.

**The leading indicators were worse than the reported results.** Net new orders fell 24% to 4,292, driven by a 23% drop in monthly absorption to 2.4 from 3.1 on essentially flat average selling communities (150.5 vs. 150.4). Backlog units fell **43% to 862** and backlog dollar value fell 42% to $670.1M. The cancellation rate rose to 12% from 10%. The West — 51% of deliveries and 56% of home sales revenue — was weakest, with orders down 32%.

**Margin compression had two distinct components.** Homebuilding gross margin fell to 21.0% from 23.3%, of which management attributed roughly 90bp to **$31.1M of real estate inventory impairments** (versus **zero in FY2024**). Total impairments and lot option abandonments in cost of home sales were $36.4M, or 1.1% of revenue, versus $4.2M and 0.1%. Adjusted homebuilding gross margin, which adds back capitalized interest and impairments, fell to 25.2% from 26.8% — so roughly 160bp of deterioration persisted after stripping out write-downs, which is the incentive and pricing effect.

**Operating leverage reversed hard.** SG&A dollars fell 10.3% to $423.9M against a 23% revenue decline, so the ratio rose to **12.6% from 10.8%** (sales and marketing 4.9% → 5.8%; G&A 5.8% → 6.8%). Approximately 55% of the SG&A base behaved as fixed.

**Cash generation deteriorated more than earnings.** Operating cash flow fell $534.6M to $161.5M from $696.1M. Management decomposed this as lower net income (−$217M), a $257.5M increase in cash used in real estate inventories, and a $148.7M decrease in cash provided by receivables. Free cash flow of $128.5M (OCF less $32.9M capex) equates to a **3.7% FCF margin**. Notably, operating cash flow was *below* net income ($161.5M vs. $241.1M), which for a builder signals inventory absorbing earnings — consistent with completed unsold homes rising from 464 to 681.

**The balance sheet remained the strongest part of the story.** Cash of $982.8M and total liquidity of $1.8B including $798.1M of revolver availability; senior notes of $647.6M net ($300M at 5.250% due June 2027, $350M at 5.700% due June 2028); loans payable of $456.5M including $450M drawn on the Term Facility at 4.9%; no revolver borrowings. Total homebuilding debt of $1,104.1M against stockholders' equity of $3,315.8M produced homebuilding debt-to-capital of 25.0% (from 21.6%) and net homebuilding debt-to-net capital of 3.5% (from −1.6%, i.e., net cash a year earlier). Covenant headroom was wide: tangible net worth $3,148.7M against a $2,343.4M minimum, leverage 5.7% against a 60% cap, interest coverage 6.4x against a 1.5x floor. Altman Z-Score of 3.03 sat in the safe zone.

Three points matter. Gross margin at 18.8% is the weakest figure in the dataset, and only about 20bp of the decline was impairment-related ($1.07M versus $1.07M a year earlier) — so the compression was pricing, incentives and cost, a worse composition than FY2025's. Orders were flat in absolute terms only because community count grew 8.6%; per-community monthly absorption fell to 2.6 from 2.8. And the effective tax rate collapsed to roughly 1.2% ($81K on $6,891K pretax) from 26.0%, which the release did not explain; federal energy tax credits against a small pretax base is the plausible mechanism, but the Q1 10-Q was not available to verify.

Cash fell $134.9M to $847.9M while real estate inventories rose $124.1M to $3,302.3M and homes under construction rose 33.3% to 1,855 (completed-and-unsold fell 31.1% to 469). Homebuilding debt was flat at $1,104.3M, so the rise in net homebuilding debt-to-net capital to 7.2% from 3.5% was driven entirely by the cash decline. Merger-related transaction expense of $5.9M was recognized in the quarter.

**Earnings delivery was weak into the sale.** Three misses in the last four reported quarters: Q1 2026 $0.15 actual vs. $0.17 (−10.7%), Q4 2025 $0.70 vs. $0.79 (−11.4%), Q2 2025 $0.68 vs. $0.69 (−1.0%), with only Q3 2025 beating at $0.64 vs. $0.53 (+21.9%). Note the Q1 2026 estimate-versus-actual figures in the surprise table differ from the $0.08 diluted GAAP EPS in the earnings release, reflecting different measurement bases; on either basis the quarter fell short.

**Capital allocation** was dominated by repurchases: 8,550,822 shares at an average $32.42 for $277.2M, versus $146.7M in FY2024, leaving only $22.8M of the $300M authorization at year-end (raised from $250M to $300M on July 23, 2025). No dividend was paid and the 10-K stated no intention to initiate one. Buying $277.2M of stock while operating cash flow was $161.5M meant repurchases were funded partly from the balance sheet and partly from the $200M September 2025 term-loan upsize — the mechanism behind the swing from net cash to 3.5% net leverage.

## Growth Analysis

There is no forward growth to analyze. No filings exist after May 14, 2026 and the entity now reports inside Sumitomo Forestry's consolidated accounts.

The last observable trajectory was contraction on every leading measure. Revenue −23% in FY2025 and −29.7% in Q1 2026; deliveries −23% and −29.2%; backlog units −43% and backlog dollars −42%; absorption −23% for the year and 2.6 from 2.8 in Q1. Total lots owned or controlled fell 12% to 32,219 from 36,490, with optioned positions down 18% versus owned down 4% — the pipeline reduction came disproportionately from the cheaper, more flexible lever, which is the right sequencing but confirms the retrenchment.

Management's own framing in the 10-K was that "demand softness is being driven primarily by consumer sentiment rather than underlying employment or household formation fundamentals," with 2026 priorities of "operational efficiency, sustainable growth, and strategic expansion" and selective incentives "with an emphasis on preserving long-term pricing discipline." The Q1 print — 500bp of adjusted gross margin decline with flat ASP — is hard to reconcile with pricing discipline; the more consistent reading is that pace was chosen over price. The offsetting consideration is that Q1 is the seasonally weakest delivery quarter for a builder whose deliveries concentrate in H2, and 736 deliveries against an 862-unit opening backlog mechanically produced poor fixed-cost absorption. One quarter does not establish a new margin level, and no subsequent quarter is observable.

A framing sensitivity rather than a forecast: applying FY2025's Q1 share of deliveries (1,040 ÷ 4,947 = 21.0%) to Q1 2026's 736 deliveries implies roughly 3,500 full-year deliveries, a further ~29% decline from 2025. Annualizing Q1 EPS (4 × $0.08 = $0.32) is almost certainly too low given seasonality and is shown only to bracket the range.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation question resolved to a single number: **$47.00 per share in cash**, with no earn-out, stock component, or contingent consideration. Against that:

- Book value per share: $3,315,834K ÷ 84,478,836 shares outstanding at 12/31/25 = **$39.25**, so $47.00 is **1.20× book**.
- $47.00 ÷ FY2025 diluted EPS of $2.72 = **17.3× trailing GAAP earnings** — trough earnings, so the multiple overstates the price paid on normalized power.
- $47.00 ÷ FY2024 diluted EPS of $4.83 = **9.7×**.
- Equity value: 84.48M shares × $47.00 ≈ **$3.97B**; using the 88.70M diluted weighted-average count, ≈$4.17B. Adding $1,104.1M of homebuilding debt and subtracting $982.8M of cash gives an enterprise value of roughly **$4.09–4.29B**. Press aggregates conflict — Reuters reported "about $4.5 billion," Orange County Business Journal "$4.9 billion," The Real Deal "$5B" — and none reconciles to a single filing-based definition. Only the $47.00 per-share figure is unambiguous.
- Premium: reported at 29% by multiple outlets, implying an unaffected price of roughly **$36.43** ($47.00 ÷ 1.29). The stock traded up ~28% to $46.32 premarket on announcement.

The valuation score of 2.0 reflects the absence of asymmetry rather than an opinion that $47.00 was cheap or dear. Once a fixed cash price is set, the distribution of outcomes is truncated at the consideration: the best case is receiving $47.00 and the downside cases are deal break (reverting toward $36 or lower, given the deterioration that followed signing) or protracted appraisal litigation. From the last observable market prints of $46.90 and $46.95, the gross spread to consideration was 0.1–0.2%, against an $82,336,000 termination fee (roughly 2.07% of derived equity value), a company MAE condition, and an outside date of August 13, 2026. That is not a compensated risk.

On whether the price was adequate: the evidence does not support a conclusion in either direction. 1.20× book is not an obviously generous multiple for a builder with $1.8B of liquidity, 25% debt-to-capital and 32,000+ lots, and the 29% premium largely reflects a depressed unaffected price rather than a rich absolute level. Conversely, Sumitomo agreed at $47.00 with FY2025's 21.0% gross margin as the visible base and then absorbed a quarter at 18.8% with EPS of $0.08 — the buyer bore that deterioration, had no financing condition, did not assert an MAE, and closed. The fairness opinion and the merger proxy's "Background of the Merger" section, which would settle the process question, were not available.

## Competitive Landscape

The most useful competitive datapoint is the company's own language. The 10-K states: "Competition in the homebuilding industry is intense, and there are relatively low barriers to entry into our business," and concedes that several primary competitors are "significantly larger, have longer operating histories and may have greater resources or lower cost of capital." The filing does not claim a durable structural moat.

Tri Pointe differentiated on design, homeowner experience, product breadth and its LivingSmart platform — positioning that monetizes in a pricing-power market and compresses quickly in an incentive-driven one. The FY2025 and Q1 2026 results are the test: with ASP flat and adjusted gross margin down 160bp in FY2025 and 500bp in Q1 2026, the premium positioning did not defend margin when absorption fell.

Geographic concentration remained high and did not improve. The West produced 51% of FY2025 deliveries and 56% of home sales revenue, and 46% of Q1 2026 deliveries (342 of 736) at a $778K ASP. East average selling communities grew 23% in FY2025 and 28% in Q1 2026, but community-count mix shift is not the same as revenue-concentration reduction, and opening communities into a market where East absorption fell 28% raises impairment risk on the newest positions. Central markets were described as absorbing "elevated inventory levels in both the resale and new home markets," and the West saw "softer demand conditions and elongated sales cycles" across every market.

The competitive position score of 3.0 continues the prior downgrade from 5.0 and reflects deteriorating trajectory — absorption, backlog, margin and relative TSR all moved against the company — layered on a self-described low-barrier industry.

The transaction itself sits inside a confirmable pattern of Japanese housing companies acquiring U.S. builders, with Sekisui House's acquisition of MDC cited as the comparable (single-source, unconfirmed against filings). One trade-press account reported that Sumitomo was attracted specifically to Tri Pointe's California entitlement and regulatory capability — the asset a financial buyer would treat as a cost center. That rationale is a journalist's characterization and appears in no filing.

## Risk Assessment

**The controlling risk is that the instrument no longer exists.** The Form 25-NSE filed May 14, 2026 removed the security from listing and registration. Three independent tool-level failures on CIK resolution for TPH are consistent with removal from the active ticker-to-CIK mapping, though that is weak corroboration on its own; the Form 25-NSE is the decisive evidence. Any price target, hold recommendation, or position sizing on TPH equity is unactionable.

**Residual and unresolved exposures.** Whether appraisal-rights proceedings under DGCL §262 remain outstanding for dissenting shares is unknown; the merger agreement expressly contemplated Dissenting Shares. How the change-of-control defaults were resolved — lender consent versus termination and repayment of the $450M Term Facility and $90.6M of Tri Pointe Connect mortgage repurchase facilities — is not confirmed by any filing available. No post-merger Form 15-12B appeared.

**Business risks that were operative before closing**, retained here for read-through value:
- **Impairment risk, realized.** $31.1M in 2025 versus zero in 2024 and $11.5M in 2023, with Item 1A warning that "further material write-downs and impairments in the value of inventory may be required."
- **California strict liability.** The 10-K notes California "imposes a ten year, strict liability tail on many construction liability claims," with potential losses "greater than those of our competitors who have smaller California operations."
- **Assumed WRECO liabilities** from the 2014 Weyerhaeuser Real Estate transaction, where WRECO self-insured a significant portion of general liability outside California and Nevada pre-merger, such that "it is likely that most of these claims will not be covered by insurance."
- **Homeowners insurance as a demand constraint** — carriers cancelling or repricing policies, with buyers unable to obtain insurance failing to qualify for mortgages. The January 2025 Southern California wildfires are cited repeatedly.
- **Off-balance-sheet land commitments** of $209.6M in non-refundable deposits against approximately $2.1B of remaining aggregate purchase price, with some positions carrying development obligations that survive termination.
- **Tariff exposure** on steel and aluminum, and vendor concentration at Tri Pointe Connect, which "relies on a third-party vendor to materially assist" with mortgage-lending infrastructure.

**Governance items.** A plaintiff-firm "fair price" investigation was announced on February 13, 2026 — a press release, not a filed complaint, and a routine feature of essentially every public-company merger. The substantive item is equity-award treatment: all pre-2026 RSUs vested and converted at $47.00, and **PSUs, vested or unvested, vested at maximum performance** and converted at $47.00, against FY2025 performance that was clearly below target on the disclosed metrics (Adjusted Pre-Tax Earnings $397.8M versus $676.3M; relative TSR 182.44 versus peer 214.56). Maximum-performance treatment on a change of control is a common pre-negotiated plan term that exists to avoid post-signing discretion, and the offsetting design point is that 2026-granted RSUs did **not** accelerate and retained original time-vesting. No inference about management motive is warranted. Insider transaction data (Form 4) was not retrievable, so the pre-announcement window was not examined — an unresolved gap rather than a finding.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (the case for having owned it, now closed).** Holders of record received $47.00 in cash at a reported 29% premium to a $36.43 unaffected price, from a strategic buyer with no financing condition, in a transaction approved by 99.99% of votes cast and completed inside three months of signing. That outcome was superior to the likely standalone path: FY2025 EPS of $2.72 was already down 44%, Q1 2026 annualized to roughly $0.32, backlog was down 43%, and the mortgage-rate channel has tightened further. The FY2025 buyback at an average $32.42 proved 69% of the eventual deal price, adding roughly $124.7M of value ($14.58 × 8.55M shares) to continuing holders. This case is fully realized and cannot be re-entered.

**Bear case (why the stock is uninvestable now).** There is no security. Beyond that, the residual scenarios are all capped: appraisal litigation recovers at most $47.00 plus statutory interest over a multi-year horizon with legal costs; a deal-break scenario (already foreclosed) would have reverted the stock toward the mid-$30s or below given the post-signing deterioration. On fundamentals, the last quarter observable showed 18.8% gross margin with almost no impairment contribution, an SG&A ratio of 17.9%, cash down $134.9M, and inventory up $124.1M while gross margin was at its cycle low. The 10-K's own "relatively low barriers to entry" language, 56% West revenue concentration, a ten-year California strict-liability tail, self-insured WRECO liabilities, and $2.1B of remaining land commitments against $209.6M of deposits describe a franchise with limited defensive characteristics in a demand downturn.

**Score trajectory.** This is the fifth consecutive AVOID, with the overall score moving 4.0 → 3.9 → 3.5 → 3.2 → 3.2. The current work confirms rather than reverses the decline and holds the score at 3.2. Competitive position stays at 3.0 (absorption, backlog and margin all deteriorating against a self-described low-barrier industry) and risk factors stay at 2.5 (the security is delisted and residual outcomes are capped and unverifiable). Financial health moves to 5.0 from 5.3 — not because of price action, but because Q1 2026 showed cash down $134.9M, inventory up $124.1M, net homebuilding debt-to-net capital rising to 7.2% from 3.5%, and gross margin at 18.8%; the offsetting strength is genuine, with $1.7B of liquidity, wide covenant headroom and an Altman Z of 3.03. Management quality is held at 5.0: compensation actually paid fell from $15.8M to $6.3M as Adjusted Pre-Tax Earnings fell 41%, which is evidence of a functioning pay-for-performance linkage, and the buyback proved accretive; offsetting this are maximum PSU vesting on change of control and the absence of any insider transaction data.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria**: There is no path to re-entry in TPH common stock. The equity was cancelled and converted into the right to receive $47.00 in cash; the entity survives as an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of Sumitomo Forestry. The only conceivable future access would be a re-IPO or carve-out of the platform by Sumitomo Forestry, of which there is no indication in any available source, or exposure through Sumitomo Forestry's own listed equity — a different security requiring a separate analysis of a Japanese parent with a materially different risk profile.

**Residual monitoring items** (for completeness rather than actionability):
1. Any court docket indicating perfected appraisal-rights claims under DGCL §262. A resolution above $47.00 would be relevant only to holders who dissented and perfected before closing.
2. Filing of a Form 15-12B, which would formally complete deregistration and close the residual verification gap around the effective date.
3. Sumitomo Forestry's segment disclosures, which will eventually provide the only forward view of these assets.

**Timeline**: The situation is closed. Appraisal proceedings, if any exist, typically run 2–4 years. No reassessment of TPH as an equity is warranted at any horizon.

**Read-through to live positions** — the useful output of this file. Q1 2026 gross margin of 18.8% with only $1.1M of impairments confirms that builder margin compression is now driven by incentives and pricing rather than write-downs, and that the write-down cycle likely still lies ahead. The 10-K notes the FOMC "decided to maintain the current target range for federal funds" in January 2026, and management framed near-term demand softness as sentiment-driven against elevated home prices and affordability constraints. That supports continued caution on the homebuilder and mortgage complex and on the forward-demand-versus-volume-data gap that has not yet closed in the aggregate housing statistics.

## Conclusion

**AVOID — overall score 3.2.** Tri Pointe Homes was acquired by Sumitomo Forestry for $47.00 per share in cash. A Form 25-NSE removing the security from listing and registration was filed May 14, 2026, no company filings exist after that date, and the provided market data returns no price history, no market capitalization and no analyst targets — all consistent with a closed transaction. The two available options snapshots show the stock pinned at $46.90 and $46.95, within pennies of the fixed consideration.

The scores reflect a business whose last observable fundamentals were deteriorating on every leading measure (revenue −29.7% in Q1 2026, gross margin 18.8% with negligible impairment contribution, SG&A at 17.9% of revenue, backlog −43% for the prior year) attached to a balance sheet that remained sound ($1.7B liquidity, 25% debt-to-capital, Altman Z 3.03, wide covenant headroom), with no remaining valuation asymmetry because the exit price was contractually fixed. Management's record is mixed but not adverse: compensation actually paid tracked declining performance downward, the FY2025 buyback at $32.42 proved 69% of the eventual deal price, and the change-of-control equity treatment was pre-negotiated and disclosed in advance.

No price target is assigned. No analyst coverage or target data is available, and the only defensible figure — the $47.00 per-share cash consideration — is a realized settlement rather than a forward valuation.
