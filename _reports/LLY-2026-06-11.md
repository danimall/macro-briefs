**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Eli Lilly has become the clearest expression of the GLP-1/incretin demand shock in large-cap pharma. FY2025 revenue grew 45% to $65.2B; net income nearly doubled to $20.6B; and Q1 2026 accelerated further to $19.8B revenue (+56% YoY) and $7.4B net income (+168% YoY). Mounjaro and Zepbound together produced 56% of 2025 revenue and 65% in Q1 2026, with Mounjaro now cited as the world's best-selling drug. The company has beaten consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters by 12% to 27%, and management raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $82–85B. This is genuine earnings inflection at a scale rarely seen in a trillion-dollar company.

The valuation debate is the crux. At ~$1,163 the trailing P/E of ~40x looks expensive in isolation, but trailing earnings substantially understate current earnings power. On consensus current-year EPS of $36.21 the multiple is ~32x, and on next-year EPS of $44.49 it is ~26x, against EPS growth running above 25% and revenue growth above 50%. That puts the PEG near 1.0 on forward numbers. Estimate revisions are positive across every horizon (current-year +4.5%, next-year +6.0% over 90 days), and the company's beat cadence means consensus is more likely a floor than a ceiling. Trailing P/E is the wrong anchor; forward and normalized earnings power is the correct one.

The case carries real risk. Concentration is extreme: six products are 82% of revenue and the two incretins alone are 65% in the most recent quarter. The U.S. government pricing deal finalized in Q1 2026 trades price for access, IRA price-setting is accelerating (Jardiance –66% in 2026, Trulicity/Verzenio selected for 2028), and Novo Nordisk reached the oral-GLP-1 market first, with Lilly's Foundayo trailing Novo's oral Wegovy in early prescribing despite superior trial data. These are reasons to size the position with discipline. We rate LLY a BUY.

## Company Overview

Lilly is a ~150-year-old Indiana-based pharmaceutical company operating as a single human-pharmaceuticals segment across ~90 countries with roughly 50,000 employees, ~12,000 in R&D. Its four therapeutic areas are cardiometabolic health, oncology, immunology, and neuroscience. The business has been reshaped by tirzepatide, a dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist marketed as Mounjaro (type 2 diabetes) and Zepbound (obesity, obstructive sleep apnea).

The competitive position rests on several structural advantages. Tirzepatide has best-in-class efficacy, with compound patent protection running to 2036 in the US, 2037 in the EU, and 2040 in Japan, a far longer runway than the near-term IRA and biosimilar threats facing legacy products. Lilly is building vertically integrated manufacturing capacity across the US (including NC, WI, IN, VA, TX, AL, PA), Ireland, Germany, and the Netherlands, which is itself a moat given the multi-year lead times for regulated injectable and oral capacity. LillyDirect, the direct-to-patient channel, reduces PBM dependence for cash-pay obesity scripts. The next-generation pipeline (orforglipron, an oral GLP-1; retatrutide, a triple agonist; and a broad label-expansion program across HFpEF, MASH, OSA, and cardiovascular outcomes) gives the franchise duration beyond the current molecules.

## Financial Analysis

The growth profile is unusual for a company of this size:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 34.1 | 45.0 | 65.2 | 19.8 (+56% YoY) |
| Net income ($B) | 5.2 | 10.6 | 20.6 | 7.4 (+168% YoY) |
| Diluted EPS | — | 11.71 | 22.95 | 8.26 |
| Gross margin | — | 81.3% | 83.0% | 81.9% |

2025 revenue growth decomposed as +50% volume, –6% price, +1% FX. Q1 2026 ran +65% volume against –13% price. The mechanism matters: enormous volume growth partly offset by deliberate and competitive price erosion. Mounjaro 2025 revenue was $23.0B (+99%) and Zepbound $13.5B (+175%); in Q1 2026 alone Mounjaro reached $8.66B and Zepbound $4.16B.

Cash generation is strong and scaling. Operating cash flow nearly doubled to $16.8B in 2025; free cash flow was $9.0B after $7.8B of capacity-driven capex. Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $5.3B. The dividend was raised to $1.73/quarter ($6.92 annualized for 2026), and $8.6B remained on the $15B buyback authorization at quarter-end.

On the balance sheet, total debt was $43.4B against equity of $31.2B at Q1 2026, a debt/equity of ~1.4x. The Altman Z-Score of ~1.25 nominally places Lilly in a "distress zone," which is a false signal. The Z-Score penalizes financial leverage and low book equity relative to liabilities, exactly the profile of a highly profitable mega-cap that retires shares and carries cheap debt. With ~32% net margins, ~$17B annual operating cash flow, $5.3B cash plus ~$10B of undrawn credit, and ROE near 78%, debt service is trivial and solvency risk is negligible. The correct framing is capital intensity plus aggressive buybacks, not distress. One genuine quality watch-item: free cash flow conversion is being depressed by the capex buildout (FCF/net income well below 100% in 2025), which is appropriate for a company adding capacity into demand but should be monitored as the build matures.

A meaningful estimation-risk note: gross-to-net adjustments are large and growing. Reductions to net sales were $62.1B in 2025, and U.S. rebate/discount/return liabilities rose from $10.3B to $15.1B. A 5% change in those estimates is roughly a $900M revenue swing. This is normal for the category but it embeds judgment in reported revenue and will scale with the franchise.

## Growth Analysis

The forward picture is anchored by management's FY2026 revenue guidance, which at the midpoint implies ~28% growth on top of the 45% delivered in 2025. Lilly's beat history supports treating consensus as conservative: four consecutive beats of 14.5%, 22.0%, 11.9%, and 27.0%. Consensus EPS revisions are positive across the board (current quarter +8.2% over 90 days, current year +4.5%, next year +6.0%), though the most recent 30-day window has been roughly flat, suggesting the rapid upward revision phase has paused while estimates digest the Q1 print.

The growth durability rests on three legs. First, the installed tirzepatide franchise continues to grow on volume even as price falls, and the addressable obesity and cardiometabolic population remains enormous. Second, Foundayo (orforglipron), the oral GLP-1 approved for obesity in April 2026 under an expedited Commissioner's National Priority Voucher review, expands the TAM: an oral pill requires no refrigeration, scales more cheaply in manufacturing, and reaches patients reluctant to inject. Orforglipron also met its primary endpoint in a Phase 3 type-2 diabetes trial. Third, retatrutide, a triple agonist with Phase 3 obesity data that analysts describe as potentially first-line, gives the franchise a next-generation weapon, alongside label-expansion programs (HFpEF, MASH, OSA, cardiovascular outcomes, peripheral artery disease) that extend revenue per molecule.

The growth is broad-based within the franchise and supported by multiple confirming pipeline signals, which raises its durability above a single-product story. The tempering factor in the near term is that Foundayo's first-month prescribing trailed Novo's oral Wegovy among both primary-care physicians and endocrinologists, per Spherix. Lilly leads on data; Novo got to the oral market first. The decisive competitive battle is retatrutide and label breadth over 2026–2028, not the first oral pill out the gate.

## Valuation Assessment

Peer multiples in pharma cluster around a median P/E of ~19.9x (JNJ 27.0x, MRK 33.8x, PFE 19.9x, BMY 16.0x, ZTS 13.0x). On trailing earnings, Lilly's ~40x is roughly double the median. That comparison is the wrong one, because the peer group is largely ex-growth or in patent-cliff management while Lilly is compounding revenue above 50%.

The correct anchor is forward and normalized earnings. With EPS growth running above 25% and accelerating revenue, the forward PEG is roughly 1.0, reasonable for a franchise of this quality and growth rate. Lilly is below the 50x-forward-P/E threshold at which premium-multiple stocks become asymmetrically vulnerable, so the positive estimate-revision signal carries weight here rather than simply reflecting consensus optimism already embedded in price.

Analyst targets reinforce the floor-not-ceiling view: mean ~$1,216, median ~$1,251, both above the current price, with a high of $1,500 and a low of $850. Given the beat cadence, the more probable path is upward target revision as consensus catches the raised guidance. The constraint on the valuation score is honest: a 26x forward multiple is not a deep-value setup, the price-erosion trajectory (–13% in Q1, –25% ex-US) compresses the margin of safety as the revenue base enlarges, and the next-year multiple still requires the growth to materialize. That keeps valuation at 6.0 rather than higher, but it supports a long position rather than capping one.

## Competitive Landscape

The obesity/incretin market is effectively a Lilly–Novo Nordisk duopoly, with the competitive front migrating to oral GLP-1s. Lilly's structural edge is efficacy and pipeline depth: tirzepatide's dual-agonist mechanism, orforglipron's diabetes-trial superiority over oral semaglutide, and retatrutide's triple-agonist profile. The vertical manufacturing buildout is a durable advantage because regulated capacity takes years to bring online and supply has been the binding constraint on the entire category.

The trajectory is mixed rather than uniformly improving, which is why competitive position scores a 7.5. Novo captured early share with oral Wegovy, and Lilly's Foundayo trailed in initial prescribing. PBM access is a live friction: CVS Caremark dropped Zepbound as a preferred obesity drug on some plans in July 2025, demonstrating formulary risk in a category where self-insured employers must opt into obesity coverage. Compounded and counterfeit incretins continue to erode price and confuse the channel. China-based competitors are emerging. The net assessment is that Lilly holds the best long-run hand on data and capacity, but the near-term oral launch and access dynamics warrant active monitoring.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile is the lowest-scoring dimension at 5.5, reflecting specific catalysts for material downside that are not fully discounted.

**Concentration.** The 10-K explicitly warns that incretin volume can swing disproportionately in any given period due to channel dynamics. Any safety signal, demand shift, or competitive displacement in tirzepatide would hit results disproportionately.

**Government pricing.** The U.S. government pricing deal includes lower Medicaid and other prices, a Medicare "GLP-1 Bridge Program" running July 1, 2026 through December 31, 2027, participation in a government direct-to-patient discount platform, and a three-year Section 232 tariff grace period conditioned on US manufacturing investment. The risk is that pricing concessions outrun the volume they unlock, and long-term Medicare obesity coverage is still being negotiated with uncertain uptake.

**IRA price-setting.** Jardiance faces government-set Medicare prices in 2026; Trulicity and Verzenio are selected for 2028. Lilly expects additional products to be selected, accelerating revenue erosion ahead of patent expiry.

**Manufacturing execution.** Tens of billions in capex and up to ~$10B in take-or-pay supply commitments make over- or under-building demand a two-sided risk, with China supply-chain and BIOSECURE/tariff exposure layered on top.

**Litigation.** Active matters include the insulin pricing MDL, 340B disputes (one at the Supreme Court cert stage), GI/NAION product-liability MDLs for the incretins, an Emgality patent reversal against Lilly in April 2026 (charge taken in Q1 2026), and an AMP qui tam loss with cert sought.

The Q1 2026 10-Q reports no material changes to risk factors versus the 10-K, meaning the government deal and IRA risks were already incorporated into the February filing rather than being fresh surprises.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term options carry a cautionary tone that should be read as event-hedging rather than a directional verdict. ATM IV of 42.0% sits modestly rich to 38.0% one-year historical vol, with the curve in backwardation (42.0% near-term versus 36.6% at six months) and an elevated 11.4% one-week put skew. This configuration is consistent with hedging into a known near-term catalyst (the implied move on the nearest expiry is only ±1.9%), and the longer-dated skew is the more informative read. Put/call volume (0.20) and OI (0.52) ratios are call-tilted, not bearish, and a block of 5,335 $1,500 July calls (3.5x OI) signals upside positioning. Short interest is negligible at 1.1% of float, so squeeze dynamics are not a factor in either direction. The near-term vol spike is priced uncertainty around an event, not a fundamental warning, and it does not override the constructive thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Volume growth in the incretin franchise continues to outrun price erosion as obesity coverage broadens; Foundayo scales the oral TAM and retatrutide establishes a next-generation first-line position; label expansions (HFpEF, MASH, CV outcomes) lengthen the runway; and capacity comes online on schedule, protecting both supply and the tariff grace period. EPS reaches the high end of the trajectory implied by FY2026 guidance and beyond, consensus targets re-rate upward, and the stock moves toward the $1,500 bull target as the market pays a quality-growth premium on rising forward earnings.

**Bear Case.** Government pricing programs (Medicare Bridge, IRA selections in 2028) compress realized price faster than volume can offset on a much larger base; Novo's first-mover advantage in oral GLP-1 erodes Lilly's oral share; a safety signal or formulary loss hits the concentrated franchise disproportionately; and the capex buildout proves to have overbuilt demand, pressuring margins and FCF. In that scenario the multiple compresses toward the peer cohort and the stock retraces toward the $850 analyst low.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Base case target: ~$1,335 over 12 months.** This applies a ~30x multiple to consensus next-year EPS, a modest discount to the current ~32x current-year multiple to acknowledge price-erosion pressure. That sits between the bear and bull scenarios and implies ~15% upside from $1,163.

**Bull case target: $1,500** (~29% upside) — high analyst target, reached if oral GLP-1 and retatrutide momentum drive consensus EPS above the current trajectory and the market sustains a premium multiple.

**Bear case target: $850** (~27% downside) — analyst low, reached if government pricing and competitive share loss compress realized price and the multiple de-rates toward peers.

**Timeframe: 12 months,** tied to identifiable catalysts: the July 1, 2026 start of the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Program, sequential Foundayo prescribing data versus oral Wegovy through H2 2026, retatrutide regulatory progress, and quarterly earnings prints where the volume/price spread is the key metric.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Incretin volume growth decelerates below ~25% YoY while price erosion accelerates beyond ~15%, signaling the volume-over-price dynamic is breaking on a larger base.
2. A material tirzepatide safety signal or a major formulary exclusion (e.g., a large PBM removing Mounjaro/Zepbound from preferred status across commercial books).
3. Two consecutive quarters of negative consensus EPS revisions, reversing the current positive-revision trend.

## Conclusion

Lilly is a trillion-dollar company growing revenue above 50% and earnings well above that, with a multi-year volume runway, a deep next-generation pipeline, and demonstrated capital discipline. The valuation that looks stretched on trailing earnings is reasonable on forward earnings: ~26x next-year EPS against 25%+ growth, a PEG near 1.0, and consensus targets above the current price that the beat cadence is likely to push higher. The risks are real and concentrated, principally the incretin concentration and the accelerating government price-setting regime, which is why the position should be sized with discipline rather than maximized. The growth-versus-valuation math, the positive estimate-revision backdrop, and the pipeline optionality support owning the stock. We rate LLY a **BUY**, with the franchise concentration and pricing trajectory as the items to monitor most closely.
