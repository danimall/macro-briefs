**Research Date: July 19, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ResMed is the global leader in devices and connected-care software for sleep-disordered breathing, running a razor/razor-blade model where CPAP/APAP flow generators pull patients onto therapy and high-margin recurring masks, accessories, and resupply drive durable revenue. The business is generating record profitability: Q3 FY26 gross margin reached 62.2% (+290 bps YoY), non-GAAP EPS grew 21%, and the balance sheet is effectively net cash. Yet the stock has fallen roughly 28% from its February 2026 highs near $273 to ~$199, and it now trades at about 17-18x forward EPS, a meaningful discount to health-care-equipment peers.

The gap between reported momentum and share price is the crux of the investment case. The de-rating is not about the trailing numbers, which are strong; it reflects two forward-looking fears that a Morgan Stanley downgrade crystallized in June 2026: GLP-1 weight-loss drugs shrinking the addressable sleep-apnea pool, and Philips Respironics returning to the US market after years under an FDA consent decree that handed ResMed its post-2021 share gains. Both risks are real and both play out over years rather than quarters, which is why they are hard to price and why the estimate picture has begun to soften at the margin: next-year consensus EPS has been cut 1.5% over 90 days even as the current year edged up.

We rate RMD a HOLD (growth_hold). The quality of the franchise, the balance-sheet strength, and the valuation discount argue against avoiding the name, and the recurring-revenue mix shift improves earnings quality. But the combination of two binary overhangs, falling forward estimates, no open-market insider buying, options markets pricing imminent stress, and a management team that bought back $500M of stock near the highs keeps this short of a BUY. The prior two reports carried a BUY; the ~28% drawdown on unchanged trailing fundamentals improves the entry, but the softening in next-year estimates and the term-structure warning in options are new, incremental negatives that raise the bar for continuing an unchanged bullish call.

## Company Overview

ResMed sells devices and cloud-connected software for obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) and respiratory disorders (COPD, neuromuscular disease) in roughly 140 countries. It manufactures primarily in Australia, Singapore, Malaysia, France, China, and the US. Two reported segments:

- **Sleep & Breathing Health** — ~88% of FY25 revenue ($4,505M), split between devices ($2,665M) and masks/other ($1,840M).
- **Residential Care Software (RCS)** — FY25 $641M; SaaS for out-of-hospital care providers including HME, home health/hospice, and senior living (includes MEDIFOX DAN in Germany).

The competitive moat rests on a dominant installed base, physician and HME channel relationships, the AirView connected-care data platform, and a global manufacturing footprint. In May 2025 ResMed acquired VirtuOx, a Medicare-enrolled independent diagnostic testing facility for in-home sleep testing, a vertical-integration step into the diagnosis funnel that carries outsized regulatory implications relative to its ~$25M cost.

## Financial Analysis

ResMed's financial profile is among the cleanest in the sector. Altman Z-Score 4.32 (safe zone), current ratio 3.44, debt/equity 0.37, ROE 23.5%, ROA 17.1%.

**Revenue** compounded at roughly 13% over three years: $3,578M (FY22) → $4,223M (FY23) → $4,685M (FY24) → $5,146M (FY25). FY25 grew 10% both reported and in constant currency. Nine-month FY26 revenue reached $4,190M (+10%).

**Margins are the standout.** Gross margin expanded from 56.7% (FY24) to 59.4% (FY25) to 62.2% in Q3 FY26, driven by component-cost improvements and manufacturing/logistics efficiencies, partly a normalization from the 2022-23 supply-chain cost spike. Operating margin ran ~32.7% (FY25); net margin 27.2%. Q3 FY26 revenue was $1,431M (+11% reported, +8% constant currency) with non-GAAP EPS of $2.86 (+21%).

**Cash generation is high-quality.** FY25 operating cash flow was $1,752M and free cash flow $1,662M (32% FCF margin), with OCF consistently exceeding net income. The company deleveraged aggressively: long-term debt fell from $1,431M (FY23) to $658M (FY25), while cash rose to $1,661M at 3/31/26 against total debt of ~$664M, leaving RMD effectively net cash. A $250M note due 7/10/26 was reclassed to short-term debt, a non-issue against $1.66B cash plus a $1.5B undrawn revolver.

**Two headwinds to model.** First, the effective tax rate rose to ~21% in Q3 FY26 from ~16% prior year, driven by OECD Pillar Two, a real FY26 EPS drag that should partially reverse from FY27 via the OECD Side-by-Side Safe Harbor. Second, growth mix is rotating: Q3 US devices grew 6% versus masks up 14%. The recurring resupply base is now the primary growth engine, which improves earnings quality and supports gross margin (mask-heavy mix carries higher margin), but it also signals that the new-patient device engine is decelerating just as the GLP-1 debate targets exactly that line.

The earnings surprise record is steady rather than spectacular: three beats and one miss over the last four reported quarters, with beats in the 1-2% range. The company delivers close to consensus, which caps the upside from beat-driven re-ratings.

## Growth Analysis

The secular tailwind is intact: care is shifting from hospital to home, and the undiagnosed OSA population, estimated in the hundreds of millions globally, dwarfs the treated base. That undiagnosed pool is the reason double-digit growth has persisted despite years of GLP-1 headlines.

The forward growth picture is genuinely two-sided. On the positive side, the mask/resupply franchise is compounding at double digits with high retention, and the VirtuOx acquisition plus expanding awareness campaigns (the 6th Global Sleep Survey across 13 countries) widen the diagnosis funnel. On ResMed's data, GLP-1 co-prescription has been associated with higher CPAP initiation and adherence, because obesity-clinic contact increases OSA screening.

On the negative side, estimate revisions have turned modestly negative where it matters most for a multi-year holder. Next-quarter consensus is down 1.3% over 30 days and next-year EPS is down 1.5% over 90 days, settling at $12.00. The current-year figure of $11.11 edged up 0.8% over 90 days, so this is deceleration at the forward edge rather than a collapse. A May 2026 Healio-reported study found GLP-1/dual agonists reduce new-onset OSA risk in obesity, a long-tail headwind that erodes future incidence over years. RCS is softening (+4% constant currency, senior-living weakness) and took a small lease impairment. Given the falling next-year estimates and decelerating device line, we score growth prospects at 6.0: solid and durable, but no longer accelerating on the metrics that drive forward re-ratings.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$199 on ~$11.11 current-year EPS, RMD trades at roughly 17.9x forward earnings; on next-year's $12.00 it is ~16.6x. Against the health-care-equipment peer median P/E of 26.1 (DXCM 32.2, STE 26.1, ZBH 22.6, HOLX 31.3), ResMed sits at a substantial discount despite superior margins, a fortress balance sheet, and comparable or better growth quality. GEHC is the only cheaper peer at 15.5x, and it trades at a discount for its own reasons.

The valuation lessons here are worth stating directly: cheapness alone has historically been a value-trap warning, not a buy signal, and it sorts backward in recent data. The relevant question is whether there is an identifiable catalyst or a specific reason the market is wrong. The bull answer is that the ~28% drawdown discounts a TAM-erosion scenario (GLP-1) that is years out while the current diagnosis funnel and undiagnosed pool support continued growth. The bear answer is that the discount is rational: two binary overhangs (Philips re-entry, GLP-1) with unknowable timing plus falling forward estimates justify a lower multiple than a clean compounder would earn.

Analyst targets span a wide $180-325 with a $234 median and $249 mean, implying material upside to consensus. On a stock with rising estimates we would treat those targets as floors, but RMD's next-year estimates are falling, so we treat the target dispersion as an honest reflection of the split view rather than a lagging floor. Net, the valuation is attractive on an absolute and relative basis, but the discount is at least partly earned, so we score valuation at 6.5 rather than higher.

## Competitive Landscape

ResMed effectively runs a duopoly with Philips in flow generators, alongside smaller players (Fisher & Paykel, React Health) that filled the void after Philips' 2021 DreamStation recall and US sales ban. That recall was a multi-year windfall and the primary driver of ResMed's post-2021 share gains. The central competitive question is how much share reverts if and when Philips fully returns; the timing is unknown, but the FY25 10-K explicitly warns a "substantial return" could reduce demand.

On GLP-1s, the evidence has so far not dented growth. The bear case is TAM shrinkage as weight loss reduces OSA severity and Zepbound's OSA approval validates a pharmaceutical alternative, with oral OSA drugs (Apnimed) adding to the threat. The bull case is that GLP-1s expand the diagnosis funnel and that co-prescribed patients show higher CPAP adherence. Continued high-single/low-double-digit device growth through FY26 is inconsistent with a near-term demand shock.

The trajectory here has softened. The prior report cut competitive position from 8.5 to 7.5 as the Philips and GLP-1 threats became more prominent in analyst dialogue and the Morgan Stanley downgrade landed. The moat remains wide, but the trajectory question — whether the position is improving, stable, or deteriorating — now reads as stable-to-slightly-eroding given the credible re-entry of a scaled competitor and a decelerating device line. We score competitive position at 6.5.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks here are material and not fully resolved:

1. **GLP-1 weight-loss drugs** — the defining debate. The 10-K names them as a potential threat to OSA severity/incidence and TAM. This is the single largest swing factor for the equity narrative, and the recent Healio study gives the bears a data point.
2. **Philips re-entry** — a scaled competitor operating under an FDA consent decree since 2021 could recover share on an unpredictable timeline. Both this and GLP-1 drove the June 2026 Morgan Stanley downgrade.
3. **VirtuOx regulatory surface** — a financially immaterial deal that changes ResMed's posture from HIPAA business associate to a directly-billing Medicare supplier under an active Corporate Integrity Agreement through 2027. Small deal, outsized compliance-risk optics.
4. **Reimbursement/policy** — exposure to CMS/Medicare, Medicaid cuts under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, potential restart of competitive bidding, VA disability-rating changes, expiring telehealth flexibilities, and the lingering 2022 AHRQ report questioning CPAP long-term outcomes.
5. **Litigation** — ongoing patent suits (Cleveland Medical, Fractus) mostly favoring ResMed, and an unsealed, not-yet-served FCA whistleblower complaint naming ResMed among multiple defendants over alleged VA overcharging; no loss estimate.
6. **Supply chain** — China rare-earth magnet export controls flagged as a specific new cost/supply risk.
7. **FX** — ~36% of revenue is ex-US; FX was a ~3pt tailwind to Q3 FY26 reported growth and can reverse.

These are specific catalysts for downside that are not fully priced, which is why we score risk factors at 5.0 rather than higher. Short interest is elevated at 12.0% of float (9.5 days to cover), confirming institutions are positioned bearishly.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV of 42.6% is rich versus 27.1% one-year historical vol (a 15.5-point spread) and sits at the 76th percentile within the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (42.6% near-term versus 31.7% at six months), which the market uses to signal event risk ahead. IV has jumped 13.1 points since mid-June while price barely moved, indicating rising uncertainty. The put/call skew is roughly balanced at the front (-0.9%) but steepens to -4.8% at six months, and the six-month put/call volume ratio is elevated. Rich IV and backwardation raise the case for patience on entry timing: the market is charging a premium for near-term protection, and a fundamental buyer would prefer to let that resolve.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($260-290):** The GLP-1 and Philips fears are overstated on a multi-year view. The undiagnosed OSA pool dwarfs the treated base, GLP-1 co-prescription expands the diagnosis funnel, and the recurring mask/resupply engine compounds at double digits with 62%+ gross margins. The tax headwind reverses from FY27. On a re-rating back toward even 20-22x forward EPS of ~$12, the stock reaches the high-$250s to $290, matching the upper half of the analyst range. Management's balance sheet supports continued buybacks and dividend growth.

**Bear Case ($155-175):** Philips recovers meaningful US share on a faster-than-expected timeline while GLP-1 adoption compresses new-patient device volumes, and the device line — already at +6% US — turns flat or negative. Next-year estimates continue to be cut, RCS softens further, and the multiple stays compressed at 13-15x forward on genuine TAM concerns. That implies roughly $155-175, or 12-22% downside from current levels.

The base case sits between these: a durable, cash-rich franchise growing high-single digits with margin support, trading at a discount that partly reflects real but slow-moving threats. That profile supports a hold rather than a buy.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with a growth_hold rationale: a positive but not compelling 12-month thesis anchored on the recurring-revenue franchise and the valuation discount, tempered by two binary overhangs and softening forward estimates.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next fiscal Q4 FY26 earnings (August 2026, roughly one year after the FY25 10-K). The device-vs-mask growth split and any device-line inflection will be the key data point.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Next-year EPS estimate revisions turn positive (currently -1.5% over 90 days), signaling the deceleration has stopped.
- Device revenue growth stabilizes or re-accelerates above high-single digits in a quarter, evidencing GLP-1 is not eroding the new-patient funnel.
- Clustered open-market insider buying near current levels, or a materially accelerated buyback at ~$199 (vs. the ~$263 average paid in 9M FY26).

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Confirmed accelerated Philips US re-entry with measurable share loss.
- A quarter of flat-to-negative device revenue combined with further cuts to forward estimates.
- Adverse resolution or costly settlement of the FCA whistleblower complaint, or a VirtuOx CIA breach.

## Conclusion

ResMed is a high-quality, financially pristine franchise trading at a discount to peers on a real drawdown. The fundamentals support the business, and the recurring mask/resupply mix improves earnings quality even as the device narrative faces scrutiny. But the ~28% decline reflects two credible, slow-moving threats (GLP-1 TAM erosion, Philips re-entry) that the market cannot yet price with confidence, and the forward evidence has softened at the margin: next-year estimates are falling, options markets are pricing imminent stress in backwardation, insiders show only programmatic selling and bought back stock near the highs, and short interest is elevated.

The trajectory of prior reports moved HOLD → BUY → BUY as valuation improved and financial health strengthened. The lower price on largely unchanged trailing fundamentals does improve the entry, but the new negatives (falling next-year estimates, the options warning, and a decelerating device line) offset the improved valuation and pull the rating back to HOLD. We would upgrade on a turn in forward estimate revisions or a device-line stabilization, and downgrade on confirmed Philips share loss. Overall score: 6.5.
