## Executive Summary

Valley National Bancorp has completed a credible earnings recovery. Net income went from $380.3M in FY2024 (depressed by a $308.8M provision) to $598.0M in FY2025, and H1 2026 delivered $334.8M with diluted EPS of $0.57 versus $0.40 a year earlier. Net interest margin expanded from 2.85% in FY2024 to 3.20% in Q2 2026, the adjusted efficiency ratio improved to 52.11% in Q2 from 55.20%, and adjusted ROATCE reached 11.98% in H1. With the Q2 print management raised guidance on net interest income (high end of 11–13%), loan growth (at or above the high end of 4–6%), and deposit growth (near the high end of 5–7%). The stock has responded, rising 57% over the trailing twelve months from a $9.08 low.

The Q2 10-Q, filed August 6, contains a change in the composition of that recovery that the raised guidance obscures. Through 2025, NIM expanded because funding costs fell faster than asset yields. In Q2 2026 the cost of interest-bearing liabilities rose 4bp to 3.10% and the cost of total deposits rose 1bp to 2.28% — the first increases of the cycle — while time deposits grew $1.5B (22% to 24% of deposits), money-market balances fell $506M, short-term FHLB advances went from zero to $375M at 3.94%, and liquid assets fell from 10.3% to 9.1% of earning assets. With the Fed on hold since December 2025 (Valley's own CECL baseline assumes no change until Q2 2028) and $11.96B of the $12.90B time-deposit book repricing within roughly eighteen months, the liability tailwind that drove 2025 is exhausted. Reaching the company's 3.30% NIM target now depends on fixed-rate asset repricing and mix shift, mechanisms that are slower and smaller than a 200bp move in deposit costs.

Two further items keep this at HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE. First, roughly 40% of the H1 EPS improvement ($0.069 of $0.17) came from lower provisioning, which occurred alongside deteriorating leading indicators: accruing past-due loans rose from 0.28% to 0.34% of loans, non-accruals from 0.87% to 0.88%, ACL/loans fell from 1.19% to 1.16%, and reserve coverage of non-accruals fell from 134.4% to 127.7%. Second, CET1 declined 28bp in six months to 10.71% while risk-weighted assets grew 5.2% against 3.4% asset growth — the mix shift into 100%-risk-weighted C&I is consuming capital faster than earnings replace it. On a probability-weighted basis I get roughly +6% price appreciation plus a 3.0% dividend against a bear case 26% below spot. That is approximately a fair risk-adjusted return, not an asymmetric one.

## Company Overview

Valley National Bancorp is the holding company for Valley National Bank, an OCC-chartered commercial bank founded in 1927 and headquartered in Morristown, New Jersey. At June 30, 2026 it held $66.3B of assets, $52.5B of gross loans, $54.1B of deposits and $7.9B of equity across 228 branches — 55% New Jersey, 18% New York, 19% Florida, and 8% in Alabama, California and Illinois. It is the largest commercial bank headquartered in New Jersey and ranks tenth by deposit share in the New York/northern New Jersey/Long Island market, first among institutions under $1 trillion in assets.

Profit concentration is extreme. In H1 2026 Commercial Banking generated $368.9M of pre-tax income (85.9% of the total) at a 3.65% segment NIM; Consumer Banking contributed $69.2M at a 2.60% NIM. Of $1,868.9M of goodwill, $1,441.1M sits in Commercial Banking. The commercial credit book is effectively the equity story.

The loan mix at June 30 was C&I $11.96B (22.8%), non-owner-occupied CRE $11.15B (21.2%), multifamily $9.03B (17.2%), owner-occupied CRE $7.69B (14.7%), construction $2.48B (4.7%), residential mortgage $5.98B (11.4%) and consumer $4.17B (8.0%). CRE including construction is 57.8% of loans. Differentiated niches include cannabis-related banking (deposits since 2020, extended to certain secured and real-estate lending in 2H2021), venture banking, homeowners-association banking, fund finance and tax-credit advisory. The bank holds an "Outstanding" CRA rating from its most recent examination.

Decomposing the +$0.17 H1 year-over-year EPS gain at a 23.5% tax rate on 558.0M diluted shares: net interest income contributed +$0.145, fee income +$0.030, expenses −$0.083, and provision relief +$0.069. The NII contribution is high-quality and repeatable at the margin; the provision contribution is not. H1'26 provisioning ran at roughly 0.16–0.17% of loans annualized. Reverting to FY2025's 0.24% on a $52B book costs about $40M pre-tax (~$0.055/share). The current run-rate embeds provisioning at the low end of the company's own 0.15–0.20% charge-off guidance.

One reconciliation note: the quoted EPS field of $1.01 is FY2025 GAAP, while the 12.26x trailing multiple implies TTM EPS of approximately $1.18 (FY2025 $1.01 less H1'25 $0.40 plus H1'26 $0.57). I use $1.18 for trailing multiples.

### Capital and the CRE ratio

| Ratio | 12/31/25 | 6/30/26 |
|---|---|---|
| CET1 | 10.99% | 10.71% |
| Total RBC | 13.77% | 13.77% |
| Tier 1 leverage | 9.63% | 9.49% |
| CRE concentration | 333% | 317% |

The 16-point decline in the reported CRE concentration ratio is almost entirely denominator-driven. Backing out the numerator from disclosed ratios and Total RBC capital gives $23,196M at year-end against $23,229M at June 30 — a 0.14% change in the measured CRE exposure against a 5.2% increase in Total RBC capital. Management attributes the improvement to organic capital accretion plus a $200M increase in Tier 2 capital from the May 2026 subordinated notes; roughly 55% of the capital increase that lowered the ratio was subordinated debt, which counts in Total RBC but not in CET1. Separately, the ratio excludes owner-occupied CRE by regulatory definition, and owner-occupied CRE grew $1,063M in H1 — essentially all of Valley's CRE growth. Total CRE including owner-occupied rose from $29.24B to $30.35B. The exclusion is standard and owner-occupied credit genuinely depends on business operations rather than property cash flow, but an investor reading "CRE concentration is falling" as evidence of shrinking exposure would be mistaken.

Meanwhile CET1 fell 28bp. Implied RWA grew $2,630M (+5.2%) versus $2,186M (+3.4%) of asset growth, lifting RWA density from 78.9% to 80.2% as the book shifted toward C&I. CET1 capital rose only $139.9M: $320.3M of net income to common less $123.5M of dividends and $72.4M of buybacks. The Fed's expedited dividend-consultation framework references a CRE concentration ratio below 300% where CRE grew 50%+ over the prior 36 months; at 317% and improving mainly through capital rather than exposure reduction, this remains a live constraint on capital return.

### Balance-sheet quality

Loans grew 12.9% annualized in Q2 against deposits at ~9.8%, and the gap was funded with CDs and FHLB advances. Loans/deposits is 96.9% against a 105% internal limit; wholesale funding is 14.8% of total funding against a 22.5% limit; $23.45B of unused non-deposit borrowing capacity remains. Uninsured deposits are ~$15.0B (28% of deposits, up from 25% at year-end 2024), covered roughly 1.93x by liquid assets plus unused capacity. Held-to-maturity securities carry a $389.6M unrealized loss ($3.76B amortized cost vs $3.37B fair value) that is not reflected in book value or regulatory capital — 6.95% of the $5,604.7M tangible common equity. Marking it at a 23.5% tax rate would take TBVPS from $10.13 to roughly $9.60. That is a valuation-framing point rather than a solvency one, given largely agency RMBS collateral and ample borrowing capacity.

Note that automated financial-health screens on VLY are unreliable: at least one vendor's "total assets" field of $243.9M corresponds to Valley's accrued interest receivable, not its $66.3B balance sheet, and Altman Z-Score models built for manufacturers will always classify a depository as distressed. Regulatory capital is the correct lens, and on that basis Valley is comfortably well-capitalized but trending the wrong way on CET1.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is running well above industry. Per the 10-Q's own citation of Federal Reserve data, Q2 2026 industry loan growth was 2.0%, C&I 3.6% and CRE 0.8%, with most banks reporting tightened CRE and C&I underwriting standards. On a matched quarterly basis Valley grew C&I 7.72% (about 2.1x industry) and total loans 3.22% (about 1.6x). C&I added $857M in Q2 and $1,000M in H1; owner-occupied CRE added $1,063M in H1; multifamily added $463M; non-owner-occupied CRE ran off $424M in H1 after $773M in FY2025; auto loans are shrinking on lower indirect dealer volumes.

The repositioning is strategically coherent — relationship C&I and owner-occupied CRE are structurally better credits than transactional investor CRE. Growth is attributed to relationship small and middle-market clients in New York, Florida and Chicago, plus specialty healthcare and fund finance, and the new commercial leadership (Gino Martocci from M&T, Patrick Smith from Santander) is a plausible source of share gain while competitors tighten.

The unresolved question is whether a bank growing C&I at roughly twice the industry rate while peers tighten is winning share or absorbing credit others declined. That will not be observable for several quarters. What is observable is that the fastest loan growth quarter of the cycle coincided with rising delinquencies, rising non-accruals and falling reserve coverage.

Consensus reflects the operating momentum but not much upside surprise: FY2026 EPS of $1.28 and FY2027 of $1.53 (+19.5%), with revisions essentially flat over 30 and 90 days (−0.4% and −0.8% for the current year). The quarterly cadence required to hit $1.28 is $0.33 then $0.36 against $0.29 and $0.30 delivered — sequential acceleration of three cents twice, requiring both NIM expansion toward 3.30% and expense moderation. Professional and legal fees are the obstacle: $54.7M in H1'26 versus $35.6M in H1'25 (+53.6%), with Q2 at $29.5M the highest quarter yet. Management told the Q2 call these should decline as transformation projects wind down; that decline is not yet visible. Amortization of tax-credit investments rose to $32.2M from $18.5M and restructuring/severance to $8.2M from $0.8M.

The offset is genuine: adjusted revenue +13.28% against adjusted expenses +6.94% produced roughly 6.3 points of positive operating leverage. If that holds while professional fees roll off, the FY27 consensus is reachable. If either leg fails, it is not.

| P/TBVPS adjusted for HTM mark (~$9.60) | ~1.51x |

Peer medians in the group are 13.0x P/E and 2.8% yield (CBSH 13.6x, PB 13.0x, WAL 9.6x, FNB 11.3x, GBCI 22.8x). VLY's 12.3x trailing is a 5–6% discount to the median. That discount is not obviously unjustified: Valley carries 57.8% CRE concentration, CET1 of 10.71% at the lower end of the peer range, a 28% uninsured deposit share, and a 12% adjusted ROATCE against franchises like Commerce Bancshares that earn materially higher returns on tangible equity with more granular deposits.

The Gordon-style cross-check is the cleanest read. At 1.43x tangible book with 12.05% adjusted ROATCE and 5% assumed growth, the implied cost of equity is (12.05 − 5)/1.43 + 5 ≈ 9.9%. That is a fair-to-full valuation for a bank with this credit and funding profile, not a distressed one. Adjusting tangible book for the unrecognized HTM loss pushes the multiple to ~1.51x and the implied cost of equity below 9.7%.

The 9.5x on FY2027 looks cheap and will screen as such. It requires three things to happen simultaneously: NIM reaching and holding 3.30%+, provisioning staying near the low end of guidance, and professional fees reverting toward the FY2025 level. Each is plausible; all three together is the bull case, not the base case. My base assumption is FY2026 EPS of $1.25 (a modest haircut for the expense trajectory that produced the Q2 one-cent miss) and FY2027 of $1.42 (haircut for the funding-cost inflection and provision normalization toward 0.22–0.24%).

That produces a base value of $16.25, triangulated three ways: 11.5x on $1.42; 1.10x on BVPS of roughly $14.6 after four quarters of retained earnings; and 1.45x on TBVPS of roughly $11.1. All three converge in the $16.1–16.4 range, +12.2% from spot.

Analyst targets run $15.00 low, $16.73 mean, $17.00 median, $18.00 high, with 12 of 15 ratings at buy or strong buy and no sells. The low target sitting above spot is notable but should not be read as independent confirmation: estimate revisions are flat-to-slightly-negative, so the sell-side is not chasing rising numbers here — the targets and my base case reflect the same set of assumptions about NIM and credit.

Valuation score of 5.3: modestly attractive on forward numbers, fully valued on tangible book and implied cost of equity, with the forward multiple's cheapness contingent on three simultaneous operational outcomes. Compared with the prior review at $14.61, the raised guidance improves the case marginally at an essentially unchanged price.

## Competitive Landscape

Valley's durable advantages are deposit density in northern New Jersey, a geographically diversified NJ/NY/FL footprint uncommon at this size, an "Outstanding" CRA standing, and niche verticals (cannabis, HOA, venture, fund finance) that larger banks serve more slowly. The executive rebuild has recruited from meaningfully larger institutions — M&T, Santander, Huntington, Investors Bancorp — consistent with an intentional upgrade to operate a $66B balance sheet heading toward $100B.

The structural weakness is funding. A 23% non-interest-bearing share and 24% time-deposit share in the New York metro market, competing against JPMorgan, Bank of America and Citi for the same commercial relationships, does not confer a funding-cost advantage. The cumulative down-cycle deposit beta of 51% from June 2024 to June 2026 means Valley retained only half the Fed's cuts — respectable, but it also means the reverse is true if rates rise, and the $12.9B repricing CD book is exposed to any upward move in funding costs while the Fed is on hold. The Q2 rate-sensitivity table shows Valley has become more rate-neutral: +300bp now yields +3.50% of NII versus +4.96% at year-end, and −100bp costs −1.11% versus −1.46%. Less downside, but also less to gain from asset repricing.

The proxy's own pay-versus-performance table shows cumulative indexed TSR of 149 for VLY against 153 for its selected peer group over 2021–2025. Valley has underperformed the peer set it chose, with FY2025 (+35% versus peers +7%) recovering most of a severe FY2024 gap. The competitive trajectory is improving in mix — the shift from transactional investor CRE to relationship C&I is the right direction — but aggregate CRE exposure grew $1.1B in H1 and the uninsured deposit share is flat quarter-over-quarter and higher than eighteen months ago. Mix improvement is not the same as concentration reduction.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit migration in a fast-growing commercial book.** Accruing past-due loans jumped $52.3M in Q2 to 0.34% of loans, driven by CRE in the 30–59 day bucket ($106.0M versus $69.5M at March 31). Three collateral-dependent CRE loans totaling $49.6M migrated to non-accrual with no allocated reserve; management asserts they are well-secured. C&I substandard rose 17.7% to $439.4M. Collateral-dependent loans grew from $416.2M to $447.6M and specific reserves from $82.0M to $90.5M. Against this, CRE criticized/classified fell from 11.10% to 10.36% of CRE and net charge-offs at 0.16–0.17% remain inside guidance. The tension worth noting: Valley placed more weight on the CECL downside scenario at June 30 than at year-end, yet the ACL ratio still fell — the reserve decline is quantitative-model-driven at a time when delinquencies are rising.

**Loan modifications.** The Q2 flow was $144.1M (0.27% of loans) versus $15.3M (0.03%) a year earlier, a 9.4x increase driven by $108.6M of CRE classified as "other than insignificant payment delay." The stock of loans modified within the prior twelve months, however, was lower: $278.9M versus $327.1M. Both are filing facts establishing different things — modification activity accelerated sharply in H1, but the rolling balance did not deteriorate because heavy late-2024/early-2025 activity rolled off. Payment defaults on previously modified loans were $5.5M (one CRE credit) versus none a year earlier.

**Concentrated exposures.** $559M of multifamily on properties more than 50% rent-regulated (mainly Manhattan, HSTPA-exposed), down from $601M. Roughly $3.0B of office CRE, of which only ~$196.4M is Manhattan, at 63% weighted-average LTV and 1.91x DSCR — better secured than the headline CRE percentage implies. Total CRE runs 59% weighted-average LTV and 1.67x DSCR. Legacy taxi medallion loans of $42.5M remain collateral-dependent non-accrual.

**NDFI/fund finance.** The FY2025 10-K disclosed roughly $1.4B of C&I lending to non-depository financial institutions, primarily short-term capital-call lines, all performing. Q2 C&I growth was partly attributed to fund finance, but the $1.4B figure was not updated. This is a fast-growing category with limited loss history that regulators and the market are increasingly focused on; industry context reported by Forbes (May 28, 2026) put bank lending to non-bank financial intermediaries at $1.47 trillion and growing faster than other loan categories. No Valley-specific credit problems have been disclosed.

**Cannabis BSA/AML.** Deposit services since 2020 and certain secured/real-estate lending since 2H2021, with marijuana still federally illegal. The December 2025 executive order directing DOJ to consider Schedule III rescheduling would not federally legalize it or remove the BSA/AML burden. Valley does not disclose the size of this book.

**Regulatory threshold escalation.** OCC Heightened Standards already apply above $50B, and the Bank made its first FDIC resolution-planning submission in 2025. At $66.3B growing ~10% annually, Valley reaches the $100B Category IV enhanced-prudential threshold in roughly four to five years, with materially higher compliance costs and likely capital and liquidity requirements. No company timeline or cost estimate has been published.

**Governance.** Ira Robbins holds Chairman, President and CEO simultaneously with no separately disclosed independent chair, at a bank executing a transformation and approaching enhanced supervisory thresholds. Twelve Item 5.02 8-Ks were filed between August 2024 and January 2026; the January 28, 2026 filing could not be retrieved, leaving one unresolved disclosure item.

**Macro.** Valley's own 10-Q notes GDP decelerating to +1.5% annualized and inflation re-accelerating to 4.2%, with the Fed assumed unchanged until Q2 2028 in its CECL baseline. Higher-for-longer with re-accelerating inflation is close to the least favorable configuration for a bank repricing $12.9B of time deposits over eighteen months, and it simultaneously pressures CRE valuations, DSCRs and residential/consumer origination volumes.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 39.3% against 27.7% one-year realized is rich (an 11.6-point spread), with the term structure in backwardation (39.3% at one month versus 30.9% at six months) and a ±6.6% implied move on the August 21 expiry. Two caveats before reading this as name-specific stress. First, the chain is thin — 55 contracts across four expirations, with a put/call volume ratio of 0.02 that is effectively untradeable data. Second, there is no scheduled Valley catalyst inside the August 21 window; the next earnings report is late October. The most likely explanation is macro event risk being priced through a low-liquidity single-name chain, and the September tenor is the one showing put-heavy volume (P/C 1.33). Put/call open interest fell from 0.99 to 0.74 since late June, a mild reduction in hedging demand rather than an accumulation of protection. The one-month skew of −1.4% is characterized as balanced.

Short interest of 10.5% of float with 6.3 days to cover is the more substantive positioning signal: institutions carry a meaningful bearish position consistent with the CRE-concentration and funding-cost concerns above. That level cuts both ways — it caps how far consensus can deteriorate from here, and a clean Q3 showing flat-to-down deposit costs would create squeeze mechanics.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $18.50 (+27.7%)

The CFO's stated view is correct: deposits lag loan originations by three to six months, and Q2's funding-cost uptick is a timing artifact of a quarter in which loans grew 12.9% annualized. Supporting evidence exists — core deposits averaged $46.0B in H1, or 76.2% of average earning assets versus 73.1% for FY2025, and non-interest-bearing balances grew $298.6M on commercial relationship inflows. Maturing brokered CDs are replaced with cheaper core funding through H2, the cost of interest-bearing liabilities resumes falling, and NIM reaches 3.30% by year-end. Professional and legal fees decline from the $29.5M Q2 peak as transformation projects complete, preserving the 6.3 points of positive operating leverage. Credit holds: the Q2 delinquency spike proves to be three well-secured CRE credits, charge-offs stay at 0.15–0.20%, and the new commercial team's C&I growth seasons without incident. FY2027 EPS reaches $1.55–1.60 and the multiple re-rates to 12–12.5x as investors credit a bank that has genuinely transitioned from investor CRE to relationship commercial banking. The 10.5% short position unwinds into the improvement. Target of $18.50 sits modestly above the $18.00 high analyst target.

### Bear Case — $10.75 (−25.8%)

The funding-cost inflection is a trend, not a lag. With the Fed on hold, $11.96B of time deposits repricing within eighteen months, and a 51% down-cycle deposit beta indicating limited pricing power, the cost of interest-bearing liabilities keeps rising. NIM stalls at 3.15–3.20% rather than reaching 3.30%, and the asset-repricing mechanism proves too slow to offset. Simultaneously, credit normalizes: provisioning returns toward 0.30% of loans, consuming roughly $66M pre-tax (~$0.09/share) versus the H1 run-rate, and a portion of the C&I book grown at twice the industry rate while peers tightened migrates to criticized status. Professional fees do not decline, and the ~$22.1M annual incremental cost of the subordinated-note refinancing (~$0.030/share, on stated coupons) persists. FY2027 EPS comes in near $1.25 rather than $1.53, and the multiple compresses to 8.5x — approximately 1.0x tangible book adjusted for the unrecognized $389.6M HTM loss. CET1 continues drifting below the 10.50% guidance floor as RWA growth outpaces retained earnings, forcing a buyback pause and re-raising the CRE-ratio dividend-consultation constraint at 317%.

### Probability-weighted view

Assigning 55% to base ($16.25), 20% to bull ($18.50) and 25% to bear ($10.75) gives an expected value of $15.33, or +5.8% price appreciation plus a 3.0% dividend — roughly +8.8% total return over twelve months. That sits at or slightly below a fair ~10% cost of equity for a bank with 57.8% CRE concentration and an unresolved funding question. The base case offers +12.2%, but the distribution is left-skewed by the credit and funding tails, and the current price provides no margin of safety against them.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** This is the fifth consecutive HOLD, and the reasoning has changed rather than repeated. The prior reviews held on valuation grounds as the stock re-rated from the $9s. This review holds because the Q2 filing introduced a genuine fork in the thesis: the mechanism of margin expansion reversed, capital declined, and credit leading indicators softened, at the same time as guidance was raised. The classification is "uncertain" deliberately — the central question (whether the Q2 funding-cost uptick is a three-to-six-month origination lag or the start of a trend) is answerable with two quarters of data, and it is not answerable now.

**Timeframe:** 12 months, with the decisive checkpoint at the Q3 2026 report in late October and a secondary rate input at the September FOMC.

- **Base case: $16.25 (+12.2%).** 11.5x FY2027 EPS of $1.42 (consensus $1.53 haircut for funding-cost drag and provision normalization toward 0.22–0.24% of loans), cross-checked at 1.10x forward BVPS of ~$14.6 and 1.45x forward TBVPS of ~$11.1.
- **Bull case: $18.50 (+27.7%).** 12.0x FY2027 EPS of $1.55 on NIM at 3.30%, professional fees normalizing, and credit holding.
- **Bear case: $10.75 (−25.8%).** 8.5x FY2027 EPS of $1.25, approximately 1.0x tangible book adjusted for the HTM mark.
- **Dividend:** $0.44 annualized, 3.04% yield, 34% payout on FY26 consensus, unchanged since 2024.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE):**
1. Q3'26 shows the cost of interest-bearing liabilities flat or lower than 3.10% quarter-over-quarter, with core deposit growth funding loan growth and no incremental short-term FHLB reliance beyond the $375M outstanding. This is the single most important datum.
2. ACL/loans stabilizes at or above 1.16% with accruing past-due loans back below 0.28% and ALL/non-accrual coverage above 135%.
3. Professional and legal fees decline sequentially below $25M in a quarter, confirming management's stated wind-down.
4. Price below approximately $12.50 (≈1.23x current TBVPS, ~8.2x FY27 consensus) with the operating trajectory intact — this would restore the margin of safety the current price lacks.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. CET1 falls below 10.50% (the bottom of the guided range) or declines a further 20bp+ with RWA growth again outpacing capital accretion.
2. Accruing past-due plus non-accrual loans exceed 1.50% of loans (from 1.23%), or net charge-offs exceed 0.25% annualized (above the 0.15–0.20% guidance).
3. NIM contracts in any quarter with the cost of interest-bearing liabilities rising 10bp+ sequentially — this would invalidate the asset-repricing bridge to 3.30%.
4. Quarterly provisioning exceeds $60M, which would consume the majority of the FY2027 consensus earnings growth.

**Reassessment:** Q3 2026 earnings, late October 2026.

## Conclusion

Valley National has executed a real operating recovery — 3.20% NIM, 52.11% adjusted Q2 efficiency, 6.3 points of positive operating leverage, adjusted ROATCE approaching 12%, and guidance raised on net interest income, loans, deposits and capital. Management has rebuilt the executive team with credible hires from larger institutions and is deliberately rotating the loan book from transactional investor CRE toward relationship C&I and owner-occupied CRE. Nothing in the Q2 filing invalidates that progress.

What the filing does establish is that the engine of the 2025–H1 2026 earnings recovery has changed. Funding costs inflected upward for the first time in the cycle, funded by $1.5B of new time deposits and a $375M FHLB draw; CET1 fell 28bp to 10.71% as risk-weighted assets grew 5.2% against 3.4% asset growth; the reported CRE concentration improvement came largely from a $200M Tier 2 subordinated-debt issuance and excludes the owner-occupied CRE where all of H1's CRE growth occurred; and roughly 40% of the H1 EPS gain came from provision relief taken while delinquencies rose and reserve coverage of non-accruals fell to 127.7%.
