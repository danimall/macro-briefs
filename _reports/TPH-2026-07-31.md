**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Tri Pointe Homes is being acquired by Sumitomo Forestry Co., Ltd. for **$47.00 per share in cash** under a merger agreement signed February 13, 2026. Two of the three principal closing conditions have been satisfied: the HSR waiting period expired at 11:59 p.m. ET on April 16, 2026, and stockholders approved the transaction the same day, with 99.99% of votes cast in favor on approximately 78% turnout of 85,135,564 shares. What remains are the absence of a restraining order or injunction, the absence of a company material adverse effect arising after signing, accuracy of representations, and covenant compliance. The outside date is August 13, 2026, extendable three months only for HSR approval (already obtained) or to resolve an injunction relating to other specified governmental consents.

The last price we can verify is **$46.95** (May 15, 2026), against $47.00 of consideration. That is a gross spread of $0.05, or **0.107%**. Three pieces of arithmetic follow and they drive the recommendation. First, over the roughly 90 days from that observation to the outside date, 0.107% annualizes to about 0.43%, well below the ~4.2% available on short Treasuries, so the position produces a *negative* return versus cash even assuming certain completion. Second, against a break price near $32, the breakeven break probability is 0.107 / (0.107 + 32) ≈ **0.33%**; a 1% break probability produces an expected value of −0.21%, and 3% produces −0.85%. Third, the break case has deteriorated materially since signing: Q1 2026 EPS came in at **$0.15**, versus a $0.17 estimate. HousingWire site data at the time of retrieval showed the 30-year fixed mortgage rate at 6.99%, and a late-July 2026 HousingWire headline referenced rising mortgage rates derailing the early-2026 housing rebound, with more than 40% of national listings seeing price cuts.

This is the fourth consecutive AVOID on TPH, and the reason has shifted rather than repeated. Earlier reports could point to a spread that at least paid something; at $46.95 the spread has been arbitraged away while the standalone floor beneath it has fallen. The recommendation is not a prediction that the merger fails. The structure (no financing condition, specific performance for both sides, 99.99% approval, a market-standard 2.06% company termination fee) is buyer-committed and closure is the overwhelmingly likely outcome. New capital deployed at the observed price is paid nothing for accepting a 32-45% tail. Shareholders already holding receive $47.00 in cash at the effective time and need do nothing.

## Company Overview

Founded in April 2009 as a Southern California fee builder, Tri Pointe operates as a regionally focused national homebuilder across **17 markets in twelve states and the District of Columbia**, with 156 active selling communities and 32,219 lots owned or controlled (15,954 owned, 16,265 controlled) at December 31, 2025.

Segment structure for FY2025:

| Segment | Deliveries | Home sales revenue | ASP |
|---|---|---|---|
| West (AZ, CA, NV, WA) | 2,506 | $1,887M | $753K |
| Central (CO, TX, UT) | 1,673 | $924M | $552K |
| East (DC, FL, MD, NC, SC, VA) | 768 | $553M | $720K |
| Tri Pointe Solutions (financial services) | — | $71.8M rev / $17.2M pre-tax | — |

Company-wide ASP of $680,000 places TPH in the move-up and luxury tiers, roughly double entry-level-focused peers, with base prices spanning $190,000 to $3.2 million. That mix is the single most important fact about the business in the current rate environment: TPH's buyer is disproportionately exposed to the $750,000 acquisition-indebtedness cap on mortgage-interest deductibility and to SALT limits, whose current relief is scheduled to revert after 2029.

The company's own competitive self-description in Item 1 is unusually candid: "relatively low barriers to entry," several primary competitors "significantly larger" with "lower cost of capital," and a possible competitive disadvantage against more geographically diversified peers. Three expansion divisions were in flight (Utah, announced September 2023; Orlando and the Coastal Carolinas, announced early 2024), and as of December 31, 2025 TPH had **not yet commenced home sales in either Orlando or the Coastal Carolinas**, roughly two years after announcement.

## Financial Analysis

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2024 | Change |

| Home sales revenue | $3,363.8M | $4,386.4M | −23.3% |
| Deliveries | 4,947 | 6,460 | −23.4% |
| ASP | ~$680K | ~$679K | flat |
| Homebuilding gross margin | 21.0% | 23.3% | −230bp |
| Adjusted HB gross margin | 25.2% | 26.8% | −160bp |
| SG&A / home sales revenue | 12.6% | 10.8% | +180bp |
| Operating income | $301.8M | $552.6M | −45.4% |
| Net income to common | $241.1M | $458.0M | −47.4% |
| Diluted EPS | $2.72 | $4.83 | −43.7% |
| Operating cash flow | $161.5M | $696.1M | −76.8% |
| Cash | $982.8M | $970.0M | +1.3% |
| Stockholders' equity | $3,315.8M | $3,335.7M | −0.6% |

The revenue decline was entirely volume; pricing held. Margin compression decomposes cleanly: roughly 90bp of the 230bp GAAP gross-margin decline came from **$31.1M of inventory impairments** ($0 in FY2024), with impairments plus lot-option abandonments totaling **$36.4M, or 1.1% of home sales revenue, versus $4.2M (0.1%)** the prior year. The remaining decline reflects higher incentives. The cleaner read is the adjusted margin: 160bp of pure price/cost erosion.

SG&A deleveraging drove most of the operating-margin fall from 12.3% to 8.7%. Dollars declined 10.3% while revenue fell 23.3%; the company shed roughly 4.8 cents of SG&A per dollar of lost revenue against a 10.8% starting ratio, so fixed cost absorbed about 6 cents of every lost revenue dollar. Funding three pre-revenue divisions is visible in G&A rising from 5.8% to 6.8% of revenue.

**Cash flow is the most instructive line.** OCF fell $534.6M, attributable to lower net income (−$217M), a $257.5M increase in cash used for real-estate inventories, and a $148.7M decrease in cash from receivables. OCF/net income moved from 1.52x to 0.67x. This is a working-capital cycle rather than an accrual-quality problem: FY2024's $696M was a liquidation number generated by a shrinking pipeline; FY2025's $161M is an investment number generated by spec building (completed-and-unsold homes rose 47% to 681) and startup-division funding. Free cash flow after $32.9M capex was $128.5M, a 3.7% margin.

Against that $128.5M, TPH repurchased **8,550,822 shares at an average $32.42 for $277.2M**. Cash was essentially flat because the term facility was upsized $200M on September 18, 2025. Net homebuilding debt-to-net capital moved from **−1.6% (net cash) to +3.5%**, and homebuilding debt-to-capital from 21.6% to 25.0%. The buyback was, on the arithmetic, part balance-sheet funded. The term draw and the buyback are temporally adjacent; I document the coincidence without asserting a causal link, since management framed the facility as general liquidity.

The balance sheet remains genuinely strong in absolute terms: $982.8M cash, $1.8B total liquidity including $798.1M of revolver availability, zero revolver drawn at both year-ends, total debt $1,194.6M, and wide covenant headroom (tangible net worth $3,148.7M against $2,343.4M required; leverage test 5.7% against ≤60%; interest coverage 6.4x against ≥1.5x). The Altman Z of 3.03 sits barely above the 2.99 threshold and is carried by balance-sheet terms rather than earnings power; the EBIT/assets component of 0.061 is the weak link and falls further on another down year. The working-capital component reads 0.00, implausible for a builder whose assets are overwhelmingly inventory, so the 3.03 should be treated as directional rather than precise.

**Q1 2026, which closes the largest gap in the prior analysis:** reported EPS of $0.15 missed the $0.17 estimate by 10.7%. Backing out reported Q2–Q4 2025 quarters ($0.68 + $0.64 + $0.70 = $2.02) from FY2025 diluted EPS of $2.72 implies a prior-year Q1 near $0.70; on that basis the year-over-year decline would be on the order of 79%, though this stitches a vendor-provided adjusted-EPS series against GAAP full-year EPS and the prior-year Q1 GAAP figure does not appear directly in the sources, so the precise magnitude is [unverified — actual: N/A]. The four-quarter record is 1 beat, 3 misses, with the two most recent quarters missing by 11.4% and 10.7%, consistent with an order book that fell faster than the P&L.

Financial health scores 5.5: solvency and liquidity are not in question, but cash conversion, earnings direction, and the return trajectory (ROE 7.3%, ROA 4.8%) all point the wrong way, and the change-of-control provisions mean roughly $450M of term debt and $91M of warehouse borrowings require lender consent or repayment at closing.

## Growth Analysis

The forward indicators are worse than the reported results:

- Net new orders **4,292 versus 5,657, −24%**, driven by a 23% fall in monthly absorption (2.4 from 3.1) on flat average community count (150.5 vs 150.4).
- West absorption **−30%** across all markets; East **−28%** *despite* a 23% increase in community count; Central **−13%**.
- Backlog at 12/31/2025: **862 units / $670.1M versus 1,517 / $1,164.6M — −43% units, −42% dollars.**
- Cancellations rose to 12% from 10%; completed-and-unsold homes rose to 681 from 464.
- Total lots owned or controlled fell 12%, with controlled lots down 18% and West controlled lots down 22%.

FY2025 deliveries fell 23% because backlog entering 2025 was down 35%. Entering 2026 with backlog down 43% on flat community count points to another materially down delivery year absent a sharp absorption recovery, and Q1 2026 EPS of $0.15 is consistent with that arithmetic. One legitimate offset: a spec-heavy model converts orders to closings within the same quarter, which structurally reduces backlog's predictive value, and the 47% spec build supports that reading. The Q1 print suggests the spec inventory did not offset much.

Management's macro framing — that "demand softness is being driven primarily by consumer sentiment rather than underlying employment or household formation fundamentals" and that the market is "positioned for improvement as sentiment normalizes" — is not corroborated by external evidence through mid-2026. HousingWire site data showed for-sale inventory above 865,000 units and rising, and a late-July 2026 headline attributing a derailed early-2026 rebound to rising mortgage rates. The causal chain is straightforward: higher long-end yields raise mortgage rates, which raise monthly payments at TPH's $680K ASP more than at entry-level price points, which suppresses absorption and forces incentives that compress gross margin, visible already in the 160bp adjusted-margin decline and the shift in captive-mortgage mix toward government-backed product (18% from 8%), ARMs (5% from 0%), lower average FICO (755 from 761) and higher LTV (79% from 77%).

Growth prospects score 2.5. For an equity converting to cash, the growth outlook matters only as the floor beneath the break case, and that floor is falling.

## Valuation Assessment

The valuation question has one dominant term: the spread.

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Merger consideration | $47.00 cash per share |
| Last observed price (May 15, 2026) | $46.95 |
| Gross spread | $0.05 / **0.107%** |
| Days to Aug 13, 2026 outside date (from observation) | ~90 |
| Annualized gross return | ~0.43% |
| Short-Treasury alternative | ~4.2% |
| One-month cash carry | ~0.35% |
| Breakeven break probability (vs $32 break price) | ~0.33% |

On a risk-adjusted basis the return is negative before any break probability is applied, because 0.107% of gross spread does not cover even one month of cash carry. Layering a modest 2-3% break probability against a 32-45% loss produces an expected value of roughly −0.6% to −1.3%.

The deal price itself is full: **$47.00 ÷ $38.94 book value per share ≈ 1.21x book** (equity of $3,315.8M over 85.1M shares), **17.3x** FY2025 diluted EPS of $2.72, and **9.7x** FY2024's $4.83. Paying 1.21x book for a business earning a 7.3% ROE means Sumitomo Forestry is underwriting mid-cycle rather than trough returns, coherent for a strategic Japanese acquirer facing a shrinking domestic housing market, and a reasonable outcome for a board whose stock had trailed its own peer index in four of five disclosed years. The headline "$4.5 billion" transaction value cannot be reconciled from filings: equity consideration computes to ~$4.00B (85,135,564 × $47.00) and enterprise value to ~$4.21B ($4.00B + $1.19B debt − $0.98B cash); the bridge to $4.5B is undisclosed and I treat it as an externally reported figure.

The standalone valuation anchor, which is what matters if the transaction fails, is now lower than the ~$32.4 repurchase VWAP proxy for the pre-announcement price. If Q1's $0.15 annualizes with normal back-half weighting to roughly $1.00-1.50 of FY2026 EPS, TPH would be a builder earning a 3-4% ROE. Peers in that condition trade at 0.65-0.80x book, which on book value near $39-40 implies **$26-32**. The 10-K itself warns the break price "could be… below the trading price of our common stock prior to the announcement," because the information disclosed after signing (43% backlog decline, 24% order decline, $31.1M impairment) was worse than the pre-announcement information set.

Valuation scores 1.5. There is no asymmetric upside; the upside is contractually capped at five cents, and the payoff is near-certain but negative after carry.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive trajectory is deteriorating, which matters for the break case:

- **West** (56% of home sales revenue): absorption −30% across all markets; community count −4%, which management attributes to "moderated growth expectations" in "more mature markets… operating closer to optimal scale." That is a harvest posture.
- **Central**: elevated inventory in both resale and new construction; TPH nonetheless opened 17 communities versus 13 the prior year into that oversupply.
- **East**: the sharpest divergence, with community count +23% while absorption fell 28% and backlog ASP fell 14%, the only segment with declining backlog ASP. Adding communities into falling absorption and falling price is the standard antecedent to impairment, and impairments duly arrived.

Relative TSR from the pay-versus-performance disclosure: 2021 161.68 vs peer 152.12; 2022 107.77 vs 118.79; 2023 205.22 vs 213.82; 2024 210.20 vs 213.34; 2025 182.44 vs 214.56. TPH exceeded its peer index in one of five years. Combined with two of three announced expansion markets still pre-revenue two years after announcement and a shrinking controlled-lot pipeline that caps 2027-2028 community growth, the standalone competitive case is weak, and the board's decision to accept cash at 1.21x book reads as value-maximizing rather than distressed.

Competitive position scores 3.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Deal-completion risk (the dominant risk).** Remaining conditions are no injunction, no post-signing company MAE, representation accuracy and covenant compliance. Delaware sets an extremely high bar for MAE termination and such definitions customarily carve out general industry and economic conditions, so a cyclical earnings decline is unlikely on its own to support termination, but I have not read Exhibit 2.1 and cannot confirm the carve-outs, whether a reverse termination fee exists, or the regulatory-efforts standard. The 10-K's description is expressly qualified by the agreement itself.

**The outside date is the sharpest dated risk.** Guidance was for a Q2 2026 close. The three-month extension is available only for HSR approval (already obtained) or to resolve an injunction relating to other specified governmental consents. If no injunction exists, the extension trigger may be unavailable, which makes August 13 a hard date after which either party could terminate. The last date on which TPH is verifiably an independent public filer is May 14, 2026; no closing 8-K, Form 25, or Form 15 was located. Continued filing after the vote is consistent with a pending close but is not proof, since the 2027 and 2028 Notes indentures may compel reporting. A reader must first establish whether the merger has already closed; if so, the position is cash and this analysis is moot.

**Mechanical execution items.** The merger triggers change-of-control events of default under the Credit Facility and each Mortgage Repurchase Agreement; the company intends to obtain consent or terminate and repay. Whether consent was obtained is unknown. Merger litigation is flagged as a generic likelihood; whether any suit or injunction application was filed is unknown.

**Interim handcuffs.** Dividends and repurchases require Sumitomo Forestry's consent, and only $22.8M of buyback authorization remained at year-end. There is no self-help path to value other than consummation.

**Standalone risks transferred at close but owned in a break.** Legacy WRECO liabilities from the 2014 Weyerhaeuser transaction are largely uninsured — WRECO self-insured much of its general-liability exposure outside California and Nevada, and the filing states it is "likely that most of these claims will not be covered by insurance." California imposes a ten-year strict-liability tail on many construction-defect claims. The January 2025 Los Angeles wildfires are referenced four times, with homeowners' insurance unavailability functioning as a direct sales-conversion constraint because lenders require coverage. Arizona water-entitlement constraints, tariffs, immigration enforcement affecting skilled labor, and oil-price exposure in Colorado and Houston are all named. Item 3 was not read, so pending case counts and warranty-reserve adequacy are unverified.

**Acquirer-side channel (hypothesis, not a claim).** A weaker yen or a higher acquirer cost of capital would raise the yen cost of a dollar acquisition. The structure — no financing condition, specific performance, an approved vote — leaves little practical room to act on any change of heart, but this is the channel through which a delay or renegotiation attempt would arrive if one occurs. Sumitomo Forestry's own financial capacity and cost of capital are not documented in the sources reviewed.

### Options Market Signal

The available options-derived data is limited to price observations: $46.90 on April 26, 2026 and $46.95 on May 15, 2026, a 0.1% move across three weeks. No implied-volatility, skew, or open-interest data accompanied the snapshots, so no directional inference is available. The pinning of the price within 0.1% of the consideration for weeks after the vote is itself informative: the market was pricing essentially no break risk and no expectation of a topping bid, consistent with the 99.99% approval and the absence of a competing proposal during the roughly two months between signing and the vote.

Risk factors score 2.5 — not because the base case is bad, but because the residual risks are unpriced at a 0.107% spread and the loss given break has widened.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($47.00, +0.1%).** The merger closes on or before August 13, 2026, no injunction is entered, no MAE is asserted, lender consents are obtained or facilities repaid, and each share converts into $47.00 cash. This is the overwhelmingly likely outcome, supported by cleared antitrust, a 99.99% approval vote, no financing condition, mutual specific-performance rights, a non-deterrent 2.06% company break fee, a strategic acquirer with a documented incentive to build U.S. scale, and dedicated event-driven capital accumulating the name (First Trust initiated 1,599,172 shares in Q1 2026). The realized return from $46.95 is five cents, less than the cash carry over any plausible remaining period.

**Bear case ($26-32, −32% to −45%).** An injunction, a successful MAE assertion, or an outside-date lapse without an available extension terminates the agreement. TPH then re-prices as a standalone builder with backlog down 43%, orders down 24%, Q1 2026 EPS of $0.15 (below the $0.17 estimate and materially below the prior-year quarter), rising impairments, a 681-unit completed-and-unsold position, three-quarters of a year of unabsorbed startup-division G&A, elevated mortgage rates, and rising resale inventory. At 0.65-0.80x book value near $39-40, that is $26-32. The company's own filing warns the break price could fall below the pre-announcement level, and the fundamentals disclosed since signing support that warning.

**Base case.** Completion at $47.00, producing a return below Treasury bills, with a low-single-digit probability of the 32-45% loss. The probability-weighted expected value from $46.95 is negative.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** A short thesis requires an identifiable event that breaks the deal. The available candidates are the August 13, 2026 outside date without an available extension, a merger-litigation injunction, or an MAE assertion premised on post-signing deterioration (Q1 EPS below estimate, backlog −43%). None of these is a positively identified event — HSR is cleared, the vote is done, no injunction application is documented, and Delaware's MAE bar with customary industry carve-outs makes termination on cyclical deterioration very unlikely.

**Timing window.** Roughly two weeks to the outside date, or already elapsed if the transaction closed. A short position entered near $46.95 faces automatic conversion at $47.00 at the effective time, requiring the short to deliver at the merger price.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** No short-interest data was available. Merger targets post-vote typically show low short interest and heavy event-driven long ownership, which makes borrow scarce and expensive rather than crowded-short.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options positioning showed a price pinned within 0.1% of the consideration with no skew or IV data available; no confirmation of a bearish thesis. Borrow cost on an approved cash-deal target is customarily elevated, which is the dominant carrying cost in a trade whose maximum gain against a closing deal is bounded at five cents.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Reward if the deal breaks: roughly 32-45%. Risk if it closes: −0.107% plus borrow, plus the operational hazard of being short into an automatic cash conversion. The nominal ratio looks extreme, but the probability weighting inverts it: at a 3% break probability the expected gain is under 1.2% against near-certain borrow costs over an indeterminate window, and the trade cannot be sized because a squeeze on any deal-progress headline is bounded only by the recall risk on borrow.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** There is no dated, identified catalyst for a break, only residual conditions with low probability. The correct expression of this view is non-ownership, not a short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria.** I would re-engage on the arbitrage only if the spread compensates for both carry and break risk. Working backwards from $47.00: earning a 4.3% annualized risk-free equivalent over 90 days requires an entry at or below **$46.51**; adding a 3% modeled break probability against a $32 break price (about 1.0% of expected loss) requires **~$46.05**; a mid-teens annualized return net of that break provision requires roughly **$44-45** with a defined closing path. Any of those levels would only appear alongside a specific, adverse deal development, which would simultaneously raise the break probability, so the practical bar is a widening driven by noise (index deletion mechanics, arb-fund forced selling) rather than by deal news.

As a standalone homebuilder equity, TPH would become interesting near **0.7x book (~$27)** with two confirming data points: monthly absorption stabilizing above 2.5 per community and net new orders turning flat or better year over year. Neither is plausible while 30-year mortgage rates sit near 7% and for-sale inventory is expanding.

**Timeline for the headwinds.** The rate channel is the binding constraint. With mortgage rates at 6.99% per the retrieved site data and external reporting attributing a derailed 2026 rebound to rising rates, mortgage rates in the high-6% range are the working assumption for the near term. TPH's high-ASP, deduction-sensitive buyer is the worst-positioned tier for that environment.

**Monitoring points.** (1) A closing 8-K, Form 25, or Form 15 — confirms conversion to cash and ends the analysis. (2) Any 8-K disclosing merger litigation, an injunction application, or an MAE notice — the only realistic break vectors. (3) Passage of August 13, 2026 without closing and without a documented extension basis — the single highest-information negative event, since the extension trigger appears to require an injunction relating to other specified governmental consents and HSR is already satisfied. (4) Confirmation of lender consent, or termination and repayment, under the Credit Facility and the two Mortgage Repurchase Agreements.

## Conclusion

**AVOID at the last observed price of $46.95.** The scores continue to decline and the reason has changed with each step: the spread has compressed to five cents while the standalone floor beneath it has fallen. At 0.107% gross, the position returns less than a Treasury bill even at 100% completion probability, and the breakeven break probability of roughly 0.33% is lower than any honest estimate of residual condition risk. The loss given break has widened since signing — Q1 2026 EPS of $0.15 versus a $0.17 estimate and materially below the prior-year quarter, backlog down 43%, completed specs up 47%, impairments up ninefold, and mortgage rates near 7% — which puts a break case at $26-32, or 32-45% below the deal price.

None of this is a bearish view on completion. The structure is buyer-committed and the transaction should close, most likely before August 13, 2026. Shareholders of record receive $47.00 in cash at the effective time and should take it. The financial health score of 5.5 reflects a balance sheet that remains sound in absolute terms — $982.8M cash, $1.8B liquidity, 25% homebuilding debt-to-capital, 6.4x interest coverage — and I have not marked it lower, because the cash-flow deterioration is a working-capital cycle rather than a solvency problem. The valuation score of 1.5 and risk score of 2.5 carry the recommendation: contractually capped upside of five cents against an uncompensated double-digit tail is an unattractive position, whatever one thinks of the underlying company.

Two evidence gaps should be closed before acting on anything here: whether the merger has already closed (no closing filing was located; the last verified public-filer date is May 14, 2026), and the current market price, which materially changes the arithmetic if it has moved from $46.95. The merger agreement itself (Exhibit 2.1) was not read, so the MAE carve-outs, any reverse termination fee, and the regulatory-efforts standard remain unverified.
