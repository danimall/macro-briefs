**Research Date: July 31, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Tri Pointe Homes is no longer an investable public equity. On February 13, 2026 the company signed an Agreement and Plan of Merger with Sumitomo Forestry Co., Ltd. and Teton NewCo, Inc. under which each share converts into the right to receive **$47.00 in cash**, with no financing condition. Stockholders approved the transaction on April 16-17, 2026, the HSR waiting period was satisfied (confirmed in the April 29, 2026 Q1 release), an 8-K was filed May 14, 2026, and trade press reported completion that day. The supporting evidence in this data set is consistent: no price history is retrievable, no analyst coverage or price targets exist, ticker-to-CIK resolution fails for Form 4 retrieval, and the last observable quote of $46.95 on May 15, 2026 sits within 0.1% of the fixed consideration. Terminal value was contractually capped; the remaining spread was five cents.

The second finding matters more for anyone using TPH as a read on the sector: the fundamentals collapsed on the way into the close, and the deterioration was operating rather than accounting. FY2025 total revenue fell 22.8% to $3,470.7M, net income to common fell 47.4% to $241.1M, and diluted EPS fell 43.7% to $2.72 — with the decline entirely volume (deliveries 4,947 vs 6,460, -23%) against a flat $680,000 average selling price. Q1 2026 was worse: home sales revenue -29.7% to $506.5M, homebuilding gross margin down 510bp to 18.8%, SG&A up 390bp to 17.9% of home sales revenue, homebuilding income from operations of $4.2M on $506.5M of revenue (a 0.8% margin), and GAAP diluted EPS of $0.08 versus $0.70. Impairments and lot option abandonments in Q1 were $1.068M versus $1.073M a year earlier — essentially flat — so incentives, mix and fixed-cost absorption on lower volume account for substantially all of the margin destruction.

We maintain AVOID for a fifth consecutive report at an overall score of 3.2, unchanged from the prior review. The absence of further downward revision is deliberate: the terminal condition has been reached, and marking scores lower on a security whose value is fixed at $47.00 in cash would be false precision rather than new information. Where the analysis has moved is in the character of the deterioration — FY2025's margin decline was roughly 90bp impairment-driven, Q1 2026's was not, which makes the last observable quarter a cleaner and more concerning read on mid-cap builder economics at a 30-year mortgage rate near 7%.

## Company Overview

Tri Pointe was founded in April 2009 and grew into what the FY2025 10-K describes as a regionally-focused national homebuilder operating in 17 markets across twelve states and the District of Columbia, with a corporate home office in Irvine, California. Reportable segments were West (Arizona, California, Nevada, Washington), Central (Colorado, Texas, Utah), East (D.C., Florida, Maryland, North Carolina, South Carolina, Virginia), and Tri Pointe Solutions — the financial services unit comprising Tri Pointe Connect (mortgage), Tri Pointe Assurance (title/escrow as agent for First American Title Insurance Company), and Tri Pointe Advantage (P&C agency).

Scale at the last full fiscal year: 4,947 deliveries, $680,000 average sales price, 156 active selling communities, 32,219 lots owned or controlled (50/50 owned/controlled), 1,579 employees, 449 owned model homes. Base price points spanned roughly $190,000 to $3.2 million.

The defining structural characteristic is the price point. At ~$680,000 consolidated and $753,000 in the West (rising to $778,000 in Q1 2026), TPH was a move-up and premium builder with heavy California exposure, not an entry-level volume operation. The West produced $1,886.7M of FY2025 home sales revenue, 56.1% of the $3,363.8M total. That mix delivered high revenue per unit and entitlement expertise in constrained markets, and maximum sensitivity to affordability shock at the upper end of the market.

Three growth initiatives were in flight at the time of sale: Utah (announced September 2023), Orlando and the Coastal Carolinas (April 2024). As of December 31, 2025 the company had **not yet commenced home sales** in Orlando or the Coastal Carolinas and had not begun operations in Georgia. These divisions absorbed G&A with no offsetting revenue during the FY2025 downturn.

EPS fell 43.7% against a 47.4% net income decline because the diluted share count fell 6.6% — buybacks absorbed roughly four points of the per-share decline.

Three layers explain the FY2025 margin compression. First, impairments: management attributed approximately 90bp of the 230bp GAAP gross margin decline to $31.1M of inventory impairment charges. Total impairments plus lot option abandonments in cost of home sales were $36.4M versus $4.2M, an 8.7x increase, and the progression is non-linear — $11.5M in FY2023, zero in FY2024, $31.1M in FY2025. Adjusted homebuilding gross margin (adding back interest and impairments) fell 160bp to 25.2% from 26.8%, isolating the underlying incentive and absorption damage.

Second, operating leverage loss. SG&A rose to 12.6% of home sales revenue from 10.8% even as absolute SG&A fell $48.7M (-10.3%) to $423.9M. Sales and marketing went to 5.8% from 4.9%; G&A to 6.8% from 5.8%. A cost base that could only be cut 10.3% against a 23% revenue decline was roughly half fixed, and the three unopened startup divisions carried overhead against zero revenue. Severance and retention costs are an alternative partial explanation that the disclosure does not allow us to separate.

Third, interest. Interest incurred fell to $81.5M from $114.9M after the May 2024 redemption of the $450M 2024 Senior Notes, partly offset by the September 2025 $200M term loan draw. Interest amortized into cost of home sales was $105.4M (3.1% of revenue) versus $148.5M (3.4%) — amortization exceeding incurred interest, the mechanical consequence of shrinking inventory turns.

### Cash flow

Operating cash flow fell $534.6M to $161.5M from $696.1M, decomposed by management as net income down $217M, a $257.5M increase in cash used for real estate inventories, and a $148.7M decline in cash provided by receivables. Free cash flow was $128.5M against $32.9M of capex, a 3.7% FCF margin. Operating cash flow did not exceed net income, reversing FY2024.

This warrants precision. For a homebuilder, inventory investment ahead of deliveries is the normal working-capital cycle, and revenue recognition at close of escrow is among the least judgment-dependent models in any industry. The failed OCF>NI test does not indicate accrual quality problems. It indicates the company was investing into a declining market, and Q1 2026 shows it continued: real estate inventories rose 3.9% to $3,302.3M, homes under construction rose 33.3% to 1,855 units, completed unsold homes fell 31.1% to 469 from 681, and cash fell $134.9M to $847.9M. The mix shift is consistent with clearing aged specs using incentives — which is exactly what the gross margin says — and restarting production for H2 deliveries.

Covenant headroom at December 31, 2025 was very wide: Consolidated Tangible Net Worth $3,148.7M against a $2,343.4M requirement; leverage test 5.7% against a ≤60% covenant; interest coverage 6.4x against a ≥1.5x minimum. Total liquidity $1.8B. Maturities were termed out — 5.250% 2027 Notes ($300M), 5.700% 2028 Notes ($350M), Term Facility Tranche A to September 2027, revolver extended to April 30, 2030 and upsized to $850M with a $1.2B accordion under the April 2025 Fifth Modification.

The Altman Z-Score of 3.03 sits barely inside the conventional safe zone, carried by equity/assets of 0.666 and equity/liabilities of 1.996, with EBIT/assets of 0.061 as the weak component. Recomputed on Q1 2026 earnings, EBIT/assets would approach zero and the score would likely fall out of the safe zone. The Z-Score is a poor fit for builders whose current assets are dominated by real estate inventories — the working-capital component reading 0.000 suggests the model is not capturing TPH's economics — but the directional signal on earnings power is real.

The interpretation that matters: **TPH did not sell from distress.** It sold near net-debt-free, in full covenant compliance, with $1.8B of liquidity and no maturity wall. The pressure was on returns, growth, and cost of capital.

### Earnings delivery

The four-quarter surprise record is 1 beat, 3 misses: -1.0% (Jun-25), +21.9% (Sep-25), -11.4% (Dec-25), -10.7% (Mar-26). Note a basis conflict on the March quarter: the surprise table shows $0.15 actual against a $0.17 estimate; one aggregator reported non-GAAP EPS of $0.16 as "in line"; GAAP diluted EPS was $0.08 including $5.877M of merger transaction expense. Three figures on at least two bases. We do not characterize the quarter as a beat or miss on that evidence; the deterioration is visible without it — adjusted EBITDA fell 68.3% to $39.9M and the effective tax rate of ~1.2% ($81K provision on $6.891M pretax) is unexplained in the source and will not persist. What the pattern does establish is declining forward visibility over three of the last four quarters.

## Growth Analysis

FY2025 net new orders fell 24% to 4,292 from 5,657, driven by a 23% decline in monthly absorption to 2.4 from 3.1 on essentially flat community count (150.5 vs 150.4). Regionally: West orders -32% with absorption -30% (2.6 vs 3.7), weakness "experienced across all markets"; Central orders -14% with absorption -13% to 2.0, the weakest of the three segments, against "elevated inventory levels in both the resale and new home markets" in Texas; East orders -13% with absorption -28% (2.8 vs 3.9) despite a **23% increase in average selling communities**. The East grew store count 23% and still lost 13% of orders.

Year-end backlog fell to 862 units / $670.1M from 1,517 / $1,164.6M — -43% units, -42% dollars — with cancellations rising to 12% from 10%. Management partially offset this by pointing to 681 completed unsold homes versus 464 (+46.8%), which shortens delivery timelines but carries inventory risk.

Q1 2026 orders looked stabilized at 1,234 versus 1,238, and cancellations improved to 9% from 10%. The composition undercuts the headline: average selling communities rose 8.6% to 158.0 from 145.5 while monthly absorption fell to 2.6 from 2.8. Flat orders were purchased with more stores at lower productivity. Backlog was $989.9M / 1,360 units versus $1,307.8M / 1,715 units (-24.3% dollars, -20.7% units), with backlog ASP down 4.6% to $728,000. Entering Q2 with backlog down a fifth in units, on a business whose deliveries concentrate in H2, the run-rate was severely impaired. Annualizing Q1 net income of $6.8M implies ~$27M for the year; applying FY2025's Q1-to-full-year relationship (26.6%) implies ~$26M. Both are sensitivities, not forecasts — Q1 carried one-time merger costs and a near-zero tax rate — and neither is a reliable level estimate.

Tri Pointe Connect's origination volume more than doubled to 2,795 loans / $1,493.7M from 1,262 / $633.5M while deliveries fell 23%. This was a business model change, not demand: effective February 1, 2024 TPH acquired the minority interest in the JV and moved from brokerage to balance-sheet lending funded by repurchase agreements. Financial services pre-tax income nonetheless *fell* to $17.2M from $24.3M as expenses rose to $54.6M from $45.9M. Credit metrics softened modestly (average FICO 755 vs 761, combined LTV 79% vs 77%, government mix 18% vs 8%, ARMs 5% vs 0%), all loans full-documentation and agency-eligible. This mix shift is not diversification — the 10-K states that "the performance of Tri Pointe Solutions largely depends on our homebuilding business."

## Valuation Assessment

Valuation is not an open question. Each share converted into $47.00 in cash. Against year-end 2025 book value per share of approximately $39.25 ($3,315,834K ÷ 84,478,836 shares), the consideration equates to **~1.20x book**. On FY2025 diluted EPS of $2.72 it is **17.3x trailing**; on FY2024's $4.83 it is **9.7x peak-cycle**. Reported premium was approximately 29% to the February 12, 2026 close (implying a pre-announcement price near $36.43) and, per trade press, roughly 42% to the 90-day VWAP. On our Q1 run-rate sensitivity of ~$27M of annual net income, the consideration would be well above 100x — which is why the deal is best understood as a strategic mid-cycle take-out rather than an earnings-multiple transaction.

Two reported figures do not reconcile and we flag rather than use them. First, the widely cited "$4.5 billion" deal value exceeds our computed equity value of ~$3.97B (84.5M shares × $47.00) and our enterprise value estimate of ~$4.2B (equity plus $1,104M homebuilding debt and $90.6M of drawn mortgage repurchase facilities, less $983M cash). Second, one local outlet published a "$4.9 billion" headline that conflicts with its own earlier reporting and with the Reuters figure; the most likely explanation is conflation with Sekisui House's reported $4.9B acquisition of M.D.C. Holdings in 2024. Neither figure changes the per-share economics, which are a filing fact.

The valuation score of 1.8 reflects the absence of any asymmetry: a fixed cash price offers no upside, and at the last observable quote of $46.95 the residual spread was $0.05, or roughly 0.1%. There is no mispricing to exploit because there is no price discovery.

For sector purposes, the interesting comparison is internal. TPH repurchased 8,550,822 shares in FY2025 at an average of $32.42 ($277.2M), roughly **0.83x** year-end book, and was acquired at **1.20x**. The $14.58 differential on those shares represents approximately $124.7M of arithmetic value accretion to remaining holders. This is arithmetic, not evidence of foreknowledge: the authorization dated to December 2024, buybacks below book are routine for builders in a downcycle, and we have no information on when negotiations began. The more likely reading is that the buyback and the sale were two responses to the same observable condition — the equity traded well below book and below peers.

## Competitive Landscape

Management framed the downturn as sentiment-driven, citing a "persistent structural supply-demand imbalance" and Millennial/Gen Z household formation, with the operational response being "moderating spec inventory, aligning starts more closely with demand" and "targeted incentives selectively" while "preserving long-term pricing discipline." The same filing concedes housing supply increased versus 2024 and that Texas carried elevated resale and new-home inventory. Q1 2026's 18.8% gross margin with negligible impairment contribution argues that incentive intensity, not sentiment, was doing the damage.

More telling is what the 10-K says about structural position: "there are relatively low barriers to entry into our business"; several primary competitors "are significantly larger, have longer operating histories and may have greater resources or **lower cost of capital**"; and TPH "may be at a competitive disadvantage with respect to larger competitors whose operations are more geographically diversified." Those are management's own words describing subscale disadvantage, and they align with the DEF 14A's most defensible sale rationale: FY2025 indexed TSR of 182.44 against a peer group at 214.56, a 32.1-point gap and the widest in the five-year series (prior gaps: -3.1, -8.6, -11.0, +9.6). Relative TSR is one of three named performance measures in the incentive plan, so the Board was watching an accelerating public-market discount it could not close through buybacks. Peer group composition is not disclosed in the extract available, so the construction of the gap is unverified.

The acquirer's logic — Sumitomo Forestry building a U.S. platform alongside DRB Group and Brightland Homes, with upstream wood products and a North Carolina truss and panel plant — is reported from a single AI-assisted secondary source characterizing Sumitomo's own materials, including the claims of ~18,000 combined annual units, ~114,000 lots, ~6.5 years of supply, and a target of 23,000 U.S. homes annually by 2030. We treat all of those as unconfirmed. Roles should be stated literally: Sumitomo Forestry was the acquirer and payer; Teton NewCo was the merger vehicle; Tri Pointe was the surviving corporation and became an indirect wholly owned subsidiary. Reported brand, Irvine home office, divisional footprint and financial-services continuity is single-source and unverified.

The durable sector observation is that foreign strategic capital took out two profitable mid-cap U.S. builders in roughly two years (Sekisui/MDC reportedly 2024, Sumitomo/TPH 2026) at premiums to depressed public multiples. Mid-cap builders that cannot access equity at book value are acquisition candidates, and the discount that makes them attractive to strategics is the same discount that makes them poor standalone longs.

## Risk Assessment

For a holder, the risks resolved at closing into cash. For anyone assessing the residual security, the operative risk is total: there is no tradable equity, no analyst coverage, and no post-March 2026 operating data. The company-specific risks documented in the last 10-K remain relevant only as sector read-through:

- **California and West concentration.** 56.1% of FY2025 home sales revenue, in a state the filing calls "one of the most highly regulated and litigious jurisdictions in the United States, which imposes a ten year, strict liability tail on many construction liability claims." The January 2025 Los Angeles-area wildfires are cited as both a cost driver and a homeowners-insurance availability problem — and where buyers cannot obtain insurance, they cannot obtain mortgages.
- **Water availability.** A dedicated risk factor covering California drought proclamations, State Water Board restrictions, moratoria on new connections, and 2023 Arizona actions constraining Phoenix-area subdivision development. Deliveries "may be delayed or prevented due to the unavailability of water, even when we have obtained water supply entitlements."
- **Land option commitments.** $209.6M of non-refundable deposits against ~$2.1B of remaining purchase price net of deposits at December 31, 2025, with some contractual obligations to complete development work even on termination. Lots controlled fell 18% (19,881 → 16,265) while lots owned fell only 4% — the correct defensive posture, executed at the cost of forfeited option premium ($36.4M of FY2025 impairments and abandonments).
- **Legacy WRECO liabilities** from the 2014 Weyerhaeuser transaction, where WRECO self-insured a significant portion of general liability outside California and Nevada, so "most of these claims will not be covered by insurance."
- **Change-of-control defaults** across the credit facility and both mortgage repurchase agreements ($90.6M drawn at a 5.7% weighted average), requiring lender consent or termination and repayment at closing. Manageable against $982.8M of cash, but it means the post-close financing structure may differ materially from the 10-K description.
- **Verification gaps.** No primary-source confirmation of closing was obtained: the May 14, 2026 8-K contents were not read, no Form 25 or Form 15 was located, and stockholder vote specifics rest on one secondary source. No Form 4 data is available. Executive change-of-control payments are unquantified because the merger proxy's Item 402(t) table was not obtained.

Macro context reinforces the operating read rather than contradicting it. Externally reported sector data post-dating the close showed a 30-year fixed mortgage rate of 6.97% with national inventory rising, and the January 2026 FOMC hold removed the near-term rate-relief catalyst. TPH's Q1 gross margin at 18.8% with almost no impairment support is corroborating hard-data evidence for continued pressure on high-ASP builders, which is why we continue to treat housing and mortgage as the clearest fundamental shorts in the consumer complex. [Note: the characterization of the market pricing a rate hike rather than cuts is not supported by the sources and has been removed.]

### Options Market Signal

No usable options structure is available — the only data point is a price move from $46.90 (April 26) to $46.95 (May 15), +0.1%. That pin, within five cents of the $47.00 consideration across three weeks spanning the stockholder vote and HSR satisfaction, is itself the signal: the market priced closing as near-certain and extinguished all optionality. No IV, skew, or open-interest data exists to challenge or confirm the fundamental view, and none should be expected for a deregistered issuer.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (not investable, ~$47.00).** For holders as of the closing date, the outcome was already the bull case: $47.00 in cash, a ~29% premium to the pre-announcement price and ~42% to the 90-day VWAP, ~1.20x year-end book, against a business whose Q1 2026 homebuilding operations ran at a 0.8% operating margin and whose backlog was down 20.7% in units. The termination fee of $82.336M (~1.8% of reported deal value) was low-end and mildly TPH-favorable, and the absence of a financing condition placed funding risk entirely on Sumitomo. Judged against a standalone path — declining absorption in all three segments, a 32.1-point relative TSR gap, an admitted cost-of-capital disadvantage, and three unopened startup divisions absorbing overhead — the cash exit was the better outcome.

**Bear case (the reason for AVOID).** There is no security to own. Terminal value is fixed at $47.00; upside is zero and the residual spread was $0.05. Attempting exposure now carries deregistration and settlement risk with no compensating return. Separately, the standalone fundamentals were deteriorating faster than the FY2025 numbers suggest: Q1 2026's 510bp GAAP and 500bp adjusted gross margin declines came almost entirely from incentives, mix and absorption rather than write-downs, homebuilding income from operations was $4.2M on $506.5M of revenue, and order stability was manufactured with 8.6% more selling communities at lower productivity per store. Three misses in the last four quarters point to eroding visibility, and a Z-Score recomputed on Q1 earnings power would likely fall out of the safe zone despite a fortress balance sheet.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID.** This is a closed-out equity, not a mispriced one.

**Re-entry criteria.** None exist for TPH common stock. Re-entry would require a re-listed public security — a Sumitomo carve-out IPO or spin of the U.S. platform — which is not contemplated in any disclosure we have seen. Absent that, the ticker is terminal.

**Timeline.** Immediate and permanent for the equity. Coverage should be discontinued once the May 14, 2026 8-K and any Form 25/Form 15 are read and closing is confirmed from a primary source. Until that confirmation exists, the residual (low-probability) scenario is a terminated deal, in which case the standalone equity would reprice toward the pre-announcement level near $36 adjusted downward for two additional quarters of margin deterioration — call it the mid-$20s to low-$30s on ~1.20x-declining book and near-breakeven homebuilding operations. That is a downside-only optionality, which reinforces AVOID rather than qualifying it.

**What to monitor instead (sector read-through).** (1) Whether high-ASP builders report the same non-impairment gross margin compression TPH showed in Q1 2026 — that would confirm incentive intensity rather than sentiment as the binding constraint; (2) whether order counts at peers are being held flat via community-count growth while absorption falls, the pattern that most easily misleads a headline screen; (3) further foreign strategic bids for sub-scale U.S. builders trading below book, which would extend the Sekisui/Sumitomo pattern and put a floor under mid-cap builder valuations independent of housing demand.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** None. The merger consideration of $47.00 in cash is fixed by contract, stockholders approved it, HSR cleared, and the transaction is reported closed as of May 14, 2026. There is no earnings event, refinancing date, or regulatory ruling ahead of the security. The outside date was August 13, 2026, extendable an additional three months for HSR or injunction resolution.

**Timing window.** Not applicable. A short would have to be established against a security whose payoff is a fixed cash amount already delivered.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** No current short interest, float, or institutional ownership data is retrievable, consistent with deregistration. Pre-close, merger-arbitrage longs dominated the register; the one relevant reported flow (an institutional 13F holder exiting 430,731 shares in Q1 2026) was a routine event-driven exit, not an insider signal.

**Borrow and options signal.** No options data of substance exists. The price pinned within 0.1% of the deal price, which is the opposite of a bearish options configuration. Borrow on a closing cash-merger target is typically expensive and callable, and recall risk at closing is absolute.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Shorting at the last observable $46.95 against a $47.00 cash payoff produces a mechanical loss of $0.05 per share plus borrow cost — a negative expected return with no offsetting upside. Reward:risk is worse than 0:1.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** There is no catalyst, no borrow, no tradable security, and the payoff is a fixed cash amount above the last traded price. Avoid without any short expression.

## Conclusion

Tri Pointe Homes ceased to be an investable equity on May 14, 2026, when Sumitomo Forestry is reported to have completed its acquisition at $47.00 per share in cash. Every retrievable market data field — no price history, no analyst targets, no CIK resolution — corroborates that status, and the last observable quote of $46.95 leaves no economic reason to hold, buy, or short the security. We reiterate AVOID for a fifth consecutive report at an overall score of 3.2, with the flat score reflecting a resolved terminal condition rather than an absence of change.

The residual analytical value sits in what the last two reporting periods showed. TPH sold from a position of balance-sheet strength ($1.8B liquidity, 25% debt/capital, 6.4x interest coverage, wide covenant headroom) and operating weakness: FY2025 revenue -22.8%, deliveries -23% on flat ASP, gross margin -230bp with $31.1M of impairments, SG&A up 180bp on half-fixed overhead, then a Q1 2026 in which gross margin fell 510bp with essentially no impairment contribution and homebuilding operations earned $4.2M on $506.5M of revenue. Order stability was bought with 8.6% more stores at declining absorption, and backlog entered Q2 down 20.7% in units. Management's own filing named the reason a sale made sense — low barriers to entry, larger competitors with a lower cost of capital, and a geographic-diversification disadvantage — while the proxy quantified the consequence in a 32.1-point relative TSR gap. Foreign strategic capital paid 1.20x book for a platform the public market valued at 0.83x. For anyone underwriting the remaining public builders at a 30-year mortgage rate near 7%, the Q1 2026 margin decomposition is the number to carry forward.
