## Executive Summary

RTX Corporation trades at $174.49 following a ~19% drawdown from its March 2026 high of $214.50. The decline was precipitated by Q1 2026 earnings that, while beating estimates by 16.1% ($1.78 vs. $1.53), contained forward commentary that disappointed investors — likely related to tariff uncertainty, the Airbus damages dispute, and constrained capital return capacity under the January 2026 executive order. The business fundamentals remain strong: $268B backlog (3x revenue), operating cash flow of $10.6B in FY2025, and Raytheon's defense bookings running at 2.2x its revenue base.

The core tension is between exceptional business quality and growth visibility on one hand, and a valuation that already prices in much of this visibility at ~35x trailing / ~25x forward P/E. This multiple represents a premium to pure-defense peers LMT (25x) and GD (22x), which is partly justified by RTX's higher growth profile but partly reflects the commercial aftermarket multiple embedded in the consolidated entity. The specific risk that prevents upgrading at current levels is the Airbus damages pursuit — a potential multi-billion-dollar liability from RTX's largest commercial engine customer, occurring while the Powder Metal Matter fleet disruption continues and with remaining accruals of only $700M. Combined with the executive order explicitly constraining buybacks during "underperformance" (which Pratt & Whitney's delivery delays could trigger), the capital return pathway that typically supports premium defense multiples is impaired.

I expect RTX to generate $6.91 in EPS for FY2026 (consensus) and compound toward $7.50+ in FY2027, driven by aftermarket growth and defense bookings conversion. The stock is a BUY on a pullback to $155-160 (22-23x forward), where the risk/reward rebalances.

## Company Overview

RTX Corporation is the world's largest aerospace and defense company by revenue ($88.6B in FY2025), operating through three segments:

- **Collins Aerospace** ($30.2B, 16.3% operating margins): Avionics, aerostructures, mechanical systems for commercial and military platforms.
- **Pratt & Whitney** ($32.9B, 7.9% margins): Commercial and military engines, including the GTF family for A320neo and the F135 powering the F-35.
- **Raytheon** ($28.0B, 11.5% margins): Missiles, air/missile defense systems (Patriot, NASAMS), naval radars (SPY-6), and advanced sensors.

Revenue splits approximately 46% U.S. government / 54% commercial and foreign direct — providing genuine diversification across defense and commercial aerospace cycles.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Growth:** $67.1B (2022) → $68.9B (2023) → $80.7B (2024) → $88.6B (2025). Organic growth of 11% in 2025, driven by Pratt & Whitney (+$4.8B from commercial aftermarket and military programs) and Collins (+$2.6B from commercial OE and aftermarket).

**Margin Recovery:** Operating margins expanded from 5.2% (2023, depressed by the $2.9B Powder Metal charge) to 8.1% (2024) to 10.5% (2025). This trajectory has room to continue as Pratt & Whitney margins (7.9%) improve toward their historical 12-14% range once GTF fleet issues normalize.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $10.6B and free cash flow of $7.9B in FY2025 represent a step-function improvement (+48% YoY in OCF). At the current $235B market cap, this is a 3.4% FCF yield — acceptable but not compelling for an industrial.

**Balance Sheet:** Net debt of approximately $28B ($37.9B gross debt less cash). Debt-to-EBITDA roughly 3.0x on trailing figures. Credit ratings upgraded to stable outlook at both Moody's (Baa1) and S&P (BBB+) during 2025, reflecting the deleveraging progress ($3.35B repaid in 2025). No near-term refinancing cliff.

**Earnings Beat Track Record:** 4 consecutive beats: +7.0%, +19.5%, +4.6%, +16.1%. This consistent execution supports confidence in forward estimates, though the magnitude of beats has been volatile.

## Growth Analysis

**$268B Backlog:** This is the single most important growth datapoint. Up 23% from $218B in December 2024, it represents 3x annual revenue. Defense bookings of $61B in 2025 against $28B in Raytheon revenue implies years of conversion ahead. Commercial backlog of $161B reflects the massive A320neo orderbook and growing engine installed base.

**Secular Tailwinds:**
1. Global rearmament: European defense spending accelerating post-Ukraine; Middle East contracts restarting; Golden Dome appropriation of $24.4B (CBO estimates $1.2T total program) positions Raytheon as a primary beneficiary through its Patriot, interceptor, and radar franchises.
2. Commercial aftermarket: The GTF installed base is building a multi-decade annuity. Each engine that enters service generates 3-5x its sale price in lifetime maintenance revenue. The current fleet disruption (while painful near-term) is accelerating shop visit revenue.
3. Munitions replenishment: The $25.4B in the defense act for munitions and supply chain resiliency directly benefits Raytheon's missile production lines.

**Forward Estimates:** $6.91 EPS for FY2026 (+0.4% actual growth over FY2025's $6.88), accelerating to $7.56 in FY2027 (+9.4%). The FY2026 flatness reflects one-time headwinds (tariffs, Powder Metal cash costs, FAS/CAS decline). Estimate revisions are modestly positive (+1.0% current year over 30 days) but next quarter has been revised down 2.8%.

## Valuation Assessment

At $174.49 with $6.91 in expected FY2026 EPS, RTX trades at 25.3x forward earnings. Against FY2027's $7.56, it trades at 23.1x.

**Peer Comparison:**
- LMT: 25.3x (pure defense, 2.7% yield, $194B backlog)
- GD: 21.8x (defense + Gulfstream commercial, 1.8% yield)
- GE Aerospace: 36.9x (pure commercial engine/services, highest-growth peer)
- HWM: 62.6x (specialty aerospace, highest margins)
- BA: 94.9x (recovery situation, not comparable)

RTX's 25x forward sits between the pure-defense primes (LMT/GD at 22-25x) and GE Aerospace (37x). This is reasonable given RTX's blended exposure, but at the upper end of what's justified by 9-10% EPS growth. The key question is whether RTX deserves a GE-like multiple for its commercial aftermarket stream or a LMT-like multiple for its defense backlog. The market is currently pricing it closer to the GE end of this spectrum.

**DCF Anchor:** $7.9B FCF growing at 8-10% annually, discounted at 9% WACC with a 3.5% terminal growth rate, produces fair value of approximately $190-200/share. At $174, there is modest upside to intrinsic value, but not the 15%+ required for a high-conviction BUY recommendation given the active risks.

**Why the market is not obviously wrong at $174:** The drawdown from $214 reflects legitimate new information — the Airbus damages dispute, tariff uncertainty, and constrained capital returns are real impairments to near-term shareholder value realization, even if the underlying business trajectory is intact. Sophisticated investors are appropriately repricing the gap between business quality and shareholder value realization when buybacks are restricted and a major customer is pursuing damages.

## Competitive Landscape

RTX's competitive position is strengthening on multiple dimensions:

1. **Only integrated engine + defense prime**: No competitor combines a top-2 commercial engine franchise, a leading avionics/systems house, and a premier missile/radar defense prime. This creates cross-selling opportunities and portfolio resilience.

2. **GTF installed base growing despite disruption**: The Powder Metal issue is a 2-3 year headwind that builds a 30-year aftermarket stream. Every A320neo delivered locks in decades of maintenance revenue at RTX's pricing.

3. **Raytheon's missile monopoly positions**: Patriot, AMRAAM, StandJAM, Tomahawk — these programs have no drop-in replacements. The global missile inventory deficit must be replenished through RTX's production lines.

4. **Golden Dome beneficiary**: RTX's selection for the Space-Based Interceptor program, combined with existing Patriot and sensor capabilities, positions it as the primary industrial beneficiary of the largest missile defense program since SDI.

Competitive risks are limited to specific sub-segments: GE Aerospace's LEAP vs. GTF in the narrowbody engine competition, and L3Harris in certain sensor/radar segments. Neither threatens RTX's overall franchise.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Airbus Damages Dispute (HIGH):** Reuters reported in March 2026 that Airbus is pursuing potential damages against Pratt & Whitney for late engine shipments. RTX's remaining Powder Metal accrual is only $700M. If Airbus extracts multi-billion-dollar damages, this would materially impair near-term cash flow and potentially require additional charges. Given Airbus's leverage (as the sole A320neo airframe), RTX's negotiating position is constrained.

**2. Executive Order on Capital Returns (MEDIUM-HIGH):** The January 2026 executive order gives the Secretary of War authority to limit dividends and buybacks for "underperforming" contractors. RTX already halted meaningful buybacks ($50M in 2025 vs. $12.9B in 2023). This removes the traditional mechanism by which defense companies return value to shareholders during periods of backlog growth. The remaining buyback authorization of ~$600M is essentially frozen.

**3. Tariff Pass-Through on Fixed-Price Contracts (MEDIUM):** Fixed-price defense contracts cannot absorb tariff cost increases without margin compression. Management states tariffs won't have a "material adverse effect" based on current conditions but acknowledges this could change with scope/rate changes. With tariffs imposed on multiple trading partners since February 2025, material and component costs will rise.

**4. Legal/DPA Compliance (MEDIUM):** Deferred prosecution agreements with DOJ for FCPA violations and defective pricing remain active. An independent compliance monitor is expected to be in place by end of Q1 2026. Any breach could result in debarment — an existential risk for a defense contractor, though low probability.

**5. Powder Metal Tail Risk (MEDIUM, declining):** Cash impact declining from ~$1.0B/year to ~$0.7B in 2026. GTF Advantage certification (April 2026) provides a path forward. Risk is declining but Airbus dispute adds complexity.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($210-220, +20-26%):** Defense bookings accelerate under Golden Dome/rearmament. Airbus dispute settles for modest amounts ($1-2B spread over multiple years, manageable within cash flow). GTF Advantage engine ramp resolves fleet issues by mid-2027. Margins expand toward 12% operating as Pratt & Whitney normalizes. EPS reaches $8.00+ by FY2028. Multiple re-rates to 27-28x forward on improved capital return visibility.

**Bear Case ($145-155, -11-17%):** Airbus extracts significant damages ($3-5B), requiring a new charge. Tariffs compress fixed-price defense margins by 100-200bps. Executive order prevents meaningful buyback resumption through 2027. EPS stays flat at $6.80-7.00. Multiple compresses to 21-22x (in line with GD) on capital return uncertainty.

**Base Case ($190-200, +9-15%):** Business executes in line with guidance. Airbus dispute resolved over 12-18 months for moderate amounts. Defense backlog converts at expected pace. EPS of $6.91 in FY2026, $7.50+ in FY2027. Multiple stabilizes at 25-26x forward.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock declines to $155-160 (22-23x forward FY2026 EPS), where downside to bear case is 5-7% vs. 25-30% upside to base/bull case.
2. Airbus dispute resolution is announced with quantified damages below $1.5B total.
3. Executive order on capital returns is rescinded or RTX receives explicit clearance to resume buybacks at $3B+ annually.

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Airbus formally files suit for damages exceeding $3B, or Pratt & Whitney takes an additional charge of >$1.5B.
2. Defense bookings decelerate to <1.5x book-to-bill for two consecutive quarters.
3. Multiple expands above 30x forward FY2027 EPS without corresponding estimate upgrades — at that point, the stock would price in all identifiable upside.

**Reassessment Timeline:** Next earnings release (Q2 2026, likely late July). Monitor for: (a) any Airbus settlement language, (b) tariff impact quantification, (c) defense booking pace, (d) capital return commentary.

## Conclusion

RTX is a high-quality aerospace and defense franchise with exceptional revenue visibility, improving financial health, and secular tailwinds from global rearmament and commercial aftermarket growth. The 4-quarter earnings beat streak and consistent execution support confidence in forward estimates.

The specific reason this remains a HOLD rather than a BUY at $174: at 25x forward earnings, the stock prices in the growth trajectory but does not adequately compensate for three active, quantifiable risks — (1) the Airbus damages pursuit where remaining accruals ($700M) may prove insufficient, (2) the executive order that explicitly constrains the capital return mechanism that typically supports premium defense multiples, and (3) near-term estimate softness (next quarter revised down 2.8%) suggesting tariff headwinds are materializing. These are specific impairments to the pathway from business quality to shareholder value realization.

The stock becomes a BUY at $155-160, where the multiple compresses to 22-23x and provides adequate margin of safety against the quantified risks. At current levels, investors are paying for execution without being compensated for the active litigation and policy risks.
