## Executive Summary

Ferguson is the largest value-added distributor to the water-and-air professional in North America — 1,746 branches, ~35,000 associates, ~5,900 delivery vehicles, more than one million SKUs — with roughly 9% share of a market management now sizes at $400 billion. The business quality is genuine and verifiable in the filings: no customer exceeds 1% of net sales, no supplier exceeds 5% of inventory purchases, ROE ran 31.8% in fiscal 2025, and net debt/adjusted EBITDA sits at approximately 1.36x inside a stated 1-2x target. The balance sheet and governance are not the issue.

The problem is the composition of growth and the price being asked for it. In fiscal 2025 (twelve months to July 31, 2025) revenue grew 3.8% on volume, while adjusted operating profit grew 0.6% and adjusted operating margin compressed 29 basis points to 9.24%; headline diluted EPS growth of 9.3% came primarily from the absence of a prior-year $137M deferred tax charge and from share retirement. In the first half of calendar 2026 the mix inverted: revenue rose 4.2% on price inflation plus 0.9% from acquisitions, "partially offset by lower sales volume." Q2 CY2026 adjusted operating profit grew 2.9% on 4.6% sales growth, showing negative operating leverage again. The only growing end market is non-residential, up ~8% on what management attributes to "large capital project activity" in commercial/mechanical, industrial and waterworks, tied to data centers, power generation, semiconductors and pharmaceuticals. US residential — half of sales, two-thirds of which is repair/maintenance/improvement — grew ~1% against a mortgage-rate backdrop that has since worsened, with 30-year rates at a one-year high and total application demand below year-ago levels.

Ferguson has responded coherently by buying $1.6B of higher-margin industrial flow control (FloWorks, announced July 13, 2026, expected to close in Q3), which deepens the technical capability its growing end markets demand. The deal is priced at roughly 13.9x standalone LTM adjusted EBITDA before the ~$45M of claimed synergies, it takes pro forma leverage to approximately 1.76-1.79x, the practical ceiling of the stated range, and by my arithmetic its first-year adjusted EPS contribution (+$0.10, rising to +$0.28 at full synergies) is roughly equivalent to what a debt-funded buyback of the same size would have produced. At 21-23x forward adjusted EPS with the repurchase lever constrained, organic volume negative, and analyst sentiment degrading (buy ratings 13 → 10 over three months), the risk/reward from $262 is approximately balanced at best. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

Ferguson is a pure distribution intermediary sitting between approximately 37,000 suppliers and a highly fragmented professional customer base. Segments are geographic: United States (95% of net sales in each of FY2023-FY2025) and Canada (5%, under the Wolseley brand). Infrastructure includes 11 regional distribution centers, six automated market distribution centers, and three import centers. The stated proposition is same-day/next-day availability plus technical services: virtual design, fabrication, valve actuation, pre-assembly, kitting, project management.

End markets are roughly half residential and half non-residential, with repair/maintenance/improvement approximately two-thirds of the total. That RMI weighting is the structural reason Ferguson's revenue is less cyclical than a pure new-construction distributor's, and it is also why current softness matters: management's own MD&A cites "weak new construction activity **and soft repair, maintenance and improvement (RMI) work**" for the half. Weakness in the defensive portion is more informative than weakness in the cyclical portion.

US customer-group mix (Q2 CY2026 vs. prior year) shows the reweighting in progress: Commercial/Mechanical 16% (from 15%), HVAC 13% (12%), Industrial 7% (6%), against Ferguson Home 20% (from 21%) and Residential Trade Plumbing 14% (15%). Waterworks held at 24%.

Ferguson changed its fiscal year-end from July 31 to December 31, filing a five-month transition report for August-December 2025, which distorts most screening data. Fiscal 2025 (to July 31, 2025), the stub period, and calendar 2026 are three non-comparable frames. Provider-supplied multiples reflect this: the data feed reports trailing P/E and forward P/E identically at 25.58 while also reporting EPS of $9.624, which at $262.185 would be 27.2x. Neither figure is usable; all multiples below are computed from filing data.

## Financial Analysis

**Fiscal 2025 (audited, twelve months to July 31, 2025).** Net sales $30,762M (+3.8%); gross profit $9,435M with gross margin up 20bp to 30.7%; SG&A $6,376M (+5.6%), rising 30bp to 20.7% of sales; operating profit $2,606M (−1.7%); adjusted operating profit $2,842M (+0.6%); net income $1,856M (+7.0%); diluted EPS $9.32 (+9.3%); adjusted diluted EPS $9.94 (+2.6%). Operating cash flow $1,908M, capex $305M, free cash flow $1,603M.

The gap between +9.3% GAAP EPS and +0.6% adjusted operating profit is the single most important number in the fiscal 2025 accounts. The effective tax rate fell 620bp to 23.4%, and MD&A attributes the net income increase primarily to the non-recurrence of a $137M deferred tax charge related to the US redomiciliation. Adjusted operating margin of 9.24% versus 9.53% shows the entire margin loss came from SG&A: the company grew gross profit dollars 4.2% and let operating costs grow 5.6%, which MD&A attributes to incentive compensation and labor/fleet/infrastructure inflation. Pricing was a headwind, with US commodity deflation of ~1%.

Segment divergence was already visible: US non-residential +6.8%, US residential +0.9%. Canada delivered $66M of adjusted operating profit on $1,493M of sales (4.4% margin) versus 9.7% in the US, and Canada is now being pruned — Q2 CY2026 Canadian sales fell 1.9% with non-core divestments cutting 3.6%.

**Calendar 2026 first half.** Net sales $16,223M (+4.2%); operating profit $1,505M (+11.6%); adjusted operating profit $1,579M (+5.1%); net income $1,080M; diluted EPS $5.56; adjusted diluted EPS $5.67 (+7.0%). Q2 specifically: sales $8,751M (+4.6%), adjusted operating profit $932M (+2.9%), adjusted operating margin 10.65% versus 10.83%, an 18bp decline, with the comparison complicated by prior-year incentive-accrual timing. For the half, adjusted operating margin improved 8bp to 9.73% as the SG&A ratio fell from 20.64% to 20.50%.

Q2 adjusted diluted EPS of $3.39 sits *below* GAAP diluted EPS of $3.43 because the reconciliation removes $38M ($0.19/share) of favorable discrete tax items, more than the restructuring and intangible add-backs. Conservative non-GAAP treatment is a mild positive on disclosure quality.

**Cash flow.** H1 CY2026 operating cash flow was $716M against $1,123M, down 36%; capex rose to $234M from $141M, so free cash flow fell 51% to $482M and FCF/net income conversion dropped from 100% to 45%. Three drivers are identifiable in the statements: cash taxes paid $421M versus $240M (+$181M, explicitly attributed to calendar-transition timing), inventory consuming $468M versus $169M, and receivables/other consuming $1,070M versus $844M. June 30 is a seasonal working-capital peak for this business (receivables $4,255M versus $3,312M at December 31 is largely seasonal build), so this half-year figure should not be annualized. As a labeled sensitivity: adding back the $181M tax timing and normalizing inventory build to the prior-year rate would put half-year OCF near $1.0-1.2B, consistent with a full-year figure in the neighborhood of FY2025's $1.9B. If the inventory build instead proves to be demand-driven overbuild, full-year FCF comes in materially below FY2025's $1,603M. This is the cleanest near-term test of the thesis and it resolves at the Q3 report.

**Capital allocation ran ahead of cash generation in the half.** Buybacks $438M plus dividends paid $347M totalled $785M against $482M of FCF (163%), funded with $775M of net new borrowing ($525M on the receivables facility, $250M on the revolver). In FY2025 total returns approximated 100% of FCF. Ferguson returns essentially all free cash flow and funds acquisitions with incremental debt, defensible at 1.1-1.4x leverage and constrained at 1.8x.

**Balance sheet.** At June 30, 2026: cash $437M, total debt $4,904M, net debt $4,467M, equity $6,201M, goodwill $2,713M, current ratio 1.73. Screens reporting "debt/equity 2.04" are computing *total liabilities* to equity; actual FY2025 financial debt/equity was $4,152M ÷ $5,832M = 0.71, because most of the liability base is trade payables and operating leases. Net debt/adjusted EBITDA is approximately 1.36x on estimated LTM adjusted EBITDA of ~$3.28B (the July-2025 month within that LTM build is estimated and is the weakest input). Debt structure is favorable: $3,850M of unsecured notes maturing April 2027 through October 2034, ~84% fixed-rate, blended cash interest ~4.2%, near-term maturities of only $150M (November 2026) and $300M (April 2027), $1.6B undrawn, and stated compliance with all covenants in effect at June 30. The supplier finance program at $76M is 1.9% of payables and is not a leverage vehicle. Altman Z of 3.25 sits in the safe zone; substituting market equity would lift it above 5.

Financial health scores 6.5: strong coverage, cheap and well-laddered debt, high asset turnover and ROE, offset by leverage moving to the top of the target range, distribution exceeding free cash flow in the half, and a conversion ratio that needs the second half to validate the seasonal explanation.

## Growth Analysis

The growth question reduces to one arithmetic fact and one exposure fact.

**The arithmetic:** in H1 CY2026, reported 4.2% revenue growth consisted of low-to-mid single-digit price inflation plus 0.9% from acquisitions, "partially offset by lower sales volume." Underlying unit demand contracted. Price-driven revenue carries less incremental gross profit per dollar than volume while fixed costs inflate regardless, which is what the 2.9%-on-4.6% Q2 operating-profit outcome shows. Fiscal 2025 was the inverse — volume-led with price as a drag — so the *quality* of the reported growth rate has deteriorated even as the rate itself is similar.

**The exposure:** non-residential (~half of sales) is compounding at 7-8% on large capital projects in commercial/mechanical, industrial and waterworks. This is the same demand pool visible elsewhere in the industrial complex — data-center, power-generation and semiconductor construction — and it is corroborated by multiple unrelated companies reporting record electrical, mechanical and grid-equipment demand. It is a real, broad, multi-source phenomenon rather than a single-company story, which raises the durability of the near-term growth path. It also means Ferguson's growth is now levered to a capex cycle concentrated among a handful of hyperscale and power buyers, at the same moment the company adds $1.6B of debt-funded exposure to the same end markets. Residential is flat nominally and therefore shrinking in real terms, with the mortgage-rate path pointing the wrong way.

Consensus reflects this equilibrium rather than an inflection. Current-year EPS estimates are $11.26, down 2.1% over 90 days; next year is $12.56, up 1.2%; quarterly estimates are unchanged within rounding. (There is an internal inconsistency in the feed: summing the reported H1 actual of $5.67 with the next two quarterly estimates of $3.30 and $3.29 gives $12.26 for calendar 2026, $1.00 above the stated current-year consensus of $11.26 — likely a fiscal-frame artifact of the year-end change. I use both anchors below.) There is no +30% revision velocity here and no earnings-inflection signal; this is a mid-single-digit compounder whose reported EPS growth has been flattered by tax normalization and a 4.7% two-year reduction in diluted shares.

The earnings-surprise record — four consecutive beats — is worth calibrating rather than celebrating. The magnitudes have collapsed from 21.3% (June 2025 quarter) to 0.4%, 0.6% and 1.1% in the three most recent quarters. That pattern is consistent with a mature, closely-guided business, and it means forward estimates carry little upside cushion.

Management has reportedly lifted full-year sales growth guidance to the top of the prior range (mid-single-digit) and raised capex guidance to $375-425M, with roughly $1.4B of year-to-date acquired revenue including FloWorks reportedly excluded from guidance. These figures come from secondary reporting on the August 10 release rather than a primary filing, and should be treated as unconfirmed. The capex step-up is verifiable in the cash flow statement ($234M versus $141M) and would take capex to ~1.23% of sales versus 0.99% in FY2025 — investment in distribution centers, branches and technology that will suppress near-term free cash flow.

Growth prospects score 5.3: mid-single-digit top line with negative organic volume, one growing end market tied to a concentrated capex cycle, a constrained buyback lever, and stable-to-slightly-negative estimate revisions.

Valuation scores 4.8: full multiple, thin margin of safety, no identified mispricing, and a fading buyback contribution to per-share growth.

## Competitive Landscape

Ferguson's moat is service density rather than product differentiation, and it is real: 1,746 branches, 11 regional DCs, six automated market DCs, ~5,900 vehicles, same-day/next-day availability on a million-SKU assortment. Roughly 9% share of a $340-400B market means both a long consolidation runway and no pricing power over the market — Ferguson takes what the market gives on price and earns its return on service, mix and cost density. Customer concentration below 1% per customer and supplier concentration below 5% of purchases are structurally excellent and unchanged.

The consolidation strategy is executing at pace: 9 acquisitions in FY2025, 10 in FY2024, 8 in FY2023, 7 in H1 CY2026 for $638M, plus FloWorks. The purchase accounting on those H1 deals is instructive: $580M of $638M (91%) allocated to goodwill and intangibles, with the acquired businesses contributing $58M of sales and a **$15M pre-tax loss** including $19M of transaction and integration costs. FloWorks was a Wynnchurch Capital platform assembled through seven add-ons since 2023 and sold at ~1.6x revenue and ~13.9x pre-synergy EBITDA. Ferguson is now competing for assets against private equity at non-distressed multiples, which caps the value creatable per dollar deployed.

Three structural threats appear in management's own risk language and deserve weight. First, supplier vertical integration: the 10-K states Ferguson "has experienced and may continue to experience competitive pressure from certain of our suppliers vertically integrating and selling their products directly to customers," and that those suppliers "can often sell their products at lower prices and maintain higher gross margins." Second, non-traditional entrants with "larger customer bases, greater brand recognition and greater resources" acquiring traditional competitors — the filing names no one, so identifying large home-improvement retailers as the referent is inference, not disclosure. Third, the technology risk factor has been updated from "generative AI" in the 10-K to "generative or **agentic** artificial intelligence" in the 10-Q's forward-looking summary. A one-word change, but it signals management's own concern that autonomous transaction agents could aggregate demand away from incumbent intermediaries. Ferguson's defense is technical value-add that cannot be disintermediated by a price-comparison agent, which is the capability set FloWorks deepens.

An unquantified sensitivity: the 10-K discloses that many products are bought under supplier rebate arrangements that "effectively reduce the costs of our products, and we manage our business to take advantage of these programs," and that rebates are periodically renegotiated. Ferguson does not size rebate income, so an investor cannot determine how much of the 31% gross margin depends on it.

Competitive position scores 6.2: leadership is durable and the mix shift toward technical industrial work is well-executed, but gross margin is flat to down 20bp, adjusted operating margin compressed in both the most recent fiscal year and the most recent quarter, and the acquisition arithmetic is getting more expensive. Trajectory is stable rather than improving.

## Risk Assessment

**Residential stagnation is realized, not hypothetical.** US residential grew 0.9% in FY2025 and ~1% in H1 CY2026, with MD&A citing soft RMI. RMI is normally the defensive two-thirds of that exposure. The macro direction is unhelpful: 30-year mortgage rates at a one-year high, total application demand below year-ago levels, and the largest US mortgage lender suspending its dividend after a quarterly loss. A residential RMI decline rather than a plateau would take Ferguson's blended revenue growth to zero or below with fixed costs inflating.

**Single-engine growth concentrated in one capex cycle.** Non-residential growth is explicitly attributed to "large capital project activity," and FloWorks' named end markets are chemicals, refining, power generation, semiconductors, pharmaceuticals and data centers. A deceleration in data-center and power capex would remove Ferguson's only growth engine immediately after it levered up to buy more of the same exposure. This is the most consequential unpriced risk in the name, and it is not company-specific — it depends on whether current AI-infrastructure spending converts to returns.

**Leverage at the ceiling constrains the EPS support.** Pro forma net debt/adjusted EBITDA of ~1.76-1.79x sits at the top of the 1-2x target. The April 30, 2026 authorization was $2.0B, replacing a $5.0B program, with only $136M executed through June 30 and monthly repurchases falling from 438,062 shares in May to 145,125 in June. Given management's own attribution of FY2025 adjusted EPS growth "primarily" to repurchases, the buyback contribution should decline over the next 12-18 months. The counter-argument: the June slowdown may be a blackout ahead of the FloWorks announcement, and deleveraging to ~1.4x within 18 months is plausible if working capital normalizes.

**FloWorks integration and quality of earnings.** Ferguson is buying a three-year sponsor roll-up. No audited FloWorks financials, EBITDA definition, or add-back schedule has been disclosed; the LTM adjusted EBITDA figure underpinning the "10x" headline may include pro forma or run-rate elements from the seven add-ons. Roughly 30% of the headline value is synergy not yet earned.

**Commodity two-sidedness.** Commodity-based products (plastic, copper, steel) are up to ~15% of US net sales, and the 10-K is explicit that deflation is as damaging as inflation — FY2025 demonstrated it with ~1% US price deflation compressing the top line. Tariff relief would create the same problem in reverse through higher-cost inventory.

**Legacy structure.** UK defined-benefit pension AOCI loss of $568M at June 30, 2026 (worsened from $464M at December 2024), with a 0.25% discount-rate move shifting the net position ~$33-35M and one additional year of life expectancy costing $41M. Separately, the tax risk factor warns "the location of tax residence of certain subsidiaries could be challenged," a residual of the UK-to-US redomiciliation, with $800M of foreign earnings treated as indefinitely reinvested.

**Risks I would not weight heavily:** cybersecurity (ISO 27001-aligned, no material incident), litigation (the 10-Q states no pending proceeding is expected to be material), and the retained-earnings discontinuity across the redomiciliation ($9,589M at July 2024 versus $5,887M at December 2024) — total equity is continuous and rising, which argues for an equity reclassification or XBRL tagging inconsistency rather than an economic event. It should not be treated as comparable in any time series.

Risk factors score 5.4: no binary event and no distress risk, but two live, partly unpriced macro dependencies (residential RMI, data-center capex) sit on both sides of a fully-valued multiple.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 28.6% against 31.0% one-year realized is fair-to-slightly-cheap (spread −2.4%), with the IV percentile within the current chain at 8% — the options market is pricing no near-term dislocation and volatility is at the low end of its own recent range. Term structure is flat (28.6% near versus 30.2% at six months), so uncertainty is evenly distributed rather than event-clustered. The put/call skew of 7.5% at the 10-day tenor is characterized as elevated put demand, easing to 4.4% at three months and inverting at six months; put/call volume at 0.15 and open-interest at 0.35 are both low, meaning hedging demand is modest in absolute size. Short interest is 1.7% of float at 2.2 days to cover — no crowded positioning either way. The composite read: a modest near-term hedging bid on an otherwise complacent options surface, consistent with a fairly-valued, low-controversy industrial. For a prospective buyer, cheap near-term volatility makes patience inexpensive.

One unexplained market-structure item: volume on August 3 (10.85M shares) and August 4 (46.18M shares) ran 6x and 27x the ~1.72M average, alongside a price gap from $234 to $250-256. The pattern is consistent with index-related or other mechanical flow; I have not verified the cause, and it means a meaningful portion of the recent 14% advance may reflect non-fundamental demand.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $320 (+22.1%)

Non-residential compounds at 7-8% through 2027 as data-center, power-generation and semiconductor construction sustains, and residential RMI stabilizes as the Fed eventually eases and mortgage rates retreat, restoring positive organic volume. Price inflation persists in finished goods. FloWorks closes in Q3 with the full $45M of synergies plus unquantified revenue synergies across Industrial, Commercial Mechanical and Waterworks, taking CY2027 adjusted EPS to ~$13.05. Working capital normalizes, second-half free cash flow reverts toward FY2025's $1.6B run rate, leverage falls back toward 1.4x by mid-2027 and reopens the buyback. The market re-rates a defensively-mixed, 31%-ROE consolidator to 24.5x. Value: 24.5x × $13.05 ≈ $320.

### Bear Case — $196 (−25.4%)

Residential RMI turns negative rather than plateauing as mortgage rates hold near 7% and the housing wealth effect reverses; the H1 inventory build proves demand-driven rather than seasonal, forcing a destocking quarter. Data-center and power capex decelerates, removing the non-residential offset just as $1.6B of debt-funded exposure to those same end markets lands. Revenue goes flat, negative operating leverage compresses adjusted operating margin below 9.5%, FloWorks synergies slip and integration costs run above plan, leverage sticks near 1.8x with the buyback effectively suspended, and adjusted EPS settles near $11.50. The multiple compresses toward Masco's 17x on a distributor with no per-share growth engine. Value: 17x × $11.50 ≈ $196.

### Base Case — $271 (+3.4%; ~+4.8% with dividend)

Mid-single-digit revenue growth continues, roughly half price and M&A; residential stays flat and non-residential grows 6-8%; adjusted operating margin holds near 9.7-9.9%; FloWorks closes and contributes $0.20-0.30 net of financing; CY2027 adjusted EPS of ~$12.90; the multiple holds at 21x, in line with the comparable set adjusted for Ferguson's superior returns and mix. Probability-weighting bull 25% / base 50% / bear 25% produces an expected 12-month total return of roughly 2%, below what a fairly-priced equity should offer.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD. Horizon for reassessment: the Q3 CY2026 report (early November 2026), which will contain the first FloWorks close disclosures, the seasonal cash-flow reversal, and a clean read on the SG&A run rate.**

**Base case target $271** — 21x estimated CY2027 adjusted EPS of $12.90 (consensus $12.56 plus ~$0.30 FloWorks). **Bull $320** (24.5x × $13.05). **Bear $196** (17x × $11.50). From $262.185: +3.4% to base, +22.1% to bull, −25.4% to bear. Analyst range $225-$310, mean $279.42.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Price at or below **$215-225** with fundamentals intact — that is 17-18x estimated CY2027 EPS, restoring a double-digit expected return to the base case and roughly matching the low end of the published analyst range.
2. **Organic volume returns to positive growth** with US residential accelerating above +3%, and adjusted operating profit growing faster than sales for two consecutive quarters (reversing the negative operating leverage of Q2 CY2026).
3. **Second-half free cash flow validating the seasonal explanation** — full-year CY2026 FCF at or above $1.5B with net debt/adjusted EBITDA back below 1.5x by mid-2027, which would restore the buyback lever.
4. Disclosure of FloWorks' audited standalone financials confirming ≥$115M of adjusted EBITDA on a clean basis, plus evidence of synergy capture ahead of the $45M plan.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE):**
1. **US residential net sales turning negative** year-over-year in any quarter, or management characterizing RMI as declining rather than soft.
2. **Non-residential growth decelerating below 4%**, or explicit commentary that large capital project activity is slowing, which removes the only growth engine.
3. **Full-year CY2026 free cash flow below $1.2B**, confirming the inventory build was demand-driven overbuild rather than seasonal timing, with net leverage above 1.9x.
4. Adjusted operating margin below 9.3% for a trailing twelve-month period, indicating cost inflation is outrunning gross profit dollars structurally rather than cyclically.

**Position guidance:** existing holders should maintain exposure — the balance sheet, dividend coverage (~30% payout), and end-market mix do not warrant selling a franchise of this quality at 21x. Prospective buyers have no entry signal at $262 and should wait for either the $215-225 zone or confirmation of positive organic volume.

## Conclusion

Ferguson is a high-quality business trading at a full price with a narrowing growth base. The verifiable strengths are substantial: leadership in a fragmented $400B market with 9% share, no customer above 1% of sales, 31.8% ROE, 1.36x net leverage on 84%-fixed debt at ~4.2% blended cost, light near-term maturities, $1.6B of undrawn liquidity, a 4.7% two-year reduction in diluted shares, and a conservative approach to non-GAAP presentation. Management has read the demand environment correctly and is reweighting the portfolio toward technical industrial work with FloWorks, which deepens the capability that defends a distributor against supplier disintermediation and agentic procurement.

Against that, three facts constrain the near-term return. Organic volume in the first half of calendar 2026 was negative, with reported growth coming from price and acquisitions — lower-quality growth than fiscal 2025's volume-led expansion. The EPS growth that supported the current multiple was substantially tax-rate normalization and share retirement, and pro forma leverage near 1.8x now limits the repurchase lever.
