**Research Date: June 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Biogen agreed to acquire Apellis for $41.00 per share in cash (approximately $5.6 billion), announced on or around March 31, 2026 at a roughly 140% premium to the prior close. The price history makes the outcome unambiguous: APLS gapped from $17.09 on March 30 to $40.23 on March 31 on 90.7 million shares of volume, then traded in a tight band between $40.2 and $41.1 for the following six weeks. The company profile states Apellis "operates as a subsidiary of Biogen Inc." as of May 14, 2026, and the final two price observations (May 14–15) show zero volume at a flat $41.03. The transaction has effectively closed or is at the final mechanical step.

At a current price of $41.03 against a $41.00 cash consideration, the merger spread is negative by three cents. There is no remaining arbitrage return, and any residual position is simply awaiting cash settlement. The analyst price target cluster (low $40.00, high $41.00, mean $40.92) confirms the Street is modeling the deal price, not a fundamental valuation. Beta of -0.25 is an artifact of a deal-pinned stock decoupled from the broad market, not a hedging characteristic.

The standalone fundamentals that justified the takeout are strong: revenue grew roughly 13x in three years to $1.0 billion in 2025, the year delivered Apellis's first GAAP profit (+$22.4 million net income, +$45 million operating cash flow), and pegcetacoplan holds category-creating positions in geographic atrophy (SYFOVRE, >60% share) and rare nephrology (EMPAVELI, approved for C3G and IC-MPGN in July 2025). Those facts explain why Biogen paid up. They do not create incremental value for a public holder at $41.03. This is a HOLD to close with an uncertain rationale only because there is no fundamental forward path left to assess; the equity has been resolved into a fixed cash claim.

## Company Overview

Apellis is a commercial-stage biopharmaceutical company headquartered in Waltham, Massachusetts, with 733 employees, built around a single molecule, pegcetacoplan, a targeted C3 complement inhibitor sold under two brand names:

- **SYFOVRE** (intravitreal pegcetacoplan), the first FDA-approved therapy for geographic atrophy secondary to age-related macular degeneration. This is the largest revenue line (~$130M in Q1 2025), with overall market share above 60% and new-patient share above 50%. Differentiation rests on every-other-month dosing flexibility and five-year GALE data (reported April 2026) showing a roughly 1.5-year delay in GA lesion growth versus sham.
- **EMPAVELI / ASPAVELI** (systemic pegcetacoplan), approved for paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria and, as of July 2025, for C3 glomerulopathy and immune-complex membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis. Ex-US systemic rights are partnered with Sobi.

The pipeline includes APL-3007 (an siRNA for GA), APL-9099 (an FcRn-targeting program), and a base-editing collaboration with Beam Therapeutics for complement-driven diseases. None of these standalone assets is relevant to the current investment decision because the company has been acquired.

## Growth Analysis

Standalone, the growth narrative had two engines: continued GA share retention against Astellas's Izervay, and the nephrology expansion (C3G/IC-MPGN) following the July 2025 approvals. The hidden swing factor on the larger franchise was co-pay assistance underfunding, which depressed commercial SYFOVRE doses, forced reliance on free samples, and led management to withhold SYFOVRE sales guidance entirely. That dynamic created genuine forward visibility risk on a standalone basis.

For the current decision, growth prospects are moot. The equity value has been fixed at $41.00 cash. Whatever the franchise's organic growth rate would have been now accrues to Biogen, which is acquiring the asset specifically to diversify away from its declining multiple sclerosis base into commercialized immunology and rare-disease revenue. The 6.0 growth score reflects the quality of the underlying business that supported the deal, not any return available to a buyer of the stock today.

## Competitive Landscape

Apellis is the category creator in two complement-driven diseases: first-to-market in GA with SYFOVRE and a differentiated C3 mechanism (versus C5 or factor-B approaches) in PNH and rare nephrology. Competitive pressure comes from Astellas's Izervay in GA and Novartis's Fabhalta (iptacopan), which reached C3G approval ahead of EMPAVELI. Management argues EMPAVELI's C3G efficacy is roughly twice the competitor's.

The competitive position is the clearest justification for the acquisition: the complement space is a strategic prize for large-cap immunology and rare-disease players, and Biogen paid a large premium to own a leading franchise it could not easily build. The 7.0 competitive score recognizes durable category leadership, tempered by single-molecule concentration and active competitive encroachment.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risks in any standalone Apellis story — single-molecule dependence on pegcetacoplan, co-pay funding shortfalls, GA safety overhang from the 2023 retinal-vasculitis concern, channel/inventory noise, and competitive entry — have been superseded by the merger. The only material risk to a current holder is deal completion, and the available evidence points strongly to the transaction being closed or at its final step:

- The company profile states Apellis operates as a Biogen subsidiary as of May 14, 2026.
- Price action shows a flat $41.03 with zero volume on May 14–15, the signature of a stock that has stopped trading on fundamentals and is awaiting cash settlement.
- Analyst targets are pinned near the deal price (mean $40.92, median $41.00).

Residual deal-break risk (antitrust, financing, material-adverse-change clauses) would, if it materialized, return the stock toward its pre-announcement level near $17, a roughly 59% downside. Given the subsidiary disclosure, that probability is low, but it is the only scenario in which the stock moves materially, which is why the risk score sits at a neutral 5.5 rather than higher.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case
The deal closes (or has closed) at $41.00 cash, holders receive consideration, and the position resolves cleanly. There is no upside beyond the $41.00 cash plus any de minimis spread. The "bull case" is simply collecting the deal price.

### Bear Case
The acquisition breaks — an antitrust block, financing failure, or material-adverse-change invocation — and the stock reverts toward its unaffected pre-announcement price near $17, a downside of roughly 59% from $41.03. The subsidiary disclosure and zero-volume terminal trading make this a low-probability tail.

The asymmetry is unattractive in both directions: capped upside at the cash price, large but low-probability downside on a break, the defining characteristic of a fully resolved merger situation.
