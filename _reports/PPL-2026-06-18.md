**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

PPL Corporation is a fully U.S.-regulated electric and gas utility holding company operating across Kentucky, Pennsylvania, and Rhode Island. Following its 2021 UK exit and 2022 acquisition of Rhode Island Energy, it is a cleaner, pure-play regulated business pursuing the classic rate-base growth model: invest capital in grid and generation, earn an authorized ROE (9.6%–11.3% across jurisdictions), and recover costs through rate cases and rider mechanisms. The company sits in the dominant utility theme of the decade, electricity demand growth from data centers and AI load, with active large-load pipelines in both its PJM (Pennsylvania) and Kentucky territories.

The central tension is financial, not strategic. Q1 2026 revenue grew 10.8% to $2,774M and EPS rose to $0.60, but operating cash flow of $557M covered barely half of the $1,058M capex. PPL is in a heavy investment cycle, generates structurally negative free cash flow, and funds the gap with debt and equity. Interest expense rose 18% YoY as the company refinances and adds leverage into a higher-for-longer rate environment, and recent financing actions — a $1.15B equity units issuance (February 2026, up to ~30.9M shares) and a $2B ATM program (~27.4M forward shares) — dilute per-share growth. This is the mechanism the market is discounting: the load-growth opportunity is real, but the share count keeps rising and the cost of capital keeps climbing.

At ~21.6x earnings and a ~3.1% dividend yield, PPL trades at a modest discount to the 22.1x peer median but does not offer asymmetric upside. The data-center thesis is now visible in regulated-utility hard data across the sector, which supports the growth case, but PPL's specific conversion is muddied by a contested Kentucky rate settlement (LG&E/KU withdrew and filed for rehearing in March 2026), Rhode Island segment underperformance, and Pennsylvania regulators actively structuring large-load tariffs to shield ordinary ratepayers from data-center costs. We rate PPL a HOLD on an income basis: a durable mid-single-digit EPS compounder with a growing dividend, priced fairly for what it delivers.

## Company Overview

PPL operates three regulated geographic segments:

- **Kentucky Regulated (LG&E + KU)** — the largest segment at roughly $19.2B of assets, and the only vertically integrated one, with regulated generation (including coal), transmission, distribution, and gas distribution. Q1 2026 segment net income was $270M.
- **Pennsylvania Regulated (PPL Electric)** — transmission and distribution only, with no owned generation; it buys power as Provider of Last Resort. Roughly $17.2B of assets, Q1 2026 net income $184M.
- **Rhode Island Regulated (Rhode Island Energy)** — electric T&D plus gas distribution, acquired in 2022. Roughly $7.6B of assets, Q1 2026 net income $36M, down from $70M a year earlier.

The competitive moat is the regulated monopoly franchise in each service territory. Earnings depend on the interplay of five regulators (PAPUC, KPSC, RIPUC, VSCC, and FERC), so regulatory outcomes are the dominant driver of returns, and they are currently mixed rather than uniformly favorable.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and earnings.** Operating revenue rose to $2,774M from $2,504M, driven by new Kentucky base rates (effective January 1, 2026), higher Pennsylvania PLR and transmission revenue, and gas. Net income was $452M versus $414M (+9.2%), with ongoing operating EPS roughly $0.63. FY2025 net income of $1,181M caps a steady recovery from the post-UK-sale trough: $756M (2022), $740M (2023), $888M (2024), $1,181M (2025).

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Capex of $1,058M was up sharply from $793M a year earlier. Total debt is roughly $20.2B against equity of $15.0B and assets of $46.3B. Interest expense rose to $224M as debt grows and refinances at higher rates. The Altman Z-Score screens near 1.03 ("distress"), but that reading is not meaningful for a capital-intensive regulated utility — the model penalizes leverage that is intrinsic to and supported by the rate-regulated model, and PPL's investment-grade ratings have been unchanged since June 2023.

**Capital allocation and dilution.** The quarterly dividend is $0.285 ($1.14 annualized), a ~3.1% yield at the current price. Funding the growth program requires continuous equity issuance via the February 2026 equity units (settling 2029) and the ATM program (at ~$35.90). Share-count growth is the principal headwind to per-share earnings, and it is the reason a credible rate-base growth story translates into only mid-single-digit EPS compounding.

**Earnings quality and surprise record.** The track record is mixed: 2 beats and 2 misses over the last four quarters, including a sharp 17.1% miss in Q2 2025. Estimate revisions are flat — current-year consensus at $1.95 has not moved in 90 days, and next-year at $2.12 is similarly stable. There is no positive revision velocity to lean on, which removes one of the strongest signals that would otherwise support a more constructive call.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis rests on data-center and large-load demand, which is now confirmed across the regulated-utility sector rather than being a single-company story. In Pennsylvania, industrial demand could nearly double in parts of the state over a decade; in Kentucky, the new Extremely High Load Factor Tariff (with minimum revenue commitments, collateral, and long-term contracts) is designed to capture this load while protecting the balance sheet. PPL's structural advantage is that it can rate-base the transmission and, in Kentucky, the generation needed to serve this demand, which extends the multi-year rate-base growth runway.

PPL is building generation in-house in Kentucky and recovering it through a new Pilot Generation Recovery (PGR) mechanism — Mill Creek Unit 5/6, the Brown battery and Unit 12, and Mercer/Marion solar. We treat the reported Blackstone generation JV as unconfirmed; primary filings show PPL building rate-based generation itself, which is the verifiable strategy.

The constraint on the growth case is twofold. First, dilution: rate-base growth in the high single digits converts to EPS growth in the mid single digits because the share count keeps rising. Second, regulatory capture of the upside: Pennsylvania regulators are explicitly moving to shield ordinary ratepayers from data-center costs, which could route the load growth into separate large-load tariffs that accrue less of the upside to PPL than a straightforward rate-base build would. The company itself flags rapid data-center demand growth as a risk vector — capital-access, revenue-recovery, and customer-affordability uncertainties — not just an opportunity. Strong EPS growth targets among the best-positioned regulated-utility peers are plausible here, but PPL's near-term delivery is clouded by the contested Kentucky outcome.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$35.38 and 21.6x trailing/forward earnings, PPL trades modestly below the 22.1x peer median. The peer set is dispersed: FirstEnergy at 25.4x, Eversource at 14.6x, Edison International at 7.9x (carrying wildfire-liability discount), Evergy at 22.1x, Alliant at 22.8x. The peer median yield is 4.0%; PPL's ~3.1% is below it, reflecting a lower payout ratio that leaves room for dividend growth but offers less current income support.

There is no earnings-inflection signal that would warrant cycle-adjusted treatment — revenue growth is ~11% but estimate revisions are flat and the surprise record is mixed, so the trailing multiple is the right lens. The modest discount to peers is not, on its own, a buy signal; in regulated utilities a cheaper multiple typically reflects a balance-sheet or regulatory drag, and here it reflects exactly that: negative FCF, heavy dilution, and an open Kentucky dispute. The analyst price-target range ($36 low, $42 median, $48 high, $41.20 mean) implies meaningful upside to the mean, but those targets embed clean execution of the rate cases and the data-center build that has not yet fully materialized.

We view fair value as roughly $36–$40 on $1.95–$2.12 of forward EPS at ~18–19x, consistent with the company's growth and risk profile. The stock is fairly priced for a mid-single-digit compounder with a 3.1% yield, which supports a HOLD rather than a value-driven BUY. We deliberately resist treating the cheaper-than-peer multiple as asymmetric upside, because the discount is doing real work pricing the financing drag.

## Competitive Landscape

PPL's competitive position is stable-to-improving on the demand side and structurally insulated by regulated franchises. The data-center load growth is a genuine tailwind, and PPL's footprint in PJM (Pennsylvania) and Kentucky places it directly in the path of large-load interconnection demand. The Kentucky vertical-integration model lets PPL rate-base generation, a lever that pure T&D operators lack.

The differentiator across regulated peers is the cleanliness of the load-growth-to-rate-base conversion. The strongest names in the sector are showing confirmed superior load growth converting into approved rate base. PPL has the load opportunity but a messier conversion: the Kentucky settlement broke down, Pennsylvania regulators are structuring large-load tariffs that may cap the upside, and Rhode Island is absorbing an ISO-NE base-ROE cut (FERC Opinion 594 → 9.57%, a $26M refund liability) plus ~$155M of hold-harmless customer credits owed in 2027-2028 from the acquisition. The franchise moat is durable; the trajectory of converting demand into recoverable rate base is the variable to watch, and it currently sits a notch below the cleanest peers.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Financing and dilution risk.** Negative FCF requires continuous debt and equity issuance. A 10% adverse rate move equates to a ~$734M debt fair-value impact, and ongoing ATM/equity-unit issuance dilutes EPS. In a higher-for-longer rate path, this is the single largest drag on per-share returns.
2. **Multi-jurisdiction regulatory risk.** Earnings depend on five regulators simultaneously. The Kentucky rate settlement broke down in March 2026 (LG&E/KU withdrew and sought rehearing), an open and contested situation that could compress the realized increase. Pennsylvania (PAPUC ruling expected Q2 2026) and Rhode Island (ruling expected Q3 2026) cases are pending.
3. **Data-center load as a double-edged sword.** Rapid demand growth creates capital-access, revenue-recovery, and affordability risks. If regulators route load into protective large-load tariffs, PPL captures less of the upside than a straightforward rate-base build would deliver.
4. **Kentucky coal/environmental exposure.** LG&E/KU still operate coal generation; CCR, ELG, MATS, and GHG rules create ARO and capex obligations. The current EPA deregulatory posture is a near-term tailwind, and costs are generally rate-recoverable, but the exposure remains.
5. **Rhode Island underperformance.** The smallest segment is absorbing the ISO-NE ROE cut, the hold-harmless credits, and a politically sensitive 36% gas distribution increase request — a persistent earnings soft spot.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and offers no directional edge. Near-term ATM IV is 18.9% against 1-year historical vol of 17.1% — fairly priced, with IV in only the 4th percentile of the current chain. Put/call skew is balanced (1.4%), the put/call volume ratio is a low 0.09, and the term structure is flat. The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±2.5%. The market is not pricing a near-term catalyst or elevated downside, consistent with the low-beta, income-oriented nature of the name. The lone anomaly is an elevated 31.2% IV reading on the August expiry, likely reflecting thin open interest around the Pennsylvania/Rhode Island rate-case resolution windows rather than informed positioning.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Data-center load drives a multi-year rate-base growth runway; PPL converts Pennsylvania and Kentucky large-load demand into approved, recoverable rate base; the Pennsylvania and Rhode Island rate cases land favorably; and a re-rating toward the high end of the peer range plus dividend growth produces a total return in the low-to-mid teens. Bull target ~$45–$48 (roughly 21x ~$2.12 EPS with sentiment recovery), aligned with the high analyst target.

**Bear case.** Higher-for-longer rates compound the negative-FCF/dilution drag; the Kentucky dispute compresses the realized increase; Pennsylvania large-load tariffs cap the data-center upside; and Rhode Island remains a persistent earnings sink. Mid-single-digit EPS growth gets diluted toward low-single-digit per-share growth, and the multiple compresses toward Eversource-style levels. Bear target ~$31–$33 (roughly 16x on flat-to-modest EPS), about 7-12% below current.

The base case sits between: a mid-single-digit EPS compounder yielding ~3.1%, delivering high-single-digit total returns with a growing dividend, fairly priced today.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** PPL is worth owning for its dividend and steady cash flows, not for asymmetric appreciation. The dividend is covered and growing, and the regulated franchise provides downside stability, but per-share growth is capped by dilution and the financing cycle.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the PAPUC ruling (expected Q2 2026), the Rhode Island ruling (expected Q3 2026), and the resolution of the Kentucky rehearing. The next quarterly earnings release is the practical reassessment point.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Kentucky rate dispute resolves favorably AND Pennsylvania/Rhode Island cases land at or above the requested increases, with confirmed large-load rate-base additions.
- Consensus EPS estimates begin rising (>5% over 90 days) on data-center-driven rate-base growth, with the financing plan visibly de-risked (less ATM reliance, stable share count).
- Price compresses toward $31–$32 with fundamentals intact, restoring a ~3.5%+ yield and a clearer margin of safety.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A dividend coverage problem emerges, or the company signals a dividend freeze to fund capex.
- Kentucky outcome deteriorates materially (ROE cut or significant disallowance) and Pennsylvania large-load tariffs are structured to exclude PPL from the data-center upside.
- A credit-rating downgrade or evidence that equity issuance is accelerating beyond planned levels, structurally diluting per-share growth toward low single digits.

## Conclusion

PPL is a competently run, pure-play regulated utility with a genuine multi-year growth opportunity from data-center load and a covered, growing dividend. The price decline from the April high near $40 to ~$35 has improved the entry point, but it has not changed the core arithmetic: structurally negative FCF, ongoing dilution, rising interest expense into a higher-for-longer rate path, a contested Kentucky settlement, and a Rhode Island soft spot together explain why the stock has lagged the utility group despite record 2025 earnings.

PPL is fairly valued for a mid-single-digit EPS compounder. The discount is doing legitimate work pricing the financing drag and regulatory ambiguity, so the cheaper multiple is not a value signal. We rate PPL a HOLD on an income basis — own it for the dividend and dividend growth, and look for a cleaner regulatory conversion or a more attractive entry point near $31–$32 before upgrading.
