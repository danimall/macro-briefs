## Executive Summary

Supernus is no longer principally a standalone equity. On August 3, 2026 the company announced a definitive all-stock merger with Indivior Pharmaceuticals at a fixed ratio of 1.5401 Indivior shares per Supernus share. Immediately before closing, Indivior stockholders — and only Indivior stockholders — receive a one-time $1.0 billion special cash dividend (approximately $8.00 per Indivior share on the 125 million basic count), funded by a $650 million Citibank term loan plus cash of the combined company. Supernus holders receive no cash and end up with 43.5% of the combined entity on a fully diluted basis. Owning SUPN today is a bet on that transaction closing, on Indivior's SUBLOCADE franchise, and on a $125 million cost-synergy target, in roughly that order.

The Q2 print released the same morning is where the standalone case weakens. Total revenue rose 32.4% to $219.1 million, but of the $53.6 million increase, $35.4 million (65.9%) was the ZURZUVAE collaboration line acquired with Sage Therapeutics on July 31, 2025 — zero in the comparable period — and $10.5 million (19.7%) was royalty, licensing and other revenue. Net product sales grew $7.7 million, or 14.4% of the increase, because $24.4 million of growth-product gains was nearly two-thirds absorbed by $16.7 million of legacy erosion. Adjusted operating earnings *fell* 23.7% to $31.2 million on that 32% revenue increase, taking adjusted operating margin from 24.7% to 14.2%. The $54.9 million APOKYN intangible impairment is excluded from the adjusted figure and explains none of that compression. Management raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $860–890 million and adjusted operating earnings to $150–180 million, while cutting GAAP operating guidance by $50 million at both ends to a loss of $20–50 million.

At $43.25, Supernus's fully diluted equity of roughly $2.70 billion implies combined post-close equity of about $6.21 billion (÷43.5%) and an enterprise value near $7.09 billion against the stated $878 million pro forma net debt. That is 8.0x the advertised $888 million pro forma adjusted EBITDA and 9.3x the $763 million figure excluding unrealized synergies. Neither multiple is demanding for a business with an 80% gross-margin lead asset and sub-1.2x leverage. Neither is cheap enough to compensate for a Qelbree generic cliff with a hard October 2, 2028 date, a treble-damages antitrust trial beginning January 25, 2027 with insurers disputing coverage, an SEC enforcement subpoena inherited with the Sage transaction, and merger deal-protection terms — including the termination fee Supernus may owe — that are not yet publicly quantified. The prior three reports scored 6.2, 6.0 and 6.0 with HOLD; the step down to 5.3 reflects identified fundamental changes (margin compression, the GAAP guidance cut, a new merger binary, a dated patent cliff), not the price decline from the $48.04 level at the July review.

## Company Overview

Supernus is a Rockville, Maryland commercial-stage CNS specialty pharmaceutical company with 778 employees at year-end 2025, up from 674. The portfolio spans ADHD, Parkinson's dyskinesia and hypomobility, postpartum depression, epilepsy, migraine, cervical dystonia and chronic sialorrhea. It is a formulation-and-commercialization business rather than a discovery house, and the pipeline reflects that: SPN-817 (Phase 2b, treatment-resistant focal seizures), SPN-820 (Phase 2b, MDD) and SPN-443 (Phase 1 initiating H2 2026). There is no disclosed near-term registrational catalyst.

Q2 2026 revenue splits into three layers. The four growth products: **Qelbree** $89.2 million (+15%; 264,545 IQVIA prescriptions, +17%, with adult prescriptions +25% versus pediatric +14%) at 40.7% of total revenue; **GOCOVRI** $37.6 million (+2% on +9% prescriptions, indicating net-price erosion); **ZURZUVAE** $35.35 million of collaboration revenue, defined as 50% of Biogen's recorded net revenue; and **ONAPGO** $13.5 million versus $1.6 million, launched April 2025. The eroding layer — Trokendi XR $8.4 million (−25%), Oxtellar XR $8.8 million (−25%), APOKYN $6.3 million (−51%) and other $1.9 million (−71%) — fell to $25.4 million, or 11.6% of revenue, from $42.1 million and 25.4% a year earlier. Royalty, licensing and other revenue was $18.0 million, +141%.

Post-merger the entity will be named Supernus, Inc., trade as SUPN on Nasdaq and be headquartered in Rockville, with Indivior as the legal issuer. CEO Jack Khattar leads the combined company and Timothy Dec continues as CFO; Indivior's Tony Kingsley becomes Board Chair of an eight-member board split four/four. Operational control tilts to the Supernus team, board control is deadlocked, and economic control sits with Indivior holders at 56.5%.

## Financial Analysis

**Standalone balance sheet is genuinely strong.** At June 30, 2026: cash and marketable securities $372.1 million ($180.0 million plus $192.1 million), up from $308.7 million at year-end 2025 with the increase attributed to operations. Current assets $723.5 million against current liabilities $347.4 million gives a 2.08x current ratio. There is no funded debt line. Total liabilities $396.9 million against equity of $1,031.2 million. Goodwill plus intangibles of $588.9 million equals 57% of book equity, leaving tangible book of roughly $442 million, or $7.60 per share.

Three balance-sheet movements remain unexplained and should be treated as open items, not as evidence of anything: interest expense of $2.315 million appeared in Q2 2026 (first ever) with no debt balance visible at June 30, following a May 20, 2026 8-K filed under Item 2.03 (Creation of a Direct Financial Obligation); goodwill declined $5.451 million with no impairment line, plausibly a Sage measurement-period adjustment; and current contingent consideration went from $31.1 million to zero, against live litigation over a withheld $27.7 million milestone.

**The income statement tells a harder story.** FY2025 revenue was $719.0 million (+8.6%), but GAAP operating income swung to −$62.3 million from +$81.7 million as SG&A rose 51% from $321.6 million to $485.6 million — 67.5% of revenue. In Q2 2026, SG&A rose 42.8% to $133.6 million, or 61.0% of revenue versus 56.5% a year earlier. Management attributes the increase to "the collaboration agreement with Biogen Inc." without quantifying the split.

That attribution supports an uncomfortable inference: Supernus books 50% of Biogen's ZURZUVAE net revenue while carrying promotional cost in its own SG&A, so the collaboration may currently be near operating breakeven or dilutive at the operating line. The alternative explanation is at least as plausible and more favorable — the SG&A step-up also reflects the ONAPGO infusion launch (nursing and field support) and continued Qelbree adult promotion, both investment-phase spend. The company does not disaggregate, and the collaboration cost-sharing terms are the single highest-value unread document in the standalone case. I hold the inference open rather than scoring on it.

**Guidance shape is the testable claim.** H1 2026 adjusted operating earnings were $60.0 million on H1 revenue of roughly $426.8 million (14.1% margin). FY guidance of $150–180 million implies H2 of $90–120 million, midpoint $105 million, on implied H2 revenue of $448.2 million — a ~23.4% H2 adjusted margin, roughly 930bps above H1. That is management asserting the spend is front-loaded. A bottom-up H2 reconstruction (Qelbree ~$190 million on school-year seasonality, GOCOVRI ~$76 million, ONAPGO ~$40 million, legacy ~$34 million, ZURZUVAE collaboration ~$78 million, royalty/other ~$30 million) sums to approximately $448 million, so the revenue guide is internally coherent. The margin guide is the stretch, and the Q3 print is the test.

Two guidance components warrant separate treatment. ONAPGO guidance of $55–70 million against H1 actual of $21.9 million requires H2 of $33.1–48.1 million; against a Q2 run rate of $13.5 million the low end needs ~+23% per-quarter stepping and the high end roughly +78% versus Q2. Conversely, Trokendi plus Oxtellar guidance of $50–60 million against H1 actual of $34.1 million implies H2 of only $15.9–25.9 million, below a Q2 run-rate annualization — this line is guided conservatively.

**Quality-of-revenue flag.** Royalty, licensing and other was approximately $29.3 million in Q1 2026 versus $7.8 million in Q1 2025, then $18.0 million versus $7.5 million in Q2. The ~$21.5 million Q1 excess is unexplained in available disclosure and plausibly a one-time licensing milestone. At 8–11% of revenue this line is now material, and a large component is royalties on products that have already gone generic — structurally finite.

**Earnings-basis caution.** Trailing GAAP EPS of −$0.68 and the resulting negative P/E of −22.6 are mechanically uninformative given the $54.9 million impairment and roughly $51 million of six-month intangible amortization. The four-quarter surprise record (beats of 67.8% to 142.2%) is on a non-GAAP basis and predates the Q2 print; the Q2 result itself is characterized inconsistently in secondary coverage (one outlet reporting an in-line result, another a shortfall), with neither specifying GAAP versus non-GAAP consensus. I treat the beat/miss framing as unresolved and rely on the reported figures.

## The Merger: What Supernus Holders Actually Receive

Verified terms: tax-free all-stock merger; exchange ratio 1.5401 Indivior shares per Supernus share, **fixed with no adjustment for share-price movement in either direction**; $1.0 billion special cash dividend to Indivior stockholders only, funded by a $650 million Citibank term loan plus combined-company cash; post-close ownership 56.5% Indivior / 43.5% Supernus fully diluted; $125 million expected annual cost synergies; pro forma net revenue approximately $2.2 billion, pro forma adjusted EBITDA $888 million including synergies, pro forma net debt approximately $878 million; target close Q4 2026 subject to both stockholder votes and regulatory approvals.

**Ownership arithmetic.** 58.164 million Supernus shares × 1.5401 = 89.58 million new Indivior shares. Against Indivior's 125 million shares that is 41.7%. The stated 43.5% solves to roughly 62.5 million Supernus fully diluted shares, consistent with the 6.34 million options (weighted-average exercise price $32.63) under the treasury method plus units. The gap is ownership dilution from Supernus's own equity awards on vesting and exercise, not an additional issuance to Indivior; a basic-share holder's economic claim is nearer 41.7% than 43.5%.

**Implied values at the $43.25 basis.** $43.25 ÷ 1.5401 = $28.08 implied ex-dividend Indivior value per share; adding the $8.00 dividend gives roughly $36.08 cum-dividend, or approximately $4.5 billion of Indivior equity value. Combined post-close equity implied by Supernus's price is $2.70 billion ÷ 43.5% = $6.21 billion, enterprise value $7.09 billion, so 8.0x the $888 million synergy-inclusive EBITDA and 9.3x the $763 million ex-synergy figure. I could not verify Indivior's live share price or current share count (it operates a buyback), so the precise merger-arbitrage spread is not computable here — a genuine gap.

**Leverage headline requires one adjustment.** $878 million ÷ $888 million = 0.989x supports the "<1x" claim, but only with $125 million of unrealized synergies in the denominator. Ex-synergies: $878 million ÷ $763 million = 1.15x. Both are modest; the point is labeling. My own bottom-up net-debt reconstruction (Supernus net cash $372.1 million at 6/30/26, Indivior $333 million term loan against $222 million cash at 12/31/25, plus a $650 million draw and a $1.0 billion dividend) yields roughly $739 million versus the stated $878 million. The ~$139 million gap most likely reflects the period mismatch and the release's explicit exclusion of transaction costs, financing fees and costs-to-achieve. Neither figure should be treated as precise.

**Structural read, stated literally.** Supernus contributes $372.1 million of the roughly $594 million combined pre-deal liquid asset pool (63%) and carries no funded debt; Indivior holders receive $8.00 per share in cash plus 56.5% of the combined equity; Supernus's own 10-Q discloses it expects "a significant reduction" in its cash and securities to fund part of that dividend. Supernus's principal non-operating contribution is unlevered balance-sheet capacity, and its holders are compensated for it solely through the exchange ratio. The ratio was negotiated with the dividend fully known, and SUBLOCADE is a larger, higher-margin, more cash-generative franchise than anything Supernus owns. The evidence that would settle adequacy is the fairness opinions and exchange-ratio analysis in the forthcoming S-4, which are unread. I draw no inference about management motive from the structure.

**What Indivior brings.** FY2025 net revenue $1,239 million (+4%); SUBLOCADE $856 million (+13%, of which management discloses 7 points from dispense-unit volume and 6 points from gross-to-net adjustments) at 69% of revenue; sublingual and other $351 million (−7%); 80% gross margin. Net income rose to $210 million from $7 million, but that swing is largely explained by litigation settlement expense collapsing to $3 million from $195 million, and EPS of $1.64 was flattered by a 12% effective tax rate (including $45 million tied to a one-time royalty payment) and by $87 million of prior-period rebate accrual releases running through revenue — roughly a third of reported operating income. FY2025 operating cash flow was **negative $27 million** against $210 million of net income, with capex of $66 million giving FCF of −$93 million; management attributes it to $208 million of higher litigation settlement payments including voluntary prepayment of the remaining DOJ liability, which normalizes underlying OCF to roughly +$181 million and FCF to roughly +$115 million.

Indivior's balance sheet is the mirror image of Supernus's: **stockholders' equity of negative $98 million**, current liabilities exceeding current assets by roughly $262 million (0.71x current ratio), a $333 million secured term loan maturing November 2030 with substantially all assets pledged and covenants restricting dividends and restricted payments, and approximately $86 million of civil opioid litigation payments expected over four years that are deliberately excluded from the contractual obligations table.

Indivior's own 10-K states that 2026 total net revenue "is expected to decline compared to 2025." The $2.2 billion combined revenue headline therefore pairs Supernus's forward guidance ($875 million midpoint) with a trailing Indivior figure the company itself guides lower. Combined near-term revenue growth is Supernus's ~+22% partially offset by Indivior shrinkage, not additive.

## Growth Analysis

Score: **5.4**. Three offsetting forces.

Supporting growth: ZURZUVAE is the fastest-growing asset (Biogen-reported US sales +53%, prescriptions +62%) in a category with essentially no branded competition; Qelbree adult prescriptions +25% into the larger, less-penetrated segment; the legacy drag has largely worked through (11.6% of revenue from 25.4%), which mechanically improves organic comparisons in FY2027; consensus estimates are rising (current year $2.67 from $2.34 ninety days ago, +14.3%; next year $4.63 from $4.22, +9.7%; both up again over thirty days).

Constraining growth: organic net product sales grew 5–6%, not 32%; the acquired half of the combination guides revenue down in 2026; SUBLOCADE's LAI share has "stabilized in the mid-seventy percent range" rather than expanding, and SUBOXONE Film's oral share fell from 16% to 14%; the pipeline is Phase 1–2b with no registrational event; and terminal value is capped by dated generic exposure.

The estimate-revision signal is positive but does not clear the threshold that would override valuation and risk concerns: +14.3% on the current year is well short of the +30% velocity associated with genuine earnings inflection, coverage is two analysts, and analyst counts are shrinking (six recommendations to four, strong-buys 1 to 0, with the data source characterizing sentiment as degrading).

## Valuation Assessment

Score: **5.5**. Cheap on multiples, for identifiable reasons.

On the $43.25 basis: 16.2x current-year consensus of $2.67 and 9.3x next-year $4.63 (standalone estimates that will require restatement to the combined share basis). Pharmaceutical peers in the comparable set trade at a 14.3x median P/E (PBH 12.2x, OGN 14.3x, ANIP 20.9x, INVA 3.9x, PRGO negative) — a heterogeneous group of cash-generative, low-growth, patent-exposed small caps, which is precisely the cohort Supernus is joining.

The more relevant frame is the combined entity. Building adjusted EPS per Supernus-equivalent share for the first full year post-close: combined adjusted EBITDA of roughly $820 million (the $763 million ex-synergy base, plus 55–60% synergy realization, net of Indivior revenue decline), less approximately $55 million of non-acquisition D&A, less roughly $59 million of net interest on $983 million gross debt, taxed at 17% given Supernus's $2.5 billion of federal NOLs (subject to Section 382/383 limitation) and Indivior's low structural rate, gives roughly $586 million of net income on 214.6 million shares — $2.73 per Indivior share, or **$4.20 per Supernus-equivalent share** at the 1.5401 ratio. At $43.25 that is 10.3x.

Ten to eleven times adjusted earnings on a business with sub-1.2x leverage and an 80% gross-margin lead asset looks reasonable, not a mispricing that requires correcting. Three things argue against paying up: the $888 million EBITDA construct is heavily adjusted and sits far above distributable cash (Indivior's normalized FCF is roughly $115 million; Supernus's adjusted operating earnings guidance midpoint is $165 million); Qelbree, at 41% of Q2 revenue, has a 30-month stay expiring October 2, 2028; and the January 2027 antitrust trial carries trebled, unquantified damages with insurers contesting coverage.

Consensus targets of $55–67 (mean $62.5, median $64.0) imply 12–14x next-year standalone EPS. I treat them as stale rather than as a floor: they predate or only partially reflect a transaction that changes the share basis, the analyst count is falling, and Supernus does not fit the profile in which targets lag reality (accelerating growth plus a compressed multiple). Prior appreciation is not part of this assessment; the stock is 27.5% below its February 25 high of $59.68 on the authoritative basis, and that decline is not itself evidence of value.

## Competitive Landscape

Score: **5.4** — trajectory mixed, with the two contested arenas mattering most.

**ADHD non-stimulants.** Qelbree's adult skew is the right kind of growth. But five-year NCE exclusivity lapsed and at least nine to ten generic filers sent Paragraph IV notices between May and November 2025 (Appco/Somerset, Aurobindo, Apotex, Zydus, Creekwood, MSN, Zenara/Biophore, Macleods twice), consolidated as *In re Viloxazine* in D.N.J. The field size confirms the asset is worth attacking.

**Parkinson's.** The most contested position. ONAPGO competes directly with AbbVie's Vyalev (continuous foscarbidopa/foslevodopa infusion, launched 2024), a far better-resourced competitor in the same niche. GOCOVRI's +9% prescriptions against +2% revenue is net-price erosion. APOKYN's −51% decline drove the impairment, though ONAPGO and APOKYN deliver the same molecule, so part of that is intentional internal substitution.

**Postpartum depression.** ZURZUVAE has no branded competition, but Supernus captures 50% of net revenue while bearing substantial promotional cost.

**Opioid use disorder (acquired).** Structurally the most attractive market in the combination: the US LAI segment grew high-teens against mid-single-digit growth for the overall buprenorphine MAT market, and SUBLOCADE holds mid-70% LAI share at 80% gross margin. The qualifications matter — share has stabilized rather than expanded, 6 of 13 points of 2025 US SUBLOCADE growth came from gross-to-net rather than volume, and 28% of Indivior revenue (SUBOXONE Film) is in structural decline.

The footprints are complementary rather than overlapping (psychiatry/neurology alongside addiction medicine), which limits revenue synergy but supports the $125 million cost target through corporate-function consolidation and pipeline rationalization. Both companies are simultaneously guiding R&D down, and management's stated aim of "flexibility to pursue additional business development opportunities" positions the combined entity as an acquisition platform rather than an R&D engine.

## Risk Assessment

Score: **4.2** — the lowest dimension, and the reason this is not a BUY.

1. **Qelbree patent cliff, dated.** ~41% of Q2 revenue. The 30-month stay bars ANDA approval until **October 2, 2028** — roughly 2.2 years of protected runway. Three Orange Book patents expire September 4, 2029; the strongest expire 2033 and 2035. Fact discovery closes November 17, 2027 with **no trial date set**, which makes a negotiated settlement with defined early-entry dates a realistic base case, as happened with Oxtellar XR and Trokendi XR. Terminal value should not be modeled to 2033. Mitigant: Supernus won at district court against Torrent on Trokendi XR before settling on appeal, so its formulation patents have survived one full merits test.

2. **APOKYN antitrust trial, January 25, 2027.** *Sage Chemical / TruPharma v. Supernus, Britannia, USWM* (D. Del. 22-cv-1302; note the plaintiff is unrelated to Sage Therapeutics). Motions to dismiss were denied as to Supernus in May 2024. Dispositive and Daubert hearing September 18, 2026; pretrial conference January 15, 2027. Antitrust damages are trebled and the company states it cannot estimate a range of loss. Compounding this, **Supernus is in coverage litigation against four insurers** (Federal, RSUI, StarStone, Old Republic), which raises net retained exposure. Whether any adverse outcome is contemplated in the $878 million pro forma net debt is not disclosed.

3. **Merger execution and break risk.** The exchange ratio is fixed with no collar, so Supernus holders bear full Indivior price risk between now and close. A Supernus-payable termination fee exists but **the amount, outside date, no-shop and fiduciary-out terms are unverified**. Interim covenants restrict business development, debt and acquisitions during pendency.

4. **Single-source manufacturing.** Britannia supplies both APOKYN and ONAPGO under an agreement with no technology-transfer assistance, silent on manufacturing know-how licensing, and terminable for convenience on 24 months' notice. New-patient ONAPGO delivery was paused in November 2025 and resumed in February 2026. A second supplier filing is targeted for Q3 2026 with potential approval mid-2027, so the exposure persists for roughly a year on a product carrying $55–70 million of guidance.

5. **Inherited Sage contingencies.** An SDNY securities class action with the motion to dismiss pending, consolidated derivative litigation, and an **SEC Division of Enforcement subpoena dated October 16, 2024 concerning the zuranolone MDD NDA**, including FDA communications and communications containing material non-public information. No loss range estimable. An open enforcement inquiry touching the regulatory history of the fastest-growing asset warrants monitoring.

6. **Customer concentration, not improving.** Cencora, Cardinal Health and McKesson each individually exceeded 15% of product revenue and collectively exceeded 76% in 2025; Indivior's three largest wholesalers were 51% of global net revenue. Product-mix diversification and counterparty concentration measure different things, and only the former is improving.

7. **Indivior's estimation surface.** Accrued rebates and returns of $585 million against annual current-period provisions of $1,526 million, with management disclosing that a one-point payer-channel shift moves the accrual $4 million.

The informative point is that for a stock in an announced all-stock merger, near-dated volatility normally collapses toward the arbitrage spread. It has not. Short interest of 10.3% of float at 4.9 days to cover corroborates active bearish positioning; it is high enough to create some squeeze risk on a positive Q3 print but not high enough to be a crowded position. The options market has not treated the merger as having de-risked the equity, which argues against paying for near-term optionality.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($60, +38.7% from $43.25)

The deal closes in Q4 2026 and the $125 million synergy target is delivered in full, taking combined adjusted EBITDA above $900 million against sub-1.2x leverage. The H2 margin ramp confirms on the Q3 print, validating management's claim that ONAPGO launch and Biogen collaboration spend is front-loaded and lifting the FY2026 adjusted operating result to the $180 million high end. ONAPGO reaches the upper half of $55–70 million after the second-supplier filing clears. The Qelbree litigation settles with entry dates beyond 2029, extending the terminal-value horizon on a 41%-of-revenue asset. Legacy erosion completes, so FY2027 organic comparisons turn genuinely positive. On roughly $4.75 of combined adjusted EPS per Supernus-equivalent share at 12.6x, or 10x on $900 million of EBITDA, $60 is achievable. The APOKYN antitrust matter settles inside insurance coverage.

### Bear Case ($32, −26.0% from $43.25)

Either the deal breaks — leaving a standalone company that pays an undisclosed termination fee, guides to a GAAP operating loss of $20–50 million, and carries 1,050bps of adjusted margin compression that the market re-rates toward 12x a ~$2.30 standalone adjusted EPS — or the January 2027 antitrust trial produces an adverse verdict with trebled damages and insurers prevailing on coverage. Alternatively, the H2 margin ramp fails: if H2 adjusted margin lands near H1's 14.1% rather than the implied 23.4%, FY2026 adjusted operating earnings come in around $120 million against guidance of $150–180 million, and the credibility of the front-loaded-spend claim collapses. Qelbree settles early to 2028, compressing terminal value on the largest asset. The 52-week low of $35.51 becomes the reference, with a break-related overshoot to the low $30s.

### Base Case ($48, +11.0% from $43.25)

The deal closes on schedule with partial synergy realization. Combined adjusted EBITDA of roughly $820 million and adjusted EPS of approximately $4.20 per Supernus-equivalent share, capitalized at 11.4x — a modest premium to the small-cap pharma peer median on account of lower leverage and the SUBLOCADE margin profile, and a discount to growth-pharma multiples on account of the 2028 Qelbree date and Indivior's declining revenue base.

Reward-to-risk to base case is roughly 0.42:1 on the authoritative basis, and 0.07:1 if measured from the $46.91 daily-history print. On either basis the asymmetry does not support new capital.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** the Q3 2026 print (early November 2026) is the decisive near-term event, because it tests the implied 23.4% H2 adjusted operating margin. Second in importance is the Form S-4 / joint proxy statement, which should quantify the termination fee, the fairness opinions and exchange-ratio analysis, change-in-control treatment of the July 29, 2026 PSU grants, and the interests of directors and officers. Third, the September 18, 2026 Daubert and dispositive hearing in the APOKYN antitrust case, then the January 25, 2027 trial date.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Q3 2026 adjusted operating margin at or above 21% with SG&A growth below revenue growth, confirming that the Biogen-collaboration and ONAPGO launch spend is genuinely front-loaded rather than structural.
2. The S-4 discloses a termination fee below roughly 3% of Supernus's equity value together with a fairness analysis supporting the 43.5% split, and Indivior's share price movement leaves the implied exchange-ratio value at least 10% above the prevailing SUPN price.
3. A Qelbree settlement or favorable Markman ruling establishing generic entry no earlier than September 2029, removing the terminal-value discount on 41% of revenue.
4. Summary judgment for Supernus in the APOKYN antitrust matter, or confirmed insurance coverage for the defence and any judgment.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Q3 2026 adjusted operating margin below 17%, or a reduction to the $150–180 million adjusted operating earnings range — either would invalidate management's front-loaded-spend explanation and reprice FY2027 consensus of $4.63.
2. Merger termination, or a Supernus board recommendation change, or an outside-date extension without a revised ratio.
3. An adverse liability finding in the APOKYN antitrust matter, or an adverse ruling in the insurance coverage litigation while trial remains scheduled.
4. A Qelbree settlement granting generic entry on or before October 2028, or a second ONAPGO supply interruption before the second-supplier approval expected mid-2027.
5. Any expansion of the SEC zuranolone inquiry into a formal enforcement action or Wells notice.

## Conclusion

**HOLD.** Supernus has become a two-part security: a standalone CNS franchise whose reported growth is roughly 85% inorganic and whose adjusted operating margin compressed 1,050bps year over year, and a fixed-ratio claim on 43.5% of a combined entity whose majority economics come from a company guiding its own revenue lower and whose stockholders — not Supernus's — receive $1.0 billion in cash immediately before closing. The pieces fit together coherently: Supernus supplies unlevered balance-sheet capacity and the management team; Indivior supplies an 80% gross-margin cash franchise and gets its covenant constraint relieved. At 8.0x synergy-inclusive pro forma EBITDA (9.3x ex-synergies) and roughly 10.3x combined adjusted earnings, the price is not demanding.

The reason to wait rather than buy is that the two most consequential facts are both unread and both dated. The S-4 will quantify the termination fee, the fairness analysis behind the 43.5% split, and the change-in-control treatment of equity awards granted five days before the announcement. The Q3 print will show whether a 930bps sequential-half margin improvement is front-loaded spend rolling off or an assertion that does not survive the quarter. Layered on top are a treble-damages trial with a hard January 25, 2027 date and contested insurance, an October 2, 2028 generic stay expiry on 41% of revenue with nine-plus filers and no trial date set, and single-source manufacturing that already interrupted supply once. Cheapness on a patent-cliff pharma with a pending fixed-ratio merger and an unquantified litigation tail is compensation for identifiable structural exposures. Existing holders should carry the position into the Q3 print and the S-4. New capital should wait for either evidence on the margin ramp or a price in the mid-$30s that widens the discount to pro forma earnings power.

Overall score 5.3, down from 6.0. The reduction is attributable to specific fundamental developments — the $50 million GAAP operating guidance cut, adjusted operating earnings falling 23.7% on 32% revenue growth, and the introduction of a fixed-ratio merger binary alongside a balance sheet that converts from net cash to roughly $878 million of net debt (pro forma combined) — not to the price decline from the prior review.
