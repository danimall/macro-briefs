## Executive Summary

Illumina has fixed the operating problem. After three years of flat-to-down revenue ($4,504M in FY2023, $4,372M FY2024, $4,343M FY2025), the business inflected in 2026: Q2 revenue of $1,159M grew 9.4%, year-to-date revenue of $2,251M grew 7.2%, GAAP operating margin widened from 18.0% to 20.2% year to date, and management raised FY2026 guidance on July 30 to $4.60-4.64B of revenue and $5.30-5.40 of non-GAAP EPS. The engine is clinical sequencing — roughly 65% of sequencing consumables, growing mid-teens excluding China and above 20% in U.S./Canada — and the company placed more than 95 NovaSeq X units in Q2 against a management framework of 50-60 per quarter set 18 months earlier. Because clinical customers take six to nine months to reach normalized consumable pull-through, those placements land as recurring revenue largely in 2027, which is the mechanical support for management's "high single-digit" 2027 growth commitment.

The problem is price. At $193.72 the equity is worth ~$29.3B on 151M shares outstanding, and with net debt of $823M at June 28, 2026, enterprise value is ~$30.1B. That is 36.2x the midpoint of guided FY2026 non-GAAP EPS, 31.9x FY2027 consensus of $6.08, roughly 32x FY2025 free cash flow of $931M, and ~23.5x management's adjusted LTM EBITDA (closer to 27-30x on a GAAP EBITDA basis of $1.0-1.1B). The peer median trailing P/E in Life Sciences Tools is 26.8x (MTD 26.8, WAT 52.2, IQV 22.4, A 26.0, MEDP 29.9). Illumina carries a 35-50% forward multiple premium to that group while guiding to 6-7% reported revenue growth and 11% non-GAAP EPS growth, of which the acquisition of SomaLogic subtracts three points. On consensus, earnings growth of ~13.4% into FY2027 against a 31.9x multiple is a PEG of roughly 2.4.

Three additional facts argue against paying that multiple. First, the quality of the reported improvement is thinner than the headline: $22M of incremental government incentives ($28M YTD 2026 vs. $6M YTD 2025) account for ~29% of the $76M year-to-date operating income increase, and year-to-date free cash flow is exactly flat at $412M despite 7.2% revenue growth. Second, the state securities case in San Mateo County was class-certified on May 15, 2026, a motion for judgment on the pleadings was denied June 26, and Illumina has accrued nothing and disclosed no estimable range — an unquantified liability at a premium multiple. Third, revealed behavior by the people closest to the asset is cautious: management repurchased 2,947k shares year to date at an average of $124.20 and bought only 32k shares between May 25 and June 28 at $145.22, leaving ~$1.78B of authorization untouched, while director and Corvex founder Keith Meister reduced his reported common position 48.9% (4,084,886 to 2,089,425 shares) into the rally at $140-203. Analyst sentiment is degrading — strong-buy/buy counts fell from 3+8 three months ago to 4+5 today — and the price sits essentially at the consensus mean target of $195.83.

I do not think the thesis is broken; I think it is priced. Base case $185, bull $225, bear $135 gives a probability-weighted return of roughly -5% with a 30% downside tail against 16% of upside. Trim into strength.

## Company Overview

Illumina designs, manufactures and sells sequencing and genotyping instruments, consumables and software, reported as one segment ("Core Illumina") since the June 2024 GRAIL spin-off. The revenue architecture is a razor/razorblade model: FY2025 was 74% consumables, 11% instruments, 15% services; 92% sequencing, 8% microarrays; 48% of shipments outside the U.S.

The platform stack runs from the MiSeq i100 (low throughput, 2024) through the NextSeq family to NovaSeq X/X Plus, the 2023 flagship built on XLEAP-SBS chemistry and marketed at the "$200 genome." Management disclosed on the Q2 call that ~83% of sequencing volume and 59% of sequencing consumables revenue have moved to NovaSeq X, with ~78% of clinical volume converted. FDA-regulated products (MiSeqDx, NextSeq 550Dx, TruSight Comprehensive Oncology under PMA) and the DRAGEN/BaseSpace/Connected Analytics informatics layer sit on top of that installed base. Intellectual property is substantial: 1,380 issued U.S. patents and 7,653 issued non-U.S. patents as of December 28, 2025, with expirations running from 2026 to 2050.

Product expansion beyond core sequencing now includes single-cell RNA prep, protein prep, the 5-base solution, StrataMap Spatial (summer 2026), a whole-genome MRD research workflow in early access, and the SomaScan/SomaSeq proteomics line acquired with SomaLogic in January 2026. Management is also building a data business ("BioInsight," including a Billion Cell Atlas with more than 300 million cells delivered to six pharma partners), which books through the services line and is not yet sized in the filings.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue inflection is genuine but geographically narrow.** Year-to-date 2026 versus 2025: U.S./Canada $1,243M (+10.5%), EMEALA $725M (+5.7%), Asia-Pacific $175M (+13.6%), Greater China $108M (-20.0%). U.S./Canada moved from 53.6% to 55.2% of revenue; Greater China from 6.4% to 4.8%. Q2 growth of +9.4% reported decomposes into +8.1% "rest-of-world organic" (management's measure, which excludes currency, acquisitions and Greater China), +6.5% organic including China, with FX and acquired revenue contributing roughly 3 points. Within sequencing, Q2 consumables were $775M (+4.7%), instruments $125M (+30.2%), service and other $154M (+13.2%). Microarrays and other grew 21% reported but declined 4% on a rest-of-world organic basis; the reported growth is SomaLogic.

The mix shift reduces geopolitical exposure but does not demonstrate customer diversification. The FY2025 10-K expanded its risk-factor heading to state that "increasing customer concentration makes us more dependent on key customers," with body text noting revenue is "increasingly derived from a small number of large, centralized laboratory customers." Q2 clinical strength was explicitly driven by "some of our largest clinical customers" placing "several multi-unit capacity expansion orders." The growth engine and the concentration risk are the same accounts, and no customer names or thresholds are disclosed.

**Earnings quality requires two adjustments.** FY2025 GAAP net income of $850M included other income of $340M, of which ~$328M was net gains on strategic investments, primarily the retained ~14.5% GRAIL stake. Removing those gains and taxing at the 21.7% headline effective rate implies roughly $593M of ex-gain net income — approximately $3.80 per diluted share against the ~$5.45 reported. The actual tax on those gains may differ, so treat this as order-of-magnitude, but the direction is clear: roughly 30% of FY2025 GAAP net income came from marking up a legacy equity holding. That source is now largely spent — marketable equity securities fell from $215M at December 28, 2025 to $88M at June 28, 2026, and year-to-date 2026 produced net losses of $(23)M on those securities. In Q2, GAAP EPS of $1.35 exceeded non-GAAP EPS of $1.31, because GAAP captured ~$39M pretax of investment gains that non-GAAP excludes. The $5.30-5.40 guidance is a non-GAAP figure and must be compared to non-GAAP consensus.

The second adjustment is government incentives. The 10-Q discloses $28M of incentive benefits recognized year to date 2026 versus $6M in 2025, with grant receivables rising from $21M to $45M. Year-to-date operating income rose $76M ($454M vs. $378M); the $22M incremental benefit therefore explains ~29% of the improvement. The filings do not disclose duration or conditions, so this may be structurally recurring (tied to Singapore/UK/EU manufacturing and R&D presence) rather than one-time, but it should not be treated as pure operating leverage either. Adjusting for it, underlying margin expansion is roughly two-thirds of the reported 220bps.

The third driver is R&D. R&D as a share of revenue went 30.1% (FY2023) → 26.7% (FY2024) → 22.3% (FY2025) → 21.7% (Q2 2026), with absolute spend falling from $1,354M to $967M and restructuring charges now at zero after a $100M cost program in early 2025. Part of that decline is the GRAIL spin-off rather than retrenchment, and the filings do not provide a clean Core-Illumina-only series, so the magnitude of the genuine cut is unestablished. Directionally, however, margin expansion has been partly financed by lower innovation spend at exactly the moment a novel competing chemistry reached market.

**Cash flow is solid but not growing.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $1,079M against $148M of capex, giving $931M of free cash flow (21.4% of revenue). Year-to-date 2026 OCF is $490M against $78M capex — $412M of FCF, precisely flat versus $412M in the prior-year period, despite 7.2% revenue growth and 220bps of GAAP margin expansion. The CFO attributed Q2's $162M FCF to tax-payment timing and a deliberate inventory build to secure critical component supply (gross inventory $697M → $778M). Q4 is seasonally largest and contains a 53rd week this year, so full-year FCF should land near or modestly above FY2025's $931M if the working-capital build reverses. I would not annualize Q2. At ~$30.1B EV, FCF yield is roughly 3.1%.

**Balance sheet is comfortable.** Total assets $6,650M; equity $2,838M; current ratio 1.80x. Debt is $2,000M principal across four $500M tranches (4.650% due September 9, 2026; 5.750% due 2027; 4.750% due 2030; 2.550% due 2031), with a $750M undrawn revolver to January 2028 and a 3.50x total-debt/adjusted-EBITDA covenant in compliance. Cash of $1,040M plus $128M of short-term investments covers the September maturity with room; the prior $500M tranche was repaid from internal resources in December 2025. Whether the 2026 notes were refinanced or retired post-dates the 10-Q and is unconfirmed. Interest coverage was 8.0x in FY2025 and 10.2x in Q2. Management cited ~1.6x gross leverage; that adds back stock compensation — on GAAP EBITDA of roughly $1.0-1.1B, leverage is closer to 2.0x. Neither figure is concerning; they should not be conflated. Net debt rose ~$467M in H1 2026, fully explained by $417M of acquisitions and intangibles plus $370M of buybacks, not by operating deterioration. A flagged Altman Z of ~2.18 is driven by negative retained earnings (the cumulative GRAIL write-offs) and 0.65x asset turnover, not present-day distress; screening D/E of 2.44x does not reconcile — interest-bearing debt/equity is 0.73x.

## Growth Analysis

The 2026 growth is clinical, and the 2027 case is mechanical. Clinical applications are ~65% of sequencing consumables and growing mid-teens ex-China (>20% in U.S./Canada). Application mix is shifting toward higher-intensity uses: oncology therapy selection is largest today, MRD is "coming" (Illumina has a whole-genome MRD research workflow in early access), and screening later. Total sequencing gigabase output on connected high/mid-throughput instruments grew more than 30% year over year, with clinical "well above that." Because clinical customers need six to nine months to normalize pull-through, the >95 NovaSeq X placements in Q2 should convert to consumables mostly in 2027. Management reiterated commitment to high-single-digit 2027 growth and 1-2 points of contribution from new products, though it declined to raise the 2027 target when pressed.

Two offsets are structural rather than cyclical. Research and applied sequencing consumables declined 7% on a rest-of-world basis in Q2, and management reaffirmed mid-to-high single-digit declines for full-year 2026 with no assumed recovery, noting only "some increased activity in terms of funding releases in the U.S." late in the quarter. NIH funding reductions are flagged in the 10-K. Research is roughly 35% of consumables and is the funnel that seeds future clinical assays, so a prolonged academic downturn compounds. Greater China has fallen from $308M implied FY2024 to $243M FY2025 to a ~$216M annualized pace in H1 2026 following the February 2025 Unreliable Entity List designation; exports resumed November 2025 but on a case-by-case approval basis, and MGI/Complete Genomics is structurally advantaged there.

The critical unresolved question is whether Q2's placements were incremental demand or pull-forward. An analyst asked directly whether the market had been "waiting for a more competitive information" ahead of Roche's launch; management answered on value proposition without addressing pull-forward. The CFO separately cited "significant progress with our supply investments in the quarter, allowing us to place over 95," which admits a supply-catch-up interpretation in which some of Q2 is a timing shift. This is testable within two to three quarters: real capacity expansion means consumables accelerate to high single digits in 2027 while instrument revenue holds low single digits as guided; pull-forward means instruments decelerate faster than guided *and* consumables disappoint.

Estimate revisions support the near-term trajectory but do not signal inflection. Current-year consensus rose 2.4% over 30 days and 2.6% over 90 days to $5.36; next year rose 2.6%/2.8% to $6.08. Next-quarter estimates rose 2.9% over 30 days while current-quarter estimates slipped 0.9%. The track record is three beats and one miss in four quarters, with Q2 FY2026 missing consensus EPS by 5.3%. This is orderly upward drift consistent with a guidance raise, not the >30% revision velocity that marks a genuine earnings inflection and justifies paying up.

Contract data cuts modestly against acceleration. The 10-K reported management-defined backlog of $738M at December 28, 2025 (up from $657M), while the 10-Q reports ~$1.0B of remaining performance obligations under multi-obligation contracts plus ~$43M of development/licensing RPO — different measures with different scopes that cannot be compared as a growth rate. What is comparable is deferred revenue, which *declined* from $346M to $333M between December 28, 2025 and June 28, 2026, with the short-term portion falling from $270M to $261M. That is consistent with a business whose obligations are satisfied within roughly six months, but it is not evidence of an accelerating order book.

- FY2026 guided non-GAAP EPS midpoint $5.35 → **36.2x**. Consensus FY2026 $5.36 → 36.1x.
- FY2027 consensus $6.08 → **31.9x**. On consensus EPS growth of 13.4% ($5.36 → $6.08), **PEG ≈ 2.4**.
- FY2025 FCF $931M → **EV/FCF 32.3x**, FCF yield 3.1%.
- Management adjusted LTM EBITDA (implied ~$1.24-1.28B) → EV/EBITDA **23.5-24.3x**; on GAAP EBITDA of ~$1.01-1.10B (FY2025 operating income $807M plus $203M D&A), **27-30x**.

Against peers, the median trailing P/E is 26.8x with a range of 22.4x (IQV) to 52.2x (WAT); MTD 26.8x and A 26.0x are the closest analogues by business model and size. Illumina's 36.2x forward multiple is a 35-50% premium to that cluster on 6-7% guided reported revenue growth. The premium is defensible only if 2027 delivers high-single-digit revenue growth with margin expansion to 25%+, and if that trajectory is durable against Roche. The market is paying for that outcome now rather than after it verifies.

Run the good case explicitly. If FY2027 revenue grows 8% to ~$5.0B with non-GAAP operating margin at 25% and share count roughly flat, non-GAAP EPS reaches approximately $6.50-6.75. At 30x that is $195-203, essentially flat from $193.72 one year forward. Meaningful upside requires the multiple to expand beyond 32x, which asks the market to pay more for a mid-single-digit-revenue-growth franchise facing a newly launched competitor than it does today.

Analyst targets confirm the stock is at consensus, not below it: mean $195.83 (1.1% above the current price basis), median $210.5, high $230, low $113. The distribution is unusually wide, and the ratings mix — 4 strong buy, 5 buy, 6 hold, 1 sell, 3 strong sell — includes four negative ratings, which is rare in life-science tools and reflects genuine disagreement about the Roche threat and the multiple. Buy-side ratings have shrunk from 11 (3 strong buy + 8 buy) three months ago to 9 today, with holds rising from 5 to 6. Where a stock is cheap relative to rising estimates, consensus targets function as stale floors; at 36x forward on modest revisions, the target and the price reflect the same optimism, and proximity to the mean target is not independent confirmation.

Two unmodeled positives deserve mention without being capitalized. The IEEPA tariff refund — the Supreme Court held in February 2026 that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs, and the Court of International Trade ordered CBP refunds in March — is a real potential one-time benefit given that Singapore-manufactured imports were the largest tariff cost driver, but Illumina discloses no dollar amount, no timing, recoveries to date immaterial, and recognition only upon receipt. The BioInsight/multiomics business is also unsized. Neither belongs in a base-case multiple.

Score: 3.8. The multiple is stretched against the growth it delivers, and I can identify no specific mispricing running in the buyer's favor at this price.

## Competitive Landscape

The moat that matters is the installed base plus the validated clinical assay ecosystem sitting on it, not the sequencer itself. Clinical labs with a reimbursed or regulated assay validated on NovaSeq X face real revalidation cost and regulatory friction to switch, and ~78% of Illumina's clinical volume is already on X with the balance targeted by year-end 2026. FDA-cleared and PMA-approved products deepen that barrier.

Roche launched AXELIOS 1 on June 29, 2026, built on proprietary sequencing-by-expansion chemistry: single-molecule reads through millions of nanopores on a **reusable CMOS sensor**, same-day whole-genome sequencing in research workflows, read lengths to ~1,500bp, validation partners Hartwig Medical Foundation and Broad Clinical Labs, application kits from 10x Genomics (a partner supplying kits, not a Roche subsidiary), and support for the open-source Google DeepVariant tool. The reusable-sensor architecture is a plausible structural attack on consumable cost per read, which is the economic core of Illumina's model.

Three facts bound the near-term threat. AXELIOS 1 is research use only, not for diagnostic procedures, so it cannot address the ~65% of Illumina's sequencing consumables that is clinical; Roche's own executives framed clinical applications as future potential. Instrument price, cost per gigabase, run economics and any independent head-to-head benchmark are undisclosed — the cost-efficiency and speed claims are Roche marketing assertions. And the immediate competitive read went Illumina's way: >95 NovaSeq X placements plus >10 NovaSeq 6000 units in the same quarter Roche launched, with instrument revenue up 31%.

The medium-term concern is the combination of that competitive entry with Illumina's own resource allocation. Illumina's stated strategy is "the highest quality insights for the lowest end-to-end cost," delivered by extending the NovaSeq X roadmap rather than forcing another instrument transition — coherent for customers who just spent capital on X, but it means competing on chemistry and software increments against a novel architecture while R&D intensity has fallen to 21.7% of revenue. The 10-K itself warns that competitors "may render one or more of our technologies obsolete" and that SBS-related patents begin expiring in 2026 without identifying which. Element Biosciences, Ultima Genomics, MGI/Complete Genomics, Oxford Nanopore, PacBio, Thermo Fisher and Qiagen round out the named set.

Trajectory assessment: near-term stable-to-improving in clinical, deteriorating in research and China, with a credible three-to-five-year erosion mechanism if SBX proves durably cheaper per read. Score 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

**Securities litigation with an unquantified exposure.** The GRAIL-era cases have bifurcated. The federal case (S.D. Cal.) was dismissed September 26, 2025 with leave to amend; a renewed motion to dismiss was fully briefed March 6, 2026 with no hearing set — favorable. The state case in San Mateo County, addressing the November 2020 and February 2021 registration statements, is the live exposure: demurrer overruled September 3, 2025; **class certification granted May 15, 2026**; judgment on the pleadings denied June 26, 2026; Illumina filed an appellate writ July 28, 2026; leave-to-amend hearing set October 30, 2026. Section 11 claims carry a lower plaintiff burden than fraud claims, and class certification plus adverse pleading rulings is the posture in which settlement pressure builds. Illumina has accrued nothing and disclosed no estimable range. This is an unquantified liability sitting behind a 36x multiple. Two stockholder litigation demands remain with the Board, which again deferred a decision on February 4, 2026.

**Customer concentration.** Newly strengthened 10-K language, corroborated by Q2 commentary that growth came from "some of our largest clinical customers" placing multi-unit orders. Magnitude undisclosed. A single large account deferring capacity expansion would show up sharply in instrument revenue.

**Pull-forward risk on Q2 placements.** Discussed above. If instrument revenue decelerates faster than the guided low-single-digit growth in H2 2026 while consumables fail to accelerate, the 2027 bridge breaks.

**Cost headwinds absent from the 10-K.** Memory chip costs are rising with strained allocations; management secured inventory for several quarters and is "sharing some of that cost increase with our customers." Taking price while a competitor claims superior cost efficiency carries more risk than usual. Middle East conflict-driven fuel, energy and shipping costs hurt gross margin and suppressed EMEALA clinical growth. FY2026 non-GAAP operating margin guidance was *maintained* at 23.4-23.6% despite the revenue raise, and the Q2-to-Q3 step from ~23% to ~24% depends on cost actions the CFO placed "during the Q3 timeframe" — an execution dependency, not a result.

**Unread material event.** An 8-K was filed August 7, 2026 under Item 8.01 (Other Events), eight days after earnings and roughly one month before the $500M 2026 Term Notes matured. Contents could not be retrieved. Plausible subjects include a refinancing, a China update, or a litigation development. Unresolved and potentially material.

**Governance and alignment.** No open-market purchases by any executive or director were observed in the period reviewed. Director and Corvex founder Keith Meister reduced reported common holdings 48.9% (4,084,886 → 2,089,425 shares) between May 12 and August 5, 2026, at $140-203, following equity-swap exercises at $102.82 with the swap position fully unwound. The natural reading is monetization of a leveraged, deeply in-the-money position, and Corvex holds through funds where redemptions and position limits may drive sales; I am not inferring a negative view of the business. The decision-relevant fact independent of motive is that the largest concentrated activist economic stake is now roughly half its prior size, reducing the probability of further activist-driven strategic change. Five-year TSR through FY2025 indexed at 36.47 versus a peer group at 122.07 — shareholders lost ~63.5% while peers gained ~22.1%, and the gap widened in FY2025. Compensation metrics (revenue, non-GAAP EPS, operating income, operating margin, relative TSR) align with what management is now delivering, but contain no return-on-capital or free-cash-flow measure, an omission for a company that destroyed several billion dollars on GRAIL. A resumed EPS PSU in FY2026 increases the payoff to repurchase.

**Capital allocation signal.** Year-to-date repurchases of 2,947k shares for $366M at an average of $124.20; Q2 spend of $123M was ~68% below Q2 2025's $384M; the monthly pace collapsed from 551k shares at $125.06 in late March/April to 32k at $145.22 between May 25 and June 28. Roughly $1.78B of authorization ($279M legacy plus a new $1.5B authorized April 2026) is unused. The slowdown may reflect cash management around the $382M SomaLogic close, contingent payments of $25M and $30M, a $50M IP purchase and the September maturity; the filings state no reason. At $193.72 the stock is ~33% above where repurchases were throttled to a trickle, so buyback support is an unlikely contributor to the FY2026 EPS bridge, making the guided +11% more dependent on operations.

**SomaLogic economics.** Total consideration $460M ($382M cash, $81M contingent fair value, less $3M), with remaining contingent obligations bought out for $30M on July 27, 2026. Pro forma disclosures imply roughly $70-75M of annualized revenue, ~1.6% of the total, and the deal is EPS-dilutive: FY2026 non-GAAP EPS growth is guided +11% reported versus +14% ex-acquisitions. Small, optionality-driven, not a needle-mover.

The term structure is in contango (41.3% near, 45.8% at three months, 43.7% at six months), skew is balanced at +2.9% on the front expiry, and put/call open interest is 0.35 — call-tilted. The August 21 tenor prices a ±6.4% move; the December tenor prices ±22.5%. Put/call *volume* of 2.67 and a 3.7x volume/OI print on the August $190 put (551 contracts vs. 148 open interest) show some fresh near-dated downside hedging, but at 0.35 OI ratio the standing book is not positioned for dislocation.

The options market prices no near-term catalyst and is mildly complacent, with cheap near-dated volatility relative to realized. That is consistent with a stock the market believes has already resolved its uncertainty, which is the setup in which an unaccrued litigation development, a China headline, or an H2 instrument deceleration is under-hedged. For holders trimming, cheap near-dated volatility and a call-tilted book make collars or covered-call overwrites relatively inexpensive. Short interest of 9.5% of float with 4.2 days to cover is moderate and does not create squeeze mechanics in either direction.

Risk score: 4.7. The class-certified, unaccrued securities case and the pull-forward question are specific, dated, asymmetric-downside catalysts that a 36x multiple does not embed.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $225 (+16% from $193.72)

Clinical sequencing compounds mid-teens ex-China as oncology therapy selection scales and MRD arrives; the >95 Q2 NovaSeq X placements prove to be genuine capacity expansion and convert to consumables through 2027, taking rest-of-world organic growth to high single digits. Research funding stabilizes, removing a 35%-of-consumables drag. Non-GAAP operating margin reaches 25%+ as the Q3/Q4 cost actions land and the ERP goes live in H1 2027. Multiomics and BioInsight add the promised 1-2 points of 2027 growth with services-like economics. A material IEEPA tariff refund arrives as an unmodeled cash benefit, and the securities writ succeeds in decertifying the state class. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS of ~$6.60-6.75 at 33-34x supports $225. Roche's SBX remains research-use-only through 2027, and the clinical moat holds.

### Bear Case — $135 (-30%)

Q2 placements were partly pull-forward and partly supply catch-up; H2 instrument revenue decelerates below the guided low single digits and 2027 consumables land at 4-5% rather than high single digits. Research declines persist through 2027 as NIH funding stays constrained, and Greater China erodes toward $180M. Memory, freight and energy cost inflation compresses gross margin while price increases meet resistance from a competitor claiming better cost per read; the maintained (not raised) margin guidance proves optimistic. Government incentives prove non-recurring, removing ~$45M of annualized operating income. The state securities class survives the appellate writ and Illumina takes a nine-figure settlement charge with no accrual in place. R&D at 21.7% of revenue proves inadequate against SBX as Roche pursues clinical claims and SBS patents lapse. FY2027 non-GAAP EPS stalls near $5.60 and the multiple compresses to 24x — near the peer median — giving $135, still well above the analyst low of $113 and the 52-week low of $88.

### Base Case — $185 (-4.5%)

FY2026 lands within guidance ($4.62B revenue, $5.35 non-GAAP EPS). 2027 revenue grows 6-8% as clinical pull-through partly offsets continued research and China declines; non-GAAP EPS reaches roughly $6.10-6.25 with margin at 24-25%. The state case settles or is decertified at a manageable cost, and the tariff refund provides a modest one-time benefit. The multiple normalizes toward 30x — still a premium to the 26.8x peer median, appropriate for the clinical franchise but below today's 31.9x on FY2027 — giving approximately $183-188. Call it $185.

Probability weighting of 50% base / 27% bull / 23% bear gives an expected return of roughly **-4.8%**, with 30% of downside against 16% of upside. That asymmetry, not any deterioration in the business, drives the rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria
