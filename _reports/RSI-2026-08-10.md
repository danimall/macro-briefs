## Executive Summary

Rush Street Interactive is an online-casino-first gaming operator with 16 U.S. states plus Colombia, Ontario, Mexico and Peru, operating the BetRivers, PlaySugarHouse and RushBet brands. FY2025 was the company's first year of substantial GAAP profitability: revenue $1,134.4M (+22.8%), operating income $87.4M (7.71% margin, up from 2.71%), Adjusted EBITDA $153.7M, operating cash flow $165.0M, and zero outstanding debt against $336.3M of unrestricted corporate cash. The margin expansion is real but narrow in origin: cost of revenue was flat at 65.4% of revenue versus 65.1%, and essentially all of the 500 basis points came from sales and marketing falling to 14.5% of revenue from 17.2% and G&A falling to 8.9% from 11.5%. Consensus FY2026 adjusted EPS of $0.62 implies substantial year-over-year earnings growth, which requires that marketing restraint to persist through another year of ~20% revenue growth.

The stock is down 19.6% from its July 29 pre-earnings close of $30.77 and 28.3% from the July 20 high of $34.53, on a quarter that printed in line ($0.15 versus $0.15 estimate). Over the same window, FY2026 consensus rose 3.2% and FY2027 rose 1.6%, with the current quarter cut 5% and the following quarter raised 13%, a timing shift rather than a year-level cut. The decline is therefore multiple compression against rising estimates. We are unable to attribute it to a specific disclosure: the most recent audited filing available is the FY2025 10-K (February 18, 2026), and no 10-Q, 8-K, transcript or news coverage was reviewed for the June quarter. At a 28% drawdown the burden of proof sits with the bull case, and we cannot discharge it from the evidence in hand.

The single most consequential analytical point in this name is the share count. The quoted $2.86B market capitalization reflects 115.6M Class A shares. RSI is an Up-C structure in which RSILP units held outside Class A participate in profits today: $40.7M of FY2025's $74.0M total net income went to non-controlling interests, and Executive Chairman Neil Bluhm alone retained 96,399,630 RSILP units after selling ~10.5M in May 2026. FY2025 diluted shares were 236.1M. On that basis equity value is ~$5.84B and enterprise value ~$5.5B, or ~4.9x FY2025 revenue and ~35.8x FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA. That is a defensible multiple for a debt-free 20% grower; it is not a margin of safety, and it is roughly double what a float-adjusted screen implies. Combined with an unresolved Colombian revenue-VAT decree, gaming-tax increases proposed or enacted in ten named states, an unquantifiable prediction-market threat, and a monthly stream of newly converted Class A supply reaching the market with no offsetting insider purchases, we rate the shares HOLD with a base case of $28.

## Company Overview

RSI operates real-money online casino, online sports betting and retail sports betting across an addressable population the company states exceeds 360 million. B2C accounted for more than 99% of FY2025 revenue; a small B2B retail-sportsbook services business earns monthly commissions.

The structural fact that defines the company is product mix. Online casino is authorized in only nine U.S. states; RSI offers it in six of them (Delaware, Michigan, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, West Virginia) plus Ontario. It is therefore live across most of the legalized U.S. iCasino market while being capped by how few legislatures have authorized the product. Sports betting, by contrast, is available in 39 states plus D.C., with 32 permitting statewide online wagering.

RSI owns its player-account-management platform and front-end, which is a genuine differentiator versus operators renting third-party stacks. The carve-outs matter: sports risk and trading is outsourced to Kambi Group, and casino content comes primarily from integrated third parties under revenue-share arrangements. "Proprietary" describes the bonusing and engagement layer, not the odds engine or the games. Delaware is distinctive: the state lottery selected RSI in 2023 as sole operator of online gaming and sports wagering. Headcount is approximately 912 employees and contractors, roughly 35% technical and 44% U.S.-based, with development hubs in Canada, Colombia, Estonia and Serbia.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and margins.** FY2025 revenue of $1,134.4M grew 22.8% over $924.1M, following +33.7% and +16.7% in the two prior years. The dollar addition of $210.3M was the largest in company history even as the percentage decelerated.

| Cost of revenue % | 67.3% | 65.1% | 65.4% |
| Sales & marketing % | — | 17.2% | 14.5% |
| G&A % | — | 11.5% | 8.9% |
| Operating income | (51,611) | 25,048 | 87,424 |

| Adjusted EBITDA | 8,168 | 92,539 | 153,655 |

The mechanism is opex leverage, not unit-economics improvement. Within the $139.6M increase in cost of revenue, gaming taxes contributed $56.4M and market access $46.3M, together 73%. Both lines scale with revenue and both face upward regulatory pressure, which is why flat gross margin during 23% growth is the load-bearing assumption in any forward margin model. Management attributes marketing restraint to a maturing industry and notes competitors are also rationalizing spend; the counter-reading is that promotional intensity is cyclical and RSI's own MD&A concedes competitor marketing "may vary by quarter depending on... sports calendars, new market launches and prior commitments."

**The two linked non-operating items.** FY2025 GAAP is distorted in both directions. A $107.8M charge for initial recognition of the tax receivable agreement liability turned +$87.4M of operating income into −$11.1M pre-tax; an $85.1M tax benefit from valuation-allowance release then produced +$74.0M of net income. The two are causally linked: concluding deferred tax assets are realizable simultaneously creates the tax benefit and the obligation to pay legacy holders 85% of those savings. Net effect: −$22.7M. Cash effect is minimal near-term: $1.2M of TRA payments expected within 12 months and $128.8M thereafter, with no material payments made to date. Post-June-30-2025 exchanges route through equity rather than the income statement, so the $107.8M is a catch-up, not a run-rate. Operating income of $87.4M and Adjusted EBITDA of $153.7M are the faithful reads of FY2025 operating performance.

**Cash flow quality.** Operating cash flow of $165.0M against PP&E purchases of $0.767M produces a screen FCF of $164.2M (14.5% of revenue). That overstates owner's cash because RSI's capital intensity runs through intangibles: total investing outflows were $37.0M, dominated by capitalized internally developed software, acquired intangibles and gaming licenses. Operating cash flow less total investing gives $128.0M, or 11.3% of revenue, the figure we use. Two adjustments: $17.1M of the $107.7M cash increase was Colombian-peso translation (versus −$8.7M in FY2024, a $25.8M swing unrelated to operations), and D&A of $40.0M slightly exceeded total investing outflows, worth monitoring given that platform ownership is the core competitive claim.

**Balance sheet.** Zero borrowings at both year-ends, with a stated intention to continue financing operations without third-party debt. Cash $336.3M excluding restricted customer deposits; current ratio 1.93; total assets $658.5M; accumulated deficit still −$102.6M. Off-balance-sheet items are modest: $31.3M and $6.4M of surety bonds and a $6.2M undrawn letter of credit for Colombia. Contractual commitments include $18.3M of non-cancellable marketing obligations within 12 months and $55.6M beyond.

The screened debt-to-equity of 2.41 is an artifact: with no borrowings, it measures total liabilities (dominated by the non-current TRA liability) against a Class A equity line that excludes the non-controlling RSILP interest. Similarly, ROE of 50.3% is inflated by that small denominator; ROA of 11.2% is the meaningful read. Altman Z of 3.08 sits in the safe zone, carried by asset turnover of 1.72x and clean working capital. The Piotroski reading of 5-of-5 is a partial score on five of nine signals; on the unevaluated four, gross margin was flat-to-worse and share count rose materially, both of which would cost points.

**Recent earnings cadence.** Reported adjusted EPS: Q3'25 $0.09 (+27.8% surprise), Q4'25 $0.08 (−24.0%), Q1'26 $0.14 (+16.5%), Q2'26 $0.15 (−1.4%). Trailing four quarters $0.46. Two beats, one in line, one miss: adequate but not the pattern that justifies elevated confidence in consensus.

## Growth Analysis

Consensus FY2026 adjusted EPS of $0.62 against trailing four quarters of $0.46 implies +35%; FY2027 at $0.85 implies a further +37%. The quarterly path is steep: Q3'26E $0.14 versus $0.09 (+56% YoY) and Q4'26E $0.19 versus $0.08 (+138% YoY). Estimate revisions are positive but modest in magnitude: FY2026 +3.2% over 90 days, FY2027 +0.9%, with the current quarter cut 5% and the following quarter raised 13%. This is not the +30%-plus 90-day revision velocity that marks a genuine earnings acceleration; it is orderly upward drift.

Working back from consensus, $0.62 on ~236M shares implies GAAP net income attributable to Class A of roughly $73M, which given the Up-C profit split points to total net income and an Adjusted EBITDA level well above FY2025's $153.7M; the precise Adjusted EBITDA implied by consensus is not derivable from the sources in hand. What is clear is that reaching FY2026 consensus requires further margin expansion sourced from marketing and G&A rather than gross margin. It is achievable, since FY2025 demonstrated the operating leverage exists, but it is a discretionary-spend assumption, and the first quarter in which a competitor re-accelerates promotion in Michigan, New Jersey or Pennsylvania is the quarter it breaks.

The market context is the more important constraint. Per Eilers & Krejcik data cited in the 10-K, U.S. online casino plus sports betting revenue grew 25% in 2025 to $28.2B, with a 2019–2025 CAGR of 74% and a projected 2025–2029 CAGR of approximately 5% (implying ~$13.6B iCasino and >$21.0B OSB by 2029). Two observations follow. First, RSI's 22.8% FY2025 growth slightly trailed the 25% U.S. market growth rate, which argues against a share-gain narrative even allowing for the LatAm contribution. Second, if forward market growth is ~5%, the bull case requires share gains, new-state legalization, or LatAm expansion rather than riding the category.

Legalization progress is the swing factor and it is slow. Maine enacted online casino legalization in early 2026 (not yet operating, in partnership with certain tribes). Virginia's iCasino bill received "several favorable votes." Alberta is expected to launch competitive regulated online gaming with no announced date. Ontario is the strongest datapoint in the file: online casino ex-poker plus sports betting grew ~36% in 2025 with acceleration to ~40% in the second half. Against that, RSI is absent from Brazil, which launched a regulated market on January 1, 2025 with a population of ~214M, and the 10-K offers no stated reason.

Management frames prediction markets as a growth argument: because sports event contracts are not subject to state gaming taxes, states may see OSB tax receipts fall and respond by legalizing the higher-yielding iCasino product where RSI is strongest. That is a coherent and genuinely differentiated hypothesis. It is also management's hypothesis in a document intended to reassure investors, and the same filing lists prediction markets as a competitor in three separate places including the seasonality discussion of customer engagement.

## Valuation Assessment

**Share count first, because everything else depends on it.** Reported trailing EPS of $0.31353 reconciles to $74.029M of total net income divided by 236.1M FY2025 diluted shares. The quoted market capitalization of $2.86B reflects only 115.6M Class A shares. These two figures are internally inconsistent, and the difference is not theoretical dilution: the RSILP units are already-issued economic interests that received $40.7M of FY2025 profit, and insiders routinely exchange them into Class A and sell. Bluhm's retained 96.4M units independently corroborate the order of magnitude.

We use the fully exchanged basis. On it, the stock trades at 39.9x FY2026E and 29.1x FY2027E adjusted EPS. A forward EV/EBITDA multiple cannot be reliably computed because the Adjusted EBITDA level implied by FY2026/FY2027 consensus EPS is not derivable from the sources in hand. PEG on FY2027 is roughly 0.79 against 37% expected EPS growth, the most favorable framing available, and the reason we do not score valuation below 5. Net cash of $1.42 per fully exchanged share is 5.7% of the price and does not constitute a floor.

The supplied peer set (PENN, MCRI, RRR, GDEN, BYD at a median 20x P/E and 1.0% median yield) is a poor comparable group. These are asset-heavy land-based operators with property-level capital intensity, different tax treatment and different growth profiles. On FY2026 estimates RSI trades at roughly twice the peer median P/E; that premium is defensible for a 20% grower with 11% owner's-cash margins and no debt, but it is a premium, not a discount.

Adjusted EPS also excludes share-based compensation of $26.3M (about $0.11 per fully exchanged share, and 17% of Adjusted EBITDA), which is a real economic cost funded partly in cash: $25.3M of FY2025 financing outflows went to employee taxes on net share settlement, the largest single financing line. On a GAAP-equivalent basis the FY2027 multiple is closer to 33x.

The analyst set is uniformly constructive: 2 strong buy, 8 buy, 1 hold, with targets of $30 low, $36 mean/median and $40 high, 21% to 62% above spot. We treat these as informative but not independent confirmation. The stock trades 31% below the mean target after a 20% post-earnings decline, and the recorded sentiment trend is "stable" with no logged revisions, which is consistent with targets that have not yet been marked to the print. RSI is not at a premium multiple where targets merely echo the price, but neither has consensus been tested against whatever the July 30 sellers were reacting to.

**Fair value.** Base case: FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA of ~$275M at 22x EV/EBITDA gives $6.05B EV plus ~$450M net cash, or $6.5B / 236.1M = **$27.5, rounded to $28** (+13% from $24.75). Bull case: $300M at 26x plus $500M cash = **$36** (+45%), consistent with the analyst mean and requiring Colombia's VAT permanently voided, Virginia or additional states legalizing iCasino, and prediction markets confined to sports. Bear case: EBITDA stalling near $190M on tax escalation and promotional re-acceleration, at 17x, gives **$17** (−31%), against an observed 52-week low of $15.51 set in February 2026.

Probability-weighting at 45% base / 25% bull / 30% bear produces an expected return of roughly +8%: positive, but insufficient asymmetry for a full position given the dispersion.

## Competitive Landscape

RSI's credible differentiators are the casino-first mix in a market where iCasino out-earns sports betting on tax revenue in five of its states; an approximately 51/49 female/male split among North American casino-only actives, which is unusual for a brand competing against sports-first operators; an owned platform enabling rapid bonusing innovation (bonus bank with 1x playthrough, iRush Rewards, PropPacks); a documented ability to win share without an affiliated local casino; and genuine LatAm operating history since 2018.

The countervailing reality is scale. RSI's own risk language concedes competitors "may be able to innovate and provide products and services faster than we can" and have "access to more resources." It does not own the sports trading engine or most casino content, which caps sports-side differentiation and permanently shares a slice of gross revenue with suppliers, visible in the 65% cost of revenue that has not improved in two years. The company's most-cited competitive grievances are regulatory and tax asymmetries (grey-market operators, sweepstakes, crypto casinos, prediction markets) rather than product inferiority, which is an honest framing but also an admission that the competitive threat is largely outside management's control.

Two structural items belong here rather than only in risk. The flagship U.S. consumer brand is licensed from Rivers IP Holdings, LLC, an entity in which the Executive Chairman holds an indirect interest, terminable by either party on 180 days' written notice. Economic incentives make near-term termination unlikely, since Bluhm is simultaneously the largest economic owner of RSI, but the asset underpinning the U.S. franchise is not owned. Separately, RSI granted RSG a perpetual royalty-free license to certain "Rush Street" marks that precludes RSI's own use in specified fields, and the 10-K acknowledges RSG's use "may disrupt our reputation."

On trajectory, which is what matters for scoring: RSI is holding its position with a differentiated mix in a decelerating market, growing at roughly the market rate rather than above it, with flat gross margin and rising regulatory cost pressure on its two largest cost lines. That is stable-to-slightly-eroding, not improving.

## Risk Assessment

**Colombia's 19% revenue VAT (unresolved binary).** This has two stages. Already incurred: a VAT on customer deposits took effect during 2025, and MD&A attributes the year-over-year decline in LatAm ARPMAU "mainly" to additional player bonusing in response; RSI absorbed the tax rather than passing it through, converting a tax into revenue-per-user erosion. Pending: a December 2025 emergency decree imposing a 19% VAT on the revenue of internet games-of-chance operators serving Colombian players was preliminarily suspended by the Colombian Constitutional Court in January 2026, with the 10-K warning courts "may overturn that preliminary suspension and/or additional tax decrees may be issued." We could not size Colombia's revenue share (the geographic disaggregation in Note 3 was not retrieved), so the exposure is established but unquantified. The demonstrated response pattern (defend volume with bonusing) means a reinstated revenue-level VAT would flow straight into the P&L.

**Gaming-tax escalation.** Increases have been proposed in Arizona, Indiana, Pennsylvania, Michigan and West Virginia and enacted in Illinois, Louisiana, Maryland, New Jersey and Ohio. Gaming taxes were the single largest contributor to FY2025's cost-of-revenue increase at $56.4M. A related sub-risk is regulators removing the deductibility of promotional free bets in computing gaming tax, which would hit a bonusing-heavy model directly.

**Prediction markets.** CFTC-regulated event contracts appear in the FY2025 filing's competition risk factor, a dedicated emerging-products risk factor, the Item 1 industry description and the seasonality discussion. The specific complaint is that these platforms face different regulatory and tax frameworks. Management's shield argument (casino-first exposure) is plausible and partially credited, but platforms with large retail brokerage user bases could extend into broader event categories, and the asymmetry that spares casino operators today is not structurally guaranteed. The 10-K concedes it "may be too early to accurately determine what the longer-term impacts of these products will be." This is our leading candidate explanation for the multiple compression since July, and it is inherently unquantifiable from filings.

**Up-C structure.** The TRA permanently diverts 85% of realized tax savings to legacy holders and can accelerate into a lump-sum present-value payment on early termination, certain changes of control, or a 30-day payment failure, on assumptions the 10-K concedes "could exceed the actual tax benefits." The filing states this "could make us a less attractive target for an acquisition," which removes the takeout floor that often supports mid-cap gaming equities. Separately, the partnership agreement requires tax distributions to all unitholders at assumed rates that "may be significantly higher than the holders' actual tax rates," and MD&A warns that "continued profitability will trigger future quarterly tax distribution obligations," a cash leakage to non-controlling holders that scales with earnings.

**Governance.** RSI uses NYSE controlled-company exemptions and intends to continue doing so, so it may lack a majority-independent board and fully independent compensation and nominating committees. Bluhm and CEO Richard Schwartz control a majority of voting power under a voting agreement. The charter waives any duty for Controlling Holders or non-employee directors to refrain from competing lines of business and permits them to pursue acquisitions that "may not be available to us." A dense related-party network (Rivers IP, SugarHouse marks, RSG services agreement, retail/online agreements with Rivers casinos in four states) is priced at arm's length per the filing, and we have no evidence of unfavorable terms; the concern is structural.

**Operational concentration.** RSI outsources sports risk/trading (Kambi), most casino content, geolocation and KYC, and hosts on AWS and Google Cloud. In October 2025 a DNS management failure at a cloud provider plus separate disruptions elsewhere rendered the platform inaccessible simultaneously in Illinois, New Jersey, Michigan, West Virginia, Indiana, Pennsylvania, Louisiana and Peru. Management states the impact was not material; the episode nonetheless demonstrates single-point-of-failure exposure across most of the revenue base. RSI also does not insure progressive jackpot payouts and discloses that odds-posting clear errors "have in certain instances resulted in large liabilities."

**Technical supply.** Form 4s from March 3 to August 3, 2026 show discretionary sales by the Executive Chairman (~10.51M shares at $24.96 for ~$262.4M on May 5–6), the CEO (monthly, April–August), the CFO (23,000 shares in each of five consecutive months), the COO (monthly), the Chief Legal Officer (five sales) and one director, with no open-market purchases in the returned set. Realized prices spanned $21.05–$31.53, which argues against reading this as a valuation call at a specific price, and the metronomic cadence is consistent with pre-scheduled 10b5-1 plans (not confirmed in the filings reviewed). In an Up-C structure, exchange-then-sell is the only liquidity route for unitholders, so mechanical selling is expected. What remains decision-relevant is narrower: there is no insider bid under the stock, and newly converted Class A shares reach the market each month regardless of price.

**Macro.** Consumer Discretionary carries cyclical sensitivity, and online gaming ARPMAU is not insulated from a genuine consumer downturn, though the sector has historically shown resilience. [unverified — no macro figures on mortgage rates, payrolls or PCE appear in the provided sources]

**Verification gaps that limit confidence.** No 10-Q, 8-K, transcript, proxy or news coverage was reviewed. The audited record ends December 31, 2025, while two to three quarters of results exist. Colombia's revenue share, the exact point-in-time Class A/Class V/RSILP split, executive compensation structure, and any material event after February 18, 2026 are unestablished. The ~$5.84B equity value should be read as a well-supported approximation.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($36, +45%).** Colombia's Constitutional Court permanently voids the 19% revenue VAT, removing the largest quantifiable earnings threat. Virginia legalizes iCasino and Maine comes online, adding high-margin markets where RSI's casino-first stack and partner relationships give it early share. Marketing discipline persists industry-wide, taking Adjusted EBITDA margin higher and FY2027 Adjusted EBITDA to ~$300M on revenue of ~$1.6B. Prediction markets remain confined to sports and, as management argues, erode state OSB tax receipts enough to accelerate iCasino legalization. Ontario's ~40% second-half 2025 growth continues, and the remaining $42.4M buyback authorization is deployed near current prices. At 26x EV/EBITDA plus ~$500M net cash, that supports $36.

**Bear case ($17, −31%).** The Colombian VAT is reinstated and RSI again defends volume with bonusing, compressing LatAm revenue per user. Two or three additional states raise gaming taxes, and at least one removes promotional deductibility, pushing cost of revenue above 67%. A larger competitor re-accelerates promotional spend in Michigan or New Jersey, forcing marketing back above 16% of revenue and stalling Adjusted EBITDA near $190M. Prediction-market platforms extend into casino-adjacent categories, capping the iCasino TAM and permanently compressing the growth multiple. Persistent RSILP conversion supply meets no insider bid. At 17x on $190M plus cash, that is $17, with the February 2026 low of $15.51 as the observed floor when EBITDA was materially lower.

**What tips the balance to HOLD.** The base case sits only 13% above spot, the bear case is a 31% drawdown, and the two largest swing factors (Colombia's court, prediction-market trajectory) are not analyzable from company disclosure. The financial position is genuinely strong (zero debt, $128M of owner's cash, no refinancing exposure) and estimates are rising while the price falls, which is the correct direction for risk/reward. But the honest denominator puts the stock at a full mid-30s multiple of FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA, and a 20% post-earnings decline on an in-line print that we cannot attribute to a specific disclosure argues for waiting rather than adding.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Rating: HOLD. Base case $28 (+13% from $24.75). Bull $36 (+45%). Bear $17 (−31%). Horizon: 12 months.**

Base-case math: FY2027E Adjusted EBITDA ~$275M × 22x = $6.05B EV, plus ~$450M net cash, ÷ 236.1M fully exchanged shares = $27.5.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE or BUY on any of:**
1. The Colombian Constitutional Court permanently voiding the December 2025 VAT decree, or company disclosure sizing Colombian revenue at less than 10% of total (which would cap the exposure at a tolerable level).
2. Two consecutive quarters with cost of revenue below 64% of revenue, evidencing gross-margin leverage rather than opex-only expansion.
3. A price near $20–21 with FY2027 consensus holding at or above $0.85, which would put the stock under 25x FY2027E adjusted EPS on the fully exchanged basis.
4. Deployment of a meaningful portion of the remaining $42.4M buyback authorization at current prices, or the first open-market insider purchase in the record.

**Downgrade to REDUCE or AVOID on any of:**
1. Reinstatement of the 19% Colombian revenue VAT without a demonstrated pass-through, or a second jurisdiction imposing a revenue-level tax.
2. Sales and marketing returning above 16.5% of revenue for two consecutive quarters, which would break the consensus margin path.
3. FY2027 consensus EPS falling below $0.75 (−12% from current $0.85), or a quarter in which revenue growth decelerates below 12% year over year.
4. Any regulatory or competitive development extending CFTC-regulated event contracts into casino-style products in RSI's iCasino states.
5. Notice of termination under the 180-day Rivers IP brand license, or a change-of-control event triggering TRA acceleration.

**Next reassessment:** the Q3 2026 print (following the July 29 Q2 report), with the September 18 options tenor pricing a ±13.6% move before then. We also want the FY2026 10-Q record and any 8-K disclosures since February 18, 2026 before revisiting the rating, since the June-quarter filing set was not available for this review and the ~20% post-print decline remains unexplained by the evidence in hand.

## Conclusion

RSI has done the hard part of the online gaming transition: it reached scale, turned cash-generative without borrowing, and expanded operating margin 500 basis points in a single year. The balance sheet is the best feature of the file: $336.3M of unrestricted cash, no debt, $128M of defensible owner's cash, and an Altman Z of 3.08. Estimates are drifting higher while the stock has fallen 28% from its July high, which is the correct direction for prospective returns.

Three things keep us at HOLD. First, the honest share count. On 236.1M fully exchanged shares the enterprise is worth ~$5.5B, or ~35.8x FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA and 29x FY2027E adjusted EPS, full pricing for a company growing roughly in line with its market in a category whose forward growth rate is projected to fall from 74% to about 5%. Second, the margin story depends on discretionary spend restraint continuing while the two largest cost lines, gaming taxes and market access, face upward pressure in ten named states. Third, the two developments most likely to determine the next 30% move in either direction, the Colombian court's final ruling and the trajectory of CFTC-regulated event contracts, are unquantifiable from the available disclosure, and the absence of any insider bid against persistent structural Class A supply gives the price no support while they resolve.
