## Executive Summary

Baker Hughes is executing a strategic transformation from a cyclical oilfield services company into a diversified energy technology platform. The Industrial & Energy Technology (IET) segment delivered 14% YoY revenue growth and 35% EBITDA growth in Q1 2026, with record orders of $4.9B and a $33.1B backlog providing multi-year revenue visibility. The company's positioning in data center power generation (~$3B in expected orders 2025-2027) and LNG infrastructure creates a differentiated secular growth narrative within the energy services sector.

The primary constraint on the investment case is leverage. Baker Hughes raised $9.9B in new debt in March 2026 to pre-fund the $13.6B Chart Industries acquisition, tripling long-term debt from $5.4B to $15.4B. While divestiture proceeds of ~$3B and annual FCF of ~$2.5B will delever over time, the balance sheet carries meaningful integration and execution risk through 2027. Simultaneously, the legacy OFSE segment faces structural headwinds from declining rig counts (-7% YoY) and Middle East disruptions (-19% YoY regional revenue).

At ~21x forward earnings with estimates declining (-10.9% over 90 days for FY2026), the stock prices in IET's secular growth story but offers limited margin of safety against Chart integration missteps or further OFSE deterioration. The thesis is constructive on a 12-18 month basis, contingent on successful Chart close and integration execution.

## Company Overview

Baker Hughes operates through two segments: Oilfield Services & Equipment (OFSE, ~52% of 2025 revenue) and Industrial & Energy Technology (IET, ~48%). The company employs approximately 56,000 people across 120+ countries.

**OFSE** provides drilling, completion, production, and subsea equipment/services to upstream E&P operators. Revenue is directly correlated with global rig counts and upstream capital spending.

**IET** manufactures gas turbines, compressors, LNG technology, and industrial products, supplemented by aftermarket services and a growing Climate Technology Solutions (CTS) business. Key growth vectors include LNG infrastructure, data center power generation (aeroderivative gas turbines), carbon capture, and AI-enabled energy optimization.

The company is actively reshaping its portfolio: divesting lower-growth OFSE assets (surface pressure control to Cactus, drilling equipment via HMH IPO, Waygate Technologies to Hexagon) while acquiring IET-aligned businesses (Chart Industries for $13.6B).

**Chart Industries Optionality:**
- Adds ~$4B+ annual revenue in process technologies for gas/liquid handling

- Vertically integrates BKR's gas/LNG/power platform
- Strengthens positioning in hydrogen, water treatment, and small-scale LNG

The company is transitioning from a mid-single-digit organic grower to a platform with double-digit growth in its highest-quality segment (IET) while managing secular decline in traditional oilfield services.

## Valuation Assessment

At $64.32, BKR trades at:
- 20.6x trailing P/E (TTM EPS $2.60, includes non-recurring items)
- ~27.9x forward P/E on current FY2026 consensus ($2.30)
- Peer median P/E: 26.0x (though peer group is not directly comparable; PSX is refining, FANG/OXY are E&P, TRGP/OKE are midstream)
- Dividend yield: 1.4% (below peer median 2.2%)

The more relevant comparison is industrial/power technology peers. Siemens Energy trades at 30-35x forward P/E. GE Vernova trades at 40-50x. On a sum-of-parts basis, if IET (~$16B pro-forma revenue post-Chart, 20%+ EBITDA margins) were valued at 20-25x EBITDA and OFSE at 8-10x, the implied equity value range is $55-$80 per share.

At $64.32, the stock sits at approximately fair value on current estimates. The bull case requires Chart synergies materializing and continued IET margin expansion. The stock has appreciated 70% from its 52-week low of $35.83, and consensus targets (mean $70.81, median $73.00) suggest ~10-13% upside, modest relative to execution risk.

The declining estimate trajectory (-10.9% over 90 days) argues against treating current multiples as cheap. The stock needs estimates to stabilize or inflect upward to support further appreciation from here.

## Competitive Landscape

**Differentiation:** Baker Hughes is unique among oilfield services peers (SLB, HAL) in having a large-scale industrial technology platform. While competitors are primarily exposed to upstream E&P spending cycles, BKR's IET segment provides exposure to long-cycle LNG, power generation, and decarbonization markets.

**Improving Competitive Position in IET:** Record backlog, margin expansion, and aeroderivative gas turbine supply tightness create favorable pricing dynamics. The partnership with Google Cloud for AI-enabled power optimization adds a technology moat. Chart acquisition strengthens vertical integration.

**OFSE Position Stable but Mature:** BKR maintains share in well construction, completions, and subsea systems, but this is a structurally shrinking market with margin pressure from declining activity.

**Key Risk — GE Dependency:** BKR licenses critical aeroderivative and heavy-duty gas turbine technology from GE Vernova and GE Aerospace. Loss of this relationship would severely impair IET's competitive position. This is a supplier concentration risk with no near-term mitigation path.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Chart Acquisition Execution ($13.6B):** The largest transformative acquisition since the 2017 GE merger. Integration of a $4B+ revenue company while managing elevated debt, extracting $325M in synergies, and maintaining IET margin trajectory is a high-complexity undertaking. Regulatory approvals pending; expected Q2 2026 close. This is the single risk that prevents a BUY recommendation: the magnitude of potential downside if integration stumbles (20-30% derating) outweighs incremental upside.

2. **Leverage Risk:** Net debt will be ~$10-12B post-Chart close (after applying cash and divestiture proceeds). At ~2.5x EBITDA initially, declining to ~2.0x by late 2027 assuming $2.5B annual FCF generation and no further M&A. In a rising-rate environment, servicing costs are material (~$600-700M annually in interest expense).

3. **OFSE Structural Decline:** Declining rig counts, OPEC+ supply return, and Middle East disruption create a ~5-8% annual revenue headwind in the legacy business. Divestitures partially mitigate but do not eliminate this drag.

4. **Estimate Deterioration:** If OFSE weakness deepens or Chart close delays, further estimate cuts are probable, creating near-term multiple compression risk.

5. **Geopolitical Sensitivity:** Middle East operations directly impacted by regional conflict. Hormuz disruptions affect client operations and BKR's own logistics.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($75-85, +17-32%):**
- Chart acquisition closes on schedule, synergies track to $325M run-rate by 2028
- IET margins expand toward 22-24% as backlog converts at higher pricing
- Data center power demand exceeds $3B guidance (upside to $5B+ through 2028)
- OFSE stabilizes as oil prices find support
- Sum-of-parts rerate: IET valued like industrial technology peer (25x+ EBITDA)
- Deleveraging faster than expected via strong FCF + additional divestitures

**Bear Case ($48-55, -15-25%):**
- Chart integration stumbles: synergies delayed, management distracted, margins compress
- OFSE deterioration accelerates (rig counts fall below 1,700, Brent drops below $65)
- Rate environment pressures servicing costs on $15B+ debt stack
- Estimate cuts continue (-15-20% more), driving multiple compression
- Key customer credit events (Mexico/Pemex exposure, Gulf state budget cuts)

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
1. Chart acquisition closes with clean regulatory approval and initial integration targets met (synergy run-rate visible by Q3 2026 earnings)
2. FY2026 EPS estimates stabilize or inflect upward (currently $2.30 and falling; stabilization at ≥$2.25 would be constructive)
3. Stock pulls back to $55-58 range (25x current FY2026 estimates), providing margin of safety for execution risk

**Downgrade Triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
1. Chart close delays beyond Q3 2026, or material regulatory conditions imposed
2. IET orders decelerate below $3.5B/quarter for two consecutive quarters (would signal demand fading)
3. OFSE margins break below 16% for consecutive quarters, indicating structural impairment beyond cycle
4. Leverage exceeds 3.0x net debt/EBITDA post-Chart for more than two quarters

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July), which should provide first visibility on Chart close timeline and initial integration commentary.

## Conclusion

Baker Hughes presents a compelling long-term transformation story. The IET segment's secular growth drivers (LNG, data centers, decarbonization) are validated by record orders and expanding margins. The company is actively shedding lower-quality OFSE assets and acquiring higher-quality technology businesses.

The specific constraint preventing a BUY recommendation at this level is the combination of elevated acquisition execution risk and declining near-term estimates. The $13.6B Chart acquisition tripled the debt load. At ~21x forward earnings on declining estimates, the stock prices in substantial success but offers limited margin of safety if integration disappoints. If Chart synergies arrive 12-18 months late, or if OFSE weakness deepens simultaneously, the stock could retrace 15-25% before the thesis ultimately plays out.

This is a growth-oriented HOLD. The structural direction is correct; the near-term risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. The stock deserves a premium to legacy oilfield services peers but needs to demonstrate Chart execution before warranting additional capital allocation.
