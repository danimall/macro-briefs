## Executive Summary

Kinsale writes excess and surplus (E&S) lines property and casualty insurance on a non-admitted basis through a single insurance subsidiary, and it does so better than almost anyone. FY2025 delivered a 75.30% combined ratio, a 29.25% return on average equity, and $1.04 billion of operating cash flow at 2.07x net income. Q2 2026 held the line on margin: a 75.5% combined ratio and $105.4 million of underwriting income, with annualized operating ROE of 24.4%. Debt is 10.3% of total capital, interest coverage is roughly 60.6x, and statutory surplus of $1.93 billion against $1.62 billion of net written premium implies a premium-to-surplus ratio of 0.84x — an over-capitalized balance sheet by E&S standards.

The problem is the top line. Gross written premium grew 19.2% in FY2024, decelerated to 5.7% in FY2025, and declined 5.0% year over year in Q2 2026. The contraction is concentrated: Commercial Property GWP fell 17.9% in FY2025 and a further 32.7% in Q2 2026, taking that division from 24.4% of GWP in 2024 to 18.9% in 2025. Ex-property, Q2 GWP grew 3.7%. Because written premium earns ratably, net earned premium growth has already fallen from 16.69% (FY2025) to 8.9% (Q2 2026) and will keep decelerating through 2027 unless writings reaccelerate. Consensus reflects this: FY2026 operating EPS of $21.13 and FY2027 of $21.65 imply roughly 2% growth, against a company that compounded diluted EPS 21.4% in 2024 and 21.8% in 2025.

We have carried a BUY on KNSL through four consecutive reviews, with the overall score drifting from 7.1 (March) to 6.8 (June). Two things have changed since June and both cut the same way. First, the stock is up approximately 15% from the $315 level at our last review, having rallied 24% off the June 3 low of $290.20. Second, Q2 confirmed that GWP is now shrinking in absolute terms rather than merely decelerating, and out-year consensus has ticked down 0.7% over 90 days. Higher price on flat-to-slightly-weaker fundamentals is a deterioration in risk/reward, and at 4.0x book with negative premium growth the asymmetry no longer supports a BUY. Base case $380 (+5.4%), bear case $280 (-22.3%). We move to HOLD.

## Company Overview

Kinsale Capital Group (Delaware holding company, formed 2009, IPO July 2016) underwrites E&S property and casualty risks through Kinsale Insurance Company, licensed in all 50 states, D.C., Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands. The FY2025 10-K reports one reportable segment. Non-admitted status confers freedom of rate and form — Kinsale sets prices and policy wordings without prior state approval, which is the regulatory basis for the business model.

Three structural features distinguish the franchise:

**Small-account, high-volume underwriting.** Average premium per policy was $12,300 in Q2 2026, down from $14,300 a year earlier. The book is composed of a very large number of modest policies across 24 commercial underwriting divisions plus two personal lines divisions, with no division exceeding 19% of FY2025 GWP.

**Vertical integration.** Claims are handled in-house rather than through third-party administrators, on a custom-built enterprise platform. This produces an expense ratio that ran 18.78% in FY2025 — materially below E&S peer norms — which allows Kinsale to price below competitors and still earn more per dollar of premium.

**Owned distribution at the margin.** Kinsale owns Aspera Insurance Services, a small consolidated broker subsidiary representing low single-digit percentages of premium.

FY2025 GWP by largest division ($ thousands): Commercial Property 374,451; Excess Casualty 276,998; General Casualty 207,888; Small Business Casualty 202,412; Construction 147,601; Small Business Property 102,413; Allied Health 96,982; Entertainment 70,049.

The moat is a cost-and-speed advantage, not an access advantage. Kinsale distributes primarily through unaffiliated brokers: three unaffiliated brokers produced 18.8%, 17.1%, and 11.7% of FY2025 GWP — 47.6% from three counterparties. In a hardening market a cost advantage compounds into volume. In a softening market it protects margin but does not protect volume. That is precisely what the last four quarters show.

The placid combined ratio conceals three shifts.

*Favorable prior-year development is doing real work.* FY2025 net favorable development was $62.8 million, or 3.99% of net earned premium. Ex-development, FY2025 combined would have been approximately 79.3% — still excellent, but roughly 400 basis points of the reported figure is a reversible input rather than an operating one. H1 2026 favorable development was $38.1 million.

*Retention has risen, so the same margin carries more net risk.* The cession rate fell from 21.0% (FY2024) to 18.3% (FY2025); net retention rose from 79.0% to 81.7%. The Q2 10-Q attributes the expense-ratio increase to lower ceding commissions from higher retention — ceding commissions received were roughly flat ($120.3M → $119.5M) while direct commissions paid rose 12.5% ($254.5M → $286.3M). Two plausible readings: management is expressing confidence in its pricing by buying less reinsurance, or the shift away from large layered property (the most reinsurance-intensive line) mechanically reduced cessions. The disclosures do not distinguish them. Either way, each dollar of equity now supports more net loss exposure.

*Operating ROE is declining even as GAAP ROE rises.* Q2 2026 annualized ROE was 35.2%; annualized operating ROE was 24.4%, down from 27.0% a year earlier. The operating figure is the one that matters for the multiple.

### Cash flow and capital

Operating cash flow: $859.8M (2023) → $976.3M (2024) → $1,043.7M (2025), or 2.07x net income in FY2025. The dominant contributor is a $605.2 million increase in gross loss reserves (from $2,285.7M to $2,890.9M) — genuine float accumulation from writing casualty premium faster than claims are paid, but a growth artifact that decelerates as writings slow. The pattern is already visible: OCF grew 13.5% in 2024 and only 6.9% in 2025. Cash tax discipline is clean — $129.6 million paid against $130.3 million of current tax expense, no deferral gap.

Debt is $224.4 million: $51 million drawn on a $100 million revolver (matures July 2027), $125 million of 5.15% notes and $50 million of 6.21% notes, both due 2034 with amortization from 2030. No principal maturities before 2027. Debt/total capital 10.28%, interest coverage roughly 60.6x. Holding-company cash is thin at $50.0 million against a parent-only pretax loss of $(10.6) million, but Kinsale Insurance may dividend up to $444.3 million to the parent in 2026 without Arkansas approval, versus $105.0 million upstreamed in 2025.

The investment portfolio underwent a large, under-discussed repositioning. Structured product rose from 39.2% to 57.6% of fixed maturities in one year, with RMBS going from $448.9 million to $1,459.3 million (12.7% → 33.6%) while corporates fell from 56.2% to 39.5%. This was active rotation — $2,494.2 million of purchases against $1,167.7 million of sales — executed with only +$3.8 million of net realized gains, so without meaningful loss recognition. Duration extended from 4.0 to 4.3 years by Q2 2026. Accumulated other comprehensive loss widened from $(30.7) million at year-end to roughly $(74) million at June 30, 2026, an approximately $43 million charge against book value in six months. About 18% of the portfolio is rated below A−. The 10-K does not disaggregate agency from non-agency RMBS, so the credit content of the rotation cannot be assessed from disclosure.

**Financial health score: 7.5.** Solvency is not the question here — leverage, coverage, statutory surplus, and dividend capacity are all comfortable, and standard distress screens (Altman Z, Piotroski) are structurally inapplicable to an insurance balance sheet where liabilities are float rather than borrowings. The deductions are the reserve-mix concentration, the duration and structured-credit extension, and the equity allocation that injects volatility into reported earnings and book value.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has inverted. GWP is contracting, the earned-premium lag guarantees continued deceleration in revenue, and consensus models flat earnings.

**The mechanics are arithmetic, not judgment.** With Q2 2026 GWP down 5.0%, net earned premium growth — already down from 16.69% in FY2025 to 8.9% in Q2 2026 — has to keep falling through 2027 absent a reacceleration in writings. Consensus FY2026 operating EPS of $21.13 and FY2027 of $21.65 imply roughly 2% growth. Estimate revisions confirm no inflection: current-year estimates up 1.6% over 30 days and 1.9% over 90 days, next-year down 0.2% and 0.7%. There is no earnings-revision buy signal present.

**What is still growing.** Net investment income rose 19.9% in Q2 on a portfolio that expanded from $3.95 billion to $5.03 billion during 2025, and float continues to accumulate from the casualty book. Casualty divisions grew double digits in FY2025 (Excess Casualty +13.0%, General Casualty +22.9%, Small Business Property +33.4%, Commercial Auto +39.0%, High Value Homeowners +34.3%). Share count fell 1.59% in H1 2026 on $163.1 million of repurchases, which mechanically supports per-share growth.

**What is shrinking.** Commercial Property, the line that benefited most from the 2022–2023 hard market, is in a two-year retreat. Average premium per policy fell 14% year over year. The expense ratio moved from 18.78% to 21.7% on lower ceding commissions, which pressures the combined ratio even with a lower loss ratio.

**Growth prospects score: 4.5**, down from 5.7 in June. The downgrade reflects GWP crossing from deceleration into contraction and out-year consensus flattening. A soft E&S market with new capacity entering is not a two-quarter phenomenon; the 2019–2021 hard market took three years to build and softening cycles historically run at least as long.

Peer set (FAF 10.0x, THG 9.9x, ORI 9.4x, MCY 6.5x, SIGI 12.6x) shows tight clustering around 9.9x. KNSL's premium is roughly 68% on forward earnings and multiples on book. The premium has historically been justified by ROE: 29.25% on average equity in FY2025 against low-to-mid-teens for the comparables, and a 75.3% combined ratio against sector norms in the mid-90s. The question is whether that ROE is durable at the current growth rate.

**The justified-P/B math is unforgiving.** Using a 9.5% cost of equity, a 4.0x book multiple requires roughly a sustained 22% ROE with 5–6% growth: (0.22 − 0.055) ÷ (0.095 − 0.055) ≈ 4.1x. Operating ROE is currently 24.4% and falling from 27.0%, and growth is negative. If operating ROE settles in the high teens as favorable development normalizes and the expense ratio holds near 21–22%, and growth runs 0–5%, the supportable multiple falls to roughly 2.5–3.0x book. On a mid-2027 BVPS of approximately $95 (Q2 2026 $89.34 plus retained earnings less buybacks and AOCI drag), that range is $240–$285.

**Normalized earnings are lower than reported.** Stripping FY2025's $62.8 million of favorable development reduces pretax underwriting income by that amount, or roughly $50 million after tax at the 20.6% effective rate — about $2.15 per share. On normalized FY2025 earnings the multiple is nearer 18–19x, and on normalized FY2027 earnings roughly 18x for a business consensus expects to grow 2%.

**Cycle-adjustment argues the other way here.** The instruction to use forward and normalized earnings rather than trailing multiples helps stocks at cyclical troughs. Kinsale is at the opposite point: FY2025 and H1 2026 earned premium reflects hard-market vintages still running through the income statement, supported by reserve releases from those vintages. Trailing earnings are closer to a cycle peak than a trough. Applying a peak-cycle multiple to peak-cycle earnings is where insurance investors most often lose money.

**Sell-side positioning is unsupportive.** Coverage stands at 1 buy, 9 holds, 2 sells, unchanged for three months. The mean target of $349.78 and median of $355.00 sit *below* the $360.44 close; the range is $259 to $405. The guidance that analyst targets act as floors applies to inflecting stocks with rising estimates and reasonable multiples — the opposite of a 4x-book insurer with flat out-year consensus and slightly negative revisions. Here the consensus target is a genuine cap on near-term expectations.

**What the market already repriced.** The stock is 25.7% below the $485 52-week high, so the de-rating from growth-compounder to high-quality-but-slow is substantially complete. That is the strongest argument against further multiple compression, and it is why we hold rather than avoid. But the stock has also rallied 24% off the $290.20 June low, so the compensation for taking reserve-mix and soft-market risk is materially thinner than it was six weeks ago.

**Valuation score: 4.8.** Not stretched to absurdity, but no identified mispricing and no catalyst. Cheapness relative to Kinsale's own history is not the same as cheapness relative to its forward earning power.

## Competitive Landscape

The decisive question is whether the contraction is cyclical discipline or share loss. Evidence exists on both sides.

**Industry-wide softening is confirmed.** S&P Global Market Intelligence data, cited by Insurance Business America (July 31, 2026), shows commercial property E&S premiums fell 2.8% in 2025 — the first annual decline since 2017. Ryan Specialty, a large wholesale broker, reported Q2 2026 organic revenue growth of 6.7% decelerating from 7.1%, with wholesale brokerage net commissions up only 4.5%, and explicitly noted casualty growth partially offset by property decline; it guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDAC margin down 50–100bps and in February 2026 approved a three-year restructuring targeting $80 million of savings at roughly $160 million of cumulative pre-tax charges. Distributors do not restructure into hard markets. New listed capacity is entering (Ategrity's record pre-announced Q2; Skyward reporting post-Apollo acquisition), a classic late-cycle marker.

**Kinsale is contracting faster than the market.** Commercial Property GWP fell 17.9% in FY2025 against an industry decline of 2.8% — roughly a 6x differential — and 32.7% in Q2 2026. Total GWP fell 5.0% while the largest wholesale channel grew 6.7% organically. Even ex-property, +3.7% trailed channel growth. These are not like-for-like measures (broker organic growth is commission revenue across multiple business lines and includes new teams and client wins; Kinsale's figure is single-carrier primary premium), so the differential overstates the gap. But the direction is unambiguous.

**Margin stability is the best evidence for the discipline reading.** Combined ratio went 75.87% → 75.30% → 75.5%. A carrier losing business to structurally better competitors typically shows margin erosion as it defends the remaining book. Kinsale shows volume decline with intact margin, which is the signature of walking away from underpriced risk. Management's stated reason — significant rate declines combined with expanding coverage on larger layered property accounts — is consistent with the S&P data.

**The honest caveat:** discipline and share loss are observationally identical over one to two years, because reported margin on a shrinking book is dominated by earned premium from prior hard-market vintages and by prior-year releases. The distinguishing test is how the 2025–2026 accident years' loss ratios mature, which will not be observable for several years.

**Competitive position score: 6.0**, down from 6.8. The cost moat is intact — an 18–19% underlying expense ratio remains structurally unmatched — but the trajectory is negative on volume, average premium per policy, and expense ratio, and level matters less than trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Long-tail casualty reserve concentration with an adverse-development signal.** Of $2,257.3 million of net outstanding liabilities (excluding unallocated claims adjustment expense), Casualty–Occurrence is $1,789.3 million, or 79.3%. IBNR plus expected development on reported claims is approximately 91.3% of that line and about 90% of the total reserve — nearly the entire liability is actuarial estimate rather than case reserve. The disclosed payout pattern is very slow: 1.7% of incurred claims paid in year one, 48.6% cumulative by year five, 87.2% by year ten, with roughly 13% settling beyond a decade. KPMG designated reserves the sole critical audit matter, citing limited historical claims data requiring industry supplementation.

The mix is the concern. Seven of nine seasoned Casualty–Occurrence accident years have developed adversely (AY2018 +19.8%, AY2017 +14.9%, AY2019 +10.6%, AY2022 +7.2%, AY2016 +6.6%, AY2023 +2.1%, AY2021 +2.9%), while the reserve releases come from short-tail Property (AY2023 −42.3%, AY2024 −25.9%) and Casualty–Claims Made (AY2021 −61.5%, AY2020 −52.8%) — the two smallest buckets at 6.3% and 14.4% of net liabilities. The 10-K narrative corroborates: FY2025's $62.8 million net favorable development comprised $70.9 million of releases particularly in property lines, offset by adverse development in construction liability (2016–2019) and actuarial assumption adjustments for construction-defect inflation uncertainty (2020–2024). Kinsale is shrinking the release-generating line while growing the adverse-developing one. If the mix shift persists, the reported combined ratio faces a structural headwind independent of pricing.

Mitigants: AY2024 developed slightly favorably and AY2023 only +2.1%, so recent vintages behave far better than 2017–2018; the construction-defect cohort is an industry-wide, disclosed problem; property AY2024 and AY2025 still carry $26.5 million and $75.6 million of IBNR, so release capacity does not vanish immediately; and KPMG's independent procedures found recorded reserves within its range.

**2. Extracontractual litigation.** Kinsale Insurance issued a $1 million general liability policy to Venetian Hills Apartments; a DeKalb County, Georgia jury awarded $140 million in December 2023. The Georgia Court of Appeals vacated the judgment on October 31, 2025 and ordered a full retrial; the plaintiff petitioned the Georgia Supreme Court on December 29, 2025. Kinsale believes exclusions apply, states adequate provision exists within existing reserves, and discloses no specific accrual. A $140 million exposure equals 7.1% of year-end 2025 equity. The appellate vacatur materially improved the posture, and resolution may take multiple years.

**3. Distribution concentration.** 47.6% of FY2025 GWP from three unaffiliated brokers, with no prior-year comparable disclosed. Note that a shifting *product* mix says nothing about *distribution* concentration; the two should not be conflated. Loss of one relationship would be a step-function volume event.

**4. Catastrophe exposure.** Net cat losses were $4.6M (2023), $25.5M (2024), $30.4M (2025, primarily the Palisades Fire) — 0.43%, 1.89%, 1.93% of net earned premium. The book is concentrated in California, Florida, and Texas at roughly half of premium (moderate confidence on precise figures). The shrinking property book mechanically reduces this exposure, one genuine benefit of the slowdown. Late-July/early-August 2026 trade press references Spokane-area wildfires destroying 700-plus structures; we have no Kinsale-specific exposure data and treat this as a Q3 watch item only.

**5. Investment portfolio.** Structured credit at 57.6% of fixed maturities, duration 4.3 years, roughly 18% rated below A−, a 12.5%-plus equity allocation marking through net income, and approximately $43 million of AOCI deterioration in H1 2026. A lengthened duration book is an active decision with a live cost. [unverified — the brief does not support specific claims about the level of the 30-year yield or Fed-independence concerns]

**6. Retention economics.** Restricted stock granted at weighted-average grant-date fair values of $502.43 (2024) and $433.62 (2025) is underwater against a $360 share price, with $35.5 million of unrecognized compensation over 2.2 years. Anti-dilutive awards rose from 1,000 (2023) to 56,000 (2025). For a franchise whose moat resides in an underwriting bench, weakened equity retention economics matter.

**7. Premium receivable credit quality.** Allowance for credit losses of $27.3 million against net receivables of $124.6 million (18.0% of gross), with write-offs jumping from $3.9 million (2024) to $10.1 million (2025). Immaterial to earnings at 0.53% of GWP, but a useful read on small-commercial insured stress that is worth monitoring.

**Risk factors score: 5.2.** The reserve-mix dynamic and the litigation tail are specific, quantifiable, and in our judgment not fully priced at 4x book.

Net read: no near-term catalyst priced, meaningful standing downside protection, and vol that is fairly priced rather than cheap enough to make optionality attractive.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $445 (+23.5%)

The property contraction is deliberate normalization from an abnormal 24.4%-of-GWP peak, and it ends. Property E&S pricing stabilizes in 2027 as capacity that entered on hard-market economics discovers it cannot underwrite at 75% combined ratios; Kinsale re-enters with pricing power intact, having preserved its loss ratio and its balance sheet through the trough. Meanwhile casualty divisions compound at double digits, net investment income grows high teens on an expanding $5 billion-plus portfolio, and buybacks at roughly 4x book retire 3–4% of shares annually. Operating ROE holds at 24%, GWP returns to high-single-digit growth, and the market re-rates toward 20x on FY2028 operating EPS of $22.50. Compensation design supports this path: the disclosed performance measures are actual underwriting profit, combined ratio, and operating ROE, with no premium- or revenue-growth metric — management is structurally not paid to chase volume into a soft market.

### Bear Case — $280 (−22.3%)

Favorable development normalizes toward zero as the property and claims-made release pipeline runs out while Casualty–Occurrence, now 79.3% of reserves and ~91% IBNR, continues to develop adversely at 5–15% off initial picks. The combined ratio drifts from 75.5% toward 82–85%, aided by an expense ratio stuck near 22% on lower ceding commissions. Earned premium declines with a two-year lag on shrinking writings. Operating ROE settles in the mid-to-high teens with 0–3% growth, and the justified P/B falls to 2.8–3.0x — roughly $265–$285 on a mid-2027 book near $95. Elevated short interest and put open interest accelerate the move. In this path the FY2025 buybacks at an average of roughly $427 per share, and the H1 2026 repurchases above the current price, look like capital deployed near a cycle peak.

### What tips the balance

The bull case requires a pricing inflection that no available evidence supports — S&P data shows the first commercial property E&S decline since 2017, the largest wholesale channel is restructuring, and new capacity is entering. The bear case requires only that reserve releases normalize, which is the base-rate outcome for any casualty writer whose release engine shrinks. Base case sits between: $380, or 17.5x FY2027 consensus operating EPS of $21.65, equivalently 4.0x a mid-2027 BVPS near $95. That is +5.4% against −22.3% downside — approximately 0.24:1 reward-to-risk to base case, and roughly 1.05:1 even comparing bull to bear. It is not a BUY setup.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Existing holders should retain the position — this is a genuinely superior underwriter with a fortress balance sheet, and the de-rating from 8x to 4x book is largely done. New capital should wait.

**Upgrade to BUY on any of:**
1. **Price at or below $300** (3.2x forward book, roughly 13.9x FY2027 consensus), which restores 25%-plus upside to our base case with the bear case largely priced.
2. **GWP growth returning to positive year over year for two consecutive quarters**, with Commercial Property decline narrowing to single digits — the specific evidence that the soft-market air pocket is closing rather than deepening. Testable at the Q3 2026 (late October) and Q4 2026 (February 2027) reports.
3. **Combined ratio at or below 77% on an ex-favorable-development basis**, demonstrating that the underlying accident-year margin, not reserve releases, supports the multiple. Requires the calendar-year development disclosure in the FY2026 10-K.

**Downgrade to AVOID on any of:**
1. **Net adverse prior-year development in any quarter**, or full-year favorable development falling below approximately 1.5% of net earned premium (from 3.99% in FY2025) — the reserve-mix thesis converting from directional concern to realized headwind.
2. **Operating ROE below 20%** on an annualized quarterly basis (from 24.4%), which would take the justified P/B below 3.0x and make the current price indefensible.
3. **Q3 2026 GWP decline widening beyond 10% year over year**, or the ex-property growth rate turning negative, which would shift the evidence from disciplined shrinkage toward share loss.
4. **An adverse Georgia Supreme Court development on Venetian Hills** combined with disclosure of a specific accrual, or any incremental extracontractual verdict of comparable scale.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results in late October 2026 are the next substantive checkpoint — specifically GWP trajectory, the ex-property growth rate, expense-ratio composition, any Spokane wildfire cat load, and the H2 development figure. The FY2026 10-K (February 2027) provides the full accident-year development triangles, which are the primary evidence on the reserve-mix question.

## Conclusion

Kinsale is a high-quality franchise trading at a price that requires it to remain a growth company at a moment when it has stopped being one. The underwriting is genuinely best-in-class: a 75.5% combined ratio, an expense ratio structurally below peers, 24.4% operating ROE, 10.3% debt/capital, 60.6x interest coverage, and a statutory premium-to-surplus ratio of 0.84x that leaves ample capital flexibility. Management's compensation design rewards underwriting profit rather than volume, and the sequence of covenant amendment, tripled buyback authorization, 47% dividend increase, and further authorization expansion is a coherent statement that incremental capital cannot earn historical returns in underwriting at current market pricing. The insider record supports rather than undercuts this: the 2026 selling was expiration-driven monetization of a July 2016 grant at a $16.00 strike, with the CEO's stake essentially unchanged, and the only discretionary transaction in the window was a small open-market purchase by the Chief Accounting Officer near the lows.
