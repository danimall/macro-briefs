**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Alarm.com is a cloud-based platform for connected properties that sells almost entirely through a network of independent security dealers. The model is asset-light, recurring, and durable: SaaS and license revenue carries a 95% renewal rate, capex runs roughly $1M per quarter, and operating cash flow more than doubled year-over-year in Q1'26 to $50.6M. The balance sheet is clean after the January 2026 repayment of $500M in convertible notes, leaving only $500M of 2.25% notes due June 2029, currently out-of-the-money, against ~$497M of cash and $200M+ of annual operating cash flow. On the operating metrics that matter (operating income, adjusted EBITDA, cash conversion) the business is healthy.

The complication is growth quality and trajectory. The stock has fallen 24% from its August 2025 high of $59.53 to $43.08, and the move is not pure noise. Near-term EPS estimates are being revised modestly lower (current quarter -3.2% over 30 days), the largest partner (ADT, 15-20% of revenue) is migrating subscribers off the full SaaS platform onto lower-value patent-royalty economics in a slow but persistent mix-shift, and the company's most interesting growth vector, EnergyHub's grid-services business, is still running an operating loss (~$6.8M in Q1'26). The headline Q1'26 net income decline is misleading, driven by an $7.4M drop in interest income after the cash note repayment and a $3.7M mark-to-market loss on equity securities rather than operating deterioration, but the genuine concern is that the core platform's growth rate is capped by the legacy runoff for the foreseeable future.

At ~16x forward EPS, ALRM trades at a meaningful discount to its application-software peer set (median P/E ~40x), and the discount is partly deserved given the absence of a clear re-acceleration catalyst. This is a quality business at a fair-to-cheap price with no obvious near-term unlock. We rate ALRM a HOLD; the downside is well-protected by cash flow and the upside requires either EnergyHub crossing into profitability or stabilization of the SaaS growth rate above the legacy drag.

## Company Overview

Alarm.com operates a B2B2C software platform for intelligently connected properties, spanning security, video surveillance and analytics, energy management, access control, electric-utility grid management, gunshot detection, water management, and emergency response. In 2025 the platforms processed over 365 billion data points from more than 170 million connected devices. The company sells through independent service-provider partners (dealers and installers) who hold the 3-5 year end-customer contracts and resell Alarm.com's SaaS subscriptions plus hardware. Alarm.com bills partners monthly on a per-subscriber basis. This produces a high-retention recurring stream but limited direct visibility into the end consumer, a structural feature management flags as a risk.

The business reports two segments. The core Alarm.com segment is roughly 90% of revenue and generated ~$38M of operating income in Q1'26. The Other segment (~10% of revenue) houses EnergyHub (utility demand-response and grid services), OpenEye (commercial video), Noonlight, CHeKT, and Shooter Detection Systems, runs at an operating loss, and represents the company's growth optionality, concentrated in the energy-transition space. Revenue mix is approximately 68% SaaS and license (high margin) and 32% hardware (low margin, ~75% cost of revenue).

The market-data feed classifies ALRM under "Financials." This is an error; ALRM is an application-software and connected-property platform, and this report treats it as such.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has compounded steadily from $842.6M in 2022 to roughly $1.011B in 2025. Q1'26 total revenue rose 11% year-over-year to $265.2M, with SaaS and license revenue up 11% to $181.5M. The earnings surprise record is consistent — four consecutive beats over the trailing four quarters, ranging from +5.4% to +21.9% — which lends some confidence to consensus estimates even as they edge lower.

Profitability shows a nuance worth dwelling on. Q1'26 operating income was $31.6M (12% margin), up modestly from $29.6M, while reported net income fell to $23.4M from $27.7M. The net income decline is almost entirely below the operating line: interest income dropped $7.4M (from $12.4M to $4.9M) after the company spent $500M of cash repaying convertible notes in January, and equity-security marks contributed a $3.7M loss. Adjusted EBITDA grew to $49.6M from $45.8M, and operating cash flow more than doubled to $50.6M from $24.1M. A naive screen reading the net income line would misclassify this quarter as deterioration; the operating reality is stronger than the headline.

The pressure points on margin are real but contained. SaaS cost of revenue rose to 15% of SaaS revenue from 13%, partly from energy/demand-response cost pass-throughs in the Other segment. Hardware remains a low-margin drag, with tariff exposure on components manufactured in Vietnam, Thailand, and Taiwan, partly offset by price increases.

The balance sheet is the strongest pillar of the thesis. Cash stood at $497.4M after the January note repayment, down from $960.6M at year-end 2025. Remaining debt is the $500M 2.25% convertible note due June 2029, out-of-the-money at the current $43 stock price with capped calls limiting dilution. Capex is minimal at roughly $1M per quarter (FY guidance $7-10M for the remaining nine months), confirming the asset-light model. On capital allocation, the company repurchased $20.0M of stock in Q1'26 (up from $5.1M a year earlier) and authorized a new $150M buyback through May 2028. There is no dividend.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is mixed, and growth is the dimension that historically sorts winners from losers most reliably, so we weight it carefully here. The positives: 11% total revenue growth, a durable 95% renewal rate, and accelerating SaaS within the Other segment (+50% year-over-year), evidence that the EnergyHub-led energy/grid strategy is gaining traction. The 2025 acquisition cadence — Resideo Grid Services into EnergyHub (Nov 2025), Bridge to Renewables (Aug 2025), and 81% of CHeKT (Feb 2025) — signals genuine conviction in building a second growth engine in distributed-energy-resource aggregation and utility demand response.

The negatives temper that. The core platform's growth rate is structurally capped by the ADT mix-shift. ADT, like Vivint before it, is migrating subscribers off the full SaaS platform onto a lower per-subscriber patent-royalty arrangement. Each migrated subscriber generates less revenue, and this legacy runoff is a slow, persistent headwind largely outside the company's control. The EnergyHub growth vector, while promising, is still loss-making and the path to profitability is unproven.

Estimate revisions reinforce caution. Consensus current-quarter EPS has been cut 3.2% over 30 days, and current-year EPS edged down 0.6% over 30 days, though both next-quarter and next-year estimates remain modestly positive over 90 days (+3.1% and +3.8%). The aggregate revision trend is broadly flat-to-negative. This is not the >30% upward revision velocity that would flag a clear acceleration; it is a steady-state business with offsetting cross-currents. Growth durability is moderate: the recurring base is highly visible, but the rate is constrained.

## Valuation Assessment

At a $43.08 price and ~16-18x trailing/forward EPS (trailing EPS $2.45, FY26 consensus $2.77, FY27 $2.96), ALRM trades at a steep discount to its application-software peer set. The peer median P/E is approximately 40x (RNG 40.3x, AGYS 67.0x, CALX 80.2x), with the cheaper comparables being lower-quality or distressed (RAMP 16.9x, SPSC 22.8x). On growth-adjusted metrics, ALRM's ~16x forward multiple against ~7-9% revenue growth and high-quality recurring economics is reasonable rather than compellingly cheap.

The analyst price targets bracket a wide range: low $40, mean $59, median $57.5, high $85. The mean implies ~37% upside. We are cautious about treating that gap as a floor. The guidance that consensus targets lag reality applies when estimates are rising sharply and the multiple is well below the growth rate. Here, estimates are flat-to-falling near-term, so the analyst dispersion more likely reflects genuine disagreement about the durability of the SaaS growth rate against the ADT drag than a stale target waiting to be revised up.

A cheap multiple alone is not a buy signal, and in the current regime cheapness has more often marked value traps than bargains. The relevant question is whether there is a specific catalyst the market is mispricing. The candidates are: (a) EnergyHub crossing into profitability, which would convert a current drag into a contributor and re-rate the growth narrative; (b) the SaaS renewal rate and pricing holding up such that core growth stabilizes above the legacy runoff; or (c) the $150M buyback meaningfully shrinking the share count at a low multiple. None of these is dated or imminent, which is why the discount persists. We score valuation modestly above neutral: the price offers downside protection and a fair entry on cash-flow yield (~9% FCF yield on minimal capex), but not the asymmetric upside that a 7+ would require.

## Competitive Landscape

Alarm.com occupies a defensible middle layer as the software backbone for the professional security-dealer channel. Its moat rests on the entrenched dealer network, high switching costs evidenced by the 95% renewal rate, and 20+ years of integration breadth across many device categories. This is a stable-to-narrow moat, not a widening one.

The competitive trajectory is the concern. Two structural forces press against the position. First, large platform owners — Amazon (Ring), Google (Nest), Apple (HomeKit), plus SimpliSafe, Wyze, and Arlo — risk commoditizing the consumer layer and pulling demand toward cheaper DIY point products, weakening the professional dealer channel that is Alarm.com's distribution. Second, and more concrete, large partners are internalizing the platform: ADT is following Vivint in migrating to royalty-only economics. On the professional side, Alarm.com competes with Honeywell, Resideo, Hikvision, Verkada, Brivo, and Napco. The competitive position is stable in the near term but faces credible erosion vectors at both the consumer and the large-partner ends, and we see the trajectory as flat-to-slightly-deteriorating rather than improving. The EnergyHub grid-services push is the one area where the company is building new competitive ground, which keeps the score off the floor.

## Risk Assessment

**Customer concentration (ADT).** A single partner represents 15-20% of revenue and the top 10 are ~43-45%. The risk is not outright loss but the slow mix-shift to lower-value royalty economics, a known, ongoing drag.

**Tariffs and hardware margin.** Components are sourced heavily from Vietnam, Thailand, and Taiwan. The April 2025 tariffs were invalidated by the Supreme Court in February 2026, a potential margin tailwind and possible refund, but management warns the government may re-impose under alternative authority. Hardware-margin uncertainty persists.

**Component supply / AI crowd-out.** A newer, specific risk: semiconductor manufacturers are shifting capacity toward high-performance AI chips, constraining DRAM/NAND availability and raising lead times and costs. Three suppliers represent 50% of hardware, one alone 31%.

**IP litigation.** The EcoFactor and Causam threads are resolving favorably (Causam dismissed March 2026; three of five EcoFactor patents found unpatentable). The live wildcard is SkyBell, a trade-secret suit filed July 2025 targeting the video-doorbell line and seeking injunctive relief and a constructive trust. Discovery closes July 2026.

**M&A and equity-investment complexity.** Heavy 2025 deal cadence plus large minority stakes (Safe Haven 32.5%, SafeStreets 24.7%, Pronet 20.3%) and a $21.5M secured loan to SafeStreets create integration, impairment, and related-party exposure against $318M of goodwill and intangibles.

These risks are mostly known and at least partially priced, but the SkyBell suit and the tariff re-imposition possibility are genuine near-term binaries that the market is not obviously discounting. We score risk near neutral.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Alarm.com is a durable SaaS compounder trading at well below the peer multiple. The 95% renewal rate and asset-light model throw off ~$200M+ of annual operating cash flow on minimal capex, a ~9% FCF yield. EnergyHub crosses into profitability over the next 18-24 months, converting a ~$27M annualized drag into a contributor and re-rating the growth narrative as a grid-services platform on the energy transition. The Supreme Court tariff invalidation eases hardware margins and could deliver refunds. The $150M buyback shrinks share count at a low multiple. Estimates resume an upward path as SaaS growth stabilizes above the legacy runoff. In this scenario the stock re-rates toward the analyst mean of $59 (+37%), with upside toward the high target of $85 if EnergyHub becomes a clear second engine.

**Bear case.** The ADT mix-shift accelerates and the legacy royalty runoff caps SaaS growth in the mid-single digits indefinitely. Big-tech DIY commoditization weakens the dealer channel, and another large partner follows ADT and Vivint to royalty-only economics. Tariffs are re-imposed under alternative authority, compressing hardware margins, while AI-driven component shortages raise costs. EnergyHub remains a perpetual drag and a goodwill-impairment candidate. The SkyBell suit produces an adverse outcome affecting the video-doorbell line. In this scenario estimates continue to drift lower and the stock retests the low-$40s and below, toward the $40 low target (-7%).

Our base case sits between these: a stable, cash-generative business growing high-single digits, with EnergyHub a slow-burning option rather than a near-term contributor. Fair value on ~17x FY27 EPS of $2.96 is approximately $50, ~16% above the current price, which is constructive but not compelling enough for a BUY absent a dated catalyst.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD with an honest "uncertain" rationale: the downside is well-protected and the business is high-quality, but neither the bull nor the bear case has a near-term, dated catalyst that tips the asymmetry decisively.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- EnergyHub / Other segment narrows its operating loss meaningfully or reaches breakeven, with management commentary on a profitability path.
- Core SaaS and license revenue growth stabilizes at or above ~10% for two consecutive quarters despite the ADT runoff, signaling the legacy drag is being out-grown.
- Forward EPS estimate revisions turn clearly positive (>+5% over 90 days) while the multiple remains below ~20x forward.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second top-10 partner announces migration to royalty-only economics, or ADT runoff accelerates materially.
- An adverse SkyBell ruling (discovery closes July 2026) producing injunctive relief affecting the video-doorbell line.
- SaaS gross margin continues to erode below ~83% alongside falling estimates, indicating structural margin loss rather than transient cost pass-through.
- Goodwill impairment on the 2025 acquisition spree.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q2'26 earnings print (the August option expiry implies a ±13.4% move around it), with the SkyBell discovery close in July 2026 as a parallel item to monitor.

## Conclusion

Alarm.com is a well-run, financially healthy SaaS/IoT platform with a clean balance sheet, strong cash conversion, and a defensible dealer-channel moat, trading at a substantial discount to application-software peers. The discount is partly deserved: the ADT mix-shift to royalty-only economics structurally caps core SaaS growth, near-term estimates are drifting lower, and the EnergyHub growth option remains loss-making with an unproven path to profitability. The 24% drawdown from the 52-week high reflects this growth-quality concern rather than a fundamental break — operating income and cash flow both rose in Q1'26, and the net income decline is a financing-and-marks artifact, not operating deterioration.

The setup is balanced. Downside is cushioned by cash, operating cash flow, and a ~9% FCF yield; upside requires a catalyst (EnergyHub profitability or SaaS re-acceleration) that is not yet dated. The current data — falling near-term estimates and the structural growth cap — does not justify pushing toward a BUY. We rate ALRM a **HOLD** and would become buyers on evidence the platform can grow past the legacy runoff or on a pullback toward the high-$30s that widens the margin of safety.
