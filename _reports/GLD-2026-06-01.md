**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SPDR Gold Shares is a passive, physically-backed grantor trust that holds allocated London Good Delivery gold bars and trades as a fractional beneficial interest in that vault. It is not an operating company, and conventional equity metrics (P/E, margins, ROE, management quality, competitive moat) are largely inapplicable. The reported "trailing P/E" of 3.08 and "EPS" of $111 are artifacts of mark-to-market gold appreciation flowing through the trust's accounts; they carry no analytical meaning. The investment thesis for GLD is the gold thesis, less a 0.40% annual sponsor fee. Everything else is custody structure, fee competition, and benchmark integrity.

After rising from roughly $2,000/oz in early 2024 to an all-time LBMA PM high near $5,405/oz on January 29, 2026, gold corrected sharply to approximately $4,608 by March 31 and now trades the GLD share around $410-417, roughly 18-20% below its January peak but still up ~36% year over year. The correction was triggered in part by the early-2026 Iran conflict, which produced a sell-off rather than the customary safe-haven bid, indicating that positioning had become extended enough that the usual hedge reflex broke down. Fund flows have turned: GLD saw net share redemptions in the March 2026 quarter (created 39M, redeemed 46.8M shares), occurring against record commodity-ETF outflows of $12.8B in March 2026.

We rate GLD a HOLD with an uncertain rationale. Gold's structural supports (record central-bank buying of ~244 tonnes in Q1 2026, U.S. fiscal concerns, and a multi-year de-dollarization trend) remain intact. But analyst commentary points to real-rate/inflation tension as a key near-term headwind, with UBS lowering its 2026 target on exactly this dynamic and bearish commentators warning the price has "outrun some demand segments." The setup is two-sided. We hold existing exposure for portfolio diversification but see no asymmetric reward at current levels, and for long-term allocators, the 0.40% fee and the resulting gold-per-share decay make cheaper clones (iShares' IAU at 0.25%, State Street's own GLDM at 0.10%) the better vehicle.

## Company Overview

GLD is the SPDR Gold Trust, a New York investment trust formed November 12, 2004, listed on NYSE Arca with cross-listings in Mexico, Singapore, Hong Kong, and Tokyo. Its fiscal year ends September 30. The trust's sole objective is to track the price of gold, less expenses, by holding 100% physically allocated gold bars.

The structure is mechanical. Shares are issued and redeemed only in 100,000-share "Baskets" by Authorized Participants (Goldman Sachs, HSBC, JPMorgan, Merrill Lynch, Morgan Stanley, RBC, UBS, Virtu) in exchange for gold. The Sponsor is World Gold Trust Services LLC, an indirect subsidiary of the World Gold Council; the Trustee is BNY Mellon; the custodians are HSBC (London) and JPMorgan (London/New York/Zurich); the marketing agent is State Street; and the auditor is KPMG. The trust has no officers, employees, or board of its own; those functions are performed by Sponsor personnel.

The trust is not registered under the Investment Company Act of 1940 and is not a commodity pool under the Commodity Exchange Act, so holders lack the regulatory protections associated with those frameworks. There are no dividends, limited voting rights, and no derivative or oppression-suit recourse.

## Financial Analysis

There is no operating business to analyze. "Financials" here are the gold price plus fund flows, and "net income" is almost entirely unrealized gold appreciation.

From the FY2025 10-K (year ended September 30, 2025):
- Gold holdings: 32.53M oz, fair value $124.43B (cost basis $77.75B; unrealized gain $46.68B)
- Net assets: $124.53B across 353.7M shares; NAV/share $352.09
- Reported "net income": $35.55B (vs. $20.36B FY24, $5.55B FY23), driven entirely by gold rising to $3,825/oz at 9/30/25 from $2,630 a year earlier
- Total return at NAV: +44.9% FY25, +40.1% FY24, +11.5% FY23
- Sole expense: the 0.40% sponsor fee, equal to $363.1M in FY25

The trust holds no cash by design; it sells exactly the gold needed to pay the fee. The XBRL feed shows negative "revenue," which is the negative sponsor fee mislabeled; this is not an operating loss.

From the 10-Q (quarter ended March 31, 2026, filed May 5, 2026):
- Gold holdings: 33.63M oz, fair value $155.0B; net assets $155.1B across 366.4M shares; NAV/share $423.34
- Share count fell during the March quarter (net −7.8M shares), confirming net outflows consistent with the broad gold-ETF redemptions of early 2026

On financial health, the appropriate framing is structural resilience, not credit quality. There is effectively no leverage (debt/equity near zero, just a small accrued fee payable), no refinancing risk, and the gold is 100% allocated with no subcustodians used since August 2020, dual custodians, twice-yearly Bureau Veritas bar counts, and KPMG flagging gold existence as its Critical Audit Matter with physical observation of counts. As a balance-sheet object, this is about as clean as a bullion vehicle gets. The one persistent structural drag is fee erosion: gold-per-share grinds down from roughly 0.0921 oz to 0.0919 oz over the past year, and compounds materially over a decade even with a flat gold price.

## Growth Analysis

"Growth" for GLD is the forward path of the gold price, and the picture is mixed.

Supports remain genuine and broad-based. Central-bank net buying ran roughly 1,051-1,086 tonnes per year over 2022-2024, structural official-sector demand tied to reserve diversification and de-dollarization, which operates on a multi-year timeline that transcends conflict cycles. Q1 2026 gold demand was reportedly a record ~$193B. U.S. fiscal and currency-risk concerns add to the bid. Analyst targets are elevated: Goldman raised its December 2026 target to $4,900 (from $4,300), a Reuters poll landed near $4,916, and JPMorgan has cited $6,300.

The countervailing forces are equally concrete. Analyst commentary points to real-rate/inflation tension as a dominant near-term headwind: UBS lowered its 2026 target in May 2026 on exactly this dynamic, and bearish commentators warn the price has "outrun some demand segments." The January Iran sell-off, where gold fell on a geopolitical shock that historically would have lifted it, signals positioning had become extended. Fund flows have turned negative, and the "crowded trade" narrative is cooling.

Net, growth prospects are neutral-to-soft from here. The structural demand story is intact, but near-term price direction is constrained by real-rate tension and unwinding positioning. This warrants a below-midpoint growth score, consistent with the declining trajectory across recent reports.

## Valuation Assessment

Standard valuation multiples do not apply. GLD trades at NAV less the embedded fee; its "valuation" is whether gold itself is cheap or expensive relative to its drivers.

Against those drivers, gold sits in a precarious zone. The bull-case demand engine (central banks, de-dollarization) is real but is the same engine that has been cited throughout the run-up; it is increasingly priced. The bear case (real-rate/inflation tension, positioning extended, flows reversing) has fresh confirmation in the anomalous Iran sell-off and UBS's downward revision. With the share at ~$410-417 and analyst gold targets spanning a wide $4,900-$6,300 (translating to meaningfully higher GLD levels) against UBS's downward revision and warnings the price has "outrun some demand," the distribution is genuinely two-sided rather than asymmetrically favorable.

There is no specific mispricing to exploit here. GLD will deliver the gold return minus 0.40%, and on a cost-efficiency basis it is structurally inferior to IAU (0.25%) and GLDM (0.10%) for identical exposure. We assign a midpoint valuation score reflecting a fairly-priced commodity proxy with no identifiable edge and a modest fee drag.

## Competitive Landscape

The competitive issue is fees. GLD charges 0.40%. iShares Gold Trust (IAU, ~$83.8B AUM) charges 0.25%, and State Street's own GLDM charges ~0.10%. Press coverage repeatedly notes investors "keep choosing IAU over GLD." GLD's durable advantage is liquidity and trading depth: the tightest spreads, the deepest options market, and the institutional default for short-term and tactical exposure. That makes it the trader's vehicle, while buy-and-hold allocators increasingly migrate to IAU and GLDM. State Street effectively cannibalizes GLD with its own lower-cost product, GLDM.

This is a stable-to-slowly-eroding competitive position. GLD is not losing its liquidity primacy, but its share of long-term allocation flows faces a structural headwind from cheaper clones. The competitive-position score has appropriately stepped down from prior reports to reflect this dynamic.

## Risk Assessment

The dominant risk is pure gold-price risk: GLD tracks gold roughly 1:1 with no hedging and no active management. Beyond that, the idiosyncratic risks are structural rather than fundamental:

1. **Uninsured custody / capped liability.** The trust does not insure its gold. Custodian liability is limited to the gold's market value at the time a loss is discovered and only for negligence, fraud, or willful default. This is a genuine tail risk, though mitigated by allocated bars, dual custodians, and verified bar counts.
2. **Unallocated-gold counterparty risk.** During create/redeem transfers, the trust is an unsecured creditor of the custodian; custodian insolvency could freeze access.
3. **Benchmark risk.** NAV depends on the LBMA Gold Price PM auction; manipulation or discontinuation could impair NAV.
4. **ETF feedback loop.** Large redemptions across gold ETFs can pressure physical gold prices, a self-reinforcing downside visible in the March 2026 outflows.
5. **No '40 Act / CEA protections; limited shareholder rights.**
6. **Real-rate / inflation regime risk.** Real-rate and inflation tension is a key headwind cited by analysts, currently weighing on gold's safe-haven function.

A footnote worth flagging: the supply/demand data cited in the 10-K comes from Metals Focus, an affiliate of the Sponsor (World Gold Council). The entity marketing gold also supplies the industry demand narrative, so treat that narrative as not fully independent. A PEO transition occurred between the November 2025 10-K (Joseph Cavatoni) and the March 2026 10-Q (Jonathan Spiegel), routine for a sponsor-administered trust but noted for completeness.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing low near-term uncertainty. Near-term ATM IV is 20.3% against 1-year historical volatility of 26.9%, leaving IV cheap by ~6.6 points, favorable for buyers of protection or for expressing a view through options rather than the underlying. The term structure is flat (near 20.3% ≈ far 20.8%), with uncertainty evenly distributed across tenors. Put/call skew is roughly balanced (2.6%), and put/call OI is 0.84, no strong directional tilt. One item: a $360 June 12 put traded 2,013 contracts against open interest of 109 (18.5x), a deeply out-of-the-money hedge ~12% below spot, consistent with some holders buying cheap downside insurance into the June event window. The IV snapshot also collapsed from 39.5% to 20.4% intraday as a near-dated print rolled off, so the headline IV percentile of 0% reflects mechanical reset rather than complacency. Options are not contradicting the neutral fundamental read; they confirm a market that sees no imminent directional catalyst but where a minority is hedging tail downside cheaply.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Central-bank buying remains structural and price-insensitive, de-dollarization compounds on a multi-year timeline, and U.S. fiscal stress and debt concerns provide a durable bid. If real-rate tension eases, for instance if the Fed shifts dovish or growth deteriorates, gold re-rates toward the $4,900-5,400 zone implied by Goldman and the Reuters poll, with JPMorgan's $6,300 as the high-end scenario. That would carry GLD back toward and through its January high near $510.

**Bear Case.** Real-rate and inflation tension persists, the opportunity cost of non-yielding gold keeps the safe-haven reflex suppressed, positioning continues to unwind, and the ETF redemption feedback loop pressures physical prices. Gold retraces, pulling GLD back toward the lower end of its recent range. The anomalous Iran sell-off indicates this regime is live.

The two cases are roughly balanced, which is why this is a HOLD rather than a directional call. The structural demand thesis is real, but the near-term tape is governed by real-rate dynamics and flows, both of which currently lean against fresh exposure.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The next FOMC meeting is a natural reassessment point, given that gold's near-term direction hinges on the real-rate path. Monitor monthly fund flows and the LBMA PM fix.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Real-rate and inflation tension eases decisively while central-bank buying continues, restoring the real-rate tailwind.
- Fund flows turn back to sustained net creations for two-plus consecutive months, confirming the crowded-trade unwind has completed.
- A clear dovish pivot from the Fed that lowers the opportunity cost of holding gold.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Persistent net redemptions accelerating alongside a sustained break below recent support levels, signaling the feedback loop has taken hold.
- A hawkish FOMC outcome that confirms the real-rate regime is durable.
- Evidence that central-bank buying is slowing materially.

For holders, on a pure cost basis, rotating long-term gold exposure into IAU or GLDM captures the same return with less fee drag; GLD's advantage is reserved for liquidity-sensitive and options-driven positioning.

## Conclusion

GLD is a high-quality, liquid, transparently-custodied proxy for spot gold. Its merits and risks are the gold market's, not a company's, and the gold market sits at a two-sided juncture: structurally supported by central-bank demand and fiscal concerns, but near-term constrained by real-rate/inflation tension, reversing fund flows, and positioning that has demonstrably overstretched (the Iran sell-off being the clearest evidence). After the correction from the January all-time high, gold is neither cheap nor expensive against its drivers, and the analyst target distribution is genuinely split. We rate GLD a HOLD with an uncertain rationale, appropriate to retain for diversification but with no asymmetric edge at current levels and a measurable fee disadvantage versus IAU and GLDM for buy-and-hold capital. The score's continued decline across recent reports reflects deteriorating flow and real-rate dynamics, which current data confirms rather than reverses.
