**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

American Assets Trust is a diversified REIT owning office (47% of revenue), retail (22%), multifamily (16%), and mixed-use/hotel (15%) properties in supply-constrained West Coast and Hawaii markets. The stock trades at $22.53, representing approximately 11.1x forward FFO ($2.03 midpoint guidance) with a 6.0% dividend yield. The company faces meaningful structural headwinds in its office portfolio (83-84.5% occupancy vs. 90%+ pre-pandemic) and carries leverage of 6.9x net debt/EBITDA, well above its 5.5x target.

The investment case hinges on two offsetting forces: significant operational risk from office leasing uncertainty and elevated leverage on one side, and extraordinary insider conviction (founder Ernest Rady purchasing $9.3M in open-market shares at $18.53-$22.67 during Feb-May 2026) combined with a defensible 6% yield on the other. FFO guidance was reaffirmed at $1.96-$2.10 for 2026, and estimate revisions have turned modestly positive (+7-10% over 30 days). However, the stock currently trades above analyst consensus targets ($20.50 mean) after a 27% rally from January lows, limiting near-term asymmetry.

This is an income-oriented hold where the yield provides adequate compensation for the risks, but where neither the growth trajectory nor the valuation discount is sufficient to justify active accumulation at current prices.

## Company Overview

AAT operates a geographically concentrated portfolio across six high-barrier-to-entry markets: San Diego, San Francisco Bay Area, Bellevue (WA), Portland (OR), Oahu (HI), and San Antonio (TX). The company is vertically integrated, handling leasing, property management, development, and redevelopment internally.

Key portfolio metrics as of Q1 2026:
- Office: ~4.3M sq ft, 84.5% leased (same-store 86%)
- Retail: ~2.4M sq ft, 97.7% leased (record rents at $30/sq ft)
- Multifamily: 2,302 units, 91.1% occupied
- Mixed-use: 369-room Embassy Suites + 94K sq ft retail in Waikiki

The founder, Ernest S. Rady, controls approximately 35.6% of shares on a fully-diluted basis and serves as Executive Chairman following the December 2024 CEO transition to Adam Wyll.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and FFO Trends:**

Revenue declined 5% in 2025 to $436M, driven by the sale of Del Monte Center, absence of $11M in one-time lease termination fees from 2024, and a $10M settlement received in 2024. Adjusting for these items, organic same-store performance was modestly positive (retail +1%, multifamily +3% cash NOI). FFO per share fell 22% from $2.58 (2024) to $2.00 (2025), a decline that overstates fundamental deterioration due to the non-recurring items.

Q1 2026 showed stabilization: revenue of $110.6M (+1.8% YoY) and FFO of $0.51/share (+$0.04 YoY). Full-year guidance of $1.96-$2.10 implies roughly flat performance vs. 2025's $2.00.

**Balance Sheet:**

Total debt stands at $1.70B with a debt-to-equity ratio of 1.59x. Net Debt/EBITDA of 6.9x significantly exceeds management's 5.5x long-term target. However, the maturity schedule provides breathing room: no maturities until 2027 ($425M), with the remainder spread across 2028-2032+. The April 2026 credit facility extension to April 2030 ($500M revolver + $100M term loan) demonstrates continued lender confidence. Investment-grade ratings from all three agencies (Baa3/BBB-/BBB) remain intact.

Liquidity of $518M ($118M cash + $400M revolver availability) is adequate for near-term obligations.

**Dividend Coverage:**

The Q1 2026 payout ratio of 111% raises legitimate sustainability concerns. Management attributes this to timing of tenant improvement capital deployed ahead of lease commencements, expecting normalization to low-to-mid 90% for remaining 2026 quarters (upper 90% full year). The normal post-IPO range is 65-85%. At $1.36 annual dividend vs. $2.03 FFO midpoint, full-year coverage is adequate at 67%, but the Q1 cash flow shortfall warrants monitoring.

**Earnings Surprises:**

AAT has missed GAAP EPS estimates for 4 consecutive quarters (-10.9% to -38.1% misses). GAAP EPS for a REIT is heavily influenced by depreciation, gains/losses on asset sales, and non-cash items. The relevant metric, FFO, was reaffirmed at guidance midpoint in Q1 2026. However, the persistent GAAP misses reflect that even adjusting for non-cash items, the company has been unable to exceed already-modest expectations.

## Growth Analysis

AAT's growth profile is constrained by several factors:

**Positive catalysts:**
- Signed-but-not-commenced leases contribute ~$0.07/share incremental FFO in 2026, with more in 2027
- La Jolla Commons Tower 3 (49% leased, proposals on additional 30%) represents material upside upon stabilization
- Retail portfolio at record rents with 97.7% occupancy provides stable base
- Multifamily same-store NOI growing 3% with near-full occupancy in San Diego
- Estimate revisions turned positive: current year +7% (30-day), next year +9.8% (30-day)

**Growth constraints:**
- Office occupancy at 83-84.5% with Genentech departure pending Q4 2026
- Waikiki hotel facing reduced Japanese tourism (ADR down 6% Q1 2026)
- Interest expense headwind (+$3.6M in 2025, likely to persist with rates elevated)
- FFO guidance midpoint ($2.03) essentially flat vs. 2025 ($2.00)
- Only 1 analyst providing estimates — extremely thin research coverage

The estimate revision trend (+7-20% across periods) is a modestly positive signal, but the absolute magnitude of estimates ($0.45-0.46/share full year) represents minimal growth. This is a stabilization story, not a growth story.

## Valuation Assessment

At $22.53, AAT trades at:
- 11.1x forward FFO ($2.03 midpoint)
- 74.8x trailing GAAP P/E (distorted by depreciation/non-cash items — not relevant for REIT valuation)
- 6.0% dividend yield
- ~$23/share implied by 11.3x high-end FFO guidance ($2.10)

**Peer comparison:**

The peer median P/E of 31.8x is not directly comparable given varying REIT structures, but ALEX (23.5x P/E, 6.7% yield) and LXP (35.3x, 5.3% yield) suggest AAT's 11x FFO multiple reflects a meaningful discount. This discount is partially justified by: (1) elevated leverage vs. peers, (2) significant office exposure in a structurally challenged sector, (3) chronic TSR underperformance vs. peer group (84.8% cumulative vs. 106.7% over 5 years).

**The critical valuation question:** Is 11x FFO cheap enough to compensate for the risks? At the $2.03 midpoint, the stock would need to re-rate to 13-14x FFO to reach $26-28 (15-24% upside). However, the stock currently trades above the analyst consensus mean target of $20.50 and at the high target of $23.00. This limits near-term technical upside unless estimates rise materially.

The 6% yield provides meaningful total return cushion even without price appreciation. At 11x forward FFO with no maturities until 2027, the stock is not distressed but also not clearly mispriced given the operational headwinds.

## Competitive Landscape

AAT's competitive positioning is bifurcated:

**Strengths:**
- Infill, supply-constrained coastal markets where new development is extremely difficult
- Vertically integrated platform enables operational efficiency
- Google, LPL, Autodesk as office anchors provide credit quality
- Retail portfolio at record occupancy/rents — defensive positioning

**Weaknesses:**
- Office demand concentrating at "top of market" benefits Class A owners, but AAT's 83-84% occupancy suggests its properties don't fully qualify as top-tier in their respective submarkets
- Competing with new multifamily supply in San Diego and Portland
- Waikiki hotel facing structural tourism shift (Japanese visitor decline)
- Single-market concentration (54% California) creates correlated risk

The company's competitive position is stable but not improving meaningfully. The spec suite strategy and TI spending on leased-but-not-commenced space are operational tactics, not structural competitive advantages.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Office Occupancy (HIGH):** If the work-from-home secular trend deepens during economic slowdown, office demand could deteriorate further. La Jolla Commons Tower 3 at 49% leased and One Beach Street at 36% represent concentrated vacancy risk.

2. **Leverage/Refinancing (MODERATE-HIGH):** $425M maturing in 2027 in the current rate environment may refinance at higher coupons that compress FFO. The investment-grade rating provides access but not rate certainty.

3. **Dividend Sustainability (MODERATE):** Consecutive quarters with payout ratio above 100% would signal structural pressure. A dividend cut would likely trigger 15-20% additional downside given the income-focused investor base.

4. **Macroeconomic Sensitivity:** AAT faces dual pressure from elevated rates (compressing valuations and increasing interest expense) and potential economic slowdown (threatening tenant demand across office and hotel segments).

5. **May 2026 Material Agreement (UNRESOLVED):** The 8-K filed May 11, 2026 with Item 3.03 (Material Modification of Securityholders' Rights) could represent a preferred stock issuance or convertible instrument that dilutes common shareholders. Combined with Rady's buying, this could also represent positioning for a strategic transaction. Ambiguity itself is a risk.

**Options Market Signal:**

ATM IV of 103.3% is extremely elevated for a REIT, reflecting significant uncertainty about the company's trajectory. The put/call OI ratio of 0.01 is essentially zero, suggesting extremely thin options liquidity rather than a directional signal. The options market is not providing useful sentiment information due to illiquidity.

**Short Interest:**

Short interest of 6.9% with 6.1 days to cover represents moderate bearish positioning — not at squeeze-risk levels but indicating meaningful skepticism among informed participants.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case:**
- Rady's $9.3M in purchases at $18.53-$22.67 signals conviction that the stock is worth materially more, potentially foreshadowing strategic action (take-private, preferred issuance to delever, JV partnership)
- Office leasing momentum (signed-but-not-commenced at $0.07/share incremental FFO, proposals on 30% additional at Tower 3) drives FFO toward high end of guidance ($2.10) or above
- 6% yield + dividend safety (67% full-year payout ratio on midpoint FFO) provides attractive total return
- Retail and multifamily segments provide defensive base that limits downside
- If rates decline, REIT re-rating would disproportionately benefit leveraged, yield-oriented names

**Bear Case:**
- Office secular decline deepens; 83% occupancy becomes the new normal or deteriorates further as Genentech departs and economic slowdown reduces tenant demand
- 2027 refinancing of $425M occurs at significantly higher rates, compressing FFO by $0.10-0.15/share
- Dividend cut becomes necessary if FFO declines below $1.80/share, triggering forced selling from income funds
- Chronic peer underperformance (84.8% cumulative TSR vs. 106.7% over 5 years) reflects structural governance/portfolio issues that Rady's buying doesn't address
- Trading above analyst consensus targets suggests near-term upside is limited; rally from $17.72 lows may have already captured the recovery

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**For HOLD (Income):**

**Upgrade triggers to BUY:**
1. Stock pulls back to $19.50 or below (9.6x FFO, 7%+ yield) while FFO guidance maintained — would create attractive entry for income with growth optionality
2. Office occupancy reaches 87%+ with visible path to 90% within 12 months
3. Rady files a 13D amendment indicating consideration of strategic alternatives or take-private

**Downgrade triggers to AVOID:**
1. Q2 or Q3 2026 FFO below $0.48/share, indicating full-year tracking below guidance low end ($1.96)
2. Dividend cut or suspension
3. Office occupancy declining below 80% (would signal structural impairment)
4. 2027 maturity refinanced at terms that compress forward FFO below $1.80/share

**Reassessment timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (late July/early August 2026). Key data points: office leasing pipeline conversion, same-store NOI trajectory, any clarity on May 2026 material agreement.

## Conclusion

AAT is a HOLD for income-oriented accounts. The 6% yield at 11x forward FFO, backed by founder open-market purchases at essentially current prices, provides sufficient fundamental support. The estimate revision trend, while modest in absolute terms, has turned positive. FFO guidance was reaffirmed, and the balance sheet has no imminent maturity pressure.

However, the stock trades above analyst consensus targets after a 27% rally from January lows. Office headwinds persist structurally with a known departure pending. Leverage remains elevated, and the Q1 payout ratio demands continued monitoring. In a rate environment where leveraged REITs with below-average growth profiles face structural headwinds, re-rating potential is limited.

Rady's insider signal is the single strongest argument against active avoidance. When the founder who already owns 35.6% spends $9.3M buying at $18.53-$22.67, the downside case must explain what he's missing. The most likely explanation is that Rady sees operational stabilization ahead (leasing pipeline, capital recycling benefits) combined with potential strategic optionality that justifies current prices for a long-duration holder. For public market participants without that optionality and timeline, the position warrants holding for income but not active accumulation at $22.53.
