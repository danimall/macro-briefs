**Research Date: July 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Sonoco has spent the past 18 months converting itself from a diversified packaging conglomerate into a focused "metal-and-paper" company. The centerpiece was the ~$3.8B December 2024 acquisition of Eviosys, Europe's leading food-can maker, funded by debt and paid down using ~$2.5B in proceeds from divesting the TFP (Toppan) and ThermoSafe (Arsenal) businesses. The transformation is real, but the reported numbers obscure more than they reveal. The $1.0B GAAP net income in 2025 is inflated by roughly $730M of after-tax divestiture gains; the honest run-rate is adjusted EPS of $5.71 and adjusted EBITDA of $1.32B. Read on that basis, the stock at ~$56 trades around 9.5–10x adjusted earnings, cheap on the surface but for identifiable reasons.

Sonoco is a stable business, with two-thirds of sales tied to defensive food/snack packaging and a record quarterly operating cash flow of $301M in Q2 2026. The issue is the risk stacked on top of a thin balance sheet. Goodwill and intangibles total ~$5.2B against book equity of only $3.6B, meaning tangible equity is deeply negative. The Metal Packaging EMEA reporting unit (the Eviosys asset) passed its Q3 2025 goodwill test with a fair-value cushion of only ~3.1%, and management explicitly flags it as "at risk of impairment in the near term." A modest EBITDA miss or a small increase in the discount rate would trigger a non-cash write-down of up to ~$1.4B, cratering already-thin equity and potentially pressuring covenants and ratings.

We rate SON a HOLD on an income basis. The ~3.9% dividend is well-covered by improving cash flow, the deleveraging story (debt down from ~$7.0B to $4.3B) is genuine, and the market's skepticism is largely priced into a low adjusted multiple. But there is no near-term catalyst for re-rating: organic volumes are soft, estimate revisions are flat-to-negative, the divestiture lever that funded the deleveraging is now spent, and the goodwill overhang caps upside. The score trajectory (6.0 → 4.7 over four months) reflects deteriorating fundamentals in growth and management quality, and this analysis confirms rather than reverses that trend.

## Company Overview

Sonoco is a ~$7.5B revenue global packaging manufacturer headquartered in Hartsville, South Carolina, with roughly 22,000 employees across ~265 operations in 37 countries. Post-transformation, it reports two core segments:

- **Consumer Packaging (~65% of sales, $4.87B in 2025):** metal food cans, ends & closures, aerosol cans (largely the acquired Eviosys/Metal Packaging EMEA business), and rigid paper containers (composite paper cans). 2025 segment operating margin 12.9%; adjusted EBITDA margin 17.2%.
- **Industrial Paper Packaging (~$2.3B in 2025):** tubes, cores, and uncoated recycled paperboard (URB). 2025 operating margin 13.6%; adjusted EBITDA margin 19.2%, the higher-margin, more resilient segment, and currently the stronger performer.

The strategic pivot was deliberate: shed diversified businesses, concentrate on metal and fiber packaging at global scale. Eviosys added ~6,500 employees and 44 plants across 17 countries and moved Sonoco to a top position in EMEA metal food cans. The trade-off is a different kind of geographic concentration risk: ~52% of sales are now outside the U.S. (48% U.S., 43% EMEA), a sharp increase in international and FX exposure versus its historically U.S.-centric base.

## Financial Analysis

The headline figures require careful adjustment:

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue (cont. ops) | $6.78B | $5.31B | $7.52B (+42%) |
| GAAP Net Income | $475M | $164M | $1,003M |
| Adjusted EPS | $5.26 | $4.89 | $5.71 |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $1.07B | $1.04B | $1.32B |
| Total Debt | ~$3.1B | ~$7.0B | $4.3B |

Three things stand out. First, the +42% revenue jump is almost entirely Eviosys (~$2.2B), not organic; organic volume/mix was flat-to-negative, with the Industrial segment posting volume declines and pricing (+$178M) carrying the organic contribution. Second, the GAAP net income is not run-rate earnings, being inflated by the divestiture gains; the cleaner read is adjusted EPS of $5.71 (up ~17% YoY) and adjusted EBITDA of $1.32B, both genuinely improved by the Eviosys addition. Third, deleveraging is the operational story, contractually required under the term-loan structure and funded by the TFP and ThermoSafe sales.

Cash generation is improving. 2025 operating cash flow was $690M and FCF $346M (a modest ~4.6% FCF margin, weighed down by capex and divestiture taxes). Q2 2026 FCF was $237M (+139% YoY). H1 2026 operating cash flow was a use of $(67)M, but that figure includes ~$103M of one-time taxes on 2025 divestiture gains; underlying generation is intact. Capex is guided down ~10% to ~$309M in 2026 to prioritize debt paydown.

The balance sheet is where the risk concentrates. The Altman Z-Score of 1.73 sits in the "distress zone," driven by high leverage, thin working capital (current ratio ~1.05), and an intangible-heavy asset base — not imminent insolvency, since Sonoco remains an investment-grade, dividend-paying issuer, but the score correctly flags the watch items. Debt/equity is 2.08. Goodwill ($2.5B) plus intangibles ($2.7B) is about 47% of total assets. Tangible equity is deeply negative, so any impairment would hit reported equity hard.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth picture is weak. Q2 2026 net sales fell 1.3% YoY (mostly the ThermoSafe divestiture), and Consumer volumes declined 1.8%. Growth is being carried by pricing and productivity, not units. The bright spots are narrow: Industrial is outperforming (North America URB trade tons +6%, mill utilization at 95%, backlogs expanding to the point of importing paper from Europe and Latin America), and Consumer paper cans are strong in EMEA/APAC (+9% on snack demand). Against this, metal aerosol and adhesive/sealant tube demand is weak, and Consumer operating profit fell 5% YoY (though +22% sequentially).

Management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance of sales $7.25–7.75B, adjusted EPS $5.80–6.20 (explicitly guiding toward the low end), adjusted EBITDA $1.25–1.35B, and operating cash flow $700–800M. The margin-improvement program targets +200 bps by end of 2028, driven by Eviosys synergy capture and procurement/productivity savings. This is a credible plan, but it is an efficiency story, not a volume-growth story; the demand backdrop is defensive at best.

Estimate revisions confirm the muted trajectory: current-quarter EPS estimates are down 5.8% over 90 days, next-quarter down 2.3%, and current/next-year essentially flat. There is no positive revision velocity here, which is what would warrant reading trailing multiples as trough earnings. The earnings track record is mixed (2 beats, 2 misses over four quarters, with the recent Q2 beat by a thin 0.9%).

## Valuation Assessment

On adjusted metrics, Sonoco is genuinely cheap. At ~$56 against $5.71 adjusted 2025 EPS (or ~$5.90 near the low end of 2026 guidance), the stock trades at roughly 9.5–10x adjusted earnings, versus a peer median P/E of 15.5. The dividend yields ~3.9% at $0.54/quarter, above the peer median of 2.6%, and is well-covered by cash flow.

Peer comparables in Paper & Plastic Packaging show wide dispersion: Sealed Air (SEE) 12.2x, Graphic Packaging (GPK) 11.6x, Crown (CCK) 15.5x, Avery Dennison (AVY) 17.9x, Amcor (AMCR) 26.4x. Sonoco's discount to the group is real, but it is a discount the market assigns for identifiable reasons: the highest leverage in the peer set, a live goodwill-impairment risk, spent divestiture optionality, negative organic volumes, and a 5-year TSR of 88.4 versus a peer group at 141.6. A low multiple that reflects structural balance-sheet and execution overhangs is a value-trap warning, not a buy signal, and the discipline here is to require an independent catalyst before rewarding the cheapness. None is currently visible.

Analyst price targets ($55 low / $69 high, $62 mean, $63 median) do imply upside from ~$56. But at a Materials-sector name with negative organic growth and flat estimates, we treat consensus targets as a two-sided range rather than a floor — the "targets are floors" logic applies to inflection stocks with rising estimates, which SON is not. Our base case is that the stock trades sideways-to-modestly-higher on the deleveraging story and yield support, with the goodwill risk capping the multiple. Fair value sits around $58–60, roughly consistent with the low-to-mid analyst range.

## Competitive Landscape

Sonoco competes in fragmented, cost-competitive packaging markets against larger and/or better-funded players. Post-Eviosys, it now competes at global scale in metal food cans (a top EMEA position). The competitive trajectory is stable-to-modestly-improving in fiber packaging — Industrial URB is running at 95% utilization with expanding backlogs, and paper cans are gaining on snack demand — but stable-to-weakening in metal aerosol and discretionary industrial categories.

Two structural dynamics matter. First, both customer and supplier consolidation are intensifying pricing pressure from both sides, a persistent headwind to margin in commoditized packaging. Second, sustainability regulation cuts both ways: pressure on single-use plastics and PFAS could favor Sonoco's fiber-based products, but EU CSRD and California climate disclosure (starting 2026) raise compliance costs. Management's argument that the transformed portfolio is more resilient across cycles is supported by the Q2 data — food/snack demand offset industrial/discretionary weakness — but resilience is a defensive attribute, not a share-gaining one. We score competitive position at neutral: the moat is neither widening meaningfully nor eroding, and the Eviosys scale benefit is offset by soft end-market volumes.

## Risk Assessment

**Goodwill impairment on Eviosys — the single most specific risk.** The Metal Packaging EMEA reporting unit passed its Q3 2025 annual goodwill test with fair value exceeding carrying value by only ~3.1% (goodwill of $1.40B). A discount-rate increase to just 12.7% (from 12.0%) or a ~3% EBITDA miss would trigger impairment. This is a live risk on an asset purchased at full price 13 months ago; a write-down of up to $1.4B would crater already-thin book equity and could pressure covenants and credit ratings.

**Leverage and refinancing.** $4.3B debt, with ~$538M maturing in 2026 and $300–600M annually 2027–2030. Roughly $0.6B is floating rate, exposing the company to the higher-for-longer rate path. With the portfolio simplified, management notes the ability to raise cash from future asset sales to delever is now largely spent; future deleveraging must come from operating cash flow, raising the stakes on Eviosys delivering and the 200-bps margin plan executing.

**Integration and FX.** Eviosys is the largest deal in company history; synergy capture, customer retention, and European margin performance all feed directly into the goodwill risk. The company runs $2.3B+ notional cross-currency net-investment hedges (in a $207M loss position at year-end 2025, offset in OCI), and carries residual Russia (~1% of sales, sanctions-exposed) and Turkey (hyperinflationary accounting) exposure via Eviosys.

**Tariffs.** Section 232 tariffs impose a 50% duty on imported steel/aluminum, a direct input cost on metal packaging. Management cites contractual pass-through ability, but with a lag, and the February 2026 Supreme Court invalidation of certain tariffs adds uncertainty.

**Management churn.** A cluster of Item 5.02 (director/officer departure/election) 8-K filings spans Jan 2025 through May 2026, including a new CFO (Paul Joachimczyk, ~34K shares) and a new CHRO (Andrea White, ~151 shares). C-suite turnover during the biggest integration in company history is a governance yellow flag.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not confirming acute near-term stress despite the elevated short interest. Near-term ATM IV is 29.8% versus 1-year historical volatility of 32.8%, a fair-to-slightly-cheap spread. Put/call skew is balanced (1.3% near-term), and the implied move for the nearest expiry is ±6.9%. The term structure is in mild backwardation (29.8% near-term vs. 26.0% at six months), and the recent options snapshot shows ATM IV collapsing from 40.6% to 29.8% as the Q2 earnings event resolved. The put/call OI ratio rose from 0.47 to 0.88, indicating some post-earnings hedge accumulation but nothing extreme. The market is not pricing an imminent catastrophic event, consistent with the goodwill risk being a slow-burn overhang tested quarterly rather than a dated binary. Short interest at 13.5% of float (7.8 days to cover) reflects institutional skepticism about leverage and integration, but is not at squeeze-inducing levels.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($66–69):** Eviosys integration delivers on synergies, the 200-bps margin plan tracks, and the Metal Packaging EMEA reporting unit's EBITDA improves enough to widen the goodwill cushion and remove the impairment overhang. Operating cash flow of $700–800M drives continued deleveraging below 3x, credit rating stabilizes, and the market re-rates the adjusted P/E from ~9.5x toward the low-teens peer range. The ~3.9% dividend provides a total-return floor while the multiple expands. This gets the stock to roughly the high end of the analyst range.

**Bear Case ($42–45):** A modest European EBITDA miss or a rate-driven discount-rate increase triggers a large non-cash goodwill impairment (up to $1.4B), craters book equity, pressures covenants/ratings, and raises borrowing costs. Organic volumes stay soft, the 200-bps margin plan slips, and with the divestiture lever spent, deleveraging stalls. The multiple compresses further and the stock retests the 52-week low ($38.65), a ~22–25% decline from current levels.

**Base Case ($58–60):** The company muddles through — deleveraging continues off operating cash flow, the dividend is maintained and modestly raised, Eviosys margins improve but not dramatically, and the goodwill unit stays just above water without fully removing the overhang. The stock trades sideways-to-modestly-higher, delivering the dividend plus low-single-digit price appreciation. This is the most probable outcome.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Horizon: 12 months, with the key checkpoints being each quarterly earnings release (management commentary on EMEA EBITDA is the leading indicator each quarter) and the annual goodwill test in Q3 2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- The Metal Packaging EMEA goodwill cushion widens materially (management commentary or disclosure indicating fair value comfortably exceeds carrying value), removing the impairment overhang.
- Positive estimate-revision inflection — current-year adjusted EPS estimates turning up rather than flat-to-down over a 90-day window.
- Confirmed deleveraging below ~3x net debt/EBITDA with a stable or improved credit rating, plus evidence the 200-bps margin plan is ahead of schedule.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A goodwill impairment charge on the Eviosys unit, or explicit disclosure that fair value has fallen below carrying value.
- A credit-rating downgrade or covenant pressure that threatens the commercial paper program.
- A dividend cut or a signal that deleveraging is stalling (operating cash flow falling below the $700M guidance floor).

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the Q3 2026 earnings release and annual goodwill test.

## Conclusion

Sonoco is a stable, cash-generative, defensively positioned packaging company trading at a genuine discount to peers on adjusted earnings, with a well-covered ~3.9% dividend and a credible deleveraging path. Those attributes support ownership for income. But the discount exists for reasons the market has correctly identified: the balance sheet is thin (tangible equity deeply negative), the $1.4B Eviosys goodwill sits on a ~3.1% cushion that management itself flags as at-risk, the divestiture lever that funded deleveraging is now spent, organic volumes are soft, estimate revisions are flat-to-down, and there has been notable C-suite churn during the largest integration in company history. The declining score trajectory (6.0 → 4.7) reflects this deterioration, and the current data confirms it.

We rate SON a **HOLD (income_hold)**. The yield and deleveraging optionality justify holding, but there is no near-term catalyst for re-rating and a specific, quarterly-tested downside risk in the goodwill overhang. We would become constructive on a widening of the Eviosys goodwill cushion plus a positive estimate-revision turn; we would move to AVOID on an impairment charge, a rating downgrade, or a dividend cut.
