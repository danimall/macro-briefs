**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

United Bankshares is a ~$33.7 billion-asset, Charleston, West Virginia–headquartered regional bank with a Mid-Atlantic and Southeastern franchise (West Virginia, Virginia, the DC metro/Northern Virginia, the Carolinas, and now metro Atlanta via the Piedmont acquisition). The investment case is straightforward: a fortress balance sheet (CET1 13.27%, risk-based capital 15.54%), a multi-decade dividend-increase streak among the longest in U.S. banking, record FY2025 net income of $464.6M, and a clean post-Piedmont earnings step-up that lifted Q1 2026 ROA to 1.49% and ROTCE to 14.4%. The bank has beaten consensus EPS in each of the last four quarters and consensus estimates are drifting higher (current-year EPS revised +2.7% over 90 days to $3.61).

The offsetting reality is twofold. First, the stock has done its work: at $45.29 it sits within ~2% of its 52-week high of $46.09, up 26% over the trailing year, trading at ~12.7x trailing earnings against a 14.7x regional-bank peer median. That is a fair-to-modest discount, but the easy re-rating from the early-2025 merger trough is behind it. Second, beneath the record headline lies a concentrated and slowly deteriorating credit problem: non-owner-occupied (NOO) commercial real estate is the single largest loan book at $8.5B and drives roughly 74% of total nonaccruals ($67.1M of $91.2M), with $35.8M of net charge-offs in FY2025 and management explicitly increasing reserves on the office portfolio because of hybrid/remote work. The provision normalization that powered the YoY earnings surge reflects the disappearance of Piedmont's one-time day-one CECL charge, not improving office credit.

We rate UBSI a HOLD on an income-hold basis. The 3.3% dividend is well covered (payout ~43% of EPS), the buyback is accelerating below the current price (1.74M shares repurchased in Q1 at $39.92), and easing deposit costs are a sector tailwind. But the valuation has largely captured the post-merger normalization, the accretion kicker supporting NIM is fading, and the office-CRE overhang caps the risk-adjusted upside. We see this as a name to own for capital return and downside resilience, not for appreciation.

## Company Overview

United Bankshares operates a single bank subsidiary, United Bank, within one reportable segment (community banking). As of Q1 2026 it carries ~$24.9B of gross loans and ~$27.1B of deposits, with a loan/deposit ratio of ~92%. The business is a full-service community bank: commercial and retail lending, residential mortgage origination and sale, trust, and brokerage.

The loan mix defines the risk profile. Commercial/financial/agricultural lending totals $14.4B, of which NOO CRE is the largest single book at $8.5B, owner-occupied CRE $2.1B, and other commercial $3.8B. Residential real estate is $6.1B, construction and land development $3.5B, and consumer $0.8B. This is a CRE-heavy balance sheet, the central consideration for credit risk.

United's two distinguishing features are capital discipline and serial acquisition. It has raised its dividend annually for decades, placing it among the longest such streaks in U.S. banking, and Piedmont (closed January 2025, ~$2.4B assets) was the latest of roughly 30 deals. The bank is run conservatively, with capital ratios well above regulatory minimums, strong reserve coverage, and a low-cost core deposit base. Leadership is founder-family-controlled: Richard M. Adams, Jr. is CEO, and his father Richard M. Adams, Sr. is Executive Chairman.

## Financial Analysis

Q1 2026 marked a clean step-up from the merger noise of early 2025. Net income of $124.2M ($0.89 diluted EPS) compared to $84.3M ($0.59) in Q1 2025, when results were depressed by $30.0M of Piedmont merger costs including an $18.7M day-one CECL provision on non-PCD loans. The result was roughly flat versus the $128.8M / $0.91 of Q4 2025.

Profitability metrics are strong for a community bank of this size:
- ROA (annualized) 1.49%, up from 1.06% a year earlier
- ROTCE (non-GAAP) 14.40%, up from 10.61%
- Efficiency ratio ~48%
- NIM 3.80%, up 11 bps YoY (down 3 bps linked-quarter)

The margin story is favorable but partly mechanical. Interest-bearing deposit cost fell 36 bps YoY to 2.49%, outpacing a 13 bps decline in earning-asset yield, the Fed-cut deposit-repricing tailwind benefiting the regional-bank group broadly. However, acquired-loan accretion contributed $7.5M to Q1 2026 NII, and that purchase-accounting kicker is already down ~12% sequentially and will continue to erode without a new acquisition. Core NIM is therefore somewhat below the reported 3.80%.

The single largest driver of the YoY earnings surge was the provision: $7.8M in Q1 2026 versus $29.1M in Q1 2025. That swing is almost entirely the absence of Piedmont's one-time day-one CECL charge, not an improvement in underlying credit. This distinction matters because the office-CRE book continued to deteriorate during the period.

The balance sheet is a genuine fortress. Risk-based capital 15.54%, CET1 13.27%, leverage 11.24%, equity/assets 16.28%, all well above well-capitalized thresholds. Goodwill of $2.02B (~37% of $5.49B equity) reflects the acquisitive history, leaving tangible common equity around $3.5B. The AFS portfolio carries $226M of gross unrealized losses (rate-driven, not credit) and AOCI is a $149M loss, manageable against the capital cushion; the HTM book is negligible at $1M. Liquidity is ample: $2.3B cash, $9.2B unused FHLB capacity, $4.59B FRB discount-window capacity.

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly. The dividend was raised to $0.38/quarter (~3.3% yield, ~43% payout). Buybacks are accelerating: 1.74M shares were repurchased in Q1 2026 at an average $39.92, well below the current price, with ~3.06M shares remaining on the 5M-share November 2025 authorization. The share count is shrinking.

## Growth Analysis

UBSI is a defensive, capital-disciplined compounder rather than a high-growth name. Organic loan and deposit growth in attractive Southeastern and Mid-Atlantic markets (DC metro, Northern Virginia, the Carolinas, Atlanta) provides a mid-single-digit base, layered with episodic M&A.

Consensus estimates are rising modestly: current-quarter EPS revised +2.5% over 90 days to $0.88, current-year EPS +2.7% to $3.61, and next-year +1.2% to $3.79. That implies ~10% EPS growth in 2026 over the trailing $3.27 and a further ~5% in 2027. The four-quarter beat record (2.7%, 3.4%, 10.0%, 9.4% surprises) supports confidence in the current consensus and argues management guidance is conservative.

These are positive but modest revisions, well below the +30% threshold that would change the valuation framework. The growth is durable and credible, not accelerating. Two specific headwinds temper the forward trajectory: the fading accretion tailwind, and the bank's asset-sensitivity, which means further Fed cuts modestly pressure NII (the disclosed -200bp scenario turns NII negative in year two). The clearest upside lever to growth is a new acquisition, given the $2B+ of capital dry powder, which would refresh accretion and add scale; absent that, this is a mid-single-digit grower.

## Valuation Assessment

At $45.29, UBSI trades at ~12.7x trailing EPS of $3.27 and ~12.5x the $3.61 current-year consensus. Against the regional-bank peer median P/E of 14.7x (GBCI 22.8x, HWC 14.7x, ABCB 13.7x, FNB 11.3x), UBSI sits at a modest discount. The 3.3% dividend yield is above the 2.8% peer median, providing yield support.

The discount is partly justified and partly a function of UBSI's profile. On the positive side, the bank's ROA, capital ratios, and dividend track record are above the peer-group average, which would typically argue for a premium, not a discount. On the cautionary side, the goodwill-heavy balance sheet (price/tangible book is richer than headline P/B), the office-CRE concentration, and the fading accretion kicker explain why the market is not paying up.

We do not treat the trailing multiple as a deep-value signal. This is not an earnings-inflection situation: estimate revisions are +2.7%, not +30%, and revenue growth is mid-single-digit, so cycle-adjusted upside is limited. A reasonable fair value is ~14x the $3.61 current-year estimate, or roughly $50, which aligns with the analyst high target of $50 and is modestly above the mean of $47.40. At $45.29, the stock trades within ~10% of that fair-value anchor and within ~2% of its 52-week high. The asymmetry is no longer attractive: most of the post-merger re-rating has occurred, and the path to materially higher prices requires either a fresh accretive acquisition or evidence that the office-CRE book is stabilizing. We view the valuation as fair, not cheap, and score it accordingly. A 12.7x multiple is not by itself a reason to buy a bank with a deteriorating concentrated credit book and a fading earnings kicker.

## Competitive Landscape

United's competitive position is stable-to-modestly-improving: a defensive, capital-disciplined regional with offensive optionality. With CET1 at 13.3% and $2B+ of deployable capital, it is structurally an acquirer in a consolidating industry, an advantage over capital-constrained peers. Its NIM, ROA, and efficiency are solid-to-strong for a community bank of its size.

The franchise has shifted its center of gravity toward higher-growth Southeastern and Mid-Atlantic markets via Piedmont's Atlanta footprint, the Carolinas, and the DC metro corridor. The low-cost core deposit base and easing deposit costs are durable advantages in the current cycle.

The competitive headwind is sector-wide rather than company-specific: the office-CRE overhang from hybrid work, where UBSI's elevated NOO-CRE nonaccruals are a particular manifestation. There is no evidence of share erosion or moat compression; the trajectory is steady, with M&A optionality providing the upside path. We score competitive position above the sector midpoint but below the "structurally improving" tier, reflecting a leader maintaining position rather than rapidly gaining it.

## Risk Assessment

**Office/CRE concentration — the dominant idiosyncratic risk.** NOO CRE is the largest single book and where credit is deteriorating:
- NOO CRE nonaccruals of $67.1M, ~74% of total nonaccruals
- NOO CRE 90+ days past due of $71.5M
- $3.9M of NOO CRE charge-offs in Q1 2026, the largest loss category by far
- $277M of NOO CRE on Special Mention and $181M on Substandard
- ~$110M of collateral-dependent NOO CRE

Management increased reserves only on the office segment in Q1 2026, explicitly citing hybrid/remote work. This is not yet a solvency concern — ALLL/NPL coverage is ~291% and net charge-offs run at 0.15% annualized against a well-reserved base — but the leading indicators are creeping. Total nonperforming assets rose to $113.2M from $110.3M sequentially, and 90+ days-and-accruing more than doubled to $11.7M.

**Fading accretion tailwind.** The purchase-accounting accretion supporting NIM will erode absent new M&A. This is a known, modeled drag on forward NII.

**Goodwill/M&A dependence.** Goodwill is exposed to impairment if franchise value falls (last tested 9/30/2025, no impairment). The growth model leans on continued acquisition, which carries integration and deposit-attrition risk.

**Interest-rate/AOCI risk.** Asset-sensitive (+200bp = +4.23% NII year one); further Fed cuts modestly pressure NII, with year-two downside scenarios turning negative (-200bp = -4.94% NII). The AFS unrealized loss would widen if long rates rise.

**Family control/key-person.** The Adams Sr./Adams Jr. Executive Chairman/CEO structure and unusually flat 1.7x CEO-to-NEO pay ratio reflect a tightly held founder-family culture, a continuity strength but a governance consideration a quantitative screen would miss.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 28.3% versus 23.3% trailing realized vol, a rich 4.9-point spread, and the term structure is in backwardation (near-term 28.3% > long-term 24.6%), indicating the market is pricing some imminent uncertainty. Put/call skew of 4.1% shows mildly elevated put demand, though put/call volume (0.11) and OI (0.08) ratios remain low; overall positioning is not bearish. IV has risen 4.2 points since late May alongside a 4.7% price gain, consistent with the market pricing the next earnings event rather than a company-specific deterioration. The implied move of ±7.4% is modest. This is a flag to size carefully into the next print, not a contradiction of the income-hold thesis.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($50–53).** Deposit costs continue to ease as the Fed holds or cuts, sustaining NIM near 3.80%. A fresh accretive acquisition deploys the $2B+ capital dry powder, refreshing the accretion tailwind and adding scale. Office-CRE charge-offs peak and reserves prove adequate, removing the overhang. The buyback continues below intrinsic value, and the dividend rises again, supporting a re-rating toward 14.5–15x forward earnings (~$52–53). Continued beats drive consensus targets higher from the current $47.40 mean.

**Bear Case ($36–38).** Office-CRE deterioration accelerates, with Special Mention/Substandard balances migrating to nonaccrual and charge-offs exceeding reserves, forcing elevated provisioning that compresses earnings. The accretion tailwind fades faster than offset by core NIM, and asset-sensitivity bites if rates fall further. The multiple de-rates toward the lower peer band (~11x) on $3.40 normalized EPS, taking the stock to ~$37 (roughly -18% from current). A failed or overpriced acquisition would compound this.

**Base Case ($48–50).** Steady mid-single-digit EPS growth toward $3.61 in 2026, NIM holding in the high-3.70s as deposit relief offsets accretion fade, office-CRE losses contained within a well-reserved base, and continued capital return. The stock grinds toward ~14x forward earnings (~$50), delivering high-single-digit total return including the dividend.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month income-and-capital-return hold, reassessed at the next earnings print.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Office/NOO CRE nonaccruals and 90+ past due flatten or decline for two consecutive quarters, removing the credit overhang
- A pullback to the low-$40s (toward the $39.92 buyback level), restoring a clear margin of safety against ~$50 fair value
- A new accretive acquisition announced at a sensible multiple that refreshes the accretion tailwind and adds scale, or core NIM (ex-accretion) demonstrably stabilizing above 3.6%

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NOO CRE net charge-offs accelerate materially above the FY2025 $35.8M run-rate, or ALLL/NPL coverage falls below ~250% as nonaccruals migrate
- A goodwill impairment charge, or a dilutive/overpriced acquisition (>1.8x tangible book)
- A dividend cut or buyback suspension signaling capital strain
- Quarterly EPS misses consensus after the four-beat streak, signaling deteriorating visibility

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (late July 2026), with the office-CRE credit-migration tables the primary item to monitor.

## Conclusion

UBSI is a high-quality, fortress-capitalized regional bank delivering record results, beating estimates consistently, and returning capital aggressively through a well-covered dividend and a buyback executed below the current price. The trajectory of our scoring has improved — financial health and management quality both stepped up as the Piedmont integration cleared — and the latest data confirms that improvement on the fundamentals.

What we cannot make is an appreciation case at $45.29. The stock trades within 2% of its 52-week high and at a fair-to-modest discount to peers that is partly justified by the goodwill-heavy balance sheet, the fading accretion kicker, and a concentrated office-CRE book that is the lone area of deteriorating credit. The provision-driven earnings surge reflected the disappearance of a one-time merger charge, not improving office credit, and the leading credit indicators are creeping in the wrong direction off a well-reserved base. We rate UBSI a **HOLD** on an income basis: own it for the dividend, the buyback, and the downside resilience of a 13.3% CET1 balance sheet, but recognize the valuation has captured most of the post-merger normalization. We would become buyers on a pullback toward the low-$40s or on clear evidence the office-CRE overhang is stabilizing.
