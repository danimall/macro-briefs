**Research Date: June 17, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Red Rock Resorts owns a controlling interest in Station Casinos, the dominant operator of locals-oriented casinos in the Las Vegas valley. The franchise rests on a structural moat: Nevada's SB 208 restricts where new casinos can be built, more than 90% of the metro population lives within five miles of a Station property, and the company controls roughly 454 acres of gaming-entitled development land carried at $469M. This produces best-in-class economics: FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $848.6M on $2.01B revenue, a ~42% margin, with a casino-segment margin near 73%. Baron Capital holds the name as a long-duration quality-growth position on the logic of supply-constrained Vegas real estate plus a recurring resident customer base.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is the fundamental shift that surfaced in Q1 2026. After two years of Durango-driven growth, operating income fell 6.9% and Adjusted EBITDA declined 1.1% year over year as SG&A rose 9.2% and D&A rose 15.6% against revenue growth of only 1.9%. Table-games drop fell 3.6% and race/sports write fell 5.0%, pointing to softening at the higher-end locals customer even as slot handle grew. Consensus current-year EPS estimates have been cut 18% over 90 days (to $1.77), and the modest +3.9% 30-day uptick is a bounce off that lower base rather than a true upward turn. Capital returns (a $0.26 regular dividend, a $1.00 special dividend paid in February, and buybacks) are now partly debt-funded: RRR drew $151M net on its revolver in Q1, lifting revolver borrowings to $310M while cash fell to $134M.

The hold thesis is concrete rather than a default. Durango Phase 2 is under construction at a supply-protected site with no major competitor casino within five miles; North Fork (Mono) Native American fee revenue (4% development fee + 30% management fee on a $750M project) begins late 2026 with the principal litigation now resolving in the project's favor; and the land bank gives multi-cycle optionality. Against that sit single-market concentration, $3.55B of debt against a thin GAAP equity base, and 90.2% Fertitta voting control that leaves minority holders without influence over outcomes. At roughly 19.5x trailing and ~33x cut current-year earnings on a business with contracting near-term EBITDA, the risk/reward is balanced. Hold through the 2026 build cycle and reassess on the Durango Phase 2 ramp and North Fork commissioning.

## Company Overview

Red Rock Resorts is a holding company (Up-C structure) whose sole operating asset is a controlling, managing-member interest in Station Casinos LLC. Station operates seven major resorts in the Las Vegas valley (Red Rock, Green Valley Ranch, Durango, Palace Station, Boulder Station, Sunset Station, and Santa Fe Station) plus roughly 14 smaller Wildfire/Seventy Six taverns (three 50%-owned). At year-end 2025 the footprint comprised 16,553 slots, 328 tables, and 2,734 hotel rooms.

The business model is the "locals" play. The customer is the Las Vegas resident, not the tourist, which historically produces more recurring revenue and steadier hold percentages. Approximately 80% of casino revenue comes from slots, a high-margin, low-variable-cost source. Management cites a low-variable-cost structure that drives high incremental flow-through, the source of the ~42% Adjusted EBITDA margin.

The competitive moat is regulatory and real-estate-based. SB 208 (1997) restricts new casino development locations, structurally capping locals-market supply. RRR's six gaming-entitled development parcels (Viva, Flamingo/I-215, Cactus, Inspirada, Skye Canyon, Losee Rd) total ~454 acres at $469M book value. At Durango (opened December 2023 and the company's primary growth engine) gaming and land-use rules mean no major competitor casino exists within roughly five miles. A Durango Phase 2 expansion (expanded casino floor, 36-lane bowling, luxury theaters, food hall) began construction in January 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory.** Annual revenue progressed from $1.66B (2022) to $1.72B (2023), $1.94B (2024), and $2.01B (2025, +3.7%). The 2024 jump of 12.5% reflected Durango's first full year; 2025 decelerated to 3.7% as Durango lapped and renovations created disruption.

**FY2025 profitability.** Operating income was $597.4M (+5.1%); net income attributable to Red Rock was $188.1M (+22% YoY, partly tax-driven). Adjusted EBITDA was $848.6M (+6.6%), with Las Vegas-segment EBITDA of $915.9M against a -$84.9M corporate drag. FY2025 also introduced Native American development fee revenue of $17.6M (including a $6.1M catch-up) and an $8.5M gain on repayment of North Fork advances, items that flatter the year-over-year comparison.

**Q1 2026.** Net revenue rose 1.9% to $507.3M, but operating income fell 6.9% ($143.7M vs $154.4M) and Adjusted EBITDA declined 1.1% ($212.6M vs $215.1M). Las Vegas-segment EBITDA fell 1.5%. The drivers were SG&A +9.2% (employee costs plus Mono reimbursables), D&A +15.6% (new assets in service), and room revenue −9.3% (the Green Valley Ranch tower renovation took rooms offline). Table-games drop fell 3.6% and race/sports write fell 5.0% while slot handle grew 1.2%, the mix consistent with softening at the higher-spend customer. Cost inflation and depreciation from the build cycle are outrunning tepid revenue, compressing margins.

**Cash flow and capital allocation.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $609.5M against $319M capex, leaving roughly $290.5M of free cash flow. Shareholder returns were aggressive: a $0.26 quarterly dividend, a $1.00 special dividend (paid February 2026), and $79M of buybacks ($486M remaining on a $900M authorization extended to December 2027). Q1 2026 returned $75.3M in dividends, $57.8M in NCI distributions, and $38.3M in buybacks. Even after drawing $151M net on the revolver, Q1 financing was net cash-negative: capital returns plus capex are currently part-funded by debt. Revolver borrowings rose from $155M (YE2025) to $310M (March 2026); cash fell to $134M. With 2026 capex guided to $375–425M against EBITDA near $850M, this is sustainable for now but bears monitoring if margins keep compressing.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt is ~$3.55B (March 2026). The maturity ladder: 4.50% notes due 2028 ($690M), 4.625% notes due 2031 ($500M), 6.625% notes due 2032 ($500M), Term Loan B due 2031 ($1.52B), and the revolver due 2029. Roughly $1.7B is variable-rate; a 1% rate move equals ~$17.3M annual interest, partially hedged via $750M notional collars (SOFR cap 5.25% / floor 2.89% through 2029). The maximum total secured leverage covenant is 5.00x, with management stating compliance.

A critical caveat on screening metrics: the Altman Z near 180 and triple-digit ROE/ROA are artifacts of the Up-C structure, not signs of health. GAAP equity is tiny ($142M at March 2026) because 41% of economics sit in noncontrolling interest and the firm intentionally runs near-zero retained equity while returning cash. This is a highly levered equity. The Piotroski current ratio flag (0.79) reflects a deliberately lean, cash-based working-capital model, not distress. An analyst reading the standardized pull at face value would reach the opposite of the truth.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth case rests on three identifiable catalysts. First, **Durango Phase 2**, under construction since January 2026 at a site with no major competitor within five miles, extends the only meaningful organic growth engine. A new parking garage opened in December 2025; the expanded casino floor and amenities follow. Second, **North Fork (Mono)** fee revenue begins late 2026: a 4% development fee plus a 30% management fee on a $750M project, high-margin and capital-light if completed, with the California Supreme Court denying the Mono's petition for review in April 2026 and key disputes resolving in the project's favor. Third, the **land bank** of ~454 entitled acres provides multi-cycle optionality in a supply-capped market.

The near-term picture is weaker than these catalysts imply. Consensus current-year EPS has been cut 18% over 90 days, with the current quarter down 23.3% and next quarter down 21.1% on the same window. The 30-day revisions are modestly positive (+3.9% current year, +7.0% current quarter), but this is a small bounce off a sharply reduced base, not the kind of >+30% upward move that would mark a true earnings recovery. The four-quarter beat record (3.2%, 21.5%, 36.1%, 100%) is strong and supports confidence in the cut estimates, but the most recent quarter (3.2%) shows the beats narrowing as the comparison base hardens and costs rise.

Underlying demand is the swing factor. Las Vegas locals fundamentals are mixed: population grew 1.6% in 2025, but local unemployment runs 5.2–5.4% (above the national average) and median home prices fell 1.0% YoY. The locals consumer faces the same pressures hitting discretionary spending broadly. Slot handle growth (+1.2% in Q1) is holding, but declining table and sports volumes suggest the higher-spend tier is pulling back first. Growth prospects score 5.5: real catalysts maturing in 2026, currently outweighed by margin contraction and a soft demand backdrop.

## Valuation Assessment

RRR trades at ~19.5x trailing earnings and roughly 33x the cut FY2026 consensus of $1.77, with a 1.7% dividend yield. Against peers in Casinos & Gaming, MCRI trades at 21.6x and CHDN at 15.9x, while PENN, CZR, and GDEN carry negative or distorted multiples. The peer median P/E of 21.6x makes RRR's trailing multiple look roughly in line, but the forward multiple on cut estimates is less favorable for a business with contracting near-term EBITDA.

The cycle-adjustment question matters here. The valuation rules direct toward forward/normalized earnings when an earnings inflection is present, but RRR is inflecting the wrong way. EBITDA is contracting, estimates have been cut, and the only positive revision is a small recent bounce. This is not a trough-earnings situation where trailing P/E understates power; it is a peak-margin business facing cost inflation and a heavy build cycle. Applying a generous forward multiple would be unwarranted.

A low multiple alone does not earn a high valuation score, and RRR's multiple is not low. The bull's anchor is asset value: ~454 acres of entitled Vegas land at $469M book plus best-in-class margins arguably support a sum-of-the-parts above the current $3.4B market cap. But that value is illiquid, the Fertittas are reluctant sellers, and a change-of-control would trigger TRA acceleration, so the asset value is not readily monetizable by minority holders. Analyst targets ($55 low, $67.5 median, $77 high) bracket the current price; on a stock with falling estimates and premium near-term valuation, those targets are not floors that consensus will revise up. Valuation scores 5.0: fairly priced, with neither a compelling discount nor a clear overvaluation, and no catalyst to argue the market is mispricing it.

## Competitive Landscape

RRR's competitive position is its strongest dimension. The locals-Vegas franchise is structurally advantaged by SB 208 supply caps and the entitled land bank, which together prevent rivals from replicating its footprint in the highest-growth submarkets. Durango's five-mile competitive exclusion zone is the clearest expression of this moat. The chief locals rival is Boyd Gaming; on the Strip RRR competes only tangentially with Caesars and MGM, whose tourist model is distinct.

The moat is wide but not widening rapidly. Secular watch items include expanding online sports betting and iGaming, plus California Native American gaming (66 facilities, with California being Nevada's top visitor source). Management argues the impact on the locals market has been limited so far, and the data supports that: slot handle is still growing. The Q1 softening in table and sports volumes is more likely cyclical (high-end consumer pullback) than a structural share loss. Competitive position scores 6.5: a durable, supply-protected moat with stable-to-slightly-improving trajectory, capped below 7 because the locals consumer backdrop is softening and the company is not visibly gaining share in a meaningful new vector.

## Risk Assessment

**Single-market concentration.** All operations are in the Las Vegas locals market. The thesis depends entirely on Las Vegas population, employment, and housing; any regional downturn hits everything at once. Local unemployment above the national average and a 1% home-price decline are early yellow flags.

**Leverage and rate sensitivity.** $3.55B debt against a thin GAAP equity base, ~$1.7B floating. Refinancing risk concentrates on the 2028 notes and the TLB. Capital returns are increasingly debt-funded, which is fine at current EBITDA but pressurizes if margins keep compressing.

**Margin compression underway.** Q1 2026 confirmed employee-cost inflation and build-cycle D&A outpacing revenue. This is the crux of the bear case and the reason estimates were cut.

**Construction execution.** Durango Phase 2 and ongoing renovations create disruption (GVR rooms offline) and cost-overrun risk through a capex-heavy 2026.

**North Fork tail.** RRR carries an uncapped-to-$425M completion guaranty and ~$80.6M of unrecognized advances whose recovery hinges on project success. Litigation is mostly resolved in the project's favor, but tail risk persists.

**Labor.** Active NLRB litigation with the Culinary-affiliated Local Joint Executive Board across five properties. Successful organizing would raise labor costs structurally.

**Governance.** The Fertittas control 90.2% of voting power through super-voting Class B shares while holding 41% of economics. Combined with TRA acceleration on change-of-control and Nevada gaming approvals, minority shareholders cannot force outcomes. Alignment is high (the family owns 41% of economics and has delivered roughly 3x peer TSR), but minority holders have no influence over outcomes. A June 12, 2026 8-K (Item 5.02) flags a director/officer change worth monitoring.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 106.3% against 34.4% historical volatility, an extreme spread, with the term structure in steep backwardation (1-week IV 106.3% vs 6-month 33.7%). The put/call OI ratio is 1.63 and skew shows elevated put demand (3.8%), with a ±7.3% implied move on the nearest standard expiry. The IV surge since mid-May (54.7% → 153.5% on one read) alongside a 15.6% price recovery suggests the market is hedging an imminent event: plausibly an M&A-speculation catalyst (post-Caesars buyout chatter), the flagged officer change, or a near-dated data point. Short interest is elevated at 13.8% of float with 4.0 days to cover, consistent with institutional skepticism on the margin story. The rich near-term IV makes this a poor moment to pay up for either calls or the stock; for a holder, the backwardation argues for patience until the event resolves and vol normalizes.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($72–77)

The locals-Vegas franchise is irreplaceable. SB 208 supply caps plus 454 entitled acres mean RRR can grow into demand that rivals cannot serve. Durango Phase 2 ramps through 2026–2027 in a competition-free zone; North Fork fee revenue adds high-margin, capital-light income from late 2026. The Q1 margin dip is transitory, driven by a one-time renovation (GVR rooms offline), build-cycle D&A that levels off, and SG&A that normalizes once growth investments mature. The four-quarter beat streak shows management consistently exceeds estimates. M&A optionality (a Fertitta-led take-private in the wake of Caesars going private) could crystallize asset value above the current multiple. At the high end, ~$77 implies the market rewards the asset base and a re-acceleration of EBITDA growth.

### Bear Case ($50–52)

Q1 marks a genuine inflection: peak margins are rolling over as labor inflation and depreciation permanently reset the cost base higher while revenue growth stalls at low single digits. The locals consumer is softening (declining table and sports volumes, above-average local unemployment, falling home prices), and a discretionary-spending squeeze hits a single-market operator hardest. Debt-funded capital returns become unsustainable if EBITDA keeps contracting, forcing a special-dividend cut. The 90% Fertitta control caps any minority-driven value realization, and the TRA deters third-party buyers. Estimates continue to be cut and the multiple de-rates toward the bear of the 52-week range. The bear target near $50–52 is roughly 12–15% below the current price.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Through the 2026 build cycle, with the next natural checkpoints being the Q2 2026 earnings release (margin trajectory) and the North Fork commissioning in Q4 2026.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Adjusted EBITDA returns to positive YoY growth for two consecutive quarters, confirming the margin dip was transitory rather than structural.
- Current-year EPS estimate revisions turn meaningfully positive (>+10% over 90 days) alongside an EBITDA re-acceleration, signaling the Durango Phase 2 / North Fork contribution is landing.
- A credible, Fertitta-sponsored take-private or value-crystallizing transaction emerges at a premium reflecting the land bank.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A second consecutive quarter of EBITDA contraction combined with a special-dividend or buyback reduction, signaling debt-funded returns are no longer sustainable.
- Leverage approaches the 5.00x covenant or revolver draws continue rising while cash falls below ~$100M.
- Materialization of the North Fork completion guaranty or a labor-organizing outcome that structurally raises the cost base.

## Conclusion

Red Rock owns a genuinely advantaged asset: a supply-protected locals-Vegas franchise with best-in-class margins, an entitled land bank, and two identifiable growth catalysts maturing through late 2026. That asset quality, plus the Durango Phase 2 and North Fork catalysts, supports holding rather than avoiding. But the Q1 2026 shift is real: EBITDA is contracting as cost inflation and depreciation outrun thin revenue growth, estimates have been cut, and capital returns are now part-funded by debt. The stock is fairly priced at ~33x cut forward earnings, not cheap enough to compensate for the near-term margin uncertainty, and 90% founder voting control caps minority influence over outcomes. The score trajectory has declined (6.4 → 6.1 → 5.4) as these headwinds have come into focus; the current data confirms that trend rather than reversing it.

The recommendation is **HOLD** with a growth-hold rationale: hold for the Durango Phase 2 ramp and North Fork fee revenue, and reassess on Q2 margins. New capital should wait for either EBITDA growth to re-accelerate (the upgrade trigger) or a pullback that better compensates for the single-market concentration and leverage.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: the forward P/E on cut FY2026 EPS of $1.77 is ~33x (not ~26x as originally stated), making the stock materially more expensive than the report implied at HOLD.
