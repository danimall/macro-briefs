**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Gilead Sciences is the dominant global HIV franchise, generating roughly $30B in annual sales with high-70s product gross margins and ~$10B of operating cash flow. The investment question is narrow and well-defined: can lenacapavir-based regimens (Yeztugo for PrEP and the BIC/LEN once-daily oral, PDUFA August 27, 2026) absorb and extend the Biktarvy revenue base before Biktarvy faces Medicare Maximum Fair Price negotiation effective 2028? Biktarvy alone is roughly 48% of product sales and about half of total company revenue, and HHS has confirmed the negotiated price will be "substantially lower." Oncology, the inflammation push, and the $11.5B M&A wave are secondary to that single transition.

The stock has fallen from a $157 high in February to $121 currently, a ~23% drawdown, while the headline data screens noisily: FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance was cut from +$8.45–8.85 to a loss of $(1.05)–$(0.65). That cut is an accounting artifact of ~$11.5B of one-time acquired-IPR&D charges from the Arcellx, Tubulis, and Ouro deals being expensed in Q2 2026. Management raised 2026 sales guidance to ~$30.2B and non-GAAP EPS remains comfortably positive (Q1 non-GAAP EPS $2.03 beat). A model reading GAAP EPS literally would badly misjudge the business. On normalized non-GAAP earnings of ~$9.59 next year, the stock trades at roughly 13x, a discount to the biotech peer median P/E of 21x, with a 2.6% dividend.

We rate GILD a HOLD. The valuation is genuinely undemanding and the dividend provides support, but we do not see a clean BUY. The forward thesis depends on a binary, dated franchise-transition outcome where the early evidence is mixed (Yeztugo scripts rising but total PrEP market not expanding, insurance gaps persisting, Florida ADAP dropping Biktarvy). Near-term estimate revisions are falling (next-quarter consensus down 8.2% over 30 days), drug-pricing policy exposure is unusually deep, and management has leaned into an aggressive, debt-funded M&A wave of early-stage assets. The risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric. We hold for the income and the cheap optionality on a successful lenacapavir transition, and we would upgrade on confirmed PrEP market expansion or favorable BIC/LEN approval, not before.

## Company Overview

Gilead operates as a single biopharmaceutical segment from Foster City, CA, anchored in antiviral medicine with growing positions in oncology, liver disease, and an emerging inflammation portfolio. Q1 2026 total revenue was $6.96B, up 4% YoY, distributed as follows:

- **HIV — $5.03B (72% of product sales), +10% YoY.** Biktarvy was $3.36B (+7%), roughly half of total company revenue. Descovy (PrEP) grew 38% to $807M on price and demand. Yeztugo (lenacapavir for PrEP, the twice-yearly injectable launched mid-2025) contributed $166M in its early ramp.
- **Oncology — $810M (+7%).** Trodelvy $402M (+37%, the standout grower); Cell Therapy (Yescarta/Tecartus) $407M (−12%, declining on competition).
- **Liver Disease — $767M (+1%).** HCV (Epclusa/Harvoni) declining; Livdelzi (PBC) ramping to $133M.
- **Veklury (remdesivir) — $144M (−52%)** as COVID hospitalizations fade.

The competitive advantages are entrenched HIV leadership, a deep antiviral R&D engine, lenacapavir's best-in-class long-acting profile, and a cash-generative base that funds aggressive business development. The structural challenge is concentration: one product (Biktarvy) carries the company, and that product is the explicit target of the 2028 Medicare negotiation.

## Financial Analysis

**Operations are strong; GAAP net income is structurally noisy.** FY2024 net income collapsed to $480M on a large CymaBay-related IPR&D charge and the EVOKE-01 Trodelvy impairment, then rebounded to $8.51B in FY2025. Gilead routinely runs large acquired-IPR&D charges through the P&L, which makes GAAP net income a poor read on the underlying business.

Q1 2026 was operationally clean:
- Revenue $6.96B (+4%); product gross margin expanded ~250 bps to 79.2% on a royalty obligation expiry and favorable mix.
- Net income $2.02B (+54%); diluted EPS $1.61 vs. $1.04, but roughly half the YoY jump came from a mark-to-market swing in equity securities (a $142M gain vs. a $426M loss prior year), a non-operating item. Non-GAAP EPS was $2.03, a 4.0% beat.
- Operating cash flow $2.54B (+45%).

**Balance sheet and capital allocation.** Cash and marketable securities are ~$8.6B; total debt $22.2B (down from $24.9B after repaying $2.75B of notes in March 2026); stockholders' equity $23.4B. The dividend was raised to $0.82/quarter (~$1.0B/qtr); buybacks ran $419M in Q1 against a $6.0B authorization. Capital priorities are explicitly R&D, BD, a growing dividend, and buybacks to offset dilution.

The flexibility caveat: Gilead established a $4.7B one-year term loan in April 2026 (initially drawing $1.1B) to help fund the M&A wave. Debt fell in Q1 but will rise with deal closings. Against ~$10B of annual operating cash flow the leverage is manageable, but financial headroom is tightening at the same moment the company is committing to early-stage clinical bets.

**The "FY2026 loss" is an accounting head-fake.** GAAP guidance moved to a loss because the ~$11.5B of acquired IPR&D is expensed in Q2 2026. Sales guidance was raised and non-GAAP EPS remains positive. We score on non-GAAP and normalized earnings power, which is intact and growing.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine is HIV, bifurcated between a declining-eventually flagship (Biktarvy) and the replacement franchise (lenacapavir). HIV grew 10% in Q1, but the durability of that rate depends entirely on the lenacapavir transition succeeding before Biktarvy's exclusivity and pricing erode.

The early lenacapavir data is the central uncertainty. Reuters reporting (5/5/26) indicates Yeztugo scripts are rising but **total PrEP scripts are not growing and US insurance coverage gaps persist** — uptake so far is switching, not market expansion. If Yeztugo is cannibalizing Descovy and Truvada rather than enlarging the PrEP market, the franchise dollars are being reshuffled rather than grown. State payer pushback is a concrete early warning: Florida removed Biktarvy from its ADAP formulary in March 2026 and restricted Descovy access, a thread worth monitoring for contagion to other states.

The near-term catalyst calendar is dense and genuinely decisive:
- **BIC/LEN once-daily oral, PDUFA August 27, 2026** — the franchise life-extension product, with ARTISTRY-1/-2 showing non-inferiority to Biktarvy.
- **Anito-cel (Arcellx) BCMA CAR-T in multiple myeloma, PDUFA December 23, 2026.**
- **Merck/Gilead once-weekly oral HIV pill** met Phase 3 endpoints in June 2026 (Gilead is a co-developer, not a customer, so this hedges its long-acting position).
- Lenacapavir PrEP launching internationally (South Africa via the Global Fund, June 2026).

Oncology is a challenger position with mixed momentum: Trodelvy is winning in TNBC and bladder (+37%) but stumbled in lung (EVOKE-01) and HR+/HER2- breast (ASCENT-07), and cell therapy is declining 12% on competition and bispecific encroachment. The Tubulis ADC and Ouro T-cell-engager deals are bets to build a credible second growth pillar, but they are early-stage and binary.

Estimate revisions reflect this caution. Next-quarter consensus EPS fell 8.2% over 30 days and 23.8% over 90 days; current-year non-GAAP is being pulled down by the GAAP/IPR&D distortion. Next-year EPS ($9.59) is roughly flat over 90 days (−0.3%), the more meaningful signal — the market expects the franchise to hold, not to inflect upward. This is a stable-to-softening near-term picture on a defensive large-cap, not the +30% revision velocity that would flag an earnings inflection.

## Valuation Assessment

On forward non-GAAP earnings, Gilead is cheap relative to peers. At $121 against next-year consensus EPS of $9.59, the stock trades at ~12.6x. The reported forward P/E of 16.5x reflects trailing/distorted earnings; the cleaner normalized figure is the low-13x range. Against a biotech peer median P/E of 21.0x (AMGN 24.3x, VRTX 26.5x, REGN 15.5x, BIIB 21.0x, UTHR 20.3x), Gilead carries a meaningful discount.

The discount is partly deserved and partly opportunity. Deserved, because Gilead has more identifiable medium-term revenue risk than its peers: Biktarvy's 2028 Medicare negotiation, an unusually deep drug-pricing policy entanglement, and a flagship-replacement question that is not yet resolved. Opportunity, because if the lenacapavir transition succeeds, the franchise extends well past 2030 and the multiple has room to re-rate toward the high-teens.

The 2.6% dividend yield (vs. a 0.0% peer median; only AMGN at 2.9% pays comparably) provides downside support and is well-covered against operating cash flow. Analyst targets span $122 (low) to $180 (high) with a $160 median, implying ~32% upside to the median. On a stock that is cheap relative to improving estimates we would treat targets as floors, but Gilead's estimates are stable-to-softening, so we read the target band as a fair distribution of outcomes around a binary catalyst rather than a stale lagging anchor.

We score valuation 6.5: genuinely undemanding on an absolute and relative basis, with a specific mispricing case (peer-discount on a defensive cash machine), but not a screaming bargain given the binary 2028 overhang and the lack of positive earnings momentum.

## Competitive Landscape

Gilead remains the HIV market leader, but the competitive frontier is moving to long-acting regimens, where lenacapavir's twice-yearly injectable profile is arguably best-in-class. The principal threat is GSK/ViiV (long-acting cabotegravir) and the emerging once-weekly oral category, where Gilead has hedged by co-developing with Merck rather than ceding the field. The defensive moat in HIV is real but being actively contested, and the transition from a dominant daily oral (Biktarvy) to long-acting products is a vulnerable handoff window during which a competitor or a payer can take share.

In oncology, Gilead is a challenger, not a leader: Trodelvy is differentiated in select tumors but has missed in others, and cell therapy is losing share. The competitive trajectory in HIV is stable-to-slightly-eroding given long-acting competition and payer pushback, and in oncology it is improving from a small base but unproven at scale. Net, the competitive position is solid but not strengthening, which supports a mid-range score of 6.0.

## Risk Assessment

The risks here are specific, dated, and material — this is the lowest of the six scores.

1. **Single-product concentration + IRA negotiation.** Biktarvy is selected for Medicare Maximum Fair Price effective 2028. This is the dominant medium-term revenue risk.
2. **Patent/generic exposure.** Descovy faces a Cipla 505(b)(2) Paragraph IV challenge (suit filed Feb 2026); the entire growth thesis hinges on transitioning to lenacapavir regimens before exclusivity erodes.
3. **Drug-pricing policy entanglement.** Beyond IRA, the December 2025 administration agreement commits Gilead to MFN pricing in Medicaid, MFN on future launches, a direct-to-patient Epclusa price, and US manufacturing investment, in exchange for a 3-year Section 232 tariff exclusion. The April 2026 proclamation of 100% tariffs on patented pharma (effective H2 2026) makes Gilead's exemption a live, non-guaranteed variable, with ~71% US revenue mix amplifying the exposure.
4. **Launch execution.** Yeztugo uptake is substitution rather than market expansion, with persistent insurance gaps.
5. **M&A/impairment risk.** The 2026 deals are early-stage assets expensed as IPR&D; Trodelvy already carries a $7.3B intangible pressured by two Phase 3 misses, with management flagging possible future impairment.
6. **Litigation.** HIV antitrust class action (Phase II, pending appeal), ~23,000-plaintiff TDF product-liability cases (bellwether March 2027), and a Texas TMFPA qui tam (trial Dec 2026).

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 48.6% versus 26.3% historical volatility — rich, at the 80th percentile within the chain — with the term structure in backwardation (48.6% near-term vs. 28.3% at six months). That configuration prices imminent, scheduled-event stress rather than durable concern, and elevated front-end vol around a known catalyst typically overstates the probability of a sustained move. The skew is call-heavy (−18.4% near-term, put/call volume ratio 0.03), and the single block of unusual activity is 9,538 short-dated $130 calls; positioning is tilted bullish, which is consistent with a defensive HOLD on a stock with a 2.6% dividend. IV has compressed sharply since the prior snapshot (82.3% → 48.6%), consistent with the post-earnings de-risking. There is no put-skew divergence to flag against our stance; the longer-dated put/call OI ratio (0.65) and modest 6-month put skew (−5.3%) are unremarkable for a large-cap biopharma carrying a 2028 pricing overhang.

Short interest is low at 2.1% of float (3.9 days to cover), so there is no crowded-short dynamic in either direction.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Gilead is a misread defensive compounder. The FY2026 GAAP loss is IPR&D accounting noise; the underlying business is growing (HIV +10%, product margins expanding to 79.2%). BIC/LEN approval in August 2026 extends the HIV franchise's exclusivity profile past 2030, lenacapavir's long-acting lead becomes the durable moat, international PrEP (South Africa, Global Fund) opens a large new market, and the M&A wave delivers a credible oncology/inflammation second pillar. On ~$9.59 normalized non-GAAP EPS, a re-rating from ~13x toward the high-teens plus the dividend gets the stock to the $160–180 analyst high. Bull target: **$165–180**.

**Bear case.** Yeztugo never expands the PrEP market — it merely cannibalizes Descovy — and BIC/LEN cannot offset the 2028 Biktarvy price cut, so HIV revenue stalls and then declines. State payer pushback spreads, the pharma tariff exemption lapses, the early-stage M&A assets disappoint and trigger impairments (Trodelvy's $7.3B intangible is already at risk), and the term-loan-funded deal wave tightens flexibility into a softer revenue base. The discount to peers persists or widens. Bear target: **$100–108** (roughly −11% to −18% from current).

The base case sits between: the franchise transition mostly works, BIC/LEN is approved on schedule, the 2028 negotiation is absorbed without a cliff, and the stock grinds toward the lower end of the analyst range as estimates stabilize. The distribution is genuinely two-sided around the August PDUFA and the unresolved PrEP-expansion question, which is why this is a HOLD rather than a BUY despite the cheap multiple.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** The defining near-term checkpoints are the BIC/LEN PDUFA, the Q2 2026 earnings report (which books the IPR&D charge and updates Yeztugo/PrEP trajectory), and the anito-cel PDUFA. The next earnings release is the immediate reassessment point.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Evidence that total PrEP scripts are expanding (not just Yeztugo substitution), or Yeztugo net adds turning clearly accretive to HIV franchise dollars over two consecutive quarters.
- BIC/LEN approval on or near the August 27 PDUFA with a favorable label, confirming the franchise life-extension.
- Next-year non-GAAP EPS revisions turning positive (currently flat) alongside reaffirmed sales guidance.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- BIC/LEN delay or rejection, or a meaningfully restrictive label.
- Multiple additional state ADAP programs following Florida in dropping Biktarvy, signaling broad payer pushback ahead of the 2028 negotiation.
- A guidance cut to underlying non-GAAP EPS or sales (distinct from the IPR&D-driven GAAP swing), or a material Trodelvy intangible impairment.
- Loss of the pharma tariff exemption with no offsetting mitigation.

## Conclusion

Gilead is a high-quality, cash-generative HIV leader trading at a justified-but-overdone discount to its biotech peers, with a well-covered dividend and a clean operational quarter behind it. The score trajectory has drifted down (7.1 in February to 6.1 now), driven by deteriorating risk and management-quality reads: the 2028 Biktarvy overhang has crystallized, drug-pricing entanglement has deepened, and the company has committed to an aggressive debt-funded M&A wave of early-stage assets while insiders sell on routine 10b5-1 plans with no open-market buying. The ~23% drawdown from the February high reflects these fundamental developments rather than volatility, which is why we do not treat the lower price as an automatic stronger buy case.

The forward outcome is binary and dated: the lenacapavir transition either replaces and extends the Biktarvy base before 2028 or it does not, and the early PrEP evidence (substitution rather than expansion, persisting insurance gaps) is not yet reassuring. With cheap valuation and dividend support on one side and a genuine binary catalyst plus policy risk on the other, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate GILD a HOLD (growth_hold) and would move to BUY on confirmed PrEP market expansion or a clean BIC/LEN approval, and to AVOID on a transition stumble or broadening payer pushback.
