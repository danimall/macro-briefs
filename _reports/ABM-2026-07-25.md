**Research Date: July 25, 2026**

## Executive Summary

ABM is one of the largest integrated facility-services providers in the U.S., generating $8.75B of FY2025 revenue across janitorial, engineering, parking, aviation, and technical/energy solutions. The investment story is a deliberate mix-shift: use a stable, cash-generative but commodity-margin janitorial base to fund expansion into structurally growing niches — data-center critical power, semiconductor workforce/equipment support, microgrids, and battery storage. That pivot is now producing visible results. H1 FY2026 revenue rose 7.3% (5.8% organic), Q2 organic growth of 6.1% was the strongest since Q3 2022, Technical Solutions grew 27% (22% organic), and the February 2026 WGNSTAR acquisition adds exposure to ~300 semiconductor sites and 60+ fab clients. The "AI data center" framing the market has attached to ABM is grounded in real demand, though it should be sized carefully: Technical Solutions is only ~11% of revenue and M&D ~18%, and ABM services others' infrastructure rather than owning it.

The offsetting reality is a thin, deteriorating margin structure and a balance sheet that has become more acquisition-built and more leveraged. Gross margin slipped from 13.1% (FY23) to 11.8% in H1 FY26 as management traded price for retention on B&I rebids and took on lower-margin, equipment-heavy Technical Solutions work. Net margin was 1.9% in FY25. Post-WGNSTAR debt is ~$1.87B against ~$1.75B of equity, goodwill of ~$2.74B now exceeds stockholders' equity, net leverage sits at 3.2x adjusted EBITDA (above the company's sub-3x target), and interest-rate swaps largely mature in 2026 just as interest expense climbs toward ~$110M. The earnings-beat record is weak: three misses in the last four quarters, including a 19.8% miss in Q1 FY26, which caps confidence in forward estimates even though they are stable.

At $47.72 the stock trades at ~18.2x trailing EPS and ~11.9x FY26 guided adjusted EPS ($3.85–$4.15), a discount to the ~22.8x peer median that is partly justified by lower margins, higher leverage, and negative tangible book. Estimate revisions are flat, insider activity is programmatic selling with no open-market buying, and the largest segment (B&I, roughly half of revenue) faces a ~300bps back-half growth headwind from a UK client exit plus persistent West Coast office weakness. This is a fairly-priced, balanced setup. We rate ABM HOLD, with the margin-recovery trajectory and B&I stabilization as the swing factors to monitor.

## Company Overview

ABM traces to a 1909 San Francisco window-washing business and today serves ~20,000 clients with ~113,000 employees, 45% of whom are unionized under 300+ collective bargaining agreements. Revenue is ~92% U.S.-based, with a smaller UK/Ireland footprint. The business is labor-intensive (labor is 68% of revenue), working-capital-heavy, and structurally low-margin, with most contracts cancelable on 30–90 days' notice but historically high retention.

Five reportable segments (FY2025 revenue / operating margin):

- **Business & Industry (B&I)** — $4.13B / 7.7%. Largest segment; commercial offices, healthcare, venues. Heavily exposed to office occupancy and hybrid-work trends.
- **Manufacturing & Distribution (M&D)** — $1.62B / 9.4%. Highest-margin core; includes data centers and now the WGNSTAR semiconductor franchise. One client ~32% of segment.
- **Aviation** — $1.12B / 5.8%. Airlines/airports; two clients ~27% of segment.
- **Education** — $0.92B / 7.3%. K-12 and higher education.
- **Technical Solutions** — $0.96B / 9.0%. Microgrids, UPS, EV charging, battery storage; fastest-growing; one client ~30% of segment.

The strategic logic is coherent: the janitorial base provides stable cash flow and cross-selling reach, into which ABM layers higher-value critical-power, semiconductor, and energy services carrying secular tailwinds. The trade-off is that this pivot introduces project lumpiness and equipment-heavy revenue that compresses blended gross margin in the near term.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** Growth has been steady in the mid-single digits: $7.81B (FY22), $8.10B (FY23), $8.36B (FY24), $8.75B (FY25, +4.6% with 3.8% organic). H1 FY26 accelerated to +7.3% (5.8% organic). Record H1 FY26 bookings of $1.2B support the forward pipeline.

**Profitability.** Operating income has been volatile: $348.8M (FY22), $409.5M (FY23), $212.0M (FY24), $311.7M (FY25). The FY24 decline was overwhelmingly a $95.7M non-cash charge to increase the fair value of RavenVolt contingent consideration in SG&A, not operational deterioration. Net income fell to $81.4M in FY24 from $251.3M, then recovered to $162.4M in FY25. Any read of ABM should treat FY24 as accounting-distorted. The genuine concern is gross margin, which has slipped from 13.1% (FY23) to 12.3% (FY25) to 11.8% (H1 FY26), driven by strategic pricing on B&I rebids and lower-margin equipment-heavy Technical Solutions projects. Consolidated segment operating margin fell ~60bps YoY in Q2 FY26, and management guides FY26 segment margin toward the low end of 7.8–8.0%.

**Cash flow.** FY25 operating cash flow was ~$234M and FCF ~$155M (FCF margin ~1.8%). H1 FY26 OCF swung +$202M (to +$128.2M from –$73.9M) as ERP-transition invoicing delays that had trapped working capital reversed. This is a real positive but a one-time normalization, not a run-rate step-change. The cleaner forward number is management's ~$250M FY26 FCF target (excluding ~$65M transformation costs, the final RavenVolt earn-out, and acquisition/restructuring costs), which against a ~$2.8B market cap implies a ~9% FCF yield.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt rose from ~$1.57B (Oct '25) to ~$1.87B (Apr '26) after funding WGNSTAR via a $255M incremental term loan. Goodwill of ~$2.74B now exceeds stockholders' equity of ~$1.75B, so tangible book is deeply negative. D/E is ~1.95; net leverage is 3.2x adjusted EBITDA, above the sub-3x target though below the 5.0x covenant. Altman Z of 2.78 sits in the grey zone; current ratio is 1.48. Interest expense is rising (~$96M FY25, ~$110M expected FY26), and interest-rate swaps largely mature in 2026, removing a hedge as rates reset.

**Capital allocation.** The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.29 (~$66M/yr, ~40% payout, ~2.5% yield). Buybacks were $121M in FY25 and $94M in H1 FY26 at an average ~$44/share, shrinking the share count from ~62.3M (FY25) to ~58.9M in Q2 FY26. This is a mature capital-return profile partly funded by debt, which warrants scrutiny given leverage above target and goodwill above equity.

## Growth Analysis

The near-term growth signal is genuinely improving. Organic growth accelerated to 5.8% in H1 FY26 from 3.8% in FY25, led by Aviation, M&D, and data-center/microgrid demand. Technical Solutions grew 27% in Q2 (22% organic) on critical-infrastructure and data-center work, and WGNSTAR contributed ~$36.6M of Q2 revenue while positioning M&D for double-digit growth via ~300 semiconductor sites.

The durability question is the mix. ABM is a services derivative of others' capex — it maintains UPS/power/microgrid infrastructure and supplies workforce/equipment support to fabs and data centers. That is a real participation in the AI-infrastructure buildout, but it is a small share of consolidated revenue (Technical Solutions ~11%, M&D ~18%) and lower-margin than the headline suggests. The largest segment, B&I (~half of revenue), is the swing factor in the wrong direction: flat revenue, West Coast office weakness, and a ~300bps back-half growth headwind from a UK client exit.

Estimate revisions are effectively flat: current-year FY26 EPS consensus of $3.98 is up only 0.5% over 90 days, next-year $4.35 up 0.2%. This is well short of the revision velocity that signals a genuine earnings acceleration. The growth is real and improving at the top line, but it is being traded against margin, and consensus is not chasing it upward. FY26 guidance of $3.85–$4.15 adjusted EPS was reaffirmed rather than raised, and the beat record is poor (one beat in four quarters), which limits confidence that even that range is conservative.

## Valuation Assessment

At $47.72, ABM trades at ~18.2x trailing EPS ($2.59) and roughly 11.9x the midpoint of FY26 guided adjusted EPS ($4.00). Against the Environmental & Facilities Services peer median P/E of 22.8x, ABM looks cheaper, but the peer set is heterogeneous (GEO at 14.4x, HCSG at 22.8x, CLH at 38.8x, VLTO at 21.3x, CWST distorted at 817x) and mostly carries higher margins and cleaner balance sheets. ABM's discount is largely explained by its 1.9% net margin, 3.2x leverage above target, negative tangible book, and thin FCF margin — a structurally lower-quality earnings stream that merits a lower multiple.

The valuation case is not a value-trap in the impaired sense — the ~9% forward FCF yield and ~2.5% dividend at a 40% payout provide real yield support, and the semiconductor/data-center mix-shift offers a legitimate growth angle. But there is no identifiable catalyst for meaningful multiple expansion. Estimate revisions are flat, the earnings-beat record is weak, gross margin is compressing, and the balance sheet is more constrained than the grey-zone Altman Z suggests. Analyst targets span a wide $45–$68 (mean $52.4, median $52), placing the current price near the low end but well below the mean; the dispersion reflects genuine disagreement rather than a consensus mispricing. On balance the stock is fairly priced for its quality and growth profile. Cheapness relative to peers is insufficient on its own: the discount is warranted, and there is no clear reason the market is wrong at today's level.

## Competitive Landscape

The facility-services industry is fragmented and price-competitive with low barriers to entry; ABM competes against regional and local operators with lower overhead. Its durable advantages are national scale, industry-specialized go-to-market, and cross-selling of higher-value technical/energy solutions into a large janitorial base. The competitive trajectory is mildly positive but uneven. On the plus side, ABM is deliberately building differentiated capability in structurally growing niches — Quality Uptime (2024, data-center UPS), RavenVolt (2022, microgrids/EV/battery), and WGNSTAR (2026, semiconductor workforce/equipment) — that separate it from commodity cleaning peers and give it exposure to the AI-infrastructure capex cycle.

On the negative side, the core B&I franchise is defending share through price ("strategic pricing" on rebids), which is eroding gross margin, and it lost a large UK client worth ~300bps of back-half B&I growth. Segment-level customer concentration is meaningful (M&D ~32%, Technical Solutions ~30%, Aviation two clients ~27%), so the growth engines carry client-loss risk. ABM is improving its mix at the high-value end while conceding pricing at the commodity end — a competitive position that is stable-to-slightly-improving, not decisively widening.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Margin compression is the central operating risk.** Gross margin has fallen to 11.8% (H1 FY26). Management argues restructuring savings ($35M targeted, $20.1M booked) and sequential improvement will restore margin later in FY26; this is the key item to verify and is not yet proven in reported results.

2. **Leverage, rising rates, and goodwill.** Swaps mature in 2026 as rates reset. A cyclical downturn plus a goodwill impairment could pressure covenants (the 2025 annual test showed all reporting units comfortably above carrying value).

3. **B&I / office-occupancy exposure.** Nearly half of revenue depends on commercial office demand; elevated vacancy and hybrid work suppress high-margin supplemental work-order services, and West Coast weakness plus the UK client exit are near-term drags.

4. **Weak recent earnings visibility.** Three misses in four quarters (−14.4%, −19.8%, −5.8%, then +1.9%) show reduced forecast reliability, partly tied to the ERP transition and margin volatility.

5. **Self-insurance and pension.** ~$558M net self-insurance reserves with recurring adverse development ($23.3M in 2025, $20.3M in 2024); a 10% change in ultimate losses moves net income ~$40M. 45% unionized with ~$588M multiemployer plan contributions and unquantifiable withdrawal-liability exposure.

6. **Tax-credit expiration.** WOTC and Federal Empowerment Zone credits expired December 31, 2025 and had not been renewed as of Q2 FY26, lifting the guided effective tax rate to 29–30% (FY26) from 26.2% (FY25).

7. **Integration and CFO transition.** WGNSTAR is a new integration; CFO David Orr was promoted internally in June 2025 amid the ERP/working-capital issues in his purview — a transition worth monitoring but not yet a red flag.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 24.5% sits below 1-year historical volatility of 27.9% (a −3.4% spread) and in the 4th percentile of its own chain — options are cheap, consistent with a low-drama, no-near-catalyst setup, and IV has collapsed from 47.3% to 24.5% as the June earnings event passed and the stock recovered 21%. The one notable tell is elevated near-term put skew (23.4%), signaling some demand for downside protection into the next print, though the term structure is in normal contango (near 24.5% < long 36.1%). The combination of cheap near-dated vol, put skew, and a modest implied ±6.8% move reflects a market pricing ABM as a stable, fully-valued name with residual downside hedging rather than an imminent directional catalyst.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$60–$68).** The semiconductor/data-center mix-shift compounds — WGNSTAR drives double-digit M&D growth, Technical Solutions sustains 20%+ growth, restructuring savings and easing B&I rebid pricing restore segment margin toward 8%, FCF hits or beats the ~$250M target, buybacks compound the shrinking share count, and the market re-rates ABM toward the peer median (~22x) on a cleaner AI-infrastructure-services narrative. FY27 EPS of ~$4.35+ at a ~15x multiple supports the low end of this range; a fuller re-rating reaches the $68 street high.

**Bear case (~$36–$40).** B&I weakness deepens (further West Coast/office softness, more client exits), gross margin fails to recover from 11.8%, higher rates and the swap roll-off push interest expense above plan, a goodwill impairment surfaces given goodwill > equity, and the debt-funded buyback becomes harder to justify at 3.2x leverage. On flat-to-down earnings at a de-rated ~10x, the stock retests the 52-week low near $37 (−22% from current).

The base case sits between: mid-single-digit organic growth, gradual margin stabilization, ~$4.00–$4.35 EPS, and a range-bound multiple. Neither tail is clearly more probable, and the flat estimate revisions plus weak beat record argue against a strong directional lean.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

Recommendation: **HOLD** (uncertain — evidence supports neither a confident growth nor a pure income call).

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Gross margin stabilizes/improves for two consecutive quarters back toward 12.5%+, confirming the strategic-pricing headwind has troughed and restructuring savings are flowing through.
- FY26 EPS consensus revisions turn decisively positive (+5% or more over 90 days) alongside a raised guidance range, or WGNSTAR/Technical Solutions demonstrably lift blended M&D margins.
- Net leverage moves back toward the sub-3x target while FCF meets/exceeds the ~$250M target, restoring balance-sheet flexibility.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- B&I revenue declines accelerate beyond the ~300bps UK-client and office headwinds, dragging consolidated organic growth below ~2%.
- Any goodwill impairment charge, given goodwill now exceeds equity.
- Interest expense and swap roll-off compress FCF materially below the ~$250M target while buybacks continue, or leverage drifts above ~3.5x.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 FY26 earnings (expected early September 2026) is the natural checkpoint to verify the margin-recovery trajectory management has guided toward, plus any update on B&I stabilization and WGNSTAR integration.

## Conclusion

ABM offers a coherent, executing strategy — funding a pivot into data-center critical power, semiconductor support, and energy services from a stable janitorial base — and the top-line acceleration (5.8% organic in H1 FY26, record bookings, 27% Technical Solutions growth) is real. The problem is that the growth is being purchased with margin (gross margin down to 11.8%) and, increasingly, with debt (leverage 3.2x, goodwill above equity, swaps rolling off). Estimate revisions are flat, the recent beat record is poor, insiders are selling programmatically with no open-market buying, and the stock is fairly valued at ~11.9x guided adjusted EPS given its below-peer margins and constrained balance sheet.

This is the third consecutive HOLD, and the trajectory of scores has drifted modestly lower on valuation and risk. New data neither reverses that drift nor argues for a stronger call: the FY26 guidance reaffirmation, flat revisions, and margin compression keep the setup balanced. We maintain **HOLD** and would look to the Q3 print for evidence that segment margin is recovering and B&I is stabilizing before turning more constructive. The ~2.5% dividend and ~9% forward FCF yield provide a floor, but there is no identifiable catalyst for meaningful re-rating at today's price.
