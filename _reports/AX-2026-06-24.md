**Research Date: June 24, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Axos Financial is a branchless, technology-driven diversified bank with roughly $29.2B in assets that has compounded book value at a high rate by combining a structurally low efficiency ratio (~41.5% in banking) with an exceptional net interest margin (consolidated 4.57%, banking 4.62%). Those advantages are real and durable relative to traditional regionals, and they have produced top-decile returns: ROAA of 1.77% and ROE of 16.3% in the March 2026 quarter. The franchise is not in question.

What has changed is the quality of the recent earnings trajectory and the direction of three underlying metrics. The headline Q3 FY26 print (diluted EPS $2.15 vs. $1.81) was flattered by a one-time $22M favorable legal settlement worth roughly $0.38/share after tax; on an adjusted basis EPS grew about 5% to $1.90. Net interest margin compressed 21 bps year-over-year as the high-yield FDIC purchased-loan book runs off and deposit costs rise. The provision for credit losses tripled to $41.0M and the net charge-off rate jumped to 0.31% from 0.09%, concentrated in the Commercial & Industrial Non-RE book and tied to a single problem loan. Every regulatory capital ratio declined year-over-year as ~19% nine-month loan growth outpaced capital generation, and the company did zero buybacks despite an open authorization.

The score trajectory shows a steady climb (6.4 in March to 7.1 in May) and an upgrade to BUY. The new data does not confirm that improvement. The credit migration, NIM compression, and capital drawdown are fundamental developments, not price noise, and consensus EPS estimates have fallen 3.5–4.0% over 90 days. At ~11x trailing/forward earnings versus a 13.7x peer median, the discount partly reflects these emerging risks rather than a clean mispricing. We move to HOLD (growth hold), with a clear path to re-upgrade once the C&I credit thread is contained and the Jenius/Capital One deposit acquisitions demonstrably defend margin.

## Company Overview

Axos operates two segments. The **Banking Business Segment** (~$28.3B assets) is Axos Bank, a federally chartered savings association regulated by the OCC and CFPB, run as a digital-first, nationwide bank with no branch network. Its lending spans single-family and multifamily mortgages, commercial real estate, C&I lending (fund finance, lender finance, asset-based, equipment leasing), and auto/consumer loans. The deposit franchise gathers funding through verticals such as title and escrow, treasury management, and bankruptcy trustee services. The **Securities Business Segment** comprises Axos Clearing, Axos Advisor Services (RIA custody), and Axos Invest (digital wealth), generating fee income plus margin- and securities-lending net interest income.

The core competitive logic is straightforward: no branches lead to low fixed operating cost, a low efficiency ratio, and the ability to pay competitive deposit rates while sustaining a wide NIM. Diversified fee streams from custody and clearing provide partial ballast against a spread-driven model. The company has also opportunistically purchased assets, most notably the December 2023 FDIC loan purchase at a 37% discount, which generated purchased-loan yields of 12–17%. That tailwind is now fading.

Axos has a June 30 fiscal year-end. Q4 FY2026 results are scheduled for July 30, 2026. CEO/President Gregory Garrabrants is named as a critical executive in the 10-K, concentrating key-man risk.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability is elite but normalizing.** Net income was $124.7M, up from $105.2M a year earlier. Nine-month net income reached $365.4M (diluted EPS $6.33; adjusted $6.25). ROAA and ROE remain top-decile for a bank.

**Margin compression is the clearest negative trend.** Consolidated NIM fell 21 bps year-over-year to 4.57% in Q3 (17 bps to 4.76% for nine months). Loan yields declined to 7.36% from 7.99%, and the high-yield FDIC purchased-loan book is running off, with average balance down to ~$630M from ~$975M. The level is still extraordinary versus regional peers, but the direction is down and the most profitable contributor is fading.

**The balance sheet is growing very rapidly.** Net loans grew to $24.96B at 3/31/26 from $21.05B at 6/30/25, driven by the Verdant equipment-leasing acquisition (~$1.0B loans/leases) plus organic C&I and CRE growth. Deposits stand at $22.4B (including ~$2.0B brokered), with ~85% of bank deposits insured or collateralized and ~$3.6B uninsured/uncollateralized.

**Capital is being consumed by growth.** Company CET1 fell to 11.65% from 12.52%; total capital to 14.32% from 15.28%; bank-level CET1, the tightest, to 10.90% from 12.42%. The bank remains comfortably "well capitalized," but the buffer is shrinking while loan growth runs at ~19% over nine months. Tangible book value per share rose to $49.72 from $44.60. FHLB advances spiked to $1.8B from $60M as a temporary bridge ahead of the Jenius deposit acquisition.

**Capital allocation has shifted entirely to growth.** Operating cash flow was $264.6M over nine months. The company executed zero buybacks in FY26 to date despite $148M of remaining authorization and an unused $150M ATM. Combined with the new $200M 7.00% subordinated note issuance (redeeming $160.5M of 4.875% 2030 notes, raising the cost of sub-debt), management is prioritizing balance-sheet expansion and acquisitions over capital return, and could need to raise capital if growth continues at this pace.

**Earnings surprise track record is strong (four consecutive beats).** Per the vendor data, recent beat percentages were 16.0% (Q3 FY26), 3.3%, 0.4%, and 4.0%. The most recent quarter's beat was substantially aided by the legal one-timer; excluding the settlement, the underlying beat cadence is more modest, consistent with the adjusted ~5% growth picture.

## Growth Analysis

The growth engine has two legs: organic loan and deposit expansion, and acquired funding. On lending, the C&I Non-RE book is now the largest portfolio at 35.1% of loans and has been the primary growth vehicle, but it is also the source of the credit deterioration discussed below. The Verdant acquisition added ~$1.0B of equipment leases but contributed only $3.6M of net income on $65.5M of revenue over nine months while driving D&A up $32.5M and adding servicing G&A. Verdant is early-stage and currently dilutive to margins.

The deposit-acquisition strategy is the more compelling forward driver. The Jenius Bank deposit purchase (~$2.3B of SMBC consumer deposits, OCC-approved 3/19/26, expected to close in the June 2026 quarter) and the Capital One IRA deposit acquisition (~$3.2B, pending OCC, expected to close in calendar 2026) together replace higher-cost and brokered funding with cheaper, stickier consumer deposits. If executed well, this is a sensible NIM defense that could stabilize or modestly reverse the margin compression. The strategic logic is sound, but it concentrates integration and execution risk in 2026.

The estimate-revision picture tempers the growth thesis. Per the vendor data, consensus current-year EPS has fallen 3.5% over 30 days and 4.0% over 90 days; next-year EPS is down 2.4% over 90 days. Negative earnings momentum, even modest, is a meaningful caution given that growth prospects are the most reliable winner-selection dimension. We score growth at 6.0: a genuinely growing franchise, but with decelerating adjusted earnings and falling estimates, not an accelerating one.

## Valuation Assessment

AX trades at ~11.1x trailing/forward earnings (EPS $7.43, price $94.35) against a Regional Banks peer median of 13.7x. On the surface this looks cheap. The discount, however, partly reflects the emerging risks rather than pure market error.

Relative to the named peers — OZK (8.4x), HOMB (11.5x), ABCB (13.7x), AUB (16.8x), HWC (14.7x) — Axos sits below the median but above the cheapest names, and it does so with materially superior returns (16.3% ROE vs. typical regional-bank mid-teens or lower) and a richer NIM. A quality-and-growth-adjusted comparison would argue Axos deserves a premium to OZK and HOMB, not a discount, which is the bull's mispricing case. The counter is that the market is now pricing the trajectory: NIM declining, charge-offs accelerating in a single C&I credit, capital ratios falling, and estimates being revised down. On a price-to-tangible-book basis ($94.35 / $49.72 = ~1.9x), Axos carries a clear premium to tangible book that the franchise quality justifies but that leaves little margin for credit disappointment.

A low P/E without an independent positive catalyst is a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal, and the cheapness signal has been reliably backward-looking. Here the "catalyst" — falling estimates and rising credit costs — runs the wrong way. We do not treat the analyst target range (low $105, high $114, median $110) as a floor here, because the conditions that make targets lag (rising estimates, accelerating growth) are absent; estimates are falling. We score valuation 5.5: cheap on a headline multiple, but the discount is partly earned, and there is no clean re-rating catalyst until credit and capital stabilize.

## Competitive Landscape

Axos's competitive position is improving-to-stable, anchored by a genuine structural cost advantage. The branchless model produces a banking efficiency ratio that traditional regionals cannot match, and the resulting NIM sits well above peers even after compressing. The diversified fee businesses (clearing, custody, advisory) add ballast and optionality that a pure spread lender lacks.

The pressures are real, however. Deposit-cost competition is the proximate driver of NIM compression — Axos's advantage in paying competitive rates is being tested as the broader rate environment and FinTech deposit competitors bid for funding. The 10-K flags FinTech disintermediation and AI-driven competitive and fraud risks. The deposit-acquisition strategy (Jenius, Capital One IRAs) is a deliberate, well-conceived response that should improve funding mix and defend the cost advantage, which supports a constructive read on trajectory.

Set against the world model's caution that Financials BUYs carry a structural negative alpha (-1.66pp across a large sample), a competitive-position score must rest on company-specific differentiation rather than sector beta. Axos clears that bar on cost structure and NIM but not decisively enough, given the credit and capital pressures, to warrant the 7.5 it carried in the prior review. We score competitive position 6.8.

## Risk Assessment

**Credit deterioration in C&I Non-RE is the single most important live risk.** Provisioning tripled to $41.0M in Q3 ($83.3M for nine months vs. $40.7M) and the net charge-off rate more than tripled to 0.31% from 0.09%. C&I Non-RE nonaccruals rose to $98.1M from $61.8M, and management repeatedly cites a specific reserve on "one C&I Non-RE loan with unique credit risk characteristics." C&I Non-RE charge-offs were $20.5M over nine months. ACL/loans held flat at 1.37% even as charge-offs accelerated, which raises the question of whether reserves are keeping pace with the migration. Overall nonaccruals as a percentage of loans fell to 0.71%, helped by the growing denominator and improving CRE/multifamily, but the direction and concentration in C&I is what matters.

**Concentration risks:** ~35.8% of RE loans in California and ~28.2% in New York; CRE is 34.2% of loans, though office is only ~$389M (~7% of specialty CRE) and weighted-average LTVs are conservative at ~50%.

**Funding sensitivity:** reliance on ~$2.0B brokered deposits and FHLB advances (temporarily spiked to $1.8B) introduces price-sensitive, potentially less-sticky funding in stress, which the deposit acquisitions are designed to mitigate.

**Capital buffer compression:** declining ratios plus zero buybacks and an unused ATM raise the probability of a future capital raise if growth persists.

**Integration risk:** Verdant plus two large deposit purchases strain controls and capital simultaneously.

**Litigation/governance:** the BofI/Axos derivative case was dismissed with prejudice (9/18/25) but is on appeal to the 9th Circuit; UFB Direct deposit-rate class actions and the COR Securities indemnification matter ($7.4M paid 4/8/26) remain. Governance carries entrenchment features (classified board, 75% supermajority provisions, no action by written consent, Delaware Section 203). Key-man risk around Garrabrants is explicit.

We score risk factors 5.0. The C&I credit thread is a specific, escalating, asymmetric risk that the market is only beginning to price, and the capital drawdown reduces the cushion to absorb it.

### Options Market Signal

Options positioning is benign and offers no contradiction to the fundamental view. Near-term ATM IV is 32.7% against 32.4% historical vol (fairly priced), with IV in the 6th percentile of the current chain and a balanced 1.5% put/call skew. The put/call OI ratio collapsed from 2.03 to 0.21 over the past month as the stock rose ~8%, indicating prior hedging unwound rather than fresh bearish positioning. The term structure is in normal contango (1m 32.7% to 6m 38.4%), and short interest is moderate at 8.5% of float with 7.7 days to cover. The implied ±6.4% move into the next expiry, with the July 30 earnings date as the next catalyst, frames the near-term risk. The options market signals neither informed bearish positioning nor a confirmed bullish setup.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** Axos is a structurally advantaged digital bank trading at ~11x earnings versus a 13.7x peer median while earning a 16.3% ROE. The NIM compression decelerates as the Jenius (~$2.3B) and Capital One IRA (~$3.2B) deposit acquisitions replace brokered and FHLB funding with cheaper, stickier consumer deposits, defending margin into FY2027. The C&I problem loan proves idiosyncratic and reserves prove adequate, with overall nonaccruals already declining as a percentage of loans. Tangible book compounds in the high teens, and the multiple re-rates toward peers as credit stabilizes. Bull case ~$114, in line with the high analyst target.

**Bear Case.** The C&I Non-RE charge-off acceleration is the leading edge of broader credit migration in the fastest-growing, largest portfolio (35.1% of loans), and the flat 1.37% ACL/loans proves insufficient, forcing larger provisions. NIM continues compressing as the FDIC purchased-loan tailwind fully fades and deposit competition intensifies. Capital ratios keep declining, forcing a dilutive raise. Adjusted EPS growth stalls in the low single digits, estimates continue falling, and the multiple compresses toward OZK/HOMB levels. Bear case ~$72, roughly the 52-week low, ~24% downside from current levels.

The asymmetry is roughly balanced from current levels: a credible path to ~$110 if the deposit strategy works and credit stabilizes, against meaningful downside toward the 52-week low if the C&I thread spreads. That balance, combined with falling estimates and a partly-earned valuation discount, supports HOLD rather than BUY.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a growth hold with a 6–12 month reassessment horizon centered on the July 30, 2026 (Q4 FY26 / fiscal year-end) print and the subsequent FY27 quarters.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- C&I Non-RE net charge-off rate stabilizes or declines back toward ~0.15% and the single problem loan is resolved without further reserve build, with ACL/loans rising to cover the migration.
- Consolidated NIM stabilizes or expands quarter-over-quarter, evidencing that the Jenius and Capital One deposit acquisitions are defending margin as intended.
- EPS estimate revisions turn positive (current-year consensus rising rather than falling), confirming an earnings inflection rather than deceleration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- C&I Non-RE charge-offs accelerate further or migrate into a second named credit, with provisions outpacing reserve builds (ACL/loans falling while NCOs rise).
- A dilutive equity or ATM capital raise to support continued loan growth, signaling the capital buffer has been exhausted.
- Bank-level CET1 falls below ~10.0%, materially eroding the well-capitalized cushion.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoint is the July 30, 2026 earnings release, with particular attention to the Jenius deposit close, the C&I credit roll-forward, and capital ratio trends.

## Conclusion

Axos remains a high-quality, structurally advantaged digital bank, and the franchise thesis is intact: branchless cost structure, elite NIM, 16.3% ROE, and a sensible deposit-acquisition strategy to defend margin. The most recent quarter reveals three fundamental developments that argue against extending the prior BUY upgrade. The GAAP earnings momentum was flattered by a one-time legal settlement (adjusted growth only ~5%); provisioning and net charge-offs tripled, concentrated in one C&I problem loan in the largest and fastest-growing portfolio; and every capital ratio declined as growth outran capital generation, with buybacks halted. Consensus estimates have fallen, the opposite of an inflection signal.

The ~11x multiple versus a 13.7x peer median is partly earned by these risks rather than a clean mispricing, and there is no positive catalyst to drive re-rating until credit and capital stabilize. We rate AX a HOLD (growth hold). The setup converts to a BUY if the C&I credit thread proves idiosyncratic, NIM stabilizes on the deposit acquisitions, and estimate revisions turn positive — concrete, monitorable conditions that should resolve over the next two to three quarters. Until then, the risk/reward is balanced rather than asymmetric.
