**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

New Jersey Resources is a diversified energy holding company anchored by a regulated natural gas distribution utility serving ~589,000 customers in suburban/coastal New Jersey. A $157M base rate increase approved in November 2024 drove NJNG's net income contribution from 46% to 65% of total net financial earnings. This shift toward regulated earnings improves earnings quality and supports a modest re-rating from its historical multiple.

The company reported Q2 FY2026 EPS of $2.20 vs. $1.89 consensus (14% beat) and raised full-year NFEPS guidance to $3.48-$3.62, supported by energy services tailwinds from severe winter weather. At $55.87, the stock trades at 15.4-16.1x forward NFEPS, roughly in line with gas utility peer median of 17.2x P/E. The 3.3% dividend yield provides income support, but the Investment Tax Credit phase-out under OBBBA creates material uncertainty for the solar growth engine, and structural negative free cash flow requires continuous capital market access.

The stock has appreciated 23% over the trailing 12 months on genuine fundamental improvement (rate case, earnings beats, guidance raises). Further upside depends on whether the market awards a pure-utility multiple to what is increasingly a regulated earnings story.

## Company Overview

NJR operates through four segments:

**Natural Gas Distribution (NJNG, 65% of NFE):** Regulated gas utility serving six New Jersey counties across 1,538 square miles. The franchise territory — suburban/coastal NJ with proximity to NYC/Philadelphia — supports above-average customer growth of ~6,000 additions annually. The Conservation Incentive Program (CIP) decouples earnings from weather variability, providing margin stability that most gas utility peers lack.

**Clean Energy Ventures (CEV, 18% of NFE):** Owns ~479 MW of commercial solar capacity across seven states. Revenue derives from electricity sales, SRECs, and ITCs. FY2025's $61.2M NFE was inflated by a $56.2M gain on residential solar portfolio sale; underlying run-rate is $5-15M.

**Energy Services (ES, 10% of NFE):** Wholesale natural gas marketing and asset management. Highly volatile — NFE collapsed from $111.5M (FY2024) to $34.9M (FY2025) due to AMA revenue recognition timing. Remaining contracted AMA revenue is ~$34M/year through FY2031.

**Storage & Transportation (S&T, 6% of NFE):** Owns Leaf River salt dome storage (32.2M Dth) in Mississippi and the Adelphia pipeline (850,000 Dth/day) in Pennsylvania.

The leverage ratio is elevated but standard for regulated utilities with predictable cash flows. The A1/A+ ratings at NJNG provide cheap debt access — recent issuances at 5.16-5.85%.

NJR bridges the gap through sale-leaseback proceeds ($251M in FY2025), debt issuances, and DRIP equity ($34.8M). This model is sustainable as long as credit markets remain accessible — a non-trivial assumption given elevated long-term rates and potential further tightening.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term growth drivers (FY2026-FY2027):**
1. Full-year run-rate of the $157M base rate increase at NJNG
2. SAVEGREEN energy efficiency program ($205M direct investment, earning 9.6% ROE)
3. Rate base growth from $430-480M annual NJNG capex
4. Customer additions of ~6,000/year (~1% growth rate)

**Medium-term headwinds (FY2028+):**
1. ITC phase-out under OBBBA eliminates solar economics after December 2027 placed-in-service deadline
2. AMA contracted revenue declining toward $34M/year and ending in FY2031
3. New Jersey's energy master plan targeting 100% clean energy by 2050 with "maximum electrification of buildings"
4. PJM interconnection queue delays impacting CEV project timelines

The organic utility growth rate, absent solar and energy services, is likely 5-7% annually — driven by rate base growth on capex deployment and modest customer additions. This is adequate for a utility but doesn't warrant premium valuation.

**Estimate Revision Trends:**
Current-year (FY2026) EPS estimate of $3.45 has been revised up 5.2% over 30 days and 6.0% over 90 days — modest positive momentum following the Q2 beat and guidance raise. Near-term quarterly estimates show sharp downward revisions (current quarter -67%, next quarter -22%), likely reflecting seasonal patterns in the gas utility business (summer quarters are structurally weak/negative).

## Competitive Landscape

**Strengths:**
- Conservation Incentive Program provides unique weather decoupling among gas utility peers
- Attractive franchise territory with above-average customer growth (1%/year vs. ~0.5% industry)
- A1/A+ NJNG credit ratings = cheapest cost of capital in the peer group
- Diversified earnings provide optionality (upside from cold winters via ES)

**Weaknesses:**
- Solar segment facing structural ITC headwind (OBBBA) — competitive disadvantage vs. peers without solar exposure
- Geographic concentration in a single state with aggressive electrification policy
- Energy services contribution declining on AMA wind-down

**Position relative to peers:** NJR's regulated utility franchise is above-average quality. The diversified model provided outsized growth (11% CAGR) during FY2021-FY2025 but now faces headwinds on two of three unregulated segments. The competitive position of the core utility is stable to improving; the portfolio-level competitive position is slightly deteriorating.

## Risk Assessment

**1. ITC Phase-Out (HIGH PROBABILITY, MODERATE IMPACT):** OBBBA eliminates the economic foundation for CEV's future solar investment. While CEV's underlying run-rate contribution is modest (~$5-15M excluding gains), the loss of a growth leg removes a key bull-case argument.

**2. Negative Free Cash Flow / Capital Access (MODERATE PROBABILITY, HIGH IMPACT):** NJR needs continuous access to credit markets. At current elevated long-term rates, the cost of new issuances is rising. Any credit market disruption, ratings downgrade, or spread widening would immediately constrain growth investment.

**3. New Jersey Electrification Policy (LOW PROBABILITY NEAR-TERM, EXISTENTIAL LONG-TERM):** No gas ban is in place today, and customer growth continues. But the 2050 electrification goal creates terminal value uncertainty for a gas distribution franchise. Discounting this risk at 15-20 year duration means limited near-term price impact but bounds terminal multiple.

**4. Energy Services Volatility:** ES contributed $34.9M in FY2025 vs. $111.5M in FY2024. Mild winters or low gas price volatility can compress this to near-zero, creating earnings shortfall risk in any given year.

**5. Environmental Remediation:** $167M accrued liability for MGP sites, recoverable through regulatory assets — but any BPU disallowance would be a direct hit.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($63-65, +13-16%):** The market re-rates NJR to full regulated utility multiple (18x forward NFEPS) as the earnings mix shift toward NJNG becomes more recognized. Energy services delivers above-guidance results in FY2026 driven by continued gas price volatility. Management identifies alternative growth investments to replace solar post-ITC. Next rate case (likely FY2027-2028) further expands rate base and earnings power.

**Bear Case ($47-49, -13-16%):** ITC phase-out kills CEV growth entirely. Mild winter eliminates ES upside. Rising interest rates increase cost of capital and compress utility multiples sector-wide. New Jersey accelerates electrification timelines. NJR de-rates to 14x forward NFEPS as a diversified utility with declining unregulated earnings.

**Base Case ($58-61, +4-9%):** NJR trades toward the analyst consensus median of $61 over 12 months as FY2026 guidance is met and the dividend provides steady income. Limited catalysts for significant re-rating beyond modest multiple expansion as regulated earnings percentage grows.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**

2. Management articulates a credible post-ITC growth strategy for CEV with specific project pipeline
3. Next NJNG rate case filing signals another material rate base expansion ($100M+ revenue increase)

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. New Jersey BPU imposes restrictions on new gas hookups or signals accelerated electrification mandate
2. Credit ratings downgraded at NJNG, impairing the cheap-financing model
3. FY2026 NFE comes in below $3.40 (below prior-year level), suggesting growth has stalled

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (August 2026), which will provide the first FY2026 Q3 data point on summer period performance and updated full-year guidance narrowing.

## Conclusion

New Jersey Resources is a well-managed gas utility with above-average franchise quality, benefiting from a recent large rate case and unique weather decoupling through its CIP mechanism. The stock has appreciated meaningfully on genuine fundamental improvement, and the current valuation at ~16x forward NFEPS is reasonable but not compelling. The 3.3% dividend yield provides adequate income support in a defensive positioning environment.

Two of three growth engines (solar and energy services) face structural headwinds — ITC phase-out for CEV and AMA wind-down for ES. The remaining regulated utility growth of 5-7% annually is solid but already reflected in the current multiple. Absent a pullback or new catalysts, this is a hold-for-income position with limited re-rating potential in either direction.

Recommendation: **HOLD** at $55.87 with 3.3% dividend yield providing total return while awaiting either a better entry point or clarity on post-ITC growth strategy.
