**Research Date: May 28, 2026**


## Executive Summary

New Jersey Resources is a diversified energy services holding company anchored by its regulated gas distribution utility (NJNG, ~65% of net financial earnings), supplemented by commercial solar (CEV, 18%), wholesale gas trading (ES, 10%), and midstream storage/transportation (S&T, 6%). The stock has risen 21% over 12 months to $56.76, driven by a constructive $157M base rate case settlement, two guidance raises in FY2026, and a 14% earnings beat in Q2 FY2026.

The investment case requires balancing a reliable regulated utility core with steady 1% customer growth, a 3.3% dividend yield, and reasonable 16.4x forward earnings against structural headwinds for the CEV solar segment (OBBBA ITC phase-out by Dec 2027), negative near-term quarterly estimate revisions (-67% for current quarter, -22% for next quarter), and limited upside to consensus targets given the recent run. The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty (51.8% IV vs. 16.7% HV) in steep backwardation, suggesting a specific event is expected to resolve within weeks.

At $56.76, the stock sits within 2% of the consensus mean target of $59.86, having absorbed most of the positive catalysts from the FY2026 guidance raises. The income profile remains attractive for yield-oriented holders, but the risk/reward for new capital is balanced.

## Company Overview

NJR operates four segments: (1) **New Jersey Natural Gas (NJNG)** — a regulated gas utility serving 589,000 customers across central/coastal New Jersey, generating the majority of earnings with weather-decoupled margins (CIP mechanism); (2) **Clean Energy Ventures (CEV)** — a 479 MW commercial solar portfolio across seven states, monetized through electricity sales, SRECs, and investment tax credits; (3) **Energy Services (ES)** — wholesale natural gas marketing and storage optimization; and (4) **Storage & Transportation (S&T)** — the Leaf River salt dome facility (32.2M Dth), Adelphia pipeline, and Steckman Ridge storage.

The company is headquartered in Wall, NJ, with a market cap of $5.6B and approximately 101M shares outstanding.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings trajectory:** NFE per share has compounded at approximately 11% annually from $2.16 (FY2021) to $3.29 (FY2025). FY2026 guidance was raised twice to $3.48–$3.62, driven by severe winter conditions boosting Energy Services and continued NJNG customer growth post-rate case.

**Q2 FY2026 performance:** Reported adjusted EPS of $2.20 vs. consensus $1.93 — a $0.27 beat (14%). This follows a pattern: 3 beats in 4 quarters, with the sole miss occurring in the seasonally weak September quarter.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt of $3.45B against equity of $2.39B (1.44x D/E) is standard for a regulated gas utility. NJNG carries A1/A+ credit ratings with stable outlook. Short-term debt declined from $292M to $196M. The company maintains $825M in undrawn credit facilities (maturing 2029).

**Cash flow challenge:** Operating cash flow of $466M trails capex of $660M, creating a persistent free cash flow deficit. NJR finances the gap through debt issuance ($200M senior notes in Aug 2025 at 5.16-5.85%) and modest equity via the dividend reinvestment program (~$35M/year, 1-2% dilution annually). This is growth-phase capital intensity, not distress, given the rate case recovery mechanism.

**Margins:** Operating margin of 39.1% and net margin of 25.8% reflect the utility-dominated earnings mix. ROE of approximately 14% exceeds the 9.6% allowed ROE at NJNG, indicating the unregulated businesses contribute above their cost of capital.

## Growth Analysis

**Near-term:** FY2026 consensus at $3.45 NFE/share (within raised guidance of $3.48–$3.62), representing ~5% growth over FY2025's $3.29. FY2027 consensus is flat at $3.45, suggesting the market views the severe winter tailwind as non-recurring for ES.

**Estimate revisions are mixed:** Full-year FY2026 estimates rose modestly (+5.2% in 30 days, +6.0% over 90 days), but near-term quarterly estimates have deteriorated sharply — current quarter down 67% to $0.03 and next quarter down 22% to $0.25. This reflects the seasonal pattern (Q3/Q4 are weak for gas utilities) and potential normalization of ES earnings after the winter spike.

**Customer growth:** NJNG adds ~6,000 customers annually (~1%), above-average for gas utilities. However, New Jersey's climate policy trajectory (Global Warming Response Act targeting 80% GHG reduction by 2050) creates a long-term structural question about the sustainability of gas hookup growth.

**CEV growth headwind:** The OBBBA legislation signed July 2025 phases out solar ITCs for projects placed in service after December 2027 unless construction begins before specified deadlines. CEV invested $249M in FY2025 and placed 93.6 MW in service. The pipeline beyond the current backlog faces uncertainty — management is "assessing impacts," indicating material strategic ambiguity. With 93% of FY2026 SRECs hedged but declining to 32% by 2029, the visibility runway is finite.

**Long-term growth target:** Management has historically guided toward 7-9% NFE/share growth. With CEV's growth engine constrained post-2027 and ES inherently volatile, achieving sustained high-single-digit growth will depend heavily on NJNG rate base expansion and potential new regulated infrastructure investment.

## Valuation Assessment

At $56.76 with FY2026 consensus NFE of $3.45, NJR trades at **16.4x forward earnings** — slightly below the peer median P/E of 17.2x. The 3.3% dividend yield matches the peer median exactly.

Relative to peers:
- OGS: 18.2x P/E, 3.3% yield
- SR: 17.2x P/E, 3.8% yield
- SWX: 26.9x P/E, 2.9% yield
- NFG: 10.5x P/E, 2.7% yield
- UGI: 12.2x P/E, 4.2% yield

NJR's slight discount to OGS and SR is explained by the earnings volatility in ES and structural uncertainty in CEV. The premium to NFG and UGI reflects NJR's superior customer growth and geographic positioning.

The consensus target range of $51–$63 (mean $59.86) implies 5.5% upside to the mean. With the stock having appreciated from $43.46 (52-week low) to $56.76 (+31%), most of the re-rating from the rate case settlement and guidance raises appears absorbed.

At current levels, the stock offers a total return profile of approximately 3.3% yield + 5% earnings growth = ~8.3% annualized total return potential, which is adequate but not compelling for a utility with rising structural risks.

## Competitive Landscape

**Regulatory positioning:** NJNG benefits from a constructive relationship with the NJ BPU. The November 2024 rate case settlement ($157M increase, 9.6% ROE, 54% equity ratio) was generous relative to peer outcomes nationally. The CIP weather decoupling mechanism effectively eliminates weather risk, validated through several years of warmer-than-normal conditions without margin impairment.

**Service territory quality:** Central/coastal New Jersey, proximate to NYC and Philadelphia, with approximately 1.7M residents. Population stability and modest growth support the 6,000 customer/year additions. This is among the better-positioned gas utility territories in the country.

**Diversification advantage:** Unlike pure-play gas utilities (OGS, SR), NJR's CEV and ES segments provide incremental earnings beyond the rate base. However, this diversification comes at the cost of greater earnings volatility and complexity. The FY2025 results illustrate this: ES swung from $111.5M to $34.9M NFE year-over-year (a 69% decline) due to AMA revenue timing and lower market volatility.

**Competitive threats:** Electrification policy represents the primary long-term competitive risk. No gas hookup moratorium currently exists in NJ, but the regulatory and political trajectory is toward decarbonization. NJR's CEV hedge against this risk is itself now under pressure from OBBBA.

## Risk Assessment

**OBBBA/ITC Phase-Out (HIGH):** This is the most material emerging risk. CEV has been a growth driver contributing 18% of NFE and investing $200-290M annually. The December 2027 placed-in-service deadline (with construction start requirements potentially as early as late 2025 for foreign material compliance) creates a strategic cliff. Existing assets are grandfathered, but the pipeline beyond current commitments is uncertain.

**Energy Services volatility (MODERATE):** ES contributed only 10% of FY2025 NFE after being 38% in FY2024. The segment benefits from severe weather and high volatility but can underperform materially in mild years. The $34M/year AMA floor through FY2031 provides only partial visibility.

**Capital market dependency (MODERATE):** Sustained negative free cash flow requires continuous access to debt and equity markets. With recent coupons at 5.16-5.85%, new debt issuance is meaningfully more expensive than the embedded cost. Rising refinancing costs compress the earned spread above allowed ROE.

**Electrification policy risk (LOW-MODERATE, long-term):** NJ regulatory proceedings on the "future of natural gas utilities" are in early stages. No imminent threat to operations, but the 20-year regulatory trajectory could constrain customer growth below the 1% historical rate.

**Environmental liabilities (LOW):** $167M accrued for former MGP site remediation (range $144-200M), largely offset by regulatory asset recovery.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing significant near-term uncertainty: 51.8% ATM IV for the June 18 expiry vs. 16.7% historical volatility — a 35pp spread that is extreme for a utility stock. The term structure is in steep backwardation (51.8% near-term vs. 17.8% at 6 months), indicating the market expects a specific event to resolve within weeks. The put/call OI ratio of 0.23 is heavily call-skewed, suggesting participants are positioned for upside moves. The elevated IV level may be tied to an upcoming binary event (possibly related to CEV project announcements or regulatory filings) that warrants monitoring.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($63, +11%):** NJNG continues 6,000+ customer additions annually, FY2026 NFE hits the upper end of guidance ($3.62), and CEV successfully accelerates project construction ahead of the OBBBA deadline, maintaining growth visibility into FY2028+. ES benefits from continued elevated nat gas volatility in a geopolitically disrupted energy market. The stock re-rates to 17.5x forward earnings, matching the peer median.

**Bear Case ($48, -15%):** CEV growth effectively stalls post-2027 as ITC eligibility expires, NJ initiates policy proceedings that signal restrictions on new gas hookups, ES reverts to below-average conditions in a normalized weather environment, and the stock de-rates to 14x forward earnings — reflecting a pure regulated utility growing at only 3-4% without the solar kicker.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Stock pulls back to $48-50 range (14-15x forward) providing a 4%+ yield entry
- Management provides concrete CEV strategy post-OBBBA showing viable growth path without ITCs
- Next base rate case filing signals continued constructive regulatory relationship with accelerated infrastructure investment

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- NJ BPU initiates formal proceeding restricting new gas service connections
- FY2027 NFE guidance below $3.30 (decline from FY2026 levels)
- Debt/equity exceeds 2.0x or credit rating downgrade

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 FY2026 earnings (expected late July/early August 2026) will provide visibility on seasonal normalization. CEV construction timeline updates expected by Q4 FY2026.

## Conclusion

NJR is a competently managed, diversified gas utility trading at a reasonable but not compelling valuation following a strong 12-month run. The 3.3% dividend yield provides income support, and the regulated utility core offers stability, but the growth story faces genuine structural uncertainty as OBBBA constrains CEV's expansion path and long-term NJ electrification policy creates headwinds for the gas distribution franchise. At 16.4x forward earnings — within 2% of the consensus target mean — the stock offers balanced risk/reward appropriate for income-oriented holders but insufficient asymmetry for new capital deployment. Hold for yield; reassess on pullback or strategic clarity.
