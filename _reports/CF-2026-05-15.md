## Executive Summary

CF Industries is the world's largest ammonia producer, currently experiencing an earnings surge driven by the Strait of Hormuz crisis that has removed substantial Middle Eastern nitrogen supply from global markets. The stock trades at a significant discount to peers on forward earnings estimates, and the company generated $1.80B in FCF in FY2025 (approximately 9.0% FCF yield on recent market cap), has no debt maturities until 2034, and has retired 20.5% of its diluted weighted-average shares over three years.

The primary risk is binary: if the Strait of Hormuz reopens or a ceasefire materializes, nitrogen prices would decline and the stock could retrace 15-25% rapidly. The current geopolitical environment has seen significant escalation with limited visible progress toward diplomatic resolution. With approximately $2.0B in cash and a $750M undrawn revolver, downside is bounded by the fundamental cost advantage CF maintains even in normalized environments. The stock is a direct beneficiary of the stagflationary regime with quantifiable upside and manageable downside.

## Company Overview

CF Industries operates nine nitrogen manufacturing complexes across the United States, Canada, and the United Kingdom. Its Donaldsonville, Louisiana facility is the world's largest ammonia production complex, representing approximately 40% of total capacity. The company produces ammonia, granular urea, UAN (urea ammonium nitrate), and ammonium nitrate, selling into agricultural, industrial, and clean energy end markets.

FY2025 revenue by segment: ammonia $2.18B (31%), UAN $2.16B (30%), granular urea $1.78B (25%), ammonium nitrate $421M (6%), and other products $545M (8%). Total revenue of $7.08B represented 19% YoY growth from the 2024 trough of $5.94B.

The company's structural advantage derives from access to North American natural gas at Henry Hub pricing (~$3.53/MMBtu in 2025), significantly below European or Asian marginal cost producers. This cost advantage positions CF among the lowest-cost nitrogen producers globally, though the company's 10-K acknowledges competition from regions with lower natural gas or other feedstock costs, sometimes aided by government subsidies.

## Valuation Assessment

CF trades at a forward P/E of approximately 14.75x based on Yahoo Finance's displayed consensus, against a Materials sector peer median of approximately 24.3x. This discount appears excessive given CF's superior FCF generation, structural cost advantages, and clean energy optionality.

- FCF yield of approximately 9.0% vs. peer median dividend yield of 2.0%
- The market appears to be pricing CF as if current earnings are largely transient, applying a normalized-year multiple to a stock benefiting from extraordinary supply disruption

**Comparison to 2022 Peak:** In 2022 (the prior geopolitical-driven peak), CF traded at 7-9x earnings. The current valuation suggests the market is already discounting a sharp normalization.

**Upside Math:** At a modest 16-18x forward P/E (still below the peer median of 24.3x), the stock would trade at $145-160 on current consensus. Consensus analyst high target is $150.

**Downside Floor:** In a full normalization scenario (Hormuz reopens, gas normalizes, nitrogen returns to 2024 levels), CF would earn ~$7-8/share. At 14x (reasonable for a low-cost producer with clean energy optionality), that implies ~$98-112 — roughly 10-20% downside from current levels. The asymmetry favors the long side.

## Competitive Landscape

CF's competitive position is strengthening:

1. **Supply removal benefits low-cost incumbents** — Middle Eastern producers (OCI, Qatar Fertiliser) are directly impacted by Hormuz closure. Russian producers face EU sanctions. European producers face uneconomic gas costs. CF is one of the few global producers with neither supply disruption nor cost disadvantage.

2. **Clean energy first-mover advantage** — Donaldsonville CCS operational, Blue Point JV with credible partners (JERA is Japan's largest energy company, Mitsui is a major trading house). These investments create a structural premium once EU CBAM and Japan's Hydrogen Society Act fully activate.

3. **Distribution moat** — CF's network of river terminals, deep-water port access, and rail connections creates a logistics advantage that would cost billions and years to replicate.

4. **North American structural advantage** — U.S. gas production continues growing. Even with Henry Hub elevated in early 2026, CF remains below European marginal cost (~$12-15/MMBtu equivalent). The cost advantage narrows but doesn't disappear.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Geopolitical Binary (HIGH):** The Hormuz crisis is the primary earnings driver. A ceasefire or diplomatic resolution would trigger a 15-25% decline within days. The April 2026 ceasefire speculation caused an approximately 9% single-day drop. Ceasefire/reopening announcements could emerge at any time, and conditions remain volatile.

**2. Natural Gas Cost Inflation:** Henry Hub averaged $6.32/MMBtu from January 1 through February 20, 2026, driven primarily by extreme winter weather, record heating demand, weather-related production curtailments, and high LNG feedgas demand — not solely by the Hormuz crisis. CF's Q1 2026 average daily Henry Hub was $4.90/MMBtu, and natural gas cost used in production was $4.57/MMBtu (up from $3.68 YoY), which reduced Q1 gross margin by approximately $76M year-over-year. While currently more than offset by nitrogen price increases, margin compression risk exists if nitrogen prices soften while gas remains elevated.

**3. Yazoo City Production Loss:** Ammonia capacity offline until Q4 2026 minimum. Reduces production but limited financial impact given pricing tailwind. Gross ammonia production guided to ~9.5M tons in 2026 (vs. 10.1M in 2025).

**4. Leadership Transition:** Christopher D. Bohn became President and CEO effective January 4, 2026, succeeding W. Anthony Will who retired. Bohn previously served as COO and CFO, and had been on the board since February 2024. CFO Gregory Cameron separated from the company in February 2026, with Richard Hoker serving as interim CFO. Bohn's deep operational experience provides continuity, but the dual CEO/CFO transition introduces some execution uncertainty, particularly for the $3.7B Blue Point JV.

**5. Blue Point Execution ($2.0B CF commitment):** ATR technology at scale is unproven for ammonia. Louisiana imposed a moratorium on new Class VI injection well permits in October 2025, creating CCS permitting uncertainty. CCS depends on Occidental/Enbridge's Pelican Sequestration Hub (still under development). Timeline (2029 production) creates multi-year overhang on capital.

**6. Q1 2026 Earnings Quality Note:** The reported $983M adjusted EBITDA included the benefit of an approximately $170M litigation settlement gain. This gain does not appear to be excluded from the adjusted EBITDA figure, meaning underlying operating EBITDA was closer to ~$810-815M — still strong, but investors should account for this when extrapolating run rates.

## Conclusion

CF Industries represents a compelling asymmetric setup: a world-class asset base trading at a significant discount to peer multiples during a period of extraordinary supply disruption. Strong EPS revision momentum, approximately 9.0% FCF yield, no near-term debt maturities, and 20%+ diluted share count reduction over three years create multiple paths to value realization.

The primary risk — geopolitical resolution — is quantifiable and bounded (14x normalized earnings implies approximately 19% downside). The market is pricing CF as though the Hormuz windfall is entirely temporary while simultaneously not pricing the clean energy optionality embedded in the Blue Point JV and Donaldsonville CCS.

This is a cycle-recovery BUY supported by all three validation gates: estimates revised sharply higher in recent months, revenue growth accelerating, and capacity investments proceeding (Blue Point, CCS). Position sizing of 2-3% reflects the binary geopolitical exposure.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** Valuation multiples (forward P/E closer to ~14.75x per Yahoo Finance rather than ~11x), Q1 EBITDA quality (~$170M litigation settlement gain not excluded from adjusted figure), and geopolitical resolution risk (conditions are volatile with ceasefire discussions reported, not "systematically eliminated") collectively suggest the risk/reward asymmetry is less favorable than originally framed, though the directional thesis remains intact.
