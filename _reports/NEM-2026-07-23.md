**Research Date: July 23, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Newmont is the world's largest gold producer and is enjoying the most favorable commodity backdrop in its history. Realized gold prices averaged $4,661/oz in the first half of 2026 versus $3,498/oz for full-year 2025, driving H1 2026 free cash flow of $5.3 billion (a record $2.2 billion in Q2 alone) and moving the balance sheet from $5.3 billion net debt at year-end 2024 to $3.4 billion net cash at the end of Q2 2026. On the raw cash-generation numbers, this is one of the strongest periods any gold major has posted.

The market is not rewarding it, and the reasons are visible in the data. Q2 2026 all-in sustaining cost jumped 58% quarter-over-quarter to $1,621/oz, driven by a two-month seismic-related stoppage at the Cadia block cave, a full quarter of higher Ghana royalties (the fixed fiscal-stability regime expired December 31, 2025), and diesel inflation. Consensus EPS estimates are falling across every window — current-year down 15.7% over 90 days, current quarter down 18.4% over 30 days — even as gold sits near record levels. Management has framed 2026 as a margin story rather than a volume story, and the stock fell after February guidance because production is muted. The five-year total shareholder return indexed to 195 against a peer-group index of 330 quantifies a durable investor grievance: Newmont has underperformed its own sector despite the gold rally, largely because of post-Newcrest integration friction and repeated single-asset operational shocks.

We rate NEM a HOLD. The valuation is not stretched (~15.0x trailing earnings, net cash, ~7% trailing FCF yield), and there is genuine optionality in the Barrick/NGM joint-venture default dispute and the recently permitted Red Chris block cave. The combination of falling estimates, rising unit costs, an unproven new CEO, and a stock caught in a wide operating range argues against a directional call in either direction. The prior report cadence (HOLD → BUY → HOLD → HOLD, with the composite score declining from 7.0 to 6.1 over five months) reflects the same tension; the new Q2 data confirms the cost concern rather than resolving it.

## Company Overview

Newmont (founded 1921, public since 1925) is the only gold producer in the S&P 500, an index-membership advantage that gives it a passive-flow and institutional-ownership base no gold peer can match. Following the ~$13.5 billion all-stock Newcrest acquisition (closed November 2023) and a subsequent divestiture program, the company runs a "core portfolio" of Tier 1 assets across the Americas, Australia, Africa, and Papua New Guinea. Managed operations include Lihir, Cadia, Tanami and Boddington (Australia/PNG), Ahafo South and Ahafo North (Ghana), Merian (Suriname), Cerro Negro (Argentina), Yanacocha (Peru), Peñasquito (Mexico), Red Chris and Brucejack (Canada). Non-managed interests include Nevada Gold Mines (38.5%, operated by Barrick), Pueblo Viejo (40%), and Fruta del Norte via Lundin Gold (32%).

FY2026 guidance is ~5.3 million attributable gold ounces plus 102kt copper and 32Moz silver. The company does not hedge gold, so it carries full leverage to spot — favorable in 2026, but the source of the "no downside protection" concern if prices reverse. Copper by-product exposure at Cadia, Boddington, and Red Chris gives some optional leverage to the energy-transition metal.

Newmont carries investment-grade ratings (S&P BBB+, Moody's A3, Fitch A-). As of the July 23, 2026 Q2 release, **Natascha Viljoen is President and CEO**, having succeeded Tom Palmer during H1 2026, a leadership transition addressed in the Management section.

## Financial Analysis

The headline financials are strong. FY2025 revenue was $22,669M (+21% YoY) despite the loss of ~$2.3 billion of revenue from divested mines; net income attributable was $7,085M versus $3,348M in 2024 and a $2,494M loss in 2023. Adjusted EBITDA was $13,480M for FY2025, rising to a TTM figure of $16,765M through Q2 2026. FY2025 operating cash flow was $10,334M against $3,035M of capex, producing $7,299M of free cash flow (a 32% FCF margin).

The balance-sheet transformation is the most underappreciated element. Newmont carried $5.3 billion of net debt at year-end 2024, repaid $3.4 billion of debt during 2025, and finished Q2 2026 with $9,009M cash against $5,083M debt — net cash of $3,411M and net debt/EBITDA of −0.2x. Total liquidity is $13.0 billion including a $4.0 billion revolver. This gives management the flexibility to fund the growth pipeline and return capital simultaneously. Since February 2024, the share count has been reduced by more than 100 million shares (~9%), with $4.3 billion remaining under the $6 billion buyback authorization. The dividend framework is unusual: total dividends are fixed at roughly $1.1 billion per year, so the per-share dividend ($0.26/quarter, $1.04 annualized) mechanically grows as buybacks shrink the count.

The key watch-item is cost. FY2025 by-product AISC was $1,358/oz. FY2026 AISC guidance is $1,680/oz with CAS guidance of $1,055/oz. The drivers — Cadia seismic downtime (which cut copper credits, mechanically raising by-product AISC), a full quarter of higher Ghana royalties, and higher diesel — are partly transient and partly structural. The Ghana royalty change (a proposed sliding scale of 5%→12% tied to the gold price) is structural and rises precisely when gold is high, compressing the incremental margin on the windfall that is supposed to reward the equity.

ROE runs ~21% and net margin ~32%. The Altman Z of ~2.3 sits in the grey zone, which is normal for a capital-intensive miner and is more than offset by the net-cash position. The financial risk here is not solvency; it is unit-cost inflation and the sensitivity of consolidated results to single-asset shocks. The earnings-surprise record is clean — four consecutive beats, including +31.7% in Q1 2026 and +24.7% in Q4 2025 — which lends some confidence to near-term delivery even as forward estimates fall.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are the weakest part of the story on a fundamental basis. Management is running a deliberate margin-over-volume strategy: FY2026 production guidance is flat-to-muted, and the February 2026 guidance drove the stock lower because investors wanted volume growth to compound the gold windfall. The growth pipeline is real but back-end loaded — Tanami Expansion 2, Cadia Panel Caves (PC1-2), the newly permitted Red Chris block cave, the Lihir Nearshore Barrier, and Cerro Negro Expansion 1 — with the Red Chris FID still pending.

The more immediate signal is negative. Consensus EPS estimates have fallen across every window: current quarter $2.18 (down 18.4% over 30 days, down 9.0% over 90 days), current year $9.38 (down 8.2% / 15.7%), next year $10.57 (down 6.8% / 4.0%). This is falling estimate momentum against a record gold price — an indication that the market has recognized cost inflation is eroding the earnings translation of higher realized prices. The estimate revision signal here is the mirror image of the strong-buy inflection pattern: rather than +30% upward revisions confirming an inflection, we have double-digit downward revisions confirming a margin squeeze. This weighs directly on the Growth Prospects score and prevents any BUY thesis on earnings momentum.

The offset is optionality. If the Red Chris block cave advances to FID and the Cadia/Tanami expansions ramp on schedule, mid-term production could re-accelerate. The Barrick/NGM dispute (discussed under Risk) could deliver inorganic growth via consolidation of Nevada assets. These are option values, not base-case growth, and they are not captured in falling near-term estimates.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$95.75, NEM trades at ~15.0x trailing earnings (EPS $6.39) and roughly 10.2x current-year consensus. The trailing FCF yield is ~7.3% on the ~$99.7 billion market cap using FY2025 FCF, and materially higher on annualized H1 2026 FCF. On absolute metrics the stock is not expensive: a net-cash balance sheet, mid-single-digit-to-high run-rate FCF yield, and a mid-teens trailing earnings multiple.

Peer comparison is complicated by the heterogeneity of the small gold-sector sample. Royal Gold trades at 24.0x (but is a royalty/streaming company with a structurally different, higher-multiple model), SSR Mining at 10.1x, and Aura Minerals at 51.4x. The peer median of 24.0x flatters the comparison because it is dominated by the royalty model; against a producer peer like SSR, Newmont trades at a modest premium justified by scale and index membership. The more telling data point is Newmont's five-year TSR discount, which the market ties to post-Newcrest integration friction and repeated operational shocks, not to a cheap starting multiple.

I am scoring valuation at 5.5 — neutral. The equity-lessons framework is explicit that a low multiple is a value-trap warning rather than a buy signal absent an independent catalyst, and Materials is a structurally negative-alpha sector where a positive raw print is usually beta. The relevant question is whether the market is mispricing the stock today. The bull case is that at $4,400+ gold with net cash, the equity is cheap on normalized FCF. The bear case is that falling estimates reflect a real, structural cost problem (Ghana royalties, geotechnical risk) that justifies the discount, and that "cheap" here is the compressed multiple of a business the market distrusts on execution. The two catalysts that could resolve the discount — a favorable Barrick/NGM outcome and a Red Chris FID — are genuine but unresolved. I treat the analyst mean target of $130 and median of $128 with caution: this is not a clean inflection stock with rising estimates, so consensus targets are not obviously stale floors. The valuation is defensible, not asymmetrically attractive.

## Competitive Landscape

Newmont's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding on trajectory. Its structural advantages are real: the largest gold production base, geographic and by-product diversification, Tier 1 orebodies, an investment-grade net-cash balance sheet, and unique S&P 500 index membership that provides a passive-flow and institutional-ownership base no peer enjoys. Alongside Barrick, it is one of the two global majors.

The trajectory concern is that Newmont has underperformed its gold-mining peer group on total return (195 vs 330 over five years) despite the record commodity environment, which is the definition of a competitive position that is not translating scale into shareholder value. The Newcrest integration added Tier 1 assets but also added geotechnical complexity (Cadia block caving, Lihir), and the April 2026 Cadia seismic event that cut copper production 43% quarter-over-quarter is a concrete example of how a maturing, geologically complex portfolio can deliver single-site shocks that move consolidated results. Rising resource nationalism (Ghana royalties, Mexico mining/water reform, PNG's new Income Tax Act, Argentina local-hire rules) is a sector-wide headwind that erodes the margin advantage of scale.

I score competitive position at 5.5. Newmont remains the industry leader by size, but leadership in a business where the leader has underperformed the group and is absorbing recurring operational and fiscal shocks does not warrant a high trajectory score. The Barrick/NGM dispute is the wildcard: a favorable resolution that consolidates Nevada would meaningfully strengthen the position; an unfavorable one leaves the status quo.

## Risk Assessment

Newmont carries a higher-than-typical concentration of idiosyncratic risks, several of them recently surfaced:

**Barrick/NGM dispute (the defining special situation).** On January 26, 2026, Newmont notified Barrick and the NGM board of alleged mismanagement, including diversion of resources from NGM to benefit Barrick's wholly-owned Fourmile property, and on February 3, 2026 sent a formal notice of default under the Nevada JV agreement. This collides with Barrick's plan to IPO/spin off its North American assets. Reuters (2026-01-23) reporting indicates Newmont holds a right of first refusal on Barrick's NGM stake and may be able to block the spin-off. This is genuinely two-sided: it could deliver upside (concessions, consolidation, or ROFR monetization) or descend into protracted, value-destructive litigation. Barrick disputes the allegations and the legal merits are unresolved.

**Ghana fiscal stability expired December 31, 2025.** The proposed sliding-scale royalty raises Ahafo costs precisely when gold is high — already visible in the Q2 2026 AISC jump. This is a structural, not transient, cost headwind.

**Single-asset geotechnical shocks.** The Cadia seismic event (April 2026) halted operations for ~two months. Block caving at Cadia, deep pits, and tailings-facility integrity are recurring hazards; Cerro Negro was suspended in early 2025 after fatalities.

**Reclamation/closure escalation.** Reclamation liabilities are ~$6.2–6.3 billion; Yanacocha water-treatment plants total ~$1.8 billion ($1.1 billion spent), normalizing to $300–400M/yr by 2028. Yanacocha Sulfides was impaired and indefinitely deferred (part of $842M FY25 impairments).

**Jurisdictional/political risk** across Peru, Argentina, Mexico, PNG, and Suriname; a securities class action filed January 2025 over 2024 outlook statements; and no gold hedging, leaving full downside exposure if prices reverse (the impairment model uses a conservative $2,500/oz long-term price, which does provide cushion).

Several of these risks are only partially priced — the Ghana royalty structure and the Barrick dispute in particular are recent and evolving. I score risk factors at 5.0.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing acute near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 139.5% against 48.1% one-year historical volatility — a 91-point spread and the 94th percentile within the chain — with a pronounced backwardation (139.5% near-term vs 41.1% at six months). The one-week implied move is ±5.6%. Put/call skew is elevated at 62.9% (elevated put demand), and ATM IV has jumped 86.6 points since early June while the price fell 11.2%. This configuration typically signals an imminent binary event; the 139.5% one-week number likely reflects a very-near-dated expiry around the Q2 release (July 23, 2026) or a thin-options artifact, but the six-month IV of 41.1% is broadly consistent with the stock's realized volatility and the pending Barrick/NGM and Red Chris catalysts. The single unusual-activity print (2,104 contracts on the October $100 call at 15.5x open interest) is mildly constructive. On balance, options positioning confirms the two-sided, event-driven character of the setup rather than a clean directional signal, and cautions against sizing into near-term vol.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$150–175).** Gold holds above $4,400/oz, the Q2 AISC spike proves largely transient (Cadia back to normal by mid-June 2026), and management demonstrates cost containment over the next two quarters. Estimate revisions turn positive as the market re-recognizes the FCF power at record gold prices. The Barrick/NGM dispute resolves favorably — operational concessions, Nevada consolidation, or ROFR monetization — and Red Chris advances to FID, providing a mid-term growth path. The net-cash balance sheet funds continued buybacks that shrink the count and grow per-share metrics, and the peer-TSR discount closes. On ~$10.50+ of normalized EPS at a re-rated mid-teens multiple, the stock approaches the high analyst target of $175.

**Bear Case (~$67–75).** Gold reverses (it sold off sharply in late January 2026), and with no hedges Newmont's unhedged leverage works against it. The Ghana royalty escalation and diesel/labor inflation prove structural, keeping AISC elevated near or above guidance and compressing incremental margins. Estimates continue to fall. The Barrick dispute becomes protracted litigation, another single-asset geotechnical shock materializes, and the new CEO's transition adds execution uncertainty. On depressed earnings and a distrust discount, the stock approaches the low analyst target of $67.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain).** The evidence does not support a confident directional call. The fundamentals are strong on cash generation but deteriorating on the margin, and the two most powerful potential catalysts (Barrick/NGM, Red Chris FID) are unresolved binaries.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 earnings (late October 2026) is the natural checkpoint — it will show whether the Q2 AISC spike was transient and whether estimate revisions stabilize. The Barrick/NGM dispute and any Red Chris FID announcement are event-driven catalysts to monitor continuously.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Q3 AISC declines back toward the $1,400s and management demonstrates the Q2 spike was transient, with current-year EPS estimates stabilizing or turning positive.
- A favorable, concrete resolution of the Barrick/NGM dispute (concessions, consolidation, or ROFR monetization) or a Red Chris block-cave FID that provides a credible mid-term production growth path.
- A pullback that materially widens the FCF-yield gap without fundamental deterioration.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Gold breaks meaningfully below the $3,500–4,000/oz range while AISC stays elevated, compressing margins with no hedge protection.
- Another material single-asset operational shock (Cadia, Lihir, or a tailings event), or the Ghana royalty escalation proving worse than the current proposal.
- Continued double-digit downward estimate revisions through Q3 combined with a stalled or hostile Barrick dispute.

## Conclusion

Newmont is generating record free cash flow and has executed one of the more impressive balance-sheet transformations in the sector — from net debt to net cash in roughly 18 months while retiring ~9% of shares. The equity is trapped between the gold windfall and a real cost problem: Q2 AISC rose sharply, the Ghana fiscal-stability regime has expired, and consensus estimates are falling across every window even as gold trades near records. The five-year peer-lagging TSR quantifies why the market assigns a persistent discount, and the deliberate margin-over-volume strategy limits the near-term growth narrative.

The valuation is reasonable but not asymmetrically cheap; in a structurally negative-alpha sector, that argues for patience rather than conviction. The genuine upside is optionality — the Barrick/NGM default dispute and the newly permitted Red Chris block cave — but these are unresolved binaries, not base-case drivers, and they are set against an unproven new CEO, elevated near-term options-implied stress, and negative estimate momentum. We rate NEM a HOLD and would revisit at Q3 earnings, upgrading on demonstrated cost containment and stabilizing estimates, or downgrading on a gold reversal, another operational shock, or a hostile turn in the Barrick dispute.

> **[FACT-CHECK FLAG]** The following corrections may warrant revisiting this recommendation: trailing P/E was overstated as ~12.4x (EPS $6.39 at ~$95.75 = ~15.0x) and trailing FCF yield was cited as ~11% run-rate in the Executive Summary/Valuation (FY2025 FCF $7,299M / ~$99.7B market cap ≈ 7.3%); the corrected multiple is less cheap than presented, though the HOLD rests primarily on cost/estimate dynamics rather than the multiple.
