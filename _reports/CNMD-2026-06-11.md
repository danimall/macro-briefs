**Research Date: June 11, 2026**

## Executive Summary

CONMED is a ~$1.0B mid-cap surgical-device maker built on a recurring "razor/razor-blade" model: roughly 85–86% of revenue comes from single-use disposables attached to an installed capital base, anchored by two differentiated franchises (the AirSeal insufflation platform in general surgery and BioBrace soft-tissue scaffolds in sports medicine). The stock has fallen 39% over the trailing year and sits near its 52-week low of $32.13, a drawdown driven by a headline 2025 earnings collapse (net income $132M → $47M; net margin 10.1% → 3.4%) and a long-dated overhang from $800M of convertible notes maturing June 2027.

The central analytical issue is that the 2025 earnings drop is largely cosmetic. Of the ~$97M decline in operating income, the bulk is explained by a $64M unfavorable swing in Biorez contingent-consideration fair value (a non-cash, non-deductible accounting item that runs through SG&A), a $22.2M portfolio-review write-off, ~$25M of consulting and operational-optimization costs, and $12.2M of advisory compensation to the former CEO. Operating cash flow rose to $170.7M and free cash flow held at ~$151M (11% margin). Q1 2026 confirmed the recovery thesis: gross margin expanded 260bp to 57.9% and net income more than doubled to $13.8M. Two material overhangs cleared in the data window: the DOJ surgeon-royalty investigation was closed without prosecution in September 2025, and on June 3, 2026 the company agreed to repurchase ~$645M of the 2027 convertible notes for ~$637M cash, retiring ~80% of the maturity wall ahead of schedule.

What keeps this a hold rather than a buy is the combination of a deeply unsettled management team, a still-leveraged balance sheet, and negative forward estimate momentum. There have been 9+ executive-departure filings in roughly 24 months, the company is operating without a permanent CFO (the CEO was appointed interim principal financial officer), and next-year consensus EPS has been cut 2.8% over 90 days. The de-risking is real and the underlying business generates cash, but the absence of a stable leadership team, a clean refinancing structure, and positive earnings revisions means the risk/reward does not yet support a long position. At ~7.2x forward earnings the downside looks contained, but a credible upgrade requires a permanent CFO and confirmation that the refinanced debt does not carry materially worse terms.

## Company Overview

CONMED develops, manufactures, and sells devices for surgical procedures across orthopedics, general surgery, gynecology, and thoracic surgery. The business is reported as a single operating segment with two product lines:

- **General Surgery (~58% of 2025 sales):** Endo-mechanical instruments for minimally invasive laparoscopy, the AirSeal insufflation platform (the company's most important proprietary franchise), smoke evacuation (Buffalo Filter), electrosurgical generators, and cardiac monitoring.
- **Orthopedic Surgery (~42% of 2025 sales):** Sports medicine including BioBrace (from the Biorez acquisition), powered instruments under the Hall brand, imaging systems, and service fees from marketing MTF allograft tissue.

The model is recurring-revenue heavy (capital products are only ~14% of sales), with international at ~44% of the total and manufacturing concentrated in the U.S. and Mexico. Growth has historically been acquisition-led (In2Bones, Biorez, Buffalo Filter), which has left the balance sheet goodwill- and intangible-heavy: $807M goodwill plus $582M intangibles, roughly 60% of total assets. The company employs ~3,900 people and is headquartered in Largo, Florida. Patrick J. Beyer is the current President and CEO following a transition from the prior CEO.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue trajectory is steady.** Sales grew $1,045M (2022) → $1,245M (2023) → $1,307M (2024) → $1,375M (2025, +5.2%). The 2025 increase was balanced (Orthopedics +5.6%, General Surgery +4.9%), driven by sports medicine/BioBrace and AirSeal, with FX roughly neutral.

**The 2025 earnings collapse is mostly non-operating.** GAAP operating income fell from $200M to $103M and net income from $132M to $47M. The decomposition matters:

- **$64M unfavorable swing in contingent consideration** (Biorez): a $41M income benefit in 2024 versus a $23M expense in 2025. Non-cash, runs through SG&A, and is not tax-deductible, which also pushed the effective tax rate to 33.8% from 18.8%.
- **$22.2M write-off** of inventory, equipment, tooling, and patents from a 2025 product-portfolio review.
- **$12.5M consulting fees** to fix manufacturing/supply chain, plus ~$12.9M of additional operational-optimization costs in SG&A.
- **$12.2M of cash and stock comp** for advisory services from the former CEO (also non-deductible).

Gross margin compressed ~150bp to 54.6% on tariffs, inflation, and the write-offs, partly offset by a $9.9M benefit from early termination of the Gore distribution deal.

**Cash flow quality confirms the underlying business is healthier than GAAP suggests.** Operating cash flow rose to $170.7M (from $167M) and free cash flow held at ~$151M, an 11% FCF margin, against light capex of ~$20M. Free cash flow rising while reported net income fell roughly two-thirds is the clearest evidence that the income-statement hit was largely accounting and one-time in nature.

**Q1 2026 supports the "kitchen-sink year" thesis.** Revenue of $317M was down 1.3% YoY, but entirely because of the planned GI exit (GI fell from $25M to $9.5M); Orthopedics grew 6.8%. Operating margin reached 8.0%, and net income rose to $13.8M ($0.45 diluted) from $6.0M. The earnings beat track record is intact: three beats and one in-line over the last four quarters, with the most recent quarter beating by 6.5%.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total debt was ~$840M (44% of capitalization) with D/E ~1.25x and an Altman Z of 2.02 (grey zone), dragged down by leverage and low EBIT-to-assets. Piotroski signals are positive (positive net income, ROA, OCF; OCF exceeds net income; current ratio 2.14). Cash is thin at ~$35–41M, but the $650M revolver provides liquidity. The June 2026 note repurchase materially improves the maturity profile (see Risk Assessment).

## Growth Analysis

The growth profile is mid-single-digit and cash-generative rather than dynamic. Management raised 2026 organic-growth guidance to 5.0–6.5% (from 4.5–6.0%), targeting revenue of ~$1.345–1.375B, with FX now a modest tailwind. The headline revenue number is depressed by the deliberate GI exit, so reported growth understates organic momentum.

The growth engines are narrow but real: BioBrace in soft-tissue repair and AirSeal in laparoscopic insufflation. Both are differentiated disposable franchises with recurring economics. The risk is that the model has historically depended on acquisitions to grow, and the GI exit is the first major divestiture, so the near-term story is portfolio cleanup and margin recovery rather than top-line acceleration.

Estimate revisions are the cautionary signal. Current-quarter consensus has nudged up (+1.7% over 90 days), but next-quarter (-0.6%) and next-year (-2.8% over 90 days, -1.2% over 30 days) estimates are falling. Current-year EPS sits at $4.37, essentially flat. The company does not exhibit the kind of positive revision velocity that would justify treating depressed trailing multiples as a buy signal. Forward EPS of ~$4.79 implies ~10% growth, but that estimate is being cut, not raised.

## Valuation Assessment

At $34.53, CNMD trades at ~7.2x forward earnings (forward EPS basis) and ~19.5x trailing GAAP earnings, the gap between the two reflecting the one-time 2025 charges. On free cash flow, ~$151M against a ~$1.04B market cap is a ~14.5% FCF yield, which is cheap for a recurring-revenue medical-device business with 57%+ gross margins.

The peer-comparison data provided is thin (a single, loss-making development-stage pharma name that is not a relevant comparable), so the valuation case rests on absolute metrics and CONMED's own history. CONMED at ~7x forward and ~14.5% FCF yield is at a steep discount to its own history and to typical med-tech multiples, though the specific peer multiples are not established in the provided sources. The question is whether that discount is mispricing or a justified penalty for leverage, leadership instability, and sub-scale competitive positioning.

The discount is partly warranted. A Z-score in the grey zone, ~$840M of gross debt, thin cash, a refinancing that just occurred at undisclosed terms, and a CFO vacancy are real reasons for the market to apply a leverage- and governance-adjusted multiple. But the magnitude (a ~7x forward multiple on a business generating 11% FCF margins with margins recovering) looks excessive if the turnaround holds and the refinanced debt is not punitively priced. This supports a valuation score above neutral but not a high-conviction one: the asymmetry is favorable, but the catalysts to close the gap (a permanent CFO, clean refinancing terms, positive revisions) are not yet in hand. The analyst consensus target of $39–40 (mean $39.6) implies ~15% upside from current levels and is a Hold-rated, not a bullish, target set.

## Competitive Landscape

CONMED competes in a consolidating, scale-driven surgical-device market against far larger and better-capitalized firms: Stryker, Smith & Nephew, Medtronic, Arthrex, and J&J/Ethicon. Its edge is in niche proprietary disposable franchises (AirSeal, BioBrace) attached to a recurring-revenue model. The 10-K explicitly flags the risk of a competitor introducing a "replacement for AirSeal," which would erode the company's most important franchise.

The competitive trajectory is more stable than improving. Industry pressures (tariffs, input inflation, supply-chain constraints, GPO/IDN purchasing leverage, China volume-based procurement, and tightening EU MDR regulation) weigh more heavily on a sub-scale player than on its larger peers. Management's decision to bring in outside operations consultants is a tacit admission that internal operations underperformed scaled competitors. The cumulative TSR (company 37.86 versus a peer-group 126.87, per the proxy) quantifies the relative disadvantage: $100 invested in CONMED became ~$38 while the peer group grew to ~$127. The Q1 2026 margin recovery is encouraging, but one quarter of self-help does not reverse a structural scale disadvantage.

## Risk Assessment

**Convertible notes — the largest risk, now substantially mitigated.** The $800M 2.250% notes due June 2027 must be repaid in cash (the ~$145 conversion price is far above the current ~$34 stock, so dilution is not the concern). The June 3, 2026 repurchase of ~$645.2M of the notes for ~$637.2M cash (~$8M discount) closes June 15, 2026. The caveat: the terms of the new debt funding the repurchase were not fully disclosed in available sources. If the refinancing carries a materially higher coupon or restrictive covenants, the interest-expense and flexibility benefit is smaller than it appears.

**Management instability — the most distinctive risk.** The 9+ executive-departure filings in roughly 24 months include a CEO transition with a costly advisory tail to the former CEO. The interim principal financial officer (Controller Andrew Moller) departed effective May 31, 2026, with the CEO himself appointed interim PFO absent a named CFO by June 1. A $1B medical-device company operating without a permanent CFO is a governance red flag and the single most important qualitative concern.

**Other risks.** Roughly 31% of products are sterilized via third-party ethylene-oxide facilities facing environmental/closure risk. The business depends heavily on the AirSeal franchise and on an MTF allograft relationship where a third party controls tissue procurement and CONMED earns only service fees. Goodwill and intangibles of ~$1.4B passed Q4 2025 impairment testing using a market-cap approach, but the depressed share price makes this a watch item. The DOJ surgeon-royalty investigation closed without prosecution (September 2025), and a Q1 2026 Supreme Court ruling on IEEPA tariffs may yield refund claims (nothing booked).

**Management signal.** In October 2025 the board suspended the dividend (paid since 2012) and pivoted to buybacks ($150M program, ~$37M repurchased in Q1 2026 at ~$43.60), signaling management views the stock as cheap while prioritizing deleveraging. The absence of any open-market C-suite buying, and a CFO selling 3,500 shares at $43 before departing, tempers that signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$45–48).** The 2025 earnings collapse was an accounting and one-time artifact; underlying FCF rose. Q1 2026 margins recovered sharply, the GI exit improves mix, and 2026 organic-growth guidance was raised. The June 2026 note repurchase removes ~80% of the 2027 maturity wall, and the DOJ investigation closed favorably. If a permanent CFO is named, refinancing terms prove benign, and margin recovery continues into 2027, a re-rating from ~7x forward toward a higher med-tech multiple on ~$4.79 of forward EPS supports $45–48.

**Bear Case (~$28–30, roughly -15%).** Leadership instability persists, the refinanced debt carries a materially higher coupon that compresses earnings, and competitive pressure on AirSeal erodes the core franchise. A grey-zone Z-score and thin cash leave little margin for execution error, and continued negative estimate revisions confirm the turnaround stalling. Depressed share price keeps goodwill impairment a live risk. At ~6x a cut forward number, the stock drifts toward $28–30.

The base case sits between these: the de-risking is real and the business generates cash, but the catalysts that would close the valuation gap (permanent CFO, clean refinancing, positive revisions) are not yet confirmed, and forward estimates are falling rather than rising.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (uncertain rationale).** The valuation is cheap on FCF and the largest balance-sheet risk has been substantially defused, but the combination of a CFO vacancy, falling next-year estimates, and undisclosed refinancing terms prevents a confident upgrade. This is a hold based on insufficient evidence for a stronger call in either direction, not on a defined 12-month appreciation thesis.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. A permanent CFO is appointed and the refinanced debt terms are disclosed at a coupon at or below the retired 2.250% notes' effective economic cost (i.e., no material interest-expense penalty).
2. Next-year consensus EPS revisions turn positive over a 30- and 90-day window, confirming the margin recovery is flowing to the bottom line rather than being offset by higher financing costs.
3. A second consecutive quarter of gross margin holding above 57% with organic orthopedics growth ≥6%.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Executive departures continue (any additional C-suite Item 5.02 filing) without a permanent CFO in place.
2. The new debt is disclosed at a materially higher coupon (e.g., >5%) or with covenants that constrain liquidity, raising refinancing cost meaningfully.
3. Gross margin reverses below ~54% or organic growth falls below the low end of guidance, indicating the Q1 recovery was transient.
4. A goodwill/intangible impairment charge against the ~$1.4B carrying value.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 2026, expected late July/early August) and the closing of the June 15 note repurchase, which together should clarify both the refinancing terms and whether the margin recovery is durable.

## Conclusion

CONMED is a turnaround story with a misread income statement. The 2025 GAAP earnings collapse was largely cosmetic: the contingent-consideration swing, write-offs, and consulting and former-CEO advisory costs against rising free cash flow. Q1 2026 margins recovered sharply, the 2027 convertible maturity wall has been ~80% defused, and the DOJ overhang cleared. At ~7x forward earnings and a ~14.5% FCF yield, the downside looks contained.

The case stops short of a buy for three reasons: management instability is severe (9+ departures in 24 months and a CEO doubling as interim CFO), the terms of the refinanced debt are not yet disclosed, and next-year consensus EPS is being cut rather than raised. These are concrete, monitorable conditions. A permanent CFO, clean refinancing terms, and a turn in estimate revisions would convert this into a buy. Until then, the risk/reward is balanced enough to hold but not to add. Maintain HOLD with a reassessment at the Q2 print and the note-repurchase close.
