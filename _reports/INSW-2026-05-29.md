**Research Date: May 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

International Seaways operates one of the largest publicly traded crude and refined-product tanker fleets in the world (67 vessels as of Q1 2026, ~8.4M dwt), with roughly 82% of TCE revenue earned in the spot market. The company is currently printing peak-cycle results: Q1 2026 net income of $286M ($3.90 adjusted diluted EPS) on VLCC spot TCE of $86,693/day, up from $33,531/day a year earlier. The balance sheet is exceptionally strong: net loan-to-value below 7%, ~$918M liquidity, Altman Z-Score 3.79, and 44% of the fleet unencumbered. Management returns the bulk of cash flow to shareholders, having formalized an 85%-of-adjusted-net-income dividend policy and declared a record $4.55/share payout (mostly a $4.43 special) in Q1.

The entire investment debate reduces to one question: where are we in the tanker cycle? Management states plainly in the 10-K that geopolitics and weather have "significantly benefitted" results since 2023 and that normalization "could cause tanker rates to decline significantly." Current rates embed a material premium from Russian sanctions, Houthi-driven Red Sea re-routing (forcing Cape of Good Hope diversions that lengthen ton-miles), Venezuela disruptions, and the Strait of Hormuz situation. The same events propping up earnings make the equity vulnerable to a de-escalation re-rating. Against that geopolitical tailwind sits a structural headwind: the total tanker orderbook climbed to 17% of the world fleet at year-end 2025, up from 14% in 2024 and 7% in 2023, with the VLCC orderbook alone adding ~19M dwt year-over-year. Rising deliveries are the classic mechanism that ends tanker up-cycles.

The stock trades at ~6.99x trailing EPS of $6.23, optically cheap, but trailing earnings represent a near-multi-decade peak rather than a trough, the situation where trailing P/E is least useful. With the share price down ~17% from its May 8 high of $92.66, C-suite insiders selling into strength, a dilutive $200M ATM equity program launched near cyclical highs, and options markets pricing imminent stress (near-term IV at 64.9% vs. 36.8% realized, term-structure backwardation, elevated put skew), the risk/reward is two-sided. We rate INSW HOLD on an income basis, with the dividend providing real support but a binary geopolitical setup capping conviction.

## Company Overview

INSW operates two reportable segments. Crude Tankers comprises VLCCs, Suezmaxes, and Aframaxes plus an asset-light U.S. Gulf ship-to-ship lightering business. Product Carriers comprises LR2, LR1, and MR vessels carrying refined products. At year-end 2025 the fleet was 70 operating vessels (62 owned, 8 chartered-in) plus four LR1 dual-fuel-ready newbuilds for 2026 delivery; by Q1 2026 it had been trimmed to 67 after selling seven of the oldest ships. Average fleet age is roughly 10.9 years.

The operating model is a hybrid: in-house commercial and technical oversight with outsourced technical management and pool-based commercial deployment across six pools (Tankers International, Maersk Suezmax, Panamax International, CPTA, Norden, Aframax International). This produces high upside leverage in strong markets and corresponding downside in weak ones.

INSW acquired CMB.Tech's 50% interest in Tankers (UK) Agencies Limited (TUKA) in January 2026, the commercial manager of the Tankers International VLCC pool, taking it to 100% ownership. Tankers International subsequently launched a new Suezmax pool (operational March 2026), into which INSW moved its Suezmaxes from the Maersk pool. This is vertical integration into pool and commercial management, adding scale, cargo access, and a small services revenue stream. The company completed a redomiciliation to Bermuda in December 2025.

Revenue and earnings declined off the 2023 peak as rates normalized, then snapped back sharply in Q1 2026. FY2025 operating margin was 41%, net margin 36.7%, ROE 15.3%, ROA 11.6%. The Q1 2026 result included $88M of vessel-sale gains; adjusted net income of $194M ($3.90 diluted) was the company's strongest quarter since Q4 2022.

The balance sheet is the most defensible part of the story. Debt/equity is 0.32, current ratio 3.71, and net debt/total capitalization fell to 16.5% at year-end 2025. In 2025 the company issued $250M of 7.125% senior unsecured 2030 bonds to retire higher-cost Ocean Yield lease financing, eliminating ~$22M/year of mandatory amortization, and entered a Korean ECA facility (up to ~$240M, SOFR+125bps) for the LR1 newbuilds. The Z-Score of 3.79 sits comfortably in the safe zone. There is no near-term refinancing cliff and no covenant stress at current asset values.

Capital allocation has been the differentiating discipline: $1B+ returned to shareholders since 2020 through a counter-cyclical playbook of selling old tonnage into strong vessel markets, building/buying modern dual-fuel-ready ships, deleveraging, and paying large variable dividends. The 85%-of-adjusted-NI payout policy ties shareholder returns directly to spot rates.

The earnings-surprise record is striking: four consecutive beats, with Q1 2026 actual EPS of $3.90 versus a $2.64 estimate (+47.6%), preceded by +25.4%, +21.6%, and +5.6% surprises. The pattern reflects how poorly the sell-side models spot-rate spikes — a genuine forecasting limitation that cuts both ways at turning points.

## Growth Analysis

The estimate-revision data is dramatic. Current-year consensus EPS jumped to $13.90 from $5.29 ninety days ago (+162.8%) and from $6.01 thirty days ago (+131.1%). The current-quarter estimate moved to $5.68 from $1.46 (+287.9% over 90 days). Under the system's normal framework, +30% revisions over 90 days combined with revenue growth is a strong BUY signal indicating the market is underestimating an inflection.

This signal must be read carefully here. The estimate revisions are not tracking a structural earnings inflection from a depressed base; they are tracking a spot-rate spike off a geopolitical premium that management itself describes as transient. The "next year" estimate tells the real story: $6.42, less than half the current-year $13.90, even after a +84.7% revision. Consensus itself expects current earnings power to roughly halve as rates normalize. Applying the inflection-stock framework, which is designed for companies recovering from troughs toward mid-cycle, would invert the actual situation. INSW is at or near a cyclical peak, and the revision velocity reflects analysts catching up to a spike that is expected to fade, not a durable re-rating.

Structural growth drivers are real but modest relative to the rate cycle: the TUKA/Tankers International vertical integration adds commercial scale and a services revenue stream; four LR1 dual-fuel newbuilds deliver in 2026; and ton-mile demand remains elevated from re-routed and sanctioned trade flows. None of these offset the dominant variable, which is the spot rate. The growth score reflects strong near-term momentum tempered by consensus's own expectation that earnings power contracts materially in 2027.

## Valuation Assessment

On trailing numbers INSW looks cheap at 6.99x EPS of $6.23 and a ~1% base dividend yield (the variable dividend produces a far higher trailing payout). The forward picture is the relevant one. On current-year consensus of $13.90, the stock trades at ~5.5x; on next-year consensus of $6.42, it trades at ~11.9x. The wide gap between those two multiples is the cycle in one statistic.

Peer comparison in Oil & Gas Storage & Transportation shows a median P/E of 24.8, but the peer set (KNTK, AM, DTM, VNOM) is dominated by stable-cash-flow midstream operators that deserve higher multiples than a spot-exposed tanker owner. The cleaner comparable is Dorian LPG (LPG) at 9.6x, another cyclical shipping name, which frames the tanker cohort at high single-digit to low double-digit trailing multiples. Tanker equities structurally trade at low multiples on peak earnings precisely because the market discounts the durability of those earnings.

The honest valuation read: INSW is neither clearly cheap nor clearly expensive. At ~$76 it trades at roughly 11-12x normalized next-year earnings, which is a fair-to-slightly-elevated multiple for a cyclical at peak rates. Net asset value provides downside support (vessel values are elevated and the balance sheet is nearly unlevered), but the 10-K flags that nine MRs are carried ~$43.3M above appraised value, so a rate downturn would pressure both earnings and asset marks. The analyst price targets ($56 low, $120 high, $91.5 median) bracket a range that is itself an admission of cycle uncertainty: a ~2.1x spread between low and high targets is unusually wide and reflects the binary geopolitical setup rather than disagreement about fundamentals.

The valuation score of 5.5 reflects a stock that is reasonably priced on normalized earnings with real NAV support, but without the asymmetric mispricing required for a high score. The market is not obviously wrong about INSW; it is pricing a cyclical peak at a cyclical multiple.

## Competitive Landscape

The tanker market is highly fragmented and competitive, with independents, oil majors, state fleets, and traders all participating; no single owner moves rates. INSW competes on fleet quality and age, operational reliability, oil-major vetting clearance, and pool scale. Peers include Scorpio Tankers (STNG), Frontline, and DHT Holdings.

INSW's competitive position has improved, consistent with the prior trajectory (competitive position rose from 6.5 to 7.5 across recent reports). The TUKA acquisition moves the company from pool participant to pool owner/manager in both VLCC and Suezmax segments, a consolidation step that improves cargo access and adds a fee stream. The balanced, modern, dual-fuel-ready fleet across all major asset classes and the fortress balance sheet enable counter-cyclical fleet trading that smaller, more levered peers cannot match. The current up-cycle is supported by constrained shipyard capacity (yards prioritizing LNG, containers, and bulkers) keeping vessel values elevated.

We hold competitive position at 7.0 rather than extending the trajectory to 7.5. The improvement is real, but the rising orderbook is an industry-wide capacity addition that will eventually compress the scale advantage's earnings benefit, and pool counterparties can exit on notice. Trajectory is positive; level is solid but not dominant.

## Risk Assessment

**Cyclicality and spot exposure — the dominant risk.** With ~82% spot revenue, earnings and the dividend swing violently with rates. The 10-K is explicit that normalization "could cause tanker rates to decline significantly."

**Hormuz/geopolitical dependence — live and two-sided.** Management flagged the Strait of Hormuz situation as "more significant" than typical disruptions, with >20M bbl/day that cannot be substituted. A de-escalation or Hormuz-reopening scenario would compress the disruption premium directly. Tanker owners that monetize the disruption premium are among the most exposed to a genuine reopening. This is the single largest swing factor in the stock.

**Rising newbuild supply.** The orderbook is the structural mechanism for ending the up-cycle as deliveries arrive.

**Vessel-value/impairment risk.** A rate downturn could trigger impairments on the nine MRs carried above appraised value and pressure covenants.

**Tax structure.** The near-zero effective tax rate relies on the U.S. Section 883 exemption and Bermuda's Qualifying International Shipping Income exclusion under Pillar Two. Both depend on substance/ownership tests — low probability, high impact if challenged.

**Capital-allocation tension.** The simultaneous record special dividend, ATM equity program launched near cyclical highs, and C-suite selling into post-earnings strength send mixed signals. The benign read is dry powder for opportunistic acquisitions; the cautious read is that management views the equity as fully valued near a peak. The April 9, 2026 8-K flagging "material modification of securityholders' rights" (likely a rights-plan replacement after the April 10 poison-pill expiry, per the brief — not read in full) warrants monitoring.

**Other:** Marshall Islands incorporation (weaker shareholder protections), holding-company dependence on subsidiary distributions, reliance on third-party technical managers, and the suspended-but-reinstatable US/China port-fee regime affecting 13 China-built vessels.

The risk score of 4.5 reflects a binary geopolitical catalyst with asymmetric downside that the market is only partially pricing, against a balance sheet that limits the magnitude of any blow-up.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM IV is 64.9% versus 36.8% realized volatility — a 28.1-point premium at the 92nd percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (64.9% near-term vs. 54.3% six-month), which the market typically prices ahead of a known binary event. Put/call skew is elevated at 7.3% (one-month), indicating downside demand, and the implied move for the nearest expiry is ±12.5%. ATM IV rose 6.5 points intraday on May 29 (58.4% to 64.9%) on a roughly flat price. A single-day call sweep of 723 contracts at the $80 June strike (6.3x open interest) shows some positioning for a near-term rebound, but the dominant signal is elevated near-term vol and put skew consistent with the binary Hormuz setup. This confirms rather than challenges the two-sided fundamental view and argues against committing to a directional BUY ahead of catalyst resolution.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Geopolitical disruptions persist or intensify — Hormuz tension continues, Red Sea re-routing endures, sanctions remain — keeping VLCC and Suezmax rates elevated through 2026. The dividend policy delivers a very high trailing yield, and the fortress balance sheet lets management keep buying modern tonnage cheaply while peers struggle. The orderbook delivers slowly into a still-tight market. In this scenario, current-year EPS approaches the $13.90 consensus and the stock re-rates toward the $120 high target on sustained earnings and dividend capture.

**Bear case.** A US-Iran framework reopens Hormuz, Red Sea transit normalizes, and the disruption premium compresses. VLCC TCE reverts toward mid-cycle levels (the $30-40k/day range seen a year ago), earnings fall toward or below the $6.42 next-year estimate, and the variable dividend contracts sharply. The orderbook begins delivering into a softening market, pressuring both rates and vessel values. Insider selling and the ATM at cyclical highs prove prescient. The stock derates toward the $56 low target.

The probability-weighted distribution is two-sided, which is why the stock is a HOLD rather than a BUY. The score sits at 6.1; a BUY at this conviction would require asymmetric upside that a peak-cycle cyclical with a fading geopolitical premium does not offer.

**Rebuttal to a BUY case.** The specific, falsifiable reason this is not a BUY: current earnings rest on a geopolitical premium that management explicitly describes as benefitting results since 2023 and warns will normalize. Tanker owners monetizing the disruption premium are the most exposed to a reopening. The next-year consensus, less than half the current-year figure, is consensus's own quantification of how transient current earnings power is. Combined with C-suite selling into the post-earnings spike, the dilutive ATM launched near the cyclical high, and an options market pricing a ±12.5% near-term move with elevated put skew, the burden of proof for a BUY at a cyclical peak is not met. This is testable: if Hormuz physically reopens with 72+ hours of sustained commercial transit, VLCC TCE would compress and the bear case activates; if disruptions persist through Q2 earnings, the bull case strengthens.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The thesis rests on the variable dividend and fortress balance sheet providing real support at current levels, against a binary geopolitical catalyst that caps directional conviction in either direction. Reassessment timeline: Q2 2026 earnings and the trajectory of the Hormuz situation over the next 1-3 months.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
1. Hormuz/Red Sea disruptions demonstrably entrench (not de-escalate) AND VLCC spot TCE holds above ~$70k/day into Q2 earnings, with next-year consensus revised upward toward $9-10 — converting the spike into more durable earnings power.
2. The stock derates meaningfully (toward the $56-60 area) on rate softness while the balance sheet and dividend coverage remain intact — improving risk/reward to clearly asymmetric.
3. Clarification of the ATM use-of-proceeds toward accretive, value-creating fleet/strategic acquisitions at attractive vessel prices.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Confirmed Hormuz reopening with 72+ hours of sustained commercial transit AND VLCC TCE reverting below ~$40k/day, with the dividend policy producing a sharply lower payout.
2. Impairment charges on the vessel fleet or covenant pressure as asset values decline.
3. Continued, larger-scale C-suite selling alongside further dilutive equity issuance, signaling management views the equity as overvalued.

## Conclusion

International Seaways is a high-quality, exceptionally well-capitalized tanker operator generating peak-cycle cash flow and returning most of it to shareholders. The financial health is top-tier, and the competitive position has improved through the Tankers International vertical integration. The problem is not the company; it is the timing. Current earnings rest on a geopolitical premium that management, consensus next-year estimates, insider selling, the ATM, and the options market all suggest is unlikely to persist at present intensity. The optically cheap ~7x trailing multiple is a cyclical-peak multiple, not a bargain.

The four-report BUY streak deserves a higher bar to continue, and the new data — a ~17% pullback from the May high concurrent with insiders selling, a dilutive ATM, and options pricing imminent stress — sharpens a binary setup rather than strengthening the bull case. We move to HOLD on an income basis. The dividend provides real support and the balance sheet eliminates blow-up risk, but the asymmetric upside required for a BUY is absent near a multi-decade earnings peak with a fading geopolitical tailwind. We would become more constructive on either entrenched disruption confirmed by sustained Q2 rates or a meaningful derating that restores asymmetry.
