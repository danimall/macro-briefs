## Executive Summary

Aramark is a $11.8B market cap pure-play food and facilities services provider operating in 16 countries, holding the #2 position in North America behind Compass Group. Following the September 2023 Vestis spin-off, the company has demonstrated solid operational momentum with FY2025 revenue of $18.5B (+6.4%), adjusted EBITDA of $1,465M, and free cash flow of $432M. Management has guided FY2026 to 7-9% organic revenue growth and 20-25% adjusted EPS growth.

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) the international segment accelerating to 17% revenue growth in Q1 FY2026, (2) Business & Industry demand surging 18% on return-to-office dynamics, and (3) operating leverage from scale. However, at $45.08 (near the 52-week high of $46.88), the stock trades at ~37x trailing P/E and ~20x forward P/E on FY2026 consensus of $2.22, which prices in substantial execution against a backdrop of CEO succession uncertainty, $5.4B in debt with rising interest rates, and a recent track record of earnings misses (2 misses in last 4 quarters). The options market is pricing imminent stress with near-term IV of 64.9% versus 23.9% historical volatility.

The 12-month growth thesis is supported by reaffirmed guidance and a favorable analyst consensus (14 of 16 at Buy/Strong Buy, median target $48). The path to the next leg higher requires: (1) successful CEO succession announcement, (2) Q2-Q4 FY2026 earnings acceleration validating guidance, and (3) leverage trending toward the sub-3x target. We rate ARMK a HOLD with specific upgrade triggers tied to these catalysts.

## Company Overview

Aramark provides food services, facilities management, and related support services across two segments: FSS United States (71% of revenue, $13.2B) and FSS International (29%, $5.3B). The company serves education institutions, healthcare facilities, businesses, sports venues, and government clients. It holds contracts with 26 major professional sports teams and approximately 30 NCAA Division I college football stadiums.

The business model is characterized by long-term contracts (5-15 years for education/sports), approximately two-thirds structured as profit & loss contracts (where Aramark bears cost risk), and significant labor intensity with ~278,000 employees. No single client exceeds 2% of revenue, and Sysco distributes approximately 43% of U.S./Canada food purchases.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Growth:**
FY2025 revenue reached $18.5B, growing 6.4% reported (approximately 4.4% organic excluding a 53rd-week benefit). The growth was broad-based: Business & Industry led at +18%, followed by Education (+5.2%), Healthcare (+4.8%), and Sports/Leisure/Corrections (+4.1%). International revenue grew 9.8%.

Q1 FY2026 (ended January 2, 2026) produced revenue of $4.83B, approximately +8% organic growth after adjusting for the 53rd-week calendar shift. The international segment delivered 17% revenue growth.

**Profitability:**
Operating margins remain thin at 4.3%, characteristic of the industry. Personnel costs represent approximately 42% of revenue and grew 8.7% in FY2025, outpacing revenue growth, driven by medical claims inflation (+$19.8M), severance, and business expansion. Supply chain efficiencies partially offset this pressure but have not yet driven meaningful margin expansion.

Adjusted operating income grew 12.1% to $792M in FY2025 and is guided to grow 12-17% in FY2026. The operating leverage thesis depends on revenue growth outpacing cost inflation, which has been inconsistent: Q4 FY2025 saw a 14.4% EPS miss.

**Cash Flow and Balance Sheet:**
FY2025 operating cash flow was $921M (up 26.8%), with free cash flow of $432M (2.3% FCF yield on current market cap). Q1 FY2026 showed seasonal cash consumption of -$902M (worse than prior year's -$587M in operating cash flow), attributed to new business mobilization costs and timing.

Total debt stands at $5.4B with a debt/equity ratio of 4.23x. Net Debt/Covenant Adjusted EBITDA is approximately 3.2x, with management targeting below 3.0x. Covenant headroom is substantial (secured debt ratio 2.26x vs. 5.125x requirement; interest coverage 4.12x vs. 2.0x minimum). The weighted average interest rate is projected to rise from 4.03% to 5.73% as legacy instruments mature.

The capital allocation tension is real: $5.4B debt, $432M FCF, a $500M buyback authorization, $489M in growth capex, and $264M in acquisitions. Deleveraging to under 3x while executing all other priorities requires substantial EBITDA growth.

**Earnings Quality Concerns:**
The recent earnings track record is weak: 1 beat, 1 in-line, and 2 misses over the last 4 quarters, including a 14.4% miss in Q4 FY2025. This undermines confidence in management's forward guidance, particularly the 20-25% EPS growth target.

## Growth Analysis

The 12-month growth thesis centers on:

1. **International acceleration:** The segment delivered 17% revenue growth in Q1 FY2026 with 16% AOI growth, representing the strongest growth vector. Expansion in the UK, Chile, and Germany is driving share gains. At 29% of revenue and growing nearly 3x faster than the U.S. segment, international is the key to above-market growth.

2. **Business & Industry return-to-office:** B&I grew 18% in FY2025 as corporate clients expand on-site dining to incentivize office attendance. This represents a secular shift from pre-COVID norms. While the growth rate may decelerate, the runway for B&I contract wins remains significant as corporations upgrade amenities.

3. **Operating leverage potential:** FY2026 guidance for 12-17% AOI growth on 7-9% organic revenue growth implies 170-250bps of operating leverage. If personnel cost inflation moderates and supply chain efficiencies compound, the margin expansion story could materialize.

4. **New contract wins:** Management is investing heavily in mobilization (contributing to the Q1 cash burn) to onboard new clients. Net new sales is a compensation metric and management is explicitly targeting share gains.

FY2026 consensus EPS of $2.22 represents approximately 82% growth over the $1.22 trailing figure, and next year consensus of $2.62 represents an additional 18% growth. These are meaningful growth rates for a services business, though partially reflecting the comparison against a soft prior year.

## Valuation Assessment

At $45.08, ARMK trades at:
- ~37x trailing EPS ($1.22)
- ~20.3x FY2026E EPS ($2.22)
- ~17.2x FY2027E EPS ($2.62)
- EV/EBITDA approximately 11.5x (FY2025 Covenant Adjusted EBITDA of $1,465M)
- FCF yield of approximately 3.6% on FY2025 FCF

Compass Group (the larger, more profitable peer) trades at approximately 25-28x forward earnings with higher margins and lower leverage. Aramark's discount to Compass on forward P/E is narrower than historical averages, suggesting the market is already pricing in the acceleration story.

The analyst consensus median target is $48 (6.5% upside), with a high of $52 (15.4% upside) and low of $35 (22.4% downside). Oppenheimer's recent raise to $50 reflects confidence in FY2026 execution.

At 20x forward earnings, the stock requires consistent execution on the 20-25% EPS growth guidance to justify the current multiple. Given 2 earnings misses in 4 quarters and the inherent margin fragility of the business (operating at 4.3% with 42% of revenue in personnel costs rising faster than revenue), there is limited margin of safety at current prices.

## Competitive Landscape

Aramark's #2 North American position provides scale advantages in procurement (Sysco relationship for 43% of purchases), contract bidding, and technology deployment. The company's competitive position is stable with some evidence of improvement:

- International share gains (17% revenue growth exceeding industry)
- B&I contract wins driven by post-COVID corporate demand
- Strategic exits from low-margin Facilities & Other accounts (-7.1% deliberate shrinkage)
- 193% TSR vs. 134% peer group over measurement period

However, Compass Group's substantially larger global scale provides procurement advantages Aramark cannot fully replicate. The thin-margin nature of the business means small competitive losses on pricing produce outsized earnings impact. Client self-operation remains a competitive threat, and food delivery aggregators create incremental pressure on captive dining populations.

## Risk Assessment

**CEO Succession (High Impact, Uncertain Timeline):** The February 2026 8-K confirms active search for John Zillmer's successor. Retention grants to both COOs ($3M each) signal the board wants operational continuity but hasn't selected an internal candidate. Strategic direction uncertainty could persist for 3-12 months. Historically, CEO transitions at services companies produce 3-6 months of multiple compression followed by re-rating if the successor is well-received.

**Leverage in Rising-Rate Environment:** At $5.4B debt with weighted average cost rising from 4.03% to 5.73%, annual interest expense is poised to increase by approximately $90M over the next 3 years. This directly compresses the EPS growth rate unless EBITDA growth fully offsets it.

**Margin Fragility:** Personnel costs growing at 8.7% versus revenue at 6.4% is unsustainable without either pricing power or productivity gains. Medical claims inflation (+$19.8M) and the 90,000+ annual hires in the U.S. create persistent upward pressure.

**Tariff/Input Cost Risk:** With ~28% of revenue in food/supply costs and two-thirds of contracts structured as P&L (limited pass-through), escalating food costs from tariffs or supply chain disruptions could materially compress already-thin margins.

**Earnings Credibility:** Two misses in four quarters weakens confidence in forward guidance. The 53rd-week comparable makes FY2026 comparisons optically challenging even if underlying growth is strong.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($50-52, +11-15%):**
- FY2026 guidance of 20-25% EPS growth is achieved or exceeded
- International segment sustains 15%+ growth, creating a structural re-rating catalyst
- New CEO announcement is well-received by the market (external hire from Compass/Sodexo with credibility)
- Leverage falls below 3x by year-end FY2026, de-risking the equity story
- B&I demand persists as corporations continue investing in on-site amenities

**Bear Case ($37-39, -13-18%):**
- Continued earnings misses force guidance reductions (margin pressures from labor/food costs)
- CEO succession is prolonged (>12 months) or the chosen successor disappoints
- Oil-driven inflation pushes food costs higher than contracts can absorb
- Consumer spending weakness reduces client willingness to upgrade food service programs
- Rising rates increase interest expense faster than EBITDA grows, stalling EPS momentum

## Conclusion

Aramark at $45.08 presents a credible growth story (7-9% organic revenue, 20-25% EPS growth guided) with specific catalysts that justify holding on a 12-month horizon. The international acceleration, B&I secular tailwind, and operating leverage potential provide a reasonable path to $48-50 if execution materializes. However, trading near the 52-week high at ~20x forward, with rising debt costs, CEO succession uncertainty, and a weak recent earnings track record, the risk/reward at current prices does not favor adding new capital. The options market's extreme near-term premium and backwardated term structure reinforce near-term caution. HOLD with clear upgrade triggers tied to execution proof and governance clarity.
