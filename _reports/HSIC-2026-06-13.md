**Research Date: June 13, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Henry Schein is the world's largest distributor of healthcare products to office-based dental and medical practices, in the middle of a governance reset driven by an activist campaign and an escalating KKR partnership. The investable question is whether the BOLD+1 strategy (shifting revenue mix toward company-manufactured specialty products and practice-management software) and the stated ~$125M profit-lift plan can expand margins enough to recover earnings power that remains roughly 35% below the 2021 peak. Net income fell from $660M (2021) to a $398M trough (2024) before a modest recovery to $419M (2025), while revenue kept rising, a divergence that defines the bull/bear debate. The Q1 FY2026 non-GAAP EPS beat ($1.32 vs $1.24 consensus, +6.3%) and reaffirmed full-year guidance show the recovery is on track, but underlying organic growth was only 2.5% with FX contributing roughly half of the headline 6.3% revenue gain.

The 12-month holding thesis has a concrete spine: a new CEO incentivized for the first time in company history with stock options (177,116 at $77.60 strike), a KKR-designated director's $692K open-market purchase at $69.19 in May, continued KKR swap accumulation toward its 19.9% standstill cap, and a board-downsizing concession that signals the value-creation program is being actively pushed. These are forward-looking signals of insiders putting capital behind a re-rating. Against this, the balance sheet is the constraint: total debt rose to $3.41B against $138M cash, goodwill of $4.28B exceeds total equity of $3.9B (tangible equity is negative), there is a $903M redeemable noncontrolling-interest put liability, and Q1 operating cash flow swung negative to -$97M.

At $81.25 the stock trades at roughly 15.2x the FY2026 consensus of $5.35 and 13.7x next year's $5.92, a discount to the distributor peer median of ~21.6x. The discount is partly earned by lower organic growth, negative tangible equity, and execution risk, but it leaves real upside if margin expansion materializes. We rate HSIC HOLD with a growth-hold rationale: the turnaround has credible insider backing and a measurable catalyst path, but organic growth and cash flow have not yet confirmed the recovery, and the asymmetry is not yet wide enough to clear our bar for a BUY.

## Company Overview

Henry Schein has more than 94 years of operating history, over 25,000 employees, operations in 34 countries, and more than 1 million customers. It reorganized into three reportable segments:

- **Global Distribution & Value-Added Services (~84% of sales):** distribution of dental merchandise and equipment plus medical products (pharmaceuticals, vaccines, surgical, diagnostics, PPE), alongside financial services, continuing education, and consulting. Gross margin ~25.8%.
- **Global Specialty Products (~12% of sales):** company-manufactured dental implants, biomaterials, endodontics, orthodontics, and orthopedics. Gross margin ~55%.
- **Global Technology (~5% of sales):** practice-management software (Henry Schein One JV with Internet Brands), e-services, and cloud platforms. Gross margin ~69%.

The strategic logic of BOLD+1 (2025–2027) is to shift mix toward the higher-margin specialty and technology segments, largely through acquisitions, while driving distribution efficiency through a unified "One Schein" go-to-market model. The core business is a classic low-margin distributor positioned between large suppliers and increasingly consolidated buyers (dental support organizations and group purchasing organizations). The moat is scale, breadth of single-source supply, distribution infrastructure, and customer relationships. The strategic vulnerability is that those buyers and suppliers both have growing leverage over distributor margins.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings power trajectory.** Net income has not recovered to prior peaks despite rising revenue: $660M (2021), $566M (2022), $436M (2023), $398M (2024), $419M (2025). Non-GAAP EPS followed: $5.05, $5.38, $4.50, $4.74, $4.97. The October 2023 cyberattack and subsequent margin pressure drove the trough; 2025 recovery was modest. Earnings power remains below the 2022 peak of $5.38.

**Q1 FY2026 (quarter ended 3/28/2026).** Net sales of $3.37B were up 6.3% YoY, with composition of 2.5% internal local-currency growth, 0.7% acquisitions, and 3.1% FX. GAAP operating income was $182M (+4%); GAAP diluted EPS $0.92 vs $0.88. Management reaffirmed full-year 2026 guidance. Gross margin improved modestly to 31.8% from 31.6%. Global Dental was the standout (+9%, equipment +8.6%), Technology grew 7% on cloud adoption, and Medical lagged at +1.7%.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow was negative $97M versus positive $37M a year earlier, a $134M swing driven by a $223M working-capital drawdown in accounts payable and accrued expenses. Q1 is seasonally the weakest quarter for HSIC working capital (inventory forward-buys peak Q4 into Q1), so some of this is timing. The YoY deterioration, DSO rising to 45.7 days from 44.1, and inventory turns slipping to 4.6 from 4.8 warrant monitoring because debt-funded buybacks and acquisitions become more expensive when internal cash generation softens.

**Balance sheet — the binding constraint.** Total debt rose to $3.41B at 3/28/26 from $3.11B at 12/27/25, with bank credit lines jumping to $1.05B from $764M, against only $138M cash. Goodwill of $4.28B and intangibles of $1.0B sit against total equity of $3.9B, meaning goodwill alone exceeds tangible book and tangible equity is negative, a direct consequence of the acquisition-heavy strategy. There is an additional $903M of redeemable noncontrolling interests: minority holders in acquired subsidiaries (notably Internet Brands' 33.6% of Henry Schein One, with put rights paused to January 2029) can force HSIC to buy them out at fair value. Weighted debt cost is roughly 4.6%–5.6%, interest expense rose to $39M from $35M, and credit facilities were amended in June 2025 to relax leverage covenants, which indicates leverage had been pressing against prior limits.

**Capital allocation.** HSIC has repurchased $6.1B of stock since 2003, added $750M to authorization in September 2025 ($655M remaining), and bought back $126M in Q1 2026. There is no dividend. The buyback program is increasingly debt-funded given the negative operating cash flow, and the KKR equity issuance was offset by a simultaneous $250M accelerated repurchase.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis is mix-shift margin expansion rather than top-line acceleration. Underlying organic growth is modest, so the value lever is moving revenue toward the specialty and technology segments. Management's stated target is a ~$125M profit lift by 2026 from the value-creation plan, reiterated at a late-May Stifel conference where the new CEO emphasized market-share gains and stable dental demand.

Estimate revisions are stable rather than rising: current-year EPS consensus moved +0.5% over 90 days to $5.35, next-year +0.1% to $5.92. The current-quarter estimate ticked up +2.9% over 90 days, but the next quarter was cut -5.6%. This is below the >30% upward revision velocity that would signal a fundamental acceleration; it is a flat-to-slightly-positive consensus consistent with a business executing a gradual recovery. The earnings beat track record is solid (3 of the last 4 quarters, +6.3% and +5.8% in two of them), which lends moderate confidence to the FY2026 guide.

Secular demand tailwinds are real but slow: the 45+ U.S. population grows ~10% by 2036, the 85+ cohort doubles by 2050, procedures continue migrating to outpatient/office settings, and U.S. health spend is projected to rise from $5.3T (2024) toward $8.6T (2033). These support volumes over a multi-year horizon but do not by themselves expand distributor margins, which is the variable that drives HSIC's earnings recovery.

## Valuation Assessment

At $81.25, HSIC trades at ~15.2x FY2026 consensus EPS of $5.35 and ~13.7x next year's $5.92. The reported trailing P/E of ~24.3x reflects depressed GAAP earnings; the forward non-GAAP multiple is the relevant anchor. Against the Health Care Distributors peer median of 21.6x (CAH 33.8x, COR 21.6x, MCK 20.5x), HSIC trades at a meaningful discount.

The discount is partially justified and partially an opportunity. It is justified by HSIC's lower organic growth (2.5% vs the scale efficiency of the McKesson/Cencora drug-distribution model), its negative tangible equity, the $903M put liability, and execution risk on a turnaround that has not yet shown margin expansion in reported numbers. It is an opportunity to the extent the BOLD+1 mix shift delivers the $125M profit lift, which would push EPS toward the high end of guidance and support a re-rating toward the peer multiple. Applying ~16x to the $5.92 forward year (a partial re-rating that still keeps a discount to peers for the lower growth profile) yields roughly $95; the analyst median target is $90 with a high of $100, consistent with this math.

I am scoring valuation at 5.5 rather than higher because the re-rating is contingent on margin proof that is not yet visible, and because the negative tangible equity and put liability mean the discount is at least partly earned rather than purely a mispricing. The case requires the operational turn to materialize rather than resting on a low multiple alone.

## Competitive Landscape

HSIC's competitive position is stable-to-slightly-eroding, which is why the score sits in the middle of the range and reflects the -1.0 decline in this dimension across prior reports. The chief dental-distribution competitor is Patterson Companies; in medical distribution it faces McKesson, Cardinal Health, and Cencora. The structural headwinds are well documented: DSO/GPO consolidation gives buyers pricing leverage over distributors, manufacturers increasingly sell direct to end users, and online price transparency and e-commerce erode the value of pure distribution. HSIC is mid-rollout of a new U.S./Canada e-commerce platform, which is both a defensive necessity and an integration risk flagged in its own internal-controls disclosure.

The differentiation strategy is pushing into proprietary specialty products and software where HSIC controls the margin rather than merely distributing third-party goods, the right response to these headwinds, and dental's +9% Q1 growth plus technology's +7% suggest the higher-value segments are gaining. But these are still ~17% of sales combined, so the mix shift is early. Supplier concentration is moderate (top 10 = ~24% of purchases, largest ~4%) with no long-term contracts and meaningful rebate dependency.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage and acquisition complexity.** The balance sheet is more leveraged than headline debt/EBITDA suggests, given the goodwill exceeding equity and the redeemable NCI put. Covenant relaxation in June 2025 confirms the leverage was tightening against limits. Negative Q1 operating cash flow, while partly seasonal, compounds this if it persists.

**Governance transition.** Founder-CEO Stanley Bergman handed the role to Frederick Lowery on March 2, 2026, after 40+ years. The 10-K carries new, dedicated risk factors for the KKR partnership (now a 19.9% ownership path with two board seats, with explicit conflict-of-interest language because KKR invests in HSIC's customers, suppliers, and competitors), activist-investor pressure, and CEO transition. Board downsizing after the 2026 annual meeting is a concession to that pressure. These are double-edged: they signal active value pursuit but also strategic-direction uncertainty.

**Cybersecurity.** The October 2023 incident materially hurt FY2023 results, with residual 2024 impact and continuing insurance recoveries. Management acknowledges HSIC may be perceived as a more vulnerable target.

**Regulatory and macro.** OBBBA (July 2025) Medicaid cuts could reduce utilization; EU MDR compliance and broad fraud-and-abuse exposure remain. Tariffs temporarily dampened U.S. dental equipment sales in mid-2025 but are not yet material. The opioid MDL is settled at immaterial amounts.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress that diverges from the constructive insider signal. Near-term ATM IV is 41.1% versus 27.4% one-year historical volatility — rich by 13.7 points and in the 63rd percentile — with a 12.9% put-call skew (elevated put demand) and a backwardated term structure (41.1% near-term vs 28.2% six-month). The implied move on the nearest expiry is ±4.2%. ATM IV jumped 11.2 points (29.9% to 41.1%) over the prior two weeks even as the stock rose 6.2%. This pattern typically reflects hedging into a specific near-dated catalyst (the June options expiry and macro events) rather than a durable directional bet; the six-month skew has flattened to -2.6%, indicating the longer-dated structure is calm. The front-end spike is event-hedging noise, but the elevated put demand is a flag against the bull case in the very near term. With short interest at 8.6% of float and 4.5 days to cover, positioning is not extreme.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($95–100).** The BOLD+1 mix shift delivers the ~$125M profit lift, specialty and technology continue compounding faster than distribution, gross margin expands beyond the modest 31.6%→31.8% creep, and FY2026 EPS reaches the upper end of the guide trajectory with FY2027 toward $5.92+. A new option-incentivized CEO and continued KKR accumulation drive a re-rating from ~15x toward the peer ~21x. At 16x on $5.92 the stock reaches ~$95; full peer-parity re-rating implies higher. The KKR director's open-market buy and KKR's swap accumulation are the strongest insider conviction signals in the file.

**Bear case ($64–68).** Organic growth stays stuck near 2.5%, FX reverses, the margin expansion fails to show in reported numbers, and net income stays ~35% below the 2021 peak. Negative operating cash flow persists beyond seasonality, debt-funded buybacks strain the balance sheet against the put liability, and the multiple stays compressed or de-rates. The analyst low target of $64 (~21% downside) frames this outcome, which corresponds to roughly 12x the FY2026 estimate.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (growth-hold).** The 12-month thesis is that the activist/KKR-driven turnaround produces visible margin expansion and a partial re-rating, supported by insider buying and an option-incentivized CEO. The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is that organic growth, estimate revisions, and cash flow have not yet confirmed the recovery, and the balance sheet earns part of the valuation discount. The risk/reward is roughly symmetric to modestly favorable, not the asymmetric setup required for a BUY.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Two consecutive quarters of organic (local-currency) growth above 4% combined with gross margin expansion above 32.5%, confirming the mix shift is flowing through.
- Operating cash flow returning to positive on a trailing-twelve-month basis with debt/EBITDA declining, removing the balance-sheet constraint.
- FY2026 EPS guidance raised, or consensus revisions turning positive by >5% over 90 days.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Organic growth turning negative or guidance cut, signaling the recovery has stalled.
- Sustained negative operating cash flow into Q2/Q3 (beyond seasonal explanation) with continued debt-funded buybacks, or a goodwill impairment.
- Departure of KKR's board influence or signs the value-creation plan is being abandoned.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next quarterly earnings (Q2 FY2026, expected early August), with the Stifel-cited $125M profit-lift progress as the key operational checkpoint.

## Conclusion

Henry Schein is a credible, insider-backed turnaround of a dominant but structurally pressured distribution franchise. The forward signals are constructive: a KKR-designated director's $692K open-market purchase, KKR's continued accumulation toward its 19.9% cap, a new CEO incentivized with options for the first time in company history, and a reaffirmed FY2026 guide backed by a 3-of-4 earnings-beat record. The improving management-quality trajectory (5.0 to 6.5 across prior reports) reflects this governance reset.

The reasons to hold rather than buy are equally concrete: net income is still ~35% below 2021, organic growth is only 2.5% with FX masking the underlying rate, consensus estimates are flat rather than accelerating, operating cash flow turned negative in Q1, and the balance sheet carries negative tangible equity plus a $903M put liability. At ~15.2x forward earnings the stock is cheap to peers, but the discount is partly earned, and the re-rating depends on margin proof that has not yet appeared in reported numbers. We maintain HOLD with a base case toward the high-$80s/low-$90s over 12 months as the turnaround plays out, and we will revisit toward BUY if Q2/Q3 deliver organic-growth acceleration and margin expansion with cash flow normalizing.
