**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

XP Inc. is the leading independent investment-distribution platform in Brazil, built on an open-architecture model that disrupted the country's bank-concentrated wealth-management industry. The business is genuinely profitable, generates a high reported ROE, and trades at a single-digit multiple of forward earnings. The bull case is straightforward: a cheap stock with rising earnings power in a structurally growing market.

The problem with that case is the composition of the growth. Both the company's own commentary and outside analysis converge on the same diagnosis: XP's 2025-2026 reacceleration was carried by its Corporate & Issuer Services (wholesale) segment, operating leverage, and market-driven appreciation in client assets, not by a recovery in retail trading or net new money. The core retail engine that defines the franchise remains soft. Layered on top is a deliberate migration of the advisor-compensation model toward fee-based structures, a strategically sound move that injects near-term revenue-mix and advisor-retention uncertainty.

The forward indicators are pointing the wrong way. Consensus EPS estimates have fallen across the current quarter (-4.1% over 90 days), next quarter (-3.1% over 90 days), and current year (-1.8% over 90 days). The company has missed consensus in three of the last four quarters, with the most recent Q1 2026 print a 5.6% miss. The stock sits at $16.20, below its 50- and 200-day moving averages, down roughly 17% from its February high and only about 4% above its 52-week low. The cheap multiple is not a mispricing the market has overlooked; it is a discount the market is applying to falling estimates, deteriorating earnings momentum, an unresolved business-model transition, and acute Brazilian macro sensitivity. We rate XP AVOID and find no specific 12-month catalyst that would justify holding through this combination of falling estimates and structural transition risk.

## Company Overview

XP Inc. is a technology-enabled financial-services platform domiciled in the Cayman Islands with operations concentrated in Brazil. It offers investment, credit, banking, insurance, and pension products at low fees, distributed primarily through a large network of independent financial advisors (IFAs, or "assessores") plus internal advisors. The company organizes itself around three pillars: (1) leadership in investments, the core retail brokerage and wealth platform; (2) a complementary retail offering in credit, cards, banking, insurance, and pension designed to deepen share-of-wallet; and (3) wholesale banking through Corporate & Issuer Services (DCM/ECM, structured products, treasury).

XP pioneered the open-architecture independent-advisory channel in a market historically dominated by Itaú, Bradesco, Santander, Banco do Brasil, and Caixa. Its moat rests on the scale of its advisor network, brand strength in the independent channel, breadth of product shelf, and an integrated technology platform. Its most direct digital-advisory rival is BTG Pactual; incumbent universal banks and the rapidly expanding fintech Nubank pressure fees from both ends of the market.

As a foreign private issuer, XP files an annual report on Form 20-F (most recently filed April 29, 2026 for FY2025) and reports interim results via Form 6-K. This limits the granularity of standardized financial disclosure relative to U.S. domestic filers, and balance-sheet capital and liquidity ratios could not be pulled from structured data, a disclosure consideration in its own right.

Market capitalization is approximately $6.6 billion at $16.20 per share, against roughly $1 billion in annualized net income run-rate.

## Financial Analysis

XP is consistently profitable. FY2025 gross revenue grew about 8% year over year to BRL 19.5 billion, which management characterized as a "challenging year" driven by a "conservative client mix": shorter investment duration, lower client market activity, and subdued retail trading. The second half reaccelerated to above 10% year-over-year growth.

The two most recent prints frame the current state precisely:

- **Q4 2025 (reported Feb 12, 2026):** Net income $239.9M; GAAP EPS $0.46; adjusted EPS $0.49 (beat $0.45 consensus). Revenue ~$949.8M, +20.4% YoY in USD. The stock rallied ~10% post-print.
- **Q1 2026 (reported May 18, 2026):** Net income $250.3M; GAAP EPS $0.47; adjusted revenue $898.7M. Adjusted EPS $0.47 was a slight miss versus $0.48 consensus, though up from $0.39 a year earlier.

Two cautions sit beneath the headline numbers. First, the USD-versus-BRL divergence is large: Q4 revenue grew ~20% in USD against management's +8% in BRL, meaning FX translation is doing meaningful work in the reported optics. Investors should track BRL operating trends, not USD-reported figures. Second, the quality of the growth is lower than the top line implies. The reacceleration has been driven by wholesale (Corporate & Issuer Services) mix shift and operating leverage, with AUC expansion attributable substantially to market appreciation and high Brazilian interest rates rather than robust net new money. If equity and fixed-income markets turn, the AUC tailwind reverses, and the higher-margin retail equity/trading business has not recovered to offset it.

The earnings-surprise record reinforces the caution. Over the last four quarters, XP has produced one beat and three misses: Q1 2026 -5.6%, Q4 2025 -1.2%, Q3 2025 -1.4%, and Q2 2025 +1.9%. The trajectory of surprises is deteriorating, with the largest miss in the most recent quarter. Reported EPS for the trailing period is $1.77 (the data's trailing P/E of 8.45x corresponds to this figure; analyst consensus uses an adjusted EPS base around $10.88 for FY in local-share terms, indicating the figures reflect different share/currency conventions; the relevant point is the multiple is low on any consistent basis).

Financial health is adequate but not a source of differentiated strength. The business is reliably profitable with high ROE, but it is a capital-markets-levered financial whose earnings are highly sensitive to the Selic policy rate, equity-market volumes, and the BRL. We score Financial Health 6.0: solid profitability and capital generation, offset by the inability to verify capital and liquidity ratios from primary structured data and by the macro-levered nature of the earnings base.

## Growth Analysis

The growth picture is the crux of the AVOID. Headline revenue resumed growth in 2H 2025 and into 2026, but the engine driving it is not the franchise's core. Retail flows and net new money remain soft; wholesale banking and operating leverage are carrying the result, and a large share of client-asset growth is market-driven rather than organic.

The forward-looking estimate data confirms negative momentum rather than acceleration:

- **Current quarter:** $2.67, down from $2.69 (30d) and $2.78 (90d), a -4.1% revision over 90 days.
- **Next quarter:** $2.73, down from $2.82 (90d), -3.1%.
- **Current year:** $10.88, down from $11.07 (90d), -1.8%.
- **Next year:** $12.33, essentially flat, with a modest +1.3% over 90 days.

The strongest forward indicator, estimate revisions, is broadly negative across near-term horizons. A Zacks upgrade to Rank #2 on May 14, 2026 cited rising estimates, but the consensus table here shows current-quarter and current-year estimates falling, and the company subsequently missed on May 18. The signal does not corroborate a reacceleration.

The fee-based advisory transition compounds the uncertainty. XP is migrating advisor compensation toward fee-based models and strengthening governance, responding to CVM scrutiny of the commission-based IFA structure. This is the right long-term move and aligns advisor and client incentives, but it disrupts the revenue mix in the near term and creates advisor-retention risk during the transition. A model transition of this kind dampens the visibility of forward growth.

We score Growth Prospects 4.5. The structural tailwind of Brazilians shifting savings out of incumbent banks is intact but maturing, and the realized growth is low-quality and being revised down.

## Valuation Assessment

At $16.20 and a forward P/E of roughly 8.5x, XP screens cheap in absolute terms and against U.S. financial peers. The bull case rests almost entirely on this multiple. The relevant question is whether the discount is a mispricing or an appropriate reflection of risk and deteriorating momentum.

The discount is doing legitimate work. A single-digit multiple on a Brazilian capital-markets-levered financial reflects: (1) macro sensitivity to Selic, BRL, and fiscal policy; (2) falling consensus estimates across near-term horizons; (3) a string of three misses in four quarters; (4) an unresolved advisory-model transition; and (5) reduced-disclosure foreign-issuer governance with a dual-class control structure. A low multiple is not sufficient for a high valuation score; it requires a specific reason the market is wrong, and here the market's discount is consistent with the fundamental facts rather than an overlooked opportunity.

Analyst price targets (low $18.76, mean $24.20, median $24.71, high $28.44) sit well above the current price, implying roughly 16% to 75% upside. On a stock with positive estimate revisions and a clear catalyst, those targets might be treated as lagging floors. That is not the situation here. Estimates are falling, the most recent quarter missed, and the stock has declined steadily from its February high of $23.13 toward its 52-week low of $15.52. When targets sit far above a price that is grinding lower on falling estimates, the targets are more likely stale on the high side than predictive; the gap between $16.20 and a $24 mean target is the market disagreeing with the sell-side, and on falling estimates the market's read is the more credible one.

We score Valuation 6.0. The multiple genuinely is low and provides some downside cushion and optionality if Brazilian rates and markets cooperate, but there is no identifiable catalyst to force a re-rating, and the discount is well-anchored to real risk.

## Competitive Landscape

XP remains the leader in the independent-advisory channel it created, and the structural shift of Brazilian savings away from incumbent-bank products into open-architecture platforms is a real, multi-year tailwind. The competitive trajectory, however, is flattening rather than improving. BTG Pactual is competing aggressively for advisors and high-net-worth clients; incumbent universal banks defend share-of-wallet; and Nubank pressures fees from the banking side. The market XP pioneered is maturing, which intensifies competition for the same advisors and clients.

High Brazilian interest rates are a double-edged competitive factor. They boost fixed-income and float-related revenue and lift client-asset values, but they push clients toward short-duration, low-activity products that compress XP's take rate and dampen the higher-margin equity and trading business. The conservative client mix management cited is a direct consequence.

The advisory-compensation transition is the swing variable. Executed well, it differentiates XP on fiduciary alignment ahead of regulatory tightening; executed poorly, it triggers advisor attrition to rivals offering more favorable economics in the interim. We score Competitive Position 5.0: a leader in a maturing market with stable-to-modestly-eroding share dynamics and a self-imposed transition risk.

## Risk Assessment

The principal risks are specific, currently-active factors:

1. **Soft retail flows.** Growth is carried by wholesale and operating leverage, not retail engagement, and AUC expansion is market-driven. A market downturn reverses the AUC tailwind without a recovered retail base to offset it.
2. **Advisory-model transition.** The shift to fee-based compensation disrupts the near-term revenue mix and risks advisor attrition during the migration. This is the most material evolving disclosure to verify in the FY2025 20-F.
3. **Brazil macro sensitivity.** Earnings are highly levered to the Selic rate, equity-market volumes, inflation, fiscal policy, and the BRL. The "conservative mix" effect directly compresses take rates.
4. **FX translation.** USD-reported results diverge materially from BRL operating performance (the +20% USD vs. +8% BRL Q4 gap), creating optical distortion in both directions.
5. **Falling estimates and miss streak.** Three misses in four quarters with consensus revising down across near-term horizons is a forward-negative combination not fully reflected at a static "cheap" multiple.
6. **Governance.** Cayman domicile, dual-class control concentrated with founders, and reduced foreign-issuer disclosure obligations limit transparency and minority influence.

We score Risk Factors 4.5: multiple specific, active risks (estimate cuts, transition, macro levers) that are not fully neutralized by the valuation discount.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty with a bearish tilt. Near-term ATM IV is 57.3% against 45.5% historical volatility, rich by an 11.8-point spread, sitting in the 61st percentile of the current chain. The put/call skew is elevated at 17.1%, indicating meaningful demand for downside protection. The term structure is flat (near 57.3% ≈ far 57.4%), with the 6-month tenor implying a ±29.8% move, so uncertainty is distributed evenly across horizons rather than concentrated in a single near-term event. Short interest is modest at 4.4% of float (4.2 days to cover), so the bearish read is not a crowded short; it reflects genuine caution rather than positioning that risks a squeeze. The combination of rich IV, elevated put skew, and falling estimates corroborates the fundamental caution rather than challenging it.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case.** XP trades at roughly 8.5x forward earnings with a high ROE in a structurally growing market. 2H 2025 revenue reaccelerated above 10%, Q1 2026 EPS grew to $0.47 from $0.39 a year earlier, and the fee-based advisory transition, once digested, aligns the model with global fiduciary trends and regulatory direction. If Brazilian rates ease in a way that revives retail trading and net new money, the AUC base and take rate both inflect, and a re-rating toward the $24 analyst mean (~+49%) becomes plausible. The bull-case target is approximately $24–28, the upper band of the analyst range, contingent on a genuine retail-flow recovery.

**Bear Case.** Estimates keep falling, the retail engine stays soft, the advisory transition triggers advisor attrition and a revenue-mix air pocket, and a Brazilian macro or FX shock compresses take rates further. The multiple, already low, can compress further on a financial with falling earnings; the 52-week low of $15.52 is the proximate floor, with a break toward $13–14 plausible on a macro deterioration, roughly 14–20% downside from $16.20.

**Base Case.** The stock grinds in a $15–18 range as wholesale and operating leverage hold results roughly flat while retail remains soft and estimates stabilize at lower levels. No catalyst forces a re-rating, and the cheap multiple persists because the discount is warranted. The probability-weighted outcome does not offer asymmetric upside; the downside to the bear case is more immediate and better-supported by current data than the upside to the bull case.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The clearest dated event is the next quarterly print (Q2 2026, likely mid-August 2026), where another miss against falling consensus or further evidence of soft retail flows would pressure the stock. Beyond that, there is no single binary catalyst; the bear thesis is a continuation of falling estimates and weak retail flows rather than a discrete cliff (no debt maturity wall, no regulatory ruling with a fixed date).

**Timing window.** The Q2 print is roughly 10 weeks out. That is a workable window, but the absence of a sharp binary event means the thesis plays out as a grind rather than a gap-down, which weakens the case for an active short versus simply avoiding.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is uncrowded; there is room to add, but it also means informed investors are not broadly positioned short, and the modest short base limits the conviction the market shares the bearish view.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options confirm caution and support the bearish lean but also raise the cost of expressing it via options. There is no indication of extreme borrow cost.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target ~$13–14 (-14% to -20%); upside risk includes a Brazilian rate-cut-driven re-rating toward $20+ (+25% or more) or an acquisition given the cheap multiple. The reward-to-risk is roughly 1:1 to at best 1.3:1, below the 2:1 threshold required for an active short, primarily because the cheap starting multiple caps further downside while macro/rate optionality creates real upside risk.

**Verdict.** NOT SHORTABLE. XP is a profitable, structurally cheap business with deteriorating momentum, not an actively collapsing one. The lack of a binary catalyst, the limited downside from an already-low multiple, and genuine upside risk from Brazilian rate easing make this a name to avoid owning rather than to short.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria.** We would revisit XP constructively on: (1) evidence of a genuine retail-flow recovery — net new money inflecting positive and AUC growth driven by organic inflows rather than market appreciation, visible in two consecutive quarterly disclosures; (2) a turn in consensus estimate revisions to positive across the current-quarter and current-year horizons; or (3) clear evidence the fee-based advisory transition is complete with advisor headcount stable and the revenue mix normalized.

**Timeline.** The headwinds — soft retail engagement, falling estimates, and the advisory transition — are likely to persist through at least the next two quarters (Q2 and Q3 2026, reporting mid-August and mid-November). The Brazilian macro variable (Selic path, BRL) could shorten or extend this window. We would not expect a durable improvement in the growth quality before late 2026 at the earliest.

## Conclusion

XP is a well-run, profitable franchise that screens cheap, and that combination will tempt value-oriented investors. The fundamental record does not support a constructive 12-month call. The recent reacceleration is carried by wholesale mix shift, operating leverage, and market-driven AUC appreciation rather than the core retail engine, which remains soft. Consensus estimates are falling across near-term horizons, the company has missed in three of its last four quarters, and an unresolved fee-based advisory transition adds revenue-mix and retention risk on top of acute Brazilian macro and FX sensitivity. The stock trades below its 50- and 200-day moving averages, down 17% from its February high. The low multiple is a warranted discount, not an overlooked mispricing, and there is no identifiable catalyst to force a re-rating within the horizon. We rate XP **AVOID** and would reassess only on a genuine, organically-driven recovery in retail flows accompanied by a turn in estimate revisions.

**Overall Score: 5.2 / 10 — AVOID**
