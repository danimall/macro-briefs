**Research Date: May 29, 2026**

## Executive Summary

SAIC is one of the largest pure-play technology and engineering services integrators to the U.S. government, deriving 98% of revenue from federal customers (~52% Department of War, ~46% Intelligence Community and civilian agencies). The investment debate centers on whether management can arrest a genuine organic revenue decline before leverage and goodwill become problems; cash flow is solid and adjusted margins are improving. FY26 revenue fell ~3% to ~$7.26B, Q3 FY26 revenue dropped 6% YoY, and on February 11, 2026 management cut FY27 revenue guidance to organic -4% to -2% (from a prior 0% to +3% target), explicitly citing two recent unfavorable recompete losses and procurement delays, "disproportionately within larger and commoditized Enterprise IT programs."

The core problem is structural. SAIC is over-indexed to the most price-competitive, least-differentiated slice of government IT, the segment where multi-award vehicles, small-business set-asides, and the new administration's efficiency drive are compressing the addressable market for large primes. Peers (CACI, Booz Allen) have leaned harder into higher-value technology and intelligence work, and SAIC's 5-year cumulative TSR of 113.75 versus a peer group at 195.65 quantifies the competitive gap. The "be more selective / shrink to higher margin" pivot, the $203M SilverEdge acquisition, and a third reorganization in two years are management's response, but execution is unproven and the prior CEO departed abruptly in October 2025.

At $103.70 the stock trades at ~13.5x earnings that are flattered by non-recurring items: a $47M IRS audit settlement that pushed the effective tax rate to ~7.5%, a $15M patent settlement, and a debt-funded buyback program that retired ~13% of shares over two years while revenue shrank. The optical "cheapness" relative to a 35.9 peer median P/E reflects a market that has correctly identified declining revenue, falling forward estimates, and elevated leverage, not a mispricing. With no identifiable 12-month catalyst for revenue stabilization and several specific downside vectors (recompete attrition, DOGE procurement disruption, goodwill impairment risk, an unresolved DOJ antitrust investigation), the risk/reward skews negative. We rate SAIC AVOID.

## Company Overview

SAIC provides IT modernization, digital and AI engineering, mission systems support, training and simulation, and ground-vehicle support to the U.S. government. The workforce of ~24,000 is heavily cleared (25% military/veteran), and the firm holds ~1,700 active contracts and task orders. Its competitive positioning rests on scale, prime-integrator status, a clearance-heavy workforce, and long-tenured customer relationships, but the company itself acknowledges in its filings that it competes increasingly on price in a commoditizing, competitively-bid market.

The business runs in two reportable segments:

- **Defense & Intelligence** (~77% of revenue, $5.58B FY26): national security solutions to the DoW and Intelligence Community. ~80% cost-reimbursement contracts; lower-margin (~8.4–8.6% adjusted operating margin) but stable.
- **Civilian** (~23%, $1.68B FY26): federal civilian and state/local work in border security, travel/trade, health, and citizen services. T&M-heavy (~67%), with higher and improving margin (13.3–14.5% adjusted operating margin).

The fiscal year ends the Friday nearest January 31; FY2026 ended January 30, 2026.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue is in organic decline, and this is the defining issue.** Revenue moved from $7.70B (FY23) to $7.44B (FY24) to $7.48B (FY25) to ~$7.26B (FY26, -3%). Adjusting for the SilverEdge acquisition, organic FY26 contraction was ~3.3%. Both segments fell in Q3 on "ramp-down in volume on existing contracts and contract completions." Management guided FY27 revenue to $7.0B–$7.2B.

**Margins present a more nuanced picture.** GAAP operating margin compressed from 10.0% (FY24, inflated by a $240M divestiture gain) to 7.5% (FY25) to 7.2% (FY26). On an adjusted basis, operating margin improved each year: 8.9% → 9.4% → 9.7%. This is the "shrink to higher margin" thesis in the data. Net income was $477M (FY24) → $362M (FY25) → $358M (FY26), a reported net margin of ~4.9%.

**Headline earnings quality is poor.** The FY26 effective tax rate of ~7.5% reflects the $47M IRS audit settlement benefit (FY2016–2019) and One Big Beautiful Bill Act R&D expensing; the normalized rate is ~15–18%. The $15M patent settlement also helped. Stripping these out, underlying earnings power is meaningfully below the headline. The trailing P/E of 13.5x is therefore computed against a flattered EPS base, making the stock less cheap than it appears.

**Cash flow is the genuine bright spot.** Operating cash flow grew to $609M (FY26) from $494M (FY25) and $396M (FY24); FCF was $577M (~7.9% FCF margin), with FY27 FCF guided above $600M. One caveat: SAIC uses a $300M MARPA receivables-sale facility that pulls cash forward (adding ~$86M to nine-month FY26 OCF), which flatters reported cash generation.

**The balance sheet is leveraged and goodwill-heavy.** Total debt is ~$2.50B against ~$1.50B of equity (D/E 3.57; total debt ~3.5x adjusted EBITDA). Goodwill of $2.94B plus intangibles of $0.76B equals ~69% of $5.35B total assets; tangible equity is deeply negative. The Altman Z-Score of 2.28 sits in the "grey zone," driven by thin equity and leverage rather than operating distress (Piotroski is 5/5 on available signals). Cash is minimal — $182M at FY26 year-end, $45M at Q3 — typical for an asset-light services firm but leaving little cushion if covenants tighten. During FY26 SAIC termed out maturities with a new $1.1B Term Loan A (2030), a $1.0B revolver (2030), and $500M of 5.875% senior notes due 2033.

**Capital allocation is aggressive and largely debt-funded.** SAIC repurchased ~4.0M shares for ~$422M in FY26, and ~28.5M shares for ~$2.5B cumulatively, cutting share count from ~52M to ~45M. The quarterly dividend is $0.37 (~$70M/yr). Retiring ~13% of shares over two years has supported EPS while revenue declined, the mechanism flattering the per-share optics.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth picture is negative on every quantitative measure that matters here. FY27 revenue is guided to organic -4% to -2%. Estimate revisions are broadly falling: next-quarter EPS estimates were cut 2.4% over 90 days, and next-year estimates fell 1.4% over the trailing 30 days. There is no estimate-revision inflection of the kind that would support a constructive growth call; the trend is the opposite.

Backlog growth coexists with revenue decline, the most important interpretive nuance. Total backlog rose to $22.6B at FY26 year-end (from $21.9B), book-to-bill improved, and FY26 net bookings were $7.8B (vs $6.6B FY25). This argues against a demand collapse, but it does not refute the recompete-loss problem: backlog can grow on new awards while large legacy programs roll off and the company loses share in commoditized Enterprise IT. Backlog conversion timing and recompete dynamics, not headline demand, are the binding constraint.

There are pockets of strength. Civilian adjusted operating margin expanded even as revenue dipped, demonstrating that the higher-value-mix strategy works where SAIC has differentiation. The July 2025 reconciliation package added roughly $150B in non-border defense and ~$175B in border/enforcement spending through GFY2029, a potential tailwind for SAIC's DoW and border-security franchises if it can win the work. The SilverEdge acquisition (IP/product-oriented intelligence solutions) is aimed at shifting mix toward differentiated, higher-margin offerings, but it contributed only $27M of FY26 revenue and is immaterial near-term.

The growth thesis depends entirely on management replacing lost commoditized revenue with higher-value work faster than the legacy base erodes. The mechanism is plausible (fewer, higher-margin programs plus SilverEdge), but the company is guiding to a wider revenue decline next year, not stabilization. That sequencing risk is unresolved.

## Valuation Assessment

SAIC trades at ~13.5x trailing earnings, well below the Diversified Support Services peer median P/E of 35.9. That gap is not evidence of mispricing. The peer median is skewed by UNF (35.9), AMTM (38.6), and a negative-earning OPLN; the more relevant government-services comparable is KBR at 9.7x P/E and a -37% discount to its 52-week high, which trades cheaper than SAIC. Within the cohort of declining-revenue federal-services names, SAIC is not an outlier on the cheap side.

The "cheapness" reflects three specific, market-recognized problems. First, the earnings base is flattered by the IRS settlement, the patent win, and buybacks; normalized EPS would lift the effective multiple. Second, forward estimates are falling, so the P/E on FY27 numbers is higher than the trailing figure suggests as the E declines. Third, leverage (D/E 3.57) and goodwill at 69% of assets raise the equity risk premium the market reasonably demands. A low multiple on shrinking, lower-quality earnings with leverage and goodwill-impairment risk is the profile of a value trap, not a setup for re-rating.

The analyst price target range is $85 (low) to $130 (high), with a $108.3 mean and $111.5 median. The current price of $103.70 sits below mean and median, but consensus is genuinely split — 2 buys, 8 holds, 1 sell — and the median has not been revised upward despite the recent price recovery. We do not treat the mean target as a floor here; this is a stock at the bottom end of its earnings quality, not an inflection candidate where targets lag rising estimates. Our base case sees fair value around $95–$100 on normalized earnings of ~$8.50–$9.00 at a ~11x multiple appropriate for a declining-revenue, leveraged integrator. The valuation score reflects neither a clear mispricing nor a structural collapse; the multiple is roughly fair for the deteriorating fundamentals.

## Competitive Landscape

The government-IT services industry is shifting toward competitively-bid multi-award vehicles (IDIQ, GSA Schedules, OTAs) and small-business set-asides, intensifying price competition and shrinking the addressable market for large primes on commoditized work. The new administration's efficiency drive (DOGE), the FAR overhaul, and the DoW Acquisition Transformation Strategy add procurement delays in the near term while creating potential upside in digital modernization, cyber, and border surveillance.

SAIC's competitive trajectory is deteriorating, which is why this dimension scores below midpoint. It is over-indexed to "larger and commoditized Enterprise IT" recompetes — the most price-competitive, least-differentiated segment — and it is now losing share there, as confirmed by the two recompete losses that drove the FY27 cut. Competitors CACI and Booz Allen have moved more decisively into higher-value technology, IP, and intelligence work. The 5-year TSR underperformance (114 vs 196 for the peer group) reflects a persistent competitive gap, not a one-year aberration. The Civilian segment's margin expansion shows SAIC can compete where it has differentiation; the question is whether that footprint is large enough to offset Enterprise IT attrition. On current evidence it is not.

## Risk Assessment

The risk profile carries several specific, identifiable downside vectors that are not fully priced:

1. **Recompete attrition.** The thesis-defining risk. Continued losses in commoditized Enterprise IT would extend the revenue decline beyond FY27 and could trigger the goodwill-impairment scenario described in SAIC's own policy.

2. **DOGE / federal efficiency disruption.** The Q3 10-Q names the Department of Government Efficiency as "driving changes in the structure and priorities of Federal agencies." Procurement delays already cost ~$26M in FY26 from the 43-day Oct–Nov 2025 shutdown.

3. **Appropriations/shutdown overhang.** DHS funding was delayed/shut down for GFY2026, directly relevant to SAIC's Civilian/border business, with a CR running to January 30, 2026 for remaining agencies.

4. **Leverage + goodwill impairment.** A sustained revenue decline could pressure reporting-unit valuations and risk a covenant breach that restricts dividends and buybacks.

5. **Regulatory threat to buybacks.** A recent executive order would limit buybacks, dividends, or distributions for defense contractors that underperform on critical defense supply contracts, directly relevant given the leveraged buyback program that is currently masking organic decline.

6. **DOJ Antitrust criminal investigation.** Two federal grand jury subpoenas (April 2022, October 2023) from the DOJ Antitrust Division remain unresolved, with no estimable liability — an unquantified tail risk absent from standardized screens.

7. **Governance instability.** Three reorganizations in two years and an abrupt "immediate departure" of the prior CEO in October 2025 ($16M transition cost) signal a company still searching for its operating model.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing imminent stress. Near-term ATM implied volatility is 50.0% against 39.6% trailing realized vol — rich by 10.4 points and at the 75th percentile of the current chain. The term structure is in backwardation (50.0% near-term vs 41.9% at six months), which the market typically uses to flag a near-dated catalyst rather than chronic risk. Put/call skew is balanced (2.2%) and put/call OI is light at 0.31, so this is not a one-sided bearish bet, but the implied ±9.5% move into the June expiry indicates the market expects a sharp event-driven repricing. Short interest at 7.5% of float (6.2 days to cover) is moderate — uncrowded enough that the bearish view is not yet consensus, but not extreme. The elevated near-term IV argues against buying premium here; for a fundamentally cautious view, the options market corroborates rather than contradicts the thesis.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case

The "shrink to higher margin" pivot succeeds: SAIC deprioritizes low-margin Enterprise IT, SilverEdge and the higher-value pipeline replace lost revenue, adjusted EBITDA margin reaches the guided 9.9–10.1%, and FCF exceeds $600M. The reconciliation-package defense and border spending flows to SAIC's DoW and Civilian franchises. Backlog converts, revenue stabilizes by FY28, and a debt-funded buyback continues to compound EPS on a shrinking share count. On normalized ~$9.50 EPS and a re-rating to ~13–14x as revenue stabilizes, the stock reaches the high-$120s to $130 (matching the Street high target).

### Bear Case

Recompete losses continue, organic decline persists beyond FY27's -2% to -4%, and the higher-value pipeline fails to scale fast enough. Revenue dropping toward $6.8B pressures goodwill, risking an impairment and a covenant test that forces a buyback halt, removing the EPS support that masks deterioration. The executive order on contractor buybacks bites, or the DOJ antitrust matter resolves unfavorably. The non-recurring tax and patent benefits do not repeat, so reported EPS steps down even without further operating deterioration. The stock retests the $81 52-week low, roughly -22% from current levels, and the Street low of $85 becomes the anchor.

The balance of evidence favors the bear case in the near term. The company is guiding to a wider decline, estimates are falling, the earnings base is flattered, and the leverage/goodwill structure amplifies the downside of continued revenue erosion.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Re-entry criteria.** We would revisit a constructive view on specific, measurable developments: (a) two consecutive quarters of organic revenue stabilization (YoY change of -1% or better) confirming the higher-value-mix pivot is replacing lost Enterprise IT; (b) FY27 revenue guidance revised back toward flat-to-positive, accompanied by upward EPS estimate revisions rather than the current downtrend; (c) a clean resolution of the DOJ antitrust investigation with no material liability; or (d) valuation compression to ~9–10x normalized EPS (roughly $80 or below) that prices in the structural challenges with asymmetric recovery upside.

**Timeline.** The structural headwinds (commoditization of Enterprise IT, DOGE-driven procurement disruption, and recompete pressure) are likely to persist through at least FY27 (ending January 2027) and possibly into FY28. The next natural reassessment points are the FY27 Q1 earnings release and any updated guidance, alongside resolution of the DHS appropriations overhang. Until then the burden of proof sits with management to demonstrate the pivot is working, and the data is not yet there.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** The most identifiable near-term catalyst is the next earnings release and any FY27 guidance refinement, where continued recompete attrition or further procurement-delay disclosures could pressure the stock. The options market's backwardation and ±9.5% implied move into the June expiry suggest the market sees a near-dated event. There is no single dated, high-conviction trigger (no debt maturity cliff, with maturities termed out to 2030/2033; no binary regulatory ruling on a fixed date). The DOJ antitrust matter is unquantified and unscheduled.

**Timing window.** A catalyst tied to the next quarterly report is roughly one to three months out. The structural decline thesis is longer-dated (12+ months), which raises carrying cost relative to conviction.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 7.5% of float with 6.2 days to cover — moderate. Not crowded enough to create acute squeeze risk, but also indicating the bearish view is not yet consensus.

**Borrow and options signal.** Near-term IV at 50.0% (75th percentile, rich vs 39.6% HV) makes long-put expressions expensive, and balanced skew (2.2%) plus light put/call OI (0.31) show the options market is not leaning hard bearish. This raises the cost of expressing the short via options and signals positioning is not yet one-sided.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear-case target is ~$81–$85 (the 52-week low and Street low), roughly -18% to -22% from $103.70. Upside risk includes a buyback-driven squeeze, a positive recompete award, or a margin/FCF beat that revives the "higher-quality earnings" narrative. With the recent recovery from $81 to $103.70 already pricing some optimism, the reward:risk on a fresh short is below 2:1, and the absence of a single dated catalyst weakens the setup.

**Verdict: NOT SHORTABLE.** The thesis is a structurally challenged business in a deteriorating competitive position, but there is no specific dated catalyst with asymmetric payoff, borrow/options costs are elevated, and the stock has already recovered meaningfully off its lows. This is a stock to avoid owning, not to actively short.

## Conclusion

SAIC is financially functional but structurally challenged. Cash generation is strong, adjusted margins are improving, and the balance sheet has been termed out, but these positives are offset by organic revenue decline, fresh recompete losses in commoditized Enterprise IT, falling forward estimates, high leverage, a goodwill-heavy balance sheet, governance instability, and several unpriced tail risks including a DOJ antitrust investigation. Headline earnings are flattered by non-recurring tax and legal items and by debt-funded buybacks that mask the underlying deterioration.

The score trajectory has been flat (5.3 → 5.3), with Financial Health improving on cash flow strength but Competitive Position and Growth Prospects declining, and the latest data confirms the negative trend rather than reversing it. The FY27 guidance cut to organic decline is new, concrete fundamental deterioration, not merely price weakness, which justifies stepping down from the prior HOLD posture. The valuation discount to peers reflects these problems, not a mispricing the market has missed. With no identifiable 12-month catalyst for revenue stabilization and a balance of evidence favoring continued erosion, we rate SAIC **AVOID** and would revisit only on demonstrated organic stabilization, upward estimate revisions, resolution of the antitrust overhang, or a deeper valuation reset.
