## Executive Summary

TSMC reported Q1 2026 results on April 16 that confirmed the company is operating at its highest profitability in history: gross margins of 66.2% (up 740bps YoY), revenue of $35.9B (+35% YoY), and net income of $18.1B (+58% YoY). This was the eighth consecutive quarter of earnings beats. Full-year 2026 guidance calls for 30%+ revenue growth in USD terms, with Q2 guided at $39-40.2B, implying continued sequential acceleration. Advanced nodes (7nm and below) represent 74-75% of wafer revenue, with HPC (which includes AI accelerators and other high-performance computing products) now at 61% of total revenue.

At approximately 25-27x forward earnings on FY2026 consensus of ~$14.31/ADR, TSMC trades at a meaningful discount to its largest customers — Nvidia (44x), AMD (58x), Broadcom (~30x) — despite having superior margins, better earnings visibility, and lower competitive risk. The stock has appreciated over 140% in the past year, but this appreciation has been driven by fundamental improvement, not multiple expansion: earnings per ADR have roughly doubled over that period.

The primary risks remain geopolitical (Taiwan Strait concentration), capex execution ($52-56B annually), and cyclicality in a market pricing perfection. The options market is pricing elevated near-term uncertainty (54.1% IV vs. 34.7% HV), which creates a headwind for timing but doesn't invalidate the fundamental case.

## Company Overview

TSMC is the world's largest dedicated semiconductor foundry, fabricating chips designed by other companies without competing against them. This pure-play model has built unmatched trust across 534 customers. The company holds a dominant global foundry market share (estimated at over 70% by third-party research firms) and dominates advanced nodes where no competitor (Samsung, Intel Foundry) operates at comparable yield or volume.

Revenue breakdown by platform: HPC (61%), Smartphones (~25%), IoT (~5%), Automotive (~5%), Digital Consumer Electronics (~4%). By geography, North America represents approximately 75% of revenue, reflecting dependence on major fabless chip designers and hyperscalers.

The company is in the midst of a $165B U.S. expansion — comprising the prior $65B plan plus an additional $100B investment that adds three new fabs, two advanced packaging facilities, and an R&D center in Arizona. Additional fabs are planned in Japan, and TSMC continues to expand 2nm capacity in Taiwan through Fab 20 (Hsinchu) and Fab 22 (Southern Taiwan Science Park), with ongoing construction of additional facilities including Fab 21.

## Growth Analysis

Six independent semiconductor demand confirmations support the AI infrastructure thesis: Korea exports +48%, SK Hynix +12%, Samsung $1T market cap, Qualcomm data center beat, GFS beat, and AMD +20% data center revenue. These represent broad-based demand validation across memory, logic, and analog.

TSMC-specific growth drivers:

1. **AI accelerator demand:** TSMC's top two customers (which the company does not identify by name in SEC filings, but which represented 19% and 17% of FY2025 revenue respectively, or 36% combined) are driving significant advanced-node demand. Multiple hyperscalers designing custom AI chips (Amazon Trainium, Google TPU, Microsoft Maia) are all manufactured by TSMC, increasing total AI demand across multiple customers.

2. **Advanced packaging (CoWoS):** The binding constraint on AI chip production is increasingly packaging, not wafer fab. TSMC's CoWoS capacity is heavily reserved by leading AI chip customers. U.S. advanced packaging facilities are planned as part of the $165B expansion, signaling this as a growth segment.

3. **2nm ramp:** TSMC is building out 2nm capacity across multiple fabs (including Fab 20 and Fab 22), representing a major concentration of next-gen manufacturing capacity. Management has indicated strong demand for leading-edge process technologies.

4. **Geographic expansion:** $165B U.S. commitment accelerating on "extremely robust" AI demand. While margin-dilutive initially (higher labor/compliance costs), it reduces geopolitical discount over time.

Consensus estimates for FY2026 EPS are approximately $14.31/ADR, with FY2027 at approximately $18.00. Given the 8-quarter beat streak, current consensus likely remains conservative. Management's 30%+ revenue growth guidance for 2026 anchors the trajectory.

## Valuation Assessment

Based on the research brief, actual trailing P/E is approximately 29-32x on TTM earnings, with forward P/E of 25-27x on FY2026 consensus.

At 25-27x forward, TSMC trades at:
- Discount to Nvidia (44x), AMD (58x), Broadcom (~30x) — companies that depend on TSMC for their products
- Premium to broad semiconductor sector (mid-teens P/E) — justified by dominant market share, 66% gross margins, and 30%+ growth
- PEG of ~0.8-0.9x on forward estimates (forward P/E 26x / growth rate 30%) — indicating growth is not fully priced

Analyst consensus target: mean approximately $457, range $351-$600. The stock is trading near all-time highs, which — given continued estimate revision momentum — suggests the market is still digesting the magnitude of the earnings improvement.

The stock's 140%+ appreciation over 12 months tracks closely with earnings growth. Multiple expansion has been modest. The market is paying roughly the same multiple for substantially higher earnings power.

**Taiwan regulatory catalyst:** Taiwan's financial regulator raised the single-stock ownership cap to 25% for eligible funds, with TSMC as the only qualifying stock. This could drive up to ~$28B in forced passive buying — a material structural demand catalyst over coming quarters.

## Competitive Landscape

TSMC's competitive position has strengthened over the past year. At leading-edge nodes (3nm and below), there is no commercially viable alternative:

- **Samsung Foundry:** Persistent yield problems at 3nm. Significantly behind on volume production. Customer defections to TSMC continue.
- **Intel Foundry Services:** Still years behind at leading edge. Intel itself is a TSMC customer for some products. The $8B CHIPS Act subsidy helps but doesn't close the technology gap before 2028-2029.
- **SMIC (China):** Limited to 7nm-equivalent without access to EUV lithography (export controlled). Cannot compete at advanced nodes.

The competitive moat is widening because: (1) each node transition compounds prior learning advantages, (2) advanced packaging expertise (CoWoS) adds another dimension competitors must master, (3) the trust model (non-compete) keeps customers locked in, and (4) massive capex ($50B+/year) raises barriers to scale.

Customer switching costs are extraordinarily high. Qualifying a new foundry for a leading-edge design takes 12-18 months minimum and billions in NRE. With strong demand, customers have every incentive to commit multi-year volumes to maintain allocation.

## Risk Assessment

**Geopolitical/Taiwan Strait (material but not imminent):** TSMC's core manufacturing remains concentrated in Taiwan. Any military conflict involving China and Taiwan could disrupt global chip supply chains catastrophically. However: (1) the probability of kinetic action in the next 12 months remains low (5-10%), (2) the Arizona expansion is the market's demanded hedge, (3) TSMC's centrality to global commerce creates mutual deterrence (China itself depends on TSMC for advanced chips). The market prices this risk as a persistent valuation discount vs. comparable US companies.

**Capex execution risk:** $52-56B annual spending is unprecedented. Arizona fabs cost significantly more than Taiwan fabs. Delays or cost overruns could compress FCF temporarily. However, the strong net cash position provides meaningful buffer.

**Cyclicality:** AI spending by hyperscalers could decelerate. A macro recession would pressure non-AI segments. Mitigant: multi-year capacity commitments with prepayments provide revenue visibility unusual for cyclical companies.

**Customer concentration:** TSMC's top two customers represented 36% of FY2025 revenue (19% and 17% respectively). While the single-largest customer's share declined from 25% in 2023 to 19% in 2025, the top 10 customers' share increased from 70% to 78% over the same period. This suggests broad top-customer concentration is rising even as single-customer dependence moderates. Mitigant: top customers face zero credible alternatives at leading-edge nodes.

**Specialty materials supply chain:** TSMC's 2025 20-F discusses risks related to raw materials including silicon wafers, gases, chemicals, and photoresist, as well as risks from military conflicts and supply-chain disruption. Prolonged disruption of specialty gases (helium, hydrogen) essential for fab operations could create operational constraints.

## Conclusion

TSMC remains a **BUY**. The company is in its strongest competitive and financial position in history — 66% gross margins, 35% revenue growth, strong net cash position, robust demand for leading-edge capacity, and no credible foundry alternative at leading edge. At 25-27x forward earnings, the valuation reflects a persistent geopolitical discount that appropriately compensates for Taiwan concentration risk while still offering upside through estimate revisions and the structural AI demand cycle validated by 6 independent data points.

The score trajectory (7.5 → 8.1 → 7.9 → 7.9) has stabilized at a level reflecting the tension between exceptional fundamentals and unresolvable geopolitical risk. No new fundamental deterioration exists — margins are expanding and estimates are rising. The stock's proximity to all-time highs after significant annual appreciation is driven entirely by earnings growth, not speculative multiple expansion. Position sizing should reflect the binary geopolitical tail risk: 2-3% portfolio allocation captures upside while limiting exposure to unlikely but catastrophic scenarios.
