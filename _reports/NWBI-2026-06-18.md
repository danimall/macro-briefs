**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Northwest Bancshares is a $16.8 billion-asset community/regional bank that has spent the past year absorbing the Penns Woods acquisition and repositioning itself from a legacy Pennsylvania thrift into a growth-oriented commercial bank headquartered in Columbus, Ohio. The headline GAAP results understate the franchise: full-year 2025 EPS of $0.92 carried $63.5 million of one-time integration costs ($42.8M merger expense plus a $20.7M CECL Day-1 provision), against an adjusted EPS of roughly $1.26 and an adjusted ROA of 1.12–1.17%. Q1 2026 confirmed the clean run-rate with record net income of $51 million ($0.34 GAAP, $0.35 non-GAAP), beating consensus by roughly 17%. Net interest margin expanded 43 basis points to 3.69%, above the low-3% range typical of regional peers.

The investment case is primarily an income story. The stock yields about 5.5% on an $0.80 annual dividend with a 125-quarter unbroken payment streak, and trades around $14.35 against tangible book value of $9.63 (~1.5x TBV, ~11x adjusted EPS / ~10.4x forward consensus EPS of $1.38). The bullish tell is a cluster of C-suite and director open-market purchases in April–May 2026 in the $13.90–$13.97 range, including the Chief Risk Officer buying $70K of stock at the height of the CRE-credit scare. Estimate revisions are modestly positive (current-year +4.9% over 90 days), and credit metrics that spiked in Q3 2025 reversed in Q4.

What holds us at HOLD rather than BUY is the combination of a GAAP dividend payout near 87%, above-average commercial real estate concentration (342% of bank capital, with the CRE book 46% concentrated in New York), and the fact that a meaningful slice of the standout NIM comes from purchase-accounting accretion that decays over time. The stock has already run ~19% off its 52-week low and sits at the analyst mean target. We see a durable yield-plus-modest-appreciation profile, not asymmetric upside.

## Company Overview

Northwest Bancshares is the holding company for Northwest Bank, founded in 1896 and now headquartered in Columbus, Ohio after relocating from its historic Pennsylvania base. At year-end 2025 it operated 161 banking offices across Pennsylvania (66.5% of deposits), New York (21.5%), Indiana (7.1%), and Ohio (4.9%), with roughly 2,169 full-time employees.

The business is a spread lender organized into two reporting lines. Personal Banking represents 55% of the $13.0 billion loan book ($7.2B): residential mortgage ($3.1B), home equity ($1.5B), vehicle loans ($2.4B), and consumer. Commercial Banking is the other 45% ($5.8B): commercial real estate ($3.3B) and C&I/commercial ($2.5B). Fee income comes from service charges, trust and wealth management (~$32M/year), SBA lending, and mortgage banking. Total assets reached $16.8 billion at year-end 2025, up from $14.4 billion, driven almost entirely by the $2.2 billion Penns Woods deal that closed July 25, 2025.

The strategic identity shift is deliberate: redeploy a granular, low-cost, sticky retail deposit base (average account ~$19,259, only ~14% truly uninsured) out of slow-growth legacy PA/NY markets and into faster-growing Columbus consumer banking and nationwide C&I lending. The deposit franchise is the genuine competitive advantage; the execution and credit risk of the pivot is the cost.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings.** FY2025 net income was $126.0 million ($0.92 diluted EPS), up 26% from $100.3 million ($0.79). The GAAP figure is depressed by the Penns Woods integration costs; adjusted EPS was $1.26 versus $1.04. The quarterly cadence tracks the integration: Q3 2025 net income was just $3 million ($0.02) on merger costs plus the Day-1 provision, Q4 2025 snapped back to $46 million ($0.31 GAAP, $0.33 adjusted), and Q1 2026 hit a record $51 million driven by C&I loan growth.

**Net interest income and margin.** NII reached $525.4 million, up 21%. NIM expanded 43 bps to 3.69% (FTE), with Q4 exit NIM at 3.69% versus 3.42% a year earlier. The drivers are favorable: a loan-mix shift toward higher-yielding commercial loans, accretion of acquired-loan fair-value marks, and declining deposit costs (cost of interest-bearing deposits fell to 1.97% in Q4 from 2.27%). The caveat is that part of the expansion is mechanical: purchase-accounting accretion contributed $4.6 million in Q4 alone and will decay over time. The underlying loan-mix shift is the more durable component.

**Fee income and efficiency.** Noninterest income of $129.3 million (+49%) is largely a clean-comp effect; 2024 was burdened by a $39.4 million securities-restructuring loss. Core fee lines grew modestly (service charges +3%, trust +7%). The efficiency ratio improved to 66.6% GAAP / 59.3% adjusted from 70.5%/64.1%, evidence of operating leverage as the deal integrates.

**Capital and balance sheet.** The bank is comfortably well-capitalized: CET1 12.32%, total risk-based capital 15.36%, leverage 9.29%. TCE/TA is 8.64% and tangible book value is $9.63/share. Net unrealized securities losses total $202M across the $2.4B book ($134M AFS, $77M HTM unrecognized); AOCI improved to ($70.7M) from ($110.9M) as rates eased, and the bank opted out of including AOCI in regulatory capital. Funding is conservative: $13.9 billion of deposits with only ~$193 million brokered (1%), backed by $3.4 billion FHLB capacity, $1.5 billion Fed, and $369 million correspondent lines.

**Capital allocation.** The $0.80 annual dividend produces a ~5.5% yield. The tension is the payout ratio: 87% GAAP in 2025 (101% in 2024), versus ~63% on adjusted EPS. Management prioritizes the dividend (125 consecutive quarterly payments) and disclosed a new $50 million buyback authorization in 2026. The dividend's safety depends on the adjusted-earnings run-rate holding; provision expense jumped 127% to $55.6 million in 2025, which illustrates how quickly credit costs can compress the GAAP coverage.

## Growth Analysis

Growth here is moderate and franchise-mix-driven rather than secular. Management guided to low-to-mid-single-digit loan growth for 2026, led by C&I (Q1 2026 average C&I growth ~$191M). The earnings trajectory is more compelling than the balance-sheet growth: the clean run-rate of ~$1.26–$1.40 adjusted EPS represents a step-change from the ~$0.79–$1.04 of prior years, achieved through NIM expansion and operating leverage rather than asset growth.

Estimate revisions are positive but modest: current-year consensus moved to $1.38 from $1.31 (+4.9% over 90 days), current quarter +5.0%, with next-year at $1.44 (+1.6%). This is meaningful momentum but well below the +30% velocity that signals a genuine earnings inflection; the inflection already happened with the Penns Woods integration and NIM repricing, and consensus is now catching up to a stabilized run-rate. The earnings surprise record (3 beats, 1 miss over four quarters; Q1 2026 +14.7%) supports confidence in the current consensus.

The durable growth lever is the deposit franchise being redeployed into Columbus and C&I. That is real but takes years to compound, and a portion of the reported NIM tailwind reverses as accretion decays. We treat growth prospects as solid-but-unspectacular for a regional bank.

## Valuation Assessment

At $14.35, NWBI trades at ~10.4x current-year consensus EPS ($1.38), ~11x adjusted trailing EPS ($1.26), and ~1.5x tangible book ($9.63). Against the regional-bank peer set (median P/E 14.4x, median yield 2.9%), NWBI screens cheaper on earnings and pays a materially higher yield (~5.5% vs. 2.9% median). On a pure P/E basis it sits below FCF (12.9x), STEL (18.7x), NBHC (16.2x), and HTH (14.4x), above only OFG (9.8x).

The discount is partially justified rather than a clean mispricing. NWBI's above-peer NIM argues for a premium, but its CRE concentration, the GAAP payout, and the purchase-accounting accretion flattering the headline NIM all argue for the discount. The low multiple reflects the market pricing genuine concentration risk, which more often keeps a stock cheap than triggers a re-rating.

Analyst targets bracket the stock tightly: low $14.00, mean/median $15.00, high $16.00. The stock is already at the analyst mean target after a ~19% run off the lows. Applying a peer-aligned ~11x to the $1.38 current-year estimate yields ~$15.20; a more generous 12x on the $1.44 next-year estimate yields ~$17.30 as a bull case. The asymmetry is modest: roughly 5–6% to base case plus the ~5.5% yield, against meaningful downside if CRE credit re-deteriorates. We score valuation as balanced: fair, not compelling.

## Competitive Landscape

NWBI's structural advantage is its deposit base: granular, low-cost, and low-flight-risk, a genuine asset in the post-SVB funding environment. That franchise let the bank expand NIM even as the industry competed for deposits, and it funds the commercial pivot cheaply.

The competitive trajectory is mixed. The loan-mix shift toward C&I and the Columbus expansion improve the earning-asset yield and broaden the growth runway beyond saturated PA/NY markets. Against that, the legacy footprint is concentrated in slow-growth regions, and the bank faces structural pressure from larger regionals, tax-advantaged credit unions, and fintech/stablecoin deposit disintermediation under the GENIUS Act of 2025, newly flagged in the FY2025 10-K. The acquired Penns Woods loans were not underwritten to NWBI standards, which created the classified-loan bulge and is the clearest near-term competitive cost of the growth strategy. On balance the position is stable, with the deposit moat durable and the trajectory dependent on clean execution of the commercial pivot.

## Risk Assessment

**Commercial real estate concentration — the primary issue.** CRE plus residential RE concentration equals 342% of total bank capital, above the 300% regulatory threshold that triggers heightened scrutiny. The CRE book is 46% concentrated in New York and 25% in PA. Nonperforming loans rose 75% to $108M in 2025; CRE 90+ day delinquencies spiked from $7.7M (2024) to a peak of $56.5M in Q3 2025 before improving to $32.7M in Q4. Classified loans were $453M (3.49% of loans) at year-end. Much of the spike was re-risk-rating of acquired Penns Woods loans (substandard acquired loans $93.2M), and the Q4 trend reversed favorably, but a 100%-downside-weighted CECL scenario would require ~$89M of additional reserves.

**Integration risk.** Penns Woods ($2.2B assets) was paid largely in stock (~18.5M new shares), diluting the count, and its loans drove the classified-loan bulge. Synergy realization is ongoing.

**Dividend sustainability.** GAAP payout of 87–101% leaves little margin if credit costs rise. The adjusted payout (~63%) is comfortable, but the high-yield thesis depends on the adjusted run-rate holding.

**NIM durability.** A portion of the 3.69% NIM is purchase-accounting accretion that decays; the reported figure flatters the near-term picture.

**Geographic and municipal exposure.** Earnings are tied to slow-growth PA/NY conditions, with $700M of potentially volatile municipal deposits plus $239M municipal loans and $90M municipal bonds.

These risks sit in a higher-for-longer rate environment that keeps regional-bank credit and deposit costs under scrutiny. The CRE concentration is the live concern that could move the stock against current consensus.

### Options Market Signal

Options data shows an extreme reported jump in ATM implied volatility (from 84.6% to 558.4%) alongside a put/call open-interest ratio collapsing toward zero. The IV reading is almost certainly a thin-options artifact in a small-cap name with minimal options liquidity, and we treat it as non-informative. The collapse in put open interest, if real, is mildly constructive (no accumulating downside hedging), but the data quality does not support a firm conclusion. Short interest at 4.9% of float with 5.3 days to cover is low and uncrowded, consistent with no concentrated bearish positioning.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$16–17)
The clean run-rate is real: adjusted EPS of $1.26 trending toward the $1.38–$1.44 consensus, ROTCE of ~13%, NIM holding above peers as the loan mix shifts to commercial. CRE credit stabilizes further (Q4 already reversed the Q3 spike), the GAAP payout normalizes toward the ~63% adjusted level as integration costs roll off, and the $50M buyback supports the share count. The C-suite buying cluster, particularly the Chief Risk Officer's $70K purchase amid the credit scare, proves prescient. The stock re-rates toward the peer ~14x P/E on $1.44, plus the ~5.5% yield, for a total return in the high-teens.

### Bear Case (~$11–12)
NY/office CRE credit re-deteriorates beyond the acquired-loan re-grading, forcing the ~$89M additional reserve build the downside CECL scenario implies. Provision expense compresses GAAP earnings below the dividend, pressuring the 125-quarter streak and the high-yield thesis that supports the stock. Purchase-accounting accretion decays faster than mix-shift offsets, compressing reported NIM. The stock de-rates toward tangible book ($9.63) or ~1.2x TBV (~$11.50), a ~20% decline, with the dividend yield doing little to cushion a credit-driven re-rating.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a 12-month income hold.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- CRE classified loans and nonaccruals decline for two consecutive quarters from the Q4 2025 levels, confirming the credit scare was acquired-loan re-grading rather than fundamental deterioration.
- GAAP dividend payout falls toward 70% on a clean (ex-integration) basis, removing the dividend-coverage overhang.
- A pullback toward $12.50–$13.00 (~1.3x TBV) that restores yield toward ~6.2% and widens the discount to peers while estimates hold.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- CRE nonaccruals re-accelerate above the Q3 2025 peak (~$82.6M) or classified loans exceed 4% of total loans.
- A dividend cut or a provision build that pushes GAAP EPS below the $0.80 dividend for two quarters.
- NIM compresses below ~3.3% as accretion decays without mix-shift offset.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (Q2 2026, late July) for credit-trend confirmation, and the year-end 2025 CECL/CRE disclosures already in hand as the baseline.

## Conclusion

Northwest Bancshares offers a defensible ~5.5% dividend yield backed by a clean adjusted-earnings run-rate, an above-peer NIM, and a granular low-cost deposit franchise, at ~10.4x forward earnings and ~1.5x tangible book. The C-suite open-market buying cluster, including the Chief Risk Officer, is a genuine confidence signal that management views the CRE credit picture as stabilizing. Against that, the stock has already run off its lows and sits at the analyst mean target; the GAAP payout, above-average NY/office CRE concentration, and NIM partly flattered by decaying accretion cap the appreciation case.

We rate NWBI a **HOLD** on an income basis. The yield-plus-modest-appreciation profile is attractive for income-oriented holders, but the risk/reward at the current price is roughly balanced rather than asymmetric, and the credit concentration tends to keep regional-bank multiples compressed rather than re-rating them. We would become more constructive on confirmation of two more quarters of improving CRE credit or on a pullback that meaningfully widens the yield and discount.
