**Research Date: July 22, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Aon is one of the three dominant global insurance brokers, a capital-light professional-services franchise generating $17.2B in 2025 revenue at a 32.4% adjusted operating margin, with excellent free-cash-flow conversion ($3.2B FCF on ~$263M of capex). The investment case has never rested on solvency or business quality, both of which are strong. The debate is narrower and specific: whether organic revenue growth, which decelerated from a steady 6% in 2023-2025 to 5% in Q1 2026 (with Wealth Solutions at just +1% and Talent soft), is a temporary air pocket or the start of a structural slowdown that would compress a multiple the market has historically awarded for reliable mid-single-digit compounding.

The margin story is currently outrunning the top-line story. Human Capital adjusted margin expanded roughly 270bps in 2025, and consolidated adjusted operating margin reached 39.1% in Q1 2026. Credit is improving in parallel: long-term debt fell from $16.3B at YE2024 to $13.5B by Q1 2026 as the NFP term loan was repaid, and Moody's upgraded its outlook to Positive in Q4 2025. Management has been actively pruning the portfolio, selling NFP's wealth-management arm for $2.3B (a $1.2B pre-tax gain) within 18 months of the ~$13B NFP purchase, using proceeds to deleverage and buy back stock. This is disciplined capital allocation, though it carries the mechanical consequence that Q1 2026 revenue faces a ~3% divestiture headwind.

The trajectory of our own view has been declining (7.3 in February, 6.8 in May, 5.8 in June), driven by growth deceleration and elevated governance-churn signals. New data stabilizes rather than reverses that trend: estimate revisions are flat rather than falling, four straight EPS beats confirm consensus is well-calibrated, and the stock has recovered from ~$310 in mid-May to ~$360. At roughly 19-20x forward earnings with organic growth still the open question, the risk/reward is balanced. We rate AON a HOLD (growth_hold): the compounding thesis remains intact but is not currently mispriced, and the near-term catalyst (evidence that organic growth reaccelerates or holds through the year) has not yet arrived.

## Company Overview

Aon operates across 120+ countries with roughly 60,000 employees, incorporated in Ireland. Following a reorganization, it reports two segments:

- **Risk Capital (~66% of revenue, $11.3B in 2025):** Commercial Risk Solutions (retail/specialty brokerage, ~$8.5B) and Reinsurance Solutions (treaty/facultative reinsurance, capital markets/ILS, ~$2.8B). This is the higher-margin engine at 34.3% adjusted operating margin.
- **Human Capital (~34% of revenue, $5.9B in 2025):** Health Solutions (~$3.8B) and Wealth Solutions (retirement consulting, pension admin, delegated investments, ~$2.1B). Margin is improving sharply, up to 32.2% adjusted from prior levels.

The moat rests on scale-driven proprietary data and analytics (Aon Business Services, Aon Client Treaty, ReMetrica, PathWise), global reach, and high recurring/retention revenue. Client diversification is genuine: the largest single client is ~1% of revenue, and no insurance carrier represents more than 10% of placed premiums. The strategic framework ("Aon United" / the "3x3 Plan," with 2026 as the final year) emphasizes cross-selling Risk and Human Capital solutions, complemented by the NFP acquisition's push into the U.S. middle market.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue.** The trajectory is strong and partly acquisition-fueled: $12.5B (2022), $13.4B (2023), $15.7B (2024), $17.2B (2025), a +9% total / +6% organic gain in the most recent year. The 2024-2025 step-up reflects the NFP acquisition (closed April 2024) layered on consistent organic growth. Q1 2026 revenue was $5.03B, +6% total / +5% organic, the modest organic deceleration that anchors the bear case.

**Profitability.** 2025 GAAP operating margin was 25.3% (up from 24.4%); adjusted operating margin was 32.4% (up from 31.5%). Reported net income of $3.7B and diluted EPS of $17.02 are inflated by a one-time $1.2B pre-tax gain on the NFP Wealth divestiture; the cleaner anchor is adjusted EPS of $17.07 (+9%). Investors should not extrapolate the headline net-income growth. Q1 2026 adjusted operating margin expanded 70bps to 39.1%, and adjusted EPS was $6.48 (+14%), though roughly $0.36 of that came from an FX tailwind.

**Cash flow.** 2025 operating cash flow was $3.5B (+15%) and free cash flow $3.2B (+14%). Capex under 2% of revenue is the classic asset-light broker model, and FCF conversion is high-quality. Q1 2026 FCF jumped to $363M (+332% YoY, partly a working-capital timing effect).

**Balance sheet.** The frequently cited Altman Z-Score of 1.07 ("distress zone") is a false signal for this model. Aon carries $17.9B in fiduciary assets (client premiums in transit) with an equal offsetting liability, grossing up the balance sheet and depressing working-capital ratios. This is standard for brokers and unrelated to solvency. Real leverage is elevated but manageable: ~$15.2B total debt against $9.4B GAAP equity (understated by buyback-driven accumulated deficit). The debt rose to fund the ~$13B NFP deal, but deleveraging is progressing: long-term debt fell from $16.3B (2024) to $14.7B (2025) to $13.5B (Q1 2026), the term loan was repaid, and €500M 2.875% notes were redeemed in February 2026 with $750M repaid in December 2025. Ratings are S&P A-/Stable, Moody's Baa2/Positive (outlook upgraded Q4 2025), Fitch BBB+/Stable. Annual interest expense is elevated at ~$815M (up from $484M in 2023), a real drag on EPS growth that the deleveraging is beginning to relieve.

**Capital allocation.** Buybacks ran ~$1.0B/yr in 2024-2025 with ~$0.8B remaining as of Q1 2026 and ≥$1B targeted for 2026. The dividend was raised 10% in April 2026 (sixth consecutive double-digit increase). Dividend yield is thin at ~0.9%, so total return depends on EPS compounding and buyback-driven per-share accretion, not income.

I score Financial Health 6.5. The cash-flow engine and improving credit trajectory are genuine strengths and the fiduciary-driven leverage optics are a non-issue; the elevated absolute debt load and ~$815M interest burden keep this from a 7+.

## Growth Analysis

The growth question is the crux. Aon's model compounds through mid-single-digit organic growth plus margin expansion plus per-share accretion from buybacks. Two of those three legs are firing: margins are expanding and buybacks continue. The organic-growth leg has softened, with Wealth Solutions at +1% and Talent/discretionary work weak.

The macro backdrop cuts both ways. A meaningful share of Aon's revenue is premium-based commission, so a softening P&C rate environment directly pressures the top line, a headwind the company flags prominently. Against this, structural tailwinds are real: rising risk complexity (cyber, climate, data-center risk, where Aon launched a proprietary Data Center Lifecycle Insurance Program in 2025), aging-population/retirement regulation driving U.K./EMEA Wealth demand, and expanding health/benefits needs.

Estimate revisions are the reassuring signal here. Consensus current-year EPS sits at $19.10, essentially flat over 30 days (-0.1%) and modestly positive over 90 days (+0.6%); next-year at $21.38, roughly flat. This is stability, not deterioration: the estimate-revision BUY threshold (a positive inflection of +30%+ over 90 days) is nowhere in play, and neither is a falling-estimate warning. The four-quarter beat streak (all four quarters, by 0.3% to 3.7%) tells us consensus is well-calibrated and management is not sandbagging into misses. Guidance was reaffirmed at Q1: mid-single-digit-plus organic growth, 70-80bps margin expansion, strong adjusted EPS growth, double-digit FCF growth.

I score Growth Prospects 5.5, down from the prior trajectory. The margin and FCF story supports continued high-single-digit adjusted EPS growth, but organic revenue at 5% with Wealth at +1% is a genuine deceleration that the reaffirmed guidance has not yet re-accelerated. Growth prospects is a directional winner-selector in our framework, but its recent predictive edge has thinned, so I weight it as a supporting rather than a decisive input.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$359, AON trades at roughly 18.8x the $19.10 current-year consensus and ~16.8x the $21.38 next-year estimate (the profile card's 19.7x reflects a lower reference price). On EV/EBITDA the stock is around 14x. The dividend yield is negligible at ~0.9%.

Analyst price targets frame a median of $390 (mean $393), a high of $445, and a low of $327; the current price sits below the median with the range skewed to the upside. Under our framework, analyst targets function as floors rather than ceilings only when a stock is genuinely cheap relative to accelerating growth. That condition is not met here: growth is decelerating, not accelerating, and estimate revisions are flat. So the ~$390 median is best read as fair-value consensus for a quality compounder, not as a lagging target destined for upward revision.

Peer context matters. Marsh McLennan, the closest comparable, typically trades at a premium to Aon, so a ~19x forward multiple is not stretched within the cohort. But cheapness relative to peers is not, by itself, a reason to expect outperformance: a "cheap" multiple on a business with softening organic growth is the value-trap configuration that historically sorts the wrong way. The external commentary captures the tension: FPA Crescent calls the multiple "undemanding" against a strong M&A record, while a June 2026 note argues the valuation "leaves little margin of safety." Both are right; this is quality-at-a-full-price.

The recovery from the mid-May low near $310 to ~$360 (roughly +15%) has already captured most of the near-term re-rating that a growth-stabilization thesis would justify. There is no identified catalyst that reprices the stock materially above consensus fair value in the near term; the swing factor is whether organic growth holds through the year.

I score Valuation 5.5. The stock is neither expensive enough to sell nor cheap enough (against its decelerating organic profile) to constitute an asymmetric buy. The absence of a specific mispricing catalyst keeps this from a 7+.

## Competitive Landscape

Aon occupies a consolidated, oligopolistic global brokerage market alongside Marsh McLennan, Willis Towers Watson, Arthur J. Gallagher, and Lockton. Its competitive position is stable-to-improving at the margin level: the data-and-analytics moat is real, client diversification is genuine, and the margin expansion demonstrates operating leverage and disciplined execution. The NFP bolt-on extended reach into the U.S. middle market, and the quick divestiture of NFP's wealth arm shows the company will prune toward higher-margin brokerage/benefits.

The competitive threats are structural rather than imminent: InsurTech and AI-driven disintermediation, client self-insurance/captives, capital-markets alternatives to traditional (re)insurance, and the industry trend of clients splitting accounts among multiple brokers. None of these has visibly dented Aon's economics yet, but they cap the upside on this dimension. The softening in Wealth and Talent is a share/demand signal worth monitoring; whether it reflects cyclical discretionary-spend weakness or competitive pressure will become clearer over the next two prints.

I score Competitive Position 6.5, consistent with the prior read. The moat is intact and the margin trajectory is favorable, but this is a market leader whose organic momentum has softened, and our framework has flagged competitive-position scoring as a low-signal dimension recently, so I do not lean on it heavily.

## Risk Assessment

The idiosyncratic risks that are not fully in the price:

1. **Organic-growth deceleration** is the central controversy. If 5% slips toward mid-single-digit-low or Wealth stays at +1%, the multiple compresses. This is the most likely source of downside.
2. **Leverage and interest burden.** Deleveraging is easing the interest drag, but a ratings reversal would raise borrowing costs. Currently in covenant compliance with an improving outlook.
3. **Insurance-pricing cyclicality.** Soft P&C rates directly pressure premium-based commission revenue.
4. **NFP integration and AAU cost creep.** Integration costs run through Q2 2026, and the AAU restructuring budget was raised 30% (from $1.0B to $1.3B) in Q4 2025 against ~$450M targeted annualized savings ($270M realized).
5. **Pension exposure.** Legacy DB plans with ~$3.7B accumulated OCI losses, sensitive to rates and asset returns.
6. **FX.** ~52% of revenue is non-USD; a ~$0.44 full-year 2026 adjusted EPS tailwind is expected at current rates, which reverses if the dollar strengthens.
7. **Vesttoo litigation legacy.** A $197M 2023 charge tied to third-party-arranged capital remains a named contingency (a $23M recovery was booked in Q3 2025).
8. **Irish incorporation.** Reduced shareholder legal protections, OECD Pillar Two minimum-tax uncertainty, and buyback/dividend dependence on distributable profits (>$30.1B available, so not currently a constraint).
9. **Governance-churn signal.** An elevated cadence of Item 5.02 director/officer-change 8-Ks (multiple in early 2026 and through 2025) merits monitoring for organizational churn during the integration phase, though some reflect ordinary board rotation.

I score Risk Factors 6.0. There are no near-term binary catalysts of large magnitude, and the most-cited "risk" (the distress-zone Z-Score) is a screening artifact, but the organic-growth question and the interest burden are real, monitorable exposures.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 32.0% against 25.4% one-year historical vol, rich, with the term structure in backwardation (32.0% near-term vs 24.9% at six months), signaling the market is pricing imminent event stress (the next earnings print). The implied one-month move is ±7.6%. Skew is balanced (put/call -0.9%) and the put/call OI ratio is a benign 0.59, so there is no directional bearish tilt in positioning; this is event-hedging rather than a bet on collapse. Short interest is negligible at 1.8% of float (2.0 days to cover), consistent with a widely-held quality name. The rich near-term IV argues for patience on any entry: a buyer is better served waiting for the post-earnings volatility crush than paying up into the print.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case (~$400-445).** Organic growth stabilizes at 5-6% through 2026 as Wealth and Talent recover, margin expansion continues (guidance of 70-80bps), deleveraging pushes Moody's to an actual upgrade, and buybacks plus the FX tailwind drive adjusted EPS toward the ~$21.38 next-year consensus and beyond. At an unchanged ~20x multiple on $21.38+ and continued upward drift, the stock reaches the low-$400s; a re-rating toward peer premiums on confirmed reacceleration reaches the $445 analyst high. This is the "quality compounder at an undemanding multiple" view.

**Bear case (~$300-310).** Organic growth slips below 5%, Wealth stays soft, and the market re-rates a decelerating broker to the mid-teens on forward earnings. The stock revisits the mid-May low near $310, roughly -14% from current, with limited downside beyond that given the FCF floor and the improving credit story. AAU cost creep and integration drag would reinforce this scenario.

**Base case (~$375).** Organic growth holds around 5%, margins expand roughly as guided, and adjusted EPS grows high-single-digits. At ~18x on next-year EPS trending toward $21.38, fair value sits around $375, modestly above current levels, a total return dominated by low-to-mid-single-digit price appreciation plus a ~0.9% dividend. The recovery from $310 has already delivered the bulk of the near-term re-rating, which is why the base case implies only modest upside from here.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the next two earnings prints (Q2 2026 in late July/early August and Q3 2026) that will confirm or refute organic-growth stabilization.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Organic growth reaccelerates back to 6%+ for two consecutive quarters, with Wealth Solutions above +3%.
- 90-day EPS estimate revisions turn positive by +3% or more, signaling consensus catching up to a reaccelerating fundamental base.
- A pullback toward the low-$320s or below on unchanged fundamentals, which would restore the asymmetric entry that the mid-May $310 level offered.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Organic growth falls below 4%, or Wealth/Talent softness spreads to Commercial Risk or Health.
- A ratings-outlook reversal or breach of a leverage covenant, or a material re-raise of the AAU restructuring budget beyond $1.3B.
- Guidance cut on margin expansion or FCF growth at any of the next two prints.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural reassessment is the Q2 2026 earnings release, which will test both the organic-growth stabilization thesis and the durability of the 39.1% Q1 margin.

## Conclusion

Aon is a genuinely high-quality, capital-light franchise with a real and improving margin story, strong free-cash-flow conversion, and a deleveraging balance sheet whose credit outlook is moving the right way. The much-cited distress-zone Z-Score is a fiduciary-accounting artifact, and reported 2025 EPS is flattered by a one-time NFP Wealth gain; the clean anchor is adjusted EPS of $17.07 (+9%), with the current-year consensus at $19.10.

The reason this is a HOLD rather than a BUY is that the one open question, organic growth decelerated to 5% with Wealth at +1%, is precisely the swing factor for the multiple, and it has not yet resolved favorably. Estimate revisions are stable, not rising, so the strongest forward-looking BUY signal is absent. Meanwhile, the ~15% recovery from the mid-May low has already priced in a stabilization outcome, leaving the stock at fair value against consensus (~$390 median vs ~$359 spot) with roughly balanced risk/reward. This is a quality business at a full price, worth holding for its compounding profile while waiting for organic growth to confirm the bull case or for a better entry. We classify this as a growth_hold: the 12-month thesis is positive appreciation contingent on organic-growth stabilization, but the catalyst has not yet arrived and the current price does not offer asymmetric upside.
