## Executive Summary

Tidewater is the world's largest publicly-traded pure-play offshore support vessel (OSV) operator, with 208 vessels across five geographic segments. The stock has doubled over the past 12 months (from ~$39 to ~$81) despite three consecutive quarterly earnings misses, declining utilization, and the emergence of a new material cost headwind from the Iran conflict ("Operation Epic Fury," adding $10-11M/quarter). Revenue growth has stalled at ~$1.35B annually following the 2023 Solstad acquisition, and the $500M Wilson Companies acquisition in Brazil represents the primary catalyst for re-acceleration.

At 13.6x forward P/E, TDW appears cheap relative to its peer median of 41x, but this discount reflects genuine uncertainty: current-quarter estimates are falling (-23% in 30 days), Q1 2026 delivered just $0.12/share EPS on heavy drydock costs and geopolitical disruption, and the 2025 headline net income of $334M was inflated by a one-time $195.8M deferred tax benefit. The underlying earnings power is closer to $140M — roughly $2.80/share — which would place the stock at ~29x normalized trailing earnings. The valuation gap narrows considerably when adjusted for this distortion.

The investment case hinges on: (1) day rate expansion of $3,000-4,000/year through 2028 as management guides, (2) successful Wilson integration driving mid-single-digit revenue growth, and (3) normalization of drydock spending and conflict costs. These are plausible but not assured, and the 11.1% short interest suggests institutional skepticism. Given three consecutive misses, declining score trajectory, and concentrated geopolitical risk, the stock warrants holding for existing positions but lacks sufficient visibility for new capital.

## Company Overview

Tidewater operates the largest fleet of OSVs globally, providing towing, anchor handling, platform supply, crew transport, and subsea support services to offshore energy producers. The fleet of 208 vessels (average age 13.1 years) operates across:

- **West Africa** — 66 vessels, $363M revenue (largest segment)
- **Europe/Mediterranean** — 50 vessels, $344M revenue
- **Americas** — 34 vessels, $270M revenue (Brazil, Mexico, Guyana, US Gulf)
- **Asia Pacific** — 19 vessels, $190M revenue
- **Middle East** — 43 vessels, $173M revenue

The company has pursued aggressive inorganic growth: the $594M Solstad acquisition (37 vessels, July 2023) and the pending $500M Wilson Companies deal (22 PSVs in Brazil, expected close late Q2 2026). This M&A strategy has transformed TDW from a mid-sized operator into the dominant listed player, though organic growth has effectively stalled since 2024.

## Growth Analysis

**Bear case for growth is strengthening.** Three data points suggest the day-rate-driven revenue expansion thesis is encountering friction:

1. **Utilization declining** — 79.0% (2024) → 76.1% (2025) → further pressure in Q1 2026. West Africa particularly weak at 65.9%.
2. **Revenue flat** — Despite 6.1% day rate improvement in 2025, volumes offset the gains entirely.
3. **Estimate revisions negative** — Current quarter estimates down 23% in 30 days. Current year estimates down 12% in 30 days.

**Bull case rests on:**

1. **Wilson acquisition** — 22 vessels in Brazil, expected to add $200-250M annualized revenue. Brazil deepwater (Petrobras pre-salt) is one of the world's most active offshore development theaters.
2. **Day rate trajectory** — Management projects $3,000-4,000/day annual increases through 2028 based on tight vessel supply. New term contracts in Q1 2026 (18 contracts, 13-month average duration) support this.
3. **Supply constraints** — Very limited global newbuild activity. Fleet aging across the industry means attrition will tighten supply further.
4. **2026 guidance maintained** — $1.43-1.48B revenue, 49-51% gross margin.

The next-year EPS estimate of $6.03 (up 21% over 90 days) reflects the expectation that the drydock cycle normalizes and Wilson contributes. If TDW can deliver $6+ EPS in 2027, the stock at $81 is trading at just 13.4x forward earnings.

## Competitive Landscape

Tidewater holds the strongest competitive position in the publicly-traded OSV universe:

- **Scale advantage** — 208 vessels, largest fleet among listed peers
- **Geographic diversification** — Five segments across all major offshore basins
- **Fleet quality** — 13.1 year average age, below industry average
- **Contract backlog** — New term contracts at rising day rates provide visibility
- **Supply moat** — Minimal newbuild activity globally; fleet replacement takes 2-3 years even if ordered today

The competitive position is improving structurally: aging global fleet + no newbuilds + rising offshore activity = pricing power for incumbents with young, well-maintained vessels. This is the strongest dimension of the TDW thesis and justifies the premium to historical valuation ranges.

Key competitive risks: Bourbon Offshore (restructured, returning to market), DOF Group expanding, and regional players in Brazil/Middle East who may compete for Wilson-type contracts.

## Conclusion

Tidewater occupies an enviable competitive position — largest OSV fleet, tightening supply, rising day rates — but near-term financial execution has been poor. Three consecutive misses, declining utilization, new conflict costs, and a deceleration from 33% revenue growth to zero have eroded the earnings momentum that previously justified the stock's premium. The score trajectory (6.7 → 6.1 → 6.0 → 5.88 today) reflects this progressive deterioration.

The stock at $81 is 13% below its 52-week high of $93 and has doubled from its lows. The forward P/E of 13.6x is attractive if 2027 estimates prove achievable, but given the miss streak and negative near-term revisions, that conditionality carries meaningful uncertainty. Consensus targets imply only 7% upside, and the 11.1% short interest suggests informed institutional bearishness.

For existing holders: maintain position with a stop-loss at the $55-60 range, representing a break of the October 2025 consolidation zone. The Wilson close and Q2 earnings will provide the information needed to make a higher-conviction call. For new capital: wait for evidence of estimate stabilization or a pullback to the $60-65 range where the risk/reward becomes more asymmetric.
