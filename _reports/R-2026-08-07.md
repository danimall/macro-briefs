## Executive Summary

Ryder has delivered a genuine structural improvement over the past three years: revenue mix has shifted so that roughly 60% of 2026 revenue comes from Supply Chain and Dedicated Transportation versus 44% in 2018, comparable EPS guidance of $14.40–$14.80 sits about 2.5x the $5.95 earned at the 2018 freight peak, and the balance sheet earned a Moody's upgrade to Baa1/stable in April 2026 while S&P and Fitch held BBB+. The equity has re-rated accordingly — up 46.8% over twelve months and 67% off the October 2025 low of $157.67, with a five-year total-return index of 353 against a 149 peer group.

The problem is what is driving current earnings. In the first half of 2026, comparable pre-tax earnings were $330M against $335M a year earlier, a 1.5% decline, and GAAP pre-tax earnings fell 4% to $304M. Comparable EPS nonetheless rose 8.3% to $6.25 because weighted average diluted shares fell 7.4% to 39,259K and the effective tax rate dropped to 25.4% from 27.7% on higher excess tax benefits from share-based compensation. In the second quarter specifically, comparable EBT rose 4.7% while comparable EPS rose 12.3% at an essentially unchanged tax rate — roughly seven of the twelve points came from the denominator. Free cash flow on the company's own definition is guided to $700–800M for 2026 versus $946M realized in 2025, a 15–26% decline in the same year comparable EPS is guided up double digits, and with $684M already banked in the first half the guide implies second-half free cash flow of $16M to $116M against a gross capex ramp to roughly $1.59B from $812M.

At $263.36 the stock trades at 18.0x the FY2026 comparable EPS midpoint of $14.60 and 14.9x the $17.72 2027 consensus. Both figures are defensible for a business earning a 17–18% ROE with over 90% of revenue under long-term contract. Neither is cheap for a lessor carrying 259% debt-to-equity into a higher-for-longer rate path, generating roughly 3% conventional free-cash-flow yield, and whose largest asset-light segment is currently running below its own margin target. We keep the rating at HOLD for a fifth consecutive review, with the overall score easing to 5.6 from 5.7. The specific catalysts that justify continued ownership — Fleet Management margin recovery from 11.5% toward low teens, used-vehicle gains guided to ~$40M against $196M realized in 2023, the automotive revenue comparison lapping in Q3 — are identifiable and asymmetric from a depressed base. They are also cycle-dependent, and the price already embeds a fair share of them.

## Company Overview

Ryder operates three reportable segments. **Fleet Management Solutions** (FY2025 revenue $5,845M, operating revenue $5,127M, EBT $501M) comprises ChoiceLease full-service leasing ($3,510M, +2%), commercial rental ($937M, −4%), SelectCare contract maintenance ($680M, −2%) and fuel services ($718M, −7%), plus a used-vehicle disposal operation that functions as a distinct earnings line. **Supply Chain Solutions** (revenue $5,459M, operating revenue $4,091M, EBT $355M) provides contract warehousing, distribution, e-commerce fulfillment and last-mile across omnichannel retail, automotive, CPG and industrial verticals. **Dedicated Transportation Solutions** (revenue $2,343M, operating revenue $1,841M, EBT $140M) bundles drivers, vehicles and route engineering under multi-year contracts, 70% of which management describes as specialized.

Two structural features matter for how the earnings stream should be valued. Management states that over 90% of revenue is generated under long-term contracts, with ChoiceLease terms of three to seven years and indexed escalators on SCS and DTS work — which explains why Ryder's earnings held through the 2023–2025 freight trough while spot-exposed truckload and LTL peers saw earnings collapse. And 60% of revenue comes from customers using more than one Ryder service, supporting retention.

One qualification on the mix-shift narrative. The FY2025 MD&A segment tables disclose an "Equipment Contribution" EBT benefit allocated to SCS ($44M) and DTS ($87M), totaling $131M, against an Eliminations EBT line of exactly −$131M. If that allocation runs from the FMS fleet to the asset-light segments as the matching magnitudes suggest, then roughly 62% of DTS's reported FY2025 EBT traces to vehicle economics rather than standalone dedicated operations, implying DTS standalone EBT near $53M on $1,841M of operating revenue. The revenue mix has genuinely shifted; a meaningful share of the reported *profit* in the asset-light segments still originates in the owned fleet. This reading rests on MD&A tables rather than the Item 8 segment footnote and should be treated as unconfirmed, though the consolidated total is unaffected either way.

Competitively, Ryder ranks #7 among TT Top 100 for-hire carriers, #6 among truckload/dedicated carriers and #8 among North American logistics companies — top-tier without dominance in any vertical.

## Financial Analysis

**A note on standard screens.** Two mechanical artifacts distort automated reads of Ryder and should be discarded. First, the XBRL revenue tag captures $8,784M — services plus fuel services revenue — against $12,665M of total revenue reported on the FY2025 income statement, producing a false picture of cost exceeding revenue. Second, an Altman Z of 0.92 rests on an EBIT/assets input of 0.002 when FY2025 EBIT was approximately $1,089M ($685M pre-tax earnings plus $404M interest), or 6.6% of $16,387M of assets. Corrected, Z computes to roughly 1.64 — still below the 1.81 threshold, but the model was calibrated on manufacturers and structurally penalizes any business whose model is owning depreciating assets funded with term debt. The decisive current credit evidence points the other way: three investment-grade ratings, one of them upgraded in April 2026.

**Earnings.** FY2025 total revenue was flat at $12,665M with operating revenue up 1% to $10,406M; EBT rose 4% to $685M, comparable EBT 2% to $730M, comparable EBITDA 3% to $2,867M, and comparable EPS 8% to $12.92. Segment margins on operating revenue tell the underlying story: FMS compressed from 13.2% (2023) to 10.1% (2024) to 9.8% (2025), SCS improved 6.4% → 8.4% → 8.7%, DTS moved 9.3% → 6.7% → 7.6%. The FMS compression was almost entirely used-vehicle cyclicality — disposal gains fell from $196M in 2023 to $72M in 2024 to $22M in 2025, an 89% decline, with per-unit proceeds down 21–23% in 2024 and a further 11–15% in 2025.

**2026 year to date.** FMS is inflecting up: Q2 EBT rose 20% to $150M, first-half EBT 14% to $249M, and the Q2 margin reached 11.5% from 9.7% — still below the stated low-teens through-cycle target. Used-vehicle pricing turned positive year over year (trucks +6%, tractors +3%) and sequentially on retail (trucks +7%, tractors +3%); the disposal result swung to a $7M gain from a $2M loss. Rental utilization returned to the 75% target from 70%, but on a 15% smaller average fleet (29,200 units versus 34,300) with pricing up only 1% — utilization was achieved by shrinking the base, and management was explicit that demand remains below both prior year and historical seasonal norms.

SCS is going the other way: Q2 EBT fell 7% to $92M and first-half EBT 12% to $164M on 7% operating revenue growth, with margin down to 8.4% from 9.7% and to 7.9% from 9.2% on a trailing basis — below management's own high-single-digit target. Causes cited were lost automotive business, OEM plants retooling, and productivity drag from new business ramping more slowly than planned. Automotive revenue fell 8% to $508M in the first half while omnichannel retail grew 20% to $724M. DTS was soft, with first-half EBT down 8% to $59M and operating revenue down 3% on lower fleet count, partly attributed to adverse development of prior-year insurance claims.

The second-quarter print itself was a marginal miss against consensus: comparable EPS of $3.73 against a $3.76 estimate, −0.8%. That makes three misses in the last four quarters (Q3 2025 −1.5%, Q4 2025 −1.5%, Q1 2026 +9.6%, Q2 2026 −0.8%). The pattern argues for modest rather than high confidence in the second-half acceleration embedded in guidance.

**Cash flow.** On Ryder's definition — operating cash flow plus proceeds from equipment and property sales, less purchases of property and revenue-earning equipment — FY2025 free cash flow was $946M, versus $133M in 2024 and negative $54M in 2023. That inflection was achieved by cutting gross capex from $3,279M (2023) to $2,694M (2024) to $2,055M (2025). The conventional measure, operating cash flow less capex, was $459M.

First-half 2026 operating cash flow fell 10% to $1,260M on a $103M receivables build and vendor payment timing, while gross capex fell 32% to $812M (ChoiceLease $605M from $832M; commercial rental $94M from $268M), lifting company-defined free cash flow 48% to $684M. Net revenue-earning equipment declined $381M in six months to $8,517M and total fleet fell to 180,300 units from 184,900. The first-half figure is substantially a defleeting artifact and should not be annualized: against unchanged FY2026 guidance of $700–800M, the implied second half is $16M to $116M, consistent with roughly $1.59B of second-half gross capex against $2.4B guided for the year. Management conceded limited visibility, noting "75% of the year from a capital perspective is probably good." If the ramp slips, 2026 free cash flow beats and 2027 fleet growth disappoints; the two outcomes are linked, and an FCF beat driven by capex deferral should not be read as a positive.

Cash taxes are a large and under-appreciated support. FY2025 income taxes paid were $52M against a $184M provision, with $59M paid in the first half of 2026, and the deferred tax liability stands at $1,824M. The Q2 10-Q flags reinstated bonus depreciation, restoration of an EBITDA basis for the interest-expense limitation, and GILTI modifications — all materially cash-favorable for a company deploying ~$2.4B of annual capex against ~$400M of interest. This is deferral, not permanent saving, and it compresses if capex growth decelerates.

**Balance sheet.** Total debt fell $188M in the first half to $7,457M, with zero new debt issued ($0 versus $594M in the prior-year first half). Equity fell to $2,879M from $3,052M on $851M of cumulative 2025–H1 2026 buybacks, taking debt-to-equity to 259% — inside management's stated 250–300% target and endorsed by all three agencies. Gross debt to trailing comparable EBITDA of ~$2,866M is roughly 2.6x; EBITDA/interest ~7.3x; EBIT/interest ~2.7x. The current ratio of 0.65 at June 30 reflects a $1,146M reclassification of two 2026 medium-term notes ($300M at 4.44%, $400M at 2.90%) plus $723M of commercial paper into current maturities, not new borrowing. Refinancing cost is immaterial: replacing the 2.90% note near 4.5% adds roughly $6.4M pre-tax, about $0.13 per share. Liquidity was $1,079M. Pension risk is largely retired at 94% funded with ~$10M of 2026 required contributions, though the $556M net actuarial loss inside a −$614M AOCI balance remains a book-value drag.

## Growth Analysis

Ryder's top line is not growing. FY2025 total revenue was flat and operating revenue up 1%; Q2 2026 operating revenue grew 3%. The growth that reaches shareholders comes from three sources, in descending order of current contribution.

**Share count.** Diluted shares fell 7.4% year over year in the first half, and shares outstanding fell to 38.35M from 39.42M at year-end. This is a finite lever: remaining authorization at June 30 was 2,874,481 shares, about 7.5% of the current count, and management allocates only roughly half of its ~$4.5B of three-year flexible capacity to buybacks *and* acquisitions combined. At the first-half repurchase pace the authorization supports roughly two more years.

**Structural cost and pricing actions.** Management reaffirmed $70M of incremental 2026 earnings benefit from a $170M multi-year program launched in 2024, of which $100M was realized across 2024–2025, sourced from FMS lease pricing and maintenance savings, DTS flex operating structure actions and SCS network optimization. Management frames these as not cycle-dependent, and the 2024–2025 delivery record supports that framing.

**Cycle recovery.** Management estimates roughly $250M of incremental EBT by the next cycle peak, mostly from rental and used-vehicle recovery in FMS plus higher omnichannel volumes. The arithmetic is coherent: used-vehicle gains alone were $196M in 2023 against ~$40M guided for 2026, supplying roughly $155M, and closing the FMS margin gap from 11.5% to low teens on ~$5.1B of operating revenue supplies $50–100M. Timing is entirely cycle-dependent, and management characterizes the current recovery as capacity-driven rather than demand-driven — capacity has exited truckload, tightening driver supply, but end demand has not accelerated. Notably, rental, normally the leading indicator, is lagging this cycle; lease and dedicated demand appeared first, reversing the usual sequence.

The guidance raise composition is instructive. Management lifted the low end of FY2026 comparable EPS guidance to $14.40 from $14.05 while holding the high end at $14.80, attributing the raise specifically to a $10M increase in expected used-vehicle gains to ~$40M, partially offset by supply chain onboarding timing — with some projects previously expected to launch in 2026 now "extended into 2027." Q3 comparable EPS is guided to $4.00–$4.20 against $3.57. With $6.25 delivered in the first half, the full-year midpoint implies $8.35 in the second half and roughly $4.25 in Q4 against $3.59, an 18% increase. That acceleration is plausible on used-vehicle gains, back-half-weighted initiative benefits and the automotive comparison lapping in Q3, but it is a step up from a first half in which comparable pre-tax earnings declined.

Estimate revisions are mildly supportive rather than a signal of inflection: current-year consensus $14.67 (+0.3% over 30 days, +0.6% over 90), next-year $17.72 (+0.8%, +1.8%). This is nowhere near the revision velocity that reliably precedes re-rating, and current-quarter estimates have drifted down 1.1% in 30 days.

**Growth Prospects: 5.2.** Operating earnings are flat to down, the reported EPS growth is denominator-driven, and the genuine upside is real but contingent on a freight cycle that management describes as supply-led with the traditional leading indicator lagging.

The supplied peer set — SAIA 51.2x, KNX 393.7x, LSTR 62.2x, ARCB 70.1x, RXO negative, median 70.1x — cannot be used at face value. Those multiples reflect trough earnings at spot-exposed carriers whose profits collapsed through the freight downturn; Ryder's contract density protected its earnings, so a P/E comparison here measures cyclical earnings position rather than relative value. The relevant comparison is Ryder against its own history and against the cash it produces.

On that basis 18.0x forward is a full re-rating. Ryder spent most of the prior decade at 8–12x forward earnings. Part of the expansion is deserved: the revenue mix is genuinely less asset-heavy, ROE has moved to 17–18% from 13% at the 2018 peak, and the credit profile improved to Baa1/BBB+. Part is harder to defend. The asset-light profit shift is less complete than the revenue shift given the $131M Equipment Contribution allocation; the 2027 consensus of $17.72 implies 21% EPS growth off a first half in which pre-tax earnings declined; and cash conversion is weak — approximately 3% conventional free-cash-flow yield offers no absolute-yield support. The 7.4% company-defined yield is not wrong, but the $500M gap between definitions is used-vehicle disposal proceeds inherent to recycling a fleet, not distributable surplus.

Published targets — low $283, high $320, mean $299.56, median $300 — all sit above spot, which superficially implies 8–22% upside. Two qualifications apply. Targets on a stock trading at a re-rated multiple with modest revision momentum are not independent confirmation; they largely embed the same 2027 growth extrapolation as the price. And sell-side sentiment is degrading: hold ratings doubled to four from two over the past month while strong-buy and buy counts were unchanged at two and five.

Our base case uses 2027 comparable EPS of $17.00 — a modest haircut to consensus for the SCS ramp slippage and the three-of-four-quarter miss record — at 16.5–17x, giving **$285**, or 8.2% above spot. Bull case assumes the cycle turns with used-vehicle gains toward $100M+, rental demand finally recovering, FMS reaching 12.5% margin and SCS ramps converting: EPS near $18.75 at 17.5x, **$325**, +23.4%. Bear case assumes the capacity-driven recovery stalls, SCS ramp delays prove structural rather than timing-related, residual values require a reset and consumer weakness hits omnichannel volumes: EPS near $13.25 at 14x, **$185**, −29.7%.

Weighting those at 30/50/20 gives a probability-weighted price return near 5% plus 1.5% of dividend — approximately fair, with clearly unfavorable asymmetry (8% base upside against 30% bear downside). That combination defines HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE.

**Valuation: 4.7.** There is no identifiable mispricing to exploit at 18x on guided non-GAAP earnings that are growing on the share count, and the downside multiple risk is larger than the upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Ryder's position is stable-to-improving in two segments and deteriorating in one.

FMS is benefiting from capacity exit. Management reports record DTS pipelines with customers who previously used for-hire carriers seeking dedicated capacity, driver turnover ticking up and days-to-hire lengthening — all consistent with tightening supply that favors outsourced providers. Ryder retains OEM build-slot access and can redeploy off-lease and dedicated vehicles into rental, giving it flexibility competitors with thinner balance sheets lack.

The July 9, 2026 EPA draft notice revising the heavy-truck NOx rule is a net positive for an incumbent lessor. OEMs had signaled potential price increases near $20,000 per vehicle, which the EPA frames as reducible by up to $6,000 in emissions-warranty costs; Cummins will stagger MY2027 engine introductions and Traton is considering the same. Higher new-truck prices inflate the replacement cost and therefore the market value of Ryder's in-service and off-lease fleet, supporting used pricing and residual values, and they strengthen the outsourcing pitch when a customer faces a five-figure sticker increase. The offsetting risk is a pre-buy followed by a demand air pocket, or outright deferral of fleet renewal. Management noted that pricing clarity itself would help both lease activity and used-vehicle sales, implying current uncertainty is a live drag.

SCS is the weak link. Margin fell 130bp on a trailing basis to 7.9%, below management's own high-single-digit target, on lost automotive business and slower-than-planned ramps. Two disclosures compound the concern: new operating lease ROU additions rose to $127M from $51M in the first half, meaning fixed warehouse commitments are expanding while margins compress and revenue starts slip into 2027; and a $10M non-cash impairment was recorded on an acquired customer relationship intangible on reduced projected cash flows. That combination — rising fixed real-estate commitments plus deferred revenue starts — is the profile that produces negative operating leverage if volumes disappoint. Management's counter is credible: Q2 2025's 9.7% was a record second quarter, the automotive comparison laps in Q3, and ramp drag on engineered facilities reverses. The next two quarters resolve it.

Two structural competitive risks deserve naming. The FY2025 10-K contains an unusually candid disclosure that warehouse automation and autonomous mobile robots "have impacted, and may continue to impact" the supply chain business, because SCS pricing is partly labor-linked — deploying the technology can reduce Ryder's own revenue. Separately, an analyst raised Amazon's entry into contract logistics on the Q2 call; management reported no encounters in RFQs and characterized Amazon's focus as retail-oriented, while noting Ryder's facilities are typically single-box, single-customer engineered sites. We have not verified the nature, scope or timing of any Amazon offering, and treat this as an unconfirmed development rather than an established threat.

Customer concentration has not improved. Top-ten customers accounted for roughly one-third of revenue in *each* of SCS and DTS in 2025, and that figure was not disclosed as improving. The shift in SCS's vertical mix from automotive toward omnichannel retail changes the industry composition of revenue; it says nothing about counterparty concentration, which is flat.

## Risk Assessment

**Residual value estimation** is the largest accounting sensitivity. The 10-K states residual value estimates for the total fleet are "at historically low levels," and a 10% reduction would add roughly $340M of depreciation over the fleet's remaining life. Ryder did reduce residuals for certain tractors effective January 1, 2026, characterizing the annual impact as immaterial. The trend is currently favorable: used pricing remains above the residuals used for depreciation, held-for-sale assets carried below cost fell to $40M from $60M, and first-half valuation adjustment losses fell to $18M from $30M. But the improvement is partly channel mix — retail share was 56% in Q2 versus 61% in Q1 against a target in the 70s — and one quarter of positive year-over-year pricing after two years of declines is a signal, not a confirmed trend.

**Self-insurance** development has already flipped from a $15M benefit in 2024 to a $3M charge in 2025, and the Q2 10-Q cites adverse prior-year claims development as a driver of the DTS decline. The accrual rose to $601M from $554M, though that balance change nets new accruals, estimate revisions and payments and cannot be attributed to development alone. Disclosed sensitivity: a 5% adverse change in actuarial estimates adds roughly $25M of 2026 operating expense. Insurers have been exiting trucking coverage, narrowing risk-transfer options.

**Leverage into a higher-for-longer path.** At 259% debt-to-equity, ~2.6x gross debt/EBITDA and 2.7x EBIT/interest coverage, Ryder is more rate-sensitive than most industrials. Near-term refinancing cost is immaterial (~$0.13/share), maturities are laddered 2027–2033, variable-rate debt is 17% of total against a 20–40% target range, and liquidity is $1,079M — so this is a valuation-multiple risk more than a solvency risk. But a leveraged, capital-intensive model financed at 4.5%+ against ~3% conventional free-cash-flow yield is structurally exposed if the long end steepens further.

**Cyclical lag.** The 10-K discloses that Ryder's business "may begin to slow before overall market slowdowns... and may recover later than overall market recoveries." The current cycle confirms it: recovery is capacity-driven rather than demand-driven, and rental — normally the leading indicator — is lagging. If the freight recovery stalls before demand joins, Ryder's recovery arrives late.

**Labor** is fragmented rather than binary: roughly 3,600 US employees under 94 separate agreements creates recurring small negotiations rather than a single cliff.

Nothing on this list is a dated binary event. That is why the risk score sits mid-range rather than low: the risks are material and stacked, but they are gradual and largely disclosed, and the biggest one — residuals — is currently trending the right way.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 31.1% sits slightly below one-year realized volatility of 33.9% (spread −2.8%, classified fair) and in the 9th percentile within the current chain — options are cheap relative to recent realized movement, which favors buying optionality rather than selling it. Term structure is flat (31.1% near, 31.9% at six months), indicating uncertainty distributed evenly rather than concentrated on a near-term catalyst. The nearest expiry prices a ±5.2% move.

The most informative change is in open interest. Put/call open interest fell to 0.52 from 1.75 between the June 24 and August 7 snapshots while price was essentially unchanged ($262.71 → $263.26), and ATM IV compressed 4.7 points from 35.8%. That combination — protection unwound, volatility bid removed, price flat — indicates the hedging demand that existed into the Q2 print has been released rather than converted into directional positioning. The 4.0% front-month put skew reads as elevated put demand, but three- and six-month skews are negative (−1.8% and −1.2%), so the put bid is confined to the two-week tenor and is not a structural bearish signal. Short interest of 4.1% of float at 2.7 days to cover offers no squeeze dynamic in either direction.

The takeaway is consistent with the fundamental read: the options market prices Ryder as a moderately volatile industrial with no near-term binary, and it is not corroborating either a bullish re-rating or an imminent break.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $325 (+23.4%)

The freight cycle turns from capacity-led to demand-led during 2027. Used-vehicle gains recover from ~$40M guided toward the $196M realized in 2023, delivering the bulk of management's $250M cycle-EBT estimate. Rental demand finally follows lease and dedicated demand, letting Ryder redeploy off-lease vehicles into rental at 75%+ utilization on a growing fleet with pricing better than the current 1%. FMS margin closes the gap from 11.5% to low teens, worth $50–100M on $5.1B of operating revenue. SCS ramps that slipped into 2027 convert at target margin, restoring the segment to high single digits, and the automotive comparison lapping in Q3 2026 removes an 8% revenue headwind. EPA-driven new-truck price inflation lifts replacement cost and therefore residual values while strengthening the outsourcing proposition. The $70M of 2026 structural initiatives extends into 2027, and continued buyback within the ~$4.5B three-year flexible capacity compounds on a share count already down 7.4%. Comparable EPS reaches roughly $18.75 in 2027; at 17.5x the stock reaches $325, in line with the $320 high published target.

### Bear Case — $185 (−29.7%)

The capacity-driven recovery stalls before end demand joins, and Ryder's disclosed tendency to recover later than the market plays out. SCS ramp delays prove structural rather than timing — record sales activity keeps converting to profit at lower margin while the 2.5x increase in new warehouse lease commitments ($127M versus $51M) becomes fixed cost against disappointing volumes, producing negative operating leverage. Consumer weakness hits omnichannel retail volumes, which were the only SCS vertical growing. Used-vehicle pricing stalls after one positive quarter, forcing a residual reset toward the ~$340M sensitivity disclosed for a 10% haircut. The buyback lever exhausts as the 2.87M-share authorization runs down and rising second-half capex absorbs cash. Comparable EPS falls toward $13.25 and the multiple compresses to 14x, closer to Ryder's decade-long 8–12x range than to today's 18x. That gives $185.

### What Would Change Our Mind

The single most decisive datapoint would be comparable pre-tax earnings growth in the second half of 2026. First-half comparable EBT of $330M against $335M means the entire EPS story currently rests on shares and taxes. If Q3 and Q4 deliver mid-single-digit or better EBT growth — plausible given the automotive lapping, used-vehicle gains and back-half initiative weighting — the 18x multiple is supported by operating momentum and the rating moves up. If comparable EBT is flat or down again while EPS still rises on buybacks, the multiple is being paid for financial engineering and the rating moves down.

## Conclusion

Ryder is a better business than it was at the 2018 freight peak, and the market has already paid for that. The mix shift is real, the credit profile improved to Baa1/BBB+ with all three agencies stable or better, the dividend rose 11% for a fourth consecutive double-digit year at a 27.7% payout, and management has been candid about the composition of its own guidance raise — attributing it to $10M of additional used-vehicle gains partly offset by supply chain projects sliding into 2027. Capital allocation has been disciplined, including a buyback that repurchased 219,300 shares at $228.27 in April, 200,437 at $238.87 in May, and just 882 shares at $270.72 in June.

The offsetting facts are equally concrete. First-half comparable pre-tax earnings fell 1.5% and GAAP pre-tax earnings fell 4%, while comparable EPS rose 8.3% on a 7.4% smaller share count and a 230bp lower tax rate. Free cash flow is guided down 15–26% in the same year EPS is guided up double digits, with the second half implying $16M to $116M against a doubling of the capex pace. The largest asset-light segment is running below its own margin target while committing 2.5x more new warehouse lease capacity. Conventional free-cash-flow yield of roughly 3% offers no absolute support. And 18.0x guided non-GAAP earnings is at the upper end of anything Ryder has sustained.

Weighting the scenarios, expected return is approximately fair with unfavorable asymmetry: 8% to base against 30% to bear. The recovery optionality is genuine — $40M of used-vehicle gains against $196M realized in 2023 is a depressed base with real upside — but it requires a cycle that management itself describes as supply-driven with the traditional leading indicator still absent. **HOLD.** Existing owners are compensated for waiting through the Q3 print; new capital has a better entry near $225–235, where the stock would trade around 13x 2027 consensus and inside management's own demonstrated buying range.
