**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Copart operates the dominant online salvage- and used-vehicle auction platform, a two-sided network connecting roughly 80% insurance-sourced supply to ~1 million members across 185+ countries. The business is a fortress on every static financial measure: ~33% net margins, near-zero debt, $3.35B in cash and Treasuries, and an Altman Z-score near 9. The problem is not solvency or quality; the growth engine that justified a 30x+ multiple for a decade has stalled. For the nine months ended April 30, 2026, total revenue was down 0.2% YoY, US service revenue fell 2.1%, and US unit volumes declined outright. International (+11% service revenue) is now the sole source of growth.

The most informative signal this year came from capital allocation, not the income statement. A company that had never repurchased a share or paid a dividend in its history bought back 43.4M shares for $1.633B (average $37.63) in nine months, cut capex from $483M to $263M, and added dividends to its stated menu of uses for cash. This is management implicitly conceding that the US core has matured. The buybacks were executed well above the current ~$30 price while insiders were selling into the same decline, a value-destroying repurchase to date paired with an absence of insider conviction.

The stock is down ~38% from its 52-week high of $50.11 and trades at ~18x forward earnings, a meaningful de-rating from its historical premium. The central question is whether the US claims softness is cyclical (premium normalization, claims rebound, ADAS-driven total-loss tailwind reasserting) or a structural behavioral step-down (consumers underinsuring/dropping coverage as premiums inflate, permanently reducing salvage supply). With that debate unresolved, an unquantified DOJ anti-money-laundering investigation as a downside tail, and no insider or estimate-revision signal pointing to a recovery, this is a HOLD. The franchise quality argues against avoiding it; the absence of a growth catalyst and the still-premium multiple on a no-growth core argue against buying it.

## Company Overview

Founded in 1982, Copart is the world's leading online salvage-vehicle auction operator, running 250+ locations across 11 countries on its proprietary VB3 ("Virtual Bidding Third Generation") internet auction technology. It sold over 4 million units in the trailing year.

The supply side is concentrated and contractual-light: ~81% of vehicles processed come from insurance companies (total-loss and recovered-stolen vehicles), with the remainder from dealers, fleets, rental companies, banks, charities, and individuals. No single customer exceeds 10% of revenue, but a concentrated set of insurers collectively drives most volume, with no long-term contracts. The buy side spans licensed dismantlers, rebuilders, used-car dealers, exporters, and increasingly the general public, with international buyers a growing share.

Copart reports two segments: United States (~82% of revenue, an asset-light agent/consignment model) and International (UK, Germany, Spain, Canada, Brazil, Ireland, Finland, and several Gulf markets, a mix of agent and principal models, with the UK also selling recycled parts via Hills Motors/GPS). The principal model in some international markets introduces inventory risk absent from the US consignment business.

The competitive moat rests on a two-sided network effect (more buyers raise realized prices, which attracts more insurer supply), a national footprint of zoning-constrained, hard-to-replicate storage land, proprietary auction technology, and deep insurer relationships built since the early 2000s. The principal US competitor is IAA, now part of RB Global. Copart's land ownership and catastrophe-event response capability (demonstrated in Hurricanes Helene and Milton) are durable structural advantages.

## Financial Analysis

The static financial picture is among the strongest in the industrials universe. For FY2025 (ended 7/31/25), revenue was $4.65B (service revenue $3.97B, +11.4%; vehicle sales $678M, roughly flat), with operating margin near 36% and net income of ~$1.55B (~33% net margin). Operating cash flow was $1.8B. The balance sheet carries ~$3.35B in cash and Treasuries against only ~$93M in leases, an undrawn $1.25B revolver, and $8.8B of stockholders' equity. There is effectively no refinancing or covenant risk.

The shift is in the nine-month FY2026 data (ended 4/30/26). Q3 total revenue rose 2.1%, but US service revenue was down 0.4% with volumes lower, and Q3 net income fell ~1.0% YoY even as EPS of $0.43 beat consensus by roughly 5%. The EPS beat was driven by buybacks and cost control, not organic growth.

Margins have held up: operating margin remained near 37% despite flat revenue, helped by the non-recurrence of $56M of FY2025 hurricane catastrophe costs. The capex cut signals a deceleration in greenfield expansion. Free cash flow conversion remains high, and the ~$142M of nine-month interest income on idle cash flatters net income while depressing return on equity.

The earnings track record is solid but no longer accelerating: three beats and one miss over the last four quarters, with the miss (-10% in the March quarter) standing out. Estimate revisions are flat-to-marginally positive: current-year EPS $1.58 (revised +0.6% over 90 days), next year $1.69 (+0.3%). These are stable, not inflecting. The data describe a mature compounder holding its ground, not a trough recovery.

## Growth Analysis

The growth thesis has changed character. For most of the past decade, the bull case was double-digit compounding driven by a secular rise in total-loss frequency: vehicles increasingly equipped with ADAS sensors and complex components become uneconomic to repair, pushing more cars into the salvage channel, against an aging US fleet (average vehicle age rose from 11.1 years in 2012 to 12.8 years in 2025). That structural driver remains intact.

A cyclical/behavioral headwind has emerged on top of it and is now visible in the numbers. Sharp insurance-premium inflation in 2024–2026 is reducing claims frequency and pushing some consumers to underinsure or drop coverage, cutting the supply of insurer-consigned salvage. Management's own language ("softer claims activity and shifting consumer insurance behavior") identifies this as the core reason for declining US volume. Copart's risk factors warned for years that higher used-car prices reduce total-loss frequency and that fewer claims reduce supply; that scenario is now playing out.

The unresolved analytical question is whether US softness is cyclical (premium normalization eventually restores claims frequency) or a structural step-down in claims behavior. The data available do not yet answer it. International expansion, particularly Germany and Spain, where insurers historically have not outsourced vehicle disposal, is the primary growth runway, but it carries execution risk, FX exposure (the company does not hedge), and principal-model margin dilution. Growth prospects score in the lower-middle of the range: the most reliable forward dimension is pointing flat-to-down on the core business, and the offset (International) is smaller and lower-quality.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$29.52, Copart trades at ~18.3x trailing/forward earnings on EPS of ~$1.59. Against the Diversified Support Services peer median P/E of 21.7, the stock screens as a modest discount, and against its own historical 30x+ range it has de-rated substantially. The peer set is heterogeneous, however; it includes federal-services contractors (LDOS at 11.2x, SAIC at 12.9x, CACI at 21.7x) that are structurally different businesses, plus RB Global (the salvage competitor) at an optically high 49.3x. The cleanest comparison is RB Global, against which Copart looks far cheaper, but RB Global's multiple reflects post-IAA-merger integration accounting rather than a clean read.

The cycle-adjusted lens does not rescue the valuation. There is no earnings recovery here: revisions are flat, revenue is flat-to-negative, and margins are already at peak. This is a mature multiple on a no-growth core, not a trough multiple on depressed earnings. Applying the principle that a low multiple is only attractive with an identifiable catalyst, the catalyst is absent: the US claims recovery is unproven, and International is too small to re-accelerate the consolidated growth rate near-term.

A premium multiple is defensible for a wide-moat, capital-light, 33%-margin duopolist with a fortress balance sheet, but ~18x for a business growing earnings in the low single digits (EPS $1.58 → $1.69, ~7%) is roughly fair, not cheap. Analyst targets (low $32, mean $41.44, median $42, high $55) sit well above the current price, but on a no-growth, premium-quality name where estimates are not rising, those targets are more likely to compress toward price than the reverse. I treat the consensus targets as stale anchors lagging the growth stall, not as floors. The valuation is balanced: real downside support from quality and cash, but no asymmetric upside without a demonstrated volume recovery.

## Competitive Landscape

The US salvage-auction industry is effectively a duopoly (Copart vs. IAA/RB Global) with high barriers to entry: zoned and entitled storage land that is genuinely scarce, entrenched insurer relationships, proprietary auction technology, and a two-sided network effect that compounds. Copart's competitive position within this structure remains strong and is not visibly eroding to a competitor; there is no evidence of share loss to IAA. The volume decline is an industry-demand phenomenon, not a market-share phenomenon.

The moat is holding on a relative basis (Copart's land, CAT-response, and international footprint are durable), but the absolute pie is shrinking in the US as claims soften. International expansion into markets where insurers traditionally retain disposal is the offensive lever, but those markets are structurally harder (Germany's insurer-disintermediated model, Brazil regulatory proposals) and lower-margin. The competitive position score sits modestly above the midpoint: a genuinely wide moat, but applied to a demand pool that has stopped growing domestically.

## Risk Assessment

Several company-specific risks are not fully reflected in a quality-franchise narrative:

1. **DOJ anti-money-laundering investigation (most material legal risk).** Since October 2023, the DOJ Consumer Protection Branch has investigated Copart's AML practices for detecting money-laundering by platform members, and has sent a letter indicating Copart "may have exposure." The loss is not estimable. For a business whose model depends on a vast international buyer base wiring funds, it carries potential fines, criminal/civil proceedings, and reputational risk, and it is unquantified.

2. **Demand-side erosion now materializing.** Revenue is highly sensitive to total-loss frequency (falls when used-car prices spike) and accident frequency (falls with ADAS, autonomous driving, fewer miles driven). The premium-driven claims softness is live in the numbers, and the cyclical-vs-structural question is unresolved.

3. **Customer concentration without long-term contracts.** A concentrated set of insurers drives most volume; sellers can terminate at will.

4. **International/principal-model risk.** Inventory risk, unhedged FX exposure, structurally harder markets.

5. **Internal financial-system migration** flagged in the 10-Q as an ongoing internal-control implementation risk.

6. **Capital-allocation execution.** The first-ever buybacks were executed well above today's price while insiders sold. To date the repurchase has destroyed value relative to the falling price.

### Options Market Signal

The near-term ATM IV of 27.1% sits modestly rich to 1-year historical volatility of 24.1%, with an IV percentile in the 3rd percentile of the current chain; the market is not pricing an imminent shock. Term structure is in contango (27.1% near vs 30.7% long-dated), a normal-to-complacent shape. Put/call skew of +3.1% near-term and an elevated put/call volume ratio (4.03) point to defensive positioning and hedging demand, consistent with a stock that has fallen ~38% and faces an open regulatory tail, but short interest at just 4.6% of float (3.7 days to cover) shows the bearish thesis is uncrowded rather than consensus. The reported jump in one ATM IV reading to an implausible level appears to be a thin-options data artifact and is treated as non-informative. Net read: the options market is mildly defensive but not signaling a near-term catalyst, which fits the unresolved cyclical-vs-structural debate.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (~$42–55)
The US claims softness proves cyclical. As premium inflation normalizes and insured-driving behavior recovers, claims frequency and total-loss frequency reassert, and the secular ADAS/aging-fleet tailwind drives US volumes back to growth. International compounds at double digits, the capital-return pivot supports EPS, and the multiple re-rates back toward the historical premium on a quality franchise with no debt and $3.4B cash. At normalized EPS on a higher multiple, the stock returns to the low-$40s to mid-$50s, matching the analyst high. The DOJ matter resolves with a manageable settlement.

### Bear Case (~$22–25)
US claims softness is structural: premium-driven underinsurance permanently lowers the salvage-supply pool, and US volumes settle into persistent low-single-digit declines. The buyback (executed above price) and capex cut confirm management has run out of high-return reinvestment, but EPS support cannot offset a shrinking top line indefinitely. The DOJ AML investigation produces a material fine and reputational damage to the international buyer base. The multiple compresses toward the low-teens appropriate for a no-growth, regulatory-overhang franchise. EPS of ~$1.55 on a 14–16x multiple yields $22–25, roughly 15–25% below current levels.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

This is a HOLD: the franchise quality is too high to avoid, but no catalyst or signal supports a buy.

**Reassessment timeline:** The next two quarterly prints (Q4 FY26 in roughly September 2026, Q1 FY27) are the natural reassessment points, specifically the US service-revenue and US-volume trend.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- US unit volume returns to positive YoY growth for two consecutive quarters, confirming the cyclical (not structural) interpretation of the claims softness.
- EPS estimate revisions turn meaningfully positive (>5% over 90 days) on re-accelerating volume, OR the stock de-rates below ~14x forward (~$24) while fundamentals remain intact, restoring genuine valuation asymmetry.
- Clustered open-market insider buying emerges, reversing the current uniform selling pattern.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- US volume decline accelerates beyond the current ~2% pace for two consecutive quarters, indicating a structural step-down.
- The DOJ AML investigation escalates to a quantified material liability or criminal proceeding.
- Management continues buybacks at elevated prices while organic revenue declines, signaling capital-allocation discipline has broken down.

## Conclusion

Copart is a financially impregnable, wide-moat franchise that has hit its first genuine growth stall in years. The static metrics (33% net margins, near-zero debt, $3.4B cash, a durable duopoly position) remain pristine. The dynamic picture has changed: US volumes are declining, the secular total-loss tailwind is being offset by a premium-driven claims headwind, and management has quietly conceded maturity through a historic pivot to buybacks (executed above today's price) and a 45% capex cut, all while insiders sell rather than buy. The ~38% de-rating has removed the most egregious overvaluation, but at ~18x forward on ~7% EPS growth with flat estimate revisions, the stock is fairly valued rather than cheap, and there is no identifiable catalyst to justify a higher multiple.

The decisive open question is cyclical-vs-structural on US claims, and the data do not yet answer it. Pairing that uncertainty with an unquantified DOJ AML tail and an absence of any positive forward signal (insider buying, estimate acceleration, volume recovery), the appropriate call is to hold a high-quality business while waiting for the volume trend and the DOJ matter to resolve. **Recommendation: HOLD.**
