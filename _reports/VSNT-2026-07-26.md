**Research Date: July 26, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Versant Media Group is the standalone company Comcast created to separate its declining NBCUniversal cable networks (MS NOW/MSNBC, CNBC, USA Network, Golf Channel, E!, SYFY, Oxygen) plus a set of digital platforms (GolfNow, Fandango, Rotten Tomatoes). It began regular-way trading January 5, 2026. Linear TV declines, and the pace is steepening (2025 network ratings fell 17% on top of an 8% drop in 2024; MVPD subscribers fell roughly 8% in each of the last two years); the investment question is whether Versant can harvest $1.5–2.0B of annual free cash flow long enough, and redeploy it into non-linear assets fast enough, to build something durable before the core shrinks below scale. That is a genuinely uncertain outcome, which is why the stock trades at ~5.5x trailing earnings.

The bull case rests on three legs: a capital-light model producing ~28% FCF margin (~$1.86B FCF on ~$6.7B revenue in FY25), a ~4% dividend supported by that cash flow, and a deliberate pivot into golf/participation (Full Swing, $530M, July 2026), FAST/OTA (Free TV Networks), and ticketing (Indy Cinema via Fandango), funded by divesting SportsEngine. The strongest confirming signal is a cluster of open-market director purchases in March 2026, most notably David Novak's ~$5.3M buy at $36.85, plus Hassell and Potter, near today's price level. That is uncommon conviction for a freshly-spun, out-of-favor name.

The bear case is that ~85% of revenue (distribution + advertising) is tied to structurally declining linear TV, the spin weakened Versant's carriage-negotiating leverage by stripping the ability to bundle with NBC/Bravo/Peacock, and management has essentially pre-announced a goodwill impairment (the $6.49B TV-networks goodwill unit cleared its year-end test by only "more than 10%"). Layered on top are ~$3.0B of new debt at a ~6.6% blended coupon, a permanent ~$200M/yr interest drag that did not exist pre-spin, standalone costs that make carve-out margins flattering, and a dual-class structure that hands Comcast CEO Brian Roberts a 33⅓% blocking vote despite negligible economics. The score trajectory (5.5 → 6.4 → 4.7) and the swing from BUY to AVOID in the prior report reflect this deterioration in visibility. We land on HOLD as an income-oriented position: the yield and FCF floor the downside, but the erosion, impairment overhang, and governance friction cap re-rating.

## Company Overview

Versant operates as a single reportable segment spanning four content markets: news/business (MS NOW, CNBC), sports (USA Network + Golf Channel, unified as "USA Sports"), entertainment/genre (E!, SYFY, Oxygen), and digital platforms (GolfNow, Fandango, Rotten Tomatoes; SportsEngine being divested). The sports rights portfolio is meaningful: WWE SmackDown (to 2029), NASCAR (to 2031), Premier League (2027-28), PGA Tour/Ryder Cup (2028-33), WNBA (2026-2036), and PAC-12 (from fall 2026).

FY2025 revenue mix: linear distribution 61% ($4.09B), advertising 24% ($1.58B), platforms 12% ($826M), content licensing 3% ($193M). The networks each carry scale in their competitive sets (~50M weekly viewers, 49–60M household reach), but that is scale in a contracting market. The spin left Versant unable to bundle these channels with NBC broadcast, Bravo, or Peacock in MVPD negotiations, an explicitly disclosed structural disadvantage given distribution is the largest revenue line.

The company was incorporated in Pennsylvania on May 1, 2025, and distributed one Versant share per 25 Comcast shares. Comcast's June 29, 2026 announcement that it will also spin off the remainder of NBCUniversal plus Sky reinforces the interpretation that Versant was the first and lower-quality tranche of a portfolio triage.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue has declined consistently: $7.45B (2023) → $7.06B (2024, −5.1%) → $6.69B (2025, −5.3%). Q1 2026 revenue was $1.69B, down 1.1% YoY, but that number flatters the underlying trend — it was helped by a one-time large content-licensing deal and by acquisitions, while core linear distribution fell 7.3% and advertising fell 5.2%. Platforms is the only growing segment (+3.9% in 2025, +9.5% in Q1 2026, driven by GolfNow transaction/tech fees).

Profitability is compressing. Net income fell from $1.54B (2023) to $1.36B (2024) to $930M (2025). Q1 2026 net income was $286M versus $367M, down 22%, with diluted EPS of $1.99 versus $2.55. Adjusted EBITDA declined from $3.06B to $2.84B to $2.42B (FY25 margin ~36%); Q1 2026 was $704M versus $757M. The 22% net-income drop is an early warning that standalone economics are weaker than the carve-out history implies: pre-separation results allocated Comcast costs and understate true standalone SG&A. Q1 2026 SG&A rose 14.3% YoY as the company absorbed public-company costs plus fees paid to NBCUniversal, which now sells Versant's domestic ad inventory for roughly two years.

Cash flow is the strength of the story. FY2025 operating cash flow was ~$2.0B against only $167M of capex, producing ~$1.86B FCF (~28% FCF margin). This is a capital-light model, and the FCF conversion versus $930M net income is well above 100%, reflecting substantial non-cash amortization.

The balance sheet carries new leverage from the spin: ~$3.0B of debt ($1.0B of 7.25% senior secured notes due 2031 plus $2.0B of Term Loans A and B), a ~6.6% weighted-average coupon (~67% fixed after $1.0B of swaps), and a $2.25B special dividend paid to Comcast as consideration for contributed assets. Net leverage is modest — roughly 1x gross against ~$2.4B EBITDA, well under the 3.5x first-lien covenant that begins Q3 2026. Q1 2026 cash was $1.19B with an undrawn $750M revolver. The ~$52M/quarter interest expense (~$200M annualized) is a new, permanent drag. Altman Z of 2.73 sits in the grey zone — not distress, but the new leverage layered onto a declining top line. The current ratio is 4.0.

Capital return: a $0.375/quarter dividend (~4.2% yield at the current $35.38) was initiated in Q1 2026, and a $1B buyback was authorized in March 2026 ($100M executed at ~$37.12, plus a $100M ASR in Q2). The tax-matters agreement caps open-market repurchases at less than 20% of shares over the two years post-spin to preserve tax-free treatment, limiting the buyback's pace.

## Growth Analysis

There is no organic growth thesis for the core business. Management explicitly expects continued subscriber losses, ratings declines, distribution-revenue erosion, and advertising migration to digital. The 17% 2025 ratings collapse — accelerating after the presidential inauguration — suggests the decline is steepening rather than stabilizing, which is the single most important negative variable.

The growth thesis, such as it is, is inorganic and early-stage: redeploy FCF into non-linear assets. The Full Swing golf-simulator acquisition ($530M, July 2026), Free TV Networks (FAST/OTA channels, January 2026), and Indy Cinema (via Fandango, December 2025) are the building blocks, funded partly by the SportsEngine divestiture (held-for-sale December 2025, mostly sold in Q2 2026 with an expected noncash tax charge). These are sensible but small relative to the ~$4.1B linear distribution base that is eroding ~7% per year. The arithmetic is unforgiving: platforms would need to compound at a high rate for many years to offset linear decline in absolute dollars.

Estimate revisions confirm the deterioration in visibility. Current-year EPS consensus fell from $6.99 to $4.27 over 90 days (−38.9%), and next-year from $7.73 to $6.69 (−13.5%). The near-term revisions show noise more than momentum — current-quarter estimates rose 11.4% over 30 days while next-quarter jumped an implausible 1,188% off a near-zero base — but the 90-day trend is clearly down, driven by standalone-cost recognition. The company has beaten twice (Q4 2025 by 26.4%, Q1 2026 by 9.5%), so consensus may be conservative, but two quarters is a thin track record. We do not treat this as a positive-inflection setup; the estimate direction over the meaningful window is negative.

## Valuation Assessment

At $35.38, Versant trades at ~5.5x trailing EPS ($6.45) and ~5.3x the $6.69 next-year consensus. Against Cable & Satellite peers, that is roughly in line to modestly above the group: Charter at 3.8x, Comcast at 4.7x (median 4.7x), with Cable One negative. The sector trades at low-single-digit multiples precisely because these are melting-ice-cube businesses; a low multiple here is the market correctly pricing terminal decline plus optionality on the pivot.

A low P/E is not, by itself, a reason to score valuation high — cheapness in structurally impaired businesses is a value trap absent a specific catalyst. The catalyst here (successful non-linear redeployment) is real but unproven and slow. The offsetting supports are concrete: a ~4% dividend covered several times over by FCF, ~$1.86B FCF against a ~$5.0B market cap (a ~37% FCF yield on a trailing basis, which is high even discounting for decline), and director open-market buying near these levels. Against those, the goodwill impairment overhang, the ~$200M annual interest drag, and falling out-year estimates argue the earnings base is lower and lower-quality than trailing figures suggest.

Analyst targets bracket the range: low $35.00, mean $44.00, median $44.50, high $52.00. On an inflection stock with rising estimates we would treat consensus as a floor, but that guardrail does not apply here — estimates are falling over the 90-day window and the growth is negative, so consensus targets are a genuine ceiling. We weight the FCF/yield support and net it against the structural erosion to a valuation score in the high-4s: real downside protection, limited upside optionality, not a compelling asymmetric buy.

## Competitive Landscape

Versant sits at the epicenter of cord-cutting. Its networks compete for shrinking MVPD carriage against streamers, social platforms, and FAST services, and for increasingly expensive sports rights against far larger players (Disney/ESPN, Netflix, Amazon, YouTube). Management's own MD&A concedes sports rights are "significantly more competitive and expensive," which pressures the one content category (sports) that still commands live audiences. The risk factors newly emphasize generative-AI competition — rivals using AI for lower-cost content and better ad targeting.

The competitive trajectory is deteriorating on the metric that matters: the spin structurally weakened carriage-negotiating leverage. Without NBC/Bravo/Peacock to bundle, renewals may come at less favorable rates, and distribution is 61% of revenue. The CNBC trademark — the crown-jewel business-news brand — is licensed from Comcast for only five years (extendable), a long-term overhang. The MSNBC→MS NOW rebrand and removal of NBCU branding introduce viewer-confusion risk. Platforms (GolfNow, Fandango) are competitively better positioned and growing, but they are ~12% of revenue. On balance, competitive position is worsening, consistent with the prior report's downgrade of this dimension to 3.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Structural, accelerating core decline (primary risk).** The 17% 2025 ratings drop suggests steepening, not stabilization. This directly threatens the FCF-harvest thesis on which the entire equity case depends.

**Goodwill impairment, essentially pre-announced.** Of $7.7B goodwill, ~$6.49B sits in the TV-networks unit, which cleared its year-end test by only "more than 10%." Management states plainly that continued cash-flow declines "are expected to result in reduced fair values… which could result in an impairment." The July 1, 2026 annual test is the near-term watch item. A ~$6.5B goodwill balance against a ~$5.0B market cap means a large writedown is plausible. Non-cash, but it would dent sentiment.

**New debt and covenant.** $3.0B of debt, a 3.5x first-lien covenant beginning Q3 2026, and ~$200M/yr of new interest expense. Covenant headroom is comfortable now (~1x) but shrinks if EBITDA erodes faster than expected.

**Governance overhang.** Brian Roberts (Comcast's CEO) holds 100% of Class B stock carrying a non-dilutable 33⅓% of combined voting power plus veto rights over mergers, asset sales, and charter changes — despite negligible economic ownership. This can entrench management and block a value-maximizing takeover, and is an unusual arrangement for a company spun out of Comcast.

**Tax/indemnity constraints.** Uncapped indemnification to Comcast if Versant breaks the spin's tax-free status, plus two-year restrictions on M&A, equity issuance, and buybacks — limiting strategic flexibility during the pivot.

**Standalone-cost trap.** Carve-out margins overstate true standalone earnings power; the Q1 SG&A jump, new interest, NBCU ad-sales fees, and the eventual end of the transition services agreement all push run-rate profitability below the historical figures.

Many of these risks are partly priced (hence ~5.5x), but the impairment timing, the accelerating ratings decline, and the governance blocking rights are specific, near-term, and asymmetric to the downside, which keeps the risk score below 5.

### Options Market Signal

ATM IV of 40.7% is essentially in line with 1-year historical volatility of 40.5% (spread 0.2%), so options are fairly priced rather than cheap. The put/call OI ratio of 2.12 and a term structure in backwardation (near-term 40.7% > long-term 36.6%) indicate the market is pricing near-term stress, consistent with the July 1 goodwill test and the coming Q2 print (SportsEngine sale charge, standalone-cost recognition). ATM IV collapsed from 254.6% to 40.7% since the June snapshot as the immediate post-spin dislocation normalized. Put/call skew of −2.2% is balanced. Net read: the options market is defensive but not panicked; the elevated put OI is a modest confirmation of the downside catalysts rather than a strong directional signal.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Versant is an undermanaged cash machine being deliberately re-potted. FCF funds the dividend, buybacks (capped by tax rules but real), and acquisitions in golf/participation, FAST/OTA, and ticketing. Director open-market buying near $36–38 signals the board sees value at those levels. If the linear base decays at a manageable single-digit pace while platforms compound and the M&A pivot builds a non-linear franchise, the stock re-rates from ~5x to ~7x on stabilizing FCF, plus you collect the dividend. Bull target ~$52 (analyst high, ~7.5x $6.69 next-year EPS with a re-rating on pivot traction).

**Bear case.** Ratings decline accelerates past 17%, carriage renewals come in weak due to lost bundling leverage, the July goodwill test triggers a multi-billion writedown, and standalone costs compress FCF faster than the pivot adds it. Estimates keep falling (already −39% current-year over 90 days), the dividend looks less secure over a multi-year horizon, and the multiple stays at trough. Bear target ~$27 (the 52-week low, ~4x a lowered ~$6.50 earnings base with sentiment impaired), roughly 24% below current.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD (income_hold).** The 4% covered dividend and ~28% FCF margin floor the downside; accelerating structural decline, the impairment overhang, and falling out-year estimates cap the upside. This is a position to own for yield and FCF harvest, not appreciation.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Linear distribution and advertising declines moderating to low-single-digits for two consecutive quarters (stabilization of the core), OR
- Platforms + acquired non-linear assets reaching a scale where consolidated revenue growth turns flat-to-positive, OR
- The July 1 goodwill test passing without material impairment AND out-year EPS revisions turning positive, OR
- A pullback toward the high-$20s that widens the FCF-yield cushion materially.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Ratings/subscriber declines accelerating beyond the 2025 pace (e.g., ratings down >20% or subs down >10%), OR
- A carriage renewal at materially unfavorable rates confirming lost negotiating leverage, OR
- Dividend coverage deteriorating or the dividend cut, OR
- EBITDA erosion pushing first-lien leverage toward the 3.5x covenant.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next natural checkpoints are the Q2 2026 earnings print (SportsEngine sale charge, first clean read on standalone SG&A) and the July 1, 2026 annual goodwill test result.

## Conclusion

Versant is a well-defined melting-ice-cube business with an above-average balance sheet, high FCF conversion, and a management team actively redeploying cash into non-linear assets. The March director buying cluster and the ~4% covered dividend are legitimate reasons not to abandon the name. But the core is declining at an accelerating pace, the spin weakened the very negotiating leverage the cash-flow thesis depends on, management has pre-flagged a probable goodwill impairment against a book nearly as large as the market cap, and out-year estimates are falling. The low multiple reflects those facts rather than a mispricing. We rate VSNT a HOLD for income, with a base-case view that the stock trades in a $35–44 range near-term (consensus mean $44, current $35.38), meaningful downside to ~$27 if decline accelerates, and upside to ~$52 only if the pivot demonstrably works. Own it for the yield and the option on re-potting; do not underwrite growth.
