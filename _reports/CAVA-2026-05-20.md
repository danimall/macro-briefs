## Executive Summary

CAVA Group delivered an exceptional Q1 FY2026 — revenue grew 32.2% YoY to $438.3M, same-restaurant sales accelerated to +9.7% (6.8% traffic-driven), and management raised full-year guidance across all metrics. The 459-unit Mediterranean fast-casual chain has validated its geographic portability, with newer markets outperforming internal targets. Zero debt, $403M in cash/investments, and a freshly upsized $150M credit facility provide ample runway for the planned 75-77 new openings this year.

The fundamental problem is valuation. At ~$9.6B market cap on $63.7M adjusted net income (trailing), the stock trades at approximately 150x earnings and 52x forward EV/EBITDA. Peer median P/E is 28.3x. The premium requires belief in CAVA reaching 1,000+ restaurants by 2032 with sustained unit economics — a plausible but execution-heavy path that leaves no room for operational stumbles, macro deterioration, or competitive encroachment. Short interest at 11.7% and the 17% drawdown from the April 2026 highs ($98.79) suggest institutional skepticism is already embedded.

The stock warrants holding for investors with existing positions given the quality of the growth trajectory, but initiating new positions at this valuation carries materially asymmetric downside risk.

## Company Overview

CAVA operates 459 fast-casual Mediterranean restaurants across 29 states and Washington, D.C., offering customizable bowls and pitas with chef-curated ingredients. The company is vertically integrated, manufacturing signature dips, spreads, and dressing bases in two facilities (Maryland, Virginia). This production infrastructure is difficult to replicate and ensures quality consistency across a rapidly expanding footprint.

Key structural advantages: first-mover scale in Mediterranean fast-casual (no comparable national competitor), 37.9% digital revenue mix, dual makeline architecture supporting both in-store and digital orders, and a $3M+ average unit volume that generates approximately 25% restaurant-level profit margins.

The company targets 1,000+ restaurants by 2032 and is currently executing on 75-77 net openings in FY2026. Drive-thru pickup is being added to a growing portion of new builds, and a $10M investment in Hyphen Technologies (automated makeline robotics) represents early-stage operational optionality.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue & Profitability:**

Q1 FY2026 revenue of $438.3M (+32.2% YoY) beat consensus by nearly $20M. Same-restaurant sales of +9.7% dramatically outpaced the 6.2% estimate, with the composition being the critical detail: 6.8% from guest traffic growth and only 2.9% from price/mix. Traffic-driven comps in an environment where most restaurant peers report traffic declines is a strong signal of genuine brand demand.

Adjusted EBITDA reached $61.7M (14.1% margin, up from 13.5% prior year). Restaurant-level profit margin held flat at 25.1%, with occupancy leverage (6.9% vs 7.4%) offset by rising third-party delivery costs (other operating at 13.3%). Food cost improved slightly to 29.1% of CAVA revenue despite tariff pressures on olive oil, sugar, rice, and lamb.

Full-year FY2025: revenue $1.18B (+22.4%), adjusted EBITDA $152.8M (+21%), adjusted net income $63.7M (+27% vs FY2024 adjusted).

**Balance Sheet:**

Zero financial debt. Cash and investments of $403M. Credit facility upsized to $150M (undrawn) with maturity extended to 2031. Current ratio 2.65x.

**Cash Flow:**

FY2025 operating cash flow was $184.8M, but capex of $158.7M for new restaurant builds consumed most of it, leaving FCF of only $26.1M. Q1 FY2026: $64.1M OCF, ~$49M capex, $15.5M FCF. Management expects FY2026 capex to exceed FY2025. The company is fully reinvesting in growth — no dividends, no buybacks, and minimal free cash flow by design.

The Altman Z-Score of 2.85 (grey zone) reflects the heavy asset base relative to current profitability, not financial distress. The Piotroski F-Score of 5/5 confirms operational health across all measurable dimensions.

## Growth Analysis

**Unit Growth:** 459 restaurants today vs. 1,000+ target by 2032 implies a ~12% CAGR in units over 6 years. The 2025 cohort is trending above $3M AUV, and newer markets (Cincinnati, St. Louis, Columbus, South Florida, Detroit) are performing above internal productivity targets. This geographic portability evidence is the strongest validation of the long-term unit story.

**Same-Store Sales:** After decelerating from 13.4% (FY2024) to 4.0% (FY2025), the Q1 2026 reacceleration to 9.7% is meaningful. Management guided conservatively at 4.5-6.5% for full-year FY2026. The traffic component (6.8%) indicates genuine demand pull rather than price-taking.

**Menu Innovation Pipeline:** Successful steak launch (2024) and recent salmon addition demonstrate the ability to drive incremental traffic through culinary innovation. A tiered loyalty program (Sea, Sand, Sun) launched in 2025 builds first-party data for personalization.

**Estimate Revisions:** Current quarter estimates revised +9.1% over 90 days; current year estimates modestly down (-6.8% over 90 days) likely reflecting conservatism around tariff uncertainty. The 30-day trend is positive across all periods (+2.7% current quarter, +1.9% current year, +2.5% next year). This is steady positive momentum, not the explosive +30% revision velocity that would signal a strong BUY on revision grounds alone.

## Valuation Assessment

This is where the investment case breaks down for new buyers. At $78.12 per share:

- Trailing P/E: ~145x (on $0.54 EPS per Phase 1 data)
- Forward P/E: ~145x (on FY2026 consensus ~$0.53)
- Forward EV/EBITDA: ~52x (on raised FY2026 midpoint of ~$186M)
- Peer median P/E: 28.3x

Even using next-year estimates of $0.73 EPS, the stock trades at ~107x — still nearly 4x the peer median. The closest comparable in growth profile is BROS (Dutch Bros) at 83x, but BROS operates a company-owned and franchise model with lower capital intensity per unit.

To justify the current valuation through a DCF lens: assuming CAVA reaches 1,000 restaurants by 2032 at $3M AUV with 25% restaurant margins, that implies ~$750M in restaurant-level profit. After corporate overhead and taxes, net income might reach $350-400M. Discounting at 10% WACC, the terminal value component dominates — meaning investors are paying today for outcomes 6+ years away with significant execution uncertainty.

The market cap implies the market expects CAVA to become a $2B+ revenue business with mid-teens net margins. That's achievable, but it requires everything to go right for an extended period.

## Competitive Landscape

CAVA's competitive position is genuinely strong. No other Mediterranean fast-casual concept operates at national scale. Sweetgreen (salads), Chipotle (Mexican), and local Mediterranean operators are indirect competitors, but none combine CAVA's vertical integration, Mediterranean specificity, and demonstrated unit economics.

The Chipotle comparison is apt structurally: both scaled a single cuisine concept nationally with strong unit economics, customization, and ingredient quality positioning. CAVA at 459 restaurants today vs. Chipotle at 3,500+ shows the addressable whitespace.

Traffic growth across all income cohorts (with lower-income consumers leading) contradicts concerns about CAVA being a premium-only brand. The brand is demonstrating broad demographic appeal, which is critical for reaching 1,000+ units in diverse markets.

Risk factors to competitive position: low barriers to concept replication (Mediterranean food is not proprietary), potential for Chipotle or other scaled operators to introduce Mediterranean offerings, and the inherent challenge of maintaining quality consistency while tripling the restaurant count.

## Risk Assessment

**Valuation Fragility (Primary Risk):** At ~145x trailing earnings, a single quarter of SSS deceleration, margin miss, or guidance reduction would likely produce a 15-25% drawdown. The Q3 FY2025 EPS miss (-5.8%) was modest, yet the stock declined from ~$90 in late July to ~$43 by late November — a 52% drawdown. This volatility profile is inherent to hyper-growth multiples.

**Tariff/Input Cost Pressure:** Management explicitly flagged tariff impacts on olive oil (single-source supplier with specific quality requirements), sugar, rice, beef, lamb, and paper products. Q1 food cost improved, but the full impact of tariff escalation has not yet hit the P&L. The gap between current food costs and where they're heading over the next 2-3 quarters represents a margin headwind that could compress restaurant-level profits by 100-200 basis points.

**Executive Departures:** COO departed September 2025, CLO departed May 2026. For a company opening 75+ restaurants per year, operational leadership continuity is critical. Doug Thompson appears to be the COO replacement (January 2026 start date), but limited track record visibility.

**Consumer Spending Environment:** While CAVA's Q1 traffic data contradicts the consumer weakness narrative, the company operates at a $12-15 average check — positioning it between QSR and casual dining. In an H2 2026 consumer weakness scenario, even resilient concepts could see traffic decelerate. CAVA's broad income-cohort appeal cuts both ways: lower-income consumers who drove Q1 growth are the most vulnerable to macro deterioration.

**Short Interest:** 11.7% of float with 5.1 days to cover represents meaningful bearish conviction from institutional investors. While squeeze dynamics exist on the upside, the elevated short base reflects informed skepticism about sustainability of current growth rates at current valuations.

## Conclusion

CAVA is a high-quality growth business with validated unit economics, genuine competitive differentiation, and a long runway for unit expansion. The Q1 FY2026 results were exceptional — traffic-led 9.7% SSS growth across all income cohorts is rare in the current environment and confirms brand strength.

The constraint is price. At ~145x trailing P/E and 52x forward EV/EBITDA, the stock prices in near-perfect execution over a multi-year horizon. The 52% drawdown from July-November 2025 following a single-quarter EPS miss demonstrates the volatility inherent to this valuation. With tariff exposure on key ingredients and consumer uncertainty heading into H2 2026, the risk of an execution stumble is elevated.

HOLD for existing investors who believe in the multi-year thesis and can tolerate 30%+ drawdowns. At today's price, new capital earns a better risk-adjusted return elsewhere until either the price compresses or the growth trajectory further validates the 1,000+ unit buildout.
