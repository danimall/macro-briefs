## Executive Summary

Rithm Capital is three businesses stapled together at very different stages of maturity. Newrez — the origination and servicing platform built around $850B of servicing UPB and $11.09B of owned MSRs — produced $363.7M of pre-tax income in 1H'26, equal to 151% of consolidated pre-tax income, on roughly $5.5B of segment equity. Genesis, the residential transitional lending book, grew 29.5% in six months and earned $60.2M pre-tax. Sculptor/Crestline asset management, the Elecor (formerly Paramount) Class A office portfolio acquired in December 2025, and a nascent annuity platform each consumed capital on a GAAP basis in the first half. The market is pricing that composite at 0.81x a derived book value of $12.33, an 18.7% discount.

The discount has identifiable causes rather than being a simple sentiment gap. Book value per share has fallen for two consecutive quarters ($12.66 → $12.51 → $12.33) because 1H'26 declared dividends of $350.1M exceeded net income attributable to Rithm of $159.0M by $191.1M; accumulated deficit widened from −$19.9M to −$226.4M. GAAP diluted EPS was $0.04 in Q2 against a $0.25 dividend. The company's non-GAAP Earnings Available for Distribution covers the dividend roughly 2.0-2.4x, but the largest add-backs are now $186.2M of purchase-accounting depreciation (1H'26, of which $131.4M is CRE) and deferred taxes, and REIT taxable income — the figure that legally governs the distribution — is not disclosed in any filing. The marginal cost of permanent capital rose to 8.50% for the May 2026 senior notes and 8.75% for the two most recent preferred series, while the Fed cut 75bp during 2025. No shares were repurchased at 0.81x book, and across roughly 50 Form 4 line items from January through August 2026 there were no open-market insider purchases.

We rate the shares HOLD on income grounds. The 10% yield plus modest recovery toward the low end of the historical price-to-book range supports a probability-weighted total return in the low teens over twelve months, which is roughly fair compensation for a levered hybrid whose two largest asset marks — an $11.09B Level 3 MSR carried at an 8.0% discount rate (down 40bp in six months) and $5.13B of trophy office at acquisition cost with 14.1% at-share vacancy — are both estimate-dependent. Existing holders are paid to wait. New capital has no urgency and no dated catalyst.

## Company Overview

Rithm describes itself as a global alternative asset manager focused on real estate, credit and financial services; its GICS classification as a mortgage REIT no longer captures the structure. As of June 30, 2026 it reports five segments, up from four at year-end, after carving Commercial Real Estate out of Asset Management in Q1.

**Origination & Servicing (Newrez).** Servicing-for-others UPB of $253.7B (from $242.8B at 12/31/25) plus owned MSRs on $596.8B of UPB carried at $11.09B. The genuine competitive asset here is the closed loop: Rithm owns the MSR, services 95.2% of it in-house (up from 90.9% at year-end), originates recapture volume against it (FY2025 recapture 18.9% overall, 36.8% on Ginnie Mae), and finances it through a $7.54B MSR-secured note facility. Four origination channels funded $63.3B in FY2025, with correspondent at roughly 66% of volume ($42.5B of $63.3B).

**Residential Transitional Lending (Genesis).** Short-duration construction, bridge and renovation loans; carrying value including consolidated entities grew from $3.915B to $5.071B in six months at a 9.7% weighted-average yield.

**Asset Management.** Sculptor (2023), Crestline (December 2025) and the Rithm Advisers, managing roughly $59-63B. Management-reported AUM was ~$63B at 12/31/25 per the 10-K and ~$59B at 3/31/26 per the Q1 release; whether that gap reflects outflows, a definitional change, or the CRE reclassification is unresolved.

**Investment Portfolio.** Residential loans, non-Agency securities, Excess MSRs on $47.9B of UPB, servicer advances, and a single-family rental book now explicitly in wind-down (676 properties held-for-sale versus 21 at year-end).

**Commercial Real Estate (Elecor).** The former Paramount Group, acquired December 19, 2025 for approximately $1.8B in total consideration and rebranded in April 2026. New York and San Francisco Class A office carried at $5.130B net against roughly $3.997B of segment debt.

### Balance sheet and liquidity

Total assets $54.109B, liabilities $44.660B, equity $9.051B including $1.633B of preferred carrying value. Total debt of $36.015B splits $18.925B recourse and $17.090B non-recourse. Derived recourse leverage is **2.75x common equity** (2.22x including preferred) — modest for the sector. Cash and equivalents $1.664B; $11.8B of undrawn (uncommitted, collateral-dependent) capacity; regulated servicers hold $1.7B of available liquidity, $1.1B above regulatory minimums, improved from $0.6B at year-end. The unsecured notes' 120% unencumbered-asset covenant is in compliance.

The pressure points: **$13.135B of debt matures July-December 2026, of which $10.414B is recourse**; $13.7B of secured financing carries margin exposure; and $6.6B face of MSR financing is subject to mandatory monthly repayment if the balance exceeds market value times maximum LTV. Most of the maturity wall is warehouse and repo that revolves in the normal course, and management states it expects to refinance, but it is the single largest liquidity dependency in the filings.

Operating cash flow was −$1,292.1M in FY2025 and +$274.8M in 1H'26. Neither figure is interpretable as earnings quality: 1H'26 included −$33.08B of originations for sale against +$32.27B of sale proceeds, so the line measures pipeline timing. It must not be annualized in either direction.

## Growth Analysis

Growth is real in two places and unproven in two others.

**Genesis** is the clearest engine: Q1'26 originations of $1.6B were up 80% year over year, sponsor count reached 266 (+40%), new sponsors funded rose 118 (+258%), and segment pre-tax income grew 47% to $60.2M in 1H'26. Committed LTVs look disciplined — bridge 68.0%, construction 73.2% LTC / 62.1% LTARV — and weighted-average original life of 21.7 months means the book self-liquidates and reprices quickly. The counterweight is speed and geography: 36.8% California concentration (mainly Los Angeles), 12.0% Florida, unfunded construction commitments up from $1.8B to $2.2B, and −$25.5M of instrument-specific credit-risk fair value adjustments in 1H'26. Absolute 90+ balances fell ($100.9M → $96.7M) as the book grew 30%, which is the stronger read; the ratio improvement from 3.7% to 2.5% is partly a denominator effect.

**Newrez** is growing servicing-for-others UPB (+4.5% in six months) and origination volume (+31% year over year in Q1), but with margin compression. Correspondent, the lowest-margin channel at 0.51-0.52% gain-on-sale, is roughly 66-70% of volume; DTC margin fell 102bp. Existing home sales were essentially flat at 4.08M in 2025 with median resale price growth decelerating to +0.4%, and the mortgage rate backdrop — a 30-year that ended 2025 at 6.27% — caps purchase-volume growth. Growth here is share and scale, not market expansion.

**Asset Management** raised and deployed credibly in Q1 (>$1B committed to Real Estate Fund V, >$2B deployed, a ~$400M CLO, >$500M of Crestline BDC commitments) but has not yet produced through-cycle GAAP profit inside Rithm, and the AUM print moved the wrong way ($63B → ~$59B) without a reconciliation.

**Elecor** growth is lease-up dependent: NYC lease occupancy +4.7% year over year and >350k sq ft of new leasing (74% San Francisco) are encouraging against 14.1% at-share vacancy and 537,500 at-share sq ft (~8.6% of portfolio rents) expiring in 2026.

**Insurance** is the smallest business and the longest-dated: CL Life holds $1.120B of investments (commercial mortgage at 9.8%, private credit at 10.3%) against $1.144B of interest-sensitive liabilities at a 6% weighted-average crediting rate, with 90% of the annuity risk ceded. That is the architecture of an annuity-funded credit flywheel, but at this scale it cannot move consolidated earnings for several years.

Consensus reflects this composite: current-year EAD of $2.28 and next-year $2.36 imply 3.5% growth. Estimate revisions are mixed — current quarter −5.0% over 30 days and −7.8% over 90 days, next quarter −2.6%/+1.4%, but the current and next full years each up ~1.1%. The near-term cuts and flat out-year path do not support a growth re-rating, and the +30% revision threshold that would argue for aggressive positioning is nowhere in sight.

## Valuation Assessment

Four anchors, pointing in slightly different directions.

**Price to book.** $10.015 against derived BVPS of $12.33 is 0.812x, an 18.7% discount. Over the trailing year the shares have traded within a $8.43-$12.74 range. The current multiple is not at a distressed extreme. The discount is not unexplained: book value is falling, and its two largest components are estimate-driven. The $11.09B MSR is a Level 3 asset whose weighted-average discount rate fell from 8.4% to 8.0% in six months, contributing to a +$409.8M valuation-input gain in 1H'26 (MSR discount rates did compress market-wide as spreads tightened, so this is not an idiosyncratic assumption). The $5.130B office carrying value reflects asset-acquisition accounting at a ~$1.8B purchase price with no goodwill, meaning any value shortfall surfaces as property impairment rather than goodwill write-off, and no independent appraisal evidence is available.

**Earnings multiple.** 4.4x current-year and 4.2x next-year EAD looks arresting until you note that EAD now adds back $186.2M of office-heavy depreciation in the half. On trailing GAAP the stock is 16.9x. Neither number is the right one; the honest statement is that the true economic multiple lies between them and cannot be pinned down without REIT taxable income, which is not disclosed.

**Yield.** ~10.0% at $10.015. Against the mortgage REIT peer set, this is a *discount* to the 13.3% median yield (STWD 12.1%, BXMT 13.6%, ARR 17.4%, ABR 13.3%). Peer P/E medians (63.5x) are not usable given BXMT at 155x and TWO at negative earnings. On the metric the sector is actually priced on, RITM is not cheap relative to peers — it is priced as a higher-quality, more diversified vehicle, which is defensible given Newrez's 19% operating ROE and 2.75x recourse leverage but removes the "screaming value" argument.

**Analyst targets.** Eleven analysts, four strong buy and seven buy, no holds; mean $13.20 (+31.8%), median $13.00, low $12.50, high $14.50. Piper Sandler cut to $12.50 from $14.00 on July 2 while maintaining Overweight. Every published target sits at or above derived book value, which means the Street's bull case is essentially "book value, eventually." Unanimous positive coverage on a stock that has fallen over the trailing year is a caution rather than confirmation — the view is already crowded, and targets that assume mean-reversion to book have not been marked to a book value that is itself declining.

Netting these: the shares are moderately cheap on asset value, expensively cheap on earnings depending on the metric chosen, and unremarkable on yield versus peers. There is a margin of safety in the book discount, but no dated catalyst to close it, and management is not treating the discount as an opportunity — no repurchases in 1H'26 despite management's own claim of an 18.6% EAD ROE. Valuation scores 5.3: mildly supportive, insufficient on its own.

## Competitive Landscape

Newrez's advantage is structural and hard to replicate: MSR ownership, in-house servicing at 95.2% of owned MSRs, recapture origination against the same book, and one of the few scaled non-bank MSR-backed term financing platforms ($6.6-6.8B face). Regulatory capital requirements — FHFA/Ginnie Mae tangible net worth, a TNW/TA ratio ≥6%, base liquidity formulas, an incremental buffer for issuers above $50B of servicing UPB, and Ginnie Mae's 6% Risk-Based Capital Ratio — function as a moat against smaller entrants while imposing a permanent capital tax that the 10-K acknowledges "could constrain our operations and adversely affect our returns."

The trajectory is mixed. Servicing scale and in-house share are improving. Origination economics are deteriorating: consolidated gain-on-sale down 8bp, DTC down 102bp, and a channel mix skewed to the lowest-margin correspondent business. The competitive map also consolidated against Rithm: Rocket, now successor by merger to Mr. Cooper, services Rithm's $47.9B of Excess MSR UPB and 20.0% of its issued non-Agency securities, with Onity servicing another 18.0%. Rithm's largest servicing counterparties are simultaneously its largest origination competitors, and Rocket/Mr. Cooper now offers comparable origination-plus-servicing integration at similar scale, intensifying competition for bulk MSR purchases and correspondent flow.

Against the alternative managers Rithm aspires to be compared with, the gap is unclosed. Sculptor plus Crestline plus the Advisers manage $59-63B, but the segment lost money on a GAAP basis in both 1H'25 and 1H'26 and its annual profitability depends on Q4 incentive crystallization. Until that segment demonstrates through-cycle GAAP earnings inside Rithm, the sum-of-the-parts case for closing the book discount rests on Newrez.

## Risk Assessment

**1. Rate sensitivity flipped and became sharply asymmetric.** From the 10-Q Item 3 disclosures:

| Parallel shift | 6/30/26 book impact | 12/31/25 |
|---|---|---|
| +50bp | **+$197.4M** | −$41.2M |
| −25bp | −$150.0M | −$64.5M |
| −50bp | **−$334.3M** | −$185.6M |

A 50bp rally now costs roughly 4.9% of common equity, or $0.60 per share, versus $0.33 six months ago, and the down-move loss is 1.7x the up-move gain. This positions the book to benefit from higher rates and to lose from the easing path consensus and Rithm's own MD&A reference (the 10-K MD&A noted two further cuts were projected for 2026). These are static parallel-shift estimates holding the hedge book constant, and Rithm repositions actively (swap notional fell from $2.476B to $0.314B in six months), so treat them as a snapshot. Lower rates would also lift origination volume and gain-on-sale, partly offsetting through the income statement.

**2. Hedge effectiveness.** In 1H'26, MSR valuation-input gains of +$409.8M were more than offset by −$581.6M of MSR economic hedge losses (interest-rate futures −$435.7M, TBAs −$90.4M), a net −$171.8M and the direct cause of $0.04 GAAP EPS in Q2. An economic hedge is supposed to lose when the hedged asset gains; the question is magnitude, and a 1.4x ratio suggests either over-hedging or duration/basis slippage between swap futures and MSR prepayment-driven duration. The direction of the $12.775B Eris futures position at 6/30/26 is not disclosed, so this remains a hypothesis about hedge construction rather than a conclusion.

**3. Elecor office concentration with unindemnified legal overhang.** Paramount is subject to an SEC Division of Enforcement investigation into prior disclosures on executive compensation, perquisites, use of corporate assets, related-party transactions, conflicts and possible control failures, and Rithm disclosed it has **no indemnification rights** against Paramount for this matter. Neither timing nor magnitude is disclosed. JV partners hold forced-sale rights at 712 Fifth Avenue, One Market Plaza and 300 Mission Street. 60 Wall Street (5% interest) has been out of service since June 2022. At-share vacancy was 14.1% and 2026 at-share expirations 537,500 sq ft as of 12/31/25, with no updated mid-year figure available.

**4. Refinancing and margin mechanics.** $10.414B of recourse debt matures by 12/31/26; $13.7B of secured financing is margin-callable; $6.6B face of MSR financing carries mandatory monthly LTV-driven repayment; agency RMBS repo runs 30-90 days.

**5. Single-vendor servicing migration.** Newrez signed a January 2026 SaaS agreement with Valon Technologies to migrate *all* servicing operations — roughly $850B of UPB — onto Valon's platform over approximately two years. Valon Mortgage separately subservices 3.4% of Rithm's owned MSRs. Migration execution risk sits across the company's largest earnings engine.

**6. Credit gauges that moved the wrong way.** Servicer-advance non-recovery provision rose to $127.5M / 4.4% from $121.5M / 3.8%, with foreclosure advances up 13.6% to $1.070B. Residential mortgage loans 90+ past due rose to 10.7% of UPB from 7.0%, though that ratio is substantially a mix effect as performing loans were securitized out of a book that shrank from $5.809B to $4.293B. The legacy Marcus consumer book has $119.0M of $204.1M UPB 90+ delinquent, already carried at $11.8M.

**7. Structural items.** Series D resets November 15, 2026 (~$14M/year incremental). REIT qualification depends partly on a private letter ruling covering Excess MSRs. Investment Company Act §3(c)(5)(C)-reliant subsidiaries exceed 60% of adjusted total assets. The Sculptor Tax Receivable Agreement carries $231.4M of undiscounted future payments computed without regard to whether Sculptor can use the tax assets. Rithm has guaranteed $129.1M of fund commitments nominally due from current and former employees, never called to date.

**8. Verification gaps that should not be treated as settled.** REIT taxable income is undisclosed, so no definitive dividend-sustainability statement is possible. The Q2 EAD of $0.60 is externally reported only. The $63B → $59B AUM change is unreconciled. An Item 4.02 non-reliance 8-K from July 2024 was not read (disclosure controls were assessed effective at 6/30/26 with no material ICFR changes in the quarter). The First Amendment to the 2023 Omnibus Incentive Plan was not read, leaving incremental potential dilution unquantified. Conversion mechanics of the Class B Profits Units of Rithm Capital Management LLC — an internally managed REIT whose executives hold profits interests in a management entity — are undetermined and are not in the reported 568.3M diluted share count.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV of 22.1% against 24.2% one-year realized volatility is fair-to-slightly-cheap (spread −2.1%), with a 20th-percentile IV reading within the chain, a balanced −1.5% put/call skew, a flat term structure (22.1% near versus 21.9% far), a 0.36 put/call volume ratio and 0.62 put/call open-interest ratio. The nearest expiry prices a ±3.2% move; the November tenor prices ±10.2%. Short interest is 7.5% of float at 7.4 days to cover.

The reading is complacency with a mild call tilt. The options market is not pricing the Item 3 rate asymmetry or the November Series D reset. For an existing holder who wants to keep the dividend but cap downside, longer-dated protection is not expensively priced relative to realized volatility. The absence of put demand also means informed positioning is not corroborating the bear case, which tempers how much weight to place on the fundamental concerns.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case (target $12.50, +24.8% price, ~+34.8% total return)

Newrez is worth more than the whole company's market capitalization implies. On $5.5B of segment equity earning a 19% annualized operating ROE, that business alone generates roughly $1.0B of pre-tax operating income annually; capitalized conservatively it approaches or exceeds the entire $5.6B equity market value, making Genesis, Sculptor/Crestline, the investment portfolio and the office book effectively free options. Genesis compounds at 30%+ with 68% bridge LTVs and 21.7-month duration. Elecor's GAAP loss is 190% non-cash D&A; pre-depreciation it earns ~$62M semi-annually, and San Francisco leasing momentum plus a completed SASB refinancing of 1325 Avenue of the Americas show the asset class is financeable at a distressed vintage. If the long end stays elevated, Rithm's flipped rate positioning adds to book rather than subtracting. Q4 Sculptor incentive crystallization can swing Asset Management from a $14.7M half-year loss to a $138M quarter, as it did in Q4'25. The dividend is covered roughly 2.4x on management's metric and the balance sheet carries only 2.75x recourse leverage. Recovery to 1.0x book plus a year of dividends is the Street's case, and it does not require anything to improve — only for the discount to close.

### Bear Case (target $8.00, −20.1% price, roughly −12% total return with partial dividend)

Book value is the anchor and it is eroding at ~1.4% per quarter because the dividend is being funded from capital on a GAAP basis. Extended twelve months, BVPS approaches $11.90 before any impairment. The quality of that book is the issue: $11.09B of Level 3 MSRs benefiting from a 40bp discount-rate compression, $5.130B of Class A office at acquisition cost with 14.1% vacancy, an SEC enforcement matter Rithm cannot pass back to the seller, and JV partners who can force sales at times not of Rithm's choosing. If the Fed cuts 50bp, book takes a $0.60/share hit; if it cuts 100bp the hit compounds while origination margins in a 66-70%-correspondent mix cannot offset it. Preferred costs are ratcheting — 8.75% on the last two series, Series D resetting to ~10.1% in November, preferred dividends already +30.6% year over year — which transfers economics from common to preferred at precisely the moment the common trades below book. Management is paid on EAD ROE, a metric that became structurally easier to hit as $186.2M of purchase-accounting D&A entered the add-backs, and received $36.7M of compensation actually paid in a year GAAP earnings fell 32%. Nobody inside the company bought stock at 0.81x book and the company repurchased nothing. If EAD compresses toward the dividend — through a weak Sculptor year, an office impairment, or a rate rally — the distribution becomes the adjustable variable, and a mortgage REIT that cuts its dividend re-rates toward 0.65x a lower book.

## Conclusion

Rithm is a genuinely profitable mortgage servicing and origination franchise wrapped in three less-seasoned businesses, priced at 0.81x a book value that has fallen for two consecutive quarters. The bull argument — Newrez alone at a 19% operating ROE on $5.5B of equity is worth much of the market capitalization, so everything else comes free — is arithmetically coherent. The reason it has not worked is that the free options carry costs the market can see: $186.2M of 1H'26 purchase-accounting-heavy depreciation that pushes GAAP earnings below the dividend, an SEC enforcement matter Rithm cannot pass back to the seller, a 40bp MSR discount-rate compression doing part of the work in the largest Level 3 asset, and a rate sensitivity that now loses $0.60 per share on a 50bp rally against a consensus easing path.

The governance and capital-allocation evidence is the least reassuring part of the file. Paying 8.5% for senior debt and 8.75% for preferred, with Series D resetting toward 10.1% in November, while the common trades 19% below book and no shares were repurchased, transfers economics from common holders to permanent-capital holders. Management is paid primarily on EAD ROE, the metric whose add-backs expanded most as Elecor entered the consolidation. Across roughly 50 Form 4 line items in 2026, not one open-market purchase.

Holders are paid roughly 10% to wait for a discount that has no dated catalyst to close, backed by 2.75x recourse leverage, $1.1B of regulated-entity liquidity above minimums, and a core business earning close to 19% on its allocated equity. That is a reasonable proposition for income exposure and an unreasonable one for new capital seeking asymmetry. **HOLD** — own it for the yield if you already do, and let either book-value stabilization with a repurchase, or a price near $8.75-9.00, make the case for adding.
