## Executive Summary

Eaton delivered the operating quarter the bulls needed and the market repriced immediately. Q2 2026 revenue of $8,531M (+21%, 14% organic) beat the $8.13B consensus, adjusted EPS of $3.15 beat $3.07, and management raised full-year organic growth to 11-13% from 9-11% and adjusted EPS to $13.40-$13.60 from $13.05-$13.50. The demand evidence underneath is stronger than the headline: rolling-twelve-month organic orders grew 41% in Electrical Americas (against +2% in the prior-year comparative) on a 1.3 book-to-bill, Electrical Global orders grew 33%, and total backlog rose from ~$19.8B at year-end to ~$24.1B, of which 71% is deliverable within twelve months — roughly $17.1B, or 57% of TTM revenue of $30.0B, already firmly committed.

The problem is price. The shares closed at $361.88 on July 29, traded to a $478.00 intraday 52-week high on August 12, and sit at $456.64 on the authoritative basis, a 26% advance in eleven sessions. That move puts ETN at 33.8x the $13.50 midpoint of guided FY2026 adjusted EPS, 28.5x FY2027 consensus of $16.02 (a PEG of 1.53 on 18.6% expected EPS growth), and approximately 46.5x TTM GAAP EPS of $9.82. The mean analyst target of $471.98 is 3.4% above spot; the median of $485.00 is 6.2% above. Consensus estimates are rising, but at +1.1% for the current year and +2.1% for next year over 90 days, the revision velocity is well below the magnitude that reliably precedes further multiple expansion from an already premium base.

Two specific things constrain the near-term risk/reward. First, full-year segment margin guidance of 24.1-24.5% against H1 actual of 22.9% ($3,664M on $15,982M) and Q2 of 23.1% implies a second-half segment margin near 25.6%, a ~250bp step-up while commodity and wage inflation is still costing 390bp of consolidated gross margin and 470bp of Electrical Americas operating margin. Second, capital returns have stopped: zero buybacks in H1 2026 against $1,862M in FY2025, share count up from 387.9M to 388.4M, and a $9.0B authorization sitting unused while net debt of $19.9B is worked down from the $9,549M Boyd Thermal purchase. Existing holders should stay. The score stabilizes at 6.0, having declined from 6.5 in May to 5.9 in July across four consecutive HOLDs: competitive position and growth improved on verified order data, and valuation deteriorated on the price move.

## Company Overview

Eaton is an Irish-domiciled electrical power management company with FY2025 revenue of $27,448M and approximately 97,300 employees. Following a Q1 2026 re-segmentation that folded legacy Vehicle and eMobility into a single Mobility unit, the business runs on four segments:

| Segment | Q2'26 Sales | Q2'26 Op Margin | Backlog | Backlog y/y | TTM organic orders | B2B |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Electrical Americas | $3,951M | 27.5% | $15,175M | +33% | +41% | 1.3 |
| Electrical Global | $2,517M | 19.8% | $3,602M | +103% (+54% organic) | +33% | 1.1 |
| Aerospace | $1,222M | 22.8% | $5,164M | +28% (+18% organic) | +17% | 1.2 |
| Mobility | $841M | 13.0% | n/d | n/d | n/d | n/d |
| **Total** | **$8,531M** | **23.1%** | **~$24.1B** | | | |

The electrical franchises sell switchgear, power distribution and assemblies, circuit protection, UPS and power quality, utility distribution equipment, and, since the March 12, 2026 Boyd Thermal close, data-center liquid cooling. Aerospace supplies fuel, hydraulic, pneumatic and fluid-conveyance systems plus, via the January 2026 Ultra PCS acquisition, electronic controls and stores-ejection systems, with a high-margin aftermarket. Mobility is being separated: on June 10, 2026 Eaton signed definitive agreements with Dana Incorporated for a Reverse Morris Trust in which Eaton shareholders are expected to own at least 50.1% of the combined entity and Eaton receives approximately $1.1B of cash, with close targeted for Q1 2027.

Portfolio transformation has been rapid and expensive: Fibrebond ($1,435M, April 2025), Resilient Power ($86M, August 2025), a $75M minority stake in SPAN (January 2026), Ultra PCS ($1,529M, January 2026), and Boyd Thermal ($9,549M net of cash, March 2026), roughly $12.6B deployed in fifteen months.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and orders.** H1 2026 sales of $15,982M grew 19% (12% organic, 6% acquisitions, 1% FX); Q2 grew 21% (14/7/0). Q3 guidance calls for 13.5-15.5% organic, above the 11.7% consensus. TTM revenue is $30,026M.

**Margins — the central debate.** Consolidated Q2 gross margin fell 350bp year-over-year to 33.5%. Management's own bridge attributes 390bp to higher commodity and wage inflation and 150bp to amortization, offset by 160bp from volume. In Electrical Americas, operating margin fell from 29.5% to 27.5% with a 470bp commodity drag against +260bp from volume. Comparable ~460bp and ~300bp inflation drags appear in Electrical Global and Aerospace. Eaton is running a price/cost catch-up with a lag, and it did make sequential progress: Electrical Americas margin expanded 190bp from Q1's 25.6% ($922M on $3,600M) to 27.5%. Reaching the 24.1-24.5% full-year segment margin guide from a 22.9% H1 base still requires H2 near 25.6%, which Q3 guidance of 24.6-25.0% is consistent with but leaves minimal cushion.

**GAAP versus adjusted.** Q2 GAAP diluted EPS fell 16% to $2.11 while adjusted EPS rose 7% to $3.15. The $1.04 gap is 33.0% of adjusted EPS, versus 14.9% in Q2 2025 and 13.4% for FY2025. Composition: amortization $0.50, acquisition/divestiture charges $0.49, restructuring $0.05. The add-backs are conventional and clearly disclosed, and purchase-accounting amortization is prescriptive rather than discretionary. The practical implication is that the 33.8x multiple on adjusted EPS is less comparable to Eaton's own trading history than it looks, because a third of the denominator is now non-GAAP adjustment.

TTM GAAP EPS reconciles to $9.82 (FY2025 $10.45 less H1'25 $4.96 plus H1'26 $4.33), which is exactly what produces the 46.5x trailing multiple. My estimate for FY2026 GAAP EPS, assuming H2 add-backs of roughly $2.00-2.10 per share as a full Boyd quarter lifts amortization and transaction charges moderate, is $9.8-$10.4, approximately 44-47x. Eaton does not guide GAAP EPS.

**Balance sheet.** Gross debt rose from $9,895M at December 31 to $20,611M at June 30; net debt from $9,092M to $19,916M. The funding was $8,500M of US notes issued March 6 (3.85%-5.45%, 2028-2056), €1,200M ($1,390M) of Euro notes March 10, an upsized $4.0B revolver (undrawn) and $4.0B CP program with $2,088M outstanding. An $8.0B delayed-draw term loan signed February 6 was terminated March 6 without fees.

TTM EBIT of $5,266M plus TTM D&A of $1,184M gives TTM EBITDA of $6,450M and net leverage of 3.09x. That understates run-rate, since TTM includes only ~3.6 months of Boyd and ~5.2 months of Ultra. As a labeled sensitivity, annualizing Q2 EBITDA of $1,734M gives 2.87x, and excluding $154M of Q2 acquisition charges, 2.64x. The defensible range is roughly 2.6x-3.1x net leverage trending down, with Q2 interest coverage of 6.7x (7.5x adding back acquisition charges). Run-rate net interest of roughly $800M annually reconciles to the new stack: 4.42% weighted on the US notes (~$376M), ~3.78% on the Euros (~$52M), and ~4.3% on CP (~$90M), roughly $518M incremental against FY2025's $241M net.

Tangible equity is negative $12,586M ($20,254M equity less $32,840M of goodwill and intangibles). For an investment-grade acquirer this is an arithmetic consequence of debt-funded M&A rather than a distress marker, but it means book-anchored screens misread the company and that an impairment would pass through a thin equity base. Goodwill plus intangibles is 1.62x total equity and 58.5% of assets, and both the Boyd and Ultra allocations remain preliminary.

**Cash flow.** H1 2026 operating cash flow of $1,634M (versus $1,156M) less $446M capex gives H1 FCF of $1,188M versus $807M. FY2026 capex guidance is ~$1.15B, explicitly to expand production capacity. H1 cash flow should not be annualized — H1'25 OCF was only 25.8% of the FY2025 total of $4,472M. FY2025 FCF was $3,553M (12.9% of revenue), a 2.0% yield on today's market cap; growing with earnings and adding Boyd/Ultra against higher cash interest and cash taxes points to roughly $3.8-4.0B, or a 2.1-2.3% FCF yield.

Working capital held up through the integration. Receivables rose 24% and inventory 15% against 21% revenue growth, but Boyd and Ultra brought $403M of receivables and $302M of inventory; netting those, underlying growth was ~16.4% and ~8.3%, both below revenue. H1 OCF/net income improved to 0.97 from 0.59. Supply chain finance obligations of $669M (12.3% of payables, paid on normal terms) are not currently a material financing lever.

**Returns on capital.** FY2025 NOPAT of ~$4,289M on invested capital of $28,561M gives 15.0% ROIC. TTM, on $3,950M NOPAT and $40,215M of invested capital, gives 9.8%. Part of that decline is a part-period earnings against full-period capital artifact, but the dilution is real until Boyd revenue scales.

**Tax.** The effective rate went from 17.1% in FY2025 to 28.1% in Q2 2026: $52M of Boyd funding withholding tax plus a structural mix shift toward US-taxed profit. For an Irish-domiciled group generating a growing share of earnings in the US, the mix component is durable and mechanically compresses EPS growth relative to EBIT growth.

## Growth Analysis

The order data is the strongest evidence in the file. Backlog moved from ~$19.8B to ~$24.1B in six months with the twelve-month-deliverable share improving from 69% to 71%. Eaton defines backlog narrowly — only orders customers are firmly committed to, explicitly excluding open orders historically subject to customer release — so the figure understates the commercial pipeline. CFO David Foster separately noted that the Electrical Americas pipeline of negotiations is up 60% year-to-date. Deferred revenue of $1,159M is a distinct and much smaller concept and should not be read as a backlog proxy.

Aerospace is the underappreciated second engine: $5,164M backlog (+28%, +18% organic), 1.2 book-to-bill, and H1 margin expansion to 24.7% from 22.6%, led by commercial aftermarket growing from $746M to $920M (+23%) and now the largest of the three Aerospace revenue lines. Ultra PCS adds defence electronic controls at accretive margin.

Boyd contributed $524M of sales and $121M of segment operating profit from March 12 to June 30, a 23.1% margin, above Electrical Global's 19.8%, so it is accretive at the segment line. Management raised Boyd's 2026 sales estimate to $1.8B from $1.7B, contributing $1.5B to reported revenue, and said Boyd beat the Q2 plan by 20%.

The honest counterweight on the deal economics: Boyd annualizes to roughly $400M of segment operating profit and Ultra to roughly $72M, about $472M combined, against roughly $500M of incremental intangible amortization (a Q2 run-rate near $1,020M annually versus an FY2025 base near $500M) and roughly $518M of incremental interest. On GAAP EBIT, the $11.1B deployed in H1 2026 is approximately year-one earnings-neutral before interest. The cash return is better — amortization is non-cash and finite-lived, giving roughly a 4.3% pre-tax cash yield in year one that rises with Boyd's growth — but this is a growth-and-synergy bet whose payoff sits in 2027-2029. Adjusted EPS grew 7% in Q2 specifically because it excludes both the amortization and the transaction charges.

Removing Mobility (Q2: $841M at 13.0% margin, -2% organic) is arithmetically accretive to both growth and margin. Two mechanics deserve attention. The Dana transaction is a split-off: Eaton shareholders may tender ETN shares in exchange for SpinCo shares, so ETN's own share count can be reduced by an amount the filing does not quantify, an unresolved variable in any 2027 per-share model. And Eaton receives cash of ~$1.1B while its shareholders, not Eaton, end up holding the majority of the combined auto-supplier equity. The transaction requires Dana stockholder approval and regulatory clearance, is intended but not assured to be tax-free, and if not consummated Eaton will proceed with a plain spin-off. The $5.1B unit valuation reported by CNBC is secondary reporting, not a filing figure.

Consensus reflects this: FY2026 $13.51 and FY2027 $16.02, implying 18.6% EPS growth. Revisions are positive across all four horizons (next quarter +2.2% over 30 days, next year +2.1% over 90 days) but modest in magnitude. The earnings surprise record is thin rather than dramatic — 1.6%, 1.5%, 0.1% and -0.4% over the last four quarters — which argues for taking guidance close to face value rather than assuming a systematic beat cushion.

## Competitive Landscape

Eaton's position is improving on the measures that matter. Vertiv reported a disappointing quarter in the week before Eaton's print, attributing it to temporary supply chain challenges, which suggests capacity and execution rather than demand are the current differentiators and that part of Eaton's beat is relative execution. Eaton is guiding to ~$1.15B of 2026 capex explicitly to expand capacity, and Boyd more than doubled its Mexican liquid-cooling manufacturing capacity in February 2026.

The "chip to grid" span is the differentiated angle: utility distribution, modular enclosures (Fibrebond), solid-state transformers (Resilient), switchgear and UPS, and now rack-level liquid cooling. If hyperscalers increasingly procure integrated power-and-cooling blocks rather than components, the span is a genuine advantage. That remains a hypothesis about buying behavior rather than a documented fact, and Eaton's own 10-K states that price is a principal method of competition in Electrical, which cuts against pricing-power assumptions, a point corroborated by the 470bp Electrical Americas commodity drag that price has not yet recovered.

The CEO's characterization of Boyd as the liquid-cooling market leader is issuer commentary relayed through secondary reporting; I found no independent share estimate confirming it. The field is contested: Vertiv's liquid-cooling stack is separately reported as gaining adoption, nVent's liquid-cooling-anchored Systems Protection is reported growing 34% with margins above 20%, and CoolIT, Motivair and newer Asian entrants are present. This is a rising-tide market with contested share.

Aerospace is the more defensible moat: certification barriers plus aftermarket lock-in, with the aftermarket line now the largest of three and growing 23%.

Customer concentration is the disclosure gap. In 2025, 22% of combined Electrical sales went to six unnamed customers and 20% of Aerospace sales to three aircraft OEMs. There is no customer-concentration risk factor in Item 1A, no prior-year comparative in the sections reviewed, and no customer-level disclosure for Boyd. The strategic direction — data centers driving growth in both electrical segments, plus a hyperscale/colocation-oriented acquisition — plausibly increases named-customer concentration. I cannot state that it is worsening, only that the trend is undisclosed and the distributor channel genuinely complicates attribution. I will not claim diversification from mix shift.

## Risk Assessment

**1. The H2 margin ramp.** Q3 guidance of 24.6-25.0% is consistent with the required path and the 190bp sequential Electrical Americas expansion shows it has started, but a 100bp shortfall on ~$16.8B of H2 sales is roughly $168M of operating profit, or about $0.32 of adjusted EPS. This is the most likely source of a Q3 disappointment.

**2. Commodity and wage inflation outrunning price.** The 390bp consolidated and 470bp Electrical Americas drags are the largest operating issue in the file. Eaton's raw-material list makes copper the obvious suspect for switchgear and distribution products, but I could not obtain independent 2026 commodity pricing, so the specific attribution is inference. A partial alternative explanation is capacity-ramp inefficiency and acquired-revenue mix, though Eaton itemizes mix separately in its bridges. The 10-K risk factor is candid that if the company cannot recoup costs without compromising competitive position, results could continue to be impacted, and separately warns that tariff-driven increases create broader cost pressure even on non-imported goods.

**3. Single-end-market dependence on a capex cycle.** Data centers drive growth in both electrical segments and were the explicit rationale for Fibrebond, Resilient and Boyd. The stock's beta to AI-capex sentiment is high and demonstrated — a 26% derating through calendar 2025 on rising earnings, then a drawdown into the June 2026 highs, then +26% in eleven sessions. A frontier-lab funding stumble or a visible hyperscaler capex deferral would compress this multiple faster than the backlog could deteriorate.

**4. Integration and impairment.** $32,840M of goodwill and intangibles equals 1.62x total equity. Boyd's allocation shifted $1,603M into finite-lived intangibles (to $7,190M, 75% of purchase price) and $1,169M out of goodwill, with consolidated deferred tax liabilities rising from $265M to $2,139M — routine completion of third-party valuations, still preliminary, with the 10-Q warning further revisions could be material. Item 4 notes management is still evaluating the effect of businesses acquired in the past twelve months on internal control over financial reporting, meaning ~$11.1B of operations are not yet fully inside the ICFR assessment. Retention economics are non-trivial: $240M of assumed Fibrebond awards vesting over six years (forfeitures payable to former Fibrebond shareholders, not retained), plus up to $50M for Resilient.

**5. Execution bench depth.** A new CEO (June 2025), new CFO (March 2026), new Chief Legal Officer (April 2025) and new CHRO (October 2025) are integrating $11.1B of acquisitions and executing a Reverse Morris Trust. The CFO succession was orderly and pre-announced, with an 8-K three months ahead and a 29-year Eaton veteran taking the seat, which removes the abrupt-departure reading. The residual point is that several key seats are new to those seats during the most complex portfolio transition in the company's recent history.

**6. Unverified credit rating position.** Pre-Boyd ratings were S&P A-/A-2 and Moody's A3/P-2, both stable, but those predate the March issuance of $9.9B of notes. Whether the agencies affirmed, revised outlooks, or downgraded after leverage roughly tripled is not established in the available record, and it bears on cost of capital and the timing of buyback resumption. There is no dedicated indebtedness risk factor in Item 1A before or after the raise.

**7. Dana transaction conditions.** Dana stockholder approval, regulatory clearance, and tax-free qualification are all outstanding. Failure routes to a plain spin-off, which forfeits the ~$1.1B cash distribution and the potential share retirement from the exchange offer.

**8. Compensation design.** The proxy's performance measures are adjusted EPS (company-selected), adjusted operating cash flow, and TSR. Adjusted EPS as defined excludes intangible amortization, acquisition/divestiture/integration charges and restructuring — precisely the three cost categories generated by $12.6B of M&A, which consumed $1.04 of Q2 GAAP EPS. This is an evidence-based structural observation, not an imputation of motive; excluding purchase-accounting amortization is standard across industrial peers and arguably closer to cash economics, and TSR's inclusion means market judgment does feed payout (FY2025 compensation actually paid of $5,884K against $7,924K granted).

### Options Market Signal

The options market is not pricing a directional view. Near-term ATM implied volatility of 40.1% against 37.5% one-year realized is fair (2.6pp spread), and IV collapsed from 90.8% pre-earnings on July 28 to 40.1% now, so the event premium has been discharged. The term structure is mildly backwardated (40.1% at one week versus 37.2% at six months), but the one-week tenor covers a single session and reflects expiry mechanics; the nearest-expiry implied move is only ±1.7%. Put/call skew is characterized as balanced at -1.1%. Put/call volume of 2.27 sits against a call-tilted open-interest ratio of 0.34, down from 1.77 pre-earnings, so positioning in size leans long while recent flow includes hedging. The one genuinely uncommon print is 1,923 contracts of September 18 $390 puts against 190 open interest (10.1x), struck 14.6% below spot at roughly the pre-earnings price — someone paying for downside insurance through the Q3 window rather than expressing a thesis. Short interest of 1.8% of float and 2.9 days to cover confirms no crowded bearish position. The market agrees with the fundamental picture and is not pricing imminent stress, which removes any contrarian entry argument from cheap volatility.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($545, +19.3%)

Electrical Americas organic orders at +41% with a 1.3 book-to-bill and $15.2B of segment backlog, plus $17.1B of total backlog deliverable inside twelve months (57% of TTM revenue), gives unusual revenue visibility for an industrial of this size. Price/cost inverts through H2 as pricing catches the commodity step-up, delivering the guided 25.6% H2 segment margin and validating the 24.1-24.5% full-year figure. Boyd, guided to $1.8B of 2026 sales at a 23.1% segment margin after beating the Q2 plan by 20%, scales into 2027 and the ~4.3% year-one cash yield on the $11.1B deployed rises toward high-single digits. Aerospace compounds independently with commercial aftermarket +23% and margin at 24.7%. The Dana RMT closes in Q1 2027, delivering ~$1.1B of cash toward debt reduction, removing a 13%-margin drag on -2% organic growth, and potentially retiring ETN shares through the exchange offer. Deleveraging from ~2.9x toward 2.0x by late 2027 restores the $9.0B repurchase authorization as an EPS lever. On FY2027 EPS revised toward $16.30 at 34x, the shares reach $545.

### Bear Case ($360, -21.2%)

The H2 margin ramp misses by 100-150bp because copper and wage inflation persist and Electrical's price-based competition prevents full recovery; FY adjusted EPS lands near the low end or below. Data-center order growth decelerates from 41% as hyperscalers digest capacity — the same dynamic that produced the 2025 derating from a TSR index of 389 to 288 on rising earnings. Boyd's liquid-cooling position proves contested rather than dominant against Vertiv, nVent, CoolIT and Motivair, and the 75%-of-purchase-price intangible allocation amortizes at ~$1.0B annually against slower-than-underwritten revenue, keeping GAAP EPS depressed near $10 while the market re-anchors on GAAP. Buybacks stay suspended through 2027, so per-share growth depends entirely on operations. On FY2027 EPS of $15.00 at 24x — a multiple consistent with the December 2025 trading range — the shares reach $360.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD** (growth thesis, poor entry). Time horizon for reassessment: the Q3 2026 print in late October 2026.

- **Base case target: $480 (+5.1%)** — 30x FY2027 consensus EPS of $16.02. A one-year-forward multiple roll below the 35.4x implied at the $478 high, consistent with the $471.98 mean and $485.00 median analyst targets.
- **Bull case: $545 (+19.3%)** — 34x FY2027 EPS of $16.30 after upward revision.
- **Bear case: $360 (-21.2%)** — 24x FY2027 EPS of $15.00.
- **Probability-weighted expected return: approximately +2%** (50% base, 25% bull, 25% bear), which does not clear the ACCUMULATE bar.

**Upgrade to ACCUMULATE requires any of:**
1. Price of $400-415 or below on unchanged guidance — 25-26x FY2027 consensus, restoring roughly 15-20% base-case upside.
2. Q3 segment margin at or above 24.6% (the low end of guidance) with the commodity/wage drag narrowing to under 250bp in Electrical Americas, confirming price/cost inversion rather than promising it.
3. FY2027 consensus revised above $16.75 alongside Electrical Americas organic orders sustaining above 30% and book-to-bill above 1.2.

**Upgrade to BUY requires** two of the above plus resumption of buybacks or confirmed post-Boyd rating affirmations at the A-/A3 level, which would establish that deleveraging is running ahead of plan.

**Downgrade to REDUCE if any of:**
1. Q3 segment margin below 24.0%, or FY guidance cut from 24.1-24.5%.
2. Electrical Americas rolling organic orders decelerate below 20% or book-to-bill falls below 1.0 — the mechanism that converts a backlog story into a multiple-compression story.
3. Boyd 2026 sales guidance reduced from $1.8B, or a measurement-period revision or impairment against the $7,190M of Boyd intangibles.
4. Net leverage rising above 3.3x, or a rating agency downgrade or negative outlook action.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 print (late October 2026) is the decisive checkpoint on the margin ramp. Secondary dates: the Dana stockholder vote and the Q1 2027 targeted RMT close, which will also determine any share retirement from the exchange offer.

## Conclusion

Eaton's operating position is the strongest it has been in this cycle. Organic orders of +41% in Electrical Americas against +2% a year earlier, backlog rising from ~$19.8B to ~$24.1B in six months, $17.1B deliverable inside twelve months, Aerospace aftermarket growing 23% at a 24.7% H1 margin, and Boyd contributing at a 23.1% segment margin above its own division — these are verifiable filing facts, and they support raising competitive position to 7.0 and growth to 7.9 from the levels carried into this review. The score's decline from 6.5 in May to 5.9 in July stabilizes at 6.0 on that evidence.

The constraint is arithmetic. At $456.64 the shares carry 33.8x guided FY2026 adjusted EPS, 28.5x FY2027 consensus, a 1.53 PEG, 46.5x TTM GAAP EPS, a 2.1-2.3% FCF yield and a 0.96% dividend yield, with the mean analyst target 3.4% away and the 52-week high 4.5% away. Roughly a third of adjusted EPS is now non-GAAP add-back, up from one-seventh a year ago, and on GAAP EBIT the $11.1B deployed in H1 is approximately year-one earnings-neutral before $518M of incremental interest — the return is real but sits in 2027-2029. Second-half guidance requires a ~250bp segment margin step-up while commodity and wage inflation still costs 470bp of Electrical Americas margin, and buybacks are suspended with the share count no longer shrinking. Calendar 2025 demonstrated that this multiple can contract 26% while earnings rise.

Own the shares for the order book, the Aerospace franchise and the Dana simplification. Do not commit new capital at 30x-plus forward with a probability-weighted expected return near 2%. The high-$390s to low-$410s (25-26x FY2027 consensus) is where the asymmetry returns, and the late-October Q3 print is where the margin question gets answered.
