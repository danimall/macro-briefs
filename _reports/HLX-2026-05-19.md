## Executive Summary

Helix Energy Solutions is a specialty offshore energy services company currently trading at $10.36/share ($1.5B market cap) in the midst of an all-stock reverse merger with Hornbeck Offshore announced April 23, 2026. Helix shareholders will own approximately 45% of the combined entity, which will operate under the Hornbeck name and ticker. The deal is expected to close in H2 2026 pending shareholder and regulatory approval, with $75M+ in annual synergies projected within three years.

The standalone business shows mixed fundamentals: a fortress balance sheet ($501M cash, net cash positive by $193M as of Q1 2026), strong FCF generation ($120M in FY2025, $59M in Q1 2026 alone), but declining earnings power (net income fell 45% YoY in 2025) driven by vessel utilization headwinds and North Sea weakness. The forward P/E of ~108x on trailing earnings reflects trough profitability rather than normalized economics. Analyst consensus targets $12.50 mean / $14 high, reflecting merger completion value.

The investment thesis rests on merger execution. At current levels, there is approximately 20% upside to analyst mean targets if the deal closes, with downside partially protected by the cash-rich balance sheet. The stock has appreciated 62% over the trailing 12 months, with most of the move occurring around the merger announcement. The 3-out-of-4 quarterly earnings miss track record and negative estimate revision trends for next year ($0.56 vs. $0.69 ninety days ago) indicate the standalone business trajectory is deteriorating, making deal completion the critical path to value creation.

## Company Overview

Helix Energy Solutions operates through four segments serving the offshore energy industry:

**Well Intervention (56% of revenue):** Purpose-built vessels providing deepwater well maintenance, production enhancement, and abandonment services in the Gulf of America, Brazil, and North Sea. Fleet of 7 intervention vessels offers lower operating costs versus drilling rigs for many subsea operations.

**Robotics (25% of revenue):** ROVs, trenchers, and specialized subsea equipment serving both oil & gas and offshore wind markets. 49% of Robotics revenues in 2025 came from renewable energy contracts — a meaningful diversification vector.

**Shallow Water Abandonment (15% of revenue):** Being divested to Chouest ahead of the Hornbeck merger. This removes approximately $200M in annual revenue.

**Production Facilities (6% of revenue):** Declining oil & gas properties (Thunder Hawk, Droshky) being wound down, with an $18M impairment charge taken in 2025.

Key customers include Shell (18% of 2025 revenue) and Petrobras (10%), with 82% of backlog concentrated in 6 customers.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:**
- FY2025 revenue: $1.291B (-5% YoY), down from a $1.359B peak in 2024
- Gross margin: 12% (compressed from 16% in 2024)
- Operating income: $65M (down 49% from $127M in 2024)
- Adjusted EBITDA: $272M (down 10% from $303M)
- FY2026 guidance: Revenue $1.2B–$1.4B; Adj. EBITDA $230M–$290M

The profitability compression stems from Well Intervention utilization dropping to 72% (from 90% in 2024), driven by the Seawell warm-stacking and 131 docking days across the fleet.

**Cash Flow:**
- FY2025 operating cash flow: $137M
- FY2025 free cash flow: $120M (9.3% FCF margin)
- Q1 2026 FCF: $59M (strong seasonal start)
- Capex remains minimal at $16M in 2025 (maintenance runs through operating costs as vessel certification)

**Balance Sheet:**
- Cash: $501M (Q1 2026)
- Total debt: $308M (primarily $300M 9.75% senior notes due 2029)
- Net cash position: +$193M
- Liquidity: $612M
- Current ratio: 2.75x

Even in the worst-case scenario of merger collapse and prolonged downturn, the net cash position and FCF generation provide substantial runway.

**Hidden Liability — Operating Leases:**
$788M in total operating lease obligations ($165M due within one year), primarily vessel charters. These represent fixed costs that must be serviced regardless of utilization and create meaningful operating leverage in both directions.

## Growth Analysis

Growth prospects are limited for the standalone entity but improve materially through the merger:

**Standalone challenges:**
- Revenue declined 5% in 2025 with flat-to-modest guidance for 2026
- Next-year EPS estimates declining: $0.56 current vs. $0.69 ninety days ago (-19.2%)
- Current quarter estimates also declining: $0.08 vs. $0.10 ninety days ago (-25%)

**Growth vectors:**

- Seawell returned to operations; both North Sea vessels contracted through Q3-Q4 2026

- Combined entity with Hornbeck targets $75M+ synergies within 3 years

**Estimate revision signal:** EPS estimates are declining across most time horizons except the immediate next quarter (+6.2% over 30 days). The 90-day revision for next year of -19.2% is a meaningful negative signal for standalone earnings power.

## Valuation Assessment

The trailing/forward P/E of ~108x reflects trough earnings of $0.10/share trailing (impacted by Q1 2026 loss of -$0.09 and the weak FY2025). On normalized consensus for FY2027 ($0.56 EPS), the stock trades at ~18.5x, reasonable relative to peers (peer median P/E: 40.3x) but reflecting declining estimate momentum.

On an EV/EBITDA basis, using the $230M-$290M guidance midpoint ($260M):
- Enterprise value: $1.5B market cap + $308M debt - $501M cash = ~$1.3B EV
- EV/EBITDA: ~5.0x on the midpoint

This is attractive for an asset-heavy services company, and the net cash position means the equity value benefits disproportionately from any operational improvement.

**Analyst targets:** Low $9.00, mean $12.50, high $14.00. Current price of $10.36 represents ~20% upside to mean and ~35% to high. Consensus is 3 Strong Buy, 1 Buy, 1 Hold.

The valuation question is whether ~45% ownership of the combined Hornbeck-Helix entity is worth the current $1.5B market cap. If the combined entity trades at 5-6x EBITDA on ~$500M combined EBITDA (estimated), the combined EV would be $2.5B-$3.0B — HLX's 45% share implies equity value of $1.1B-$1.35B plus HLX's net cash of ~$200M, suggesting current price roughly reflects fair value on conservative combined estimates. Upside comes from synergy realization ($75M would boost combined EBITDA to $575M+).

## Competitive Landscape

Helix occupies distinct niches within the fragmented offshore services market:

**Strengths:**
- Purpose-built well intervention vessels provide cost advantages over drilling rig alternatives
- Only vertically integrated shallow water decommissioning provider on GoM shelf (though being divested)
- Growing offshore wind trenching capability with specialized assets and multi-year contracts
- Schlumberger (SLB) alliance for integrated intervention technology
- Jones Act compliance provides domestic market protection

**Vulnerabilities:**
- Larger, better-capitalized competitors (TechnipFMC, Oceaneering, Baker Hughes) in most segments
- Post-merger dilution reduces HLX shareholder exposure to Helix's highest-margin assets
- 82% backlog concentration in 6 customers creates revenue cliff risk
- Shell (18%) and Petrobras (10%) concentration means single customer decisions significantly impact utilization

**Trajectory:** The competitive position is stable but not strengthening. The merger with Hornbeck creates scale but also exposes HLX shareholders to the lower-margin OSV business.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Merger Execution Risk (HIGH):** All-stock deal with 45/55 ownership split, CEO transition to Todd Hornbeck, regulatory and shareholder approvals pending. TD Cowen characterized the deal economics as "less compelling." If the deal collapses, the stock likely returns toward the $8-9 pre-announcement range.

2. **Oil Price Sensitivity:** Oil prices dropped 20% in 2025, and customer budgets exhibit 6-12 month lag to spot prices. The offshore spending environment remains uncertain given tariff and geopolitical disruption.

3. **Earnings Deterioration:** 3 of 4 quarterly misses and declining estimates across most periods. If the merger delays or fails, the stock must be valued on deteriorating standalone fundamentals.

4. **Operating Lease Obligations:** $788M in fixed charter costs create significant downside operating leverage. In a prolonged downturn, these obligations constrain operational flexibility.

5. **Customer Concentration:** 82% of backlog in 6 customers. Shell alone is 18% of revenue. Any single customer's budget reallocation materially impacts results.

6. **North Sea Structural Headwinds:** UK EPL at 38% through 2030 depresses operator spending in a key market for Helix's fleet.

## Investment Thesis

Well Intervention utilization reaches 85%+ by H2 2026. The market values the combined entity at 6x EBITDA on $550M+ combined EBITDA. Decommissioning secular demand accelerates. ## Conclusion

HLX is a merger event stock with a 6-9 month catalyst path to value realization. The standalone business shows declining earnings momentum but operates from exceptional balance sheet strength. The 20% upside to analyst consensus targets reflects merger completion probability, while the ~23% downside to bear case represents deal failure risk.

The appropriate stance is HOLD for existing positions, with the merger close being the primary value driver. At current prices, the risk/reward is moderately positive (20% upside vs. 23% downside) but not asymmetric enough to warrant new positions at $10.36 — the stock has already appreciated 62% over 12 months and is within 4% of its 52-week high. The deal's dilutive ownership split (only 45% for HLX shareholders), CEO transition, and declining standalone fundamentals create legitimate uncertainty about long-term value creation post-close. Hold for the merger catalyst while monitoring deal progress, with a clear plan to reassess upon completion or any material delay.
