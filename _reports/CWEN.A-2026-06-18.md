**Research Date: June 18, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Clearway Energy is one of the largest U.S. owners of contracted clean-energy generation, structured as a yieldco that converts long-dated power purchase agreements into a growing dividend stream. The FY2025 portfolio spans roughly 12.9 GW gross across 27 states — about 10.1 GW of wind, solar and battery storage plus 2.8 GW of dispatchable California gas — with offtake agreements averaging a 12-year remaining duration and a weighted-average counterparty credit rating of Moody's Baa1. The economically meaningful metrics grew in 2025: Adjusted EBITDA reached $1,217M (+6%), CAFD reached $430M (top end of original guidance), and net income attributable to the company was +$169M ($1.43 EPS) despite a headline consolidated net loss of $(231)M driven by non-cash HLBV tax-equity accounting allocated to noncontrolling interests.

The forward thesis is a growth-of-cash-flow story underpinned by AI/data-center power demand. Management reaffirmed 2026 CAFD guidance of $470-510M (10-18% growth) and a 2030 CAFD/share target of $2.90-$3.10, with 5-8%+ growth beyond. Concrete contracted catalysts now exist: a 20-year Microsoft PPA on the Mt. Storm repower, three Google PPAs totaling ~1.1 GW announced January 2026, and Royal Slope (520 MW solar+storage) serving Washington data-center load. A pending charter amendment collapsing the public dual-class structure (CWEN.A vs. CWEN) is directly material to Class A holders, improving liquidity and removing the largely symbolic voting differential.

The case against full conviction is governance and structure. A material weakness in internal controls over HLBV calculations was newly identified in Q4 2025, sitting in the exact area producing the volatile loss line. The company has no employees and is bound to sponsor CEG (ultimately TotalEnergies and GIP/BlackRock) via a perpetual master services agreement, with CEG controlling 54.89% of votes and change-of-control triggers embedded in PPAs and financings. Leverage is $8.7B at a sub-investment-grade BB/Ba2 corporate rating, and the equity is rate-sensitive in a higher-for-longer environment. The asset growth is real and contracted; the wrapper around it carries persistent risk. We rate CWEN.A a HOLD on a growth basis with a 12-month reassessment at the next earnings prints and the share-class vote outcome.

## Company Overview

Clearway Energy operates as a yieldco: it owns operating clean-energy assets and distributes the resulting cash flow as a growing dividend. The business runs through two segments. Renewables & Storage (98% of 2025 generation) holds wind, solar and BESS under long-term contracts. Flexible Generation holds dispatchable California gas reliability assets — El Segundo, Marsh Landing, Walnut Creek, Carlsbad — with roughly 91-97% of revenue not tied to GHG-emitting dispatch.

The growth engine is a hybrid of sponsor dropdowns and third-party M&A. Sponsor Clearway Energy Group develops projects and sells ("drops down") completed assets to CWEN, which owned 58.62% of the operating entity (Clearway Energy LLC) at year-end 2025. CEG owns 41.38% economically but 54.89% of the voting power. CWEN has had no employees since January 1, 2025; all operations run through CEG under a master services agreement.

The key structural detail for a Class A holder is the share-class distinction: CWEN.A is the Class A common stock (one vote per share); CWEN is Class C (1/100th vote). The two carry identical economic rights and both received $1.77/share in 2025. The pending charter amendment that collapses this public dual-class structure has its mechanical footprint in the April-May 2026 Form 4 Class A→Class C conversions by TotalEnergies, BlackRock and insiders.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and operating profit.** Total operating revenue was $1,429M in 2025, up from $1,371M (2024) and $1,314M (2023) — steady mid-single-digit growth from new solar/BESS and wind additions. Adjusted EBITDA was up 6% from $1,146M.

**The headline loss is an accounting artifact.** The consolidated net loss of $(231)M (vs. $(63)M in 2024) is misleading. Net income attributable to Clearway, Inc. was +$169M (vs. $88M), with EPS of $1.43 (vs. $0.75). The gap reflects $(400)M allocated to noncontrolling/redeemable NCI, primarily $(694)M of HLBV tax-equity accounting losses — a non-cash, non-economic feature of tax-equity partnership structures. For this business, CAFD and Adjusted EBITDA are the metrics that govern the dividend, and both grew.

**Cash flow.** CAFD was up from $425M. Operating cash flow was $688M (vs. $770M); the $82M decline was driven mostly by the $63M Mt. Storm commodity-contract buyout. Maintenance capex is genuinely light — only $32M budgeted for 2026 — because growth capex is funded externally.

**Balance sheet and leverage.** Total assets are $16.7B against the $8.7B of long-term debt and $5.8B equity. Of that debt, $6.2B is non-recourse facility-level debt at non-guarantor subsidiaries; corporate-level debt is ~$2.5B (Senior Notes 2028/2031/2032 plus revolver). The BB/Ba2 ratings (S&P/Moody's) are stable and typical for a leveraged yieldco. Liquidity was $1,061M at year-end (down from $1,330M on growth spend), including $243M revolver availability. Near-term maturities include $1,139M of facility-level debt due 2026 (incl. Honeycomb $490M and Oahu $75M construction-related items that convert/refinance) and the $850M 2028 Senior Notes. In January 2026 the company issued $600M of 5.75% Senior Notes due 2034 and repaid the revolver, addressing part of the corporate stack. Interest expense rose $80M in 2025, of which roughly $60M was interest-rate-swap mark-to-market.

**Tax.** No material cash taxes are expected through 2030 (NOLs plus ITC/PTC carryforwards). 2025 distributions were a non-taxable return of capital given the cumulative E&P deficit, a modest holder positive.

**Earnings surprise pattern.** The reported four-quarter EPS surprise record (1 beat, 3 misses, with swings of -386% and +452%) is essentially uninformative for this company. GAAP EPS is dominated by HLBV/NCI noise rather than operational performance; the cash-flow guide (CAFD at the top of its range) is the better signal of delivery. We do not read the GAAP miss pattern as a fundamental deterioration.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth thesis is specific and contracted, which is the basis for holding rather than avoiding. Three components matter on a 12-month horizon:

1. **Reaffirmed near-term cash growth.** 2026 CAFD guidance against $430M delivered in 2025 implies ~9-19% growth. CAFD/share and relative TSR are the explicit pay-for-performance metrics, aligning management incentives with the distribution growth that drives the equity.

2. **Signed hyperscaler offtake.** The data-center demand thesis is now contracted. Mt. Storm (335 MW WV wind repower) will sell to Microsoft under the 20-year PPA on completion in 2H26. The three Google PPAs (~1.1 GW) cover Goat Mountain (TX wind, 360 MW, 15-yr, ~$200M capex, 2027), Swan Solar (MO, 650 MW, 20-yr, ~$215M, 2028), and Catamount (WV wind, 20-yr, ~$155M, 2028). Royal Slope (WA, 520 MW solar+storage, 20-yr) serves a municipal utility supplying data-center load (2027, ~$200M). These are offtake relationships — the hyperscalers buy power; Clearway/CEG own the assets — so the cash flows accrue to CWEN, not the tech companies.

3. **Captive pipeline depth.** CEG's late-stage pipeline is 11.2 GW, with ~2 GW of data-center contracts signed in the prior 12 months. Committed near-term dropdowns include Deriva (613 MW solar, ~$210-230M net, 1H26) and Spindle/Rosamond South II BESS (~$90M, 2H26). The 2025 execution record (Rosamond South I, Luna Valley, Daggett 1, Pine Forest, Honeycomb, Catalina, Tuolumne, Dan's Mountain) demonstrates the dropdown machine is functioning.

The principal growth caveat is the July 2025 federal tax law, which accelerates the wind/solar PTC/ITC phase-out (construction must begin by 7/4/2026, in-service by 12/31/2027) and adds foreign-entity-of-concern content restrictions. Management asserts CEG's pipeline is insulated through at least 2030. We weight the signed PPAs as durable and the post-2027 pipeline visibility as somewhat policy-contingent.

## Valuation Assessment

Standard market data (price, P/E, yield, beta, analyst targets) was unavailable in the provided dataset, so valuation here is framed on cash-flow fundamentals rather than a precise multiple comparison.

On the economically meaningful metrics, the equity is a CAFD/dividend-yield instrument. At $1.77 annualized 2025 dividend (raised to $0.4602/quarter for 2026, continuing a ~2% sequential cadence), the security offers an income profile growing in the high-single to low-double digits on the CAFD line. The 2030 CAFD/share target provides a forward anchor; if achieved, it supports continued dividend growth and a re-rating if the path is de-risked.

We do not assign a high valuation score for two reasons. First, the security's value is dominated by interest-rate-sensitive discounting of a contracted dividend stream; in a higher-for-longer rate environment, the yield required to clear rises and the equity compresses, which is the central reason the broader regulated/contracted-power complex has derated. Second, while the cash-flow growth is real, the governance overhang (material weakness, sponsor entrenchment, BB/Ba2 leverage) is a structural discount that does not resolve on a 12-month view. The valuation is fair rather than asymmetric: there is upside if the share-class simplification improves liquidity and the hyperscaler cash flows compound, but the rate channel and credit-spread sensitivity bound the near-term re-rating. We score valuation at the midpoint, reflecting an income-supported floor without an identified, dated mispricing catalyst that would justify rewarding cheapness.

## Competitive Landscape

Clearway's competitive edge is scale, regional market knowledge, a low-operating-cost renewable fleet, long-dated investment-grade contracts, and a captive development pipeline through CEG. The structural tailwind is broad-based: U.S. power demand from AI/data centers (external estimates cite ~100 GW of incremental demand by 2035) is driving hyperscalers toward long-term PPAs with operators that can deliver contracted clean generation at scale. Clearway's signed Google and Microsoft deals place it directly in this flow.

The competitive position is improving at the asset/contracting layer — the company is converting development inventory into 15-20 year investment-grade-backed cash flows that its smaller competitors cannot match for scale. The offsetting consideration is that this advantage is borrowed from CEG; Clearway does not develop projects itself, so its competitive moat is contingent on the sponsor's pipeline and willingness to drop assets at accretive prices. Customer concentration is a structural feature: SCE (~22%) and PG&E (~16%) account for ~38% of revenue, and the 10-K explicitly recalls PG&E's 2019-2020 bankruptcy halting distributions from certain assets. The hyperscaler contracts diversify the counterparty base over time, a constructive trajectory.

## Risk Assessment

The risks that distinguish this company from sector boilerplate are concentrated and partly newly surfaced:

1. **Material weakness in internal controls (NEW, Q4 2025).** Management identified ineffective controls over HLBV calculations used to allocate income to tax-equity NCI — the exact mechanism producing the volatile loss line. Remediation timing is unconfirmed, and Q1 2026 again showed a $(163)M consolidated net loss on the same dynamics. This is a control-quality flag rather than evidence of cash-flow misstatement, but it elevates governance risk and is not fully priced for minority holders.

2. **Sponsor entrenchment.** With no employees, a perpetual master services agreement CWEN largely cannot terminate, CEG controlling the board and the votes, and change-of-control triggers in PPAs and financings that activate if CEG drops below 50% voting power, the structure protects the sponsor more than minority public holders. Related-party dropdown pricing carries inherent conflicts; the Mt. Storm sale-and-repurchase round-trip ($152M out, $336M back for the repowered interests) illustrates the mechanics.

3. **Leverage and rate sensitivity.** The $8.7B debt at BB/Ba2 in a higher-for-longer environment creates refinancing and discount-rate risk. The equity is a yield vehicle whose price moves inversely with the long end.

4. **Customer concentration.** SCE + PG&E at ~38% of revenue is a real tail given California utility credit history.

5. **Policy/tax-credit phase-out.** The July 2025 law accelerates PTC/ITC step-down and adds content restrictions; management's "insulated through 2030" claim is plausible for signed projects but carries execution risk for later pipeline.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** The data-center demand cycle converts CEG's 11.2 GW pipeline into a multi-year stream of contracted, investment-grade-backed cash flows. 2026 CAFD lands in the upper half of the guide, the 2030 CAFD/share target is reaffirmed and de-risked by signed Google/Microsoft offtake, the dividend continues its ~2% quarterly cadence, and the share-class simplification improves CWEN.A liquidity. As rate expectations stabilize, the yield instrument re-rates. The HLBV material weakness is remediated without restatement.

**Bear case.** Higher-for-longer rates compress the equity through both the discount-rate channel and wider credit spreads on the BB/Ba2 debt. The material weakness reveals deeper control problems or delays the next filing. CEG drops down assets at unattractive prices or slows the pipeline, and the tax-credit phase-out impairs post-2027 projects more than management indicates. SCE or PG&E credit deterioration interrupts distributions, as PG&E's bankruptcy once did.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** 12 months, anchored to the next two earnings prints (CAFD delivery against guidance), the share-class simplification shareholder-vote outcome and effective date, and disclosure on HLBV material-weakness remediation in subsequent 10-Q/10-K filings.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- 2026 CAFD tracking toward or above the top of the guided range, plus confirmed accretive dropdown pricing on Deriva and Spindle/Rosamond South II.
- Material-weakness remediation confirmed without restatement, removing the governance overhang.
- A clear easing of the rate path that improves the discounting backdrop for the contracted dividend stream, alongside completed share-class consolidation improving liquidity.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Evidence the HLBV material weakness extends to a restatement or signals broader control failure.
- A CAFD guidance cut or a dividend-coverage deterioration (CAFD payout ratio rising materially toward unsustainable levels).
- Sponsor pipeline slowdown, a dropdown priced punitively against minority holders, or a CEG ownership change that triggers PPA/financing change-of-control provisions.
- SCE or PG&E credit event that interrupts distributions.

## Conclusion

Clearway delivers what a yieldco is supposed to: growing contracted cash flow, a rising dividend, and a credible multi-year path to the 2030 CAFD/share target backed by signed hyperscaler offtake and a deep captive pipeline. The asset-level story is improving and the GAAP loss headline is a non-cash accounting artifact, not operational deterioration. What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the wrapper: a newly disclosed material weakness in the exact accounting area driving the loss line, structural sponsor entrenchment with weak minority protections, sub-investment-grade leverage, and an equity whose price is tightly coupled to the rate path. The pending share-class simplification is a genuine, dated, Class-A-specific positive that supports holding through the reassessment window. We rate CWEN.A a growth-oriented HOLD, upgradeable on remediation confirmation plus accretive dropdown execution, and downgradeable on any control or coverage deterioration.
