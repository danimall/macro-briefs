**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Gartner is the dominant global research and advisory franchise, with 13,000+ enterprise clients and a subscription model that historically commanded a premium multiple on the strength of recurring revenue, high margins, and negative working capital. That premium has collapsed. The stock fell from a 52-week high of $437.84 to a low of $139.18 and now trades around $176.67, down roughly 62% over the past year. The de-rating reflects a genuine deterioration in the metrics that lead revenue. Insights contract value grew just +1% FX-neutral in 2025 and +1.0% in Q1 2026, GTS wallet retention fell from 102% to 96%, and GBS retention fell from 106% to 99%. Existing clients are net reducing their spend, the most concrete evidence that the bear case has substance.

Two forces are working against each other. On the cheap side: at the consensus adjusted FY26 EPS of $13.69 and FY27 of $15.34, the stock trades at roughly 12.9x and 11.5x forward earnings, below the IT-services peer median of 14.6x and far below the 25-30x range Gartner held for most of the prior decade. Free cash flow remains strong (Q1 2026 FCF +29% to $371M), the balance sheet is asset-light, and management is repurchasing stock aggressively (~10% share-count reduction over five quarters). On the expensive-for-what-you-get side: organic revenue turned negative in Q1 (-4.3% FX-neutral, -1.4% ex-divestiture), the reported EPS growth is almost entirely buyback arithmetic, and the central swing factor — whether generative AI commoditizes the premium-research product — is unresolved and partly evidenced by the falling wallet retention.

We rate IT a HOLD. The valuation has compressed to a level where a stabilization in contract value would support meaningful re-rating, but the stagnating organic growth, the AI displacement overhang, a federal securities class action, and 24.2% short interest leave the outcome genuinely two-sided. This is a name to monitor on its leading indicators (CV growth, wallet retention) rather than to underwrite on the current multiple alone.

## Company Overview

Gartner operates three segments. **Insights** (~78% of revenue, $5.07B in 2025) is the core subscription engine, split into Global Technology Sales (GTS, the larger ~$3.9B contract-value book serving technology users and providers) and Global Business Sales (GBS, ~$1.25B serving non-IT functional leaders in HR, finance, and supply chain). Roughly 77% of contracts are multi-year, mostly billable on signing and non-cancelable, which produces the negative-working-capital cash dynamics that define the model. **Conferences** ($645M, +11%) is the highest-growth segment, anchored by the Symposium/Xpo series. **Consulting** ($552M, -1%) is project-based and lumpy.

The moat rests on a 40-year brand built around perceived independence, a proprietary content library, a network of 2,400+ analysts, and operating leverage from distributing one research base across subscriptions, events, and consulting. Management's own 10-K flags low barriers to entry and rising competition from free internet information and AI/LLM-generated content; the company is candid that its core asset (packaged expert information) sits in the path of generative AI.

## Financial Analysis

Revenue grew from $5.48B (2022) to $5.91B (2023) to $6.27B (2024) to $6.50B (2025, +4% reported, +3% FX-neutral). The trajectory has flattened, and **Q1 2026 revenue declined to $1.51B (-1.5% reported, -4.3% FX-neutral)**. Part of that is the Digital Markets divestiture, but adjusted revenue was still -1.4% FX-neutral and Insights revenue was flat (-0.1% FX-neutral).

The leading indicator confirms the slowdown. **Insights contract value grew only +1% FX-neutral in 2025 to $5.16B, and +1.0% in Q1 2026 to $5.3B.** The weakness is concentrated in retention: GTS wallet retention 102% → 96%, GBS 106% → 99%. Wallet retention below 100% means the average existing client renews at a lower dollar value than the prior year. GTS contract value was roughly flat to slightly negative; Q1 2026 GTS CV grew just +0.4% FX-neutral.

Reported profitability fell sharply, but the headline overstates the deterioration. Operating income declined -11% to $1.03B (margin ~15.8% vs 18.5%), and net income dropped -42% to $729M (GAAP EPS $9.65 vs $16.00). The drivers are largely non-recurring: a $150M Digital Markets goodwill impairment, the absence of a 2024 one-time $300M event-cancellation insurance gain, $56.6M of workforce-reduction charges, and a normalized 24.7% tax rate versus an artificially low 9.6% in 2024 (which had benefited from a ~$162M IP-transfer tax benefit). On an adjusted basis, the consensus is FY26 EPS of $13.69 and FY27 of $15.34.

Cash generation remains the genuine strength. Operating cash flow was $1.29B (down from $1.48B, which included the insurance proceeds), and Q1 2026 FCF rose +29% to $371M. The balance sheet is asset-light, with ~$1.7B cash and $3.0B of fixed-rate senior notes laddered from 2028 to 2035, no revolver drawn (the $274M balance was repaid in late 2025). The Altman Z-Score of 1.3 should be disregarded as a solvency signal; it reflects minimal book equity (~$320M) produced by a decade of buybacks against a cash-generative model, not financial distress.

Capital allocation is the buyback machine. Gartner repurchased $2.0B of stock in 2025 (7.0M shares) and $535M in Q1 2026, reducing diluted share count from 78.3M to 70.0M (~10%). The board added $600M to the authorization in April 2026. This is the primary EPS lever: Q1 GAAP EPS rose +17.3% on essentially flat operating performance. Investors should read EPS growth as buyback-fueled rather than organic.

## Growth Analysis

The organic growth picture is stagnant. Wallet retention below 100% across both Insights segments indicates that the installed base is contracting in dollar terms, not expanding. Conferences (+11%) is the only segment growing at a healthy rate, and Consulting is declining.

The estimate revision trend is modestly positive: current-year adjusted EPS revised +4.2% over 30 days and +4.0% over 90 days; next year +4.2% / +5.9%. These are constructive but well short of the +30% velocity that signals an earnings recovery from a trough, and much of the EPS revision is mechanical, driven by buyback share-count reduction rather than revenue acceleration. Management did raise full-year adjusted EBITDA, adjusted EPS, and FCF guidance at the Q1 print and characterized contract value as "accelerating," but that acceleration is off a low base and does not yet establish a durable growth re-acceleration.

The federal exposure is a specific drag. Gartner retained less than half of its federal Insights contract value from December 2024 into 2025, with federal Insights CV falling to ~$126M and additional termination-for-convenience notices in Q1 2026. This is manageable as a share of a $5.2B book but signals how quickly contract value can erode when a customer cohort comes under budget pressure. Whether public-sector weakness foreshadows broader commercial softness is unresolved.

## Valuation Assessment

At ~$176.67 against adjusted FY26 EPS of $13.69 and FY27 of $15.34, Gartner trades at ~12.9x and ~11.5x forward earnings. That sits below the IT-services peer median of 14.6x (EPAM 14.6x, CTSH 11.6x, ACN 14.5x, IBM 22.6x) and far below Gartner's own 25-30x multiple of the prior decade. On a free-cash-flow basis, the FCF yield is attractive for a high-margin subscription business.

The market is wrong about something specific only if contract value and wallet retention stabilize. Gartner's subscription model, margins, and recurring revenue warrant a premium to lower-quality IT-services peers like EPAM and CTSH, which would put fair value above the current ~13x. That is the bull-case mispricing argument. The counterargument is that the compressed multiple is a rational discount for genuine structural risk: the AI displacement threat plus a base that is shrinking in dollar terms. A low multiple on a business with deteriorating leading indicators is a value trap until the indicators turn.

Consensus targets bracket the uncertainty: low $140, mean/median $167, high $203. With the stock now above the mean target and the multiple already compressed, the analyst-target-as-floor logic does not apply cleanly here; this is a stagnating franchise where the discount reflects a real, unresolved swing factor. We score valuation moderately positive (the cheapness is real) but cap it for the value-trap risk.

## Competitive Landscape

Gartner faces no single dominant competitor but diffuse pressure. The franchise still leads research and advisory by scale, brand, and analyst depth, and there is no near-term rival capable of replicating the proprietary content library. The trajectory, however, is the concern. Wallet retention falling across both segments suggests either budget pressure, reduced perceived value, or early AI substitution. Management itself flags low barriers to entry and LLM competition. The peer set has also de-rated heavily (EPAM -54% from 52-week high, ACN -45%, CTSH -38%), indicating the entire IT-services and advisory complex is being repriced for AI-driven demand uncertainty, not Gartner alone.

The structural debate is whether premium human-analyst research retains value as generative AI commoditizes information access. LLMs surfacing substantive answers reduce the incentive to visit Gartner content; clients can load proprietary research into internal models; and chatbots may train on Gartner-exposed datasets. Gartner launched AskGartner in August 2025 as a defensive AI product, a sensible response but not yet proof that the moat holds. Within the broader software bifurcation, where data-infrastructure providers benefit from AI while application-layer products face displacement, Gartner's packaged-information product sits closer to the at-risk side, despite its IT sector classification.

## Risk Assessment

**AI/LLM disruption (the central risk).** The 10-K devotes expanded language to this. It is partly priced into the 62% drawdown, but the magnitude of potential demand erosion is genuinely uncertain, and the falling wallet retention is the first tangible data point consistent with the bear thesis. This is the dominant swing factor in either direction.

**Contract value and wallet-retention erosion.** Wallet retention below 100% is the most concrete near-term risk and the metric most correlated with future revenue. A further leg down would convert "decelerating" into "declining."

**Securities class action.** Multiple firms filed §10(b) suits with a class period of Feb 4, 2025 – Feb 2, 2026 and a May 18, 2026 lead-plaintiff deadline, alleging Gartner misled investors about growth and projected revenue. The class period brackets the optimistic early-2025 guidance and the disappointing early-2026 disclosure. Gartner's liquidity can absorb a settlement, but it adds headline risk through 2026-2027.

**Federal government exposure.** The single-year loss of more than half of federal Insights CV shows how fast a cohort can shrink under spending cuts.

**Buyback timing.** The $2.0B repurchased in 2025 was largely executed at far higher prices than the current quote, which destroyed value as the stock fell. Continued buybacks near $160-177 are more sensible, but the track record on timing is poor.

**Short interest and squeeze dynamics.** At 24.2% of float and 7.5 days to cover, positioning is heavily bearish. This cuts both ways: it reflects genuine structural concern but also creates squeeze risk, and the ~9% move from $162 (May 29) to ~$177 (June 1) on heavy volume is consistent with short-covering pressure.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM IV is 57.3% against 54.6% historical vol — roughly fair in spread but high in absolute terms (65th percentile within the chain). The term structure is in **backwardation** (near-term 57.3% > long-term 52.3%), which signals the market is pricing imminent event risk, with a ±10.1% implied move on the June 18 expiry. IV compressed from 67.5% to 57.3% as the stock rallied 22% off the May 13 low, indicating some resolution of near-term uncertainty into the rally. The put/call OI ratio of 0.30 is call-heavy and skew is balanced (+2.6%), so options positioning is not aggressively bearish despite the high short interest, consistent with a two-sided setup. The backwardation and elevated absolute IV point to outcomes that resolve around discrete data points (next earnings, contract-value disclosure) rather than a steady trend.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case.** Contract value stabilizes and re-accelerates above 3% FX-neutral, wallet retention recovers toward 100%, and the AI fears prove overblown as clients continue to pay for synthesized, credentialed research that LLMs cannot reliably replicate. Strong FCF and a continued ~5-7% annual buyback compound EPS, and the multiple re-rates from ~13x toward the high teens as the growth scare fades. On adjusted FY27 EPS of $15.34 at 17-18x, the stock reaches ~$260-275. Analyst high target is $203.

**Bear case.** Generative AI structurally erodes demand for premium research, wallet retention continues to slide below 95%, contract value turns negative, and the securities litigation produces an extended overhang. The multiple stays at or below peer levels on declining earnings, and the buyback can no longer mask organic contraction. On flat-to-declining adjusted EPS at 11-12x, the stock revisits the $140 area (analyst low) or below.

Base case sits between: organic stagnation persists (CV +1-3%), buybacks keep EPS rising mid-single-digits, and the stock holds a low-teens multiple on slowly growing adjusted earnings. On ~$14.50 blended forward EPS at ~13-14x, fair value is roughly $190-200.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (Q2 2026, expected early August), which will provide updated contract value and wallet-retention figures — the two metrics that determine which thesis is playing out. The May 18, 2026 lead-plaintiff deadline and any subsequent litigation developments are secondary monitoring points.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Insights contract value reaccelerates above +3% FX-neutral for two consecutive quarters.
- GTS wallet retention recovers above 100% (currently 96%).
- Concrete evidence that AskGartner / AI-enabled products are driving net-new contract value rather than defending the base, demonstrating the moat survives AI.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- Contract value growth turns negative or wallet retention falls below 95%.
- The securities class action discloses a material, quantified liability or evidence of broader misstatement.
- Revenue declines accelerate beyond the divestiture effect (organic FX-neutral revenue worse than -3%).

## Conclusion

Gartner is a high-quality cash machine whose multiple has compressed to genuinely cheap levels, but it is cheap for a reason the company itself cannot yet refute: organic growth has stalled, the installed base is shrinking in dollar terms, and generative AI poses a credible threat to the value of packaged human research. The Q1 guidance raise, +29% FCF growth, and modestly rising estimates argue for stabilization; the +1% contract value, sub-100% wallet retention, federal CV collapse, securities litigation, and 24% short interest argue the de-rating is justified. At ~$177 the risk/reward is close to balanced, and wallet retention is the single metric that will reveal which way it resolves.

We rate IT a **HOLD (uncertain)**. The strong FCF, asymmetric upside if contract value stabilizes, and squeeze risk on a 24%-short float argue against shorting; the stagnant organic growth and unresolved AI overhang argue against buying. We would become constructive on a confirmed two-quarter inflection in contract value and wallet retention, and turn negative if those leading indicators deteriorate further.
