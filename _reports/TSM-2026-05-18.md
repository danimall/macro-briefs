## Executive Summary

TSMC is the world's dominant advanced semiconductor foundry, operating with near-monopoly control over leading-edge chip manufacturing at 7nm and below. Q1 2026 delivered the fourth consecutive record quarter: revenue of $35.9 billion (+41% YoY), net income up 58%, and gross margins at a record 66.2%. Management guided full-year 2026 revenue growth above 30% in USD terms and raised capex to the high end of the $52-56 billion range, with Q2 guided at $39-40.2 billion (10% sequential growth).

The investment case rests on three pillars: (1) structural monopoly positioning where most leading-edge AI accelerators rely on TSMC for manufacturing, (2) multi-year demand visibility with 2nm capacity sold out through 2026 and shortages expected beyond 2027, and (3) margin expansion driven by pricing power in supply-constrained advanced nodes. At approximately 25x FY2027 consensus EPS ($19.29, rising), the valuation is reasonable for a company compounding earnings at 25-30% with Q1 2026 operating margins of 58.1%.

The principal risk is geopolitical: the vast majority of TSMC's advanced capacity sits in Taiwan, and any military escalation in the Taiwan Strait would be catastrophic for the stock regardless of fundamental performance. This risk justifies a 2-3% position size rather than the larger allocation the fundamentals alone would warrant. Elevated Taiwan Strait tensions remain front-of-mind, though TSMC's accelerating geographic diversification (Arizona, Japan, Germany) provides a slow-moving partial hedge.

## Company Overview

TSMC operates as a pure-play foundry: it manufactures semiconductor chips designed by its customers but does not design or sell its own chips. This business model positions the company as essential infrastructure for the entire semiconductor industry, collecting revenue from all sides of competitive battles (Nvidia vs. AMD vs. Google custom silicon vs. Amazon Trainium — all manufactured at TSMC).

Revenue composition in Q1 2026: High Performance Computing (HPC), which includes AI-related demand, represented 61% of revenue, smartphones approximately 25%, and the remainder split across IoT, automotive, and other segments. Advanced nodes (7nm and below) comprised 74% of wafer revenue, with 3nm at 25%. This mix shift toward AI and advanced nodes is the primary driver of margin expansion.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Trajectory:** Q1 2026 revenue of $35.9B represents ~41% YoY growth, the fourth consecutive record quarter, and the eighth consecutive quarter of double-digit growth. Q2 2026 is guided at $39-40.2B, implying approximately 30-34% YoY growth. Full-year 2026 guidance of >30% revenue growth (raised from prior guidance) translates to approximately $158B or more in annual revenue, based on 2025 full-year revenue of $121.4B.

**Profitability:** Q1 2026 gross margin of 66.2% is a company record, up from approximately 60% two years ago. Operating margin at 58.1% demonstrates extraordinary operating leverage. Net income of $17.6B in Q1 alone ($70B annualized run-rate) grew 58% YoY. The margin expansion occurring simultaneously with massive global expansion (which typically dilutes margins) confirms pricing power in supply-constrained advanced nodes.

**Capital Expenditure:** $52-56B planned for 2026 (now expected at high end), up ~37% YoY. This includes five simultaneous 2nm fabs in Taiwan, continued Arizona expansion, Japan second fab, and Germany construction. The capex-to-revenue ratio of approximately 35% is high but supported by pre-sold capacity and multi-year demand visibility.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow is sufficient to fund most of the capex program internally. The company maintains a consistent dividend (ADR yield ~0.87%) and does not need external financing for its expansion.

**Earnings Trajectory:** Consensus EPS estimates show approximately $15.45 for FY2026 and $19.29 for FY2027, per analyst consensus data. The company has consistently beaten consensus in recent quarters.

## Growth Analysis

TSMC's growth drivers are structural rather than cyclical:

1. **AI infrastructure buildout:** Hyperscalers are spending $200B+ annually on AI infrastructure. Most leading-edge AI accelerator chips are manufactured at TSMC regardless of designer. AI-related revenue is growing >50% in 2026 per management guidance.

2. **Technology node advancement:** The transition from 5nm to 3nm to 2nm drives higher ASPs per wafer. Each node generation delivers ~15-20% higher revenue per wafer at comparable volumes.

3. **Advanced packaging (CoWoS):** Increasingly critical for AI chip performance, and TSMC dominates this capability. CoWoS capacity is the current binding constraint on AI chip supply.

4. **Geographic expansion:** Arizona (reported profitable in its first full year of mass production), Japan (second fab shifting to 3nm per Bloomberg reporting, expected by 2028), and Germany provide growth avenues while reducing geographic concentration risk.

5. **Market expansion:** Management has indicated an expectation for the global semiconductor market to reach $1.5T by 2030 (up from prior $1T estimate), with AI as the primary growth driver.

The key question is durability. With 2nm capacity sold out through 2026 and shortages projected beyond 2027, near-term demand risk is minimal. Medium-term risk comes from potential cyclical correction in AI spending — but the breadth of customers (all major hyperscalers plus Nvidia, AMD, Qualcomm, Apple) and applications (training, inference, edge AI) suggests this is structural demand rather than a single-customer bet.

## Competitive Landscape

TSMC's competitive position is arguably the strongest of any company in global technology:

- **No peer at the leading edge:** Intel Foundry has failed to execute at 7nm and below. Samsung Foundry suffers chronic yield issues at advanced nodes. SMIC in China is constrained by EUV export controls. No competitor is likely to credibly threaten TSMC's advanced node dominance before 2028-2029 at the earliest, based on publicly disclosed competitor roadmaps and yield data.

- **Widening moat:** The gap between TSMC and competitors is widening. Intel has repeatedly delayed its foundry roadmap. Samsung lost orders (reportedly from Qualcomm and others) due to yield issues. TSMC's 2nm ramp puts it further ahead.

- **Structural barriers:** Advanced chip manufacturing requires $20B+ per fab, decades of process expertise, thousands of specialized engineers, and deep customer relationships. The switching cost for customers (2-3 years to requalify at a new foundry) creates enormous lock-in.

- **Platform positioning:** TSMC benefits from every AI architecture advancement. When Nvidia launches new GPUs, when Google designs new TPUs, when Amazon expands Trainium — all require TSMC. This eliminates "picking the wrong horse" risk within AI.

The competitive trajectory is improving. TSMC is scoring more advanced packaging wins, more custom silicon programs, and greater node leadership with each generation. The only realistic competitive threat is political (forced technology transfer) rather than commercial.

## Risk Assessment

**Taiwan Strait Geopolitical Risk (HIGH):** Approximately 85-90% of TSMC's advanced manufacturing capacity remains in Taiwan. Military escalation — even a blockade without invasion — would devastate the stock. Elevated U.S.-China tensions regarding Taiwan remain a persistent overhang. Geographic diversification (Arizona, Japan, Germany) will take 5-10 years to meaningfully reduce this concentration.

**Customer Concentration (MODERATE):** Nvidia, Apple, AMD, Amazon, and Google account for a disproportionate share of revenue. If AI spending enters an "air pocket," the 61% HPC revenue concentration creates vulnerability. This is partially mitigated by the breadth of applications and the structural nature of AI infrastructure spending.

**Capex Intensity (MODERATE):** $56B annual capex creates significant fixed-cost obligations. If demand were to slow unexpectedly, the combination of high depreciation and committed expansion would compress margins and FCF. Sold-out capacity through 2027 reduces this risk in the near term.

**Export Control Risk (LOW-MODERATE):** US restrictions on China chip exports could evolve to restrict TSMC's ability to serve certain customers or require additional compliance costs. TSMC has already restricted shipments to Huawei per US requirements.

**Middle East Conflict (LOW):** Management addressed potential disruptions to specialty gas supplies (helium, hydrogen) from Middle East conflict on the Q1 2026 call but maintains multiple suppliers and safety inventory. No near-term operational impact expected.

## Conclusion

TSMC represents the highest-quality direct exposure to the AI infrastructure buildout, combining dominant positioning, record profitability, accelerating growth, and multi-year demand visibility. The stock at ~25x FY2026 and ~20x FY2027 earnings — with those estimates rising — offers adequate compensation for the geopolitical risk that is the single dominant concern.

The BUY recommendation reflects: (1) consensus estimates still rising rapidly and likely conservative given TSMC's recent beat streak, (2) competitive moat widening with no credible threat at advanced nodes for several years based on competitor roadmaps, (3) forward valuation of ~20x FY2027 EPS providing margin of safety relative to growth rate (PEG ~0.7x), and (4) platform positioning ensuring TSMC benefits regardless of which specific AI chip architecture wins.

The recommendation carries an explicit position-size constraint of 2-3% to manage the non-diversifiable Taiwan geopolitical tail risk, which remains elevated. This is a high-conviction fundamental thesis with a hard constraint from a single exogenous risk factor that cannot be hedged at reasonable cost.
