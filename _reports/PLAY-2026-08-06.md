## Executive Summary

Dave & Buster's has become a small equity claim on a large, levered enterprise. At $10.45 the market capitalization is $363.5M against economic net debt of approximately $1,931M excluding operating leases, so equity represents 15.8% of enterprise value. Trailing twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA of $423.7M implies an EV/EBITDA of 5.4x and, more importantly, a break-even multiple of 4.56x — below that level the equity is worth nothing. One turn of EBITDA multiple equals $12.18 per share, or 116% of the current price. Every analytical question about this stock reduces to whether EBITDA stabilizes.

The evidence says it has not. Comparable store sales were -5.0% in fiscal 2025 and -5.4% in Q1 fiscal 2026, with management attributing both to reduced walk-in traffic. Entertainment revenue — 62.9% of the mix at a 91.9% gross margin — declined 4.9% then 5.9%, so the deterioration is concentrated in the highest-contribution line. Adjusted EBITDA has fallen from $555.6M (FY2023) to $423.7M TTM, a 21.4% decline, and roughly 25% on a like-for-like basis once occupancy payments now booked as interest on failed sale-leaseback properties are added back. Revenue per store operating week fell 5.9% in Q1 to $177K. The company delivered four consecutive earnings misses, the most recent by 74%, and consensus next-year EPS has moved from +$0.09 to -$0.55 over ninety days.

Three facts prevent this from being a distress trade and three prevent it from being an opportunity. Not distress: the revolver matures November 2029 and the incremental term loans November 2031, liquidity is $499.1M, and the December 2025 covenant amendment lifted the maximum net total leverage test from 3.50x to 4.00x, taking headroom from roughly 5% to roughly 17%. Not an opportunity: pre-tax ROIC of 3.4% sits well below a 7.9% weighted-average cost of debt, so incremental capital destroys value; the turnaround's author resigned on August 3, 2026, seven weeks after guiding to positive comps; and the base case on plausible multiple and EBITDA assumptions clears at roughly the current price with a left tail that goes to zero. We maintain **AVOID**, and — given 41.8% short interest, 5.5 days to cover, and call-heavy near-term options skew — explicitly decline to recommend it as a short.

## Company Overview

The company operated 243 venues at February 3, 2026 (179 Dave & Buster's across 43 states, Puerto Rico and Canada; 64 Main Event across 22 states), updated to 247 after Q2 openings. The two brands are managed and reported as a single operating segment with two reporting units, so Main Event's standalone economics — acquired in fiscal 2022 — are not visible to outside investors.

The economics are arcade-first. Fiscal 2025 entertainment revenue of $1,323.5M (62.9% of total) carried a 91.9% gross margin; food and beverage of $779.3M ran at roughly a 75% product margin. Cash is collected in advance through game cards and gift cards and recognized as credits are consumed: $43.0M of entertainment deferrals and $8.8M of gift card deferrals sitting on the February 2026 balance sheet were recognized in Q1, about 9.3% of quarterly revenue. That is a structural feature of the model, not a one-off benefit, and it is why conventional working-capital and current-ratio screens misread this balance sheet.

Growth initiatives are a shift to smaller formats (fiscal 2025 D&B openings averaged roughly 28,000 sq ft versus a fleet average of 36,800, with future targets near 25,000) and capital-light international franchising — five partnerships covering India, Australia, the Dominican Republic, the Philippines and Mexico, 37 planned locations, six open as of June 2026. Franchise revenue sits inside a combined "Other" line of $5.6M in Q1 and is immaterial to the thesis on any near-term view.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and comps.** Revenue peaked at $2,205.3M in FY2023 (a 53-week year) and has declined in each subsequent year to $2,102.8M in FY2025, despite store count rising from roughly 217 to 243. Comparable revenue fell $98.0M in FY2025, partly offset by $75.0M from new stores. In Q1 FY2026, comparable revenue fell $29.2M against $18.1M of noncomparable contribution. Total revenue was $559.2M, down 1.5%. The store base is masking the underlying decline, which means the mask gets thinner as the new-unit pipeline shrinks.

**Margins.** Product costs genuinely improved: food and beverage cost fell from 26.4% to 24.8% of F&B revenue and entertainment cost from 8.5% to 8.1% of entertainment revenue, leaving blended product gross margin higher year over year at 85.7%. The operating margin collapse from 10.3% to 4.1% is a fixed-cost and depreciation story. Payroll rose $12.3M on falling revenue (24.5% → 25.5% of sales), other store operating rose $34.7M (32.4% → 34.5%), and depreciation and amortization rose $41.2M to $279.4M — 13.3% of revenue, an extraordinary figure that reflects the FY2023-24 capex boom now running through the P&L against a smaller revenue base.

One cost lever deserves flagging: management attributes part of the entertainment cost improvement to "lower redemptions due to certain ticket payout adjustments and redemption center pricing changes." Customers now receive fewer tickets per game and/or surrender more tickets per prize. That lever has a finite runway and is being pulled in precisely the category where traffic is falling. A benign reading — that prior payouts were over-generous — is equally consistent with the disclosure; the two cannot be separated from what is filed.

**Cash flow, and what "free cash flow" means here.** The company defines Adjusted Free Cash Flow as operating cash flow less capital expenditures **plus payments from landlords on sale-leaseback transactions**. On that basis Q1 FY2026 was +$25.3M (113.8 − 105.3 + 16.8). On a conventional basis (OCF less capex) the company burned $217.9M in FY2024 and $100.6M in FY2025, turning modestly positive at +$8.5M in Q1 FY2026 only after capex was cut. Management's fiscal 2026 guide of "over $100 million in free cash flow" is on the adjusted definition and is therefore partly a real-estate monetization target. Sale-leaseback proceeds are a legitimate recurring funding source for a real-estate-heavy operator, and the definition is disclosed in a footnote, but the label is not comparable to how most investors use the term.

Q1's $113.8M of operating cash flow also included a $27.1M accounts payable build. Excluding working capital, cash from operations was approximately $87.2M against $105.3M of cash capex — roughly -$18M before payables timing and before landlord proceeds. Q1 is seasonally strong and Q3 is the weakest quarter per the 10-K, so this is a sensitivity rather than a run-rate.

The more durable concern is reinvestment adequacy. FY2025 maintenance capital ($45.0M) plus games ($58.3M) plus remodels ($60.9M) totaled $164.2M against $279.4M of D&A. Remodel spending fell 74% year over year and is running near $32M annualized in fiscal 2026, with only eight D&B remodels planned for the entire year. Positive adjusted free cash flow is being produced in part by deferring reinvestment in a physical-experience business whose competitive premise is the quality of the venue.

**Capital structure at May 5, 2026.** Cash $19.6M; revolver drawn $150.0M; term loans $1,380.5M face; failed sale-leaseback financing liability $387.2M; finance leases $33.0M. Economic net debt excluding operating leases is $1,931.1M, or 4.56x TTM Adjusted EBITDA. Adjusting EBITDA downward by the $27.2M of occupancy payments recorded as interest gives a like-for-like 4.87x. On a rating-agency lease-adjusted basis — $3,576.3M of total obligations against EBITDAR near $637.7M — leverage is approximately 5.6x. TTM interest expense of $154.1M consumes 36% of Adjusted EBITDA; adding roughly $214M of annualized operating lease cost, financing plus rent absorbs about 58% of EBITDAR.

Pre-tax ROIC is 3.4% (TTM operating income $69.8M over roughly $2,031M of invested capital excluding operating leases), against a 7.9% weighted-average effective interest rate. Even normalizing for $36.9M of FY2025 impairments and disposal losses, returns sit below the cost of debt. That is the central financial fact: the enterprise is not earning its capital cost, and the equity is the residual.

Book equity of $99.6M sits beneath $920.8M of goodwill and tradenames, implying roughly -$821M of tangible book value. Treasury stock of $1,146.6M for 28.72M shares implies an average repurchase cost near $39.92 — approximately four times the current price — executed while net debt rose by roughly $890M.

**The covenant.** The reported net total leverage ratio was 3.33x at May 5, 2026 ($1,536.2M covenant net debt over $461.9M Credit Adjusted EBITDA) against a 4.00x maximum. Credit Adjusted EBITDA adds back pre-opening costs ($18.4M), impairments and disposal losses ($35.0M), share-based compensation ($19.1M) and $19.8M of facility-specific items, and the covenant debt definition excludes the $387.2M sale-leaseback financing liability. The gap between the covenant's 3.33x and our 4.6-4.9x economic estimate is fully explained by contractual, disclosed definitions.

Headroom: Credit Adjusted EBITDA can fall to $384.1M before breaching, roughly 17%. Under the pre-amendment 3.50x test the threshold would have been $438.9M, leaving 5.0% at the same balance sheet. The most plausible reading of the December 2025 amendment is that it pre-empted a breach the comp trajectory made likely within a few quarters; management may equally have sought flexibility proactively, and the filings state no reason. Two mechanical points matter: the required EBITDA floor rises $25M for every incremental $100M drawn on the revolver, and cutting new-unit growth reduces Credit Adjusted EBITDA by shrinking the pre-opening add-back, partially offsetting the leverage benefit of lower capex.

All funded debt is floating (Term SOFR + 3.25%) following the FY2024 redemption of the $440M of 7.625% notes. Item 3 discloses $15.3M of annual sensitivity per 100bp, and no interest rate hedges appear in the sections reviewed. This is a live exposure given that all funded debt floats.

A drafting note that should not be misread: the Q1 press release's forward-looking paragraph references going-concern risk and covenant waivers. No audited filing contains a going-concern qualification; management affirms twelve-month liquidity sufficiency and covenant compliance. Treat the press-release language as legacy template text from the COVID-era relief period.

## Growth Analysis

There is no growth thesis on the current evidence, and the estimate revision data requires careful reading.

The automated revision summary reads "estimates broadly rising." That is an artifact of applying percentage-change logic to negative numbers over a 30-day window. The substantive picture: current-quarter consensus fell from $0.33 to $0.19 over ninety days (-43%); current-year consensus fell from -$0.37 to -$0.89; next-year consensus fell from +$0.09 to -$0.55. Consensus has moved from expecting a return to profitability next year to expecting a loss. The 30-day moves (+3.0%, +2.8%, 0.0%) are rounding on small negative figures, not momentum. The strong positive-revision signal that would ordinarily argue for weighting forward estimates over trailing results is absent here; the revision vector points the other way.

The delivery record reinforces this. Four consecutive misses: -65.9%, -18.3%, -384.2%, -74.1%. A company that has missed four straight times, guided to positive comps for the balance of the year, and then lost the CEO who issued that guidance does not warrant confidence in consensus.

Three genuine positives deserve acknowledgment. First, food and beverage revenue grew 5.1% in FY2025 and 6.5% in Q1, credited to the "back-to-basics" menu and Eat & Play Combo attach rates — the restaurant half of the business is working. Second, the small-format strategy appears to preserve productivity: Q1 noncomparable stores generated roughly $150.6K per operating week versus $175.9K for the comp base, but on an estimated 34,000 sq ft blended footprint that translates to approximately $4.43 per sq ft per week versus the system's $4.32. Lower capital per store at comparable per-foot productivity is the right response to a declining-productivity fleet, though it also confirms new stores no longer earn the historical honeymoon premium. Third, international franchising is capital-light and scalable, with 37 planned locations against six open.

None of these offsets a -5.4% comp in a business where 63% of revenue and the overwhelming majority of gross profit come from the declining category. Guidance implies a swing from -5.4% to positive comps within three quarters. We have no basis to underwrite that, and the executive who issued it has left.

## Valuation Assessment

Peer P/E comparison is uninformative — PLAY's trailing and forward EPS are negative (-$1.40 trailing), and the restaurant peer median P/E of 33x reflects a set of businesses with materially different leverage. The analysis has to run through enterprise value.

At $10.45: market cap $363.5M, economic net debt $1,931.1M, EV $2,294.6M. That is 5.42x TTM Adjusted EBITDA of $423.7M, or 5.79x the like-for-like $396.5M that adds back occupancy costs booked as interest. Restaurant and experiential operators of similar quality typically transact in a 6-8x range, so on a headline basis the enterprise looks inexpensive. The equity does not follow, because of where it sits in the structure.

The sensitivity is extreme. One turn of EBITDA multiple equals $12.18 per share (116% of the price). A 10% change in EBITDA at a constant multiple equals $6.60 per share (63% of the price). The market is currently ascribing approximately 0.86 turns of EBITDA to the equity above the 4.56x break-even.

Scenario arithmetic on the frozen $10.45 basis:

| Case | EBITDA | Multiple | EV | Less net debt | Equity | Per share | Return |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear | $360M (-15%) | 5.0x | $1,800M | $1,931M | ~$0 | **$2 or less** | -81% or worse |
| Base | $410M (-3%) | 5.5x | $2,255M | $1,931M | $324M | **$9.30** | -11% |
| Bull | $465M (+10%) | 6.0x | $2,790M | $1,931M | $859M | **$24.70** | +136% |

Two observations. The base case — comps improving to roughly -2% to -3% and EBITDA declining modestly — clears at or slightly below the current price. And the bear case is not a drawdown; it is a wipeout, because a 15% EBITDA decline at a 5.0x multiple puts enterprise value below net debt. The distribution is not "cheap with downside"; it is a wide two-tailed option where the negative tail terminates at zero.

Analyst targets — low $12.00, mean $17.00, median $14.00, high $25.00, with three buys and eight holds — all sit above the market price. On a name with sharply negative ninety-day revisions, four consecutive misses and a CEO change, stale targets above the market are not independent confirmation of value; they are lagging indicators being revised down (BMO reportedly cut from $24 to $22 in June, per secondhand reporting). We use the disclosed $12-25 range as the reported target band, not as a floor.

We score valuation 4.0. This is not a value trap in the sense of a permanently compressed multiple on stable earnings — the convexity is real and the enterprise multiple is defensible. But asymmetric upside requires either a specific mispricing we can identify or a catalyst that stabilizes EBITDA, and we can identify neither. A 5.4x enterprise multiple on a declining, capital-intensive, secularly challenged cash flow stream financed with floating-rate debt at 4.6-4.9x economic leverage is approximately the right price.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K defines competition unusually broadly: movie theaters, sporting events, bowling, arcades, nightclubs, restaurants, theme parks, home internet and video gaming, streaming, **legalized casino gambling and online sports betting**. That breadth is a disclosure of the actual problem. The company's moat is the bundle — food, alcohol, games, large-format sports viewing in one location, which it argues "cannot be easily replicated at home or elsewhere without having to visit multiple destinations" — and none of the individual components is defensible.

The redemption-game core is the most substitutable part of the offering against the fastest-growing competitor for the same discretionary dollar. Legal sports betting and expanded casino access deliver the identical psychological product — small stakes, variable reward, social context — with no travel cost and no ticket-redemption friction. That is a secular headwind, not a cyclical one, and it lands directly on the 63% of revenue carrying a 92% gross margin.

Two data points argue the decline is at least partly company- or category-specific rather than purely macro. Casual dining peers are stabilizing — Dine Brands reported IHOP outperformance with Applebee's showing slow improvement in Q2 2026 — while PLAY's comps remain at -5.4%. And competitive supply continues to be funded: Level99 reportedly added $50M to fund expansion of its gaming concept (a single, unverified sidebar reference, treated as directional only). Meanwhile the company's own defense of the venue experience has weakened: remodel capital fell 74% in FY2025 and only eight remodels are planned for all of fiscal 2026, while ticket payout adjustments reduce the perceived value of game play.

Long-lived asset impairments of $17.8M in FY2025 (against $3.9M and $1.7M in the two prior years) plus $19.1M of losses on property and equipment corroborate that a subset of the fleet is not earning its keep. Goodwill and tradenames of $920.8M were tested qualitatively with no impairment concluded, against a $363.5M market capitalization — a conclusion worth monitoring at the next annual test.

Trajectory, which matters more than level: deteriorating. Score 3.5.

## Risk Assessment

**Leverage against a declining base.** Economic net leverage of 4.6-4.9x with EBITDA down 21-25% from peak, comps still -5.4%, and interest consuming 36% of Adjusted EBITDA. Covenant headroom of roughly 17% requires Credit Adjusted EBITDA to stay above $384.1M. The covenant test is described as applying "when applicable," which may indicate a springing test tied to revolver utilization rather than an unconditional quarterly test — unresolved from the filings and material to breach probability.

**Floating-rate exposure.** $15.3M of annual pre-tax cost per 100bp with no disclosed hedges. With all funded debt floating, this is an active exposure rather than a modeling footnote.

**Consumer exposure.** Walk-in traffic to discretionary entertainment venues is among the first line items households cut. The historical record for buy-side selection in Consumer Discretionary is poor even before adding leverage.

**Management discontinuity.** Three CEOs in roughly twenty months. Chris Morris departed December 2024; Tarun Lal was hired July 2025 after a seven-month vacancy and resigned August 3, 2026 after thirteen months, citing family in India, with an advisory arrangement through at least fiscal 2027. CFO Darin Harper becomes CEO; Cory Hatton is interim CFO with a search underway. The CMO, Chief Legal Officer and Chief Technology & Digital Officer were all appointed in June 2026 — the team that must execute was assembled in the final weeks of the tenure of the CEO who hired them. The August 4 8-K was filed under Items 5.02 and 9.01 only, with no Item 2.02 or 7.01, so on the filing record no guidance update accompanied the change. Whether the June guidance stands is unconfirmed. The stock fell roughly 8% on the announcement.

**Lease and landlord concentration.** $1,645.2M of operating lease liabilities against $1,295.1M of right-of-use assets — a $350.1M excess consistent with landlord incentives received and/or above-market rents. Leases generally cannot be cancelled without substantial economic penalty, and venue traffic depends on regional malls, lifestyle centers and big-box centers.

**Tariffs and supply concentration.** A significant percentage of inventory is sourced outside the United States from a limited game-supplier base, with tariff cost pressure cited in H2 FY2025 and continuing in Q1 FY2026.

**Tail items.** No amounts have been remitted to any state for unredeemed gift cards based on the company's own legal assessment, with disclosure that a successful state challenge could make liabilities materially higher; deferred gift card revenue was $16.5M. Expanding state gambling regimes could force changes to game mix, ticket-win limits or prize offerings. An FTC settlement requires maintenance of an information security program, with no monetary assessments.

**What is not a risk.** There is no near-term maturity wall — revolver November 2029, incremental term loans November 2031 — and $499.1M of liquidity. The Altman Z-Score of 0.43 substantially overstates near-term bankruptcy probability: the working-capital and equity terms are structurally penalized by a negative-working-capital prepayment model and $1,146.6M of treasury stock. Its underlying message about the absence of an equity cushion is nonetheless corroborated independently.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility is 78.3% against 71.4% realized — modestly rich, but at only the 4th percentile within the current chain, and down sharply from 141.7% on June 8 (pre-Q1 print), which reflects post-event vol crush rather than resolved uncertainty. The term structure is in contango: 78.3% at 14 days, 97.9% at the September 18 tenor, 82.9% at January 2027. The September tenor prices a ±27.3% move and spans the Q2 report — that is where the market has placed the event risk, and correctly so given the guidance question left open by the CEO transition.

Two positioning signals cut against the bear case tactically. Put/call open interest has collapsed from 2.18 to 0.88 since June, and near-term skew is call-heavy at -8.6% (with the September tenor at +4.5%, i.e. put-tilted through the print). Combined with 41.8% short interest of float and 5.5 days to cover, the near-dated structure looks like squeeze positioning rather than accumulating downside protection. For a fundamentally negative view, this is the wrong tape on which to be short. Options data do not change our fundamental assessment; they change the recommended expression of it.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull case ($23-25).** Comps inflect on the back-to-basics menu, the refreshed remodel specification and tripled game investment ($18.0M to $58.3M), reaching flat-to-positive by Q4. Adjusted EBITDA recovers toward $465M. Capex stays near $350M, GAAP free cash flow turns durably positive, and net debt falls. The enterprise re-rates to 6.0x, producing roughly $859M of equity value — $24.70 per share, a 136% gain. With 41.8% of the float short and 5.5 days to cover, the move would be violent and front-loaded. Harper's continuity (Main Event CFO, CEC Entertainment controller) plus a Hill Path-affiliated board seat and 22% insider ownership create a plausible strategic-alternatives path — sale-leaseback of the remaining owned real estate, a Main Event divestiture, or a whole-company transaction at enterprise multiples the equity stub magnifies. *Note: Hill Path's specific stake size is unquantified in the sources; the ~22% figure is aggregate ownership by all directors and officers.*

**Bear case ($2 or less).** Comps stay at -4% to -6% as sports betting and casino expansion continue to absorb the redemption-game wallet and the consumer discretionary environment stays soft. Adjusted EBITDA declines to $360M. Credit Adjusted EBITDA approaches the $384.1M covenant floor, forcing either an equity-value-destroying amendment, asset sales at distressed prices, or a capital raise at a fraction of the current price. Enterprise value at 5.0x on $360M is $1,800M — below net debt of $1,931M. The equity is a residual claim that goes to approximately zero without any bankruptcy filing required. Deferred reinvestment (maintenance plus games plus remodels of $164.2M against $279.4M of D&A) compounds the traffic problem into fiscal 2027-28.

**Base case ($9.30).** Comps improve to roughly -2% to -3% as new formats and menu work partially offset traffic decline. Adjusted EBITDA settles near $410M. The multiple holds at 5.5x, producing $324M of equity value and an 11% loss from $10.45. Free cash flow on the adjusted definition meets or approaches the $100M guide, but a meaningful share is landlord proceeds rather than operating generation, so net debt is roughly flat. Covenant compliance holds without incident.

The base case delivers no expected return, the bull case requires an EBITDA inflection that four consecutive quarters of misses and sharply negative estimate revisions do not support, and the bear case is a total loss rather than a drawdown. That distribution is why we avoid.

## Short Candidate Assessment

**Catalyst.** Q2 FY2026 results, expected early-to-mid September 2026 (quarter ended approximately August 4). The specific test is whether the new CEO reaffirms, revises or withdraws the June 15 guidance of positive comps for the remainder of the year and over $100M of adjusted free cash flow. A withdrawal or a fifth consecutive miss would be materially negative. Secondary catalysts: the FY2026 annual goodwill and tradename test on $920.8M of intangibles against a $363.5M market capitalization, and quarterly covenant reporting against the $384.1M Credit Adjusted EBITDA floor.

**Timing window.** Roughly six weeks to the Q2 print. The September 18 options tenor at 97.9% implied volatility and a ±27.3% implied move confirms the market has identified the same event.

**Short interest and squeeze risk.** Short interest is 41.8% of float — 11.33M shares against 34.79M outstanding — with 5.5 days to cover. Insiders and directors hold approximately 22%, which materially reduces true tradable float and amplifies the effective crowding. This is close to the practical ceiling for a shortable equity.

**Borrow and options signal.** Options positioning contradicts the bearish thesis at the front end. Put/call open interest fell from 2.18 to 0.88 since June, near-term skew is call-heavy at -8.6%, and near-term implied volatility sits at the 4th percentile of the chain. Implied volatility of 78.3% at 14 days against 71.4% realized means paying up for protection while the structure suggests other participants are positioned for upside. Carrying cost on a 41.8%-short name is likely substantial.

**Downside target and risk/reward.** Bear target $2 (-81%); realistic near-term downside on a guidance withdrawal is perhaps $7 (-33%). Upside risk on a comp inflection, guidance reaffirmation, or any strategic-alternatives headline is $18-25 (+72% to +139%), with squeeze mechanics that would compress the move into days. On the realistic near-term case that is roughly 33% reward against 72%+ risk — well under 1:1.

**Verdict: CROWDED SHORT.** The thesis is directionally sound but the mechanics are prohibitive. Short interest at 41.8% of a float reduced by 22% insider ownership, 5.5 days to cover, a stock already 63% below its 52-week high, call-heavy near-term skew, and an asymmetry that runs against the position all argue for avoidance rather than active shorting. Anyone determined to express the view should do so through defined-risk long puts at the September tenor rather than borrowed stock, accepting that a ±27.3% implied move means the option is not cheap.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: AVOID. Expected duration of headwinds: four to eight quarters.** The comp decline has persisted for seven-plus quarters, the depreciation burden from the FY2023-24 capex cycle runs through the P&L for several more years, and the competitive pressure from sports betting and casino expansion is structural. A credible turnaround requires two consecutive quarters of comp improvement plus a permanent CFO, neither of which can be observed before roughly Q4 FY2026 (reporting spring 2027).

**Re-entry criteria — any two of the following would move us to HOLD; all four to a constructive view:**

1. **Comparable store sales improve to -2.0% or better for two consecutive quarters**, with entertainment revenue decline narrowing to under 3%. This is the single variable that determines everything else; entertainment is where the margin lives.
2. **TTM Adjusted EBITDA stabilizes above $430M** with Credit Adjusted EBITDA holding above $450M, restoring covenant headroom above 20% without a further amendment.
3. **A permanent CFO is appointed and management reaffirms or resets guidance on a conventional free-cash-flow basis** — operating cash flow less capex, excluding landlord proceeds — at a positive figure. Two consecutive quarters of positive GAAP free cash flow with maintenance plus games plus remodel capital approaching D&A would be the confirming evidence.
4. **Price implies under 4.2x EV/EBITDA on a stabilized $400M base** — approximately $2.5-3.0 per share on current net debt — at which point the option premium in the stub is priced closer to the distribution of outcomes.

**Conditions that would move us to an active short (from CROWDED SHORT to SHORT CANDIDATE):**
- Short interest falls below 25% of float while comps remain worse than -4%.
- Guidance is formally withdrawn, or a fifth consecutive miss arrives with comps deteriorating below -6%.
- Credit Adjusted EBITDA falls below $410M, putting the covenant within roughly 6% and making an amendment or capital raise probable within two quarters.

**Next reassessment: Q2 FY2026 results, early-to-mid September 2026.** The specific items to check are the comp print, whether guidance survives the CEO change, Adjusted EBITDA against the $384.1M covenant floor, and whether capex discipline holds or reverses.

## Conclusion

This is our fourth consecutive AVOID on PLAY, and the bar for repeating a call should rise each time. Two developments since our last review argue for reconsideration: the maturity profile is genuinely benign (2029 revolver, 2031 term loans, $499.1M of liquidity), and Q1 produced positive free cash flow on a conventional basis (+$8.5M) for the first time in years. We have raised financial health from 2.0 to 2.5 to reflect that the near-term solvency risk is lower than a 2.0 implies.

Everything else moved the wrong way. Comps deteriorated from -5.0% to -5.4%. Entertainment revenue decline accelerated from -4.9% to -5.9%. Next-year consensus EPS moved from +$0.09 to -$0.55 over ninety days, and current-year from -$0.37 to -$0.89. The CEO who authored the positive-comp guidance resigned seven weeks after issuing it, leaving an interim CFO and a C-suite appointed in June 2026.
