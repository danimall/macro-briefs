**Research Date: June 3, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Dollar General is the largest U.S. discount retailer by store count (20,959 stores across 48 states and Mexico), serving a low- and fixed-income customer base from small-box rural and small-town locations. The company is roughly two years into an operational turnaround led by returning CEO Todd Vasos, and FY2025 (year ended January 30, 2026) marked the turn: net income rose 34% to $1.51 billion, diluted EPS recovered to $6.85 from a $5.11 trough, operating margin expanded from 4.22% to 5.16%, and same-store sales accelerated to +3.0% from +1.4%. The recovery is self-help in nature, driven primarily by lower shrink, lower damages, higher markups, and SKU/inventory rationalization rather than by robust end-market demand.

The Q1 FY2026 print (reported June 2, 2026) extended the recovery: net sales +3.4% to $10.79 billion, comps +2% (traffic +1.4%), EPS of $2.00 beating consensus by roughly 3%, and a raised full-year EPS guidance of $7.20–$7.45. Management reiterated that DG continues to gain share as pressured consumers trade down, a dynamic corroborated across the value-retail sector (Dollar Tree posted a large beat and rose 17%; Five Below beat). At $106, the stock trades at roughly 14.5x guided EPS, below the 19.6x Consumer Staples retail peer median and roughly 33% below its February 2026 high of $158.

The stock has de-rated despite improving fundamentals because of a converging set of policy and governance overhangs. A federal SNAP benefit cliff is hitting DG's core customer (consumables are 82% of sales) precisely as the turnaround matures; the Work Opportunity Tax Credit expired for hires after December 31, 2025, threatening the effective tax rate; and an external CEO transition (JJ Fleeman from Ahold Delhaize, named March 24, 2026) introduces continuity risk amid a year of heavy executive churn. The investment question is whether these headwinds are now adequately discounted at ~14.5x. We think they largely are, which supports a HOLD with appreciation potential toward the high-$110s/low-$120s on continued execution, but the negative estimate-revision drift and management discontinuity prevent us from moving to a BUY.

## Company Overview

Dollar General operates a deliberately simple model: small-box stores (~7,500 sq ft of selling space, ~8,500 in the newer format) offering everyday necessities at everyday low prices, typically ≤$10 per item, located within five miles of roughly 75% of the U.S. population. Approximately 80% of stores sit in towns of 20,000 people or fewer, a rural footprint where grocery competition is sparse and which Walmart cannot easily replicate.

The FY2025 merchandise mix was consumables 82.0%, seasonal 10.1%, home 5.2%, apparel 2.7%. This skew is the central strategic tension: consumables drive traffic but carry the lowest margins, while seasonal/home/apparel carry the highest. The heavy consumable weighting structurally caps gross margin and ties the demand profile tightly to the financial health of low-income households.

Growth concepts include Project Renovate (full remodels) and the newer Project Elevate (partial remodels), with ~4,250+ combined remodels in FY2025; the Mexico format ("Mi Súper Dollar General," launched 2023); pOpshelf (a non-consumables concept now troubled and paused); DG Media Network (retail media); and delivery (myDG plus third parties). The strategic posture has shifted from footprint expansion to productivity of the existing base — new-store openings fell to 589 in FY2025 from 987 in FY2023, while remodels accelerated, a response to elevated new-store opening and occupancy costs.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and comps.** Revenue grew steadily: $37.8B (FY2023) → $38.7B (FY2024) → $42.7B (FY2025, +5.2%). FY2025 same-store sales (traffic +1.6%, ticket +1.4%) accelerated from the prior year. Q1 FY2026 sustained the trend.

**The margin story is the core narrative.** Operating profit collapsed from $3.33B (6.32% margin, FY2023) to a $1.71B trough (4.22%, year ended January 2025), then recovered to $2.20B (5.16%, FY2025). Net income moved $2.42B → $1.13B (trough) → $1.51B. FY2025 gross margin expanded 107 bps to 30.7%, driven primarily by lower shrink, higher markups, and lower damages, partly offset by a higher LIFO provision. The shrink-reduction effort is the dominant earnings lever — DG had at one point reported roughly $1B in lost or stolen merchandise, and profit recovered as that figure declined.

**Cash flow and balance sheet.** Operating cash flow rose to $3.63B in FY2025 (+$638M YoY); free cash flow was $2.39B (5.6% margin). Capex was disciplined down to $1.24B from $1.70B in FY2023 (fewer new stores, more remodels). Inventory was cut 6% (−7% per store) through SKU rationalization, a genuine working-capital improvement.

**Leverage is the watch item, not a solvency concern.** Total debt is $4.6B; D/E is 2.64; the Altman Z of 2.26 sits in the grey zone but largely reflects ~$13.7B of operating-lease obligations and retail asset intensity rather than distress. DG repaid $1.7B of long-term debt in FY2025 and redeemed three note tranches early. In March 2025 the company amended its revolver to loosen the leverage covenant and tighten the fixed-charge ratio (a "Covenant Relief Period"), during which it was barred from buybacks. Moody's downgraded DG to Baa3 from Baa2 in 2025; S&P holds it at BBB; both outlooks improved to Stable. ROE is 17.8%, ROA 4.9%.

**Capital allocation.** The dividend is held flat at $0.59/quarter (~$520M/yr, ~2.1% yield). There have been no share buybacks since 2022, with ~$1.38B of authorization dormant; management says it will resume "at the appropriate time." The clear priority is deleveraging and reinvestment over shareholder returns.

**Earnings quality and surprise track record.** DG has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters (Q1 FY2026 +3.0%, prior +13.7%, +31.4%, +15.2%). The beats lend credibility to the raised FY2026 guidance, but the surprise magnitudes are compressing as the easy shrink-driven comparisons lap.

## Growth Analysis

The 12-month growth case has three legs.

First, the self-help margin recovery has further runway. Shrink improved in FY2025 but damages remain elevated, leaving room for continued damage reduction to support gross margin. Inventory and SKU rationalization is ongoing. This is the controllable, visible portion of the story.

Second, the trade-down dynamic is benefiting DG and the broader value channel. Q1 management reiterated continued market-share gains as pressured consumers seek value, and the broad-based sector beats confirm the trend extends across discount retail. DG's everyday-low-price proposition to low-income shoppers is structurally well-positioned for share capture even in a soft macro.

Third, capital-return optionality is latent. With the covenant relief period behind it, ~$2.4B of annual FCF, and the dormant buyback authorization, DG has the capacity to resume repurchases once leverage targets are met — a potential re-rating catalyst on a 6–18 month horizon.

The constraints are equally specific. Consensus EPS estimates are drifting lower despite the beat: current-quarter estimates are −0.9% over 30 days and −3.9% over 90 days; current-year and next-year revisions are roughly flat to modestly negative. This negative revision momentum is the clearest counter-signal to the growth thesis, and it reflects the policy cliff (below) rather than operational deterioration. Future EPS growth must increasingly come from sales rather than shrink once the self-help runway exhausts, and the macro backdrop for the low-income consumer is hostile. Management's raised guidance implies low-single-digit EPS growth off the FY2025 base — solid but not the kind of acceleration that would justify a premium multiple.

## Valuation Assessment

At $106, DG trades at roughly 14.5x the midpoint of raised FY2026 guidance ($7.33) and ~15.5x trailing EPS. Against the Consumer Staples retail peer set, this is a clear discount: peer median P/E is 19.6x (DLTR 18.7x, BJ 19.6x, TGT 16.8x, PSMT 33.5x, COST 48.1x). DG screens cheapest in the cohort other than Target, which carries its own structural concerns.

The analyst target distribution sits well above spot — mean $138, median $139, low $90, high $175. The stock trades below even a level consistent with a modest re-rating toward the peer median. A 16x multiple on guided $7.33 implies ~$117; the peer-median 19.6x implies ~$144, broadly consistent with the analyst mean.

The valuation is genuinely supportive, but it is not deeply asymmetric for two reasons. First, the discount to peers is partly warranted: DG carries the highest consumable mix (most exposed to SNAP cuts), the most leverage, an unresolved WOTC tax headwind, and a CEO transition. Second, the negative estimate-revision drift means the forward "E" is at modest risk of being trimmed, which would lift the effective multiple. We therefore score valuation favorably (the market is plausibly over-discounting a recovering business that is gaining share) but stop short of treating the gap to analyst targets as a high-confidence upside, given falling estimates and a drawdown that reflects sophisticated sellers pricing the policy overhang.

## Competitive Landscape

DG's competitive position is stable-to-improving on trajectory. Direct competitors named in the 10-K are Walmart, Dollar Tree, and Family Dollar. DG's differentiation is rural convenience and proximity rather than the lowest absolute price — a defensible niche given the rural footprint and within-five-miles coverage. The trade-down cycle is currently expanding DG's relevant customer base, and FY2025 share gains plus accelerating comps support an improving-trajectory read.

The structural vulnerability is e-commerce. DG "currently does not offer traditional online shopping to a significant degree," a gap that matters as even low-income shoppers adopt delivery. This is partly mitigated by myDG/third-party delivery and the DG Media Network, with a delivery subscription pilot planned later in 2026, but DG is behind Walmart on omnichannel. Supplier concentration is moderate (top two vendors at 11% and 8% of purchases). The pOpshelf concept is a failed growth vector — expansion paused, stores closed/converted, and the majority of FY2024's $214.2M impairment recorded — but it is small relative to the core.

## Risk Assessment

**SNAP / government-benefit cliff (most acute, escalating).** The 10-K explicitly flags SNAP reductions, "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" work requirements, and certain states excluding product categories beginning January 2026. External data confirm severity: roughly 3.5M people lost SNAP access in six months, and food-restriction waivers are projected to reach 7.5M households by end-2026. With consumables at 82% of sales to a low-income base, this is a direct, quantifiable demand headwind, and management noted on the Q1 call that shoppers are already cutting back on food due to higher gas prices.

**WOTC expiration.** The Work Opportunity Tax Credit expired for hires after December 31, 2025. Immaterial in FY2025, but the 10-K warns of a "significant negative impact to the effective tax rate in future years" absent reauthorization — a structural EPS headwind not in trailing numbers.

**Shrink/damage reversion.** The entire FY2025 margin recovery rests on shrink and damage improvement. Damages remain elevated, and a reversion would directly reverse the EPS gains.

**Leverage and covenant constraint.** D/E of 2.64 and the March 2025 covenant relief amendment limit financial flexibility and delay buybacks. This is a flexibility constraint, not a solvency concern given ~$2.4B FCF and investment-grade ratings.

**Tariffs / IEEPA uncertainty.** ~4% of purchases are direct imports, but many domestic vendors import (substantial China exposure). The February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling invalidating IEEPA tariffs creates uncertainty over refunds and replacement tariffs — a live but not-yet-material swing factor.

Most of these risks are now visible in the drawdown and the negative estimate revisions, which is why we score risk near the midpoint rather than at the low end.

### Options Market Signal

The options market is pricing near-term stress. Near-term ATM IV is 53.7% against 1-year historical volatility of 37.2% (rich by 16.5 points, 69th percentile within the chain), and the term structure is in backwardation (53.7% near-term vs 42.3% at six months), indicating the market expects an imminent event to be resolved, consistent with post-earnings positioning and the ongoing policy-cliff debate. Put/call skew is balanced at 1.8% near-term and modestly call-tilted (negative skew) at one-to-six months, and the P/C open-interest ratio collapsed from 1.99 to 1.07 over the past week as IV fell 11.3 points alongside a 1.9% price gain. The net read is that bearish positioning has been unwinding into the beat rather than building — a mild positive for the holding case, not a divergence that contradicts the fundamental view.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case (~$144, +36%).** Shrink and damage reduction continue, FY2026 EPS lands at or above the top of the $7.45 guidance, trade-down share gains persist as the sector tailwind continues, the WOTC is reauthorized or the SNAP impact proves milder than feared, and DG resumes buybacks against the dormant $1.38B authorization. A re-rating toward the 19.6x peer median on ~$7.40 EPS supports ~$144, consistent with the analyst mean of $138.

**Bear Case (~$85, −20%).** The SNAP cliff compresses consumable traffic and ticket through 2026–27, the WOTC expiration lifts the tax rate and trims EPS, damages revert and gross margin gives back the FY2025 gains, and the external CEO disrupts continuity mid-turnaround. Estimates get cut to the low-$7s or below, and the stock de-rates toward 12x on a lower base, near the analyst low of $90.

**Base Case (~$117, +10%).** Comps stay in the +2–3% range, the self-help margin runway delivers modest further gains partly offset by the WOTC/tax drag, and EPS lands near the guidance midpoint of $7.33. A 16x multiple supports ~$117 on a 12-month view.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the FY2026 quarterly cadence (next print ~September 2026) and clarity on SNAP/WOTC policy effects flowing through H2 results.

**Upgrade triggers (to BUY):**
- Consensus EPS estimate revisions turn positive (current-year revisions moving from −0.2% toward +3% or better over 90 days), confirming the policy headwinds are milder than feared.
- Management formally resumes share buybacks, signaling the deleveraging priority is satisfied and confirming capital-return optionality.
- Two consecutive quarters of comps at or above +3% with gross-margin expansion sustained, demonstrating the recovery extends beyond shrink self-help into durable demand-side share gains.

**Downgrade triggers (to AVOID):**
- A quarterly comp turns negative or traffic declines, indicating the SNAP cliff is overwhelming the trade-down tailwind.
- FY2026 guidance is cut below $7.00, or the effective tax rate steps up materially from WOTC expiration without offset.
- Gross margin contracts year-over-year, signaling shrink/damage reversion and erosion of the entire FY2025 recovery mechanism.

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings (~September 2026) and the timing of any buyback resumption announcement.

## Conclusion

Dollar General presents a genuine operational turnaround — recovering margins, accelerating comps, four consecutive earnings beats, and a raised FY2026 outlook — trading at a clear discount to peers after a drawdown from its February high. The growth thesis for holding is concrete: the shrink/damage self-help runway is not exhausted, the trade-down dynamic is delivering broad-based value-retail share gains, and latent buyback capacity offers a re-rating catalyst once leverage targets are met. That combination supports appreciation toward the high-$110s base case over 12 months.

What keeps this a HOLD rather than a BUY is the convergence of a quantifiable policy cliff (SNAP benefit cuts hitting an 82%-consumables, low-income customer base; the WOTC expiration threatening the tax rate) with negative consensus estimate-revision drift and an external CEO transition arriving mid-turnaround against a backdrop of heavy 2025 executive churn. These risks are largely priced after the de-rating, but they cap the conviction and the forward "E" carries genuine downside skew. We rate DG a HOLD with a growth rationale, with a clear path to an upgrade on positive estimate revisions, buyback resumption, or two quarters of sustained comp and margin strength.
