## Executive Summary

United Bankshares is a $6 billion market cap regional bank headquartered in West Virginia with $33.7 billion in total assets, operating across 9 states in the Mid-Atlantic and Southeast. The company delivered strong FY2025 results: EPS of $3.27 (+18.9% YoY), NIM expansion of 29bp to 3.78%, and ROATCE of 13.95%, driven by its January 2025 Piedmont Bancorp acquisition and lower deposit costs. Q1 2026 continued the trend with $0.89 EPS beating consensus by 2.7%.

At $43.65 and 12.1x forward earnings, the stock appears reasonably valued relative to regional bank peers (median 13.7x), with a 3.5% dividend yield supported by a 46% payout ratio and 52 consecutive years of increases. The company is well-capitalized (CET1 13.44%) with ample liquidity. However, the investment case is constrained by CRE concentration risk (57% of loans in CRE/construction, including office exposure that already produced $21.8M in charge-offs in 2025), the inevitable fade of $33.7M in acquired loan accretion, and a macro environment where potential rate hikes could pressure asset quality in rate-sensitive portfolios.

The stock has appreciated 19.7% over the past year, reflecting the market's recognition of post-acquisition earnings power. At current levels, the risk/reward is roughly symmetric: upside to consensus targets ($47 median) requires continued clean credit performance that the CRE portfolio may not deliver given structural office market headwinds and a rising rate environment.

## Company Overview

United Bankshares operates through its sole banking subsidiary, United Bank, providing commercial and consumer banking services across West Virginia, Virginia, Maryland, Washington D.C., Pennsylvania, Ohio, North Carolina, South Carolina, and Georgia. The company has grown primarily through acquisitions — 33 bank acquisitions since 1982 — with the most recent being Piedmont Bancorp ($2.4 billion in assets, 16 branches) closed January 2025, providing entry into the Atlanta MSA.

The loan portfolio totals $24.7 billion, with commercial real estate representing the dominant category: non-owner occupied CRE at $8.34 billion (33.75% of loans), total CRE plus construction at approximately $14.1 billion (57% of loans). The company also maintains meaningful hotel/accommodations exposure at $1.6 billion (6.4% of loans). Deposits total approximately $27 billion, with noninterest-bearing demand deposits comprising 24% of the base.

## Financial Analysis

**Profitability:**
FY2025 net interest income grew 21% to $1.102 billion, with NIM expanding 29bp to 3.78%. This was driven by two factors: (1) a 35bp decline in deposit costs as rate cuts allowed repricing, and (2) $33.7 million in acquired loan accretion from Piedmont (vs. $9.3 million in 2024). Net income reached $464.6 million, generating ROAA of 1.41% and ROATCE of 13.95%.

The efficiency ratio is approximately 48.5%, competitive for a bank of this size. Noninterest income of $135.2 million (+9.3%) was partially offset by the intentional runoff of mortgage banking operations, which declined 40% as the company exited third-party origination.

**Capital and Liquidity:**
CET1 of 13.44% and total risk-based capital of 15.72% leave substantial buffers above regulatory minimums. Tangible common equity is approximately $3.3 billion after deducting $2.02 billion in goodwill. Unused FHLB borrowing capacity of $9.19 billion and Fed Discount Window capacity of $4.66 billion provide deep liquidity reserves.

**Capital Return:**
Total capital return of $335.5 million in 2025 (72% of net income), comprising $209 million in dividends and $126.5 million in share repurchases (3.59 million shares at an average of $35.24). The repurchases at $35.24 represent a 24% return at current prices — well-timed execution by management.

**Asset Quality:**
Provision for credit losses more than doubled to $53.9 million. Nonperforming assets increased 50% to $110.3 million, with nonaccrual loans up 71% to $96.5 million. Net charge-offs on non-owner occupied CRE jumped from 0.03% to 0.45%, driven by two Northern Virginia office loans from the same sponsor whose financial condition "significantly deteriorated." The allowance-to-NPL coverage ratio declined from 370% to 293%.

**Q1 2026:**
Net income of $124.2 million ($0.89/share) beat consensus by 2.7%, with revenue of $316.6 million (+9.2% YoY). This validates normalized post-integration earnings power of approximately $3.60/share annualized.

## Growth Analysis

Forward consensus estimates project $3.61 EPS for FY2026 and $3.79 for FY2027, representing 10.4% and 5.0% growth respectively. Estimate revisions are modestly positive: current quarter raised 2.5% over 30 days, current year raised 2.7%. Constructive but modest, and well below the threshold that would signal a fundamental re-rating.

Growth drivers include: (1) continued NIM benefits from deposit repricing as $4.46 billion in time deposits mature in 2026, (2) the Atlanta market platform from Piedmont providing organic loan growth opportunities, (3) potential further acquisitions given excess capital, and (4) operating leverage from integration cost savings.

Growth constraints: (1) acquired loan accretion of $33.7 million will fade over 2-3 years, mechanically compressing NIM by an estimated 10-15bp, (2) mortgage banking continues to shrink intentionally, (3) organic loan growth faces headwinds from elevated rates and commercial real estate softness, (4) a potential rate hike environment could pressure both deposit costs and loan demand.

The organic growth profile without acquisitions is approximately 4-6% EPS growth — adequate for a dividend-paying bank stock but not sufficient to drive meaningful multiple expansion.

## Valuation Assessment

At $43.65, UBSI trades at 12.1x forward EPS ($3.61 FY2026) and approximately 11.5x FY2027 earnings ($3.79). The dividend yield is 3.5% with a 46% payout ratio. Price-to-tangible book is approximately 1.8x.

Against peers: the regional bank peer median is 13.7x earnings with a 2.8% yield. UBSI's discount to the median P/E partially reflects its higher CRE concentration and lower growth rate. FNB Corp trades at 10.8x (cheaper but with lower margins), while Glacier Bancorp trades at 22.3x (premium for lower-risk profile and faster deposit growth). Ameris Bancorp at 13.3x is the closest comp — similar acquisition-driven growth model with less CRE concentration.

Analyst consensus targets range from $45 to $50 (median $47), implying 7-15% upside. Given that the stock already trades within 3% of the low-end target, the risk-adjusted upside is limited unless estimates continue rising or multiples expand.

The valuation is neither compelling nor stretched. At 12.1x with a 3.5% yield, the total return proposition is approximately 8-10% annually (3.5% yield + 5-6% earnings growth), which is adequate for an income-oriented position but does not meet the bar for a high-conviction BUY in a sector where the system has demonstrated zero edge historically.

## Competitive Landscape

UBSI competes across a broad geographic footprint against national banks (Wells Fargo, Bank of America, Truist), larger regionals (Atlantic Union, South State, FNB Corp), and community banks. Its competitive advantages include scale-based cost efficiency, a long relationship-banking history, and a now-diversified geographic base spanning high-growth markets (Atlanta, DC Metro, Charlotte, Charleston SC).

The Piedmont acquisition enters UBSI into the highly competitive Atlanta MSA with only 16 branches — a subscale position that requires either organic growth over many years or further acquisitions to achieve meaningful market share. The company's franchise value is evidenced by its 24% noninterest-bearing deposit ratio and organic deposit growth of $994 million in 2025 (excluding Piedmont).

Competitive position is stable rather than improving: the 5-year TSR of 145.17% slightly trails the peer group at 149.15%, and the company's growth strategy remains dependent on acquisitions rather than organic market share gains.

## Risk Assessment

**CRE Concentration — Primary Idiosyncratic Risk:**
With 57% of loans in CRE and construction, UBSI has disproportionate exposure to a segment facing structural headwinds. The Northern Virginia office charge-offs ($21.8 million from two loans at a single sponsor) are a leading indicator rather than a one-off. The 10-K explicitly acknowledges "greater risk of loss in the office portfolio due to continued hybrid and remote work" as a factor in loss modeling. In a macro environment where 30Y yields are above 5% and rate hikes are being discussed, CRE refinancing stress is intensifying nationally. UBSI's CRE-to-capital ratio is elevated relative to regulatory guidance thresholds.

**Acquired Loan Accretion Fade:**
The $33.7 million contribution in 2025 (approximately 13bp of NIM) is non-recurring and will decline over 2-3 years. Absent new acquisitions, this creates a mechanical NIM headwind of 4-5bp annually.

The $4.46 billion in maturing time deposits become a liability rather than an opportunity if rates rise.

**Options Market Signal:**
IV at 23.5% (equal to 1-year historical vol) suggests the options market prices no elevated near-term risk. Put/call skew is modestly elevated at 5.3%, indicating some hedging demand but nothing extreme. The term structure is in normal contango (23.5% near-term vs. 30.6% at 6 months), reflecting typical uncertainty around earnings and macro events. Low OI P/C ratio of 0.11 suggests limited institutional put protection — the options market is not confirming material near-term downside risk.

Short interest at 4.0% of float with 5.9 days to cover is modestly elevated for a regional bank. This level typically reflects CRE-related bears rather than fundamental existential risk.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($48-50):**
NIM expansion continues as $4.46 billion in time deposits reprice lower in 2026. Credit quality stabilizes with no further significant CRE losses. Atlanta franchise generates above-average loan growth. Another accretive acquisition in 2026 generates positive estimate revisions. At $3.80+ EPS and 13x P/E (re-rating toward peer median), the stock reaches $49-50. Total return with dividends approaches 15-18%.

**Bear Case ($36-38):**
CRE office stress spreads beyond the two Northern Virginia loans. Rising rates pressure refinancing across the $8.34 billion non-owner occupied CRE book. Provision expense doubles again. Earnings compress to $3.00-3.20 as credit costs absorb NIM gains. Multiple compresses to 11x on elevated loss expectations. Stock declines 13-18% from current levels.

**Base Case ($45-47):**
Modest NIM compression from accretion fade offset by organic growth. Credit costs remain elevated but manageable (provision of $60-70 million annually). EPS of $3.55-3.65 in 2026 at 12.5-13x P/E = $44-47. Total return of 5-10% including dividends.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. Stock declines to $38-39 (below 11x forward earnings) on broad regional bank selloff unrelated to UBSI-specific credit deterioration — creating a clear valuation opportunity with dividend yield above 4%
2. CRE office nonperforming loans stabilize or decline for two consecutive quarters while charge-offs normalize below 0.10%
3. Announcement of another accretive acquisition that demonstrably diversifies the loan portfolio away from CRE concentration

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. Nonperforming CRE loans exceed $200 million (currently $110.3 million total NPAs) or charge-offs on CRE exceed 0.75% annualized
2. Quarterly provision exceeds $30 million for two consecutive quarters (indicating systemic portfolio deterioration rather than idiosyncratic loss)
3. NIM declines below 3.50% absent specific one-time factors

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July) — will provide visibility on (1) time deposit repricing trajectory, (2) CRE credit trends, and (3) Piedmont integration organic growth contribution.

## Conclusion

United Bankshares is a well-run, well-capitalized regional bank with a 52-year dividend growth streak, reasonable 12.1x valuation, and strong recent earnings momentum. The specific factor preventing a BUY recommendation is the CRE concentration: at 57% of loans, with office-specific charge-offs already materializing, and a macro environment where potential rate hikes would intensify CRE refinancing stress across the portfolio. A 4% cumulative loss rate on the $8.34 billion non-owner occupied CRE portfolio alone would consume nearly all of the approximately $3.3 billion in tangible equity.

The probability-weighted expected outcome is a modest positive total return of 5-10% (yield + moderate appreciation), which is adequate for an income-oriented hold but insufficient to overcome the asymmetric downside risk from CRE deterioration in the current rate environment. The stock is a reasonable income holding for investors who believe CRE credit will remain contained, but the risk/reward does not justify new capital allocation at $43.65.
