## Executive Summary

WM's core business is working. FY2025 Collection and Disposal average yield was +3.8% on volume growth of only +0.9%, with municipal solid waste landfill yield at +6.5% — direct evidence of pricing power in a permit-constrained disposal oligopoly. In H1 2026 that translated into operating margin of 18.3% versus 17.4%, and free cash flow of $2,024M versus $1,293M (+56.5%) as sustainability growth capital expenditure fell to $136M from $288M. Management cut FY2026 revenue guidance to $26.275–26.475B while raising adjusted operating EBITDA margin guidance to 31.0–31.2%, an explicit decision to trade revenue for margin and cash.

The problem is what the market pays for that. At $227.68 the stock trades at 33.98x trailing GAAP EPS of $6.70, 28.0x the FY2026 consensus of $8.13, and 24.9x the FY2027 consensus of $9.14 — against consensus estimates that have fallen in every window measured (current quarter -0.6%, next quarter -3.5%, current year -0.9%, next year -1.2%) and a track record of three misses in the last four quarters. Republic Services, the closest comparable, grew revenue 4.6% in the same quarter and raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $7.23–7.28; WM grew 4.0%, missed modestly on revenue, and lowered the top-line guide. RSG trades at 29.8x trailing versus WM's 33.98x. The relative multiple runs the wrong way against the relative operating trend.

Two things are unresolved and both matter more than the guidance headline. First, Healthcare Solutions — the $7.2B Stericycle acquisition — carries $8,758M of segment assets against revenue that declined 1.0% in H1 2026, and the FY2025 10-K names Healthcare reporting units as carrying "a greater risk of impairment." The segment crossed to +$2M of operating income in Q2, so the cost side is improving, but the revenue base is eroding and consolidated bad-debt provision nearly doubled to $93M, attributed primarily to Healthcare "data and system challenges." Second, roughly $150–190M of pre-tax-equivalent Section 48 investment tax credit benefit runs off through 2027, creating a mechanical 2028 EPS headwind that few models carry. Base case $245 (+7.6%, plus 1.66% dividend), bull $275 (+20.8%), bear $190 (-16.6%). That distribution is approximately fair. **HOLD.**

## Company Overview

WM is North America's largest integrated solid waste company, reporting five segments. Collection and Disposal (East Tier and West Tier combined) produced FY2025 net revenue of $20,704M — 82.1% of the $25,204M total — and $5,777M of segment operating income against total company operating income of $4,308M, the difference absorbed by a $1,436M Corporate and Other loss. Recycling Processing and Sales contributed $1,492M of revenue, Renewable Energy $478M, and Healthcare Solutions $2,508M.

The moat is the landfill network and its permitting position. WM operates 257 landfills (253 solid waste, 4 hazardous) with a weighted-average remaining life of approximately 38 years on permitted airspace and 39 years including expansion airspace; 94 sites have 41+ years remaining. Expansion permits were granted at 13 landfills in 2025, converting 66 million tons from expansion to permitted capacity. New landfill permits are effectively unobtainable in most U.S. jurisdictions, so this asset base cannot be replicated.

The second structural advantage is measurable in the revenue tables: FY2025 gross operating revenues were $30,895M against net revenues of $25,204M, meaning $5,691M — 18.4% of gross — was intercompany. WM collects waste in its own trucks and disposes of it in its own landfills, capturing the disposal margin rather than paying it away.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue composition matters more than the headline

FY2025 revenue rose $3,141M, or 14.2%. The 10-K bridge decomposes this as +$820M internal growth (3.7%), +$2,365M acquisitions (10.7%), -$31M divestitures, -$13M FX. Roughly 75% of reported growth was the timing effect of owning Stericycle for twelve months in 2025 versus two months in 2024. Underlying organic growth was 3.7%, of which 2.8 points was yield and 0.9 points volume.

Within yield, pricing is strong: commercial +5.1%, residential +5.4%, transfer +4.9%, industrial +2.8%, landfill +2.6%. Offsetting, Recycling yield was -$166M as single-stream commodity prices fell roughly 20% year over year on domestic paper mill closures and reduced demand for recycled content.

H1 2026 revenue was $12,911M (+3.7%); Q2 was $6,684M (+4.0%), approximately 0.4% below consensus.

### Margins inflected upward in 2026 after diluting in 2025

FY2025 operating income was $4,308M, 17.1% of revenue, versus 18.4% in FY2024. The 130bp decline decomposes: operating expenses **improved** 110bp (60.7% → 59.6% of revenue), SG&A worsened 50bp on Stericycle consulting and technology costs, D&A worsened 110bp as intangible amortization jumped from $180M to $421M, and impairments/unusual items were $248M versus $82M. The core cost line got better; the acquisition accounting got worse.

H1 2026 reversed the direction: operating income $2,366M at 18.3% versus 17.4%, with Q2 at 18.7% versus 17.9%. Management raised the margin guide while cutting the revenue guide, which is the clearest statement available about how the business is being run.

### Earnings: the acquisition has not yet paid for itself

FY2025 net income attributable to WM was $2,708M, diluted EPS $6.70, versus $2,746M and $6.81 — net income down 1.4% and EPS down 1.6% despite 14.2% revenue growth. The mechanism is arithmetic: incremental interest expense of +$314M (total interest $912M versus $598M) plus incremental intangible amortization of +$241M equals $555M of pre-tax cost from the deal's financing and accounting, against $245M of incremental consolidated operating income.

H1 2026 GAAP diluted EPS was $3.74; Q2 was $1.95. Q2 GAAP EPS sat modestly below a roughly $1.99 GAAP consensus figure, while the widely reported beat was on an adjusted basis. The $8.13 FY2026 consensus in the estimate tables is an adjusted number and should not be compared to GAAP actuals.

### Cash flow is the real improvement

FY2025 operating cash flow was $6,043M (+12.1%), capex $3,227M (flat), free cash flow on WM's definition $2,937M versus $2,317M (+26.8%). The composition shifted: capex to support the business rose to $2,594M from $2,281M while sustainability growth capex fell to $633M from $950M.

H1 2026: OCF $3,227M (+17.2%), capex $1,280M (down $283M), FCF $2,024M (+56.5%). Sustainability growth capex fell to $136M from $288M. The 10-K states the mechanism directly — "planned reductions in capital investment in our sustainability growth projects as we move from peak construction of this portfolio into a period where we will harvest strong returns."

The company's FY2026 FCF guidance was not obtainable, so I will not annualize the half. Holding FY2026 total capex near $2.7–2.9B against OCF of roughly $6.4–6.6B implies FCF of approximately $3.6–3.9B, a 23–33% increase over FY2025. That is my calculation, not guidance, and it carries two offsets: landfill development spending is guided near $874M for 2026 versus $832M of landfill additions in 2025, so support capex is not falling; and 2025 cash taxes benefited from bonus depreciation and Section 48 credits that are running off. One caution on quality: with capex at $2.8B against FY2025 D&A of $2,863M, capital spending would fall below depreciation. That is defensible when the reduction is growth capex rather than maintenance, but it means the FCF step-up is a level change rather than a compounding rate.

### Balance sheet: adequate, and getting no better

Total debt was $22,907M at 12/31/2025, down from $23,900M, but **$23,362M at 6/30/2026** — up $455M in the half, because buybacks resumed. Cash is $201M. Net debt/Operating EBITDA (using the proxy's $7,438M) is 3.05x. Interest coverage is 4.72x on operating income and 8.16x on EBITDA. The $3.5B revolver has $2.2B unused and available.

The near-term funding picture requires attention: $3.7B of debt matures or reprices within twelve months — $1.8B of tax-exempt bonds with expiring term interest periods, $1.1B of commercial paper, $223M of 7.1% notes due August 2026, $364M of 2.6% Canadian notes due September 2026 — against $2.2B of revolver capacity. WM classified $3.0B of that as long-term on intent and ability to refinance. Given $20.2B of commercial paper issued and $20.4B repaid in 2025 and investment-grade standing, this is manageable. It is nonetheless a live sensitivity to a credit-market disruption, a scenario the 10-K itself acknowledges.

Long-dated obligations: landfill asset retirement obligation $3,305M, environmental remediation $231M, undiscounted final capping/closure/post-closure $5,353M with $4,200M falling after 2030.

A note on screening artifacts: a mechanical Altman Z-Score of 1.32 places WM in the distress zone, but the calculation appears to use book equity in the X₄ term rather than market equity. Substituting the $91.1B market capitalization for ~$10.0B of book equity against ~$35.8B of liabilities adds roughly 1.36 to the score, lifting it to about 2.68 — grey zone, not distress. Three of the model's five inputs structurally penalize this business model: working capital is negative by design (current ratio 0.89) because service is billed in advance, asset turnover of 0.55 is low precisely because the landfill network is the moat, and leverage is high against contractual cash flows. Solvency is not a live concern. The grey-zone reading is still informative about slack: 3.05x leverage with $201M of cash and rising debt leaves little.

### Capital allocation

No repurchases in 2025 (versus $262M in 2024) as capital went to deleveraging. In December 2025 the board authorized up to $3.0B of repurchases and signaled a quarterly dividend increase from $0.825 to $0.945 for 2026 — $3.78 annualized, +14.5%. At ~403M diluted shares the dividend costs approximately $1,523M, covered 1.93x by FY2025 FCF. More than $1B was returned in Q2 2026 alone. Tuck-in M&A slowed to $404M in 2025 from $7,503M in 2024.

## Growth Analysis

The realistic growth profile is 3–4% revenue with mid-to-high-single-digit EBITDA and EPS growth, driven by price above inflation, margin expansion, and share count reduction.

Three items complicate the 2026 read. First, a 2025 California wildfire cleanup tailwind inflated the West Tier base: the 10-K attributes West Tier volume gains, $33M of higher mandated fees, and part of the segment's $196M (+7.3%) operating income increase to wildfire activity. Management quantified the comp on the Q2 call — Operating EBITDA +5.5% reported, +9.1% excluding prior-year wildfire. That reframes the apparent deceleration as substantially a base effect, though WM has never disclosed a dollar figure and the 360bp adjustment is management's own non-GAAP framing.

Second, the revenue-for-margin trade is deliberate and visible in three places: residential volume declines attributed in the 10-K to "our strategic exit from lower-margin contracts" while residential yield rose 5.4%; the simultaneous revenue cut and margin raise; and H1 margin expansion with capex down. The alternative reading — that "strategic exit" is the standard framing for municipal contracts lost on price — cannot be ruled out from the filings.

Third, Renewable Energy carries three simultaneous policy exposures. RIN values are pressured by retroactively lowered 2024 RFS blending volumes, low 2026–2027 targets, and expanded small refinery exemptions. The Section 48 ITC cumulative benefit of $400–425M is roughly $321M recognized ($184M in 2025, $137M in 2024), leaving ~$79–104M realized through 2027; the H1 2026 benefit was already down to $53M from $89M and the effective tax rate rose to 21.2% from 20.5%. The alternative fuel tax credit, worth ~$60M annually pre-tax, expired 12/31/2024. And RNG pipeline interconnection delays contributed to the revenue guidance cut — timing outside WM's control.

Recycling is the underappreciated positive. Segment operating income swung from +$86M to -$80M, but $160M of that was a single impairment for suspending a plastic film and wrap recycling operation. Excluding the charge, segment income was roughly +$80M versus $86M — essentially flat while commodity prices fell 20%. The automation program is absorbing commodity beta, which supports the credibility of the raised margin guide.

Against all of that, consensus is moving down, not up. Next-quarter estimates fell 3.5% in 30 days and FY2027 fell 1.2%. There is no estimate-revision inflection here to lean on.

## Competitive Landscape

The 10-K is candid about the competitive setting: "We encounter intense competition from governmental, quasi-governmental and private service providers based on pricing, and to a much lesser extent, the nature of service offerings, particularly in the residential line of business." The industry is described as "comparatively mature and stable."

WM's structural position within it is strong and not deteriorating: 257 landfills with 38–39 year weighted-average life, 94 sites with 41+ years, 13 expansion permits granted in 2025, and 18.4% internalization. The observable output of that position is 3.8% blended Collection and Disposal yield on 0.9% volume growth, and 6.5% MSW landfill yield.

Where the trajectory is less favorable is the comparison to RSG. Same industry, same disposal oligopoly, same quarter: RSG +4.6% and guidance raised, WM +4.0% and revenue guidance lowered. That weakens a purely sector-demand explanation for WM's shortfall. Four candidate WM-specific drivers exist, three of which are choices or timing — the wildfire comp, deliberate residential contract shedding, and RNG interconnection delays — and one that is genuinely concerning, the Healthcare Solutions revenue decline. The businesses are not fully comparable: RSG has no segment at the scale of WM's Healthcare Solutions and a smaller RNG platform, so part of WM's drag has no RSG analogue by construction.

Where WM has taken more risk than the sector is in two places. The Stericycle acquisition moved it into small-quantity regulated medical waste and secure information destruction — fragmented markets with low switching costs and no landfill moat. And the sustainability capex program (~$1.58B across 2024–2025) added commodity and policy exposure the core business does not carry. The recycling automation portion appears to be working; the plastics adjacency did not.

## Risk Assessment

**Healthcare Solutions goodwill and revenue erosion (highest-impact idiosyncratic risk).** The FY2025 10-K states that Healthcare reporting units "have a greater risk of impairment should their operations or market conditions experience a significant downturn," naming revenue growth, cost, and discount-rate assumptions specifically. Segment assets were $8,758M at 6/30/2026, against a $7.2B purchase enterprise value. Revenue declined 1.2% in Q2 and 1.0% in H1, while operating income improved to +$2M in Q2 from -$23M. The decisive question is whether the revenue decline is a billing/systems artifact — the 10-K attributes the near-doubling of consolidated bad-debt provision to $93M primarily to Healthcare "driven by data and system challenges," and improving Healthcare DSO mildly supports the fixable reading — or share loss in a low-switching-cost market. Some of the decline is disclosed portfolio pruning (the Spain and Portugal subsidiaries were divested in 2025), but the split is not isolable from the filings. An impairment on this segment would be non-cash and would not affect the credit profile, but it would validate the bear case on capital allocation.

**Tax-credit runoff.** Approximately $79–104M of Section 48 ITC benefit remains, realized through 2027. The expired alternative fuel credit removed roughly $60M of annual pre-tax benefit. Together these imply a 2028 headwind on the order of $150–190M pre-tax-equivalent absent replacement credits. Production tax credits may partially offset; the 10-K says the benefit is not yet quantified "due, in part, to the lack of regulatory guidance."

**Refinancing sensitivity.** Interest expense rose $314M to $912M in 2025 and contractual interest is $942M in 2026. With $3.7B repricing or maturing within twelve months against $2.2B of revolver availability, WM depends on continued commercial paper and tax-exempt market access. WM also carries a 1.66% dividend yield, so its multiple is sensitive to moves in long-term interest rates independent of operations.

**Landfill accounting judgment.** Depletion per ton rose 9.4% to $6.96 on 128.4M depletable tons. 2025 included +$217M of revisions to landfill liabilities, +$38M to remediation, and a $45M closure impairment. Twenty-four landfills have 0–5 years of remaining life. The 10-K notes that if expansion capacity can no longer be included in recoverability tests, WM "may be required to recognize an asset impairment or incur significantly higher depletion expense."

**Litigation and regulatory.** A one-year DOJ deferred prosecution agreement (May 2026) resolved criminal and civil investigations relating to Stericycle's ESOL Retail Controlled Substances business, removing an inherited overhang; the monetary amount was not captured. San Jacinto River Waste Pits carries a ~$100M recorded liability with an April 2026 EPA Unilateral Administrative Order. A Delaware DNREC order from April 2026 is under appeal.

**Balance-sheet slack.** WM resumed buybacks while total debt rose $455M in H1. That is a defensible choice at 3.05x against contractual cash flows, but it removes flexibility, and the 10-K itself identifies capex and buyback deferral as the levers it would pull in a market disruption.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term ATM implied volatility of 21.0% sits essentially on top of 20.4% trailing realized volatility (spread 0.6%), at the 8th percentile of the current chain — the options market prices no near-term event. Term structure is in contango, rising to 25.2% at the December tenor with put/call skew widening from -0.9% at one month to -3.9% at six months and an implied move of ±10.9%. Since the prior snapshot on May 27, ATM IV fell from 23.4% to 21.0% while the price rose 5.6%, and put/call open interest rose from 0.47 to 0.62. The put/call volume ratio of 3.40 looks alarming in isolation but sits against an open-interest ratio of 0.62, which is call-tilted; single-day volume in a low-volume options chain (178 contracts across five expirations) is weak evidence.

The composite reading is near-term complacency with modestly increasing longer-dated downside interest, consistent with the view that the identifiable risks — a Healthcare impairment decision at year-end testing, the FY2026 guidance close, and the 2028 tax-credit step-down — sit beyond the next month. The options data neither contradicts nor strengthens a HOLD. Short interest of 1.6% of float and 2.8 days to cover confirms there is no crowded bearish position to squeeze.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case — $275 (+20.8%)

The revenue-for-margin trade compounds. FY2026 adjusted operating EBITDA margin lands at or above 31.2%; the wildfire comp anniversaries in H2 2026 and reported EBITDA growth converges toward the 9.1% underlying rate. Sustainability capex stays near a $300M annual run-rate while the completed RNG plants generate gas sales, so free cash flow settles at $4.0B+ and the $3.0B repurchase authorization retires 3%+ of shares annually at these prices. Healthcare revenue stabilizes as billing systems are remediated, converting the segment's fixed-cost base into high-margin recovery, with the Q2 crossover to +$2M of operating income as the first evidence. Recycling automation continues offsetting commodity beta as it did through a 20% price decline. FY2027 adjusted EPS reaches $9.50, and 29x on a business with 38-year landfill life and contractual, recession-resistant revenue gives $275. Defensive subscription revenue re-rates as a relative haven.

### Bear Case — $190 (-16.6%)

The revenue guidance cut is the leading edge rather than a base effect. RSG raising guidance in the same quarter indicates the softness is WM-specific: residential "strategic exits" are competitive losses, Healthcare continues shedding revenue, and the segment is written down against $8,758M of carrying assets, validating a $7.2B deal that has already cost EPS two years running ($555M of incremental interest and amortization against $245M of incremental operating income). The Section 48 runoff removes $150–190M of pre-tax-equivalent benefit into 2028, and the effective tax rate steps up. Free cash flow improvement proves to be a level change from deferred growth capex rather than a compounding trend, with landfill development spending rising to ~$874M. FY2027 adjusted EPS comes in near $8.00 and the multiple compresses to 24x — in line with, rather than above, RSG — giving $192. Continued pressure on a 1.66%-yielding, 3.05x-levered name accelerates the derating.

### Base Case — $245 (+7.6%, ~9.3% including dividend)

Organic revenue grows 3.5–4%, adjusted operating EBITDA margin lands within guidance, and free cash flow reaches $3.6–3.9B. FY2027 adjusted EPS of $9.00–9.05 at 27x gives $244; $3.75B of FCF at a 3.8% required yield gives $245. Healthcare stabilizes near flat revenue without an impairment, and buybacks contribute 2–3% of share-count reduction. The multiple holds near RSG rather than expanding.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Recommendation: HOLD.** Horizon: 12 months, with the decisive checkpoints at the Q3 2026 print (late October 2026), the FY2026 close and annual goodwill test (Q4 reporting, February 2027), and the FY2027 guidance framework issued with those results.

**Why HOLD rather than ACCUMULATE.** Probability-weighted expected return of roughly +6% including the dividend does not clear the bar for staged accumulation in a name where estimates are falling in every measured window and three of the last four quarters missed. The stock sits at 28.0x FY2026 consensus versus RSG at a lower trailing multiple with better relative momentum, so there is no margin of safety at $227.68. The margin and free-cash-flow evidence is real, which keeps this out of REDUCE territory: FCF +56.5% in H1, operating margin +90bp, and a 14.5% dividend increase are not the signature of a deteriorating franchise.

**Why HOLD rather than REDUCE.** The core franchise is intact and pricing at 3.8% yield on flat volumes. The FCF harvest is documented in the filings with a stated mechanism, dividend coverage is 1.93x, and the balance sheet is investment grade with 4.72x interest coverage. Fair value sits above the current price, not below.

This is the fourth consecutive HOLD, and the overall score has declined from 7.2 (February) to 5.66. The decline this cycle is located in valuation and risk, not in operations: financial health improved on the free-cash-flow inflection, while valuation compressed because the price rose 5.6% since the May review at the same time that FY2026 revenue guidance and FY2026/FY2027 consensus EPS were cut. A higher price on lowered estimates is a legitimate reduction in the valuation score. Competitive position and management quality are unchanged.

**Upgrade triggers (to ACCUMULATE or BUY):**
1. Healthcare Solutions net revenue returns to year-over-year growth for two consecutive quarters with segment operating income sustained above breakeven — this resolves the goodwill question and converts an $8.76B liability-shaped asset into an earnings contributor.
2. FY2026 free cash flow of $3.7B+ confirmed against company guidance, with FY2027 capex guided at or below $2.9B — validating the harvest as durable rather than deferral.
3. Price below $200 (approximately 22x FY2027 consensus and a ~4.7% normalized FCF yield), which would restore adequate margin of safety without any change in fundamentals.

**Downgrade triggers (to REDUCE or AVOID):**
1. A Healthcare Solutions goodwill impairment of $1B+ at the annual test, or a third consecutive quarter of Healthcare revenue decline accelerating beyond -2%.
2. FY2027 consensus EPS falling below $8.75 (currently $9.14, down 1.2% in 30 days) — continued negative revisions at this pace would remove the earnings support for the base case.
3. Collection and Disposal average yield falling below 3.0%, which would indicate the pricing engine that underwrites the entire thesis is losing traction.
4. FY2027 total debt/Operating EBITDA above 3.3x with buybacks continuing, indicating capital returns funded by leverage rather than cash flow.

**Reassessment timeline:** Q3 2026 results in late October, with an interim check on any Item 2.06 (impairment) 8-K filing.

## Conclusion

WM owns an irreplaceable asset base — 257 landfills, 38-year weighted-average life, 18.4% internalization — and is converting that position into price: 3.8% Collection and Disposal yield on 0.9% volume, operating margin up 90bp in H1 2026, and free cash flow up 56.5% as peak sustainability construction rolls off. Management is deliberately shedding low-margin revenue to compound margin and cash, and raised the margin guide in the same period it cut the revenue guide. That is the right trade for a business of this type.

The stock does not offer adequate compensation for the specific risks attached to it. At 28.0x FY2026 and 24.9x FY2027 consensus — with those estimates falling in every window and three misses in the last four quarters — WM trades at a premium to Republic Services, which grew faster and raised guidance in the same quarter. The Stericycle acquisition has reduced EPS in each of its first two years ($555M of incremental interest and amortization against $245M of incremental operating income), its revenue base is contracting 1.0%, and the 10-K names its reporting units as carrying elevated impairment risk against $8,758M of segment assets. Roughly $150–190M of pre-tax-equivalent tax-credit benefit lapses into 2028. Debt rose $455M in H1 as buybacks resumed against $201M of cash and $3.7B repricing within twelve months.

Base case $245 (+7.6%), bull $275 (+20.8%), bear $190 (-16.6%). With the dividend, the probability-weighted return is roughly 6% — approximately fair, with no urgency in either direction. Existing owners should hold for the cash-flow harvest and a 14.5% dividend increase. New capital should wait for either evidence that Healthcare Solutions has stabilized or a price near $200.
