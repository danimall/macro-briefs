## Executive Summary

Scorpio Tankers reported Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $3.02, beating consensus by approximately 10.8%, with Q2 2026 booked pool/spot rates nearly doubling Q1 actuals — LR2 pool/spot rates at $96,000/day versus $50,830/day realized in Q1. At $82.23/share, the stock trades at a low multiple relative to current earnings power, which has been revised sharply upward by analysts over the past 90 days. The company has approximately $1.4B in unrestricted cash, a $500M buyback authorization representing approximately 12% of market cap, and is executing a disciplined fleet renewal strategy while industry supply growth is expected to remain around 3% over the next three years per management.

The investment thesis rests on the conjunction of three factors: (1) structural supply tightness in the product tanker market, (2) elevated ton-mile demand from geopolitical trade route disruptions — including Red Sea/Houthi rerouting and broader Middle East/Iran/Hormuz-related disruptions reducing regional exports and requiring longer-haul sourcing, and (3) management's demonstrated discipline in returning capital rather than over-ordering at cycle peaks. The key risk is rate normalization — if geopolitical disruptions resolve and rates revert to $30-40K/day levels, earnings power falls by 50-60%.

## Company Overview

Scorpio Tankers operates one of the largest publicly-listed product tanker fleets, with 87 vessels as of early May 2026, transporting refined petroleum products (gasoline, diesel, jet fuel, naphtha) globally. The fleet consists primarily of LR2 (105-115K DWT), MR (45-55K DWT), and Handymax (35-40K DWT) vessels built predominantly between 2014–2020. The company is incorporated in the Marshall Islands, managed by Scorpio Group affiliates, and trades on the NYSE. While the current operating fleet is product-tanker-focused, the company has disclosed two VLCC newbuildings for H2 2028 delivery, and its 20-F describes its business as the seaborne transportation of crude oil and refined petroleum products.

The fleet operates a hybrid commercial model: spot market exposure through pool participation plus selective time charters. This provides upside capture during rate spikes while the time charter component offers visibility. The company recently locked in two LR2 vessels on 5-8 year charters at $30,500-$33,000/day, establishing a floor for a portion of the fleet.

## Growth Analysis

Revenue growth is entirely rate-driven in this business. The relevant growth metric is the ton-mile demand trajectory and supply/demand balance.

**Demand Drivers (Positive):**
- Global refining capacity shifted East (Middle East, India, China) while consumption remains concentrated in the West and Europe, creating longer voyage distances
- Red Sea/Houthi disruptions forcing Cape of Good Hope re-routing, with EIA noting disruptions kept Cape flows above historical averages
- Broader Middle East conflict, including Iran/Strait of Hormuz-related disruptions, reducing regional exports and requiring barrels to be sourced from more distant markets (per company discussion and EIA analysis)
- Russian sanctions restructuring product trade flows, generally adding distance
- Environmental regulations (EEXI, CII) effectively reducing available fleet capacity by slowing older vessels

**Supply Drivers (Positive for Rates):**
- Management expects only ~3% industry fleet growth over the next three years
- Global orderbook remains low relative to fleet age profile
- Shipyard capacity constrained (priority going to container and LNG)
- Environmental compliance requirements accelerating older vessel exits

**Estimate Revisions:**
Analyst estimates have been revised sharply upward in recent months. The Zacks consensus for Q1 had been revised 61.5% higher over the prior 30 days leading into earnings. Broader current-quarter and current-year estimates have also moved materially higher, reflecting the improvement in the rate environment. This magnitude of upward revision, combined with consistent beats, indicates consensus is still catching up to the rate environment.

## Valuation Assessment

At $82.23, STNG trades at a low multiple relative to 2026 earnings power. Against a shipping peer group, this reflects the cyclical peak-earnings concern — investors historically pay low multiples for shipping at cyclical peaks.

**Why the market may be too pessimistic:**
1. Industry supply growth of approximately 3% over 3 years is among the lowest in decades for product tankers
2. Environmental regulations create a structural capacity constraint
3. Multiple geopolitical disruptions to trade lanes show no signs of near-term resolution
4. The company is actively returning capital, not building recklessly into the cycle

**Valuation Framework:**
- Current price: $82.23
- Forward P/E on current earnings trajectory: approximately 6-7x
- Analyst targets have moved materially higher following Q1 results and the BofA double upgrade
- BofA upgraded STNG from Underperform to Buy (double upgrade) with a reported target of $100
- Even at a conservative 8x mid-cycle earnings of $8-9/share, fair value approximates $64-72. At current elevated earnings power with any sustainability, 8x earnings supports a materially higher valuation.

## Competitive Landscape

STNG maintains competitive advantages through:

1. **Scale** — One of the largest product tanker fleets (87 vessels), providing commercial pool bargaining power and customer breadth
2. **Fleet Quality** — Eco-friendly, scrubber-fitted vessels with lower fuel costs than older competitors
3. **Commercial Optimization** — Pool participation across multiple vessel classes provides rate diversification
4. **Capital Discipline** — Selling at cycle peaks; maintaining financial flexibility; significant newbuilding program timed for future delivery

Versus peers (Torm, Hafnia, Ardmore, INSW), STNG's combination of fleet size, vessel quality, and capital returns positions it as a premium product tanker equity.

The product tanker market structure itself is favorable: limited orderbook, aging global fleet, environmental regulations constraining effective supply, and structurally longer trade routes. These conditions can persist independently of any single geopolitical event.

## Risk Assessment

**Primary Risks:**

1. **Rate Cyclicality (HIGH):** The most significant risk. Q2 booked pool/spot rates are historically exceptional. Mean-reversion toward $40-50K/day would meaningfully reduce earnings. Historical tanker cycles have inflicted 60-70% drawdowns from peak earnings.

2. **Geopolitical Normalization (MODERATE):** Resolution of Red Sea/Houthi disruptions, broader Middle East conflicts, or Strait of Hormuz tensions could rapidly compress ton-miles. Low-probability near-term but high-impact.

3. **Convertible Dilution (LOW-MODERATE):** The $375M convertible notes at a conversion price of ~$100.39 could add approximately 3.74M shares on full conversion. The concurrent repurchase partially offsets this, and the company may elect cash settlement.

4. **Fleet Size Transition (MODERATE):** Selling 15 vessels reduces revenue capacity near-term. Newbuildings arrive across 2026-2029, but there is an interim period of reduced earning capacity.

5. **Related Party Governance (LOW):** Scorpio Group management affiliation (SCM/SSM are related parties controlled by the Lolli-Ghetti family) creates potential conflicts, though capital allocation actions have been consistently shareholder-friendly.

### Options Market Signal

## Conclusion

STNG at $82.23 offers compelling value as a direct beneficiary of the current global energy supply chain disruption. The combination of a low forward earnings multiple, a 12%-of-market-cap buyback, 4 consecutive earnings beats, and substantial upward estimate revisions positions this as one of the higher-conviction energy trades available. Supply growth is structurally constrained (management expects ~3% industry growth over 3 years), environmental regulations are removing effective capacity, and multiple geopolitical disruptions show no near-term resolution path. The risk is binary and geopolitically driven — a resolution of trade route disruptions would compress rates rapidly — but the expected value favors ownership. Maintain BUY with $98 base case target.

> **[POST-REVIEW FLAG]** The Q2 EPS extrapolation of "$5–6+" relies on pool/spot bookings that cover only a portion of fleet days and does not account for lower time-charter rates, unbooked days, or operating costs; the actual Q2 blended result will be materially lower than what headline pool/spot rates alone would imply. Additionally, several analyst-target figures and estimate-revision percentages cited in the thesis could not be independently verified against primary sources, and STNG's broader newbuilding commitments (4 MR, 4 LR2, 2 VLCC) represent more capital deployment than the report's original framing suggested. These factors may moderate the strength of the valuation case supporting the $98 target.
