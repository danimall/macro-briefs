## Executive Summary

American Financial Group is a pure-play specialty commercial P&C insurer operating 36 niche business units through Great American Insurance Group. The company trades at ~12x forward earnings ($11.11 FY2026E) against a peer median of 14.2x, despite a 10-year combined ratio averaging 90.5% vs. the commercial lines industry at 98.1%. This 760-basis-point structural underwriting advantage, combined with a 20-year streak of dividend increases and total capital returns covering ~95% of net income, makes AFG a high-quality franchise available at a discount.

The discount exists for identifiable reasons: specialty casualty combined ratios deteriorated from 88.8% (2023) to 96.0% (2025) driven by social inflation — adverse prior year reserve development of $175M in 2025, up from $96M two years earlier. Alternative investment returns collapsed to 2.5% annualized in 2025 vs. 6.1% in 2024, dragging ~$100M of pre-tax income out of the earnings stream. Both headwinds are real but neither is structural to the franchise. Rate remediation in casualty lines (6-8% ex-workers' comp) is already in progress, and management expects alt investment normalization beginning H2 2026.

At $130.69, the stock sits 12% below its 52-week high and offers a 2.7% dividend yield on the regular dividend alone — with special dividends adding 3-4% annually. The combination of below-peer valuation, durable underwriting superiority, and two identifiable earnings recovery catalysts supports a BUY recommendation with a base case target of $150.

## Company Overview

AFG is controlled by the Lindner family (~24% ownership) and operates exclusively in specialty commercial P&C insurance. The company exited its annuity/life businesses years ago. Its 36 business units span three sub-segments: Property & Transportation (39% of NWP, $2.77B), Specialty Casualty (46%, $3.25B), and Specialty Financial (15%, $1.09B). Gross written premiums totaled $10.69B in 2025. All principal subsidiaries carry A.M. Best A+ (Superior) ratings.

The decentralized operating model grants local underwriting authority while centralizing investment management and administration. Compensation is profit-based at the business unit level, penalizing volume growth at the expense of profitability. Management explicitly states it will "refrain from writing business that is not expected to produce an underwriting profit even if it is necessary to limit premium growth."

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue and Profitability:** Total revenue was $8.17B in 2025, with net earned premiums of $7.05B (essentially flat YoY). GWP growth decelerated from 9% to 2%. Core EPS declined 4.3% to $10.29 from $10.75, driven primarily by the alt investment shortfall. The GAAP combined ratio improved 20bps to 91.0%.

The segment-level story is critical. Property & Transportation posted a combined ratio of 87.6% with underwriting profit of $342M (up sharply, aided by record crop yields and minimal catastrophe losses). Specialty Financial remained strong at 83.1%. Specialty Casualty deteriorated to 96.0% from 91.2%, with underwriting profit falling 54% from $279M to $129M. The driver: adverse prior year reserve development of $175M in other liability (occurrence) lines, up from $210M in 2024 and $96M in 2023.

**Investment Income:** Fixed-income yields expanded to 5.13% on a $16.1B portfolio (96% investment grade, 4.4-year average life), producing $656M in non-alternative investment income (+5% YoY). Alternative investment returns of 2.5% on ~$2.42B in limited partnerships and CLOs represent the key earnings drag. The gap between 2.5% and a normalized 6% return equates to roughly $85M pre-tax, or ~$1.00/share after-tax.

**Balance Sheet:** Shareholders' equity of $4.82B ($57.73/share; stock trades at 2.3x book). Debt-to-capital of 27.5% (within 30% target), elevated slightly by the September 2025 $350M note issuance. No debt maturities until 2030. Parent cash of $529M plus $450M undrawn revolver. Maximum subsidiary dividends available without regulatory approval: ~$1.08B in 2026.

**Cash Flow and Capital Returns:** Operating cash flow of $1.53B in 2025. Total capital returned to shareholders: ~$804M ($705M in dividends including specials, $99M in buybacks) — a 95% payout ratio vs. $842M net income. ROE of 17.5%. The $1.50/share special dividend declared in February 2026 signals management's confidence in ongoing earnings power.

## Growth Analysis

Premium growth is decelerating: GWP grew just 2% in 2025 vs. 9% in 2024. Renewal rate increases averaged ~5% overall (6% ex-workers' comp), down from 7% in 2024. The P&C rate cycle is moderating but remains positive.

Consensus estimates project $11.11 in current-year EPS and $12.03 next year — implying 8% and 8.3% growth, respectively. Growth comes from two sources: (1) specialty casualty rate remediation flowing through the book as re-priced policies earn, and (2) normalization of alternative investment returns. Management's expectation of improved alt returns "beginning in H2 2026" is plausible given the mean-reverting nature of PE/VC fund distributions, though the timing is inherently uncertain.

Estimate revisions are essentially flat: current-year EPS revised +0.2% over 30 days but -2.5% over 90 days. This reflects the market pricing in continued social inflation headwinds against modest premium growth. The revision trajectory has stabilized after the negative trend but is not confirming acceleration.

Organic growth levers are limited in the near term. AFG's discipline — refusing to write unprofitable business — means premium growth is constrained when rate adequacy is in question, particularly in casualty lines. Long-term, the company's niche expertise and 36 independently managed business units provide diversified avenues for growth as individual markets harden.

## Valuation Assessment

AFG trades at ~12x trailing EPS ($10.08) and ~11.8x forward FY2026E ($11.11). The peer median is 14.2x. This 16% discount has widened over the past six months, coinciding with the specialty casualty deterioration and weaker alt investment returns.

At 2.3x book value, AFG trades at a modest premium to tangible equity — reasonable for a business generating 17.5% ROE. The dividend yield on the regular dividend is 2.7%, with special dividends adding 3-4% annually (total yield of ~5.5-6.5% depending on the year). This compares to the peer median yield of 3.4%.

Applying the peer median 14.2x to current-year consensus of $11.11 implies $158. A more conservative 13.5x multiple (reflecting the casualty overhang) yields $150. At 12x next-year EPS of $12.03, fair value is $144. The analyst consensus target mean is $140.50 with a high of $155.

The valuation discount over-penalizes two transient headwinds against a franchise with sustained underwriting superiority and two decades of consecutive dividend increases.

## Competitive Landscape

AFG's 10-year average combined ratio advantage of 760bps over the commercial lines industry has persisted across rate cycles, catastrophe years, and varying investment climates. It reflects genuine underwriting skill embedded in the decentralized model and niche specialization.

Over 55% of 2025 GWP comes from businesses ranking in the top 10 among competitors. The company's focus on small- to mid-market commercial specialty lines provides natural barriers: these are complex, relationship-driven niches where scale economies in underwriting knowledge (not premium volume) matter most.

Catastrophe exposure is well-managed: the January 2026 reinsurance renewal provides $205M excess of $70M, plus $350M from the Riverfront Re catastrophe bond for losses above $275M. Maximum modeled 500-year cat loss is less than 3% of equity.

Against peers: AFG's 12x P/E sits below Assurant (14.2x), GNW (16.7x), and KMPR (17.7x), while above LNC (7.6x, which is a fundamentally different business with significant life/annuity exposure). Among true specialty P&C peers, companies like Arch Capital and RenaissanceRe command premiums of 10-15x book. AFG's 2.3x book is modest for its ROE profile.

## Risk Assessment

**Social Inflation (HIGH):** The most critical risk. Specialty casualty reserve development has worsened in each of the past three years ($96M → $210M → $175M in adverse development). The 10-K quantifies sensitivity: a 1% change in cost trends on the other liability (occurrence) book impacts net earnings by $94M. If social inflation trends persist or accelerate, this line alone could erase 8-10% of earnings annually. Rate increases of 6-8% in non-workers' comp lines are the remediation mechanism, but there is a multi-quarter lag between re-pricing and reserve impact.

**Alternative Investment Volatility (MEDIUM-HIGH):** The ~$85M pre-tax swing between normalized and current returns creates meaningful earnings uncertainty. These investments (PE/VC funds, CLOs) are illiquid and marked quarterly. In a recession or credit stress scenario, both distributions and marks could deteriorate further.

**Reserve Adequacy (MEDIUM-HIGH):** $15.09B in unpaid loss reserves is 3.1x shareholders' equity. The reserving challenge in long-tail casualty lines is genuine — the industry is collectively grappling with whether current reserves adequately capture social inflation trends. Favorable development in workers' comp ($108M in 2025) partially offsets, but the casualty tail is longer and harder to estimate.

**Crop Insurance Concentration (MEDIUM):** Exceptional 2025 crop results masked deterioration elsewhere. This business is inherently weather-dependent and subject to FCIC reinsurance/SRA terms set by government policy.

**PFAS and Legacy Liabilities (LOW-MEDIUM):** Gross A&E reserves of $460M and specific PFAS risk factor language represent emerging tail risks. Survival ratios are well above industry averages (27.7x vs. 8.2x) but these exposures are inherently unpredictable.

## Investment Thesis

### Bull Case ($155-160)
- Alternative investment returns normalize toward 5-6% in H2 2026, adding $1.00-1.50/share to EPS
- Specialty casualty rate remediation begins flowing through earned premiums by mid-2026, stabilizing combined ratios in the 92-94% range
- Higher-for-longer rates sustain and expand fixed-income yields above 5.13%
- Multiple re-rates toward the peer median on improved earnings visibility
- Special dividends continue at $3-5/share annually, providing ~3-4% additional yield
- 14x $11.50 = $161

### Bear Case ($110-115)
- Social inflation accelerates — specialty casualty combined ratio breaches 100%
- Adverse reserve development in other liability lines reaches $250M+ annually
- Alternative investment returns remain depressed through 2026 as credit stress materializes
- Crop insurance reverts to average or below-average results
- P&C rate cycle turns negative, compressing premium growth
- 10x $11 EPS = $110 (-16% downside)

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Specialty casualty combined ratio exceeds 98% for two consecutive quarters, indicating rate remediation is failing to offset social inflation
2. Total adverse prior year reserve development exceeds $300M annually, suggesting systemic reserve inadequacy
3. Management eliminates or significantly reduces special dividends without a corresponding increase in repurchases, signaling undisclosed earnings deterioration

## Conclusion

AFG is a high-quality specialty P&C insurer trading at a meaningful discount to peers due to two identifiable and quantifiable headwinds: specialty casualty deterioration from social inflation and depressed alternative investment returns. The franchise quality — sustained underwriting outperformance, A.M. Best A+ ratings, consecutive dividend growth, and aligned family ownership — is not in question. The social inflation headwind is real and requires monitoring, but rate remediation is underway and the alt investment earnings gap represents a concrete recovery catalyst with management-guided timing. Total shareholder yield of 5.5-6.5% including specials provides meaningful downside support. BUY with a 12-month base case target of $150.
