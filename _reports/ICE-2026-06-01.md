**Research Date: June 1, 2026**

## Executive Summary

Intercontinental Exchange operates the most diversified franchise in financial-market infrastructure: regulated exchanges and clearing houses (including the NYSE and the benchmark Brent crude and TTF natural-gas complexes), a fixed-income pricing and data business, and a near-monopoly position in U.S. residential mortgage software. FY2025 net revenue (revenue less transaction-based pass-throughs) reached $9.93B, up 7%, with adjusted diluted EPS of $6.95 (+14%) and free cash flow of $3.87B. Recurring revenue has climbed from 34% of the total in 2014 to 51% in 2025, which structurally de-risks the model relative to volume-dependent exchange peers. Q1 CY2026 was, in the CFO's words, the strongest quarter in the company's history, with adjusted EPS of $2.35 beating consensus by 2.9% on energy-trading volatility.

The investment case rests on a clear price/fundamental divergence. The stock has fallen roughly 22% from its August 2025 high of $189.35 to $147.85, while consensus current-year EPS estimates rose 6.5% over the past 90 days to $8.14 and next-year estimates rose 3.4% to $8.82. At the current price, ICE trades at approximately 18.2x current-year and 16.8x next-year consensus EPS, against a peer-group median P/E of 32.4x. The analyst low target of $174 sits 18% above the current price; the mean of $199.53 implies 35% upside. The company has beaten consensus in each of the last four quarters and is buying back stock under a fresh $3.0B authorization while raising the dividend.

The reasons to be cautious are specific rather than thematic. ICE has committed $2.0B to prediction-market operator Polymarket (with an option for up to $1.0B more) and built a crypto-custody business through ICE Digital Trust, pushing the company beyond its regulated-infrastructure core into territory the 10-K itself flags for reputational and governance risk. Insider activity over the past year is entirely option-exercise-and-sell with no open-market buying, and the score trajectory shows management quality and competitive position both declining over the past month. The recommendation is BUY, anchored on valuation and recurring-revenue durability rather than on momentum or insider conviction.

## Company Overview

ICE reports three segments, measured on net revenue (revenue less transaction-based expenses, the metric management uses to track the business):

- **Exchanges (55%, ~$5.41B net revenue, 74% operating margin):** 13 regulated exchanges and 6 clearing houses spanning energy (Brent crude plus ~800 oil products, global natural gas/TTF, environmental and carbon markets), agricultural softs (coffee, cocoa, cotton, sugar), financial futures (interest rates, MSCI/FTSE equity indices), and the NYSE (cash equities and options, roughly 70% of S&P 500 listings and ~75% of ETF AUM). This is the highest-margin part of the business and the source of most of the cyclical upside.
- **Fixed Income & Data Services (24%, ~$2.42B revenue, 39% operating margin):** Evaluated pricing on 3M+ securities, ICE Data Indices, CDS clearing via ICE Clear Credit, and ICE Global Network connectivity. Annual subscription value reached $1.99B, up 8.3% year over year, the recurring engine of the model.
- **Mortgage Technology (21%, ~$2.10B revenue):** End-to-end U.S. residential mortgage workflow (Encompass origination, MSP servicing, MERS, Simplifile closing), built through the $11.4B Ellie Mae acquisition (2020) and the $11.8B Black Knight acquisition (2023).

The competitive moats are deep liquidity in benchmark contracts where the contract itself becomes the price reference (Brent, TTF, coffee, cocoa, sugar), central-counterparty network effects, proprietary recurring data, and a dominant U.S. mortgage-software footprint. The diversity across asset classes means no single market dominates the transaction book, which is the structural argument for why ICE deserves a higher multiple than a pure-play volume exchange.

The growth trajectory is broad-based. GAAP net income rose 20% and adjusted EPS rose 14%. Energy was the standout driver: energy futures revenue up 16%, total futures volume up 13%, open interest up 17%. Cash-equities ADV rose 40% on retail participation. Consolidated GAAP operating margin was 50% (adjusted 60%), net margin ~27%, and FCF margin ~34%, best-in-class for the sector.

Mortgage Technology inflected to a GAAP operating profit of +$14M in 2025, from –$170M in 2024 and –$276M in 2023, as Black Knight synergies landed and headcount fell. On an adjusted basis the segment earned $859M at a 41% margin. This matters because the segment is operating at the trough of the mortgage cycle: transaction-based mortgage revenue is geared to origination volume, which remains suppressed by elevated rates. The operating leverage is real and not visible in trailing numbers.

A note on the balance sheet: the Altman Z-Score of 0.68 ("distress") and the current ratio of 1.02 are artifacts of the clearing-house model, not signs of stress. ICE custodies $81.2B of clearing-member margin and guaranty funds that appear as matched current assets and liabilities. Strip those out and ICE is a high-margin, cash-generative business. The relevant leverage figure is $19.6B of corporate debt (roughly 3.9x adjusted EBITDA, D/E ~0.68x net of clearing balances), with a 14-year weighted-average maturity at a 3.7% weighted cost. The company made $1.3B of net debt repayment in 2025. Any screen relying on the raw Z-Score would badly misread this business.

Capital allocation has been disciplined. ICE resumed buybacks in 2025 ($1.3B / 7.7M shares) with a new $3.0B authorization effective 2026, and raised the dividend to $0.52/quarter (FY2025 paid $1.92, ~$1.1B total). 2026 capex is guided to $740–790M.

## Growth Analysis

The forward growth case has three legs. First, the recurring base: ASV in Fixed Income & Data grew 8.3% and recurring revenue is now 51% of the total, providing a durable floor that compounds regardless of trading volatility. Second, the Mortgage Technology call option: at ~$2.1B revenue and 41% adjusted margins, a rate-cut-driven origination recovery would deliver outsized incremental profit on a largely fixed cost base. Third, the U.S. Treasury clearing initiative: ICE Clear Credit received SEC approval in February 2026 to add Treasury clearing (cash live, repo expected Q4 2026), positioning ICE to compete against incumbent DTCC/FICC ahead of the SEC's Treasury clearing mandate. The 10-K explicitly caveats adoption as uncertain, so this is optionality rather than a base-case driver.

Estimate revisions support the near-term picture. The current-quarter estimate dipped 3.2% over 30 days (to $1.92), which tempers the otherwise-positive momentum but does not break it. The earnings track record is consistent — four straight beats (2.9%, 1.2%, 5.4%, 1.3%) — which warrants confidence in current consensus rather than skepticism. The revision velocity here (+6.5% current year) is positive but well short of the >30% threshold that would signal a trough-earnings re-rating; this is a steady compounder, not a turnaround.

I am not crediting the headline +48% Q1 gross-revenue figure as growth; that number is inflated by transaction-based and Section 31 pass-throughs and overstates the economics. The relevant Q1 signal is the adjusted-EPS beat and the record net-revenue print driven by energy volatility.

The discount is not justified by inferior quality: ICE's margins, recurring-revenue share, and free-cash conversion are at or above the group. The market is pricing in (a) the Polymarket/crypto strategic uncertainty, (b) the mortgage segment's continued cyclical depression, and (c) a general de-rating of the stock from its August high.

The specific reason the market appears wrong: it is valuing ICE's recurring infrastructure and data businesses at a discount to less-diversified peers while assigning essentially zero value to the Mortgage Technology recovery optionality and the Treasury-clearing land grab. With estimates rising and a forward P/E below 17x next-year against the peer median, the analyst low target of $174 (+18%) functions as a floor rather than a ceiling for a name where consensus is revising upward. A re-rating to even 20x next-year EPS implies $176; 22x implies $194, close to the analyst mean.

The drawdown is moderate-to-substantial (~22% from the high), so the burden is to explain what sellers are missing. The price decline tracks the broad de-rating and energy-volatility normalization fears rather than any fundamental break: net revenue hit a record in Q1, estimates are up, and FCF is intact. This is improving risk/reward on unchanged-to-improving fundamentals.

## Competitive Landscape

ICE's competitive position is strong but the trajectory is mixed, which is why the score has come off its prior peak. The energy franchise (Brent, TTF) and exclusive index licenses are durable, liquidity-driven moats that competitors cannot easily replicate. The CDS clearing and evaluated-pricing businesses carry network and switching-cost advantages. The mortgage-software position is a regulated quasi-monopoly, though it operates under an FTC Consent Order from the Black Knight deal with ongoing FFIEC/CFPB oversight.

The pressures are real. NYSE listings face perennial Nasdaq competition. The Treasury-clearing entry is unproven and contested: CME is also entering and DTCC/FICC is the entrenched incumbent. The 10-K names AI commoditization of data products as an explicit threat: clients could use AI to build in-house pricing and pressure evaluated-pricing fees, a high-margin revenue line. Regulatory cross-currents (EMIR 3.0 Active Account Requirement, Basel III Endgame, BoE CCP reforms, market-data fee challenges, EUDR effects on cocoa/coffee volumes) collectively chip at the edges of the franchise without threatening the core. The net assessment is a wide-moat business whose moat is being tested at the margins, justifying a 7.5 rather than the 8.5 the position carried earlier.

## Risk Assessment

**Strategic drift into crypto and prediction markets.** The Polymarket commitment plus ICE Digital Trust crypto custody represents a meaningful departure from regulated infrastructure. The 10-K adds new risk factors for crypto-key loss, custody breaches, and reputational risk from evolving prediction-market business models. ICE is a strategic investor in and data partner with Polymarket; it does not own or control the platform. This is the single most idiosyncratic risk and the one least priced by consensus; it is the primary reason the risk score is held at 6.0 rather than higher.

**Clearing-house tail risk.** ICE is buyer-to-every-seller across 6 CCPs holding $81.2B of margin, of which $72.9B is U.S. Treasuries. A clearing-member default combined with a collateral-value shock could exceed ICE's contributed skin-in-the-game ($381M) and default insurance. ICE Clear Credit is FSOC-designated systemically important. Low probability, high severity, structurally embedded.

**Treasury-clearing cross-contamination.** Running Treasury and CDS clearing in the same legal entity (ICE Clear Credit) is flagged in the 10-K as a risk; adoption is uncertain.

**Mortgage cyclicality and goodwill.** Mortgage Technology revenue is geared to origination volume. $30.6B goodwill plus $15.4B intangibles equal ~34% of assets, mostly from Ellie Mae/Black Knight. A sustained mortgage downturn could trigger an impairment, though none has occurred to date.

**Regulatory.** Brexit equivalence extends only to June 30, 2028; EMIR 3.0 could pull euro short-rate clearing out of ICE Clear Europe; market-data fee scrutiny could compress high-margin data revenue.

### Options Market Signal

Options pricing is cautious but not bearish. Near-term ATM IV of 31.2% is rich relative to 21.5% trailing realized vol (a 9.7-point spread), so the market is pricing elevated uncertainty into the next few weeks. The put/call OI ratio rose from 1.21 to 2.02 alongside the 5.5% price decline, and the put/call volume ratio sits at 2.62 — elevated put demand that argues against chasing the entry aggressively. Skew is balanced at the front (-0.3%) but steepens to -3.4% at the 6-month tenor, indicating accumulating downside hedging into the fall rather than acute near-term fear. The term structure is essentially flat (31.2% near, 30.0% far). The options market corroborates that this is not a clean low-volatility entry, and a buyer should expect chop. It does not invalidate the fundamental case; it argues for scaling in rather than a single-clip purchase.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($194–210):** Energy volatility persists, Treasury clearing gains traction, and a rate-cut cycle revives mortgage origination, driving the embedded call option in Mortgage Technology into the money. EPS reaches ~$9.00–9.50 in FY2027 and the multiple re-rates from ~17x toward the low end of the peer band (~22x), producing $194–210. Buybacks accelerate per-share growth. This matches the analyst mean to high ($251) range.

**Bear Case ($125–135):** Energy volatility normalizes and the transaction book contracts; the Polymarket/crypto venture produces a write-down or reputational event; mortgage rates stay high and the Mortgage Technology recovery is deferred; data-fee regulation and AI commoditization compress the high-margin recurring lines. EPS stalls near $8.00 and the multiple stays depressed at ~16x, yielding ~$128, roughly 13% downside from current. The bounded downside (the franchise generates $3.87B FCF and the recurring base is sticky) is what makes the risk/reward asymmetric.

The asymmetry favors the long side: roughly 35% upside to the base/mean and ~13% downside to the bear case, on a business with a 51% recurring-revenue floor.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

- **Base case target: $176 over 12 months.** 20x next-year consensus EPS = $176. This sits between the bear ($128) and bull ($200+) cases and reflects a partial re-rating toward (still well below) the peer median as estimates continue rising and the mortgage optionality is incrementally recognized. Upside to base case: ~19%.
- **Bull case target: $200** (+35%), matching the analyst mean — a fuller re-rating to ~22x next-year EPS plus mortgage-cycle and Treasury-clearing upside.
- **Bear case target: $128** (–13%) — energy normalization, mortgage stall, and a Polymarket/crypto setback with the multiple stuck at ~16x.
- **Timeframe:** 12 months, anchored to the next four earnings reports (next: late July 2026) and the Q4 2026 Treasury-repo clearing launch.
- **Upside/downside from current ($147.85):** +19% to base, +35% to bull, –13% to bear.

**Thesis invalidation triggers:**
1. Current-year consensus EPS revisions turn negative (below $8.00) over two consecutive months, signaling that energy/recurring momentum has broken.
2. A material Polymarket or ICE Digital Trust write-down, regulatory action, or reputational event exceeding ~$500M.
3. A goodwill impairment in Mortgage Technology, or a sustained decline in Fixed Income & Data ASV growth below ~5% YoY, indicating the recurring base is eroding.

## Conclusion

ICE is a wide-moat, cash-generative financial-infrastructure franchise trading at roughly half the peer-median multiple while consensus estimates are rising and the company just printed its best quarter on record. The ~22% drawdown from the high reflects a de-rating and energy-normalization fears rather than any fundamental break; net revenue, FCF, and estimate revisions all point the right way. The 51% recurring-revenue base provides a downside floor, and the Mortgage Technology segment is an unrecognized rate-cut call option that turned GAAP-profitable at trough volumes.

The case is not without blemishes. The Polymarket and crypto-custody moves push ICE into territory the company's own filings flag as reputationally risky, insider activity shows no open-market conviction buying, and the competitive moat is being tested by AI, regulation, and the unproven Treasury-clearing entry. These factors hold the overall score at 7.0 rather than higher and warrant scaling into the position rather than a single purchase, given rich near-term implied volatility. On balance, the valuation gap against high-quality peers, the rising estimates, and the asymmetric risk/reward (roughly 19% to base case versus 13% to bear) support a **BUY**.
