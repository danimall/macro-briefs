## Executive Summary

Texas Instruments is executing on the most significant shift in its financial profile in a decade. After a 6-year elevated capex cycle ($4.5-5.0B annually) that compressed margins and FCF during a simultaneous revenue downturn, the company is now emerging with 300mm manufacturing capacity ready, revenue accelerating (+19% YoY in Q1 2026, data center +90%), and capex normalizing to $2-3B in 2026. This combination produces rapid FCF expansion — from $1.5B in FY2024 to an estimated $6-7B in FY2026.

The Q1 2026 earnings beat (EPS $1.68 vs. $1.38 estimate, +21.6% surprise) confirmed the inflection and drove a 16% single-day stock appreciation. Estimate revisions have surged (+19.7% for FY2026, +22.8% for the current quarter over 30 days), and the AI data center power management opportunity positions TXN's analog portfolio at the center of the industry's fastest-growing demand vector.

At $289, the stock has appreciated 80% from its November 2025 low of $152.73, now trades above the analyst consensus mean target of $279, and commands a forward P/E of ~38x FY2026E earnings ($7.67). While estimate revisions are strongly positive, the current price already discounts the recovery. China supply-chain exposure (~50% of products shipped into China) creates a specific binary risk that is underpriced at current multiples, particularly given the current tariff environment.

## Company Overview

Texas Instruments is the world's largest analog semiconductor company, generating $17.7B in FY2025 revenue across approximately 80,000 products sold to over 100,000 customers. The business operates through two primary segments: Analog (79% of revenue, encompassing power management and signal chain) and Embedded Processing (15%, including microcontrollers and connectivity). End-market exposure spans Industrial (33%), Automotive (33%), Data Center (9%), Personal Electronics (21%), and Communications Equipment (3%).

TXN's structural competitive advantage centers on its 300mm wafer manufacturing capability, which provides approximately 40% cost-per-chip advantage over the 200mm standard that dominates analog production. The company has invested approximately $18B+ in manufacturing capacity since 2020, building fabs in Sherman, TX and converting a facility in Lehi, UT. These investments are now largely complete, shifting TXN from an investment phase to a harvesting phase.

The company maintains direct sales relationships (80%+ of revenue), reducing distributor dependency and providing superior visibility into end-market demand. Its dividend aristocrat status (22 consecutive years of increases) and $18.6B remaining buyback authorization reflect a capital return discipline that has defined the company's shareholder value proposition.

## Financial Analysis

**Revenue Recovery:** Revenue troughed at $15.6B in FY2024 after declining from the $20.0B 2022 peak. FY2025 recovered to $17.7B (+13%), and Q1 2026's $4.83B quarterly revenue annualizes to ~$19.3B. Management's Q2 2026 guidance midpoint of $5.2B implies continued acceleration, with consensus FY2026 revenue tracking toward $20B+.

**Margin Inflection:** The critical margin story reflects operating leverage as elevated depreciation ($2.16B annualized from the capex cycle) is spread across a growing revenue base. Q1 2026 operating margin reached 37.5%, up from 32.5% a year ago and 34.1% for full-year 2025. Analog segment margins expanded to 41.7% (from 38.6%), while Embedded improved to 16.9% (from 11.3%) as LFAB utilization increases.

**FCF Acceleration:** This is the central financial thesis. TTM FCF surged from $1.5B (FY2024) to $4.35B (Q1 2026 TTM), a 154% YoY improvement. With FY2026 capex guided to $2-3B vs. ~$4.6B in FY2025, and revenue growing 20%+, FCF could approach $6-7B for full-year 2026. At the current $263B market cap, this implies FCF yield improving from sub-1% in 2024 to approximately 2.3-2.7% in 2026.

**Balance Sheet:** Cash and short-term investments of $5.1B against $12.9B long-term debt (D/E 1.13x). Current ratio of 4.35x provides substantial liquidity. However, the pending $7.5B Silicon Labs acquisition (expected H1 2027) will add approximately $5-6B in incremental debt, pushing total leverage toward $18-20B. Interest expense already rose from $214M (2022) to $543M (2025) as low-rate debt matures.

**Capital Return:** TTM dividends of $5.05B plus $982M in buybacks returned $6.0B to shareholders. The $5.68 annualized dividend per share yields 1.97% at the current price.

## Competitive Landscape

TXN's competitive position is the strongest dimension of the investment case. The 300mm manufacturing advantage (40% cost-per-chip vs. 200mm) is now fully built out while competitors remain largely on 200mm. This creates structural margin superiority that will widen as utilization increases.

The analog semiconductor market is $70B+ and highly fragmented — TXN holds mid-teens market share. Unlike digital semiconductors where Moore's Law drives rapid obsolescence, analog products have long lifecycles (10-15 years), which means TXN's 80,000+ product portfolio generates recurring revenue with minimal redesign cost.

TXN's 800V DC power architecture positions it as a preferred power management supplier for next-generation AI infrastructure. ADI is also pursuing this opportunity, but TXN's cost structure and scale provide advantages in high-volume deployment.

Competitive threats from Chinese analog manufacturers (3Peak, SG Micro) are real at the low end but limited in their ability to address high-performance industrial and automotive applications where qualification processes are lengthy and reliability requirements are stringent.

## Risk Assessment

**China Supply Chain Exposure (HIGH SEVERITY):** Products shipped into China represent approximately 50% of revenue — far larger than the 20% customer-headquartered-in-China figure suggests. In the current tariff environment, this creates material binary risk. Escalation of US-China trade restrictions, reciprocal Chinese tariffs, or accelerated Chinese supplier qualification efforts could disrupt a significant portion of TXN's revenue with limited near-term mitigation options.

**Inventory Risk (MODERATE):** At 209 days, inventory remains well above historical norms. Management characterizes this as strategic positioning with broad-based, low-obsolescence products. The declining trend (from 241 days) supports this narrative, but a demand deceleration would convert this inventory into a margin headwind.

**Silicon Labs Acquisition (MODERATE):** $7.5B all-cash deal pushing total debt toward $20B. China MOFCOM regulatory approval is uncertain. Integration execution adds complexity during a period when TXN should be focused on maximizing returns from its new capacity.

**Cyclicality:** Semiconductor cycles have compressed to 2-3 year durations. With Q1 2026 showing +19% growth and inventories still elevated at some customers, demand moderation in H2 2026 or 2027 is plausible.

**Multiple Compression Risk:** At 33x FY2027E, any disappointment in the growth trajectory (one weak quarter, China disruption, inventory buildup at customers) could compress the multiple back toward 25-28x — yielding 15-25% downside even without fundamental deterioration.
