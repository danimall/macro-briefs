## Executive Summary

Glacier Bancorp operates a differentiated decentralized community banking model across 9 Mountain West and Southwest states with $32B in assets. The company is experiencing a sustained net interest margin recovery — NIM has expanded for 9 consecutive quarters from 2.73% (2023) to 3.80% (Q1 2026), with management targeting 4%+ by H2 2026. This creates a visible and partially "locked-in" earnings trajectory as $3B in loans reprice at 75-100bps higher and the low-yielding bond portfolio ($7.1B at 1-2% yields) runs off.

The complication is valuation. At $47.90 and ~24x trailing EPS ($1.99), GBCI trades at a significant premium to the regional bank peer median P/E of 12.4x. Even on a forward basis (~$3.16 consensus for FY2026), the stock sits at approximately 15x forward earnings, still well above peers. The market appears to be paying for the NIM expansion story and GBCI's historically conservative credit culture, but four consecutive quarterly earnings misses and only 3% organic loan growth (ex-acquisitions) suggest the premium may be fragile. The stock offers 13-25% upside to analyst targets ($54-$60), but the miss streak and elevated valuation relative to fundamentally similar peers limit conviction for a BUY recommendation.

## Company Overview

Glacier Bancorp is a Montana-headquartered bank holding company operating through 18 separately branded bank divisions across Montana, Idaho, Utah, Washington, Wyoming, Colorado, Arizona, Nevada, and Texas. The company's core differentiator is its decentralized structure — each division retains its own name, management team, and community identity while leveraging centralized capital, technology, and compliance functions.

The company holds dominant deposit market share in Montana (26.9% of served-county FDIC deposits) and Wyoming (15.3%), with strong positions in Idaho (10.8%) and Nevada (5.9%). Growth markets (Utah, Colorado, Arizona, Texas) represent smaller share with meaningful expansion opportunity. The 2025 acquisition of Guaranty Bancshares ($3.4B assets) opened Texas, the company's largest new market entry in a decade.

Key business characteristics: 65% CRE loan concentration with conservative average LTV of 57%, 30% non-interest bearing deposits, minimal brokered deposits ($4M), and 164 consecutive quarterly dividends. The company's growth strategy relies heavily on serial acquisition of community banks, supplemented by modest organic growth.

## Financial Analysis

**Earnings Trajectory:** Net income bottomed at $190M in 2024 and recovered to $239M in 2025, with Q1 2026 at $82.1M (+51% YoY). The run-rate annualizes to approximately $328M, representing a potential 65% EPS growth from the 2025 reported figure of $1.99.

**Net Interest Margin:** The primary earnings driver. NIM expanded from 2.73% (2023 trough) to 3.80% in Q1 2026 — a 107bp recovery over 9 consecutive quarters. Management targets 4%+ by H2 2026 based on three structural tailwinds: (a) $3B in loans repricing at 75-100bps higher over 12 months, (b) new originations at 6.5-6.75%, and (c) the $7.1B bond portfolio (1-2% yields) running off and being reinvested at materially higher rates.

**Balance Sheet Strength:** CET1 at 12.71% (well above 6.5% threshold). FHLB advances fully repaid in Q1 2026 (down from $1.8B end-2024). Total liquidity availability of ~$9B. Tangible book value per share of $21.01, up 12% YoY.

**Cash Flow:** Operating cash flow of $374M and free cash flow of $348M in 2025, comfortably covering the $172M annual dividend obligation.

**Efficiency:** Improved from 66.7% (2024) to 62.5% (2025), with management targeting 54-55% by Q4 2026. Full-year expense guidance of $756-766M.

**Concerning Trends:** Four consecutive quarterly earnings misses (Q2-Q4 2025, Q1 2026), missing by an average of 7%. This suggests either structural over-estimation by analysts or persistent execution shortfalls that management has not corrected. Additionally, NPAs increased 148% during 2025 to $68.9M (0.22% of assets) and early-stage delinquencies surged to $78.8M — partially acquisition-related but still directionally concerning.

## Growth Analysis

GBCI's growth profile has two distinct components:

**Acquisition-Driven Growth (Primary Engine):** The 2025 acquisitions of Guaranty Bancshares ($3.4B assets) and Bank of Idaho ($1.4B assets) added $4.7B in total assets, $3.2B in loans, and $3.8B in deposits. Management continues to pursue Texas M&A with "multiple conversations" ongoing. The regulatory environment is described as "more predictable and supportive" for bank M&A.

**Organic Growth (Secondary):** Excluding acquisitions, 2025 loan growth was approximately 3% and deposit growth was just 1%. Q1 2026 showed 2% annualized loan growth (seasonal weakness). The Texas division is showing 6%+ loan growth, outpacing legacy markets. Management expects acceleration in Q2/Q3.

**EPS Growth:** Consensus projects $3.16 for FY2026 (+59% YoY from $1.99) and $3.65 for FY2027 (+16% YoY). The growth is primarily NIM-driven rather than balance sheet expansion-driven, which provides some durability but limited upside surprise potential.

Estimate revisions are flat — current quarter estimates unchanged over 30 days, up only 1.1% over 90 days. The NIM normalization is gradual, visible, and already well-understood by the market.

## Valuation Assessment

At $47.90, GBCI trades at:
- **~24x trailing P/E** (on $1.99 TTM EPS)
- **15.2x forward P/E** (on $3.16 FY2026 consensus)
- **13.1x FY2027 P/E** (on $3.65 consensus)
- **2.3x tangible book value** ($21.01 TBV/share)
- **1.5x book value** ($32.42 BVPS)

Peer regional banks trade at a median of 12.4x earnings. GBCI's forward P/E of 15.2x represents a 23% premium to peers. The market is paying for: (a) the NIM expansion trajectory, (b) a historically conservative credit culture with 0.06-0.08% net charge-offs, and (c) strategic optionality from the Texas expansion.

Whether this premium is justified depends on execution. The four consecutive earnings misses are directly relevant — if management cannot deliver on the 4%+ NIM target or the 54-55% efficiency ratio target, the premium compresses. Analyst consensus targets range from $54-$60, implying 13-25% upside, but these targets reflect the same NIM expansion assumptions already embedded in the stock price.

The 2.8% dividend yield provides modest income support but is below the peer median of 3.0-3.6%.

The valuation is not objectively expensive on forward estimates, but the premium to peers combined with a perfect miss record (4/4 quarters below estimates) creates an unfavorable setup for near-term re-rating.

## Competitive Landscape

GBCI's competitive advantages are genuine but incremental:

1. **Decentralized model** creates local loyalty that larger banks struggle to replicate. The 18 separate brands maintain community relationships that drive deposit retention and loan origination.

2. **Dominant Montana/Wyoming positioning** provides stable, low-cost deposit funding in markets with limited competition from money-center banks.

3. **Conservative credit underwriting** (57% average LTV on CRE, $795K average balance, geographic diversification) differentiates from peers who took more aggressive positions during the low-rate era.

4. **Acquisition platform** allows consolidation of smaller community banks that face succession/scale challenges.

The competitive position is stable rather than improving. Organic growth of 3% is below-average for the peer group. The Texas market entry is positive but GBCI is a small player in a highly competitive banking market. Fintech competition and internet-based lending create secular headwinds for community banks in deposit gathering and lending.

The 5-year total shareholder return of 111.65 vs. peer group of 152.71 (approximately 41 percentage points of underperformance) suggests the market structurally discounts the serial-acquirer model, likely due to dilution from stock-based deals and the $1.48B goodwill balance (35% of equity).

## Risk Assessment

**CRE Concentration (Primary Risk):** 65% of the loan portfolio is commercial real estate ($13.6B). While individual loan characteristics are conservative (57% LTV, $795K average), a broad regional real estate downturn would create correlated losses across the portfolio. The 10-K notes that office exposure is 11.8% of CRE, largely suburban/rural — limiting CBD office risk but not eliminating it.

**Goodwill Impairment Risk:** $1.48B of goodwill (35% of equity) from serial acquisitions. Any write-down directly reduces capital ratios and earnings. This risk is elevated in a rising-rate environment where acquired franchises may not achieve projected returns.

**Credit Quality Deterioration:** NPAs up 148% in 2025 (to 0.22% of assets); early-stage delinquencies up to $78.8M. While still historically low, the trajectory is adverse. A $27.3M non-accrual C&I loan appeared in 2025. Q1 2026 NPAs ticked up to 25bps. With elevated rates and CRE refinancing stress becoming visible nationally, GBCI's Mountain West CRE exposure warrants elevated monitoring.

**Execution Risk on NIM Target:** Management's 4%+ NIM target by H2 2026 requires continued loan repricing, deposit cost control, and no reversal in rate expectations. If the Fed hikes, the deposit cost structure could deteriorate faster than asset repricing benefits accumulate.

**Earnings Miss Pattern:** Four consecutive misses averaging -7%. This suggests either systematic analyst overestimation (and therefore current $3.16 estimate may be high) or operational friction in integration that management is not communicating.

### Options Market Signal

Near-term IV of 27.0% sits below 1-year historical vol of 30.3%, with the IV percentile at just 8% — complacent positioning. Put/call OI ratio of 1.12 is modestly elevated but not alarming. The contango term structure (27% near, 33% far) reflects normal conditions without imminent stress. Balanced put/call skew (0.5%) confirms no directional conviction in the options market. The market is not pricing any near-term catalyst for GBCI, consistent with the flat estimate revision picture.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($54-$60; +13% to +25%):**
NIM reaches 4%+ by H2 2026 as management targets. Efficiency ratio compresses to 54-55%. Full-year 2026 EPS hits or exceeds $3.16, with 2027 at $3.65+. Texas operations scale faster than expected with another strategic acquisition. Credit quality normalizes (recent deterioration was acquisition-related, not systemic). Proposed regulatory capital relief (75-80bps CET1) unlocks buyback or special dividend. Stock re-rates to 17x forward earnings = $62.

**Bear Case ($38-$42; -13% to -21%):**
The earnings miss pattern continues through FY2026. CRE portfolio shows accelerating stress as national CRE maturities create broader contagion, even in Mountain West markets. NIM expansion stalls at 3.9% as deposit costs rise or loan growth disappoints. Goodwill impairment from Guaranty or Bank of Idaho. Stock de-rates to peer median of 12.4x forward earnings = $39.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade Triggers (to BUY):**
1. GBCI delivers an earnings beat (breaking the 4-quarter miss streak) on Q2 2026 results, confirming NIM expansion trajectory is executable
2. Stock pulls back below $42 (13.3x forward FY2026 EPS, approaching peer median valuation)
3. Organic loan growth accelerates to 5%+ annualized for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating growth beyond acquisition dependency

**Downgrade Triggers (to AVOID):**
1. NPAs exceed 50bps of assets (from current 25bps), signaling CRE stress is broadening
2. NIM stalls below 3.9% for two consecutive quarters, invalidating management's 4%+ target
3. 5th consecutive earnings miss by >5%, confirming systematic overestimation of earnings power

**Reassessment Timeline:** Q2 2026 earnings (expected late July 2026). Either the miss streak breaks and NIM expansion continues, or execution concerns become structural.

## Conclusion

Glacier Bancorp is a well-managed community bank with a visible NIM expansion pathway and a differentiated operating model. The insider buying cluster in October/November 2025 (well-timed, multi-executive) and the structural tailwinds from asset repricing support the directional earnings thesis. However, the stock's significant P/E premium to regional bank peers combined with four consecutive earnings misses and modest organic growth create insufficient margin of safety for a BUY at current levels. The CRE concentration (65% of loans) with CRE refinancing stress nationally and rates elevated adds risk that the premium valuation does not adequately compensate.

At ~15x forward earnings for a bank growing EPS 16% in FY2027 (PEG ~0.9x), the valuation is not unreasonable on an absolute basis. The issue is relative: peers with similar characteristics trade at 10-13x. GBCI needs to demonstrate that its credit quality holds and its NIM targets are achievable (not just forecasted) before the premium is justified. Wait for either a valuation reset (below $42) or fundamental confirmation (first earnings beat in over a year).
