## Executive Summary

Healthcare Realty Trust is the largest pure-play medical outpatient building (MOB) REIT in the United States with ~$10.3B in gross investments across 502 properties. The company is in the late stages of a post-merger restructuring that has involved $1.1B in asset dispositions (2025), a 23% dividend cut, $1.25B in total debt reduction since 2022, and aggressive share repurchases at prices well below current levels. Same-store NOI growth accelerated to 7.1% in Q1 2026 with occupancy reaching 92.1%, representing some of the strongest organic growth in the healthcare REIT sector.

The investment case rests on the transformation from an overleveraged, dilutive post-merger portfolio into a higher-quality, right-sized portfolio generating growing FFO per share despite shrinking total revenue. Normalized FFO/share has risen from $1.56 (FY2024) to $1.61 (FY2025) to $1.64 annualized (Q1 2026). However, the stock has already appreciated 42% from its 52-week low and now trades within 1% of the 52-week high and essentially at the analyst consensus mean target of $20.45. With EPS estimates declining across all forward periods and $1.1B in debt maturing over the next 18 months in an elevated rate environment, the risk/reward from current levels is approximately balanced.

## Company Overview

Healthcare Realty Trust operates as a self-managed REIT focused exclusively on medical outpatient buildings, primarily those located on or adjacent to major hospital campuses. The portfolio spans 27 states with ~29 million square feet across 502 consolidated properties plus a 30% weighted average interest in 62 unconsolidated joint ventures. The tenant base is highly diversified (no single tenant exceeds 10% of revenue) across 30+ physician specialties including surgery centers, imaging centers, cancer treatment facilities, and diagnostic labs.

The lease structure is predominantly net (58%) and modified gross (30%), with 96% featuring fixed base rent escalators averaging 2.9% annually. Weighted average remaining lease term is 4.4 years. The company self-manages ~92% of its portfolio, providing operational control and reducing third-party leakage.

The central narrative is post-merger cleanup. The 2022 combination with Healthcare Trust, Inc. occurred at peak commercial real estate valuations. Since then, the company has written off all $250.5M in goodwill, taken ~$860M in cumulative property impairments (2023-2025), and disposed of $1.1B in non-core assets during 2025 at a 6.7% weighted average cap rate. This is effectively an admission that the merger overpaid for certain assets, and management is working systematically to improve the surviving portfolio's quality.

## Financial Analysis

**Income & Cash Flow:**
- Q1 2026 rental income: $267.6M (down 7.4% YoY due to dispositions)
- Same-store cash NOI: $153.6M (+7.1% YoY, accelerating from 5.0% full-year 2025)
- Normalized FFO/share: $0.41 (Q1 2026), up from $0.39 in Q1 2025 (+5.1%)
- FAD: $448M (FY2025), implying ~$1.28/share annualized
- Operating cash flow: $457M (FY2025)

Per-share metrics are improving despite total revenue declining because: (1) disposed assets were at lower yields than the retained portfolio, (2) interest expense fell 19.9% YoY in Q1 to $43.9M from deleveraging, and (3) same-store growth is accelerating.

**Balance Sheet:**
- Total debt: $4.1B (down from $5.35B in 2022)
- D/E ratio: 0.98x
- Weighted average effective rate: ~4.5%
- Debt service coverage: 3.5x (covenant minimum: 1.5x)
- Unencumbered assets: ~$10.4B gross book value
- Revolver availability: $1.2B

The $1.25B in debt reduction since the merger is meaningful. However, $600M in Senior Notes mature in August 2026 and $500M in July 2027. Refinancing at current market rates (likely 5.5-6.0%+ for BBB-rated REITs) will increase interest costs by $15-25M annually relative to the maturing tranches, partially offsetting same-store NOI gains.

**Dividend:**
- Current: $0.96/share annualized (4.7% yield at $20.36)
- Normalized FFO payout ratio: ~59%
- FAD payout ratio: ~75%
- The 23% dividend cut from $0.31 to $0.24/quarter freed ~$100M+ annually for buybacks and debt reduction

The dividend is well-covered and the reduced payout provides significant retained capital for value-accretive uses.

**Capital Allocation:**
- $100M in share repurchases in Q1 2026 at ~$17.38 average price
- $400M remaining on $500M authorization (expires Oct 2026)
- Zero acquisitions in 2025 or Q1 2026
- ~$106M in active development, ~$291M in redevelopment

Management buying back shares at ~$17.38 when the stock now trades at $20.36 represents a ~17% gain on that deployed capital.

## Growth Analysis

Same-store cash NOI growth of 7.1% in Q1 2026 is among the strongest in the healthcare REIT sector. This is driven by:
- Occupancy improvement to 92.1% (from ~90.5% portfolio-wide)
- Contractual rent escalators averaging 2.9%
- Positive cash leasing spreads (2.7% in FY2025)
- Operational improvements from the self-management model

However, growth prospects are tempered by several factors:
1. **Estimate revisions are uniformly negative.** Current quarter EPS estimates fell 37% over 90 days, next quarter fell 65.6%, current year fell 22.2%, and next year fell 66.7%. This counters the positive same-store narrative.
2. **Total revenue continues shrinking** as dispositions reduce the portfolio. Same-store growth must exceed the drag from portfolio shrinkage.
3. **Occupancy at 92.1% is approaching natural ceiling** for MOB portfolios (typically 93-95% peak). Incremental gains become harder.
4. **Lease escalators of 2.9% may lag inflation** in the current environment, meaning real NOI growth from existing leases could compress.
5. **Development pipeline is modest** (~$397M total) relative to the $10.3B portfolio, limiting external growth contribution.

The normalized FFO/share growth trajectory ($1.56 → $1.61 → $1.64 annualized) is positive but modest, suggesting 3-5% annual FFO/share growth is the achievable range assuming continued buybacks offset disposition drag.

## Valuation Assessment

At $20.36, Healthcare Realty trades at:
- Forward P/FFO: ~12.4x ($1.64 annualized normalized FFO)
- Dividend yield: 4.7%
- Implied cap rate: approximately 6.2-6.5% (based on NOI/enterprise value)

**Peer comparison:**
- SBRA: 32.9x P/E, 5.7% yield
- CTRE: 26.1x P/E, 3.8% yield
- LTC: 15.2x P/E, 5.8% yield
- MPT: Negative P/E, 7.0% yield (distressed)

HR's negative GAAP P/E (-34.9x) is misleading due to non-cash impairment charges; on an FFO basis the valuation is reasonable relative to peers. The 4.7% yield sits below the peer median of 5.7%, suggesting the market is pricing in the growth differential (HR's same-store growth exceeds most peers).

**NAV estimate:** With $10.3B in gross assets, ~$4.1B in debt, and ~347M diluted shares, a rough NAV range is $16-20/share depending on cap rate assumptions (6.0-7.5% range). The stock at $20.36 trades at approximately 1.0x NAV.

**Critical concern:** The stock is near the 52-week high ($20.60) and within 0.5% of the analyst consensus mean target ($20.45). With 8 of 11 analysts rating it HOLD, the consensus view is that fair value has been reached. The analyst high target of $22.00 provides only 8% upside.

## Competitive Landscape

Healthcare Realty's position as the largest pure-play MOB REIT provides scale advantages in property management and tenant relationships. The on-campus/near-campus focus (adjacent to health systems) creates a locational moat — hospitals cannot easily replicate these adjacencies.

However, competitive pressures are increasing:
- Leasing commissions as a percentage of NOI rose from 4.3% (2023) to 7.5% (2025), indicating the company must spend more to attract and retain tenants
- Rent abatements increased from $14.3M to $20.9M over the same period
- Purchase options on $1.1B in properties give health system tenants the ability to "cherry-pick" the best assets at potentially below-market prices
- The company notes some competitors have lower costs of capital

The shift toward outpatient care delivery is a genuine structural tailwind. CMS site-neutral payment policies could benefit on-campus MOBs specifically (HR's core niche). The aging US population creates sustained demand growth for healthcare facilities broadly.

Counterbalancing these tailwinds is policy uncertainty from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, which HR's 10-K specifically flags as potentially affecting demand for properties and tenant viability through Medicaid/Medicare reimbursement changes.

## Risk Assessment

**Near-term debt maturities:** Refinancing at current rates (likely 150-200bps above existing coupons) creates a $15-25M annual interest cost headwind. This is the most quantifiable near-term risk.

**Policy risk:** The OBBBA legislation creates uncertainty around healthcare reimbursement that could affect tenant financial health and lease renewal rates. This is a new risk factor added to the 10-K, suggesting management views it as material.

**Impairment history:** $860M in cumulative impairments (2023-2025) plus $250.5M in goodwill writeoff demonstrates that portfolio value was systematically overstated. While impairments have declined sharply in Q1 2026, additional writedowns remain possible on the remaining portfolio.

**Estimate deterioration:** Negative EPS estimate revisions across all forward periods (ranging from -10% to -67% over 90 days) diverge from strong same-store operating metrics. This divergence warrants monitoring.

**Restructuring not complete:** $7.6M in restructuring charges in Q1 2026 alone (vs. $0.5M in Q1 2025), combined with CFO change, auditor change, and ongoing organizational transformation suggest the company is still not operating at steady-state.

**Interest rate sensitivity:** As a yield-sensitive REIT, HR is directly affected by the trajectory of long-term rates. Any further rate increases would pressure the stock.

## Investment Thesis

**Bull Case ($22-24):**
- Same-store NOI growth sustains at 5-7% as occupancy trends toward 93-94%
- Continued buybacks at current pace ($400M remaining) drive 5-8% FFO/share growth annually
- Rate environment moderates, compressing cap rates and expanding REIT multiples
- Company transitions from restructuring mode to growth mode post-2027 debt refinancing
- 14-15x P/FFO on $1.75 FFO/share = $24.50-$26.25 by late 2027

**Bear Case ($15-17):**
- Rate hikes materialize, pushing yields higher, compressing REIT valuations
- OBBBA legislation reduces healthcare system profitability, increasing tenant defaults
- Debt refinancing at 5.5-6.0% increases interest expense by $25M+ annually, offsetting NOI gains
- Same-store growth decelerates as occupancy plateaus and leasing costs continue rising
- Stock re-rates to 10-11x FFO on $1.55/share = $15.50-$17.05

**Base Case ($20-21):**
- The current price approximately reflects fair value: 12-13x normalized FFO with 4.7% yield in a higher-rate environment. Adequate compensation for a turnaround REIT with good same-store metrics but policy uncertainty, refinancing risk, and no external growth.

## Investment Horizon & Exit Criteria

**Upgrade triggers (HOLD → BUY):**
- Price decline to $17-18 (yield above 5.3%, P/FFO below 11x) providing adequate margin of safety
- 10Y Treasury yield declining below 4.5%, removing the rate headwind for REIT valuations
- Resolution of OBBBA policy uncertainty with neutral-to-positive healthcare reimbursement outcome
- Demonstrated successful refinancing of the August 2026 $600M maturity at spread tightening

**Downgrade triggers (HOLD → AVOID):**
- Same-store NOI growth decelerating below 3% for two consecutive quarters
- Occupancy declining below 90% (reverting to pre-improvement levels)
- Additional material impairments exceeding $100M, suggesting portfolio quality still deteriorating
- Dividend cut below $0.20/quarter, signaling further cash flow stress

**Reassessment timeline:** Next earnings report (likely July/August 2026) will provide Q2 same-store data and update on August 2026 debt refinancing plans. The August 2026 maturity itself is the most important near-term event.

## Conclusion

Healthcare Realty has executed a credible post-merger turnaround: deleveraging by $1.25B, cutting the dividend to fund buybacks at meaningful discounts to current price, and delivering accelerating same-store NOI growth. Management's insider purchases and capital allocation decisions demonstrate alignment with shareholders.

However, the stock has already re-rated from $14.09 to $20.36 (+42%) and now trades at approximately fair value based on FFO multiples, NAV, and analyst consensus targets. Negative estimate revisions, $1.1B in near-term debt maturities in a hostile rate environment, and policy uncertainty from healthcare legislation create balanced risks against the positive operational momentum. With 8 of 11 analysts at HOLD and consensus target at $20.45, the market agrees that fair value has been approximately reached.

For income-oriented investors, the 4.7% yield with 59% FFO payout ratio and ongoing buyback support provides adequate risk-adjusted return for a holding position. The clear action is to wait for either (a) a price pullback creating a more favorable entry or (b) confirmation that refinancing risk has been navigated successfully before committing additional capital.
